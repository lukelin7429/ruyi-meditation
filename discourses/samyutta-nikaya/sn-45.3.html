Why this discourse

Where SN 45.2 shows Ānanda offering the modest claim and being corrected, this discourse shows Sāriputta arriving at the Buddha's own corrected formula directly and unprompted — “good friends, companions, and associates are the whole of the spiritual life” — and receiving praise (“good, good, Sāriputta!”) rather than any correction at all, confirming this is a genuine teaching two different senior disciples are shown testing, not a lesson particular to Ānanda's own understanding.

Reading guide

The same claim, offered correctly from the start

Sāriputta states “the whole of the spiritual life” directly, without first offering “half” the way Ānanda did — confirmed by reading this discourse's own opening exchange directly, the difference between the two discourses is not in the final formula but in how each disciple arrives at it.

Compressed, not merely repeated

The eightfold path's own eight factors, spelled out one by one in SN 45.2, are compressed here into a single run-on list, and the same is true of the closing “another way to understand” argument — confirmed by direct comparison, this discourse assumes its own reader already knows the fuller version rather than restating it at length.

Key terms

sakalamevidaṁ brahmacariyaṁ — “the whole of the spiritual life” — Sāriputta's own claim, offered directly and confirmed without correction
sādhu sādhu, sāriputta — “good, good, Sāriputta!” — the Buddha's own response, praise rather than the correction Ānanda received in SN 45.2
kalyāṇamittatā — “good friends, companions, and associates” — the identical quality both this discourse and SN 45.2 concern
ariyaṁ aṭṭhaṅgikaṁ maggaṁ bhāveti — “develop the noble eightfold path” — the structural first reason, compressed here into a single list rather than SN 45.2's own factor-by-factor treatment
maṁ āgamma kalyāṇamittaṁ — “by relying on me as a good friend” — the second reason, identical in wording to SN 45.2's own

The text

The complete discourse. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. Then Sāriputta went up to the Buddha, bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him: “Sir, good friends, companions, and associates are the whole of the spiritual life.” “Good, good, Sāriputta! Good friends, companions, and associates are the whole of the spiritual life. A mendicant with good friends, companions, and associates can expect to develop and cultivate the noble eightfold path. And how does a mendicant with good friends develop and cultivate the noble eightfold path? It’s when a mendicant develops right view, right purpose, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right immersion, which rely on seclusion, fading away, and cessation, and ripen as letting go. That’s how a mendicant with good friends develops and cultivates the noble eightfold path. And here’s another way to understand how good friends are the whole of the spiritual life. For, by relying on me as a good friend, sentient beings who are liable to rebirth, old age, and death, to sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress are freed from all these things. This is another way to understand how good friends are the whole of the spiritual life.”
