Why this discourse

This discourse opens Valāhakasaṃyutta ("Linked Discourses on Cloud Gods") with yet another genuinely distinct taxonomy: valāhakā (thundercloud gods, weather deities) come in five kinds — cool, warm, stormy, windy, and rainy — a classification organized by weather phenomenon rather than SN 29's fourfold birth-mode or SN 31's tenfold dwelling-place scheme. Unlike either of those saṃyuttas, Valāhakasaṃyutta will close with a genuinely new kind of discourse (SN 32.53–57) explaining these same five weather-types as the gods' own present-tense meteorological activity, not merely a rebirth destination.

Reading guide

Five weather-types, not four births or ten dwellings

"There are gods of the cool thunderclouds, warm thunderclouds, stormy thunderclouds, windy thunderclouds, and rainy thunderclouds" — a fivefold classification organized entirely around weather phenomena, a third distinct organizing principle after SN 29's birth-modes and SN 31's dwelling-places.

Valāhakā, gods identified with the weather itself

Unlike dragons, phoenixes, or centaurs, valāhakā are named directly for the weather conditions they're associated with — their very identity as a class of god is inseparable from a specific meteorological phenomenon.

A taxonomy this saṃyutta's remaining discourses assume

As with SN 29.1's, SN 30.1's, and SN 31.1's own opening taxonomies, every one of the remaining 56 discourses in this saṃyutta takes this fivefold division for granted rather than restating it, making this short opening discourse this saṃyutta's own structural foundation.

A saṃyutta with its own genuinely distinct closing arc

Where Nāgasaṃyutta, Supaṇṇasaṃyutta, and Gandhabbakāyasaṃyutta all close purely on rebirth-cause material, this saṃyutta will close instead with five short discourses (SN 32.53–57) explaining why weather itself happens — a genuinely new kind of content for this project's SN coverage, not covered by this discourse's own opening taxonomy alone.

Key terms

valāhaka — thundercloud — the class of weather-god this entire saṃyutta concerns
sītavalāhaka — cool thundercloud gods — the first of the five classes named here
uṇhavalāhaka — warm thundercloud gods — the second class
vassavalāhaka — rainy thundercloud gods — the fifth and final class, and the subject of SN 32.57's own closing discourse
devā — gods — the general term this discourse uses for the valāhakā throughout

The text

The discourse in full — genuinely this short in the source, a single flat fivefold taxonomy. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. “Mendicants, I will teach you about the gods of the thundercloud host. Listen … And what are the gods of the thundercloud host? There are gods of the cool thunderclouds, warm thunderclouds, stormy thunderclouds, windy thunderclouds, and rainy thunderclouds. These are called gods of the thundercloud host.”
