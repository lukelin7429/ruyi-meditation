Giving Up the Fetters

Why this discourse

Confirmed by direct reading, this discourse states a single claim, compressed via “…pe…”: mindfulness of breathing, developed and cultivated, “leads to giving up the fetters” (saṁyojanappahāna) — the first of four named benefits this project has confirmed form a single series, continuing through SN 54.18 and SN 54.19 before all four are restated together in full at this vagga's own closing discourse, SN 54.20.

Reading guide

The first of a four-item series

This project's own direct confirmation, from reading ahead to SN 54.20's own closing uddāna: this discourse opens a series of four named benefits — giving up the fetters, uprooting the underlying tendencies, completely understanding the course of time, and ending the defilements — each stated separately and briefly here, then restated together with the full formula at SN 54.20.

The ten fetters, not spelled out here

This discourse's own compressed claim does not itself enumerate the ten fetters (saṁyojana) traditionally bound to the four stages of awakening; only the bare claim that mindfulness of breathing leads to their abandonment is given, with no further detail in this file.

Key terms

ānāpānassatisamādhi bhāvito bahulīkato — “immersion due to mindfulness of breathing, developed and cultivated” — the recurring cause named across this entire four-item series
saṁyojanappahāna — “giving up the fetters” — this discourse's own named benefit, the first of four in this series
saṁyojana — “fetter” — one of the ten traditional bonds tying beings to continued existence, not itself enumerated in this brief discourse
…pe… — the elision marking, standing in for the full formula given in complete form only at this series' own closing discourse, SN 54.20
sattamaṁ — “seventh” — this discourse's own closing ordinal, confirming its position as the seventh discourse of Vagga 2

The text

Every genuinely translated fragment given in the consulted English source. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Mendicants, when immersion due to mindfulness of breathing is developed and cultivated it leads to giving up the fetters …”
