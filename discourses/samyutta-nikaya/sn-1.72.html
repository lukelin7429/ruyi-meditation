A Chariot

Why this discourse

The question asks about outward signs of identity across four different things: what marks a chariot, what marks fire, what marks a nation, and what marks a woman? The answer names a visible signal for each: a banner for a chariot, smoke for fire, its ruler for a nation, and, in the verse's own social framework, a husband for a woman.

Reading guide

Outward signs, not inner essence

Every one of this discourse's four answers names something visible from a distance rather than an intrinsic quality: a banner (dhaja) identifies a chariot before its rider is seen, smoke identifies a fire before its flame is visible, a ruler identifies a nation to outsiders. The question is about recognition, not definition.

A social framework worth naming plainly

The verse's fourth answer — that a woman is known by her husband (patinā) — extends the same ‘identifying marker’ logic to a person, but does so from the specific social framework of the verse's own time and setting, in which marital status served as a visible social marker for women in a way it typically did not for men. This reading guide names that standpoint directly, in keeping with how it treated SN 1.58's related line.

A brief riddle without further elaboration

Unlike several riddles in this collection that build to a doctrinal point, this discourse offers no further layer beyond its four direct answers — a simple set of observations about how identity becomes visible, without extending the pattern into ethical or spiritual territory.

Key terms

ratha — “a chariot” — this discourse's title, and the first item its riddle asks about.
dhajena — “by a banner” — the visible marker identifying a chariot from a distance.
dhūmena — “by smoke” — the marker identifying fire before its flame is directly seen.
raññā — “by its ruler” — the marker identifying a nation to outsiders.
patinā — “by her husband” — the verse's final answer, spoken from the specific social standpoint of its own time and setting.

The text

The discourse in full, read plainly for what it is. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“What is a chariot known by? What is fire known by? What is a nation known by? What is a woman known by?”

§2“A chariot is known by a banner. Fire is known by smoke. A nation is known by its ruler. A woman is known by her husband.”
