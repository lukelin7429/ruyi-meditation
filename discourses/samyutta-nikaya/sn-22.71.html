With Rādha

Why this discourse

This discourse breaks from the eight-discourse matched template that has run since SN 22.63, introducing a named individual disciple for the first time in this vagga: Venerable Rādha, whose question is more precisely and technically phrased than the unnamed mendicant's recurring “teach me in brief” request. Rādha asks specifically how one knows and sees so that there is no “I-making, mine-making, or underlying tendency to conceit” regarding “this conscious body and externally for all signs.” This discourse also matters structurally: Rādha is significant enough a figure that the next full saṃyutta in this collection, SN 23, is built entirely around him.

Reading guide

A more technical question than the recurring "teach me in brief"

Unlike the eight preceding discourses' identical opening request, Rādha's question is specific and technically precise from the outset: how does one know and see so that there is no ahaṅkāra (I-making), mamaṅkāra (mine-making), or underlying tendency to conceit (mānānusaya) for “this conscious body” (saviññāṇaka kāya) and externally for all signs (nimitta)? This vocabulary of I-making and mine-making recurs in a small number of discourses across the canon associated specifically with Rādha as questioner.

An answer combining two already-established formulas

The Buddha's answer joins two teachings already given separately elsewhere in this book: SN 22.48's elevenfold scope (past, future, or present; internal or external; solid or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near, covering “all” of an aggregate) and the negated three-clause formula from SN 22.8 and SN 22.15 (“this is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self”). Combined, they answer Rādha's precise question with an equally precise instruction.

A questioner who will anchor an entire saṃyutta of his own

Rādha's significance extends beyond this single discourse: SN 23, the saṃyutta immediately following the completion of Khandhavagga's own coverage, is titled Rādhasaṃyutta and built entirely around him as the recurring questioner across its own set of discourses. This discourse, appearing here within Khandhavagga, is an early appearance of a figure who will receive far more sustained attention later in the collection.

The vagga's penultimate discourse, setting up its final pair

SN 22.72 immediately after this discourse will feature a second, related individual — Venerable Surādha — whose question pushes the same concern further, closing Arahantavagga with a pair of named-disciple discourses after eight discourses built around an unnamed mendicant.

Key terms

ahaṅkāra — “I-making” — the first of three conditions Rādha asks how to end, part of this discourse's distinctive technical vocabulary.
mamaṅkāra — “mine-making” — the second condition, paired with I-making throughout this discourse.
mānānusaya — “underlying tendency to conceit” — the third condition, connecting back to SN 22.35-36's earlier use of anusaya (underlying tendency).
saviññāṇaka kāya — “this conscious body” — the specific object of Rādha's concern, alongside “all external signs” (nimitta).
n'etaṁ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā — “this is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self” — the negated formula from SN 22.8 and SN 22.15, combined here with the elevenfold scope formula.

The text

The discourse in full. Elided repetitions (feeling, perception, and choices, each following the same pattern spelled out in full for form and consciousness) are given exactly as bilara-data preserves them. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. Then Venerable Rādha went up to the Buddha … and asked him, “Sir, how does one know and see so that there’s no I-making, mine-making, or underlying tendency to conceit for this conscious body and externally for all signs?” “Rādha, one truly sees any kind of form at all—past, future, or present; internal or external; solid or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near: <em>all</em> form—with right understanding: ‘This is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self.’

§2consciousness at all—past, future, or present; internal or external; solid or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near: <em>all</em> consciousness—with right understanding: ‘This is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self.’ That’s how to know and see so that there’s no I-making, mine-making, or underlying tendency to conceit for this conscious body and externally for all signs.” … And Venerable Rādha became one of the perfected.
