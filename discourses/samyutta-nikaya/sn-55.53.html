Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 55.53

With Dhammadinna

Dhammadinnasutta — Sappaññavagga

Setting
Varanasi, the deer park at Isipatana
Speakers
The Buddha and the lay follower Dhammadinna, with five hundred companions
Form
A request for advice, a first recommendation declined honestly, then a second, accepted teaching
Length
2 minutes to read
Northern parallel
No individual parallel confirmed by this project's own check
Significance
★★★★★ — a rare instance of lay followers honestly declining the Buddha's own first teaching, echoing SN 55.7's own villagers, before receiving a more accessible one

Why this discourse

Confirmed by direct reading, when Dhammadinna and five hundred lay followers ask for advice, the Buddha first recommends meditating on his own “discourses that are deep, profound, transcendent, dealing with emptiness” — and the group responds not with silent acceptance but honest declination, citing their own household life with children, sandalwood, and jewelry, asking instead for something “since we are established in the five training rules” already.

Reading guide

A first recommendation, honestly declined

Confirmed by direct reading, this discourse echoes SN 55.7's own pattern of villagers stating their own worldly circumstances plainly: rather than pretending to accept a teaching beyond their own current capacity, Dhammadinna's own group states directly, “it's not easy for us…please teach us further” — a genuine negotiation, not passive reception.

A second teaching, calibrated to what was asked

Confirmed by direct reading, the Buddha does not insist on the emptiness teaching or express disappointment; he simply gives the four factors instead — confirming this saṃyutta's own recurring pattern of meeting disciples where they actually stand, already seen at SN 55.7.

A collective self-declaration, five hundred strong

Confirmed by direct reading, the group's own response is a collective version of the individual self-declarations already met at SN 55.3, 55.27, and SN 55.39: “these four factors of stream-entry …are found in us, and we embody them,” confirmed en masse by the Buddha's own closing words, “you've all declared the fruit of stream-entry.”

Key terms

gambhīrā gambhīratthā lokuttarā suññatāpaṭisaṁyuttā suttantā — “discourses…that are deep, profound, transcendent, dealing with emptiness” — the Buddha's own first recommendation, declined by the group
na no etaṁ sukaraṁ — “it's not easy for us” — the group's own honest, direct declination
pañcasu sikkhāpadesu ṭhitā — “established in the five training rules” — the group's own stated existing foundation, the basis for their own request for further teaching
ime cattāro sotāpattiyaṅgā bhagavatā desitā amhesu saṁvijjanti — “these four factors of stream-entry …are found in us, and we embody them” — the group's own collective self-confirmation
lābhā vo, dhammadinnā, suladdhaṁ vo — “you're all fortunate, Dhammadinna, so very fortunate!” — the Buddha's own closing confirmation, addressed to the group collectively

The text

Every genuinely translated fragment given in the consulted English source. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At one time the Buddha was staying near Varanasi, in the deer park at Isipatana. Then the lay follower Dhammadinna, together with five hundred lay followers, went up to the Buddha, bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him: “May the Buddha please advise and instruct us. It will be for our lasting welfare and happiness.” “So, Dhammadinna, you should all train like this: ‘From time to time we will undertake and dwell upon the discourses spoken by the Realized One that are deep, profound, transcendent, dealing with emptiness.’ That’s how you should train yourselves.” “Sir, we live at home with our children, using sandalwood imported from Kāsi, wearing garlands, fragrance, and makeup, and accepting gold and currency. It’s not easy for us to undertake and dwell from time to time upon the discourses spoken by the Realized One that are deep, profound, transcendent, dealing with emptiness. Since we are established in the five training rules, please teach us further.”

§2“So, Dhammadinna, you should all train like this: ‘We will have experiential confidence in the Buddha … the teaching … the Saṅgha … And we will have the ethical conduct loved by the noble ones … leading to immersion.’ That’s how you should train yourselves.” “Sir, these four factors of stream-entry that were taught by the Buddha are found in us, and we embody them. For we have experiential confidence in the Buddha … the teaching … the Saṅgha … And we have the ethical conduct loved by the noble ones … leading to immersion.” “You’re all fortunate, Dhammadinna, so very fortunate! You’ve all declared the fruit of stream-entry.”
