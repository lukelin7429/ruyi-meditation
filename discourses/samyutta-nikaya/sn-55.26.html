Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 55.26

Anāthapiṇḍika (1st)

Paṭhamaanāthapiṇḍikasutta — Saraṇānivagga

Setting
The home of the householder Anāthapiṇḍika, Sāvatthī
Speakers
Venerable Sāriputta, Venerable Ānanda, and the householder Anāthapiṇḍika
Form
A deathbed visit, a ten-point self-examination, verses of appreciation, and the Buddha's own closing praise of Sāriputta
Length
3–4 minutes to read
Northern parallel
No individual parallel confirmed by this project's own check
Significance
★★★★★ — a distinctive counseling method: not instructing the dying Anāthapiṇḍika, but having him examine his own already-present qualities directly, with his own pain subsiding as a result

Why this discourse

Confirmed by direct reading, the dying Anāthapiṇḍika sends for Sāriputta exactly as Dīghāvu once sent for the Buddha at SN 55.3. But Sāriputta's own method here is genuinely distinctive: rather than teaching new content, he has Anāthapiṇḍika examine ten qualities he already possesses — confidence in the Buddha, teaching, and Saṅgha, ethical conduct, and the seven remaining factors of the eightfold path — each closing with “seeing in yourself…that pain may die down on the spot,” and it genuinely does.

Reading guide

A self-examination, not new instruction

Confirmed by direct reading, Sāriputta's own repeated structure — “you don't have the distrust in the Buddha that causes…and you have experiential confidence in the Buddha…seeing in yourself that experiential confidence, that pain may die down on the spot” — directs Anāthapiṇḍika's own attention inward to what he already possesses, rather than adding anything new.

Ten points, not four

Confirmed by direct reading, Sāriputta's own examination extends beyond the usual four factors to a full ten: confidence in the Buddha, teaching, and Saṅgha, ethical conduct, then right view, purpose, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, immersion, knowledge, and freedom — the eightfold path plus right knowledge and right freedom, its own ten-factor expansion.

The pain subsides, genuinely

Confirmed by direct reading, this is not merely a rhetorical close: “Anāthapiṇḍika's pain died down on the spot,” and he immediately serves his own guests food before Sāriputta departs, praising him with verses on faith, ethics, confidence, and vision of the truth.

Sāriputta's own skill, confirmed by the Buddha directly

Confirmed by direct reading, when Ānanda reports the visit to the Buddha, he responds: “Sāriputta is astute…he has great wisdom, since he can analyze the four factors of stream-entry in ten respects” — the Buddha's own explicit confirmation of the tenfold expansion just witnessed.

Key terms

gilāno ābādhiko dukkhito bāḷhagilāno — “sick, suffering, gravely ill” — Anāthapiṇḍika's own condition, the identical phrase already met describing Dīghāvu at SN 55.3
attani passato taṁ vedanaṁ ṭhānaso paṭippassambheyya — “seeing in yourself…that pain may die down on the spot” — Sāriputta's own repeated closing formula, once for each of ten points
dasahi ākārehi cattāri sotāpattiyaṅgāni vibhajituṁ — “he can analyze the four factors of stream-entry in ten respects” — the Buddha's own closing praise of Sāriputta's own distinctive skill
micchādiṭṭhi...sammādiṭṭhi — “wrong view…right view” — the first of the seven further points, beginning this discourse's own extension into the eightfold path plus knowledge and freedom
yo tathāgate saddho niviṭṭho, mūlajātāya suppatiṭṭhito — “whoever has faith in the Realized One, unwavering and well grounded” — the opening line of Sāriputta's own closing verses of appreciation

The text

Every genuinely translated fragment given in the consulted English source. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. Now at that time the householder Anāthapiṇḍika was sick, suffering, gravely ill. Then he addressed a man, “Please, my friend, go to Venerable Sāriputta, and in my name bow with your head to his feet. Say to him: ‘Sir, the householder Anāthapiṇḍika is sick, suffering, gravely ill. He bows with his head to your feet.’ And then say: ‘Sir, please visit him at his home out of sympathy.’” “Yes, sir,” that man replied. He did as Anāthapiṇḍika asked. Sāriputta consented with silence. Then Venerable Sāriputta robed up in the morning and, taking his bowl and robe, went with Venerable Ānanda as his second monk to Anāthapiṇḍika’s home. He sat down on the seat spread out, and said to Anāthapiṇḍika, “I hope you’re keeping well, householder; I hope you’re all right. And I hope the pain is fading, not growing, that its fading is evident, not its growing.” “Sir, I’m not keeping well, I’m not getting by. The pain is terrible and growing, not fading; its growing is evident, not its fading.”

The three refuges, examined“Householder, you don’t have the distrust in the Buddha that causes an unlearned ordinary person to be reborn—when their body breaks up, after death—in a place of loss, a bad place, the underworld, hell. And you have experiential confidence in the Buddha: ‘That Blessed One is perfected, a fully awakened Buddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, holy, knower of the world, supreme guide for those fit for training, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed.’ Seeing in yourself that experiential confidence in the Buddha, that pain may die down on the spot. You don’t have the distrust in the teaching that causes an unlearned ordinary person to be reborn—when their body breaks up, after death—in a place of loss, a bad place, the underworld, hell. And you have experiential confidence in the teaching: ‘The teaching is well explained by the Buddha—apparent in the present life, immediately effective, inviting inspection, relevant, so that sensible people can know it for themselves.’ Seeing in yourself that experiential confidence in the teaching, that pain may die down on the spot. You don’t have the distrust in the Saṅgha that causes an unlearned ordinary person to be reborn—when their body breaks up, after death—in a place of loss, a bad place, the underworld, hell. And you have experiential confidence in the Saṅgha: ‘The Saṅgha of the Buddha’s disciples is practicing the way that’s good, direct, systematic, and proper. It consists of the four pairs, the eight individual persons. This Saṅgha of the Buddha’s disciples is worthy of offerings dedicated to the gods, worthy of hospitality, worthy of a religious donation, and worthy of veneration with cupped palms. It is the supreme field of merit for the world.’ Seeing in yourself that experiential confidence in the Saṅgha, that pain may die down on the spot. You don’t have the unethical conduct that causes an unlearned ordinary person to be reborn—when their body breaks up, after death—in a place of loss, a bad place, the underworld, hell. Your ethical conduct is loved by the noble ones, unbroken, impeccable, spotless, and unmarred, liberating, praised by sensible people, not mistaken, and leading to immersion. Seeing in yourself that ethical conduct loved by the noble ones, that pain may die down on the spot. You don’t have the wrong view that causes an unlearned ordinary person to be reborn—when their body breaks up, after death—in a place of loss, a bad place, the underworld, hell. You have right view. Seeing in yourself that right view, that pain may die down on the spot.

The remaining seven points, and recoveryYou don’t have the wrong purpose … wrong speech … wrong action … wrong livelihood … wrong effort … wrong mindfulness … wrong immersion … wrong knowledge … wrong freedom … You have right freedom. Seeing in yourself that right freedom, that pain may die down on the spot.” And then Anāthapiṇḍika’s pain died down on the spot. Then he served Sāriputta and Ānanda from his own dish. When Sāriputta had eaten and washed his hand and bowl, Anāthapiṇḍika took a low seat and sat to one side. Venerable Sāriputta expressed his appreciation to him with these verses.

Sāriputta's own verses“Whoever has faith in the Realized One, unwavering and well grounded; whose ethical conduct is good, praised and loved by the noble ones; who has confidence in the Saṅgha, and correct view: they’re said to be prosperous, their life is not in vain. So let the wise devote themselves to faith, ethical behaviour, confidence, and insight into the teaching, remembering the instructions of the Buddhas.” After expressing his appreciation to Anāthapiṇḍika with these verses, Sāriputta got up from his seat and left.

The Buddha's own praise of SāriputtaThen Ānanda went up to the Buddha, bowed, and sat down to one side. The Buddha said to him, “So, Ānanda, where are you coming from in the middle of the day?” “Sir, Venerable Sāriputta advised the householder Anāthapiṇḍika in this way and that.” “Sāriputta is astute, Ānanda. He has great wisdom, since he can analyze the four factors of stream-entry in ten respects.”
