Why this discourse

This discourse extends SN 22.73's compressed contrast by adding two further terms before gratification, drawback, and escape: origin (samudaya) and disappearance (atthaṅgama). The unlearned/learned structure stays exactly as compressed as SN 22.73's, but the set of understanding now being contrasted grows from three items to five, gathering into one sentence several of the analytical categories this book has developed separately — origin and cessation recall SN 22.56's four rounds, while gratification, drawback, and escape recall SN 22.26-28.

Reading guide

Two new terms added before the familiar triad

Where SN 22.73 named only gratification, drawback, and escape, this discourse prefixes two further terms: origin and disappearance. The unlearned ordinary person now fails to understand five things about each aggregate; the learned noble disciple understands all five.

A compressed echo of SN 22.56's four rounds

Origin and disappearance (or cessation) are two of the four items SN 22.56 spelled out in precise, per-aggregate detail — form originating from fuel, feeling and perception and choices from contact, consciousness from name and form. This discourse does not repeat those specific conditions; it simply names the categories, assuming the reader can supply the detail from SN 22.56 if needed.

Five terms in one sentence, still no elaboration

Despite doubling the number of items from SN 22.73's three to this discourse's five, the discourse's overall structure remains just as compressed — a single sentence for the unlearned, a single sentence for the learned, with no further explanation of what origin, disappearance, gratification, drawback, or escape actually amount to for any given aggregate.

A pattern this vagga will continue to compress further

SN 22.75 immediately after this discourse will state only the positive half of this same five-term contrast, dropping the unlearned/learned comparison structure entirely — continuing this vagga's pattern of presenting the same content through progressively different compressions.

Key terms

samudaya — “origin” — the first of two new terms this discourse adds, recalling SN 22.56's four rounds.
atthaṅgama — “disappearance” — the second new term, paired with origin as the aggregate's arising and passing.
assāda, ādīnava, nissaraṇa — “gratification, drawback, escape” — the same triad from SN 22.73, unchanged, now following the two new terms.
assutavā puthujjano — “unlearned ordinary person” — the same figure from SN 22.73, now lacking understanding of five things rather than three.
sutavā ariyasāvako — “learned noble disciple” — the same figure from SN 22.73, now possessing the fuller five-part understanding.

The text

The discourse in full. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. “Mendicants, an unlearned ordinary person doesn’t truly understand the origin, the disappearance, the gratification, the drawback, and the escape when it comes to form, feeling, perception, choices, and consciousness. A learned noble disciple does truly understand the origin, the disappearance, the gratification, the drawback, and the escape when it comes to form, feeling, perception, choices, and consciousness.”
