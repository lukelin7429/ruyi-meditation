Why this discourse

This discourse is confirmed by direct reading to be structurally near-identical to the immediately preceding discourse, SN 47.37 — the same fourfold, object-by-object chain leading to freedom from death — but with “completely understood” (pariññāta) replacing “desire given up” (chanda pahīyati) as the pivotal middle term, pairing understanding and the abandonment of desire as two distinct routes to the same realization.

Reading guide

A near-twin of SN 47.37, one term substituted

Confirmed by direct reading, this discourse's own structure — observing an object, then a middle term, then realizing freedom from death, repeated separately for each of the four objects — is IDENTICAL to SN 47.37's own structure, with only the middle term changed from “desire given up” to “completely understood.”

Pariññā -- understanding, not merely knowing about

The term “pariññāta” (“completely understood”) is confirmed by direct reading to carry a stronger sense than ordinary knowledge in this context — a thorough, exhaustive understanding of an object's own true nature, traditionally distinguished in the commentarial literature from merely knowing facts about it.

Two routes, honestly distinct, to the same destination

This project's own honest comparison of this discourse with SN 47.37 confirms the texts present understanding and the giving up of desire as TWO SEPARATE routes, each sufficient on its own, to the identical outcome of freedom from death — rather than treating them as the same process under two different names.

Key terms

pariññāta — “completely understood” — this discourse's own pivotal term, replacing SN 47.37's own “desire given up”
pariññā — “complete understanding” — a thorough, exhaustive understanding of an object's own true nature, traditionally distinguished from merely knowing facts about it
kāyassa pariññātattā amataṁ sacchikataṁ hoti — “from the body being completely understood, freedom from death is realized” — the pattern repeated with the appropriate object substituted in each of the four sections
amataṁ sacchikataṁ hoti — “freedom from death is realized” — the identical outcome shared with SN 47.37, reached here through understanding rather than through giving up desire
cattāro satipaṭṭhānā — “the four kinds of mindfulness meditation” — the practice through which each object comes to be completely understood

The text

The complete discourse. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1 — the body“Mendicants, there are these four kinds of mindfulness meditation. What four? It’s when a mendicant meditates by observing an aspect of the body—keen, aware, and mindful, rid of covetousness and displeasure for the world. As they do so they completely understand the body. When the body is completely understood they realize freedom from death.

§2 — feelingsThey meditate observing an aspect of feelings—keen, aware, and mindful, rid of covetousness and displeasure for the world. As they do so they completely understand feelings. When feelings are completely understood they realize freedom from death.

§3 — the mindThey meditate observing an aspect of the mind—keen, aware, and mindful, rid of covetousness and displeasure for the world. As they do so they completely understand the mind. When the mind is completely understood they realize freedom from death.

§4 — principlesThey meditate observing an aspect of principles—keen, aware, and mindful, rid of covetousness and displeasure for the world. As they do so they completely understand principles. When principles are completely understood they realize freedom from death.”
