Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 2.24

With Ghaṭīkāra

Ghaṭīkārasutta

Setting
At Sāvatthī; no further narrative detail given
Speakers
The Buddha and the godling Ghaṭīkāra
Form
A four-round question-and-answer dialogue in verse, building to a shared recollection of a past life
Length
~3 minutes to read
Northern parallel
A rough counterpart may exist in the Chinese Saṃyukta-āgama (T99), though this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★★★★☆ — best read alongside SN 1.50, this book's earlier telling of the same reunion

Why this discourse

This discourse retells, in a fuller question-and-answer form, the story SN 1.50 already told at the close of this book's predecessor: seven mendicants reborn in the Aviha realm, and the godling who knows how — the potter Ghaṭīkāra, once the Buddha's closest friend in a past life under the previous Buddha Kassapa. Where SN 1.50 built to this revelation across one extended exchange, this discourse stages it as four distinct rounds of question and answer, each round pressing further into how the godling could possibly know what he claims.

Reading guide

The same story, a different telling

This is not a new narrative but a second telling of SN 1.50's own material — the same seven named mendicants (Upaka, Palagaṇḍa, Pukkusāti, Bhaddiya, Bhaddadeva, Bāhudantī, Piṅgiya), the same potter Ghaṭīkāra, the same epithet ‘Bhaggava’, and the same closing claim that both old friends bear their final body.

Four rounds, each asking how he knows

Unlike SN 1.50's more continuous unfolding, this version structures the revelation as four escalating questions: who are they, whose teaching did they understand, whose teaching did you yourself understand to say such deep words — each answer prompting a harder question about the source of the godling's knowledge.

Crossing the bog, Death's dominion

The Buddha's first question uses imagery not present at SN 1.50's telling: crossing ‘the bog’ and ‘Death's dominion so hard to pass’, adding a new descriptive frame to material this book has already covered once.

Where name and form cease with no residue

Ghaṭīkāra's answer to the question of whose teaching the seven understood adds a line of doctrinal content absent from SN 1.50 — naming specifically the cessation of name and form (nāmarūpa) — a detail this fuller telling includes that the earlier, more narrative-focused version did not.

Two tellings, one friendship

Reading this discourse against SN 1.50 shows the same underlying story can be told with different emphases: SN 1.50 as this book's most emotionally weighted closing discourse, this one as a more systematic dialogue building its revelation through explicit, stacked questions.

Key terms

Aviha — “Aviha” — the pure abode where the seven named mendicants have been reborn, already named at SN 1.50.
ogha — “the bog” — this discourse's own image for what the seven have crossed, distinct from SN 1.50's vocabulary.
nāmarūpa — “name and form” — whose cessation Ghaṭīkāra names as the teaching the seven understood, a detail this fuller telling adds.
Bhaggava — “Bhaggava” — the clan name the Buddha uses to address Ghaṭīkāra, identical to SN 1.50's address.
antimadeha — “final body” — the shared closing description of both old friends, echoing SN 2.14's use of the same term for the arahant.

The text

The discourse in full: the four-round dialogue building to Ghaṭīkāra's revelation and the Buddha's confirmation. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Standing to one side, the godling Ghaṭīkāra spoke this verse in the Buddha’s presence:

§2“Seven mendicants reborn in Aviha have been freed. With the complete ending of greed and hate, they’ve crossed over clinging to the world.”

§3“Who are those who’ve crossed the bog, Death’s dominion so hard to pass? Who, after leaving behind the human body, have risen above celestial yokes?”

§4“Upaka and Palagaṇḍa, and Pukkusāti, these three; Bhaddiya and Bhaddadeva, and Bāhudantī and Piṅgiya. They, after leaving behind the human body, have risen above celestial yokes.”

§5“You speak well of them, who have let go the snares of Māra. Whose teaching did they understand to cut the bonds of rebirth?”

§6“None other than the Blessed One! None other than your instruction! It was your teaching that they understood to cut the bonds of rebirth. Where name and form cease with no residue left behind; understanding this teaching, they cut the bonds of rebirth.”

§7“The words you say are deep, hard to understand, so very hard to wake up to. Whose teaching did you understand to be able to say such things?”

§8“In the past I was a potter in Vebhaliṅga called Ghaṭīkāra. I took care of my parents as a lay follower of Buddha Kassapa. I refrained from sexual intercourse, I was chaste, spiritual. We lived in the same village; in the past I was your comrade.

§9I am the one who understands that these seven mendicants have been freed. With the complete ending of greed and hate, they’ve crossed over clinging to the world.”

§10“That’s exactly how it was, just as you say, Bhaggava. In the past you were a potter in Vebhaliṅga called Ghaṭīkāra. You took care of your parents as a lay follower of Buddha Kassapa. You refrained from sexual intercourse, you were chaste, spiritual. We lived in the same village; in the past you were my comrade.”

§11“That’s how it was when those friends of old met again. Both of them are evolved, and bear their final body.”
