Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 24.39

The Self Is Formed and Formless

Rūpīarūpīsutta — Dutiyagamanavagga

Setting

Why this discourse

The third corner of this vagga's new four-part set: the self claimed both formed and formless simultaneously after death — combining SN 24.37 and SN 24.38's positions into a single claim, exactly as SN 24.17 combined SN 24.15 and 24.16's existence claims for the tathāgata foursome.

Reading guide

Both positions, held together

"The self is formed and formless, and is healthy after death" (rūpī ca arūpī ca attā hoti arogo paraṁ maraṇā) — asserting both SN 24.37's formed claim and SN 24.38's formless claim at once, rather than choosing between them.

The same logical move seen at SN 24.17

This combined position exactly parallels SN 24.17's own treatment of the tathāgata foursome's third corner ("both still exists and no longer exists") — in both cases, the third corner of a four-part catuṣkoṭi affirms two claims already established as opposed, rather than synthesizing a genuinely new middle position.

A parallel structure across two different questions

That this vagga's new bodily-form catalog follows the identical four-corner shape already used for the tathāgata's existence question is itself worth noting: the same logical scaffolding, reapplied wholesale to a different subject matter, rather than built fresh for each new topic.

One corner remains

SN 24.40 will close this set with the fourth and final position: neither formed nor formless — completing the parallel with SN 24.18's own closing role for the tathāgata foursome.

Key terms

rūpī ca arūpī ca attā hoti — the self is formed and formless — this discourse's claim, combining SN 24.37 and SN 24.38's positions
arogo paraṁ maraṇā — free of disease after death — the shared qualifier continuing across this set
catuṣkoṭi — "four corners" — the logical pattern this discourse's third corner follows, paralleling SN 24.17's treatment of the tathāgata foursome
peyyāla — an elision mark — compressing this discourse to its bare opening claim
upādāya... abhinivissa — grasping... and insisting on — the formula this discourse's compression trusts the reader to supply

The complete discourse as bilara-data preserves it: the opening claim alone. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).
