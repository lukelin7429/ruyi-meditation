Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 45.1

Ignorance

Avijjāsutta — Avijjāvagga

Setting
Sāvatthī, Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery
Speakers
The Buddha, addressing the mendicants directly
Form
Two mirrored nine-step causal chains, opening this book's own first saṃyutta
Length
~1 minute to read
Northern parallel
No individual parallel confirmed by this project's own check
Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — short, two clear parallel chains

Why this discourse

This discourse opens Book V (Mahāvagga, the Great Book, SN 45–56) with a causal chain this project has not seen stated quite this way before: ignorance itself, not merely one factor among others, is named as what precedes and produces wrong view, which in turn produces each of the remaining seven wrong factors of the eightfold path in strict sequence — and the identical chain, reversed, runs from knowledge through all eight right factors. The eightfold path itself, this book's own governing framework, is presented here as something that can run in either direction depending on what precedes it.

Reading guide

One chain running backward through the path

Wrong view produces wrong purpose, which produces wrong speech, then wrong action, wrong livelihood, wrong effort, wrong mindfulness, and finally wrong immersion — each of the eightfold path's own eight factors named in exactly its usual order, but here as a chain of causation rather than a list of virtues to cultivate.

Ignorance and knowledge as what starts each chain

Ignorance itself “precedes the attainment of unskillful qualities,” with lack of conscience and prudence following along before wrong view even arises — and knowledge precedes skillful qualities the identical way, confirmed by reading both halves of this discourse directly: not simply “ignorance is bad, knowledge is good,” but a specific claim about what comes first in each causal sequence.

Key terms

avijjā — “ignorance” — what this discourse names as preceding the entire chain of eight wrong factors, giving this discourse and its own vagga their shared name
micchādiṭṭhi — “wrong view” — the first link the chain of ignorance produces, itself producing each of the remaining seven wrong factors in turn
ahirikaṁ anottappaṁ — “lack of conscience and prudence” — what this discourse says follows along with ignorance, before wrong view itself arises
vijjā — “knowledge” — the mirrored starting point of this discourse's own second chain, producing right view and, through it, all eight right factors
sammādiṭṭhi... sammāsamādhi — “right view... right immersion” — the eightfold path's own eight factors, named here as the final links of the knowledge-based chain

The text

The complete discourse. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Setting

§1So I have heard. At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. There the Buddha addressed the mendicants, “Mendicants!” “Venerable sir,” they replied. The Buddha said this:

Ignorance, and the chain of eight wrong factors

§2“Mendicants, ignorance precedes the attainment of unskillful qualities, with lack of conscience and prudence following along. An ignoramus, sunk in ignorance, produces wrong view. Wrong view produces wrong purpose. Wrong purpose produces wrong speech. Wrong speech produces wrong action. Wrong action produces wrong livelihood. Wrong livelihood produces wrong effort. Wrong effort produces wrong mindfulness. Wrong mindfulness produces wrong immersion.

Knowledge, and the chain of eight right factors

§3Knowledge precedes the attainment of skillful qualities, with conscience and prudence following along. A knowing one, firm in knowledge, produces right view. Right view produces right purpose. Right purpose produces right speech. Right speech produces right action. Right action produces right livelihood. Right livelihood produces right effort. Right effort produces right mindfulness. Right mindfulness produces right immersion.”
