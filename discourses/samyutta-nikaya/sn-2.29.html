Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 2.29

With Susīma

Susīmasutta

Setting
At Sāvatthī; no further narrative detail given
Speakers
The Buddha, Venerable Ānanda, and the godling Susīma with his assembly
Form
A prose dialogue expanding into an assembly's radiant display, closed by two matching verses
Length
~3.5 minutes to read
Northern parallel
A rough counterpart may exist in the Chinese Saṃyukta-āgama (T99), though this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★★★★☆ — the cascading similes for light reward slow, attentive reading

Why this discourse

The Buddha opens this discourse not with a godling's approach but with his own direct question to Ānanda: do you endorse Sāriputta? Ānanda's answer catalogs Sāriputta's qualities at length, and while this praise is still being spoken, the godling Susīma arrives with a large assembly, agreeing so completely that the godlings themselves display a rainbow of bright colors, described through a cascade of four similes before closing on matched verses from Susīma and the Buddha.

Reading guide

A question the Buddha asks, not one he is asked

Uniquely among this book's discourses, the opening exchange here is initiated by the Buddha himself questioning Ānanda, rather than a godling approaching to question or praise the Buddha — the entire discourse grows outward from this reversed initiative.

Ānanda's cascading list of qualities

Ānanda's praise names Sāriputta's wisdom in five overlapping terms — great, widespread, laughing, swift, sharp, penetrating — before moving to character (few wishes, content, secluded, aloof, energetic) and finally to conduct (giving and accepting advice, accusing and criticizing wickedness), building a portrait through accumulation rather than a single summarizing claim.

An assembly's praise made visible as light

When Susīma's assembly of godlings hears this same praise confirmed, their reaction is not verbal but visual: ‘uplifted and overjoyed, full of rapture and happiness’, they display a rainbow of bright colors — an assembly's shared feeling made externally visible, unlike anywhere else in this book.

Four similes for one radiance

The text does not settle for one comparison but offers four in succession — a lustrous, eight-faceted beryl gem; a smith-wrought gold pendant; the Morning Star in a clear autumn sky; the rising sun dispelling all darkness — each restating the same underlying image of shining radiance from a different angle.

A verse pair sharing three lines, differing in one

Susīma's closing verse and the Buddha's reply share their first three lines exactly, differing only in the fourth: Susīma frames Sāriputta's excellence through the Buddha's own praise, while the Buddha's version instead says Sāriputta, developed and well-tamed, ‘awaits his time’ — a further instance of this book's single-line-substitution technique, here applied to praise of a third person rather than to teaching.

Key terms

anujānāti — “endorse” — the Buddha's opening question to Ānanda, asking whether he approves of Sāriputta.
mahāpañña — “great wisdom” — the first of several overlapping terms Ānanda uses to describe Sāriputta's wisdom.
vevaṇṇiyaṁ dassesuṁ — “displayed a rainbow of bright colors” — the assembly's visible reaction to hearing Sāriputta's praise confirmed.
osadhitārakā — “the Morning Star” — the third of four similes describing the assembly's radiance.
kālaṁ āgamayaṁ — “awaits his time” — the Buddha's substituted final line, differing from Susīma's closing verse.

The text

The discourse in full, with the elided repetitions of Sāriputta's praise skipped over as blank in the source. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. Then Venerable Ānanda went up to the Buddha, bowed, and sat down to one side. The Buddha said to him, “Ānanda, do you endorse Sāriputta?”

§2“Sir, who on earth would not endorse Venerable Sāriputta unless they’re a fool, a hater, delusional, or mentally deranged? Venerable Sāriputta is astute, he has great wisdom, widespread wisdom, laughing wisdom, swift wisdom, sharp wisdom, and penetrating wisdom. He has few wishes, he’s content, secluded, aloof, and energetic. He gives advice and accepts advice; he accuses and criticizes wickedness. Who on earth would not endorse Venerable Sāriputta unless they’re a fool, a hater, delusional, or mentally deranged?”

§3“That’s so true, Ānanda! That’s so true! Who on earth would not endorse Venerable Sāriputta unless they’re a fool, a hater, delusional, or mentally deranged?” And the Buddha repeated all of Ānanda’s terms of praise.

§4While this praise of Sāriputta was being spoken, the godling Susīma approached the Buddha, escorted by a large assembly of godlings. He bowed, stood to one side, and said to him:

§5“That’s so true, Blessed One! That’s so true, Holy One! Who on earth would not endorse Venerable Sāriputta unless they’re a fool, a hater, delusional, or mentally deranged?” And he too repeated all the terms of praise of Sāriputta, adding,

§6“For I too, sir, whenever I go to an assembly of godlings, frequently hear the same terms of praise.”

§7While this praise of Sāriputta was being spoken, the godlings of Susīma’s assembly—uplifted and overjoyed, full of rapture and happiness—displayed a rainbow of bright colors. Suppose there was a beryl gem that was naturally lustrous, eight-faceted, well-worked. When placed on a cream rug it would shine and glow and radiate. In the same way, the godlings of Susīma’s assembly displayed a rainbow of bright colors. Suppose there was a pendant of Black Plum River gold, fashioned by a deft smith, well wrought in the forge. When placed on a cream rug it would shine and glow and radiate. In the same way, the godlings of Susīma’s assembly displayed a rainbow of bright colors.

§8Suppose that in the autumn the heavens were clear and cloudless. At the crack of dawn, the Morning Star shines and glows and radiates. In the same way, the godlings of Susīma’s assembly displayed a rainbow of bright colors. Suppose that in the autumn the heavens were clear and cloudless. As the sun is rising to the firmament, having dispelled all the darkness of space, shines and glows and radiates. In the same way, the godlings of Susīma’s assembly displayed a rainbow of bright colors.

§9Then the godling Susīma spoke this verse about Venerable Sāriputta in the Buddha’s presence: “He’s considered astute, Sāriputta, free of anger. Few in wishes, gentle, tamed, the seer shines in the Teacher’s praise!”

§10Then the Buddha replied to Susīma with this verse about Venerable Sāriputta: “He’s considered astute, Sāriputta, free of anger. Few in wishes, gentle, tamed; developed and well-tamed, he awaits his time.”
