Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 18.6

Perceptions

Saññāsutta

Setting
Sāvatthī
Speakers

Form

Length
~1 minute to read
Northern parallel

Why this discourse

This discourse turns to perception, saññā, the mental process of recognizing and labeling what is encountered. Unlike SN 18.3-5, which named their six kinds after sense doors (eye consciousness, eye contact, and so on), this discourse instead names its six kinds after sense objects, directly echoing SN 18.2's own list: perception of sights, perception of sounds, and so on through perception of ideas.

Reading guide

A shift back to object-based naming

Where the three discourses immediately before this one named their six categories after sense doors, this discourse returns to naming them after sense objects — perception of sights, not eye-perception — directly echoing SN 18.2's own list rather than SN 18.3-5's.

Perception as recognition, not raw sensation

Saññā names the specific mental act of recognizing and labeling — identifying a sight as blue, a sound as a voice — distinct from the bare contact or feeling examined in the discourses immediately before this one, adding a further layer to the sweep's account of experience.

The same six objects, examined from a new angle

Sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and ideas reappear here exactly as in SN 18.2, but now the question isn't whether these objects themselves are impermanent; it's whether the mind's perception of them is.

A subtler target than the objects it perceives

By shifting the target from the object to the perception of the object, this discourse moves one step further into subjective process, continuing this saṃyutta's progression from coarser to increasingly internal categories of analysis.

Continuing an unbroken six-discourse rhythm

Faculty, object, consciousness, contact, feeling, and now perception: six discourses in strict succession, each applying the identical three-question template to a progressively more refined layer of how experience is constructed.

Key terms

rūpasaññā … saddasaññā … gandhasaññā … rasasaññā … phoṭṭhabbasaññā … dhammasaññā — “perception of sights … sounds … smells … tastes … touches … ideas” — the six kinds this discourse examines, named after objects rather than faculties.
saññā — “perception” — the mental act of recognizing and labeling what is encountered, distinct from bare contact or feeling.
…pe… — the peyyāla elision mark — the same compression convention continuing from the discourses before this one.
aniccā — “impermanent” — the template's first term, applied here to each of the six kinds of perception.
chaṭṭhaṁ — “the sixth” — this discourse's closing numbering tag.

The text

The discourse as preserved in the source, elided around SN 18.1's fuller sentence structure. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. “What do you think, Rāhula? Is perception of sights permanent or impermanent?” “Impermanent, sir.” … “… perception of sounds … perception of smells … perception of tastes … perception of touches … Is perception of ideas permanent or impermanent?” “Impermanent, sir.” … “Seeing this, a learned noble disciple grows disillusioned with the perception of sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and ideas. …”
