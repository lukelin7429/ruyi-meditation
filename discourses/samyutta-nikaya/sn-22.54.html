Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 22.54

Seeds of Plants

Bījasutta — Upayavagga

Setting
Sāvatthī
Speakers
The Buddha, teaching through a question-and-answer exchange with the assembled mendicants
Form
An extended agricultural simile mapped point by point onto SN 22.53's teaching on consciousness
Length
~3 minutes to read
Northern parallel
A rough counterpart may exist in the Chinese Saṃyukta-āgama (T99), though this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★★☆☆☆ — the simile itself is vivid and concrete, making an otherwise abstract teaching easier to hold onto

Why this discourse

This discourse takes SN 22.53's abstract teaching on consciousness's involvement and gives it a concrete, agricultural image. The Buddha names five kinds of plants — propagated from roots, trunks, cuttings, joints, or regular seeds — and asks whether they would grow if intact, unspoiled, fertile, and well-kept, but lacking earth and water. The mendicants answer no. Would they grow if earth and water were present? Yes. The Buddha then maps each element of the simile directly onto the earlier teaching: the four grounds for consciousness (form, feeling, perception, choices) are like the earth; relishing and greed are like the water; and consciousness with its fuel is like the five plants themselves.

Reading guide

A question posed before its answer is given

As in several other discourses in this book (SN 22.49's dialogue with Soṇa, SN 22.35's mendicant narrative), the Buddha secures the mendicants' own confirmed understanding through direct questioning before drawing the conclusion himself: would these plants grow without earth or water? No. Would they grow with earth and water present? Yes. Only after this exchange does the Buddha supply the mapping onto consciousness.

A precise three-part mapping

The simile's mapping is exact and explicit, not left for the listener to infer: the four grounds for consciousness (form, feeling, perception, and choices) should be seen like the earth element; relishing and greed should be seen like the water element; consciousness together with its fuel should be seen like the five kinds of plants. Each term in the simile corresponds to a specific term in SN 22.53's abstract teaching, with nothing left unmapped.

The five kinds of plants, standing for a single process

The discourse names five distinct modes of plant propagation (root, trunk, cutting, joint, and seed) as the counterpart to “consciousness with its fuel” — not five different kinds of consciousness, but five different starting conditions that nonetheless all follow the identical growth pattern once earth and water (the aggregates and relishing) are present. The diversity of starting points does not change the underlying mechanism.

The same teaching, the same closing sequence

After completing the simile, the discourse returns to SN 22.53's exact wording — consciousness getting involved with, supported by, and established on each aggregate; the impossibility of describing it independently; and the identical resolution through giving up greed, cutting off support, and the full extended liberation sequence ending in personal extinguishment. The simile illustrates without replacing any part of the original teaching.

Key terms

mūlabīja, khandhabīja, phaḷubīja, aggabīja, bījabīja — “plants propagated from roots, trunks, cuttings, joints, and regular seeds” — the five kinds of plant propagation named at the discourse's opening.
pathavīdhātu — “the earth element” — what the four grounds for consciousness (form, feeling, perception, and choices) should be seen as resembling.
āpodhātu — “the water element” — what relishing and greed should be seen as resembling.
viññāṇaṁ sāhāraṁ — “consciousness with its fuel” — what the five kinds of plants should be seen as resembling, completing the three-part mapping.
vuddhiṁ virūḷhiṁ vepullaṁ āpajjeyyuṁ — “would reach growth, increase, and maturity” — the question posed of the plants, and by direct extension of consciousness itself.

The text

The discourse in full. Elided repetitions in the second half (feeling and perception, each following the same pattern spelled out in full for form, choices, and consciousness) are given exactly as bilara-data preserves them. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Mendicants, there are five kinds of plants. What five? Plants propagated from roots, trunks, cuttings, or joints; and those from regular seeds are the fifth.

§2Suppose these five kinds of plants were intact, unspoiled, not weather-damaged, fertile, and well-kept. But there’s no earth or water. Then would these five kinds of plants reach growth, increase, and maturity?” “No, sir.” “Suppose these five kinds of plants were intact, unspoiled, not weather-damaged, fertile, and well-kept. And there is earth and water. Then would these five kinds of plants reach growth, increase, and maturity?” “Yes, sir.”

§3“The four grounds for consciousness should be seen as like the earth element. Relishing and greed should be seen as like the water element. Consciousness with its fuel should be seen as like the five kinds of plants.

§4As long as consciousness continues, it would get involved with form, supported by form, established on form. And with a sprinkle of relishing, it would grow, increase, and mature.

§5Or as long as consciousness continues, it would get involved with choices, supported by choices, established on choices. And with a sprinkle of relishing, it would grow, increase, and mature.

§6Mendicants, suppose you say: ‘Apart from form, feeling, perception, and choices, I will describe the coming and going of consciousness, its passing away and reappearing, its growth, increase, and maturity.’ That is not possible.

§7If a mendicant has given up greed for the form element, the support is cut off, and there is no establishment of consciousness.

§8consciousness element, the support is cut off, and there is no establishment of consciousness. Since that consciousness does not become established and does not grow, not making choices, it is freed. Being free, it’s stable. Being stable, it’s content. Being content, they’re not anxious. Not being anxious, they personally become extinguished. They understand: ‘Rebirth is ended … there is nothing further for this place.’”
