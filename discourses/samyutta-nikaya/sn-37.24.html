Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 37.24

Five Precepts

Pañcasīlasutta — Dutiyapeyyālavagga

Setting
Not restated in this discourse
Speakers
The Buddha, addressing Anuruddha
Form
A fivefold list built from the five precepts kept, closed by the vagga's own colophon and uddāna
Length
~1 minute to read
Northern parallel
A rough counterpart may exist in the Chinese Saṃyukta-āgama (T99), though this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number

★★☆☆☆ — closing the mirror pairing this project has traced across two full vaggas

Why this discourse

This discourse closes Dutiyapeyyālavagga by reversing SN 37.14's own broken-precept list into the precepts kept: refraining from killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, lying, and intoxicants, leading to “a good place, a heavenly realm” in place of hell. The vagga's own closing uddāna then confirms the prediction made explicitly at SN 37.23: this collection is “sukkapakkhe pakāsito” — “declared on the bright side” — the exact mirror of Paṭhamapeyyālavagga's own closing self-description, confirmed rather than assumed.

Reading guide

The five precepts, kept rather than broken

Where SN 37.14 named killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, lying, and intoxicant use as threats leading to unfavorable rebirth, this discourse names abstention from each of the same five as leading to heavenly rebirth — the identical ethical content, framed as kept precepts rather than broken ones.

A prediction confirmed exactly, not approximately

SN 37.23's own reading guide named the expected wording “sukkapakkhe pakāsito” before reading this discourse's own colophon — the actual text matches that prediction precisely, confirming Paṭhamapeyyālavagga's own dark/bright framing was a genuine, verifiable structural design rather than this project's own retrospective interpretation.

Two vaggas, one deliberate architecture now fully traced

Across 24 discourses, this project has now confirmed: a reciprocal opening pair (SN 37.1–2) with no mirror needed since it was already even-handed, a descriptive suffering list (SN 37.3) and a temporally-structured triad (SN 37.4) with no mirrors at all, a nine-part character template reversed term by term (SN 37.5–13 against SN 37.15–23), and a precept-based closing pair (SN 37.14 against this discourse) — a complete, traceable architecture rather than a loose collection.

What the pairing does and doesn't establish

Confirming this deliberate mirror structure doesn't resolve every question a modern reader might bring to SN 37.1–14's own content; it does establish that the text itself, on its own terms, frames the earlier material as one moral valence balanced by an equally weighted opposite, not as an unqualified final verdict.

Key terms

pāṇātipātā paṭivirato — “refraining from killing living creatures” — the first of the five precepts, reversing SN 37.14's own pāṇātipātī
sugatiṁ saggaṁ lokaṁ upapajjati — “reborn in a good place, a heavenly realm” — the reversed destination, replacing SN 37.14's own fourfold hell-bound phrase
Dutiyapeyyālavaggo — the vagga's own name, “the second chapter of abbreviated texts,” confirmed in the colophon closing this discourse
sukkapakkhe pakāsito — “declared on the bright side” — the vagga's own uddāna closing on this explicit self-description, confirming the prediction made at SN 37.23
dasamaṁ — “tenth” — this discourse's own untranslated closing marker, its position as the vagga's own final discourse

The text

The complete discourse: the five precepts kept, leading to heavenly rebirth. The vagga's own closing colophon and uddāna, left untranslated in the Pali root, are described rather than quoted above. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Anuruddha, when ladies have five qualities, when their body breaks up, after death, they are reborn in a good place, a heavenly realm. What five? They don’t kill living creatures, steal, commit sexual misconduct, lie, or consume beer, wine, and liquor intoxicants. When ladies possess these five qualities, when their body breaks up, after death, they are reborn in a good place, a heavenly realm.”
