Why this discourse

The question is a riddle with an implied trick: both gods and humans enjoy food, so what is the name of the spirit who does not? Rather than naming a specific being, the reply reframes the whole premise: those who give with faith and a clear, confident heart partake of food's fruit in this world and the next; by implication, whoever has never given anything, never having sown that cause, is the one who goes without — not because of what kind of spirit they are, but because of what they never did.

Reading guide

A riddle that isn't answered as asked

The question asks for a name — what is this spirit called — and the reply supplies no name at all. Instead of identifying a specific being, it redirects entirely toward a cause: generosity, or its absence, as what actually determines who partakes of food's benefit and who doesn't.

Food as a stand-in for merit, not merely a meal

Read literally, the riddle sounds like it concerns actual eating; the reply's answer only makes sense once ‘food’ is understood more broadly, as the fruit of merit that follows a person into their next life. Those who give partake of that fruit ‘in this world and the next’; those who never gave have nothing of that kind waiting for them.

An implied answer, not a stated one

The reply never directly says who the spirit without food actually is. Left unstated, but strongly implied by the reply's second verse urging the hearer to dispel stinginess, is that the miserly — whether reborn human, animal, or as a hungry spirit unable to receive what is offered them — are exactly the ones who go without.

A fourth discourse on giving in a row

SN 1.41 on rescuing what's given from a burning world, SN 1.42 on what each specific gift produces, and now this discourse on who partakes of food's fruit: giving has been this vagga's central concern since its first discourse, more consistently than any earlier vagga in this collection.

Key terms

anna — “food” — this discourse's title, and the riddle's literal and figurative subject.
yakkho — “spirit” — the being the opening riddle asks to be named, left unidentified when the reply redirects the question instead.
saddhāya denti — “those who give with faith” — the reply's actual subject, replacing the question's search for a named spirit.
vippasannena cetasā — “with a clear and confident heart” — the quality of mind paired with faith in the reply's description of true givers.
macchariyamalaṁ — “the stain of stinginess” — what the reply's second verse urges dispelling, echoing the same term already met at SN 1.32.

The text

The discourse in full: a riddle about who goes without food, answered by redirecting toward generosity itself. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Both gods and humans enjoy their food. So what’s the name of the spirit who doesn’t enjoy food?”

§2“Those who give with faith and a clear and confident heart, partake of food in this world and the next. So you should dispel stinginess, overcoming that stain, and give a gift. The good deeds of sentient beings support them in the next world.”
