Ensnared

Uḍḍitasutta

Why this discourse

This discourse names four separate claims about the world's condition: craving ensnares it, old age surrounds it, mortality fastens it shut, and suffering is what it's grounded on. Together the four answers build a composite picture — not one governing force, as in SN 1.61 through SN 1.65, but four distinct relationships between the world and what afflicts it.

Reading guide

Four verbs, four distinct relationships

Each answer uses a different verb to describe a different kind of affliction: uḍḍita, ‘ensnared,’ suggests a trap; parivārita, ‘surrounded,’ suggests encirclement; pihita, ‘fastened shut,’ suggests confinement; and grounding (patiṭṭhita) suggests a foundation rather than an external threat at all.

Suffering as ground, not merely as a threat

The verse's final answer is structurally different from its first three: rather than naming another external force acting on the world, it names suffering as what the world is grounded on — treating suffering not as one threat among several but as the very foundation the other three afflictions operate within.

A set of four claims about to be precisely rearranged

This discourse's four question-and-answer pairs recur in the next discourse, SN 1.68, with their order changed in a specific way: rather than a simple reversal, the two halves of the sequence swap places as blocks — the third and fourth claims here become the first and second there, and vice versa. This is a new kind of textual play in this collection, distinct from single-word substitution or cumulative addition.

Key terms

uḍḍita — “ensnared” — this discourse's title, and the image applied to craving's hold on the world.
taṇhuḍḍito loko — “craving has ensnared the world” — the first answer, picturing a trap rather than a burden or wound.
jarāya parivārito — “surrounded by old age” — the second answer, shared exactly with SN 1.66's second claim.
maccunā pihito loko — “the world is fastened shut by mortality” — the third answer, picturing confinement rather than a trap.
dukkhe loko patiṭṭhito — “the world is grounded on suffering” — the fourth answer, naming a foundation rather than an external threat.

The text

The discourse in full: four distinct relationships between the world and what afflicts it. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“By what is the world ensnared? By what is it surrounded? By what is the world fastened shut? On what is the world grounded?”

§2“Craving has ensnared the world. It’s surrounded by old age. The world is fastened shut by mortality. The world is grounded on suffering.”
