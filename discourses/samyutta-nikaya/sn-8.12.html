Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 8.12

With Vaṅgīsa

Vaṅgīsasutta

Setting
Sāvatthī, Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery
Speakers
Venerable Vaṅgīsa, alone, newly arahant
Form
An autobiographical verse of reflection, spoken in the bliss of freedom
Length
~3 minutes to read
Northern parallel
A rough counterpart may exist in the Chinese Saṃyukta-āgama (T99), though this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★★★☆☆ — a satisfying, autobiographical close to the entire saṃyutta

Why this discourse

This discourse closes Vaṅgīsasaṃyutta by finally revealing what all eleven preceding discourses were building toward: Vaṅgīsa has newly attained arahantship, and while experiencing the bliss of that freedom, looks back over his own life in verse. He recalls his earlier years wandering ‘drunk on poetry’ from village to village, long before he ever met the Buddha; his growing faith on encountering the teaching of aggregates, sense fields, and elements; his going forth; and finally, in the verse's closing lines, a declaration of the very attainments — the three knowledges, psychic power, and skill in reading others' minds — that he spent SN 8.9 and SN 8.10 praising in Koṇḍañña and Moggallāna. The struggling, self-correcting junior monk of SN 8.1 has arrived.

Reading guide

A life recalled from its very beginning

Unlike any earlier discourse in this saṃyutta, this one reaches back before Vaṅgīsa's ordination entirely, to a life spent wandering as an itinerant poet, ‘drunk on poetry’, moving village to village and town to town — establishing where his gift came from before describing what it became.

Faith arising specifically from a doctrinal teaching

The verse doesn't describe a vague or emotional conversion; it names precisely what the Buddha taught him — the aggregates, sense fields, and elements — grounding his faith in specific doctrinal content rather than charisma alone.

A reflection extending beyond his own personal gain

Before returning to his own attainment, Vaṅgīsa's verse pauses to note that the Buddha's awakening benefited ‘many’, naming monks and nuns generally who ‘see that they've reached certainty’ — situating his own story within a much larger one.

A closing list that answers the saṃyutta's earlier praise

The final stanza's declaration — knowing his past lives, purified clairvoyance, mastery of the three knowledges, psychic power, skill in reading others' minds — directly echoes the qualities Vaṅgīsa praised in Koṇḍañña (SN 8.9) and Moggallāna (SN 8.10), closing the arc from admirer to possessor of these same attainments.

A struggling junior monk's arc completed

Read against SN 8.1's opening, where Vaṅgīsa battled lust as a newly ordained junior left alone to guard a dwelling, this closing discourse completes a real narrative arc across the saṃyutta — not merely a collection of separate praise-poems, but the story of one monk's path from struggle to arahantship.

Key terms

kāveyyamattā vicarimha pubbe — “we used to wander, drunk on poetry” — Vaṅgīsa's own account of his pre-monastic life as an itinerant poet.
khandhāyatanadhātuyo — “the aggregates, sense fields, and elements” — the specific teaching that gave rise to his faith.
bahunnaṁ vata atthāya, bodhiṁ ajjhagamā muni — “it was truly for the benefit of many that the sage achieved awakening” — his reflection situating his own story within a larger one.
tisso vijjā anuppattā, kataṁ buddhassa sāsanaṁ — “I've attained the three knowledges, and fulfilled the Buddha's instructions” — his direct declaration of arahantship.
cetopariyāyakovido — “expert in encompassing the minds of others” — the closing quality, echoing what he praised in both Koṇḍañña and Moggallāna earlier in this saṃyutta.

The text

The discourse in full, closing Vaṅgīsasaṃyutta. The final colophon — the only line of the closing uddāna translated into English in the source — is quoted verbatim below, following this site's established convention for saṃyutta-closing lines. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At one time Venerable Vaṅgīsa was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. Now at that time Vaṅgīsa had recently attained perfection. While experiencing the bliss of freedom, on that occasion he recited these verses:

§2“We used to wander, drunk on poetry, village to village, town to town. Then we saw the Buddha, and faith arose in us.

§3He taught me Dhamma: the aggregates, sense fields, and elements. When I heard his teaching I went forth to homelessness.

§4It was truly for the benefit of many that the sage achieved awakening—for the monks and for the nuns who see that they’ve reached certainty.

§5It was so welcome for me to be in the presence of the Buddha. I’ve attained the three knowledges, and fulfilled the Buddha’s instructions.

§6I know my past lives, my clairvoyance is purified, I am master of three knowledges, attained in psychic power, expert in encompassing the minds of others.”

§7The Linked Discourses with Vaṅgīsa are complete.
