Why this discourse

This discourse answers a mendicant's own direct question — what does “accomplished in the faculties” (indriyasampanna) actually mean? — with a definition this vagga has not used before: developing each of the five faculties in a way that “leads to peace, leads to awakening” (upasamagāmiṁ sambodhagāmiṁ), naming the faculties' own proper DIRECTION rather than their degree of completion.

Reading guide

A question posed directly, breaking from the bare declarative pattern

Confirmed by direct reading, this discourse departs from the bare declarative form used throughout most of this vagga — it opens with a mendicant's own narrative approach and direct question, matching the form this project met earlier at SN 48.18's own discourse, though the two ask about different things.

A new qualifying phrase: leading to peace, leading to awakening

Confirmed by direct reading, this discourse's own answer defines “accomplished in the faculties” through a phrase not used in the earlier discourses of this vagga: each faculty must be developed “upasamagāmiṁ sambodhagāmiṁ” (“leading to peace, leading to awakening”) — naming the faculties' own proper orientation, not their degree of completion as the earlier scales did.

Direction, not degree -- a different axis of measurement

This project's own direct comparison confirms this discourse measures the faculties along a genuinely DIFFERENT axis than SN 48.12-18: those earlier discourses distinguished levels by HOW MUCH of the faculties one has; this discourse instead asks whether the faculties, however much one has, are being developed in the RIGHT DIRECTION.

Key terms

indriyasampanno — “accomplished in the faculties” — the term the mendicant's own question asks the Buddha to define
upasamagāmī — “leading to peace” — the first half of this discourse's own new qualifying phrase, describing the faculties' own proper direction
sambodhagāmī — “leading to awakening” — the second half, paired with “leading to peace”
bhāveti — “develops” — the verb applied to each of the five faculties in this discourse's own answer
ettāvatā — “to this extent, in this way” — the discourse's own closing word, marking the completed definition

The text

The complete discourse. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1 — the mendicant's own questionThen a mendicant went up to the Buddha, bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him: “Sir, they speak of someone who is ‘accomplished in the faculties’. How is someone accomplished in the faculties defined?”

§2 — the Buddha's own answer“Mendicant, it’s when a mendicant develops the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, immersion, and wisdom that lead to peace and awakening. This is how someone who is accomplished in the faculties is defined.”
