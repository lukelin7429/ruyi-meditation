Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 35.109

Things That Tighten Fetters

Saṁyojaniyasutta — Yogakkhemivagga

Setting

under 1 minute to read

Why this discourse

This discourse makes a conceptual move this project has already met applied to other targets: distinguishing the things prone to a defilement from the defilement itself. The six sense faculties — eye through mind — are named things that tighten the fetters (saṁyojaniyā dhammā), but the fetter (saṁyojana) proper is identified narrowly as the desire and greed (chandarāga) for them, not the faculties themselves. A sense faculty is not, in itself, a bond; wanting it is. The distinction matters practically: what must be abandoned is never the eye or the mind as such, only the craving that fastens onto them.

Reading guide

A distinction, not a list

The discourse's own question names two things at once — “the things that tighten the fetters” and “the fetter” itself — and the answer is careful to keep them apart: the six faculties are the first, the desire and greed for them is the second.

The faculty is not the bond

Naming the eye itself as merely “something that tightens the fetters,” rather than as the fetter, keeps the object of practice precise: nothing needs to be destroyed or escaped in the sense faculties themselves, only the chandarāga (desire-and-greed) that fastens onto them.

A single sentence, six times over

The same one-line formula — “the eye is something that tightens the fetters; the desire and greed for it is the fetter” — is run across all six faculties, with the four in the middle folded into a single peyyāla ellipsis.

A pair completed by the discourse just after it

SN 35.110, immediately following, repeats this exact structure for a second pair of terms — things that fuel grasping, and grasping itself — making these two discourses a matched set built on the identical logical move.

Key terms

saṁyojaniyā dhammā — things that tighten the fetters — the six sense faculties themselves, named as prone to bondage without being bondage
saṁyojana — the fetter — identified narrowly as the desire and greed for a sense faculty, not the faculty itself
chandarāga — desire and greed — the precise term the discourse isolates as what actually binds, at any of the six sense faculties
cakkhu — the eye — the first faculty given in full before the remaining four are folded into a peyyāla ellipsis
mano — the mind — the sixth and last faculty named, closing the formula's single run through all six

The text

The complete discourse: a single definitional formula run across all six sense faculties. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Mendicants, I will teach you the things that tighten the fetters, and the fetter. Listen … What are the things that tighten the fetters? And what is the fetter?

§2The eye is something that tightens the fetters. The desire and greed for it is the fetter. The ear … nose … tongue … body … mind is something that tightens the fetters. The desire and greed for it is the fetter. These are called the things that tighten the fetters, and this is the fetter.”
