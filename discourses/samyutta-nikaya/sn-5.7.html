Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 5.7

With Upacālā

Upacālāsutta

Setting
At Sāvatthī, at the root of a tree, for the day's meditation
Speakers
The nun Upacālā and Māra the Wicked
Form
A tempting offer naming five specific heavens, refused wholesale, then a striking image of the entire world on fire
Length
~1.5 minutes to read
Northern parallel
A rough counterpart may exist in the Chinese Saṃyukta-āgama (T99), though this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★★★★☆ — continues the pair traditionally identified as sisters alongside SN 5.6 and SN 5.8

Why this discourse

Where SN 5.6's Cālā rejected rebirth in general terms, Māra here tries something more specific with Upacālā, asked plainly where she wants to be reborn: not wanting rebirth ‘anywhere’, she is offered a tempting list of five named heavenly realms, promised delight if she sets her heart on them. Her refusal names each realm back to him individually before delivering one of this saṃyutta's most striking images: the entire world ablaze, on fire, smoldering, rocking — with her own mind set instead on the one place Māra cannot reach at all.

Reading guide

A specific offer, not a general temptation

Unlike SN 5.6's more abstract exchange about rebirth as such, Māra here names five distinct heavenly realms by type — the thirty-three gods, the gods of Yama, the Joyful Deities, and others — a concrete menu of options rather than a vague promise, suited to Upacālā's own equally specific opening answer that she wants rebirth ‘nowhere’.

Every option refused by the same underlying logic

Upacālā's reply doesn't evaluate each heaven separately as more or less desirable; it repeats Māra's own list back to him only to dismiss all five together with a single reason — they remain ‘bound with the bonds of sensuality’ and fall back under Māra's sway eventually, however exalted they may seem.

Even heaven still counted as Māra's territory

The verse's underlying claim is significant: these are not framed as neutral realms outside Māra's reach that simply don't interest her, but as domains still within his ‘sway’, meaning ordinary heavenly rebirth, however pleasant, does not actually represent an escape from him at all.

The whole world on fire: a shared image with a famous discourse

‘All the world is on fire, all the world is smoldering, all the world is ablaze, all the world is rocking’ echoes the imagery of this canon's famous Fire Sermon (Ādittapariyāya), applying that same vocabulary of universal burning to the entire cosmos of possible rebirths Māra has just offered, heaven included.

A place defined only by what it lacks

Upacālā's own preferred destination is described entirely negatively — not shaking, not burning, not frequented by ordinary people, a place ‘where Māra cannot go’ — naming what it is not rather than positively describing it, letting its distance from everything Māra has just offered define it by contrast.

Key terms

Upacālā — “Upacālā” — the nun this discourse follows, traditionally identified alongside Cālā and Sīsupacālā as sisters, though the discourses themselves do not state this directly.
Tāvatiṁsa Yāma — “the thirty-three gods…those of Yama” — two of the five named heavenly realms in Māra's offer.
kāmabandhana — “the bonds of sensuality” — what Upacālā says still binds every one of the offered realms.
sabbo pajjālito loko — “all the world is ablaze” — the verse's central image, echoing this canon's Fire Sermon.
agati yattha mārassa — “where Māra cannot go” — Upacālā's own closing description of her preferred destination, defined by what it lacks.

The text

The discourse in full: Māra's specific offer of five heavenly realms and Upacālā's wholesale refusal. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. Then the nun Upacālā robed up in the morning … and sat at the root of a tree for the day’s meditation. Then Māra the Wicked went up to Upacālā and said to her, “Nun, where do you want to be reborn?” “I don’t want to be reborn anywhere, sir.”

§2“There are the gods of the thirty-three, and those of Yama; also the Joyful Deities, the gods who love to create, and the gods who control that imagined by others. Set your heart on such places, and you’ll undergo delight.”

§3“The gods of the thirty-three, and those of Yama; also the Joyful Deities, the gods who love to create, and the gods who control that imagined by others—they’re bound with the bonds of sensuality; they fall under your sway again.

§4All the world is on fire, all the world is smoldering, all the world is ablaze, all the world is rocking. My mind adores that place where Māra cannot go; it’s not shaking or burning, and not frequented by ordinary people.”

§5Then Māra the Wicked, thinking, “The nun Upacālā knows me!” miserable and sad, vanished right there.
