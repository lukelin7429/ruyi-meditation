Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 22.65

When You Take Pleasure

Abhinandamānasutta — Arahantavagga

Setting
Sāvatthī
Speakers

Length
~2 minutes to read

A rough counterpart may exist in the Chinese Saṃyukta-āgama (T99), though this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — identical structure to SN 22.63-64, the third of eight matched discourses

Why this discourse

This discourse continues the vagga's matched set with a third substitution: “when you take pleasure (abhinandamāna), mendicant, you're bound by Māra. Not taking pleasure, you're free from the Wicked One.” “Taking pleasure” recalls SN 22.29's earlier chained equation (taking pleasure in an aggregate is taking pleasure in suffering itself) and SN 22.28's universal claim about why sentient beings are aroused by the aggregates' gratification. Here the same underlying concept is bound directly to Māra's dominion, continuing this vagga's practice of naming several distinct but related mental acts as each independently sufficient grounds for bondage or freedom.

Reading guide

A term already explored from two earlier angles

“Taking pleasure” (abhinandati) is the exact verb SN 22.29 used to identify pleasure in an aggregate with pleasure in suffering itself, and the same underlying concept SN 22.28's universal conditional argument invoked to explain why sentient beings are aroused by the aggregates at all. This discourse compresses both of those earlier treatments into the vagga's recurring Māra formula.

The identical frame, the third substitution in a row

As in SN 22.63 and SN 22.64, every other element of the narrative is unchanged: the request for brevity, the compact formula, the mendicant's confirmed explanation across all five aggregates, and his subsequent attainment. Three consecutive discourses have now used this exact shell with three different central terms.

A cluster of three related mental acts

Grasping (SN 22.63), conceiving (SN 22.64), and taking pleasure (this discourse) together form a loose cluster of related but distinct ways a mendicant might remain bound to the aggregates — through active holding, through mental construction, and through affective enjoyment respectively. The vagga does not rank them or claim one is more fundamental than the others; each is presented as its own complete teaching.

A shift in register signaled by what comes next

This is the last of the vagga's matched discourses to use the Māra framing specifically; SN 22.66 immediately after it will shift to a simpler formula built directly around impermanence, opening a second cluster of four discourses (SN 22.66-69) that dispenses with the Māra imagery entirely in favor of the more familiar vocabulary used throughout most of this book.

Key terms

abhinandamāna — “when you take pleasure” — this discourse's title term, the same verb SN 22.29 used to identify pleasure in an aggregate with pleasure in suffering itself.
Māra — the personification of death and temptation, invoked here identically to SN 22.63-64's formula.
assāda — “gratification” — the noun form connected to this discourse's verb, central to SN 22.26-28's earlier gratification/drawback/escape triad.
saṅkhittena — “in brief” — the mendicant's request, unchanged from SN 22.63-64.
arahataṁ ahosi — “became one of the perfected” — the identical outcome closing this discourse, as in SN 22.63-64.

The text

The discourse in full. Elided repetitions (feeling, perception, and choices, each following the same formula spelled out in full for form and consciousness) are given exactly as bilara-data preserves them. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Then a mendicant went up to the Buddha … and asked him, “Sir, may the Buddha please teach me Dhamma in brief. When I’ve heard it, I’ll live alone, withdrawn, diligent, keen, and resolute.” “When you take pleasure, mendicant, you’re bound by Māra. Not taking pleasure, you’re free from the Wicked One.” “Understood, Blessed One! Understood, Holy One!”

§2“But how do you see the detailed meaning of my brief statement?” “Sir, when you take pleasure in form you’re bound by Māra. Not taking pleasure, you’re free from the Wicked One.

§3consciousness you’re bound by Māra. Not taking pleasure, you’re free from the Wicked One. That’s how I understand the detailed meaning of the Buddha’s brief statement.”

§4“Good, good, mendicant! It’s good that you understand the detailed meaning of what I’ve said in brief like this. When you take pleasure in form you’re bound by Māra. Not taking pleasure, you’re free from the Wicked One. When you take pleasure in feeling …

§5consciousness you’re bound by Māra. Not taking pleasure, you’re free from the Wicked One. This is how to understand the detailed meaning of what I said in brief.” … And that mendicant became one of the perfected.
