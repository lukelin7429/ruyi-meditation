Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 22.39

In Line With the Teaching

Anudhammasutta — Natumhākavagga

Setting
Sāvatthī
Speakers
The Buddha alone, addressing the assembled mendicants
Form
A single chain of three steps, opening the vagga's closing quartet
Length
~2 minutes to read
Northern parallel
A rough counterpart may exist in the Chinese Saṃyukta-āgama (T99), though this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★★☆☆☆ — opens a quartet whose remaining three discourses vary this one's opening step

Why this discourse

This discourse opens the vagga's closing quartet by naming what “practicing in line with the teaching” (dhammānudhammapaṭipanna) actually consists of: living full of disillusionment for the five aggregates. Disillusionment leads to complete understanding; complete understanding leads to freedom from the aggregates; and that freedom is, in turn, freedom from rebirth, old age, and death, and from the entire list of afflictions that follow from them — sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress. The chain closes with the Buddha's own direct declaration: “they're freed from suffering, I say.” This discourse's full statement of the chain is what the three discourses immediately after it will each vary by a single opening term.

Reading guide

A definition of "practicing in line with the teaching"

The discourse opens by naming its own subject precisely: when a mendicant is practicing in line with the teaching (anudhamma), this — what follows — is what that consists of. The definition is not left implicit; the discourse states outright that it is defining the term named in its own title.

A three-step chain from disillusionment to freedom

The chain itself has three explicit steps: living full of disillusionment (nibbidābahula) for the five aggregates; from this, completely understanding (parijānāti) them; and from complete understanding, being freed (vimuccati) from them. Each step depends strictly on the one before it, in the same dependency structure seen in SN 22.12's earlier liberation chain.

The fullest description of freedom in this vagga

Where many discourses in this book close on the compact phrase “freed from suffering,” this discourse spells out what that freedom specifically includes: freedom from rebirth, old age, and death, and from sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress — the fullest single description of the aggregates of dukkha found together anywhere in Natumhākavagga, before compressing it all into the final summary “they're freed from suffering, I say.”

A template the vagga's closing quartet will vary

This discourse's opening step — living full of disillusionment — is what the three discourses immediately after it will each replace with a different meditation practice: observing impermanence (SN 22.40), observing suffering (SN 22.41), and observing not-self (SN 22.42), each still leading to the identical closing declaration.

Key terms

anudhamma — “in line with the teaching” — the discourse's title term and the practice it defines, naming correct conduct relative to the Dhamma as a whole.
nibbidābahula — “full of disillusionment” — the chain's opening step, describing a sustained disposition rather than a single momentary insight.
parijānāti — “completely understands” — the chain's second step, the same pariññā defined earlier in this vagga and in SN 22.23 within Bhāravagga.
vimuccati — “is freed” — the chain's third and final step, the same verb used in SN 22.12's earlier liberation chain.
dukkhasmā parimuccati, vadāmi — “they're freed from suffering, I say” — the discourse's closing declaration, marked with the Buddha's explicit personal voice, as in SN 22.29 earlier in this book.

The text

The discourse in full. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. “Mendicants, when a mendicant is practicing in line with the teaching, this is what’s in line with the teaching. They should live full of disillusionment for form, feeling, perception, choices, and consciousness. Living in this way, they completely understand form, feeling, perception, choices, and consciousness. Completely understanding form, feeling, perception, choices, and consciousness, they’re freed from these things. They’re freed from rebirth, old age, and death, from sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress. They’re freed from suffering, I say.”
