Why this discourse

After three discourses of pure analysis, this discourse returns to the body with the vagga's most graphic image yet: possessions, honor, and popularity cut through the outer skin, then the inner skin, then the flesh, then the sinews, then the bone, until they reach the marrow itself and keep pressing. Unlike most of this saṃyutta's images, this one is stated as a direct claim rather than introduced through a simile — the danger itself is described as doing the cutting, not merely compared to something that cuts.

Reading guide

A direct claim, not a simile this time

Most images in this saṃyutta compare possessions, honor, and popularity to something else — a hook, a jackal, gale-force winds; this discourse instead states directly that they themselves cut through skin and flesh, collapsing the distance between image and claim that a simile would normally preserve.

A sequence that only deepens, never widens

The list of what gets cut moves in one direction only — outer skin, inner skin, flesh, sinew, bone, marrow — each layer named in order, giving the image a controlled, escalating structure rather than a loose list of injuries.

Reaching the marrow and continuing to press

The discourse doesn't stop once the deepest layer is reached; āhacca tiṭṭhati, “keeps pressing,” specifies that even having penetrated to the marrow, the harm doesn't relent or withdraw.

The shortest form of an image the next discourse expands

This discourse states the cutting image with no narrative frame at all; the discourse immediately following it supplies an explicit simile — a rope tightened around the shin — that gives this same sequence a concrete scenario to unfold within.

Physical language for a non-physical danger

Possessions, honor, and popularity are not literally capable of cutting flesh; the discourse's choice to describe them exactly as if they were signals how seriously this saṃyutta wants the danger's effects to be felt, not merely acknowledged.

Key terms

chaviṁ chindati — “cuts through the outer skin” — the first and shallowest layer named in this discourse's sequence.
cammaṁ … maṁsaṁ … nhāruṁ … aṭṭhiṁ — “the inner skin … the flesh … the sinews … the bone” — the successive layers the image penetrates, named in strict order.
aṭṭhimiñjaṁ āhacca tiṭṭhati — “reaches the marrow and keeps pressing” — the image's endpoint, specifying that the harm continues even once it has penetrated fully.
dāruṇo — “grim” — the discourse's opening characterization, before the cutting image is stated.
aṭṭhamaṁ — “the eighth” — this discourse's closing numbering tag.

The text

The discourse in full. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1At Sāvatthī. “Possessions, honor, and popularity are grim … They cut through the outer skin, the inner skin, the flesh, sinews, and bones, until they reach the marrow and keep pressing. So grim are possessions, honor, and popularity. …”
