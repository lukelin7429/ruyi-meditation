Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 19.21

A Bad Novice Nun

Pāpasāmaṇerīsutta

Setting
Rājagaha, on Vulture's Peak Mountain

Why this discourse

This discourse closes both the fivefold monastic sequence and the entire saṃyutta by restoring full narrative detail, mirroring SN 19.1's completeness rather than continuing SN 19.18-20's extreme brevity. Moggallāna describes seeing a novice nun whose outer robe, bowl, belt, and body were all burning, blazing, and glowing as she screamed in pain. The Buddha confirms the vision, explains he had seen her before but stayed silent to spare others the harm of disbelief, and reveals she was once a bad novice nun in the time of the Buddha Kassapa's dispensation, burning in hell for many hundreds of thousands of years before this residual consequence.

Reading guide

A closing discourse that mirrors the opening's completeness

After four consecutive discourses of increasing brevity (SN 19.17-20), this discourse restores the full frame narrative — the vision described in detail, the Buddha's confirmation, the explanation of past silence, and the specific karmic history — matching SN 19.1's structure almost exactly.

The fivefold sequence completed at its final rank

Sāmaṇerī, a novice nun, completes the sequence begun at SN 19.17: bhikkhu, bhikkhunī, sikkhamānā, sāmaṇera, and now sāmaṇerī, covering every rank the tradition recognizes across both the male and female ordination paths.

The identical explanation for past silence reused word for word

The Buddha's reasoning here — that speaking of this being earlier would have led to disbelief, which would have been harmful to those who disbelieved — repeats SN 19.1's explanation almost verbatim, framing the entire saṃyutta as bookended by the same justification for revealing these visions now.

A closing discourse's own translated colophon

Following this discourse's account, the source includes an untranslated uddāna verse and closing material, but the saṃyutta's own colophon — the Linked Discourses with Mark are complete — is rendered in full, confirming this collection's formal end and naming Venerable Lakkhaṇa, Moggallāna's questioning companion, as the figure the entire saṃyutta is named after.

Twenty-one discourses building one sustained argument

Read as a whole, this saṃyutta doesn't merely catalog disconnected grotesque images; it builds a sustained case that visible, karmic consequence extends across every kind of wrongdoer — animal-killers, hunters, adulterers, sorcerers, a queen, an executioner, and now every rank of the ordained community — confirmed at every step by the Buddha's own direct testimony.

Key terms

sāmaṇerī — “a novice nun” — this discourse's title term, completing the fivefold sequence's coverage of every ordained rank.
saṅghāṭipi ādittā sampajjalitā sajotibhūtā — “her outer robe was burning, blazing, and glowing” — the full description restored here, matching SN 19.17's fuller treatment rather than SN 19.18-20's brevity.
dīgharattaṁ ahitāya dukkhāya — “for their lasting harm and suffering” — the Buddha's reasoning for his past silence, repeated nearly word for word from SN 19.1.
bahūni vassasatasahassāni niraye paccitvā — “having burned in hell for many hundreds of thousands of years” — specifying this apparition as a residual consequence following a primary period of suffering already served.
lakkhaṇasaṁyuttaṁ samattaṁ — “the Linked Discourses with Mark are complete” — the saṃyutta's own closing colophon, translated in full by the source, naming Venerable Lakkhaṇa.

The text

The discourse in full, restoring SN 19.1's complete frame narrative, followed by the saṃyutta's own closing colophon. The closing uddāna verse of discourse titles is not translated in the source and is not quoted here; see the reading guide above. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Just now, reverend, as I was descending from Vulture’s Peak Mountain I saw a novice nun flying through the air. Her outer robe, bowl, belt, and body were burning, blazing, and glowing as she screamed in pain. It occurred to me: ‘Oh lord, how incredible, how amazing! That there can be such a sentient being, such a spirit, such an incarnation!’”

§2Then the Buddha said to the mendicants: “Mendicants, there are disciples who live full of vision and knowledge, since a disciple knows, sees, and witnesses such a thing. Formerly, I too saw that novice nun, but I did not speak of it. For if I had spoken of it others would not have believed me, which would be for their lasting harm and suffering. That female novice used to be a bad novice nun in the time of the Buddha Kassapa’s dispensation. As a result of that deed she burned in hell for many years, many hundreds, many thousands, many hundreds of thousands of years. Now she experiences the residual result of that deed in such an incarnation.”

§3 · ColophonThe Linked Discourses with Mark are complete.
