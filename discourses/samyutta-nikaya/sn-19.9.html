Saṃyutta Nikāya · Discourse 19.9

Needle Hairs (2nd)

Dutiyasūcilomasutta

Setting
Rājagaha

~1 minute to read

Why this discourse

This discourse reuses SN 19.8's needle image under an explicitly marked title — Dutiyasūcilomasutta, “the second Needle Hairs” — but elaborates it considerably: the needles bore into his head and out his mouth, into his mouth and out his chest, into his chest and out his belly, into his belly and out his thighs, into his thighs and out his calves, and into his calves and out his feet. The cause named is sūcaka, “an informant,” someone who spreads damaging reports or slander — a term sharing its root with sūci, needle, the image itself.

Reading guide

A named sequel, not a coincidental repeat

The Pali title itself marks this discourse as explicitly second, Dutiyasūciloma, distinguishing it from a mere reuse of an existing name and confirming the source itself recognizes these two discourses as a deliberate pair.

An itinerary through the entire body, not a single wound

Rather than describing needles simply rising and falling as in SN 19.5-8, this discourse traces a specific path — head, mouth, chest, belly, thighs, calves, feet — giving the torment a traceable, itemized geography absent from any earlier discourse in this saṃyutta.

A wordplay linking image and cause directly

Sūcaka, an informant, and sūci, a needle, share an obvious root; this discourse's choice of cause for its needle-based torment isn't incidental but plays directly on the linguistic connection between piercing speech and piercing implements.

Piercing speech given a piercing consequence

An informant's characteristic wrongdoing is verbal — passing on damaging words — and this discourse's elaborate itinerary of needles piercing clean through the body gives that verbal harm an unusually direct and literal physical correspondence.

The most elaborate torment so far, marking a genuine escalation

Compared to every discourse before it in this saṃyutta, this discourse's step-by-step itinerary represents a clear escalation in descriptive detail, suggesting the saṃyutta reserves its most elaborate imagery for causes it wants to underline rather than distributing detail evenly across every discourse.

Key terms

dutiyasūcilomasutta — “the second Needle Hairs discourse” — this discourse's own title, explicitly marking it as a deliberate sequel to SN 19.8.
sīse pavisitvā mukhato nikkhamanti — “bored into his head and out his mouth” — the first leg of this discourse's itemized itinerary of piercing.
sūcako — “an informant” — sharing its root with sūci, needle, linking this discourse's cause directly to its central image.
…pe… — the peyyāla elision mark — used here only for the frame narrative, not for the detailed itinerary itself, which is given in full.
navamaṁ — “the ninth” — this discourse's closing numbering tag.

The text

The discourse as preserved in the source, with its detailed itinerary of piercing given in full even as the surrounding frame narrative remains elided. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Just now, reverend, as I was descending from Vulture’s Peak Mountain I saw a man whose body hairs were needles flying through the air. The needles bored into his head and out his mouth, into his mouth and out his chest, into his chest and out his belly, into his belly and out his thighs, into his thighs and out his calves, and into his calves and out his feet. And he screamed in pain. …” … “That being used to be an informant right here in Rājagaha. …”
