Saṃyutta Nikāya

Selected Connected Discourses of the Buddha

The Saṃyutta Nikāya organizes the Buddha's teaching by theme — one "connection" (saṃyutta) for discourses on dependent origination, one for the five aggregates, one for the six sense fields, one for the noble eightfold path, and dozens more, gathered into five books. Depending on how the many short, thematically-clustered entries are counted, the whole collection runs to well over 2,900 individual suttas — by far the largest of the four main Nikāyas after the Aṅguttara, and the least practical to work through one full study page at a time.

This collection began as a curated selection of twenty widely quoted discourses spanning all five books, chosen because they contain some of the most famous and doctrinally central teachings in the entire Pāli Canon — including the Buddha's first sermon, the second sermon on non-self, and the Fire Sermon. It is now being extended toward complete coverage, working in order from Book I forward. Each entry, old or new, has the same reading guide, full Sujato translation, and self-check quiz as our other series.

A note on scope and on how to read this page: like our Aṅguttara Nikāya series, this collection includes a number of merged pages where the source itself compresses several sutta-numbers into a single abbreviated (peyyāla) text — a row spanning a range like SN 12.93–213 is one such page, not a single discourse. Rows below Book I that are not yet linked are listed for context and are being added in order.
