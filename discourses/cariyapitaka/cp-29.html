Cariyapitaka · Story 29

The Baby Quail’s Conduct

Vaṭṭaka Cariyā — The Chapter With Yudhañjaya · The Perfection of Truth (3rd of 6)

Setting
No external narrative frame; the Buddha speaks in the first person about his past life as a newly hatched quail in Magadha
Speaker
The Buddha, recounting his life as the baby quail
Form
Eleven verses of first-person narration, including a formal declaration of truth
Length
1–2 minutes to read
Northern parallel
This reading guide does not assert a specific matching text for this story in other Buddhist literatures.
Difficulty
★★☆☆☆ — introduces a specific literary device worth understanding on its own terms

Why this text

A newly hatched quail, too young to fly or walk, is abandoned by his fleeing parents as a forest fire closes in. With no physical means of escape, he does something this collection has not shown before: he makes a formal, public ‘declaration of truth’, relying on the truth itself, rather than any action, to turn the fire back.

Reading guide

A helplessness stated without embellishment

The quail's situation is described in the plainest physical terms: ‘wings that do not fly’, ‘feet that do not walk’, abandoned in the nest by parents who saved themselves — no rescue is coming, and no ordinary action is available to him.

A different kind of power invoked

Facing the fire, the quail does not struggle or pray for aid; he invokes ‘the quality of virtue in the world, truth, purity, and mercy’ directly, framing his own truthful statement as a real force with real consequences, not merely words.

A declaration that states the situation exactly as it is

The declaration itself is not a request or a wish; it simply restates the quail's helplessness precisely: ‘I have wings that do not fly! I have feet that do not walk! Mother and father have fled!’ — followed by a direct command to the fire itself: ‘go back!’

An effect stated as immediate and total

The story reports the result without qualification: the flames withdrew sixteen leagues, ‘as if they had come to water’. This kind of formal truth-telling with a claimed miraculous effect, sometimes called a ‘truth-act’ in scholarship on Indian literature, recurs again in Cp 30's fish king and Cp 31's Dark Light.

Key terms

sacca — “truth” — the perfection this story illustrates, the third of six stories on this theme.
saccakiriyā — an “act of truth” or “truth-act” — a formal declaration of a true fact, relied upon as a source of real power; this story is the first of several examples of the device in this chapter.
Magadha — the region named as the quail's home.
Jātaveda — an epithet for fire, addressed directly in the quail's declaration — ‘Jātaveda the fire: go back!’
cariyā — “conduct” — this collection's title, and the word used for each of its thirty-five stories, this one its twenty-ninth.

The text

The text in full: eleven verses, including the quail's formal declaration of truth. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Then again when I was a baby quail in Magadha, I was young, with wings unsprouted, just a scrap of meat in the nest.

§2With food she brought in her beak my mother nurtured me. I survived through her touch, for I had no physical strength.

§3One year, in the hot season, the forest caught fire. It came towards us, a conflagration with a blackened trail.

§4The fire roared and rumbled with great crests of flame, gradually burning closer to me.

§5Alarmed and terrified at the fierce fire, my mother and father abandoned me in the nest, saving themselves.

§6I beat my feet and wings, but I had no physical strength. When unable to leave, I thought this there:

§7‘Those to whom I ought to run, scared and trembling with fear, have escaped, leaving me behind. How should I act today?

§8There is the quality of virtue in the world, truth, purity, and mercy. By this truth I will make a supreme declaration of truth.’

§9Inspired by the power of Dhamma, recollecting the former Victors, relying on the power of truth, I made a declaration of truth.

§10‘I have wings that do not fly! I have feet that do not walk! Mother and father have fled! Jātaveda the fire: go back!’

§11As I declared this truth the great crested flames withdrew sixteen leagues, as if they had come to water. There is no-one to equal my truthfulness: this is my perfection of truth.”
