Why this text

This poem closes the five-poem refrain sequence begun at Ja 41 with a tale strikingly similar to the previous poem's — a well-meaning act of care, misapplied, causing exactly the death it was meant to prevent — and Sujato's comment flags a specific word in the opening line that even the traditional commentary cannot satisfactorily explain.

Reading guide

A second version of the same tragic pattern

The verse restates the proverb once more: ‘better is an intelligent foe than a foolish sympathizer’ — then delivers its own grim proof: ‘see that wretched Rohinī, grieving after killing her mother.’ Per Sujato's comment, this tale is ‘similar to the previous story’: a servant girl kills her mother with a pestle while trying to rid her of flies.

A word even the tradition itself struggles with

Sujato's comment flags a specific difficulty: the opening word ‘yañce’ is ‘hard to explain’, with the traditional commentary offering ‘several options, none of which are terribly convincing’. Sujato's own suggestion is that it might be ‘an old corruption’ of a phrase used in the previous poem, ‘na tveva’ — an honest acknowledgment that not every textual puzzle in this collection has a fully satisfying answer.

Key terms

seyyo amitto medhāvī — “better is an intelligent foe” — this poem's version of the chapter's closing proverb pair (shared with Ja 44).
bālānukampako — “a foolish sympathizer” — the disqualified alternative, echoing Ja 44's 'inconsiderate friend'.
yañce — an opening word Sujato's comment describes as “hard to explain”, possibly an old corruption of ‘na tveva’ from the previous poem.
Rohiṇijātaka — the traditional title of this tale, named for the servant girl at its center.
Ja 44 — the previous poem, sharing both this poem's proverb structure and its underlying pattern of well-meaning harm.

The text

The text in full: a single verse, closing this chapter's refrain sequence, with a genuine textual puzzle discussed above. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Better is an intelligent foe than a foolish sympathizer. See that wretched Rohinī, grieving after killing her mother.
