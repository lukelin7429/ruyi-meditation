Why this text

The verse speaks only of trees, but per Sujato's comment the underlying story is about tree-dwelling deities, advised to cluster together in the forest rather than set up home alone in villages out of greed for offerings — a case where the verse's surface image and its story's real subject diverge, echoing the safety-in-numbers theme already opened at this site's own Ja 31.

Reading guide

A proverb about solitary trees and the wind

The verse states its proverb plainly: it is well for many relatives, even trees born in the forest — the wind sweeps off one standing alone, even the enormous lord of the forest itself. Size alone is no protection against isolation.

Per Sujato's comment, the verse is about trees, but the story is about something else

Sujato's comment notes that while the verse speaks only of trees, the story it illustrates is about the deities who live in trees: they are advised to dwell only in those clustered together in the forest. Some deities, however, greedy for human offerings, set up home instead in trees worshipped as shrines by villages — standing alone, and so exposed. This reading guide notes a resonance, without asserting a direct textual connection, with this site's own Ja 31, which opens its own chapter on the same underlying theme: safety found in clustering together rather than settling alone.

Key terms

Sādhū sambahulā ñātī — “it is well for the many relatives” — the verse's opening statement of its proverb.
Vāto vahati ekaṭṭhaṁ — “the wind sweeps off one standing alone” — the verse's central image of isolated vulnerability.
brahantampi vanappatiṁ — “even the enormous lord of the forest” — the verse's point that size alone offers no protection against standing alone.
Rukkhadhammajātaka — the traditional title of this tale, ‘The Law of Trees’.
Ja 31 — this site's already-completed page, ‘Nests’, opening its own chapter on a resonant theme of safety found in clustering together.

The text

The text in full: a single verse. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1It is well for the many relatives, the trees born in the forest. The wind sweeps off one standing alone, even the enormous lord of the forest.
