Jataka · No. 76

Unsuspecting

Asaṅkiya — Book of the Ones · Chapter Eight (Varuṇavagga) · Poem 6 of 9

Why this text

Per Sujato's comment, an ascetic is unafraid even in the presence of bandits, for the simple reason that he has nothing to lose — a fearlessness this reading guide notes, without overstating the connection, as a kind of answer to Ja 70's gardener two chapters back, whose renunciation was never quite complete.

Reading guide

A declaration of fearlessness, in the first person

The verse speaks directly, in the ascetic's own voice: ‘I am unsuspecting in the village; in the wilderness there's no peril for me: I ride upon the straight path with love and compassion.’ Two supports for this fearlessness are named together — having nothing left to lose, and a settled practice of love and compassion toward whoever he meets.

Per Sujato's comment, a cross-reference and a modest resonance

Sujato's comment points to the same idiom for ‘mounted’ (samārūḷha, literally travelling along as if riding a horse) at this site's own MN 12. This reading guide also notes, without asserting a direct textual connection, a resonance with Ja 70 two chapters back: there, a gardener-turned-ascetic could not quite let go of his own spade; here, an ascetic genuinely without possessions has, correspondingly, genuinely nothing left to fear.

Key terms

Asaṅkiyomhi gāmamhi — “I am unsuspecting in the village” — the verse's opening declaration, giving this poem its traditional title.
araññe natthi me bhayaṁ — “in the wilderness there's no peril for me” — the same fearlessness extended from village to wilderness alike.
Ujuṁ maggaṁ samārūḷho — “I ride upon the straight path” — the same idiom Sujato's comment cross-references at this site's own MN 12.
mettāya karuṇāya — “with love and compassion” — named alongside having nothing to lose as the second support for this fearlessness.
Asaṅkiyajātaka — the traditional title of this tale, ‘Unsuspecting’.

The text

The text in full: a single verse. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1I am unsuspecting in the village; in the wilderness there’s no peril for me: I ride upon the straight path with love and compassion.
