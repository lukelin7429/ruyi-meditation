Jataka · No. 54

The What-fruit

Kiṅphala — Book of the Ones · Chapter Six (Āsīsavagga) · Poem 4 of 10

Setting

Speaker
A wise caravan leader

Length
Under 1 minute to read

Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — one short verse of practical deduction

Why this text

This poem completes Ja 53's matched pair with a different kind of everyday deduction: a caravan leader reasons that fruit growing untouched on an accessible tree, right beside a well-traveled road, cannot possibly be as sweet as it looks — because if it were, someone would already have picked it clean.

Reading guide

A conclusion reached from what's absent, not what's present

The verse gives the caravan leader's own reasoning: ‘this tree is not hard to climb, nor is it far from the village. For this reason I know this tree is not sweet-fruited.’ Per Sujato's comment, the tree is laden with fruit that looks like mango, but the wise caravan leader deduces it must be the poisonous ‘what-fruit’ (kiṁphala) precisely because it is so easy to reach and so untouched.

The same deductive refrain, a different domain entirely

Where Ja 53's deduction concerned a social plot detected through human behavior, this poem's deduction is purely practical and botanical — showing this chapter's shared refrain applies just as well to reading nature carefully as to reading people.

Key terms

durāruho — “hard to climb” — what the tree is notably not, contributing to the caravan leader's suspicion.
ākāraṇena jānāmi — “for this reason I know” — the deductive refrain shared word for word with the previous poem, Ja 53.
kiṁphala — the “what-fruit”, a poisonous fruit resembling mango, giving this poem its traditional title.
Kiṁphalajātaka — the traditional title of this tale, ‘The What-fruit’.
duma — “tree”, per Sujato's comment specially indicating a flowering tree in this context.

The text

The text in full: a single verse, completing the matched pair begun at Ja 53. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1This tree is not hard to climb, nor is it far from the village. For this reason I know this tree is not sweet-fruited.
