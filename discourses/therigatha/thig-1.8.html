Mittā (1st)

Why this text

This verse and the one immediately after it, Thig 1.9, share nearly identical wording — a template repeated with only the addressee's name and one key phrase changed. Here, addressed to Mittā (‘friend’), the instruction centers on appreciating ‘spiritual friends’, a concept the wider tradition treats as close to the whole of the path.

Reading guide

A name meaning friend, turned toward friendship itself

‘Mittā’ means ‘friend’. The instruction built on her name does not simply tell her to be a good friend, but to ‘appreciate your spiritual friends’ — gratitude for companionship on the path, not just the quality of friendliness.

Faith named as the starting point

The verse opens by naming what brought Mittā to this point: ‘having gone forth in faith’ — renunciation framed as an act of trust, not calculation.

A near-template shared with the very next poem

Compare this verse to Thig 1.9, addressed to Bhadrā: the structure, most of the wording, and the closing image of ‘sanctuary from the yoke’ are identical, with only the name and the specific object of appreciation changed.

A concept larger than this one short verse

‘Spiritual friendship’ (kalyāṇamittatā) is elsewhere in the canon described by the Buddha as effectively the whole of the spiritual life, not merely a support for it — this brief instruction rests on a much larger claim about how central companionship is to the path.

Key terms

Mittā — a nun's name, also the Pali word for ‘friend’ — the instruction plays directly on this meaning.
kalyāṇamittatā — “spiritual friendship” or “admirable companionship” — elsewhere in the canon described as close to the whole of the spiritual path, not merely a support for it.
saddhā — “faith” or “confidence” — named as what led Mittā to go forth from household life.
yoga-kkhema — “sanctuary from the yoke” — the same compound term closing Thig 1.6, appearing again here as the aim of developing skillful qualities.
cariyā — not used here; another of this book's unattributed poems, closing with no formula naming a perfection.

The text

The text in full: a single four-line verse, with no attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Having gone forth in faith, appreciate your spiritual friends, Mittā. Develop skillful qualities for the sake of sanctuary from the yoke.
