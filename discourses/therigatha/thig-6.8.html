Why this text

This poem closes the Book of the Sixes by drawing together several threads from earlier in the collection: its opening confession repeats Thig 2.10's exactly, its teacher's instruction is the most complete doctrinal list given anywhere so far, and its account of a night's progress echoes the three watches of Thig 5.11's thirty nuns — closing with ‘the mass of darkness shattered’ not once, but twice in a single poem.

Reading guide

An opening confession, repeated exactly

‘Four or five times I left my dwelling; I had failed to find peace of heart, or any control over my mind’ is identical to Thig 2.10's opening — the same confession, now given by a further woman closing this book.

The fullest doctrinal instruction in the collection so far

‘The elements and sense fields, the four noble truths, the faculties and the powers, the awakening factors, and the eightfold path’ combines more named categories in a single teaching than any earlier instruction in this collection — a cumulative instruction, not a single compact phrase.

Three watches, echoing the thirty nuns

‘In the first watch of the night, I recollected my past lives... in the last watch... I shattered the mass of darkness’ tracks the same three-watch structure that marked the group realization in Thig 5.11, here applied to one individual's single night.

A phrase, shattered twice in one poem

‘The mass of darkness’ is shattered once in the third watch of the night, and again in the poem's very last line, ‘on the seventh day I stretched out my feet, having shattered the mass of darkness’ — the only poem in this collection to use the phrase twice within itself.

Key terms

Vijayā — this poem's speaker, whose account closes the Book of the Sixes.
dhātuāyatanāni — “the elements and sense fields” — the first category in this poem's cumulative doctrinal instruction.
cattāri ariyasaccāni — the four noble truths, named as part of the same extended teaching.
bojjhaṅgaṭṭhaṅgika magga — “the awakening factors, and the eightfold path” — the final categories completing this poem's instruction.
tamokkhandha — “the mass of darkness” — shattered twice within this single poem, unique among this collection's poems.

The text

The text in full: six verses, closing the Book of the Sixes. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Four or five times I left my dwelling; I had failed to find peace of heart, or any control over my mind.

§2I approached a nun and politely questioned her. She taught me the Dhamma: the elements and sense fields,

§3the four noble truths, the faculties and the powers, the awakening factors, and the eightfold path for the attainment of the highest goal.

§4After hearing her words, I did her bidding. In the first watch of the night, I recollected my past lives.

§5In the middle watch of the night, I purified my clairvoyance. In the last watch of the night, I shattered the mass of darkness.

§6I then meditated pervading my body with rapture and bliss. On the seventh day I stretched out my feet, having shattered the mass of darkness.
