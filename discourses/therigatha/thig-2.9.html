Therigatha · 2.9

Abhayā

Why this text

This verse follows immediately after Thig 2.8's instruction from a daughter to her mother, and addresses someone named Abhayā directly — the same name identifying the previous poem's speaker. Read together, the two verses suggest a family whose spiritual concerns run in both directions between a mother and her daughter.

Reading guide

A direct address, following a direct instruction

Where Thig 2.8 instructed a mother to examine the body, this verse opens by addressing ‘Abhayā’ on a related theme: ‘the body is fragile, yet ordinary people are attached to it’ — a continuation of the same concern in a new verse.

A resolve stated plainly

‘I'll lay down the body, aware and mindful’ states an intention with unusual directness — not fear of death, but a clear-eyed readiness, held together with awareness rather than denial.

Difficulty acknowledged, not minimized

‘Though subject to so many painful things’ opens the second verse honestly, before crediting ‘love of diligence’ as what carried the speaker through to ‘the ending of craving’ despite that difficulty.

A closing formula repeated exactly in the next poem

This verse's final four lines — ‘though subject to so many painful things... fulfilled the Buddha's instructions’ — recur word for word as the closing verse of Thig 2.10, immediately after this one, shared between two otherwise distinct poems.

Key terms

Abhayā — the figure addressed in this verse, sharing a name with the speaker of Thig 2.8's instruction to her own mother.
kāya — “body” — described here as ‘fragile’, the same object of examination named in Thig 2.8.
appamāda — “diligence” — credited directly, alongside love for it, as what carried the speaker through difficulty to the ending of craving.
taṇhākkhaya — “the ending of craving” — named as the attainment reached, closing this verse's second half.
cariyā — not used here; another of this book's poems with no closing attribution, closing instead on a formula repeated word for word in the very next poem.

The text

The text in full: two verses, with no attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Abhayā, the body is fragile, yet ordinary people are attached to it. I’ll lay down the body, aware and mindful.

§2Though subject to so many painful things, I have, through my love of diligence, reached the ending of craving, and fulfilled the Buddha’s instructions.
