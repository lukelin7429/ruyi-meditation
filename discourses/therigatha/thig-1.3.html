Why this text

This poem repeats Thig 1.2's basic pattern — a name used as its own instruction, paired with a lunar image — but with a telling difference in attribution: where Muttā's verse is credited to the Buddha addressing her, this verse's closing note credits it to ‘the senior nun Puṇṇā’ herself, already carrying the title ‘senior’ that names this whole collection.

Reading guide

Another name turned into instruction

‘Puṇṇā’ means ‘full’ or ‘fulfilled’. As with Muttā, the verse addresses her using her own name as a directive: ‘Puṇṇā, be filled with good qualities’.

A different lunar phase than Thig 1.2

Where Muttā's verse used an eclipse ending, Puṇṇā's uses the moon at its fullest: ‘like the moon on the fifteenth day’ — completion rather than release, though both draw on the same recurring image of the moon.

A specific outcome named directly

The verse's second half states a concrete result of that fullness: ‘when your wisdom is full, shatter the mass of darkness’ — wisdom pictured as something that accumulates until it becomes powerful enough to break through ignorance entirely.

Attributed to the poet herself, already senior

Unlike Thig 1.2, this poem's attribution does not credit the Buddha as speaker; it simply states ‘this verse was recited by the senior nun Puṇṇā’ — the title ‘therī’ this whole collection is named for, already applied to her within its very first book.

Key terms

Puṇṇā — a nun's name, also the Pali word for ‘full’ or ‘fulfilled’ — the verse plays directly on this double meaning, as Thig 1.2 does with Muttā.
therī — “senior nun” — the title this poem's own attribution applies to Puṇṇā, the same word naming this whole collection.
paññā — “wisdom” — named directly as what becomes full in this verse's second half, powerful enough to shatter ignorance.
avijjā — “ignorance” — the ‘mass of darkness’ this verse says full wisdom shatters.
cariyā — not used in this poem; unlike the Cariyapitaka, no closing formula names a ‘perfection’ here — the Therigatha's own closing convention is a simple attribution note instead.

The text

The text in full: one verse, with its closing attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Puṇṇā, be filled with good qualities, like the moon on the fifteenth day. When your wisdom is full, shatter the mass of darkness.

§2That is how this verse was recited by the senior nun Puṇṇā.
