Khuddakapatha · Text 4

The Boy’s Questions

Kumārapañha

Setting
No narrative setting is given in the text itself; commentarial tradition associates it with teaching a young novice
Speaker
Neither questioner nor answerer is named within the text
Form
Ten question-and-answer pairs, numbered one through ten, each pairing ‘What is the [number]?’ with a short doctrinal answer
Length
1–2 minutes to read
Northern parallel
Numbered doctrinal catechisms of this kind are widespread teaching devices across Buddhist traditions; this reading guide does not assert one specific matching text
Difficulty
★★☆☆☆ — the ten answers compress a wide span of core doctrine into single phrases

Why this text

This text is a catechism: ten questions, ‘What is the one?’ through ‘What is the ten?’, each answered with a short numbered doctrinal list — from ‘all sentient beings are sustained by food’ at one, up to the ten factors of a person called ‘perfected’ at ten. Later commentarial tradition holds it was taught by the Buddha to his son and novice, Rāhula, as a way of compressing a wide span of doctrine into a form a beginner could memorize — though the text as it stands here names no speaker or audience.

Reading guide

A numbered catechism

Each of the ten items follows the same pattern: a question naming a number, then a short answer naming that many things. The pattern itself, not any narrative, is what holds the text together.

From food to the noble eightfold path

The ten answers move through some of the tradition's most central teaching lists: beings sustained by food (one), name and form (two), the three feelings (three), the four noble truths (four), the five grasping aggregates (five), the six interior sense fields (six), the seven awakening factors (seven), and the noble eightfold path (eight).

Nine and ten close the list

The ninth answer, the nine abodes of sentient beings, is a less commonly encountered classification than the items before it. The tenth answer names not a list of things but a person: one endowed with ten factors is called ‘perfected’ — the ten factors traditionally understood as the eightfold path completed, plus right knowledge and right liberation.

A teaching device, not a doctrinal exposition

None of the ten answers is explained or expanded within this text; each is stated as a compressed label, assuming the questioner already knows — or is being pointed toward — the fuller teaching each number stands for.

Attributed to Rāhula by later tradition

The commentarial association of this catechism with Rāhula, the Buddha's son and one of the tradition's most celebrated novices, fits its form: a numbered list well suited to memorization by someone new to the teaching, rather than a discourse addressed to an already advanced audience.

Key terms

kumārapañha — “the boy's questions” — this text's own title.
āhāra — “food” or “nutriment” — the answer given to ‘what is the one?’, all sentient beings said to be sustained by it.
nāmarūpa — “name and form” — the answer to ‘what is the two?’, also a key link in the chain of dependent origination.
pañcupādānakkhandhā — “the five grasping aggregates” — the answer to ‘what is the five?’, a core analysis of what a person is made of.
asekha — “beyond training”, i.e. perfected — the tenth answer describes such a person as endowed with ten factors, traditionally the eightfold path plus right knowledge and right liberation.

The text

The text in full: ten question-and-answer pairs, numbered one through ten. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1What is the one? All sentient beings are sustained by food.

§2What is the two? Name and form.

§3What is the three? Three feelings.

§4What is the four? Four noble truths.

§5What is the five? Five grasping aggregates.

§6What is the six? Six interior sense fields.

§7What is the seven? Seven awakening factors.

§8What is the eight? The noble eightfold path.

§9What is the nine? Nine abodes of sentient beings.

§10What is the ten? One endowed with ten factors is called “perfected”.
