Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 4.230

Witless

Duppaññasutta — Duccaritavagga — the twenty-third chapter of the Fours

Setting
None stated; the Duccaritavagga gives no location for this discourse, and it is addressed to the mendicants directly
Speakers
The Buddha alone, addressing the mendicants
Form
A mostly elided line whose closing sentence is written out in full, the same four qualities as AN 4.220
Length
under a minute to read
Northern parallel
This reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number for this discourse
Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — the run’s close, partly restored to full wording

Why this discourse

Faithless, unethical, lazy, witless — AN 4.220’s tetrad, closing this chapter’s own run through the pool of qualities AN 4.202 assembled. Where AN 4.220 closed its chapter in full, unelided form, this discourse takes a middle course: mostly elided, but with its final sentence, the astute person’s complete description, written out in full.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

Faithlessness, lack of ethics, laziness, and witlessness keep a fool broken and blameworthy; faith, ethics, energy, and wisdom keep an astute person intact and praiseworthy, closing this chapter’s reused material.

The pool’s last two terms

Kusīto, lazy, and duppañño, witless, are the same two items AN 4.220 drew from AN 4.202’s sevenfold list to close the previous chapter. Between AN 4.229 and this discourse, this chapter has now drawn on the identical two tetrads AN 4.219 and AN 4.220 drew from that list — the same split of terms, in the same order, repeated across two chapters.

Partial restoration

Unlike the fully elided AN 4.224 through AN 4.229, this discourse’s astute-person half ends with its complete closing sentence spelled out rather than compressed to …pe…. This is a smaller version of what AN 4.220 did for the whole discourse at the end of the previous chapter: a signal, short of full restoration, that a run of compression is coming to its close.

What this chapter’s main run has now shown

From AN 4.222 through this discourse, nine consecutive discourses have repeated, content for content, the nine tetrads this series first read at AN 4.212 through AN 4.220, replacing that chapter’s hell/heaven formula with the khata formula borrowed from AN 4.3. Together with AN 4.221’s own borrowing from AN 4.148–149, this chapter has, across its first ten discourses, composed almost nothing that was not already written down somewhere else in the collection — and has done so, evidently, deliberately, using the collection’s existing material as building blocks for a structurally new chapter.

Key terms

kusīta / duppañña — “lazy” and “witless” — the same two terms AN 4.220 drew from AN 4.202’s sevenfold list to close the previous chapter.
khataṁ upahataṁ attānaṁ pariharati — this chapter’s formula, here reaching the end of its main run.
paṇḍito viyatto sappuriso — “an astute, competent true person” — the character-type whose description is restored to full wording at the close of this discourse.
āraddhavīriya / paññavā — “energetic” and “wise” — the positive counterparts closing this discourse and this chapter’s reused material.
peyyāla — “repetition-series” — here only partially applied, with the closing sentence restored to full wording.

The text

The discourse mostly elided, its closing sentence restored to full wording. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

The discourse

§1“A foolish person … brims with much wickedness. … They’re faithless, unethical, lazy, and witless. … An astute person … brims with much merit. … They’re faithful, ethical, energetic, and wise. When an astute, competent true person possesses these four qualities they keep themselves healthy and whole. They don’t deserve to be blamed and criticized by sensible people, and they create much merit.”
