Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 4.247

Worthy of a Monument

Thūpārahasutta — Āpattibhayavagga — the twenty-fifth chapter of the Fours

Setting
None stated; the Āpattibhayavagga gives no location for this discourse, and it is addressed to the mendicants directly
Speakers
The Buddha alone, addressing the mendicants
Form
A list of four, stated once with no elaboration
Length
under a minute to read
Northern parallel
Lists of stūpa-worthy figures appear elsewhere in the Nikāyas and in later commentarial literature; this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★★☆☆☆ — brief, but its fourth item is worth pausing on

Why this discourse

Four figures the discourse says are worthy of a memorial monument, a thūpa: a fully awakened Buddha, an Independent Buddha, a disciple who has awakened by following a Buddha's teaching, and a wheel-turning monarch. The first three share the same basic achievement in different degrees; the fourth shares none of it, and its presence on the list is the discourse's most interesting feature.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

A monument is fitting for a Buddha, an Independent Buddha, an awakened disciple, or a wheel-turning monarch.

Three awakened figures, one scale

Sammāsambuddha, a fully awakened Buddha who discovers the path and teaches it; paccekabuddha, an Independent Buddha who awakens without a teacher but does not found a teaching for others; and sāvaka, a disciple who awakens by following a Buddha's teaching. These three sit on a single scale of the same achievement, distinguished by whether the path was discovered or received, and whether it was then taught onward.

A fourth figure who shares none of that

The cakkavatti, the wheel-turning monarch, is not awakened in any sense the other three share. This is a figure from the tradition’s political imagination: an ideal ruler whose reign is just, whose authority extends without conquest by force, and who governs by principle rather than by the sword. Placed fourth on this list, a purely secular figure of merit is given the same physical honor, a monument, as three figures whose accomplishment is liberation itself.

Why the pairing is worth taking seriously rather than explaining away

It would be tempting to read the wheel-turning monarch’s inclusion as symbolic or honorary, a courtesy extended to political power rather than a genuine parallel. The discourse gives no such qualification. It states the four together, without ranking, and lets the list stand as it is: the tradition recognized more than one kind of achievement as worthy of permanent public memory, and one of those kinds was purely this-worldly, purely a matter of how a person exercised power over others.

What a monument is for

A thūpa is a physical structure, typically enshrining relics, built to be visited, circumambulated, and remembered by. Naming who deserves one is, in effect, naming what a community should organize its collective memory and devotion around — and this discourse’s answer includes both the end of suffering and the just exercise of worldly power as things worth building a monument to.

Key terms

thūpa — “monument, stūpa” — a structure typically enshrining relics, built for visitation and remembrance.
sammāsambuddha — “a fully awakened Buddha” — one who discovers the path independently and teaches it to others.
paccekabuddha — “an Independent Buddha” — one who awakens without a teacher but does not found a teaching for others to follow.
sāvaka — “disciple” — here, one who has reached awakening by following a Buddha's teaching.
cakkavatti — “wheel-turning monarch” — an ideal just ruler, a purely secular figure given equal standing on this list with three fully awakened ones.

The text

The discourse in full: the four worthy of a monument. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Four worthy of a monument

§1“Mendicants, these four are worthy of a monument. What four? A Realized One, a perfected one, a fully awakened Buddha; an Independent Buddha; a disciple of a Realized One; and a wheel-turning monarch. These four are worthy of a monument.”
