Why this discourse

A framework for evaluating any claim to enlightenment, one's own included: five possible causes behind such a declaration — stupidity, corrupt wishes, madness, overestimation, or, in the fifth and final case, the genuine article — distinguishing sincere error from deliberate deception and both from actual attainment.

Reading guide

Declaring one's own attainment

Aññābyākaraṇa, declaring enlightenment, names the act of claiming final knowledge for oneself. This discourse lists five possible circumstances behind such a claim, only the last of which the discourse treats as genuine.

Four false declarations

Stupidity and folly (simply not understanding what is being claimed); corrupt wishes, a wishful or ambitious temperament (deliberately claiming falsely out of desire for status or gain); and madness or mental disorder (an involuntary, pathological cause) — three distinct roots for a false claim, none of them identical to the others.

Overestimation, a category apart

The fourth cause, adhimāna, names something different in kind from the first three: a sincere but mistaken self-assessment, someone who has genuinely misjudged their own progress rather than lied, been deranged, or simply failed to understand the claim. This is a specific, recognized technical category in its own right, distinct from deliberate deception.

The fifth, true declaration

The genuine case is given no elaboration at all beyond a single word, sammadeva, ‘rightly’ — in sharp contrast to the four false types, each of which is named with a specific cause.

A practical framework

Read plainly, this list offers a way to evaluate any claim to enlightenment one encounters — including one's own — by distinguishing the possible causes behind it, rather than simply accepting or dismissing the claim outright.

What comes next

AN 5.94 turns to something calmer: five ways of living comfortably, culminating in the four absorptions and full liberation.

Key terms

aññābyākaraṇa — “declaring enlightenment” — this discourse's central act, evaluated across five possible causes.
pāpiccha — “corrupt wishes”, a wishful or ambitious temperament — the second false cause.
ummāda / cittakkhepa — “madness”, mental derangement — the third false cause.
adhimāna — “overestimation” — a sincere but mistaken self-assessment, distinct in kind from deliberate deception or pathology.
sammadeva — “rightly, correctly” — the fifth and only genuine case, given no further elaboration.

The text

The discourse in full: five possible causes behind a claim to enlightenment. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Five ways of declaring enlightenment

§1“Mendicants, there are five ways of declaring enlightenment. What five? One declares enlightenment out of stupidity and folly. Or because of corrupt wishes, being of wishful temperament. Or because of madness and mental disorder. Or out of overestimation. Or one declares enlightenment rightly. These are the five ways of declaring enlightenment.”
