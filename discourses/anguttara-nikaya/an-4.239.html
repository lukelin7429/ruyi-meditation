Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 4.239

Blameworthy

Sāvajjasutta — Kammavagga — the twenty-fourth chapter of the Fours

Setting
None stated; the Kammavagga gives no location for this discourse, and it is addressed to the mendicants directly
Speakers
The Buddha alone, addressing the mendicants
Form
Two parallel statements, placement in hell or heaven, on a tetrad of blameworthy or blameless conduct and view
Length
under a minute to read
Northern parallel
This reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number for this discourse
Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — a brief return to a familiar formula, with new vocabulary

Why this discourse

After eight discourses on the four kinds of deeds, this chapter briefly returns to the yathābhataṁ nikkhitto formula first met at AN 4.212 — placement in hell or heaven as if delivered there — filling its familiar three-conduct-plus-view tetrad with a new pair of adjectives: blameworthy and blameless.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

Blameworthy conduct in body, speech, and mind, plus blameworthy view, delivers a person to hell as if placed there; the blameless counterparts deliver them to heaven.

A familiar template, a new pair of terms

Sāvajjena kāyakammena, sāvajjāya vacīkammena, sāvajjena manokammena, sāvajjāya diṭṭhiyā — blameworthy deeds by body, speech, and mind, and blameworthy view. This is the same three-conduct-plus-view tetrad first read at AN 4.212 and repeated with substitutions across AN 4.213, AN 4.222, and AN 4.223, now filled with yet another pair of qualifying adjectives: sāvajja and anavajja, blameworthy and blameless, rather than the earlier discourses’ plain duccarita/sucarita, bad/good.

Why blame, specifically

Sāvajja was already met in this series at AN 4.222, where the fool sāvajjo ca hoti sānuvajjo viññūnaṁ, deserves blame and criticism from sensible people, under the khata formula. This discourse takes that same quality of blameworthiness and, rather than treating it as a consequence a person incurs, makes it a description of the conduct itself — deeds and a view that are blameworthy in their own character, not merely deeds that later earn blame.

A short discourse that ties two formulas together

Read alongside AN 4.222, this discourse shows the same vocabulary, sāvajja, doing work in both this chapter’s hell/heaven formula and the previous chapter’s khata formula — further evidence, consistent with what this series observed at AN 4.227, that specific terms move freely between the different consequence-formulas this collection uses, while the formulas themselves stay distinct.

Key terms

sāvajja / anavajja — “blameworthy” and “blameless” — the pair of qualifying terms filling this discourse’s tetrad, already met describing a consequence at AN 4.222.
yathābhataṁ nikkhitto — “placed as if delivered there” — the formula from AN 4.212, briefly returning after this chapter’s run of deed-focused discourses.
kāyakamma, vacīkamma, manokamma — deeds by body, speech, and mind — the same threefold division of conduct used throughout this series, here termed kamma rather than carita.
diṭṭhi — “view” — the fourth, closing item of the tetrad, as at AN 4.212.
sānuvajja — “deserving criticism” — the related term from AN 4.222’s khata formula, describing consequence rather than character.

The text

The discourse in full: hell for the four, heaven for their opposites. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Placed in hell

§1“Mendicants, someone with four qualities is placed in hell as if delivered there. What four? Blameworthy deeds by way of body, speech, and mind, and blameworthy view. Someone with these four qualities is placed in hell as if delivered there.

Placed in heaven

§2Someone with four qualities is placed in heaven as if delivered there. What four? Blameless deeds by way of body, speech, and mind, and blameless view. Someone with these four qualities is placed in heaven as if delivered there.”
