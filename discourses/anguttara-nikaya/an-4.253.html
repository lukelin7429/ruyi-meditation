Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 4.253

Expressions (4th)

Catutthavohārasutta — Āpattibhayavagga — the twenty-fifth chapter of the Fours

Setting

Speakers

Form

Why this discourse

Truthfully saying one has seen, heard, thought, or known something one actually has — the fourth and final combination, completing this chapter's atomized treatment of the diṭṭha-suta-muta-viññāta vocabulary. With this discourse, the Āpattibhayavagga closes, and the source colophon marks something larger closing alongside it: the entire fifth paṇṇāsaka of the Fours, five chapters and fifty-three discourses that began at AN 4.201.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

Truthfully saying one has seen, heard, thought, or known something one actually has is one of the four noble ways of speaking.

The set, completed

Diṭṭhe diṭṭhavāditā, sute sutavāditā, mute mutavāditā, viññāte viññātavāditā — honest claim, the positive counterpart already read paired with false denial at AN 4.218 and AN 4.228. With this discourse, all four logical combinations of experience and claim — true claim, true denial, false claim, false denial — have now each received an independent discourse in this chapter: AN 4.250, 251, 252, and this one.

A vocabulary's history, now complete across this series

It is worth pausing at this close to survey the full path this vocabulary has taken. Introduced whole at AN 4.24, to describe a Realized One’s unaffectedness. Given as a mirrored pair under the hell/heaven formula at AN 4.217–218. Given as a mirrored pair again under the khata formula at AN 4.227–228. And now given as four fully independent statements under a noble/ignoble frame, AN 4.250–253. Four terms, unchanged in meaning across nine discourses and four chapters, organized three structurally different ways without ever needing to be redefined.

The chapter closes, and so does its paṇṇāsaka

The source colophon marks two closures at once here: Āpattibhayavaggo pañcamo, the Āpattibhayavagga, the fifth [vagga of its group]; and immediately after, Pañcamo paṇṇāsako samatto, the fifth paṇṇāsaka is complete. This is a larger milestone than any chapter-close this series has yet marked: not merely the end of a ten- or eleven-discourse chapter, but the end of the entire five-chapter group — Sappurisavagga, Parisāvagga, Duccaritavagga, Kammavagga, and Āpattibhayavagga — that opened at AN 4.201 and now closes here at AN 4.253, fifty-three discourses in total.

What comes next

The chapters that follow, beginning with the Abhiññāvagga at AN 4.254, belong to whatever structure organizes the material beyond this paṇṇāsaka’s close. This reading guide has not yet verified how the traditional numbering treats that material and will establish it directly when that chapter is reached, rather than asserting a structure here in advance.

Key terms

diṭṭhe diṭṭhavāditā — “truthfully saying one has seen what one has seen” — the fourth and final combination, completing this chapter's set.
ariyavohāra — “noble expression” — this discourse's frame, matching AN 4.251's for honest denial.
Āpattibhayavaggo pañcamo — “the Āpattibhayavagga, the fifth” — the source colophon's label, marking this as the fifth and final vagga of its paṇṇāsaka.
pañcamo paṇṇāsako samatto — “the fifth paṇṇāsaka is complete” — the colophon marking the close of the entire five-chapter group beginning at AN 4.201.
diṭṭha suta muta viññāta — the fourfold division of experience, unchanged across all nine discourses and four chapters this vocabulary has appeared in across this series.

The text

The discourse in full: the fourth and final expression, completing the set. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

A noble expression

§1“Mendicants, there are these four noble expressions. What four? Saying you’ve seen, heard, thought, or known something, and you have. These are the four noble expressions.”
