Why this discourse

Four qualities, and a claim about what having them amounts to: the practice is apaṇṇaka, a sure bet, and the groundwork for ending the defilements has been laid. The word is a gambling term, and it is the title of the chapter this discourse opens. What makes a practice a sure thing turns out to be four unremarkable qualities in combination.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

Ethical, learned, energetic, and wise — four qualities that between them make the outcome certain.

Apaṇṇaka

The word is from the vocabulary of dice and wagering: a throw that cannot lose, a bet with no losing side. Sujato’s ‘sure bet’ keeps the gambling sense, which older translations tended to lose with ‘unfailing’ or ‘incontrovertible’.

The register matters. AN 4.3 used a dice image for the person who praises and criticizes carelessly — you choose a losing hand with your own mouth — and this is the same field of metaphor used positively. A practice with these four is a throw that cannot come out badly.

It is worth being precise about what is claimed. Not that awakening is guaranteed on a timetable, but that the groundwork has been laid — yoni cassa āraddhā hoti āsavānaṁ khayāya, the ground or womb of it has been begun. What is certain is that the conditions are now in place.

The four

Ethical (sīlavā), learned (bahussuto), energetic (āraddhavīriyo), and wise (paññavā). Four words, no definitions.

The set is worth comparing with the four of AN 4.1 — ethics, immersion, wisdom, freedom. Two overlap. What has been dropped is immersion and freedom; what has been added is learning and energy.

That substitution has a logic. AN 4.1 named the components of the goal; this names the components of the effort. Immersion and freedom are what one arrives at; learning and energy are what one brings. Ethics and wisdom appear on both lists because they function in both roles.

Why energy is on the list

Āraddhavīriya, of aroused energy, is the one item that no amount of the others supplies. A person can be ethical, learned, and wise and simply not do the work; the collection says so repeatedly, most sharply in AN 4.6, where much learning without practice is ranked below little learning with it.

Its presence here is what makes the claim a claim. A list of three could describe a condition; adding energy makes it describe a person who is moving. That is why the outcome can be called certain: what remains is only continuation.

Why the discourse is four lines long

This is the shortest kind of text in the collection and the chapter opens with two of them — this and AN 4.72, which has the identical frame with a different four inside.

The pair works as a matched set for recitation: same opening sentence, same closing sentence, four words swapped in the middle. A reciter holds one sentence and two lists.

Read on a page they look insubstantial. What they are is the chapter’s title stated twice with two different contents, establishing the frame that AN 4.73 through AN 4.80 will fill with much longer material.

Using it

The four make a usable checklist, and the useful question is which one is missing. Most practitioners have a stable answer, and it is rarely ethics.

The discourse’s claim is conditional on all four, which is the point of a list rather than a hierarchy. Three of four does not produce a sure bet; it produces a person who has most of what is needed, which is a different and much commoner situation.

Key terms

apaṇṇaka — “a sure bet” — from the vocabulary of dice: a throw that cannot lose. The chapter takes its name from this word.
yoni — “groundwork”, literally womb or origin — what has been begun for the ending of defilements; the conditions are in place.
āraddhavīriya — “energetic”, of aroused energy — the item no amount of the others supplies.
bahussuta — “learned” — carried over from AN 4.6, where its limits were set out.
āsavānaṁ khaya — “the ending of defilements” — what the groundwork is for, and the collection’s standard term for the goal.

The text

The discourse in full. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Four things

§1“Mendicants, when a mendicant has four things their practice is a sure bet, and they have laid the groundwork for ending the defilements. What four? It’s when a mendicant is ethical, learned, energetic, and wise. When a mendicant possesses these four things their practice is a sure bet, and they have laid the groundwork for ending the defilements.”
