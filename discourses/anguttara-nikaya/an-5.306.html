Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 5.306

Untitled Discourse on Greed (4th)

No Pali title given in the source, marked only by a tilde — Rāgapeyyāla, the twenty-ninth and final chapter of the Fives

Why this discourse

A formula that sounds familiar but genuinely is not. This discourse's development list is faith, energy, mindfulness, immersion, and wisdom — the more widely known version of the five faculties. It is easy to mistake this for the sekhabala that opened this book at AN 5.1, but that formula, and every recurrence of it since, substitutes conscience and prudence for mindfulness and immersion. This is a different list, appearing under this exact wording for the first time in this project.

Reading guide

A name that invites confusion

This discourse's five items — saddhindriya, vīriyindriya, satindriya, samādhindriya, paññindriya — are the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, immersion, and wisdom, the version of this formula most commonly cited across Buddhist traditions generally.

Not the book's own recurring variant

This book's own formula, first stated at AN 5.1 as the sekhabala and repeated as the tathāgatabala at AN 5.11 and again at AN 5.204, substitutes conscience (hiri) and prudence (ottappa) for mindfulness and immersion. AN 5.204 itself already flagged this as ‘a different formula from the more famous five bala’ — a difference this discourse's own wording now confirms directly, since it uses the terms AN 5.204 said this book does not use.

Genuinely new material, this late in the book

This exact five-item combination — with mindfulness and immersion rather than conscience and prudence — does not appear anywhere earlier in this project's coverage of the Fives. Its first full appearance, under this precise wording, is here, four discourses before the book's very last page.

Faculties, and powers to follow

This discourse names the five as indriya, faculties; AN 5.307, immediately next, restates the identical five under the bala, powers, framing — the same faculty/power distinction this book has drawn before, now applied to this different underlying list.

What follows

AN 5.307 supplies this chapter's fifth and final individually stated development list: the same five qualities once more, named this time as powers.

Key terms

saddhindriya, vīriyindriya, satindriya, samādhindriya, paññindriya — the faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, immersion, and wisdom — the widely known version of this formula, distinct from this book's own recurring variant.
sekhabala — “powers of a trainee”, this book's own formula from AN 5.1: faith, conscience, prudence, energy, wisdom — substituting conscience and prudence for this discourse's mindfulness and immersion.
sati, samādhi — mindfulness and immersion — the two items that distinguish this discourse's list from the sekhabala/tathāgatabala formula used elsewhere in this book.
hiri, ottappa — conscience and prudence — the two items this book's own recurring formula uses in place of this discourse's mindfulness and immersion.
rāgassa abhiññāya — “for insight into greed” — the identical frame shared by all five opening discourses of this chapter.

The text

The discourse in full: the five faculties, reframed as what should be developed for insight into greed. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Five things to develop, for insight into greed

§1“For insight into greed, five things should be developed. What five? The faculties of faith, energy, mindfulness, immersion, and wisdom. For insight into greed, these five things should be developed.”
