Why this discourse

The exact same five-precept structure as AN 5.171, restated with one addition: the lay follower in question is explicitly described as ‘living at home’ (agāraṁ ajjhāvasati) — grounding the previous discourse's abstract timidity/assurance in ordinary household life.

Reading guide

An identical structure, one phrase added

The same five precepts, the same negative and positive framing as AN 5.171 — but now every clause specifies the lay follower as ‘living at home’, making explicit what was only implicit before: this is about household life specifically, not some other kind of lay practice.

An abbreviated middle

The source text itself marks the middle three precepts (stealing, sexual misconduct, lying) with an ellipsis (‘…pe…’), spelling out only the first and last in full — a signal that the compilers treated this as a close restatement of AN 5.171, not an independently composed discourse.

Not self-assured, versus self-assured at home

The negative framing shifts slightly from AN 5.171's ‘overcome by timidity’ to the more directly stated ‘not self-assured’ (avisārado) — a small wording change around an unchanged underlying concern.

Why repeat the same content twice

Read alongside AN 5.171 and the discourse that follows, this repetition isn't redundancy for its own sake: three near-identical statements of the same five-precept foundation, each with a different outcome-framing (timidity/assurance, then non-assurance/assurance-at-home, then hell/heaven at AN 5.173), together give this chapter's opening a cumulative, almost liturgical weight.

What follows

AN 5.173 completes this opening triad with its starkest framing yet: not timidity or assurance, but rebirth itself, in hell or heaven.

Key terms

agāraṁ ajjhāvasati — “living at home” — the phrase this discourse adds, specifying household life explicitly.
avisārado — “not self-assured” — this discourse's negative framing, a variant of AN 5.171's ‘overcome by timidity’.
pe — the standard abbreviation marker, here compressing the middle three precepts into an ellipsis.
pañcasīla — “the five precepts” — unchanged from AN 5.171, the shared content of this opening triad.
upāsako — “lay follower” — the subject of every discourse in this chapter.

The text

The discourse in full: the same five precepts, now framed for household life explicitly. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Not self-assured, living at home

§1“A lay follower living at home with five qualities is not self-assured. What five? They kill living creatures, steal, commit sexual misconduct, lie, and consume beer, wine, and liquor intoxicants. A lay follower living at home with these five qualities is not self-assured.

Self-assured, living at home

§2A lay follower living at home with these five qualities is self-assured. What five? They don’t kill living creatures, steal, commit sexual misconduct, lie, or consume beer, wine, and liquor intoxicants. A lay follower living at home with these five qualities is self-assured.”
