Aṅguttara Nikāya

Selected Numbered Discourses of the Buddha

The Aṅguttara Nikāya organizes the Buddha's teaching entirely by number — one chapter (nipāta) for teachings framed around a single item, one for pairs, one for triads, and on up through elevens. Depending on how you count its many short, formulaic entries, it runs anywhere from roughly 2,300 to well over 8,000 individual suttas — by far the largest of the four main Nikāyas, and the least practical to work through one full study page at a time.

This collection began as a curated selection of eighteen widely quoted discourses drawn from the Threes through the Elevens. It is now being extended toward complete coverage, working in order from the Ones forward. Each entry, old or new, has the same reading guide, full Sujato translation, and self-check quiz as our other series.

A note on scope and on how to read this page: the Ones and Twos consist largely of very short one-line formulas, which are gathered into chapters (vagga) of roughly ten and published here one chapter to a page — so a row like AN 1.1–10 is a complete chapter, not a single discourse. From the Threes onward each discourse gets its own page. Rows below the Ones that are not yet linked are listed for context and are being added in order.
