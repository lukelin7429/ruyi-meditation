Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 3.147

Blameworthy

Sāvajjasutta — Maṅgalavagga — the fifteenth chapter of the Threes

Setting
None stated; the discourse continues from AN 3.1, whose setting at Sāvatthī is understood to hold
Speakers
The Buddha alone, addressing the mendicants
Form
A matched pair: blameworthy deeds place someone in hell, blameless deeds place someone in heaven, each defined across body, speech, and mind
Length
≈30 seconds to read
Northern parallel
The threefold classification of deeds by body, speech, and mind as the basis for a good or bad rebirth is foundational across the Chinese Āgamas; this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — the formula this whole short set of four discourses shares, here in its second of four appearances

Why this discourse

Someone with three qualities is placed in hell “as if delivered there” — and someone with the opposite three is placed in heaven the same way. Here the three qualities are blameworthy deeds and blameless deeds, by body, speech, and mind. Where AN 3.146 names conduct by its inner quality, unskillful, this discourse names the identical structure by the censure it deserves — a shift from what an act is to how it should be judged.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

Blameworthy deeds of body, speech, and mind place a person in hell; Blameless deeds of the same three place a person in heaven, both “as if delivered there.”

"As if delivered there"

Yathābhataṁ nikkhitto, the discourse’s recurring phrase, pictures a person set down in hell or heaven the way a parcel is carried and set down exactly where it was addressed — not sent there by another’s judgment, but arriving as the direct, mechanical consequence of what was already carried. The image removes any sense of an external verdict being handed down; the destination is simply where the deeds were always going.

Body, speech, mind: the exhaustive three

Kāyakamma, vacīkamma, manokamma — deeds of body, speech, and mind — are the collection’s standard exhaustive division of everything a person does. Nothing falls outside the three, which is why this discourse, like several others in this vagga, needs no fourth category.

What "blameworthy" names here

“blameworthy,” sāvajja — literally “with fault,” naming conduct by the censure it deserves rather than by its inner quality, a social register distinct from “unskillful”'s more internal sense.

One of a short set of four

AN 3.146 through 3.149 share this identical formula and differ only in which adjective fills the slot: unskillful, blameworthy, uneven, and impure, each paired with its opposite. Reading all four in a row makes the formula’s stability visible — it is built to hold any one of several near-synonymous descriptions of the same underlying judgment.

Answered again, differently, just after

AN 3.151 restates this exact adjective pair using a different image entirely — not a cosmic destination but a person keeping themselves “broken and damaged” or “intact and unscathed,” blamed or praised by sensible observers in this very life. The two discourses describe the same moral fact from two different vantages: one from the far side of death, one from the view of anyone watching right now.

Key terms

yathābhataṁ nikkhitto — “placed there as if delivered” — the recurring image of a parcel set down exactly where it was addressed, not sent by external verdict.
kāyakamma, vacīkamma, manokamma — “deeds of body, speech, mind” — the collection’s standard exhaustive division of everything a person does.
blameworthy — “blameworthy,” sāvajja — literally “with fault,” naming conduct by the censure it deserves rather than by its inner quality, a social register distinct from “unskillful”'s more internal sense.
blameless — the opposite quality, placing someone in heaven by the same mechanism.
niraya, sagga — “hell, heaven” — the two destinations, named without further cosmological description in this discourse.

The text

The discourse in full: blameworthy deeds and hell, blameless deeds and heaven. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Blameworthy deeds, and hell

§1“Someone with three qualities is placed in hell as if delivered there. What three? Blameworthy deeds by way of body, speech, and mind. Someone with these three qualities is placed in hell as if delivered there.

Blameless deeds, and heaven

§2Someone with three qualities is placed in heaven as if delivered there. What three? Blameless deeds by way of body, speech, and mind. Someone with these three qualities is placed in heaven as if delivered there.”
