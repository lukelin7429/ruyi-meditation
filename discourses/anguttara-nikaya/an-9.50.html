Extinguishment in a Certain Respect

Tadaṅganibbānasutta — Sāmaññavagga — the fifth chapter of the Nines

Why this discourse

The source reduces this discourse, like AN 9.48 and AN 9.49 before it, to a single line — ‘they speak of “extinguishment in a certain respect”’ — followed by an ellipsis and the marker ‘ninth,’ applying the chapter's shared template to a term that elsewhere names a genuine technical category: the temporary suppression of one specific defilement by its direct opposite, short of complete and final extinguishment.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

By the pattern established throughout this chapter, reaching any of the nine progressive attainments is what the Buddha meant by ‘extinguishment in a certain respect’ — qualifiedly at each of the first eight stages, definitively only at the ninth — though the source, as at AN 9.48 and AN 9.49, no longer spells this out.

A genuine technical category, named briefly

‘Tadaṅga-nibbāna,’ literally ‘extinguishment by that factor,’ names a real doctrinal distinction found elsewhere in the tradition: the temporary suppression of one specific defilement by cultivating its direct opposite — hate suppressed by love, for instance — a genuine but partial and reversible achievement, distinct from full, final extinguishment.

The third and last of this run's fully elided stubs

This is the third and final discourse in this chapter reduced to a single surviving line, closing out the run begun at AN 9.48. Where those two discourses named bare extinguishment and full extinguishment respectively, this one names the most technically specific of the three — and receives exactly the same minimal treatment regardless.

What comes next restores the full picture

AN 9.51, immediately following, breaks this pattern of minimal treatment: it is given in full again, one final time, and closes not only this chapter but the entire First Fifty of the Book of the Nines — the run of compression this discourse belongs to giving way, at the chapter's very close, to full and deliberate elaboration once more.

Key terms

tadaṅganibbānaṁ — “extinguishment in a certain respect,” literally “extinguishment by that factor” — a genuine technical category elsewhere naming temporary suppression of one defilement by its direct opposite.
…pe… — the Pāli peyyāla marker, left untranslated as an ellipsis — the source's own signal to supply the omitted content.
navamaṁ — “ninth” — the bare ordinal closing this discourse, marking its place among the chapter's ten discourses (not to be confused with the ninth meditative attainment).
pariyāyena — “in a qualified sense” — not present in this discourse's own text, supplied by inference from AN 9.42 through AN 9.47's shared pattern.
nippariyāyena — “in a definitive sense” — likewise supplied by inference, reserved for the ninth attainment throughout this chapter.

The text

The discourse exactly as it survives in the source: one line, an ellipsis, and an ordinal number. Nothing has been added. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).
