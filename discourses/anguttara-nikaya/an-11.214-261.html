Why this discourse

The same eleven-factor cowherd simile and eight contemplations apply to the six kinds of feeling that arise specifically from contact — feeling born of eye contact, ear contact, nose contact, tongue contact, body contact, and mind contact — forty-eight further discourses (AN 11.214–261).

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

The identical cowherd simile and eight contemplations now apply to the six kinds of feeling born of contact — through the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind — the fifth of this chapter's ten categories.

Why feeling matters more than the others

In the dependent origination chain this sequence echoes, feeling is the specific link where craving takes hold or does not: contact conditions feeling, and feeling conditions craving, making the contemplations applied here — especially observing feeling as impermanent — a point of real leverage, not simply one more category in a mechanical series.

Named for its cause, not itself

Each of the six feelings in this page's list is named for the contact that gives rise to it — ‘feeling born of eye contact’, not simply ‘eye feeling’ — making explicit the causal link between the fourth category (contact) and this fifth one.

The same forty-eight, at the sequence's midpoint

Six kinds of feeling times eight contemplations gives forty-eight discourses, matching AN 11.214 through AN 11.261 exactly — the fifth of ten categories, marking roughly the midpoint of this chapter's full ten-category sequence.

Key terms

cakkhusamphassajā vedanāya, sotasamphassajā vedanāya — “feeling born of eye contact”, “feeling born of ear contact” — the first two of the six feelings, each explicitly named for its causal source.
vedanā — “feeling” — the fifth step in this chapter's sequence, and the pivotal link in dependent origination where craving may or may not take hold.
manosamphassajā vedanāya — “feeling born of mind contact” — the sixth and final feeling, completing the list.
taṇhā — not named directly here, but “craving”, the link feeling conditions in the dependent origination chain this category echoes.
aniccānupassī...paṭinissaggānupassī — the same eight contemplations from AN 11.22–29, again left entirely implicit.

The text

The compressed text in full: the same pattern, now applied to the six kinds of feeling born of contact. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).
