Why this discourse

The same eleven-factor cowherd simile and eight contemplations apply to the six kinds of craving for each sense object — sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and ideas — forty-eight further discourses (AN 11.358–405).

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

The identical cowherd simile and eight contemplations now apply to the six kinds of craving — for sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and ideas — the eighth of this chapter's ten categories.

The link this sequence has been building toward

AN 11.214–261's guide flagged feeling as the point where craving takes hold or does not. This page names that very consequence directly: craving (taṇhā), the link dependent origination identifies as arising from feeling and, left unchecked, driving the entire cycle of further becoming and rebirth.

Three kinds of craving, though compressed here

Elsewhere in the canon craving is analyzed into three kinds — for sensual pleasure, for continued existence, and for non-existence — a finer distinction this compressed page does not draw out, treating craving here simply by its six sense-object categories rather than by its underlying motivations.

The same forty-eight, two categories from the close

Six kinds of craving times eight contemplations gives forty-eight discourses, matching AN 11.358 through AN 11.405 exactly — the eighth of ten categories, with only thought and consideration remaining before this chapter's massive mirror page closes the whole structure.

Key terms

rūpataṇhāya, saddataṇhāya, gandhataṇhāya, rasataṇhāya, phoṭṭhabbataṇhāya, dhammataṇhāya — “craving for sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and ideas” — the six kinds of craving, named for their sense objects.
taṇhā — “craving” — the eighth step in this chapter's sequence, and the link dependent origination identifies as arising directly from feeling.
rūpataṇhā — “craving for sights” — the first of the six, opening this page's list.
kāmataṇhā, bhavataṇhā, vibhavataṇhā — “craving for sensual pleasure, for continued existence, for non-existence” — the canon's own finer threefold analysis of craving, not drawn out in this compressed page's six-object treatment.
aniccānupassī...paṭinissaggānupassī — the same eight contemplations from AN 11.22–29, again left entirely implicit.
