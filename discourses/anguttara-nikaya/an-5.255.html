Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 5.255

Giving Up Stinginess

Macchariyappahānasutta — Upasampadāvagga — the twenty-sixth chapter of the Fives

Why this discourse

The same five stinginess domains from AN 5.254, restated once more, but under a different and stronger frame: not simply a list, and not ranked, but named directly as what the entire holy life (brahmacariya) is lived in order to abandon.

Reading guide

The same list, a stronger claim

Dwellings, families, material things, praise, and the teachings — the identical five domains from AN 5.254. What changes here is not the list but the claim made about it: this discourse states that the spiritual life itself is lived (vussati) specifically to give up and cut out (pahānāya samucchedāya) these five.

A purpose statement, not a ranking

AN 5.254 closed by ranking the five; this discourse instead makes a purpose claim, repeating the identical ‘given up and cut out’ formula across all five domains individually before restating it once more for the set as a whole.

Two verbs, both strong

Pahāna (giving up) and samuccheda (cutting out, uprooting) are paired throughout, not left as a single softer verb. The discourse is not describing gradual reduction; it names complete removal as the standard.

Framing the whole holy life around five specific faults

Naming five particular faults as the very purpose of an entire spiritual life is a strong claim. It sits alongside other places in this collection where a single narrow concern is treated as encapsulating the whole of practice, rather than diminishing the discourse's ambition, this framing raises the stakes of the ordinary-sounding fault of stinginess.

What follows

AN 5.256 turns the same five domains into a direct attainment-blocking formula: stinginess not yet given up prevents entering the first absorption.

Key terms

brahmacariya — “the holy life” — the entire spiritual path, here said to be lived specifically to abandon these five stinginess domains.
pahānāya samucchedāya — “to give up and cut out” — the paired verb formula naming complete removal, not gradual reduction.
vussati — “is lived” — the verb naming the holy life itself as the vehicle for this abandonment.
macchariya — “stinginess” — the identical five-domain formula carried over unchanged from AN 5.254.
samuccheda — “cutting out” or “uprooting” — a stronger image than simple relinquishment, implying removal at the root.

The text

The discourse in full: the same five domains, reframed as the holy life's own purpose. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

The purpose of the holy life

§1“Mendicants, the spiritual life is lived to give up and cut out these five kinds of stinginess. What five? Stinginess with dwellings, families, material things, praise, and the teachings. The spiritual life is lived to give up and cut out these five kinds of stinginess.”
