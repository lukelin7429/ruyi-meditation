Why this discourse

The five-component path pentad already named at AN 5.92 — ethics, immersion, wisdom, freedom, and the knowledge and vision of freedom — reappears under an entirely different heading: the qualities that make a mendicant worthy of offerings and the supreme field of merit for the world.

Reading guide

A third framing for a familiar pentad

Sīla, samādhi, paññā, vimutti, vimuttiñāṇadassana, already named at AN 5.92 as five ‘accomplishments’, reappears here under a new heading: the basis for a mendicant's worthiness of offerings.

The 'worthy of offerings' formula, in full

Āhuneyya (worthy of offerings dedicated to the gods), pāhuneyya (worthy of hospitality), dakkhiṇeyya (worthy of a religious donation), añjalikaraṇīya (worthy of veneration with cupped palms), and anuttaraṁ puññakkhettaṁ lokassa (the supreme field of merit for the world) — a five-part stock formula found widely across the canon describing full worthiness of veneration and support.

Why this pentad earns that formula

The progression from ethics through liberation and its own reflective knowledge represents, in this collection's own account, the complete path. Naming it as the basis for ‘worthiness of offerings’ makes explicit what completing the path is understood to earn in terms of communal respect and support.

Contrast with AN 5.92

Same five items, different application: there, named simply as accomplishments in their own right; here, named as the specific basis for a lay community's veneration.

What comes next

AN 5.108 restates the identical ‘worthy of offerings’ formula, naming the same five components but qualified as belonging to an ‘adept’ rather than simply ‘accomplished’.

Key terms

āhuneyya — “worthy of offerings dedicated to the gods”.
dakkhiṇeyya — “worthy of a religious donation”.
anuttaraṁ puññakkhettaṁ lokassa — “the supreme field of merit for the world”.
sīla / samādhi / paññā / vimutti / vimuttiñāṇadassana — the same five-component path pentad first named at AN 5.92.
sampanna — “accomplished, endowed with” — the term describing possession of each quality here.

The text

The discourse in full: the path pentad, now named as the basis for worthiness of offerings. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Five qualities of worthiness

§1“Mendicants, a mendicant with five qualities is worthy of offerings dedicated to the gods, worthy of hospitality, worthy of a religious donation, worthy of veneration with cupped palms, and is the supreme field of merit for the world. What five? It’s when a mendicant is accomplished in ethics, immersion, wisdom, freedom, and the knowledge and vision of freedom. A mendicant with these five qualities is worthy of offerings dedicated to the gods, worthy of hospitality, worthy of a religious donation, worthy of veneration with cupped palms, and is the supreme field of merit for the world.”
