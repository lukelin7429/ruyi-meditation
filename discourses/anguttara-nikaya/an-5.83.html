Why this discourse

A well-known stock formula for scheming, acquisitive conduct, found at several points across the early canon: deceit, flattery, hinting, belittling, and using gains already received to chase further gains — five distinct faces of the same underlying manipulation aimed at material support.

Reading guide

Five terms, one behavioral cluster

Unlike AN 5.81's basic reactivity or AN 5.82's roots-plus-interpersonal-faults, this discourse's five terms are entirely about manipulative pursuit of material support — a single behavioral cluster viewed from five angles.

Kuhaka and lapaka

Kuhaka names a deceiver, one who fakes spiritual qualities to attract support. Lapaka names a flatterer, one who ingratiates through talk.

Nemittika and nippesika

Nemittika names a hinter, one who drops hints designed to extract gifts without asking outright. Nippesika names a belittler, one who undermines others by comparison in order to look better.

Using gains to chase further gains

The fifth item, lābhena lābhaṁ nijigīsitā, names a specific tactic: using a gift already received to angle for a larger one in return — giving a small offering, for instance, in order to prompt a bigger one back.

The mirrored positive

As at AN 5.81–82, the beloved senior mendicant is described simply by the negation of all five terms, with nothing further added.

What comes next

AN 5.84 restates a five-item list already familiar from this nipāta's very opening: faith, conscience, prudence, energy, and wisdom.

Key terms

kuhaka — “a deceiver” — one who fakes spiritual qualities to gain support.
lapaka — “a flatterer” — one who ingratiates through talk.
nemittika — “a hinter” — one who drops hints to extract gifts without asking outright.
nippesika — “a belittler” — one who undermines others by comparison.
lābhena lābhaṁ nijigīsitā — “using gains to chase further gains” — using a gift already received to angle for a larger one.

The text

The discourse in full: five faces of manipulative pursuit of support, then their negation. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Unlikable

§1“Mendicants, a senior mendicant with five qualities is unlikable and disagreeable to their spiritual companions, not respected or admired. What five? They use deceit, flattery, hinting, and belittling, and they use material things to chase after other material things. A senior mendicant with these five qualities is unlikable and disagreeable to their spiritual companions, not respected or admired.

Dear and beloved

§2A senior mendicant with five qualities is dear and beloved to their spiritual companions, respected and admired. What five? They don’t use deceit, flattery, hinting, or belittling, and they don’t use material things to chase after other material things. A senior mendicant with these five qualities is dear and beloved to their spiritual companions, respected and admired.”
