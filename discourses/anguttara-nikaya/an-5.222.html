Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 5.222

Long Wandering (2nd)

Dutiyadīghacārikasutta — Dīghacārikavagga — the twenty-third chapter of the Fives

None stated; the discourse continues from AN 5.1, whose setting at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove is understood to hold
Speakers

Form
two paired five-item lists, identical frame to AN 5.221
Length
~1 minute to read
Northern parallel
not identified in this collection
Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — short, a variant restatement

Why this discourse

The second of this pair of wandering discourses, keeping AN 5.221's opening and closing items unchanged but replacing its concern with learning with a concern for spiritual achievement itself — the same instability, measured against practice rather than study.

Reading guide

The same frame, a different middle concern

As in AN 5.221, contracting severe illness and lacking friends close the list unchanged. What changes is the opening three items: rather than learning, this discourse names spiritual achievement (adhigama) as what constant wandering undermines.

Five drawbacks of instability for practice

Not achieving what's unachieved; what's achieved falling away; and losing confidence in some of what's achieved — echoing AN 5.212's language for a disruptive Saṅgha member, now applied to the disruption caused by one's own restless travel.

Five matching benefits

Achieving the unachieved; what's achieved not falling away; confidence in what's achieved; avoiding severe illness; and having friends — the exact mirror, closing the same way as AN 5.221.

Learning and achievement, two related costs of the same instability

Read together, AN 5.221 and AN 5.222 suggest constant wandering damages practice on two fronts: intellectually, by preventing sustained learning, and experientially, by preventing sustained meditative progress — the same root problem, shown from two angles.

A matched pair, in this nipāta's established style

This close pairing — identical frame, varied middle — follows the same pattern already seen at AN 5.215–216 and elsewhere: a single underlying concern given two complementary statements rather than one exhaustive list.

Key terms

anadhigataṁ nādhigacchati — “doesn't achieve the unachieved” — the first drawback, echoing AN 5.212's language for a disruptive Saṅgha member.
adhigatā parihāyati — “what's achieved falls away” — the second drawback, actual spiritual regression.
adhigatenekaccena avisārado — “lacking confidence in some of what's achieved” — the third drawback.
gāḷhaṁ rogātaṅkaṁ phusati, mittavā — “contracts severe illness, has friends” — the two items unchanged from AN 5.221.
samavatthacāra — “a reasonable, measured amount of wandering” — the shared positive counterpart across both discourses.

The text

The discourse in full: the same structure as AN 5.221, with spiritual achievement in place of learning. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Five drawbacks of long, aimless wandering

§1“Mendicants, there are these five drawbacks for someone who likes long and aimless wandering. What five? You don’t achieve the unachieved. What you have achieved falls away. You lose confidence in some things you’ve achieved. You contract a severe illness. You don’t have any friends. These are the five drawbacks for someone who likes long and aimless wandering.

Five benefits of a reasonable amount of wandering

§2There are these five benefits of a reasonable amount of wandering. What five? You achieve the unachieved. What you have achieved doesn’t fall away. You’re confident in some things you’ve achieved. You don’t contract severe illness. You have friends. These are the five benefits of a reasonable amount of wandering.”
