Stinginess (1st)

Why this discourse

This discourse marks the structural turn this chapter's trio had been building toward: the careless-judgment items disappear entirely, and stinginess — now covering four of the five classical domains — stands entirely on its own.

Reading guide

Judgment items dropped, stinginess alone

Unlike AN 5.236–238, this discourse names no careless praise or criticism at all — its full five items concern stinginess and a single related consequence, nothing else.

Four of the five classical stinginess domains

Stinginess regarding monasteries, families, material things, and now praise (vaṇṇamacchariya) — four of the five domains from AN 5.223–224's classical formula, one short of the complete list.

A closing item retained from the earlier trio

Wasting a gift given in faith closes this discourse's list, exactly as in AN 5.236–238 — the one element carried through every discourse across this chapter's second half.

A four-domain stinginess, one domain short

The single classical domain still missing here is stinginess with the teaching itself (dhammamacchariya) — already flagged in this project as the most surprising item in the full formula, since it treats hoarding spiritual knowledge as comparable to hoarding possessions.

One discourse remains to complete the set

AN 5.240, this chapter's closing discourse, adds exactly that missing domain, completing the full classical five-part stinginess formula this whole trio-and-pair sequence has been building toward.

Key terms

āvāsamacchariya, kulamacchariya, lābhamacchariya — “stinginess over monasteries, families, and material things” — the first three domains, carried over from AN 5.237–238.
vaṇṇamacchariya — “stinginess over praise” — the fourth domain, newly added in this discourse.
dhammamacchariya — “stinginess over the teaching” — the one classical domain still missing here, added only at AN 5.240.
saddhādeyyaṁ vinipāteti — “wastes a gift given in faith” — the closing item, retained from the earlier trio.
macchariya — “stinginess” — now the sole organizing theme of this discourse's list, with careless judgment dropped entirely.

The text

The discourse in full: four domains of stinginess, standing on their own. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Five qualities placed in hell

§1“Mendicants, a resident mendicant with five qualities is placed in hell as if delivered there. What five? They’re stingy regarding monasteries, families, material things, and praise. And they waste a gift given in faith. A resident mendicant with these five qualities is placed in hell as if delivered there.

Five qualities placed in heaven

§2A resident mendicant with five qualities is placed in heaven as if delivered there. What five? They’re not stingy regarding monasteries, families, material things, and praise. And they don’t waste a gift given in faith. A resident mendicant with these five qualities is placed in heaven as if delivered there.”
