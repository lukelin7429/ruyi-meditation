Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 6.48

Apparent in the Present Life (2nd)

Dutiyasandiṭṭhikasutta — Dhammikavagga — the fifth chapter of the Sixes

Setting
None stated in the source
Speakers
A brahmin, questioning the Buddha
Form
The same question and return-question structure as AN 6.47, with a genuinely different second triad
Length
~2 minutes to read
Northern parallel
As with AN 6.47, verification through direct self-observation recurs across the Chinese Āgamas; this reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number
Difficulty
★★#9734;☆☆ — a close companion to AN 6.47, worth reading for its one genuine difference rather than skimmed as a repeat

Why this discourse

AN 6.48 asks the identical question as AN 6.47 — this time from an unnamed brahmin rather than the wanderer Sīvaka — and the Buddha's method is identical: the same return-question about greed, hate, and delusion. But checked directly against the source, the discourse's second triad is not a repeat of AN 6.47's “greedy, hateful, delusional ideas.” It asks instead about corruption (saṅkilesa) leading to deeds of body, speech, and mind — a different, though related, set of three.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

As at AN 6.47, a teaching is apparent in the present life because one can know directly whether greed, hate, and delusion are present or absent in oneself; this discourse's second triad extends the same self-checking method to corruption that leads to physical, verbal, and mental deeds, rather than to greedy, hateful, and delusional ideas.

What is identical to AN 6.47

The opening question, the Buddha's return-question structure, the first triad (greed, hate, delusion checked for presence and absence), and the closing conversion formula all match AN 6.47 closely. A reader could reasonably expect the second triad to match as well — and checked directly, it does not.

What genuinely differs, verified against the Pāli

Where AN 6.47's second triad names sarāgā, sadosā, samohā dhammā — greedy, hateful, delusional ideas, distinct thought-patterns — this discourse's second triad names kāyaduccaritasaṅkilesa, vacīduccaritasaṅkilesa, manoduccaritasaṅkilesa — corruption leading to misconduct of body, speech, and mind. One version checks colored mental states; the other checks whether corruption has actually led, or would lead, to conduct in each of the three doors of action.

Why the difference is worth noting, not smoothing over

Following this guide's own established practice at AN 6.25, 6.29, and 6.33, the resemblance between two similarly titled, similarly structured discourses is exactly the situation that calls for checking rather than assuming. Here the difference is genuine and specific: one companion discourse verifies awareness of mental coloring, the other verifies awareness of corruption oriented toward actual conduct.

Two questioners, one method

That the identical teaching method satisfies both a wandering ascetic of another sect (AN 6.47) and a brahmin (AN 6.48) — each converting by the same route, confirming a simple claim about their own mind — suggests the method's appeal was not limited to any one audience or background.

Key terms

saṅkilesa — “corruption” — the term distinguishing this discourse's second triad from AN 6.47's “ideas” (dhammā).
kāyaduccaritasaṅkilesa — “corruption leading to physical misconduct” — the first item of this discourse's distinct second triad.
manoduccaritasaṅkilesa — “corruption leading to mental misconduct” — the third item, closing the triad.
sarāgā dhammā — “greedy ideas” — AN 6.47's corresponding first item in its own, different second triad, for direct comparison.
saraṇaṁ gata — “gone for refuge” — the brahmin's closing declaration, matching Sīvaka's conversion at AN 6.47.

The text

The discourse in full: a brahmin's question, and the Buddha's six confirmed cases, including the distinct second triad. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

A brahmin's question

§1Then a certain brahmin went up to the Buddha, and exchanged greetings with him. When the greetings and polite conversation were over, he sat down to one side and said to the Buddha: “Worthy Gotama, they speak of ‘a teaching apparent in the present life’. In what way is the teaching apparent in the present life, immediately effective, inviting inspection, relevant, so that sensible people can know it for themselves?”

Greed, and the pattern established

§2“Well then, brahmin, I’ll ask you about this in return, and you can answer as you like. What do you think, brahmin? When there’s greed in you, do you understand ‘I have greed in me’? And when there’s no greed in you, do you understand ‘I have no greed in me’?” “Yes, sir.” “Since you know this, this is how the teaching is apparent in the present life, immediately effective, inviting inspection, relevant, so that sensible people can know it for themselves.

Hate, delusion, and corruption in body, speech, and mind

§3What do you think, brahmin? When there’s hate … delusion … corruption that leads to physical deeds … corruption that leads to speech … When there’s corruption that leads to mental deeds in you, do you understand ‘I have corruption that leads to mental deeds in me’? And when there’s no corruption that leads to mental deeds in you, do you understand ‘I have no corruption that leads to mental deeds in me’?” “Yes, sir.” “Since you know this, this is how the teaching is apparent in the present life, immediately effective, inviting inspection, relevant, so that sensible people can know it for themselves.” “Excellent, worthy Gotama! Excellent! … From this day forth, may the worthy Gotama remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge for life.”
