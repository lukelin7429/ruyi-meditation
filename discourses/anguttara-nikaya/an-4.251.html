Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 4.251

Expressions (2nd)

Why this discourse

Truthfully saying one has not seen, heard, thought, or known something one has not — the honest counterpart to AN 4.250's false claim, given here its own discourse as one of the four noble expressions. Where AN 4.217 folded this same content into a single discourse's heaven half, this chapter gives it independent standing.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

Truthfully saying one has not seen, heard, thought, or known something one has not is one of the four noble ways of speaking.

The honest counterpart, standing alone

Adiṭṭhe adiṭṭhavāditā, asute asutavāditā, amute amutavāditā, aviññāte aviññātavāditā — truthfully denying what one has not experienced, across all four modes. At AN 4.217 this content filled the heaven half of a single discourse, following the false claim named in its hell half. Here it receives its own discourse, with its own opening formula and its own closing restatement, rather than functioning as the second half of a paired structure.

What independence changes, and what it does not

Nothing about the content is different. What changes is emphasis: giving truthful denial its own discourse states it as a positive practice worth naming on its own terms, not merely as the absence of AN 4.250's fault. A reader encountering this discourse without AN 4.250 beside it would still receive a complete, self-standing instruction — unlike AN 4.217's heaven half, which depended on its neighboring hell half for its full sense as one term of a pair.

The pattern this chapter is building

With this discourse, two of the four combinations have now been given independent treatment: the false claim at AN 4.250, and its honest mirror here. Two remain, and the chapter’s evident plan is to give each of the remaining two — false denial and honest claim — the same independent standing, completing the set of four as four separate, self-contained statements rather than two paired discourses.

Key terms

adiṭṭhe adiṭṭhavāditā — “truthfully saying one has not seen what one has not seen” — the honest counterpart to AN 4.250's false claim.
ariyavohāra — “noble expression” — this discourse's frame, opposed to AN 4.250's anariyavohāra.
diṭṭha suta muta viññāta — the fourfold division of experience, unchanged from its first definition at AN 4.24.
saccavāditā — “truth-telling” — not named directly in this discourse, but the quality its content embodies.
vohāra — “expression, way of speaking” — naming this discourse and its three companions.

The text

The discourse in full: one of the four noble expressions, named. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

A noble expression

§1“Mendicants, there are these four noble expressions. What four? Saying you haven’t seen, heard, thought, or known something, and you haven’t. These are the four noble expressions.”
