Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 10.119

The Ceremony of Descent (1st)

Paṭhamapaccorohaṇīsutta — Paccorohaṇivagga — the twelfth chapter of the Tens, the Chapter on the Ceremony of Descent

Setting
None stated in the source
Speakers
The brahmin Jānussoṇi questioning the Buddha
Form
A real ritual described in detail, then reinterpreted as the ten-factor path
Length
~3 minutes to read
Chapter's namesake
This discourse gives the chapter its name, Paccorohaṇivagga, the Chapter on the Ceremony of Descent — though it is the seventh of ten discourses rather than the first, a pattern already seen with several earlier chapter names in this nipāta
Difficulty
★★★☆☆ — genuine ethnographic detail paired with familiar doctrinal content

Why this discourse

Encountering the brahmin Jānussoṇi dressed for an actual fire-worship purification rite, the Buddha asks what is going on, receives a detailed firsthand description of brahmanical practice, and offers in response a “noble descent” so persuasive that Jānussoṇi declares the brahmin version not worth a sixteenth part of it.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

Where the brahmins' ceremony of descent involves bathing, new robes, fresh grass, a bed of cow dung, and three nighttime risings to worship fire with cupped palms, the noble descent taught in this training is different entirely: reflecting that each wrong factor from view to freedom has a bad result in this life and the next, and so giving it up, descending from it, one factor at a time.

A ritual described with real ethnographic detail

As at AN 10.107's bone-washing ceremony, the Buddha encounters a genuine contemporary practice and asks about it directly rather than assuming knowledge of it — and Jānussoṇi's answer is unusually specific: the bathing, the new linen robes, the handful of fresh grass, the bed made of cow dung and grass between the boundary and the fire chamber, the threefold nighttime rising to declare “we descend, lord!” to the fire, and the feast that follows.

The same word, radically reinterpreted

Rather than inventing a new term, the Buddha takes Jānussoṇi's own word, paccorohaṇī, “descent,” and gives it an entirely new referent: not physically descending from a raised bed at dawn to worship fire, but reflectively descending from — that is, abandoning — each of the ten wrong factors in turn, one reflection and one act of letting go per factor.

A brahmin's own verdict, and a conversion

Jānussoṇi's response is unusually strong even for this project's many wanderer-conversion narratives: he does not merely praise the Buddha's teaching but explicitly quantifies the comparison, declaring the brahmin ceremony “not worth a sixteenth part” of the noble one, before taking refuge for life — the chapter's own namesake discourse closing with one of its clearest conversions.

Key terms

paccorohaṇī — “the ceremony of descent” — the brahmanical ritual's own name, and this discourse's title, reinterpreted entirely by the Buddha's answer.
paccorohāma bhavantaṁ — “we descend, lord!” — the brahmins' own threefold nighttime declaration to the fire, part of the ritual Jānussoṇi describes in detail.
diṭṭhadhammikopi samparāyikopi vipāko — “a bad result in both this life and the next” — the reflection a noble disciple makes about each wrong factor, prompting its abandonment.
micchādiṭṭhiyā paccorohati — “they descend from wrong view” — the Buddha's own reinterpretation of “descent” as abandoning a wrong factor, repeated for all ten.
kalaṁ nāgghati soḷasiṁ — “not worth a sixteenth part” — Jānussoṇi's own quantified verdict comparing the brahmin ceremony to the noble descent.

The text

The discourse in full: Jānussoṇi's description of the brahmin ceremony, and the Buddha's reinterpretation as the noble descent from the ten wrong factors. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

A brahmin ritual, described in detail

§1Now, at that time it was the sabbath. The brahmin Jānussoṇi had bathed his head and dressed in a new pair of linen robes. Holding a handful of fresh grass, he stood to one side not far from the Buddha. The Buddha saw him, and said, “Brahmin, why have you bathed your head and dressed in a new pair of linen robes? Why are you standing to one side holding a handful of fresh grass? What’s going on today with the brahmin clan?” “Worthy Gotama, today is the ceremony of descent for the brahmin clan.” “But how do the brahmins observe the ceremony of descent?” “Well, worthy Gotama, on the sabbath the brahmins bathe their heads and dress in a new pair of linen robes. They make a heap of fresh cow dung and spread it with green grass. Then they make their beds between the boundary and the fire chamber. That night they rise three times and worship the fire with cupped palms: ‘We descend, lord! We descend, lord!’ And they serve the fire with abundant ghee, oil, and butter. And when the night has passed they serve the brahmins with delicious fresh and cooked foods. That’s how the brahmins observe the ceremony of descent.” “The ceremony of descent observed by the brahmins is quite different from that observed in the training of the Noble One.” “But worthy Gotama, how is the ceremony of descent observed in the training of the Noble One? Worthy Gotama, please teach me this.”

The noble descent

§2“Well then, brahmin, listen and apply your mind well, I will speak.” “Yes, worthy sir,” Jānussoṇi replied. The Buddha said this: “It’s when a noble disciple reflects: ‘Wrong view has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong view, they descend from wrong view. ‘Wrong purpose has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong purpose, they descend from wrong purpose. ‘Wrong speech has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong speech, they descend from wrong speech. ‘Wrong action has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong action, they descend from wrong action. ‘Wrong livelihood has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong livelihood, they descend from wrong livelihood. ‘Wrong effort has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong effort, they descend from wrong effort. ‘Wrong mindfulness has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong mindfulness, they descend from wrong mindfulness. ‘Wrong immersion has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong immersion, they descend from wrong immersion. ‘Wrong knowledge has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong knowledge, they descend from wrong knowledge. ‘Wrong freedom has a bad result in both this life and the next.’ Reflecting like this, they give up wrong freedom, they descend from wrong freedom. This is the ceremony of descent in the training of the Noble One.”

Jānussoṇi's verdict

§3“The ceremony of descent observed by the brahmins is quite different from that observed in the training of the Noble One. And, worthy Gotama, the ceremony of descent observed by the brahmins is not worth a sixteenth part of a master of the ceremony of descent observed in the training of the Noble One. Excellent, worthy Gotama! … From this day forth, may the worthy Gotama remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge for life.”
