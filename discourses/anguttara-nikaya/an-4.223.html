Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 4.223

Ungrateful

Akataññutāsutta — Duccaritavagga — the twenty-third chapter of the Fours

Setting
None stated; the Duccaritavagga gives no location for this discourse, and it is addressed to the mendicants directly
Speakers
The Buddha alone, addressing the mendicants
Form
The same formula as AN 4.222, with its fourth item changed to ingratitude, and the first sign of elision entering this chapter's run
Length
~1 minute to read
Northern parallel
This reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number for this discourse
Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — a direct repeat, worth noting for its source rather than its content

Why this discourse

The same substitution the previous chapter made at AN 4.213 — wrong view swapped for ingratitude — made again here, in the khata formula this chapter has been using since AN 4.222. Between the two chapters, this exact single-item swap has now happened twice, on two different underlying formulas.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

Bad conduct in body, speech, and mind, plus ingratitude, keeps a fool broken and blameworthy; the four good counterparts, including gratitude, keep an astute person intact and praiseworthy.

A repeated substitution

AN 4.213 made this same change to AN 4.212’s tetrad: the fourth slot, held there by wrong view, became akataññutā akataveditā, ingratitude and thanklessness. This discourse makes the identical substitution to AN 4.222’s tetrad. The result is content already discussed at both AN 4.212 and AN 4.213 — where this reading guide covered why ingratitude sits in the same tetrad slot as bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct, and what the doubled terms akataññutā and akataveditā specifically name — now under this chapter’s formula rather than the previous chapter’s.

Where the elision begins

This discourse is also the point where the chapter’s run of full statements gives way to compression. Its heaven half is written with the source text’s own …pe… marks standing in for the full restatement already given in the hell half, the first sign in this chapter of the heavier abridgment that will dominate AN 4.224–4.229.

Two swaps, one technique

Set beside its counterpart in the previous chapter, this discourse confirms that the single-item-swap technique is not tied to any one formula. It was demonstrated on the yathābhataṁ nikkhitto formula at AN 4.212–213, and it works identically on the khata formula here. The technique belongs to how the collection treats content, not to any particular wrapper around it.

Key terms

akataññutā akataveditā — “ingratitude and thanklessness” — the same doubled term first read at AN 4.213, occupying the same tetrad slot here.
khataṁ upahataṁ attānaṁ pariharati — “keeps themselves broken and damaged” — this chapter’s formula, from AN 4.3, carried over from AN 4.222.
kāyaduccarita, vacīduccarita, manoduccarita — bad conduct by body, speech, and mind — the three items held constant across this discourse and AN 4.222.
micchādiṭṭhi — “wrong view” — the item this discourse’s fourth slot replaces, occupying the same position at AN 4.222 as it does at AN 4.212.
peyyāla — “repetition-series” — the abridgment device that enters this chapter for the first time in this discourse’s heaven half.

The text

The discourse largely in full, with the heaven half beginning to compress. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

The discourse

§1“When a foolish, incompetent untrue person has four qualities they keep themselves broken and damaged. They deserve to be blamed and criticized by sensible people, and they brim with much wickedness. What four? Bad conduct by way of body, speech, and mind, and being ungrateful and thankless. An astute person … brims with much merit. … Good conduct by way of body, speech, and mind, and being grateful and thankful. …”
