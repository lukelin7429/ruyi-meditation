Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourses 7.618–644

Untitled Discourses on Greed

Rāgapeyyāla, continued — the eleventh chapter of the Sevens

Setting
None stated in the source
Speakers
The Buddha alone, addressing the mendicants
Form
One sentence, cross-multiplying the three seven-item lists already given in full against nine further verbs, representing twenty-seven individually numbered discourses
Length
~1 minute to read
Nine verbs, not ten

Difficulty
★★☆☆☆ — compressed, but small in scale compared to what follows it

Why this discourse

AN 7.618–644 takes the three seven-item lists already given in full at AN 7.615–617 — the awakening factors, and two lists of seven perceptions — and runs each of them against nine further verbs for greed specifically, compressing twenty-seven discourses into a single sentence.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

For the complete understanding of greed, its complete ending, giving it up, its ending, its vanishing, its fading away, its cessation, its giving away, and its letting go — nine further verbs beyond the direct knowledge already covered at AN 7.615–617 — the same three seven-item lists should be developed.

Nine verbs, completing the ten for greed alone

AN 7.615–617 each paired one seven-item list with abhiññā, direct knowledge, giving three discourses. This block supplies the remaining nine verbs — pariññā (complete understanding), parikkhaya (complete ending), pahāna (giving up), khaya (ending), vaya (vanishing), virāga (fading away), nirodha (cessation), cāga (giving away), and paṭinissagga (letting go) — each crossed against the same three lists, for nine times three equals twenty-seven further discourses, all addressed to greed.

No new content, only new combinations

Nothing in this block is conceptually new: every item in every one of the three lists was already given in full at AN 7.615–617, and every verb here follows the standard progression this book has met before in other closing blocks. What this block adds is purely the systematic combination of already-known parts.

Setting the template for the chapter's final expansion

This block completes greed's own portion of the Rāgapeyyāla — three discourses of direct knowledge (AN 7.615–617) plus these twenty-seven (nine verbs times three lists) for a total of thirty discourses addressed to greed alone. The chapter's final and largest block, AN 7.645–1124, applies this identical template — now all ten verbs together — to sixteen further defilements beyond greed.

Key terms

pariññā, parikkhaya, pahāna — “complete understanding, complete ending, giving up” — the first three of nine verbs this block adds beyond direct knowledge.
khaya, vaya, virāga — “ending, vanishing, fading away” — the fourth through sixth verbs.
nirodha, cāga, paṭinissagga — “cessation, giving away, letting go” — the seventh through ninth verbs, completing the set of nine.
rāgassa — “of greed” — the single defilement every verb and every list in this block is addressed to; the chapter's remaining block will apply the same structure to sixteen further defilements.
bojjhaṅgā, dve saññā — the awakening factors and two lists of seven perceptions — the three lists given in full at AN 7.615–617 and assumed without restatement throughout this entire block.

The text

The formula exactly as the source compresses it: nine verbs, each implicitly crossed against the three lists already given in full. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Nine verbs for greed, completing its own portion of this chapter

§1“For the complete understanding of greed … complete ending … giving up … ending … vanishing … fading away … cessation … giving away … For the letting go of greed, these seven things should be developed.”
