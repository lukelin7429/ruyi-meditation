Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 5.16

The Peak, Again

Punakūṭasutta — Balavagga — the second chapter of the Fives

Why this discourse

The title says it plainly: puna, again. This discourse takes AN 5.12’s claim that wisdom is chief among the powers, and its bungalow-peak simile, and restates both without change — except that the powers in question are now the standard five, faith, energy, mindfulness, immersion, wisdom, rather than the sekhabala. It is the clearest confirmation this chapter offers that its ranking of wisdom is not tied to one particular five-item list, but survives the switch between the two.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

Of the five powers — faith, energy, mindfulness, immersion, wisdom — wisdom is the chief, holding and binding everything together, exactly as AN 5.12 said of the sekhabala.

What changed, and what did not

Compare the two discourses directly and the only substitution is the noun phrase naming which five items are meant. The verbs, the simile, the ranking claim, even the sentence structure are unchanged. This is a stronger form of repetition than anything seen so far in the Fives — not a formula reused with one qualifier added, as at AN 5.9, but the identical argument transplanted wholesale onto a different list.

Why this particular repetition earns its own discourse

A skeptical reader might ask why the collection needed two separate discourses to make one claim about two different lists, rather than a single discourse noting the claim holds for both. The likely answer, consistent with the reasoning offered already at AN 5.9, is that each five-item list circulates and is chanted as its own self-contained unit; a claim about the sekhabala does not automatically transfer, in oral practice, to the bala unless it is actually stated for the bala as well.

A ranking now doubly confirmed

With this discourse, wisdom’s status as chief has been claimed for both of this chapter’s five-item power lists, in the same words, using the same image. A reader who has followed the chapter this far has now seen the strongest possible statement, within this material, that whichever version of ‘the five powers’ is under discussion, wisdom is being treated as what completes and secures the rest.

What follows

AN 5.17 turns the chapter toward a different concern entirely: not the five powers at all, but a different five-item set — ethics, immersion, wisdom, freedom, and the knowledge and vision of freedom — and whether a mendicant who has them shares them with others. The four discourses closing this chapter, AN 5.17 through 5.20, form their own self-contained unit.

Key terms

puna — “again” — the word giving this discourse its title, marking it as a deliberate restatement rather than new material.
kūṭa — “peak, roof-ridge” — the identical image from AN 5.12, transplanted here without change.
bala — “power” — here unambiguously the standard five powers, following the pivot made at AN 5.13.
agga — “chief, foremost” — the ranking word applied to wisdom a second time, now for a second five-item list.
saṅgāhika saṅghātaniya — “that which holds together, that which binds together” — the paired verbs from AN 5.12, repeated here word for word.

The text

The discourse in full: the standard five powers restated, wisdom named chief a second time, and the identical bungalow simile. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Wisdom, the chief power, restated

§1“Mendicants, there are these five powers. What five? The powers of faith, energy, mindfulness, immersion, and wisdom. These are the five powers. Of these five powers, the power of wisdom is the chief. It holds and binds everything together. It’s like a bungalow. The roof-peak is the chief point, which holds and binds everything together. In the same way, of these five powers, the power of wisdom is the chief. It holds and binds everything together.”
