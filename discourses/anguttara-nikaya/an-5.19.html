Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 5.19

The Welfare of Neither

Tatiyahitasutta — Balavagga — the second chapter of the Fives

Setting
None stated; the discourse continues from AN 5.1, whose setting at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove is understood to hold
Speakers
The Buddha alone, addressing the mendicants
Form
Both halves of AN 5.17's formula negated together
Length
~1 minute to read
Northern parallel
This reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number for this variant beyond the parallel already noted at AN 5.17
Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — the simplest of the four cases, both conditions failing together

Why this discourse

The third case closes the logical square without ambiguity: a mendicant neither personally accomplished in ethics, immersion, wisdom, freedom, and the knowledge and vision of freedom, nor encouraging others toward them. Where AN 5.17 and 5.18 each offered something worth weighing — genuine private attainment against genuine outward generosity — this discourse offers nothing to weigh. It names the case where both fail at once.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

A mendicant neither personally accomplished in the five, nor encouraging others in them, is practicing for neither their own welfare nor that of others.

The simplest of the four cases

AN 5.17 and AN 5.18 each required a reader to weigh something genuine against something missing — real attainment against silence, or real generosity against lack of grounding. This discourse asks nothing to be weighed. Both halves of the formula are negated together, and the outcome follows without needing any qualification.

Why the collection still gives this case its own discourse

A reader might expect this, the least interesting case logically, to be folded into one of the others or skipped. The chapter does neither. Keeping all four combinations as separate, equally weighted discourses is itself a small argument: the collection treats the logical square as worth completing in full, not only in its more psychologically interesting corners.

What this case is not saying

The discourse does not say a mendicant in this position is beyond help, or condemns them further than the bare description. It states a fact about their present practice, in the same flat register used for the other three cases, and stops there. Whatever consequence might follow is left to other discourses in this series to address, not this one.

One case remaining

AN 5.20 completes the square, and closes the chapter, with the fourth combination: personally accomplished and encouraging others. Reading all four discourses as a set makes plain that this final combination, not either of the two partial ones, is what the whole four-discourse unit has been building toward.

Key terms

neva…no — “neither…nor” — the paired negation opening this discourse, distinguishing it from AN 5.17 and 5.18's single negations.
tatiya — “third” — the ordinal in this discourse's title, marking its place in the four-part unit.
attahita — “one's own welfare” — negated here alongside parahita, both failing together for the first time in the unit.
parahita — “others' welfare” — likewise negated, completing the discourse's double failure.
aṅga — “factor, quality” — the word this discourse uses for the five items, in place of dhamma, the term AN 5.17 used for the identical list.

The text

The discourse in full: neither personal accomplishment nor encouraging others. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Neither accomplished, nor encouraging others

§1“Mendicants, a mendicant with five qualities is practicing neither for their own welfare, nor that of others. What five? It’s when a mendicant is not personally accomplished in ethics, immersion, wisdom, freedom, or the knowledge and vision of freedom. Nor do they encourage others in these qualities. A mendicant with these five qualities is practicing neither for their own welfare, nor that of others.”
