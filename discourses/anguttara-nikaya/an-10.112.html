Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 10.112

An Adept (2nd)

Why this discourse

Closing this chapter with the shortest possible restatement, the Buddha declares the ten qualities of an adept unprompted, without even the minimal frame of a mendicant's question that opened AN 10.111.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

There are ten qualities of an adept: an adept's right view, right purpose, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right immersion, right knowledge, and right freedom — the same definition as AN 10.111, now declared without being asked.

The last step in stripping away every remaining frame

AN 10.111 still retained a question-and-answer structure, however brief; this discourse removes even that, opening directly with the Buddha's own unprompted declaration — the final stage in a chapter-long process of stripping successive framings away from one stable content.

A chapter's structure fully revealed by its own final page

Read as a whole, this chapter has moved from two isolated fulfillment teachings (AN 10.101–102), through eight variously dressed repetitions of one ten-factor teaching (AN 10.103–110), to two direct definitions of that same content as the mark of a finished practitioner (AN 10.111–112) — a clear three-part architecture only fully visible once the final discourse is reached.

Closing with completion, fittingly

It is difficult to imagine a more fitting note to close a chapter that began with perceptions for ascetics still early in training (AN 10.101) than this final, unprompted declaration of what the fully finished practitioner, the asekha, actually possesses — training's own beginning and its completion framing the chapter from opposite ends.

Key terms

dasa asekhā dhammā — “ten qualities of an adept” — this discourse's own opening declaration, stated without any preceding question.
asekhāya sammādiṭṭhiyā — “an adept's right view” — the first factor, identical in wording to AN 10.111's own opening item.
Samaṇasaññāvaggo — “the Chapter on Perceptions for Ascetics” — the chapter's own name, closing here with its untranslated colophon.
Tatiyapaṇṇāsaka — “the Third Fifty” — the larger structural span this chapter opens, continuing after this chapter's close.
uddāna — a summary verse naming, in brief, all the discourses just covered — here closing the chapter, left untranslated in the English text.

The text

The discourse in full: the ten qualities of an adept, declared without a preceding question. The chapter's own colophon and uddāna verse, in Pāli only, are described but not reproduced. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

Ten qualities of an adept

§1“Mendicants, there are ten qualities of an adept. What ten? An adept’s right view, right purpose, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right immersion, right knowledge, and right freedom. These are the ten qualities of an adept.”
