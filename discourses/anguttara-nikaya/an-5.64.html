Aṅguttara Nikāya · Discourse 5.64

Growth (2nd)

Dutiyavaḍḍhisutta — Saññāvagga — the seventh chapter of the Fives

Speakers

Form

Length
~30 seconds to read

This reading guide does not assert a specific matching sutra number for this variant beyond the parallel already noted at AN 5.63

Why this discourse

This discourse could, in principle, not exist: nothing about AN 5.63's content requires restating for it to apply equally to a woman. That the collection produced it anyway — a full, separate discourse in feminine grammatical form, ariyasāvikā rather than a masculine default silently covering both — is itself worth reading as a deliberate choice, not an accident of transmission.

Reading guide

The teaching in one sentence

A female noble disciple who grows in faith, ethics, learning, generosity, and wisdom grows nobly, taking on what is essential and excellent in this life.

Identical content, complete grammatical restatement

Every substantive claim in this discourse matches AN 5.63 exactly — the same five items, the same outcome, the same closing image of taking hold of what is essential. What changes is thorough and consistent: ariyasāvikā for ariyasāvako, sārādāyinī varādāyinī for sārādāyī varādāyī, feminine forms replacing masculine ones throughout both the prose and the closing verse.

What this pairing implies about the collection's assumptions

A tradition confident that a masculine grammatical form automatically covered women as well would have had no reason to produce this discourse at all. Its existence suggests the opposite assumption was at least sometimes operative: that a teaching aimed explicitly and separately at women mattered enough to be composed and preserved as its own discourse, not folded silently into the male version.

Upāsikā, named directly in the verse

The closing verse names its subject sīlavatī upāsikā, a virtuous laywoman, rather than only repeating ariyasāvikā. This gives the discourse's subject a concrete social identity — not an abstract disciple in either gender, but specifically a laywoman practicing in the world, matching AN 5.63's parallel use of a general term for its own subject.

Read against AN 5.55, earlier in this chapter

This discourse and AN 5.55's account of a mother, a son, and a stark warning about women sit in the same chapter, only nine discourses apart. This reading guide does not attempt to resolve the tension between them into a single, tidy statement about how this collection regards women; both discourses are genuinely present in the text, addressed to different concerns, and a reader doing this material justice should hold both rather than letting one silently stand for the whole.

Key terms

ariyasāvikā — “female noble disciple” — the feminine grammatical form used throughout this discourse, paired deliberately with AN 5.63's masculine ariyasāvako.
sārādāyinī varādāyinī — “taking hold of what is essential and excellent” — the feminine form of AN 5.63's closing image, otherwise unchanged.
sīlavatī upāsikā — “virtuous laywoman” — the closing verse's concrete social identity for its subject, paralleling AN 5.63's own general term.
vaḍḍhi — “growth” — the same frame as AN 5.63, unchanged between the two discourses.
saddhā sīla suta cāga paññā — “faith, ethics, learning, generosity, wisdom” — the identical five items, now the fifth appearance of this list in this nipāta.

The text

The discourse in full: the five kinds of growth for a female noble disciple, closing with a matching verse. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Mendicants, a female noble disciple who grows in five ways grows nobly, taking on what is essential and excellent in this life. What five? She grows in faith, ethics, learning, generosity, and wisdom. A female noble disciple who grows in these five ways grows nobly, taking on what is essential and excellent in this life.

The closing verse

§2She who grows in faith and ethics, wisdom, and both generosity and learning—a virtuous laywoman such as she takes on what is essential for herself in this life.”
