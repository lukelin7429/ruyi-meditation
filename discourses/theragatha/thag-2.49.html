Why this text

Sandhita recalls a single perception gained beneath the Bodhi Tree, mindful, becoming one with the Buddha — thirty-one eons ago, he says, and it is that very perception which has now brought the ending of defilements. His poem closes not only Chapter Five, but the entire Book of the Twos: 49 poems, now complete.

Reading guide

Beneath the Bodhi Tree, a single perception

The first verse recalls a specific moment: beneath a Bodhi Tree, ‘bright green and growing’, mindfully gaining ‘one perception, focused on the Buddha’. The second verse gives this memory a precise span — thirty-one eons — and states plainly that this same perception is what has now brought the ending of defilements. Like Thag 1.96's eighty koti years and Thag 1.108's stated age of one hundred twenty, this poem joins this collection's occasional use of a concrete number where most declarations speak only of eons or lifetimes in the abstract; this reading guide does not attempt to identify which Buddha's Bodhi Tree is meant.

Not just a chapter's end, but the Book's

After this poem, the Pali carries vaggo pañcamo, ‘the fifth chapter is finished’, followed by this chapter's own uddāna, naming all nine monks in sequence: Kumārakassapa, Dhammapāla, Brahmāli, Mogharāja, Visākha (the abbreviated form of Visākhapañcālaputta), Cūḷaka, Anūpama, Vajjita, and Sandhita himself — who alone receives an added epithet, kilesarajavāhano, ‘carrier-away of the dust of defilement’. A single epithet on the last name alone is not a new pattern: it is the same arrangement already found closing Chapter Three, making two of this book's five chapters share this exact form — alongside Chapters One and Two's shared collective epithet, and Chapter Four's own scattered, mid-list variant. Three patterns across five chapters, not five different ones.

The Book of the Twos itself concludes

Immediately after this chapter's uddāna, with no narrative transition at all, the text carries Dukanipāto niṭṭhito — ‘the Book of the Twos is concluded’. This is the same kind of book-level closing marker this collection first met at Thag 1.120, where the Book of the Ones ended with Ekakanipāto niṭṭhito after its own twelfth chapter.

A tally, not a verse of praise

A separate section follows, headed Tatruddānaṁ, giving the Book of the Twos its own summary: ninety-eight verses in total (two per poem, across forty-nine poems), forty-nine elders, ‘skilled in the way’. This is a genuinely different close than the Book of the Ones received: Thag 1.120's book-level summary was a verse of praise, calling its hundred and twenty elders' ‘task done, undefiled’ and ‘well recited... by the great sage’. Here, the Book of the Twos closes on plain arithmetic — a count of verses and elders — with no comparable language of praise. Sujato leaves both this book's uddāna and its closing marker untranslated, as with every chapter marker and uddāna so far; this reading guide paraphrases their content without treating either as part of the verse proper.

49 elders, five chapters, a second milestone reached

With this poem, the Book of the Twos is complete on this site: 49 poems across five chapters, following the Book of the Ones (120 poems, twelve chapters) already finished. The running total now stands at 169 of 264 poems. Nineteen further books remain, the next being the Book of the Threes — sixteen poems — whose own structure, chapters if any, and uddāna conventions will need their own fresh check before work continues, exactly as this book's own conventions had to be verified fresh after the Book of the Ones.

Key terms

ekaṁ buddhagataṁ saññaṁ — “one perception, focused on the Buddha” — what Sandhita recalls gaining beneath the Bodhi Tree.
vaggo pañcamo — “the fifth chapter is finished” — the untranslated marker closing Chapter Five.
kilesarajavāhano — “carrier-away of the dust of defilement” — the epithet this chapter's uddāna adds to Sandhita's own name alone.
Dukanipāto niṭṭhito — “the Book of the Twos is concluded” — the untranslated marker closing the entire book, distinct from this chapter's own closing marker.
Tatruddānaṁ — “and here is its summary” — heading the book-level tally of ninety-eight verses and forty-nine elders that follows the book's closing marker.

The text

The text in full: two verses, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Beneath the Bodhi Tree, bright green and growing, being mindful, my perception became one with the Buddha.

§2It’s been thirty one eons since I gained that perception; and it’s due to that perception that I’ve attained the ending of defilements.
