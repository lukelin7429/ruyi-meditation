Theragatha · 1.26

Abhaya

Why this text

Abhaya's verse compresses cause and effect into two steps: hearing the Buddha's fine words, then penetrating ‘the subtle truth’ — described with one of this collection's most exact images, ‘like a hair-tip with an arrow’.

Reading guide

An epithet naming a traditional lineage

‘Kinsman of the Sun’ translates ādiccabandhu, a traditional epithet for the Buddha referring to the Sakyan clan's own claimed solar lineage — a title of ancestry rather than a description of his teaching itself.

Precision measured against something nearly impossible to hit

‘Like a hair-tip with an arrow’ describes piercing something almost too fine a target to strike at all — not a general image of skill, but a specific measure of exactness applied to understanding a subtle truth.

Hearing and penetrating, with no described interval

The verse moves directly from ‘having heard’ to ‘I penetrated’, with no account of prolonged practice in between — a compressed cause-and-effect structure, unlike verses elsewhere in this collection that describe years or seasons of training.

Key terms

ādiccabandhu — “Kinsman of the Sun” — a traditional epithet for the Buddha, referring to the Sakyan clan's claimed solar lineage.
subhāsita — “fine words” or “well-spoken” — describing what Abhaya heard.
nipuṇa — “subtle” — describing the truth Abhaya says he penetrated.
paccabyadhi — “pierced through” — the verb naming this verse's central action.
vālagga — “hair-tip” — the verse's image of an almost impossibly precise target.

The text

The text in full: one verse, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Having heard the fine words of the Buddha, Kinsman of the Sun, I penetrated the subtle truth, like a hair-tip with an arrow.
