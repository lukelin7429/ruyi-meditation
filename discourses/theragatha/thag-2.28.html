Theragatha · 2.28

Bharata

Why this text

Bharata addresses a companion by name — ‘come, Nandaka, let's go to visit our preceptor’ — proposing they ‘roar their lion's roar’ before the Buddha together, since the goal their teacher set them going forth for, the ending of all fetters, has now been reached.

Reading guide

An invitation, addressed by name

Unlike most poems in this book, this one opens as direct speech to a named companion: ‘Come, Nandaka, let's go to visit our preceptor’. The poem is framed as an invitation rather than a solitary reflection or declaration.

A lion's roar, before the best of Buddhas

The proposed action is vivid and public: ‘we'll roar our lion's roar before the best of Buddhas’ — a standard idiom for a bold, public declaration of one's own attainment, made in this case as a shared act rather than a solitary one.

A goal reached together

The second verse explains why: the teacher gave them going forth ‘out of compassion’, and ‘that goal’ — the ending of all fetters — has now been reached. The poem reports a shared attainment (‘we have reached’), not only Bharata's own.

A plausible link to the poem just before it

The companion addressed here is named Nandaka, the same name as the speaker of Thag 2.27, immediately before this poem in the chapter. No comment confirms these are the same person, but the adjacency and the shared name make this the most natural reading; this guide presents it as a plausible connection rather than a confirmed one.

Key terms

upajjhāyassa — “of the preceptor” — the figure Bharata proposes visiting, named without further identification here.
sīhanādaṁ — “lion's roar” — a standard idiom for a bold, public declaration of one's own attainment.
buddhaseṭṭhassa — “the best of Buddhas” — the epithet used for the one before whom the roar will be made.
anukampāya — “out of compassion” — why the sage is said to have given the two companions going forth.
sabbasaṁyojanakkhayo — “the ending of all fetters” — the shared goal the poem reports as now reached.

The text

The text in full: two verses, without a translated attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Come, Nandaka, let’s go to visit our preceptor. We’ll roar our lion’s roar before the best of Buddhas.

§2The sage gave us the going forth out of compassion, so we could realize the ending of all fetters—now we have reached that goal.
