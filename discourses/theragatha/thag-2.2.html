Theragatha · 2.2

Bhāradvāja the Alms-Gatherer

Bhāradvāja — The Book of the Twos · Chapter One · Poem 2 of 10

Setting

Why this text

Bhāradvāja opens by rejecting an extreme — you can't live by fasting alone, though food itself doesn't bring peace of heart — then turns to a sharper danger than hunger: the veneration he receives in respectable households, which he calls a swamp, and a subtle dart, hard for a lesser person to give up.

Reading guide

Neither fasting nor food itself is the point

The verse opens by denying that starving oneself is a viable path — ‘you can't live by fasting’ — but immediately denies the opposite too: food doesn't lead to peace of heart either. Seeing how this ‘bag of bones’ depends on food, he wanders on, still seeking, rather than resting in either extreme.

Honor as a swamp, a dart hard to extract

The second verse turns from food to something more insidious: the homage and veneration he receives ‘in respectable families’. He names it a swamp (paṅka) that ‘they’ recognize as such, then sharpens the image further — honor is a subtle dart, hard to extract, and hard for a reprobate to give up. Two images in four lines, moving from mire to a barbed point lodged under the skin.

Still wandering, not yet arrived

Unlike many Book of the Ones verses that speak from already having reached the goal, this poem's first verse ends mid-motion — ‘I wander, seeking’ — a present-tense, still-in-process voice rather than a retrospective declaration of attainment.

The same monk, silent elsewhere on this site

This site's Udāna collection includes Ud 4.6, ‘The Alms-Gatherer’, where the Buddha watches this same Piṇḍola Bhāradvāja sit in disciplined silence and distills what he sees into six lines summarizing monastic training as a whole — there, the Buddha speaks and Bhāradvāja says nothing; here, Bhāradvāja speaks for himself about a danger the Buddha's silent portrait doesn't address directly.

Key terms

anaya — “wrong method” or ruinous course — what this verse says life can't be sustained by, opening with fasting as an example.
esā — “seeking” — the verb closing the first verse, describing Bhāradvāja's own ongoing search rather than a completed attainment.
paṅka — “swamp” or “mire” — what Bhāradvāja calls the homage and veneration he receives in respectable households.
sallaṁ — “dart” or “arrow” — the second image for honor, ‘subtle’ and ‘hard to extract’.
kāpurisa — “lesser person” or “reprobate” — who the verse says finds honor hardest of all to give up.

The text

The text in full: two verses and the closing attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1You can’t live by fasting, but food doesn’t lead to peace of heart. Seeing how this bag of bones is sustained by food, I wander, seeking.

§2They know it’s just a swamp, this homage and veneration in respectable families. Honor is a subtle dart, hard to extract, and hard for a reprobate to give up.

§3That is how these verses were recited by the senior venerable Bhāradvāja the Alms-Gatherer.
