Why this text

Where most poems in this collection describe an individual path, Visākhapañcālaputta gives explicit guidance on conduct toward fellow monastics — rooted, Sujato's comment notes, in specific Vinaya procedures — before closing on how subtle insight and mature ethics make extinguishment not hard to reach.

Reading guide

Communal ethics, not individual attainment

The first verse is a list of prohibitions addressed to the Saṅgha's own conduct: don't suspend others from the community, don't raise objections against them, don't disparage or raise one's voice against one who has crossed to the far shore, don't praise oneself among the assemblies — be measured in speech and true to one's vows. This is a different register from most of this collection's individual declarations, closer in spirit to Thag 1.34 and 1.58's concerns with monastic decorum, though those poems address conduct toward visiting laywomen rather than conduct within the Saṅgha itself.

Formal Vinaya procedure, named by the comment

Sujato's comment on the poem's opening line states plainly: ‘this refers to specific procedures of Vinaya’. The word translated ‘suspend’ (ukkhipe) names a formal disciplinary act removing a monk's standing in the community for specific offenses — not a general call to be nice to one another, but a pointed instruction about a real monastic procedure.

Even the far-gone are not to be disparaged

The first verse's prohibition extends to one who has ‘crossed to the further shore’ (pāragataṁ), a standing epithet for an arahant — implying that restraint in speech applies regardless of another person's attainment, not only toward the ordinary or the struggling.

A name, abbreviated in this chapter's own uddāna

Visākhapañcālaputta, ‘Visākha, son of Pañcāla’, named by paternal lineage in full here, is later abbreviated to simply ‘Visākha’ in this chapter's own closing uddāna — the same kind of abbreviation this collection has already found in Chapter Four, where Sambulakaccāna became simply Kaccāna.

Key terms

ukkhipe — “suspend [from the Saṅgha]” — a formal Vinaya procedure, according to Sujato's comment.
pāragataṁ — “one who has crossed to the further shore” — a standing epithet for an arahant.
cattavaṇṇaṁ...byāhare — “praise oneself among the assemblies” — the conduct prohibited in the first verse's closing line.
susukhumanipuṇatthadassinā — “seeing a meaning so subtle and fine” — describing the insight named in the second verse.
matikusalena — “skilled in thought” — one of the qualities the second verse names as making extinguishment not hard to reach.

The text

The text in full: two verses, without a translated attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1One should not suspend others from the Saṅgha, nor raise objections against them; and neither disparage nor raise one’s voice against one who has crossed to the further shore. One should not praise oneself among the assemblies, not restless, measured in speech, and true to your vows.

§2For one who sees the meaning so very subtle and fine, who is skilled in thought and placid in manner, who has cultivated mature ethics—it’s not hard to gain extinguishment.
