Theragatha · 2.24

Valliya (3rd)

Valliya — The Book of the Twos · Chapter Three · Poem 4 of 10

Why this text

Valliya vows to do the duty of one whose energy is strong and who longs to wake up, without fail — then turns to address a teacher directly: ‘teach me the path’, promising to know it with wisdom as surely as the Ganges knows the ocean.

Reading guide

A vow, stated with confidence

The first verse names ‘the duty of one whose energy is strong’ and ‘the duty of one who longs to wake up’, then commits to it outright: ‘that I'll do, I won't fail’, closing with a direct challenge — ‘see my energy and vigor!’

A request, not a declaration

The second verse shifts register entirely: rather than reporting an attainment already reached, Valliya directly addresses someone — presumably a teacher — asking to be taught ‘the direct route whose objective is freedom from death’. This poem is a petition, a rarer mode in this book than retrospective or declarative verses.

A simile of certainty, not yet of attainment

The poem closes with a simile — ‘I'll know it with wisdom, as the Ganges knows the ocean’ — expressing confidence in a future outcome, not reporting a result already reached. The image pictures inevitability (a river's flow reaching the sea) rather than completion.

Completing this collection's Valliya trilogy

This is the third and final monk named Valliya in the Theragātha: Valliya (1st) at Thag 1.53 addressed a rain god from a literal hut; Valliya (2nd) at Thag 2.3 pictured a monkey caught by wisdom at a five-doored hut; this poem, Valliya (3rd), abandons hut imagery altogether for a direct vow and request — the most different in register of the three.

Key terms

daḷhavīriyena — “whose energy is strong” — the quality named in the first verse's opening duty.
boddhumicchatā — “who longs to wake up” — the second duty named alongside strong energy.
maggamakkhāhi — “teach me the path” — the direct request opening the second verse.
añjasaṁ amatogadhaṁ — “the direct route whose objective is freedom from death” — what Valliya asks to be taught, a phrase that recurs later in this same chapter.
gaṅgāsotova sāgaran — “as the Ganges knows the ocean” — the poem's closing simile of confident, inevitable arrival.

The text

The text in full: two verses, without a translated attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1The duty of one whose energy is strong; the duty of one who longs to wake up: that I’ll do, I won’t fail—see my energy and vigor!

§2Teach me the path, the direct route whose objective is freedom from death. I’ll know it with wisdom, as the Ganges knows the ocean.
