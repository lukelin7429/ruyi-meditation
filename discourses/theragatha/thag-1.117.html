Theragatha · 1.117

Yasa

Yasa — The Book of the Ones · Chapter Twelve · Poem 7 of 10

Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — plain vocabulary, one striking juxtaposition

Why this text

Yasa opens describing himself ‘well-anointed and well-dressed, adorned with all my jewellery’ — then, with no transition at all, declares he has ‘attained the three knowledges and fulfilled the Buddha's instructions’. The verse never explains how one half connects to the other.

Reading guide

Luxury and liberation, side by side

Suvilitto suvasano, sabbābharaṇabhūsito — ‘well-anointed and well-dressed, adorned with all my jewellery’ — describes a state of lavish personal ornamentation. The very next line moves directly to tisso vijjā ajjhagamiṁ, ‘I've attained the three knowledges’, with nothing bridging the two halves — no account of leaving the luxury behind, no narrative of transformation, just the two states placed directly next to each other.

A name matching a famous conversion story

The wider Vinaya tradition tells of a wealthy young man named Yasa from Vārāṇasī who, disgusted one night by the sight of his own sleeping household, fled into the forest and became the sixth person to attain arahantship under the Buddha — a famous early conversion story built entirely on the contrast between opulent lay life and sudden renunciation. No comment survives to confirm this verse describes that same Yasa, but the specific shape of the contrast — luxury immediately followed by attainment, with the same jarring absence of transition — fits the story's own shape closely enough that this reading guide treats the connection as suggestive, while still not asserting it as confirmed.

A variant on the collection's familiar formula

Tisso vijjā ajjhagamiṁ, ‘I reached the three knowledges’, uses ajjhagamiṁ (‘I reached’) rather than anuppattā (‘attained’), the verb this collection's much more frequently repeated formula uses (Thag 1.24, 1.55, 1.66, 1.107, 1.108). Same basic claim, different verb — a variant rather than an exact repeat.

Key terms

suvilitta — “well-anointed” — describing perfumed or oiled skin, a mark of luxury.
sabbābharaṇabhūsita — “adorned with all ornaments” — sabba (‘all’) plus ābharaṇa (‘ornament’) plus bhūsita (‘adorned’).
tisso vijjā — “the three knowledges” — the same standard set named in Thag 1.107 and elsewhere.
ajjhagama — “reached, arrived at” — a variant verb for the same claim usually phrased with anuppatta.
Yasa — “glory, renown” — also the name of a wealthy young man's famous conversion story in the wider Vinaya tradition.

The text

The text in full: one verse, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1I’m well-anointed and well-dressed, adorned with all my jewellery. I’ve attained the three knowledges and fulfilled the Buddha’s instructions.
