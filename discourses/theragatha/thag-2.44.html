Theragatha · 2.44

Mogharāja

Mogharāja — The Book of the Twos · Chapter Five · Poem 4 of 9

Setting
A dialogue, addressed to Mogharāja by name, without a translated attribution
Speaker
An unnamed questioner, then Mogharāja himself in reply
Form
Two four-line verses — a concerned question, then a contented answer
Length
under 1 minute to read
Northern parallel
This reading guide does not assert a specific matching text for this poem in other Buddhist literatures.
Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — plain, conversational language

Why this text

An unnamed voice asks Mogharāja how he will survive the dark, cold nights of winter, given his rough appearance but good heart and constant absorption — and Mogharāja answers not with complaint, but with plain contentment: he'll make his bed under a thatched roof in grain-rich Magadha, ‘like those who live in comfort’.

Reading guide

Addressed by name, directly

The first verse speaks to Mogharāja by name and by description — ‘your skin is nasty but your heart is good’, ‘always immersed in samādhi’ — before raising a practical worry: how will he get by in the cold, dark nights of winter? This direct address by name recalls the collection's earlier instances of one person addressing another directly, such as Thag 1.83's address to Sīha in the Book of the Ones.

An answer in contentment, not complaint

The second verse doesn't deny the hardship — it simply answers it: he's heard the Magadhans have abundant grain, and he'll make his bed under a thatched roof ‘like those who live in comfort’. No claim of hardened endurance, no supernatural comfort — just a plain, practical answer to a plain question.

A name that may match this site's own Sutta Nipāta

This site's Sutta Nipāta collection already includes Snp 5.16, The Questions of Mogharāja — the fifteenth of Bāvarī's sixteen student-brahmins, who asks the Buddha how to view the world so the King of Death does not see him, and receives the answer ‘look upon the world as empty’. No surviving comment on this poem confirms the identification, but Mogharāja is a distinctive enough name — unlike common names such as Kassapa or Tissa — that this reading guide treats the two as plausibly the same person, in the same spirit as its treatment of Nanda and Upavāṇa earlier in this book.

Key terms

chavipāpaka cittabhaddaka — “your skin is nasty but your heart is good” — the opening compliment-with-caveat addressed to Mogharāja.
satataṁ samāhito — “always immersed in samādhi” — how Mogharāja is described.
hemantikasītakālarattiyo — “the dark, cold nights of winter” — the practical worry raised in the first verse.
sampannasassā — “abundant with grain” — describing Magadha, in the answer.
pālālacchannako — “a bed under a thatched roof” — Mogharāja's plain, contented answer.

The text

The text in full: two verses, without a translated attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1“Your skin is nasty but your heart is good; Mogharāja, you’re always immersed in samādhi. But in the nights of winter, so dark and cold, how will you get by, monk?”

§2“I’ve heard that all the Magadhans have an abundance of grain. I’ll make my bed under a thatched roof, just like those who live in comfort.”
