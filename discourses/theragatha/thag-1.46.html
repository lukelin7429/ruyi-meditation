Why this text

Samiddhi's verse opens quietly — faith, growing mindfulness and wisdom, a serene mind — before pivoting sharply: ‘make whatever illusions you want, it doesn't bother me’, a direct challenge to an unnamed adversary, never named outright.

Reading guide

A calm report, then a sudden pivot to defiance

The verse's first four lines read as a settled, unremarkable self-report — faith, growth, serenity. Its final two lines shift register entirely, addressing someone directly with an open challenge, ‘make whatever illusions you want’.

An adversary implied, not named

Unlike Nandiya's verse (Thag 1.25), which addresses Māra directly as ‘Dark One’, this verse's ‘you’ is never named — the conjuring of rūpāni, illusory forms or apparitions, strongly implies the same adversary without stating it outright.

Illusion specifically named as the threat

Rūpāni, ‘forms’ or ‘illusions’, names the specific kind of attack being dismissed — not physical harm or argument, but conjured appearances meant to disturb, declared powerless against Samiddhi's serenity.

Key terms

saddhā — “faith” — the stated reason Samiddhi went forth.
vuḍḍha — “grown” or “increased” — describing his mindfulness and wisdom.
susamāhita — “well immersed” or “serene” — describing his mind before the verse's pivot.
rūpa — “form” or “illusion” — the specific kind of conjured attack this verse dismisses.
byādhayissasi — “you will afflict” — the verb this verse denies has any power over Samiddhi.

The text

The text in full: one verse, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1I went forth out of faith from the lay life to homelessness. My mindfulness and wisdom have grown, my mind is serene. Make whatever illusions you want, it doesn’t bother me.
