Why this text

Brahmāli's poem asks whose faculties have become serene, like horses tamed by a charioteer, envied even by the gods — then answers with the very same lines, turned from ‘whose’ to ‘mine’: this collection's second poem built from a question and its own mirrored answer, after Thag 2.36 (Nitaka) earlier in this book.

Reading guide

A question, answered in its own words

The first verse asks: ‘whose faculties have become serene, like horses tamed by a charioteer? With conceit and defilements given up, who is such as envied by even the gods?’ The second verse answers with the identical lines, only the pronoun changed from ‘whose’ (kassa) to ‘mine’ (mayhaṁ) — the same device found at Thag 2.36, there built around a mind ‘like a rock’ rather than tamed horses.

A closing particle, appearing twice

Thag 2.36's question and answer form one continuous quotation, its closing quote-particle (iti) appearing only once, at the very end. This poem's Pali text carries that same closing particle a second time, already at the end of the first verse (tādinoti) as well as at the end of the second — a textual detail this reading guide notes without claiming to resolve what it implies about how the two verses were originally meant to be heard.

Horses tamed, an image used differently before

This collection has used horse imagery before — Thag 1.16 and 1.45 (Book of the Ones) each compare a person to ‘a good thoroughbred’ in opposite narrative directions. This poem's image is more specific still: not a horse's own character, but horses ‘tamed by a charioteer’, a formal simile (sārathi) not used in either earlier instance.

Key terms

indriyāni — “faculties” — what the poem describes as become serene.
samathaṅgatāni — “become serene, calmed” — the state the faculties are said to have reached.
sārathinā sudantā — “tamed by a charioteer” — the simile for the faculties' taming.
pahīnamānassa anāsavassa — “with conceit and defilements given up” — the condition described in both verses.
tādino — “such a one” — a standing epithet in the early canon for one who is unmoved, unaffected, steady.

The text

The text in full: two verses, without a translated attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Whose faculties have become serene, like horses tamed by a charioteer? With conceit and defilements given up, who is such as envied by even the gods?

§2My faculties have become serene, like horses tamed by a charioteer. With conceit and defilements given up, I am such as envied by even the gods.
