Theragatha · 1.14

The Novice Sīvaka

Sīvaka — The Book of the Ones · Chapter Two · Poem 4 of 10

Setting

Speaker

Length
under 1 minute to read

Difficulty
★☆☆☆☆ — a quoted exchange, a striking split image, and a closing aphorism

Why this text

Sīvaka's mentor tells him, ‘let's leave here, Sīvaka’ — and Sīvaka answers not with agreement or refusal but with a striking image: his body still lives in the village, but his mind has already gone to the wilderness, present there even when he is lying down. ‘You can't chain those who know’, the verse closes.

Reading guide

A mentor's own words, quoted directly

Unlike most poems in this chapter, this verse opens by quoting another person's speech directly: ‘my mentor said to me: “let's leave here, Sīvaka”’. Sīvaka's own verse becomes his answer to that remark, rather than a self-contained statement.

A body in one place, a mind in another

‘My body lives in the village, but my mind has gone to the wilderness’ separates physical location from mental location entirely — and the next line extends this further: ‘I go there even when lying down’, meaning the mind's presence in the wilderness does not depend on active effort or waking attention.

The collection's first novice, not yet an elder

This poem's own file title identifies Sīvaka as a sāmaṇera, a novice not yet fully ordained — distinct from the thera (‘senior’) title carried by every other named speaker encountered so far in this collection.

A personal answer, closing as a general maxim

The verse's final line, ‘you can't chain those who know’, shifts from Sīvaka's own particular situation to a general statement about anyone who has come to understand — his mentor's remark answered, in the end, with a principle rather than a plain yes or no.

Key terms

upajjhāya — “preceptor” or “mentor” — the one who speaks the verse's opening line, prompting Sīvaka's answer.
sāmaṇera — “novice” — Sīvaka's own status, per this poem's file title, distinct from the thera title used elsewhere in this collection.
gāma — “village” — where Sīvaka says his body still lives.
arañña — “wilderness” or “forest” — where Sīvaka says his mind has already gone.
na bandhanti — “they cannot chain” — the verse's closing verb, naming what cannot be done to ‘those who know’.

The text

The text in full: one verse, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1My mentor said to me: “Let’s leave here, Sīvaka.” My body lives in the village, but my mind has gone to the wilderness. I go there even when lying down—you can't chain those who know.
