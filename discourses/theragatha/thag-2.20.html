Why this text

Sirima closes Chapter Two with a mirrored pair of verses: if others praise someone with no immersion, they praise in vain; if others criticize someone who does have immersion, they criticize in vain — in both cases the actual presence or absence of immersion is what matters, not the opinion offered.

Reading guide

A mirrored pair: praise, then blame

The two verses run in exact parallel structure — the first verse states that praise given to someone ‘with no immersion’ is void, since ‘one has no immersion’ regardless of what others say; the second verse reverses every term, applying the identical logic to criticism aimed at someone who ‘does have immersion’.

Immersion as the only relevant fact

Both verses converge on the same point from opposite directions: external opinion, whether praise or blame, is ‘moghaṁ’, ‘in vain’ or ‘futile’, because it doesn't change the actual presence or absence of samādhi. The self's own state is the only fact that matters.

A different angle again on external judgment

This is the fourth poem in the Book of the Twos so far to take up how external opinion or gain relates to actual attainment (after Thag 2.2, 2.5, and 2.17 on honor and gains) — but where those poems warned against honor as a danger or a misallocated resource, this poem takes a more detached view: praise and blame alike are simply void, neither harmful nor beneficial, when measured against what's actually true.

A chapter's own closing line, left untranslated

Immediately after this poem, the Pali carries vaggo dutiyo, ‘the second chapter is finished’ — followed by this chapter's own uddāna, naming all ten monks in sequence: Cunda (an abbreviation of Mahācunda), Jotidāsa, Heraññakāni, Somamitta, Sabbamitta, Kāḷa (an abbreviation of Mahākāḷa), Tissa, Kimila (Kimbila), Nanda, and Sirimā — closing with the exact same collective epithet already seen closing Chapter One: ‘dasa therā mahiddhikā’, ‘these ten senior monks of great power’, word for word identical to Chapter One's own closing line. This mnemonic is absent from Sujato's translation and not included in this page's text.

Key terms

asamāhito — “with no immersion” — the condition of the person praised in vain in the first verse.
susamāhito — “with immersion” — the reversed condition of the person criticized in vain in the second verse.
moghaṁ — “in vain” or “futile” — the shared verdict both verses reach about external opinion.
samādhi — “immersion” — the actual fact both verses treat as the only thing that matters, regardless of what others say.
vaggo — “chapter” — the structural unit this poem closes, marked in the untranslated Pali colophon ‘vaggo dutiyo’, ‘the second chapter is finished’.

The text

The text in full: two mirrored verses, without a translated attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1If others praise one who has no immersion, they praise in vain, as one has no immersion.

§2If others criticize one who does have immersion, they criticize in vain, as one does have immersion.
