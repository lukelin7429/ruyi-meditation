Why this text

‘Just as a mother would be good to her beloved only child, so, to creatures all and everywhere, let one be good’ — the same core image, and the term ekaputta, ‘only child’, found in this site's own translation of the Discourse on Love (Khuddakapatha, Kp 9).

Reading guide

A shared image with this site's Discourse on Love

This verse's mother-and-only-child image shares its central term, ekaputta, with the Khuddakapatha's Kp 9, the Discourse on Love — though the wording is not identical between the two, the same core image of total, protective care extended toward one child is common to both.

From the narrowest case to the widest possible scope

The verse's structure moves in one direction: starting from the most intimate, particular relationship imaginable — a mother and her only child — and extending the same quality outward to ‘all and everywhere’, the broadest scope a sentence could name.

Goodness named as a skill, not only a feeling

The verb kusalī siyā, translated ‘would be good’, carries the sense of skillfulness as much as warmth — goodness toward others framed partly as something practiced and developed, not purely a spontaneous emotion.

Key terms

ekaputta — “only child” — the term shared with this site's own translation of the Discourse on Love (Kp 9).
piya — “beloved” or “dear” — describing the child in this verse's opening image.
kusalī — “good” or “skillful” — the specific quality this verse extends from one child to all beings.
pāṇa — “creature” or “living being” — the object of this verse's universal extension.
sabbattha — “everywhere” — the verse's closing word, naming its widest possible scope.

The text

The text in full: one verse, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Just as a mother would be good to her beloved only child; so, to creatures all and everywhere, let one be good.
