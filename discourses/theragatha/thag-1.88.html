Why this text

Ajjuna describes lifting himself from water to shore while ‘being swept away by the great flood’ — and in that same moment, still being swept along, he says he ‘penetrated the truths’. The insight doesn't come after the crisis is over; the verse places it in the middle of it.

Reading guide

Insight in the middle of the flood, not after

Vuyhamāno mahogheva, saccāni paṭivijjhahaṁ: ‘while being swept along by the great flood, I penetrated the truths’. Vuyhamāno is a present participle — the sweeping is still happening, ongoing, at the moment the verse describes the breakthrough. This is a different temporal structure from a more conventional telling, where crisis would come first, followed by escape, followed by insight: here the insight and the peril coincide.

Ogha, a standard image for what sweeps beings along

Ogha (‘flood’) is one of the wider canon's standard images for what carries beings through saṁsāra — traditionally enumerated as four: the floods of sensuality, of continued existence, of views, and of ignorance. This verse doesn't specify which, or how many; it simply names ‘the great flood’ as the thing being escaped.

'The truths', left unspecified

Saccāni (‘truths’) most likely points toward the four noble truths, the canon's most common referent for this word in the plural — but the verse itself doesn't say ‘four’, or name them individually. This reading guide treats the likely referent as background, not something the verse itself confirms.

A name that may mean 'bright, white'

Ajjuna can also function as an ordinary adjective meaning ‘white, bright, clear’ — it is also the name of a tree valued for its pale bark. Whether this elder's name draws on that sense, this reading guide does not press further.

Key terms

ogha — “flood” — a standard canonical image for what sweeps beings through saṁsāra, traditionally counted as four.
vuyhamāna — “being swept away” — a present passive participle, describing an action still underway.
paṭivijjhati — “penetrates, breaks through to understanding” — the verb behind this verse's central claim.
sacca — “truth” — here in the plural, most likely pointing toward the four noble truths, though the verse does not name them individually.
Ajjuna — possibly “white, bright, clear” — also the name of a tree valued for its pale bark.

The text

The text in full: one verse, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1I was able to lift myself up from the water to the shore. While being swept away by the great flood, I penetrated the truths.
