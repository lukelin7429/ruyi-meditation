Why this text

Puṇṇamāsa gives up the five hindrances for the sake of sanctuary from the yoke, then takes the Dhamma itself as a mirror for knowing and seeing himself, examining his own body inside and out until it appears hollow — the identical closing image used one poem earlier, by Vītasoka, with a literal mirror instead of a figurative one.

Reading guide

Giving up the hindrances, for sanctuary

The first verse states plainly that the five hindrances have been given up, ‘for the sake of sanctuary from the yoke’ — yogakkhema, a term for safety from what binds one to continued existence.

The Dhamma itself as a mirror

The same verse then supplies this poem's central image: ‘I took Dhamma as a mirror, for knowing and seeing myself’. Where Thag 2.25 (Vītasoka) used a literal handheld mirror at the moment of a haircut, this poem makes the teaching itself the reflective surface.

The same closing image, one poem later

The second verse examines the body ‘all of it, inside and out’, closing with the identical phrase used to close Thag 2.25: ‘my body appeared hollow’. Two consecutive poems, two different mirrors — one literal, one the Dhamma itself — arriving at the same image for the body's nature.

A second Puṇṇamāsa, closing a book in each case

Puṇṇamāsa (1st), at Thag 1.10, closed Chapter One of the Book of the Ones. This second monk of the same name appears mid-chapter here rather than at a chapter's close, sharing only the name and a broadly similar register of settled, examined attainment.

Key terms

nīvaraṇe — “hindrances” — the five given up in this poem's opening line.
yogakkhemassa — “sanctuary from the yoke” — what giving up the hindrances is said to secure.
dhammādāsaṁ — “the Dhamma as a mirror” — this poem's central image, taking the teaching itself as a reflective surface.
santarabāhiraṁ — “inside and out” — describing the thoroughness of the body's examination in the second verse.
tuccho kāyo — “hollow body” — the identical closing image already used to close Thag 2.25, one poem earlier.

The text

The text in full: two verses, without a translated attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1I gave up the five hindrances for the sake of sanctuary from the yoke. I took Dhamma as a mirror for knowing and seeing myself.

§2I examined this body, all of it, inside and out. Internally and externally my body appeared hollow.
