Theragatha · 2.25

Vītasoka

Vītasoka — The Book of the Twos · Chapter Three · Poem 5 of 10

Difficulty
★★☆☆☆ — a vivid physical scene turning suddenly inward

Why this text

Vītasoka describes a barber approaching to shave his head; picking up a mirror to examine his body first, he sees it appear hollow, darkness lifting — the literal cutting of his hair becoming, in the same breath, the poem's image for no more future lives.

Reading guide

A barber, a mirror, and an unplanned insight

The first verse is entirely concrete: a barber approaches to shave Vītasoka's head, and before the shaving itself, he picks up a mirror and examines his own body. Nothing in the setup signals that insight is coming — the scene reads as an ordinary preparation for ordination.

The body seen hollow, and hair as a double image

The second verse turns without transition: the body ‘appeared hollow’, darkness disappears, and then ‘my fancy hairdo has been cut off’ — a line that works on two levels at once, the literal haircut about to happen and a figure for everything elaborate and self-adorning now cut away, closing on ‘no more future lives’.

A name shared with a figure in later Buddhist legend

The name Vītasoka, ‘free of sorrow’, is also borne by a figure in later Aśokan legend — the emperor Aśoka's own brother, in materials such as the Divyāvadāna. Nothing in this poem or in bilara-data connects the two, and this guide does not assert they are the same person; the name itself is simply attested in more than one Buddhist textual tradition.

Key terms

kappako — “barber” — the figure approaching to shave Vītasoka's head, opening the poem.
ādāsaṁ — “mirror” — picked up to examine the body before the literal shaving takes place.
tuccho kāyo — “hollow body” — what Vītasoka says appears to him, the turning point of the poem.
colā — “fancy hairdo” or elaborate adornment — cut off in the same line that closes on no more future lives.
punabbhavo — “future life” or renewed existence — denied outright in the poem's closing line.

The text

The text in full: two verses, without a translated attribution. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1The barber approached to shave my head. I picked up a mirror and examined my body.

§2My body appeared hollow; I once was blind, but the darkness disappeared. My fancy hairdo has been cut off: now there’ll be no more future lives.
