Why this text

Gosāla's verse reads almost like a plan for the day: eat honey and milk-rice at Veḷugumba, then, dexterously probing the rise and fall of the aggregates, return to his forest hill to foster seclusion — ordinary logistics and meditative practice folded into the same short itinerary.

Reading guide

Ordinary planning, voiced as verse

Where many verses in this collection describe an attainment or a teaching, this one states something closer to a plan: a specific meal at a specific place, then a specific destination — the stuff of daily monastic life, given the same verse form as loftier declarations.

A wordplay Sujato's note unpacks

‘Dexterously probing’ translates padakkhiṇaṁ sammasanto. Sujato's note explains that padakkhiṇaṁ is used here adverbially, its sense reinforcing the rare verb sammasanto through a shared root: dakkha means ‘right-handed, dexterous’, while mas means ‘stroke, probe’ — a deliberate echo built into the line's own sound.

Meditation folded into an itinerary

The contemplation itself — the rise and fall of the aggregates — is not set apart as a separate, formal occasion, but placed directly between a meal and a walk home, practice woven into the ordinary movement of a day.

Key terms

madhupāyasa — “honey and milk-rice” — the specific meal Gosāla names in this verse's opening line.
padakkhiṇaṁ — used adverbially here as “dexterously”, per Sujato's note reinforcing the following verb through a shared root.
sammasanto — “probing” or “contemplating” — a rare verb, per Sujato's note, describing how Gosāla examines the aggregates.
khandhānaṁ udayabbayaṁ — “the rise and fall of the aggregates” — the specific object of Gosāla's contemplation.
viveka — “seclusion” — what Gosāla says he will foster upon returning to his forest hill.

The text

The text in full: one verse, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1I’ll eat honey and milk-rice in Veḷugumba. And then, dexterously probing the rise and fall of the aggregates, I’ll return to my forest hill and foster seclusion.
