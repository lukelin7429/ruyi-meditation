Sumana (1st)

Sumana (1st) — The Book of the Fives · Chapter One · Poem 4 of 12

Why this text

Unlike most declarations in this collection, this poem speaks directly to someone: ‘with purified knowledge, free of doubt, I declare it in your presence’. Sumana reports his attainment to his own preceptor as a graduation report, closing with thanks — ‘your instruction was not in vain’.

Reading guide

Addressed throughout to one specific person

Where most of this collection's declarations speak to no one in particular, this poem is a direct report: ‘I declare it in your presence’, ‘I learned your teachings well’, ‘your instruction was not in vain’. Every verse keeps the preceptor (upajjhāya) in view as its addressee, making this one of this collection's clearest examples of a graduation report delivered to a specific teacher, rather than a general declaration or a report to family.

Attainment witnessed personally, not taken on faith

The second verse specifies how the Dhamma was realized: ‘witnessing it for myself, not based on hearsay’ (anītiho). Even addressed gratefully to a teacher, the poem insists the realization itself was personal and direct, not simply accepted secondhand.

Gratitude, closing the report

The final verse turns from declaration to thanks: ‘you advised me in noble observances; sympathetic, you helped teach me’, closing on ‘I, your pupil, am fully trained’ (antevāsimhi sikkhito). The relationship named throughout the poem — teacher and pupil — is honored explicitly at its very end, not left implicit.

Key terms

upajjhāyo — “mentor, preceptor” — who the poem addresses throughout.
sayaṁ dhammo anītiho — “the Dhamma... witnessing it for myself, not based on hearsay” — the personal, direct nature of the realization.
byākaromi tavantike — “I declare it in your presence” — the formal report made directly to the preceptor.
sabbe me āsavā khīṇā — “all my defilements are ended” — the declaration closing the fourth verse.
antevāsimhi sikkhito — “I, your pupil, am fully trained” — the poem's closing acknowledgment of the teacher-pupil relationship.

The text

The text in full: five verses, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1My mentor helped me to learn, hoping I would practice those teachings. Aspiring to freedom from death, I’ve done what had to be done.

§2I’ve realized the Dhamma, witnessing it for myself, not based on hearsay. With purified knowledge, free of doubt, I declare it in your presence.

§3I know my past lives, my clairvoyance is purified, I’ve realized my heart’s goal, and fulfilled the Buddha’s instructions.

§4Being diligent in the training, I learned your teachings well. All my defilements are ended; now there’ll be no more future lives.

§5You advised me in noble observances; sympathetic, you helped teach me. Your instruction was not in vain—I, your pupil, am fully trained.
