Why this text

For the second poem in a row, this verse is not first-person testimony but direct address — and unlike Thag 1.82, which spoke to an anonymous ‘child’, this one names its own listener within the verse itself: ‘Meditate diligently, Sīha’. Whoever is speaking urges him toward tireless practice, closing with a vivid instruction to ‘quickly discard this bag of bones’.

Reading guide

A verse that names its own listener

Sīhappamatto vihara, ‘live diligently, Sīha’, builds the addressee's name directly into the verse's opening compound — a different technique from Thag 1.82, which addressed an unnamed ‘child’ one poem earlier. Both verses share the same underlying structure, though: entirely second-person exhortation, with no narrative frame and no identification of who is speaking.

Whose voice is this?

This reading guide does not resolve who is urging Sīha on. Elsewhere in the wider canon, the Buddha frequently addresses individual monks directly by name in exactly this way, which would fit the pattern here — but the verse gives no attribution, and this reading guide does not assume it.

A vivid closing image, new to this collection

Jaha sīghaṁ samussayaṁ, ‘quickly discard this bag of bones’, uses samussaya (‘heap, aggregate’) for the body in a way not seen earlier in this collection — a blunt, physical image for letting go of the body itself, alongside this verse's more familiar call to ‘develop skillful qualities’.

A name shared with a well-known lay donor

Sīha (‘lion’) is also the name of General Sīha, a well-known figure already covered elsewhere on this site (AN 7.57), remembered in the wider canon for his generosity and his conversion from Jainism to the Buddha's teaching. Traditional accounts describe him remaining a lay donor rather than taking ordination, which argues against this being the same person — a more specific reason for caution than the usual ‘same name, no confirmation’, though still not a settled conclusion.

Key terms

appamatta — “diligent, heedful” — built into the verse's opening address to Sīha.
rattindivaṁ — “day and night” — ratti (‘night’) plus divā (‘day’), describing the pace of the practice urged.
bhāveti — “develops, cultivates” — the verb behind this verse's instruction to develop skillful qualities.
samussaya — “heap, aggregate” — here used for the body itself, in this verse's closing image.
Sīha — “lion” — also the name of a well-known lay donor covered elsewhere on this site (AN 7.57).

The text

The text in full: one verse, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1Meditate diligently, Sīha, tireless all day and night. Develop skillful qualities, and quickly discard this bag of bones.
