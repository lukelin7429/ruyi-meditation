Why this text

Vacchagotta declares himself ‘a master of the three knowledges’, ‘a great meditator’, and ‘an expert in serenity of heart’, having ‘realized my heart's goal’. His name itself removes an ambiguity that has followed this collection's earlier ‘Vaccha’ names: gotta, ‘clan’, attached directly to Vaccha, states the clan affiliation outright.

Reading guide

Three knowledges, a great meditator

Tevijjo (‘master of the three knowledges’), mahājhāyī (‘great meditator’), and cetosamathakovido (‘expert in serenity of heart’) build a compact, three-part self-description before the verse turns to its closing claim: sadattho me anuppatto, ‘I've realized my heart's own goal’. It is one of this collection's more standard testimony verses, without a distinctive image or narrative frame of its own.

A sixth Vaccha, and the first to remove the ambiguity

Vacchagotta is the sixth name in this collection built on the word vaccha, after Cūḷavaccha, Mahāvaccha, and Vanavaccha (1st) in Chapter Two, Ukkhepakatavaccha in Chapter Seven, and Vacchapāla in Chapter Eight. Unlike Vacchapāla, where this reading guide couldn't determine whether ‘vaccha’ meant the common noun ‘calf’ or the clan name, gotta (‘clan, lineage’) attached directly here removes any doubt: this elder is explicitly named as a member of the Vaccha clan.

A name shared with a famous questioner, but not a distinctive one

Vacchagotta is also the name most associated, throughout the wider canon, with a wanderer who repeatedly questioned the Buddha on unanswered points. But ‘Vacchagotta’ functions as a clan designation — ‘of the Vaccha clan’ — rather than a unique personal name, so this reading guide treats any specific identification with that wanderer as no more likely than the many other same-clan-name cases already noted in this collection.

Key terms

tevijja — “master of the three knowledges” — te (‘three’) plus vijjā (‘knowledge’).
mahājhāyī — “great meditator” — mahā (‘great’) plus jhāyī (‘one who meditates’).
cetosamathakovida — “expert in serenity of heart” — ceto (‘heart, mind’) plus samatha (‘serenity’) plus kovida (‘expert’).
sadattha — “one's own goal, the heart's goal” — sa (‘own’) plus attha (‘goal’).
gotta — “clan, lineage” — attached directly to Vaccha in this elder's name, removing the ambiguity seen in Thag 1.71 (Vacchapāla).

The text

The text in full: one verse, with no separate attribution line in this chapter. Translation: Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0, SuttaCentral).

§1I am a master of the three knowledges, I’m a great meditator, an expert in serenity of heart. I’ve realized my heart’s goal and fulfilled the Buddha’s instructions.
