The Complete Technical Definition

諦分別經 — 舍利弗把濃縮的四句話完整展開
Part 1

Lecture Notes

Back at the deer park, and a curious handoff

This discourse is set at the very place the wheel was first set rolling. The Buddha briefly restates the four noble truths in the same compressed form as Session 1 — and then withdraws, leaving Sāriputta to give their complete technical definition. This handoff itself is worth noting as a teaching choice: the founder's compressed statement and a senior disciple's full unpacking are both preserved as canonical, rather than the fuller version replacing or correcting the shorter one.

Suffering, defined completely

Where Session 1 named suffering in a single word, this discourse spells it out in full: birth, aging, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, distress, association with the disliked, separation from the liked, not getting what one wants — and, in the phrase that gathers all of it, "the five grasping aggregates are suffering." Each item is itself further defined; nothing is left for the listener to guess at.

The origin: craving in three forms

The second truth names one cause — craving — but specifies three directions it takes: craving for sensual pleasures, craving for continued existence, and, tellingly, craving for nonexistence. That third form matters: even the wish to simply stop existing is still, on this analysis, a form of craving, not an escape from it. The origin of suffering is not any particular desired object but the underlying momentum of relishing and grasping wherever it lands.

Cessation, and the path, factor by factor

Cessation is defined as that same craving's complete fading away, giving up, releasing, letting go of, and not relying on. The path is then given its full eight-factor definition — right view, thought, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, and immersion — each factor itself defined in technical detail, not merely listed by name. The discourse closes by noting that the wheel, once set rolling, continues to roll through exactly this kind of complete, careful unpacking.

Before you read the sutta itself
  • A compressed teaching and its full technical unpacking are both preserved, not one replacing the other. The Buddha's brief restatement and Sāriputta's complete definition sit side by side in the same discourse.
  • The second truth's third form of craving is easy to miss and important not to. Craving for nonexistence — the wish to simply stop — is still craving, not an exit from the mechanism this course has been tracing.
  • Nothing in the complete definition is left for the listener to guess at. Each item within suffering, origin, cessation, and the path is itself further defined, down to the individual factor.

Part 3

Discussion Questions

  1. Why might the tradition preserve both the compressed statement (Session 1) and the complete technical unpacking (this session) as equally canonical, rather than treating the fuller version as simply superior?
  2. "Craving for nonexistence" is a subtle and easy-to-miss category. Can you think of a case — your own, or one you've observed — where a wish to simply stop, disengage, or disappear functioned as a form of craving rather than as freedom from it?
  3. The discourse defines the five grasping aggregates as suffering itself, not merely a cause of it. What is the difference between "life causes suffering" and "the very process of grasping at experience, examined closely, is what suffering names"?
  4. The Buddha withdraws and lets Sāriputta teach in his place. What does this choice suggest about how the Buddha understood the difference between originating a teaching and being the only person qualified to fully explain it?
