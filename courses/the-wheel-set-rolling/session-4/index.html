Session 4 · The concrete payoff of actually seeing the four truths

A Fingernail of Dust

指甲經 — 已經結束的苦，跟還剩下的一點點
Part 1

Lecture Notes

A gesture, not just an image

Where similar discourses in this collection ask listeners to imagine a pond, a river, or a mountain, this one opens with something physically present in the moment: an actual speck of dirt on the Buddha's actual fingernail, held up against the whole earth. The comparison has a tangible, immediate quality the imagined versions don't quite share — you could, in principle, have been standing there watching him do it.

A term that names an entire way of being accomplished

"One who has comprehended" is the term this teaching turns on — a noble disciple accomplished in view is defined here precisely as someone for whom this comparison already holds true: almost all suffering over, only a trace remaining, the way a speck of dirt on a fingernail is nothing against the mass of the whole earth.

A specific number, not a vague reassurance

The remainder isn't left vague. It's given a precise upper bound — at most seven more lives — the standard technical description of a stream-enterer's remaining rebirths before final liberation. This is not poetic exaggeration standing in for "a little less suffering." It is a specific, bounded claim about how much remains.

Why this closes the course

Three sessions of this course have moved through announcement, complete technical definition, and deliberate restriction — all of it, so far, description of a mechanism. This final sutta answers the question those three sessions leave open: does actually seeing these four truths change anything, concretely, for a real person? The answer given is not "eventually, perhaps" but a specific, measurable claim — not a hundredth, not a thousandth, not a hundred-thousandth part of the suffering remains, once this is genuinely seen.

Before you read the sutta itself
  • Seeing the four truths is described as producing an actual, bounded change, not a vague improvement. "At most seven more lives" is a specific technical claim, not poetic reassurance.
  • The comparison is physically immediate, not merely imagined. A real speck of dirt on a real fingernail, visible in the moment — unlike the pond, river, or mountain similes used elsewhere in the same collection.
  • This session answers what the previous three left as description alone. Announcement, definition, and restriction all describe a mechanism; this sutta states, concretely, what happens when a person actually sees it.

Part 3

Discussion Questions

  1. Why might a real, physically present speck of dirt make a stronger teaching image here than an imagined pond or mountain would, for this specific claim?
  2. The remaining suffering is given a precise bound — seven more lives at most — rather than left as a vague "much less." What does the specificity add, pedagogically, that a vaguer reassurance wouldn't?
  3. Having read all four sessions of this course, would you now say the four noble truths are primarily a diagnosis, a technical system, a restricted curriculum, or a description of an achievable result? Defend your answer, or argue that it's genuinely more than one of these at once.
  4. This sutta measures progress in a way (fractions, precise rebirth counts) that may feel almost mathematical for a spiritual teaching. Does that precision make the claim more or less credible to you, and why?
