Session 2 · Following the conversation

At That Time, the World-Honored One

Session 1 got you into the room. Now you have to follow the conversation — and the canon gives you no quotation marks, no capital letters, and no verb tenses to do it with. What it gives you instead is a set of formulas that mark who spoke, in which direction, and what kind of move they were making.

時，長者須菩提在大眾中即從座起，偏褒右肩，
右膝著地，合掌恭敬而白佛言

Then the elder Subhūti rose from his seat in the assembly, bared his right shoulder, placed his right knee on the ground, joined his palms in reverence, and addressed the Buddha, saying…

Part 1

This is the next sentence

Last session you read the opening of the Diamond Sutra: the Buddha at Jeta's Grove with 1,250 monks. Two sentences later he has walked into Śrāvastī, begged his food, come back, washed his feet, and sat down. Then the sentence above happens, and the sutra proper begins.

We are not skipping around. Sessions 1, 2, and 3 walk straight down the opening of one text, in order, which is the only honest way to learn to read one.

Part 2

爾時 — the cut between shots

爾時 means at that time, and it does the work a paragraph break or a scene change does in English. It appears in 1,020 of the canon's 1,482 sutras — sixty-nine percent — and when you see it, the camera has moved.

It pairs almost always with 世尊, World-Honored One, which is what the texts call the Buddha when they are being formal about him. 佛 is his name in narration; 世尊 is how he is addressed and how the narrator refers to him at a solemn moment. Both are extremely common and they are not interchangeable in tone.

    Tap a character above.

    One practical note: 爾 ranks 116th in the canon and you will meet it almost nowhere except inside 爾時. Do not learn it as a word. Learn the pair.

    Part 3

    Speech has a directionThe most useful thing in this session

    Classical Chinese marks the social direction of speech in the verb itself, and the canon is rigid about it.

    A disciple speaking to the Buddha uses 白: 白佛言, addressed the Buddha, saying. The ordinary meaning of 白 is the color white; here it is a verb, and it means to report upward to someone senior. It occurs 13,381 times across 747 sutras.

    The Buddha speaking to a disciple uses 告: 佛告須菩提, the Buddha told Subhūti. It occurs 13,090 times across 740 sutras — almost exactly as often, which makes sense, since scripture is mostly people taking turns.

    The two are never swapped. A monk does not 告 the Buddha. So when you find either verb you immediately know which way the sentence is pointing, before you have understood a single other word in it.

      Tap a character above.

      言 closes the formula. It sits at the end — 白佛言, 佛告須菩提…言 — and everything after it is the quotation. That is your quotation mark.

      Part 4

      The body of respect

      Before Subhūti says anything he does five things: 從座起 rises from his seat, 偏褒右肩 bares his right shoulder, 右膝著地 puts his right knee to the ground, 合掌 joins his palms, 恭敬 reverently. Then he speaks.

      Notice the ranks. 偏 is 1,184th in the canon. 褒 is 1,436th. 肩 is 1,101st, 膝 1,034th. These are genuinely rare characters — and you have just met all four in a single clause.

      This is the same lesson as the garden in Session 1, and it is the central skill of the course, so here it is as a rule:

      When rare characters cluster, you are looking at a block

      Scripture's rarest characters almost never appear alone. They arrive in fixed strings — a place name, a gesture, a title, a transliterated Sanskrit word — that recur unchanged across hundreds of texts. Recognize the string; do not drill the characters. Your four hundred characters are spent on the connective tissue, not on these.

        Tap a character above.

        Part 5

        何以故 — the canon interrogates itself

        Buddhist scripture argues, and it argues by asking itself questions. Three connectives carry almost all of that structure, and once you can see them the shape of an argument appears on the page even where the content is still beyond you.

        ConnectiveForceTimesSutras
        何以故Why is that? — a question the text then answers itself25,706457
        是故Therefore — the conclusion is arriving19,513764
        復次Furthermore — a new point, the canon's paragraph break18,551489

        何以故 is not a rhetorical flourish. It is a structural signal: the sentence before it was a claim, and the sentence after it is the reason. In the Diamond Sutra it appears again and again, and each time it tells you the text is about to undercut what it just said.

          Part 6

          A complete exchangeFour turns, in the order the text gives them

          Here is Subhūti's question and the Buddha's reply, in sequence. Read it for the traffic pattern first — who is speaking to whom — and only then for the content. Watch the speech verb change on every turn, and then vanish.

          Turn 1 · Subhūti asks · upward

          而白佛言：「希有世尊！如來善護念諸菩薩，善付囑諸菩薩。世尊！善男子、善女人，發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，應云何住？云何降伏其心？」

          …and addressed the Buddha, saying: "How rare, World-Honored One! The Thus-Come One well watches over the bodhisattvas, well entrusts the bodhisattvas. World-Honored One — a good man or good woman who has set their mind on unsurpassed perfect awakening: how should they abide? How should they subdue their mind?"

          Turn 2 · the Buddha approves · bare 佛言, no addressee

          佛言：「善哉，善哉！須菩提！如汝所說：『如來善護念諸菩薩，善付囑諸菩薩。』汝今諦聽，當為汝說。」

          The Buddha said: "Excellent, excellent! Subhūti! It is as you say — the Thus-Come One well watches over the bodhisattvas, well entrusts the bodhisattvas. Listen closely now, and I will tell you." Here the verb is the bare 佛言 with nobody named: he is answering, not initiating.

          Turn 3 · Subhūti assents · no speech verb at all

          「唯然。世尊！願樂欲聞。」

          "Just so. World-Honored One! Gladly would we hear it." Seven characters, and nothing marks who is speaking. The canon drops the formula once the turn-taking is established, exactly as English drops "he said" in a fast exchange. The vocative 世尊 is the only clue, and it is enough — only a disciple would use it.

          Turn 4 · the Buddha begins · downward

          佛告須菩提：「諸菩薩摩訶薩應如是降伏其心…」

          The Buddha told Subhūti: "The bodhisattva-mahāsattvas should subdue their mind thus…" The verb returns, and with an addressee, because a new movement is starting.

          Four turns, four different ways of marking speech: 白佛言 going up, bare 佛言 answering, nothing at all, then 佛告 going back down. In Classical Chinese scripture the speech verb is the punctuation.

          Part 7

          Check yourself

          爾時世尊告阿難言：「此是無量壽佛極樂世界。」

          "At that time the World-Honored One addressed Ānanda, saying: 'This is the Land of Ultimate Bliss of the Buddha of Immeasurable Life.'"

          Ratnakūṭa Sutra 大寶積經, T0310. The complete downward formula: 爾時 scene change, 世尊 speaker, 告 direction, 阿難 addressee, 言 quotation opens. 世尊告 alone occurs 4,002 times across 439 sutras. The next sentence in the original tells Ānanda to rise and 合掌恭敬 — the gesture block from Part 4.

          爾時，須菩提白佛言：「世尊！當何名此經？我等云何奉持？」佛告須菩提…

          "At that time Subhūti addressed the Buddha, saying: 'World-Honored One! What should this sutra be called? How are we to uphold it?' The Buddha told Subhūti…"

          Both directions in one passage, from the Diamond Sutra itself: 白 going up, then 告 coming back down. Subhūti is asking what to name the text he is sitting inside. Note the two question words — 當何名 what should it be called, 云何 in what manner — both built on 何.

          何以故？若取非法相，即著我、人、眾生、壽者。是故不應取法，不應取非法。

          "Why is that? If one grasps the mark of non-dharma, one is attached to self, to person, to being, to life span. Therefore one should not grasp at dharmas, nor grasp at non-dharmas."

          Both connectives doing their jobs in one breath: 何以故 opens the reason, 是故 lands the conclusion. And 非 appears twice — the third negation, which is Session 3's business. Diamond Sutra, T0235.

          Session 3 — The Grammar of Not

          You can now get into a sutra and follow who is talking. What they are actually saying is mostly negative — and that is not a figure of speech. The most common character in the entire Buddhist canon is 無, there is not. The second is 不. Together they account for one character in every twenty-seven.

          Running total after Session 2: 48 characters, 9 formulas, and the opening of the Diamond Sutra read in the original.

          Where the numbers come from

          Every frequency on this page was counted, not estimated. The corpus is the 1,482 texts tagged 三藏/經 in the Taishō canon — 26,422,454 Chinese characters after stripping front matter, headings, and the CBETA apparatus. Ranks are positions in the frequency list built from that corpus; formula counts are string occurrences, given with the number of separate sutras they appear in, because a formula used 10,000 times in one text is a quirk while one used in 700 texts is a convention.

          Counting scripture separately from commentary matters. Run the same analysis over a corpus that includes the śāstras and the vocabulary tilts hard toward Abhidharma technical terms, and the character list ends up teaching you to read treatises rather than sutras. This course teaches sutras first. On how Ru-Yi translates and why, see Translation Method.
