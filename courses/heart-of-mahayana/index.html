The Heart of the Mahāyāna

大乘之心 — the great vehicle's own words, starting with its shortest and most essential text
Pilot · Session 1 live Beginner · Collected Topics recommended Taught in English Free

Where The Buddha's Own Words reads the earliest layer of scripture, this course reads the Mahāyāna's own — the sūtras that shaped Buddhism across China, Japan, Korea, Tibet, and Vietnam. Same format: lecture notes, primary text, discussion, assignment — but the texts themselves are stranger, denser, and, in places, deliberately built to break ordinary logic before rebuilding it on more honest ground.

We start with the shortest scripture in the entire Mahāyāna canon and, arguably, its single most important text: the Heart Sūtra — Prajñāpāramitā-hṛdaya, "the heart of the Perfection of Wisdom." At roughly 260 Chinese characters, it is short enough to memorize in an afternoon and dense enough to spend a lifetime unpacking.

A note on the text: the full bilingual translation, reading guide, and self-check quiz for the Heart Sūtra already exist as their own dedicated page in Ru-Yi's Mahāyāna Sūtras collection — Xuanzang's 649 CE Chinese text (public domain) alongside an original English translation. This course session doesn't repeat that page; it wraps a class session around it.

What you'll be able to do
  1. Read the Heart Sūtra's wall of negations without the two classic misreadings — nihilism ("nothing exists") and word-games — landing instead on what the text actually claims.
  2. Apply the negation framework from Collected Topics Session 5 to a real, load-bearing scriptural text — not just a logic exercise, but a genuine reading tool.
  3. Explain the line "form is emptiness, emptiness is form" accurately, including why the sūtra insists on running the equation in both directions.
  4. (As later sessions open) move from the Heart Sūtra's negations into the Diamond Sūtra's method and the path of the bodhisattva more broadly.
