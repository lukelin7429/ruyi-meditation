Session 3 · The same claim, made visible rather than argued

A Lump of Foam

聚沫經 — 五個譬喻，五蘊各自一個
Part 1

Lecture Notes

Five images, one method

Each of the five similes in this discourse follows an identical structure: something that looks substantial from a distance turns out, on close and rational examination, to be completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial — and the corresponding aggregate is said to be the same, examined the same way. Where Session 2 argued for this conclusion logically, this discourse simply shows it.

Foam, a bubble, a mirage

Form is like a lump of foam carried on the Ganges — bulky in appearance, empty on inspection. Feeling is like a bubble that forms and pops right away in autumn rain — momentary by nature, gone before it can be held. Perception is like a shimmering noon mirage in the hot season — convincing at a distance, nothing up close.

A banana trunk, and a magic trick

Choices are like a banana trunk: someone searching for heartwood cuts one down, unrolls its coiled sheaths layer by layer, and finds no sapwood, let alone heartwood — only more layers, all the way through. Consciousness is like a magic trick performed at a crossroads — convincing in the moment of display, with nothing behind it once examined. The set moves from the visibly composite (foam, a bubble) to the actively deceptive (a mirage, an illusion) — five different ways of being not what they first appear.

From description to urgency

The prose closes into verse, restating all five similes in a single compressed stanza, then turns to what happens when vitality, warmth, and consciousness leave the body — it lies there, "food for others, insentient," called "this illusion, this lament of fools," since "no core is found here." The verses end not with more description but with practical urgency: examine the aggregates by day or by night, give up all fetters, and live as though your head were on fire, aspiring to the state that does not pass.

Before you read the sutta itself
  • Five aggregates, five distinct images, one identical method. Each simile shows something that looks substantial from a distance and turns out hollow on close, rational examination — not merely asserted, but demonstrated by the image itself.
  • The set moves from the visibly composite to the actively deceptive. Foam and a bubble are obviously insubstantial; a mirage and a magic trick actively fool the eye — a deliberate escalation across the five images.
  • The discourse ends in urgency, not just description. "Live as though your head were on fire" turns five images of insubstantiality into a direct, practical spur, not a resting point for contemplation alone.

Part 3

Discussion Questions

  1. Which of the five similes lands most vividly for you personally, and why? Does it correspond to the aggregate you find hardest to see clearly in your own experience?
  2. The banana-trunk image for choices (saṅkhārā) is unusual: you don't find nothing at the center, you find more layers, all the way through. How is "more layers, no heartwood" different from simple emptiness or absence?
  3. The verses close with "no core is found here" and an image of the body after death as "food for others, insentient." Why might the discourse choose to end on this particular image rather than a gentler summary?
  4. Compare this session's method (five vivid images) to Session 2's method (a step-by-step logical argument) for reaching the same conclusion about the five aggregates. Which is more persuasive to you, and does the tradition need both, or would one alone suffice?

Option A — Find your own image

For one aggregate not covered as vividly as you'd like by this sutta's five images, invent your own comparable image — something that looks substantial from a distance and turns out hollow on close examination — drawn from your own life rather than the ancient world.

Option B — From description to urgency

The verses turn from calm description to "live as though your head were on fire." Write half a page on what that shift in register does for you as a reader, and whether your own understanding of the five aggregates currently sits more on the descriptive or the urgent side of that line.
