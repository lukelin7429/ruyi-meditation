Session 4 · The honest, lived difficulty of actually absorbing any of this

With Channa

闡陀經 — 道理都懂，心裡卻不安
Part 1

Lecture Notes

Intellectual agreement without settled confidence

This session closes the course somewhere the previous three don't go: inside the actual experience of trying to absorb what has been argued and shown. The monk Channa reports that he can follow the reasoning — form, feeling, perception, choices, and consciousness are impermanent, and there is no self or anything belonging to a self in any of them — and yet, hearing this, "my mind does not launch out, gain confidence, settle, or become decided." Understanding the argument and being settled by it turn out to be two different things.

Anxiety circling back to a single unanswerable question

Channa names precisely what happens instead: "because of anxiety, grasping arises and keeps turning back to the thought: 'so who then is my self?'" This is worth taking seriously as an honest report, not a failure to be corrected quickly. Channa himself recognizes this reaction as diagnostic — "that doesn't happen for someone who sees the teaching" — and so goes looking specifically for someone who can help him actually see it, not merely restate it to him again.

Seeking out a specific teacher, by trust rather than seniority

Channa doesn't approach just anyone. He goes looking for a particular kind of help, and finds it in Ānanda — chosen not for institutional seniority but for trust that this specific person can actually help. This detail matters for this course's own pedagogy: understanding the three marks, this session suggests, may require not just better arguments but the right person to sit with while the anxiety plays itself out.

A teaching heard directly from the Buddha, and the middle way it offers

Rather than composing a new response, Ānanda offers a teaching he himself heard directly from the Buddha, and its core move short-circuits the existence/non-existence anxiety directly: seeing the world's origin rightly rules out the concept of non-existence; seeing its cessation rightly rules out the concept of existence. What arises is just suffering arising; what ceases is just suffering ceasing — a knowledge independent of what anyone else says, avoiding both extremes through the middle way of dependent origination.

Before you read the sutta itself
  • Intellectual agreement and settled confidence are genuinely different things. Channa can follow every step of the reasoning from Sessions 1 through 3 and still find his mind unsettled — this course treats that gap honestly rather than pretending it away.
  • The anxious question "so who then is my self?" is itself diagnostic, not a personal failure. Channa recognizes his own reaction as a sign the teaching hasn't yet been fully seen — and responds by seeking help, not by forcing a resolution.
  • The resolution comes through the middle way, not through choosing a side. Neither "there is a self" nor "there is no self" — dependent origination sidesteps the existence/non-existence question entirely, tracking only what arises and what ceases.

Part 3

Discussion Questions

  1. Channa's honest report — full intellectual agreement, no settled confidence — may be more relatable than the instant freedom of Session 2's five ascetics. Which account feels closer to your own experience of trying to absorb a difficult truth: instant clarity, or an unsettled gap between understanding and confidence?
  2. Channa treats his own anxious circling as diagnostic rather than shameful, and responds by seeking a specific trusted person rather than more argument. Is there a difference, in your own experience, between what more reasoning can fix and what only a trusted person's presence can help settle?
  3. The resolution offered avoids both "there is a self" and "there is no self" as extremes, tracking only what arises and what ceases. Does this middle way actually resolve Channa's anxiety, in your reading, or does it simply relocate the question somewhere else?
  4. Having now completed all four sessions — the verse, the argument, the images, and this session's honest difficulty — what would you say is this course's single thread, and where does your own understanding currently sit along it?
