Teaching Like the Buddha

像佛陀一樣教書 — the suttas read not for their doctrine, but for how he taught it
7 sessions · all live No prior course required Taught in English Free · every text on this site

Every sutta in the Pali Canon is, among other things, a record of someone teaching someone. A village that trusts no one. A son young enough to need a water-pot, not an argument. A confident debater sent to win. A monk who got the doctrine backwards. A man threatening to quit unless his questions are answered. A murderer mid-chase. A community about to lose him for good. This course reads seven of those scenes for the method inside them, not only the doctrine.

Most courses on Buddhism ask what the Buddha taught. This one asks a narrower, more practical question, aimed at anyone who teaches for a living: watching him work, session by session, what can be learned about how to teach — when to give a method instead of an answer, when to sequence a hard idea behind three easy ones, when to refuse a question outright, and what a teacher owes a student after the teaching is over?

Seven scenes of him teaching, read the way an educator would read them — not for what he believed, but for what he did.

The promise of this course
Where the texts come from

All seven suttas are read in full on this site, in Bhikkhu Sujato's complete CC0 translation of the Pali Canon — freely quotable, with a structured reading guide, the full text, and a ten-question self-check quiz built into every one. This course adds a teaching-method lens on top; it does not retranslate anything.

What you'll be able to do
  1. Name at least seven distinct teaching moves the Buddha uses across these suttas — giving a testable method, sequencing a hard idea, refusing a question, reaching an "unreachable" student — and recognize them by name, not just by vague impression.
  2. Read a full Pali sutta for its pedagogy specifically, a skill most translations and commentaries don't teach you to look for.
  3. Apply at least one method from this course directly to your own teaching, in writing, by the end of each session's assignment.
  4. Trace one thread across all seven suttas — the same underlying commitment to a student's independence, from the first session's test to the last session's final words.
  5. Know where to go next in the Pali Canon, because you will have read closely in three of its four major nikāyas.

What you'll need
  • Nothing to buy. Every sutta is on this site in full, in Bhikkhu Sujato's CC0 translation, with its own reading guide and quiz.
  • No prior course required. This course stands on its own; Read a Whole Sūtra is a natural companion if you want more practice reading a full discourse slowly.
  • An actual teaching practice, if you have one. Every assignment asks you to apply the session's method to real students, a real classroom, or a real lesson — this course is built for people who teach, not only people who study.
  • A willingness to read past the famous line. Six of these seven suttas are known in popular culture for one quotable sentence, stripped of its context. This course reads the whole scene each sentence came from.
