Session 7 · The final lesson: teaching designed to outlast the teacher

Be Your Own Island

大般涅槃經 — 最後一課：教學怎麼在老師離開後繼續
Part 1

Lecture Notes

Teaching to the very end

DN 16, the longest discourse in the entire Pali Canon, covers the Buddha's last months: political counsel to King Ajātasattu's minister on the conditions that keep a republic from declining, the journey to Vesālī, a severe illness he deliberately suppresses by will so as not to attain final Nibbāna "without addressing the community," and finally Cunda's last meal, the passing between the twin sal trees, and the cremation. Through all of it, he keeps teaching — not because the material changes, but because his own death is, to him, simply one more instance of the single truth he has spent his life teaching.

Building in a way to check him after he's gone

Before any of the final scenes, the Buddha gives the four great references (mahāpadesa): if anyone — a monk, a group of monks, elder monks, even a single respected elder — claims "I heard this from the Buddha's own mouth," that claim is not to be accepted or rejected on the speaker's authority. It is to be checked, word by word, against the discourses and the discipline already known. If it fits, accept it; if it doesn't, reject it, however senior the source. This is the Kālāma Sutta's method (Session 1), rebuilt as an institution — a mechanism for testing what is claimed in the teacher's name after the teacher can no longer answer for it himself.

Be your own island

Earlier in his final months, ill and in pain, the Buddha tells Ānanda directly: "Therefore, Ānanda, be your own island, your own refuge, with no other refuge; let the Dhamma be your island, your refuge, with no other refuge." (attadīpā viharatha attasaraṇā anaññasaraṇā, dhammadīpā dhammasaraṇā ananñasaraṇā.) Asked later whether he will appoint a successor to lead the community, he refuses — the Dhamma and the discipline already taught will be the teacher after he is gone. He does not solve the problem of his own absence by naming a person. He solves it by making sure the teaching does not depend on one.

The last words

His final instruction to the assembled monks: "All conditioned things are of a nature to decay — strive on with diligence" (vayadhammā saṅkhārā, appamādena sampādetha). Not a summary of doctrine, not a final secret withheld until the end. The same ordinary truth — that everything conditioned passes — applied, without exception or self-pity, to his own passing, and left as the last thing his students hear him say.

Before you read the sutta itself
  • A teacher's final task is making the teaching not depend on them. No successor is named. The Dhamma and discipline already given are declared sufficient — teaching designed from the start to survive the teacher.
  • Session 1's method, turned into an institution. The four great references extend the Kālāma Sutta's "test it yourself" all the way to claims made in the Buddha's own name after his death — the same skill, built to outlast him.
  • He teaches his own death exactly as he taught everything else. No exception is carved out for himself. The truth that applies to every conditioned thing is allowed to apply, without softening, to him.

Part 3

Discussion Questions

  1. Why might the Buddha refuse to name a successor, when doing so would seem to remove a great deal of uncertainty for the community he leaves behind? What is he protecting by refusing?
  2. Trace the four great references back to Session 1's Kālāma Sutta. What has changed between "test any teacher's claim by its consequences" and "test any claim made in the Buddha's own name against the existing texts"? What has stayed the same?
  3. "Be your own island" is addressed to Ānanda specifically, who by tradition had not yet reached full awakening at the time. Why might this be the exact moment such an instruction is needed — not for the most advanced student, but for one still depending heavily on the teacher?
  4. Having now completed all seven sessions of this course, what would you say is the single teaching-method thread that runs through all of them — from the Kālāmas to Rāhula to Upāli to the raft to the poisoned arrow to Aṅgulimāla to this final scene?
