The girl in the hall of mirrors

A teaching that demands nothing at all, given by a child, in a palace anybody may walk into
Reading time about twenty minutes Before class Session 3 — the contrast is the point The text Part 30 of 454
Part 1

Who you'll meetA princess, a room, and the shortest instruction in the pilgrimage

Teacher 10 of 53 Maitrāyaṇī 彌多羅尼 a girl, in the palace at the city called Lion's Vigor Part 30 of 454

Sudhana has just walked into a fire. The teacher who sent him there names the next one, and it is a child.

Everything about this encounter is the previous one inverted. The brahmin stood alone in a place nobody would approach; Maitrāyaṇī sits in a palace with a stream of ordinary people walking in through an unguarded gate. The brahmin's teaching cost Sudhana his doubt, his repentance, and a climb up a mountain of blades. Maitrāyaṇī's teaching is four words long: look at this hall.

Teacher nine demanded everything and teacher ten demands nothing, and the text gives no sign whatever that one is deeper than the other.

She is also the last of the Ten Abodes, which is why the episode closes the first stage of the path. Fazang, whose commentary divides the pilgrimage into five stages, ends his entire Ten Abodes section here — on a girl, in a room, asking a boy to look at the furniture.

Three things worth having before you start
  • 慈女 — "Girl of Kindness." Fazang's gloss on her name: 智德內圓、慈相外彰, "her wisdom-virtue perfectly complete within, her outward mark of kindness manifest without" — named for both at once. The Sanskrit, he records, means "kindness that rescues beings."
  • The abode of coronation. Fazang's name for the tenth and last of the Ten Abodes — "a position already crowned rather than still advancing toward one." He notes that her father is a king, and reads the princess as fitting that abode exactly.
  • 依報 and 正報 — "environmental retribution" and "personal retribution": the world a being is born into, and the being themselves. Ordinary Buddhist cosmology keeps them apart. This episode runs one straight through the other, and Fazang is precise about which direction.
Part 2

The episodeOne part — short narrative, then a very long list

The story itself takes about ten minutes. It is then followed by one of the longest enumerations in the chapter: a hundred and sixteen dhāraṇī-gates, named one after another. Read the narrative closely; then read enough of the list to feel its scale, and let the rest go past. Fazang sorts all hundred and sixteen into ten groups by function, and the note tells you which.

Part 3

Three moments, read closely

1. Nobody is keeping anybody out.

Before Sudhana meets the teacher he notices the traffic. It is a small observation and the sūtra gives it a whole paragraph.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 30 · at the palace gate

"At that time countless people were entering the palace; Sudhana asked them: 'Where are all of you going now?' They answered: 'We wish to go before the girl Maitrā to hear and receive the true Dharma.' At that time Sudhana thought thus: 'This palace gate opens and admits freely, without any obstruction.'"

時，無量人眾悉入宮中，善財問言：「諸人今者為詣何所？」答言：「我等欲詣彌多羅女聽受正法。」爾時，善財作如是念：「此王宮門自在出入無所障礙。」
Read this passage in full →

Put this beside the previous two arrivals. Meghaśrī took seven days to find, alone on a mountain. The brahmin was surrounded by fire on four sides. Here there is a queue, and the queue walks straight in.

The sūtra is not being casual about access. Sudhana's own first thought on arriving is about the gate — 自在出入無所障礙, entering and leaving at will, nothing in the way. A teacher this advanced, holding a Dharma-gate she received from thirty-six Ganges-rivers' worth of buddhas, is available to whoever walks up the steps.

2. The whole teaching, in one sentence.

Sudhana asks his usual question — how does a bodhisattva learn the practice and cultivate the path. Every other teacher so far has answered with a discourse. She answers with an instruction to use his eyes.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 30 · the instruction, and what it opened

"Good son, look closely and contemplate the adornment of this very Dharma-hall."

"At that time Sudhana beheld, within every single pillar of lapis lazuli, within every single wall of vajra, within every single maṇi mirror … within every single golden bell, within every single jeweled tree … within every single jeweled necklace — he beheld entirely, equal to the whole Dharma-realm, every tathāgata, from their very first raising of the mind, cultivating the bodhisattva's own practice, fulfilling the great vow, adorned with merit, attaining perfect awakening, turning the pure Dharma-wheel, even manifesting entry into nirvāṇa without remainder — just as one beholds the moon's own reflection within clear water."

善男子！汝諦觀此法堂莊嚴。……爾時，善財見一一瑠璃柱中、一一金剛壁中、一一摩尼鏡中……一一金鈴中、一一寶樹中……一一寶瓔珞中，悉見法界等一切如來，從初發心，修菩薩行，成滿大願，功德莊嚴，成等正覺，轉淨法輪，乃至示現無餘涅槃，如淨水中見月影像。
Read this passage in full →

Notice what is not happening. The hall does not change. No light is emitted, nothing descends, the girl performs no display. Sudhana is told to look at what is already in front of him, and the looking is the entire event. The simile at the end is doing careful work too — 如淨水中見月影像, like the moon's reflection in clear water. The moon is not inside the water. The reflection is not a second moon. And there is no useful sense in which the water is lying.

Fazang draws a precise contrast here, and it is worth carrying for the rest of the course. Much later, Samantabhadra's own pores will manifest entire worlds; that is 正報現依, a person manifesting the world. This scene is the reverse — 依報現正, the environment manifesting the persons. Same principle, opposite direction. From here on, whenever you meet one of these visions, it is worth asking which way round it runs.

3. Thirty-six Ganges-rivers of teachers, and one gate.

Sudhana asks what the Dharma-gate is called. It is the Perfection of Wisdom's Universal Adornment, and the way she came by it matters more than the name.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 30 · where she learned it, and her disclaimer

"Good son, this is the Dharma-gate of the Perfection of Wisdom's Universal Adornment; at the presence of thirty-six Ganges-River-sands' worth of buddhas, I cultivated this Dharma-gate, and each of those tathāgatas, through a different gate, caused me to enter this very Dharma-gate of the Perfection of Wisdom's Universal Adornment."

"Good son, I know only this Dharma-gate of the Perfection of Wisdom's Universal Adornment … such merit as this, how could I possibly know it, how could I possibly explain it?"

善男子！是般若波羅蜜普莊嚴法門；我於三十六恒沙佛所修此法門，彼諸如來各以異門，令我入此般若波羅蜜普莊嚴法門。……善男子！我唯知此般若波羅蜜普莊嚴法門……如是功德，我當云何能知、能說？
Read this passage in full →

Thirty-six Ganges-rivers of buddhas, every one of them teaching her a different way into the same place. Fazang's note on why gives three reasons in a single breath: 攝別歸普故、一切成一故、顯一甚深故廣多故 — "gathering the particular back into the universal; everything becoming one; showing that this one is profoundly deep, and therefore vast."

Which is, incidentally, a description of the pilgrimage Sudhana is on. Fifty-three teachers, each with a different gate, all of them opening on the same thing. The girl has already done what the boy is doing. Then she disclaims it, like everyone else.

Part 4

One practice, for this week

Look at the room you are already in

Pick a room you are in every day and have stopped seeing. Pick one object in it — a chair, a cup, a window frame, a cable. Then give it ten uninterrupted minutes.

Not meditation on the object, and not admiration of it. Trace what is actually in it. What is it made of, and where did that come from out of the ground. Who shaped it, on what machine, in what building, in what country. Who moved it, and how far. Who decided its dimensions, and how long ago. Keep going until the object stops being one thing.

This is not what Sudhana saw, and the difference is worth being honest about — he saw every buddha's entire career in a bell, and you will see a supply chain. But the move is the same move: the instruction was to look at a room, the room was already full, and nothing needed to be added to it. Maitrāyaṇī does not give Sudhana a vision. She gives him a sentence, and he does the rest with his eyes.
Part 5

Five questions

Answer from what you read. One attempt each.

Question 1 of 5
What does Sudhana notice before he even reaches Maitrāyaṇī?
Correct: C. 此王宮門自在出入無所障礙. Put it beside the seven-day search for Meghaśrī and the wall of fire around the brahmin. The sūtra varies access deliberately, and here it is simply open.
Question 2 of 5
Sudhana asks how a bodhisattva learns the practice and cultivates the path. What does Maitrāyaṇī tell him to do?
Correct: B. 汝諦觀此法堂莊嚴. That is the whole instruction. Nothing in the hall changes afterwards; the only thing that happens is that he looks.
Question 3 of 5
What does Sudhana see in each pillar, wall, mirror, bell and jeweled necklace?
Correct: D. 如淨水中見月影像. The simile is doing real work: the moon is not in the water, the reflection is not a second moon, and the water is not lying.
Question 4 of 5
Fazang contrasts this vision with a much later scene where Samantabhadra's own pores manifest entire worlds. What is the contrast?
Correct: A. Same one-in-all principle, opposite direction. Worth tracking as more such scenes accumulate across the remaining teachers — the last session of this course ends on the other kind.
Question 5 of 5
How did Maitrāyaṇī come by her Dharma-gate?
Correct: C. Fazang's reason: 攝別歸普故、一切成一故 — "gathering the particular back into the universal; everything becoming one." Which is also an exact description of the road Sudhana is walking.

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

Last week a teacher asked Sudhana to walk into a fire. This week a teacher asked him to look at a room. Why do you assume the first one was deeper?

Most of us do assume it, and the assumption runs deeper than taste. Difficulty feels like evidence: if it cost something, it must have been worth something. But nothing in the text ranks them, and the structure quietly argues the other way — the girl, not the ascetic, is where Fazang closes the entire first stage of the path, and the position she holds is the one already crowned. Ask what you have been discounting because it was too easy to get to, too available, or handed to you by somebody too young.

Session 5 · The man who sold incense

The Ten Abodes are finished and the Ten Practices begin. Six teachers on, Sudhana reaches an elder called Universal Eye, Sublime Fragrance — a man who, Fazang notes from the Sanskrit, is by trade an incense-seller. He knows every illness there is and how to treat it, and every incense there is and how to blend it; and when he burns a single pellet, it fills the whole Dharma-realm. An ordinary trade, turned whole into the teaching.
