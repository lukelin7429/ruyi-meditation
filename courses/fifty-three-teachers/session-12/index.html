Session 12 · Beyond the Ten Grounds · Teacher 51 of 53

The tower

The longest single episode in the canon, opened and closed by the same small gesture
Reading time about twenty-five minutes Before class Session 11 The text Parts 248 to 412 of 454
Part 1

Who you'll meetA bodhisattva, and a building

Teacher 51 of 53 Maitreya 彌勒 at the tower named Adorned Pure Treasury, in the land Sea-Torrent Parts 248–412 of 454

One hundred and sixty-five parts. To put that in scale: the whole of Session 1 was two, and this course's other eleven teachers together occupy about twenty. Maitreya's episode is the longest sustained scene in the Buddhist canon, and most of it is a description of what is inside one room.

The structure is simple enough to hold in your head. Sudhana is told where the tower is and who is in it. He arrives. Maitreya praises him at enormous length, in public, in front of a crowd. Sudhana asks to be let in. Maitreya snaps a finger. The door opens by itself, Sudhana steps through, and the door shuts behind him. Then a hundred and fifteen parts of what he sees. Then Maitreya snaps his finger a second time and tells him to get up.

The tower is not built. The text says it arose "from the bodhisattva's own roots of goodness cultivated in ages past, from the bodhisattva's own sovereign vows, from every spiritual power and wisdom-force" — a structure made out of somebody's practice, which you can walk into.
Three things worth having before you start
  • 阿僧祇 asaṃkhyeya — "incalculable." It appears so many times in the description of the interior that Fazang reads the repetition itself as doing the philosophical work: nothing in the tower is merely numerous, every feature is literally uncountable.
  • 事事無礙法界 — the unobstructed interpenetration of phenomena, the signature idea of the Huayan school. Fazang marks its first explicit appearance in this chapter at a single line in Part 386, and this session quotes that line.
  • Maitreya is the buddha yet to come. Everything in this episode is being shown to Sudhana by someone who has not yet become a buddha and will. That matters at the end.

Part 3

Three moments, read closely

1. The smallest gesture in the pilgrimage.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 385 · the door

"At that time, the youth Sudhana, reverently circling the bodhisattva Maitreya, joined his palms and said: 'I ask only that you, great sage, open the tower's own gate, and let me enter.' At that time, the bodhisattva Maitreya simply snapped his right finger, and the door opened of its own accord. Sudhana entered at once — and once he had entered, the door closed again behind him."

爾時，善財童子敬遶彌勒菩薩，合掌白言：「唯願大聖開樓觀門，令我得入。」爾時，彌勒菩薩即彈右指，門自然開，善財即入；入已，還閉。
Read this passage in full →

Fazang reads the snap as the passage's first concrete signal that ordinary scale has stopped applying — "a tower whose door yields to the same small gesture regardless of its own boundless interior." Nothing is forced, nothing is unlocked, no power is exerted. A finger moves.

And then: 入已，還閉 — the door shuts behind him. Four characters, easy to read past, and they are the difference between a view and an entry. Whatever this is, he is now inside it and outside is no longer available.

Worth noticing too what Maitreya had said a moment earlier, which Fazang flags as strange: that entering the tower will let Sudhana fully understand the bodhisattva path — said before he enters. Everything Maitreya has just spent dozens of parts saying is thereby reframed as preparation for something words could not convey.

2. A hundred thousand towers, none obstructing any other.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 386 · the interior

"At that time, Sudhana observed the tower: vast and boundless, exactly like open space itself, its ground made of every kind of jewel. There were an asaṃkhyeya of windows and parapets … an asaṃkhyeya of golden bells, sounding of themselves in subtle, wondrous tones … And within the tower itself there were a hundred thousand further wondrous towers, none obstructing any other, each adorned and ornamented exactly as already described."

爾時，善財觀察樓觀，廣大無量，猶如虛空，眾寶為地。有阿僧祇窓牖，却敵欄楯……阿僧祇金鈴，自然演出微妙音聲。……於樓觀內，具有百千諸妙樓觀，不相障礙，莊校嚴飾，亦如上說。
Read this passage in full →

不相障礙 — not obstructing one another. Fazang identifies this as the chapter's first explicit statement of what Huayan thought would later formalise as 事事無礙法界: countless towers occupying what would ordinarily be mutually exclusive space, each one entire and complete without displacing any other.

It is worth being clear about how odd the claim is, because the imagery is beautiful enough to slide past. This is not "the tower is very large." A large tower would still have one thing in each place. The claim is that a hundred thousand complete towers are each wholly present, and none of them is anywhere else.

3. The second snap.

A hundred and fifteen parts later, the vision ends. What ends it is a run of similes — a palace in which every form in the cosmos can be seen, a monk in absorption, the great ocean, a mirage-city, an illusionist — and then one sentence.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 402 · the end of the vision

"It is like a person who beholds a gandharva's own city, without any obstruction at all. Sudhana the youth was likewise: within the tower, he saw every dharma without any obstruction … At that time, the bodhisattva Maitreya gathered back his own awesome spiritual power, and at once snapped his finger, saying to Sudhana: 'Good man, arise from samādhi.'"

譬如人見乾闥婆城，無所障礙；善財童子亦復如是，於樓觀中，見一切法，無所障礙。……爾時，彌勒菩薩攝威神力，即時彈指告善財言：「善男子！汝從定起。」
Read this passage in full →

Read that last sentence and then look back at everything before it. 汝從定起 — arise from samādhi. The whole vision, dozens of parts of it, happened inside a single absorption; no time elapsed in the world outside. The same gesture that opened the tower closes it, and the closing line retroactively tells you what kind of place you have been.

The similes do the same work from the other direction. 乾闥婆城 — the mirage-city, the canon's standard image for something vivid, structured and groundless — is applied to what Sudhana has just seen, alongside illusion and dream. The text is not undercutting the vision. It is telling you what category it belongs to, and it waits until afterwards to do so, because telling you beforehand would have prevented the entry.

Part 4

One practice, for this week

Go in, and let the door shut

Pick one thing you have been sampling rather than entering — a book you keep dipping into, a practice you do when convenient, a relationship you maintain at arm's length, a piece of work you have been circling. Give it one unbroken session with the door shut: phone elsewhere, an end time, no exits.

Ninety minutes is plenty. The length is not the point; 入已，還閉 is the point.

Then notice which of the two gestures your week is actually made of. Most of our attention is the second finger-snap — the one that ends absorption and returns us to the room — applied several times a minute. Sudhana gets a hundred and fifteen parts between the two snaps. You are unlikely to manage that, but you can probably manage one afternoon, and the difference between sampling and entering is not gradual.
Part 5

Five questions

Answer from what you read. One attempt each.

Question 1 of 5
How does Maitreya open the tower?
Correct: B. 即彈右指，門自然開，善財即入；入已，還閉. Fazang reads the snap as the first signal that ordinary scale no longer applies — the same small gesture opens a boundless interior. And the door shutting is what makes it an entry rather than a view.
Question 2 of 5
What does the text say the tower arose from?
Correct: D. Not built. Its existence is itself already a teaching about what accumulated practice can manifest — a structure made of somebody's path, which another person can walk into.
Question 3 of 5
What is inside the tower, in the line Fazang singles out?
Correct: C. Fazang marks this as the chapter's first explicit statement of 事事無礙法界. Not "very large" — a large space still has one thing in each place. Each tower is wholly present, and none is anywhere else.
Question 4 of 5
Why does Fazang think the repetition of 阿僧祇 (asaṃkhyeya) matters?
Correct: A. Windows, banners, necklaces, crowns, nets, bells, dust-motes, birds, flowers, jewel-lights — each one incalculable. The repetition is the argument.
Question 5 of 5
How does the whole vision end, and what does that ending reveal?
Correct: C. The same gesture that opened it closes it. And the similes just before — mirage-city, illusionist, dream — name what category the vision belongs to, but only afterwards; saying it beforehand would have prevented the entry.

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

Maitreya told him the entering would do it, not the explaining. What have you been trying to understand instead of enter?

This is the hinge of the episode and it is slightly insulting to a course like this one, which is made entirely of explanation. Dozens of parts of Maitreya's speech are reframed, by his own sentence, as preparation for something the speech could not deliver. That does not make the speech worthless — Sudhana needed it, and you have needed eleven sessions — but it does put the reading in its place. Name one thing you have read about for years and not done. Then notice how comfortable the reading has been, and how exactly it has been shaped to postpone the door.
