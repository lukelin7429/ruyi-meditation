The man who sold incense

A working trade turned whole into a Dharma-gate — and an order of operations worth arguing about
Reading time about twenty minutes Before class Session 4 The text Part 36 of 454
Part 1

Who you'll meetA healer, a merchant, and a question about what comes first

Teacher 16 of 53 Universal Eye, Sublime Fragrance 普眼妙香 an elder, once an incense-seller, in the city called Universal Gate Part 36 of 454

The Ten Abodes closed with Maitrāyaṇī. The Ten Practices have been running for five teachers now — a monk who never leaves a single walking-path, a boy who can calculate every world there is, a laywoman who feeds every being who comes to her, an elder with a treasury that rains down whatever is needed, another whose house has ten storeys and a different order of being on each. Sudhana arrives at the sixth, in a city ringed by a hundred thousand smaller ones, and finds an old man sitting on a throne made of incense.

Ask him how a bodhisattva cultivates the path and he begins with a list of diseases. Wind, cold and fever. Madness. Possession. Poisoning. Illness caused by spells. He knows all of them, he says, and can treat every one.

Then comes the sentence that makes this session worth a place in thirteen. Having cured them, he bathes them in fragrant water, dresses them in good clothes and jewellery, feeds them properly, gives them treasure — and only then does he teach any Dharma at all.

然後為說種種法門 — "and only then do I expound for them every kind of Dharma-gate." Four characters of sequencing, and an entire position on what religion owes people.

Fazang then adds a note from outside the Chinese text that reframes everything above it: in the Sanskrit, this elder is a tradesman. He sells incense. The Dharma-gate he holds did not arrive from somewhere else and get illustrated with a merchant's props — it grew out of the job.

Three things worth having before you start
  • The Ten Practices (十行) — the second of the five stages Fazang reads this pilgrimage by, teachers 11 to 20. Where the Ten Abodes were about establishing the mind, these are about what it does in the world. Fazang names this teacher's own station the sixth, 善現行, the "Practice of Excellent Manifestation."
  • The four contemplations — the oldest piece of practical Buddhist psychology there is: a different meditation prescribed for a different dominant disposition. You will meet the full set in this episode, in one sentence.
  • One pellet of incense — 一丸香. Keep the scale in mind. This is not a cosmic quantity of anything. It is what a shop sells.

Part 3

Three moments, read closely

1. The order of operations.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 36 · what happens before the teaching

"Good son, whoever throughout the ten directions falls ill comes before me, and I am able to heal them all. Once I have removed their affliction, I bathe them in fragrant water, adorn them with fragrant flowers and necklaces, fine robes and superior garments, make offering to them with delicacies and food and drink, and bestow upon them countless rare treasures; and only then do I expound for them every kind of Dharma-gate …"

善男子！十方眾生諸有病者，來詣我所，我悉能治。除其患已，沐浴香湯，香華瓔珞，名衣上服而莊嚴之，肴膳飲食而供養之，無量珍寶而惠施之，然後為說種種法門……

Count the steps. Cure the illness. Wash them. Dress them — and not plainly: flowers, necklaces, fine robes and superior garments. Feed them well. Give them money. Then talk.

This is not the Dharma delivered with material help, as a package. It is the Dharma held back until the body is comfortable, dignified and solvent, and the sequence is stated in the order the sūtra states it. The teaching is not the payment for the food, and the food is not the hook for the teaching — the treasure is given before the sermon, which means the sermon cannot be what the treasure was for.

It is also worth noticing what is not here. No requirement that the sick person be a Buddhist, or become one. No test of worthiness. 十方眾生諸有病者，來詣我所 — whoever throughout the ten directions falls ill and comes to me.

2. Four people, four different medicines.

Then the teaching itself begins, and it does not begin with doctrine. It begins with triage.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 36 · the four contemplations

"For those with much greed and desire, I teach the contemplation of impurity; for those with much hatred and anger, I teach the contemplation of loving-kindness; for those with much folly and delusion, I teach the contemplation of the marks of dharmas; for those of evenly-mixed conduct, I teach the supreme Dharma-gate."

貪欲多者，教不淨觀；瞋恚多者，教慈心觀；愚癡多者，教法相觀；等分行者，教勝法門……

Four lines, and a whole theory of teaching inside them: there is no single practice that is correct, only a practice correct for this person. The physician's logic has been carried straight across from the body into the mind, which is why the two halves of the episode are one thing and not two.

Fazang counts the movements precisely — removing the body's suffering, giving the body ease, healing the mind through these four contemplations, and only then "properly imparting the great practice" through fourteen phrases, eleven of which he maps one by one onto the ten perfections. The care taken over the ordering is not incidental. It is the argument.

3. One pellet, and a note from the Sanskrit.

The second half of the episode turns to what the man actually sells. He names ten incenses — Beyond Naming King, New Head, Supreme, Awakening, Bright Mark, Aloeswood, Firm, Sandalwood, Cloud, and the incense that Stills Every Faculty — and then says what happens when he burns one.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 36 · the offering

"When I burn even a single pellet of incense, it fills the whole Dharma-realm throughout the ten directions, reaching every tathāgata and their own retinue; a canopy of incense adorns the whole Dharma-realm — an incense palace, an incense wall, an incense pavilion, an incense balustrade … an incense net, an incense image, an incense light, an incense ornament, a rain of incense-clouds — adorning the whole Dharma-realm throughout the ten directions, every buddha, and their own retinue."

乃至燒一丸香時，充滿十方一切法界，一切如來及其眷屬；香帳莊嚴一切法界，香宮殿、香垣牆、香樓閣、香欄楯……香網、香形像、香光明、香莊嚴具、香雲雨，莊嚴十方一切法界，一切諸佛及其眷屬。

An entire cosmos built out of one shop's stock. And then Fazang's note, which is the most quietly radical sentence in this session: 以梵本中是賣香人故 — "because, in the Sanskrit original, this elder is by trade an incense-seller." On that reading, as the note puts it, "the whole Dharma-gate, on this reading, grows directly out of an ordinary merchant's own vocation rather than being a symbol chosen from outside it".

Which settles a question this course keeps circling. The pilgrimage is not a series of encounters with religious professionals who happen to have interesting props. A man who knows incense because he sells incense arrives at a Dharma-gate through the knowing. His mastery and his trade are the same mastery. Fazang's gloss on his name says as much in six characters: 智照根藥普熏眾生 — "his wisdom illumines the roots and remedies of every ailment, universally scenting sentient beings."

Part 4

One practice, for this week

Meet the body first

The next time somebody tells you about a difficulty — a real one, not a conversational one — notice what you reach for. Most of us reach for a frame: an interpretation, a reassurance, a principle, a thing we once read.

Do the other thing instead. Find the material need and meet it, and say nothing wise at all. A meal. A lift. Money, if money is what it is. An hour of the boring practical task. The form filled in.

Then leave the insight unsaid — not forever, but until later, and only if they ask. Universal Eye does not withhold the Dharma because the Dharma is unimportant; he holds it until the person in front of him is fed, clothed and solvent, because a teaching handed to someone in pain can function as a way of not helping. Notice how strong the urge to say the wise thing is. That urge is the object of this practice.

Question 1 of 5
What does Universal Eye do for a sick person before expounding any Dharma?
Correct: D. 然後為說種種法門. The treasure comes before the teaching, which means the teaching cannot be what the treasure was for. And the condition for all of it is simply falling ill and coming.
Question 2 of 5
He prescribes four different contemplations for four kinds of person. Which pairing is right?
Correct: B. 貪欲多者，教不淨觀；瞋恚多者，教慈心觀；愚癡多者，教法相觀；等分行者，教勝法門. There is no practice that is simply correct — only one correct for this person. The physician's logic, carried from the body into the mind.

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

What do you offer people first — and what does the order say about what you think you are for?

There is a version of religious life, and a version of helping generally, in which the material act is the wrapper and the meaning is the gift. This episode reverses it without argument: the body is treated, the person is made comfortable and given money, and the Dharma comes afterwards, to whoever is still there. Try the uncomfortable form of the question. If the people you help stopped wanting anything you had to say, would you still turn up?
