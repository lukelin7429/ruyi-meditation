The boy who knew every art

Forty-two letters, and no seam at all between learning to read and perfect wisdom
Reading time about twenty minutes Before class Session 10 The text Parts 224 to 233 of 454
Part 1

Who you'll meetA teacher of children, and the pupil he hands Sudhana to

Teachers 43 and 44 of 53 Universal Friend 遍友 · Skilled in Every Art 善知眾藝 a teacher of children, and a boy — both at Kapilavastu Parts 224–233 of 454

Sudhana comes down out of a heaven, walks into the city, and finds a man named Universal Friend — 遍友, "friend to all" — whose job is teaching children. He bows and asks the question he has asked forty-two times already.

And the schoolteacher's entire episode, the whole of Part 224, is one sentence of reply:

"Good man, here there is a boy named Skilled in Every Art, who has learned the bodhisattva's own wisdom of letters. You may go and ask him, and he shall teach you."

That is all of it. He claims no liberation, teaches nothing, and points at a child in his own classroom. Fazang treats this as complete rather than deficient: the chain of fifty-three sometimes advances "not through a teaching received but through a teaching correctly located," and pointing is Universal Friend's whole contribution.

So Sudhana goes to the pupil. And the pupil's liberation, when he names it, is the alphabet.

Three things worth having before you start
  • The arapacana syllabary — forty-two Sanskrit seed-syllables, in the order a, ra, pa, ca, na… It is not a mystical list. It is the sequence children were taught their letters in, the Indian equivalent of an alphabet chart on a classroom wall.
  • 字智 — "the wisdom of letters," which is what the schoolteacher says this boy has learned. The phrase carries both meanings at once and the text never separates them: knowing your letters, and the wisdom that letters open.
  • A fanqie gloss in the source. At the seventh syllable the Chinese adds 徒假反, a pronunciation note. The translator wanted these letters said correctly, not merely read for meaning — an unusual piece of philological care, and a hint about what kind of text this is.

Part 3

Three moments, read closely

1. The referral that is the whole teaching.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 224 · Universal Friend, complete

"Good man, here there is a boy named Skilled in Every Art, who has learned the bodhisattva's own wisdom of letters. You may go and ask him, and he shall teach you."

善男子！此有童子名善知眾藝，學菩薩字智。汝可問之，當為汝說。
Read this passage in full →

Every teacher in this course has ended with a referral. This one is the referral, with nothing in front of it. And notice the direction it runs: a teacher of children sends the most famous seeker in the canon to a child in his own class, without apology and without explaining himself.

Set that beside Session 2, where an eminent physician got off his throne to prostrate before a beginner. The pilgrimage has now inverted the teacher–student relation twice, in opposite directions, and treated both as ordinary.

2. Reciting the letter A.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 226 · the first of the forty-two gates

"Reciting the letter A, he enters the prajñā-pāramitā gate named the bodhisattva's own dignity and virtue, each its own distinct domain. Reciting the letter Ra, he enters the prajñā-pāramitā gate named equality, a single flavor, supreme and boundless. Reciting the letter Pa, he enters the prajñā-pāramitā gate named the Dharma-realm without a differing mark …"

唱阿字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名菩薩威德各別境界；唱羅字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名平等一味最上無邊；唱波字時，入般若波羅蜜門，名法界無異相……
Read this passage in full →

The boy says he constantly recites and upholds these — 我恒唱持 — the root letters that enter into his liberation. Not contemplates. Not decodes. Recites, the way a child does, out loud, in order, over and over.

Fazang reads the choice as deliberate: the most basic possible act of literacy instruction turns out to contain, for someone reciting with full realization, the entire scope of perfect wisdom. The syllabary is not upgraded into something else. It stays an alphabet. What changes is who is saying it.

3. Birdsong, harvests, and nothing left unrealized.

Then, at the end, the boy describes what those who have this liberation also know — and the list leaves the classroom entirely.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 233 · the closing sentence, unbroken

"They are further skilled in observing the patterns of heaven and the configurations of earth, the auspicious and inauspicious signs of physiognomy, the calls of birds and beasts, the clouds, mists, and weather, the abundance or scarcity of each year's own harvest, and the safety or peril of a nation — every worldly art such as these, there is none they have not thoroughly trained in, mastering each to its very root. They are further able to discern the transcendent Dharma as well … without doubt, without obstruction, without ignorance or darkness, without dullness, without vexation, without sinking, and with nothing left unrealized."

又善觀察天文地理、人相吉凶、鳥獸音聲、雲霞氣候、年穀豐儉、國土安危，如是世間所有技藝莫不該練，盡其源本。又能分別出世之法……無疑無礙，無愚闇，無頑鈍，無憂惱，無沈沒，無不現證。
Read this passage in full →

Read those two sentences as one movement, because that is how the text writes them. Weather forecasting, harvest yields, whether the country is safe — 莫不該練，盡其源本, trained in every one of them, each mastered to its very root — and then, with no transition whatever, the transcendent Dharma, with nothing left unrealized.

The reading note names what is happening: worldly skill and transcendent realization "are never treated as two separate competencies requiring two separate trainings." This course has been circling that claim since Session 5. Here the text stops circling and simply writes the two into one sentence.

Part 4

One practice, for this week

Teach somebody something elementary, out loud

Find the most basic thing you know how to do and teach it to one person who cannot do it yet. Letters. A knot. How to read a bus timetable, boil rice, hold a pen, send an email that gets answered. Elementary is the requirement, not a concession.

Then watch what happens to your own attention. Teaching the alphabet requires you to say each letter correctly, in order, at the speed of the person learning — which is exactly the practice this boy describes: 恒唱持, constantly reciting and upholding.

Two things to notice. First, how strong the pull is to move on quickly to something more interesting — that pull is the object of the exercise. Second, whether you can do it without signalling that you know more advanced things. Universal Friend does not mention what else he knows. He names his pupil and stops.

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

What did you stop finding interesting once you could do it?

Almost everything anyone knows deeply was once the hardest thing they had ever attempted, and then became invisible. Reading is the clearest case — you cannot now look at this sentence and not read it — and this episode puts precisely that at the summit of a path forty-three teachers long. The question is not whether you should find the alphabet profound. It is what you have filed as basic, handled automatically, and therefore stopped being present for; and whether "I already know that" has ever been anything other than a description of where your attention no longer goes.
