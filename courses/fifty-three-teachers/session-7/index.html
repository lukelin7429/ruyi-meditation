A teacher from another religion

The boldest move in the pilgrimage, made without a word of argument
Reading time about twenty minutes Before class Session 6 The text Part 41 of 454
Part 1

Who you'll meet出家外道 — a renunciant of another path, and the last of the Ten Practices

Teacher 20 of 53 According with Every Sentient Being 隨順一切眾生 a renunciant of another path, on a mountain north of the city Contentment Part 41 of 454

The previous teacher, a laywoman, finishes her teaching and sends Sudhana on. The Chinese names the next teacher plainly: 出家外道, a renunciant — of another path. Not a Buddhist. Not a sympathiser. Someone who has gone forth into a religious life that is not this one, and who is the twentieth of fifty-three, the last teacher of the entire second stage.

Nothing in the episode marks this as a problem. There is no framing, no apology, no suggestion that the boy is being tested, and — this is the part worth sitting with — no conversion. The teacher does not turn out to be a bodhisattva in disguise who drops the costume. He teaches; Sudhana bows; Sudhana leaves; the road continues.

Session 3 asked whether you would follow a teacher whose method appalled you. Session 6 asked whether you would trust one whose conduct looked monstrous. This one asks something smaller and much harder to dodge: would you take instruction from someone of another religion, and leave them in it?

Fazang, who is not embarrassed either, glosses the man's name as a case of skill running deliberately against appearances: 巧智順機方便反道 — "his skillful wisdom accords with capacity, even while his own skillful means outwardly runs contrary to the [orthodox] Way." He records the Indian original of the name more plainly still: "Constant Practice Everywhere."

Three things worth having before you start
  • 外道 — literally "outside the path," the standard Chinese Buddhist term for a non-Buddhist religious teaching. It is not a neutral word and this course will not pretend it is. What matters is that the sūtra applies it to a man and then sends its hero to sit at his feet.
  • Reaching Every Place (至一切處) — his Dharma-gate, and the last of the Ten Practices. Fazang reads the name itself as the sign the whole stage has culminated: 明十行滿故也, "this reveals that the Ten Practices are now complete."
  • Ninety-six — the traditional count of non-Buddhist schools in India. Keep it in mind for the sentence where it appears; the arithmetic of that sentence is the whole session.

A Mountain That Glows Like Sunrise, and a Teacher No One Recognizes

A city entered at sunset, a mountain seen at midnight, a climb at dawn — and a teacher who says he has taught everyone in the city without a single one of them knowing who he is.

Read it →

Fazang reads the staging as doctrinally exact rather than atmospheric: the mountain glowing at midnight is the light of the Dharma breaking the darkness of both extremes; the dawn is obstacles exhausted and the stage fully revealed; the climb is coming into correspondence with the position. He also notes the timing — arriving at dusk, leaving at dawn — repeats the previous teacher's exactly, marking a second time in succession that this stage is at its limit.

Part 3

Three moments, read closely

1. The arrival, and what the text calls him.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 41 · the climb

"Gradually passing through cities, towns, and villages, at sunset he entered the city Contentment, and sought everywhere, asking: 'Where is the renunciant According with Every Sentient Being now?' At the hour of midnight, he beheld, to the north of this city, a great mountain, its light shining forth as brightly as the sun at its very first rising. At that time, at dawn, Sudhana left the city and climbed to the top of this mountain, and from afar beheld this renunciant walking in meditation in a quiet place, his sublime form surpassing even the Brahmā king, surrounded by a retinue of ten thousand Brahmā-heaven attendants."

漸漸經由城邑、聚落，於日沒時，入知足城，周遍推求：「隨順一切眾生外道今在何所？」於中夜時，見彼城北有一大山，光明照耀如日初出。爾時，善財天明出城，登彼山上，遙見外道靜處經行，成就妙色、超逾梵王，一萬梵天眷屬圍遶。

The Chinese calls him 外道 twice in three lines, without softening and without hostility. He is walking in meditation. His form surpasses the Brahmā king's. Ten thousand attendants of the Brahmā heaven are around him — which is to say the text gives him the retinue of a major religious figure of a different cosmology, and simply lets that stand.

Sudhana bows his head at his feet and asks the same question he has asked nineteen times before. He does not ask what school the man belongs to.

2. What he actually teaches — and the first item on the list.

The answer opens with a survey of every kind of being there is, in every destiny, and then divides them by what they are attached to: "some are attached to wrong views, some delight in the two lesser vehicles, some delight in the Great Vehicle." He teaches all three. And the first method he names is not doctrine at all.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 41 · the first of his skillful means

"Sometimes I teach every kind of worldly craft and art, wishing to cause sentient beings to attain the dhāraṇī-gate of every skillful art; sometimes I gather in sentient beings through the four means of attraction, wishing all to attain omniscience; sometimes I extol every perfection …"

或教世間種種技藝，欲令眾生得諸巧術陀羅尼門；或以四攝攝取眾生，欲令一切得薩婆若；或歎諸波羅蜜……

Worldly crafts and arts, first. Before the perfections, before the mind of awakening, before any praise of the Tathāgata — teaching people a skill, so that they come by way of the skill to a gate of the Dharma. The whole list that follows runs from there upward, and Session 11 of this course will meet a boy whose entire teaching is the same move carried to its limit.

3. And none of them know who I am.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 41 · the sentence this session exists for

"Furthermore, good son, every one of the people within this city of Contentment, men and women, old and young, according to what is fitting, I teach and deliver entirely, and yet none of these sentient beings know who I truly am; throughout this Jambudvīpa, ninety-six kinds of heretical wrong view exist, and I expound every kind of Dharma for these beings, severing their every wrong view …"

復次，善男子！此知足城內一切人民，男女長幼，隨其所應，我悉化度，彼諸眾生莫知我誰；此閻浮提九十六種外道邪見，我悉為彼種種說法，斷其邪見……
Read this passage in full →

Work through what that sentence contains. A man the text calls 外道 is cutting off 外道邪見 — the wrong views of the ninety-six schools — and doing it from inside. Nobody in the city knows who he is. There is no platform, no debate, no announcement. 莫知我誰: they do not know who I am.

Two things follow, and the second is uncomfortable in a way worth keeping.

The first is that the teaching travels without the label. Everything that changes these people reaches them unbranded, and the sūtra treats that as a mark of accomplishment rather than a loss. This is the practice named "Reaching Every Place," and a teaching that needs its own name attached has not reached everywhere.

The second is that the episode is not a piece of modern religious pluralism, and reading it as one flattens it. The man is severing wrong views, not affirming that everybody is already right. What the text does is more specific and more interesting than tolerance: it puts the teacher outside and the teaching inside, and refuses to make either of those facts an obstacle.

Part 4

One practice, for this week

Learn one thing from outside, and leave the label on the shelf

Find something genuinely useful that comes from a tradition you do not belong to — another religion, another school, another discipline entirely. Not a comparison exercise, and not an appreciation. Something you will actually use this week.

Then do the harder half. Take it without taking the tradition, and without needing to convert it first. Do not translate it into your own vocabulary to make it safe. Do not tell yourself that it was secretly your own tradition all along.

The teacher on the mountain is not a Buddhist. Sudhana does not make him one, does not stay, and does not argue. He bows, learns, and walks south. If you find yourself needing to relabel the thing you learned before you can use it, that is the object of the practice — notice how much of your resistance was to the source rather than to the content. You did that audit once already in Session 3; this is the same audit with the stakes on your own side.

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

If what you have to offer only works when your name is on it, what exactly are you offering?

The teacher on this mountain measures his own accomplishment by the fact that nobody knows it is his. That is a hard standard for anyone who teaches, writes, runs an organisation, or raises children, and it is not the same as modesty — modesty is about how you speak of the work, and this is about whether the work still functions once the attribution is gone. Try it against something specific you do. Then ask the version that stings: how much of what you call conviction is attachment to the label the conviction came wrapped in?

Session 8 · The sea-captain

The Ten Practices are finished and the Ten Transferences begin. Sudhana finds a man on the shore where the ships are moored, surrounded by a hundred thousand merchants. He knows every treasure in the sea and what each is worth; the whirlpools, and how to leave them; the palaces of the nāgas and the asuras beneath the water; the colour of the water, the constellations, the hour, and whether a ship is sound. A working life, set out as a bodhisattva's practice with nothing added to it.
