The one who meets you in the dark

The first teacher on this road who is not human — and a list of the shapes she takes
Reading time about twenty minutes Before class Session 9 The text Parts 54 to 56 of 454
Part 1

Who you'll meetA night-deity above the Buddha's own birthplace

Teacher 31 of 53 Vāsantī 婆娑婆陀夜天 a night-deva, in open space above the city of Kapilavastu Parts 54–56 of 454

The Ten Transferences are finished. Fazang marks the turn explicitly — 自下從婆娑婆陀至瞿夷有十善知識，明十地位行, "from here, from Vāsantī through to Gopā, there are ten spiritual friends, revealing the practice of the Ten Grounds stage" — the pilgrimage's fourth great division.

And it opens on somebody who is not a person at all.

Sudhana enters the city by its eastern gate not long after sunset, and has to look up to find his teacher: she is above the city, in open space, on a lotus seat inside a jewelled pavilion, gold-bodied, robed in vermilion, her hair bound like Brahmā's — and every star and constellation appears on her body.

Fazang notes a detail worth carrying through the next several teachers: 此九種夜天，依梵本皆是女天，表是慈悲之狀也 — "these nine kinds of night-deva are, according to the Sanskrit original, all female devas, marking the very mark of compassion."

Nine of the ten teachers of this whole stage are night-deities. The stage that represents the highest reaches of the bodhisattva path, in the text most concerned with who counts as a teacher, is carried almost entirely by non-human women who work after dark.

Three things worth having before you start
  • Why Kapilavastu. Fazang gives the reason in one line: 表初地已上生佛家，故在佛生城也 — "it marks that, from the first ground upward, one is born into the buddha's own household — hence [she is placed] in the very city where the Buddha himself was born." The address is the doctrine.
  • Two readings of her name. 婆羅婆薩那, "Reliance Without Fear" — which is exactly what she teaches; and elsewhere 婆僧多, "Spring," 表能生長萬行，如春時也, "marking her ability to give rise to and grow every practice, the way springtime does."
  • Why a night-deity at all. 在於夜中耀光救物，故以為名 — "shining light in the darkness of night to rescue beings is why she bears this title" — which Fazang then reads doctrinally as realized wisdom breaking through the obstruction of darkness itself.

Part 3

Three moments, read closely

1. Nine kinds of person, nine different minds.

Her first answer is not about rescue. It is a list of what she generates toward whom, and it repays slow reading.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 54 · what she raises toward whom

"Good son, toward evil sentient beings I give rise to a mind of great loving-kindness; toward sentient beings of unwholesome karma I give rise to a mind of great compassion; toward sentient beings who cultivate goodness I give rise to a mind of joy; toward sentient beings both good and evil I give rise to a mind free of duality; toward defiled sentient beings I give rise to a mind of purity; toward sentient beings on a wrong path I give rise to a mind of the true path …"

善男子！我於惡眾生發大慈心，於不善業眾生發大悲心，於修善眾生發歡喜心，於善惡眾生發無二心，於染污眾生發清淨心，於邪道眾生發正道心……
Read this passage in full →

Read the first two again. The greatest kindness goes to the worst people, and the greatest compassion to those doing the most harm. That is not a sentiment about tolerance; it is an allocation, and it is the reverse of how attention is usually distributed. Then notice the fourth: toward beings both good and evil, a mind free of duality — which is where the list stops sorting people at all.

2. The shapes.

Then she describes when she works, and the answer is: the worst hours. Not a metaphorical darkness — a specific one, with a list of what is out in it.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 54 · the hour, and the forms

"In the darkness of night, when people are still, when ghosts, spirits, and thieves are abroad, when monks have set aside their own dignified bearing, when heavy clouds and dust and smoke obscure everything and neither sun nor moon can be seen … Should sentient beings encounter peril at sea … I then take on the form of a ship, or the form of a king of horses, a king of elephants, a king of dogs, a king of the asuras, a king of the sea-spirits … For beings upon dry land, I become the pure moon, or the stars and constellations, a torch, a flash of lightning, the light of every jewel …"

於夜闇人靜，鬼神、盜賊所遊行時，比丘離威儀時，重雲煙塵昏蔽、日月不見色時……我於爾時或作船形，或作馬王、象王、狗王、阿脩羅王、海神王形……為陸地眾生，或作淨月及諸星宿、炬火、電光、諸寶光明……
Read this passage in full →

Two things in that list are worth stopping on. The first is 比丘離威儀時 — "when monks have set aside their own dignified bearing." The hour she works is defined partly as the hour when the religious professionals are off duty. She is not listed among them and never claims to be.

The second is the forms themselves. A ship. A horse. A dog. On a mountain: a fruit tree, a flowing spring, a bird with a beautiful voice, level ground. She does not appear as a goddess and deliver a teaching. She appears as the thing that would help — and most of the things on the list are not people, and several are not even alive.

3. Where she is standing, and why that is the whole point.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 54 · the first sight of her

"With the eye of great wisdom he contemplated the ten directions, and beheld this night-deva dwelling above that city, within open space; seated upon a throne of fragrant lotus within a jeweled pavilion, her body the color of true gold … Upon her very body appeared every star and constellation, and by their light she taught and delivered the sentient beings of boundless worlds, causing them to depart from every evil path."

大智慧眼觀察十方，見彼夜天於彼城上虛空中住；處寶樓閣香蓮華座，身如真金……於其身上現一切星宿，及其光明化度無量世界眾生，遠離惡道。
Read this passage in full →

The stars are on her. Whoever is lost at sea and takes a bearing from a constellation is, in this text's arrangement, already being helped by a teacher they will never meet, never name, and never thank — and that is the form the help takes at the opening of the highest stage of the path.

Which brings the city back into focus. She is above Kapilavastu — the Buddha's own birthplace — because, on Fazang's reading, the first ground is where one is born into the buddha's household. The pilgrimage arrives at the stage named for the Buddha's own lineage, and the first teacher it finds there is a nameless light in the sky that sailors have been steering by for as long as there have been sailors.

Part 4

One practice, for this week

Be the thing, not the advice

Find one person this week who is in genuine difficulty, and work out what shape would actually help — not what you would like to say to them.

Vāsantī's list is instructive precisely because it is so unspiritual. A ship. A spring. Level ground. A light you can see from a long way off. Translate that: a lift. A meal cooked and left. An hour of childcare. A form filled in. Sitting in a waiting room so they are not alone in it. Something with a shape.

One condition, taken from the text: she never announces herself. Nobody rescued by a ship stops to thank a night-deity. Try to do the thing in a way that does not require them to know it was you, and notice how much of your impulse to help was attached to being seen helping. That noticing is the practice; the meal is just a meal.
Part 5

Five questions

Answer from what you read. One attempt each.

Question 1 of 5
What does Fazang say about the nine night-deities of this stage?
Correct: B. Nine of the ten teachers of the Ten Grounds — the stage representing the highest reaches of the path — are non-human women who work after dark.
Question 2 of 5
Toward whom does Vāsantī say she raises a mind of great loving-kindness?
Correct: A. The list is an allocation, and it runs the opposite way to how attention usually goes. Its fourth item then stops sorting altogether: toward beings both good and evil, a mind free of duality.
Question 3 of 5
Among the hours she names as her working time is 比丘離威儀時. What does it mean?
Correct: D. Her hour is defined partly by the absence of the people usually assumed to be doing this work. She is not among them and never claims to be.
Question 4 of 5
What forms does she take for those in danger?
Correct: C. She appears as the thing that would help. Most of the list is not a person, and several items are not alive.
Question 5 of 5
Why, on Fazang's reading, is the first teacher of the Ten Grounds placed at Kapilavastu?
Correct: B. The address is the doctrine. Fazang also glosses the city's name — "properly 'Kapilavastu,' meaning 'City of Yellow Things': the ancient sage Kapila ['Yellow-Head'] once dwelt here before this later became a city, hence the name."

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

Who has already helped you in a form you did not recognise as help?

The logic of this episode is that the assistance arrives as a ship, a light, a patch of level ground — as circumstance rather than as a person with a message — and that it is therefore almost never credited. You can take that devotionally if you like, but it also works as a plain question about your own history: the job that came at the right moment, the person who was simply there, the road that happened to be passable. You are unlikely to be able to distinguish luck from a night-deity, and this session does not ask you to. It asks whether you have ever gone back and counted.
