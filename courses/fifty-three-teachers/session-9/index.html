The woman they warned him about

The episode anthologies leave out — and the one where the doubt belongs to the crowd, not the boy
Reading time about twenty-five minutes Before class Sessions 3 and 6 The text Part 48 of 454
Part 1

Who you'll meetA woman in a palace, and a town with opinions

Teacher 25 of 53 Vasumitrā 婆須蜜多 in the deep palace, city of Jewel Adornment, in the land Perilous and Difficult Part 48 of 454

Sudhana walks into a city and asks for directions, and something happens that has not happened before: the bystanders start discussing him.

This is the third of the three teachers Fazang names as wearing one of the three poisons openly. Session 3 was wrong view — an ascetic on a mountain of blades. Session 6 was wrath — a king surrounded by torture. This one is craving, and it is the episode most often dropped from anthologies and reading lists. This course said in Session 1 it would not apologise for these teachers and would not pretend they were simple. So we read it whole.

In Sessions 3 and 6 the doubt was Sudhana's. Here Sudhana never doubts for a moment. The doubt belongs to strangers — and they are not being malicious. They think they are defending him.
Before you read: what the text says, and what it does not
  • What she teaches is departure from desire. Her Dharma-gate is 離欲實際清淨法門, "the Pure True Limit of Freedom from Desire." The direction of travel in every sentence is out: whoever comes bound by desire leaves free of it.
  • Nobody in the episode is gratified. The text lists ten modes of contact and attaches a named samādhi to each. It never describes anyone getting what they came for.
  • The commentary supplies its own limit. Fazang closes the page by citing the Compendium of the Mahāyāna to the effect that work of this kind "belongs entirely to a bodhisattva at the very utmost stage — nothing a lower stage of practice could ever attempt to imitate." That sentence is in the tradition, not added by this course, and it is there for the obvious reason.

Part 3

Three moments, read closely

1. The town takes a view.

Before Sudhana reaches the gate, the sūtra stops and reports what people are thinking. Read it slowly, because the shape of it is the point.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 48 · those who did not know her

"This boy here, his bearing composed and measured, his mind tranquil, his every faculty tamed and subdued, departed far from heedlessness, inversion, and confusion … this is no man given over to the inversion of tainted desire; free of any thought of carnal desire, not sunk in the mire of desire … already able to refrain from whatever ought not be done — what possible motive could he have in seeking out this woman?"

今此童子，威儀庠序，其心寂靜，調伏諸根，遠離放逸、顛倒、惑亂……此非染欲顛倒之人；無情欲想，不沒欲泥……所不應作，已能不為，有何等意而求此女？
Read this passage in full →

Notice who this paragraph is about. It is a long, admiring inventory of Sudhana's virtues, and it exists to make one point: a man like this cannot possibly want what men want. The woman herself is not described at all. She does not need to be, because everybody already knows.

Then the second group speaks, and the sūtra gives them the last word:

"Excellent! This boy has gained a great and true benefit, and is indeed able to seek out a woman of such profound wisdom." — 乃能推求深智女人. The difference between the two groups is not moral sensitivity. It is stated plainly by the text: 有人不知彼女深智慧者, "there were people who did not know of this woman's own profound wisdom." One group had the information. The other had the reputation, and filled in the rest.

2. Ten kinds of contact, and where every one of them goes.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 48 · her own account of the gate

"Good son, I have accomplished the Dharma-gate of the Pure True Limit of Freedom from Desire … Should any sentient being bound by desire come before me, I expound the Dharma for them, and they all depart from desire entirely, attaining the samādhi of the unattached domain; whoever beholds me attains the samādhi of joy; whoever converses with me attains the samādhi of the unobstructed, sublime voice; whoever takes hold of my hand attains the samādhi of going before every buddha-land …"

善男子！我已成就離欲實際清淨法門……若有眾生，欲所纏者，來詣我所，為其說法，皆悉離欲，得無著境界三昧；若有見我，得歡喜三昧；若有眾生與我語者，得無礙妙音三昧；若有眾生執我手者，得詣一切佛剎三昧……
Read this passage in full →

The list runs to ten and does not flinch at the end of it — Fazang glosses the last two terms from the Sanskrit without euphemism: āliṅgya, "to embrace and touch," and acumbita, "to kiss the mouth," each granting its own named concentration.

But track the grammar rather than the vocabulary. Every clause has the same subject and the same destination: whoever comes → attains a samādhi. She is never the one who receives anything. There is no description of her pleasure, or anyone's. And the whole list is framed, before it starts, by 皆悉離欲 — they all depart from desire entirely. A passage about sex that contains no satisfaction in it is doing something other than what it looks like from outside.

And then the question Sudhana actually asks — not how do you do this, but where did this come from. Her answer is startlingly ordinary. Lifetimes ago she was a married woman, "the wife of an elder, named Good Woman." A buddha entered her city; she went out into the road with her husband and offered him a jewelled crown. Mañjuśrī, who was attending the Buddha, taught her, and she raised the mind of awakening. That is the whole origin: one gift, handed over in the street.

3. Why the boy does not doubt.

Fazang asks the question this course has been building toward for three sessions: Sudhana doubted the king outright, so why not here?

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 48 · Fazang's reading note

"Wrath and wrong view directly obstruct the bodhisattva path, whereas desire, though it runs parallel to compassion, obstructs practice only weakly — which is why the Sūtra of Determining the Vinaya says a bodhisattva would rather give rise to a hundred thousand thoughts of desire than a single thought of anger."

以前二親障菩薩道故，貪愛順悲障行劣故，是故《決定毘尼經》菩薩寧起百千貪心，不起一瞋等
Read this note in full →

貪愛順悲 — desire runs parallel to compassion. Both move toward another person; one wants to consume and the other wants to help, but they share a direction, which is why the text can put a teacher here at all. Anger does not share it. Fazang adds a second, plainer reason too: Sudhana has already been tested by the harder two, so a milder one no longer shakes him.

He also reads the palace itself as a diagram — the ten-layered jewelled wall as the ten grounds, the ten rows of trees as the ten perfections, the ten-layered moat as the precept-conduct held within those grounds, the water of eight excellent qualities as the eight dhyānas. Nothing in the setting is ornament. The whole building is the path, drawn around a woman whose teaching runs directly through the one thing most paths are built to route around.

And here is this course's own note. The reading that turns this episode into permission has done real harm, repeatedly, and the tradition anticipated it: Fazang's last move on the page is to quote the Compendium of the Mahāyāna saying that work of this kind belongs to a bodhisattva at the very utmost stage, and that nothing a lower stage could attempt to imitate. Read plainly, the episode says the opposite of what it is usually accused of saying — everyone who arrives leaves free of the thing they arrived with, and the one who is never described as getting anything is the teacher.

Part 4

One practice, for this week

Catch yourself supplying a motive

This week, notice the moment you hear that someone met with someone — a colleague and a rival, an ex and a friend, a politician and a donor, two people at your workplace who have no obvious reason to be talking. Notice the story that arrives in your head, unbidden, before any information does.

Then do the two-part check the episode runs. What do I actually know about the second person? And: whose character did my story just assess — theirs, or the one I was busy defending?

The crowd in this episode is not cruel. They are complimenting Sudhana at length while they do it. That is what makes them worth studying: the whole apparatus runs on affection for one person and a reputation attached to another, and it produces a completely wrong conclusion without anyone intending harm. If you find nothing to write down this week, you were not watching closely enough.

Five questions

Answer from what you read. One attempt each.

Question 1 of 5
Who doubts in this episode?
Correct: C. Sudhana never doubts here at all. The text is explicit about what divides the two groups: information. One knew her; the other had the reputation and filled in the rest.
Question 2 of 5
Whose character does the doubting crowd's long paragraph actually describe?
Correct: B. 有何等意而求此女？ She is never described. She does not need to be — everyone already knows, which is exactly the mechanism the episode is exposing.
Question 3 of 5
What does her Dharma-gate do to whoever comes to her bound by desire?
Correct: D. Every clause has the same destination, and the teacher is never described as receiving anything. Her gate is named for the direction of travel: 離欲實際清淨法門, the Pure True Limit of Freedom from Desire.
Question 4 of 5
Fazang explains why desire can host a teacher where wrath and wrong view could not. What is his reason?
Correct: A. Both desire and compassion move toward another person; one consumes and one helps, but they share a direction. Anger does not. Fazang adds a second reason: Sudhana had already been tested by the harder two.
Question 5 of 5

Correct: C. One gift, handed over in the street by a married woman. The most notorious teacher on the road has the plainest origin story in this course.

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

The people who got it wrong were defending someone they admired. What have you done wrong while protecting someone?

This is the uncomfortable shape of the episode and the reason it is worth a session. The crowd is not a mob. They are fond of Sudhana, they can see what kind of person he is, and their entire misjudgement is built out of that fondness pointed in the wrong direction — protect him, and the woman becomes whatever the protecting requires. Most of the harm done in families, institutions and movements is done in exactly this posture, by people who would describe themselves as loyal. The question is not whether you are loyal. It is what your loyalty has cost somebody you never bothered to find out about.
