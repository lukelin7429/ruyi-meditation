Part 1

Who you'll meetA brahmin standing in fire, and the first teacher Sudhana refuses

Teacher 9 of 53 Skillful Life 方便命 a brahmin ascetic, in the land called Seeking Advancement Parts 28–29 of 454

This is the episode people leave out.

Sudhana arrives in a land called Seeking Advancement and finds a brahmin performing the austerity of the five fires: fire on all four sides like a great mountain, and within it a mountain of knives "towering to no limit". The man is standing on top of the knives, about to throw himself down into the flames. Sudhana bows, and asks his usual question.

The answer is an instruction. Climb the knives. Jump into the fire. Then every bodhisattva practice will be entirely pure.

And Sudhana — who has bowed at the feet of every teacher so far without a word of hesitation — thinks: this man is a demon.

What makes this session worth a place in a course of thirteen is not the ordeal. It is what the text does with the refusal. The doubt is set down in full, in the boy's own words, with nothing softened. Then a cosmos of witnesses arrives to testify. Then Sudhana repents, leaps, and finds that neither the blades nor the fire do what blades and fire do. And then Fazang, three centuries later, writes a note saying that the doubt was the correct response, and that without it there would have been no way to tell a true teacher from a false one.

Before you read: what this session is not
  • It is not an instruction. Nothing in this course asks you to harm yourself, and nothing in this episode transfers to a real room with a real teacher in it. In life, a teacher who tells you to injure yourself has told you everything you need to know, and the answer is to leave. This session is about reading a text, not about obeying anyone.
  • It is not a story about blind faith. Sudhana does not jump on command. He refuses, he is given independent corroboration from a very large number of witnesses, and only then does he act. The sequence matters and the text is careful about it.
  • 五熱炙身 — "scorching his body with the five fires": a well-known brahminical austerity, fires on four sides and the sun overhead. The sūtra has picked a practice from outside its own tradition, and then made it the teaching.
  • 相 (appearance) — the outward form a thing takes. Fazang's whole analysis turns on this word: what has gone wrong here is not the teacher but the appearance, and appearance is exactly what doubt is for.

Part 3

Three moments, read closely

1. The instruction, and the sentence Sudhana says to himself.

The teaching is eleven words in Chinese and it is entirely unambiguous.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 28 · the brahmin's instruction, and Sudhana's thought

"Good son, if you are now able to climb this mountain of knives and throw yourself into this mass of fire, every bodhisattva practice will become entirely pure."

"Could this not be some māra, or someone possessed by māra? Could this not be one who is not truly a good spiritual friend, yet manifests the appearance of one — could this not be some evil bodhisattva? And now he makes for me a difficulty of my very life, a difficulty for my roots of goodness, a difficulty for omniscience itself — this is surely not the true teaching, but some perilous, evil path."

善男子！汝今若能登此刀山，投火聚者，菩薩諸行皆悉清淨。……此將非魔魔所使耶？非善知識而現善知識相，將非惡菩薩耶？而今為我作壽命難、作善根難、薩婆若難，此非正教險惡道耳。
Read this passage in full →

Read the doubt closely and notice how good it is. Sudhana does not merely recoil; he reasons. A human birth is hard to get, he thinks, and freedom from the difficult conditions is harder, and meeting a buddha's era harder still, and meeting a good spiritual friend hardest of all — and this man is asking me to throw the whole sequence away. The objection is not squeamishness. It is an argument from the value of the very opportunity the pilgrimage depends on.

And then the decisive move: 非善知識而現善知識相 — "one who is not truly a good spiritual friend, yet manifests the appearance of one." Sudhana has correctly identified the only thing he can actually check, and found it unreliable.

2. Fazang: he was right to doubt, and the text needs him to.

The commentary does not treat this as a lapse to be excused. It asks the obvious question and answers it in the boy's favour.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 28 · Fazang's reading note

"Sudhana is already a great being — why does he doubt at all? Because the outward appearance reverses the ordinary path, doubt is, in principle, the appropriate response. Were this not so, there would be no way to distinguish the false from the true."

善財是大人，何故疑者？以相是反道，理須疑也。若不爾者，無以甄別邪正
Read this note in full →

The chorus in the sky exists for the mirror-image reason — 顯示實德故, "to make his real virtue manifest," since "without this, there would likewise be no way to discern the true from the false." Doubt and evidence are a matched pair. The episode is not asking Sudhana to stop checking; it is showing him what checking looks like when the thing in front of you does not fit any category you have.

Fazang then goes further, and this is the part worth slowing down for. He identifies three places in the whole pilgrimage where a teacher's appearance matches one of the three poisons directly — this ascetic resembling wrong view, a king still to come resembling wrath, and a courtesan still to come resembling craving. All three are in this course, as Sessions 3, 6 and 9. He then sets out four different ways affliction can be said to relate to the Buddha-dharma, and rules out the first three here: it is not that the affliction is empty and the emptiness is the Dharma; not that affliction is a hook used as skillful means; not that a bodhisattva deliberately keeps a trace of it. The fourth is different in kind — 當相則是, "taken directly, just as it appears, it simply is" — and Fazang says plainly that this is the one that applies, that it is 極難解, extremely difficult to understand, and that this passage is what it looks like.

Which is to say: the fire is not a symbol of something gentler. There is no decoding that makes the knives go away.

3. The repentance, the verse, and what the fire actually did.

After the last witness has spoken, Sudhana goes back to the brahmin's feet: "Just now I doubted your sacred teaching, going against the teaching of a spiritual friend — I ask only that you, great sage, accept my repentance." The brahmin answers in verse, and the verse says: "Whoever seeks awakening should accord with the teaching of a spiritual friend, extinguishing every doubt, single-mindedly and constantly reverent." Hold that sentence next to Fazang's note from ten minutes ago and notice that they do not agree. We will come back to it.

Then he climbs.

Entering the Dharma-Realm, Part 29 · the leap

"At that time Sudhana the Boy then climbed the mountain of knives and threw himself into the mass of fire; before he had even reached its midst, he already attained the bodhisattva's own Samādhi of Abiding in Peace; and, having reached the very flame itself, he further attained the bodhisattva's own Samādhi of Tranquil, Peaceful, Illumining Bliss. Having attained this samādhi, he said: 'How extraordinary, great sage! This mountain of knives and this great mass of fire — when my body touched them, I felt only peace and bliss.'"

爾時，善財童子即登刀山，自投火聚，未至中間，即得菩薩安住三昧；既至火焰，復得菩薩寂靜安樂照明三昧。得三昧已，白言：「甚奇。大聖！如是刀山及大火聚，我身觸時，安隱快樂。」
Read this passage in full →

Fazang's reading of the timing is exact, and it is not decorative. The first samādhi comes before the fire, on the knives — because "the knife should sever, and this reverses it, and so he abides in peace." The second comes at the flame itself, where "tranquility is the very principle realized; bliss is the wisdom that realizes it; illumination is realization and wisdom perfectly matched." Two reversals, in order, each matched to the instrument that was supposed to destroy him.

And Sudhana's closing line, Fazang notes, is 後得智 — subsequently attained wisdom. It is testimony from someone who has already crossed through, not a claim made in advance. Nobody could have told him. That is the whole shape of the episode.

Part 4

One practice, for this week

Separate the content from the packaging — once, in writing

Pick one thing you have rejected without examining: a tradition, a practice, a book, a political position, a person's whole way of doing things. Something you decided about quickly.

Write two short lists. What I actually object to in the content. And: what I object to in the 相 — the appearance, the register, the clothes, the vocabulary, who else likes it. Be honest about which list is longer.

This is not an exercise in talking yourself out of your objections. Fazang's point cuts both ways: appearance is unreliable evidence, which means a repellent appearance is weak grounds for rejection and an attractive one is weak grounds for trust. If the content list stands up on its own, your objection survives, better founded than before. If there is nothing on it, you have learned something.

Sudhana does not resolve his doubt by deciding to trust harder. He resolves it with evidence, from a very large number of independent sources, about what this man's practice had actually done. That is a reasonable standard, and it is available to you.

Part 5

Five questions

Answer from what you read. One attempt each.

Question 1 of 5
What does the brahmin tell Sudhana to do when asked how a bodhisattva cultivates the path?
Correct: D. 汝今若能登此刀山，投火聚者，菩薩諸行皆悉清淨. No metaphor is offered, no explanation is given, and the instruction is the entire answer to the question.
Question 2 of 5
Sudhana's doubt turns on one specific thing he says he cannot rely on. What is it?
Correct: B. The doubt is well argued — a human birth is hard to obtain, a good friend harder still, and this man is asking him to throw all of it away. But its hinge is that appearance can be counterfeited, and appearance is all he has to go on.
Question 3 of 5
How does Fazang's reading note assess Sudhana's doubt?
Correct: C. 以相是反道，理須疑也。若不爾者，無以甄別邪正. And the chorus of witnesses exists for the matching reason in reverse — 顯示實德故, without which "there would likewise be no way to discern the true from the false." Doubt and evidence are a pair.
Question 4 of 5
Fazang lists four ways affliction can relate to the Buddha-dharma, and says only the fourth applies here. What is the fourth?
Correct: A. B, C and D are the first three, and Fazang rules out each of them for this passage. The fire is not standing in for something gentler — which is precisely why the episode is as hard to read as it is.
Question 5 of 5
What happens when Sudhana finally climbs and jumps?
Correct: C. 安隱快樂. Fazang matches each samādhi to its instrument — the knife that should sever is reversed first, then the fire that should burn — and notes that Sudhana's report is 後得智, testimony from someone already through, not a claim anyone could have made for him in advance.

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

The commentary says the doubt was necessary. The brahmin's verse says extinguish every doubt and follow. Which one is this episode actually teaching?

Do not resolve it too quickly, and be suspicious of any reading that makes the tension disappear — including the comfortable one where Fazang is right and the verse is just devotional throat-clearing. Both are in the text, forty lines apart, and the pilgrimage is not embarrassed by either. One possibility worth testing: that the verse is addressed to a boy who has already checked, and means something quite different when said to someone who has not. Another: that the text really does want both, and that living with the contradiction is the actual skill being taught. You will meet the same problem twice more in this course, in Sessions 6 and 9, each time with the stakes moved somewhere new.
