Session 4 · The stories he told

The wager

Three cakes, one bet, and a burglar who cannot believe his luck
Reading time under two minutes Before class nothing at all Where it is from Story 14, Fascicle 44
Part 1

A question, before any of this

What would you give up in order to be right?

Not in principle. In practice, in your actual kitchen, with a specific person. Most people answer that question as though the stakes were dignity or truth. This week's story suggests the honest answer is usually smaller, more absurd, and considerably more expensive.

It is the shortest thing in this course — four sentences — and it is roughly two thousand years old. People were still retelling it a thousand years later in China, and it turns up in versions across Asia and Europe, which is what happens to a story that keeps being true.

Last week two men saw what they were doing and walked out of their lives. These two do not see anything at all.

That contrast is deliberate on my part, and worth holding all week. Session 3's craftsman and painter both had a moment of appalling clarity at dawn. The couple in this story get a moment of comparable clarity handed to them in the middle of the night, and it goes straight past them.

Part 3

Three momentsPlaces worth stopping — in a story with about four of them

1 · Count the cakes

The opening line

"Long ago there were a husband and wife who ate three cakes together, one each, and there was one left over that they meant to break and divide. The wife said: do not divide it; I shall wager with you. Let neither of us speak; whoever speaks first loses and whoever speaks last gets it."

昔有夫婦。共食三䴵。人各一枚。餘一欲破分。婦言。莫分。與君共賭。各自不語。先語者失。後語者得。
See this passage in the full fascicle →

Three cakes. They have already eaten one each. The story establishes this in eight characters and it is doing something precise: nobody in this house is hungry. The thing being fought over is not food. It is the surplus.

Notice too that the argument never actually happens. There is no quarrel over the last cake. There is a reasonable plan — break it and divide it — and then the wife proposes something else instead. A game. With rules, a win condition, and a prize.

This is worth sitting with, because it is how most domestic conflict actually begins. Not with anger. With a structure — a silent framework that turns a small shared thing into something one of you can win.

2 · What it looks like from outside

The middle of the night

"So they shut their mouths; and in the middle of the night a burglar came in and saw the two of them sitting and not speaking, and, supposing this was some great terror, dared not make a sound; and he gathered up their goods and carried them out of the door."

於是閉口至于中夜。竇土偷入。見其二人坐而不語。謂是大怖不敢作聲。收斂其物擔將出戶。
See this passage in the full fascicle →

This is the best line in the story and the one almost nobody quotes. A thief walks into a house at night and finds a married couple sitting bolt upright in silence, staring at nothing, in the dark. And he is frightened. 謂是大怖 — he takes it for some great terror, something too strange to risk, and he does not dare make a sound.

So he robs them very, very quietly.

Hold the two views side by side. From inside, this is a game with a cake at the end of it. From outside, it is a household so rigid and so wordless that a professional criminal treads carefully around it. Nobody looking at a silent family from the doorway has any idea what it is about, and the people inside have no idea what it looks like. The burglar is the only person in the story who is behaving sensibly, and he is the burglar.

3 · He is not wrong

The end, entire

"The wife cried out: you are a man — how can you let the goods go? Her husband said: I win, I win — now I get the big cake. And people blamed and laughed at them and called them great fools."

婦大喚曰。汝是丈夫那置物去。夫言。我勝我勝。今得大䴵。眾人責笑謂大顛癡。
See this passage in the full fascicle →

Here is the thing that makes this a great story rather than merely a funny one. The husband is correct.

Go back to the terms: whoever speaks first loses; whoever speaks last gets it. The wife spoke first. He spoke second. Everything he says is accurate, the rules were agreed by both parties, and he has won cleanly. There is no cheating here and no misunderstanding. He has simply been right, all the way down, into total loss.

And notice what his wife says when she finally breaks. Not "the burglar!" — she says you are a man, how can you let the goods go? She loses the game, in the same breath, by attacking him rather than the thief. Even her surrender is a move.

Then the final clause, which is the story's whole verdict: people blamed and laughed at them and called them great fools. That is it. That is the ending.

What this story refuses to do
  • Nobody learns anything. Last week's two men saw themselves clearly and changed their lives. This couple is handed a night of perfect clarity — a stranger walked out with everything they own while they watched — and it lands on neither of them.
  • There is no moral attached. The story does not tell you what to do instead. It simply reports the laughter of the neighbours and stops.
  • It does not make them villains. They are not cruel, or greedy, or unfaithful. They are a normal couple who were offered a game and could not decline it. That is the only thing wrong with them, and it costs them the house.
Part 4

One thing to tryThis week · two minutes · a piece of paper

Name the cake

There is very likely an argument running in your life right now — the slow kind, weeks or years old, that does not need to be restarted because it never quite stopped.

Write down what the prize actually is. Not the principle. Not "respect" or "being heard" or "fairness" — those are the names people give the game while they are inside it. The object. Who loads the dishwasher. Whose account the money sits in. Which of you says it first. Who was right about the thing in 2019.

Then write down the terms. How does someone win? What would the other person have to do for it to be over? If you cannot state the win condition in one sentence, that is worth knowing — the couple in this story could state theirs exactly, which is the only reason the husband could be so completely right.

You are not being asked to concede anything. Just to write down, in daylight, what is on the table. Most people find the cake is a good deal smaller once it has a name.

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

What is the cake?

Somewhere in your life a rule is being enforced that nobody wrote down, over an object smaller than the effort of enforcing it. The husband in this story was not stupid about the rules — he understood them perfectly, which is exactly how he lost everything in the house. Being right about the terms and being wrong about the game are two completely different things, and only one of them is visible from inside.
