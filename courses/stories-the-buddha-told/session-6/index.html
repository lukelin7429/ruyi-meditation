Session 6 · The stories he told

Buying wisdom

A thousand taels of gold for a piece of advice with nothing in it
Reading time about two minutes Before class nothing at all Where it is from Story 18, Fascicle 44
Part 1

A question, before any of this

What is a piece of advice actually worth?

Awkward question, because the honest answer is that you cannot tell at the time. Advice arrives with no price on it and usually no obvious use. You find out what a sentence was worth years later, on a particular evening, and by then you have generally forgotten who said it.

This week's story is about a man who pays an enormous sum for a single instruction, gets something that sounds like a swindle, and finds out what it was worth the same night. It is two minutes long and much lighter than last week's — though it comes within a few seconds of being the darkest story in the course, which is a thing the text mentions almost in passing.

He did not buy information. Nothing he was told contains any. He bought a delay.

Part 3

Three momentsPlaces worth stopping

1 · Notice what he was going to spend the money on

The opening — a poor man comes home rich

"Long ago there was a man, poor and of no use, who took up trading and went to sea and gathered treasure and came back to his country. He met a good friend and said: I was always poor before and have now got these goods, enough to manage on. If my mother will not have it my way, I mean to leave her and live apart; and if my wife will not have it my way, I mean to look for another."

昔有一人。貧窮無用。治生入海採寶還國。遇善知識言。我素貧窮今得此物。足以自諧。若母不可。我意當捨母居去。若婦不可。我意我當更索。
See this passage in the full fascicle →

Before a single word about wisdom, look at what the money has already done. He has been home about five minutes and the wealth has been fully converted — not into a house or a business, but into a plan for replacing his family.

And he is not asking anyone's opinion. Read the grammar of it: if my mother will not have it my way. The condition is not whether they are right. It is whether they agree. He has come back from the sea with enough money that other people have become optional, and he is announcing it to a friend in the street.

Now watch what the friend does, because it is the second most interesting thing in the story. He does not argue. He does not defend the mother, or point out that the wife has waited years, or say any of the sensible things a friend says. He says: there is a wise man near here, and you can buy wisdom from him for a thousand taels of gold.

A man who has just announced he can now afford to discard his family is told, in effect: fine — then you can afford this too.

2 · The advice has nothing in it

What a thousand taels of gold buys

"The man did as he said and went into a settlement that served the Buddha and asked about it, and was answered: whenever a thing is in doubt, walk seven steps forward and seven steps back, and do so three times, and wisdom will arise of itself."

答曰。夫所疑事。前行七步却行七步。如是至三智慧自生。
See this passage in the full fascicle →

Read it as a customer. You have handed over a fortune and received a walking instruction.

There is no content in it whatsoever. Nothing about mothers, or wives, or money, or anger, or how to tell whether you are being unfair. No principle you could write on a wall. No way to be wiser tomorrow than you are today. It does not even tell you to think — "wisdom will arise of itself" puts the burden on nobody at all. Fourteen paces, three times, and that is the whole product.

Which is exactly why it works, and why the story is cleverer than it first appears. What he bought was not information. It was a delay — and, crucially, a delay with a shape. Not "count to ten," which you can do while still holding the knife and still being right. Seven steps forward puts your body somewhere else. Seven steps back brings you to where you were, changed by the walk. Three times, because once is not enough to break a moment that has already made up its mind.

3 · The night, and the price

What happens a few hours later

"That night the man went home and saw his mother sleeping beside his wife, and, supposing it was another man, drew his blade meaning to kill him; but in his mind it did not seem right, and he lit a great lamp and looked from a distance and reflected: this morning I bought wisdom. And he went forward and back three times in that way, and his mother woke."

其人夜歸家。見母伴婦眠。謂是他男。拔刀欲殺。意中不掩。然大燈火遙照思惟。朝買智慧。如是前却三反。母便覺悟。
See this passage in the full fascicle →

A man comes home in the dark, sees two shapes in his bed, and is a moment away from killing his own mother. The story spends one clause on it, the way it spends one clause on most things.

But look closely at the order of what saves them, because it is not what you would guess. The remembered advice comes fourth.

The sequence, in the order the text gives it
  • 意中不掩 — "in his mind it did not seem right." A small wrongness arrives first, before any technique. He has it already; he has always had it.
  • He lit a great lamp. More light on the situation.
  • 遙照 — he looked from a distance. More space between him and it.
  • Then he remembers what he bought that morning, and walks.
  • The instruction did not supply the doubt. It gave the doubt somewhere to go — and long enough to get there.

Then the last line, which is the story's actual argument about advice:

The revaluation

"The man exclaimed: this is truly wisdom — and how should it be worth only a thousand taels of gold? And he gave him three thousand taels more."

此人歎言。真為智慧。何但堪千兩金。即復與三千兩金。
See this passage in the full fascicle →

He goes back and pays four times the asking price, unprompted, for something he already owns. Nobody asked him to. The transaction was complete and the seller had no further claim.

And that is the point about advice, put as sharply as a story can put it: the price was not settled in the morning. It was settled that night. Before that night, a thousand taels was a swindle. After it, four thousand was a bargain, and the words had not changed at all.

Part 4

One thing to tryThis week · costs nothing · must be physical

Buy yourself the seven steps

Pick one specific trigger. Not a general resolution to be calmer — a single, identifiable moment you can recognise while it is happening.

Some candidates: the message that makes your chest tighten. The instant before you press send. The moment you become certain you know what somebody meant by that. The second sentence of an argument you have had before.

Attach something physical to it. Stand up. Leave the room and come back. Walk to the end of the corridor and return. Put the phone in another room and go and get it. It has to move your body and it has to bring you back — that is the shape of the thing he was sold, and neither half works alone.

It does not have to be wise. This is the part people resist. You are not trying to have a better thought during the walk; the man in the story does not think of anything at all while pacing. You are buying the interval in which the doubt you already have can catch up with you.

Once, this week, is enough. He only ever used it once.

Part 6

One question, left open

Sit with this

What do you do at full speed that cannot be undone?

Everyone has a category. Words in a particular kind of argument. Decisions made at a particular hour. Something that gets sent, or said, or signed while the certainty is still hot. The man in this story was not short of judgement — the wrongness reached him before the advice did. He was short of the fourteen paces it needed in order to arrive in time.
