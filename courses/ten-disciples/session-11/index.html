Part 1

Who he wasThe only job interview in the canon

Thirty years into his teaching, the Buddha asks the assembly who can take on two duties at once: uphold the twelve divisions of scripture, and attend to his own daily needs, without letting either slip.

Five hundred arhats volunteer. Every one is turned down. Maudgalyāyana works out that the Buddha's heart is set on Ānanda, and has to press him repeatedly before he will accept. And Ānanda's acceptance comes with three conditions, negotiated in advance.

經律異相, Fascicle 15 · Ānanda's eight qualities

"I am weak and unworthy of such service; but should the Buddha grant me three requests, I shall accept the sangha's own charge. First, should the Tathāgata ever wish to give me his own old robes, let me be permitted to decline them; second, should I ever be invited separately by some individual donor, let me be permitted to decline such invitations; third, let me be permitted to come and go without any fixed schedule. Mañjuśrī said: excellent — he has thought ahead to avoid any occasion for suspicion."

阿難曰。穢弱不堪奉給。若佛與我三願者。當從僧命。一者如來設賜故衣。聽我不受。二者設受檀越別請。聽我不從。三者聽我出入無有時節。文殊言善哉。預見譏嫌。
Read this story in full →

預見譏嫌 — he foresaw the grounds for criticism. Before starting the most privileged post in the sangha, he removed every material benefit it could have carried: no hand-me-downs from the Buddha, no private dinner invitations, no special access hours. A man about to spend twenty-five years closer to the Buddha than anyone, arranging in advance that nobody could ever say he profited by it.

The list of what he accomplished includes the two things his epithet is built on — hearing any of the twelve divisions of scripture once and never needing to ask again, "retaining it as perfectly as water poured from one vessel into another" — and one that is easy to read past. Fourth: "though still possessed of the beginnings of affliction himself," he followed the Buddha in and out of every royal and noble household without desire arising at the sight of any woman there.

始具煩惱 — he still had afflictions. The tradition puts that in the middle of the list of his eight inconceivable qualities, without apology.

The other story worth this session is his seven dreams. He wakes with his hair standing on end: waters on fire; the sun setting while he carries Mount Sumeru on his head; a monk floundering in a cesspit while someone walks across his head to clean ground; elephant calves harrying a great elephant until it leaves, then starving where they stand; and last, a dead lion-king named Kesin, untouched by any bird or beast, none daring to approach — until worms emerged from within its own body and devoured its flesh.

Every one, the Buddha explains, is a prophecy about the corruption of his own teaching. And the seventh is explicit:

經律異相, Fascicle 15 · the seventh dream

"While the Buddha dwells in this world, he expounds the Dharma widely, and after his own nirvāṇa, no outside heterodox teaching will ever be capable of destroying it — only his own disciples, from within, will bring about its ruin."

佛在世間廣說經法。佛泥曰後無有外道能壞此法。但由弟子當自壞我法。

The man who would be responsible for transmitting the whole of it is given, in advance, a set of dreams about how it fails — and the answer is never persecution.

Part 2

In his own wordsHe is not one of the twenty-five — he is the one being taught

Ānanda is absent from the Śūraṅgama's twenty-five gates for a reason unlike Kātyāyana's or Rāhula's: he is the sūtra's protagonist. The Śūraṅgama opens with Ānanda in danger and proceeds as the Buddha's extended answer to him. He does not testify among the twenty-five because he is the person the twenty-five are being recited to.

Which is, in miniature, his whole position in the tradition: present for everything, addressed by everything, and the last to arrive.

Part 3

The Southern columnThe thickest in the course, and it says one thing

Five entries. Nobody else has more than two.

This site's reading guide to the chapter where he appears states the fact and then draws the conclusion:

"No one else on the entire list gets more than two. That concentration is not modesty about the other fifteen — it is the tradition saying, in the only way a list can, that everything it possesses came through one man's memory. Two entries later, Upāli is named foremost in having memorized the monastic law, and between the two of them stands the whole of what was recited at the first council."

The Visuddhimagga adds one detail that matters more than a biography would. Discussing where the four analytical knowledges arise, it makes a point of saying they belong not only to accomplished arhats but to trainees still on the path — and the first name it gives as an example is his:

"Herein, in the chief disciples and the great disciples, they reach their classification at the stage beyond training. In such figures as Elder Ānanda, the householder Citta, the lay devotee Dhammika, the householder Upāli, and the laywoman Khujjuttarā, they reach it at the stage of the trainee."

Read the company he is keeping there: three laypeople and a laywoman. The Buddha's own attendant, foremost in having heard, is filed by the South's great handbook among those who had it before they were done — which is exactly the position he occupies at the First Council, and exactly what Session 12 is about.

Part 4

The rebukeStanding at a gate with a bowl, because his teacher has a cold

The last of the ten, and the smallest errand in the chapter. The Buddha's body is slightly unwell and needs cow's milk. Ānanda takes a bowl and goes to stand at a brahmin's gate early in the morning. Vimalakīrti asks what he is doing there, and Ānanda tells him.

Vimalakīrti, to Ānanda

"Stop, stop, Ānanda! Do not speak this way! The Tathāgata's body is a body of vajra — every evil in it already cut off, every good in it fully gathered — what illness could it have? What affliction could it have? Go quietly, Ānanda! Do not slander the Tathāgata; do not let anyone else hear such a crude claim … If some heterodox teacher or brahmin ascetic heard these words, they would think: 'What kind of teacher is this, who cannot even save himself from illness, and yet claims to save others who are ill?' Go quietly and quickly, and do not let anyone hear."

止，止！阿難！莫作是語！如來身者，金剛之體，諸惡已斷，眾善普會，當有何疾？當有何惱？默往！阿難！勿謗如來，莫使異人聞此麁言……外道、梵志若聞此語，當作是念：「何名為師？自疾不能救，而能救諸疾人？」可密速去，勿使人聞。
Read this passage in full →

Notice how different this is from the other nine. Vimalakīrti is not expanding a definition or dismantling an attainment. He is telling Ānanda to be quiet and get off the street before someone hears. Twice: 默往 and 可密速去.

And Ānanda's reaction is the most disorienting line any of the ten says: 實懷慚愧，得無近佛而謬聽耶 — "I truly felt ashamed, and wondered: could it be that, close as I am to the Buddha, I have somehow misheard him?"

The man whose entire function is having heard correctly wonders whether he has misheard. If that goes, everything goes — every sūtra in this course begins "Thus have I heard," and this is the ear.

Then the thing that happens in no other episode. A voice comes from the sky, and it does not take Vimalakīrti's side outright:

The Vimalakīrti Sūtra · the voice from the sky

"Ānanda, it is as the householder says. It is only because the Buddha has appeared in this world of the five corruptions that he manifests and practices in this way, in order to deliver beings. Go, Ānanda — take the milk, and do not be ashamed."

阿難！如居士言。但為佛出五濁惡世，現行斯法，度脫眾生。行矣，阿難！取乳勿慚。
Read this passage in full →

取乳勿慚 — take the milk, and do not be ashamed. Vimalakīrti is right about the Dharma-body; Ānanda is right about the errand. Alone among the ten, Ānanda is sent back to finish what he was doing.

It is a fitting place for the ten to end. Nine disciples are shown the larger version of their own speciality and fall silent. The tenth is told that the small, literal, embarrassing task in front of him was correct all along — and that the world he is standing in is the reason.

Part 5

One practice, for this week

Fetch the milk

There is a kind of person who is so aware of the large view that the small task becomes embarrassing to them — who cannot do the literal, unglamorous, slightly undignified thing in front of them without feeling that somebody more enlightened would be above it.

This week, find your version of standing at a gate with a bowl. The form to fill in. The lift to the airport. The message to the relative. Do it without upgrading it into something meaningful, and without apologising for it.

And take the second half of the instruction as seriously as the first: 取乳勿慚 — and do not be ashamed. The voice from the sky does not tell Ānanda he was right and Vimalakīrti was wrong. It tells him the errand stands because of where he is standing — a world of five corruptions, containing an ill body that needed milk. Most of the care anyone has ever received for you has had this shape, and none of it was diminished by not being the Dharma-body.

Part 7

One question, left open

Sit with this

"Could it be that, close as I am to the Buddha, I have somehow misheard him?" — what would it cost you to be wrong about the thing you are most reliable at?

For Ānanda the answer is: everything, and not only for him. He is the ear the whole tradition passes through; every sūtra in this course opens by claiming to be what he heard. The chapter lets him wobble — genuinely, for a paragraph — before a voice from the sky steadies him, and the steadying does not consist of telling him he was clever. It tells him the errand was real because the world is. Ten sessions in, notice that not one of these men was improved by being told they were right, and not one of them was destroyed by being told they were not.
