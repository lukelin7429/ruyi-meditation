Session 6 · Sections 21–24

Not the least thing whatsoever

Level ground, with nothing higher and nothing lower
Reading time about thirty-five minutes Before class Sessions 1–5 The text Diamond Sūtra, sections 21–24
Part 1

Section 21The strongest word the sūtra uses about anyone

Section 21Not Speaking, Yet Spoken非說所說分

"Subhūti, do not say that the Tathāgata has the thought, 'I should teach a teaching.' Do not think this way. Why is this? If someone says the Tathāgata has taught a teaching, they slander the Buddha, because they cannot understand what I have taught. Subhūti, one who teaches, teaches no teaching at all — that is what is called teaching."

「須菩提！汝勿謂如來作是念：『我當有所說法。』莫作是念。何以故？若人言：如來有所說法，即為謗佛，不能解我所說故。須菩提！說法者，無法可說，是名說法。」

Read this section in its place — Diamond Sūtra, sections 21–24 →

謗佛 — slander the Buddha. It is the harshest term in the sūtra, and it is aimed not at opponents or heretics but at admirers: at anyone who says the Buddha taught something.

Read carelessly this looks like mysticism, or like a paradox for its own sake. It is neither. The reason is given in the same sentence: because they cannot understand what I have taught. To say the Buddha taught a teaching is to make the teaching a thing — an object handed across, possessable, a doctrine one could have. Section 6 already said that grasping at the mark of a teaching installs the four notions. Saying he taught one does the same thing on his behalf.

And the formula closes it in the usual three steps: one who teaches, teaches no teaching at all — that is what is called teaching. The teaching is not cancelled. Twenty sections of it have just happened. What is denied is that anything was handed over.

Section 21continued — neither beings nor non-beings非說所說分

At that time the venerable Subhūti said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, in ages to come, will there be beings who hear this teaching and give rise to faith?" The Buddha said: "Subhūti, they are neither beings nor non-beings. Why is this? Subhūti, what are called 'beings,' the Tathāgata says, are no beings at all — that is why they are called beings."

爾時，慧命須菩提白佛言：「世尊！頗有眾生，於未來世，聞說是法，生信心不？」佛言：「須菩提！彼非眾生，非不眾生。何以故？須菩提！眾生眾生者，如來說非眾生，是名眾生。」

Read this section in its place →

Subhūti asks the same question he asked in section 6 — will anyone in the future believe this? — and gets a different kind of answer. Not yes, and they will be rare, but a refusal of the terms of the question.

Note the double negation and that it is not left standing: neither beings nor non-beings, and then immediately the formula, which returns the word. The Madhyamaka elective's Session 8 watched Jizang refuse to let "neither" become a resting place. The same discipline is at work here, in a text six centuries older.

Part 2

Section 22Two words of confirmation

Section 22Nothing Whatsoever to Attain無法可得分

Subhūti said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, when the Buddha attained unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening — did he attain nothing at all?" The Buddha said: "So it is, so it is, Subhūti. In unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, I did not attain the least thing whatsoever — that is exactly why it is called unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening."

須菩提白佛言：「世尊！佛得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。為無所得耶？」佛言：「如是，如是。須菩提！我於阿耨多羅三藐三菩提乃至無有少法可得，是名阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。」

Read this section in its place →

如是，如是 — so it is, so it is. This is the shortest section in the sūtra and the only place where the Buddha simply confirms something twice without elaborating.

Which is fitting, because Subhūti is no longer asking a question here. He is checking a conclusion he has reached himself, twenty-one sections in, and the answer is that he has it.

乃至無有少法可得 — not the least thing whatsoever. The phrase forecloses the obvious escape route. A reader can accept that no grand thing was attained while quietly reserving a small one: some subtle shift, some residue, something to show for it. 少法, the least thing, is aimed precisely there.

Not "he attained something indescribable." Not the least thing whatsoever.
Part 3

Section 23Level ground — and the instruction that sits inside it

Section 23Pure Mind, Wholesome Action淨心行善分

"Furthermore, Subhūti, this teaching is level and equal, with nothing higher and nothing lower — that is why it is called unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. Free of self, free of person, free of being, free of lifespan, cultivate every wholesome teaching, and unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening is attained. Subhūti, what is called a wholesome teaching, the Tathāgata says, is no wholesome teaching at all — that is why it is called a wholesome teaching."

「復次，須菩提！是法平等，無有高下，是名『阿耨多羅三藐三菩提』；以無我、無人、無眾生、無壽者，修一切善法，則得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。須菩提！所言善法者，如來說即非善法，是名善法。」

Read this section in its place →

是法平等，無有高下 — level and equal, with nothing higher and nothing lower. And the sentence does something worth noticing: it makes that levelness the reason for the name. It is called un-surpassed precisely because there is no higher and lower in it — not because it sits at the top of a ladder, but because there is no ladder.

Then the instruction, and this is the sentence to take away from the whole session: free of self, free of person, free of being, free of lifespan, cultivate every wholesome teaching.

Both halves at once. Six sessions of a text that has denied attainment, denied teaching, denied marks, and denied that anything was ever handed over — and here, in the middle of it, an unambiguous instruction to cultivate every wholesome teaching. Not some. Every.

This is the sūtra's answer to the objection that its readers would inevitably raise, and that the Madhyamaka course watched being raised in Chapter 24: if nothing is attained and nothing is fixed, why do anything? The answer is not an argument. It is an instruction placed exactly where the objection would form, and the two clauses are not in tension — the first is the condition under which the second is done.

And then, inevitably, the formula lands on wholesomeness itself. Even good is not a fixed thing; and that is why it can be called good.

Taixu's lecture notes, 1927 — as rendered on this site

""Level, with nothing higher and nothing lower" is the hinge — the awakened nature is not increased in a sage or diminished in an ordinary being; it is the identical ground under both. Because it is level in this way, every wholesome action — the six pāramitās and everything built on them — genuinely leads toward it, provided it is practiced free of the four notions that would otherwise turn the practice back into more self-building."

Part 4

Section 24The comparison at its largest

Section 24Merit and Wisdom Beyond Compare福智無比分

"Subhūti, if someone gathered up every Mount Sumeru, king of mountains, in a billion-world universe, made them into a mass of the seven treasures, and gave it all away; and if someone else took up even a single four-line verse of this Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra, upheld it, recited it, and explained it to others — the first person's merit would not be a hundredth part of the second, not a fraction in a hundred thousand million, not any fraction that number or likeness could express."

「須菩提！若三千大千世界中，所有諸須彌山王，如是等七寶聚，有人持用布施；若人以此《般若波羅蜜經》，乃至四句偈等，受持讀誦，為他人說，於前福德百分不及一，百千萬億分、乃至算數譬喻所不能及。」

Read this section in its place →

The comparison has now escalated three times — treasure, then bodies, now every world-mountain in a billion-world universe turned into treasure — and this time the sūtra spells out the arithmetic explicitly: not a hundredth, not a fraction in a hundred thousand million, not any fraction that number or likeness could express.

That last clause settles the reading Session 3 proposed. If the difference were one of quantity, some ratio would express it, however enormous. The sūtra says no ratio will, which is a statement that the two things are not commensurable at all. Countable against uncountable, not much against more.

What Session 6 establishes
  • Saying the Buddha taught something slanders him — because it makes the teaching a thing that was handed over.
  • "Neither beings nor non-beings" is not left standing. The formula returns the word, as it always does.
  • Not the least thing whatsoever — 少法, aimed precisely at the small attainment a reader reserves.
  • Level ground is why it is called unsurpassed. Not the top of the ladder; no ladder.
  • Cultivate every wholesome teaching — an unambiguous instruction placed exactly where the objection would form.
  • No ratio expresses the difference, which means the two are not commensurable rather than very unequal.
Part 5

One practiceBoth clauses of section 23

Level ground

Section 23 is the only place in the sūtra where the two halves of the whole teaching are given in a single sentence, and it is the best test of whether a reader has taken it wrongly.

For one week, keep a short list — three or four items — of things you actually do that you would call wholesome. Nothing aspirational; only what you already do.

Then, each day, check them against the two clauses, in this order. Did I do it? — the second clause, cultivate every wholesome teaching. If the answer is no, the first clause is not the problem.

And only then: where did the mind land? — the first clause. On being someone who does this; on the person who benefited; on the record.

The order matters and is the point of the exercise. A reader who has absorbed the sūtra's denials and quietly stopped doing anything has kept the first clause and dropped the second, which section 23 does not permit. The levelness of the ground is not an excuse to stand still on it.

One question to sit with

Has anything you have read in six sessions made you do less?

Section 23 treats that as the diagnostic question, and it is a fair one to ask of any teaching that spends its time denying things. The sūtra's own answer is to put an instruction to cultivate every wholesome teaching in the same sentence as its most sweeping denial, and to leave the two standing together.
