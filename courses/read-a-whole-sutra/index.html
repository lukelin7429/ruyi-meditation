Read a Whole Sūtra

讀一部經 — eight short scriptures, first line to last
8 sessions · 7 live Beginner · no background needed Taught in English Free · all texts on this site

Ask someone who has read widely about Buddhism how many sūtras they have actually read from the first line to the last, and the honest answer is usually none. Not from laziness — the English-language Buddhist world is built that way. It teaches practice superbly and hands you the scriptures in fragments: a verse on a retreat handout, a line quoted in a book, a chapter excerpted in an anthology. You can spend twenty years in it and never once have the experience of opening a text, reading it whole, and closing it.

This course exists to give you that experience eight times. Every text here is short enough to read in one sitting, complete, and already translated on this site — nothing to buy, nothing to track down. Each session sets the text up in a paragraph, sends you off to read the whole thing, then comes back and reads three passages closely with you. By the end you will not have read about eight sūtras. You will have read them.

Most people who love Buddhism have never read a single scripture all the way through. In eight weeks, you will have read eight.

The promise of this course
What you'll be able to do
  1. Say honestly that you have read eight complete Buddhist scriptures — not excerpts, not summaries, not somebody's retelling.
  2. Read a sūtra the way it was built to be read — noticing its shape, its refrains, and the place where it turns — instead of mining it for quotable lines.
  3. Hold four registers of the tradition without collapsing them into each other — how to live, how to see, what to trust, what to promise.
  4. Use a classical Chinese commentary the way a reader should — as a second opinion, not a verdict — and notice when a commentator is adding something the text itself never said.
  5. Tell the difference between a text's devotional power and its canonical standing, and hold both honestly at once.
  6. Open any sūtra in this site's collection and know how to begin.
The arc of the eight

The order is not chronological and not by length. It moves through four registers, in the order a reader can actually take them — conduct first, because it asks nothing you have to believe; vows last, because by then you will know what you would be promising.

What you'll need
  • Nothing to buy. All eight texts are on this site, in full, in Ru-Yi's own English translation beside the Chinese.
  • No background at all. Every technical term is explained where it first appears. Nothing earlier in this Academy is assumed.
  • Time to actually read. The shortest text here takes four minutes; the longest, about forty. The one thing this course cannot do for you is the reading.
  • A willingness to finish something you dislike. At least one of these eight will rub you the wrong way. Read it to the end anyway — that is where the course does its real work.
How this sits beside the other courses

Sessions 3 and 4 share their texts with The Heart of the Mahāyāna, on purpose. That course reads the Heart and Diamond Sūtras through the logic of negation taught in Collected Topics — a sharp instrument, for readers who want to take the text apart. This course assumes none of that and asks a different question — not how does this argument work, but what is it like to read this scripture all the way through. Take either first. They are built to be read against each other.
