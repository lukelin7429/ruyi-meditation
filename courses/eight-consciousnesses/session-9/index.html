Reading time about thirty-five minutes Before class Sessions 1–8 The text Cheng Weishi Lun, fascicles 8–9
Part 1

Three ways of being realThe framework that reorganises everything so far

Eight sessions have produced a great deal of machinery. The three natures are how Yogācāra files it — and unlike much of what has come before, this framework is not primarily descriptive. It is diagnostic. It tells you what kind of mistake you are making.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 8 · the three natures

"Through this and that universal discrimination, one universally discriminates all manner of things; this discriminated, attached-to nature has no essence of its own whatsoever. The dependent nature is that which discrimination, arising from conditions, produces. The perfectly accomplished nature is what, within that dependent nature, is forever free of the former, discriminated-and-attached-to nature. Hence this and the dependent nature are neither different nor not different — like impermanence and similar characteristics, nor is it that seeing one precludes seeing the other."

頌曰：由彼彼遍計，遍計種種物，此遍計所執，自性無所有。依他起自性，分別緣所生。圓成實於彼，常遠離前性。故此與依他，非異非不異，如無常等性，非不見此彼。
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 8, "The Three Natures" →

Three natures, and it is critical to see that they are not three kinds of thing. They are one situation described at three levels — which is why the verse has to insist that the third and the second are "neither different nor not different."

The discriminated nature
遍計所執性
What you take things to be: fixed, independent, standing on their own. Has no essence whatsoever.
The dependent nature
依他起性
What is actually happening: conditioned arising, moment by moment. Real, though not independently.
The perfectly accomplished nature
圓成實性
The dependent nature with the first removed. Not a further thing — suchness.

The treatise's illustration is the most economical statement in the session: the third nature is what you have when you take the second and subtract the first. Nothing new appears. You do not go anywhere. What changes is that a superimposition stops.

Part 2

The word "former," and why it saves the whole systemA single character doing enormous work

The treatise then performs a close reading of its own verse, phrase by phrase, and one of its observations is the most consequential sentence in fascicle 8.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 8 · reading the verse

"The phrase "within that" shows that the perfectly accomplished nature and the dependent nature are neither identical nor separate. The phrase "forever free of" shows that the falsely grasped-at nature of a grasping subject and grasped object, in principle, is eternally nonexistent. The word "former" conveys that the dependent nature is not itself empty"

說「於彼」言：顯圓成實與依他起，不即不離；「常遠離」言：顯妄所執能、所取性，理恒非有。「前」言義顯不空依他；
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 8, "The Perfectly Accomplished Nature" →

The verse said the third nature is freedom from the former nature — the first one. Not freedom from the second. And the treatise points out that this single word is what prevents the whole system from collapsing into nihilism: the dependent nature is not itself empty.

What is empty is the superimposition — the fixity, the independence, the sense of things standing on their own. What remains is the conditioned process, which is not a delusion and does not need removing. This is the distinction Session 2 first raised and Session 8 restated: the target has never been existence, only independence.

Take what is happening. Subtract what you added. Nothing new appears.

The other phrase worth holding is eternally nonexistent. The fixed, independent selves and objects were never there — not once, not for an instant, not before the analysis and not during it. There is no moment in the past when your independent self existed and later stopped. Awakening, on this model, does not remove anything real. It stops adding something that was never real.

And the verse's closing comparison is precise: the relation of the third nature to the second is like that of impermanence to a thing. Impermanence is not a separate item alongside the cup; it is a true characteristic of the cup, not identical with it and not found apart from it. Suchness stands to conditioned arising in exactly that way.

Part 3

Five stagesAnd an honest statement of how long this takes

Fascicle 9 turns to the path, and it begins with a precondition that a modern reader may find unwelcome.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 9 · the two lineages

"First, the innately abiding lineage, meaning the uncontaminated dharma-cause, naturally possessed since beginningless time, depending upon and adhering to the root consciousness. Second, the lineage accomplished through cultivation, meaning what is brought about through habituation from hearing the dharma-realm's outflow teaching and the wisdom born from such hearing, and the like."

一、本性住種姓，謂無始來依附本識，法爾所得無漏法因。二、習所成種姓，謂聞法界等流法已，聞所成等熏習所成。
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 9, "The Path in Five Stages" →

Two lineages — and they are exactly the two kinds of seed from Session 4, applied to the capacity for awakening itself. Something uncontaminated in the storehouse from beginningless time, and something built by hearing the teaching and turning it over. Consistent, and also uncomfortable: the treatise is saying the capacity is partly a given, not wholly an achievement.

Then the five stages. It is worth having them all in view at once, because the shape of the road is itself informative.

1 · Provisioning
資糧位
Accumulating merit and understanding. Long. Nothing is seen yet.
2 · Applied practice
加行位
Intensive preparation aimed directly at the breakthrough.
3 · Realization
通達位
The path of seeing. Brief — a single moment. Suchness is directly known.
4 · Cultivation
修習位
The ten grounds. By far the longest stage, and it comes after seeing.
5 · The ultimate
究竟位
Buddhahood. The transformation of the basis is complete.

The proportions are the lesson. The moment of seeing is the third of five stages, not the last — and the stage that follows it is the longest of all. Whatever the path of seeing accomplishes, it is not finishing. Session 6 explained why: the innate self-grasping in the seventh consciousness is subtle, and the treatise said plainly that it is removed only across the ten grounds. Seeing it does not end it.

Part 4

The moment of seeingDefined entirely by what is absent

The third stage gets its own verse, and it is the strangest in the treatise, because it describes the central event of the Buddhist path exclusively in negatives.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 9 · the path of seeing

"When, with respect to the object taken, wisdom is entirely without discrimination and without anything attained, since it does not grasp at any of the various forms of conceptual proliferation — at that time, one abides in Consciousness-Only, free of the characteristics of both graspings."

頌曰：若時於所緣，智都無所得，爾時住唯識，離二取相故。
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 9, "The Path of Seeing" →

Not a vision. Not a certainty. Not an experience of the absolute. Without anything attained — 無所得. The treatise explains why the negative formulation is not modesty but precision: a mind that still involves something attained is one in which conceptual proliferation still manifests. If there is something you got, there is a getter and a gotten, and those are the two graspings that were supposed to have dropped away.

This is also the sharpest internal check the tradition offers, and it can be applied without any attainment at all. Any account of an awakening that includes a content — I saw that…, I became…, I realised the true nature of… — has, by this definition, not described the path of seeing. It has described something that happened to somebody.

The treatise adds that the wisdom that realizes suchness and suchness itself are perfectly equal. Not a knower meeting a known — the distinction that Session 5 spent its length establishing, here failing to apply. The four parts described how ordinary cognition works. This describes a cognition that is not structured that way, which is why it cannot be reported in the ordinary vocabulary and why the verse resorts to subtraction.

What Session 9 establishes
  • Three natures, one situation. What you add, what is happening, and the second with the first removed.
  • The dependent nature is not empty. One word in the verse prevents the whole system from collapsing into nihilism.
  • The superimposition was never there — not removed at awakening, but never once real.
  • Suchness stands to process as impermanence stands to a cup — a true characteristic, neither identical nor separate.
  • Seeing is the third stage of five, and the stage after it is the longest. Seeing is not finishing.
  • The path of seeing has no content. If something was attained, it was not this.
Part 5

One practiceSeparating the second nature from the first

What you added

The three natures are meant to be used as a diagnostic, and the diagnosis can be run on any ordinary difficulty. What follows is not a meditation; it is closer to an editing exercise.

Take one situation currently causing you trouble — a person, a decision, a piece of work. Write down what is happening, in as much detail as you like. Do not censor it.

Now go through what you wrote and mark every place you have described something as fixed: a permanent trait, an unchangeable circumstance, what someone is, what you are. "He is dismissive." "I'm not the kind of person who." "It's just how the department works."

Then rewrite the passage with every one of those replaced by a description of a process with conditions. Not "he is dismissive" but what he did, when, under what circumstances, how often, and what else was going on.

The rewritten version is the dependent nature; what you deleted is the discriminated nature. Two things usually become visible: how much of the original was superimposition, and that the second version is harder to write and less satisfying to read — which is a fair indication of what the fixity was doing for you.

One question to sit with

If the fixed version of your life was never once true, what have you been grieving?

The treatise's answer is oddly consoling and not at all sentimental: nothing was lost, because the thing whose loss you feel was a superimposition on a process that is still running. That is not a claim that the grief is mistaken — only that what it is about may be.

Session 10 · Transformation at the base
