Session 6 · The seventh consciousness

The one that takes it for a self

A consciousness with one object, one activity, and four permanent companions
Reading time about thirty-five minutes Before class Sessions 3–5 The text Cheng Weishi Lun, fascicles 4–5
Part 1

The part with no Western nameWhy a seventh consciousness was needed at all

If you have followed the course this far, a question should have been accumulating. Session 3 said the storehouse is unknowable, neutral, and never feels anything. Session 1 said the sense of being a self is produced. But by what? Not the storehouse — it does not know anything in the ordinary sense. Not the thinking mind — the sense of self does not switch off when you stop thinking about yourself, and it does not go away in dreamless sleep.

6Thinking mind意識
7Manas末那識
8Storehouse阿賴耶識

The seventh consciousness is the answer, and it is the most distinctive item in Yogācāra. Other Buddhist schools had six. This school added one, and the addition is not a refinement — it changes what the sense of self is. Not a belief, not a habit of thought, not a cultural construction. A continuously running cognitive process with its own object.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 4 · the manas verses

"Next, the second transformation is the consciousness called manas. It turns in dependence on that [first consciousness], takes it as its object, and has deliberation as its nature and characteristic."

"Four afflictions constantly accompany it: self-delusion and self-view, together with self-conceit and self-love, and other factors such as contact besides."

"It belongs to the covered-yet-neutral moral category, and is bound according to wherever it is born. It is absent in arhats, in cessation-attainment, and on the supramundane path."

次第二能變，是識名末那，依彼轉，緣彼，思量為性相。
四煩惱常俱，謂：我癡、我見，并我慢、我愛，及餘觸等俱。
有覆無記攝，隨所生所繫。阿羅漢、滅定、出世道無有。

Everything about this consciousness is in those three lines. It has exactly one object — the storehouse. It has exactly one activity — deliberation, 思量, a continuous considering-and-appraising. And it is never without its four companions.

Part 2

One object, held without pauseWhat manas is actually looking at

The relation between the seventh and the eighth is the engine of the whole model, and it is worth stating slowly.

The storehouse is a stream — changing every instant, carrying the accumulated freight of everything that has happened. Manas observes it. But it does not observe it accurately. It sees a continuity, and it reads that continuity as an entity. It sees something that is always there, and concludes that there is something that is always there, and that this something is itself.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 4 · the constant object

"from beginningless time until the transformation of basis, this manas spontaneously and constantly takes the storehouse consciousness as its object, and is associated with four root afflictions."

謂從無始，至未轉依，此意任運恒緣藏識，與四根本煩惱相應。
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 4, "The Four Afflictions That Never Leave Manas" →

Three words in that sentence carry the weight. Spontaneously (任運) — it runs by itself, not as anything you do. Constantly (恒) — without interruption, including in dreamless sleep, under anaesthetic, and between thoughts. And from beginningless time until the transformation of basis — meaning it does not stop when you understand that it is happening. Session 10 is about what "transformation of basis" means; the point here is that it names something further along than comprehension.

This explains the odd persistence of the thing. You can follow the argument in Session 1, agree with every step, and still flinch when someone insults you. There is no contradiction. The argument was conducted in the sixth consciousness. The flinch belongs to a different process, which was not consulted and has not stopped.

The mistake is not being made by the part of you that is reading this.
Part 3

Four afflictions, and why exactly fourDelusion, view, conceit, love — all with the same prefix

Manas never travels alone. Four afflictions accompany it permanently, and the treatise defines each in a single clause.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 4 · the four defined

"Self-delusion is ignorance: being benighted about the mark of self, deluded about the principle of no-self — hence it is called self-delusion. Self-view is self-grasping: falsely conceiving what is not a self to be a self — hence it is called self-view. Self-conceit is arrogance: relying on this clung-to self, it makes the mind swell with pride — hence it is called self-conceit. Self-love is craving for self: growing deeply attached and infatuated with this clung-to self — hence it is called self-love."

我癡者：謂無明，愚於我相，迷無我理，故名我癡。我見者：謂我執，於非我法，妄計為我，故名我見。我慢者：謂倨傲，恃所執我，令心高舉，故名我慢。我愛者：謂我貪，於所執我，深生耽著，故名我愛。
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 4, "The Four Afflictions That Never Leave Manas" →

Notice that these are not four separate problems. They are one movement in four stages, and the Chinese makes it visible: every one of them begins with 我, self. Not-knowing produces a misidentification; the misidentification produces something to defend; what is defended becomes what is cherished.

Cherishing. Deeply attached and infatuated with the clung-to self.

The treatise then asks its own best question: there are ten root afflictions in the standard list — why does manas have only these four? The answer is precise and tells you a great deal about how the model works. Anger cannot be here, because it loves what it looks at; you do not resent what you cherish. Doubt cannot be here, because self-view has already decided the matter, and a decision leaves no room for a question. And no second view can be here, because a single mind cannot hold two acts of discriminating judgment at once.

So the seventh consciousness is not the seat of your bad temper. It is something quieter and worse-placed: a permanent, untroubled, affectionate certainty about something that is not the case.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 4 · what the four do

"These four constantly arise, disturbing and clouding the inner mind, and causing the outer, evolving consciousnesses to become permanently defiled. It is because of these that sentient beings revolve through birth and death, unable to escape — hence they are called "afflictions.""

此四常起，擾濁內心，令外轉識恒成雜染。有情由此生死輪迴，不能出離，故名煩惱。
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 4, "The Four Afflictions That Never Leave Manas" →

This is the claim that makes the seventh consciousness matter practically rather than only architecturally. Manas does not merely hold a false view privately. It colours everything upstream. Every act of seeing, hearing and thinking passes through a mind already committed to there being someone this is happening to — which is why ordinary perception is never quite neutral, and why the correction cannot be made at the level of perception.

Part 4

Covered, but not wickedAnd the three places where it stops

The verse's moral classification is easy to skim and worth pausing on: manas is covered-yet-neutral (有覆無記). It is not morally bad. It does not commit wrongdoing, and it produces no karma of its own. What it does is cover — obscure, veil, keep something from being seen.

This distinction matters, and it is one the tradition insists on. The problem at the root of things is not wickedness. It is a structural obscuration that operates continuously, impartially, and without malice. You cannot repent your way out of it, because it is not a fault. It is a lens.

The verse ends by naming the only three conditions in which manas is absent: in arhats, in cessation-attainment, and on the supramundane path. The second is instructive — a meditative attainment in which it temporarily stops, which is exactly the state Session 3 used to argue for the eighth consciousness in the first place. It does not stop in ordinary sleep. It does not stop in deep concentration. It does not stop when you are absorbed in work and "lose yourself," which turns out to be a figure of speech.

Fascicle 5 gives the argument that it must exist, and the form is the same one used for the eighth: identify something everyone accepts, show the six cannot account for it. The best of them concerns ordinary wholesome activity. You can act generously without any thought of yourself present — and yet the act still reinforces, rather than dissolves, the sense of being a person who did something. Something must be adding that, and it cannot be the sixth consciousness, which was occupied with the giving.

What Session 6 establishes
  • The sense of self is a process, not a belief. It has an object — the storehouse — and it runs whether or not you are thinking.
  • It misreads continuity as identity. Something is always there, so it concludes something is always there, and that it is itself.
  • Four afflictions, one movement: not knowing, misidentifying, elevating, cherishing.
  • No anger and no doubt here — it cannot resent what it loves, and self-view has already settled the question.
  • Covered, not wicked. An obscuration that produces no karma of its own — which is why it cannot be repented away.
  • It does not stop when you understand it. It stops in arhats, in cessation, and on the supramundane path.
Part 5

One practiceCatching the fourth affliction in the wild

The flinch

Manas cannot be observed directly — it takes the unknowable as its object, and it is not the thing doing your looking. But its effects upstream are observable, and the fourth affliction, self-love, is the easiest to catch, because it is fastest.

For three days, watch for the flinch: the involuntary contraction when something touches your standing. Being corrected in front of someone. Being left out. Hearing your work described inaccurately. Catching your own reflection unexpectedly.

Do not analyse it and do not try to stop it. Note only two things. How fast was it? — specifically, did it arrive before or after you had time to think anything. And what was being protected? Not "my reputation" or "my feelings," which are already stories. Something simpler and more like a position being defended.

What the exercise can show is the timing, and the timing is the whole point. If the flinch reliably precedes thought, then whatever produced it was not thinking — and that is the one thing about the seventh consciousness that ordinary experience can actually corroborate.

One question to sit with

If something in you has been affectionately certain of your existence for as long as there has been a you, what would it even mean to argue with it?

The treatise's answer is that you would not argue with it, because it is not holding a position it could be talked out of. What changes it is not persuasion but a change at the base — which is the subject of the last session of this course, and is described there as a transformation rather than a correction.
