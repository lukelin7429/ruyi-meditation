Session 10 · Where the course has been going

Transformation at the base

The eight become four wisdoms — and nothing is replaced
Reading time about thirty-five minutes Before class Sessions 1–9 The text Cheng Weishi Lun, fascicle 10
Part 1

Turning, not swappingWhat 轉依 actually names

The phrase has been arriving since Session 6 without explanation: the transformation of the basis, 轉依. It is where the course ends, and the whole weight of the model rests on getting the word right.

1–5The five senses前五識
6Thinking mind意識
7Manas末那識
8Storehouse阿賴耶識

轉 is to turn, to revolve, to convert — the same character used in 轉識, the "evolving" consciousnesses, and in 恒轉, the constant flowing of the torrent. 依 is what something rests on, its basis. So: the basis turns.

Not: the basis is discarded and a better one installed. Not: you acquire something you did not have. The very thing that has been going wrong is the thing that turns. This is the same grammar as Session 9's three natures — take what is happening, subtract what was added, and nothing new appears.

It is worth pausing on how unusual this is as a soteriology. There is no arrival anywhere else. There is no true self uncovered beneath the false one. The storehouse that has been carrying your accumulated freight since beginningless time does not stop and hand over; it turns, and what it turns into is described next.

Part 2

Four wisdomsEach one is a consciousness you have already met

The treatise's account of buddhahood is not a description of a state. It is a list of four functioning minds, and the elegance of the scheme is that each maps onto one of the eight.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 10 · the great perfect mirror wisdom

"First, the mind associated with the great, perfect mirror wisdom: this mind is free of every discrimination; its object and mode of activity are subtle and difficult to know; it neither forgets nor is deluded; every objective aspect is, in nature and characteristic, pure, free of every defilement, possessing purely pure and perfect virtue; it serves as the basis sustaining both manifest activity and seeds, capable of manifesting and generating the reflected images of body, land, and wisdom, without interruption, throughout all future time — like a great, perfect mirror, manifesting every form of image."

一、大圓鏡智相應心品：謂此心品，離諸分別，所緣、行相，微細難知，不忘不愚，一切境相，性相清淨，離諸雜染，純淨圓德，現種依持，能現、能生身、土智影，無間無斷，窮未來際，如大圓鏡，現眾色像。
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 10, "What Is Attained — The Four Wisdoms" →

Read that against Session 3 and the correspondence is exact. The storehouse's object was unknowable; the mirror wisdom's object is subtle and difficult to know. The storehouse held seeds without letting them be lost; the mirror sustains both manifest activity and seeds. The storehouse ran without interruption; so does this. Every structural feature is retained.

What has changed is one thing. The storehouse was morally indeterminate and clung to as a self. The mirror is free of every discrimination, and nothing is holding it. Same function, no grasping — and the image is the point: a mirror holds nothing and reflects everything, and is not altered by what passes across it.

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 10 · the wisdom of equality

"Second, the mind associated with the wisdom of equality: this mind contemplates all dharmas, oneself, and other sentient beings, as entirely equal alike. It is constantly and jointly associated with great compassion and loving-kindness,"

二、平等性智相應心品：謂此心品，觀一切法，自、他有情，悉皆平等。大慈悲等，恒共相應，
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 10, "What Is Attained — The Four Wisdoms" →

This is the one to dwell on. The second wisdom is the turned form of manas — the consciousness whose entire business was dividing self from not-self, permanently and without pause. Turned, it becomes the faculty that sees self and others as entirely equal alike.

And notice what it is now constantly joined to. Manas was never without its four afflictions. The wisdom of equality is never without great compassion and loving-kindness. The permanence is the same; the content is inverted. Compassion here is not an attitude someone adopts. It is what that machinery does once it stops dividing.

The organ of self-concern, turned, is the organ of compassion. It is not replaced. It is the same thing, no longer dividing.

The remaining two follow the same pattern. The wisdom of wondrous contemplation is the turned sixth consciousness — still discerning particular and shared characteristics, exactly as the thinking mind always did, but now "operating without obstruction," severing others' doubts rather than generating its own. And the wisdom of accomplishing what is to be done is the turned five senses: the faculties that merely took in sights and sounds become the means by which a buddha acts in the world, "accomplishing whatever task the power of one's original vow calls for."

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 10 · consciousness into wisdom

"These four minds, taken together, entirely encompass every conditioned virtue belonging to the stage of buddhahood, and are obtained by transforming the minds associated with the contaminated eighth, seventh, sixth, and five sense-consciousnesses, respectively. Although wisdom is not itself consciousness, it operates in dependence on consciousness, and, since consciousness is the more prominent factor, it is said that consciousness is transformed into wisdom."

此轉有漏八、七、六、五識相應品，如次而得。智雖非識，而依識轉，識為主故，說轉識得。
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 10, "What Is Attained — The Four Wisdoms" →

Even here the treatise refuses to let its own formula harden. Wisdom is not itself consciousness — the phrase "consciousness is transformed into wisdom" is a manner of speaking, adopted because consciousness is the more prominent factor, and, the text adds, in order to encourage beings to rely on wisdom and relinquish consciousness. Ten fascicles in, it is still marking its own vocabulary as provisional.

Part 3

The last refusal, and the last verseHow a treatise this exacting decides to stop

The treatise has one more move before it closes, and it is the one this course has watched it make at every level. Having described buddha-bodies and buddha-lands in detail, it immediately declines to let them be taken as real things standing on their own: the perceived aspects, though depending on consciousness to transform and manifest, are not genuinely real in the same way that consciousness's own nature, as the dependent nature, is genuinely real. Even a pure land is a transformation. Even at the end, the rule holds.

Then the title, explained: three parts establishing Consciousness-Only — its characteristics, its nature, its stages. Which is exactly the shape of this course: Sessions 3 to 7 were the characteristics, Sessions 8 and 9 the nature, Sessions 9 and 10 the stages.

And then, after ten fascicles of the most exacting technical argument in the Buddhist canon — hundreds of mental factors, dozens of rival masters weighed against each other, every position tested and most of them rejected — this is the last thing it says:

Cheng Weishi Lun · fascicle 10 · the closing dedication

"Having, in reliance on the sacred teachings and correct reasoning, distinguished the meaning of Consciousness-Only's nature and characteristics, may the merit thereby obtained be bestowed upon all beings, that we may together swiftly ascend to unsurpassed awakening."

已依聖教及正理，分別唯識性相義，所獲功德施群生，願共速登無上覺。
Read this passage in its place — fascicle 10, "The Treatise's Own Name, and Its Closing Dedication" →

No claim to have settled anything. No summary of results. The last gesture of the most rigorous work in this tradition is to give away whatever it was worth — and the word is together, 共.

That ending is not decoration on the argument; it follows from it. A treatise that spent fascicle 7 insisting there is not one consciousness but many, and fascicle 10 describing the seventh consciousness turning into the faculty that sees self and other as equal, could not coherently end with a private accomplishment.

What Session 10 establishes — and what the course has argued
  • 轉依 is a turning, not a replacement. Nothing is discarded and nothing new is installed.
  • Each wisdom is a consciousness you already have. Eighth to mirror, seventh to equality, sixth to contemplation, five senses to action.
  • Compassion is structural. It is what the organ of self-concern does once it stops dividing — not an attitude adopted.
  • The vocabulary stays provisional to the last page. "Consciousness becomes wisdom" is a manner of speaking.
  • The treatise ends by giving the merit away — the only ending consistent with everything it argued.
Part 4

Where to go from hereThe rest of the library

The Cheng Weishi Lun is complete on this site in all ten fascicles, Chinese beside English, and this course has quoted perhaps a fiftieth of it. Almost everything has been left out — the ten arguments for the eighth consciousness, the four conditions and ten causes, the twelve links, the ten grounds with their obstacles and their kinds of suchness. If any session here made you want the full argument rather than a route through it, the treatise itself is the next thing to read.

Yogācāra · 成唯識論 · Taishō T31, no. 1585

The Cheng Weishi Lun, complete

Ten fascicles, Chinese source beside Ru-Yi's own English translation, with reading notes throughout. Xuanzang's 659 CE synthesis of ten Indian commentaries on Vasubandhu's Thirty Verses.

Beyond it, the same library holds the hundred fascicles of the Yogācārabhūmi, the Mahāyānasaṃgraha, the Madhyāntavibhāga, the Abhidharmasamuccaya and the Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra — the rest of the school, all translated here.

And the obvious next course is the argument on the other side. Yogācāra says the dependent nature is not empty. Madhyamaka is the school that spent centuries pressing on exactly that claim, and Nothing Stands, the second Layer 4 elective, reads Nāgārjuna at length.

Part 5

One practiceThe last one, and the smallest

The same faculty, not dividing

Nine sessions of practices have each isolated one piece of the machinery. This one is for the claim that the second wisdom rests on: that the faculty producing self-concern and the faculty producing compassion are not two faculties.

Sit for fifteen minutes. Spend the first five noticing the ordinary background hum of self-reference — the low, continuous monitoring of how things stand for you. Do not suppress it; just register that it is running.

Then bring to mind one person you find genuinely difficult. Not an enemy; someone who reliably irritates you. Notice that the same monitoring immediately reorganises itself around them: how they affect you, what they owe you, how you compare.

Now do not try to feel warmly toward them. Instead, hold one question steadily, without answering it: is the thing doing the comparing capable of doing anything else? When it produces an answer, drop the answer and hold the question again.

This is not a technique for generating compassion, and it will very likely not produce any. What it can show is the machinery from Session 6 running in real time on a real case — which is the closest ordinary experience gets to the claim this session ends on. The treatise puts the actual turning at buddhahood, and it would be a poor finish to a course this careful to imply otherwise.

One question to close on

If nothing about you has to be got rid of, and nothing has to be added, what exactly is the work?

Ten sessions have given one answer, and it is worth stating plainly at the end. The work is not acquisition and not destruction. It is that a mistake which is being made continuously, below the level of choice, by a faculty you cannot reach directly, stops being made — and the treatise is honest that this takes longer than understanding it, and that understanding it is nonetheless where anyone has to begin.
