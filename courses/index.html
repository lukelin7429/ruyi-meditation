Fifty-Three Teachers

五十三位老師 — Sudhana walks south, and learns from whoever is in front of him

A boy is told to go south and find a teacher. Each teacher he finds teaches him one thing, insists it is the only thing they know, and sends him on to somebody else — fifty-three times. Thirteen sessions follow a route through them: a physician, a girl, an incense-seller, a sea-captain, a king who only appears to be cruel, a teacher from another religion, and at the end a tower large enough to hold the cosmos.

The Heart of the Mahāyāna

大乘之心 — the great vehicle's own words, starting with the Heart Sūtra

Reads the Mahāyāna's own scriptures the way The Buddha's Own Words reads the earliest suttas — starting with the shortest, most essential text in the whole canon. The Three Fires

三把火 — the psychology of greed, hatred, and delusion, across six suttas

Reads six suttas on greed, hatred, and delusion as a mechanism rather than a list of sins — the attention that lets them arise, all three named as one fire, each one taken singly with its own specific method, and the shortest definition of their ending in the whole Pali Canon. Pairs naturally with the Abhidhamma's own technical chapter on the same three roots.

Boundless in Every Direction

四梵住 — loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity, across four suttas

Two brahmins argue over whose teacher correctly taught the path to union with Brahmā; the Buddha's answer replaces an unverifiable ritual claim with four cultivable states anyone can develop and check. Reads the foundational practice text, the argument against empty credential, why the four abodes are not interchangeable, and eleven concrete benefits of keeping the practice up.

The Owner of Your Deeds

業與轉世 — kamma read as causal fit, not fate or a cosmic ledger

"Sentient beings are the owners of their deeds and heir to their deeds" — unpacked across four suttas into a precise technical definition, the pattern it produces, the pattern's own honest limits (a genuinely observed single case never licenses a universal law), and the one deed that ends the whole mechanism. Pairs naturally with the Abhidhamma's own chapter on death and rebirth-linking.

The shape of the whole thing

Five layers, one path

The Academy's courses are not a shelf of unrelated offerings. They are five layers of one path — each built on the texts the layer before it taught you to read. This is the whole plan, including the parts not built yet.

Stories the Buddha Told

All 10 live
佛陀講過的故事 · 10 sessions

The door for someone who has never encountered Buddhism at all. One story a session, one question a session, and not a single technical term anywhere. No doctrine is taught; it is simply assumed that a person who has been moved by a story will want to know where it came from.

Built from the fifty fascicles of the 經律異相 anthology and the Buddhacarita, both complete on this site.

Read a Whole Sūtra

All 8 live
讀一部經 · 8 sessions

The core of the Academy. Eight short scriptures, each read from the first line to the last — conduct, then wisdom, then trust, then vows. The promise is plain and unusual: finish the course and you will have read eight complete Buddhist scriptures, not eight summaries of them.

All eight texts already translated on this site, Chinese beside English.

Six Questions

All 9 live
六個問題 · 9 sessions

Six questions anyone asks eventually — who am I, what is mind, why do I suffer, what is death, what counts as living well, is any of it meaningful. Each one answered twice, once from the Southern tradition and once from the Northern, side by side, so you can watch two schools work on the same problem and decide for yourself what changed. Almost nothing in English does this.

From the Visuddhimagga on one side; Yogācāra, Madhyamaka, and the Pure Land scriptures on the other — all translated on this site from Pāli and Chinese.
3

Fifty-Three Teachers

Sessions 1–7 live
五十三位老師 — 善財的旅程 · one teacher per session

The Academy's signature course, and the one that exists nowhere else. Sudhana walks south and studies under fifty-three teachers in turn — a physician, a girl, a man who used to sell incense, a sea-captain, a king who only appears to be cruel, and a teacher from outside the tradition altogether. It is a road story about the plainest and most demanding idea in the tradition: that you can learn from anyone.

From Ru-Yi's own translation of the Gaṇḍavyūha — the Avataṃsaka's final book, complete on this site across all 454 parts.

Coming
學派深入 · electives

For the few who walk the first three layers and want to go further: Yogācāra as Buddhist psychology, Madhyamaka as the dismantling of every position, and the Perfection of Wisdom literature at full length rather than in summary. These are electives, and they are meant to be hard.

The hundred fascicles of the Yogācārabhūmi, the Cheng Weishi Lun, the Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra, and the Madhyamaka treatises — all on this site.

Layers are published in order, and each is announced here as it opens. The texts themselves do not wait for the courses — the whole sūtra collection and Theravāda library are already open, in full, for anyone who wants to read ahead.
