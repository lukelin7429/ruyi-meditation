Session 5 · Two kinds of "no"

What It Means to Deny Something

遮遣與成立 — a denial that clears space, and a denial that quietly fills it back in
Before class: Sessions 1–4 — same four moves, new subject.

Same toggle as before — every debate line below has both versions.

Part 1

The Puzzle

Compare two negative sentences:

"There is no elephant in this room."
"He is not rich."

Both use "no" / "not." Both deny something. But read them again and notice what's left over after the denial. The first sentence tells you exactly one thing: no elephant. It doesn't hint at what is in the room — could be a chair, could be nothing, could be a piano. The denial just clears space and stops.

The second sentence does something sneakier. "Not rich" doesn't just clear space — it leaves you assuming he has some amount of money, just not a lot. You'd be surprised if it turned out he had zero dollars and no possessions at all; "not rich" quietly smuggled in "has some means" even while it was busy denying "wealthy." One negation leaves nothing behind. The other leaves something standing.

Part 2

The New IdeaTwo kinds of negation

Quick recap:

"So…""It follows that…" "That's not true.""The reason is not established." "That doesn't prove it.""There is no pervasion." "Yes, I agree.""I accept it."
Flat Denial無遮 (med dgag)
Denies something and implies nothing else in its place."No elephant in this room" — nothing about what IS there
Loaded Denial非遮 (ma yin dgag)
Denies something while quietly implying something specific remains."Not rich" — implies some, just not much, wealth

The classic textbook case: "the heavy man does not eat during the day." On the surface it's just a denial about daytime eating. But everyone who hears it draws the same extra conclusion without being told: he must eat at night — otherwise how is he heavy? The sentence is doing two jobs at once: denying one thing, implying another.

Part 3

Worked ExampleSorting a denial into the right bucket

Challenger
"So, 'there is no elephant in this room' is a Loaded Denial, because it's a negative sentence." "It follows that this is an affirming negation, because it is a negative statement."
Testing whether "negative sentence" alone is enough to sort it.
Defender
"That doesn't prove it!" "There is no pervasion."
Being negative is true of both kinds — it can't be the sorting test. This sentence implies nothing specific: it's a Flat Denial.
Challenger
"So, 'the heavy man doesn't eat during the day' is a Loaded Denial, because it implies he eats at night." "It follows that this is an affirming negation, because it implies nighttime eating."
This time the test is the actual test: something specific is left standing.
Defender
"Yes, I agree." "I accept it."
A positive claim survives the denial — that's exactly what makes it Loaded.
The actual test, not the shortcut
  • Wrong test: "Does it use the word 'not'?" — both kinds do, so this sorts nothing.
  • Right test: "After the denial, is anything specific left implied?" Nothing left → Flat Denial. Something specific left → Loaded Denial.
Why this one matters for you specifically

If you teach English, you already teach a cousin of this skill under a different name — reading comprehension questions that ask "what does the text imply, not just state?" This session's Loaded Denial is exactly that instinct, formalized into a testable category.

Part 4

PracticeSame three-move check as always

1. "So, 'this shirt is not blue' is a Loaded Denial, because it uses the word 'not.'"
"That doesn't prove it!""There is no pervasion." — using "not" is true but doesn't sort anything. "Not blue" implies nothing about which color it actually is — red, green, anything. It's a Flat Denial.
2. "So, 'he is not poor' is a Loaded Denial, because it implies he has at least some money."
"Yes, I agree.""I accept it." — something specific survives the denial: at least some means. Loaded Denial.
3. "So, 'unicorns do not exist' is a Loaded Denial, because it names a specific creature."
"That doesn't prove it!""There is no pervasion." — naming something specific is true, but nothing positive is implied about what does exist instead. Flat Denial.
