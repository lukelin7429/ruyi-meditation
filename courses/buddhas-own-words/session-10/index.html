Session 10 · Emptiness & letting go

The Anatomy of a Moment

MN 148 — Chachakka Sutta, "The Six Sets of Six"
Before class: Read MN 148. It's the most technical text in our course, and it repeats itself on purpose — the repetition is the meditation. Don't rush. Read it the way you'd trace a diagram slowly with your finger.
Part 1

Lecture Notes

Slowing a moment down until you can see inside it

Everything so far has been leading here. In Session 9 we found no fixed self among the changing parts of experience. But how does experience actually work, moment to moment, with no self running it? MN 148 answers by slowing a single instant of experience — seeing a color, hearing a word — almost to a stop, and showing the impersonal machinery inside it. The Buddha counts six things, each in a set of six (hence the odd title), but you only need to feel the chain they form.

How one moment of experience assembles itself
  1. A sense (say, the eye) —
  2. meets its object (a form) —
  3. giving rise to awareness of it (seeing) —
  4. the three together make contact —
  5. out of contact arises a feeling-tone (pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral) —
  6. and toward that feeling springs craving (wanting more, or wanting it gone).

Six times over — for eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind — the same chain runs. Notice there is no "self" listed anywhere in it. It's a process assembling and dissolving, conditioned link by link, with nobody behind the curtain. This is one of the things "emptiness" points at: experience is empty of a separate self running it, though it is vividly, undeniably happening.

Where the whole thing turns — the feeling link

The Buddha then shows exactly where we get caught. Bound up with each feeling lies a hidden reflex — the tradition calls these underlying tendencies:

  1. Meet a pleasant feeling and, unexamined, the reflex of grasping fires — pull it closer. (Greed.)
  2. Meet an unpleasant feeling and the reflex of pushing away fires — get rid of it. (Aversion.)
  3. Meet a neutral feeling and the reflex of not-noticing fires — drift, miss it entirely. (Delusion.)

Here is the hinge of the whole path. If you take these feelings as mine — "I am pleased," "I am hurt" — the reflex owns you and the moment tightens into craving and suffering. But if, right at the feeling, you see clearly "this is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self," the reflex has nothing to grab. The chain, so to speak, is cut where it does the most good.

"Seeing this, a well-taught disciple grows disenchanted… through dispassion the heart is freed. When it is freed there comes the knowledge: it is freed."

That word disenchanted isn't gloom. It's the spell breaking — the compulsive pull of "want it / hate it / ignore it" losing its grip, so that experience can simply be met, and released. This is what all ten sessions have been quietly walking toward: not adding something exotic, but seeing clearly enough to let go.

Three ideas to carry out of today
  • Experience is a process, not a possession. Sense → object → contact → feeling → craving, with no self hidden inside running it.
  • The feeling link is the hinge. Grasping, aversion, and drift all fire right there. Meet the feeling clearly and the reflex finds nothing to hold.
  • Letting go is the fruit of seeing, not forcing. "Disenchantment" is the spell breaking — freedom, not deadness.
Part 2

Primary TextRead & annotate

Read MN 148 — the six sixes, the not-self refrain applied to each link, the three underlying tendencies, and the turn to liberation. As you read, mark up your copy:

!surprises you / hits hard
?you don't understand
★want to remember
~want to push back on

Don't fight the repetition — ride it. Pick one sense (the eye is easiest) and trace its full chain slowly in the margin, in your own words. Once you see it clearly for one sense, the other five are the same shape.

Part 3

Discussion Questions

  1. Walk one real recent moment through the chain — something you saw or heard that grabbed you. Can you locate the exact point where "feeling" turned into "craving"?
  2. The Buddha says there's no self anywhere in the chain, yet experience clearly happens. Does "a process with no owner" make sense to you, or does it still feel like something's missing?
  3. Neutral feelings trigger the reflex of not-noticing. What in your daily life do you routinely space out on — and what might change if you actually met it?
  4. "Disenchantment" sounds negative in English, but here it means freedom. Have you ever had a compulsion simply lose its grip once you saw it clearly? What was that like?
  5. Looking back across all ten sessions — from the poisoned arrow to this moment-by-moment chain — what thread do you now see running through the whole path?
Part 4

AssignmentChoose one · bring it next session

Option A — Catching the hinge

Several times this week, when something pleasant or unpleasant grabs you, pause and try to catch the feeling before the reflex — and silently note "this is not mine." You won't always manage it; that's fine. Write a few lines on what you noticed about the gap between feeling and reaction.

Option B — Mapping one moment

Choose one vivid moment from your week and write it up as the six-link chain, in plain language — sense, object, awareness, contact, feeling, craving — naming each link as it actually unfolded for you. Then note: where could it have been released, and what would that have taken?

Part 5

Self-Check

Take the short quiz for MN 148 on its reading page. You don't need all thirty-six items — you need the chain (sense → … → craving) and the three tendencies bound to pleasant, unpleasant, and neutral feeling. Bring anything still knotted.
