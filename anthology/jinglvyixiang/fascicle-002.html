A Jackal Teaches the King of the Gods

This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation of fascicle 2 of the Jīng Lǜ Yì Xiàng (經律異相): "Heavenly Beings, Part 2" (天部下), eleven self-contained stories of the desire- and form-realm gods — Śakra bowing before a dying jackal to receive the Dharma, a deva who takes refuge in the Three Jewels in the last instant before a hellish rebirth, a king's vanishing merit and a heavenly being's inexhaustible water, and the songs of heaven heard by a wandering musician.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 2 complete Source: Taishō T2121, vol. 53, fascicle 2 Compiled by: Bǎochàng and others, 516 CE

Translation Method

Where fascicle 1 was continuous cosmological description, fascicle 2 shows the compendium's more typical texture: eleven short, self-contained narratives, each with its own title, its own cast, and its own closing citation. The translation keeps every story's structure intact — title, narrative, and source — rather than smoothing them into one continuous essay, since that discreteness is exactly how this leishu is meant to be read and consulted.

1. Śakra Receives the Precepts from a Jackal

A jackal, dying in a well after being chased by a lion, recites a verse of confession so extraordinary that Śakra, king of the gods, descends with eighty thousand devas to hear him teach — and is told, first, that he has not shown the jackal proper respect.

Chinese Source

昔比摩國從陀山有一野干。為師子所逐墮一丘野井。已經三日開心分死。自說偈言。

一切皆無常　恨不飯師子
奈何罪厄身　貪命無功死
無功已可恨　復污人中水
懺悔十方佛　願垂照我心
前世諸惡業　現償皆令盡
從是值明師　修行盡作佛

Ru-Yi Translation

Long ago, on Mount Congtuo in the country of Bimo, there was a jackal. Chased by a lion, it fell into a wild well on a hillside. Three days had already passed, its heart resolved to accept death, and it spoke this verse of its own accord:

"All things are impermanent — I regret only that I did not become the lion's meal.
What use is this offending, hemmed-in body, clinging to life and dying for nothing?
Dying for nothing is regret enough — and now I foul the water that people drink.
I confess before the buddhas of the ten directions; may you look with compassion on my heart.
Every evil karma of my past lives — let it all be repaid and exhausted here and now.
From this, may I meet a wise teacher, and through practice at last become a buddha."

帝釋聞之。與八萬諸天追尋所在飛到井側。曰不聞聖教久幽冥無導師。向說非凡語願為宣法教。答曰。天帝無教訓。大不識時宜。法師在下自處其上。初不修敬。而問法要。帝釋垂天衣接取野干叩頭懺悔。天帝言曰。憶念我昔曾見世人欲聞正法。先敷高坐莊飾清淨後請法師。諸天即各脫天寶衣積為高座。野干昇座曰。有二大因緣。一者說法開化天人福無量故。二者為報施食恩故豈得不說。

Śakra heard this, and together with eighty thousand devas flew in search of the place until they reached the side of the well. He said, "For a long time we have heard no sacred teaching, benighted with no guide; what you have just spoken is no ordinary speech — please expound the Dharma for us." The jackal answered: "You, the heavenly lord, are without proper instruction — you fail entirely to recognize what is fitting to the occasion. The Dharma teacher is below, while you place yourself above him; without first showing reverence, you ask for the essentials of the Dharma." Śakra let down his heavenly robe, drew the jackal up by it, and bowed his head in apology. The heavenly lord said: "I recall that in the past I have seen how, when people of the world wished to hear the true Dharma, they would first spread out a high seat, adorned and purified, before inviting the Dharma teacher." The devas then each removed their own heavenly jeweled robes and piled them into a high seat. The jackal ascended the seat and said: "There are two great reasons I cannot but speak: first, that expounding the Dharma to open and transform gods and men brings immeasurable merit; second, to repay the kindness of the food I was once given — how could I fail to speak?"

天帝白曰。得免井厄功報應大。云何說法報恩不及此耶。答曰。生死其宜。各有其人。有人貪生。有人樂死。有愚癡人。不知死後更生。違遠佛法。不值明師。殺盜婬欺。惟惡是與。如此之人。貪生畏死。死墮地獄。有智慧人。奉事三寶。遭遇明師。改惡修善。孝養父母。敬事師長。眷屬和從。謙敬下物。如斯之人。惡生樂死。死生天上。帝釋曰。如尊所誨。全其軀命。無功夫者。願聞施食施法。野干答曰。布施飲食。濟一日之命。施珍寶物。濟一世之乏。增益生死。繫縛因緣。說法教化。名為法施。能令眾生出世間道。一者得羅漢。二者辟支佛。三者佛道。此三樂人。皆從聞法。如說修行。又諸眾生。免三惡道。受人天福樂。皆由聞法。是故佛說。以法布施。功德無量。

The heavenly lord said, "To be freed from the calamity of the well is already so great a merit — how could expounding the Dharma to repay a kindness not measure up to that?" The jackal answered: "Life and death each suit different people, each according to his kind. Some cling to life; some delight in death. There are those foolish and deluded who do not know that after death there is further birth, who turn their backs on the Buddha's teaching, never meet a wise teacher, kill, steal, commit sexual misconduct, and deceive, doing nothing but evil. Such people cling to life and fear death, and at death fall into hell. There are those of wisdom who serve the Three Jewels, meet a wise teacher, correct their evils and cultivate the good, care filially for their parents, revere their teachers and elders, live in harmony with their family, and are humble toward others. Such people find life hateful and death a delight, and at death are born in heaven." Śakra said, "As the venerable one has instructed, then preserving my own bodily life was of no real merit at all — I wish now to hear of the giving of food and the giving of the Dharma." The jackal answered: "To give food and drink saves a life for a single day; to give precious treasures relieves want for a single lifetime — but both only add to the causes that bind one to birth and death. To expound the Dharma and teach others is called the giving of the Dharma, and it alone leads sentient beings onto the path beyond the world: to the fruit of the arhat, to the pratyekabuddha, or to buddhahood — these three joys all come from hearing the Dharma and practicing as it teaches. And all sentient beings who escape the three evil destinies and receive the blessings of birth as human or god do so entirely through hearing the Dharma. This is why the Buddha said the merit of giving the Dharma is immeasurable."

天帝曰。師今此形。為是業報。應化身耶。答曰。是罪業報。非應化也。天人曰。我意謂。是菩薩聖人應現濟物。方聞罪果。未知其故。願聞因緣。野干曰。昔生波羅㮈波頭摩城。為貧家子。剎利種姓。幼懷聰朗。特好學習。至年十二。隨逐明師。在於深山。辛苦奉事。翹勤不懈。師亦晨夜。切磋教授。不失時節。經五十年。九十六種。經書讖記論。醫方呪術。瞻相吉凶。災異禍福。靡所不達。高才智慧。名聞四遠。乃自思惟曰。今獲濟拔。皆由和上教化之恩。其功難報。家既貧乏。無可供養。唯當賣身以報師恩。師曰。山居道士。乞食自存。正無所乏。何用毀賣貴身為供我也。子今成就智慧辯才。當轉教化天下人民。為法燈明。教化之功。豈不足報於我之恩。遂住山中。乞食自資。不久國王崩。群臣集國內學士五百餘人。講論七日。勝者為王。是貧家子。享受王位。盡國財力。供養師及父母。

The heavenly lord said, "Teacher, is this form of yours now a karmic retribution, or a responsive transformation-body?" He answered, "It is the retribution of my offenses — not a responsive transformation." The heavenly being said, "I had supposed you a bodhisattva, a sage manifesting to save beings — only now do I hear it is the fruit of an offense. I do not yet know the reason; I wish to hear the causes and conditions." The jackal said: "Long ago I was born in the city of Padma in Vārāṇasī, the son of a poor family of the kṣatriya caste. From childhood I was clever and bright, and especially fond of study. At the age of twelve I followed a wise teacher into the deep mountains, serving him with hardship and unflagging diligence; the teacher too, morning and night, instructed and refined me without ever missing the proper time. After fifty years, I had mastered all ninety-six schools — scriptures, prophetic records, treatises, medicine, spells, physiognomy, and the signs of calamity and fortune, omitting nothing. My talent and wisdom became known in every direction. I then reflected: 'That I have gained this deliverance is entirely owing to my teacher's kindness in instructing me — a debt too great to repay. But my family being poor, I have nothing to offer him; I should sell myself into servitude to repay his kindness.' My teacher said, 'A hermit dwelling in the mountains, living by begging alms, truly lacks for nothing — why destroy and sell your own precious self to support me? Now that you have perfected your wisdom and eloquence, you should instead go teach and transform the people of the world, becoming a lamp of the Dharma — is the merit of that teaching not repayment enough for my kindness?' So I remained in the mountains, sustaining myself by begging. Before long the king of that country died; the assembled ministers gathered more than five hundred scholars from throughout the realm, and for seven days they debated, the victor to become king. I, the poor family's son, won, and so received the kingship, and with the whole strength of the country's wealth I supported my teacher and my parents."

後安陀羅國。與摩羅婆耶國。共相誅罰多年不剋。安陀羅王。召其群臣。當作何方。得摩羅婆耶國。諸臣答曰。唯有波羅㮈波頭摩國。王出生寒賤。奉持十戒。不犯外欲。雖有宮女。年並長宿。撿括國中。不問豪賤。選擇名女。足一百人。年少端正。能悅意者。齎持重寶。并諸婇女。以相貢獻。彼若納受。從其借兵。并力攻戰。無往不伏。即隨臣計。時悉獻上。王大歡喜。簡閱強兵百萬。以送助之。百日苦戰。死者過半。摩羅婆王。悉被刑斬。方得乃勝。由此美女。忘失本志。奢婬著樂。不理國政。百官群僚。相與作亂。良民之子。掠為奴婢。風雨不時。飢餓滿道。異方怨敵。遂來侵掠。從是其國。遂致亡沒。生地獄中。受眾楚毒。籍先學慧力。自識宿命。心自悔責。改往修來。須臾捨壽。生餓鬼中。復加懺謝。修念十善。須臾捨壽。受野干身。猶識先緣。復行十善。近逢師子。墮此井中。開心分死。冀得生天離苦受樂。由汝接我。違失本願。方經辛苦。何時當免。是故我說。汝濟我命。無功夫也。

"Afterward, the kingdom of Andhra had been at war with the kingdom of Mālava for many years without victory. The king of Andhra summoned his ministers and asked what plan might win him the kingdom of Mālava. His ministers answered: 'Only through the kingdom of Padma in Vārāṇasī. Its king, though born of humble origin, upholds the ten precepts and does not transgress against outside desires; though he has palace women, they are all advanced in years. Search throughout our country, without regard for rank, and select a hundred beautiful young women, pleasing in appearance — send them, together with great treasures, as tribute to him. If he accepts them, borrow troops from him, join forces, and attack — there will be no resistance we cannot overcome.' The king followed his ministers' plan and sent everything as proposed. I was overjoyed, and mustered a million elite troops to send in support. After a hundred days of bitter fighting, more than half the soldiers died, but the king of Mālava was finally captured and executed, and victory was won at last. But because of these beautiful women, I forgot my original resolve, indulged in extravagance and lust, and neglected the affairs of state; the many officials rose up in rebellion; the children of good people were seized as slaves; the weather turned against the seasons and famine filled the roads; enemies from foreign lands came to invade and plunder. From this the kingdom was ruined and lost, and I was reborn in hell, suffering every torment. But by the power of the wisdom I had learned before, I recalled my own past lives, and my heart was filled with remorse; I corrected my past ways and cultivated what was to come, and in a short time cast off that life, to be reborn among the hungry ghosts, where I again performed repentance and cultivated mindfulness of the ten good deeds; in a short time more I cast off that life as well, taking on this jackal's body, still remembering my past causes and again practicing the ten good deeds. Recently I encountered a lion and fell into this well, my heart resolved to accept death, hoping to be born in heaven and escape suffering for happiness — but because you drew me out, my original vow was thwarted, and I must now go on enduring hardship, not knowing when I shall be free of it. This is why I say that your saving my life was no true merit at all."

吾所以入衣得出者。一不違天志願。志願不遂。生大苦惱。施人苦惱。在在所生。求願不得。二為諸天欲得聞法。若人悋法。世世所生。聾盲喑啞。諸根閉塞。生於邊地。癡騃無知。若生好處。情識闇鈍。所學不成。自致苦惱。三為通法化。開悟天人。即為法施。法施之利。能令眾生知死有生。作善獲福。為惡受殃。修道得道。轉身所生。智慧明了。常識宿命。若生天上。為諸天師。若生人間。為金輪王。十善化世。智慧光明漸漸增長。成菩薩行。至無生忍。財施如燈。但明小室。法施若日遠照天下。時天帝釋與八萬天從受十善法。先以十方便調伏諸根。謂六波羅蜜慈悲喜捨。時天問曰。今還天宮。和上何時捨此罪報得生天上。野干曰。剋後七日當捨此身生兜率天。汝等便可願生彼天。多有菩薩說法教化。七日命盡生兜率王宮。復識宿命行十善道(出未曾有經上卷)。

"The reason I entered your robe and came out is threefold: first, so as not to go against the heavenly being's own aspiration — for an aspiration left unfulfilled produces great suffering, and one who causes such suffering in others will find his own wishes thwarted wherever he is reborn. Second, because the devas wished to hear the Dharma — for if a person begrudges the Dharma, then life after life he will be born deaf, blind, and mute, his faculties all obstructed, born in a border land, foolish and ignorant, and even if born somewhere fortunate his understanding will be dull, his learning will come to nothing, and he will bring suffering on himself. Third, to let the Dharma's transformative power flow through and awaken gods and men — this itself is the giving of the Dharma. The benefit of giving the Dharma lets sentient beings know that death is followed by further birth, that good brings merit and evil brings calamity, and that cultivating the path leads to attaining it; from body to body as one is reborn, one's wisdom grows ever clearer, and one constantly remembers one's past lives — if born in heaven, one becomes a teacher to the gods; if born among humans, one becomes a wheel-turning king of gold, transforming the world through the ten good deeds, one's wisdom and light gradually increasing until one accomplishes the practice of a bodhisattva and reaches the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas. Giving wealth is like a lamp that lights only a small room; giving the Dharma is like the sun, shining far across the whole world." At that time Śakra, together with his eighty thousand heavenly followers, received the ten good precepts, first subduing their faculties through the ten skillful means — namely the six perfections together with loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity. The devas then asked: "Now that we return to the heavenly palace, when will the venerable teacher cast off this karmic retribution and be born in heaven?" The jackal said: "In exactly seven days I shall cast off this body and be born in Tuṣita Heaven; you may then wish to be born in that same heaven, where there are many bodhisattvas expounding the Dharma." After seven days his life ended and he was born in the palace of Tuṣita's king, where he again recalled his past lives and practiced the path of the ten good deeds (Source: Adbhutadharma Sūtra, upper fascicle).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The jackal's opening rebuke — that Śakra, king of all the gods, has approached a Dharma teacher without first showing him proper respect — is the story's real point, and everything that follows only elaborates it. Rank in the heavens counts for nothing when it comes to the Dharma; what matters is who has heard the teaching and who has not, and that hierarchy runs in exactly the opposite direction from worldly or even heavenly status. It is worth noticing, too, that the jackal's own account of his downfall — a wise, successful king undone not by conquest but by the hundred women he won through it — is a compact and rather sober narrative given here almost in passing, worthy of its own separate reading.

2. Śakra Keeps a Vow of Non-Retaliation

Having taken a vow never to retaliate against harm, Śakra is attacked by the asura king Vemacitrin — with an outcome that turns the asura's own violence back on him without Śakra lifting a hand.

Chinese Source

過去世時。有天帝釋。白佛言。我今受戒。乃至佛法住世。盡我形壽。有惱我者。要不反報。加惱於彼。時毘摩質多羅阿修羅王。聞天帝釋受如是戒。聞已執持利劍逆道而來。時天帝釋遙見。即遙告言。阿修羅住。縛汝勿動即不得動。帝釋言汝若約誓不作亂者然後當放。阿修羅王即說偈言。

貪欲之所趣　及瞋恚所趣
妄言之所趣　謗毀賢聖趣
我若嬈亂者　趣同彼趣趣

釋提桓因復告言。放汝令去隨汝所安。往詣佛所具以白佛。佛言。善哉(出天帝釋受戒經)。

Ru-Yi Translation

In a past age, there was a heavenly lord Śakra who said to the Buddha: "I have now received a precept: for as long as the Buddha's teaching remains in the world, for the whole span of my life, should anyone trouble me, I will on no account retaliate or trouble him in return." At that time the asura king Vemacitrin, hearing that Śakra had received such a precept, took up a sharp sword and came against him along the road. Śakra, seeing him from a distance, called out: "Asura, stand still — you shall be bound and unable to move," and at once he could not move. Śakra said, "If you will pledge an oath not to cause disorder, I will then release you." The asura king then spoke a verse:

"Wherever greed and desire lead, and wherever anger leads,
wherever false speech leads, and wherever slander of the wise leads —
should I now cause disorder, may I go to that very same destination."

Śakra then said, "I release you; go wherever you find peace." He then went before the Buddha and told him everything. The Buddha said, "Well done" (Source: Sūtra on Śakra Devānām Indra's Receiving of the Precepts).

3. Śakra's Near-Rebirth as a Donkey

The five signs of a deva's death appear on Śakra himself, foretelling rebirth in a potter's donkey — and a single sincere refuge, taken in the instant before death, is enough to undo it entirely.

Chinese Source

昔者天帝釋五德離身自知命盡。當生陶家受驢胞胎。愁憂自念。三界之中濟人苦厄唯有佛耳。馳往佛所稽首伏地至心歸命佛法聖眾。未起之間其命忽終便入驢母胎中。時驢解走破壞坏器。其主打之尋時傷胎。其神即還入故身中。五德還備。復為天帝。佛讚善哉。殞命之際歸命三尊。罪對已畢不更懃苦。佛為說偈。帝釋聞之達罪福之變。解興衰之本。遵寂滅之行。得須陀洹道(出法句譬喻經第三卷)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Long ago, the heavenly lord Śakra found the five signs of decline departing from his body, and knew of himself that his life was at its end, that he was about to be born into a potter's household to take on the womb of a donkey. Grieving, he thought to himself: "Within the three realms, the only one who can save people from suffering is the Buddha." He hastened to where the Buddha was, bowed his head to the ground, and with utmost sincerity took refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the noble assembly. Before he had even risen, his life suddenly ended, and he entered the womb of a mother donkey. At that moment the donkey broke loose and shattered some pottery vessels; its owner beat it, and in that very instant injured the womb. His spirit at once returned to his former body, the five signs restored, and he became the heavenly lord once more. The Buddha praised him, saying, "Well done" — for at the very moment of his life's ending he had taken refuge in the Three Jewels, and the retribution for his offense was thereby exhausted, so that he need not undergo that suffering further. The Buddha then spoke a verse for him. Śakra, hearing it, penetrated the transformations of offense and merit, understood the root of rise and decline, and took up the practice of quiescence, attaining the fruit of stream-entry (Source: Dharmapada with Similes, fascicle 3).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The narrow margin in this story is the point: Śakra's own past merit runs out on a schedule nothing can extend, and only an act of refuge taken in the instant before death — not a lifetime's accumulated status as king of the gods — changes the outcome. The donkey's owner beating it at exactly the moment needed reads, in the compendium's own logic, not as coincidence but as karma completing itself through whatever vessel is available, the offense "paid" the instant genuine refuge is taken.

4. The Deva Sivali's Past Giving

A king's decision to share credit for his own charity teaches an unexpected lesson about how merit is actually accounted for.

Chinese Source

時悉鞞梨天子白佛言。我自過去世時國王名悉鞞梨。於四城門普施為福。城內交道皆亦布施。時第一夫人言。王大作福德而我先。王言城東門外布施作福悉皆屬汝。諸子復言。城南門外所作施福悉皆屬汝。時有大臣復白王言。城西門外所作施福悉皆屬汝。時諸將士復白王言。城北門外相與。時諸庶民復白王。王答言。於其城內四交道頭所作施福悉屬汝等。爾時國王夫人大臣將士庶民悉皆惠施。於諸功德我先所作。惠施功德於茲則斷。時我所使諸作福者還至我所為我作禮而白我言。大王當知。諸修福處夫人王子大臣將士及諸庶民。各據其處行施作福。大王所施於茲則斷。我時答言。諸方歲輸應入。我者分半入庫半於彼惠施。我先長夜如是惠施常得可愛念可意福報。常受快樂無有窮極。以斯福業果報入大功德聚。譬如五大河合為一流。所謂恒河無有人能量。其河水百千萬億㪷㪶之數。功德果報不可稱量。悉得入於大功德聚。時悉鞞梨天子聞佛所說。歡喜禮足即沒不現(出悉鞞梨天子諸佛說偈經)。

Ru-Yi Translation

The deva Sivali said to the Buddha: "In a past age of my own, I was a king named Sivali. At the four city gates I gave alms broadly as an act of merit, and I likewise gave alms at every crossroads within the city. Then my chief consort said, 'The king performs such great merit, yet I was there first' — so I said, 'The merit of the giving outside the east gate shall belong entirely to you.' My sons said the same, so I gave them the south gate; a great minister said the same, so I gave him the west gate; the generals and soldiers said the same, so I gave them the north gate; and when the common people said the same, I answered, 'The merit of the giving at the crossroads within the city shall belong entirely to all of you.' From that time on, my consort, sons, ministers, soldiers, and people all performed the giving themselves — but the merit of my own original giving was thereby cut off entirely. Those I had appointed to oversee the works of merit then came and told me: 'Great king, know that at every place of merit-making, your consort, your princes, your ministers, your soldiers, and your people each now carry out the giving themselves — while your own giving has, in all this, been cut off.' I answered: 'Whatever tribute comes in yearly from every region, let half go into the treasury and half go to that same giving.' Through this long practice over many nights, I constantly received a reward both dear and pleasing, and happiness without end. Through the fruit of this merit I entered a great mass of merit — just as the five great rivers join into a single stream, that is, the Ganges, which no one can measure, its waters numbering in the hundreds of thousands of millions of measures — so too this fruit could not be reckoned, and all of it entered that great mass of merit." When the deva Sivali had finished speaking, he rejoiced, bowed at the Buddha's feet, and vanished from sight (Source: Sūtra of the Buddhas' Verses for the Deva Sivali).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The king never regrets giving his family and court the credit for his own charity — that is not the story's complaint. The puzzle he raises is a genuine one about how merit works: if I dedicate the outcome of my own good deed to someone else, whose account does it land in? The sūtra's answer — that his response of funding further giving from the treasury itself produced merit beyond calculation — suggests the compendium's underlying view: merit dedicated away does not vanish from the giver, provided the giver keeps on giving.

5. The Sun-King Asks About the Sun and Moon

The deva who governs the sun asks the Buddha directly what conduct earns that office — and its lunar counterpart.

Chinese Source

日天王與無數天人來詣佛所稽首言。以何等行得為日天炤四天下。復以何緣而為月天炤除夜冥。佛言。有四事。一常憙布施。二修身慎行。三奉戒不犯。四然燈於佛寺。若於父母沙門道人皆殖光明。又身口意行不殺等十善。佛言。又有四事得為月王。一布施貧匱。二奉持五戒。三恭事三尊。四冥設燈光於君父師寺(出超日明三昧經下卷)。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Sun-King, together with countless heavenly beings, came before the Buddha, bowed his head, and said: "Through what conduct does one become the sun-deva, illuminating the four continents? And through what causes does one become the moon-deva, dispelling the darkness of night?" The Buddha said: "There are four things: constantly delighting in giving; cultivating the body and being careful in conduct; upholding the precepts without transgression; and lighting lamps at Buddhist temples — likewise planting light for one's parents, for śramaṇas, and for those who practice the way — together with refraining from killing and the rest of the ten good deeds in body, speech, and mind." The Buddha further said: "There are also four things by which one becomes the moon-king: giving to the poor; upholding the five precepts; reverently serving the Three Jewels; and quietly setting out lamplight for one's sovereign, one's father, one's teacher, and the temple" (Source: Sūtra on the Samādhi Surpassing the Sun's Brightness, latter fascicle).

6. A Trāyastriṃśa Deva's Seven Signs

A deva foresees rebirth as a piglet, is taught the three refuges by the Buddha himself, and is born instead as a child who takes refuge before he can even speak — recognizing, years later, the very monks he met in heaven.

Chinese Source

昔忉利宮有一天。壽命垂盡有七種瑞。一者項中光滅。二者頭上華萎。三者面色變。四者衣上有塵。五者腋下汗出。六者身形變。七者離本坐。即自思惟。壽終之後下生鳩夷那竭國疥癩母䐗腹中作豚。甚豫愁苦不知當作何計。有天語言。今佛在此為母說經。唯佛能脫卿之罪耳。即到佛所稽首作禮未及發問。佛告天子。一切萬物皆歸無常。汝素所知何為憂愁。天具白佛。佛言。欲離豚身當誦三自歸。如是日三。天從佛教晨夜自歸。却後七日天即壽盡。下生維耶離國作長者子。

Ru-Yi Translation

Long ago in the Trāyastriṃśa palace there was a deva whose life was about to end, and seven signs appeared: the light at his neck went out, the flowers on his head wilted, his complexion changed, dust appeared on his robe, sweat flowed from beneath his arms, his bodily form changed, and he could no longer bear to remain on his own seat. He reflected that after his life ended he would descend to be born in the country of Kuśinagara, in the womb of a scabby, leprous sow, to become a piglet. Deeply distressed, he did not know what to do. A deva told him: "The Buddha is now here, expounding a sūtra for his mother — only the Buddha can free you from this fate." He went at once before the Buddha and bowed his head, but before he could even ask, the Buddha said to him: "All the myriad things return to impermanence — this you already know; why then do you grieve?" The deva told the Buddha everything. The Buddha said: "If you wish to escape the body of a piglet, you should recite the three refuges" — and so, three times a day, the deva took refuge morning and night, following the Buddha's teaching. Seven days later the deva's life ended, and he descended to be born as the son of an elder in the country of Vaiśālī.

在母胞胎日三自歸。始生墮地亦跪自歸。其母勉身又無惡露。母傍侍婢怖而棄走。母亦深怪。墮地即語。謂之熒惑意欲殺之。退自念言。我少兒子。若殺此兒父必罪我。徐白長者殺之不晚。母即收兒往白長者。產男墮地便長跪叉手自歸三寶。闔門怪之謂之熒惑。父言。止止。此兒非凡人。世人百歲不曉自歸三尊。況初墮地而能稱南無佛。好養視之慎無輕慢。兒遂長大年向七歲。與其輩類於道邊戲。遇舍利弗目連。兒前禮之眾聖驚怪小而能禮。兒言。道人不復見識。具說天上遇佛復見識。我本在天上應生惡道。遇佛慈愍教令自歸故得為人。

While still in his mother's womb he took the three refuges three times a day; the moment he was born and fell to the ground he likewise knelt and took refuge. His mother's labor was easy, with no impure discharge; the maidservant attending her, terrified, fled her post, and his mother too was greatly astonished — the moment he fell to the ground he spoke, and they took this for an evil omen and wished to kill him. But then she thought, "This is my own young son — if I kill him, his father will surely blame me" — and resolved to tell the elder first. She took up the child and told the elder that a son had been born who, the moment he fell to the ground, had knelt with folded hands and taken refuge in the Three Jewels; the whole household was astonished and took it for an evil omen. The father said, "No, no — this child is no ordinary person. Men of the world may live a hundred years and never know how to take refuge in the Three Jewels, yet this one, the moment he fell to the ground, could invoke homage to the Buddha. Care for him well, and never treat him lightly." The child grew up, and by the age of seven was playing by the roadside when he encountered Śāriputra and Maudgalyāyana. The child bowed to them; the assembled sages were astonished that one so young could bow so properly. The child said, "Do you no longer recognize me?" and told them how he had met the Buddha in heaven, saying: "I was originally in heaven, due to be reborn into an evil destiny, but through the Buddha's compassion, who taught me to take refuge, I was able to become human instead."

比丘即為呪願言。折梨祇兒語目連等及舍利弗。願以我言因請世尊諸菩薩僧并及仁等也。受而歸去具白父母。願辦其供令具甘美。父母愛之從其所言。異其年幼開發大意。又奇所作探識宿命。為極珍妙盡世名味。供具精細過踰兒意。佛及眾僧各作神足來至兒舍飯。佛為說經。兒及父母內外親屬皆得阿惟越致(出折伏羅漢經)。

The monks then pronounced a blessing: "Cirika, go tell Maudgalyāyana and Śāriputra that, on my word, they should invite the World-Honored One, the bodhisattvas, and the sangha, together with all of you as well." Receiving this, he went home and told his parents everything, asking that offerings be prepared, rich and delicious. His parents, loving him, followed what he asked, marveling that one so young could conceive so great an intention, and, astonished further, sought out his past lives — and prepared offerings of the most exquisite delicacies the world afforded, finer even than the child had asked. The Buddha and the assembled sangha each came by their own supernormal power to the child's house to take the meal. The Buddha then expounded a sūtra, and the child, his parents, and all his relatives within and without attained the stage of non-retrogression (Source: Sūtra on the Subduing of an Arhat).

7. A Deva's Hand Saves Five Hundred Merchants

A caravan lost in the wilderness is saved by water flowing from a stranger's fingertips — repayment, the story insists, for a debt of kindness planted lifetimes earlier.

Chinese Source

昔有導師。與五百賈人共行作估。到大曠野飢渴困極。歸命世尊及釋梵四王怖懅無計。于時道師登高遠望。見有林木飛鳥往趣冀當有水。俱共犇走不久得至。唯見樹木周匝生草其地清潔。導師故謂賈人等。咸共穿地取水必當可得。適共議已。時有天人遙從天上瞻此導師及五百人困乏水漿。如申臂頃來到其所住于樹上。申其右手從五。指間流出八味甘美之水。供於導師及五百人。各各取用而無窮盡皆得飽滿。所以者何。宿命親親俱種恩福。故使天人念之來下以給美水各得安隱(出譬喻經第三卷)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Long ago there was a caravan leader who, together with five hundred merchants, traveled on business. Reaching a vast wilderness, they grew exhausted with hunger and thirst, and in their terror, having no other recourse, took refuge in the World-Honored One and in Śakra, Brahmā, and the Four Heavenly Kings. The caravan leader climbed to a high place and, gazing into the distance, saw trees and birds in flight, toward which he hoped there might be water; together they ran and soon arrived, finding trees standing round about, grass growing, and the ground clean and clear. The caravan leader said, "Let us all dig into the earth for water — surely it can be found." No sooner had they agreed than a heavenly being, gazing down from heaven and seeing them exhausted for want of water, arrived at that place in the time it takes to extend an arm, and dwelt there in the tree; extending his right hand, from between its five fingers there flowed water of eight excellent flavors, which he offered to the caravan leader and the five hundred men. Each took as much as he wished, and it never ran out, and all were fully satisfied. And why was this so? Because in a past life they had all, as kin, planted the seeds of mutual kindness — and for this reason the heavenly being remembered them and descended to give them sweet water, so that each found peace and ease (Source: Sūtra of Similes, fascicle 3).

8. A Deva's Rebirth as a Pig, Averted

Śakra himself talks a grieving, dying deva through the one act — taking refuge — that changes his coming rebirth from a butcher's sow to the household of a great elder.

Chinese Source

昔三十三天命欲終時。有五瑞應現在前。華萎。衣裳垢穢。腋下汗出。玉女減少。不樂本座。譬如身生疥瘙癰瘡。三十三天有一天子。生五異瑞愁憂呻吟。時帝釋聞之問言。於彼天宮是何等天愁憂呻吟。天子答言。有一天子現五瑞應。善哉為彼天子。釋提桓因往詣其所語言。何為愁憂呻吟。乃拍髀為答言。有異災怪。釋提桓因為說偈言。

一切行無常　生者必有盡
夫生輒有死　此滅為最樂

天子言。我不聞此。釋提桓因言。一切恩愛皆有別離。天子言。云何而不懷憂。今此天宮種種五欲皆當別離。命終即生羅閱城猪胎。所食者是糞。方為屠膾所殺。我今見此是以懷愁耳。時釋提桓因語天子言。汝今自歸命佛法僧。所以然者。佛說偈言。

諸有歸命佛　不趣三惡道
受福天人間　後逮涅槃界

天子叉手便作是言。世尊一切智。徹視見觀。願見救濟。我今歸命佛歸命法歸命比丘僧。遂不處䐗胎。生羅閱祇城第一長者家。見便歡喜不能自勝(出增一阿含第十九卷)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Long ago, when a certain deva's life in Trāyastriṃśa was about to end, five signs appeared: his flowers wilted, his garments grew soiled, sweat flowed from beneath his arms, his celestial maidens dwindled, and he no longer delighted in his own throne — as if his body had broken out in sores. This deva, showing the five signs, grieved and groaned aloud. Śakra, hearing this, asked which deva it was, and went to him and asked why he grieved so. The deva struck his own thigh and answered that some strange calamity had befallen him. Śakra then spoke a verse:

"All conditioned things are impermanent; whatever is born must come to an end.
Birth is inevitably followed by death — and this very cessation is the highest joy."

The deva said, "I have never heard such a thing." Śakra said, "All love and affection must end in separation." The deva said, "How then could I not grieve? All the pleasures of this heavenly palace must now be parted from — for when my life ends I shall be born into the womb of a sow in Rājagṛha, eating only filth, and in the end be slaughtered by a butcher. It is because I see this that I grieve." Śakra then said: "You should now take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha — for as the Buddha said in a verse:

"Whoever takes refuge in the Buddha shall not go to the three evil destinies;
he receives the blessings of birth as god or human, and afterward attains nirvāṇa."

The deva then folded his hands and said: "The World-Honored One is all-wise, seeing all things — I beg to be saved. I now take refuge in the Buddha, take refuge in the Dharma, take refuge in the Sangha." And so he did not enter the sow's womb after all, but was instead born into the household of the foremost elder of Rājagṛha, and on seeing this he rejoiced beyond measure (Source: Ekottarika Āgama, fascicle 19).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Stories 6 and 8 are companion pieces, both turning on the same mechanism: a deva sees his own rebirth coming with total clarity, and a single act of refuge, performed with real sincerity in the time remaining, redirects it entirely. Placed back to back, they make the compendium's argument by repetition rather than by explaining it once — refuge is not a formality reserved for humans in temples, but something that works at the very edge of any being's karma, gods included.

9. The Maiden on the Inexhaustible Lotus

A single flower, offered to an image out of sudden faith, becomes a throne that provides for its owner's every need.

Chinese Source

有一天女。坐一蓮花上縱廣百由旬。此花獨妙殊於餘者。所欲資生之具隨念皆從花出。進止隨身。目連問言。作何善行受報如此。天女答言。迦葉佛滅度後。遺全身舍利。與佛諸弟子建七寶塔。高廣四十里。時我作女人出見寶塔中像。信敬情發念佛功德。脫頭上花奉獻於像(出雜藏經)。

Ru-Yi Translation

There was once a heavenly maiden sitting upon a lotus flower a hundred yojanas across; this flower alone was wondrous, surpassing all others, and whatever she needed arose from the flower according to her thought, moving and resting always with her. Maudgalyāyana asked what good deed she had performed to receive such a reward. The heavenly maiden answered: "After the Buddha Kāśyapa entered final nirvāṇa, his relics were left entire, and his disciples built for them a stūpa of the seven treasures, forty li high and broad. At that time I was a woman who went out and saw the image within that jeweled stūpa; faith and reverence arose in me, and, thinking of the Buddha's merit, I took the flower from my own head and offered it to the image" (Source: Miscellaneous Repository Sūtra).

10. The Jeweled Maiden's Voice

A single sound, endlessly multiplying into exactly the teaching each listener needs.

Chinese Source

自在天王有天寶女名曰善口。於一語中顯出百千娛樂音聲。於彼一一音聲中。復出百千音聲。佛子當知。一善口聲。出生無量聲。隨其所應悉令開解(出華嚴經第二十九卷)。

Ru-Yi Translation

The heavenly king Vaśavartin has a jeweled heavenly maiden named Sukanthā, "Good Voice." Within a single word of hers there emerge a hundred thousand delightful sounds, and within each of those sounds there emerge yet a hundred thousand further sounds. Sons of the Buddha, know this: from a single sound of Sukanthā's, immeasurable sounds are born, each according to what is needed, bringing understanding to all who hear it (Source: Avataṃsaka Sūtra, fascicle 29).

11. The Heavens Descend to a Musician's Song

A wandering lutenist is asked by a heavenly maiden to play, and the resulting song names, in verse, exactly what earns a place among the gods.

Chinese Source

過去世時。拘薩羅國有人彈琴。名曰鹿牛。行息中野有六廣大天宮。天女來語鹿牛言。阿舅阿舅為我彈琴。我當歌舞。鹿牛鼓琴六天歌舞。第一謌辭曰。

若男子女人　勝妙衣惠施
施衣因緣故　所生得殊勝
施所盡妙物　生天隨所欲
見我居宮殿　乘虛而遊行
天身如金聚　天女百中勝
觀察斯福德　迴向中中最

餘天辭粗相類(文多不載)鹿牛亦禮。禮竟天忽然不現(出過去彈琴人經)。

Ru-Yi Translation

In a past age, in the kingdom of Kosala, there was a man who played the lute, named Lugniu, "Deer-Ox." Resting once during a journey in the wilderness, he came upon the six vast heavenly palaces. A heavenly maiden came and said to him, "Uncle, uncle, play the lute for me, and I shall sing and dance." Lugniu played, and the gods of the six heavens sang and danced. The first song's words ran:

"If a man or a woman gives fine and excellent robes as alms,
through the causes of giving robes, what is born thereby is superior.
Having given the finest things, one is born in heaven according to one's wish,
and sees oneself dwelling in a palace, roaming freely through the open sky.
The heavenly body is like a mass of gold; the heavenly maiden surpasses a hundred others.
Contemplating this very merit, may it be dedicated to the very highest of all."

The words of the other heavens were roughly similar in kind (too long to record here). Lugniu, too, bowed in reverence, and when the bow was done, the gods suddenly vanished from sight (Source: Sūtra of the Man Who Played the Lute in a Past Age).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Fascicle 2 as a whole shows the compendium's real ambition: not to systematize doctrine but to demonstrate, story after story, that the entire hierarchy of gods — from Śakra down to an anonymous dying deva — is subject to the same laws of karma and the same opening offered by refuge and giving as any human reader. A jackal outranks the king of the gods on the Dharma; a sow's womb is one sincere refuge away from an elder's household. The heavens, in other words, are not an escape from this logic but its clearest demonstration.
