Bǎochàng · 516 CE, Liang Dynasty · 經律異相

A comparative compendium of sūtra and vinaya.

The Jīng Lǜ Yì Xiàng (經律異相, T2121) — "Different Forms Compiled from Sūtra and Vinaya" — is the largest surviving Chinese Buddhist leishu: fifty fascicles of narrative excerpts, gathered under imperial decree in 516 CE by the monk Bǎochàng and a team of collaborators, and sorted by subject rather than by source text. Its table of contents runs from the heavens down through hell, taking in buddhas, bodhisattvas, monastics of every rank, kings and queens, laypeople, animals, and the damned along the way. This page is the entrance for reading it fascicle by fascicle, as the translation proceeds.

Why this compendium matters

By the early sixth century the Chinese canon already ran to thousands of fascicles, far more than any one reader could work through to find, say, every canonical story about a particular kind of king, or every account of a specific hell. Emperor Wu of Liang commissioned this compendium to solve exactly that problem: a single reference work, organized by topic, that any student of the dharma could consult directly.

Every entry closes with a citation to its source sūtra or vinaya text, in the tradition's own citation style — this is a work of compilation and arrangement, not original composition, and it names its sources throughout.

Translation approach

Unlike a scholastic treatise built from tight logical argument, this text is narrative through and through — stories, causal accounts (因緣), and parables. The translation favors a readable narrative voice over a rigid sentence-by-sentence rendering, while still keeping proper names, place names, and technical terms (karma, the realms of rebirth, monastic titles, and so on) precise and consistent across fascicles. Classical text is set alongside the English for every story, and each fascicle names the source citations the compendium itself provides.

A note on structure. Each fascicle groups several numbered stories under a shared heading — the heading names the fascicle's subject (for example, "Heavenly Beings, Part 1"), and each story within it keeps the compendium's own sequential numbering (its title ending in "一," "二," "三," and so on). The translation preserves both levels: the fascicle's overall theme, and each story as its own self-contained unit.
