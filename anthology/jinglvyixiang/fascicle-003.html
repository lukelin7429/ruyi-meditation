The Earth: Its Kingdoms, Trees, and Seas

This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation of fascicle 3 of the Jīng Lǜ Yì Xiàng (經律異相): "The Earth" (地部) — Jambudvīpa's kingdoms and the naming of Rājagṛha, Sudatta's purchase of Jetavana and Śāriputra's magical contest with the six heretic teachers, the ten great mountains and the eight causes of earthquakes, eight legendary trees, the great rivers and the ocean's eight virtues, three wondrous jewels, and finally the paradise continent of Uttarakuru.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 3 complete Source: Taishō T2121, vol. 53, fascicle 3 Compiled by: Bǎochàng and others, 516 CE

Translation Method

Fascicle 3 moves from the heavens to the earth beneath them: geography, sacred sites, and the natural world, still organized as discrete named entries rather than continuous argument. Two entries stand out for length and fame — the naming of Rājagṛha and Sudatta's purchase of Jetavana — and are translated in full rather than summarized, since both are foundational narratives in the Buddhist tradition and the compendium preserves them essentially whole.

Jambudvīpa: Kingdoms, Distances, and the Naming of Rājagṛha

A geography of the known world as the compendium's compilers understood it — kingdoms, peoples, and produce, then precise distances between the holy sites of the Buddha's life, and finally three competing legends for why Rājagṛha alone, among many royal cities, is named "the King's Houses."

Chinese Source

閻浮提內。有十六大國八萬四千城八國王四天子。東有晉國天子人民熾盛。南有天竺國天子土地多名象。西有大秦國天子土地饒金璧玉。北有月支國天子土地好馬。八萬四千城中。六千四百種人。萬種音響。五十六萬億丘聚。魚有六千四百種。鳥有四千五百種。獸有二千四百種。樹有萬種。草有八千種。雜藥有七百四十種。雜香有四十三種。寶百二十一種。正寶七種。海中有二千五百國。百八十國食五穀。二千三百二十國食魚鱉黿。五國王。一王主五百城。第一王名斯梨。國土盡事佛不事眾邪。第二王名迦羅。土地出七寶。第三王名不羅。土地出四十三種香及白琉璃。第四王名闍耶。土地出必鉢胡椒。第五王名那頗。土地出白珠及七色琉璃。五大國城多黑短小相去六十五萬里。從是但有海水無有人民。去鐵圍山百四十萬里。中一八崛摩殺人處在舍衛國東八萬十里。佛所化處亦一處。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within Jambudvīpa there are sixteen great kingdoms, eighty-four thousand cities, and eight kings, four of them Sons of Heaven. In the east is the kingdom of Jin, whose people are numerous and flourishing; in the south is the kingdom of India, whose land abounds in famous elephants; in the west is the kingdom of Da Qin, whose land is rich in gold and jade; in the north is the kingdom of the Yuezhi, whose land is famed for its fine horses. Among the eighty-four thousand cities there are six thousand four hundred kinds of people, ten thousand kinds of speech, and five hundred and sixty thousand myriad hamlets; six thousand four hundred kinds of fish, four thousand five hundred kinds of birds, two thousand four hundred kinds of beasts, ten thousand kinds of trees, eight thousand kinds of grasses, seven hundred and forty kinds of medicinal herbs, forty-three kinds of fragrances, one hundred and twenty-one kinds of treasures, and seven true treasures. Within the seas there are two thousand five hundred kingdoms; one hundred and eighty live on the five grains, and two thousand three hundred and twenty live on fish and turtles. There are five kings, each ruling five hundred cities: the first, Siri, whose kingdom serves the Buddha alone; the second, Kāla, whose land produces the seven treasures; the third, Bula, whose land produces forty-three kinds of incense and white beryl; the fourth, Jaya, whose land produces long pepper; the fifth, Nabha, whose land produces white pearls and beryl of seven colors. The peoples of these five kingdoms are mostly dark, short, and small, six hundred and fifty thousand li apart from one another; beyond this lies only sea, with no inhabitants, all the way to the Cakravāḍa mountains, one million four hundred thousand li distant. Within this expanse lies Aṅgulimāla's place of killing, east of Śrāvastī — the one place, too, where the Buddha carried out his transformative teaching there.

拘夷那竭國在迦維羅國之東南一千里。王舍國在迦維羅衛國之東南二千二百里。佛得道處在王舍城東南二百里。維耶離國在迦維衛國之東一千八百里。㮈女國在維耶離城南三里道西。拘睒彌國在迦維羅衛國之西南千二百里。葉波國在迦維羅衛國之東千二百八十里。難國在迦維羅衛國之東三千二百里。舍衛國在迦維羅衛之西五百里。波羅㮈國在迦維衛國之西九百六十里。佛轉法輪處在波羅㮈國之北二十里。樹名香淨。降伏魔處也。波羅㮈私國在舍衛國之南千四百里。中間有恒水東南流。耆闍崛山有五百岳。佛誦經在中岳。王舍國在中岳之下(出十二遊經)。

Kuśinagara lies a thousand li southeast of Kapilavastu. Rājagṛha lies two thousand two hundred li southeast of Kapilavastu. The place of the Buddha's attainment of the path lies two hundred li southeast of Rājagṛha. Vaiśālī lies one thousand eight hundred li east of Kapilavastu, and Āmrapālī's country three li south of Vaiśālī, west of the road. Kauśāmbī lies one thousand two hundred li southwest of Kapilavastu; Campā, one thousand two hundred eighty li east; the land of Nan, three thousand two hundred li east. Śrāvastī lies five hundred li west of Kapilavastu. Vārāṇasī lies nine hundred sixty li west of Kapilavastu, and the place where the Buddha turned the wheel of the Dharma lies twenty li north of Vārāṇasī, at a tree named Fragrant Purity — the very place he subdued Māra. A further Vārāṇasī-country lies one thousand four hundred li south of Śrāvastī, the Ganges flowing southeast between them. Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa has five hundred peaks; the Buddha recited sūtras on the middle peak, and Rājagṛha lies beneath it (Source: Sūtra of the Buddha's Twelve Journeys).

閻浮提內方圓近遠及所出有

問曰。如舍婆提迦毘羅婆。波羅㮈城。皆有諸王王舍。何故獨名此城為王舍。答曰。有人言。是摩伽陀王有子一頭兩面四臂。時人以為不祥。王裂其身首棄之曠野。羅剎女鬼名梨羅。還合其身而乳養之。後大成人力并諸國。取萬八千王置名此山五山中。以大力勢治閻浮提。因名此山為王舍城。復次有人言。摩伽陀王先所住城中失火。一燒一作如是至七。國人疲役。王集諸智人問其意故有言。宜應易處。王見此五山周匝如城。即作宮殿於中止住。故名王舍城。

Why "Rājagṛha," the King's Houses

Question: Since Śrāvastī, Kapilavastu, and Vārāṇasī all likewise have kings and royal residences, why is this city alone named "the King's Houses"? Answer: Some say that the king of Magadha once had a son with one head, two faces, and four arms; taking this as an ill omen, the king tore the child's body apart and abandoned it in the wilderness. A rākṣasī named Līlā joined the pieces back together and suckled and raised the child. Grown to great strength, he later conquered every kingdom, took eighteen thousand kings captive, and placed them within these five mountains, ruling all of Jambudvīpa by his might — and so these mountains came to be called Rājagṛha. Others say instead that the city where the king of Magadha had previously dwelt burned down and was rebuilt seven times over, exhausting the people with labor; the king asked his wise men what to do, and one advised him to move. Seeing that these five mountains encircled a space like a city wall, he built his palace within them and dwelt there — and so it was named Rājagṛha.

復次往古世時。此國有王名婆藪。厭世出家學作仙人。時居家婆羅門與諸出家仙人共論議。居家婆羅門言。天祀中應殺生噉肉。出家仙人言不應。共諍云云。諸出家婆羅門言。此有大王出家作仙人汝等信不。居家婆羅門言。出家仙人言。我以此人為證。後日當問。居家婆羅門先到婆藪所。語婆藪仙人。明日論議。汝當助我。諸出家仙人問。天祀中應殺生噉肉不。婆藪仙人言。應殺生噉肉。此生在天祀中死故得生天上。出家仙人言。汝大不是。汝大妄語即唾之言。罪人滅去。時婆藪仙人。尋陷入地沒踝。是初開大罪門。諸出家仙人言。汝應實語。若故妄語者。汝身當陷入地中。婆藪言。我知為天故殺羊噉肉無罪。即陷至膝。如是稍沒至腰至頸。出家仙人言。汝今妄語得現世報。更以實語雖入地下我能出汝令得免罪。婆藪思惟。我貴重人不應兩種語。又四違陀法中讚祀天法。我一人死當何足計。一心言。應天祀中殺生噉肉無罪。於是舉身投地。從是以來常用婆藪仙人法。於天祀中殺羊。當下刀時言婆藪殺汝。

Others tell it thus: in ancient times, this country had a king named Vasu, who, wearied of the world, went forth from home and trained as a seer. A householder brahmin and several seers who had gone forth debated whether the rites of heaven-sacrifice should include killing animals and eating meat; the householder held that they should, the seers that they should not, and neither could persuade the other. The seers proposed calling on the great king-turned-seer as witness. The householder brahmin went first to Vasu and asked him to support his side the next day. When the seers then asked their question, Vasu answered: "One should kill animals and eat meat — for a creature that dies within the rite of heaven-sacrifice thereby attains rebirth in heaven." The seers cried, "You are gravely mistaken — this is monstrous falsehood," and spat at him, saying, "Let this sinner be destroyed." At once Vasu began to sink into the earth up to his ankles — the very first opening of the gate to grave transgression. The seers said, "Speak the truth, or you will sink further" — but Vasu, reflecting that he was a man of high standing who should not speak two different ways, and that the four Vedas themselves praise the rite of heaven-sacrifice, declared with single-minded resolve, "Killing animals and eating meat in the rite of heaven-sacrifice is no offense" — and at once threw his whole body down into the earth. From that time on, whenever a sheep is killed in the rite of heaven-sacrifice, at the moment the knife descends, it is still said, "Vasu kills you."

藪之子名曰廣車。嗣位為王。亦厭世法而不能出家。如是思惟。我父出家生入地中。若治天下復作大罪。我今當何以自處。時空中聲言。汝若行見難值希有處應是中作舍住。未經幾時。王出田獵見有鹿走疾如風。王便逐之百官侍從無能及者。前見五山周匝峻固。其地莊嚴有天華香聞天伎樂。是處希有未曾所見。今我正當此中舍住。即捨本城住此山中。從是已後次第止住故名王舍城(出大智論第三卷)。

Vasu's son, named Vast Chariot, succeeded him as king. He too was wearied of worldly ways but unable to go forth from home, and reflected: "My father went forth and yet was born sinking into the earth — if I govern the realm, I too will commit grave offenses. What then should I do?" A voice from the sky told him: "If, in your travels, you come upon a place difficult to encounter, rare and wonderful, build your dwelling there." Not long after, the king went hunting and, chasing a deer swift as the wind, outran all his officials and attendants, until he saw these five mountains, encircling a steep, secure space, its ground adorned with heavenly flowers and heavenly music sounding within it — a place the like of which he had never seen. "I should make my dwelling here," he thought, and abandoned his former city to dwell among these mountains. From that time onward, each king in succession has resided there — and so it is named Rājagṛha, the City of the King's Houses (Source: Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra, fascicle 3).

Monasteries: Two Foundation Stories

Two of Buddhism's most famous monastery-foundation narratives: Elder Kalanda's donation of the Bamboo Grove, and Sudatta's purchase of Jetavana at Prince Jeta's price of gold enough to cover the ground — followed by Śāriputra's magical contest against the six heretic teachers' champion, Raudrākṣa, to win the right to build it at all.

Chinese Source
迦蘭陀長者施佛精舍事

有豪貴長者。名迦蘭陀。追惜我園施與尼揵。不得奉佛及僧。臥不安席。有大鬼將軍。名曰半師。承佛神旨。即召閱叉推逐尼揵。裸形無恥不應止此。鬼師奉勅。撾打尼揵拖拽器物。尼揵怖走曰。此何惡人暴害乃爾。鬼師答言。長者迦蘭陀當持竹園作佛精舍。大鬼將軍半師見使逐汝輩耳。明日尼揵共責數長者。長者心悅吾願遂矣。答尼揵曰。此諸鬼神強暴含瞋懼必作害。不如委去更求所安。尼揵忿恨即日悉去。長者修立精舍。僧房坐具眾嚴都畢。行詣樹下請佛及僧。眾祐受施止頓化濟。靡不欣樂(出中本起上卷)。

Ru-Yi Translation
Elder Kalanda's Gift of a Monastery to the Buddha

There was a wealthy elder named Kalanda, who regretted having given his own garden to the Nirgrantha ascetics, since it meant he could not offer it to the Buddha and the sangha, and he lay awake unable to rest. A great spirit-general named Bànshī, receiving the Buddha's own spiritual command, summoned the yakṣas to drive out the Nirgranthas, saying, "You go about naked and shameless — you should not remain here." They beat the Nirgranthas and dragged away their belongings; the terrified Nirgranthas fled, crying, "What manner of evil men would treat us so?" The spirit-general answered: "The elder Kalanda intends to offer this Bamboo Grove as a monastery for the Buddha — it is the great spirit-general Bànshī who sent us to drive you out." The next day the Nirgranthas came to reproach the elder, who, secretly delighted that his wish was now fulfilled, said to them: "These spirits are violent and full of rage — I fear they will do you harm; better that you leave and seek shelter elsewhere." Resentful, they departed that very day. The elder then built the monastery — quarters, sitting mats, and every adornment complete — and went to invite the Buddha and the sangha, who accepted the offering and dwelt there, teaching and delivering beings, and there was no one who did not rejoice in it (Source: Zhong Ben Qi Sūtra, upper fascicle).

須達多買園以立精舍

須達多白佛言。舍衛城中人多信邪。如來大慈唯願顧臨到舍衛城。佛言。彼無精舍云何得去。須達言。弟子營起願見聽許。世尊默然。願遣舍利弗指授摸則。即命共往案行周遍無可意處。唯太子祇陀園其地平正林樹欝茂遠近得中。須達以白太子。太子笑言。欲用遊戲。慇懃再三。太子言。若能以黃金布地令間無空者便當相與。須達曰。諾謹隨其價。太子祇陀言。我戲語耳。須達言。太子不應妄語即共興訟。時首陀會天化作一人下為評詳言。夫太子法不應妄語。價既已決。不宜中悔。遂斷與之。便使人象負金出。八十頃中。須臾欲滿。殘餘少地(雜阿含經云五百步)須達思惟。何藏金足。祇陀問言。嫌貴置之。答曰不也。祇陀念言。佛必大德乃使斯人輕寶乃爾。即教語須達園地屬卿。樹林屬我。我自上佛(經云我自為佛造立門樓常使如來經行出入)便就施功。

Sudatta's Purchase of Jetavana

Sudatta said to the Buddha: "The people of Śrāvastī mostly follow false teachings — may the Tathāgata, in his great compassion, visit that city." The Buddha said, "There is no monastery there — how could I go?" Sudatta said, "Your disciple will build one." The World-Honored One consented in silence, and sent Śāriputra to instruct him. Together they surveyed the whole area but found nowhere satisfactory except Prince Jeta's garden, level, lush, and conveniently distant. Sudatta told the prince of his wish; the prince, laughing, said he only jested, but at Sudatta's repeated pressing finally said, "If you can cover the ground entirely with gold, leaving no gap, I will give it to you." Sudatta agreed to whatever price was set, but when the prince claimed he had only been joking, Sudatta insisted a prince should not go back on his word, and they took the matter to court. A deva of the Śuddhāvāsa heaven, transformed into a man, arbitrated that a prince's word, once given, should not be revoked — and so the garden was awarded to Sudatta. He had men bring gold on elephants and spread it over the ground; it very nearly covered the whole eighty qing, with only a small patch left uncovered (the Saṃyukta Āgama says five hundred paces). When Prince Jeta asked whether he now resented the price, Sudatta said no — and the prince, reflecting that the Buddha must be a person of extraordinary virtue for a man to treat such wealth so lightly, told him: "The ground shall be yours; the trees and groves shall be mine — I myself will offer them to the Buddha" (the sūtra adds: "I myself will build the gate-tower, that the Tathāgata may always walk in and out through it"). And so the building began.

六師聞之往白國王。長者須達買祇陀園欲為瞿曇興立精舍。聽我徒眾與共斠術。沙門得勝便聽起立。若其不如不得起也。王召須達而問之言。今此六師云。卿買園欲為瞿曇起立精舍。求共沙門弟子。斠其伎術。若得勝者聽立精舍。苟其不如便不得起。須達歸家著垢膩衣愁惱不樂。時舍利弗明日時到著衣持鉢至須達家。即問之曰。何故不樂。須達具答。此六師輩出家來久精誠有素所學伎術無能及者。我今知尊人伎藝能與斠不。舍利弗言。正使此輩六師之眾。滿閻浮提數如竹林。不能動吾足上一毛。欲斠何等自恣聽之。須達歡喜更著新衣沐浴香水等。即往白王。我以問之恣聽其意。王告六師。今聽汝等共沙門斠術。

When the six heretic teachers heard of this, they demanded of the king that their own followers first contest their arts against the Buddha's śramaṇa, and that the monastery be built only if the śramaṇa won. The king summoned Sudatta and told him so, and Sudatta returned home in soiled clothes, grieving. The next day Śāriputra, robed and bowl in hand, came to Sudatta's house and asked why he was unhappy. Sudatta explained that the six teachers had trained for so long that none could match their arts, and he did not know whether Śāriputra could contend with them. Śāriputra said, "Even if the followers of these six teachers filled the whole of Jambudvīpa as thick as a bamboo grove, they could not move a single hair on my foot — let them contend in whatever they please." Sudatta, overjoyed, bathed and dressed anew, told the king he consented freely, and the king informed the six teachers that the contest was granted.

六師宣告國人。却後七日當於城外與沙門斠術。舍衛國中十八億人。時彼國法擊鼓會眾。若擊銅鼓八億人集。若打銀鼓十四億人集。若振金鼓一切皆集。七日期滿至平博處椎擊金鼓。一切都集。六師徒眾有三億人。是時人民悉為國王及其六師敷施高座。爾時須達為舍利弗而施高座。時舍利弗在一樹下入諸禪定。而作是念。此會大眾習邪來久憍慢自高草芥群生當以何德而降伏之。思惟是已即立誓言。若我無數劫中慈孝父母敬尚沙門婆羅門者。我初入會一切大眾為我作禮。六師見眾已集而舍利弗獨未來到。便白王言。瞿曇弟子自知無術。眾會既集怖畏不來。王告須達。斠時已到。佛弟子宜來談論。

The six teachers proclaimed that in seven days the contest would take place outside the city. Śrāvastī held eighteen hundred million people, summoned by drum according to the country's own law — a bronze drum gathering eight hundred million, a silver drum fourteen hundred million, and a golden drum bringing everyone without exception. When the seventh day came and the golden drum was struck at the open ground, everyone gathered — the six teachers' followers alone numbered three hundred million. High seats were set up for the king and the six teachers, and Sudatta set one up for Śāriputra. Śāriputra, seated beneath a tree in meditative absorption, reflected: "This assembly has long practiced false views, proud and self-exalting — by what virtue shall I subdue them?" and vowed: "If, through countless kalpas, I have truly been filial to my parents and honored śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas, then the moment I enter this assembly, may the whole gathering bow to me." Seeing the assembly gathered but Śāriputra still absent, the six teachers told the king that Gautama's disciple, knowing he had no skill, was too afraid to come. The king told Sudatta the time had come for the Buddha's disciple to appear.

時須達至舍利弗所長跪白言。大德大眾已集。願來詣會。時舍利弗從禪定起。更正衣服。以尼師檀著左肩上。徐詳而步如師子王往詣大眾。是時眾人及諸六師忽然起立如風靡草不覺作禮。時舍利弗便昇須達所敷之座。六師眾中有一弟子名勞度差。善知幻術。於大眾前呪作一樹。自然長廣蔭覆眾會枝葉欝茂華果各異。眾人咸言。此變乃是勞度差所作。時舍利弗便以神力作旋嵐風。吹拔樹根倒著於地碎為微塵。眾人皆言舍利弗勝。復呪作一池。其池四邊面皆以七寶。池水之中生種種華。舍利弗又化作一大六牙白象。其一牙上有七蓮華。一一華上有七玉女。其象徐詳往詣池邊。并唅其水池即時滅。復作一山。七寶莊嚴眾池樹木華果茂盛。舍利弗又化作金剛力士。以金剛杵遙用指之。山即破壞無有遺餘。

Sudatta went to Śāriputra and told him the assembly had gathered. Śāriputra rose from his absorption, adjusted his robes, and walked slowly and with dignity, like a lion-king, toward the assembly — and at once the whole crowd and the six teachers rose to their feet, as grass bends before the wind, bowing without even realizing it. Śāriputra ascended the seat Sudatta had prepared. Among the six teachers' followers was a disciple named Raudrākṣa, skilled in illusion, who conjured before the assembly a tree that grew of itself, tall and lush, its shade covering the whole gathering. The crowd credited this to Raudrākṣa — until Śāriputra, by his own power, raised a whirlwind that uprooted the tree and shattered it to dust, and the crowd declared him the victor. Raudrākṣa then conjured a jeweled pool full of flowers; Śāriputra transformed himself into a great six-tusked white elephant, drank up its water, and the pool vanished. Raudrākṣa conjured a jeweled mountain; Śāriputra transformed himself into a vajra-wielding warrior and shattered it to nothing with a single gesture.

Raudrākṣa conjured a ten-headed dragon that rained treasure and shook the earth with thunder; Śāriputra became a garuḍa-king and tore it apart. Raudrākṣa conjured a huge, powerful bull, bellowing and charging; Śāriputra became a lion and devoured it. Raudrākṣa transformed himself into a monstrous yakṣa, fire burning on its head and streaming from its eyes and mouth; Śāriputra transformed into King Vaiśravaṇa, and as the terrified yakṣa fled through fire that had sprung up on every side, it found only the space beside Śāriputra cool and free of flame — it prostrated itself, begged for its life, and the moment shame arose in its heart the fire went out of itself. The crowd proclaimed Śāriputra's victory complete. He then rose into the sky, displayed the four dignified postures, performed eighteen transformations, and returned to his seat; the assembly, seeing his power, rejoiced, and as he expounded the Dharma, each attained the fruits of the path according to his own merit. Three hundred million of the six teachers' own followers went forth to study under Śāriputra.

長者須達共舍利弗往圖精舍。手捉繩頭。時舍利弗欣然含笑。須達問言。尊人何笑。答曰。汝始於此經營地。而六欲天中宮殿已成。即借道眼須達悉見。問舍利弗。是六欲天何處最樂。舍利弗言。第四天中少欲知足。恒有一生補處菩薩。來生其中法訓不絕。須達言曰。我正當生第四天中。出言已竟餘宮悉滅。復更捉繩。時舍利弗慘然憂色。即問尊者何故憂色。答言。汝今見此地中蟻子不耶。汝於過去毘婆尸佛。亦於此地為彼世尊起立精舍。而此蟻子猶在此中生。乃至迦葉佛時亦復如是。九十一劫受一種身。

The elder Sudatta and Śāriputra went to lay out the monastery grounds, each holding one end of the measuring cord, when Śāriputra suddenly smiled. Asked why, he said, "You have only just begun, and already a palace stands ready for you in the six heavens of desire" — and lent Sudatta his own divine eye to see it. Asked which of the six heavens was most joyful, Śāriputra answered that the fourth, where desires are few and a bodhisattva in his final rebirth is always present teaching the Dharma without cease. Sudatta declared, "Then I shall be born in that fourth heaven" — and instantly every other palace vanished, leaving only that one. He took up the cord again, and this time Śāriputra's face grew sorrowful. Asked why, he said: "Do you see these ants in the ground? Under the past Buddha Vipaśyin, you likewise built a monastery for him on this very ground, and these same ants were already living here then — and so it was again under the Buddha Kāśyapa. For ninety-one kalpas they have kept this one form of body."

The monastery was built as a dwelling for the Buddha, its plaster made fragrant with fine sandalwood, with twelve hundred separate rooms and a hundred and twenty summoning-bells. Sudatta asked the king to send an envoy inviting the Buddha, who came from Rājagṛha surrounded by the fourfold assembly, radiating light that shook heaven and earth, liberating beyond number all he passed along the way. As he neared Śrāvastī the whole populace gathered with offerings to welcome him; on arrival his great light illuminated the entire three-thousandfold world, and at the pressure of his toe upon the ground the earth shook, instruments sounded of themselves, and the blind saw, the deaf heard, the mute spoke, and the crippled were made whole. All eighteen hundred million people, witnessing these signs, came rejoicing to the Buddha, who, prescribing the Dharma as a remedy suited to each one's affliction, brought every one of them to the fruits of the path (Source: Sūtra of the Wise and the Foolish, fascicle 10; the Saṃyukta Āgama, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, the Zhong Ben Qi Sūtra, and various vinaya texts largely agree).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The story quietly undercuts its own spectacle twice. First, all that magical combat — trees, elephants, mountains, dragons, a yakṣa duel — turns out to be theater for a crowd that needs a public reason to change its mind; Śāriputra's actual argument for the Dharma comes only afterward, in ordinary teaching, once the contest has done its work of gathering an audience. Second, and more pointedly, the vision of ants unchanged since the time of two previous buddhas, ninety-one kalpas apart, on the very same patch of ground Sudatta is right now purchasing at such expense, relativizes the entire transaction: measured against that timescale, gold-covered earth and even the offer of rebirth in the fourth heaven are both, in their own way, small.

The Eight Causes of a Great Earthquake

The Buddha, at Śrāvastī, told the monks there are eight causes by which the earth shakes greatly. This earth, sixty-eight thousand yojanas deep, is upheld by water resting on empty space; when the wind in that space stirs, the water stirs with it, and the earth shakes — the first cause. A monk of mastered supernormal power, regarding the earth as freely as his own palm, can make it shake — the second. Devas of great power can likewise shake it — the third. When a bodhisattva in Tuṣita is about to descend and take rebirth, the earth shakes — the fourth. When a bodhisattva knows himself newly conceived in his mother's womb, it shakes — the fifth. When he knows the ten months are complete and he is about to be born, it shakes — the sixth. When he goes forth, sits at the seat of enlightenment, subdues Māra, and attains perfect awakening, it shakes — the seventh. And when the Tathāgata enters the realm of nirvāṇa without remainder, it shakes an eighth and final time (Source: Ekottarika Āgama, fascicle 24).

The Poison Tree

In a government garden at Śrāvastī grew a beautiful poison tree; those who rested beneath it suffered splitting headaches, aching backs, and some even died there. The keeper cut it down again and again with a long axe, but within ten days it always grew back exactly as before, and — because it was so beautiful — no one heeded the warning, and his own relatives, drawn by its shade, lost their lives there in turn, until the keeper stood alone in grief. A wise man finally told him he must destroy the root, not merely the branches; fearing this would mean his own death, he instead went forth to study the path. The Buddha then said:

"Cut down a tree without destroying its root, and though cut, it will grow again;
cut down craving without destroying its root, and again and again suffering will grow anew."

His heart awakened, and, reproaching himself, he at once attained the first fruit of the path (Source: Sūtra on Cutting Down the Poison Tree).

The Four Great Rivers

Four great rivers flow from the pool of Anavatapta into the ocean: Gaṅgā, issuing from a golden elephant's mouth, circling the pool once before flowing to the eastern sea; Sindhu, from a silver ox's mouth, to the southern sea; Vakṣu, from a beryl horse's mouth, to the western sea; and Sītā, from a crystal lion's mouth, to the northern sea. Each has four further named tributaries, and in fact five hundred lesser ones apiece — two thousand rivers in all, every one finally reaching the great ocean (Source: [Mahā]vibhāṣā[-śāstra], fascicle 2).

五大河

西流者名恒。南流者名耶云。東流者有兩。一名沙陸。一名阿夷越。北流者名墨。皆流澍于海。立天地來雨落河澍水無增減。去其舊名。合為一海水也(出海八德經)。

The Five Great Rivers

The river flowing west is the Ganges; south, the Yamunā; two flow east, the Śrāvatī and the Aciravatī; and north flows the Mahī. All pour into the sea, which, since heaven and earth began, has never increased or decreased despite the rain and rivers constantly pouring in, each river losing its own name and merging into the single water of the ocean (Source: Sūtra on the Eight Virtues of the Ocean).

大海八德

佛遊無勝國。常以十五日。為諸沙門說戒坐定。佛默無言。阿難曰。坐定。世尊乃曰。諸沙門中。有心邪行違者。非其下賤所能執行。清濁相違。吾不說也。目連入定觀見。即謂之曰。起非爾俗人所應坐處。不肯時起牽臂使出曰爾無至德。心懷六邪。何敢以臭溷之體。坐天香之座。爾是棄人。非沙門矣。佛告沙門。觀彼巨海有八種德。其廣即汪洋無涯。其深則有不測之底。稍入稍深無前所礙。斯一德也。潮不過期。斯二德也。海含眾寶靡所不苞。死屍臭朽海不容焉。斯三德也。海懷眾珍。無求不得。斯四德也。普天之下有五大河。流入于海。皆去舊名。合為一海。斯五德也。五河萬流雨落恒澍。海中水如故曾無增減。斯六德也。海有眾魚因軀巍巍。第一魚身長四千里。第二魚身長八千里。第三魚身長萬二千里。第四魚身長萬六千里。第五魚身長二萬里。第六魚身長二萬四千里。第七魚身長二萬八千里。斯七德也。海水通鹹邊中如一斯八德也(出海八德經)。

The Ocean's Eight Virtues

The Buddha, traveling in the country of Anupama, sat as always on the fifteenth day to recite the precepts, but remained silent. When Ānanda noted the assembly was seated, the Buddha said there was one among the monks of crooked mind and errant conduct, and that pure and impure could not mix, so he would not speak. Maudgalyāyana, perceiving who it was, seized the man's arm, dragged him out, and told him he had no place among a assembly so pure. The Buddha then told the monks to consider how the great ocean holds eight virtues: its vastness and unfathomable depth, ever deeper the further one enters without obstruction — the first; its tide, never missing its appointed time — the second; that it holds every treasure yet will not tolerate a rotting corpse within it — the third; that it holds every rare gem, and nothing sought within it goes unfound — the fourth; that the world's great rivers, on entering it, all lose their former names and become the one sea — the fifth; that ceaseless rain and river-flow never increase or decrease its waters — the sixth; that it holds fish of towering size, the largest twenty-eight thousand li long — the seventh; and that its water is uniformly salt from edge to center — the eighth (Source: Sūtra on the Eight Virtues of the Ocean).

明月摩尼珠

明月摩尼珠。多在龍腦中。若眾生有福德者。自然得之。猶如地獄。自生治罪之器。此寶亦名如意珠。常出一切寶物。衣服飲食隨意所欲。得此珠者。毒不能害。火不能燒。或云。是帝釋所執。金剛與阿修羅鬪時。碎落閻浮提。又言。諸過去久遠佛舍利。法既滅盡。變成此珠以為利益(出大智論第五十九卷)。

The Bright-Moon Maṇi Jewel

The bright-moon maṇi jewel is most often found within a dragon's brain, and a sufficiently meritorious being obtains it spontaneously — just as hell spontaneously produces its own instruments of punishment. Also called the wish-fulfilling jewel, it constantly produces treasure, clothing, and food according to whatever is wished, and whoever holds it cannot be harmed by poison or fire. Some say it is a fragment of Śakra's own vajra weapon, shattered during his battle with the asuras and fallen into Jambudvīpa; others say it is the relics of buddhas from ages past, transformed for the world's benefit once their teaching had entirely passed away (Source: Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra, fascicle 59).

生寶珠

大海中有四寶珠。一切眾寶皆從之生。若無四珠。一切寶物漸就滅盡。諸小龍神不能得見。唯娑伽羅龍王密置深寶藏中。此深寶藏有四種名。一名眾寶積聚。二名無盡寶藏三名遠熾然。四名一切莊嚴聚(出華嚴經第三十卷)。

The Jewels That Give Rise to All Treasure

Within the great ocean lie four jewels from which every treasure is born; without them, all treasure would gradually vanish. Even the lesser dragon spirits cannot see them, for the dragon-king Sāgara keeps them hidden in a deep treasury named, in its four aspects, the Mass of Accumulated Treasures, the Inexhaustible Treasury, the Far-Blazing, and the Mass of All Adornments (Source: Avataṃsaka Sūtra, fascicle 30).

光明大寶

大海之中。有四燃熾光明大寶。一名日藏光明大寶。二名離涸光明大寶。三名火珠光明大寶。四名究竟無餘光明大寶。若大海中無此四寶。四域天下金剛圍山。乃至非想非非想處皆悉漂沒。日藏光明。能變海水為酪。離涸光明。能變海酪為蘇。火珠光明。能燃海蘇。究竟無餘光明大寶。能然海蘇永盡無餘(出華嚴經第四十卷)。

The Four Blazing Jewels

Within the great ocean lie four great jewels of blazing light — Sun-Store, Freedom-from-Drought, Fire-Pearl, and Ultimate Remainderless — without which the four continents, the Cakravāḍa mountains, and even the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception would all be submerged. Sun-Store turns the sea's water to curd; Freedom-from-Drought turns that curd to ghee; Fire-Pearl ignites the ghee; and Ultimate Remainderless burns it away entirely, leaving nothing at all (Source: Avataṃsaka Sūtra, fascicle 40).

The Four Continents Compared, and Uttarakuru

A systematic comparison of milk, lifespan, and facial form across the four continents, followed by a full description of Uttarakuru — a paradise where food, clothing, music, and even children arise without labor, and death itself comes and goes without grief.

Ru-Yi Translation
Milk, Lifespan, and Facial Form Across the Four Continents

In Jambudvīpa, Maitreya was asked how much milk a child drinks from birth to age three: one hundred eighty measures, not counting what it consumed in the womb. In Pūrvavideha to the east, a child drinks one thousand eight hundred measures over the same span; in Aparagodānīya to the west, eight hundred eighty. In Uttarakuru to the north, a newborn is simply set at a crossroads, where passersby offer a finger to suck, and within seven days it reaches full maturity — that land has no milk at all, and beings in the intermediate state there subsist on the wind alone. Jambudvīpa's people live a hundred years; Pūrvavideha's, two hundred fifty; Aparagodānīya's, five hundred; Uttarakuru's, a thousand; and intermediate-state beings, seven days. Jambudvīpa's faces are broad above and narrow below; Pūrvavideha's, perfectly round; Aparagodānīya's, narrow above and broad below; Uttarakuru's, perfectly square; and the faces of intermediate-state beings resemble those of Paranirmitavaśavartin Heaven (Source: Sūtra on the Not-Yet-Arisen Beings of the Three Realms).

欝單曰

北欝單曰天下。周匝廣長。各四十萬里。有種種山。其河兩邊。有種種樹及種種華。水中有船。以四寶作之。浴池名難陀。其水涼濡。底沙皆金。周匝有陛。四寶作之。金陛銀桄銀陛金桄。琉璃陛水精桄。水精陛琉璃桄。有種種蓮華。華若斷者。汁出如乳味甘如蜜。光照四十里。其香亦聞四十里。池東有河。名已味。池南有河。名修竭。池西有河。名大土。池北有河。名善種。是諸河水皆有華樹。以四寶成。池東有園。名賢上。欄楯行樹亦四寶成。園中香樹出種種香。有衣被樹。有瓔珞樹。出種種衣被瓔珞。有音樂樹。出種種音樂。樹高七里。有高六里五里四里三里二里。池南有園觀。名與賢。池西有園觀。名羅越。池北有園觀。名常有華。諸樹所出。及其高卑。亦如東園。

Uttarakuru, the Northern Paradise-Continent

The northern continent of Uttarakuru measures four hundred thousand li in every direction. It has mountains of every kind, and along its riverbanks grow trees and flowers of every kind; boats of the four treasures float on its waters. A bathing pool named Nandā, its water cool and soft, its bottom sand of gold, is ringed with steps of the four treasures — golden steps with silver railings, silver with gold, beryl with crystal, crystal with beryl. Its lotuses, if broken, weep a sap sweet as honey, their light and fragrance both carrying forty li. Four named rivers flow from its four sides, each lined with flowering trees of the four treasures, and gardens surround it on every side — fragrant trees, robe-trees and jewel-trees yielding clothing and ornaments, and music-trees producing every kind of music, some as tall as seven li, others six, five, four, three, or two.

北方天下樹曲交路。天人在上。男女異處。有淨潔粳米。不種自生。出一切味。若欲行婬。意起相視無所言說。男子便前行。女人隨後。至園觀中共相娛樂。或二三日。或至七日。隨意罷去。不相屬也。女人懷妊。七八日便生。持著四徼道中。若有人從四面來者。與指[口*宿]出乳飲之。過七日已。自以福德。即自長大。如閻浮人年二十。若二十五也。周匝四方。有水名阿耨多羅。後夜雲起雨八味水。如人飲食。地若油塗塵土不起。草樹常有。華實皆香。如香熱時。有亂風吹掃上賢園觀。伊蘭風生。吹落華至人膝。此天下人皆入園中遊戲相娛無所係屬。人欲食時。取淨潔粳米。以焰味珠光爨其下。飯熟則四方至隨皆食之。食亦不盡。

Throughout this land the trees bend and interlace above the roads, and the people dwell there, men and women each in their own place. Pure, unhusked rice grows of itself without sowing, yielding every flavor. Intimacy begins with nothing more than a glance; the man walks ahead, the woman follows, and they part again after two, three, or up to seven days, with no lasting bond formed. A woman who conceives gives birth after only seven or eight days, and the child, set down at a crossroads, is offered a finger by whoever passes to drink milk from; within seven days, by the power of its own merit, it grows to the full stature of a person of twenty or twenty-five in Jambudvīpa. Water named Anuttara surrounds the land on every side, and in the small hours clouds rise and rain down water of eight flavors as food itself; the ground raises no dust, plants are ever fragrant, and a warm wind sweeps fallen blossoms up to people's knees as they wander the gardens at will, bound to no one. When hungry, people cook the pure rice over a flame-jewel's own light, and once cooked it is shared by all who come from every direction, and is never exhausted.

有樹名象兜。交曲上合如交落。人民在上止宿。男女異處。人齒髮紺青長八寸。人面色同長短等。皆壽千歲。死生欲界諸天。天壽終。生閻浮提大豪貴家。大小便利。地裂受之受已還合。死時好衣服莊嚴之不啼哭。置四徼道中。欝遮鳥舉置北方天下外(出樓炭經第一卷)。

There is a tree named Xiangdou, its branches interlacing above like a canopy, where the people dwell, men and women apart; their teeth and hair are a lustrous blue-green, eight inches long, their faces all alike, their height all equal, and they live a thousand years. At death they are born among the desire-realm gods, and when that heavenly life ends, they are born into a great and noble household in Jambudvīpa. To relieve themselves, the earth simply opens and closes again beneath them; at death, they are dressed and adorned without weeping and set at a crossroads, where a bird named Yuzhe carries them beyond the borders of this northern land (Source: Lokaprajñapti Sūtra, fascicle 1).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Uttarakuru is worth reading against fascicle 1's account of the desire-realm heavens: it offers nearly everything those heavens offer — food and pleasure without labor, effortless conception and birth, painless death — while its people remain human, not gods. Traditional commentary treats this as a warning rather than an aspiration: precisely because life there is so free of friction, Uttarakuru is the one human realm the texts describe as unfavorable for Dharma practice, since a being needs some friction — old age, sickness, loss, the felt shortness of a hundred years rather than a comfortable thousand — to be moved toward liberation at all. Ease, in this cosmology, is not the same thing as the good.
