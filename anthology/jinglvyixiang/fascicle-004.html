The Buddha's Advent and Final Nirvāṇa

This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation of fascicle 4 of the Jīng Lǜ Yì Xiàng (經律異相): "The Buddha's Advent and Final Nirvāṇa, Part 1" (應始終佛部第一) — the compendium's own compiled life of the Buddha, drawn from the Lalitavistara and related biographical sūtras: his ancient-kalpa origin among the Śākyas, birth and the thirty-two marks, the four sights and the great departure, six years of austerity, the defeat of Māra and enlightenment, the funeral instructions given to Ānanda, and a long, richly detailed account of his final nirvāṇa at Kuśinagara.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 4 complete Source: Taishō T2121, vol. 53, fascicle 4 Compiled by: Bǎochàng and others, 516 CE

Translation Method

Fascicle 4 is a single continuous narrative — the compendium's own compiled biography of the Buddha, drawn mainly from the Lalitavistara (普耀經) — rather than a set of unrelated short stories. It is translated here as one sustained account, in the source's own order, since the compilers themselves clearly intended it to be read as a single life rather than as separable anecdotes.

The Lineage of the One Who Attained the Path

The Śākya clan's own origin story: a king wrongly executed, whose blood — mixed with clay by his grieving teacher — transforms into the first Śākya son and daughter, followed by Dīpaṃkara Buddha's prediction of Śākyamuni's own eventual buddhahood.

Chinese Source

如來昔在久遠劫時。行菩薩道。為大國王。父母崩亡。讓國與弟。獨行求道。見一婆羅門。姓瞿曇氏。從之受學。因同其姓。入於深山。禪思念道。乞食還國。國人不識。呼小瞿曇。自於城外甘蔗園中。起立精舍。有五百大賊。劫盜官財。經園邊過。明日步蹤遂錄菩薩。以木貫身立大樹下血流於地。大瞿曇氏飛來問曰。有何罪酷乃至爾乎。官人放弩射而殺之。大瞿曇泣下沾棺。取血濕土以為泥團。持還精舍。置左右二器中曰。是道士若至誠者。天神當使血化為人。却後十月左即成男。右即成女。姓瞿曇氏。男名舍夷。賢劫中寶佛時又號釋迦。越壽五百萬歲者是也(出十二遊經)佛在摩竭提界。善勝道場元吉樹下。德力降魔。度二估客。惟定光如來。拜吾佛名云。汝於來世九十一劫。當得作佛。字釋迦文。十號具足。如我今也。吾從是來。積功累行。六度四等修持不勌。功報無遺大願成果(出中本起上卷)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Long ago, in a distant kalpa, the Tathāgata practiced the bodhisattva path as the king of a great country. When his parents died, he ceded the kingdom to his younger brother and went forth alone in search of the path. He met a brahmin of the Gautama clan and studied under him, thereby taking the same clan name, and entered deep into the mountains to meditate on the path, begging for food and returning to the country — but the people there no longer recognized him, and called him "Little Gautama." He built a hermitage in a sugarcane grove outside the city. Five hundred great bandits, having plundered the royal treasury, passed by the edge of this grove; the next day their tracks were followed and the bodhisattva was seized in their place. He was impaled on a stake beneath a great tree, his blood flowing onto the ground. His teacher, the elder Gautama, flew to the scene and asked what grave crime could have brought him to this — but the officials shot him with crossbows and killed him regardless. The elder Gautama wept, his tears falling on the coffin; he gathered the blood-soaked earth into two clay balls, carried them back to the hermitage, placed them in two vessels, and said, "If this ascetic was truly sincere, may the heavenly spirits cause this blood to become human." Ten months later, the ball on the left had become a boy and the one on the right a girl, both of the clan Gautama; the boy was named Śākya — the very one who, in this Fortunate Kalpa, at the time of the Buddha Ratnasambhava, was also called Śākyamuni, whose lifespan exceeds five million years (Source: Sūtra of the Buddha's Twelve Journeys). The Buddha, at the site of enlightenment in Magadha, beneath the tree of original auspiciousness, subdued Māra by the power of his virtue and delivered two merchants — it was there that Dīpaṃkara Buddha bestowed on me the prediction: "In a future age, ninety-one kalpas hence, you shall become a buddha, named Śākyamuni, complete with the ten epithets, just as I am now." From that time I accumulated merit and practice without tiring, cultivating the six perfections and the four immeasurables, and my reward has now come to nothing wasted, my great vow brought to fruition (Source: Zhong Ben Qi Sūtra, upper fascicle).

Manifestation of Birth in the Royal Palace

The devas' choice of the Śākya clan, conception and the ten thousand auspicious signs, birth beneath the Plakṣa tree, the seer Asita's prophecy and the thirty-two marks, the schooling contest, the archery contest for Gopī's hand, and finally the four sights and the great departure into homelessness.

Chinese Source

究竟菩薩。在兜率天。諸天共議。當使菩薩現生何氏。種英天子問曰。一生補處降神何種。答曰。種姓有六十德者。我當降之(文多不載)唯有釋家久植德本。迦維羅衛人。大小和穆上下相承。國富民樂渴仰一乘。且白淨王性行仁賢。夫人姓瞿曇氏。溫良忠善護身口意。已五百世為菩薩母。王后晏寢。菩薩化乘白象。冠日之精。入于胎中。身心安樂猶如深禪。詣無憂樹下。遣使啟王。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bodhisattva, having reached his final stage in Tuṣita Heaven, the assembled devas debated into which clan he should be born. A deva named Excellent Seed asked into what lineage the one in his final rebirth should descend, and the answer came: one whose lineage possesses sixty virtues (much of the discussion is omitted here). Only the Śākya clan had long planted such roots of virtue: the people of Kapilavastu lived in harmony, high and low, their country wealthy and content, all thirsting after the one vehicle. King Śuddhodana was benevolent and wise, and his consort, of the Gautama clan, gentle and loyal, guarding body, speech, and mind — she had already been the bodhisattva's mother for five hundred lifetimes. As the queen lay at rest, the bodhisattva, riding a white elephant crowned with the essence of the sun, entered her womb, his body and mind at ease as in deep meditation, and sent word to the king from beneath the Aśoka tree.

時無憂林有十種瑞。一忽然廣博。二土石變為金剛。三寶樹行列。四沈香莊嚴。五華鬘充滿。六眾寶流出。七池生芙蓉。八天龍夜叉合掌而住。九天女恭敬。十一切諸佛放光普照。王大歡喜。后身輕軟。不想三毒。諸有疾者手摩必愈。既滿十月臨產之時。有三十二瑞。一後園林木自然生果。二陸地出青蓮大如車輪。三枯樹生華。四七寶車至。五地中寶藏自然涌出。六名香好華遍布遠近。七雪山五百師子羅住城門。八五百白象皆住殿前。九細雨澤香。十百味飲食給諸飢渴(文多不載與諸本起同)。四月八日夜明星出時。后思園觀遊憐鞞樹下。三千國土六反震動。沸宿隕落樹即屈枝。母即攀執。諸天散華。從右脇生。身長丈六。即行七步。寶華承足。舉手住而言。天上天下唯我為尊。三界皆苦何可樂者。釋梵奉侍。四王接上金案。龍降香水以充洗浴。五百青衣各生力士。白馬產駒。黃羊生羔(瑞應本起云奴名車匿馬名揵陟諸本起大同細異)。後七日母便命終。生忉利天。

Ten auspicious signs then appeared in the Aśoka grove: it grew vast and spacious, earth and stone turned to vajra, jeweled trees stood in rows, aloeswood adorned the grove, garlands filled every space, treasure flowed forth of itself, lotuses bloomed in the pools, devas and nāgas stood in reverence, heavenly maidens paid homage, and all buddhas everywhere shone their light. The queen's body grew light and soft, her touch alone healing the sick. As the ten months drew to a close, thirty-two further signs appeared: fruit ripened out of season, blue lotuses as large as cartwheels sprang from dry ground, withered trees flowered, carriages of the seven treasures arrived, buried treasure welled up of itself, fragrance and flowers spread far and near, five hundred lions and five hundred white elephants gathered at the city gates and palace, a fine fragrant rain fell, and food of a hundred flavors appeared for the hungry (much else is omitted here, largely agreeing with the other biographical sūtras). On the eighth day of the fourth month, as the morning star appeared, the queen strolled to the garden, and beneath a Plakṣa tree — as the three thousand lands shook six times and a constellation fell — the tree bent its branch of itself for the mother to hold; the devas scattered flowers, and the child was born from her right side, one zhang six feet tall, and at once walked seven steps, a lotus springing up beneath each foot. He raised his hand and declared: "In heaven above and on earth below, I alone am the honored one; all three realms are suffering — what is there in them to delight in?" Śakra and Brahmā attended him, the Four Heavenly Kings received him on a golden table, and nāgas bathed him with fragrant water; five hundred maidservants each bore a mighty son that same hour, a mare foaled, and a ewe lambed (the Sūtra of Auspicious Responses names the servant Chandaka and the horse Kaṇṭhaka). Seven days later the mother's life ended, and she was born in Trāyastriṃśa Heaven.

太子幼稚應須料理。時有說者。唯大愛道是大子姨母。清淨無夫當能育養。時白淨王。詣大愛道求為乳哺。愛道奉。雪山梵志名阿夷頭耆。見太子悲歎流涕。王問其故。答曰。仰慶大王。生此神人。昨天地大動。其正為此。我之相法。太子有三十二相。一體軀金色。二頂有肉髻。三其髮紺青。四眉間白毫。五項出日光。六目睫紺色。七上下俱眴。八口四十齒。九齒白齊平。十方頰車。十一廣長舌。十二合滿堂。十三師子膺。十四身方正。十五脩臂。十六指長。十七足跟滿。十八安平正。十九內外握。二十合曼掌。二十一手千輻輪理。二十二足千輻輪理。二十三陰馬藏。二十四鹿腨腸。二十五鉤瑣骨。二十六毛右旋。二十七一孔一毛。二十八皮毛細軟。二十九不受塵水。三十胸有萬字(瑞應同少二)。身有此者。若在家。為轉輪聖王。七寶自至。若出家為自然佛。傷我年已晚暮。不覩佛興。是故悲耳。王厚相賞給。告大愛道。深加敬護(出普耀經第二卷又出釋迦譜第一卷)。

The infant prince needed care, and it was decided that only Mahāprajāpatī, his maternal aunt, being chaste and without a husband, could raise him; the king asked her to nurse the child, and she consented. A brahmin seer of the Snow Mountains, Asita, on seeing the prince, wept, and told the king he rejoiced that so divine a being had been born, and that the earth's trembling the day before was on his account. According to physiognomy, the prince bore the thirty-two marks: a golden body, a fleshy crown-protrusion, hair of a lustrous dark blue-green, a tuft of white hair between the brows, light like the sun from the crown, blue-green eyelashes blinking together above and below, forty teeth white and even, square jaws, a broad, long tongue, a voice that could fill a hall, a lion's chest, a squarely proportioned body, long arms and fingers, full heels resting flat on the ground, webbing between fingers and toes, soft full palms marked with a thousand-spoked wheel, the same on the feet, concealed male organs as a horse's, calves like a deer's, interlocking bones, hair curling to the right with a single hair from each pore, fine soft skin that dust and water could not cling to, and the character "萬" upon the chest (the Sūtra of Auspicious Responses gives the same, save for two). Whoever bears this body becomes, if a householder, a wheel-turning sage king to whom the seven treasures come of themselves, or, if he goes forth, a buddha of his own accord. "It grieves me," Asita said, "that my own years are too far advanced for me to live to see the Buddha's rise — this is why I weep." The king rewarded him generously and told Mahāprajāpatī to give the child the deepest care (Source: Lalitavistara, fascicle 2; also the Śākya Genealogy, fascicle 1).

太子七歲。乘羊車。眾釋導從。往詣書師。師名選友。太子問曰。師有何書見教。答曰。有梵佉留法。可相教也。太子曰。異書有六十四種。何止二耶。師曰。願聞其名。太子答曰。梵書佉留書。護眾書。疾堅書。龍鬼書。乾闥婆書。阿須倫鹿輪書。天腸書。轉數書。觀空書(文多不載)欲以何書而見教耶。師不能解。讚歎而已。太子為諸童子。分別本末。勸發道心(瑞應經云書缺二字以問師師不應達反啟其志)。太子年至十七。王為納妃。簡選數千。最後得一小國王。姓瞿曇氏。波須弗(梁言善覺)女名瞿夷。端正無比淨如蓮花。八國爭娉悉未許與。王召現之。今為太子結娉卿女。善覺愁憂。若不許者必見征伐。若許與者八國成怨。女言。白淨王國。武藝最勝。諸國所憚。王勅國內。却後七日。太子現術。能者宜集(長阿含經云執杖釋種女云有術藝者妻之)調達撲殺一象。太子擲出城外。天文地理八萬異術。無有及太子者。調達手搏太子少。太子接擲空中三反不損。復共射鼓。調達射中。四十里鼓不能過難。太子引弓弓皆軟折。問有異弓任吾用者不。王曰。亡祖用弓奇異無雙無能用者。在天寺中。取給太子。一切諸釋無能上者。太子用射中百里鼓。箭沒地中涌泉自出至鐵圍山。三千剎土六反震動。即以瞿夷。為太子第一夫人。隨世習俗現相娛樂(瑞應經云太子納妻久不交接一手指妃腹後乃生男)又取移施長者女。名耶惟檀。為第二夫人。又取釋種長者女。名曰鹿野。為第三夫人。太子當作飛行皇帝。立三時殿。置六萬婇女。羅云從天變沒化現而生。

At seven, the prince rode in a goat-carriage, escorted by the Śākyas, to his writing-teacher, Viśvāmitra. Asked what script he intended to teach, the teacher named the Brāhmī and Kharoṣṭhī scripts; the prince pointed out that sixty-four scripts existed, then named them all — Brāhmī, Kharoṣṭhī, and scripts for protecting the multitude, for swiftness, for nāgas and spirits, for gandharvas, and more (much else is omitted here) — asking which of these the teacher meant to teach him. The teacher, unable to answer, could only praise him, and the prince himself explained the roots and branches of each script to the other children, inspiring in them the resolve for the path. At seventeen, the king sought a consort for him from among several thousand candidates, settling at last on Gopī, daughter of the minor king Daṇḍapāṇi — matchless in beauty, pure as a lotus, and already contested by eight kingdoms. Daṇḍapāṇi, fearing either refusal or the other kingdoms' enmity, proclaimed a contest of skill, and in seven days the prince outmatched every rival: he threw an elephant's carcass clear of the city, mastered eighty thousand arts beyond compare, threw the wrestler Devadatta into the air unharmed, and, when every available bow proved too weak for him, drew his own great-grandfather's ancient bow from its shrine and shot an arrow a hundred li into a drum, the arrow's impact opening a spring that reached all the way to the Cakravāḍa mountains as the three thousand lands shook six times. Gopī became his first consort; he also took Yaśodharā, daughter of the elder Yiṣi, as his second, and Mṛgajā, daughter of a Śākya elder, as his third. Destined to become a flying emperor, he built three palaces for the three seasons with sixty thousand palace women, and his son Rāhula descended from heaven and manifested his own birth in time.

太子後出東城門。王勅嚴治道路。莫令不淨。太子威神之所建立。天化老人。頭白齒落。目冥耳聾。柱杖僂步。太子知而故問。此何人也。御者曰。是名老人。太子曰。人命如流難可得再。非獨為人天下皆爾。迴車還宮愍念不樂。後出城南門。遇見疾人。大腹羸瘦臥于道側。問于御者。答曰。病人。太子曰。萬物無常有身有苦。吾亦當然。即還入宮。後出城西門。見一死人。室家悲哭。御者曰。死人。人生有死。如春有冬。人物一貫。太子曰。夫死痛矣。精神劇矣。吾見死者。形壞體化。而神不滅。吾不能復以死受生。往來五道勞我精神。迴車而還。復於他日。出城北門。見一沙門。衣服齊整手執法器。御者曰。此名比丘。棄捨情欲。心憙一切。欲度十方。太子曰。善哉。是吾所樂。我不辭王位而出家者。此則不應。

The prince went out through the eastern gate; though the king had ordered the roads cleared of every impurity, the devas conjured an old man — white-haired, toothless, dim-eyed, deaf, bent over his cane. The prince, knowing full well, asked what manner of person this was. "An old man," the charioteer said. The prince said, "Human life flows on like a stream, never to be had twice — and this is true of the whole world, not one person alone," and returned to the palace, grieving. Through the southern gate he later saw a sick man, swollen and wasted, and reflected, "All things are impermanent; wherever there is a body, there is suffering, and I too shall be no different." Through the western gate he saw a dead man, his household weeping, and the charioteer explained that death follows life as winter follows spring; the prince said, "Death is a bitter thing — I see that when a person dies, the form is destroyed and the body transformed, yet the spirit does not perish, and I can no longer bear to keep taking on birth through death, wandering wearily among the five destinies." Later, through the northern gate, he saw a monk, his robes neat, a ritual implement in hand — "one who has cast off worldly desire and wishes to deliver the ten directions," the charioteer explained. The prince said, "Excellent — this is exactly what I long for. If I do not renounce my royal position and go forth from home, this would not be right."

即時靜夜入王宮殿。光照遠近。父王覺起。即啟父曰。諸天勸助。今應出家。父王悲泣。何所志願。何時當還。太子欲得四願。一者不老。二者無病。三者不死。四者不別。假使父王與此四願。不復出家。王曰。自古及今無有得者。益大愁悲。即勅五百釋子多勇力者。宿衛四門。城門開閉聲聞四十里。裘夷意疑不離其側。太子念道清淨不宜在家。當處山林研精行禪。時年二十九。以四月七日夜半後。裘夷時得五夢。即便驚覺。太子問之。對曰。夢見須彌崩。明月落地。珠光忽滅。頭髮自墮。人奪我蓋。菩薩知夢為我身耳。曰須彌不崩。明月續照。珠光不滅。頭髮不墮。傘蓋猶存。且自安寐。慎莫憂失。夜觀伎女百節皆空。譬如芭蕉。鼻涕目淚。樂器縱橫。顧視其妻。具見形體。腦髓髑髏心肝腸胃。外是革囊中盛臭穢。猶如假借。當還亦不得久。三界無怙惟道是恃。欲界諸天住於空中。法行天子。遙白太子。時已至矣。佛星適現。喚車匿起。被於揵陟。四天王與無數夜叉龍等。皆被鎧甲。從四方來。稽首致敬。諸天恐有留難。即遣厭神。入宮厭寐。城中男女悉皆寢極。孔雀眾鳥莫不疲臥。車匿悲泣。門鑰不開。四神捧舉馬足。踰出宮城。帝釋前導。放大淨光詣佛樹下(出普耀經第三卷又出第四卷)。

That very night he entered the royal chamber and woke his father, saying the devas had urged him on and he must now go forth. His father, weeping, asked what he wished and when he would return; the prince asked for four things instead — freedom from old age, sickness, death, and separation — which, if granted, would keep him home. The king, admitting no one had ever attained such things, set five hundred strong Śākya youths to guard the gates day and night, while Gopī, suspecting something, would not leave his side. Resolved that the path required a purity unsuited to household life, the prince, then twenty-nine, waited until after midnight on the seventh day of the fourth month, when Gopī woke from five ominous dreams — Sumeru collapsing, the moon falling, a pearl's light dying, her hair falling out, her parasol seized from her — which he gently reassured her all concerned himself alone and had not truly happened. That night he looked on his sleeping musicians and saw their bodies as hollow as the plantain tree, mucus and tears and instruments strewn about; he looked on his own wife and saw beneath her form only skull, organs, and filth in a bag of skin, borrowed and soon to be returned. The devas of the desire realm called down that the time had come; he woke Chandaka to saddle Kaṇṭhaka, as the Four Heavenly Kings and countless spirits gathered in reverence, and a spirit of drowsiness put the whole sleeping city into its deepest sleep. When the gate-locks would not open, four spirits lifted the horse bodily over the palace wall, Śakra leading the way in great light toward the Bodhi tree (Source: Lalitavistara, fascicles 3 and 4).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Notice how carefully this account motivates the departure before it ever happens: not a sudden impulse, but four separate confrontations — old age, sickness, death, and a monk who names an alternative — each processed in full, then a fifth, private confrontation that night with the sleeping dancers and his own wife, seen for exactly what a body is beneath its surface. The four impossible wishes he asks of his father (no old age, no sickness, no death, no separation) are not a child's bargaining; they are the precise diagnosis the rest of his life's search will address one by one.

Ru-Yi Translation

Ānanda asked about funeral rites. The Buddha said his own should follow that of a wheel-turning sage king: the body bathed in fragrant water, wrapped in cotton, then bound in five hundred lengths of white cloth, placed in a golden coffin filled with sesame oil, itself set within an iron casing and an outer casing of sandalwood, then heaped with fragrant firewood and cremated; once the fire died, the relics should be gathered and a stūpa raised at a crossroads, its finial marked and banners hung, so that all who saw it would be moved and benefited. Four kinds of persons deserve such a stūpa: a tathāgata, a pratyekabuddha, a śrāvaka, and a wheel-turning king, each honored with incense, flowers, banners, and music. The Buddha then had a couch laid between the twin sāla trees, his head to the south and face to the north — since the Dharma, once it spread, would long abide in the northern lands — and lay down on his right side like a reclining lion. Asked how many kinds of burial exist in Jambudvīpa, the Buddha answered, "Countless" — his own land practiced burial by water, fire, and stūpa; the people of China favored carved, bejeweled carriages, music, and chanted songs to please the departed, the body richly dressed and coffined and escorted by many thousands to a splendid resting place; some border peoples kept the coffin in a stone chamber to be opened and the bones washed in times of illness, seeking a cure through merit; and some had no coffin at all, keeping the remains on a high platform to be brought down and prayed over when illness struck. The Buddha said that those who wished to cultivate merit within his own teaching should instead diligently practice the six perfections and uphold the ten good deeds, which alone lead to rebirth in heaven and toward the unsurpassed path (Source: Sūtra of Consecration, fascicle 6; also Dīrgha Āgama, fascicle 1).

The Buddha said, "I shall now, for your sake, put an end to poverty — let the unsurpassed rain of the Dharma fall upon you, that you may perfect the perfection of giving." The assembly rejoiced, declaring Cunda's offering, once accepted by the Buddha, made him a true child of the Buddha.

佛言。純陀。今正是時。如來正爾當般涅槃。第二第三亦復如是。純陀舉聲號哭。復白大眾。我等今共。五體投地。同聲勸佛。莫般涅槃。佛告純陀。莫大啼哭自亂汝心。我以哀愍汝及一切。是故今日欲入涅槃。何以故。諸佛法爾有為亦然。速辦所施。不宜久停。佛又從面門放五色光。照純陀身。純陀身持餚饍。疾往佛所。憂悲悵怏。猶願矜哀住壽一劫。佛言汝欲令我久住者。宜當奉最後具足檀波羅蜜。一切菩薩天人雜類。異口同音唱言。奇哉純陀。成就大福。我等無德。所設供具則為唐捐。世尊欲令一切眾望滿足。於自身上。一一毛孔化無量佛。一一諸佛各有無量諸比丘僧。悉皆示現受其供養。釋迦如來。自受純陀所奉設者。純陀所持粳糧成熟之食。摩伽陀國滿足八斛。以佛神力。皆悉充足一切大會(長阿含經小異文多不載)。

"Cunda," the Buddha said, "the time has truly come" — repeating this three times — until Cunda, weeping, led the whole assembly in prostrating themselves and begging the Buddha not to enter nirvāṇa. The Buddha told him not to disturb his own mind with such weeping, for it was precisely out of compassion that he wished to enter nirvāṇa that very day — such is simply the nature of all conditioned things, even for a buddha — and that Cunda should make haste with his offering rather than delay. Cunda brought his prepared meal, still hoping against hope the Buddha might yet be persuaded to remain a kalpa, but was told that the surest way to keep him "long" was instead to complete this final act of giving. The whole assembly, fearing their own offerings would now be wasted beside Cunda's singular merit, watched as the Buddha, wishing to satisfy every hope at once, manifested from every pore of his body countless buddhas each attended by countless monks, all appearing to receive an offering — yet it was Śākyamuni himself who accepted what Cunda had actually prepared, a mere eight measures of cooked rice by Magadhan measure, which by the Buddha's own power proved sufficient to satisfy the entire vast assembly.

有疾右脇而臥。如他病人。告迦葉菩薩。是諸眾生。不知大乘方等密語。便謂。如來真實有疾。今於娑羅雙樹間。示現倚臥師子之床欲入涅槃。令諸未得阿羅漢果眾弟子等及諸力士生大憂苦。令天人阿修羅等大設供養。又使諸人。以千端疊。纏裹其身。七寶為棺(菩薩從兜術天下經云如來自擗三衣各取三條鋪金棺裏以襯身臥兩脚相累手以鉢鍚付授阿難)盛滿香油。積諸香木。以火焚之。唯除二端。不可得燒。一極襯身。二最在外。為諸眾生。分散舍利。以為八分。一切聲聞弟子咸言。如來入於涅槃。當知如來。亦不畢定入於涅槃。何以故。如來常住不變易故。已願聞正法。當棄貪婬。遵承我教。精進行道。是為最後佛之遺命。宜共慎之。汝諸比丘。觀佛儀容。難得覩見。却後一億四千餘歲。乃至彌勒佛耳。

Reclining on his right side as though genuinely ill, he told the bodhisattva Kāśyapa that sentient beings, not understanding the secret language of the Mahāyāna's Vaipulya teachings, would suppose the Tathāgata truly sick — but that he manifested this apparent illness deliberately, between the twin sāla trees, so that those disciples not yet arhats, and the mighty ones among them, would be moved to grief, and devas, humans, and asuras alike moved to great offerings, wrapping his body in a thousand lengths of cloth within a coffin of the seven treasures, filled with fragrant oil and cremated — save for two portions of cloth left unburned, so that his relics might later be divided into eight shares for the benefit of all beings. And though all his śrāvaka disciples would say "the Tathāgata has entered nirvāṇa," it should be understood that the Tathāgata, in the final sense, never truly enters nirvāṇa at all, since the Tathāgata abides forever, unchanging; his last testament was simply that they should cast aside greed and lust, uphold his teaching, and practice the path with diligence — for his very form and bearing, once gone, would not be seen again until Maitreya's own advent, some hundred million four hundred thousand years hence.

佛臨涅槃。地大振動。諸天世人皆悉驚怖。諸有幽冥。日月光明。所不至處。皆蒙大明。各得相見。天散華香。時佛滅度。六欲天王。金毘羅神。密迹力士。佛母摩耶。雙樹娑羅園神。各作偈頌。諸比丘悲慟。各自歔欷而言。如來滅度何其駃哉。群生長衰世間眼滅。阿那律告諸比丘。止止勿悲。諸天在上。儻有怪責。諸比丘問。上有幾天。答曰。充塞虛空豈可計量。皆於空中徘徊。搔擾悲號。擗踊歔欷而言。如來滅度何其駃哉。群生長衰世間眼滅(長阿含與雙卷泥洹大同出大涅槃第一卷)阿難比丘今何所在。文殊師利言。在娑羅林外。去此十二由旬。為六萬四千億魔之所嬈亂。是諸魔眾悉自變身。為如來像。說種種法種種示現。阿難念言。昔所未見。誰之所作。將非釋迦。欲發是語。都不從意。阿難受大苦惱。不能得來。佛說大陀羅尼呪。文殊受之。至阿難所。為魔誦說。諸魔王等發菩提心。文殊即與阿難俱還佛所(出菩薩從兜率天下經)。

As the Buddha drew near to nirvāṇa, the earth shook greatly, and even the darkest places the light of sun and moon had never reached were suddenly illuminated; when he passed, the kings of the six desire heavens, various spirits, and his own mother Māyā each composed a verse of lament, and the monks sighed to one another that the Tathāgata's passing had come so swiftly, the world's own eye now extinguished. Aniruddha told them not to grieve so loudly lest the countless devas above take offense at the disturbance. Meanwhile, asked where Ānanda was, Mañjuśrī explained he was some distance away, tormented by tens of thousands of Māra's hosts who had each taken on the Buddha's own likeness to confuse him; the Buddha's own dhāraṇī spell, carried to him by Mañjuśrī, at last dispelled the demons — some of whom, in the process, awakened the aspiration for enlightenment themselves — and Mañjuśrī returned with Ānanda to the Buddha's side.

諸末羅。集於種種供養。復竟一日以佛舍利。置於床上。諸末羅童舉床。皆不能勝。阿那律言。汝等且止。諸天欲留七日。展諸人民皆得供養。如來三從金棺裏出金色臂。問阿難至。平治道路灑掃燒香未。云何阿難。吾前後所出。方等大乘。汝悉得不。阿難對曰。唯佛知之。佛言如是。諸經今為畢竟。七日末羅童子捧舉四角擎。汝可持幡蓋。燒香散華。作眾伎樂前後導從。安詳而行。入城東門。遍諸街巷。出城北門。渡熙連禪河。到天冠寺。末羅使臣。積香木竟。火燒不然。阿那律言。諸天意以滅火。迦葉將五百弟子從波波國還。願見佛身。迦葉遇一尼乾。手執曼陀羅華。問曰。知我師在不。答曰。滅來七日。我從彼得此華。迦葉不悅。五百弟子婉轉號咷。不能自勝。悲欷言曰。善逝涅槃何其駃哉。時跋難陀言。且莫啼哭。我等於摩呵羅邊。或得解脫。迦葉催諸比丘。疾疾執持衣鉢。往拘尸城。及見舍利。問阿難曰。世尊舍利可得見不。答曰。劫貝裹。白疊纏。內金棺中。藏鐵槨內。衣以香薪。即欲焚難可得見。迦葉三請。答曰如初。前至香[卄/積]。佛蹋重棺現於兩足(四分律云棺槨自開出現雙足)足下輪相有諸異色。即問阿難。答云。女人心軟。前禮佛時。淚墮手捉。迦葉作禮。大眾同拜。繞樹三匝。各興偈頌。香[卄/積]處處不燒自然(長阿含雙卷涅槃略同文多不載)火極熾盛。難可止息。娑羅樹神以力滅之(出涅槃長阿含雙卷泥洹又出菩薩從兜術天下經)。

The Malla people gathered offerings, but when they tried to lift the bier bearing the Buddha's body, none could bear its weight — Aniruddha explained the devas wished it kept seven days so all the people might make their own offerings first. Three times the Tathāgata extended his golden arm from the coffin to ask after Ānanda and the road's preparations, and to confirm that Ānanda had retained every teaching he had given. After the seven days, the procession carried the body in state through the city and across the Hiraṇyavatī River to the Devamukuṭa shrine — but the funeral pyre would not catch fire, for Mahākāśyapa, returning from afar with five hundred disciples, wished to view the Buddha's body first. Learning from a passing ascetic that the Buddha had already died seven days before, Kāśyapa and his disciples wept without restraint, though Upananda urged them not to weep, since freedom from old restraints might now be at hand. Hastening to Kuśinagara, Kāśyapa asked three times to see the Buddha's body, and was told each time that it was already sealed within its coffin and casing, ready for cremation — until, coming before the pyre himself, the Buddha extended both his feet from within the coffin to be seen, their soles marked with wheels of unusual color; Kāśyapa, learning that a woman's tears had once fallen there as she wept before the Buddha, bowed in reverence with the whole assembly, circling the pyre three times, each reciting a verse — and only then, once the fire at last caught and blazed almost beyond control, did the spirit of the sāla trees finally quench it by his own power (Source: Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra; the Dīrgha Āgama's two-fascicle Nirvāṇa Sūtra; also the Sūtra of the Bodhisattva's Descent from Tuṣita).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The compendium is careful to frame the Buddha's evident suffering — the "illness," the reclining posture, the entire spectacle of grief — as a deliberate teaching device rather than an actual event befalling him, addressed explicitly to "sentient beings who do not understand the secret language of the Vaipulya teachings." This is the same doctrinal move later elaborated at length in the Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra: the Buddha's body is described as suffering and dying only because beings need to see impermanence enacted in order to learn from it, while the Tathāgata himself, in the final sense, neither suffers nor ceases. Cunda's episode makes the same point from the other direction — his fear that his gift will be wasted, and the Buddha's insistence on accepting his actual rice over any conjured substitute, together argue that an ordinary person's sincere offering outranks any cosmic display.

Māyā's Five Signs of Decline

Queen Māyā, in heaven, foresees her son's death in a dream and descends to grieve at his coffin — and the Buddha rises to greet his own mother one last time, in a scene the compendium frames explicitly as a lesson for future generations about filial devotion.

Chinese Source

佛般涅槃。摩耶夫人在於天上。五衰相現。一頭上華萎。二腋下汗出。三項中光滅。四兩目數瞬。五不樂本座。又得五夢。一須彌山崩。四海水竭。二羅剎奔走挑人眼目。三天失寶冠身無光明。四寶珠幢倒失如意珠。五師子嚙身痛如刀割。得此夢已即便驚寤。此非吉祥。我昔在於白淨王宮。因晝寢中。得希有夢。見一天子。身黃金色。乘白象王。從諸天子。作妙伎樂。觀日之精。入我右脇。身心安樂。即便懷妊。悉達太子為世照明。今此五夢甚可怖畏。必是我子涅槃之相。時阿那律殯佛既畢。昇忉利天。偈告摩耶。摩耶氣絕良久與諸眷屬下雙樹間。見僧伽梨及鉢錫。執之號慟。絕而復蘇曰。我子福度天人。今此諸物空無有主。佛以神力令諸棺蓋自然開發。佛合掌而起。放大光明。問訊母言。遠屈來下。諸行法爾。願勿啼泣。阿難雖自抑忍。白佛。後世眾生必當問我。佛臨滅度復何所說。云何答之。佛告阿難。汝當答言。佛已入涅槃。摩耶夫人下。如來為後不孝眾生。從金棺出合掌問訊。并說上偈諸敬。此經名為佛臨滅度母子相見經。如是受持(出摩耶經下卷)。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the Buddha entered nirvāṇa, Lady Māyā, in heaven, showed the five signs of decline and had five ominous dreams — Sumeru collapsing, rākṣasas gouging out eyes, a deva losing his crown and light, a jeweled banner toppling, a lion's bite like a knife's cut. Waking with a start, she recalled her own dream long ago of a golden deva on a white elephant entering her right side, by which she had conceived the prince who would illuminate the world — and knew at once these new dreams could only mean her son's own nirvāṇa. Aniruddha, having completed the funeral rites, ascended to Trāyastriṃśa Heaven to tell her in verse; overcome and reviving only after a long while, Māyā descended with her attendants to the twin sāla trees, and, finding only his empty robe and bowl there, wailed that her son who had delivered devas and men now left these things ownerless. The Buddha, by his own power, caused the coffin to open of itself, and rose with palms joined in a great light to greet his mother, telling her gently that all conditioned things are simply so by nature, and that she should not weep. Ānanda, restraining his own grief, asked the Buddha how he should answer future generations who would surely ask what was said at this final meeting; the Buddha told him to answer simply that after his nirvāṇa, when Lady Māyā came down, the Tathāgata — for the sake of unfilial beings yet to come — rose from his coffin with palms joined to greet her, and spoke this verse of reverence. This sūtra is named the Sūtra of the Mother and Son Meeting as the Buddha Draws Near to His Final Passing, and is to be received and upheld accordingly (Source: Māyā Sūtra, latter fascicle).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The sūtra is unusually explicit about its own purpose: the Buddha rises from his coffin specifically "for the sake of unfilial beings of ages to come" — meaning the entire scene exists as an object lesson, staged after death, so that future readers cannot claim the demands of filial piety end when a parent is out of reach. A being who has already entered the state beyond birth and death still interrupts it to greet his grieving mother; the implied argument is that ordinary people, whose parents are still living, have far less excuse to neglect them.
