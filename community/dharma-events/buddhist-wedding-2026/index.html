A wedding before the Buddha.

On the first day of winter, Venerable Yang Hui officiated at the Buddhist wedding of Richard Kuo and Dom Jones at Ru-Yi.

DateSaturday, November 7, 2026
Lunar date9th month, 29th day · Start of Winter
OfficiantVenerable Yang Hui
EmceeLuke Lin (Chinese & English)
PlaceRu-Yi Meditation Center, Fengguidou

On November 7, 2026, the first day of winter on the traditional calendar, Ru-Yi Meditation Center held a Buddhist wedding on Fengguidou mountain. Venerable Yang Hui officiated as Richard Kuo (郭嘉禾) and Dom Jones (郭慧心) made their vows before the Buddha, surrounded by the nuns, family, friends, and members of our English classes and study circles. The whole ceremony was held in Chinese and English.

Richard is a businessman who has organized camps and mentored students at many schools across the United States. Dom has taught at Ru-Yi since early 2025 as the Teacher Dom behind our Thursday English School, and chairs the Advocacy Board of My Culture Connect, visiting schools every Tuesday as a volunteer to promote character education and the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Both have given much of their lives to young people. Dom is a Buddhist, so the two chose to make their promises here, before the Buddha.

Morning

Refuge, and a table full of tangyuan皈依・湯圓

That morning, Dom took refuge in the Three Jewels and received the Five Precepts from Venerable Yang Hui, with the Dharma name Ru Hui (如慧).

Meanwhile, the nuns and volunteers gathered around a table rolling tangyuan, small pink and white rice balls. In Taiwan, tangyuan carry three wishes: they are round, for a life that is whole and complete; they are sweet, for sweet days together; and they are made of sticky rice, so the two will always stick together.

Rolling tangyuan in the morning, a sweet blessing for the couple.

3:00 PM

Vows before the Buddha佛前許願

Family and friends took their seats in the courtyard, and the ceremony began with the sound of the bell.

  1. Entering and bowing. During the Incense Offering Chant (爐香讚), Richard and Dom, dressed in red and gold, walked the red carpet, offered incense, and bowed three times to the Buddha.
  2. Chanting together. Everyone chanted the Heart Sutra, with the Chinese, pinyin, and English on screen so friends from overseas could chant along.
  3. The officiant’s questions. Venerable Yang Hui asked the vows in Chinese and in English, and the couple answered, “I do.”
  4. Personal vows. Richard and Dom each read vows they had written for each other, looking back on the road they have traveled and promising to keep growing and supporting each other.
  5. Rings and certificate. The couple exchanged rings before the officiant, then signed and sealed the Buddhist wedding certificate.
  6. Teaching and blessing. Venerable Yang Hui gave a Dharma talk and blessing (translated into English by Luke Lin) and presented a gift. To close, the couple bowed three times to thank their family and friends.

Top: bowing to the Buddha; personal vows. Bottom: the officiant’s questions; sealing the certificate.

Evening

Song, cake, and a courtyard feast歌聲・素宴

In traditional African attire, Dom sang a traditional song for everyone. Guests turned their chairs to the center of the courtyard and clapped and stepped to the rhythm, and many got up to dance. Then the couple cut a cake topped with the numbers 1314, which in Chinese sounds like “for a lifetime” (一生一世).

At five o’clock, as the light faded, everyone sat down to a vegetarian feast in the courtyard, and the couple went from table to table to thank their guests. From the morning refuge to the evening meal, it was a day full of Dharma joy and laughter.

The wedding feast in the courtyard at dusk.
