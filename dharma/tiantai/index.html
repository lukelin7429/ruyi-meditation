Tiantai — a reading path

Tiantai takes its name from the mountain in Zhejiang where Zhiyi (538–597) taught. It reads the whole of the Buddha's teaching through the Lotus Sūtra, and it insists that doctrine and meditation belong together, like the two wings of a bird.

Every Tiantai text on this site is gathered here and arranged in an order you can follow, from a short beginner's manual to Zhiyi's three great works and the later masters who argued over them. All translations are free to read.

Zhiyi's three great works

Tiantai's foundation is three sets of lectures by Zhiyi, written down by his disciple Guanding. Together they explain the Lotus Sūtra's meaning, its words, and how to practice it. All three are complete on this site, together with Zhiyi's treatise on meditation and Zhanran's commentary on the first.

Zhanran and the Song masters

Later masters

In the eighth century Zhanran revived the school and defended it against Huayan and Chan. In the Song dynasty Zhili defined orthodox Tiantai in a long debate over his works. These shorter texts are where the school's sharpest ideas are argued out.

Zhanran · 1 fascicle · 5 parts · Complete

The Vajra Scalpel 金剛錍

A dream dialogue arguing that even walls, tiles, grasses, and trees have buddha-nature, ending with forty-six questions and advice on how to teach it.

 Zhanran · Complete

A short essay, taken from Zhanran's commentary on the Profound Meaning, showing ten pairs, such as matter and mind or self and other, to be not two.

Tiantai and the Pure Land

Pure Land

Many Tiantai masters were devoted Pure Land practitioners. For them, contemplating Amitābha is contemplating the mind, because the mind already holds the Buddha and his land. Read these with the site's Contemplation Sūtra.

Scriptures Tiantai relies on

Sūtras

Besides the Lotus, Tiantai teachers cite these sūtras constantly. Zhiyi called the Lotus and the Nirvāṇa Sūtra together the teaching of the fifth and final period.

Dharmakṣema · 40 fascicles · Complete

Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra 大般涅槃經

The Buddha's final teaching that nirvāṇa is permanent, blissful, self, and pure, and that every being has buddha-nature. The Vajra Scalpel builds its argument on it.

 Kumārajīva

Vimalakīrti Sūtra 維摩詰所說經

The layman whose silence teaches non-duality. Zhiyi lectured on it at length late in his life.

Home of the story of the bodhisattva Joyful Root, which Tiantai teachers cite when they speak of "forcefully administering the poison."

Vocabulary

Key ideas

A handful of terms run through every Tiantai text. Here they are in brief, with the place on this site where each is worked out.

三諦圓融

The three truths

Every dharma is at once empty, provisionally existing, and the middle. The three are not stages; each one contains the other two.

See: The Great Calming and Contemplation →
一心三觀

Threefold contemplation in one mind

The practice that matches the three truths: contemplating emptiness, the provisional, and the middle in a single moment of mind.

See: The Great Calming and Contemplation →
一念三千

Three thousand in a single thought

Ten realms, each containing all ten, with ten suchnesses and three worlds: a single moment of mind includes all of reality.

See: The Great Calming and Contemplation →
五時八教

Five periods and eight teachings

Tiantai's classification of all the Buddha's sūtras by when, how, and what he taught, with the Lotus as the culmination.

See: Outline of the Teachings and Contemplation →
六即

The six identities

From identity in principle to ultimate identity: beings are buddhas from the start, yet the stages of practice are real and must not be skipped.

See: Outline of the Teachings and Contemplation →
三因佛性

Three causes of buddha-nature

The correct cause (reality itself), the revealing cause (wisdom), and the conditioning cause (practice), all three inherent in every being.

See: The Vajra Scalpel →
性具

Inherent inclusion

The nature already includes all dharmas, defiled and pure alike. This is why, for Tiantai, even the insentient have buddha-nature.

See: The Vajra Scalpel →
本跡

Origin and trace

The Buddha who attained awakening under the tree is the trace. The Buddha awakened since the inconceivably distant past is the origin.

See: Words and Phrases of the Lotus Sūtra →
四種三昧

The four samādhis

Constantly sitting, constantly walking, half walking and half sitting, and neither walking nor sitting: four frames for practice.

See: The Great Calming and Contemplation →
Who wrote what

Lineage

The teachers behind the texts on this page, from the school's founding to the twentieth century.

  1. Huisi 慧思515–577

    Zhiyi's teacher, who guided him to his first awakening while reciting the Lotus Sūtra.

  2. Zhiyi 智顗538–597

    The founder in practice. Author of the Profound Meaning, Words and Phrases, Great Calming and Contemplation, and the Graduated Dharma Gate.

  3. Guanding 灌頂561–632

    Zhiyi's disciple, who recorded and edited his lectures.

  4. Zhanran 湛然711–782

    Revived the school and wrote its standard commentaries. Author of The Vajra Scalpel.

  5. Siming Zhili 四明知禮960–1028

    Defined Song-dynasty orthodoxy and composed the repentance manual behind the Great Compassion Repentance.

  6. Ouyi Zhixu 蕅益智旭1599–1655

    A late-Ming master of Tiantai and Pure Land. Author of the Outline of the Teachings and Contemplation.

  7. Baojing 寶靜1899–1940

    A modern Tiantai lecturer. His talks on Zhiyi's Essentials of Sitting Meditation are on this site.
