Chapter 5 · Vīthimuttasaṅgaha · Complete

What Happens Outside Any Process

This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha's fifth chapter, Vīthimuttasaṅgahavibhāga ("The Compendium of the Process-Freed") — the mirror image of Chapter 4. Where that chapter mapped consciousness inside an active cognitive process, this one maps consciousness at rebirth-linking, in the passive life-continuum, and at death: four planes of existence, four modes of rebirth-linking, sixteen kinds of kamma, the fourfold advent of death, and the chapter's famous passage describing the actual mechanics of dying and being reborn. Translated in full, with the Pāli alongside our English, closing with the chapter's own colophon.

Translation Method

The Pāli source is the Pali Text Society's critical edition of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha, edited by Ven. Hammalawa Saddhātissa, cross-checked against the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana (Sixth Council) romanized text maintained by the Vipassana Research Institute (VRI, tipitaka.org) for internal consistency.

The English translation is Ru-Yi's own, made directly from the Pāli. No existing English translation is consulted, paraphrased, or used as a guide for wording or structure — including Bhikkhu Bodhi's copyrighted A Comprehensive Manual of Abhidhamma, the standard modern edition of this text. That volume's tables for this chapter (5.1–5.7) are, by its own editorial note, not part of Anuruddha's root text; several — including the 31-realms chart and the sense-sphere-heaven life-span table — were contributed by Ven. U Sīlānanda or drawn from Ven. Nārada Mahāthera's earlier Manual. This page builds its own plain summary tables directly from the root text's own numbers, laid out for reference rather than reproduced from any published table design.

Reading notes are grounded only in the chapter's own internal structure and its cross-references to Chapters 1 and 3. Each translated passage also carries a short vocabulary list for readers learning Pāli alongside the text.

From Process to What Lies Outside It

Chapter 4 mapped consciousness inside an active cognitive process. This chapter turns to its mirror image: consciousness at rebirth-linking, and in the passive life-continuum and death that frame every cognitive process from outside.

Pali Source

Vīthicittavasen'evaṃ pavattiyam udīrito
Pavattisangaho nāma sandhiyaṃ dāni vuccati.

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus the compendium of occurrence has been told by way of process consciousness, in the course of existence. Now it will be told at rebirth-linking.

Pali Source

Catasso bhūmiyo, catubbidhā paṭisandhi, cattāri kammāni, catudhā maraṇ'uppatti cā ti vīthimuttasangahe cattāri catukkāni veditabbāni.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the compendium of the process-freed, four sets of four should be understood: the four planes, the fourfold rebirth-linking, the four kinds of kamma, and the fourfold advent of death.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
vīthimuttaadj.

process-freed — vīthi ("process") + mutta ("released"). Not liberation in a spiritual sense; simply consciousness occurring outside an active cognitive process, as rebirth-linking, life-continuum, or death.

catukkant.

a set of four — the chapter's own organizing device, used four times over: this chapter, unusually among the nine, is built almost entirely out of four parallel fourfold schemes.

The Four Planes — Woeful and Blissful

The chapter opens its cosmology with the four broad planes, then immediately subdivides the two sense-sphere planes — woeful and blissful — into their constituent realms.

Pali Source

Tattha apāyabhūmi, kāmasugatibhūmi, rūpāvacarabhūmi, arūpāvacarabhūmi cā ti catasso bhūmiyo nāma.

Tāsu nirayo, tiracchānayoni, pettivisayo, asurakāyo cā ti apāyabhūmi catubbidhā hoti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, the four planes are: the woeful plane, the sensuous blissful plane, the fine-material-sphere plane, and the immaterial-sphere plane.

Among these, the woeful plane is fourfold: hell, the animal kingdom, the sphere of petas, and the host of asuras.

Pali Source

Manussā, cātummahārājikā, tāvatiṃsā, yāmā, tusitā, nimmānarati, paranimmitavasavattī cā ti kāmasugatibhūmi sattavidhā hoti. Sā pan'āyaṃ ekādasavidhā pi kāmāvacarabhūmicc'eva saṅkhaṃ gacchati.

Ru-Yi Translation

The sensuous blissful plane is sevenfold: the human [realm], the Realm of the Four Great Kings, the Realm of the Thirty-Three, the Yāma [realm], Tusita, Nimmānarati, and Paranimmitavasavatti. This — eleven kinds altogether [4 woeful + 7 blissful] — is reckoned as the sense-sphere plane.

Sense-sphere plane — 11 realms
Woeful (apāya) — 4hell, animals, petas, asuras
Blissful (sugati) — 7human, Four Great Kings, Thirty-Three, Yāma, Tusita, Nimmānarati, Paranimmitavasavatti
Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
apāyam.

the woeful plane — apa- ("away from") + aya ("happiness, gain"): literally, "devoid of happiness."

tāvatiṃsā

"the Thirty-Three" — named for a legendary band of thirty-three virtuous men reborn there together as its ruling deities, chief among them Sakka.

paranimmitavasavattī

"those who lord over the creations of others" — the highest sense-sphere heaven, whose gods enjoy pleasures conjured for them by others rather than creating their own.

The Four Planes — Fine-Material and Immaterial

The sixteen fine-material realms are grouped by which jhāna corresponds to rebirth there — including the five Pure Abodes, open only to non-returners — followed by the four immaterial realms and the chapter's first closing verse.

Pali Source

Brahmapārisajjā, brahmapurohitā, mahābrahmā cā ti paṭhamajjhānabhūmi. Parittābhā, appamāṇābhā, ābhassarā cā ti dutiyajjhānabhūmi. Parittasubhā, appamāṇasubhā, subhakiṇhā cā ti tatiyajjhānabhūmi. Vehapphalā, asaññasattā, suddhāvāsā cā ti catutthajjhānabhūmī ti rūpāvacarabhūmi soḷasavidhā hoti.

Avihā, atappā, sudassā, sudassī, akaniṭṭhā cā ti suddhāvāsabhūmi pañcavidhā hoti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Brahmā's Retinue, Brahmā's Ministers, and Mahā Brahmā are the first-jhāna plane. Minor Lustre, Infinite Lustre, and Radiant Lustre are the second-jhāna plane. Minor Aura, Infinite Aura, and Steady Aura are the third-jhāna plane. Great Reward, the non-percipient beings, and the Pure Abodes are the fourth-jhāna plane. Thus the fine-material-sphere plane is sixteenfold.

The Durable, the Serene, the Beautiful, the Clear-Sighted, and the Highest — these are the fivefold Pure Abodes.

Pali Source

Ākāsānañcāyatanabhūmi, viññāṇañcāyatanabhūmi, ākiñcaññāyatanabhūmi, n'evasaññān'āsaññāyatanabhūmi cā ti arūpabhūmi catubbidhā hoti.

Ru-Yi Translation

The realm of infinite space, the realm of infinite consciousness, the realm of nothingness, and the realm of neither-perception-nor-non-perception: thus the immaterial-sphere plane is fourfold.

Pali Source

Puthujjanā na labbhanti suddhāvāsesu sabbathā
Sotāpannā ca sakadāgāmino cā pi puggalā.
Ariyā n'opalabbhanti asaññāpāyabhūmisu
Sesaṭṭhānesu labbhanti ariyā'nariyā pi ca.
Idam ettha bhūmicatukkaṃ.

Ru-Yi Translation

Worldlings are never found in the Pure Abodes at all, nor are stream-enterers or once-returners. Noble ones are not found in the non-percipient realm or the woeful planes. In the remaining places, both noble ones and non-noble ones are found. Herein, this is the fourfold [scheme of] planes.

2nd jhāna — 3Minor Lustre, Infinite Lustre, Radiant Lustre
3rd jhāna — 3Minor Aura, Infinite Aura, Steady Aura
4th jhāna — 3 + 5 Pure AbodesGreat Reward, Non-percipient, + Durable/Serene/Beautiful/Clear-Sighted/Highest

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
suddhāvāsam.

Pure Abode — suddha ("pure") + āvāsa ("dwelling"). Rebirth here is closed to anyone below the third stage of awakening (non-return); once reached, final liberation follows without further descent.

asaññasattam.

non-percipient being — dwells in the fourth-jhāna plane yet, uniquely, without any consciousness at all, for the entire span of that existence.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The fine-material plane compresses five jhānas into four tiers, and the text never explains why — it simply lists three realms per tier and expects the reader to already know, from the Suttanta's four-jhāna scheme, that the Abhidhamma's second and third jhānas (distinguished only by whether vitakka or vicāra has dropped) collapse into a single Suttanta "second jhāna." The realm structure is following the coarser, older fourfold count even though Chapter 1 committed this whole work to the finer fivefold one — a small but real seam between two systems the text never bothers to smooth over.

The Four Modes of Rebirth-Linking — Woeful and Sense-Sphere

Rebirth-linking itself now divides fourfold by plane, with the sense-sphere modes worked out in full detail, down to which specific citta performs the role in each case.

Pali Source

Apāyapaṭisandhi, kāmasugatipaṭisandhi, rūpāvacarapaṭisandhi, arūpāvacarapaṭisandhi cā ti catubbidhā hoti paṭisandhi nāma.

Tattha akusalavipāk'opekkhāsahagata-santīraṇaṃ apāyabhūmiyaṃ okkantikkhaṇe paṭisandhi hutvā tato paraṃ bhavangaṃ pariyosāne cavanaṃ hutvā vocchijjati. Ayam ekā v'āpāyapaṭisandhi nāma.

Ru-Yi Translation

Rebirth-linking is fourfold: rebirth-linking in the woeful plane, in the sensuous blissful plane, in the fine-material sphere, and in the immaterial sphere.

Herein, the unwholesome-resultant investigating citta accompanied by equanimity, at the moment of descent into the woeful plane, becomes the rebirth-linking; thereafter, [becoming] the life-continuum, at the end it becomes the death [consciousness] and is cut off. This alone is the single woeful rebirth-linking.

Pali Source

Kusalavipāk'opekkhāsahagata-santīraṇaṃ pana kāmasugatiyaṃ manussānañ c'eva jaccandhādihīnasattānaṃ bhummassitānañ ca vinipātikāsurānaṃ paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cutivasena pavattati.

Mahāvipākāni pan'aṭṭha sabbatthā pi kāmasugatiyaṃ paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cutivasena pavattanti. Imā nava kāmasugatipaṭisandhiyo nāma. Sā pan'āyaṃ dasavidhā pi kāmāvacarapaṭisandhicc'eva saṅkhaṃ gacchati.

Ru-Yi Translation

The wholesome-resultant investigating citta accompanied by equanimity occurs as rebirth-linking, life-continuum, and death for congenitally blind and similarly deficient humans in the sensuous blissful plane, and for [certain] earth-bound [spirits] and fallen asuras.

The eight great resultants occur as rebirth-linking, life-continuum, and death everywhere in the sensuous blissful plane. These nine are the sensuous-blissful rebirth-linkings. This — tenfold altogether [1 woeful + 9 blissful] — is reckoned as sense-sphere rebirth-linking.

Pali Source

Cātummahārājikānaṃ pana devānaṃ dibbāni pañcavassasatāni āyuppamāṇaṃ. Manussagaṇanāya navutivassasatasahassappamāṇaṃ hoti. Tato catugguṇaṃ tāvatiṃsānaṃ, tato catugguṇaṃ yāmānaṃ, tato catugguṇaṃ tusitānaṃ, tato catugguṇaṃ nimmānaratīnaṃ, tato catugguṇaṃ paranimmitavasavattīnaṃ devānaṃ āyuppamāṇaṃ.

Ru-Yi Translation

The life-span of the gods of the Four Great Kings is five hundred celestial years — by human reckoning, nine million years. Four times that is the life-span of the Thirty-Three; four times that again, the Yāma gods; four times that, Tusita; four times that, Nimmānarati; four times that, Paranimmitavasavatti.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
okkantikkhaṇa

"the moment of descent" — okkanti ("descent, entry") + khaṇa ("moment"). A poetic term for the instant of conception, when rebirth-linking consciousness first establishes itself.

vinipātikāsura

"fallen asura" — a woeful-plane class distinct from the asuras who contend with the Thirty-Three gods; these live near human villages, scavenging discarded food.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Only two cittas ever perform woeful rebirth-linking, and both are already familiar from Chapter 1: the unwholesome-resultant and wholesome-resultant investigating cittas accompanied by equanimity — the very same rootless resultants Chapter 3's Compendium of Functions listed as capable of the rebirth/life-continuum/death role. What this section adds is not a new citta but a new fact about an old one: which specific beings get which of the two. A congenitally blind or deformed human being, on this account, is not reborn human by a different mechanism than an animal or hell-being — the same weak, rootless resultant citta is doing the work, only now ripening from wholesome rather than unwholesome kamma.

The Four Modes of Rebirth-Linking — Sublime

Fine-material and immaterial rebirth-linking follow the jhāna a meditator possessed at death, correlated one-to-one with the corresponding resultant citta and realm — closing with the chapter's second summary verse.

Pali Source

Paṭhamajjhānavipākaṃ paṭhamajjhāna bhūmiyaṃ paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cutivasena pavattati. Tathā dutiyajjhānavipākaṃ tatiyajjhānavipākañ ca dutiyajjhānabhūmiyaṃ, catutthajjhānavipākaṃ tatiyajjhānabhūmiyaṃ, pañcamajjhānavipākaṃ catutthajjhānabhūmiyaṃ. Asaññasattānaṃ pana rūpam eva paṭisandhi hoti. Tathā tato paraṃ pavattiyaṃ cavanakāle ca rūpam eva pavattitvā nirujjhati. Imā cha rūpāvacarapaṭisandhiyo nāma.

Paṭham'āruppādivipākāni paṭhamāruppādibhūmisu yathākkamaṃ paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cutivasena pavattanti. Imā catasso āruppapaṭisandhiyo nāma.

Ru-Yi Translation

The first-jhāna resultant occurs, as rebirth-linking, life-continuum, and death, in the first-jhāna plane. So too the second-jhāna resultant and the third-jhāna resultant, in the second-jhāna plane; the fourth-jhāna resultant, in the third-jhāna plane; the fifth-jhāna resultant, in the fourth-jhāna plane. For non-percipient beings, however, materiality itself occurs as rebirth-linking; so too, during the course of existence and at the moment of death, only materiality occurs and ceases. These are the six fine-material rebirth-linkings.

The first-immaterial resultant, and so on, occur, in due order, as rebirth-linking, life-continuum, and death in the first-immaterial plane and so on. These are the four immaterial rebirth-linkings.

Pali Source

Tesaṃ pana ākāsānañcāyatanūpagānaṃ devānaṃ vīsati kappasahassāni āyuppamāṇaṃ. Viññāṇañcāyatanūpagānaṃ devānaṃ cattāḷīsa kappasahassāni, ākiñcaññāyatanūpagānaṃ devānaṃ saṭṭhi kappasahassāni, n'evasaññānāsaññāyatanūpagānaṃ devānaṃ caturāsīti kappasahassāni āyuppamāṇaṃ.

Ru-Yi Translation

The life-span of the gods who have reached the base of infinite space is twenty thousand aeons; of those who have reached infinite consciousness, forty thousand aeons; of those who have reached nothingness, sixty thousand aeons; of those who have reached neither-perception-nor-non-perception, eighty-four thousand aeons.

Pali Source

Paṭisandhi bhavangañ ca tathā cavanamānasaṃ
Ekam eva tath'ev'ekavisayañ c'ekajātiyaṃ.
Idam ettha paṭisandhicatukkaṃ.

Ru-Yi Translation

The rebirth-linking, the life-continuum, and likewise the death-consciousness in a single birth: these are one [and the same citta], and likewise of one object. Herein, this is the fourfold rebirth-linking.

Immaterial realmLife-span (aeons)
Infinite space20,000
Infinite consciousness40,000
Nothingness60,000
Neither-perception-nor-non-perception84,000
Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
kappam.

aeon — an immense cosmic time-span; the tradition distinguishes an "interim aeon," twenty of which make an "incalculable aeon," four of which make a "great aeon," the figure typically meant in these life-span verses from the second jhāna plane upward.

ekavisaya

"of one object" — the closing verse's own compressed reminder that rebirth-linking, bhavaṅga, and death all share not only one citta-type but literally the very same object throughout an entire existence.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Non-percipient beings get a strange, single-clause exception buried in the middle of an otherwise perfectly regular one-jhāna-to-one-realm correlation: "materiality itself occurs as rebirth-linking." Every other fine-material and immaterial rebirth-linking in this section is a resultant citta; here alone, rebirth-linking is not a citta at all, but bare matter — the vital nonad mentioned later in the chapter — because for the entire span of that existence, consciousness is simply absent. It is the one place in this whole 89/121-citta system where the answer to "which citta performs this function" is: none.

The Four Kinds of Kamma — By Function and By Priority

The chapter's third "four" is really four fours: sixteen kinds of kamma in all, sorted first by the function a kamma performs, then by which kamma takes priority in generating the next rebirth.

Pali Source

Janakaṃ, upatthambakaṃ, upapīḷakaṃ, upaghātakañ cā ti kiccavasena.

Ru-Yi Translation

By way of function: productive, supportive, obstructive, and destructive.

Pali Source

Garukaṃ, āsannaṃ, āciṇṇaṃ, kaṭattā kammañ cā ti pākadānapariyāyena.

Ru-Yi Translation

By way of the manner of giving result [priority in ripening]: weighty, death-proximate, habitual, and reserve kamma.

By function (kicca)By priority (pākadāna)
Productive (janaka)Weighty (garuka)
Supportive (upatthambaka)Death-proximate (āsanna)
Obstructive (upapīḷaka)Habitual (āciṇṇa)
Destructive (upaghātaka)Reserve (kaṭattā)
Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
janakaadj.

productive — the only kind of kamma able to generate a new rebirth-linking; every one of the sixteen types below still falls under one function or another, but only a "full course of action" ever produces janaka-kamma's specific effect.

garukaadj.

weighty — on the wholesome side, the jhānas; on the unwholesome side, the five "immediate" heinous crimes (killing one's mother or father, killing an Arahant, wounding a Buddha, or causing a schism in the Saṅgha) together with a fixed wrong view denying kamma altogether.

āsannaadj.

death-proximate — kamma performed or vividly recalled immediately before the final javana process; the traditional basis for the custom of reminding a dying person of their good deeds.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

These two fours answer two different questions that are easy to conflate: "what can this kamma do?" (function) versus "which kamma wins, when several are competing to determine the very next rebirth?" (priority). A single act can shift between every one of the four functions depending on circumstance — a killing that never gets the chance to generate rebirth can still obstruct or support other kamma in the meantime — while the four priority-kammas form a strict pecking order: weighty pre-empts death-proximate, which pre-empts habitual, which pre-empts reserve, only if nothing higher in the order is present.

The Four Kinds of Kamma — By Time and By Place

The third and fourth fours sort kamma by when it can ripen, then by which plane of existence its result belongs to — closing the whole kammacatukka with a summary verse.

Pali Source

Diṭṭhadhammavedanīyaṃ, upapajjavedanīyaṃ, aparāpariyavedanīyaṃ, ahosikammañ cā ti pākakālavasena cattāri kammāni nāma.

Ru-Yi Translation

By way of the time of giving result: immediately-effective, subsequently-effective, indefinitely-effective, and defunct kamma — these four are called kamma [by time].

Pali Source

Tathā akusalaṃ, kāmāvacarakusalaṃ, rūpāvacarakusalaṃ, arūpāvacarakusalañ cā ti pākaṭṭhānavasena.

Ru-Yi Translation

Likewise, by way of the place of giving result: unwholesome, sense-sphere wholesome, fine-material-sphere wholesome, and immaterial-sphere wholesome.

Pali Source

[Closing on the sublime wholesome kammas:] ... Janeti sadisaṃ pākaṃ paṭisandhippavattiyaṃ. Idam ettha kammacatukkaṃ.

Ru-Yi Translation

... produces, [each] according to plane, a like result at rebirth-linking and in the course of existence. Herein, this is the fourfold kamma.

By time (pākakāla)By place (pākaṭṭhāna)
Immediately-effective (diṭṭhadhammavedanīya)Unwholesome (akusala)
Subsequently-effective (upapajjavedanīya)Sense-sphere wholesome
Indefinitely-effective (aparāpariyavedanīya)Fine-material wholesome
Defunct (ahosi)Immaterial wholesome
Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
diṭṭhadhammavedanīya

"to be experienced in this very life" — generated by the very first of a javana process's seven moments, the weakest of the series; if it finds no opportunity to ripen in this life, it lapses to ahosi, defunct.

aparāpariyavedanīya

"to be experienced indefinitely" — generated by the five middle javana moments, the strongest of the seven; unlike the other three time-categories, this kind of kamma never becomes defunct for as long as saṃsāra continues — not even a Buddha or Arahant is exempt from it.

ahosi-kamma

"has-been kamma" — not a distinct fourth type of volition, but simply what immediately-effective or subsequently-effective kamma becomes once its one window to ripen has closed unused.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The "by time" scheme quietly assigns a moral weight to each of the seven javana moments that nothing earlier in the text states outright: the first moment (weakest) generates only this-life kamma, the last moment (second-weakest) generates only next-life kamma, and the five moments in between — the strongest of the run — generate kamma that can ripen at any future point and never expires. Intensity of habituation, not any special virtue or vice, is what makes a volition inescapable; the middle of a repeated sequence is simply where force accumulates most.

The Fourfold Advent of Death

The chapter now turns to death itself, formally defined as the cutting-off of the life faculty, and distinguishes the four circumstances under which it arrives.

Pali Source

Āyukkhayena, kammakkhayena, ubhayakkhayena, upacchedakakammunā cā ti catudhā maraṇ'uppatti nāma.

Ru-Yi Translation

Through the expiry of the life-span, through the expiry of the kammic force, through the expiry of both together, and through a severing kamma: thus the advent of death is fourfold.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
āyukkhaya / kammakkhaya

expiry of life-span / expiry of kammic force — two independent limits on a life, either of which can end it first; when the life-span runs out before the rebirth-generating kamma does, that kamma may generate a further rebirth on the same or a higher plane.

upacchedaka-kamma

severing kamma — a destructive kamma powerful enough to end a life before either the natural life-span or the sustaining kammic force has run out; the tradition's term for what would ordinarily be called an untimely death.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The first three causes are traditionally grouped as "timely death," the fourth alone as "untimely" — and the difference maps exactly onto the kammacatukka's function-based scheme two sections back: the first three are simply what happens when productive (janaka) kamma runs its natural course to the end of one or both of its two limits, while the fourth is destructive (upaghātaka) kamma doing precisely the job that section already defined it by — cutting off another kamma's result before its time.

The Process of Death and Rebirth

The chapter's most celebrated passage: a single continuous description of what a dying mind actually apprehends, and how that apprehension carries straight through into the next existence.

Pali Source

Tathā ca marantānaṃ pana maraṇakāle yathārahaṃ abhimukhībhūtaṃ bhavantare paṭisandhijanakaṃ kammaṃ vā taṃkammakaraṇakāle rūpādikam upaladdhapubbam upakaraṇabhūtañ ca kammanimittaṃ vā anantaram uppajjamānabhave upalabhitabbaṃ upabhogabhūtañ ca gatinimittaṃ vā kammabalena channaṃ dvārānaṃ aññatarasmiṃ paccupaṭṭhāti.

Ru-Yi Translation

And so, for those who are dying, at the time of death there presents itself, according to circumstance, through one or another of the six doors, by the power of kamma: either the kamma that is to produce rebirth-linking in the next existence; or a sign of kamma — a visible form or the like, formerly apprehended, that served as the instrument in the performance of that kamma; or a sign of destiny — [something] to be obtained and made use of in the immediately arising existence.

Pali Source

Tato paraṃ tam eva tath'opaṭṭhitaṃ ālambanaṃ ārabbha vipaccamānakakammānurūpaṃ parisuddham upakkiliṭṭhaṃ vā upalabhitabbabhavānurūpaṃ tatth'onataṃ va cittasantānaṃ abhiṇhaṃ pavattati bāhullena. Tam eva vā pana janakabhūtaṃ kammam abhinavakaraṇavasena dvārappattaṃ hoti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Thereafter, taking up that very object thus presented, the stream of consciousness flows on, again and again, mostly inclining toward it, in accordance with the kamma that is ripening — whether pure or defiled — and in conformity with the existence to be obtained. Or that very productive kamma reaches the door in the manner of being freshly done.

Pali Source

Paccāsannamaraṇassa tassa vīthicittāvasāne bhavangakkhaye vā cavanavasena paccuppannabhavapariyosānabhūtaṃ cuticittam uppajjitvā nirujjhati.

Tasmiṃ niruddhāvasāne tass'ānantaram eva tathāgahitaṃ ālambanam ārabbha savatthukaṃ avatthukam eva vā yathārahaṃ avijjānusayaparikkhittena taṇhānusayamūlakena sankhārena janīyamānaṃ sampayuttehi pariggayhamānaṃ sahajātānam adhiṭṭhānabhāvena pubbangamabhūtaṃ bhavantarapaṭisandhānavasena paṭisandhisankhātaṃ mānasaṃ uppajjamānam eva patiṭṭhāti bhavantare.

Ru-Yi Translation

For one thus close to death, at the end of a cognitive process, or at the dissolution of the life-continuum, the death consciousness — the completion of the present existence — arises in the manner of passing away, and ceases.

Immediately after it has ceased, taking up that very object so apprehended, supported by a base or baseless as the case may be, generated by a volitional formation rooted in the latent tendency of craving and enveloped by the latent tendency of ignorance, seized upon by its associated states, forerunning the co-arisen states as their support — the mind called rebirth-linking, linking the two existences together, arises and becomes established, just as it arises, in the new existence.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
kamma / kammanimitta / gatinimitta

kamma, sign of kamma, sign of destiny — already introduced in Chapter 3, here shown in their actual operation as the three possible objects the dying mind's javana process apprehends and hands forward.

abhinavakaraṇa

"in the manner of being freshly done" — a striking detail: a kamma re-presenting itself at death does not appear as a memory of something past, but as though it were happening for the very first time, right now.

avijjānusaya / taṇhānusaya

the latent tendency of ignorance / of craving — the "twin roots of the round of existence" the text names as generating every rebirth-linking without exception, functioning here as its efficient cause.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Notice what does the actual linking: not a self, not a soul crossing over, but an object. The last javana process of the old life apprehends some kamma, sign of kamma, or sign of destiny; the death consciousness inherits that same object; and the rebirth-linking consciousness of the new existence inherits it in turn, continuous in content though nothing substantial has crossed the gap. The text's own later summary states this even more starkly: rebirth-linking, life-continuum, and death consciousness within one life are said to be "of one object" (ekavisaya) — and now this passage shows that even the boundary between two entirely separate lives is bridged by that same continuity of object, not by any traveling entity.

Objects at the Threshold, and the Determination of Rebirth

The chapter specifies exactly what kind of object — present or past, and through which door — each plane's rebirth-linking can take, then states the rule governing where a being who dies with a given rebirth-consciousness can and cannot be reborn.

Pali Source

Rūpāvacarapaṭisandhiyā pana paññattibhūtaṃ kammanimittam ev'ālambanaṃ hoti. Tathā āruppapaṭisandhiyā ca mahaggatabhūtaṃ paññattibhūtañ ca kammanimittam eva yathārahaṃ ālambanaṃ hoti.

Asaññasattānaṃ pana jīvitanavakam eva paṭisandhibhāvena patiṭṭhāti. Tasmā te rūpapaṭisandhikā nāma. Arūpā arūpapaṭisandhikā. Sesā rūpārūpapaṭisandhikā.

Ru-Yi Translation

For fine-material-sphere rebirth-linking, the object is only a sign of kamma that has become a concept. Likewise, for immaterial rebirth-linking, the object is only a sign of kamma — a sublime state or a concept, as fits the case.

For non-percipient beings, only the vital nonad becomes established as rebirth-linking. Hence they are called "reborn with matter [alone]." Immaterial beings are called "reborn with mind [alone]." The rest are called "reborn with both matter and mind."

Pali Source

Āruppacutiyā honti heṭṭhimāruppavajjitā
Paramāruppasandhī ca tathā kāme tihetukā.
Rūpāvacaracutiyā aheturahitā siyuṃ
Sabbā kāmatihetumhā kāmesv'eva pan'etarā.
Ayam ettha cutipaṭisandhikkamo.

Ru-Yi Translation

From an immaterial death, [rebirth occurs] excluding the lower immaterial [realms], including the highest immaterial rebirth-linking, and likewise [rebirth] in the sense sphere with three roots. From a fine-material death, [rebirth occurs] excluding the rootless. From all three-rooted [deaths] in the sense sphere, [rebirth may occur anywhere]; but the rest [two-rooted or rootless deaths], only within the sense sphere. Herein, this is the procedure of death and rebirth-linking.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
jīvitanavakant.

the "vital nonad" — a group of nine material phenomena including the life faculty; for non-percipient beings, this bare cluster of matter itself performs the role rebirth-consciousness plays everywhere else.

rūpapaṭisandhika / arūpapaṭisandhika / rūpārūpapaṭisandhika

reborn with matter only / mind only / both — a threefold classification of all rebirth cutting cleanly across the ordinary plane divisions: non-percipient beings (matter only), immaterial-plane beings (mind only), everyone else (both).

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The closing rule is a one-way ratchet with a single narrow exception. A three-rooted sense-sphere death can lead anywhere — sense sphere, fine-material, or immaterial — because a three-rooted being is capable of any kind of kamma, wholesome or otherwise. But descent only ever runs in the direction of fewer roots, never the reverse within a single transition: a fine-material death cannot produce a rootless rebirth, and an immaterial death cannot regress to a lower immaterial realm, though it can still step all the way down to the sense sphere. What looks at first like an arbitrary asymmetry is simply the same fact stated twice: rootedness at death constrains rootedness at rebirth, but never the other way around.

The Wheel Turns: Vīthimuttasaṅgaha Complete

Having covered the mechanics, the chapter closes by describing the unbroken cycle these mechanics sustain — a single citta type flowing on as bhavaṅga between processes, then the whole sequence of rebirth-linking, life, and death beginning again, "like the wheel of a cart."

Pali Source

Icc'evaṃ gahitapaṭisandhikānaṃ pana paṭisandhinirodhānantarato pabhuti tam ev'ālambanam ārabbha tad eva cittaṃ yāva cuticittuppādā asati vīthicittuppāde bhavass'angabhāvena bhavangasantatisankhātaṃ mānasaṃ abbocchinnaṃ nadīsoto viya pavattati. Pariyosāne ca cavanavasena cuticittaṃ hutvā nirujjhati. Tato parañ ca paṭisandhādayo rathacakkam iva yathākkamaṃ eva parivattantā pavattanti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus, for those who have taken rebirth-linking in this way, from immediately after the cessation of the rebirth-linking [citta] onward, that same citta, taking up that same object — being, as a factor of existence, called the continuity of the life-continuum — flows on unbroken like the current of a river, for as long as no cognitive process occurs, until the arising of the death consciousness. At the end, in the manner of passing away, it becomes the death consciousness and ceases. Thereafter, rebirth-linking and the rest occur again, revolving in due order, like the wheel of a cart.

Pali Source

Paṭisandhibhavangavīthiyo
Cuti c'eha tathā bhavantare
Puna sandhibhavangam icc'ayaṃ
Parivattati cittasantati.

Paṭisankhāya pan'etam addhuvaṃ
Adhigantvā padam accutaṃ budhā
Susamucchinnasinehabandhanā
Samam essanti cirāya subbatā.

Iti Abhidhammatthasaṅgahe Vīthimuttasaṅgahavibhāgo nāma pañcamo paricchedo.

Ru-Yi Translation

Rebirth-linking, life-continuum, cognitive processes, and death — thus here, and likewise in the next existence, again rebirth-linking and life-continuum: this stream of consciousness keeps turning.

But having reflected on this as unstable, and having reached the undying state, the wise — their bonds of craving thoroughly cut off — long disciplined in virtue, attain peace.

Thus ends, in the Compendium of the Things Contained in the Abhidhamma, the fifth chapter, called the Compendium of the Process-Freed.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
nadīsoto

"like the current of a river" — the text's own simile for the bhavaṅga stream: not a single enduring thing, but an unbroken succession of momentary cittas, each replacing the last.

rathacakkant.

the wheel of a cart — the chapter's closing image for saṃsāra itself: rebirth-linking, life-continuum, process, and death revolving in fixed sequence, life after life.

accutaadj.

"undying, unfalling" — a- (not) + cuta (past participle of cavati, "to fall away, die"); a poetic epithet for Nibbāna, the one state the wheel described throughout this chapter does not turn through.

This closes the translation of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha's fifth chapter, Vīthimuttasaṅgaha ("The Compendium of the Process-Freed") — the four planes of existence and their thirty-one realms, the four modes of rebirth-linking, the sixteen kinds of kamma, the fourfold advent of death, the celebrated passage on the actual process of dying and being reborn, and the determination of where rebirth can and cannot lead, closing with the chapter's own colophon and its two verses on impermanence and the undying state. The sixth chapter, Rūpasaṅgaha — the compendium of matter, the twenty-eight material phenomena that make up the physical side of existence — continues from here, the next undertaking for this translation.
