Chapter 3 · Pakiṇṇakasaṅgaha · Complete

Six More Ways to Cut the Same 89

This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha's chapter 3, Pakiṇṇakasaṅgahavibhāga ("The Compendium of the Miscellaneous") — the same 89 (121) cittas from Chapter 1, now re-sorted six further ways: by feeling, by root, by function, by door, by object, and by physical base. Each of the six compendiums is translated in full, with the Pāli alongside our English, closing with the chapter's own colophon.

Status: chapter 3 complete, all six compendiums Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: PTS (Saddhātissa) edition, cross-checked VRI/CST Author: Ācariya Anuruddha, date uncertain (c. 8th–12th c. CE)

Translation Method

The Pāli source is the Pali Text Society's critical edition of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha, edited by Ven. Hammalawa Saddhātissa, cross-checked against the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana (Sixth Council) romanized text maintained by the Vipassana Research Institute (VRI, tipitaka.org) for internal consistency.

The English translation is Ru-Yi's own, made directly from the Pāli. No existing English translation is consulted, paraphrased, or used as a guide for wording or structure — including Bhikkhu Bodhi's copyrighted A Comprehensive Manual of Abhidhamma, the standard modern edition of this text. That volume's eight numbered charts for this chapter (Tables 3.1–3.8) are, by its own editorial note, not part of Anuruddha's root text — most were contributed by Ven. U Sīlānanda for that specific edition. This page accordingly builds no equivalent tables of its own; every count below comes directly from the root text's own verses and prose.

Reading notes are grounded only in the chapter's own internal structure and its cross-references back to Chapter 1 (which this chapter constantly presupposes), and in classifications that are standard, uncontested Abhidhamma doctrine across the tradition. Each translated passage also carries a short vocabulary list for readers learning Pāli alongside the text.

One Reality, Six Angles

Having analyzed consciousness and its 52 factors by their own intrinsic natures in Chapters 1–2, the author now announces a change of method: taking consciousness alone (with its cetasikas understood to travel with it), he will re-sort the same 89 cittas by six further principles.

Pali Source

Sampayuttā yathāyogaṃ tepaṇṇāsa sabhāvato
Cittacetasikā dhammā tesaṃ dāni yathārahaṃ.
Vedanā-hetuto kicca-dvār'ālambana-vatthuto
Cittuppādavasen'eva sangaho nāma nīyate.

Ru-Yi Translation

Having thus explained, each in its fitting way, the fifty-three associated states — consciousness and mental factors — by way of their intrinsic nature, now their combination is set forth by way of feeling, root, function, door, object, and base, taking consciousness alone as the basis.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
tepaṇṇāsa

fifty-three — the 89 (or 121) cittas are here treated as one single "state" (since every citta shares the one characteristic of cognizing an object), plus the 52 distinct cetasikas: 1 + 52 = 53.

pakiṇṇakaadj.

miscellaneous, scattered — the chapter's own title-word, already met in Chapter 2 for the six "occasional" cetasikas; here it names a chapter of loose, cross-cutting classification schemes rather than a single unified topic.

cittuppādavasena

"by way of the arising of consciousness" — the chapter's stated method: analysis proceeds citta by citta, even though every citta still carries its full retinue of cetasikas along with it.

The Compendium of Feeling

The first compendium re-sorts all 89 cittas by their accompanying feeling — first the coarse threefold scheme, then the more precise fivefold one already used throughout Chapter 1.

Pali Source

Tattha vedanāsangahe tāva tividhā vedanā: sukhā, dukkhā, adukkhamasukhā cā ti. Sukhaṃ, dukkhaṃ, somanassaṃ, domanassaṃ, upekkhā ti ca bhedena pana pañcadhā hoti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, in the compendium of feeling, first, feeling is threefold: pleasant, painful, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant. By further division it is fivefold: pleasure, pain, joy, displeasure, and equanimity.

Pali Source

Tattha sukhasahagataṃ kusalavipākaṃ kāyaviññāṇaṃ ekam eva. Tathā dukkhasahagataṃ akusalavipākaṃ kāyaviññāṇaṃ.

Somanassasahagatacittāni pana lobhamūlāni cattāri, dvādasa kāmāvacarasobhanāni, sukhasantīraṇa-hasanāni ca dve ti aṭṭhārasa kāmāvacaracittāni c'eva paṭhama-dutiya-tatiya-catutthajjhāna-saṅkhātāni catucattāḷīsa mahaggata-lokuttaracittāni cā ti dvāsaṭṭhividhāni bhavanti.

Domanassasahagatacittāni pana dve paṭighasampayuttacittān'eva. Sesāni sabbāni pi pañcapaññāsa upekkhāsahagatacittān'evā ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, wholesome-resultant body-consciousness alone is accompanied by pleasure. Likewise, unwholesome-resultant body-consciousness alone is accompanied by pain.

The cittas accompanied by joy number sixty-two: eighteen sense-sphere cittas — four rooted in greed, twelve sense-sphere beautiful, and two [rootless: joyful investigating and smiling] — together with forty-four sublime and supramundane cittas reckoned at the first, second, third, and fourth jhānas.

The cittas accompanied by displeasure are only the two associated with resistance. All the rest — fifty-five in all — are accompanied by equanimity.

Pali Source

Sukhaṃ dukkhaṃ upekkhā ti tividhā tattha vedanā
Somanassaṃ domanassam iti bhedena pañcadhā.
Sukham ekattha dukkhañ ca domanassaṃ dvaye ṭhitaṃ
Dvāsaṭṭhīsu somanassaṃ pañcapaññāsake'tarā.

Ru-Yi Translation

Feeling, therein, is threefold: pleasure, pain, and equanimity; by further division, with joy and displeasure, it is fivefold. Pleasure and pain each stand in one; displeasure, in two; joy, in sixty-two; the rest [equanimity], in fifty-five.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
sukha / dukkha (as vedanā)

here narrowly bodily pleasure and pain — distinct from the broader Buddhist sense of dukkha as the unsatisfactoriness of all conditioned existence, which the chapter's own Guide-layer discussion is careful to separate.

somanassa / domanassa

joy and displeasure — the mental counterparts of sukha/dukkha, already familiar from Chapter 1's greed- and hatred-rooted cittas.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Only body-consciousness among the five pairs of sense-consciousness ever carries a feeling other than equanimity — and this table is the reason why the count of 121 cittas here exceeds 89: 1 + 1 + 62 + 2 + 55 = 121, using the finer jhāna-graded reckoning for the sublime and supramundane cittas that Chapter 1 closed with. Read against Chapter 1's own citta-by-citta enumeration, this compendium is simply that same information filed under a different heading — proof that the "89-versus-121" question from the end of Chapter 1 was never a special case, but the chapter's standing convention whenever jhāna level actually matters to the count.

The Compendium of Roots

The second compendium sorts consciousness by its concomitant hetu, "root" — the factor that gives a citta firmness and stability, as a root gives a tree.

Pali Source

Hetusangahe hetū nāma lobho doso moho alobho adoso amoho cā ti chabbidhā bhavanti.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the compendium of roots, the roots are sixfold: greed, hatred, delusion, non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion.

Pali Source

Tattha pañcadvārāvajjana-dvipañcaviññāṇa-sampaṭicchana-santīraṇa-votthapana-hasana-vasena aṭṭhārasa ahetukacittāni nāma. Sesāni sabbāni pi ekasattati cittāni sahetukān'eva.

Tatthā pi dve momūhacittāni ekahetukāni. Sesāni dasa akusalacittāni c'eva ñāṇavippayuttāni dvādasa kāmāvacarasobhanāni cā ti dvāvīsati dvihetukacittāni.

Dvādasa ñāṇasampayutta-kāmāvacarasobhanāni c'eva pañcatiṃsa mahaggata-lokuttaracittāni cā ti sattacattāḷīsa tihetukacittāni.

Ru-Yi Translation

Herein, by way of five-door-adverting, the two sets of fivefold sense-consciousness, receiving, investigating, determining, and smiling, eighteen cittas are called rootless. All the remaining seventy-one cittas are with roots.

Among these, the two cittas of sheer delusion have one root. The remaining ten unwholesome cittas together with the twelve sense-sphere beautiful cittas dissociated from knowledge — twenty-two in all — have two roots.

The twelve sense-sphere beautiful cittas associated with knowledge, together with the thirty-five sublime and supramundane cittas — forty-seven in all — have three roots.

Pali Source

Lobho doso ca moho ca hetū akusalā tayo
Alobhādosāmoho ca kusalābyākatā tathā.
Ahetuk'aṭṭhāras'ekahetukā dve dvāvīsati
Dvihetukā matā sattacattāḷīsa tihetukā.

Ru-Yi Translation

Greed, hatred, and delusion are the three unwholesome roots; non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion, likewise, are wholesome and indeterminate. Rootless are eighteen; with one root, two; with two roots, twenty-two, it is held; with three roots, forty-seven.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
hetu / mūla

root — a mental factor that "establishes firmness," in the Abhidhamma sense strictly narrower than the Suttas' general use of hetu for any cause (kāraṇa).

tihetukaadj.

"three-rooted" — every sublime and supramundane citta, and every knowledge-associated sense-sphere beautiful citta, qualifies; a citta needs all three of non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion together to reach this grade.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The eighteen rootless cittas here are the exact same eighteen from Chapter 1, now viewed from a different angle — and Chapter 2's association section already told us why: the seven universal cetasikas are the only mental factors these eighteen cittas ever carry, and none of the six roots is among the universals. Rootlessness, in other words, was never a separate fact about these eighteen cittas — it falls straight out of which cetasikas Chapter 2 already showed they can and cannot hold.

The Compendium of Functions

The third compendium — the chapter's longest — classifies every citta by the specific task (kicca) it performs, whether within an active cognitive process or during the passive, process-freed phases of existence.

Pali Source

Kiccasangahe kiccāni nāma paṭisandhi-bhavanga-āvajjana-dassana-savana-ghāyana-sāyana-phusana-sampaṭicchana-santīraṇa-votthapana-javana-tadārammaṇa-cutivasena cuddasavidhāni bhavanti.

Paṭisandhi-bhavanga-āvajjana-pañcaviññāṇa-ṭṭhānādivasena pana tesaṃ dasadhā ṭhānabhedo veditabbo.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the compendium of functions, there are fourteen functions: rebirth-linking, life-continuum, adverting, seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching, receiving, investigating, determining, javana, registration, and dying.

Their division by stage, however, should be understood as tenfold: rebirth-linking, life-continuum, adverting, the fivefold sense-consciousness [as a single stage], and so on.

Pali Source

Tattha dve upekkhāsahagatasantīraṇāni c'eva aṭṭha mahāvipākāni ca nava rūpārūpavipākāni cā ti ekūnavīsati cittāni paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cutikiccāni nāma.

Āvajjanakiccāni pana dve. Tathā dassana-savana-ghāyana-sāyana-phusana-sampaṭicchanakiccāni ca.

Tīṇi santīraṇakiccāni. Manodvārāvajjanam eva pañcadvāre votthapanakiccaṃ sādheti.

Āvajjanadvayavajjitāni kusalākusala-phala-kriyā cittāni pañcapaññāsa javanakiccāni.

Aṭṭha mahāvipākāni c'eva santīraṇattayañ cā ti ekādasa tadārammaṇakiccāni.

Ru-Yi Translation

Herein, nineteen cittas — the two equanimity-accompanied investigating cittas, the eight great resultants, and the nine fine-material- and immaterial-sphere resultants — perform the functions of rebirth-linking, life-continuum, and dying.

Two perform the function of adverting. Likewise, two each perform the functions of seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching, and receiving.

Three perform the function of investigating. The mind-door-adverting citta alone accomplishes the function of determining in the five [sense] doors.

Excluding the two adverting cittas, the fifty-five wholesome, unwholesome, resultant [fruition], and functional cittas perform the function of javana.

The eight great resultants together with the three investigating cittas — eleven in all — perform the function of registration.

Pali Source

Tesu pana dve upekkhāsahagatasantīraṇacittāni paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cuti-tadārammaṇa-santīraṇavasena pañcakiccāni nāma.

Mahāvipākāni aṭṭha paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cuti-tadārammaṇavasena catukiccāni.

Mahaggatavipākāni nava paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cutivasena tikiccāni.

Somanassasahagataṃ santīraṇaṃ santīraṇa-tadārammaṇavasena dukiccaṃ. Tathā votthapanañ ca votthapanāvajjanavasena.

Sesāni pana sabbāni pi javana-manodhātuttika-dvipañcaviññāṇāni yathāsambhavam ekakiccānī ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, the two equanimity-accompanied investigating cittas perform five functions: rebirth-linking, life-continuum, dying, registration, and investigating.

The eight great resultants perform four functions: rebirth-linking, life-continuum, dying, and registration.

The nine sublime resultants perform three functions: rebirth-linking, life-continuum, and dying.

The joy-accompanied investigating citta performs two functions: investigating and registration. Likewise the determining citta [performs two]: determining and adverting.

All the rest — the javanas, the triple mind-element, and the two sets of fivefold sense-consciousness — perform, according to circumstance, only one function each.

Pali Source

Paṭisandhādayo nāma kiccabhedena cuddasa
Dasadhā ṭhānabhedena cittuppādā pakāsitā.
Aṭṭhasaṭṭhi tathā dve ca nav'aṭṭha dve yathākkamaṃ
Eka-dvi-ti-catu-pañca kiccaṭṭhānāni niddise.

Ru-Yi Translation

Cittas beginning with rebirth-linking are declared fourteenfold by division of function, tenfold by division of stage. In order, sixty-eight, two, nine, eight, and two are to be pointed out as having one, two, three, four, and five functional stages, respectively.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
kiccant.

function, task — what this whole compendium classifies by. Note the difference from Chapter 1's citta-names, which often name a citta after only one of several functions it can perform.

votthapanant.

determining — not a distinct citta in its own right, but a second function the mind-door-adverting citta performs when it occurs within a five-door process, as this section makes explicit.

tadārammaṇant.

registration — literally "having that object": taking, for two mind-moments, the same object the javanas just apprehended.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This section is the theoretical backbone of the cognitive-process chapter still to come (Chapter 4): the fourteen named functions and the order in which the text lists them — rebirth-linking, life-continuum, adverting, the five sense-functions, receiving, investigating, determining, javana, registration, dying — is exactly the sequence a single cognitive process runs through, from the arrest of the life-continuum to its resumption. What Chapter 3 supplies here is the census: exactly how many, and which, cittas are qualified to occupy each of those fourteen job-slots. Chapter 4 will supply the choreography — the order and conditions under which they actually get called up.

The Compendium of Doors

The fourth compendium sorts cittas by the door (dvāra) — the channel of access to an object — through which each one arises, including the nineteen "door-freed" cittas that arise independent of any door at all.

Pali Source

Dvārasangahe dvārāni nāma cakkhudvāraṃ sotadvāraṃ ghānadvāraṃ jivhādvāraṃ kāyadvāraṃ manodvārañ cā ti chabbidhāni bhavanti.

Tattha cakkhum eva cakkhudvāraṃ tathā sotādayo sotadvārādīni. Manodvāraṃ pana bhavangan ti pavuccati.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the compendium of doors, the doors are sixfold: the eye door, ear door, nose door, tongue door, body door, and mind door.

Herein, the eye itself is the eye door; likewise the ear, and so on, for ear door and the rest. But the life-continuum is called the mind door.

Pali Source

Tattha pañcadvārāvajjana-cakkhuviññāṇa-sampaṭicchana-santīraṇa-votthapana-kāmāvacarajavana-tadārammaṇavasena chacattāḷīsa cittāni cakkhudvāre yathārahaṃ uppajjanti. Tathā pañcadvārāvajjana-sotaviññāṇādivasena sotadvārādīsu pi chacattāḷīs'eva bhavantī ti. Sabbathā pi pañcadvāre catupaññāsa cittāni kāmāvacarān'evā ti veditabbāni.

Manodvāre pana manodvārāvajjana-pañcapaññāsajavana-tadārammaṇavasena sattasaṭṭhi cittāni bhavanti.

Ekūnavīsati paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cutivasena dvāravimuttāni.

Ru-Yi Translation

Herein, forty-six cittas — by way of five-door-adverting, eye-consciousness, receiving, investigating, determining, sense-sphere javana, and registration — arise, according to circumstance, in the eye door. Likewise in the ear door and the rest, forty-six each arise, by way of five-door-adverting, ear-consciousness, and so on. Altogether, the fifty-four sense-sphere cittas should be understood as occurring in the five doors.

In the mind door, however, sixty-seven cittas occur: mind-door-adverting, the fifty-five javanas, and registration.

Nineteen cittas are door-freed, by way of rebirth-linking, life-continuum, and dying.

Pali Source

Tesu pana dvipañcaviññāṇāni c'eva mahaggata-lokuttarajavanāni cā ti chattiṃsa yathārahaṃ ekadvārikacittāni nāma. Manodhātuttikaṃ pana pañcadvārikaṃ.

Sukhasantīraṇa-votthapana-kāmāvacarajavanāni chadvārikacittāni.

Upekkhāsahagatasantīraṇa-mahāvipākāni chadvārikāni c'eva dvāravimuttāni ca. Mahaggatavipākāni dvāravimuttān'evā ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, thirty-six — the two sets of fivefold sense-consciousness together with the sublime and supramundane javanas — are, according to circumstance, single-door cittas. But the triple mind-element arises through five doors.

The joyful investigating, determining, and sense-sphere javana cittas are six-door cittas.

The equanimity-accompanied investigating cittas and the great resultants are both six-door and door-freed. The sublime resultants are door-freed only.

Pali Source

Ekadvārikacittāni pañcachadvārikāni ca
Chadvārikavimuttāni vimuttāni ca sabbathā.
Chattiṃsati tathā tīṇi ekatiṃsa yathākkamaṃ
Dasadhā navadhā cā ti pañcadhā paridīpaye.

Ru-Yi Translation

Single-door cittas, five-door cittas, six-door cittas, six-door-or-door-freed cittas, and wholly door-freed cittas: in order, thirty-six, three, thirty-one, ten, and nine — thus should the five kinds be shown.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
dvārant.

door — here purely a channel of cognitive access, distinct from the physical "base" (vatthu) the next compendium takes up, though the first five doors and first five bases happen to coincide.

dvāravimuttaadj.

door-freed — vimutta ("released") + dvāra. Not "free" in a liberative sense; simply arising outside any active cognitive process, as rebirth-linking, life-continuum, or dying.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The mind door gets a definition unlike the other five: not a piece of matter, but the bhavaṅga itself — the same passive stream of consciousness this chapter's Compendium of Functions just described flowing "between" cognitive processes. That single identification quietly answers a question Chapter 1 left open: how does a wholly new, unprompted train of thought ever begin, with no physical stimulus at all? The mind door was there the whole time, simply named after its resting function rather than its access function.

The Compendium of Objects

The fifth compendium sorts cittas by their ārammaṇa or ālambana — the object taken. Five kinds correspond to the five physical senses; the sixth, "mental object," gathers everything a sense door cannot reach, Nibbāna included.

Pali Source

Ālambanasangahe ālambanāni nāma rūpārammaṇaṃ saddārammaṇaṃ gandhārammaṇaṃ rasārammaṇaṃ phoṭṭhabbārammaṇaṃ dhammārammaṇañ cā ti chabbidhāni bhavanti.

Tattha rūpam eva rūpārammaṇaṃ. Tathā saddādayo saddārammaṇādīni. Dhammārammaṇaṃ pana pasāda-sukhumarūpa-citta-cetasika-nibbāna-paññattivasena chadhā sangayhanti.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the compendium of objects, the objects are sixfold: visible-form object, sound object, smell object, taste object, tangible object, and mental object.

Herein, visible form itself is the visible-form object; likewise sound and the rest are sound object and so on. But mental object is gathered under six heads: the sensitive [matter of the sense faculties], subtle matter, consciousness, mental factors, Nibbāna, and concepts.

Pali Source

Tattha cakkhudvārikacittānaṃ sabbesam pi rūpam eva ārammaṇaṃ. Tañ ca paccuppannam eva. Tathā sotadvārikacittādīnam pi saddādīni. Tāni ca paccuppannāni yeva.

Manodvārikacittānaṃ pana chabbidham pi paccuppannam atītaṃ anāgataṃ kālavimuttañ ca yathārahaṃ ālambanaṃ hoti.

Dvāravimuttānañ ca pana paṭisandhi-bhavanga-cuti-saṅkhātānaṃ chabbidham pi yathāsambhavaṃ yebhuyyena bhavantare chadvāragahitaṃ paccuppannaṃ atītaṃ paññattibhūtaṃ vā kammakammanimittagatinimittasammataṃ ālambanaṃ hoti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Herein, for all eye-door cittas, visible form alone is the object, and that only present. Likewise for ear-door cittas and the rest: sound and so on, and that too only present.

But for mind-door cittas, the object is of all six kinds, and — present, past, future, or independent of time — as fits the occasion.

For the door-freed cittas — namely, rebirth-linking, life-continuum, and dying — the object is likewise sixfold, according to circumstance: usually whatever was apprehended at one of the six doors in the immediately preceding existence, whether present, past, or conceptual, and reckoned as kamma, the sign of kamma, or the sign of destiny.

Pali Source

Tesu cakkhuviññāṇādīni yathākkamaṃ rūpādi-ekekālambanān'eva. Manodhātuttikaṃ pana rūpādipañcālambanaṃ. Sesāni kāmāvacaravipākāni hasanacittañ cā ti sabbathā pi kāmāvacarālambanān'eva.

Akusalāni c'eva ñāṇavippayuttakāmāvacarajavanāni cā ti lokuttaravajjita-sabbālambanāni.

Ñāṇasampayuttakāmāvacarakusalāni c'eva pañcamajjhānasaṅkhātaṃ abhiññākusalañ cā ti arahattamaggaphalavajjita-sabbālambanāni.

Ñāṇasampayuttakāmāvacarakriyā c'eva kriyābhiññā-votthapanañ cā ti sabbathā pi sabbālambanāni.

Āruppesu dutiyacatutthāni mahaggatālambanāni. Sesāni mahaggatacittāni pana sabbāni pi paññattālambanāni. Lokuttaracittāni nibbānālambanānī ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, eye-consciousness and the rest each take, respectively, only one of visible form and so on. But the triple mind-element takes all five [sense objects], visible form and the rest. The remaining sense-sphere resultants together with the smiling citta always take only sense-sphere objects.

The unwholesome cittas, together with the sense-sphere javanas dissociated from knowledge, take all objects except the supramundane.

The sense-sphere wholesome cittas associated with knowledge, together with the wholesome direct-knowledge citta reckoned as the fifth jhāna, take all objects except the path and fruit of Arahantship.

The sense-sphere functional cittas associated with knowledge, together with the functional direct-knowledge citta and the determining citta, take absolutely all objects.

Among the immaterial cittas, the second and fourth take sublime objects. All the remaining sublime cittas take conceptual objects. The supramundane cittas take Nibbāna as object.

Pali Source

Pañcavīsa parittamhi cha cittāni mahaggate
Ekavīsati vohāre aṭṭha nibbānagocare.
Vīsānuttaramuttamhi aggamaggaphalujjhite
Pañca sabbattha chacceti sattadhā tattha sangaho.

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-five cittas [range] over the limited [sense-sphere object]; six, over the sublime; twenty-one, over concepts; eight, over Nibbāna. Twenty [range] over all but the supramundane; five, over all but the topmost path and fruit; and six, over absolutely all. Sevenfold, thus, is the combination here.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
ārammaṇa / ālambana

object — two Pali words used interchangeably in this chapter for the same thing, from roots meaning "to delight in" and "to hang on to," respectively.

paññattif.

concept — a "timeless" (kālavimutta) object alongside Nibbāna, since a concept, lacking intrinsic nature (sabhāva), never truly arises or passes away to begin with.

kammanimitta / gatinimitta

sign of kamma / sign of destiny — two of the three kinds of object a dying person's mind may take, determining the character of the door-freed cittas of the next existence.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Only functional cittas — never wholesome, never unwholesome — are ever said to "take absolutely all objects," and the reason follows directly from a fact Chapter 1 already established: functional cittas belong only to Arahants, who alone can know a fellow Arahant's own path and fruition consciousness without that access itself constituting kamma. For a trainee or worldling, even wisdom-associated sense-sphere consciousness stops short of one specific object — the path and fruit of Arahantship — precisely because reaching that object is what the citta in question has not yet, by definition, done.

The Compendium of Bases

The sixth and final compendium sorts cittas by vatthu, the physical base each depends on — distinct from a door, since a base supports a citta's very existence rather than merely channeling its object.

Pali Source

Vatthusangahe vatthūni nāma cakkhu-sota-ghāna-jivhā-kāya-hadayavatthu cā ti chabbidhāni bhavanti.

Tāni kāmaloke sabbāni pi labbhanti. Rūpaloke pana ghānādittayaṃ natthi. Arūpaloke pana sabbāni pi na saṃvijjanti.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the compendium of bases, the bases are sixfold: the eye-, ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and heart-base.

All of these are found in the sense-sphere world. But in the fine-material world, the three beginning with the nose are absent. In the immaterial world, none of them exist at all.

Pali Source

Tattha pañcaviññāṇadhātuyo yathākkamaṃ ekantena pañcappasādavatthūni nissāy'eva pavattanti. Pañcadvārāvajjana-sampaṭicchanasaṅkhātā pana manodhātu ca hadayaṃ nissitā yeva pavattanti. Avasesā pana manoviññāṇadhātusaṅkhātā ca santīraṇa-mahāvipāka-paṭighadvaya-paṭhamamagga-hasana-rūpāvacaravasena hadayaṃ nissāy'eva pavattanti.

Avasesā kusal'-ākusala-kriyā'-nuttaravasena pana nissāya vā anissāya vā. Āruppavipākavasena hadayaṃ anissāy'evā ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Herein, the five elements of sense-consciousness occur, respectively, entirely in dependence on the five sensitive bases. But the mind-element — namely, five-door-adverting and receiving — occurs only in dependence on the heart. And what remains, the mind-consciousness-element — namely, investigating, the great resultants, the two associated with resistance, the first path, smiling, and the fine-material-sphere cittas — occurs only in dependence on the heart.

What remains further — the wholesome, unwholesome, functional, and supramundane cittas — occur either dependent on or independent of it. The immaterial resultants occur entirely independent of the heart-base.

Pali Source

Chavatthuṃ nissitā kāme satta rūpe catubbidhā
Tivatthuṃ nissit'āruppe dhātv'ekānissitā matā.
Tecattāḷīsa nissāya dvecattāḷīsa jāyare
Nissāya ca anissāya pāk'āruppā anissitā ti.

Iti Abhidhammatthasaṅgahe Pakiṇṇakasaṅgahavibhāgo nāma tatiyo paricchedo.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the sense sphere, seven elements are held to depend on six bases; in the fine-material sphere, four depend on three bases; in the immaterial sphere, the single element depends on none. Forty-three cittas arise in dependence [on a base]; forty-two arise either dependent or independent; the immaterial resultants arise independent of any base.

Thus ends, in the Compendium of the Things Contained in the Abhidhamma, the third chapter, called the Compendium of the Miscellaneous.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
vatthunt.

base — a physical support for consciousness itself, distinct from a door (dvāra), which merely channels access to an object; the Paṭṭhāna's own phrase is simply "that matter in dependence on which mind occurs."

hadayavatthunt.

heart-base — not named in the canonical Abhidhamma Piṭaka itself, but supplied by the later commentaries as the referent of the Paṭṭhāna's unnamed "that matter."

viññāṇadhātuf.

consciousness-element — the sevenfold grouping (five sense-consciousnesses, mind-element, mind-consciousness-element) this section's base-analysis is organized around.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Compare this compendium's opening list of six bases to the six doors two sections back: the first five are named identically (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body), and it would be easy to assume door and base simply mean the same thing twice. The chapter is careful to block that assumption at the outset — a door is a channel through which cittas reach an object, a base is a physical support for a citta's own occurrence — and then proves the distinction matters with a real case: eye-sensitivity is the door for every citta in an eye-door process, but the base only of eye-consciousness itself; every other citta in that same process (adverting, receiving, investigating, and the rest) is based on the heart instead. Access and support, in other words, can come apart even within a single unbroken sequence of cittas.

Pakiṇṇakasaṅgaha Complete

This closes the translation of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha's third chapter, Pakiṇṇakasaṅgaha ("The Compendium of the Miscellaneous") — the same 89 (121) cittas from Chapter 1, re-sorted six further ways: by feeling (5-fold), root (4-fold), function (14 functions in 5 combination-patterns), door (5-fold), object (7-fold), and physical base (3-fold), closing with the chapter's own colophon. The fourth chapter, Vīthisaṅgaha — the compendium of the cognitive process, choreographing the fourteen functions this chapter has just catalogued into the actual sequence of a single act of cognition — continues from here, the next undertaking for this translation.
