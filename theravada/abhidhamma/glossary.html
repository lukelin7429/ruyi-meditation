Pāli Glossary

Every term explained in a chapter's own "Key vocabulary" box, gathered here into a single alphabetical index across all nine chapters of Ru-Yi's translation of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha. Terms that recur — like adhipati in both Chapter 7 and Chapter 8, or rūpa in both Chapter 1 and Chapter 6 — show every definition side by side, each linked back to its chapter. Use this page to trace a single word across the whole work, or simply to look one up.

How to Use This Index

Each entry below reproduces, word for word, the vocabulary-box definition given the first time (or every time) a term is introduced in a chapter. Where a term appears in more than one chapter, both definitions are shown — sometimes the two chapters gloss the same word slightly differently, reflecting the angle each chapter approaches it from. Click a "Ch. N" tag to jump to that chapter's own page, where the term appears in its full textual context.

Note: this index covers only terms that received their own dedicated vocabulary-box entry somewhere in the nine chapters — not every Pāli word used in the translation. For the running Pāli text itself, see each chapter's own Pali Source / Ru-Yi Translation columns.

A

a-saṅkhārika
Ch. 1

unprompted (spontaneous) / prompted (induced by another's suggestion or one's own deliberation) — a distinction that recurs through the unwholesome and beautiful sense-sphere cittas.

abbocchinnaadj.
Ch. 4

"unbroken, uninterrupted" — a- + vi-ucchinna. The chapter's closing word for the whole picture it has just drawn: cognitive process and bhavaṅga alternating without any gap, for an entire lifetime.

abhinavakaraṇa
Ch. 5

"in the manner of being freshly done" — a striking detail: a kamma re-presenting itself at death does not appear as a memory of something past, but as though it were happening for the very first time, right now.

accutaadj.
Ch. 5

"undying, unfalling" — a- (not) + cuta (past participle of cavati, "to fall away, die"); a poetic epithet for Nibbāna, the one state the wheel described throughout this chapter does not turn through.

adhimokkham.
Ch. 2

decision — literally "releasing onto [the object]." The characteristic of conviction; compared to an unshakable stone pillar.

adhipatim.
Ch. 7

predominant — compared to a king ruling over his ministers (the faculties, each governing only its own sphere): only one predominant can dominate a given citta at a time, and only in javanas with two or three roots.

Ch. 8

predominance — object-predominance (a compellingly attractive object dominating the mind that takes it) is close kin to object decisive-support, differing mainly in force: one attracts and dominates, the other strongly enables arising.

āhaccapaccayaṭṭhiti
Ch. 8

a famously terse phrase — literally something like "the striking-establishment of the condition" — glossed by later commentators as pointing to a condition's own specific mode of efficacy, beyond bare sequence.

āhāram.
Ch. 7

nutriment — a strong supporting condition, not only edible food: contact sustains feeling, volition sustains rebirth (since volition is kamma), and consciousness sustains the whole mind-body compound.

ahetukaadj.
Ch. 1

rootless — a- + hetuka. Lacking all six roots (the three unwholesome and the three "bright" wholesome-or-indeterminate roots), which makes these cittas weaker and less stable than rooted ones.

ahetuka (rebirth)
Ch. 4

double-rooted / rootless rebirth-consciousness — a being born this way can never, in that lifetime, become an Arahant or attain any jhāna, since the wisdom-root needed for both is absent from the start.

ahirika
Ch. 2

shamelessness and fearlessness of wrongdoing — the absence, respectively, of disgust and of dread at misconduct. Their positive counterparts (hiri, ottappa) appear later as "the guardians of the world."

ahosi-kamma
Ch. 5

"has-been kamma" — not a distinct fourth type of volition, but simply what immediately-effective or subsequently-effective kamma becomes once its one window to ripen has closed unused.

ākāsānañcāyatana
Ch. 1

base of infinite space — ākāsa ("space") + ānañca ("infinity"). The first immaterial attainment, reached by contemplating the space left behind once a kasina sign is removed.

ālambana
Ch. 3

object — two Pali words used interchangeably in this chapter for the same thing, from roots meaning "to delight in" and "to hang on to," respectively.

anāgāmi
Ch. 1

stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning, and Arahantship — the four stages of awakening, each cutting off a further set of fetters binding one to future rebirth.

anaññātaññassāmītindriya
Ch. 7

the three "supramundane knowledge" faculties — respectively the knowledge of the path of stream-entry, the six intermediate supramundane knowledges, and the knowledge of the fruit of Arahantship; three names tracking one advancing wisdom.

anantara
Ch. 8

proximity and contiguity — nearly synonymous, differing mainly in emphasis: proximity stresses that nothing intervenes, contiguity that the succession follows the mind's own fixed order; even an Arahant's death consciousness fails this condition, since nothing follows it.

animitta
Ch. 6

void, signless, desireless — three aspects under which the very same unconditioned reality can be approached: empty of greed, hatred, and delusion; free of their signs; free of their craving.

aniyatayogī
Ch. 2

unfixed adjunct / fixed adjunct — 11 factors named here (envy, avarice, worry, the 3 abstinences, the 2 illimitables, conceit, sloth, torpor) are unfixed; the remaining 41 of the 52 are fixed wherever they belong.

aññamaññaadj.
Ch. 8

mutuality — "each other." A special, strictly reciprocal case of conascence, compared to the three legs of a tripod, each supporting and supported by the other two at once.

aññasamānaadj.
Ch. 2

"common to the other" — añña + samāna. Non-beautiful cittas are "other" relative to beautiful ones and vice versa; these 13 factors are common to both.

aññātāvindriya
Ch. 7

the three "supramundane knowledge" faculties — respectively the knowledge of the path of stream-entry, the six intermediate supramundane knowledges, and the knowledge of the fruit of Arahantship; three names tracking one advancing wisdom.

aññindriya
Ch. 7

the three "supramundane knowledge" faculties — respectively the knowledge of the path of stream-entry, the six intermediate supramundane knowledges, and the knowledge of the fruit of Arahantship; three names tracking one advancing wisdom.

anottappa
Ch. 2

shamelessness and fearlessness of wrongdoing — the absence, respectively, of disgust and of dread at misconduct. Their positive counterparts (hiri, ottappa) appear later as "the guardians of the world."

anuloma
Ch. 4

preparation, access, conformity, change-of-lineage — the four (or three, for keen faculties) preliminary sense-sphere javanas that lead the mind up to the threshold of absorption itself.

Ch. 9

conformity — the tenth knowledge, "conforming" both to the eight insight-knowledges that precede it and to the supramundane path about to follow; it is not a separate stage so much as the final threshold moment of the ninth.

anupādinna
Ch. 6

clung-to / not clung-to — kamma-born matter is "clung-to" because it was acquired through kamma rooted in craving and views; note that some kamma-born phenomena can also arise from other causes, so this pair is not a strict either-or.

anupādisesa
Ch. 6

with residue / without residue remaining — the "residue" is the five aggregates: an Arahant still living experiences Nibbāna with the aggregates remaining; at the Arahant's death, Nibbāna without any residue of aggregates.

anusayam.
Ch. 7

latent disposition — from anuseti, "lies along with." A defilement dormant in the mental continuum until conditions bring it to the surface; eradicated stage by stage only by the supramundane paths.

ānuttaraadj.
Ch. 1

unsurpassed, supreme — a- (not) + anuttara's own negation, i.e. "having no [anu]uttara above it"; used here as a poetic synonym for lokuttara to fit the verse's metre.

aparāpariyavedanīya
Ch. 5

"to be experienced indefinitely" — generated by the five middle javana moments, the strongest of the seven; unlike the other three time-categories, this kind of kamma never becomes defunct for as long as saṃsāra continues — not even a Buddha or Arahant is exempt from it.

apāyam.
Ch. 5

the woeful plane — apa- ("away from") + aya ("happiness, gain"): literally, "devoid of happiness."

appamaññāf.
Ch. 2

illimitable — a- (not) + pamāṇa ("measure"). Only 2 of the traditional 4 brahmavihāras (loving-kindness, compassion, appreciative joy, equanimity) count as distinct cetasikas here; the other two are modes of adosa and tatramajjhattatā already listed.

appanāf.
Ch. 4

absorption — originally a highly developed form of vitakka that fixes the mind on its object; extended by usage to cover every jhāna, path, and fruition attainment, even the higher jhānas where vitakka itself is absent.

appaṇihita
Ch. 6

void, signless, desireless — three aspects under which the very same unconditioned reality can be approached: empty of greed, hatred, and delusion; free of their signs; free of their craving.

arahatta
Ch. 1

stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning, and Arahantship — the four stages of awakening, each cutting off a further set of fetters binding one to future rebirth.

ārammaṇa
Ch. 3

object — two Pali words used interchangeably in this chapter for the same thing, from roots meaning "to delight in" and "to hang on to," respectively.

ariyasaccant.
Ch. 7

noble truth — ariya ("noble") + sacca ("truth"); called noble because penetrated by the noble ones, taught by the Noble One, and leading to nobility.

arūpapaṭisandhika
Ch. 5

reborn with matter only / mind only / both — a threefold classification of all rebirth cutting cleanly across the ordinary plane divisions: non-percipient beings (matter only), immaterial-plane beings (mind only), everyone else (both).

āsannaadj.
Ch. 5

death-proximate — kamma performed or vividly recalled immediately before the final javana process; the traditional basis for the custom of reminding a dying person of their good deeds.

asaññasattam.
Ch. 4

non-percipient being — a class of being in the fine-material plane in which consciousness itself is altogether absent; body persists without any mind at all.

Ch. 5

non-percipient being — dwells in the fourth-jhāna plane yet, uniquely, without any consciousness at all, for the entire span of that existence.

āsavam.
Ch. 7

taint — literally "that which flows out" (ā- + savati), used of pus oozing from a wound or of long-fermented liquor; the commentaries gloss it as flowing all the way up to the highest plane, or up to the very threshold of the path.

asekkha
Ch. 4

beyond training / still training — Arahants, who have finished the path, versus the seven kinds of noble trainee (stream-enterers through those on the path to Arahantship) still progressing along it.

āsevanant.
Ch. 8

repetition — from āsevati, "to cultivate, practise." Each javana in a run strengthens the next of the same ethical quality, "as a student grows more proficient through repeated study"; a path citta never itself becomes this condition, since it is followed only by resultant fruition, not another javana of its own kind.

asubhant.
Ch. 9

foulness — the ten stages of a decaying corpse, an especially potent antidote to sensual lust, already met at the very outset of the Visuddhimagga's own concentration section.

atimahanta
Ch. 4

very great, great, slight, very slight — the four grades this whole chapter's sense-door analysis turns on, defined purely by how many mind-moments the object can still sustain the process for once it enters range.

atiparitta
Ch. 4

very great, great, slight, very slight — the four grades this whole chapter's sense-door analysis turns on, defined purely by how many mind-moments the object can still sustain the process for once it enters range.

atthacchāyā
Ch. 8

"the shadow of a thing" — the chapter's own striking image for how a concept, though not itself ultimately real, still becomes an object of consciousness: a shadow cast by real things, not a real thing itself.

atulaadj.
Ch. 1

peerless, unequalled — a- (not) + tula ("weighed, matched"). One of the Buddha's standard epithets.

avacara
Ch. 1

"sphere, that which frequents" — the suffix distinguishing kāmāvacara (sense-sphere), rūpāvacara (fine-material-sphere), and arūpāvacara (immaterial-sphere) consciousness.

āvajjanant.
Ch. 1

adverting, turning toward — the citta that simply turns the mind's attention to an object presenting itself, before any actual seeing, hearing, or knowing occurs. Pañcadvāra- (five-door) and mano-dvāra- (mind-door) name where it occurs.

avibhūta
Ch. 4

clear / obscure — the mind door's own two-way version of the five-door's fourfold grading, distinguished by the prominence of the object or the strength of the consciousness cognizing it.

avijjāf.
Ch. 8

ignorance — identical with the cetasika delusion (moha); in the Abhidhamma method specifically non-knowledge of eight things: the Four Noble Truths, the pre-natal past, the post-mortem future, both together, and dependent arising itself.

avijjānusaya
Ch. 5

the latent tendency of ignorance / of craving — the "twin roots of the round of existence" the text names as generating every rebirth-linking without exception, functioning here as its efficient cause.

avinibbhogarūpa
Ch. 6

inseparable matter — the four great essentials plus colour, smell, taste, and nutritive essence: eight phenomena present in literally every material object, however simple; a group made of just these eight alone is called a "pure octad" (suddhaṭṭhaka).

āyatanant.
Ch. 1

base — here, a dwelling-place for the citta of an attainment, not the sense-base sense of the word used elsewhere in the Abhidhamma.

āyukkhaya
Ch. 5

expiry of life-span / expiry of kammic force — two independent limits on a life, either of which can end it first; when the life-span runs out before the rebirth-generating kamma does, that kamma may generate a further rebirth on the same or a higher plane.

B

balant.
Ch. 7

power — the same five spiritual qualities as the faculties (indriya) above, re-described as "unshakable by their opposites" rather than merely "controlling their own domain."

bhāsissaṃ
Ch. 1

"I will speak" — future tense of bhāsati. A reminder that this text was composed to be recited and memorized, not merely read.

bhavangacalana
Ch. 4

vibration and arrest of the life-continuum — the two moments in which an impinging object first disturbs, then cuts off, the passive bhavaṅga stream before the process proper begins.

bhavangupaccheda
Ch. 4

vibration and arrest of the life-continuum — the two moments in which an impinging object first disturbs, then cuts off, the passive bhavaṅga stream before the process proper begins.

bhūmif.
Ch. 1

plane — the second verse's axis, the very same fourfold division (sense, fine-material, immaterial, supramundane) the chapter opened with.

bodhipakkhiya
Ch. 7

"on the side of enlightenment" — bodhi ("awakening") + pakkhiya ("belonging to the side/wing of"). A rare Sutta phrase that becomes the standard umbrella term for these 37 factors in later literature.

bojjhaṅgam.
Ch. 7

enlightenment factor — bodhi + aṅga. Grouped in the tradition into two balancing triads (investigation/energy/zest against sluggishness; tranquillity/concentration/equanimity against excitation), with mindfulness overseeing both.

brahmavihāra
Ch. 9

divine abode — the four illimitables, so named because they are the mental dwelling-places of the Brahmā deities; the same four cetasika-level qualities discussed in Chapter 2 (loving-kindness as a mode of non-hatred, equanimity as a mode of neutrality of mind).

budhām. pl.
Ch. 1

the wise — the closing verse's own word for whoever can hold both the 89-fold and 121-fold counts in mind as two views of one and the same set of cittas.

C

cakkhu-/sota-/ghāna-/jivhā-/kāya-viññāṇa
Ch. 1

eye-, ear-, nose-, tongue-, and body-consciousness — the five kinds of sense-door cognition, collectively called dvi-pañcaviññāṇa, "the two sets of fivefold sense-consciousness" (one set as unwholesome-resultant, one as wholesome-resultant).

caritant.
Ch. 9

temperament — a person's natural character, determined by the kamma that produced their rebirth-linking consciousness; lustful pairs with faithful, hateful with intellectual, deluded with discursive, since each pair shares a structural resemblance despite differing in ethical quality.

catukkant.
Ch. 5

a set of four — the chapter's own organizing device, used four times over: this chapter, unusually among the nine, is built almost entirely out of four parallel fourfold schemes.

cetasika
Ch. 1

the four ultimates themselves: consciousness, the factors that arise with it, matter, and the unconditioned element. This chapter takes up only the first.

ceto-yutta
Ch. 2

"yoked to mind" — ceto (an oblique form of citta) + yutta ("joined"). The word cetasika itself is built on this same root: "belonging to citta."

chabbisuddhi
Ch. 7

the sixfold purification — the mundane stages preceding the seventh, supramundane purification (taken up fully in Chapter 9); an expansion of the more familiar three-part virtue/concentration/wisdom scheme.

chandam.
Ch. 2

desire [to act] — an ethically neutral wish to accomplish something, sharply distinguished from lobha (greed), which is always unwholesome.

citta
Ch. 1

the four ultimates themselves: consciousness, the factors that arise with it, matter, and the unconditioned element. This chapter takes up only the first.

cittakkhaṇam.
Ch. 4

mind-moment — the basic unit of this chapter, itself divisible into uppāda, ṭhiti, and bhaṅga (arising, presence, dissolution). Traditionally said to be so brief that billions elapse in a single eye-blink.

cittuppādavasena
Ch. 3

"by way of the arising of consciousness" — the chapter's stated method: analysis proceeds citta by citta, even though every citta still carries its full retinue of cetasikas along with it.

D

dasaka
Ch. 6

decad / nonad — "group of ten" / "group of nine," the naming convention for kamma-born kalāpas: the 8 inseparables + life + one distinguishing sense faculty = 10, or without a distinguishing faculty = 9 (the vital nonad).

dhammavicaya
Ch. 7

"investigation of states" — the enlightenment-factor name for wisdom (paññā), insight into mental and material phenomena exactly as they are.

dhātuf.
Ch. 7

element — dhāreti, "to bear [its own nature]." Splits the mind base and mental-object base of the twelve āyatanas into finer distinctions: seven separate consciousness-elements instead of one undifferentiated "mind."

diṭṭhadhammavedanīya
Ch. 5

"to be experienced in this very life" — generated by the very first of a javana process's seven moments, the weakest of the series; if it finds no opportunity to ripen in this life, it lapses to ahosi, defunct.

diṭṭhigatant.
Ch. 1

"gone into a view" — a settled opinion; unqualified, it means wrong view. Sampayutta / vippayutta: associated with / dissociated from.

domanassant.
Ch. 1

displeasure, unpleasant mental feeling — du- ("bad") + manas. Unlike somanassa and upekkhā, domanassa is always unwholesome; it accompanies these two cittas alone among all 89.

Ch. 3

joy and displeasure — the mental counterparts of sukha/dukkha, already familiar from Chapter 1's greed- and hatred-rooted cittas.

duhetuka
Ch. 4

double-rooted / rootless rebirth-consciousness — a being born this way can never, in that lifetime, become an Arahant or attain any jhāna, since the wisdom-root needed for both is absent from the start.

duka
Ch. 2

couplet, pair — the prompted/unprompted pairing that structures this whole section; since the two never differ in cetasika content, the text treats each pair as a single unit.

dukkha (as vedanā)
Ch. 3

here narrowly bodily pleasure and pain — distinct from the broader Buddhist sense of dukkha as the unsatisfactoriness of all conditioned existence, which the chapter's own Guide-layer discussion is careful to separate.

dvārant.
Ch. 3

door — here purely a channel of cognitive access, distinct from the physical "base" (vatthu) the next compendium takes up, though the first five doors and first five bases happen to coincide.

dvāravimuttaadj.
Ch. 3

door-freed — vimutta ("released") + dvāra. Not "free" in a liberative sense; simply arising outside any active cognitive process, as rebirth-linking, life-continuum, or dying.

dvaya
Ch. 2

couplet, pair — the prompted/unprompted pairing that structures this whole section; since the two never differ in cetasika content, the text treats each pair as a single unit.

dvipañcaviññāṇa
Ch. 2

"the two sets of fivefold [sense-]consciousness" — the ten simplest cittas from Chapter 1 (eye- through body-consciousness, unwholesome- and wholesome-resultant). Too rudimentary to carry initial application, decision, or energy.

E

ekaggatāf.
Ch. 1

one-pointedness [of mind] — the single factor present in every jhāna citta without exception, from the first through the fifth.

Ch. 2

one-pointedness — the same term met in Chapter 1's jhāna formulas, here present in every citta at a rudimentary level, not only in absorption.

ekālambana
Ch. 2

having the same object, having the same physical base — the remaining two marks, which exclude co-arisen matter (which shares no object) from counting as a cetasika.

ekaṃ
Ch. 1

"one" — each item in these lists is literally "one [consciousness], [qualified thus]." The numbering (1, 2, 3…) is not a modern editorial addition; it is how the root text itself counts its cittas.

ekanirodha
Ch. 2

arising together, ceasing together — eka- ("one, same") + uppāda/nirodha. The first two of the four defining marks.

ekavatthuka
Ch. 2

having the same object, having the same physical base — the remaining two marks, which exclude co-arisen matter (which shares no object) from counting as a cetasika.

ekavisaya
Ch. 5

"of one object" — the closing verse's own compressed reminder that rebirth-linking, bhavaṅga, and death all share not only one citta-type but literally the very same object throughout an entire existence.

ekuppāda
Ch. 2

arising together, ceasing together — eka- ("one, same") + uppāda/nirodha. The first two of the four defining marks.

G

gabbhaseyyaka
Ch. 6

womb-born — one of the four traditional modes of birth (egg-, womb-, moisture-, and spontaneous-born); womb-born beings, unlike moisture-born or spontaneously-born ones, start with only three decads and grow the rest — eye, ear, and so on — gradually.

gantham.
Ch. 7

knot — ties the mind to "the body," here meaning any aggregation, mental or physical, present or future; covetousness and ill will are greed and hatred outright, the other two are modes of wrong view.

gaṇuttamam.
Ch. 1

"the finest of communities" — gaṇa (group, company) + uttama (foremost); here the Saṅgha, the Noble Order.

garukaadj.
Ch. 5

weighty — on the wholesome side, the jhānas; on the unwholesome side, the five "immediate" heinous crimes (killing one's mother or father, killing an Arahant, wounding a Buddha, or causing a schism in the Saṅgha) together with a fixed wrong view denying kamma altogether.

gatinimitta
Ch. 3

sign of kamma / sign of destiny — two of the three kinds of object a dying person's mind may take, determining the character of the door-freed cittas of the next existence.

Ch. 5

kamma, sign of kamma, sign of destiny — already introduced in Chapter 3, here shown in their actual operation as the three possible objects the dying mind's javana process apprehends and hands forward.

gocaraggāhika
Ch. 6

"object-grasping" (figuratively) — the five sense sensitivities; matter itself cannot literally cognize anything, but is spoken of this way because it serves as the base for the consciousness that does.

gotrabhūnt.
Ch. 4

"change-of-lineage" — gotra ("lineage") + bhū ("becoming"). For jhāna, the crossing from the sense-sphere lineage to the sublime; for the first path, the crossing from the worldling's lineage to the noble one's.

Ch. 9

change-of-lineage — already met in Chapter 4's absorption process; here it marks the passage from the "lineage" of worldlings to that of the noble ones, the first citta ever to take Nibbāna as object, though it cannot yet dispel the defilements the way the path itself does.

H

hadayavatthunt.
Ch. 3

heart-base — not named in the canonical Abhidhamma Piṭaka itself, but supplied by the later commentaries as the referent of the Paṭṭhāna's unnamed "that matter."

hasituppādam.
Ch. 1

smile-producing consciousness — hasita ("smiling") + uppāda ("arising"). Said to occur only in Arahants, including Buddhas, when they smile at some sense-sphere phenomenon.

hetu
Ch. 1

root — the organizing principle of this whole opening section: consciousness classed by which of the three unwholesome roots (greed, hatred, delusion) is dominant in it.

Ch. 3

root — a mental factor that "establishes firmness," in the Abhidhamma sense strictly narrower than the Suttas' general use of hetu for any cause (kāraṇa).

I

iddhipādam.
Ch. 7

means to accomplishment — the same four states as the predominants (adhipati) just met, but named iddhipāda specifically when applied to achieving the goal of the Buddha's teaching rather than any goal whatsoever.

issā
Ch. 2

envy and avarice — resentment at another's success, and unwillingness to share one's own. Paired opposites of the illimitables muditā and cāga (generosity).

J

janakaadj.
Ch. 5

productive — the only kind of kamma able to generate a new rebirth-linking; every one of the sixteen types below still falls under one function or another, but only a "full course of action" ever produces janaka-kamma's specific effect.

jātif.
Ch. 1

kind, nature — the first verse's axis: is this citta unwholesome, wholesome, resultant, or functional? Resultant and functional cittas are together called abyākata, "indeterminate," being neither wholesome nor unwholesome themselves.

javati
Ch. 4

"runs" — the verb this passage uses for the javana phase's characteristic swift repetition, the same root that gives javana its name ("running swiftly").

jhānaṅgant.
Ch. 1

jhāna factor — the same five (vitakka, vicāra, pīti, sukha, ekaggatā) whose presence or absence defined the fine-material cittas; here used to grade the supramundane cittas the same way.

Ch. 7

jhāna factor — used here in the broader sense of "close contemplation" (upanijjhāyana) of an object, so these seven occur even outside actual meditative absorption; displeasure is the sole exclusively-unwholesome member.

jīvitanavakant.
Ch. 5

the "vital nonad" — a group of nine material phenomena including the life faculty; for non-percipient beings, this bare cluster of matter itself performs the role rebirth-consciousness plays everywhere else.

jjhānant.
Ch. 1

absorption — from jhāyati, "to meditate, to burn up [the hindrances]." Each successive jhāna is reached by dropping the coarsest remaining factor of the one before it.

K

kalāpam.
Ch. 6

group, cluster — the basic unit in which matter actually occurs; no material phenomenon ever arises alone, always bundled with at least the eight inseparables.

kamma
Ch. 5

kamma, sign of kamma, sign of destiny — already introduced in Chapter 3, here shown in their actual operation as the three possible objects the dying mind's javana process apprehends and hands forward.

kammakkhaya
Ch. 5

expiry of life-span / expiry of kammic force — two independent limits on a life, either of which can end it first; when the life-span runs out before the rebirth-generating kamma does, that kamma may generate a further rebirth on the same or a higher plane.

kammanimitta
Ch. 3

clung-to / not clung-to — kamma-born matter is "clung-to" because it was acquired through kamma rooted in craving and views; note that some kamma-born phenomena can also arise from other causes, so this pair is not a strict either-or.

upanissayam.
Ch. 8

decisive support — a strong, weighty form of dependence, threefold: tied to a compelling object, to an immediately-preceding mental state, or — most broadly — to any past state at all (pakatūpanissaya, "natural decisive support") strong enough to influence what arises much later.

upekkhā
Ch. 1

joy and equanimity — su ("pleasant") + manas ("mind"); and neutral feeling, leaning toward neither gladness nor dejection.

V

vajjitaadj.
Ch. 2

excluded, apart from — the past participle of vajjeti, doing heavy lifting in every occasional's definition: each is defined by which cittas it is absent from, not a positive list of where it occurs.

vānant.
Ch. 6

"entanglement, weaving" — the root text's own preferred etymology for nibbāna: not merely "blown out," but a departure (nikkhanta) from the entanglement of craving, which binds one to saṃsāra.

vatthunt.
Ch. 3

base — a physical support for consciousness itself, distinct from a door (dvāra), which merely channels access to an object; the Paṭṭhāna's own phrase is simply "that matter in dependence on which mind occurs."

vatthudhamma
Ch. 7

"entity" — the 72 distinct realities Chapters 1–6 have described: 1 citta (all 89 cittas share one nature), 52 cetasikas, 18 concretely-produced material phenomena, and 1 Nibbāna (1+52+18+1=72).

vibhāga
Ch. 6

enumeration, classification, origination, grouping, occurrence — the chapter's own five-part table of contents, announced in this single opening verse.

vibhūta
Ch. 4

clear / obscure — the mind door's own two-way version of the five-door's fourfold grading, distinguished by the prominence of the object or the strength of the consciousness cognizing it.

vicāra
Ch. 1

initial and sustained application [of mind to the object] — the first two jhāna factors, both dropped by the second jhāna.

vicikicchā
Ch. 1

doubt and restlessness — the two cittas "of sheer delusion" (momūha) are distinguished from each other only by which of these two factors is prominent; neither carries the prompted/unprompted pair.

vimokkhamukha
Ch. 9

door to emancipation — whichever of the three contemplations a meditator's insight happens to settle on, becoming their immediate approach to the path; the three names apply loosely to every path and fruit regardless, since all take Nibbāna as object and Nibbāna itself is void, signless, and desireless at once.

vinipātikāsura
Ch. 5

"fallen asura" — a woeful-plane class distinct from the asuras who contend with the Thirty-Three gods; these live near human villages, scavenging discarded food.

viññāṇadhātuf.
Ch. 3

consciousness-element — the sevenfold grouping (five sense-consciousnesses, mind-element, mind-consciousness-element) this section's base-analysis is organized around.

viññattif.
Ch. 6

intimation — a special modification of the air element (bodily) or earth element (vocal), both originating from consciousness, by which one's intentions become visible or audible to another.

vipākam.
Ch. 1

resultant — the "ripening" of past kamma. Note that only body-consciousness carries a bodily feeling (pain or pleasure); the other four sense-doors and the two rootless mind-doors here all carry the mental feeling of equanimity, with one single exception (item 14).

vipassanā
Ch. 9

calm and insight — samatha (from sam-, "to become peaceful") is one-pointedness developed to the level of jhāna; vipassanā ("seeing in diverse ways") is the direct meditative perception of impermanence, suffering, and non-self, unique to the Buddha's teaching.

vipassanupakkilesa
Ch. 9

imperfection of insight — ten genuinely impressive experiences (a radiant aura, intense zest, blissful tranquillity, and the rest) that arise as knowledge of comprehension matures, easily mistaken by an inexperienced meditator for the path itself, which is precisely why this purification exists.

vippayuttaadj.
Ch. 8

dissociation — the one condition on this list that requires the conditioning and conditioned states to be of different types entirely, one mental and one material, "like water and oil placed together, remaining distinct."

viratif.
Ch. 2

abstinence — deliberate restraint from a specific wrong deed when the occasion to do it has actually arisen; not the same as simply never having the opportunity.

visayappavatti
Ch. 4

"presentation of an object" — visaya ("object, range") + pavatti. Names how forcefully or clearly an object registers, not its physical size.

visesetvā
Ch. 2

"having distinguished, with this difference" — the marker word the text uses to say the five prompted greed-cittas repeat the unprompted pattern exactly, with one addition: sloth and torpor.

visuddhif.
Ch. 9

purification — each of the seven is a support (paccaya, in the Chapter 8 sense) for the one that follows; the first corresponds to the path's virtue factor, the second to its concentration, the last five to its wisdom.

visuṃ visuṃ
Ch. 2

"separately, one by one" — the pattern for the 16 mundane occurrences of the abstinences (8 wholesome + 8 functional cittas): only ever one abstinence at a time, never two together.

vitakka
Ch. 1

initial and sustained application [of mind to the object] — the first two jhāna factors, both dropped by the second jhāna.

vīthif.
Ch. 4

street, path — used here in the technical sense of "process": the fixed sequence a cognitive act runs through.

vīthimuttaadj.
Ch. 5

process-freed — vīthi ("process") + mutta ("released"). Not liberation in a spiritual sense; simply consciousness occurring outside an active cognitive process, as rebirth-linking, life-continuum, or death.

vīthiniyamam.
Ch. 4

the fixed rule of process — explicitly said NOT to apply once absorption is underway and well-practiced, in sharp contrast to every sense-sphere process described earlier in the chapter, which is rigidly fixed.

votthapanant.
Ch. 2

determining — another name for the mind-door-adverting citta when it occurs within a five-door process, met already in Chapter 1's cognitive-process cittas.

Ch. 3

determining — not a distinct citta in its own right, but a second function the mind-door-adverting citta performs when it occurs within a five-door process, as this section makes explicit.
