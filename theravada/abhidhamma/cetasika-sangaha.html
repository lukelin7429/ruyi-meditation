Chapter 2 · Cetasikasaṅgaha · Complete

What Arises With Consciousness?

This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha's chapter 2, Cetasikasaṅgahavibhāga ("The Compendium of Mental Factors") — all 52 cetasikas enumerated in their seven groups, then examined by two complementary methods: association (sampayoganaya), which asks of each mental factor, "which types of consciousness does it join?"; and combination (saṅgahanaya), which asks of each type of consciousness, "which mental factors compose it?" Closing with the chapter's own colophon.

Translation Method

The Pāli source is the Pali Text Society's critical edition of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha, edited by Ven. Hammalawa Saddhātissa, cross-checked against the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana (Sixth Council) romanized text maintained by the Vipassana Research Institute (VRI, tipitaka.org) for internal consistency.

The English translation is Ru-Yi's own, made directly from the Pāli. No existing English translation is consulted, paraphrased, or used as a guide for wording or structure — including Bhikkhu Bodhi's copyrighted A Comprehensive Manual of Abhidhamma, the standard modern edition of this text. That volume's numbered summary charts for this chapter (Tables 2.1–2.4) are, by its own editorial note, not part of Anuruddha's root text at all — they were contributed by Ven. U Sīlānanda and Ven. Nārada Mahāthera for that specific edition. This page accordingly builds no equivalent tables of its own; every number given below comes directly from the root text's own verses and prose, translated on its own terms.

Reading notes are grounded only in the chapter's own internal structure and cross-references, and in classifications that are standard, uncontested Abhidhamma doctrine across the tradition — never in a modern scholar's specific interpretation. Each translated passage also carries a short vocabulary list for readers learning Pāli alongside the text.

Fifty-Two States, Sharing Consciousness's Fate

The chapter opens by defining what a cetasika is at all — not by content, but by four shared characteristics that bind every mental factor to the consciousness it arises with.

Pali Source

Ekuppāda-nirodhā ca ekālambana-vatthukā
Cetoyuttā dvipaññāsa dhammā cetasikā matā.

Ru-Yi Translation

Arising together and ceasing together [with consciousness], having the same object and the same base [as consciousness], and joined to mind: fifty-two states are recognized as mental factors.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
ekuppāda / ekanirodha

arising together, ceasing together — eka- ("one, same") + uppāda/nirodha. The first two of the four defining marks.

ekālambana / ekavatthuka

having the same object, having the same physical base — the remaining two marks, which exclude co-arisen matter (which shares no object) from counting as a cetasika.

ceto-yutta

"yoked to mind" — ceto (an oblique form of citta) + yutta ("joined"). The word cetasika itself is built on this same root: "belonging to citta."

Reading note — Ru-Yi

All four marks are needed, and each rules out a specific false positive. "Arising together" alone would wrongly admit mind-produced and kamma-produced matter, which also arises at the same moment as its citta. "Ceasing together" excludes that matter, since it typically outlasts the citta by many mind-moments. "Same object" then separates cetasikas from the two kinds of matter that do arise and cease with citta exactly — bodily and vocal intimation — because matter, unlike every mental state, cognizes no object at all. Only "same base" remains to complete the picture, in the sensuous and fine-material worlds where mind and matter share a physical seat.

The Seven Universals

The 52 cetasikas fall into seven traditional groups (numbered I–VII in the root text itself). The first two groups — thirteen factors in all — are called "ethically variable" (aññasamāna): they take on whatever moral color the rest of the citta has. The first of these two groups is the seven universals, present in literally every citta.

Pali Source

Katham? I. (1) Phasso, (2) vedanā, (3) saññā, (4) cetanā, (5) ekaggatā, (6) jīvitindriyaṃ, (7) manasikāro cā ti satt'ime cetasikā sabbacittasādhāraṇā nāma.

Ru-Yi Translation

How? I. (1) Contact, (2) feeling, (3) perception, (4) volition, (5) one-pointedness, (6) the life faculty, and (7) attention: these seven mental factors are called common to all consciousness.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
phassam.

contact — from phusati, "to touch." Not physical impact, but the mental "touching" of the object that opens each act of cognition.

saññāf.

perception — the factor that marks and recognizes an object, so it can be recognized again.

ekaggatāf.

one-pointedness — the same term met in Chapter 1's jhāna formulas, here present in every citta at a rudimentary level, not only in absorption.

manasikāram.

attention — literally "making-in-mind." Turns the associated states toward the object, distinct from vitakka, which applies them onto it.

The Six Occasionals

The second half of the "ethically variable" group: six factors that, unlike the universals, arise only in particular types of consciousness — not all.

Pali Source

II. (1) Vitakko, (2) vicāro, (3) adhimokkho, (4) viriyaṃ, (5) pīti, (6) chando cā ti cha ime cetasikā pakiṇṇakā nāma. Evam ete terasa cetasikā aññasamānā ti veditabbā.

Ru-Yi Translation

II. (1) Initial application, (2) sustained application, (3) decision, (4) energy, (5) zest, and (6) desire: these six mental factors are called occasional. Thus these thirteen mental factors, together, should be understood as the ethically variable ones.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
pakiṇṇakaadj.

occasional, scattered — pakiṇṇa ("strewn, dispersed"). Not because they are rare, but because unlike the universals they don't touch every citta.

adhimokkham.

decision — literally "releasing onto [the object]." The characteristic of conviction; compared to an unshakable stone pillar.

chandam.

desire [to act] — an ethically neutral wish to accomplish something, sharply distinguished from lobha (greed), which is always unwholesome.

aññasamānaadj.

"common to the other" — añña + samāna. Non-beautiful cittas are "other" relative to beautiful ones and vice versa; these 13 factors are common to both.

The 13 aññasamāna factors are complete: 7 universals + 6 occasionals.

The Fourteen Unwholesome Factors

The third group is fixed in its moral color: these 14 arise only in unwholesome consciousness, never elsewhere.

Pali Source

III. (1) Moho, (2) ahirikaṃ, (3) anottappaṃ, (4) uddhaccaṃ, (5) lobho, (6) diṭṭhi, (7) māno, (8) doso, (9) issā, (10) macchariyaṃ, (11) kukkuccaṃ, (12) thīnaṃ, (13) middhaṃ, (14) vicikicchā cā ti cuddas'ime cetasikā akusalā nāma.

Ru-Yi Translation

III. (1) Delusion, (2) shamelessness, (3) fearlessness of wrongdoing, (4) restlessness, (5) greed, (6) wrong view, (7) conceit, (8) hatred, (9) envy, (10) avarice, (11) worry, (12) sloth, (13) torpor, and (14) doubt: these fourteen mental factors are called unwholesome.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
ahirika / anottappa

shamelessness and fearlessness of wrongdoing — the absence, respectively, of disgust and of dread at misconduct. Their positive counterparts (hiri, ottappa) appear later as "the guardians of the world."

issā / macchariya

envy and avarice — resentment at another's success, and unwillingness to share one's own. Paired opposites of the illimitables muditā and cāga (generosity).

thīna / middha

sloth and torpor — sluggishness of the mental factors and of consciousness itself, always arising together, opposed to energy (vīriya).

kukkuccant.

worry, remorse — specifically regret over wrongs already done or rights left undone, not general anxiety.

The Nineteen Beautiful Universals

The fourth group opens the "beautiful" (sobhana) half of the enumeration: 19 factors present in every beautiful citta without exception — the largest single group of the 52.

Pali Source

IV. (1) Saddhā, (2) sati, (3) hiri, (4) ottappaṃ, (5) alobho, (6) adoso, (7) tatramajjhattatā, (8) kāyapassaddhi, (9) cittapassaddhi, (10) kāyalahutā, (11) cittalahutā, (12) kāyamudutā, (13) cittamudutā, (14) kāyakammaññatā, (15) cittakammaññatā, (16) kāyapāguññatā, (17) cittapāguññatā, (18) kāyujjukatā, (19) cittujjukatā cā ti ekūnavīsat'ime cetasikā sobhanasādhāraṇā nāma.

Ru-Yi Translation

IV. (1) Faith, (2) mindfulness, (3) shame, (4) fear of wrongdoing, (5) non-greed, (6) non-hatred, (7) neutrality of mind, (8) tranquillity of the mental body, (9) tranquillity of consciousness, (10) lightness of the mental body, (11) lightness of consciousness, (12) malleability of the mental body, (13) malleability of consciousness, (14) wieldiness of the mental body, (15) wieldiness of consciousness, (16) proficiency of the mental body, (17) proficiency of consciousness, (18) rectitude of the mental body, and (19) rectitude of consciousness: these nineteen mental factors are called common to the beautiful.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
tatramajjhattatāf.

neutrality of mind — literally "there-in-middleness." A synonym for upekkhā not as neutral feeling but as balance and impartiality; becomes equanimity toward beings.

kāya-/citta-passaddhi, lahutā, mudutā, kammaññatā, pāguññatā, ujjukatā

six qualities, each doubled — one form for "the mental body" (the cluster of cetasikas), one for consciousness itself: tranquillity, lightness, malleability, wieldiness, proficiency, and rectitude. 6 × 2 = 12 of the 19.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Look at what these twelve paired factors are doing structurally: each names the absence of a specific defilement's effect — tranquillity opposes restlessness and worry, lightness opposes sloth and torpor, malleability opposes wrong view and conceit, wieldiness opposes the remaining hindrances, proficiency opposes lack of faith, and rectitude opposes hypocrisy. The 19 universal-beautiful factors are not simply "the good version of the bad list" in general — each pair is the precise structural mirror of a named defilement from the unwholesome fourteen just enumerated.

Abstinences, Illimitables, and Wisdom — the 52nd

Three short remaining groups complete the beautiful factors — each variable, arising only when its specific function is actually exercised — followed by the chapter's first summary verse.

Pali Source

V. (1) Sammāvācā, (2) sammākammanto, (3) sammā-ājīvo cā ti tisso viratiyo nāma.

VI. (1) Karuṇā, (2) muditā pana appamaññāyo nāmā ti.

VII. Sabbathā pi paññindriyena saddhiṃ pañcavīsat'ime cetasikā sobhanā ti veditabbā.

Ettāvatā ca: Teras'aññasamānā ca cuddas'ākusalā tathā
Sobhanā pañcavīsā ti dvipaññāsa pavuccare.

Ru-Yi Translation

V. (1) Right speech, (2) right action, and (3) right livelihood: these three are called abstinences.

VI. (1) Compassion and (2) appreciative joy: these are called the illimitables.

VII. Together with the wisdom faculty, these twenty-five in all should be understood as the beautiful mental factors.

Thus far: thirteen are ethically variable, fourteen likewise are unwholesome, and twenty-five are beautiful — so fifty-two, it is said.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
viratif.

abstinence — deliberate restraint from a specific wrong deed when the occasion to do it has actually arisen; not the same as simply never having the opportunity.

appamaññāf.

illimitable — a- (not) + pamāṇa ("measure"). Only 2 of the traditional 4 brahmavihāras (loving-kindness, compassion, appreciative joy, equanimity) count as distinct cetasikas here; the other two are modes of adosa and tatramajjhattatā already listed.

paññindriyant.

the wisdom faculty — paññā ("wisdom") + indriya ("faculty"). A synonym here for ñāṇa and amoha (non-delusion), the single 52nd factor standing alone in its own group.

All 52 cetasikas now enumerated: 7 universals + 6 occasionals + 14 unwholesome + 19 beautiful universals + 3 abstinences + 2 illimitables + 1 wisdom faculty.

Two Ways to Read the Same Fifty-Two

Having listed all 52, the chapter now turns to analyze them two ways: which cittas does each factor join (association), and which factors compose each citta (combination). It opens with a verse announcing the shift, then a compact rule of thumb.

Pali Source

Tesaṃ cittāviyuttānaṃ yathāyogam ito paraṃ
Cittuppādesu paccekaṃ sampayogo pavuccati.

Satta sabbattha yujjanti yathāyogaṃ pakiṇṇakā
Cuddas'ākusalesv'eva sobhanesv'eva sobhanā.

Ru-Yi Translation

From here on, the association of these mind-conjoined states, each in its fitting way, with the types of consciousness will be told.

Seven are joined everywhere; the occasionals, each in its own fitting way; the fourteen only with the unwholesome states; and the beautiful only with the beautiful.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
sampayogam.

association — saṃ- + payoga ("application"). The method that starts from a cetasika and asks which cittas it can join.

saṅgaham.

combination, inclusion — the reverse method, met already in Chapter 1's title; here it starts from a citta and asks which cetasikas compose it.

Association — The Ethically Variable Factors

The seven universals need no elaboration — they simply join every one of the 89 cittas. The six occasionals are each defined by exclusion: the text names which cittas each one skips.

Pali Source

Kathaṃ? (i) Sabbacittasādhāraṇā tāva satt'ime cetasikā sabbesu pi ekūnanavuti cittuppādesu labbhanti.

Pakiṇṇakesu pana:

(ii) Vitakko tāva dvipañcaviññāṇa-vajjita-kāmāvacaracittesu c'eva ekādasasu paṭhamajjhānacittesu cā ti pañcapaññāsa cittesu uppajjati.

(iii) Vicāro pana tesu c'eva ekādasasu dutiyajjhānacittesu cā ti chasaṭṭhi cittesu jāyati.

(iv) Adhimokkho dvipañcaviññāṇa-vicikicchāsahagata-vajjita-cittesu.

(v) Viriyaṃ pañcadvārāvajjana-dvipañcaviññāṇa-sampaṭicchana-santīraṇa-vajjita-cittesu.

(vi) Pīti domanass'-upekkhāsahagata-kāyaviññāṇa-catutthajjhāna-vajjita-cittesu.

(vii) Chando ahetuka-momūha-vajjita-cittesu labbhati.

Ru-Yi Translation

How? (i) First, these seven factors common to all consciousness are found in all eighty-nine types of consciousness.

Among the occasionals, however:

(ii) Initial application arises in fifty-five types of consciousness: in the sense-sphere cittas, excluding the two sets of fivefold sense-consciousness, together with the eleven types of first-jhāna consciousness.

(iii) Sustained application arises in sixty-six types of consciousness: in those [fifty-five], together with the eleven types of second-jhāna consciousness.

(iv) Decision [arises] in the types of consciousness excluding the two sets of fivefold sense-consciousness and that accompanied by doubt.

(v) Energy [arises] in the types of consciousness excluding five-door-adverting, the two sets of fivefold sense-consciousness, receiving, and investigating.

(vi) Zest [arises] in the types of consciousness excluding those accompanied by displeasure, those accompanied by equanimity, body-consciousness, and fourth-jhāna consciousness.

(vii) Desire is found in the types of consciousness excluding the rootless and the [two] cittas of sheer delusion.

Pali Source

Te pana cittuppādā yathākkamaṃ:
Chasaṭṭhi pañcapaññāsa ekādasa ca soḷasa
Sattati vīsati c'eva pakiṇṇakavivajjitā.
Pañcapaññāsa chasaṭṭhi 'ṭṭhasattati tisattati
Ekapaññāsa c'ekūnasattati sapakiṇṇakā.

Ru-Yi Translation

Those types of consciousness, in order, are: sixty-six, fifty-five, eleven, sixteen, seventy, and twenty — each without its occasional. Fifty-five, sixty-six, seventy-eight, seventy-three, fifty-one, and sixty-nine — each with its occasional.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
vajjitaadj.

excluded, apart from — the past participle of vajjeti, doing heavy lifting in every occasional's definition: each is defined by which cittas it is absent from, not a positive list of where it occurs.

dvipañcaviññāṇa

"the two sets of fivefold [sense-]consciousness" — the ten simplest cittas from Chapter 1 (eye- through body-consciousness, unwholesome- and wholesome-resultant). Too rudimentary to carry initial application, decision, or energy.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Notice which count uses which citta-scheme, and why: initial application and sustained application are counted on the 121-fold scheme (55 and 66, since jhāna level matters — vitakka drops out at the second jhāna and every supramundane citta graded to that level), while decision, energy, and desire use the plain 89-fold count (their presence or absence doesn't depend on which jhāna a supramundane citta is graded to). Zest reverts to the 121-fold scheme once more, since it tracks jhāna level exactly as vitakka and vicāra do. The text switches schemes silently, expecting the reader to already know from Chapter 1 which distinctions are jhāna-sensitive and which are not.

Association — The Fourteen Unwholesome Factors

The unwholesome factors divide cleanly along the lines already drawn in Chapter 1's twelve unwholesome cittas: four universal to all twelve, and ten distributed according to which root or roots are active.

Pali Source

(i) Akusalesu pana moho ahirikaṃ, anottappaṃ, uddhaccañ cā ti cattāro'me cetasikā sabbākusalasādhāraṇā nāma. Sabbesu pi dvādas'ākusalesu labbhanti.

(ii) Lobho aṭṭhasu lobhasahagatesv'eva labbhati.

(iii) Diṭṭhi catūsu diṭṭhigatasampayuttesu.

(iv) Māno catūsu diṭṭhigatavippayuttesu.

(v) Doso, issā, macchariyaṃ, kukkuccañ ca dvīsu paṭighasampayuttacittesu.

(vi) Thīnaṃ, middhaṃ pañcasu sasaṅkhārikacittesu.

(vii) Vicikicchā vicikicchāsahagatacitte yeva labbhatī ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

(i) Among the unwholesome factors, these four — delusion, shamelessness, fearlessness of wrongdoing, and restlessness — are called universal to the unwholesome. They are found in all twelve unwholesome cittas.

(ii) Greed is found only in the eight cittas accompanied by greed.

(iii) Wrong view [is found] in the four associated with wrong view.

(iv) Conceit [is found] in the four dissociated from wrong view.

(v) Hatred, envy, avarice, and worry [are found] in the two cittas associated with resistance.

(vi) Sloth and torpor [are found] in the five prompted cittas.

(vii) Doubt is found only in the citta accompanied by doubt.

Pali Source

Sabbāpuññesu cattāro lobhamūle tayo gatā
Dosamūlesu cattāro sasaṅkhāre dvayaṃ tathā
Vicikicchā vicikicchācitte cā ti catuddasa
Dvādas'ākusalesv'eva sampayujjanti pañcadhā.

Ru-Yi Translation

Four are found in all unwholesome states; three, in those rooted in greed; four, in those rooted in hatred; and likewise two, in the prompted. Doubt is found in the citta accompanied by doubt. Thus these fourteen are associated with the twelve unwholesome cittas alone, in five ways.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
sabbākusalasādhāraṇa

"common to all the unwholesome" — the four-factor core (moha, ahirika, anottappa, uddhacca) present in every unwholesome citta without exception, parallel in structure to the seven citta-universals.

sasaṅkhārikaadj.

prompted — the same term from Chapter 1's greed- and hatred-rooted cittas. Sloth and torpor need a citta already "induced," never a spontaneous one.

Association — The Beautiful Factors

The beautiful factors are analyzed in the same four sub-groups as their own enumeration: the 19 universals, the 3 abstinences, the 2 illimitables, and wisdom on its own.

Pali Source

(i) Sobhanesu pana sobhanasādhāraṇā tāva ekūnavīsat'ime cetasikā sabbesu pi ekūnasaṭṭhi sobhanacittesu saṃvijjanti.

(ii) Viratiyo pana tisso pi lokuttaracittesu sabbathā pi niyatā ekato'va labbhanti. Lokiyesu pana kāmāvacarakusalesv'eva kadāci sandissanti visuṃ visuṃ.

(iii) Appamaññāyo pana dvādasasu pañcamajjhānavajjita-mahaggatacittesu c'eva kāmāvacarakusalesu ca sahetuka-kāmāvacarakiriyacittesu cā ti—aṭṭhavīsaticittesv'eva—kadāci nānā hutvā jāyanti.

(iv) Paññā pana dvādasasu ñāṇasampayutta-kāmāvacaracittesu c'eva sabbesu pañcatiṃsa mahaggata-lokuttaracittesu cā ti sattacattārīsa cittesu sampayogaṃ gacchatī ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

(i) Among the beautiful factors, first, these nineteen universal beautiful factors are present in all fifty-nine beautiful cittas.

(ii) The three abstinences, in the supramundane cittas, are always found together, fixed as one. But in the mundane, they appear only sometimes, and only separately, in the sense-sphere wholesome cittas.

(iii) The illimitables arise, at times and separately, in twenty-eight cittas: the twelve sublime cittas excluding the fifth jhāna, together with the sense-sphere wholesome and the sense-sphere functional-with-roots cittas.

(iv) Wisdom enters into association with forty-seven cittas: the twelve sense-sphere cittas associated with knowledge, together with all thirty-five sublime and supramundane cittas.

Pali Source

Ekūnavīsati dhammā jāyant'ekūnasaṭṭhisu
Tayo soḷasacittesu aṭṭhavīsatiyaṃ dvayaṃ
Paññā pakāsitā sattacattārīsavidhesu pi
Sampayuttā catudh'evaṃ sobhanesv'eva sobhanā.

Ru-Yi Translation

Nineteen states arise in fifty-nine cittas, three in sixteen, and two in twenty-eight; wisdom is declared to be in forty-seven. Thus the beautiful are associated only with the beautiful, in four ways.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
niyataadj.

fixed, certain — the abstinences in supramundane cittas are niyata: all three or none, never a partial set, unlike their occasional, one-at-a-time appearance in mundane virtue.

visuṃ visuṃ

"separately, one by one" — the pattern for the 16 mundane occurrences of the abstinences (8 wholesome + 8 functional cittas): only ever one abstinence at a time, never two together.

Fixed and Unfixed Adjuncts — Turning to Combination

Before switching methods, the chapter names which cetasikas are "fixed" — invariably present wherever they occur at all — and which are "unfixed," appearing only sometimes even within their proper citta.

Pali Source

Issā-macchera-kukkucca-viratī-karuṇādayo
Nānā kadāci māno ca thīna-middhaṃ tathā saha.
Yathāvuttānusārena sesā niyatayogino
Sangahañ ca pavakkhāmi tesaṃ dāni yathārahaṃ.

Ru-Yi Translation

Envy, avarice, worry, the abstinences, and compassion and the rest [appreciative joy] arise separately and only at times; so does conceit; so too, together, do sloth and torpor. The remaining factors, following what has been said, are fixed adjuncts. I shall now speak, accordingly, of their combination.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
aniyatayogī / niyatayogī

unfixed adjunct / fixed adjunct — 11 factors named here (envy, avarice, worry, the 3 abstinences, the 2 illimitables, conceit, sloth, torpor) are unfixed; the remaining 41 of the 52 are fixed wherever they belong.

11 unfixed + 41 fixed = 52. The chapter now shifts from association (cetasika → citta) to combination (citta → cetasika).

Combination — The Supramundane Cittas

The saṅgahanaya begins where Chapter 1 ended: with the eight supramundane cittas, graded fivefold by jhāna level exactly as before.

Pali Source

Chattiṃs'ānuttare dhammā pañcatiṃsa mahaggate
Aṭṭhatiṃsā pi labbhanti kāmāvacarasobhane.
Sattavīsaty'apuññamhi dvādas'āhetuke ti ca
Yathāsambhavayogena pañcadhā tattha sangaho.

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirty-six states are found in the supramundane; thirty-five, in the sublime; thirty-eight, in the sense-sphere beautiful. Twenty-seven, in the unwholesome; twelve, in the rootless. According to how they arise, the combination there is fivefold.

Pali Source

Kathaṃ? (i) Lokuttaresu tāva aṭṭhasu paṭhamajjhānikacittesu aññasamānā terasa cetasikā appamaññāvajjitā tevīsati sobhanacetasikā cā ti chattiṃsa dhammā sangahaṃ gacchanti.

(ii) Tathā dutiyajjhānikacittesu vitakkavajjā.

(iii) Tatiyajjhānikacittesu vitakka-vicāravajjā.

(iv) Catutthajjhānikacittesu vitakka-vicāra-pītivajjā.

(v) Pañcamajjhānikacittesu pi upekkhāsahagatā te eva sangayhantī ti. Sabbathā pi aṭṭhasu lokuttaracittesu pañcakajjhānavasena pañcadhā va sangaho hotī ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

How? (i) First, in the eight supramundane first-jhāna cittas, thirty-six states combine: the thirteen ethically-variable factors, together with the twenty-three beautiful factors excluding the two illimitables.

(ii) Likewise in the second-jhāna cittas, excluding initial application [35].

(iii) In the third-jhāna cittas, excluding initial and sustained application [34].

(iv) In the fourth-jhāna cittas, excluding initial application, sustained application, and zest [33].

(v) In the fifth-jhāna cittas too, those same [factors], accompanied by equanimity, combine [33]. Thus altogether, in the eight supramundane cittas, the combination is fivefold by way of the five jhānas.

Pali Source

Chattiṃsa pañcatiṃsā ca catuttiṃsa yathākkamaṃ
Tettiṃsa dvayam icc'evaṃ pañcadh'ānuttare ṭhitā.

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirty-six, thirty-five, and thirty-four in order, then thirty-three twice over: thus they stand, five ways, in the supramundane.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
sangahaṃ gacchati

"goes into combination" — the fixed idiom this whole section repeats for every citta group: literally "arrives at inclusion."

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The illimitables are excluded from every supramundane citta, at every jhāna level — the only such total exclusion in this section — because compassion and appreciative joy always take living beings as their object, while every supramundane citta takes Nibbāna. This is the clean logical consequence of what the association section already established: the illimitables were said to arise only in 28 specific mundane and sublime cittas, and none of the 40 supramundane cittas was ever on that list.

Combination — The Sublime (Fine-Material and Immaterial) Cittas

The same fivefold jhāna-grading now applies to the 27 mundane sublime cittas (15 fine-material + 12 immaterial, counted together as mahaggata, "grown great").

Pali Source

Mahaggatesu pana: (i) Tīsu paṭhamajjhānikacittesu tāva aññasamānā terasa cetasikā viratittayavajjitā dvāvīsati sobhanacetasikā cā ti pañcatiṃsa dhammā sangahaṃ gacchanti. Karuṇā-muditā pan'ettha paccekam eva yojetabbā.

(ii) Tathā dutiyajjhānikacittesu vitakkavajjā.

(iii) Tatiyajjhānikacittesu vitakka-vicāravajjā.

(iv) Catutthajjhānikacittesu vitakka-vicāra-pītivajjā.

(v) Pañcamajjhānikacittesu pana paṇṇarasasu appamaññāyo na labbhantī ti. Sabbathā pi sattavīsati mahaggatacittesu pañcakajjhānavasena pañcadhā va sangaho hotī ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the sublime cittas: (i) first, in the three first-jhāna cittas, thirty-five states combine: the thirteen ethically-variable factors, together with the twenty-two beautiful factors excluding the three abstinences. Here compassion and appreciative joy are to be added in one at a time.

(ii) Likewise in the second-jhāna cittas, excluding initial application [34].

(iii) In the third-jhāna cittas, excluding initial and sustained application [33].

(iv) In the fourth-jhāna cittas, excluding initial application, sustained application, and zest [32].

(v) In the fifteen fifth-jhāna cittas, the illimitables are not obtained at all [30]. Thus altogether, in the twenty-seven sublime cittas, the combination is fivefold by way of the five jhānas.

Pali Source

Pañcatiṃsa catuttiṃsa tettiṃsa ca yathākkamaṃ
Battiṃsa c'eva tiṃseti pañcadhā va mahaggate.

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirty-five, thirty-four, and thirty-three in order, then thirty-two, then thirty: five ways in the sublime.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
mahaggataadj.

"grown great, sublime" — mahā + gata. Chapter 1's collective term for the fine-material and immaterial spheres together, reused here as the combination method's own organizing category.

paccekam yojetabbā

"should be joined one at a time" — how compassion and appreciative joy enter these cittas: never together, since one commiserates with suffering and the other rejoices at success, contrary modes that cannot coexist.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Compare this section's exclusions to the supramundane section just above, and the difference in what each jhāna level drops is identical — vitakka at the second jhāna, vicāra at the third, pīti at the fourth — because both sets of cittas are graded by the very same five-jhāna scale set out in Chapter 1. What differs is only the starting count (36 vs. 35) and what gets excluded from every level without exception: illimitables from the supramundane, abstinences from the sublime. The abstinences make sense to exclude here for the same structural reason as the illimitables above — deliberate restraint from a specific wrongdoing has no place in a citta absorbed in jhāna, since no opportunity for transgression is even present to the mind.

Combination — The Sense-Sphere Beautiful Cittas

The 24 sense-sphere beautiful cittas are analyzed by their own four "couplets" (prompted/unprompted pairs), repeated across wholesome, resultant, and functional — a twelvefold combination overall.

Pali Source

Kāmāvacara-sobhanesu pana kusalesu tāva paṭhamadvaye aññasamānā terasa cetasikā pañcavīsati sobhanacetasikā cā ti aṭṭhatiṃsa dhammā sangahaṃ gacchanti. Appamaññā viratiyo pan'ettha pañca pi paccekam eva yojetabbā.

(ii) Tathā dutiyadvaye ñāṇavajjitā. (iii) Tatiyadvaye ñāṇasampayuttā pītivajjitā. (iv) Catutthadvaye ñāṇapītivajjitā te eva sangayhanti.

Kiriyacittesu pi virativajjitā tath'eva catūsu pi dukesu catudhā va sangayhanti. Tathā vipākesu ca appamañña-virati-vajjitā te eva sangayhantī ti. Sabbathā pi catuvīsati kāmāvacara-sobhanacittesu dukavasena dvādasadhā va sangaho hotī ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the sense-sphere beautiful cittas, first in the wholesome: in the first couplet, thirty-eight states combine — the thirteen ethically-variable factors together with the twenty-five beautiful factors. Here the two illimitables and the three abstinences — five in all — are each to be added in one at a time.

(ii) Likewise in the second couplet, excluding knowledge [37]. (iii) In the third couplet, associated with knowledge, excluding zest [37]. (iv) In the fourth couplet, those same, excluding both knowledge and zest [36].

In the functional cittas, in the same four couplets, those combine in the same four ways, except that the abstinences are excluded [35, 34, 34, 33]. So too in the resultant cittas, those combine, except that both the illimitables and the abstinences are excluded [33, 32, 32, 31]. Thus altogether, in the twenty-four sense-sphere beautiful cittas, the combination is twelvefold by way of the couplets.

Pali Source

Aṭṭhatiṃsa sattatiṃsa dvayaṃ chattiṃsakaṃ subhe
Pañcatiṃsa catuttiṃsa dvayaṃ tettiṃsakaṃ kriye.
Tettiṃsa pāke battiṃsa dvay'ekatiṃsakaṃ bhave
Sahetukāmāvacara puñña-pāka-kriyā mane.

Na vijjant'ettha viratī kriyāsu ca mahaggate
Anuttare appamaññā kāmapāke dvayaṃ tathā.
Anuttare jhānadhammā appamaññā ca majjhime
Viratī ñāṇapīti ca parittesu visesakā.

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirty-eight, then twice thirty-seven, then thirty-six, in the wholesome; thirty-five, then twice thirty-four, then thirty-three, in the functional; thirty-three, then twice thirty-two, then thirty-one, in the resultant — thus [runs the count] for the sense-sphere mind with roots, wholesome, resultant, and functional.

Here the abstinences are absent in the functional and in the sublime; the illimitables, in the supramundane; and both together, in the sense-sphere resultant. In the supreme [supramundane], the jhāna factors mark the distinctions; in the middling [sublime], the illimitables [and jhāna factors]; and in the limited [sense-sphere beautiful], the abstinences, knowledge, and zest.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
dvaya / duka

couplet, pair — the prompted/unprompted pairing that structures this whole section; since the two never differ in cetasika content, the text treats each pair as a single unit.

parittaadj.

"limited" — this section's own poetic term for the sense sphere, contrasted with majjhima ("middling," the sublime) and anuttara ("supreme," the supramundane) in the closing verse's three-tier summary.

Combination — The Twelve Unwholesome Cittas

The unwholesome cittas are analyzed root by root, exactly following Chapter 1's own ordering: greed, then hatred, then delusion.

Pali Source

Akusalesu pana lobhamūlesu tāva paṭhame asaṅkhārike aññasamānā terasa cetasikā akusalasādhāraṇā cattāro cā ti sattarasa lobhadiṭṭhīhi saddhiṃ ekūnavīsati dhammā sangahaṃ gacchanti.

(ii) Tath'eva dutiye asaṅkhārike lobhamānena. (iii) Tatiye tath'eva pītivajjitā lobhadiṭṭhīhi saha aṭṭhārasa. (iv) Catutthe tath'eva lobhamānena.

(v) Pañcame pana paṭighasampayutte asaṅkhārike doso issā macchariyaṃ kukkuccañ cā ti catūhi saddhiṃ pītivajjitā te eva vīsati dhammā sangayhanti. Issā-macchariya-kukkuccāni pan'ettha paccekam eva yojetabbāni.

(vi) Sasaṅkhārikapañcake pi tath'eva thīna-middhena visesetvā yojetabbā.

(vii) Chanda-pīti-vajjitā pana aññasamānā ekādasa akusalasādhāraṇā cattāro cā ti paṇṇarasa dhammā uddhaccasahagate sampayujjanti.

(viii) Vicikicchāsahagatacitte ca adhimokkhavirahitā vicikicchāsahagatā tath'eva paṇṇarasa dhammā samupalabbhantī ti. Sabbathā pi dvādas'ākusalacittuppādesu paccekaṃ yojiyamānā pi gaṇanavasena sattadhā va sangahitā bhavantī ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the unwholesome, first in those rooted in greed: in the first, unprompted [citta], nineteen states combine — the thirteen ethically-variable factors together with the four universal unwholesome factors [seventeen], together with greed and wrong view.

(ii) Likewise in the second, unprompted, with greed and conceit [nineteen]. (iii) In the third, likewise, excluding zest, with greed and wrong view [eighteen]. (iv) In the fourth, likewise, with greed and conceit [eighteen].

(v) In the fifth — unprompted, associated with resistance — those same [seventeen], excluding zest, combine together with these four: hatred, envy, avarice, and worry [twenty]. Here envy, avarice, and worry are each to be added in one at a time.

(vi) In the five prompted cittas, [the count proceeds] likewise, distinguished by the addition of sloth and torpor.

(vii) In the citta accompanied by restlessness, excluding desire and zest, fifteen states combine — eleven ethically-variable factors together with the four universal unwholesome factors.

(viii) In the citta accompanied by doubt, lacking decision, that same fifteen — accompanied by doubt — are obtained. Thus altogether, in the twelve unwholesome cittas, though each is composed individually, by count they fall into seven kinds of combination.

Pali Source

Ekūnavīs'aṭṭhārasa vīs'ekavīsa vīsati
Dvāvīsa paṇṇarase ti sattadh'ākusale ṭhitā.
Sādhāraṇā ca cattāro samānā ca dasā'pare
Cuddas'ete pavuccanti sabbākusalayogino.

Ru-Yi Translation

Nineteen, eighteen, twenty, twenty-one, twenty, twenty-two, fifteen: thus they stand, seven ways, in the unwholesome. The four universals, and ten others held in common: these fourteen are declared to be joined with all the unwholesome.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
visesetvā

"having distinguished, with this difference" — the marker word the text uses to say the five prompted greed-cittas repeat the unprompted pattern exactly, with one addition: sloth and torpor.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The closing verse's last line quietly corrects a number that might look right but isn't: the "fourteen joined with all the unwholesome" is not the same fourteen as the fourteen unwholesome cetasikas enumerated earlier in the chapter. It is the four universal-unwholesome factors plus ten of the thirteen ethically-variable factors (all except chanda, desire, and pīti, zest, which the eleventh and twelfth combinations above just excluded) — a genuinely different set of fourteen that happens to share the same count. The text trusts the reader to notice this from the arithmetic alone.

Combination — The Eighteen Rootless Cittas

The chapter's last combination group returns to Chapter 1's rootless cittas — the weakest, most passive class — where the count of combining factors never exceeds twelve.

Pali Source

Ahetukesu pana hasanacitte tāva chandavajjitā aññasamānā dvādasa dhammā sangahaṃ gacchanti.

(ii) Tathā votthapane chanda-pīti-vajjitā. (iii) Sukhasantīraṇe chanda-viriya-vajjitā. (iv) Manodhātuttika-ahetukapaṭisandhiyugale chanda-pīti-viriya-vajjitā. (v) Dvipañcaviññāṇe pakiṇṇakavajjitā te yeva sangayhantī ti. Sabbathā pi aṭṭhārasasu ahetukesu gaṇanavasena catudhā va sangaho hotī ti.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the rootless, first in the smile-producing citta: twelve ethically-variable factors, excluding desire, combine.

(ii) Likewise in the determining citta, excluding desire and zest. (iii) In the joy-accompanied investigating citta, excluding desire and energy. (iv) In the triple mind-element and the pair of rootless rebirth-linking cittas, excluding desire, zest, and energy. (v) In the two sets of fivefold sense-consciousness, those same [factors], excluding all the occasionals, combine. Thus altogether, in the eighteen rootless cittas, by count, the combination falls into four kinds.

Pali Source

Dvādas'ekādasa dasa satta cā ti catubbidho
Aṭṭhāras'āhetukesu cittuppādesu sangaho.
Ahetukesu sabbattha satta sesā yathārahaṃ
Iti vitthārato vutto tettiṃsavidhasangaho.

Ru-Yi Translation

Twelve, eleven, ten, and seven: thus the combination among the eighteen rootless cittas is fourfold. Among all the rootless, the seven [universals are always present]; the rest, as fitting. Thus, in detail, the combination has been told in thirty-three ways.

Key vocabulary — for Pāli learners
votthapanant.

determining — another name for the mind-door-adverting citta when it occurs within a five-door process, met already in Chapter 1's cognitive-process cittas.

manodhātuttika

"the mind-element triad" — a collective name for three cittas together: five-door-adverting and the two kinds of receiving consciousness.

tettiṃsavidha

"thirty-three-fold" — the chapter's own final tally of every distinct combination pattern counted across this whole method: 5 (supramundane) + 5 (sublime) + 12 (sense-sphere beautiful) + 7 (unwholesome) + 4 (rootless) = 33.

Cetasikasaṅgaha Complete

The chapter closes with a verse directing the student back to both methods together, then its own colophon.

Pali Source

Itthaṃ cittāviyuttānaṃ sampayogañ ca sangahaṃ
Ñatvā bhedaṃ yathāyogaṃ cittena samam uddise.

Iti Abhidhammatthasaṅgahe Cetasikasaṅgahavibhāgo nāma dutiyo paricchedo.

Ru-Yi Translation

Having thus understood the association and the combination of these mind-conjoined states, one should point out their division, each in its fitting way, together with its consciousness.

Thus ends, in the Compendium of the Things Contained in the Abhidhamma, the second chapter, called the Compendium of Mental Factors.

This closes the translation of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha's second chapter, Cetasikasaṅgaha ("The Compendium of Mental Factors") — all 52 cetasikas enumerated in their seven traditional groups, then examined by the method of association (which cittas each factor joins) and the method of combination (which factors compose each citta), thirty-three combination patterns in all. The third chapter, Pakiṇṇakasaṅgaha — a further sixfold cross-analysis of consciousness by root, feeling, function, door, object, and physical base — continues from here, the next undertaking for this translation.
