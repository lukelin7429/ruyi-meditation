Lesson 13 of the Pāli Course · Verbs

Optative, Imperative & Future

Three more ways to bend a verb, each far more regular than Lesson 12's aorist: commands, wishes, and the future. Each simply inserts its own marker between the stem and Lesson 11's familiar personal endings.

Level: beginner Prerequisite: Lesson 12 Part II · Lesson 9

The Imperative: Commands

Built on the present stem, with its own set of endings — the second-person singular is simply the bare stem itself (or the stem plus -hi), the most direct way to give an order in the language.

PersonSingularPlural
1stpassāmilet me seepassāmalet us see
2ndpassa, passāhisee!passathasee! (to many)
3rdpassatulet him/her seepassantulet them see
Reading note — Ru-Yi

The most famous imperative in the entire Canon is two words long: "Ehi bhikkhu" — "Come, monk!" — the formula by which the Buddha himself ordained his earliest disciples, simply by speaking it. Ehi is the 2nd singular imperative of eti, "to come/go" (an irregular verb whose imperative happens to be one of the most memorable words in the language). Some early ordination accounts continue with "cara brahmacariyaṃ" — "practice/wander the holy life" — where cara is the 2nd singular imperative of carati, "to walk, practice," following exactly the regular pattern in the table above.

The Optative: Wishes and Possibilities

Marked by inserting -eyya- before the ending — the single most recognizable marker in this lesson. It covers English "would," "should," "may," and polite requests.

PersonSingularPlural
1stpasseyyaṃI would/should seepasseyyāmawe would see
2ndpasseyyāsiyou would seepasseyyāthayou would see
3rdpasseyyahe/she would seepasseyyuṃthey would see
...na paribhaveyya kañci kvaci...
"...one should not despise anyone, anywhere..." — from the Karaṇīyamettā Sutta, the classic public-domain verse on loving-kindness. Paribhaveyya is a 3rd singular optative, carrying the "should/would" sense this whole verse of ethical instruction depends on.

The Future

Marked by inserting -ssa- before the ending, then conjugating exactly like the present tense in Lesson 11 — the most mechanically regular of everything in this lesson.

PersonSingularPlural
1stpassissāmiI will seepassissāmawe will see
2ndpassissasiyou will seepassissathayou will see
3rdpassissatihe/she will seepassissantithey will see
So dhammaṃ desessati.
"He will teach the Dhamma." (deseti + future -ssa-, still recognizable as the same causative-class verb noted in Lesson 11)

Practice: Naming the Mood/Tense

  1. gaccha! — (bare command)
  2. gaccheyyāma
  3. gamissanti
  4. gacchatu
Check your answers

1. Imperative, 2nd singular — "Go!"

2. Optative, 1st plural — "we should/would go."

3. Future, 3rd plural — "they will go."

4. Imperative, 3rd singular — "let him go."
