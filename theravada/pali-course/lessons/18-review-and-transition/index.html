Lesson 18 of the Pāli Course · Part II complete

Review and Transition to Reading

Thirteen lessons ago, Lesson 4 asked you to read Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa using only sounds and syllables — every word a fixed unit to memorize, not yet something you could analyze. This lesson returns to that exact sentence and takes it apart completely, using every tool Part II has given you.

Level: beginner Prerequisite: Lesson 17 Part II · Lesson 14 of 14

Part II, in Thirteen Lessons

Lessons 5–8 — NounsGrammatical case as endings, not word order; the a-stem and ā-stem paradigms; i-stem and u-stem by recognition.
Lessons 9–10 — PronounsPersonal (ahaṃ, tvaṃ), demonstrative/3rd-person (ta-), interrogative (ka-), relative (ya-) and the yo...so correlative.
Lessons 11–14 — VerbsPresent, aorist (by recognition), optative/imperative/future, participles and the absolutive -tvā.
Lessons 15–17 — Word-buildingSandhi at word boundaries; all six compound types, tappurisa through avyayībhāva.

Capstone: Namo Tassa, Fully Analyzed

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa.
"Homage to Him, the Blessed One, the Worthy One, the Perfectly Self-Awakened One."
Namo
Indeclinable, "homage" — governs a dative object (Lesson 5: the dative marks a recipient).
tassa
Demonstrative ta- (Lesson 9), masculine dative/genitive singular — "to him." Pāli's only third-person pronoun, doing double duty as "that."
bhagavato
Irregular -ant-stem noun (flagged in Lesson 9 as a special class), dative/genitive singular — "to the Blessed One," agreeing with tassa in case and number.
arahato
Same irregular declension pattern as bhagavato — "to the Worthy One."
sammāsambuddhassa
An a-stem noun (Lesson 6's paradigm exactly), dative/genitive singular ending -assa — but also a kammadhāraya compound (Lesson 16): sammā ("rightly") describing sambuddha ("awakened one"). And sambuddha itself is saṃ + buddha, the exact niggahīta-assimilation sandhi rule from Lesson 3 and formalized in Lesson 15.
Reading note — Ru-Yi

Five words, five different lessons converging: a pronoun's case (5, 9), an irregular noun class flagged early and never forgotten (9), a regular noun paradigm (6), a compound type (16), and a sandhi rule (3, 15). This is what "grammar before vocabulary breadth" was for — you didn't need eighteen lessons to read five new words; you needed them to read these same five words completely, for the first time.

Part III Begins Now

The reading track opens immediately after this lesson.
