Why the Fruit Is Called Śramaṇa

Fascicle 66 asks why fruits realized by a worldly path — once-returning and non-returning — still earn the name fruit of a śramaṇa, works through King Ajātaśatru's sutra on the fruit visible here and now and the Lion's Roar, Bhadrika, and Cunda sutras on the four true śramaṇas, offers twenty-two independent reasons why renunciation alone is praised as a kindness impossible to repay, and closes with the systematic ledger of exactly which learner- and undefiled dharmas belong to which of the four fruits. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Why Worldly-Path Fruits Still Bear the Name Śramaṇa

Opening the fourth part of the Sentient-Being Nyāsa, the treatise asks why the fruits of once-returning and non-returning — realized through a worldly rather than an undefiled path — are nonetheless called fruits of a śramaṇa. Three proposed answers involving a future undefiled path, a constantly-continuing undefiled attainment, and the vajra-like absorption are each refuted in turn, before the treatise settles on its own resolution: the name is simply established from the majority, since most of what these two fruits contain was in fact attained by the noble path itself.

Chinese Source

結蘊第二中有情納息第三之四問前說聖道是沙門性有為無漏。及諸擇滅是此果故名沙門果。無漏道力所證得者可立此名。世俗道力所證得者如一來不還果。云何名沙門果。答如無漏道離欲界染若倍若全立果分齊。如是聖者以世俗道離欲界染。若倍若全亦立一來及不還果。故二亦得沙門果名。尊者僧伽筏蘇說曰。以世俗道離欲染時亦修未來。諸無漏道所得二果是彼果故。亦得建立沙門果名。彼不應作是說。所以者何。未來聖道未有作用。如何於彼此得果名。有餘師說。以世俗道離欲染時無漏道得恒相續轉。所得二果是彼果故。亦得建立沙門果名。彼不應作是說。所以者何。得無斷結證斷作用。如何於彼此得果名。復有說者。以世俗道得二果者。金剛喻定現在前時。總證三界見修所斷一味斷得。前所得斷是此果故名沙門果。此定是真沙門性故。彼亦不應作如是說。所以者何。得二果時未得此定。若得此定失彼果名。如何彼二名沙門果。應作是說。從多立名多是聖道所得果故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Continuing the Sentient-Being Nyāsa within the second, the Fetters Aggregate, part four. One asks: it was earlier stated that the noble path is the very nature of a śramaṇa, conditioned and undefiled, and that the cessations-through-discernment are this path's own fruit, and are for this reason called the fruit of a śramaṇa — this name may properly be established for what is realized by the power of an undefiled path. But for what is realized by the power of a worldly path, as with the fruit of once-returning or of non-returning, how can this too be named a fruit of a śramaṇa? The answer: exactly as an undefiled path becoming free of the desire realm's own defilement, whether by half or wholly, establishes the fruit's own precise boundary, so too does a noble one, becoming free of the desire realm's own defilement by a worldly path, whether by half or wholly, likewise establish the fruit of once-returning and of non-returning — and it is for this reason that both may also receive the name fruit of a śramaṇa. The Venerable Saṃghavasu says as follows: at the moment a worldly path becomes free of desire's own defilement, one also cultivates a future undefiled path, and the two fruits it attains are that path's own fruit — hence they too may properly be established under the name fruit of a śramaṇa. This ought not to be said, for what reason? Because a future path has not yet come into operation — how, then, could a fruit-name be established with reference to it? Other masters say: at the moment a worldly path becomes free of desire's own defilement, an undefiled attainment turns in constant, unbroken continuity, and the two fruits it attains are that attainment's own fruit — hence they too may properly be established under the name fruit of a śramaṇa. This too ought not to be said, for what reason? Because it is the very function of cutting off without interruption, and realizing this cutting-off, that is meant here — how, then, could a fruit-name be established with reference to that other attainment? There are yet others who say: as for one who attains the two fruits by a worldly path, at the moment the vajra-like absorption becomes presently manifest, one comprehensively realizes, in a single flavor, the cutting-off abandoned by both seeing and cultivation across the three realms — the cutting-off previously attained is this very absorption's own fruit, and is therefore called the fruit of a śramaṇa, since this absorption is the true nature of a śramaṇa. This too ought not to be said in this way, for what reason? Because, at the moment the two fruits are attained, this absorption has not yet been attained — and, once this absorption is attained, the name of those two fruits is lost. How, then, could those two be named the fruit of a śramaṇa? One should instead say: the name is established from the majority, since the majority is what is attained by the power of the noble path.

謂世俗道得二果時。三界一切見所斷斷。皆是聖道力所得故名沙門果。雖有欲界六品九品修所斷斷非聖道得。然從多分亦得建立沙門果名一無漏得總所得故。

That is, at the moment the two fruits are attained by a worldly path, the cutting-off of every affliction abandoned by seeing across the three realms is, in every case, attained by the power of the noble path, and it is for this reason that they are named fruit of a śramaṇa. Although there is, in the desire realm, the cutting-off of six or nine grades of affliction abandoned by cultivation that is not attained by the noble path, still, from the majority, the name fruit of a śramaṇa may likewise properly be established, since a single undefiled attainment comprehensively gathers all that is attained.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The refutation pattern here is worth noticing for its own sake: each rejected theory tries to locate some undefiled element precisely at the moment of attainment to justify the name, and each fails for the same structural reason — whatever undefiled element is invoked either does not yet exist, does not yet function, or belongs to a different attainment than the fruit it is meant to explain. The treatise's own answer sidesteps this entirely by appealing to proportion rather than to any single decisive undefiled component.

2. Nominal Fruits and Fruits of Real Meaning

The treatise classifies its four fruits into two nominal fruits — once-returning and non-returning — and two fruits of real meaning — stream-entry and arhatship — offering four independent rationales for the split: proportion of worldly versus undefiled attainment, joint versus sole possession, provisional shared declaration between two paths, and vehicle-restriction to disciples alone. A final proposed rationale based on majority scope (disciple, solitary-realizer, and Buddha all attaining the last two fruits) is rejected by the compilers as failing to track the real nominal/real-meaning distinction at all.

Chinese Source

問如是所說四沙門果幾是假名。幾是實義。答二是假名。謂一來不還果。二是實義。謂預流阿羅漢果。問何故一來不還果名假名果。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: of these four fruits of a śramaṇa thus stated, how many are nominal designations, and how many bear real meaning? The answer: two are nominal designations, namely, the fruits of once-returning and of non-returning; two bear real meaning, namely, the fruits of stream-entry and of arhatship. One asks: for what reason are the fruits of once-returning and of non-returning named nominal fruits, while

預流阿羅漢果名實義果耶。答諸世俗道是假名道。中間二果少彼所得。以多從少名假名果。諸無漏道是實義道。初後二果全彼所得。

the fruits of stream-entry and of arhatship are named fruits of real meaning? The answer: every worldly path is a nominal path, and the middle two fruits are but a lesser portion of what it attains — since the greater part follows the lesser, they are named nominal fruits. Every undefiled path is a path of real meaning, and the first and last fruits are wholly attained by it,

故初後果名實義果。復次中間二果有漏無漏二道共得名假名果。假名即是共所得義。如共有者名假名物。初後二果唯是無漏道力所得名實義果。實義即是獨所得義。如獨有者名實義物。復次中間二果世俗聖道假設名言而證得故。名假名果。謂彼二道在未來時假作義言。既同一事誓一所作一應隨喜。初後二果唯聖道得。非由二道假設名言而證得故。名實義果。有餘師說。假名果者。謂初二果唯聲聞乘所證得故。實義果者。謂後二果一切聲聞獨覺大覺皆證得故。

and it is for this reason that the first and last fruits are named fruits of real meaning. Further, the middle two fruits are jointly attained by both the contaminated and the undefiled path, and are therefore named nominal fruits — nominal designation being precisely the meaning of what is jointly attained, exactly as a thing held jointly is called a nominal possession. The first and last fruits are attained solely by the power of the undefiled path, and are therefore named fruits of real meaning — real meaning being precisely the meaning of what is attained alone, exactly as a thing held alone is called a possession of real meaning. Further, the middle two fruits are realized through the worldly and the noble path provisionally making a shared declaration, and are therefore named nominal fruits — that is, these two paths, while still future, provisionally make the declaration that, sharing this one same task, vowing this one same undertaking, they shall rejoice together in this one same outcome. The first and last fruits are attained solely by the noble path, not realized through any two paths provisionally making a shared declaration, and are therefore named fruits of real meaning. Other masters say: the nominal fruits are so called because the first two fruits are realized solely by the vehicle of the disciple; the fruits of real meaning are so called because the latter two fruits are realized by every disciple, solitary-realizer, and greatly-awakened one alike —

多是實義少是假名。評曰。彼不應作是說。少多非表假實義故。此中前說於理為善。所以者何。沙門果者。是沙門性力所引得。唯無漏道是沙門性。成就彼者名真沙門。故彼所得二果名實義沙門果。

the majority thus bearing real meaning, the minority bearing nominal designation. The compilers say: this ought not to be said, since majority and minority do not express the distinction between nominal and real meaning. Of these, the position stated first is the sound one, for what reason? Because the fruit of a śramaṇa is what is drawn forth by the very power of śramaṇahood, and only the undefiled path is śramaṇahood — one who possesses it is named a true śramaṇa, and it is for this reason that the two fruits it attains are named fruits of a śramaṇa with real meaning,

諸世俗道非沙門性。成就彼者名假沙門。故彼所得二果名假名沙門果。問道是有為有下中上隨因力生可名為果。斷是無為無下中上非因所生云何名果。答斷雖不生而是所得加行證故亦名為果。謂瑜伽師住高山頂或居靜室。節量飲食減省睡眠及資身具。受持小七大七日法頂安定鎮行毱法杖。從今日沒至明日出。發起殊勝勇猛精進。

while every worldly path is not śramaṇahood, and one who possesses it is named a nominal śramaṇa, and it is for this reason that the two fruits it attains are named nominal fruits of a śramaṇa. One asks: a path is conditioned, possessing lower, middle, and upper grades arising according to the power of its cause, and so may properly be named a fruit — but a cutting-off is unconditioned, possessing no lower, middle, or upper grade, and is not born of any cause, so how can it be named a fruit? The answer: although a cutting-off does not arise, it is nevertheless what is attained through effort and realization, and for this reason it too is named a fruit. That is, as when a practitioner of yoga dwells atop a high mountain, or abides in a quiet cell, moderating his food and drink, reducing his sleep and the requisites of his body, upholding the observances of the lesser and greater seven-day vigil, standing firm atop the peak, walking with the practice-staff in hand, from this day's setting sun until the next day's rising, giving rise to superior, courageous diligence,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The four accepted rationales are not really four different arguments but four ways of restating one underlying fact from different angles — that only an undefiled path is śramaṇahood proper — while the rejected fifth rationale is instructive precisely because it is superficially plausible yet orthogonal to the actual criterion: universality of attainment across vehicles has nothing to do with whether a fruit is jointly produced by two paths or solely by one.

3. The Ascetic's Effort and the Fruit's Many Classifications

An unconditioned cutting-off, possessing no arising and no cause, still earns the name fruit, the treatise argues, because it is the attained goal of genuine effort — illustrated by a mountaintop ascetic's austerities and a farmer's own six months of labor, both praised in the very same terms once their harvest is finally gathered in. The section then closes with a rapid-fire classification of the four fruits across dhyāna, the formless attainments, the path of seeing and cultivation, patient acceptance, cognition, and their finer subdivisions.

Chinese Source

展轉引生無漏聖道。由斯證得四沙門果。時示導者讚慰彼言。善哉善哉。汝能精進修正加行今得此果如務農者於六月中修治畦壠耘秋稼穡後收子實積置場中。舊務農者讚慰彼曰。善哉善哉。汝於六月多設劬勞今得此果。故道與斷俱得果名。如是所說四沙門果。問幾是靜慮果。答四是靜慮及眷屬果。問幾是無色果。答一。謂阿羅漢果。問幾是根本靜慮果。答二。謂不還果阿羅漢果。問幾是靜慮近分果。答四謂初靜慮近分非餘靜慮。中間如根本說。問幾是根本無色果。答一。謂阿羅漢果。問幾是無色近分果。答無。問幾是見道果。答三。謂除阿羅漢果。問幾是修道果。答三。謂除預流果。問幾是忍果。答三。謂除阿羅漢果。問幾是智果。答三謂除預流果。

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The farmer simile does real philosophical work here, not merely rhetorical decoration: it makes vivid exactly how an unconditioned cessation, which itself never arises or acts, can still be called a fruit in the ordinary sense that matters to the person who attained it — the grain in the granary is not itself an action, yet no one hesitates to call it the fruit of six months' labor.

4. King Ajātaśatru's Sutra on the Fruit Visible Here and Now

The treatise turns to the well-known sutra in which King Ajātaśatru asks the Buddha whether there is a fruit of a śramaṇa visible in this very life, answered with the parable of a royal servant who renounces the household and is thereafter honored rather than reclaimed — before the treatise clarifies that this fifth, visible fruit is not a real fifth fruit at all, but merely the provisional benefit accruing to renunciation itself, since the world mistakes the outward form of a monk for true śramaṇahood.

Chinese Source

如契經說。摩揭陀主吠提呬子未生怨王。來詣佛所到已頂禮世尊雙足退坐一面而白佛言。為有現見沙門果不。佛言亦有。王問云何。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the scripture states: Ajātaśatru, lord of Magadha, son of Vaidehī, came to where the Buddha was, and, having arrived, bowed his head to the World-Honored One's own two feet, withdrew, and sat to one side, saying to the Buddha: is there a fruit of a śramaṇa visible here and now? The Buddha said: there is indeed. The king asked: how so?

世尊告曰。我今問王。應隨意答。若王給侍或諸僮僕不自在者。有時見王昇高臺殿設五伎樂與諸眷屬歡娛嬉戲。彼既見已作是念言。

The World-Honored One told him: I shall now question the king — answer just as you see fit. Suppose one of the king's own attendants or servants, himself without freedom, at some time beholds the king ascending a lofty tower-hall, the five kinds of music arrayed, sporting and delighting together with his own retinue — having beheld this, he thinks to himself:

我亦是人如何不爾。然王宿世多修福業。故於今生受斯勝報。我於今者應修勝業亦當如王眾所欽羨。作是念已便捨家法。剃除鬚髮被服袈裟。受持三歸及清淨戒。於十業道能斷能修。王餘使人於外見已。尋還啟白具陳上事。請王追取如本驅策。王聞其言如所請不。

I too am a person — why should it not be so for me as well? And yet the king, in a former life, cultivated abundant merit, and it is for this reason that, in this present life, he receives so superior a reward; I too, in this present life, ought to cultivate superior deeds, that I too might be admired by the multitude just as the king is admired. Having thought this, he thereupon forsakes the household dharma, shaves off his hair and beard, dons the monastic robe, receives and upholds the three refuges and pure precepts, and, with respect to the ten courses of action, is able both to abandon and to cultivate them. One of the king's other servants, beholding this from outside, at once returns and reports, fully recounting the above matter, requesting that the king recall him and drive him back to his former servitude just as before. Having heard this report, would the king act just as requested, or not?

王言不也。若有此人我應往見禮敬供養。如彼本時供事於我。我於今者而供事之盡其形壽。施與衣服飲食醫藥房舍臥具。及諸資緣令無匱乏。佛告王曰。如此之事豈非現見沙門果耶。王白佛言。誠如聖教。問諸沙門果實唯有四。何故此經復說第五。答真沙門果實唯有四。此說現見沙門果者。但是出家近士用果。問出家既非真沙門性。如何說有沙門果耶。答出家雖非真沙門性。而世假立沙門性名。故諸世間見出家者。便謂我見如是沙門。是故出家近士用果。

The king said: no. Were there such a person, I would myself go to see him, revering and honoring him with offerings — exactly as he formerly served and attended upon me, so, in this present time, would I serve and attend upon him for the whole span of his life, bestowing upon him robes, food and drink, medicine, dwelling, and bedding, and every other requisite, so that he should lack for nothing. The Buddha told the king: is a matter such as this not itself a fruit of a śramaṇa visible here and now? The king said to the Buddha: it is truly as the sacred teaching says. One asks: the fruits of a śramaṇa are, in reality, only four in number — for what reason does this scripture speak further of a fifth? The answer: the true fruits of a śramaṇa are, in reality, only four; what this passage speaks of as a fruit of a śramaṇa visible here and now is merely the useful benefit accruing to renunciation as a lay devotee's own resource. One asks: since renunciation is not, in itself, the true nature of a śramaṇa, how can it be said to possess a fruit of a śramaṇa? The answer: although renunciation is not, in itself, the true nature of a śramaṇa, the world nevertheless provisionally establishes the name of śramaṇa-nature for it — and it is for this reason that, whenever the world beholds one who has renounced the household, it at once supposes: I here behold just such a śramaṇa. It is for this reason that renunciation, as a lay devotee's own resource,

亦得假立沙門果名。此現見名表非實義。師子吼經復作是說。唯我法內有四沙門。謂初沙門乃至第四。外道法內無真沙門及婆羅門。

likewise properly receives the provisional name fruit of a śramaṇa. This name 'visible here and now' expresses precisely what is not of real meaning. The Lion's Roar Sutra further states as follows: only within my own teaching are there four śramaṇas, namely, the first śramaṇa, up through the fourth; within the teaching of the outsiders there exists neither a true śramaṇa nor a true brāhmaṇa,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The king's own reaction — that he would honor, not reclaim, his former servant — is the entire argument in miniature: the visible fruit consists precisely in this shift of social regard, a real and observable change utterly independent of whether the renunciant has attained anything at all in the technical, fourfold sense the treatise has been defining throughout.

5. The Lion's Roar, Bhadrika, and Cunda Sutras on the Four Śramaṇas

Three scriptures are cited in turn on what makes a true śramaṇa: the Lion's Roar Sutra's claim that only the Buddha's own teaching contains four real śramaṇas, glossed as the four fruits themselves — in the treatise's own order by the Venerable Pārśva, in reverse, rank-first order by others; the Bhadrika Sutra's identification of the four śramaṇas with the four approaches wherever the eightfold path is found; and the Cunda Sutra's altogether different fourfold division into the śramaṇa of the superior path (the Buddha and solitary-realizers), the śramaṇa who shows the path (Śāriputra and the arhats), the śramaṇa who sustains the path (Ānanda and every learner), and the śramaṇa who defiles the path (exemplified by the wayward monk Mahālla).

7. The Corrupted Monk's Right View, and the Three People Hard to Repay

8. Twenty-Two Reasons Why Renunciation Alone Is Singled Out

A long cascade of independent reasons unfolds for why the sutra praises urging renunciation, and not merely urging lay precepts, as a kindness impossible to repay: renunciation is both cause and fruit of the monastic code, entry into the noble dharma in both its worldly and ultimate sense, freedom from menial affairs and from every suffering, the manifestation of the Buddha's own worldly and ultimate appearance, the study of his birth-body and dharma-body, entry into the ocean of his dharma, and the guarantee of direct realization of the four truths — before the cascade turns to a series of threefold attainments (moral codes, wholesome aggregates, paths, faculties, forms of awakening, kinds of sagehood), bodily and mental transformations, and finally the vivid simile of the painted conch shell, whose two rival glosses — durability against outer damage, or purity against inner staining — are anchored by Vasumitra's tale of the ancient seers Śaṅkha and Likhita and capped by the plain observation that even a precept-breaking monk still surpasses a precept-keeping layperson.

9. Seven Reasons Why Only the First and Last Fruits Are Named

Turning to why the hard-to-repay sutra names only the first and last of the four fruits — stream-entry and arhatship — while passing over once-returning and non-returning in silence, the treatise offers seven independent explanations: that the passage in fact comprehensively covers all four fruits already, since the pure dharma-eye covers the first three and the ending of contaminants the fourth; that naming the beginning and the end of a series names the whole series; that attaining the first and last fruits already guarantees the middle two by strict sequential necessity; that only the first and last are attained purely by the undefiled path rather than partly by a worldly one; that both alone are attained by transcending the peak of existence; and that the passage speaks generally of the path and cutting-off of seeing versus cultivation, using the first and last fruits as convenient stand-ins for these two broader categories.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The recurring exclusion clause — that the path toward a superior fruit is not gathered within the fruit one currently holds — is the ledger's single organizing principle, applied identically across all three lower fruits: possessing the ambition and the practice aimed at the next fruit is categorically different from possessing that next fruit itself, and the treatise refuses to blur this line even where the practitioner's own effort is already well underway.

11. The Arhat's Non-Learner Dharmas, and the Undefiled Dharmas of Stream-Entry and Once-Returning

Because an arhat seeks no further, superior fruit, every one of his own non-learner dharmas — every aid to practice, every uninterrupted and liberation path, every path of further progress he has ever traversed — is gathered wholly within the fruit of arhatship, with none of the earlier fruits' exclusion clause applying. The treatise then turns to the undefiled dharmas, conditioned and unconditioned together, possessed by stream-enterers and once-returners, adding a subtler exclusion beyond the learner-dharma ledger already given: not only the paths of further progress but also the fetters they alone cut off, and even the karmically neutral non-analytical cessations a practitioner may possess, are excluded from the fruit itself, since a wholesome fruit cannot gather within it what is either not yet properly its own or is not wholesome at all.
