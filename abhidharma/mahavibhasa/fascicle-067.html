The Fourfold Ledger of Possession

Fascicle 67 completes the learner- and undefiled-dharma containment ledger for non-returners and arhats, recasts the whole question of dharma-possession as a fourfold tetralemma across all four fruits, and turns to the long debate over whether one of resolve-through-faith ever sharpens his faculty to become one who has attained through vision — canvassing rival theories, the Venerable Buddhapālita's six distinguishing marks, and the detailed ledger of exactly where and how much is gained and lost at each stage of faculty-change. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 67 continues the Sentient-Being Nyāsa (結蘊第二中有情納息第三之五), completing the dharma-containment ledger begun in the previous fascicle, recasting it as a systematic fourfold tetralemma, and turning to the extended technical debate over faculty-change (轉根) between the two learner lineages. 1. The Non-Returner's and Arhat's Remaining Undefiled Dharmas

Opening part five of the Sentient-Being Nyāsa, the treatise completes the containment ledger begun in the previous fascicle, working through the undefiled dharmas possessed by non-returners and by arhats. Each fruit gathers within it the faculties of its own lineage, whether of resolve-through-faith or of attainment-through-vision, together with the relevant cutting-off of dharmas abandoned by seeing and cultivation, while excluding the paths of further progress aimed at a fruit not yet attained and the karmically neutral non-analytical cessations a practitioner may happen to possess.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The arhat's own ledger introduces a genuinely new distinction not needed for the three lower fruits — liberated-in-season versus liberated-beyond-season — since an arhat's lineage is sorted by a different axis than a learner's, no longer resolve-through-faith versus attainment-through-vision, but the capacity to enter cessation at will versus the need for favorable conditions; the underlying logic of the ledger itself, however, remains exactly the same as before.

2. The Fourfold Analysis Introduced: What Stream-Enterers and Once-Returners Possess

The treatise now recasts its entire containment question as a tetralemma, since possession and fruit-inclusion turn out to stand in a relation now broader, now narrower rather than simple identity. A stream-enterer possesses contaminated dharmas — wholesome, defiled, and neutral-and-unobscured — that the purely undefiled fruit cannot contain; conversely the fruit contains the case of a not-yet-attained or already-lost fruit that a given individual does not, at that moment, possess. Only the mutual overlap forms the third alternative, and only what is named in none of these three forms the residual fourth. The same fourfold pattern is then carried through for once-returners.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The tetralemma is doing real conceptual work that a simple yes/no could not: it separates, cleanly, four genuinely different relations — possessed-but-excluded, contained-but-not-possessed, both, and neither — that a flatter question like 'is this dharma the fruit?' would collapse into a single, misleading answer.

3. The Fourfold Analysis Completed: Non-Returning and Arhatship

The same tetralemma is worked through for non-returners and arhats, with one structural variation for the arhat's own case: since arhatship admits of no further, superior fruit to seek, the excluded first alternative shrinks to only the non-analytical cessations he happens to possess, no further paths of progress remaining to exclude, while the undefiled wholesome of the residual fourth alternative simply becomes every dharma still belonging to a learner.

4. Does One of Resolve-through-Faith Change Faculty to Become One Who Has Attained through Vision?

A genuinely difficult question opens: does a practitioner of the resolve-through-faith lineage ever sharpen his faculty into the attainment-through-vision lineage? Both a yes and a no generate scriptural difficulties — if yes, the Faculties Aggregate's own list of exceptions to "every faculty-change is a passing from one fruit to another" ought to have named it but does not; if no, the treatise's own statement that the fruit of stream-entry can be "not yet attained" or "already lost," the Cognition Aggregate's account of a stream-enterer's three concentrations, and the Identity-Body Treatise's account of an arhat who regresses, turns faculty, and regains his fruit, all become impossible to explain. The Venerable Saṃghavasu resolves this by holding that faculty-sharpening at the learner's stage is nothing but a case of attaining a new fruit under another name — a proposal the compilers reject outright.

5. The Rival "No Faculty-Change" Theory and Its Own Scriptural Reconciliations

A rival school denies outright that resolve-through-faith ever changes faculty into attainment-through-vision, forcing an entirely different explanation for how the fruit of stream-entry can be spoken of as "not yet attained" or "already lost." Two positions are canvassed — that the fruit simply has no past or future attainment at all, or that the fruit comes in three internal grades (lower, middle, upper), so that reaching a higher grade renders the lower grades "transcended" and hence nominally "lost," even without any literal faculty-change ever having occurred.

7. Three Marks Upheld, Three Rejected: The Abhidharma Masters Assess Buddhapālita's Six Points

The Abhidharma masters subject Buddhapālita's six marks to independent scrutiny, accepting the first three — desire realm only, dhyāna-based only, undefiled-path only — as sound, but rejecting the latter three as resting on an indeterminate generalization: mastery of dharma-cognition versus subsequent cognition, prior regression versus never having regressed, and fruit-abiding versus abiding in the path toward a superior fruit each turn out, on inspection, to admit of both possibilities rather than only the one Buddhapālita named.
