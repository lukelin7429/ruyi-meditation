Sixty-Five Equipoises

Fascicle 169 continues the Subsumption Nyāsa (攝納息第三之四), applying the doctrine of possession, attainment, relinquishment, and regression to the dhyānas and formless spheres in every tetralemma combination, before stepping back to catalogue all sixty-five equipoises (等至) recognized by this tradition and working out, attainment by attainment, exactly which others serve as its cause, its immediately-preceding condition, and its object. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Possessing All Three, Possessing Just One

Fascicle 169 turns the possession-doctrine (成就) from fascicle 168 directly onto the dhyānas and formless spheres themselves, working through every logically possible combination of the flavor-associated, pure, and uncontaminated three-fold classification established many fascicles ago. The method is a rigorous tetralemma: for each combination the treatise asks whether it can occur, supplies a real example when it can, and explains — often at real length — exactly why the remaining combinations are logically excluded.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The recurring closing move in this section — carefully explaining why certain combinations are simply impossible rather than merely unmentioned — reveals something about how rigorously this tradition polices its own categories: a live possibility left unaddressed would be a gap in the system, so every excluded case gets its own dedicated proof of exclusion.

2. Attaining, Relinquishing, Regressing

The treatise now runs the identical tetralemma method across three further verbs of possession-dynamics: attaining (得) a dhyāna or formless sphere for the first time, relinquishing (捨) one, and regressing (退) from one already attained — each verb producing its own distinct pattern of which three-fold combinations are possible and which are excluded, since attainment, relinquishment, and regression turn out to behave by different rules even though they operate on the very same three-fold classification.

Is there a case of attaining the flavor-associated four dhyānas but neither the pure nor the uncontaminated? Is there a case of attaining the uncontaminated four dhyānas but neither the flavor-associated nor the pure? Answer: there is — this refers to someone who, based on the fourth dhyāna, enters correctly determined liberation from birth, or who attains the fruit of arhatship. Here, "the moment of entering correctly determined liberation from birth" refers to the moment the patient acceptance of suffering-in-the-Dharma arises — at that moment, the uncontaminated four dhyānas, not having existed before, are now attained, without the flavor-associated or the pure. "The moment of attaining the fruit of arhatship" refers to the moment the knowledge of extinction arises — at that moment, the uncontaminated four dhyānas, though all previously relinquished, are now attained, without the flavor-associated or the pure. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The fascicle ends mid-argument, still working through the formless spheres' object-relations, which is itself a small reminder of how this text was originally organized: not as self-contained chapters but as one continuous unbroken argument, arbitrarily divided into scroll-length fascicles by the physical constraints of manuscript production centuries after its composition.
