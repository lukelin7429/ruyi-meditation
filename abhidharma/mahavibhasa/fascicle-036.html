A Knowledge That Never Arrives

Fascicle 36 names the predominant wholesome roots of the desire realm — a bodhisattva's own insight and a buddha's own final exhaustion of every contaminant — before an extended investigation into the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization, genuinely acquired and dependent on a noble one's own body, yet structurally prevented from ever once actually manifesting before him. The fascicle closes with the six constant abidings that make an arhat's mind forever equanimous, and a cascade of images explaining why the wholesome roots of all three realms are cultivated all at once, at the single moment of the knowledge of exhaustion. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 36 continues the third part of the Shamelessness Nyāsa (無慚愧納息第五之三), opened in fascicle 34. Terms established in earlier fascicles, such as the path of seeing and cultivation, the nine grades of affliction, and the knowledge of exhaustion, recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Predominant and Subtly Co-Arising Wholesome Roots of the Desire Realm

Fascicle 36 opens the mirror image of fascicle 35's unwholesome-roots discussion, working through why the sūtra passes over the middling grade of wholesome root and why it speaks only of the desire realm rather than the form or formless realms as well — since the higher realms' own wholesome roots depend on the desire realm's as their gateway and preparatory basis, and since only the desire realm admits of the very possibility of “cutting off wholesome roots” that gives the subtle grade its own distinct meaning.

Chinese Source

云何欲界增上善根。云何微俱行善根。乃至廣說。問何故作此論。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is a predominant wholesome root of the desire realm? What is a subtly co-arising wholesome root? And so on, at length. One asks: why is this treatise composed?

答為廣分別契經義故。謂契經說彼猶有微俱行善根未斷。從此有餘善法當起由是清淨。契經雖作是說而不廣分別其義。亦不說云何欲界增上善根。云何微俱行善根。經是此論所依根本。彼所不分別者。今應廣分別之故作斯論。問何故此中不說中品善根耶。答是作論者意欲爾故。乃至廣說。復次應說而不說者。當知此義有餘。復次已說初後即已顯中。如初後上下趣入已出加行究竟。應知亦爾。

The answer: it is composed in order to fully discriminate the meaning of this sūtra passage. As the sūtra states: he still possesses a subtly co-arising wholesome root, not yet cut off, from which there will further arise wholesome dharmas, and it is on this account that he will attain purification — yet, having said this much, the sūtra does not discriminate its meaning fully, nor does it state what is meant by a predominant wholesome root of the desire realm, or what is meant by a subtly co-arising wholesome root. That sūtra is the fundamental basis upon which this treatise depends, and whatever it has left undiscriminated ought now to be discriminated fully, and it is for this reason that this treatise is composed. One asks: why does this passage not speak of the middling grade of wholesome root? The answer: this is simply because the author of the treatise intended it so, and so on, at length. Further, that it ought to be stated but is not stated here should be understood as a matter of surplus meaning left implicit. Further, having already spoken of the first and the last, the middle is thereby already indicated as well — exactly as, in speaking of first and last, or superior and inferior, or entering and emerging, or the beginning of preparatory effort and its ultimate completion, this same principle should be understood to apply.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the higher realms' wholesome roots go unmentioned specifically because they cannot be “cut off” in the technical sense fascicle 35 explored is a nice piece of continuity across these two fascicles: the entire apparatus of predominant-versus-subtle grading turns out to depend on the very possibility of loss that only exists within the desire realm, so the higher realms simply have no occasion for this particular distinction to apply.

2. The Predominant Wholesome Roots: A Bodhisattva's Insight and a Buddha's Exhaustion

The treatise defines the predominant wholesome root of the desire realm with striking specificity: the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization attained by the Bodhisattva at the very moment of entering correct and determined progress, and the wholesome roots of non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion attained by the Tathāgata at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion — before canvassing four rival readings of why these two, rather than any other pairing, are singled out for comparison, and what that comparison is actually meant to establish.

Chinese Source

復次若麁現易了易可施設。易顯易說者此中說之。中品不爾是故不說。復次中品攝在上下品中故不別說。復次上下品少世所希奇是以故說。中品極多非希奇故略而不說。問何故此中但說欲界善根不說色無色界耶。答是作論者意欲爾故。乃至廣說。復次應說而不說者當知此義有餘。復次色無色界善根。以欲界善根為門為加行為趣入路。若說此應知亦已說彼故不別說。復次此中但說近對治故。謂次前說二不善根彼近對治。即是欲界二種善根是故偏說。復次欲界增微善根易現易了易施設故此中說之。色無色界增上善根雖易現易了易可施設而微者不爾是故不說。上二界無斷善義故微善根相難可施設。云何欲界增上善根。答菩薩入正性離生時所得欲界現觀邊世俗智。及如來得盡智時所得欲界無貪無瞋無癡善根。如是善根於欲界繫諸善根中最為勝故說名增上。云何微俱行善根。答斷善根時最後所捨。由捨彼故名斷善根。如是善根是欲界繫生得善中下下品攝名微俱行。問此中何故以盡智時所得善根對現觀邊世俗智辨差別耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, this passage speaks only of whatever is coarse, manifest, easily understood, easily designated, easily displayed, and easily expressed; the middling grade is not so, and it is for this reason that it is not spoken of here. Further, the middling grade is already subsumed within the superior and inferior grades, and is therefore not separately mentioned. Further, the superior and inferior grades are rare and extraordinary in the world, and it is for this reason that they alone are mentioned; the middling grade is exceedingly common, and, not being extraordinary, is omitted and not mentioned in brief. One asks: why does this passage speak only of the wholesome roots of the desire realm, and not of the form or formless realms? The answer: this is simply because the author of the treatise intended it so, and so on, at length. Further, that it ought to be stated but is not stated here should be understood as a matter of surplus meaning left implicit. Further, the wholesome roots of the form and formless realms take the wholesome roots of the desire realm as their gateway, their preparatory basis, and the path by which one enters into them; in speaking of the latter, the former ought to be understood as thereby already spoken of as well, and it is for this reason that they are not separately mentioned. Further, this passage speaks only of the proximate antidote — that is, the two unwholesome roots spoken of just before this passage have, as their proximate antidote, precisely these two wholesome roots of the desire realm, and it is for this reason that this passage speaks of them in particular. Further, the subtle wholesome root that increases within the desire realm is easily manifest, easily understood, and easily designated, and it is for this reason that this passage speaks of it; although the predominant wholesome roots of the form and formless realms are likewise easily manifest, easily understood, and easily designated, their subtle wholesome roots are not so, and it is for this reason that they are not spoken of here — since the higher two realms admit of no sense of "cutting off wholesome roots," their subtle wholesome roots are correspondingly difficult to designate. What is a predominant wholesome root of the desire realm? The answer: it refers to the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization belonging to the desire realm, obtained by the Bodhisattva at the moment of entering correct and determined progress, and the wholesome roots of non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion belonging to the desire realm, obtained by the Tathāgata at the moment of attaining the knowledge of exhaustion — such wholesome roots as these, being supreme among all the wholesome roots bound to the desire realm, are accordingly called "predominant." What is a subtly co-arising wholesome root? The answer: it refers to whichever wholesome root is the very last to be relinquished at the time wholesome roots are cut off, this relinquishing itself being called "the cutting off of wholesome roots" — such a wholesome root as this, subsumed within the very lowest grade of the innately born wholesome roots bound to the desire realm, is accordingly called "subtly co-arising." One asks: why does this passage compare the wholesome roots obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion against the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization, in order to work out their distinction?

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The final position's own resolution — that this passage isn't really comparing two things against each other at all, but rather naming the Bodhisattva's and Tathāgata's own attainments as simply superior to whatever the two lesser vehicles achieve — models something this treatise does repeatedly: a seemingly technical comparative question dissolves once the compilers recognize that comparison was never quite the point, and that a passage's real work is to establish rank rather than symmetry.

3. A Grammatical Puzzle, and the Etymology of “On the Border of Direct Realization”

A precise grammatical puzzle opens: since this conventional knowledge is only attained after one has already entered correct and determined progress, why does the sūtra speak of attaining it “at the moment of entering”? The treatise resolves this through the same present-tense-for-a-past-event device it has used before — “Great King, whence do you now come?” asked of one who has, in fact, already arrived — before working out, through several competing etymologies, exactly why this particular knowledge earns the name “on the border of direct realization” at all.

Chinese Source

有作是說。此中不以盡智時所得善根對現觀邊世俗智而辨差別。然以現觀邊世俗智對現觀邊世俗智而辨差別。謂現觀邊世俗智聲聞者劣。獨覺者中。菩薩者勝故。又以盡智時所得善根對盡智時所得善根而辨差別。謂盡智時所得善根聲聞者劣。獨覺者中。如來者勝故。有餘師說。此中亦以盡智時所得善根對現觀邊世俗智而辨差別。謂菩薩現觀邊世俗智是劣。盡智時所得善根是勝。聲聞獨覺亦爾。復次菩薩現觀邊世俗智。勝於獨覺盡智時所得善根。獨覺現觀邊世俗智。勝於聲聞盡智時所得善根故。或有說者。此中不欲辨二差別但明此二平等無異。謂此皆由越有頂得現觀邊世俗智。越見所斷有頂得故。盡智時所得善根。越修所斷有頂得故。問二乘亦爾何故不說。答彼不能越見修所斷有頂習氣非增上故。復有說者。此中不欲說二差別及二平等。但說菩薩欲界現觀邊世俗智。及如來盡智時所得欲界善根。是欲界增上善根勝二乘所得故。問若爾聲聞獨覺豈無增上善根。答彼復展轉望餘下類說名增上非望上乘。

Ru-Yi Translation

There are those who say: this passage does not, in fact, compare the wholesome roots obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion against the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization; rather, it compares the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization against itself, in order to work out its distinction — that is, the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization belonging to a śrāvaka is inferior, that belonging to a pratyekabuddha is middling, and that belonging to a bodhisattva is supreme. And it compares the wholesome roots obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion against themselves, in order to work out their distinction — that is, the wholesome roots obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion belonging to a śrāvaka are inferior, those belonging to a pratyekabuddha are middling, and those belonging to a Tathāgata are supreme. There are other masters who say: this passage does indeed compare the wholesome roots obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion against the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization, in order to work out their distinction — that is, a bodhisattva's own conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization is inferior, while the wholesome roots obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion are supreme; and the same holds for the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha. Further, a bodhisattva's own conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization surpasses a pratyekabuddha's own wholesome roots obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion; and a pratyekabuddha's own conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization surpasses a śrāvaka's own wholesome roots obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion. There are others who say: this passage does not, in fact, intend to work out any distinction between these two at all, but rather clarifies that these two are, in truth, equal, with no difference between them — that is, both alike are attained by transcending the peak of existence, the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization being attained by transcending the acquisition of the peak of existence's own affliction abandoned by seeing, and the wholesome roots obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion being attained by transcending the acquisition of the peak of existence's own affliction abandoned by cultivation. One asks: the two vehicles, too, attain these — why, then, does this passage not mention them? The answer: they are unable to transcend the habitual traces of the peak of existence's own affliction abandoned by seeing and by cultivation, and it is for this reason that their attainment is not called "predominant." There are yet others who say: this passage does not, in fact, intend to speak either of a distinction or of an equality between these two, but rather speaks only of the bodhisattva's own conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization belonging to the desire realm, and the Tathāgata's own wholesome roots of the desire realm obtained at the moment of the knowledge of exhaustion, as being the predominant wholesome roots of the desire realm, since these surpass what is attained by the two vehicles. One asks: if so, then do the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha possess no predominant wholesome roots of their own at all? The answer: theirs, too, in turn, are called "predominant" when compared against whatever lies below them, but not when compared against the vehicle above them.

問現觀邊世俗智入正性離生已方得何故言入時得耶。答理應言入已方得而說入時得者。此已入名入時於近說遠聲。如說大王今者從何所來。此亦已來而說今來。如說受樂受時如實知受樂受等。此亦已受名受時。如說斷苦斷樂入第四靜慮。此亦於苦已斷名斷。如說思惟何法入慈等至。此亦已入名入。如說阿羅漢心解脫欲漏有漏無明漏。此於欲漏亦已解脫名解脫。此中亦爾。已入名入時。復有說者。正應說菩薩入正性離生時得現觀邊世俗智。以諸諦初智皆名正性離生諸忍名入故。謂苦集滅類忍入苦集滅類智時名得現觀邊世俗智。如金剛喻定現在前時名得盡智時此亦如是。問何故此智名現觀邊。答現觀苦邊集邊滅邊。得此智故名現觀邊。有說。此是諸瑜伽師觀聖諦時傍修得故名現觀邊尊者妙音說曰。此智近現觀故名現觀邊。如近村物名曰村邊。問此現觀邊所修世俗善法四蘊五蘊為自性。何故但說世俗智耶。答以智增故說名為智。猶如見道五蘊為自性見增故名見。金剛喻定四蘊五蘊為自性。定增故名定。四種通行四蘊五蘊為自性。通增故名通。此亦如是。問苦現觀邊欲界世俗智與色界世俗智何者為勝。答色界者勝以界勝故集滅現觀邊亦爾。問集現觀邊欲界世俗智苦現觀邊色界世俗智何者為勝。答欲界者所依勝故勝。色界者界勝故勝。以滅問苦集亦爾。問苦現觀邊欲界世俗智集現觀邊色界世俗智何者為勝。答色界者二事勝故勝。一界勝二所依勝。以苦問滅以集問滅亦爾。問苦現觀邊欲界世俗智集現觀邊欲界世俗智何者為勝。答集現觀邊勝。以所依勝故以苦問滅。以集問滅亦爾。如欲界色界亦爾。問何故現觀邊世俗智法智時不修。答法智於彼非田非器乃至廣說。復次此智現觀邊修故名現觀邊世俗智。若法智時亦修者應名現觀中世俗智非現觀邊。復次先說此智越有頂見所斷故得。非法智時能越有頂見所斷故不修此智。復次於一一諦所作已辦加行息時能修此智。住法智時多有所作加行未息。謂法智時雖知欲界苦而未知色無色界苦。雖斷欲界集而未斷色無色界集。雖證欲界滅而未證色無色滅。故法智時不修此智。復次於一一諦現觀究竟及斷見此所斷盡時能修此智。住法智時無如是事是故不修。問何故道類智時不修此智。答道類智於此非田非器。乃至廣說。復次此智是見道眷屬繫屬見道道類智是修道故不修此智。復次此智是向道眷屬繫屬向道。道類智是果道故不修此智。復次此智是隨信行隨法行相續中修道類智時。名信勝解見至相續是故不修此智。復次此智名現觀邊於三諦有邊聲轉故修此智。如說薩迦耶苦邊。薩迦耶集邊。薩迦耶滅邊。而不說薩迦耶道邊故道類智時不修此智。問因論生論。何故於三諦有邊聲轉非於道諦。答以其有能知一切苦。斷一切集證一切滅。而無有能修一切道。佛亦於道得修習修俱不盡故無邊聲轉。復次若諦有漏無漏道俱能有所作者有邊聲轉。道諦唯無漏道能有所作故無邊聲轉。如有漏無漏道。世間出世間道。有味無味道。耽嗜依出離依道。當知亦爾。復次苦諦是有是有果於彼有邊聲轉。謂苦集諦是有是有果。滅諦雖非有而是有果。

One asks: the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization is attained only after one has already entered correct and determined progress — why, then, does the passage speak of attaining it "at the moment of entering"? The answer: in principle, it ought to say that this is attained only after one has already entered, yet, in speaking of it as attained "at the moment of entering," this refers to what has, in fact, already entered, spoken of, in a manner drawing the distant near, as though it were happening now — exactly as it is said, "Great King, whence do you now come?" — though he has, in fact, already come, it is nonetheless said that he "now comes"; exactly as it is said, "at the moment of experiencing a pleasant feeling, one truly knows that one experiences a pleasant feeling," though this, too, refers to a feeling already experienced, yet spoken of as "at the moment of experiencing"; exactly as it is said, "cutting off suffering, cutting off pleasure, one enters the fourth dhyāna," though this, too, refers to suffering already cut off, yet spoken of as "cutting off"; exactly as it is said, "contemplating what dharma does one enter the attainment of loving-kindness," though this, too, refers to what has already been entered, yet spoken of as "entering"; exactly as it is said, "the arhat's mind is liberated from the contaminant of desire, the contaminant of existence, and the contaminant of ignorance," though this, too, with respect to the contaminant of desire, refers to what has already been liberated, yet spoken of as "liberated" — so too is it here: what has, in fact, already entered is spoken of as "at the moment of entering." There are yet others who say: it ought properly to be said that the Bodhisattva attains the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization at the very moment of entering correct and determined progress, since every truth's own first knowledge is called "correct and determined progress," and every forbearance is called "entering" — that is, at the very moment the subsequent-knowledge forbearances of suffering, origin, and cessation give rise to the subsequent knowledges of suffering, origin, and cessation, one is said to attain the conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization — exactly as, at the very moment the vajropama-samādhi manifests before one, one is said to attain the knowledge of exhaustion; so too is it here. One asks: why is this knowledge called "on the border of direct realization"? The answer: it directly realizes the border of suffering, the border of origin, and the border of cessation, and it is through attaining this knowledge that it is called "on the border of direct realization." There are those who say: this knowledge is cultivated incidentally by yogic practitioners while they contemplate the noble truths, and it is for this reason that it is called "on the border of direct realization" — exactly as an object found near a village is called "the village's border." The Venerable Ghoṣa states as follows: this knowledge lies near to direct realization, and it is for this reason that it is called "on the border of direct realization." One asks: the mundane wholesome dharma cultivated on the border of direct realization takes the four aggregates, or the five aggregates, as its own nature — why, then, does the passage speak of it only as "conventional knowledge"? The answer: it is because knowledge is here the predominant factor that it is called "knowledge" — exactly as the path of seeing takes the five aggregates as its own nature, yet, because seeing is the predominant factor, it is called "seeing"; exactly as the vajropama-samādhi takes the four aggregates, or the five aggregates, as its own nature, yet, because concentration is the predominant factor, it is called "concentration"; exactly as the four modes of practice take the four aggregates, or the five aggregates, as their own nature, yet, because penetrating insight is the predominant factor, they are called "modes of practice" — so too is it here. One asks: with respect to the border of direct realization of suffering, which is superior — the conventional knowledge of the desire realm, or that of the form realm? The answer: that of the form realm is superior, since its realm is superior — and the same holds for the border of direct realization of origin and of cessation as well. One asks: with respect to the border of direct realization of origin, which is superior — the conventional knowledge of the desire realm, or, with respect to the border of direct realization of suffering, that of the form realm? The answer: that of the desire realm is superior, since its support is superior; that of the form realm is superior, since its realm is superior. As with suffering compared to cessation, so too should this be understood. One asks: with respect to the border of direct realization of suffering, which is superior — the conventional knowledge of the desire realm, or, with respect to the border of direct realization of origin, that of the form realm? The answer: that of the form realm is superior, since it is superior in two respects — first, its realm, and second, its support. As with suffering compared to cessation, and origin compared to cessation, so too should this be understood. One asks: with respect to the border of direct realization of suffering, which is superior — the conventional knowledge of the desire realm, or, with respect to the border of direct realization of origin, that of the desire realm? The answer: that on the border of direct realization of origin is superior, since its support is superior. As with suffering compared to cessation, and origin compared to cessation, so too should this be understood; and, just as with the desire realm, so too with the form realm. One asks: why is this conventional knowledge on the border of direct realization not cultivated at the time of dharma-knowledge? The answer: dharma-knowledge is, at that time, neither a field nor a vessel for it, and so on, at length.
