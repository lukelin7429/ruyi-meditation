The Terminal Feeling at Death

Fascicle 190 continues the Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa (念住納息第一之四): bodily versus mental feeling distinguished nine ways; feeling with and without relish, addiction, and renunciation; mind with and without craving, hatred, and delusion, and why a mind remains "with craving" even when craving is not currently active; seven arguments for why a wholesome mind is called great despite being rare; the internal hindrances, fetters, and awakening-factors; observing one's own increase and decrease of craving, hatred, and delusion through vivid similes; and the terminal feeling at the very moment of death, including which of the four great elements can sever the vital point and why the Buddha alone never experiences it. This page carries Ru‑Yi's complete translation.

1. Bodily Feeling and Mental Feeling

Continuing the previous fascicle's account of knowing feeling as it really is, the treatise now distinguishes bodily from mental feeling through nine successive criteria — sense-door, discrimination, characteristic, tense, object-type, repetition, spontaneity, and finally support and object — before the Venerable Vasumitra and the Bhadanta each offer their own accounts of where the line actually falls.

Chinese Source

受樂身受苦身受不苦不樂身受。及苦心受時如實知。此一智謂世俗一切身受及苦心受唯有漏故。起世俗智則如實知受樂心受不苦不樂心受時此四智。謂法類世俗道此二心受通有漏無漏故。以四智知廣釋如上。問此中何者名身受何者名心受耶。答若受在五識身名身受。在意地名心受。復次若受無分別名身受。有分別名心受。復次若受取自相境名身受。取自共相境名心受復次若受取現在境名身受。取三世及非世境名心受。復次若受取事別境名身受。取事別及和合境名心受。復次若受一墮境者名身受。數數墮境者名心受。復次若受於境率爾轉者名身受。思度轉者名心受。復次若受以色為所依色為所緣者名身受。

Ru-Yi Translation

When one experiences a pleasant bodily feeling, a painful bodily feeling, a neither-painful-nor-pleasant bodily feeling, or a painful mental feeling, one knows it as it really is by a single cognition alone — namely, the conventional cognition — since every bodily feeling and every painful mental feeling is exclusively contaminated, and hence only the conventional cognition arises to know it as it really is. When one experiences a pleasant mental feeling or a neither-painful-nor-pleasant mental feeling, one knows it as it really is by these four cognitions — namely, the cognition of the Dharma, the subsequent cognition, the conventional cognition, and the cognition of the path — since these two mental feelings extend to both the contaminated and the uncontaminated, and are hence known by four cognitions, to be explained at length as above. Question: here, what is named bodily feeling, and what is named mental feeling? Answer: feeling that occurs within the five sense-consciousness bodies is named bodily feeling; feeling that occurs at the stage of mind is named mental feeling. Furthermore, feeling without discrimination is named bodily feeling; feeling with discrimination is named mental feeling. Furthermore, feeling that takes a self-characteristic object is named bodily feeling; feeling that takes an object of both self- and shared-characteristic is named mental feeling. Furthermore, feeling that takes a present object is named bodily feeling; feeling that takes an object belonging to any of the three times, or to no time at all, is named mental feeling. Furthermore, feeling that takes a single discrete thing as its object is named bodily feeling; feeling that takes either a single discrete thing or a combination of things as its object is named mental feeling. Furthermore, feeling that settles upon its object only once is named bodily feeling; feeling that settles upon its object again and again is named mental feeling. Furthermore, feeling that operates spontaneously with respect to its object is named bodily feeling; feeling that operates through deliberation is named mental feeling. Furthermore, feeling whose support is form and whose object is form is named bodily feeling;

以非色為所依色非色為所緣者名心受。

feeling whose support is not form, and whose object is either form or non-form, is named mental feeling.

如色非色。如是有對無對積聚非積聚和合不和合廣說亦爾。尊者世友說曰。如佛所說。彼於爾時受於二受。謂身受心受。云何身受。

Just as with form and non-form, so, too, is it to be explained at length with resistant and non-resistant, aggregated and non-aggregated, and combined and uncombined. The Venerable Vasumitra said: as the Buddha states, at that time he experiences two kinds of feeling — namely, bodily feeling and mental feeling. What is bodily feeling?

云何心受。答無有受是身受一切受皆是心受。所以者何。心相應故。然諸受若依五根轉此受名身受。恒以身為增上故。若依意根轉此受名心受。恒以心為增上故。復作是說。無有受是身受。一切受皆是心受心相應故。然諸受依取至境三根轉此受名身受恒作想故。

What is mental feeling? Answer: there is, in fact, no feeling that is bodily feeling — every feeling alike is mental feeling, since it is invariably conjoined with mind. Yet feeling that operates in reliance on the five [sense] faculties is named bodily feeling, since the body is invariably its predominant condition; feeling that operates in reliance on the mental faculty is named mental feeling, since mind is invariably its predominant condition. He further says: there is no feeling that is bodily feeling — every feeling alike is mental feeling, since it is conjoined with mind. Yet feeling that operates in reliance on the three faculties that take an object in direct contact is named bodily feeling, since it invariably forms a perception,

依取不至境三根轉此受名心受不恒作想故。大德說曰。若是身受彼亦心受耶。答若是身受彼亦心受。有是心受而非身受。謂若計度外事於內取相及於事取補特伽羅并法處所攝色。心不相應行無為相。

while feeling that operates in reliance on the three faculties that take an object not in direct contact is named mental feeling, since it does not invariably form a perception. The Bhadanta said: is whatever is bodily feeling thereby also mental feeling? Answer: whatever is bodily feeling is indeed also mental feeling; but there is what is mental feeling yet not bodily feeling — that is, whatever speculates about external matters, forms a mark internally, and, with respect to a given matter, grasps at a person, or takes the form subsumed under the dharma-sphere, the formations dissociated from mind, or the mark of the unconditioned —

如此類受皆名心受。以於非實有境分別轉故。是謂身受心受差別。

feeling of this class is entirely named mental feeling, since it operates by discriminating with respect to an object that does not truly exist. This, then, is what distinguishes bodily feeling from mental feeling.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The nine successive definitions of bodily versus mental feeling — by sense-door, by discrimination, by characteristic, by tense, by object-type, by repetition, by spontaneity, and finally by support and object — read like the treatise circling the same distinction from every available angle, unwilling to settle for a single criterion when a phenomenon this basic to experience deserves to be triangulated.

2. Feeling With and Without Relish, Addiction, and Renunciation

Two more pairs of feeling are analyzed — feeling with or without relish, and feeling with an addictive or a renunciate basis — each raising the same puzzle: since every painful feeling seems able to serve as a resting-place for craving, why does the treatise allow that some painful feeling is nonetheless "without relish"? The answer locates a small measure of the renunciate even within contaminated pain.

Chinese Source

受樂有味受。苦有味受。不苦不樂有味受。及苦無味受時如實知。

Ru-Yi Translation

by a single cognition alone — namely, the conventional cognition — since every feeling with relish, and painful feeling without relish, is contaminated, and hence only the conventional cognition arises to know it as it really is. When one experiences a pleasant feeling without relish or a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling without relish, one knows it as it really is by four cognitions — namely, the cognition of the Dharma, the subsequent cognition, the conventional cognition, and the cognition of the path — since these two kinds of feeling without relish extend to both the contaminated and the uncontaminated, and are hence known by four cognitions, to be explained at length as above. Here, "relish" refers to the nature of craving's own delight and attachment — feeling that serves as a resting-place for that craving is named feeling with relish. Some say: "relish" refers to the nature of clinging and attachment belonging to every affliction — feeling that serves as a resting-place for these is named feeling with relish; feeling that does not serve as a resting-place for craving, or for every affliction, is named feeling without relish. Question: if so, then every painful feeling ought to be named "with relish," since all of it is able to serve as a resting-place for craving or for every affliction. And, as the Prakaraṇapāda states, what is the dharma with relish? It is every contaminated dharma. What is the dharma without relish? It is every uncontaminated dharma. Why, then, is it said here that there exists a painful feeling without relish? Answer: although painful feeling is contaminated, it does possess a small measure of the nature of a basis without relish, and it is with reference to this that painful feeling is spoken of as having a basis without relish — that is, it possesses a nature that runs contrary to affliction, and does not mix with affliction. Other masters say: although painful feeling is entirely contaminated, there exists a kind that is able to draw forth an uncontaminated feeling without relish, in accordance with ultimate significance, and it is with reference to this that painful feeling without relish is spoken of, since it is able temporarily to subdue every relish.

受樂耽嗜依。受苦耽嗜依。受不苦不樂耽嗜依。受及苦出離依。受時如實知。此一智謂世俗。以一切耽嗜依。受及苦出離依受皆有漏故。起世俗智則如實知受樂出離依。受不苦不樂出離依受時如實知此四智。謂法類世俗道如前釋。此中耽嗜謂愛耽著性故。若受與彼為安足處名耽嗜依受。有說。耽嗜名一切煩惱執著性故。若受與彼為安足處名耽嗜依受。苦受不與愛或一切煩惱為安足處名出離依受。問若爾一切苦受皆應名耽嗜依。皆能與愛或一切煩惱為安足處故。又如品類足說。云何耽嗜依法謂有漏法。云何出離依法謂無漏法。何故此中說有苦出離依受耶。答苦受雖有漏而有少出離依性。

When one experiences a pleasant feeling with an addictive basis, a painful feeling with an addictive basis, a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling with an addictive basis, or a painful feeling with a renunciate basis, one knows it as it really is by a single cognition alone — namely, the conventional cognition — since every feeling with an addictive basis, and painful feeling with a renunciate basis, is contaminated, and hence only the conventional cognition arises to know it as it really is. When one experiences a pleasant feeling with a renunciate basis or a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling with a renunciate basis, one knows it as it really is by these four cognitions — namely, the cognition of the Dharma, the subsequent cognition, the conventional cognition, and the cognition of the path — to be explained as before. Here, "addiction" refers to the nature of craving's own attachment — feeling that serves as a resting-place for that craving is named feeling with an addictive basis. Some say: "addiction" refers to the nature of clinging belonging to every affliction — feeling that serves as a resting-place for these is named feeling with an addictive basis; painful feeling that does not serve as a resting-place for craving, or for every affliction, is named feeling with a renunciate basis. Question: if so, then every painful feeling ought to be named "with an addictive basis," since all of it is able to serve as a resting-place for craving or for every affliction. And, as the Prakaraṇapāda states, what is the dharma with an addictive basis? It is every contaminated dharma. What is the dharma with a renunciate basis? It is every uncontaminated dharma. Why, then, is it said here that there exists a painful feeling with a renunciate basis? Answer: although painful feeling is contaminated, it does possess a small measure of the nature of a renunciate basis,

依彼故說苦出離依受。謂彼有與煩惱相違及與煩惱不相雜性。有餘師說。苦受雖皆是有漏而有能引發隨順勝義出離依無漏受者。依彼故說苦出離依受以能暫時伏諸耽嗜故。

and it is with reference to this that painful feeling with a renunciate basis is spoken of — that is, it possesses a nature that runs contrary to affliction, and does not mix with affliction. Other masters say: although painful feeling is entirely contaminated, there exists a kind that is able to draw forth an uncontaminated feeling with a renunciate basis, in accordance with ultimate significance, and it is with reference to this that painful feeling with a renunciate basis is spoken of, since it is able temporarily to subdue every addiction.

3. Mind With and Without Craving, Hatred, and Delusion

A careful argument establishes that a mind is properly called "with craving" because it is bound by craving, not merely because craving is currently active within it — since a mind can be free of craving's active presence in this very moment and still remain bound by it — before extending the same analysis to hatred and delusion, and to the broader categories of defiled and undefiled, contracted and scattered, and low and uplifted mind.

Chinese Source

如說有貪心如實知有貪心。乃至廣說。有二義故。心名有貪一貪相雜故。二貪所繫故。有說。此中依相雜有貪而作論。有說。此中依二種有貪而作論。所以者何。若唯依相雜有貪而作論者則有漏善。

Ru-Yi Translation

As it is said, one, having a mind with craving, knows as it really is that one has a mind with craving — and so on at length. There are two senses in which a mind is named "with craving": first, that craving is mixed together with it; and second, that it is bound by craving. Some say: here, the treatise is composed with reference to a mind with craving in the sense of being mixed with it. Some say: here, the treatise is composed with reference to both senses of a mind with craving. Why is this? Because, were the treatise composed with reference solely to the sense of being mixed with craving, then a contaminated wholesome mind,

及無覆無記心等應亦名離貪心。然彼亦是有貪心貪所繫故。如有貪心應知有瞋心等亦爾。此中貪所繫故名有貪心。貪對治故名離貪心。如是說者好。謂貪所繫故名有貪心。貪對治故名離貪心。若說貪相應故名有貪心。貪不相應故名離貪心者則貪不相應餘煩惱相應心等應名離貪心。然彼亦是有貪心貪所繫故。若說貪相應故名有貪心。貪對治故名離貪心者。則貪不相應染污心及無覆無記一分善心。應非有貪心亦非離貪心。然彼亦是有貪心貪所繫故。有貪心如實知有貪心。此一智謂世俗智。離貪心如實知離貪心。此四智謂法類世俗道皆如前釋。瞋所繫故名有瞋心。瞋對治故名離瞋心廣說如貪。有瞋心如實知有瞋心。此一智謂世俗如前釋。離瞋心如實知離瞋心。此三智謂法世俗道。此中依近對治說故不說類智。以類智品道非欲界近對治故餘如前釋。癡所繫故名有癡心。癡對治故名離癡心。如是說者好。謂癡所繫故名有癡心。癡對治故名離癡心。若說癡相應故名有癡心。癡不相應故名離癡心者。則無覆無記一分善心。亦應名離癡心。然彼亦名有癡心癡所繫故。若說癡相應故名有癡心。癡對治故名離癡心者。則無覆無記一分善心應非有癡心。亦非離癡心。然彼亦是有癡心癡所繫故。有癡心如實知有癡心。此一智謂世俗。離癡心如實知離癡心。此四智謂法類世俗道如前釋。染心者謂染污心煩惱相應故。不染心者謂善心煩惱相違故。有說。善心無覆無記心皆名不染心煩惱不相應故。染心如實知染心此一智謂世俗。不染心如實知不染心。此四智謂法類世俗道如前有癡離癡心釋。略心者。謂善心於所緣略攝故。散心者謂染污心於所緣馳散故。略心如實知略心。此四智謂法類世俗道。散心如實知散心此一智謂世俗。此隨所應如前釋。迦濕彌羅外諸師言。略心者謂眠相應心以說心略名眠故。如說眠云何。謂眠者所有眠夢不能持身心略為性。問若爾者。此中所說云何通。如說略心如實知略心此四智。乃至廣說。答此文應作是說。略心散心下心如實知略心散心下心此一智謂世俗。舉心如實知舉心此四智如前說。評曰。彼不應作是說。

As for "a defiled mind": this refers to a defiled mind, since it is conjoined with affliction. As for "an undefiled mind": this refers to a wholesome mind, since it runs contrary to affliction. Some say: a wholesome mind and a neutral, unobscured mind are both alike named an undefiled mind, since neither is conjoined with affliction. One knows as it really is a defiled mind by a single cognition alone — namely, the conventional cognition. One knows as it really is an undefiled mind by these four cognitions — namely, the cognition of the Dharma, the subsequent cognition, the conventional cognition, and the cognition of the path — to be explained as with the mind with delusion and the mind free of delusion above. As for "a contracted mind": this refers to a wholesome mind, since it gathers itself in briefly with respect to its object. As for "a scattered mind": this refers to a defiled mind, since it races and scatters with respect to its object. One knows as it really is a contracted mind by these four cognitions — namely, the cognition of the Dharma, the subsequent cognition, the conventional cognition, and the cognition of the path. One knows as it really is a scattered mind by a single cognition alone — namely, the conventional cognition — each correspondingly to be explained as before. Masters outside Kashmir say: "a contracted mind" refers to a mind conjoined with sleep, since it is said that a mind contracted is named sleep — as it is said, what is sleep? It is that which, possessing dreams, is unable to sustain the body and mind, its nature being one of contraction. Question: if so, then how is what is stated here to be reconciled — as it is said, one knows as it really is a contracted mind by these four cognitions, and so on at length? 4. Small Mind and Great Mind

Confronting the plain fact that defiled minds vastly outnumber wholesome ones in the world, the treatise offers seven independent arguments for why a wholesome mind is nonetheless called "great" and a defiled one "small" — by purity rather than frequency, by cost, by roots, by concomitant aggregates, by future cultivation, and by the sheer disproportion between a single moment's patient acceptance severing ten latent tendencies at once and no defiled mind ever possessing comparable power.

6. Observing One's Own Increase and Decrease of Craving, Hatred, and Delusion

Vivid similes from ordinary life — a man's craving escalating from childhood through his prime, anger mounting from unspoken irritation to plotted revenge, a heretic's delusion deepening as he masters false doctrine — illustrate what counts as the weak, middling, and superior grades of affliction, with the mirror-image sequence offered for their decrease.

7. The Terminal Feeling at the Moment of Death

Settling what a sūtra means by the fierce, injurious feeling at the very moment of death — illustrated by King Sunetra's fainting after over-indulgence — the treatise defines it precisely as the feeling of the severing of the vital point and the extinguishing of the life faculty, and explains why the water, fire, and wind elements, but not earth, are capable of severing it.

8. The Buddha's Body and the Arrangement of the Joints

The fascicle closes by specifying exactly who experiences the severing of the vital point — every ordinary being and every kind of noble one except the Buddha, whose faculties are extinguished all at once rather than gradually — before a striking physiological hierarchy of joint-strength running from ordinary beings, through great beasts, nāga-strength and Nārāyaṇa-strength beings, up to the Buddha's own body, whose joints are coiled together like a dragon.
