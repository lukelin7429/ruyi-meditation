The Three Obstructions and the Naming of Avīci

Fascicle 115 classifies karma by the feeling it produces, works through an extended formal matrix of every scheme for classifying karma, explains how feeling divides between body and mind, and opens the treatise on the Three Obstructions — afflictive, karmic, and retributive — through the cases of Aṅgulimāla and Śrīgupta and four competing explanations for the name of Avīci hell. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 115 continues the Bad-Conduct Nyāsa (惡行納息第一之四), moving from karma classified by feeling through a long formal matrix of classification schemes, into the treatise's opening account of the Three Obstructions. Terms established across fascicles 93–114 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Karma Correlated with Feeling, and the Five Kinds of Feeling

Fascicle 115 opens with a new threefold classification of karma — that conducive to pleasant feeling, to painful feeling, and to neither-pleasant-nor-painful feeling — introduced through a careful fivefold analysis of what "feeling" itself means (feeling as own-nature, as presently manifest, as object-condition, as associated, and as ripening), before the treatise asks whether these karmas definitely produce the feeling named in them, canvassing several answers ranging from strict determinism to a looser sense in which the name merely marks which karma, among all possible karmas, is uniquely capable of producing that feeling when it does ripen.

Ru-Yi Translation

There are three karmas: bodily, verbal, and mental karma. There are further three karmas: karma conducive to pleasant feeling, karma conducive to painful feeling, and karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Question: Why compose this treatise? Answer: In order to analyze the meaning of the sūtra. For the sūtra speaks of karma conducive to pleasant feeling and so forth, but though it speaks in this way, it does not explain the matter fully — the fuller explanation is as given above. Some further say: although the earlier discussion analyzed bodily and the other two karmas, it did not yet analyze karma conducive to pleasant feeling and the rest; wishing now to analyze these, this treatise is composed. The term “feeling” spoken of here has five kinds in all: first, feeling as own-nature; second, feeling as presently manifest; third, feeling as object-condition; fourth, feeling as associated; fifth, feeling as ripening. Feeling as own-nature is as stated for the three feelings — pleasant feeling, painful feeling, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Feeling as presently manifest is as the Sūtra on the Gateway of the Great Cause of Conditions says: “Ānanda, you should know that when one experiences pleasant feeling, the other two feelings thereby cease. You should know that this pleasant feeling so experienced is impermanent, suffering, subject to dissolution and destruction, and free of self and what belongs to self; painful feeling and neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling should likewise be so known.” Feeling as object-condition is as the Vijñānakāya says: eye and form, taken as condition, give rise to eye-consciousness; the coming-together of the three brings about contact, and contact, taken as condition, brings about feeling. One should know that this feeling takes form, not the person, as its object — form is the condition, arisen from eye-contact, for the feeling that has the non-person as its object; and so it is, in full, up to mind and mental objects. Feeling as associated is as it is said: there are dharmas associated with pleasant feeling, dharmas associated with painful feeling, and dharmas associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. What are the dharmas associated with pleasant feeling? Those dharmas associated with pleasant feeling. What are the dharmas associated with painful feeling? Those dharmas associated with painful feeling. What are the dharmas associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling? Those dharmas associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Feeling as ripening is as stated here: karma conducive to pleasant feeling, karma conducive to painful feeling, karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Among these five kinds of feeling, it is with respect to feeling as ripening that this treatise is composed. What is karma conducive to pleasant feeling? Desire-realm wholesome karma, up to and including wholesome karma of the third dhyāna ground. What is karma conducive to painful feeling? Unwholesome karma. What is karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling?

謂廣果繫善業。及無色界繫善業。問順樂受業決定能感樂受異熟果耶。餘二受業為問亦爾。若決定者。此後所說當云何通。如後論言。頗有業不感身心受異熟。而感異熟耶。答有。謂諸業感色心不相應行異熟。若不定者。何故說名順樂受業。順苦受業。順不苦不樂受業。有說定感。謂順樂受業。決定能感樂受異熟。由此故名順樂受業。乃至順不苦不樂受業。決定能感不苦不樂受異熟。由此故名順不苦不樂受業。問若爾。後所說云何通。如說。頗有業不感身心受異熟。而感異熟耶。乃至廣說。答彼業定感彼受異熟。及感色心不相應行。然所感受不常現前。所感色等則恒相續。於所感受不現前時。說彼受業唯感色心不相應行。是故無過。如此則二文善通。復有說者。順樂受業不定。能感順樂受異熟。乃至順不苦不樂受業不定。能感不苦不樂受異熟。問若爾。何故此業名順樂受等耶。答順樂受業。雖不定感樂受異熟。然感樂受異熟者唯此業非餘。故說此為順樂受業。餘二受業所說亦爾。復有說者。順樂受業。雖不定感樂受異熟。然如樂受能與喜樂作所依止。令喜樂生相續而轉作安足處。餘異熟果亦能如是。故名順樂受業。順苦受業。

Brṛhatphala-realm wholesome karma, and formless-realm wholesome karma. Question: Does karma conducive to pleasant feeling definitely bring about the ripening of pleasant feeling? The question applies equally to the other two feeling-karmas. If it is definite, how is what is said afterward to be reconciled? For a later passage of the treatise says: Is there karma that does not bring about the ripening of bodily or mental feeling, yet still brings about a ripening? Answer: There is — namely karma that brings about the ripening of form and of factors dissociated from mind. If it is not definite, why then is it named karma conducive to pleasant feeling, karma conducive to painful feeling, karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling? Some say it is definitely so brought about: karma conducive to pleasant feeling definitely brings about the ripening of pleasant feeling, and for this reason is named karma conducive to pleasant feeling; and so on up to karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, which definitely brings about the ripening of neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, and for this reason is so named. Question: If so, how is the later passage to be reconciled — the statement that there is karma which does not bring about the ripening of bodily or mental feeling, yet still brings about a ripening, and so on in full? Answer: That karma definitely brings about the ripening of that feeling, and also brings about factors dissociated from mind; but the feeling it brings about does not always manifest, while the form and so forth it brings about continue without interruption. At a time when the feeling it brings about does not manifest, it is said that that feeling-karma brings about only factors dissociated from mind. Hence there is no fault, and the two passages are thereby well reconciled. Some further say: karma conducive to pleasant feeling is indefinite in bringing about the ripening of pleasant feeling, and so on up to karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling being indefinite in bringing about the ripening of neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Question: If so, why is this karma named karma conducive to pleasant feeling and the rest? Answer: Although karma conducive to pleasant feeling does not definitely bring about the ripening of pleasant feeling, still, whatever does bring about the ripening of pleasant feeling is only this karma and no other; hence it is called karma conducive to pleasant feeling — and what is said of the other two feeling-karmas is likewise so. Some further say: although karma conducive to pleasant feeling does not definitely bring about the ripening of pleasant feeling, still, just as pleasant feeling can serve as the support for joy and happiness, causing joy and happiness to arise and continue in succession, becoming their resting ground, so too can its other possible ripening-fruits do the same; hence it is named karma conducive to pleasant feeling. As for karma conducive to painful feeling —

雖不定感苦受異熟。然如苦受能與憂苦作所依止。令憂苦生相續而轉作安足處。餘異熟果亦能如是。故名順苦受業。順不苦不樂受業。雖不定感不苦不樂受異熟。然如不苦不樂受。不能與喜樂憂苦作所依止。不能令喜樂憂苦生。相續而轉作安足處。餘異熟果亦復如是。故名順不苦不樂受業。復有說者。如樂受能長養所依。餘異熟亦爾。故名順樂受業。如苦受能損害所依。餘異熟亦爾。故名順苦受業。如不苦不樂受。非能長養亦非損害所依。餘異熟亦爾。故名順不苦不樂受業。問欲界乃至第三靜慮。有不苦不樂受異熟不。

— although it does not definitely bring about the ripening of painful feeling, still, just as painful feeling can serve as the support for sorrow and suffering, causing sorrow and suffering to arise and continue in succession, becoming their resting ground, so too can its other possible ripening-fruits do the same; hence it is named karma conducive to painful feeling. As for karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling: although it does not definitely bring about the ripening of neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, still, just as neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling cannot serve as the support for joy, happiness, sorrow, or suffering, and cannot cause joy, happiness, sorrow, or suffering to arise and continue in succession, becoming their resting ground, so too are its other possible ripening-fruits likewise incapable; hence it is named karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Some further say: just as pleasant feeling can nourish that on which it depends, so too can its other ripening-fruits; hence it is named karma conducive to pleasant feeling. Just as painful feeling can injure that on which it depends, so too can its other ripening-fruits; hence it is named karma conducive to painful feeling. Just as neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling can neither nourish nor injure that on which it depends, so too are its other ripening-fruits; hence it is named karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Question: From the desire realm up through the third dhyāna, is there a ripening of neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling at all?

若有者。此中所說云何通。如說。云何順不苦不樂受業。謂廣果繫善業。及無色界繫善業。若無者。靜慮中間所有善業。當言能感何受異熟。或有說者。下地無有不苦不樂受異熟。所以者何。下地法麁。此受微細。下地不寂靜。此受寂靜。復有說者。下地有情所起善業。皆為求樂受故起。無有希求不苦不樂受者。是故下地所有善業。不感此受異熟。問彼亦無有求苦受者。何故下地感此異熟。答欲界有情為求樂故多造苦因。故雖不求而感彼異熟。捨受寂靜無有求樂受而造捨受業者。是故無彼異熟。問靜慮中間所有善業。當言能感何受異熟。答感初靜慮喜根異熟。復有說者。感初靜慮樂受異熟。問若爾。後文云何通。如說。頗有業感心受異熟。非身耶。答有。謂善無尋業。答彼文應作是說。謂善無尋無伺業。而不作是說者有何意耶。應知此中言勢減少。或有說者。靜慮中間所有善業。

If there is, how is what is said here to be reconciled — the statement, what is karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling? Brṛhatphala-realm wholesome karma, and formless-realm wholesome karma. If there is not, then what feeling-ripening should be said to be brought about by the wholesome karma of the intermediate dhyāna? Some say: the lower grounds have no ripening of neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Why is this? The lower grounds’ dharmas are coarse, while this feeling is subtle; the lower grounds are not calm, while this feeling is calm. Some further say: the wholesome karma arisen among beings of the lower grounds is entirely arisen for the sake of seeking pleasant feeling — none seek neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling; hence the wholesome karma of the lower grounds does not bring about the ripening of this feeling. Question: but neither do they seek painful feeling — why then does the lower ground bring about that ripening? Answer: beings of the desire realm, in seeking pleasure, create many causes of suffering; hence, though they do not seek it, they still bring about that ripening. Equanimity is calm, and there is no one who, seeking pleasant feeling, creates karma for equanimity; hence there is no such ripening. Question: what feeling-ripening should be said to be brought about by the wholesome karma of the intermediate dhyāna? Answer: it brings about the ripening of the joy faculty of the first dhyāna. Some further say: it brings about the ripening of the pleasure feeling of the first dhyāna. Question: if so, how is a later passage to be reconciled — the statement, is there karma that brings about the ripening of mental feeling but not bodily feeling? Answer: there is — namely wholesome karma free of investigation. Answer: that passage should properly say wholesome karma free of both investigation and analysis; as for why it is not so stated, what is the intent? One should know that the wording here has simply been abbreviated. Some further say: the wholesome karma of the intermediate dhyāna —

雖不感受異熟果。而能感色心不相應行。問若爾。何故此後論言。

— though it does not bring about the ripening of feeling, is still able to bring about factors dissociated from mind. Question: if so, why does the treatise go on to say —

善無尋業感心受耶。答但言能感心受非身。不言唯感心受異熟。非感色心不相應行。復有說者。下地亦有不苦不樂受異熟。問此中所說當云何通。如說。云何順不苦不樂受業。謂廣果繫善業。及無色界繫善業。答此中但顯順不苦不樂受業異熟最後邊際。謂第四靜慮是有色地邊。無色界是三界邊。復有說者。此中但顯此受異熟不共田器。誰是此受異熟不共田器。謂第四靜慮。及無色界。復有說者。下地雖有此受異熟。然為餘受所覆。相不明了不久相續。從廣果上更無餘受。唯有此受明了相續。是故偏說。諸說下地無不苦不樂受異熟者。彼說欲界下三靜慮阿羅漢等。住威儀心入涅槃。廣果阿羅漢。住威儀心異熟心入涅槃。無色界阿羅漢。住異熟心入涅槃。諸說下地亦有不苦不樂受異熟者。彼說欲界四靜慮阿羅漢等。

— that wholesome karma free of investigation brings about mental feeling? Answer: it merely says that it can bring about mental feeling and not bodily feeling; it does not say that it brings about only the ripening of mental feeling and not factors dissociated from mind. Some further say: the lower grounds too have a ripening of neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. Question: how then is what is said here to be reconciled — the statement, what is karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling? Brṛhatphala-realm wholesome karma, and formless-realm wholesome karma. Answer: this passage shows only the very furthest limit of the ripening of karma conducive to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling — the fourth dhyāna is the limit of the realm possessing form, and the formless realm is the limit of the three realms. Some further say: this passage shows only the field and vessel unshared by this feeling’s ripening — and what is the field and vessel unshared by this feeling’s ripening? The fourth dhyāna, and the formless realm. Some further say: although the lower grounds do have this feeling’s ripening, it is covered over by other feelings, its character unclear and not lasting long in succession; from Bṛhatphala upward there is no other feeling at all, only this feeling, clear and continuous — hence it is spoken of exclusively there. Those who say the lower grounds have no ripening of neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling hold that arhats of the desire realm and the lower three dhyānas enter nirvāṇa abiding in a deportment-mind; arhats of Bṛhatphala enter nirvāṇa abiding in either a deportment-mind or a ripening-mind; and arhats of the formless realm enter nirvāṇa abiding in a ripening-mind. Those who say the lower grounds too have a ripening of neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling hold that arhats of the desire realm and the four dhyānas —

住威儀心異熟心入涅槃。無色界阿羅漢。住異熟心入涅槃。

— enter nirvāṇa abiding in either a deportment-mind or a ripening-mind, while arhats of the formless realm enter nirvāṇa abiding in a ripening-mind.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The three competing answers to whether karma "conducive to pleasant feeling" must in fact produce pleasant feeling quietly rehearse a live philosophical worry about how names track dispositions rather than guarantees: the treatise's own preferred move — that the name survives even when the correlation is only reliable rather than absolute, so long as no other karma could produce that feeling instead — lets a naming convention remain stable and useful without ever overstating what it actually promises.

2. The Subsumption Matrix Opens

A long and highly formal stretch of the treatise now sets to work testing whether each of the many threefold schemes for classifying karma already established — bodily, verbal, and mental; past, future, and present; wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral; the trainee's, the adept's, and neither; and karma bound to each of the three realms — fully subsumes, is fully subsumed by, or merely intersects with each of the others, methodically identifying in every case exactly which residual category of karma escapes the subsuming scheme entirely.

Chinese Source

已說前後三業自性。今當顯示雜無雜相。為前攝後。後攝前耶。答前攝後。非後攝前。不攝者何。謂無記業無漏業。此中前三業通有記無記。後三業唯有記。前三業通有漏無漏業。後三業唯有漏。是故言前攝後非後攝前。不攝者何。謂無記業及無漏業。三業謂身語意業。復有三業。謂過去未來現在業。復有三業謂善不善無記業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having explained the intrinsic nature of the earlier and later sets of three karmas, we should now show whether they mix with one another or not — does the earlier subsume the later, or the later the earlier? Answer: the earlier subsumes the later, not the later the earlier. What is not subsumed? Neutral karma and undefiled karma. Here the earlier three karmas are common to both determinate and neutral, while the later three karmas are only determinate; the earlier three karmas are common to both contaminated and undefiled, while the later three karmas are only contaminated. Hence it is said that the earlier subsumes the later, not the later the earlier, and what is not subsumed is neutral karma and undefiled karma. There are three karmas: bodily, verbal, and mental karma. There are further three karmas: past, future, and present karma. There are further three karmas: wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral karma.

復有三業。謂學無學非學非無學業。復有三業。謂見所斷修所斷無斷業。為前攝後。後攝前耶。答隨其事展轉相攝。所以者何。以身語意業自性。或過去或未來或現在。或善或不善或無記。或學或無學或非學非無學。或見所斷或修所斷或無斷。故言隨其事展轉相攝。

There are further three karmas: the trainee’s, the adept’s, and neither the trainee’s nor the adept’s karma. There are further three karmas: karma abandoned by seeing, abandoned by cultivation, and not to be abandoned. Does the earlier subsume the later, or the later the earlier? Answer: they subsume one another according to their respective matters. Why is this? Because the intrinsic nature of bodily, verbal, and mental karma is itself either past, future, or present; either wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral; either the trainee’s, the adept’s, or neither the trainee’s nor the adept’s; either abandoned by seeing, abandoned by cultivation, or not to be abandoned. Hence it is said that they subsume one another according to their respective matters.

三業謂身語意業。復有三業。謂欲色無色界繫業。為前攝後。後攝前耶。答前攝後。非後攝前。不攝者何。謂無漏業。此中前三業通有漏無漏。後三業唯有漏。是故言前攝後非後攝前。不攝者何。謂無漏業。

There are three karmas: bodily, verbal, and mental karma. There are further three karmas: karma bound to the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm. Does the earlier subsume the later, or the later the earlier? Answer: the earlier subsumes the later, not the later the earlier. What is not subsumed? Undefiled karma. Here the earlier three karmas are common to both contaminated and undefiled, while the later three karmas are only contaminated. Hence it is said that the earlier subsumes the later, not the later the earlier, and what is not subsumed is undefiled karma.

四業如前說。三業謂順現法受等業。為四攝三。三攝四耶。答應作四句。有四非三。謂能斷諸業學思。欲界繫善不善不定業。色界繫善不定業。有三非四。謂無色界繫善決定業。有四亦三。謂欲界繫善不善決定業。及色界繫善決定業。有非四非三。謂除能斷諸業學思。餘無漏業。無色界繫善不定業。及無記業。四業如前說。三業謂順樂受等業。為四攝三。三攝四耶。答應作四句。有四非三。謂能斷諸業學思。有三非四。謂無色界繫善業。有四亦三。謂欲界繫善不善業。色界繫善業。有非四非三。謂除能斷諸業學思。餘無漏業。及無記業。

8. The Three Obstructions Begin: What Is the Afflictive Obstruction?

9. Blazing and Sharp: A Tetralemma for Affliction and Wholesome Roots

A precise distinction is drawn between an affliction's presently manifest possession (which alone establishes the obstruction, since mere latent possession would erase all difference among beings) and its two independent qualities of being "blazing" (frequently active) and "sharp" (of superior grade) — yielding a tetralemma in which, surprisingly, a mild-but-frequent affliction constitutes the true obstruction, escalating by increments toward cutting off the wholesome roots, while a severe-but-infrequent affliction does not, since its very infrequency lets it be gradually worn away instead. 10. The Karmic Obstruction: The Five Heinous Deeds, and Whether They Can Be Averted

The karmic obstruction is defined as the five ānantarya deeds — killing one's mother, one's father, or an arhat, causing a schism in the saṅgha, and maliciously drawing a buddha's blood — singled out from other equally grave misdeeds through five converging reasons (their fixed and extremely grave nature, their exclusive destination in hell, their fixed timing in the very next life, their uniformly disagreeable fruit, and the ease of identifying who commits them). A genuine dispute then follows over whether the preparatory action leading to such a deed is itself fixed in outcome, resolved through the celebrated cases of Aṅgulimāla and Śrīgupta, both of whom plotted against the Buddha yet were "turned back" — not because ānantarya karma can be undone, but because neither had, in fact, yet directed his lethal preparation at the Omniscient One as such. The retributive obstruction is then defined as birth in hell, the animal or ghost realms, Uttarakuru, or the heaven of no perception.

— namely, this one is capable of killing his mother, this one his father, and so on up to this one being capable of drawing a buddha’s blood. Apart from these five, all other good and evil conduct is without these five causes and conditions of being easy to see and easy to know, and hence is not so spoken of.

問諸無間加行能滿彼果業。此於彼果為定不定。若言定者此中何故不說。又尊者指鬘室利毱多云何能轉。若不定者。害生命納息所說云何通。如說頗有未害生殺生未滅。此業異熟定生地獄耶。答有如作無間業加行時命終。或有說者。此業於彼果定。問害生命納息則為善通。而此中何故不說。答此中應說五無間業及彼加行。而不說者有何意耶。答此已說在五無間中。五無間業用此為加行故。若說果當知已說加行。問尊者指鬘云何能轉。答彼猶未作無間加行。是故彼說我今且未殺母且當飯食。問豈非欲害一切智耶。答爾時彼於非一切智起害加行。非於一切智。由是因緣世尊化作凡流苾芻。入踏婆林勿彼尊者。於一切智起殺加行不可救療。若諸有情於一切智起殺加行。如殑伽沙數如來應正等覺。亦不能救令脫地獄。故知彼於非一切智起殺加行。非於一切智。問室利毱多云何能轉。答彼亦不作無間加行。是故彼雖密設火穽及雜毒食。而心念言。如來若是一切智者自當避之。若非一切智者便當殄滅。勿令幻惑食噉世間故。彼非於一切智所起殺加行是以可轉。復有說者。此業於彼果不定。問此中不說則為善通。尊者指鬘室利毱多業亦可轉。害生命納息當云何通。答諸無間加行能滿彼果業。此於彼果有定有不定。害生命納息說。彼定者。尊者指鬘室利毱多所可轉易。是不定者。如是二說俱為善通。云何異熟障。謂諸有情處。那落迦。傍生。鬼界。北拘盧洲。無想天處。問餘洲亦有異熟為障。如扇搋迦半擇迦無形二形等。此中何故不說。答此中應說而不說者當知此是有餘之說。是以前說此中三障皆有餘說。復有說者。此中但說決定為障。

Question: as for the preparatory action of the ānantarya deeds that fulfills that karmic fruit — is this fixed or not fixed with respect to that fruit? If it is said to be fixed, then why is this not so stated here? Moreover, how could the Venerable Aṅgulimāla and Śrīgupta have been able to be turned back from it? If it is not fixed, then how is what is said in the Nyāsa on the Taking of Life to be reconciled — the statement, is there one who has not yet killed a living being, whose killing is not yet completed, whose karmic ripening is nonetheless certain to bring about rebirth in hell? Answer: there is — as when one dies at the very moment of undertaking the preparatory action for an ānantarya deed. Some further say: this karma is fixed with respect to that fruit. Question: then the Nyāsa on the Taking of Life is indeed well reconciled — but why is this not stated here? Answer: here it should properly speak of the five ānantarya deeds together with their preparatory action, and as for why it is not so stated, what is the intent? Answer: this has already been stated as included within the five ānantarya deeds themselves, since the five ānantarya deeds make use of this as their preparatory action; once the fruit has been stated, one should know that the preparatory action has thereby already been stated. Question: how was the Venerable Aṅgulimāla able to be turned back? Answer: he had not yet undertaken the ānantarya preparatory action itself; hence he said, “For now I shall not yet kill my mother — for now I shall first eat my meal.” Question: but was he not intent on killing the Omniscient One? Answer: at that time he had undertaken the preparatory action of killing directed toward one who was not the Omniscient One, not toward the Omniscient One himself. For this reason the World-Honored One transformed himself into an ordinary bhikṣu and entered the Tāḍa Grove to intercept him, since if that venerable one had undertaken the preparatory action of killing directed at the Omniscient One himself, it could never have been remedied — for if any being undertakes the preparatory action of killing directed at the Omniscient One, then even as many tathāgata-arhat-fully-awakened-ones as there are grains of sand in the Ganges could not save him from hell. Hence one knows that he undertook the preparatory action of killing directed toward one who was not the Omniscient One, not toward the Omniscient One himself. Question: how was Śrīgupta able to be turned back? Answer: he too had not undertaken the ānantarya preparatory action itself; hence, although he secretly prepared a pit of fire and food laced with poison, he thought to himself, “If the Tathāgata is indeed omniscient, he will surely avoid it of his own accord; if he is not omniscient, he shall then be destroyed, lest he go on deceiving and devouring the world.” Since the preparatory action of killing he undertook was not directed at the Omniscient One as such, it could therefore be turned back. Some further say: this karma is not fixed with respect to that fruit. Question: then it is indeed well reconciled that it is not stated here, and the karma of the Venerable Aṅgulimāla and Śrīgupta can likewise be turned back — but how then is the Nyāsa on the Taking of Life to be reconciled? Answer: the preparatory action of the ānantarya deeds that fulfills that karmic fruit is, with respect to that fruit, in some cases fixed and in some cases not fixed. What the Nyāsa on the Taking of Life speaks of as fixed refers to what could not be turned back; the Venerable Aṅgulimāla and Śrīgupta represent what could be turned back, that is, what is not fixed — and so both statements are thereby well reconciled. What is the retributive obstruction? It is birth in any of these states among beings: hell, the animal realm, the realm of hungry ghosts, the northern continent of Uttarakuru, and the heaven of no perception. Question: but other continents too have a ripening that constitutes an obstruction, as with paṇḍakas, hermaphrodites, and those without sexual organs or with both — why is this not spoken of here? Answer: this too should properly be spoken of, and as for why it is not so stated, one should know that this too is an incomplete teaching; hence it was said above that all three obstructions here have incomplete teachings. Some further say: here only what is fixed is spoken of as an obstruction —

彼非決定。由彼有情所有異熟。或有為障或不為障是以不說。

— what is not fixed being excluded, since for those beings whatever ripening they have may or may not constitute an obstruction, and hence it is not spoken of.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The resolution of the Aṅgulimāla and Śrīgupta cases is a remarkably careful piece of casuistry: rather than simply declaring ānantarya karma sometimes reversible, the treatise preserves its fixed, irrevocable character completely by relocating the loophole into a technicality of intent — neither man had yet, at the moment his plot was foiled, formed a lethal intention specifically toward the Buddha's omniscience itself, so nothing irreversible had actually been set into motion at all.

11. How Many Obstructions One Continuum Can Hold, and Which Is Heaviest

Two closing questions round out the three-obstructions discussion: how many can coexist within a single continuum (never all three without the afflictive obstruction present, since it alone can give rise to the other two), and which is heaviest — with opinion split between the retributive obstruction (irreversible once its fruit has ripened, however reversible its cause once was) and the karmic or afflictive obstructions (weightier for standing further upstream in the causal chain that produces the other two).

Chinese Source

問如是三障於一相續可成就幾。答或但成一。謂於三種隨一一成就或成就二。謂煩惱障業障。或煩惱障異熟障。無有成就業障異熟障非煩惱障者。由此亦無成就三者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: of these three obstructions, how many can a single continuum possess at once? Answer: it may possess only one — any single one of the three; or it may possess two — the afflictive obstruction together with the karmic obstruction, or the afflictive obstruction together with the retributive obstruction. There is no possessing of the karmic obstruction and the retributive obstruction without the afflictive obstruction, and hence there is likewise no possessing of all three at once.

問如是三障何者最重。或有說者。異熟障重。所以者何。因時可轉。果時不可轉故。復有說者。業障最重所以者何。業障能引異熟障故如是說者。煩惱障重。以煩惱障能引業障。業障復能引異熟障。如是皆以煩惱為本是故最重。

Question: of these three obstructions, which is the heaviest? Some say: the retributive obstruction is heaviest, for what reason? Because at the time of the cause it can still be turned back, while at the time of the fruit it can no longer be turned back. Some further say: the karmic obstruction is heaviest, for what reason? Because the karmic obstruction is able to draw forth the retributive obstruction. According to this account: the afflictive obstruction is heaviest, since the afflictive obstruction is able to draw forth the karmic obstruction, and the karmic obstruction is in turn able to draw forth the retributive obstruction — thus all are rooted in affliction, and for this reason it is heaviest.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The disagreement over which obstruction is "heaviest" turns out to hinge on two entirely different, unstated criteria — reversibility at a given moment, versus causal priority in the chain that produces the others — and the treatise's willingness to let both stand as valid answers suggests that "heaviest" was never a single well-defined measure to begin with, only a question that different vantage points answer differently.

12. Three Kinds of Greatest Sin

Among the three grave forms of evil conduct, schism-causing false speech is named the very greatest, since it alone brings a full eon's worth of suffering in Avīci — yet elsewhere mental karma is named greatest, and elsewhere still wrong view, an apparent conflict resolved through no fewer than six different classificatory lenses (karma versus affliction versus evil conduct; the three karmas; the five views; the five ānantarya deeds; the paths abandoned by seeing versus cultivation; and volition itself versus what volition produces versus what is not karma at all) — before a final, technical question asks why false speech alone, rather than the accompanying bodily and mental karma also present, is blamed for the schism itself, since only false speech remains active at every stage from preparation through to completion.

Chinese Source

三惡行中何者最大罪。謂破僧虛誑語。此業能取無間地獄一劫壽量異熟苦果。餘業不定故。問此說破僧虛誑語為最大罪。餘處復說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the three kinds of evil conduct, which carries the greatest sin? It is the false speech that causes a schism in the saṅgha, for this karma is able to bring about a ripened fruit of suffering in Avīci hell lasting a full eon in duration, while other karmas are not fixed in this way. Question: this passage says that the false speech causing schism is the greatest sin, yet elsewhere it is said —

意業為最大罪。餘處復說。邪見為最大罪。此三大罪有何差別。或有說者。罪有三種。一業。二煩惱。三惡行。業中意業為大罪。煩惱中邪見為大罪。惡行中破僧虛誑語為大罪。復有說者。惱亂大眾故意業為大罪。滅一切善根故邪見為大罪。能感大苦異熟果故。破僧虛誑語為大罪。復有說者。三業中意業為大罪。五見中邪見為大罪。五無間業中破僧虛誑語為大罪。復有說者。見所斷業道中邪見為大罪。修所斷業道中。破僧虛誑語為大罪。見修所斷業中意業為大罪。復有說者。依思業故說意業為大罪。依思所造業故。說破僧虛誑語為大罪。依非業故說邪見為大罪。復有說者。依能起業故說意業為大罪。依所起業故說破僧虛誑語為大罪。依非業故說邪見為大罪。復有說者。依能轉業故說意業為大罪。依所轉業故。說破僧虛誑語為大罪。依非業故說邪見為大罪。是名三種大罪差別。

— that mental karma is the greatest sin, and elsewhere still it is said that wrong view is the greatest sin. What distinguishes these three great sins? Some say: sin is of three kinds — karma, affliction, and evil conduct. Among karmas, mental karma is the great sin; among afflictions, wrong view is the great sin; among evil conduct, the false speech causing schism is the great sin. Some further say: because it disturbs the great assembly, mental karma is the great sin; because it destroys every wholesome root, wrong view is the great sin; because it is able to bring about a fruit of great suffering as its ripening, the false speech causing schism is the great sin. Some further say: among the three karmas, mental karma is the great sin; among the five views, wrong view is the great sin; among the five ānantarya deeds, the false speech causing schism is the great sin. Some further say: among the paths of karma abandoned by seeing, wrong view is the great sin; among the paths of karma abandoned by cultivation, the false speech causing schism is the great sin; among karma abandoned by both seeing and cultivation, mental karma is the great sin. Some further say: with respect to karma that is itself volition, mental karma is called the great sin; with respect to karma brought about by volition, the false speech causing schism is called the great sin; with respect to what is not karma at all, wrong view is called the great sin. Some further say: with respect to karma that initiates, mental karma is called the great sin; with respect to karma that is initiated, the false speech causing schism is called the great sin; with respect to what is not karma at all, wrong view is called the great sin. Some further say: with respect to karma that turns, mental karma is called the great sin; with respect to karma that is turned, the false speech causing schism is called the great sin; with respect to what is not karma at all, wrong view is called the great sin. These are called the distinctions among the three great sins.

問彼破僧時。亦有身業往來加行思惟及餘語業。何故但說虛誑語能破僧耶。答若破僧時加行究竟。一切時有者此中說之。於諸業中唯虛誑語加行究竟。一切時有能令僧壞是故偏說。餘業不爾是故不說。

Question: at the time of causing a schism, there is also bodily karma in the coming and going of the preparatory action, deliberation, and other verbal karma as well — why then is it said that false speech alone is able to cause the schism? Answer: whatever is present at every stage, from preparatory action through to completion, at the time of causing the schism, is what is spoken of here. Among all these karmas, only false speech is present at every stage, from preparatory action through to completion, capable of causing the saṅgha’s ruin, and hence it is spoken of exclusively; the other karmas are not like this, and hence are not so spoken of.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Six different lenses each crowning a different candidate as the "greatest sin" is not incoherence but a demonstration of method: each ranking is perfectly consistent once its own criterion is made explicit, and the treatise's real point may be less about which sin truly wins than about how thoroughly the answer depends on which classificatory frame is doing the judging.

13. Why Is It Called Avīci? Four Theories on a Name

The fascicle closes with a meditation on etymology: Avīci, the "hell without interval," first receives the caution that a name need not perfectly track its referent's own nature, before four candidate explanations are weighed — that no pleasant feeling ever manifests there even briefly, unlike the fleeting, non-retributive respites (a cool breeze, a voice calling "revive, revive!") granted in other hells; that the sheer number of beings born there leaves no gap between them (explicitly rejected, on the analogy that beings capable of the requisite extreme evil, like those capable of the requisite extreme good needed for the Peak of Existence, are in fact rare); and finally the accepted answer — that beings who commit such grave evil obtain bodies so vast that, filling every possible space within the hell, they leave no interval anywhere at all.

Chinese Source

問如說能取無間地獄果。何故名無間地獄耶。答此假立名假立想。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: as it is said that this karma is able to bring about the fruit of Avīci hell — why is it named the hell of no interval? Answer: this is a name conventionally established, a notion conventionally established;

不必如名悉有其義。又此地獄亦名無間。亦名熱鐵猛焰熾然攢射支體。亦名常於六觸處門受諸苦惱。亦名自受業所招苦。復有說者。

it need not be the case that the name in every respect corresponds to the meaning. Moreover, this hell is also called “no interval,” also called “where blazing flames of molten iron pierce the limbs like arrows,” also called “where one constantly experiences all manner of suffering and affliction through the gateways of the six sense-contacts,” and also called “suffering brought upon oneself by one’s own karma.” Some further say:

以於此中無間無隙可令樂受暫現在前故名無間。問餘地獄中。為有歌舞及飲食等喜樂事耶。答餘地獄中雖無異熟喜樂。而有等流喜樂。如施設論說。等活地獄有時有分涼風暫吹。或聞如是音聲唱言。等活等活。時彼有情忽然還活。支節血肉平復如本。暫生喜樂。無間地獄無如是事故名無間。復有說者。生彼有情其數甚多。

— it is because, within this hell, there is no interval, no gap at all, that could allow pleasant feeling even briefly to manifest, that it is named “no interval.” Question: in the other hells, are there matters of joy and pleasure such as song, dance, and food and drink? Answer: although the other hells have no pleasure that is itself a ripening, they do have a pleasure that is merely of the same outflow. As the Prajñaptiśāstra says: in the Reviving Hell, there are times when a cool breeze occasionally blows, or when one hears a voice calling out, “Revive, revive!” — at which those beings suddenly come back to life, their limbs, flesh, and blood restored just as before, giving rise to a brief joy and pleasure. Avīci hell has no such matter, and hence is named “no interval.” Some further say: the beings born there are so vast in number —

無間無隙故名無間。此說不然。所以者何。上品惡行生彼地獄。世間有情不皆能起上品惡行。如要修習上品妙行方生有頂。世間有情不皆能起上品妙行。是故生有頂者少。生無間者亦爾。故彼非說。

— that there is no interval, no gap between them, and hence it is named “no interval.” This account, however, is not so. Why is this? It is only through superior-grade evil conduct that one is born in that hell, and beings of the world are not all capable of undertaking superior-grade evil conduct — just as it is only through the cultivation of superior-grade good conduct that one is born at the Peak of Existence, and beings of the world are not all capable of undertaking superior-grade good conduct; hence those born at the Peak of Existence are few, and likewise those born in Avīci are few. Hence that account does not hold.

問若爾云何名無間。答依異熟果說名無間。以諸有情造大惡業生彼地獄。得廣大身一一身形悉皆廣大。遍彼多處中無間隙故名無間。

Question: if so, why then is it named “no interval”? Answer: it is named “no interval” with respect to the ripened fruit — because the beings who create such great evil karma and are born in that hell obtain vast and enormous bodies, each one of whose forms is utterly vast, filling up every place throughout that hell without any interval or gap remaining, it is therefore named “no interval.”

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The treatise's own explicit rejection of the population-density theory of Avīci's name, on the elegant ground that only a rare few are ever capable of the extreme evil required to be reborn there at all, is a quietly important corrective: this most feared of all hells is not, on the tradition's own reckoning, densely crowded because such wickedness is common, but sparsely populated because it is vanishingly rare — its horror lies in the enormity of what each individual body there endures, not in numbers.
