The Odometer of the Path

Fascicle 92 works through full comprehension and realization through cessation across each faculty's own presence, cessation, and path of cutting-off, then cross-references possession against a new fivefold bondage classification — tracking a practitioner's own exact progress from ordinary being to full liberation as a precise, countable reduction. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 92 complete, 7 sections Source: Taishō T1545, vol. 27, fascicle 92 Topic: 十門納息 → 遍知 → 滅作證 → 九結三縛十隨眠六垢十纏 (full comprehension, realization through cessation, the fivefold bondage classification), Fetters Aggregate

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 92 continues the Ten Gates Nyāsa (結蘊第二中十門納息第四之二十二), completing possession (成就) for the path of cutting-off, then working through full comprehension (遍知) and realization through cessation (滅作證), before opening a fivefold bondage classification — the nine fetters (九結), three bonds (三縛), ten latent tendencies (十隨眠, a distinct tenfold reckoning), six defilements (六垢), and ten entanglements (十纏) — cross-referenced against possession and tracked stage by stage across the entire path. Terms established in earlier fascicles recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

Classical text is set in the left-hand column below, our English in the right.

1. Possession, Concluded: The Path of Cutting-Off

Fascicle 92 opens exactly where fascicle 91 broke off, closing out the possession gateway for each faculty's own path of cutting-off — the eye faculty's own path of cutting-off, taken as object both directly and as an object-of-that-object, possessed by every sentient being without exception, while its own increasing latent tendency is possessed only by one not yet free of the formless realm's own defilement.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the bare path of cutting-off is possessed universally, while the latent tendency that increases with respect to it is possessed only conditionally, captures a genuinely important distinction this whole apparatus depends on: the path itself, as an object available to be taken up by any mind whatsoever, belongs to everyone, but the affliction capable of clinging to that object belongs only to those who have not yet advanced far enough to have exhausted it.

2. Full Comprehension, Applied to Direct Presence, Cessation, and the Path of Cutting-Off

The full-comprehension gateway is now worked through for all three modifiers in turn: the eye faculty's own direct-object full comprehension is attained at the exhaustion of craving for the sphere of infinite space, with no fetter yet exhausted at that point; its own cessation, attained at the exhaustion of craving for form, exhausts an ordinary being's own thirty-one latent tendencies or a noble one's own three, again with no fetter yet exhausted; and its own path of cutting-off, attained at the exhaustion of craving for the formless, exhausts three latent tendencies and three fetters at once.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the path of cutting-off is the only one of the three modifiers whose full comprehension actually exhausts a fetter, while the faculty's own bare presence and its own cessation both attain full comprehension with no fetter exhausted at all, reveals something precise about what a fetter actually tracks: a fetter is bound to the completed, functioning path of cutting-off specifically, not to the mere fact of craving for a given ground having ended, since it is only the path itself — the active engagement with cutting off affliction — that the fetters' own final exhaustion is keyed to.

3. Realization Through Cessation, Applied to All Three Modifiers

The parallel realization-through-cessation gateway is worked through with the same three modifiers, its own answers converging on a single dramatic threshold: for nearly every faculty, whether taken directly, as its own cessation, or as its own path of cutting-off, the complete realization of all ninety-eight latent tendencies and all nine fetters occurs only upon attaining the fruit of arhatship itself — the intermediate stages exhausting only a handful of latent tendencies at a time, with no fetter yet fully realized, until that final threshold is crossed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That each faculty carries its own distinct threshold ground before its own cessation can first be said to be unpossessed — the second dhyāna for the sexed faculties, the third for pleasure, the fourth for joy — is a quiet confirmation of exactly where each faculty itself ceases to operate: a faculty's own cessation cannot become unpossessed at some arbitrary point in the cosmology, only at the specific ground beyond which the faculty itself, and therefore anything that could be a cessation of it, no longer has anywhere left to be found.

7. Non-Possession, Cross-Referenced with the Fivefold Bondage Classification

The fascicle closes with the mirror image of the entire preceding cross-reference: for whoever does not possess a given faculty — its own bare presence, its own cessation, its own path of cutting-off, taken directly or as an object-of-that-object — the same fivefold count of fetters, bonds, latent tendencies, defilements, and entanglements is worked out symmetrically, closing with the treatise's own final gesture handing the remaining permutations over to the reader: the remaining gateways should be discerned and stated according to the pattern already given.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That non-possession of a faculty can itself carry a nonzero count of fetters and latent tendencies — someone who does not yet possess the body faculty at all is still bound by six fetters and nine latent tendencies, not zero — is a genuinely counterintuitive result worth pausing on: not possessing a faculty is not the same as having transcended everything that faculty is bound up with, since the very absence of a faculty (as in the formless realm, which lacks form altogether) can itself still be accompanied by whatever affliction belongs to the state of not yet having progressed past needing that faculty in the first place.
