Same Realm, Different Realm

Fascicle 72 continues the Ten Gates Nyāsa's opening chapter on the eighteen elements: citta, manas, and vijñāna distinguished by name, time, and function, then an exhaustive technical ledger of whether a faculty, its object, and the resulting consciousness must all share one realm — worked through eye and color, the body, nose, tongue, touch, and finally mind across every dhyāna, the fourteen transformation-minds, and death and rebirth itself. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 72 continues the Ten Gates Nyāsa (結蘊第二中十門納息第四之二), still within its opening chapter on the eighteen elements. Much of this fascicle consists of an exhaustive, highly repetitive technical enumeration — the treatise's own preferred method for proving a general principle by working through every one of its possible combinations rather than merely asserting it. Terms established in earlier fascicles recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Mind-Consciousness Defined, and Citta, Manas, Vijñāna: Same Word Three Times, or Three Genuine Distinctions?

Opening part two of the Ten Gates Nyāsa, the treatise defines mind-consciousness exactly as it defined eye-consciousness, then explains why the six consciousness-elements alone receive no separate counterpart-class treatment — they are defined by their own arising and function, and are, in any case, already gathered within the mind-element's own broader treatment. The treatise then turns to whether citta, manas, and vijñāna name three genuinely different things or merely one thing three times over, illustrated by fire's own ten names or Śakra's own ten epithets, before offering a rich alternative: the three do differ, by name, by time (past named manas, future named citta, present named vijñāna), by classificatory home, by root meaning, and above all by function — citta traveling far, manas leading the way, vijñāna sustaining rebirth's own continuity, each illustrated by its own verse.

Ru-Yi Translation

Continuing the Fetters Aggregate within the Ten Gates Nyāsa, part two. One asks: what is the mind-consciousness-element? The answer: mind-consciousness, arising conditioned by mind and by dharma, is named the mind-consciousness-element. The questions and answers analyzing this here should be understood exactly as with the eye-consciousness-element. One asks: for what reason do the six consciousness-elements not speak of any counterpart-class at all? The answer: that this ought to be stated, yet is not stated, should be understood to carry a further meaning here. Further, the six consciousness-elements are manifest precisely through their own arising — a mind established with reference to arising has no counterpart-class established for it, since only what has actually arisen is spoken of as a same-class instance. Further, the six consciousness-elements are manifest precisely through their own function — a mind established with reference to function has no counterpart-class established for it, since whatever lacks this function is spoken of only as a same-class instance. Further, the six consciousness-elements are, in every case, gathered within the mind-element — it has already been stated that the mind-element possesses its own counterpart-class, and this should be understood as already having stated the same for the six consciousness-elements as well, and it is for this reason that this is not stated again. One asks: granting this, the six consciousness-elements ought not to be established as separate elements at all, since these six are themselves already gathered within the mind-element. The answer: although they are indeed gathered within the mind-element, they are nonetheless established as separate, since faculty, object, and consciousness each number six respectively, and it is for this reason that the distinction of the six consciousnesses is further separately stated. One asks: within the various scriptures, citta, manas, and vijñāna are spoken of — what distinction is there among these three? There are those who say: there is, in fact, no distinction at all — citta is precisely manas, and manas is precisely vijñāna, these three terms differing only in sound, never in meaning, exactly as fire is named fire, and is likewise named flame-crest, and is likewise named the blazing one,

Exactly as Śakra, Lord of the Gods, is likewise named Śakra-of-the-hundred-sacrifices, and is likewise named Puraṃdara, and is likewise named Maghavan, and is likewise named Vāsava, and is likewise named Kauśika, and is likewise named Śacīpati, and is likewise named Indra, and is likewise named the thousand-eyed one, and is likewise named the honored one of the Thirty-Three Gods — this one single lord thus bearing ten distinct names, the sound differing while the substance remains undifferentiated. Exactly as, within the Abhidharma, sensation is named sensation, and is likewise named equal-sensation, and is likewise named differentiated-sensation, and is likewise named felt-sensation, and is likewise named the destiny of sensation — this one single sensation thus bearing five distinct names, the sound differing while the substance remains undifferentiated — and it is for this reason that the scripture, in speaking of citta, manas, and vijñāna, speaks of a difference in sound alone, never a genuine distinction. There are, further, those who say:

心意識三亦有差別。謂名即差別。名心名意名識異故。復次世亦差別。謂過去名意。未來名心。現在名識故。復次施設亦有差別。謂界中施設心。處中施設意。蘊中施設識故。復次義亦有差別。謂心是種族義。意是生門義。識是積聚義。復次業亦有差別。謂遠行是心業。如有頌曰。

citta, manas, and vijñāna do indeed bear a genuine distinction among them — that is, they differ in name, the very terms citta, manas, and vijñāna themselves differing. Further, they differ in time — the past is named manas; the future is named citta; the present is named vijñāna. Further, they differ in classificatory designation — citta is designated within the elements; manas is designated within the sense-fields; vijñāna is designated within the aggregates. Further, they differ in meaning — citta bears the meaning of lineage and clan; manas bears the meaning of a gate of birth; vijñāna bears the meaning of accumulation. Further, they differ in their own function — traveling far is the very function of citta, as a verse states:

能遠行獨行　無身寐於窟調伏此心者　解脫大怖畏前行是意業。如有頌曰。

'Capable of traveling far, journeying alone, without any body, dwelling within a cave — whoever subdues this very mind is freed from every great terror.' Leading the way is the very function of manas, as a verse states:

諸法意前行　意尊意所引意染淨言作　苦樂如影隨續生是識業。如契經說。入母胎時識若無者。羯剌藍等不得成就。

'Every dharma is led by manas, manas its own sovereign, manas its own guide — manas speaks and acts, whether defiled or pure, and suffering and happiness follow it exactly as a shadow follows.' Sustaining the continuity of rebirth is the very function of vijñāna, as the scripture states: were consciousness not present at the moment of entering a mother's womb, the very kalala-stage and the rest could never be achieved,

故知續生是識業用。復次彩畫是心業。如契經說。苾芻當知。諸傍生趣由心彩畫。有種種色。歸趣是意業。如契經說。苾芻當知。如是五根各別所行各別境界。意根總領受彼所行境界。意歸趣彼作諸事業。了別是識業。如契經說。苾芻當知。識能了別種種境事。復次滋長是心業。思量是意業。分別是識業。脇尊者言。滋長分割是心業。思量思惟是意業。分別解了是識業。應知此中滋長者是有漏心。分割者是無漏心。思量者是有漏意。思惟者是無漏意。分別者是有漏識。解了者是無漏識。心意識三是謂差別。

and it is thereby known that sustaining the continuity of rebirth is precisely the very function of vijñāna. Further, painting in color is the very function of citta, as the scripture states: monks, you should know, it is by the mind's own painting in color that the destinies of animals possess every kind of varied hue. Turning toward and proceeding is the very function of manas, as the scripture states: monks, you should know, these five faculties each possess their own distinct sphere of activity, their own distinct object, while the mind-faculty comprehensively receives whatever sphere of activity they range over, manas turning toward it and thereby undertaking every kind of activity. Discerning is the very function of vijñāna, as the scripture states: monks, you should know, consciousness is capable of discerning every kind of object and circumstance. Further, nourishing and growing is the very function of citta; pondering is the very function of manas; discriminating is the very function of vijñāna. The Venerable Pārśva says: nourishing-and-growing and dividing-apart are the very function of citta; pondering and reflecting are the very function of manas; discriminating and understanding are the very function of vijñāna — and it should be understood that, here, the nourisher-and-grower is the contaminated mind, while the divider-apart is the undefiled mind; the ponderer is the contaminated manas, while the reflector is the undefiled manas; the discriminator is the contaminated vijñāna, while the understander is the undefiled vijñāna. Such, then, is the distinction among citta, manas, and vijñāna.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That citta, manas, and vijñāna can be honestly described both as mere synonyms and as three functionally distinct aspects of one mental process is not really a contradiction this treatise needs to resolve: a single fire genuinely does bear ten names without becoming ten fires, and the deeper account of function — traveling, leading, sustaining rebirth — simply describes three real capacities of that same single mental continuum, differently emphasized depending on which capacity a given passage of scripture happens to be pointing at.

2. Eye, Color, and Eye-Consciousness: Must They Share the Same Realm?

The treatise asks whether the eye, the color it sees, and the eye-consciousness thereby generated must all belong to one and the same realm, answering that they may be either same-realm-bound, when a being's own eye and its object share his own present realm, or different-realm-bound, when a lower-realm being uses a borrowed, higher-realm eye to see a lower-realm color — in which case the resulting eye-consciousness always settles at the first dhyāna, however much higher the borrowed eye's own level may be. A worked example running through every combination of desire-realm and first-through-fourth-dhyāna eyes and colors shows that eye and color may each independently range across every level, yet the consciousness they jointly produce is never anything but first-dhyāna.

4. Nose, Tongue, and Body: Simpler Cases

Nose and smell admit of no cross-realm mixing whatsoever — a desire-realm nose and a desire-realm smell alone can ever generate a desire-realm nose-consciousness, since no smell-conditioned nose-consciousness belonging to any other realm exists at all, and the very same holds, without further argument, for taste and for touch's own dedicated organs. Of the six consciousness-bodies, the first five — eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body — possess no discrimination whatsoever; only the sixth, mind-consciousness, sometimes discriminates and sometimes does not, discriminating only outside of meditative absorption, since deliberative discrimination is inseparably paired with an unsettled mind. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The single quiet constant running beneath this entire, dizzying permutation of numbers is worth naming explicitly: eye-consciousness itself is never wholesome once any defilement remains anywhere in the relevant ground — it can only ever be defiled or neutral — and it is solely the discriminating mind-consciousness that follows immediately after, never the raw sensory awareness itself, that is capable of being wholesome at all, which is precisely why severing the wholesome roots entirely still leaves a wholesome thought able to arise afterward, however corrupted the initial seeing itself may be.
