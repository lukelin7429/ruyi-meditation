How the Gods Make Love

Fascicle 113 breaks each of the ten paths of karma into preparation, root act, and aftermath, works through an extended casuistry of theft and sexual misconduct across the six heavens, and closes with a cosmological vision of how karma reshapes the very fabric of the world. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 113 continues the Bad-Conduct Nyāsa (惡行納息第一之二), moving from the three-stage anatomy of each path of karma through an extended casuistry into the three kinds of karma and the etymology and cosmological consequences of the ten paths of karma. Terms established across fascicles 93–112 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Ten Paths of Karma, Broken into Preparation, Root Act, and Aftermath

Fascicle 113 continues the Bad-Conduct Nyāsa by asking whether the three kinds of bad conduct gather in the ten unwholesome paths of karma, or the reverse — the three named briefly but broad in matter, the ten named broadly but narrow, so that the three gather the ten, with a remainder left over exactly as a great vessel covering a smaller one still leaves something uncovered. Each of the ten unwholesome paths of karma is then broken into its own three stages: killing, given its own vivid case study of a butcher tracking, buying, binding, and finally slaughtering a sheep, its skinning and sale afterward counting as mere aftermath to a root act already complete the instant life was cut off.

The butcher's own drawn-out sequence — tracking the sheep, buying it, binding it, striking it, and only then delivering the fatal blow — turns an act that might seem morally instantaneous into a genuinely extended process with its own internal stages, each carrying separate karmic weight: the treatise's insistence on locating the exact moment life is cut off, distinct from everything leading up to it and everything that follows, reflects a demand for precision that refuses to let an act's undeniable gravity blur its own internal structure.

2. Theft, Sexual Misconduct, and the Four Verbal Paths

The same threefold breakdown continues through taking what is not given (plotting, then genuinely lifting the object from its place, then disposing of it), sexual misconduct (approach through gifts and tokens of affection, then union itself, with real disagreement over whether the path of karma completes at the very moment of union or only once passion is fully spent), and the four verbal paths of karma — false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, and idle chatter — each traced from its own preparatory approach through the decisive utterance to whatever follows.

3. Five Qualities, and How Many Volitions Complete a Path of Karma

The seven bodily and verbal root paths of karma are shown to carry five qualities at once — being bad conduct, a violation of the precepts, non-restraint, karma, and a path of karma — with every later, merely subsequent act carrying four of these five, since "path of karma" applies only at the very moment volition itself reaches completion. A remarkably intricate accounting follows of how many volitions can complete at once: a lone thief who kills the very sheep he steals completes two paths of karma in a single moment; a gang of bandits, one dragging off a cart, one killing, one committing adultery, may together complete as many as eight at once, though the three mental paths of karma, requiring no accomplice, never co-arise with one another.

The band of thieves who, within a single moment, together drag off a cart, cut off a life, and commit adultery with the household's wife is a vivid, almost cinematic illustration of a genuinely technical point: karmic completion is tracked per continuum, not per event, so that a single coordinated crime scene can register as several entirely separate paths of karma reaching completion simultaneously across several different perpetrators, each accountable only for what his own volition brought to completion.

4. Which Realms and Destinies Can Commit How Many

A careful survey maps which of the ten unwholesome paths of karma are even possible within each realm and destiny: hell lacks killing (since none there can end another's life) and several other paths for want of the requisite objects, though harsh speech and idle chatter remain; the northern continent of Uttarakuru, its inhabitants' fixed thousand-year lifespan and inherently gentle nature, lacks killing, theft, sexual misconduct, false speech, and divisive speech entirely, its consenting lovers parting in shame if the tree above them refuses to lower its branches over them; while the desire realm's own heavens, and the human destiny's three continents, possess the full ten.

10. A New Topic: Three Kinds of Karma, and Why It Is the Most Profound Subject of All

12. Why Called "Karma"?

15. How Each Path of Karma Reshapes the World, Across the Aeon's Own Decline and Increase

An extraordinarily vivid and extended cosmological vision follows: each of the ten unwholesome paths of karma, as it grows dominant, visibly degrades the external world itself — killing drains every object of its own luster and stability; theft invites frost, hail, and calamity; sexual misconduct breeds contention; false speech brings foulness; divisive speech breaks the very ground into ravines and chasms; harsh speech turns even gold and silver dull and unworkable; idle chatter causes all things to perish before reaching genuine fruition; and craving, malice, and wrong view each visibly shrink, wither, and finally empty the natural world of its own abundance. This cosmic sympathy is anchored in the great aeon's own cyclical rhythm: at the aeon's very beginning, Jambudvīpa's own inhabitants possess measureless lifespans, fill the land in vast, virtuous numbers, and enjoy the finest natural foods in abundance under the ten wholesome paths' own full flourishing; by the aeon's own decline, human lifespan has fallen to a mere ten years, the population has dwindled to only ten thousand people, famine has reduced diet to coarse millet and weeds, and the ten evil paths of karma reign in full — mirrored, in exact reverse, by the ten wholesome paths of karma's own capacity to restore the world, and the aeon itself, to fragrance, fertility, and radiant abundance.

17. Why Volition Itself Is Not a Path of Karma

A final, careful puzzle closes the fascicle's own theoretical discussion: since volition is karma, and its own domain of completion is path of karma, why is volition itself never named a path of karma? Three answers converge: exactly as a king's own road is not the king himself, path of karma is what volition traverses, not volition itself; a path of karma requires two distinct dharmas co-arising together, whereas volition together with volition would be a single dharma doubling upon itself; and, most subtly, a path of karma requires some further dharma genuinely present together with volition in the very same moment — which is why, when one sends an agent to kill on one's behalf, the sender's own long-since-vanished volition cannot itself constitute the path of karma, since one's own continuum, not another's, is what a path of karma is reckoned against.
