The Anatomy of the In-Between

Fascicle 70 gives the fullest anatomy of the intermediate state found anywhere in this treatise — how long it lasts, its size and shape, whether it is clothed or fed, the bodhisattva's own radiant exception, and the precise mechanics of entering the womb down to the gandharva's own two minds of craving and aversion. It closes with a sequence of vivid narrative test-cases, including Devadatta swallowed alive and Śakra's own six-fold explained verse before the Buddha. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 70 continues the Sentient-Being Nyāsa (結蘊第二中有情納息第三之八), moving from the previous fascicle's debate over the intermediate state's bare existence to its full concrete anatomy — duration, size, form, clothing, nourishment, its many names, and the precise mechanics of conception — before closing with a run of narrative test-cases that probe the boundary between a genuine death-and-rebirth and a mere apparent one. Terms established in earlier fascicles recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Finding Copulating Parents Against Impossible Odds

Opening part eight of the Sentient-Being Nyāsa, the treatise asks how long a being abides in the intermediate state, answering that it must be brief, since it searches ceaselessly through the six sense-gates for its own rebirth. A genuine puzzle follows: what happens when the destined father and mother are separated by impossible distance, or when their own desire runs against its natural season, exactly as a horse ruts only in spring? The treatise's own answer is startling in scope — poison cannot harm, blades cannot wound, fire cannot burn, water cannot drown the being racing toward its own conception, and the sheer force of karma bends even an unwilling, chaste parent's own resolve, or heightens desire itself out of season, to guarantee that rebirth is bound together exactly where karma has already determined it must be.

Chinese Source

結蘊第二中有情納息第三之八問住中有位為經幾時。答經於少時速求生故。謂住中有。於六處門遍求生緣。速往和合。問若受中有即遇生緣此彼和合可速往彼。與彼緣會於中結生。若遇生緣不和合者。如何彼住不經多時。如有父在迦濕彌羅國。母在至那。或有母在迦濕彌羅國。父在至那。如是生緣難可和合。如何中有速往結生。答應知有情作父母業有定不定故。於父母有可轉義不可轉義。若於父母俱可轉者。即往餘父母和合處結生。若於父可轉於母不可轉者。即彼女人性雖貞潔受持五戒具足威儀。而必與餘男子和合。令中有者速往結生。若於母可轉於父不可轉者。即彼男子性雖賢良受持五戒威儀具足。而必與餘女人和合。令中有者速往結生。若於父母俱不可轉者。即彼有情未命終位。由業力故令其父母。雖有住緣而不顧戀。必起相趣和合之心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Continuing the Sentient-Being Nyāsa within the second, the Fetters Aggregate, part eight. One asks: for how long a span does one abide at the stage of the intermediate state? The answer: only for a brief span, since it swiftly seeks rebirth — that is, while abiding in the intermediate state, it searches everywhere, through the six sense-gates, for the very condition of its own rebirth, and swiftly proceeds to where that condition is fulfilled. One asks: if, upon receiving the intermediate state, one immediately encounters the very condition of rebirth, then this condition and that being converge, and rebirth is bound together right there — but if the condition of rebirth one encounters does not converge with it at all, how could it abide there without passing a great span of time? As when a father dwells in the country of Kashmir while the mother dwells in China, or a mother dwells in Kashmir while the father dwells in China — the condition of rebirth being so difficult to bring into convergence, how could the intermediate state still swiftly proceed to bind rebirth together? The answer: one should understand that a sentient being's own karma, in making a father and mother, is of two kinds — fixed and not fixed — and that a father or mother may, accordingly, be either alterable or unalterable. If both father and mother are alterable, the being proceeds instead to wherever some other father and mother are converging, and binds rebirth together there. If the father is alterable but the mother is not, then that very woman, however chaste and virtuous her own nature, upholding the five precepts and complete in her own deportment, will nevertheless certainly unite with some other man, so that the one in the intermediate state may swiftly proceed to bind rebirth together. If the mother is alterable but the father is not, then that very man, however worthy and good his own nature, upholding the five precepts and complete in his own deportment, will nevertheless certainly unite with some other woman, so that the one in the intermediate state may swiftly proceed to bind rebirth together. If neither father nor mother is alterable at all, then, before that very sentient being has yet died, by the force of his own karma, his own father and mother, though they may possess every condition for remaining apart, will nevertheless feel no attachment to remaining so at all, and will certainly give rise to a mutual desire to come together in union.

彼相趣時於所經處。毒不能害刃不能傷。火不能燒水不能溺。及餘種種夭橫因緣。皆不能礙必得和合。令彼有情既命終已。適受中有即往結生。問若諸有情欲常增者可隨中有速往結生。若有欲心不常增者。如何中有隨往結生。如馬春時欲心增盛餘時不爾。牛於夏時欲心增盛餘時不爾。狗於秋時欲心增盛餘時不爾。熊於冬時欲心增盛餘時不爾。如何有情適受中有令彼和合而往結生。答由彼有情住中有位業增上力令其父母非時欲心亦得增盛。相趣和合彼得結生。

At the very moment they proceed toward each other, along whatever path they travel, poison cannot harm them, blades cannot wound them, fire cannot burn them, water cannot drown them, and every other kind of cause that might otherwise bring an untimely death is powerless to obstruct them, and union is certainly achieved — so that, once that sentient being has died, he proceeds, the very moment he receives the intermediate state, directly to where rebirth is bound together. One asks: if every sentient being's own desire were constantly heightened, it might be granted that the intermediate state could swiftly proceed to bind rebirth together — but, for those whose desire is not constantly heightened, how could the intermediate state still proceed to bind rebirth together in the very same way? A horse's own desire is heightened in spring, but not at other times; a cow's own desire is heightened in summer, but not at other times; a dog's own desire is heightened in autumn, but not at other times; a bear's own desire is heightened in winter, but not at other times — how, then, could a sentient being, the very moment he receives the intermediate state, cause that union to occur and thereby proceed to bind rebirth together? The answer: by the very superior force of the karma of that sentient being abiding in the intermediate state, the desire of his own father and mother is caused to become heightened even out of its proper season, and thus, proceeding toward each other in union, rebirth is bound together for him.

有餘師說。相似類中亦得結生故無有失。謂馬春時欲心增盛餘時不爾。驢一切時欲心增盛。應生馬中者以非時故轉生驢中。牛於夏時欲心增盛餘時不爾。野牛恒時欲心增盛。應生牛中者以非時故轉生野牛中。狗於秋時欲心增盛餘時不爾。野干恒時欲心增盛。應生狗中者以非時故轉生野干中。熊於冬時欲心增盛餘時不爾。羆一切時欲心增盛。應生熊中者以非時故轉生羆中。雖彼形相與餘相似而眾同分如本不轉。以諸中有不可轉故。如是中有住經少時。必往結生速求生故。尊者設摩達多說曰。中有極多住七七日。四十九日定結生故。尊者世友作如是說。中有極多住經七日。彼身羸劣不久住故。問若七日內生緣和合彼可結生。若爾所時生緣未合彼豈斷壞。

Other masters say: rebirth may also be bound together within a merely similar-seeming kind, and so no fault is incurred at all — that is, a horse's own desire is heightened in spring, but not at other times, while a donkey's own desire is heightened at every time; one destined to be reborn as a horse, being out of season, is instead reborn as a donkey. A cow's own desire is heightened in summer, but not at other times, while a wild ox's own desire is heightened at every time; one destined to be reborn as a cow, being out of season, is instead reborn as a wild ox. A dog's own desire is heightened in autumn, but not at other times, while a jackal's own desire is heightened at every time; one destined to be reborn as a dog, being out of season, is instead reborn as a jackal. A bear's own desire is heightened in winter, but not at other times, while a brown bear's own desire is heightened at every time; one destined to be reborn as a bear, being out of season, is instead reborn as a brown bear. Although such a being's own outward form resembles that of some other species, its own common class of being remains, exactly as originally destined, unaltered, since no intermediate state whatsoever can be altered in this respect. The intermediate state thus abides only for a brief span before certainly proceeding to bind rebirth together, since it swiftly seeks rebirth. The Venerable Śarmadatta says: the intermediate state abides, at the very most, for forty-nine days, since rebirth is certainly bound together by the forty-ninth day. The Venerable Vasumitra says as follows: the intermediate state abides, at the very most, for seven days, since that body is so feeble and weak that it cannot abide for long. One asks: granting that, should the condition of rebirth converge within these seven days, rebirth may indeed be bound together — but, for however long the condition of rebirth has not yet converged, would this not mean that the being is thereby broken off entirely?

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The parade of dangers powerless to stop this journey — poison, blade, fire, flood — reads almost like a warrior's epic, and that is precisely the point: karma's own guarantee that a being reaches its destined rebirth is described with the same absolute, no-exceptions language a text might otherwise reserve for describing a bodhisattva's own invulnerability, showing just how total this treatise takes the causal force of ripening karma to be.

2. Three Views on How Long the Intermediate State Lasts, and a New Question Opens

Three masters offer three different answers to how long, at the very most, the intermediate state can last: the Venerable Śarmadatta says forty-nine days; the Venerable Vasumitra says seven, since so feeble a body cannot endure longer; and the Great Virtuous One rejects any fixed limit at all, holding that an intermediate being who has not yet found its rebirth-condition simply dies and is reborn again and again, indefinitely, within that very state, never suffering any actual break in continuity. The fascicle then turns to a new question: what size is an intermediate being's own body?

Chinese Source

答彼不斷壞。謂彼中有乃至生緣未和合位。數死數生無斷壞故。大德說曰。此無定限。謂彼生緣速和合者。此中有身即少時住。若彼生緣多時未合。此中有身即多時住。乃至緣合方得結生。故中有身住無定限。問中有形量大小云何。答欲界中有如五六歲小兒形量。

Ru-Yi Translation

The answer: it is not broken off at all — that is, for as long as the condition of rebirth has still not converged, that intermediate state dies and is reborn again and again, without any genuine break in continuity whatsoever. The Great Virtuous One says: there is, in fact, no fixed limit to this at all — that is, for one whose own condition of rebirth converges swiftly, that intermediate body abides only for a brief span; for one whose own condition of rebirth has not converged for a long span of time, that intermediate body abides for that same long span of time, right up until the condition converges and rebirth is finally bound together — and so an intermediate body's own span of abiding has, in truth, no fixed limit at all. One asks: what is the size of the intermediate being's own bodily form? The answer: a desire-realm intermediate being is the size of a child of five or six years,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The Great Virtuous One's proposal — that an unfulfilled intermediate being cycles through repeated deaths and rebirths within the intermediate state itself, rather than simply persisting unchanged for a long duration — is a strikingly elegant solution to what would otherwise be an awkward asymmetry: it makes the intermediate state's own vulnerability to death perfectly consistent whether it lasts seven days or seventy years, since the underlying unit of experience never actually exceeds its own natural brief span.

3. Size and Form: A Small Child's Body with Sharp Faculties

A desire-realm intermediate being is the size of a five- or six-year-old child, while a form-realm intermediate being retains the full stature of its own death-existence prime. The apparent puzzle — how so small a body could still give rise to the fully adult passions of craving and aversion toward its coming parents — is resolved by distinguishing bodily size from sharpness of faculty, illustrated by a small painting of an old man that still, despite its diminutive scale, unmistakably displays every mark of age.

Chinese Source

色界中有如本有時形量圓滿。問若欲界中有如五六歲小兒形量。云何於父母起顛倒想生愛恚耶。答形量雖小而諸根猛利。如本有時能作諸事業。如壁等上畫老人形。其量雖小而有老相。問菩薩中有其量云何。答如住本有盛年時量。三十二相莊嚴其身。八十隨好而為間飾。身真金色圓光一尋。由此菩薩住中有時。照百俱胝四大洲等。如百千日一時俱照。梵音深妙令人樂聞。如美音鳥其聲清亮。

Ru-Yi Translation

while a form-realm intermediate being is of the full, complete size he possessed in his own death-existence. One asks: if a desire-realm intermediate being is the size of a child of five or six years, how could he possibly give rise to the distorted perception of craving and aversion toward his own coming father and mother? The answer: although his own bodily size is small, his own faculties are sharp and forceful, capable, exactly as in his own death-existence, of performing every kind of undertaking — exactly as a painting of an old man upon a wall, though small in its own size, still displays every mark of old age. One asks: what is the size of a bodhisattva's own intermediate-state body? The old-man painting is a genuinely apt image for the distinction being drawn: a miniature portrait does not paint a miniature old age, it paints old age at a smaller scale, and the treatise is making exactly the same claim about the intermediate being's own mind — cognitive and emotional maturity scales independently of the container that happens to house it.

4. The Bodhisattva's Radiant Intermediate-State Form

A bodhisattva's own intermediate state departs entirely from the desire-realm norm: full adult stature, the thirty-two major and eighty minor marks, a golden body, and a halo extending a full fathom, by which he illuminates a hundred koṭis of world-systems all at once, his voice as clear and pleasing as the call of the kalaviṅka bird, his own wisdom and vision entirely free of defilement.

5. The White-Elephant Verse Explained Away as a Mere Dream-Omen

Dharmasugata's famous verse describing the bodhisattva entering the womb as a white elephant is dismissed outright as carrying no scriptural authority at all, being merely literary verse composed with more rhetorical flourish than strict accuracy. Sought out anyway for its underlying intent, the verse is read as describing not the intermediate being's actual form but the auspicious dream-sign his mother beheld, subsequently interpreted as favorable by the brahmins she consulted — since a bodhisattva who has not fallen into an evil destiny for ninety-one aeons could hardly enter his own final womb in the form of an animal.

Chinese Source

白象相端嚴　具六牙四足正知入母腹　寢如仙隱林答此不須通非三藏故。文頌所說或然不然。諸文頌者言多過實。若必須通應求彼意隨現夢相故作是說。謂彼國中夢見此相以為吉瑞。

Ru-Yi Translation

'The white elephant, its own marks dignified and fine, complete with six tusks and four feet, with correct awareness entered its mother's own womb, reclining there as a sage retired within a forest.' The answer: this need not even be explained away at all, since it is not drawn from the Tripiṭaka — a verse composed in mere literary form may or may not accord with truth. Were it necessary to explain it away regardless, one should seek out its own underlying intent, which speaks with reference to a dream-sign that then appeared — that is, within that very country, to dream of such a sign was taken to be an auspicious omen,

故菩薩母夢見此事欲令占相。諸婆羅門聞已咸言。此相甚吉。故法善現作如是說。亦不違理。菩薩已於九十一劫不墮惡趣。況最後身受此中有而入母胎。是故智者不應依彼所說文頌。而言菩薩所受中有如白象形。

and it is for this reason that the bodhisattva's own mother dreamed of this very sign, wishing to have it interpreted. Every brahmin who heard of it declared, with one voice, that this sign was most auspicious indeed — and it is for this reason that Dharmasugata composed his own verse in this way, which likewise carries no contradiction with reason. The bodhisattva had already, across ninety-one aeons, never once fallen into an evil destiny — how much less, then, in his own very last body, would he receive this intermediate state and enter his mother's womb in the form of an animal? A wise person ought not, therefore, rely upon this literary verse and thereby claim that the intermediate state the bodhisattva received had the form of a white elephant.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Refusing to let a beloved and famous verse override the treatise's own doctrinal account of the bodhisattva's radiant human-formed intermediate state is a clean demonstration of how this literature ranks its sources: canonical prose is authoritative, but even a widely beloved verse composed outside the Tripiṭaka carries no binding weight at all, however emotionally resonant its imagery may be.

6. Are the Intermediate Being's Faculties Complete?

The treatise settles that an intermediate being's own faculties are, in every case, complete, since a first reception of ripened result must be perfect — rejecting the rival view that faculties match whatever is incomplete in the coming death-existence, since a being searching through all six sense-gates for its own rebirth-place cannot afford any genuine deficiency there. A refinement follows: this completeness excludes the male and female faculty specifically, which a desire-realm intermediate being bound for egg- or womb-birth does possess throughout the intermediate state itself, becoming incomplete only once actually inside the egg or womb.

Chinese Source

問中有諸根為具不具。答一切中有皆具諸根。初受異熟必圓妙故。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: are the intermediate being's own faculties complete, or incomplete? The answer: every intermediate being's own faculties, in every case, are complete, since the very first reception of a ripened result must, of necessity, be perfect and exquisite.

有作是說。中有諸根亦有不具。隨本有位所不具根彼亦不具。如印印物像現如印。如是中有趣本有故。如本有時有根不具。此中初說於理為善。謂中有位於六處門遍求生處根必無缺。此說眼等非女男根。色界中有無彼根故。欲界中有彼亦不定。當受卵胎二類生者。

There are those who say: an intermediate being's own faculties may also be incomplete, matching whatever faculty is incomplete at the corresponding stage of his own coming death-existence — exactly as an image stamped by a seal appears exactly as that seal itself appears, so too does the intermediate state correspond to the coming death-existence, and, exactly as that coming death-existence's own faculties may be incomplete, so too are the intermediate being's own. Of these, the position stated first is the sound one — that is, at the stage of the intermediate state, since one searches everywhere, through the six sense-gates, for the very place of his own rebirth, his own faculties there must, of necessity, be without any deficiency at all. This statement refers to the eye and the rest, not to the male or female faculty, since a form-realm intermediate being possesses neither of these faculties at all; as for a desire-realm intermediate being, this is likewise indeterminate — for one destined to be reborn by way of the egg-born or womb-born modes,

住中有位有女男根。至卵胎中方有不具。若不爾者應無當受卵胎生義。

while abiding at the stage of the intermediate state, he does indeed possess the male or female faculty, becoming incomplete only once he actually arrives within the egg or the womb — for, were this not so, there could be no sense at all in which he is destined for an egg-born or womb-born rebirth.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The exception carved out for the male and female faculty is a small but telling piece of biological realism within an otherwise abstract doctrinal system: this treatise is willing to let its general principle of faculty-completeness bend around the concrete, observable fact that a fetus or hatchling's own sexual characteristics are simply not yet differentiated at the earliest stage of gestation.

7. The Intermediate Being's Shape Matches Its Destination, Not Its Origin

An intermediate being's own outward shape matches the destiny it is heading toward, not the body it has just left — refuting the rival view that it should instead resemble the dying body, which collapses entirely for a formless-realm death giving rise to a desire- or form-realm intermediate being, since the formless realm has no bodily form at all for it to resemble. A further puzzle asks how a single womb could safely house intermediate beings of all five destinies at once, including a fire-wreathed hell-being, resolved by noting that even a hell-being's own death-existence is not perpetually ablaze, and that karma itself prevents these radically different beings from ever actually touching or scorching one another.

Chinese Source

問一切中有形狀云何。答中有形狀如當本有。謂彼當生地獄趣者。

9. Clothing in the Intermediate State: Modesty, the Bodhisattva, and the Nun Śuklā's Vow

10. Does the Intermediate Being Need Food? Subsisting on Fragrance Alone

A form-realm intermediate being needs no solid food at all; a desire-realm intermediate being does require sustenance, but not in any ordinary sense — the theory that it simply eats and drinks whatever food and water it happens to pass is rejected on the grounds that intermediate beings are so overwhelmingly numerous, packed as densely as rice poured into a granary, that the whole world's food supply, sufficient only to feed a single species of dog, could never provide for even one destiny's worth of them, let alone all five, and a body this subtle would in any case be torn apart by any coarse, heavy substance. The settled account has the intermediate being subsist on fragrance alone — the meritorious partaking the light scent of pure flowers and fruit, the meritless partaking the faint scent of filth and rot — since a fragrance so minute in quantity can still provide for beings this numerous.

11. The Many Names of the Intermediate State

The intermediate state carries four distinct names, each capturing a different facet of what it is: "intermediate state" itself, for dwelling subtly and imperceptibly between a death-existence and a rebirth-existence, gathered within no destiny at all; "gandharva," for subsisting on fragrance, a name belonging to the desire realm alone; "seeker of existence," for its uniquely forceful, single-minded search for a coming rebirth through all six sense-gates; and "mind-made," for being born from mind alone rather than from karma, ripened result, or sexual desire, joining in this respect the very first humans at the dawn of an aeon, the form and formless realms, and every transformation-body.

14. Where Does the Intermediate Being Enter the Womb? The Birth-Canal, the Bodhisattva's Right Flank, and Matched Merit

Though an intermediate being's own body is unobstructed by anything, it is nonetheless bound, by the sheer inconceivable force of karma, to enter the womb specifically through the birth-canal — the very place it desires — which is also why, among twins, the one born second is reckoned the elder, since whoever enters first must necessarily emerge last. The bodhisattva, a wheel-turning king, and a solitary-realizer alike are said to enter instead through the mother's own right flank, correct awareness free of any sexual craving toward her, though other masters hold that only the bodhisattva's merit is supreme enough to dispense with the birth-canal entirely. A final principle governs every case regardless of rank: entry into the womb requires the father's, mother's, and child's own respective merit to be genuinely matched, with each party in an unequal union compensating by perceiving itself as either superior or inferior to its match.

15. Mutual Obstruction Among Intermediate Beings, and Whether They Outrun Supernormal Power

Though unobstructed by every wall, cliff, and tree, intermediate beings may still obstruct one another — but only within their own kind, a hell-bound being blocking only another hell-bound being, and so on; one further theory holds that the coarser, heavier destinies obstruct the finer ones but never the reverse, so that a hell-bound being obstructs the intermediate beings of all five destinies while a god-bound being obstructs only its own kind. 16. Can Intermediate Beings See One Another? Can Ordinary Eyes See Them?

The five greater signs — no longer reversible once they appear — are soiled clothing, a withered flower-crown, sudden sweat beneath the arms, a sudden foul odor, and a simple unwillingness to remain seated upon one's own throne. Śakra himself, the fascicle reveals, had already seen all five lesser signs manifest, with the irreversible greater signs soon to follow, when terror sent him to the Buddha for rescue.

The closing criterion — that genuine death and rebirth erases memory of one's former life, while these transformed beings plainly retained it — is a wonderfully practical, almost forensic test for settling an otherwise abstract metaphysical question: rather than arguing from first principles about what death and rebirth must involve, the treatise simply asks what a person who had actually undergone it would be like, and checks the case at hand against that ordinary, observable standard.

Fascicle 70 Complete

This closes fascicle 70 of Ru-Yi's translation, drawn from the treatise's true CBETA boundary. Fascicle 70 gave the fullest anatomy of the intermediate state found anywhere in this treatise: how long it lasts, its size and shape, the bodhisattva's own radiant exception, whether its faculties are complete, its manner of travel and posture, whether it is clothed and whether it needs food, its many names, the precise mechanics of entering the womb down to the gandharva's own two minds of craving and aversion, mutual obstruction and mutual sight among intermediate beings, and closed with a sequence of narrative test-cases — Devadatta and Virūḍhaka swallowed alive, Śakra's own six-fold explained verse and the five signs of a god's approaching death, and the first humans who became serpents and elephants — each used to sharpen exactly what does, and does not, count as a genuine death and rebirth.
