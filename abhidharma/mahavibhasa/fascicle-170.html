Every Stage's Full Inventory

Fascicle 170 completes the Subsumption Nyāsa's (攝納息第三之五) causal analysis of the sixty-five equipoises — cause versus object, then immediately-preceding condition versus object, worked through every pure and uncontaminated dhyāna and formless sphere — before closing with a vivid worked example of simultaneous gain and loss, and a full table of the minimum and maximum equipoise-count possessed at every stage of the path, up through the Buddha and both types of pratyekabuddha. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 170 continues the Subsumption Nyāsa (攝納息第三之五) within the Concentration Aggregate (定蘊第七), directly following fascicle 169's tetralemma analysis of the sixty-five equipoises. Terms established across fascicles 93–169 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence. Given the density of the numeric enumerations in this fascicle, every count has been checked against the source text.

1. The Pure Dhyānas and Formless Spheres: Cause Versus Object

Fascicle 170 opens by finishing the causal-versus-object tetralemma project that fascicle 169 broke off mid-argument, now applying it to every pure dhyāna and pure formless sphere in turn — for each one, precisely how many other equipoises take it as their cause without taking it as their object, as their object without taking it as their cause, as both, or as neither.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Watching the same four-cornered method run identically across the pure dhyānas as it did for the uncontaminated ones in fascicle 169 reveals the underlying architecture of this whole multi-fascicle project: the treatise is not inventing a new method for each new category, but patiently applying one single rigorous tool to an ever-widening set of objects, trusting the reader to recognize the pattern rather than re-explaining it each time.

2. The Uncontaminated Attainments: Cause Versus Object, Concluded

The cause-versus-object analysis concludes with the uncontaminated dhyānas and formless spheres, completing the picture begun in fascicle 169 for how a noble attainment's causal ancestry differs from what it merely takes as its object of contemplation.

3. A New Pairing: Immediately-Preceding Condition Versus Object

One who has attained the fruit of arhatship possesses, at minimum, thirteen — namely, one reborn in the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception who is a wisdom-liberated arhat, possessing seven uncontaminated attainments, one pure attainment, four liberations, and the basis of the superknowledge of the extinction of contaminants — and, at maximum, fifty-seven, namely, one who has attained the unhindered understandings, non-contention, and wish-knowledge, possessing everything except the eight flavor-associated attainments. The Buddha invariably possesses all fifty-seven. Among pratyekabuddhas, the group-following type is like a śrāvaka of the desire realm, while the rhinoceros-horn-like type is like the Buddha.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The final, almost throwaway comparison — that the group-following pratyekabuddha resembles an ordinary desire-realm śrāvaka while the rhinoceros-horn-like solitary type resembles the Buddha himself — is a striking closing note: even within this most solitary and taciturn category of awakened being, the tradition still insists on a hierarchy of spiritual accomplishment, measured by the very same sixty-five-item inventory that has organized this entire fascicle.
