The King Śakra Deceived

Fascicle 34 discriminates two kinds of full understanding through the folk tale of a greedy king cruelly baited by Śakra with tales of wealth beyond the ocean, before turning to the Three Refuges — redirected, in each case, away from the visible and conditioned toward the Dharma-body, cessation itself, and the noble dharma that actually constitutes the Saṅgha — and closes by opening a new nyāsa on shamelessness and lack of moral dread, the two dark dharmas the Buddha names as capable of destroying the world. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 34 completes the sixth part of the Love-and-Respect Nyāsa (愛敬納息第四之六), opened in fascicle 29, before opening the first part of the Shamelessness Nyāsa (無慚愧納息第五之一). Terms established in earlier fascicles, such as the four noble truths, the nine grades of affliction, and the path of seeing and cultivation, recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Two Kinds of Full Understanding: Through Knowledge, Through Cutting-Off

Fascicle 34 opens by discriminating the sūtra's own distinction between full understanding through knowledge and full understanding through cutting-off — the former comprising every kind of knowledge, vision, illumination, and direct realization, contaminated as well as uncontaminated, provided it observes a dharma's own particular and shared characteristics with sufficient clarity, and the latter comprising the permanent cutting-off of craving, hatred, delusion, and every affliction whatsoever.

Chinese Source

如契經說。有二遍知。謂智遍知及斷遍知。乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答為廣分別契經義故。謂契經說有二遍知。謂智遍知及斷遍知。契經雖作是說。而不廣辨其義。彼是此論所依根本。彼所不說者此皆應說之。復次前說斷知諸取。而未分別斷知取義。今欲分別故作斯論。云何智遍知。答諸智見明覺現觀。是謂智遍知。此中諸名同顯一義。以本論主於諸字義得善巧故作種種說。謂對治無知故名智。對治惡見故名見。對治無明故名明。對治邪覺故名覺。對治邪現觀故名現觀。問此中為說何等智等名智遍知。有作是說。唯說無漏說現觀故。所以者何。非世俗智可名現觀。復有說者。通說有漏無漏智等名智遍知。所以者何。遍知諸法。契經多說世俗智故。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the sūtra states: there are two kinds of full understanding — namely, full understanding through knowledge, and full understanding through cutting-off — and so on, at length. One asks: why is this treatise composed? The answer: it is composed in order to fully discriminate the meaning of this sūtra passage. As the sūtra states: there are two kinds of full understanding, namely full understanding through knowledge and full understanding through cutting-off, yet, having said this much, the sūtra does not discriminate its meaning fully. That sūtra is the fundamental basis upon which this treatise depends, and whatever it has left unstated ought now to be stated. Further, the foregoing has already spoken of the cutting-off and full understanding of the various graspings, without yet discriminating the meaning of "cutting-off and full understanding" itself, and it is in order to discriminate this that this treatise is now composed. What is full understanding through knowledge? The answer: whatever knowledge, vision, illumination, awakening, and direct realization there is — this is called full understanding through knowledge. Here, these various names alike reveal one and the same meaning; it is because the author of this treatise is skilled in the various meanings of these terms that he expresses it through a variety of wordings — that is, it is called "knowledge" because it serves as the antidote to ignorance; it is called "vision" because it serves as the antidote to wrong view; it is called "illumination" because it serves as the antidote to obscuring darkness; it is called "awakening" because it serves as the antidote to wrongful cognition; it is called "direct realization" because it serves as the antidote to a wrongful direct realization. One asks: which kind of knowledge, in this context, is spoken of as "full understanding through knowledge"? There are those who say: it refers solely to uncontaminated knowledge, since the passage speaks of "direct realization," and mundane knowledge cannot properly be called "direct realization." There are others who say: it broadly refers to both contaminated and uncontaminated knowledge, since it is knowledge that fully understands all dharmas, and the sūtras frequently speak of mundane knowledge in this connection.

問豈世俗智亦名現觀。答明了觀察是現觀義。世俗智中。有能明了觀察法者亦名現觀。非唯無漏得現觀名。故城喻經作如是說。我未證得三菩提時。如實現觀生緣老死。非未證得三菩提時已有觀緣起。真實無漏智可名現觀。由此故知有世俗智亦名現觀。問何等世俗智亦名智遍知。答除勝解作意相應世俗智。餘聞思修觀自共相。

One asks: can mundane knowledge, too, really be called "direct realization"? The answer: to observe clearly and distinctly is the meaning of "direct realization"; among mundane forms of knowledge, there exist those capable of clearly and distinctly observing a dharma, and these, too, are called "direct realization" — it is not that only the uncontaminated may bear the name "direct realization." Hence the Nagara Sūtra states: before I attained perfect enlightenment, I had already, in a genuine and direct way, observed birth conditioning aging and death — it is not that, only once perfect enlightenment was attained, there then first existed an observation of dependent origination; a genuine, uncontaminated knowledge alone may properly be called "direct realization." From this it is known that mundane knowledge, too, may be called "direct realization." One asks: which kind of mundane knowledge, specifically, is called "full understanding through knowledge"? The answer: excepting the mundane knowledge associated with the mental factor of intentional application through resolved conviction, every other kind — whether arising from hearing, from reflection, or from cultivation — that observes the particular and shared characteristics of dharmas,

諸世俗智極明了者亦得名現觀。亦名智遍知。聞所成慧者。如觀十八界自共相等。思所成慧者。如持息念四念住等。修所成慧者。如煖頂忍世第一法等。此及無漏慧俱名智遍知。云何斷遍知。答諸貪永斷。瞋癡永斷。一切煩惱永斷。是謂斷遍知。問於所緣境能遍知故立遍知。名斷無所緣及遍知用何故名遍知。答斷是智果故亦名遍知。

whenever it is of the utmost clarity and distinctness among mundane forms of knowledge, may likewise be called "direct realization," and is likewise called "full understanding through knowledge." Wisdom arisen from hearing refers, for example, to the observation of the eighteen elements' own particular and shared characteristics; wisdom arisen from reflection refers, for example, to mindfulness of breathing and the four establishments of mindfulness; wisdom arisen from cultivation refers, for example, to warmth, summit, forbearance, and the supreme mundane dharma — these, together with uncontaminated wisdom, are alike called "full understanding through knowledge." What is full understanding through cutting-off? The answer: the permanent cutting-off of craving, the permanent cutting-off of hatred and delusion, the permanent cutting-off of every affliction whatsoever — this is called full understanding through cutting-off. One asks: it is by virtue of fully understanding a given object that the name "full understanding" is established — cutting-off possesses no object of its own, nor does it perform the function of full understanding, so why is it, too, called "full understanding"? The answer: cutting-off is the fruit of knowledge, and it is for this reason that it, too, is called "full understanding" —

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The insistence that even mundane knowledge may earn the name “direct realization,” provided it observes clearly enough, quietly widens this whole category beyond the rarefied uncontaminated wisdom one might expect it to require — the treatise grounds this in the Buddha's own pre-enlightenment observation of dependent origination, refusing to let “direct realization” become a technical term available only after the fact of awakening itself.

2. Why Cutting-Off Is Called “Full Understanding”: Five Masters Debate

A genuinely intricate debate unfolds over why mere cutting-off, which performs no function of understanding anything, nonetheless earns the name “full understanding.” Five successive masters propose and are refuted in turn — that it is the fruit of wisdom generally, that it should be called “relinquishing” or “principled” full understanding instead, that it bears knowledge's own characteristic the way a past eye still counts as an eye, that it is the fruit of mundane knowledge — before the Vibhāṣā's own verdict: forbearance belongs to knowledge's own family, the way a child belongs to his father's clan, and the cutting-off that is forbearance's fruit inherits the family name.

Chinese Source

如阿羅漢。是解果故亦說名解。天眼天耳是通果故亦說名通。內六處等是業果故說名故業。此中亦爾。斷是智果亦名遍知。問修所斷斷是智果故。可說為遍知。見所斷斷既是忍果。云何名遍知。尊者僧伽筏蘇說曰。此是慧果故名遍知。謂遍知有二種。一慧為性。二智為性。彼不應作是說。契經但說有二遍知。一智遍知。二斷遍知。智遍知者智為自性。斷遍知者斷為自性。斷是智果故名遍知。

Ru-Yi Translation

just as an arhat, being the fruit of liberation, is likewise called "liberation"; just as the divine eye and divine ear, being the fruit of supernormal penetration, are likewise called "penetration"; just as the inner six sense-bases, being the fruit of karma, are spoken of as "former karma." So too is it here: cutting-off, being the fruit of knowledge, is likewise called "full understanding." One asks: the cutting-off belonging to the path of cultivation, being the fruit of knowledge, may properly be spoken of as "full understanding" — but the cutting-off belonging to the path of seeing, being the fruit of forbearance, how can it be called "full understanding"? The Venerable Saṅghavasu states as follows: this, too, is the fruit of wisdom, and it is for this reason that it is called "full understanding" — that is, full understanding is of two kinds: first, taking wisdom as its own nature; second, taking knowledge as its own nature. This position ought not to be maintained, since the sūtra states only that there are two kinds of full understanding: first, full understanding through knowledge; second, full understanding through cutting-off. Full understanding through knowledge takes knowledge as its own nature; full understanding through cutting-off takes cutting-off as its own nature — cutting-off, being the fruit of knowledge, is called "full understanding."

未曾有處說慧遍知。遍知是智作用名故。脇尊者曰。應說此斷名捨遍知。棄捨生死得此斷故。尊者妙音作如是說。應說此斷名理遍知。見最勝法最勝義理。得此究竟最勝斷故。此二所說皆不了義。

There exists nowhere any statement speaking of "full understanding through wisdom," since "full understanding" is a name for the function proper to knowledge alone. The Venerable Pārśva says: this cutting-off ought to be called "relinquishing full understanding," since it is through attaining this cutting-off that one relinquishes and casts off birth and death. The Venerable Ghoṣa states as follows: this cutting-off ought to be called "principled full understanding," since it is through beholding the supreme dharma and the supreme meaning and principle that one attains this ultimate, supreme cutting-off. Both of these statements fail to grasp the definitive meaning,

俱不釋斷名遍知故。尊者佛護作如是說。斷雖於境無遍知用。而約相說亦名遍知。如過去未來眼。雖不見色而約相說亦得名眼。耳乃至意諸心所等。應知亦然。此亦如是。斷是智相故名遍知。彼說非理。所以者何。此斷恒無遍知用故。有餘師說。忍所斷斷是世俗智果故亦名遍知。彼說非理。欲界乃至無所有處。是事可爾。有頂地中見所斷斷。如何可說世俗智果。世俗智於彼無永斷用故。又異生位用世俗道斷下八地見所斷時未名遍知。至見道位立遍知時無世俗智。如何說為世俗智果。復有說者。見所斷斷是無漏智。士用果故亦名遍知。謂無漏智為斷欲界第六無間道證一來果時。通得三界見所斷斷。欲界六品修所斷斷為一來果。用無漏智為斷。欲界第九無間道證不還果時。通得三界見所斷斷。及得欲界修所斷斷。為不還果。金剛喻定現在前時通得三界見修所斷斷。為阿羅漢果。是故彼斷亦名遍知。彼亦不應作如是說。見道六遍知應非遍知故。應作是說。忍是智眷屬。是智種族故亦名為智。斷是彼果故名遍知。如喬答摩種中生者名喬答摩。此亦應爾。問一身見斷亦名遍知。何緣乃說諸貪永斷。乃至一切煩惱永斷名斷遍知。答雖一結斷亦名遍知。

since neither of them explains why cutting-off is called "full understanding." The Venerable Buddharakṣita states as follows: although cutting-off performs no function of fully understanding its object, it, too, is called "full understanding" with reference to its own characteristic — just as the eye of the past or the future, though it does not actually see visible form, is nonetheless, with reference to its own characteristic, still called "the eye"; and the same should be understood to hold for the ear, and on through the mind and its mental factors. The same is true here as well: cutting-off, being the characteristic mark of knowledge, is called "full understanding." That position, too, is not correct, since this cutting-off never at any time performs the function of fully understanding anything. There are other masters who say: the cutting-off belonging to forbearance is the fruit of mundane knowledge, and it is for this reason that it, too, is called "full understanding." That position, too, is not correct — within the desire realm, up through the sphere of nothing-whatsoever, this might perhaps hold; but, within the peak of existence, the cutting-off belonging to the path of seeing — how could this possibly be called the fruit of mundane knowledge, since mundane knowledge performs no function of permanently cutting anything off there? And, further, at the stage of an ordinary being, when one uses the mundane path to cut off what is abandoned by seeing across the eight lower stages, this is not yet called "full understanding"; only upon reaching the stage of the path of seeing, when "full understanding" is established, does no mundane knowledge remain present at all — how, then, could it be called the fruit of mundane knowledge? There are yet others who say: the cutting-off belonging to the path of seeing is the fruit, through the efficient cause of an agent's own action, of uncontaminated knowledge, and it is for this reason that it, too, is called "full understanding" — that is, when uncontaminated knowledge, cutting off the sixth uninterrupted path of the desire realm, realizes the fruit of a once-returner, one thereby fully attains the cutting-off of what is abandoned by seeing across the three realms, together with the cutting-off, through uncontaminated knowledge, of the six grades of the desire realm's own affliction abandoned by cultivation, for the sake of the fruit of a once-returner; when, cutting off the ninth uninterrupted path of the desire realm, one realizes the fruit of a non-returner, one thereby fully attains the cutting-off of what is abandoned by seeing across the three realms, together with the cutting-off of the desire realm's own affliction abandoned by cultivation, for the sake of the fruit of a non-returner; and when the vajropama-samādhi manifests before one, one thereby fully attains the cutting-off of what is abandoned by seeing and by cultivation across the three realms, for the sake of the fruit of arhatship — and it is for this reason that this cutting-off, too, is called "full understanding." This position, too, ought not to be maintained, since the six full understandings belonging to the path of seeing would thereby fail to be full understandings at all. It ought rather to be said: forbearance is a member of the family of knowledge, belonging to knowledge's own lineage, and it is for this reason that it, too, is called "knowledge"; cutting-off, being its fruit, is called "full understanding" — just as one born within the Gautama clan is called "Gautama," so too should this be understood. One asks: the cutting-off of even a single instance of the view of a real self is likewise called "full understanding" — why, then, does the passage speak of the permanent cutting-off of craving, and so on, up through the permanent cutting-off of every affliction, as "full understanding through cutting-off"? The answer: although the cutting-off of even a single fetter is indeed called "full understanding,"

然此中說圓滿遍知。一切結盡方名圓滿斷遍知故。

this passage speaks specifically of complete and perfect full understanding, which is called "complete and perfect full understanding through cutting-off" only once every fetter whatsoever has been exhausted.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The Gautama-clan analogy closing this debate is a lovely piece of conceptual economy: it lets the treatise grant cutting-off the name “full understanding” without ever claiming cutting-off itself performs any cognitive function, since a child inherits his clan's name through lineage and relation, not through personally sharing his father's own accomplishments — cutting-off is knowledge's offspring, not knowledge's equal.

3. The Story of King Bahulobha: A Verse on Empty Promises

Illustrating “full understanding” applied to knowledge, the treatise relates an extraordinary folk tale: a greedy king, deposed by his own ministers for plundering his people, is later restored by his guilt-stricken brother, only to prove so insatiable that he seizes the throne back by force — at which point Śakra, lord of the gods, disguises himself as a wandering brahmin and cruelly baits the king with tales of a fabulously wealthy land beyond the ocean, vanishing before the appointed day of departure and leaving the king in inconsolable grief, until the young bodhisattva Śākyamuni arrives and, through verses reproving desire, both frees his own mind of craving and heals the king's broken heart.

Chinese Source

復次世尊。或時於智說遍知聲。或時於斷說遍知聲。於智說遍知聲者。如伽他說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the Blessed One, at times, applies the word "full understanding" to knowledge, and, at other times, applies the word "full understanding" to cutting-off. As for its application to knowledge, this is as the verse states:

儒童賢寂靜　能益諸世間有智能遍知　貪愛生眾苦有智言應作　不作不應言智者應遍知　有言無作者多求王因緣是此頌根本。謂昔有王名曰多求。稟性兇暴貪求無厭。

"The youthful one, virtuous and tranquil, is able to benefit every world; the wise are able to fully understand that craving gives rise to every suffering. What the wise say ought to be done, should they not do it, ought not to be said; the wise ought to fully understand — those who merely speak without doing are many." The origin of this verse lies in the story of King Bahulobha. That is, there was, in ancient times, a king named Bahulobha, whose nature was fierce and violent, whose greed and craving knew no satiety,

劫奪國人所有財寶。於是臣佐議共退之。以其次弟紹繼王位。時多求王至國邊邑。編草作屨以自存活。如是多時。弟王忽憶。問諸臣曰。大兄何在。諸臣答言。聞在邊邑作屨自活。王聞憂惱作是思惟。兄既如是。我今何用居王位耶。即時召來封以一邑。人多附彼食便不供。如是更封二邑三邑乃至半國。猶故無厭。遂復興兵殺弟自立。時天帝釋知已念言。今此惡王不識恩義。我今應往誑而惱之。遂自化作婆羅門像。戴帽披苫執缾持杖。至彼王所作吉祥言。

who plundered the wealth and treasure of his own people. His ministers, in council together, thereupon deposed him, and installed his younger brother to succeed him upon the throne. King Bahulobha then went to a border town of the kingdom, where he wove sandals from straw in order to sustain himself, and lived thus for a long while. His brother, the new king, suddenly recalled him, and asked his ministers: where is my elder brother now? The ministers answered: we have heard that he dwells in a border town, sustaining himself by weaving sandals. Hearing this, the king was filled with sorrow, and thought as follows: since my elder brother has come to such a state, of what use is it for me to remain upon the throne? He at once summoned him back, and enfeoffed him with a single town. Yet many people flocked to him there, and the food supplied was insufficient, so the king enfeoffed him further with a second town, then a third, until he had granted him half the kingdom — and still he was not satisfied, and in the end raised an army, slew his brother, and set himself up as king once more. At that time, Śakra, lord of the gods, learning of this, thought to himself: this wicked king recognizes neither kindness nor righteousness — I shall now go and deceive him, so as to trouble him. He thereupon transformed himself into the likeness of a brahmin, wearing a cap and a coarse straw garment, holding a water-vessel and a staff, and came before the king, uttering auspicious words,

讚歎呪願在一面立。王言。梵志。從何所來。婆羅門言。從大海外。王言。海外有何事耶答言。我見有一國土。安隱豐樂多諸人眾。奇珍異寶充滿其國。王言。我力可得彼不。婆羅門言。往必可得。王復問言。誰相導引。答言。我能。王言。幾日當可進途。婆羅門言。却後七日。言已便去。時多求王招集兵眾。辦諸資具至第七日。嚴駕將行。覓婆羅門竟不能得。王遂憂惱作是念言。彼若來者當得大利。坐失彼故所期不獲遂入憂室煩冤而坐。舉國無人解其愁毒。時釋迦菩薩生彼國中大婆羅門村。少因緣故來至王都。聞如是事心生悲愍。謂諸臣曰。吾能解王心中愁結。諸臣歡喜引至王前。以吉祥言讚歎呪願在一面立。為王宣說義品伽他。

praising and blessing him, and stood to one side. The king said: brahmin, whence have you come? The brahmin answered: from beyond the great ocean. The king said: what is there, beyond the ocean? He answered: I have seen a certain land, secure, at ease, and prosperous, its people numerous, filled with rare treasures and marvelous jewels. The king said: might I, with my own strength, obtain it? The brahmin answered: going there, you would surely obtain it. The king further asked: who shall guide the way? He answered: I myself am able. The king said: after how many days might we set out? The brahmin answered: seven days from now. Having said this, he departed. King Bahulobha then mustered his troops, prepared every provision, and, on the seventh day, made ready his chariot to set forth — but, seeking the brahmin, he was unable to find him anywhere. The king thereupon fell into sorrow, thinking: had he come, I should have gained great benefit; but, having lost him, what I hoped for has not been realized — and he entered his chamber of grief, sitting there in melancholy distress, and no one in the whole kingdom was able to relieve his bitter sorrow. At that time, the bodhisattva Śākyamuni had been born in that country, in a great brahmin village, and, on account of some minor matter, came to the royal capital, where, hearing of this affair, a compassionate sorrow arose in him. He said to the ministers: I am able to untie the knot of sorrow within the king's own heart. The ministers rejoiced, and led him before the king, where, with auspicious words of praise and blessing, he stood to one side, and expounded for the king the verses of the Arthavargīya:

趣求諸欲人　常起於悕望所欲若稱遂　心便大歡喜趣求諸欲人　常起於悕望所欲若不遂　惱壞如箭中如是次第為王盡說彼義品中呵欲頌已。即時菩薩自離欲染。王聞遂解心中憂毒。即為菩薩而說初頌。儒童賢寂靜。乃至廣說。此頌義顯無勞分別。菩薩為王說第二頌。有智言應作。乃至廣說。此頌前半呵責帝釋。謂有智者說許他言必定應作。若不欲作便不應言。後半責王。謂有智者應當遍知若有發言而不作者欲相擾惱。然無實事。王何專直信彼虛言。誰當有能海外來者。誰復能往海彼岸耶。

"One who pursues the objects of desire forever gives rise to hope and expectation; if what he desires comes to pass, his mind then rejoices greatly.
