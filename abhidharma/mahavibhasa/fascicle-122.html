The Emperor's New Karma

Fascicle 122 opens the Manifest-and-Unmanifest Nyāsa with a parable mocking skepticism about unmanifest karma, devotional vows sustained by a single daily flower, an exhaustive matrix of bodily and verbal action across every realm, and the metaphysics of magical transformation. 1. Are Manifest and Unmanifest Karma Even Real? The Parable of the Emperor's New Clothes

Fascicle 122 opens the Manifest-and-Unmanifest Nyāsa, the fourth and final nyāsa of the Karma Aggregate, by refuting the Dārṣṭāntika claim that manifest and unmanifest karma possess no real own-substance at all — mocked through a wonderfully cutting parable: a seducer convinces a woman to undress by promising an invisible "heavenly garment" only he can see, and when she protests her nakedness, tells her the garment is simply too subtle for her own eyes. The Vaibhāṣika turns this exact parable back on the skeptics themselves, then marshals scriptural citations, the case of Ajātaśatru's regicide (whose crime was completed only through unmanifest karma bridging the gap between his manifest act and his father's actual death), and the threefold classification of form itself as proof that unmanifest karma must be real.

Chinese Source

若成就身表。彼成就此無表耶。如是等章。及解章義。既領會已。

Ru-Yi Translation

Does one who possesses bodily manifest action thereby possess this unmanifest action? Having now grasped this chapter heading and its explanatory meaning in full, together with other such headings,

次應廣釋。問何故作此論。答為止他宗顯己義故。謂譬喻者。說表無表業無實體性。所以者何。若表業是實可得依之令無表有。然表業無實。云何能發無表令有。且表業尚無無表云何有而言有者。是對法諸師矯妄言耳。如人遇見美女為染近故。語言。汝可解去人服。吾衣汝天衣。女聞歡喜如言為解。彼人即前種種摩觸。恣心意已語言。天衣已為汝著。女言。我今體露如是。寧死不露天衣何在。彼答之言。天服微妙唯我見之非汝能見。如是愚人本無天衣。

— we should next explain it in full. Question: why compose this treatise? Answer: in order to put a stop to another school's position and to make clear our own doctrine. That is, the Dārṣṭāntikas say that manifest and unmanifest karma possess no real own-substance at all. Why do they say this? If manifest action were real, one might grant that unmanifest action, depending upon it, could exist; but manifest action itself is not real — how, then, could it give rise to unmanifest action and make it exist? Given that manifest action itself does not even possess unmanifest action, how can it be said to exist at all, they say — this is nothing but a deceitful, empty claim on the part of the Ābhidharmikas. It is like a man who, encountering a beautiful woman and wishing to draw close to her out of desire, says to her, "You should take off your ordinary human clothes, and I shall dress you in a garment of heaven." The woman, delighted to hear this, undresses just as he says. The man then approaches and fondles her in various ways, and, having indulged his own desire to the full, says to her, "The heavenly garment has now been put upon you." The woman says, "I am now bare like this — I would sooner die than remain uncovered — where is this heavenly garment?" He answers her, "The heavenly robe is so subtle that only I can see it, not you." Just so, this foolish man never had any heavenly garment at all —

況為他著。諸對法者所說亦爾。本無表業。況有依表所起無表。故對法者妄興此論。又表無表若是色者。青黃赤白為是何耶。復云何成善不善性。若因搖動成善惡性。花劍等動何故不爾。為止如是譬喻者意。顯自所宗。表無表業皆是實有。故作斯論。若諸表業無實體者。則與契經相違。如契經言。愚夫希欲說名為愛。愛所發表說名為業。又契經言。在夜尋伺猶如起煙。旦動身語猶如發焰。若無表業無實體者。則亦與契經相違。如契經說。色有三攝一切色。有色有見有對。有色無見有對。有色無見無對。若無無表色者。則應無有三種建立。無第三故。又若撥無表無表色。吠題呬字。未生怨王。應當不觸害父無間。謂發表位父命猶存。父命終時表業已謝。

— how much less could he have put one upon her. What the Ābhidharmikas say is exactly the same, they claim: there was never any real manifest action to begin with, how much less any unmanifest action arising in dependence upon it — hence, they say, the Ābhidharmikas have wrongly set forth this treatise. Moreover, they ask, if manifest and unmanifest action were form, then which color — blue, yellow, red, or white — would they be? And how, further, could they come to have a wholesome or unwholesome nature? If a wholesome or evil nature is brought about merely through motion, why do the motions of a flower or a sword not likewise become wholesome or evil? In order to put a stop to this Dārṣṭāntika notion and to make clear our own doctrine — that manifest and unmanifest karma are both genuinely real — this treatise is composed. If manifest action possessed no real own-substance, this would contradict the sūtra, as the sūtra says: the fool's own craving is called desire, and what this desire gives rise to as manifest action is called karma. The sūtra further says: nocturnal deliberation and examination are like smoke arising, while the bodily and verbal action stirred at dawn is like the flame that then bursts forth. If unmanifest action possessed no real own-substance, this too would contradict the sūtra, as the sūtra says: form is of three kinds, comprising all form — form that is visible and resistant, form that is invisible yet resistant, and form that is invisible and non-resistant. If there were no such thing as unmanifest form, there could be no such threefold classification at all, for there would be no third category. Moreover, if one denies unmanifest action and unmanifest form, then Vaidehī's son, King Ajātaśatru, ought not to have incurred the ānantarya crime of killing his father at all — for at the moment he committed the manifest act, his father's life still continued, and by the time his father's life actually ended, that manifest action had already ceased —

由先表力得後無表故。未生怨觸無間業。又彼杖髻出家外道。亦應不觸害應無間。謂發表位目連命猶存。目連涅槃時表業已謝由先表力得後無表故。彼外道觸無間業。又若撥無表無表業。應無建立三品有異。謂住律儀品。住不律儀品。住非律儀非不律儀品。然彼所言此表無表體。若是色青黃赤白為是何耶。此責不然非顯色外無別色故。當知身表是形非顯。語表是聲亦非顯色二種。無表法處色攝故不可責以同青等。然諸色處總有四種。一有色處唯顯可了非形。

— it is only through the power of the earlier manifest action that the later unmanifest action was obtained, and hence Ajātaśatru did incur the ānantarya deed. Moreover, that ascetic of the staff-topped matted hair, an outside-path follower who went forth, ought likewise not to have incurred the crime equivalent to an ānantarya deed — for at the moment he committed the manifest act, Maudgalyāyana's life still continued, and by the time Maudgalyāyana attained parinirvāṇa, that manifest action had already ceased — it is only through the power of the earlier manifest action that the later unmanifest action was obtained, and hence that outside-path follower did incur the crime equivalent to an ānantarya deed. Moreover, if one denies unmanifest action and unmanifest karma, there could be no establishing of the three distinct grades — namely, abiding in restraint, abiding in non-restraint, and abiding in neither. As for their own claim — that if this substance of manifest and unmanifest action is form, then which of blue, yellow, red, or white is it — this objection does not hold, since it is not the case that there is no form other than visible color. One should know that bodily manifestation is shape, not visible color, and verbal manifestation is sound, likewise not visible color; and unmanifest action is included within form belonging to the dharma sense-field, and hence cannot be challenged with the same objection as visible color. Now, the sense-fields possessing form are, in total, of four kinds: first, a form-possessing sense-field cognizable only as visible color, not as shape;

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Turning the Dārṣṭāntikas' own parable back against them is a genuinely sharp piece of philosophical jujitsu: rather than simply asserting unmanifest karma's reality by fiat, the Vaibhāṣika accepts the skeptics' own rhetorical weapon — a story mocking belief in something invisible and unprovable — and shows that the skeptics' own theory of manifest action alone commits exactly the same fallacy the parable was meant to expose, since manifest action too, on their account, has no more demonstrable substance than the woman's imagined robe.

2. Four Kinds of Visible Form, and Which Paths Definitely Produce Unmanifest Karma

A precise taxonomy answers the skeptics' further challenge — if manifest and unmanifest action were form, which color would they be? — by distinguishing four kinds of form-possessing sense-fields (cognizable by color alone, by shape alone, by both, or by neither), showing that bodily manifestation is properly shape rather than color, sidestepping the objection entirely. The fascicle then works out precisely which paths of karma definitely produce unmanifest action regardless of circumstance, settling on a refined rule: every root path except sexual misconduct definitely has unmanifest action with variable manifest action, while sexual misconduct alone reverses the pattern at its preparatory stage.

Chinese Source

二有色處唯形可了非顯。三有色處顯形俱可了。四有色處顯形俱不可了。顯可了非形者。謂青黃赤白影光明闇。形可了非顯者。謂身表色。顯形俱可了者。謂所餘若顯若形俱可了色。顯形俱不可了者。謂空界色。又如所說。若身搖動成善惡性。花劍等動何不爾者。此亦不然。有根法異。無根法異。身是有情數攝。由心運動能表有善惡心心所法。花劍等不爾故。表無表決定實有。然表無表依身而起有依一分。如彈指舉足等。一分動轉作善惡業有依具分。如禮佛逐怨等。舉身運動作善惡業。此中隨所依身。極微數量表業亦爾。如表數量無表亦爾。問隨彼彼業若有表即有無表耶。若有無表即有表耶。或有說者。七根本業道決定具有表與無表。加行後起表業定有無表不定。唯猛利纏。及殷重信。所作發無表非餘。有說。

Ru-Yi Translation

— second, a form-possessing sense-field cognizable only as shape, not as visible color; third, a form-possessing sense-field cognizable as both visible color and shape; fourth, a form-possessing sense-field cognizable as neither visible color nor shape. Cognizable as visible color but not shape refers to blue, yellow, red, white, shadow, light, brightness, and darkness. Cognizable as shape but not visible color refers to the form that constitutes bodily manifestation. Cognizable as both refers to every other form cognizable both ways. Cognizable as neither refers to the form of empty space. As for the further objection — that if bodily motion brings about a wholesome or evil nature, then why do the motions of a flower or a sword not do likewise — this too does not hold, since there is a difference between what possesses faculties and what does not: the body belongs to the class of sentient beings, and, moved by mind, is able to manifest the presence of wholesome or evil mind and mind-associated dharmas, whereas a flower or a sword is not so — hence manifest and unmanifest action are certainly and genuinely real. Now, manifest and unmanifest action arise in dependence upon the body, and this dependence may be partial — as with snapping a finger or raising a foot, where the motion of a single part accomplishes a wholesome or evil karma — or it may be complete, as with bowing to the Buddha or pursuing an enemy, where the motion of the whole body accomplishes a wholesome or evil karma. In each case, manifest action corresponds in scale to the number of atomic particles of the body depended upon, and unmanifest action likewise corresponds to the same scale as manifest action. Question: for any given karma, if there is manifest action, is there necessarily unmanifest action as well? And if there is unmanifest action, is there necessarily manifest action as well? Some say: the seven root paths of karma definitely possess both manifest and unmanifest action; the preparatory action and after-arising stages definitely have manifest action, but their unmanifest action is not fixed — only when performed with a fierce entanglement, or with deep and weighty faith, does unmanifest action arise, and not otherwise. Some say:

七根本業道無表定有表則不定。若自作者有表若遣他作唯得無表。

— the seven root paths of karma definitely have unmanifest action, but their manifest action is not fixed — if one performs the act oneself, there is manifest action, but if one has another perform it, one obtains only the unmanifest action,

加行後起如前說。評曰。應作是說。除欲邪行餘根本業道。無表定有表則不定。若自作即時究竟者彼有表業。若遣他作或究竟時。表已息者則唯有無表。若欲邪行表亦定有加行位決定有表。無表不定如前說。後起位定有無表。表則不定若作則有。不作則無。當知此說散心所作。若隨支分定散差別。有表無表如理應思。

— with the preparatory action and after-arising stages explained as before. The verdict: one should say that, apart from sexual misconduct, the other root paths of karma definitely have unmanifest action, while their manifest action is not fixed — if one performs the act oneself and it reaches completion at once, one has manifest action; if one has another perform it, or if the manifest action has already ceased by the time of completion, one has only the unmanifest action. As for sexual misconduct, its manifest action too is definitely present — at the preparatory stage, manifest action is definitely present, while its unmanifest action is not fixed, as explained before; at the after-arising stage, unmanifest action is definitely present, while manifest action is not fixed, present if one performs the act and absent if one does not. One should know that this discussion concerns action performed with a distracted mind — as for the distinctions of fixed and distracted mind across the various components of the act, one should reason through manifest and unmanifest action accordingly.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The shape-versus-color distinction is a genuinely elegant piece of conceptual rescue: it does not deny that manifest karma is form, only that "form" must mean visible color specifically — by relocating bodily manifestation into the category of shape, a category no less real for being differently perceived, the treatise dissolves an objection that looked devastating only because it had smuggled in an unnecessarily narrow definition of what form could be.

3. How a Single Daily Flower Sustains a Lifetime of Merit

A beautiful cluster of devotional vows illustrates how unmanifest karma, once generated with sufficient sincerity, can persist uninterrupted for an entire lifetime through the smallest possible daily gesture: a vow never to eat before offering as little as a single flower or stick of incense to a Buddha image, or before giving away as little as a single handful of food, or before sweeping as little as a single foot's width of temple ground — each sustaining a continuous stream of merit for one's whole life, exactly mirrored by an equally modest daily act of harming an enemy sustaining a continuous stream of demerit instead.

Chinese Source

彼非律儀非不律儀所攝。妙行惡行由三因緣無表不斷。一由意樂不息。二由加行不捨。三由限勢未過。由意樂不息加行不捨者。如於佛像窣堵波等。起淳淨心恭敬供養。所發無表從初剎那乃至意樂未息。或加行未捨已來相續不斷。若意樂息及加行捨無表便斷。限勢未過者。謂淳淨心及猛利纏所作善惡。隨彼勢力無表不斷。如猛利纏殺捃多蟻。所發無表盡形相續。淳淨心所作亦爾。謂如有人起殷重信。修營供具奉施眾僧。燒香散花種種供養。或於佛說如是日月五年會等。請諸眾僧種種供養。起淳淨心發身語業。乃至意樂未息。或加行未捨無表不斷。若意樂息及捨加行無表便斷。餘處中行廣說亦爾。復有處中妙行惡行。盡眾同分無表不斷。妙行者。如有立願。若不供養諸佛形像窣堵波等終不先食。彼於日日隨力所能。

Ru-Yi Translation

That good conduct and evil conduct included within neither restraint nor non-restraint have their unmanifest action continue uninterrupted through three conditions — first, because the intention has not subsided; second, because the effort has not been abandoned; third, because the fixed duration of its force has not yet expired. As to the intention not subsiding and the effort not being abandoned: this is as when one, before an image of the Buddha or a stūpa, gives rise to a mind of pure devotion, offering reverent worship — the unmanifest action thus generated continues uninterrupted from its very first moment for as long as the intention has not subsided or the effort has not been abandoned; once the intention subsides or the effort is abandoned, the unmanifest action ceases. As to the fixed duration of its force not yet having expired: this refers to good or evil deeds performed with a mind of pure devotion or with a fierce entanglement, whose unmanifest action continues uninterrupted for exactly as long as that force lasts — as when, through a fierce entanglement, one kills the ant Kuṇḍalī, the unmanifest action thus generated continues for the rest of one's life; and deeds performed with a mind of pure devotion are likewise so — as when a certain person, giving rise to deep and weighty faith, prepares offerings and presents them to the assembly of monks, burning incense, scattering flowers, and making various offerings, or, in accordance with what the Buddha has taught, holds such gatherings for a day, a month, or five years, inviting the assembly of monks and making various offerings, giving rise to a mind of pure devotion and generating bodily and verbal karma — this continues uninterrupted for as long as the intention has not subsided or the effort has not been abandoned; once the intention subsides and the effort is abandoned, the unmanifest action ceases — and the same is explained in full for moderate conduct as well. There is, further, moderate good and evil conduct whose unmanifest action continues uninterrupted for the entire span of one's class-nature. As to good conduct: this is as when one takes a vow, "I shall not eat before first making an offering to images of the buddhas, stūpas, and the like," and, day after day, according to one's own ability,

下至一花一香供養。盡眾同分無表不斷。或有立願。若不施他資具命緣。終不先食。彼於日日隨力所能。下至施他一摶之食半搩手衣。盡眾同分無表不斷。或有立願。若於三寶不先供養。終不先食。彼於日日隨力所能。下至施僧一器之食。或復塗掃一足之地。

— makes an offering of as little as a single flower or a single stick of incense, one's unmanifest action continues uninterrupted for the entire span of one's class-nature. Or one may take a vow, "I shall not eat before first giving another the means and conditions of sustaining life," and, day after day, according to one's own ability, gives another as little as a single handful of food or a half-span of cloth, one's unmanifest action continues uninterrupted for the entire span of one's class-nature. Or one may take a vow, "I shall not eat before first making an offering to the Three Jewels," and, day after day, according to one's own ability, gives the saṅgha as little as a single bowl of food, or sweeps and cleans as little as a single foot's width of ground,

盡眾同分無表不斷。或有立願每年某日施諸貧乏。或供養僧。即取少物以供彼用。留所餘財以為儲擬。盡眾同分無表不斷。如是等是名妙行。惡行者。如有立願。我當日日於彼怨所作諸衰損。若不作者終不先食。即於日日下至一打或一惡言訶罵毀辱。盡眾同分無表不斷。如是等是名惡行。

— one's unmanifest action continues uninterrupted for the entire span of one's class-nature. Or one may take a vow to give to the poor and needy, or to make offerings to the saṅgha, on a certain day each year, taking only a small portion of one's own goods for that use while setting aside the remainder as a reserve — one's unmanifest action continues uninterrupted for the entire span of one's class-nature. Cases such as these are called good conduct. As to evil conduct: this is as when one takes a vow, "I shall, day after day, inflict some harm or loss upon my enemy, and if I fail to do so, I shall not eat," and thereupon, day after day, does as little as strike a single blow or utter a single word of abuse and reviling — one's unmanifest action continues uninterrupted for the entire span of one's class-nature. Cases such as these are called evil conduct.

或有造作諸佛形像窣堵波等諸供養具。書寫三藏所攝正法。造聖僧像建僧伽藍。給施衣藥諸資身具。安立福舍種殖樹林。造井橋船階道處等。此諸表業所發無表。具由三緣相續不斷。一由意樂。二由所依。三由事物。由意樂者。謂緣彼事深生歡喜意樂不息。由所依者。謂所依身同分相續命未終位。由事物者。謂所修建佛像等事未都壞滅。如是三緣隨闕一種。由前所發無表便斷。是名妙行。惡行者。謂造罟網刀箭等事。應准前說。是謂所說表無表業略毘婆沙。

There is, further, the making of images of the buddhas, stūpas, and other objects of offering, the copying out of the true Dharma comprised in the Tripiṭaka, the making of images of noble monks, the building of monasteries, the giving of robes, medicine, and other requisites of life, the establishment of houses of merit and the planting of groves, and the building of wells, bridges, boats, staircases, roads, and the like — the unmanifest action generated by these manifest deeds continues uninterrupted through the full complement of three conditions: first, the intention; second, the physical basis; third, the object. As to the intention: this means that, taking that deed as object, one's deep joy and delight has not subsided. As to the physical basis: this means that the class-nature-continuum of the body one depends upon has not yet reached the end of its life. As to the object: this means that the thing constructed — the image of the Buddha and the like — has not yet been entirely destroyed. Should any one of these three conditions fail, the unmanifest action previously generated is thereby cut off — this is called good conduct. As to evil conduct: this refers to the making of nets, snares, knives, arrows, and the like, to be understood according to the same pattern as before. This is called the brief exposition of the Vibhāṣā on manifest and unmanifest karma.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The vow of a single daily flower is one of this tradition's most quietly democratic teachings: it insists that the scale of merit generated has nothing to do with the scale of the gift itself, only with the binding force of a sincere vow renewed moment to moment — meaning the poorest devotee, capable of nothing more than a single blossom each day, can sustain exactly the same uninterrupted river of merit as the wealthiest patron of elaborate ceremonies.

4. The Body-Manifestation Matrix Begins

8. Why There Is No Future Manifest Karma At All

A clean metaphysical rule closes the past/future/present triad: there is no such thing as possessing future manifest bodily action whatsoever, since manifest action, by its very nature, cannot be pre-fashioned before it actually arises — one cannot make use of a karma not yet performed. 12. Karma and Fruit Across Realms, and the Metaphysics of Magical Transformation

A precise rule governs karma bound to a given realm — its fruit is generally bound to that same realm — but a fascinating exception opens an extended technical debate: a form-realm meditative path can produce a desire-realm transformation-body and desire-realm transformation-speech through supernormal power, and this counts as the form-realm path's own exertion-fruit even though the transformation itself belongs to a different realm entirely. 13. Can a Transformed Body Speak Without a Body at All?

The fascicle closes on a genuinely curious puzzle: can transformation-speech ever arise apart from a transformed body altogether?
