Between Death and Rebirth

Fascicle 68 completes the technical profile of faculty-change — how many paths it requires, whether they are contaminated or undefiled, and the arhat's own sixfold hierarchy from prone-to-regression up through unshakeable — before turning to an entirely new topic: the intermediate state between death and rebirth. Three rival views are refuted, and a systematic fourfold ledger tracks exactly how dying, being reborn, and experiencing a given realm's own existence-mode can come apart across the desire, form, and formless realms. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 68 continues the Sentient-Being Nyāsa (結蘊第二中有情納息第三之六), completing the extended debate over faculty-change (轉根) begun in the previous fascicle, then opening an entirely new topic within the same nyāsa: the intermediate state (中有, antarābhava) between death and rebirth. Terms established in earlier fascicles recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Does Faculty-Change Discard the Cutting-Off at the Peak of Existence?

Opening part six of the Sentient-Being Nyāsa, the treatise resolves a genuine puzzle: when a practitioner already free of the sphere of nothingness's own defilement turns his faculty from resolve-through-faith to attainment-through-vision, he relinquishes the undefiled counteractive path at the three formless stages — but the cutting-off those paths achieved survives, sustained instead by the worldly counteractive path that continues alongside it. Pressed further with the harder case of a practitioner only partly free of the very peak of existence, where no worldly counteraction remains to sustain anything, the compilers rule that the cutting-off is indeed lost, yet the afflictions never reappear, exactly as an ordinary person who dies free of a lower stage's defilement never sees that stage's afflictions return.

Chinese Source

結蘊第二中有情納息第三之六問已離無所有處染信勝解轉根作見至時。既捨三無色無漏對治道於三地斷為亦捨不。設爾何失。二俱有過。所以者何。若捨彼斷云何不成就彼三地煩惱。若不捨者云何捨彼道而不捨斷耶。答應作是說。不捨彼斷。問既捨彼道如何不捨三地斷耶。答下三無色地有二對治道。一者世俗。二者無漏。學轉根時雖捨彼無漏而不捨世俗。

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The worldly path's quiet role as a kind of insurance policy is the real discovery here: an undefiled path can be relinquished without loss so long as some other mechanism — even a comparatively crude worldly counteraction — still holds the resulting cutting-off in place, and only when no such backup exists does the treatise concede an actual, if practically inconsequential, loss.

2. How Many Paths Are Used in Each Kind of Faculty-Change?

The treatise asks how many aids to practice, uninterrupted paths, and liberation paths are required for the two chief kinds of faculty-sharpening: one of resolve-through-faith becoming one who has attained through vision, and an arhat liberated-in-season becoming one unshakeable. The compilers settle on a single aid, uninterrupted path, and liberation path for the first — a learner's faculty being easily turned — but nine each for the second, since an arhat's long-cultivated faculty is as hard to relinquish and reattain as tearing down and rebuilding a house is harder than building it new.

Chinese Source

問信勝解轉根作見至時。用幾加行道幾無間道幾解脫道而轉根耶。

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The house-building simile captures something the numbers alone would not: the very same kind of transition — trading a lesser faculty for a sharper one — costs vastly more effort at the non-learner's stage not because the goal is harder to reach, but because what must first be dismantled is so much more deeply set.

3. Contaminated or Undefiled, New or Previously Attained?

Two further technical questions round out the profile of each faculty-change: whether the paths involved are contaminated or undefiled, and whether they are freshly attained or previously possessed. In both cases the aid to practice may be either, reflecting its preparatory, still-flexible character, while the uninterrupted and liberation paths proper are undefiled and never previously attained — with one exception, the arhat's ninth liberation path, whose future cultivation spans both categories since it simultaneously establishes wholesome roots across all three realms at once.

Chinese Source

問信勝解轉根作見至時。加行道等為有漏為無漏耶。答彼加行道或有漏或無漏。未來所修通有漏及無漏。彼無間道一向無漏。未來所修亦唯無漏彼解脫道一向無漏。未來所修有作是說。亦唯無漏。復有說者。爾時通修有漏無漏。問時解脫阿羅漢轉根作不動時。加行道等為有漏為無漏耶。答彼加行道或有漏或無漏。未來所修通有漏及無漏。九無間道八解脫道一向無漏。未來所修亦唯無漏。第九解脫道一向無漏。未來所修通有漏及無漏。彼於爾時隨其所應兼修三界諸善根故。

His liberation path is, without exception, undefiled; as for what is cultivated for the future, there are those who say this too is solely undefiled, while others say that, at that time, both the contaminated and the undefiled are cultivated together. Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the aid to practice alone can be contaminated while the uninterrupted and liberation paths cannot is a small but telling structural fact: the aid to practice is preparatory scaffolding, still negotiable in its own moral character, while the decisive moments of a faculty-change — the actual cutting away of the old faculty and the actual grasping of the new — admit no such flexibility at all.

4. Lineage, Fruit-Classification, and the Six Kinds of Arhat

The treatise classifies each faculty-change's aid, uninterrupted, and liberation paths by lineage — resolve-through-faith versus attainment-through-vision, liberated-in-season versus liberated-beyond-season — and by whether they belong to a fruit-path or a path toward a superior fruit, the latter possibility vanishing entirely for an arhat, who has no superior fruit left to seek. It then introduces the arhat's own sixfold hierarchy — prone to regression, capable of reflection, protective of the dharma, steadfastly abiding, capable of penetration, and unshakeable — tracing the faculty relinquished and gained at each successive step upward.

Chinese Source

信勝解轉根作見至時。加行無間道是信勝解道攝。解脫道是見至道攝。時解脫阿羅漢轉根作不動時。加行道九無間道八解脫道是時解脫道攝。第九解脫道是不時解脫道攝。信勝解轉根作見至時。若住果而轉根者。彼加行無間解脫道皆是果道攝。若住勝果道而轉根者。彼加行無間道是勝果道攝。解脫道是果道攝。時解脫阿羅漢轉根作不動時。彼加行無間解脫道皆是果道攝。彼無勝果道故。信勝解轉根作見至時。若住果而轉根者。彼捨果道得果道。若住勝果道而轉根者。彼捨果道及勝果道唯得果道。時解脫阿羅漢轉根作不動時。唯捨果道得果道。無學位無勝果道故。阿羅漢有六種。謂退法思法護法安住法堪達法不動法。退法阿羅漢轉根作思法時。彼捨退法根得思法根。思法阿羅漢轉根作護法時。彼捨思法根得護法根。

One asks: at the moment one of resolve-through-faith turns his faculty to become one who has attained through vision, if he abides in the nature of prone-to-regression, does turning the faculty of prone-to-regression thereby immediately attain the faculty of the nature unshakeable, or does turning the faculty of prone-to-regression attain only the faculty of the nature capable-of-reflection, advancing by degrees until finally, at the very end, attaining the faculty of the nature unshakeable? And so on, up through: if he abides in the nature of steadfastly-abiding, does turning the faculty of the nature steadfastly-abiding thereby immediately attain the faculty of the nature unshakeable, or does turning the faculty of the nature steadfastly-abiding attain only the faculty of the nature capable-of-penetration, and only by further turning the faculty of the nature capable-of-penetration does one then attain the faculty of the nature unshakeable? There are those who say: if one abides in the nature of prone-to-regression, turning the faculty of the nature prone-to-regression thereby immediately attains the faculty of the nature unshakeable, not by advancing through degrees; and so on, up through: if one abides in the nature of steadfastly-abiding, turning the faculty of the nature steadfastly-abiding thereby immediately attains the faculty of the nature unshakeable, not by advancing through degrees. For what reason is this so? Because faculty-turning at the learner's stage differs from that at the non-learner's stage — that is, at the non-learner's stage, both relinquishing and attaining are difficult, requiring considerable effort and being accomplished only by degrees, while this is not so at the learner's stage, and it is for this reason that the turning there is accomplished all at once.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The six-arhat hierarchy reads almost like a ladder of increasing security rather than increasing wisdom: each rung up trades a faculty vulnerable to some specific failure — regression, needing protection, needing a stable environment — for one immune to it, culminating in the unshakeable arhat who needs nothing external at all to preserve what he has attained.

5. The Compilers Reject "One Leap to Unshakeable," and Restate the Gradual Account

A proposed shortcut theory holds that a learner prone to regression, upon turning his faculty even once, leaps directly to the nature of unshakeable, skipping every intermediate grade, since a learner's faculties are supposedly easy to relinquish and attain unlike a non-learner's. The compilers reject this outright: ease or difficulty of faculty-change tracks only the number of uninterrupted and liberation paths employed, never whether the transition is gradual or sudden, and the correct account has the learner advancing by exactly the same single step at a time as the arhat does, merely using one path apiece instead of nine.

Chinese Source

評曰彼不應作是說。捨得難易但由無間解脫多少。不由漸頓而有差別。應作是說。若住退法種性者。轉退法種性根但得思法種性根。

Ru-Yi Translation

12. Suppression Is Not Abandonment: Refuting the View That Merely Suppressing Affliction Yields Higher Rebirth

The treatise turns to refute the Dārṣṭāntikas' claim that merely suppressing a lower stage's affliction, without fully cutting it off, already suffices for rebirth in a higher stage, and along with it the Vibhajyavādins' confusion, born of denying the intermediate state altogether, over how one not yet free of a given stage's defilement could ever die without simply reappearing in that same stage or lower. The refutation rests on a single principle: a lower stage's own affliction actively obstructs a higher stage's own virtues, so that only their full abandonment, never mere suppression, opens the way upward — confirmed by the absurd consequence that mere hearing-and-reflection wisdom, fully capable of suppressing affliction across all nine stages of the three realms, would otherwise guarantee liberation from rebirth that only genuine cultivation-wisdom can actually deliver.

13. The Closing Ledger: How Many Fail to Be Reborn for Want of Full Freedom from Defilement

The fascicle closes by tallying exactly how many kinds of practitioner, still bound by a given realm's own defilement, fail to be reborn beyond it — two for the desire realm, four for the form realm, and four again for the formless realm — each count defended by the same merging principle used throughout this fascicle, collapsing what first appears as seven or nine superficially distinct cases into a smaller number of genuinely distinct kinds. A final closing statement confirms that every use of "being reborn" throughout this entire discussion has spoken specifically with reference to excluding a rebirth-existence, leaving the intermediate state's own arising untouched by the same restriction.
