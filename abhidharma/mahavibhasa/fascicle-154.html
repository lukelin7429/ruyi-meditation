Cremated Yet Unharmed: The Elder Revived

Fascicle 154 opens with the story of the elder Revived, cremated while in cessation-attainment yet unharmed, works through a sprawling tetralemma census of who possesses this attainment and its fruit, examines the no-perception heaven's own faculty census and inevitable fall, and closes with exhaustive censuses of faculty inclusion and association. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. The Elder Revived: Cremated in Cessation-Attainment, Yet Unharmed

Fascicle 154 opens with cessation-attainment's own extraordinary protective power — one abiding within it cannot be burned, drowned, poisoned, stabbed, or killed — illustrated by the sūtra's own account of the elder Revived, who entered this attainment outside a city, was mistaken for dead by passersby and cremated on the spot, and emerged the next morning to beg for alms entirely unharmed, earning his name from the astonished onlookers who had just burned him.

Chinese Source

契經中說住滅定者。不為火所燒。水所漂。毒所中。刃所害。他所殺。問何故住滅定者有如是勝利。尊者世友作如是說。由此滅定是不害法故。住此者非害所害。有說。此定有大威德。為諸威德天神護之故不可害。有說。得靜慮者靜慮境界。具神通者神通境界。俱不思議故不可害。有說。此定無心非無心者有生有死故不可害等活契經是此論緣起者。有佛名羯洛俱村馱。有第一雙弟子。一名極遠。二名等活。尊者等活曾於一城中作多教化眾所知識。後於城邊多人行處。入滅盡定。時有放牧採樵行人見已咸言。今此尊者端坐滅度誠為異哉。我等宜應焚燒供養。便取種種牛糞乾薪。埋積其身焚之捨去。尊者明旦從定而起。振迅衣服。於日初分。持鉢入城徐行乞食。時焚燒者見已驚言。我等昨燒其屍。而今復來乞食。城中人眾等皆唱言。今此沙門死而還活。由斯故名等活尊者。應知身不燒者由滅定力故。衣不燒者由神通力故。有說。身及衣俱不燒者。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the sūtra states: one abiding in the attainment of cessation cannot be burned by fire, swept away by water, harmed by poison, injured by a blade, or killed by another. One might ask: why does one abiding in this attainment enjoy such extraordinary benefits? The Venerable Vasumitra explains: because this attainment is itself a dharma of non-harm, one abiding within it can neither harm nor be harmed. Another position holds that this attainment carries great and awesome power, protected by mighty and awesome spirits, and so cannot be harmed. Another position holds that one who has obtained dhyāna possesses the very domain of dhyāna, and one who possesses supernormal power possesses the very domain of supernormal power, both alike beyond ordinary comprehension, and so cannot be harmed. Another position holds that this attainment, being mindless rather than genuinely without mind altogether, still admits of birth and death, and so cannot be harmed. The sūtra concerning the elder Revived is the very occasion that gave rise to this present discussion. Long ago, there was a Buddha named Krakucchanda, who had a foremost pair of disciples, one named Vidūra and the other named Revived. The Venerable Revived had once taught extensively within a certain city, becoming widely known and respected there; afterward, at a spot frequented by many people just outside that city, he entered the attainment of cessation. At that time, herders, woodcutters, and other travelers, seeing him there, all exclaimed: "how extraordinary, that this venerable one should sit upright and pass into final extinction!" — and they resolved to cremate him in reverence, gathering every manner of dried cattle dung and firewood, piling it around his body, setting it ablaze, and then departing. The following morning, the venerable one rose from his attainment, shook out his robes, and, in the early part of the day, took up his begging bowl and walked slowly into the city to beg for alms. Those who had cremated him, seeing this, cried out in alarm: "we burned his body just yesterday, and yet here he is again, come begging for food!" And the people of the city all proclaimed together: "this monk has died, and yet has come back to life!" — and it was for this reason that he came to be called the Venerable Revived. It should be understood that his body was not burned through the power of the attainment of cessation itself, while his robes were not burned through the power of his own supernormal ability. Another position holds that both his body and his robes alike were left unburned

皆由滅定力故。是故彼經是此論緣起。

entirely through the power of the attainment of cessation. It is for this reason that this particular sūtra serves as the very occasion giving rise to the present discussion.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The elder's own quiet reappearance the morning after his own cremation — simply shaking out his robes and walking into town to beg, as if nothing at all had happened — gives this fascicle's opening a wonderfully deadpan quality: the miracle itself is treated almost casually, the real drama belonging entirely to the villagers' own shock rather than to any triumphant display on the elder's part.

2. Why Offering to One Just Emerged From Cessation Brings Such Great Reward

A traditional teaching-simile claims that offering to one who has just emerged from cessation-attainment invariably yields its karmic fruit within this very life — a claim the treatise carefully qualifies as coming not from scripture but from Abhidharma tradition, which may or may not hold in every case, before offering five complementary explanations for why such an offering carries such weight: it touches every meditative attainment the practitioner passed through on the way in and out, it resembles offering food to one who has, in effect, briefly touched nirvāṇa itself and returned.

Chinese Source

傳喻中說。若有施從滅定起者。彼必成順現法受業。問何故施從滅定起者。必成順現法受業耶。答此不必須通。所以者何。此非素怛纜毘奈耶。阿毘達磨教但是傳喻所說。諸傳喻說或然不然若必欲通者。應知此施或得現果。或得大果。彼順世間意所樂故。但說現果。問何故施從滅定起者。或得現果或得大果。答若有施從此定起者。則為施從一切靜慮解脫等持等至起者。所以者何。諸欲入出滅盡等至。必先起欲界善心。次入初靜慮。如是乃至入非想非非想處。從此無間入滅盡定。從此定出或起非想非非想處。或起無所有處。如是乃至還起初靜慮。復入欲界善心。彼由如是功德熏相續故。能令施者或得現果或得大果。有說。從滅定起者。威儀寂靜來往語言。衣著飲食皆悉詳審。諸清信長者婆羅門等。見已咸生希有之想。以慇淨心施諸資具故。得現果或得大果。有說。得此定者名稱普聞過於餘定。諸清信長者婆羅門等。聞已皆生奇特之想。以慇淨心施諸資具故。得現果或得大果。有說。住滅定者諸食皆斷。若施從此起者。則為施無食者。食謂住有漏有心定者。雖斷段食而食有漏觸思識食。住無漏定者雖斷真實四食。而有相似觸思識食。住滅定者一切皆無。是故施從此起者。則為施於無食者食。由此因緣。或得現果。或得大果。有說。若有施從此定起者。則為施到涅槃還來者食。以此定似涅槃故。謂如入無餘依涅槃界者。滅一切有所緣法心心所法。不起不滅。住滅定者。亦滅一切有所緣法。心心所法不起不滅故似涅槃。是故施從此定起者。則為施到涅槃還來者食。由此因緣或得現果。或得大果。復次非但施從滅定起者。能得現果。若施五種補特伽羅。皆得現果。一從滅定起。二從慈定起。

Ru-Yi Translation

A traditional teaching-simile states: whoever receives an offering from one who has just emerged from the attainment of cessation will invariably create karma whose fruit ripens within this very life. One might ask: why should offering to one just emerged from this attainment invariably yield its fruit within this very life? Answer: this need not necessarily be reconciled at all, for the simple reason that this account does not derive from the sūtras or the Vinaya proper, but is merely a traditional teaching-simile from the Abhidharma school, and such teaching-similes may or may not hold true in every case. If one nonetheless insists on reconciling it, one should understand that such an offering may yield its fruit either within this very life or as a great fruit later on, the simile speaking only in terms of what accords with worldly expectation and so mentioning only the immediate fruit. One might ask: why, then, does an offering to one emerged from this attainment sometimes yield its fruit within this very life and sometimes yield a great fruit instead? Answer: whoever offers to one emerged from this attainment is, in effect, offering to one emerged from every dhyāna, liberation, concentration, and attainment at once — for whoever enters and emerges from the attainment of cessation must first give rise to a wholesome desire-realm mind, then enter the first dhyāna, and so on in turn up through the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, entering the attainment of cessation immediately from there; and upon emerging from that attainment, one gives rise once more to the mind of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, or of the sphere of nothing-at-all, and so on in turn back down to the first dhyāna once more, and finally to a wholesome desire-realm mind again — and it is through this thorough saturation of one's own continuity with such manifold merit that the giver may come to receive either an immediate or a great fruit. Another position holds that one emerged from this attainment carries himself with such calm and settled deportment in coming and going, in speech, in dress, and in eating and drinking, that pious elders and brahmins, upon seeing him, are filled with a sense of the truly extraordinary, and it is through the sincerity and purity of the mind with which they then offer him provisions that they come to receive either an immediate or a great fruit. Another position holds that one who has obtained this attainment enjoys a reputation surpassing every other kind of attainment, and pious elders and brahmins, hearing of this, are filled with a sense of the truly remarkable, and it is through the sincerity and purity of the mind with which they then offer him provisions that they come to receive either an immediate or a great fruit. Another position holds that one abiding in this attainment has entirely severed every form of nourishment; whoever offers to one emerging from it is, in effect, offering to one who has no nourishment at all — for one abiding in an attainment that possesses mind and is merely contaminated, though he has cut off physical morsel-food, still retains the contaminated forms of contact-food, volition-food, and consciousness-food; one abiding in an uncontaminated attainment, though he has cut off the genuine fourfold nourishment altogether, still retains their resembling counterparts in contact, volition, and consciousness; but one abiding in the attainment of cessation has none of these whatsoever — and it is for this reason that offering to one emerging from this attainment is, in effect, offering food to one who has no food at all, and it is through this very cause that one comes to receive either an immediate or a great fruit. Another position holds that whoever offers to one emerging from this attainment is, in effect, offering food to one who has already reached nirvāṇa and returned from it, since this attainment so closely resembles nirvāṇa itself — for exactly as one who has entered the realm of nirvāṇa without remainder extinguishes every mind and mental factor that takes any object whatsoever, neither arising nor ceasing again, so too one abiding in the attainment of cessation likewise extinguishes every mind and mental factor that takes any object whatsoever, neither arising nor ceasing — and it is on account of this very resemblance to nirvāṇa that offering to one emerging from this attainment is, in effect, offering food to one who has reached nirvāṇa and returned, and it is through this cause that one comes to receive either an immediate or a great fruit. Furthermore, it is not only offering to one emerged from this attainment that can yield an immediate fruit — offering to any of five kinds of persons can likewise yield such a fruit: first, one emerged from the attainment of cessation; second, one emerged from the concentration of loving-kindness;

三從無諍定起。四從見道起。五從初得盡智起復次若施五種補特伽羅。能得大果。一父。二母。三病者。四說法師。五近佛地菩薩。

third, one emerged from the concentration free of contention; fourth, one emerged from the path of seeing; and fifth, one who has just newly obtained the knowledge of exhaustion. Furthermore, offering to any of a further five kinds of persons can yield a great fruit: one's own father, one's own mother, one who is ill, a teacher of the Dharma, and a Bodhisattva close to Buddhahood.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The comparison of offering to one emerged from cessation with offering food to "one who has reached nirvāṇa and returned" is a striking image precisely because of how literally the analogy is drawn: cessation-attainment's own extinguishing of every mind and mental factor is treated as a genuine, temporary rehearsal of nirvāṇa's own defining feature, making the meditator briefly, functionally indistinguishable from someone who has already crossed over and come back.

3. A Sprawling Census: Who Possesses This Attainment, Who Regresses, and Which Arhats Qualify

The fascicle turns to an intricate tetralemma census: does whoever possesses cessation-attainment also possess its ripening-fruit? Does whoever regresses from the attainment also regress from arhatship itself? And among the six canonical types of arhat and their corresponding trainee dispositions, which combinations of "regressive type" and "liberated in both respects" or "bodily witness" actually occur — worked out through six parallel tetralemmas covering every possible pairing.

Chinese Source

問滅盡定於眾同分為亦能引。為但滿耶。答此但能滿而不能引所以者何。唯業能引眾同分。此非業故。問此定為順現法受。為順次生受。為順後次受。為順不定受耶。答此定或順次生受。或順後次受。或順不定受。非順現法受。已生有頂不起此定故。問此於何處受何異熟。答此於非想非非想處受四蘊異熟。問諸成就滅定。亦成就彼異熟耶。答應作四句。有成就滅定非彼異熟。謂生欲色界已得滅定。及得已不退。命終生非想非非想處。滅定異熟不現在前。有成就彼異熟。非滅定。謂得滅定已而退。命終生非想非非想處。滅定異熟現在前。有成就滅定。亦彼異熟。謂得滅定已不退。命終生非想非非想處。滅定異熟現在前。有非成就滅定。亦非彼異熟。謂生欲色界不得滅定。設得已退。若生空處識處無所有處。若不得滅定。生非想非非想處。及得已而退。命終生非想非非想處。滅定異熟不現在前。問諸退滅定。亦退阿羅漢果耶。答應作四句。有退滅定。非阿羅漢果。謂學者退滅定。及無學者退滅定。而不起結。有退阿羅漢果。非滅定。謂慧解脫阿羅漢退。及先學位起滅定已。得阿羅漢果。起上位結退。有退滅定亦阿羅漢果。謂先學位起滅定已。得阿羅漢果。起下位結退。及無學位起滅定已。起三界結隨一而退。有非退滅定。亦非阿羅漢果。謂除前相。阿羅漢有六種。謂退法。思法。護法。安住法。堪達法。不動法。問諸退法彼一切俱解脫耶。答應作四句。有退法非俱解脫。謂退法不得滅定。有俱解脫非退法謂思法乃至不動法。已得滅定。有退法亦俱解脫。謂退法已得滅定。有非退法非俱解脫。謂思法乃至不動法不得滅定。如退法阿羅漢對俱解脫作四句。餘五阿羅漢對俱解脫亦爾。如是成六四句。如無學道有六種阿羅漢。學道亦有六種種性。謂退法種性。乃至不動法種性。問諸退法學彼一切身證耶。答應作四句有退法學非身證。謂退法學不得滅定。有身證非退法學。謂思法學。乃至不動法學。已得滅定有退法學亦身證。謂退法學已得滅定。有非退法學亦非身證。謂思法學乃至不動法學不得滅定。如退法學對身證作四句。餘五種性對身證亦爾。如是亦成六四句。

Ru-Yi Translation

One might ask: does this attainment of cessation serve to draw forth a new class of rebirth, or merely to bring one's present class of rebirth to full completion? Answer: it merely brings one's present class of rebirth to full completion; it cannot itself draw forth a new class of rebirth, since only karma can draw forth a class of rebirth, and this attainment is not itself karma. One might ask: does this attainment ripen its fruit within this very life, in the immediately following life, or only in some later life still further beyond, or does it ripen indeterminately? Answer: it may ripen its fruit in the immediately following life, or in some later life still further beyond, or indeterminately, but never within this very life, since one who has already reached the peak of existence never gives rise to this attainment there. One might ask: in precisely what place, and through precisely what kind of ripening-fruit, is this fruit actually received? Answer: it is received within the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception itself, as the ripening-fruit of the four aggregates there. One might ask: does whoever possesses this attainment of cessation also possess its own ripening-fruit? Answer: this calls for a tetralemma. There is one who possesses the attainment of cessation yet not its ripening-fruit — namely, one born in the desire or form realm who has already obtained the attainment and has not regressed from it, whose ripening-fruit of cessation has not yet become present at death, and who is then reborn into the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. There is one who possesses this attainment's ripening-fruit yet not the attainment itself — namely, one who, having already obtained this attainment, has regressed from it, and, at death, is reborn into the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception with the attainment's own ripening-fruit becoming present. There is one who possesses both the attainment and its ripening-fruit together — namely, one who, having already obtained this attainment without regressing from it, is, at death, reborn into the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception with the attainment's own ripening-fruit becoming present. And there is one who possesses neither — namely, one born in the desire or form realm who has never obtained this attainment, or who obtained it but has since regressed, and who is reborn into the sphere of infinite space, of infinite consciousness, or of nothing-at-all; or one who has never obtained this attainment and is reborn into the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception; or one who obtained it but has since regressed and is, at death, reborn into that same sphere with the attainment's own ripening-fruit not becoming present. One might ask: does whoever regresses from this attainment of cessation also regress from the fruit of arhatship? Answer: this too calls for a tetralemma. There is one who regresses from the attainment of cessation yet not from the fruit of arhatship — namely, a trainee who regresses from this attainment, or one beyond training who regresses from it without giving rise to any fetter. There is one who regresses from the fruit of arhatship yet not from this attainment — namely, an arhat liberated through wisdom who regresses, or one who, while still a trainee, gave rise to this attainment and, having obtained the fruit of arhatship, then regresses through the arising of a higher-stage fetter. There is one who regresses from both together — namely, one who, while still a trainee, gave rise to this attainment and, having obtained the fruit of arhatship, then regresses through the arising of a lower-stage fetter, or one beyond training who, having given rise to this attainment, regresses through the arising of a fetter belonging to any of the three realms. And there is one who regresses from neither — namely, every remaining case beyond those already described. There are six kinds of arhat — the regressive type, the aspiring type, the guarding type, the abiding type, the advancing type, and the immovable type. One might ask: are all of the regressive type also liberated in both respects? Answer: this calls for a tetralemma. There is one of the regressive type who is not liberated in both respects — namely, a regressive-type arhat who has not obtained this attainment. There is one liberated in both respects who is not of the regressive type — namely, one of the aspiring type up through the immovable type who has already obtained this attainment. There is one both of the regressive type and liberated in both respects — namely, a regressive-type arhat who has already obtained this attainment. And there is one neither of the regressive type nor liberated in both respects — namely, one of the aspiring type up through the immovable type who has not obtained this attainment. Exactly as with the regressive-type arhat set against being liberated in both respects, so too, mutatis mutandis, with each of the remaining five kinds of arhat set against being liberated in both respects — yielding six such tetralemmas altogether. Exactly as the path of the one beyond training comprises these six kinds of arhat, so too the path of the trainee comprises six corresponding dispositions — the regressive-type disposition, up through the immovable-type disposition. One might ask: are all trainees of the regressive-type disposition also bodily witnesses? Answer: this too calls for a tetralemma. There is a trainee of the regressive-type disposition who is not a bodily witness — namely, one of the regressive-type disposition who has not obtained this attainment. There is a bodily witness who is not of the regressive-type disposition — namely, one of the aspiring-type disposition up through the immovable-type disposition who has already obtained this attainment. There is one both of the regressive-type disposition and a bodily witness — namely, one of the regressive-type disposition who has already obtained this attainment. And there is one who is neither of the regressive-type disposition nor a bodily witness — namely, one of the aspiring-type disposition up through the immovable-type disposition who has not obtained this attainment. Exactly as with the regressive-type disposition set against being a bodily witness, so too, mutatis mutandis, with each of the remaining five dispositions set against being a bodily witness — yielding six further tetralemmas.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The sheer combinatorial thoroughness here — six kinds of arhat, six corresponding trainee dispositions, each checked in turn against two separate honorific titles through its own tetralemma — is Abhidharma classification at its most exhaustive: rather than simply asserting which combinations are possible, the treatise walks through every logical permutation to confirm precisely which cells of the matrix are actually populated.

4. A Logic Puzzle on Mind's Successor, Then Rebirth Into the No-Perception Heaven

A genuinely intricate logical puzzle opens, distinguishing what serves as the immediately preceding condition for mind from what counts as mind's own immediately following state — since these two relations, though closely related, do not perfectly coincide around the edges of cessation-attainment, requiring their own separate tetralemma to sort out exactly which instants of the attainment satisfy which relation. The same block then shifts to a precise faculty census surrounding the no-perception heaven — eight faculties extinguished and eight restored upon rebirth there.

Chinese Source

問若法是心等無間。彼法是心無間耶。答應作四句。有法是心等無間。非心無間謂除滅定初剎那。及餘有心位諸餘滅定剎那。及出滅定心心所法。有法是心無間。非心等無間。謂滅定初剎那。及餘有心位。彼生老住無常。有法是心等無間。亦心無間。謂滅定初剎那。及餘有心位心心所法。有法非心等無間。亦非心無間。謂除滅定初剎那。及餘有心位。彼生老住無常。諸餘滅定剎那。及出滅定心位。彼生老住無常。問若法是心等無間。彼法是滅定無間耶。答應作四句。有法是心等無間。非滅定無間。謂滅定初剎那。及餘有心位心心所法。有法是滅定無間。非心等無間。謂除滅定初剎那及餘有心位。彼生老住無常。諸餘滅定剎那。及出滅定心位。彼生老住無常。有法是心等無間。亦滅定無間。謂除滅定初剎那及餘有心位。諸餘滅定剎那。及出滅定心心所法。有法非心等無間。亦非滅定無間。謂滅定初剎那。及餘有心位。彼生老住無常。生無想天幾根滅。答八。謂眼耳鼻舌身命意捨根。問此說從何處生無想天耶。

Exactly as with the faculty of the yet-unknown, so too with the already-known faculty and the faculty of complete knowledge — the stages alone differ, everything else remaining the same.
