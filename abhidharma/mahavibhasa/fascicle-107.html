Dharma-Cognition and the Fetters Above

Fascicle 107 tracks the cultivation of knowledge stage by stage across the entire path, resolves the tetralemma of dharma-cognition and subsequent cognition at the path of seeing's own sixteenth moment, and explains why dharma-cognition alone can cut off the fetters of the upper two realms. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 107 continues the Cultivation-of-Knowledge Nyāsa (修智納息第四之三), moving from an exhaustive stage-by-stage cultivation survey into the tetralemma of dharma-cognition and subsequent cognition, the four conditions, and why dharma-cognition alone reaches the upper realms' own fetters. Terms established across fascicles 93–106 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. What Is Cultivated, Present and Future, at Every Stage

Fascicle 107 opens the third part of the Cultivation-of-Knowledge Nyāsa with an exhaustive, stage-by-stage accounting of exactly which kinds of knowledge are cultivated in the present and drawn forth for the future, tracking an ordinary being's progress freeing himself from the desire realm's own defilement, then each dhyāna and formless attainment in turn, through the nine unimpeded paths and eight (or nine) paths of liberation belonging to each stage.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The sheer procedural exhaustiveness on display here — every dhyāna, every formless attainment, every unimpeded and liberation path individually tracked — is itself a kind of argument: by refusing any shortcut or general rule that might paper over an exception, the treatise insists that the cultivation of knowledge is not one uniform process repeating identically at every stage, but a finely differentiated sequence whose details actually change according to where a practitioner stands on the path.

2. Ordinary Beings, the Noble Path of Seeing, and Why It Differs

The same exhaustive method turns to an ordinary being's cultivation of the four immeasurables, the liberations, mastery-bases, and totality-bases, the contemplation of impurity, mindfulness of breathing, and the four wholesome roots of warmth, summit, forbearance, and the supreme mundane dharma, then to drawing forth the five superknowledges — before pivoting to the noble one's own path of seeing, where a governing rule emerges: whatever virtue presently manifests is exactly what gets cultivated for the future, with no cultivation of anything belonging to a different category, unlike the path of cultivation which is free to cultivate both same-category and different-category virtues alike.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The explanation for why the path of seeing cultivates solely same-category virtue while the path of cultivation cultivates both — because the path of seeing's own domain, object, and characteristic activity are each individually fixed, while the path of cultivation's are not — is a compact illustration of how this tradition typically explains an asymmetry: not by simple stipulation, but by tracing it back to a more basic structural difference (fixed versus unfixed) from which the asymmetry then follows as a natural consequence.

Watching the same underlying question — how many kinds of knowledge, and which ones — receive a different numerical answer depending on whether the path taken is undefiled or worldly, and depending on which specific unimpeded or liberation path within a given stage is in view, drives home a point easy to miss at a glance: no single number can summarize "how much knowledge does freeing oneself from defilement cultivate," since the true answer is a lattice of conditional cases, not a constant.

4. Faith-Followers, Time-Liberated Arhats, and Sharpened Faculties

The same tracking extends to two further transformations: the follower through faith sharpening his own faculties to become one who has arrived at insight, and the time-liberated arhat sharpening his own faculties to become unshakeable — each traced through both the undefiled and worldly paths, both before and after freeing himself from the desire realm's own defilement, with the fascicle noting an open disagreement among unnamed teachers over exactly how many kinds of knowledge are cultivated within a single path of liberation.

It is solely the unimpeded path that is capable of cutting off the various fetters — it is by virtue of this that the treatise states here that the knowledge of suffering, and the rest, is incapable of cutting off the fetters abandoned by seeing.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the fetters abandoned by seeing can never be cut off by the corresponding fruit-knowledge itself — only by the unimpeded path that immediately precedes it — is a precise and important piece of Sarvāstivāda technical architecture: the knowledge that bears a truth's own name is, in each case, the achievement that follows the cutting-off, not the instrument that performs it, a distinction this fascicle's closing tetralemmas take considerable care to keep sharply drawn.
