A New Chapter: Sixteen Kinds of Contact, and the Divine Eye

Fascicle 149 opens the Contact Nyāsa, defending contact's own reality against the Dṛṣṭāntikas, defining and cross-tabulating its sixteen kinds, and closing with the divine eye's own nature, name, and the puzzle of observing a transformation-body sixteen thousand yojanas tall. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 149 complete, 17 sections Source: Taishō T1545, vol. 27, fascicle 149 Topic: 根蘊第六中觸納息第三之一 begins (the Faculties Aggregate; the Contact Nyāsa opens)

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. Fascicle 149 opens the Contact Nyāsa (觸納息第三之一), a new chapter within the Sixth Aggregate following the conclusion of the Existence Nyāsa in fascicle 148. Terms established across fascicles 93–148 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. A New Chapter Opens: Sixteen Kinds of Contact, and Is Contact Even Real?

Fascicle 149 opens an entirely new nyāsa within the Faculties Aggregate — the Contact Nyāsa — listing sixteen distinct kinds of contact before immediately confronting a rival school's most basic challenge: the Dṛṣṭāntikas hold that contact has no real substance of its own at all, citing the sūtra's own description of eye, form, and eye-consciousness simply "coming together" as contact, with nothing separate ever found beyond those three.

Chinese Source

有十六觸謂有對觸。增語觸。明觸。無明觸。非明非無明觸。愛觸。恚觸。順樂受觸。順苦受觸。順不苦不樂受觸。眼觸。耳觸。

Ru-Yi Translation

There are sixteen kinds of contact: resistance-contact, designation-contact, illumination-contact, non-illumination-contact, neither-illumination-nor-non-illumination-contact, craving-contact, hatred-contact, pleasant-feeling-contact, painful-feeling-contact, neutral-feeling-contact, eye-contact, ear-contact,

鼻觸。舌觸。身觸。意觸。云何有對觸。乃至云何意觸。如是等章。及解章義既領會已。當廣分別。問何故作此論。答為止他宗顯己義故。謂譬喻者說。觸非實有。所以者何。契經說故。如契經說。眼及色為緣生眼識。三和合觸等。離眼色眼識外實觸體不可得。為遮彼意顯觸體是實有。若觸體非實有者。便違經說。如契經說。觸為緣受。若無觸者但應說六處緣受。或說無緣不應言觸緣受。又若觸體非實有者。應說緣起唯十一支。契經不應說有十二。

nose-contact, tongue-contact, body-contact, and mind-contact. "What is resistance-contact?" And so on, up through "what is mind-contact?" — such are the chapter headings, and such is the explanation of their meaning. Having grasped this, a full analysis should now be given. Why raise this discussion at all? To block a rival school's own position and to make clear this treatise's own view. The Dṛṣṭāntikas hold that contact is not truly real, on the grounds that the sūtra itself states: "the eye and form serve as conditions for the arising of eye-consciousness; the coming together of these three is contact" — and, apart from the eye, form, and eye-consciousness, no separate, genuinely real substance called "contact" can ever be found. To block this view, the present discussion makes clear that contact's own substance is indeed real — for if contact's own substance were not real, this would contradict what the sūtra itself states: "contact serves as a condition for feeling." If there were truly no contact, the sūtra ought simply to have said that the six sense-bases serve as a condition for feeling, or, having no condition to name, ought not to have spoken of contact conditioning feeling at all. Moreover, if contact's own substance were not real, the sūtra ought to speak of dependent origination as having only eleven links, not twelve as it actually does.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That an entire nyāsa can open by defending the very existence of its own subject matter is a reminder of how contested even the most basic Abhidharma categories could be: before any classification of contact's sixteen kinds can proceed, the treatise first has to win the more fundamental argument that contact is a real thing at all, not just a convenient way of describing three other things meeting.

2. Two Vivid Similes Defend Contact's Reality

The defense of contact's reality continues with two memorable images: just as the moon, a moon-loving jewel, and a vessel coming together condition the arising of water — without this meaning water performs its watery function without ever truly existing — so too the coming together of a faculty, its object, and consciousness conditions the arising of contact, without this meaning contact performs its function of conditioning feeling without ever truly existing. Five further reasons are then offered for why the Faculties Aggregate specifically is the place to take up this defense.

Chinese Source

又若觸體非實有者。但應說有九大地法。然說有十故觸實有。問若觸實有云何會釋彼所引經。答彼經意說三法和合為緣生觸非於無體得有生義。此若不生云何緣受。譬如月月愛珠。及器和合為緣生水。非無水生得有水用。又如日日愛珠。粖薪和合為緣生火。非無火生得有火用。如是根境及識和合為緣生觸非無觸生得有觸用。觸用謂能為緣生受。是故欲止他說顯觸實有而作斯論。問何緣根蘊分別觸耶。答彼作論者意欲爾故乃至廣說。復次此不應問。所以者何。前已說一一蘊中具一切義故。復次一切法觸所集起。根由觸生故分別觸。復次心心所以觸為命。觸所引觸所轉觸力故現在前此中有根故分別觸。復次先安立諸觸後辯根相應。根以觸為章故應先分別觸。

Ru-Yi Translation

Moreover, if contact's own substance were not real, there ought to be only nine great-class mental factors found universally in every mind — yet the sūtra speaks of ten, and it is for this reason that contact must indeed be real. One might ask: if contact is indeed real, how, then, is the passage the Dṛṣṭāntikas themselves cite to be reconciled? Answer: that sūtra passage means that the coming together of three dharmas serves as a condition for the arising of contact — it does not mean that contact arises without any real substance of its own at all. And if contact did not itself arise, how could it possibly condition feeling? It is exactly like the coming together of the moon, the moon-loving jewel, and a vessel, which serves as a condition for the arising of water — this does not mean that water performs its watery function without ever itself truly arising. Or exactly like the coming together of the sun, the sun-loving jewel, and kindling, which serves as a condition for the arising of fire — this does not mean that fire performs its fiery function without ever itself truly arising. In just the same way, the coming together of a faculty, its object, and consciousness serves as a condition for the arising of contact — this does not mean that contact performs its own function, namely serving as a condition for feeling, without ever itself truly arising. It is precisely to block this rival view and to make clear that contact is indeed real that the present discussion is raised. One might then ask: why does the Faculties Aggregate take up the analysis of contact at all? Answer: the composer of this treatise simply intended it so, and so on, as explained at length before. Furthermore, this question ought not even to be raised, since it has already been explained that each and every aggregate already contains the full range of possible meanings within it. Furthermore, every dharma is gathered up and set going by contact, and since the faculties themselves arise through contact, contact is therefore analyzed here. Furthermore, mind and mental factors take contact as their very life; drawn forth by contact, turned by contact, and manifesting through contact's own power, they are thereby present before us — and since faculties are found within this very process, contact is analyzed here. Furthermore, the various kinds of contact are first established, and only afterward is the faculties' own association explained — and since the faculties take contact as their chapter heading, contact must accordingly be analyzed first.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The sun-jewel-and-kindling image for fire and the moon-jewel-and-vessel image for water are drawn from a genuinely ancient store of natural-philosophy lore about gem-conditioned combustion and condensation — and their deployment here shows this tradition reaching outside its own technical vocabulary for whatever illustrative resources, however folkloric, could make an abstract metaphysical point land more vividly.

3. Why Exactly Sixteen? Six Reasons for a Very Particular Number

Before analyzing each kind of contact individually, the treatise pauses to justify its own chosen number: contact could equally well be described as a single unified thing, as two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, or ten kinds, or as effectively limitless if broken down moment by moment — yet the treatise settles on exactly sixteen, justified by six independent principles of classification: what is taken as object, obstruction and its antidote, intrinsic nature, opposition and accord, association, and dependence.

Chinese Source

問諸聖教中。或說一觸如心所中立觸心所。十大地法中立觸大地法。或說二觸謂有漏無漏縛解繫不繫。或說三觸。謂下中上善不善無記。或說四觸。謂三界繫及不繫。或說五觸。謂三界繫及學無學。或說六觸。謂眼觸乃至意觸。或說七觸。謂見苦所斷。乃至修所斷。并學無學或說八觸。謂見苦所斷乃至修所斷。及見道修道無學道。或說九觸謂下下乃至上上。或說十觸。謂欲界繫。乃至非想非非想處繫及不繫。若約相續剎那分別則有無量。何故此中於一等廣說十六。於無量略說十六耶。答由六因緣不略不廣說十六觸。謂所緣故。障治故自性故。違順故相應故所依故。由所緣故立有對觸增語觸。由障治故立明觸無明觸。由自性故立非明非無明觸。由違順故立愛觸恚觸。由相應故立順樂受觸。順苦受觸順不苦不樂受觸由所依故立眼觸乃至意觸。

Ru-Yi Translation

One might ask: throughout the noble teachings, contact is sometimes spoken of as a single thing — as when "contact" is established as one of the mental factors, and as the single great-class mental factor found universally in every mind; sometimes as two — contaminated and uncontaminated, bound and liberated, bound to a realm and unbound; sometimes as three — inferior, middling, and superior, or wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral; sometimes as four — bound to each of the three realms, or unbound; sometimes as five — bound to each of the three realms, or belonging to the trainee or the one beyond training; sometimes as six — eye-contact through mind-contact; sometimes as seven — cut off by seeing suffering, and so on through cut off by cultivation, together with belonging to the trainee or the one beyond training; sometimes as eight — cut off by seeing suffering, and so on through cut off by cultivation, together with belonging to the path of seeing, the path of cultivation, or the path of the one beyond training; sometimes as nine — from the very lowest grade up through the very highest; sometimes as ten — bound to the desire realm, and so on through bound to the peak of existence, or unbound; and if one further analyzes it by way of successive moments within a single continuum, the count becomes altogether limitless. Why, then, does the present passage speak at length of exactly sixteen where a single, unified account would do, yet speak only briefly of sixteen where a limitless account might otherwise be given? Answer: sixteen kinds of contact are spoken of — neither abbreviated further nor expanded further — for six distinct reasons: on the basis of what is taken as object, on the basis of obstruction and its antidote, on the basis of intrinsic nature, on the basis of opposition and accord, on the basis of association, and on the basis of what it depends upon. On the basis of what is taken as object, resistance-contact and designation-contact are established; on the basis of obstruction and its antidote, illumination-contact and non-illumination-contact are established; on the basis of intrinsic nature, neither-illumination-nor-non-illumination-contact is established; on the basis of opposition and accord, craving-contact and hatred-contact are established; on the basis of association, pleasant-feeling-contact, painful-feeling-contact, and neutral-feeling-contact are established; and on the basis of what it depends upon, eye-contact through mind-contact are established.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the very same subject matter, contact, can be truthfully described using anywhere from one category to limitless ones depending purely on which classificatory principle is applied is a clear statement of this tradition's own working assumption: a number is never simply given by the world itself, but is always relative to whatever specific analytical lens one chooses to look through it with.

4. Resistance-Contact and Designation-Contact: Two Contacts Sorted by Their Object

The individual definitions begin with the first classificatory pair — resistance-contact, associated with the five sense-consciousnesses and confined to resistant objects, and designation-contact, associated with mental consciousness alone, whose very name is explained through no fewer than three different etymologies, each locating its own special "amplification of language" in a different place: its intrinsic nature, its object, or simply the fact that it takes names themselves as object.

Chinese Source

云何有對觸。答五識身相應觸。問何故此觸名有對答以有對法為所緣故。問增語觸亦以有對法為所緣。何故此觸獨名有對。答此觸以初故得名增語觸。更以餘緣建立。有說。此觸但以有對法為所緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

"What is resistance-contact?" Answer: contact associated with the five sense-consciousness bodies. One might ask: why is this contact named "resistance"? Answer: because it takes resistant dharmas as its object. One might then ask: designation-contact likewise takes resistant dharmas as its object — why, then, is this particular contact alone named "resistance"? Answer: this contact receives the name first, since it comes first; the other is established through a different consideration entirely. Another position holds that this contact takes only resistant dharmas as its object, whereas

增語觸亦緣餘法。有說。此觸所依所緣皆是有對。增語觸所緣雖或有對所依不爾故別立名。云何增語觸。答意識身相應觸問何故此觸名增語。答由此觸自性語增故名增語。問云何此觸自性語增。答有對觸唯欲色界繫。此觸通三界繫及不繫。又有對觸唯欲界。初靜慮地可得。此觸一切地可得。又有對觸唯有漏。此觸通有漏無漏。由此等故自性語增。有說。此觸所緣語增故名增語。問云何此觸所緣語增。答有對觸唯以有色法為所緣。此觸通緣有色無色。又有對觸但以有對法為所緣。此觸通緣有對無對。又有對觸但以有漏法為所緣。此觸通緣有漏無漏。又有對觸但以有為法為所緣此觸通緣有為無為。由此等故所緣語增。有說。增語者。謂名此觸緣名故名增語。雖亦緣義而非不共故。隨不共立名依別立通名。如苦集智等。

designation-contact also takes non-resistant dharmas as its object. Another position holds that this contact's basis and object are both resistant, whereas designation-contact, though its object may sometimes be resistant, does not have a resistant basis — hence a separate name is established for it. "What is designation-contact?" Answer: contact associated with the mental-consciousness body. One might ask: why is this contact named "designation"? Answer: because, through this contact, language itself is amplified — hence it is called "designation." One might then ask: how, exactly, does language become amplified through this contact's own intrinsic nature? Answer: resistance-contact is bound only to the desire and form realms, whereas this contact pervades all three realms and the unbound alike; moreover, resistance-contact is obtainable only in the desire realm and the first dhyāna, whereas this contact is obtainable at every ground; moreover, resistance-contact is only contaminated, whereas this contact pervades both the contaminated and the uncontaminated — and it is for reasons such as these that its own intrinsic nature amplifies language. Another position holds that it is this contact's own object that amplifies language. One might then ask: how, exactly, does language become amplified through this contact's own object? Answer: resistance-contact takes only material dharmas as its object, whereas this contact takes both material and immaterial dharmas as its object; moreover, resistance-contact takes only resistant dharmas as its object, whereas this contact takes both resistant and non-resistant dharmas as its object; moreover, resistance-contact takes only contaminated dharmas as its object, whereas this contact takes both contaminated and uncontaminated dharmas as its object; moreover, resistance-contact takes only conditioned dharmas as its object, whereas this contact takes both conditioned and unconditioned dharmas as its object — and it is for reasons such as these that its own object amplifies language. Another position holds that "designation" means that this contact takes names themselves as its object, and is therefore called "designation" — for although it may also take meanings as its object, this is not what is distinctive about it; a name is given on the basis of what is distinctive, while the general term applies more broadly, exactly as with the knowledge of suffering, the knowledge of origin, and the like.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The final etymology offered for "designation-contact" — that it is named for taking names as its object, even though it may also take meanings as object, since a name is properly given on the basis of what is distinctive rather than what is merely also present — is a small but genuinely useful piece of naming theory: it explains why a term can be accurate without being exhaustive, capturing only whatever feature actually sets its referent apart from its neighbors.

5. Illumination, Non-Illumination, Craving, Hatred, and the Three Feeling-Contacts

The definitions continue through the pair sorted by obstruction and its antidote — illumination-contact, associated exclusively with the three uncontaminated faculties, and non-illumination-contact, associated with every affliction — followed by the single intrinsic-nature category left over once both illumination and defilement are excluded, and then the pairs sorted by opposition, accord, and association: craving-contact, hatred-contact, and the three feeling-contacts.

Chinese Source

云何明觸。答無漏觸即三無漏根相應觸。云何無明觸。答染污觸即一切煩惱隨煩惱相應觸。云何非明非無明觸。答不染有漏觸。此中問答各有二遮。謂非明者遮明觸。非無明者遮無明觸。不染者遮染污觸。有漏者遮無漏觸。由此遮故此體唯攝一切有漏善無覆無記觸。云何愛觸。答貪相應觸。即三界五部所斷六識身俱貪相應觸。

Ru-Yi Translation

"What is illumination-contact?" Answer: uncontaminated contact — namely, contact associated with the three uncontaminated faculties. "What is non-illumination-contact?" Answer: defiled contact — namely, contact associated with every affliction and secondary affliction. "What is neither-illumination-nor-non-illumination-contact?" Answer: undefiled contaminated contact. In this passage, each question-and-answer pair carries two exclusions: "neither illumination" excludes illumination-contact; "nor non-illumination" excludes non-illumination-contact; "undefiled" excludes defiled contact; "contaminated" excludes uncontaminated contact. Through these exclusions, this category's own substance comes to comprise solely every contaminated, wholesome or unobscured-neutral form of contact. "What is craving-contact?" Answer: contact associated with craving — namely, contact co-arisen with craving found among the six consciousness-bodies, cut off across all three realms and all five categories of cutting-off.

云何恚觸。答瞋相應觸。即五所斷六識身俱瞋相應觸。云何順樂受觸。答樂受相應觸。即樂喜根相應觸。云何順苦受觸。答苦受相應觸。即苦憂根相應觸。云何順不苦不樂受觸。答不苦不樂受相應觸。即捨根相應觸。云何眼觸。答眼識身相應觸。乃至云何意觸。

"What is hatred-contact?" Answer: contact associated with hatred — namely, contact co-arisen with hatred found among the six consciousness-bodies, cut off across the five categories of cutting-off. "What is pleasant-feeling-contact?" Answer: contact associated with pleasant feeling — namely, contact associated with the pleasure- and joy-faculties. "What is painful-feeling-contact?" Answer: contact associated with painful feeling — namely, contact associated with the pain- and displeasure-faculties. "What is neutral-feeling-contact?" Answer: contact associated with neither-pleasant-nor-painful feeling — namely, contact associated with the equanimity-faculty. "What is eye-contact?" Answer: contact associated with the eye-consciousness body. And so on, up through "what is mind-contact?"

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The precise double-exclusion logic defining "neither-illumination-nor-non-illumination-contact" — neither uncontaminated nor defiled, leaving only what is undefiled yet still contaminated — shows this tradition's fondness for defining a middle category not by any positive feature of its own, but purely by process of elimination from the two extremes flanking it on either side.

6. The Six Sense-Contacts, and a Sprawling Census of Which Contact Includes Which

Answer: it is not composed of sinew, bone, blood, or flesh at all, but is the pure, subtle matter produced by the form realm's own great elements — capable of unobstructed seeing, yet its own substance remains invisible; included among the eye-element, the eye-base, and the eye-faculty — this is what is called the divine eye. Having shown its own intrinsic nature, the passage now goes on to explain its name. One might ask: for what reason is it called the "divine" eye? Answer: because this eye is exceptionally excellent, it is called "divine" — the world, in speaking of anything superlative, tends to use the term "divine," exactly as one speaks of "divine" robes, "divine" ornaments, or "divine" food and drink, in every case using "divine" simply to name something exceptional; the same holds true here as well.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The observation that "divine" here functions the same way it does in everyday speech about "divine" robes or "divine" food — as an intensifier marking excellence, not a literal claim about celestial manufacture — is a useful piece of demystification: the divine eye's own name turns out to be far less mysterious, and far more linguistically ordinary, than its exotic reputation might suggest.

17. Where the Divine Eye Manifests, and the Puzzle of Seeing With a Body Too Small to See

The fascicle closes with a rich, almost comic thought-experiment: the divine eye always manifests symmetrically in both eye-sockets at once, never favoring one side over the other, and never appearing blind, missing, or dazzled — but if a desire-realm being conjures a transformation-body sixteen thousand yojanas tall, so vast that an ordinary human body could not even span the width of one of its toes, from what location does the divine eye actually observe forms at that colossal scale? Four different, genuinely creative solutions are offered in turn.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for its own realm, it is bound to the form realm; as for its own ground, it is found within the four dhyānas proper, never within their proximate-access levels nor within the formless attainments. Why is this so? Because wherever a ground possesses the mastery-concentration on which supernormal penetration depends, that ground can support the divine eye; the proximate-access levels and the formless attainments possess no such mastery-concentration, and it is for this reason that no ground among them possesses the divine eye. One might ask: why do the proximate-access levels and the formless attainments possess no such mastery-concentration on which supernormal penetration depends? Answer: because their calming and their insight are not evenly balanced there; because they are not accomplished through either the four-factor or the five-factor structure; and because they are not classed among the grounds leading toward joy. One might ask: if a being born in the desire realm cultivates and obtains the divine eye, at what location does it manifest when it becomes present? Answer: it manifests precisely at the location of the naturally born eye-faculty. One might then ask: if the naturally born eye-faculty has already been destroyed, at what location does the divine eye then manifest?

答即於曾有眼根處起。問若彼處所合為一段。不可知者復何處起。

Answer: it manifests precisely at the location where the eye-faculty once existed. One might then ask: if that very location can no longer be identified as any single, fixed spot, at what location does it then manifest?

答即於應有眼根處起。問諸起天眼現在前者。為有左起右不起耶。

Answer: it manifests precisely at the location where the eye-faculty ought to exist. One might then ask: among those for whom the divine eye becomes manifest and present, is there ever a case where it becomes manifest on the left side but not the right,

右起左不起耶。左劣右中耶。左中右上耶。右劣左中耶。右中左上耶。答不如是起謂起天眼者必二處眼俱起。等劣等中等上。一切天眼無瞎無闕。亦無眩亂及彼同分。問若生欲界化作色究竟萬六千踰繕那身。天眼現前觀彼色時。人身長三肘半。或四肘尚不遍彼足指。為住何處觀彼色耶。為上為下。有說。以神境通住上而見。如營舍人處上觀下。如是居上觀下眾色。有說。住下而見如行像者處下觀上。如是居下觀上眾色。有說。彼時以神境智證通。延廣此身令遍所化萬六千踰繕那身量而觀眾色。有說。欲界亦有萬六千踰繕那身。應與所化色究竟身俱生。若時化作色究竟身。爾時欲界三肘半或四肘身便滅。彼萬六千踰繕那身續起。即依常眼處所而觀眾色。如是說者。如彼生處異熟身量化身亦爾。如從色界來欲界時。

or manifest on the right but not the left, or inferior on the left and middling on the right, or middling on the left and superior on the right, or inferior on the right and middling on the left, or middling on the right and superior on the left? Answer: no such thing occurs — whenever the divine eye manifests, it invariably manifests in both locations at once, equally inferior, equally middling, or equally superior in both; every divine eye is entirely free of blindness, of any missing part, of dazzled confusion, and of anything belonging to that same unfortunate class. One might ask: if a being born in the desire realm conjures up, through transformation, a body reaching the very Akaniṣṭha heaven's own height of sixteen thousand yojanas, and the divine eye then manifests to observe forms at that scale, given that a human body stands only three and a half or four cubits tall and could not even span the width of a single toe on such a transformed body, from what location does it observe those forms — from above, or from below? One position holds that one abides above by means of one's own supernormal locomotive power and observes downward from there, exactly as someone standing above a building looks down upon what lies below, observing the many forms below in just this way while abiding above. Another position holds that one abides below and observes from there, exactly as someone walking beside a great statue looks upward from below, observing the many forms above in just this way while abiding below. Another position holds that, at that very moment, by means of the realization attained through supernormal locomotive power, one's own body is stretched and expanded until it fully matches the scale of the transformed body reaching sixteen thousand yojanas, and it is from within that vastly expanded body that the many forms are then observed. Another position holds that the desire realm, too, already possesses a sixteen-thousand-yojana-scale body, which must co-arise together with whatever Akaniṣṭha-scale form is being conjured through transformation — so that, at the very moment the transformed Akaniṣṭha body is conjured, the desire realm's own three-and-a-half or four-cubit body simply ceases, and that sixteen-thousand-yojana body arises to continue in its place, observing the many forms from precisely the ordinary location of the eye-base as it always has. On this last account, exactly as the ripening-produced body's own scale differs according to wherever a being happens to be born, so too does the transformed body's own scale — exactly as, when coming from the form realm down into the desire realm,

化作化身還如欲界異熟身量。此作彼身當知亦爾。隨彼所住觀見眾色。

one's own conjured transformation-body still matches the scale of one's own desire-realm ripening-produced body; the same holds, mutatis mutandis, for this transformation working in the opposite direction as well — one should understand this to work in just the same way, observing the many forms according to whatever scale of body one happens, in each case, to be abiding within.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The four competing solutions to the giant-body puzzle — perching above and looking down, standing below and looking up, stretching one's own body to match the transformation's scale, or positing a hidden sixteen-thousand-yojana body already latent within the desire realm itself, ready to replace the ordinary one the instant it is needed — are a wonderfully concrete illustration of this tradition's willingness to let genuine cosmological imagination run alongside its driest technical censuses, treating even a seemingly whimsical scale-mismatch as a problem worth four serious, structurally different answers rather than a single tidy one.
