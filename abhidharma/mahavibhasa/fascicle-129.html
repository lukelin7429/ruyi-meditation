The Bird That Cannot Find the Sky's Edge

Fascicle 129 tells the beloved story of the monk Aśvajit's quest through every heaven to learn where the four great elements ultimately cease, the Buddha's reframing of the question through the parable of a bird unable to find the edge of the sky, a precise map of which absorption eliminates which physical and mental factor, and a rich closing account of the four nutriments. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Aśvajit's Quest: Ascending Through Every Heaven in Search of an Answer

Fascicle 129 opens with one of the treatise's most memorable narratives. The monk Aśvajit, wondering where the four great elements ultimately cease, uses his meditative power to travel upward through heaven after heaven — the Four Great Kings, the Thirty-Three, Yāma, Tuṣita, Nirmāṇarati, Paranirmita-vaśavartin, and finally the Brahma world itself — each divine assembly in turn deferring the question to whatever authority stands above it, until even Mahābrahmā, cornered and unable to answer, resorts to grandiose evasion before finally admitting, in private, that he does not know either.

Chinese Source

大種蘊第五中大造納息第一之三諸四大種依何定滅。乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答欲顯諸佛出現世間有大功德故。如施設論說。贍部洲邊繞大海際。有轉輪王路廣一踰繕那。諸轉輪王若不出世水所覆沒無能遊履。若出世時海水周減一踰繕那。此路乃現平飾清淨底布金砂。栴檀香水自然灑潤。輪王每欲巡此洲時。導從四軍而遊此路。如是諸佛未出世時。無有能依諸根本地斷煩惱者。若佛十力大法輪王出世間時。根本地現平等清淨。布覺分砂灑戒定水。佛與無數那庾多眷屬。依之趣入無畏涅槃宮。問此中云何顯佛出世有大功德。答佛不出世此問尚無況有能答。如佛昔在室羅筏城住誓多林。時有苾芻名曰馬勝。是阿羅漢。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth Aggregate, the Great Elements: the Great-Elements-and-Derivatives Nyāsa, Chapter One, Part Three. On what absorption does the extinction of the four great elements depend? And so on in full. Question: why compose this treatise? Answer: in order to display the great merit of the buddhas' appearance in the world. As the Prajñaptiśāstra says: at the edge of Jambudvīpa, ringing the great ocean, there is a road for a wheel-turning king, one yojana wide — and, if no wheel-turning king appears in the world, this road remains submerged beneath the water, so that none can travel it; but when such a king does appear, the ocean recedes on every side by a full yojana, and this road then appears, smooth and pure, its bed strewn with golden sand, sprinkled of its own accord with sandalwood-scented water — and, whenever the wheel-turning king wishes to make his circuit of this continent, he travels this road escorted by his four armies. Just so, before any buddha appears in the world, there is no one capable of cutting off the afflictions by relying on the fundamental grounds of absorption; but when a buddha, the great Dharma-wheel-turning king endowed with the ten powers, appears in the world, these fundamental grounds appear level and pure, strewn with the sand of the factors of awakening, sprinkled with the water of moral discipline and concentration — and the Buddha, together with countless myriads of followers, relies upon them to advance into the fearless palace of nirvāṇa. Question: how does this passage display the great merit of a buddha's appearing in the world? Answer: without a buddha's appearance, this very question could not even be asked, let alone answered. As, once, when the Buddha was dwelling at Jeta's Grove near the city of Śrāvastī, there was a bhikṣu named Aśvajit, an arhat,

獨於靜室作是思惟。諸四大種何處永滅。為欲知故入勝等持。即以定心於誓多林沒。欻然出在四大王眾天。從定而起問彼天眾。諸四大種何處永滅。天眾答曰。我等不知是四大種何處永滅。然我所事四大天王。智慧威德並皆殊勝。彼或能了可往問之。尊者即時詣四王所。作如上問皆曰不知。復共仰推三十三天眾。三十三天眾復。

— who, alone in a quiet cell, reflected to himself: where do the four great elements ultimately cease? Wishing to know this, he entered a superior state of absorption, and, with his mind thus concentrated, vanished from Jeta's Grove, suddenly reappearing in the Heaven of the Four Great Kings. Rising from his absorption, he asked the divine assembly there: where do the four great elements ultimately cease? The assembly replied: we do not know where the four great elements ultimately cease — but the Four Great Kings whom we serve are surpassing in wisdom and majestic virtue; perhaps they can answer this, and you should go and ask them. The venerable one then went at once to the Four Kings and asked the same question, and they, too, said they did not know, but together deferred upward to the assembly of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three, who in turn

推帝釋。帝釋仰推夜摩天眾。夜摩天眾推蘇夜摩天子。蘇夜摩天子推覩史多天眾。覩史多天眾推珊覩史多天子。珊覩史多天子。推樂變化天眾。樂變化天眾推妙變化天子。妙變化天子推他化自在天眾。他化自在天眾推妙自在天子。妙自在天子推梵天眾。如是尊者遍問欲天竟。不能知大種滅處。欲往梵世入勝等持。復以定心自在宮沒。梵眾天出。從定而起作如上問。梵眾咸曰。我等不知有大梵王。是梵大梵作者化者。為一切父自在生育。具大威德無與等者。

— deferred to Śakra; Śakra, in turn, deferred upward to the assembly of the Yāma heaven; the assembly of the Yāma heaven deferred to the divine son Suyāma; Suyāma deferred to the assembly of Tuṣita heaven; the assembly of Tuṣita heaven deferred to the divine son Santuṣita; Santuṣita deferred to the assembly of the Nirmāṇarati heaven; the assembly of the Nirmāṇarati heaven deferred to the divine son Sunirmita; Sunirmita deferred to the assembly of the Paranirmita-vaśavartin heaven; the assembly of the Paranirmita-vaśavartin heaven deferred to the divine son Vaśavartin; and Vaśavartin, in turn, deferred to the assembly of the Brahma heaven. Thus, having questioned every heaven of the desire realm in turn, the venerable one was still unable to learn where the great elements cease, and, wishing to go to the Brahma world, once more entered a superior state of absorption, and, with his mind thus concentrated, vanished from the Heaven of Mastery over Others' Emanations, reappearing among the assembly of Brahma's own retinue. Rising from his absorption, he asked the same question as before, and the assembly of Brahmā's retinue all replied: we do not know, but there is a Great Brahmā King — this Brahmā, the Great Brahmā, the maker, the creator, the father of all beings, self-existent and self-generating, endowed with great majestic virtue, having no equal,

無有不見不了不識。彼定能知仁應往問。尊者即問大梵所在。梵眾答曰。我亦不知大梵天王定所在處。仁欲見者隨處諦求。即有光明於中而現。尊者馬勝遂發誠心。願大梵王於此眾現。應時大梵即放光明。便自化身為童子像。首分五頂形貌端嚴。在梵眾中隨光而現。尊者前進問曰。大仙諸四大種何處永滅。梵王不達作矯亂言。

— there being nothing he does not see, does not comprehend, does not know — he alone can surely answer this, and you should go and ask him. The venerable one at once asked where the Great Brahmā might be found, and the assembly of Brahmā's retinue replied: we, too, do not know exactly where the Great Brahmā King dwells — but if you sincerely seek him, wherever you search, a light will appear there. The Venerable Aśvajit accordingly gave rise to a sincere wish that the Great Brahmā King would manifest himself before this assembly — and, at that very moment, the Great Brahmā emitted a radiant light, and transformed himself into the likeness of a youth, his head crowned with five peaks, his form dignified and fine, appearing amid the assembly of Brahmā, following the light. The venerable one stepped forward and asked: Great Sage, where do the four great elements ultimately cease? Brahmā, unable to answer, spoke evasive and deceptive words,

苾芻當知我是大梵。是自在者作者。化者。生者養者為一切父。此是諂誑所發語業。尊者告曰。我不問仁梵非梵等。但問大種何處永滅。爾時大梵知此苾芻非矯亂言卒能酬遣。便執兩手引出眾外。此是諂誑所發身業。出眾外已謝尊者言。我實不知大種滅處。然諸梵眾咸謂我是自在作者無不知見。若我眾中云不知者。是諸梵眾便見輕蔑。尊者自失近捨如來。遠勞見問致無所獲。今可速還詣佛請問。如佛所說應正受持。馬勝既聞梵王推佛。歡喜辭退復入等持。

— saying, "Bhikṣu, you should know that I am the Great Brahmā, the sovereign, the maker, the creator, the begetter, the nurturer, the father of all" — this being deceptive verbal karma born of flattery. The venerable one told him, "I did not ask whether you are Brahmā or not — I ask only where the great elements ultimately cease." At this, the Great Brahmā, realizing that this bhikṣu could not be put off by evasive words, took him by both hands and drew him outside the assembly — this being deceptive bodily karma born of flattery. Once outside the assembly, he apologized to the venerable one, saying, "I truly do not know where the great elements cease. Yet my entire retinue believes me to be sovereign and all-creating, with nothing beyond my knowledge or sight — and, were I to admit before my own assembly that I do not know, they would come to hold me in contempt. You yourself have erred, in setting aside the Tathāgata nearby only to travel far and trouble me with this question, gaining nothing for it. You should now quickly return and ask the Buddha instead, and receive and uphold whatever he teaches." Having heard Brahmā defer to the Buddha, Aśvajit, delighted, took his leave and once more entered absorption,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Brahmā's private confession — that he cannot admit ignorance before his own worshipful retinue without losing their reverence — is a remarkably candid piece of religious satire: even a figure claiming to be the self-existent creator of all things is shown to be more concerned with maintaining his followers' image of him than with truth itself, and it takes a single persistent question from an arhat to expose the gap between a god's public performance and his private uncertainty.

2. The Buddha's Answer: The Bird That Cannot Find the Edge of the Sky

Returning empty-handed, Aśvajit receives from the Buddha not an answer but a correction: his question was never properly framed to begin with, no more answerable than a bird's futile search for the edge of the sky. The Buddha reframes the question in verse — where are the four elements, in all their qualities, utterly abandoned, and name-and-form ceases without remainder? — and answers with an equally poetic verse describing a boundless, markless consciousness surpassing everything else in vastness, in which all such elements and their derivatives are finally, wholly given up.

Chinese Source

即以定心於梵世沒。欻然還出誓多林中。從定而起整理衣服。往世尊前恭敬作禮。問四大種何處永滅。爾時世尊為說不見邊際鳥喻云。汝亦然乃至梵宮遍請所問。不得邊際還至此中。猶如彼鳥不得邊際。然汝所問不合問儀。隨此而答亦乖答理。汝欲問者。當如是問。

Ru-Yi Translation

— and, with his mind thus concentrated, vanished from the Brahma world, suddenly reappearing once more within Jeta's Grove. Rising from his absorption, he straightened his robes and, approaching the World-Honored One, bowed in reverence and asked where the four great elements ultimately cease. The World-Honored One then told him the parable of a bird unable to find the edge of the sky, saying: "You are just the same — having traveled as far as Brahmā's own palace, questioning along the way, you have found no boundary, and have returned here just as that bird, finding no edge, must also return. Yet your question was not properly framed to begin with, and any answer following it would likewise depart from the proper form of an answer. If you wish to ask this, you should ask it in this way:

四大與短長　細麁淨不淨於何處永棄　名色滅無餘此問隨順應如是答。

"'The four great elements, together with the long and the short, The fine and the coarse, the pure and the impure — Where are they utterly abandoned, Where do name and form cease without remainder?' Asked in accordance with this form, the question should be answered in this way:

識不見無邊　周遍廣大性更無餘廣大　能映奪此者四大與短長　細麁淨不淨於是處永棄　名色滅無餘有說。此中佛說聖道。世尊於此說識聲故。有說。此中佛說涅槃。

"'Consciousness, without mark, boundless, All-pervading, vast in its own nature, Surpassed by nothing else more vast, By which this alone is outshone and overcome — It is here that the four great elements, together with the long and the short, The fine and the coarse, the pure and the impure, are utterly abandoned, Here that name and form cease without remainder.'" Some say that here the Buddha is speaking of the noble path, since the World-Honored One here uses the term "consciousness." Others say that here the Buddha is speaking of nirvāṇa itself,

以說無邊識不見故。若無佛出世說正法者則雖梵王亦多愚惑。若佛出世宣說正法則八歲勤策亦能解了。設有來問長老知耶。諸四大種何處永滅。彼若誦持大種蘊者。即言依四定或依未至滅。世若無佛此等便無故。佛出世有大功德。

— since it speaks of a boundless, markless consciousness. If no buddha had appeared in the world to teach the true Dharma, even the Great Brahmā King himself would remain thoroughly confused on this matter — but, because a buddha did appear and proclaim the true Dharma, even an eight-year-old novice monk can now readily understand it. Were someone to come and ask, "Venerable elder, do you know where the four great elements ultimately cease?", any novice who has merely memorized the Great Elements Aggregate could answer at once, "They cease based on the four absorptions, or on the preparatory stage" — and, were there no buddha in the world, even this much would not exist. Hence a buddha's appearance in the world carries great merit indeed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The Buddha's reframing does something subtler than merely answering a mystical riddle: it exposes that Aśvajit's original question already assumed the great elements were a kind of substance located somewhere in space, waiting to be found — and only once that assumption is corrected, replacing "where" with a question about consciousness and cessation, does an answer even become possible, making the entire episode as much a lesson in how to ask a question well as in what the answer itself contains.

3. Two Masters Disagree: Does 'Without Remainder' Mean Only the Noble Path?

A sharp scholarly dispute follows between two named masters, Timira and Ghoṣavarman, over how strictly to read the treatise's own claim that the elements are extinguished "without remainder": Timira insists this can only mean permanent extinction accomplished by noble ones through the noble path specifically, since the base sūtra speaks only of the seven fundamental grounds of absorption; Ghoṣavarman argues for a more inclusive reading covering both noble and mundane paths, both noble ones and ordinary beings — a reading the treatise's own verdict favors, reconciled against the narrower sūtra through the classic image of the Abhidharma as a mirror and lamp illuminating what the sūtras leave only partly stated.

Chinese Source

諸四大種及所造色依何定滅。答依四定或依未至滅定。或名道或名行迹。或名對治。或名作意。義無差別。滅或名斷。或名離染。或名為盡。或名離繫。義亦無別。昔於此部有二論師。一名時毘羅。

Ru-Yi Translation

On what absorption do the four great elements and derived form depend for their extinction? Answer: on the four absorptions, or on the preparatory stage. "Absorption" may also be called the path, the track, the antidote, or attention — these names carry no difference in meaning; "extinction" may also be called cutting-off, freedom from defilement, exhaustion, or freedom from bondage — these too carry no difference in meaning. In the past, within this school, there were two treatise-masters — one named Timira,

二名瞿沙伐摩。尊者時毘羅作如是說。此中但說永斷無餘斷。無隨縛斷。無少分斷。無影像斷。說聖者斷非異生斷。說聖道作用。非世俗道作用。所以者何。依七依經以立斯論。彼經但說根本地故。

— and the other named Ghoṣavarman. The Venerable Timira held that this passage speaks only of permanent extinction, extinction without remainder, never partial extinction, fractional extinction, or merely representational extinction; that it speaks of extinction accomplished by noble ones, not by ordinary beings; and that it speaks of the function of the noble path specifically, not of the mundane path — for why is this? Because this treatise is established on the basis of the Sūtra of the Seven Dependencies, and that sūtra speaks only of the fundamental grounds of absorption,

無有異生。或諸聖者依根本地起世俗道能離染故。尊者瞿沙伐摩作如是說。此中但說永斷無餘斷。無隨縛斷。無少分斷。無影像斷。

— with no mention of an ordinary being, or even a noble one, giving rise to a mundane path based on the fundamental grounds in order to become free of defilement. The Venerable Ghoṣavarman likewise held that this passage speaks only of permanent extinction, extinction without remainder, never partial extinction, fractional extinction, or merely representational extinction,

此說應理。餘則不然。所以者何。此中通說諸聖者斷。及異生斷。

— yet he held that this passage speaks of extinction by both noble ones and ordinary beings alike. This account accords with reason; the other does not, for why is this? Because this passage speaks generally of extinction accomplished by noble ones as well as by ordinary beings,

聖道作用。及世俗道作用故。問依七依經以立此論。彼經唯說諸根本地。無有異生。或諸聖者依根本地起世俗道。能離諸染云何說通。答由此故言阿毘達磨是諸經鏡燈焰光明。諸契經中未宣說者此中宣說。未示現者此中示現。經說有餘。此中無餘。經說有異意。

— and of the function of both the noble path and the mundane path together. Question: this treatise is established on the basis of the Sūtra of the Seven Dependencies, and that sūtra speaks only of the fundamental grounds, with no mention of an ordinary being, or even a noble one, giving rise to a mundane path based on the fundamental grounds to become free of defilement — how, then, is this to be reconciled? Answer: it is on this very account that the Abhidharma is said to be the mirror, lamp, flame, and light of the sūtras — what the sūtras have not proclaimed, this treatise proclaims; what they have not made manifest, this treatise makes manifest. Where the sūtra leaves something unstated, this treatise leaves nothing unstated; where the sūtra carries a different intent,

此中無異意。阿毘達磨中言多盡理。由此經論二說善通。

— this treatise carries no different intent, but simply states matters more exhaustively within the Abhidharma — and it is on this account that the two accounts, of the sūtra and of this treatise, are both properly reconciled.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Invoking the Abhidharma's own authority to explain away an apparent narrowing in the base sūtra is a bold interpretive move: rather than reading the sūtra's silence about ordinary beings and the mundane path as a deliberate restriction, the treatise treats that silence as simply incomplete, trusting its own more exhaustive analysis to fill in what the earlier, more compressed teaching left unstated.

4. Precisely Which Absorption Extinguishes the Elements at Each Level

A detailed technical map follows, specifying exactly which absorption is needed to finally eliminate the great elements and derived form bound to each realm — desire-realm elements yield to the mere preparatory stage of the first dhyāna, while elements bound to progressively higher dhyānas require progressively deeper absorption, with a careful distinction throughout between eliminations achievable only through the noble path (in the fundamental levels themselves) and those achievable through either the noble or the merely mundane path (in the preparatory, access-level stages that lack a properly noble path of their own).

Chinese Source

大種造色依四定滅者。謂依四靜慮依未至滅者。謂依初靜慮近分。

Ru-Yi Translation

As to the great elements and derived form being extinguished based on the four absorptions: this means based on the four dhyānas. As to being extinguished based on the preparatory stage: this means based on the preparatory stage of the first dhyāna,

及靜慮中間空無邊處近分。此中靜慮無色近分。靜慮中間皆名未至。並未能入勝根本地。而能現前斷煩惱故。問契經唯說根本名。

— together with the intermediate dhyāna and the preparatory stage of the sphere of infinite space. Here, the preparatory stages of the dhyānas and formless attainments, together with the intermediate dhyāna, are all called "the not-yet-attained," since, though not yet having entered the superior, fundamental ground itself, they are nevertheless capable of presently cutting off affliction. Question: the sūtra speaks only of the name "fundamental" —

依何故此中說依未至。有作是說。此中應言依四定或未至滅。不應言依未至。而言依者有何意耶。此文再說根本地故。依四定者說四依定。依未至者謂舉諸依。顯諸未至。非即說此未至為依。如說入城未入城耶。雖再說城言無別城事。彼亦如是。有餘師說。此中依言總說諸定。非但根本。皆能與道作所依故。然七依定就勝而說大種造色五地所繫。謂欲界四靜慮。是故離第四靜慮染時。乃究竟斷。然離彼染或依初靜慮近分。或依四靜慮。或依靜慮中間。或依空無邊處近分。若依空無邊處近分。離彼染時通。聖者及異生。唯世俗道非聖道。若依餘地離彼染時。唯聖者非異生。唯聖道非世俗道。此中說究竟所滅大種造色。謂第四靜慮所繫故。言依四定或依未至滅。若欲界所繫大種造色。但應言依未至滅。依初靜慮近分滅故。初靜慮所繫大種造色。應言依初定或依未至滅。依初靜慮靜慮中間。及前二靜慮近分滅故。第二靜慮所繫大種造色。應言依二定。或依未至滅。依前二靜慮靜慮中間。初及第三靜慮近分滅故。

— on what basis, then, does this treatise here speak of being based on "the not-yet-attained"? Some say: this passage ought to say "based on the four absorptions, or on what has not yet been attained," and ought not to say merely "based on the not-yet-attained" — but, since it does say "based on," what is its intent? It is simply that this passage speaks twice of the fundamental ground: "based on the four absorptions" names the four dependent absorptions, while "based on the not-yet-attained" merely raises the various dependencies in order to display the various not-yet-attained stages, not to state that this "not-yet-attained" itself serves as the basis — just as one might say, "entering the city, or not yet entering the city," where the word "city" is spoken twice without there being two distinct cities meant; it is the same here. Other masters say: the word "based on" here refers to absorption in general, not only the fundamental grounds, since every one of them is capable of serving as the basis for the path. Yet the seven dependent absorptions are spoken of here with reference to what is superior — the great elements and derived form are bound to five grounds, namely the desire realm and the four dhyānas, and hence it is only upon becoming free of the fourth dhyāna's defilement that their extinction is finally complete. 9. A Dispute Over What the Question Is Actually Asking

A final subtlety in this cluster distinguishes two different questions that could easily be mistaken for one another: which fruit a person presently abides in, once a given elimination is complete, versus which fruit's own technical classification that elimination itself belongs to. The treatise's verdict insists the former is what is actually being asked throughout — a person who becomes free of the form realm's defilement and later attains non-returning is never described as having eliminated the great elements "under the classification of" that fruit, since non-returning is defined narrowly as covering only desire-realm cutting-off specifically.

10. The Four Nutriments, and Whether Nourishment Ever Truly Transforms Anything

A substantial new topic closes the fascicle: the sūtra's account of the four nutriments — morsel-food, contact, volition, and consciousness — each nourishing the faculties and increasing the great elements. A subtle metaphysical puzzle then asks whether nourishment happens to an already-nourished dharma or a not-yet-nourished one, resolved by denying both options: rather, both possibilities already exist, dormant, within the future, and the nourishing condition simply causes the not-yet-nourished dharma to cease and the nourished one to arise in its place — nourishment as substitution, never literal transformation.

11. Nourishing Without Being Nutriment: Why the Undefiled Never Qualifies

A rich set of counter-examples distinguishes genuine nutriment from mere nourishing effect: contact, volition, or consciousness belonging to a different realm, a different ground, or — most strikingly — the undefiled kind entirely, can all, as a matter of fact, nourish the faculties and increase the great elements, yet none of these earn the technical name "nutriment." The undefiled specifically is excluded through an extended, almost devotional contrast: nutriment sustains, perpetuates, and entrenches the entire cycle of suffering, craving, and rebirth, while the undefiled diminishes, severs, and dismantles that very cycle — and whatever nourishing effect the undefiled does have is only ever temporary, never capable of sustaining existence to any final completion.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Reducing something as universally familiar as "eating" down to a precise enumeration of thirteen specific real entities, mapped exactly onto the standard elements-sense-fields-aggregates framework, is characteristic of this tradition's insistence that no ordinary human activity, however mundane, escapes the same rigorous analytical treatment given to the most abstract metaphysical categories.

13. Why Called 'Nutriment' at All? And a Closing Threefold Division

The fascicle closes with etymology: these four factors earn the name "nutriment" through their especially strong, proximate capacity to draw forth, continue, sustain, generate, nourish, and increase existence — a judgment several masters agree only the Buddha himself, having fully realized every dharma's true characteristics and functions, was competent to make, since every dharma with outflow could in principle qualify to some lesser degree. A final classificatory note offers nutriment's two basic aspects — drawing forth future existence, and sustaining present existence — alongside an alternative threefold division into karma-nutriment (volition), birth-nutriment (consciousness), and nourishment-nutriment (morsel-food together with contact).

Chinese Source

已說自體。所以今當說。何故名食。食是何義。答牽有義是食義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having explained their essential substance, we should now explain why they are called "nutriment." What is the meaning of "nutriment"? Answer: the sense of drawing forth existence is the sense of nutriment;

續有義。持有義。生有義。養有義。增有義。是食義。此四於有能牽。乃至能增故名為食。問若牽有。乃至增有是食義者。諸有漏法皆應名食。何故但說四耶。脇尊者言。唯佛世尊究竟了達諸法性相。亦知勢用非餘能知。若法有食相作用可立食者。即便立之。無者不立。尊者世友作如是言。此是世尊有餘之說。略說影說有觀待說。佛觀化宜而說法故。尊者妙音說曰。佛知此四牽有續有持有生有養有增有體相勢用。強盛隣近故立為食。餘法不爾故不說食。有餘師言。如是四法極能長養諸界趣生老死世間。令其流轉故立為食。餘則不爾。或有說者。食有二相。一牽引當有令現在前。二任持今有令相續住。有餘師說。食有三種。一者業食。二者生食。三者長養食。業食謂思。生食謂識。長養食者。謂段與觸。

— so too are the senses of continuing existence, sustaining existence, generating existence, nourishing existence, and increasing existence. It is because these four factors are capable of drawing forth, continuing, sustaining, generating, nourishing, and increasing existence that they are called "nutriment." Question: if drawing forth existence, and so on up through increasing existence, is the sense of nutriment, then every dharma with outflow ought to be called nutriment — why, then, is only these four so named? The Venerable Pārśva says: only the Buddha, the World-Honored One, has thoroughly and completely realized the true characteristics of all dharmas, and knows their function as well, a knowledge beyond any other's reach — and, whatever dharma bears the mark and function of nutriment, he establishes as such, and whatever does not, he does not so establish. The Venerable Vasumitra explains it this way: this is one of the World-Honored One's own supplementary teachings, spoken briefly, allusively, and dependent on context, since the Buddha teaches the Dharma with regard to what is fitting for those he converts. The Venerable Ghoṣa explains: the Buddha knows that these four factors' capacity to draw forth, continue, sustain, generate, nourish, and increase existence, in their very essential nature, characteristic, and function, is especially strong and proximate, and hence establishes them as "nutriment" — other dharmas being otherwise, and hence not so called. Other masters say: it is because these four factors are especially capable of sustaining every realm, destiny, birth, old age, and death of the world, causing it to continue turning, that they are established as "nutriment" — other dharmas being otherwise. Others say: nutriment carries, in general, two aspects — first, drawing forth a future existence to make it presently manifest; and second, sustaining a presently-existing existence to keep it continuing. Other masters say: nutriment is of three kinds — karma-nutriment, birth-nutriment, and nourishment-nutriment. Karma-nutriment means volition; birth-nutriment means consciousness; and nourishment-nutriment means morsel-food together with contact.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Attributing the precise boundary of "nutriment" entirely to the Buddha's own unique competence — rather than to any derivable rule available to lesser beings — is a candid admission that this category's own edges are ultimately a matter of authoritative judgment rather than logical necessity: the treatise can explain why these four factors deserve the name once told, but concedes that discovering the boundary in the first place required an insight no ordinary reasoning could have supplied on its own.
