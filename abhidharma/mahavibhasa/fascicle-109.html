The Seven Noble Persons

Fascicle 109 opens the Seven Noble Persons Nyāsa, tracking how much knowledge and concentration each of the seven kinds of noble person has attained, then turns to an extended sequence of associative tetralemmas testing knowledge against the concentrations, faculties, and limbs of enlightenment and of the path. 1. The Seven Noble Persons Nyāsa Opens

Fascicle 109 opens an entirely new nyāsa — the Seven Noble Persons Nyāsa — asking, of the follower through faith, the follower through the dharma, the one resolved through faith, one who has arrived at insight, the bodily witness, the wisdom-liberated, and the both-liberated, how many of the eight kinds of knowledge each has attained. Unlike the earlier Fetters Aggregate, which composed its own treatise in reliance upon only five kinds of persons (since it took affliction as its own gateway, and the wisdom-liberated and both-liberated possess none), this discussion takes knowledge and concentration as its gateway instead, and so extends to all seven. A dense, moment-by-moment tabulation follows, tracking exactly how many of the eight kinds of knowledge each type of person has attained at each successive stage of the path of seeing.

Ru-Yi Translation

Of the follower through faith, up through the both-liberated, how many of the eight kinds of knowledge does each attain, and how many does he not attain? Such chapters as these, together with their own explanatory meaning — having already comprehended them, they ought now to be fully explained in turn. One asks: for what reason is this treatise composed? The answer: it is in order to put a stop to what other schools maintain, and to display the correct principle — that is, there are those who hold that there exists, in genuine truth, no nature of attainment or of non-attainment; in order to put a stop to this same holding, and to display that the nature of attainment, and of non-attainment, is decisively and genuinely real, this treatise is composed. One asks: for what reason does this discussion here, and the Concentration Aggregate afterward, both compose their treatise in reliance upon seven kinds of persons, while the preceding Fetters Aggregate's own Unwholesome Nyāsa composed its treatise in reliance upon solely five kinds of persons? The answer: it is because the treatise's own compiler wished it to be so —

乃至廣說。復次前結蘊中依有結者而作論故。不說後二補特伽羅。

and so on, stated at length. Further, the preceding Fetters Aggregate composed its treatise in reliance upon whoever possesses fetters, and it is thereby that it did not speak of the latter two kinds of persons.

This discussion, and the Concentration Aggregate, compose their treatise in reliance upon whoever possesses knowledge and concentration, and it is thereby that they, too, speak of the latter two kinds. Further, the preceding Fetters Aggregate takes "person" as its chapter-heading, and "affliction" as its gateway, and it is thereby that it did not speak of the latter two kinds; this discussion, and the Concentration Aggregate, take "person" as their chapter-heading, and "knowledge and concentration" as their gateway, and it is thereby that they, too, speak of the latter two kinds — that is, although the wisdom-liberated and the both-liberated are without affliction, they nonetheless possess knowledge and concentration, and it is thereby that all seven kinds of persons are fully stated. The seven kinds of persons are broadly distinguished exactly as within the preceding Fetters Aggregate's own Unwholesome Nyāsa. The answer: as for the follower through faith, with respect to the eight kinds of knowledge, he may attain one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, or eight, since the meaning is not fixed — that is, at the time of the forbearance regarding the dharma-cognition of suffering, whoever is without the knowledge of others' minds attains one; whoever possesses the knowledge of others' minds attains two — if he has not yet become free of the desire realm's own defilement and enters right determinacy, he does not attain the knowledge of others' minds, and is thereby named "without the knowledge of others' minds" — he attains solely a single kind, worldly knowledge; if he has already become free of the desire realm's own defilement and enters right determinacy, he attains the knowledge of others' minds, and is thereby named "possessing the knowledge of others' minds" — he attains the two kinds of knowledge, worldly knowledge and the knowledge of others' minds. At the time of the forbearance of the dharma-cognition of suffering and the forbearance of the subsequent cognition of suffering, whoever is without the knowledge of others' minds attains three; whoever possesses the knowledge of others' minds attains four — within these two mind-moments, if he does not attain the knowledge of others' minds, he attains solely three, namely, dharma-cognition, the knowledge of suffering, and worldly knowledge; if he attains the knowledge of others' minds, he then attains four, namely, the preceding three together with the knowledge of others' minds. What is stated hereafter, regarding the further increase of knowledge, should be understood in accordance with what precedes. As for the follower through the dharma, too, being just the same — exactly as the follower through faith may attain one, up through eight — this is because these two noble ones share the same ground, the same path, the same support, and the same freedom from defilement, differing solely in faculty: the follower through faith is gathered within the dull faculties; the follower through the dharma is gathered within the sharp faculties. As for the one resolved through faith, with respect to the eight kinds of knowledge, he may attain seven or eight — namely, without the knowledge of others' minds, seven; with the knowledge of others' minds, eight — that is, if he has not yet become free of the desire realm's own defilement, he does not attain the knowledge of others' minds, and thereby attains solely seven; if he has already become free of the desire realm's own defilement, he, too, attains the knowledge of others' minds, and thereby attains all eight in full. As for one who has arrived at insight, too, being just the same as the one resolved through faith — he may attain seven, or attain all eight in full.

以此二聖地。等道等所依身等離染亦等。唯根有異准前應知。身證慧解脫俱解脫。於此八智皆成就者。以此三聖皆已離欲染具成就八智故。

These two noble ones, too, share the same ground, the same path, the same support, and the same freedom from defilement, differing solely in faculty, exactly as should be understood in accordance with what precedes. As for the bodily witness, the wisdom-liberated, and the both-liberated, all attaining these eight kinds of knowledge in full: this is because these three noble ones have all already become free of the desire realm's own defilement, and thereby attain all eight kinds of knowledge in full.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The simple reason given for why the earlier Fetters Aggregate stops at five persons while this discussion needs all seven — a difference not in how many kinds of noble person genuinely exist, but in which single gateway a given discussion happens to organize itself around — is a small but important piece of interpretive housekeeping: it explains why the very same seven-fold cast of characters can be treated as five in one context and seven in another without either count actually being wrong.

2. Attainment Across the Three Times

The identical tabulation is now run again, this time distinguishing exactly how many kinds of knowledge each of the seven persons possesses belonging to the past, the future, and the present respectively — since forbearance itself is never knowledge, every moment of forbearance registers as zero kinds of knowledge in the present, while the corresponding kind of knowledge that immediately follows registers at once in the present and is carried forward into the future.

Chinese Source

隨信行乃至俱解脫。於八智成就過去幾未來幾現在幾。問何故作此論。答為止他宗顯正理故。謂或有執過去未來俱非實有。為遮彼執顯彼實有。復次前雖總說成就八智。未依三世分別多少。今欲說之故作斯論。答隨信行於八智。苦法智忍時無他心智過去未來一。現在無有他心智過去未來二。現在無者無他心智有他心智義如前說。

Of the follower through faith, up through the both-liberated, of the eight kinds of knowledge, how many does each attain belonging to the past, how many belonging to the future, and how many belonging to the present? One asks: for what reason is this treatise composed? The answer: it is in order to put a stop to what other schools maintain, and to display the correct principle — that is, there are those who hold that the past and the future are both without any genuine substance whatsoever; in order to put a stop to this same holding, and to display that they genuinely possess substance. Further, although it was already stated above, in general, that eight kinds of knowledge are attained, it had not yet been distinguished, by way of the three times, how many or how few — it is now wished to state this, and it is thereby that this treatise is composed. The answer: as for the follower through faith, with respect to the eight kinds of knowledge: at the time of the forbearance of the dharma-cognition of suffering, whoever is without the knowledge of others' minds attains, belonging to the past and the future, one; belonging to the present, none. Whoever possesses the knowledge of others' minds attains, belonging to the past and the future, two; belonging to the present, none. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The stipulation that an already-ceased attainment is "still attained" unless lost through one of exactly three conditions — attaining a fruit, sharpening the faculties, or retrogression — is a precise piece of technical bookkeeping worth pausing over: attainment, in this system, is not simply a fact about whether something once occurred, but a status that persists by default and can be revoked only through a short, enumerated list of specific events, never through the mere passage of time.

3. How Many Kinds of Knowledge Manifest at Once?

A new topic opens by refuting three rival errors at once: that no two kinds of knowledge could ever manifest together; that direct realization observes all four truths simultaneously, with four kinds of knowledge arising all at once; and that many consciousnesses, and many kinds of knowledge, could co-arise side by side within a single moment. Against all three, the treatise insists that a single moment's single act of knowing can, quite legitimately, be described under two or even three different names at once — as dharma-cognition and the knowledge of suffering together, for instance, one and the same mental event named twice over by virtue of what it counteracts and what aspect it takes.

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: for what reason is this treatise composed? The answer: it is in order to put a stop to what other schools maintain, and to display the correct principle — that is, there are those who hold that the various kinds of knowledge, though each individually capable of manifesting in the present, are incapable of manifesting as two at once; in order to put a stop to this same holding, and to display that a kind of knowledge's manifestation in the present may be one, or two, or even three kinds. There are, further, those who hold that, at the time of entering direct realization, all four truths are observed at once, and all four kinds of knowledge arise all at once; in order to put a stop to this same holding, and to display that, at the time of direct realization, each truth is observed separately, there being no sense in which two truth-specific kinds of knowledge arise together at once, how much less several. There are, further, those who hold that many consciousnesses co-arise, and many kinds of knowledge arise side by side; in order to put a stop to this same holding, and to display that a single sentient being, within a single moment, gives rise to solely a single consciousness, and that the very substance of knowledge is likewise so, it is by virtue of this same cause and condition that this treatise is composed.
