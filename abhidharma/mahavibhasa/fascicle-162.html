A New Nyāsa Opens

Fascicle 162 concludes the Attainment Nyāsa — running continuously since fascicle 157 — with the four immeasurables' inability to sever defilement and a survey of karmic ripening, then opens the Object-Condition Nyāsa with the eightfold taxonomy of meditative attainments and why the desire realm and the peak of existence both lack the noble path. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 162 concludes the Attainment Nyāsa (得納息第一之六) and opens the Object-Condition Nyāsa (緣納息第二之一) within the Concentration Aggregate (定蘊第七). Terms established across fascicles 93–161 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. The Four Immeasurables: What One Contemplates, and a Puzzle About "Entry"

Fascicle 162 opens with the four immeasurables — loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity — each defined by its own contemplation, then raises a subtle grammatical puzzle: does "entering" the concentration of loving-kindness describe the aspect of the immediately preceding mental state, or the aspect loving-kindness itself takes once manifest? The resolution draws on a delightful catalogue of scriptural parallels where a word for something already complete is used, by convention, to describe what led up to it — asking a king "where have you come from" after he has already arrived, or speaking of "experiencing" a feeling that, by the time it is known, has already passed.

Chinese Source

思惟何等入慈定。答與有情樂。思惟何等入悲定。答拔有情苦。思惟何等入喜定。答慶諸有情。思惟何等入捨定。答於有情捨。問此中為說等無間緣行相。為說慈等俱生行相。若說等無間緣行相者。

Ru-Yi Translation

"By contemplating what does one enter the concentration of loving-kindness?" Answer: by contemplating giving happiness to sentient beings. "By contemplating what does one enter the concentration of compassion?" Answer: by contemplating relieving sentient beings of suffering. "By contemplating what does one enter the concentration of sympathetic joy?" Answer: by contemplating rejoicing over all sentient beings. "By contemplating what does one enter the concentration of equanimity?" Answer: by contemplating equanimity toward sentient beings. Question: does this passage speak of the aspect belonging to the immediately preceding condition, or does it speak of the aspect co-arisen with loving-kindness and the rest? If it speaks of the aspect belonging to the immediately preceding condition,

慈等現前復何行相。若說慈等俱生行相者。何故作是說。思惟何等入慈定。乃至廣說。有說。此中說等無間緣行相。問若爾慈等現前復何行相。答即作如是四種行相。謂與有情樂。乃至於有情捨。有說。此說慈等俱生行相。問若爾何故作是說。思惟何等入慈定。乃至廣說。答此中說已入名入。於近說遠聲。如說大王從何處來。此於已來名來。如說依空三摩地入正決定。此亦已入名入。所以者何。非世第一法空三摩地相應故。如說入正性離生得現觀邊世俗智。此亦已入名入。所以者何。三類智忍時。方得彼智故。如說受樂受時如實知我受樂受。此亦說已受名受。所以者何。無有自知現在受故。如說斷樂斷苦先喜憂沒。不苦不樂捨念清淨。入第四靜慮具足住。此亦已斷名斷。所以者何。離欲界染時已斷苦根故。如說阿羅漢心解脫欲漏心解脫有漏無明漏。此亦已解脫名解脫。所以者何。離欲界染時已解脫欲漏故。此中亦爾已入名入是故無過。問慈無量等等無間緣。為亦但作如此行相。為更作餘行相耶。答初修業者。唯作如是四種行相能引慈等。若已成滿隨其所欲。亦作所餘苦等行相引生慈等。慈等起時唯作如前四種行相。

then what aspect does loving-kindness and the rest itself take once currently manifest? And if it speaks of the aspect co-arisen with loving-kindness and the rest, why does it say "by contemplating what does one enter the concentration of loving-kindness," and so on in full? Some hold: this passage speaks of the aspect belonging to the immediately preceding condition. Question: if so, then what aspect does loving-kindness and the rest itself take once currently manifest? Answer: it takes precisely these same four aspects — namely, giving happiness to sentient beings, and so on down to equanimity toward sentient beings. Others hold: this passage speaks of the aspect co-arisen with loving-kindness and the rest. Question: if so, why does it say "by contemplating what does one enter the concentration of loving-kindness," and so on in full? Answer: this passage uses the word "enter" to mean what has already been entered, using a word for what is near to speak of what is in fact more distant — just as one might ask a great king, "from where have you come?" even though he has, strictly speaking, already arrived, this too is a case of using the word "come" for what has already come; or as it is said that one enters correctly determined liberation from birth in dependence on the emptiness-concentration — this too is a case of using the word "enter" for what has already been entered, since the mundane-supreme-dharma stage is not itself associated with the emptiness-concentration; or as it is said that, upon entering correctly determined liberation from birth, one attains the mundane wisdom bordering on direct realization — this too is a case of using the word "enter" for what has already been entered, since it is only at the moment of the three kinds of class-knowledge patient acceptance that this wisdom is actually attained; or as it is said that, at the very moment of experiencing a pleasant feeling, one truly knows "I am experiencing a pleasant feeling," this too is a case of using the word "experience" for what has already been experienced, since there is no such thing as directly knowing a feeling at the very instant it is present; or as it is said that, having severed happiness and severed suffering, with joy and displeasure already having earlier subsided, one dwells fully in the fourth dhyāna, neither painful nor pleasant, its equanimity and mindfulness purified — this too is a case of using the word "severed" for what has already been severed, since the faculty of suffering is already severed at the very moment one becomes free from the defilement of the desire realm; or as it is said that an arhat's mind is liberated from the outflow of desire, liberated from the outflow of existence, and liberated from the outflow of ignorance — this too is a case of using the word "liberated" for what has already been liberated, since one is already liberated from the outflow of desire at the very moment one becomes free from the defilement of the desire realm. The very same holds here as well: the word "enter" is used for what has already been entered, and so there is no fault in this reading. Question: as for the aspect belonging to the immediately preceding condition of loving-kindness and the other immeasurables — does one take only this aspect, or does one also take other aspects besides? Answer: a beginning practitioner takes only these four aspects in order to draw forth loving-kindness and the rest; but one who has already brought the practice to completion may, as he wishes, also take the remaining aspects such as suffering and the like in order to draw forth loving-kindness and the rest — yet once loving-kindness and the rest has actually arisen, it takes only these same four aspects as before.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The chain of scriptural precedents assembled to defend a single grammatical reading — a king already arrived still being asked whence he came, a meditator already liberated still described as being liberated, feeling already experienced still described as being experienced — reveals something charming about how this tradition handles language: ordinary speech routinely describes a completed state using the vocabulary of its own approach, and the commentators treat this as simply how language works, not as an error needing correction.

2. Why the Immeasurables Cannot Sever Any Defilement

A sequence of increasingly technical reasons explains why loving-kindness and its companions, however cultivated, never actually sever a single binding fetter — wrong aspect, wrong kind of attention, wrong object, wrong scope — before reconciling this with sūtra passages that seem to say the opposite, via the celebrated distinction between temporary suppression and final eradication, paired with an entire taxonomy of severing-types that echoes throughout Buddhist scholastic literature.

Chinese Source

慈斷何繫結。答無。悲喜捨斷何繫結。答無。問何故四無量不斷煩惱。答行相異故。謂十九行相能斷煩惱。無量非彼行相。四行相是無量。斷煩惱不以此行相故。復次無量是勝解作意。唯真實作意能斷煩惱。復次無量是增益作意。唯不增益作意能斷煩惱。復次無量唯緣現在。要緣三世。或無為道能斷煩惱。復次無量緣有情。要法想能斷煩惱。復次無量緣一分境。非緣一分境道能斷煩惱。復次無間道能斷煩惱。無量是解脫道時得故。問若無量不能斷煩惱者。經說云何通。如說慈若習。若修。若多修習。則能斷瞋。悲若習若修。若多修習。則能斷害。喜若習若修。若多修習。能斷不樂。捨若習若修。若多修習。能斷貪恚。答斷有二種。一暫時斷。二究竟斷。契經說。暫時斷此。阿毘達磨遮究竟斷。如是則二說善通。如暫時斷究竟斷。如是有餘斷。無餘斷。有影斷。無影斷。有隨縛斷。無隨縛斷。摧枝葉斷。拔根本斷。制伏纏斷。害隨眠斷。應知亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Does loving-kindness sever any binding fetter?" Answer: no. "Do compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity sever any binding fetter?" Answer: no. Question: why do the four immeasurables fail to sever any defilement? Answer: because their aspect differs from what is required. That is, it is the nineteen aspects that are capable of severing defilement, and the immeasurables do not take those aspects — the immeasurables take only these four aspects, and severing defilement is not accomplished by means of these aspects. Furthermore, the immeasurables are a resolve-attention; only a true-reality attention is capable of severing defilement. Furthermore, the immeasurables are an attention of augmentation; only a non-augmenting attention is capable of severing defilement. Furthermore, the immeasurables take only the present as their object, whereas defilement can only be severed by a path that takes all three time-periods as its object, or that takes the unconditioned as its object. Furthermore, the immeasurables take sentient beings as their object, whereas defilement can only be severed by a path that takes the very notion of dharmas as its object. Furthermore, the immeasurables take only a partial object, whereas defilement can only be severed by a path that does not take merely a partial object. Furthermore, it is only the uninterrupted path that is capable of severing defilement, whereas the immeasurables are attained at the moment of the path of liberation. Question: if the immeasurables are incapable of severing defilement, how is a certain sūtra passage to be reconciled — namely, that loving-kindness, if practiced, cultivated, and repeatedly cultivated, is capable of severing hatred; that compassion, if practiced, cultivated, and repeatedly cultivated, is capable of severing the wish to harm; that sympathetic joy, if practiced, cultivated, and repeatedly cultivated, is capable of severing displeasure; and that equanimity, if practiced, cultivated, and repeatedly cultivated, is capable of severing craving and ill will? Answer: severing is of two kinds — first, temporary severing; second, final severing. The sūtra speaks of this temporary severing; the Abhidharma rules out final severing — and, understood in this way, the two accounts are both properly reconciled. Just as there is temporary severing and final severing, so too should it be understood that there is severing with a residue and severing without a residue, severing with a shadow and severing without a shadow, severing that still trails a binding tendency and severing free of any such tendency, severing that merely crushes the branches and leaves and severing that uproots the very root, severing that merely suppresses the manifest bond and severing that destroys the latent defilement itself.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The taxonomy of paired severing-terms tucked into this passage's closing lines — temporary/final, with-residue/without-residue, with-shadow/without-shadow, branch-crushing/root-uprooting — reads like a glossary the tradition assembled once and then simply cites by name whenever the same distinction resurfaces, a reminder that behind any single technical claim in this literature often stands a whole prefabricated conceptual toolkit ready to be deployed.

3. Why Not Even a Pure Dhyāna Can Sever Its Own Ground's Defilement

The same non-severing verdict extends to the pure (mundane, undefiled-in-name-only) dhyānas, with an unusually rich cascade of analogies for why a concentration cannot even sever the very defilement native to its own ground: a bound person cannot untie himself, intimate friends are not abandoned for their faults, husband and wife do not quarrel, and a lion cub, though it mimics the hunt, cannot yet actually kill.

Chinese Source

淨初靜慮斷何繫結。答無。乃至淨非想非非想處斷何繫結。答無。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Does the pure first dhyāna sever any binding fetter?" Answer: no. And so on down to: "Does the pure sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception sever any binding fetter?" Answer: no.

此中淨初靜慮等。謂根本地非近分。問何故淨初靜慮等不斷煩惱。

Here, "the pure first dhyāna" and so on refers to a fundamental ground, not a proximate-access ground. Question: why does the pure first dhyāna and the rest fail to sever any defilement?

答下地煩惱雖所應斷。而彼斷已此方現前。無復可斷自地煩惱。雖現可得非所對治無力能斷。於上亦然故不斷煩惱。問不能斷上是義可爾。何故不能斷於自地。答自地煩惱所繫縛故。如人被縛不能自解。又與自地諸煩惱等。同一縛故無力勝彼。又自地愛所親愛故。

Answer: although a lower ground's own defilement is indeed the kind of thing that can be severed, it is only once that defilement has already been severed that this pure dhyāna itself becomes currently manifest — there thus remains no defilement of a lower ground left for it to sever. As for its own ground's defilement, although this is indeed currently present and obtainable, this pure dhyāna is not itself the antidote for it, and so lacks the power to sever it — and the very same holds with respect to the grounds above it, which is why it fails to sever any defilement at all. Question: that it cannot sever a higher ground's defilement makes sense — but why can it not sever its own ground's defilement either? Answer: because it is itself bound by its own ground's very defilement, just as a person who is bound cannot untie himself. Furthermore, it is bound by the very same bond as its own ground's defilements, and so lacks the power to overcome them. Furthermore, it is intimately cherished by its own ground's own craving,

如人親友雖劣不捨。又自地煩惱所[夌*欠]雜故。謂展轉無間而現在前故不能斷。又自地善法與自地煩惱。不相忌難猶如夫妻。又如旃荼羅子與長者子交不相敬憚故不能斷。又無間道能斷煩惱。淨根本地非無間道故不能斷。又無所顧乃能斷之。於自地法非無所顧故不能斷。問何故有漏道不能斷自地。及上地無漏道則能斷耶。答以無漏道是不繫法。於有漏法無非是勝是故能斷。又有漏道作六行相。厭下地欣自地故唯斷。下無漏道作十六行相。厭背一切地故能遍斷。問有漏道亦作十六行相。何故不能遍斷。答彼雖學作聖道行相。不明了故不斷煩惱如師子子未能害獸。

just as one does not abandon a dear friend even though he may be of lesser worth. Furthermore, it is inextricably interwoven with its own ground's defilement, arising in mutual, uninterrupted succession together with it, and so is incapable of severing it. Furthermore, its own ground's wholesome dharmas coexist with its own ground's defilement without ever begrudging or challenging one another, just like husband and wife; or like the son of an outcast associating with the son of a nobleman, neither showing the other any deference or apprehension — and it is for this reason that it cannot sever it. Furthermore, it is only the uninterrupted path that is capable of severing defilement, and a pure fundamental ground is not itself an uninterrupted path, and so cannot sever it. Furthermore, only a mind with no regard for a thing is capable of severing it, and this pure dhyāna does not hold its own ground's dharmas with no regard at all, and so cannot sever it. Question: why is it that a contaminated path cannot sever its own ground's defilement, or that of the grounds above, while an uncontaminated path can? Answer: because the uncontaminated path is itself an unfettered dharma, and is superior, without exception, to every contaminated dharma, and is therefore capable of severing it. Furthermore, a contaminated path takes only six aspects, being weary of the lower ground and delighting in its own ground, and so can sever only that much; whereas an uncontaminated path takes sixteen aspects, being weary of and turning away from every ground alike, and so is capable of severing them all without exception. Question: a contaminated path can also take sixteen aspects — why, then, is it incapable of severing them all without exception? Answer: although it does indeed learn to take on the very aspects of the noble path, it does so without genuine clarity, and so cannot sever defilement — just as a lion cub, though it may imitate its mother's actions, is not yet capable of actually killing prey.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Watching the text reach for domestic and social metaphors — marriage, friendship, caste relations — to explain a point of technical soteriology is a reminder that Abhidharma argument, however dry its outer form, draws its persuasive force from recognizably human situations: a contaminated dhyāna's failure to sever its own ground's defilement is made vivid precisely by comparing it to the everyday tolerances that hold a household together.

4. Liberations, Totality-Bases, and the Precise Severing-Scope of Each Knowledge

The non-severing verdict runs through the eight liberations and the bases and totalities of the mastery-practice, before a genuinely different picture emerges for the various kinds of knowledge: dharma-knowledge severs desire-realm defilement, class-knowledge severs form- and formless-realm defilement, and the mind-reading knowledge severs nothing at all — followed by a precise map of exactly which of the four truth-knowledges and three gateway-concentrations (emptiness, wishlessness, signlessness) can sever which of the five categories of defilement across all nine grounds.

Chinese Source

初第二第三解脫斷何繫結答無。問何故此三不斷煩惱。答行相異故。謂若以此行相斷煩惱不以此行相作前三解脫。若以此行相作前三解脫。不以此行相斷煩惱。又前三解脫是勝解作意。唯真實作意能斷煩惱。又前三解脫是增益作意。唯不增益作意能斷煩惱。又前三解脫緣自相境。唯共相境道能斷煩惱。又前三解脫唯緣現在。要緣三世。或無為道能斷煩惱。又前三解脫唯緣一蘊少分。要緣五蘊四蘊。或非蘊道能斷煩惱。又前三解脫緣一分境。非緣一分境能斷煩惱。又無間道能斷煩惱。前三解脫解脫道時得故。空無邊處解脫斷何繫結。答或空無邊處。或識無邊處。或無所有處。或非想非非想處。或無。或空無邊處者。謂依空無邊處解脫。離空無邊處染。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Do the first, second, and third liberations sever any binding fetter?" Answer: no. Question: why do these three fail to sever any defilement? Answer: because their aspect differs from what is required. That is, whatever aspect is capable of severing defilement is not the aspect that constitutes these first three liberations, and whatever aspect constitutes these first three liberations is not the aspect capable of severing defilement. Furthermore, these first three liberations are a resolve-attention; only a true-reality attention is capable of severing defilement. Furthermore, these first three liberations are an attention of augmentation; only a non-augmenting attention is capable of severing defilement. Furthermore, these first three liberations take a particular characteristic as their object, whereas defilement can only be severed by a path that takes a universal characteristic as its object. Furthermore, these first three liberations take only the present as their object, whereas defilement can only be severed by a path that takes all three time-periods as its object, or that takes the unconditioned as its object. Furthermore, these first three liberations take only a small portion of a single aggregate as their object, whereas defilement can only be severed by a path that takes the full five or four aggregates as its object, or that is not itself confined to the aggregates at all. Furthermore, these first three liberations take only a partial object, whereas defilement can only be severed by a path that does not take merely a partial object. Furthermore, it is only the uninterrupted path that is capable of severing defilement, whereas these first three liberations are attained at the moment of the path of liberation. "Does the liberation of the sphere of infinite space sever any binding fetter?" Answer: it may sever that of the sphere of infinite space, or the sphere of infinite consciousness, or the sphere of nothing-at-all, or the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, or it may sever none at all. As for severing that of the sphere of infinite space: this refers to the uninterrupted paths that, based on the liberation of the sphere of infinite space, become free from the defilement of the sphere of infinite space.

諸無間道乃至或非想非非想處者。謂依空無邊處解脫。離非想非非想處染。諸無間道或無者。謂此諸加行解脫勝進道所攝。空無邊處解脫。及世俗空無邊處解脫。識無邊處解脫斷何繫結。答或識無邊處。或無所有處。或非想非非想處。或無。或識無邊處者。謂依識無邊處解脫。離識無邊處染。諸無間道。乃至或非想非非想處者。

and so on down to severing that of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception: this refers to the uninterrupted paths that, based on the liberation of the sphere of infinite space, become free from the defilement of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. As for severing none at all: this refers to the preparatory, liberation, and further-advancing paths subsumed under the liberation of the sphere of infinite space, together with the mundane liberation of the sphere of infinite space. "Does the liberation of the sphere of infinite consciousness sever any binding fetter?" Answer: it may sever that of the sphere of infinite consciousness, or the sphere of nothing-at-all, or the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, or it may sever none at all. As for severing that of the sphere of infinite consciousness: this refers to the uninterrupted paths that, based on the liberation of the sphere of infinite consciousness, become free from the defilement of the sphere of infinite consciousness. As for severing that of the sphere of nothing-at-all, and so on down to severing that of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception:

Others hold: this passage speaks specifically of attaining something in its entirety — such a one attains only a partial portion of it, and it is for this reason that it is not stated. "Is there ever a case of attaining both the flavor-associated and the pure first dhyāna, without attaining the uncontaminated?" Answer: yes — namely, at the moment of dying in the Brahma-world or above and being reborn in the Brahma-world, since both are attained together at the moment of taking on the intermediate-existence body. "Is there ever a case of attaining both the flavor-associated and the uncontaminated first dhyāna, without attaining the pure?" Answer: no. Question: at the moment an arhat gives rise to the binding defilement of the Brahma-world and thereby regresses, he attains both of these together — why, then, does this passage say "no"? Answer: it ought properly to be said "yes," and this is exactly the case just described — and its saying "no" should be understood as an incompleteness in the text. Others hold: "attaining the uncontaminated first dhyāna" here means specifically what one did not possess before and now comes to possess — since one already possessed it before, its attainment is not mentioned here. Others hold: as already stated above, the uncontaminated is itself an excellent quality, and it is fitting to point out its attainment at a moment of advancement, but not fitting to point it out at a moment of regression, and it is for this reason that it is not mentioned. "Is there ever a case of attaining both the pure and the uncontaminated first dhyāna, without attaining the flavor-associated?" Answer: yes —

謂聖者欲界愛盡時。以得不還果時二俱得故。頗有得味相應淨無漏初靜慮耶。答無。問起梵世纏退阿羅漢果時。三種俱得何故不說。

namely, at the moment a noble one's craving for the desire realm is exhausted, since both are attained together at the moment of attaining the non-returner's fruit. "Is there ever a case of attaining all three — the flavor-associated, the pure, and the uncontaminated first dhyāna — at once?" Answer: no. Question: at the moment of giving rise to the binding defilement of the Brahma-world and thereby regressing from the arhat's fruit, all three are attained together — why is this not stated?

答爾時淨以少分得故不說。無漏如前說。

Answer: at that moment, only a partial portion of the pure is attained, and it is for this reason that it is not stated; the uncontaminated case is as explained above.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the exact same person's spiritual status can be sliced three different ways at once — which of the three dhyāna-kinds he possesses, which he could newly attain, which he stands to relinquish — dramatizes just how much information this tradition packs into a single moment of a practitioner's life: no single label like "noble one" or "ordinary worldling" comes close to capturing everything this fascicle considers worth distinguishing about where, exactly, someone stands.

10. Attainment vs. Possession, and Relinquishment vs. Regression: Four Careful Distinctions

The fascicle closes by nailing down four subtly different technical terms through a cascade of competing definitions: "attainment" names the very first instant of gaining something, "possession" every instant of continuing to hold it; "relinquishing" covers loss at either an advancing or a regressing moment, while "regressing" names only the latter — with a final summary tally of six kinds of possession, six of non-possession, five of attainment, three of relinquishment, and two of regression, all just for the first dhyāna's three-way classification.

Chinese Source

問得與成就有何差別。有說。名即差別。謂名得。名成就。有說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: what is the difference between "attainment" and "possession"? Some hold: the very name already differs — one is named "attainment," the other "possession." Others hold:

未得而得名得。已得而得名成就。有說。最初得名得。後數數得名成就。有說。先不成就而成就名得。先成就而成就名成就。有說。

coming to possess what one did not possess before is called "attainment"; continuing to possess what one already possessed before is called "possession." Others hold: the very first instance of coming to possess something is called "attainment"; every repeated instance thereafter is called "possession." Others hold: coming to possess something one did not previously possess is called "attainment"; continuing to possess something one already previously possessed is called "possession." Others hold:

先無繫屬而有繫屬名得。先有繫屬而有繫屬名成就。有說初得名得。得已不斷名成就。有說。初獲名得。得已不失名成就。是故得唯在初成就。通初後得成就。是謂差別頗有捨味相應初靜慮非淨無漏耶。答有。謂梵世愛盡時。如是等文如本論說。隨其所應與得相違應廣說。問梵世愛盡時亦捨退分淨。何故不說。答此中說一切捨。彼唯少分捨。是故不說。問得果練根及退位。皆有唯捨無漏初靜慮非餘。此中何故不說。有說。應說而不說者。當知有餘。有說。此中說捨而不得者。彼捨還得。是故不說。問捨不成就有何差別。答名即差別。名捨名不成就。如是等如前得成就相違應廣說。

coming to stand in a binding relationship where none existed before is called "attainment"; continuing to stand in a binding relationship that already existed before is called "possession." Others hold: the very first instance of attaining something is called "attainment"; continuing, once attained, without interruption, is called "possession." Others hold: the very first securing of something is called "attainment"; continuing, once secured, without loss, is called "possession" — and it is for this reason that "attainment" occurs only at the very first instant, while "possession" spans both the first instant and every instant thereafter. Such, then, is the difference between the two. "Is there ever a case of relinquishing a flavor-associated first dhyāna, without relinquishing its pure or uncontaminated counterpart?" Answer: yes — namely, at the moment one's craving for the Brahma-world is exhausted. Passages such as these are found stated in the base treatise itself, and, as appropriate, the full complementary set opposite to "attainment" should likewise be worked out in detail. Question: at the moment one's craving for the Brahma-world is exhausted, one also relinquishes the pure attainment conforming to regression — why is this not stated? Answer: this passage speaks specifically of relinquishing something in its entirety — such a one relinquishes only a partial portion of it, and it is for this reason that it is not stated. Question: at the moment of attaining a fruit, at the moment of refining one's faculties, and at the moment of regression, there is, in every case, only the relinquishing of the uncontaminated first dhyāna and nothing else — why is this not stated here? Some hold: it ought to have been stated, and its omission should be understood as an incompleteness in the text. Others hold: this passage speaks specifically of relinquishing something without ever attaining it again — such a one, having relinquished it, comes to attain it once again, and it is for this reason that it is not stated. Question: what is the difference between "relinquishing" and "not possessing"? Answer: the very name already differs — one is named "relinquishing," the other "not possessing" — and, in every other respect, the full complementary set opposite to "attainment" and "possession," as explained above, should likewise be worked out in detail.

頗有退味相應初靜慮。非淨無漏耶。答無。以捨煩惱不名退故。頗有退淨初靜慮。非味相應無漏耶。答有。謂異生從欲界愛盡退時。

"Is there ever a case of regressing from a flavor-associated first dhyāna, without regressing from its pure or uncontaminated counterpart?" Answer: no, since relinquishing a defilement is never itself called "regression." "Is there ever a case of regressing from a pure first dhyāna, without regressing from its flavor-associated or uncontaminated counterpart?" Answer: yes — namely, at the moment an ordinary worldling regresses from his exhausted craving for the desire realm.

頗有退淨無漏初靜慮非味相應耶。答有。謂聖者從欲界愛盡退時。

"Is there ever a case of regressing from both the pure and the uncontaminated first dhyāna, without regressing from the flavor-associated?" Answer: yes — namely, at the moment a noble one regresses from his exhausted craving for the desire realm.

頗有餘退耶。答無。問起上二界纏退無學果。及從種性退時。皆有退無漏初靜慮非餘。此中何故不說。有說。應說而不說者。當知此義有餘。有說。此中說退而不得者。彼雖退而還得。是故不說。問捨退何差別。有說。名即差別。謂名為捨。名為退。有說。捨通勝進及退時。退唯退時。有說。捨通損減及增益時。退唯損減時。有說。捨通上下時。退唯下時。有說。捨通盛衰時。退唯衰時。有說。捨通味相應等三。退唯二種除味相應。有說。捨通利鈍根。退唯鈍根。是謂捨退差別。

Question: what is the difference between "relinquishing" and "regressing"? Some hold: the very name already differs — one is named "relinquishing," the other "regressing." Others hold: "relinquishing" spans both a moment of advancement and a moment of regression, while "regressing" applies only to a moment of regression. Others hold: "relinquishing" spans both a moment of diminishment and a moment of increase, while "regressing" applies only to a moment of diminishment. Others hold: "relinquishing" spans both a higher and a lower state, while "regressing" applies only to a lower state. Others hold: "relinquishing" spans both a flourishing and a declining state, while "regressing" applies only to a declining state. It should accordingly be understood that this discussion involves six kinds of possession, six kinds of non-possession, five kinds of attainment, three kinds of relinquishment, and two kinds of regression, all in order to clarify the differences among the flavor-associated, the pure, and the uncontaminated as applied to the first dhyāna. As it is said, "the first dhyāna, and so on down to the sphere of nothing-at-all, should be explained in just the same way" — this is only a general statement, and the specific differences within it should be understood according to their own proper principles. The sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, lacking all three kinds, is not displayed in the same fashion — yet, with respect to the two kinds it does possess, the very same pattern as above should likewise be understood accordingly.
