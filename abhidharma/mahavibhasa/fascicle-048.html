Five Trees That Choke a Forest

Fascicle 48 continues the Unwholesome Nyāsa through four further complete taxonomies of affliction — the four floods, four yokes, four graspings, and four bodily ties — before turning to the five hindrances, illustrated through a parable of five trees whose tiny seeds grow massive enough to smother an entire forest, and closing with two bandits who blind and bind a traveler on a perilous road, leaving him no possible escape. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. A Third Affliction Taxonomy: The Four Floods

Fascicle 48 continues the Unwholesome Nyāsa with yet another fourfold taxonomy of affliction — the desire-flood, existence-flood, view-flood, and ignorance-flood — defined technically as one hundred and eight factors in total, and etymologically as carrying the sense of sweeping beings along, of surging and pouring forth, drowning them within the endless wandering of birth and death.

2. Why View Gets Its Own Flood, and the Four Graspings Open

A genuine puzzle opens: view was folded into the broader desire- and existence-contaminants earlier, so why does it earn its own separate flood category here? The treatise answers through a vivid image — view's own restless, keen aspect doesn't accord with the sense of "detaining and dwelling" that defines a contaminant, but does accord with the sense of sweeping and surging that defines a flood, exactly as yoking two equally restless oxen to one cart destroys it, while pairing a restless ox with a slow one holds steady. A companion image follows for outsiders trapped by their own views — an aged elephant, mired in a swamp, sinking only deeper the more it struggles — before the fascicle opens a fourth taxonomy entirely, the four graspings, with their own etymology of firewood feeding a blaze and a silkworm spinning its own fatal cocoon.

3. Why Ignorance and the Four Views Split Differently Among Graspings

The treatise explains why ignorance, unlike view, is never granted its own separate grasping category — grasping requires the capacity to select among dharmas, and ignorance, dark and benighted by its own nature, is incapable of selecting anything at all. It then turns to why four of the five views collapse into a single "view-grasping" while clinging to rules and vows earns its own separate category: this specific view alone directly opposes and runs contrary to the noble path itself, deceiving both the inward, Buddhist path (through misguided rituals of purification) and the outward, non-Buddhist path (through every manner of pointless austerity) alike.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Denying ignorance a grasping-category specifically because it cannot select is a precise diagnostic distinction: grasping, on this account, is never simply blind attachment but always involves some minimal discriminating judgment, however mistaken, about which object to seize — and ignorance's own total absence of judgment disqualifies it from a category that, however corrupted, still requires the basic cognitive machinery of choosing.

4. Why Desire-Realm and Higher-Realm Afflictions Split Into Different Graspings

The treatise turns to a genuinely interesting etymological puzzle: why is the fourth grasping called the "grasping of self-doctrine," when it can't be named for its own aspect (that would properly describe self-view alone) or its own object (every dharma is, after all, without self)? The answer sidesteps both horns, defining the split instead by dependency structure — desire-realm afflictions operate through external objects, sexual desire, and dependence on another's body, while form- and formless-realm afflictions operate entirely through internal, settled, concentrated states, capable of drawing forth vastly larger bodies and far longer lifespans than desire alone could ever produce.

Ru-Yi Translation

If it is named for its own aspect, then the view of the perishable aggregation of self ought properly to be named the grasping of self-doctrine, since it is precisely this view's own aspect that operates in terms of a self. If it is named for its own object, then, since every dharma is without self, how could it possibly be called the grasping of self-doctrine? The answer: it is named neither for its own aspect nor for its own object, since either would incur the fault just stated — rather, the desire realm's own afflictions, excepting view, are established as the grasping of desire, while the afflictions of the form and formless realms, excepting view, are established as the grasping of self-doctrine. One asks: why is this so? The answer: the desire realm's own afflictions operate in dependence on sexual desire, in dependence on external objects, in dependence on requisites, and in dependence on another's own body, and it is for this reason that they are established as the grasping of desire; the afflictions of the form and formless realms, standing in contrast to these, operate in dependence on what is internal, and it is for this reason that they are established as the grasping of self-doctrine. 5. Why Some Taxonomies Include the Entanglements and Others Don't

The treatise resolves a technical inconsistency across its own several taxonomies: the desire-contaminant, desire-flood, desire-yoke, and grasping of desire all explicitly include the ten entanglements, while the corresponding existence-categories do not — resolved by noting that the higher realms' own entanglements are simply too few and too constrained in their operation to warrant inclusion. 13. Why Some Hindrances Stand Alone and Others Come in Pairs

The treatise explains its own pairing logic: covetous desire, hatred, and doubt are each individually capable of "bearing the full weight" of their own hindrance, while torpor and drowsiness need each other's combined weight to do so, exactly as a strong roof-beam is used alone while a weak one requires a matching partner. Each hindrance, or paired hindrance, is shown to share a single characteristic food and a single antidote — covetous desire feeding on beauty and countered by contemplating unattractiveness, hatred feeding on what seems hateful and countered by loving-kindness, torpor-and-drowsiness sharing a food of dejection, yawning, and mental weakness, countered by insight, and restlessness-and-worry sharing a food of nostalgic and anxious thought, countered by calm-abiding. It then offers a plausible causal account for why the five arise in exactly this order — obtaining a desired object draws forth covetous desire, losing it draws forth hatred, and so on through torpor, drowsiness, restlessness, worry, and finally doubt — before dividing the five into a fuller count of ten through three further distinctions.

16. Why Shamelessness, Envy, Anger, and the Six Defilements Never Made the List Either

The fascicle closes with a systematic accounting of several further plausible candidates for hindrance-status, each rejected for its own specific reason: shamelessness and lack of moral dread, though pervading every unwholesome mind, allow a wrongdoer too many skillful means at the very moment of transgression for their own concealing sense to manifest clearly; envy and stinginess trouble both destinies and both monastic and lay communities so broadly that they are classified as fetters instead; anger and concealment, and the six defilements more broadly, simply lack a hindrance's own distinctively subtle, quietly recurring character, being too coarse and agitated by comparison.
