The Cultivation Census, Applied Again

Fascicle 164 completes the first dhyāna's four-way cultivation census — including the case of cultivating neither the pure nor the uncontaminated — then runs the exact same exhaustive method a second time for the fourth dhyāna. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. The First Dhyāna's Tetralemma Concludes: Cultivating Neither Pure Nor Uncontaminated

Fascicle 164 opens by completing the previous fascicle's four-way census for the first dhyāna, working through the final alternative — every circumstance under which a practitioner cultivates neither the pure nor the uncontaminated first dhyāna at all, including any defiled or neutral mind, the mindless attainments, and rebirth among the gods of no-perception, each explained by its own specific incapacity to serve as a basis for cultivation.

有不修淨初靜慮亦非無漏。謂已得非初靜慮世俗智無漏智現在前。

As for any neutral mind whatsoever becoming currently manifest: this refers to any of the three realms' own unobscured-neutral mind, since such a mind, being unstable, unabiding, and insubstantial in its own nature, feeble like a rotten seed, can only cultivate something once its own nature is instead stable, abiding, substantial, and vigorous. that, although a wholesome mind does indeed arise among those gods, it is not itself a basis for cultivation, and so has no cultivating relationship at all. This passage speaks specifically of one born in a ground capable of cultivation who nonetheless does not cultivate —

though there is likewise a failure to cultivate among those born elsewhere, this is not mentioned, being easy enough to understand on its own. Just as stated for the first dhyāna, so too should the very same be said for the second and third dhyānas — since, at the final path of liberation for becoming free from a lower ground's defilement, one may in each case either enter the fundamental ground or fail to enter it.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The three vivid images offered for why defiled minds, neutral minds, and the mindless attainments each fail to cultivate anything — a heavy, sluggish mind that needs to become light and energetic first, a rotten seed too insubstantial to grow, and the sheer impossibility of any mental cultivation happening without a mind at all — turn a purely negative finding (nothing is cultivated here) into a small gallery of concrete reasons why cultivation itself has real prerequisites.

2. The Same Enormous Census, Now for the Fourth Dhyāna

The entire four-part tetralemma from the previous fascicle is now run a second time, in full, for the fourth dhyāna instead of the first — cultivating the pure without the uncontaminated, the uncontaminated without the pure, both together, and neither — cataloguing once again the exhaustive combinatorics of specific practices and practitioner-types (the five superknowledges, the immeasurables, the liberations, the paired emptiness/wishless/signless contemplations, refining one's faculties, interwoven practice) that produce each alternative.

Ru-Yi Translation

"If one cultivates the pure fourth dhyāna, does one also cultivate the uncontaminated? Conversely, if one cultivates the uncontaminated fourth dhyāna, does one also cultivate the pure?" Answer: a full tetralemma should be formed. There is a case of cultivating the pure fourth dhyāna without cultivating the uncontaminated — namely, when an already-attained pure fourth dhyāna becomes currently manifest; or when a not-yet-attained pure fourth dhyāna becomes currently manifest, without thereby cultivating the uncontaminated. As for an already-attained pure fourth dhyāna becoming currently manifest: this refers to ordinary worldlings and noble ones alike — whether trainee or, indeed, the Tathāgata himself — who, for the sake of dwelling happily in this very life and the like, give rise to a previously-attained mundane fourth dhyāna currently manifest — at such a moment, its own force does not even extend to its own second instant, let alone being capable of cultivating any other future excellent quality; yet, since the very state of being currently manifest is itself practice-cultivation, this is properly called "cultivating the pure fourth dhyāna." As for a not-yet-attained pure fourth dhyāna becoming currently manifest, without thereby cultivating the uncontaminated: this refers to an ordinary worldling becoming free from the defilement of the third dhyāna, at the final path of liberation, and, for the sake of becoming free from the defilement of the fourth dhyāna, if the fourth dhyāna itself serves as the preparatory practice, at that preparatory path; or that same ordinary worldling, based on the fourth dhyāna, drawing forth the five superknowledges, at every preparatory path, the five uninterrupted paths, and the three paths of liberation, giving rise to the three immeasurables, the pure liberation, the last four bases of totality, the first eight totality-bases, the contemplation of impurity, the mindfulness-foundations, the threefold meaning-contemplation, and the heat, summit, patient-acceptance, and mundane-supreme-dharma stages. At all such moments, one gives rise to a previously-unattained pure fourth dhyāna currently manifest, without cultivating the uncontaminated. There is a case of cultivating the uncontaminated fourth dhyāna without cultivating the pure — namely, when an already-attained uncontaminated fourth dhyāna becomes currently manifest; or when a not-yet-attained uncontaminated fourth dhyāna becomes currently manifest, without thereby cultivating the pure; or when a not-yet-attained mundane wisdom other than the fourth dhyāna, or a not-yet-attained uncontaminated wisdom other than the fourth dhyāna, becomes currently manifest, cultivating the uncontaminated fourth dhyāna without the pure. As for an already-attained uncontaminated fourth dhyāna becoming currently manifest: this refers to noble ones — whether trainee or, indeed, the Tathāgata himself — who, for the sake of dwelling happily in this very life and the like, give rise to a previously-attained uncontaminated fourth dhyāna currently manifest — at such a moment, its own force does not even extend to its own second instant, let alone being capable of cultivating any other future excellent quality; yet, since the very state of being currently manifest is itself practice-cultivation, this is properly called "cultivating the uncontaminated fourth dhyāna."
