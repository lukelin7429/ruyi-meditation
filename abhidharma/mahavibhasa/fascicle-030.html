The Strength of Nārāyaṇa

Fascicle 30 continues the Love-and-Respect Nyāsa with offering and reverence toward the Three Jewels and a strict hierarchy of who may teach whom, works through why the Buddha's own praise of his disciples never constitutes an offering received from them, then turns to an entirely new topic — the Bodhisattva's bodily strength, escalating through unit-conversion scales from ordinary oxen through legendary elephants and the elephant-king Airāvaṇa to a declaration of boundless strength proven by a single unmoving hair — before closing with the Buddha's ten powers of mind. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Offering-and-Reverence Toward the Three Jewels and One's Teachers

Fascicle 30 opens by combining the two topics fascicle 29 treated separately, offering and reverence, into a single investigation of how they operate together toward one object. The treatise works out a careful asymmetry: since the Buddha has already reached the ultimate with respect to every dharma, sentient beings can only ever offer him material goods, never the Dharma itself, since no one exists who could teach him anything unprecedented — a restriction the treatise then extends, in a strict descending hierarchy, all the way down to ordinary practitioners of sharp versus dull faculties.

Chinese Source

云何供養恭敬乃至廣說。問何故復作此論。答前雖別說供養恭敬自性。而未總說於一境轉。今欲顯示此二於一境轉故作斯論云何供養恭敬。答如有一類於佛法僧親教軌範及餘隨一有智尊重同梵行者。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is offering-and-reverence, and so on, at length? One asks: why is this treatise further composed? The answer: although the foregoing has already separately explained the inherent nature of offering and of reverence, it has not yet, taken together, explained their operation with respect to a single object; this treatise now wishes to reveal that these two operate together with respect to a single object, and it is for this reason that this treatise is composed. What is offering-and-reverence? The answer: if there is a certain kind of person who, toward the Buddha, the Dharma, the Saṅgha, his preceptor, his instructor, or any other wise and revered fellow practitioner of the pure conduct,

施設供養恭敬而住。若於是處有供養及恭敬。是謂供養恭敬。此中一類義如前說。謂異生或聖者。於佛施設供養恭敬而住者。唯施設財供養恭敬而住非法供養。所以者何。佛於諸法已得究竟。不復從他受學法故。無有能為世尊說法令生未曾有善巧覺慧故。於法施設供養恭敬而住者。有本不說敬養法言。以於涅槃無緣義故。前說供養謂能為緣涅槃無緣是故不說。而有本說者雖於涅槃無生長義而有於彼令顯了義謂以財法供養涅槃令諸有情恭敬求證由斯展轉斷障證得。復次法有二種。一世俗謂名身等法。二勝義謂究竟涅槃。雖於勝義法無生長義而於世俗法有生長義。是故於法亦有敬養。問諸施法物誰應受之。答施世俗法物說法師應受。或應以此書寫正法施勝義法物。應勤加守護猶如守護窣堵波物。於僧施設供養恭敬而住者通財法二供養。財供養者謂以香華衣食等物供養僧眾。或設五年大會等事名財供養僧。法供養者謂以三契聲等為眾宣說正法。或在眾中論議決擇。或復處眾讚美功德申述所願令眾歡悅。諸如是等名法供養僧。於親教軌範及餘隨一有智尊重同梵行者。施設供養恭敬而住者。亦通財法二種供養。財供養者謂以衣鉢飲食湯藥及餘隨一沙門資具而供養之。法供養者謂以三藏勸令受持。或為解釋令無疑滯。或復勸請令修正行。諸如是等名法供養。於上所說三寶師友殊勝境中隨應供養恭敬而住。故餘境中不決定有准前應說。問供養恭敬何處具有。答欲界具有非色無色界。於欲界中四趣具有非捺落迦以地獄中無財供養唯有法故。問此財法供養誰設誰受。答佛於一切有情能設財法二供養。彼隨所應能受。一切有情於佛隨應能設財供養非法供養。無能為佛說法者故。及不能生佛未曾有善巧覺慧故。

sets forth offering and reverence and dwells accordingly — wherever there is, in such a case, both offering and reverence — this is called offering-and-reverence. Here, "a certain kind" has the same meaning as stated above, referring either to an ordinary being or to a noble one. One who, toward the Buddha, sets forth offering and reverence and dwells accordingly sets forth only material offering and reverence, not Dharma offering. Why is this so? It is because the Buddha has already attained the ultimate with respect to all dharmas, and no longer needs to receive the Dharma in study from another, and there exists no one able to expound the Dharma to the Blessed One so as to give rise in him to any unprecedented, skillful understanding. As for one who, toward the Dharma, sets forth offering and reverence and dwells accordingly — there are certain versions of the text that do not speak of "offering and reverence" with respect to the Dharma, on the grounds that nirvāṇa admits of no sense of "serving as a condition"; the offering spoken of above means precisely serving as a condition, and, since nirvāṇa admits of no such sense, it is accordingly not mentioned. Yet there are other versions of the text that do speak of it, holding that, although there is, with respect to nirvāṇa, no sense of "growth and increase," there is nonetheless a sense in which it may be made manifest — that is, by offering material goods and the Dharma to nirvāṇa, one causes sentient beings to revere it and to seek its realization, and, through this in due course, cuts off the obstructions and attains realization. Further, the Dharma is of two kinds: first, conventional, referring to the dharmas of name-bodies and the rest; second, ultimate, referring to nirvāṇa itself, the final goal. Although, with respect to the ultimate Dharma, there is no sense of "growth and increase," there is, with respect to the conventional Dharma, a sense of "growth and increase," and it is for this reason that offering and reverence are also spoken of with respect to the Dharma. One asks: as for the objects given in Dharma offering, who ought to receive them? The answer: as for objects given as conventional Dharma, the Dharma-preaching master ought to receive them; or one may, by writing out the true Dharma, thereby offer an object of the ultimate Dharma, which ought to be diligently guarded and protected, exactly as one guards and protects the belongings of a stūpa. As for one who, toward the Saṅgha, sets forth offering and reverence and dwells accordingly, this extends to both kinds of offering, material and Dharma. As for material offering: it refers to making offerings to the assembly of monks with incense, flowers, robes, food, and the like, or to holding such events as a great quinquennial assembly, both of which are called material offering to the Saṅgha. As for Dharma offering: it refers to expounding the true Dharma to the assembly by means of the three modes of recitation, or to debating and resolving points of doctrine within the assembly, or further to praising virtues and expressing one's aspirations before the assembly so as to give them joy — all such things as these are called Dharma offering to the Saṅgha. As for one who, toward his preceptor, his instructor, or any other wise and revered fellow practitioner of the pure conduct, sets forth offering and reverence and dwells accordingly, this too extends to both kinds of offering, material and Dharma. As for material offering: it refers to making offerings of robes, alms bowls, food and drink, medicine, and any other article proper to a monk. As for Dharma offering: it refers to urging him to uphold the three collections of scripture, or to explaining them so as to leave him free of doubt, or further to exhorting and requesting him to cultivate correct practice — all such things as these are called Dharma offering. It is with respect to the superior objects already spoken of above — the Three Jewels, and one's teachers and friends — that offering and reverence are set forth and dwelt in accordingly; in every other object, this is accordingly not fixed, and should be understood exactly as stated above. One asks: in what place are offering and reverence found in their entirety? The answer: they are found in their entirety within the desire realm, not within the form or formless realms; within the desire realm, they are found in their entirety among the four destinies, not within the hells, since the hells possess no material offering, only Dharma offering. One asks: as for this material and Dharma offering, who sets it forth, and who receives it? The answer: the Buddha is able to set forth both material and Dharma offering to every sentient being, who receive it each according to what is fitting; every sentient being, toward the Buddha, is able only to set forth material offering, not Dharma offering, since there exists no one able to expound the Dharma to the Buddha, and since none is able to give rise in the Buddha to any unprecedented, skillful understanding.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The passage's careful hedging over whether nirvāṇa itself can even be “offered to” — some textual traditions deny it outright, since the unconditioned admits no sense of growth, while others retain the language as a way of making devotion to nirvāṇa itself visible and effective — is a small window into how seriously this literature takes the boundary between conventional and ultimate truth even in a devotional context that might, on the surface, seem to call for less philosophical caution.

2. A Strict Hierarchy: Who May Teach Whom

The treatise extends the same asymmetry that governs offering to the Buddha into a full descending hierarchy — a pratyekabuddha may teach everyone but the Buddha, Śāriputra may teach everyone but the Buddha and pratyekabuddhas, Maudgalyāyana everyone but those three, and so on down through every possible rank, arriving finally at the general principle that one of sharper faculties may always offer the Dharma to one of duller faculties, but never the reverse.

Chinese Source

獨覺除佛於一切有情能設財法二供養。彼隨所應能受。一切有情於獨覺隨應能設財供養。非法供養。舍利子除佛獨覺。於一切有情能設財法二供養。彼隨所應能受。一切有情於舍利子隨應能設財供養非法供養。大目乾連除佛獨覺及舍利子。於一切有情能設財法二供養彼隨所應能受。一切有情於大目乾連隨應能設財供養非法供養。

Ru-Yi Translation

A pratyekabuddha, excepting the Buddha, is able to set forth both material and Dharma offering to every sentient being, who receive it each according to what is fitting; every sentient being, toward a pratyekabuddha, is able only to set forth material offering, not Dharma offering. Śāriputra, excepting the Buddha and pratyekabuddhas, is able to set forth both material and Dharma offering to every sentient being, who receive it each according to what is fitting; every sentient being, toward Śāriputra, is able only to set forth material offering, not Dharma offering. Mahāmaudgalyāyana, excepting the Buddha, pratyekabuddhas, and Śāriputra, is able to set forth both material and Dharma offering to every sentient being, who receive it each according to what is fitting; every sentient being, toward Mahāmaudgalyāyana, is able only to set forth material offering, not Dharma offering.

乃至利根者於鈍根者隨應能設財法二供養。彼隨所應能受。鈍根者於利根者隨應能設財供養非法供養彼隨所應能受。

And so on, down to: one of sharp faculties is able to set forth both material and Dharma offering to one of dull faculties, who receives it as is fitting; one of dull faculties is able only to set forth material offering, not Dharma offering, to one of sharp faculties, who receives it as is fitting.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This hierarchy is a striking piece of social epistemology: spiritual attainment here creates a strict one-directional channel for genuine teaching, structurally identical at every level from Buddha-and-pratyekabuddha down to sharp-versus-dull ordinary practitioners — the same logic that exempts the Buddha from receiving instruction turns out to govern every single rung of the ladder beneath him as well.

3. An Objection: Did the Buddha Not Receive Dharma Offering from Śroṇa Koṭikarṇa?

A pointed scriptural objection follows: the sūtra explicitly records the Buddha praising the monk Śroṇa Koṭikarṇa's beautiful recitation of the Dharma — does this not mean the Buddha received Dharma offering after all? The treatise responds with an unusually detailed narrative explaining the actual circumstance: Śroṇa Koṭikarṇa, sent by his preceptor Kātyāyana to petition the Buddha on behalf of frontier monks, was too overawed to speak, so the Buddha housed him privately and drew out his recitation only to grant him the confidence needed to finally voice his request — the praise, several masters argue, served that pastoral purpose, or rewarded a past life of pure conduct, or served some other end entirely, but never constituted the Buddha's own reception of teaching.

Chinese Source

問若無以法供養佛者。契經所說當云何通。如說苾芻善哉善哉。汝乃能以和雅清妙明了易解美亮音聲。諷誦正法令我歡喜。答世尊欲令聞俱胝耳得無畏故作如是說。非佛於彼受法供養。謂彼親教迦多衍那遣詣佛所。為居邊國寒地苾芻求請五事。一常澡浴。二以皮作襯身敷具。三請恒著周足革屣。四請持律為第五人得受具戒。五請若有苾芻遣使持衣與餘苾芻。彼苾芻若不受我等當如何處分此衣聞俱胝耳承親教勅來詣佛所。世尊威重釋梵護世尚不能側近正觀。況彼輒敢申請佛知是事告阿難曰。汝可將彼至我寢室敷設臥具而安置之。阿難如教佛與同止至夜後分。知彼倦息便告之言。汝應為我誦所解法聞俱胝耳以三契聲誦所解法。世尊歡喜為欲令彼得無所畏能申所請。是故讚言善哉。善哉乃至廣說。有作是說。世尊讚彼過去所修業道清淨感得如是美妙音聲。令人樂聞故作是說。非為於彼受法供養。有餘師說。世尊讚彼能善誦持波羅衍拏見諦經等故作是說。非為於彼受法供養。或有說者。以彼苾芻在豐馬國作諸佛事。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: if there is no one who offers the Dharma to the Buddha, then how is what the sūtra states to be reconciled? As it is stated: "Monk, excellent, excellent! You are indeed able, with a gentle, clear, subtle, distinct, easily understood, beautiful and resonant voice, to chant the true Dharma, causing me to rejoice." The answer: the Blessed One, wishing to cause Śroṇa Koṭikarṇa to attain fearlessness, spoke in this way; it was not that the Buddha received Dharma offering from him. That is, his preceptor Kātyāyana had sent him to where the Buddha was, in order to request, on behalf of the monks dwelling in the cold border regions, five matters: first, to bathe constantly; second, to use a hide as bedding beneath the body; third, to be permitted to constantly wear shoes covering the whole foot; fourth, to be permitted, in the case of one who upholds the vinaya, to be counted as the fifth person needed for the full ordination ceremony; fifth, to be permitted that, if a monk should send someone bearing a robe to another monk, and that monk should decline to accept it, they might then decide for themselves how to dispose of the robe. Śroṇa Koṭikarṇa, receiving his preceptor's instruction, came to where the Buddha was; yet the Blessed One's majesty and weight are such that even Śakra, Brahmā, and the guardians of the world are unable to draw near and look upon him directly — how much less would he presume to make such a request? The Buddha, knowing this, said to Ānanda: you may bring him to my own sleeping chamber, arrange bedding there, and settle him in. Ānanda did as instructed, and the Buddha dwelt together with him. Toward the latter part of the night, knowing that Śroṇa Koṭikarṇa had grown weary and was resting, the Buddha said to him: you should chant for me the Dharma you have mastered. Śroṇa Koṭikarṇa then, in the three modes of recitation, chanted the Dharma he had mastered. The Blessed One rejoiced, and, wishing to cause him to attain fearlessness and to be able to voice his request, therefore praised him, saying "excellent, excellent," and so on, at length. There are those who say: the Blessed One praised the purity of the karmic path he had cultivated in the past, which had brought about such a beautiful and wondrous voice, pleasing to hear, and it was for this reason that he spoke thus — not because he received Dharma offering from him. There are other masters who say: the Blessed One praised him for his skillful recitation and upholding of the Pārāyaṇa and the Satyadarśana-sūtra and the like, and it was for this reason that he spoke thus — not because he received Dharma offering from him. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The image of the Buddha quietly housing an overawed young monk in his own sleeping chamber overnight, drawing out his hidden confidence one recitation at a time, is one of the more tender character portraits in this entire treatise — a reminder that even the most rigorously systematic Abhidharma literature preserves, embedded within its doctrinal argumentation, genuinely warm narrative material about the Buddha's own pastoral care for an anxious disciple.

4. Further Cases, and the Opening of a New Topic: Bodily Strength

The treatise closes its survey of the Buddha's praise with further named examples — Aniruddha asked to teach on firmness while the Buddha's own back ached, Maudgalyāyana praised for authority before the Śākyas, Śāriputra compared to a lion's roar — before tackling a final, harder case: the Buddha's own joyful praise of Ānanda's teaching on diligence, explained through the vivid story of the Buddha's back pain healed by his own recollection of the very diligence Ānanda happened to be teaching at that moment. A last reconciliation, of a Vinaya passage where the Buddha rejoices at the growth of others' liberation, treats their spiritual progress as inseparable from his own concern, before the fascicle pivots to a wholly new topic: bodily strength, opened by refuting three rival sectarian positions that would deny it any distinct existence.

Chinese Source

如佛讚歎舍利子言。汝能說法如師子吼。汝所說者是決定說。問餘契經說復云何通。如說佛告阿難陀言善哉善哉。如汝所說精進速證無上菩提。我聞汝言深生歡喜。答佛以慶喜所說應時故說此言非為受法。謂薄伽梵為化有情曾涉遠路勞倦背痛詣一樹下。四疊七條以為臥具五條覆體枕僧伽胝。如師子王右脇而臥告阿難曰。汝可為諸苾芻說法不應虛度。爾時阿難承佛聖旨為苾芻眾說七覺支言。諸仁者念等覺支是我世尊自覺自說依厭離滅迴向於捨。如是乃至捨等覺支廣說亦爾。世尊聞彼說精進時便起前際憶念智見。我於過去三無數劫由精進力所修加行速得圓滿。疾證無上正等菩提。念已發生殊勝歡喜。由斯喜力背痛便愈。尋起整衣結跏趺坐。告阿難曰汝為大眾說精進耶。阿難白佛。唯然世尊佛讚彼曰善哉善哉。我由精進速證菩提。汝今說之故我歡喜。讚應時說非受供養。問毘奈耶說復云何通。如說阿難我今增益出離善法極生歡喜。若不受他法供養者如何增長出離善法。答佛以他事為己事故。他善法增便作是說謂有情類多依佛法淨信出家。受具足戒誦持三藏居阿練若寂靜思惟。入正決定得果離欲乃至漏盡或種生天解脫種子。佛知是事甚大歡喜作如是念。無量有情以我威力世出世間善法增長。彼之所作即是我事深可慶喜故作是說。然佛世尊定不於他受法供養。法身功德極圓滿故。生身必待衣食等資故有於他受財供養。云何身力乃至廣說。問何故作此論答為止他宗顯正義故。謂或有執。身力身劣無別自體。

Ru-Yi Translation

exactly as the Buddha praised Śāriputra, saying: "you are able to expound the Dharma like the roar of a lion; what you have spoken is a determined teaching." One asks: how is what a further sūtra passage states to be reconciled? As it is stated: the Buddha said to Ānanda, "excellent, excellent! As you have said, through diligence one swiftly realizes unsurpassed enlightenment — hearing your words, I feel deep joy." The answer: the Buddha spoke these words because Ānanda's own words happened to be apt to that moment, not because he received the Dharma. That is, the Blessed One, having once traveled a long road for the sake of transforming sentient beings, grew weary, and his back ached, so he approached a tree, and, folding a robe of seven strips fourfold as bedding, and covering his body with a robe of five strips, resting his head on his outer robe, lay down on his right side like a lion king, and said to Ānanda: you should expound the Dharma to the monks — you should not let the time pass in vain. 6. Formal Definition, and Refuting All Three Rivals Through a Single Wrestling Simile

The treatise now defines bodily strength and weakness formally, classifies them under the sense-object of touch and under body- and mental consciousness, and delivers its refutation of all three rival positions through a single vivid image: two wrestlers whose wrists, the instant they cross, immediately reveal which is stronger — a fact no amount of mental power, diligence, or unfixed relative comparison alone could explain, since only a genuine, determinate physical substance could be known that instantly through mere touch.

The claim that even the Buddha's own physical strength must decline with age, despite his Dharma-body's untouched perfection, is a quietly significant piece of doctrine: it refuses to let devotion collapse the crucial Sarvāstivāda distinction between the compounded, karma-ripened physical body and the perfection the Buddha has actually realized, insisting that reverence for the latter need never require denying the plain physical facts of the former.

14. The Ten Powers, Concluded: Karma, Knowledge of the Unmanifest, and Why Only a Buddha Holds Them

The analysis continues through wholesomeness, the three realms, the learner/beyond-training distinction, and what is abandoned by seeing versus cultivation, before the treatise addresses a genuinely puzzling technical question — how can even unmanifest karma, invisible in principle, ever be known? — answered by reasoning from fruit back to cause, from the coarse to the subtle, from what is directly seen to what is not.
