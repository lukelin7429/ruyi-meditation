A New Chapter: Existence, Rebirth, and the Four Fruits

Fascicle 147 opens the Existence Nyāsa, tracing what a being first obtains at conception across the three realms, whether contemplating one realm can liberate another, and closing with an exhaustive census of what is gained, relinquished, possessed, and cut off at each of the four noble fruits. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 147 complete, 17 sections Source: Taishō T1545, vol. 27, fascicle 147 Topic: 根蘊第六中有納息第二之一 begins (the Faculties Aggregate; the Existence Nyāsa opens)

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 147 opens the Existence Nyāsa (有納息第二之一), a new chapter within the Sixth Aggregate following the conclusion of the Faculty Nyāsa in fascicle 146. Terms established across fascicles 93–146 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

Classical text is set in the left-hand column below, our English in the right.

1. A New Chapter Opens: What Is 'Existence,' and What Does a Fetus First Obtain?

Fascicle 147 opens an entirely new nyāsa within the Faculties Aggregate — the Existence Nyāsa — defining "existence" as the continuity of a sentient being's own five aggregates across rebirth, and asking how many karma-produced faculties a being first obtains in that continuity. Egg-born, womb-born, and moisture-born beings obtain only two at conception, body and life, prompting a searching debate over whether the earliest embryonic kalala stage secretly already contains the seeds of every other faculty at once.

Chinese Source

欲有相續最初得幾業所生根如是等章及解章義。既領會已。當廣分別。然有聲目多義。如前廣說。此中說續眾同分有情數五蘊名有。

Ru-Yi Translation

"In desire-existence, in the continuity of rebirth, how many karma-produced faculties are first obtained," and so on — such are the chapter headings, and such is the explanation of their meaning. Having grasped this, a full analysis should now be given. Yet the term "existence" carries many senses, exactly as explained at length earlier. Here, "existence" refers specifically to the continuity of a sentient being's own class of aggregates, comprising the five aggregates that make up a living being.

相續有五。亦如前說。此中依二相續作論。謂中有相續生有相續欲有相續最初得幾業所生根。答卵生胎生濕生。得二。謂身根命根。

Continuity itself is fivefold, again exactly as explained earlier. This passage frames its question around two of these five kinds of continuity — the continuity of the intermediate state and the continuity of rebirth-existence. "In desire-existence, in the continuity of rebirth, how many karma-produced faculties are first obtained?" Answer: for egg-born, womb-born, and moisture-born beings, two are obtained — the body-faculty and the life-faculty.

問最初羯邏藍位亦得餘色根不。若得者。如何於少時頃便得爾所根耶。又此中何故不說。毘奈耶說復云何通。如說。於母腹中二根初得。謂身與命。若損害彼。乃至廣說。若不得者。何故經說天眼觀知是男是女。答有言亦得。問如何於少時頃便得爾所根耶。答爾時雖無諸色根相而已具得彼根種子。如清鹽水酥蜜沙糖酒等。和合貯在一器。若以草端霑取一渧。於中具有鹽等諸味。羯邏藍位應知亦爾。一切色根種子皆具。問若爾。何故此中不說答應說而不說者。

One might ask: at the very first, embryonic kalala stage, does a being also obtain the remaining form-faculties? If so, how could so many faculties possibly be obtained in so brief a span of time? And why, moreover, does this passage fail to mention it — how, then, is the Vinaya's own statement to be reconciled, where it says that in the mother's womb, two faculties are obtained at first, the body and the life-faculty, and that if these are harmed, and so on, as stated at length? But if the remaining form-faculties are not obtained at that stage, why does the sūtra say that the divine eye can observe and know whether the fetus is male or female? Answer: some say the remaining form-faculties are indeed obtained. One might then ask: how could so many faculties possibly be obtained in so brief a span of time? Answer: at that stage, although none of the form-faculties' own marks are yet manifest, their seeds have already been fully obtained — exactly as pure salt water, ghee, honey, granulated sugar, and wine, all mixed together and stored in a single vessel, will yield, if one dips the tip of a blade of grass into it and draws out a single drop, a drop that fully contains the taste of the salt and every other ingredient at once. The kalala stage should be understood in just the same way — the seeds of every form-faculty are already fully present there. One might then ask: if so, why does this passage fail to mention it? Answer: it ought to have been mentioned, and simply was not;

當知此義有餘。有說。此中說皆得者。餘得不定。是以不說。如生盲等不得眼等。問毘奈耶說復云何通。答彼說能持諸餘根者。謂身根能持諸餘色根。命根能持餘非色根。是故偏說。有說。此位不得餘色根問天眼云何觀知男女。答羯邏藍時雖無男女根。而有男女相。由觀彼相得知男女。所以者何。非彼已有男女二根可說觀故。

one should understand that there is a further point of meaning here still to be drawn out. Another position holds that this passage, in saying "obtained," means obtained by everyone without exception; since what is obtained by some but not others is not fixed, it is therefore left unmentioned — just as one born blind, and the like, do not obtain the eye-faculty and so forth. One might ask: how, then, is the Vinaya's own statement to be reconciled? Answer: that passage speaks of what sustains all the remaining faculties — the body-faculty sustaining all the remaining form-faculties, and the life-faculty sustaining all the remaining non-form-faculties — hence it singles these two out for special mention. Another position holds that at this stage the remaining form-faculties are not yet obtained at all. One might then ask: how, then, can the divine eye observe and know whether the fetus is male or female? Answer: at the kalala stage, although the male and female faculties themselves are not yet present, the male and female characteristics already are, and it is by observing these characteristics that one comes to know the sex — for it is not the case that the two faculties themselves, male and female, are already present and thereby observed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The image of a single drop drawn from a jar of mixed salt water, ghee, honey, sugar, and wine — a drop that somehow carries the taste of every ingredient at once — is one of this session's most vivid similes: it lets the treatise claim that a kalala embryo, though visibly possessing almost nothing yet, already contains the seeds of every faculty it will eventually display, compressed into something too small to show any of them individually.

2. How the Divine Eye Sees a Fetus's Sex Before Any Sex-Faculty Exists

The debate sharpens around a genuine puzzle: if the male and female faculties are not yet present in the earliest embryo, how can the divine eye see whether a fetus is male or female at all? Four different resolutions are offered — perhaps the faculties genuinely are already present as latent seeds; perhaps only the physical characteristics are visible, not the faculties themselves; perhaps the sūtra's own description of fetal posture settles the matter; or perhaps the divine eye simply observes the intermediate-state being's own final moment, before it ever enters the womb at all.

Chinese Source

有餘師說。依經故知。經說。若胎是男。依母右脇向背蹲坐。若胎是女。依母左脇向腹蹲坐。得天眼者。觀此差別依經而說。或有說者。觀中有知。謂天眼觀中有後位。若是男子。入於母胎。知此羯邏藍是男非女。若女入者。復知此位是女非男。如是說者。羯邏藍位未得餘色根。鉢羅奢佉位中方乃得故。化生得六或七或八。無形者六。謂眼耳鼻舌身命根。一形者七。謂前六及男女根隨一。二形者八謂前六及男女根。問餘無色根爾時亦得。謂意五受信等五根。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet another master holds that this is known through the sūtra: the sūtra states that if the fetus is male, it crouches leaning against the mother's right flank, facing her back; if the fetus is female, it crouches leaning against the mother's left flank, facing her belly — and one who has obtained the divine eye observes this very difference, speaking of it on the sūtra's own authority. Still others hold that this is known by observing the intermediate state: the divine eye observes the intermediate-state being at its final phase, and if it is a male about to enter the mother's womb, one thereby knows that this kalala is male, not female; if a female is entering, one likewise knows that this stage is female, not male. On this account, at the kalala stage the remaining form-faculties have not yet been obtained at all, only coming to be obtained at the later praśākhā stage. A being of transformation-birth obtains six, seven, or eight faculties at once: one without any bodily form obtains six — the eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body faculties, and the life-faculty; one of a single sexual form obtains seven — the preceding six, plus whichever one of the male or female faculty applies; one of dual sexual form obtains eight — the preceding six, plus both the male and female faculties. One might then ask: are the remaining formless faculties also obtained at this same moment — namely the mind-faculty, the five feeling-faculties, and faith with its four companions?

此中何故不說。答此中應說而不說者。當知有餘有說。爾時一切得者。此中則說。餘無色根。雖有得者而非一切。是故不說。謂上地歿生下地時。雖得彼根。若自地歿還生自地。彼皆不得。是故不說。有說。此中但問初得業所生者。初受生位餘無色根。雖有得者而非業生。故此不說。後位所得雖業所生。而非初得。故亦不說。

Why does this passage fail to mention them? Answer: this too ought to have been mentioned, and simply was not; one should understand that there is a further point of meaning here still to be drawn out. Another position holds that this passage speaks only of what is obtained by everyone without exception at that moment; the remaining formless faculties, although obtained by some, are not obtained by all — as when a being dies in a higher realm and takes rebirth in a lower one, that being does obtain the corresponding faculty there, yet a being who dies in its own realm and is reborn again into that very same realm does not newly obtain it — hence it is left unmentioned. Another position holds that this passage asks specifically about what is first obtained as karma-produced; at the very moment of first taking rebirth, although the remaining formless faculties may indeed be obtained, they are not themselves karma-produced at that moment, hence they are not mentioned here; and whatever is obtained at a later stage, although it may indeed be karma-produced, is not itself a first obtaining, hence that too is left unmentioned.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Watching this tradition wrestle honestly with a question that has no single settled answer — offering four genuinely different, uncombined solutions rather than forcing a false consensus — is a useful corrective to any assumption that Abhidharma commentary always converges on one official position: sometimes the most intellectually honest move is simply to preserve several live possibilities side by side.

3. Six Faculties for a Form-Realm Rebirth, One for a Formless Rebirth

The census concludes with form-realm and formless-realm rebirths — six faculties obtained at once for the former, a single, solitary life-faculty for the latter — before the fascicle turns to an entirely new investigation, structurally reminiscent of the seeing/cultivation census many fascicles earlier: can contemplating dharmas bound to one realm bring full and exhaustive knowledge of that very realm, or of a different one entirely?

Chinese Source

色有相續最初得幾業所生根。答六。謂眼耳鼻舌身命根。無色有相續最初得幾業所生根。答一。謂命根。頗思惟欲界繫法。遍知欲界耶。乃至廣說。此中思惟者。是取所緣義。遍知者。是究竟斷義。

Ru-Yi Translation

"In form-existence, in the continuity of rebirth, how many karma-produced faculties are first obtained?" Answer: six — the eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body faculties, and the life-faculty. "In formless-existence, in the continuity of rebirth, how many karma-produced faculties are first obtained?" Answer: one — the life-faculty. "Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the desire realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the desire realm, and so on, as stated at length?" Here, "contemplates" carries the sense of grasping something as an object; "full and exhaustive knowledge" carries the sense of cutting something off completely, once and for all.

此文顯示緣彼彼界法離彼彼界染。或不能離。

This passage shows that taking the dharmas of one or another realm as one's object can, in some cases, free one from the contaminations of that very realm, and in other cases cannot.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That formless-realm rebirth requires obtaining only the bare life-faculty and nothing else is a striking minimalism: an entire mode of sentient existence, on this account, can be built on the single thinnest thread of continuity, with every other faculty simply absent rather than merely reduced.

4. Contemplating One Realm Can Never Liberate You From Another

The investigation works methodically through all nine possible realm-pairings: the desire realm can only liberate itself; the form realm can liberate both itself and, through the path of liberation alone, the desire realm below it; the formless realm, reserved for noble ones only, can liberate itself and, again only through the path of liberation, the form realm below it — but no realm can ever liberate one bound to a realm above it, and the desire and formless realms, being at opposite extremes, can never liberate each other at all.

Chinese Source

頗思惟欲界繫法。遍知欲界耶。答遍知此通異生及聖者。通無間道及解脫道。若世俗道離欲染時。九無間道緣欲界法離欲界染。若無漏道苦集法智離欲染時。九無間道九解脫道。緣欲界法離欲界染。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the desire realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the desire realm?" Answer: yes, one can — this holds true both for the ordinary being and for the noble one, and applies to both the uninterrupted path and the path of liberation. When freedom from desire-realm contamination is achieved through the mundane path, nine uninterrupted paths take the dharmas of the desire realm as their object in freeing one from desire-realm contamination; when freedom from desire-realm contamination is achieved through the uncontaminated path — by means of the dharma-knowledge and dharma-patient-acceptance of suffering and of origin — nine uninterrupted paths and nine paths of liberation take the dharmas of the desire realm as their object in freeing one from desire-realm contamination.

頗思惟欲界繫法。遍知色界耶。答不遍知。頗思惟欲界繫法。遍知無色界耶。答不遍知。問何故俱不遍知。答欲界是不定界。非修地非離染地。色無色界是定界。是修地是離染地。非緣不定界非修非離染地法。能離定界修地離染地染。有說。欲界是麁界色無色界是細界。非緣麁界法能離細界染。有說。欲界是下界。色界是中界。

"Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the desire realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the form realm?" Answer: no, one cannot. "Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the desire realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the formless realm?" Answer: no, one cannot. One might ask: why can neither be attained? Answer: the desire realm is an unfixed realm, neither a realm of cultivation nor a realm from which contamination is removed, whereas the form and formless realms are fixed realms, realms of cultivation, realms from which contamination is removed — and one cannot, by taking as object dharmas of an unfixed realm that is neither a realm of cultivation nor a realm of removed contamination, thereby remove the contamination of a fixed realm, a realm of cultivation, a realm of removed contamination. Another position holds that the desire realm is a coarse realm, while the form and formless realms are subtle realms — one cannot, by taking as object the dharmas of a coarse realm, thereby remove the contamination of a subtle realm. Another position holds that the desire realm is a lower realm, the form realm a middle realm,

無色界是妙界。非緣下界法能離中妙界染。有說。欲界是劣界。色無色界是勝界。非緣劣界法能離勝界染。由此等義故不遍知。頗思惟色界繫法。遍知色界耶。答遍知。此通異生及聖者。通無間道及解脫道。若世俗道離色染時。九無間道緣色界法離色界染。若無漏道苦集類智。離色染時。九無間道九解脫道。緣色界法離色界染。

and the formless realm a sublime realm — one cannot, by taking as object the dharmas of a lower realm, thereby remove the contamination of a middle or sublime realm. Another position holds that the desire realm is an inferior realm, while the form and formless realms are superior realms — one cannot, by taking as object the dharmas of an inferior realm, thereby remove the contamination of a superior realm. It is for reasons such as these that full and exhaustive knowledge cannot be attained in either case. "Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the form realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the form realm?" Answer: yes, one can — this holds true both for the ordinary being and for the noble one, and applies to both the uninterrupted path and the path of liberation. When freedom from form-realm contamination is achieved through the mundane path, nine uninterrupted paths take the dharmas of the form realm as their object in freeing one from form-realm contamination; when freedom from form-realm contamination is achieved through the uncontaminated path — by means of the class-knowledge and class-patient-acceptance of suffering and of origin — nine uninterrupted paths and nine paths of liberation take the dharmas of the form realm as their object in freeing one from form-realm contamination.

頗思惟色界繫法。遍知欲界耶。答遍知。此通異生及聖者。唯解脫道非無間道。謂世俗道離欲染時。九解脫道緣色界法離欲界染。

"Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the form realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the desire realm?" Answer: yes, one can — this holds true both for the ordinary being and for the noble one, but only through the path of liberation, never the uninterrupted path — for when freedom from desire-realm contamination is achieved through the mundane path, nine paths of liberation take the dharmas of the form realm as their object in freeing one from desire-realm contamination.

頗思惟色界繫法。遍知無色界耶。答不遍知。問何故不遍知。答色界是麁界無色界是細界非緣麁界法能離細界染。有說。色界是中界。無色界是妙界。非緣中界法能離妙界染。有說。色界是劣界。

"Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the form realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the formless realm?" Answer: no, one cannot. One might ask: why can this not be attained? Answer: the form realm is a coarse realm, while the formless realm is a subtle realm — one cannot, by taking as object the dharmas of a coarse realm, thereby remove the contamination of a subtle realm. Another position holds that the form realm is a middle realm, while the formless realm is a sublime realm — one cannot, by taking as object the dharmas of a middle realm, thereby remove the contamination of a sublime realm. Another position holds that the form realm is an inferior realm, while the formless realm

無色界是勝界。非緣劣界法能離勝界染。由此等義故不遍知。

is a superior realm — one cannot, by taking as object the dharmas of an inferior realm, thereby remove the contamination of a superior realm. It is for reasons such as these that full and exhaustive knowledge cannot be attained here.

頗思惟無色界繫法。遍知無色界耶。答遍知。此唯聖者非異生。通無間道及解脫道。謂無漏道苦集類智離無色染時。九無間道九解脫道。緣無色界法離無色界染。

"Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the formless realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the formless realm?" Answer: yes, one can — but this holds true only for the noble one, never the ordinary being, and applies to both the uninterrupted path and the path of liberation. This is because, when freedom from formless-realm contamination is achieved through the uncontaminated path — by means of the class-knowledge and class-patient-acceptance of suffering and of origin — nine uninterrupted paths and nine paths of liberation take the dharmas of the formless realm as their object in freeing one from formless-realm contamination.

頗思惟無色界繫法。遍知欲界耶。答不遍知。問何故不遍知。答以極遠故。非觀極遠界法能離極遠界染。

"Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the formless realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the desire realm?" Answer: no, one cannot. One might ask: why can this not be attained? Answer: because the two realms are simply too far apart from one another — one cannot, by observing the dharmas of a realm at the very furthest extreme, thereby remove the contamination of a realm at the opposite furthest extreme.

頗思惟無色界繫法。遍知色界耶。答遍知。此通異生及聖者。唯解脫道非無間道。謂世俗道離色染時。九解脫道緣無色界法離色界染。

"Can one who contemplates dharmas bound to the formless realm thereby attain full and exhaustive knowledge of the form realm?" Answer: yes, one can — this holds true both for the ordinary being and for the noble one, but only through the path of liberation, never the uninterrupted path — for when freedom from form-realm contamination is achieved through the mundane path, nine paths of liberation take the dharmas of the formless realm as their object in freeing one from form-realm contamination.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The three parallel explanations offered for why a lower realm can never liberate a higher one — coarse cannot dislodge subtle, low cannot dislodge sublime, inferior cannot dislodge superior — read almost as three restatements of a single geometric intuition: liberation, on this account, always moves from a more refined vantage point looking down, never from a cruder one reaching up, and the desire and formless realms sit at such extreme opposite ends of that scale that neither can ever reach the other at all.

5. Counting the Faculties That Actually Do the Liberating Work

Having settled which realms can liberate which, the treatise counts precisely how many faculties perform this liberating work in each case — seven by the mundane path and eight by the uncontaminated path for the desire realm, seven and ten for the form realm, and a full eleven for the formless realm, with a careful qualification about which faculty serves as the final uninterrupted path or path of liberation depending on whether one is working from the fundamental meditative level or its proximate access-level neighbor.

Chinese Source

幾根遍知欲界。答世俗道七。無漏道八。世俗道七者。謂意捨信等五根。無漏道八者。謂前七及已知根。

Ru-Yi Translation

How many faculties achieve full and exhaustive knowledge of the desire realm? Answer: seven by the mundane path, eight by the uncontaminated path. The seven of the mundane path are the mind-faculty, equanimity, and faith with its four companions; the eight of the uncontaminated path are these same seven plus the already-known faculty.

幾根遍知色界。答世俗道七。無漏道十。世俗道七者。謂意捨信等五根。無漏道十者。謂前七及喜樂已知根。

How many faculties achieve full and exhaustive knowledge of the form realm? Answer: seven by the mundane path, ten by the uncontaminated path. The seven of the mundane path are the mind-faculty, equanimity, and faith with its four companions; the ten of the uncontaminated path are these same seven plus joy, pleasure, and the already-known faculty.

幾根遍知無色界。答十一。謂前十及具知根。已知根為無間道。具知根為解脫道。當知此依最後位說。問離欲界染時最後解脫道。容有根本初靜慮現前。彼以捨根為無間道。喜根為解脫道。何故不依最後位說世俗道或八。無漏道或九耶。答此文應作是說。世俗道七或八。無漏道八或九。而不說者。當知有餘。有說。此中說決定者。謂離非想非非想處染時。定以具知根為最後解脫道。離欲界染時。非定以喜根為最後解脫道。有於爾時不能即入根本地故。然於爾時多依近分故唯說捨。

How many faculties achieve full and exhaustive knowledge of the formless realm? Answer: eleven — the preceding ten plus the faculty of complete knowledge, with the already-known faculty serving as the uninterrupted path and the faculty of complete knowledge serving as the path of liberation; one should understand this is stated with reference to the very final stage. One might ask: at the very final path of liberation in becoming free from desire-realm contamination, it is entirely possible for the fundamental first dhyāna itself to manifest, in which case equanimity serves as the uninterrupted path and joy serves as the path of liberation — why, then, does this passage not state the mundane path as seven or eight, and the uncontaminated path as eight or nine, with reference to that very final stage? Answer: this passage ought to have been phrased as: the mundane path is seven or eight, the uncontaminated path is eight or nine — and that it was not so phrased should be understood as a further point still to be drawn out. Another position holds that this passage speaks only of what is fixed and certain: at freedom from the contamination of the peak of existence, the faculty of complete knowledge is fixedly the very final path of liberation, whereas at freedom from desire-realm contamination, joy is not fixedly the final path of liberation, since at that moment one is sometimes unable to enter the fundamental level at all — and since at that moment reliance is placed for the most part on the proximate concentration alone, only equanimity is mentioned.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The rising count from seven-or-eight up through eleven as the realm being liberated grows more refined is a small numerical signature of a larger pattern this fascicle keeps returning to: subtler achievements recruit a wider supporting cast of faculties, not a narrower one, contrary to what a simpler picture of 'refinement equals reduction' might predict.

6. Which Faculties Vanish for Good, and Which Faculties Mark Each of the Four Fruits

The fascicle's final blocks work out the precise logical relationship between each pair of terms: every suffering-knowledge is indeed an uncontaminated knowledge of suffering, but not every uncontaminated knowledge of suffering is itself suffering-knowledge, since origin-knowledge can also take suffering as its object; the same asymmetry holds in reverse for origin-knowledge, while cessation-knowledge and path-knowledge each match their own uncontaminated knowledge perfectly, in both directions, without exception.

Ru-Yi Translation

"Is every suffering-knowledge itself an uncontaminated knowledge of suffering? And, conversely, is every uncontaminated knowledge of suffering itself a suffering-knowledge?" Answer: every suffering-knowledge is indeed an uncontaminated knowledge of suffering, since uncontaminated knowledge, when it operates through the four aspects of impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self with regard to the matter of suffering, is called suffering-knowledge. But there exists uncontaminated knowledge of suffering that is not itself suffering-knowledge — namely, the origin-knowledge that knows suffering, since uncontaminated knowledge, when it operates through the four aspects of cause, origin, arising, and condition with regard to the matter of suffering, is called origin-knowledge. It has already been explained that there is no origin apart from suffering, and it is for this reason that origin-knowledge's own object is also called "suffering."

"Is every cessation-knowledge itself an uncontaminated knowledge of cessation?" Answer: yes, it is. "And, conversely, is every uncontaminated knowledge of cessation itself a cessation-knowledge?" Answer: yes, it is. "Is every path-knowledge itself an uncontaminated knowledge of the path?" Answer: yes, it is. "And, conversely, is every uncontaminated knowledge of the path itself a path-knowledge?" Answer: yes, it is. This is because it has already been explained that the aspects and objects of cessation-knowledge and path-knowledge are both unmixed with one another. If uncontaminated knowledge takes the cessation-through-analysis as its object and operates through the four aspects of cessation, calm, sublimity, and definitive departure, it is called cessation-knowledge; if uncontaminated knowledge takes the noble path as its object and operates through the four aspects of the path, the correct method, accomplishment, and deliverance, it is called path-knowledge.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That cessation-knowledge and path-knowledge alone achieve a perfectly symmetrical, two-way equivalence with their own uncontaminated knowledge — while suffering-knowledge and origin-knowledge each only manage a one-way implication — is the fascicle's own quietly elegant closing payoff: the earlier explanation of shared objects for suffering and origin turns out to predict, with exact precision, exactly where the logical symmetry breaks down and exactly where it holds perfectly intact.
