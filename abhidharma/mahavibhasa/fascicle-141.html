What Every Factor of Awakening Actually Is

Fascicle 141 gives the complete self-nature and etymology of every one of the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment, the four dhyānas, the eight liberations, the ten totalities, and the five supernormal penetrations — including the story of Ānanda and the testing brahmin, and why these powers follow no fixed sequence at all. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. 1. What Is Mindfulness, Really? One Substance Behind Four Names

Fascicle 141 turns from fascicle 140's exhaustive numerical ledger to something more foundational: the actual self-nature and etymology of each of the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment already tallied there. The four foundations of mindfulness turn out, beneath their fourfold appearance, to be a single substance — wisdom itself — divided by object into body, feeling, mind, and dharmas, exactly as a single wisdom-faculty, wisdom-power, discrimination-of-dharmas awakening-factor, and correct view are all, at bottom, the very same underlying reality wearing five different institutional hats.

Chinese Source

大種蘊第五中執受納息第四之五四念住者。一身念住。二受念住。三心念住。四法念住。然此念住總說唯一。謂心所中一慧自性。根中慧根。力中慧力。覺支中擇法覺支。道支中正見。或分為二。謂有漏無漏。或分為三。謂下中上。或分為四。謂三界繫及不繫。或分為五。謂三界繫及學無學。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth Aggregate, the Great Elements: the Grasped Nyāsa, Chapter Four, Part Five. As for the four foundations of mindfulness: first, the foundation of mindfulness of the body; second, of feeling; third, of mind; fourth, of dharmas. Yet, speaking in the most general terms, this foundation of mindfulness is but a single thing — namely, among the mental factors, the single self-nature of wisdom; among the faculties, the wisdom-faculty; among the powers, the wisdom-power; among the awakening-factors, the discrimination-of-dharmas awakening-factor; among the path-factors, correct view. It may be divided into two — contaminated and uncontaminated; or into three — lower, middling, and upper; or into four — bound to the three realms, and unbound; or into five — bound to the three realms, and belonging to the trainee or the one beyond training;

乃至若以相續剎那差別分別則有無量。問世尊何故於此義中。開少合多唯說四種。答佛依正慧行相所緣。麁細不同建立四種。問若爾何故契經中說觀內外俱十二種別。答不過四故但說四種。如七葉樹七生預流。彼亦如是。問此體是慧何故世尊說為念住。答慧由念力得住所緣故名念住。或此慧力令念住境故名念住。此二於境展轉相資勝於餘法故說念住。廣辯念住如餘處說。

— and so on, up through, if distinguished by continuum and by moment, it would admit of countless divisions. Question: why does the World-Honored One, in this particular context, open up what is few into many and gather what is many into few, speaking of only four kinds? Answer: the Buddha, on the basis of correct wisdom's own aspect and object, coarse or fine, establishes these four kinds. Question: if so, why does the sūtra elsewhere speak of contemplating the internal and the external together as twelve distinct kinds? Answer: since these never exceed four in kind, only four kinds are spoken of here — exactly as the seven-leaved tree and the seven lifetimes of a stream-enterer are likewise so. Question: this self-nature is wisdom — why, then, does the World-Honored One call it a "foundation of mindfulness"? Answer: it is by the power of mindfulness that wisdom comes to rest upon its object, and hence it is called a "foundation of mindfulness"; or, it is by the power of this wisdom that mindfulness comes to rest upon its object, and hence it is called a "foundation of mindfulness" — these two, mutually sustaining one another with respect to their object, surpassing every other dharma in this regard, are what give this its name. The foundations of mindfulness are explained more fully elsewhere.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the Buddha is described as deliberately "opening up what is few into many, and gathering what is many into few" — choosing four categories neither too coarse nor too fine — reveals something important about how this tradition understands its own teaching method: a classification's own size is not a fact about reality waiting to be discovered, but a pedagogical choice made at exactly the grain size a listener can actually absorb and act upon.

2. The Four Right Efforts: One Diligence, Four Simultaneous Functions

The four right efforts receive the identical treatment: a single substance, diligence, exercising four distinct functions within a single moment — cutting off unwholesome dharmas already arisen, preventing those not yet arisen, generating wholesome dharmas not yet arisen, and expanding those already present — compared vividly to a single lamp that simultaneously burns its wick, consumes its oil, heats its vessel, and dispels darkness, all through one continuous flame.

Chinese Source

四正斷者。一於已生惡不善法為令斷故。生欲發懃攝心持心。二於未生惡不善法為不生故。餘說如前。三於未生善法為令生故。餘說如前。四於已生善法為令安住不忘倍修增廣故。餘說如前。然此正斷或總為一。謂心所中一精進體。根中精進根。力中精進力。覺支中精進覺支道支中正精進。或分為二。謂有漏無漏。或分為三。謂下中上。或分為四。謂三界繫及不繫。或分為五。謂三界繫及學無學。乃至若以相續剎那差別分別則有無量。問世尊何故於此義中開少合多唯說四種。答一精進體於剎那中。作用不同建立四種。謂於已生未生惡法斷及不生故。復於未生已生善法生及增廣故。如燈一念四用差別。謂燒炷盡油熱器破闇彼亦如是。問法蘊等論說。斷已生惡不善法。即具四種。謂於已生惡不善法為令斷故。生欲發懃攝持心者。彼斷已生惡不善位。亦能令彼惡不善法未生不生。復令善法未生得生已生住等。乃至說修已生善法令安住等亦具有四。謂於已生善法為令安住不忘倍修增廣故。生欲發懃攝持心者。彼修已生諸善法位。亦能令彼惡不善法已生者斷。未生不生。復令善法未生者生。如是便成十六正斷。何故於此但說四耶。答依修行者差別意樂。至加行位故作是說。謂彼先時起一意樂。至加行位便具四種。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the four right efforts: first, with respect to unwholesome, evil dharmas already arisen, giving rise to the wish to cut them off, exerting diligence, gathering the mind, and sustaining the mind; second, with respect to unwholesome, evil dharmas not yet arisen, doing so in order that they not arise, the rest explained as before; third, with respect to wholesome dharmas not yet arisen, doing so in order that they arise, the rest explained as before; fourth, with respect to wholesome dharmas already arisen, doing so in order that they remain firmly established, are never forgotten, and are doubly cultivated and expanded, the rest explained as before. Yet these right efforts, taken together, are but a single thing — namely, among the mental factors, the single self-nature of diligence; among the faculties, the diligence-faculty; among the powers, the diligence-power; among the awakening-factors, the diligence awakening-factor; among the path-factors, correct effort. They may be divided into two — contaminated and uncontaminated; into three — lower, middling, upper; into four — bound to the three realms, and unbound; into five — bound to the three realms, and belonging to the trainee or the one beyond training; and so on, up through, if distinguished by continuum and by moment, admitting of countless divisions. Question: why does the World-Honored One, in this context, open up what is few into many and gather what is many into few, speaking of only four kinds? Answer: the single substance of diligence, within a single instant, exercises differing functions, and it is on this basis that these four kinds are established — that is, with respect to unwholesome dharmas already or not yet arisen, cutting them off or preventing their arising; and, with respect to wholesome dharmas not yet or already arisen, giving rise to them or expanding them — exactly as a single lamp, in a single instant, performs four distinct functions at once: burning the wick, consuming the oil, heating the vessel, and dispelling darkness — just so is it here. Question: the Dharma-Aggregation treatise and the rest say that cutting off unwholesome dharmas already arisen already possesses all four in full — that is, with respect to unwholesome, evil dharmas already arisen, giving rise to the wish to cut them off, exerting diligence, gathering and sustaining the mind — since, at the very stage of cutting off what has already arisen, one is also thereby capable of preventing what has not yet arisen from arising, and of causing wholesome dharmas not yet arisen to arise, and those already arisen to remain and the rest; and, likewise, cultivating wholesome dharmas already arisen so that they remain firmly established and the rest — that is, with respect to wholesome dharmas already arisen, giving rise to the wish that they remain firmly established, are never forgotten, and are doubly cultivated and expanded, exerting diligence, gathering and sustaining the mind — since, at the very stage of cultivating wholesome dharmas already arisen, one is also thereby capable of cutting off unwholesome dharmas already arisen, preventing those not yet arisen from arising, and causing wholesome dharmas not yet arisen to arise as well — such that this would then amount to sixteen right efforts in all. Why, then, does this passage speak of only four? Answer: this is spoken with reference to the practitioner's own particular resolve at the very stage of preparatory practice — that is, one who has earlier given rise to a single resolve, upon reaching the stage of preparatory practice, comes to possess all four in full.

如是依彼四種意樂。至加行位故作是說。然加行時皆唯有四。不過四故但說四種。如由意樂加行故說。如是由趣入加行故說。由依處加行故說。由勝解加行故說應知亦爾。已說自性當說所以。問此四何緣說為正斷。答由此四種能正斷故。問前二可爾。後二云何。答以初為名故無有失。或此四種皆有斷義。謂前二斷煩惱障。後二斷所知障。修善法時斷無知故。暫斷永斷俱名斷故。有處說此名為正勝。無倒策發成勝事故。

It is in just the same way, with reference to each of the four resolves in turn, that this is spoken at the stage of preparatory practice — yet, at the actual moment of preparatory practice, there are always precisely four, never more, and hence only four kinds are spoken of. Just as this is said with reference to resolve and preparatory practice, so too, in exactly the same way, should it be understood with reference to the practice of entering in, the practice of one's own basis, and the practice of resolute understanding. Having now explained their self-nature, we should explain why they are so named. Question: for what reason are these four called "right efforts"? Answer: because these four are capable of correctly cutting off. Question: this makes sense for the first two — but what of the latter two? Answer: they are named after the first, and hence no fault follows; or, all four alike carry the sense of "cutting off" — the first two cutting off the obstruction of affliction, the latter two cutting off the obstruction of what is knowable, since, in cultivating wholesome dharmas, one cuts off ignorance as well, and both a temporary and a permanent cutting-off alike deserve the name "cutting off." In some passages, these are instead called "correct exertions," since, spurred on without any inversion, they bring about a superior undertaking.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The lamp image does real philosophical work beyond mere illustration: it shows how a single, genuinely unified cause can produce four functionally distinct effects without needing to be divided into four separate substances to do so — diligence remains one thing throughout, its apparent fourfold nature simply naming four different relationships it bears to what surrounds it.

3. Evil and Unwholesome: A Fine Distinction, and Six Ways to Explain 'Already Arisen'

A careful technical distinction separates "evil" (obscured-yet-neutral dharmas, or the desire realm's own view of self and extreme-grasping view) from "unwholesome" (everything else defiled) — followed by a genuinely rich cascade of explanations for why already-arisen evil dharmas are specifically "cut off" while those not yet arisen are merely "prevented from arising": already exercising a function within one's own continuum versus not yet doing so, already obstructing the noble path versus not yet, already having taken and given a result versus not yet, and several further variations on this same basic theme.

Chinese Source

問惡與不善何差別。答惡謂有覆無記。不善謂諸不善。有作是說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: what is the difference between "evil" and "unwholesome"? Answer: "evil" refers to what is obscured-yet-neutral; "unwholesome" refers to what is genuinely unwholesome. Some say:

惡謂色無色界。及欲界少分染法。不善謂欲界多分染法。有說。惡謂欲界身邊二見品。不善謂欲界諸餘煩惱等。問何故惡不善法已生者說令斷。未生者說令不生。答已生者於自相續。已有作用故說令斷。未生者於自相續。未有作用故說令不生。有說。已生者於自相續。已障聖道故說令斷。未生者於自相續。未障聖道故說令不生。

— "evil" refers to the defiled dharmas of the form and formless realms, and a portion of the desire realm's own defiled dharmas; "unwholesome" refers to the greater portion of the desire realm's own defiled dharmas. Others say: "evil" refers to the desire realm's own view of a real self and extreme-grasping view; "unwholesome" refers to every other affliction of the desire realm. Question: why are already-arisen evil and unwholesome dharmas said to be "cut off," while those not yet arisen are said to be "prevented from arising"? Answer: what has already arisen already exercises its own function within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "cut off"; what has not yet arisen has not yet exercised any function within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "prevented from arising." Others say: what has already arisen has already obstructed the noble path within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "cut off"; what has not yet arisen has not yet obstructed the noble path within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "prevented from arising."

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Offering half a dozen genuinely distinct explanations for a single, fairly narrow terminological choice — why "cut off" applies to the past-arisen and "prevented" to the not-yet-arisen — shows this treatise's own conviction that even small linguistic choices in scripture are never accidental: a difference in verb choice this minor is still treated as worth explaining from every conceivable angle, rather than being waved away as a stylistic preference.

4. More Explanations for Cutting Off — and How a Vanishing Moment Can Still 'Abide'

The cascade of explanations continues through several further pairs — already versus not-yet having taken an outflow- or ripening-result, already versus not-yet having repaid a same-class or universally-active cause, already versus not-yet having burned and afflicted — before turning to a genuinely elegant puzzle: since every wholesome dharma perishes the very instant it arises, how can any of them be said to "remain firmly established" or be "doubly cultivated and expanded"? The answer distinguishes two portions of wholesome dharma, one conducive to abiding and one conducive to further progress, both descriptions applying not to any single frozen moment but to a continuum's own successive, forward movement.

Chinese Source

有說。已生者於自相續。已取果與果故說令斷。未生者於自相續。

Others say: what has already arisen has already taken and given its own result within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "cut off"; what has not yet arisen has not yet

Others say: what has already arisen has already taken its own outflow-result and its own ripening-result within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "cut off"; what has not yet arisen has not yet taken its own outflow-result and ripening-result within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "prevented from arising." Others say: what has already arisen has already repaid its own same-class cause and universally-active cause within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "cut off"; what has not yet arisen has not yet repaid its own same-class cause and universally-active cause within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "prevented from arising." Others say: what has already arisen has already burned and afflicted within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "cut off"; what has not yet arisen has not yet burned or afflicted within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "prevented from arising." Others say: what has already arisen has already given rise to reproach, has already stained and defiled, has already stuck and mired, and has already fallen into evil intention within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "cut off";

what has not yet arisen has not yet given rise to reproach, has not yet stained or defiled, has not yet stuck or mired, and has not yet fallen into evil intention within one's own continuum, and hence is said to be "prevented from arising." Question: whatever wholesome dharma is cultivated, arises and perishes as swiftly as it does, with no single moment ever remaining stationary past its own arising — how, then, can it be said that a wholesome dharma already arisen is caused to "remain firmly established, never forgotten, and doubly cultivated and expanded"? Answer: it should be understood that this refers to wholesome dharmas of two portions — the portion conducive to abiding, and the portion conducive to further progress. "Caused to remain firmly established, never forgotten" refers to the portion conducive to abiding; "doubly cultivated and expanded" refers to the portion conducive to further progress — both spoken of, with reference to the continuum's own successive progress, as "remaining established" and the rest, and hence no fault follows. Others say: this refers to wholesome dharmas of three grades — lower, middling, and upper. "Caused to remain firmly established, never forgotten" refers to relying on the lower-grade wholesome root to advance to the middling grade; "doubly cultivated and expanded" refers to relying on the middling-grade wholesome root to advance to the upper grade — though any single moment, once arisen, immediately perishes, this is spoken of with reference to the continuum as a whole.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Resolving the apparent contradiction between momentary impermanence and the sūtra's own language of "remaining established" by relocating the entire claim from a single instant to an unfolding continuum is a technique this treatise reaches for again and again: whenever ordinary language seems to demand a persisting thing, the solution is never to abandon momentariness, but to find the pattern across successive moments that the language was actually describing all along.

5. Right Effort Everywhere: Even Heaven Needs an Antidote

A pointed geographic question asks how the four right efforts, so obviously suited to the desire realm's own unwholesome, evil dharmas, could possibly exist within the higher, purer realms as well — answered by distinguishing the presence of a fault from the presence of its corresponding virtue, and, more precisely, by a fivefold taxonomy of antidotes (abandonment, cutting-off, sustaining, distancing, and aversion-and-destruction) of which the higher grounds retain three even while lacking the first two, and the formless realm retains two even while lacking three.

Chinese Source

此正斷欲界有四。色無色界亦爾。問欲界有惡不善法可說有四。云何上界亦說四耶。答彼雖過患不具。而具有彼功德。有說。彼雖無所治而有能治。問未至地可爾上地云何。答有多種對治。謂捨對治。斷對治。持對治。遠分對治。厭壞對治。上地雖無捨及斷對治。而有餘對治。問靜慮可爾無色云何。答無色雖無壞對治。而有持及遠分對治。

Answer: though they lack the corresponding faults in full, they do possess the corresponding virtues in full. Others say: though they lack what is to be treated, they do possess what treats it. Question: this makes sense for the not-yet-attained stage — but what of the higher grounds? Answer: there are several kinds of antidote — the antidote of abandonment, the antidote of cutting-off, the antidote of sustaining, the antidote of distancing, and the antidote of aversion-and-destruction. The higher grounds, though lacking the antidote of abandonment and of cutting-off, do possess the remaining antidotes. Question: this makes sense for the dhyānas — but what of the formless realm? Answer: the formless realm, though lacking the antidote of aversion-and-destruction, does possess the antidote of sustaining and of distancing.

此正斷學無學位各具四種。問學位可爾有惡不善法故。無學云何。

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The fivefold taxonomy of antidotes, mapped so precisely onto which grounds retain which subset, reveals a subtle insight about spiritual purification generally: even the most refined states of consciousness are never simply beyond the need for any antidote whatsoever — they merely require a progressively narrower and more specialized kind, the coarsest tools falling away first while the subtlest ones remain useful even at the very summit of existence.

6. Which Effort Claims Nirvāṇa as Its Own Object?

A charming closing puzzle asks which of the four right efforts governs the diligence exercised when nirvāṇa itself is taken as an object of contemplation — competing answers nominate each of the four in turn, since cutting-off is itself nirvāṇa, nirvāṇa is itself non-arising, wholesome dharmas arise upon taking it as object, and wholesome dharmas increase upon doing so — before the treatise settles, characteristically, on all four at once: taking analytical cessation as one's object accomplishes every one of the four right efforts' own functions simultaneously.

13. Faculty, Power, and the Seven Factors of Awakening

The five faculties and five powers are shown to be the very same five mental factors — faith, diligence, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom — under two different names, distinguished only by whether one emphasizes their capacity to generate wholesome dharmas ("faculty") or their capacity to overcome opposition ("power"), with every position genuinely possessing both senses at once. 21. Are the Penetrations Trained or Simply Unlocked? Reconciling Two Treatises

A genuine scholarly puzzle asks whether the five penetrations are attained purely through deliberate preparatory practice or purely as an automatic byproduct of becoming free from a given ground's own defilement, since two authoritative treatises appear to give contradictory answers — resolved, most persuasively, by distinguishing within "attainment through freedom from defilement" itself a portion already previously practiced from a portion never previously practiced, each treatise simply describing one half of the very same underlying, twofold reality.

Chinese Source

若加行得者。智蘊所說云何通。如說若成就現在他心智。亦成就過去未來耶。答如是。若離染得者。此蘊前說云何通。如說以無間道證神境智通時。念住現在修一。未來修四等。非起先得有未來修。

Ru-Yi Translation

22. Uninterrupted Path or Liberation Path? A Careful Tetralemma

A further technical dispute asks whether the penetrations belong to the uninterrupted path or the liberation-path, since a single instant of the swift, exclusively affliction-cutting uninterrupted path could hardly accomplish something as elaborate as dividing one body into many — resolved firmly in favor of the liberation-path, exactly like the fruits of the ascetic life themselves, before a final tetralemma cleanly distinguishes "penetration" (superior wisdom specifically, wholesome or neutral) from "wholesome wisdom" in general, the divine eye and ear standing as penetrations that are never themselves wholesome wisdom at all.

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: are the penetrations subsumed under the uninterrupted path, or under the liberation-path? If subsumed under the uninterrupted path, how is the sūtra's own statement to be reconciled?

如說分一為多合多為一。乃至廣說。非無間道一剎那中有如是事。

As it says, dividing one into many, and combining many into one, and so on, in full — for a single instant of the uninterrupted path could hardly accomplish such a feat.

若解脫道攝者。品類足說當云何通。如說通云何。謂善慧天眼耳通解脫道。是無記慧云何名通。答諸通是解脫道攝。如沙門果解脫道攝非無間道。此亦如是。問品類足說當云何通。答彼所說通與此說異。彼說善慧皆名為通。以說一切法皆是所通達故。此中所說勝慧名通。此通或善或是無記。通與善慧得作四句。有通非善慧。謂天眼耳通。有善慧非通。謂除通餘善慧。有俱是。謂餘四通。有俱非。謂除前相。

But if subsumed under the liberation-path, how is the Prakaraṇapāda's own statement to be reconciled? As it says: what is a "penetration"? Namely, the liberation-path belonging to wholesome wisdom, the divine eye, and the divine ear — but is not neutral wisdom, too, properly called a "penetration"? Answer: the penetrations are indeed subsumed under the liberation-path, exactly as the fruits of the ascetic life are subsumed under the liberation-path, never the uninterrupted path — it is the very same here. Question: how, then, is the Prakaraṇapāda's own statement to be reconciled? Answer: what that treatise means by "penetration" differs from what is meant here — there, it says that wholesome wisdom in general is called "penetration," since it says that every dharma alike is what is penetrated and understood; here, what is spoken of is superior wisdom specifically, called "penetration," and this penetration may be either wholesome or neutral. "Penetration" and "wholesome wisdom" form a tetralemma between them: there is what is "penetration" but not "wholesome wisdom" — namely, the divine eye and divine ear penetrations; there is what is "wholesome wisdom" but not "penetration" — namely, every other kind of wholesome wisdom besides the penetrations; there is what is both — namely, the remaining four penetrations; there is what is neither — namely, everything excluded from the foregoing.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The closing tetralemma — carefully separating what counts as a "penetration" from what counts as "wholesome wisdom," with each category containing members the other entirely lacks — is a fitting capstone to this fascicle's own governing method: even after a term has been defined, explained, and traced through a dozen technical dimensions, the treatise still insists on one final check to confirm that this term and its nearest neighbor are not silently being conflated.

23. Do the Powers Always Arrive in the Same Order? Devadatta and Aniruddha Say No

A closing narrative asks whether the five penetrations necessarily arise in the very sequence the Buddha names them — locomotion, then divine eye, then divine ear, then reading minds, then recalling past lives — following a plausible practical logic (traveling to the gods first, then seeing them, hearing them, knowing their minds, and finally recognizing whether one has met them before) — before being firmly overturned: some practitioners attain only a single penetration, like Devadatta's own locomotion or Aniruddha's own divine eye, proving that no fixed sequence, forward or reverse, actually governs how these powers unfold.

Chinese Source

問此五通為如說而生為不爾。有作是說。如說而生。謂先起神境智通故佛前說。乃至後起宿住智通故佛後說。謂彼聞色界天而不能往故。先起神境通往。而不能見其色故。次起天眼通見而不能聞其語故。次起天耳通聞而不能知其心故。次起他心通知彼心已未知宿世曾相遇不故。後起宿住通。世尊依此次第而說。如是說者。次第無定。謂或先起神境通。乃至或先起宿住通。或有唯得神境通。如天授等。或有唯得天眼通。如善星等。是故諸通無順入逆入。亦無超越次第。如諸等至。或如無量解脫勝處遍處。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: do these five penetrations arise in the very sequence in which they are spoken of, or not necessarily so? Some say: they do arise in this very sequence — that is, the penetration of supernormal locomotion arises first, and it is for this reason that the Buddha speaks of it first, and so on, up through the penetration of recollecting past lives arising last, and it is for this reason that the Buddha speaks of it last — that is, upon hearing that there are gods in the form realm, yet being unable to go there, one first gives rise to the penetration of supernormal locomotion, in order to go; and, having gone, yet being unable to see their form, one next gives rise to the penetration of the divine eye, in order to see; and, having seen, yet being unable to hear their speech, one next gives rise to the penetration of the divine ear, in order to hear; and, having heard, yet being unable to know their minds, one next gives rise to the penetration of others' minds, in order to know; and, having known their minds, yet not knowing whether one has ever encountered them in some past life, one finally gives rise to the penetration of recollecting past lives — and it is on the basis of this very sequence that the World-Honored One speaks of them in this order. According to this final account, however, there is no fixed sequence at all — that is, one may give rise first to the penetration of supernormal locomotion, or, alternately, first to the penetration of recollecting past lives, and so on; and there are those who attain only the penetration of supernormal locomotion, such as Devadatta and the like, and those who attain only the penetration of the divine eye, such as Aniruddha and the like — and hence none of these penetrations follows any fixed forward or reverse sequence, nor any fixed order of transcendence, exactly as with the various attainments, or as with the immeasurables, the liberations, the stations of mastery, and the totalities.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the Buddha's own narratively satisfying explanation for the sequence — go there, see them, hear them, know them, remember them — turns out not to describe any actual necessity, but merely one coherent story among many possible orderings, is a rare moment in this treatise where a genuinely appealing explanation is deliberately set aside in favor of a less tidy, more accurate account: narrative coherence, however satisfying, is not allowed to substitute for what the evidence of actual practitioners' own varied attainments demonstrates.

24. Two Kinds of Light: Manifested Magic vs. Simply Being There

A final, delicately observed question closes the fascicle: both the penetration of supernormal locomotion and the penetration of the divine eye are accompanied by light when cultivated — so what distinguishes them? The answer is precise and small: locomotion's own light may be either magically created or simply, naturally present; the divine eye's own light, by contrast, is never magically created, existing only in its own natural, unmanufactured form.

Chinese Source

問修起神境天眼通時。俱有光明此有何異。答若修神境所引光明。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: when cultivating and giving rise to the penetration of supernormal locomotion and the penetration of the divine eye, both are accompanied by light — what difference is there between the two? Answer: as for the light drawn forth by cultivating the penetration of supernormal locomotion,

或化所為。或自性有。若修天眼所引光明。非化所為唯自性有。

it may be either magically created, or naturally present of its own accord; as for the light drawn forth by cultivating the penetration of the divine eye, it is never magically created, but is naturally present of its own accord only.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Ending an entire fascicle of grand cosmological and soteriological machinery on so fine a distinction — whether an accompanying light happens to be manufactured or simply already there — shows this tradition's own comfort with letting the biggest and smallest questions sit side by side without any sense of mismatch: a query this delicate about the phenomenology of light is treated with exactly the same seriousness as the nature of liberation itself.
