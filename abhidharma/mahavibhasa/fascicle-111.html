1. Does Attaining One Kind of Knowledge Entail Attaining Another?

Fascicle 111 opens with a brief but foundational survey: if one attains dharma-cognition, does one thereby attain subsequent cognition? The knowledge of others' minds? Worldly knowledge? Each answer turns on a precise technical sense of "attained" — an already-attained dharma remains attained unless positively lost, and once dharma-cognition, subsequent cognition, and the four truth-specific kinds of knowledge are first attained, they are thereafter constantly attained together with the knowledge of others' minds and worldly knowledge, each according to its own conditions of freedom from defilement and retrogression.

Chinese Source

若成就法智。彼類智耶。答若得。此中得者。已得名得。謂苦類智現前以後。後諸得言皆准此釋。設成就類智彼法智耶。答如是。謂苦法智現前以後。法智苦智恒成就故。若成就法智彼他心智耶。答若得不失。此中得者。謂已離欲染。不失者。謂不退起欲界煩惱。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one attains dharma-cognition, does one thereby attain subsequent cognition? The answer: if one has attained it. Here, "attained" refers to what is already attained being named attained — that is, after the subsequent cognition of suffering has already manifested in the present. Every subsequent use of the term "attained" ought to be interpreted according to this same standard. Suppose one attains subsequent cognition — does one thereby attain dharma-cognition? The answer: this is so, that is, after the dharma-cognition of suffering has already manifested in the present, dharma-cognition and the knowledge of suffering are thereafter constantly attained. If one attains dharma-cognition, does one thereby attain the knowledge of others' minds? The answer: if one has attained it, and not lost it. Here, "attained" refers to already having become free of the desire realm's own defilement; "not lost" refers to not having retrogressed into giving rise to the desire realm's own affliction.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The governing principle running through this entire fascicle — attainment persists by default, and is lost only through specific, enumerable events, never through mere passage of time — was already established in fascicle 109, and its reappearance here as the foundation for a far more elaborate structure shows how load-bearing that single technical stipulation turns out to be: an enormous analytical apparatus can be built on top of it precisely because it never needs re-justifying, only re-applying.

2. Dharma-Cognition Across the Three Times: A Governing Method

A new and highly systematic method opens: does attaining past dharma-cognition entail attaining it belonging to the future? To the present? And in every possible combination of the three times taken two or three at a time? Each of these questions is answered by locating exactly which moment of the sixteen-moment path of seeing, or which stage of the path of cultivation, could produce that specific combination of attainments — down to the precise mind-moment, the specific fruit of renunciation, and whether the faculties have been sharpened. Having worked through all six question-and-answer combinations in full, the treatise closes by noting that the identical sixfold method applies, without further modification beyond substituting the appropriate name, to subsequent cognition and to each of the four truth-specific kinds of knowledge.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The phrase "at what stage does this occur" recurs so often across this fascicle that it risks reading as mere formula — yet each time, the treatise supplies a genuinely different, precisely specified answer, tracking a practitioner's exact position within the sixteen moments of the path of seeing or the path of cultivation. The apparent repetitiveness is not padding: it is the method itself, applied with total consistency to guarantee that no possible combination of past, future, and present attainment is ever left unlocated on the map of the path. The closing note — "as dharma-cognition runs through six such questions, so, too, should subsequent cognition, and the rest, be understood" — then performs the same economizing function seen elsewhere in this broader nyāsa: a fully worked example, established once with total precision, becomes a template the treatise trusts its reader to extend to structurally identical cases without needing every instance spelled out.

3. The Knowledge of Others' Minds Across the Three Times

The identical sixfold method is now applied to the knowledge of others' minds, complicated by a genuinely richer set of conditions than dharma-cognition alone required: attainment now depends on which realm a practitioner is reborn within, whether he has become free of the desire realm's own defilement, and, for the learner specifically, whether the undefiled knowledge of others' minds has been given rise to and not yet lost — conditions that compound in ways dharma-cognition's own simpler path-of-seeing framework never had to accommodate.

4. Worldly Knowledge: The Simplest Case

Worldly knowledge, by contrast, offers the simplest possible case: since every sentient being whatsoever attains it belonging to both the past and the future without exception, four of the six questions collapse into an immediate "yes," leaving only the question of its presently manifesting (which depends solely on not giving rise to any undefiled wisdom, and not being within a mindless stage) to require any further specification at all.

5. Dharma-Cognition Against Subsequent Cognition, in Full, and Naming the Method

The method now turns to genuinely cross-pairing two different kinds of knowledge against one another across the three times: if one attains past dharma-cognition, does one thereby attain past subsequent cognition? Future subsequent cognition? Present subsequent cognition? And so on, through every combination — each answer once again located with total precision at a specific moment of the path of seeing or a specific stage of the path of cultivation. Having exhausted this pairing, the treatise names the very method it has been using all along: the lesser sevenfold analysis, in which one kind of knowledge is set against exactly one other across seven governing questions, extended without further modification to every remaining pair of kinds of knowledge.
