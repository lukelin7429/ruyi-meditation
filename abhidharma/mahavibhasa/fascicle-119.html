The Cakya Hunters and the Pearl-Swallowing Goose

Fascicle 119 works through the exact conditions and ranking of the five ānantarya deeds, tells the beloved stories of the Cakya hunting family's sudden conversion and the monk who let himself be tortured rather than doom a goose, and closes with a detailed taxonomy of the four kinds of restraint. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Does Intermediate Existence Include Ānantarya Ripening? Refuting Six Wrong Views

Fascicle 119 opens still within the Killing-of-Life Nyāsa, refuting a cascade of increasingly narrow denials — that intermediate existence does not exist at all; that it exists but never leads to unhappy destinies; that it leads there but never to hell; that it leads to hell but not for ānantarya-committers; that it does so but without any ānantarya ripening occurring within it; and that such ripening occurs but only across the four mental aggregates, never the aggregate of form — before establishing that intermediate existence occurs throughout every birth-place in the realm possessing form, ripening the aggregate of form as well.

Chinese Source

頗有業不善順苦受異熟未熟乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答為止他義顯己義故。謂或有說。無有中有。或復有說。雖有中有而生惡趣者無。或復有說。生惡趣者雖有中有而生地獄者無。或復有說。生地獄者雖有中有而先造無間業者無。或復有說。先造無間業者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Is there karma unwholesome and conducive to painful feeling whose ripening has not yet ripened, and so on in full? Question: why compose this treatise? Answer: in order to put a stop to another school's position and to make clear our own doctrine. That is, some say there is no intermediate existence at all; some further say that, although there is intermediate existence, none who are born in an unhappy destiny pass through it; some further say that, although those born in an unhappy destiny do pass through intermediate existence, none who are born in hell do so; some further say that, although those born in hell do pass through intermediate existence, none who have previously committed an ānantarya deed do so; some further say that, although those who have previously committed an ānantarya deed do pass through intermediate existence,

雖有中有而中有中不受無間異熟。或復有說。雖住中有亦受無間異熟。而但受四蘊不受色蘊。欲遮此等種種僻執顯有中有。於有色界一切生處無不皆有。於中亦受色蘊異熟。由是因緣故作斯論。

— they do not experience the ripening of the ānantarya deed within that intermediate existence; some further say that, although one does experience the ripening of the ānantarya deed while abiding in intermediate existence, one experiences only the ripening of the four aggregates, not the aggregate of form. In order to refute these various mistaken attachments and to show that intermediate existence exists in every place of birth throughout the realm possessing form, without exception, and that within it one experiences the ripening of the aggregate of form as well, this treatise is composed for this reason.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The six-fold cascade of denials being refuted here traces a single retreating line of defense, each position conceding one more inch to its opponent while still trying to hold the next: this rhetorical structure — deny the whole, then the general case, then the specific case, then the specific detail — is itself a recognizable pattern of a losing argument, and the treatise's systematic dismantling of each retreat in turn leaves no ground left to fall back to.

2. Creating vs. Increasing Karma: A Dozen Criteria

An extended technical inquiry distinguishes "creating" karma from "increasing" it — the latter reserved for karma that reaches genuine completion, whether measured by fulfilling an entire required complement (all three evil conducts, all five ānantarya deeds, all ten unwholesome paths, or even all 3,200 merits behind a bodhisattva's final birth), by deliberateness versus impulse, by fixed versus indeterminate timing, by whether the deed is proudly proclaimed among one's peers, or by whether cause and condition have converged such that the fruit is now guaranteed.

Chinese Source

頗有業不善順苦受異熟未熟。非不初受異熟果。而起染污心耶。答有。如造作增長無間業。已此業最初地獄中有異熟果生。問造作增長何差別。有說。無差別。有說。名則差別。此名造作。此名增長。有說義亦有差別。謂或有由一惡行墮諸惡趣。或有由三。若由一惡行墮惡趣者。彼加行時但名造作不名增長。若至究竟名為造作亦名增長。若具由三墮惡趣者。造一二時但名造作不名增長。若具造三名為造作亦名增長。如三惡行三妙行亦爾。差別者生善趣。復次或有由一無間墮於地獄。或具由五。若由一者彼加行位。但名造作不名增長。若至究竟名為造作亦名增長。若具由五造一至四。但名造作不名增長。若具造五名為造作亦名增長。復次或有由一不善業道墮諸惡趣。或具由十。若由一者彼加行位。但名造作不名增長。若至究竟名為造作亦名增長。若具由十造一至九。但名造作不名增長。若具造十名為造作亦名增長。如十不善業道。十善業道亦爾。差別者生善趣。復次或有由多妙行感一眾同分。如諸菩薩最後眾同分。由三十二百福所感。若造一百福至三十一百福。但名造作不名增長。若時具造三十二百福。名為造作亦名增長。復次或有業故思所造。或有業非故思造。若故思所造名為造作亦名增長。若非故思所造。但名造作不名增長。審思而造。率爾而造。說亦爾。復次或有業順三時受。或有業順不定受。順三時受名為造作亦名增長。順不定受但名造作不名增長。順決定受。順不定受。說亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Is there karma unwholesome and conducive to painful feeling whose ripening has not yet ripened, yet which is not incapable of giving rise to a defiled mind at the very first moment its ripening-fruit is received? Answer: there is — as with ānantarya karma that has been created and increased, whose ripening-fruit first arises within the intermediate existence leading to hell. Question: what is the difference between "creating" and "increasing"? Some say: there is no difference. Some say: the difference is merely nominal — this is called creating, that is called increasing. Some say: the meaning itself also differs. That is, one may fall into an unhappy destiny through a single evil deed, or one may fall through the full complement of three; if one falls into an unhappy destiny through a single evil deed, then at the time of its preparatory action it is called only creating, not increasing, while once it reaches completion it is called both creating and increasing; if one falls through the full complement of three, then while creating only one or two of them it is called only creating, not increasing, while once all three are fully created it is called both creating and increasing — and just as with the three evil conducts, so too with the three good conducts, the difference being that one is thereby born in a fortunate destiny instead. Further, one may fall into hell through a single ānantarya deed, or through the full complement of five; if through a single one, then at the stage of its preparatory action it is called only creating, not increasing, while once it reaches completion it is called both creating and increasing; if through the full complement of five, then while creating only one up through four of them it is called only creating, not increasing, while once all five are fully created it is called both creating and increasing. Further, one may fall into an unhappy destiny through a single unwholesome path of karma, or through the full complement of ten; if through a single one, then at the stage of its preparatory action it is called only creating, not increasing, while once it reaches completion it is called both creating and increasing; if through the full complement of ten, then while creating only one up through nine of them it is called only creating, not increasing, while once all ten are fully created it is called both creating and increasing — and just as with the ten unwholesome paths of karma, so too with the ten wholesome paths of karma, the difference being that one is thereby born in a fortunate destiny instead. Further, one may bring about a single class-nature through many good conducts, as with the bodhisattva's own final class-nature, brought about by three thousand two hundred meritorious deeds; while creating from one up through three thousand one hundred ninety-nine of these merits, it is called only creating, not increasing, while once all three thousand two hundred are fully created, it is called both creating and increasing. Further, there is karma deliberately created, and karma not deliberately created; if deliberately created, it is called both creating and increasing; if not deliberately created, it is called only creating, not increasing — and it is likewise said for what is created through careful deliberation versus created impulsively. Further, there is karma whose ripening is conducive to one of the three fixed times, and karma whose ripening is indeterminate; that conducive to one of the three fixed times is called both creating and increasing, while that whose ripening is indeterminate is called only creating, not increasing — and it is likewise said for karma whose ripening is fixed versus whose ripening is indeterminate.

復次或有業順別定受。或有業順不別定受。順別定受者。名為造作亦名增長。順不別定受者但名造作不名增長。復次或有業時分定異熟亦定。或有異熟定。時分不定。二俱定者名為造作亦名增長。唯一定者但名造作不名增長。復次有不善業順惡趣受。有不善業順善趣受。前名造作亦名增長。後名造作不名增長。善業與此相違。說亦爾。復次有不善業。不善為助伴有不善業。善業為助伴。前名造作亦名增長。後名造作不名增長。善業與此相違。說亦爾。復次有不善業無作見邪見。迷因果相續中生。有不善業有作見正見。不迷因果相續中生。前名造作亦名增長。後名造作不名增長。善業與此相違。說亦爾。復次有不善業壞戒壞見。有不善業壞戒不壞見。前名造作亦名增長。後名造作不名增長。善業與此相違。說亦爾。復次有不善業加行壞意樂壞。有不善業加行壞意樂不壞。前名造作亦名增長。後名造作不名增長。善業與此相違。說亦爾。復次有業作已不捨不變不吐不依對治。有業作已捨變吐依對治。前名造作亦名增長。後名造作不名增長。如是有業三時覺察。三時不覺察。作已無悔。作已有悔作已隨念。作已不隨念。數數憶念。不數數憶念。

Further, there is karma whose ripening is conducive to a specifically fixed experience, and karma whose ripening is not conducive to any specifically fixed experience; the former is called both creating and increasing, the latter only creating, not increasing. Further, there is karma whose timing is fixed and whose ripening is also fixed, and karma whose ripening alone is fixed while its timing is not; that in which both are fixed is called both creating and increasing, while that in which only one is fixed is called only creating, not increasing. Further, there is unwholesome karma conducive to an unhappy destiny, and unwholesome karma conducive to a fortunate destiny; the former is called both creating and increasing, the latter only creating, not increasing — and wholesome karma is spoken of in the reverse way. Further, there is unwholesome karma accompanied by an unwholesome companion, and unwholesome karma accompanied by a wholesome companion; the former is called both creating and increasing, the latter only creating, not increasing — and wholesome karma is spoken of in the reverse way. Further, there is unwholesome karma that arises within a continuum bearing no view of action and wrong view, confused about cause and effect, and unwholesome karma that arises within a continuum bearing a view of action and right view, not confused about cause and effect; the former is called both creating and increasing, the latter only creating, not increasing — and wholesome karma is spoken of in the reverse way. Further, there is unwholesome karma that damages both moral discipline and right view, and unwholesome karma that damages moral discipline but not right view; the former is called both creating and increasing, the latter only creating, not increasing — and wholesome karma is spoken of in the reverse way. Further, there is unwholesome karma whose preparatory action and intention are both corrupted, and unwholesome karma whose preparatory action alone is corrupted while the intention is not; the former is called both creating and increasing, the latter only creating, not increasing. In just this way, there is karma noticed at all three of its stages, and karma not noticed at any of the three; karma done without any subsequent regret, and karma done with subsequent regret; karma done and afterward called to mind, and karma done and not afterward called to mind; karma repeatedly recollected, and karma not repeatedly recollected —

說亦爾。復次若業能取果與果。名為造作亦名增長。若業取果不能與果。但名造作不名增長。尊者世友說曰。若所作業意樂迴向意樂顯示。為同類者稱讚顯說。是名造作亦名增長。若所作業與此相違。但名造作非名增長。有作是說。若所作業一切種圓滿。一切種究竟。如造制多嚴飾周畢。此名造作亦名增長。若所作業與此相違。但名造作不名增長。大德說言。若所作業眾緣和合必。定感果名為造作亦名增長。若所作業與此相違。但名造作不名增長。如是等有無量門。是名造作增長差別。

— all likewise spoken of in the same pattern. Further, if karma is able to both take hold of a fruit and actually deliver that fruit, it is called both creating and increasing; if karma takes hold of a fruit yet is unable to deliver it, it is called only creating, not increasing. The Venerable Vasumitra explains: if the karma one performs is dedicated with intention, revealed with intention, and praised and proclaimed before one's own kind, this is called both creating and increasing; if the karma one performs is otherwise, it is called only creating, not increasing. Some say: if the karma one performs is complete in every respect and finished in every respect, like a stūpa built and adorned to full completion, this is called both creating and increasing; if the karma one performs is otherwise, it is called only creating, not increasing. The Great Virtuous One says: if the karma one performs comes together with its full complement of conditions such that it will certainly bring about its fruit, this is called both creating and increasing; if the karma one performs is otherwise, it is called only creating, not increasing. There are countless approaches such as these — these are the distinctions between creating and increasing.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The proclaiming-among-peers criterion for "increased" karma — Vasumitra's own contribution to this list — introduces something genuinely social into an otherwise solitary moral calculus: an act's karmic weight is not determined solely by the deed and the mind behind it, but partly by whether the doer has folded it into a public identity, dedicated and displayed for others of the same kind to recognize and praise, turning a private act into a claimed reputation.

3. The Five Ānantarya Deeds: Nature, Naming, and Two Grounds

The five ānantarya deeds — killing mother, father, or an arhat, causing schism, and maliciously drawing a buddha's blood — are shown to be uniformly bodily or verbal karma (only the schism is verbal), named "without interval" for two reasons (fixed to ripen only in the very next life, and fixed to ripen only in hell, never any other destiny), and grounded in two distinct wrongs: betraying the kindness of one's own nurturing (killing a parent) and destroying a field of merit (the remaining three). The crime itself requires two conditions together — undertaking the preparatory action and the result actually reaching completion.

Chinese Source

無間業有五種。一害母。二害父。三害阿羅漢。四破和合僧。五起惡心出佛身血。問此五無間業以何為自性。答以身語業為自性。前三後一身業為自性。第四一種語業為自性。是故此五三界三處一蘊所攝。三界者色聲法界。三處者色聲法處。一蘊者色蘊。是名無間業自性。已說自性。所以今當說。問何故名無間。答由二緣故說名無間。一遮現後。二遮餘趣。遮現後者。此五但是順次生受。非順現法受及順後次受。故名無間。遮餘趣者。謂此決定於地獄受。非雜餘趣。故名無間。由二因緣建立無間。一背恩養。二壞德田。背恩養者。謂害母害父。壞德田者。謂餘三種。由二因緣得無間罪。

Ru-Yi Translation

There are five kinds of ānantarya deeds: first, killing one's mother; second, killing one's father; third, killing an arhat; fourth, causing a schism in the harmonious saṅgha; fifth, with malicious intent, drawing a buddha's blood. Question: what is the own-nature of these five ānantarya deeds? Answer: their own-nature is bodily and verbal karma — the first three and the last are of the nature of bodily karma, while the fourth alone is of the nature of verbal karma. Hence these five are included within three realms, three sense-fields, and one aggregate: the three realms are the form realm, sound realm, and dharma realm; the three sense-fields are the form field, sound field, and dharma field; the one aggregate is the aggregate of form. This is the own-nature of the ānantarya deeds. Having explained their own-nature, we should now explain the reason for it. Question: why are they called ānantarya, "without interval"? Answer: they are so named for two reasons: first, to exclude the present life and later lives; second, to exclude other destinies. As to excluding the present life and later lives: these five ripen only in the very next life, never ripening in the present life nor in some later life beyond, and hence are named "without interval." As to excluding other destinies: these five are definitely experienced in hell, never intermixed with any other destiny, and hence are named "without interval." They are established for two reasons: first, betraying the kindness of one's own nurturing; second, destroying a field of merit. As to betraying the kindness of nurturing: this refers to killing one's mother or father. As to destroying a field of merit: this refers to the remaining three. The crime of an ānantarya deed is obtained through two conditions:

一起加行二果究竟。雖起加行果不究竟。彼不得無間罪。雖果究竟不起加行。亦不得無間罪。雖起加行果不究竟者謂如有人欲害其母。母覺知已藏穀[卄/積]中。有餘女人在母寢處。其人既至謂是己母以刀害之。害已方更往穀[卄/積]中揩拭刀刃刀觸母身因茲喪命。起加行時果未究竟。果究竟時已無加行。由此不成無間之罪。

— first, undertaking the preparatory action; second, the result reaching completion. If one undertakes the preparatory action but the result does not reach completion, one does not obtain the ānantarya crime; if the result reaches completion without one having undertaken the preparatory action, one likewise does not obtain the ānantarya crime. As to undertaking the preparatory action without the result reaching completion: this is as when a certain person, wishing to kill his own mother, is found out by her, and she hides herself within a stack of grain, while another woman happens to be in the mother's own sleeping place; that person, arriving, believes this woman to be his own mother and kills her with a knife, and, having killed her, then goes to wipe the blade clean upon the stack of grain, at which the blade touches his mother's body, and she thereby loses her life — at the time the preparatory action was undertaken, the result had not yet reached completion, and by the time the result reached completion, the preparatory action was already over; hence this does not constitute the crime of an ānantarya deed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Splitting the five ānantarya deeds into two categories of wrongdoing — betraying nurture versus destroying merit — does real conceptual work by showing these five acts, so often grouped together as simply "the worst possible crimes," are not one undifferentiated category of evil but two structurally different kinds of violation: one an offense against a debt of gratitude owed to specific individuals, the other an offense against the very possibility of the Dharma's transmission to everyone.

4. Preparation and Completion Must Coincide: The Tragedy of Mistaken Identity

Two devastating narratives illustrate why both conditions must coincide: a man who sets out to kill his hiding mother instead kills a stranger in her bed by mistake, then unwittingly kills his real mother afterward while merely wiping his blade clean on the haystack where she is hidden — preparation and completion never truly meeting in either death, and hence no ānantarya crime in either case; and a man who, fearing bandits on a dangerous road, urges his elderly parents onward so forcefully that they stumble and die, a completed result with no murderous preparation behind it at all.

Chinese Source

有果究竟不起加行者。謂如有人扶持父母經嶮路過。恐其賊來。推逼令進。父母顛仆因即喪命。果雖究竟非加行起。是故要起加行及果究竟方成無間。問頗有起加行及果究竟。而不得無間罪耶。答有。謂與所殺俱時命終。無後眾同分可成就彼罪故。有兄遣弟自往害母弟依兄教俱得無間。若弟遣他及共他害。唯弟得無間。有兄遣弟令他害母。弟依兄教俱得無間。若弟自害及共他害唯弟得無間。

Ru-Yi Translation

As to the result reaching completion without the preparatory action having been undertaken: this is as when a certain person, supporting his parents as they pass along a dangerous road, and fearing bandits might come, urges and presses them onward, whereupon his parents stumble and thereby lose their lives; although the result reaches completion, it was not arisen from any preparatory action — hence it is necessary that both the preparatory action be undertaken and the result reach completion before an ānantarya deed is constituted. Question: is there a case where both the preparatory action is undertaken and the result reaches completion, yet the ānantarya crime is still not obtained? Answer: there is — namely, when the killer dies at the same moment as the one killed, since there is then no subsequent class-nature in which that crime could be established. If an elder brother orders his younger brother to go himself and kill their mother, both the younger brother, in accordance with his elder brother's instruction, and the elder brother himself obtain the ānantarya crime; if the younger brother instead orders another to do it, or does it jointly with another, only the younger brother obtains the ānantarya crime. If an elder brother orders his younger brother to have another kill their mother, both the younger brother, in accordance with his elder brother's instruction, and the elder brother obtain the ānantarya crime; if the younger brother kills her himself, or does it jointly with another, only the younger brother obtains the ānantarya crime.

有兄遣弟共他害母。弟依兄教俱得無間。弟若自害及但令他。唯弟得無間。有兄遣弟母來當害。弟依兄教俱得無間。若母去方害唯弟得無間。如兄遣弟。兄遣於妹弟遣於兄遣姊亦爾。如遣害來遣害去住坐臥亦爾。如害母害父亦爾。害阿羅漢。出佛身血。遣使差別類此應知。若非人殺非人父母不得無間罪。非人殺人人殺非人應知亦爾。唯有人類殺人父母方得無間。問若扇搋半擇迦無形二形殺害父母得無間不答不得。所以者何。彼身法爾志力微劣不能作律儀。不律儀器故。尊者世友作如是說。扇搋迦等殺害父母不得無間。所以者何。彼於父母無愛敬心。可先現前今滅壞故。復次彼於父母無勝慚愧。可先現前今滅壞故。大德說言。扇搋迦等煩惱增故定惡趣攝。惡趣攝故無無間罪。問諸傍生類殺害父母得無間不。答不得。

If an elder brother orders his younger brother to kill their mother jointly with another, both the younger brother, in accordance with his elder brother's instruction, and the elder brother obtain the ānantarya crime; if the younger brother kills her himself, or merely has another do it, only the younger brother obtains the ānantarya crime. If an elder brother orders his younger brother to kill their mother when she arrives, both the younger brother, in accordance with his elder brother's instruction, and the elder brother obtain the ānantarya crime; if he instead kills her only once she has already left, only the younger brother obtains the ānantarya crime. Just as with an elder brother ordering a younger, so too it is with a brother ordering a sister, or ordering an elder brother, or ordering an elder sister; just as with ordering the killing upon arrival or upon departure, so too it is with standing, sitting, or lying down; and just as with killing one's mother, so too with killing one's father, killing an arhat, and drawing a buddha's blood — the distinctions concerning sending an agent should be understood accordingly for each. If a non-human kills its non-human parent, the ānantarya crime is not obtained; the cases of a non-human killing a human, or a human killing a non-human, should be understood in the same way — only when a human being kills a human parent is the ānantarya crime obtained. 5. Whose Mother? Mistaken Killing, Surrogate Mothers, and a Double Crime That Isn't

Further refinements test the boundary of "mother" and "father": killing a non-mother believed to be one's mother, or one's actual mother believed to be a stranger, incurs no ānantarya crime either way — only killing while correctly recognizing the victim as one's parent counts; a parent who has changed sex still counts as a parent for this purpose; and, in a case of an embryo transplanted into a second woman's body, only the original birth mother's killing incurs the crime, never the surrogate who raised the child, though ordinary filial duty is still owed to her. 6. Killing a Trainee Isn't Ānantarya, and the Bandit Who Became a Monk

Killing a trainee — even one who will later become an arhat, or one who has only just fallen from arhatship back to trainee status — never counts as destroying a field of merit, since a trainee's mixture of merit and fault disqualifies the category entirely; and even at the very last moment of falling from arhatship, when the trainee has already regained non-learner status, the killer's own murderous intent was still fixed on the earlier, imperfect person, not the one he actually killed. The rule's full weight lands in a Vinaya story: a bandit who murdered a company of monks flees and is ordained without proper vetting, only to be exposed with horror at the execution of his former accomplices — and the Buddha rules that ignorance of one victim's arhat-status changes nothing, since the crime lies in the indiscriminate murderous intent itself, which happened to strike a field of merit regardless of what the killer knew.

7. Schism Absorbs All Other Ānantarya Deeds, and Whether Anyone Committed All Five

8. Ranking the Five Deeds, and Why the Buddha's Body Bears No Scars

The five ānantarya deeds are ranked by severity — schism worst (destroying the Dharma-body), then drawing blood, then killing an arhat, then killing one's mother, then, least severe, one's father, since a mother's kindness outweighs a father's and a field of merit outweighs a field of mere kindness — before confronting a real puzzle: since a buddha's adept-qualities cannot literally be harmed, how can drawing his blood be an ānantarya crime at all? Vasumitra's answer turns on the malicious intent behind the deed and the milk-jar logic of dependence, and a careful tetralemma distinguishes intent-to-strike from intent-to-kill in determining exactly which cases of drawing blood actually qualify. The section closes with the lovely explanation for why a buddha's body carries no scars whatsoever — the direct karmic fruit of a bodhisattva who, across three incalculable eons, compassionately restored every maimed body and every damaged sacred image or shrine he ever encountered.

10. Formal Vows and Genuine Protection: The Monk and the Pearl-Swallowing Goose

A precise tetralemma distinguishes formally undertaking abstention from genuinely protecting every being: the Cakya sons exemplify protection without formal vows; one who takes vows yet still breaks them through carelessness exemplifies formal abstention without protection; and the ideal case — both together — is embodied in one of the tradition's most beloved parables. Accused of theft, the monk is tortured nearly to death rather than reveal the truth — until the enraged jeweler kills the goose itself, and only then does the monk ask to examine its body, revealing the pearl within. The distinction between the ninth uninterrupted path (which cuts off precept-violation itself) and the preceding eight (which cut off only the afflictions causing it) is a fine but consequential piece of psychological realism: it recognizes that eliminating the underlying craving or hatred that drives bad conduct is not automatically the same achievement as eliminating whatever damage that conduct has already done — only the final, culminating stage of the antidote reaches back to address the violation as such, not merely its ongoing causes.

12. Which Restraints Follow the Mind, and an Exhaustive Case for Why Not

Three of the four restraints vary continuously with whatever mental state currently arises, while prātimokṣa restraint alone remains fixed regardless — and the treatise offers an almost exhaustive list of independent justifications for this asymmetry: prātimokṣa restraint is coarser, more sluggish, obtained through communal ritual rather than private attainment, dependent on manifest rather than unmanifest karma, and does not arise-abide-cease in lockstep with mind the way the other three do. A cascade of reductio arguments follows, each showing that if prātimokṣa restraint did vary with the mind, it would collapse into contradiction — becoming unwholesome whenever an unwholesome thought arose, shifting realm and training-status moment to moment, undermining the entire concept of seniority in the precepts, and rendering its own four fixed conditions of forfeiture meaningless.

13. Breaking a Vow, Confession, and the Lamp in a Dark Room

A genuine dispute over what happens when a precept is broken plays out between "foreign" masters (restraint is lost, replaced by a suspended third state, and formal confession genuinely restores it) and the Kashmiri masters (restraint is never actually lost at all — one merely enters a state called "neither restraint nor non-restraint" that is also, confusingly, still called "abiding in restraint," the way a wealthy debtor is still called wealthy even while owing money — with confession simply dropping the debtor label rather than restoring anything substantively new).
