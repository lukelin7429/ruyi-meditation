Sudden and Gradual

Fascicle 63 completes the nine kinds of full understanding — tracking exactly how many each of eight kinds of person, the pratyekabuddha, and the bodhisattva possesses at every stage of the path — before opening the Fetters Aggregate's own third nyāsa, the Sentient-Being Nyāsa, with an unforgettable frame story: a venerable one who, while still suffering within his mother's own womb, understood the fetters' danger, and denounced them aloud at the very moment of his own birth. The fascicle closes with a systematic account of exactly when the fetters abandoned by seeing and by cultivation are bound and freed suddenly, and when only gradually, across all three realms. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 63 completes the nine kinds of full understanding, then opens the Fetters Aggregate's own third nyāsa — the Sentient-Being Nyāsa (結蘊第二中有情納息第三之一) — asking whether the fetters abandoned by seeing and by cultivation are bound and freed suddenly or gradually across the three realms. Terms established in earlier fascicles recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Relinquishing and Attaining Full Understanding

The treatise tracks, moment by moment along the path of seeing and cultivation, exactly when a noble one relinquishes an old kind of full understanding and attains a new one — most moments involve neither, some involve gaining one outright, and the transition from stream-entry's own path of seeing to the first grade of cultivation involves relinquishing five kinds at once to gain a sixth. The same ledger extends to regression: an arhat who regresses through the formless, form, or desire realm's own wrapping-defilement in turn relinquishes and reacquires different combinations of the nine, down to a noble one not yet free of the desire realm's own defilement, for whom regression changes nothing on this particular ledger at all.

Chinese Source

中問答不依異生。但依聖者為有。聖者於九遍知無捨得不。答有。

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That regression can itself trigger a relinquishing-and-attaining event on this very same ledger — not just a separate story about fetters returning, but a technical change of which named full understanding a person currently possesses — shows how tightly interwoven fascicle 62's regression debate and this fascicle's full-understanding taxonomy actually are: the same underlying event is being tracked by two different, mutually consistent accounting systems at once.

2. Which Absorption, Which Path

The treatise classifies all nine kinds of full understanding along several further axes at once: all nine are fruits of absorption and its retinue, but only two are fruits of the formless attainments; five (or, on rival counts, eight) are fruits of the fundamental absorptions specifically; six belong to the path of seeing and three to the path of cultivation; and, while only two are fruits of the worldly path, all nine are fruits of the uncontaminated path, since the power of the uncontaminated path alone is capable of reaching every one of them.

Chinese Source

如是九遍知問幾是靜慮果。答九是靜慮及眷屬果。問幾是無色果。

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Running the same nine items through five or six independent classification schemes in quick succession is a compact demonstration of how densely cross-referenced this taxonomy actually is: no single axis — absorption-fruit, path-of-seeing-fruit, dharma-cognition-fruit — fully determines a given full understanding's own place in the system, and only the intersection of several axes at once pins each of the nine down completely.

3. When Is Craving for Form Truly Exhausted?

A genuine puzzle occupies three successive masters: for someone already free of the form realm's own defilement before entering the noble path, at exactly what moment does he attain the full understanding of the exhaustion of craving for form? Three proposed answers — at the very moment of attaining the third fruit, later upon becoming free of the formless realm's first attainment, or later still at arhatship itself — are each refuted in turn, the treatise settling instead on a subtler answer: only once such a person actually gives rise to the path of superior progress beyond his current fruit does he retroactively earn this full understanding, a conclusion defended by tracing out the absurd consequence that would follow — a genuine noble one dying without the uncontaminated pleasure-faculty — were this not so.

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: for one who has already become free of the form realm's own defilement before entering the state of correct determination, when does he attain the full understanding of the exhaustion of craving for form? The Venerable Saṃghavasu says: he attains it at the moment of the subsequent cognition regarding the path, since, at that very moment, he is named as abiding in the fruit and also as abiding in the approach toward the next. The verdict: this explanation should not be given, since the moment of abiding in the fruit is not properly named abiding in the approach — that is, at the very moment of attaining the fruit, not even a single thought-moment of the approach's own path has yet become presently manifest — how, then, could it be named the approach? There are further masters who say: he attains the full understanding of the exhaustion of craving for form only afterward, once he becomes free of the sphere of infinite space's own defilement, since it is then that he cultivates the future uncontaminated absorption-grounds that counteract that very cutting-off. This explanation, too, should not be given, since, at that moment, he cultivates only the future uncontaminated formless-attainment counteractions, not the form-realm counteraction. There are further those who say: he attains this full understanding of the exhaustion of craving for form only later still, once he subsequently attains the fruit of arhatship, at the moment the vajra-like absorption becomes presently manifest, since it is then that he comprehensively realizes, as a single, unified possession of freedom from binding, the cutting-off of every affliction abandoned by both seeing and cultivation across all three realms at once. This explanation, too, should not be given, since, at that moment, every cutting-off is comprehensively gathered into one, and is named the full understanding of the exhaustion of every fetter whatsoever — how, then, could it be said that the full understanding of the exhaustion of craving for form is attained there? It should instead be said: it is precisely at the moment he certainly gives rise, from the fruit itself, to the path of superior progress becoming presently manifest that he then attains this full understanding of the exhaustion of craving for form. Were one not to grant that he certainly gives rise, from the fruit, to the superior-fruit path becoming presently manifest, then, for those who have already become free of the third absorption's own defilement and, relying on a lower ground, enter the state of correct determination at the moment of the subsequent cognition regarding the path, attaining the third fruit — since they have not yet given rise to the superior-fruit path becoming presently manifest, were such a one to die and be reborn in the fourth absorption or in a formless realm, he ought then to fail to possess the uncontaminated pleasure-faculty. But, were this so, it would contradict the Ten-Gates Nyāsa, which states, when asked who possesses the pleasure-faculty, that the answer is: whoever is reborn in the sphere of universal purity, or below it, or, being a noble one, above it. In order to avoid this very fault, it must be granted that, once a fruit has been attained, one certainly gives rise, from that fruit, to the superior-fruit path — and it is precisely at that moment that one is properly said to attain the full understanding of the exhaustion of craving for form.

should such a one pass through a further rebirth, he is certainly a family-to-family. If one who has already become free of the desire realm's own seventh or eighth grade of affliction enters the state of correct determination, at the moment of the subsequent cognition regarding the path, one attains the fruit of once-returner; should such a one pass through a further rebirth, he is certainly a one-interval. Were one not to grant that, having attained the noble fruit, he certainly gives rise, from that fruit, to the superior-fruit path, then, should such a one pass through a further rebirth, how could he come to possess the uncontaminated counteracting faculty belonging to the third, fourth, seventh, or eighth grade at all?

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The argument's structure is a clean example of proof by absurd consequence in this tradition's own hands: rather than arguing directly for the preferred timing, the treatise shows that denying it would force a genuine noble one, dying at exactly the wrong moment, into contradicting an entirely separate treatise's own settled doctrine about who possesses the pleasure-faculty — and it is this collision between two independently established doctrines, not direct argument, that ultimately settles the timing question.

4. Does Nine Contain Everything, or Everything Contain Nine?

The treatise poses the mirror question to fascicle 59's own "containing" debate: does the ninefold full understanding contain every cutting-off whatsoever, or does every cutting-off contain the ninefold? The answer is asymmetric — every cutting-off contains the nine, since its own substance is broad enough to include them, but the nine cannot contain every cutting-off, their own substance being too narrow — and four separate categories of cutting-off are shown to fall outside the nine entirely: the earliest moments of the path of seeing, before its first two full understandings are complete, and three intermediate stages of the path of cultivation, one for each realm, where the conditions establishing that realm's own full understanding remain not yet satisfied.

Ru-Yi Translation

Does the ninefold full understanding contain every kind of full understanding, or does every kind of full understanding contain the ninefold? The answer: every kind contains the nine, not the nine every kind. Here, "the nine" is exactly as stated above; "every kind" refers to these nine together with every other cutting-off — since the substance of "every kind" is broad, it is capable of containing the nine, while the substance of these nine is narrow, and is incapable of containing every kind — exactly as a large vessel is capable of covering a small one, but a small vessel is incapable of covering a large one. What, then, is not contained? That is, once the cognition regarding suffering has arisen, prior to the cognition regarding its arising, the exhaustion of the three realms' own fetters abandoned by seeing suffering is not contained among the nine — that is, the entire cutting-off of the desire realm's own dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering, attained at the moment the receptivity regarding suffering ceases and the cognition regarding suffering arises, together with the entire cutting-off of the form and formless realms' own dharmas abandoned by seeing suffering, attained at the moment the subsequent receptivity regarding suffering ceases and the subsequent cognition regarding suffering arises — such cutting-offs as these are not contained among the nine, since the conditions establishing the path of seeing's own first two kinds of full understanding are not yet satisfied. 8. The Pratyekabuddha and the Bodhisattva

11. Sudden and Gradual Binding in the Desire Realm

The treatise works through all four logical possibilities for the desire realm's own two sections of fetters: sudden binding does occur, when an ordinary person who has become free of that realm's defilement regresses or is reborn there from a higher realm, instantly reacquiring however many grades correspond to the wrapping-defilement that triggered it; sudden freeing likewise occurs, the nine grades being cut off all at once, grade by grade, "like grass cut down in successive swaths"; but gradual binding never occurs, since a fetter abandoned by seeing and one abandoned by cultivation are never acquired in sequence, one before the other; while gradual freeing does occur, since noble ones always cut off what is abandoned by seeing before what is abandoned by cultivation.

14. How Many Paths Does It Take?

The treatise settles two further technical questions about becoming free of any given ground's nine grades of defilement. On whether three paths suffice or nine are required, an initial position — that ordinary persons need only three paths while noble ones require nine — is rejected as backward, since it would make ordinary persons appear more efficient than noble ones; the correct account instead distinguishes them by what they cut off with those nine paths, ordinary persons bundling seeing and cultivation together while noble ones cut off the entire ninefold fetter abandoned by seeing suddenly in a single path, reserving the nine for what is abandoned by cultivation alone. On whether uniform preparatory effort suffices, the treatise declares the matter genuinely unfixed, ranging anywhere from a single preparatory effort to nine.
