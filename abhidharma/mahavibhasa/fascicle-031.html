A Buddha Disguised as a Potter

Fascicle 31 completes the unique buddha-dharmas begun in fascicle 30 — the four kinds of fearlessness, great compassion's extended etymology culminating in a buddha who becomes a potter, hunter, or boatman to reach beings no other way could save, the three foundations of mindfulness, and the seven and five excellent qualities that close the eighteen unique buddha-dharmas — before opening a foundational new topic: analytical cessation, non-analytical cessation, and the cessation of impermanence, worked through an escalating debate over how many entities cessation truly comprises. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 31 continues the third part of the Love-and-Respect Nyāsa (愛敬納息第四之三), opened in fascicle 29. Terms established in earlier fascicles, such as the ten powers, the sixteen aspects, and the four noble truths, recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. The Four Kinds of Fearlessness

Fascicle 31 opens by continuing directly from the ten powers, naming the Buddha's four kinds of fearlessness — the fearlessness of perfect enlightenment, of the permanent exhaustion of contaminants, of proclaiming what obstructs the path, and of proclaiming the path of emergence — each cast in the same formula: a challenge no ascetic, brahmin, god, demon, or Brahmā anywhere in the world could ever mount, met with a lion's roar that turns the great wheel of Brahmā.

The Buddha's ten powers have already been explained; next, the four kinds of fearlessness shall be explained. What are these four? First, the fearlessness of perfect and complete enlightenment. As the sūtra states: "I am one who has perfectly and completely awakened to all dharmas — should there exist, in the world, any ascetic, brahmin, god, demon, or Brahmā, who, relying on the Dharma, should raise an objection, or cause it to be recollected that there is no such thing as perfect and complete enlightenment with respect to these dharmas — there exists no such possibility; and, were there to exist such a possibility, I would, with respect to this matter, having correct vision grounded in nothing else, attain ease, free of any dread or fear, and would myself proclaim: I abide in the rank of the great sage, and, amid the great assembly, rightly roar the lion's roar and turn the great wheel of Brahmā, which no ascetic, brahmin, god, demon, or Brahmā in the world is able to turn back." Second, the fearlessness of the permanent exhaustion of contaminants. As the sūtra states: "I have already permanently exhausted every contaminant — should there exist, in the world, any ascetic, brahmin, god, demon, or Brahmā, who, relying on the Dharma, should raise an objection, or cause it to be recollected that there exists some contaminant not yet permanently exhausted — there exists no such possibility; and, were there to exist such a possibility," and so on, at length. Third, the fearlessness of proclaiming the obstructing dharmas. As the sūtra states: "I proclaim, for the sake of my disciples, dharmas capable of obstructing the path — defilement by them will certainly obstruct one's progress. Should there exist, in the world, any ascetic, brahmin, god, demon, or Brahmā, who, relying on the Dharma, should raise an objection, or cause it to be recollected that these obstructing dharmas, though defiling, do not in fact obstruct — there exists no such possibility; and, were there to exist such a possibility," and so on, at length.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The formulaic repetition across all four fearlessnesses — the same impossible challenge, the same unshakable confidence, the same lion's roar — is not padding but the whole point: each fearlessness names a different specific claim, yet the treatise insists the underlying psychological state, complete freedom from any possible refutation, is structurally identical every time, since genuine fearlessness cannot be partial or occasional without ceasing to be fearlessness at all.

2. The Four Fearlessnesses Analyzed, and Their Distinction from the Ten Powers

The treatise works through the four fearlessnesses' own technical profile — their realm, stage, support, aspects, objects, and more — before revealing that each fearlessness is in fact identical to one specific power already explained, and generalizing this into the striking claim that each of the ten powers actually subsumes all four fearlessnesses and vice versa, yielding forty powers and forty fearlessnesses in reality, though conventionally spoken of as only ten and four. A first cluster of definitions then works to distinguish power from fearlessness despite this deep interpenetration.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The move from “ten powers, four fearlessnesses” to “in truth, forty of each” is a small masterclass in how this treatise handles apparent redundancy in canonical enumerations: rather than treating the mutual subsumption as evidence that the two categories collapse into one, it insists that the conventional count of fourteen names the same reality the technical count of eighty describes, with the smaller number simply naming what is “most manifestly apparent” about the same underlying accomplishment.

3. Nine Pairs of Distinctions Between Power and Fearlessness

A genuinely profound metaphysical question follows: when different sentient beings each attain liberation, do they all realize one and the same cessation held in common, or does each realize an entirely separate cessation of his own? 17. A Final Question: Is Cessation Identical to the Aggregates?

The fascicle closes on a sharp philosophical dilemma. If analytical cessation's own nature were simply identical to the aggregates, then, since every sentient being has always already possessed the aggregates, everyone would already have been liberated from the very beginning — an absurd conclusion. Yet if cessation were merely the bare absence of the aggregates, it becomes unclear what the noble path could possibly be diligently cultivating toward, since a mere absence requires no cultivation at all. The treatise's own resolution: cessation is neither of these — it is a distinct nature, genuinely obtained, yet obtained specifically within the very same contaminated aggregates it liberates one from.
