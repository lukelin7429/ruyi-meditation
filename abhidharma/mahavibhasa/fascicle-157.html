A New Aggregate: Is Attainment Real?

Fascicle 157 opens the entire Seventh Aggregate, the Concentration Aggregate, with the Attainment Nyāsa's foundational argument that attainment and non-attainment are genuinely real entities — then works through an exhaustive cross-tabulation of both across time, moral quality, realm, path-stage, and abandonment-category. 1. A New Aggregate Opens: Is Attainment Even Real?

Fascicle 157 opens the Seventh Aggregate, the Concentration Aggregate, with its own first nyāsa, the Attainment Nyāsa — and the treatise begins not with technical detail but with first principles, asking why it composes this discussion at all: to refute the view that the past and future do not exist, and to establish attainment (prāpti) as a genuinely real entity against the Sautrāntika position that "possession" is a mere conventional label, like five fingers conventionally called a fist.

Chinese Source

諸得過去法。彼得過去耶。如是等章及解章義。既領會已應廣分別。問何故作此論。答欲止他宗顯己義故。謂或有執。過未是無而說現在是無為法。為遮彼執顯過未有現非無為故作斯論。所以者何。若無過去未來者。應無有情成就彼法。及不成就如第二頭。第三手。第六蘊。第十三處。第十九界等。無有成就不成就者。然有成就過去未來。及不成就故知實有。或復有執。成就非實有法。如譬喻者作如是論。諸有情類不離彼法。說名成就此無實體。但是觀待分別假立。如五指合名之為拳。離即非拳故非實有。如是有情不離彼法說名成就。離即不成就故體非實有。問彼何故立此論耶。答依契經故。如契經說有轉輪王成就七寶。若此成就是實有者。應成就他身及非有情數。謂彼輪王若成就輪寶神珠寶者則法壞。亦是有情數法。亦是非有情數法。若成就象寶馬寶者則趣壞。亦是人趣亦傍生趣。若成就女寶者則身壞。亦是男身亦是女身。若成就主藏臣寶兵將寶者則業壞。亦是王亦是臣。勿有此過故成就非實有。為止彼宗顯成就體是實有故而作斯論。若成就體非實有者便違經說。如說有學成就八支。漏盡阿羅漢成就十支。若成就非實者。彼聖者有漏心現前。及無心時便不成就三世聖道。云何成就八支十支。以支皆是無漏法故。又若成就非實有者。復違餘經。如餘經說。此補特伽羅成就善法及不善法。若成就非實有者。彼起善法時應不成就不善法。起不善法時應不成就善法。起無記法時應俱不成就。又若成就非實有者。復違餘經。如說若苾芻成就七妙法者。於現法中多住喜樂。彼應成就一妙法或不成就。謂七妙法隨一現前。時彼苾芻但成就一。以七妙法皆慧為性。尚無二慧俱起況當有七。若起餘法現在前時則七妙法皆不成就。又若成就非實有者。復違餘經。如說如來應正等覺成就十力。彼應但說成就一力。或不成就。謂若隨起一力現前。餘九離身便不成就。以十力皆慧為體。無二慧俱起故。若起餘法現在前時。是則十力皆不成就。又若成就非實有者。復有餘過異生應名離三界染。諸阿羅漢應名異生。謂諸異。生起善無覆無記心。及無心時身中現無煩惱。復不成就過去未來。豈不名為離三界染。諸阿羅漢起有漏心及無心時。現無聖道。復不成就過去未來。豈非異生無聖法故若爾便為大過。是故成就決定實有。問若成就是實有者。譬喻者所引經云何通。答彼說自在名為成就。謂轉輪王於自七寶攝御自在。假說成就非如成就學八支等。或復有執。成就雖實有體。而不成就。無有實體。為遮彼執顯不成就亦有實體故作斯論。若不成就無實體者。成就亦無實體。觀不成就說成就故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Whatever dharma is obtained as past — is it obtained as past?" Such chapter-headings and their explanatory glosses, once grasped, call for full analysis. Question: why is this treatise composed? Answer: to check other schools and to make our own position clear. For there are those who hold that the past and future do not exist, yet who nonetheless speak of the present as though it were unconditioned [permanent] — and it is to block this view, and to establish that the past, future, and present are all real while remaining distinct from the unconditioned, that this treatise is composed. Why so? If there were no past and future, then no sentient being could possess [成就, i.e. attain and retain] any dharma at all, nor fail to possess it — like a second head, a third hand, a sixth aggregate, a thirteenth sense-base, or a nineteenth sense-element, none of which anyone either possesses or fails to possess, since they do not exist at all. But beings genuinely do possess past and future dharmas, and genuinely do fail to possess them — hence the past and future must be real. There are, further, those who hold that possession itself is not a real entity. The Sautrāntikas argue thus: when a class of sentient beings is said to be inseparable from a certain dharma, this is called "possession," yet possession itself has no real substance — it is merely a conventional designation posited relative to something else, just as five fingers joined together are conventionally called a "fist": once separated, there is no fist, precisely because a fist was never a real entity to begin with. In just the same way, when sentient beings are inseparable from a certain dharma, this is called "possession" — yet once separated, there is no possession, precisely because possession itself was never real. Question: on what grounds do they establish this position? Answer: on the authority of scripture. A sūtra states that a wheel-turning king possesses the seven treasures. If this possession were a real entity, the argument runs, then the king would have to possess another being's own body, or even something not classed as a sentient being at all — for if he possesses the wheel-treasure or the wish-fulfilling-jewel-treasure, then the very distinction between dharma-categories collapses, since these are at once classed as belonging to sentient beings and as not belonging to sentient beings. If he possesses the elephant-treasure or the horse-treasure, then the distinction between destinies collapses, since these belong at once to the human destiny and to the animal destiny. If he possesses the woman-treasure, then the distinction of bodily sex collapses, since this is at once a male body and a female body. And if he possesses the treasurer-minister-treasure or the general-treasure, then the distinction of karmic role collapses, since these are at once king and minister. To avoid this absurdity, the Sautrāntikas conclude, possession cannot be a real entity. It is precisely to check that school and to establish that the substance of possession is genuinely real that this treatise is composed. For if the substance of possession were not real, this would directly contradict what the sūtras themselves say. A sūtra states that the trainee possesses the eight factors of the noble path, while the outflow-exhausted arhat possesses the ten factors. If possession were not real, then whenever such a noble one's mind was accompanied by outflows, or whenever that noble one lacked a mind altogether, as in the attainments of no-perception or of cessation, he would fail to possess the noble path belonging to any of the three time-periods — so how could he still be said to possess the eight or the ten factors? For every one of those factors is, by definition, an uncontaminated dharma, which cannot be present in an outflow-accompanied or mindless moment. Furthermore, if possession were not real, this would contradict yet other sūtras. Another sūtra states that a certain person possesses both wholesome and unwholesome dharmas. If possession were not real, then at the very moment a wholesome dharma arose, that person ought to fail to possess any unwholesome dharma, and at the moment an unwholesome dharma arose, he ought to fail to possess any wholesome dharma, and at the moment a neutral dharma arose, he ought to fail to possess either at all. Furthermore, if possession were not real, this would contradict yet other sūtras. Another sūtra states that a monk who possesses the seven excellent dharmas dwells abundantly, in this very life, in joy and happiness. If possession were not real, then such a monk ought to possess only a single excellent dharma at a time, or none at all — since whichever one of the seven excellent dharmas happens to be currently manifest, that monk would then possess only that one, given that all seven excellent dharmas share wisdom as their very nature, and there can never be two instances of wisdom co-arising at once, let alone seven; and whenever some other dharma is manifest instead, then all seven excellent dharmas alike ought to go unpossessed. Furthermore, if possession were not real, this would contradict yet other sūtras. Another sūtra states that the Tathāgata, the Worthy One, the Perfectly Enlightened One, possesses the ten powers. If possession were not real, then he ought to be said to possess only a single power at a time, or none at all — for whichever one of the ten powers happens to be currently manifest, the other nine, being absent from him at that moment, ought to go unpossessed, given that all ten powers share wisdom as their very substance, and there can never be two instances of wisdom co-arising at once; and whenever some other dharma is manifest instead, then all ten powers alike ought to go unpossessed. Furthermore, if possession were not real, a further absurdity follows: an ordinary worldling would have to be called one who has left behind the defilements of the three realms, while arhats would have to be called ordinary worldlings. For when an ordinary worldling gives rise to a wholesome or an unobscured-neutral mind, or lacks a mind altogether, no defilement is currently manifest in his mind-stream, and, on the no-possession view, he would then fail to possess any defilement of the past or the future either — would this not make him one who has left behind the defilements of all three realms? Likewise, when an arhat gives rise to a contaminated mind, or lacks a mind altogether, no noble path is currently manifest, and he would then fail to possess the noble path of the past or the future either — would this not make him, absurdly, an ordinary worldling devoid of any noble dharma at all? If it were so, this would be a very grave error indeed. Therefore possession is, without question, a real entity. Question: if possession is a real entity, then how is the sūtra passage cited by the Sautrāntikas themselves to be explained? Answer: what that sūtra calls "possession" there simply means sovereign mastery — that is, the wheel-turning king exercises full sovereign control over his own seven treasures; this is only a conventional manner of speaking about "possession," and is not possession in the same sense as the trainee's possession of the eight factors and so on. There are, moreover, those who hold that although possession has real substance, non-possession has no real substance at all. It is to check that view, and to establish that non-possession too has real substance, that this treatise is composed. For if non-possession had no real substance, then possession itself would likewise have no real substance, since possession is only ever spoken of in contrast to non-possession.

如觀夜立晝。觀闇立明。皆實有體。此。亦如是。又不成就是成就近對治。更互相違。如貪無貪瞋無瞋癡無癡定亂等。若無實體何所相違成近對治。又不成就若無體者。應不施設斷諸煩惱。謂聖道起斷諸煩惱。非如以刀割物以石磨物但斷繫得證離繫得令諸煩惱不成就起說名為斷。故知實有不成就性。或復為欲斷疑網故而作斯論。

Just as night is established relative to day, and darkness relative to light, and both are genuinely real entities — so it is here as well, with possession and non-possession. Furthermore, non-possession stands as the proximate antidote to possession, the two being directly opposed to one another, exactly as craving is opposed to non-craving, hatred to non-hatred, delusion to non-delusion, or a settled mind to a distracted one. If non-possession had no real substance, what could it possibly oppose, or serve as the proximate antidote to? Furthermore, if non-possession had no real substance, then one could not even speak of severing the defilements at all. For when the noble path arises and severs the defilements, this does not happen the way a knife cuts an object or a stone grinds it down; rather, it simply severs the bond-attainment that ties the defilement to the mind-stream and realizes the attainment of separation from that bond, causing the arising of the defilement's own non-possession — and it is this alone that is called "severing." Hence non-possession must genuinely exist as a real entity. This treatise is also composed, further, in order to cut through a net of doubt.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The cascade of sūtra citations marshaled here — the wheel-turning king's seven treasures, the trainee's eight path-factors and the arhat's ten, a person's simultaneous possession of wholesome and unwholesome dharmas, a monk's seven excellent dharmas, the Tathāgata's ten powers, and the absurd consequence that ordinary worldlings and arhats would trade places — is argumentation by sheer accumulated weight: no single sūtra proves attainment is real, but the fact that the nominalist reading breaks all of them at once is treated as decisive.

2. A Doubt About Timing: Does Attainment Always Share Its Dharma's Own Moment?

A natural doubt arises from an earlier discussion of characteristics (lakṣaṇa): since a conditioned characteristic never diverges in time-period or instant from the dharma it characterizes, does attainment behave the same way toward the dharma it is attainment of — or can the two actually come apart in time?

Chinese Source

謂雜蘊說過去法生老住無常當言過去。乃至廣說。勿有生疑。如相與法不異世。不異剎那。得與法亦爾。如得與法有異世有異剎那。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Miscellaneous Aggregate states that a past dharma's own arising, aging, abiding, and impermanence should themselves be called "past," and so on in full — and this might give rise to a doubt: perhaps, just as a conditioned characteristic and the dharma it characterizes never diverge in time-period nor in instant, attainment and its dharma behave the very same way; or perhaps, instead, attainment and its dharma can actually diverge in time-period and diverge in instant —

相與法亦爾。為令此疑得決定故。明相與法定不異世。不異剎那。

and characteristic and dharma might likewise diverge, one could wonder. In order to settle this doubt with certainty, the treatise makes clear that a characteristic and the dharma it characterizes definitely never diverge in time-period, nor diverge in instant,

而得與法有同有異。謂三世法一一各有三世得故。問何故相與所相世及剎那決定無異。得與所得或同或異耶。答相與所相。必同一果。相隨行不相離。無前後。於同聚法不能棄捨。得與所得不同一果。非定相隨非不相離。或有前後。於同聚法或能棄捨。如諸樹皮性離於樹。是故為止他宗顯於己義及令疑者得決定故。設不止他顯己令疑決定。但於法相相應義中。應顯所明故作斯論。

whereas attainment and its dharma do show both sameness and difference — for a dharma belonging to any one of the three time-periods has, in each case, an attainment belonging to all three time-periods. Question: why is it that a characteristic and the dharma it characterizes are necessarily never different in time-period or in instant, while attainment and the dharma attained can be either the same or different? Answer: a characteristic and the dharma it characterizes necessarily share a single fruit — the characteristic accompanies its dharma without ever separating from it, admits no priority or posteriority between them, and cannot be discarded so long as the two remain within the very same cluster. Attainment and the dharma attained, however, do not share a single fruit in this way — they are not necessarily mutually accompanying, nor necessarily inseparable, may admit priority and posteriority between them, and can, within the very same cluster, sometimes be discarded — much as bark, though belonging to a tree, can nonetheless be separated from it. It is therefore in order to check other schools and to make clear our own position, and to bring certainty to those in doubt, that this treatise is composed; or, absent any need to check others or clarify our own position, simply in order to make explicit, within the topic of the mutual association among dharma-characteristics, what ought to be made explicit — for this reason too the treatise is composed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The resolution turns on a sharp technical distinction between two kinds of relationship: a characteristic and its dharma share a single fruit and can never be discarded independently, like an inseparable escort, while attainment and its dharma merely tend to travel together without being bound to — comparing the latter to bark that belongs to a tree yet can still be peeled away from it.

3. Eleven Kinds of Obtainable Dharmas, and Two Patterns of Attainment Across Time

The treatise catalogs the eleven categories of dharma capable of being obtained at all — four in the desire realm, three each in the form and formless realms, plus the uncontaminated — and introduces a key technical distinction: some dharmas have attainment that is "temporally mixed and instant-mixed" (spanning all three time-periods regardless of when the dharma itself occurs), while others have attainment that tracks the dharma's own time-period exactly, never mixing.

Chinese Source

諸得過去法彼得過去耶。此中得言欲何所顯。謂獲成就。云何知然。如施設論說。得云何謂獲成就。獲云何。謂得成就。成就云何。謂獲得。得獲成就聲雖有別而義無異。所得法類有十一種。欲界有四。謂善不善。有覆無記。無覆無記。色界有三。除不善。無色界亦爾。及無漏法。欲界善不善無覆無記各具五蘊。有覆無記唯有四蘊。色界三各具五蘊。無色界三各唯四蘊。無漏法具五蘊。及擇滅非擇滅。除虛空無為非所得法故。此中欲界善不善色。若在過去有三世得。若在未來唯有未來得。若在現在有二世得。謂未來現在。善不善有覆無記四蘊。及無覆無記中。通果心俱生品四蘊。彼得世雜剎那雜。謂在過去未來現在。皆具三世得故。無覆無記一切色蘊異熟生四蘊。及威儀路。工巧處。多分四蘊彼得世不雜剎那不雜。若在過去得亦過去。若在未來得亦未來。若在現在得亦現在。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Whatever dharma is obtained as past — is it obtained as past?" Here, what does the word "obtain" [得, prāpti] itself intend to convey? It means to secure and to possess. How is this known? The *Prajñapti-śāstra* states: what is "obtaining"? It is securing and possessing. What is "securing"? It is obtaining and possessing. What is "possessing"? It is securing and obtaining. Though the terms "obtain," "secure," and "possess" differ in wording, their meaning is identical. The dharmas that can be obtained fall into eleven categories: in the desire realm there are four — wholesome, unwholesome, obscured-neutral, and unobscured-neutral; in the form realm there are three, excluding the unwholesome; likewise in the formless realm, three; and, in addition, the uncontaminated dharmas. The desire realm's wholesome, unwholesome, and unobscured-neutral categories each comprise the full five aggregates, while its obscured-neutral category comprises only four aggregates, lacking form. Each of the form realm's three categories comprises the full five aggregates; each of the formless realm's three categories comprises only four aggregates. The uncontaminated dharmas comprise the full five aggregates, together with analytical and non-analytical cessation — space, being an unconditioned dharma, is excluded, since it cannot be obtained at all. Now, as for the desire realm's wholesome and unwholesome matter: if it lies in the past, it has attainments in all three time-periods; if it lies in the future, it has only a future attainment; if it lies in the present, it has attainments in two time-periods, namely future and present. As for the wholesome, unwholesome, and obscured-neutral four non-material aggregates, together with the unobscured-neutral four aggregates belonging to the class arisen simultaneously with a resultant-fruit mind — their attainment is both temporally mixed and instant-mixed, meaning that whether the dharma itself lies in the past, future, or present, it possesses attainments spanning all three time-periods alike. But the unobscured-neutral dharmas comprising every kind of matter, the four aggregates produced by ripening, and most of the four aggregates belonging to deportment and to craftsmanship — their attainment is neither temporally mixed nor instant-mixed: if the dharma lies in the past, its attainment likewise lies only in the past; if in the future, its attainment likewise lies only in the future; if in the present, its attainment likewise lies only in the present.

威儀路四蘊中。善串習者如佛馬勝。及餘有情所善串習。并工巧處四蘊中善串習者。如佛妙業天子。及餘有情所善串習。彼得亦皆世雜剎那雜。謂在三世各有三世得。故色界善五蘊有覆無記。及無覆無記中。通果心俱生品四蘊。彼得世雜剎那雜。謂在三世各有三世得故不定。善色蘊如欲界善不善色蘊。說一切有覆無記無覆無記色蘊。及威儀路異熟生四蘊。彼得世不雜剎那不雜。隨在彼世。即唯有彼世得。故無色界善有覆無記四蘊彼得世雜剎那雜。謂在三世各有三世得故。異熟生四蘊彼得世不雜剎那不雜。隨在彼世。即唯有彼世得故。無漏五蘊彼得。亦世雜剎那雜。謂在三世各有三世得故。此則總說。若別說者。諸未曾得無漏五蘊。及未曾得有漏修所成。并未曾得聞思所成。彼最初得。若在未來彼法唯有未來得。若在現在彼法則有未來現在得。若在過去彼法則有三世得。擇滅非擇滅法雖非三世攝而有三世得。然擇滅得有二種。謂有漏無漏。有漏擇滅得。由離欲界染乃至無所有處染故起。是世俗道類。若未離染彼滅唯有過去未來得。若已離染彼滅。即有三世得。無漏擇滅得。

Among the four aggregates of deportment, however, those that have been thoroughly practiced to mastery — as with the Buddha's own disciple Aśvajit, and with other beings who have likewise thoroughly practiced them — and, likewise, among the four aggregates of craftsmanship, those thoroughly practiced to mastery — as with the Buddha's own disciple the god Sukarmadeva, and with other beings who have likewise thoroughly practiced them — their attainment, too, is both temporally mixed and instant-mixed, meaning that in each of the three time-periods there is an attainment belonging to all three time-periods. As for the form realm's wholesome five aggregates, its obscured-neutral aggregates, and the unobscured-neutral four aggregates belonging to the class arisen with a resultant-fruit mind — their attainment is also temporally mixed and instant-mixed, meaning that in each of the three time-periods there is an attainment belonging to all three time-periods; this, however, is not fixed. The wholesome aggregate of form behaves like the desire realm's wholesome and unwholesome aggregate of form described above — that is to say, every obscured-neutral and unobscured-neutral aggregate of form, together with the four aggregates of deportment produced by ripening, have an attainment that is neither temporally mixed nor instant-mixed: wherever the dharma itself lies, its attainment lies there too, and there alone. Likewise the formless realm's wholesome and obscured-neutral four aggregates have an attainment that is temporally mixed and instant-mixed, meaning that in each of the three time-periods there is an attainment belonging to all three time-periods; while its four aggregates produced by ripening have an attainment that is neither temporally mixed nor instant-mixed — wherever the dharma itself lies, its attainment lies only there. The uncontaminated five aggregates likewise have an attainment that is temporally mixed and instant-mixed, meaning that in each of the three time-periods there is an attainment belonging to all three time-periods. This, however, is only a general statement. Stated more precisely: as for any uncontaminated five aggregates never previously obtained, any contaminated dharma cultivated through meditative practice never previously obtained, and any dharma accomplished through hearing or reflection never previously obtained — their very first attainment, if it lies in the future, has only a future attainment; if it lies in the present, has both a future and a present attainment; and if it lies in the past, has attainments in all three time-periods. Analytical and non-analytical cessation, though not themselves classed under any of the three time-periods, nonetheless have attainments in all three time-periods. Now, the attainment of analytical cessation is of two kinds, contaminated and uncontaminated.
