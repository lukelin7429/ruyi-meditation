The Nyāsa of Views: A Catalogue of Wrong Views

Fascicle 198 opens the Nyāsa of Views (見納息) — the fifth and final Nyāsa of the Views Aggregate — with a systematic catalogue of the heterodox view-tenets condemned in the sūtras: denying giving and the fruit of conduct, denying rebirth and even biological parentage, denying arhats and the noble path, an annihilationist funeral rite, inverted praise for receiving over giving, strict fatalism that denies any consequence to violence, the seven indestructible bodies, and, at greatest length, a rival cosmology of staggering numerical elaboration. Each view is sorted by which noble truth's direct seeing cuts it off, and the Venerable Vasumitra traces, case by case, the real but flawed reasoning — misread observation, shallow concentration, or the influence of evil companions — that gives rise to it. This page carries Ru‑Yi's complete translation.

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

This fascicle names specific rival teachers and their doctrines — Pūraṇa Kāśyapa's six classes of superior rebirth, figures resembling Makkhali Gosāla's fatalism, and views resembling Ajita Kesakambalī's materialism — presented not as caricature but as reconstructed positions with their own internal logic, each traced to a specific chain of reasoning from observation, concentration, or the influence of companions. Terms established across fascicles 93–197 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. "There Is No Giving, No Fruit of Conduct": Denying the Law of Cause and Effect

The Nyāsa of Views opens by cataloguing the heterodox view-tenets condemned in the sūtras, sorting each by which noble truth's direct seeing cuts it off. The first is nihilism about karma itself — no giving, no fruit of excellent or evil conduct — traced by the Venerable Vasumitra to a real but badly reasoned induction from directly observed, contradictory cases: some who kill live long, some who give grow poor.

Chinese Source

見蘊第八中見納息第五之一諸有此見無施與乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答欲釋契經中所說見趣令知斷故。所以者何。於生死中起大執著引大無義。為大依取者莫如見趣。此等廣說如智蘊五事納息。諸有此見無施與無愛樂無祠祀無妙行惡行。此謗因邪見見集所斷。無施與等如上釋。此邪見者顯彼自性。見集所斷者顯彼對治。謂於集諦忍智已生於彼所有不正推尋不正分別。顛倒見不平等取便永斷滅沒。復次此見依集處轉故見集時則斷。如草頭露日出則乾。此亦如是無妙行惡行果此謗果邪見見苦所斷。此邪見者顯彼自性。見苦所斷者顯彼對治。謂於苦諦忍智已生。於彼所有不正推尋不正分別顛倒見不平等取便永斷滅沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

[Views Aggregate, Chapter Eight, Nyāsa of Views, Part One of Part Five — 見蘊第八中見納息第五之一.] Whoever holds this view, that there is no giving — and so on, as stated at length. Question: why is this treatise composed? Answer: in order to explain the view-tenets spoken of in the sūtras, so that they may be understood and abandoned. Why is this? Because, among all that gives rise to great attachment within birth-and-death and draws one toward great meaninglessness, and among all that serves as a great basis for grasping, nothing surpasses the view-tenets. These are explained at length in the Five Things Section of the Cognition Aggregate. Whoever holds this view — that there is no giving, no delighting in giving, no sacrificial offering, no excellent or evil conduct — this is a wrong view that denies causation, to be abandoned by seeing origin. 'No giving' and the rest are as explained above. That it is a wrong view reveals its intrinsic nature; that it is to be abandoned by seeing origin reveals its antidote — that is, once forbearance and cognition regarding the truth of origin have arisen, all the improper deliberation, improper discrimination, distorted views, and unbalanced grasping belonging to it are thereupon permanently cut off, extinguished, and eliminated. Furthermore, since this view operates within the domain of origin, it is cut off precisely at the moment of seeing origin — just as dew on the tip of a blade of grass dries away the moment the sun rises; this is likewise so. 'There is no fruit of excellent or evil conduct' — this is a wrong view that denies the fruit, to be abandoned by seeing suffering. That it is a wrong view reveals its intrinsic nature; that it is to be abandoned by seeing suffering reveals its antidote — that is, once forbearance and cognition regarding the truth of suffering have arisen, all the improper deliberation, improper discrimination, distorted views, and unbalanced grasping belonging to it are thereupon permanently cut off, extinguished, and eliminated.

復次此見依苦處轉故見苦時即斷。如草頭露日出則乾。此亦如是。

Furthermore, since this view operates within the domain of suffering, it is cut off precisely at the moment of seeing suffering — just as dew on the tip of a blade of grass dries away the moment the sun rises; this is likewise so.

然此但說彼見自性及對治。不說等起。彼等起云何。尊者世友說曰。有諸外道現見世間有殺生長壽離殺短壽。有盜豐財離盜乏財。

Yet this only explains the intrinsic nature and antidote of this view, without explaining what gives rise to it. What, then, gives rise to it? The Venerable Vasumitra explains: there are certain non-Buddhist ascetics who observe directly in the world that some who kill living beings live long, while some who abstain from killing die young; that some who steal grow rich, while some who abstain from stealing become poor;

有慳而富樂施而貧。有損惱他無病安樂。有不惱他而多疾苦。見如是等相違事已便作是念。無施與無愛樂乃至無妙行惡行果。若有者則應殺生一切短壽。乃至不惱他者無病安樂。現見相違故知決定無施與乃至廣說。然彼外道不善了知妙行惡行果有遠近故。於現見事中不如理尋思而起此見。有說。外道得世俗定見少時分不知終始因果差別。見行惡者有得生天。見造善者有墮惡趣。便作是念無施與無愛樂乃至廣說。或有說者。有諸外道不因現見亦不因定。但由隨順惡友教故說無施與乃至廣說。

that some who are stingy grow prosperous, while some who delight in giving grow poor; that some who injure and torment others are healthy and at ease, while some who do not torment others suffer much illness. Having observed such contradictory circumstances, they then reflect: there is no giving, no delighting in giving, and so on up through no fruit of excellent or evil conduct — for, if such fruits existed, then all who kill living beings ought to die young, and, up through, those who do not torment others ought to be healthy and at ease; but what is directly observed contradicts this, and so it is known with certainty that there is no giving, and so on, as stated at length. Yet these ascetics, not properly understanding that the fruit of excellent and evil conduct may be near or far off, reason incorrectly about what is directly observed and give rise to this view. Some say: these ascetics attain a mundane concentration and see only a brief span of time, not knowing the full course of cause and effect from beginning to end; observing some who commit evil yet attain rebirth in heaven, and some who perform good deeds yet fall into an evil destiny, they then reflect: there is no giving, no delighting in giving, and so on, as stated at length. Others further say: there are certain ascetics who arrive at this view neither through direct observation nor through concentration, but solely by following the teaching of evil companions, and so say that there is no giving, and so on, as stated at length.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Vasumitra's explanations of what gives rise to each wrong view are strikingly charitable in method even while judging the conclusions false: these ascetics are shown reasoning from genuine, directly observed anomalies — a thief who prospers, a generous man who starves — and failing only because they cannot see far enough in time to find where the anomaly resolves. The treatise catalogues error as bad induction from real data, not as invention from nothing.

2. No This World, No Other World, No Spontaneous Birth, No Father or Mother

A cluster of views denying rebirth, the intermediate existence, and even biological parentage — the last defended by a verse reducing conception to chance combination, with no debt of gratitude owed to father or mother at all.

Chinese Source

無此世無他世無化生有情。此謗因邪見或見集所斷。或謗果邪見見苦所斷。問他世是不現見說無可爾。此世現見何故言無。答彼諸外道無明所盲於現見事亦復非撥。不應責無明者愚盲者墮坑。復有說者。彼諸外道但謗因果不謗法體。無此世者謂無此世為他世因。或無此世為他世果。無他世者謂無他世為此世因。或無他世為此世果。無化生有情者有諸外道作如是說。諸有情生皆因現在精血等事。無有無緣忽然生者。譬如芽生必因種子水土時節無有無緣而得生者。故定無有化生有情。此或撥無感化生業。或復撥無所感化生。或有說者。化生有情所謂中有。無此世他世者謗無生有。無化生有情者謗無中有。有諸外道言中有無。彼說但應從此世間至彼世間更無第三世間可得。此或撥無感中有業。或復撥無所感中有。或撥中有為生有因。或撥中有為死有果。此邪見者顯彼自性。或見集所斷或見苦所斷者顯彼對治廣說如前。無父無母此謗因邪見見集所斷。問世間父母皆所現見彼以何故謗言無耶。答彼諸外道無明所盲乃至廣說。有說。彼諸外道謗無父母感子之業不謗其體。彼作是論父母自以愛染心故不為子故。然以精血和合緣故彼類自生非謂父母有感子業。如因濕葉糞土等故有諸蟲生。非濕葉等有感蟲業此亦如是。故彼外道有如是頌。

Ru-Yi Translation

'There is no this world, no other world, no spontaneously born beings' — this is a wrong view that either denies causation, to be abandoned by seeing origin, or denies the fruit, to be abandoned by seeing suffering. Question: it may well make sense to deny the other world, since it is not directly observed — but why deny this world, which is directly observed? Answer: these ascetics, blinded by ignorance, deny even what is directly observed; one should not reproach them for this, since the ignorant and the blind fall into pits all the same. Others further say: these ascetics deny only the causal relation, not the substance of the dharmas themselves — 'no this world' means that this world is not the cause of the other world, or that this world is not the fruit of the other world; 'no other world' means that the other world is not the cause of this world, or that the other world is not the fruit of this world. As for 'no spontaneously born beings': there are certain ascetics who say that the birth of every sentient being comes only from present semen and blood and the like, and that there is no such thing as sudden birth without a cause — just as, when a sprout grows, it must depend on a seed, water, soil, and season, and there is no such thing as growth without a cause; therefore it is certain that there are no spontaneously born beings. This either denies the karma that brings about spontaneous birth, or denies the spontaneous birth that such karma brings about. Others further say: spontaneously born beings mean the intermediate existence — 'no this world, no other world' denies rebirth-existence, and 'no spontaneously born beings' denies the intermediate existence. There are certain ascetics who say the intermediate existence does not exist, holding that one passes only from this world directly to that world, with no third world to be found in between. This either denies the karma that brings about the intermediate existence, or denies the intermediate existence that such karma brings about, or denies that the intermediate existence is the cause of rebirth-existence, or denies that the intermediate existence is the fruit of death-existence. That it is a wrong view reveals its intrinsic nature; that it is to be abandoned by seeing origin, or by seeing suffering, reveals its antidote, explained at length as before. 'No father, no mother' — this is a wrong view that denies causation, to be abandoned by seeing origin. Question: father and mother are directly observed everywhere in the world — why, then, do they deny them? Answer: these ascetics, blinded by ignorance, and so on, as stated at length. Some say: these ascetics deny the karma of father and mother that brings about a child, not their substance — they hold that a father and mother, acting solely out of a mind of lustful attachment, do not act for the sake of a child; rather, on account of the mere combination of semen and blood as condition, that kind of being spontaneously arises, not that the father and mother possess any karma that brings about the child — just as, on account of damp leaves and dung and the like, various insects arise, yet the damp leaves and the like do not possess any karma that brings about the insect; this is likewise so. It is for this reason that these ascetics have a verse that says —

男女染心合　女值時無病我從此自有　彼於我何為或有說者。彼諸外道謗父母義不謗其體。如因濕葉糞等生蟲。葉等於蟲非父非母。如是因彼不淨而生。彼復何緣獨於生者有重恩德名父母耶。是故彼類說無父母。此邪見者顯彼自性見集所斷者顯彼對治廣說如前。此中但說彼自性對治不說等起。彼等起云何。尊者世友說曰。有諸外道天暴雨時見諸浮泡便作是念。此從何來滅至何所。但因水雨忽起忽滅。如是有情緣合故生。緣離故死。不從前世來至此生。亦非此生往至後世便決定說無此世無他世。又見世間父母生子水土生芽所見皆從緣合而有。便作是念何處當有化生有情。

'When a man and woman, minds inflamed, come together, and the woman, when the time is right, is without illness, I thereby come to exist of myself — what, then, do they do for me?' Others further say: these ascetics deny the meaning of 'father' and 'mother,' not their substance — just as, on account of damp leaves and dung and the like, insects arise, yet the leaves and the like are neither father nor mother to the insect, since it arises solely from that impure matter as its cause — so too here: by what reason, then, should those alone who give rise to a being be said to hold a weighty debt of kindness and be named father and mother? It is for this reason that these ascetics say there is no father and mother. That it is a wrong view reveals its intrinsic nature; that it is to be abandoned by seeing origin reveals its antidote, explained at length as before. This only explains its intrinsic nature and antidote, without explaining what gives rise to it. What, then, gives rise to it? The Venerable Vasumitra explains: there are certain ascetics who, when heavy rain falls from the sky, observe the floating bubbles on the water and then reflect: from where do these come, and to where do they vanish? — they merely arise suddenly and vanish suddenly on account of the water and rain. In just the same way, they hold, sentient beings are born when conditions combine, and die when conditions disperse, not coming from a former life into this one, nor going from this life into a later one; and so they declare with certainty that there is no this world and no other world. They further observe that, in the world, father and mother give birth to a child, and water and soil give rise to a sprout, and everything observed arises from the combination of conditions; they then reflect: where, then, could there be any spontaneously born being at all?

又見蟲生因濕葉等廣如前說。然彼外道不知情與非情生類有別四生有情。藉緣不等內法外法緣性各異。故於少分相似事中不正尋思起此諸見。復有說者。彼諸外道得世俗定有諸有情從上地及餘世界歿來生此間。或此間歿生於上地及餘世界。彼觀此類不見所從及所往處。便起此見無此世無他世。又彼獲得麁淺定故觀去來世時但見生有。不見中間中有之身以微細故。由此便說無化生有情。又因定力觀諸有情或從怨家來作父母。或從妻子兄弟姊妹來作父母。乃至或從駝驢狗等雜類之身來作父母。復從父母作彼形類便作是念。此如客舍有何決定。由此便說無父無母。復有說者。彼諸外道不因現見亦不因定。但由隨順惡友教故說無此世乃至廣說。

They further observe insects arising from damp leaves and the like, as explained at length before. Yet these ascetics do not know that sentient and non-sentient kinds of birth are distinct, that the four kinds of birth-beings depend on unequal conditions, and that internal and external dharmas differ in the nature of their conditions; and so, from a few superficially similar cases, they reason incorrectly and give rise to these various views. Others further say: these ascetics attain a mundane concentration and observe certain beings passing away from a higher realm or another world-system to be reborn here, or passing away from here to be reborn in a higher realm or another world-system; observing this class of beings, they do not perceive where they came from or where they are going, and so give rise to this view that there is no this world and no other world. Further, because the concentration they have attained is coarse and shallow, when they observe the passing away and rebirth of beings they perceive only the rebirth-existence, and, because the intermediate body between them is subtle, they do not perceive it; on this basis they declare that there are no spontaneously born beings. Further, by the power of concentration, they observe certain beings who come to be their father or mother from having been an enemy, or who come to be their father or mother from having been a wife, child, brother, or sister, and even beings who come to be their father or mother from having had the body of a camel, donkey, dog, or other such mixed kind — and, from being father and mother, they in turn come to take on that same kind of form; they then reflect: this is just like a traveler's inn — what certainty can there be in it? On this basis they declare that there is no father and no mother. Others further say: these ascetics arrive at this view neither through direct observation nor through concentration, but solely by following the teaching of evil companions, and so say that there is no this world, and so on, as stated at length.

3. No Arhats, No True Attainment, No Proper Path

Views that deny the noble path and its fruit outright, rooted in ascetics observing that arhats still age, sicken, and die like anyone else, and mistaking the extinction of the faculties in nirvāṇa for either suffering or mere annihilation.

Chinese Source

諸有此見世間無阿羅漢。此謗道邪見見道所斷。此謗道邪見者顯彼自性。見道所斷者顯彼對治。謂於道諦忍智已生。於彼所有不正推尋不正分別顛倒見不平等取便永斷滅沒。復次此見於道處轉故見道時即斷。如草頭露日出則乾。此亦如是無正至。此謗滅邪見見滅所斷。正至謂涅槃是無漏道所應至故。此謗滅邪見者顯彼自性。見滅所斷者顯彼對治。謂於滅諦忍智已生。於彼所有不正推尋不正分別顛倒見不平等取便永斷滅沒。復次此見於滅處轉故見滅時即斷。如草頭露日出則乾。此亦如是無正行此世他世則於現法知自通達作證具足住。我生已盡梵行已立所作已辦不受後有。如實知此謗道邪見見道所斷。無正行此世他世者謂彼撥無四種正行。則苦遲通等此是謗有學道。餘是謗無學道。此謗道邪見者顯彼自性。見道所斷者顯彼對治廣說如前。此中但說彼見自性及對治。不說等起。彼等起云何。尊者世友說曰。有諸外道見阿羅漢有老病死及受諸苦。同餘有情便說世間無阿羅漢。即是謗無阿羅漢法。又聞涅槃諸根永滅便作是念彼應是苦。復聞涅槃諸行寂滅便作是念彼應是無。又見聖者形貌飲食同餘有情便謂彼無一切聖道。然彼外道不知聖者有漏身異無漏身異涅槃寂樂。非苦非無故起如是差別邪見。有說。外道得世俗定不能觀見聖道涅槃。便作是言無阿羅漢乃至廣說。有說。外道不因現見亦不因定。但由隨順惡友教故。便言世間無阿羅漢乃至廣說。有說。此中應說始騫持事。彼事即是此見等起。諸有此見。乃至活有命者死已斷壞無有。此四大種士夫身。廣說乃至。此邊執見斷見攝。見苦所斷。此中乃至活有命者死已斷壞無有者。彼執有我名為命者。此命者乃至此生未死恒有。死已更不相續名斷壞無有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whoever holds this view — that there are no arhats in the world — this is a wrong view that denies the path, to be abandoned by seeing the path. That it is a wrong view that denies the path reveals its intrinsic nature; that it is to be abandoned by seeing the path reveals its antidote — that is, once forbearance and cognition regarding the truth of the path have arisen, all the improper deliberation, improper discrimination, distorted views, and unbalanced grasping belonging to it are thereupon permanently cut off, extinguished, and eliminated. Furthermore, since this view operates within the domain of the path, it is cut off precisely at the moment of seeing the path — just as dew on the tip of a blade of grass dries away the moment the sun rises; this is likewise so. 'There is no proper attainment' — this is a wrong view that denies cessation, to be abandoned by seeing cessation, since proper attainment means nirvāṇa, which is what the uncontaminated path is meant to attain. That it is a wrong view that denies cessation reveals its intrinsic nature; that it is to be abandoned by seeing cessation reveals its antidote — that is, once forbearance and cognition regarding the truth of cessation have arisen, all the improper deliberation, improper discrimination, distorted views, and unbalanced grasping belonging to it are thereupon permanently cut off, extinguished, and eliminated. Furthermore, since this view operates within the domain of cessation, it is cut off precisely at the moment of seeing cessation — just as dew on the tip of a blade of grass dries away the moment the sun rises; this is likewise so. 'There is no proper conduct in this world or the other, such that, in this very life, one knows for oneself, directly realizes, and dwells having attained: my births are exhausted, the holy life has been established, what was to be done has been done, and there is no further existence to be undergone — knowing this truly' — this is a wrong view that denies the path, to be abandoned by seeing the path. 'No proper conduct in this world or the other' means that they deny the four kinds of proper conduct — namely, painful and slow, and so forth — this denies the path of the trainee, while the rest denies the path beyond training. That it is a wrong view that denies the path reveals its intrinsic nature; that it is to be abandoned by seeing the path reveals its antidote, explained at length as before. This only explains the intrinsic nature and antidote of this view, without explaining what gives rise to it. What, then, gives rise to it? The Venerable Vasumitra explains: there are certain ascetics who observe that arhats grow old, fall ill, and die, and undergo suffering just like other beings, and so declare that there are no arhats in the world — that is, they deny that the dharma of arhatship exists at all. Further, hearing that in nirvāṇa the faculties are permanently extinguished, they then reflect that this must be suffering; further hearing that in nirvāṇa all formations are stilled, they then reflect that this must be mere non-existence. Further, observing that the physical appearance and eating habits of the noble ones are the same as those of other beings, they then hold that they possess no noble path at all. Yet these ascetics do not know that the noble ones' contaminated body differs from their uncontaminated body, and that the tranquil bliss of nirvāṇa is neither suffering nor mere non-existence, and it is for this reason that they give rise to these various distinct wrong views. Some say: these ascetics attain a mundane concentration but are unable to perceive the noble path or nirvāṇa, and so declare that there are no arhats, and so on, as stated at length. Some say: these ascetics arrive at this view neither through direct observation nor through concentration, but solely by following the teaching of evil companions, and so declare that there are no arhats in the world, and so on, as stated at length. Some say: here one should speak of a certain ascetic's matter — that matter is precisely what gives rise to this view. Here, 'up through, that a living being with a life-force, once dead, is utterly broken apart and ceases to exist' means that they grasp at a real self, named the life-force — this life-force, they hold, exists continuously up until this present life has not yet died, but, once dead, does not continue any further, and this is called being utterly broken apart and ceasing to exist.

9. The Seven Indestructible Bodies

A pluralist-materialist view — earth, water, fire, wind, suffering, pleasure, and the life-force as seven independent, indestructible "bodies" that never truly touch or affect one another, so that even beheading someone does not count as killing, since a blade merely passes through the gaps between them. Because it grasps at each element as permanently abiding, the treatise classifies this not as annihilationism but as its opposite: eternalism.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The seven-bodies view is easy to mistake for a forerunner of scientific materialism — matter reduced to a few independent, indestructible elements — yet the treatise classifies it under eternalism, not annihilationism, because what actually does the metaphysical work is not the elements themselves but the claim that each one persists forever unchanged and untouched. Materialism and a belief in the permanent self turn out, on this classification, to be much closer cousins than either side would probably admit.

11. Doubling the Count, and Strict Karmic Fatalism

A variant view doubles the required span of wandering before liberation; a final, related view holds that every experience a person undergoes, without exception, has past deeds alone as its cause, denying any role for present effort — distinguished from the orthodox teaching not by affirming karma's power, which both agree on, but by refusing to admit that anything besides the past ever conditions the present.
