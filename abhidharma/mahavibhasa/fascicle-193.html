Realms, Latent Afflictions, and the Ten Perceptions

Fascicle 193 continues the Nyāsa of the Three States of Existence (三有納息第二之二) with the close of its long combinatorial accounting of aggregates relinquished and attained at every death and rebirth among the three realms, then turns to two linked doctrinal questions: why the latent afflictions of one realm never underlie the dharmas of another, and how the three realms are properly established — not by ground or by station of rebirth, but by the relinquishing of craving. A digression into the literal shape of the cosmos follows, before the fascicle closes with the ten perceptions and a rigorous four-fold analysis of what it means to cultivate a perception as against merely contemplating it. This page carries Ru‑Yi's complete translation.

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 193 opens by finishing the aggregate-accounting exposition that closed fascicle 192, then moves into new terrain: the mutual exclusion of latent afflictions across realms, the criteria for establishing the three realms, a rare glimpse of debate over the literal shape of the cosmos, and the opening analysis of the ten perceptions. Terms established across fascicles 93–192 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The formless realm's internal transitions use only one or two aggregates throughout — no bodily or vocal expression is even possible there, since it has no matter at all — yet the treatise still runs the full wholesome/defiled/neutral combinatorics on what little remains: feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness alone, stripped of everything material.

The debate between realms stacked vertically and realms arrayed side by side never actually gets settled — the treatise records both positions without a verdict — which is itself instructive: for an Abhidharma so exacting about categorial boundaries, on the literal shape of the cosmos it is content to leave two live options standing, so long as each can be squared with the doctrine that liberation reaches the whole of a realm at once.

If, by means of the impermanence-aspect dharma-mindfulness-foundation taking the perception of impermanence as its object, one relinquishes the defilement of the desire realm up through the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, and takes this as the preliminary effort — throughout the paths of preliminary effort, uninterrupted path, and liberation; if by means of this kind of dharma-mindfulness-foundation one refines the faculties from liberated-by-conviction into arrived-through-insight, or from liberated-dependent-on-time into unshakeable, and takes this as the preliminary effort — throughout the paths of preliminary effort, uninterrupted path, and liberation; if by means of this kind of dharma-mindfulness-foundation one engages in the mixed cultivation of dhyāna; and, for those who hold that all dharmas are of ultimate meaning, when the analytical knowledge of meaning arises, together with the cognition of aspiration, the limiting concentration, the formless liberations, and the subtle mind immediately prior to entering the attainment of cessation — some say also when the concentrations of wishlessness and signlessness arise — at all such times one cultivates the perception of impermanence, because the perception of impermanence is manifest in the present, and one also contemplates the perception of impermanence, because one's object is the perception of impermanence. at all such times one does not cultivate the perception of impermanence, because the perception of impermanence is not manifest in the present, and one also does not contemplate the perception of impermanence, because one's object is not the perception of impermanence. Masters outside Kaśmīra explain it differently: there is neither cultivating the perception of impermanence nor contemplating the perception of impermanence when one takes other dharmas as one's object while cultivating other perceptions — as when, taking the aggregates of feeling, formations, and consciousness as one's object, one gives rise to other perceptions from within the aggregate of perception apart from the perception of impermanence.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Cultivating and contemplating a perception turn out to be mutually exclusive at any given moment precisely because both are defined by what serves as the current object: cultivating impermanence means impermanence itself fills the mind, while contemplating it means taking that very perception as an object from a further, reflective remove — the same structural point the text will now apply, tersely, to all nine remaining perceptions in turn.

13. The Remaining Nine Perceptions Follow the Same Pattern

The perception of suffering-in-impermanence and non-self-in-suffering repeat the same fourfold analysis under their own names; the perception of foulness, distaste for food, non-delight in the entire world, death, abandonment, dispassion, and cessation are each distinguished simply by naming what each takes as its own object when manifest.

Fascicle 193 Complete

This closes fascicle 193 of Ru‑Yi's translation. The fascicle opens by finishing the long combinatorial accounting, begun in fascicle 192, of exactly which wholesome, defiled, and neutral aggregates are relinquished, attained, cease, and manifest before the mind at every death and rebirth among the three realms — desire, form, and formless, upward and downward alike. It then turns to why the latent afflictions belonging to one realm never underlie and increase in the dharmas of another, answered through the twin reasons of conflated realms and mismatched objects; to how the three realms are properly established, settling on the relinquishing of craving over ground or station of rebirth, and characterizing each realm through a long cascade of paired presences and absences; to a rare recorded debate over whether the three realms are stacked vertically or arrayed side by side, raised against the teaching attributed to Droṇodana (陀羅達多); and to why non-pervasive latent afflictions stay confined to their own category of dharmas to be abandoned. The fascicle closes by opening the ten perceptions, working out in full the four-fold analysis of cultivating the perception of impermanence as against merely contemplating it, stage by stage from warmth and summit through the path beyond training, before noting that the remaining nine perceptions follow the identical pattern under their own names and objects.
