The Garland of Concentration

Fascicle 104 defines the three concentrations of emptiness, wishlessness, and signlessness in full technical detail, then turns to the garland simile, the elder's own dwelling place, and Śāriputra's own unshakeable calm against grief and a drunken ascetic's boast alike. 1. The Four Inversions, and Why Stream-Enterers Are Singled Out

Fascicle 104 opens by refuting the Vibhajyavādins' claim that there exist twelve inversions, eight abandoned by seeing alone and four requiring cultivation to fully remove — the Vaibhāṣika position insists there are only ever four (mistaking the impermanent for permanent, suffering for pleasant, the impure for pure, and non-self for self), every one of them abandoned the instant suffering itself is directly seen. Their own-nature is pinned to two and a half of the five views, and a precise three-part test — decisive discernment, false augmentation, and unilateral inversion — explains why wrong view and clinging to precepts and vows narrowly miss qualifying. A final puzzle asks why only the stream-enterer, not every faith-follower or dharma-follower who has equally cut off the inversions, is singled out for mention — resolved because the stream-enterer, still keeping house, wife, and fine bedding, invites a doubt about whether he has truly succeeded that the still-more-visibly-renunciate practitioner never does.

Chinese Source

諸預流者。於四顛倒幾已斷。幾未斷耶。答一切已斷。問何故作此論。答為止他宗顯正理故。謂或說有十二顛倒。八唯見所斷。四通修所斷。如分別論者。問彼依何量作如是說。答依契經故。謂契經說。於無常起常想顛倒心顛倒見顛倒。於苦起樂。於無我起我。於不淨起淨想顛倒心顛倒見顛倒。由此故知一切顛倒有十二種。於中八種唯見所斷。謂常我中各取三種樂。淨中各唯取見顛倒。四種通修所斷。謂樂淨中各取想心二顛倒。此中見所斷者。苦類智忍現在前時方畢竟斷。修所斷者。金剛喻定現在前時方畢竟斷。為止彼意顯諸顛倒唯有四種唯見所斷故作斯論。謂此問言諸預流者於四顛倒幾已斷幾未斷。即止顛倒有十二種。此復答言一切已斷即止顛倒通修所斷無修所斷法預流已斷故。又若顛倒有十二種通修所斷便違契經。如契經說。佛告苾芻。若四顛倒所顛倒者。當知彼是愚夫異生。此經既說有四顛倒。應知顛倒非十二種。既說顛倒所顛倒者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the four inversions, how many has every stream-enterer already cut off, and how many has he not yet cut off? The answer: he has already cut off all of them. One asks: for what reason is this treatise composed? The answer: it is in order to put a stop to what other schools maintain, and to display the correct principle — that is, there are those who say: there exist twelve inversions, eight abandoned solely by seeing, four pervading both seeing and cultivation, as the Vibhajyavādins hold. One asks: upon what standard does this same school rely in making this claim? The answer: they rely upon the scripture — that is, the scripture states: with respect to what is impermanent, giving rise to the notion, the mind, and the view of permanence is an inversion of notion, of mind, and of view; with respect to suffering, giving rise to pleasure; with respect to non-self, giving rise to self; with respect to what is impure, giving rise to the notion, the mind, and the view of purity, is an inversion of notion, of mind, and of view — and it is thereby known that every inversion whatsoever numbers twelve kinds. Among these, eight kinds are abandoned solely by seeing — that is, within permanence and within self, each takes three; within purity, each takes solely the inversion of view. Four kinds pervade both seeing and cultivation — that is, within pleasure and within purity, each takes the two inversions of notion and of mind. Here, whatever is abandoned by seeing is only finally and completely cut off at the very moment the forbearance regarding suffering, a subsequent cognition, manifests in the present; whatever is abandoned by cultivation is only finally and completely cut off at the very moment the vajra-like concentration manifests in the present. In order to put a stop to this same intention, and to display that every inversion is solely of four kinds, solely abandoned by seeing, this treatise is composed — that is, this same question states: among the four inversions, how many has every stream-enterer already cut off, and how many has he not yet cut off — this already puts a stop to there being twelve kinds of inversion; the answer, further, states that he has already cut off all of them — this already puts a stop to there being any inversion pervading cultivation, since there exists no dharma abandoned by cultivation that the stream-enterer has not already cut off. Further, were there twelve kinds of inversion, some pervading cultivation, this would then contradict the scripture — exactly as the scripture states: the Buddha said to the monks: whoever is inverted by the four inversions, you should know, is an ignorant, ordinary being. Since this same scripture already states that there exist four inversions, it should thereby be understood that the inversions do not number twelve; since it already states that there is one who is inverted by them,

當知彼是愚夫異生。應知顛倒非修所斷。問若諸顛倒唯有四種。分別論者所引契經當云何通。答想心二種實非顛倒。親近顛倒故。顛倒相應故。亦名顛倒。問若爾受等諸心所法亦應名顛倒。彼與心想義相似故。答世間但於心想二種說顛倒名。非受思等是故但說心想顛倒。佛隨世俗而說法故。

you should know that he is an ignorant, ordinary being — it should thereby be understood that the inversions are not abandoned by cultivation. One asks: if the inversions number solely four kinds, then how is one to properly reconcile the scripture cited by the Vibhajyavādins? The answer: notion and mind, these two, are, in genuine truth, not themselves an inversion at all — it is because they draw near to the inversion, and are associated with the inversion, that they, too, are named an inversion. One asks: if this is so, then feeling and the other mental concomitants, too, ought to be named an inversion, since their own meaning resembles that of mind and notion. The answer: worldly convention speaks of the name "inversion" solely with respect to mind and notion, these two — not with respect to feeling, volition, and the rest — and it is for this reason that the text speaks solely of the inversion of mind and of notion, since the Buddha teaches the dharma in accordance with worldly convention.

問此四顛倒自性是何。答見為自性。問若爾五見中幾見為自性。答此以見苦所斷二見半為自性。謂有身見見取全。及邊執見中常見。

One asks: what is the very own-nature of these four inversions? The answer: they take view as their own-nature. One asks: if this is so, then, among the five views, how many are their own-nature? The answer: they take, as their own-nature, two and a half of the views abandoned by seeing suffering — that is, the whole of the view of self and of view-grasping, together with the view of permanence among the extreme view.

二見半非顛倒自性。謂邪見戒禁取全。及邊執見中斷見。有作是說。此四顛倒於五見中三見各一分以為自性。謂有身見中我見是我顛倒自性非我所見。邊執見中常見是常顛倒自性非斷見。見取中見苦所斷執樂淨見是樂淨顛倒自性非餘見取。問何故唯此是顛倒耶。

The remaining two and a half views are not the own-nature of the inversions — that is, the whole of wrong view and of clinging to precepts and vows, together with the view of annihilation among the extreme view. There are those who say: these four inversions take, as their own-nature, a single portion of each of three of the five views — that is, within the view of self, the view of "I" is the own-nature of the inversion of self, not the view of "mine"; within the extreme view, the view of permanence is the own-nature of the inversion of permanence, not the view of annihilation; within view-grasping, the clinging, abandoned by seeing suffering, to pleasure and to purity is the own-nature of the inversion of pleasure and of purity, not any other portion of view-grasping. One asks: for what reason is it solely these that constitute the inversions?

答以三事故建立顛倒一推度性故。二妄增益故。三一向倒故。邪見斷見雖是推度性及一向倒。而非妄增益壞事轉故。戒禁取雖是推度性及妄增益。而非一向倒。亦於少分實處轉故。謂有色界道能淨欲界有。無色界道能淨色界有。上地道能淨下地。與無漏道斷染證滅義相似故。如是名為顛倒自性我物自體相分本性。

The answer: it is by virtue of three matters that an inversion is established — first, being of the very nature of decisive discernment; second, being a false augmentation; third, being a unilateral inversion. Wrong view and the view of annihilation, although they are indeed of the very nature of decisive discernment and are unilateral inversions, are nonetheless not a false augmentation, since they turn by way of destroying a genuine matter. Clinging to precepts and vows, although it is indeed of the very nature of decisive discernment and is a false augmentation, is nonetheless not a unilateral inversion, since it, too, turns, in a small part, upon a genuine matter — that is, the path belonging to the form realm is capable of purifying existence within the desire realm; the path belonging to the formless realm is capable of purifying existence within the form realm; a path belonging to a ground above is capable of purifying a ground below — this being similar in meaning to the undefiled path's own cutting off of defilement and witnessing of cessation. This, then, is named the inversions' own own-nature, own substance, own inherent nature, and own characteristic.

已說自性所以今當說。問何故名顛倒。顛倒是何義。答於麁諦理顛倒而轉故名顛倒。由此四顛倒唯見苦所斷。問何故四顛倒唯見苦所斷。答由此唯於苦處轉故。見苦諦時此便永斷。復次由此唯於果處轉故。若見果時此便永斷。復次此四顛倒有是有身見。有依有身見有身見斷時。餘亦隨斷對治同故。由此四顛倒唯見苦所斷。復次苦諦麁顯於中迷謬過失極重。賢聖所訶故立顛倒。三諦微隱於中迷謬過非極重。不甚被訶不立顛倒。如人晝日平地顛蹶。世共訶笑非夜嶮處。復次諸瑜伽師現觀苦已心無顛倒。故見苦時顛倒皆斷。設見苦已未見餘三。便出觀者若有問言。此五取蘊為常無常。彼便定答皆是無常。一剎那後必不住故。復若問言此五取蘊為樂為苦。彼便定答一切是苦如熱鐵丸。復若問言此五取蘊為淨不淨。彼便定答皆是不淨如糞穢聚。復若問言此五取蘊有我無我。彼便定答皆無有我。定無作者。無遣作者。亦無受者。無遣受者。唯空行聚。故四顛倒唯見苦所斷。苦類智時一切已斷故。

Having already stated their own-nature, what they are so named for ought now to be stated. One asks: for what reason are they named inversions? What, precisely, is the meaning of "inversion"? The answer: it is because they turn inverted with respect to the coarse truth-principle that they are named inversions — it is on account of this that the four inversions are abandoned solely by seeing suffering. One asks: for what reason are the four inversions abandoned solely by seeing suffering? The answer: because they turn solely with respect to the domain of suffering, and it is at the very moment of seeing the truth of suffering that they are thereby eternally cut off. Further, because they turn solely with respect to the domain of the fruit, and it is at the very moment of seeing the fruit that they are thereby eternally cut off. Further, these four inversions are, in part, themselves the view of self, and, in part, dependent upon the view of self; at the very moment the view of self is cut off, the rest, too, are thereby cut off along with it, their own counteraction being the same, and it is on account of this that the four inversions are abandoned solely by seeing suffering. Further, the truth of suffering is coarse and manifest, and the fault of being deluded and mistaken with respect to it is exceedingly grave, censured by the noble ones, and it is for this reason that an inversion is established there; the remaining three truths are subtle and hidden, and the fault of being deluded and mistaken with respect to them is not exceedingly grave, not greatly censured, and no inversion is established there — exactly as a person who stumbles in broad daylight upon level ground is, in common, censured and mocked by the world, unlike one who stumbles at night in a perilous place. Further, once the various practitioners of yoga have already directly realized suffering, their own mind is without inversion, and it is for this reason that, at the very moment of seeing suffering, every inversion is cut off — even were one, having already seen suffering, not yet to have seen the remaining three, and were to emerge from this same contemplation, were someone then to ask him: are these five aggregates of clinging permanent, or impermanent? — he would necessarily and decisively answer that they are, in every case, impermanent, since they do not abide even a single moment longer; were one, further, to ask him: are these five aggregates of clinging pleasant, or suffering? — he would necessarily and decisively answer that they are, in every case, suffering, exactly like a heated iron ball; were one, further, to ask him: are these five aggregates of clinging pure, or impure? — he would necessarily and decisively answer that they are, in every case, impure, exactly like a heap of filth; were one, further, to ask him: do these five aggregates of clinging possess a self, or are they without self? — he would necessarily and decisively answer that they are, in every case, without any self at all, certainly without any agent, without anyone to command an agent, and without any experiencer, without anyone to command an experiencer, being solely a gathering of empty formations — and it is for this reason that the four inversions are abandoned solely by seeing suffering, since, at the very moment of the subsequent cognition of suffering, every one of them has already been cut off.

問若爾亦有隨信隨法行者。已斷此四顛倒。此中何故不說。而但說言諸預流者一切已斷耶。答應說而不說者當知此義有餘。復次隨信隨法行者有差別相有已斷盡。謂苦類智已生。有未斷盡。謂苦類智未生。是故不說。諸預流者無差別相。皆已斷故此中說之。復次欲令疑者得決定故偏說預流。謂預流者。猶畜妻子處妙臥具。摩執女人著妙衣服。塗飾香花驅役僮僕。捶打縛錄諸有情類。同諸異生有見生疑未斷顛倒。是故說彼已斷顛倒。一來准此故不別說。諸隨信行隨法行者。有漏善心尚不現起。況起染污無覆無記。於彼無疑是故不說。

One asks: if this is so, then there likewise exist those who follow through faith or through the dharma who have already cut off these same four inversions — for what reason, here, does the text not speak of them, but speak solely of every stream-enterer having already cut them off? The answer: this ought indeed to be so stated, and that it is not so stated here should be understood to be a case whose meaning has been left over for elsewhere. Further, those who follow through faith or through the dharma possess a differentiated characteristic — there is one who has already cut them off entirely, this referring to one in whom the subsequent cognition of suffering has already arisen; there is one who has not yet cut them off entirely, this referring to one in whom the subsequent cognition of suffering has not yet arisen — and it is for this reason that they are not spoken of here; every stream-enterer possesses no such differentiated characteristic, having already cut them off without exception, and it is for this reason that they are spoken of here. Further, it is in order to bring resolution to those who harbor doubt that the stream-enterer is specifically singled out and spoken of — that is, the stream-enterer still keeps a wife and children, dwells upon fine bedding, holds and caresses women, adorns himself with fine garments, applies fragrance and flowers, and commands his own servants, whipping and binding various sentient beings, appearing, in this respect, no different from any other ordinary being — those who observe this give rise to doubt as to whether he has genuinely cut off the inversions, and it is for this reason that it is stated that he has already cut off the inversions. The same principle should be applied, without further separate discussion, to the once-returner. As for those who follow through faith or through the dharma, even a contaminated wholesome mind scarcely manifests in the present for them, how much less a defiled or neutral-and-unobscured one — there being no doubt whatsoever with respect to them, they are not spoken of here.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The three-part test for what counts as a genuine inversion is a small masterclass in precise conceptual boundary-drawing: wrong view and the view of annihilation fail to qualify not because they are somehow less wrong than the four true inversions, but because they destroy a genuine matter rather than falsely augmenting one, while clinging to precepts and vows fails for the opposite reason — it is a false augmentation, yet not wholly unilateral, since ground-appropriate practices genuinely do purify a lower ground. An inversion, on this account, requires a very specific combination of being decisively wrong, falsely adding something that was never there, and being wrong about literally everything at once — a demanding standard that turns out to admit only four members.

2. Why the Stream-Enterer Still Feels Desire

A genuinely delicate puzzle asks how the stream-enterer or once-returner, having already cut off every inversion, can still give rise to a defiled craving toward the objects of the five desires — surely craving requires believing the object to be pleasant and pure? The resolution allows that a notion of pleasure does arise, but insists this differs from the inversions cut off at stream-entry: it targets a mere object, not the truth-principle itself, and possesses only a small genuine basis, unlike a full unilateral inversion. Two unforgettable similes dramatize how affliction can override even clear-eyed knowledge — a fastidious brahmin's son who demands his own finger be purified with a branding iron rather than washed, then unconsciously thrusts his burned finger into his own mouth from the pain; and a person with a painful sore who, against better judgment, applies wet dog dung simply because the doctor said it would ease the pain.

Chinese Source

問預流一來。於五欲境起染愛時。為起樂淨想。為起苦不淨想耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: when the stream-enterer or the once-returner gives rise to a defiled craving with respect to the objects of the five desires, does he give rise to a notion of pleasure and of purity, or does he give rise to a notion of suffering and of impurity?

設爾何失。若起樂淨想者如何非顛倒。若起苦不淨想者。如何於境起染愛耶答應作是說。起樂淨想。問若爾何故非顛倒耶。答先作是說。由三事故建立顛倒。預流一來於境所起樂及淨想。雖是妄增益。而非推度性。非見性故。亦非一向顛倒。有少實事故。復次於諦理所起樂淨想者是顛倒攝預流一來皆已斷盡。於境事所起樂淨想者非顛倒攝。預流一來猶未斷盡容有現前。有作是說。預流一來於五欲境起染愛時。於彼唯起苦不淨想。問若爾何故起染愛耶。答彼雖於境知苦不淨。而無始來串習煩惱。燒然身心熱痛所逼。不覺於境而起染愛。如有樂淨婆羅門子。其指有時遇觸不淨。詣鍛師所求火淨之。鍛師語言。幸有灰等洗以清水足可使淨。何能忍苦求火鍛之。婆羅門言。灰水等洗不能令淨。要當為我以火淨之。於是鍛師以熱鐵鉗而鉗其指。時婆羅門熱苦所逼。不覺振手內指口中。彼雖審知指為不淨。苦逼不覺內置口中。預流一來染愛亦爾。復次彼雖於境知苦不淨。而無始來串習煩惱。燒然身心熱痛所逼。為止息故於境染愛。如軟弱者身患癰瘡苦痛難忍。醫語之言。汝可當以濕狗糞塗苦痛可息。其人聞已速取塗之。彼雖審知狗糞不淨。為止苦故而取塗之。預流一來染愛亦爾。故彼雖起苦不淨想。而於五欲起染愛事。諸預流者。於空無願無相三三摩地。成就過去幾未來幾現在幾。問何故作此論。答為止他宗顯正理故。謂或有說。過去未來無實自性。或復有說。無實成就不成就性為遮彼意欲顯實有過去未來成就性等故作斯論。答未來一切。過去若已滅不失。現在若現在前。此中未來一切者具成就三。過去若已滅不失者。隨一二三已滅非練根故。失則成就過去爾所。現在若現在前者。若隨一現在前則成就現在一。且道類智初剎那時不成就過去。無一剎那已生滅故。

Suppose either were so — what fault would there be? If he gives rise to a notion of pleasure and of purity, how could this fail to be an inversion? If he gives rise to a notion of suffering and of impurity, how could he then give rise to a defiled craving with respect to that same object? The answer: it ought to be said that he gives rise to a notion of pleasure and of purity. One asks: if this is so, then for what reason is this not an inversion? The answer: it was earlier stated that it is by virtue of three matters that an inversion is established; the notion of pleasure and of purity that the stream-enterer or the once-returner gives rise to with respect to an object, although it is indeed a false augmentation, is nonetheless not of the very nature of decisive discernment, since it is not of the nature of view, and is likewise not a unilateral inversion, since there exists some small genuine matter to it. Further, whatever notion of pleasure and of purity arises with respect to the truth-principle is gathered within the inversions, and every stream-enterer and once-returner has already cut it off entirely; whatever notion of pleasure and of purity arises with respect to an object is not gathered within the inversions, and the stream-enterer and once-returner have not yet cut it off entirely, this still being capable of manifesting in the present. There are those who say: when the stream-enterer or the once-returner gives rise to a defiled craving with respect to the objects of the five desires, he gives rise, toward them, solely to a notion of suffering and of impurity. One asks: if this is so, then for what reason does he give rise to a defiled craving? The answer: although he indeed knows the object to be suffering and impure, yet, from beginningless time, having been habituated to affliction, his own body and mind burning and pressed by a searing pain, he, without being aware of it, gives rise to a defiled craving with respect to that same object — exactly as there was a brāhmaṇa's son, fond of purity and pleasure, whose own finger once happened to touch something impure, and who went to a blacksmith, requesting that it be purified with fire. The blacksmith said to him: fortunately, there exists ash and the like, capable of washing it with pure water — how could you possibly bear the pain of requesting it be forged clean with fire? The brāhmaṇa said: washing with ash and water is incapable of making it genuinely pure — it must be purified for me with fire. Thereupon the blacksmith, with heated iron tongs, gripped his own finger. At that time, the brāhmaṇa, pressed by a searing pain, without being aware of it, shook his own hand and placed his own finger inside his own mouth — although he had, all along, genuinely known his own finger to be impure, pressed by the pain, he was unaware of placing it within his own mouth — the stream-enterer and the once-returner's own defiled craving, too, is just the same. 4. Why Exactly Three? Not Two, Not a Thousand

A precise justification explains why the Buddha establishes exactly three concentrations rather than the two their own substance would suggest, or the countless variations a moment-by-moment analysis could multiply: three converging conditions — counteraction (each concentration targets a specific affliction), mental resolve (wishlessness names a distinct psychological stance), and object (signlessness names a distinct kind of object) — together carve out exactly three genuinely distinct concentrations from what could otherwise proliferate endlessly. 10. Śāriputra's Own Unshakeable Calm

A deeply moving narrative dramatizes what dwelling in emptiness actually looks like. 12. The Many Meanings of "Signless"

The fascicle closes by untangling the several distinct senses in which the term "signless" gets used throughout the canon — sometimes for the emptiness concentration itself, sometimes for the path of seeing, for unshakeable mental liberation, or for the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. A genuinely touching story illustrates the point: a dull-faculty monk who had attained "signless mental concentration" spent six years following Ānanda everywhere, hoping to overhear an explanation of its fruit without ever daring to ask directly, until he finally worked up the courage — receiving Ānanda's own answer that its fruit is liberation itself, "neither sinking" (since the view of self is cut off) nor "rising" (since the view of what belongs to self is cut off), holding every formation exactly as a dike holds back a pool. The *Dharmamudrā-sūtra*'s own closing definition confirms that this same "signless" concentration, whatever name it goes by, is ultimately gathered within the very own-nature of emptiness itself.
