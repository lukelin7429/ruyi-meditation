Why Eight Kinds of Knowledge?

Fascicle 106 works out the full technical architecture of the eight kinds of knowledge — why eight, what each name means, their complete profile of realm, ground, aspect, and object, and how each gathers in small portions of the others. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 106 continues the Cultivation-of-Knowledge Nyāsa (修智納息第四之二), completing the treatise's own opening architecture of the eight (and ten) kinds of knowledge before moving into their full technical profile and mutual containment. Terms established across fascicles 93–105 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. How Many Kinds of Knowledge Gather In All the Rest?

Fascicle 106 opens the second part of the Cultivation-of-Knowledge Nyāsa's own opening discussion, continuing directly from fascicle 105's closing question of why exactly eight kinds of knowledge: a cascading series shows that one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, or eight kinds of knowledge can each, in their own way, be said to gather in every kind of knowledge whatsoever — from dharma-cognition alone (since its own substance simply is dharma) up through the full eight named at the treatise's own outset.

Chinese Source

或有一智攝一切智。謂法智非如法智以智體是法故。或有二智攝一切智。謂有漏智無漏智。或有三智攝一切智。謂法智類智世俗智。

Ru-Yi Translation

There is a single kind of knowledge that gathers in every kind of knowledge — namely, dharma-cognition, since dharma-cognition is not analogous to the rest, its own substance being dharma itself. There are two kinds of knowledge that gather in every kind of knowledge — namely, contaminated knowledge and undefiled knowledge. There are three kinds of knowledge that gather in every kind of knowledge — namely, dharma-cognition, subsequent cognition, and worldly knowledge.

或有四智攝一切智。謂前三智加他心智。或有五智攝一切智。謂世俗智及苦集滅道智。或有六智攝一切智。謂前五智加他心智。或有七智攝一切智。謂八智中除他心智。或有八智攝一切智。謂此中說法智類智他心智世俗智苦智集智滅智道智。問若此八智攝一切智。

There are four kinds of knowledge that gather in every kind of knowledge — namely, the preceding three kinds of knowledge, together with the knowledge of others' minds. There are five kinds of knowledge that gather in every kind of knowledge — namely, worldly knowledge, together with the knowledge of suffering, of accumulation, of cessation, and of the path. There are six kinds of knowledge that gather in every kind of knowledge — namely, the preceding five kinds of knowledge, together with the knowledge of others' minds. There are seven kinds of knowledge that gather in every kind of knowledge — namely, among the eight kinds of knowledge, excluding the knowledge of others' minds. There are eight kinds of knowledge that gather in every kind of knowledge — namely, what is stated here: dharma-cognition, subsequent cognition, the knowledge of others' minds, worldly knowledge, the knowledge of suffering, of accumulation, of cessation, and of the path. One asks: if these eight kinds of knowledge gather in every kind of knowledge,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The sheer range of correct answers to "how many kinds of knowledge gather in all the rest" — one is right, and so is two, and so is eight — is itself an instructive result: it shows that a classification scheme's own coarseness or fineness is not a fact about the world so much as a choice about how much distinction the occasion calls for, and the treatise is comfortable holding several such choices as simultaneously valid.

2. Ten Kinds of Knowledge, Mapped Back Onto Eight

A further eight named kinds of knowledge from elsewhere in the canon — the knowledge of the abiding of dharmas, of nirvāṇa, of death and birth, of the exhaustion of the outflows, of recollection of former abodes, the wondrous knowledge of aspiration, and the knowledge of exhaustion and of non-arising — are each mapped precisely onto the treatise's own eight, with the Venerable Ghoṣa offering his own alternative count for several of them (the knowledge of death and birth as four kinds rather than worldly knowledge alone, recollection of former abodes as six kinds, the wondrous knowledge of aspiration as all eight) against which the treatise's own verdict is repeatedly preferred.

Chinese Source

復有八智。謂法住智。涅槃智。死生智。漏盡智。宿住隨念智。妙願智。盡智。無生智。如是八智何智攝耶。答隨其所應皆攝在此。

Ru-Yi Translation

there further exist eight kinds of knowledge — namely, the knowledge of the abiding of dharmas, the knowledge of nirvāṇa, the knowledge of death and birth, the knowledge of the exhaustion of the outflows, the knowledge of recollection of former abodes, the wondrous knowledge of aspiration, the knowledge of exhaustion, and the knowledge of non-arising — by which of these eight kinds of knowledge, then, are these further eight gathered in? The answer: in accordance with what is fitting, each is, in every case, gathered in among these same eight.

謂法住智是知因智。正知三界下中上果法所住因故。彼智即此四智所攝。謂法類世俗集智。涅槃智是知滅智彼智即此四智所攝。謂法類世俗滅智。死生智即此世俗智所攝。尊者妙音說。死生智四智所攝。謂法類世俗苦智。評曰。應知此中前說應理。漏盡智諸有欲令緣漏盡法故名漏盡智者。四智所攝。謂法類世俗滅智。諸有欲令漏盡身得故名漏盡智者。八智所攝。宿住隨念智世俗智所攝。尊者妙音說。宿住隨念智六智所攝。即八智中除他心智。緣過去法故。及除滅智。緣有為法故。評曰。應知此中前說應理。妙願智世俗智所攝。尊者妙音說。妙願智八智所攝。謂十智中除盡無生智。是見性故。評曰。應知此中前說應理。盡智無生智俱六智所攝。除他心智非見性故。及除世俗智是無漏故。由此八智攝一切智。

That is: the knowledge of the abiding of dharmas is the knowledge of causes, rightly knowing the causes by which the lower, middling, and upper fruits of the three realms' own dharmas abide — that same knowledge is gathered within these same four kinds of knowledge, namely, dharma-cognition, subsequent cognition, worldly knowledge, and the knowledge of accumulation. The knowledge of nirvāṇa is the knowledge of cessation — that same knowledge is gathered within these same four kinds of knowledge, namely, dharma-cognition, subsequent cognition, worldly knowledge, and the knowledge of cessation. The knowledge of death and birth is gathered within worldly knowledge alone. The Venerable Ghoṣa states: the knowledge of death and birth is gathered within four kinds of knowledge, namely, dharma-cognition, subsequent cognition, worldly knowledge, and the knowledge of suffering. The verdict: it should be understood that the position stated first here accords with principle. As for the knowledge of the exhaustion of the outflows: for whoever wishes to hold that it is so named because it takes the exhaustion of the outflows as its object, it is gathered within four kinds of knowledge, namely, dharma-cognition, subsequent cognition, worldly knowledge, and the knowledge of cessation; for whoever wishes to hold that it is so named because it is attained within a body free of the outflows, it is gathered within all eight kinds of knowledge. As for the knowledge of recollection of former abodes: it is gathered within worldly knowledge alone. The Venerable Ghoṣa states: the knowledge of recollection of former abodes is gathered within six kinds of knowledge — namely, among the eight kinds of knowledge, excluding the knowledge of others' minds, since it takes the past as its object, and excluding the knowledge of cessation, since it takes a conditioned dharma as its object. The verdict: it should be understood that the position stated first here accords with principle. The wondrous knowledge of aspiration is gathered within worldly knowledge alone. The Venerable Ghoṣa states: the wondrous knowledge of aspiration is gathered within all eight kinds of knowledge — namely, among the ten kinds of knowledge, excluding the knowledge of exhaustion and of non-arising, since it is of the very nature of vision. The verdict: it should be understood that the position stated first here accords with principle. The knowledge of exhaustion and the knowledge of non-arising are, both, gathered within six kinds of knowledge, excluding the knowledge of others' minds, since it is not of the very nature of vision, and excluding worldly knowledge, since it is undefiled. It is by virtue of this that these eight kinds of knowledge gather in every kind of knowledge.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The recurring pattern here — the Venerable Ghoṣa proposes a broader mapping, and the verdict narrows it back — is worth noticing as a stylistic signature: this named authority consistently argues for greater inclusiveness in how a given attainment is classified, and the treatise's own governing voice consistently answers by insisting on a stricter, more conservative reading, a small but consistent difference in temperament between the two that recurs across many such exchanges.

3. Why Eight? Two Competing Accounts

The Venerable Saṃghavasu proposes his own elegant reduction: strictly speaking, there is only a single "determinate knowledge," which becomes contaminated or undefiled, then dharma-cognition or subsequent cognition according to what it counteracts, then the knowledge of others' minds according to preparatory effort, then four further kinds according to which of the four truths' own aspects it turns upon — the eight names, on this account, being successive specifications of one and the same underlying capacity. A five-fold rationale is then offered for why exactly eight kinds are established (counteraction, inherent nature, preparatory effort, aspect, and aspect-with-object together), followed by the Venerable Vāmalabdha's own alternative: that the eight kinds of knowledge exist specifically to counteract four kinds of confusion — regarding realm, mind, dharmas, and the truths.

Chinese Source

尊者僧伽筏蘇說曰。應說一智。謂決定智以決定義是智義故。此決定智。有二差別。一者有漏。二者無漏。若有漏者。由自性故名世俗智。若無漏者。由對治差別故。復立二種。謂對治欲界者名法智。對治色無色界者名類智。即前三智若能知他心心所法名他心智。又無漏智由行相別復立四智。若於苦諦作四行相轉者名苦智。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Venerable Saṃghavasu speaks as follows: it ought to be said that there is a single kind of knowledge — namely, determinate knowledge, since the meaning of being determinate is the meaning of knowledge. This same determinate knowledge admits of two distinctions: the first is contaminated, the second is undefiled. If it is contaminated, then, by virtue of its own inherent nature, it is named worldly knowledge. If it is undefiled, then, by virtue of a distinction in what it counteracts, two further kinds are established — that which counteracts the desire realm is named dharma-cognition; that which counteracts the form and formless realms is named subsequent cognition. These same preceding three kinds of knowledge, if capable of knowing the mind and mental concomitants of another, are named the knowledge of others' minds. Further, undefiled knowledge, by virtue of a distinction in aspect, further establishes four kinds — whatever turns by way of the four aspects with respect to the truth of suffering is named the knowledge of suffering,

乃至若於道諦作四行相轉者名道智。問若決定智唯有一種或二或三。云何此中立有八智。答以五事故立有八智。一對治故立法類智。二自性故立世俗智。三加行故立他心智。四行相故立苦集智。

and so on, up through, whatever turns by way of the four aspects with respect to the truth of the path is named the knowledge of the path. One asks: if determinate knowledge is solely of a single kind, or two, or three, how, here, are eight kinds of knowledge established? The answer: it is by virtue of five matters that eight kinds of knowledge are established — first, by virtue of what is counteracted, dharma-cognition and subsequent cognition are established; second, by virtue of inherent nature, worldly knowledge is established; third, by virtue of preparatory effort, the knowledge of others' minds is established; fourth, by virtue of aspect, the knowledge of suffering and of accumulation are established;

五行相所緣故立滅道智。尊者左受作如是說。對治四種愚故立有八智。四種愚者。一界愚。二心愚。三法愚。四諦愚。對治界愚故立法類智。對治心愚故立他心智。對治法愚故立世俗智。對治諦愚故立苦集滅道智。是名八智自性我物自體相分本性已說智自性所以今當說。問何故名智。智是何義。答決定義是智義。問若爾疑相應慧應不名智。於所緣境不決定故。答彼亦是智一剎那頃於所緣境亦決定故。然此聚中疑勢用勝。令心於境多剎那中猶豫不決。說名疑聚。如三摩地一剎那中於境恒住。有時若與掉舉相應。令多剎那於境轉易說名為亂。又如有情若多貪者說名貪行。若多瞋者說名瞋行。若多癡者說名癡行。一一非不有餘煩惱。此亦如是故無有失。

fifth, by virtue of aspect and object together, the knowledge of cessation and of the path are established. The Venerable Vāmalabdha speaks as follows: it is in order to counteract four kinds of confusion that eight kinds of knowledge are established. The four kinds of confusion are: first, confusion regarding realm; second, confusion regarding mind; third, confusion regarding dharmas; fourth, confusion regarding the truths. In order to counteract confusion regarding realm, dharma-cognition and subsequent cognition are established; in order to counteract confusion regarding mind, the knowledge of others' minds is established; in order to counteract confusion regarding dharmas, worldly knowledge is established; in order to counteract confusion regarding the truths, the knowledge of suffering, of accumulation, of cessation, and of the path are established. This, then, is named the eight kinds of knowledge's own inherent nature, own substance, own self-nature, own characteristic, and own fundamental nature — this having already stated the very own-nature of knowledge, what it is so named for ought now to be stated. One asks: for what reason is it named knowledge? What, precisely, is the meaning of "knowledge"? The answer: the meaning of being determinate is the meaning of knowledge. One asks: if this is so, then wisdom associated with doubt ought not to be named knowledge, since, with respect to its object, it is not determinate. The answer: this, too, is knowledge, since, within a single moment, it, too, is determinate with respect to its object; it is simply that, within this same aggregation, the force of doubt is dominant, causing the mind, across many moments with respect to its object, to remain undecided and hesitant, and it is thereby named an aggregation of doubt — exactly as concentration, within a single moment, abides constantly upon its object, yet, at certain times, when associated with restlessness, causes many moments, with respect to its object, to shift and change, and is thereby named distraction. Further, exactly as a sentient being who is much given to craving is named one who conducts himself by craving; one who is much given to aversion is named one who conducts himself by aversion; one who is much given to delusion is named one who conducts himself by delusion — in every single case, this does not mean he is without any further affliction whatsoever — this, too, is just the same, and it is thereby without fault.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Saṃghavasu's account and Vāmalabdha's account are not really in competition so much as answering two different questions: Saṃghavasu explains how a single underlying capacity comes to bear eight different names, while Vāmalabdha explains why exactly these eight functions are needed in the first place — one traces a genetic unfolding from unity to plurality, the other a functional necessity from four gaps in the practitioner's own understanding, and the treatise is content to let both stand as complementary, not rival, explanations.

4. What Does "Knowledge" Even Mean?

Having settled why there are eight, the treatise asks what the very word "knowledge" means: the core answer is determinacy — a decisive settling upon its object — defended against the objection that wisdom associated with doubt, being by definition undecided, ought then not to count as knowledge at all. The resolution distinguishes momentary determinacy (present in every single mental instant) from the coarser, extended indecision that gives an entire mental episode its name, exactly as concentration is genuinely stable moment to moment even when a larger episode gets called "distracted," or as a person prone to craving is still called "one who conducts himself by craving" despite plainly having other afflictions too.

Chinese Source

譬喻者說。若心有智則無無知。若心有疑則無決定。若心有麁則無有細。然對法者所說法相如鬧叢林。謂一心中。有智有無知。有非智非無知。有疑有決定。有非疑非決定。有麁有細。有非麁非細。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Dārṣṭāntikas say: if the mind possesses knowledge, then it is without ignorance; if the mind possesses doubt, then it is without determinacy; if the mind possesses the coarse, then it is without the subtle. Yet what the Ābhidharmikas state regarding the characteristics of dharmas is like a noisy thicket — that is, within a single mind, there is knowledge and there is ignorance; there is what is neither knowledge nor ignorance; there is doubt and there is determinacy; there is what is neither doubt nor determinacy; there is the coarse and there is the subtle; there is what is neither coarse nor subtle.

阿毘達磨諸論師言。許法俱生斯有何失。謂諸心所展轉力生一心相應相用各別智。謂般若無知謂無明非智非無知。謂餘心所法。疑謂猶豫。決定謂智。非疑非決定。謂餘心所法。麁謂尋。細謂伺。非麁非細。謂餘心所法。如諸色法異類俱生。心所亦爾。故無有失。

The various Ābhidharmika treatise-masters say: granting that dharmas co-arise, what fault is there in this? That is, the various mental concomitants, through their mutual, reciprocal force, give rise to a single mind's own associated dharmas, each with its own distinct function — wisdom is named knowledge; ignorance is named that which is neither knowledge nor ignorance, referring to the remaining mental concomitants; doubt is named hesitation; determinacy is named knowledge; that which is neither doubt nor determinacy refers to the remaining mental concomitants; the coarse is named initial inquiry; the subtle is named sustained inquiry; that which is neither coarse nor subtle refers to the remaining mental concomitants — exactly as the various dharmas of form, differing in kind, co-arise together, the mental concomitants, too, are just the same, and it is thereby without fault.

有作是說。於所緣境重決擇義是智義。諸有漏慧於所緣境無始時來數數決擇故皆名智。諸無漏慧重決擇者皆名為智。唯無漏忍於四聖諦。未重決擇故不名智。復有說者。由二義故說名為智。謂證知義。及了知義。證知義者。謂證知苦乃至證知道故名智。了知義者。謂了知自相續了知他相續故名智。

There are those who say: the meaning of weighty, decisive discrimination with respect to an object taken is the meaning of knowledge — every contaminated wisdom, from beginningless time, having repeatedly engaged in decisive discrimination with respect to its object, is, in every case, named knowledge; every undefiled wisdom that engages in weighty, decisive discrimination is, in every case, named knowledge — solely the undefiled forbearance, with respect to the four noble truths, not yet having engaged in weighty, decisive discrimination, is not named knowledge. There are, further, those who say: it is by virtue of two meanings that it is named knowledge — namely, the meaning of confirming knowledge, and the meaning of thorough knowledge. As for the meaning of confirming knowledge: it refers to confirming the knowledge of suffering, and so on, up through, confirming the knowledge of the path, and it is thereby named knowledge. As for the meaning of thorough knowledge: it refers to thoroughly knowing one's own continuum and thoroughly knowing another's continuum, and it is thereby named knowledge.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The three similes assembled here — knowledge-within-doubt, concentration-within-distraction, and one-affliction-within-many — all make the identical philosophical move: they insist that naming an entire mental episode by its single most dominant feature is a convenience of ordinary language, not a claim that the episode's every constituent moment shares that feature, a distinction this treatise treats as important enough to defend with three separate illustrations rather than one.

5. A Noisy Thicket: Can Knowledge and Ignorance Coexist?

The Dārṣṭāntikas hold that a single mind cannot simultaneously possess knowledge and ignorance, doubt and determinacy, or the coarse and the subtle — one member of each pair necessarily excludes the other. The Ābhidharmikas reply that the true situation is like a noisy thicket, thick with apparently contradictory growth: a single mind-moment genuinely does contain knowledge and ignorance, doubt and determinacy, coarseness and subtlety, all at once, since these are simply the distinct functions of distinct mental concomitants arising together, each doing its own separate work within one and the same moment — wisdom serving as knowledge, delusion as ignorance, hesitation as doubt, and so on, exactly as differing forms of physical matter can co-arise without contradiction.

Chinese Source

已總說諸智所以一一所以今當說。問何故名法智。答智體是法故名法智。問若爾餘智亦體是法。何故不名法智耶。答雖一切智體皆是法。而但於一立法智名如十八界。十二處。七覺支。六隨念。四念住。四證淨。四無礙解。三寶。三歸。皆體是法而但於一建立法名。此亦如是故不應責。復次法智但有一名。謂共名。餘智有二名。謂共不共名。為簡別故說不共名。復次初覺知法故名法智。後覺知法故名類智。復次若初得法證淨相應智故名法智。此後所得故名類智。復次於現見法。得現量智故名法智。此後所得故名類智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having already, in general, stated the reason for each of the various kinds of knowledge being so named, each single reason ought now to be stated in turn. One asks: for what reason is it named dharma-cognition? The answer: its own substance is dharma itself, and it is thereby named dharma-cognition. One asks: if this is so, then the remaining kinds of knowledge, too, have dharma as their own substance — for what reason, then, are they not named dharma-cognition? The answer: although every kind of knowledge whatsoever has dharma as its own substance, the name "dharma" is established solely with respect to this one alone — exactly as the eighteen elements, the twelve sense-fields, the seven limbs of enlightenment, the six recollections, the four applications of mindfulness, the four confirmed purities, the four kinds of unhindered understanding, and the Three Jewels and three refuges, are all, every one, of the very substance of dharma, and yet the name "dharma" is established solely with respect to one among them — this, too, is just the same, and ought not to be pressed with an objection. Further, dharma-cognition possesses solely a single name — namely, its shared name; the remaining kinds of knowledge possess two names — namely, a shared name and an unshared name — and it is in order to distinguish it that the unshared name is spoken. Further, it is the dharma first known that is named dharma-cognition; it is the dharma known afterward that is named subsequent cognition. Further, it is by virtue of the knowledge associated with confirmed purity in the dharma, first attained, that it is named dharma-cognition; it is what is attained afterward that is named subsequent cognition. Further, it is with respect to a directly witnessed dharma, attaining direct-perception knowledge, that it is named dharma-cognition; it is what is attained afterward that is named subsequent cognition.

復次欲界多有非法煩惱。謂忿恨覆惱嫉慳等相應煩惱。若智是彼近對治者名法智。色無色界無有如是非法煩惱。對治彼者法智後生故名類智復次若智六地所攝。能緣一地說名法智。若智九地所攝。能緣八地說名類智。此依有漏地說。復次若智六地所攝。能緣六地說名法智。若智九地所攝。能緣九地說名類智。此依無漏地說。復次若智對治十八界十二處五蘊者名法智。若智對治十四界十處五蘊者名類智。復次若智對治善不善無記五蘊者名法智。若智對治善無記五蘊者名類智。復次若智對治福非福不動行者名法智。若智對治福及不動行者名類智。復次若智對治段食婬欲愛者名法智若智對治諸定愛者名類智。問何故名他心智。答知他心故名他心智。問此亦知他諸心所法。何故但名他心智耶。答以期心故謂修觀者先起意樂。

Further, the desire realm possesses in abundance the afflictions of non-dharma — namely, the associated afflictions of indignation, resentment, concealment, vexation, envy, and stinginess, and the like; whatever knowledge serves as their immediate counteraction is named dharma-cognition; the form and formless realms are without any such afflictions of non-dharma, and whatever counteracts them, being given rise to after dharma-cognition, is thereby named subsequent cognition. Further, if a kind of knowledge is gathered within six grounds, capable of taking a single ground as its object, it is named dharma-cognition; if a kind of knowledge is gathered within nine grounds, capable of taking eight grounds as its object, it is named subsequent cognition — this being stated with reference to the contaminated grounds. 10. Why "Worldly Knowledge"?

A final cluster of etymologies addresses worldly knowledge: though it does know a small portion of supreme-meaning dharmas (the aggregates, elements, sense-fields, the four truths), it is named worldly because it mostly knows ordinary worldly things — men, women, jars, carts, houses; because, like the provisional title "king" given by common consent to someone not truly of royal lineage, it is only conventionally granted the name "knowledge" at all; because it is universally and undisputedly acknowledged by every sentient being, exactly as an elder monk or one who delights the assembly earns his own title by common consent; and because it is covered over by ignorance exactly as a thing inside a vessel is covered by the vessel itself.

The Vibhajyavādins are refuted for holding that a dharma gathers in another's nature rather than its own; against this, dharma-cognition is shown to gather in itself (subdivided further by ground, by truth, and by time) plus small portions of the knowledge of others' minds and the four truth-specific kinds of knowledge, wherever these possess their own dharma-cognition-aligned portion — subsequent cognition, the knowledge of others' minds, worldly knowledge, and the knowledge of suffering, accumulation, cessation, and the path are each, in turn, subjected to the identical exhaustive treatment.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This closing exercise in mutual containment performs, in miniature, the entire fascicle's governing method one last time: no kind of knowledge is ever simply "one of the eight" in isolation, since each one's own true extent turns out to overlap partially with several others, and only a fully worked-out map of exactly which small portion gathers within which other portion can finally settle what each kind of knowledge genuinely contains.
