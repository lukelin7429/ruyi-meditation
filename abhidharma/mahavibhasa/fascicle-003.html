Mounting the Elephant to Tame It

This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation of fascicle 3 — the Miscellaneous Chapter's first section continues (雜蘊第一中世第一法納息第一之二), still entirely on supreme mundane dharma. The fascicle asks whether the dharma's own attainment (得, prāpti) counts as itself, how many foundations of mindfulness and conditions it involves, what its supramundane counterpart would be, works through a six-term etymology of "foremost," debates who really relinquishes an ordinary being's nature when the relinquisher is itself still one, defines what it means to "gain the nature of a noble one" and "enter the certainty of rightness that leaves birth behind" through six rival readings including the Venerable Vasumitra's, and closes with an extended doctrinal dispute — against the Mahāsāṃghikas, Vātsīputrīyas, Mahīśāsakas, and Dharmaguptakas — over why this dharma belongs to the form realm and could never belong to the desire realm. Fascicle 4 continues the same topic.

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

This fascicle continues directly from Fascicle 2, still within the same nyāsa (納息) section on supreme mundane dharma (世第一法) — terms established there, such as the certainty of rightness (正性離生), the immediately-antecedent condition (等無間緣), and the forbearance of the dharma-knowledge of suffering (苦法智忍), recur constantly here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Does Its Own Attainment Count as Supreme Mundane Dharma?

Fascicle 3 opens with a puzzle left dangling from Fascicle 2's closing question: the form and mind-dissociated formations accompanying supreme mundane dharma were shown to count as itself — but what about its own attainment (得, prāpti), the possession-factor Sarvāstivāda posits for every dharma a person comes to have?

Chinese Source

問已知世第一法。隨轉生住老無常。亦是世第一法。彼得為亦是世第一法不。設是或非俱有何失。若彼得亦是世第一法者。得聖果已。順決擇分應重現前。若彼得非世第一法者。何故沙門果得。是沙門果。而世第一法得。非世第一法耶。答彼得定非世第一法。得聖果已。順決擇分不重起故。問何故沙門果得。是沙門果。而世第一法得。非世第一法耶。答沙門果成就所顯故。沙門果得是沙門果。世第一法等無間緣所顯。彼得既非等無間緣。亦不隨順如彼生等。故彼得非世第一法。煖頂忍得。亦非煖等勿得聖已重現前故。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: it is already known that the arising, abiding, aging, and impermanence that arise together with supreme mundane dharma are themselves supreme mundane dharma — but is its attainment (得, prāpti) also supreme mundane dharma? Whichever one answers, what fault would follow? If its attainment, too, is supreme mundane dharma, then once the noble fruit is attained, the nirvedhabhāgīya factor ought to occur present again. But if its attainment is not supreme mundane dharma, then why is it that the attainment of the śramaṇa-fruit counts as the śramaṇa-fruit itself, while the attainment of supreme mundane dharma does not count as supreme mundane dharma? The answer: its attainment is certainly not supreme mundane dharma, since once the noble fruit is attained, the nirvedhabhāgīya factor never occurs present again. One then asks: why is it that the attainment of the śramaṇa-fruit counts as the śramaṇa-fruit, while the attainment of supreme mundane dharma does not count as supreme mundane dharma itself? The answer: the śramaṇa-fruit is defined by its being possessed — the attainment of the śramaṇa-fruit simply is the śramaṇa-fruit. Supreme mundane dharma, by contrast, is defined by its serving as the immediately-antecedent condition — and since its attainment cannot itself serve as that condition, nor does it accord with it the way the accompanying arising and the rest do, its attainment is not supreme mundane dharma. The same holds for the attainment of heat, summit, and forbearance — none of these, either, ever occurs present again once the noble state is attained.

有作是說。彼得亦是世第一法。問若爾得聖果已。順決擇分應重現前。答許彼一分現前亦無有過。謂相應者不重現前。不相應者有重現前。煖頂忍得亦復如是。有餘師說。彼俱起得亦是世第一法。後起者非故無前失。煖等亦爾。評曰。若彼俱起若後起得。一切皆非世第一法。種類同故。煖等亦爾。是故初說於理為善。問何故世第一法。生住老無常。亦是世第一法而得非耶。答生等與彼同一果。

Some hold instead: its attainment, too, is indeed supreme mundane dharma. One asks: if so, then once the noble fruit is attained, should not the nirvedhabhāgīya factor occur present again? The answer: granting that one portion of it occurs present again causes no difficulty — namely, the associated portion does not occur present again, while the dissociated portion does. The same holds for the attainment of heat, summit, and forbearance. Other masters say: only the attainment that arises together with it is supreme mundane dharma; the attainment that arises afterward is not, and so no such fault as above follows. The same holds for heat and the rest. The verdict: whether co-arising or arising afterward, no attainment at all is supreme mundane dharma, since it belongs to a different category altogether; the same holds for heat and the rest. The first position given above is therefore the sound one. One asks: why is it that the arising, abiding, aging, and impermanence that arise together with supreme mundane dharma are themselves supreme mundane dharma, while its attainment is not? The answer: arising and the rest share the very same result with it,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This debate turns entirely on a distinction the treatise never abandons: sharing a result, an outflow, a birth and death with a dharma (as the accompanying formations do) is not the same as merely being caused by or related to it (as prāpti is). The "verdict" (評曰) format seen here — recording multiple positions, then explicitly ruling one the sound view — is rarer than the treatise's usual practice of simply listing rival opinions unranked, and marks a passage the compilers treat as more settled than most.

2. Why Attainment Fails Where Accompanying Arising Succeeds

The treatise now gives the precise reason accompanying arising, abiding, aging, and impermanence pass the test that attainment fails.

Chinese Source

相隨行不相離。常和合無前無後。相與所相未嘗相離。由此亦是世第一法。得與彼法不同一果。不相隨行。性相離不和合。或前或後。得與所得有時相離。譬如樹皮或時離樹。是故得非世第一法。

Ru-Yi Translation

accompany it without ever separating from it, are constantly conjoined with it, occur neither before nor after it, and the marked and its mark are never once apart — for this reason, they too are supreme mundane dharma. Attainment, by contrast, does not share the same result with the dharma it attains; it does not accompany it; its nature is separate and unconjoined; it may occur before or after; and the attainment and what is attained can, at times, come apart — just as bark can, at times, come away from a tree. This is why attainment is not supreme mundane dharma.

煖頂忍得亦復如是。

The same holds for the attainment of heat, summit, and forbearance.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The bark simile is doing precise conceptual work: bark and tree normally travel together, closely enough that one might mistake the relationship for identity, yet bark can come away while the tree endures — exactly the separability that disqualifies prāpti from counting as the dharma it merely attaches to.

3. Mindfulness, and the Four Conditions It Serves and Has

Two compact technical questions follow: how many of the four foundations of mindfulness does supreme mundane dharma count as, and which of the four classical conditions does it serve as, and depend on, itself?

Chinese Source

問世第一法為幾念住。答現在唯一。雜緣法念住未來具四。似見道故。問世第一法為幾緣。答為四緣。謂因等無間所緣增上緣。為因緣者。謂與彼相應俱有同類等法為因緣。為等無間緣者。謂與苦法智忍為等無間緣。為所緣緣者。謂與能緣此心心所法為所緣緣。為增上緣者。謂除自性。與餘一切有為法為增上緣。問世第一法有幾緣。答有四緣。有因緣者。謂此相應俱有同類等法。有等無間緣者。謂已生增上忍。有所緣緣者。謂欲界五蘊。有增上緣者。謂除自性餘一切法。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: how many foundations of mindfulness does supreme mundane dharma count as, in the present? The answer: in the present, only one — the mixed-object foundation of mindfulness on dharmas; in the future, it counts as all four, since it resembles the path of seeing. One asks: how many conditions does supreme mundane dharma itself serve as? The answer: it serves as all four — the causal condition, the immediately-antecedent condition, the objective condition, and the dominant condition. As the causal condition: it is the causal condition for the dharmas associated with and co-arising with it, of the same kind and so on. As the immediately-antecedent condition: it is this condition for the forbearance of the dharma-knowledge of suffering. As the objective condition: it is this condition for the mind and mental factors able to take it as their object. As the dominant condition: excepting only itself, it is this condition for every other conditioned dharma. One asks: how many conditions does supreme mundane dharma itself have? The answer: it, too, has all four. It has a causal condition: the dharmas associated with and co-arising with it, of the same kind and so on. It has an immediately-antecedent condition: the already-arisen superior forbearance. It has an objective condition: the five aggregates of the desire realm. It has a dominant condition: every other dharma, excepting only itself.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The double question — what conditions does X serve as for other dharmas, and what conditions does X itself depend on — is a standard analytical pair this treatise runs for nearly every dharma it examines; readers will see the same two-sided format recur throughout the remaining fascicles.

4. What Is "Supramundane Supreme Dharma"?

If there is a "supreme mundane dharma," reason suggests there ought to be a supramundane counterpart — a claim the root text never states outright but which seven rival candidates compete to fill.

Chinese Source

問文雖不說義必應有。云何出世第一法耶。答苦法智忍是。謂此能持一切聖道故。有餘師說。金剛喻定是。謂此能得一切結盡遍知果故。有作是說。初盡智是。謂此能持一切無學法故。或有說者。阿耨多羅三藐三菩提是。謂於一切有為法中此最勝故。復有說者。涅槃界是。謂於一切有為無為法中此最勝故。有說。阿羅漢最後聖道剎那是。謂如異生位最後剎那心名世第一法。如是阿羅漢最後剎那無漏心。名出世第一法。有說。阿羅漢最後心是。謂如異生位最後心是世第一法。如是阿羅漢最後心。是出世第一法。評曰。彼不應作是說。以阿羅漢最後心非出世法故。此諸說中初說為善。由此能任持一切聖道故。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: although the root text does not state it in so many words, the meaning must surely require it — what, then, is "supramundane supreme dharma"? The answer: it is the forbearance of the dharma-knowledge of suffering, since this alone is able to uphold every noble path. Other masters say: it is the vajropama-samādhi ("diamond-like concentration"), since this alone is able to attain the result of full knowledge of the exhaustion of every fetter. Some say: it is the very first exhaustion-knowledge, since this alone is able to uphold every dharma belonging to one beyond training. Others say: it is unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, since this is supreme among all conditioned dharmas. Others say still: it is the nirvāṇa-realm, since this is supreme among all conditioned and unconditioned dharmas. Some say: it is the very last moment of the noble path in an arhat — just as, in an ordinary being, the last moment of mind is named supreme mundane dharma, so too the very last, uncontaminated moment of mind in an arhat is named supramundane supreme dharma. Some say: it is simply an arhat's very last moment of mind — just as, in an ordinary being, the very last moment of mind is supreme mundane dharma, so too an arhat's very last moment of mind is supramundane supreme dharma. The verdict: this last position should not be maintained, since an arhat's very last moment of mind is not itself a supramundane dharma. Among all these positions, the first is the sound one, since it alone is able to uphold every noble path.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Seven candidates for a term the root text never actually uses is a striking amount of collective effort spent filling in a silence — a reminder that this treatise treats the Jñānaprasthāna's own structure (here, the mundane/supramundane parallelism suggested by "supreme mundane dharma" naming itself as mundane) as meaningful enough to demand completion, even where the root text stops short of stating the missing half explicitly.

5. Wholly Supreme, or Only Partially?

The treatise now turns to the reason behind the name "supreme mundane dharma" itself, opening with an analogy to how an acclaimed person's reputation is investigated, then posing a dilemma about the scope of the claim "foremost."

Chinese Source

何故名世第一法。乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答前雖說世第一法自性。而未說彼立名因緣。今欲說故。譬如有人世稱最勝。理應說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is it named "supreme mundane dharma"? — and so on, as explained at length. One asks: why was this further passage composed? The answer: although the essential nature of supreme mundane dharma has already been explained above, the reason behind its very name has not yet been explained, and this is now taken up. It is like a person universally acclaimed as supreme — reason demands that one also explain

彼立名因緣。為以族姓。為色為力富貴眷屬名最勝耶。此亦如是故作斯論。何故名世第一法。答如是心心所法。於餘世間法。為最為勝。為長為尊。為上為妙。故名世第一法。此心心所。於餘世法為都勝故說名第一。為分勝故名第一耶。設爾何失。若都勝故名第一者。此豈能勝現觀邊世俗智。然現觀邊所修世俗智。是見道眷屬隨屬。見道慧力殊勝。此法不爾。又此豈勝雜修靜慮。然彼等至及所感生。不共異生。此法不爾。又此豈勝初盡智時所修善根。然修彼時離一切障所依清淨。此法不爾。又此豈勝空空。無願無願。無相無相。三三摩地。然彼尚能厭惡聖道。況於有漏。此法不爾。若分勝故名第一者。煖頂忍等應亦名第一。各勝彼彼下位善根故。有作是說。此法都勝故名第一。然約能開聖道門說。非據一切。謂現觀邊世俗智等。雖有如前所說勝事。然皆無力開聖道門。此法獨能。

the reason behind that person's very acclaim — is it their clan, their form, their strength, their wealth and rank, or their entourage that makes them acclaimed as supreme? It is exactly the same here, and this is why the passage was composed. Why is it named "supreme mundane dharma"? The answer: because this mind and its mental factors are, in relation to every other mundane dharma, the utmost, the supreme, the highest, the most honored, the foremost, and the finest — for this reason it is named supreme mundane dharma. Is this mind and these mental factors named "foremost," in relation to every other mundane dharma, because they are wholly supreme, or only partially supreme? Suppose either — what fault would follow? If named "foremost" because wholly supreme, then does it truly surpass the conventional wisdom bordering direct realization? Yet the conventional wisdom cultivated bordering direct realization is itself a close attendant of the path of seeing, sharing in the exceptional power of the path of seeing's own wisdom — this dharma is not like that. Again, does it truly surpass the mixed cultivation of the dhyānas? Yet that attainment, and the rebirth it brings about, are not shared even by ordinary beings — this dharma is not like that. Again, does it truly surpass the wholesome root cultivated at the moment of the very first exhaustion-knowledge? Yet when that is cultivated, the basis is purified, free of every obstruction — this dharma is not like that. Again, does it truly surpass the three concentrations — emptiness upon emptiness, wishlessness upon wishlessness, signlessness upon signlessness? Yet those are able to turn away in disgust even from the noble path itself, let alone from what is merely contaminated — this dharma is not like that. If named "foremost" because only partially supreme, then heat, summit, and forbearance, too, ought equally to be named "foremost," since each surpasses the wholesome roots of the stage directly below it. Some hold instead: this dharma is named "foremost" because it is wholly supreme, but only with respect to its ability to open the gate of the noble path, not with respect to everything whatsoever — for the conventional wisdom bordering direct realization and the rest, though they have the superior qualities described above, are all powerless to open the gate of the noble path, while this dharma alone can do so.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The four counterexamples — the wisdom bordering direct realization, mixed cultivation of the dhyānas, the wholesome root at first exhaustion-knowledge, and the three gates of concentration — each name a genuine competitor: dharmas that outdo supreme mundane dharma along some specific dimension (attendance on the path of seeing's own power, an attainment unique to noble ones, purity of basis, capacity to turn even from the noble path). The proposed resolution narrows the claim "foremost" to one function alone — opening the gate of the noble path — rather than abandoning it.

6. Supreme in Opening the Gate Alone

A stronger version of the "wholly supreme" reading follows, arguing that every other excellent quality's own value is, in the end, borrowed from this one dharma's unique function — before the treatise settles on a middle position.

Chinese Source

是故都勝。或有說者。此法於餘一切事勝故名第一。謂現觀邊世俗智等。所有勝事皆由此成。所以者何。彼諸勝事若無此法開聖道門。體尚不修。況有勝用。要由此法開聖道門。方修彼體乃有勝用。彼諸勝事既由此成。故此於餘一切事勝。有餘師說。此法分勝故名第一。問若爾煖等應亦名第一。各勝彼彼下位善根故。答彼於二分中俱非最勝故。謂世善法總有二分。一依異生。二依聖者。世第一法。雖於聖者世俗智等不名最勝。而於異生。所得靜慮。無量解脫。勝處遍處。乃至所得第一。有思及不淨觀。持息念諸念住。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is therefore wholly supreme in this one respect. Others say: this dharma is named "foremost" because it surpasses every other thing — for the conventional wisdom bordering direct realization, and every other excellent quality, all come to be precisely through it. Why is this? Because, without this dharma opening the gate of the noble path, those excellent qualities could not even be cultivated in their own substance, let alone put to excellent use — only once this dharma opens the gate of the noble path can their substance be cultivated at all, and their excellent use follow. Since all those excellent qualities come to be through this dharma, it therefore surpasses every other thing. Other masters say: this dharma is named "foremost" because it is only partially supreme. One asks: if so, then heat and the rest, too, ought equally to be named "foremost," since each surpasses the wholesome roots of the stage directly below it. The answer: heat and the rest are not supreme in either of two respects at once — for every mundane wholesome dharma falls, in total, into two divisions: that belonging to ordinary beings, and that belonging to noble ones. Supreme mundane dharma, though not named supreme in relation to a noble one's own conventional wisdom and the rest, is nonetheless supreme, within the division belonging to ordinary beings, over every meditative attainment, every immeasurable, every liberation, every mastery-basis, every totality-basis, up to whatever is foremost among them, over volition and the contemplation of impurity, the mindfulness of breathing, and every foundation of mindfulness,

三義觀七處善。煖頂忍中。皆悉最勝。煖等不爾。故此獨稱世第一法。問此何義故名第一耶。答此最勝故。能引第一故。得第一果故。趣第一性故。是第一義。有作是說。此能摧伏第一有故是第一義。有餘師說。此是異生最後心故。如高幢頂更無有上。是第一義。

over the threefold-meaning contemplation, skill in the seven bases, and heat, summit, and forbearance themselves — in every one of these, it is supreme; heat and the rest are not. This is why it alone is called supreme mundane dharma. One asks: in what sense, exactly, is it named "foremost"? The answer: because it is itself supreme; because it is able to draw forth what is foremost; because it attains the foremost result; because it advances toward the foremost nature — this is the sense of "foremost." Some hold instead: it is able to subdue the very summit of existence — this is the sense of "foremost." Other masters say: it is an ordinary being's very last moment of mind, and, like the very tip of a tall banner, has nothing above it — this is the sense of "foremost."

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The eventual resolution — supreme only within the division of mundane wholesome dharmas belonging to ordinary beings, not against a noble one's own conventional wisdom — quietly answers the counterexamples from the previous section: those competitors either belong to noble ones or lie outside this specific comparison class altogether, so the narrower claim survives where the broader one could not.

7. Six Synonyms for "Foremost"

The root verse lists six honorifics together — utmost, supreme, highest, honored, foremost, finest — and the treatise now asks whether these are mere synonyms or carry six distinct senses, answering with four separate schemes of differentiation.

Chinese Source

問此中所說。為最為勝。為長為尊。為上為妙。有何差別。或有說者。無有差別。皆是讚述第一義故。復有說者。亦有差別。且名則差別。謂此名最。乃至名妙。復次對諸善根亦有差別。謂此對聞所成名為最。對思所成名為勝。對不淨觀持息念念住等名為長。對煖名為尊。對頂名為上。對忍名為妙。復次約所依地亦有差別。謂此依未至定名為最。依初靜慮名為勝。依靜慮中間名為長。依第二靜慮名為尊。依第三靜慮名為上。依第四靜慮名為妙。復次依義不同。亦有差別。謂此至邊頂故名為最。上品攝故名為勝。作吉祥故名為長。體昇進故名為尊。性堅牢故名為上。滿所願故名為妙。復次體用有異。亦有差別。謂此能作苦法智忍等無間緣。故名為最。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: among the terms stated here — utmost, supreme, highest, honored, foremost, finest — what difference is there among them? Some say: there is no difference at all; each merely praises and restates the single sense of "foremost." Others say: there is indeed a difference — beginning simply with their names: this one is named "utmost," and so on, up to "finest." Further, they differ with reference to the various wholesome roots: set against the wisdom born of learning, it is named "utmost"; against the wisdom born of reflection, "supreme"; against the contemplation of impurity, the mindfulness of breathing, and the foundations of mindfulness, "highest"; against heat, "honored"; against summit, "foremost"; against forbearance, "finest." Further, they differ with reference to the stage they depend on: depending on the stage of proximate concentration, it is named "utmost"; on the first dhyāna, "supreme"; on the intermediate dhyāna, "highest"; on the second dhyāna, "honored"; on the third dhyāna, "foremost"; on the fourth dhyāna, "finest." Further, they differ according to distinct senses of meaning: because it reaches the very summit, it is named "utmost"; because it belongs to the superior grade, "supreme"; because it brings about auspiciousness, "highest"; because its substance advances upward, "honored"; because its nature is firm and stable, "foremost"; because it fulfills every aspiration, "finest." Further, they differ according to distinct functions of its substance: because it is able to serve as the immediately-antecedent condition for the forbearance of the dharma-knowledge of suffering, it is named "utmost";

The fascicle closes with three more similes defending the "already cut off, nothing left to cut" resolution of an apparent contradiction, the Venerable Ghoṣaka's own supporting argument and its refutation, why supreme mundane dharma itself cannot cut off any affliction, and a final logical proof — from the fault of a dharma taking itself as its own object — that it cannot possibly belong to the desire realm.

Chinese Source

心於所緣不速取捨。有勝勢力伏諸煩惱。故能趣入正性離生。如池水上有浮萍等。龍象大石投入其中。經久離散。難可還合。如是色界亦有暫時斷蓋等道。而可保信。廣說如前。是故應知欲界無有斷蓋等道。色界不爾。由此應言世第一法唯色界繫。非欲界繫。問世第一法。不能斷結。何故乃言若以欲界道能斷蓋制纏。令欲界纏不復現起。如是世第一法。應言欲界繫等。答世第一法。雖不斷結而此善根勝妙第一。在深遠處。宜應與彼能斷結道。同在一地。是故應以此道證之。復次世第一法。引生見道定與見道同在一地。見道既能斷諸煩惱。故斷結道可為此證。復次世第一法。既於欲界極生厭患。應與能治欲界惑道。同在一地。故可引彼。證此法有。問若爾世第一法唯應在未至地。唯未至地能斷欲界諸煩惱故。上地應無。答對治有二種。一斷對治。二厭壞對治。未至地。於欲界具二對治。上五地於欲界雖無斷對治。而有厭壞對治故。彼亦有世第一法。尊者妙音作如是說。色界六地於欲界惑。皆得具有二種對治。

Ru-Yi Translation

and the mind, toward its object, does not grasp and release in rapid succession; it has the superior power to subdue the afflictions, and so can advance into the certainty of rightness that leaves birth behind. It is like duckweed floating on a pond's surface: when a great elephant-sized boulder is thrown into it, the duckweed parts for a long time and can hardly close back together again. The form realm is exactly the same: it, too, has a path able to temporarily cut off the hindrances and the rest, and this can be relied upon, as explained at length above. One should therefore understand that the desire realm has no path able to cut off the hindrances and the rest, while the form realm does. On this basis, it should be said that supreme mundane dharma belongs to the form realm alone, and not to the desire realm. One asks: supreme mundane dharma is itself unable to cut off the fetters — why, then, does the passage say that only if a path of the desire realm were able to cut off the hindrances and restrain the entanglements would supreme mundane dharma be said to belong to the desire realm, and reason from this to its actual realm? The answer: although supreme mundane dharma cannot itself cut off the fetters, this wholesome root is supreme, subtle, and foremost, occupying a deep and remote position; it is fitting that it should share the very same stage as the path able to cut off the fetters, and so that path can be used as evidence for it. Further: supreme mundane dharma draws forth the path of seeing, and must necessarily share the very same stage as the path of seeing; since the path of seeing is able to cut off the afflictions, the fetter-cutting path can serve as evidence here too. Further: since supreme mundane dharma has already given rise to the deepest disgust toward the desire realm, it ought to share the very same stage as the path able to counteract the afflictions of the desire realm, and so that path can be adduced as evidence that this dharma exists there. One asks: if so, then supreme mundane dharma ought to exist only on the stage of proximate concentration, since only that stage is able to cut off the afflictions of the desire realm — it ought not to exist on any higher stage. The answer: there are two kinds of counteragent — the counteragent of cutting off, and the counteragent of weakening through disgust. The stage of proximate concentration possesses both counteragents with respect to the desire realm; the five higher stages, though lacking the counteragent of cutting off with respect to the desire realm, do possess the counteragent of weakening through disgust — and so supreme mundane dharma exists on those stages too. The Venerable Ghoṣaka speaks as follows: all six stages of the form realm possess both counteragents with respect to the afflictions of the desire realm.

上五地道非不能斷。由未至地先已斷故。雖有斷力而無可斷。譬如六人。同一冤家。而共議言隨於何處。獲者便害。於中一人先獲害之。其餘五人雖有害力而無可害。又如六人各持一燈。相與次第入一闇室。初燈入時諸闇皆破。餘雖有力無闇可除。又如日光初中後分。無不皆與夜闇相違。日初出時。破闇皆盡。餘雖有力無闇可破。如是六地。於欲界惑雖皆能斷。廣說如前。問云何得知。色界六地於欲界惑具二對治。彼作是言。依上五地入見道者。於欲界斷分別作證。別起無漏離繫得故。若上五地於欲界惑。無斷對治此事應無。評曰。不應作如是說。彼於欲界煩惱畢竟。無有能斷者故。誰言彼地有無漏得。於欲界斷分別作證。而復引彼證此義耶。

It is not that the five higher stages' paths are unable to cut off the afflictions — it is only that, since the stage of proximate concentration has already cut them off first, they retain the power to cut off yet have nothing left to cut. It is like six people who share a single enemy, and together agree: wherever he is found, whoever finds him shall slay him — one of them finds and slays him first, and though the remaining five still have the power to slay him, there is nothing left for them to slay. Or again, it is like six people, each holding a lamp, entering a dark room one after another in sequence: when the first lamp enters, every bit of darkness is already dispelled, and though the rest still have the power to dispel darkness, there is no darkness left for them to dispel. Or again, it is like the sun's light in its early, middle, and late phases, every one of which is opposed to the darkness of night: when the sun first rises, the darkness is entirely dispelled, and though its light in the later phases still has the power to dispel darkness, there is no darkness left for it to dispel. The six stages are exactly the same: with respect to the afflictions of the desire realm, though every one of them is able to cut them off, as explained at length above. One asks: how is it known that the six stages of the form realm possess both counteragents with respect to the afflictions of the desire realm? They reply as follows: one who enters the path of seeing by relying on any of the five higher stages nonetheless attains, with respect to the desire realm, the direct realization of cutting off through discernment, since a distinct uncontaminated attainment of freedom from bondage arises there — if the five higher stages lacked the counteragent of cutting off with respect to the desire realm, this could not be the case. The verdict: this position should not be maintained, since those stages are, in the end, entirely unable to cut off the afflictions of the desire realm at all. Who ever said that those stages possess an uncontaminated attainment realizing, through discernment, the cutting off of the desire realm's afflictions — and on what basis, then, is this cited as evidence for the position in question?

是故前說於理為善。問因論生論。世第一法。何故不能斷諸煩惱。答世第一法彼於爾時善根微小。法身未長而有威勢。以善根微小法身未長故不能斷惑。有威勢故不為煩惱之所摧伏。如師子子身小未長而有威勢。以身小未長故不能害獸。有威勢故不為諸獸之所侵害。或有說言。世第一法唯一剎那故不能斷。問苦法智忍亦一剎那云何能斷。答苦法智忍。雖一剎那而有相續起故能斷惑。世第一法無如是事故不能斷。有餘師言。世第一法。加行道攝故不能斷。要無間道方能斷故。於此義中復有分別。問何緣世第一法。唯在色界繫耶。答以彼色界能為三道三地三根等無間緣。又能引發初法智品次類智品。餘界不然。故世第一法唯是色界繫。問世第一法何緣定非欲界繫耶。答欲界非定界非修界非離染界。要定界修界離染界。乃有世第一法故。復次欲界是卑賤界是麁重界是下劣界。要尊勝界。細輕界。勝妙界。乃有世第一法。復次若世第一法是欲界繫者。便有能緣自性過故。謂彼若是欲界繫者。為緣自性。為不能緣。若緣自性有違理失。自性不能取自性故。若不能緣便違本論。

The position given first, therefore, is the sound one. One asks, as a further question arising from this discussion: why is supreme mundane dharma itself unable to cut off the afflictions? The answer: at that very moment, supreme mundane dharma's wholesome root is still slight, and its dharma-body not yet grown, though it already possesses formidable power. Because its wholesome root is slight and its dharma-body not yet grown, it is unable to cut off the afflictions; because it already possesses formidable power, it is not itself overwhelmed by the afflictions — just as a lion cub, its body small and not yet grown, already possesses formidable power: because its body is small and not yet grown, it cannot yet prey upon other animals, but because it already possesses formidable power, it is not itself preyed upon by them. Others say: supreme mundane dharma lasts exactly a single moment, and this is why it cannot cut off the afflictions. One asks: the forbearance of the dharma-knowledge of suffering, too, lasts exactly a single moment — how, then, is it able to cut them off? The answer: although the forbearance of the dharma-knowledge of suffering lasts exactly a single moment, it arises in continuous succession, and this is why it is able to cut them off; supreme mundane dharma has no such continuous succession, and this is why it cannot. Other masters say: supreme mundane dharma belongs to the preparatory path, and this is why it cannot cut off the afflictions — only the uninterrupted path is able to do so. Within this general topic, a further distinction should be drawn. One asks: for what reason does supreme mundane dharma belong to the form realm alone? The answer: because the form realm alone is able to serve as the immediately-antecedent condition for all three paths, all three stages, and all three faculties, and is able to draw forth both the first class of dharma-knowledge and the following class of subsequent-knowledge — no other realm can do this; hence supreme mundane dharma belongs to the form realm alone. One asks: for what reason is supreme mundane dharma certainly not bound to the desire realm? The answer: the desire realm is not a realm of concentration, not a realm of cultivation, not a realm free of defilement — only a realm of concentration, of cultivation, and free of defilement can possess supreme mundane dharma at all. Further: the desire realm is a base realm, a coarse and heavy realm, an inferior realm — only an honored and superior realm, a subtle and light realm, a supreme and excellent realm, can possess supreme mundane dharma. Further: if supreme mundane dharma belonged to the desire realm, the fault of a dharma taking its own essential nature as its own object would follow — for if it belonged to the desire realm, then either it would take its own essential nature as its object, or it would be unable to take it as an object at all. If it took its own essential nature as its object, this would be an unreasonable fault, since an essential nature cannot grasp itself as its own object. If it were unable to take it as an object, this would contradict the root treatise,

如後文說。若緣此法起苦法智忍。即緣此法起世第一法。苦法智忍。遍緣欲界五蘊為境。此亦應然。是故世第一法。決定非欲界繫。

as a later passage states: whatever object gives rise to the forbearance of the dharma-knowledge of suffering, that very same object gives rise to supreme mundane dharma. Since the forbearance of the dharma-knowledge of suffering universally takes the five aggregates of the desire realm as its object, the same should hold true here as well — and this is why supreme mundane dharma is certainly not bound to the desire realm.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The lion cub is the fascicle's most memorable image, and its logic is precise: formidable power and the ability to actually prey on something are two entirely separate facts, and a creature — or a dharma — can genuinely possess the first while still lacking the second. This directly answers a worry a careful reader might have been carrying since Section 5: if supreme mundane dharma is "supreme," why can it not itself cut off a single affliction? The lion cub says: supremacy of a certain kind does not entail capacity of every kind.

The fascicle's final argument is worth tracing through, since it closes out the entire desire-realm question with pure logic rather than another simile: if supreme mundane dharma belonged to the desire realm, it would have to take some object, and the root treatise elsewhere establishes that it must take the very same object as the forbearance of the dharma-knowledge of suffering — namely, the five aggregates of the desire realm. But the five aggregates of the desire realm include supreme mundane dharma itself, if it belonged there — which would force it to take its own essential nature as its own object, an impossibility the treatise has ruled out on general grounds (a substance cannot grasp itself). The only way out is for supreme mundane dharma to belong to a different realm entirely, so that "the five aggregates of the desire realm" never includes itself. It is a clean piece of reasoning to end a very long fascicle on.
