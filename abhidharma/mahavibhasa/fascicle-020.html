The Fingertip That Cannot Touch Itself

Fascicle 20 continues the Knowledge Nyāsa's twelfth part (智納息第二之十二), closing the ripening-cause investigation through an extraordinary run of scriptural puzzles — the sea-monster whose crushed body turned the ocean to blood, Anavatapta's single meal yielding fourteen royal rebirths, and only an arhat's own body able to feel hell's suffering while still human — before the sixth and final of the six causes, the efficient-cause, opens and closes in the very same fascicle, ending on the homely image of a fingertip that can never touch itself. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 20 continues the Knowledge Nyāsa opened in Fascicle 9, now in its twelfth part (之十二), and opens mid-argument, directly continuing fascicle 19's own closing question about karma and the class of homogeneous being. Terms established in earlier fascicles, such as the ripening-cause's own basic definition and the homogeneous-cause, recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. The Sea-Monster, the Meal-Offering, and the Salt-Simile: One Rebirth or Many?

Fascicle 20 opens mid-debate: can a single moment of karma draw forth only one class of homogeneous being, or can it draw forth many? Four vivid scriptural citations seem to demand “many” — a king who harmed countless beings falls into hell, then by residual karma becomes a monstrous sea-beast whose crushed body turns the ocean to blood; Anavatapta's single meal-offering yielding fourteen royal rebirths; Mahākāśyapa's single bowl of rice yielding two thousand rebirths in paradise and heaven; and the Salt-Simile Sutra's account of karma meant for hell instead ripening in this very life — while three further citations insist karma and destiny are always paired one-to-one.

Chinese Source

問一剎那業為但能引一眾同分。為亦能引多眾同分耶。設爾何失。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: can a single moment of karma draw forth only a single class of homogeneous being, or can it also draw forth many classes of homogeneous being? Suppose either — what fault follows?

若一剎那業但能引一眾同分者。施設論說當云何通。如說有諸眾生曾在人中。或作國王或作大臣。具大勢力非理損害無量眾生。稅奪資財供給自身及諸眷屬。由是惡業死墮地獄。經無量時受大苦惱。

If a single moment of karma can draw forth only a single class of homogeneous being, how is the Prajñaptiśāstra to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states: there were certain sentient beings who, once dwelling among humankind, served either as a king or as a great minister, wielding great power and, without just cause, harming countless beings, exacting taxes and plundering wealth to supply themselves and their household — and by this unwholesome karma, upon death, fell into hell, where they underwent great suffering and torment across an immeasurable span of time.

從彼捨命復由殘業生大海中。受惡獸身其形長大。噉食無量水陸眾生。亦為無量眾生唼食。遍著其體如拘執毛。既受苦痛不能堪忍。

Upon relinquishing that life, by their own residual karma, they were then born within the great ocean, receiving the body of a monstrous beast, its form immense, devouring countless creatures of water and land alike, while itself being devoured by countless creatures in turn, its entire body infested as though covered with clinging hair — and, unable to bear the resulting agony,

以身揩突頗胝迦山在彼身蟲俱被殘害。遂令海水縱廣百千踰繕那量皆變成血。又若爾者。尊者無滅所說本事復云何通。如說。具壽。

it rubbed its body against a mountain of sphaṭika crystal, whereupon the very creatures infesting its body were themselves crushed and destroyed together with it, so that the ocean's waters, across many hundreds of thousands of yojanas in breadth, were entirely turned to blood. And, if that is so, how is the Venerable Anavatapta's own recorded past deed to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states: Venerable One,

我以一食施福田故。七生天上作大天王。七生人中為大國主。又若爾者。大迦葉波所說本事復云何通。如說。具壽。我以一器稗子米飯施福田故。千返生彼北俱盧洲自然衣食。千返生彼三十三天受大快樂。又若爾者。鹽喻經說復云何通。如說。一類補特伽羅造作增長爾許惡業有應地獄受者。或現法受。有應現法受者往地獄受。若一剎那業亦能引多眾同分者施設論說當云何通。如說。由業種種差別勢力。施設諸趣種種差別。由趣種種差別勢力。施設諸生。種種差別。由生種種差別勢力。施設異熟種種差別。由異熟種種差別勢力。施設諸根種種差別。由根種種差別勢力。施設補特伽羅種種差別。又若爾者。通達經說復云何通。如說。云何應知諸業差別。謂別業生地獄。別業生傍生。別業生餓鬼。別業生天上。別業生人中。又若爾者。云何建立順現法受等三業差別。又若爾者。施設論說復云何通。如說。造作增長上殺生業。身壞命終墮無間獄。中生餘處下復生餘。乃至廣說。答應作是說。一剎那業唯能引一眾同分。問若爾。施設論說當云何通。如說。有諸眾生曾在人中。乃至廣說。答由殘業者由別趣業。謂彼人中造作增長地獄傍生二惡趣業。地獄中受地獄業已。殘傍生業生大海中。故此殘聲非望一業。

by the merit of a single meal offered to a field of merit, I was reborn seven times in the heavens as a great heavenly king, and reborn seven times among humankind as a great sovereign lord. And, if that is so, how is the Great Kāśyapa's own recorded past deed to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states: Venerable One, by the merit of a single vessel of coarse rice offered to a field of merit, I was reborn a thousand times in Uttarakuru, enjoying spontaneously arising clothing and food, and reborn a thousand times in the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, enjoying great bliss. And, if that is so, how is the Salt-Simile Sutra to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states: a certain kind of person creates and increases so very much unwholesome karma that, though he ought properly to experience its ripening in hell, instead experiences its ripening within this very life — for there is a kind of person who, though he ought to experience his karma's ripening in hell, instead goes there and experiences it there. But if a single moment of karma can also draw forth many classes of homogeneous being, how is the Prajñaptiśāstra to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states: through the power of karma's own manifold differences, the manifold differences among the destinies of rebirth are established; through the power of the destinies' own manifold differences, the manifold differences among births are established; through the power of births' own manifold differences, the manifold differences among ripening fruits are established; through the power of ripening fruits' own manifold differences, the manifold differences among the faculties are established; through the power of the faculties' own manifold differences, the manifold differences among individual persons are established. And, if that is so, how is the Penetration Sutra to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states: how is one to understand the manifold differences among karmas? One should understand that a distinct karma gives rise to hell, a distinct karma gives rise to the animal realm, a distinct karma gives rise to the realm of hungry ghosts, a distinct karma gives rise to the heavens, and a distinct karma gives rise to the human realm. And, if that is so, how, then, should the threefold distinction among karma received in this very life, and so on, even be established at all? And, if that is so, how is the Prajñaptiśāstra to be further reconciled with this — namely, as it states: one who commits and increases the superior grade of the killing of life, upon the destruction of the body and the ending of life, falls into the uninterrupted hell, while a middling such karma leads to rebirth elsewhere, and an inferior such karma leads to rebirth elsewhere still, and so on, as stated at length? The answer: it should be said that a single moment of karma is able to draw forth only a single class of homogeneous being. One asks: if that is so, how is the Prajñaptiśāstra to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states: there were certain sentient beings who, once dwelling among humankind, and so on, as stated at length? The answer: "by residual karma" here refers to a distinct karma belonging to a different destiny — that is, that person, while among humankind, created and increased two distinct unwholesome karmas, one leading to hell and one leading to the animal realm; having already experienced the hell-karma's own fruit within hell, he was, by the residual animal-realm karma, then born within the great ocean — and so this word "residual" here does not refer to some leftover portion of one and the same karma.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Marshaling four scriptural citations for “many” against three for “one” before the treatise even states its own position is a striking rhetorical structure: rather than building toward its own view gradually, the treatise first lets both sides of the dispute make their strongest possible case in full, trusting that a decisive resolution — arriving only after the counterfactual reductio already familiar from this fascicle's discussion of wrong view — will be more convincing for having answered every citation on its own terms.

2. Speaking Only of the First Cause: The Farmer Who Planted a Little

The treatise resolves Anavatapta's and Mahākāśyapa's own extravagant multiplication of fruit by locating it entirely in a manner of speaking: both monks describe only the initiating cause of a long chain of later repeated giving, memory, and vow, just as a farmer who plants a mere handful of seed across many years may still say “I planted little and reaped much,” or a merchant trading a single coin over decades may say the same of his fortune — with three further, non-competing explanations offered besides, including the striking suggestion that Mahākāśyapa's single act of giving contained three distinct grades of wholesome karma at once, severally yielding his human kingship, his heavenly kingship, and his final arhatship in the Śākya clan.

Chinese Source

問尊者無滅所說本事復云何通。如說。具壽。我以一食施福田故。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: the Venerable Anavatapta's own recorded past deed — how, then, is it to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states: Venerable One, by the merit of a single meal offered to a field of merit,

乃至廣說。答彼顯初因故作是說。謂彼先以一食施故。生富貴家多饒財寶。由宿生念或本願力。復以百千財食布施。如是展轉經於多生。常好布施受大富樂。彼依初因故作是說。譬如農夫以少種子多年種殖。展轉增長至百千斛。唱如是言。我殖少種今獲百千。又如商客以一金錢多時貿易至於千萬。唱如是言。我用一錢經求滋息。

and so on, as stated at length? The answer: that passage speaks only with reference to the initial cause, and so speaks in this way — that is, he first, by that single meal-offering, was born into a wealthy and eminent household, richly endowed with wealth and treasure, and then, whether through a memory carried over from a past life or through the power of an original vow, further offered wealth and food by the hundreds and thousands, and so, life after life in succession, ever delighted in giving and ever enjoyed great wealth and bliss — yet he speaks only with reference to that very first, initiating cause. It is just as a farmer who, planting only a scant few seeds across many years, harvests, in the end, hundreds and thousands of measures of grain, and yet says: I planted but a little, and have now reaped hundreds and thousands — or just as a merchant who, trading with but a single gold coin over a long span of time, arrives, in the end, at many millions, and yet says: with a single coin, I have sought and gained this increase —

今至千萬。此等皆依最初因故作如是說。尊者亦爾。復次或彼尊者於一生中先施一食後復數施。由此引發多人天因。彼依最初故作是說。復次或彼尊者施一食時起多思願。由此引得天上人中多異熟果。復次或彼尊者因施一食。起下中上三品善業。由下業故生在人中得為人主。由中業故生於天上復作天王。由上業故於最後身生釋氏家。多饒財寶出家修道成阿羅漢。由此理趣則已釋通大迦葉波所說本事。問鹽喻經說復云何通。如說。一類補特伽羅。乃至廣說。

arriving, in the end, at many millions — all such people speak only with reference to that very first, initiating cause, and this Venerable One is exactly the same. Further, it may be that this Venerable One, within a single lifetime, first offered a single meal and afterward offered many further times, and it is by this that he drew forth many rebirths among gods and humans, yet he speaks only with reference to the very first offering. Further, it may be that this Venerable One, at the very moment of offering that single meal, gave rise to many vows and aspirations, and it is by this that he drew forth many distinct ripening fruits among gods and humans. Further, it may be that this Venerable One, through the act of offering that single meal, gave rise to a lower, a middling, and an upper grade of wholesome karma all at once — by the lower grade of karma, he was born among humankind and became a human sovereign; by the middling grade of karma, he was born among the gods and became, in turn, a heavenly king; and by the upper grade of karma, in his very final body, he was born into the clan of the Śākyas, richly endowed with wealth and treasure, and, going forth from household life to cultivate the path, attained arhatship — and it is through this very principle that the Great Kāśyapa's own recorded past deed has already been reconciled as well. One asks: the Salt-Simile Sutra — how, then, is it to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states: a certain kind of person, and so on, as stated at length?

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The image of a single meal-gift secretly containing three separate grades of karma, each ripening into an entirely different destiny across different lifetimes, does real work beyond merely rescuing a scriptural citation from apparent exaggeration: it shows the treatise's characteristic refusal to treat “a single karmic act” as necessarily indivisible, treating even one brief moment of generosity as potentially layered with multiple, separately-ripening causal strata bound for different fruits.

3. The Cook's Wet Hand: Only an Arhat Can Feel Hell's Suffering in a Human Body

The Salt-Simile Sutra's own puzzle — killing-karma "meant for hell" instead ripening in this life — receives an escalating series of solutions, from two people committing parallel but differently-cultivated killings, to one person's two acts, to one act with two potential fruits, before the treatise settles on its final verdict: a single killing-karma draws forth only hell, full stop — but a person who, after that killing, diligently cultivates the path all the way to arhatship can draw that very hell-suffering forward to be experienced within his present human body instead, without any rebirth ever actually occurring, since a class-of-being-drawing karma can never be lent out to another destiny. Only an arhat's body, infused by superior concentration and wisdom, can bear this — illustrated by the cook who wets his hand with water before reaching into scalding rice, and is not burned.

Chinese Source

有作是說。彼說二人造二種業感二異熟。謂有二人俱害生命。一不勤修身戒心慧。彼少福故往地獄中受此業異熟果。一能勤修身戒心慧。彼多福故即於人中受此業異熟果。問經言爾許。說何義耶。答若少若等若相似故說名爾許。有餘師說。彼說一人造二種業感二異熟。謂有一人害二生命。一業能引地獄異熟。一業能引人中異熟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Some say: that sutra speaks of two people who each create two distinct kinds of karma, thereby experiencing two distinct ripening fruits — that is, there were two people who both took life, one of whom did not diligently cultivate body, precept, mind, and wisdom, and, having little merit, went to hell to experience that karma's own ripening fruit, while the other did diligently cultivate body, precept, mind, and wisdom, and, having much merit, experienced that karma's own ripening fruit right there among humankind. One asks: the scripture speaks of "so very much" — what meaning does this convey? The answer: whether it is a little, or an equal amount, or something merely resembling it, it is alike spoken of as "so very much." There are yet other masters who say: that sutra speaks of a single person who creates two distinct kinds of karma, thereby experiencing two distinct ripening fruits — that is, there was a single person who took two lives, one karma drawing forth a ripening in hell, and the other karma drawing forth a ripening among humankind —

彼若不修身戒心慧。便往地獄受別業異熟果若能勤修身戒心慧。即於人中受別業異熟果。復有說者。彼說一人造一種業感二異熟。謂有一人害一生命。此業能引地獄異熟。兼復能引人中異熟。彼若不修身戒心慧。便往地獄受異熟果。人中異熟果住不生法中。若能勤修身戒心慧。即於人中受異熟果。地獄異熟果住不生法中。評曰。

and, if this person did not cultivate body, precept, mind, and wisdom, he would go to hell to experience the distinct ripening fruit of that karma; but if he was able to diligently cultivate body, precept, mind, and wisdom, he would experience the distinct ripening fruit of that karma right there among humankind. There are yet further masters who say: that sutra speaks of a single person who creates a single kind of karma, thereby experiencing two distinct ripening fruits — that is, there was a single person who took a single life, this one karma being able to draw forth both a ripening in hell and, together with it, a ripening among humankind — and, if this person did not cultivate body, precept, mind, and wisdom, he would go to hell to experience that karma's own ripening fruit there, while its human-realm ripening fruit would remain fixed among the dharmas that never arise; but if he was able to diligently cultivate body, precept, mind, and wisdom, he would experience that karma's own ripening fruit right there among humankind, while its hell-realm ripening fruit would remain fixed among the dharmas that never arise. The verdict:

彼不應作是說。勿有業壞趣壞過故。業壞者。謂一業亦是地獄業亦是人業。趣壞者。應一異熟亦是地獄趣亦是人趣果似因故。應作是說。彼說一人造一種業感一異熟。謂有一人害一生命。此業能引地獄異熟。彼若不能精勤修道成阿羅漢。便往地獄受此業果。彼人若能精勤修道成阿羅漢。便能引取地獄苦事。人身中受此業。不能引眾同分。引眾同分業不可寄受故。由此尊者世友說言。頗有能引地獄苦事人中受不。答曰有能。謂若證得阿羅漢果殊勝定慧薰修身故能為此事。非諸有學及諸異生能為此事。譬如廚人以水漬手雖探熱飯而不被燒。若不漬手即便被燒。此亦如是。故唯無學能為此事。

this position ought not to be maintained, lest the fault of a corrupted karma and a corrupted destiny follow. A corrupted karma would mean that a single karma is at once a hell-karma and also a human-karma; a corrupted destiny would mean that a single ripening fruit is at once a hell-destiny fruit and also a human-destiny fruit, since a fruit resembles its own cause. It should instead be said: that sutra speaks of a single person who creates a single kind of karma, thereby experiencing a single ripening fruit — that is, there was a single person who took a single life, this karma being able to draw forth a ripening in hell, and, if this person was unable to diligently practice the path to the point of attaining arhatship, he would go to hell to experience that karma's own fruit there; but if this person was able to diligently practice the path to the point of attaining arhatship, he would then be able to draw the very suffering-experience of that hell-karma into being experienced right there within his human body, without this karma being able to draw forth any class of homogeneous being at all, since a class-of-being-drawing karma cannot be lent out to be experienced by another. It is on this very account that the Venerable Vasumitra stated: is there a case of one who is able to draw the very suffering-experience proper to hell into being experienced right there within a human body? The answer: there is — namely, whoever has attained the fruit of arhatship, his body having been infused through cultivation by superior concentration and wisdom, is thereby able to accomplish this; no ordinary learner, and no ordinary being, is able to accomplish this. It is just as a cook who, having first wetted his hand with water, may then reach into steaming-hot rice without being burned, whereas, had he not wetted his hand, he would certainly be burned — the case here is exactly the same, and it is for this reason that only one beyond further training is able to accomplish this.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The rejected middle positions all fail for the identical reason that recurs throughout this fascicle — the fear of a “corrupted karma, corrupted destiny” fault, where a single karma or a single fruit ends up straddling two distinct destinies at once — and the final, accepted solution is elegant precisely because it avoids this fault while still explaining the sūtra literally: the hell-experience is genuinely felt, genuinely caused by that karma, and genuinely occurs during this human life, yet at no point does any rebirth or destiny-crossing actually take place, since concentration alone, not karma's own class-of-being-drawing function, is doing the work of relocating where the suffering is felt.

4. A Rejected Middle Path: Karma That Is Sometimes Fixed, Sometimes Not

One further attempt to split the difference is raised and dismissed: perhaps karma itself is simply inconsistent in kind, some of it chaotically mixed and reversible (explaining the “many destinies” citations) and some of it cleanly fixed and irreversible (explaining the “one destiny” citations) — rejected for the same corrupted-karma, corrupted-destiny fault as before, leaving the treatise's own single verdict, that one moment of karma draws forth exactly one class of homogeneous being, standing unqualified.

Chinese Source

有餘師說。一剎那業亦能引多眾同分。問若爾。前難善通。後難云何通耶。答有情造業品類不定。有所造業雜亂可轉。有所造業不雜亂不可轉。若雜亂可轉者。如前所引。若不雜亂不可轉者。如後所引。如是前後二難俱通。評曰。彼不應作是說。勿有業壞趣壞過故。應作是說一剎那業唯能引一眾同分。

Ru-Yi Translation

There are yet other masters who say: a single moment of karma is also able to draw forth many classes of homogeneous being. One asks: if that is so, then the earlier difficulty is soundly resolved — but how, then, is the later difficulty to be resolved? The answer: the very kinds of karma that sentient beings create are themselves not fixed in a single pattern — there is karma that is chaotically mixed and reversible, and there is karma that is not chaotically mixed and irreversible. If it is chaotically mixed and reversible, then the matter is as cited earlier; if it is not chaotically mixed and irreversible, then the matter is as cited afterward — and so both the earlier and the later difficulties are thereby resolved. The verdict: this position ought not to be maintained, lest the fault of a corrupted karma and a corrupted destiny follow. It should instead be said: a single moment of karma is able to draw forth only a single class of homogeneous being.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This is the third distinct attempt in a row to resolve the same tension through some form of case-by-case flexibility, and each attempt fails for exactly the same structural reason — a reminder that, for this treatise, elegant flexibility is not automatically a virtue: a rule admitting too many exceptions risks smuggling back in the very confusion of destinies the entire discussion exists to rule out, and the treatise consistently prefers a single universal rule, however much explanatory labor it then requires elsewhere, to a patchwork of case-sensitive carve-outs.

5. What a Being Actually Receives in Hell, Among Animals, and Among Ghosts

Having settled the class-of-being question, the treatise catalogues, destiny by destiny, exactly what ripening fruit a being actually receives once reborn: hell offers only unwholesome ripening across form, feeling, and the dissociated formations; the animal and hungry-ghost realms offer both wholesome and unwholesome ripening side by side — raising the further question of why some animals and ghosts are beautiful and others hideous, resolved by whether their dominant unwholesome karma is “escorted” by wholesome karma suppressing its ugliness, or by further unwholesome karma compounding it.

Chinese Source

復次但由不善引眾同分業生地獄中。既生彼已唯受不善法異熟色心心所法不相應行。色者九處除聲。心心所法者苦受及相應法。不相應行者。命根眾同分得生老住無常。但由不善引眾同分業。生傍生鬼趣中。既生彼已受善不善法異熟。色心心所法不相應行不善法異熟。色者。九處除聲。心心所法者。苦受及相應法。不相應行者。

9. The Dhyānas and Formless Spheres: Where Grade Stops Mattering

The three-grade analysis reaches its limit once cultivation deepens: the first dhyāna's three grades of karma yield an undifferentiated fruit for anyone reborn there, while the second, third, and fourth dhyānas each sort their three grades into entirely separate heavens — down to the five pure-abode heavens reached only through increasingly refined grades of “mixed cultivation” of dhyāna — before every one of the four formless spheres reverts to the first dhyāna's own undifferentiated pattern. 17. The Sixth and Final Cause Opens: Refuting Seven Rival Views of Causation Itself

The fascicle's closing investigation opens the sixth and final of the six causes, the efficient-cause — the broadest and most minimal of all six, defined ultimately by nothing more than the absence of obstruction. The treatise refutes seven distinct rival positions in a single sweep: that dharmas arise uncaused; that they arise caused but cease uncaused; that only conditioned dharmas can be an efficient-cause; that every efficient-cause must actively take up and give a fruit; that a dharma's own self-nature can cause itself; that unconditioned dharmas, too, possess their own efficient-cause; and that a later dharma can never cause an earlier one.

20. Why a Fingertip Cannot Touch Itself: The Case Against Self-Causation

The fascicle, and the entire six-causes investigation launched in fascicle 16, closes with an extended cascade of arguments against a dharma's own self-nature ever serving as its own efficient-cause: cause and effect would collapse into total indistinction; self-nature has no benefit, harm, increase, decrease, advance, or retreat with respect to itself; it is neither its own condition of any of the four kinds; it would violate every plainly observed fact of the world, since a fingertip cannot touch itself, an eye cannot see itself, a knife cannot cut itself, and even the strongest person cannot carry himself on his own back; it has no mastery or independence over itself, and does not depend on itself the way a standing person depends on a staff; and, since an efficient-cause is defined by the absence of obstruction, a dharma's own self-nature is precisely what obstructs that very self-nature from acting upon itself.
