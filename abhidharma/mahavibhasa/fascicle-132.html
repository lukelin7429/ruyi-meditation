Do Atoms Ever Touch?

Fascicle 132 works through deep atomistic puzzles about touching, gaps, and visibility, refutes those who deny the past and future are real, and builds an exhaustive combinatorial matrix of possession across the three time periods and the desire and form realms — closing with a puzzle about which state of mind can summon a higher realm's own elements into a desire-realm body. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 132 continues the Condition Nyāsa (緣納息第二之二). This fascicle is unusually dense with formulaic combinatorial matrices, characteristic of the treatise's own exhaustive style — the translation preserves this repetitive structure faithfully rather than smoothing it away, since the repetition itself is part of how the argument is made. Terms established across fascicles 93–131 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. How Can Hard Become Soft Without Betraying Its Own Nature? And Do the Elements Leave Gaps?

Fascicle 132 opens with a pair of physics puzzles. First: how can water freeze into unyielding ice, or gold melt into flowing liquid, without any dharma "abandoning its own characteristic"? The answer applies the treatise's own established mechanism — hard and non-hard both already exist, dormant, in the future, and the arising condition simply causes one to cease and the other to take its place. Second: does a mass of the great elements contain gaps between them? Competing views hold either that gaps exist but the space-element itself conceals them, like a woman hidden in a thicket, or that there are no gaps at all, the elements pressing together in unbroken continuity while still never merging into a single substance, since each retains its own distinct function.

Chinese Source

大種蘊第五中緣納息第二之二問若堅不堅物轉相作者。諸法云何不捨自相。云何相作。如水性軟至冬凝結。斧斫猶難金等性堅。若置炎鑪便銷如水。如是等豈非諸法捨自相耶。答非諸堅物轉作不堅。亦非不堅轉成堅物。然堅不堅法住未來世。若遇堅緣。則不堅法滅堅法續生。遇不堅緣則堅法滅不堅續生。餘亦如是。故無諸法捨自相過。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth Aggregate, the Great Elements: the Condition Nyāsa, Chapter Two, Part Two. Question: if hard and non-hard substances can transform into one another, how do dharmas avoid abandoning their own characteristic — and how, exactly, does this transformation occur? Water, soft by nature, freezes solid in winter, resisting even an axe, like the hardness of gold and the rest; yet, placed over a blazing fire, gold too melts and flows like water — do such transformations not amount to dharmas abandoning their own characteristic? Answer: it is not that a hard substance itself is transformed into something non-hard, nor that a non-hard substance is transformed into something hard. Rather, both the hard dharma and the non-hard dharma already exist within the future, and, when the condition for hardness is met, the non-hard dharma ceases and the hard dharma arises in succession; when the condition for non-hardness is met, the hard dharma ceases and the non-hard dharma arises in succession — and likewise for every other such case. Hence there is no fault of any dharma abandoning its own characteristic.

問大種等聚中有間隙不。設爾何失。二俱有過。若有間隙寧不相離。間隙若無何不成一。答有說。此有間隙空界於中相雜住故。問若爾。云何名不相離。答空界於中能自隱匿。令於諸物見不相離。

Question: within a mass composed of the great elements, are there gaps between them? Either way, what fault would follow — both possibilities show difficulty. If there are gaps, how are the elements not thereby separated? But if there are no gaps at all, why do they not simply merge into a single substance? Answer: some say there are indeed gaps, within which the element of space intermingles. Question: if so, how can the elements still be called inseparable? Answer: the element of space, within these gaps, conceals itself, causing every object to appear inseparable,

如叢中女自蔽其身。有說。此無間隙展轉相逼無間住故。問若爾。

— just as a woman hidden within a thicket conceals herself from view. Others say: there are no gaps at all — the elements press against one another continuously, dwelling without any interval whatsoever. Question: if so,

云何不成一耶。答雖無間隙而不成一。如蘊處界三世等中。間隙雖無而不成一。彼亦如是。又大種等自體作用。各各異故不可成一。

— why do they not simply merge into a single substance? Answer: though there is no gap between them, they still do not merge into a single substance — just as the aggregates, sense-fields, and elements, or the three time periods, though having no gap between them, still do not merge into a single entity, so too it is here. Moreover, the great elements each possess their own distinct essential substance and function, and hence cannot merge into a single substance.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The recurring "dormant in the future, cause and effect merely substituted" mechanism, now applied to something as visually dramatic as ice melting into water, shows how far this tradition is willing to push a single metaphysical commitment: even the most ordinary observed transformation in the physical world is reanalyzed as strict substitution between pre-existing possibilities, never a a single substance genuinely changing what it is.

2. Do Atoms Ever Actually Touch? A Meditation on Drifting Dust

A precise atomistic puzzle asks whether atoms genuinely touch one another at all — resolved firmly in the negative, since touching would require either total contact (collapsing distinct atoms into one) or partial contact (implying atoms have distinguishable parts, which by definition they cannot). What holds an aggregated mass together despite this non-touching is instead the wind-element's own cohesive power. A four-way analysis then catalogues touching and non-touching as cause and effect in every combination, closing with the quietly beautiful image of drifting dust motes, continuing their own kind in perpetual non-contact.

Chinese Source

問諸極微互相觸不。設爾何失。二俱有過。若相觸者寧不成一。或成有分。若不相觸擊時應散或應無聲。答應作是說。極微互不相觸。若觸則應或遍或分。遍觸則有成一體過。分觸則有成有分失。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: do atoms touch one another? Either way, what fault would follow — both possibilities show difficulty. If they touch, how do they not merge into a single substance, or else acquire distinguishable parts? But if they do not touch, then, when struck together, they ought either to scatter apart, or produce no sound at all. Answer: it should be said that atoms do not touch one another — for, were they to touch, this touching would have to be either total or partial: total touching would incur the fault of merging into a single substance, and partial touching would incur the fault of acquiring distinguishable parts,

然諸極微更無細分。問聚色相擊寧不散耶。答風界攝持故令不散。

— yet atoms possess no finer parts whatsoever. Question: when aggregated masses of form strike one another, why do they not simply scatter apart? Answer: it is the wind-element's own power of cohesion that prevents them from scattering.

問豈不風界能飄散耶。答有能飄散。如壞劫時。有能攝持。如成劫時。問若不觸者。即相擊時云何發聲。答即由此因故使聲發。若相觸者。如何發聲。謂諸極微體相觸者。手等相和體應相糅。中無間隙如何發聲。尊者世友作如是說。若諸極微互相觸者。彼應得住至後剎那。大德說言。實不相觸。但於合集無間生中。隨世俗諦假名相觸。問諸是觸物。為是觸為因故生。為非觸為因故生。諸非觸物。為非觸為因故生。為是觸為因故生。答有時是觸為因生於非觸。謂和合物正離散時。有時非觸為因生於是觸。謂離散物正和合時。有時是觸為因生於是觸。謂和合物復和合時。有時非觸為因生於非觸。謂向遊塵同類相續。

Question: does the wind-element not also scatter things apart? Answer: it may indeed scatter things apart, as at the destruction of an eon, or hold things together, as at the formation of an eon. Question: if atoms do not touch, then, when struck together, how does sound arise? Answer: it is precisely on account of this very non-touching that sound is able to arise — for, were they truly to touch, how could sound arise at all? For, were atoms' own substances actually to touch one another, then, when hands and the like come together, their substances would have to blend together as one, with no gap remaining between them — how, then, could any sound be produced? The Venerable Vasumitra explains it this way: were atoms truly to touch one another, they would have to remain fixed in place, persisting into the very next instant. The Great Virtuous One says: they truly do not touch at all — it is only within their continuous, uninterrupted aggregation that they are conventionally, provisionally spoken of as "touching." Question: is whatever is touching produced by touching as its cause, or by non-touching as its cause? And is whatever is not touching produced by non-touching as its cause, or by touching as its cause? Answer: sometimes touching serves as the cause producing non-touching — as when an aggregated object is in the very process of dispersing apart; sometimes non-touching serves as the cause producing touching — as when a dispersed object is in the very process of coming together; sometimes touching serves as the cause producing touching — as when an already-aggregated object aggregates further still; and sometimes non-touching serves as the cause producing non-touching — as with drifting motes of dust, continuing on in their own similar succession.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The image of dust motes drifting through a sunbeam, illustrating "non-touching producing non-touching," is a rare moment of pure visual poetry inside an otherwise rigorously analytical passage — a fitting emblem for the atoms this whole discussion concerns, themselves invisible, weightless, and never quite making contact with anything, including each other.

3. Are Atoms Visible? And Why Past and Future Must Be Real

A brief dispute over whether atoms are visible or invisible — the Venerable Ghoṣa saying visible to the wisdom-eye alone, the Abhidharma masters insisting invisible to any physical or divine sight — gives way to a much larger undertaking: refuting an unnamed school that denies the reality of the past and future altogether, and an outside path that inverts causation entirely, claiming a river's forward surge proves later moments force earlier ones into being. The refutation shows this inversion would collapse every ordinary causal relationship — parents born from children, seeds sprouting from shoots, results preceding the acts that produce them.

Chinese Source

問極微當言可見不可見耶。答尊者妙音作如是說。極微當言可見。

Ru-Yi Translation

The various Abhidharma treatise-masters say that atoms should be said to be invisible, being incapable of being seen by the physical or divine eye — for this question is not asked with reference to the wisdom-eye, since, with respect to the wisdom-eye, no dharma differs from any other in this regard. Is there past great-element production of past form? And so on in full. Question: why compose this treatise? Answer: in order to refute others' positions and display our own. For there is another school that denies the reality of the past and the future, holding that the present alone is truly conditioned — and it is in order to refute this view, and to show that the past and future are real, and that the present too is conditioned, that this treatise is composed, as explained in full before. There is, moreover, an outside path that, on the basis of an analogy drawn from observing worldly phenomena, holds that a conditioned dharma's later moment serves as cause for its earlier moment — arguing thus: "We observe that a spring's water is forced forward by what comes after it, surging in a continuous flow — just so, every dharma is forced into the present by a future dharma, and forced into the past by a present dharma, and hence a conditioned dharma's later moment serves as cause for its earlier moment." It is now in order to refute this outside path's own claim, and to show instead that a conditioned dharma's earlier moment is capable of generating its later moment, never the reverse, that this treatise is composed — for, were it otherwise, this would contradict causal origination both within and beyond the person: parents would then be born from their own children, the eye and form would arise from eye-consciousness, ignorance and the rest would arise from formations and the rest, seeds would arise from sprouts, and one would experience the fruit of pleasure or pain before creating the wholesome or unwholesome karma that causes it, and would attain awakening before undertaking the practice that leads to it — one would obtain a result before ever performing the act that produces it, and would lose what one has not yet even done — and, were this so, there could be no rational path to liberation at all. Moreover, this treatise is composed in order to refute the claim that the great elements and the derived form they produce must always necessarily belong to the same time period, and to show that they may indeed belong to different time periods. It is on account of these causes and conditions that this treatise is composed. Resistant derived form, and unmanifest form that follows the mind, are each produced by great elements belonging to that same time period in which they themselves occur — that is, past form is produced by past elements, present form by present elements, and future form by future elements. Unmanifest form arisen from manifest expression, however, is of three further kinds, each produced at a different time — the very first instant, like resistant form, is produced by great elements of the same time period,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The outside path's causal inversion, however strange it sounds stated abstractly, is refuted not by abstract argument but by simply following it to its logical conclusion across every domain at once — biology, perception, agriculture, karma, and the path to awakening — showing that a single mistaken reading of a flowing spring, taken seriously, would make the entire observable order of cause and effect impossible.

4. A Three-Stage Rule for Which Vintage of Elements Sustains an Unbroken Precept

An intricate rule governs which great elements — past, present, or future — produce a given instance of derived form: ordinary resistant form and meditation-linked unmanifest form always match their own time period exactly, but manifest-triggered unmanifest form (the ongoing precept-restraint launched by an initial act of expression) follows a subtler three-stage pattern, its first instant matching the triggering elements' own time, its later instants drawing increasingly on past, and eventually all three time periods together, as the restraint's own continuity becomes progressively independent of the moment that first launched it.

Chinese Source

第二剎那若在過現。俱為過去大種所造。若在未來通為現未大種所造。後諸剎那過現如前。若在未來通為三世大種所造。所以者何。

Ru-Yi Translation

— but the second instant, whether it falls in the past or the present, is produced solely by past great elements; if it falls in the future, it is produced by either present or future great elements; and every subsequent instant follows this same pattern for the past and present, while, if it falls in the future, it may be produced by great elements of all three time periods. For why is this?

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That an ongoing precept-restraint's dependence on its own originating elements loosens progressively over time — matching them exactly at first, then drawing only on the past, then eventually on all three time periods — models something genuinely apt about how a vow actually functions: the initial commitment matters decisively at the moment it is made, but its continued life increasingly draws on accumulated momentum rather than remaining tethered to that single founding instant.

5. The Great Combinatorial Matrix Begins: Past Elements, Past Form

The now-familiar exhaustive tetralemma matrix returns, this time cross-tabulating possession of past great elements against possession of past derived form across every developmental stage and realm — establishing, among other things, that trainees reborn in the formless realm possess past derived form only if they had already both attained and let go of some undefiled path before their rebirth there, a qualification the treatise carefully specifies rather than leaving implicit.

A striking asymmetry emerges: unlike derived form, the great elements themselves are never possessed across past or future time, only in the present moment of their actual manifestation. A final, concrete puzzle closes the fascicle: a desire-realm being who has attained form-realm-level wholesome mind possesses form-realm derived form without the form-realm elements themselves being currently manifest — so which state of mind actually causes those elements to manifest within a desire-realm body?
