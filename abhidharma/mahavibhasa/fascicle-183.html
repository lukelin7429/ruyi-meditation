How the True Dharma Will Perish

Fascicle 183 closes the Non-Returner Nyāsa (不還納息第四之九/十) and opens the Single Practice Nyāsa (一行納息第五之一): why the earth-deities cry out first at the turning of the Dharma-wheel, why three world-historic events resonate to three different heavens, what actually counts as "the Dharma-wheel," whether the places of a Buddha's own life are fixed across every Buddha, what the true Dharma is, the full eschatological account of how it will one day perish through internal corruption rather than external persecution, refutations of denying future cultivation and a single undifferentiated path, and the opening of a new topic — the three concentrations, empty, wishless, and signless — with its exhaustive sixfold analysis across the three times. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Why the Earth-Deities Cry Out First

Fascicle 183 opens by finishing the Non-Returner Nyāsa (不還納息第四之九/十, its final stretch) with a question about the sūtra's own account of the earth-deities crying out the news of the first turning of the Dharma-wheel: why the earth-deities specifically, and not the other classes of deities present? The treatise offers a cascade of reasons — the earth-deities dwell nearest the Buddha, had constantly guarded him since before his birth, are simply the most excitable and joy-prone class of deity, and stand first in a natural relay of proximity that carries the news, lamp to lamp, all the way up to the Brahmā world within a single moment, despite sound's own momentary nature making a literal journey impossible.

Chinese Source

轉法輪已地神唱聲展轉宣告乃至廣說問會中亦有餘天神眾發聲相告。何緣但說地神唱聲。答由彼地神先發聲故。問地神何故先發聲耶。答以彼最近佛所住故。復次彼恒隨佛而作衛故。謂從菩薩處胎初生踰城出家及修苦行乃至成佛轉法輪時。恒隨衛護令無留難。今見如來轉法輪已歡喜踊躍。自慶先來所施功勞今得果滿故先唱告。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once the Dharma-wheel has been turned, the earth-deities cry out, and this proclamation is passed on in succession — and so on at length. Question: within that assembly there were also other classes of deities who likewise cried out to one another — why is it said that the earth-deities alone cried out? Answer: because it was the earth-deities who cried out first. Question: why did the earth-deities cry out first? Answer: because they dwell closest to wherever the Buddha is. Furthermore, it is because they have constantly guarded and attended the Buddha — that is, from the time he dwelt in the womb as a bodhisattva, through his birth, his crossing over the city wall to go forth, his cultivation of austerities, and on up to his attaining Buddhahood and turning the Dharma-wheel, they had constantly guarded and attended him, allowing no hindrance to arise. Now, seeing the Tathāgata having turned the Dharma-wheel, they leapt for joy, congratulating themselves that the labor they had long expended had now come to its full fruition, and it is for this reason that they were the first to cry out and proclaim it.

復次時會雖有餘天神眾而彼地神性輕躁故是以先唱。如今眾中性輕躁者多喜高聲彼亦如是。復次地神性多喜慶。見此希有極懷歡喜故先發聲。復次此是近遠次第法故。謂地神先唱。次虛空神。次四大王眾天。如是展轉經須臾頃聲至梵世。問聲是剎那性若此處生必此處滅。云何可說至梵世耶。答依展轉義故作是說。謂地神唱已虛空神唱展轉宣告乃至梵天如燃燈法展轉增廣有說。轉法輪時無量天眾皆悉來集既聞法已各還所住。宣告自部故說展轉聲至梵世。有說。

Furthermore, although other classes of deities were likewise present in that assembly, it was the earth-deities, being of a naturally excitable disposition, who therefore cried out first — just as, among a crowd today, those of an excitable disposition are the ones most given to shouting; it is the same in this case. Furthermore, the earth-deities are by nature given to rejoicing, and, beholding so rare an event, were overcome with such great joy that they were the first to cry out. Furthermore, this follows the natural order of near to far — that is, the earth-deities cry out first, then the space-deities, then the hosts of the Four Great Heavenly Kings, and so on in succession, until, within a single brief moment, the sound reaches the Brahmā world. Question: sound is of a momentary nature — arising in one place, it must perish in that very place — how, then, can it be said to reach the Brahmā world? Answer: this is said with reference to the meaning of successive transmission — that is, once the earth-deities have cried out, the space-deities cry out in turn, and this proclamation is passed on in succession, clear up to the Brahmā heaven, just as a flame is passed from lamp to lamp, growing and spreading in succession. Some say: at the time the Dharma-wheel was turned, countless hosts of deities had all gathered together, and, having heard the Dharma, each returned to his own abode and proclaimed it to his own kind, and it is with reference to this successive proclamation that the sound is said to reach the Brahmā world. Some say:

彼時發聲亦無決定先後但是說者敘述次第法應如是故作是說。地神先唱乃至廣說。

at that time there was no fixed order of precedence among those who cried out at all; it is simply that the one relating this account follows the natural convention of narrating things in sequence, and it is for this reason that it is said, "the earth-deities cried out first," and so on at length.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Solving the puzzle of how a strictly momentary sound could travel all the way to the Brahmā world by treating it as a relay rather than a journey — each class of deity hearing, then re-uttering the news to the next rank up, like a flame passed from lamp to lamp — lets the treatise honor its own metaphysics of momentariness while still making sense of scripture's own claim that the news traveled that far.

2. Three Sounds, Three Heavens

A wheel-turning king's own appearance sends its news only as far as the Paranirmita-vaśavartin heaven, the turning of the Dharma-wheel reaches the Brahmā world, and the Buddha's own awakening reaches all the way to the Akaniṣṭha heaven — and the treatise works out why each event's own resonance is calibrated to a different audience: the wheel-king's ten wholesome courses of action ripen specifically among desire-realm heavens eager for more devout subjects; the Brahmā king's own investment in the Dharma-wheel's turning, having entreated it himself, caps its reach at his own world; and only the Buddha's own boundless renown, cultivated across countless lifetimes, reaches every plane without exception.

Chinese Source

如契經說轉輪王出世時聲至他化自在天。佛為憍陳那等轉法輪時聲至梵世。佛得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提時聲至色究竟天。問何故如是三種差別。答轉輪王出世時以十善法。教導有情。此法必於欲界天中受異熟果。六欲天眾皆生歡喜我等眷屬不久增多。是故輪王出世聲至他化自在天。梵天王請佛轉法輪彼聞佛轉法輪深慶隨喜。是故轉法輪時聲至梵世。淨居天覺悟菩薩令踰城出家求無上智。彼聞菩薩得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提極懷喜慰。是故初成佛時聲至色究竟天。復次轉輪王是受欲者故出世時聲極欲界不至離欲地。佛轉法輪時於一眾會有尊卑勝劣。此事唯至梵世。是故轉法輪時聲至梵天。唯有如來名聲高遠無所不至。是故佛得無上菩提時其聲遍及所應至處。乃至色究竟天。設當有頂有情有耳識者聲亦徹彼。所以者何。如來久修名稱業故。復次諸有情類造作增長大名稱業有上中下。下者如轉輪王。中者如憍陳那等。上者謂佛。是故於彼聲有近遠。復次諸有情類造作增長淨尊貴業有上中下。下者如轉輪王中者如憍陳那等。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the sūtra states, when a wheel-turning king appears in the world, the sound reaches the Paranirmita-vaśavartin heaven; when the Buddha turns the Dharma-wheel for Kauṇḍinya and the rest, the sound reaches the Brahmā world; and when the Buddha attains unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, the sound reaches the Akaniṣṭha heaven. Question: why are there these three distinct measures? Answer: when a wheel-turning king appears in the world, he instructs sentient beings in the ten wholesome courses of action, and this dharma is bound to ripen into its fruit specifically among the heavens of the desire realm; the hosts of the six desire-realm heavens all rejoice, thinking, "our own following will before long increase," and it is for this reason that, when a wheel-king appears, the sound reaches the Paranirmita-vaśavartin heaven. The Brahmā king had entreated the Buddha to turn the Dharma-wheel, and, hearing that the Buddha has done so, rejoices deeply and gives himself over to sympathetic joy, and it is for this reason that, when the Dharma-wheel is turned, the sound reaches the Brahmā world. The devas of the Pure Abodes had awakened the bodhisattva, causing him to cross over the city wall and go forth in search of unsurpassed wisdom, and, hearing that the bodhisattva has attained unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, are overcome with the deepest joy and consolation, and it is for this reason that, at the moment he first becomes a Buddha, the sound reaches the Akaniṣṭha heaven. Furthermore, a wheel-turning king is one who still partakes of sensual pleasure, and so, when he appears in the world, the sound extends only throughout the desire realm, not reaching any ground free of desire; when the Buddha turns the Dharma-wheel, there is, within that single assembly, a hierarchy of the honored and the humble, the superior and the inferior, and this matter extends only as far as the Brahmā world, and it is for this reason that, when the Dharma-wheel is turned, the sound reaches the Brahmā heaven; only the Tathāgata's own renown is so lofty and far-reaching that it extends everywhere without exception, and it is for this reason that, at the moment the Buddha attains unsurpassed awakening, its sound pervades every place it is fitting to reach, clear up to the Akaniṣṭha heaven — and, were there even a sentient being at the very peak of existence possessed of an auditory consciousness, the sound would penetrate there as well, since the Tathāgata has, for so long, cultivated the very karma of renown itself. Furthermore, among the various classes of sentient beings who create and augment the karma of great renown, there are superior, middling, and inferior degrees — the inferior being exemplified by the wheel-turning king, the middling by Kauṇḍinya and the rest, and the superior by the Buddha himself — and it is for this reason that the sound reaches near or far accordingly. Furthermore, among the various classes of sentient beings who create and augment the karma of pure honor, there are likewise superior, middling, and inferior degrees — the inferior being exemplified by the wheel-turning king, the middling by Kauṇḍinya and the rest,

上者謂佛。是故聲至有近有遠。尊者世友說曰。諸有情類恭敬讚歎父母師長。沙門婆羅門佛獨覺。及佛弟子。身語意業有上中下。下者如轉輪王。中者如憍陳那等。上者謂佛。是故聲至三處不同。

and the superior by the Buddha himself — and it is for this reason that the sound reaches near or far accordingly. The Venerable Vasumitra states: among the various classes of sentient beings, the bodily, verbal, and mental karma of reverently praising one's own parents, teachers and elders, ascetics, brahmins, the Buddha, pratyekabuddhas, and the Buddha's own disciples is likewise of superior, middling, and inferior degree — the inferior being exemplified by the wheel-turning king, the middling by Kauṇḍinya and the rest, and the superior by the Buddha himself — and it is for this reason that the sound reaches these three different places in three different measures.

問上地亦有聲何故轉法輪時聲唯至梵世。答語表業聲唯至彼故。復次語言行唯至彼地語言行者所謂尋伺。復次乃至彼地得有耳識非上地故。有說。唯至梵世得具起善染污無覆無記語有表聲非上地故。

Question: sound exists in the higher grounds as well — why, then, does the sound reach only as far as the Brahmā world when the Dharma-wheel is turned? Answer: because it is only up to that ground that the manifest form of vocal karma, sound, extends. Furthermore, it is only up to that ground that verbal activity extends — "verbal activity" referring to initial and sustained investigation. Furthermore, it is only up to that ground that an auditory consciousness can be present, not in any of the higher grounds. Some say: it is only up to the Brahmā world that one can give rise, complete and in full, to wholesome, defiled, and unobscured-neutral speech together with its manifest vocal form, not in any of the higher grounds.

復次唯至梵世得具起善染污無覆無記耳識現前非上地故。有說。若處有眾生差別轉法輪時聲則至彼。復次若處作三千世界分齊者聲至於彼。有說。梵世是世間有情所尊重處。是故轉法輪時聲但至彼。

Furthermore, it is only up to the Brahmā world that one can give rise, complete and in full, to a wholesome, defiled, or unobscured-neutral auditory consciousness manifesting before the mind, not in any of the higher grounds. Some say: the sound reaches, at the time the Dharma-wheel is turned, only as far as there is a differentiation among sentient beings. Furthermore, the sound reaches only as far as marks the boundary of a trichiliocosm. Some say: the Brahmā world is the place most honored by the sentient beings of this world, and it is for this reason that, when the Dharma-wheel is turned, the sound reaches only that far.

復有說者。上地諸天亦名為梵。是故轉法輪時聲至梵世者。不唯至初靜慮地。

Some further say: the devas of the higher grounds, too, are named "Brahmā," and hence, when it is said that the sound reaches the Brahmā world at the turning of the Dharma-wheel, this does not refer only to the ground of the first dhyāna.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Calibrating how far each of three world-historic events resonates according to who has a genuine stake in hearing about it — desire-realm gods invested in a wheel-king's rule, Brahmā invested in his own answered entreaty, and literally everyone invested in a Buddha's awakening — treats cosmic news itself as governed by relevance rather than by mere physical proximity, extending exactly as far as there exists an audience for whom the news actually matters.

3. What Counts as the Dharma-Wheel: The First and the Last

Not every dharma the Buddha ever taught counts as the Dharma-wheel — only what actually causes someone to enter the path of seeing does — yet, since countless disciples across the Buddha's own teaching career met this criterion, the treatise narrows the honorific further still: only the very first and the very last person to attain true understanding under the Buddha's own teaching are specifically named as instances of "the Dharma-wheel," Kauṇḍinya at the beginning and Subhadra at the very end of the Buddha's own teaching life.

Chinese Source

問佛所說法盡名法輪耶。答不也。唯令入見道者乃名法輪。問若爾聞佛說法入見道者多。何故不皆名法輪耶。答彼一切雖皆是法輪而最初最後得正解者說為法輪。初謂憍陳那等。後謂蘇跋達羅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: is every dharma the Buddha teaches named the Dharma-wheel? Answer: it is not. Only what causes one to enter the path of seeing is named the Dharma-wheel. Question: if so, then many, upon hearing the Buddha's teaching, enter the path of seeing — why, then, are not all of these named the Dharma-wheel? Answer: although every one of these is indeed the Dharma-wheel, it is specifically the very first and the very last to attain true understanding who are spoken of as the Dharma-wheel — the first referring to Kauṇḍinya and the rest, and the last referring to Subhadra.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Reserving the name "Dharma-wheel" for only the first and the last of countless equally genuine breakthroughs is a small but telling piece of narrative economy: the treatise treats the beginning and the ending of an entire teaching career as the two moments worth memorializing by name, letting everything in between remain real but unnamed, bracketed by the two events that give the whole career its own shape.

4. Are the Places of the Buddha's Life Fixed?

A genuinely striking historical-critical puzzle follows: is the place where a Buddha turns the Dharma-wheel fixed across every Buddha, or does it vary? Citing the past Buddha Dīpaṃkara's own differently located first sermon against a verse insisting every Buddha turns the wheel at this same spot, the treatise offers both sides fair treatment, explicitly noting that neither the Dīpaṃkara narrative nor the verse is drawn from the Sūtra, Vinaya, or Abhidharma proper, and hence neither strictly needs reconciling at all — before laying out, for the "fixed" position, four unshakeable sites (the Bodhi tree, the place of the first sermon, the site of descent from the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, and the site of the great miracle), each defended by its own vivid supporting legend, from a wheel-turning king's own flying chariot mysteriously halting above the Bodhi tree to a stone lion roaring to settle a property dispute between monks and tīrthikas.

Chinese Source

問一切佛轉法輪處為定不耶。若定者然燈佛本事當云何通。如說然燈佛於燈光城喝利多羅山轉正法輪。乃至廣說。若不定者達摩蘇部底所說頌云何通。如說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: is the place where every Buddha turns the Dharma-wheel fixed, or not? If it is fixed, how is the account of the Buddha Dīpaṃkara's own past life to be reconciled, where it is said that the Buddha Dīpaṃkara turned the true Dharma-wheel at Mount Hāritāla, in the city of Dīpavatī, and so on at length? And if it is not fixed, how is the verse spoken by Dharmasubhūti to be reconciled? As it is said:

應念過去佛　於此迦尸宮仙論施鹿林　亦初轉妙法有說。應言轉法輪處定問若爾然燈佛本事當云何通。答此不必須通。所以者何。此非素怛纜毘柰耶阿毘達磨所說。但是傳說諸傳所說或然不然。若必欲通者應知過去燈光城即是今婆羅痆斯。過去喝利多羅山即是今仙人鹿苑。若作是說。佛轉法輪處定者。彼說有四處定二處不定。四處定者謂菩提樹處。轉法輪處。天上來下處。現大神變處。二處不定者謂佛生處及般涅槃處。云何得知菩提樹處定。答曾聞過去有轉輪王。導從四兵飛空而過至菩提樹上。其輪便止欲前不得。王遂惶恐作是思惟。我今將無欲失王位或命難耶。時菩提樹神即白王曰。大王勿怪王不失位亦無命難。王不見下菩提樹耶。此中有金剛座一切菩薩皆於此座證得無上正等菩提。王欲過者可避此處從餘道往。時王便下種種供養菩提樹已從餘道去。以是事故知菩提樹處定。轉法輪處定者如前所引法善現頌。復云何知天上來下處定。答曾聞佛去世後此處有難事起諸苾芻等並皆捨去。外道異學來居其中。後諸苾芻來索其處。語外道曰。此是我師天上來處可速避去。諸外道言。此是我等常所住處。因此二眾大興鬪諍。近住城中長者居士諸官僚等來解其諍而不能得。乃至王自解之亦不能定。時諸苾芻告外道曰。今當與汝俱設誠言應屬誰者當有瑞相。外道言爾。彼遂先請而空無驗。苾芻即復作誠諦言。此處若是一切如來昇三十三天為慈母說法經三月已下來處者當現瑞相。時彼住處大石柱上有石師子即便哮吼。外道驚恐即時捨去。從師子口復出眾寶花鬘纏遶石柱皆悉周遍。時眾觀者歎未曾有。於是苾芻遂共居止。

"One ought to recall the past Buddhas — in this very palace of Kāśi, the sages' own debating-ground, the Deer Park — they too first turned the wondrous Dharma."

Some say: it should rather be said that the place of turning the Dharma-wheel is fixed. Question: if so, how is the account of the Buddha Dīpaṃkara's own past life to be reconciled? Answer: this need not necessarily be reconciled at all. Why is this? Because this account is not stated in the Sūtra, the Vinaya, or the Abhidharma, but is merely a traditional account, and traditional accounts may be either so or not so. Yet, if one insists on reconciling it, it should be understood that the ancient city of Dīpavatī is none other than the present Vārāṇasī, and that the ancient Mount Hāritāla is none other than the present Deer Park of the Sages. Now, according to this view — that the place where the Buddha turns the Dharma-wheel is fixed — it is said that there are four fixed places and two unfixed places. The four fixed places are: the place of the Bodhi tree; the place of turning the Dharma-wheel; the place of descending from heaven; and the place of displaying the great miracle. The two unfixed places are: the Buddha's own birthplace, and the place of his parinirvāṇa. How is it known that the place of the Bodhi tree is fixed? Answer: it has been heard that, once, in the past, there was a wheel-turning king who, leading his fourfold army, flew through the air and, upon reaching a point above the Bodhi tree, found his own wheel-treasure come to a halt, unable to proceed further. The king, alarmed, reflected: "could I now be about to lose my own kingship, or even my life?" At that time, the deity of the Bodhi tree addressed the king, saying: "Great King, do not be alarmed — you shall lose neither your kingship nor your life. Does Your Majesty not see the Bodhi tree below? Beneath it lies the vajra seat, upon which every bodhisattva alike realizes unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. If Your Majesty wishes to pass by, you may avoid this place and proceed by another route." The king thereupon descended and made every kind of offering to the Bodhi tree, then departed by another route. It is on account of this event that the place of the Bodhi tree is known to be fixed. That the place of turning the Dharma-wheel is fixed is shown by the verse of Dharmasubhūti already cited above. How, further, is it known that the place of descending from heaven is fixed? Answer: it has been heard that, after the Buddha had passed from the world, some calamity arose at this place, and all the monks abandoned it, whereupon tīrthika practitioners of other schools came to dwell there instead. Later, when the monks came to reclaim the place, they said to the tīrthikas: "this is the place where our own teacher descended from heaven — you must vacate it at once." The tīrthikas replied: "this has long been our own dwelling-place." On account of this, the two parties came into fierce dispute; the elders, householders, and officials dwelling in the nearby city came to settle the quarrel, but were unable to do so, and even when the king himself attempted to settle it, he too could not resolve it. At that time the monks said to the tīrthikas: "let us now, together with you, each make a solemn declaration, and let there appear a miraculous sign showing to whom this place truly belongs." The tīrthikas agreed to this. They made their own declaration first, but no sign appeared at all. The monks then made their own solemn, truthful declaration: "if this place is indeed where every Tathāgata alike ascends to the Trāyastriṃśa heaven to teach the Dharma to his own departed mother, and descends again after three months, then let a miraculous sign now appear." At that moment, upon a great stone pillar standing at that very place, a stone lion suddenly roared aloud. The tīrthikas, terrified, at once fled and abandoned the place. 5. What Is the True Dharma?

Turning to define "the true Dharma" itself, the treatise identifies it strictly with the uncontaminated faculties, powers, awakening-factors, and path-factors — carefully excluding their contaminated, preparatory counterparts, since only what is genuinely free of fault earns the name — then distinguishes two kinds of true Dharma (the conventional, meaning the scriptures' own names and syllables, and the ultimate, meaning the noble path itself) sustained by two kinds of practitioner (those who uphold the teaching, and those who uphold its actual realization). This grounds a memorable reinterpretation of the Buddha's own famous statement that admitting women to the monastic order would shorten his Dharma's own lifespan from a thousand years to five hundred: not a literal timetable at all, but a statement about the firmness of liberation specifically, later reinterpreted again as depending entirely on whether the eight special rules for nuns are actually observed.

6. How the True Dharma Will Perish

The fascicle's most extraordinary passage is a full eschatological narrative of the Dharma's own eventual extinction: barbarian kings will ravage India, burning scriptures and slaughtering monks, until a righteous eastern king defeats them and gathers every surviving monk into a single generously provisioned kingdom — where, ironically, the very abundance of support corrupts monastic discipline, leaving only two genuine practitioners, the arhat Surata and the Tripiṭaka master Śikha. When Surata insists the precepts be recited in full rather than abbreviated, claiming he alone still keeps them to their very furthest extent, the master's own outraged disciples kill him — extinguishing the ultimate true Dharma on the spot — and a chain of retaliatory killings extinguishes the conventional true Dharma as well, the catastrophe going unnoticed by the world itself for seven full days, on account of the Buddha's own past-life karma of habitually concealing others' faults.

7. Refuting Denial of Future Cultivation and a Single Undifferentiated Path

The treatise turns to refute three rival positions at once — that there is no such thing as future cultivation, that the noble path is unconditioned, and that the noble path is a single, undifferentiated thing across all three times — each refutation following from a stark consequence: without future cultivation, virtue could never increase and the Buddha's own omniscience would be incomplete; an unconditioned path could never be cultivated at all, since cultivation is definitionally something conditioned dharmas undergo; and a single undifferentiated path would make nonsense of scripture's own casual references to Buddhas across the three times. A parallel refutation follows against those who deny any genuine moment of arising or ceasing altogether, holding a conditioned dharma to possess only "not-yet-arisen" and "already-arisen" states — the treatise insisting that arising belongs precisely to the future and ceasing precisely to the present, this exact temporal placement itself proving that conditioned dharmas really do arise and cease, not merely shift between hidden and manifest.

8. A New Nyāsa Begins: The Three Concentrations

A genuine milestone: the Non-Returner Nyāsa (不還納息), which has run since fascicle 174, now closes, and the treatise opens the Single Practice Nyāsa (一行納息第五之一) with the three concentrations — the empty, the wishless, and the signless — asking the foundational question of whether possessing any one of these three guarantees possessing the others. The answer establishes that the empty and the wishless are always attained together, being counteractions to the very same afflictions and arising within the very same mind-moments on the path, while the signless follows a slightly different rule, attained only once the patient acceptance of the cognition of the Dharma of cessation has arisen, after which it too is possessed constantly alongside the other two.

Although, within the four mind-moments of the direct realization of origin on the path of seeing, and within the three mind-moments of the direct realization of the path, one attains the wishless but not the empty, yet, since the very first moment of transition from non-possession to possession necessarily brings both together at once, one thereafter constantly possesses both together. Does one who possesses the empty thereby possess the signless? Answer: if one has attained it — "attained" here meaning already attained — that is, once the patient acceptance of the cognition of the Dharma of cessation has arisen, one thereafter constantly possesses the signless. And does one who possesses the signless thereby possess the empty? Answer: yes, since, at the moment one comes to possess the signless, one has necessarily already attained the empty beforehand. Does one who possesses the wishless thereby possess the signless? Answer: if one has attained it. And does one who possesses the signless thereby possess the wishless? Answer: yes — this is to be explained exactly as with the empty in relation to the signless.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Opening this new section by immediately refuting the view that only "possession" is real while "non-possession" is a mere conventional fiction is a quietly significant metaphysical commitment: it insists that the absence of an attainment is just as much a real fact about a person's own mental continuum as its presence, setting up the entire elaborate sixfold temporal analysis that follows on the premise that both possessing and not-yet-possessing are equally substantial states to be tracked.

9. The Sixfold Analysis of Possessing the Empty Concentration Across the Three Times

The fascicle closes with an extraordinarily granular technical exercise: running the empty concentration through every possible combination of possession across the past, present, and future — does possessing it in the past guarantee possessing it in the future? In the present? In both at once? — with each answer keyed to the exact meditative stage at which it holds, down to specifying precisely which mind-moment of which direct realization a given practitioner must occupy. The treatise closes by noting that this entire sixfold analysis, run in full for the empty concentration, applies equally, with the appropriate adjustments, to the wishless and the signless as well.
