Sorting the Faculties by Trainee, Ripening, and Cutting-Off

Fascicle 144 works through which of the twenty-two faculties belong to the trainee, the one beyond training, or neither, offers nine definitions of these three categories, explains why displeasure alone can never be neutral, defends karmic ripening and the mundane path's own power to cut off affliction, and closes with a precise census of which underlying tendencies are cut off by seeing versus by cultivation. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Sorting Twenty-Two Faculties by Stage of the Path

Fascicle 144 turns to a new organizing question for the twenty-two faculties: of these, how many belong strictly to the trainee, how many to the one beyond training, how many to neither, and how many require further distinction depending on the specific state of the practitioner? The answer sorts them cleanly — two belong exclusively to the trainee, one exclusively to the one beyond training, ten to neither, and nine, including the mind-faculty itself, demanding a genuinely threefold breakdown depending on the practitioner's own current condition.

Chinese Source

根蘊第六中根納息第一之三此二十二根。幾學。幾無學。幾非學非無學。答二學。一無學。十非學非無學。九應分別。二學者。謂未知當知根已知根。一無學者。謂具知根。十非學非無學者。謂七色命苦憂根。九應分別者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth Aggregate, the Faculties: the Faculty Nyāsa, Chapter One, Part Three. Of these twenty-two faculties, how many belong to the trainee, how many to the one beyond training, and how many to neither? Answer: two belong to the trainee, one to the one beyond training, ten to neither, and nine require further distinction. The two belonging to the trainee are the faculty of what is yet unknown but about to be known, and the faculty of what is already known. The one belonging to the one beyond training is the faculty of complete knowledge. The ten belonging to neither are the seven form-faculties, the life-faculty, the pain-faculty, and the displeasure-faculty. The nine requiring further distinction —

謂意樂喜捨信等五根。此皆應作三分而答。故名應分別。一作學分。二作無學分。三作非學非無學分。分別論者言。此應作分別記。故名應分別。謂意根或學或無學或非學非無學。云何學。謂學作意相應意根。此復云何。謂從苦法智忍。乃至金剛喻定相應意根。云何無學。謂無學作意相應意根。此復云何。謂盡智。無生智。無學正見相應意根。云何非學非無學。謂有漏作意相應意根。

— are the mind-, pleasure-, joy-, and equanimity-faculties, together with the faith-faculty and the remaining four. Each of these must be answered by way of a threefold distinction, and hence they are said to "require distinction": one portion belonging to the trainee, one to the one beyond training, and one to neither. The Vibhajyavādins say that this passage ought to have made this very distinction, and it is for this reason that they, too, are called "the distinguishers." As for the mind-faculty: it may belong to the trainee, to the one beyond training, or to neither. What is meant by belonging to the trainee? The mind-faculty associated with a trainee's own attention. What is this, more precisely? The mind-faculty associated with attention ranging from suffering-dharma-patient-acceptance up through the vajra-like concentration. What is meant by belonging to the one beyond training? The mind-faculty associated with the attention of one beyond training. What is this, more precisely? The mind-faculty associated with the knowledge of exhaustion, the knowledge of non-arising, and the correct view of one beyond training. What is meant by belonging to neither? The mind-faculty associated with contaminated attention.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the mind-faculty alone requires a full threefold breakdown — trainee, beyond training, or neither, depending entirely on what kind of attention presently accompanies it — captures something important about mind's own peculiar flexibility among the faculties: unlike the physical senses, permanently fixed to whichever category their possessor happens to occupy, mind moves freely between all three classifications from one moment to the next.

2. The Mind-Faculty's Own Hidden Complexity

A remarkably intricate internal anatomy unfolds within the mind-faculty alone: wholesome mind divides into contaminated (itself split into preparatory, freedom-from-defilement, and birth-attained varieties, with preparatory further divided into hearing-and-reflection-based and cultivation-based) and uncontaminated (trainee or beyond-training); defiled mind divides into what is cut off by seeing or by cultivation; and neutral mind divides into the deportment-related, the artisan-related, the ripening-born, and the transformation-related — an entire miniature taxonomy nested inside what looked, from the outside, like a single simple faculty.

Chinese Source

此復云何。謂有三種。何等為三。謂善染污無覆無記。善復有三。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is this, more precisely? It is of three kinds. What are these three? Wholesome, defiled, and neutral-and-indeterminate. Wholesome, in turn, is of three kinds —

謂加行得。離染得。生得。加行得者。復有三種。謂聞所成。思所成。修所成。聞思所成者。謂不淨觀。持息念。念住等相應。修所成者。謂煗頂忍世第一法。現觀邊世俗智。靜慮無量無色解脫。勝處遍處等相應。離染得者。謂由離染時得。則靜慮無量無色解脫。

— attained through preparatory practice, attained through freedom from defilement, and attained by birth. Attained through preparatory practice is, further, of three kinds — attained through hearing, attained through reflection, and attained through cultivation. Attained through hearing or reflection refers to what is associated with the contemplation of impurity, mindfulness of breathing, the foundations of mindfulness, and the like. Attained through cultivation refers to what is associated with the stages of heat, summit, patient acceptance, and the supreme mundane dharma, the mundane knowledge at the very edge of direct realization, the dhyānas, the immeasurables, the formless attainments, the liberations, the stations of mastery, the totalities, and the like. Attained through freedom from defilement refers to what is obtained at the very moment of becoming free from defilement — namely, what is associated with the dhyānas, immeasurables, formless attainments, liberations, stations of mastery, totalities, and the like.

勝處遍處等相應。生得者。謂生時所得。善染污有二種。謂見所斷。修所斷。無覆無記有四。謂威儀路。工巧處。異熟生。及變化等。通果心如意根。樂根喜根捨根信根精進根念根定根慧根亦爾。

Attained by birth refers to what is obtained at the moment of birth itself. Wholesome and defiled are each of two kinds — cut off by seeing, and cut off by cultivation. Neutral-and-indeterminate is of four kinds — the deportment-related, the artisan-related, ripening-born, and transformation-produced. The mind associated with the penetrating supernormal power is likewise so. The pleasure-, joy-, and equanimity-faculties, and the faith-, diligence-, mindfulness-, concentration-, and wisdom-faculties, are all likewise so —

然有差別。謂信等五。除染無記餘隨所應。問何故此中明意根等。

— yet with this distinction: for the faith-faculty and the remaining four, exclude the defiled and neutral kind, and apply the rest as fitting. Question: why does this passage, in speaking of the mind-faculty and the rest, mention only what is associated with attention, and nothing else?

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Discovering an entire nested taxonomy hiding inside a single faculty already established many fascicles earlier is a reminder of how this tradition's own classificatory ambition never really stops: no category, however settled it may have seemed when first introduced, is ever treated as too basic to eventually receive its own further, internal subdivision.

3. Why Only Attention Gets Named

A pointed question asks why, among all the many mental factors that could accompany the mind-faculty, the treatise specifically names only its association with attention — answered through a cascade of explanations locating attention's own unique conducing power: exactly as, among the formations dissociated from mind, only the characteristic of arising itself is singled out as conducive to a dharma's own coming-to-be, attention alone, among mind's many companions, carries the specific mark of alerting and drawing the mind toward its object.

Chinese Source

唯說作意相應非餘。答彼作論者意欲爾故。乃至廣說。脇尊者曰。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: it is simply the intention of the treatise's own author, that this order be followed — and so on, in full. The Venerable Pārśva says:

此不應問一切有疑。說餘相應亦生疑故。但不違理隨說無過。有說。作意順生心勝故。謂相應法中順生心者唯作意勝。如不相應中順生法者唯有生相。是故偏說。有說。作意是警覺相。是牽引相。

this ought not to be asked at all, since every possible associated dharma equally invites the same doubt — so long as this does not contradict the proper principle, no fault follows from whatever is said. Others say: attention is superior in conducing to the arising of mind, since, among the dharmas associated with mind, only attention is superior in this specific conducing function, exactly as, among formations dissociated from mind, only the characteristic of arising itself is superior in conducing to a dharma's own coming-to-be — and hence attention alone is singled out for mention. Others say: attention carries the mark of alerting, and the mark of drawing forth,

令意根等取所緣勝。是故偏說。有說。作意能生諸法勝於餘法。是故偏說。

causing the mind-faculty and the rest to grasp their object in a superior way, and hence it alone is singled out for mention. Others say: attention is capable of generating every dharma in a way superior to every other dharma, and hence it alone is singled out for mention.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The parallel drawn between attention among mental factors and the mere characteristic of arising among dissociated formations is a small but elegant piece of structural reasoning: in both cases, an entire class of otherwise equal-seeming companions turns out to contain one member specifically responsible for setting the whole process into motion, and it is this initiating role, rather than any greater intrinsic importance, that earns it special mention.

4. Does a Trainee's Faculty Always Belong to a Trainee? A Four-Cornered Answer

An intricate tetralemma investigates whether a faculty classified as belonging to "the trainee" in general is always actually possessed by any given trainee-person — the answer decisively no, since one following faith does not possess the specific faculties belonging to one following the Dharma, one abiding at an early stage of the path does not possess the faculties of a later stage, and so on through an exhaustive catalog of trainee sub-types, each possessing only its own particular slice of the trainee's full faculty-repertoire.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That even within the single broad category "trainee," no individual practitioner actually possesses every faculty that category as a whole comprises is a valuable corrective to treating any of this tradition's classifications as describing a single uniform individual: a general category can be real and useful even when no single member of it ever instantiates the category in its complete, generic form.

5. The Same Puzzle for the One Beyond Training

The identical four-cornered analysis is now run for the one beyond training, with the very same result: the fixed-time-liberated arhat does not possess the faculties specific to the any-time-liberated arhat, the Buddha does not possess the faculties specific to the śrāvaka or solitary-realized buddha, and so on — confirming that "beyond training," no less than "trainee," names a broad category no single individual ever fully embodies in every one of its possible variations at once.

10. Reopening an Old Debate: Does Karma Really Ripen?

The treatise turns to a foundational metaphysical dispute about karmic ripening itself, refuting three separate rival positions in turn: the Dṛṣṭāntikas, who hold that only volition can be a genuine ripening-cause and only feeling a genuine ripening-result, rather than all five aggregates alike; the Kāśyapīya school, who hold that a ripening-cause's own substance is entirely destroyed the instant its result finally arises, exactly as a seed vanishes once its sprout has grown; and certain outside-path teachers who deny that good and evil karma bring about any ripening-result whatsoever.

The Kāśyapīya image of a seed vanishing the instant its sprout appears is a genuinely intuitive picture of causation that this treatise nonetheless firmly rejects: insisting that a cause remains genuinely present even after its result has arisen is this tradition's own quiet refusal to let causation be modeled as a simple transfer or exchange, in which the cause's own reality is spent and used up in producing what follows from it.

11. Only Displeasure Ripens on Its Own — Tracing the Rest

A precise census determines exactly which of the twenty-two faculties carry a genuine ripening-result: only the displeasure-faculty does so unconditionally, eleven faculties never do, and the remaining ten — including the mind-faculty itself — depend entirely on whether the specific instance in question happens to be contaminated or uncontaminated, wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral, at that particular moment.

12. Ripening as Becoming Something Else

A compact etymology settles why karmic causation is called "ripening" at all: it ripens as something of a genuinely different kind from its own cause, distinguished from the more familiar "same-class" continuation where a wholesome thought simply gives rise to a further wholesome thought of its own kind. This same section then reopens the treatise's recurring defense of the mundane path's own genuine power to cut off affliction, citing the outside-path teacher Udraka Rāmaputra's own well-documented progress through the formless attainments as concrete proof that ordinary, unenlightened effort really can permanently sever an underlying tendency, not merely suppress its visible manifestation.

14. Seeing and Cultivation: Never Fully Separate, Yet Named for What Dominates

A subtle closing puzzle asks how "cut off by seeing" and "cut off by cultivation" can be cleanly distinguished at all, given that genuine cultivation (non-heedlessness) is present within the path of seeing, and genuine seeing (wisdom) is present within the path of cultivation. The resolution, drawn from the Venerable Vasumitra, names each path for whichever quality dominates within it — wisdom abundant and non-heedlessness scarce within the sharp, swift path of seeing, and the reverse within the patient, repeated path of cultivation — memorably compared to a sharp blade severing an object in a single stroke against a dull one requiring many repeated cuts to accomplish the very same task.

15. Counting Aspects: Two, Three, Four, or Five Paths to the Same Cutting-Off
