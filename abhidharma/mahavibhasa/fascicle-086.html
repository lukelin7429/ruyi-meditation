How the Latent Tendencies Increase

Fascicle 86 opens an entirely new topic — a systematic analysis of how the ninety-eight latent tendencies increase with respect to the faculties, elements, sense-fields, truths, dhyānas, and every named list of fetters, floods, and hindrances, closing with the full desire-realm breakdown. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 86 continues the Ten Gates Nyāsa (結蘊第二中十門納息第四之十六), opening a new topic: for each of the ninety-eight latent tendencies (九十八隨眠), how many increase with respect to each faculty, element, sense-field, truth, aggregate, and fetter, distinguished throughout by the bond of an object (所緣縛) and the bond of association (相應縛). Terms established in earlier fascicles recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Why This Treatise Is Composed

A new nyāsa opens with a sweeping question: of the ninety-eight latent tendencies, how many increase with respect to each dharma, from the eye faculty all the way to the formless realm's own latent tendency of ignorance abandoned by cultivation? Four reasons are given for composing so vast an undertaking — to display the meaning of the ten gateways already established, to refute the view that an arhat's own faculties are somehow undefiled, to refute the view that afflictions are baseless distortions with no genuinely real object, and to refute the denial that any real bond through an object or through association exists at all. A further, more pastoral motive closes the list: to prevent a practitioner who has merely suppressed his afflictions for a time from mistaking that temporary calm for genuine liberation, by showing him plainly that the latent tendencies still increase with respect to his own eye and the rest.

Ru-Yi Translation

The eye faculty, up through the formless realm's own latent tendency of ignorance abandoned by cultivation — among the ninety-eight latent tendencies, how many latent tendencies increase with respect to each of these, one by one? One asks: for what reason is this treatise composed? The answer: it is in order to display the meaning of the very gateway relied upon in the preceding chapter — that is, in reliance upon the forty-two chapters already stated, the meaning of the ten gateways, such as the latent tendencies and the rest, has already been displayed. Further, it is in order to put a stop to what other schools maintain, and to display the correct principle — that is, there are those who hold that the eye faculty and the like, present even within the body of the non-learner, are themselves undefiled, and, in order to put a stop to this holding, it is displayed that the eye faculty and the like are solely contaminated, since they are what the various latent tendencies increase with respect to. There are, further, those who hold that afflictions are distortions possessing no genuinely real object, and, in order to put a stop to this holding, it is displayed that every affliction does indeed possess a genuinely real object.

There are, further, those who hold that there exists no such thing as the meaning of a bond through an object, or a bond through association — for if there were a meaning of bondage through an object, then taking an undefiled dharma as one's object ought likewise to constitute a meaning of bondage; and if there were a meaning of bondage through association, then, once a given affliction had already been cut off, its own former association ought likewise still to constitute a bond. In order to put a stop to this holding, it is displayed that there does indeed exist a meaning of bondage through an object and through association — as for bondage through an object: this refers solely to the fact that, when a contaminated latent tendency takes something as its object, it must certainly increase with respect to it, while, although it may take an undefiled dharma as its object, it does not thereby increase with respect to it, and it is for this reason that this does not constitute a meaning of bondage. As for bondage through association: this requires that the very affliction one is associated with has not yet been cut off — once that affliction has already been cut off, although a former association did once exist, no meaning of bondage remains. Further, it is in order to put a stop to the fact that, despite genuinely being bound, one might give rise to an overweening arrogance with respect to one's own person, supposing oneself already liberated — that is, one who has merely, for the time being, suppressed his own afflictions might still declare of himself that he has already attained liberation, and it is in order to cause him to know that, with respect to his own eye faculty and the rest, the latent tendencies of craving and the like still increase, that this is stated. Further, it is in order to analyze at length the meaning of a certain scripture — that is, within a certain scripture, it is stated that, with respect to the eye faculty and the rest, the latent tendencies of craving and the like increase, and, although this much is stated, it is not analyzed at any length, and, wishing now to analyze it, this treatise is composed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The pastoral reason tucked in among these more technical refutations is easy to overlook, yet it may be the entire treatise's own quiet ethical center: a doctrine this exhaustively technical, cataloguing which of ninety-eight latent tendencies increases with respect to which dharma across every realm and ground, ultimately exists to keep a sincere practitioner from the specific, ordinary danger of mistaking a temporary lull in affliction for the real thing — the whole apparatus of bondage and increase is, in the end, aimed at protecting someone from overestimating himself.

2. The Five Categories, and Two Refinements of the Count

The basic framework is laid down: dharma sorts into five categories — abandoned by seeing suffering, accumulation, cessation, and the path, and abandoned by cultivation — and it is the latent tendencies of these same five categories that increase with respect to it, suffering's and accumulation's own categories each borrowing the other's universally pervasive latent tendency, cessation's and the path's each borrowing every universally pervasive latent tendency outright. This coarse five-fold count is then refined twice over: first into ten kinds of dharma answering to nine kinds of latent tendency (splitting each category into associated and dissociated, or defiled and undefiled, and splitting the universally pervasive latent tendencies into pervasive and non-pervasive), and then, more finely still, into forty-one kinds of dharma answering to thirty-six kinds of latent tendency, reckoned by category alone rather than by realm.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The steady narrowing from five categories, to nine kinds of latent tendency, to thirty-six — each pass keeping the same five-fold skeleton but subdividing it further along a new axis (associated/dissociated, pervasive/non-pervasive, contaminated-object/undefiled-object) — is itself a small lesson in how this literature builds precision: rather than proposing a wholly new classification at each stage, it simply asks the same five categories a slightly sharper question each time, until the count is fine enough to specify, for every single kind of dharma, exactly which bond (of an object, of association, or both) attaches it to exactly which latent tendencies.

3. Object-Bondage and Association-Bondage, Worked Through Category by Category

The forty-one-by-thirty-six framework is now worked through in full, category by category and, within each category, dharma by dharma — the view of self, the extreme view, and the remaining views and conceit abandoned by seeing suffering; the ignorance not shared with others; the dissociated dharma of each category; the wrong view, doubt, and view-grasping abandoned by seeing cessation; craving, aversion, and conceit abandoned by cultivation. A single governing principle recurs throughout: a dharma's own associated latent tendency binds it by both the bond of an object and the bond of association, while every other latent tendency of its own category, and the universally pervasive latent tendency of the adjacent category, binds it solely by the bond of an object.

5. Pleasure, Joy, Sorrow, and Why No Undefiled Dharma Is Ever Increased

The pleasure-, joy-, and sorrow-faculties receive individually careful treatment, since each spans several grounds with subtly different rules at each — the joy-faculty's own case is especially intricate, since, although it never arises associated with aversion or doubt (their mournful aspect being incompatible with joy's own delightful one), it is nonetheless still bound by every remaining latent tendency of its own realm through the bond of an object alone, even those it is never actually associated with. The three undefiled faculties, by contrast, admit of no increase whatsoever, and four converging reasons are given for why no undefiled dharma ever is: it is not the matter of a view of self, a distortion, or a latent tendency; the view of self does not grasp it as a self; it is not, itself, a matter of the latent tendencies' own increase, only of their taking-as-object; and it is not a place where such increase occurs — the whole principle summarized in the memorable image that latent tendencies cling to a dharma exactly as dust and grime cling easily to what is moist and oily, while the undefiled, possessing no such moisture, offers them nothing to adhere to.

6. Four Masters on Why the Undefiled Offers No Foothold

Four teachers each offer their own simile for this same principle, and their differences are as instructive as their agreement. The Venerable Ghoṣa compares it to a mighty king's own awesome countenance, before which a caṇḍāla's own child trembles in fear rather than approaching. 11. The Ninety-Eight Latent Tendencies, Realm by Realm

The fascicle closes by returning to its own opening question and answering it explicitly, realm by realm and category by category, for the desire realm's own ninety-eight latent tendencies: the ten latent tendencies abandoned by seeing suffering, and the seven abandoned by seeing accumulation, are each increased by the full set proper to their own category; the seven abandoned by seeing cessation and the eight abandoned by seeing the path, excepting in each case the undefiled-object ignorance not shared with others, are increased by every remaining latent tendency together with the universally pervasive ones; and the four abandoned by cultivation are increased by their own category together with the universally pervasive latent tendencies.
