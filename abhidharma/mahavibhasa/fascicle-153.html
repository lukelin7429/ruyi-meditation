Two Monks, a Famous Dispute, and a Celebrated Catechism

Fascicle 153 traces cessation-attainment through Śāriputra and Udāyin's famous dispute over the mind-made-body gods, two monks who died waiting to emerge from a brief vow, the scholastic debate over when a Bodhisattva attains it, and the nun Dharmadinnā's celebrated catechism with the layman Viśākha. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Perception, Subtle, and Very Subtle: Three Grades Within One Ground

Fascicle 153 opens by refining the language used to describe entering cessation-attainment, sorting the four formless spheres into "perception," "subtle," and "very subtle" mind, before asking what actually distinguishes these three when all three sometimes describe the very same ground — resolved through several answers, from a mere difference in name to genuinely different capacities for future cultivation of the path.

Chinese Source

問此中何者是想。何者是微細。何者是微微。有說。空無邊處識無邊處是想。無所有處是微細。非想非非想處是微微。有說。空無邊處識無邊處無所有處是想。非想非非想處是微細。若心心所法為等無間緣。入滅盡定是微微。有說。非想非非想處亦是想。亦是微細。亦是微微。所以者何。非想非非想處。有上中下。上者是想。

Ru-Yi Translation

One might ask: within this discussion, what exactly is meant by "perception," what by "subtle," and what by "very subtle"? One position holds that the spheres of infinite space and infinite consciousness are "perception"; the sphere of nothing-at-all is "subtle"; and the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is "very subtle." Another position holds that the spheres of infinite space, infinite consciousness, and nothing-at-all are all "perception," while the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is "subtle," and mind and mental factors serving as the immediately preceding condition for entering the attainment of cessation constitute the "very subtle." Still another position holds that the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is itself all three at once — "perception," "subtle," and "very subtle" together. Why is this so? Because that sphere itself admits of superior, middling, and inferior grades — its superior grade is "perception,"

中者是微細。下者是微微。問若俱一地有何差別。有說名則差別。

its middling grade is "subtle," and its inferior grade is "very subtle." One might ask: if these three all belong to one and the same ground, what distinguishes them?

謂此名為想。此名微細。此名微微。有說。品亦差別。謂上者名想。中者是微細。下者是微微。有說。想微細現在前時能修未來聖道。微微現在前時不修。有說。想微細現在前時四念住隨一現在修。未來四。微微現在前時。法念住現在修。未來三。除身念住。

One position holds that they differ merely in name — this one is called "perception," this one "subtle," and this one "very subtle." Another position holds that they differ in grade as well — the superior grade is called "perception," the middling grade "subtle," and the inferior grade "very subtle." Another position holds that when "perception" or "subtle" mind becomes present, one is able to cultivate a noble path for the future, whereas when "very subtle" mind becomes present, one does not cultivate any such path. Another position holds that when "perception" or "subtle" mind becomes present, one of the four foundations of mindfulness is currently cultivated and all four are cultivated for the future; when "very subtle" mind becomes present, only the foundation of mindfulness with respect to dharmas is currently cultivated, and only three are cultivated for the future, excluding the foundation of mindfulness with respect to the body.

有說。想微細有曾得。有未曾得。微微唯未曾得。

Another position holds that "perception" and "subtle" mind may each have been previously obtained or never yet obtained, whereas "very subtle" mind has only ever been never yet obtained.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the very same sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception can, on one reading, simultaneously be "perception," "subtle," and "very subtle" all at once, sorted only by its own superior, middling, and inferior grades, is a reminder of how much this tradition treats even its most exalted meditative territory as internally graded rather than uniform: reaching a named attainment is never quite the end of the story, since the attainment itself still admits of finer shades within.

2. Why the Mind Entering Cessation Is Always Contaminated, the Mind Emerging Sometimes Not

A cluster of precise distinctions follows: the mind of entry may be either previously obtained or entirely new, while the mind of emergence is always previously obtained, since entry demands considerable effort while emergence is its natural opposite; and the mind of entry is always contaminated, weak and unstable like a rotten seed uniquely suited to cutting off mind itself, while the mind of emergence may be either contaminated or uncontaminated.

Chinese Source

問此中因論生論何故入滅定心。通曾得未曾得。出滅定心唯曾得耶。答入定心多加行用功力。極作意起故。通曾得未曾得。出定心與上相違故唯曾得。有說。入定心能策勵增益發起於定故。通曾得未曾得。出定心與此相違故唯曾得。有說。入定心唯有漏故。通曾得未曾得。出定心通有漏無漏故唯曾得。問此中因論生論何故入定心唯有漏。出定心通有漏無漏。答入定心隨順心斷故唯有漏。由此心不堅不強。無勢力久住。不令餘心無間而起。如朽敗種故於心斷極為隨順。出定心與此相違。故通有漏無漏。有說。入定心以定為寂靜非無漏道。以有為寂靜故唯有漏。有說。以此定是次第定。從非想非非想地心無間而起。聖道極至無所有處故。入定心唯是有漏。

Ru-Yi Translation

One might ask, since one question naturally gives rise to another: why is the mind entering the attainment of cessation sometimes previously obtained and sometimes never yet obtained, while the mind of emergence is only ever previously obtained? Answer: the mind of entry depends on considerable preparatory effort and exertion, arising through the power of an intensely focused attention, and so it may be either previously obtained or never yet obtained; the mind of emergence, being the very opposite of this, is only ever previously obtained. Another position holds that the mind of entry has the capacity to spur onward, reinforce, and actually give rise to the attainment itself, and so it may be either previously obtained or never yet obtained; the mind of emergence, being the very opposite of this, is only ever previously obtained. Another position holds that the mind of entry is only ever contaminated, and so it may be either previously obtained or never yet obtained; the mind of emergence, being either contaminated or uncontaminated, is only ever previously obtained. One might ask, since one question naturally gives rise to another: why is the mind of entry only ever contaminated, while the mind of emergence may be either contaminated or uncontaminated? Answer: the mind of entry is conducive to the very cutting-off of mind itself, and so it is only ever contaminated — being neither firm nor forceful, lacking the power to abide for any length of time, and never itself giving rise to any subsequent mind without interruption, exactly like a rotten seed, it is thus exceptionally conducive to the cutting-off of mind. The mind of emergence, being the very opposite of this, may be either contaminated or uncontaminated. Another position holds that the mind of entry takes the attainment itself as calm and still, yet is not itself an uncontaminated path, since it takes what is conditioned as calm and still, and so it is only ever contaminated. Another position holds that this attainment is a sequential attainment, arising immediately after the mind of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception without interruption, and, since the noble path reaches at most only as far as the sphere of nothing-at-all, the mind of entry is therefore only ever contaminated.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The rotten-seed image for the mind of entry is a vivid piece of psychological description: a mental state has to be genuinely fragile and incapable of propagating itself in order to successfully extinguish an entire mental continuum, and comparing it to a seed too spent to germinate captures exactly the kind of deliberate self-weakening this transition requires.

3. What Does the Mind of Entry or Emergence Actually Take as Its Object?

A subtle puzzle opens: both the mind of entering and the mind of emerging from cessation take the attainment itself as their object — so why does entering never simply become emerging, and emerging never simply become entering? The answer distinguishes intention (wishing to enter versus wishing to emerge) and temporal object (the future attainment versus the past one), before a careful refusal to specify exactly which future or past instant is meant, since those instants exist in an undifferentiated jumble with no fixed sequence among them.

Chinese Source

問入滅定心為何所緣。出滅定心為何所緣。答入定心緣定。出心亦爾。問若入定心緣定。出心亦爾者云何不入時則出。出時則入耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

One might ask: what does the mind of entering the attainment of cessation take as its object? And what does the mind of emergence take as its object? Answer: the mind of entry takes the attainment itself as its object; the mind of emergence does likewise. One might then ask: if the mind of entry takes the attainment as its object, and the mind of emergence does likewise, why does entering not immediately mean emerging, and emerging not immediately mean entering?

答入定時期心欲入。出定時期心欲出。由期心故無有錯亂。又入定心緣未來定。出定心緣過去定。由所緣故亦無錯亂。問若入定心緣未來定者。為緣幾許未來定耶。有說。但緣初剎那定。有說。通緣應相續起者。評曰應說此入定心。緣未來定。而不可說緣何剎那。

Answer: the mind of entry anticipates and wishes to enter; the mind of emergence anticipates and wishes to emerge — and it is through this very anticipation that no confusion between the two ever arises. Moreover, the mind of entry takes the future attainment as its object; the mind of emergence takes the past attainment as its object — and it is through this difference of object, too, that no confusion arises. One might ask: if the mind of entry takes the future attainment as its object, precisely how much of that future attainment does it take as its object? One position holds that it takes only the very first instant of the attainment as its object; another position holds that it takes as its object however many instants will actually go on to arise in succession. As the assessors conclude, it should be said that this mind of entry takes the future attainment as its object, yet it cannot be specified which particular instant it does or does not take as its object,

不緣何剎那。以未來定有多剎那未有先後。雜亂住故。問若出定心緣過去定。為緣幾許過去定耶。答有說。但緣最後剎那定。有說。

since the future attainment comprises many instants, none of which yet stand in any settled order before or after one another, existing instead in an undifferentiated, jumbled state. One might ask: if the mind of emergence takes the past attainment as its object, precisely how much of that past attainment does it take as its object? One position holds that it takes only the very last instant of the attainment as its object; another position holds that

通緣曾相續起者評曰。應說此出定心緣過去定。而不可說緣何剎那。不緣何剎那。以過去定有多剎那相雜住故。

it takes as its object however many instants actually did arise in succession. As the assessors conclude, it should be said that this mind of emergence takes the past attainment as its object, yet it cannot be specified which particular instant it does or does not take as its object, since the past attainment comprises many instants existing together in a jumbled, intermixed state.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The refusal to specify which particular instant of a future or past attainment is taken as object — insisting only that some instant is, without saying which — is a notably disciplined piece of philosophical restraint: rather than manufacturing a false precision the underlying metaphysics cannot actually support, the treatise simply admits the limits of what can meaningfully be said here.

4. The City-Gate Simile: How Can You 'Extinguish' What Hasn't Happened Yet?

A genuinely sharp metaphysical puzzle opens: since the past has already ceased, the future hasn't arrived, and the present never abides even an instant, in what sense can cessation-attainment be said to "extinguish" mind and mental factors at all? The resolution turns on the vivid image of a besieged city — blocking every road, shutting every gate, and raising banners so no one may pass — used to explain how the attainment prevents future mind and mental factors from ever continuing, without needing to "destroy" something that doesn't yet exist.

Chinese Source

問入滅盡定時。滅何等心心所法。為過去為未來。為現在。若過去者過去已滅。復何所滅。若未來者未來未至。云何可滅。若現在者現在不住。復云何滅。設非定力亦自滅故。答應作是說。滅於未來。問未來未至云何可滅。答住現在世。遮於未來心心所法。令不相續故說為滅。如斷城路閉門。竪幢不令人入出。說名除寇。此亦如是。有說。通滅未來現在。問現必不住。復云何滅。設非定力亦自滅故。答先現在世心心所法。令有緣法續起而滅。今現在世心心所法。不令有緣法續起而滅。此由誰力。所謂定滅。問出滅定時。

Ru-Yi Translation

One might ask: upon entering the attainment of cessation, exactly which mind and mental factors are extinguished — those of the past, the future, or the present? If those of the past, the past has already ceased — what further extinguishing could there be? If those of the future, the future has not yet arrived — how could it possibly be extinguished? And if those of the present, the present never abides even for an instant — how, then, could it be extinguished, since it would cease on its own even without the attainment's own power? Answer: it should be explained that it is the future that is extinguished. One might then ask: the future has not yet arrived — how could it possibly be extinguished? Answer: abiding within the present, one blocks the mind and mental factors of the future, preventing their continuity — and it is on this basis that this is called "extinguishing," exactly as blocking the roads into a city, shutting its gates, and raising its banners so that no one may enter or leave is called "clearing out the bandits" — the same holds true here. Another position holds that both the future and the present are extinguished together. One might then ask: the present never abides even for an instant — how, then, could it be extinguished? Answer: formerly, present mind and mental factors would cease only after first giving rise to some further, connected dharma in succession; now, this present mind and these mental factors cease without giving rise to any such connected dharma in succession at all — and by whose power does this occur? By the very power of the attainment's own extinguishing. One might ask: upon emerging from the attainment of cessation,

何等心心所法現在前耶。有作是說。先所遮止住未來世心心所法。

exactly which mind and mental factors become present? One position holds that whatever mind and mental factors of the future had previously been blocked and held back now become present.

今現在前。有餘師說。餘未來世心心所法今現在前。先所遮止住不生法不可復起。評曰。應說起未來世心心所法。而不可說起何等。

Other masters hold instead that other, different mind and mental factors of the future now become present, since whatever had previously been blocked and held back from arising can never arise again afterward. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The besieged-city image elegantly dissolves what looks like a genuine paradox: "extinguishing the future" sounds nonsensical taken literally, since nothing there yet exists to extinguish, but reframed as "blocking the road so nothing further can arrive," the very same claim becomes not just coherent but almost mundane, like a checkpoint rather than a demolition.

5. Where Can Cessation-Attainment First Arise? Only in the Desire Realm

A settled position emerges: cessation-attainment can be neither possessed as past nor cultivated as future, exactly like the divine eye, and — crucially — it can first be given rise to only within the desire realm, since only there can a Buddha's own teaching actually be heard; only after first attaining it there can the lingering power of habituation later bring it forth again following rebirth in the form realm.

Chinese Source

不起何等。以未來世有多剎那。未有先後雜亂住故。問滅盡定有得過去修未來耶。有說。此定無得過去及修未來。如天眼耳。若作是說。無得過去修未來者。彼說定初剎那唯成就現在定。餘剎那成就過去現在。出此定已唯成就過去。有說。此定有得過去及修未來。

Ru-Yi Translation

since the future comprises many instants existing in an undifferentiated, jumbled state, none of which yet stand in any settled order before or after one another. One might ask: does this attainment of cessation admit of past possession or future cultivation? One position holds that this attainment has neither past possession nor future cultivation, exactly like the divine eye and the divine ear. If this position is accepted, then only the very first instant of the attainment is possessed as present; every subsequent instant is possessed as both past and present; and once one has emerged from the attainment, only the past remains possessed. Another position holds that this attainment does admit of both past possession and future cultivation, exactly like the knowledge of others' minds and the knowledge of recollecting past abodes.

如他心智宿住智等。若作是說。有得過去未來修者。彼說定初剎那成就未來現在定。餘剎那成就三世。出此定已成就過去未來。如是說者。應如初說。若謂此定有得過去修未來者。退此定已後還起時。應說還得先所得者。而實此定退復起時。名得未曾得定。如不犯重而還家者。後更出家名得未曾得戒。彼亦如是。問此滅定何處起。答在欲色界非無色界。若初起唯欲界。若此起已於此定退命終生色界中。由串習力復能現起。餘者不能。云何知然。如契經說。

If this position is accepted, then the very first instant of the attainment is possessed as both future and present; every subsequent instant is possessed across all three times; and once one has emerged from the attainment, both past and future remain possessed. As the assessors conclude, it should be said, as in the first position, that this attainment has neither past possession nor future cultivation — for if one held that this attainment did admit of both, then, upon regressing from it and later giving rise to it once more, one ought properly to say that one has thereby regained what was previously obtained; yet in fact, when this attainment regresses and is later given rise to once more, this is called obtaining an attainment never before obtained — exactly as one who, without ever having committed a grave transgression, returns to lay life and later goes forth again, is said to obtain a moral discipline never before obtained; the same holds true here. One might ask: where can this attainment of cessation first arise? Answer: within the desire and form realms, never within the formless realm. If it is first given rise to at all, it is given rise to only within the desire realm; once given rise to there, should one regress from it and, at the end of that life, be reborn into the form realm, the lingering power of that earlier habituation can still bring it forth once more there — but this is not possible for any other being. Locating the very first possible arising of the highest meditative attainment in this entire cosmology specifically in the desire realm — the realm most associated with craving and distraction, not refinement — is a striking inversion: what makes the desire realm special here is not its subtlety but simply that it is the only place a Buddha's own liberating words can actually be spoken and heard.

6. Śāriputra and Udāyin Clash Over the Mind-Made-Body Gods

A famous canonical episode unfolds: Śāriputra teaches that a monk complete in discipline, concentration, and wisdom might, after death, be reborn among the "mind-made-body" gods and there continue entering and emerging from cessation-attainment — and Udāyin flatly contradicts him, three times over, convinced this is impossible since anyone capable of the attainment must already be bound for the formless realm, where the attainment can never again be given rise to at all.

Chinese Source

尊者舍利子告苾芻眾言。若苾芻戒定慧具足者。能數數入出滅受想定。彼於現法及將死時。若不能辨如來聖旨。命終超段食天處。生在意成身天中。於彼復能數數入出滅想受定。斯有是處應如實知。

Ru-Yi Translation

the Venerable Śāriputra addressed the assembly of monks, saying: "if a monk is complete in moral discipline, concentration, and wisdom, he is capable of repeatedly entering and emerging from the attainment of the cessation of feeling and perception. Whether or not he is able, in this very life or at the moment of death, to fully realize the Tathāgata's own noble intention, it is entirely possible that, at the end of this life, transcending the heavens of physical morsel-food, he will be reborn among the mind-made-body gods, and there, too, will be capable of repeatedly entering and emerging from this same attainment of the cessation of feeling and perception" — one should know this to be genuinely possible.

時具壽鄔陀夷。在彼會坐。語尊者舍利子言。彼苾芻生意成身天。

At that time, the Venerable Udāyin, who was present in that same assembly, said to the Venerable Śāriputra: "such a monk, reborn among the mind-made-body gods,

能數入出滅想受定無有是處第二第三亦如是說。問何故具壽鄔陀夷再三違逆尊者舍利子。答彼之所疑非無處所。彼作是念。得此定者必已離無所有處染。命終應生非想非非想處。於彼必無起此定理。

could never possibly be capable of repeatedly entering and emerging from the attainment of the cessation of feeling and perception — there is no such possibility at all." This same objection was repeated a second and a third time. One might ask: why did the Venerable Udāyin so repeatedly and stubbornly contradict the Venerable Śāriputra? Answer: his own doubt was not without some basis, for he reasoned to himself: "whoever has already obtained this attainment must already have become free from the contamination of the sphere of nothing-at-all, and, at the end of that life, ought properly to be reborn into the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, where it is simply impossible ever to give rise to this attainment again."

又彼不了舍利子意。是故現前再三違逆。問舍利子有何意趣。彼具壽云何不了。答舍利子說生色界者。鄔陀夷說生無色界者。舍利子說退者。鄔陀夷說不退者。由此不了故三違之。問尊者何故不開悟彼。而致重違逆耶。答尊者念言。誰能開悟如是愚執自是者耶。有說。尊者念欲開悟。由再三違逆故彼意便止。如箭喻經說。有眾多增上慢苾芻。於佛前各自讚美。我生已盡。乃至廣說。佛時欲為說斷慢法。由諸苾芻自讚不止故彼心便息。世尊具足普緣大悲。尚於慢人說法心息。何況尊者舍利子耶。有說。尊者作如是念。此所論事必聞於佛。佛當以此呵鄔陀夷及阿難陀。當使此誡經歷千載。令無智者不敢違於智人所說。尊者復念。如是苾芻於大眾中再三違我竟。無同梵行者隨喜我之所說。今應詣佛決判此事。念已則時往至佛所。頂禮雙足退坐一面。告苾芻眾若苾芻戒定慧具。乃至廣說。

Moreover, he had failed to grasp the Venerable Śāriputra's own true intention, and it was for this reason that he openly contradicted him, again and again, three times over. One might ask: what, exactly, was the Venerable Śāriputra's own true intention, and why did the Venerable Udāyin fail to grasp it? Answer: Śāriputra was speaking of rebirth into the form realm; Udāyin understood him to mean rebirth into the formless realm. Śāriputra was speaking of one who has regressed from the attainment; Udāyin understood him to mean one who has not regressed. It was through this failure of mutual understanding that the threefold contradiction arose. One might ask: why did the Venerable Śāriputra not simply enlighten him directly, rather than allowing such a persistent and repeated contradiction to stand? Answer: the Venerable Śāriputra reflected: "who could possibly enlighten one so foolishly convinced of his own rightness?" Another position holds that the Venerable Śāriputra did indeed wish to enlighten him, but, seeing the objection repeated three times over, simply let the matter rest — exactly as in the Discourse on the Simile of the Arrow, where a great many overly confident monks each boasted before the Buddha, saying, "my own birth is already exhausted," and so on, as stated at length, and the Buddha, though at that time wishing to teach the dharma of overcoming conceit, likewise let the matter rest once the monks' own boasting would not cease — for if the World-Honored One himself, endowed with universal, all-encompassing great compassion, could still let a matter rest even in the case of the conceited, how much more so the Venerable Śāriputra? Another position holds that the Venerable Śāriputra reflected: "this very matter now under discussion will surely reach the Buddha's own ears, and the Buddha will then rebuke Udāyin and Ānanda over it, so that this admonition may endure for a thousand generations, ensuring that the ignorant never again dare to contradict what the wise have said." The Venerable Śāriputra further reflected: "this monk has now openly contradicted me three times before this entire assembly, and not a single one of my fellow practitioners of the holy life has come forward to affirm what I have said — I ought now to go before the Buddha himself and have this matter properly settled." Having reflected thus, he at once went to where the Buddha was, bowed his head at the Buddha's own two feet, withdrew, and sat to one side, and addressed the assembly of monks: "if a monk is complete in moral discipline, concentration, and wisdom," and so on, exactly as stated at length before.

8. Why the Formless Realm Can Never Host This Attainment, and Who Can Be Reborn There After Regressing

The episode's resolution confirms Śāriputra's own position and opens a wider explanation: dhyānas and formless attainments can be freshly given rise to upon rebirth through the power of prior cause, karma, or the sheer natural law governing cosmic destruction, but cessation-attainment can only ever be first given rise to through hearing a Buddha's own teaching — and it can never arise at all for a being reborn in the formless realm, since a mindless attainment there would leave the life-faculty with no form and no mind to depend on whatsoever, indistinguishable from outright death. A careful clarification addresses an apparent overreach: the sūtra states that relying on cessation-attainment prevents future rebirth's suffering, yet this attainment cannot itself actually cut off any affliction — resolved by explaining that a trainee or one beyond training, emerging from this brief taste of total stillness, is inspired by its very subtlety to reflect on how much more profound nirvāṇa itself must be, and it is this reflection, not the attainment's own power, that actually drives the cutting-off of every remaining affliction.

如說。由得依住此滅定故不受當來生老死苦。不起彼集。問滅定不能斷諸煩惱。云何得有如是說耶。答應觀此中所說意趣。謂諸有學出滅定已作是思惟。此滅定中心心所法。暫滅暫息須臾不行。尚有如是寂靜微妙。何況涅槃。有為諸行永滅永息究竟不行。由此為先斷餘煩惱。滅相續蘊入無餘依涅槃界。若諸無學出滅定已作是思惟。廣說乃至由此為先滅相續蘊入無餘依涅槃界依此密意故作是說。非謂滅定能斷煩惱。

Ru-Yi Translation

As it is said: "it is by relying on and abiding in this attainment of cessation that one no longer receives the future suffering of birth, old age, and death, and no longer gives rise to their accumulation." One might ask: this attainment of cessation cannot itself actually cut off any affliction — how, then, can such a statement be made? Answer: one should examine the true intention behind this statement. A trainee, upon emerging from the attainment of cessation, reflects as follows: "within this attainment of cessation, mind and mental factors are extinguished, stilled, and altogether inoperative even for the briefest instant, and even this already carries such calm, subtle stillness — how much more so nirvāṇa itself, where every conditioned formation is permanently extinguished, permanently stilled, and never again operative at all!" and it is on the strength of this very reflection that he goes on to cut off every remaining affliction, extinguish the aggregates of his own continuity, and enter the realm of nirvāṇa without remainder. One beyond training, likewise emerging from the attainment of cessation, reflects in just the same way, and so on, as stated at length, going on to extinguish the aggregates of his own continuity and enter the realm of nirvāṇa without remainder on the strength of this same reflection. It is with this hidden intention in mind that this statement is made — it does not mean that the attainment of cessation is itself actually capable of cutting off any affliction.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The distinction drawn here — between an experience's own causal power and the reflection it merely inspires — is a valuable piece of conceptual hygiene applicable well beyond this specific case: cessation-attainment functions less like a tool that does the liberating work itself and more like a preview that makes the practitioner want to finish the job through entirely separate means.

18. Four Combinations of Vowing to Enter and Vowing to Exit, and Why Only Four Formless Spheres Are Named

The fascicle closes with the Prajñaptiśāstra's own fourfold combinatorics of vowing — some vow only to enter, some only to exit, some both, and some neither — resolved by distinguishing genuine mastery over the mind of entry from mastery over the mind of emergence, before a final technical note explaining why, upon emergence, the canonical formula mentions only the four formless attainments: these alone, among all the meditative grounds, permit either a "reverse sequential" or a "reverse leaping" exit, skipping over intervening spheres entirely.

Chinese Source

施設論說。有作願入滅定。不作願出。有作願出滅定。不作願入。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Prajñaptiśāstra states: there is one who vows to enter the attainment of cessation but does not vow to emerge from it; there is one who vows to emerge from the attainment of cessation but does not vow to enter it;

有作願入滅定亦作願出。有不作願入滅定不作願出。此中作願入滅定不作願出者。謂如有一作是願言。我當入滅定。不作是願。我當出滅定。四有想定隨一現前。彼入滅定從滅定出。四有想定隨一現前。作願出滅定。不作願入者。謂如有一不作願言。我當入滅定。

there is one who vows both to enter and to emerge; and there is one who vows neither to enter nor to emerge. Here, "vowing to enter the attainment of cessation but not vowing to emerge" refers to a case such as one who makes precisely this vow: "I shall now enter the attainment of cessation," without also making the vow, "I shall now emerge from the attainment of cessation" — whichever one of the four attainments possessing perception happens to become present, he enters the attainment of cessation and then, from the attainment of cessation, emerges, whichever one of the four attainments possessing perception happens to become present. "Vowing to emerge from the attainment of cessation but not vowing to enter" refers to a case such as one who does not make the vow, "I shall now enter the attainment of cessation,"

而作是願。我當出滅定。四有想定隨一現前。彼入滅定從滅定出。

but does make the vow, "I shall now emerge from the attainment of cessation" — whichever one of the four attainments possessing perception happens to become present, he enters the attainment of cessation and then, from the attainment of cessation, emerges, whichever one of the four attainments possessing perception happens to become present.

四有想定隨一現前。作願入滅定。亦作願出者。謂如有一作是願言。我當入滅定。亦作是願。我當出滅定。四有想定隨一現前。彼入滅定從滅定出。四有想定隨一現前。不作願入滅定。不作願出者。謂如有一不作願言。我當入滅定。亦不作願我當出滅定。四有想定隨一現前彼入滅定從滅定出。四有想定隨一現前。問作願入出及唯作願入者是事可爾。以入定者必當出故。不作願入出。及唯作願出者。云何可爾。非不欲入而入定故。答此中一切皆欲入定。皆欲出定。然依入出定心有自在不自在故作是說。謂有於入定心得自在非出定心。彼作願出定不作願入。有於出定心得自在非入定心。

"Vowing both to enter the attainment of cessation and to emerge from it" refers to a case such as one who makes precisely this vow: "I shall now enter the attainment of cessation," and also makes the vow, "I shall now emerge from the attainment of cessation" — whichever one of the four attainments possessing perception happens to become present, he enters the attainment of cessation and then, from the attainment of cessation, emerges, whichever one of the four attainments possessing perception happens to become present. "Vowing neither to enter the attainment of cessation nor to emerge from it" refers to a case such as one who does not make the vow, "I shall now enter the attainment of cessation," nor the vow, "I shall now emerge from the attainment of cessation" — whichever one of the four attainments possessing perception happens to become present, he enters the attainment of cessation and then, from the attainment of cessation, emerges, whichever one of the four attainments possessing perception happens to become present. One might ask: making a vow both to enter and to emerge, and simply making a vow to enter alone, are both readily understandable, since whoever enters an attainment must, of necessity, eventually emerge from it — but how can it possibly make sense to vow neither to enter nor to emerge, or to vow to emerge alone, without ever having wished to enter in the first place? For surely one would never enter an attainment one had no wish to enter at all. Answer: in every one of these four cases, one genuinely wishes both to enter and to emerge — the distinction being drawn instead on the basis of whether one has genuine mastery over the mind of entering or the mind of emerging. That is to say, some have genuine mastery over the mind of entry but not over the mind of emergence, and it is such a person who vows to emerge but does not vow to enter; some have genuine mastery over the mind of emergence but not over the mind of entry,

彼作願入定不作願出。有於入出定心俱得自在。彼不作願入定亦不作願出。有於入出定心俱不自在。彼作願入定亦作願出。問四有想定何者是耶。答四無色定。問何故出滅定時但說起四無色定。答以四無色定於出滅定時。可作逆次出逆超出故。謂若以非想非非想處心出滅定者。彼若則起無所有處心是逆次出。若則起識無邊處心是逆超出。若以無所有處心出滅定者彼若則起識無邊處心是逆次出。

and it is such a person who vows to enter but does not vow to emerge; some have genuine mastery over both the mind of entry and the mind of emergence together, and it is such a person who vows neither to enter nor to emerge; and some have genuine mastery over neither the mind of entry nor the mind of emergence, and it is such a person who vows both to enter and to emerge. One might ask: which four attainments are meant by these "four attainments possessing perception"? Answer: the four formless attainments. One might ask: why, upon emerging from the attainment of cessation, does the passage speak only of giving rise to the four formless attainments? Answer: because these four formless attainments alone, upon emerging from the attainment of cessation, can be exited either in reverse sequential order or in a reverse leaping order — that is to say, if one emerges from the attainment of cessation with the mind of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, and then gives rise to the mind of the sphere of nothing-at-all, this is a reverse sequential emergence; if one instead gives rise to the mind of the sphere of infinite consciousness, this is a reverse leaping emergence. If one emerges from the attainment of cessation with the mind of the sphere of nothing-at-all, and then gives rise to the mind of the sphere of infinite consciousness, this is a reverse sequential emergence;

若則起空無邊處心是逆超出。是故但說四無色定。

and if one instead gives rise to the mind of the sphere of infinite space, this is a reverse leaping emergence — and it is for this reason that the passage speaks specifically of these four formless attainments alone.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The puzzle of vowing neither to enter nor to exit, despite obviously wanting both, is resolved with real subtlety: a vow, on this account, marks not desire but the absence of automatic mastery — one only bothers vowing over whichever transition does not already come effortlessly, which means the most accomplished practitioners, mastering both entry and exit alike, are precisely the ones who need make no vow about either.
