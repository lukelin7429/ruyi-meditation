Masteries and Totalities

Fascicle 85 opens with a monk's veiled question mapping seven realms onto the eight liberations, then treats the eight masteries and ten totalities at length — the Venerable Aniruddha and the four goddesses, whether the Buddha's own body can be an object of impurity-contemplation, and the puzzle of "earth itself is my very self" resolved through Devadatta's own episode of swift decline. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. The Monk's Veiled Question: Seven Realms and the Eight Liberations

A monk approaches the Buddha with a curiously indirect question: why are seven distinct realms — brightness, purity, the four formless spheres, and cessation — designated as they are? The treatise reveals the whole exchange as an elaborate veil: the monk, wishing to conceal his own attainments out of genuine modesty, poses his question with the eight liberations hidden behind unfamiliar names, and the Buddha, discerning this wish, answers in the very same coded language, mapping brightness and purity onto the first three liberations, the four formless realms onto the four formless liberations, and cessation onto the liberation of the cessation of perception and feeling itself. Several further interpretive layers follow — a second view compresses the same seven names into a summary of leaving behind the desire, form, and formless realms' own defilement in varying degrees of brevity and extension, while the Dārṣṭāntikas go further still, accusing the transmitted scripture's own wording of having been corrupted by careless reciters, substituting a supposed "realm of purity" where an original "realm of extensiveness" once stood.

Chinese Source

如契經說。有一苾芻來詣佛所。頂禮佛足退坐一面。而白佛言。世尊。說有明界淨界。空無邊處界。識無邊處界。無所有處界。非想非非想處界。滅界。如是七界緣何施設。世尊告曰。緣闇故施設明界。緣不淨故施設淨界。緣色趣故施設空無邊處界。緣邊際故施設識無邊處界。緣所有故施設無所有處界。緣有身故施設非想非非想處界。緣有身滅故施設滅界。問此中苾芻依何事問。世尊復以何事而答。答此中苾芻依八解脫覆相而問。佛亦以此覆相而答。明界者。謂初二解脫。淨界者。謂第三解脫。四無色處界者。謂四無色解脫。滅界者。謂想受滅解脫。問何故苾芻依八解脫覆相而問答。

Ru-Yi Translation

As a scripture states, there was a certain monk who came to where the Buddha was, bowed his head at the Buddha's own feet, and withdrew to sit to one side, and said to the Buddha: World-Honored One, you have spoken of there being a realm of brightness, a realm of purity, the realm of the sphere of infinite space, the realm of the sphere of infinite consciousness, the realm of the sphere of nothingness, the realm of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, and the realm of cessation — with reference to what are these seven realms designated? The World-Honored One told him: it is with reference to darkness that the realm of brightness is designated; with reference to impurity that the realm of purity is designated; with reference to the destiny of form that the realm of the sphere of infinite space is designated; with reference to a boundary that the realm of the sphere of infinite consciousness is designated; with reference to what exists that the realm of the sphere of nothingness is designated; with reference to the existence of a body that the realm of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is designated; and with reference to the cessation of the existence of a body that the realm of cessation is designated. One asks: with reference to what matter did this monk ask his question, and with reference to what matter did the World-Honored One, in turn, answer him? The answer: here, this monk asked his question with the eight liberations veiled behind his own wording, and the Buddha, too, answered him with this same veiling. As for the realm of brightness: this refers to the first two liberations. As for the realm of purity: this refers to the third liberation. As for the realm of the four formless spheres: this refers to the four formless liberations. As for the realm of cessation: this refers to the liberation of the cessation of perception and feeling. One asks: for what reason did this monk pose his question with the eight liberations veiled behind his own wording?

以彼苾芻少欲喜足覆藏己善。不欲令他知自有德故作此問。問何故世尊以八解脫覆相而答。答欲令苾芻意樂滿故。謂彼苾芻作如是念。若佛為我覆相而答。八解脫者豈不善哉。由此世尊覆相而答。

It was because that monk, possessing few desires and being easily contented, wished to conceal his own wholesome qualities, not wishing others to know that he himself possessed such merit, and it was for this reason that he posed his question in this way. One asks: for what reason did the World-Honored One, in turn, answer with the eight liberations veiled behind his own wording? The answer: it was in order to fully satisfy that monk's own underlying wish — that is, that monk reflected as follows: were the Buddha to answer me with a veiled wording, would this not itself be excellent regarding the eight liberations? And it was on this account that the World-Honored One answered him with a veiled wording.

此中緣闇故施設明界者。闇謂欲界緣色處貪。初二解脫是彼對治故緣彼立。緣不淨故施設淨界者。不淨謂初二解脫第三解脫。是彼對治故緣彼立。緣色趣故施設空無邊處界者。色趣謂第四靜慮第四解脫。是彼對治故緣彼立。緣邊際故施設識無邊處界者。邊際謂空無邊處。彼住色邊際故。第五解脫是彼對治故緣彼立。緣所有故施設無所有處界者。所有謂識無邊處彼有無邊行相轉故。第六解脫是彼對治故緣彼立。緣有身故施設非想非非想處界者。有身謂無所有處。此猶有生死身非全無所有故。第七解脫是彼對治故緣彼立。緣有身滅故施設滅界者。有身滅謂非想非非想處。以彼能滅無所有處有身法故。第八解脫是彼對治故緣彼立。有作是說。此中苾芻依略廣離三界染覆相而問。世尊亦以此覆相而答。明界者。略顯離欲界染加行道。此緣闇施設者。闇謂欲界緣五欲貪。色界加行是彼對治故緣彼立。淨界者略顯離欲界染。空無邊處界者。略顯離色界染。

Here, as for it being with reference to darkness that the realm of brightness is designated: darkness refers to the desire realm's own craving for the color-sense-field, and, since the first two liberations serve as its antidote, it is with reference to this that they are established. As for it being with reference to impurity that the realm of purity is designated: impurity refers to the first two liberations, and, since the third liberation serves as its antidote, it is with reference to this that it is established. As for it being with reference to the destiny of form that the realm of the sphere of infinite space is designated: the destiny of form refers to the fourth dhyāna, and, since the fourth liberation serves as its antidote, it is with reference to this that it is established. As for it being with reference to a boundary that the realm of the sphere of infinite consciousness is designated: a boundary refers to the sphere of infinite space, since it abides at the very boundary of form, and, since the fifth liberation serves as its antidote, it is with reference to this that it is established. As for it being with reference to what exists that the realm of the sphere of nothingness is designated: what exists refers to the sphere of infinite consciousness, since it turns with the boundless aspect, and, since the sixth liberation serves as its antidote, it is with reference to this that it is established. As for it being with reference to the existence of a body that the realm of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is designated: the existence of a body refers to the sphere of nothingness, since it still possesses the body of birth and death, not being entirely without existence, and, since the seventh liberation serves as its antidote, it is with reference to this that it is established. As for it being with reference to the cessation of the existence of a body that the realm of cessation is designated: the cessation of the existence of a body refers to the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, since it is capable of extinguishing the dharma of the existence of a body found within the sphere of nothingness, and, since the eighth liberation serves as its antidote, it is with reference to this that it is established. There are those who say: here, this monk posed his question with reference to becoming free of the defilement of the three realms, in brief and at length, veiled behind his own wording, and the World-Honored One, too, answered him with this same veiling. As for the realm of brightness: this displays, in brief, the preliminary-practice path of becoming free of the desire realm's own defilement — as for its being designated with reference to darkness: darkness refers to the desire realm's own craving for the five objects of desire, and, since the form realm's own preliminary practice serves as its antidote, it is with reference to this that it is established. As for the realm of purity: this displays, in brief, becoming free of the desire realm's own defilement. As for the realm of the sphere of infinite space: this displays, in brief, becoming free of the form realm's own defilement.

識無邊無所有非想非非想處界者。廣顯離無色界染。滅界者略顯離無色界染。此中有說。明界者略顯離欲界染。唯未至定斷欲闇故。

As for the realms of the spheres of infinite consciousness, nothingness, and neither-perception-nor-non-perception: these display, at length, becoming free of the formless realm's own defilement. As for the realm of cessation: this displays, in brief, becoming free of the formless realm's own defilement. There are those who say, here: as for the realm of brightness: this displays, in brief, becoming free of the desire realm's own defilement, since only the stage of not-yet-arrived cuts off the desire realm's own darkness.

淨界者廣顯離色界染。以四靜慮等皆名淨故。空無邊處界者。略顯離色界染。後三無色處界者。廣顯離無色界染滅界者。略顯離無色界染。欲界唯有一地染故。略顯對治色無色界各有四地染故。廣略顯彼對治。譬喻者說。此中苾芻依八等至覆相而問。佛亦以此覆相而答。然此經文誦者增減。謂增減界而減廣界。明界者。謂初二靜慮此緣闇施設者。闇謂外緣諸蓋。初二靜慮是彼對治故緣彼立。淨界者。謂第三靜慮。廣界者。謂第四靜慮。四無色處界即四無色處。

As for the realm of purity: this displays, at length, becoming free of the form realm's own defilement, since all four dhyānas and the rest are named pure. As for the realm of the sphere of infinite space: this displays, in brief, becoming free of the form realm's own defilement. As for the realm of the latter three formless spheres: this displays, at length, becoming free of the formless realm's own defilement. As for the realm of cessation: this displays, in brief, becoming free of the formless realm's own defilement — the desire realm possessing only a single ground's own defilement, and it is for this reason that its antidote is displayed only in brief; the form and formless realms each possessing four grounds' own defilement, and it is for this reason that their antidote is displayed both at length and in brief. The Dārṣṭāntikas say: here, this monk posed his question with the eight attainments veiled behind his own wording, and the Buddha, too, answered him with this same veiling. Yet this scriptural passage's own wording has been altered by its reciters — that is, the realm of purity has been added, and the realm of extensiveness has been omitted. As for the realm of brightness: this refers to the first two dhyānas — as for its being designated with reference to darkness: darkness refers to the external hindrances taken as object, and, since the first two dhyānas serve as their antidote, it is with reference to this that they are established. As for the realm of purity: this refers to the third dhyāna. As for the realm of extensiveness: this refers to the fourth dhyāna. As for the realm of the four formless spheres: this refers precisely to the four formless spheres themselves.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The monk's own reason for veiling his question — not wanting others to know he already possessed such attainments — is a small but genuinely touching detail: the entire elaborate coded exchange that follows exists only because a person of real spiritual accomplishment preferred concealment to recognition, and the Buddha's own choice to answer in kind is itself a quiet act of respect for that preference, honoring the monk's own reticence rather than overriding it with a plainer answer.

2. How Are These Realms Obtained? Attainment or Cutting-Off?

The monk presses further: by what concentration are these seven realms obtained? Two readings of this question yield two different answers. If asking how each realm's own substance is attained, six of the seven are obtained through "their own conduct's own concentration" — meaning each realm's own immediately preceding preliminary stage — while only the realm of cessation requires a further, more fundamental concentration, the very root concentration of every state of existence, sometimes called the concentration of superior existence or the concentration of the cessation of perception and feeling itself. If instead asking how each realm's own affliction is cut off, the first six are obtained through both this same preliminary concentration and the undefiled path together, while the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception's own defilement can be cut off by the undefiled path alone — and the realm of cessation's own cutting-off requires nothing less than nirvāṇa itself, since it is only at the moment of final nirvāṇa that the very attainment of cessation is finally relinquished.

Chinese Source

即此經說。時彼苾芻聞佛所說歡喜敬受。復白佛言。世尊。明界乃至滅界由何定得。佛告苾芻。如是諸界由自行餘定得。苾芻聞已歡喜敬受禮佛而去。此中有說。彼問得界。復有說者。彼問得斷。

Ru-Yi Translation

This same scripture further states: at that time, this monk, having heard what the Buddha stated, rejoiced and received it with reverence, and again said to the Buddha: World-Honored One, the realm of brightness, up through the realm of cessation — by what concentration are these obtained? The Buddha told the monk: such realms as these are obtained through their own conduct's own further concentration. The monk, having heard this, rejoiced and received it with reverence, and, bowing to the Buddha, departed. There are those who say, here, that the monk was asking about the attainment of the realm; others, further, say that he was asking about the cutting off of the realm.

若問得界彼問意言。如是諸界由何定得彼體。佛答意言。明界乃至非想非非想處界。由自行定得。自行定者謂自近分。由自近分離下地染得自地解脫。謂由初靜慮近分離欲界染得初二解脫。由第四靜慮近分離第三靜慮染得淨解脫。由空無邊處近分離第四靜慮染得空無邊處解脫。乃至由非想非非想處近分。離無所有處染得非想非非想處解脫。唯有滅界由餘定得。餘定者謂有所依定。即是非想非非想處。彼是諸有根本定故。或有說。此名有勝定。於諸有中此最勝故。或說此名想受滅定。是想受滅入出定故。由此定得第八解脫。

If he was asking about the attainment of the realm, the intent of his question was this: by what concentration are these various realms' own substances obtained? The Buddha's own intent in answering was this: the realm of brightness, up through the realm of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, are obtained through their own conduct's own concentration. As for their own conduct's own concentration: this refers to their own respective preliminary stage — that is, it is by one's own respective preliminary stage becoming free of a lower ground's own defilement that one's own ground's own liberation is obtained — that is, it is by the first dhyāna's own preliminary stage becoming free of the desire realm's own defilement that the first two liberations are obtained; it is by the fourth dhyāna's own preliminary stage becoming free of the third dhyāna's own defilement that the pure liberation is obtained; it is by the sphere of infinite space's own preliminary stage becoming free of the fourth dhyāna's own defilement that the liberation of the sphere of infinite space is obtained, and so on, up through, it is by the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception's own preliminary stage becoming free of the sphere of nothingness's own defilement that the liberation of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is obtained. Only the realm of cessation is obtained through a further concentration — as for this further concentration: this refers to a concentration that possesses a further support, namely, the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception itself, since it is the very root concentration of every state of existence. There are those who say this is named the concentration of superior existence, since, among every state of existence, this is the most supreme. Others, further, say this is named the concentration of the cessation of perception and feeling, since it is by this very concentration that one enters and emerges from the cessation of perception and feeling — it is by this concentration that the eighth liberation is obtained.

若問得斷彼問意言。如是諸界由何定得彼斷。佛答意言。明界乃至無所有處界。由自行餘定得。自行定者。謂自近分即有漏定。餘定者謂諸無漏定。欲界乃至無所有處。皆由此二定離染得彼斷。非想非非想。處界但由餘定得。唯無漏定能離非想非非想處染得彼斷。

If he was asking about the cutting off of the realm, the intent of his question was this: by what concentration are these various realms' own cutting-off obtained? The Buddha's own intent in answering was this: the realm of brightness, up through the realm of the sphere of nothingness, are obtained through their own conduct's own further concentration. As for their own conduct's own concentration: this refers to their own respective preliminary stage, that is, a contaminated concentration. As for this further concentration: this refers to every undefiled concentration. The desire realm, up through the sphere of nothingness, all obtain their own cutting-off, becoming free of defilement, through both of these two kinds of concentration. The realm of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is obtained solely through this further concentration, since it is only the undefiled concentration that is capable of becoming free of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception's own defilement, thereby obtaining its own cutting-off.

故滅界但由餘得。餘謂有身滅即是涅槃。般涅槃時捨滅盡定說名為斷。由餘涅槃而得彼斷故說彼由餘得。

It is for this reason that the realm of cessation, too, is obtained solely through this further attainment — this further attainment referring to the cessation of the existence of a body, namely, nirvāṇa itself, since, at the time of final nirvāṇa, the relinquishing of the attainment of cessation is spoken of as cutting-off, and it is by this further attainment of nirvāṇa that this cutting-off is obtained, and it is for this reason that it is said to be obtained through this further attainment.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the realm of cessation alone, among all seven, requires nirvāṇa itself to be genuinely "cut off" is a striking claim about the structure of the entire path: every other realm's own affliction admits of removal through concentration and the undefiled path while the practitioner still lives and continues cultivating, but the very attainment of cessation persists, in some sense, until the final moment of parinirvāṇa itself, making it the one stage whose own completion is inseparable from the ending of the path altogether.

3. The Eight Masteries: Own-Nature and Meaning

A new topic opens — the eight masteries, progressing from observing a small quantity of external form while still possessing an internal notion of form, through observing a large quantity, and then repeating both stages once the internal notion has already been relinquished, before culminating in four masteries over the specific colors blue, yellow, red, and white. Their shared own-nature is the wholesome root of non-greed, counteracting greed, and the very meaning of "mastery" is twofold: mastering one's own object, and mastering every affliction — since even a practitioner not literally capable of overcoming every object he takes may still be named a master, so long as no affliction arises with respect to whatever object he does take.

Chinese Source

八勝處者。一內有色想觀外色少。二內有色想觀外色多。三內無色想觀外色少。四內無色想觀外色多。內無色想觀外諸色青黃赤白。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the eight masteries: first, possessing an internal notion of form, one observes a small quantity of external form; second, possessing an internal notion of form, one observes a large quantity of external form; third, lacking any internal notion of form, one observes a small quantity of external form; fourth, lacking any internal notion of form, one observes a large quantity of external form. As for lacking an internal notion of form, observing every kind of external form — blue, yellow, red, and white —

復為四種。如是八種名八勝處。問此八勝處自性是何。答無貪善根以為自性對治貪故。若兼取相應隨轉。則欲界者以四蘊為自性。色界者以五蘊為自性。如是名為勝處自性。我物自體相分本性。已說自性所以今當說。問何故名勝處。勝處是何義。答勝所緣境故名勝處。復次勝諸煩惱故名勝處。雖觀行者非一切能勝所緣境。而於所緣不起煩惱亦名為勝。如契經說。於此處勝故名勝處。此八勝處界者皆是色界。地者前四勝處在初二靜慮及未至定靜慮中間。後四勝處在第四靜慮。所依者皆依欲界身起。行相者一切皆非分明行相。

this is, further, of four kinds, and it is in this way that these eight kinds are named the eight masteries. One asks: what is the own-nature of these eight masteries? The answer: the wholesome root of non-greed serves as their own-nature, counteracting greed; if one further takes the associated dharmas that turn along with it, then, within the desire realm, four aggregates serve as their own-nature, and, within the form realm, five aggregates serve as their own-nature. Such, then, is named the own-nature of the masteries — their own thing, own substance, own characteristic, own portion, and own inherent nature. Own-nature has already been discussed; what is to be discussed next shall now be stated. One asks: for what reason is it named a mastery? What is the meaning of mastery? The answer: it is because it masters its own object that it is named mastery. Further, it is because it masters every affliction that it is named mastery — although a practitioner of contemplation is not, in every case, able to master every object he takes, still, if he gives rise to no affliction with respect to his own object, this, too, is named mastery, exactly as a scripture states, since it is superior with respect to this domain, it is named mastery. As for the realm of these eight masteries: all belong to the form realm. As for their ground: the first four masteries exist within the first two dhyānas, the stage of not-yet-arrived, and the intermediate dhyāna; the latter four masteries exist within the fourth dhyāna. As for what they depend upon: all arise in dependence upon a body of the desire realm. As for their aspect: every one of them lacks a clear, distinct aspect.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The observation that mastery does not require literally overpowering one's object, only remaining free of affliction toward it, is a meaningful loosening of what could otherwise sound like an impossibly grand claim: the eight masteries are not promising total control over every visual field a practitioner might encounter, only an inner freedom from affliction that holds regardless of what the object itself happens to be.

4. Aniruddha and the Four Goddesses

A vivid story tests whether the eight masteries genuinely take every form of the desire realm as their object without exception. Four delightful goddesses, offering themselves in service to the Venerable Aniruddha, boast of their own complete mastery over transforming into any of the four colors — and Aniruddha, wishing to cultivate the contemplation of impurity toward them, finds himself unable to give rise to any notion of impurity whatsoever, whether entering the first dhyāna or the fourth, whether the goddesses appear in their own varied colors or, at his own request, uniformly blue, yellow, red, or finally white. Concluding that heavenly beauty simply cannot be overcome, he closes his eyes in silence — and the goddesses, perceiving that his own mind holds no defiled attachment at all, vanish in shame, neither his concentration overcoming their beauty nor their beauty overcoming his concentration, exactly as two evenly matched strongmen wrestle to a draw.

Chinese Source

所緣者皆緣欲界一切色處。問若爾經說當云何通。尊者無滅在室羅筏住一精舍。爾時有四悅意天女。來至尊者座前而立。白言。我等於四色處轉變自在。隨所愛色皆能化作。以相娛樂隨心所玩。衣服嚴具皆能現之。願垂納受以充供侍。尊者無滅作是念言。我應緣彼起不淨觀。作是念已入初靜慮。緣彼不能起不淨想。乃至後入第四靜慮緣亦不能起不淨想。復作是念。此天女身有種種色惑亂人意。

5. Can the Buddha's Own Body Be an Object of Impurity-Contemplation?

A further, more delicate question follows directly from Aniruddha's story: can anyone at all take the Buddha's own bodily radiance as an object and give rise to a notion of impurity toward it? Several positions are canvassed — that no one at all is capable of this, the Buddha's own body being too radiant and unblemished; that only the Buddha himself, and no ordinary being, disciple, or solitary-realizer, could manage it; or that the answer depends on which of two kinds of impurity-contemplation is meant, one observing form's own faults directly (which indeed cannot take the Buddha as object), the other observing form's own dependent origination (which can). A further technical clarification addresses whether the contemplation of impurity belongs to the mental ground alone or extends to the five sense-consciousnesses as well — the answer being the mental ground alone, the five senses merely serving as the expedient trigger that draws it forth, exactly as ordinary sense-perception's own immediately following mental process genuinely belongs to mind alone despite depending on the senses to initiate it.

7. The Ten Totalities: Own-Nature and Meaning

The final member of this classic triad opens — the ten totalities of blue, yellow, red, white, earth, water, fire, wind, the sphere of infinite space, and the sphere of infinite consciousness — sharing the wholesome root of non-greed as their own-nature. The very meaning of "totality" rests on two conditions at once: being uninterrupted, since the mental application involved is never intermixed with anything else, and being vast and expansive, since the object it takes is boundless in its own characteristic — the Great Virtuous One summarizing both conditions together as an object so broad and expansive that it admits of no gap or interruption whatsoever.

8. Why the Higher Totalities Exist at Higher Grounds

Why do the first eight totalities exist within the fourth dhyāna while the ninth and tenth exist within the sphere of infinite space and of infinite consciousness respectively? 9. Individual Totalities, and Why Even Space and Consciousness Admit "Above" and "Below"

Each totality's own particular definition follows the same template established for blue: pervading every direction, above, below, and to every side, without duality and without measure, giving rise to a single, seamless notion of that totality's own object. A genuine puzzle arises for the latter two totalities specifically: since space and consciousness possess no spatial location whatsoever, how can they be said to admit of "above" and "below" at all? The resolution locates this directionality not in the object itself but in the concentration a practitioner depends upon, which does admit of three genuine grades — a superior grade named "above," an inferior grade named "below," and a middling grade named "to the side" — and, further, in the practitioner's own bodily location within the cosmic hierarchy of heavens, each realm serving as lower, middling, or upper only in comparison to its neighbors.

10. Why the Third Dhyāna Alone Lacks Liberation, Mastery, and Totality

A final structural puzzle closes out this whole triad: why does the third dhyāna alone, among every ground from the desire realm to the peak of existence, possess none of the liberations, masteries, or totalities? A cluster of converging reasons answers this: it is simply not a fitting vessel for them; the specific cravings the first two dhyānas' impure liberations and masteries counteract, and the disturbances the pure liberation and later attainments require an undisturbed ground to overcome, are either already absent by the third dhyāna or still too disruptive there; the third dhyāna, though distant from the desire realm, is not itself the most supreme among the dhyānas; and, most memorably, it possesses the very finest experience of pleasure among all the pleasures of birth and death, a pleasure so supreme that it risks becoming its own trap, causing a practitioner to grow deeply attached and confused rather than pressing onward toward liberation.

Further, the third dhyāna, exactly like the third formless attainment, possesses no abundance of merit, and it is for this reason that it possesses no liberation and the rest — that is, the spheres of infinite space and of infinite consciousness possess the merit of the boundless aspect; the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception possesses the merit of the attainment of cessation; the sphere of nothingness possesses neither the boundless aspect nor the attainment of cessation, and it is for this reason that this ground's own merit is diminished — the third dhyāna, exactly like that ground, likewise possesses no merit of liberation, mastery, or totality. Further, the third dhyāna possesses the very supreme experience of pleasure among the pleasures of birth and death, capable of causing a practitioner to become deeply attached and confused, and it is for this reason that it possesses no liberation, mastery, or totality. One asks: if so, it ought then to possess none of the remaining merits either, such as the immeasurables, the superknowledges, and the rest. The answer: to say that its merit is diminished is not to say that it is entirely without merit whatsoever — were this ground to possess none of the immeasurables and the rest, this dhyāna would then be utterly without merit at all. Further, the immeasurables, taking sentient beings in general as their object, comprehensively pervading through wisdom, range freely within this ground, and it is thereby that it comes to possess the merit of liberation, mastery, and totality; taking form and the rest individually as their object, subduing every affliction, being a matter especially difficult to accomplish, this is why it lacks this merit here.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That the third dhyāna's own supreme pleasure is treated as a genuine liability rather than simply a reward is a striking inversion of ordinary spiritual expectation: the very attainment that might seem like unambiguous progress turns out to be precisely why this ground cannot support the liberations and totalities at all, the pleasure's own intensity working against further advancement exactly to the degree that it succeeds in being pleasurable.

11. "Earth Itself Is My Self": Devadatta's Spittle

A scripture describing one who has attained the totality of earth as reflecting "earth itself is my very self" raises an intricate puzzle: since attaining this totality requires having already become free of the third dhyāna's own defilement, any view of self such a person gives rise to ought to belong to the fourth dhyāna — yet the totality itself takes only the desire realm's own form as its object, so how can the very same object be shared between a fourth-dhyāna view of self and a desire-realm totality? Several resolutions are offered, the most vivid drawing on Devadatta's own infamous episode: having attained dhyāna, he once transformed himself by supernormal power into a small child to win Prince Ajātaśatru's affection, and, greedy for the prince's offerings, swallowed the child's own spittle placed in his mouth — at which very moment he instantly declined from his own dhyāna, only to swiftly regain it and resume the transformed child's play as though nothing had happened. A view of self arising through a totality, the treatise suggests, is exactly this same swift decline and swift recovery: a momentary lapse into a desire-realm self-view, taking desire-realm earth as its object, followed by an immediate return to the fourth dhyāna's own totality once more.

Chinese Source

如契經說。得地遍處定者。作如是念。地即是我我即是地。地之與我無二無別。得餘遍處定者。隨自所緣廣說亦爾。問得遍處定者。

Ru-Yi Translation

As a scripture states, one who has attained the totality of earth's own concentration reflects as follows: earth itself is my very self; my very self is itself earth; earth and my self are without duality, without distinction. One who has attained the concentration of every remaining totality, each according to its own object, is stated in just the same way. One asks: whoever has attained the concentration of a totality

必已離第三靜慮染。彼所起我見必是第四靜慮。若第四靜慮我見。

must certainly have already become free of the third dhyāna's own defilement, and the view of self he thereby gives rise to must certainly belong to the fourth dhyāna. Yet, if the view of self belongs to the fourth dhyāna,

必唯緣第四靜慮地等前八遍處。但緣欲界色處為境。如何可說與彼我見同緣地等。有作是說。此中於未得彼定者說得彼定者聲。如非沙門說名沙門。非婆羅門說名婆羅門。謂以欲界聞思所成初學修習地等遍處。猶未能斷欲界煩惱。未得根本地等遍處。故緣地等容起我見。或有說者。仍舊名說亦不相違。如有國王。雖失王位。仍舊名說亦呼為王。如是先得地遍處等。今雖退失仍名得者。彼起欲界我見。緣欲界地等計我於理無違。復有說者。依速入出遍處我見。

it must certainly take solely the fourth dhyāna's own earth and the like, among the first eight totalities, as its object — how, then, can it be said that it takes the desire realm's own form-sense-field alone as its object, and yet is said to share the identical object as this view of self, which takes earth and the like as its object? There are those who say: here, the very word "attained" is spoken with reference to one who has not, in fact, yet attained that concentration, exactly as one who is not a renunciant may still be spoken of as a renunciant, or one who is not a brahmin may still be spoken of as a brahmin — that is, by means of the hearing-and-reflection wisdom of the desire realm, a beginner practices and cultivates earth and the like as a totality, yet is not yet capable of cutting off the desire realm's own affliction, and has not yet attained the root totality of earth and the like — and it is for this reason that, taking earth and the like as his object, he may still give rise to a view of self. There are those who say: this simply retains the old name in speaking of it, and this, too, raises no contradiction — exactly as a certain king, although he has already lost his own royal position, may still be addressed, retaining his old name, as "king" — so too, one who formerly attained the totality of earth and the like, though he has now declined and lost it, is still named one who has attained it, and it is his giving rise to a view of self within the desire realm, taking the desire realm's own earth and the like as his object and conceiving of a self there, that raises no contradiction with principle. There are those who further say: this speaks with reference to swiftly entering and emerging from the totality's own view of self,

作如是說亦不相違。謂彼先得地遍處等。後速退起欲界我見。緣執欲界地等為我。速復還得第四靜慮地遍處等彼緣欲界地等為境。如提婆達多先得靜慮。以神境通力變作小兒。著金縷俗衣作五花頂。

and it is for this reason that this, too, raises no contradiction — that is, one who has formerly attained the totality of earth and the like, afterward, in a swift decline, gives rise to a view of self within the desire realm, taking the desire realm's own earth and the like as his object and conceiving of a self there, and then swiftly regains the fourth dhyāna's own totality of earth and the like once more — exactly as Devadatta, who had formerly attained dhyāna, by the power of supernormal power over the domain, transformed himself into a small child, adorned with a golden thread and secular robes, his hair arranged in five topknots,

and sat playfully upon the lap of Prince Ajātaśatru, causing the prince to know that this was, in fact, the Venerable Devadatta. At that time, Ajātaśatru, filled with tender affection, embraced and played with him, and, kissing him, placed his own spittle into the child's mouth. Devadatta, greedy for gain and offering, thereupon swallowed this spittle, and it was for this reason that the Buddha rebuked him, saying: you are one who eats a dead body's own spittle. At the very moment he swallowed the spittle, he at once declined from his own dhyāna, and swiftly regained it once more, causing the very transformed body he had made to continue playing upon the prince's lap exactly as before — the view of self arising through a totality, too, should be understood in the corresponding manner. There are further masters who say: here, this refers to a view of self that grasps the fourth dhyāna's own earth and the like as a self — the first eight totalities, too, taking the fourth dhyāna's own earth and the like as their object. One asks: do the first eight totalities not, in fact, take the desire realm's own color as their object? The answer: they take both the desire realm's own color and the fourth dhyāna's own color as their object. One asks: the gods of the form realm are of a single, uniform white color — how, then, can one take them as object and conceive of blue and the rest with respect to them? The answer: although the sentient beings of the form realm are of a single, uniform white color, insentient matter there, too, possesses blue and the rest. The compilers say: one ought not to speak in this way — if a view of self were associated and co-arisen with the eight totalities, one might then raise the difficulty of how a single associated, co-arisen dharma could take either the fourth dhyāna or the desire realm as its object; yet a view of self is never, in fact, associated with, or co-arisen with, the eight totalities, and it is for this reason that this difficulty ought not to be raised at all — and yet the same single sentient being who is said to conceive of a self is also named one who has attained concentration, since, owing to his own view of self, he is named one who conceives of a self, and, owing to his own totality, he is named one who has attained concentration. His own view of self grasps the fourth dhyāna's own earth and the like as a self, while his own eight totalities take the desire realm's own color as their object, these not occurring at the very same time, and it is thereby that this scripture is well reconciled — this view of self and these eight totalities merely sharing the very same supporting body, while their own object differs.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Devadatta's own spittle-swallowing episode does far more narrative work here than its comic surface suggests: it supplies a concrete, psychologically vivid template for how an attainment as advanced as dhyāna itself can be instantly and completely lost through a single moment of craving, and just as instantly regained once that craving passes — proving, by dramatic example, that the swift decline-and-recovery this puzzle requires is not a theoretical possibility invented to save the scripture, but something the tradition already knew could genuinely happen to an accomplished practitioner.

12. Concentration Versus Totality, and the Threefold Distinction

Two closing technical clarifications round out this run of material. The "concentration" of earth and the rest, spanning the desire realm and all four dhyānas, is distinguished from the "totality" of earth and the rest, confined solely to the fourth dhyāna, by name, by stage (preliminary versus fully accomplished), by role (cause versus fruit), and by scope (partial versus fully pervasive understanding). And liberation, mastery, and totality themselves are finally distinguished by grade of wholesome root (inferior, middling, superior), by role (cause alone, cause and fruit together, or fruit alone), and by function (turning away and abandoning, mastering one's object, or expanding that object without limit) — with a strict entailment closing the whole discussion: attaining mastery guarantees having already attained liberation, and attaining totality guarantees having already attained both liberation and mastery, since one enters mastery from liberation and totality from mastery in an unbreakable sequence.

Further, at the stage of cause, it is named the concentration of earth and the rest; at the stage of fruit, it is named the totality of earth and the rest. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The strict, one-directional entailment closing this whole triad — totality implies mastery implies liberation, but never the reverse — is a fitting capstone to the entire sequence: rather than three independent, freely combinable attainments, liberation, mastery, and totality turn out to form a single cumulative ladder, where each higher rung necessarily contains everything achieved on every rung below it, so that reaching the very summit, the totalities themselves, means having already, along the way, mastered everything this whole extended treatment has covered.
