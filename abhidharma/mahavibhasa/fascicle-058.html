Traversing the Six

Fascicle 58 turns the Single-Class-of-Conduct Nyāsa toward a new question: not which object two fetters share, but how a single fetter's own binding across the past, future, and present relates to itself and to other fetters. Every fetter is sorted into two classes — self-characteristic and shared-characteristic — each with its own rule for how certainly it binds across time, before the treatise works craving's full six-fold ledger and then compares it exhaustively against hatred, envy, stinginess, conceit, and ignorance in turn. 1. Two Kinds of Fetters, and the Framework of "Traversing the Six"

Fascicle 58 opens by sorting every fetter into two classes: those that delude with respect to their own characteristic — craving, hatred, conceit, envy, and stinginess — and those that delude with respect to a shared characteristic — ignorance, view, grasping, and doubt. Each class follows its own rule for how its binding holds across time: the self-characteristic fetters bind the future with certainty whenever uncut, but their past and present binding depend on whether they previously arose and remain uncut, or are currently manifest; the shared-characteristic fetters bind both past and future with certainty whenever uncut, leaving only the present indefinite. This scaffolding is named "traversing the six, the lesser seven, the greater seven, and the abridged Vibhāṣā."

For a given matter that is bound by the past craving-fetter, is it likewise bound in the future? — and so on, at length. Here, all the fetters are of two kinds: first, those that delude with respect to their own characteristic; second, those that delude with respect to a shared characteristic. Those that delude with respect to their own characteristic are craving, hatred, conceit, envy, and stinginess; those that delude with respect to a shared characteristic are ignorance, view, grasping, and doubt. Among the fetters that delude with respect to their own characteristic, the craving-fetter is capable of binding the matter of the five sections across all three realms — with respect to the future, if uncut, it certainly binds every matter across all three times; with respect to the past, it is indefinite, that is, with respect to a given matter, if it previously arose there and remains uncut, it binds, while if it never previously arose there, or, having arisen, was already cut off, it does not bind; with respect to the present, it is likewise indefinite, that is, with respect to a given matter, if it is presently manifest there, it binds, while if it is not presently manifest, it does not bind. Conceit is exactly the same. The hatred-fetter is capable of binding the matter of the five sections within the desire realm alone — with respect to the future, if uncut, it certainly binds every matter across all three times; with respect to the past, it is indefinite, that is, with respect to a given matter, if it previously arose there and remains uncut, it binds, while if it never previously arose there, or, having arisen, was already cut off, it does not bind; with respect to the present, it is likewise indefinite, that is, with respect to a given matter, if it is presently manifest there, it binds, while if it is not presently manifest, it does not bind. The envy-fetter is capable of binding the matter of the desire realm's own section abandoned by cultivation alone — with respect to the future, if uncut, it certainly binds every matter across all three times; with respect to the past, it is indefinite, that is, with respect to a given matter, if it previously arose there and remains uncut, it binds, while if it never previously arose there, or, having arisen, was already cut off, it does not bind; with respect to the present, it is likewise indefinite, that is, with respect to a given matter, if it is presently manifest there, it binds, while if it is not presently manifest, it does not bind. The fetters that delude with respect to a shared characteristic are capable of binding the matter of the five sections across all three realms — with respect to both the past and the future, if uncut, they certainly bind every matter across all three times; with respect to the present, they are indefinite, that is, with respect to a given matter, if it is presently manifest there, they bind, while if it is not presently manifest, they do not bind. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The asymmetry built into this classification is worth pausing on: a self-characteristic fetter's relationship to its own past is genuinely uncertain in a way its relationship to the future never is, since the future is simply whatever remains uncut, while the past requires the additional fact of having actually arisen there before — a structural reminder that, in this system, "not yet cut off" and "already arisen" are two entirely separate conditions that happen to coincide in the future tense alone.

2. Craving's Own Six-Fold Ledger

The treatise works through craving's own binding across every pairing and grouping of the three times in exhaustive detail — past implies future, future does not always imply past, and a genuine four-case tetralemma emerges once two or three times are considered together — before generalizing the entire pattern first to hatred, conceit, envy, and stinginess, and then, in simplified form, to the view-fetter as a representative of the shared-characteristic class, whose past and future bind each other outright while only the present remains conditional.

4. Craving Against Hatred: The Full Cross-Tense Ledger

The treatise now compares craving against a second fetter entirely, tracking every combination of tense between the two: past craving against past hatred, against future hatred, against present hatred, and then against every possible pairing and tripling of hatred's own three tenses at once, each case built from the same recurring elements — whether a fetter previously arose and remains uncut, whether it is currently manifest, and whether, once cut off or never arisen, it fails to bind at all.
