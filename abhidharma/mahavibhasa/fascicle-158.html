Does Attainment Need Its Own Attainment?

Fascicle 158 continues the Attainment Nyāsa with the classic "attainment of an attainment" infinite-regress puzzle and its resolution, then works through the fifteen attainments of the path of seeing, a systematic comparison of the four truths against their attainments, and a chain of scholastic disputes over the scope of liberation-attainment. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Eight Ways Attainment Differs From Non-Attainment, and Three Kinds of Attainment

Fascicle 158 continues within the Attainment Nyāsa with a tidy list of eight gateways distinguishing attainment from non-attainment — contaminated/uncontaminated, moral quality, realm, path-stage, abandonment-category, defilement, and ripening-status — before classifying attainment itself into three kinds, each following a different source for its own nature: a conditioned dharma's own causal efficacy, a realized path's power in the case of analytical cessation, and mere dependence on the life-faculty in the case of non-analytical cessation.

Chinese Source

問得非得何差別。答名即差別。謂名得。名非得。復次得有漏無漏。非得唯有漏。復次得善不善無記。非得唯無記。復次得三界繫及不繫。非得唯三界繫。復次得學無學非學非無學。非得唯非學非無學。復次得見所斷。修所斷。不斷。非得唯修所斷。復次得染污不染污。非得唯不染污。復次得異熟非異熟。非得唯非異熟。復次得有異熟無異熟。非得唯無異熟。復次得與所得。法或俱起。或不俱起。非得與所不得法必不俱起。復次得苦集道三諦攝。非得唯苦集諦攝。以如是等門。應知得非得差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: what is the difference between attainment and non-attainment? Answer: the very name already differs — one is named "attainment," the other "non-attainment." Furthermore, attainment may be either contaminated or uncontaminated, while non-attainment is exclusively contaminated. Furthermore, attainment may be wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral, while non-attainment is exclusively neutral. Furthermore, attainment may be fettered to any of the three realms or unfettered, while non-attainment is exclusively fettered to the three realms. Furthermore, attainment may belong to a trainee, a non-trainee, or to neither, while non-attainment exclusively belongs to neither trainee nor non-trainee. Furthermore, attainment may be abandoned by seeing, abandoned by cultivation, or unabandoned, while non-attainment is exclusively abandoned by cultivation. Furthermore, attainment may be defiled or undefiled, while non-attainment is exclusively undefiled. Furthermore, attainment may be a ripening-result or not a ripening-result, while non-attainment is exclusively not a ripening-result. Furthermore, attainment may itself possess a ripening-result or lack one, while non-attainment exclusively lacks one. Furthermore, an attainment and the dharma it attains may either co-arise or not co-arise together, while a non-attainment and the dharma it fails to obtain never co-arise together. Furthermore, attainment may be subsumed under any of the three truths of suffering, of origin, or of the path, while non-attainment is exclusively subsumed under the truths of suffering and of origin. By means of gateways such as these, the differences between attainment and non-attainment should be understood.

second, the attainment of analytical cessation; and third, the attainment of non-analytical cessation. The attainment of a conditioned dharma follows the particular nature and category of the dharma it attains, since a conditioned dharma possesses its own causal efficacy and can thereby draw forth its own attainment. The attainment of analytical cessation follows the particular nature and category of the very path that realizes it, since analytical cessation itself possesses no causal efficacy at all, but is sought and realized solely through the power of the path — it is the path, at the moment of realization, that draws forth that cessation's attainment. The attainment of non-analytical cessation follows the particular nature and category of whatever it itself depends upon, since non-analytical cessation likewise possesses no causal efficacy, is not something sought by any path, and its attainment becomes manifest solely in dependence upon the life-faculty and one's own class of homogeneous existence. Question: what nature and category does non-attainment itself follow? Answer: it definitely does not follow the dharma it fails to obtain, since the two are directly opposed; nor does it follow whatever is sought by a path or a non-path, since it operates solely in dependence upon the life-faculty and one's class of homogeneous existence — rather, it follows whatever it itself depends upon. Question: if both non-attainment and the attainment of non-analytical cessation alike follow whatever they themselves depend upon, and that basis may be either a ripening-result or purely of the outflow-continuing kind, which of these two natures do they follow? Answer: they follow the outflow-continuing nature, since its meaning applies universally, whereas a ripening-result does not apply universally and so is not taken as the basis for this rule. Question: if non-attainment followed the particular nature and category of the dharma it fails to obtain, what fault would follow? Answer: one whose wholesome roots have been severed would then have to possess wholesome dharmas; one already freed from the defilement of desire would then have to possess unwholesome dharmas; every non-trainee would then have to possess defilement; every ordinary worldling would then have to possess the uncontaminated dharmas of all three vehicles; one who has regressed from a fruit would then have to possess that fruit; one who has forsaken the approach to a fruit would then have to possess that approach; and the non-attainment of the two kinds of cessation would then have to be unconditioned. Owing to these and similar faults, non-attainment cannot be said to vary according to the particular nature and category of the dharma it fails to obtain.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That non-attainment is barred from ever tracking the specific nature of the dharma it fails to obtain — on pain of absurdities like a wholesome-root-severed person still counting as possessing wholesome dharmas — reveals a deep design principle: non-attainment is deliberately built as a thin, uniform placeholder rather than a rich mirror of what it negates, precisely so that its own properties never accidentally leak information about the very dharma it is the absence of.

2. The Attainment of an Attainment, and Why This Does Not Spiral Into Infinite Regress

A classic puzzle now surfaces: if attainment is itself a real entity, does it too need its own attainment to be possessed — and does that attainment need one in turn, forever? The treatise's answer is the celebrated three-dharma solution: within a single instant, a dharma, its attainment, and the attainment's own attainment-of-attainment mutually attain one another in a closed loop, so that no fourth term is ever required.

Chinese Source

問所說得為更有得為無耶。若更有得者得傳有得。何非無窮。若更無得者。此得由何可說成就。答應說得。復有得。問若爾應成無窮。答無窮亦無有過。由此生死難斷。難越。或無量得皆一剎那俱生。而滅無無窮過。如是說者。一剎那中但有三法。一彼法。二得。三得得。由得故成就彼法及得得。由得得故成就得。由更互相得故非無窮。是故說色蘊行蘊一得得。乃至識蘊行蘊一得得。有為無為一得得。問為一一法各別有得。為不爾耶。或有說者。俱有法同一得得。問若爾不應作如是說。色蘊行蘊一得得等。答欲顯法與得得。無異得故作如是說。然實不無五蘊四蘊一得得義。有說。一一法各別得得。唯除得得。與彼法同一得得。問若爾便有無窮過失。以得皆有生住異滅。生等復有得與得得。彼得得得復有生等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: as for the attainment just spoken of, does it in turn have its own attainment, or does it not? If it does have its own attainment, then that attainment would in turn have to be attained by yet another attainment, and so on — why would this not run on without end? If it does not have its own attainment, then by what means could this original attainment itself ever be said to be possessed? Answer: it should be said that attainment does have a further attainment. Question: if so, would this not entail an infinite regress? Answer: an infinite regress here entails no real fault. It is precisely because of this that saṃsāra is so hard to sever, so hard to cross beyond. Alternatively, all of these countless attainments co-arise and cease together within a single instant, and so entail no fault of infinite regress. According to this latter account, within a single instant there are only three dharmas: first, the dharma itself; second, its attainment; and third, the attainment of that attainment. Through the attainment, one possesses the dharma itself and the attainment-of-attainment; through the attainment-of-attainment, one possesses the attainment. Because each attains the other in turn, no infinite regress results. It is for this reason that one speaks of a single attainment-of-attainment shared between the aggregate of matter and the aggregate of formations, and likewise a single attainment-of-attainment shared between the aggregate of consciousness and the aggregate of formations, and a single attainment-of-attainment shared between the conditioned and the unconditioned. Question: does each individual dharma have its own separate attainment, or is this not so? Some hold that co-arisen dharmas share one and the same attainment-of-attainment. Question: if so, then one should not speak, as above, of a single attainment-of-attainment shared between the aggregate of matter and the aggregate of formations, and so on. Answer: this is said precisely in order to make clear that a dharma and its attainment-of-attainment have no separate attainment apart from each other — though in truth there is no reason the five aggregates and the four aggregates could not each share their own single attainment-of-attainment. Others hold that each individual dharma has its own separate attainment-of-attainment, with the sole exception that the attainment-of-attainment shares a single attainment-of-attainment with the dharma it belongs to. Question: if so, would this not entail the very fault of infinite regress? For an attainment itself has arising, abiding, changing, and ceasing, and arising and the rest in turn have their own attainment and attainment-of-attainment, and that attainment-of-attainment's own attainment-of-attainment in turn has arising and the rest,

and proceeding on in this way an infinite regress would indeed result. Answer: what real fault would an infinite regress here entail? Or else, all of these dharmas co-arise and cease together within a single instant, and so entail no fault of infinite regress. According to this account, a dharma and its arising and the rest share one and the same attainment-of-attainment, since a characteristic and the thing it characterizes stand in such extremely close proximity. By this means one can properly account for the single shared attainment-of-attainment between the aggregate of matter and the aggregate of formations, and so on, while also, as before, avoiding the fault of infinite regress. As was said of attainment, so too non-attainment stands opposed to attainment, and should be explained in the very same expanded manner. There is, however, no such thing as a non-attainment of a non-attainment occurring at the same time — for whenever a dharma belonging to a sentient being is currently manifest, it must necessarily co-occur with its own attainment; hence, at the very moment a non-attainment arises, it is decisively never co-arisen with a non-attainment of itself, nor with the dharma it fails to obtain, these three dharmas being mutually opposed to one another. Speaking of these as occurring at different times, however, is not thereby ruled out.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Watching two live disagreements nested inside one another here — first, whether infinite regress is even a real problem (some masters shrug and say saṃsāra is hard to escape precisely because of such regresses) or a fake one dissolved by simultaneity; second, whether every dharma has its own attainment-of-attainment or co-arising dharmas share one — is a rare glimpse of a foundational metaphysical puzzle still visibly under construction, with the tidy three-dharma loop presented as the winning solution only after both false starts are shown their own way out.

3. Are Past and Future Attainments Themselves Possessed? A Second Regress, and a Second Resolution

A second, structurally identical worry now arises one level up: if a past or future attainment is itself possessed by a further attainment, doesn't the doubling repeat at every moment, spiraling toward an unbounded number of attainments stretching back through beginningless time? The treatise defends the reality of past and future attainment anyway — citing the very fact that śramaṇa-fruits are not relinquished every single instant, and that the noble path is relinquished only at three specific occasions — arguing that even an unbounded regress carries no real fault, or, on the alternative account, that all these attainments simply co-arise within one instant.

Chinese Source

That two entirely different "no real fault" strategies are offered back to back — first, boldly accepting the infinite regress and reframing it as simply what makes saṃsāra so hard to escape, then, more cautiously, denying the regress ever occurs at all because everything is simultaneous — shows a tradition comfortable holding two independently sufficient defenses in reserve rather than staking everything on one, in case a later opponent finds a hole in either single move.

4. Four Ways Attainment Can Relate in Time to Its Dharma, and Six Kinds of Dharma-Attained

Attainment relative to its own dharma may occur beforehand, afterward, simultaneously, or in none of these three relations at once — mapped onto six corresponding categories of dharma-attained, from ripening-produced dharmas (simultaneous attainment only) to analytical and non-analytical cessation (no fixed temporal relation to their attainment at all, and yet still genuinely attained).

Chinese Source

二在彼法後。三與彼法俱。四非彼法前後及俱。若所得法則有六種。一有所得法唯有俱得。如異熟生等。二有所得法唯有前得。如三類智邊世俗智等。有說。此等亦有俱得。三有所得法唯有俱得後得。如別解脫戒等。四有所得法唯有俱得前得。如道類智忍等。五有所得法具有前後俱得。如所餘善染污等。六有所得法不可說有前後俱得。而有諸得。謂擇滅非擇滅。必無有法唯有法後得者。現在前時必有得故。一切非得總有三種。一在彼法前。二在彼法後三非彼法前後及俱。所不得法亦有三種。一有所不得法。唯有彼前非得謂未來情數畢竟不生法。及入無餘涅槃最後剎那心等。二有所不得法。通有彼前彼後非得。謂餘隨應有情數法。三有所不得法。無彼前後及俱非得而有非得。謂擇滅非擇滅。必無非得可與法俱。以法現在前時。是所得者必有得故。非所得者。無得無非得故。亦無唯有彼後非得。非無始來恒成就彼。未捨必起彼類盡故。然諸非得性羸劣故。唯成就現在。一一剎那得已即捨。於未得彼法及已捨位。

Ru-Yi Translation

first, a dharma attained that has only a simultaneous attainment, as with ripening-produced dharmas and the like; second, a dharma attained that has only a preceding attainment, as with the mundane wisdom bordering on the three kinds of knowledge — some hold that these too have a simultaneous attainment; third, a dharma attained that has only a simultaneous attainment and a following attainment, as with the individually-liberating precepts and the like; fourth, a dharma attained that has only a simultaneous attainment and a preceding attainment, as with the patient acceptance belonging to the class-knowledge of the path and the like; fifth, a dharma attained that has a preceding, following, and simultaneous attainment all three, as with the remaining wholesome and defiled dharmas and so on; and sixth, a dharma attained of which one cannot speak of any preceding, following, or simultaneous attainment, yet which nonetheless does have attainments — namely, analytical and non-analytical cessation. There is definitely no dharma that has only a following attainment and no other, since at the very moment any dharma becomes currently manifest, it must already have an attainment. Non-attainment, altogether, is of three kinds: first, that which precedes its own dharma; second, that which follows its own dharma; and third, that which is neither before, after, nor simultaneous with its own dharma. The dharma not obtained is likewise of three kinds: first, a dharma not obtained that has only a preceding non-attainment — this refers to a future dharma belonging to a sentient being that will altogether never arise, and to the very last mind-moment before entering final nirvāṇa without remainder, and the like; second, a dharma not obtained that has both a preceding and a following non-attainment — this refers to every other dharma belonging to a sentient being, as appropriate; and third, a dharma not obtained that has no preceding, following, or simultaneous non-attainment at all, and yet does have non-attainment — this refers to analytical and non-analytical cessation. There is definitely no non-attainment simultaneous with its own dharma, since whenever a dharma capable of being obtained becomes currently manifest, it must necessarily already have an attainment, while whatever is not the kind of dharma capable of being obtained has neither attainment nor non-attainment at all. Nor is there any non-attainment that occurs only afterward, since it is not the case that one has, from beginningless time onward, always already possessed a given dharma — once one has not yet relinquished it, its own class must necessarily arise and eventually be exhausted. Non-attainment, however, is by nature weak, and is only ever possessed in the present: the very instant it is obtained it is already relinquished, and it continuously persists during the period before the dharma is obtained and after it has already been relinquished

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That analytical and non-analytical cessation sit outside this entire temporal taxonomy yet still count as having attainment is a reminder of how differently the unconditioned behaves in this system: an ordinary conditioned dharma's attainment is pinned to a specific moment relative to the dharma itself, while cessation's attainment floats free of that constraint entirely, tracking instead the path or the basis that draws it forth.

5. The Fifteen Attainments of the Path of Seeing, and the Causal Network Among Them

12. Closing Questions: Cultivation, Cognition, and the Strange Case of Attainment Without Non-Attainment

A final cluster of questions closes the fascicle: whether a dharma's own duty to be cultivated, abandoned, or wearied of always transfers to its attainment (yes, with analytical cessation as the single recurring exception); how many consciousnesses, knowledges, and latent defilements track attainment versus non-attainment; and a set of striking edge cases — relinquishing every attainment at final nirvāṇa without thereby gaining any non-attainment, and dharmas like non-analytical cessation that by their very nature have attainment but can never have non-attainment at all.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That entering final nirvāṇa without remainder relinquishes attainment without producing non-attainment is a quietly profound closing image: non-attainment itself depends on a continuing basis capable of failing to possess something, so the very event that ends all possession also removes the condition under which non-possession could ever be meaningfully asserted — leaving, in the end, neither having nor not-having, but simply nothing left to ask the question of.
