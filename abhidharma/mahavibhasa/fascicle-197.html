Conditions, Contact, and the Classification of Everything

Fascicle 197 continues the Nyāsa of Cognition (智納息第四之二) with the object- and dominant-conditions, an ingenious defense of mental contact as a genuine "threefold combination" despite its faculty, object, and consciousness belonging to different time-periods, and a debate over the true intrinsic nature of restraint of the sense-faculties that closes with the position of two named historical arhat-brothers from Kaśmīra. The fascicle's second half is a vast combinatorial exercise, classifying every dharma twice over by systematically excepting one category after another — the four truths, matter, the visible, the contaminated, the conditioned, past and future, wholesome and unwholesome, and more — before closing with the claim that a single element, sense-field, and aggregate together subsume everything without remainder. This page carries Ru‑Yi's complete translation.

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

The fascicle's second half is dominated by two long, formulaic exclusion sequences — "excepting X and the dharma-sense-field" and "excepting X and dharmas certain not to arise" — run across the same roughly twenty paired categories each time. The translation preserves this repetition faithfully rather than compressing it, since the repetition itself is part of what the treatise is demonstrating. Terms established across fascicles 93–196 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. The Object-Condition Is Never Absent

Mind and mental factors are always fixed with respect to some object — but fixed how precisely? The treatise weighs whether this fixity operates at the level of the sense-field as a whole, at the level of a specific color such as blue, or at the level of the individual moment, settling the question by considering what would happen to the store of dharmas waiting to arise if fixity ran too fine-grained.

2. The Dominant Condition Is Never Absent

If the combination of conditions for a dharma's arising and ceasing is never actually absent, why do dharmas not arise and cease constantly? Vasumitra's answer distinguishes the combination that produces arising from the combination that produces ceasing, illustrated by the memorable image of a man crushed beneath a collapsing embankment, unable even to rise a second time before being crushed again.

Chinese Source

若法與彼法作增上。或時此法與彼法非增上耶。答無時非增上。問何故作此論。答為止愚於增上緣性執增上緣非實有者意。顯增上緣體性實有故作斯論。問緣和合故諸法生滅。此緣無有不和合時。諸法云何不恒生滅。尊者世友作如是言。諸法生滅和合各異。謂餘緣和合故諸法生。餘緣和合故諸法滅。是故不恒生滅。復次此法生已餘法隨生有多剎那次第隣逼。是故無有重生功能如人墮涯隤壤所壓欲起復壓。彼人爾時尚不能動何況得起。諸法亦然。是故無有恒生滅過。餘義廣說如雜蘊智納息。諸意觸彼一切三和合觸耶。乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答欲止他宗顯己義故。謂或有執。心所則心。

3. Mental Contact as a Threefold Combination

A genuine puzzle: eye-contact and the other four sense-contacts are easily called a "combination" of faculty, object, and consciousness, since all three arise simultaneously — but mental contact's faculty belongs to the past while its consciousness belongs to the present. The treatise resolves this by redefining combination itself: not mere simultaneity, but cooperation without conflict toward a single result, a redefinition credited to the Venerable Ghoṣa.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Rather than abandon the claim that mental contact is a genuine "combination," the treatise redefines what combination itself means — shifting the criterion from simultaneous arising to cooperative function toward one shared result. It is a clean example of a general concept flexing to protect a specific doctrinal commitment the tradition was unwilling to give up.

4. Conceit as Self-Grasping and Non-Tranquility; Karma and Restraint

Conceit earns its place in a chapter nominally devoted to views because it alone, among every affliction, operates in a manner resembling views — and separately, in a manner resembling every affliction's non-tranquility. A further tetralemma then distinguishes karma from restraint and non-restraint of the faculties, opening the question of what restraint of the faculties actually consists in.

9. A Single Element, Sense-Field, and Aggregate That Subsumes Everything

The fascicle closes by pulling back from the combinatorics to a unifying claim: the dharma-element, the mental sense-field, and the aggregate of matter, taken together, subsume every dharma without remainder — the same totality of reality viewed through three different classificatory lenses at once.

Chinese Source

頗有一界一處一蘊攝一切法耶。答有。一界謂法界。一處謂意處。

Ru-Yi Translation

Is there a single element, a single sense-field, and a single aggregate that subsumes every dharma? Answer: there is. The single element is the dharma-element; the single sense-field is the mental sense-field;

一蘊謂色蘊。如是則攝一切法盡。所以者何。一切法不出五事。謂色心心所法不相應行無為色蘊攝色。意處攝心。法處攝餘。是故攝一切法。復次一切法不出十八界。於中色蘊攝十色界。意處攝七心界。法界攝法界故攝一切法。復次一切法皆入蘊界處中。此三展轉相攝。謂色蘊攝十色界十色處法界法處少分。意處攝七心界。識蘊法界攝法處受想行蘊色蘊少分。是故此三攝一切法。

and the single aggregate is the aggregate of matter. In this way, every dharma is thereby subsumed entirely. Why is this? Every dharma does not exceed five kinds of thing — namely, matter, mind, mental factors, formations dissociated from mind, and the unconditioned. The aggregate of matter subsumes matter; the mental sense-field subsumes mind; the dharma-sense-field subsumes the rest — it is for this reason that every dharma is subsumed. Furthermore, every dharma does not exceed the eighteen elements: among these, the aggregate of matter subsumes the ten material elements, the mental sense-field subsumes the seven mental elements, and the dharma-element subsumes the dharma-element — it is for this reason that every dharma is subsumed. Furthermore, every dharma enters entirely within the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields, and these three mutually subsume one another in turn — that is, the aggregate of matter subsumes the ten material elements, the ten material sense-fields, and a portion of the dharma-element and dharma-sense-field; the mental sense-field subsumes the seven mental elements; the aggregate of consciousness and the dharma-element subsume the dharma-sense-field, the aggregates of feeling, perception, and formations, and a portion of the aggregate of matter — it is for this reason that these three subsume every dharma.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

After dozens of paragraphs subtracting one category from the whole of reality at a time, the fascicle's real payoff is this closing move: the same totality can be carved up by the five-thing scheme, by the eighteen elements, or by the mutual overlap of aggregates, elements, and sense-fields, and all three carvings agree perfectly. The preceding combinatorics were never really about exclusion for its own sake — they were stress-testing one coordinate system against reality from every angle to confirm it holds.

Fascicle 197 Complete

This closes fascicle 197 of Ru‑Yi's translation. The fascicle opens with the object-condition and the dominant condition, both shown to be constantly present without exception, before turning to a genuine puzzle: how mental contact, whose faculty belongs to the past while its consciousness belongs to the present, can still be called a contact of the "threefold combination" — resolved by redefining combination itself as cooperation toward one result rather than mere simultaneity. The treatise then analyzes conceit as a form of self-grasping and non-tranquility resembling views among the afflictions, works through a tetralemma distinguishing karma from restraint and non-restraint of the faculties, and surveys a remarkable range of theories on what restraint of the faculties actually consists in, closing with the position of two named historical arhat-brothers from Kaśmīra. A further tetralemma on possession and non-possession leads into the fascicle's dominant feature: two vast, parallel combinatorial sequences that classify every dharma by systematically excepting one paired category after another — the four noble truths, matter and the immaterial, the visible and invisible, the resistant and non-resistant, the contaminated and uncontaminated, the conditioned and unconditioned, the three time-periods, the three moral qualities, dharmas bound to each realm, and dharmas grouped by which stage of the path abandons them — specifying exactly how many elements, sense-fields, and aggregates subsume what remains in each case, twice arriving at an exclusion so total that nothing is left to classify at all. The fascicle closes by showing that a single element, sense-field, and aggregate together subsume every dharma without remainder. Fascicle 198 continues within the Nyāsa of Cognition.
