The Views Aggregate Opens

Fascicle 187 closes the Concentration Aggregate (定蘊第七) and opens the eighth and final major division of the treatise, the Views Aggregate (見蘊第八), with its very first Nyāsa: the Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa (念住納息第一之一). The three kinds of mindfulness-foundation — intrinsic nature, mixed, and objective support — are distinguished and matched to different practitioners; only the mixed kind, accomplished through cultivation, taking dharmas as its object, actually severs affliction; wisdom is established as its true nature though it is named for mindfulness; and the fascicle closes by asking why body is contemplated first and dharmas last, and what it means for each foundation to be perfected. This page carries Ru‑Yi's complete translation.

1. The Three Kinds of Mindfulness-Foundation

Opening the eighth and final major division of the treatise — the Views Aggregate (見蘊第八) — with its first Nyāsa, the Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa, the text distinguishes three kinds of mindfulness-foundation grounded in three separate sūtra passages: the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature (wisdom alone), the mixed mindfulness-foundation (wisdom together with its conjoined aids), and the mindfulness-foundation of objective support (wisdom together with everything it can take as object) — and explains why every explanatory sentence in the canon collapses back to intrinsic nature alone, the one kind that can never be lost or corrupted.

Chinese Source

有四念住。謂身受心法念住。如是等章。及解章義既領會已。應廣分別。問何故作此論。答為欲解釋契經義故。如契經說。有四念住。謂身念住。乃至法念住。雖作是說而不分別。若修身念住彼受耶。乃至廣說。彼經是此論所依根本。彼所不分別者。今盡應說故作斯論。然佛說有三種念住。一自性念住。二相雜念住。三所緣念住。於何處說自性念住耶。答如契經說。有一趣道能令有情清淨超滅憂苦。謂四念住。何等為四。謂於身循身觀念住。乃至於法循法觀念住。復何處說相雜念住耶。答如契經說。若有說。善法聚者即四念住。是為正說。所以者何。淳具圓滿善法聚者唯四念住。何等為四。謂於身循身觀。乃至廣說。復何處說所緣念住耶。答如契經說。若有說。一切法即四念住。是為正說。所以者何。具足攝受一切法者唯四念住。何等為四。謂於身循身觀念住。乃至廣說。此中三經標句如其次第。別說自性相雜所緣念住。釋句皆說自性念住。

Ru-Yi Translation

There are four mindfulness-foundations — namely, the mindfulness-foundations of the body, of feeling, of mind, and of dharmas. Such chapter headings as this, once their meaning has been grasped, are to be analyzed at length. Question: why is this treatise composed? Answer: it is in order to explain the meaning of a sūtra passage — as a sūtra states, there are four mindfulness-foundations, namely the mindfulness-foundation of the body, and so on up to the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas; although the sūtra speaks in this way, it does not analyze whether one who cultivates the mindfulness-foundation of the body thereby cultivates that of feeling, and so on at length — that sūtra is the root on which this treatise depends, and what it leaves unanalyzed must now be stated in full, and it is for this reason that this treatise is composed. Now, the Buddha spoke of three kinds of mindfulness-foundation: first, the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature; second, the mixed mindfulness-foundation; and third, the mindfulness-foundation of objective support. Where is the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature spoken of? Answer: as a sūtra states, there is a single path leading to the purification of sentient beings, transcending grief and sorrow — namely, the four mindfulness-foundations. What are the four? Contemplating the body by way of the body as a mindfulness-foundation, and so on up to contemplating dharmas by way of dharmas as a mindfulness-foundation. Where, further, is the mixed mindfulness-foundation spoken of? Answer: as a sūtra states — if it is said that the accumulation of wholesome dharmas is precisely the four mindfulness-foundations, this is the correct statement, for why is this? Because the accumulation of wholesome dharmas, pure and perfectly complete, is precisely the four mindfulness-foundations alone. What are the four? Contemplating the body by way of the body, and so on at length. Where, further, is the mindfulness-foundation of objective support spoken of? Answer: as a sūtra states — if it is said that every dharma is precisely the four mindfulness-foundations, this is the correct statement, for why is this? Because that which fully subsumes every dharma is precisely the four mindfulness-foundations alone. What are the four? Contemplating the body by way of the body as a mindfulness-foundation, and so on at length. Here, the topic-sentences of these three sūtras, each in its own order, speak separately of the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature, the mixed mindfulness-foundation, and the mindfulness-foundation of objective support; while the explanatory sentences all speak of the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature,

since the words "contemplate by way of" all indicate wisdom alike. Question: why is it that the World-Honored One, in the topic-sentences, speaks now of the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature, now of the mixed mindfulness-foundation, and now of the mindfulness-foundation of objective support, yet, in the explanatory sentences, speaks only of the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature? Answer: it is in order that the ultimate mindfulness-foundation should suffer no loss or corruption. Mindfulness-foundation is of two kinds: first, ultimate; and second, conventional. The mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature is the ultimate; the rest are conventional. The ultimate mindfulness-foundation can never, at any time, be lost or corrupted, and it is for this reason that the Buddha, in the explanatory sentences, speaks throughout of intrinsic nature. Some say: it is by the power of the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature that the mixed and the objective-support kinds are named mindfulness-foundation at all. Some say: it is in order to display that all three kinds of mindfulness-foundation alike have wisdom as their intrinsic nature — that is, this single nature of wisdom, when it operates through the aid of conjoined dharmas mixed together with it, is precisely what is named the mixed mindfulness-foundation; and when, by the power of its objective support, it is able to observe comprehensively, it is precisely what is named the mindfulness-foundation of objective support. Since the Buddha, in the sūtras, spoke of these three kinds of mindfulness-foundation, the Ābhidharmikas, too, everywhere compose their treatises with reference to these three kinds of mindfulness-foundation. How is this known to be so? As it is said: what is the mindfulness-foundation of the body? It is the wisdom that takes the body as its object; and so on up to: what is the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas? It is the wisdom that takes dharmas as its object — this is what is meant by speaking of the locus of the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature, and this is precisely what the sūtra states as "there is a single path," and so on at length. As it is said: what is the mindfulness-foundation of the body? It is the contaminated and uncontaminated wholesomeness produced by the path predominantly concerned with the body; and so on up to: what is the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas? It is the contaminated and uncontaminated wholesomeness produced by the path predominantly concerned with dharmas — this is what is meant by speaking of the locus of the mixed mindfulness-foundation, and this is precisely what the sūtra states as "the accumulation of wholesome dharmas is precisely the four mindfulness-foundations," and so on at length. As it is said: what is the mindfulness-foundation of the body? It is the ten form-spheres together with the form subsumed under the dharma-sphere. What is the mindfulness-foundation of feeling? It is the six classes of feeling. What is the mindfulness-foundation of mind? It is the six classes of consciousness. What is the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas? It is whatever is not subsumed under the feeling-aggregate and is not form, within the dharma-sphere — this is what is meant by speaking of the locus of the mindfulness-foundation of objective support, and this is precisely what the sūtra states as "every dharma" —

即四念住。如是阿毘達磨所說與契經相應。是故此中亦依三種念住而作論。

is precisely the four mindfulness-foundations. Thus what the Abhidharma states accords with the sūtra, and it is for this reason that this treatise, too, is composed here with reference to the three kinds of mindfulness-foundation.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The treatise's own resolution of why every explanatory sentence collapses to the intrinsic-nature mindfulness-foundation, even where the topic-sentence names the mixed or objective-support kind, is a small but genuine piece of hermeneutics: the ultimate teaching (勝義) is what cannot be lost, and Xuánzàng's Sarvāstivāda source treats that permanence itself as the reason a definition always resolves to it, regardless of which pedagogical door a given sūtra passage happens to walk through.

2. Taught According to the Practitioner

The Buddha is said to have matched each of the three mindfulness-foundations to a different kind of listener — by which one they were ignorant of, by their stage of practice, by their aspiration toward the extensive or the abbreviated, by the keenness of their faculties, and by which of three modes their wisdom was born through.

Chinese Source

問世尊為何等有情說自性念住。乃至為何等有情說所緣念住耶。答為即愚彼三種有情說三念住。謂愚自性念住者為說自性念住。乃至愚所緣念住者為說所緣念住。復次有情行有差別。謂初業等。為初業者說所緣念住。為已習行說相雜念住。為已超作意說自性念住。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: for what kind of sentient being does the World-Honored One teach the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature, and so on up to, for what kind of sentient being does he teach the mindfulness-foundation of objective support? Answer: it is precisely for the three kinds of sentient being ignorant, respectively, of each of the three kinds of mindfulness-foundation, that he teaches the three kinds of mindfulness-foundation — that is, for one ignorant of the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature, and so on up to, for one ignorant of the mindfulness-foundation of objective support, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of objective support. Furthermore, sentient beings differ in their practice — namely, the beginner and the rest: for the beginner, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of objective support; for one who has already habituated his practice, he teaches the mixed mindfulness-foundation; and for one who has already transcended mere mental application, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature.

復次有情樂欲有差別。謂廣略中樂略者為說自性念住。樂中者為說相雜念住。樂廣者為說所緣念住。復次有情根有差別。謂利中鈍為利根說自性念住。為中根說相雜念住。為鈍根說所緣念住。復次有情智有差別。謂開發生智分別生智引導生智。開發生智為說自性念住。分別生智為說相雜念住。引導生智。為說所緣念住。為如是等所化有情故。佛說此三種念住。

Furthermore, sentient beings differ in their aspiration — namely, for the extensive, the abbreviated, and the middling: for one who delights in the abbreviated, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature; for one who delights in the middling, he teaches the mixed mindfulness-foundation; and for one who delights in the extensive, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of objective support. Furthermore, sentient beings differ in their faculties — namely, the keen, the middling, and the dull: for the keen-facultied, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature; for the middling-facultied, he teaches the mixed mindfulness-foundation; and for the dull-facultied, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of objective support. Furthermore, sentient beings differ in their wisdom — namely, wisdom born of disclosure, wisdom born of analysis, and wisdom born of guidance: for wisdom born of disclosure, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature; for wisdom born of analysis, he teaches the mixed mindfulness-foundation; and for wisdom born of guidance, he teaches the mindfulness-foundation of objective support. It is for the sake of converting sentient beings of these various kinds that the Buddha taught these three kinds of mindfulness-foundation.

3. Which Mindfulness-Foundation Actually Severs Affliction

A cascading series of eliminations narrows down, from the three kinds of mindfulness-foundation, to the three kinds of wisdom that constitute the mixed kind, to the four objects of the kind accomplished through cultivation, to the mixed and unmixed objects of the dharma-mindfulness-foundation itself — establishing, at each stage, exactly why only the narrowest category is capable of severing affliction, while insisting that every broader category still serves a genuine, if preparatory, function.

Chinese Source

問此三念住誰斷煩惱。答唯相雜念住能斷煩惱非餘。問何故自性念住不能斷煩惱耶。答若離助伴唯慧不能斷煩惱故。問何故所緣念住不能斷煩惱耶。答彼作意普散故。唯總略所緣作意能斷煩惱。問何故相雜念住能斷煩惱耶。答具二緣故。謂攝受助伴故。及總略所緣作意故。問若爾。修餘念住應成無用。答彼能引發相雜念住非為無用。有說。斷有二種。一暫時斷。二究竟斷。修餘念住能暫時斷故非無用。相雜念住復有三種。謂聞思修所成差別。問此三何者能斷煩惱。答修所成能斷煩惱非餘。問何故聞所成不能斷煩惱耶。答此必依名乃於義轉。唯不待名於義轉道能斷煩惱。問何故思所成不能斷煩惱耶。答由此作意是不定地所攝故。唯定地所攝道能斷煩惱。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: of these three mindfulness-foundations, which one severs affliction? Answer: only the mixed mindfulness-foundation is able to sever affliction, and none other. Question: why is the mindfulness-foundation of intrinsic nature unable to sever affliction? Answer: because wisdom alone, apart from its aids, is unable to sever affliction. Question: why is the mindfulness-foundation of objective support unable to sever affliction? Answer: because its mental application is diffuse and scattered — only a mental application that takes its object comprehensively and in summary form is able to sever affliction. Question: why is the mixed mindfulness-foundation able to sever affliction? Answer: because it is complete with two conditions — namely, that it is aided by its supporting factors, and that its mental application takes its object comprehensively and in summary form. Question: if so, then cultivating the other mindfulness-foundations ought to be useless. Answer: they are able to draw forth the mixed mindfulness-foundation, and are therefore not useless. Some say: severance is of two kinds — first, temporary severance; and second, final severance. Cultivating the other mindfulness-foundations is able to bring about temporary severance, and is therefore not useless. The mixed mindfulness-foundation is, moreover, of three further kinds — namely, distinguished as accomplished through hearing, through reflection, and through cultivation. Question: of these three, which one is able to sever affliction? Answer: only that accomplished through cultivation is able to sever affliction, and none other. Question: why is that accomplished through hearing unable to sever affliction? Answer: because it necessarily operates upon its meaning only by depending on a name — only a path that operates upon its meaning without depending on a name is able to sever affliction. Question: why is that accomplished through reflection unable to sever affliction? Answer: because this mental application is subsumed under a ground not fixed in concentration — only a path subsumed under a ground fixed in concentration is able to sever affliction.

Question: if so, then cultivating the other two kinds ought to be useless. Answer: they are able to draw forth that accomplished through cultivation — that is, that accomplished through hearing is able to draw forth that accomplished through reflection, and that accomplished through reflection is able to draw forth that accomplished through cultivation, and that accomplished through cultivation is able to sever affliction — and they are therefore not useless. Some say: severance is of two kinds, to be explained at length as before. Some say: because each of them takes each single aggregate separately as its object — only a path that takes the four or five aggregates comprehensively as its object, or that takes what is apart from any aggregate, is able to sever affliction. 4. Enumerating the Mindfulness-Foundations, and Why Exactly Four

As with the divine eye in the preceding fascicle, mindfulness-foundation admits of being counted in one, two, three, four, five, six, eight, nine, twelve, eighteen, twenty-four, thirty-six, or seventy-two ways — before the treatise asks why the Buddha nonetheless consistently teaches exactly four, answering with six alternative but structurally parallel explanations: the four inverted views, the four nutriments, the four consciousness-stations, the five aggregates, the four non-cultivations, and concordance with the four cultivations.

Ru-Yi Translation

As with the view of self and the view of what belongs to a self, so, too, with ignorance regarding self and ignorance regarding what belongs to a self. Some say: "dwelling in the contemplation of the body by way of the body internally" counteracts craving for self; "dwelling in the contemplation of the body by way of the body externally" counteracts craving for one's possessions; and "dwelling in the contemplation of the body by way of the body both internally and externally" counteracts both kinds of craving together — and so on up to dharmas, in just the same way. Question: at what point should the mindfulness-foundation of the body, and so on up to the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas, be said to be perfected? Answer: perfection is to be known by two conditions: first, analysis of the object; and second, the increase of wholesome roots. "Analysis of the object" means that, at a given time, one is able to analyze the object down to the very moment and the very atom, or is able to analyze the object solely by the moment. "The increase of wholesome roots" means proceeding from the inferior to give rise to the middling, and from the middling to give rise to the superior. It is by this that the perfection of mindfulness-foundation should be known. Some say: perfection should be known by the shifting of effortful practice — that is, the yogic practitioner first analyzes the body; having analyzed the body, he shifts the discerning wisdom that analyzed the body and next analyzes feeling; having analyzed feeling, he shifts the discerning wisdom that analyzed feeling and next analyzes mind; having analyzed mind, he shifts the discerning wisdom that analyzed mind and analyzes dharmas — just as a farmer channels water to irrigate his fields: once the first plot is full, he channels the water to irrigate the second; once the second is full, he channels it to irrigate the third; and once the third is full, he channels it to irrigate the fourth; it is the same here. Some say: perfection should be known by the accomplishment of the mark of enmity. The mark of enmity is of two kinds: first, that which causes one to cease delighting; and second, that which causes anger to arise. Here, only that which causes one to cease delighting is spoken of as the mark of enmity — that is, the yogic practitioner, having analyzed the body, thereupon ceases to delight in the body, yet still delights in feeling and the rest; having analyzed feeling, he once again ceases to delight in feeling, yet still delights in mind and the rest; having analyzed mind, he once again ceases to delight in mind, yet still delights in dharmas; and, having analyzed dharmas, he thereupon ceases to delight in any objective realm whatsoever — this should be understood, and it is at that moment that mindfulness-foundation is perfected.

Fascicle 187 Complete

This closes fascicle 187 of Ru‑Yi's translation. Fascicle 187 opens the eighth and final major division of the treatise, the Views Aggregate (見蘊第八), with the first part of its Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa (念住納息第一之一): the three kinds of mindfulness-foundation — intrinsic nature, mixed, and objective support — each matched to a different kind of practitioner; a cascading analysis narrowing down to the single kind, accomplished through cultivation and taking dharmas as its object, that is actually able to sever affliction; the many ways of enumerating the mindfulness-foundations and the six parallel explanations for why exactly four are taught; wisdom established as intrinsic nature though named for mindfulness, through nine distinct images; a dozen answers for why each foundation is named for what it is, and why only the fourth claims the name "dharma"; three kinds of order and several accounts of why body is contemplated first and dharmas last; the reconciliation of internal and external mindfulness-foundation through the charnel-ground contemplation as preliminary effortful practice; three distinct definitions of internal and external, including the Venerable Pārśva's identification of internal with the present moment itself; and three images of what it means for a mindfulness-foundation to be perfected.
