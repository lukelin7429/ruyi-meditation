Present and Future Cultivation, From Stream-Entry to Arhatship

Fascicle 140 completes the Grasped Nyāsa with an exhaustive ledger of exactly what is cultivated in the present versus the future at every single stage of the path — stream-entry, the once-returner, the non-returner, all five supernormal powers, and finally arhatship itself — closing with why the formless realm possesses no precepts at all. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. A Cascade of Rival Doctrines to Refute

Fascicle 140 continues Part Four of the Grasped Nyāsa with an extraordinary list of rival positions the treatise sets out to refute in a single stroke — that there is no future cultivation of the path at all, that two minds can operate together, that the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment cannot all arise at a single moment, that faith and the rest exist only in uncontaminated form, that the awakening-factors and joy belong to stages they do not, that stream-enterers already possess genuine dhyāna, that patient acceptance is itself knowledge, that ordinary beings never cut off affliction, that noble ones never use a mundane path, and half a dozen further claims besides — setting up an exhaustive ledger, tracked moment by moment through every stage of the path, that will settle each dispute in turn.

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth Aggregate, the Great Elements: the Grasped Nyāsa, Chapter Four, Part Four. By the uninterrupted path realizing the fruit of stream-entry, when cultivating that path, of the four foundations of mindfulness, how many are cultivated in the present, and how many in the future? And so on, in full. Question: why compose this treatise? Answer: in order to refute a rival doctrine and to establish our own correct doctrine. For some hold that there is no future cultivation at all, since one could hardly attain something not yet actually done — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that future cultivation does indeed exist, even though what has not yet arisen has already arisen in kind, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that two minds can operate together at once, since seeing and hearing and the rest occur simultaneously — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that no two minds ever operate together, since a single moment's swift turning merely resembles simultaneity without truly being so, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that the four right efforts and the rest cannot all function at the same single moment, since their causes differ — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that these can indeed be obtained simultaneously, their four kinds sharing a single, undifferentiated substance, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that faith and the rest exist only in their uncontaminated form, since the sūtra says that ordinary beings lack the faith-faculty and the rest — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to make clear that faith and the rest are common to both the contaminated and the uncontaminated, the sūtra speaking only of the uncontaminated kind specifically, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that the faculties and powers differ in their own substance, on account of their differing degrees of superiority and inferiority — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that, though their positions differ, their functioning as faculty or power shares a single, undifferentiated substance, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that the awakening-factors are common to the contaminated as well, since the sūtra speaks of cultivating the mindfulness-awakening-factor together with the contemplation of impurity — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that the awakening-factors are uncontaminated only, the sūtra's own "together" referring to a shared underlying cause, not genuine simultaneity, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that joy is present at the access-stage, since the sūtra speaks of relying on joy to cut off the displeasure of renunciation — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to make clear that the access-stage lacks joy, the sūtra's own language of "already cut off" and "about to be cut off" both being loosely called "cutting off," that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that correct speech, correct action, and correct livelihood cannot occur at the same single moment, since a single instant cannot contain two bodily or verbal acts — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that all three precepts arise together, given rise to by the three faculties at once, and can indeed be obtained simultaneously, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that stream-enterers and once-returners have also already attained genuine dhyāna — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that neither has attained it, since neither has yet become free of sensory desire, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that patient acceptance is itself already knowledge — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that patient acceptance is not knowledge, since it has not yet reached a fully determinate judgment with respect to the truths as its object, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that ordinary beings do not cut off afflictions, since they have not yet seen the truths — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to make clear that ordinary beings, too, cut off afflictions, seeing what is coarse and the rest, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that noble ones never cut off afflictions by means of a mundane path — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that noble ones freely make use of whichever path serves, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that the higher grounds also possess the correct-intention path-factor — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that the higher grounds lack this factor, since they lack discursive thought, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that the formless realm also possesses uncontaminated precepts — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to show that it lacks precepts altogether, since it lacks form, that this treatise is composed. Others, further, hold that the three concentrations differ only in meaning while sharing a single substance — it is in order to refute this attachment, and to make clear that their substance, too, differs, since their respective aspects differ, that this treatise is composed. It is in order to refute these various rival doctrines, each in turn, and to make clear the correct teaching, that this treatise is composed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Cataloging seventeen distinct rival positions before answering a single one of them is a striking rhetorical choice: rather than refuting each doctrine in isolation, the treatise signals that a single, unified technical ledger — tracking exactly what is cultivated in the present versus the future at every stage of the path — is powerful enough to dissolve all seventeen disputes at once, each one turning out to be a special case of the very same underlying confusion about how cultivation actually works.

2. Stream-Entry's Own Ledger: One Mindfulness in Hand, Four Waiting in the Future

The ledger begins with the uninterrupted path that realizes the very fruit of stream-entry itself: of the four foundations of mindfulness, only one — the mixed-object foundation of mindfulness of dharmas, since it is here that the truths are actually seen — is cultivated in the present, while all four exist as future cultivation at once, refuting both the claim that no future cultivation exists and the claim that multiple minds could somehow operate together to cultivate all four in the present simultaneously.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

By the uninterrupted path realizing the fruit of stream-entry, when cultivating that path — of the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right efforts, the four bases of supernormal power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven awakening-factors, the eight path-factors, the four dhyānas, the four immeasurables, the four formless attainments, the eight liberations, the eight stations of mastery, the ten totalities, the eight kinds of knowledge, and the three concentrations — how many are cultivated in the present, and how many in the future? Answer: of the foundations of mindfulness, one is cultivated in the present, four in the future. Of the right efforts and the bases of power, four are cultivated in both present and future. Of the faculties and powers, five are cultivated in both present and future. Of the awakening-factors, six are cultivated in both present and future. Of the path-factors, eight are cultivated in both present and future. There is no dhyāna, no immeasurable, no formless attainment, no liberation, no station of mastery, no totality, no knowledge, no concentration — cultivated in present or future. As for the foundations of mindfulness, one in the present and four in the future — this refutes both the doctrine of two minds operating together and the doctrine denying future cultivation. For, were two minds to operate together at a single moment, then all four foundations of mindfulness would have to be cultivated in the present at once — yet the four foundations of mindfulness never operate together, since a single mind, associated with only one kind of wisdom, cannot possess all four kinds of wisdom at once.

Nor can these four be established as sharing a single substance, differing only in aspect and object, since a single continuum cannot give rise to many minds arising together — lest this imply the existence of many sentient beings within a single continuum. And, if there were no such thing as future cultivation, then the wholesome quality being cultivated would have no capacity to increase or expand, and undertaking extensive preparatory effort would be entirely wasted labor, exerting much effort for little gain; further, at the moment of first attaining Buddhahood, one would not yet possess every virtue in full — and it is in order to avoid faults such as these that future cultivation does indeed exist. The future, being vast in scope, allows the full cultivation of all four at once, while the present's own operative force is capable of drawing forth the corresponding future kind of dharma, bringing it into manifestation. As for "one in the present" — this refers to the foundation of mindfulness of dharmas with a mixed object, since it is here that the path of seeing the truths occurs. As for the right efforts and the bases of power, four in both present and future — this refutes the claim that the right efforts and the bases of power cannot exist together at a single moment: the substance of right diligence is a single thing, its function likened to a lamp, which, at a single moment, possesses four distinct functions at once; and a single concentration, arising from four distinct causes, receives, accordingly, four distinct names,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That a single present moment can seed all four future foundations of mindfulness at once, without needing four simultaneous minds to do so, elegantly resolves what looks like a genuine paradox: cultivation, on this account, is never a matter of the present containing everything it produces, but of a single, narrow, focused act in the present having a far wider causal reach into what becomes available afterward.

3. Why the Higher Faculties Have No Room Here Yet

The same moment of realization is shown to fully activate the five faculties, five powers, seven awakening-factors, and eight path-factors all at once in both present and future — since faculty and power, despite their different names, share one undivided substance — while pointedly excluding any dhyāna, immeasurable, formless attainment, liberation, station of mastery, totality, worldly knowledge, or concentration beyond the single wishless concentration, each exclusion refuting a specific rival claim that stream-enterers already possess some higher attainment they have, in fact, not yet reached.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The insistence that faculty and power name the very same underlying reality under two different aspects — one describing a capacity's power to arise, the other its power to overcome opposition — is a small but important piece of conceptual economy: rather than positing five additional entities alongside the five faculties, the treatise simply gives the same five a second name for a second function, refusing to multiply substances beyond what the phenomena actually require.

4. Once-Returner Through the Noble Path Directly

The ledger turns to a being who has already become "doubly free" of the desire realm's own defilement — having cut off six of its nine grades beforehand through the ordinary six-aspect method available even to unenlightened beings — and who enters the noble path directly to realize the fruit of the once-returner, carrying the identical pattern of cultivation already established for stream-entry, with the sole addition of the wishless concentration now present in both time-frames rather than merely a single moment.

That an ordinary, unenlightened being can independently cut off six of nine grades of desire-realm defilement before ever entering the noble path at all is a striking concession about the reach of purely mundane spiritual effort: enlightenment's own technical apparatus, on this account, is not required to make substantial progress — it is required only to finish what ordinary discipline has already carried most of the way there.

5. Once-Returner Two Ways: The Mundane Path and the Uncontaminated Path

Two entirely separate routes from stream-entry to the fruit of the once-returner are now compared side by side: the mundane path, cultivated with only mundane knowledge in the present and lacking any awakening-factor or path-factor there at all, precisely because the sūtra's language of joint cultivation with the contemplation of impurity refers only to a shared underlying cause, never true simultaneity — against the uncontaminated path, which activates every awakening- and path-factor immediately, refuting outright the claim that the contaminated could ever count as genuine awakening.

18. Why the Formless Realm Has No Precepts at All

The fascicle closes with a rich metaphysical digression explaining why the formless realm's own path-factors number only four, missing correct speech, action, and livelihood entirely: precept itself is form, and the formless realm simply has no great elements to produce it — yet, since uncontaminated precept, unlike its contaminated counterpart, is never bound to any particular realm but merely produced by whatever great elements happen to underlie its own bodily basis, and the formless realm has no bodily basis of any kind, uncontaminated precept, too, finds nothing there to depend upon, leaving that realm entirely without precept in either its contaminated or its uncontaminated form.
