The Nāga-Roar Beside the Mandākinī Pool

Fascicle 185 continues the Single Practice Nyāsa (一行納息第五之三): a fourfold analysis of cultivating the three concentrations in pairs, fetter-abandonment fixed by counteraction, the four modes of mental application by which one enters the fixed nature of the noble ones, the mindfulness-foundations of the cognitions of exhaustion and non-arising, a denial that the first two dhyānas possess any distinct uncontaminated pleasure-faculty, the five modes of rising from a meditative equipoise, a refutation of the Vibhajyavādins on arhatship at the very peak of existence, and the celebrated Vinaya episode of Mahāmaudgalyāyana's nāga-roar. 1. Cultivation of the Three Concentrations: The Fourfold Analysis

The fascicle opens the second half of the Single Practice Nyāsa (一行納息) with a systematic tetralemma analysis of cultivation (修) across the three concentrations — empty (空), wishless (無願), and signless (無相) — treating cultivation as attainment (得修) and habituation (習修) in every pairing, and closing with a careful specification of exactly who is excluded from cultivation altogether: every ordinary worldling, every defiled or neutral mind, and those absorbed in the attainments of no-perception or of cessation.

Answer: it is in order to refute the denial of the past and future two times, and the position that there is no future cultivation, displaying that the past and future times exist and that there is indeed future cultivation, that this treatise is composed. Cultivation is of four kinds: first, cultivation as attainment; second, cultivation as habituation; third, cultivation as counteraction; and fourth, cultivation as elimination. As for "every ordinary worldling": since the three concentrations spoken of here are exclusively uncontaminated, and ordinary worldlings possess none of these at all, there is no possibility of cultivation for them. As for "a defiled mind": a defiled mind is of a nature conducive to regression, conjoined with heaviness and indolence; only a mind of a nature conducive to advancement, conjoined with buoyancy and diligence, is capable of cultivation. As for "a neutral mind": a neutral mind is not firm, not stable, not substantial, feeble like a rotten seed; only a firm, stable, substantial, and vigorous mind is capable of cultivation. As for "within the attainment of no-perception or of cessation": these lack mind altogether, and only a state possessing mind is capable of cultivation. As for "reborn in the heaven of no-perception": some say that, once reborn there, one never gives rise to a wholesome mind at any time; some say that, although one may give rise to a wholesome mind there, it does not serve as a basis for cultivation. 2. Fetter-Abandonment by Counteraction

The treatise turns to a second determination of the three concentrations' functions — not by simultaneous operation, but by fixed counteraction (對治決定) — mapping which fetters are severed by which concentration alone, by pairs, or by all three together, and reconciling this with the mundane path's role in an ordinary worldling's own abandonment.

It was only with respect to two moments of mind that he actually gave rise to deliberate, discriminating awareness — namely, the mind at the very moment of first entering concentration, and the mind at the very moment of finally emerging from it — while, with respect to the succession of mental moments in between, he did not examine the matter with careful discrimination, and it was for this reason that he spoke as he did — and there is, in this, no fault at all.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The resolution offered for Mahāmaudgalyāyana's own account is worth noticing for its method: rather than declaring the Vinaya passage corrupt (one view the treatise reports and sets aside) or simply asserting his infallibility, it reconstructs the psychological mechanics of a mind moving so swiftly between nine successive absorptions that only its first and last moments register as deliberate, discriminated awareness — turning an apparent contradiction into a fact about the phenomenology of very rapid meditative transition.
