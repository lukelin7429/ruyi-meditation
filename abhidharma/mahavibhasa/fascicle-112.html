Three Roots of Good and Evil

Fascicle 112 opens the entire Karma Aggregate with the three kinds of bad conduct and the three unwholesome roots — craving, aversion, and delusion — then mirrors this precisely with the three kinds of good conduct and the three wholesome roots. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 112 opens the Karma Aggregate (業蘊第四), the fourth of the treatise's eight great aggregates, with the Bad-Conduct Nyāsa (惡行納息第一之一). Terms established across fascicles 93–111 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. The Karma Aggregate Opens: Three Kinds of Bad Conduct

Fascicle 112 opens the entire Karma Aggregate — the treatise's fourth of eight — with its own opening Bad-Conduct Nyāsa, asking whether the three kinds of bad conduct gather in the three unwholesome roots, or the reverse. The three kinds of bad conduct are named directly from scripture: bodily bad conduct (cutting off life, taking what is not given, and sexual misconduct), verbal bad conduct (false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, and idle chatter), and mental bad conduct (covetousness, malice, and wrong view) — though the Bhagavān, the treatise notes, speaks solely of the root path of karma itself, leaving the preparatory and subsequent actions surrounding it to this treatise's own broader definition.

Chinese Source

三惡行三不善根。為前攝後後攝前耶。如是等章及解章義。既領會已。次應廣釋。問何故作此論。答為欲分別契經義故。如契經說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Do the three kinds of bad conduct gather in the three unwholesome roots, or do the three unwholesome roots gather in the three kinds of bad conduct? Such chapters as these, together with their own explanatory meaning — having already comprehended them, they ought now to be fully explained in turn. One asks: for what reason is this treatise composed? The answer: it is in order to distinguish the meaning of the scripture — that is, as the scripture states:

有三惡行三不善根。契經雖作是說而不廣辯。亦未說三惡行攝三不善根。三不善根攝三惡行。契經是此論根本。彼未說者今應說之。

there exist three kinds of bad conduct and three unwholesome roots. Although the scripture makes this same statement, it does not broadly discuss it, nor has it stated that the three kinds of bad conduct gather in the three unwholesome roots, or that the three unwholesome roots gather in the three kinds of bad conduct. The scripture is this treatise's own root, and whatever it has not yet stated ought now to be stated,

故作斯論。

and it is thereby that this treatise is composed.

三惡行者。謂身惡行。語惡行。意惡行。云何身等惡行。如世尊說。何者身惡行。謂斷生命。不與取。欲邪行。何者語惡行。謂虛誑語。離間語。麁惡語。雜穢語。何者意惡行。謂貪欲瞋恚邪見。

The three kinds of bad conduct are: bodily bad conduct, verbal bad conduct, and mental bad conduct. What, precisely, are bodily bad conduct, and the rest? Exactly as the Bhagavān states: what is bodily bad conduct? It refers to cutting off life, taking what is not given, and sexual misconduct. What is verbal bad conduct? It refers to false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, and idle chatter. What is mental bad conduct? It refers to covetousness, malice, and wrong view.

應知此中世尊唯說根本業道所攝惡行。不說業道加行後起所攝惡行。此發智論通說所有不善身業。若是業道所攝。若非業道所攝。

It should be understood that, here, the Bhagavān speaks solely of the bad conduct gathered within the root path of karma, not the bad conduct gathered within the preparatory action and the subsequent action of the path of karma. This *Jñānaprasthāna* speaks broadly of every unwholesome bodily karma whatsoever, whether gathered within a path of karma or not gathered within a path of karma —

如是一切名身惡行。通說所有不善語業。若是業道所攝。若非業道所攝。如是一切名語惡行。通說所有不善意業。若是業道所攝。若非業道所攝。如是一切名意惡行。

all of this, in every case, is named bodily bad conduct. It speaks broadly of every unwholesome verbal karma whatsoever, whether gathered within a path of karma or not gathered within a path of karma — all of this, in every case, is named verbal bad conduct. It speaks broadly of every unwholesome mental karma whatsoever, whether gathered within a path of karma or not gathered within a path of karma — all of this, in every case, is named mental bad conduct.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The distinction between scripture's own narrower definition (the root path of karma alone) and this treatise's own broader one (encompassing preparatory and subsequent action as well) sets the tone for the entire fascicle: scripture speaks for a general audience in terms of what is most vivid and recognizable, while the treatise's own technical apparatus insists on tracking every act that genuinely belongs to a given category, however peripheral to ordinary moral attention it might seem.

2. The Saṅgītiparyāya's Own Fuller Accounting

The *Saṅgītiparyāya* is shown expanding on scripture's own threefold list at every turn — adding conduct toward one's own wife alongside outright sexual misconduct, gathering in the preparatory and subsequent actions surrounding each path of karma, and introducing an entirely further category: conduct "drawn forth by improper reasoning," itself given rise to by improper attention, encompassing defiled-and-neutral karma and even certain neutral bodily and verbal acts performed in a manner simply improper to the occasion — before the treatise's own verdict narrows all this back down: bad conduct, properly speaking, is solely unwholesome and solely bound to the desire realm.

Chinese Source

如此論中攝諸惡行。集異門論亦作是說。故彼論言。何者身惡行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Exactly as this treatise gathers in the various kinds of bad conduct, the *Saṅgītiparyāya*, too, speaks in just the same way — that same treatise states: what is bodily bad conduct?

謂斷生命。不與取。欲邪行。如是所說隨順契經。又言復次斷生命。不與取。非梵行。如是復攝前所不攝。於自妻室所起欲行。又言。有餘不善身業。如是復攝所有業道加行後起又言。有餘諸有非理所引身業。問何者非理所引身業。前所未攝今更攝耶。答前說自性。今說等起。此誰所等起。謂非理作意。復有說者。此是一切有覆無記。及一分無覆無記身業。一切有覆無記身業者。謂初靜慮地諂愛等所起。一分無覆無記身業者。謂應如是去來。如是行住。如是坐臥。如是裁割。如是縫綴。而不如是去來行住乃至縫綴。此等身業作不應作。不應理故攝在非理所引品中。由此名身惡行。若作是說。身惡行則通不善無記欲界色界。然惡行唯不善唯欲界。是故前說者好。又彼論言。何者語惡行。謂虛誑語。離間語。麁惡語。

It refers to cutting off life, taking what is not given, and sexual misconduct — this same statement accords with the scripture. It further states: further, cutting off life, taking what is not given, and conduct that is not pure — in this way, it further gathers in what was not gathered before, namely, the very conduct of desire that arises with respect to one's own wife. It further states: there is a further unwholesome bodily karma — in this way, it further gathers in whatever belongs to the preparatory action and the subsequent action of the path of karma. It further states: there is, further, whatever bodily karma is drawn forth by improper reasoning. One asks: what, precisely, is bodily karma drawn forth by improper reasoning — is this gathering in what was not gathered before? The answer: what was stated before speaks of inherent nature; what is stated now speaks of what gives rise to it. By whom is this given rise to? Namely, by improper attention. There are, further, those who say: this refers to every defiled-and-neutral bodily karma, and a portion of the undefiled-and-neutral bodily karma. Every defiled-and-neutral bodily karma refers to what is given rise to by the flattery and craving, and the like, belonging to the ground of the first dhyāna. A portion of the undefiled-and-neutral bodily karma refers to the fact that one ought to go and come, walk and stand, sit and lie down, cut and sew, in such-and-such a way, yet one does not go and come, walk and stand, up through, cut and sew, in this same way — such bodily karma as this, performing what ought not to be performed, being improper, is gathered within the category drawn forth by improper reasoning, and it is thereby named bodily bad conduct. Were it stated in this way, bodily bad conduct would then pervade the unwholesome and the neutral, the desire realm and the form realm; yet bad conduct is solely unwholesome, solely belonging to the desire realm, and it is thereby that the position stated first is the good one. That same treatise, further, states: what is verbal bad conduct? It refers to false speech, divisive speech, and harsh speech,

雜穢語。如是所說隨順契經。又言。有餘不善語業。如是復攝所有業道加行後起。又言。有餘諸有非理所引語業。問何者非理所引語業。前所未攝今更攝耶。答前說自性。今說等起。此誰所等起。謂非理作意。復有說者。此是一切有覆無記。及一分無覆無記語業。

and idle chatter — this same statement accords with the scripture. It further states: there is a further unwholesome verbal karma — in this way, it further gathers in whatever belongs to the preparatory action and the subsequent action of the path of karma. It further states: there is, further, whatever verbal karma is drawn forth by improper reasoning. One asks: what, precisely, is verbal karma drawn forth by improper reasoning — is this gathering in what was not gathered before? The answer: what was stated before speaks of inherent nature; what is stated now speaks of what gives rise to it. By whom is this given rise to? Namely, by improper attention. There are, further, those who say: this refers to every defiled-and-neutral verbal karma, and a portion of the undefiled-and-neutral verbal karma.

一切有覆無記語業者。謂初靜慮地諂愛等所起。一分無覆無記語業者。謂應說一言二言多言男言女言非男女言去來今言。而不如是說一乃至去來今言。此等語業說不應說。不應理故攝在非理所引品中。由此名語惡行。若作是說。語惡行則通不善無記欲界色界。然惡行唯不善唯欲界。是故前說者好。又彼論言。何者意惡行。謂貪欲瞋恚邪見。如是所說隨順契經。又言。有餘不善意業。如是復攝諸不善思。又言。有餘諸有非理所引意業。問何等非理所引意業。

Every defiled-and-neutral verbal karma refers to what is given rise to by the flattery and craving, and the like, belonging to the ground of the first dhyāna. A portion of the undefiled-and-neutral verbal karma refers to the fact that one ought to speak a single word, two words, many words, a word for a man, a word for a woman, a word for neither man nor woman, or a word for what has gone, is coming, or is now — yet one does not speak in this way, up through, a word for what has gone, is coming, or is now — such verbal karma as this, speaking what ought not to be spoken, being improper, is gathered within the category drawn forth by improper reasoning, and it is thereby named verbal bad conduct. Were it stated in this way, verbal bad conduct would then pervade the unwholesome and the neutral, the desire realm and the form realm; yet bad conduct is solely unwholesome, solely belonging to the desire realm, and it is thereby that the position stated first is the good one. That same treatise, further, states: what is mental bad conduct? It refers to covetousness, malice, and wrong view — this same statement accords with the scripture. It further states: there is a further unwholesome mental karma — in this way, it further gathers in the various unwholesome forms of volition. It further states: there is, further, whatever mental karma is drawn forth by improper reasoning. One asks: what, precisely, is mental karma drawn forth by improper reasoning —

前所未攝今更攝耶。答前說自性。今說等起。此誰所等起謂非理作意。復有說者。此是一切有覆無記。及一分無覆無記意業。名非理所引意業。一切有覆無記非理所引意業者。謂欲界薩迦耶見邊執見相應意業。及色無色界一切煩惱相應意業。一分無覆無記非理所引意業者。謂諸意業起如前說。無覆無記非理所引身業語業。若作是說。意惡行則通不善及以無記。又通三界。然惡行唯不善唯欲界。

is this gathering in what was not gathered before? The answer: what was stated before speaks of inherent nature; what is stated now speaks of what gives rise to it. By whom is this given rise to? Namely, by improper attention. There are, further, those who say: this refers to every defiled-and-neutral mental karma, and a portion of the undefiled-and-neutral mental karma, named mental karma drawn forth by improper reasoning. Every defiled-and-neutral mental karma drawn forth by improper reasoning refers to the mental karma associated with the desire realm's own view of self and extreme view, and every mental karma associated with the various afflictions of the form and formless realms. A portion of the undefiled-and-neutral mental karma drawn forth by improper reasoning refers to the various mental karma that arises exactly as already stated for the undefiled-and-neutral bodily karma and verbal karma drawn forth by improper reasoning. Were it stated in this way, mental bad conduct would then pervade the unwholesome and the neutral, and, further, pervade all three realms; yet bad conduct is solely unwholesome, solely belonging to the desire realm.

是故前說者好。

It is thereby that the position stated first is the good one.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The neutral bodily and verbal acts drawn in under "improper reasoning" — walking, sitting, cutting, and sewing done simply the wrong way, without any of the outright unwholesome content one would expect from a discussion of bad conduct — are a genuinely striking expansion: even an act with no unwholesome content whatsoever, if merely performed improperly, momentarily threatens to count as bad conduct in the *Saṅgītiparyāya*'s own fuller reckoning, before the treatise's own final verdict restores a stricter and more familiar boundary.

3. The Prajñaptiśāstra's Own Fivefold Reckoning

The *Prajñaptiśāstra* is shown organizing matters differently still: bodily and verbal bad conduct each named only three or four representative kinds, yet each shown to gather in every genuine instance of that same conduct — including, memorably, the person who mutters heresies alone in an empty place, whose verbal bad conduct escapes the fourfold list simply because no other sentient being ever comes to understand what he has said — while mental bad conduct alone is treated separately, gathering in the entire own-nature of four aggregates at once, so that the whole edifice ultimately rests on nothing but the five aggregates themselves.

Chinese Source

如發智論。集異門論攝身惡行及語惡行。施設論中所說亦爾。唯除意惡行別有所攝故彼論言。問為身三惡行攝一切身惡行為一切身惡行。攝身三惡行耶。答一切攝三。非三攝一切。不攝者何。謂非斷命。以手杖等捶擊有情。及非邪行。於所應行作不淨行。起飲酒等諸放逸業。由不正知失念。受用諸飲食等。及不能避諸犯戒者。諸如是等所起身業非三所攝。問諸犯戒者無量。云何能避。雖復捨此還近彼故。答所在皆有。欲離實難。能不隨染。是為真避。故有說言。身雖在遠而隨彼習。即名親近。身雖在近不隨彼習。即名遠離。問為語四惡行攝一切語惡行。為一切語惡行攝語四惡行耶。答一切攝四。非四攝一切。不攝者何。謂如有一。獨處空閑作如是說。無有惠施。無有親愛。無有祠祀。如是等語惡行。世間有情不生領解。非四所攝。問為意三惡行攝一切意惡行。為一切意惡行攝意三惡行耶答一切攝三。非三攝一切。不攝者何。謂貪欲瞋恚邪見。俱生受想行識非三所攝彼論中意惡行攝四蘊自性。如是施設五蘊自性為諸惡行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Exactly as the *Jñānaprasthāna* and the *Saṅgītiparyāya* gather in bodily bad conduct and verbal bad conduct, so, too, does what is stated within the *Prajñaptiśāstra*, excepting solely mental bad conduct, which it gathers in separately, since that same treatise states: one asks, do the three kinds of bodily bad conduct gather in every kind of bodily bad conduct, or does every kind of bodily bad conduct gather in the three kinds of bodily bad conduct? The answer: every kind gathers in the three; the three do not gather in every kind. What is not gathered in? Namely, whatever is not cutting off life, striking a sentient being with a hand, a staff, and the like, and whatever is not sexual misconduct, engaging in impure conduct with respect to what ought to be engaged with, giving rise to various acts of heedlessness such as drinking wine, and, through lack of proper knowledge and loss of mindfulness, making use of food and drink, and the like, and being incapable of avoiding those who violate the precepts — such bodily karma as these, given rise to, is not gathered within the three. One asks: those who violate the precepts are measureless — how could one possibly avoid them all? Even were one to abandon this one, one would again draw near that one. The answer: wherever one may be, there they are — to wish to be entirely free of them is genuinely difficult; being capable of not following their own defiled conduct is what constitutes genuine avoidance. It is therefore said: though the body may be far off, if one follows their own habitual conduct, this is named drawing near; though the body may be close by, if one does not follow their own habitual conduct, this is named being far removed. One asks: do the four kinds of verbal bad conduct gather in every kind of verbal bad conduct, or does every kind of verbal bad conduct gather in the four kinds of verbal bad conduct? The answer: every kind gathers in the four; the four do not gather in every kind. What is not gathered in? Namely, exactly as there is one who, dwelling alone in an empty, secluded place, speaks in this way: there is no giving, no affection, no offering — such verbal bad conduct as this, since the various sentient beings of the world do not come to understand it, is not gathered within the four. One asks: do the three kinds of mental bad conduct gather in every kind of mental bad conduct, or does every kind of mental bad conduct gather in the three kinds of mental bad conduct? The answer: every kind gathers in the three; the three do not gather in every kind. What is not gathered in? Namely, sensation, notion, formations, and consciousness that co-arise with covetousness, malice, and wrong view — these are not gathered within the three. Within that same treatise, mental bad conduct gathers in the very own-nature of four aggregates — in this way, the own-nature of the five aggregates is posited for the various kinds of bad conduct.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The lone heretic muttering to himself in an empty place, his own verbal bad conduct somehow escaping formal classification simply because no one is present to understand it, is a genuinely curious edge case: it suggests that this tradition's own taxonomy of bad conduct is not purely a matter of the inner intention behind an act, but is also sensitive to whether that act actually lands within a shared social world capable of registering it at all.

4. Why the Treatises Differ from Scripture

A careful methodological reflection explains why the *Jñānaprasthāna* and the *Saṅgītiparyāya* consistently narrow what counts as bad conduct to its own strict inherent nature, while scripture and the *Prajñaptiśāstra* range far more broadly: the treatises speak by reliance on supreme meaning, scripture by reliance on worldly convention — the same distinction, the treatise notes, that separates provisional from definitive meaning, and the direct realization of worldly truth from the direct realization of supreme truth. A second account offers a complementary explanation: scripture gathers in whatever the world finds censurable (chiefly the root act itself, not its surrounding preparation), while the *Prajñaptiśāstra* gathers in whatever is merely admixed with bad conduct, whether or not it is bad conduct's own genuine inherent nature.

Chinese Source

問此發智論。集異門論。與佛契經。及施設論攝諸惡行。何故不同。答依二種門說諸惡行。一依世俗。二依勝義。謂佛契經及施設論。依世俗門說諸惡行。此發智論集異門論。依勝義門說諸惡行。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: for what reason do this *Jñānaprasthāna* and the *Saṅgītiparyāya* differ from the Buddha's own scripture and the *Prajñaptiśāstra* in how they gather in the various kinds of bad conduct? The answer: it is by reliance upon two kinds of approach that the various kinds of bad conduct are spoken of — the first is by reliance upon worldly convention; the second is by reliance upon supreme meaning. That is, the Buddha's own scripture and the *Prajñaptiśāstra* speak of the various kinds of bad conduct by reliance upon the approach of worldly convention; this *Jñānaprasthāna* and the *Saṅgītiparyāya* speak of the various kinds of bad conduct by reliance upon the approach of supreme meaning.

如依世俗勝義分別。如是依不了義了義。有別意趣無別意趣。有別因緣無別因緣。世俗諦現觀勝義諦現觀等。應知亦爾。復有說者。

Exactly as the distinction is made by reliance upon worldly convention and supreme meaning, so, too, should it be understood by reliance upon what is of provisional meaning and what is of definitive meaning, what carries a separate intention and what carries no separate intention, what has a separate cause and condition and what has no separate cause and condition, and the direct realization of the truth of worldly convention and the direct realization of the truth of supreme meaning, and the like. There are, further, those who say:

由三緣故所攝不同。謂自性故。相雜故。世譏嫌故。此發智論集異門論。說其自性。施設論中說其相雜。若法雖非惡行自性。而與惡行相和雜故。亦得其名。於契經中說世譏嫌。以諸世間於根本業道多生譏嫌。非於業道加行後起。由此緣故攝諸惡行經論不同。如是名為惡行自性。

it is by virtue of three conditions that what is gathered in differs — namely, by virtue of inherent nature, by virtue of admixture, and by virtue of what the world finds censurable. This *Jñānaprasthāna* and the *Saṅgītiparyāya* speak of their own inherent nature; the *Prajñaptiśāstra* speaks of their admixture — that is, even a dharma that is not, itself, the inherent nature of bad conduct, if admixed together with bad conduct, likewise earns its name. The scripture speaks of what the world finds censurable, since the various worldly people, for the greater part, give rise to censure with respect to the root path of karma, not with respect to the preparatory action and the subsequent action of the path of karma. It is by virtue of this same condition that the various kinds of bad conduct are gathered differently across the scripture and the treatises. This, then, is what is termed the own-nature of bad conduct.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Offering two entirely different, non-competing explanations for the very same textual divergence — one framed around worldly-versus-supreme meaning, the other around inherent nature, admixture, and worldly censure — is characteristic of how this tradition handles apparent inconsistency across its own authoritative sources: rather than forcing a single explanation to do all the work, it lets two genuinely different accounts stand side by side, each illuminating a different facet of why scripture and treatise need not always speak in exactly the same register.

5. Why "Bad," and Why "Conduct"?

A rich etymological cascade explains the very name "bad conduct": "bad" because these acts are to be loathed and censured, exactly as one speaks of a bad wife, bad clothing, or a bad place; "conduct" because each act traverses and relies upon its own proper domain — killing, harsh speech, and malice traversing the domain of sentient beings; stealing, sexual misconduct, and covetousness the domain of requisites; the verbal offenses the domain of names; wrong view the domain of name-and-form itself. A further account explains the peculiar skill with which bad conduct is performed: those who do evil possess a genuine adroitness, causing the world not to recognize their own wrongdoing even as they commit it.

16. Why "Good," and Why "Conduct"? The Mirror Image
