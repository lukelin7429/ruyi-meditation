Devadatta's Schism and the Ten Unwholesome Paths

Fascicle 116 closes the Bad-Conduct Nyāsa with Devadatta's schism, the Buddha's own ancient karma, and the greatest fruits of good and evil conduct, then opens the Wrong-Speech Nyāsa with a vivid survey of the ten unwholesome paths of karma traced to their roots in greed, hatred, and delusion. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 116 completes the Bad-Conduct Nyāsa (惡行納息第一之五) and opens the Wrong-Speech Nyāsa (邪語納息第二之一), moving from Devadatta's schism and the treatise's closing survey of the greatest karmic fruits into a new chapter's account of wrong livelihood and the ten unwholesome paths of karma. Terms established across fascicles 93–115 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Which Kalpa? The Verse on Schism and Avīci

Fascicle 116 opens still within the Bad-Conduct Nyāsa, resolving a technical question left hanging: the verse condemning whoever splits the saṅgha to a full eon's abiding in Avīci names a middling eon specifically — proven by the Vinaya's own prophecy that Devadatta, though bound for hell, will eventually be reborn human and attain pratyekabuddha-awakening beyond even Śāriputra's reach, meaning his eon-long sentence must be escapable before a middling eon runs its full course. The Venerable Vasumitra explains the terminology precisely: even a shortened, diminished eon still earns the name of a whole eon, exactly as a shortened day of guard-duty is still called a full day's watch.

Chinese Source

問此業能取一劫壽果為是何劫。或有說者。是成劫。復有說者。是壞劫。復有說者。是大劫。如是說者此是中劫。由彼亦有不盡中劫而得脫故。如毘奈耶說。提婆達多。當於人壽四萬歲時來生人中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: as for this karma bringing about a fruit of one eon's lifespan, which kind of eon is meant? Some say: it is the eon of formation. Some further say: it is the eon of destruction. Some further say: it is the great eon. According to this account, it is a middling eon, since there are also cases of escaping before a middling eon is exhausted — for the Vinaya says that Devadatta, when the human lifespan reaches forty thousand years, will come to be born among humans,

必定當證獨覺菩提。舍利子等所不能及。問如是伽他當云何通。

— and will certainly attain pratyekabuddha-awakening, a thing that even Śāriputra and the like cannot match. Question: how, then, is the following verse to be reconciled?

諸有破僧人　破壞和合僧生無間地獄　壽量經劫住尊者世友作如是說。減一劫住亦名一劫。如世間人於減一日。住持所作亦名直日。此亦如是。

Whoever splits the saṅgha, Shattering its harmonious accord, Is born in Avīci hell, Abiding there a full eon's span. The Venerable Vasumitra explains it thus: even an eon diminished still bears the name of a whole eon — just as, among people of the world, one who keeps watch through even a shortened day is still said to have kept a full day's watch. It is just the same here.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The Devadatta prophecy performs a quietly remarkable piece of doctrinal reconciliation: rather than softening the horror of an eon in Avīci, it makes the eon itself negotiable in length while leaving the crime's gravity fully intact — Devadatta's ultimate destiny as a pratyekabuddha is not a suspension of justice but proof that even the very worst karma, once its fruit is exhausted, still yields to whatever wholesome seeds remain underneath it.

2. Why Lifespan Alone, and Why Schism Outweighs Wounding the Buddha

Two technical puzzles are addressed in sequence: why the crime of causing schism is named only by its lifespan-fruit in hell, when it actually ripens as all five aggregates there (with lifespan given pride of place as what sustains and marks the passage of all the others); and why causing a schism outweighs even maliciously drawing the Buddha's own blood — through a remarkable string of comparisons contrasting harm to the Buddha's physical body against harm to his Dharma-body, a single act of sudden violence against months of patient, calculated manipulation of impressionable young monks, and a private wound against a catastrophe that brings every practitioner's spiritual progress to a total halt.

Chinese Source

問此破僧罪亦能取地獄五蘊異熟。何故但言取一劫壽。或有說者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: this crime of causing schism in the saṅgha also brings about the ripening of the five aggregates in hell — why, then, does it speak only of a lifespan of one eon? Some say:

以壽為先。世尊總說取五蘊果。復有說者。此中世尊說最勝法。謂五蘊中壽命最勝是故偏說。復有說者。壽命能持一切五蘊。令不散壞是故偏說。復有說者。壽從初生盡眾同分無有間斷。令眾同分亦無間斷。餘法不爾是故不說。復有說者。由壽量故表知世間。或增或減。或進或退。或興或衰。是故偏說問何故破僧得劫住罪。非起惡心出佛身血耶。答若起惡心出佛身血壞佛生身。若破壞僧壞佛法身。一切如來應正等覺。敬重法身不重生身。復有說者。若起惡心出佛身血但毀尊重。若破壞僧即名毀壞尊重所重。若起惡心出佛身血但傷大師。若破壞僧即名傷損大師所師。如大師。如是法王歸依依趣亦爾。有說。彼起殺心出佛身血。但是加行罪。以佛法爾不可害故。破僧不爾是根本罪。如加行根本。如是加行究竟亦爾。復有說者。若起惡心出佛身血。不多發起廣大加行。但由率爾卒暴而傷。破僧不爾。要由發起廣大加行。或經一月乃至四月。方便誘誑諸新學苾芻令彼順已。然後能破是以罪重。復有說者。若起惡心出佛身血不惱亂大眾。雖傷佛身不能令佛生惱亂心。亦不諠擾。以作此事世間眾生。或有聞者或不聞故。若破壞僧極大諠擾惱亂大眾。

— it is because lifespan comes first; the World-Honored One speaks of taking the fruit of the five aggregates as a whole under this one heading. Some further say: here the World-Honored One speaks of the supreme dharma — among the five aggregates, lifespan alone is supreme, and hence it is spoken of exclusively. Some further say: lifespan is able to sustain all five aggregates, keeping them from scattering and decaying, and hence it is spoken of exclusively. Some further say: lifespan, from the very moment of birth until the class-nature is exhausted, is without interruption, and it is lifespan that keeps the class-nature itself likewise without interruption — no other dharma is like this, and hence they are not spoken of. Some further say: it is by the measure of lifespan that the world is known to increase or decrease, to advance or decline, to flourish or wane, and hence it is spoken of exclusively. Question: why does causing schism bring about the crime of an eon's abiding, while maliciously drawing the Buddha's blood does not? Answer: to maliciously draw the Buddha's blood harms the Buddha's own born body, while to cause a schism harms the Buddha's own Dharma-body — and every tathāgata-arhat-fully-awakened-one honors and reveres the Dharma-body, not the born body. Some further say: to maliciously draw the Buddha's blood merely injures what is revered, while to cause a schism is to injure and destroy what is revered by the revered themselves. To maliciously draw the Buddha's blood merely wounds the Great Teacher, while to cause a schism is to wound and injure that which the Great Teacher himself takes as teacher — for just as with the Great Teacher, so too it is with the Dharma-king who is the very refuge and resort of all. Some say: to arouse a murderous intent and draw the Buddha's blood is merely a crime of preparatory action, since it is the very nature of a buddha that he cannot actually be harmed; causing schism is not like this — it is a root offense. And exactly as with preparatory action and root offense, so too it is with the initiation and the completion of the act. Some further say: to arouse a malicious intent and draw the Buddha's blood does not require setting in motion a vast and extensive preparatory action, but occurs merely through a sudden, abrupt act of violence; causing schism is not like this — it necessarily requires setting in motion a vast and extensive preparatory action, sometimes spanning a full month or as long as four months, skillfully enticing and deceiving newly ordained bhikṣus until they fall into line, and only afterward is the schism achieved — and this is why its crime is heavier. Some further say: to arouse a malicious intent and draw the Buddha's blood does not disturb the great assembly — although it wounds the Buddha's own body, it cannot arouse in the Buddha any disturbed state of mind, nor does it cause any general uproar, since of the beings of the world who hear of this deed, some hear of it and some do not; whereas to cause a schism in the saṅgha brings about the greatest possible uproar and disturbance to the great assembly.

以破僧時應得入正性離生者。不得入正性離生。應得果證者不得果證。應離欲者不得離欲。應盡漏者不得盡漏。不得誦持思惟三藏。

At the time of the schism, those who ought to enter the state of certainty as to what is right are unable to enter it; those who ought to attain the realization of a fruit are unable to attain it; those who ought to become free of desire are unable to become free of desire; those who ought to exhaust the contaminants are unable to exhaust them; they are unable to recite, uphold, and reflect upon the Tripiṭaka;

不得靜處思惟諸法。修習靜慮無色等至。不得種殖三乘種子。三千大千世界法輪不轉。展轉聲至淨居諸天。令其覺慧不得安靜明了現行。若所破僧還和合時。應入正性離生者。即入正性離生。應得果證者即得果證。應離欲者得離欲。應盡漏者得盡漏。便有誦持思惟三藏。在空閑處思惟諸法。修習靜慮無色等至。亦能種殖三乘種子。三千大千世界法輪復轉。展轉聲至淨居諸天。令其覺慧復得安靜明了現行。由是因緣。若起惡心出佛身血。不能生起經劫住罪。

— they are unable to reflect upon the dharmas in a place of solitude, nor to cultivate the attainments of the dhyānas and the formless absorptions; they are unable to plant the seeds of the three vehicles; the Dharma-wheel does not turn throughout the trichiliocosm, and the news, spreading in turn, reaches even the gods of the Pure Abodes, disturbing their clear and calm wisdom, preventing it from manifesting undisturbed and lucid. But once the split saṅgha is reunited in harmony, those who ought to enter the state of certainty as to what is right at once enter it; those who ought to attain the realization of a fruit at once attain it; those who ought to become free of desire become free of desire; those who ought to exhaust the contaminants exhaust them; there is then reciting, upholding, and reflecting upon the Tripiṭaka, reflecting upon the dharmas in places of solitude, cultivating the attainments of the dhyānas and the formless absorptions, and the seeds of the three vehicles can also again be planted; the Dharma-wheel again turns throughout the trichiliocosm, and the news, spreading in turn, reaches the gods of the Pure Abodes, letting their clear and calm wisdom again manifest undisturbed and lucid. For this reason, to arouse a malicious intent and draw the Buddha's blood is unable to give rise to the crime of an eon's abiding,

若破壞僧便能生起經劫住罪。

— while to cause a schism in the saṅgha is indeed able to give rise to the crime of an eon's abiding.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The treatise's own ranking — that splitting the saṅgha is worse than wounding the Buddha himself — only makes sense once one accepts its deeper premise that the Buddha's true body is the Dharma he embodies and transmits, carried forward by the very community that schism tears apart: on this view, wounding the man is a tragedy, but wounding the institution that carries his teaching to everyone else is the graver theft, since it steals the path itself from countless others who would otherwise have walked it.

3. What a Split Sangha Actually Is, and Two Kinds of Schism

A precise ontological distinction opens this section: the split saṅgha itself is a neutral factor dissociated from mind (mere disharmony, no more morally loaded than a broken tool), while the crime of causing that split is a separate thing entirely — unwholesome false-speech karma — exactly as the state of having retrogressed differs from the unwholesome act that caused the retrogression. The treatise then locates where schism can occur (only among humans, and only in the desire realm) and distinguishes its two forms: splitting by separate ritual observances, achievable on any of the three continents, versus splitting by founding an entirely rival teacher and path, possible only on Jambudvīpa, illustrated through Devadatta's own five ascetic counter-practices meant to outshine the Buddha's eightfold path.

Chinese Source

問僧破以何為自性。答以不和合無覆無記不相應行為自性。是不相應行蘊所攝。即餘處說復有所餘如是種類不相應行。是故僧破異破僧罪異。僧破是不和合性無覆無記。是不相應行蘊所攝。破僧罪是虛誑語。不善語業色蘊所攝如退體異退法亦異。退體是不成就性無覆無記。不相應行蘊所攝。退法是不善。有覆無記五蘊所攝。此亦如是僧破異破僧罪異。由此僧破僧所成就。破僧罪破僧人成就。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: what is the own-nature of a split saṅgha? Answer: its own-nature is disharmony, an unobscured-neutral factor dissociated from mind, included within the aggregate of factors dissociated from mind — this is exactly what is meant elsewhere by "there are yet further factors dissociated from mind of this same kind." Hence a split saṅgha is one thing, and the crime of causing the schism is another: the split saṅgha is the state of disharmony, unobscured-neutral, included within the aggregate of factors dissociated from mind, while the crime of causing schism is false speech, an unwholesome verbal karma, included within the aggregate of form. It is just as retrogression itself differs from the state of retrogression: retrogression itself is a state of non-possession, unobscured-neutral, included within the aggregate of factors dissociated from mind, while the state of retrogression is unwholesome, obscured-neutral, included within the five aggregates. It is just the same here: the split saṅgha is one thing, the crime of causing schism another — for this reason, it is the saṅgha itself that possesses the split, while it is the one who causes the schism who possesses the crime of causing it.

問何處破僧。答在欲界人趣。若破羯磨僧通在三洲。若破法輪僧唯贍部洲。所以者何。若處有大師可得及道可得。即於是處有破法輪。餘洲無有大師及道。是故亦無破法輪者。譬如世間若處有王是處有偽王起。若處有力士是處有捔力者起。此亦如是。若於是處有大師。是處有邪師起。若處有道是處有邪道起。法爾邪正同處相違問破羯磨僧破法輪僧有何差別。答破羯磨者。謂一界內有二部僧各各別住。作布灑陀羯磨說戒。破法輪者。謂立異師異道。如提婆達多。言我是大師。非沙門喬答磨。五法是道。非喬答磨所說八支聖道。所以者何。若能修習是五法者。速證涅槃非八支道。云何五法。一者盡壽著糞掃衣。二者盡壽常乞食食。三者盡壽唯一坐食。

Question: where does the splitting of the saṅgha occur? Answer: it occurs in the desire realm, among the human destiny. Splitting the saṅgha through separate ritual acts can occur across all three continents; splitting the saṅgha through the founding of a rival dharma-wheel occurs only on Jambudvīpa. Why is this? Wherever a great teacher and a path can be found, there the dharma-wheel can be split; the other continents have no great teacher and no path, and hence there is no one there to split any dharma-wheel. It is like the world at large: wherever there is a king, there arises a false king; wherever there is a strongman, there arises a rival contender in strength — it is just the same here. Wherever there is a great teacher, there arises a false teacher; wherever there is a path, there arises a false path — it is simply the nature of things that the false and the true, occupying the same ground, stand opposed. Question: what is the difference between splitting the saṅgha by separate ritual acts and splitting it by founding a rival dharma-wheel? Answer: splitting by ritual acts means that, within a single boundary, two groups of monks dwell apart from one another, each performing the poṣadha rite and the recitation of the precepts separately. Splitting by founding a rival dharma-wheel means setting up a different teacher and a different path — as when Devadatta said, "I myself am the great teacher, not the ascetic Gautama; these five practices are the true path, not the Noble Eightfold Path taught by Gautama, for whoever is able to cultivate these five practices swiftly realizes nirvāṇa, not through the eightfold path." What are the five practices? First, wearing rag-robes for life; second, always begging for food for life; third, eating only a single sitting's meal for life;

四者盡壽常居逈露。五者盡壽不食一切魚肉血味鹽酥乳等。是謂破羯磨僧破法輪僧差別問於破僧時極少幾人成破僧事。答破羯磨僧極少八人。四人已上方名為僧三人不爾。於一界內有二部僧各各別住。作布灑陀羯磨說戒。乃得名為羯磨壞故。破法輪僧極少九人。

— fourth, dwelling always in the open air for life; fifth, eating none of fish, meat, blood, salt, ghee, or milk for life. This is the difference between splitting the saṅgha by ritual acts and splitting it by founding a rival dharma-wheel. Question: at the time of splitting the saṅgha, what is the minimum number of persons required for the schism to be accomplished? Answer: splitting the saṅgha by ritual acts requires a minimum of eight persons — for only four persons or more are properly called a saṅgha, while three are not, and it is only when, within a single boundary, two groups of monks each dwell apart and separately perform the poṣadha rite and recite the precepts that it can be called a splitting by ritual acts. Splitting the saṅgha by founding a rival dharma-wheel requires a minimum of nine persons,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The retreat/state-of-retreat distinction applied here to schism is a small masterpiece of Abhidharma precision: it insists that the community's fractured condition and the individual's guilty act are two entirely different kinds of thing, occupying different metaphysical categories altogether — a reminder that even the most devastating communal event decomposes, under sufficiently careful analysis, into a neutral structural fact and a separately assignable moral responsibility.

4. Minimum Numbers, Who Can Split the Sangha, and the Six Forbidden Times

A cluster of precise, almost legal specifications follows: splitting by ritual observance requires a minimum of eight persons, splitting by rival dharma-wheel a minimum of nine (Devadatta himself counted as a fifth vote-recipient among a minimum four true and five false adherents); the exact moment of completion is debated across several proposals, from the casting of ballots to moving fully out of sight and hearing of the original community; and only "view-conduct" persons of firm, unwavering intention can found a rival teaching (never those of mere "craving-conduct"), and only men (never women or paṇḍakas, disqualified from the very role of teacher, though capable of assisting) — before six specific periods when the sangha is entirely unsplittable are enumerated: the very beginning and end of a teaching's life, before its first internal controversies emerge, before it has formally organized, before its first pair of foremost disciples is established, and after the founding teacher's own death.

Chinese Source

以一界內有二部僧各各別住。於無慚愧部中。定別有一眾所尊重能教誨者。當知則是提婆達多。於正眾中極少四人。於邪眾中極少五人。如是極少下至九人則法輪僧壞。問齊何當言法輪僧壞。答施設論說。提婆達多自為第五皆共受籌。齊此當言法輪僧壞。復有說者。作表白已。復有說者。離所聞處。復有說者。離所見處。復有說者離見聞處。如是說者。若由意樂誓受餘師。謂彼愚癡諸苾芻眾。由定意樂發如是心作如是語。提婆達多是我大師非佛世尊。齊此當言法輪僧壞。問何等種類補特伽羅破法輪僧。答補特伽羅有二種。一者愛行。二者見行。諸見行者破法輪僧非愛行者。以見行者所有意樂堅固猛利。於雜染清淨品所作決定無有退轉。諸愛行者無如是事故不能破。又唯男子破法輪僧。非諸女人亦非扇搋半擇迦等。所以者何。破法輪時法爾自安立為大師。而諸女人非增上器。

Ru-Yi Translation

— since, within a single boundary, two groups of monks must each dwell apart from one another, and among the shameless group there must definitely be one further person, honored by the assembly and capable of instructing it, who should be understood to be Devadatta himself; within the true assembly there must be a minimum of four, and within the false assembly a minimum of five — thus a minimum of nine in all is required before the dharma-wheel saṅgha can be said to be split. Question: at what point should the dharma-wheel saṅgha be said to be split? Answer: the Prajñaptiśāstra says: once Devadatta, as the fifth, together with the others all receive the ballots, at that point the dharma-wheel saṅgha should be said to be split. Some further say: once the formal announcement has been made. Some further say: once they have moved beyond hearing range. Some further say: once they have moved beyond sight range. Some further say: once they have moved beyond both sight and hearing range. According to this last account: if it is a matter of settled intention and vowed acceptance of a different teacher — that is, if those foolish bhikṣus, by settled intention, give rise to such a thought and utter such words as, "Devadatta is my great teacher, not the Buddha, the World-Honored One" — at that point the dharma-wheel saṅgha should be said to be split. Question: what kind of person is capable of splitting the dharma-wheel saṅgha? Answer: there are two kinds of persons — those of craving-conduct, and those of view-conduct. It is those of view-conduct who are capable of splitting the dharma-wheel saṅgha, not those of craving-conduct, since those of view-conduct possess an intention that is firm and sharp, fixed and unwavering with respect to what is defiling and what is purifying, whereas those of craving-conduct lack this quality and so cannot accomplish the split. Moreover, only males can split the dharma-wheel saṅgha, never women, nor paṇḍakas, nor the like. Why is this? At the time of splitting the dharma-wheel, one must, as a matter of course, establish oneself as a great teacher — and women are not a vessel of that higher capacity,

於大師非分故不能破。然能廣作破僧方便。猶如麁喜苾芻尼等。諸扇搋半擇迦無形二形皆是愛行。諸愛行者所有意樂不堅不猛。於染淨品皆不決定。是故彼類不能破僧。

— being disqualified from the role of great teacher, and hence unable to accomplish the split themselves, although they can widely assist in the preparatory means of causing a schism, as with the bhikṣuṇī Sthūlanandā and the like. All paṇḍakas, whether without sexual organs or with both, are of craving-conduct, and those of craving-conduct possess an intention that is neither firm nor sharp, and are undecided with respect to what is defiling and what is purifying — hence that kind is unable to split the saṅgha.

Question: at what periods can the dharma-wheel saṅgha be split? Answer: during six periods the saṅgha cannot be split, and at all other times it can. As to why not at the beginning or the end: during these two periods, the assembly of bhikṣus is united in a single flavor within the noble teaching and cannot be split. As to why not before the two boils have yet broken out: this means the time before the two boils of precept-breaking and wrong-view have yet arisen within the noble teaching. As to why not before the community has yet come together and bound its boundary: it is only when, within a single boundary, two groups of monks dwell apart, holding differing views, that it can properly be called a schism. As to why not before the first pair of foremost disciples has yet been established: at a time before the first pair has yet been established, there can definitely be no one capable of splitting the dharma-wheel saṅgha, since it is the very nature of things that every buddha has a first pair of noble and worthy disciples, and if the dharma-wheel saṅgha should be split, this first pair, before even a day and night has passed, will bring it back into harmony. As to why not after the great teacher's parinirvāṇa: if, after the great teacher's own parinirvāṇa, someone were to say, "I myself am the great teacher, not the Tathāgata," everyone together would rebuke him, saying, "While the great teacher was alive, why did you not say that you yourself were the great teacher? For this reason it is certain that during these six periods the dharma-wheel cannot be split, while at all other times the dharma-wheel can be split.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The six forbidden periods trace out something like the natural lifecycle of vulnerability in any institution built around a founding vision: too young to have yet developed the internal fault lines a schism would exploit, too old (after the founder's death) for a rival claimant to be credible, and protected in between by the settled authority of designated senior successors — schism, on this account, is not a random threat but one confined to a specific structural window when authority is established but not yet so deeply rooted that it cannot be contested.

5. Mind, Feeling, and Whether the Buddha's Own Following Can Ever Be Split

Technical questions about which consciousness and which of the five feelings accompany the act of splitting are canvassed inclusively (any of the six, any of the five, depending on the account), before turning to the deeper question of whose mind bears the fruit (the fully ordained monk's, or more remotely the mere renunciant's) and who can actually be split away — ordinary beings only, never a single noble one, a point the World-Honored One is said to have declared without exception. This sets up the section's central puzzle: since Devadatta plainly did split the sangha, how can the Buddha's own following still be called unsplittable? Two answers are offered — Vasumitra's, that "the World-Honored One's retinue" technically names only the noble ones among his followers, and the Great Virtuous One's finer distinction between an inner retinue of unshakeable noble ones and an outer retinue of ordinary beings who remain genuinely vulnerable.

6. Was the Buddha Present? A Buddha's Own Ancient Karma

A vivid narrative answers whether the Buddha was physically present when the schism occurred: dwelling within the same monastic boundary but absent from the assembly itself, having personally warned Devadatta against the act, and then — when the warning failed — turning his own faculty of retrocognition upon his own distant past, discovering that he himself, innumerable eons before, had once split apart the following of another sage, and that this very karma was now ripening before him. Seeing the schism as therefore inevitable, he withdrew to his quiet chamber to sit in silent meditation while it unfolded. The section closes on whether every buddha faces such an event — one view holding this a fate unique to Śākyamuni's own specific past karma, another citing the case of Puṣpottara under Kāśyapa Buddha as proof that even other buddhas' communities have been struck by comparable catastrophes.

7. Schism First or Wholesome Roots Cut First? A Closing Tetralemma

The Bad-Conduct Nyāsa closes on a genuinely elegant puzzle: did Devadatta split the saṅgha before or after cutting off his own wholesome roots? The majority view insists schism came first, since only an intact reputation for discipline, learning, and eminence could ever have succeeded in seducing new monks away, and a root-severed Devadatta could never have pulled it off at all — a claim then defended through a careful tetralemma distinguishing the act of splitting from the specific mental state (believing the false teaching false, and the schism itself blameworthy) required to earn the full eon-long punishment, while the Great Virtuous One proposes instead that both crimes' preparatory actions were undertaken simultaneously. A second tetralemma of named examples then maps out every combination of committing an ānantarya deed and cutting off wholesome roots, before the fascicle's closing return to the earlier feeling-based classification: the single greatest fruit among the three kinds of good conduct is the volition present in the meditative attainment of the peak of existence.

10. A New Nyāsa Begins: Is Wrong Speech the Same as Wrong Livelihood?

A new chapter opens — the Wrong-Speech Nyāsa — directed against the Dārṣṭāntikas' claim that right and wrong livelihood possess some separate own-nature apart from ordinary bodily and verbal karma. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The mercy-killing doctrine attributed to the Mūka tribe is a genuinely unsettling piece of moral anthropology precisely because its internal logic is coherent on its own terms — death as restoration to "new and superior faculties," suffering ended rather than caused — and the treatise's diagnosis is exact: this is not malice but delusion, a sincerely held but catastrophically mistaken theory of karma and its fruit, which is precisely why it counts as killing born from ignorance rather than from hatred or greed, even though its real-world consequence is identical.

12. Stealing and Sexual Misconduct, Each from Three Roots

The same threefold analysis continues through theft (from greed — professional plunder and recruited banditry; from hatred — spiteful destruction of a victim's property; and from delusion, via a brahminical doctrine claiming all land was divinely granted to brahmins alone, making its reclamation from kṣatriyas no theft at all) and sexual misconduct (from greed and from hatred straightforwardly, and from delusion via two more heretical doctrines — a brahminical quota system permitting extramarital relations until one's caste-appropriate wife-count is met, and a Western mleccha view holding that no woman, including one's own mother, sister, or daughter, can ever truly be violated, since all women are by nature communal property like ripened fruit or a public thoroughfare).

13. False Speech, Divisive Speech, and Harsh Speech from Three Roots

Three further unwholesome speech-paths receive the same threefold treatment: false speech (from greed — hired orators employed by kings to seduce the unsubdued; from hatred; and from a delusion the treatise itself flags as still genuinely faulty despite its plausible cover story — that lying to a hunter or bandit to save a life is blameless, a belief that, however understandable, hardens through repetition into habitual deceit); divisive speech (from greed, illustrated by a wonderfully mercenary brahmin calculating that two feuding patrons will each pay him separately rather than share a single gift between them; and from a delusion claiming that sowing discord among already-disreputable households actually reduces the total evil in the world); and harsh speech (from greed — corrupt officials and military scribes; and from a delusion in which an entire ascetic lineage of fire-worshippers mistakes their own founder's natural bad temper for a spiritual virtue worth imitating).
