Crookedness, Filth, and Turbidity

Fascicle 117 surveys the seven restraints against twelve criminal trades of non-restraint, re-describes the three evil conducts as crookedness, filth, and turbidity through vivid similes, and works through an extended debate on what truly instigates an action — causal or momentary — closing with a careful comparison of good conduct, purity, and silence. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Restraint and Non-Restraint: Seven Communities, Twelve Criminal Trades

Fascicle 117 continues the Wrong-Speech Nyāsa with a new topic: the sevenfold restraint (abstaining from each of the seven bodily and verbal unwholesome paths) held by the seven Buddhist communities, set against its opposite — non-restraint — embodied in twelve named criminal trades, from butchers and fishers to executioners, jailers, and the vākṣīlaka who kills a man-eating python for a merchant caravan's bounty. A closing verse and Ghoṣa's own gloss extend the twelfth category to corrupt officials who torture prisoners or levy taxes with a poisoned heart.

Chinese Source

有律儀。有不律儀。有住律儀者。有住不律儀者。云何律儀。謂有七種。即離斷生命。乃至離雜穢語。云何不律儀。謂亦有七種。即斷生命乃至雜穢語。云何住律儀者。謂有七眾。一苾芻。二苾芻尼。三正學。四勤策男。五勤策女。六近事男。七近事女。云何住不律儀者謂有十二種不律儀家。一屠羊。二屠鷄。三屠猪。四捕鳥。五捕魚。六遊獵。七作賊。八魁膾。九縛龍。十守獄。十一煮狗。十二婆具履迦。此中屠羊者。為活命故懷殺害心。若買若賣養飼斷命。如是一切皆名屠羊。屠鷄屠猪亦復如是。捕鳥者。為活命故採捕眾鳥。捕魚等亦如是。縛龍者。為活命故習呪龍蛇。或言縛象。煮狗者。謂栴茶羅等諸穢惡人。婆具履迦者。謂有傍生名婆具羅。即是蟒類。恒於曠野吞食商侶。有人專能殺之。取商侶價以自活命。由此故名婆具履迦。有說。罝弶名婆具羅。有人為活命故恒設罝弶。取諸眾生故名婆具履迦。有說。獵主名婆具履迦。如有頌言。

Ru-Yi Translation

There is restraint, and there is non-restraint; there are those who abide in restraint, and there are those who abide in non-restraint. What is restraint? It has seven kinds: abstaining from taking life, and so on up to abstaining from idle chatter. What is non-restraint? It likewise has seven kinds: taking life, and so on up to idle chatter. What is one who abides in restraint? There are seven communities — first, bhikṣus; second, bhikṣuṇīs; third, probationary nuns; fourth, male novices; fifth, female novices; sixth, laymen; seventh, laywomen. What is one who abides in non-restraint? There are twelve kinds of non-restraint households — first, sheep-butchers; second, poultry-butchers; third, pig-butchers; fourth, bird-trappers; fifth, fishers; sixth, hunters; seventh, thieves; eighth, executioners; ninth, snake-binders; tenth, jailers; eleventh, dog-cooks; twelfth, vākṣīlaka. As to sheep-butchers: for the sake of sustaining life, harboring a murderous intent, whether buying, selling, raising, or feeding animals for slaughter — all such activity is called sheep-butchering, and poultry-butchering and pig-butchering are just the same. Bird-trappers are those who, for the sake of sustaining life, catch and take various birds; fishers and the like are likewise. Snake-binders are those who, for the sake of sustaining life, practice charming snakes and serpents — some say this means the binding of elephants. Dog-cooks refers to caṇḍālas and other such impure and evil persons. As for vākṣīlaka: there is an animal called vākura, a kind of python, which constantly devours merchant travelers in the wilderness; there are those who specialize in killing such creatures, taking payment from the merchants to sustain their own life by this means — hence they are called vākṣīlaka. Some say vākura means a snare, and that there are those who, for the sake of sustaining life, constantly set snares to catch various creatures, and are hence called vākṣīlaka. Some say a hunting master is called vākṣīlaka, as there is a verse that says:

鹿出婆具履迦苦　終不還投婆具羅智者棄凡俗出家　終不還歸苦迫迮尊者妙音作如是說。若受上命訊問獄囚。肆情暴虐加諸苦楚。或非理斷事。或毒心賦稅。如是一切皆名住不律儀者。

A deer, once free of the hunter's snare's own torment, Never again returns to fall into that snare; A wise one, having abandoned the common world to go forth, Never again returns to the affliction and constraint he left. The Venerable Ghoṣa explains: if one, receiving orders from a superior, interrogates prisoners and, giving free rein to one's own temper, inflicts brutal and violent suffering upon them, or judges cases contrary to what is right, or levies taxes with a poisoned heart — all such persons are likewise called those who abide in non-restraint.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The inclusion of corrupt magistrates and tax officials alongside butchers and executioners in the roster of "non-restraint" trades is a quietly pointed piece of social commentary: it refuses to let institutional authority launder what would otherwise be recognized as cruelty, insisting that a judge who tortures prisoners for pleasure occupies the very same moral category as a man who slaughters animals for a living.

2. How Restraint and Non-Restraint Are Obtained, and Why Grade Never Changes

Restraint and non-restraint are shown to be obtained through slightly different mechanisms — restraint solely through a formal vow, non-restraint either through vow or simply through the deed itself — before a striking rule emerges: whatever grade of mind (inferior, middling, or superior) one holds at the very moment of receiving restraint fixes that restraint's grade for the rest of one's life, regardless of any later effort. This yields the genuinely startling conclusion that a newly ordained monk who took his vows with a superior mind can outrank, in restraint alone, an arhat who took his with an inferior mind.

Chinese Source

問如是諸律儀要受方得。此不律儀亦如是耶。或有說者。亦由受得。謂手執殺具誓從今日乃至命終。常作此業以自活命。爾時便得此不律儀。復有說者。雖執殺具自立誓言。然彼不得此不律儀。由二緣得。一由作業。二由受事。由作業者。謂生不律儀家最初作彼殺生等業。爾時便得此不律儀。由受事者。謂生餘家為活命故懷殺害心。往屠羊等不律儀所。作是誓言我從今者乃至命終。常作汝等所作事業以自活命。爾時便得此不律儀。復有說者。此亦最初作彼業時。方乃獲得此不律儀。彼說不律儀唯一緣得。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: since these various restraints are obtained only through the taking of a vow, is non-restraint likewise obtained in this way? Some say: it too is obtained through the taking of a vow — that is, when one takes up an instrument of killing and vows, "From this day until the end of my life, I shall constantly perform this activity to sustain my own life," at that moment one obtains this non-restraint. Some further say: although one takes up an instrument of killing and makes such a vow, one does not thereby obtain this non-restraint — it is obtained through two conditions: first, through performing the act; second, through undertaking the occupation. Through performing the act: this means that one born into a non-restraint household, at the very first moment of performing the act of killing living beings and the like, thereby obtains this non-restraint. Through undertaking the occupation: this means that one born into another household who, for the sake of sustaining life, harbors a murderous intent and goes to a place of non-restraint such as a sheep-butcher's, and makes this vow, "From this day until the end of my life, I shall constantly perform the very activity you yourselves perform, to sustain my own life," at that moment obtains this non-restraint. Some further say: this too is obtained only at the very first moment of actually performing that act — this last account holds that non-restraint is obtained through this single condition alone.

若有以下品心起有表業受諸律儀。盡眾同分彼諸律儀下品隨轉。雖於後時勵力發起身語意攝惡行妙行。然彼律儀常下品轉更不增長。

If one, with an inferior mind, gives rise to manifest karma and receives the various restraints, then throughout the entire span of that class-nature, those restraints continue to function only at the inferior grade — although one may afterward, through effort, give rise to evil or good conduct comprised in bodily, verbal, or mental action, that restraint always continues to function at the inferior grade and never increases further.

若有以中品心起有表業受諸律儀。盡眾同分彼諸律儀中品隨轉。雖於後時勵力發起身語意攝惡行妙行。然彼律儀常中品轉不增不減。

If one, with a middling mind, gives rise to manifest karma and receives the various restraints, then throughout the entire span of that class-nature, those restraints continue to function only at the middling grade — although one may afterward, through effort, give rise to evil or good conduct comprised in bodily, verbal, or mental action, that restraint always continues to function at the middling grade, neither increasing nor decreasing.

若有以上品心起有表業受諸律儀。盡眾同分彼諸律儀上品隨轉。雖於後時勵力發起身語意攝惡行妙行。然彼律儀常上品轉更不損減。

If one, with a superior mind, gives rise to manifest karma and receives the various restraints, then throughout the entire span of that class-nature, those restraints continue to function only at the superior grade — although one may afterward, through effort, give rise to evil or good conduct comprised in bodily, verbal, or mental action, that restraint always continues to function at the superior grade and never diminishes further.

故如是。問頗有新學苾芻成就上品律儀。而阿羅漢成就下品律儀耶。答有。謂有新學苾芻以上品心。起有表業受諸律儀。有阿羅漢以下品心。起有表業受諸律儀。如是新學苾芻成就上品律儀。而阿羅漢成就下品律儀。

— hence it is thus. Question: is there a case where a newly ordained bhikṣu possesses restraint of the superior grade, while an arhat possesses restraint only of the inferior grade? Answer: there is — namely, a newly ordained bhikṣu who, with a superior mind, gave rise to manifest karma and received the various restraints, while an arhat who, with an inferior mind, gave rise to manifest karma and received the various restraints; in this way the newly ordained bhikṣu possesses restraint of the superior grade, while the arhat possesses restraint only of the inferior grade.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The claim that a freshly ordained novice's restraint can technically outrank an arhat's is a carefully limited one, and its limits are the whole point: restraint itself is fixed forever at the grade of the founding vow's mental intensity, entirely independent of the practitioner's subsequent spiritual attainment — a reminder that this specific technical category was never meant to track overall sanctity, only the strength of a single originating intention.

3. Why a Sheep-Butcher's Non-Restraint Extends to Every Being

A puzzle is posed and resolved: since a sheep-butcher has no wish to kill any creature but sheep, why does his non-restraint (his continuous liability to the karma of killing) extend to every being in existence, not sheep alone? The answer turns on shared vulnerability rather than actual intent — every being whatsoever shares the same aggregates, elements, and sense-fields that make a sheep killable, and were every being to appear before him in a sheep's form, his murderous intent would extend to every one of them without exception.

Chinese Source

若有最初以下品纏斷眾生命。於此眾生得下品斷生命所攝。及不律儀所攝表無表業。於餘一切有情身上。唯得下品不律儀所攝無表業。若彼後時隨以下中上品纏斷眾生命。於此眾生唯得下中上品。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one, at the very outset, with an inferior entanglement, takes a being's life, then with respect to that particular being one obtains the inferior-grade manifest and unmanifest karma comprised within the taking of life and within non-restraint; and with respect to every other being, one obtains only the inferior-grade unmanifest karma comprised within non-restraint. If, at some later time, one takes a being's life with an inferior, middling, or superior entanglement, then with respect to that particular being one obtains only the corresponding inferior, middling, or superior grade

斷生命所攝表無表業。更不得不律儀所攝表無表業。先已得故如是最初以中品纏以上品纏。廣說亦爾。斷生命等。隨別漸得不律儀業普頓得故。問如屠羊者不欲殺餘眾生。何故此人普於一切有情所得不律儀耶。答雖於羊處起不律儀。然諸有情一切皆有羊蘊界處。又彼惡心境界寬遍。故於一切得不律儀。無有是處為分別故。設諸有情皆作羊像。來住前者於彼一切。皆起惡心皆欲殺害。是故於一切有情所得不律儀。

— of manifest and unmanifest karma comprised within the taking of life, but does not obtain again the manifest and unmanifest karma comprised within non-restraint, since this was already obtained previously. It is just the same when one's first entanglement is of the middling or superior grade, explained in full accordingly — for the taking of life and the like is obtained gradually with respect to each particular being, while non-restraint itself is obtained universally and all at once. Question: since a sheep-butcher has no wish to kill any other kind of being, why does this person obtain non-restraint universally, with respect to every being whatsoever? Answer: although his non-restraint arises specifically with respect to sheep, still, every being whatsoever possesses the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields that a sheep also possesses; moreover, the scope of his own evil intent is broad and all-encompassing, and so he obtains non-restraint with respect to all beings. There is no case where this could be otherwise, made for the sake of a fine distinction — for even if every being whatsoever were to take on the form of a sheep and come to stand before him, he would give rise to an evil intent toward every one of them and wish to kill every one of them. Hence he obtains non-restraint with respect to every being whatsoever.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The thought experiment of every being appearing in sheep's form before the butcher is a clever way of exposing what his non-restraint actually consists of: not a specific grudge against sheep as such, but a general readiness to kill whatever presents itself in a killable guise — which is precisely why the disposition, once formed, attaches to the whole of sentient existence rather than to one particular species.

4. The Full Grid: Components and Causes of Restraint and Non-Restraint

A precise combinatorial grid maps every possible way restraint or non-restraint can be obtained with respect to every being, cross-referencing whether it comes through all seven components (every path from killing to idle chatter) and whether it comes through all three causes (the inferior, middling, and superior grades, or greed, hatred, and delusion) — with the same structure worked out separately for restraint via the layman's, novice's, and full monastic precepts, and a careful technical note distinguishing the mere one-day fast (not a fixed part of the restraint system) from lifelong ordination.

Chinese Source

住不律儀者。有於一切有情得不律儀。非由一切支。非由一切因。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among those who abide in non-restraint: there are those who obtain non-restraint with respect to every being, but not through every component, nor through every cause.

有於一切有情得不律儀。由一切支。非由一切因。有於一切有情得不律儀。由一切因。非由一切支。有於一切有情得不律儀。由一切支。由一切因。若由一切支。由一切因。得不律儀非於一切有情者。此類無有一切有情者。即是一切有情之類。一切支者。謂斷生命乃至說雜穢語。一切因者。謂下中上纏。或貪嗔癡。有於一切有情得不律儀。非由一切支。非由一切因者謂以下纏斷眾生命。或中或上非餘亦不起餘支。有於一切有情得不律儀。由一切支非由一切因者。謂以下纏斷眾生命。乃至說雜穢語。或中或上非餘。有於一切有情得不律儀。由一切因非由一切支者。謂以下中上纏斷眾生命不起餘支。有於一切有情得不律儀。由一切支由一切因者。謂以下中上纏斷眾生命。乃至說雜穢語。住律儀者。有於一切有情得律儀。非由一切支。非由一切因。有於一切有情得律儀。由一切支。

There are those who obtain non-restraint with respect to every being through every component, but not through every cause. There are those who obtain non-restraint with respect to every being through every cause, but not through every component. There are those who obtain non-restraint with respect to every being through every component and through every cause. And as for obtaining non-restraint through every component and through every cause but not with respect to every being — there is no such case, since "every being" simply is the class comprising every being. "Every component" means the taking of life, and so on up to idle chatter. "Every cause" means the inferior, middling, and superior entanglement, or greed, hatred, and delusion. As to obtaining non-restraint with respect to every being through neither every component nor every cause: this means taking life with an inferior, middling, or superior entanglement — only one of these, not the others — and not giving rise to any other component either. As to obtaining it through every component but not every cause: this means taking life, and so on up through idle chatter, with only one of the inferior, middling, or superior entanglements, not the others. As to obtaining it through every cause but not every component: this means taking life with the inferior, middling, and superior entanglements all together, without giving rise to any other component. As to obtaining it through every component and through every cause: this means taking life, and so on up through idle chatter, with the inferior, middling, and superior entanglements all together. Among those who abide in restraint: there are those who obtain restraint with respect to every being, but not through every component, nor through every cause. There are those who obtain restraint with respect to every being through every component,

非由一切因。有於一切有情得律儀。由一切支。由一切因。若於一切有情得律儀。由一切因非由一切支者。此類無有。若由一切支由一切因得律儀。非於一切有情者。此亦無有一切有情者。即是一切有情之類。一切支者。謂離斷生命乃至離雜穢語。一切因者。謂下中上品心。或無貪無瞋無癡。有於一切有情得律儀。非一切支非一切因者。謂以下心受近事勤策戒。或中或上或二非餘。有於一切有情得律儀。亦一切支非一切因者。謂以下心受近事乃至苾芻戒。或中或上或二非餘。有於一切有情得律儀。亦一切支亦一切因者。謂以下中上心。如次受近事勤策苾芻戒。問若以下中上心。如次受近住近事勤策戒時。即名於一切有情得律儀。亦一切因非一切支。何以言無。答此中但依盡壽律儀作論不依晝夜。所以者何。彼名為齋。於律儀中非決定故。

— but not through every cause. There are those who obtain restraint with respect to every being through every component and through every cause. As to obtaining restraint with respect to every being through every cause but not through every component — there is no such case. As to obtaining restraint through every component and through every cause but not with respect to every being — there is likewise no such case, since "every being" simply is the class comprising every being. "Every component" means abstaining from taking life, and so on up to abstaining from idle chatter. "Every cause" means the inferior, middling, and superior grade of mind, or non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion. As to obtaining restraint with respect to every being through neither every component nor every cause: this means receiving the layman's or the novice's precepts with an inferior mind — or a middling or superior mind, or two of these but not all — but not the others. As to obtaining it through every component but not every cause: this means receiving the layman's precepts, up through the bhikṣu's precepts, with an inferior mind — or a middling or superior mind, or two of these but not all. As to obtaining it through every component and through every cause: this means receiving the layman's, novice's, and bhikṣu's precepts in turn with an inferior, then a middling, then a superior mind. Question: if one receives the one-day precepts, the layman's precepts, and the novice's precepts in turn with an inferior, then a middling, then a superior mind, should this not likewise be called obtaining restraint with respect to every being through every cause but not every component — why then is it said there is no such case? Answer: this discussion here is conducted solely with respect to lifelong restraint, not with respect to a single day and night, since that one-day observance is called a fast, and is not fixed within the category of restraint proper.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This grid's careful exclusion of the one-day fast from the technical category of "restraint" is a small but revealing piece of housekeeping: it draws a hard line between a genuinely fixed, lifelong commitment and a renewable daily observance, insisting that however sincerely undertaken, a vow with a built-in expiration date belongs to a different conceptual category altogether than one that binds for life.

5. Gradual Restraint, Instant Non-Restraint, and Four Ways Each Is Released

A structural asymmetry is drawn between the two states: restraint, being difficult to obtain, is received and increased gradually, while non-restraint, being easy to obtain, arrives all at once — though the Gandhāran and Kashmiri masters disagree over whether a mute born into a butcher's family can obtain only bodily non-restraint (Gandhāra) or whether non-restraint, unlike restraint, simply has no incomplete components at all (Kashmir). The two schools disagree again over what happens when someone in non-restraint takes the eight precepts for a single day: does non-restraint definitely resume the next morning, or does one pass through a suspended state that is neither restraint nor non-restraint? The section closes with the four conditions releasing each state — training given up, sex changed, wholesome roots cut, or class-of-existence abandoned for restraint; prātimokṣa vows taken, dhyāna-restraint attained, sex changed, or class-of-existence abandoned for non-restraint — plus a live dispute over whether merely resolving to give up one's killing tools, without any formal vow, is enough to end non-restraint at all.

Chinese Source

問如受律儀於下品後復得中品。於中品後復得上品。諸不律儀亦如是耶。或有說者。如得律儀不律儀亦爾。所以者何。諸善律儀作大功用。作大加行尚數數得。況不律儀。如是說者。律儀漸得非不律儀。所以者何。律儀難得以難得故漸受漸得。不律儀易得以易得故頓受頓得。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: just as one who has received restraint at the inferior grade may afterward obtain the middling grade, and afterward the superior grade, is non-restraint likewise so? Some say: non-restraint is just like restraint in this respect, for why is this? If wholesome restraint, requiring great effort and great exertion, can still be obtained again and again, how much more so non-restraint. According to this last account: restraint is obtained gradually, but non-restraint is not, for why is this? Restraint is difficult to obtain, and being difficult to obtain, it is received gradually and obtained gradually; non-restraint is easy to obtain, and being easy to obtain, it is received all at once and obtained all at once.

問如善律儀有支不具。諸不律儀亦如是耶。答健馱羅國諸論師言。

Question: just as wholesome restraint may have its components incomplete, is non-restraint likewise so? Answer: the treatise-masters of Gandhāra say:

不律儀業有支不具。若諸有情生在種種不律儀家。生便瘖瘂盡眾同分不能言說。彼但可得身三業性不律儀業。不得語四。迦濕彌羅國諸大論師咸作是說。諸不律儀無支不具。如善律儀漸次受者有支不具。諸不律儀則不如是。無漸受故易可得故。問住不律儀者受八戒齋時。捨不律儀得律儀。至明旦時捨律儀還得不律儀耶。答健馱羅國諸論師言。住不律儀者受八戒齋時。捨不律儀得律儀。至明旦時捨律儀還得不律儀。得律儀故不律儀斷。捨律儀故不律儀續。迦濕彌羅國諸大論師咸作是說。住不律儀者受八戒齋時。捨不律儀得律儀。至明旦時捨律儀不得不律儀。得律儀故捨不律儀。分齊極故又捨律儀。是故爾時名非律儀非不律儀。若彼有情盡眾同分。不復作者不得不律儀。若復作者還得不律儀。盡壽律儀由四緣捨。一捨所學。二二形生。三斷善根。四捨眾同分。諸持律者說。法滅沒時為第五緣。謂法滅沒時。一切所學。出家受具。結界羯磨。悉皆息滅。是故爾時律儀亦捨。如是說者。當於爾時先得律儀不捨。已出家者猶名出家。已受具者猶名受具。未出家者無復出家。未受具者無復受具。依此故言一切息滅。

— non-restraint karma can indeed have its components incomplete; for if beings are born into various non-restraint households and are, from birth, mute for the entire span of that class-nature, unable to speak, they can obtain only non-restraint karma of the nature of the three bodily actions, and cannot obtain the four verbal ones. The great treatise-masters of Kashmir, however, all say: non-restraint has no incompleteness of components at all — it is wholesale unwholesome restraint received gradually that may have incomplete components, but non-restraint is not like this, since it is not received gradually and is easily obtained. Question: when one abiding in non-restraint receives the eight precepts of the fast day, does he give up his non-restraint and obtain restraint, and then, when the next morning comes, give up that restraint and obtain non-restraint once again? Answer: the treatise-masters of Gandhāra say: when one abiding in non-restraint receives the eight precepts of the fast day, he gives up his non-restraint and obtains restraint, and when the next morning comes, he gives up that restraint and obtains non-restraint once again — for since restraint is obtained, non-restraint is thereby cut off, and since restraint is given up, non-restraint thereby continues once more. The great treatise-masters of Kashmir, however, all say: when one abiding in non-restraint receives the eight precepts of the fast day, he gives up his non-restraint and obtains restraint, but when the next morning comes and he gives up that restraint, he does not thereby obtain non-restraint, since it was the very obtaining of restraint that caused the giving-up of non-restraint, and since the fixed limit of that restraint's own duration has now been reached, restraint too is given up; hence, at that time, he is named neither one who abides in restraint nor one who abides in non-restraint. If, throughout the remaining span of that being's class-nature, he does not act again, he does not obtain non-restraint; if he does act again, he obtains non-restraint once more. Lifelong restraint is given up through four conditions: first, giving up one's training; second, a change of sex; third, cutting off one's wholesome roots; fourth, giving up one's class-nature. Those who uphold the Vinaya say that the disappearance of the Dharma constitutes a fifth condition — that is, when the Dharma disappears, all training, going forth, receiving full ordination, and the formal binding of monastic boundaries all cease entirely, and hence restraint too is given up at that time. According to this last account: at that very time, whoever had previously obtained restraint does not give it up — one already gone forth is still called one gone forth, and one already fully ordained is still called one fully ordained; it is only that one not yet gone forth can no longer go forth, and one not yet fully ordained can no longer be fully ordained — and it is with reference to this that it is said that everything ceases entirely.

諸不律儀由四緣捨。一受別解脫律儀。二得靜慮律儀。三二形生。

Non-restraint is given up through four conditions: first, receiving prātimokṣa restraint; second, attaining dhyāna-restraint; third, a change of sex;

四捨眾同分。問如善律儀捨所學。捨此不律儀亦如是耶。答或有說者。若能決定捨諸殺具。爾時捨不律儀。如是說者。雖復決定捨諸殺具。若不受戒得善律儀。終不得名捨不律儀。

— fourth, giving up one's class-nature. Question: just as wholesome restraint is given up through giving up one's training, is this non-restraint given up in just the same way? Answer: some say: if one is able to resolve firmly to give up one's implements of killing, at that moment one gives up non-restraint. According to this last account: even though one may resolve firmly to give up one's implements of killing, if one does not receive the precepts and obtain wholesome restraint, one can never be said to have given up non-restraint.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The Kashmiri masters' proposal of a genuinely third state — neither restraint nor non-restraint — for the morning after a one-day vow lapses is philosophically the more careful answer: rather than forcing every moral status into one of two named boxes, it allows for a limbo that simply reflects the truth of the matter, that a lapsed temporary vow does not automatically revert a person to their prior condition, but leaves them, until they act again, in a genuinely unclassified state.

6. Crookedness: Why Flattery and Deceit Are Confined to the Lower Grounds

A new topic opens: the three evil conducts re-described through the lens of crookedness, filth, and turbidity — flattery and deceit named crookedness, illustrated through a cascade of vivid similes (winding ravines and tangled thickets; crooked timber impossible to bring to market; a mind that reverses itself mid-action; a corpse's stench that repels the pure; and, most strikingly, even a buddha's own great compassion withdrawing from the incurably deceitful, as a good physician finally gives up a hopeless patient) — before the section explains why flattery and deceit occur only in the desire realm and the first dhyāna, never higher: the higher grounds lack the social hierarchies, the sense-consciousness bodies, and the very rationale for gradual purification that flattery depends upon.

Chinese Source

三惡行。三曲穢濁。乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答為欲分別契經義故。如契經說。有三惡行。三曲穢濁。契經雖作是說。而不廣辯廣說如前。復有說者。前納息中已分別三惡行。而未分別三曲穢濁。

There are three evil conducts, and three of crookedness, filth, and turbidity, and so on in full. Question: why compose this treatise? Answer: in order to analyze the meaning of the sūtra. For the sūtra says: there are three evil conducts, and three of crookedness, filth, and turbidity — though the sūtra speaks in this way, it does not explain the matter fully; the fuller explanation is as given above. Some further say: the earlier nyāsa already analyzed the three evil conducts, but did not yet analyze crookedness, filth, and turbidity;

今欲分別故作斯論。

wishing now to analyze these, this treatise is composed.

三曲穢濁。謂身曲身穢身濁。語曲語穢語濁。意曲意穢意濁。三曲云何。謂諂所起身語意業。所以者何。諂名為曲。由曲相法所起三業說名為曲。是彼果故。問復何因緣諂名為曲。答直相違故。如有頌言。

The three of crookedness, filth, and turbidity are: bodily crookedness, bodily filth, bodily turbidity; verbal crookedness, verbal filth, verbal turbidity; mental crookedness, mental filth, mental turbidity. What is the threefold crookedness? It is the bodily, verbal, and mental karma arisen from flattery and deceit. Why is this? Flattery and deceit is itself called crookedness, and the three karmas arisen from this crooked-marked dharma are likewise called crooked, being its very fruit. Question: for what reason, further, is flattery and deceit called crookedness? Answer: because it is the very opposite of straightness. As there is a verse that says:

諸盤迴屈曲　不平直不正嶮坑㵎稠林　是皆喻其諂復有說者。以諸有情諂所損污。難出生死難入涅槃。猶如曲木難出稠林難入聚落。此亦如是故名為曲。復有說者。以諸有情諂所損污。諸所作事將現在前。復還棄背將欲出言復還內止。其性嶮惡難得意趣。難可共交故名為曲。復有說者。以諸有情諂所損污。諸聰慧者皆應遠離。如樂淨人逃避塚間死屍臭穢。正直所厭故名為曲。

Whatever coils, winds, and bends, Whatever is uneven, crooked, and untrue, Dangerous pits, ravines, and tangled thickets — All these are similes for flattery and deceit. Some further say: because beings damaged by flattery and deceit find it difficult to escape saṃsāra and difficult to enter nirvāṇa, just as crooked timber is difficult to bring out of a tangled thicket and difficult to bring into a village — it is just the same here, and hence it is called crookedness. Some further say: because beings damaged by flattery and deceit, when about to undertake some act, then turn back and abandon it, and when about to speak, then hold back and say nothing, their very nature being treacherous and evil, difficult to fathom their true intent, and difficult to associate with — hence it is called crookedness. Some further say: because beings damaged by flattery and deceit are shunned by all who are wise, just as one who loves purity flees the foul stench of a corpse in the charnel ground, being loathed by the straightforward — hence it is called crookedness.

復有說者。以諸有情諂所損污。諸佛於彼亦捨大悲。如諂病人良醫所棄。障礙正化故名為曲。問諂在何處。答在欲界初靜慮非上地。

Some further say: because beings damaged by flattery and deceit are such that even the buddhas set aside their great compassion toward them, just as a deceitful patient is abandoned even by a good physician, since it obstructs proper instruction — hence it is called crookedness. Question: where does flattery and deceit occur? Answer: in the desire realm and the first dhyāna, not in the higher grounds.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The claim that even a buddha's compassion withdraws from the incurably deceitful is one of the tradition's more unsettling admissions, precisely because it seems to contradict compassion's own supposedly unconditional nature — yet the text's own logic is coherent: compassion that could still somehow reach a mind structured to reverse and hide itself at every turn would not be compassion succeeding, but instruction failing to find any surface to land on at all, and the withdrawal marks the limit of what teaching itself can do, not a limit on caring.

7. Filth: Why Hatred Alone Earns a Doubled Name

Turning to hatred as filth, the treatise faces down an obvious objection — surely every affliction is filth in some sense — and answers with a verse (also, and confusingly, cited to praise greed's own destructive reach) explaining that hatred alone earns the name of filth twice over, since it uniquely defiles both its own bearer's continuum (contorting the body, disturbing the mind, making one repellent even to look at) and the continuum of others it strikes. A cascade of explanations follows for why hatred is confined to the desire realm alone — the absence there of shame's opposite, of pain and sorrow, of sexual differentiation, of craving for food and sex, of any cause for enmity, and finally the presence of a specific antidote: loving-kindness cultivated in meditative equipoise.

Question: why is there no flattery and deceit in the higher grounds? Answer: the higher grounds are neither a field, nor a vessel, nor a ground, nor a basis for flattery and deceit, and since they are none of these, it does not occur there. Some further say: one strives toward the higher grounds precisely in order to remove flattery and deceit — if flattery and deceit were still present in the higher grounds, one ought not to exert oneself seeking to reach them. If whatever exists in the lower grounds also existed in the higher, then it would not be proper to posit a gradual process of extinguishing defilements; and if no gradual process of extinguishing defilements were posited, there could then be no final and complete extinguishing at all; and if there were no final and complete extinguishing, there would be no liberation; and if there were no liberation, there would also be no saṃsāra, and hence everything whatsoever would be nonexistent. Wishing to avoid such a consequence, it is held that the higher grounds have no flattery and deceit. Some further say: wherever kings and ministers are established, and distinctions of noble and lowly rank among beings are established, there flattery and deceit occurs — and since no such thing obtains in the higher grounds, there is no flattery and deceit there, for wherever there are kings, ministers, chieftains, and distinctions of noble and lowly rank, they must necessarily harbor flattery and deceit in their mutual dealings with one another. Some further say: wherever there are consciousness-bodies together with investigation and analysis, and manifest bodily and verbal karma of their own particular nature, there flattery and deceit occurs — and since the higher grounds lack all these dharmas, there is no flattery and deceit there. What is the threefold filth? It is the bodily, verbal, and mental karma arisen from hatred. Why is this? Hatred is itself called filth, and the three karmas arisen from this filth-marked dharma are likewise called filth, being its very fruit. Question: are not all the afflictions themselves filth? As there is a verse that says:

世間諸穢草　能穢污良田如是諸貪穢　穢污諸含識世間諸穢草　能穢污良田如是諸穢穢　穢污諸含識慢愛無明。餘煩惱頌說亦如是。何故此中唯瞋名穢。答雖諸煩惱皆名為穢。然唯瞋恚有二穢名故獨名穢。如上頌言如是諸穢穢穢污諸含識。復有說者。由此瞋恚穢自相續。穢他相續。勝餘煩惱故說名穢。云何穢自相續。謂若瞋恚現在前時。舉身麁強惱悴顰蹙戰掉不安。如鬼所著人不喜見。云何穢他相續。謂若瞋恚惱亂他時令他塵垢。或受鞭捶乃至喪命。問瞋在何處。答在欲界。非上二界。問何故上二界無瞋耶。答非田非器乃至廣說。復有說者。為除瞋故求趣上界廣說如前。復有說者。若於是處有慳嫉結則有瞋恚。所以者何。以諸有情依慳嫉結。於他相續起瞋恚故。上界不爾故無瞋恚。

The weeds and rank grasses of this world Are able to defile a fertile field; Just so this filthiest of all filth Defiles every kind of sentient being. Pride, craving, and ignorance, and the other afflictions, are spoken of in the same verse form. Why, then, is hatred alone here called filth? Answer: although every affliction is indeed called filth, only hatred bears the name of filth twice over, and hence it alone is called filth — as the verse above says, "this filthiest of all filth defiles every kind of sentient being." Some further say: because this hatred defiles its own continuum and defiles the continuum of others, surpassing the other afflictions in this respect, it alone is called filth. How does it defile its own continuum? When hatred manifests, the whole body becomes coarse and rigid, distressed and haggard, the brow furrowed, trembling and ill at ease, like one possessed by a demon, unpleasant for others to behold. How does it defile the continuum of others? When hatred disturbs another, it causes them defilement, or subjects them to lashings and beatings, up to the loss of life itself. Question: where does hatred occur? Answer: in the desire realm, not in the two higher realms. Question: why is there no hatred in the two higher realms? Answer: because they are neither a field, nor a vessel, and so on in full, as before. Some further say: one strives toward the higher realms precisely in order to remove hatred, explained in full as before. Some further say: wherever there is the fetter of miserliness and envy, there is hatred, for why is this? Because beings, relying upon the fetter of miserliness and envy, give rise to hatred within the continuum of others — and since the higher realms are not like this, they have no hatred.

復次若處有無慚無愧。則有瞋恚上界不爾。復次若處有苦憂根。則有瞋恚上界不爾。復次若處有男女根。則有瞋恚上界不爾。復次若處有段食愛及婬欲愛。則有瞋恚上界不爾。釋皆如前。是故上界無有瞋恚。復有說者。若於是處有怨害因則有瞋恚。怨害因者名九惱事。色無色界無怨害因故無有瞋。是故尊者妙音說言。怨害因緣則令瞋轉。或有說者。若所依身乾燥麁強則有瞋恚。上界依身潤澤柔濡故無瞋恚。復有說者。色無色界有瞋對治。謂等引慈故無有瞋。

Further, wherever there is shamelessness and lack of dread of blame, there is hatred, and the higher realms are not like this. Further, wherever there is the pain faculty and the sorrow faculty, there is hatred, and the higher realms are not like this. Further, wherever there is the male or female faculty, there is hatred, and the higher realms are not like this. Further, wherever there is craving for morsel-food and craving for sexual union, there is hatred, and the higher realms are not like this — the explanations for each being as given before. For this reason the higher realms have no hatred. Some further say: wherever there is the cause of enmity and injury, there is hatred; the cause of enmity and injury is called the nine grounds of vexation, and since the form and formless realms have no cause of enmity and injury, they have no hatred — hence the Venerable Ghoṣa says that it is the cause and condition of enmity and injury that sets hatred in motion. Some say: wherever the body one depends upon is dry, coarse, and rigid, there is hatred, while in the higher realms the body one depends upon is moist and soft, and hence there is no hatred. Some further say: the form and formless realms possess an antidote to hatred, namely loving-kindness cultivated in meditative equipoise, and hence there is no hatred there.

如於是處若有吠藍婆風。是處雲場終不得住上界亦爾。有瞋對治等引中慈。吠藍婆風故瞋雲場於彼不住。

It is like a place where the vairambha wind blows, where storm-clouds can never remain gathered — the higher realms are just the same: possessing the antidote to hatred, the loving-kindness of meditative equipoise, which acts as this very vairambha wind, the storm-cloud of hatred cannot remain there at all.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The final image — loving-kindness in meditative equipoise acting as a vairambha wind that scatters hatred's storm-clouds before they can gather — reframes the higher realms' freedom from hatred not as a passive absence but as an active, ongoing suppression: hatred is not simply unavailable there, it is continuously and specifically prevented, which quietly implies that the potential for hatred is a more universal feature of unawakened mind than its patchy actual distribution across the realms might suggest.

8. Turbidity: Greed's Wider Reach, and a Closing Tetralemma

Greed is named turbidity through a threefold etymology — it stains as a dye stains cloth, it debases as excess appetite is universally recognized as base, and it clouds as attachment displaces purity with defiling habit — and, notably, greed's own reach extends the furthest of the three, present from the desire realm all the way up through the very peak of conditioned existence. A closing tetralemma maps how the three evil conducts and the threefold crookedness-filth-turbidity relate: fully overlapping only in the desire realm's flattery-, hatred-, and greed-driven conduct, while first-dhyāna and higher afflictive karma counts as crookedness or turbidity without counting as evil conduct proper.

Chinese Source

三濁云何。謂貪所起身語意業。所以者何。貪名為濁。由濁相法所起三業說名為濁。是彼果故。問何因緣故貪名為濁。答能染濁故。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the threefold turbidity? It is the bodily, verbal, and mental karma arisen from greed. Why is this? Greed is itself called turbidity, and the three karmas arisen from this turbidity-marked dharma are likewise called turbidity, being its very fruit. Question: for what reason is greed called turbidity? Answer: because it is able to defile and cloud.

世間染色說名為濁。如世間說根濁莖濁枝濁葉濁花濁果濁。此皆能染故名為濁。復次濁者是鄙下義。世間並謂多貪欲者名為鄙濁。復次濁者是不清淨義。由貪蔽心習近染法捨淨法故。問貪在何處。答在欲界乃至非想非非想處。已說自性今當顯示雜無雜相。為三惡行攝三曲穢濁。三曲穢濁攝三惡行耶。答應作四句。有惡行非曲穢濁。謂除欲界諂瞋貪所起身語意惡行。諸餘身語意惡行。有曲穢濁非惡行。謂初靜慮諂貪所起身語意業。及餘色無色界貪所起意業。

In the world, whatever stains a color is called turbid — just as the world speaks of a turbid root, a turbid stalk, a turbid branch, a turbid leaf, a turbid flower, a turbid fruit — all of these being able to stain, and hence called turbid. Further, turbidity carries the sense of what is base and lowly — the world commonly calls one of excessive greed base and turbid. Further, turbidity carries the sense of what is impure — because greed obscures the mind, drawing it near to defiling dharmas and causing it to abandon pure ones. Question: where does greed occur? Answer: from the desire realm up through the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. Having explained their own-nature, we should now show whether these mix with one another or not. Do the three evil conducts subsume the threefold crookedness, filth, and turbidity, and do these in turn subsume the three evil conducts? Answer: a tetralemma should be constructed. There is evil conduct that is not crookedness, filth, or turbidity — namely, apart from the bodily, verbal, and mental evil conduct of the desire realm arisen from flattery, hatred, and greed, all other bodily, verbal, and mental evil conduct. There is crookedness, filth, or turbidity that is not evil conduct — namely, the bodily, verbal, and mental karma of the first dhyāna arisen from flattery and greed, and the mental karma of the remaining form and formless realms arisen from greed.

有惡行亦曲穢濁。謂欲界諂瞋貪所起身語意惡行。有非惡行非曲穢濁。謂除前相。相謂所名如前說。謂於色蘊中除不善色。及諂貪所起有覆無記色。取餘色蘊。於行蘊中除不善思。貪瞋邪見及諂貪所起有覆無記思。取餘相應不相應行蘊。及三蘊全并無為法。如是一切作第四句故言謂除前相。

There is what is both evil conduct and crookedness, filth, or turbidity — namely, the bodily, verbal, and mental evil conduct of the desire realm arisen from flattery, hatred, and greed. And there is what is neither evil conduct nor crookedness, filth, or turbidity — namely, whatever falls outside the categories just named. "The categories just named" means: within the aggregate of form, apart from unwholesome form and obscured-neutral form arisen from flattery and greed, taking the remaining aggregate of form; within the aggregate of formations, apart from unwholesome volition, and volition associated with greed, hatred, and wrong view, and obscured-neutral volition arisen from flattery and greed, taking the remaining associated and dissociated aggregate of formations; and the three complete aggregates together with the unconditioned dharmas — all of this together constitutes the fourth case, and hence it is said, "whatever falls outside the categories just named."

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Greed's uniquely wide reach — the only one of the three defilements present at every level up to the very summit of existence — is a sobering structural fact easy to miss amid the vivid imagery devoted to flattery and hatred: however dramatic hatred's storm-clouds and flattery's crooked timber may be as similes, it is greed alone that the entire cosmology of the three realms cannot escape, present in some form at every possible station a being might occupy.

9. What Truly Drives an Action: Causal and Momentary Instigation

A genuinely intricate technical debate opens: what actually instigates bodily and verbal karma — a single "causal" initiating mind plus a separate "momentary" accompanying mind at the instant of action? Can the five sense-consciousnesses ever serve as either, or is mental consciousness alone capable of initiating and accompanying, with the sense-consciousnesses merely along for the ride? The debate turns on how to explain "seeing one's own bodily gesture" and on a vivid counter-example — a man struck without warning who strikes back before any deliberate thought can form — before the discussion extends to why the passive, feeble ripening-mind can never instigate action, and why a mind on the path of seeing (subtle, inward-turned) cannot serve as momentary instigator even though it may serve as the more distant causal one, illustrated by a moving story of a man who dies partway through a vowed five-year almsgiving festival, is reborn wealthy through the power of that unfinished vow, and, recalling his past life, carries it through to completion.

10. Good Conduct, Purity, and Silence: Three Names, One Reality — Almost

Three of the treatise's near-synonyms for wholesome action are compared: good conduct and purity turn out to be simply two names for the identical reality (bodily good conduct just is bodily purity), with contaminated worldly good conduct still earning the name "pure" for possessing a partial purity even short of the undefiled ideal. Silence, however, breaks the pattern entirely — it belongs solely to the arhat's own mind, meaning that ordinary or trainee good conduct is never silence, and even the arhat's mind itself, not being technically karma, is silence without being good conduct at all. The section closes by asking why silence is established only via the aggregates of form and consciousness among the five — variously explained as marking a beginning and an end, a coarsest and a subtlest, or simply because the arhat's truly silent mind can only be inferred through the bodily and verbal action that reveals it.

Some further say: this is stated here in order to show the very first and the very last — the first being the aggregate of form, the last being the aggregate of consciousness, just as one speaks of the first and the last in speaking of entering and emerging, proceeding and having crossed over, the preparatory stage and the final stage, which one should understand in the same way. Some further say: this is stated here in order to show the coarsest and the subtlest — among the five aggregates, the aggregate of form is the coarsest, and the aggregate of consciousness is the subtlest. Some further say: true silence belongs only to the mind of the adept, and by what means is this mind of the adept to be inferred? By means of bodily and verbal karma — hence silence is established with respect to the mind of the adept together with bodily and verbal karma alone. Question: why does silence belong solely to the adept? Answer: silence can be found only in the body of the adept, and cannot be found in the body of the trainee or of one who is neither trainee nor adept. Arising from this very discussion, a further question arises: why can silence be found only in the body of the adept, and not elsewhere? Answer: because this silence is the most supreme dharma, and a supreme dharma cannot be found in an inferior body — for why is this? If one speaks of a supreme dharma, this means the dharma of the adept, supreme over the dharma of one who is not a trainee and the rest; and if one speaks of a supreme person, this means the person who is an adept, supreme over the person who is a trainee and the rest. Some further say: in the body of the adept, the afflictions and mental discursiveness have been finally and utterly extinguished, and silence is thereby perfected — hence silence is established there; other bodies are not like this, and hence it is not established with respect to them.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That true silence turns out to be inferred rather than directly observed — known only through the bodily and verbal conduct that serves as its outward evidence — is a quietly profound epistemological admission buried inside a technical classification: the treatise is effectively conceding that the arhat's own innermost stillness is never an object anyone else can witness directly, only a conclusion drawn, with confidence but never certainty, from what that stillness allows a person to no longer do or say.

11. Evil Conduct and Improper Attention: What Overlaps, What Doesn't

Having distinguished good conduct, purity, and silence by name and function, the treatise turns to a parallel question: does all evil conduct arise from improper attention, and does everything arisen from improper attention count as evil conduct? For bodily and verbal karma the answer is asymmetric — every evil conduct is indeed improper-attention-arisen, but plenty of improper-attention-arisen action is merely neutral (an afflicted first-dhyāna gesture, or simply moving clumsily) rather than genuinely evil. Mental karma requires its own separate tetralemma, since greed, hatred, and wrong view as mental evil conduct are not, strictly, "arisen from" attention in the way bodily and verbal action is — they simply are the improper attention itself, a subtle but important conceptual distinction.

12. The Mirror Image: Good Conduct and Proper Attention

The fascicle closes by running the identical analysis in the opposite direction: good bodily and verbal conduct is entirely coextensive with karma arisen from proper attention except for a residue of merely neutral, unobjectionable action (correct but unremarkable comings and goings, or speaking exactly the words called for); and mental good conduct again requires its own tetralemma, since non-greed, non-hatred, and right view are not "arisen from" proper attention in the ordinary causal sense but simply constitute it directly.
