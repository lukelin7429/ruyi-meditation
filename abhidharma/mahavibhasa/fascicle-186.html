The Three Groupings and the Five Superknowledges

Fascicle 186 continues the Single Practice Nyāsa (一行納息第五之四): the threefold classification of every sentient being into wrong-fixed, right-fixed, and indeterminate groupings; possessing, attaining, and relinquishing the awakening-factors against the broader class of uncontaminated dharmas; full understanding by cognition and by abandonment; how one develops the divine eye and ear without ever having seen or heard in this present life; why only the upper three noble fruits can regress while stream-entry never does; what is attained again upon regression or rebirth; the reach of the five superknowledges; and a closing combinatorial analysis of the vajra-like concentration's uninterrupted path. This page carries Ru‑Yi's complete translation.

1. Wisdom, Illumination, and the Three Groupings of Sentient Beings

Fascicle 186 opens the fourth part of the Single Practice Nyāsa (一行納息第五之四) by analyzing a sūtra distinction between the fixed and the indeterminate, the wise and the ignorance-bound, before turning to the treatise's own threefold classification of every sentient being — fixed in a wrong nature (having committed the five deeds of immediate retribution), fixed in a right nature (trainee or non-trainee), or indeterminate — mapped in exhaustive detail across realm, destiny, birth, and sphere, and closing with a set of explanations for why the Buddha, having already established this threefold sorting beneath the bodhi tree, still surveys the world with the Buddha-eye six times each day and night.

Chinese Source

諸不定彼一切非聰慧無明趣耶乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答為欲分別契經義故。如契經說。諸有情類有定有不定。有聰慧明趣。有非聰慧無明趣。雖作是說而不分別相攝差別。彼經是此論所依根本。

Ru-Yi Translation

Is every indeterminate being thereby without wisdom, bound to ignorance? — and so on at length. Question: why is this treatise composed? Answer: it is in order to analyze the meaning of a sūtra passage — that is, as a sūtra states, among sentient beings there are those who are fixed and those who are indeterminate, those who are possessed of wisdom and bound to illumination, and those who are without wisdom and bound to ignorance; although the sūtra speaks in this way, it does not analyze how these categories are mutually included or differ from one another — that sūtra is the root on which this treatise depends,

彼所不說者今盡應說。故作斯論。問諸不定彼一切非聰慧無明趣耶。答諸不定彼一切非聰慧無明趣。有非聰慧無明趣而非不定。謂邪定。問諸定彼一切聰慧明趣耶。答諸聰慧明趣彼一切定。有定彼非聰慧明趣。謂邪定。此中非聰慧者謂愚夫異生無聖慧故。聰慧者謂諸聖者有聖慧故。云何知然。契經說故。如契經說。佛告苾芻。

and what that sūtra leaves unsaid must now be stated in full, and it is for this reason that this treatise is composed. Question: is every indeterminate being thereby without wisdom, bound to ignorance? Answer: every indeterminate being is indeed without wisdom, bound to ignorance; but there is one who is without wisdom, bound to ignorance, yet is not indeterminate — namely, one who is wrongly fixed. Question: is every fixed being thereby possessed of wisdom, bound to illumination? Answer: every being possessed of wisdom, bound to illumination, is indeed fixed; but there is one who is fixed, yet is not possessed of wisdom, bound to illumination — namely, one who is wrongly fixed. Here, "without wisdom" refers to the foolish ordinary worldling, who lacks the noble wisdom; "possessed of wisdom" refers to the noble ones, who possess the noble wisdom. How is this known to be so? Because a sūtra states it — as a sūtra states, the Buddha told the monks:

於汝意云何。諸有於苦聖諦如實知。於苦集聖諦苦滅聖諦。趣苦滅行聖諦如實知。或有於此不如實知。此中說何是聰慧者。苾芻白佛。如我解佛所說義者。諸有於苦聖諦。乃至於趣苦滅行聖諦如實知名聰慧者。非不如實知。由此故知聖是聰慧異生非聰慧。聰慧者是明趣明所依故。非聰慧者是無明趣無明所依故。諸不定彼一切非聰慧。無明趣者謂諸不定必是異生。一切異生皆非聰慧。無明趣攝。有非聰慧無明趣而非不定。謂邪定者謂諸邪定亦必異生。彼於邪性定故不名不定。諸聰慧明趣彼一切定者。謂諸聖者於正性定故說名為定。

"what do you think — among those who know as it really is the noble truth of suffering, the noble truth of the origin of suffering, the noble truth of the cessation of suffering, and the noble truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering, and those who do not know these as they really are — which of these, here, are to be called possessed of wisdom?" The monks said to the Buddha: "as I understand the meaning of what the Buddha has said, those who know as they really are the noble truth of suffering, and so on up to the noble truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering, are to be called possessed of wisdom — not those who do not know them as they really are." By this it is known that the noble ones are possessed of wisdom, and ordinary worldlings are without wisdom; those possessed of wisdom are bound to illumination, since illumination is their basis, while those without wisdom are bound to ignorance, since ignorance is their basis. "Every indeterminate being is without wisdom, bound to ignorance" means that every indeterminate being is necessarily an ordinary worldling, and every ordinary worldling, being without wisdom, is subsumed under "bound to ignorance." "There is one who is without wisdom, bound to ignorance, yet is not indeterminate — namely, one who is wrongly fixed" means that one wrongly fixed is likewise necessarily an ordinary worldling, but, being fixed in a wrong nature, is not named indeterminate. "Every being possessed of wisdom, bound to illumination, is indeed fixed" means that the noble ones, being fixed in a right nature, are named fixed.

諸不定彼一切不成就等覺支耶。答諸不定彼一切不成就等覺支。有不成就等覺支而非不定。謂邪定是異生故邪性定故。諸定彼一切成就等覺支耶。答諸成就等覺支彼一切定。有定而不成就等覺支。謂邪定如前釋。有三聚。一邪性定聚。二正性定聚。三不定聚邪性定聚。謂成就五無間業。正性定聚謂成就學無學法。不定聚謂唯成就餘有漏法及無為。是名三聚自性。界者邪性定聚一界少分。謂欲界。正性定聚三界少分。不定聚亦爾。趣者邪性定聚一趣少分。謂人。正性定聚二趣少分。謂人天。不定聚三趣全。謂地獄傍生餓鬼。二趣少分。謂人天。生者邪性定聚一生少分。謂胎生。正性定聚二生少分。謂胎生化生。不定聚二生全。謂卵生濕生。二生少分。謂餘二生。有說。邪性定聚三生少分。除化生。正性定聚四生少分不定聚亦爾。處者有說。邪性定聚三處少分。正性定聚五處全。二十四處少分。不定聚五處全。二十四處少分。如是說者。諸行有四十處。不定聚十一處全。二十四處少分。譬喻者說。從無間地獄乃至有頂皆有三聚。彼說般涅槃法名正性定聚。不般涅槃法名邪性定聚。不決定者名不定聚。評曰。如前說者好。此依集異門說。若依施設論說。邪性定聚謂五無間業。若彼因彼果彼等流彼異熟及成就彼法補特伽羅。正性定聚謂學無學法。若彼因彼果彼等流。及成就彼法補特伽羅。不定聚謂諸餘法。若彼因彼果。彼等流彼異熟。及成就彼法補特伽羅。是名三聚自性。界者如前說。趣者邪性定聚二趣少分。謂地獄及人。正性定聚亦二趣少分。謂人天。

Is every indeterminate being thereby one who does not possess the awakening-factors? Answer: every indeterminate being does indeed not possess the awakening-factors; but there is one who does not possess the awakening-factors, yet is not indeterminate — namely, one who is wrongly fixed, since he is an ordinary worldling fixed in a wrong nature. Is every fixed being thereby one who possesses the awakening-factors? Answer: every being who possesses the awakening-factors is indeed fixed; but there is one who is fixed yet does not possess the awakening-factors — namely, one who is wrongly fixed, to be explained as before. There are three groupings: first, the grouping fixed in a wrong nature; second, the grouping fixed in a right nature; and third, the indeterminate grouping. The grouping fixed in a wrong nature refers to one who has accomplished the five deeds of immediate retribution; the grouping fixed in a right nature refers to one who has accomplished the dharmas of the trainee or the non-trainee; and the indeterminate grouping refers to one who has accomplished only the remaining contaminated dharmas together with the unconditioned — these are named the intrinsic natures of the three groupings. As to realm: the grouping fixed in a wrong nature is a portion of one realm, namely the desire realm; the grouping fixed in a right nature is a portion of all three realms, and the indeterminate grouping likewise. As to destiny: the grouping fixed in a wrong nature is a portion of one destiny, namely the human; the grouping fixed in a right nature is a portion of two destinies, namely human and heavenly; and the indeterminate grouping is the whole of three destinies — namely hell, animal, and hungry ghost — together with a portion of two destinies, namely human and heavenly. As to birth: the grouping fixed in a wrong nature is a portion of one kind of birth, namely womb-birth; the grouping fixed in a right nature is a portion of two kinds of birth, namely womb-birth and miraculous birth; and the indeterminate grouping is the whole of two kinds of birth, namely egg-birth and moisture-birth, together with a portion of the remaining two kinds of birth. Some say: the grouping fixed in a wrong nature is a portion of three kinds of birth, excepting miraculous birth, while the grouping fixed in a right nature is a portion of four kinds of birth, and the indeterminate grouping likewise. As to sphere: some say the grouping fixed in a wrong nature is a portion of three spheres, the grouping fixed in a right nature is the whole of five spheres together with a portion of twenty-four spheres, and the indeterminate grouping is the whole of five spheres together with a portion of twenty-four spheres. According to this latter view, since formations occupy forty spheres in all, the indeterminate grouping is the whole of eleven spheres together with a portion of twenty-four spheres. The Dārṣṭāntikas say that all three groupings exist all the way from the hell of uninterrupted torment up to the peak of existence — they say that the dharma of final nirvāṇa is named the grouping fixed in a right nature, the dharma of not attaining final nirvāṇa is named the grouping fixed in a wrong nature, and what is undetermined is named the indeterminate grouping. Assessment: the earlier explanation is preferable. This account has been given with reference to the Saṃgītiparyāya; according to the Prajñaptiśāstra, however, the grouping fixed in a wrong nature refers to the five deeds of immediate retribution, together with their causes, their effects, their outflows, their retributions, and the person who accomplishes those dharmas; the grouping fixed in a right nature refers to the dharmas of the trainee and the non-trainee, together with their causes, their effects, their outflows, and the person who accomplishes those dharmas; and the indeterminate grouping refers to every remaining dharma, together with its causes, its effects, its outflows, its retributions, and the person who accomplishes those dharmas — these are named the intrinsic natures of the three groupings. As to realm, this is as already explained above. As to destiny: the grouping fixed in a wrong nature is a portion of two destinies, namely hell and human, and the grouping fixed in a right nature is likewise a portion of two destinies, namely human and heavenly.

不定聚二趣全。謂傍生餓鬼。三趣少分。謂地獄人天。生者邪性定聚二生少分。謂胎生化生。正性定聚四生少分。不定聚亦爾。處所分別如應當知。毘奈耶說。世尊於菩提樹下建立一切有情為三聚。

The indeterminate grouping is the whole of two destinies, namely animal and hungry ghost, together with a portion of three destinies, namely hell, human, and heavenly. As to birth: the grouping fixed in a wrong nature is a portion of two kinds of birth, namely womb-birth and miraculous birth; the grouping fixed in a right nature is a portion of four kinds of birth, and the indeterminate grouping likewise. The analysis by sphere is to be understood correspondingly. The Vinaya states that the World-Honored One, beneath the bodhi tree, established every sentient being into three groupings —

謂齊爾許名邪定聚。齊爾許名正定聚。齊爾許名不定聚。問為依有情分齊建立。為依法分齊耶。若依有情者。云何非是得有情海邊際耶。若依法者。聲聞亦能如是建立。佛與聲聞有何不共。有說。依有情建立。問若爾。云何非是得有情海邊際耶。答佛得有情海邊際亦無有過。然總相得非別相。謂一切有情不出四生。如是而得。有說。依法建立。問若爾。聲聞亦能如是建立。佛與聲聞有何不共。

that is, saying, "exactly so many are named the grouping fixed in a wrong nature, exactly so many are named the grouping fixed in a right nature, and exactly so many are named the indeterminate grouping." Question: was this established with reference to a delimitation of sentient beings, or with reference to a delimitation of dharmas? If with reference to sentient beings, then how is this not tantamount to fathoming the very outer limit of the ocean of sentient beings? If with reference to dharmas, then a śrāvaka, too, would be able to establish it in just this way — what, then, distinguishes the Buddha from a śrāvaka? Some say: it was established with reference to sentient beings. Question: if so, then how is this not tantamount to fathoming the very outer limit of the ocean of sentient beings? Answer: there is no fault even in saying that the Buddha fathoms the very outer limit of the ocean of sentient beings, since this is grasped only in its general character, not in each particular — that is, it is grasped simply as, "every sentient being does not go beyond the four kinds of birth." Some say: it was established with reference to dharmas. Question: if so, then a śrāvaka, too, would be able to establish it in just this way — what, then, distinguishes the Buddha from a śrāvaka?

答聲聞因從佛聞。佛無師自能建立。是為不共。有說。若三千世界及千歲以來依有情建立。若餘世界及餘時依法建立。問若佛於菩提樹下已建立有情為三聚者。何故復言晝夜六時以佛眼觀世間耶。答先雖建立三聚。而未觀分位差別。誰於何時從邪定聚入不定聚。誰於何時從不定聚入邪定聚或正定聚。欲知此故復以佛眼晝夜觀察。

Answer: a śrāvaka's establishing it depends on having heard it from the Buddha, whereas the Buddha, without a teacher, is able to establish it entirely of himself — this is what distinguishes them. Some say: with respect to the trichiliocosm and the past thousand years, it was established with reference to sentient beings; with respect to every other world-system and every other time, it is established with reference to dharmas. Question: if the Buddha had already established all sentient beings into the three groupings beneath the bodhi tree, why does it also say that, six times each day and night, he surveys the world with the Buddha-eye? Answer: although the three groupings had already been established earlier, their particular stages had not yet been observed — who, at what time, passes from the grouping fixed in a wrong nature into the indeterminate grouping, and who, at what time, passes from the indeterminate grouping into the grouping fixed in a wrong nature or the grouping fixed in a right nature. It is in order to know this that he further surveys with the Buddha-eye, day and night.

有說。欲顯佛於一切時分恒樂思惟利他事故。有說。欲顯世尊大悲熏心無懈倦故。尊者世友說曰。有諸有情數數從餘世界來生此土。

Some say: it is in order to display that the Buddha, at every moment of time, constantly delights in reflecting on how to benefit others. Some say: it is in order to display that the World-Honored One's mind, suffused with great compassion, is never indolent or weary. The Venerable Vasumitra said: there are sentient beings who repeatedly come from other world-systems to be reborn in this land —

由先未建立故。今以佛眼觀之重為建立。尊者覺天說曰。佛以法為師。欲恭敬承事法故。晝夜六時以佛眼觀察世間。尊者妙音說曰。

since they had not been established previously, he now surveys them with the Buddha-eye and establishes them anew. The Venerable Buddhadeva said: the Buddha takes the Dharma as his own teacher, and it is in order to honor and serve the Dharma that he surveys the world with the Buddha-eye six times each day and night. The Venerable Ghoṣa said:

佛欲顯己所作審諦故。雖建立而復觀察。

the Buddha wishes to display that what he himself has done is thorough and exact, and it is for this reason that, although he has already established the groupings, he surveys them once again.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Vasumitra, Buddhadeva, and Ghoṣa each offer a different reason why the Buddha, having already sorted all beings into three groupings beneath the bodhi tree, still surveys the world with the Buddha-eye six times daily — a small gallery of named Sarvāstivāda masters disagreeing amicably about the psychology of omniscience itself.

2. Possessing, Attaining, Relinquishing: The Awakening-Factors and the Uncontaminated

A careful fourfold analysis distinguishes possessing, attaining, relinquishing, and regressing from the awakening-factors against the broader class of uncontaminated dharmas — reconciling the claim that an ordinary worldling already possesses cessation not through deliberate discernment from the very outset with the further claim that he can, in a meaningful sense, still be said to attain it.

Chinese Source

諸成就等覺支。彼成就無漏法耶。答諸成就等覺支彼成就無漏法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Does one who possesses the awakening-factors thereby possess the uncontaminated dharmas? Answer: one who possesses the awakening-factors does indeed possess the uncontaminated dharmas,

以等覺支是無漏故。有成就無漏法非等覺支。謂諸異生。以諸異生皆成就非擇滅。隨離何品染亦成就擇滅故。諸不成就等覺支。彼不成就無漏法耶。答無不成就無漏法。以必成就非擇滅故。有不成就等覺支。謂諸異生。以諸異生必不成就有為無漏法故。

since the awakening-factors are themselves uncontaminated. There is one who possesses the uncontaminated dharmas yet does not possess the awakening-factors — namely, every ordinary worldling, since every ordinary worldling possesses cessation not through deliberate discernment, and, upon becoming free of the defilement of whatever grade, also possesses cessation through discernment. Is there any who does not possess the awakening-factors and thereby does not possess the uncontaminated dharmas? Answer: there is no one who does not possess the uncontaminated dharmas, since one necessarily possesses cessation not through deliberate discernment; but there is one who does not possess the awakening-factors — namely, every ordinary worldling, since every ordinary worldling necessarily does not possess the conditioned uncontaminated dharmas.

諸得等覺支彼得無漏法耶。答諸得等覺支彼得無漏法。有得無漏法非等覺支。謂諸異生。問等覺支言得。是事可爾。入正性離生時未曾得而得故。無漏法云何言得。無有本來不成就非擇滅故。有說。

Does one who attains the awakening-factors thereby attain the uncontaminated dharmas? Answer: one who attains the awakening-factors does indeed attain the uncontaminated dharmas; there is one who attains the uncontaminated dharmas yet does not attain the awakening-factors — namely, every ordinary worldling. Question: it may well be said that one attains the awakening-factors, since, upon entering the fixed nature of the noble ones, one attains what one had never before attained; but how can it be said that one attains the uncontaminated dharmas, given that there has never been a time when one did not already possess cessation not through deliberate discernment? Some say:

此文但應言得等覺支。不應言得無漏法。有說。此中無漏法有二品。一覺支二擇滅。入正性離生時得覺支。隨離何品染時得擇滅。

this passage ought to speak only of attaining the awakening-factors, and ought not to speak of attaining the uncontaminated dharmas. Some say: here, the uncontaminated dharmas are of two kinds — first, the awakening-factors, and second, cessation through discernment. Upon entering the fixed nature of the noble ones, one attains the awakening-factors; upon becoming free of the defilement of whatever grade, one attains cessation through discernment —

異生於八地見修所斷隨離何品皆得擇滅。故作是說。有說。此中無漏法有三品。一覺支二擇滅三非擇滅。入正性離生時得覺支。隨離何品染時得擇滅。隨於何時得非擇滅。問豈不異生本皆成就非擇滅耶。何故言得。答雖本成就而依勝進位中亦可言得。如或以施。或以戒。或以聞。或以思。或以不淨觀持息念念住。或以煗頂忍世第一法等故。令惡趣等法得非擇滅。復次若以種類言之不應言得。若以事差別言之亦可言得。剎那剎那隨彼彼事各別得故。諸捨等覺支彼捨無漏法耶。答無全捨等覺支。亦無全捨無漏法。必無聖者還為異生故。亦無有情不成就非擇滅故。然非不有隨分捨義故言無全捨。諸退等覺支彼退無漏法耶。答無全退等覺支。亦無全退無漏法。義如前釋。復次為止摩訶僧祇部說預流果有退。及止譬喻者不許有非擇滅法。故作是說。無全捨全退等覺支及無漏法。諸未斷彼未遍知耶。乃至廣說。此中依二種遍知作論。一智遍知。二斷遍知。於智遍知中。有說。唯無漏智。有說。通有漏無漏智。諸說唯無漏智者。彼說苦現觀時於五所斷法由智遍知故名遍知。於見苦所斷法由斷遍知故名斷。集現觀時亦爾。差別者說自諦。滅現觀時於滅諦由智遍知故名遍知。於見滅所斷法由斷遍知故名斷。道現觀時亦爾。差別者說自諦。修道中隨起何智於隨何所緣法。由智遍知故名遍知。於隨何所斷法。由斷遍知故名斷。是謂此處略毘婆沙。諸未斷彼未遍知耶。答諸未遍知彼未斷。要由智知乃能斷故。有未斷非未遍知。謂若智遍知故已遍知。非斷遍知故已斷此謂苦智已生集智未生。於四所斷法由智遍知故已遍知。非斷遍知故已斷。集智已生滅智未生。於三所斷法滅智已生道智未生。於二所斷法道智已生未離修所斷染。於一所斷法由智遍知故已遍知。非斷遍知故已斷。

since an ordinary worldling, across the eight grounds, attains cessation through discernment upon becoming free of the defilement of whatever grade abandoned by seeing and by cultivation — and it is for this reason that this is said. Some say: here, the uncontaminated dharmas are of three kinds — first, the awakening-factors; second, cessation through discernment; and third, cessation not through deliberate discernment. Upon entering the fixed nature of the noble ones, one attains the awakening-factors; upon becoming free of the defilement of whatever grade, one attains cessation through discernment; and at whatever moment, one attains cessation not through deliberate discernment.
