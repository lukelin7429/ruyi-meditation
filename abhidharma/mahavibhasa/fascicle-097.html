1. Mindfulness, Investigation-of-Dharma, Effort, and Joy: Which Co-Arise?

Fascicle 97 opens a new, exhaustive apparatus: for every pair among the seven awakening-factors and eight path-branches, is whatever dharma is associated with one also associated with the other? Since association is itself a genuinely existent relation (against Dārṣṭāntika denial that any two mental concomitants can be said to co-arise as a real relation at all), each pair yields a tetralemma sized to how broadly each member's own scope extends — a lesser tetralemma when both pervade every ground and every undefiled mind, a middling tetralemma when one is narrower, and later a greater tetralemma when both are narrower in different ways. Mindfulness pairs against investigation-of-dharma, joy, and its own identity with right mindfulness; investigation-of-dharma pairs against effort and joy, its identity with right view deferred; effort pairs against joy and its own identity with right effort.

3. Defining Worldly Right View and Worldly Right Knowledge

A new topic opens: worldly right view (世俗正見) and worldly right knowledge (世俗正智) — first refuting the Dārṣṭāntika claim that wholesome mental-consciousness wisdom drawn forth by the five senses, or arising at the very moment of death, fails to qualify as vision at all, then defining worldly right view as the contaminated wholesome wisdom of mental consciousness (attained through preparatory effort, through freedom from defilement, or innately), and worldly right knowledge as this same wisdom together with the wholesome wisdom of the five sense-consciousnesses — illustrated through vivid examples: the wholesome eye-consciousness that arises on beholding one's own parents or a buddha, the wholesome tongue-consciousness that arises only for a contemplative practitioner mindfully observing his own food. A tetralemma, possession analysis, and cutting-off analysis close out the topic.

4. Why "Worldly"? Change, Ruin, and the Venerable Fengshan's Question

A genuinely elegant etymological puzzle asks why these forms of wisdom are called "worldly" at all: is it because they are subject to change and ruin, or because they are the very basis of craving? Since the noble path, too, is subject to change and ruin, yet is never called worldly, the resolution refines the criterion through four converging distinctions — the noble path may change, but it does not continue existence, perpetuate birth-and-death, proceed toward suffering, or establish craving's own ground the way worldly dharmas do. The Venerable Fengshan's own scriptural dialogue with the Buddha confirms the etymology directly: it is because the twelve sense-fields are subject to change that they are named worldly, with a final tetralemma distinguishing "change" (subtle, momentary) from "ruin" (coarse, affecting a continuity or something external).

5. Undefiled Vision and Knowledge, and Why the Noble Path Can Still Be Relinquished

Undefiled vision and undefiled knowledge receive the same careful treatment already given their worldly counterparts — vision being every undefiled wisdom except the knowledge of exhaustion and of non-arising, knowledge being every undefiled wisdom except the undefiled forbearances — before a genuinely striking puzzle closes the section: if the undefiled is never "cut off" in the ordinary sense (since only what is defiled requires cutting off, exactly as only a soiled garment requires washing), how does one reconcile this with the Buddha's own raft simile, in which even the dharma itself — the noble path — is said to be something the monk ought eventually to relinquish? The answer distinguishes two kinds of cutting-off: cutting off through the exhaustion of craving, which the noble path never undergoes, and cutting off through relinquishment, which it does — illustrated through monks who, having already attained arhatship, continue practicing the path out of gratitude until the body's own four hundred and four diseases press upon them, at which point the Buddha tells them to relinquish it at last and enter the nirvāṇa without remainder.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The learner's own asymmetric achievement — wrong view fully cut off the instant the path of seeing completes, while wrong knowledge (the broader, defiled wisdom of the five senses and the remaining afflictions) still requires nine further grades of cultivation to fully exhaust — is a compact illustration of a pattern this entire translation project has traced since the Fetters Aggregate: a single decisive moment of insight can permanently close off an entire category of affliction, while other categories, no less real, simply require sustained, incremental labor that no flash of understanding can shortcut.

9. Right View and Right Knowledge, General and Complete

The fascicle closes by defining right view and right knowledge in their fullest, most general sense — not merely "worldly" right view as earlier, but the wholesome mental-consciousness wisdom not gathered within the knowledge of exhaustion and of non-arising (right view), and this same wisdom together with the five senses' own wholesome wisdom, not gathered within the undefiled forbearances (right knowledge) — each spanning both the contaminated and the undefiled. The full tetralemma, possession, and cutting-off apparatus closes the fascicle exactly as it closed the parallel worldly and undefiled analyses earlier, confirming that, speaking with reference to the ultimate, only the arhat possesses right view and right knowledge belonging to every ground in their entirety.
