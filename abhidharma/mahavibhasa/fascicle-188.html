The Single Path and the Four Wholesome Roots

Fascicle 188 continues the Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa (念住納息第一之二): a second threefold division of mindfulness-foundation into hearing, reflection, and cultivation; how it predominates differently in the Buddha, the pratyekabuddha, and the śrāvaka; the precise sequence from preliminary effortful practice through to the first wholesome root, "heat"; five interpretations of what breaks through ignorance; whether the Buddha reached the very limit of mindfulness-foundation before his final nirvāṇa; the monk's proper domain; guiding an assembly without joy or grief; seven candidate meanings for why mindfulness-foundation is called "the single path," including the parable of the single-gated frontier city; and a closing combinatorial analysis of cultivation across the four wholesome roots. This page carries Ru‑Yi's complete translation.

1. Three More Kinds: Hearing, Reflection, and Cultivation

The Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa continues with a second threefold division — mindfulness-foundation accomplished through hearing, through reflection, and through cultivation — distinguished by whether understanding still depends on a name, mapped unevenly across the three realms, and shown to form a strict causal chain in which each stage can only produce itself and the one above it, never the one below.

Chinese Source

復次有三種念住。謂聞思修所成差別。此中有說。於佛所說十二分教。受持讀誦思量分別名聞所成念住。依聞起思。依思起修。依修能斷煩惱。如依金鑛出金。依金出金剛。依金剛能壞石等堅物。脅尊者言。於佛所說十二分教。受持讀誦思量分別。是生得慧。依生得慧起聞所成念住。依聞起思。依思起修。依修能斷煩惱。如依種子生芽。依芽生莖。依莖生枝葉。依枝葉生花菓。問此三念住何差別。有說。名則差別。謂此名聞所成念住。乃至此名修所成念住。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, there are three kinds of mindfulness-foundation — namely, distinguished as accomplished through hearing, through reflection, and through cultivation. Here, some say: retaining, reciting, reflecting upon, and analyzing the twelve divisions of the Buddha's own teaching is named the mindfulness-foundation accomplished through hearing; relying on hearing, reflection arises; relying on reflection, cultivation arises; and relying on cultivation, one is able to sever affliction — just as, relying on the ore, gold is extracted, and, relying on gold, a diamond is extracted, and, relying on the diamond, one is able to shatter stone and other hard substances. The Venerable Pārśva says: retaining, reciting, reflecting upon, and analyzing the twelve divisions of the Buddha's own teaching is innate wisdom; relying on innate wisdom, the mindfulness-foundation accomplished through hearing arises; relying on hearing, reflection arises; relying on reflection, cultivation arises; and relying on cultivation, one is able to sever affliction — just as, relying on the seed, the sprout is generated, and, relying on the sprout, the stem is generated, and, relying on the stem, the branches and leaves are generated, and, relying on the branches and leaves, the flowers and fruit are generated. Question: what distinguishes these three mindfulness-foundations? Some say: it is merely the name that differs — that is, this one is named the mindfulness-foundation accomplished through hearing, and so on up to, this one is named the mindfulness-foundation accomplished through cultivation.

有說。聞所成念住一切時依名於義轉。謂素怛纜說有何義。毘柰耶說有何義。阿毘達磨說有何義。鄔波拕耶說有何義。阿遮利耶說有何義。餘書論說有何義。思所成念住或依名。或不依名於義而轉。

Some say: the mindfulness-foundation accomplished through hearing, at every time, operates upon its meaning by depending on a name — that is, asking what meaning the Sūtra states, what meaning the Vinaya states, what meaning the Abhidharma states, what meaning the preceptor states, what meaning the teacher states, and what meaning the remaining treatises and commentaries state; the mindfulness-foundation accomplished through reflection operates upon its meaning either depending on a name, or without depending on a name;

修所成念住一切時離名於義轉。譬如三人俱在池浴。初人未學浮。

the mindfulness-foundation accomplished through cultivation, at every time, operates upon its meaning apart from any name — just as three people are alike bathing in a pool: the first has not yet learned to swim,

第二半學。第三善學。未學浮者一切時依岸浴。半學浮者或依岸或離岸而浴。善學浮者恒時離岸在中而浴。如第一人聞所成亦爾。如第二人。思所成亦爾。如第三人修所成亦爾。是謂此三念住差別。

the second has half learned, and the third has thoroughly learned. The one who has not yet learned to swim bathes, at every time, depending on the shore; the one who has half learned bathes sometimes depending on the shore and sometimes apart from the shore; and the one who has thoroughly learned bathes, at every time, apart from the shore, out in the middle of the pool — just as with the first person, so, too, with that accomplished through hearing; just as with the second person, so, too, with that accomplished through reflection; and just as with the third person, so, too, with that accomplished through cultivation. This, then, is what distinguishes these three mindfulness-foundations.

問此聞思修所成念住。何界有幾種。答欲界有二。謂聞思所成非修。所以者何。欲界是不定界非修地非離染地。作意修時便墮思中故。色界有二。謂聞修所成非思。所以者何。色界是定界是修地是離染地。作意思時便墮修中故。無色界有一。謂修所成。然修所成亦不墮界。有餘師說。欲界具有三種。餘如前說。有說。欲色界各具三種。餘如前說。有說。三界皆具三種。唯修所成亦不墮界。評曰。初說者好。問此諸念住誰為誰因。答聞為三種因。思唯思因非聞因。以彼劣故非修因。以界別故。修唯修因非聞因。以彼劣故非思因。以彼劣及界別故。誰為誰果。答聞唯聞果思聞思果非修。修聞修果非思。

Question: of these mindfulness-foundations accomplished through hearing, reflection, and cultivation, how many kinds exist in which realm? Answer: the desire realm possesses two — namely, those accomplished through hearing and through reflection, but not through cultivation. Why is this? Because the desire realm is a realm not fixed in concentration, neither a ground of cultivation nor a ground of freedom from defilement, and, when mental application turns to cultivation, it thereupon falls into reflection instead. The form realm possesses two — namely, those accomplished through hearing and through cultivation, but not through reflection. Why is this? Because the form realm is a realm fixed in concentration, a ground of cultivation, and a ground of freedom from defilement, and, when mental application turns to reflection, it thereupon falls into cultivation instead. The formless realm possesses one — namely, that accomplished through cultivation; yet that accomplished through cultivation does not, in fact, fall within any realm at all. Other masters say: the desire realm possesses all three kinds, the rest being as already explained. Some say: the desire and form realms each possess all three kinds, the rest being as already explained. Some say: all three realms alike possess all three kinds, only that accomplished through cultivation not falling within any realm at all. Assessment: the first explanation is preferable. Question: which of these mindfulness-foundations is the cause of which? Answer: that accomplished through hearing is the cause of all three; that accomplished through reflection is the cause only of itself, not of that accomplished through hearing, since it is inferior to it, nor of that accomplished through cultivation, since they belong to different realms; and that accomplished through cultivation is the cause only of itself, not of that accomplished through hearing, since it is inferior to it, nor of that accomplished through reflection, since it is both inferior to it and belongs to a different realm. Which is the effect of which? Answer: that accomplished through hearing is the effect only of that accomplished through hearing; that accomplished through reflection is the effect of both that accomplished through hearing and through reflection, but not of that accomplished through cultivation; and that accomplished through cultivation is the effect of both that accomplished through hearing and through cultivation, but not of that accomplished through reflection.

問此三念住誰現前修幾。答聞所成現前時唯修聞非思修。思所成現前時唯修思非聞修。此中聞思剎那現前時以習修故名修。非修未來以勢劣故。則以此故唯修自不修他。修所成現前時能修三種。聞思自力雖不能修未來。而由他力有未來修義。

Question: of these three mindfulness-foundations, when one manifests currently before the mind, how many does it cultivate? Answer: when that accomplished through hearing manifests currently before the mind, it cultivates only that accomplished through hearing, not that accomplished through reflection or through cultivation; when that accomplished through reflection manifests currently before the mind, it cultivates only that accomplished through reflection, not that accomplished through hearing or through cultivation. Here, when hearing or reflection manifest currently before the mind for a single moment, it is on account of habituated cultivation that this is named cultivation; it does not cultivate the future, since its own force is too weak — and it is for this reason that it cultivates only itself, not the others. When that accomplished through cultivation manifests currently before the mind, it is able to cultivate all three kinds; although that accomplished through hearing or through reflection cannot, by its own power, cultivate the future, there is, nevertheless, a sense in which future cultivation occurs by the power of another.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The swimmer analogy is unusually vivid for this literature: the beginner clings to the shore (needs a name to anchor meaning), the intermediate sometimes lets go, and the one who has thoroughly learned swims perpetually out in the open water, meaning operating without any verbal scaffold at all — a rare case where the treatise reaches for embodied skill rather than doctrinal category to explain how understanding matures.

2. Buddha, Pratyekabuddha, and Śrāvaka: Which Predominates

Although all three possess every kind of mindfulness-foundation, cultivation predominates in the Buddha, reflection in the pratyekabuddha, and hearing in the śrāvaka — a distinction traced to a further threefold set of mindfulness-foundations named for the consummation of verbal expression, of reflection, and of renunciation, each of which, the treatise notes, can equally well be classed under any of the first three.

Chinese Source

問聞思修所成念住。佛獨覺聲聞各有幾種。答佛具三種而修所成為勝。以自然覺及具力無畏等功德故。獨覺亦具有三而思所成為勝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: of the mindfulness-foundations accomplished through hearing, reflection, and cultivation, how many kinds does each of the Buddha, the pratyekabuddha, and the śrāvaka possess? Answer: the Buddha possesses all three kinds, yet that accomplished through cultivation predominates in him, since he possesses spontaneous awakening together with such merits as the powers and the fearlessnesses. The pratyekabuddha, too, possesses all three, yet that accomplished through reflection predominates in him,

以自思惟覺而無力無畏等功德故。聲聞亦具三種而聞所成為勝。以從聞他音入聖道故。如說。

since he possesses awakening through his own reflection alone, without the merits of the powers and the fearlessnesses. The śrāvaka, too, possesses all three, yet that accomplished through hearing predominates in him, since he enters the noble path by hearing another's voice. As it is said:

我聖弟子聞杖具足斷不善法修習善法。復有三種念住。謂言說究竟念住。思惟究竟念住。出離究竟念住。應知此三則聞思修所成念住。如其次第。然聞等三種念住一切皆可名聞所成。如說。

"my noble disciples, complete with the staff of hearing, sever unwholesome dharmas and cultivate wholesome dharmas." There are, furthermore, three kinds of mindfulness-foundation — namely, the mindfulness-foundation of the consummation of verbal expression, the mindfulness-foundation of the consummation of reflection, and the mindfulness-foundation of the consummation of renunciation. It should be understood that these three are, respectively, precisely the mindfulness-foundations accomplished through hearing, reflection, and cultivation, in their own order. Yet these three kinds of mindfulness-foundation — hearing, and the rest — may every one of them alike be named accomplished through hearing. As it is said:

多聞能知法　多聞能離罪多聞捨無義　多聞得涅槃一切皆可名思所成。如說。思者是業。慮者是慧。彼所說慮則思所成。一切皆可名。修所成如說。云何應修法。謂一切善有為法。

"through much hearing, one is able to know the Dharma; through much hearing, one is able to be free of transgression; through much hearing, one relinquishes what is meaningless; through much hearing, one attains nirvāṇa." Every one of them alike may also be named accomplished through reflection. As it is said: "volition is karma; consideration is wisdom" — what is there spoken of as consideration is precisely what is accomplished through reflection. Every one of them alike may also be named accomplished through cultivation, as it is said: "what is the dharma that should be cultivated? It is every wholesome conditioned dharma."

3. From Preliminary Practice to the First Wholesome Root

A precise technical sequence is laid out: the contemplation of impurity, mindfulness of breathing, and the mental application on the elements as preliminary effortful practice; the four mindfulness-foundations of intrinsic nature arising body, feeling, mind, then dharmas; the mixed-object and threefold-contemplation stages; and the full unfolding through hearing-accomplished, reflection-accomplished, and finally cultivation-accomplished mindfulness-foundation — this last, taking all four truths at once with sixteen modes of activity, named the very first of the four wholesome roots, "heat."

Chinese Source

問何謂念住加行。云何自相種性雜緣。及聞思修所成念住生起次第。答不淨觀持息念界作意。是謂念住加行。則此為先入自相種性身念住。則身念住為先入自相種性受念住。則受念住為先入自相種性心念住。則心念住為先入自相種性法念住。從自相種性法念住起雜緣法念住。從雜緣法念住起三義觀。從三義觀有聞所成身念住。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: what is the effortful practice preliminary to mindfulness-foundation? And what is the order in which the self-characteristic-and-own-nature kind, the mixed-object kind, and the mindfulness-foundations accomplished through hearing, reflection, and cultivation arise? Answer: the contemplation of impurity, mindfulness of breathing, and the mental application on the elements — these are named the effortful practice preliminary to mindfulness-foundation. Taking this as what precedes, one enters the self-characteristic-and-own-nature mindfulness-foundation of the body; taking the mindfulness-foundation of the body as what precedes, one enters the self-characteristic-and-own-nature mindfulness-foundation of feeling; taking the mindfulness-foundation of feeling as what precedes, one enters the self-characteristic-and-own-nature mindfulness-foundation of mind; and taking the mindfulness-foundation of mind as what precedes, one enters the self-characteristic-and-own-nature mindfulness-foundation of dharmas. From the self-characteristic-and-own-nature mindfulness-foundation of dharmas, the mixed-object mindfulness-foundation of dharmas arises. From the mixed-object mindfulness-foundation of dharmas, the threefold contemplation arises. From the threefold contemplation, the mindfulness-foundation of the body accomplished through hearing arises,

Immediately following this, the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas arises, taking up its sixteen modes of activity, arising by taking the four truths as its object. From the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas accomplished through reflection, the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas accomplished through cultivation arises, first taking up the modes of activity of impermanence and so on up to non-self, arising by taking the truth of suffering as its object; next taking up the modes of activity of cause and so on up to condition, arising by taking the truth of origin as its object; next taking up the modes of activity of cessation,

This mindfulness-foundation of dharmas, taking up sixteen modes of activity by taking the four truths as its object in just this way, is named the initial stage of heat. This, then, is the order in which the self-characteristic-and-own-nature kind, the mixed-object kind, and the mindfulness-foundations accomplished through hearing, reflection, and cultivation arise, drawn forth by the effortful practice preliminary to mindfulness-foundation. Question: why do the mindfulness-foundations accomplished through hearing and through reflection each first give rise to the mindfulness-foundation of the body, taking up twelve modes of activity by taking the three truths as its object, only afterward giving rise to the other mindfulness-foundations, whereas the mindfulness-foundation accomplished through cultivation first gives rise directly to the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas, taking up sixteen modes of activity by taking the four truths as its object? Answer: because it is a disposition never before attained, the modes of activity are attained only gradually — the coarse aggregate is contemplated first, and the subtle aggregate afterward. Among the five aggregates, the form-aggregate is the coarsest, and hence it is contemplated first, giving rise to the mindfulness-foundation of the body first. Yet, since the body, feeling, and mind are all alike subsumed under the three truths, the mindfulness-foundation of the body takes up twelve modes of activity by taking the three truths as its object. In this way, the mindfulness-foundation of the body accomplished through hearing draws forth the mindfulness-foundation of feeling accomplished through hearing, the mindfulness-foundation of feeling accomplished through hearing draws forth the mindfulness-foundation of mind accomplished through hearing, and the mindfulness-foundation of mind accomplished through hearing draws forth the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas accomplished through hearing — and this mindfulness-foundation of dharmas accomplished through hearing takes up sixteen modes of activity by taking the four truths as its object. Once the four mindfulness-foundations accomplished through hearing have been gradually perfected, they are then able to draw forth the four mindfulness-foundations accomplished through reflection; and, once the four mindfulness-foundations accomplished through reflection have been gradually perfected, they are further able to draw forth the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas accomplished through cultivation. Since this is a disposition already attained before, its modes of activity remain firm and stable with respect to every aggregate — and hence it first gives rise directly to the mindfulness-foundation of dharmas,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The gold-ore-to-diamond image and Pārśva's parallel seed-to-fruit image both insist on the same point from different angles: hearing, reflection, and cultivation are not three independent options a practitioner might choose among, but stages of a single refining or growing process, where each stage is unusable for severing affliction precisely because it is still on its way to becoming the next.

4. Breaking Through Ignorance

Five distinct interpretations are offered for what a sūtra means by "cultivating the mindfulness-foundation of the straight, right vow" that is able to break through ignorance and give rise to illumination — ranging from the patient acceptance of the cognition of the Dharma of suffering, to the vajra-like concentration itself, to the Venerable Pārśva's own reading in terms of the three wholesome and unwholesome roots.

Chinese Source

先緣苦諦作四行相。乃至後緣道諦作四行相。如契經說。如是修直正願念住能破無明發起於明。此中說。何名修直正願念住耶。有作是說。此中說苦法智忍以此能破無明。則欲界見苦所斷十隨眠發起於明則苦法智。有說。此中說道類智忍以此能破無明。則色無色界見道所斷十四隨眠發起於明則道類智。有說。此中說金剛喻定以此能破無明。則非想非非想處軟軟品煩惱發起於明則盡智。有說。此中說一切無間道以此能破無明。則彼所斷煩惱品發起於明則彼品解脫道脇尊者言。此中說方便善巧所攝受善慧名修直正願念住。以此能破無明。則三不善根發起於明則三善根。

Ru-Yi Translation

first taking the truth of suffering as its object, taking up four modes of activity, and so on up to, lastly taking the truth of the path as its object, taking up four modes of activity. As a sūtra states: cultivating the mindfulness-foundation of the straight, right vow in this way is able to break through ignorance and give rise to illumination. Here it may be asked: what is meant by "cultivating the mindfulness-foundation of the straight, right vow"? Some say: this refers to the patient acceptance of the cognition of the Dharma of suffering — it is by this that ignorance is broken through, the ten latent tendencies abandoned by seeing suffering in the desire realm thereby giving rise to illumination, this illumination being the cognition of the Dharma of suffering. Some say: this refers to the patient acceptance of the subsequent cognition of the path — it is by this that ignorance is broken through, the fourteen latent tendencies abandoned by seeing the path in the form and formless realms thereby giving rise to illumination, this illumination being the subsequent cognition of the path. Some say: this refers to the vajra-like concentration — it is by this that ignorance is broken through, the weakest-of-the-weak grade of affliction of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception thereby giving rise to illumination, this illumination being the cognition of exhaustion. Some say: this refers to every uninterrupted path — it is by this that ignorance is broken through, the class of affliction severed by each thereby giving rise to illumination, this illumination being the path of liberation of that same class. The Venerable Pārśva says: this refers to the wholesome wisdom subsumed under skillful expedient means, named cultivating the mindfulness-foundation of the straight, right vow — it is by this that ignorance is broken through, the three unwholesome roots thereby giving rise to illumination, this illumination being the three wholesome roots.

5. Did the Buddha Reach the Limit of Mindfulness-Foundation?

A verse describing the Buddha's own conduct as boundless, with "no track," is set against the doctrine that he perfected mindfulness-foundation before his final nirvāṇa — resolved through three paired distinctions between drawing something forth and making use of it, between acquiring it and it residing in the body, and between accomplishing it and its manifesting before the mind.

Chinese Source

A four-term chain — mindfulness-foundation, correct principle, the noble path, and the deathless — receives six parallel interpretations, each mapping the same four stages onto a different technical distinction already established across the treatise, from the three kinds of mindfulness-foundation to the path of seeing, cultivation, and the non-trainee.

11. The Four Wholesome Roots: A Combinatorial Analysis of Cultivation

Introducing the four (or, per the Western masters, six) kinds of cultivation, the fascicle closes with an exhaustive combinatorial mapping of present and future cultivation across the four wholesome-root stages leading to the path of seeing — heat, summit, patient acceptance, and the supreme mundane dharma, each in its initial and increasing grade — tracking exactly when cultivation is confined to the same class of mindfulness-foundation and when it extends to different classes, and why the supreme mundane dharma, like the path of seeing itself, admits of no further garment to be stripped away.
