An Exhaustive Ledger of Every Faculty's Conditions

Fascicle 146 works through a census of causal correspondence, the four-category analysis of having or lacking an object, the noble-one/ordinary-being tetralemma across six glosses, and closes with an exhaustive ledger of which conditions each of the twenty-two faculties bears toward every other. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 146 complete, 13 sections Source: Taishō T1545, vol. 27, fascicle 146 Topic: 根蘊第六 → 根納息第一之五 concludes (the Faculties Aggregate; the Faculty Nyāsa concludes)

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 146 continues and concludes the Faculty Nyāsa (根納息第一之五). Terms established across fascicles 93–145 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Fourteen Faculties, and How Many Ways to Count a Cause

Fascicle 146 opens with a fresh census: of the twenty-two faculties, how many are "associated by cause"? The treatise walks through no fewer than six different exegetical frameworks — one built around a single cause, one around two, one around three, one around four, one around five, and one around all six causes at once — each arriving at the same headline number, fourteen, while explaining that number on entirely different technical grounds.

Chinese Source

此二十二根。幾因相應。乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答欲止說因緣法非實有者意。顯因緣法決定實有。亦為遮止愚於相應執相應法非實者意。令知相應是實有故。而作斯論。於此義中。有說。依一因作論。謂相應因。由此中說相應言故。依彼意趣釋此文者。此二十二根。幾因相應。答十四。謂意五受信等五三無漏根。此是相應因自體。根與相應因自體法相應故。名因相應。幾因不相應。答八。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Of these twenty-two faculties, how many are 'associated by cause,' and so on, as stated at length?" Why raise this question at all? To block the view that causal dharmas are not truly real, and to show that causal dharmas are indeed real; and likewise to block the view of one confused about association, who holds that associated dharmas are not real, making it known that association is genuinely real. One position frames the question around a single cause alone, the associated cause, since this passage speaks specifically of "association." On that reading: of the twenty-two faculties, how many are associated by cause? Fourteen — the mind-faculty, the five feeling-faculties, faith and its four companions, and the three uncontaminated faculties. These are themselves the very substance of the associated cause, and a faculty is called "associated by cause" precisely because it is associated with the dharma that is the substance of the associated cause. How many are not associated by cause? Eight.

謂七色命根。問此八既非相應因體。如何乃說因不相應。答此八雖非相應因體。而與相應因體不相應故。說為因不相應。斯有何失。

Namely the seven form-faculties and the life-faculty. If these eight are not themselves the substance of the associated cause, how can they be described as "not associated by cause" at all? Because, although they are not themselves the substance of the associated cause, they are simply not associated with whatever is the substance of the associated cause — hence they are called "not associated by cause." What fault could this possibly carry?

幾因相應因不相應。答即前十四少分因相應。少分因不相應。少分因相應者。謂自性於他性少分因不相應者。謂自性於自性。幾非因相應。非因不相應。答即前十四少分。非因相應。少分非因不相應少分非因相應者。謂自性於自性。少分非因不相應者。謂自性於他性。有說。此中依二因作論。謂相應因俱有因。由此二因恒與彼法不相離故。依彼意趣釋此文者。此二十二根。幾因相應。答十四。

How many are both associated by cause and not associated by cause? The same fourteen, in part associated by cause and in part not. "In part associated by cause" means one's own nature in relation to another's nature; "in part not associated by cause" means one's own nature in relation to itself. How many are neither associated by cause nor not associated by cause? The same fourteen, in part. "In part neither associated by cause" means one's own nature in relation to itself; "in part neither not associated by cause" means one's own nature in relation to another's nature. Another position frames the question around two causes at once, the associated cause and the co-existent cause, since these two causes are never separate from the dharmas they condition. On that reading: of the twenty-two faculties, how many are associated by cause? Fourteen.

此是二因自體。根與二因自體法相應故。名因相應。後三問答如前應知。有說此中依三因作論。謂相應因。俱有因。同類因。由此三因通三性故。有說。此中依四因作論。謂除同類遍行二因。由此四因通三世故。有說。此中依五因作論。謂除能作因。以通無為非親勝故。有說。此中依六因作論。由此所說因言總故。然相應法或作六因自體。或作五因自體。或作四因自體。如大種蘊廣說。依彼意趣釋此文者。此二十二根。幾因相應。答十四。謂六因自體。根與六因自體法相應。五因自體。根與五因自體法相應。四因自體。根與四因自體法相應故名因相應。後三問答如前應知。

These are the very substance of the two causes, and a faculty is called "associated by cause" because it is associated with the dharma that is their substance; the remaining three question-and-answer pairs should be understood exactly as before. Another position frames the question around three causes — the associated cause, the co-existent cause, and the homogeneous cause — since these three causes pervade all three moral qualities. Another position frames it around four causes, excluding only the homogeneous and the universal causes, since these four pervade all three time-periods. Another position frames it around five causes, excluding only the efficient cause, since it pervades even the unconditioned and lacks any close, decisive function. Another position frames it around six causes altogether, since the very term "cause" used in this passage is meant comprehensively; an associated dharma may thus stand as the substance of six causes, of five, or of four, exactly as explained at length in the Great Elements Aggregate. On that reading: of the twenty-two faculties, how many are associated by cause? Fourteen — those that are the substance of six causes are associated with dharmas that are the substance of six causes; those that are the substance of five causes are associated with dharmas that are the substance of five causes; those that are the substance of four causes are associated with dharmas that are the substance of four causes — and each is accordingly called "associated by cause." The remaining three question-and-answer pairs should again be understood as before.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Watching six different schools of interpretation converge on the identical number fourteen while disagreeing completely about which causes are actually doing the work is a small but telling demonstration of how much interpretive latitude this tradition tolerates beneath a stable surface answer: the destination is fixed, but the commentarial road built to reach it is explicitly left open to dispute.

2. A Sighted Person Sees a Blind One: Four Categories of Having an Object

A parallel census opens for a different property entirely — not what causes a faculty, but what kind of object a faculty itself takes. Four categories emerge: faculties whose object itself has an object, faculties whose object lacks any object of its own, faculties capable of both, and faculties that fall into neither, each illustrated with the treatise's own memorable image of a sighted person watching either another sighted person or someone born blind.

Chinese Source

此二十二根。幾緣有緣。乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答欲止說所緣緣非實者意。顯所緣緣是實有故。而作斯論。此二十二根。幾緣有緣。答十三少分。謂意樂喜憂捨信等五三無漏根少分。有所緣法為此所緣。故說此為緣有緣句。如明眼者見明眼人。彼明眼人復有所見。緣有緣句應知亦爾。幾緣無緣。答一十三少分。一者謂苦根。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Of these twenty-two faculties, how many take an object that itself has an object, and so on, as stated at length?" Why raise this question at all? To block the view that the object-condition is not real, and to show that the object-condition is indeed real. Of the twenty-two faculties, how many belong to the "has an object that has an object" category? Thirteen in part — the mind-faculty, pleasure, joy, displeasure, and equanimity, and faith and its four companions, and the three uncontaminated faculties, each in part. A dharma that itself takes an object is what is meant by this object here, hence this is called the "has an object that has an object" phrase — just as a sighted person who sees another sighted person, and that second sighted person likewise has something they see; the "has an object that has an object" phrase should be understood in just the same way. How many belong to the "has an object without an object" category? Thirteen in part. One is the pain-faculty.

十三少分者。如前說。無所緣法為此所緣。故說此為緣無緣句。如明眼者見生盲人。彼生盲人更無所見。緣無緣句當知亦爾。幾緣有緣緣無緣。答即前十三少分。有所緣無所緣法。為此所緣。故說此為緣有緣緣無緣句。如明眼者見明眼人及生盲人。彼明眼人復有所見。彼生盲人更無所見緣有緣緣無緣句。應知亦爾。有餘謂。此第三句義。即合初二更無異體。此說不然。與本論相違故。如十門說。緣有緣法是有為緣隨眠隨增。緣無緣法是一切隨眠隨增。緣有緣緣無緣法。是有為緣隨眠隨增。非緣有緣非緣無緣法。是有漏緣隨眠隨增。然有意識并相應法。一剎那頃總緣有緣及無緣法。是故如前所說為善。幾非緣有緣非緣無緣。答八。謂七色命根。由此不緣有所緣無所緣法。故說此為非緣有緣非緣無緣句。如生盲人。都無所見。此句亦爾。

The remaining thirteen in part are as already described — a dharma without an object is what is meant by this object here, hence this is called the "has an object without an object" phrase, just as a sighted person who sees a person born blind, and that blind person has nothing further that they see; the "has an object without an object" phrase should be understood in just the same way. How many belong to the "has an object that has an object, and has an object without an object" category? The same thirteen in part — a dharma that both has and lacks an object is what is meant by this object here, hence this is called the "has an object that has an object, and has an object without an object" phrase, just as a sighted person who sees both a sighted person and a person born blind, where the sighted person has something further that they see and the blind person has nothing further that they see; this phrase should be understood in just the same way. Some hold that this third category carries no meaning distinct from a mere combination of the first two, with no separate substance of its own — but this account does not hold, since it contradicts the root treatise, which speaks of these matters through ten separate gateways: a dharma that has an object that has an object is the object-condition of the conditioned, and underlying tendencies increase upon it; a dharma that has an object without an object is the object-condition of everything, and underlying tendencies increase upon it; a dharma that has both is the object-condition of the conditioned, and underlying tendencies increase upon it; a dharma that has neither is the object-condition of what is contaminated, and underlying tendencies increase upon it. Yet mental cognition, together with its associated dharmas, can in a single instant take both a has-object and a without-object dharma together as its object all at once, which is exactly why the account already given should be preferred. How many belong to "neither has an object that has an object nor has an object without an object"? Eight — the seven form-faculties and the life-faculty. Since these do not take as their object anything that either has or lacks an object, they are called "neither has an object that has an object nor has an object without an object," just as a person born blind who sees nothing at all; this phrase should be understood in just the same way.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The image of sight watching sight, and sight watching blindness, does real conceptual work here: it distinguishes a faculty's own bare capacity to observe from the separate question of whether whatever it is observing has any onward capacity to observe something else in turn — a distinction easy to blur without the concrete image holding it in place.

3. The Same Four Categories, Applied to Everything Else in the Abhidharma Universe

Having established the four categories for the faculties themselves, the treatise extends them across the entire Abhidharma inventory: the eighteen elements, twelve bases, five aggregates, six elements, and then every major doctrinal classification in turn — form and formlessness, contamination and its absence, the three time-periods, the three realms, the trainee and the one beyond training, what is cut off by seeing or cultivation, the four immeasurables, and more.

Chinese Source

此中略示二十二根。緣有緣等四句差別十門所說。十八界等亦應以此四句分別。謂十八界中十色界為第四句。五識界為第二句。意界意識界為前三句。法界具為四句。十二處中十色處為第四句。意處為前三句。法處具為四句。五蘊中色蘊為第四句。受想識蘊為前三句。行蘊具為四句。如蘊取蘊亦爾。六界中五色界為第四句。識界為前三句。有色法。可見法。有對法。無為法。滅諦。為第四句。

Ru-Yi Translation

This passage briefly indicates how these four categories concerning the twenty-two faculties' having or lacking an object divide across ten distinct gateways; the eighteen elements and so forth should likewise be analyzed through these same four categories. Among the eighteen elements, the ten form-elements belong to the fourth category; the five sense-consciousness elements belong to the second category; the mental element and the mental-consciousness element belong to the first three categories; and the dharma-element is fully distributed across all four categories. Among the twelve bases, the ten form-bases belong to the fourth category; the mental base belongs to the first three categories; and the dharma-base is fully distributed across all four categories. Among the five aggregates, the form aggregate belongs to the fourth category; the feeling, perception, and consciousness aggregates belong to the first three categories; and the formations aggregate is fully distributed across all four categories — and the same holds for the grasping aggregates. Among the six elements, the five form-elements belong to the fourth category; the consciousness-element belongs to the first three categories. Whatever is form, whatever is visible, whatever is resistant, whatever is unconditioned, and the truth of cessation — these all belong to the fourth category.

無色法。無見法。無對法。有漏法。無漏法。有為法。過去未來現在法。善不善無記法。欲色無色界繫法。學無學非學非無學法。見修所斷不斷法。苦集道諦。靜慮無色。皆具為四句。四無量中。若取自性。緣住同分心有情。則為第三句。若緣不住同分心有情。則為第二句。若并取相應隨轉。緣住同分心有情。則為第三第四句。

Whatever is formless, invisible, and non-resistant; whatever is contaminated or uncontaminated; whatever is conditioned; whatever belongs to past, future, or present; whatever is wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral; whatever is bound to the desire, form, or formless realm; whatever belongs to the trainee, the one beyond training, or neither; whatever is cut off by seeing, cut off by cultivation, or altogether uncuttable; the truths of suffering, origin, and the path; and the dhyānas and the formless attainments — every one of these is fully and without exception distributed across all four categories. Among the four immeasurables, if one takes only their own intrinsic nature, and they take as their object sentient beings who share one's own class of mental continuum, they belong to the third category; if instead they take as object beings not of one's own class, they belong to the second category; and if one further includes their associated and concomitant dharmas, while still taking as object beings of one's own class, they belong to the third and fourth categories together.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That a single fourfold distinction, first introduced to sort twenty-two faculties, turns out to apply cleanly across virtually every other classification this tradition maintains is a striking display of the Abhidharma's own systematic ambition: the same conceptual tool, once forged, is expected to fit the entire doctrinal machine, not just the part it was built for.

4. Noble Ones, Ordinary Beings, and Six Different Ways to Draw the Line Between Them

After finishing the object-relation gateways through the liberations, masteries, totalities, concentrations, knowledges, and afflictions, the treatise turns to a rich new tetralemma: which faculties belong to "this dharma" and which to "the ordinary being"? No fewer than six competing glosses are offered for what these two terms even mean — the noble one versus the ordinary being; the patient acceptance of suffering versus the mundane supreme dharma; moral restraint versus its absence; an unimpaired faculty versus an impaired one; and, opening a sixth, unsevered wholesome roots versus severed ones.

Chinese Source

若緣不住同分心有情。則為第二第四句。初三解脫八勝處前八遍處。若取自性為第二句。若并取相應隨轉。則為第二第四句。滅受想解脫為第四句。餘四解脫具為四句。後二遍處為第三第四句。滅智無相等持為第二句。他心智為初句。餘六智二等持為前三句。諸門煩惱中。五識相應者為第二句。意識相應者為前三句。是中差別應思。廣說諸根此法彼根異生耶。設根異生彼根此法耶。答諸根此法彼根非異生。諸根異生彼根非此法。問何謂此法。何謂異生。答此法謂聖者。異生即異生。諸根此法彼根非異生者。謂諸無漏根唯聖者成就。非諸異生。諸根異生彼根非此法者。謂見所斷根。唯異生成就非諸聖者。有說。此法者。謂住苦法智忍。異生者。謂住世第一法。諸根此法彼根非異生者。謂苦法智忍俱生諸根。唯住苦法智忍者現起。非住世第一法者。諸根異生彼根非此法者。謂世第一法俱生諸根。唯住世第一法者現起。非住苦法智忍者。有說。此法者。謂住律儀。異生者。謂住不律儀。諸根住律儀者所起。彼根非住不律儀者所起。諸根住不律儀者所起。彼根非住律儀者所起。有說。此法者。謂不缺根。異生者。謂缺根。如扇[打-丁+虎]半擇迦無形二形等。諸根不缺根者所起。彼根非缺根者所起。諸根缺根者所起。彼根非不缺根者所起。有說此法者。謂不斷善根。異生者。

That the treatise needs six entirely separate glosses before it can even state what "this dharma" and "ordinary being" mean is itself instructive: rather than treating the noble-one/ordinary-being boundary as a single fixed line, this tradition treats it as a family of related but genuinely distinct boundaries — moral, meditative, physiological, and soteriological all at once — each cutting the same underlying territory a little differently.

5. Pure Abodes, Severed Roots, and What Each Aggregate Actually Contains

The tetralemma concludes with its final two glosses — the group fixed for correct or wrong attainment, and the five Pure Abodes set against the five stations of ordinary beings — before the fascicle pivots to a wholly new census: how many faculties does each of the five aggregates actually include? The form aggregate claims seven; the feeling aggregate claims five outright plus a fractional three; perception, tellingly, claims none at all.

Chinese Source

已知根與已知根。為因等無間所緣增上。因者三因。即相應俱有同類因。與七色根命根苦根。為一增上。與憂根未知當知根為所緣增上。與餘根。為因等無間所緣增上。餘根者。謂意樂喜捨信等五具知根。此亦具緣等故總說。然因有異謂與意等九根。為三因。即相應俱有同類因。與具知根為一因。即同類因。具知根與具知根。為因等無間所緣增上。因者三因。即相應俱有同類。與七色根命根苦根。為一增上。與憂根未知當知根已知根。為所緣增上。與餘根為因等無間所緣增上。餘根者。謂意樂喜捨信等五根。因者三因。即相應俱有同類因。等無間者。謂具知根等無間。意等九根現在前。

The already-known faculty bears cause, immediacy, object, and dominance toward the already-known faculty; the cause comprises three causes, the associated, co-existent, and homogeneous causes. It bears dominance alone toward the seven form-faculties, the life-faculty, and the pain-faculty; it bears object-dominance toward the displeasure-faculty and the faculty of the yet-unknown; it bears cause, immediacy, object, and dominance toward the remaining faculties — namely, the mind-faculty, pleasure, joy, equanimity, and faith with its four companions and the faculty of complete knowledge. The faculty of complete knowledge bears cause, immediacy, object, and dominance toward the faculty of complete knowledge; the cause comprises three causes, the associated, co-existent, and homogeneous causes. It bears dominance alone toward the seven form-faculties, the life-faculty, and the pain-faculty; it bears object-dominance toward the displeasure-faculty, the faculty of the yet-unknown, and the already-known faculty; it bears cause, immediacy, object, and dominance toward the remaining faculties — namely, the mind-faculty, pleasure, joy, equanimity, and faith with its four companions; the cause comprises three causes, the associated, co-existent, and homogeneous causes; "immediacy" means that the mind-faculty and the other eight faculties arise immediately after the faculty of complete knowledge.

所緣者。謂具知根與意等九根為所緣。增上者。謂不礙生及唯無障。

"Object" means that the faculty of complete knowledge takes the mind-faculty and the other eight faculties as its object; "dominance" means through mere non-obstruction of arising and simple absence of hindrance.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That even the three most exalted faculties in this entire system — the very markers of a path moving irreversibly toward liberation — still submit to exactly the same mechanical four-condition ledger applied to the eye or the pain-faculty is a fitting closing note: nothing in this cosmology, however soteriologically advanced, is exempt from the same exhaustive causal bookkeeping demanded of everything else.

Fascicle 146 continues and concludes the Faculty Nyāsa (根納息第一之五): a fresh census of causal correspondence across six exegetical frameworks; the four-category analysis of having or lacking an object, extended across the entire Abhidharma inventory; an extended tetralemma distinguishing the noble one from the ordinary being through six separate glosses; a full element-base-aggregate census of the wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral roots and of the faculty-dharma itself; three expository passes on whether a faculty can condition the arising of a faculty; and, closing both the fascicle and the entire Faculty Nyāsa, an exhaustive faculty-by-faculty ledger of cause, immediacy, object, and dominance running from the eye-faculty through the three uncontaminated faculties. Fascicle 147 opens an entirely new nyāsa within this same Sixth Aggregate: the Existence Nyāsa (根蘊第六中有納息第二之一), turning from the faculties themselves to the karmic origins of a being's continuity across rebirth.
