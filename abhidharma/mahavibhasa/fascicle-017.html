The Grass That Grows Without a Seed

Fascicle 17 continues the Knowledge Nyāsa's ninth part (智納息第二之九), first completing an exhaustive combinatorial map of which precepts “follow along” with mind through every stage of the path — from the sixteen moments of entering certainty to the puzzle of Buddhas awakening under wildly different human lifespans. The fascicle's second half opens the homogeneous-cause (同類因) debate, working through a cascade of scriptural objections before closing with a genuinely empirical dispute over whether grass can grow from bare mud with no seed ever planted. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 17 continues the Knowledge Nyāsa opened in Fascicle 9, now in its ninth part (之九). Terms established in earlier fascicles, such as the four noble truths, the nine uninterrupted paths of freeing oneself from defilement, and the co-existent-cause, recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Which Precepts Follow Mind? Why the Desire Realm Fails the Test

Fascicle 17 opens the question of which bodily and verbal karma “follows along” with mind moment by moment, rather than persisting independently once performed. The form realm's own precepts and the uncontaminated precepts qualify; the desire realm's own precepts do not — because the desire realm is neither a fit field nor a fit vessel for such a relationship, lacking any concentration, cultivation, or freedom from defilement capable of sustaining it.

Chinese Source

問身語業何等隨心轉。何等不隨心轉耶。答色界戒及無漏戒隨心轉。欲界戒及餘身語業不隨心轉。問何故欲界戒不隨心轉耶。答欲界於彼隨心轉戒。非田非器乃至廣說。復次欲界非定界非修地非離染地故。無如是道戒可隨轉。色界是定界是修地是離染地故。有如是道戒可隨轉。復次欲界戒義。問欲界心言汝能為我。斷破戒及起破戒煩惱不若能者我隨汝轉欲界心義。答欲界戒言我不能戒義。言若不能我何為隨汝轉如人怖怨。問他人曰。汝能為我作救護不。若能者當依止汝隨汝而轉他。答不能彼便語言汝若不能。我何為依止。汝隨汝轉此亦如是。問色界善心一切皆有隨轉戒不。答非一切有。謂初靜慮有六善心無隨轉戒。一善眼識。二善耳識。三善身識。四死時善心。五起表善心。六聞所成慧相應善心。第二第三第四靜慮。有二善心無隨轉戒。謂死時善心及聞所成心。問何故無色界無隨轉戒耶。答彼界於戒非田非器乃至廣說。復次戒是色一分攝。彼界無色故亦無戒。復次戒是大種所造。彼無大種故亦無戒。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: which bodily and verbal karma follows along with mind, and which does not? The answer: the form realm's own precepts, together with the uncontaminated precepts, follow along with mind; the desire realm's own precepts, and every other bodily and verbal karma, do not follow along with mind. One asks: why do the desire realm's own precepts not follow along with mind? The answer: the desire realm is, with respect to a mind-following precept of this kind, neither a fit field nor a fit vessel — and so on, as stated at length. Further, the desire realm is not a realm of concentration, not a stage of cultivation, and not a stage of freedom from defilement, and so it possesses no path-precept of this kind capable of following mind; the form realm, being a realm of concentration, a stage of cultivation, and a stage of freedom from defilement, does possess a path-precept of this kind capable of following mind. Further, as to the meaning of the desire realm's own precept: one asks, suppose a desire-realm mind were to say to it, "are you able, for my sake, to cut off precept-violation and the afflictions that give rise to precept-violation? If you are able, I shall follow your own course" — this is the meaning of a desire-realm mind. The answer: the desire realm's own precept would have to reply, "I am not able" — this is the very meaning of this precept; and, being unable, it could then be asked, "if you are not able, why should I follow your course at all?" — it is just as when a person, frightened of an enemy, asks another, "are you able to protect and rescue me? If you are able, I shall rely on you and follow your own course" — and that other replies, "I am not able" — whereupon the first person says, "if you are not able, why should I rely on you at all? You should instead follow my own course" — the case here is exactly the same. One asks: do all wholesome minds of the form realm possess a mind-following precept? The answer: not all do — that is, the first dhyāna has six kinds of wholesome mind lacking a mind-following precept: first, wholesome eye-consciousness; second, wholesome ear-consciousness; third, wholesome body-consciousness; fourth, the wholesome mind at the moment of death; fifth, the wholesome mind giving rise to manifest action; sixth, the wholesome mind associated with wisdom born of learning. The second, third, and fourth dhyānas have two kinds of wholesome mind lacking a mind-following precept — namely, the wholesome mind at the moment of death, and the mind associated with wisdom born of learning. One asks: why does the formless realm have no mind-following precept at all? The answer: that realm is, with respect to a precept of this kind, neither a fit field nor a fit vessel — and so on, as stated at length. Further, precept is included as a portion of material form, and since that realm has no material form at all, it likewise has no precept. Further, precept is composed of the great elements, and since that realm has no great elements at all, it likewise has no precept.

問如雖無無漏大種而有無漏戒。如是彼界雖無大種何妨有戒耶。答無漏戒非大種力故成無漏。但由心力隨無漏心所等起故。有漏戒由大種力繫屬界地故不相似。復次戒者對治破戒及起破戒煩惱。無色界道不能對治。破戒及起破戒煩惱故彼無戒。問因論生論何故無色界道。不能對治破戒及起破戒煩惱耶。答彼唯欲界。無色於欲有四事遠故無對治。四事遠者。一界地遠。二所依遠。三所緣遠。四對治遠。問若爾第二第三第四靜慮。亦無破戒及起破戒煩惱。對治彼應無戒。答對治有二種。一斷對治。二厭壞對治。上三靜慮於破戒及起破戒煩惱。雖無斷對治而有厭壞對治。如世尊說。聖弟子入不動心解脫。能斷不善修習善法。非彼身中猶有不善可斷。然依過患對治故作是說。無色界於破戒及起破戒煩惱。無斷對治亦無厭壞對治是故無戒。

One asks: just as, although there are no uncontaminated great elements, there is still an uncontaminated precept, why, then, should the absence of great elements in that realm prevent it from having a precept? The answer: the uncontaminated precept does not become uncontaminated through the power of the great elements at all, but solely through the power of mind, arising together with the uncontaminated mind it accompanies; the contaminated precept, by contrast, is bound to a particular realm and stage through the power of the great elements — and so the two cases are not analogous. Further, precept serves as the antidote to precept-violation and to the afflictions that give rise to precept-violation; the formless realm's own path is unable to serve as an antidote to precept-violation and its afflictions, and so that realm has no precept. One asks — a further question arising from this very discussion — why is the formless realm's own path unable to serve as an antidote to precept-violation and its afflictions? The answer: precept-violation belongs only to the desire realm, and the formless realm is separated from the desire realm by four kinds of distance, and so possesses no antidote to it. These four kinds of distance are: first, distance of realm and stage; second, distance of support; third, distance of object; fourth, distance of antidote. One asks: if that is so, then the second, third, and fourth dhyānas, too, are free of precept-violation and its afflictions — ought they not, then, likewise to lack any precept as an antidote to them? The answer: antidote is of two kinds: first, the antidote of cutting off; second, the antidote of wearying and destroying. The three higher dhyānas, with respect to precept-violation and its afflictions, lack the antidote of cutting off, yet do possess the antidote of wearying and destroying — as the World-Honored One states: a noble disciple who has entered the unshakable liberation of mind is able to cut off the unwholesome and cultivate the wholesome — not because there is still something unwholesome remaining within his own body to be cut off, but because this is spoken with reference to the antidote directed against the very faults themselves. The formless realm, with respect to precept-violation and its afflictions, possesses neither the antidote of cutting off nor the antidote of wearying and destroying, and so it has no precept at all.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The imagined dialogue here is doing real philosophical work through personification: a desire-realm precept is asked, in effect, whether it can protect the mind from further precept-violation, and must honestly answer “I am not able” — at which point there is no reason for mind to keep following its lead. This is a vivid way of saying that mind-following precept requires an ongoing protective relationship, not a one-time act, and the desire realm's own precepts, however sound they may be as isolated undertakings, simply lack the sustained antidote power that concentration and the uncontaminated path provide.

2. Path-Precept and Concentration-Precept: A Fourfold Grid

The treatise refines mind-following precept into two distinct kinds — precept co-existent with the (uncontaminated) path, and precept co-existent with (form-realm) concentration — before canvassing three competing views on how these two categories actually relate: are they mutually exclusive, does one wholly contain the other, or do they overlap only partially, depending on whether the precept in question belongs to a fundamental or a merely proximate stage?

Chinese Source

隨心轉戒總有二種。一道俱有戒。二定俱有戒。道俱有戒者謂無漏戒。定俱有戒者謂色界戒。若是道俱有戒彼非定俱有戒。若是定俱有戒彼非道俱有戒。有作是說道俱有戒謂無漏戒。定俱有戒謂一切有漏無漏隨心轉戒。彼作是說。一切道俱有戒皆是定俱有戒。或是定俱有戒而非道俱有戒。謂有漏隨心轉戒。有餘師說。道俱有戒謂無漏戒。定俱有戒謂根本靜慮有漏無漏戒。依如是說應作四句。有是道俱有戒非定俱有戒。謂近分地諸無漏戒。有是定俱有戒非道俱有戒。謂根本地諸有漏戒。有是道俱有戒亦是定俱有戒。謂根本地諸無漏戒。有非道俱有戒亦非定俱有戒。謂近分地諸有漏戒。彼師依得復作四句。有得道俱有戒非定俱有戒。謂未離欲染入正性離生十六心頃。若已離欲染依未至定入正性離生。見道十五心頃。若諸聖者為離欲染起加行道。九無間道八解脫道。若未離欲染信勝解鍊根。作見至所有加行。無間解脫道。若諸聖者未離欲染依未至定。

Ru-Yi Translation

Mind-following precept, in total, is of two kinds: first, the precept co-existent with the path; second, the precept co-existent with concentration. The precept co-existent with the path is the uncontaminated precept; the precept co-existent with concentration is the form realm's own precept. Whatever is a path-co-existent precept is not a concentration-co-existent precept, and whatever is a concentration-co-existent precept is not a path-co-existent precept. Some say instead: the path-co-existent precept is the uncontaminated precept, while the concentration-co-existent precept is every mind-following precept, whether contaminated or uncontaminated. On this position, every path-co-existent precept is also a concentration-co-existent precept, though there is a case of a concentration-co-existent precept that is not path-co-existent — namely, a contaminated mind-following precept. There are yet other masters who say: the path-co-existent precept is the uncontaminated precept, while the concentration-co-existent precept is the contaminated or uncontaminated precept belonging to a fundamental dhyāna. On this position, four alternatives should be formulated: there is a case of a path-co-existent precept that is not concentration-co-existent — namely, any uncontaminated precept of a proximate stage; there is a case of a concentration-co-existent precept that is not path-co-existent — namely, any contaminated precept of a fundamental stage; there is a case of a precept that is both path-co-existent and concentration-co-existent — namely, any uncontaminated precept of a fundamental stage; there is a case of a precept that is neither path-co-existent nor concentration-co-existent — namely, any contaminated precept of a proximate stage. These masters, with reference to attainment, further formulate four alternatives: there is a case of attaining the path-co-existent precept but not the concentration-co-existent precept — namely, during the sixteen moments of entering the certainty of rightness while not yet free of the desire realm's defilement; or, for one already free of the desire realm's defilement who enters the certainty of rightness in dependence on the stage of proximate concentration, during the fifteen moments of the path of seeing; or, for noble ones giving rise to the preparatory path in order to become free of the desire realm's defilement, across the nine uninterrupted paths and eight paths of liberation; or, for one not yet free of the desire realm's defilement who, through resolute faith, sharpens his own faculties, undertaking the preparatory, uninterrupted, and liberation paths belonging to one who has attained vision; or, for noble ones not yet free of the desire realm's defilement, in dependence on the stage of proximate concentration,

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The disagreement here is not merely terminological — it has real consequences for exactly which precepts a given practitioner is understood to hold at any given moment. The third position's introduction of proximate versus fundamental stage as the deciding factor is characteristic of this treatise's overall method: rather than settling for a clean binary distinction, it keeps subdividing until the four-alternative grid genuinely tracks every combination that can actually occur along the path.

3. The Exhaustive Combinatorics of Attaining Both, Neither, or Just One

What follows is one of the treatise's most exhaustive combinatorial passages: an itemized survey of every point along the path — from the sixteen moments of entering certainty, through each stage of freeing oneself from the nine levels of defilement, to the specialized preparatory paths for the immeasurables, the liberations, and the supernormal powers — sorted into which practitioners attain the path-precept alone, the concentration-precept alone, both together, or neither.

Chinese Source

起無量不淨觀持息念及念住等。如是等時得道俱有戒非定俱有戒。

Ru-Yi Translation

giving rise to the immeasurables, the contemplation of impurity, mindfulness of breathing, the foundations of mindfulness, and the like — at all such times, one attains the path-co-existent precept but not the concentration-co-existent precept.

有得定俱有戒非道俱有戒。謂諸異生離欲染一切最後解脫道。即彼為離初靜慮染依初靜慮起加行道。及一切最後解脫道。即彼為離第二靜慮染依第二靜慮起加行道。及一切最後解脫道。即彼為離第三靜慮染。依第三靜慮起加行道。及最後解脫道。即彼為離第四靜慮染。依第四靜慮起加行道。若諸異生依根本靜慮。引發諸通起加行道。五無間道三解脫道。若起無量不淨觀持息念念住煖頂忍世第一法。初三解脫八勝處前八遍處。及無色界歿生色界時。色界上地歿生下地時。如是等時。得定俱有戒非道俱有戒。有得道俱有戒亦得定俱有戒。謂已離欲染依未至定。入正性離生道類智時。若依上地入正性離生十六心頃。若諸聖者離欲染最後解脫道。即彼為離初靜慮乃至非想非非想處染。諸加行道九無間道九解脫道。已離欲染信勝解練根。作見至所有加行無間解脫道時。解脫阿羅漢練根作不動諸加行道。九無間道九解脫道。若雜修初靜慮乃至第四靜慮時。若諸聖者引發諸通起加行道。五無間道三解脫道。若諸聖者已離欲染依未至定。等起無量解脫勝處遍處。不淨觀持息念及諸念住。若起無礙解邊際定無諍願智。空空無願無願無相無相想微細心。如是等時得道俱有戒亦得定俱有戒。有不得道俱有戒亦不得定俱有戒。謂諸異生為離欲染。起加行道九無間道八解脫道。依未至定靜慮中間起煖頂忍世第一法。若諸異生依第二第三第四靜慮近分。為離初第二第三靜慮染。起加行道九無間道八解脫道。若諸異生依空無邊處乃至非想非非想處近分。為離第四靜慮乃至無所有處染。起加行道九無間道九解脫道。若諸異生未離欲染。或已離欲染。依未至等諸近分定。或無色定起無量解脫勝處遍處。及不淨觀持息念并念住等諸功德時。若諸聖者依未至等諸近分定。起有漏無漏成就諸功德時。依無色定起有漏無漏成就諸功德時。一切不定及無心位如是等時。不得道俱有戒亦不得定俱有戒。彼師依捨復作四句。有捨道俱有戒非定俱有戒。謂得預流果或一來果。或漸次者得不還果。未離欲染信勝解練根。得見至從一來果一來勝果道。及預流果預流勝果道退時。從預流果退時者。謂從練根所得果退。如是等時捨道俱有戒非定俱有戒。有捨定俱有戒非道俱有戒。謂諸異生從離欲染。乃至從離第三靜慮染退。若諸異生及諸聖者。欲色界歿生無色界。若色界歿生欲界。若諸異生從根本地勝功德退。如是等時捨定俱有戒非道俱有戒。有捨道俱有戒亦捨定俱有戒。謂諸聖者從離欲染。乃至從離非想非非想處染退。若依四靜慮及靜慮中間得不還果。若得阿羅漢果已離欲染。信勝解練根得見至時。解脫阿羅漢練根得不動。從不還果不還勝果道。及阿羅漢果阿羅漢勝果道退時。如是等時捨道俱有戒亦捨定俱有戒。有不捨道俱有戒亦不捨定俱有戒。謂除前相諸餘位。彼依成就復作四句。有成就道俱有戒非定俱有戒。

There is a case of attaining the concentration-co-existent precept but not the path-co-existent precept — namely, for ordinary beings, at the very last liberation-path moment of becoming free of the desire realm's defilement; or that same being, in order to become free of the first dhyāna's defilement, giving rise to the preparatory path in dependence on the first dhyāna, together with the very last liberation-path moment; or that same being, in order to become free of the second dhyāna's defilement, giving rise to the preparatory path in dependence on the second dhyāna, together with the very last liberation-path moment; or that same being, in order to become free of the third dhyāna's defilement, giving rise to the preparatory path in dependence on the third dhyāna, together with the very last liberation-path moment; or that same being, in order to become free of the fourth dhyāna's defilement, giving rise to the preparatory path in dependence on the fourth dhyāna; or, for ordinary beings, in dependence on a fundamental dhyāna, giving rise to the preparatory path for drawing forth the supernormal powers, across the five uninterrupted paths and three paths of liberation; or when giving rise to the immeasurables, the contemplation of impurity, mindfulness of breathing, the foundations of mindfulness, heat, summit, forbearance, and supreme mundane dharma, the first three liberations, the eight bases of mastery, the first eight totality-spheres; or at the moment of dying in the formless realm and being reborn in the form realm, or dying in a higher stage of the form realm and being reborn in a lower one — at all such times, one attains the concentration-co-existent precept but not the path-co-existent precept. There is a case of attaining both the path-co-existent and the concentration-co-existent precept together — namely, for one already free of the desire realm's defilement, in dependence on the stage of proximate concentration, at the moment of the subsequent-knowledge of the path upon entering the certainty of rightness; or, for one already free of the desire realm's defilement, sharpening his own faculties through resolute faith to become one of vision, at the moment of the preparatory, uninterrupted, and liberation paths involved; or for one liberated as an arhat sharpening his faculties to become unshakable, across the preparatory paths, nine uninterrupted paths, and nine liberation paths; or when mixing the cultivation of the first dhyāna through the fourth dhyāna together; or, for noble ones, giving rise to the preparatory path for drawing forth the supernormal powers, across the five uninterrupted paths and three liberation paths; or, for noble ones already free of the desire realm's defilement, in dependence on the stage of proximate concentration, giving rise together to the immeasurables, the liberations, the bases of mastery, the totality-spheres, the contemplation of impurity, mindfulness of breathing, and the foundations of mindfulness; Were it maintained otherwise, then there would be a case of one who, relying on the fourth dhyāna, attains the fruit of arhatship, cultivating uncontaminated dharmas belonging to all nine stages for the future, all of these future dharmas being affiliated with this present cultivation — yet were he later to give rise to the noble path of some other stage, he would then no longer be able to cultivate any further uncontaminated dharma for the future, since no other noble path remains affiliated with this present one — and this ought then to place that cultivation among the past and present rather than counting as a homogeneous-cause at all, which would directly contradict what has just been stated here — namely, that an earlier-arising wholesome root serves as a homogeneous-cause for a later-arising one — and so on, as stated at length. Lest such a fault follow, it is therefore sound, in principle, to maintain that the future time-period possesses no homogeneous-cause at all.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The status-versus-substance distinction deployed here is one of Sarvāstivāda's most important conceptual tools, and this passage shows it doing exactly the work it was built for: it lets the school affirm, without contradiction, both that a future dharma genuinely exists (as substance) and that it does not yet actually function as a cause (lacking causal status) — precisely the combination needed to deny a future homogeneous-cause while still upholding the doctrine that all three time-periods are real.

20. Does Matter Have a Homogeneous-Cause? The Grass That Grows Without a Seed

The fascicle's final debate turns from abstract time-theory to something almost empirical: does material form itself possess a homogeneous-cause at all? Masters from beyond the border regions say no, citing plainly observed cases — grass sprouting from deep-dug mud with no seed ever planted there, and grass and trees growing atop bare rooftops and mountain peaks — as proof that matter arises purely through the coincidence of external conditions. The masters of Kashmir disagree, affirming that matter does possess a homogeneous-cause (excepting only the very first moment of uncontaminated form), and explain away the apparent counterexamples through hidden seeds already present in the soil, or seeds carried to those unlikely places by wind or by a bird's beak.

Chinese Source

問色法為有同類因不。外國諸師有作是說。一切色法無同類因。但藉餘緣和合力起現見鑿地深踰百肘。從彼出埿日曝風吹。後逢天雨即便生草。又復現見屋脊山峯。先無種子亦生草樹。故知色法無同類因。問若爾此論大種蘊說當云何通。如說過去大種造色。與未來等大種造色為因增上。答我於他論何事須通。若必須通應作是說。

Ru-Yi Translation

One asks: does material form possess a homogeneous-cause? The masters from beyond the border regions say as follows: no material dharma whatsoever possesses a homogeneous-cause — matter arises solely through the combined power of other conditions coming together. It is plainly observed that, when the earth is dug more than a hundred cubits deep, the mud brought up from below, exposed to sun and wind, and later meeting with rain, gives rise to grass, even though no prior seed was ever present there; and it is likewise plainly observed that grass and trees grow atop rooftops and mountain peaks, where no seed existed beforehand either — and from this it is known that material form has no homogeneous-cause at all. One asks: if that is so, how is this treatise's own Great Elements Chapter to be reconciled with this — namely, as it states that a past instance of the great elements, and the derived form composed from them, serve as a cause and a dominant condition for a future instance of the great elements and their derived form? The answer: what obligation do I have to reconcile another's own treatise? But if one must indeed reconcile it, it should be said as follows:

增上緣力有近有遠。有在此身有在餘身。若近在此身者說名為因。

the power of the dominant condition may be near or far, present within this very body or present within another body — when it is near, present within this very body, it is called "cause";

若遠在餘身者說名增上。迦濕彌羅國諸論師言。色法亦有同類因。

when it is far, present within another body, it is called "dominant condition." The masters of Kashmir say: material form, too, does possess a homogeneous-cause,

唯除初無漏色。問若爾雖通大種蘊說。彼所引事當云何通。答所出埿中先有種子。餘緣闕故草未得生。後遇眾緣即便生草。又彼埿聚屋脊山峯。草樹生者風吹鳥銜。種子來至故得如是。

with the sole exception of the very first moment of uncontaminated form. One asks: if that is so, then, although this reconciles the Great Elements Chapter's own statement, how is the empirical case cited above to be reconciled with this? The answer: within the mud brought up from below, a seed was indeed already present beforehand, but, lacking the other necessary conditions, the grass had not yet been able to grow; later, upon meeting with the full complement of conditions, the grass then grew. And as for the grass and trees growing atop that mud heap, the rooftop, or the mountain peak — these arose because the wind carried, or a bird's beak brought, a seed to that very place, and it is on this account that this occurred.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This closing exchange is a genuine glimpse of empirical reasoning operating inside a scholastic Buddhist text: the border-region masters are, in effect, making an argument from observed regularities against unseen causes, very much like an early inductive skeptic, while the Kashmiri masters respond not by denying the observations but by proposing a more complete causal story — hidden seeds, windborne dispersal — that preserves the general principle without denying the evidence. The debate ends fascicle 17 on this vivid, oddly modern-sounding note of botanical dispute standing in for one of Abhidharma's driest technical questions.
