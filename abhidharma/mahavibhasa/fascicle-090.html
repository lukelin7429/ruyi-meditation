Who Possesses These Dharma

Fascicle 90 opens who possesses each faculty across the realms, resolves the tetralemma on "great man" through Mahāprajāpatī's own example, works through full comprehension and realization through cessation, and closes by revealing the entire five-position architecture of the Ten Gates Nyāsa. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 90 complete, 11 sections Source: Taishō T1545, vol. 27, fascicle 90 Topic: 十門納息 → 成就 → 遍知 → 滅作證 (possession, full comprehension, realization through cessation), Fetters Aggregate

Translation Method

The Chinese source is Xuánzàng's seventh-century translation of the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra (阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論), preserved in the Taishō canon (T1545, vol. 27) via the CBETA digital edition — public domain text, translated directly from the Chinese. The English wording throughout is Ru-Yi's own, and the reading notes are original, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda scholarship rather than a specific named commentator's lecture transcript.

Fascicle 90 continues and closes out the Ten Gates Nyāsa (結蘊第二中十門納息第四之二十), completing the directed-thought/sustained-reflection and five-feeling gateways, then opening possession (成就, prāpti), full comprehension (遍知, parijñā), and realization through cessation (滅作證) — before the compilers themselves reveal the whole ten-gateway architecture underlying fascicles 77 through 90, and close with a combinatorial coda. Terms established in earlier fascicles recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Directed Thought and Sustained Reflection, Concluded

The fascicle opens by closing out the vitarka/vicāra classification carried over from fascicle 87 — against the Dārṣṭāntikas' claim that every defiled mind everywhere possesses both directed thought and sustained reflection, the treatise confirms that these belong only to the desire realm and first dhyāna, sustained reflection alone to the intermediate dhyāna, and neither from the second dhyāna upward. The latent tendency that increases with respect to the eye faculty spans all three possibilities across its own five grounds, while the female faculty's own latent tendency, confined to the desire realm, possesses only the coarser pairing.

Chinese Source

眼根乃至無色界修所斷無明隨眠。一一所增隨眠當言有尋有伺無尋唯伺無尋無伺耶。問何故作此論。答為止他宗顯正理故。謂或有說。尋伺是心麁細相故乃至有頂。諸染污心皆有尋伺。是故尋伺三界皆有。如譬喻者。為遮彼執顯唯欲界及初靜慮。未至定中有尋有伺。靜慮中間無尋唯伺。第二靜慮乃至有頂無尋無伺。由此因緣故作斯論。眼根所增隨眠具三者。謂緣眼根所增隨眠。通欲界四靜慮五地遍行及修所斷。如是隨眠若在欲界及初靜慮有尋有伺。若在靜慮中間無尋唯伺。若在第二第三第四靜慮無尋無伺故說具三。後說具三者皆准此應知。然無尋無伺地。或多或少。女根所增隨眠。有尋有伺者。彼所增隨眠唯在欲界故。眼耳身識界所增隨眠。或有尋有伺或無尋唯伺者。眼等五識雖有尋有伺。而在初靜慮者。亦為靜慮中間隨眠作所緣縛故。彼所增隨眠亦通無尋唯伺。初靜慮所增隨眠。或有尋有伺或無尋唯伺者。初靜慮言總顯未至根本靜慮中間三地諸法故說有二。設唯顯根本亦必有二。靜慮中間初靜慮等所有隨眠緣三地故。餘文易了故不分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

The eye faculty, up through the formless realm's own latent tendency of ignorance abandoned by cultivation — ought the latent tendency that increases with respect to each of these, one by one, be said to possess both directed thought and sustained reflection, to possess no directed thought but only sustained reflection, or to possess neither? One asks: for what reason is this treatise composed? The answer: it is in order to put a stop to what other schools maintain, and to display the correct principle — that is, there are those who say: since directed thought and sustained reflection are the coarse and subtle characteristics of mind, then, up through the peak of existence, every defiled mind possesses both directed thought and sustained reflection, and it is for this reason that directed thought and sustained reflection are present throughout all three realms, as the Dārṣṭāntikas hold. In order to put a stop to this holding, it is displayed that only within the desire realm and the first dhyāna, together with the preliminary stage bordering upon it, are both directed thought and sustained reflection present; within the intermediate dhyāna, only sustained reflection without directed thought is present; and from the second dhyāna up through the peak of existence, neither is present — and it is by virtue of this cause and condition that this treatise is composed. As for the latent tendency that increases with respect to the eye faculty possessing all three: this refers to the fact that the latent tendency that increases with respect to the eye faculty pervades the desire realm and the four dhyānas' own five grounds, being universally pervasive and abandoned by cultivation — such a latent tendency, when within the desire realm and the first dhyāna, possesses both directed thought and sustained reflection; when within the intermediate dhyāna, possesses only sustained reflection without directed thought; and when within the second, third, or fourth dhyāna, possesses neither — and it is for this reason that it is said to possess all three. Every subsequent case said to possess all three should be understood according to this same pattern, though the grounds possessing neither directed thought nor sustained reflection may be more or fewer in number. As for the latent tendency that increases with respect to the female faculty possessing both directed thought and sustained reflection: this is because the latent tendency that increases with respect to it exists solely within the desire realm. As for the latent tendency that increases with respect to the eye-, ear-, and body-consciousness elements possessing either both directed thought and sustained reflection, or only sustained reflection without directed thought: although the eye and the other four sense-consciousnesses do indeed possess both directed thought and sustained reflection, when existing within the first dhyāna, they also serve as the object-of-bondage for the intermediate dhyāna's own latent tendency, and it is for this reason that the latent tendency that increases with respect to them also extends to possessing only sustained reflection without directed thought. As for the latent tendency that increases with respect to the first dhyāna possessing either both directed thought and sustained reflection, or only sustained reflection without directed thought: the term "first dhyāna" here refers, in general, to the dharma of all three grounds — the preliminary stage, the fundamental stage, and the intermediate dhyāna — and it is for this reason that two possibilities are stated; even if it referred solely to the fundamental stage, there would still necessarily be two possibilities, since the latent tendencies belonging to the intermediate dhyāna, the first dhyāna, and the rest all take these three grounds as their object. The remaining text is easily understood, and it is therefore not further analyzed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That a single latent tendency taking the eye faculty as its object can itself possess three entirely different characteristics — both directed thought and sustained reflection in one ground, only sustained reflection in another, neither in a third — depending solely on which ground it happens to occupy, is a vivid reminder that these characteristics belong to the latent tendency's own location, not to its object: the eye faculty itself never changes, yet the very same craving for it takes on a different subtlety at every successive stage of concentration.

2. Which Feeling Each Latent Tendency Is Associated With

A parallel investigation closes out the five-feeling classification — against the view that mind and its mental concomitants never truly associate, arising only in bare succession, the treatise confirms both that pleasure, suffering, joy, and sorrow are each confined to their own specific grounds, and that mind and its concomitants genuinely do arise together, sharing a real bond of association. The eye faculty's own latent tendency, spanning the desire and form realms, is associated with joy, sorrow, and equanimity in the desire realm, joy and equanimity in the first two dhyānas, pleasure and equanimity in the third, and equanimity alone in the fourth — four faculties in all, excepting only suffering, which belongs solely to the five sense-consciousnesses.

Chinese Source

眼根乃至無色界修所斷無明隨眠一一所增隨眠。當言樂根苦根喜根憂根捨根相應耶。問何故作此論。答為止他宗顯正理故。謂或有執。樂苦二受隨所依身。從欲界乃至第四靜慮皆有。喜憂二受隨所依心。從欲界乃至非想非非想處皆有。為遮彼意欲顯樂受唯在欲界初及第三靜慮。苦憂二受唯在欲界。喜受唯在欲界初二靜慮。唯有捨受遍在諸地。或復有執。心心所法次第而起互不相應。如譬喻者。為遮彼意顯心心所俱時而生有相應義。由此因緣故作斯論。眼根所增隨眠四根相應除苦根者。眼根所增隨眠通欲色界五地遍行及修所斷。若在欲界喜憂捨根相應。若在初二靜慮喜捨根相應。若在第三靜慮樂捨根相應。若在第四靜慮唯捨根相應。是故總說四根相應。苦根唯與五識相應。五識中無緣眼根等諸隨眠故彼定不與苦根相應。女根所增隨眠三根相應除樂苦根者。女根所增隨眠唯在欲界。欲界樂苦俱在五識故。彼不與樂苦相應。意根所增隨眠五根相應者。意根通與五受相應故相應縛五根相應。若所緣縛有四除苦。

Ru-Yi Translation

The eye faculty, up through the formless realm's own latent tendency of ignorance abandoned by cultivation — ought the latent tendency that increases with respect to each of these, one by one, be said to be associated with the pleasure-faculty, the suffering-faculty, the joy-faculty, the sorrow-faculty, or the equanimity-faculty? One asks: for what reason is this treatise composed? The answer: it is in order to put a stop to what other schools maintain, and to display the correct principle — that is, there are those who hold that the two feelings of pleasure and suffering, following whatever body they depend upon, are present from the desire realm up through the fourth dhyāna, while the two feelings of joy and sorrow, following whatever mind they depend upon, are present from the desire realm up through the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. In order to put a stop to this intention, it is displayed that the feeling of pleasure exists solely within the desire realm and the first and third dhyānas; the feelings of suffering and sorrow exist solely within the desire realm; the feeling of joy exists solely within the desire realm and the first two dhyānas; and it is solely the feeling of equanimity that pervades every ground. There are, further, those who hold that mind and its mental concomitants arise in succession, one after another, and are never mutually associated, as the Dārṣṭāntikas hold. In order to put a stop to this intention, it is displayed that mind and its mental concomitants arise together at one and the same time, possessing a genuine meaning of association — and it is by virtue of this cause and condition that this treatise is composed. As for the latent tendency that increases with respect to the eye faculty being associated with four faculties, excepting the suffering-faculty: the latent tendency that increases with respect to the eye faculty pervades the desire and form realms' five grounds, being universally pervasive and abandoned by cultivation — when within the desire realm, it is associated with the joy-, sorrow-, and equanimity-faculties; when within the first two dhyānas, it is associated with the joy- and equanimity-faculties; when within the third dhyāna, it is associated with the pleasure- and equanimity-faculties; when within the fourth dhyāna, it is associated solely with the equanimity-faculty — and it is for this reason that it is said, in general, to be associated with four faculties. The suffering-faculty is associated solely with the five sense-consciousnesses, and, since there exists no latent tendency among the five sense-consciousnesses that takes the eye faculty and the like as its object, it is certain that this latent tendency is never associated with the suffering-faculty. As for the latent tendency that increases with respect to the female faculty being associated with three faculties, excepting the pleasure- and suffering-faculties: the latent tendency that increases with respect to the female faculty exists solely within the desire realm, and, since the desire realm's own pleasure and suffering both belong to the five sense-consciousnesses, it is not associated with pleasure or suffering. As for the latent tendency that increases with respect to the mind faculty being associated with five faculties: the mind faculty pervades association with all five feelings, and it is for this reason that, by the bond of association, it is associated with five faculties; if reckoned by the bond of an object, there are four, excepting suffering.

三重三摩地所增隨眠。四根相應除苦根者。苦根唯緣色等五境非定等故。三三摩地唯無漏故。不增隨眠非此所說餘文易了不復分別。

As for the latent tendency that increases with respect to the three further concentrations being associated with four faculties, excepting the suffering-faculty: the suffering-faculty takes solely form and the like, the five external objects, as its own object, and is not itself a concentration or the like; the three concentrations, being solely undefiled, are not increased by any latent tendency at all, and this is not what is spoken of here. The remaining text is easily understood, and it is therefore not further analyzed,

恐諸智者生厭惓故。

since it is feared that this would cause the wise to grow weary of it.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The suffering-faculty's own permanent exclusion from every latent tendency examined in this section — not because suffering itself is somehow immune to affliction, but because no latent tendency exists among the five sense-consciousnesses capable of taking these particular objects — is a small technical point with a real payoff: it shows that a feeling's absence from a given association is not always a statement about that feeling's own nature, but sometimes simply a structural fact about which consciousnesses happen to carry which latent tendencies at all.

3. Who Possesses These Dharma, and Why Possession Itself Is Real

An entirely new topic opens with a foundational metaphysical defense: against those who hold that "possessing" and "not possessing" are mere conventional labels with no genuinely real basis, the treatise insists that possession is itself an ultimately real property, without which the scriptures' own repeated distinctions between the ordinary being and the noble one, the learner and the non-learner, could never be soundly established — exactly as the dying and the born are conventional designations resting on the ultimately real dharma of dying and being born, so too does the conventional label "possessor" rest on an ultimately real property of possession within a given individual's own bodily continuum.

Chinese Source

眼根乃至無色界修所斷無明隨眠。誰成就。誰不成就。問何故作此論。答為止他宗顯正理故。謂或有執。無實成就不成就性唯假建立。欲顯成就不成就性是實有物。若不爾者。便違契經。如契經說。應知如是補特伽羅成就善法及不善法。又契經說。我成就十力四無所畏等。若無成就不成就性。異生聖者有學無學斷善根者不斷善者。決定建立皆不得成。決定因緣不可得故。然成就者不成就者是世俗有。若成就性不成就性是勝義有。如死生者是世俗有。諸死生法是勝義有。入出定者是世俗有。所入出定是勝義有。作者受者是世俗有。業異熟果是勝義有。如是成就不成就性是勝義有。施設成就不成就者是世俗有。謂若身中有成就性名成就者。若彼身中有不成就性名不成就者。如樹等是假色等。四塵是實。如是補特伽羅是假。色等五蘊是實。於此假者身相續中。依得非得說有成就不成就法。由此因緣故作斯論。

Ru-Yi Translation

The eye faculty, up through the formless realm's own latent tendency of ignorance abandoned by cultivation — who possesses these, and who does not possess them? One asks: for what reason is this treatise composed? The answer: it is in order to put a stop to what other schools maintain, and to display the correct principle — that is, there are those who hold that there exists no genuinely real property of possessing or not possessing, and that these are established merely as conventional designations. It is in order to display that the property of possessing and of not possessing is a genuinely real entity that this treatise is composed — for, were it not so, this would contradict the scriptures. As the scripture states: one should understand that such-and-such an individual possesses wholesome dharma and unwholesome dharma; and, further, the scripture states: I possess the ten powers, the four kinds of fearlessness, and the rest. If there existed no property of possessing or not possessing, then the fixed distinctions among the ordinary being and the noble one, the learner and the non-learner, the one who has cut off his own wholesome roots and the one who has not, could none of them be established at all, since no fixed cause or condition for them could be found. Yet the possessor and the non-possessor themselves are conventionally existent, while the property of possessing and of not possessing is ultimately existent — exactly as the one who dies and the one who is born are conventionally existent, while the dharma of dying and of being born are themselves ultimately existent; exactly as the one who enters and emerges from concentration is conventionally existent, while the concentration itself, entered and emerged from, is ultimately existent; exactly as the agent and the experiencer are conventionally existent, while karma and its ripened fruit are ultimately existent — so too is the property of possessing and of not possessing ultimately existent, while the designation "possessor" or "non-possessor" is conventionally existent. That is, if within a given body there exists the property of possessing, it is named a possessor; if within that same body there exists the property of not possessing, it is named a non-possessor — exactly as a tree and the like are a conventional form, while the four elements are themselves real; so too is a given individual a conventional designation, while the five aggregates of form and the rest are themselves real. It is with reference to this conventional individual's own continuum of bodily existence, in terms of attainment and non-attainment, that the dharma of possessing and not possessing is spoken of — and it is by virtue of this cause and condition that this treatise is composed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The chain of analogies offered here — the one who dies and the dharma of dying, the one who enters concentration and the concentration entered, the agent and the karma performed — is a small masterclass in this tradition's own layered ontology: at every level, a conventionally useful label ("person," "possessor," "agent") turns out to name nothing more than a genuinely real underlying process or property, and the entire argument for possession's own reality rests on refusing to let the convenience of everyday language be mistaken for the absence of anything real underneath it.

4. The Sense Faculties Across the Realms and Embryonic Stages

The eye and its four companion faculties are possessed by every being of the form realm and by anyone in the desire realm who has attained them and not yet lost them — tracked in precise detail through the five embryonic stages of kalala, arbuda, peśī, ghana, and praśākhā, and through decay, mutilation, or being devoured. The body faculty alone is possessed throughout the form realm as well, while the female and male faculties are excluded from the form and formless realms entirely — not merely because craving has been left behind, but because these realms possess no food consumed in morsels, no shame or moral dread to require concealment, and, ultimately, no use for them at all.

Chinese Source

眼根色界及欲界已得不失。成就者。色界諸天皆具根故。決定成就眼等五根。此具根言依顯者說。女男根不顯無亦名具根。又容有者彼皆成就故說具根。非一切有勿憂苦等彼亦有故。及欲界已得者。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the eye faculty being possessed by one within the form realm, and by one within the desire realm who has already attained it and not yet lost it: since every one of the form realm's own heavenly beings possesses complete faculties, they certainly possess the eye and the remaining four faculties. This phrase "possesses complete faculties" is stated with reference to what is manifest — since the female and male faculties are not manifest there, this is not to say they lack them, yet they, too, are still called possessing complete faculties. Further, as for what may possibly be present: since all of these are indeed possessed, it is said that they possess complete faculties — this does not mean that every single thing is present, for it should not be supposed that sorrow, suffering, and the like are also present there. As for the desire realm's own "already attained":

謂鉢羅奢佉位以彼不失者。謂不爛壞不墮落不蟲食不被害。無色界及欲界未得已失不成就者。無色界無色故不成就眼等根。及欲界未得者。謂羯剌藍頞部曇閉尸鍵南位。已失者。謂爛壞墮落蟲食被害耳鼻舌根亦爾者。如眼根說身根欲色界成就者。有色界生必有身根故。無色界不成就者彼無色故。女男根欲界已得不失成就者。如眼根說。色無色界及欲界。未得已失不成就者。色無色界無女男根無婬愛故。厭捨此根生彼界故。色無色界無段食故。必因段食有此根故。因無慚愧有此根故。又女男根彼無用故。問若爾。鼻舌彼亦應無。彼無嗅香甞味事故。答鼻舌二根於彼有用。謂莊嚴身及起言說。女男二根令身醜陋。有慚愧者必隱覆之。尊者妙音作如是說。

this refers to the praśākhā stage; as for "not yet lost": this refers to not having decayed, not having fallen away, not having been eaten by worms, and not having been injured. As for the formless realm, and the desire realm's own "not yet attained" and "already lost," being non-possessors: the formless realm, having no form, does not possess the eye and the remaining faculties; and as for the desire realm's own "not yet attained": this refers to the stages of kalala, arbuda, peśī, and ghana; as for "already lost": this refers to having decayed, having fallen away, having been eaten by worms, or having been injured. As for the ear, nose, and tongue faculties being just the same: their pattern should be understood exactly as with the eye faculty. As for the body faculty being possessed within the desire and form realms: whoever is born within the form realm necessarily possesses the body faculty; as for it not being possessed within the formless realm: this is because that realm has no form. As for the female and male faculties being possessed within the desire realm by one who has already attained them and not yet lost them: their pattern should be understood exactly as with the eye faculty. As for them not being possessed within the form and formless realms, and by one within the desire realm who has not yet attained them or has already lost them: the form and formless realms have no female or male faculty, since they possess no carnal craving, having grown weary of and abandoned this faculty in order to be born within those realms; the form and formless realms possess no food consumed in morsels, since this faculty necessarily arises in dependence upon food consumed in morsels, and arises in dependence upon the absence of shame and moral dread; further, the female and male faculties would be of no use there. One asks: if this is so, then the nose and tongue faculties, too, ought to be absent there, since there exists there no matter of smelling fragrance or tasting flavor. The answer: the nose and tongue faculties are indeed of use there, serving to adorn the body and to give rise to speech, whereas the female and male faculties cause the body to appear unsightly, and one possessed of shame and moral dread will certainly conceal them. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The reasoning given for why the nose and tongue faculties persist into the form realm while the female and male faculties do not is genuinely revealing: it is not enough for a faculty's usual objects (smell, taste, sexual differentiation) to be absent — the faculty must also serve some ongoing function, and since the nose and tongue still adorn the body and enable speech while the female and male faculties would only render the body unsightly, requiring concealment by anyone possessed of shame, function rather than mere object-availability turns out to be the deciding criterion for what a higher realm's body still needs.

5. Mahāprajāpatī and the Meaning of "Great Man"

A striking scriptural puzzle follows: if the form and formless realms possess no male faculty, are their inhabitants then not "great men"? The treatise resolves this by relocating the very meaning of "great man" away from anatomy and onto function — the capacity to become free of desire and accomplish wholesome deeds — citing the scripture's own declaration that the four approaches and four fruits are all called "great man," and, most memorably, that Mahāprajāpatī, the Buddha's own foster mother, though a woman, entered the noble path, attained the fruit, exhausted the outflows, and is herself called a great man on that very basis.

Chinese Source

上二界無招彼業故。問色無色界既無男根應非丈夫。答色無色界有丈夫用故名丈夫。丈夫用者。謂能離欲能成善事故名丈夫。如契經說。四向四果皆名丈夫。非諸女人皆無向果。如契經說。此大生主。雖是女人而入聖道。得果盡漏亦名丈夫。於此義中應作四句。

Ru-Yi Translation

the upper two realms possess no karma capable of drawing forth these faculties. One asks: since the form and formless realms possess no male faculty, ought they not, then, to be devoid of a "great man"? The answer: the form and formless realms do indeed possess the function of a great man, and it is for this reason that they are called "great man" — the function of a great man being the capacity to become free of desire and to accomplish wholesome deeds, and it is for this reason that one so capable is called a great man. As the scripture states: the four approaches and the four fruits are all called "great man" — it is not the case that every woman whatsoever is devoid of an approach or a fruit. As the scripture states: this Mahāprajāpatī, although a woman, entered the noble path, attained the fruit, exhausted the outflows, and is likewise called a great man. With respect to this same meaning, a tetralemma ought to be cast.

或有丈夫不成就男根。謂生色無色界等。或有成就男根而不名丈夫。如扇搋半擇迦等。或有丈夫亦成就男根。謂具男根離欲染等。

There is one who is a great man yet does not possess the male faculty — this refers to one born within the form or formless realms and the like. 8. Full Comprehension: How Many Latent Tendencies End When a Faculty Ends

An entirely new investigation opens: at the precise moment each faculty attains full comprehension — meaning the complete exhaustion of the craving that takes it as object — how many of the ninety-eight latent tendencies are thereby exhausted, and how many of the nine fetters come to an end? Against those who deny that ordinary beings can genuinely cut off any latent tendency at all, capable only of temporary suppression, the treatise insists that an ordinary being can indeed cut off every latent tendency abandoned by seeing and cultivation up through the sphere of nothingness, excepting only the very peak of existence — full comprehension itself being, strictly speaking, the fruit of cutting-off (analytical cessation) rather than a form of knowledge, though named "comprehension" by courtesy of its own cause.

9. Realization Through Cessation: A Second, Finer-Grained Gateway

A closely related but subtly different gateway opens: realization through cessation, tracking not just the initial moment a latent tendency is cut off but every subsequent moment its own cessation is realized anew — eighty-nine distinct stages in all, against those who hold that liberation occurs in a single sudden stroke, like waking abruptly from a dream, and against those who insist the uninterrupted path can only cut off affliction while the path of liberation alone can realize its cessation. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The sheer, deliberate repetitiveness of this closing stretch is itself the point being made: by piling up permutation after permutation of the same handful of modifiers — cessation, path of cutting-off, direct object, object-of-that-object — applied to the same handful of gateways, the compilers make visible, more vividly than any explicit statement could, that the combinatorial possibilities genuinely are without measure, and that the treatise's own choice to work through only a fraction of them in full detail across fourteen fascicles was never a claim to completeness but a deliberate, bounded sample from an genuinely inexhaustible space — leaving the rest, quite literally, for the wise to discern and select on their own.
