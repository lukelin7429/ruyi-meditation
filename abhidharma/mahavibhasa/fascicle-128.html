The Egg, the Womb, and the Matrix

Fascicle 128 works through the standard checklist of dharma-categories for derived form, asks why an embryo cannot yet manifest bodily or verbal expression, and constructs an exhaustive combinatorial matrix cross-tabulating the great elements against wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral form across the three realms — closing with a compact master-summary. This fascicle is unusually dense with formulaic combinatorial matrices, characteristic of the treatise's own exhaustive style — the translation preserves this repetitive structure faithfully rather than smoothing it away, since the repetition itself is part of how the argument is made. Terms established across fascicles 93–127 recur here and are not re-explained at each occurrence.

1. Why Not Ask 'How Many Are Colored'? Two Gates Illuminating Each Other

Fascicle 128 opens with a methodological puzzle: why does the treatise ask how many of the great elements' derivative sense-fields are visible or invisible, yet never asks how many are "colored" or "colorless"? Several answers compete — it is simply the base treatise's own stylistic preference; the question would be trivial since every derived form is already colored by definition; or, most interestingly, the treatise deliberately poses a redundant-seeming question anyway (how many are unconditioned, when the answer is obviously none) purely so that two structurally parallel "gateways" can illuminate each other, each teaching something the other's silence would have left unclear.

Chinese Source

大種蘊第五中大造納息第一之二大種所造處。幾有見。幾無見。問何故不問幾有色幾無色。答彼作論者意欲爾故。隨彼意欲而造論。但不違法性便不應責有說。所造無無色故。不得問言幾有色。幾無色問亦無無為。後不應問幾有為幾無為。答亦不應問。而後問者當知彼是有餘之說。有說。欲以種種文種種說。莊嚴於義令易解故。復次欲以二門二階二略二明二炬二影二光互相顯示。如無所造是無色故不問。如是無所造是無為故亦不應問。如無所造是無為而得問。如是雖無所造是無色亦應問。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth Aggregate, the Great Elements: the Great-Elements-and-Derivatives Nyāsa, Chapter One, Part Two. Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many are visible, and how many invisible? Question: why does the treatise not ask how many are colored and how many colorless? Answer: it is simply the intention of the treatise's own author — he composed the treatise following his own preference, and, so long as this does not contradict the proper form of the Dharma, this ought not to be demanded as an objection. Some say: since nothing derived is colorless, the question "how many are colored, how many colorless" cannot properly be asked; and since nothing derived is unconditioned either, the question "how many are conditioned, how many unconditioned" ought equally not to be asked afterward. Answer: this question, too, ought not to be asked — and that it is nevertheless asked later should be understood as belonging to a further, supplementary account. Some say: it is in order to adorn the meaning with a variety of wording and a variety of expression, making it easier to understand. Further, it is in order to mutually illuminate two gateways, two stages, two summaries, two lights, two torches, two shadows, and two beams — just as the question "how many are colorless" is not asked, since nothing derived is colorless, so too the question "how many are unconditioned" ought equally not to be asked; yet just as the question "how many are unconditioned" is nevertheless asked, though nothing derived is unconditioned, so too the question "how many are colorless" ought equally to be asked, even though nothing derived is colorless —

如是二門互相影示。令知前後問答理通。有餘師言。此中分別所造諸色幾有見等。既舉所造諸色為章。寧復可問幾有色等。以諸色言即顯有色。色體皆有變礙義故。有彼色義故名有色。非如有衣及有子等。

— these two gateways thus mutually illuminate one another, making known that the earlier and later questions and answers are logically consistent throughout. Other masters say: this passage analyzes how many of the various forms produced by the great elements are visible and the rest — and, having already raised "the various forms produced" as the chapter's own heading, how could one then further ask how many of them are "colored"? For the very term "form" already shows that they are colored, since the essential nature of form always carries the sense of change and resistance — it is precisely by possessing this sense of form that something is called "colored," unlike the sense in which one speaks of "having a robe" or "having a child," where possession is merely incidental rather than definitional.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The claim that two questions can "mutually illuminate" each other even when one of them has an entirely predictable, trivial answer is a subtle piece of pedagogical theory: the treatise treats the deliberate asking of an apparently unnecessary question as itself informative, since seeing exactly why an answer is trivial can teach something that silently omitting the question never would.

2. Visible, Resistant, With Outflow: Running the Standard Checklist

The treatise begins working through its standard checklist of dharma-categories, applied specifically to the eleven sense-fields produced by the great elements. Only the form sense-field is visible, since only it can be seen by an eye, its shape displayed and compared, or cast as a reflection; the remaining ten are invisible, with a careful accounting for exactly which portion of the touch and dharma sense-fields count, since not every instance of either is actually derived form. The same partial-versus-whole precision governs resistance, the presence or absence of outflow, and conditioned status, with every unconditioned category returning a flat "none."

Chinese Source

大種所造處幾有見。答一謂色處。問何故色處名為有見。答眼根名見有見用故。所見色處有能見眼故名有見。如有主等或復見者。是能顯示色處相麁。可相顯示在此在彼相狀差別。所顯示色有能顯示故名有見。如有名等。或復見者是諸影像。唯有色處可有影像故名有見。餘則不爾。幾無見。答八二少分。八者謂眼耳鼻舌身聲香味處。二少分者。謂觸處法處。雖諸觸處皆無見攝。而非一切是所造色。若所造色亦無見者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。法處亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many are visible? Answer: one — namely, the form sense-field. Question: why is the form sense-field called visible? Answer: because the eye faculty is called "seeing," possessing the function of vision, and the form sense-field that is seen possesses an eye capable of seeing it, and hence is called visible — just as one speaks of "having an owner." Or, again, "seeing" here refers to the capacity of the form sense-field's own characteristic to be displayed coarsely, its distinct location and shape here or there being capable of being shown — since what is shown possesses something capable of showing it, it is called visible, just as one speaks of "having a name." Or, again, "seeing" here refers to the various reflected images — since only the form sense-field can bear a reflected image, it alone is called visible, while the others are not so. How many are invisible? Answer: eight entirely, plus two partially. The eight are the eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body sense-fields, and the sound, smell, and taste sense-fields. The two partial ones are the touch sense-field and the dharma sense-field — for though every instance of the touch sense-field is included within the invisible, not every instance of it is derived form; only that portion of it which is both invisible and derived is what is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial." The dharma sense-field is likewise so.

大種所造處幾有對。答九一少分。九者謂五內處及外四處。一少分者謂觸處。雖諸觸處皆有對攝。而非一切是所造色。若所造色亦有對者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。幾無對。答一少分謂法處。雖諸法處皆無對攝。而非一切是所造色。若所造色亦無對者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。然有對有三種一障礙有對。謂十色處。二境界有對謂五色根及心心所。三所緣有對。謂心心所。此中說障礙有對非餘。

Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many possess resistance? Answer: nine entirely, plus one partially. The nine are the five internal sense-fields and the four external ones. The one partial one is the touch sense-field — for though every instance of the touch sense-field possesses resistance, not every instance of it is derived form; only the portion that is both resistant and derived is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial." How many lack resistance? Answer: one, partially — namely, the dharma sense-field, for though every instance of the dharma sense-field lacks resistance, not every instance of it is derived form; only the portion that both lacks resistance and is derived is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial." Now, resistance is of three kinds: first, obstructive resistance, belonging to the ten form-sense-fields; second, objective resistance, belonging to the five form-faculties together with mind and mental factors; and third, cognitive resistance, belonging to mind and mental factors. What is meant here is obstructive resistance, not the others.

大種所造處幾有漏。答九二少分。九者如前說。二少分者。謂觸處法處。雖諸觸處皆有漏攝。而非一切是所造色。若所造色亦有漏者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。非諸法處。皆有漏攝。亦非一切是所造色。若所造色亦有漏者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。幾無漏。答一少分謂法處。非諸法處皆無漏攝。亦非一切是所造色。若所造色亦無漏者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。

Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many have outflow? Answer: nine entirely, plus two partially. The nine are as explained before. The two partial ones are the touch sense-field and the dharma sense-field — for though every instance of the touch sense-field has outflow, not every instance of it is derived form, and only the portion both having outflow and derived is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial"; and, since not every instance of the dharma sense-field has outflow, nor is every instance of it derived form, only the portion both having outflow and derived is meant here, and hence it too is said to be only "partial." How many are without outflow? Answer: one, partially — namely, the dharma sense-field, for not every instance of the dharma sense-field is without outflow, nor is every instance of it derived form; only the portion both without outflow and derived is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial."

大種所造處幾有為。答九二少分。九者如前說。二少分者。謂觸處法處。雖諸觸處皆有為攝。而非一切是所造色。若所造色亦有為者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。非諸法處皆有為攝。亦非一切是所造色。若所造色亦有為者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。幾無為。答無。彼皆有為故。

Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many are conditioned? Answer: nine entirely, plus two partially. The nine are as explained before. The two partial ones are the touch sense-field and the dharma sense-field — for though every instance of the touch sense-field is conditioned, not every instance of it is derived form, and only the portion both conditioned and derived is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial"; and, since not every instance of the dharma sense-field is conditioned, nor is every instance of it derived form, only the portion both conditioned and derived is meant here, and hence it too is said to be only "partial." How many are unconditioned? Answer: none, since all of them are conditioned.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The recurring formula — "though every instance of this sense-field falls into that category, not every instance of it is derived form, so only the derived portion is meant here" — is a small but telling piece of logical hygiene: the treatise refuses to let a true general statement about a whole sense-field quietly stand in for the narrower, more precise claim actually being made about just its derived subset.

3. Past, Present, Wholesome, Neutral: The Checklist Continues

The same rigorous partial-accounting method extends across time (past, future, and present, admitting the identical qualification each time) and moral quality — only the form, sound, and dharma sense-fields can ever be wholesome or unwholesome derived form, while seven sense-fields entirely, and three more partially, fall under neutral.

Chinese Source

大種所造處幾過去。答十一少分。謂除意處。雖諸眼處皆所造攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many are past? Answer: ten entirely, plus one partially — that is, excluding the mind sense-field. Though every instance of the eye sense-field is included within the derived,

而非一切皆在過去。有在未來現在世故。若是所造亦過去者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。耳鼻舌身色聲香味處亦爾。非諸觸處皆所造攝。亦非一切皆在過去。若是所造亦過去者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。法處亦爾。如說過去。未來現在說亦如是。有數等故。

— not every instance of it belongs to the past, since some belong to the future or the present; only the portion both derived and past is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial." The ear, nose, tongue, and body sense-fields, and the form, sound, smell, and taste sense-fields, are likewise so. Not every instance of the touch sense-field is included within the derived, nor is every instance of it past; only the portion both derived and past is meant here, and hence it too is said to be only "partial." The dharma sense-field is likewise so. Just as this is said of the past, so too should it be said of the future and the present, since they, too, admit of counting in this same way.

大種所造處幾善。答三少分。謂色聲法處雖諸色處皆所造攝。而非一切皆是善性。若是所造亦善性者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。

Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many are wholesome? Answer: three, all partially — namely, the form, sound, and dharma sense-fields. Though every instance of the form sense-field is included within the derived, not every instance of it is wholesome in nature; only the portion both derived and wholesome is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial."

聲處亦爾。非諸法處皆所造攝。亦非一切皆是善性。若是所造亦善性者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。幾不善。答三少分如前說。幾無記。答七三少分。七者謂五內處及香味處。三少分者。謂色聲觸處。雖諸色處皆所造攝。而非一切皆是無記。若是所造亦無記者。

The sound sense-field is likewise so. Not every instance of the dharma sense-field is included within the derived, nor is every instance of it wholesome; only the portion both derived and wholesome is meant here, and hence it too is said to be only "partial." How many are unwholesome? Answer: the same three, partially, as explained before. How many are neutral? Answer: seven entirely, plus three partially. The seven are the five internal sense-fields together with the smell and taste sense-fields. The three partial ones are the form, sound, and touch sense-fields — for though every instance of the form sense-field is included within the derived, not every instance of it is neutral; only the portion both derived and neutral is meant here,

是此所問餘非所問故說少分。聲處亦爾。雖諸觸處皆無記攝。而非一切皆是所造。若是無記亦所造者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。

— and hence it is said to be only "partial." The sound sense-field is likewise so. Though every instance of the touch sense-field is included within the neutral, not every instance of it is derived; only the portion both neutral and derived is meant here, and hence it too is said to be only "partial."

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That moral quality (wholesome, unwholesome, neutral) turns out to require far more sense-fields to be invoked, and far more careful partial qualification, than a simple category like "past" or "future" reflects something genuine about the underlying phenomena: temporal location is a comparatively coarse-grained fact true of everything alike, while moral valence cuts unevenly across the very same set of sense-fields, present in some, absent in others, and only partially present in a third group.

4. Realm-Bound, Trainee, Cut Off: Completing the Checklist

The checklist concludes with realm-binding (the smell and taste sense-fields belong wholly to the desire realm, since they have no place in the formless realm's absence of matter and, being tied to eating, barely feature in higher states even within the form realm), trainee and adept status (confined almost entirely to the dharma sense-field), and the cutting-off of afflictions — with the flat, simple answer that no form whatsoever is ever cut off by insight alone, only by sustained cultivation or, for a small remainder, not cut off at all.

Chinese Source

大種所造處幾欲界繫。答二九少分。二者謂香味處。九少分者。謂五內處。及色聲觸法處。雖諸眼處皆所造攝。而非一切皆欲界繫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many are bound to the desire realm? Answer: two entirely, plus nine partially. The two are the smell and taste sense-fields. The nine partial ones are the five internal sense-fields together with the form, sound, touch, and dharma sense-fields — for though every instance of the eye sense-field is included within the derived, not every instance of it is bound to the desire realm,

若是所造亦欲界繫者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。耳鼻舌身色聲處亦爾。非諸觸處皆所造攝。亦非一切皆欲界繫。若是所造亦欲界繫者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。法處亦爾。幾色界繫。答九少分如前說。幾無色界繫。答無。彼無色故。

— only the portion both derived and bound to the desire realm being meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial." The ear, nose, tongue, and body sense-fields, and the form and sound sense-fields, are likewise so. Not every instance of the touch sense-field is included within the derived, nor is every instance of it bound to the desire realm; only the portion both derived and bound to the desire realm is meant here, and hence it too is said to be only "partial." The dharma sense-field is likewise so. How many are bound to the form realm? Answer: the same nine, partially, as explained before. How many are bound to the formless realm? Answer: none, since that realm has no form.

大種所造處幾學。答一少分。謂法處。非諸法處皆學所攝。亦非一切皆是所造。若是所造亦學攝者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。幾無學。答一少分如前說。幾非學非無學。答九二少分。九者謂內五處。及外四處。二少分者。謂觸處法處。雖諸觸處皆非學非無學攝。而非一切皆是所造。若是非學非無學亦所造者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。非諸法處皆非學非無學攝。亦非一切皆是所造。若是非學非無學。亦所造者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。

Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many are trainee? Answer: one, partially — namely, the dharma sense-field, for not every instance of the dharma sense-field is included within the trainee category, nor is every instance of it derived; only the portion both derived and trainee is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial." How many are adept? Answer: the same one, partially, as explained before. How many are neither trainee nor adept? Answer: nine entirely, plus two partially. The nine are the five internal sense-fields together with the four external ones. The two partial ones are the touch sense-field and the dharma sense-field — for though every instance of the touch sense-field is included within the neither-trainee-nor-adept category, not every instance of it is derived; only the portion both neither-trainee-nor-adept and derived is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial"; and, since not every instance of the dharma sense-field is included within the neither-trainee-nor-adept category, nor is every instance of it derived, only the portion both neither-trainee-nor-adept and derived is meant here, and hence it too is said to be only "partial."

大種所造處幾見所斷。答無。必無諸色見所斷故。幾修所斷。答九二少分。九者如前說。二少分者。謂觸處法處。雖諸觸處皆修所斷攝。而非一切皆是所造。若修所斷亦所造者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。非諸法處皆修所斷攝。亦非一切皆是所造。若修所斷亦所造者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。幾不斷。答一少分。謂法處。

Among the sense-fields produced by the great elements, how many are cut off by insight? Answer: none, since no form is ever cut off by insight. How many are cut off by cultivation? Answer: nine entirely, plus two partially. The nine are as explained before. The two partial ones are the touch sense-field and the dharma sense-field — for though every instance of the touch sense-field is included within what is cut off by cultivation, not every instance of it is derived; only the portion both cut off by cultivation and derived is meant here, and hence it is said to be only "partial"; and, since not every instance of the dharma sense-field is included within what is cut off by cultivation, nor is every instance of it derived, only the portion both cut off by cultivation and derived is meant here, and hence it too is said to be only "partial." How many are not cut off at all? Answer: one, partially — namely, the dharma sense-field,

非諸法處皆不斷攝。亦非一切皆是所造。若是所造亦不斷者。是此所問餘非所問故說少分。

— for not every instance of the dharma sense-field is included within what is never cut off, nor is every instance of it derived; only the portion both derived and never cut off is meant here, and hence it too is said to be only "partial."

Reading note — Ru-Yi

That no form of any kind can be cut off merely by insight — only through cultivation — quietly reinforces a structural asymmetry running throughout this literature: insight alone can sever conceptual, view-based afflictions in a single decisive stroke, but the deeply conditioned habits bound up in the body's own material existence yield only to sustained, repeated practice over time.

5. Does Having the Elements Mean Having Derived Form? From Ocean Giants to Invisible Mites

A new topic opens: whoever possesses the great elements always possesses derived form as well — with one striking exception, noble ones reborn in the formless realm, who retain a purely technical, precept-linked "accompanying form" despite having no matter whatsoever. The scale of ordinary embodiment, meanwhile, ranges from beings in the ocean measuring hundreds of yojanas, through the vast body of the asura king Rāhu, up to Akaniṣṭha heaven's sixteen-thousand-yojana forms, down to mosquitoes and water-insects too small for the human eye to see at all.

Chinese Source

若成就大種彼所造色耶。答諸成就大種。彼成就所造色。有成就所造色非大種。謂諸聖者生無色界。誰成就大種及所造色。謂欲色界一切有情。此則總說。若別說者。或有有情。成多大種及多造色。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one possesses the great elements, does one possess derived form? Answer: whoever possesses the great elements possesses derived form. There is one who possesses derived form but not the great elements — namely, noble ones reborn in the formless realm. Who possesses both the great elements and derived form? Every being within the desire and form realms — this is the general account. Speaking specifically, some beings possess many great elements and much derived form,

或有有情。成少大種及少造色。成多大種及多造色者。如大海中有諸有情所得身形。或百二百三百四百五六七百踰繕那量。如曷邏呼阿素洛王所得身形其量廣大。如色究竟所得身形。一萬六千踰繕那量。成少大種及少造色者。猶如蚊蠛水中虫等。乃至極細非人眼境。問何故聖者生無色界。唯成就造色非大種耶。答彼界無色。又不成就他界大種。以有漏法越地捨故。造色不爾通無漏故異地成就。無有聖者不成就無漏戒。是故聖生彼界唯成就造色。於中學成就學隨轉色。無學成就無學隨轉色。

— and others possess few great elements and little derived form. As to possessing many great elements and much derived form: the bodies of beings within the great ocean may measure one, two, three, four, five, six, or seven hundred yojanas; the body of King Rāhu, lord of the asuras, is vaster still; and the bodies of beings in Akaniṣṭha heaven measure sixteen thousand yojanas.
