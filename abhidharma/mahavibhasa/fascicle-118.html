Is the Buddha's Voice a Ripening?

Fascicle 118 stages a formal debate over whether the Buddha's own voice is a karmic ripening, refutes backward-flowing causation, works through an extended matrix of how body, speech, and mind ripen together and apart, and opens the Killing-of-Life Nyāsa with a precise anatomy of what killing actually is. This page carries Ru-Yi's complete translation.

1. Is the Buddha's Own Voice a Karmic Ripening? A Formal Debate

Fascicle 118 opens with a genuine cross-school dispute: the Vātsīputrīyas and Vibhajyavādins, citing a scriptural passage on why the bodhisattva obtained a Brahmā-voice, hold that sound itself can be a ripening-fruit of karma. The Vaibhāṣika "correct-principle theorist" is drawn into a formal debate exchange — complete with the opponent's own attempt to declare him defeated by self-contradiction — before resolving the tension: the bodhisattva's past karma produced the subtle physical basis (the Tathāgata's throat) from which wondrous speech can arise, but the sound itself, arising fresh from present causes, is never the ripening.

Chinese Source

諸法由業得。彼法當言是善不善無記耶。乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答為止他宗顯己義故。謂犢子部。分別論者。欲令音聲是異熟果。問彼由何量作如是說。答由聖言故。如施設論說。何緣菩薩感得梵音大士夫相。菩薩昔餘生中。離麁惡語此業究竟得梵音聲。由此說故彼便計聲是異熟果。為遮此意顯一切聲非異熟果故作斯論。

Ru-Yi Translation

Dharmas obtained through karma — should these be said to be wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral? And so on in full. Question: why compose this treatise? Answer: in order to put a stop to another school's position and to make clear our own doctrine. That is, the Vātsīputrīyas and the Vibhajyavādins wish to hold that sound is itself a ripening-fruit. Question: on what authority do they make this claim? Answer: on the authority of a scriptural statement — as the Prajñaptiśāstra says: for what reason does the bodhisattva obtain the mark of the great being's own Brahmā-voice? Because the bodhisattva, in his former lives, abstained from harsh and evil speech, and this karma, upon reaching completion, obtained the Brahmā-voice. On the basis of this statement, they reckon that sound is itself a ripening-fruit. In order to refute this notion and to show that no sound whatsoever is a ripening-fruit, this treatise is composed.

諸法由業得。彼法當言是善不善無記耶。答依異熟果諸法由業得彼法是無記。問何故作是說。答或有諸法雖由業得而非無記。如諸律儀不律儀等。為簡彼法故作是說。依異熟果諸法由業得彼法是無記此中犢子部分別論者。問應理論者言。定作是說依異熟果諸法由業得彼法是無記耶。此是審定他宗之言。若不審定他所立宗。便難他者則無有能與他作過。亦是徵難他所不說故審定言。汝今忍可定作是說。依異熟果諸法由業得。彼法是無記耶。應理論者答言如是。

Dharmas obtained through karma — should these be said to be wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral? Answer: dharmas obtained through karma, with respect to the ripening-fruit, are neutral. Question: why is it said in this way? Answer: because there are dharmas that, although obtained through karma, are not neutral — such as the various restraints and non-restraints — and it is in order to set these apart that it is said in this way: dharmas obtained through karma, with respect to the ripening-fruit, are neutral. Here, a Vātsīputrīya or Vibhajyavādin questions the correct-principle theorist, saying: do you indeed hold, definitively, that dharmas obtained through karma, with respect to the ripening-fruit, are neutral? This is a statement made to first pin down the opponent's own position, for if one does not first pin down what position the opponent actually holds, one cannot then fault him for anything, and it is also a way of pressing him on what he has not yet stated — hence this pinning-down statement: do you now accept and definitively hold that dharmas obtained through karma, with respect to the ripening-fruit, are neutral? The correct-principle theorist answers: yes, this is so.

彼復問言為何所欲。如來善心說語。妙音美音和雅音悅意音。此語是善耶。應理論者答言如是。彼便難言。聽我說汝負處失處違自言處。若作是說。依異熟果諸法由業得彼法是無記。則不應言如來善心說諸妙音美音和雅音悅意音此語是善。作是說者不應道理。若作是說如來善心說諸妙音美音和雅音悅意音。此語是善則不應言依異熟果諸法由業得彼法是無記。而作是說不應道理。應理論者釋彼難言。應作是說。菩薩昔餘生中。造作增長感異熟果大宗葉業。由是因緣展轉出生如來咽喉微妙大種。從此能生妙語音聲而聲非異熟。

He then further asks: and what do you further hold — is the speech that the Tathāgata utters with a wholesome mind, wondrous in sound, beautiful in sound, gentle and elegant in sound, delightful in sound, wholesome speech? The correct-principle theorist answers: yes, this is so. He then presses the objection, saying: hear now, let me point out your defeat, your loss, and your self-contradiction. If you hold that dharmas obtained through karma, with respect to the ripening-fruit, are neutral, then you ought not to say that the speech the Tathāgata utters with a wholesome mind — wondrous, beautiful, gentle and elegant, delightful in sound — is wholesome speech; to say so is unreasonable. But if you hold that the speech the Tathāgata utters with a wholesome mind — wondrous, beautiful, gentle and elegant, delightful in sound — is wholesome speech, then you ought not to say that dharmas obtained through karma, with respect to the ripening-fruit, are neutral; and to say so is likewise unreasonable. The correct-principle theorist resolves this objection, saying: one should rather explain it thus — the bodhisattva, in his former lives, created and increased the great foundational karma that brings about a ripening-fruit, and it was through this cause and condition that, by successive transmission, the Tathāgata's own subtle great elements of the throat came to be produced, from which this wondrous speech-sound can then arise — while the sound itself is not the ripening.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The opponent's rhetorical move — "let me point out your defeat, your loss, your self-contradiction" — preserves a genuine piece of ancient Indian debate procedure within the treatise itself: before a rebuttal proper, the challenger first pins down exactly which two of the opponent's own commitments supposedly cannot both be true, a formal discipline that keeps a philosophical dispute from sliding into simple assertion and counter-assertion.

2. Nine Reasons Sound Is Never a Ripening-Fruit, and Two Causes Behind a Bodhisattva's Vow

Nine independent arguments converge on the same conclusion: sound belongs too many causal removes from its originating karma; it follows the speaker's will in a way no true ripening ever could; it generates further sound rather than another ripening; it is produced by present exertion, not solely prior karma; it can be wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral, while ripening is neutral alone; and, most tellingly, one can observe cases where beings in fortunate circumstances utter unpleasant sounds and vice versa — a plain empirical counter-example to the ripening theory. The section closes with the two causes, seeing and hearing, that move a bodhisattva to vow for the Brahmā-voice, illustrated by the simile of admiring another's magnificent hall and music and resolving to earn the very same for oneself.

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: if no sound whatsoever is a ripening-fruit, how is the statement of the Prajñaptiśāstra to be reconciled? Answer: it is stated with reference to a successive, indirect cause,

然一切聲非異熟果。問何故諸聲非異熟果。或有說者。聲屬第三傳。謂最初業生諸大種。大種生聲。聲屬第三故非異熟果。復有說者。聲屬第五傳。謂初業生異熟大種。異熟大種生長養大種。長養大種生等流大種。從此等流大種生聲。聲屬第五故非異熟果。復有說者。聲隨欲轉非異熟法可隨欲轉。復有說者。聲復生聲非從異熟復生異熟。有說。聲是現在加行所發。異熟果是先業所起。復有說者。離初靜慮染時語表便斷。若是異熟者應離三界染時方斷。復有說者。聲有三種。謂善不善無記。異熟果唯無記。有說。若聲是異熟者生可愛趣。應一切時出如意聲。生非可愛趣。應一切時出不如意聲。現見有時與此相違。是故聲非異熟。復有說者。聲有間斷異熟色無間斷。是故聲非異熟。然諸菩薩由二因緣。發願求佛大士夫相微妙梵音。一由曾見。二由曾聞。由曾見者。謂彼菩薩。曾見諸佛處大集會。為諸有情以梵音聲。宣說正法摧伏異論。微妙深遠具丈夫相。由曾聞者。謂彼菩薩。具聞如來以梵音聲。宣說正法乃至具丈夫相。爾時菩薩見聞歡喜深心愛樂。則便誓受順彼正因。我諸禁戒梵行精進。皆當迴向願於未來。得住如是大士行類。由此意樂復以種種上妙香花供具音樂。供養諸佛獨覺聲聞制多形像。承事供養父母師長同梵行者。修如是等殊勝福時。一一迴求此梵聲相。又勤淨除二種業道。謂麁惡語。及雜穢語。由勤淨除麁惡語故。得大士相微妙梵音。由此梵音摧伏一切外道他論。由勤淨除雜穢語故。

— and no sound whatsoever is a ripening-fruit. Question: why is no sound a ripening-fruit? Some say: sound belongs to the third transmission — that is, the initial karma first produces the great elements, and the great elements produce sound; since sound belongs to the third remove, it is not a ripening-fruit. Some further say: sound belongs to the fifth transmission — that is, the initial karma produces the ripening great elements, the ripening great elements produce the nutriment-sustained great elements, the nutriment-sustained great elements produce the outflow-consistent great elements, and it is from these outflow-consistent great elements that sound is produced; since sound belongs to the fifth remove, it is not a ripening-fruit. Some further say: sound follows the will and can be altered at will, while no ripening dharma can be altered at will. Some further say: sound gives rise to further sound, whereas a ripening never again gives rise to another ripening. Some say: sound is brought forth by present exertion, while a ripening-fruit arises from prior karma. Some further say: manifest verbal karma ceases upon becoming detached from the defilement of the first dhyāna alone, whereas, if it were a ripening, it ought to cease only upon becoming detached from the defilement of all three realms. Some further say: sound is of three kinds — wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral — while a ripening-fruit is neutral alone. Some say: if sound were itself a ripening, then one born in an agreeable destiny ought at all times to utter agreeable sounds, and one born in a disagreeable destiny ought at all times to utter disagreeable sounds — yet one plainly observes cases contrary to this, and hence sound is not a ripening. Some further say: sound has gaps and interruptions, while ripened form has none — hence sound is not a ripening. Now, bodhisattvas, through two causes and conditions, vow to seek the great being's mark of the buddha's own subtle Brahmā-voice: first, through having once seen it; second, through having once heard it. Through having once seen it: this means that the bodhisattva once saw, at a great assembly presided over by a buddha, that buddha proclaiming the true Dharma to beings with a Brahmā-voice, subduing rival doctrines, subtle and profound, possessed of the mark of the great being. Through having once heard it: this means that the bodhisattva once fully heard the Tathāgata proclaiming the true Dharma with a Brahmā-voice, and so on up to being possessed of the mark of the great being. At that time, the bodhisattva, having seen and heard this, rejoiced and, with a deeply loving and delighted mind, vowed to undertake the proper causes according with it — that all his own precepts, pure conduct, and diligent effort should be dedicated, with the wish that in the future he should come to abide in the very same class of great being's conduct. Moved by this very intention, he further makes offerings of various superb incense, flowers, offerings, and music to the images of buddhas, pratyekabuddhas, śrāvakas, and stūpas, and reverently serves and provides for his parents, teachers, and fellow practitioners of pure conduct — and, whenever cultivating such superior merit, he dedicates each act toward seeking this very mark of the Brahmā-voice. He further diligently purifies himself of two paths of karma — harsh speech, and idle chatter. Through diligently purifying himself of harsh speech, he obtains the great being's mark of the subtle Brahmā-voice, by which this Brahmā-voice subdues every rival doctrine of the outside paths. Through diligently purifying himself of idle chatter,

感得言詞威肅清亮。由此言詞映奪一切世俗異論。譬如有人見他處在華妙堂閣。陳列五樂歡娛自恣。聞他拊奏五樂音聲。作是思惟。

— he obtains speech that is dignified, solemn, clear, and resonant, by which this speech outshines and overwhelms every worldly rival doctrine. It is like a certain person who sees another dwelling in a magnificent hall, arrayed with the five kinds of music, reveling freely in pleasure, and who, hearing the sound of another's music being played, thinks to himself:

我於何時當得如是處妙堂閣。陳列五樂歡娛自適。既思惟已勤加功力。積集財寶如其所願皆能辦之。菩薩亦爾由見及聞。發願求佛梵音聲相諸業。過去乃至廣說。問何故作此論。答諸有不顧去來愚三世者。欲令過去未來是無。唯說現在無為是有。為遮彼執顯過去未來是實有法故作斯論。復次所以作此論者。為欲遮止外道所說故。

— "When shall I too come to dwell in such a magnificent hall, arrayed with the five kinds of music, reveling freely in delight?" And having so reflected, he diligently exerts his own effort, accumulating wealth, until he is able to accomplish everything just as he had wished. The bodhisattva is just the same: through having seen and heard, he vows to seek the karma that brings about the buddha's own mark of the Brahmā-voice-sound. Are past dharmas, and so on in full — Question: why compose this treatise? Answer: because there are those who, disregarding past and future and being ignorant of the three times, wish to hold that the past and the future do not exist, and say that only the present and the unconditioned exist. In order to refute this attachment and to show that the past and future are dharmas that really exist, this treatise is composed. Reading note — Ru-Yi

The nine-fold refutation's most quietly devastating argument is the empirical one — that agreeable destinies do not reliably produce agreeable voices, nor disagreeable destinies disagreeable ones — since it refuses to settle the matter by definition or authority alone, instead appealing to what anyone can simply observe about how voices actually vary; a philosophical debate over what counts as karmic ripening is, in the end, answered by checking against ordinary experience.

3. Why Causation Runs Only Forward: Refuting a Backward-Flowing Time

A different opponent's theory is dismantled: that later dharmas cause earlier ones, as a spring's later-arriving water is said to press the earlier water into flowing forth. The treatise shows this reverses every accepted chain of dependent origination, both internal (ignorance conditioning formations, not the other way around) and external (seed producing sprout, not sprout producing seed), and would produce absurdities that strike at the heart of the entire moral cosmos — receiving a karmic fruit before ever committing the deed, falling into hell before committing the crime, attaining buddhahood before ever practicing the path. A dense technical matrix then works out exactly how a karma's own temporal location (past, present, or future) constrains where its fruit can fall, cross-referenced against three different technical senses of "present" (momentary, phase-based, and class-nature-based), before opening a new fourfold combinatorial inquiry into how bodily, verbal, and mental karma variously combine to produce agreeable and disagreeable ripening.

Chinese Source

彼說言一切諸法後為前因。猶如泉水後逼於前令涌令注。如是後水為前水因。諸法亦爾由後念法所推逼故。令從未來起入現在。復從現在滅入過去。是故未來為現在因。現為過因。為遮彼執明一切法。前為後因非後前因。若說後法為前因者。便違內外諸法緣起。

Ru-Yi Translation

— that school holds that every dharma has what comes later as the cause of what comes earlier, just as, in a spring, the later water presses upon the earlier water, causing it to well up and flow forth, so that the later water is the cause of the earlier water; and that dharmas are just the same, in that it is precisely because they are pressed and driven forward by the dharma of a later moment that they arise out of the future and enter the present, and then, from the present, cease and pass into the past — hence the future is the cause of the present, and the present is the cause of the past. In order to refute this attachment and to make clear that every dharma has what comes earlier as the cause of what comes later, never what comes later as the cause of what comes earlier — for if one holds that a later dharma is the cause of an earlier one, this directly contradicts the dependent origination of every dharma, both internal and external —

違內法緣起者。謂應行緣無明。乃至老死緣生。因於子息而有父母。眼識為緣生於眼色。乃至意識為緣生於意法。從頞部曇生羯邏藍。乃至從老生於中年。違外法緣起者。謂應芽為種因。乃至果為花因如是等若爾則有大過。謂未作應得。應先受善惡異熟。後造善惡業。先墮無間獄。後造五逆罪。先受輪王位。後造輪王業。先證阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。然後修菩薩行。若未作而得。亦應已作而失。如此則無繫縛出離。勿有此過。是故諸法前為後因。非後前因。由是因緣故作斯論。諸業過去彼果過去耶。答彼果或過去。或未來。或現在果。謂異熟果由已滅等差別故。成三種諸業未來彼果未來耶。答如是。以非果先因在後故。諸業現在彼果現在耶。答彼果或現在。或未來。所釋如前。此中有說。依剎那現在作論。依此所說諸業過去。彼果隨在何世。而彼業皆有四種。謂順現法受。乃至順不定受。諸業未來諸業現在。隨所有果彼業亦四。而不應言諸業現在彼果現在。非此剎那造業。即此剎那受異熟果故。或有說者。此中依分位現在而作論。依此所說諸業過去彼果過去。未來現在。諸業未來諸業現在彼果未來。皆如前說。諸業現在彼果現在者。彼業有二。謂順現法受。順不定受。復有說者。此中依一眾同分現在作論。依此所說諸業過去。彼果或過去者如前說。或未來者彼業有二。謂順後次受。及順不定受。或現在者彼業有三。除順現法受。諸業未來亦如前說。諸業現在彼果或現在者。彼業有二。謂順現法受。及順不定受。或未來者彼業有三。除順現法受。問頗有順現法受業。因在過去果在未來耶。答有。謂依剎那分位現在。不依一眾同分現在而說。復有說者。亦依一眾同分現在而說。謂如有人。造作增長順現法受業已。未獲與果率爾命終。爾時即名因在過去果在未來。頗有順現法受業。因在過去果在現在。或因在現在果在未來耶。若依一眾同分現在為問。應答言無。尊者妙音說有。謂如前說。頗有如身業感異熟果。語業意業不爾耶。乃至廣說。今於此中方便顯示三業所感愛非愛果。後見蘊中方便顯示分位差別。此中所問先答黑品。後答白品。是名此處略毘婆沙。頗有如身業感異熟果。語業意業不爾耶。答有。如身不護語護。彼於爾時有善心或無記心。謂於今時起不善身表業。由此發起無表業隨轉。及於今時或先時起善語表業。由此發起無表業隨轉。即於爾時善心現起或無記心。此中身業感非愛異熟。語業感愛異熟。意業若善心起感愛異熟。若無記心起。不感愛非愛異熟。又如身護語不護。彼於爾時有不善心或無記心。謂於今時起善身表業。由此發起無表業隨轉。及於今時或由先時起不善語表業。由此發起無表業隨轉。即於爾時不善心現起或無記心。此中身業感愛異熟。語業感非愛異熟。意業若不善心起。感非愛異熟。若無記心起。不感愛非愛異熟。是名如身業感異熟果。語業意業不爾。

As for contradicting the dependent origination of internal dharmas: this means that formations are conditioned by ignorance, and so on up through birth being conditioned by aging-and-death; that it is on account of the child that there come to be parents; that eye-consciousness, taken as condition, gives rise to eye and form, and so on up through mental consciousness, taken as condition, giving rise to mind and mental objects; and that the kalala embryonic stage arises from the arbuda stage, and so on up through the middle years arising from old age. As for contradicting the dependent origination of external dharmas: this means that the sprout would be the cause of the seed, and so on up through the fruit being the cause of the flower. If this were so, there would follow a great fault — that one would obtain a result before ever performing the deed, that one would first experience the ripening of good or evil before creating the good or evil karma, that one would first fall into Avīci hell before committing the five ānantarya deeds, that one would first receive the position of a wheel-turning king before creating the karma of a wheel-turning king, that one would first realize unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening and only afterward cultivate the bodhisattva practices. 10. A Definite Hell Despite an Unfinished Killing, and Why There Can Be a Sin of Killing at All

A vivid set of scenarios shows that even an interrupted ānantarya deed can guarantee hell as its fruit — a would-be matricide seized by authorities mid-attempt, a mother who kills her attacking son in self-defense, a mother's own guardian deity striking the son down while she survives, or a son overcome by remorse partway through who then takes his own life instead, still falling to hell exactly as if he had completed the act. This sets up the section's deepest question: since ultimately there is no real "being," only aggregates, elements, and sense-fields, how can there be a genuine sin of killing at all? Three masters offer converging answers — Vasumitra's parallel to the notion of a self persisting despite no self existing; another's appeal to what the aggregates make possible to believe (permanence, pleasure, purity); and the Great Virtuous One's account of the threefold awareness ("I shall kill," "I am killing," "I have killed") that these very aggregates give rise to — closing with the rule that the sin requires both a preparatory action and the result's actual completion, never one alone.

11. Which Aggregate, In Which Time, Is Actually Killed?

A genuinely difficult metaphysical puzzle follows: since the past has already ceased, the future has not yet arrived, and the present does not abide, which aggregate, at which time, is actually the object of killing? The resolution locates the true object in the future — one "kills" by obstructing the very causes and conditions that would otherwise bring a future continuum's aggregates into being — with a further debate over whether the fast-vanishing present aggregate counts too, since cutting short its potency before it would naturally cease still prevents what follows from continuing, exactly as a jar's breaking spills the milk inside it even though the milk itself was never directly struck. Related debates follow on whether only form (touchable by weapons) or all five aggregates can be "killed," and whether killing two victims with a single effort produces one act of manifest karma or two.

12. Near-Misses and Edge Cases: Doomed Lifespans, Mutual Slaying, Coercion, and Silent Crimes

A closing sequence of precisely calibrated edge cases tests the killing-sin's exact boundaries: attacking someone whose lifespan was ending anyway incurs no sin only if the attack shortens nothing at all, even a single moment's difference being sufficient for full culpability; attacking someone fatally and then dying oneself before their actual death incurs no sin, since one's own continuum ends before the guilt can be established in it; two combatants who kill each other simultaneously likewise incur no sin, for the identical reason; acting under a king's compulsion still typically incurs the sin unless one was genuinely willing to sacrifice one's own life rather than comply; and the section closes with three memorable cases of crime committed without the expected medium — ordering a killing by speech alone, committing false speech through a bodily gesture alone, and, most strikingly, committing both sins at once through nothing but a sage's own resentful thought and the customary silent gesture of declaring one's purity during the poṣadha confession rite.
