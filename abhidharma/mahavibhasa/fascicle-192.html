The Three States of Existence

Fascicle 192 closes the Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa (念住納息第一之六) with the final scenes of the Buddha's own parinirvāṇa — Aniruddha's knowledge of the Buddha's hidden mind, Mahākāśyapa's fragrant milk, and Ānanda's vigil — before turning to the four states of existence (proper, death, intermediate, rebirth) and opening the Nyāsa of the Three States of Existence (三有納息): the many sūtra senses of "existence," why the noble path is excluded from that category, and an exhaustive accounting of what is relinquished and what continues at death and rebirth across every realm. This page carries Ru‑Yi's complete translation.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: fascicle 192 complete, 8 sections Source: Taishō T1545, vol. 27, fascicle 192 Topic: 見蘊第八, 念住納息第一之六 → 三有納息第二之一 — the four states and three states of existence
Milestone: partway through this fascicle, the treatise opens the Nyāsa of the Three States of Existence (三有納息) — the second Nyāsa of the Views Aggregate, following the Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa that ran from fascicle 187 through the opening of this one.

Fascicle 192 closes the narrative of the Buddha's own final nirvāṇa begun in fascicle 191, then shifts into a new stretch of doctrinal analysis inside the Views Aggregate (見蘊第八): the four states of existence, and the opening of the Three States of Existence Nyāsa. 1. Ānanda's Question and the Elephant Fording the River

The narrative of the Buddha's parinirvāṇa concludes: Ānanda's question to Aniruddha about whether the Buddha has already died or is merely in the attainment of cessation, and how Aniruddha could know the content of a mind he could not directly perceive — resolved through the image of an elephant-king fording a deep river, known not by direct sight but by the tracks left at the water's edge.

Chinese Source

爾時世尊說此語已便入初靜慮。次第乃至入滅盡定。時尊者阿難則問尊者阿泥律陀言。世尊今者已般涅槃耶。答言未也。但是入滅盡定耳。復問云何知耶。答言我親從佛聞。世尊入第四靜慮依不動寂靜定而般涅槃世間眼滅。問尊者阿難為知故問為不知耶。若知者何故復問。若不知者云何名知佛心耶。有說。彼尊者知之。問若爾者何故復問。答亦有知而故問為發起言論故。如說世尊知而故問。復次尊者阿難欲顯尊者阿泥律陀有勝德故雖知而問。謂彼尊者雖有勝德而眾不知。欲令其知是以故問。有說。彼尊者不知。問若爾者云何名知佛心耶。答佛出定已阿難知其前心而彼時不知者以佛猶在定故。復次彼尊者若住常性心時能知佛心。爾時彼心由二憂惱所覆沒故不知佛心。一者失眷屬憂惱。二者失大師憂惱。問尊者阿泥律陀云何知耶。有說。世尊臨涅槃時入共聲聞等至故彼得知。評曰。不應作是說。以佛爾時現入不共一切聲聞獨覺靜慮解脫等持等至故。問若然者彼云何知。答世尊爾時起共聲聞獨覺入出定心。彼由此知是彼境故。猶如象王渡深河時。若正在河中則人無知者。但觀入出水跡則知象王所入出處。如是世尊若住甚深等至河時。一切聲聞獨覺不能現見。但觀如入出水跡入出定心便知。入如是定從如是定出。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the World-Honored One, having spoken these words, thereupon entered the first dhyāna, and, in succession, up to entering the attainment of cessation. The Venerable Ānanda then asked the Venerable Aniruddha, saying: has the World-Honored One now already entered final nirvāṇa? He answered: not yet — this is merely the attainment of cessation. Ānanda further asked: how do you know this? He answered: I have heard it directly from the Buddha himself that the World-Honored One will enter the fourth dhyāna and, in reliance upon the unshakeable, tranquil concentration, enter final nirvāṇa, and the eye of the world will be extinguished. Question: did the Venerable Ānanda ask this question knowing the answer already, or not knowing it? If he knew it, why did he ask again? If he did not know it, how could he be said to know the Buddha's own mind? Some say: that venerable one did indeed know it. Question: if so, then why did he ask again? Answer: there is also such a thing as asking deliberately although one already knows, in order to give rise to discussion — just as it is said that the World-Honored One, too, sometimes asked deliberately although he already knew. Furthermore, the Venerable Ānanda wished to display that the Venerable Aniruddha possessed a superior merit, and, though he himself knew, he asked nonetheless — that is, although that venerable one possessed a superior merit, the assembly did not know of it, and, wishing to make it known to them, he therefore asked deliberately. Some say: that venerable one did not, in fact, know it. Question: if so, then how could he be said to know the Buddha's own mind? Answer: once the Buddha had risen from concentration, Ānanda came to know his preceding mind; the reason he did not know it at that earlier moment was that the Buddha was still in concentration. Furthermore, that venerable one, when abiding in his ordinary, constant state of mind, would indeed have been able to know the Buddha's own mind; but, at that particular time, his own mind was overwhelmed and submerged by two griefs — first, the grief of losing his own kinsman, and second, the grief of losing his great Teacher — and it was for this reason that he did not know the Buddha's own mind. Question: how, then, did the Venerable Aniruddha come to know it? Some say: at the moment of his final nirvāṇa, the World-Honored One entered an attainment shared in common with the śrāvakas, and it was by this that Aniruddha was able to know it. Assessment: this ought not to be said, since, at that time, the Buddha manifestly entered dhyānas, liberations, concentrations, and attainments not shared in common with any śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha at all. Question: if this is so, then how did he come to know it? Answer: at that time, the World-Honored One gave rise to a mind of entering and rising from concentration that is shared in common with śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, and it was by means of this shared object that Aniruddha came to know it — just as, when the elephant-king fords a deep river, no one is able to perceive him while he is directly in the midst of the river, yet, by observing the tracks where he entered and emerged from the water, one is able to know the very places where the elephant-king entered and emerged. It is the same here: while the World-Honored One abides within the river of profound attainment, no śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha at all is able directly to perceive him, yet, by observing, as it were, the tracks of his entering and emerging from the water — that is, his mind of entering and rising from concentration — one comes to know that he has entered such-and-such a concentration and risen from such-and-such a concentration.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The elephant-fording-the-river simile resolves a genuinely delicate narrative problem — how could Aniruddha know the content of a mind he could not directly perceive? — by locating knowledge not in direct sight of the deep water itself but in the visible tracks at its edges, a model of inference from effect to cause that the text treats as no less certain than direct perception.

2. The Fragrant Milk and the Two Unburned Garments

Mahākāśyapa extinguishes the fire consuming the Buddha's own body with four streams of fragrant milk descending from the sky, explained through a careful physical account of which substances can and cannot extinguish flame; Ānanda's seven-day vigil closes with a verse on the two garments that alone survived the pyre, given four distinct symbolic readings.

Chinese Source

如說爾時尊者大迦葉波作是念。我當以何滅焚如來身火。尋作是念。我今應以香乳滅之。則起心時便有四道香乳從空而下由此令焚如來身火一時而滅。問何故必以香乳滅焚如來身火耶。答欲顯與諸仙人相似法故。謂彼國俗若仙人命終則以乳滅焚身之火。若受欲者命終則以酒滅焚身之火。佛於諸仙中勝則是第一仙人。故今亦以香乳滅火。復次欲令佛設利羅極清淨故以乳灌之。復次佛生身是乳所長養故。今設利羅亦以乳浴之。復次乳性肥而能滅火。或有物雖性肥而不能滅火。乃令其熾如酥油等。有物雖能滅火而性不肥如水酒等。唯乳性肥又能滅火故唯以乳。由此義故尊者大迦葉波。現四道香乳用滅如來焚身之火。

Ru-Yi Translation

As it is said, at that time the Venerable Mahākāśyapa reflected as follows: with what should I extinguish the fire consuming the Tathāgata's own body? He then reflected further: I ought now to extinguish it with fragrant milk. The very moment this thought arose, four streams of fragrant milk descended from the sky, and, by this, the fire consuming the Tathāgata's own body was extinguished all at once. Question: why was it necessary to extinguish the fire consuming the Tathāgata's own body specifically with fragrant milk? Answer: it is in order to display a practice resembling that of the various seers — that is, the custom of that land was that, when a seer died, milk was used to extinguish the fire consuming his body, whereas, when one given over to sensual desire died, wine was used to extinguish the fire consuming his body. The Buddha, being supreme among every seer, is himself the very foremost of seers, and it was for this reason that fragrant milk, too, was now used to extinguish the fire. Furthermore, it was in order that the Buddha's own relics should be utterly pure that they were bathed in milk. Furthermore, the Buddha's own born body was itself nourished and sustained by milk, and it was for this reason that his relics, too, were now bathed in milk. Furthermore, milk is, by its own nature, rich, and is able to extinguish fire; there exist substances that, though rich by nature, are unable to extinguish fire, and even cause it to blaze more fiercely instead — such as ghee and oil; and there exist substances that, though able to extinguish fire, are not rich by nature — such as water and wine. Milk alone is both rich by nature and able to extinguish fire, and it was for this reason that milk alone was used. It was on account of this meaning that the Venerable Mahākāśyapa manifested four streams of fragrant milk in order to extinguish the fire consuming the Tathāgata's own body.

如說爾時尊者阿難佛涅槃已經七晝夜右遶焚如來火聚。說伽他言。

As it is said, at that time the Venerable Ānanda, seven days and nights after the Buddha's final nirvāṇa, circumambulated the blazing pyre of the Tathāgata's own remains, keeping it to his right, and spoke a verse, saying:

千衣纏佛葬　唯二衣不燒謂外及襯身　此為奇特事問何故世尊唯二衣不燒耶。答有信敬佛諸天神等威力所持令不燒故。有說。是佛願力所持令內外淨故。謂一衣在內持如來馱都令不散染。

"a thousand garments wrapped the Buddha's own burial, yet only two garments were not burned — the outer one, and the one worn against the body — this indeed is a marvel." Question: why were only two of the World-Honored One's garments not burned? Answer: it was by the majestic power of the gods and spirits, out of faith and reverence for the Buddha, that they were sustained and kept from burning. Some say: it was by the power of the Buddha's own vow, sustaining them so that both the inner and outer should remain pure — that is, the one garment on the inside held the Tathāgata's own relics together, preventing them from scattering and becoming defiled,

一衣在外持所有灰令不飄坌。有說。此表如來正法有內外二種護故。內護者謂清淨苾芻苾芻尼等。外護者謂淨信國王大臣等。此則正法威力令其不燒。有說。此表如來內心外身俱清淨故。心清淨者謂已永離一切煩惱并習氣故。身清淨者謂從最勝相異熟業所引生故。

while the one garment on the outside held all the ashes together, preventing them from being scattered and blown about. Some say: this signifies that the Tathāgata's own true Dharma possesses two kinds of protection, internal and external — the internal protection being the pure monks and nuns, and the external protection being kings, ministers, and others of pure faith — and it was the majestic power of the true Dharma itself that kept these garments from burning. Some say: this signifies that the Tathāgata was pure both in mind within and in body without — pure in mind, since he had already permanently severed every affliction together with its residual habitual traces; and pure in body, since it was drawn forth and generated by the supremely excellent, marked karma of retribution.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Assigning Mahākāśyapa's four streams of milk to a rule about what can and cannot extinguish fire — some rich substances feed flame, some fire-quenching substances aren't rich, milk alone is both — turns a miraculous gesture into a claim answerable to ordinary physical reasoning, characteristic of how this treatise handles even its most legendary material.

3. The Four States of Existence

The treatise turns to a systematic account of the proper, death, intermediate, and rebirth states of existence — each defined, named, and cross-classified by duration, defilement, contamination, and the same-class or different-class character of its mind — before an intricate combinatorial analysis of how these states relate to the "five formations," the older term for the five aggregates that an earlier Buddha used before Śākyamuni renamed them.

Chinese Source

如說四有。謂本有中有生有死有。有聲目多義如前廣說。此中有聲說屬眾同分有情數五蘊名有。云何本有。答除生分死分諸蘊中間諸有。此則一期五蘊四蘊為性。問何故此有說名本有。答此是前時所造業生故名本有。問若爾餘有亦是本有皆前時所造業所生故。答若是前時所造業生麁顯易覺明了現見者說名本有。餘雖前時所造業生而微隱難覺非明了現見是以不說。云何死有。答死分諸蘊則命終時五蘊四蘊為性。云何中有。答除死分生分諸蘊中間諸有則二有中間五蘊為性。問何故此有說名中有。答此於二有中間生故。名中有。

Ru-Yi Translation

As it is said, there are four states of existence — namely, the proper state, the intermediate state, the rebirth-state, and the death-state. The term "existence" carries many meanings, as already explained at length above; here, the term "existence" refers to the five aggregates belonging to a sentient being, subsumed under a single class of common existence, and is so named. What is the proper state? Answer: it refers to every state of existence lying between the portion of rebirth and the portion of death, having as its own nature the five aggregates, or the four aggregates, throughout that entire span of life. Question: why is this state named the "proper" state? Answer: because it is generated by karma performed at an earlier time, it is named the proper state. Question: if so, then every other state, too, is likewise generated by karma performed at an earlier time — why is only this one so named? Answer: what is generated by karma performed at an earlier time, yet is coarse, manifest, easily perceived, and clearly and directly seen, is named the proper state; the others, although likewise generated by karma performed at an earlier time, are subtle, hidden, difficult to perceive, and not clearly and directly seen, and it is for this reason that they are not so named. What is the death-state? Answer: it refers to the aggregates belonging to the portion of death — that is, the five aggregates, or the four aggregates, at the very moment of dying. What is the intermediate state? Answer: it refers to every state of existence lying between the aggregates of the portion of death and the portion of rebirth — that is, the five aggregates lying between these two states of existence. Question: why is this state named the "intermediate" state? Answer: because it arises between these two states of existence, it is named the intermediate state.

問若爾餘有亦是中有皆於二有中間生故。答若於二有中間生非趣所攝者名中有。餘雖二有中間生而是趣攝不名中有。云何生有。答生分諸蘊則結生時五蘊四蘊為性。問此四有幾剎那幾相續。答二剎那謂死有生有二相續謂餘有。問此四有幾染污幾不染污。答皆通二種。問此四有心幾染污幾不染污。答生有心唯染污。餘心染污不染污。問此四有幾有漏幾無漏。答皆唯有漏。問此四有時心幾有漏幾無漏。答二唯有漏謂死有生有時心。二通有漏無漏謂餘有時心。問此四有時幾起同分心幾起不同分心。答二唯起同分心謂死有生有時。二起同分不同分心謂餘有時。

Question: if so, then every other state, too, arises between these same two states of existence — why is only this one named the intermediate state? Answer: what arises between these two states of existence, yet is not subsumed under any destiny, is named the intermediate state; the others, although they, too, arise between these two states of existence, are subsumed under a destiny, and are hence not named the intermediate state. What is the rebirth-state? Answer: it refers to the aggregates belonging to the portion of rebirth — that is, the five aggregates, or the four aggregates, at the very moment of forming the connective link of a new existence. Question: of these four states of existence, how many last a single moment, and how many last a continuous succession? Answer: two last a single moment — namely, the death-state and the rebirth-state; and two last a continuous succession — namely, the remaining two states. Question: of these four states of existence, how many are defiled, and how many are undefiled? Answer: all of them extend to both kinds. Question: of the minds belonging to these four states of existence, how many are defiled, and how many are undefiled? Answer: the mind of the rebirth-state is exclusively defiled; the minds of the others may be either defiled or undefiled. Question: of these four states of existence, how many are contaminated, and how many are uncontaminated? Answer: all of them are exclusively contaminated. Question: of the minds belonging to these four states of existence, how many are contaminated, and how many are uncontaminated? Answer: two are exclusively contaminated — namely, the minds of the death-state and the rebirth-state; and two extend to both the contaminated and the uncontaminated — namely, the minds of the remaining two states. Question: at these four states of existence, how many give rise to a same-class mind, and how many to a different-class mind? Answer: two give rise exclusively to a same-class mind — namely, at the death-state and the rebirth-state; and two give rise to either a same-class or a different-class mind — namely, at the remaining two states.

諸欲有彼一切有五行耶乃至廣說。此中諸蘊以行聲說。過去如來應正等覺說蘊為行。今釋迦牟尼如來應正等覺說行為蘊。以先佛說五行今佛說五取蘊故。此阿毘達磨中說五行者。欲顯今佛所說五蘊則是先佛所說五行故。問何故先佛說蘊為行今佛說行為蘊耶。答佛觀所化有情隨所應而說故。謂先佛所化應聞說行而得正解。今佛所化應聞說蘊而得正解。問何故名行。答流轉故名行謂前生諸蘊由後生諸蘊故流轉。或後生諸蘊由前生諸蘊故流轉。若生欲界以欲界心為同分心與彼命根眾同分為同分故。以色無色界及無漏心為不同分心。不與彼命根眾同分為同分故。若生色界以色界心為同分心。以欲無色界及無漏心為不同分心。若生無色界以無色界心為同分心。

Does every instance of desire-existence thereby possess the five formations? — and so on at length. Here, the aggregates are spoken of under the term "formations." A past Tathāgata, Arhat, Fully and Perfectly Awakened One spoke of the aggregates as "formations"; the present Śākyamuni Tathāgata, Arhat, Fully and Perfectly Awakened One speaks of "formations" as the aggregates — that is, an earlier Buddha spoke of the "five formations," whereas the present Buddha speaks of the "five aggregates of grasping." That this Abhidharma text speaks of the "five formations" is in order to display that the five aggregates spoken of by the present Buddha are precisely the five formations spoken of by an earlier Buddha. Question: why did an earlier Buddha speak of the aggregates as "formations," while the present Buddha speaks of "formations" as the aggregates? Answer: the Buddha teaches according to whatever accords with the sentient beings he is converting — that is, those converted by an earlier Buddha attained correct understanding upon hearing them spoken of as "formations," whereas those converted by the present Buddha attain correct understanding upon hearing them spoken of as "aggregates." Question: why are they named "formations"? Answer: because they flow and transmigrate, they are named formations — that is, the aggregates of an earlier life flow and transmigrate by means of the aggregates of a later life, or the aggregates of a later life flow and transmigrate by means of the aggregates of an earlier life. If one is born in the desire realm, a mind of the desire realm is a same-class mind, since it belongs to the same class as the life-faculty and the class of common existence of that being, while a mind of the form or formless realm, or an uncontaminated mind, is a different-class mind, since it does not belong to the same class as the life-faculty and the class of common existence of that being. If one is born in the form realm, a mind of the form realm is a same-class mind, while a mind of the desire or formless realm, or an uncontaminated mind, is a different-class mind. If one is born in the formless realm, a mind of the formless realm is a same-class mind,

以無漏心為不同分心。

while an uncontaminated mind is a different-class mind.

諸欲有彼一切五行耶。設五行彼一切欲有耶。答應作四句。有欲有非五行。謂欲界有情住不同分心及住無想滅盡定。彼時欲有唯二行故。有五行非欲有。謂色界有想天住同分心。若無想天不得無想。

Does every instance of desire-existence thereby possess the five formations? And does every instance of the five formations thereby belong to desire-existence? Answer: a full tetralemma must be formed. There is a case of desire-existence without the five formations — that is, a being of the desire realm abiding in a different-class mind, or abiding in the attainment of no-perception or of cessation, at which time his desire-existence possesses only two formations. There is a case of the five formations without desire-existence — that is, a perceiving god of the form realm abiding in a same-class mind, or a non-perceiving god not yet having attained the state of no-perception,

爾時皆具五行彼無想天有心位。必不起不同分心故而彼行非欲有。

at which time both alike are complete with the five formations — since a non-perceiving god, at the stage possessing mind, never gives rise to a different-class mind — yet these formations do not belong to desire-existence.

有欲有亦五行。謂欲界有情住同分心。彼時欲有具五行故。有非欲有亦非五行。謂色界有想天住不同分心及住無想滅盡定。若無想天得無想若生無色界。爾時但有二行或四行或一行。彼行又非欲有故。諸色有有想天彼一切五行耶。設五行彼一切色有有想天耶。答應作四句。有色有有想天非五行。謂色界有想天住不同分心。及住無想滅盡定。爾時色有有想天皆唯二行故。有五行非色有有想天。

There is a case of both desire-existence and the five formations — that is, a being of the desire realm abiding in a same-class mind, at which time his desire-existence is complete with the five formations. There is a case of neither desire-existence nor the five formations — that is, a perceiving god of the form realm abiding in a different-class mind, or abiding in the attainment of no-perception or of cessation; or a non-perceiving god who has attained the state of no-perception; or one born in the formless realm — at which time there are only two formations, or four formations, or a single formation, and these formations, moreover, do not belong to desire-existence. Does every perceiving god of form-existence thereby possess the five formations? And does every instance of the five formations thereby belong to a perceiving god of form-existence? Answer: a full tetralemma must be formed. Answer: everyone who relinquishes formless-existence and continues on into form-existence does indeed have every dharma of the formless realm cease, and every dharma of the form realm manifest before the mind — that is, one who dies in the formless realm and is reborn in the form realm: at the moment of proceeding from the death-existence to the intermediate existence, "relinquishing formless-existence" refers to the formless realm's own death-existence, "continuing on into form-existence" refers to the form realm's own intermediate existence, "the dharmas of the formless realm ceasing" refers to the aggregates of the formless realm's own death-existence, and "the dharmas of the form realm manifesting before the mind" refers to the aggregates of the form realm's own intermediate existence. There is a case of a dharma of the formless realm ceasing and a dharma of the form realm manifesting before the mind, which is nevertheless not a case of relinquishing formless-existence and continuing on into form-existence — that is, without dying, a dharma of the formless realm ceases and a dharma of the form realm manifests before the mind: what is this case? It refers to the sphere of infinite consciousness ceasing, immediately followed by the fourth dhyāna manifesting before the mind; or the sphere of infinite space ceasing, immediately followed by the third or the fourth dhyāna manifesting before the mind.

Fascicle 192 Complete

This closes fascicle 192 of Ru‑Yi's translation. Fascicle 192 closes the Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa (念住納息第一之六) with the final scenes of the Buddha's own parinirvāṇa: Aniruddha's knowledge of the Buddha's hidden mind through the elephant-fording-the-river simile, Mahākāśyapa's fragrant milk extinguishing the funeral pyre, and Ānanda's seven-day vigil and verse on the two unburned garments. It then opens a new stretch of the Views Aggregate (見蘊第八): the four states of existence — proper, death, intermediate, and rebirth — cross-classified by duration, defilement, and contamination, and their relation to the "five formations"; and the opening of the Nyāsa of the Three States of Existence (三有納息), reconciling the many sūtra senses of "existence," explaining through five substantive criteria why the noble path itself is excluded from that category despite formally qualifying, considering and refining "vessel of suffering" and "something fearsome" as candidate definitions, and closing with an exhaustive combinatorial accounting of what is relinquished, attained, ceases, and manifests before the mind at death and rebirth across every pairing of realms, cross-referenced against prior moral discipline.
