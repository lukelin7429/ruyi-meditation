Knowing a Feeling as It Really Is

Fascicle 189 continues and closes the Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa (念住納息第一之三): completing the combinatorial analysis of cultivation across the four wholesome roots and the path of seeing; an exhaustive survey of what ordinary worldlings and noble ones cultivate at every stage of practice; a long series of tetralemmas working through every pairing among the four mindfulness-foundations; each foundation fully specified by cognition, ground, faculty, concentration, and object; and a genuine puzzle in the philosophy of mind — how one can know a feeling as it really is at the very moment of experiencing it. This page carries Ru‑Yi's complete translation.

Fascicle 189 continues and closes the Mindfulness-Foundations Nyāsa (念住納息第一之三) inside the Views Aggregate (見蘊第八), directly following fascicle 188's combinatorial analysis of cultivation across the four wholesome roots. This fascicle is unusually dense even by this treatise's own standards in its middle sections, before closing with one of the more genuinely philosophical passages in the entire work. 1. Completing the Wholesome-Root Combinatorics

The fascicle opens by finishing the previous fascicle's exhaustive tracking of present and future cultivation through the direct realizations of the four truths and into the path of cultivation proper — closing with the plain observation that, from this point on, only the quantity of mindfulness-foundations cultivated is worth tracking, since ordinary worldlings possess no noble mode of activity to track at all.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The closing of the wholesome-root combinatorics with a single blunt fact — every ordinary worldling "possesses no noble mode of activity at all" — marks a real shift in the text's own register: from here forward it stops tracking which modes of activity are cultivated, since for the unawakened there simply are none to track, and reports only bare quantity instead.

2. What Ordinary Worldlings and Noble Ones Cultivate at Every Stage

An exhaustive survey maps present and future cultivation of the four mindfulness-foundations across every stage of removing defilement from each of the nine grounds, and across the contemplation of impurity, mindfulness of breathing, the liberations, the bases of vanquishing, the totality-bases, the five superknowledges, the four immeasurables, the unimpeded understandings, wish-knowledge, terminal concentration, and the attainment of cessation — distinguishing throughout between the ordinary worldling's own limited range and the noble one's fuller cultivation.

3. Cultivating One Mindfulness-Foundation While Cultivating Another

A long series of tetralemma analyses works through every pairing among the four mindfulness-foundations — body and feeling, body and dharmas, feeling and mind, feeling and dharmas, mind and dharmas — determining, for each, exactly when cultivating one entails cultivating another, when it does not, when both occur together, and when neither occurs at all, with the case of a merely scattered wholesome mind candidly acknowledged as resisting the treatise's own categories.

5. Knowing a Feeling as It Really Is

A genuine puzzle in the philosophy of mind: since cognizing a feeling and the feeling itself cannot occupy the same moment of mind together, how can a sūtra say that, while experiencing pleasure, one knows as it really is that one experiences pleasure? Furthermore, the cognition of others' minds knows only mind and mental factors, whereas what is here called "knowing as it really is" also knows the support and the object of mind and mental factors. Furthermore, the cognition of others' minds takes each single dharma as its object one at a time, whereas what is here called "knowing as it really is" also takes many dharmas comprehensively as its object all at once — and it is for these reasons that the cognition of others' minds is not spoken of. Question: further, why are the cognitions of suffering and of origin not spoken of here? Some say: these, too, ought to be spoken of here, and that they are not spoken of should be understood as having a further significance to it. Some say: the cognitions of suffering and of origin are cognitions whose mode of activity is aversion, whereas what is here called "knowing as it really is" is a cognition whose mode of activity is delight, and hence they are not spoken of. Some say: the cognitions of suffering and of origin take as their object what is to be shunned, whereas what is here called "knowing as it really is" takes as its object what is to be delighted in.

有說。苦集智憎惡所緣而轉。此中如實智愛樂所緣而轉。有說。此中說如實知有漏無漏心心所法。然有漏心心所法無始數知。又麁近易了起世俗智則能了知不待起無漏智。是故無有捨世俗智而以苦集智知者。無漏心心所法昔來未知。又微細難了起無漏智乃能知之。

Some say: the cognitions of suffering and of origin operate by despising their object, whereas what is here called "knowing as it really is" operates by loving and delighting in its object. Some say: what is spoken of here is knowing as it really is the contaminated and uncontaminated mind and mental factors; yet the contaminated mind and mental factors have, from beginningless time, been known again and again, and, being coarse, near at hand, and easy to comprehend, when the conventional cognition arises, it is able to comprehend them without needing to await the arising of an uncontaminated cognition — and it is for this reason that there is no case of setting aside the conventional cognition in order to know them by the cognitions of suffering and of origin instead. The uncontaminated mind and mental factors, by contrast, have never been known before, and, being subtle and difficult to comprehend, it is only upon the arising of an uncontaminated cognition that one is able to know them.

是故此中如實知有漏心心所法。唯世俗智如實知無漏心心所法。謂餘三智由此不說有苦集智。有說。此中說如實知有漏無漏心心所法事差別相。彼有漏者。起世俗智則知更不起苦集智以難起故。及起時但知總相故。彼無漏者起世俗智不能知故便起道智。然道智生雖不如前唯差別知。然以總相知差別事故有道智無苦集智。有說。此中說。如實知有漏無漏心心所法行相差別。然有漏心心所法多非緣諦行相。設所有者亦未善成就故。但以世俗智作非諦行相。如實知其行相差別。無漏心心所法皆是緣諦行相極善成就故。還以道智作諦行相如實知其行相差別。是故此中無苦集智不說盡無生智者。此是見蘊但說諸見性智。彼非見性智是故不說。此中法智者。謂知法智品樂受。類智者。謂知類智品樂受。世俗智者。謂知有漏樂受。

It is for this reason that, here, the contaminated mind and mental factors are known as they really are by the conventional cognition alone, while the uncontaminated mind and mental factors are known as they really are by the remaining three cognitions — and it is on this account that the cognitions of suffering and of origin are not said to be present here. Some say: what is spoken of here is knowing as they really are the distinct particular instances of the contaminated and uncontaminated mind and mental factors. As for the contaminated: once the conventional cognition arises, one thereby knows them, and one does not further give rise to the cognitions of suffering and of origin, since these are difficult to give rise to, and, moreover, when they do arise, they know only the shared characteristic. As for the uncontaminated: since the conventional cognition arising is unable to know them, one thereupon gives rise to the cognition of the path — yet, although the arising of the cognition of the path does not, as with the former case, know each particular instance alone, it nevertheless knows the distinct particular matter by way of the shared characteristic, and hence there is the cognition of the path but not the cognitions of suffering and of origin. Some say: what is spoken of here is knowing as they really are the distinct modes of activity of the contaminated and uncontaminated mind and mental factors. Yet the contaminated mind and mental factors are, for the most part, not of a mode of activity that takes a truth as its object, and, even where such a mode of activity exists, it has not yet been well accomplished — and hence it is only by the conventional cognition, in a mode of activity not taking a truth as its object, that their distinct modes of activity are known as they really are. The uncontaminated mind and mental factors, by contrast, are entirely of a mode of activity that takes a truth as its object, and this has been extremely well accomplished — and hence it is, instead, by the cognition of the path, in a mode of activity taking a truth as its object, that their distinct modes of activity are known as they really are. It is for this reason that the cognitions of suffering and of origin are absent here. As for why the cognitions of exhaustion and of non-arising are not spoken of: this is the Views Aggregate, and hence only the cognitions that are of the nature of view are spoken of — those two cognitions, not being of the nature of view, are for this reason not spoken of. Here, "the cognition of the Dharma" refers to that which knows pleasant feeling belonging to the class of the cognition of the Dharma; "the subsequent cognition" refers to that which knows pleasant feeling belonging to the class of the subsequent cognition; "the conventional cognition" refers to that which knows contaminated pleasant feeling;

道智者。謂知無漏樂受。雖無一樂受四智所知。謂有漏者一智知。

and "the cognition of the path" refers to that which knows uncontaminated pleasant feeling. Although there is no single instance of pleasant feeling known by all four cognitions at once — since a contaminated instance is known by only one cognition —

無漏者三智知。然以總說故言四智如實知樂受。有說。無漏樂受四智所知。受苦受時如實知受苦受此一智謂世俗。以苦受唯有漏故世俗智知。受不苦不樂受時如實知受不苦不樂受。此四智謂法類世俗道。如知樂受說。

and an uncontaminated instance is known by three cognitions — it is nevertheless said, speaking generally, that pleasant feeling is known as it really is by four cognitions. Some say: uncontaminated pleasant feeling is in fact known by all four cognitions. When one experiences a painful feeling, one knows as it really is that one experiences a painful feeling by a single cognition alone — namely, the conventional cognition — since painful feeling, being exclusively contaminated, is known by the conventional cognition alone. When one experiences a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, one knows as it really is that one experiences a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling by four cognitions — namely, the cognition of the Dharma, the subsequent cognition, the conventional cognition, and the cognition of the path — to be explained as with the knowledge of pleasant feeling.
