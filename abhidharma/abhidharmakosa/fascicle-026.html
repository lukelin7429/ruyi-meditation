The Ten Kinds of Knowledge

Fascicle 26 opens Chapter 7 (分別智品第七之一, Distinguishing Knowledge), the treatise's systematic account of knowledge (智) itself — how it relates to the forbearance and view already discussed throughout Chapter 6, and the tenfold classification (worldly, dharma, subsequent, the four truth-specific knowledges, knowledge of others' minds, and the two knowledges completing arhatship) that will structure the rest of this chapter.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress, 6 sections so far Source: Taishō T1558, vol. 29, fascicle 26 Author: Vasubandhu · Translator into Chinese: Xuánzàng

1. Forbearance, Knowledge, and View; the Ten Kinds of Knowledge

Chapter 6 spoke often of "forbearance" (忍) and "knowledge" (智), and later of "right view" and "right knowledge," without ever pausing to sort out how these three — forbearance, knowledge, and view — actually relate to one another. Chapter 7 opens by settling this directly: is there forbearance that fails to count as knowledge? Is there knowledge that fails to count as view?

Chinese Source

前品初說諸忍諸智，於後復說正見正智。為有忍非智耶？為有智非見耶？頌曰： 聖慧忍非智， 盡無生非見， 餘二有漏慧， 皆智六見性。 論曰：慧有二種，有漏、無漏，唯無漏慧立以聖名。此聖慧中八忍非智性，自所斷疑未已斷故。可見性攝，推度性故。盡與無生二智非見性，已息求心不推度故。所餘皆通智見二性，已斷自疑、推度性故。諸有漏慧皆智性攝，於中唯六亦是見性，謂五染污見、世正見為六。如是所說聖有漏慧，皆擇法故並慧性攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

The previous chapter, at its outset, spoke of the various forbearances and knowledges, and later on also spoke of right view and right knowledge. Is there forbearance that is not knowledge? Is there knowledge that is not view? Verse: among noble wisdom, forbearance is not of the nature of knowledge, and the knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising are not of the nature of view; the remaining two groups are entirely of the nature of knowledge, and six are of the nature of view. Explanation: wisdom is of two kinds, contaminated and uncontaminated; only uncontaminated wisdom is given the name "noble." Among this noble wisdom, the eight forbearances are not of the nature of knowledge, since the doubt regarding what they themselves are to sever has not yet been fully severed at that point; they are, however, included within the nature of view, since they are of an investigative nature. The two knowledges, exhaustion and non-arising, are not of the nature of view, since the mind's seeking has already ceased, and they no longer investigate. The remaining knowledges are all common to both the nature of knowledge and the nature of view, since they have already severed their own doubt and remain of an investigative nature. All contaminated wisdom is included within the nature of knowledge; among these, only six are also of the nature of view — namely, the five defiled views, together with mundane right view, making six. Thus both the noble wisdom and the contaminated wisdom just discussed, since both consist in the discernment of dharmas, are alike included within the nature of wisdom.

Having placed forbearance, knowledge, and view in relation to one another, the treatise turns to knowledge's own internal classification — the tenfold scheme that will organize everything Chapter 7 has to say.

Chinese Source

智有幾種？相別云何？頌曰： 智十總有二， 有漏無漏別， 有漏稱世俗， 無漏名法類。 世俗遍為境， 法智及類智， 如次欲上界， 苦等諦為境。 論曰：智有十種攝一切智：一世俗智、二法智、三類智、四苦智、五集智、六滅智、七道智、八他心智、九盡智、十無生智。如是十智總唯二種，有漏、無漏性差別故。如是二智相別有三，謂世俗智、法智、類智。前有漏智總名世俗，多取瓶等世俗境故。後無漏智分法、類別。三中世俗遍，以一切有為無為為所緣境。法類二種如其次第，以欲上界四諦為境。

Ru-Yi Translation

How many kinds of knowledge are there, and what distinguishes their individual character? Verse: knowledge, ten in total, reduces to two by nature — contaminated and uncontaminated differ; the contaminated is called worldly; the uncontaminated is named dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge. Worldly knowledge takes everything universally as its object; dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge, respectively, take the desire realm's and the upper realms' four truths as their object. Explanation: knowledge is of ten kinds, encompassing every kind of knowledge: worldly knowledge, dharma knowledge, subsequent knowledge, knowledge of suffering, knowledge of origination, knowledge of cessation, knowledge of the path, knowledge of others' minds, knowledge of exhaustion, and knowledge of non-arising. These ten kinds, taken altogether, reduce to only two, since they differ in nature between contaminated and uncontaminated. These two, further distinguished in character, come to three: worldly knowledge, dharma knowledge, and subsequent knowledge. The former, contaminated knowledge, is entirely named worldly, since it mostly takes worldly objects such as pots and the like. The latter, uncontaminated knowledge, divides into the distinct categories of dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge. Among these three, worldly knowledge is universal, since it takes every conditioned and unconditioned thing as its object. Dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge, respectively, take the four truths of the desire realm and of the upper realms as their object.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the forbearance/knowledge/view analysis as a tidy piece of housekeeping that resolves a genuine ambiguity built up across the previous chapter's constant use of all three terms: forbearance turns out to be a kind of view but not yet knowledge (it still carries unresolved doubt about the very affliction it is severing, in the instant before confirmation), while the two capstone knowledges of arhatship, exhaustion and non-arising, are knowledge but not view (having ceased all further investigation, there is nothing left to "survey"). Everything else in the noble path's eight remaining knowledges counts as both. We have kept this three-way distinction exact, since the entire point of the passage is that these three terms — easy to treat as loosely interchangeable — pick out genuinely different, only partially overlapping categories.

We should also note that "dharma-knowledge" and "subsequent-knowledge" (法智／類智) here reintroduce, in ordinary prose, the same two knowledges already used as the standard building blocks for the sixteen-moment path of seeing described in fascicle 23 — this passage is best read as the treatise stepping back to formally define, chapter by chapter, categories it has already been relying on informally throughout the preceding material.

2. Dharma- and Subsequent-Knowledge Split Four Ways, and the First Arising of Exhaustion and Non-Arising

Having established dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge as the two uncontaminated kinds of knowledge, the treatise now shows how each further divides according to which of the four truths it takes as its object — and how this connects back to the two capstone knowledges, exhaustion and non-arising, that complete arhatship.

Chinese Source

即於如是二種智中，頌曰： 法類由境別， 立苦等四名， 皆通盡無生， 初唯苦集類。 論曰：法智、類智由境差別分為苦、集、滅、道四智。如是六智若無學攝，非見性者名盡、無生。此二初生唯苦、集類，以緣苦、集六種行相觀有頂蘊為境界故。金剛喻定境同此耶？緣苦、集因，緣滅、道異。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within these two kinds of knowledge, verse: dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge, according to differences in their object, are given the four names beginning with suffering; each is common to being named exhaustion or non-arising; but their first arising is exclusively of the suffering- and origination-subsequent-knowledge type. Explanation: dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge, according to differences in their object, divide into the four knowledges of suffering, origination, cessation, and path. Among these six knowledges, whenever they are included within a non-trainee's possession and are not of the nature of view, they are named exhaustion and non-arising instead. When these two — exhaustion-knowledge and non-arising-knowledge — first arise, they are exclusively of the suffering- and origination-subsequent-knowledge type, since they take as their object the peak-of-existence aggregates as contemplated through the six aspects of suffering and origination. Does the diamond-like concentration share this same object? With respect to suffering and origination, the cause is the same; with respect to cessation and path, it differs.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag how this passage quietly connects two things this translation has treated separately until now: the four truth-specific knowledges (suffering, origination, cessation, path) are not a new addition alongside dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge, but simply those same two knowledges sorted by which truth they take as object — and "exhaustion" and "non-arising," the two knowledges that mark the completion of arhatship (introduced in fascicle 24 alongside the diamond-like concentration), turn out to be the very same six knowledges again, renamed once they occur specifically within a non-trainee's mind and no longer carry the investigative character that would qualify them as "view." The fuller explanation of exactly what makes a knowledge "exhaustion" or "non-arising" specifically — the self-referential "I have already known suffering" judgment — is signaled here but developed later in this same chapter.

We should also flag the closing exchange about the diamond-like concentration's object as genuinely compressed, cross-referencing technical material on that concentration's structure developed at greater length in fascicle 24 rather than restating it here; we have translated the question and answer literally rather than reconstructing the fuller argument the passage assumes but does not repeat.

3. Knowledge of Others' Minds: What It Cannot Know, and Who Can Track a Stranger's Path of Seeing

The treatise turns next to knowledge of others' minds (他心智) — which of the nine other kinds of knowledge can actually constitute it, and, more strikingly, exactly what limits govern it: a mind reader in this scheme cannot know just anything, and the ability to track a complete stranger through the sixteen swift moments of the path of seeing turns out to depend sharply on who is doing the watching.

Chinese Source

於前所說九種智中，頌曰： 法類道世俗， 有成他心智， 於勝地根位， 去來世不知， 法類不相知， 聲聞麟喻佛， 如次知見道， 二三念一切。 論曰：有法類道及世俗智成他心智，餘則不然。此智於境有決定相，謂不知勝及去來心。勝心有三，謂地、根、位。地謂下地智不知上地心。根謂信解時解脫根智不知見至不時解脫心。位謂不還聲聞應果、獨覺大覺。前前位智不知後後勝位者心，此智不知去來心者唯以現在他相續中能緣心等為境界故。又法類品不互相知，謂法智攝諸他心智不知類品，類智所攝諸他心智不知法品，由法類智以欲上界全分對治為所緣故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the nine other kinds of knowledge already discussed, verse: dharma-knowledge, subsequent-knowledge, path-knowledge, and worldly knowledge can constitute knowledge of others' minds; but minds superior in ground, faculty, or stage, and minds in the past or future, are not known by it. The dharma-type and subsequent-type do not know each other. Śrāvakas, the rhinoceros-horn-like pratyekabuddha, and the Buddha, respectively, know the path of seeing to the extent of two moments, three moments, and all of it. Explanation: dharma-knowledge, subsequent-knowledge, path-knowledge, and worldly knowledge can constitute knowledge of others' minds; the rest cannot. This knowledge has a fixed, determinate character with respect to its object — namely, it does not know minds that are superior to its own, nor minds in the past or future. "Superior mind" is of three kinds: by ground, by faculty, and by stage. By ground: knowledge belonging to a lower ground does not know the mind of a higher ground. By faculty: knowledge belonging to the convinced-through-faith or timely-liberated type does not know the mind of the attained-through-seeing or non-timely-liberated type. By stage: the ranking runs non-returner, arhat, pratyekabuddha, and the greatly-awakened Buddha — the knowledge of each earlier stage does not know the mind of one at a later, superior stage. This knowledge does not know past or future minds, since it takes as its object only the currently-arising, objectively-cognizing mind and mental factors within another's present mental continuum. Moreover, the dharma-type and subsequent-type do not know each other: the knowledge-of-others'-minds included within dharma-knowledge does not know a subsequent-type mind, and that included within subsequent-knowledge does not know a dharma-type mind, since dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge each take the complete counteragent for the desire realm or the upper realms, respectively, as their own restricted object.

The treatise then works out a striking practical case: since the path of seeing itself proceeds too swiftly for knowledge of others' minds to occur during it, how does a śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha manage to observe a stranger actually passing through it?

Chinese Source

此他心智見道中無，總觀諦理極速轉故，然皆容作此智所緣。若諸有情將入見道，聲聞獨覺預修加行，為欲知彼見道位心。彼諸有情入見道位，聲聞法分加行若滿，知彼見道初二念心。若為更知類分心故，別修加行至加行滿，彼已度至第十六心，雖知此心非知見道。麟喻法分加行若滿，知彼見道初二念心。若為更知類分心故，別修加行至加行滿，知彼第八集類智心。以此但由下加行故。有說：知初二及第十五心，世尊欲知不由加行，於彼見道一切能知。

Ru-Yi Translation

This knowledge of others' minds does not occur during the path of seeing itself, since the path of seeing contemplates the principle of the truths as a whole, proceeding extremely swiftly; nevertheless, minds at the path of seeing can all serve as the object of this knowledge. When sentient beings are about to enter the path of seeing, śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas who wish to know their minds cultivate preparatory effort in advance. Once those beings enter the stage of the path of seeing: if a śrāvaka's preparatory effort for the dharma-portion is complete, they know the first two mind-moments of that person's path of seeing. If, wishing to further know the subsequent-portion mind as well, the śrāvaka separately cultivates preparatory effort until complete, by the time it is complete that person has already progressed all the way to the sixteenth mind-moment — and although the śrāvaka then knows this mind, this is no longer knowing a mind at the path of seeing, since the sixteenth moment already belongs to the path of cultivation. For a rhinoceros-horn-like pratyekabuddha: if their preparatory effort for the dharma-portion is complete, they likewise know the first two mind-moments. If, wishing to further know the subsequent-portion mind, they separately cultivate preparatory effort until complete, they come to know that person's eighth mind-moment, the subsequent-knowledge-of-origination moment — since even their separately cultivated effort remains only of a lesser grade. Some say instead that a pratyekabuddha knows the first two moments and the fifteenth. The Blessed One, wishing to know, needs no preparatory effort at all — with respect to that person's path of seeing, he is able to know every single moment directly.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the three-part restriction on knowledge of others' minds (never superior in ground, faculty, or stage; never past or future; never crossing between the dharma- and subsequent-knowledge tracks) as establishing this ability as something considerably more constrained than an unlimited telepathic power — it is itself bound by the same hierarchy of attainment that governs everything else in this treatise, so that only a mind at or below one's own level, in one's own temporal present, is ever accessible.

We should also flag the graduated capability among śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, and Buddha as a vivid illustration of just how fast the sixteen-moment path of seeing (introduced in fascicle 23) is held to move: a śrāvaka's best preparatory effort only ever catches the first two moments directly, and by the time a second round of effort is complete for the upper-realm truths, the subject under observation has already finished the entire sixteen-moment sequence and moved on. Only the Buddha, requiring no preparatory effort at all, tracks every single moment as it happens. We have preserved the "some say" variant regarding the pratyekabuddha's exact reach (eighth moment versus first-two-plus-fifteenth) as the open alternative the text itself records, rather than resolving it ourselves.

4. Exhaustion Versus Non-Arising: The Exact Self-Referential Judgment, and How the Ten Knowledges Overlap

The treatise now pins down precisely what distinguishes the knowledge of exhaustion from the knowledge of non-arising — a distinction that turns out to rest on a specific first-person judgment an arhat makes about their own attainment.

Chinese Source

盡、無生智二相何別？頌曰： 智於四聖諦， 知我已知等， 不應更知等， 如次盡無生。 論曰：如本論說：云何盡智？謂無學位若正自知：我已知苦、我已斷集、我已證滅、我已修道，由此所有智、見、明、覺、解、慧、光、觀，是名盡智。云何無生智？謂正自知：我已知苦，不應更知，廣說乃至我已修道，不應更修，由此所有廣說乃至是名無生智。如何無漏智可作如是知？迦濕彌羅諸論師說：從二智出後得智中作如是知，故無有失。由此後得二智別故，表前觀中二智差別。有說：無漏智亦作如是知，然說見言乘言便故，或於諦理現照轉故。由此本論亦作是言。且諸智亦是見。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the difference in character between the knowledge of exhaustion and the knowledge of non-arising? Verse: with respect to the four noble truths, the knowledge that knows "I have already known suffering" and so on is, respectively, exhaustion; the knowledge that further knows "there is no need to know it again" and so on is non-arising. Explanation: as the root treatise states, what is the knowledge of exhaustion? At the non-trainee stage, if one directly knows for oneself: I have already known suffering, I have already severed the origin, I have already realized cessation, I have already cultivated the path — whatever knowledge, seeing, clarity, awakening, understanding, wisdom, illumination, and insight accompanies this is called the knowledge of exhaustion. What is the knowledge of non-arising? It is directly knowing for oneself: I have already known suffering, and need not know it again — stated fully, up through I have already cultivated the path, and need not cultivate it again — whatever accompanies this, stated fully as before, is called the knowledge of non-arising. How can uncontaminated knowledge make such a first-person judgment? The Kashmiri masters say: it is within the subsequently-attained knowledge arising after emerging from these two knowledges that such a judgment is made, hence there is no fault in the description. Because this subsequently-attained pair of knowledges differ from each other, this reveals the distinction between the two knowledges within the preceding direct contemplation itself. Others say: uncontaminated knowledge itself does indeed make such a judgment directly, and the description in terms of subsequently-attained knowledge is merely a convenient manner of speaking, or reflects how the uncontaminated knowledge operates by directly illuminating the principle of the truths in the present moment. It is on this basis that the root treatise makes this statement. These knowledges, moreover, are likewise instances of view.

Having distinguished all ten kinds of knowledge individually, the treatise closes this stretch of the discussion with a compressed accounting of how much they overlap and include one another.

Chinese Source

如是十智相攝云何？謂世俗智攝一全一少分，法類智各攝一全七少分，苦集滅智各攝一全四少分，道智攝一全五少分，他心智攝一全四少分，盡無生智各攝一全六少分。

Ru-Yi Translation

How do these ten knowledges include one another? Worldly knowledge includes one kind entirely and a portion of one other. Dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge each include one entirely and portions of seven others. The knowledges of suffering, origination, and cessation each include one entirely and portions of four others. Path-knowledge includes one entirely and portions of five others. Knowledge of others' minds includes one entirely and portions of four others. The knowledges of exhaustion and non-arising each include one entirely and portions of six others.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the exhaustion/non-arising distinction as turning on a subtle two-step structure: exhaustion is the confirming judgment ("I have already known suffering..."), while non-arising is a further judgment layered on top of it ("...and there is no need to know it again"). The treatise then honestly flags its own philosophical puzzle — how can uncontaminated knowledge, ordinarily understood as directly apprehending the truths themselves, also make a first-person, self-referential judgment about its own activity? — and records two unresolved positions: the Kashmiri masters locate this reflective judgment in a separate "subsequently-attained" knowledge following the direct realization, while others hold the direct, uncontaminated knowledge makes the judgment itself, treating the subsequently-attained framing as loose, convenient language rather than a strict metaphysical claim. We have preserved both readings as the passage presents them, without adjudicating between them.

We should also flag the closing subsumption count honestly: the passage states how many other knowledges each of the ten "includes" (wholly or partially) without spelling out the underlying cross-tabulation that would let a reader verify each figure independently, much as the seven-noble-ones passage in fascicle 25 did with its large combinatorial total. We have translated the stated figures exactly as given rather than reconstructing the full overlap logic ourselves.

5. Seven Grounds for the Tenfold Division, and an Asymmetry Between Dharma- and Subsequent-Knowledge

Having worked through all ten kinds of knowledge individually, the treatise steps back to justify the classification scheme itself: why exactly ten, rather than some other number, given that everything ultimately reduces to just two kinds by nature?

Chinese Source

何緣二智建立為十？頌曰： 由自性對治， 行相行相境， 加行辦因圓， 故建立十智。 論曰：由七緣故立二為十：一自性故立世俗智，非勝義智為自性故。二對治故立法類智，全能對治欲上界故。三行相故立苦集智，此二智境體無別故。四行相境故立滅道智，此二行相境俱有別故。五加行故立他心智，非此不知他心所法，本修加行為知他心，雖成滿時亦知心所，而約加行故立他心智名。六事辦故建立盡智，事辦身中最初生故。七因圓故立無生智，一切聖道為因生故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why are the two original kinds of knowledge established as ten? Verse: by intrinsic nature, by counteragent, by aspect, by aspect-and-object, by preparatory effort, by task-completion, and by cause-fulfillment — on these grounds, the ten kinds of knowledge are established. Explanation: on seven grounds, the two are established as ten. First, by intrinsic nature, worldly knowledge is established, since its intrinsic nature is not that of ultimate knowledge. Second, by counteragent, dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge are established, since each is able to fully counteract affliction in the desire realm or the upper realms, respectively. Third, by aspect, the knowledges of suffering and origination are established, since these two knowledges' objects are not distinct in substance from each other, only in the aspect under which each contemplates that same object. Fourth, by both aspect and object, the knowledges of cessation and path are established, since, for these two, both aspect and object are distinct from each other. Fifth, by preparatory effort, knowledge of others' minds is established — not that it fails to know others' mental factors as well as their minds, but because the original preparatory effort was undertaken specifically in order to know others' minds; although, once this knowledge is fully accomplished, it also comes to know mental factors, it is named knowledge of others' minds with reference to that original preparatory aim. Sixth, by task-completion, the knowledge of exhaustion is established, since it is the very first knowledge to arise within one whose task has been accomplished. Seventh, by cause-fulfillment, the knowledge of non-arising is established, since it arises only once every noble path serving as its cause has been fully completed.

One asymmetry remains to be addressed: dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge were said to fully counteract the desire realm and the upper realms, respectively, each confined to its own domain. Does this division hold without exception in both directions?

Chinese Source

如上所言法智類智，全能對治欲上界法，為有少分治上欲耶？頌曰： 緣滅道法智， 於修道位中， 兼治上修斷， 類無能治欲。 論曰：修道所攝滅道法智，兼能對治上界修斷，欲之滅道勝上界故，已除自怨能兼他故。由此類智無能治欲。

Ru-Yi Translation

As stated above, dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge each fully counteract the desire realm and the upper realms, respectively — but does either extend even a small portion into the other's domain? Verse: dharma-knowledge taking cessation or the path as its object, at the stage of the path of cultivation, additionally counteracts what is severed by cultivation in the upper realms; subsequent-knowledge, however, has no ability to counteract the desire realm. Explanation: the cessation- and path-type dharma-knowledge included within the path of cultivation is additionally able to counteract what is severed by cultivation in the upper realms, since the desire realm's cessation and path surpass those of the upper realms — having already eliminated the nearer enemy, it is able to additionally handle the farther one as well. For this reason, subsequent-knowledge has no corresponding ability to reach back down and counteract the desire realm.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the seven-ground justification as a genuinely tidy piece of classificatory reasoning: rather than treating "ten kinds of knowledge" as an arbitrary traditional list, the treatise shows each addition to the basic contaminated/uncontaminated split answers a specific organizing question — what is it by nature, what does it counteract, under what aspect, toward what object, through what preparatory aim, marking what completion, fulfilled by what cause. The seven grounds are not all of the same kind (some carve by object, some by function, some by the practitioner's own biographical milestone), and the treatise does not flag this heterogeneity as a problem — the list is offered simply as jointly sufficient to generate all ten names from the underlying two natures.

We should also flag the closing asymmetry as resting on a specific, compressed technical claim — that "the desire realm's cessation and path surpass those of the upper realms" — which the passage treats as already established rather than argued for here; we have translated the stated reasoning (nearer enemy eliminated first, enabling extension to the farther one) exactly as given, without reconstructing the fuller doctrinal basis for why cessation and path specifically do not split as sharply by realm as suffering and origination do.

6. Which Knowledge Has Which Aspects

The treatise now maps each of the ten knowledges onto the "aspects" (行相) under which it contemplates its object — the specific lens, such as "impermanent" or "suffering," through which a given knowledge apprehends what it takes as its object — promising a full explanation of the sixteen classical aspects later in this same chapter.

Chinese Source

於此十智中誰有何行相？頌曰： 法智及類智， 行相俱十六， 世俗此及餘， 四諦智各四。 他心智無漏， 唯四謂緣道， 有漏自相緣， 俱但緣一事。 盡無生十四， 謂離空非我。 論曰：法智、類智一一具有非常、苦等十六行相，十六行相後當廣釋。世智有此及更有餘，能緣一切法自共相等故。苦等四智一一各有緣自諦境四種行相。他心智中，若無漏者唯有緣道四種行相，由此即是道智攝故；若有漏者取自所緣心心所法自相境故。如境自相行相亦爾，故此非前十六所攝。如是二種於一切時一念但緣一事為境，謂緣心時不緣心所，緣受等時不緣想等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these ten kinds of knowledge, which possesses which aspects? Verse: dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge each possess all sixteen aspects. Worldly knowledge has these as well as others. The four truth-specific knowledges each have their own truth's four aspects. Uncontaminated knowledge of others' minds has only the four aspects pertaining to the path; contaminated knowledge of others' minds takes its object's own particular characteristic directly — both kinds, in every case, take only a single thing as their object. Exhaustion and non-arising each have fourteen, excluding "empty" and "non-self." Explanation: dharma-knowledge and subsequent-knowledge, each individually, possess all sixteen aspects — impermanent, suffering, and so on; the sixteen aspects will be explained fully later. Worldly knowledge has these as well as still others, since it is able to take every dharma's particular and shared characteristics, and more, as its object. The four knowledges of suffering and the rest each individually have the four aspects belonging to contemplating their own respective truth. Among knowledge of others' minds: if uncontaminated, it has only the four aspects pertaining to taking the path as object, since this portion is precisely included within path-knowledge; if contaminated, it takes as its object the particular characteristic of the mind and mental factors that are its object directly, and its aspect is correspondingly particular rather than general — hence this is not included among the sixteen aspects just described. Both of these two kinds, at every moment, in a single mind-moment, take only a single thing as their object: when taking mind as object, they do not simultaneously take mental factors as object, and when taking feeling and the like as object, they do not simultaneously take perception and the like as object.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag that this passage explicitly defers its own key term — the "sixteen aspects" it repeatedly invokes (impermanent, suffering, and so on, four per truth) are named but not yet defined, with the treatise promising a full explanation later in this chapter. We have translated the passage exactly as it stands, naming the aspects only as far as the source text itself does at this point, rather than importing the fuller sixteen-aspect scheme from elsewhere in the tradition ahead of where this treatise chooses to introduce it.

We should also flag the fine distinction drawn for knowledge of others' minds: its uncontaminated portion is absorbed entirely into path-knowledge and so shares path-knowledge's four aspects, while its contaminated portion instead apprehends another's mind or mental factors under their own particular characteristic rather than any of the sixteen general aspects — and, either way, it can only ever hold one single object (a mind, or a mental factor, never both at once) in a given moment, a constraint the treatise states as a bare fact about how this knowledge operates without further explaining why simultaneity is impossible here specifically.
