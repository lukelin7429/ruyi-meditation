# Abhidharmakośa Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Abhidharmakośa* (阿毘達磨俱舍論, Taishō T1558, no. 1558, vol. 29). They exist so that terminology stays consistent across all thirty fascicles.

## Source and independence from prior translations

- Base text: Taishō T1558, via the CBETA digital edition (public domain). We translate from this Chinese text directly.
- The only complete modern English translation is Leo M. Pruden's rendering of Louis de La Vallée Poussin's French *Abhidharmakośabhāṣyam* (Asian Humanities Press, 1988–91), still under copyright. We may consult it privately to check difficult passages, but the English wording, sentence structure, and section framing on this site must be our own. Do not paraphrase it sentence-by-sentence.
- Chinese commentaries such as Yen-pei's (演培) *俱舍論頌講記* are likewise consulted for context only, not quoted at length.
- Unlike the Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra and the Cheng Weishi Lun on this site, this treatise does not (yet) follow a single named teacher's lecture transcript. Reading notes are Ru-Yi's own, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma scholarship, and are labeled "Reading note — Ru-Yi" rather than attributed to an external commentator. If a suitable source becomes available later, note this explicitly when adopting it, rather than silently changing the attribution convention partway through.

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 阿毘達磨 | Abhidharma | Left untranslated as the proper term; glossed on first use as "facing the dharma." |
| 對法 | Abhidharma | The two-character Chinese rendering of the same term; translate as "Abhidharma," not literally as "facing dharma," except when the text is explicitly explaining the etymology. |
| 法 | dharma(s) | Keep as "dharma," not "phenomenon" or "thing," except in loose English framing sentences. Defined by the treatise itself as "that which holds its own particular mark" (能持自相). |
| 有漏 | contaminated | āsrava-present; opposite of 無漏. |
| 無漏 | uncontaminated | anāsrava; the pure, liberating kind of wisdom, dharma, or aggregate. |
| 蘊 | aggregate(s) | skandha. |
| 界 | element(s) | dhātu — the eighteen elements analyzed in Chapter 1. |
| 處 | sense field(s) | āyatana — the twelve sense fields. |
| 隨眠 | latent affliction(s) | anuśaya — Chapter 5's subject. |
| 資糧 | provisions | saṃbhāra; the preparatory requisites for the path. |
| 擇法 | discernment of dharmas | the technical definition of 慧 (wisdom/prajñā) given in fascicle 1. |
| 世親 | Vasubandhu | author. |
| 玄奘 | Xuánzàng | translator into Chinese, 651 CE. |
| 地 | realm(s) | bhūmi, in the cosmological sense (desire realm, four dhyānas, four formless attainments — nine levels total), not "ground" or "stage." |
| 靜慮 | dhyāna | left untranslated as the proper term (initial/first dhyāna, second dhyāna, etc.), not "meditative absorption" except in loose framing. |
| 等至 | meditative attainment(s) | samāpatti; distinct from 靜慮/dhyāna — used when the text is discussing actively entering an attainment rather than the realm one is born into. |
| 依 / 所依 | support | āśraya — what a consciousness or faculty leans on to arise. |
| 等無間緣 | immediately-antecedent condition | samanantara-pratyaya, one of the four classical conditions (pratyaya). |
| 得 | possession | prāpti — a formation dissociated from mind; central to fascicle 4's major debate over whether it is a real, distinct dharma. |
| 非得 | non-possession | the reverse of 得; always undefiled-neutral in nature. |
| 種子 | seed | bīja — in fascicle 4's rival "transformed basis" theory, defined via 相續轉變差別 (continuum, transformation, distinction). |
| 異生性 | ordinary-being-nature | pṛthagjanatva — the non-possession of the noble path; debated at length in fascicle 4. |
| 同分 / 眾同分 | class-commonality | nikāya-sabhāga — a formation dissociated from mind explaining shared "kind-ness" among members of the same class; introduced in fascicle 5. |
| 學 / 無學 | trainee / non-trainee | śaikṣa / aśaikṣa — one still progressing on the noble path vs. one who has completed it (arhatship); "path of no-more-learning" for 無學道. |
| 見道 / 修道 | path of seeing / path of cultivation | darśana-mārga / bhāvanā-mārga. |
| 擇滅 / 非擇滅 | cessation through discernment / cessation not through discernment | pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha / apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha — two of the three unconditioned dharmas. |
| 六因 | the six causes | ṣaḍ-hetavaḥ — introduced in fascicle 6: 能作因 "efficient cause" (kāraṇa-hetu), 俱有因 "co-existent cause" (sahabhū-hetu), 同類因 "homogeneous cause" (sabhāga-hetu), 相應因 "associated cause" (saṃprayuktaka-hetu), 遍行因 "universal cause" (sarvatraga-hetu), 異熟因 "ripening cause" (vipāka-hetu). |
| 士用果 | agent-efficacy result | puruṣakāra-phala — the kind of result two dharmas produce when they are mutually a co-existent cause (俱有因) of one another. |
| 心隨轉法 | dharmas that follow mind's course | dharmas co-arising with mind in lockstep — mental factors, the two moral restraints, and mind's own characteristics; defined in fascicle 6 by matching time, result, and moral quality with mind. |
| 四緣 | the four conditions | catuḥ-pratyaya — introduced in fascicle 7: 因緣 "causal condition" (hetu-pratyaya, = five of the six causes), 等無間緣 "immediately-antecedent condition" (samanantara-pratyaya, mind/mental-factors only), 所緣緣 "objective condition" (ālambana-pratyaya), 增上緣 "dominant condition" (adhipati-pratyaya, = the efficient cause). |
| 生因/依因/立因/持因/養因 | generative / support / establishing / sustaining / nurturing cause | introduced in fascicle 7 — five named subdivisions of the efficient cause (能作因), specifically describing how the four great elements (大種) cause derived form (所造色) to arise, be supported, be established, continue uninterrupted, and grow. |
| 十二心 | the twelve minds | introduced in fascicle 7: the desire, form, and formless realms' wholesome/unwholesome/covered-neutral/uncovered-neutral minds (form and formless lack "unwholesome"), plus the two uncontaminated minds, 學 "trainee's" and 無學 "non-trainee's". Used as the basis for an extended combinatorial analysis of the immediately-antecedent condition (等無間緣). |
| 化心 | transformation-mind | nirmāṇa-citta — a sub-variety of uncovered-neutral mind (無覆無記) exercised specifically in producing supernormally-created forms; introduced in fascicle 7, formally named as part of the twenty-mind subdivision. |
| 加行得 / 生得 | effortfully-acquired / innately-acquired | prāyogika / upapatti-labha — the two varieties every realm's wholesome mind splits into in fascicle 7's twenty-mind subdivision; matches "acquired through effortful practice" / "innately acquired" already used for the same distinction in fascicle 4. |
| 異熟生 / 威儀路 / 工巧處 | karmic-ripening / bodily deportment / skilled crafts | vipāka-ja / īryāpatha / śilpasthāna — the sub-varieties the desire realm's (and, minus skilled crafts, the form realm's) uncovered-neutral mind splits into in fascicle 7; matches usage already established in fascicle 3. |
| 自相 / 共相 | particular characteristic / shared characteristic | svalakṣaṇa / sāmānya-lakṣaṇa — established in fascicle 1; reused in fascicle 7 for the first two of the three kinds of mental application (作意). |
| 作意 | mental application | manasikāra — in fascicle 7, classified three ways (attending to a particular characteristic, to a shared characteristic, or through resolute understanding/勝解) and also, separately, by source (accomplished through hearing/聞所成, through reflection/思所成, through cultivation/修所成, or innately-acquired/生得), paralleling but distinct from the classical three-wisdoms triad (聞思修所成慧) already introduced in fascicle 6. |
| 三界 processes (fascicle 8 cosmology) | six desire-heavens: Heaven of the Four Great Kings / Heaven of the Thirty-Three / Yama Heaven / Tuṣita Heaven / Heaven of Enjoying Transformations / Heaven of Mastery over Others' Transformations. Eight great hells: Reviving / Black-Rope / Crushing-Together / Wailing / Great Wailing / Scorching / Great Scorching / Uninterrupted. Four continents: Jambudvīpa / Pūrvavideha / Aparagodānīya / Uttarakuru. | Introduced in fascicle 8's opening cosmology; keep these exact English names for every later reference to the desire realm's twenty places. |
| 色界十七處 | the form realm's seventeen heavens | Introduced in fascicle 8: first dhyāna — Heaven of Brahmā's Retinue / Heaven of Brahmā's Ministers / Great Brahmā Heaven; second dhyāna — Heaven of Lesser Light / Heaven of Immeasurable Light / Heaven of Light-Sound; third dhyāna — Heaven of Lesser Purity / Heaven of Immeasurable Purity / Heaven of All-Pervasive Purity; fourth dhyāna (eight) — Cloudless / Merit-Born / Great Reward / Without Affliction / Without Heat / Skillful Appearance / Skillful Vision / Ultimate Form (Akaniṣṭha). The Kashmiri masters count only sixteen, treating the Great Brahmā Heaven as a tower within the Heaven of Brahmā's Ministers rather than a separate place. |
| 四無色 | the four formless spheres | ākāśānantyāyatana / vijñānānantyāyatana / ākiṃcanyāyatana / naivasaṃjñānāsaṃjñāyatana — "sphere of infinite space," "sphere of infinite consciousness," "sphere of nothing-at-all," "sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception"; introduced in fascicle 8. Distinguished only by kind of birth, not by any spatial location — the formless realm has no place at all. |
| 邪命外道 | an Ājīvika heretic | Ājīvika — a real, historically attested rival ascetic movement contemporary with early Buddhism; introduced in fascicle 8 as Śāriputra's interlocutor in the debate over what "desire" refers to. |
| 四生 | the four kinds of birth | egg-birth (卵生) / womb-birth (胎生) / moisture-birth (濕生) / transformation-birth (化生); introduced in fascicle 8. Humans and animals uniquely span all four; hells, gods, and the intermediate-existence being are exclusively transformation-born; ghosts span only womb- and transformation-birth. |
| 揭路荼 / 那落迦 (as used for hell-beings) | garuḍa / hell-being (nāraka) | garuḍa — a mythical bird-being; introduced in fascicle 8 in the discussion of transformation-birth. 那落迦/nāraka for "hell-being" already used consistently since earlier fascicles' hell discussions. |
| 目連 | Maudgalyāyana | one of the Buddha's two chief disciples (with Śāriputra); introduced in fascicle 8 as the addressee of the hungry ghost's lament. |
| 中有 | intermediate existence | antarābhava — the five-aggregate being arising between the existence of death and the existence of birth; introduced and extensively defended in fascicle 8's closing debate. Also called 健達縛/gandharva in the specific context of entry into a mother's womb. |
| 五不還 | the five non-returners | introduced in fascicle 8: 中般 "attains final nirvana in the interval," 生般 "attains it upon birth," 無行般 "without exertion," 有行般 "through exertion," 上流 "streams upward." |

Keep this table growing as new recurring terms appear in later fascicles — add a row rather than improvising a fresh translation of a term that already has an entry.

## Structure

Follow the nine traditional chapters (品) and the fascicle divisions given by the mnemonic verse 界二根五世間五，業六隨三賢聖四，智二定二破我一 — see the [Abhidharmakośa overview page]({{ '/abhidharma/abhidharmakosa/' | relative_url }}) for the full chapter-to-fascicle map. Within a fascicle, structure sections around the root verses (頌) and their prose commentary (長行), matching how the Chinese text itself alternates between the two.

## Register

- Plain English, close to the Chinese, not a loose paraphrase — matching the method already used for the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra and the Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra on this site.
- Where the Chinese compresses a technical argument into very few characters, it is fine for the English to expand slightly for clarity, but do not add explanation that belongs in a reading note instead of the translation itself.
- Mark every fascicle's status honestly (e.g. "opening section only") rather than implying more is finished than actually is.
