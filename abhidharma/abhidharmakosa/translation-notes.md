# Abhidharmakośa Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Abhidharmakośa* (阿毘達磨俱舍論, Taishō T1558, no. 1558, vol. 29). They exist so that terminology stays consistent across all thirty fascicles.

## Source and independence from prior translations

- Base text: Taishō T1558, via the CBETA digital edition (public domain). We translate from this Chinese text directly.
- The only complete modern English translation is Leo M. Pruden's rendering of Louis de La Vallée Poussin's French *Abhidharmakośabhāṣyam* (Asian Humanities Press, 1988–91), still under copyright. We may consult it privately to check difficult passages, but the English wording, sentence structure, and section framing on this site must be our own. Do not paraphrase it sentence-by-sentence.
- Chinese commentaries such as Yen-pei's (演培) *俱舍論頌講記* are likewise consulted for context only, not quoted at length.
- Unlike the Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra and the Cheng Weishi Lun on this site, this treatise does not (yet) follow a single named teacher's lecture transcript. Reading notes are Ru-Yi's own, grounded in standard Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma scholarship, and are labeled "Reading note — Ru-Yi" rather than attributed to an external commentator. If a suitable source becomes available later, note this explicitly when adopting it, rather than silently changing the attribution convention partway through.

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 阿毘達磨 | Abhidharma | Left untranslated as the proper term; glossed on first use as "facing the dharma." |
| 對法 | Abhidharma | The two-character Chinese rendering of the same term; translate as "Abhidharma," not literally as "facing dharma," except when the text is explicitly explaining the etymology. |
| 法 | dharma(s) | Keep as "dharma," not "phenomenon" or "thing," except in loose English framing sentences. Defined by the treatise itself as "that which holds its own particular mark" (能持自相). |
| 有漏 | contaminated | āsrava-present; opposite of 無漏. |
| 無漏 | uncontaminated | anāsrava; the pure, liberating kind of wisdom, dharma, or aggregate. |
| 蘊 | aggregate(s) | skandha. |
| 界 | element(s) | dhātu — the eighteen elements analyzed in Chapter 1. |
| 處 | sense field(s) | āyatana — the twelve sense fields. |
| 隨眠 | latent affliction(s) | anuśaya — Chapter 5's subject. |
| 資糧 | provisions | saṃbhāra; the preparatory requisites for the path. |
| 擇法 | discernment of dharmas | the technical definition of 慧 (wisdom/prajñā) given in fascicle 1. |
| 世親 | Vasubandhu | author. |
| 玄奘 | Xuánzàng | translator into Chinese, 651 CE. |
| 地 | realm(s) | bhūmi, in the cosmological sense (desire realm, four dhyānas, four formless attainments — nine levels total), not "ground" or "stage." |
| 靜慮 | dhyāna | left untranslated as the proper term (initial/first dhyāna, second dhyāna, etc.), not "meditative absorption" except in loose framing. |
| 等至 | meditative attainment(s) | samāpatti; distinct from 靜慮/dhyāna — used when the text is discussing actively entering an attainment rather than the realm one is born into. |
| 依 / 所依 | support | āśraya — what a consciousness or faculty leans on to arise. |
| 等無間緣 | immediately-antecedent condition | samanantara-pratyaya, one of the four classical conditions (pratyaya). |
| 得 | possession | prāpti — a formation dissociated from mind; central to fascicle 4's major debate over whether it is a real, distinct dharma. |
| 非得 | non-possession | the reverse of 得; always undefiled-neutral in nature. |
| 種子 | seed | bīja — in fascicle 4's rival "transformed basis" theory, defined via 相續轉變差別 (continuum, transformation, distinction). |
| 異生性 | ordinary-being-nature | pṛthagjanatva — the non-possession of the noble path; debated at length in fascicle 4. |
| 同分 / 眾同分 | class-commonality | nikāya-sabhāga — a formation dissociated from mind explaining shared "kind-ness" among members of the same class; introduced in fascicle 5. |
| 學 / 無學 | trainee / non-trainee | śaikṣa / aśaikṣa — one still progressing on the noble path vs. one who has completed it (arhatship); "path of no-more-learning" for 無學道. |
| 見道 / 修道 | path of seeing / path of cultivation | darśana-mārga / bhāvanā-mārga. |
| 擇滅 / 非擇滅 | cessation through discernment / cessation not through discernment | pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha / apratisaṃkhyā-nirodha — two of the three unconditioned dharmas. |
| 六因 | the six causes | ṣaḍ-hetavaḥ — introduced in fascicle 6: 能作因 "efficient cause" (kāraṇa-hetu), 俱有因 "co-existent cause" (sahabhū-hetu), 同類因 "homogeneous cause" (sabhāga-hetu), 相應因 "associated cause" (saṃprayuktaka-hetu), 遍行因 "universal cause" (sarvatraga-hetu), 異熟因 "ripening cause" (vipāka-hetu). |
| 士用果 | agent-efficacy result | puruṣakāra-phala — the kind of result two dharmas produce when they are mutually a co-existent cause (俱有因) of one another. |
| 心隨轉法 | dharmas that follow mind's course | dharmas co-arising with mind in lockstep — mental factors, the two moral restraints, and mind's own characteristics; defined in fascicle 6 by matching time, result, and moral quality with mind. |
| 四緣 | the four conditions | catuḥ-pratyaya — introduced in fascicle 7: 因緣 "causal condition" (hetu-pratyaya, = five of the six causes), 等無間緣 "immediately-antecedent condition" (samanantara-pratyaya, mind/mental-factors only), 所緣緣 "objective condition" (ālambana-pratyaya), 增上緣 "dominant condition" (adhipati-pratyaya, = the efficient cause). |
| 生因/依因/立因/持因/養因 | generative / support / establishing / sustaining / nurturing cause | introduced in fascicle 7 — five named subdivisions of the efficient cause (能作因), specifically describing how the four great elements (大種) cause derived form (所造色) to arise, be supported, be established, continue uninterrupted, and grow. |
| 十二心 | the twelve minds | introduced in fascicle 7: the desire, form, and formless realms' wholesome/unwholesome/covered-neutral/uncovered-neutral minds (form and formless lack "unwholesome"), plus the two uncontaminated minds, 學 "trainee's" and 無學 "non-trainee's". Used as the basis for an extended combinatorial analysis of the immediately-antecedent condition (等無間緣). |
| 化心 | transformation-mind | nirmāṇa-citta — a sub-variety of uncovered-neutral mind (無覆無記) exercised specifically in producing supernormally-created forms; introduced in fascicle 7, formally named as part of the twenty-mind subdivision. |
| 加行得 / 生得 | effortfully-acquired / innately-acquired | prāyogika / upapatti-labha — the two varieties every realm's wholesome mind splits into in fascicle 7's twenty-mind subdivision; matches "acquired through effortful practice" / "innately acquired" already used for the same distinction in fascicle 4. |
| 異熟生 / 威儀路 / 工巧處 | karmic-ripening / bodily deportment / skilled crafts | vipāka-ja / īryāpatha / śilpasthāna — the sub-varieties the desire realm's (and, minus skilled crafts, the form realm's) uncovered-neutral mind splits into in fascicle 7; matches usage already established in fascicle 3. |
| 自相 / 共相 | particular characteristic / shared characteristic | svalakṣaṇa / sāmānya-lakṣaṇa — established in fascicle 1; reused in fascicle 7 for the first two of the three kinds of mental application (作意). |
| 作意 | mental application | manasikāra — in fascicle 7, classified three ways (attending to a particular characteristic, to a shared characteristic, or through resolute understanding/勝解) and also, separately, by source (accomplished through hearing/聞所成, through reflection/思所成, through cultivation/修所成, or innately-acquired/生得), paralleling but distinct from the classical three-wisdoms triad (聞思修所成慧) already introduced in fascicle 6. |
| 三界 processes (fascicle 8 cosmology) | six desire-heavens: Heaven of the Four Great Kings / Heaven of the Thirty-Three / Yama Heaven / Tuṣita Heaven / Heaven of Enjoying Transformations / Heaven of Mastery over Others' Transformations. Eight great hells: Reviving / Black-Rope / Crushing-Together / Wailing / Great Wailing / Scorching / Great Scorching / Uninterrupted. Four continents: Jambudvīpa / Pūrvavideha / Aparagodānīya / Uttarakuru. | Introduced in fascicle 8's opening cosmology; keep these exact English names for every later reference to the desire realm's twenty places. |
| 色界十七處 | the form realm's seventeen heavens | Introduced in fascicle 8: first dhyāna — Heaven of Brahmā's Retinue / Heaven of Brahmā's Ministers / Great Brahmā Heaven; second dhyāna — Heaven of Lesser Light / Heaven of Immeasurable Light / Heaven of Light-Sound; third dhyāna — Heaven of Lesser Purity / Heaven of Immeasurable Purity / Heaven of All-Pervasive Purity; fourth dhyāna (eight) — Cloudless / Merit-Born / Great Reward / Without Affliction / Without Heat / Skillful Appearance / Skillful Vision / Ultimate Form (Akaniṣṭha). The Kashmiri masters count only sixteen, treating the Great Brahmā Heaven as a tower within the Heaven of Brahmā's Ministers rather than a separate place. |
| 四無色 | the four formless spheres | ākāśānantyāyatana / vijñānānantyāyatana / ākiṃcanyāyatana / naivasaṃjñānāsaṃjñāyatana — "sphere of infinite space," "sphere of infinite consciousness," "sphere of nothing-at-all," "sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception"; introduced in fascicle 8. Distinguished only by kind of birth, not by any spatial location — the formless realm has no place at all. |
| 邪命外道 | an Ājīvika heretic | Ājīvika — a real, historically attested rival ascetic movement contemporary with early Buddhism; introduced in fascicle 8 as Śāriputra's interlocutor in the debate over what "desire" refers to. |
| 四生 | the four kinds of birth | egg-birth (卵生) / womb-birth (胎生) / moisture-birth (濕生) / transformation-birth (化生); introduced in fascicle 8. Humans and animals uniquely span all four; hells, gods, and the intermediate-existence being are exclusively transformation-born; ghosts span only womb- and transformation-birth. |
| 揭路荼 / 那落迦 (as used for hell-beings) | garuḍa / hell-being (nāraka) | garuḍa — a mythical bird-being; introduced in fascicle 8 in the discussion of transformation-birth. 那落迦/nāraka for "hell-being" already used consistently since earlier fascicles' hell discussions. |
| 目連 | Maudgalyāyana | one of the Buddha's two chief disciples (with Śāriputra); introduced in fascicle 8 as the addressee of the hungry ghost's lament. |
| 中有 | intermediate existence | antarābhava — the five-aggregate being arising between the existence of death and the existence of birth; introduced and extensively defended in fascicle 8's closing debate. Also called 健達縛/gandharva in the specific context of entry into a mother's womb. |
| 五不還 | the five non-returners | introduced in fascicle 8: 中般 "attains final nirvana in the interval," 生般 "attains it upon birth," 無行般 "without exertion," 有行般 "through exertion," 上流 "streams upward." |
| 五位 (胎內) | the five embryonic stages | kalala / arbuda / peśī / ghana / praśākhā — kept untranslated as Sanskrit terms; introduced in fascicle 9. Kept distinct from the unrelated 五位 used elsewhere (e.g. 三際/三世 stage-schemes) — context disambiguates. |
| 十二支緣起 | the twelve links of dependent origination | pratītyasamutpāda; introduced in fascicle 9: 無明 ignorance / 行 formations / 識 consciousness / 名色 name-and-form / 六處 the six sense-bases / 觸 contact / 受 feeling / 愛 craving / 取 grasping / 有 existence / 生 birth / 老死 aging-and-death. Mapped across three lifetimes (past/present/future = prior/middle/later periods, 前際/中際/後際). |
| 緣起 / 緣已生法 | dependent origination / dependently-arisen dharmas | the same twelve links named for their causal aspect (緣起) vs. their resultant aspect (緣已生法); introduced in fascicle 9. |
| 四取 | the four kinds of grasping | introduced in fascicle 9: 欲取 desire-grasping (for the five sense-pleasures), 見取 view-grasping (the sixty-two views, 六十二見), 戒禁取 precept-and-vow-grasping, 我語取 "the discourse of self" (self-view/self-conceit). |
| 播輸鉢多 / 伐羅勺迦 | Pāśupata / Vārṣagaṇya | rival ascetic schools; introduced in fascicle 9 alongside Jain ascetics and brahmins as examples of precept-and-vow-grasping. |
| 《象迹喻經》 | the Elephant's Footprint Simile Sutra | cited in fascicle 9's debate over whether the "staged" twelve-link reading matches scripture's own definitions. |
| 《大因緣經》/《辯緣起經》 | the Mahānidāna Sutra / the Discourse Distinguishing Dependent Origination | cited in fascicle 9 as scriptural sources for the three-lifetime, full-five-aggregates account of name-and-form. |
| 有對觸 / 增語觸 | resistant contact / designation-contact | introduced in fascicle 10: the first five kinds of contact (eye through body), named for depending on faculties with physical resistance, vs. the sixth (mind-contact), named because only mental consciousness can attach a name/concept to what it apprehends. |
| 明觸 / 無明觸 / 非明非無明觸 | clarity-contact / ignorance-contact / contact that is neither clarity nor ignorance | introduced in fascicle 10 as one of several cross-cutting eightfold/sixteenfold classifications of the six/two kinds of contact, alongside association with craving/hostility and with the three feelings (順樂/苦/不苦不樂受). |
| 身受 / 心受 | bodily feeling / mental feeling | vedanā produced by the five physical senses' contact vs. by mind-contact; introduced in fascicle 10. |
| 十大地法 / 大善地法 / 大煩惱地法 / 大不善地法 | the ten mahābhūmika (great-ground) dharmas / great wholesome-ground dharmas / great afflicted-ground dharmas / great unwholesome-ground dharmas | introduced in fascicle 10 in the debate over whether feeling arises simultaneously with contact. The ten mahābhūmika dharmas are not enumerated by name in that passage — only the abstract "ground" criterion (bhūmi, in the sense of a class of mental states, e.g. wholesome/unwholesome/neutral) that qualifies a dharma as "great-ground" is defined there. Note: this is a different sense of 地/bhūmi from the cosmological "realm" sense already tabled above (desire realm, dhyānas, etc.) — context disambiguates. |
| 意近行 | mental examination | manaḥ-upavicāra — introduced in fascicle 10: joy, grief, and equanimity, each paired with the six sense objects, giving eighteen kinds; the name puns on 近 "proximate" and 行 "roaming," describing the mind's repeated movement toward an object under an afflicted feeling's pull. Distinct from 作意/mental application (manasikāra), already tabled for fascicle 7. |
| 六恆住 | the six constant abodes | introduced in fascicle 10 as the noble one's unmoved equanimity toward each of the six sense objects, taught by Vasubandhu (in his own dissenting voice, against the Vaibhāṣika reading of mental examination) as a counterexample showing not every instance of joy/grief/equanimity is an afflicted "mental examination." |
| 三十六師句 | the thirty-six master-verses | introduced in fascicle 10: the eighteen mental examinations doubled along the axis of 耽嗜依 "basis for indulgence" (afflicted) vs. 出離依 "basis for renunciation" (wholesome); attributed to the Buddha ("大師," the Great Teacher) in this passage, though we have not identified the specific sūtra source. |
| 四有：中有／生有／本有／死有 | the four states of existence: intermediate / birth / fundamental / death | introduced in fascicle 8 (中有/intermediate existence defended at length there) and formally named as a set of four in fascicle 10: 生有 the instant of rebirth-linking, 本有 the ordinary span of a life between birth and death, 死有 the instant of dying, 中有 the interval between death and the next rebirth-linking. Fascicle 10 establishes that 生有 is always afflicted, while the other three can be wholesome, afflicted, or neutral; 中有 does not occur in the formless realm. |
| 三種緣起譬喻：種/龍/草根樹莖/糠裹米、有糠米/草藥/花、成熟飲食 | the three similes for affliction, karma, and ripened result: seed/dragon/grass-root-and-tree-trunk/husk-wrapped-rice; husked rice/medicinal herb/flower; cooked food | introduced in fascicle 10 to distinguish the twelve links' three functional groupings (煩惱 affliction, 業 karma, 異熟果事 the matter of ripened result): affliction self-perpetuates, karma is inert without affliction's "wrapping," and ripened result is causally terminal (cannot itself ripen into a further ripened result). |
| 四食 | the four nutriments | āhāra — introduced in fascicle 10: 段食 edible food (desire realm only; subtle for intermediate-existence beings and eon-initial gods, coarse otherwise; substance is fragrance/flavor/touch, not visible form), 觸食 contact, 思食 volition (mental karma), 識食 consciousness (the consciousness aggregate); the latter three are contaminated but extend across all three realms. |
| 部多 / 求生 | already-born / seeker of birth | introduced in fascicle 10 alongside the four nutriments: 部多 refers to beings already born into any of the five destinies (identified specifically with arhats in this passage's closing line), while 求生 refers to the intermediate-existence being (or, more broadly, anyone whose craving is not yet exhausted). |
| 順決擇分 | the aids to penetration | nirvedha-bhāgīya — the preparatory stage of practice leading toward the path of seeing; mentioned in fascicle 10 as a rejected Vaibhāṣika identification of the "ordinary being in the Jambudvīpa forest," on the grounds that no scripture actually places one who has attained this stage specifically in that location. |
| 贍部林中異生 / 後身菩薩 | the ordinary being in the Jambudvīpa forest / the bodhisattva in his final existence | introduced in fascicle 10: a sūtra figure whose feeding is said to outweigh feeding a hundred desireless non-Buddhist ascetics, resolved by the treatise (after rejecting three other candidates) as referring to the bodhisattva in his last life before awakening, still technically an unenlightened "ordinary being." |

| 器世間 | the container world | bhājana-loka — the physical cosmos itself (mountains, seas, continents), as distinct from 有情世間 "the world of sentient beings"; introduced as fascicle 11's opening topic, a direct structural pivot from fascicle 10's close of the sentient-beings material. |
| 踰繕那 | yojana | left untransliterated as the standard unit of distance throughout fascicle 11's cosmology; traditionally reckoned at roughly 7–15 km depending on source, but we do not convert it to modern units in the translation itself. |
| 洛叉 / 億 (as units in fascicle 11's cosmology) | 100,000 (as a bare multiplier) | lakṣa — confirmed by direct textual cross-check in fascicle 11 (the verse's "十六洛叉" and the prose's "十六億" state the identical figure for the wind wheel's thickness), 億 functions as a synonym for lakṣa (100,000) in this specific cosmological passage, NOT the modern Chinese value of 100,000,000. All numeral compounds using either character in this fascicle's measurements are converted to plain decimal yojana counts on this basis. Do not assume this 億=100,000 convention applies outside this specific numerical context without re-verifying — later fascicles or other numerical passages may use 億 in its ordinary sense. |
| 三千大千世界 | the great trichiliocosm | trisāhasra-mahāsāhasra-loka-dhātu — the "three-thousandfold great thousand-world system," the largest standard unit of Buddhist cosmology; introduced in fascicle 11 as the scale at which the wind/water/gold wheel structure is established. |
| 蘇迷盧 / 妙高山 | Mount Sumeru | the axial mountain at the center of each world-system; introduced in fascicle 11. |
| 半 (numeral suffix in fascicle 11's cosmology) | half of the nearest larger place-value already named | e.g. "三千四百半" = 3,400 + half of the hundreds-place just named (50) = 3,450, NOT 3,400.5; "七千半" = 7,000 + half of the thousands-place just named (500) = 7,500. Confirmed by cross-checking against circumference figures independently given in the same passages (diameter × 3 must equal the stated circumference). When "半" instead follows a bare small number with no larger place-value word in the same numeral (e.g. "三半" = 3.5 yojana, per Jambudvīpa's south side), it does mean a literal "and a half" of the base unit. We got this wrong once (fascicle 11, section 1) before catching it via this cross-check; see that section's note for the correction record. |
| 金剛座 | the Vajrāsana (Diamond Seat) | the fixed physical location within Jambudvīpa, reaching down to the gold wheel, where every bodhisattva sits to attain awakening; introduced in fascicle 11. |
| 中洲 | intermediate continent(s) | the eight smaller satellite continents, two beside each of the four great continents; introduced in fascicle 11: Cāmara / Aparacāmara (beside Jambudvīpa), Deha / Videha (beside Pūrvavideha), Śāṭha / Uttaramantriṇa (beside Aparagodānīya), Kurava / Kaurava (beside Uttarakuru). |
| 無熱惱池 | Lake Anavatapta (the Lake Without Heat) | introduced in fascicle 11: a fifty-yojana square lake north of Jambudvīpa's black mountains and Great Snow Mountain, inaccessible to anyone without supernormal penetration, source of the four great rivers (殑伽河 Gaṅgā, 信度河 Sindhu, 徙多河 Sītā, 縛芻河 Vakṣu). |
| 贍部林 | the Jambu forest | introduced in fascicle 11 beside Lake Anavatapta; gives Jambudvīpa its name, either from the forest itself or from its fruit (the text offers both etymologies without choosing between them). |
| 㮈落迦 / 大㮈落迦 | hell / great hell | naraka / mahā-naraka; introduced in fascicle 11's cosmology alongside the eight hot hells already named in fascicle 8 (kept consistent: Reviving / Black-Rope / Crushing-Together / Wailing / Great Wailing / Scorching / Great Scorching / Uninterrupted). |
| 十六增 | the sixteen surrounding torments | introduced in fascicle 11: four per side (ember-pit / corpse-mire / blade torment [itself threefold: blade-road, sword-leaf forest, iron-thorn forest] / scalding river), on all four sides of each of the eight hot hells. |
| 八寒地獄 | the eight cold hells | introduced in fascicle 11, named onomatopoeically after the sounds their freezing inhabitants make: Arbuda, Nirarbuda, Aṭaṭa, Hahava, Huhuva, Utpala, Padma, Mahāpadma (the last three also describing lotus-like cracking of the skin). |
| 琰魔王國 | King Yama's kingdom | introduced in fascicle 11: the base dwelling-place of ghosts, 500 yojanas beneath Jambudvīpa, equal to it in extent. |
| 蘇迷盧四層級 | Sumeru's four terraces | introduced in fascicle 11: home to the yaksha hosts Karoṭapāṇi, Mālādhāra, and Sadāmada on the first three, and the Four Great Heavenly Kings themselves on the fourth — the origin of "the Heaven of the Four Great Kings." |
| 三十三天 / 善見城 / 殊勝殿 / 善法堂 | the Heaven of the Thirty-Three / Sudarśana (the city) / the Vaijayanta Palace / the Sudharmā Hall | introduced in fascicle 11 atop Sumeru's summit: Śakra's capital city, his palace within it, and the hall where the Thirty-Three deliberate right from wrong. Four parks (Caitraratha, Pāruṣya, Miśravana, Nandana) and the Pārijāta tree (whose fragrance travels even against the wind) surround the city. |
| 空居天 / 地居天 | sky-dwelling gods / earth-dwelling gods | introduced in fascicle 11: earth-dwelling gods live on Sumeru's terraces and summit (the Four Kings and the Thirty-Three); sky-dwelling gods live in freestanding palaces resting on nothing (Yāma Heaven upward, plus the sun and moon's own inhabitants). |
| 欲生 / 樂生 | desire-birth / bliss-birth | introduced in fascicle 11: desire-birth's three kinds are distinguished by whose transformed objects of desire a being enjoys (present/self-transformed/other-transformed, mapping onto the desire realm's hierarchy); bliss-birth's three kinds are distinguished by which flavor of joy-and-bliss is experienced across the three dhyānas' nine places of birth. The text itself flags an open question: why the joyless, blissless intermediate dhyāna is still classed as a "bliss-birth" heaven. |
| 色究竟天 / 礙究竟天 | Akaniṣṭha ("Ultimate Form") / "Ultimate Obstruction Heaven" | the highest heaven, introduced in fascicle 11; two etymologies offered without choosing between them — the last outpost of form before the formless realm, or the point where form's "obstruction" is finally exhausted. |
| 小千界 / 中千界 / 大千界 | small chiliocosm / medium chiliocosm / great chiliocosm | introduced in fascicle 11: a thousand complete world-systems (four continents through a Brahmā-world) make one small chiliocosm; a thousand small chiliocosms make one medium chiliocosm; a thousand medium chiliocosms make one great chiliocosm — the whole undergoing one shared formation and destruction. Gives precise technical grounding to "the great trichiliocosm" (三千大千世界) already used in this fascicle's opening section. |
| 中夭 | untimely death | introduced in fascicle 11: every realm admits of it except Uttarakuru (fixed at 1,000 years); specific individuals are exempt regardless of realm — a one-more-birth bodhisattva in Tuṣita, one in his final existence, a buddha, one predicted to buddhahood, a buddha's emissary, faith-/dharma-followers, a bodhisattva, a wheel-turning king, and the mother carrying either of the last two. |

| 極微 / 字 / 剎那 | atom / syllable / instant | introduced in fascicle 12 as the smallest possible units of form, name, and time respectively; an instant is defined as either the span in which a dharma attains its own essential nature, or the time for a moving object to cross one atom's width (empirically anchored at 1/65th of a finger-snap). |
| 距離單位鏈（極微→踰繕那）/ 時間單位鏈（剎那→年） | the sevenfold distance-unit chain (atom to yojana) / the time-unit chain (instant to year) | introduced in fascicle 12: distance climbs sevenfold at each of eleven invented steps (atom, particle, metal-dust, water-dust, rabbit-hair-dust, sheep-hair-dust, ox-hair-dust, gap-dust, louse-egg, louse, barley grain, finger-joint) before switching to ordinary units (finger, cubit, bow-length, krośa, yojana); time uses fixed multipliers (120 instants = 1 tatkṣaṇa, 60 = 1 lava, 30 = 1 muhūrta, 30 = 1 day-night, 30 days = 1 month, 12 months = 1 year). |
| 壞劫 / 成劫 / 中劫 / 大劫 | destruction kalpa / formation kalpa / medium kalpa / great kalpa | introduced in fascicle 12: destruction runs from hell-beings ceasing rebirth to the container world's total exhaustion (spreading upward via beings' spontaneous discovery of dhyāna, then via cosmic fire up to the Brahmā world); formation is the exact reverse, top-down from the Brahmā world back to hell. A great kalpa = 80 medium kalpas (20 each for formation, abiding, destruction, emptiness); the abiding kalpa's 20 medium kalpas = 1 pure decrease (immeasurable→10 years) + 18 increase-decrease cycles (10↔80,000 years) + 1 pure increase (10→80,000). |
| 阿僧企耶 | asaṃkhyeya ("incalculable") | introduced in fascicle 12 as the 60th (52nd surviving) number in the Vimokṣa Sūtra's numeral chain, each step ×10 from "one"; the sūtra's own list is missing eight names by the time of this citation. Three incalculable kalpas (累積大劫至阿僧企耶再乘三) are needed for a bodhisattva's full path — "incalculable" means not countable by ordinary means, not literally infinite, which is why the text can still say "three" of them. |
| 六波羅蜜多 | the six perfections | ṣaṭ-pāramitā; introduced in fascicle 12 as what a bodhisattva cultivates as "the great provisions of merit and wisdom" across three incalculable kalpas. |
| 下士 / 中士 / 上士 | the lesser person / the middling person / the greater person | introduced in fascicle 12's verse on motivation: the lesser person seeks his own happiness, the middling person (śrāvaka/pratyekabuddha) seeks the cessation of his own suffering, the greater person (bodhisattva) seeks his own suffering and others' happiness, taking others as himself. |
| 五濁 | the five defilements | pañca-kaṣāya; introduced in fascicle 12 as what makes beings too degraded for a buddha to teach once lifespan falls below a hundred years: 壽濁 lifespan, 劫濁 the kalpa itself, 煩惱濁 affliction, 見濁 views, 有情濁 sentient beings — the first two erode external conditions, the middle two erode wholesome conduct, the last erodes the person directly. |
| 部行獨覺 / 麟角喻獨覺 | the group-dwelling pratyekabuddha / the rhinoceros-horn-like pratyekabuddha | introduced in fascicle 12: the former was previously a śrāvaka (or, on another view, an ordinary being who had cultivated the aids to penetration) who attains the fruit without a teacher present; the latter is solitary by necessity and requires a full hundred great kalpas of prior preparation. Both are capable of teaching but choose not to. |
| 轉輪王：金/銀/銅/鐵輪王 | wheel-turning king: gold/silver/copper/iron-wheel king | introduced in fascicle 12: rules four/three/two/one continent(s) respectively; appears only while lifespan is at least eighty thousand years; like a buddha, never coexists with another wheel-turning king anywhere in the world at the same time. |
| 一佛多界辯論 | the debate over one buddha versus many buddhas across world-systems | introduced in fascicle 12: one view holds a single buddha suffices for the entire universe (citing a Śāriputra exchange and reading the Brahmajāla Sūtra's "mastery over this trichiliocosm" as describing reach under deliberate effort, not an absolute limit); the view the text ultimately favors holds that boundless world-systems each have their own buddha simultaneously, by analogy with wheel-turning kings, while still allowing only one buddha per single world-system at a time (for lack of purpose, a bodhisattva's founding vow, reverence, and urgency). |
| 劫初人類起源敘事 | the origin-of-humanity narrative | introduced in fascicle 12: humans begin as radiant, sexless, mind-made beings like form-realm gods; craving for an earthly essence, then an earth-cake, then forest vines, then wild rice, each in turn vanishing once craved, progressively coarsens the body until sexual organs, desire, hoarding, private property, theft, the first hired king (giving rise to "kṣatriya" and "Mahāsammata"), brahmins, punishment, killing, and finally false speech all arise in strict causal sequence. |
| 小三災 / 大三災 | the three small calamities / the three great calamities | introduced in fascicle 12: the small calamities (sword, plague, famine) strike at a medium kalpa's end when lifespan bottoms out at ten years, lasting seven days/months/years respectively, remediable by minor virtuous acts; the great calamities (fire, water, wind) periodically destroy an entire world-system down to its last atom, peaking respectively at the second, third, and fourth dhyānas, in a fixed cycle of eight sets of seven fire calamities, seven water calamities, and one wind calamity (totaling 64, matching Ābhāsvara's kalpa-lifespan). |
| 極微常住辯（對外道） | the debate against non-Buddhist atomism | introduced in fascicle 12: a rival school holds atoms are eternal and must survive total cosmic destruction to "seed" the next world; the treatise counters that karma-produced wind serves this function instead, and extends into a lengthy nominalist argument (cloth vs. thread, substance vs. quality) denying that composite wholes are anything beyond their parts differently arranged. |

| 思業 / 思已業 | volitional karma / karma-after-volition | introduced in fascicle 13, opening Chapter 4: the sūtra's own twofold division of karma — volition itself, and what volition produces through body and speech — which the treatise further divides into bodily, verbal, and mental karma. |
| 身表 / 身表業 | bodily manifestation / manifested bodily karma | vijñapti; introduced in fascicle 13 as the Vaibhāṣika's candidate for what a bodily karmic act physically is — argued, via the momentariness proof, to be shape (not motion); the Sautrāntika instead deny shape's reality and redefine manifested karma as volition itself, operating through the body's "gate." |
| 剎那滅論證 | the proof of universal momentariness | introduced in fascicle 13: cessation is a non-existence, and only a positive effect can require a cause, so cessation needs no trigger — everything conditioned must already be ceasing at every instant. Used to rule out real motion (a rapid succession of momentary, stationary instances only appears to travel), which in turn establishes that bodily manifestation must be shape, not motion. |
| 形色實有／假有辯 | the debate over whether shape (saṃsthāna) is real or merely conceptual | introduced in fascicle 13: the Vaibhāṣika hold shape is real, distinct matter; the Sautrāntika argue it is only a conceptual label applied to color-atoms arranged in a pattern (long = densely massed one direction, round = uniformly filling every direction), using the same nominalist strategy as the cloth/thread debate in fascicle 12. |
| 無表 / 無表業 / 無表色 | unmanifested karma / unmanifested matter | avijñapti; introduced in fascicle 13 as the subtle karmic potency that persists after a manifested act ends (most famously a monastic vow) — the central disputed topic of this fascicle. Vaibhāṣika offer seven scriptural proofs it is real, distinct matter; Sautrāntika reinterpret all seven via a "perfumed"/gradually-transforming mental continuum instead, without ever fully resolving the dispute. |
| 熏習 | perfuming | vāsanā; introduced in fascicle 13 as the Sautrāntika's alternative to unmanifested karma — a subtle imprint volition leaves on the mental continuum, which then transforms and differentiates on its own without needing continuous conscious attention. An early, more modest ancestor of the later Yogācāra "seed" (bīja) theory. |
| 執受 | graspable physical basis | upādāna (technical sense, distinct from the more common "appropriation/fuel" sense); introduced in fascicle 13's classification of karma — matter bound up with a living body's own capacity for sensation (damaging it produces feeling), as opposed to matter physically present but outside this sensitive domain (e.g. unmanifested karma, a fingernail clipping). |
| 等流 | natural outflow | niṣyanda; introduced in fascicle 13 — a technical category for an effect that resembles its immediate cause closely enough to count as the same general type continuing forward, contrasted with effects produced by more specialized processes (e.g. matter "nourished" by meditative concentration). |
| 等起（因等起／剎那等起） | initiation (causal initiation / momentary initiation) | introduced in fascicle 13: causal initiation is a distant cause that merely sets up a later action in advance (e.g. a view-affliction); momentary initiation is present at the very instant the action occurs. Afflictions abandoned through seeing can only causally initiate; the five sense-consciousnesses can only momentarily initiate; ordinary cultivation-abandoned mental consciousness can do both; uncontaminated and neutral ripened-arising states do neither. |
| 四種善／不善（勝義／自性／相應／等起） | the fourfold framework for wholesome/unwholesome (ultimate reality / intrinsic nature / association / initiation) | introduced in fascicle 13: nirvāṇa is wholesome by ultimate reality; shame, conscience, and the wholesome roots are wholesome by intrinsic nature (like medicine); mental factors paired with these are wholesome by association (like medicine-water); bodily/verbal karma initiated by these is wholesome by initiation (like medicine-water-nourished milk). Unwholesome mirrors this exactly in reverse. |
| 那伽 | Nāga | transliterated, not translated, in fascicle 13's verse on the Buddha being "in meditation" in all four postures — a traditional honorific epithet for a buddha or arhat, carrying connotations of both the mythic serpent-being and a great elephant's imperturbable strength; kept as a proper term since translating it loses this resonance. |

| 律儀 / 不律儀 / 非律儀非不律儀 | restraint / non-restraint / neither | saṃvara; introduced in fascicle 14 as unmanifested karma's threefold division — restraint blocks bad conduct's continuity, non-restraint commits one to it, "neither" is everything else. Three kinds of restraint: 別解脫律儀 (prātimokṣa, desire-realm precepts), 靜慮生律儀 (meditation-born, form-realm), 道生律儀 (path-born, uncontaminated) — the latter two arise automatically with the corresponding mind and "follow the mind"; prātimokṣa does not. |
| 別解脫律儀八眾／四體 | the eight named vows of individual liberation, four real substances | introduced in fascicle 14: bhikṣu, bhikṣuṇī, śikṣamāṇā, śrāmaṇera, śrāmaṇerī, upāsaka, upāsikā, upavāsa (upoṣadha) — reduce to four substances (bhikṣu/śrāmaṇera/upāsaka/upavāsa) since paired vows differ only by the holder's sex, proven by sex-change never causing the vow to be relinquished and reacquired, only renamed. |
| 近事 / 近住 / 勤策 / 苾芻 precept-counts | upāsaka (5) / upavāsa (8) / śrāmaṇera (10) / bhikṣu (all) | introduced in fascicle 14: upāsaka = killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, lying, alcohol; upavāsa adds non-chastity (replacing mere "misconduct"), self-adornment/entertainment, high beds, improper-time eating; śrāmaṇera splits the adornment/entertainment precept in two and adds handling money; bhikṣu = the complete code. Eight upavāsa branches group as 4 śīla-branches (root offenses) + 1 heedfulness-branch (alcohol) + 3 restraining-branches (indulgence). |
| 別解脫五名 | the five names for the vow of individual liberation / for non-restraint | introduced in fascicle 14: śīla ("cool," levels dangerous karma) / wholesome conduct / karma / restraint / prātimokṣa (only the first instant); non-restraint mirrors exactly: bad conduct / bad precept-vow / karma / non-restraint / karma-path (only the first instant). |
| 十種得具戒法 | the ten traditional modes of full ordination | introduced in fascicle 14: by nature (Buddha, pratyekabuddhas) / entering correct assurance (the five monks) / "Come, monk" (Yaśas) / faith in the Buddha as teacher (Mahākāśyapa) / skillful answering (Sudāyin) / accepting the eight special rules (Mahāprajāpatī) / sent messenger (a nun we tentatively render "Dharmadatta," 法授尼 — identification not fully certain) / quorum of five with a Vinaya-holder (border regions) / quorum of ten (Middle Country, the ongoing standard) / threefold refuge recitation (the sixty worthy ones). |
| 歸依佛法僧 | taking refuge in the Three Jewels | introduced in fascicle 14: refuge in the Buddha = refuge in the aśaikṣa (beyond-training) dharmas that constitute buddhahood (exhaustive-knowledge etc.), not the physical body alone — though the body is the qualities' indispensable support, which is why shedding the Buddha's blood is still an ānantarya offense; refuge in Dharma = nirvāṇa/pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha; refuge in Saṅgha = the training/beyond-training dharmas of the eight kinds of noble persons. Refuge's own nature is manifested verbal karma; its purpose is rescue. Closes with the well-known verse contrasting false refuge (mountains, groves, shrines) with true refuge via the Four Noble Truths. |
| 性罪 / 遮罪 | root offense / proscribed offense | introduced in fascicle 14: a root offense (killing, stealing, etc.) is inherently wrong regardless of any precept; a proscribed offense (alcohol, for the upāsaka) is wrong only because specifically forbidden. Whether drinking alcohol is itself a root or merely proscribed offense is a live, unresolved dispute between the Vinaya masters and the Ābhidharmika masters recorded in fascicle 14 without being settled. |
| 窣羅 / 迷麗耶 / 末陀 | surā / maireya / madya | introduced in fascicle 14: surā is alcohol fermented from grain/cooked food, maireya from other substances; either becomes "madya" only once actually capable of causing intoxication. Alcohol alone among proscribed offenses is called "the basis of heedlessness" (放逸處). |

| 多羅樹斷頭喻 | the withered-palm-tree simile | introduced in fascicle 15: just as a palm tree, once its crown is severed, can never again grow, a monastic who commits one of the four gravest (pārājika) offenses can never again make their remaining training-points "grow and flourish" — used by the Kashmir Vaibhāṣika to argue a grave offense severs every restraint at once, distinct from their earlier debt-analogy treatment of lesser violations. |
| 焦種喻 | the burnt-seed analogy | introduced in fascicle 15: a grave offender is never re-ordained not because they retain bhikṣu-restraint a second ordination would duplicate, but because their continuum's extreme shamelessness has lost the very capacity to generate going-forth restraint at all — like a scorched seed that can never sprout again regardless of replanting. |
| 阿世耶 | underlying disposition | āśaya; introduced in fascicle 15 as the totalizing mental disposition (wholesome or corrupted) that grounds restraint and non-restraint's universal scope across all sentient beings — not a case-by-case inventory of who one would actually harm or benefit. Used to explain why a butcher's non-restraint extends even to kin they'd never harm, and left an open puzzle regarding hypothetical past/future embodiments (fascicle 15 section 7, unresolved in the text itself). |
| 業定不定 / 順現法受・順次生受・順後次受 | determinate/indeterminate karma; ripening in this life / the next life / later lives | introduced in fascicle 15: determinate karma arises from heavy affliction, a pure mind, habitual practice, or an exalted field of merit (Three Jewels, an accomplished practitioner), or from harming one's parents. Vaibhāṣika read the three "ripens-in" categories literally (fixed to a specific life); other teachers read them as merely "whichever occasion it first ripens." Five special fields of merit (emerging from cessation-attainment, freedom-from-conflict, loving-kindness, the path of seeing, or the path of cultivation) always ripen immediately. |
| 心狂 | mental derangement | introduced in fascicle 15 as distinct from ordinary defiled mind (four-case analysis: deranged-not-disturbed, disturbed-not-deranged, both, neither); arises from five causes — karmic ripening, fright, injury, elemental discord, or grief — occurring only in mental consciousness. Not itself a direct karmic ripened result but arises from one (unevenly-composed great elements). Hell-beings are constantly deranged; noble ones (except buddhas) remain susceptible only via elemental discord, never karmic ripening, fright, injury, or grief. |

| 黑黑異熟 / 白白異熟 / 黑白俱異熟 / 非黑非白無異熟 | black karma with a black ripened result / white with white / black-and-white (mixed) with black-and-white / neither black nor white, with no ripened result | introduced in fascicle 16: all unwholesome karma is black; form-realm wholesome karma is white; desire-realm wholesome karma is black-and-white, since mixed with unwholesome karma there — a label describing an entire continuum's pattern, not any single act; uncontaminated karma capable of exhausting all three is "neither," undefiled but also not productive of a white result (this second "neither" stated with hidden intent, since the Buddha elsewhere calls the qualities of one beyond training "purely white"). |
| 三牟尼 / 三清淨 | the three sages (bodily, verbal, mental) / the three purities | introduced in fascicle 16 alongside the black/white classification, distinguishing sagehood and purity by which of the three doors (body, speech, mind) each pertains to. |
| 加行 / 根本業道 / 後起 | preparatory action / root act / follow-up action | introduced in fascicle 16 as the three-stage structure underlying every karma-path: preparatory action leads up to it, the root act is the single completing instant, and follow-up action is everything that follows — illustrated by a sheep-butcher's killing (weapon-raising through the fatal blow through the corpse's disposal) and by a śrāmaṇera's ordination ceremony (entering the platform through the third formal reading through the post-ordination instructions). |
| 十業道 completion | the ten karma-paths' precise definitions | introduced in fascicle 16, closing out the treatise's account of karma: killing (premeditated intent + correct target + no accident), theft (special cases: a stupa's property counts as the Tathāgata's, buried treasure as the sovereign's, saṅgha common property as belonging to all disciples), sexual misconduct (wrong object/orifice/place/time), false speech (defiled mind + differing intent + hearer's correct understanding), divisive speech and harsh speech (same "correct understanding" completion standard as false speech), frivolous speech (disputed: any other defiled speech, or specifically flattery/song/heterodox treatises/lamentation), greed (an evil wish for another's property — disputed whether this covers only coarse instances or all desire-realm craving), hatred (malice wishing harm on a being), and wrong view (denying karma, its fruit, and the noble ones, per the classic nihilist-view sūtra formula). |
| 三根感業道 | which of the three roots completes each karma-path's root act | introduced in fascicle 16: killing, harsh speech, and hatred are completed by hatred; theft, sexual misconduct, and greed by greed; wrong view by delusion; false, divisive, and frivolous speech by any of the three roots. |
| 見聞覺知 | seen / heard / sensed / known | introduced in fascicle 16 in the Vaibhāṣika's account of testimony: eye-consciousness is "seen," ear-consciousness "heard," mental-consciousness "known," and nose/tongue/body-consciousness together "sensed" (grouped since smell, taste, and touch are morally neutral like something inert). The treatise then rejects using the sūtra to Mahāprajāpatī as proof of this specific scheme, and records two alternative teacher accounts (direct sense-witness/hearsay/inference/mental-realization; and eye-witness/hearsay/self-reasoning/self-experience) without settling which is correct. |
| 表業無表業之難 | the puzzle of unmanifested karma arising without manifested karma | introduced in fascicle 16: killing and false speech can each occur through only one channel (speech alone, or bodily gesture alone) without the other — but a sage's silent inward anger, or a formal monastic suspension performed with neither bodily movement nor speech, is said to still generate both faults, which the treatise itself flags as a genuine, unresolved difficulty given that unmanifested karma elsewhere always depends on an accompanying manifested act. |

| 業道 (三唯道，七業亦道) | why the term "karma-path" applies differently across the ten | introduced in fascicle 17: for greed/hatred/wrong-view, the associated volition is the "karma" and these three are merely its "path"; for the other seven, the act itself is both the karma and the path volition travels through — a single compound term doing double duty, which the treatise itself flags as applying two different relationships under one label. |
| 斷善根 / 續善根 | cutting off the wholesome roots / restoring them | introduced in fascicle 17: only complete, upper-grade wrong view (denying both cause and effect) can sever desire-realm, born-of-maturation wholesome roots — possible only for humans of three continents (not Uttarakuru), only in male/female bodies, only for view-temperament (not craving-temperament) persons; its substance is non-possession. Restored through doubt or right view arising; an ānantarya offender can restore them only when about to die into hell or at the moment of that hellish rebirth, never within the same life otherwise. Debated throughout as gradual vs. sudden, with the treatise leaning gradual as better fitting the root text. |
| 斷善根與邪定四句 | the four-cornered analysis of cutting off wholesome roots vs. being fixed in a wrong destiny | introduced in fascicle 17: these are related but distinct — Pūraṇa cut off wholesome roots without being fixed in a wrong destiny; Ajātaśatru was fixed in a wrong destiny without cutting off wholesome roots (via his ānantarya offense instead); Devadatta was both; everyone else is neither. |
| 業道思俱轉 | how many karma-paths can co-arise with one volition | introduced in fascicle 17: unwholesome karma-paths range from 1 to 8 co-arising (never 9 or 10, since the three mental paths can't co-arise via their own power alone); wholesome karma-paths range up to all 10, with 1, 8, and 5 specifically excluded under the narrow, formal-configuration reading (though nothing is excluded under a fully comprehensive reading). |
| 業道三果（異熟／等流／增上） | the three fruits of each karma-path: ripened / outflow / dominant | introduced in fascicle 17: ripened fruit is rebirth in hell (for unwholesome) or the heavens (for wholesome); outflow fruit is the corresponding trait in one's next human life (e.g. killing → short lifespan, theft → poverty); dominant fruit affects one's external environment (e.g. killing → lackluster surroundings, theft → frost and hail). |
| 五果（異熟／等流／離繫／士用／增上） | the five kinds of fruit: ripened / outflow / freedom-from-bondage / personal-effort / dominant | introduced in fascicle 17 as the general fivefold fruit scheme underlying the karma-paths' threefold fruit — freedom-from-bondage is the cessation a path's power directly realizes; personal-effort is a result actively brought about through exertion. The treatise cross-tabulates how many of these five each type of karma produces, by moral nature, time, meditative stage, training-status, and abandonment-status. |
| 邪命 | wrong livelihood | introduced in fascicle 17: bodily/verbal karma arising from greed specifically (as opposed to wrong speech/action, arising from hatred/delusion), singled out as its own category because greed-driven misconduct is unusually difficult to root out. Debated whether this covers all greed-driven bodily/verbal karma or only greed tied to one's material subsistence — the treatise favors the broader reading, citing the Sūtra on the Aggregate of Precepts' classification of watching contests like elephant fights as wrong livelihood. |
| 引業 / 滿業 | projecting karma / completing karma | introduced in fascicle 17: a single act of karma projects only a single rebirth (one instance of shared class-membership), but that rebirth's specific fulfillment — bodily completeness, faculties, proportions — comes through the joint contribution of many further acts of karma, like a painter outlining a figure before filling in its colors. Illustrated by Aniruddha's account of one act of merit ripening across many lifetimes, which the treatise resolves as a chain of successive acts rather than one act projecting many rebirths. |
| 三障（業障／煩惱障／異熟障） | the three grave obstructions: karmic / afflictive / ripened-result | introduced in fascicle 17: the karmic obstruction is specifically the five ānantarya offenses (killing mother, father, or an arhat; causing a schism; wounding a buddha with malicious intent); the afflictive obstruction is persistently recurring (not merely intense) affliction; the ripened-result obstruction is rebirth in a bad destiny, in Uttarakuru, or in the non-percipient heaven. Ānantarya offenses can only be committed by ordinary human men and women of three continents, never by paṇḍakas or animals (with one dissenting view allowing an unusually aware animal). |

| 破僧 | causing a schism in the monastic community | introduced in fascicle 18: technically an instance of the false-speech karma-path (persuading others of a rival teaching), not bodily violence; its "brokenness" is a formation dissociated from mind possessed by the divided community, not the instigator, who instead possesses the false-speech offense itself. Requires a fully ordained monk of view-temperament, pure conduct, acting away from the Buddha's presence, against ordinary beings only; a Dharma-wheel schism needs 9+ monastics in Jambudvīpa only, a lesser formal-procedure schism needs 8+ across three continents; lasts only one night. |
| 破法輪僧不能時（六位） | the six circumstances when a Dharma-wheel schism is impossible | introduced in fascicle 18: very early or very late in a buddha's teaching career; before the "blisters" of doubt about precepts/view arise; before tranquility-and-insight practice is first paired; after the buddha's parinirvāṇa; before a territorial boundary is established. |
| 無間同類業 | the five ānantarya "counterparts" | introduced in fascicle 18: defiling one's arhat mother-nun, killing a settled bodhisattva or a noble-in-training, seizing away a community's harmony, and destroying a stūpa — close enough in kind to the true ānantarya offenses to be grouped as a secondary tier, though not necessarily resulting in Avīci rebirth the way the true five do. |
| 住定菩薩 | the "settled" bodhisattva | introduced in fascicle 18: a bodhisattva becomes irreversibly settled from the moment he begins cultivating the karma for the thirty-two marks — guaranteed thereafter, until buddhahood, rebirth only in fortunate destinies, eminent families, superior faculties, and unfailingly male form, with unbroken memory of past lives and inexhaustible compassion even toward those who mistreat him ("the priceless serving-attendant"). |
| 三十二相業 | the karma producing the thirty-two marks | introduced in fascicle 18: cultivated only in Jambudvīpa, only in a male body, only through volition arising directly in a buddha's presence ("cultivation consisting in thought"), across a full hundred eons (Śākyamuni compressed this to 91 through exceptional diligence) — each mark requiring a hundred units of merit, whose precise measure the treatise leaves unsettled among three competing accounts. |
| 三無數劫 | the three incalculable eons | introduced in fascicle 18: Śākyamuni's bodhisattva preparation, during which he venerated 75,000 / 76,000 / 77,000 buddhas respectively, encountering Ratnaśikhin, Dīpaṃkara, and Vipaśyin at the close of each eon (in reverse order), and — at his very first arousal of the resolve to buddhahood — an earlier buddha also named Śākyamuni, whom he took as his direct inspiration and namesake. |
| 六波羅蜜多 completion | the six pāramitās, completed at four milestone moments | introduced in fascicle 18: giving (universal compassionate giving, down to eyes and marrow) is completed alone; discipline and patience together (enduring the body being cut apart without anger); vigorous effort alone (praising the buddha Tiṣya for seven days without lowering a raised foot, leaping nine eons ahead); concentration and wisdom together (the diamond-like concentration immediately preceding awakening). |
| 三福業事（施／戒／修） | the three grounds for meritorious deeds: giving / precepts / cultivation | introduced in fascicle 18, paralleling the ten-karma-paths analysis: each ground may count as "merit," "meritorious karma," "ground of merit," or combinations thereof depending on which specific element (bodily/verbal karma vs. its initiating volition vs. concurrent mental factors) is under discussion. Giving's essential nature is the act of relinquishing (not the object given), done for the sake of honoring or benefiting another — not out of fear, hope of advantage, or greed. |
| 施主／財／田 三異 | how giver, gift, and recipient field each shape giving's result | introduced in fascicle 18: the giver's faith/precepts/learning and manner of giving (reverent/hands-on/timely/harmless) determine respect/enjoyment/timeliness/security of the resulting wealth; the gift's color/fragrance/taste/texture determine beauty/reputation/popularity/bodily comfort; the field's destiny/suffering/gratitude/virtue multiply the result (e.g. 100x for an animal, 1000x for a precept-breaker, 100,000,000x for a precept-holder). A bad field still yields a good result if the giver's intent is good — "the seed and its fruit are not reversed by the soil" — though a bad field may diminish the fruit's quantity. |
| 造業／增長業 | created karma vs. "increased" (fully consummated) karma | introduced in fascicle 18: karma becomes "increased" only once five conditions are met — careful deliberation, completion (reaching whatever threshold of acts triggers a bad rebirth), absence of remorse or countermeasure, having accomplices, and certainty of ripening. Distinct from the "determinate/indeterminate" ripening-time distinction introduced in fascicle 15. |
| 業輕重六因 | the six factors determining a karma's weight | introduced in fascicle 18: follow-up action, the field, the root act, the preparatory action, the volition, and the underlying disposition — whichever factors are of upper or lower grade determine whether the resulting karma is a correspondingly higher or lower grade. |
| 順福分／順解脫分／順決擇分 | wholesome dharmas conducive to merit / to liberation / to penetrating insight | introduced in fascicle 18: merit-conducive dharmas bring a desirable worldly result; liberation-conducive dharmas definitely lead to nirvāṇa (marked by hair standing on end and weeping upon hearing the Dharma's core teachings, like sprouts revealing a hidden seed); penetration-conducive dharmas are specifically the four aids to penetration (warmth, summit, forbearance, supreme worldly dharma), explained at greater length later in the treatise. |

| 隨眠 | anuśaya / latent tendency | introduced in fascicle 19, opening Chapter 5 (分別隨眠品, the Anuśaya-nirdeśa): the dormant, latent afflictions without which karma has no power to generate further existence. Six roots — greed, hatred, conceit, ignorance, views, doubt — expanding to seven (splitting greed into desire-greed and existence-greed), ten (splitting views into five), and finally ninety-eight (crossed with three realms and five categories of abandonment). Genuine three-way debate over its essential nature: Vaibhāṣika hold it simply is the affliction itself; Sautrāntika (favored by the treatise) hold it is a "seed" — a functional capacity neither associated with nor dissociated from mind, dormant as anuśaya and manifest as paryavasthāna (entanglement) upon activation, closely paralleling the "perfuming" (vāsanā) theory of unmanifested karma from fascicle 13. |
| 五見（薩迦耶見／邊執見／邪見／見取／戒禁取） | the five views: self-view / extreme views / wrong view / view-attachment / precept-and-vow attachment | introduced in fascicle 19: self-view clings to "I"/"mine" in the five aggregates; extreme views cling to permanence or annihilation of that presumed self; wrong view categorically denies karma/its-fruit/the-noble-ones (judged the single worst kind of error, since it subtracts real things rather than merely adding unreal ones); view-attachment clings to an inferior (contaminated) view as supreme; precept-and-vow attachment clings to an ineffective practice as a genuine cause of purity or path to liberation. |
| 四顛倒（常／樂／淨／我） | the four inversions: permanence / pleasure / purity / self | introduced in fascicle 19: mistaking the impermanent for permanent, suffering for pleasurable, impure for pure, and non-self for self — each drawn from a specific one of the five views (extreme views for permanence; view-attachment for pleasure/purity; self-view for self). Strictly speaking only the view itself is "inverted"; accompanying perception and mind are inverted only in a borrowed, secondary sense. Permanently eliminated at stream-entry. |
| 慢七種／九種 | conceit's seven kinds / alternative ninefold classification | introduced in fascicle 19: ordinary conceit, excessive conceit, conceit-of-excessive-conceit, self-conceit, conceit of overestimation, deficiency-conceit, wrong conceit — with the root treatise's alternative ninefold scheme derived by crossing three of these seven (conceit, excessive conceit, deficiency-conceit) against whether the comparison object is inferior, equal, or superior. |
| 遍行隨眠 | pervasive anuśayas | introduced in fascicle 19: eleven anuśayas (seven views, two doubts, two independent-ignorances — those abandoned by seeing suffering and origin) capable of rapidly extending across all five abandonment-categories within their own realm/stage, not simultaneously but in rapid succession. Nine of these eleven (excluding self-view and extreme views, which can only ever take one's own aggregates as object) can additionally take a higher realm/stage as object, though never a lower one. |
| 隨增（所緣隨增／相應隨增） | latent increase: through object / through association | introduced in fascicle 19: the mechanism by which an unabandoned anuśaya's grip continues to strengthen — through an object already "appropriated" by that stage's self-view/craving (like moisture soaking into cloth already exposed to it), or through the mental states it is directly associated with. Uncontaminated and higher-stage objects, even when technically taken as object, generate no latent increase, since they were never appropriated as "mine" to begin with. |
| 有身見無記（俱生／分別生） | why (innate) self-view can count as morally neutral | introduced in fascicle 19: self-view, extreme views, and their associated delusion in the desire realm are classified as neutral rather than unwholesome, since they don't conflict with generosity/precepts, concern only one's own affairs, and the annihilationist extreme view even "accords with liberation" in a limited sense (praised by the Buddha as the best non-Buddhist view, per fascicle 19). Refined further: only innate/spontaneous self-view (as in animals) is neutral; deliberately reasoned-through self-view is unwholesome. |
| 四種記（一向記／分別記／反詰記／捨置記） | the four ways to answer a question: categorical / analytical / counter-questioning / setting-aside | introduced in fascicle 19: distinct from, but the source of, the sūtras' famous "fourteen neutral matters" (the metaphysical questions the Buddha declined to answer) — that sūtra usage of "neutral" refers specifically to the setting-aside category, not to the unrelated technical sense of "morally neutral" (無記) used throughout this chapter's classification of the anuśayas. |

| 三世實有 | the Sarvāstivāda doctrine that past, present, and future dharmas all truly exist | introduced in fascicle 20 as this school's own namesake doctrine ("the teaching that all exists"), defended via scripture (the Buddha's teaching on renunciation toward past form) and logic (consciousness always needs a real object) — then subjected to sustained Sautrāntika critique across the fascicle: the "thirteenth sense-base" reductio, the lamp analogy for how "past/future exist" really just means having-once-existed/certain-to-exist, and the treatise's own closing verdict that literal three-times realism is "not properly teaching in accordance with the sacred doctrine." |
| 法救／妙音／世友／覺天 四家三世說 | the four masters' accounts of how a dharma persists across time: class (Dharmatrāta) / characteristic (Ghoṣaka) / state (Vasumitra) / relativity (Buddhadeva) | introduced in fascicle 20: Dharmatrāta's "class" theory (a reshaped gold vessel) is rejected as indistinguishable from Sāṃkhya transformation; Ghoṣaka's "characteristic" theory (a man attached to one of several wives) is rejected as leaving the three times entangled; Buddhadeva's "relativity" theory (a woman who is both mother and daughter) is rejected as requiring one moment to be all three times at once; Vasumitra's "state/activity" theory (a counting-token's positional value) is judged best, then itself subjected to the treatise's sharpest critique — if substance is eternal, nothing coherently obstructs its activity, and the very meaning of "time" collapses. |
| 十六法十六識 | the sixteen dharma-categories and sixteen consciousness-types | introduced in fascicle 20 as a combinatorial framework (three realms × five abandonment-categories, plus the uncontaminated) for systematically working out which type of consciousness can take which category of dharma as its object — used as scaffolding for the treatise's worked example of how many anuśayas undergo latent increase with respect to the pleasure faculty (7), a consciousness taking it as object (12), and a further consciousness taking that consciousness as object (14). |
| 十隨眠次第生 | the sequence in which the ten anuśayas typically arise | introduced in fascicle 20: ignorance → doubt → wrong view → self-view → extreme views → precept-and-vow attachment → view-attachment → greed → conceit → hatred, each drawing forth the next — tracing how confusion about the truths escalates through settled wrong view, a defended self-identity built around that view, and finally pride and hostility toward those who disagree. Describes only the typical sequence; afflictions arising outside it follow no fixed order. |
| 漏／瀑流／軛／取 | outflow (āsrava) / flood (ogha) / yoke (yoga) / clinging (upādāna) | introduced in fascicle 20 as the sūtras' four collective names for the anuśayas together with their entanglements: outflow (3 kinds: desire, existence, ignorance — delusion excluded from the first two as too passive, ignorance singled out as "the root of all existence"); flood and yoke (4 kinds each, identical substance, splitting out views since their sharpness disqualifies them from "outflow" specifically); clinging (4 kinds: desire, views, precept-and-vow, self-doctrine — precept-and-vow split out as uniquely deceiving both householders and renunciants; ignorance excluded as lacking the active "grasping" character clinging requires, though it rides along when combined with other afflictions). |

| 九結 | the nine bonds (saṃyojana) | introduced in fascicle 21: desire, hatred, conceit, ignorance, view, clinging, doubt, jealousy, and stinginess — the sūtras' further collective name for the anuśayas and entanglements. The three views (self-view, extreme views, wrong view) are grouped into a single "view-bond" since they share a common defiled character; the two kinds of clinging (view-attachment, precept-and-vow attachment) form a separate "clinging-bond" since both cling to something inferior as supreme; jealousy and stinginess alone, among all the entanglements, are elevated to bonds in their own right for being especially disruptive to social harmony and to generosity respectively. |
| 五順下分結／五順上分結 | the five lower-portion bonds / the five upper-portion bonds | introduced in fascicle 21: the lower-portion bonds (self-view, precept-and-vow attachment, doubt, desire-craving, ill will) bind beings to the desire realm specifically; the upper-portion bonds (craving for form, craving for the formless, restlessness, conceit, ignorance) bind beings to the two upper realms, persisting even in non-returners. |
| 三縛 | the three bondages | introduced in fascicle 21: greed, hatred, and ignorance — a threefold summary of affliction distinguished from "secondary affliction" (the remaining, less fundamental afflictive factors) mainly by scope and centrality rather than by a sharp categorical line. |
| 八 (或十) 纏，溯其根本 | the eight (or ten) entanglements, traced to their roots | introduced in fascicle 21: torpor, restlessness, drowsiness, remorse, jealousy, stinginess, lack of shame, and lack of conscience (with concealment and flattery/deceit sometimes added to make ten); the treatise traces concealment's root to an unresolved debate (greed or ignorance or both) rather than assigning it a single settled cause. |
| 六垢 | the six affliction-stains | introduced in fascicle 21: a further sūtra-derived collective term for a subset of secondary afflictions, distinguished by which are abandoned specifically by seeing the truths versus by cultivation. |
| 蓋（五蓋：欲貪／瞋恚／惛眠／掉悔／疑） | the five hindrances (nīvaraṇa): desire-and-greed / ill will / torpor-and-drowsiness / restlessness-and-remorse / doubt | introduced in fascicle 21: torpor-and-drowsiness and restlessness-and-remorse are each combined into a single hindrance because each pair shares the same "food" (what sustains it), "cure" (its counteragent), and function; the treatise gives three competing accounts of why exactly these five are chosen (correspondence to the five path-aggregates, in two rival orderings; a sequential five-cause account tied to meditative practice) without declaring a winner among them. |
| 斷四因（遍知所緣／斷能緣／斷所緣／對治起） | the four causes by which an affliction is severed: full knowledge of the object / severing what takes it as object / severing the object itself / the arising of a counteragent | introduced in fascicle 21: afflictions abandoned by seeing the truths are severed by the first three causes; afflictions abandoned by cultivation are severed by the fourth alone. |
| 四對治（斷／持／遠／厭） | the four counteragents: severance / maintenance / distancing / aversion | introduced in fascicle 21: the verse's own stated order (severance, maintenance, distancing, aversion) is explicitly said by the commentary not to match how they actually unfold in practice, where aversion in fact comes first — an unresolved discrepancy between verse and commentary that the text does not reconcile. |
| 遠性四種（相／治／處／時） | the four kinds of "distance": characteristic / counteragent / place / time | introduced in fascicle 21 to explain the "distancing" counteragent; temporal distance is ultimately explained not as duration but as separation from a dharma's own intrinsic characteristic (未得/已捨自相) — closely paralleling Vasumitra's "state/activity" account of the three times from fascicle 20. |
| 遍知（智遍知／斷遍知，九種） | full understanding (parijñā): understanding-by-knowledge / understanding-by-severance, in nine kinds | introduced in fascicle 21: six kinds correspond to the six seeing-abandonment severances (grouped by realm and by which of the four truths), three correspond to the three cultivation-abandonment severances (named the exhaustion of the lower-portion bonds, of craving for form, and of all bonds); a full understanding requires the joint presence of specific conditions (undefiled separation, freedom from the peak of existence, extinguishing the "double cause," and — for the three knowledge-fruits only — transcending an entire realm). |

| 分別賢聖品 | Distinguishing the Noble Ones | Chapter 6, opened in fascicle 22 (Ārya-pudgala-nirdeśa) — shifts from the anuśayas themselves (Chapter 5) to the path and the persons who walk it. |
| 見道／修道 | the path of seeing / the path of cultivation | introduced in fascicle 22: the path of seeing is exclusively undefiled, since it swiftly counters all three realms' afflictions at once; the path of cultivation, unfolding gradually, can be either undefiled or worldly. |
| 三苦（苦苦／行苦／壞苦） | the three kinds of suffering: the suffering of suffering / of formations / of change | introduced in fascicle 22: every contaminated formation is suffering by union with one of these three — displeasing formations via suffering-of-suffering, pleasing formations via suffering-of-change, and all remaining (neutral) formations via suffering-of-formations, this last layer perceptible only to the noble ones (illustrated by the eyelash-on-palm-versus-eye simile). |
| 樂受實有論辯 | the debate over whether pleasure genuinely exists | introduced in fascicle 22: some schools hold all feeling is ultimately suffering (citing "all feelings are suffering" and the unreliable-causes argument); the Abhidharma masters (and this treatise) hold pleasure is real, reconciling the "all feeling is suffering" sūtra via hidden intention (密意, tied to impermanence) rather than literal denial, using the Ānanda dialogue's question-direction as textual evidence. |
| 二諦（世俗諦／勝義諦） | the two truths: conventional truth / ultimate truth | introduced in fascicle 22: a thing is conventional if the cognition of it ceases once physically broken apart or analytically resolved by wisdom (a jar, water); it is ultimate if that cognition survives such decomposition (form's partless ultimate particles, feeling, etc.) — the classical Abhidharma formulation later reinterpreted by Madhyamaka for different purposes. |
| 生因／起因（業／愛） | cause of coming-into-being / cause of continuation: karma / craving | introduced in fascicle 22: karma is the "seed," determining a rebirth's specific kind (realm, destiny); craving is the "water," sustaining continuation indifferently to kind — hence craving alone is named "the origin" in some sūtras (the more prominent, continuation-determining cause), even though karma and ignorance are also, properly, causes. |
| 三慧（聞／思／修所成慧） | the three wisdoms: born of hearing / reflection / meditation | introduced in fascicle 22: distinguished by object (name alone / name and meaning / meaning alone) via the deep-water swimming simile, but properly classified by their generating cause (hearing an authoritative teaching / correct reasoning / meditative cultivation), as the "grass-fed ox" naming analogy clarifies. |
| 四聖種 | the four noble lineages | introduced in fascicle 22: contentment with whatever robes, food, and bedding one receives (the first three), plus delight in abandonment and cultivation (the fourth) — antidotes to four kinds of craving-arising described in a sūtra on robes/food/bedding/existence, the first three quieting craving temporarily and the fourth eliminating it permanently. |
| 不淨觀（骨鎖觀三位） | impurity-contemplation: the three stages of skeleton-contemplation | introduced in fascicle 22, one of the two gates of entering cultivation (for desire-dominated temperaments): initial practice (expand the visualized skeleton-field outward to the ocean's edge, then contract back to one skeleton), matured cultivation (remove bones down to half a skull), and transcending mental application (fix the mind between the eyebrows) — imaginative mental application that suppresses but does not sever affliction. |
| 持息念（六因：數隨止觀轉淨） | mindfulness of breathing: the six factors of counting, following, fixing, observing, turning, purifying | introduced in fascicle 22, the second gate of entering cultivation (for thought-dominated temperaments): named "mindfulness" though its essential nature is wisdom, sustained by mindfulness; unlike impurity-contemplation, uses genuine (non-imaginative) mental application, exclusive to those within the Buddha's teaching. |

| 四念住 | the four foundations of mindfulness: body, feeling, mind, dharmas | introduced in fascicle 23: cultivated for insight after calm-abiding is established, observed through both specific characteristics (自相) and shared characteristics (共相: impermanence, suffering, non-self); each has a threefold structure (own-nature/wisdom, mixed-with-associates, object-based), progressing from unmixed objects to a fully combined dharma-mindfulness observing all four together. |
| 四善根（煖／頂／忍／世第一法） | the four wholesome roots: warmth / summit / patience / supreme mundane dharma | introduced in fascicle 23, collectively "favorable to the decisive distinction" (順決擇分): warmth and summit fully observe all four truths through sixteen aspects and remain "movable" (retrogressible); patience narrows at its upper grade to a single truth (suffering), single realm (desire); supreme mundane dharma matches that narrowed scope for one instant, immediately preceding the path of seeing — each securing a specific guarantee (eventual nirvāṇa; wholesome roots never cut off; no bad rebirth; direct uninterrupted entry into noble status). |
| 十六行相 | the sixteen aspects | introduced in fascicle 23: four aspects per truth — suffering (impermanence, suffering, emptiness, non-self), origin (cause, origin, arising, condition), cessation (cessation, peace, excellence, freedom), path (path, suchness, practice, deliverance) — cultivated in full from the wholesome root of warmth onward. |
| 十六心（聖諦現觀） | the sixteen mind-moments: direct realization of the noble truths | introduced in fascicle 23: immediately following supreme mundane dharma, dharma-patience/dharma-knowledge/analogy-patience/analogy-knowledge arise in that order for each of the four truths in turn (suffering, origin, cessation, path) — sixteen moments total; patience always functions as the "uninterrupted path," knowledge as the "path of liberation." Only the first fifteen belong to the path of seeing (each sees a truth never seen before); the sixteenth (analogy-knowledge regarding the path) already belongs to the path of cultivation, since nothing remains unseen for the first time. |
| 三種現觀（見／緣／事） | the three kinds of direct realization: by seeing / by object / by matter | introduced in fascicle 23 to refute other schools' claim that all four truths are realized instantaneously: only "by seeing" requires the full sixteen-moment gradual sequence (confirmed by explicit sūtra citation); "by object" and "by matter" can validly support looser "instantaneous" claims (mastery over remaining truths; severing/realizing/cultivating already set in motion upon seeing suffering). |
| 隨信行／隨法行 → 信解／見至 | followers of faith / followers of dharma → convinced-through-faith / attained-through-seeing | introduced in fascicle 23: dull- and sharp-faculty noble ones respectively, within the fifteen moments of the path of seeing; upon reaching the sixteenth moment (path of cultivation), they take new names reflecting the same faculty distinction, no longer "following" but "abiding" in a fruit. |
| 向果／住果（預流／一來／不還向） | aiming toward a fruit / abiding in a fruit | introduced in fascicle 23: how much cultivation-abandoned affliction one had already severed via a worldly path before the path of seeing determines which of the three fruits (stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning) one is "aiming toward"; upon reaching the sixteenth moment, one "abides in" that same fruit — never arhatship, which the path of seeing alone cannot yet secure. |
| 預流（極七返） | the stream-enterer: guaranteed at most seven more returns | introduced in fascicle 23: one who has severed no cultivation-abandoned affliction; "stream" names the undefiled paths collectively, "first" the first entrant. Vaibhāṣika masters read "seven returns" as seven within each of the human and heavenly realms separately (28 total, corroborated by a Kāśyapīya sūtra citation), not seven combined. Called one who "never falls back" and "makes an end of suffering" — illustrated by the iron-ball-versus-iron-bowl verse (identical weight, different outcome, depending on the shape wisdom gives it). |

| 家家／一間 | family-to-family / one-interval | introduced in fascicle 24: a stream-enterer advancing toward once-returning, with two or three desire-realm rebirths left (heavenly or human family-to-family); a once-returner advancing toward non-returning, with exactly one rebirth left (one-interval) — these titles apply only to gradual post-path-of-seeing advancement, not to practitioners who entered already partially detached. |
| 不還七種（中／生／有行／無行般涅槃／上流／行無色四／現般） | the sevenfold non-returner | introduced in fascicle 24: intermediate-existence, birth, with-effort, without-effort nirvāṇa (form realm); the "upstream" type (mixed-cultivation → Akaniṣṭha via complete/half leap/full traverse, or no mixed-cultivation → peak of existence); four formless-realm types; and nirvāṇa in the present life — sevenfold by sūtra count, though fine analysis of just one sub-type (intermediate-existence) alone yields 2,592 combinations, and the five form-realm types together total 12,960. |
| 雜修靜慮 | mixed cultivation of the dhyānas | introduced in fascicle 24: alternating undefiled and defiled mind-moments within the fourth dhyāna, narrowing from many, to blocks of two, to a single defiled moment sandwiched between two undefiled ones — the technique underlying the "upstream" non-returner's ascent, practiced for rebirth, present pleasure, or to prevent retrogression. |
| 身證 | body-witness (kāyasākṣin) | introduced in fascicle 24: a non-returner who has attained cessation-concentration (nirodha-samāpatti); named for the body specifically because mind is entirely absent during that concentration — the actual "witnessing" occurs retrospectively, upon emerging, when a now-conscious body registers a profound, nirvāṇa-like stillness never experienced before. Not counted among the eighteen kinds of noble-ones-in-training, since cessation-concentration is neither a training nor a training's fruit. |
| 金剛喻定 | the diamond-like concentration (vajropama-samādhi) | introduced in fascicle 24: the ninth and final uninterrupted path, severing the peak of existence's last grade of affliction; possesses the functional capacity to shatter every remaining latent affliction. Immediately followed by the "knowledge of exhaustion" (盡智), which yields the fruit of arhatship (no-more-learning); for the "immovable" lineage specifically, the "knowledge of non-arising" (無生智) follows instead, since such an arhat can never retrogress. |
| 沙門性／沙門果（八十九種） | asceticism's nature / asceticism's fruit, in eighty-nine kinds | introduced in fascicle 24: the undefiled path itself is asceticism's nature; its fruit is both conditioned (liberation-path moments) and unconditioned (analytical cessations) — 8 for seeing-abandonment plus 81 (9 grades × 9 stages) for cultivation-abandonment. The sūtras name only 4 "fruits," selected by five causes (relinquishing a prior path, attaining a superior one, gathering severances together, attaining eight knowledges, cultivating all sixteen aspects at once) that only stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning, and arhatship jointly satisfy. |
| 法輪（三轉十二行相） | the wheel of Dharma: three turnings, twelve aspects | introduced in fascicle 24: describes the Buddha's first sermon to Kauṇḍinya and the first five disciples. Per truth: "this is [the truth]," "this ought to be understood/severed/realized/cultivated," "this has already been [done]" — three turnings × four aspects (eye/knowledge/illumination/awakening) = twelve, applied to all four truths. Vaibhāṣika: the three turnings map to seeing/cultivation/no-more-learning; the treatise holds the whole three-turning framework, not the path of seeing alone, earns the name "wheel." Two rival accounts of the wheel-simile: dynamic qualities (speed, etc.) vs. the eightfold path mapped onto a wheel's parts (Venerable Ghoṣaka). |

| 六種阿羅漢（退／思／護／安住／堪達／不動法） | the six kinds of arhat: retrogressible / thought / protective / stable / capable-of-penetrating / immovable-dharma | introduced in fascicle 25: the first five arise from the convinced-through-faith lineage and are collectively "timely liberation" (待時解脫，must wait for favorable conditions); the sixth, immovable-dharma, arises from the attained-through-seeing lineage and is "non-timely liberation" (不待時，manifests on demand). Retrogressible-dharma is always already possessed beforehand; the other five can also be newly attained through faculty-refining (練根). Defined by a specific psychological profile each — most strikingly, "thought-dharma" (思法) constantly contemplates self-harm out of fear of retrogression, a detail the treatise records without further ethical comment. |
| 時解脫／不時解脫 | timely liberation / non-timely liberation | introduced in fascicle 25 alongside the six kinds of arhat (see above); also the pair of "names" the faith-follower/dharma-follower continuum acquires at the non-trainee stage, after "faith-follower"/"dharma-follower" (path of seeing) and "convinced-through-faith"/"attained-through-seeing" (path of cultivation) — one continuous person renamed at each of three stages. |
| 阿羅漢退不退辯（毘婆沙師 vs. 經部師） | the Vaibhāṣika-Sautrāntika debate over whether arhatship itself can retrogress | introduced in fascicle 25: Vaibhāṣika hold five of the six lineages can retrogress from the fruit itself; Sautrāntika hold no retrogression from arhatship is possible at all, only from a temporary "joyful abiding in the present life" distinct from the fruit — supported by scripture, the Venerable Godhika precedent, and a closing dilemma (either the counteragent path is complete, in which case retrogression is impossible, or it isn't, in which case the person was never truly an arhat). The treatise presents both sides at comparable length without declaring a winner, closing on the Vaibhāṣika's terse restatement of their own position. |
| 練根 | faculty-refining | introduced in fascicle 25: converting from a duller to a sharper lineage. At the non-trainee stage takes nine uninterrupted/liberation paths each (long habituation); at the trainee stage, one each. Always uncontaminated; depends on a body of the three continents' humans only; non-trainees may rely on all nine grounds (excluding the peak of existence is itself included, since "nine" = not-yet-attained + intermediate dhyāna + four dhyānas + three lower formless spheres), trainees on only six (excluding the three formless spheres, since no trainee-fruit belongs to formless-realm grounds). |
| 七聖者／六事 | the seven noble ones, reducing to six real substances | introduced in fascicle 25: faith-follower, dharma-follower, convinced-through-faith, attained-through-seeing, body-witness, wisdom-liberated, both-liberated. The first two/next two/timely-non-timely-liberated names track two continuous persons (dull-faith type, sharp-wisdom type) renamed at each of three stages (seeing/cultivation/non-trainee) — six named stages for two lives. Body-witness, wisdom-liberated, and both-liberated are cross-cutting labels (cessation-concentration attained; concentration paired with wisdom or not) rather than further substances. The faith-follower alone divides into a stated total of "one hundred million, forty-seven thousand, eight hundred twenty-five" combinatorial kinds (根×性×道×離染×依身); we could not independently reproduce this figure from the stated factors (our own multiplication gives 147,825) and have flagged, not silently corrected, the discrepancy. |
| 三十七菩提分法 | the thirty-seven limbs of awakening (bodhipakṣa-dharma) | introduced in fascicle 25: four foundations of mindfulness, four right exertions, four bases of supernormal power, five faculties, five powers, seven awakening-factors, eightfold noble path — reduce to ten real substances (wisdom, diligence, concentration, faith, mindfulness, joy, equanimity, pliancy, precept, inquiry), or eleven on the Vaibhāṣika's count (splitting precept into bodily/verbal). Awakening-factors and path-factors are exclusively uncontaminated (established only at the paths of seeing/cultivation); the other five groups are common to both contaminated and uncontaminated. Ground-by-ground count shrinks from all 37 in the first dhyāna down to 22 in the desire realm and at the peak of existence (no uncontaminated dharma at either extreme). |
| 四證淨（佛法僧戒） | the four kinds of confirmed purity: regarding the Buddha, the Dharma, the Saṅgha, and precepts | introduced in fascicle 25: attained regarding Dharma and precepts upon seeing any of the first three truths, plus Buddha and Saṅgha upon completing the path of seeing with the truth of the path. Real substance is only two (faith; precept). Two similes for the fixed order (Buddha/Dharma/Saṅgha/precept): doctor/medicine/caregiver, and guide/road/companion/vehicle — pure moral discipline (precept) comes last because it only manifests once faith in all three jewels is complete. |
| 四道（加行／無間／解脫／勝進） | the four kinds of path: preparatory / uninterrupted / liberation / supplementary-advancement | introduced in fascicle 25 as the basic scaffolding underlying every path-of-seeing/cultivation discussion in the treatise: the uninterrupted path severs the obstruction to be severed; the liberation path is what arises first upon that severance; preparatory precedes the uninterrupted path; supplementary-advancement is everything else. |
| 四通行（苦樂遲速） | the four kinds of conduct: painful/pleasant × slow/quick | introduced in fascicle 25: pleasant conduct depends on the four root dhyānas (calm and insight balanced); painful conduct depends on the formless spheres, not-yet-attained stage, or intermediate dhyāna (calm and insight uneven); each further divides by dull (slow) or sharp (quick) faculty. |

| 十智 | the ten kinds of knowledge | introduced in fascicle 26, opening Chapter 7 (分別智品, Distinguishing Knowledge): worldly (世俗智), dharma (法智, desire realm), subsequent (類智, upper realms), the four truth-specific knowledges (苦/集/滅/道智), knowledge of others' minds (他心智), exhaustion (盡智), and non-arising (無生智) — the last two being the same six knowledges again, renamed once possessed by a non-trainee with the self-referential "I have already known..." judgment. Forbearance (忍) is view but not knowledge (still-unresolved doubt); exhaustion/non-arising are knowledge but not view (no further investigation); the remaining eight are both. |
| 他心智的限制 | knowledge of others' minds' built-in limits | introduced in fascicle 26: never knows a mind superior in ground, faculty, or stage; never past or future minds; dharma-type and subsequent-type never know each other; never occurs during the path of seeing itself (though minds there can be its object from outside); grasps only a mind or mental factor's own particular characteristic, never what that mind is thinking about or how. Śrāvakas/pratyekabuddhas/the Buddha differ sharply in how much of a stranger's sixteen-moment path of seeing they can track (two moments / three or fifteen / all sixteen, respectively). |
| 十六行相 | the sixteen aspects | introduced in fascicle 26: four each for suffering (impermanent/suffering/empty/non-self), origination (cause/origin/arising/condition), cessation (cessation/calm/excellence/release), and path (path/suchness/practice/deliverance) — each aspect a targeted counteragent to one specific wrong view (e.g. "impermanent" counters the view of permanence). Real substance is sixteen, not seven (rejecting a minority view collapsing origination/cessation/path to one substance each). Multiple traditional glosses exist side by side; the treatise offers its own preferred explanation alongside at least two older ones without fully harmonizing them. |
| 十八愛行／執我十八種 | the eighteen kinds of self-grasping craving | introduced in fascicle 26 while glossing origination's four aspects: five forms each of grasping a present-total or future-total self, four forms each of grasping a future-particular or continuously-reborn self (5+5+4+4=18), each further modulated by "definitely," "in such-and-such a way," "subject to change," or bare existence/non-existence. Matches the classical aṭṭhārasa taṇhāvicaritāni scheme. |
| 修智地位表 | the knowledge-cultivation tables | introduced in fascicle 26: an extended combinatorial account of exactly how many of the ten knowledges are presently manifesting versus cultivated for the future at every stage of practice — the path of seeing's fifteen moments, the path of cultivation's detachment process (by ground and path-type: preparatory/uninterrupted/liberation/supplementary-advancement), the first moment of arhatship (nine or ten, by faculty), faculty-refining, mixed cultivation of the dhyānas, and the supernormal powers. Worldly knowledge and knowledge of others' minds behave as a see-saw at the peak of existence specifically. The very first moment of exhaustion-knowledge alone cultivates all nine grounds' contaminated virtues at once (the "newly crowned king" simile) — but a being born directly into a higher realm never cultivates the lower grounds this way. |
| 四種修（得修／習修／對治修／除遣修） | the four kinds of cultivation: possession / practice / counteragent / elimination | introduced in fascicle 26, closing Chapter 7's first half: possession- and practice-cultivation apply to wholesome conditioned dharmas; counteragent- and elimination-cultivation apply to contaminated dharmas; a contaminated wholesome dharma has all four. "Cultivation" strictly means newly securing something not previously possessed through genuine effort — regaining something once relinquished does not count, since it lacks that same causal power. The foreign masters' proposed sixth and fifth categories (guarding the senses, contemplating the body) are rejected by the Kashmiri masters as already covered by counteragent-/elimination-cultivation. |

| 十八不共法 | the eighteen dharmas unique to a Buddha | introduced in fascicle 27: the ten powers (十力), four fearlessnesses (四無畏), three foundations of mindfulness (三念住), and great compassion (大悲) — cultivated only at the moment of a Buddha's first exhaustion-knowledge, possessed by no other noble one. Illustrated by the Śāriputra/hunted-dove episode (even the wisest arhat's knowledge is finite) and the Buddha's Nārāyaṇa-level bodily strength (sevenfold legendary-strongman scale: elephant → musk elephant → Mahānāgna → Praseṇajit → Baladeva → Cāṇūra → Nārāyaṇa). |
| 三種圓德（因／果／恩） | the three perfect virtues: cause / fruit / beneficence | introduced in fascicle 27 as a devotional passage inspiring "love and reverence": cause = four qualities of bodhisattva-path cultivation (without remainder, over three incalculable kalpas, uninterrupted, without arrogance); fruit = knowledge/severance/majestic-power/physical-body, each fourfold; beneficence = liberating beings from the three evil destinies. Closes with the "great mountain of treasure" simile and the Buddha's own verse: "if, in the Buddha's field of merit, one plants even a small portion of goodness, one first obtains a superior destiny, and afterward certainly attains nirvāṇa." |
| 共功德（無諍／願智／四無礙解） | qualities shared with other noble ones: non-contention / knowledge of aspiration / the four unimpeded understandings | introduced in fascicle 27: non-contention is an arhat's preemptive knowledge preventing others' affliction from arising by taking himself as object (immovable lineage only, per fascicle 25's six kinds of arhat); knowledge of aspiration extends this to any object; the four unimpeded understandings (dharma/meaning/language/eloquence) always arrive together as a set once any one is attained. All six depend on "boundary" concentration (邊際定, a perfected fourth dhyāna, both "universally accommodating" across every ground and "increasing to the ultimate" within the fourth dhyāna's own nine sub-grades) — attained by other noble ones only through deliberate effort, but by the Buddha instantaneously and completely at his first exhaustion-knowledge. |
| 六通 | the six supernormal powers | introduced in fascicle 27: supernormal locomotion (神境), the divine eye (天眼), the divine ear (天耳), knowledge of others' minds (他心), recollection of past lives (宿住), and exhaustion of contaminants (漏盡) — the last noble-ones-only, the first five also available to ordinary beings. Reach across world-systems scales by attainer (śrāvaka/pratyekabuddha/Buddha: 1000/2000/3000, or 2000/3000/countless at maximal effort). Acquired variously through cultivation, birth, spells, medicine, karma, or (for others'-minds specifically) physiognomy — no birth-acquired form exists among humans. The last three (recollection of past lives, death-and-rebirth, exhaustion of contaminants) are honored with the separate name "the three insights" (三明) only when possessed by a non-trainee, since a trainee's confusion always eventually reasserts itself. |
| 天眼天耳 | the divine eye and divine ear | introduced in fascicle 27 as genuine physical faculties, not mere clairvoyance: "pure form" (淨色) made of a dhyāna-ground's subtle great elements, existing alongside the ordinary eye/ear. Three sourcing types: cultivation-acquired (the real, defined faculty), birth-acquired (gods), and "divine-like" (似天 — nāgas, spirits, a wheel-turning king's treasure-minister, bodhisattvas, intermediate-existence beings — resembling but not sharing the dhyāna-based substance). Morally neutral (associated with eye-/ear-consciousness), which the treatise reconciles with "depends on the four dhyānas" by locating the dependency in the supporting faculty, not the neutral consciousness itself. |
| 能化心十四 | the fourteen transformation-minds | introduced in fascicle 27: the fruit (not the power itself) of supernormal locomotion — each of the four root dhyānas supports a transformation-mind reaching its own ground and every ground below (2+3+4+5=14). Morally neutral; cannot emerge "directly" from concentration the way an ordinary meditator does, but must pass through a dedicated chain of transformation-minds (the "door" simile). Illustrated by the Venerable Mahākāśyapa's skeletal remains, preserved (by his own aspiration-power, or by deities, per a recorded disagreement) until the future Buddha Maitreya's era. |

| 十八支十一實事 | eighteen branch-names, eleven real substances | introduced in fascicle 28, opening Chapter 8 (分別定品第八之一, Distinguishing Concentration): the four dhyānas' eighteen named branch-factors reduce to eleven real substances by an overlap-counting method; the first two dhyānas' "bliss" branch is argued to be pliancy (輕安), not a feeling, since the five sense-consciousnesses are absent in dhyāna and a single mind cannot hold two feelings at once — defended against a rival scriptural citation (a sūtra listing delight/joy/pliancy/bliss/concentration as five separate items) via a "pliancy-wind" mechanism generating genuine bodily bliss without contradicting that absence. |
| 內等淨（信根） | inner equanimous purity, identified with the faith-faculty | introduced in fascicle 28: the second dhyāna's distinctive branch, replacing investigation and inquiry; argued not to be a separate real substance but identical with the faith-faculty (信根) already established among the wholesome mental factors — parallel to the "bliss is pliancy" identification made for the first two dhyānas. |
| 味／淨／無漏三等至 | the three varieties of attainment: tainted / pure / uncontaminated | introduced in fascicle 28: every root attainment (the four dhyānas, four formless spheres) admits of these three, except the peak of existence, which lacks the uncontaminated (too "dim and weak" to support it). Tainted is acquired through birth or falling back; pure through freedom from affliction or birth; uncontaminated only through freedom from affliction. Only uncontaminated concentration and the pure approach-concentrations can actually sever affliction — the root pure attainments cannot. A detailed combinatorial scheme governs which attainment, once present, can generate which others (never leaping more than three levels). |
| 四種淨定（順退分／順住分／順勝進分／順決擇分） | the four kinds of pure concentration: conducive to regression / stability / distinction / the decisive discrimination | introduced in fascicle 28: only the fourth kind can generate uncontaminated attainment, since it alone "conforms to" the uncontaminated; the other three conform respectively to affliction, one's own ground, and the ground above. The peak of existence lacks the third (no higher ground to advance toward). |
| 超等至 | the practice of "transcending" attainments | introduced in fascicle 28: an advanced technique of moving through the eight attainments forward/reverse, uniformly/mixing contaminated with uncontaminated, first level-by-level and then by skipping exactly one level — never two. Restricted to human beings of the three continents who are arhats both fully free of affliction and possessing complete mastery over concentration (excluding those "liberated in due season" and those "attained through view," each missing one of the two requirements). |
| 中間靜慮 | the intermediate dhyāna | introduced in fascicle 28: a distinct meditative ground between the first and second dhyānas, possessing inquiry (伺) without investigation (尋) — the only ground anywhere in the eight attainments admitting this kind of internal, non-canonical subdivision. Possesses all three varieties (tainted/pure/uncontaminated); cultivating it extensively yields rebirth as Mahābrahmā. |
| 三解脫門（空／無願／無相）及三重等持 | the three gates of liberation (emptiness / wishlessness / signlessness) and their "doubled" forms | introduced in fascicle 28: each keyed to specific aspects of the Four Truths (emptiness: emptiness/non-self; signlessness: the four aspects of cessation, since nirvāṇa is free of ten "marks"; wishlessness: the remaining ten aspects, all regarded as ultimately to be relinquished, path included). The uncontaminated variety alone is called a "gate of liberation." The doubled forms (空空／無願無願／無相無相) take a previously-cultivated uncontaminated samādhi itself as object, to strip away attachment to the attainment as an accomplishment — solely contaminated, restricted to the same narrow class of practitioner as the transcending-attainment practice. |

| 四無量（慈悲喜捨） | the four immeasurables: loving-kindness, compassion, joy, equanimity | introduced in fascicle 29, opening Chapter 8's second half (分別定品第八之二): each an antidote to a specific obstruction (malice, lack of rejoicing, sensual craving, hatred); defended against the "isn't this a delusion" objection since the aspiration is sincere and free of distortion even though it cannot literally make anyone happy; cultivated only by humans, first via a seven-grade method (three grades of loved ones, one neutral, three of enemies) before geographic scope expands; only suppress, never sever, affliction. |
| 八解脫 | the eight liberations | introduced in fascicle 29: two impurity-contemplations of external form, one purity-contemplation, the four formless attainments, and cessation-attainment as the eighth — entering the eighth via a "subtle-subtle mind" (微微心), the most refined mental state immediately preceding cessation. Third dhyāna alone has no liberation of its own, since its peak bliss would destabilize the practice. |
| 八勝處／十遍處 | the eight mastery-bases / ten totality-bases | introduced in fascicle 29, mapped directly onto the liberations but each a further advance: a liberation only lets one turn away from an object, a mastery-base lets one actively control it (contemplate it however desired without affliction arising), and a totality-base saturates awareness with it completely, without any gap. Standard progression: liberation → mastery-base → totality-base. |
| 三種特殊因緣（因力／業力／法爾力） | three special causes for higher rebirth: the power of cause / karma / natural law | introduced in fascicle 29: explains how beings already born in the form/formless realms newly give rise to a higher dhyāna — prior cultivation (因力), the ripening of birth-causing karma itself carrying enough force to draw forth the corresponding attainment (業力, since one cannot be reborn above without already being free of the affliction below), or, form realm only, the spontaneous intensification of wholesome states when a world-system approaches destruction (法爾力, absent above the level unaffected by the three calamities). |
| 破執我品第九（補特伽羅辯） | Chapter 9, Refuting the Self: the pudgala debate | begins in fascicle 29, immediately after the colophon closing the treatise's eight analytical chapters — an appended, largely versification-free prose essay refuting the Vātsīputrīya school's doctrine of a "person" (補特伽羅, pudgala) neither identical to nor different from the five aggregates. Their defense — the pudgala "depends on" the aggregates as fire depends on fuel — is dismantled by testing every available reading of "depends on" (gathering, causation, simultaneity), each collapsing into either real difference or flat identity, never the claimed third option. Followed by an extended sweep of scriptural citation (the Sūtra on the Person, the twelve sense-spheres, King Bimbisāra, the arhat nun Śailā's "chariot" verses, the verses to Bhadrāli) establishing that "person" is only a conventional label for the aggregate-continuum — including a sharp rebuttal of the Vātsīputrīya's attempt to dismiss inconvenient, widely-attested citations as "not recited in our own canon." Closes fascicle 29 by resolving the objection that no-self would undermine the Buddha's omniscience: "all-knowing" refers to the continuum's capability, not literal simultaneous cognition of everything (the "fire consuming everything" simile). Continues into fascicle 30 with the "burden-bearer" sūtra.

| 破執我品第九之二（龜毛喻／龍軍喻／牝虎銜子喻） | Chapter 9 continues: the tortoise's fur, Nāgasena, and the tigress carrying her cub | introduced in fascicle 30, completing Chapter 9 from fascicle 29: the burden-bearer sūtra (重擔即五取蘊自逼害，荷者即前念引後念); why the Buddha refuses a whole family of ill-formed questions (life-force vs. body, is the world eternal, does the Tathāgata exist after death) — illustrated by the tortoise's-fur non-question and the elder Nāgasena's mango-tree counter-question to King Piṇḍola; and the Vatsagotra wanderer sūtra's tigress-carrying-her-cub verses, ranking denial of even the conventional self (annihilationism) as more dangerous than affirming a real self (eternalism), since it "destroys the cub of one's own wholesome deeds." |
| 記憶／認知機制（制怛羅能憶／天授能行／識帶彼相） | memory and cognition without a self: "Caitra remembers," "Devadatta walks," consciousness "bears the object's aspect" | introduced in fascicle 30: memory requires two jointly necessary conditions (attention toward the object, and a specific kind of mental differentiation as cause); a grammatical analysis of the possessive case ("whose remembering?") reduces "ownership" to a cause-and-effect relationship, illustrated by "Caitra's cattle"; "Devadatta walks" and "consciousness cognizes" both name a causal continuum's own successive arising, not an agent acting on an object — consciousness does nothing to its object, but arises "bearing that object's aspect" (帶彼相), as a fruit is said to "repay" its cause. |
| 地離香等喻（地無別有） | the earth-and-fragrance simile | introduced in fascicle 30: refuting a heterodox theory that all minds arise from a self "conjoined" with the mental faculty, an opponent claims the self supports mind and mental factors the way earth supports fragrance and the other sense qualities — Vasubandhu turns this back on them, since there is no earth apart from fragrance either, only a conventional name for a collection of qualities; the self, by the same logic, is only a name for the aggregate-continuum. |
| 業果相續轉變差別（種芽莖葉花果喻／拘櫞花色喻） | karma's continuum, transformation, and differentiation: the seed-to-fruit and citron-dye similes | introduced in fascicle 30, closing the treatise's account of karma without an agent: a fruit never arises directly from a perished seed but only through the seed's continuum passing through sprout, stem, leaf, and flower (相續: unbroken sequence; 轉變: each moment differing from the last; 差別: the final superior capacity that triggers the result) — karma works identically, and which karmic "flower" ripens first at death is determined by weight, recency, and habituation, not permanence. A ripened result cannot itself ripen again, illustrated by a citron flower dyed red: the resulting red fruit-flesh generates no further reddening. |
| 破執我品終／俱舍論三十卷圓滿 | the end of Chapter 9, and the completion of all thirty fascicles | fascicle 30 closes both Chapter 9 and the entire Abhidharmakośa with three verses on the "broad road to nirvāṇa's palace" that "a thousand sages have traveled" — the treatise's own final exhortation to seek the eye of wisdom. Ru-Yi's translation runs continuously from fascicle 1 (界品, the elements) through this closing verse, covering all nine traditional chapters per the mnemonic 界二根五世間五，業六隨三賢聖四，智二定二破我一. |

## Structure

Follow the nine traditional chapters (品) and the fascicle divisions given by the mnemonic verse 界二根五世間五，業六隨三賢聖四，智二定二破我一 — see the [Abhidharmakośa overview page]({{ '/abhidharma/abhidharmakosa/' | relative_url }}) for the full chapter-to-fascicle map. Within a fascicle, structure sections around the root verses (頌) and their prose commentary (長行), matching how the Chinese text itself alternates between the two.

## Register

- Plain English, close to the Chinese, not a loose paraphrase — matching the method already used for the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra and the Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra on this site.
- Where the Chinese compresses a technical argument into very few characters, it is fine for the English to expand slightly for clarity, but do not add explanation that belongs in a reading note instead of the translation itself.
- Mark every fascicle's status honestly (e.g. "opening section only") rather than implying more is finished than actually is.
