The treasury of Abhidharma.

The Abhidharmakośa — "Treasury of Abhidharma" — is Vasubandhu's systematic account of Sarvāstivāda scholastic Buddhism, six hundred root verses with his own prose commentary, sharpened throughout by Sautrāntika objections. Preserved in full in Xuánzàng's Chinese translation, it remains the standard entry point into Abhidharma study across every East Asian Buddhist school. This page is the entrance for reading it chapter by chapter, fascicle by fascicle.

How to read this collection

The structure below is complete: all nine chapters and all thirty fascicles of Xuánzàng's translation, mapped out using the traditional mnemonic verse that generations of students have memorized to keep the chapters and fascicle counts straight.

Ru-Yi's own plain-English translation and study notes are now complete across all thirty fascicles, from the opening chapter on the elements through the closing verses of Chapter 9's Refutation of the Self — see below for the full table of contents.

What each essay will include

  • A plain-English translation, made fresh from Xuánzàng's Chinese.
  • Key Sanskrit and Chinese technical terms, explained once and used consistently.
  • Where a passage is disputed, a note on the Sarvāstivāda position and Vasubandhu's own Sautrāntika reply.
  • Questions for reflection or class discussion.

A note on sources. This translation is made directly from the Chinese text in the Taishō canon (大正藏 T1558, vol. 29), which is in the public domain. The only complete modern English version — Leo Pruden's translation of Louis de La Vallée Poussin's French Abhidharmakośabhāṣyam (Asian Humanities Press, 1988–91) — is still under copyright and is not reproduced here; readers who want a full English edition today should seek that book out directly. Likewise, Chinese commentaries such as Yen-pei's (演培) 俱舍論頌講記 are consulted for context but not quoted at length.

The Nine Chapters

Traditionally remembered by the verse 界二根五世間五，業六隨三賢聖四，智二定二破我一 — "Elements two, Faculties five, World five; Karma six, Latencies three, Noble Persons four; Knowledge two, Attainments two, Refuting the Self one" — which gives the fascicle count for each chapter.

Dhātu-nirdeśa
分別界品
The elements: the five aggregates, twelve sense fields, and eighteen elements that make up all experience.

Indriya-nirdeśa

Loka-nirdeśa

The world: the three realms of existence, the six destinies, and the twelve links of dependent origination.

Chapter 4
Karma-nirdeśa
分別業品
Karma: the varieties of action, how it is accumulated, and how it ripens — including the precepts as a form of karma.
Fascicles 13–18
Chapter 5
Anuśaya-nirdeśa
分別隨眠品
The latent afflictions: the underlying tendencies — desire, aversion, and views — that keep beings bound to rebirth.
Fascicles 19–21
Chapter 6
Mārga-pudgala-nirdeśa
分別賢聖品
The path and the noble persons: how the path is cultivated, and the four stages of realization it leads to.
Fascicles 22–25
Chapter 7
Jñāna-nirdeśa
分別智品
Knowledge: the ten kinds of knowledge, and the qualities unique to a Buddha or an arhat.
Fascicles 26–27
Chapter 8
Samāpatti-nirdeśa
分別定品
Meditative attainments: the four dhyānas, the formless attainments, and the varieties of samādhi.
Fascicles 28–29
Chapter 9
Pudgala-viniścaya
破執我品
An appendix outside the 600 verses: a direct refutation of the Pudgalavādin claim that a real "person" underlies the aggregates.
