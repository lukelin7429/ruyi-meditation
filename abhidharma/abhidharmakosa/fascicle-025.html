The Six Kinds of Arhat

Fascicle 25 continues the fourth part of Chapter 6 (分別賢聖品第六之四, Distinguishing the Noble Ones), picking up directly from fascicle 24's account of arhatship to classify the arhat into six distinct kinds by lineage — from "retrogressible," most vulnerable to falling back, through "immovable," permanently secure — including the sūtras' own vivid, sometimes unsettling psychological portrait of how each type relates to the possibility of losing what has been attained.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress, 2 sections so far Source: Taishō T1558, vol. 29, fascicle 25 Author: Vasubandhu · Translator into Chinese: Xuánzàng

1. The Six Kinds of Arhat, and Their Origins

Continuing directly from the immovable-lineage arhat's transition from the knowledge of exhaustion to the knowledge of non-arising, the treatise asks whether arhats, like stream-enterers and the others, admit of further distinctions by lineage. They do: six kinds in total — retrogressible-dharma, thought-dharma, protective-dharma, stable-dharma, capable-of-penetrating-dharma, and immovable-dharma. The first five all arise from the "convinced-through-faith" nature already possessed at the stage of noble-one-in-training, and are collectively called "timely, cherished liberation of mind" (or simply "timely liberation," the fuller name abbreviated much as "ghee-pot" might be shortened to "pot"), since their liberation must wait upon favorable conditions converging — adequate provisions, freedom from illness, a suitable place — before concentration can even be entered. The sixth, immovable-dharma, arises instead from the "attained-through-seeing" nature, and is called "immovable liberation of mind," or "non-timely liberation," since its concentration manifests whenever desired, with no need to wait for anything.

Chinese Source

如前所說，不動應果初盡智後起無生智。諸阿羅漢如預流等有差別不？亦有。云何？頌曰：阿羅漢有六，謂退至不動，前五信解生，總名時解脫，後不時解脫，從前見至生。論曰：於契經中說阿羅漢由種性異故有六種，一者退法、二者思法、三者護法、四安住法、五堪達法、六不動法。於此六中前之五種，從先學位信解性生，即此總名時愛心解脫，恒時愛護及心解脫故。亦說名為時解脫者，以要待時及解脫故。略初言故，如言酥瓶。由此待時方能入定，謂待資具無病處等勝緣合時方入定故。不動法性說名為後，即此名為不動心解脫，以無退動及心解脫故。亦說名為不時解脫，以不待時及解脫故。謂三摩地隨欲現前，不待勝緣和合時故。或依暫時、畢竟解脫，建立時解脫、不時解脫名，容有退墮時、無退墮時故，此從學位見至性生。

Ru-Yi Translation

As already explained, the immovable-lineage arhat gives rise to the knowledge of non-arising immediately following the first knowledge of exhaustion. Do arhats, like stream-enterers and the others, likewise admit of further distinctions? Yes, indeed. What are they? Verse: there are six kinds of arhat, from retrogressible up through immovable; the first five arise from the convinced-through-faith lineage, collectively called timely liberation; the last is non-timely liberation, arising from the attained-through-seeing lineage. Explanation: the sūtras state that arhats, owing to differences in lineage, are of six kinds: retrogressible-dharma, thought-dharma, protective-dharma, stable-dharma, capable-of-penetrating-dharma, and immovable-dharma. Among these six, the first five arise from the convinced-through-faith nature already possessed at the stage of being a noble-one-in-training; these are collectively called timely, cherished liberation of mind, since they must constantly cherish and protect their attainment. They are also called timely liberation, since they must wait for suitable time in order to attain liberation — the fuller term abbreviated, just as one might shorten ghee-pot to pot. It is for this reason that they must wait for the right time to enter concentration, waiting until favorable conditions converge — adequate provisions, freedom from illness, a suitable place — before they are able to enter. The immovable-dharma nature, spoken of last, is called immovable liberation of mind, since it involves no retrogression or disturbance. It is also called non-timely liberation, since it need not wait for any particular time to attain liberation — its concentration manifests whenever desired, without needing to wait for favorable conditions to converge. Alternatively, the names timely and non-timely liberation are established with reference to temporary versus final liberation, since the former admits of times when retrogression is possible, while the latter does not; this last type arises from the attained-through-seeing nature.

Are these six lineages always already possessed beforehand, or can they also be newly attained through practice? Neither exclusively: the retrogressible-dharma lineage is always already possessed beforehand, but the remaining five — including thought-dharma — can also be newly attained afterward, through the refining of faculties from a lower starting lineage. Each of the six is defined by a distinct psychological profile. "Retrogressible-dharma" describes one who falls back from what has been attained upon encountering even the slightest adverse condition. "Thought-dharma" describes one who, fearing the loss of their attainment through retrogression, constantly contemplates ending their own life rather than face that fall. "Protective-dharma" describes one who, delighting in what has been attained, actively guards it. "Stable-dharma" describes one who does not retrogress even without actively guarding their attainment, apart from especially strong adverse conditions, yet likewise does not advance further without especially strong effort. "Capable-of-penetrating-dharma" describes one whose nature is capable of skillfully cultivating the refining of faculties, swiftly reaching the immovable stage. "Immovable-dharma," finally, describes one who will certainly never retrogress at all.

Chinese Source

如是所明六阿羅漢所有種性，為是先有、為後方得？不定。云何？頌曰：有是先種性，有後練根得。論曰：退法種性必是先有，思法等五亦有後得。謂有先來是思法性，有先退法性後練根成思，乃至不動隨應當說。言退法者，謂遇少緣便退所得，非思法等。言思法者，謂懼退失恒思自害。言護法者，謂於所得喜自防護。安住法者，離勝退緣雖不自防亦能不退，離勝加行亦不增進。堪達法者，彼性堪能好修練根速達不動。不動法者，彼必無退。

Ru-Yi Translation

Regarding these six kinds of arhat-lineage just explained, are they always already possessed beforehand, or can they also be newly attained afterward? Neither exclusively — it is not fixed. How so? Verse: some are already possessed as a prior lineage; others are attained afterward through the refining of faculties. Explanation: the retrogressible-dharma lineage is always already possessed beforehand; the remaining five, including thought-dharma, can also be newly attained afterward. That is, some possess the thought-dharma nature already from the start, while others possess the retrogressible-dharma nature at first and later attain thought-dharma through the refining of faculties — and the same pattern should be understood, as appropriate, all the way up through immovable-dharma. Retrogressible-dharma means one who, upon encountering even a slight adverse condition, immediately retrogresses from what has been attained, unlike the thought-dharma type and the rest. Thought-dharma means one who, fearing the loss of their attainment through retrogression, constantly contemplates ending their own life. Protective-dharma means one who, delighting in what has been attained, actively guards and protects it. Stable-dharma means one who, apart from especially strong conditions for retrogression, does not retrogress even without actively guarding their attainment, yet, apart from especially strong effort, likewise does not advance further. Capable-of-penetrating-dharma means one whose nature is capable of skillfully cultivating the refining of faculties, swiftly reaching the immovable stage. Immovable-dharma means one who will certainly never retrogress.

Among these six lineages, at the earlier stage of being a noble-one-in-training, the first two (retrogressible and thought) both lack constant and reverent effort, distinguished from each other only by a difference in faculty; the third (protective) possesses constant effort alone; the fourth (stable) possesses reverent effort alone; the fifth (capable-of-penetrating), though possessing both kinds of effort, remains of dull faculty; the sixth (immovable), of sharp faculty, possesses both. None of these names describes a certainty rather than a mere possibility: "retrogressible" does not mean one will definitely retrogress, nor does "capable-of-penetrating" mean one will definitely reach the immovable stage — hence all six kinds of arhat can, in principle, be found across all three realms. Some teachers, however, read these names more strictly, as guaranteeing their respective outcomes; on that view, all six types exist fully only within the desire realm, while the form and formless realms contain only the "stable" and "immovable" types, since beings there lack any occasion for retrogression, self-harm, self-protective anxiety, or the cultivation of faculty-refining in the first place.

Chinese Source

此六種性，先學位中初二闕恒時及尊重加行，由根有異故有差別。第三唯有恒時加行，第四唯有尊重加行，第五具二而是鈍根，第六利根具二加行。退法種性非必定退，乃至堪達非必能達，但約容有建立此名，故六阿羅漢通三界皆有。若執退者必定應退，乃至堪達必能達者，彼執欲界具足有六，色無色界中唯安住、不動，彼無退失、自害、自防及修練根故唯有二。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these six lineages, at the earlier stage of being a noble-one-in-training, the first two lack both constant and reverent effort, distinguished from each other by a difference in faculty. The third possesses constant effort alone. The fourth possesses reverent effort alone. The fifth possesses both, yet remains of dull faculty. The sixth, of sharp faculty, possesses both kinds of effort. The retrogressible-dharma lineage does not necessarily always retrogress, nor does the capable-of-penetrating type necessarily always reach the immovable stage — these names are established merely with reference to the possibility, not the certainty, of such outcomes. Hence, all six kinds of arhat can be found across all three realms. If, however, one holds that retrogressible necessarily means certain retrogression, and capable-of-penetrating necessarily means certain attainment of immovability, then, on this view, all six kinds exist fully only within the desire realm, while the form and formless realms contain only the stable and immovable types, since beings there lack occasion for retrogression, self-harm, self-protection, or the cultivation of faculty-refining, and so only these two types exist there.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the "thought-dharma" arhat's defining trait as one of the most psychologically striking and, by modern standards, unsettling details in the entire treatise: such an arhat, out of fear of losing their attainment through retrogression, is described as constantly contemplating ending their own life rather than face falling back. We present this exactly as this classical scheme describes it, as a documented feature of this tradition's own account of arhat psychology, without endorsing the premise or resolving the evident tension between this description and Buddhist ethical teaching on preserving life generally — the text itself offers no further reflection on this tension at this point, and we have not supplied one it does not contain.

We should also note the treatise's honest acknowledgment of two competing readings of what these six names actually guarantee — a looser reading (mere possibility, allowing all six types everywhere) and a stricter reading (guaranteed outcomes, restricting four of the six types to the desire realm alone) — presented as a genuine, unresolved interpretive disagreement rather than the treatise declaring one correct. We have kept both readings visible exactly as offered.

2. Who Retrogresses From What, and Why Seeing-Abandoned Affliction Never Returns

Among the six kinds of arhat, the immovable lineage admits of no retrogression whatsoever. Among the remaining five, retrogressible-dharma alone cannot fall further from its own lineage, since that lineage already occupies the lowest position with nothing further below it; the other four (thought, protective, stable, capable-of-penetrating) can retrogress from their very lineage. All five can retrogress from their currently held fruit — but a lineage or fruit already possessed at an earlier, more fundamental stage (as an ordinary worldling, or as a noble-one-in-training, reinforced by both worldly and transcendent paths) is never lost; only a lineage newly attained later, through faculty-refining, admits of falling back. This yields a specific count of possible subsequent courses for each type after retrogression: retrogressible-dharma has three (advancing again, falling back to noble-one-in-training, or simply remaining and eventually attaining nirvāṇa from that position); thought-dharma has four (the same three, plus falling all the way back to retrogressible-dharma itself); and protective, stable, and capable-of-penetrating have five, six, and seven respectively, each gaining one further possible course. When any of these four types falls back to the stage of noble-one-in-training and later regains arhatship, they return specifically to the retrogressible-dharma lineage, never directly back to their original, higher one — regaining the original lineage directly would actually constitute an advance, not what should properly be called a retrogression.

Chinese Source

誰從何退？為性為果？頌曰：四從種性退，五從果非先。論曰：不動種性必無退理。前之五種皆有退義，於中後四有從性退，退法一種無退性理，由此種性最居下故。五種皆有從果退義，雖俱有退然並非先。謂諸無學先學位中所住種性，彼從此性必無退理，學無學道所成堅故。若諸有學先凡位中所住種性，彼從此性亦無退理，世出世道所成堅故。若住此位後修練根所得思等四種種性，彼從此性容有退理。二先位中住思等性，必亦無退此所得果，唯先退法有退果義。又亦無退先所得果，後所得果容有退義，是故定無退預流果。由此應果退法有三：一增進根、二退住學、三住自位而般涅槃。思法有四，三如前說，更加一種退住退性。餘三如次有五六七，應知後後一一增故。思法等四退住學位，時還住退非餘。若異此者，得勝種性故，應是進非退。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these six kinds of arhat, who retrogresses from what — from their lineage, or merely from their fruit? Verse: four retrogress from their very lineage; five retrogress from their fruit, but never from what came before. Explanation: the immovable lineage certainly admits of no retrogression. The preceding five kinds all admit of the possibility of retrogression; among these, the latter four can retrogress from their very lineage itself, while the retrogressible-dharma type alone has no lineage left to retrogress from, since this lineage already occupies the very lowest position. All five admit of the possibility of retrogressing from their fruit, though, even where retrogression occurs, it is never from what came before. The lineage that a now-arhat had already possessed at the earlier stage of being a noble-one-in-training — from that lineage, such a person cannot retrogress, since it has been made firm by both the path of training and the path of no-more-learning. Likewise, the lineage a noble-one-in-training had already possessed at the still-earlier stage of being an ordinary worldling cannot be retrogressed from, since it has been made firm by both worldly and transcendent paths. However, the lineage of thought-dharma and the other three, when newly attained through the refining of faculties, does admit of the possibility of retrogression. For one who already possessed the thought-dharma nature or the like at either of the two earlier stages, such a person certainly cannot retrogress from the fruit thereby attained — retrogression from the fruit applies only to one whose original lineage was retrogressible-dharma. Moreover, one can never retrogress from an earlier-attained fruit; only the later-attained fruit admits of retrogression, and it is for this reason that one can never retrogress from the fruit of stream-entry. On this basis, a retrogressible-dharma arhat has three possible subsequent courses: advancing further, retrogressing to noble-one-in-training, or remaining at their current position and eventually attaining nirvāṇa. The thought-dharma type has four courses, the same three plus retrogressing all the way to retrogressible-dharma. The remaining three types have, respectively, five, six, and seven possible courses, each successive type gaining one more. When the thought-dharma type and the other three retrogress to noble-one-in-training and later regain arhatship, they return specifically to the retrogressible lineage, nothing else — were this not so, it would actually constitute an advance, not a retrogression.

Why is it that one can never retrogress from an earlier-attained, lower fruit specifically? Because what is severed by seeing is grounded in something with no real basis at all: self-view takes a presumed "self" as its foundation, and every seeing-abandoned affliction has this view as its root — yet since "self" does not actually exist, these afflictions are grounded in nothing real, and it is precisely for this reason that there is no possibility of ever retrogressing back into them. This might seem to suggest such afflictions take "nothing" as their object, but this is not so — their object is the truths themselves (the aggregates mistakenly grasped as self), which genuinely exist, even though the affliction grasps them inaccurately. Every affliction, in some sense, fails to grasp its object accurately — yet a real difference remains: cultivation-abandoned afflictions (like greed or hatred toward something pleasing or displeasing) target a characteristic genuinely present, at least in some sense, in the object itself, while seeing-abandoned afflictions (self-view, extreme views) impose a "self" that the object never possessed in the first place, making them uniquely "groundless" in a way cultivation-abandoned afflictions are not.

Chinese Source

何緣定無退先果者？以見所斷依無事故。謂有身見依我處轉，見所斷惑此見為根，我體既無名依無事，以無事故必無退理。若爾，應說此惑緣無，非此緣無，諦為境故。然於諦境不如實緣，諸煩惱中誰不如是？雖皆如是而有差別，以修斷惑各有別事，即是可意不可意等，於所緣境此相非無。見所斷惑計有我等，非諸諦境有我等相，以無事故與修斷別。謂於色等所緣境中，我見妄增作者受者自在而轉，非實我性。邊執見等隨此而生，故並說為依無事惑。若修所斷貪瞋慢癡，色等境中唯起染著增背高舉不了行轉，故並說為依有事惑。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is it that one can certainly never retrogress from an earlier-attained fruit? Because what is severed by seeing is grounded in no real basis. Self-view operates by taking a presumed self as its basis; the afflictions abandoned by seeing all have this view as their root. Since the substance of self does not actually exist, these are said to be grounded in no real basis, and, precisely because they lack any real basis, there is certainly no possibility of retrogressing into them. If this is so, one ought to say that these afflictions take nothing as their object — no, that is not so; their object is not literally nothing, since the truths themselves genuinely serve as their object. However, with respect to the object of the truths, this affliction fails to grasp it accurately — but is this not equally true of every affliction? Although this is true of all afflictions alike, there remains a real difference: cultivation-abandoned afflictions each have their own distinct basis, namely, being pleasing or displeasing and so forth, and this characteristic is genuinely not absent from the object they take. Seeing-abandoned afflictions, by contrast, imagine there to be a self, yet the object of the truths does not actually possess any characteristic of self at all, and it is precisely because of this complete lack of any real basis that seeing-abandoned afflictions differ from cultivation-abandoned ones. With respect to an object such as form, self-view falsely superimposes an agent, an experiencer, a sovereign controller operating there, none of which reflects the object's true nature. Extreme views and the rest arise following upon this same self-view, and so are likewise described as grounded in no real basis. Cultivation-abandoned greed, hatred, conceit, and delusion, by contrast, merely give rise to attachment, aversion, arrogance, and confusion with respect to a genuinely present characteristic, and so are described as grounded in a real basis.

A further, complementary explanation appeals to how these two kinds of affliction actually arise. Cultivation-abandoned affliction arises without careful deliberation, being dull and obscure in nature; seeing-abandoned affliction arises only through careful deliberation, being reasoned and inferential in nature. A noble one who fails to deliberate carefully can indeed lapse into a momentary mindfulness-failure with respect to coarse, everyday matters — much as one might mistake a coiled rope for a snake only in a careless, unreflective glance — but this never happens when deliberation is careful. Hence a noble one can experience retrogression with respect to cultivation-abandoned affliction, which can slip in without careful thought, but never with respect to seeing-abandoned affliction, since that requires genuine, deliberate reasoning to arise at all — and a noble one who deliberates carefully will simply see the truth-principle correctly every time. It is for this reason that a noble one's severance by seeing admits of absolutely no possibility of retrogression.

Chinese Source

又見斷惑於諦理中執我我所斷常見等，非諦中有少我等事。見斷貪等緣此而生，是故皆名依無事惑。修所斷惑於色等中謂好醜等，然色等境非無少分好醜等別，是故可名依有事惑。又見斷惑迷諦理起名依無事，修所斷惑迷麁事生名依有事。諦理真實，揩定可依。聖慧已證必無退理，事相浮偽無定可依，斷迷彼惑有失念退。或修斷惑非審慮生，昧鈍性故。見所斷惑由審慮生，推度性故。聖不審慮於麁事中失念或生，審慮不爾，如於繩等率爾謂蛇。故修斷惑聖有退起，非由率爾可起見惑。聖若審慮便見諦理，故聖見斷定無退義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Moreover, seeing-abandoned afflictions grasp, with respect to the principle of the truths, notions such as self, what belongs to self, annihilation, and permanence, yet the truths themselves contain not the slightest trace of any such self — and seeing-abandoned greed and the rest arise by taking these false notions as object, hence they are all called afflictions grounded in no real basis. Cultivation-abandoned afflictions, by contrast, regard objects such as form as beautiful or ugly, yet such objects are not entirely without at least some genuine trace of such distinctions, hence these can rightly be called afflictions grounded in a real basis. Moreover, seeing-abandoned afflictions arise through confusion regarding the principle of the truths, hence grounded in no real basis; cultivation-abandoned afflictions arise through confusion regarding coarser, more concrete matters, hence grounded in a real basis. The principle of the truths is genuinely real and provides a fixed basis to rely upon — once a noble one's wisdom has realized it, there is certainly no possibility of retrogression. Superficial phenomena, by contrast, are unstable and provide no fixed basis, and so, even after severing the confusion concerning them, a lapse of mindfulness leading to retrogression can still occur. Alternatively: cultivation-abandoned afflictions arise without careful deliberation, being dull in nature; seeing-abandoned afflictions arise through careful deliberation, being reasoned in nature. A noble one who fails to deliberate carefully can lapse into a mindfulness-failure regarding coarse matters, but not when deliberation is careful, just as one might mistake a rope for a snake only in a careless glance. Hence noble ones can experience retrogression with respect to cultivation-abandoned affliction, but seeing-abandoned affliction can never arise merely through carelessness. If a noble one deliberates carefully, they simply see the truth-principle correctly, hence a noble one's severance by seeing certainly admits of no retrogression.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the "grounded in no real basis versus grounded in a real basis" distinction as a genuinely subtle piece of philosophical reasoning explaining an otherwise puzzling asymmetry: why can a noble one backslide on cultivation-abandoned affliction (still feeling occasional anger or craving) but never on seeing-abandoned affliction (never again believing in a literal self)? The treatise's answer locates the difference not in the intensity or type of affliction but in what each affliction actually targets — a genuinely present, if impermanent, quality (pleasant, unpleasant) versus a complete fiction (a self that never existed at all) — reinforced by a second, complementary explanation appealing to the deliberate, reasoned nature of self-view specifically, which cannot simply "slip back in" the way a careless emotional reaction can.

We should also note the treatise offers two independent explanations for this same phenomenon (the real-basis distinction, and the careful-deliberation distinction) without indicating whether one supersedes the other or whether both are meant to hold together as complementary accounts — we have presented both exactly as given, in the sequence the text offers them, without forcing a single unified explanation the passage itself does not provide.
