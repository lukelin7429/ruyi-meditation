Family-to-Family, and One-Interval

Fascicle 24 continues the third part of Chapter 6 (分別賢聖品第六之三, Distinguishing the Noble Ones), picking up directly from fascicle 23's account of the stream-enterer to classify the once-returner and non-returner in detail — the "family-to-family" practitioner advancing toward once-returning, the "one-interval" practitioner advancing toward non-returning, and the elaborate classical taxonomy of the non-returner's many specific types.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress, 2 sections so far Source: Taishō T1558, vol. 29, fascicle 24 Author: Vasubandhu · Translator into Chinese: Xuánzàng

1. Family-to-Family and One-Interval: Advancing Toward the Second and Third Fruits

Continuing directly from the stream-enterer's classification, the treatise now traces how a stream-enterer advances toward the fruit of once-returning. Upon severing three or four grades of desire-realm cultivation-abandoned affliction, attaining the corresponding undefiled counteragent faculties, and having only two or three more rebirths remaining within desire-realm existence, such a person becomes a "family-to-family" practitioner — of two kinds: "heavenly family-to-family," reborn into two or three more heavenly families before attaining cessation, and "human family-to-family," reborn into two or three more human families instead. There is no category for exactly five grades severed, since severing the fifth grade always brings the sixth along with it — a single grade of affliction alone cannot obstruct a fruit's attainment. Upon severing the sixth grade, the fruit of once-returning itself is attained: such a person goes once more to the heavens and returns once more to the human realm before final cessation, hence the name, and is also called "one of attenuated greed, hatred, and delusion," since only their lowest grade now remains.

Chinese Source

已辯住果未斷修惑名為預流，生極七返。今次應辯斷位眾聖，且應建立一來向果。頌曰：斷欲三四品，三二生家家，斷至五二向，斷六一來果。論曰：即預流者進斷修惑，若三緣具轉名家家：一由斷惑，斷欲修斷三四品故。二由成根，得能治彼無漏根故。三由受生，更受欲有三二生故。頌中但說初後緣者，預流果後說進斷惑，成能治彼諸無漏根。義准已成，故不具說。然復應說三二生者，以有增進，於所受生或少或無或過此故。何緣此無斷五品者？以斷第五必斷第六，非一品惑能障得果，猶如一間未越界故。應知總有二種家家：一天家家，謂欲天趣生三二家而證圓寂，或一天處或二或三。二人家家，謂於人趣生三二家而證圓寂，或一洲處或二或三。即預流者進斷欲界一品修惑乃至五品，應知轉名一來果向，若斷第六成一來果，彼往天上一來人間而般涅槃名一來果，過此以後更無生故。此或名曰薄貪瞋癡，唯餘下品貪瞋癡故。

Ru-Yi Translation

We have now explained the stream-enterer, who abides in the fruit without having severed any cultivation-abandoned affliction, guaranteed at most seven more returns of rebirth. Next we should explain the noble ones at the stage of severance, beginning with those aiming toward and abiding in the fruit of once-returning. Verse: severing three or four grades of desire-realm affliction, with three or two more rebirths remaining, makes one family-to-family; severing up to five grades still means aiming toward the second fruit; severing six grades attains the fruit of once-returning. Explanation: that very same stream-enterer, advancing to sever cultivation-abandoned affliction, transforms into what is called family-to-family once three conditions are fulfilled: first, by severance of affliction, having severed three or four grades of desire-realm cultivation-abandoned affliction; second, by the perfecting of faculties, having attained the undefiled faculties capable of countering that affliction; third, by the taking of rebirth, undergoing, from this point, either three or two more rebirths within desire-realm existence. The verse states only the first and last of these conditions, since it is thereby already established, by implication, that the corresponding undefiled counteragent faculties have also been attained. As for why three or two rebirths specifically: as one continues to advance, the number of rebirths one will yet undergo may be fewer, or may even reach zero. Why is there no category for one who has severed exactly five grades? Because whoever severs the fifth grade will necessarily also sever the sixth — a single grade of affliction alone is not capable of obstructing the attainment of a fruit, just as will be explained for the one-interval practitioner as well. There are two kinds of family-to-family practitioner: heavenly family-to-family, reborn into two or three families within the heavenly destinies before attaining complete cessation, whether within one heavenly place or two or three; and human family-to-family, reborn into two or three families within the human destiny, whether within one continent or two or three. That very same stream-enterer, advancing to sever one grade of desire-realm cultivation-abandoned affliction up through five grades, transforms into one aiming toward the fruit of once-returning. Upon severing the sixth grade, one attains the fruit of once-returning itself — such a person goes once to the heavens and returns once to the human realm before attaining complete cessation, hence the name, since beyond this there is no further rebirth. This stage is also called one of attenuated greed, hatred, and delusion, since only the lowest grade of these remains.

The treatise then traces the once-returner's advance toward non-returning. Upon severing the seventh or eighth grade of desire-realm affliction, attaining the corresponding undefiled faculties, and having only one more rebirth remaining, such a person becomes "one-interval" — a single grade of affliction can obstruct the fruit of non-returning specifically because severing it means transcending the desire realm's boundary entirely, comparable to how determinate karma across the three times constitutes the most severe obstruction discussed in earlier fascicles. "Interval" names the single remaining gap — either the one remaining rebirth, or the one remaining grade of affliction — that still stands between this practitioner and complete cessation. Someone who had already severed the third, fourth, seventh, or eighth grade of affliction before ever entering the path of seeing (having arrived pre-equipped with prior worldly detachment) is not called "family-to-family" or "one-interval" even after attaining the corresponding fruit, until they have cultivated the subsequent superior path themselves — these titles belong specifically to those advancing gradually through cultivation after the path of seeing, not to those who arrive already detached. Upon severing the ninth and final grade, one attains the fruit of non-returning, certain never again to be reborn within the desire realm — this severance is called "the severance of the five lower-portion bonds," since, although two or three of these bonds may already have been severed earlier at stream-entry, only now are all five gathered together as fully and finally severed.

Chinese Source

已辯一來向果差別，次應建立不還向果。頌曰：斷七或八品，一生名一間，此即第三向，斷九不還果。論曰：即一來者進斷餘惑，若三緣具轉名一間：一由斷惑，斷欲修斷七八品故。二由成根，得能治彼無漏根故。三由受生，更受欲有餘一生故。頌中但說初後二緣，不說成根，義如前釋。如何一品惑障得不還果？由彼若斷便越界故。前說三時業極為障，應知煩惱亦與業同，越彼等流異熟地故。間謂間隔，彼餘一生為間隔故，不證圓寂。或餘一品欲修所斷惑為間隔故，不得不還果。有一間者說名一間，即斷修惑七八品者，應知亦名不還果向。先斷三四七八品惑入見諦者，後得果時乃至未修後勝果道，仍不名曰家家一間，未成治彼無漏根故。若斷第九成不還果，必不還來生欲界故。此惑名曰五下結斷，雖必先斷或二或三，然於此時總集斷故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having explained the distinctions among those aiming toward and abiding in the fruit of once-returning, we should next establish the distinctions for those aiming toward and abiding in the fruit of non-returning. Verse: severing seven or eight grades, with one more rebirth remaining, is called one-interval — this constitutes aiming toward the third fruit; severing all nine grades attains the fruit of non-returning. Explanation: that very same once-returner, advancing to sever the remaining affliction, transforms into what is called one-interval once three conditions are fulfilled: first, by severance of affliction, having severed the seventh or eighth grade of desire-realm cultivation-abandoned affliction; second, by the perfecting of faculties, having attained the undefiled counteragent faculties; third, by the taking of rebirth, undergoing only one further rebirth within desire-realm existence. The verse states only the first and last of these conditions, for the same reason already explained. How can a single grade of affliction obstruct the attainment of the fruit of non-returning? Because, once that grade is severed, one thereby transcends the realm entirely. It was explained earlier that karma determinately ripening across the three times constitutes the most severe kind of obstruction; affliction functions in just the same way here, since it transcends the very stage to which its equal-outflow and ripened-fruition effects belong. Interval means a gap — since that person's one remaining rebirth constitutes an interruption, they do not yet realize complete cessation; or, alternatively, since the one remaining grade of affliction constitutes an interruption, they do not yet attain the fruit of non-returning. One who has this one interval remaining is named one-interval; one who has severed the seventh or eighth grade of cultivation-abandoned affliction is likewise called one aiming toward the fruit of non-returning. One who had already severed the third, fourth, seventh, or eighth grade of affliction before entering the seeing of the truths — even after later attaining the corresponding fruit, so long as they have not yet cultivated the subsequent superior path, is still not called family-to-family or one-interval, since they have not yet fully attained the undefiled counteragent faculties for that stage. Upon severing the ninth grade, one attains the fruit of non-returning, since one will certainly never again return to be reborn within the desire realm. This affliction-severance is called the severance of the five lower-portion bonds, since, although two or three of these may indeed have already been severed earlier, it is only at this point that they are all, collectively, gathered together as fully severed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the careful distinction the treatise draws between practitioners who advance gradually through the nine grades of affliction after the path of seeing (who earn the titles "family-to-family" and "one-interval" as they progress) and practitioners who had already achieved partial worldly detachment before ever entering the path of seeing at all — the latter attain the corresponding fruit directly at the sixteenth mind-moment without passing through these intermediate titles, since the titles specifically track a process of gradual cultivation these practitioners have, in effect, already completed by other means beforehand. We have kept this distinction clear, since it explains why the same numerical grade of severance can correspond to a named intermediate stage for one practitioner but not for another.

We should also note the cross-reference this passage draws to the karma discussion in earlier fascicles (determinate karma across the three times as "the most severe obstruction") to explain why the ninth grade of affliction alone, rather than any earlier grade, marks the actual boundary of the desire realm — a structural parallel between how karma and affliction each function as decisive, boundary-marking obstructions rather than merely cumulative ones.

2. The Five Types of Non-Returner Bound for the Form Realm

The treatise now takes up the sūtras' own detailed classification of the non-returner, sevenfold in total. Five of these seven concern one who takes further rebirth in the form realm: intermediate-existence nirvāṇa (attaining nirvāṇa while still in the intermediate state between death in the desire realm and rebirth in the form realm); birth nirvāṇa (attaining it not long after that rebirth, thanks to a diligently cultivated, swiftly advancing path); nirvāṇa-with-effort (attaining it only after long, sustained exertion, having diligent cultivation but lacking the swift path); nirvāṇa-without-effort (attaining it without extensive exertion after a long period, lacking both); and the "upstream" type, who cannot attain cessation directly upon rebirth in the form realm at all, but must ascend still higher first. The "nirvāṇa" spoken of for the birth-nirvāṇa type specifically means nirvāṇa with residue — the aggregates still remaining — since such a being lacks the mastery needed to relinquish their own lifespan at will, which nirvāṇa without residue would require.

Chinese Source

依不還位，諸契經中以種種門建立差別，今次應辯彼差別相。頌曰：此中生有行，無行般涅槃，上流若雜修，能往色究竟，超半超遍歿，餘能往有頂，行無色有四，住此般涅槃。論曰：此不還者總說有七，且行色界差別有五：一中般涅槃、二生般涅槃、三有行般涅槃、四無行般涅槃、五者上流。此於中間般涅槃故，說此名曰中般涅槃。如是應知，此於生已，此由有行此由無行般涅槃故，名生般等。此上流故，名為上流。言中般者，謂往色界住中有位便般涅槃。言生般者，謂往色界生已不久便般涅槃，以具勤修速進道故。此中所說般涅槃者，謂有餘依。有餘師說：亦無餘依。此不應理，彼於捨壽無自在故。有行般者，謂往色界生已，長時加行不息，由多功用方般涅槃，此唯有勤修無速進道故。無行般者，謂往色界生已經久，加行懈息不多功用便般涅槃，以闕勤修速進道故。

Ru-Yi Translation

With respect to the stage of non-returning, the sūtras establish various distinctions through various approaches; we should now explain these distinguishing characteristics. Verse: among these, birth, with-effort, and without-effort nirvāṇa; the upstream type, if mixed cultivation, can proceed to Akaniṣṭha, by complete leap, half leap, or full traverse; the remainder can proceed to the peak of existence; those proceeding to the formless realm are of four kinds, attaining nirvāṇa there. Explanation: this non-returner is spoken of, in total, as sevenfold; among these, the varieties for one who takes rebirth in the form realm number five: intermediate-existence nirvāṇa, birth nirvāṇa, nirvāṇa-with-effort, nirvāṇa-without-effort, and the upstream type. Because this one attains nirvāṇa in the intermediate existence, it is called intermediate-existence nirvāṇa. Likewise, having already been reborn, one attains nirvāṇa with effort or without effort, hence those names. Because this one flows upward, it is called upstream. Intermediate-existence nirvāṇa means one who, going toward the form realm, attains nirvāṇa while abiding in the intermediate-existence state. Birth nirvāṇa means one who, having been reborn in the form realm, attains nirvāṇa not long afterward, possessing a diligently cultivated, swiftly advancing path. The nirvāṇa spoken of here refers to nirvāṇa with residue. Other teachers say it can also be without residue; this does not accord with reason, since such a being lacks mastery over relinquishing their own lifespan. Nirvāṇa-with-effort means one who, having been reborn in the form realm, applies sustained, unceasing effort over a long period, attaining nirvāṇa only through extensive exertion, possessing diligent cultivation but lacking the swiftly-advancing path. Nirvāṇa-without-effort means one who, having been reborn in the form realm for a long time, relaxes effort and attains nirvāṇa without extensive exertion, lacking both diligent cultivation and the swiftly-advancing path.

"Upstream" carries the sense of "ascending," since one who dies in the desire realm and is reborn in the form realm is not able to realize cessation there directly, but must be reborn still higher first. This type divides into two by both cause and result: whether one has practiced "mixed cultivation" of the dhyānas or not, and whether Akaniṣṭha (the highest form-realm heaven) or the peak of existence (the highest formless attainment) serves as the final destination. One who has practiced mixed cultivation proceeds all the way to Akaniṣṭha, and this type further divides into three — "complete leap," "half leap," and "full traverse." Complete leap describes one who, having fully practiced mixed cultivation of all four dhyānas while still in the desire realm, loses mastery of the upper three through adverse conditions, dies attached to the pleasure of the first dhyāna, and is reborn in the heaven of Brahmā's Retinue — yet, by the power of habituation from a past life, regains mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna there, and, dying from that lowest heaven, leaps directly to rebirth in Akaniṣṭha, the very highest, skipping everything in between at once.

Chinese Source

言上流者是上行義，以流與行其義一故。謂欲界歿往色界生，未即於中能證圓寂，要轉生上方般涅槃。即此上流差別有二，由因及果有差別故。因差別者，此於靜慮由有雜修無雜修故。果差別者，色究竟天及有頂天為極處故。謂若於靜慮有雜修者，能往色究竟方般涅槃。即此復有三種差別，全超、半超、遍歿異故。言全超者，謂在欲界於四靜慮已具雜修，遇緣退失上三靜慮，以初靜慮愛味為緣，命終上生梵眾天處。由於先世串習勢力，復能雜修第四靜慮，從彼處歿生色究竟，最初處歿生最後天，頓越中間是全超義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Upstream carries the meaning of ascending, since stream and ascending share the same underlying sense here. That is, this type dies in the desire realm and is reborn in the form realm, but is not able to realize complete cessation there directly, and must be reborn still higher before attaining nirvāṇa. This upstream type itself divides into two, distinguished by both cause and result. The difference in cause lies in whether one has practiced mixed cultivation of the dhyānas or not. The difference in result lies in whether Akaniṣṭha or the peak of existence serves as one's final destination. One who has practiced mixed cultivation of the dhyānas is capable of proceeding all the way to Akaniṣṭha before attaining nirvāṇa there. This type further divides into three: complete leap, half leap, and full traverse. Complete leap means: while still in the desire realm, one has already fully practiced mixed cultivation of all four dhyānas, but then, encountering adverse conditions, loses the upper three dhyānas, and, with attachment to the pleasure of the first dhyāna as condition, dies and is reborn in the heaven of Brahmā's Retinue. Yet, by the power of habituation from a previous life, one becomes capable once more of mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna, and, dying from that place, is reborn directly in Akaniṣṭha, dying in the very first place and being reborn in the very last, leaping over everything in between all at once.

Half leap describes one who instead progresses gradually through the lower Pure Abodes, and, at some intermediate point, is able to leap over only one place to reach Akaniṣṭha — a leap that is real but not total. Full traverse describes one who progresses through every single place in succession, only finally reaching Akaniṣṭha at the very last, dying along the way in every place without exception. No non-returner is ever reborn a second time in a place already occupied before, since such a person always seeks further advancement in rebirth, never remaining at an equal or lower level — which is precisely what fulfills the meaning of "non-returner": one who will never return to a place already occupied, not even their own original place, let alone anywhere lower. The other type of "upstream" practitioner — lacking mixed cultivation of the dhyānas — instead dies successively through every place in the form realm except the five Pure Abodes, then rises through the three formless attainments in turn, finally attaining nirvāṇa only upon reaching the peak of existence itself. Between the two upstream types, the first is an "insight-practitioner" and the second a "calm-practitioner," distinguished by whether wisdom or concentration is more delighted in.

Chinese Source

言半超者，從彼漸次生下淨居，乃至中間能越一處生色究竟，超非全故名為半超。聖必不生大梵天處，僻見處故、一導師故。言遍歿者，從彼漸次於一切處皆遍受生，最後方能生色究竟，一切處死故名遍歿。無不還者，於已生處受第二生，由彼於生容求勝進，非等劣故。即由此故不還義滿，必不還生曾生處故。尚不生本處，況有生於下。應知此謂二上流中由有雜修靜慮因故往色究竟般涅槃者，餘於靜慮無雜修者能往有頂方般涅槃，謂彼先無雜修靜慮，由於諸定愛味為緣，此歿遍生色界諸處，唯不能往五淨居天。色界命終於三無色次第生已，後生有頂方般涅槃。二上流中前是觀行、後是止行，樂慧樂定有差別故。二上流者於下地中得般涅槃見不違理，而言此往色究竟天及有頂天為極處者，由此過彼無行處故，如預流者極七返生。此五名為行色界者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Half leap means: from that place, one is reborn progressively through the lower Pure Abodes, up to some intermediate point, and is then able to leap over one place to reach Akaniṣṭha, the leap not being total, hence half leap. A noble one will certainly never be reborn in the heaven of Mahābrahmā, since that place is associated with a distorted view, and because there can be only one guide in that realm. Full traverse means: from that place, one is reborn progressively through every single place in succession, only finally reaching Akaniṣṭha at the very last, dying in every single place along the way, hence full traverse. No non-returner ever takes a second rebirth in a place already occupied, since such a person always seeks further advancement, never remaining at an equal or lower level. It is precisely for this reason that the meaning of non-returner is fully satisfied: such a person will certainly never return to a place already occupied before, not even their own original place, let alone anywhere lower. This describes the first of the two upstream types. The other, lacking mixed cultivation of the dhyānas, proceeds only as far as the peak of existence: lacking mixed cultivation from the start, and with attachment to the pleasure of the various concentrations as condition, dies and is reborn successively throughout all the places of the form realm except the five Pure Abodes, then, dying out of the form realm, is reborn successively through the three formless attainments, and only then, at the peak of existence, finally attains nirvāṇa. Between these two upstream types, the first is an insight-practitioner and the second a calm-practitioner, distinguished by delight in wisdom or in concentration. It does not contradict reason that either upstream type might attain nirvāṇa at some lower stage; it is said that Akaniṣṭha and the peak of existence are their respective final destinations because, beyond these points, there is no further place to go, comparable to how the stream-enterer is guaranteed at most seven returns. These five are collectively called those who proceed to rebirth in the form realm.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag this classification as an extraordinarily elaborate cosmological and soteriological taxonomy, mapping specific meditative and karmic trajectories onto specific heavenly rebirths by name (Brahmā's Retinue, the Pure Abodes, Akaniṣṭha) — we have kept the technical vocabulary and the precise sequencing of each sub-type (complete leap, half leap, full traverse) exactly as given, since the distinctions depend on the exact number and order of realms traversed, not merely on a general sense of "higher versus lower" attainment.

We should also note the treatise's explicit exclusion of noble ones from rebirth in the heaven of Mahābrahmā specifically — attributed to that realm's association with a "distorted view" (Mahābrahmā's own delusion, discussed in earlier fascicles, of believing himself to be the creator of all beings) and the structural point that there can only be one "guide" figure in that heaven, leaving no room for a second, genuinely awakened teacher to also reside there. We have preserved this specific doctrinal exclusion exactly as stated, without further elaboration beyond what the passage itself provides.
