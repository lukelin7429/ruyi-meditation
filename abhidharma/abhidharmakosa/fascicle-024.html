Family-to-Family, and One-Interval

Fascicle 24 continues the third part of Chapter 6 (分別賢聖品第六之三, Distinguishing the Noble Ones), picking up directly from fascicle 23's account of the stream-enterer to classify the once-returner and non-returner in detail — the "family-to-family" practitioner advancing toward once-returning, the "one-interval" practitioner advancing toward non-returning, and the elaborate classical taxonomy of the non-returner's many specific types.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress, 6 sections so far Source: Taishō T1558, vol. 29, fascicle 24 Author: Vasubandhu · Translator into Chinese: Xuánzàng

1. Family-to-Family and One-Interval: Advancing Toward the Second and Third Fruits

Continuing directly from the stream-enterer's classification, the treatise now traces how a stream-enterer advances toward the fruit of once-returning. Upon severing three or four grades of desire-realm cultivation-abandoned affliction, attaining the corresponding undefiled counteragent faculties, and having only two or three more rebirths remaining within desire-realm existence, such a person becomes a "family-to-family" practitioner — of two kinds: "heavenly family-to-family," reborn into two or three more heavenly families before attaining cessation, and "human family-to-family," reborn into two or three more human families instead. There is no category for exactly five grades severed, since severing the fifth grade always brings the sixth along with it — a single grade of affliction alone cannot obstruct a fruit's attainment. Upon severing the sixth grade, the fruit of once-returning itself is attained: such a person goes once more to the heavens and returns once more to the human realm before final cessation, hence the name, and is also called "one of attenuated greed, hatred, and delusion," since only their lowest grade now remains.

Chinese Source

已辯住果未斷修惑名為預流，生極七返。今次應辯斷位眾聖，且應建立一來向果。頌曰：斷欲三四品，三二生家家，斷至五二向，斷六一來果。論曰：即預流者進斷修惑，若三緣具轉名家家：一由斷惑，斷欲修斷三四品故。二由成根，得能治彼無漏根故。三由受生，更受欲有三二生故。頌中但說初後緣者，預流果後說進斷惑，成能治彼諸無漏根。義准已成，故不具說。然復應說三二生者，以有增進，於所受生或少或無或過此故。何緣此無斷五品者？以斷第五必斷第六，非一品惑能障得果，猶如一間未越界故。應知總有二種家家：一天家家，謂欲天趣生三二家而證圓寂，或一天處或二或三。二人家家，謂於人趣生三二家而證圓寂，或一洲處或二或三。即預流者進斷欲界一品修惑乃至五品，應知轉名一來果向，若斷第六成一來果，彼往天上一來人間而般涅槃名一來果，過此以後更無生故。此或名曰薄貪瞋癡，唯餘下品貪瞋癡故。

Ru-Yi Translation

We have now explained the stream-enterer, who abides in the fruit without having severed any cultivation-abandoned affliction, guaranteed at most seven more returns of rebirth. Next we should explain the noble ones at the stage of severance, beginning with those aiming toward and abiding in the fruit of once-returning. Verse: severing three or four grades of desire-realm affliction, with three or two more rebirths remaining, makes one family-to-family; severing up to five grades still means aiming toward the second fruit; severing six grades attains the fruit of once-returning. Explanation: that very same stream-enterer, advancing to sever cultivation-abandoned affliction, transforms into what is called family-to-family once three conditions are fulfilled: first, by severance of affliction, having severed three or four grades of desire-realm cultivation-abandoned affliction; second, by the perfecting of faculties, having attained the undefiled faculties capable of countering that affliction; third, by the taking of rebirth, undergoing, from this point, either three or two more rebirths within desire-realm existence. The verse states only the first and last of these conditions, since it is thereby already established, by implication, that the corresponding undefiled counteragent faculties have also been attained. As for why three or two rebirths specifically: as one continues to advance, the number of rebirths one will yet undergo may be fewer, or may even reach zero. Why is there no category for one who has severed exactly five grades? Because whoever severs the fifth grade will necessarily also sever the sixth — a single grade of affliction alone is not capable of obstructing the attainment of a fruit, just as will be explained for the one-interval practitioner as well. There are two kinds of family-to-family practitioner: heavenly family-to-family, reborn into two or three families within the heavenly destinies before attaining complete cessation, whether within one heavenly place or two or three; and human family-to-family, reborn into two or three families within the human destiny, whether within one continent or two or three. That very same stream-enterer, advancing to sever one grade of desire-realm cultivation-abandoned affliction up through five grades, transforms into one aiming toward the fruit of once-returning. Upon severing the sixth grade, one attains the fruit of once-returning itself — such a person goes once to the heavens and returns once to the human realm before attaining complete cessation, hence the name, since beyond this there is no further rebirth. This stage is also called one of attenuated greed, hatred, and delusion, since only the lowest grade of these remains.

The treatise then traces the once-returner's advance toward non-returning. Upon severing the seventh or eighth grade of desire-realm affliction, attaining the corresponding undefiled faculties, and having only one more rebirth remaining, such a person becomes "one-interval" — a single grade of affliction can obstruct the fruit of non-returning specifically because severing it means transcending the desire realm's boundary entirely, comparable to how determinate karma across the three times constitutes the most severe obstruction discussed in earlier fascicles. "Interval" names the single remaining gap — either the one remaining rebirth, or the one remaining grade of affliction — that still stands between this practitioner and complete cessation. Someone who had already severed the third, fourth, seventh, or eighth grade of affliction before ever entering the path of seeing (having arrived pre-equipped with prior worldly detachment) is not called "family-to-family" or "one-interval" even after attaining the corresponding fruit, until they have cultivated the subsequent superior path themselves — these titles belong specifically to those advancing gradually through cultivation after the path of seeing, not to those who arrive already detached. Upon severing the ninth and final grade, one attains the fruit of non-returning, certain never again to be reborn within the desire realm — this severance is called "the severance of the five lower-portion bonds," since, although two or three of these bonds may already have been severed earlier at stream-entry, only now are all five gathered together as fully and finally severed.

Chinese Source

已辯一來向果差別，次應建立不還向果。頌曰：斷七或八品，一生名一間，此即第三向，斷九不還果。論曰：即一來者進斷餘惑，若三緣具轉名一間：一由斷惑，斷欲修斷七八品故。二由成根，得能治彼無漏根故。三由受生，更受欲有餘一生故。頌中但說初後二緣，不說成根，義如前釋。如何一品惑障得不還果？由彼若斷便越界故。前說三時業極為障，應知煩惱亦與業同，越彼等流異熟地故。間謂間隔，彼餘一生為間隔故，不證圓寂。或餘一品欲修所斷惑為間隔故，不得不還果。有一間者說名一間，即斷修惑七八品者，應知亦名不還果向。先斷三四七八品惑入見諦者，後得果時乃至未修後勝果道，仍不名曰家家一間，未成治彼無漏根故。若斷第九成不還果，必不還來生欲界故。此惑名曰五下結斷，雖必先斷或二或三，然於此時總集斷故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having explained the distinctions among those aiming toward and abiding in the fruit of once-returning, we should next establish the distinctions for those aiming toward and abiding in the fruit of non-returning. Verse: severing seven or eight grades, with one more rebirth remaining, is called one-interval — this constitutes aiming toward the third fruit; severing all nine grades attains the fruit of non-returning. Explanation: that very same once-returner, advancing to sever the remaining affliction, transforms into what is called one-interval once three conditions are fulfilled: first, by severance of affliction, having severed the seventh or eighth grade of desire-realm cultivation-abandoned affliction; second, by the perfecting of faculties, having attained the undefiled counteragent faculties; third, by the taking of rebirth, undergoing only one further rebirth within desire-realm existence. The verse states only the first and last of these conditions, for the same reason already explained. How can a single grade of affliction obstruct the attainment of the fruit of non-returning? Because, once that grade is severed, one thereby transcends the realm entirely. It was explained earlier that karma determinately ripening across the three times constitutes the most severe kind of obstruction; affliction functions in just the same way here, since it transcends the very stage to which its equal-outflow and ripened-fruition effects belong. Interval means a gap — since that person's one remaining rebirth constitutes an interruption, they do not yet realize complete cessation; or, alternatively, since the one remaining grade of affliction constitutes an interruption, they do not yet attain the fruit of non-returning. One who has this one interval remaining is named one-interval; one who has severed the seventh or eighth grade of cultivation-abandoned affliction is likewise called one aiming toward the fruit of non-returning. One who had already severed the third, fourth, seventh, or eighth grade of affliction before entering the seeing of the truths — even after later attaining the corresponding fruit, so long as they have not yet cultivated the subsequent superior path, is still not called family-to-family or one-interval, since they have not yet fully attained the undefiled counteragent faculties for that stage. Upon severing the ninth grade, one attains the fruit of non-returning, since one will certainly never again return to be reborn within the desire realm. This affliction-severance is called the severance of the five lower-portion bonds, since, although two or three of these may indeed have already been severed earlier, it is only at this point that they are all, collectively, gathered together as fully severed.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the careful distinction the treatise draws between practitioners who advance gradually through the nine grades of affliction after the path of seeing (who earn the titles "family-to-family" and "one-interval" as they progress) and practitioners who had already achieved partial worldly detachment before ever entering the path of seeing at all — the latter attain the corresponding fruit directly at the sixteenth mind-moment without passing through these intermediate titles, since the titles specifically track a process of gradual cultivation these practitioners have, in effect, already completed by other means beforehand. We have kept this distinction clear, since it explains why the same numerical grade of severance can correspond to a named intermediate stage for one practitioner but not for another.

We should also note the cross-reference this passage draws to the karma discussion in earlier fascicles (determinate karma across the three times as "the most severe obstruction") to explain why the ninth grade of affliction alone, rather than any earlier grade, marks the actual boundary of the desire realm — a structural parallel between how karma and affliction each function as decisive, boundary-marking obstructions rather than merely cumulative ones.

2. The Five Types of Non-Returner Bound for the Form Realm

The treatise now takes up the sūtras' own detailed classification of the non-returner, sevenfold in total. Five of these seven concern one who takes further rebirth in the form realm: intermediate-existence nirvāṇa (attaining nirvāṇa while still in the intermediate state between death in the desire realm and rebirth in the form realm); birth nirvāṇa (attaining it not long after that rebirth, thanks to a diligently cultivated, swiftly advancing path); nirvāṇa-with-effort (attaining it only after long, sustained exertion, having diligent cultivation but lacking the swift path); nirvāṇa-without-effort (attaining it without extensive exertion after a long period, lacking both); and the "upstream" type, who cannot attain cessation directly upon rebirth in the form realm at all, but must ascend still higher first. The "nirvāṇa" spoken of for the birth-nirvāṇa type specifically means nirvāṇa with residue — the aggregates still remaining — since such a being lacks the mastery needed to relinquish their own lifespan at will, which nirvāṇa without residue would require.

Chinese Source

依不還位，諸契經中以種種門建立差別，今次應辯彼差別相。頌曰：此中生有行，無行般涅槃，上流若雜修，能往色究竟，超半超遍歿，餘能往有頂，行無色有四，住此般涅槃。論曰：此不還者總說有七，且行色界差別有五：一中般涅槃、二生般涅槃、三有行般涅槃、四無行般涅槃、五者上流。此於中間般涅槃故，說此名曰中般涅槃。如是應知，此於生已，此由有行此由無行般涅槃故，名生般等。此上流故，名為上流。言中般者，謂往色界住中有位便般涅槃。言生般者，謂往色界生已不久便般涅槃，以具勤修速進道故。此中所說般涅槃者，謂有餘依。有餘師說：亦無餘依。此不應理，彼於捨壽無自在故。有行般者，謂往色界生已，長時加行不息，由多功用方般涅槃，此唯有勤修無速進道故。無行般者，謂往色界生已經久，加行懈息不多功用便般涅槃，以闕勤修速進道故。

Ru-Yi Translation

With respect to the stage of non-returning, the sūtras establish various distinctions through various approaches; we should now explain these distinguishing characteristics. Verse: among these, birth, with-effort, and without-effort nirvāṇa; the upstream type, if mixed cultivation, can proceed to Akaniṣṭha, by complete leap, half leap, or full traverse; the remainder can proceed to the peak of existence; those proceeding to the formless realm are of four kinds, attaining nirvāṇa there. Explanation: this non-returner is spoken of, in total, as sevenfold; among these, the varieties for one who takes rebirth in the form realm number five: intermediate-existence nirvāṇa, birth nirvāṇa, nirvāṇa-with-effort, nirvāṇa-without-effort, and the upstream type. Because this one attains nirvāṇa in the intermediate existence, it is called intermediate-existence nirvāṇa. Likewise, having already been reborn, one attains nirvāṇa with effort or without effort, hence those names. Because this one flows upward, it is called upstream. Intermediate-existence nirvāṇa means one who, going toward the form realm, attains nirvāṇa while abiding in the intermediate-existence state. Birth nirvāṇa means one who, having been reborn in the form realm, attains nirvāṇa not long afterward, possessing a diligently cultivated, swiftly advancing path. The nirvāṇa spoken of here refers to nirvāṇa with residue. Other teachers say it can also be without residue; this does not accord with reason, since such a being lacks mastery over relinquishing their own lifespan. Nirvāṇa-with-effort means one who, having been reborn in the form realm, applies sustained, unceasing effort over a long period, attaining nirvāṇa only through extensive exertion, possessing diligent cultivation but lacking the swiftly-advancing path. Nirvāṇa-without-effort means one who, having been reborn in the form realm for a long time, relaxes effort and attains nirvāṇa without extensive exertion, lacking both diligent cultivation and the swiftly-advancing path.

"Upstream" carries the sense of "ascending," since one who dies in the desire realm and is reborn in the form realm is not able to realize cessation there directly, but must be reborn still higher first. This type divides into two by both cause and result: whether one has practiced "mixed cultivation" of the dhyānas or not, and whether Akaniṣṭha (the highest form-realm heaven) or the peak of existence (the highest formless attainment) serves as the final destination. One who has practiced mixed cultivation proceeds all the way to Akaniṣṭha, and this type further divides into three — "complete leap," "half leap," and "full traverse." Complete leap describes one who, having fully practiced mixed cultivation of all four dhyānas while still in the desire realm, loses mastery of the upper three through adverse conditions, dies attached to the pleasure of the first dhyāna, and is reborn in the heaven of Brahmā's Retinue — yet, by the power of habituation from a past life, regains mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna there, and, dying from that lowest heaven, leaps directly to rebirth in Akaniṣṭha, the very highest, skipping everything in between at once.

Chinese Source

言上流者是上行義，以流與行其義一故。謂欲界歿往色界生，未即於中能證圓寂，要轉生上方般涅槃。即此上流差別有二，由因及果有差別故。因差別者，此於靜慮由有雜修無雜修故。果差別者，色究竟天及有頂天為極處故。謂若於靜慮有雜修者，能往色究竟方般涅槃。即此復有三種差別，全超、半超、遍歿異故。言全超者，謂在欲界於四靜慮已具雜修，遇緣退失上三靜慮，以初靜慮愛味為緣，命終上生梵眾天處。由於先世串習勢力，復能雜修第四靜慮，從彼處歿生色究竟，最初處歿生最後天，頓越中間是全超義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Upstream carries the meaning of ascending, since stream and ascending share the same underlying sense here. That is, this type dies in the desire realm and is reborn in the form realm, but is not able to realize complete cessation there directly, and must be reborn still higher before attaining nirvāṇa. This upstream type itself divides into two, distinguished by both cause and result. The difference in cause lies in whether one has practiced mixed cultivation of the dhyānas or not. The difference in result lies in whether Akaniṣṭha or the peak of existence serves as one's final destination. One who has practiced mixed cultivation of the dhyānas is capable of proceeding all the way to Akaniṣṭha before attaining nirvāṇa there. This type further divides into three: complete leap, half leap, and full traverse. Complete leap means: while still in the desire realm, one has already fully practiced mixed cultivation of all four dhyānas, but then, encountering adverse conditions, loses the upper three dhyānas, and, with attachment to the pleasure of the first dhyāna as condition, dies and is reborn in the heaven of Brahmā's Retinue. Yet, by the power of habituation from a previous life, one becomes capable once more of mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna, and, dying from that place, is reborn directly in Akaniṣṭha, dying in the very first place and being reborn in the very last, leaping over everything in between all at once.

Half leap describes one who instead progresses gradually through the lower Pure Abodes, and, at some intermediate point, is able to leap over only one place to reach Akaniṣṭha — a leap that is real but not total. Full traverse describes one who progresses through every single place in succession, only finally reaching Akaniṣṭha at the very last, dying along the way in every place without exception. No non-returner is ever reborn a second time in a place already occupied before, since such a person always seeks further advancement in rebirth, never remaining at an equal or lower level — which is precisely what fulfills the meaning of "non-returner": one who will never return to a place already occupied, not even their own original place, let alone anywhere lower. The other type of "upstream" practitioner — lacking mixed cultivation of the dhyānas — instead dies successively through every place in the form realm except the five Pure Abodes, then rises through the three formless attainments in turn, finally attaining nirvāṇa only upon reaching the peak of existence itself. Between the two upstream types, the first is an "insight-practitioner" and the second a "calm-practitioner," distinguished by whether wisdom or concentration is more delighted in.

Chinese Source

言半超者，從彼漸次生下淨居，乃至中間能越一處生色究竟，超非全故名為半超。聖必不生大梵天處，僻見處故、一導師故。言遍歿者，從彼漸次於一切處皆遍受生，最後方能生色究竟，一切處死故名遍歿。無不還者，於已生處受第二生，由彼於生容求勝進，非等劣故。即由此故不還義滿，必不還生曾生處故。尚不生本處，況有生於下。應知此謂二上流中由有雜修靜慮因故往色究竟般涅槃者，餘於靜慮無雜修者能往有頂方般涅槃，謂彼先無雜修靜慮，由於諸定愛味為緣，此歿遍生色界諸處，唯不能往五淨居天。色界命終於三無色次第生已，後生有頂方般涅槃。二上流中前是觀行、後是止行，樂慧樂定有差別故。二上流者於下地中得般涅槃見不違理，而言此往色究竟天及有頂天為極處者，由此過彼無行處故，如預流者極七返生。此五名為行色界者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Half leap means: from that place, one is reborn progressively through the lower Pure Abodes, up to some intermediate point, and is then able to leap over one place to reach Akaniṣṭha, the leap not being total, hence half leap. A noble one will certainly never be reborn in the heaven of Mahābrahmā, since that place is associated with a distorted view, and because there can be only one guide in that realm. Full traverse means: from that place, one is reborn progressively through every single place in succession, only finally reaching Akaniṣṭha at the very last, dying in every single place along the way, hence full traverse. No non-returner ever takes a second rebirth in a place already occupied, since such a person always seeks further advancement, never remaining at an equal or lower level. It is precisely for this reason that the meaning of non-returner is fully satisfied: such a person will certainly never return to a place already occupied before, not even their own original place, let alone anywhere lower. This describes the first of the two upstream types. The other, lacking mixed cultivation of the dhyānas, proceeds only as far as the peak of existence: lacking mixed cultivation from the start, and with attachment to the pleasure of the various concentrations as condition, dies and is reborn successively throughout all the places of the form realm except the five Pure Abodes, then, dying out of the form realm, is reborn successively through the three formless attainments, and only then, at the peak of existence, finally attains nirvāṇa. Between these two upstream types, the first is an insight-practitioner and the second a calm-practitioner, distinguished by delight in wisdom or in concentration. It does not contradict reason that either upstream type might attain nirvāṇa at some lower stage; it is said that Akaniṣṭha and the peak of existence are their respective final destinations because, beyond these points, there is no further place to go, comparable to how the stream-enterer is guaranteed at most seven returns. These five are collectively called those who proceed to rebirth in the form realm.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag this classification as an extraordinarily elaborate cosmological and soteriological taxonomy, mapping specific meditative and karmic trajectories onto specific heavenly rebirths by name (Brahmā's Retinue, the Pure Abodes, Akaniṣṭha) — we have kept the technical vocabulary and the precise sequencing of each sub-type (complete leap, half leap, full traverse) exactly as given, since the distinctions depend on the exact number and order of realms traversed, not merely on a general sense of "higher versus lower" attainment.

We should also note the treatise's explicit exclusion of noble ones from rebirth in the heaven of Mahābrahmā specifically — attributed to that realm's association with a "distorted view" (Mahābrahmā's own delusion, discussed in earlier fascicles, of believing himself to be the creator of all beings) and the structural point that there can only be one "guide" figure in that heaven, leaving no room for a second, genuinely awakened teacher to also reside there. We have preserved this specific doctrinal exclusion exactly as stated, without further elaboration beyond what the passage itself provides.

3. Completing the Seven; the Alternate Ninefold Scheme; the "Seven Excellent Persons"

Beyond the five form-realm types, those who proceed to the formless realm divide into four kinds — corresponding to becoming free of form-realm craving while still in the desire realm and then dying directly into one of the four formless attainments, distinguished by which of the four rebirth-and-cessation patterns applies. Together with the preceding five, this makes six kinds of non-returner. A seventh and final kind proceeds to neither the form nor the formless realm at all, attaining nirvāṇa right where they already are, in the desire realm itself — called "nirvāṇa in the present life" (dṛṣṭadharma-parinirvāṇa). Together, these seven constitute the complete classical scheme.

Chinese Source

行無色者差別有四，謂在欲界離色界貪，從此命終生於無色。此中差別唯有四種，由生般涅槃有差別故，此并前五成六不還。復有不行色無色界，即住於此能般涅槃，名現般涅槃，并前六為七。於行色界五不還中，復有異門顯其差別。頌曰：行色界有九，謂三各分三，業惑根有殊，故成三九別。論曰：即行色界五種不還總立為三，各分三種故成九種。何等為三？中生上流有差別故。云何三種各分為三？且中般涅槃分為三種，速、非速、經久得般涅槃，由三火星喻所顯故。生般涅槃亦分三種，生有行等般涅槃故。此皆生已得般涅槃，是故並應名為生般。於上流中亦分三種，超、半超等有差別故。然諸三種一切皆由速、非速、經久得般涅槃故，更互相望無雜亂失。

Ru-Yi Translation

Those who proceed to the formless realm are distinguished into four kinds: one who, while still in the desire realm, becomes free of form-realm craving, and, upon dying from that state, is reborn directly in the formless realm. The distinctions here number exactly four, corresponding to differences among the rebirth-and-cessation types. Together with the preceding five, this makes six kinds of non-returner. There is, further, one who proceeds to neither the form nor the formless realm, but instead attains nirvāṇa right where they are — this is called nirvāṇa in the present life. Together with the preceding six, this makes seven. Among the five non-returners who proceed to the form realm, there is a further approach revealing additional distinctions. Verse: those proceeding to the form realm number nine — three main types, each divided into three further kinds — distinguished by differences in karma, affliction, and faculty. Explanation: these same five kinds are, in total, grouped into three main categories — intermediate-existence, birth, and upstream — each further divided into three, making nine in total. Intermediate-existence nirvāṇa divides into three: attaining nirvāṇa swiftly, not swiftly, or only after a long time, illustrated by the analogy of three kinds of spark. Birth nirvāṇa likewise divides into three, corresponding to birth nirvāṇa, nirvāṇa-with-effort, and nirvāṇa-without-effort, all grouped together since all three attain nirvāṇa only after rebirth. The upstream category likewise divides into three: complete leap, half leap, and full traverse. Yet all three main categories, in every case, are ultimately distinguished by the same swift/not-swift/long-delayed pattern, so there is no confusion between them.

These three main categories are further distinguished specifically by differences in karma, affliction, and faculty: the first two (intermediate-existence and birth) divide into their three sub-types by differences in currently manifest affliction (lower, middle, or upper grade) and in faculty (upper, middle, or lower), not by differences in karma; the third (upstream) divides into its three sub-types specifically by differences in the karma conducive to a subsequent existence. This yields the full three-by-three, nine-part scheme. Yet the sūtras themselves speak of only "seven destinies of the excellent person" (sapta satpuruṣa-gatayaḥ) rather than nine, because the "upstream" category, despite its internal sub-variation, is treated as a single unified destiny — intermediate-existence and birth each contributing three, upstream contributing only one, for seven in total. This designation is reserved for these seven alone, rather than extended to every noble-one-in-training, for two specific reasons: only these seven act exclusively wholesomely, never unwholesomely at all, and only these seven proceed onward into the higher realms without ever returning — neither guarantee holding for noble-ones-in-training generally.

Verse: the seven destinies of the excellent person are established because the upstream type carries no further distinction for this purpose, because these alone act only wholesomely, never unwholesomely, and because they proceed onward without ever returning. Explanation: intermediate-existence and birth each contribute three, while upstream contributes only one — the sūtras establish the seven destinies on this basis. Since all upstream types share the characteristic of flowing upward, they are named upstream collectively and treated as one category. Destiny carries the sense of proceeding onward, and while the remaining noble ones in training likewise perform wholesome karma, this alone does not earn them the designation. Only these seven act exclusively wholesomely, never unwholesomely — the remaining ones do not share this exclusivity. An objection notes that a sūtra defines an "excellent person" simply as one who has attained the right view of a noble-one-in-training, without restricting this to the seven types just described — suggesting the term should apply much more broadly. The treatise concedes the point from a different angle: the remaining noble-ones-in-training can indeed also be said to possess something of the nature of "excellent person," since all of them have attained lasting restraint from the five kinds of unwholesome deed and have already severed much of their unwholesome affliction. Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the "three sparks" analogy used to distinguish swift, delayed, and long-delayed intermediate-existence nirvāṇa — likely referring to a spark struck from iron that either extinguishes almost instantly, travels a short distance before dying out, or travels much further — though the source text here states the analogy by name without spelling out each of the three cases individually; we have preserved it as a named reference rather than supplying a reconstructed explanation the passage itself does not provide.

We should also note the treatise's careful handling of an apparent scriptural tension: one sūtra passage seems to define "excellent person" broadly enough to include any noble-one-in-training, while the specific "seven destinies" classification is clearly narrower. Rather than treating this as a contradiction, the treatise resolves it by distinguishing two different senses of the same term — a broader, looser sense (in which most noble-ones-in-training qualify) and a narrower, technical sense (reserved for the seven with the strongest guarantees) — a pattern of terminological disambiguation this project has seen recur throughout the treatise's approach to potentially conflicting scriptural statements.

4. Prior Rebirths, Śakra's Mistake, and Mixed Cultivation of the Fourth Dhyāna

Do noble ones who had already undergone rebirths in the desire realm before attaining the noble path exhibit these same seven-destiny distinctions? No. A noble one who had already undergone rebirth within the desire realm before attaining non-returning will certainly not proceed to rebirth in the form or formless realms at all, since, having attained that fruit, they are guaranteed to attain nirvāṇa within this very present life. One who had already undergone rebirth as a noble one within the form realm, however, can still proceed to a higher rebirth in the formless realm, much like the "upstream" types reaching the peak of existence discussed earlier. This bears on a curious remark attributed to Śakra, lord of the gods, who reportedly said: "I have heard there is a heaven called Akaniṣṭha; after I eventually fall from my current position, I shall be reborn there." The Vaibhāṣika masters explain that Śakra spoke this way out of an imprecise grasp of the Abhidharma's true technical picture, and that the Buddha, wishing to please him, simply did not correct the statement. Both of these types of already-reborn noble ones are permanently excluded from two further possibilities open to others: refining their faculties from dull to sharp, and retrogression — since their rebirth has already brought their cultivated faculties to full maturity, resting now on an especially superior physical basis.

Chinese Source

Verse: a noble one who has already undergone rebirth within the desire realm does not proceed to rebirth in the other realms; both this type and one who does proceed to a higher rebirth lack the possibility of refining their faculties or of retrogression. Explanation: if, while already a noble one, one has undergone prior rebirth within the desire realm before attaining non-returning, such a person will certainly not proceed to rebirth in the form or formless realms, since, having attained the fruit of non-returning, they are certain to attain nirvāṇa within this very present life. If, however, one has already undergone prior rebirth as a noble one within the form realm, it remains possible for such a person to proceed to a higher rebirth within the formless realm, comparable to those proceeding to the form realm who ultimately reach the peak of existence. Yet Śakra, the lord of the gods, is recorded as having said: I have heard there is a heaven called Akaniṣṭha; after I eventually fall from my current position, I shall be reborn there. The Vaibhāṣika masters explain this as follows: Śakra said this because he did not properly understand the true characteristics of the Abhidharma, and the Buddha, in order to please him, did not correct this statement. These same two types — one who has already undergone rebirth within the desire realm, and one who has proceeded from there to a higher rebirth — definitely cannot undergo either the refining of faculties or retrogression. Why is this not admitted? Because it definitely does not occur. Why does it definitely not occur? Because, having already undergone rebirth, their cultivated faculties have already become fully mature, and because they have attained an especially superior physical basis.

Why can a noble-one-in-training who has not yet become free of desire-realm craving not attain nirvāṇa in an intermediate-existence state at all? Because their noble path is not yet fully matured, is not yet easily able to manifest, and their remaining latent afflictions have not yet been reduced to an extremely weak degree — the Vaibhāṣika masters add that desire-realm dharmas are extremely difficult to transcend, and such a person still has substantial work remaining (severing both unwholesome and morally-neutral affliction, attaining a further fruit, transcending all three realms entirely), work an intermediate-existence state alone cannot accomplish. The treatise then turns to the "upstream" practitioner's key technique — mixed cultivation of the dhyānas — asking which dhyāna comes first, how completion is measured, and why it is practiced at all. Mixed cultivation must begin with the fourth dhyāna specifically, since its concentration is the most capable of supporting the practice and, among all modes yielding ease, the most excellent. An arhat or non-returner undertaking this practice first enters the fourth dhyāna and manifests many successive undefiled moments, then draws forth many defiled (worldly) moments, then returns to many undefiled moments — cycling back and forth with the count in each phase gradually shrinking, until only two undefiled moments remain, followed by two defiled, immediately followed again by two undefiled: this marks the completion of the preparatory stage.

The Vaibhāṣika masters explain this as follows: desire-realm dharmas are extremely difficult to transcend, and such a person still has much remaining work to do — they must still advance to sever both the unwholesome and morally-neutral kinds of affliction, must still advance to attain either the second or third fruit, and must still transcend the dharmas of all three realms entirely — and one abiding merely in the intermediate-existence state does not possess the capacity to accomplish all of this. Verse: one first practices mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna, its completion marked by a single moment mixed with undefiled moments, practiced for the sake of rebirth, present pleasure, and to prevent retrogression through the arising of affliction. Explanation: whoever wishes to practice mixed cultivation of all four dhyānas must first practice mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna specifically, since its concentration is the most capable of supporting this practice, and, among all modes of practice yielding ease, it is the most excellent. Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the Śakra anecdote as a notable moment of scriptural diplomacy the Vaibhāṣika masters attribute to the Buddha: rather than correcting a revered deity's technically imprecise understanding of Akaniṣṭha's actual cosmological position, the Buddha is said to have simply let the statement stand, out of consideration for Śakra's happiness. We have presented this exactly as the Vaibhāṣika masters explain it, as their own interpretive gloss on the anecdote rather than a claim this project independently verifies.

We should also note the mixed-cultivation-of-the-fourth-dhyāna technique as an extraordinarily precise, almost mathematical meditation protocol — beginning with many alternating blocks of undefiled and defiled mental moments and narrowing, block by block, until the pattern collapses to a single defiled moment sandwiched between two undefiled ones. We have kept the exact numerical progression (many, many; then two, two, two; then one, one, one) as the text presents it, since the specific narrowing pattern is precisely what the passage is describing, not an incidental detail.

5. Why Five Pure Abodes; the "Body-Witness"; and the Staggering Sub-Type Count

The treatise now assigns mixed cultivation's three purposes to specific practitioners. Among non-returners, those of sharp faculties practice it for present-life pleasure and rebirth in the Pure Abodes; those of dull faculties practice it also to prevent retrogression, out of fear of falling back — in both cases, the practice keeps them at a distance from concentration tainted by attachment to its own flavor. Among arhats, sharp-faculty practitioners pursue it for present pleasure, dull-faculty ones also to guard against affliction-driven retrogression. This raises a further question: why are there exactly five Pure Abodes? Because mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna itself comes in five grades — lower, middle, upper-upper, superior-upper, and utmost — completed respectively by three mind-moments (undefiled, then defiled, then undefiled again), six, nine, twelve, and fifteen moments. These five grades, in corresponding order, bring about rebirth in the five Pure Abodes, the power of the undefiled moments habituating and infusing the defiled ones to produce this result — though other teachers attribute the five Pure Abodes instead to the progressive intensification of the five spiritual faculties (faith and the rest).

Chinese Source

Those of dull faculties practice it also to prevent retrogression, since they fear falling back. This mixed cultivation practice keeps one at a distance from concentration tainted by attachment to its own flavor. Among arhats, those of sharp faculties practice it for the sake of pleasure in the present life; those of dull faculties practice it also to guard against and prevent retrogression through the arising of affliction. Non-returners practice mixed cultivation of the dhyānas for the sake of rebirth in the Pure Abodes. Why are there exactly five Pure Abodes? Verse: because there are five grades of mixed cultivation, there are five corresponding Pure Abodes for rebirth. Explanation: because the mixed cultivation and habituation of the fourth dhyāna has five grades, the Pure Abodes number exactly five. What are these five grades? They are distinguished as lower, middle, upper-upper, superior-upper, and utmost. The first grade is completed once three mind-moments manifest: first undefiled, then arising defiled, then arising undefiled again. The second grade requires six moments; the third, nine; the fourth, twelve; the fifth, fifteen. These five grades of mixed cultivation of the dhyāna, in corresponding order, bring about rebirth in the five Pure Abodes. It should be understood that the power of the undefiled instants habituates and infuses the defiled instants, and this brings about rebirth in the Pure Abodes. Other teachers say instead: it is by the progressive intensification of the five spiritual faculties that rebirth in the five Pure Abodes is brought about.

The sūtras also speak of a non-returner called "body-witness" (kāyasākṣin) — a non-returner who has attained the cessation-concentration (nirodha-samāpatti, the deepest attainment, in which even perception and feeling cease) is renamed this way. Since mind itself is entirely absent during cessation-concentration, the "witnessing" here is attributed to the body specifically rather than to mind: more precisely, upon emerging from that concentration, such a person newly experiences a conscious body in profound stillness, and reflects that this state is the most tranquil imaginable, extremely resembling nirvāṇa itself — having thus personally realized this bodily stillness, they earn the name "body-witness." Yet "body-witness" is not counted among the sūtras' eighteen kinds of noble-ones-in-training, since that classification is established specifically with reference to the undefiled three trainings (moral discipline, concentration, wisdom) and their fruits — and cessation-concentration itself is neither a training nor a training's fruit, so no distinction among trainees is drawn on its basis.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The sūtras speak of a non-returner called body-witness. On the basis of what superior quality is this name established? Verse: a non-returner who has attained the cessation-concentration is renamed body-witness. Explanation: possessing the attainment of cessation-concentration is called having attained cessation-concentration; a non-returner who possesses this attainment within their own person is accordingly renamed body-witness. Such a non-returner is called body-witness because they have personally, bodily witnessed and attained a state resembling nirvāṇa itself. Why is this person said to be a witness specifically through the body? Because mind is entirely absent, and because this attainment arises in dependence on the body. In truth, it should rather be said: upon emerging from cessation-concentration, such a person newly attains what they had not attained before, a conscious body in a state of stillness, and reflects: this cessation-concentration is the most tranquil state, extremely resembling nirvāṇa. Having thus personally realized this stillness of the body, they are called body-witness. Because it is through both the attainment and the knowledge that manifests afterward that one realizes this bodily stillness, the sūtras speak of eighteen kinds of noble-ones-in-training. Why is body-witness not included among these? Because it lacks the relevant basis. What is the relevant basis? The eighteen kinds are established with reference to distinctions among the undefiled three trainings and their fruits. Cessation-concentration itself is neither a training nor a training's fruit, so the distinctions among trainees are not drawn with reference to it.

The coarse distinguishing characteristics of the non-returner are as described above; analyzed in fine detail, however, the number of distinct types reaches into the many thousands. Take "intermediate-existence" nirvāṇa alone: classified by faculty, it already yields three kinds (lower, middle, upper); by meditative stage, four kinds (proceeding to the first dhyāna and so on); by spiritual lineage, six kinds (the "retrogressible" lineage and the rest); by place, sixteen kinds (Brahmā's Retinue and the other specific heavens); by stage-of-detachment, thirty-six kinds (ranging from fully bound within the form realm up through freedom from the fourth dhyāna's eighth grade of defilement). Combining place, lineage, detachment, and faculty together yields a grand total of two thousand five hundred ninety-two: within a single place there are six lineages; each lineage divides into nine by stage-of-detachment (from fully bound up through freedom from the eighth grade); six times nine makes fifty-four; fifty-four times the sixteen places makes eight hundred sixty-four; and multiplying by the three faculties triples this to two thousand five hundred ninety-two — for "intermediate-existence" nirvāṇa alone.

Chinese Source

不還差別麁相如是，若細分析數成多千。其義云何？且如中般約根建立便成三種，下中上根有差別故。約地建立則成四種，往初定等有差別故。約種性建立則成六種，退法種性等有差別故。約處建立成十六種，梵眾天等處差別故。約地離染成三十六，色界具縛乃至已離第四靜慮八品染故。約處、種性、離染、根建立總成二千五百九十二。云何如是？且於一處種性有六，一一種性約離染門差別成九，謂隨何地具縛為初乃至已離八品為後，如是六九成五十四，以十六處乘五十四成八百六十四，以根乘之復成三倍故總成二千五百九十二。

Ru-Yi Translation

The coarse distinguishing characteristics of the non-returner are as described above; if analyzed in fine detail, the number of distinct types reaches into the many thousands. How is this so? Take intermediate-existence nirvāṇa: established with reference to faculty, it yields three kinds, distinguished by lower, middle, or upper faculties. Established with reference to meditative stage, it yields four kinds, distinguished by whether one proceeds to the first dhyāna and so on. Established with reference to spiritual lineage, it yields six kinds, distinguished by the retrogressible lineage and the rest. Established with reference to place, it yields sixteen kinds, distinguished by Brahmā's Retinue and the other specific heavens. Established with reference to stage-of-detachment, it yields thirty-six kinds, ranging from being fully bound within the form realm up through having become free of the eighth grade of the fourth dhyāna's defilement. Established jointly with reference to place, lineage, stage-of-detachment, and faculty together, the total reaches two thousand five hundred ninety-two. How is this arrived at? Within a single place, there are six lineages; for each single lineage, the distinctions by stage-of-detachment number nine, ranging from being fully bound at the outset up through having become free of the eighth grade at the end. Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag the "body-witness" etymology as a genuinely careful piece of technical reasoning, addressing a real puzzle: how can anyone "witness" anything through the body specifically, when mind is entirely absent during the very concentration in question? The treatise's resolution locates the actual witnessing not in the concentration itself but in the moment of emerging from it, when a now-conscious body registers a profound stillness the practitioner has never experienced before — the "witness" is thus a retrospective recognition, not something occurring during the mindless state itself. We have preserved this precise, two-step account exactly as argued, rather than the looser gloss that a "witness" simply means "one who has attained cessation-concentration" without this further explanation.

We should also flag the 2,592-subtype combinatorial calculation as an extraordinary, almost playful demonstration of this tradition's appetite for exhaustive classification — and note that the passage presents this purely as an illustrative example for "intermediate-existence" nirvāṇa specifically, not as a claim that the entire non-returner category totals this number (the other six types would each generate their own similarly enormous combinatorial totals, not summed here). We have reproduced the stated arithmetic (6×9=54, 54×16=864, ×3=2592) exactly as the passage walks through it, without independently re-deriving whether each of the four classifying dimensions (place, lineage, detachment, faculty) is fully independent of the others in every case — the text itself does not address this question, presenting the multiplication as straightforwardly valid.

6. Arhatship: Aiming Toward the Fourth Fruit, and the "Diamond-Like Concentration"

Summing the combinatorial totals across all five form-realm non-returner types (the same exercise just performed for intermediate-existence nirvāṇa applied to birth, with-effort, without-effort, and upstream nirvāṇa as well), the treatise arrives at a grand total of twelve thousand nine hundred sixty distinct sub-types. Having thus explained the third fruit in full, the treatise turns to the fourth and final fruit: arhatship. A non-returner advancing to sever the cultivation-abandoned afflictions of the form and formless realms — from the first grade of the first dhyāna all the way up through the eighth grade of the peak of existence — is, throughout this entire span, renamed "aiming toward arhatship." Within this stage, the ninth and final uninterrupted path, which severs the peak of existence's ninth and last grade of affliction, is given a special name: the "diamond-like concentration" (vajropama-samādhi), since it possesses the functional capacity to shatter every remaining latent affliction without exception, whether already shattered or not, and is the single most excellent concentration among all those capable of severing affliction.

Chinese Source

如是乃至上流亦爾。總計五種，積數合成一萬二千九百六十。已辯第三向果差別，次應建立第四向果。頌曰：上界修惑中，斷初定一品，至有頂八品，皆阿羅漢向。第九無間道，名金剛喻定，盡得俱盡智，成無學應果。論曰：即不還者進斷色界及無色界修所斷惑，從斷初定一品為初，至斷有頂八品為後，應知轉名阿羅漢向。即此所說阿羅漢向中，斷有頂惑第九無間道亦說名為金剛喻定，一切隨眠皆能破故、先已破故。不破一切實有，能破一切功能。諸能斷惑無間道中，此定相應最為勝故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The same holds, in corresponding fashion, all the way through the upstream type as well. Summing across all five categories, the accumulated total comes to twelve thousand nine hundred sixty. Having explained the distinctions of the third fruit and what aims toward it, we should next establish the fourth fruit and what aims toward it. Verse: among the cultivation-abandoned afflictions of the upper realms, from severing the first grade of the first dhyāna up through the eighth grade of the peak of existence, all these stages are aiming toward arhatship. The ninth uninterrupted path is called the diamond-like concentration; exhaustion is attained together with it, the knowledge of exhaustion arises, and one becomes the fruit of no-more-learning, the worthy one. Explanation: that very same non-returner, advancing to sever the cultivation-abandoned afflictions of the form and formless realms, from severing the first grade of the first dhyāna at the outset, up through severing the eighth grade of the peak of existence at the end, should be understood to be renamed aiming toward arhatship throughout. Within this stage, the ninth uninterrupted path, which severs the ninth-grade affliction of the peak of existence, is also called the diamond-like concentration, since it is capable of shattering every remaining latent affliction without exception, whether already shattered or not. It is not that it literally shatters every genuinely existing thing; rather, it possesses the functional capacity to shatter everything that remains. Among all the uninterrupted paths capable of severing affliction, the concentration associated with this particular moment is the most excellent of all.

The diamond-like concentration is said to admit of many further sub-varieties, distinguished by knowledge, mode of activity, and object, since it can be relied upon across any of nine meditative stages. Based on the "not-yet-arrived" preparatory stage, one account counts fifty-two varieties: eight from analogy-knowledge of suffering and origin (four aspects each, taking the peak of existence as object), eight from dharma-knowledge of cessation and the path, thirty-two from analogy-knowledge of cessation (four aspects, separately for each of the eight lower stages), and only four from analogy-knowledge of the path (taken as a single unified object across all eight stages together, since paths of the same category share a common causal continuity). The same pattern applies to the four intermediate dhyāna stages; for the formless attainments, the counts drop to twenty-eight, twenty-four, and twenty respectively, since no dharma-knowledge applies to a formless basis. Other teachers instead count eighty varieties, treating the path-analogy-knowledge as separately counted for each of the eight stages rather than unified, adding twenty-eight to the total; still others count one hundred sixty-four, treating cessation-analogy-knowledge as having both a separate and a combined count for each stage — and all agree the count grows still further once spiritual lineage and faculty are factored in as well.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The diamond-like concentration is said to be of many kinds, since it arises as the uninterrupted path severing the ninth grade of affliction at the peak of existence, and can be relied upon across any of nine stages. Its varieties, distinguished by knowledge, mode, and object, number fifty-two when based on the not-yet-arrived stage: the analogy-knowledge regarding suffering and the origin, taking the peak of existence as object, each has four aspects, giving eight; the dharma-knowledge regarding cessation and the path, each has four aspects, giving eight; the analogy-knowledge regarding cessation, taking the cessation of each of the eight lower stages as object, has four aspects in each case, totaling thirty-two; the analogy-knowledge regarding the path, taking the path of the eight stages as object, has, in total, only four aspects, since the path counteracting the eight stages shares a common causal continuity and must take all eight together as a single object. This yields fifty-two within the not-yet-arrived stage; the same holds for the four intermediate dhyāna stages. For boundless space, twenty-eight; boundless consciousness, twenty-four; nothingness, twenty — since a formless basis has no dharma-knowledge, only analogy-knowledge. Other teachers say the varieties number eighty within the not-yet-arrived stage, since the analogy-knowledge regarding the path, taking the path of each of the eight stages as object, is separately counted with its own four aspects for each, adding twenty-eight to the total; the same holds for the four intermediate dhyāna stages, with forty, thirty-two, and twenty-four for the three formless attainments. Still others hold the varieties number, in total, one hundred sixty-four within the not-yet-arrived stage, since the analogy-knowledge regarding cessation has both a separate and a combined count for each of the eight stages, adding one hundred twelve to the first total; the same holds for the four intermediate dhyāna stages, with fifty-two, thirty-six, and twenty-four for the three formless attainments. If further distinguished by spiritual lineage and faculty, the varieties become even more numerous, to be worked out by the same reasoning.

Because the diamond-like concentration severs the peak of existence's very last grade of affliction, it gives rise to the "knowledge of exhaustion" (kṣaya-jñāna), arising together with the attainment of that affliction's complete exhaustion. The diamond-like concentration is the very last uninterrupted path in the entire process of severing affliction; the knowledge of exhaustion is the very last path of liberation — and because this path of liberation arises, for the very first time, together with the complete exhaustion of every contaminant, it earns the name "knowledge of exhaustion." The moment this knowledge arises, one immediately becomes the fruit of no-more-learning, the arhat, having attained the dharma of the fruit worthy of no further training. Since there is no further, distinct fruit remaining to train toward, the name "no-more-learning" applies; and since such a person is now fit only to act for the benefit of others, and worthy of veneration by all beings still afflicted, the name "arhat" (worthy one) is likewise established.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

We should flag that this section's numerical elaboration (52, 80, or 164 sub-varieties of the diamond-like concentration, depending on which teachers' counting method is followed) is genuinely dense technical bookkeeping presupposing the full sixteen-aspect and dharma/analogy-knowledge framework established earlier in this fascicle and in fascicle 23 — we have translated the stated arithmetic and reasoning faithfully, without attempting to independently verify every multiplication, since the passage presents three competing scholastic counting conventions side by side without declaring a preferred one.

We should also flag the beautifully compact double etymology given for "arhat" at the close of this section — worthy because no further training remains to be sought (hence fit only to act for others' benefit) and worthy because deserving of veneration by all afflicted beings — presented as two complementary aspects of a single meaning rather than competing accounts, which we have preserved together exactly as the text closes this passage ("義准已成," "both meanings are thereby established").
