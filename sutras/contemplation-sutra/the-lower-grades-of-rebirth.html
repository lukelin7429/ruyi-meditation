The Lower Grades of Rebirth

下輩生想：下品上生、下品中生、下品下生

The nine grades reach their floor: people who have done real harm, met at the very last moment of life by a stranger's guidance and, in the sūtra's most quoted and most debated line, saved by nothing more than ten sincere recitations of a name.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T365 · Kālayaśas's translation, 5th century CE Commentary guide: Shandao's 觀經四帖疏 (7th c.), compared with Jingying Huiyuan's 觀無量壽經義疏 (6th c.)
This part covers
The sixteenth and final contemplation: the three lower grades of rebirth — lower-upper, lower-middle, and lower-lower — completing the nine grades begun in Part 5.
The central tension
The larger Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra's Eighteenth Vow explicitly excludes anyone guilty of the five heinous transgressions or slander of the true Dharma from rebirth — yet lower-lower birth in this sūtra appears to describe exactly such a person, saved by ten recitations at the end of life. Shandao's resolution, and Jingying Huiyuan's markedly different account of who this person actually is, are both given below.

27. Lower-Upper Birth

Chinese Source

佛告阿難及韋提希：「下品上生者，或有眾生作眾惡業，雖不誹謗方等經典，如此愚人，多造惡法，無有慚愧，命欲終時遇善知識，為讚大乘十二部經首題名字。以聞如是諸經名故，除却千劫極重惡業。智者復教合掌叉手，稱南無阿彌陀佛。稱佛名故，除五十億劫生死之罪。爾時彼佛，即遣化佛，化觀世音，化大勢至，至行者前，讚言：『善哉！善男子！汝稱佛名故諸罪消滅，我來迎汝。』作是語已，行者即見化佛光明，遍滿其室，見已歡喜，即便命終。乘寶蓮花，隨化佛後，生寶池中，經七七日蓮花乃敷。當花敷時，大悲觀世音菩薩，及大勢至菩薩，放大光明，住其人前，為說甚深十二部經。聞已信解，發無上道心。經十小劫，具百法明門，得入初地；是名下品上生者；得聞佛名、法名及聞僧名，聞三寶名即得往生。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said to Ānanda and Vaidehī: "As for lower-upper birth: there may be beings who have done many evil deeds, and though they have not slandered the great scriptures, such foolish people have committed much wrongdoing, without a trace of shame. When such a person's life is about to end, they meet a good friend, who praises for them the titles of the twelve divisions of the great Mahāyāna scriptures. Simply from hearing the names of these scriptures, they remove a thousand aeons of extremely heavy evil karma. The wise friend further teaches them to press their palms together and say, 'Homage to Amitābha Buddha.' Because they utter the Buddha's name, they remove the karmic offenses of fifty koṭis of aeons of birth and death. At that moment, that Buddha sends forth a transformation-buddha, a transformation-Avalokiteśvara, and a transformation-Mahāsthāmaprāpta, who come before the practitioner and say in praise: 'Excellent, good man — because you have uttered the Buddha's name, your offenses are dissolved, and I have come to welcome you.' Having said this, the practitioner sees the transformation-buddha's light filling the whole room; seeing this, they rejoice, and their life then ends. Mounted on a jeweled lotus flower, they follow behind the transformation-buddha and are born within the jeweled pond. After forty-nine days, the lotus flower opens. As the flower opens, the greatly compassionate Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva and Mahāsthāmaprāpta Bodhisattva release great light, and standing before this person, expound for them the profound twelve divisions of the scriptures. Hearing this, they believe and understand, and give rise to the mind set on the unsurpassed path. After ten small aeons have passed, they are complete in the hundred gateways of Dharma-illumination, and enter the first ground. This is called lower-upper birth — to hear the name of the Buddha, the name of the Dharma, and the name of the Saṅgha, to hear the name of the Three Jewels at all, is itself enough to attain rebirth."

Reading note — after Shandao, compared with Jingying Huiyuan

Shandao poses a question the passage itself invites: why does hearing the titles of the entire twelvefold division of the scriptures remove only a thousand aeons of offense, while a single utterance of the Buddha's name removes fifty koṭis of aeons — a vastly larger figure? His answer turns on the practitioner's condition in the moment: a person this burdened with wrongdoing, now also crushed by the suffering of dying, cannot actually take in an extended teaching — even a good friend's lengthy explanation reaches a mind too scattered and overwhelmed to absorb it fully. A single name, by contrast, is one thing, simple enough for a dying, terrified mind to hold onto and repeat; and because that one act of holding on is done with the whole of whatever attention remains, its effect is disproportionately greater, not smaller, than a longer teaching only partly received.

On who this person is, Jingying Huiyuan reads the entire lower grade differently from Shandao in a way worth stating plainly rather than passing over. Huiyuan holds that all three lower-grade people were, in some past life, genuine Mahāyāna practitioners — the larger Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, in his reading, describes this same category of person as someone who has already given rise to bodhicitta and heard the profound teaching with joy and without doubt; it is only in the present life that they have met bad conditions and fallen into wrongdoing, and this sūtra, he says, "singles out their faults" because that is what the present life happens to show, not because their entire causal history is one of evil. Shandao's commentary, by contrast, offers no such softening for this or the two lower grades that follow: on his reading these are simply ordinary people who have done real wrong, full stop, with no hidden Mahāyāna history posited to make the outcome less startling — the entire weight of their rescue falls on Amitābha's vow-power alone, reached through the sincere utterance of his name. Both readings appear in serious, careful commentary; this site presents them side by side rather than choosing between them.

28. Lower-Middle Birth

Chinese Source

佛告阿難及韋提希：「下品中生者，或有眾生，毀犯五戒、八戒及具足戒，如此愚人，偷僧祇物，盜現前僧物，不淨說法，無有慚愧，以諸惡法而自莊嚴。如此罪人，以惡業故應墮地獄。命欲終時，地獄眾火一時俱至，遇善知識以大慈悲，即為讚說阿彌陀佛十力威德，廣讚彼佛光明神力，亦讚戒、定、慧、解脫、解脫知見。此人聞已，除八十億劫生死之罪。地獄猛火化為涼風，吹諸天華。華上皆有化佛菩薩，迎接此人。如一念頃，即得往生七寶池中蓮花之內，經於六劫，蓮花乃敷。當華敷時，觀世音、大勢至，以梵音聲安慰彼人，為說大乘甚深經典。聞此法已，應時即發無上道心。是名下品中生者。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said to Ānanda and Vaidehī: "As for lower-middle birth: there may be beings who have broken the five precepts, the eight precepts, or the full monastic precepts, and such foolish people have stolen property belonging to the monastic community, whether held in common or set aside for individual use, and have taught the Dharma for corrupt gain, without a trace of shame, adorning themselves instead with every kind of wrongdoing. Such a person, on account of this evil karma, should by rights fall into hell. When their life is about to end, the fires of hell arrive all at once — and they meet a good friend, who, out of great compassion, praises for them Amitābha Buddha's ten powers and majestic virtue, extolling at length that Buddha's light and spiritual power, and also praising his discipline, concentration, wisdom, liberation, and the knowledge and vision of liberation. Hearing this, the person removes the karmic offenses of eighty koṭis of aeons of birth and death. The raging fires of hell transform into a cool wind, blowing heavenly flowers, and upon these flowers stand transformation-buddhas and bodhisattvas, who come to welcome this person. In the space of a single thought, they attain rebirth within a lotus flower in the pond of the seven treasures. After six aeons have passed, the lotus flower opens. As the flower opens, Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta, with voices like Brahmā's own, comfort this person, and expound for them the profound scriptures of the Mahāyāna. On hearing this teaching, they at once give rise to the mind set on the unsurpassed path. This is called lower-middle birth."

Reading note — after Shandao

Shandao does not linger long on this middle grade beyond tracking its place in the escalating structure of the lower three — a heavier offense than lower-upper birth (theft from the monastic community and corrupt teaching, rather than merely "much wrongdoing"), met by a correspondingly more dramatic rescue: the fires of hell itself are already arriving, not merely threatened, when the good friend's praise of Amitābha intervenes and transforms them into a cool wind mid-arrival. The lotus's opening, delayed six full aeons rather than the seven-times-seven days of the grade above, tracks the same escalation — the heavier the karma met at the very end, the longer the interval before the newly reborn practitioner is ready to receive the teaching that awaits them.

29. Lower-Lower Birth

Chinese Source

佛告阿難及韋提希：「下品下生者，或有眾生作不善業，五逆、十惡，具諸不善。如此愚人以惡業故，應墮惡道，經歷多劫，受苦無窮。如此愚人臨命終時，遇善知識，種種安慰，為說妙法，教令念佛，彼人苦逼不遑念佛。善友告言：『汝若不能念彼佛者，應稱歸命無量壽佛。』如是至心令聲不絕，具足十念，稱南無阿彌陀佛。稱佛名故，於念念中，除八十億劫生死之罪。命終之時見金蓮花，猶如日輪，住其人前，如一念頃，即得往生極樂世界。於蓮花中滿十二大劫，蓮花方開。當花敷時，觀世音、大勢至以大悲音聲，即為其人廣說實相，除滅罪法。聞已歡喜，應時即發菩提之心；是名下品下生者。是名下輩生想，名第十六觀。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said to Ānanda and Vaidehī: "As for lower-lower birth: there may be beings who have committed unwholesome acts — the five heinous transgressions, the ten evil deeds, every kind of wrongdoing. Such a foolish person, on account of this evil karma, should by rights fall into the evil destinies, passing through many aeons of unending suffering. When such a foolish person's life is about to end, they meet a good friend, who comforts them in every way and expounds the wondrous Dharma for them, teaching them to hold the Buddha in mind — but this person is so crushed by suffering that they have no chance to do so. The good friend tells them: 'If you cannot hold that Buddha in mind, then you should simply say, I take refuge in Amitāyus Buddha.' In this way, with a sincere mind, making the sound unbroken, they complete ten thoughts, saying, 'Homage to Amitābha Buddha.' Because they utter the Buddha's name, in thought after thought they remove the karmic offenses of eighty koṭis of aeons of birth and death. At the moment their life ends, they see a golden lotus flower, like the disk of the sun, standing before them, and in the space of a single thought, they attain rebirth in Sukhāvatī. Within the lotus flower, a full twelve great aeons pass before the flower opens. As the flower opens, Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta, with voices of great compassion, expound for this person, at length, the true mark of reality, and the Dharma that dissolves all offense. Hearing this, they rejoice, and at once give rise to the mind of awakening. This is called lower-lower birth. This is called the contemplation of the lower grades of rebirth, the sixteenth contemplation."

Reading note — after Shandao, compared with Jingying Huiyuan

This is the sūtra's most quoted and most contested passage, and Shandao confronts directly the objection any careful reader of the wider Pure Land canon will already have in mind: the larger Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra's Eighteenth Vow states plainly that its promise of rebirth does not extend to anyone guilty of the five heinous transgressions or of slandering the true Dharma — and this person appears to be exactly that, saved anyway by ten bare recitations at the very last moment. Shandao's resolution is what he calls the "gate of restraint" (抑止門): the exclusion clause in the Eighteenth Vow, he argues, is not a permanent bar but a deterrent, aimed at beings who have not yet committed these acts — a warning severe enough to stop someone from ever crossing that line in the first place. Once the act is already done, he reasons, compassion does not then withdraw and abandon the offender to what the exclusion clause threatens; the vow's warning and the sūtra's rescue are addressed to two different moments — one to a choice not yet made, the other to a life already lived and now ending. He treats slander of the Dharma the same way: the sūtra's own silence about slanderers among the saved here is read as directed at future slander, not as a second, permanent exclusion layered onto genuine deathbed repentance.

Here again, Jingying Huiyuan's account differs in a way worth stating without softening either side. Where Shandao's lower-lower person is, without qualification, someone who has simply done terrible things, Huiyuan reads all three lower grades — including this one — as people who "in a past life cultivated the Mahāyāna," whose present offenses, however severe, sit on top of an older, hidden history of genuine practice; their rescue at the end, on his reading, draws on merit and inclination already present from before, newly reactivated by the good friend's guidance, rather than created from nothing in a dying person's final ten thoughts. Shandao's account asks for no such prior merit: the whole weight of this person's rescue rests on Amitābha's vow-power alone, met by nothing more than a sincere utterance of his name at the very last possible moment. Both are careful, serious readings of the same eleven lines of text, and they say meaningfully different things about how far grace of this kind is meant to reach. This site leaves the question open, as it has at other points in this sūtra's most consequential passages, rather than resolving it on the translator's own authority.
