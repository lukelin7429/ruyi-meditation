The Upper Grades of Rebirth

上輩生想：上品上生、上品中生、上品下生

The sūtra turns from contemplation to conduct: nine grades of rebirth, open to whoever meets death with sincerity, deep faith, and the wish to be reborn — beginning with the three upper grades, and a centuries-old disagreement over exactly who they describe.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T365 · Kālayaśas's translation, 5th century CE Commentary guide: Shandao's 觀經四帖疏 (7th c.), compared with Jingying Huiyuan's 觀無量壽經義疏 (6th c.)
This part covers
The fourteenth contemplation: the three upper grades of rebirth — upper-upper, upper-middle, and upper-lower — the first section of what Shandao calls the "dispersed good" (散善) of the nine grades, as distinct from the sixteen contemplations' "meditative good" (定善) covered in Parts 2 through 4.
A live disagreement
Shandao and Jingying Huiyuan read the nine grades very differently — Huiyuan holds that the upper grades describe bodhisattvas who have already reached specific advanced stages through their own cultivation; Shandao insists all nine grades, top to bottom, describe ordinary unawakened beings relying entirely on Amitāyus's vow. Both readings are given below, without resolving between them.

21. Upper-Upper Birth

Chinese Source

佛告阿難及韋提希：「凡生西方有九品人。上品上生者，若有眾生願生彼國者，發三種心，即便往生。何等為三？一者、至誠心。二者、深心。三者、迴向發願心。具三心者必生彼國。復有三種眾生，當得往生。何等為三？一者、慈心不殺，具諸戒行。二者、讀誦大乘方等經典。三者、修行六念，迴向發願生彼佛國。具此功德，一日乃至七日，即得往生。生彼國時，此人精進勇猛故，阿彌陀如來與觀世音及大勢至，無數化佛，百千比丘，聲聞大眾，無量諸天，七寶宮殿，觀世音菩薩執金剛臺，與大勢至菩薩至行者前。阿彌陀佛放大光明，照行者身，與諸菩薩授手迎接。觀世音、大勢至與無數菩薩，讚歎行者，勸進其心。行者見已，歡喜踊躍。自見其身乘金剛臺，隨從佛後，如彈指頃，往生彼國。生彼國已，見佛色身眾相具足，見諸菩薩色相具足。光明寶林，演說妙法。聞已即悟無生法忍。經須臾間歷事諸佛，遍十方界，於諸佛前次第受記。還至本國，得無量百千陀羅尼門，是名上品上生者。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said to Ānanda and Vaidehī: "All those born in the west fall into nine grades. As for upper-upper birth: if there are beings who wish to be born in that land and give rise to three minds, they are thereby born there. What are these three? First, the sincere mind. Second, the deep mind. Third, the mind that dedicates merit and vows to be reborn. Whoever holds these three minds will certainly be born in that land. There are, further, three kinds of beings who will attain rebirth. What are these three? First, those who hold a compassionate mind that does no killing, and who are complete in every precept. Second, those who read and recite the great scriptures of the Mahāyāna. Third, those who practice the six recollections, and dedicate this merit with the vow to be reborn in that Buddha's land. Whoever is complete in this merit, whether for one day or as long as seven days, will thereby attain rebirth. When such a person is born in that land, because they were vigorous and courageous in their practice, Amitābha the Tathāgata, together with Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta, countless transformation-buddhas, hundreds of thousands of monks, an assembly of śrāvakas, and immeasurable devas, with palaces of the seven treasures — Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva holding a vajra platform — will come, with Mahāsthāmaprāpta Bodhisattva, before the practitioner. Amitābha Buddha releases a great light, illuminating the practitioner's body, and together with the bodhisattvas extends his hand to receive them. Avalokiteśvara, Mahāsthāmaprāpta, and countless bodhisattvas praise the practitioner, encouraging their resolve. Seeing this, the practitioner rejoices and leaps for joy, and sees their own body mounted upon the vajra platform, following behind the Buddha; in the space of a finger-snap, they are reborn in that land. Once reborn there, they see the Buddha's bodily form, complete in every mark, and see the bodhisattvas' forms, complete in every mark as well. Light and jeweled groves proclaim the wondrous Dharma, and on hearing it, they at once awaken to the acceptance of the non-arising of all dharmas. In the space of a moment, they pass among every buddha, traversing the worlds of the ten directions, and receive, before each buddha in turn, the prediction of their own awakening. Returning then to their own land, they attain immeasurable hundreds of thousands of dhāraṇī-gateways. This is called upper-upper birth."

Reading note — after Shandao, compared with Jingying Huiyuan

Shandao devotes an extraordinary amount of commentary to unpacking the "three minds" named at the opening of this passage, since he treats them as the real substance of every one of the nine grades, not only the first. The sincere mind (至誠心) means that whatever is done in body, speech, or mind toward rebirth must be done with real sincerity, not an outward show of diligence concealing an inwardly divided heart. The deep mind (深心) is, in his gloss, two convictions held at once without contradiction: a settled acceptance that one is, in fact, an ordinary being mired in karmic offense with no way out by one's own strength, and an equally settled trust that Amitāyus's forty-eight vows carry precisely such a being to rebirth regardless. The mind that dedicates merit and vows rebirth (迴向發願心) is simply the resolve to direct whatever good one has done, however small, toward that one aim, without wavering when others insist it cannot work. For this last point Shandao offers his most famous image: a traveler walking west, trapped between a river of fire to the south and a river of water to the north, both without end, with only a narrow white path a few inches wide running between them toward the far shore — bandits and wild beasts pressing from behind, the flames licking one side of the path and the waves washing the other. A voice from the near shore urges him forward; a voice from the far shore promises to protect him if he keeps walking without turning back. He goes forward without looking back, and reaches the other side. Shandao's own gloss on the parable: the near shore is this burning world, the far shore is Sukhāvatī, the bandits are the six senses and the five aggregates posing as friends, the two rivers are craving and anger, and the narrow white path is the thin, precious wish for rebirth that a being's own greed and hatred have not yet managed to drown out — the voice behind is Śākyamuni's teaching, urging the traveler on after his own departure from the world; the voice ahead is Amitāyus's vow, calling him the rest of the way.

On who this grade actually describes, Shandao is in open disagreement with a body of earlier commentary that Jingying Huiyuan's own reading closely represents. Huiyuan's 觀無量壽經義疏 classifies the nine grades according to the specific stage of bodhisattva attainment each is thought to already hold before death: upper-upper birth as bodhisattvas of the fourth ground and above, upper-middle as bodhisattvas within the first through third grounds, upper-lower as bodhisattvas from the stage of provisional lineage up to the first ground — a reading grounded in matching the sūtra's stated outcomes (attaining the acceptance of non-arising "at once," or "within one small aeon," or "within three small aeons") against the technical stage-durations given in treatises such as the Yogâcārabhūmi. Shandao's rebuttal is direct: bodhisattvas of the fourth ground and above, he writes, already possess bodies free of ordinary birth and death, dwell in a reward-land, constantly hear a reward-buddha teach, and radiate compassion across the ten directions in an instant — such beings would have no conceivable need of Vaidehī's intercession or any request to be reborn in Sukhāvatī at all. Reading the sūtra's own stated qualification for this very grade — as little as one to seven days of sincere practice — he concludes that all nine grades, without exception, describe ordinary unawakened beings (凡夫), distinguished from one another only by what kind of conditions they happened to meet at the end of life, not by any attainment already reached before arriving. This site does not resolve the disagreement; both readings come from serious, careful commentators working closely with the same text, and the difference between them turns on a real and still-debated question about how literally to read the sūtra's own stated timescales.

22. Upper-Middle Birth

Chinese Source

「上品中生者，不必受持讀誦方等經典。善解義趣，於第一義心不驚動，深信因果，不謗大乘；以此功德，迴向願求生極樂國。行此行者命欲終時，阿彌陀佛與觀世音及大勢至，無量大眾，眷屬圍繞，持紫金臺至行者前，讚言：『法子！汝行大乘，解第一義，是故我今來迎接汝。』與千化佛一時授手。行者自見坐紫金臺，合掌叉手，讚歎諸佛，如一念頃，即生彼國七寶池中。此紫金臺如大寶花，經宿即開。行者身作紫磨金色，足下亦有七寶蓮華。佛及菩薩俱放光明，照行者身，目即開明。因前宿習，普聞眾聲，純說甚深第一義諦。即下金臺，禮佛合掌讚歎世尊。經於七日，應時即於阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，得不退轉。應時即能飛至十方，歷事諸佛，於諸佛所修諸三昧。經一小劫得無生法忍，現前受記，是名上品中生者。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"As for upper-middle birth: such a person need not have received, read, or recited the great scriptures of the Mahāyāna, but understands their meaning well, and their mind is not shaken by the ultimate truth; they believe deeply in cause and effect, and do not slander the Mahāyāna. With this merit, they dedicate it and vow to be born in Sukhāvatī. When such a practitioner's life is about to end, Amitābha Buddha, together with Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta, surrounded by an immeasurable retinue, brings a platform of purple gold before the practitioner, and says in praise: 'Child of the Dharma, you have practiced the Mahāyāna and understood the ultimate truth — for this reason I have come now to receive you.' Together with a thousand transformation-buddhas, he extends his hand at once. The practitioner sees themselves seated upon the platform of purple gold, presses their palms together, and praises the buddhas; in the space of a single thought, they are reborn in the pond of the seven treasures in that land. This platform of purple gold is like a great jeweled flower, and opens after a single night has passed. The practitioner's body becomes the color of true purple-gold, and beneath their feet, too, are lotus flowers of the seven treasures. The Buddha and the bodhisattvas together release light, illuminating the practitioner's body, and their eyes open at once. Because of what they were already accustomed to before, they hear every voice around them proclaiming, purely, the profound truth of the first principle. They then descend from the golden platform, bow to the Buddha, press their palms together, and praise the World-Honored One. After seven days have passed, they at once attain non-retrogression in unsurpassed, perfect awakening. At once they are able to fly to the ten directions, passing among the buddhas there, and cultivate every samādhi in the presence of each. After one small aeon has passed, they attain the acceptance of the non-arising of all dharmas, and receive, in that buddha's presence, the prediction of their own awakening. This is called upper-middle birth."

Reading note — after Shandao

Shandao draws attention to a single, precise word in this passage's opening: "need not" (不必) have received and recited the Mahāyāna scriptures. He reads this as a deliberate loosening from the grade above it — upper-upper birth, in his account, does include practitioners who recite the great scriptures among its three alternate qualifying paths; upper-middle birth explicitly does not require this, asking only genuine understanding and unshaken confidence in emptiness and cause and effect together. The distinguishing mark of this whole grade, for him, is not a different category of person but a slightly lesser intensity of practice — which is also why, on his reading, the welcoming retinue that comes for this grade carries a platform of purple gold rather than the vajra platform of the grade above, and why the newly reborn practitioner needs a single night before the flower opens, rather than opening at the very instant of arrival.

He also flags a small but easily overlooked detail about time: the passage's timeline — the flower opening after one night, buddhahood's non-retrogression secured after seven days — should not, in his reading, be assumed to mean earthly time as experienced here. He notes elsewhere in his commentary on the grade above this one that a period stated as "seven days" in this land may correspond to no more than a single thought-moment in Sukhāvatī itself, given how differently the two places are said to measure duration — a caution worth carrying into every grade's stated timescale, not only this one.

23. Upper-Lower Birth

Chinese Source

「上品下生者，亦信因果，不謗大乘，但發無上道心，以此功德，迴向願求生極樂國。彼行者命欲終時，阿彌陀佛及觀世音并大勢至，與諸眷屬持金蓮華，化作五百化佛，來迎此人。五百化佛一時授手，讚言：『法子！汝今清淨，發無上道心，我來迎汝。』見此事時，即自見身坐金蓮花。坐已華合，隨世尊後，即得往生七寶池中。一日一夜蓮花乃開，七日之中乃得見佛。雖見佛身，於眾相好心不明了，於三七日後乃了了見。聞眾音聲，皆演妙法。遊歷十方，供養諸佛。於諸佛前聞甚深法，經三小劫得百法明門，住歡喜地。是名上品下生者。是名上輩生想，名第十四觀。作是觀者，名為正觀。若他觀者，名為邪觀。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"As for upper-lower birth: such a person likewise believes in cause and effect, and does not slander the Mahāyāna, but has simply given rise to the mind set on the unsurpassed path; with this merit, they dedicate it and vow to be born in Sukhāvatī. When that practitioner's life is about to end, Amitābha Buddha, together with Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta and their retinue, carries a golden lotus flower, manifesting five hundred transformation-buddhas, to welcome this person. The five hundred transformation-buddhas extend their hands at once, saying in praise: 'Child of the Dharma, you are now pure, having given rise to the mind set on the unsurpassed path — I have come to welcome you.' On seeing this, the practitioner sees themselves seated on a golden lotus flower; once seated, the flower closes, and, following behind the World-Honored One, they attain rebirth in the pond of the seven treasures. After a single day and night, the lotus flower opens; within seven days, they come to see the Buddha. Though they see the Buddha's body, their mind is not yet clear about his full array of excellent marks, and only after three full weeks do they see it with complete clarity. They hear every sound proclaiming the wondrous Dharma, and travel through the ten directions, making offerings to every buddha. In the presence of these buddhas they hear the profound Dharma, and after three small aeons have passed, they attain the hundred gateways of Dharma-illumination, and abide on the ground of joy. This is called upper-lower birth. This is called the contemplation of the upper grades of rebirth, the fourteenth contemplation. To contemplate in this way is called right contemplation; to contemplate otherwise is called wrong contemplation."

Reading note — after Shandao

Shandao notes that this grade's stated qualification is the barest of the three: not scriptural learning, not even the fully articulated confidence of the grade above it, but simply "the mind set on the unsurpassed path" — the bare arising of the wish for awakening itself, joined to ordinary belief in cause and effect and a refusal to disparage the Mahāyāna. He treats this as the natural floor of the upper grade as a whole: everything above upper-lower birth adds more — scriptural learning, deeper unshaken confidence — but this grade shows what the upper grade requires at minimum, which turns out to be strikingly little.

He connects this passage to a distinction he develops at length elsewhere in his commentary, between what he calls "correct practices" (正行) — reciting this sūtra and the other Pure Land sūtras, contemplating and holding Sukhāvatī's adornments in mind, bowing to, calling on, and praising Amitāyus specifically — and "sundry practices" (雜行), every other kind of good deed, genuine as it may be. Within the correct practices, he further isolates one as the "rightly settled karma" (正定業): sustained, uninterrupted mindfulness of Amitāyus's own name, which he holds to be in direct accord with that Buddha's vow in a way no other practice, however meritorious, quite matches. This distinction — drawn from Shandao's broader commentary rather than from this passage alone — became, transmitted through his writings, one of the central pillars on which the Japanese teacher Hōnen later built an entire school of practice centered on the recitation of Amitāyus's name above every other observance.
