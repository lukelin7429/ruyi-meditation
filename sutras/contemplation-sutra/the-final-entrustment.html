The Final Entrustment

得益分、流通分

Vaidehī and her five hundred attendants awaken on the spot. Ānanda asks what this teaching should be called — and the Buddha's answer, on Shandao's reading, quietly collapses everything taught across sixteen contemplations and nine grades into a single instruction: hold the name.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T365 · Kālayaśas's translation, 5th century CE Commentary guide: Shandao's 觀經四帖疏 (7th c.)
This part covers
Vaidehī and her five hundred attendants awakening to the acceptance of non-arising on the spot (得益分); Ānanda's question about the sūtra's name, and the Buddha's answer, including this sūtra's own account of what it means to hold the name of Amitāyus (流通分); and the Buddha's return through the air to Vulture Peak, where Ānanda retells the entire teaching to the wider assembly.
Closing the frame
This part closes the narrative frame opened in Part 1 — Vaidehī, imprisoned and grieving, now stands as the first person in this sūtra to reach the very benefit she once begged the Buddha to show her.

30. Vaidehī and Her Five Hundred Attendants Awaken

Chinese Source

爾時世尊說是語時，韋提希與五百侍女，聞佛所說，應時即見極樂世界廣長之相，得見佛身及二菩薩。心生歡喜，歎未曾有，豁然大悟，得無生忍。五百侍女發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，願生彼國。世尊悉記，皆當往生，生彼國已，獲得諸佛現前三昧。無量諸天，發無上道心。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the World-Honored One had spoken these words, Vaidehī, together with her five hundred attendant women, on hearing what the Buddha had said, at that very moment beheld the vast and far-reaching form of Sukhāvatī, and saw the Buddha's own body and the two bodhisattvas. Their hearts filled with joy, and they exclaimed at what they had never known before, and, opening wide in sudden understanding, attained the acceptance of the non-arising of all dharmas. The five hundred attendant women gave rise to the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and vowed to be born in that land. The World-Honored One gave to each of them, in turn, the prediction that they would all attain rebirth there; and once reborn in that land, they would obtain the samādhi of beholding every buddha directly present. Immeasurable devas, too, gave rise to the mind set on the unsurpassed path.

Reading note — after Shandao

Shandao reads this moment as the sūtra's frame closing exactly where it opened: Vaidehī, who began this entire teaching shut in a locked room, stripped of her own necklaces in grief, begging for nothing more than "a place free of grief and affliction," is now the first person within the sūtra's own narrative to reach the benefit she once merely asked to be shown. Her attendants receiving individual predictions of their own future rebirth — not a collective promise but each woman named in turn — he reads as underscoring that what has just happened is not a generic blessing extended to a crowd, but something granted to each of five hundred particular people, on the same terms available to the reader arriving at this sūtra centuries later.

31. Ānanda Asks the Sūtra's Name

Chinese Source

爾時阿難，即從座起，前白佛言：「世尊！當何名此經？此法之要，當云何受持？」佛告阿難：「此經名『觀極樂國土無量壽佛觀世音菩薩大勢至菩薩』，亦名『淨除業障生諸佛前』。汝當受持，無令忘失！行此三昧者，現身得見無量壽佛及二大士。若善男子及善女人，但聞佛名、二菩薩名，除無量劫生死之罪，何況憶念！若念佛者，當知此人即是人中芬陀利花，觀世音菩薩、大勢至菩薩為其勝友，當坐道場，生諸佛家。」 佛告阿難：「汝好持是語。持是語者，即是持無量壽佛名。」 佛說此語時，尊者目連、尊者阿難及韋提希等，聞佛所說，皆大歡喜。

Ru-Yi Translation

Then Ānanda rose from his seat and came forward, saying to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, what should this sūtra be called? And how should this essential teaching be upheld?" The Buddha said to Ānanda: "This sūtra is called 'The Contemplation of the Land of Sukhāvatī, of Amitāyus Buddha, of Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, and of Mahāsthāmaprāpta Bodhisattva'; it is also called 'The Purification and Removal of Karmic Hindrance, and Birth in the Presence of Every Buddha.' You must uphold this teaching, and never let it be forgotten. Whoever practices this samādhi will, in this very body, come to see Amitāyus Buddha and the two great bodhisattvas. If there is a good son or good daughter who merely hears the Buddha's name and the two bodhisattvas' names, this alone removes the karmic offenses of immeasurable aeons of birth and death — how much more so if they hold them in mind! Whoever is mindful of the Buddha should be known as the puṇḍarīka among people; Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva and Mahāsthāmaprāpta Bodhisattva become their finest friends, and they will sit in the very place of awakening, and be born into the household of every buddha." The Buddha said to Ānanda: "Uphold these words well. To uphold these words is nothing other than to uphold the name of Amitāyus Buddha." When the Buddha had spoken these words, the Venerable Maudgalyāyana, the Venerable Ānanda, and Vaidehī and the others, hearing what the Buddha had said, all rejoiced greatly.

Reading note — after Shandao

Shandao treats the Buddha's closing instruction to Ānanda as the single most consequential sentence in the entire sūtra, and it is worth being precise about why. Everything taught across the sixteen contemplations and the nine grades of rebirth — the meditative good of ground, tree, and pond, and the dispersed good of precepts, filial care, and dying repentance — has, up to this point, been a wide field of different practices for different capacities. Ānanda then asks the most practical question available: given all of this, what does it actually mean to "uphold" this teaching, going forward? The Buddha's answer does not summarize the sixteen contemplations, or the nine grades, or offer a balanced instruction to practice a bit of everything. It names one specific act: "to uphold these words is nothing other than to uphold the name of Amitāyus Buddha" (持是語者，即是持無量壽佛名).

Shandao's own gloss on this moment, in his closing remarks on the sūtra as a whole, states the conclusion he draws from it without qualification: "though this sūtra sets out both the meditative and the dispersed gates of goodness, looking to the Buddha's own fundamental vow, his intent is that beings should single-mindedly and exclusively recite Amitābha's name" (望佛本願，意在眾生一向專稱彌陀佛名). On this reading, the sixteen contemplations and the nine grades were never meant as sixteen and nine separate equally weighted paths; they were the necessary means by which the sūtra could meet beings of every different capacity where each of them stood, before arriving, in this final instruction, at what the entire teaching was pointing toward all along. It was precisely this passage, read together with the ninth contemplation's account of the Buddha's embracing light in Part 3, that later Japanese Pure Land teachers — Hōnen chief among them — took as scriptural grounds for centering an entire tradition of practice on the recitation of this one name above every other observance this sūtra describes.

32. The Return to Vulture Peak

Chinese Source

爾時世尊，足步虛空，還耆闍崛山。爾時，阿難廣為大眾說如上事。無量人、天、龍、神、夜叉，聞佛所說，皆大歡喜，禮佛而退。

Ru-Yi Translation

Then the World-Honored One, walking on the open air, returned to Vulture Peak. And Ānanda then related, at length, to the great assembly there, everything that had happened as told above. Immeasurable humans, devas, dragons, spirits, and yakṣas, hearing what the Buddha had said, all rejoiced greatly, bowed to the Buddha, and withdrew.

Reading note — after Shandao

Shandao points out a structural feature of this sūtra easy to miss on a single reading: everything from Vaidehī's imprisonment in Part 1 up through the previous section took place inside the royal palace, witnessed only by Vaidehī, Ānanda, and Maudgalyāyana — the Buddha having traveled there in secret, vanishing from Vulture Peak and appearing in the palace, precisely so that Ajātaśatru would not learn of it. The wider assembly on Vulture Peak, including the twelve hundred and fifty monks and the bodhisattvas named at the very opening of the sūtra, never witnessed any of it directly; they only learn what happened, secondhand, through Ānanda's retelling once the Buddha has returned. Shandao reads this two-part structure — a private teaching given first, then publicly retransmitted through a single reliable witness — as itself part of the sūtra's content: the entire teaching reaches every later reader only because Ānanda, true to the vow he made at its very start to "uphold this teaching, and proclaim the Buddha's words widely to the many," carried it out of a locked room and into the world.
