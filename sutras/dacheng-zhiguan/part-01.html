The Prefaces and the One Mind as Support: The Empty and Nonempty Tathāgatagarbha

The Dharma Gate of Mahāyāna Calming-and-Contemplation (T1924), attributed to Huisi of Nanyue (515–577), was lost in China and returned from Japan around the year 1000; this part opens with the two prefaces written for its first printing, by the official Zhu Di and by Zunshi, which tell that story. The work itself is a dialogue between a śramaṇa and a visitor. After defining calming and contemplation, the śramaṇa announces five topics and begins the first: the "support" of practice, which is the one mind. He explains its names (the mind pure in its own nature, suchness, buddha-nature, the Dharma-body, the tathāgatagarbha, the dharma-realm, the Dharma-nature) and traces how ignorance "in the seed phase" and "in the fruit phase" perfume the mind and wear away. He then describes the mind's substance as free of marks, as neither one with nor different from its illusory marks, and as the "empty" and "nonempty" tathāgatagarbha. Here the text makes its distinctive claim: the mind's substance inherently possesses both a pure and a defiled nature, while only phenomenal defilement can be removed. The part ends with the mutual inclusion of phenomena, shown by imagining a pore and a city, and by dreams in which years pass during a meal. The vocabulary is largely that of the Awakening of Faith.

Ru-Yi Translation

Since [the Buddha] showed extinction in the Crane Grove, the worthies and sages who have appeared in response to the world are not one. All have penetrated great wisdom by the six perfections and the myriad practices, abiding securely in the dharma-realm and rescuing the deluded multitudes, and the gold-lettered texts and jeweled scrolls are fully contained in the storehouse of all dharmas. But as for emptying all dharmas and realizing every nature, not displaying the body in the three realms, the intent reaching the true source of right awakening and revealing the true mark of Vairocana — this is seen in the Calming-and-Contemplation of Great Master Nanyue. The Great Master was one of the holy assembly at the Buddha's gathering on Vulture Peak; for three lifetimes his conditions for teaching lay at the peaks of Heng. Secretly receiving the Buddha's intent and personally hearing the sound of the Dharma, he gathered together the essentials of the mind of Aśvaghoṣa and Nāgārjuna and possessed the complete meaning of bodhi and nirvāṇa. Therefore he composed the Calming-and-Contemplation in two treatises, upper and lower, dispelling the true and the false in a single thought and making clear that substance and mark leave no trace: the empty fist opens into a hand, and there is no thing to be seen. From this the principle of calming-and-contemplation is revealed, and through this its gate of tranquility and illumination is entered. It is the root of the lineage of transcending the world and the guide of the seed of buddhahood: without passing through asaṃkhyeya [eons], one leaps directly to the stage of the sages.

Ru-Yi Translation

Alas! Though this teaching was greatly manifest, showing and opening [the way] to those to come, the human world had not yet seen it widely; it drifted overseas for some five hundred years. In the Xianping era, the Japanese monk Jakushō (Ch. Jizhao) crossed the sea with this teaching, and it returned again to our sagely court. In the summer, in the fourth month of the fourth year of Tianxi, Zunshi, teaching master of Tianzhu on Mount Lingyin, wishing to make the buddha-seed of every life all become the supreme condition, had its text carved [for printing], and further asked me for a preface. I reflect again that the wish-fulfilling pearl, as it is called, has already returned to Hepu; the empty room gives forth whiteness, and one sees the Dharma-body while sitting. Considering that I have not yet reached [the point of] boring into it and looking up at it, praise from me would have nothing to recommend it. I only wish that all believing minds who see it may practice it, and that those who practice it may realize it; that they be unmoved before all objects and undefiled amid all dharmas; that once receiving it they never regress, and once attaining it they attain it forever; and that to the end of the future they may always be born together with Great Master Nanyue and carry out the work of the Tathāgata.

Ru-Yi Translation

Calming-and-contemplation is function; it is rooted in clarity and stillness. Clarity and stillness are virtue; they are rooted in the one nature. The nature's substance being originally awake is called clarity; awakening's substance being originally tranquil is called stillness; clarity and stillness being nondual is called substance. If the substance has nothing divided in it, where would clarity and stillness lodge? If the substance has nothing it does not possess, then clarity and stillness are there. Speaking of the substance, it is not one yet always one; speaking of the virtue, it is nondual yet always two. It is simply divided yet undivided, simply one yet not one. Substance and virtue never change: forcibly named, this is the nature of the myriad dharmas. Substance and virtue have no abiding: forcibly named, this is the root of the myriad dharmas.

Chinese Source

萬法者復何謂也？謂舉體明靜之所為也。何其然乎？良由無始本覺之明疆照，照生而自惑謂之昏；無始無住之本隨緣，緣起而自亂謂之動。昏動既作，萬法生焉，揑目空華豈是他物？故云：不變隨緣名之為心，隨緣不變名之為性。心昏動也，性明靜也。若知無始即明而為昏，故可了今即動而為靜。於是聖人見其昏動可即也、明靜可復也，故因靜以訓止，止其動也；因明以教觀，觀其昏也。使其究一念即動而靜、即昏而明，昏動既息，萬法自亡，但存乎明靜之體矣，是為圓頓，是為無作。是如來行，是照性成修，修成而用廢。誰論止觀體顯而性泯，亦無明靜豁然；誰寄無所名焉，為示物旨歸。止成謂之解脫，觀成謂之般若，體顯謂之法身。是三即一、是一即三，如伊三點、如天三目，非縱橫也、非一異也，是為不思議三德，是為大般涅槃也。

Ru-Yi Translation

And what are the myriad dharmas? They are what the whole substance of clarity and stillness does. How is this so? It is because the clarity of beginningless original awakening forcibly illuminates; illumination arises and deludes itself, and this is called dimness. The root without abiding, beginningless, follows conditions; conditions arise and disturb themselves, and this is called movement. Once dimness and movement are produced, the myriad dharmas arise. The flowers in the sky [seen when] pressing the eyes — how could they be some other thing? Hence it is said: "Unchanging yet following conditions is called mind; following conditions yet unchanging is called nature." Mind is dimness and movement; nature is clarity and stillness. If one knows that from beginningless time it was clarity itself that became dimness, then one can understand that now movement itself becomes stillness. Thereupon the sages saw that dimness and movement can be made identical [with clarity and stillness], and that clarity and stillness can be restored. So on the basis of stillness they taught calming, to calm the movement; on the basis of clarity they taught contemplation, to contemplate the dimness. They caused [people] to investigate to the end that a single thought is still right within movement and clear right within dimness. Once dimness and movement cease, the myriad dharmas vanish of themselves, and only the substance of clarity and stillness remains. This is the perfect-and-sudden; this is the unconditioned. This is the practice of the Tathāgata; this is illuminating the nature to accomplish cultivation; when cultivation is accomplished, the function is set aside. Who would speak of calming-and-contemplation? The substance is revealed and the nature vanishes; there is no clarity or stillness either, [only] vast openness. Who would lodge [a name]? It has nothing by which to be named; [names are given] to show beings where to return. Calming accomplished is called liberation; contemplation accomplished is called prajñā; the substance revealed is called the Dharma-body. These three are one, and this one is three, like the three dots of the Siddhaṃ letter i, like the three eyes of the god — neither vertical nor horizontal, neither one nor different. These are the inconceivable three virtues; this is the great parinirvāṇa.

Ru-Yi Translation

Ah! This Dharma, from the time [the Buddha] hid his light in the Crane Grove, was conferred on Mahākāśyapa; Kāśyapa conferred it on Ānanda; from Ānanda on, lamp succeeded lamp, until the eleventh, Aśvaghoṣa. Aśvaghoṣa conferred it on Nāgārjuna, and Nāgārjuna lodged this Dharma in words in the Treatise on the Middle. When the treatise crossed to the eastern Xia, only Chan Master Huiwen of the Huai river understood it, and he conferred it on Great Master Nanyue. Nanyue, following it, illuminated the mind and so returned to the nature; he attained the purity of the six faculties, and his stage bordered on that of the sages. In this the function of calming-and-contemplation is verified.

Ru-Yi Translation

Our Great Master, regretting that later generations would not hear of it, brought forth these several tens of thousands of words from his great compassionate mind, and titled it Mahāyāna Calming-and-Contemplation, also called The One Vehicle, also called Detailed Presentation of the Essentials of Mind. It is divided into two fascicles: the first opens the understanding of calming-and-contemplation; the second shows the practice of calming-and-contemplation. Understanding and practice being complete, they are like eyes and feet, enabling us, settled and at ease, without shifting, to be carried to the cool pond. Ah! As for this text, the years were long and distant, and so it lay hidden overseas; [now] the way is about to be practiced again. Indeed, in the third year of Xianping, the Japanese Great Master Entsū, Jakushō, turned his staff away from Fusang and floated his cup across the lands of the Xia. Having climbed the Mao ridge, he opened his case and brought out the scroll; Zunshi, a śramaṇa of Tianzhu, was the first to obtain it. Vice Director of Revenue Zhu Di placed a preface at its head and gave from his salary to carve blocks and circulate it widely. How great is this Dharma! It first came by transmission from the west, like the rising of the moon; now it returns again from the east, like the rising of the sun. Its white image and round radiance in the end circle back to our land. Therefore I have set out a general preface to record its concealment and manifestation.

Someone asked a śramaṇa: "The nature we are endowed with is of this [one] stuff, but what is entrusted to cultivation differs. Ru-Yi Translation

The śramaṇa said: "Excellent, child of the Buddha! You are able to arouse this unsurpassed mind and delight to hear the practice of the Mahāyāna. Right now you have already gone beyond the sphere of the two vehicles — how much more if you wish to hear and practice it! Yet although you have aroused this superior mind, you must rely on practice to accomplish its virtue. But methods of practice differ in myriad ways, and the entrances to the path are not one. Now, relying on the sūtras and treatises, I will briefly explain for you the two gates of Mahāyāna calming and contemplation; by relying on this Dharma you will quickly be able to accomplish what you wish." The visitor said: "Excellent! Please explain it, to fulfill my intent, and also so that others may benefit in turn; then the transmission of the lamp will not be cut off, and this will repay the Buddha's kindness." The śramaṇa said: "Listen attentively and take it in well; I will explain it for you.

Chinese Source

所言止者，謂知一切諸法從本已來性自非有，不生不滅，但以虛妄因緣故，非有而有。然彼有法，有即非有，唯是一心，體無分別。作是觀者，能令妄念不流，故名為止。所言觀者，雖知本不生、今不滅，而以心性緣起不無虛妄世用，猶如幻夢非有而有，故名為觀。外人曰：余解昧識微，聞斯未能即悟，願以方便更為開示沙門曰：然。更當為汝廣作分別，亦令未聞尋之取悟也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"What is called calming means knowing that all dharmas, from the very beginning, are by nature themselves nonexistent, neither arising nor ceasing, but because of false causes and conditions they exist though nonexistent. Yet those existent dharmas, existing, are not existent; they are only the one mind, whose substance is without discrimination. One who contemplates in this way can keep deluded thoughts from flowing; therefore it is called calming. What is called contemplation means that although one knows [dharmas] originally do not arise and now do not cease, yet because of the conditioned arising of the mind's nature, false worldly functions are not absent, like illusions and dreams that exist though nonexistent; therefore it is called contemplation." The visitor said: "My understanding is dim and my discernment slight; hearing this, I cannot awaken at once. Please explain it further for me by expedient means." The śramaṇa said: "So be it. I will make extensive distinctions for you, so that those who have not heard may also seek it out and awaken."

Such names are measureless and boundless; therefore it says "many names."

The Meaning of the Names: Pure Mind, Suchness, Buddha-Nature

Chinese Source

次辨釋名義。問曰：云何名為自性清淨心耶？答曰：此心無始以來雖為無明染法所覆，而性淨無改，故名為淨。何以故？無明染法本來與心相離故。云何為離？謂以無明體是無法，有即非有；以非有故，無可與心相應，故言離也。既無無明染法與之相應，故名性淨，中實本覺故名為心，故言自性清淨心也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the meaning of the names. Question: Why is it called the mind pure in its own nature? Answer: Although from beginningless time this mind has been covered by the defiled dharmas of ignorance, its nature is pure and unchanged; therefore it is called pure. Why? Because the defiled dharmas of ignorance are originally separate from the mind. How are they separate? The substance of ignorance is a nonexistent dharma: existing, it is not existent. Because it is not existent, there is nothing that can be associated with the mind; therefore it is said to be separate. Since there are no defiled dharmas of ignorance associated with it, it is called pure in nature; being at its core original awakening, it is called mind. Therefore it is called the mind pure in its own nature.

Chinese Source

問曰：云何名為真如？答曰：一切諸法依此心有，以心為體望於諸法，法悉虛妄，有即非有，對此虛偽法，故目之為真。又復諸法雖實非有，但以虛妄因緣而有生滅之相。然彼虛法生時此心不生，諸法滅時此心不滅，不生故不增、不滅故不減，以不生不滅、不增不減，故名之為真。三世諸佛及以眾生，同以此一淨心為體。凡聖諸法自有差別異相，而此真心無異無相，故名之為如。又真如者，以一切法真實如是，唯是一心，故名此一心以為真如。若心外有法者，即非真實，亦不如是，即為偽異相也。是故《起信論》言：「一切諸法從本已來，離言說相，離名字相，離心緣相。畢竟平等，無有變異，不可破壞，唯是一心，故名真如。」以此義故自性清淨心復名真如也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Why is it called suchness? Answer: All dharmas exist in dependence on this mind. Taking the mind as substance and looking toward all dharmas, the dharmas are all false: existing, they are not existent. In contrast with these false and spurious dharmas, it is designated "true." Further, although all dharmas are really nonexistent, through false causes and conditions they have the marks of arising and ceasing. ◎問曰：違本起違末，便違不二之體，即應並有滅離之義也。何故上言法界法爾具足二性，不可破壞耶？答曰：違本雖起違末，但是理用故，與順一味，即不可除。違末雖依違本，但是事用故，即有別義，是故可滅。以此義故，二性不壞之義成也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The contrary root gives rise to the contrary branch, and so goes against the nondual substance; then both should have the meaning of being extinguished and separated. Why did you say above that the dharma-realm naturally fully possesses the two natures, which cannot be destroyed? Answer: Although the contrary root gives rise to the contrary branch, it is a function of principle, so it is of one flavor with the accordant and cannot be removed. Although the contrary branch depends on the contrary root, it is a phenomenal function, so it has a distinct meaning; therefore it can be extinguished. For this reason, the meaning that the two natures are indestructible is established.

問曰：我仍不解染用違心之義，願為說之。答曰：無明染法實從心體染性而起，但以體闇故，不知自己及諸境界從心而起。亦不知淨心具足染淨二性，而無異相，一味平等。以不知如此道理，故名之為違。智慧淨法實從心體而起，以明利故，能知己及諸法皆從心作。復知心體具足染淨二性而無異相，一味平等。以如此稱理而知，故名之為順。如似窮子實從父生，父實追念，但以癡故，不知己從父生，復不知父意，雖在父舍，不認其父，名之為違。復為父誘說，經歷多年，乃知己從父生，復知父意，乃認家業，受父教勅，名之為順。眾生亦爾，以無明故，不知己身及以諸法悉從心生。復遇諸佛方便教化，故隨順淨心，能證真如也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: I still do not understand the meaning of the defiled function going against the mind; please explain it for me. Answer: The defiled dharmas of ignorance really arise from the defiled nature of the mind's substance, but because their substance is dark, they do not know that they themselves and all spheres arise from the mind. Nor do they know that the pure mind fully possesses the two natures of defilement and purity without differing marks, of one flavor and equal. Because they do not know this principle, it is called going against. The pure dharmas of wisdom really arise from the mind's substance, and because they are clear and sharp, they can know that they themselves and all dharmas are made by the mind. They further know that the mind's substance fully possesses the two natures of defilement and purity without differing marks, of one flavor and equal. Because they know in accord with principle in this way, it is called according. It is like the destitute son: he was really born of his father, and his father really kept thinking of him, but because of his foolishness he did not know he was born of his father, nor did he know his father's intention; though he was in his father's house, he did not recognize his father. This is called going against. Then, being led on by his father's persuasion over many years, he came to know that he was born of his father, and further knew his father's intention; he then acknowledged the family estate and received his father's instructions. This is called according. Sentient beings are also like this: because of ignorance, they do not know that their own bodies and all dharmas are born from the mind. Then, meeting the buddhas' expedient teaching and transformation, they accord with the pure mind and can realize suchness.

問曰：既說無明染法與心相違，云何得熏心耶？答曰：無明染法無別有體故，不離淨心。以不離心故，雖復相違，而得相熏。如木出火炎，炎違木體而上騰，以無別體不離木故，還燒於木。後復不得聞斯譬喻，便起燈爐之執也。此明心體具足染性，名為不空也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since you say that the defiled dharmas of ignorance go against the mind, how can they perfume the mind? Answer: Because the defiled dharmas of ignorance have no separate substance, they are not apart from the pure mind. Because they are not apart from the mind, though they go against it, they can perfume it. It is like the fire's flame that comes out of wood: the flame goes against the wood's substance and soars upward, yet because it has no separate substance and is not apart from the wood, it turns back and burns the wood. Later [readers], hearing this simile, must not then give rise to attachment to [the image of] a lamp or a stove. This explains that the mind's substance fully possesses the defiled nature, and is called not empty.

Chinese Source

次明心體具足染事者，即彼染性為染業熏故，成無明住地及一切染法種子。依此種子現種種果報，此無明及與業果即是染事也。然此無明住地及以種子果報等，雖有相別顯現，說之為事；而悉一心為體，悉不在心外。以是義故，復以此心為不空也。譬如明鏡所現色像，無別有體，唯是一鏡，而復不妨萬像區分不同。不同之狀皆在鏡中顯現，故名不空鏡也。是以《起信論》言：「因熏習鏡，謂如實不空。一切世間境界悉於中現，不出不入、不失不壞，常住一心，以一切法即真實性故。」以此驗之，具足世間染法，亦是不空如來藏也。上來明具足染淨二法，以明不空義竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining that the mind's substance fully possesses defiled phenomena: that defiled nature, being perfumed by defiled karma, becomes the abiding ground of ignorance and the seeds of all defiled dharmas. In dependence on these seeds, various fruits of retribution appear. This ignorance together with karma and its fruits are defiled phenomena. Yet although this abiding ground of ignorance, the seeds, the fruits of retribution, and so on appear with distinct marks and are called phenomena, they all take the one mind as substance and none of them is outside the mind. For this reason, this mind is again taken as not empty. It is like the colored images manifested in a bright mirror: they have no separate substance and are only the one mirror, yet this does not prevent the myriad images from being distinct and different. Their different shapes all appear within the mirror; therefore it is called the nonempty mirror. Hence the Awakening of Faith says: "The mirror as cause of perfuming: this means it is truly nonempty. All the spheres of the world appear in it, neither going out nor coming in, neither lost nor destroyed, constantly abiding as the one mind, because all dharmas are themselves the real nature." Verifying it by this, fully possessing worldly defiled dharmas is also the nonempty tathāgatagarbha. The explanation above of fully possessing the two dharmas of defilement and purity, to make clear the meaning of not empty, is complete.

Possessed Simultaneously and from Beginning to End

Chinese Source

問曰：所言藏體具包染淨者，為俱時具、為始終具耶？答曰：所言如來藏具染淨者，有其二種：一者性染性淨、二者事染事淨，如上已明也。若據性染性淨，即無始以來俱時具有。若據事染事淨，即有二種差別：一者，一一時中俱具染淨二事；二者，始終方具染淨二事。此義云何？謂如來藏體具足一切眾生之性，各各差別不同，即是無差別之差別也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: As for saying that the storehouse's substance embraces defilement and purity, does it possess them simultaneously, or does it possess them from beginning to end? Answer: As for the tathāgatagarbha possessing defilement and purity, there are two kinds: first, inherent defilement and inherent purity; second, phenomenal defilement and phenomenal purity, as already explained above. In terms of inherent defilement and inherent purity, it possesses them simultaneously from beginningless time. In terms of phenomenal defilement and phenomenal purity, there are two distinctions: first, at each and every time, both phenomena, defiled and pure, are possessed together; second, only from beginning to end are the two phenomena of defilement and purity possessed. What does this mean? The substance of the tathāgatagarbha fully possesses the natures of all sentient beings, each distinct and different; this is difference without difference.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the nature of each and every one of these sentient beings, from the very beginning, there are further measureless and boundless natures — namely, all worldly defiled dharmas such as the six paths and the four modes of birth, suffering and joy, beauty and ugliness, lifespans and bodily sizes, folly and wisdom; and all world-transcending pure dharmas such as the causes and fruits of the three vehicles. Such measureless differentiated dharma-natures are all possessed, none lacking, in the nature of each and every sentient being. For this reason, the tathāgatagarbha from the very beginning possesses simultaneously the two natures of defilement and purity. Because it possesses the defiled nature, it can manifest the defiled phenomena of all sentient beings and so on; therefore this storehouse is taken as the originally abiding Dharma-body within obstruction, also called buddha-nature. Because it also possesses the pure nature, it can manifest the pure virtues of all buddhas and so on; therefore this storehouse is taken as the Dharma-body emerged from obstruction, also called the inherently pure Dharma-body, also called inherently pure nirvāṇa.

Because the seed-functions differ, at a single time the retributions received differ: some attain buddhahood, some attain the fruits of the two vehicles, some enter the three evil destinies, some are born among gods and humans; and within each destiny there are further measureless differences. Chinese Source

然此一一凡聖，雖於一時之中受報各別，但因緣之法無定故，一一凡聖無始以來，具經諸趣，無數迴返。後遇善友，教修出離，學三乘行及得道果。以此論之，一一眾生始終乃具染淨二事。何以故？以一眾生受地獄身時無餘趣報，受天報時亦無餘趣報，受一一趣中一一身時亦無餘身報。又受世間報時不得有出世果，受出世果時無世間報。以是義故，一眾生不得俱時具染淨二事，始終方具二事也。一切眾生亦如是，是故如來之藏有始終方具染淨二事之義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet although each and every ordinary being and sage receives a different retribution at a single time, because dharmas of causes and conditions are not fixed, each and every ordinary being and sage has, from beginningless time, passed through every destiny, going around countless times. Later, meeting good friends, they are taught to cultivate emancipation, study the practices of the three vehicles, and attain the fruits of the path. Considered in this way, each and every sentient being comes to possess the two phenomena of defilement and purity only from beginning to end. Why? Because when one sentient being receives a hell body, it has no retribution of other destinies; when it receives heavenly retribution, it also has no retribution of other destinies; when it receives each body within each destiny, it also has no retribution of other bodies. Further, when receiving worldly retribution, one cannot have the world-transcending fruit; when receiving the world-transcending fruit, there is no worldly retribution. For this reason one sentient being cannot possess defiled and pure phenomena simultaneously, but possesses the two phenomena only from beginning to end. All sentient beings are likewise. Therefore the tathāgatagarbha has the meaning of possessing the two phenomena of defilement and purity only from beginning to end.

Difference without Difference: The Pore and the Mirror

Chinese Source

問曰：如來之藏具如是等無量法性之時，為有差別？為無差別？答曰：藏體平等，實無差別，即是空如來藏。然此藏體復有不可思議用，故具足一切法性，有其差別，即是不空如來藏。此蓋無差別之差別也。此義云何？謂非如泥團具眾微塵也。何以故？泥團是假，微塵是實，故一一微塵各有別質，但以和合成一團泥。此泥團即具多塵之別，如來之藏即不如是。何以故？以如來藏是真實法，圓融無二故。是故如來之藏全體是一眾生一毛孔性，全體是一眾生一切毛孔性。如毛孔性，其餘一切所有世間一一法性亦復如是。如一眾生世間法性，一切眾生所有世間一一法性，一切諸佛所有出世間一一法性亦復如是，是如來藏全體也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: When the tathāgatagarbha possesses such measureless dharma-natures, are they differentiated or undifferentiated? Answer: The storehouse's substance is equal and really without differentiation; this is the empty tathāgatagarbha. Yet this storehouse's substance also has an inconceivable function, so it fully possesses all dharma-natures, and these are differentiated; this is the nonempty tathāgatagarbha. This is difference without difference. What does this mean? It is not like a lump of clay containing many motes of dust. Why? A lump of clay is provisional, motes of dust are real; each mote has its own separate stuff, and only by combining do they form a lump of clay. This lump of clay contains the distinctions of many motes, but the tathāgatagarbha is not like this. Why? Because the tathāgatagarbha is a true and real dharma, perfectly interfused and nondual. Therefore the whole substance of the tathāgatagarbha is the nature of a single pore of a single sentient being, and its whole substance is the nature of all the pores of a single sentient being. As with the nature of pores, so too with the nature of each and every other worldly dharma whatsoever. As with the worldly dharma-natures of a single sentient being, so too with each worldly dharma-nature of all sentient beings and each world-transcending dharma-nature of all buddhas: each is the whole substance of the tathāgatagarbha.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore, taking up the nature of a single pore of a single sentient being includes the natures of all the worldly dharmas of all sentient beings, and includes the natures of all the world-transcending dharmas of all buddhas. As taking up the nature of one pore includes all dharma-natures, so taking up each of the other worldly dharma-natures likewise includes all dharma-natures. As taking up each worldly dharma-nature includes all dharma-natures, so taking up each world-transcending dharma-nature likewise includes all dharma-natures. Further, taking up the phenomenon of a single pore includes all worldly and world-transcending phenomena. As taking up the phenomenon of one pore includes all phenomena, so taking up any phenomenon whatever among all the other worldly and world-transcending [dharmas] likewise includes all worldly and world-transcending phenomena. Why? Because all worldly and world-transcending phenomena take those worldly and world-transcending natures as substance. Therefore, because worldly and world-transcending natures are merged in substance and include each other, worldly and world-transcending phenomena too are perfectly interfused, including each other without obstruction. Therefore a sūtra says: "Mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without distinction."

Ru-Yi Translation

It is like a bright mirror whose substance possesses the natures of all images, each distinct and different; this is difference without difference. If this mirror's substance originally lacked the meaning of differentiated image-natures, then even if many forms came before it, images would never appear. A blazing fire, though bright and clear, cannot manifest images, because it originally has no image-nature. Since we see that a mirror can manifest images, we know for certain that it originally possesses image-natures. For this reason, this one bright mirror can at a single time manifest all images, pure and foul. And the pure images do not hinder the foul, nor the foul the pure; without hindrance and without obstruction, the functions of pure and foul are distinct. Although there is this distinction between image-natures and image-marks, they are perfectly interfused and not different, only one mirror. Why? Because this mirror's whole substance is the image-nature of a single pore, and its whole substance is the image-nature of all pores. As with the image-nature of a pore, so too with each other minute image-nature, each coarse and large image-nature, each pure image-nature, each foul image-nature, and so on: each is the mirror's whole substance. Therefore, taking up the image-nature of one pore includes all the other image-natures. As taking up the image-nature of one pore includes all image-natures, so taking up each of the other image-natures likewise includes all image-natures. Further, taking up the image-mark of one pore includes all image-marks. As taking up the image-mark of one pore includes all image-marks, so taking up each of the other image-marks likewise includes all image-marks. Why? Therefore, because all image-natures are merged in substance and include each other, all image-marks too merge with and include each other.

Ru-Yi Translation

By this simile, that all buddhas and all sentient beings share the one pure mind, the tathāgatagarbha, without hindering each other, should be believable. Therefore a sūtra says: "It is like a bright, clear mirror: according to what is before it, the face's image appears; each does not know the other; the nature of karma is also like this." What does this mean? "A bright, clear mirror" is a simile for the substance of the pure mind. "According to what is before it" is a simile for the pure mind's substance possessing all dharma-natures, so that it can receive all perfuming and, according to the differences of perfuming, manifest different retributions. "The face" is a simile for the two kinds of karma, defiled and pure. "Image appears" is a simile for the two natures of defilement and purity of the mind's substance, which, in dependence on the force of perfuming, manifest the two retributions of defilement and purity. "Each does not know the other" is a simile for the pure mind and karmic retribution each not knowing the other. "Karma" is the two kinds of karma, defiled and pure, matching "the face" above. "Nature" is the two natures of defilement and purity of the true mind, matching the bright mirror possessing all image-natures above. "Is also like this" concludes and establishes this meaning as a whole.

For this reason, if the truth of the real includes all the phenomenal marks in the worldly truth completely, then each and every phenomenal mark in the worldly truth also includes all the phenomenal marks in the worldly truth completely. This principle has already been fully explained above; there is no need to raise further objections.

Chinese Source

問曰：若言世諦之中一一事相即是真諦全體者，此則真心遍一切處，與彼外道所計神我遍一切處，義有何異耶？答曰：外道所計，心外有法，大小、遠近、三世、六道歷然是實，但以神我微妙廣大故，遍一切處猶如虛空。此即見有實事之相異神我，神我之相異實事也。設使即事計我，我與事一，但彼執事為實，彼此不融。佛法之內即不如是，知一切法悉是心作，但以心性緣起，不無相別；雖復相別，其唯一心為體。以體為用故，言實際無處不至，非謂心外有其實事，心遍在中，名為至也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: If you say that within the worldly truth each and every phenomenal mark is the whole substance of the truth of the real, then the true mind pervades all places. How does this differ in meaning from the spiritual self that non-Buddhists imagine to pervade all places? Answer: What non-Buddhists imagine is that there are dharmas outside the mind — large and small, far and near, the three times and the six paths, distinctly real — only that, because the spiritual self is subtle, vast, and great, it pervades all places like space. This is seeing that the marks of real phenomena differ from the spiritual self, and the mark of the spiritual self differs from real phenomena. Even if they imagine a self identical with phenomena, self and phenomena being one, they still grasp phenomena as real, and this and that do not merge. Within the Buddhadharma it is not like this. It knows that all dharmas are made by the mind; only because of the conditioned arising of the mind's nature, distinct marks are not absent; yet although the marks are distinct, they take only the one mind as substance. Because substance serves as function, it is said that the reality-limit reaches every place — it does not mean that there are real phenomena outside the mind and the mind pervades and is within them, which is called reaching.

The visitor imagined a small pore and replied: "I already see it clearly." The śramaṇa said: "Close your eyes and recall in imagination a great city, several tens of li across. Can you see it?" The visitor imagined the city and replied: "I see it clearly in my mind." The śramaṇa said: "Do the pore and the city differ in size?" The visitor said: "They differ." The śramaṇa said: "Were the pore and the city just now only made by the mind?" The visitor said: "They were made by the mind." The śramaṇa said: "Does your mind have small and large?" The visitor said: "The mind has no form or mark; how could it be seen to have large and small?" The śramaṇa said: "When you imagined the pore, did you make it with a reduced bit of mind, or did you make it using the whole one mind?" The visitor said: "The mind has no shape or parts; how could one reduce it and use a bit? Therefore I used the whole of one thought to imagine the pore." The śramaṇa said: "When you imagined the great city, did you make it using only your own one mind, or did you also obtain other people's minds and spirits to make it together?" The visitor said: "I made the city using only my own mind; there was no other person's mind."

The śramaṇa said: "Then the whole substance of the one mind makes only one small pore, and its whole substance can also make a great city. Since the mind is one and without large and small, the pore and the city both take the whole one mind as substance; you should know that the pore and the city are merged in substance and equal. For this reason, taking up the small to take in the large, there is no large that is not small; taking up the large to include the small, there is no small that is not large. If, from the meaning of the equality of the mind's substance, one looks at those marks of large and small, they are originally nonexistent, neither arising nor ceasing; there is only the one true mind.

"For this reason, if one discusses the dream from the standpoint of waking, the long time in the dream is unreal; if one discusses waking from the standpoint of the dream, the meal's time while awake is also false. If waking and dreaming are discussed according to principle, long and short include each other — the long time is short and the short time is long — without hindering the distinction of long and short. Further, even though they share the one mind as substance, if the long time were made using the whole one mind and the short time made with a reduced bit of mind, then too long and short could not include each other. Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Attribution and transmission. The text is traditionally attributed to Huisi (Nanyue, 515–577); its authorship has long been questioned, and the translation says only "attributed to Huisi." Both prefaces tell how the Japanese monk Jakushō (Ch. Jizhao, Great Master Entsū) brought it to China in the Xianping era (998–1003; Zunshi gives Xianping 3 = 1000) and how Zunshi had it printed in Tianxi 4 (1020). Zunshi speaks of two fascicles; the Taishō text has four. Zunshi's count of Aśvaghoṣa as "the eleventh" patriarch is his own.

Awakening of Faith (T1666). Quoted three times and named: "apart from the mark of speech…," the "mirror as cause of perfuming," and "the greatness of function". In the last, the Taishō Awakening of Faith reads "good causes and fruits" (善因果); our text has "good and evil causes and fruits" (善惡因果). The list "light of great wisdom… true cognizance… permanence, bliss, self, and purity" (unit 41) abridges the Awakening of Faith's list of the meanings of suchness. The site has not yet translated the Awakening of Faith.

Sūtra quotations located. "There is no nirvāṇa for anything…" (units 19, 35): Laṅkāvatāra (T670). "The tathāgatagarbha is the cause of the good and the not-good": Laṅkāvatāra (T670/T671/T672). "If the ground breaks down, they too accordingly break down": Śrīmālā (T353), on the abiding ground of ignorance. "The mind's nature is one; how can it give birth to various fruits of retribution?" and the bright-mirror verse (unit 58): Avataṃsaka, "Bright Difficulties" chapter (T278); the Taishō verse reads 猶如明淨鏡，隨其面像現. "Mind, Buddha, and sentient beings…": Avataṃsaka (T278), worded as on the site's Mohe Zhiguan and Fahua Xuanyi. "The Dharma-bodies of all buddhas are only one Dharma-body," "the past is the future…" (T278 reads "the future is the past"), and "all the eons of the three times" are also from the Avataṃsaka. "If there were a dharma surpassing nirvāṇa…": Prajñāpāramitā (T227, T220).

Quotations matched to site English. "The ocean of perfect and universal knowing of all buddhas arises from the mind's own contemplation" follows the site's Contemplation Sūtra (eighth contemplation). "Ignorance and wisdom: their nature is without duality… the true nature" follows the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra (明 = "wisdom" there; elsewhere in this text 明 is "clarity"). "Yet constantly cultivates pure lands, teaching and converting all sentient beings" adapts the site's Vimalakīrti ("Buddha Path" verses), where the last word is 群生 "the multitude of beings"; our text reads 諸眾生. The "destitute son" (unit 53) is the site's Lotus name for the parable in "Faith and Understanding."

Sources not located. "Delusion about conditions" (於緣中癡); "Flowing on is birth-and-death; not flowing is nirvāṇa"; "Buddha-nature, the great king, is not a fabricated dharma"; "The equal true dharma-realm: the Buddha does not deliver sentient beings" (the first line also appears in T1920, the Treatise on Contemplating Mind); the treatise line "If sentient beings had no buddha-nature…"; the pair "within pure dharmas one does not see a single dharma increase / within afflicted dharmas… decrease" (similar wording is cited by Uisang, T1887A). "This very Dharma-body, flowing on in the five paths, is called sentient beings" is close to the Anūnatvāpūrṇatva (T668) but not verbatim there. "Birth-and-death depends on the tathāgatagarbha" echoes the Śrīmālā.

Ālaya consciousness (阿梨耶識). The text uses the older transliteration 阿梨耶 (as in the Awakening of Faith), not 阿賴耶. It distinguishes "the ālaya consciousness in the seed phase" (子時阿梨耶識) and "the ālaya consciousness of retribution" (果報阿梨耶識), with a defiled and a pure portion.

Seed and fruit ignorance. 子時無明 "ignorance in the seed phase" (= 住地無明, 迷理無明) and 果時無明 "ignorance in the fruit phase" (= 迷境無明, 迷事無明) are this text's own terms; "phase" renders 時. Quasi-consciousness (似識) is identified as the sixth and seventh consciousnesses.

Inherent defilement (性染). Units 43–65 hold that the mind's substance originally possesses a defiled nature as well as a pure one, and that only phenomenal defilement (事染) is removed. Later Tiantai writers, especially Zhili and, in the twentieth century, Dixian (諦閑) in his Commentary (述記), read this as an early statement of inherent evil (性惡); the translation does not import that reading, and 性染 is kept distinct from 性惡.

Three natures. The text's three natures (分別性, 依他性, 真實性 — Paramārtha's names, not 遍計所執／圓成實) are treated in Part 2. In unit 54 the Awakening of Faith phrase 真實性 is already rendered "the real nature," the same English used for the third nature.

Doubtful readings. Unit 20: "this grasping too is deluded thinking, also called illusory marks (虛相)" — 虛執 "illusory grasping" would fit the argument better. Unit 25: 為無明塵故 is translated as if it were 無明盡 "because ignorance is exhausted." Unit 21: CBETA punctuates 此就智慧，佛以明淨心為佛性; the translation reads 此就智慧佛，以明淨心為佛性 ("in terms of the Buddha of wisdom").

Alternatively (unit 5). Zunshi's 誰論止觀體顯而性泯，亦無明靜豁然；誰寄無所名焉 is punctuated oddly. Alternatively: "Who would speak of calming-and-contemplation once the substance is revealed and the nature vanishes? There is no clarity or stillness either; vast and open, who would lodge anything there?" 疆 is read as 強 "forcibly."

Alternatively (unit 60). 理體用不改之性: "unchanging natures of the function of the principle-substance." Alternatively: "natures that do not change in principle, substance, or function."

Lamp and stove (unit 54). "Attachment to [the image of] a lamp or a stove" seems to warn against taking the simile as a flame that has a separate fuel and container; the meaning is not certain.

Seven days as an eon (unit 72). Compare the site's Vimalakīrti, "Inconceivable" chapter, where the bodhisattva extends seven days into an eon and contracts an eon into seven days.
