Shared and Unshared Marks, Mind-Consciousness, and the Three Natures

This part finishes the first of the work's five topics, the "support" of calming-and-contemplation, and covers the second and most of the third. It opens with the last two of six distinctions about the one mind: why practice removes one's own delusion but not another's, and why some beings see the same world while others do not (the "consciousness of shared and unshared marks," illustrated by hungry ghosts who see the Ganges as fire or pus). Fascicle Three then asks why the mind is relied upon and by what: mind-consciousness, which knows names and meanings, perfumes the "root consciousness" until it becomes nondiscriminating wisdom. A long refutation follows of a practitioner who simply stops thinking and calls the blank state the samādhi of suchness; the text grants it value only as a gradual step. The "spheres" of practice are the three natures, given in Paramārtha's terms: discriminated, dependent, and real, each with a pure and a defiled form. The third topic then lays out, step by step, how one contemplates and calms within each nature, with the similes of a child and a mirror, flowers in the sky, a rabbit conjured from a handkerchief, and a dream.

The Fifth Distinction: Counteracting Delusion and Receiving Retribution

Chinese Source

次明第五治惑受報同異所由。問曰：如來之藏既具一切世法、出世法，種子之性及果報性，若眾生修對治道，熏彼對治種子性，分分成對治種子事用時，何故彼先所有惑染種子事即分分滅也？即能治所治種子皆依性起，即應不可一成一壞。答曰：法界法爾所治之法，為能治之所滅也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the fifth, the reasons for sameness and difference in counteracting delusion and receiving retribution. Question: Since the tathāgatagarbha possesses all worldly and world-transcending dharmas — the nature of seeds and the nature of fruits of retribution — when sentient beings cultivate the path of counteraction and perfume the nature of counteracting seeds, so that portion by portion it becomes the phenomenal function of counteracting seeds, why do the phenomena of the seeds of delusion and defilement that they had before cease portion by portion? The seeds that counteract and the seeds that are counteracted both arise in dependence on the nature; so it should not be that one is accomplished and the other destroyed. Answer: The dharma-realm is naturally so: the dharma that is counteracted is extinguished by what counteracts.

Chinese Source

問曰：所治之事既為能治之事所滅者，所治之性亦應為能治之性所滅。答曰：不然，如上已說，事法有成有敗故，此生彼滅性義無始並具；又復體融無二故，不可一滅一存也。是故眾生未修治道之前，雙有能治所治之性。但所治染法之性，依熏起用；能治淨法之性未有熏力，故無用也。若修治道之後，亦並具能治所治之性，但能治之性依熏力故分分起於淨用；所治之性無所熏力，被對治故，染用分分損減。是故經言：「但治其病，而不除法。」法者，法界法爾，即是能治所治之性，病即是所治之事。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since the phenomenon that is counteracted is extinguished by the phenomenon that counteracts, the nature that is counteracted should also be extinguished by the nature that counteracts. Answer: Not so. As already said above, because phenomenal dharmas have accomplishment and decay, this arises and that ceases; but the natures, in meaning, are possessed together from beginningless time; and further, because they are merged in substance and nondual, it cannot be that one is extinguished and the other remains. Therefore, before sentient beings cultivate the path of counteraction, they have both the nature that counteracts and the nature that is counteracted. But the nature of the defiled dharmas that are counteracted gives rise to function in dependence on perfuming, while the nature of the pure dharmas that counteract does not yet have the force of perfuming, and so has no function. After they cultivate the path of counteraction, they likewise possess both the nature that counteracts and the nature that is counteracted; but the nature that counteracts, by the force of perfuming, gives rise portion by portion to pure functions, while the nature that is counteracted has no force of perfuming and, being counteracted, its defiled functions diminish portion by portion. Therefore a sūtra says: "Only cure the illness, and do not remove the dharmas." The "dharmas" are the dharma-realm being naturally so, namely the nature that counteracts and the nature that is counteracted; the "illness" is the phenomenon that is counteracted.

問曰：能治所治可爾。其未修對治者，即無始已來具有一切故業種子，此種子中即應備有六道之業。又復一一眾生各各本具六道果報之性，何不依彼無始六道種子，令一眾生俱時受六道身耶？答曰：不得。何以故？以法界法爾故。但可具有無始六道種子在於心中，隨一道種子偏彊偏熟者，先受果報。隨是一報之中，不妨自雜受苦樂之事，要不得令一眾生俱受六道之身。後若作菩薩自在用時，以悲願力故，用彼故業種子，一時於六道中受無量身，教化眾生也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: [The case of] what counteracts and what is counteracted may be so. As for those who have not yet cultivated counteraction, from beginningless time they possess the seeds of all past karma, and these seeds should include the karma of the six paths. Further, each and every sentient being originally possesses the natures of the fruits of retribution of the six paths. Why do they not, in dependence on those beginningless seeds of the six paths, cause one sentient being to receive the bodies of the six paths at the same time? Answer: That cannot be. Why? Because the dharma-realm is naturally so. One can only possess the beginningless seeds of the six paths in the mind; whichever path's seeds are especially strong and especially ripe, that fruit of retribution is received first. Within that one retribution, this does not prevent one from receiving a mixture of events of suffering and pleasure; but in no case can one sentient being be made to receive the bodies of the six paths together. Later, when one has become a bodhisattva with sovereign function, by the power of compassion and vows one uses those seeds of past karma to receive measureless bodies at one time among the six paths, teaching and converting sentient beings.

問曰：據一眾生即以一心為體，心體之中實具六道果報之性。復有無始六道種子，而不得令一眾生一時之中俱受六道之報者，一切諸佛、一切眾生亦同。以一心為體故，雖各各自具六道果報之性及六道種子，亦應一切凡聖次第先後受報，不應一時之中有眾多凡聖。答曰：不由以一心為體故，便不得受眾多身，亦不由以一心為體故，要須一時受眾多身。但法界法爾，若總據一切凡聖，雖同一心為體，即不妨一時俱有一切凡聖；若別據一眾生，雖亦一心為體，即不得一時俱受六道報也。若如來藏中唯具先後受報之法，不具一時受報之法者，何名法界法門具一切法耶！

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: In terms of one sentient being, the one mind is taken as substance, and within the mind's substance the natures of the fruits of retribution of the six paths are really possessed; there are also the beginningless seeds of the six paths; and yet one sentient being cannot be made to receive the retributions of the six paths together at one time. All buddhas and all sentient beings are the same: because they take the one mind as substance, then, although each possesses on its own the natures of the fruits of retribution of the six paths and the seeds of the six paths, all ordinary beings and sages too should receive retribution in sequence, one after another, and there should not be many ordinary beings and sages at one time. Answer: It is not because the one mind is taken as substance that one cannot receive many bodies; nor is it because the one mind is taken as substance that one must receive many bodies at one time. It is only that the dharma-realm is naturally so: if one takes all ordinary beings and sages together, although they share the one mind as substance, this does not prevent all ordinary beings and sages from existing together at one time; if one takes a single sentient being separately, although it too takes the one mind as substance, it cannot receive the retributions of the six paths together at one time. If the tathāgatagarbha possessed only the dharma of receiving retribution one after another, and did not possess the dharma of receiving retribution at one time, how could it be called the dharma-gate of the dharma-realm that possesses all dharmas?

問曰：上言據一眾生，即以一心為體。心體雖具染淨二性，而淨事起時能除染事者，一切諸佛一切眾生既同。以一心為體，亦應由佛是淨事，故能治餘眾生染事。若爾者，一切眾生自然成佛，即不須自修因行。答曰：不由以一心為體故，染淨二事相除。亦不由以一心為體故，染淨二法不得相除。亦不由別心為體故，凡聖二事不得相除。但法界法爾，一切凡聖雖同一心為體而不相滅。若別據一眾生，雖亦一心為體，即染淨二事相除也。如來之藏唯有染淨相除之法，無染淨不相除法者，何名法界法爾具一切法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Above you said that in terms of one sentient being, the one mind is taken as substance. Although the mind's substance possesses the two natures of defilement and purity, when pure phenomena arise they can remove defiled phenomena. All buddhas and all sentient beings are the same in this. Since they take the one mind as substance, it should also be that, because the Buddha is pure phenomena, he can counteract the defiled phenomena of the other sentient beings. If so, all sentient beings would become buddhas naturally and would not need to cultivate causal practices themselves. Answer: It is not because the one mind is taken as substance that the two phenomena of defilement and purity remove each other. Nor is it because the one mind is taken as substance that the two dharmas of defilement and purity cannot remove each other. Nor is it because separate minds are taken as substance that the two phenomena of ordinary beings and sages cannot remove each other. It is only that the dharma-realm is naturally so: all ordinary beings and sages, although they share the one mind as substance, do not extinguish one another; if one takes a single sentient being separately, although it too takes the one mind as substance, the two phenomena of defilement and purity remove each other. If the tathāgatagarbha had only the dharma of defilement and purity removing each other, and not the dharma of defilement and purity not removing each other, how could it be said that the dharma-realm is naturally so and possesses all dharmas?

Chinese Source

問曰：向者兩番都言法界法爾，實自難信。如我意者所解，謂一一凡聖各自別有淨心為體。何以故？以各各一心為體故，不得於一心中俱現多身，所以一一凡聖不俱受無量身。又復各各依心起用故，不妨俱時有眾多凡聖，此義即便。又復一一眾生各以別心為體故，一一心中不容染淨二法，是故能治之法熏心時，自己惑滅。以與他人別心故，不妨他惑不滅，此義亦便。何為辛苦堅成一切凡聖同一心耶？答曰：癡人！若一切凡聖不同一真心為體者，即無共相平等法身。是故經言：「由共相身故，一切諸佛畢竟不成佛」也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Both times just now you said, "The dharma-realm is naturally so"; this is truly hard to believe. As I understand it, each and every ordinary being and sage has its own separate pure mind as substance. Why? Because each takes one mind as substance, many bodies cannot appear together in one mind; that is why each and every ordinary being and sage does not receive measureless bodies together. Further, because each gives rise to function in dependence on its own mind, this does not prevent there being many ordinary beings and sages at the same time; this meaning is convenient. Further, because each and every sentient being takes a separate mind as substance, each and every mind cannot hold the two dharmas of defilement and purity; therefore, when the counteracting dharma perfumes the mind, one's own delusion ceases; and because one's mind is separate from other people's, this does not prevent others' delusion from not ceasing. This meaning too is convenient. Why go to such pains to insist that all ordinary beings and sages share one mind? Answer: Fool! If all ordinary beings and sages did not share one true mind as substance, there would be no equal Dharma-body of shared marks. Therefore a sūtra says: "Because of the body of shared marks, all buddhas ultimately do not become buddhas."

汝言一一凡聖各各別心為體，故於一心中不得俱現多身，是故一眾生不俱受無量身者，如《法華》中所明，無量分身釋迦俱現於世，亦應不得以一法身為體。若彼一切釋迦唯以一心為法身者，汝云何言一心不得俱現多身耶？若一心既得俱現多身者，何為汝意欲使一一凡聖各別一心為體故，方得俱時有凡聖耶？又復經言：「一切諸佛身，唯是一法身。」若諸眾生法身不反流盡源即是佛法身者，可言一切眾生在凡之時各各別有法身。既眾生法身即是諸佛法身，諸佛法身既只是一，何為一一凡聖各各別有真心為法身耶？又復善才童子自見遍十方佛前悉有己身，爾時豈有多心為體耶？又復一人夢中一時見無數人，豈可有無數心與彼夢裡諸人為體耶？又復菩薩以悲願力用故業受生之時，一念俱受無量種身，豈有多淨心為體耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

You say that each and every ordinary being and sage takes a separate mind as substance, so that many bodies cannot appear together in one mind, and that therefore one sentient being does not receive measureless bodies together. [Then] as the Lotus explains, measureless emanation-bodies of Śākyamuni appear together in the world; these too should not be able to take one Dharma-body as substance. If all those Śākyamunis take only one mind as the Dharma-body, how can you say that one mind cannot manifest many bodies together? If one mind can manifest many bodies together, why do you wish to make each and every ordinary being and sage take a separate one mind as substance, so that only then can there be ordinary beings and sages at the same time? Further, a sūtra says: "The bodies of all buddhas are only one Dharma-body." If the Dharma-body of sentient beings were not itself the Buddha's Dharma-body when it reverses the flow and exhausts the source, one could say that all sentient beings, while they are ordinary, each have a separate Dharma-body. Since the Dharma-body of sentient beings is the Dharma-body of the buddhas, and the Dharma-body of the buddhas is only one, why would each and every ordinary being and sage have a separate true mind as Dharma-body? Further, the youth Sudhana saw himself present before the buddhas everywhere in the ten directions; at that time, were there many minds as substance? Further, a person in a dream sees countless people at one time; could there be countless minds serving as substance for the people in that dream? Further, when a bodhisattva, by the power of compassion and vows, uses past karma to take birth, in a single thought he receives measureless kinds of bodies together; are there many pure minds as substance?

又復汝言一一凡聖各以一心為體，一心之中不得容於染淨二法，故所以能治之法熏心時，自己惑滅，以與他別心故，不妨他惑不滅。此義為便者，一人初修治道時，此人惑染心悉應滅盡。何以故？以一心之內不容染淨二法故。若此人淨法熏心，心中有淨法時仍有染法者，此人應有二心。何以故？以他人與我別心故。我修智時，他惑不滅，我今修智，自惑亦復未滅，定如須有二心。若使此人唯有一心，而得俱有染淨二法者，汝云何言以一心之內不容染淨二法，故淨生染滅耶？是故諸大菩薩留隨眠惑在於心中，復修福智，淨法熏心而不相妨。又復隨眠之惑與對治之智同時而不相礙，何為一心之內不得容染淨二法耶？以是義故，如來之藏一時具包一切凡聖，無所妨礙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, you say that each and every ordinary being and sage takes one mind as substance, and that one mind cannot hold the two dharmas of defilement and purity, so that when the counteracting dharma perfumes the mind one's own delusion ceases, and because one's mind is separate from others', this does not prevent others' delusion from not ceasing. If this meaning is convenient, then when a person first cultivates the path of counteraction, this person's deluded and defiled mind should cease entirely. Why? Because within one mind the two dharmas of defilement and purity cannot be held. If, when this person's pure dharmas perfume the mind and there are pure dharmas in the mind, there are still defiled dharmas, this person must have two minds. Why? Because others have minds separate from mine: when I cultivate wisdom, their delusion does not cease; now I cultivate wisdom and my own delusion has not yet ceased either; so certainly there must be two minds. If this person has only one mind and yet can have the two dharmas of defilement and purity together, how can you say that because one mind cannot hold the two dharmas of defilement and purity, purity arises and defilement ceases? Therefore the great bodhisattvas retain latent delusions in the mind and further cultivate blessing and wisdom; pure dharmas perfume the mind, and they do not hinder each other. Further, latent delusions and counteracting wisdom are simultaneous and do not obstruct each other; how can it be that within one mind the two dharmas of defilement and purity cannot be held? For this reason the tathāgatagarbha at one time includes all ordinary beings and sages, without any hindrance.

問曰：既引如此道理，得以一心為體不妨一時有多凡聖者，何為一眾生不俱受六道報耶？又復修行之人一心之中俱有解惑種子不相妨者，有何道理得以智斷惑耶？答曰：蠓蟲！如上已言，法界法爾，一心之中具有一切凡聖。法界法爾，一一凡聖各各先後隨自種子彊者受報，不得一人俱受六道之身。法界法爾，一心之中一時具有凡聖，不相除滅。法界法爾，一切凡聖雖同一心，不妨一一凡聖各自修智，自斷其惑。法界法爾，智慧分起，能分除惑，智慧滿足，除惑皆盡，不由一心之內不容染淨故斷惑也。法界法爾，惑未盡時，解惑同體，不由別有心故，雙有解惑。是故但知，真心能與一切凡聖為體，心體具一切法性。如即時世間、出世間事得成立者，皆由心性有此道理也。若無道理者，終不可成。如外道修行不得解脫者，由不與心性解脫道理相應也。法界法爾，行與心性相應，所作得成。行若不與心性相應，即所為不成就。此明第五治惑受報不同所由竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since you have cited such principles, so that taking the one mind as substance does not prevent there being many ordinary beings and sages at one time, why does one sentient being not receive the retributions of the six paths together? Further, if in the one mind of a practitioner the seeds of understanding and of delusion are both present without hindering each other, by what principle can delusion be cut off by wisdom? Answer: Gnat! As already said above, the dharma-realm is naturally so: within the one mind all ordinary beings and sages are possessed. The dharma-realm is naturally so: each and every ordinary being and sage receives retribution in turn according to whichever of its own seeds are strong; one person cannot receive the bodies of the six paths together. The dharma-realm is naturally so: within the one mind ordinary beings and sages are possessed at one time without removing or extinguishing each other. The dharma-realm is naturally so: although all ordinary beings and sages share the one mind, this does not prevent each and every ordinary being and sage from cultivating wisdom for itself and cutting off its own delusion. The dharma-realm is naturally so: when wisdom arises in part, it can remove delusion in part; when wisdom is complete, the removal of delusion is entirely exhausted; it is not because one mind cannot hold defilement and purity that delusion is cut off. The dharma-realm is naturally so: while delusion is not yet exhausted, understanding and delusion share the same substance; it is not because there are separate minds that understanding and delusion are both present. Therefore simply know that the true mind can serve as substance for all ordinary beings and sages, and that the mind's substance possesses the natures of all dharmas. Just as the establishment, at this very time, of worldly and world-transcending phenomena is all because the mind's nature has this principle — if there were no principle, they could never be accomplished — so non-Buddhists who practice do not attain liberation because they do not accord with the principle of liberation of the mind's nature. The dharma-realm is naturally so: when practice accords with the mind's nature, what is done is accomplished; if practice does not accord with the mind's nature, what is done is not accomplished. This completes the fifth, explaining the reasons for the differences in counteracting delusion and receiving retribution.

The Sixth Distinction: The Consciousness of Shared and Unshared Marks

Chinese Source

次明第六共相不共相識。問曰：一切凡聖既唯一心為體，何為有相見者、有不相見者？有同受用者、有不同受用者？答曰：所言一切凡聖唯以一心為體者，此心就體相論之，有其二種：一者，真如平等心，此是體也，即是一切凡聖平等共相法身。二者，阿梨耶識，即是相也。就此阿梨耶識中復有二種：一者，清淨分依他性，亦名清淨和合識，即是一切聖人體也；二者，染濁分依他性，亦名染濁和合識，即是一切眾生體也。此二種依他性雖有用別，而體融一味，唯是一真如平等心也。以此二種依他性體同無二故，就中即合有二事別：一者共相識、二者不共相識。何故有耶？以真如體中具此共相識性、不共相識性故。一切凡聖造同業熏此共相性故，即成共相識也。若一一凡聖各各別造別業，熏此不共相性故，即成不共相識也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the sixth, the consciousness of shared and unshared marks. Question: Since all ordinary beings and sages take only the one mind as substance, why do some see one another and some not? Why do some share in enjoyment and some not? Answer: As for saying that all ordinary beings and sages take only the one mind as substance, this mind, discussed in terms of substance and mark, is of two kinds. First, the equal mind of suchness: this is the substance, the equal Dharma-body of shared marks of all ordinary beings and sages. Second, the ālaya consciousness: this is the mark. Within this ālaya consciousness there are again two kinds: first, the dependent nature of the pure portion, also called the pure combined consciousness, which is the substance of all sages; second, the dependent nature of the defiled and turbid portion, also called the defiled and turbid combined consciousness, which is the substance of all sentient beings. Although these two kinds of dependent nature differ in function, their substance is merged and of one flavor; they are only the one equal mind of suchness. Because these two kinds of dependent nature are the same in substance and nondual, within them there are, taken together, two distinct phenomena: first, the consciousness of shared marks; second, the consciousness of unshared marks. Why are there these? Because within the substance of suchness are possessed the nature of the consciousness of shared marks and the nature of the consciousness of unshared marks. When all ordinary beings and sages create the same karma and perfume this nature of shared marks, it becomes the consciousness of shared marks. When each and every ordinary being and sage separately creates separate karma and perfumes this nature of unshared marks, it becomes the consciousness of unshared marks.

Calming-and-Contemplation in the Dependent Nature

Chinese Source

次明依他性中止觀體狀者，亦先從觀入止。所言觀者，謂因前分別性中止行，知法無實故。此中即解一切五陰六塵，隨一一法悉皆心作，但有虛相，猶如想心所見，似有境界，其體是虛。作此解者，即名為觀。作此觀已，復作是念：「此等虛法但以無明妄想妄業熏心故，心似所熏之法顯現。猶如熱病因緣，眼中自現空華，然此華體相有即非有，不生不滅。我今所見虛法亦復如是，唯一心所現，有即非有，本自無生，今即無滅。」如是緣心遣心，知相本無故，虛相之執即滅，即名從觀入止。既知諸法有即非有，而復知不妨非有而有，似有顯現，即名從止起觀。若從此止行徑入真實性觀者，此即名從止入觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the dependent nature: here too, first from contemplation one enters calming. As for contemplation: because, through the practice of calming within the discriminated nature above, one knows that dharmas are without reality, here one understands that all the five aggregates and the six sense-objects, each and every dharma, are made by the mind and have only illusory marks, like what is seen by the imagining mind: there seems to be a sphere, but its substance is illusory. Making this understanding is called contemplation. Having made this contemplation, one further thinks: "These illusory dharmas — only because ignorance, deluded thinking, and deluded karma perfume the mind, the mind manifests resembling the dharmas that perfume it. It is like the causes and conditions of a fever, through which flowers in the sky appear of themselves in the eyes; yet the substance and marks of these flowers, existing, are not existent; they neither arise nor cease. The illusory dharmas I now see are also like this: only manifested by the one mind, existing, they are not existent; originally they did not arise of themselves, and now they do not cease." Thus, by taking the mind as object and dismissing the mind, one knows that marks are originally nonexistent, and the grasping of illusory marks ceases; this is called entering calming from contemplation. Having known that dharmas, existing, are not existent, one also knows that this does not prevent them, not existing, from existing, seemingly existing and manifesting; this is called giving rise to contemplation from calming. If from this practice of calming one proceeds directly to the contemplation of the real nature, this is called entering contemplation from calming.

Calming-and-Contemplation in the Real Nature

Chinese Source

次明第三真實性中止觀體狀者，亦先從觀入止。所言觀者，因前依他性中止行，知一切法有即非有故，所以此中即知一切法本來唯心，心外無法。復作是念：「既言心外無法，唯有一心，此心之相何者是也？為無前二性故，即將此無以為心耶？為異彼無外別有淨心耶？」作此念時，即名為觀。即復念言：「無是無法，對有而生，有尚本來不有，何有無法以為淨心？又復無法為四句攝，淨心即離四句，何得以此無法為淨心也？」作此念時，執無之心即滅，則名為止。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the third, the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the real nature: here too, first from contemplation one enters calming. As for contemplation: because, through the practice of calming within the dependent nature above, one knows that all dharmas, existing, are not existent, here one knows that all dharmas are from the beginning only mind and that there are no dharmas outside the mind. Further one thinks: "Since it is said that there are no dharmas outside the mind and there is only the one mind, what is the mark of this mind? Is it that, because the former two natures are absent, this absence is taken as the mind? Or is there, different from that absence, a separate pure mind?" When one thinks this, it is called contemplation. Then one further thinks: "Absence is a dharma of absence, arising in contrast to existence. Since existence is originally not existent, how could there be a dharma of absence to be the pure mind? Further, a dharma of absence is included in the four alternatives, while the pure mind is apart from the four alternatives; how could this dharma of absence be the pure mind?" When one thinks this, the mind that grasps absence ceases; this is called calming.

Chinese Source

又從此止更入觀門，觀於淨心，作如是念：「二性之無既非是心者，更有何法以為淨心？又復此心為可見耶？為不可見耶？為可念耶？為不可念耶？」作此分別時，即名為觀。即復念言：「心外無法，何有能見此心者？何有能念此心者？若更緣念此心，即成境界，即有能緣所緣，即是心外有智能觀此心，何名為如？又復我覓心之心，體唯是淨心，何有異法可緣可念也？但以妄想習氣故，自生分別。」分別之相，有即非有，體唯淨心。又復設使分別，即知正是淨心分別也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, from this calming one enters the gate of contemplation again and contemplates the pure mind, thinking thus: "Since the absence of the two natures is not the mind, what further dharma is the pure mind? Further, can this mind be seen, or can it not be seen? Can it be thought of, or can it not be thought of?" When one makes this discrimination, it is called contemplation. Then one further thinks: "Outside the mind there are no dharmas; how could there be anything that can see this mind? How could there be anything that can think of this mind? If one further takes this mind as object and thinks of it, it becomes a sphere, and then there is what takes objects and what is taken as object; that would mean there is a wisdom outside the mind that can contemplate this mind; how could it be called 'such'? Further, the mind with which I search for the mind is in substance only the pure mind; how could there be a different dharma to be taken as object or thought of? It is only because of the habit-energy of deluded thinking that discrimination arises of itself." The marks of discrimination, existing, are not existent; their substance is only the pure mind. Further, even supposing one discriminates, one knows that it is precisely the pure mind discriminating.

Chinese Source

喻如眼見空華，聞言華是眼作，有即非有，唯有自眼。聞此語已，知華本無，不著於華。反更開眼自覓己眼，竟不能見。復謂種種眼根是己家眼，何以故？以不知能覓之眼即是所覓眼故。若能知華本無，眼外無法，唯有自眼，不須更覓於眼者，即不以眼覓眼。行者亦爾，聞言心外無法，唯有一心故，即使不念外法，但以妄想習氣故，更生分別，覓於淨心。是故當知，能覓淨心者即是淨心。設使應生分別，亦即是淨心，而淨心之體常無分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is like the eyes seeing flowers in the sky: one hears it said that the flowers are made by the eyes — existing, they are not existent, and there are only one's own eyes. Having heard these words, one knows that the flowers are originally nonexistent and is not attached to the flowers. Then one turns around and opens one's eyes to search for one's own eyes, and in the end cannot see them. Further, one takes the various eye-faculties to be one's own eyes. Why? Because one does not know that the eye that searches is the very eye being searched for. If one can know that the flowers are originally nonexistent, that there are no dharmas outside the eyes, only one's own eyes, and that there is no need to search further for the eyes, then one does not use the eyes to search for the eyes. The practitioner is also like this: hearing it said that there are no dharmas outside the mind and only the one mind, one no longer thinks of external dharmas; yet, only because of the habit-energy of deluded thinking, discrimination arises further and one searches for the pure mind. Therefore one should know that what can search for the pure mind is precisely the pure mind. Even if discrimination should arise, it too is the pure mind, and yet the substance of the pure mind is always without discrimination.

Chinese Source

作此解者，名為隨順真如，亦得名為止門。久久修習，無明妄想習氣盡故，念即自息，名證真如。亦無異法來證，但如息波入水，即名此真如為大寂靜止門。復以發心已來，觀門方便及以悲願熏習力故，即於定中興起大用。或從定起，若念、若見、若心、若境種種差別，即是真如用義也，此名從止起觀。又復熾然分別而常體寂，雖常體寂而即緣起分別，此名止觀雙行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Making this understanding is called according with suchness; it can also be called the gate of calming. Cultivating it for a long, long time, because the habit-energy of ignorance and deluded thinking is exhausted, thought ceases of itself; this is called realizing suchness. Nor does some different dharma come to realize it; it is only like a wave subsiding into the water. This suchness is then called the gate of calming of great tranquility. Further, because of the force of the perfuming of the expedients of the gate of contemplation and of compassion and vows since the arousing of the mind, within concentration the great function arises. Or, emerging from concentration, whether thought or seeing, whether mind or object, with all kinds of distinctions — this is the meaning of the function of suchness; this is called giving rise to contemplation from calming. Further, discriminating blazingly yet always tranquil in substance, and though always tranquil in substance, yet arising by conditions and discriminating — this is called practicing calming and contemplation together.

The Three Non-Natures and the Order of Practice

Chinese Source

上來三番明止觀二門，當知觀門即能成立三性緣起為有，止門即能除滅三性得入三無性。入三無性者，謂除分別性，入無相性；除依他性，入無生性；除真實性，入無性性。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the three rounds above explaining the two gates of calming and contemplation, one should know that the gate of contemplation can establish the conditioned arising of the three natures as existent, and the gate of calming can remove and extinguish the three natures and enter the three non-natures. Entering the three non-natures means: removing the discriminated nature, one enters the nature of no marks; removing the dependent nature, one enters the nature of no arising; removing the real nature, one enters the nature of no nature.

Chinese Source

就真實性中所以有四番明止觀者，但此窮深之處微妙難知，是故前示妄空非實，除妄空以明止，即是無性性；次一顯即偽是真，息異執以辨寂，即是無真性，是故無性性，或名無無性，或云無真性也；第三一重止觀者，即是根本真如三昧；最後第四一重止觀者，即是雙現前也。又復行者若利机深識，則不須從第一分別性修，但徑依第二依他性修。此依他性亦得名分別性，以具有二性義也。若不能如是者，即須次第從第一性修，然後依第二性修，依次而進也。終不得越前二性徑依第三性修也。又復雖是初行，不妨念念之中三番並學，資成第三番也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for why there are four rounds explaining calming-and-contemplation within the real nature: it is only because this place, where depth is exhausted, is subtle and hard to know. Therefore the first shows that the deluded emptiness is unreal, and by removing the deluded emptiness explains calming; this is the nature of no nature. The next reveals that the false is the true, and by ceasing the grasping of difference distinguishes tranquility; this is no true nature. Therefore the nature of no nature is also named the nature of no absence, or called no true nature. The third layer of calming-and-contemplation is the fundamental samādhi of suchness. The last, fourth layer of calming-and-contemplation is the two being present together. Further, if the practitioner is of sharp capacity and deep understanding, he need not cultivate from the first, the discriminated nature, but may cultivate directly in dependence on the second, the dependent nature. This dependent nature can also be called the discriminated nature, because it possesses the meaning of both natures. If one cannot do this, one must cultivate in order from the first nature, then cultivate in dependence on the second nature, advancing in sequence. In no case may one skip the first two natures and cultivate directly in dependence on the third nature. Further, even a beginner is not prevented from learning all three rounds together in every thought, to help accomplish the third round.

Chinese Source

問曰：既言真實性法，有何可除？若可除者，即非真實。答曰：執二無以為真實性者，即須除之，故曰無無性。妄智分別淨心謂為可觀者，亦須息此分別異相，示其無別真性可得分別，故言無真性。但除此等於真性上橫執之真，非謂除滅真如之體。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since it is called the dharma of the real nature, what is there to remove? If it can be removed, it is not real. Answer: Whoever grasps the absence of the two as the real nature must have this removed; therefore it is called the nature of no absence. When deluded wisdom discriminates the pure mind and takes it to be something that can be contemplated, one must also cease this discrimination of different marks, showing that there is no separate true nature that can be discriminated; therefore it is called no true nature. This removes only the "true" that is wrongly grasped upon the true nature; it does not mean removing and extinguishing the substance of suchness.

The Simile of the Handkerchief Rabbit

Chinese Source

復更有譬喻能顯三性止觀二門，今當說之。譬如手巾本來無兔，真實性法亦復如是，唯一淨心，自性離相也。加以幻力，巾似兔現。依他性法亦復如是，妄熏真性，現六道相也。愚小無知，謂兔為實，分別性法亦復如是，意識迷妄，執虛為實。是故經言：「一切法如幻。」此喻三性觀門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, there is a simile that can reveal the two gates of calming and contemplation of the three natures; I shall now speak of it. It is like a handkerchief, on which originally there is no rabbit. The dharma of the real nature is also like this: it is only the one pure mind, apart from marks in its own nature. Add the power of illusion, and the handkerchief appears as if a rabbit. The dharma of the dependent nature is also like this: deluded perfuming of the true nature manifests the marks of the six paths. The foolish and the young, without knowledge, take the rabbit to be real. The dharma of the discriminated nature is also like this: mind-consciousness, deluded and confused, grasps the illusory as real. Therefore a sūtra says: "All dharmas are like illusions." This is a simile for the gate of contemplation of the three natures.

Chinese Source

若知此兔依巾似有，唯虛無實，無相性智亦復如是，能知諸法依心似有，唯是虛狀，無實相性也。若知虛兔之相唯是手巾，巾上之兔有即非有、本來不生，無生性智亦復如是，能知虛相唯是真心，心所現相，有即非有，自性無生也。若知手巾本來是有，不將無兔以為手巾，無性性智亦復如是，能知淨心本性自有，不以二性之無為真實性。此即喻三無性止門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one knows that this rabbit seems to exist in dependence on the handkerchief, only illusory and without reality — the wisdom of the nature of no marks is also like this: it can know that dharmas seem to exist in dependence on the mind, only illusory forms, without the nature of true marks. If one knows that the mark of the illusory rabbit is only the handkerchief, and that the rabbit on the handkerchief, existing, is not existent and originally does not arise — the wisdom of the nature of no arising is also like this: it can know that illusory marks are only the true mind, and that the marks manifested by the mind, existing, are not existent and are without arising in their own nature. If one knows that the handkerchief originally exists, and does not take the absence of the rabbit to be the handkerchief — the wisdom of the nature of no nature is also like this: it can know that the pure mind originally exists in its own nature, and does not take the absence of the two natures to be the real nature. This is a simile for the gate of calming of the three non-natures.

Chinese Source

是故若欲捨離世諦，當修止門，入三無性。若欲不壞緣起，建立世諦，當修觀門，解知三性。若不修觀門，即不知世諦所以緣起；若不修止門，即不知真諦所以常寂。若不修觀門，便不知真即是俗；若不修止門，即不知俗即是真。以是義故，須依幻喻，通達三性三無性。如幻喻能通達三性三無性，其餘夢、化、影、像、水月、陽焰、乾城、餓鬼等喻，但是依實起虛，執虛為實者，悉喻三性，類以可知。若直以此等諸喻依實起虛故，偏喻依他性亦得也。但虛體是實，即可喻真實性；虛隨執轉，即可喻分別性。是故此等諸喻通譬三性。解此喻法次第無相，即可喻三無性也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore, if one wishes to abandon the worldly truth, one should cultivate the gate of calming and enter the three non-natures. If one wishes not to destroy conditioned arising and to establish the worldly truth, one should cultivate the gate of contemplation and understand the three natures. If one does not cultivate the gate of contemplation, one does not know why the worldly truth arises by conditions; if one does not cultivate the gate of calming, one does not know why the truth of the real is always tranquil. If one does not cultivate the gate of contemplation, one does not know that the true is the conventional; if one does not cultivate the gate of calming, one does not know that the conventional is the true. For this reason one must rely on the simile of illusion to penetrate the three natures and the three non-natures. As the simile of illusion can penetrate the three natures and the three non-natures, so the other similes — dreams, transformations, shadows, images, the moon in water, mirages, gandharva cities, hungry ghosts, and so on — in which the illusory arises in dependence on the real and the illusory is grasped as real, are all similes for the three natures; by analogy this can be known. If, simply because in these similes the illusory arises in dependence on the real, one takes them exclusively as similes for the dependent nature, that too is acceptable. But the substance of the illusory being real can be a simile for the real nature, and the illusory turning according to grasping can be a simile for the discriminated nature. Therefore these similes illustrate all three natures. Understanding these simile-dharmas, in order, as without marks can be a simile for the three non-natures.

The Simile of the Dream

Chinese Source

又更分別夢喻，以顯三性三無性。譬如凡夫慣習諸法故，即於夢中心現諸法，依他性法亦復如是，由無始已來果時無明，及以妄想熏習真實性故，真心依熏現於虛相果報也。彼夢裡人為睡蓋所覆故，不能自知己身、他身皆是夢心所作，即便執為實事，是故夢裡自他種種受用得成。分別性法亦復如是，意識為果時無明所迷故，不知自他，咸是真心依熏所作，便即妄執為實，是故自他種種受用得成也。是以經言：「是身如夢，為虛妄見。」虛者即是依他性，妄者即是分別性，此即緣起三性為觀門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the simile of the dream is distinguished again to reveal the three natures and the three non-natures. It is like an ordinary person who, because of habitual familiarity with dharmas, has dharmas appear in his mind in a dream. The dharma of the dependent nature is also like this: because ignorance in the fruit phase and deluded thinking have perfumed the real nature since beginningless time, the true mind, in dependence on perfuming, manifests illusory marks and fruits of retribution. The person in the dream, being covered by the hindrance of sleep, cannot know by himself that his own body and others' bodies are all made by the dreaming mind, and grasps them as real phenomena; therefore within the dream the various enjoyments of self and others are accomplished. The dharma of the discriminated nature is also like this: mind-consciousness, deluded by ignorance in the fruit phase, does not know that self and others are all made by the true mind in dependence on perfuming, and deludedly grasps them as real; therefore the various enjoyments of self and others are accomplished. Hence a sūtra says: "This body is like a dream — it is an illusory and deluded seeing." "Illusory" is the dependent nature; "deluded" is the discriminated nature. This is the conditioned arising of the three natures, as the gate of contemplation.

Chinese Source

然此夢中所執為實者，但是夢心之相，本無有實。分別性法亦復如是，但是虛想，從心所起，本來無實，即是無相性也。又彼夢中虛相有即非有，唯是夢心，更無餘法。依他性法亦復如是，自他虛相有即非有，唯是本識，更無餘法，即是無生性也。又彼夢心即是本時覺心，但由睡眠因緣，故名為夢心，夢心之外無別覺心可得。真實性法亦復如是，平等無二，但以無明染法熏習因緣故，與染和合，名為本識。然實本識之外無別真心可得，即是無性性法。此即除滅三性為止門也。以是喻故，三性三無性即可顯了。此明止觀體狀中，約染濁三性以明止觀體狀竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet what is grasped as real within this dream is only a mark of the dreaming mind, originally without reality. The dharma of the discriminated nature is also like this: it is only illusory imagining arising from the mind, originally without reality; this is the nature of no marks. Further, the illusory marks in that dream, existing, are not existent; they are only the dreaming mind, and there is no other dharma. The dharma of the dependent nature is also like this: the illusory marks of self and others, existing, are not existent; they are only the root consciousness, and there is no other dharma; this is the nature of no arising. Further, that dreaming mind is the mind that was awake before; only because of the causes and conditions of sleep is it called the dreaming mind; apart from the dreaming mind no separate waking mind can be found. The dharma of the real nature is also like this: it is equal and nondual; only because of the causes and conditions of the perfuming of the defiled dharmas of ignorance does it combine with defilement and is called the root consciousness. Yet in reality, apart from the root consciousness no separate true mind can be found; this is the dharma of the nature of no nature. This is removing and extinguishing the three natures, as the gate of calming. By this simile the three natures and the three non-natures can be made clear. This completes the explanation, within the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation, of the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation in terms of the defiled and turbid three natures.

Calming-and-Contemplation in the Pure Three Natures

Chinese Source

次明清淨三性中止觀體狀。就中亦有三番：一明分別性中止觀體狀、二明依他性中止觀體狀、三明真實性中止觀體狀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the pure three natures. Within this there are also three rounds: first, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the discriminated nature; second, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the dependent nature; third, explaining the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the real nature.

Chinese Source

第一、分別性中止觀體狀者，謂知一切諸佛菩薩所有色身，及以音聲、大悲大願、依報眾具、殊形六道，變化施設，乃至金軀現滅、舍利分頒、泥木彫圖、表彰處所，及以經教威儀、住持等法，但能利益眾生者，當知皆由大悲大願之熏，及以眾生机感之力，因緣具足，熏淨心故。心性依熏，顯現斯事。是故唯是真性緣起之能，道理即無實也。但諸眾生有無明妄想故，曲見不虛。行者但能觀察，知此曲見執心，是無明妄想者，即名為觀。以知此見是迷妄故，強作心意，觀知無實，唯是自心所作。如是知故，實執止息，即名為止。此是分別性中從觀入止也。◎

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation within the discriminated nature: one knows that the form bodies of all buddhas and bodhisattvas, and their voices, great compassion and great vows, the furnishings of their dependent [reward], their distinct forms in the six paths, their transformations and arrangements, and even the golden body appearing to pass into extinction, the relics being distributed, images carved and painted in clay and wood, the places that display them, as well as the scriptural teachings, the deportment, the maintenance [of the Dharma], and other such dharmas — whatever can benefit sentient beings — one should know that these are all due to the perfuming of great compassion and great vows together with the power of the resonating capacities of sentient beings: the causes and conditions being complete, they perfume the pure mind, and the mind's nature, in dependence on perfuming, manifests these phenomena. Therefore they are only the capacity of the conditioned arising of the true nature, and in principle they are without reality. But sentient beings, having ignorance and deluded thinking, see them crookedly as not illusory. If the practitioner can simply observe and know that this crooked seeing and grasping mind is ignorance and deluded thinking, this is called contemplation. Because one knows this seeing is delusion, one forcibly applies the mind and contemplates and knows them as without reality, only made by one's own mind. Because one knows in this way, the grasping of them as real ceases; this is called calming. This is entering calming from contemplation within the discriminated nature.

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

Vimalakīrti quotations. "Remove only the illness, and not the dharmas" (unit 13) follows the site's Vimalakīrti, "Inquiring after the Illness" (T475: 但除其病，而不除法). In unit 1 our text reads 但治其病 ("only cure the illness"), apparently a free citation of the same line. "This body is like a dream — it is a false and empty seeing" is the site's wording; because our text glosses 虛 and 妄 separately as the dependent and the discriminated natures, unit 60 renders it "an illusory and deluded seeing".

Avataṃsaka quotations. "The bodies of all buddhas are only one Dharma-body" (unit 5) is from the verses of Bhadraśrī in the "Bright Difficulties" chapter (T278: 文殊法常爾…一切諸佛身，唯是一法身); the full verse is quoted in Part 3. "It is not what consciousness can cognize, nor is it the sphere of the mind" (unit 26) is also in T278 (非識所能識，亦非心境界). Sudhana seeing himself before all buddhas (unit 5; the text writes 善才 for 善財) refers to the Gaṇḍavyūha section; the passage was not pinpointed.

Awakening of Faith (T1666). "What neither arises nor ceases, combined with what arises and ceases, is called the ālaya consciousness" abridges T1666 (which adds 非一非異). "The mind as suchness is apart from the mark of mental objects" condenses T1666's list (wording as in Part 1). "The ālaya consciousness has two portions: awakening and nonawakening" paraphrases T1666's "this consciousness has two meanings." "Because of dependence on original awakening there is nonawakening; because of dependence on nonawakening there is the deluded mind": T1666 reads 依本覺故而有不覺，依不覺故說有始覺 ("…there is initial awakening"); our text's second clause differs.

Śrīmālā (T353). "The mind pure in its own nature… that mind is defiled by afflictions" (unit 16) is from the Śrīmālā: 自性清淨心而有染污…彼心為煩惱所染.

Sources not located. "Because of the body of shared marks, all buddhas ultimately do not become buddhas" (unit 5); "Because in the seed phase the ālaya consciousness serves as the root seed for all dharmas" (unit 16, cited as a treatise); "All dharmas are like illusions" (unit 59) is a common formula (the site's Vimalakīrti has 諸法如幻相) and was not traced to one text.

Three natures (三性). 分別性 / 依他性 / 真實性 are Paramārtha's names (later 遍計所執性 / 依他起性 / 圓成實性); rendered the discriminated / dependent / real nature. The text's use is its own: each nature has a pure and a defiled (染濁 "defiled and turbid") form, and unit 48 gives two further schemes, one of which places all three natures inside the dependent nature. The three non-natures (三無性) are the nature of no marks, of no arising, and of no nature; unit 57 adds the alternative names 無無性 "the nature of no absence" and 無真性 "no true nature." In units 55–58 無 is rendered "absence" so the argument about "the absence of the two natures" stays visible.

Shared and unshared marks (unit 7). The hungry ghosts who see the Ganges as fire, dry ground, or pus and blood are a stock example in Yogācāra literature (one water seen four ways). 共不共相識 is kept literal ("the consciousness of shared and unshared marks"). Rebirth with Amitāyus is given as an example of a land created by shared karma.

Root consciousness (本識). Unit 16 equates the root consciousness, the ālaya consciousness, the combined consciousness (和合識), the seed consciousness, and the consciousness of retribution, and gives the water simile: the true mind is the water (substance), the root consciousness its flowing (mark), the sixth and seventh consciousnesses its waves (function).

Interpretive readings kept out. Zhili and later Dixian read this text's "pure" and "defiled" three natures, and the stainless pure mind possessing a "contrary function" (units 31–32, 35–37, 43), in terms of the Tiantai doctrine of inherent evil (性惡). The translation keeps the text's own terms (性染 inherent defilement, 違用 the contrary function, 示違 showing the contrary).

Apparent copying errors. Unit 28: 依地性 is read as 依他性 "the dependent nature." Unit 53: 非此觀已 is read as 作此觀已 "having made this contemplation." Unit 7: 時顧 ("those who for a time") and 聽依 ("rely on it"; perhaps 所依) are uncertain. Units 3–4: CBETA punctuates 一切眾生亦同。以一心為體; the translation reads the two clauses together.

Alternatively. Unit 1: 事法有成有敗故，此生彼滅性義無始並具 is read "this arises and that ceases; but the natures, in meaning, are possessed together." Alternatively: "the meaning of the natures — that this arises and that ceases — is possessed together from beginningless time." Unit 54: 緣心遣心 "by taking the mind as object and dismissing the mind"; alternatively, "taking [marks] as conditions in the mind and dismissing them by the mind."

The blank-mind practice (unit 26). The visitor's practice of "not thinking of names and meanings" is refuted at length but finally called "a calming dharma of its own kind" and "a gradual dharma-gate," useful as a lock on "the restlessness of the monkey" if one does not grasp it.

Exclamations. 癡人 ("Fool!", unit 5) and 蠓蟲 ("Gnat!", unit 6) are the śramaṇa's own rebukes; they are translated, not softened.
