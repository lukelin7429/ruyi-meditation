What Calming-and-Contemplation Removes and Does; Bowing, Eating, and the Latrine

This final part opens Fascicle Four and completes the five topics. It first finishes the third topic, calming-and-contemplation within the "pure" three natures: the practitioner contemplates the buddhas' pure virtues as made by the mind and then as one with his own mind. Questions follow on how buddhas and sentient beings can be one in substance yet distinct, and why another's merit can benefit oneself only when it is recognized, as in the Lotus parable of the destitute son. The fourth topic sets out what each practice removes and attains, nature by nature, and explains that understanding does not cut off delusion head-on but perfumes its seeds away, like incense in foul-smelling clothes. The fifth describes the activity of accomplished calming and contemplation in a passage of parallel phrases and closes with verses on the one mind as gold that can be cast as a snake or a buddha. The work ends with practical instructions: bowing to the buddhas, eating, and using the latrine, each done first by contemplation (imagining the food, the bowl, or the place transformed and offered) and then by calming (seeing all of it as the one mind).

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the gate of calming-and-contemplation within the dependent nature: because of the gate of calming above, here one knows that the pure virtues of the buddhas are only illusory and provisional marks made by the mind. Because illusory marks arising by conditions are not absent, the pure function can be perfectly manifest, showing the requital of the perfuming causes of vast eons. And further, because it gathers in and transforms in response to conditions, its moisture can reach even the fine grasses, displaying the boundless power of appeal that calls it forth. This is the conditioned arising of the pure mind, tranquil yet always functioning. Making this understanding is called the gate of contemplation. Because one uses this gate of contemplation as an expedient, one can know that the virtues of benefiting oneself and benefiting others that arise from the pure mind, existing, are not existent, functioning yet always tranquil. Understanding in this way is called the gate of calming. This calming and contemplation should be practiced together; practicing them one after the other is also acceptable.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the gate of calming-and-contemplation within the real nature: because of the practice of calming above, one knows that the pure virtues of the buddhas are only the one mind; this is called contemplation. Further, one knows that the pure mind of the buddhas is the pure mind of sentient beings, and the pure mind of sentient beings is the pure mind of the buddhas, nondual and without distinction. Because there is no distinction, one does not contemplate the buddhas' pure mind outside one's own mind. Because one does not search for the Buddha's mind outside the mind, discrimination ceases of itself and the deluded mind has ceased. Further, one knows that my mind and the Buddha's mind are originally a single suchness; therefore this is called calming. This is called the gate of calming-and-contemplation within the real nature.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the pure three natures above, first, within the first nature, one enters calming from contemplation, and from this practice of calming enters contemplation within the second nature. Again from this contemplation one enters calming, and from this calming enters contemplation within the third nature. Again from this contemplation one enters calming, and so one attains the equality of my mind and the Buddha's mind as a single suchness; this is entering cultivation along a single track, fulfilled. Further, because great compassion and expedients have perfumed the mind since the arousing of the mind, within concentration function arises and flourishes abundantly: there is no task it does not do, no mark it does not make; the great function of the dharma-realm is without obstruction or hindrance; this is called emerging cultivation. Functioning, it is tranquil; tranquil, it functions; this is the two being present together. Even an ordinary being at this very time can cultivate tranquility and function together in this way. What does this mean? Knowing that all dharmas, existing, are not existent, is being always tranquil while functioning; not existing, yet existing, with seeming dharmas not absent, is called always functioning while tranquil. Therefore form is itself emptiness; it is not that form ceases and only then becomes empty.

Buddhas and Sentient Beings: The Same and Distinct

Chinese Source

問曰：既言佛心、眾生心無二無別，云何說有佛與眾生之異名？答曰：心體是同，復有無障礙別性。以有別性故，得受無始已來我執熏習。以有熏力別故，心性依熏，現有別相。以約此我執之相故，說佛與眾生二名之異也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since you say the mind of the buddhas and the mind of sentient beings are nondual and without distinction, how can one speak of the different names "buddha" and "sentient being"? Answer: The mind's substance is the same, yet it also has an unobstructed distinct nature. Because it has a distinct nature, it can receive the perfuming of self-grasping from beginningless time. Because the force of perfuming is distinct, the mind's nature, in dependence on perfuming, manifests distinct marks. In terms of these marks of self-grasping, the different names "buddha" and "sentient being" are spoken of.

Chinese Source

問曰：諸佛既離我執，云何得有十方三世佛別也？答曰：若離我執，證得心體平等之時，實無十方三世之異。但本在因地，未離執時，各別發願、各修淨土、各化眾生，如是等業差別不同，熏於淨心。心性依別熏之力故，現此十方三世諸佛依正二報相別，非謂真如之體有此差別之相。以是義故，一切諸佛常同常別，古今法爾，是故經言：「文殊法常爾，法王唯一法。一切無礙人，一道出生死。一切諸佛身，唯是一法身。」此即同異雙論。若一向唯同無別者，何故經言「一切諸佛身，一切無礙人」？若一向唯別不同者，何故經言「唯是一法身，一道出生死」？以是義故，真心雖復平等，而復具有差別之性。若解明鏡一質，即具眾像之性者，則不迷法界法門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since the buddhas are apart from self-grasping, how can there be distinct buddhas of the ten directions and the three times? Answer: When one is apart from self-grasping and has realized the equality of the mind's substance, there is really no difference of the ten directions and the three times. But originally, in the causal ground, when they had not yet left grasping, each made separate vows, each cultivated a pure land, each converted sentient beings; such karma, distinct and different, perfumed the pure mind. The mind's nature, by the force of distinct perfuming, manifests these distinct marks of the dependent and principal rewards of the buddhas of the ten directions and the three times; it does not mean that the substance of suchness has these marks of differentiation. For this reason all buddhas are always the same and always distinct; past and present it is naturally so. Therefore a sūtra says: "Mañjuśrī, the Dharma is ever thus: the Dharma King has only one Dharma; every unobstructed person goes beyond birth-and-death by the one path; the bodies of all buddhas are only one Dharma-body." This discusses sameness and difference together. If it were only the same, without distinction, why would the sūtra say "the bodies of all buddhas," "every unobstructed person"? If it were only distinct and not the same, why would the sūtra say "only one Dharma-body," "goes beyond birth-and-death by the one path"? For this reason, although the true mind is equal, it also possesses the nature of differentiation. If one understands that a bright mirror, one in substance, possesses the nature of all images, one will not be deluded about the dharma-gate of the dharma-realm.

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Because the true mind has the nature of differentiation, buddhas and sentient beings are each different; because the substance of the true mind is nondual, all ordinary beings and sages are only one Dharma-body. Then it should also be that because there is a distinct nature, others cultivate and I do not; and because the substance is one, others cultivate and I attain the path. Answer: Because there is the meaning of distinction, others' cultivating is not my cultivating; because the substance is one, cultivating and not cultivating are equal. Even so, if one understands this meaning of sameness of substance, the virtues others cultivate also have the capacity to benefit oneself. Therefore a sūtra says: "If a bodhisattva knows that all the merits of all buddhas are his own merits, this is a remarkable dharma." And further a sūtra says: "With all bodhisattvas sharing one storehouse of good roots." Therefore the practitioner should know that the merits made by buddhas, bodhisattvas, the holy ones of the two vehicles, ordinary beings, gods, humans, and so on, are all his own merits, and therefore he should rejoice in them.

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: If so, all ordinary beings should attain the path naturally. Answer: If this true mind had only the meaning of sameness, one could do without practice and attain the path through others; and further there would be no distinction between the bodily marks of self and others. Since suchness also has the meaning of a different nature, and so there can be the difference of self and other, why would one rely entirely on others in seeking the path? One should simply cultivate merit oneself, and also know that what others cultivate is one's own virtue; thus helping one another to accomplish it, one can attain the path with surpassing speed. How could one rely entirely on others? Further, one must know: if one only cultivates by oneself and does not know that what others cultivate is one's own, one again does not obtain others' benefit. It is just like the destitute son, who did not know that his father was his own father and the wealth his own wealth, and so for twenty-odd years suffered poverty, lodging in a grass hut; this is the meaning. Therefore, relying on causes and depending on conditions, one quickly accomplishes it. If one seeks alone without depending on others, one can obtain only the wage for clearing away dung.

問曰：上言諸佛淨德者有幾種？答曰：略言有其二種：一者自利、二者利他。自利之中復有三種：一者法身、二者報身、三者淨土。利他之中復有二種：一者順化、二者違化。順化之中有其二種：一者應身及摩菟摩化身、二者淨土及雜染土。此是諸佛淨德。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: How many kinds of pure virtues of the buddhas were spoken of above? Answer: Briefly, there are two kinds: first, benefiting oneself; second, benefiting others. Within benefiting oneself there are again three kinds: first, the Dharma-body; second, the reward-body; third, the pure land. Within benefiting others there are again two kinds: first, accordant conversion; second, contrary conversion. Within accordant conversion there are two kinds: first, the response-body and the manomaya (mind-made) transformation-body; second, the pure land and the mixed and defiled land. These are the pure virtues of the buddhas.

問曰：利他之德對緣施設，權現巧便，可言無實，唯是虛相，有即非有。自利之德即是法報二身，圓覺大智，顯理而成常樂我淨。云何說言有即非有？答曰：自利之德實是常樂我淨，不遷不變。正以顯理而成故，故得如是。復正以顯理而成故，即是心性緣起之用。然用無別用，用全是心；心無別心，心全是用。是故以體體用，有即非有，唯是一心而不廢常用；以用用體，非有即有，熾然法界而不妨常寂。寂即是用，名為觀門；用即是寂，名為止行。此即一體雙行，但為令學者泯相入寂故，所以先後別說止觀之異，非謂佛德有其遷變。又復色即是空，名之為止；空非滅色，目之為觀。世法尚爾，何況佛德而不得常用常寂者哉！

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The virtues of benefiting others are set up in response to conditions, provisional manifestations and skillful expedients; one can say they are unreal, only illusory marks, existing yet not existent. The virtues of benefiting oneself are the two bodies, Dharma and reward, the great wisdom of perfect awakening, which reveals principle and accomplishes permanence, bliss, self, and purity. How can you say that, existing, they are not existent? Answer: The virtues of benefiting oneself are truly permanence, bliss, self, and purity, unshifting and unchanging. Precisely because they are accomplished by revealing principle, they can be so. And precisely because they are accomplished by revealing principle, they are the function of the conditioned arising of the mind's nature. Yet the function is no separate function: the function is wholly the mind; the mind is no separate mind: the mind is wholly the function. Therefore, when substance is taken as the substance of function, existing, it is not existent; it is only the one mind, yet this does not set aside its constant function. When function is taken as the function of substance, not existing, it exists; it blazes through the dharma-realm, yet this does not hinder its constant tranquility. Tranquility being function is called the gate of contemplation; function being tranquility is called the practice of calming. This is the twofold practice of one substance; it is only in order to let learners erase marks and enter tranquility that the difference of calming and contemplation is explained separately, one before the other; it does not mean that the Buddha's virtues shift and change. Further, form is itself emptiness: this is named calming; emptiness is not the cessation of form: this is termed contemplation. If even worldly dharmas are like this, how much more could the Buddha's virtues not be always functioning and always tranquil!

問曰：佛德有即非有，不妨常住者，眾生亦有即非有，應不妨不滅。答曰：佛德即理顯，以成順用故，所以常住。眾生即理隱，以成違用故，所以生滅。常住之德雖有即非有，而復非有而有，故不妨常住。生滅之用亦雖有即非有，而復非有而有，故不妨生滅也。此約清淨三性以明止觀體狀竟。第三番體狀竟也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: If the Buddha's virtues, existing, are not existent, and this does not prevent their permanent abiding, then sentient beings too, existing, are not existent, and this should not prevent their not ceasing. Answer: The Buddha's virtues are principle revealed, forming the accordant function; therefore they abide permanently. Sentient beings are principle hidden, forming the contrary function; therefore they arise and cease. Although the virtue of permanent abiding, existing, is not existent, it is also, not existing, existent; therefore this does not prevent permanent abiding. Although the function of arising and ceasing likewise, existing, is not existent, it is also, not existing, existent; therefore this does not prevent arising and ceasing. This completes the explanation of the substance and form of calming-and-contemplation in terms of the pure three natures. The third round, the substance and form, is complete.

The Fourth Round: What Calming-and-Contemplation Cuts Off and Attains

Chinese Source

次明第四止觀除障得益。就中復有三門分別：一約分別性以明除障得益、二約依他性以明除障得益、三約真實性以明除障得益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the fourth, the obstructions that calming-and-contemplation removes and the benefits it attains. Within this there are again three gates of distinction: first, explaining the removal of obstructions and the attainment of benefits in terms of the discriminated nature; second, explaining the removal of obstructions and the attainment of benefits in terms of the dependent nature; third, explaining the removal of obstructions and the attainment of benefits in terms of the real nature.

Chinese Source

初明分別性中所除障者，謂能解不知境虛執實之心，是無明妄想故，即是觀行成。以觀成故，能除無明妄想上迷妄。何謂迷妄之上迷妄？謂不知迷妄是迷妄，即是迷也。以此迷故，即執為非迷，復是妄想。此一重迷妄因前一重上起，故名迷妄之上迷妄也。是故行者雖未能除不了境虛執實之心，但能識知此心是癡妄者，即是能除癡妄之上迷妄也。此是除障，以除障故堪能進修止行，即是得益。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining the obstructions removed within the discriminated nature: one understands that the mind that does not know that objects are illusory and grasps them as real is ignorance and deluded thinking; this is the accomplishment of the practice of contemplation. Because contemplation is accomplished, one can remove the confused delusion upon ignorance and deluded thinking. What is "confused delusion upon confused delusion"? Not knowing that confused delusion is confused delusion is confusion; because of this confusion, one grasps it as not confusion, and this again is deluded thinking. This layer of confused delusion arises upon the previous layer; therefore it is called confused delusion upon confused delusion. Therefore, although the practitioner cannot yet remove the mind that does not understand that objects are illusory and grasps them as real, if he can recognize and know that this mind is foolish delusion, he can remove the confused delusion upon foolish delusion. This is the removal of obstruction; because the obstruction is removed, he is able to advance and cultivate the practice of calming; this is the attainment of benefit.

Chinese Source

又此迷妄之上迷妄，更以喻顯，如人迷東為西，即是妄執。此是一重迷妄也。他人語言：「汝今迷妄，謂東為西。」此人猶作是念：「我所見者非是迷妄。」以不知故，執為非迷者，復為妄想。此即迷妄之上重生迷妄。此人有何過失？謂有背家浪走之過。若此人雖未醒悟，但用他語，信知自心是迷妄者，即無迷妄之上迷妄。此人得何利益？謂雖復迷妄未醒，而得有向家之益。雖復證知諸法是虛，但能知境虛是無明執實是妄想者，即常不信己之所執，堪能進修止行，漸趣涅槃。若都不知此者，即當隨流苦海，增長三毒，背失涅槃寂靜之舍也。此明分別性中觀行斷得之義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, this confused delusion upon confused delusion is shown again by a simile. It is like a person confused about east, who takes it for west; this is deluded grasping. This is one layer of confused delusion. Another person tells him: "You are now confused and deluded, taking east for west." This person still thinks: "What I see is not confused delusion." Because he does not know, grasping it as not confusion is again deluded thinking. This is confused delusion arising again upon confused delusion. What fault does this person have? He has the fault of turning his back on home and wandering wildly. If this person, though not yet awakened, simply accepts the other's words and believes and knows that his own mind is confused and deluded, then he has no confused delusion upon confused delusion. What benefit does this person gain? Although his confused delusion is not yet awakened, he gains the benefit of facing home. Although one may realize and know that dharmas are illusory, if one simply knows that [regarding] the illusoriness of objects there is ignorance, and that grasping them as real is deluded thinking, one never trusts what one grasps, and is able to advance and cultivate the practice of calming and gradually approach nirvāṇa. If one does not know this at all, one follows the flow in the sea of suffering, increases the three poisons, and turns one's back on and loses the dwelling of the tranquility of nirvāṇa. This explains the meaning of the cutting off and attaining of the practice of contemplation within the discriminated nature.

Chinese Source

所言分別性中止行除障得益者，謂依彼觀行作方便故，能知諸法本來無實。實執止故，即是能除果時迷事無明及以妄想也。復於貪瞋漸已微薄，雖有罪垢，不為業繫。設受苦痛，解苦無苦，即是除障。復依此止即能成就依他性中觀行，故無塵智用隨心行故，即是得益。此明分別性中止行除障得益。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the removal of obstruction and attainment of benefit of the practice of calming within the discriminated nature: by using that practice of contemplation as an expedient, one can know that dharmas are originally without reality. Because the grasping of reality is calmed, one can remove ignorance in the fruit phase deluded about phenomena, together with deluded thinking. Further, greed and anger have gradually become slight; although there are stains of offense, one is not bound by karma. Even if one undergoes pain, understanding suffering, there is no suffering; this is the removal of obstruction. Further, in dependence on this calming one can accomplish the practice of contemplation within the dependent nature, so that the function of the wisdom of no sense-objects follows the mind's activity; this is the attainment of benefit. 凡大小便利亦有止觀。所言觀者，當於穢處作是念言：「此等不淨悉是心作，有即非有。我今應當變此不淨，令作清淨。」即想此穢處作寶池寶渠，滿中清淨香水，或滿酥酪。自想己身作七寶身，所棄便利即香乳酥蜜等。作此想已，持施一切眾生。即復知此淨相唯是心作，虛相無實，是名觀門。

Ru-Yi Translation

When urinating and defecating, too, there is calming-and-contemplation. As for contemplation: at the place of filth one should think: "All this impurity is made by the mind; existing, it is not existent. I should now transform this impurity and make it pure." Then imagine this place of filth as a jeweled pond and jeweled channel filled with pure fragrant water, or filled with ghee and curds. Imagine one's own body as a body of the seven treasures, and the urine and excrement discharged as fragrant milk, ghee, honey, and so on. Having made this imagining, take it and give it to all sentient beings. Then further know that these pure marks are only made by the mind, illusory marks without reality. This is called the gate of contemplation.

Chinese Source

所言止門者，知此不淨之處及身所棄不淨之物，唯是過去惡業熏心故，現此不淨之相可見。然此心相有即非有，唯是一心，平等無念。即名止門。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the gate of calming: know that this impure place and the impure things the body discharges appear as impure marks that can be seen only because past evil karma has perfumed the mind. Yet these marks of the mind, existing, are not existent; they are only the one mind, equal and without thought. This is called the gate of calming.

Chinese Source

問曰：上來所有淨不淨法，雖是心作，皆由過去業熏所起。何得現世假想變之，即從心轉？答曰：心體具足一切法性，而非緣不起。是故溷中穢相，由過業而得現；寶池酥酪，無往緣而不發。若能加心淨想，即是寶池酥酪之業熏心，故淨相得生，厭惡之心、空觀之心，即是除滅不淨之緣。淨熏心故，穢相隨滅。此蓋過去之業定能熏心起相，現世之功亦得熏心顯妙用也。如此於大小便處假想熏心而改變之，其餘一切淨穢境界，須如是假想熏心，以改其舊相，故得現在除去憎愛，亦能遠與五通為方便也。然初學行者未得事從心轉，但可閉目假想為之，久久純熟，即諸法隨念改轉。是故諸大菩薩，乃至二乘小聖、五通仙人等，能得即事改變，無而現有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: All the pure and impure dharmas above, although made by the mind, arise from the perfuming of past karma. How can deliberate imagining in the present life transform them, so that they turn according to the mind? Answer: The mind's substance fully possesses the natures of all dharmas, yet without conditions they do not arise. Therefore the foul marks of the latrine appear through past karma, while the jeweled pond and the ghee and curds, lacking past conditions, do not appear. If one can apply the mind to pure imagining, that is the karma of the jeweled pond and the ghee and curds perfuming the mind, and so pure marks can arise; and the mind of revulsion and the mind of the contemplation of emptiness remove and extinguish the conditions of impurity. Because purity perfumes the mind, the foul marks cease accordingly. For past karma can certainly perfume the mind and give rise to marks, and present effort too can perfume the mind and manifest wondrous functions. As one transforms the place of urination and defecation by perfuming the mind with deliberate imagining, so all other pure and foul spheres should be perfumed by deliberate imagining to change their old marks; thereby one can remove aversion and attachment in the present, and also remotely serve as an expedient for the five spiritual powers. Yet beginners who have not yet attained the turning of phenomena according to the mind can only do it with eyes closed, by deliberate imagining; after long practice, when it is pure and ripe, dharmas change according to thought. Therefore great bodhisattvas, and even the lesser sages of the two vehicles and the immortals with the five spiritual powers, can transform phenomena directly, making what does not exist appear.

問曰：諸聖人等種種變現之時，何故眾生有見不見？答曰：由共相識故得見，由不共相識故不見。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: When sages manifest various transformations, why do some sentient beings see them and some not? Answer: Through the consciousness of shared marks one sees them; through the consciousness of unshared marks one does not see them.

Chinese Source

問曰：菩薩神通與二乘神通有何差別？答曰：二乘神通但由假想而成，以心外見法，故有限有量。菩薩神通由知諸法悉是心作，唯有心相，心外無法，故無限無量也。又菩薩初學通時亦從假想而修，但即知諸法皆一心作。二乘唯由假想習通，但言定力，不言心作。道理論之，一等心作，但彼二乘不知，故有差別也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is the difference between the spiritual powers of bodhisattvas and the spiritual powers of the two vehicles? Answer: The spiritual powers of the two vehicles are accomplished only through deliberate imagining; because they see dharmas outside the mind, they are limited and measured. The spiritual powers of bodhisattvas, because they know that dharmas are all made by the mind, only marks of the mind, with no dharmas outside the mind, are unlimited and measureless. Further, when bodhisattvas first learn the powers, they too cultivate them through deliberate imagining, but they know that dharmas are all made by the one mind. The two vehicles practice the powers only through deliberate imagining; they speak of the power of concentration and do not speak of being made by the mind. Discussed in principle, both alike are made by the mind; only the two vehicles do not know it; therefore there is a difference.

Reading Notes for Part 3

Translator's notes

Avataṃsaka verse (unit 7). "Mañjuśrī, the Dharma is ever thus…" is from the verses of Bhadraśrī in the "Bright Difficulties" chapter (T278). The line "every unobstructed person goes beyond birth-and-death by the one path" follows the site's Mohe Zhiguan. "The mind makes the tathāgatas" (unit 35) is from T278's Yama Palace verses (應當如是觀，心造諸如來).

Quotations matched to site English. "This mind makes the buddha; this mind is the buddha" and "an ocean of perfect and universal knowing" follow the site's Contemplation Sūtra. "Form is itself emptiness; it is not that form ceases and only then becomes empty" follows the site's Vimalakīrti ("Entering the Dharma-Gate of Nonduality"). "To give a single meal to all beings…" follows the site's Vimalakīrti (Mahākāśyapa's episode); our text reads 諸賢聖 for the sūtra's 眾賢聖. "He makes offerings to the immeasurable koṭis of tathāgatas…" follows the site's Vimalakīrti ("Buddha Path" verses), but T475 reads 無有分別想 "no thought that discriminates," while our text reads 相, so "mark" replaces "thought." "This person walks a deviant path and cannot see the Tathāgata" (Diamond Sūtra, T235) follows the site's Mohe Zhiguan. The destitute son, the grass hut, and the "wage for clearing away dung" follow the site's Lotus, "Faith and Understanding" (the sūtra says twenty years; our text "twenty-odd years").

"Crooked seeing of the śrāvakas" (unit 35). From the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T374/T375: 聲聞緣覺曲見), where the "crooked view" is taking the Buddha's descent from Tuṣita, birth, and final nirvāṇa as real events.

Sources not located. "If a bodhisattva knows that all the merits of all buddhas are his own merits, this is a remarkable dharma" and "With all bodhisattvas sharing one storehouse of good roots" (unit 8). The verses on the King of Fragrance Treasuries (unit 36) appear to be the author's own; compare the incense clouds of the Avataṃsaka.

Removal of obstruction (斷得, 除障得益). The fourth topic is "what calming-and-contemplation cuts off and attains," worked out as "removing obstructions and attaining benefits" for each practice in each nature. Unit 20 rejects "removal by direct opposition" (敵對除), which it ascribes to the Lesser Vehicle, in favor of gradual perfuming of seeds.

Fruit-phase ignorance (unit 15). The calming of the dependent nature removes "ignorance in the fruit phase deluded about principle" (果時迷理無明). In Part 1 "ignorance deluded about principle" was another name for ignorance in the seed phase; the text's terminology is not fully consistent here, and the translation keeps the wording.

Stages (unit 22). 十解 "the ten understandings" (= the ten abidings) and 十迴向 "the ten dedications"; 地前 / 地上 "before the grounds" / "on the grounds." The text gives three alternative schemes and then says every practice can be done at every stage, differing only in clarity.

The activity passage (unit 23). A parallel-prose list of a buddha's deeds: the seven steps and raised hands at birth, sitting under the jeweled tree, the "five mountains" (probably the five peaks around Rājagṛha, i.e., Vulture Peak) shining while "the eight trees" (the four pairs of sāla trees at the parinirvāṇa) hide their radiance. 怕 in 怕兮凝湛 is read as 泊 "still."

Practical instructions (units 34–46). Bowing, eating, and using the latrine are each done by a gate of contemplation (deliberately imagining the scene transformed and offered) and a gate of calming (seeing it all as the one mind, without grasping even the mark of the one mind). Eating includes feeding "the eighty thousand families of worms" in the body, a figure also used in the site's Mohe Zhiguan. 假想 is rendered "deliberate imagining" and 想心 "the imagining mind."

Doubtful reading (unit 14). 雖復證知諸法是虛，但能知境虛是無明執實是妄想者 is translated literally ("although one may realize…"). The argument suggests 雖未證知 "although one has not yet realized." The phrase 境虛是無明 is elliptical; the bracket "[regarding]" marks the gap.

Alternatively (unit 11). 以體體用…以用用體 is rendered "when substance is taken as the substance of function… when function is taken as the function of substance." Alternatively: "using the substance to embody the function… using the function to employ the substance."

Names. 盧舍那 Rocana Buddha (the Buddha of the Avataṃsaka assembly); 摩菟摩 manomaya, "mind-made," transformation-body; 香藏王 "the King of Fragrance Treasuries" (not identified).

Tiantai readings kept out. Zhili and Dixian read the "contrary conversion" of buddhas (unit 10) and the gold-snake verse (units 28–31) as statements of inherent evil; the translation keeps the text's words, "contrary" and "the contrary and accordant natures."
