A Question About Suffering in the Pure Land

Mahāsthāmaprāpta's own crown, walking, and sitting each distinguish him further from Avalokiteśvara — his sitting alone is described as shaking entire buddha-lands and summoning Amitābha and Avalokiteśvara to divide their own bodies and gather like clouds. This sets up a genuine doctrinal puzzle: how can any of this be described as "liberating beings from suffering" in a land the Amitābha Sūtra itself insists contains no suffering at all?

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 3 of 4: 定善義 (Meaning of the Contemplative Good)

A Crown That Reflects Every Pure Land, and a Distinctive Gait

Chinese Source

五、從此菩薩天冠下，至皆於中現已來，正明天冠莊嚴之相與觀音不同。即有其四。一明冠上寶華多少。二明一一華上寶臺多少。三明一一臺中映現十方諸佛淨土。四明他方土現，彼此都無增減。六、從頂上肉髻下，至普現佛事已來，正明肉髻寶瓶之相。七、從餘諸身相已下，指同觀音也。

八、從此菩薩行時下，至如極樂世界已來，正明行與觀音不同相。即有其四。一明行不同相。二明震動遠近相。三明所震動處華現多。四明所現之華高而且顯，多諸瑩飾以類極樂莊嚴也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, from "this bodhisattva's own heavenly crown" down through "all appear within it," this makes plain that the adornment of his own heavenly crown differs from Avalokiteśvara's own. There are four points within it. First, this makes plain the number of jeweled flowers upon this crown. Second, this makes plain the number of jeweled platforms upon each and every one of these flowers. Third, this makes plain that, within each and every one of these platforms, the pure lands of the buddhas of the ten directions are reflected and revealed. Fourth, this makes plain that, as these other realms appear, there is no increase or decrease whatsoever, either here or there.

Sixth, from "upon the crown-protuberance of his head" down through "broadly manifesting the Buddha's own works," this makes plain the mark of the jeweled vase upon his own crown-protuberance. Seventh, from "as for the rest of his own bodily marks" onward, this points out that these are identical to Avalokiteśvara's own.

Eighth, from "when this bodhisattva walks" down through "resembling the World of Ultimate Bliss itself," this makes plain that his own manner of walking differs from Avalokiteśvara's own. There are four points within it. First, this makes plain the distinct mark of his own walking. Second, this makes plain the reach of the shaking this causes, both near and far. Third, this makes plain that, wherever this shaking occurs, manifold flowers appear there. Fourth, this makes plain that the flowers thus manifested stand tall and clearly visible, adorned with manifold splendor resembling the very adornment of the Land of Ultimate Bliss itself.

A note on this reflection

The claim that every other realm reflected within his crown's own jeweled platforms appears "with no increase or decrease whatsoever, either here or there" quietly resolves a puzzle that could otherwise trouble a literal-minded reader: reflecting an entire pure land within a platform does not diminish that land itself, nor does the platform's own capacity ever run short no matter how many realms it holds. This is the same principle already established for Vairocana's own crown adornments and for the countless pure lands shown within Vaidehī's earlier vision — containment without depletion.

A Question: Suffering, in the Land of Ultimate Bliss?

Chinese Source

九、從此菩薩坐時下，至度苦眾生已來，正明坐不同觀音相。即有其七。一明坐相。二明先動本國相。三明次動他方遠近相。四明動搖下上佛剎多少相。五明彌陀觀音等分身雲集相。六明臨空側塞皆坐寶華。七明分身說法各應所宜。

問曰：「《彌陀經》云：『彼國眾生無有眾苦、但受諸樂，故名極樂。』何故此經分身說法乃云度苦者，有何意也？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, from "when this bodhisattva sits" down through "liberates sentient beings from suffering," this makes plain that his own manner of sitting differs from Avalokiteśvara's own. There are seven points within it. First, this makes plain the mark of his own sitting. Second, this makes plain the mark of his own land shaking first. Third, this makes plain the mark of other realms then shaking, both near and far. Fourth, this makes plain the mark of the number of buddha-lands, below and above, thus set into motion. Fifth, this makes plain the mark of Amitābha and Avalokiteśvara likewise dividing their own bodies and gathering together like clouds. Sixth, this makes plain that, throughout the sky, densely filling every space, all are seated upon jeweled lotus flowers. Seventh, this makes plain that these divided bodies each expound the Dharma according to what is fitting for each occasion.

Question: "The Amitābha Sūtra says: 'The beings of that land are without any manifold suffering whatsoever, receiving only every manner of bliss — hence it is named the Land of Ultimate Bliss.' Why, then, does this present sūtra speak of these divided bodies expounding the Dharma so as to liberate beings from suffering? What is the meaning of this?"

A note on this question

This is a genuine internal tension between two scriptures Shandao treats as equally authoritative, not a rhetorical setup for an easy answer — the Amitābha Sūtra's own explicit definition of that land as free of "any manifold suffering whatsoever" appears to directly contradict this sūtra's own language of bodhisattvas "liberating beings from suffering" within that very land. The answer, given in full in the next part, turns out to hinge on a distinction between two entirely different senses of the words "suffering" and "bliss" — one measured against this defiled world, the other measured entirely within the pure land's own internal hierarchy of attainment.
