The Nine Grades Complete

Twelve full kalpas — the longest confinement of any grade among the nine — and then the final lotus opens. Shandao's closing verse for the lower rank completes not only the nine grades themselves, but the sūtra's entire main exposition: both the contemplative good of the thirteen contemplations and the dispersed good of the three blessings and nine grades now stand finished.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 4 of 4: 散善義 (Meaning of the Dispersed Good)

Twelve Kalpas, and the Cause of Awakening at Last

Chinese Source

五、從於蓮華中滿十二劫已下，正明第十門中到彼華開遲疾不同。六、從觀音、大勢下，至發菩提心已來，正明第十一門中華開已後得益有異。即有其三。一明二聖為宣甚深妙法。二明除罪歡喜。三明後發勝心。七、從是名已下，總結。上來雖有七句不同，廣解下品下生竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, from "within this lotus flower, a full twelve kalpas pass" onward, this makes plain, within the tenth gate, the differing speed, swift or slow, with which the lotus flower opens once that land is reached.

Sixth, from "Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta" down through "gives rise to the aspiration for awakening," this makes plain, within the eleventh gate, the differing benefits gained once this flower has opened. There are three points within it. First, this makes plain these two sages expounding a profound and wondrous Dharma. Second, this makes plain the joy of having this offense removed. Third, this makes plain this being then giving rise to this supreme aspiration.

Seventh, from "this, then, is" onward, this gathers together the general conclusion. Though the above has unfolded across seven distinct points, this concludes the full explanation of the lowest rebirth of the lower rank.

A note on this final wait

Twelve kalpas stands as the longest of any confinement among all nine grades — longer than the six kalpas of the eighth grade, longer still than the immediacy granted the very first. Yet the outcome, once that wait ends, is identical in kind to every grade before it: the same two sages, the same profound Dharma, and, at last, the very aspiration for awakening that even the gravest of offenses could not finally prevent from arising.

A Verse Gathering the Entire Lower Rank

Chinese Source

讚云：「下輩下行下根人，十惡五逆等貪瞋，四重偷僧謗正法，未曾慚愧悔前愆。終時苦相如雲集，地獄猛火罪人前，忽遇往生善知識，急勸專稱彼佛名。化佛菩薩尋聲到，一念傾心入寶蓮，三華障重開多劫，於時始發菩提因。」上來雖有三位不同，總解下輩一門之義竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

A verse of praise says: "Those of the lower rank, of lowest practice, of lowest capacity, committed the ten evils, the five heinous crimes, and every manner of greed and anger; the four grave offenses, theft from the Saṅgha, slander of the true Dharma — never once did they feel shame, nor repent of their former transgressions. As death draws near, the marks of suffering gather like clouds, the fierce fires of hell stand before this offender; then, all at once, a good friend versed in the way of rebirth is encountered, urgently exhorting single-minded recitation of that Buddha's own name. Transformation-buddhas and bodhisattvas arrive in answer to that very sound, and in a single thought this being's heart inclines wholly, entering the jeweled lotus; three flowers, their obstruction grave, open only after many kalpas — and it is then, at last, that the cause of awakening first arises."

Though the above has unfolded across three distinct positions, this concludes the full explanation of the meaning of the single gate of the lower rank.

A note on this milestone

This verse closes not only the lower rank, but the entire nine grades of rebirth — and with them, the sūtra's complete main exposition. The thirteen contemplations of the contemplative good, spoken in answer to Vaidehī's own request, and the three blessings and nine grades of the dispersed good, spoken by the Buddha of his own accord, together form the whole of what this sūtra actually teaches about how rebirth is attained. Every grade, from the vajra platform of the very first down to the twelve-kalpa wait of the very last, has now been drawn together into a single, complete account. What remains is what the sūtra's own assembly does with what it has just heard.

Nine Grades Complete

This closes Part 103, and with it the nine grades of rebirth in their entirety — the lower rank spanning Parts 95 through 103, and the full nine grades spanning Parts 72 through 103. Part 104 turns to a new section of the sūtra altogether: the benefit section (得益分), where Vaidehī herself, upon beholding Amitābha, attains insight into the non-arising of all things, and her attendants each resolve to seek this same rebirth.
