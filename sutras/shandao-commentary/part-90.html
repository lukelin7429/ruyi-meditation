Closing the Upper Rank

The third grade closes quietly, its own lotus opening after a single day and night rather than the immediacy of the first grade or the single night of the second — a final, precise measure of relative practice. Shandao then steps back from all three grades individually to offer a single verse gathering them together.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 4 of 4: 散善義 (Meaning of the Dispersed Good)

A Single Day and Night, and the Third Grade Complete

Chinese Source

五、從一日一夜已下，正明第十門中到彼華開時節不同。六、從七日之中下，至皆演妙法已來，正明第十一門中華開已後得益不同。七、從遊歷十方下，至住歡喜地已來，正明他方得益，亦名後益也。八、從是名已下，總結。上來雖有八句不同，廣解上品下生竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, from "a single day and a single night" onward, this makes plain, within the tenth gate, the differing timing of the lotus opening once that land is reached.

Sixth, from "within seven days" down through "all expound the wondrous Dharma," this makes plain, within the eleventh gate, the differing benefits gained once this lotus has opened.

Seventh, from "traveling throughout the ten directions" down through "abides upon the ground of joy," this makes plain the benefit gained in other realms — this too is called the further benefit.

Eighth, from "this, then, is" onward, this gathers together the general conclusion. Though the above has unfolded across eight distinct points, this concludes the full explanation of the lowest rebirth of the upper rank.

A Verse Gathering the Entire Upper Rank

Chinese Source

讚云：「上輩上行上根人，求生淨土斷貪瞋，就行差別分三品，五門相續助三因。一日七日專精進，畢命乘臺出六塵，慶哉難逢今得遇，永證無為法性身。」上來雖有三位不同，總解上輩一門之義竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

A verse of praise says: "Those of the upper rank, of highest practice, of superior capacity, seek rebirth in the pure land, severing greed and anger; according to the differences in their own practice, they divide into three grades, five gates sustained without interruption, supporting the three causes. Whether striving with utmost diligence for a single day or for seven, when life at last is done, they ride this platform, emerging beyond the six sense-objects entirely; how joyful, how rare an encounter, that we should meet with this today — forever to realize the very body of the unconditioned Dharma-nature itself."

Though the above has unfolded across three distinct positions, this concludes the full explanation of the meaning of the single gate of the upper rank.

A note on this verse

The verse's own final line — "forever to realize the very body of the unconditioned Dharma-nature itself" — names the ultimate horizon toward which all three grades of the upper rank, for all their differences in capacity and practice, are equally directed. What the preceding sixteen parts have carefully distinguished — vajra platform against purple gold against golden lotus, immediate opening against a single night against a single day — the closing verse deliberately collapses back into a single shared destination. The differences in speed and splendor, real as they are, all converge on the same final realization. This closes the entire upper rank of the nine grades.

Upper Rank Complete

This closes Part 90, and with it the upper rank in its entirety — all three of its grades, the highest degree of practitioner among the nine, spanning Parts 72 through 90.
