The Nine Grades' Own Text, Turned Against Their Misreading

Having refuted the "other masters'" reading by reasoning alone in the previous part, Shandao now works through all nine grades of rebirth one at a time, citing the sūtra's own wording against the very claim that reading was built on. Ordinary sinners, thieving monks, and the merely filial turn out, on his reading, to be exactly who the nine grades were describing all along.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 1 of 4: 玄義分 (Profound Meaning)

The Upper Three Grades

Chinese Source

第三、重舉九品返對破者。諸師云：上品上生人是四地至七地已來菩薩者。何故《觀經》云：「三種眾生當得往生。何者為三：一者但能持戒修慈。二者不能持戒修慈，但能讀誦大乘。三者不能持戒讀經，唯能念佛法僧等。此之三人，各以己業專精勵意。一日一夜，乃至七日七夜相續不斷。各迴所作之業，求願往生。命欲終時，阿彌陀佛及與化佛菩薩大眾放光授手，如彈指頃，即生彼國。」以此文證，正是佛去世後，大乘極善上品凡夫，日數雖少，作業時猛，何得判同上聖也。然四地七地已來菩薩，論其功用，不可思議。豈藉一日七日之善，華臺授手，迎接往生也？此即返對上上竟。

次對上中者。諸師云：是初地四地已來菩薩者。何故《觀經》云：「不必受持大乘。」云何名不必？或讀不讀，故名不必。但言善解，未論其行。又言「深信因果，不謗大乘。以此善根迴願往生。命欲終時，阿彌陀佛及與化佛菩薩大眾一時授手，即生彼國。」以此文證，亦是佛去世後，大乘凡夫，行業稍弱，致使終時迎候有異。然初地四地已來菩薩，論其功用，如《華嚴經》說，乃是不可思議。豈藉韋提致請，方得往生也？返對上中竟。

次對上下者。諸師云：是種性以上至初地已來菩薩者。何故《觀經》云：「亦信因果。」云何亦信？或信不信，故名為亦。又言「不謗大乘，但發無上道心。唯此一句以為正業，更無餘善。迴斯一行，求願往生。命欲終時，阿彌陀佛及與化佛菩薩大眾一時授手，即得往生。」以斯文證，唯是佛去世後，一切發大乘心眾生，行業不強，致使去時迎候有異。若論此位中菩薩力勢，十方淨土隨意往生。豈藉韋提為其請佛，勸生西方極樂國也？返對上下竟。

即此三品去時有異。云何異？上上去時，佛與無數化佛一時授手。上中去時，佛與千化佛一時授手。上下去時，佛與五百化佛一時授手。直是業有強弱，致使有斯差別耳！

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, refuting it again by citing the nine grades' own text against it. These masters say that the person born in the upper-upper grade is a bodhisattva from the fourth ground through the seventh ground. Why, then, does the Contemplation Sūtra say: "Three kinds of beings will attain rebirth there. What are the three? First, those able only to keep the precepts and cultivate loving-kindness; second, those unable to keep the precepts and cultivate loving-kindness, but able only to recite the Great Vehicle scriptures; and third, those unable to keep the precepts or recite the sūtras, but able only to be mindful of the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha. Each of these three people, with single-minded, exacting resolve devoted to their own particular karma, whether for a single day and night or continuing unbroken for seven days and seven nights, dedicates whatever karma they have thus made, seeking and vowing to be reborn there. When their life comes to its end, Amitābha Buddha, together with transformation-buddhas, bodhisattvas, and the great assembly, emit light and extend their hands to receive them, and, as swiftly as a finger-snap, they are reborn in that land." Taking this text as proof, this is plainly, after the Buddha's own passing, an ordinary being of the Great Vehicle, of the very highest grade of goodness, whose days of practice, though few, were marked by fierce, intense effort — how could this possibly be judged the same as a sage of the grounds above? For a bodhisattva from the fourth ground through the seventh ground, considering his own accomplished function, is inconceivable — how could he possibly need to rely on a single day's, or seven days', worth of goodness, to be received by a hand extended from a lotus throne, welcomed into rebirth? This concludes the refutation, by the text itself, of the upper-upper grade.

Next, addressing the upper-middle grade. These masters say this is a bodhisattva from the first ground through the fourth ground. Why, then, does the Contemplation Sūtra say: "It is not necessary to have upheld the Great Vehicle"? What does "not necessary" mean here? Whether he has read it or not — this is why it says "not necessary." It says only that he understands it well, without ever discussing his own actual practice. It further says: "He deeply believes in cause and effect, and does not slander the Great Vehicle. Dedicating this wholesome root, he vows to be reborn there. When his life comes to its end, Amitābha Buddha, together with transformation-buddhas, bodhisattvas, and the great assembly, extend their hands to him all at once, and he is reborn in that land." Taking this text as proof, this, too, is, after the Buddha's own passing, an ordinary being of the Great Vehicle, whose practice and karma are somewhat weaker, and so his welcome, at the moment of death, differs accordingly. Yet a bodhisattva from the first ground through the fourth ground, considering his own accomplished function, is, as the Avataṃsaka Sūtra says, entirely inconceivable — how could he possibly need to rely on Vaidehī's own request in order to attain rebirth at all? This concludes the refutation of the upper-middle grade.

Next, addressing the upper-lower grade. These masters say this is a bodhisattva from the stage of fixed lineage up through the first ground. Why, then, does the Contemplation Sūtra say: "He also believes in cause and effect"? What does "also" mean here? Whether he believes it or not — this is why it says "also." It further says: "He does not slander the Great Vehicle, but only gives rise to the unsurpassed aspiration for the path. Taking this single line alone as his true practice, with no further goodness besides — dedicating this one practice, he vows to be reborn there. When his life comes to its end, Amitābha Buddha, together with transformation-buddhas, bodhisattvas, and the great assembly, extend their hands to him all at once, and he attains rebirth." Taking this text as proof, this is nothing but, after the Buddha's own passing, any being at all who has given rise to the Great Vehicle aspiration, whose practice and karma are not yet strong, and so his welcome, at the moment of departure, differs accordingly. Were one to consider the actual power and strength of a bodhisattva at this rank, he could be reborn at will in any pure land of the ten directions — how, then, could he possibly need Vaidehī to request the Buddha on his behalf, urging him toward rebirth in the western land of Ultimate Bliss? This concludes the refutation of the upper-lower grade.

These same three grades differ, then, at the very moment of their departure. How do they differ? At the upper-upper's departure, the Buddha, together with countless transformation-buddhas, extends his hand all at once. At the upper-middle's departure, the Buddha, together with a thousand transformation-buddhas, extends his hand all at once. At the upper-lower's departure, the Buddha, together with five hundred transformation-buddhas, extends his hand all at once. This is simply because their karma differs in strength, which is exactly what brings about this difference.

The Middle Three Grades

Chinese Source

次對中輩三人者。諸師云：中上是小乘三果者。何故《觀經》云：「若有眾生受持五戒八戒，修行諸戒不造五逆，無眾過患。命欲終時，阿彌陀佛與比丘聖眾放光說法，來現其前，此人見已即得往生。」以此文證，亦是佛去世後，持小乘戒凡夫，何小聖也？

中中者，諸師云：見道已前內凡者。何故《觀經》云：「受持一日一夜戒，迴願往生。命欲終時，見佛即得往生。」以此文證，豈得言是內凡人也？但是佛去世後，無善凡夫，命延日夜，逢遇小緣，授其小戒，迴願往生。以佛願力，即得生也。若論小聖，去亦無妨。但此《觀經》，佛為凡說，不干聖也。

中下者，諸師云：小乘內凡已前，世俗凡夫唯修世福求出離者。何故《觀經》云：「若有眾生，孝養父母，行世仁慈。命欲終時，遇善知識，為說彼佛國土樂事，四十八願等。此人聞已，即生彼國。」以此文證，但是不遇佛法之人，雖行孝養，亦未有心希求出離。直是臨終遇善，勸令往生。此人因勸迴心，即得往生。又此人在世自然行孝，亦不為出離故行孝道也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, addressing the three people of the middle rank. These masters say the middle-upper is a person of the third fruit of the lesser vehicle. Why, then, does the Contemplation Sūtra say: "If there is a sentient being who upholds the five precepts or the eight precepts, cultivating every precept, committing none of the five heinous offenses, free of every fault — when his life comes to its end, Amitābha Buddha, together with an assembly of bhikṣu-sages, emits light and expounds the Dharma, appearing before him, and this person, upon seeing this, attains rebirth at once"? Taking this text as proof, this, too, is, after the Buddha's own passing, an ordinary being who upholds the precepts of the lesser vehicle — how could this be any kind of lesser-vehicle sage at all?

As for the middle-middle: these masters say this is an "inner ordinary being," one who has not yet seen the path directly. Why, then, does the Contemplation Sūtra say: "Upholding the precepts for a single day and night, and dedicating this, vowing to be reborn there — when his life comes to its end, he beholds the Buddha and attains rebirth at once"? Taking this text as proof, how could this possibly be called an inner ordinary being at all? This is nothing but, after the Buddha's own passing, an ordinary being without any particular goodness, whose life extends only a day or a night further, who happens to encounter some minor condition, is given some minor precept, and dedicates this, vowing to be reborn there — and, through the power of the Buddha's own vow, attains rebirth at once. Were one to speak of a lesser-vehicle sage, there would certainly be no harm in his going there as well — but this Contemplation Sūtra is spoken by the Buddha for the sake of ordinary beings; it has nothing to do with sages at all.

As for the middle-lower: these masters say this is an ordinary, worldly being, prior even to being an "inner ordinary being" of the lesser vehicle, cultivating only worldly blessings while seeking to escape the world. Why, then, does the Contemplation Sūtra say: "If there is a sentient being who is filial and devoted to his parents, and carries out worldly benevolence and kindness — when his life comes to its end, he encounters a good teacher, who explains for him the blissful matters of that buddha's land, and the forty-eight vows, and the rest; and this person, upon hearing this, is reborn at once in that land"? Taking this text as proof, this is simply a person who has never once encountered the Buddha-dharma at all — though he carries out filial devotion, he has never yet had any mind that seeks to escape the world at all. It is only that, at the very moment of death, he happens to encounter goodness, and is urged toward rebirth — and this person, on account of this urging, turns his mind around, and attains rebirth at once. And further, such a person, in his own life, carries out filial devotion entirely naturally, not for the sake of seeking escape from the world at all.

The Lower Three Grades, and Shandao's Conclusion

Chinese Source

次對下輩三人者。諸師云：此等之人乃是大乘始學凡夫，隨過輕重分為三品，未有道位，難辨階降者。將謂不然。何者？此三品人，無有佛法世俗二種善根，唯知作惡。何以得知？如下上文說，但不作五逆謗法，自餘諸惡悉皆具造，無有慚愧。乃至一念命欲終時，遇善知識，為說大乘，教令稱佛一聲。爾時阿彌陀佛，即遣化佛菩薩來迎此人，即得往生。但如此惡人，觸目皆是。若遇善緣，即得往生。若不遇善，定入三塗，未可出也。

下中者，此人先受佛戒，受已不持即便毀破。又偷常住僧物、現前僧物，不淨說法，乃至無有一念慚愧之心。命欲終時，地獄猛火一時俱至，現在其前。當見火時，即遇善知識，為說彼佛國土功德，勸令往生。此人聞已，即便見佛，隨化往生。初不遇善，獄火來迎。後逢善故，化佛來迎。斯乃皆是彌陀願力故也。

下下者，此等眾生，作不善業，五逆十惡，具諸不善。此人以惡業故，定墮地獄，多劫無窮。命欲終時，遇善知識，教稱阿彌陀佛勸令往生。此人依教稱佛，乘念即生。此人若不遇善，必定下沈。由終遇善，七寶來迎。

又看此《觀經》定善，及三輩上下文意，總是佛去世後，五濁凡夫，但以遇緣有異，致令九品差別。何者？上品三人，是遇大凡夫。中品三人，是遇小凡夫。下品三人，是遇惡凡夫。以惡業故，臨終藉善，乘佛願力，乃得往生。到彼華開，方始發心。何得言是始學大乘人也。若作此見，自失誤他，為害茲甚。今以一一出文顯證。欲使今時善惡凡夫同沾九品，生信無疑，乘佛願力，悉得生也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, addressing the three people of the lower rank. These masters say that such people are ordinary beings only just beginning to study the Great Vehicle, divided into three grades according to the weight of their transgressions, not yet having attained any stage of the path at all, and difficult to distinguish by rank. I take this to be mistaken as well. Why is this so? These three grades of people possess neither of the two kinds of wholesome root, whether of the Buddha-dharma or of worldly virtue — they know only how to commit evil. How is this known? As the text on the upper of these three grades itself says: they simply have not committed any of the five heinous offenses or slandered the Dharma — every other kind of evil, they have committed in full, without the slightest sense of shame. Then, at the very moment their life is about to end, in a single thought, they encounter a good teacher, who expounds the Great Vehicle for them, and teaches them to call upon the Buddha's name even once. At that moment, Amitābha Buddha at once sends transformation-buddhas and bodhisattvas to welcome this person, and he attains rebirth at once. But people as evil as this meet the eye everywhere one looks. Should they encounter a wholesome condition, they attain rebirth at once; should they fail to encounter one, they will certainly fall into the three evil destinies, with no escape at all.

As for the lower-middle: this person has previously received the Buddhist precepts, but, having received them, fails to keep them, and breaks them outright. He further steals property belonging to the permanent assembly of monks, or property set out before them, teaches the Dharma with impure motives, and carries not even a single moment's sense of shame. When his life comes to its end, the fierce fires of hell arrive all at once, appearing before him. At the very moment he sees these flames, he happens to encounter a good teacher, who explains for him the merit of that buddha's land, and urges him toward rebirth — and this person, upon hearing this, at once beholds the Buddha, and, following this transformation, attains rebirth. At first, having not yet encountered goodness, the flames of hell come to greet him; but then, having finally encountered goodness, transformation-buddhas come to greet him instead. All of this, without exception, comes about through the power of Amitābha's own vow.

As for the lowest of the lower, the lower-lower: such beings commit unwholesome karma, the five heinous offenses and the ten evils, every kind of unwholesome act in full. On account of this evil karma, such a person is certain to fall into hell, for kalpas without end. When his life comes to its end, he encounters a good teacher, who teaches him to call upon the name of Amitābha Buddha, and urges him toward rebirth. This person, following this teaching, calls upon the Buddha's name, and, riding upon this very thought, is reborn at once. Had this person not encountered goodness, he would certainly have sunk down into hell — but because, at the very end, he encountered goodness, the seven treasures come to welcome him instead.

Looking again at this sūtra's own contemplative goodness, and at the text and meaning of these three ranks, upper, middle, and lower, all of it, taken together, describes ordinary beings of the five turbidities, living after the Buddha's own passing — differing only in what condition they happen to encounter, and it is exactly this that brings about the differences among the nine grades. How so? The three people of the upper grade are ordinary beings who happen to encounter the Great Vehicle. The three people of the middle grade are ordinary beings who happen to encounter the lesser vehicle. The three people of the lower grade are ordinary beings who happen to encounter evil. It is precisely because of their evil karma that, at the very end of life, relying on goodness and riding upon the power of the Buddha's own vow, they attain rebirth at all — and it is only upon arriving there, once their lotus opens, that they first give rise to the aspiration for enlightenment at all. How, then, could one possibly call these people already "beginning students of the Great Vehicle"? Should one hold to this view, one would lead oneself astray and mislead others as well — a harm exceedingly grave indeed. I have now, one by one, brought forward the text itself as clear proof, precisely so that the good and evil ordinary beings of the present age alike may equally share in these nine grades, giving rise to faith without any doubt, and, riding upon the power of the Buddha's own vow, all without exception attain rebirth there.

A note on this refutation

The rhetorical strategy across this entire part is consistent and cumulative: for each grade, Shandao does not offer a competing interpretation so much as ask the reader to notice what the sūtra's own words already say, plainly, once the assumption of prior sagehood is set aside. A person "unable to keep the precepts, unable to recite the sūtras" is not, on any natural reading, a bodhisattva of the fourth ground; a monk who "steals property belonging to the assembly" and feels "not even a single moment's sense of shame" is not an inner ordinary being close to stream-entry. The argument gathers real force in its final turn: the nine grades, Shandao proposes, are not differentiated by spiritual attainment at all, but purely by what condition a dying, ordinary person happens to encounter — a good teacher who explains the Great Vehicle, a lesser teaching, or nothing but the flames of hell arriving first, before goodness ever reaches him. Attainment, on this reading, is not what qualifies someone for rebirth; it is not even present yet at the moment of departure. It begins only "once their lotus opens" in that land — meaning the nine grades describe nine different kinds of ordinary crisis, met by nine different last-minute rescues, not nine ranks of prior spiritual accomplishment.

Part 4 Complete

This closes Part 4: the nine grades worked through one by one, each shown to describe an ordinary being meeting a particular condition at the moment of death, not a sage already advanced along the path.
