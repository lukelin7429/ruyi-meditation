Closing the Highest Grade

This grade closes with its own most vivid scene: Amitābha arriving in person, an entire retinue at his side, a golden platform carried before them, light reaching the dying practitioner directly. What follows arrives with a speed nothing earlier in this fascicle has matched — no waiting lotus, no gradual opening, but immediate entry into the assembly itself.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 4 of 4: 散善義 (Meaning of the Dispersed Good)

Dedication, and a Warning Against Possessing Nothing at All

Chinese Source

七、從迴向發願已下，正明各各迴前所修之業，向所求處。八、從具此功德已下，正明修行時節延促。上盡一形，下至一日、一時、一念等，或從一念十念至一時一日一形。大意者，一發心已後誓畢此生，無有退轉，唯以淨土為期。又言具此功德者，或一人具上二，或一人具下二，或一人三種盡具。或有人三種無分者，名作著人皮畜生，非名人也。又不問具三不具三，迴盡得往生。應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, from "dedicating this and vowing" onward, this makes plain each and every practitioner dedicating the practice cultivated above toward the very destination sought.

Eighth, from "possessing this merit" onward, this makes plain the differing duration, extended or brief, of the time spent in this cultivation — at the very most, across an entire lifetime; at the very least, for a single day, a single hour, or even a single thought-moment; or perhaps from a single thought or ten thoughts extending to a single hour, a single day, or an entire lifetime. The general intent is this: once the mind is given rise to, one vows to carry this through to the end of this present life, without ever once retreating, taking the pure land alone as one's own destination.

Further, as for "possessing this merit": it may be that a single person possesses the first two of these three practices; or a single person possesses the latter two; or a single person possesses all three in full. Should there be a person who possesses none of these three at all, such a person is called an animal wearing human skin — not truly called a human being at all. Further, whether one possesses all three or not, dedicating whatever one has shall bring about rebirth all the same. This should be understood.

Amitābha Arrives in Person, With a Golden Platform

Chinese Source

九、從生彼國時下，至往生彼國已來，正明臨命終時聖來迎接不同去時遲疾。即有其十一。一明標定所歸之國。二明重顯其行指出決定精勤者，亦是校量功德強弱。三明彌陀化主身自來赴。四明觀音已下，更顯無數大眾等皆從彌陀來迎行者。五明寶宮隨眾。六明重觀音勢至共執金臺至行者前。七明彌陀放光照行者之身。八明佛既舒光照及，即與化佛等同時接手。九明既接昇臺，觀音等同聲讚勸行者之心。十明自見乘臺從佛。十一正明去時遲疾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, from "at the moment of being reborn in that land" down through "attains rebirth in that very land," this makes plain the differing ways in which the sages come to welcome the practitioner at the moment life is about to end, and the differing speed, swift or slow, with which they then depart. There are eleven points within it. First, this makes plain the marking out and fixing of the very land one is destined for. Second, this makes plain the renewed revealing of this practice, pointing out one who is resolute and diligent — this too serves to measure the relative strength of this merit. Third, this makes plain Amitābha, the teaching-lord of transformation, arriving there in person. Fourth, this makes plain Avalokiteśvara and the rest, further revealing that countless multitudes all come together with Amitābha to welcome the practitioner. Fifth, this makes plain a jeweled palace accompanying this very assembly. Sixth, this makes plain, once more, Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta together holding a golden platform, arriving before the practitioner. Seventh, this makes plain Amitābha emitting light to illuminate the practitioner's own body. Eighth, this makes plain that, once the Buddha's own light has extended and reached them, he, together with the transformation-buddhas, extends his own hand to receive them at the very same moment. Ninth, this makes plain that, once received and raised onto this platform, Avalokiteśvara and the others, with a single voice, praise and encourage the practitioner's own mind. Tenth, this makes plain the practitioner beholding themselves riding upon this platform, following the Buddha. Eleventh, this makes plain the differing speed, swift or slow, with which they then depart.

No Waiting Lotus, and the Highest Grade Complete

Chinese Source

十、從生彼國已下，正明金臺到彼，更無華合之障。十一、從見佛色身下，至陀羅尼門已來，正明金臺到後得益不同。即有其三。一者初聞妙法，即悟無生。二者須臾歷事，次第授記。三者本國他方，更證聞持二益。十二、從是名已下，總結。上來雖有十二句不同，廣解上品上生義竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, from "reborn in that land" onward, this makes plain that this golden platform, upon reaching that land, encounters no obstruction whatsoever of a still-closed lotus flower.

Eleventh, from "beholding the Buddha's own bodily form" down through "the gate of dhāraṇī," this makes plain the differing benefits gained once this golden platform has arrived. There are three points within it. First, upon first hearing the wondrous Dharma, one awakens at once to the acceptance of non-arising. Second, in but a brief moment, passing through a sequence of events, one receives, in turn, a prediction of future buddhahood. Third, whether in that very land or in other realms, one further attains the twofold benefit of hearing and retaining the Dharma.

Twelfth, from "this, then, is" onward, this gathers together the general conclusion. Though the above has unfolded across twelve distinct points, this concludes the full explanation of the meaning of the highest rebirth of the upper rank.

A note on this immediacy

The explicit denial of "any obstruction whatsoever of a still-closed lotus flower" gains its full force only against everything already established about the alternative: the water contemplation's own verse describing beings waiting inside unopened lotuses "as though still within the womb," and the ground contemplation's own account of doubt requiring a bodhisattva's intervention to resolve. This highest grade, built from three genuinely real minds sustained without wavering, bypasses that entire waiting period — the lotus simply does not close in the first place. This closes the very first, and highest, of the nine grades of rebirth this sūtra describes.
