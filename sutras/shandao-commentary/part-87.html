The Second Grade: Understanding Emptiness

The second of the nine grades belongs to someone slightly different from the first: not necessarily a reader of scripture, but someone whose own mind remains settled and untroubled before the deepest claims of Mahāyāna philosophy — that ordinary and sage, this defiled world and the unconditioned, are ultimately not two separate things at all.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 4 of 4: 散善義 (Meaning of the Dispersed Good)

Not Two Different Things: An Undisturbed Understanding of Emptiness

Chinese Source

次就上品中生位中，亦先舉，次辨，後結。即有其八。一、從上品中生者已下，總舉位名，即是大乘次善凡夫人也。二、從不必受持下至生彼國已來，正明第六第七第八門中迴所修業，定指西方。即有其四。一明受法不定，或得讀誦不得讀誦。二明善解大乘空義，或聽聞諸法一切皆空，生死無為亦空，凡聖明闇亦空，世間六道、出世間三賢十聖等，若望其體性畢竟不二，雖聞此說其心坦然，不生疑滯也。三明深信世出世苦樂二種因果，此等因果及諸道理不生疑謗。若生疑謗即不成福行，世間果報尚不可得，何況得生淨土？此即合第三福第二第三句也。四明迴前所業標指所歸。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, within the position of the middle rebirth of the upper rank, the text likewise first raises the matter, next distinguishes it in detail, and finally concludes it. There are eight points in all.

First, from "the middle rebirth of the upper rank" onward, this generally names this grade — this is itself the ordinary being of the next degree of goodness who has studied the Mahāyāna.

Second, from "not necessarily upholding and reciting" down through "attains rebirth in that land," this makes plain, within the sixth, seventh, and eighth gates, the dedication of the practice cultivated, fixed and pointed directly toward the West. There are four points within it. First, this makes plain that the manner of receiving this teaching is not fixed — one may either succeed in reading and reciting the sūtras, or not. Second, this makes plain skillfully understanding the meaning of Mahāyāna emptiness — hearing, perhaps, that every phenomenon whatsoever is empty, that birth-and-death and the unconditioned alike are empty, that the ordinary and the sage, the bright and the dark, are alike empty, that this mundane world's six destinies and the transcendent world's three worthy stages and ten sagely grounds, when measured against their own underlying nature, are ultimately not two different things at all — and though one hears this very teaching, one's own mind remains at ease, giving rise to no doubt or hesitation whatsoever. Third, this makes plain deep faith in the twofold cause and effect of suffering and happiness, mundane and transcendent alike, giving rise to no doubt or slander regarding these very causes and effects and their underlying principles. Should one give rise to doubt or slander, one's own practice of merit could not even be accomplished — and if even a mundane retribution could not thereby be obtained, how much less could rebirth in the pure land be attained? This corresponds to the second and third clauses of the third blessing described earlier. Fourth, this makes plain dedicating the practice cultivated above, marking out and pointing toward the very destination sought.

A note on this capacity

This grade's own defining feature is worth contrasting directly with the first: where the highest grade specified sūtra recitation as one of its three suitable practices, this second grade explicitly says "the manner of receiving this teaching is not fixed — one may either succeed in reading and reciting the sūtras, or not." What replaces textual study here is something closer to a settled philosophical composure — hearing the most radical claims of Mahāyāna emptiness doctrine without the mind growing "doubtful or hesitant." This is intellectual equanimity treated as its own form of spiritual capacity, distinct from and not reducible to devotional practice or scriptural learning.

Amitābha Arrives Upon a Platform of Purple Gold

Chinese Source

三、從行此行者下，至迎接汝已來，正明彌陀與諸聖眾持臺來應。即有其五。一明行者命延不久。二明彌陀與眾自來。三明侍者持臺至行者前。四明佛與聖眾同聲讚歎述本所修之業。五明佛恐行者懷疑故言我來迎汝。四、從與千化佛下，至七寶池中已來，正明第九門中眾聖授手去時遲疾。即有其五。一明彌陀與千化佛同時授手。二明行者既蒙授手，即自見身已坐紫金之臺。三明既自見坐臺，合掌仰讚彌陀等眾。四明正去時遲疾。五明到彼止住寶池之內。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "one who practices this practice" down through "come to welcome you," this makes plain Amitābha and the assembly of sages holding this platform and coming in response. There are five points within it. First, this makes plain that the practitioner's own life is not long to continue. Second, this makes plain Amitābha and the assembly coming there in person. Third, this makes plain attendants holding the platform, arriving before the practitioner. Fourth, this makes plain the Buddha and the assembly of sages, with a single voice, praising and recounting the very practice this being has cultivated. Fifth, this makes plain the Buddha, fearing that the practitioner might harbor doubt, therefore saying: "I have come to welcome you."

Fourth, from "together with a thousand transformation-buddhas" down through "within the pond of seven jewels," this makes plain, within the ninth gate, the assembly of sages extending their own hands, and the differing speed, swift or slow, with which they then depart. There are five points within it. First, this makes plain Amitābha, together with a thousand transformation-buddhas, extending his own hand at the very same moment. Second, this makes plain that the practitioner, having received this extended hand, beholds themselves already seated upon a platform of purple gold. Third, this makes plain that, having beheld themselves seated upon this platform, they join their own palms and look up in praise of Amitābha and his assembly. Fourth, this makes plain the precise speed, swift or slow, with which they then depart. Fifth, this makes plain their arrival there, coming to rest within the very pond of seven jewels.

A note on this platform

The shift from a golden vajra platform in the highest grade to a platform of purple gold here is not incidental description — it is a precise measure of relative attainment, made explicit in the very next part, where Shandao states outright that the strength of one's own practice determines which platform one receives. Even within a single overall rank, the sūtra's own imagery tracks fine gradations of practice with a consistency this commentary has maintained since its account of calibrated benefit all the way back in Fascicle 2.
