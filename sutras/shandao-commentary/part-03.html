Against "Other Masters": The Nine Grades Are for Ordinary Beings

Shandao's sixth gate, "reconciling apparent contradictions among sūtras and treatises," opens with the single most consequential argument in this entire commentary — and, arguably, in the whole history of Chinese Pure Land thought. He first lays out, fully and fairly, a reading he attributes to unnamed "other Dharma-masters": that the nine grades of rebirth in the Contemplation Sūtra describe bodhisattvas and lesser-vehicle sages already advanced along the path, not ordinary beings at all. He then refutes it, point by point, by reasoning alone — before turning, in the next part, to the sūtra's own text.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 1 of 4: 玄義分 (Profound Meaning)

Gate Six, Opening: Six Matters to Reconcile

Chinese Source

六、和會經論相違，廣施問答，釋去疑情者。就此門中，即有其六：一、先就諸法師解九品之義，二、即以道理來破之，三、重舉九品返對破之，四、出文來證定為凡夫不為聖人，五、會通別時之意，六、會通二乘種不生之義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, reconciling apparent contradictions among sūtras and treatises, setting out questions and answers at length to dispel doubt. Within this gate there are six matters: first, presenting how various Dharma-masters interpret the meaning of the nine grades; second, refuting this by reasoning; third, refuting it again by citing the nine grades' own text against it; fourth, bringing forward textual proof that this is fixed as being for ordinary beings, not for sages; fifth, reconciling the meaning of "deferred time"; and sixth, reconciling the meaning of "the seed of the two vehicles does not arise there."

1. How Other Masters Read the Nine Grades

Chinese Source

初言諸師解者。先舉上輩三人。「言上上者，是四地至七地已來菩薩。何故得知？由到彼即得無生忍故。上中者，是初地至四地已來菩薩。何故得知？由到彼經一小劫得無生忍故。上下者，是種性以上至初地已來菩薩。何故得知？由到彼經三小劫始入初地故。此三品人皆是大乘聖人生位。」次舉中輩三人者。諸師云：「中上是三果人。何以得知？由到彼即得羅漢故。中中者是內凡。何以得知？由到彼得須陀洹故。中下者是世善凡夫，厭苦求生。何以得知？由到彼經一小劫得羅漢果故。此之三品唯是小乘聖人等也。下輩三人者，是大乘始學凡夫，隨過輕重分為三品，共同一位。求願往生者未必然也，可知。」

Ru-Yi Translation

First, presenting how various Dharma-masters interpret this. They begin with the three people of the upper rank. "The upper-upper is a bodhisattva from the fourth ground up through the seventh ground. How is this known? Because, upon arriving there, he attains at once the acceptance of non-arising. The upper-middle is a bodhisattva from the first ground up through the fourth ground. How is this known? Because, upon arriving there, he attains the acceptance of non-arising only after one small kalpa. The upper-lower is a bodhisattva from the stage of the fixed lineage up through the first ground. How is this known? Because, upon arriving there, he does not enter the first ground until three small kalpas have passed. These three grades of people are all bodhisattvas of the Great Vehicle, already at a sagely stage of rebirth." Next, they present the three people of the middle rank. These masters say: "The middle-upper is a person of the third fruit. How is this known? Because, upon arriving there, he attains arhatship at once. The middle-middle is an inner ordinary being, close to the path. How is this known? Because, upon arriving there, he attains stream-entry. The middle-lower is an ordinary being of worldly goodness, wearied by suffering and seeking rebirth. How is this known? Because, upon arriving there, he attains the fruit of arhatship only after one small kalpa. These three grades belong entirely to the sages of the lesser vehicle." As for the three people of the lower rank: these are said to be ordinary beings only just beginning to study the Great Vehicle, divided into three grades according to the weight of their transgressions, all sharing this same single rank. But whether those who seek and vow to be reborn there are actually like this at all is not necessarily so, as should be recognized.

A note on what is being refuted

Shandao presents this reading with unusual fairness before dismantling it — no straw man, but a coherent, internally consistent scheme mapping all nine grades of the sūtra's own rebirth-typology onto the standard Mahāyāna path-structure: bodhisattva grounds for the upper three, śrāvaka fruits for the middle three, and merely "beginning" Mahāyāna practitioners for the lower three. The stakes of this reading are exactly what make Shandao's rejection of it consequential: if the nine grades describe beings already this advanced, then the Contemplation Sūtra offers nothing to an ordinary, flawed, still-struggling practitioner at all — it becomes a description of what already-realized beings experience upon arrival, not a path open to anyone reading it. Everything Shandao argues from this point forward, across the rest of this commentary, depends on this reading being wrong.

2. Refuting This by Reasoning

Chinese Source

第二、即以道理來破者。上言初地至七地已來菩薩者。如《華嚴經》說：「初地已上七地已來，即是法性生身、變易生身。」斯等曾無分段之苦。論其功用，已經二大阿僧祇劫，雙修福智人法兩空。並是不可思議，神通自在轉變無方。身居報土，常聞報佛說法。悲化十方，須臾遍滿，更憂何事，乃藉韋提為其請佛，求生安樂國也？以斯文證，諸師所說，豈非錯也。答上二竟。上下者。上言從種性至初地已來者，未必然也。如經說：「此等菩薩名為不退。身居生死，不為生死所染。如鵝鴨在水，水不能濕。」如《大品經》說：「此位中菩薩，由得二種真善知識守護故不退。何者？一是十方諸佛。二是十方諸大菩薩。常以三業外加於諸善法無有退失，故名不退位也。」此等菩薩，亦能八相成道，教化眾生。論其功行，已經一大阿僧祇劫，雙修福智等。既有斯勝德，更憂何事，乃藉韋提請求生也？以斯文證。故知諸師所判，還成錯也。此責上輩竟。

次責中輩三人者。諸師云：中上是三果者。然此等之人，三塗永絕，四趣不生。現在雖造罪業，必定不招來報。如佛說言：「此四果人，與我同坐解脫床。」既有斯功力，更復何憂，乃藉韋提請求生路？然諸佛大悲於苦者，心偏愍念常沒眾生，是以勸歸淨土。亦如溺水之人，急須偏救。岸上之者，何用濟為？以斯文證，故知諸師所判，義同前錯也。以下可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, refuting this by reasoning. Above, it was said that the upper-upper through the upper-middle are bodhisattvas from the first ground through the seventh ground. As the Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "From the first ground up through the seventh ground, these are already bodies born of the Dharma-nature, bodies of transformative rebirth." Such beings have never once known the suffering of fragmentary birth and death at all. Considering their own accomplished function, they have already passed through two great asaṃkhyeya kalpas, cultivating merit and wisdom together, emptied of both self and phenomena. All of this is inconceivable — their spiritual powers freely transforming without any fixed limit. Their bodies dwell in a reward-land, constantly hearing a reward-body buddha expound the Dharma; their compassionate transformation reaches the ten directions, filling them in an instant. What further cause for worry could they possibly have, that they should need to rely on Vaidehī to request the Buddha on their behalf, seeking rebirth in the land of Peace and Bliss? Taking this text as proof, is it not plain that what these masters say is simply mistaken? This concludes the answer to the first two grades. As for the upper-lower: above, it was said this is a bodhisattva from the stage of fixed lineage up through the first ground — but this, too, is not necessarily so. As the sūtra says: "Such bodhisattvas are called non-retrogressing. Their bodies dwell within birth and death, and yet are not stained by birth and death — like a goose or a duck upon the water, which the water cannot wet." As the Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: "A bodhisattva at this stage, through the protection of two kinds of true good friends, does not retrogress. What are they? First, the buddhas of the ten directions; second, the great bodhisattvas of the ten directions — constantly adding, through their own three karmic activities from without, to every wholesome dharma, so that nothing is ever lost. This is why it is called the stage of non-retrogression." Such bodhisattvas are likewise able to accomplish the eight aspects of a buddha's own life and teach and transform sentient beings. Considering their own accomplished practice, they have already passed through one great asaṃkhyeya kalpa, cultivating merit and wisdom together, and more. Given that they already possess such excellent virtue, what further cause for worry could they have, that they should need to request rebirth through Vaidehī? Taking this text as proof, one thus knows that what these masters have judged is, once again, mistaken. This concludes the reproach of the upper rank.

Next, reproaching the three people of the middle rank. These masters say the middle-upper is a person of the third fruit. Yet such a person has already forever cut off the three evil destinies, and is no longer reborn in any of the four unfortunate paths at all. Even should he, in the present, commit some offense, it will certainly not bring about any future retribution. As the Buddha himself says: "This person of the fourth fruit sits together with me upon the very same seat of liberation." Given that he already possesses this kind of power, what further worry could remain, that he should need to request the path of rebirth through Vaidehī? Yet the buddhas, in their great compassion, hold a special pity in their hearts precisely for those who suffer, those constantly sunk in the depths — and it is for their sake that they urge a return to the Pure Land. It is just like a person drowning in water, who urgently needs to be rescued above all others — what use would it be to save one already standing safely on the shore? Taking this text as proof, one thus knows that what these masters have judged carries the very same error as before. What follows may be understood in the same way.

A note on this refutation

Shandao's argument here is not textual but psychological, run the same way against every grade in turn: a being already this advanced would have no conceivable motive to need Vaidehī's request, or the sūtra's own teaching, at all. A bodhisattva of the seventh ground already dwells in a reward-land hearing a reward-buddha teach directly; a stream-enterer has already forever escaped the three evil destinies. If the sūtra's promise were addressed to beings like these, it would be solving a problem none of them actually has. The drowning-man image that closes this section — "what use would it be to save one already standing safely on the shore?" — states Shandao's real objection in a single line: a teaching's own urgency is evidence about who it was written for. A rescue this dramatic, offered this insistently, is aimed at people who are actually drowning, not people who long ago reached dry land.

Part 3 Complete

This closes Part 3: the reading Shandao rejects, laid out in full, and the first wave of his refutation — that beings this advanced along the path would have no plausible reason to need this sūtra's teaching at all. Part 4 turns to his second wave of attack: citing the nine grades' own text, one by one, against the very reading that claims to describe them.
