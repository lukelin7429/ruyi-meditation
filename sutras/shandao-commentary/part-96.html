A Single Name, Five Million Kalpas

At the very edge of death, a good friend first recites the scriptures, then abandons them for a single name — and the difference in what each removes becomes this commentary's sharpest statement yet of why the name alone, and not the vast wealth of scripture behind it, is the practice this teaching ultimately urges.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 4 of 4: 散善義 (Meaning of the Dispersed Good)

Why a Single Name Outweighs Twelve Divisions of Scripture

Chinese Source

四、從命欲終時下，至生死之罪已來，正明造惡人等臨終遇善聞法。即有其六。一明命延不久。二明忽遇往生善知識。三明善人為讚眾經。四明已聞經功力除罪千劫。五明智者轉教稱念彌陀之號。六明以稱彌陀名故除罪五百萬劫。問曰：「何故聞經十二部但除罪千劫，稱佛一聲即除罪五百萬劫者，何意也？」答曰：「造罪之人障重，加以死苦來逼，善人雖說多經，食受之心浮散。由心散故，除罪稍輕。又佛名是一，即能攝散以住心，復教令正念稱名，由心重故，即能除罪多劫也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, from "when life is about to end" down through "the offense of birth and death," this makes plain that these beings who have committed evil, at the very end of life, happen to encounter a good friend and hear the Dharma. There are six points within it. First, this makes plain that this being's own life is not long to continue. Second, this makes plain suddenly encountering a good friend versed in the way of rebirth. Third, this makes plain this good person expounding and praising the manifold scriptures on this being's behalf. Fourth, this makes plain that, having heard these scriptures, the power of this merit removes a thousand kalpas of offense. Fifth, this makes plain that this wise one then further teaches this being to call upon Amitābha's own name. Sixth, this makes plain that, by calling upon Amitābha's name, five million kalpas of offense are removed.

A question is asked: "Why is it that hearing the twelve divisions of scripture removes only a thousand kalpas of offense, while calling the Buddha's name a single time removes at once five million kalpas of offense? What is the meaning of this?"

The answer: "This person who has committed offenses bears a heavy obstruction, and is further pressed upon by the suffering of death; though this good person expounds many scriptures, the mind that receives and takes them in is scattered and unsettled. Because the mind is scattered, the offense removed is comparatively slight. But the Buddha's name is a single thing, and it is able to gather in this scattered mind and cause it to settle; moreover, this being is taught to call this name with right mindfulness — and because the mind is thereby made weighty and concentrated, it is able to remove offense across many kalpas."

A note on this answer

Shandao's explanation turns entirely on the condition of the mind at the moment of death, not on any comparison of the intrinsic worth of scripture against the name. A dying mind, pressed by mortal suffering, cannot hold the vast content of "the twelve divisions of scripture" — it remains scattered, and what it takes in remains correspondingly shallow. A single name, by contrast, is simple enough to gather that same scattered mind into one point of concentrated attention. The name is not praised here as containing more merit than scripture in the abstract; it is praised as the one practice still available to a mind no longer capable of anything more elaborate.

A Transformation-Assembly Answers the Sound

Chinese Source

五、從爾時彼佛下，至生寶池中已來，正明第九門中終時化眾來迎去時遲疾。即有其六。一明行者正稱名時，彼彌陀即遣化眾應聲來現。二明化眾既已身現，即同讚行人。三明所聞化讚但述稱佛之功，我來迎汝，不論聞經之事。然望佛願意者，唯勸正念稱名往生義疾。不同雜散之業，如此經及諸部中處處廣歎，勸令稱名，將為要益也。應知。四明既蒙化眾告及即見光明遍室。五明既蒙光照報命尋終。六明乘華從佛生寶池中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, from "at that time, this buddha" down through "reborn within the jeweled pool," this makes plain, within the ninth gate, the transformation-assembly coming to welcome this being at the moment life ends, and the speed, swift or slow, with which they then depart. There are six points within it. First, this makes plain that, at the very moment this practitioner calls this name, Amitābha at once dispatches a transformation-assembly, appearing in answer to that very sound. Second, this makes plain that, once this transformation-assembly has appeared bodily, they together praise this practitioner. Third, this makes plain that the praise heard from this transformation-assembly speaks only of the merit of calling the Buddha's name — "I have come to welcome you" — and makes no mention at all of the matter of hearing the scriptures. Yet, looking to the Buddha's own intent in this vow, it is precisely this: to urge, above all, right mindfulness in calling this name, for the meaning and the swiftness of rebirth it brings are unlike any mixed and scattered practice. This is why this sūtra, and the other scriptures throughout, everywhere extol this at length, urging beings to call this name, taking it as the essential benefit. One should know this. Fourth, this makes plain that, having received word from this transformation-assembly, this being at once beholds light filling the entire room. Fifth, this makes plain that, having received this illumination, this being's own retribution-bound life then comes to its end. Sixth, this makes plain riding upon a lotus flower, following the Buddha, to be reborn within the jeweled pool.

A note on this term

"The karma of right settled practice" (正定之業), a phrase this commentary introduced earlier at Part 79, receives its clearest illustration here: the transformation-assembly's own praise, at the very threshold of death, names only the calling of the name, saying nothing of the scriptures just heard moments before. Shandao reads this silence as deliberate — evidence, drawn directly from the sūtra's own narrative choices, of what the Buddha's vow was actually built to reward.
