The Middle Rank Begins

The middle rank opens, as the upper rank did, with its own eleven-gate framework, before the fourth grade begins a genuine shift in kind: no longer a Mahāyāna practitioner at all, but an ordinary being of Hīnayāna capacity, upholding fasting precepts whose own power, Shandao states plainly, cannot dissolve the gravest offenses.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 4 of 4: 散善義 (Meaning of the Dispersed Good)

Eleven Gates, Applied Once More

Chinese Source

十五、就中輩觀行善文前總料簡，即為十一門。一者總明告命。二者正明辨定其位。三者正明總舉有緣之類。四者正明辨定三心以為正因。五者正明簡機堪與不堪。六者正明受法不同。七者正明修業時節延促有異。八者正明迴所修行，願生彌陀佛國。九者正明臨命終時，聖來迎接不同、去時遲疾。十者正明到彼華開遲疾不同。十一者正明華開已後得益有異。上來雖有十一門不同，廣料簡中輩三品竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifteenth, before turning to the text on the practice and goodness of the middle rank, I shall first offer a general examination. This comprises eleven gates in all. The first generally makes plain the issuing of this command. The second makes plain the distinguishing and fixing of each grade's own position. The third makes plain the general raising up of the class of beings who share this karmic affinity. The fourth makes plain the distinguishing and fixing of the three minds as the correct cause. The fifth makes plain the distinguishing of capacities, between those able to receive this teaching and those not. The sixth makes plain the differing ways in which this teaching is received. The seventh makes plain the differing duration, whether extended or brief, of the time spent cultivating this practice. The eighth makes plain the dedication of this cultivated practice toward the vow to be reborn in Amitābha's own buddha-land. The ninth makes plain the differing ways in which the sages come to welcome the practitioner at the moment life is about to end, and the differing speed, swift or slow, with which they then depart. The tenth makes plain the differing speed, swift or slow, with which the lotus flower opens once that land is reached. The eleventh makes plain the differing benefits gained once that flower has opened. Though the above has unfolded across these eleven distinct gates, this concludes the broad examination of the three grades of the middle rank.

A Different Capacity: Hīnayāna Fasting Precepts

Chinese Source

次就中品上生位中，亦先舉，次辨，後結。即有其八。一、從佛告阿難已下，總明告命。二從中品上生者，正明辨定其位，即是小乘根性上善凡夫人也。三、從若有眾生下，至無眾過患已來，正明第五第六門中受法不同，即有其四。一明簡機堪與不堪。二明受持小乘齋戒等。三明小戒力微不消五逆之罪。四明雖持小戒等不得有犯，設有餘愆，恒須改悔必令清淨，此即合上第二戒善之福也。然修戒時，或是終身，或一年一月一日一夜一時等，此時亦不定，大意皆畢命為期，不得毀犯也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, within the position of the highest rebirth of the middle rank, the text likewise first raises the matter, next distinguishes it in detail, and finally concludes it. There are eight points in all.

First, from "the Buddha told Ānanda" onward, this generally makes plain the issuing of this command.

Second, as for "the highest rebirth of the middle rank": this makes plain the distinguishing and fixing of this grade's own position — this is itself the ordinary being of superior goodness possessed of Hīnayāna capacity.

Third, from "should there be sentient beings" down through "free of every fault," this makes plain, within the fifth and sixth gates, the differing way in which this teaching is received. There are four points within it. First, this makes plain the distinguishing of capacity, between those able to receive this teaching and those not. Second, this makes plain receiving and upholding the Hīnayāna fasting precepts and the rest. Third, this makes plain that the power of these lesser precepts is slight, and cannot dissolve the offense of the five heinous crimes. Fourth, this makes plain that, though one upholds these lesser precepts and the rest, one must never transgress them — and should some remaining fault occur, one must constantly repent and reform, ensuring purity without fail. This corresponds to the root of goodness belonging to the precepts described in the second blessing above. As for the time spent cultivating these precepts, this may be for an entire lifetime, or for a single year, a single month, a single day and night, or a single hour — this duration, too, is not fixed, but the general intent is always to take this as one's own commitment until the end of this present life, never transgressing it.

A note on this shift

The transition from the upper rank to the middle rank marks a genuine change in the population this sūtra addresses, not merely a step down in intensity within the same kind of practitioner: the upper rank's three grades were all explicitly Mahāyāna practitioners; the middle rank's fourth grade is named directly as "Hīnayāna capacity." Shandao's own blunt assessment — that these lesser precepts are simply too weak to dissolve the five heinous crimes — is a rare moment of explicit doctrinal limitation in this commentary, distinguishing this grade sharply from the third grade of the upper rank, where a single sincere thought was already declared sufficient regardless of prior offense.

Part 91 Complete

This closes Part 91. Part 92 completes this fourth grade in full: dedication of this practice, monks rather than bodhisattvas arriving to welcome this practitioner (since Hīnayāna capacity calls forth a Hīnayāna assembly), and the attainment of arhatship itself as this grade's own closing benefit.
