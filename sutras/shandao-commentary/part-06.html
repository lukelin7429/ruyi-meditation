The Seed of the Two Vehicles, and Vaidehī's Own Attainment

Shandao closes his sixth gate with a question this site's own Ten Doubts and Rebirth Treatise pages have already met in different guises: how can Vasubandhu's own Rebirth Treatise say that "the seed of the two vehicles does not arise" in Amitābha's land, when other sūtras plainly describe śrāvakas dwelling there? And he closes the fascicle itself with his seventh and final gate — settling, against a natural but mistaken reading, exactly when Vaidehī herself attained the acceptance of non-arising.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1753, 觀無量壽佛經疏, by Shandao (善導, 613–681) Fascicle 1 of 4: 玄義分 (Profound Meaning)

6. The Seed of the Two Vehicles Does Not Arise There

Chinese Source

第六、會通二乘種不生義者。

問曰：「彌陀淨國為當是報、是化也？」

答曰：「是報非化。云何得知？如《大乘同性經》說：『西方安樂阿彌陀佛是報佛報土。』又《無量壽經》云：『法藏比丘在世饒王佛所行菩薩道時，發四十八願。』一一願言：『若我得佛，十方眾生稱我名號，願生我國，下至十念，若不生者不取正覺。』今既成佛，即是酬因之身也。又《觀經》中，上輩三人臨命終時，皆言阿彌陀佛及與化佛來迎此人。然報身兼化共來授手，故名為與。以此文證故知是報。然報、應二身者眼目之異名，前翻報作應，後翻應作報。凡言報者，因行不虛定招來果，以果應因故名為報。又三大僧祇所修萬行必定應得菩提，今既道成，即是應身。斯乃過現諸佛辨立三身，除斯已外更無別體。縱使無窮八相名號塵沙，剋體而論，眾歸化攝。今彼彌陀現是報也。」

問曰：「既言報者，報身常住永無生滅。何故《觀音授記經》說阿彌陀佛亦有入涅槃時？此之一義若為通釋？」

答曰：「入不入義者，唯是諸佛境界。尚非三乘淺智所闚，豈況小凡輒能知也。雖然，必欲知者，敢引佛經以為明證。何者？如《大品經．涅槃非化品》中說云：『佛告須菩提：「於汝意云何，若有化人作化人，是化頗有實事不空者不？」須菩提言：「不也，世尊！」佛告須菩提：「色即是化，受想行識即是化，乃至一切種智即是化。」須菩提白佛言：「世尊！若世間法是化，出世間法亦是化。所謂四念處、四正勤、四如意足、五根、五力、七覺分、八聖道分、三解脫門，佛十力、四無所畏、四無礙智、十八不共法，并諸法果，及賢聖人，所謂須陀洹、斯陀含、阿那含、阿羅漢、辟支佛、菩薩摩訶薩、諸佛世尊，是法亦是化不？」佛告須菩提：「一切法皆是化。於是法中有聲聞法變化，有辟支佛法變化，有菩薩法變化，有諸佛法變化，有煩惱法變化，有業因緣法變化。以是因緣故，須菩提！一切法皆是化。」須菩提白佛言：「世尊！是諸煩惱斷，所謂須陀洹果、斯陀含果、阿那含果、阿羅漢果、辟支佛道，斷諸煩惱習，皆是變化不？」佛告須菩提：「若有法生滅相者皆是變化。」須菩提言：「世尊！何等法非變化？」佛言：「若法無生無滅是非變化。」須菩提言：「何等是不生不滅非變化？」佛言：「無誑相涅槃，是法非變化。世尊！如佛自說，諸法平等，非聲聞作，非辟支佛作，非諸菩薩摩訶薩作，非諸佛作，有佛無佛諸法性常空，性空即是涅槃。云何涅槃一法非如化？」佛告須菩提：「如是，如是！諸法平等，非聲聞所作乃至性空即是涅槃。若新發意菩薩聞是一切法皆畢竟性空乃至涅槃亦皆如化者，心則驚怖。為是新發意菩薩故，分別生滅者如化、不生不滅者不如化耶。」』今既以斯聖教驗知彌陀定是報也。縱使後入涅槃，其義無妨，諸有智者應知。」

問曰：「彼佛及土既言報者。報法高妙，小聖難階。垢障凡夫云何得入？」

答曰：「若論眾生垢障，實難欣趣，正由託佛願以作強緣，致使五乘齊入。」

問曰：「若言凡夫小聖得生者，何故天親《淨土論》云：『女人及根缺、二乘種不生』？今彼國中現有二乘，如斯論教若為消釋？」

答曰：「子但誦其文不闚理，況加以封拙懷迷，無由啟悟。今引佛教以為明證，却汝疑情。何者？即《觀經》下輩三人是也。何以得知？如下品上生云：或有眾生，多造惡法，無有慚愧。如此愚人命欲終時，遇善知識為說大乘，教令稱阿彌陀佛。當稱佛時，化佛菩薩現在其前，金光華蓋迎還彼土，華開已後觀音為說大乘。此人聞已即發無上道心。」

問曰：「種之與心有何差別？」

答曰：「但以取便而言，義無差別。當華開之時，此人身器清淨正堪聞法，亦不簡大小。但使得聞即便生信，是以觀音不為說小，先為說大。聞大歡喜即發無上道心，即名大乘種生，亦名大乘心生。

又當華開時，觀音先為說小乘者，聞小生信，即名二乘種生，亦名二乘心生。此品既爾，下二亦然。此三品人俱在彼發心，正由聞大即大乘種生。由不聞小故，所以二乘種不生。凡言種者即是其心也。上來解二乘種不生義竟。女人及根缺義者，彼無故，可知。又十方眾生，修小乘戒行，願往生者，一無妨礙，悉得往生。但到彼先證小果，證已即轉向大。一轉向大以去，更不退生二乘之心，故名二乘種不生。前解就不定之始，後解就小果之終也。應知。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, reconciling the meaning of "the seed of the two vehicles does not arise there."

Question: "Is Amitābha's own Pure Land properly a reward-land, or a transformation-land?"

Answer: "It is a reward-land, not a transformation-land. How is this known? As the Sūtra on the Same Nature of the Great Vehicle says: 'The western land of Peace and Bliss, Amitābha Buddha, is a reward-buddha's own reward-land.' And the Immeasurable Life Sūtra says: 'When the bhikṣu Dharmākara, while still in the world, was practicing the bodhisattva path in the presence of the buddha Lokeśvararāja, he made forty-eight vows.' Each and every one of these vows says: 'If, once I have attained buddhahood, beings of the ten directions call upon my name and vow to be born in my land, even down to as few as ten thoughts, and yet fail to be born there — then may I not accept complete enlightenment.' Now that he has already attained buddhahood, this is exactly the body that has answered and repaid its own cause. And further, in the Contemplation Sūtra, when each of the three people of the upper rank comes to the end of their life, it says, in every case, that Amitābha Buddha, together with transformation-buddhas, comes to welcome this person. Since a reward-body, together with transformation-bodies, comes together to extend a hand, this is why it is said "together with." Taking this text as proof, one thus knows this is a reward-body. Now, "reward-body" and "response-body" are simply two different names for the same eye, seen two ways — the earlier translations render it "reward," the later translations render it "response." Generally speaking, "reward" means that a cause, once cultivated in practice, is never in vain, but certainly brings about a corresponding fruit — and because the fruit answers the cause, it is called "reward." And further, the ten thousand practices cultivated across three great asaṃkhyeya kalpas must certainly result in the attainment of bodhi — and now that this path is accomplished, this is exactly a "response-body." This is precisely how buddhas of the past and present alike distinguish and establish the three bodies; apart from these, there is no further, separate body at all. Amitābha, as he now appears, is a reward-body."

Question: "Given that this is called a reward-body — a reward-body abides forever, with neither birth nor cessation at all. Why, then, does the Sūtra of Avalokiteśvara's Prediction say that Amitābha Buddha, too, will one day enter nirvāṇa? How is this one point to be reconciled?"

Answer: "The question of entering, or not entering, nirvāṇa belongs entirely to the domain of the buddhas themselves. It lies beyond even the shallow wisdom of the three vehicles to peer into at all — how much less could an ordinary, lesser being ever come to know it? And yet, should one truly wish to know it, I will venture to cite the Buddha's own sūtra as clear proof. What does it say? As the Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra's own chapter on "Nirvāṇa Is Not a Transformation" states: "The Buddha said to Subhūti: 'What do you think — if a transformed being were to create yet another transformed being, would this transformation possess any real, non-empty substance at all?' Subhūti said: 'No, World-Honored One!' The Buddha said to Subhūti: 'Form is itself a transformation; sensation, perception, mental formation, and consciousness are themselves transformations; and even omniscience itself is a transformation.' Subhūti said to the Buddha: 'World-Honored One! If worldly dharmas are transformations, are transcendent dharmas transformations as well? Namely: the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right efforts, the four bases of spiritual power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, the eightfold noble path, the three gates of liberation, the Buddha's ten powers, his four fearlessnesses, his four unobstructed wisdoms, his eighteen unique qualities, together with the fruits of every dharma, and every worthy and sage — namely the stream-enterer, the once-returner, the non-returner, the arhat, the pratyekabuddha, the bodhisattva-mahāsattva, and every buddha, World-Honored One — are these dharmas transformations as well?' The Buddha said to Subhūti: 'Every dharma is a transformation. Within these dharmas there is the transformation of the śrāvaka's own dharma, the transformation of the pratyekabuddha's own dharma, the transformation of the bodhisattva's own dharma, the transformation of every buddha's own dharma, the transformation of the dharma of affliction, and the transformation of the dharma of karmic cause and condition. For this very reason, Subhūti, every dharma is a transformation.' Subhūti said to the Buddha: 'World-Honored One! Are the severing of every affliction — namely the fruit of stream-entry, the fruit of once-returning, the fruit of non-returning, the fruit of arhatship, the path of the pratyekabuddha, the severing of every habitual affliction — are all of these transformations as well?' The Buddha said to Subhūti: 'Whatever dharma bears the mark of arising and ceasing is a transformation.' Subhūti said: 'World-Honored One! What dharma, then, is not a transformation?' The Buddha said: 'A dharma that neither arises nor ceases is not a transformation.' Subhūti said: 'What, then, is this "neither arising nor ceasing," this "not a transformation"?' The Buddha said: 'Nirvāṇa, whose very mark admits of no falsehood at all, is a dharma that is not a transformation. World-Honored One, as the Buddha himself has said, all dharmas are equal — made by no śrāvaka, made by no pratyekabuddha, made by no bodhisattva-mahāsattva, made by no buddha; whether a buddha exists or not, the nature of every dharma is constantly empty, and this emptiness of nature is itself nirvāṇa. How, then, could nirvāṇa, this one single dharma, not be like a transformation as well?' The Buddha said to Subhūti: 'Just so, just so! Every dharma is equal, made by no śrāvaka, and so on, up to: the emptiness of nature is itself nirvāṇa. But should a bodhisattva who has only just given rise to the aspiration hear that every dharma, without exception, is ultimately empty of nature, even up to nirvāṇa itself being like a transformation, his own mind would be startled and afraid. It is for the sake of this bodhisattva who has only just given rise to the aspiration that arising-and-ceasing dharmas are distinguished as being like a transformation, while the unborn and unceasing are distinguished as not being like one at all.'" Now, given this sacred teaching, one thus confirms that Amitābha is certainly a reward-body. Even should he, at some later time, enter nirvāṇa, this makes no difficulty for this meaning at all — every person of wisdom should understand this."

Question: "If that buddha and his land are indeed called a reward-body and reward-land, then this reward-dharma is lofty and sublime, difficult even for lesser sages to ascend to. How, then, could ordinary beings, defiled and obstructed as they are, ever manage to enter it?"

Answer: "Considering sentient beings' own defilement and obstruction, it is indeed difficult for them to aspire toward it and reach it on their own — but it is precisely by relying on the Buddha's own vow as a powerful supporting condition that beings of all five vehicles alike are able to enter there together."

Question: "If it is said that ordinary beings and lesser sages alike can attain rebirth there, then why does Vasubandhu's own Pure Land Treatise say: 'Women, and those with deficient faculties, and the seed of the two vehicles, are not born there'? Yet that land, as it actually is, does contain the two lesser vehicles — how is this teaching of the treatise to be resolved?"

Answer: "You merely recite its text without peering into its actual principle — and, worse still, cling to your own narrow confusion, with no way to arrive at genuine understanding. I will now cite the Buddha's own teaching as clear proof, to dispel this doubt of yours. What does it say? This refers exactly to the three people of the lower rank in the Contemplation Sūtra. How is this known? As the text on the upper of the lower grade says: there are sentient beings who have committed much unwholesome conduct, without the slightest sense of shame. When such a foolish person's life comes to its end, he encounters a good teacher, who expounds the Great Vehicle for him, and teaches him to call upon Amitābha's name. At the very moment he calls upon the Buddha's name, transformation-buddhas and bodhisattvas appear before him, and, amid golden light and a jeweled canopy, welcome him back to that land — and, once his lotus has opened, Avalokiteśvara expounds the Great Vehicle for him. This person, upon hearing this, at once gives rise to the unsurpassed aspiration for the path."

Question: "What difference is there between 'seed' and 'mind'?"

Answer: "These are simply spoken of according to convenience — there is no actual difference in their meaning. At the very moment his lotus opens, this person's own bodily vessel is pure, and precisely fit to hear the Dharma — nor is any distinction made between greater and lesser vehicles at this point. It is simply that, the moment he is able to hear it, he at once gives rise to faith — and it is for this reason that Avalokiteśvara does not expound the lesser vehicle for him, but expounds the greater vehicle first instead. Hearing the greater vehicle, and rejoicing in it, he at once gives rise to the unsurpassed aspiration for the path — and this is exactly what is called 'the seed of the Great Vehicle arising,' also called 'the mind of the Great Vehicle arising.'

"And further, were Avalokiteśvara, at the moment the lotus opens, to expound the lesser vehicle first instead, and this person, hearing the lesser vehicle, were to give rise to faith in it, this would be exactly what is called 'the seed of the two vehicles arising,' also called 'the mind of the two vehicles arising.' As this grade is, so too are the two below it. These three grades of people, all alike, give rise to this aspiration only once there — precisely because they hear the Great Vehicle, the seed of the Great Vehicle arises within them. And precisely because they never hear the lesser vehicle at all, the seed of the two vehicles simply does not arise. What is generally meant by 'seed' here is exactly this same mind. This concludes the explanation of the meaning of 'the seed of the two vehicles does not arise.' As for the meaning of 'women, and those with deficient faculties': that land simply has none of these at all, as should be recognized. And further, sentient beings of the ten directions who cultivate the precepts and practice of the lesser vehicle, and who vow to be reborn there, encounter not the slightest obstruction at all, and all, without exception, attain rebirth. It is simply that, upon arriving there, they first realize the lesser fruit, and, having realized it, at once turn toward the greater. Once they have turned toward the greater, they never again retrogress into giving rise to the mind of the two vehicles — and this is exactly why it is called 'the seed of the two vehicles does not arise.' The first explanation concerns the very beginning, when the outcome is not yet settled; the second explanation concerns the very end, once the lesser fruit has already been reached. This should be understood."

A note on this reconciliation

Shandao's resolution here runs on the same logic Tanluan already reached, a century and a half earlier, in his own commentary on the Rebirth Treatise: śrāvakas may be present in Amitābha's land without that land's own conditions ever producing one from scratch. But Shandao gets there by an entirely different route — not Tanluan's citation-chain proving śrāvakas exist there and then explaining the discrepancy, but a direct account of what actually happens the moment a newly-arrived being's lotus opens. Whether that being hears the Great Vehicle taught first, or the lesser vehicle first, depends only on what teaching Avalokiteśvara happens to give — and this, on Shandao's reading, is precisely what "seed" means throughout the treatise: not a fixed spiritual rank carried over from a prior life, but simply "the mind" that forms in that first moment of hearing. The three grades of the lower rank — the very worst sinners in the entire nine-grade scheme, already established across Parts 3 and 4 as ordinary beings rescued at the very last possible moment — turn out to be exactly the beings this whole reconciliation depends on. It is their own lotus-opening, and no one else's, that Shandao points to as proof that the treatise's "seed" is a description of first response, not a permanent classification.

Gate Seven: When Vaidehī Attained Non-Arising

Chinese Source

第七、料簡韋提聞佛正說得益分齊者。

問曰：「韋提既言得忍。未審何時得忍？出在何文？」

答曰：「韋提得忍，出在第七觀初。經云：『佛告韋提：「佛當為汝分別解說除苦惱法。」說是語時，無量壽佛住立空中，觀音勢至侍立左右。時韋提應時得見，接足作禮，歡喜讚歎，即得無生法忍。』何以得知？如下利益分中說言：『得見佛身及二菩薩，心生歡喜，歎未曾有。廓然大悟得無生忍。』非是光臺中見國時得也。」

問曰：「上文中說言：見彼國土極妙樂事，心歡喜故，應時即得無生法忍。此之一義云何通釋？」

答曰：「如此義者，但是世尊酬前別請舉勸利益方便之由序。何以得知？次下文中說言：『諸佛如來有異方便令汝得見』，次下日想、水想、氷想乃至十三觀已來，盡名異方便也。欲使眾生於此觀門一一得成，見彼妙事心歡喜故即得無生。斯乃直是如來慈哀末代，舉勸勵修，欲令積學之者無遺聖力冥加現益故也。」

證曰：「掌握機糸，十有三結。條條順理，以應玄門訖。此義周三，呈前證者矣。上來雖有七段不同，總是文前玄義。料簡經論相違妨難，一一引教證明。欲使信者無疑，求者無滯。應知。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, analyzing exactly what benefit Vaidehī received upon hearing the Buddha's own true teaching.

Question: "It is said that Vaidehī attained the acceptance of non-arising. It is not yet clear precisely when she attained it — where in the text does this appear?"

Answer: "Vaidehī's attainment of this acceptance appears at the very opening of the seventh contemplation. The sūtra says: 'The Buddha said to Vaidehī: "I shall now explain for you, one by one, the methods that remove suffering and vexation." Just as he spoke these words, the Buddha of Immeasurable Life stood in the air before them, with Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta attending at his left and right. At that very moment, Vaidehī beheld him, and, touching her head to his feet in reverence, rejoicing and offering praise, she at once attained the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas.' How is this known? As the text on the section of benefits further on says: 'Having attained the sight of the Buddha's own body and his two bodhisattvas, her mind gave rise to joy, and she praised this as something never before seen — and, in a state of complete, open realization, she attained the acceptance of non-arising.' This was not attained at the moment she saw the lands within the radiant platform."

Question: "The text above states that, upon seeing that land's own supremely wondrous and blissful scenery, her mind rejoiced, and she attained, at that very moment, the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas. How is this one point to be reconciled with the other?"

Answer: "This particular passage is simply the World-Honored One's own preface, responding to her earlier specific request, raising up and encouraging the beneficial skillful means that follow. How is this known? In the text immediately following, it says: 'Every buddha, every Tathāgata, has further skillful means that let you come to see it' — and everything that follows, the contemplation of the sun, the contemplation of the water, the contemplation of ice, all the way through the thirteenth contemplation, are, every one of them, exactly these 'further skillful means.' The purpose is to have sentient beings, through each and every one of these contemplative gates in turn, actually accomplish them — so that, seeing that land's own wondrous scenery, their minds rejoice, and they thereby attain non-arising. This is nothing other than the Tathāgata's own compassionate grief for this final age, raising up and encouraging diligent practice, so that those who accumulate this learning may lack nothing of the sacred power's own hidden support and its present benefit."

A closing verification: "Grasping this whole matter firmly in hand, there are thirteen knots to untie in all. Each and every one, following its own principle in turn, has now brought us to the close of this profound gate. This meaning has now gone three full rounds, each time presenting its own proof before it. Though the above has unfolded across seven distinct sections, all of them together form the profound meaning that precedes the text itself. In analyzing the apparent contradictions and difficulties between sūtras and treatises, I have, one by one, cited the teaching itself as proof — so that those who already believe may be left with no further doubt, and those still seeking may encounter no further obstruction. This should be understood."

End of Fascicle One: The Profound Meaning

A note on this closing gate

Shandao's own precision here matters more than it might first appear. An easier, more devotional reading would locate Vaidehī's breakthrough at the sūtra's most visually spectacular moment — her first sight, within the Buddha's radiant platform, of every pure land in the ten directions spread before her. Shandao insists otherwise: that vision was preparatory, "the World-Honored One's own preface," not the moment of realization itself. Her actual attainment comes only later, at the seventh contemplation, when Amitābha, Avalokiteśvara, and Mahāsthāmaprāpta stand directly before her in the air. The distinction is not pedantic — it is the same distinction, in miniature, that runs through this entire fascicle: seeing a wondrous scene, however moving, is not yet the same thing as the direct encounter that actually transforms a mind. Vaidehī's own path to realization, on Shandao's reading, runs through the same sequence of contemplations the sūtra will go on to teach every other practitioner after her — proof, in the very first figure the sūtra ever follows, that its promise is not rhetorical.

Fascicle 1 Complete

This closes Fascicle 1, the Xuanyi Fen (玄義分, "Profound Meaning") — Shandao's full sevenfold analytical framework, from the sūtra's own preface and name through his sustained defense of a single, load-bearing claim: that the Contemplation Sūtra, in every one of its nine grades, addresses ordinary, flawed beings, not sages already advanced along the path. This claim is what makes everything that follows in this commentary possible — a Pure Land practice genuinely open to "the good and evil ordinary beings of the present age" alike, exactly as Shandao states at the close of Part 4. Fascicles 2 through 4 turn to the sūtra's own text directly: its narrative preface, the sixteen contemplations themselves, and the nine grades of rebirth in full detail, each read through the framework this first fascicle has now established.

Translation of Fascicle 2, the Xufen Yi (序分義, "Meaning of the Preliminary Section"), is in progress and will continue this collection.
