Shandao's Commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra.

A four-fascicle, 46,000-character commentary — the single most historically consequential work of exegesis in Chinese Pure Land Buddhism, and the direct source of Hōnen's founding of Japan's Jōdo-shū school five centuries later. This collection translates Shandao's own commentary in full, fascicle by fascicle, presenting his own voice directly rather than as a secondary source cited elsewhere.

How to read this collection

This is the largest single work on this site's Pure Land shelf — 46,102 Chinese characters across four fascicles, roughly fifteen times the length of the Rebirth Treatise and its commentary combined. Given its scale, this collection is being translated fascicle by fascicle, following the same approach already used for this site's translation of Yuanying's commentary on the Śūraṅgama Sūtra: Shandao's own prose, including both his citations of the base sūtra and his own extended argument, is translated directly and continuously, without a further layer of secondary "reading notes" from another commentator — since Shandao's own voice, arguing genuinely contested questions in his own right, is the primary content here.

Fascicle 1, the Xuanyi Fen (玄義分, "Profound Meaning"), is complete: Shandao's sevenfold analytical framework, including his sustained, historically decisive argument that the sūtra's nine grades of rebirth describe ordinary beings, not sages already advanced along the path. Fascicle 2, the Xufen Yi (序分義, "Meaning of the Preliminary Section"), is complete as well: the sūtra's full narrative preface, from Ajātaśatru's rebellion through Vaidehī's imprisonment, her vision of every pure land, and her choice of Amitābha's own realm, closing with the three blessings and the five turbidities. Fascicle 3, the Dingshan Yi (定善義, "Meaning of the Contemplative Good"), is now complete as well: all thirteen of Shandao's own detailed contemplations, from the sun and water through the ground, the jeweled trees, ponds, and towers, the lotus throne, and finally the image, true body, and two great attendant bodhisattvas — including the historically pivotal "the mind itself creates the Buddha" passage and the three affinities that shaped centuries of later Pure Land practice. Fascicle 4, covering the nine grades of rebirth in full detail, is still to come.

Why this commentary matters

  • It is the source of a single line — on single-mindedly holding fast to Amitābha's name as "the rightly established practice" — that led Hōnen to found Jōdo-shū, Japan's first independent Pure Land school, in the twelfth century.
  • Its central argument — that the Contemplation Sūtra's nine grades of rebirth describe ordinary, flawed beings rather than sages — reshaped how an entire tradition read its own founding text.
  • It already anchors the reading notes across this site's own translation of the Contemplation Sūtra; this collection translates it directly and in full.

The Commentary's Four Fascicles

Shandao's own four-part structure follows the sūtra's own shape: first its doctrine and framework, then its narrative opening, then its sixteen contemplations, then its nine grades of rebirth.

Fascicle 1 · 玄義分
Profound Meaning
Shandao's sevenfold analytical framework — the sūtra's name, doctrinal classification, and his sustained argument, against "other masters," that the nine grades describe ordinary beings, not sages.

Meaning of the Preliminary Section
