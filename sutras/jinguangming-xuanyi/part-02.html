Contemplation of Mind, Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching

The second fascicle completes Explaining the Name and then treats the remaining four headings. It opens by arguing that simile and text should both be kept, then gives an explanation of the name by contemplation of mind: each of the ten sets of three dharmas from Fascicle One is found in a single thought of the practitioner's own mind, contemplated as identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle, with the six identities as stages. This part includes a rebuttal of "people who practice emptiness" who see no reason to revere sūtras and images. Zhiyi then explains the fuller title "emperor-king" through three meanings and shows how the ten sets gather in all Dharma-gates, sūtras, and stages. The essence of the sūtra is the dharma-nature, or Dharma-body, in which the Buddha roams; its cardinal doctrine is the buddha-fruit, shown through the Buddha's lifespan; its function is extinguishing evil and producing good, seen in the Repentance and Praise chapters. Against earlier classifications, the sūtra is placed among the Vaipulya teachings: the third flavor, the miscellaneous treasury, and the shared teaching accompanied by the distinct and the perfect. In the Song dynasty the contemplation-of-mind section became the center of a dispute over its authenticity; the translation takes no position on it.

Simile and Text: Both Kept

Chinese Source

◎問：舊云，此經從譬得名。云何矯異而依文耶？答：非今就文而害於譬。若苟執譬，復害於文。義有二途，應須兩存。故前云義推疎遠，依文親近。若鈍根人以譬擬法，若利根人即法作譬。下文云「如深法性安住其中。即於是典金光明中，而得見我釋迦牟尼。」又〈空品〉云「為鈍根故起大悲心。」鈍人守指守株，寧知兔月？利人懸解不，須株指（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The old [masters] say that this sūtra gets its name from a simile; why deviate from them and follow the text? Answer: It is not that, now following the text, we harm the simile; but if one clings to the simile, one in turn harms the text. The meaning has two routes, and both should be kept. Hence it was said above: inference from meaning is distant and remote; following the text is close and near. A person of dull faculties uses the simile to approximate the dharma; a person of sharp faculties takes the dharma itself as the simile. The text below says, "In the deep dharma-nature one dwells at peace; then within this scripture, the Golden Light, one gets to see me, Śākyamuni." Further, the "Emptiness" chapter says, "For the sake of those of dull faculties, arousing a mind of great compassion." Dull people guard the finger and guard the stump — how would they know the rabbit and the moon? Sharp people understand directly and do not need stump or finger (and so on).

Explaining the Name by Contemplation of Mind

Chinese Source

次、觀心釋名者，何故須是？上來所說，專是聖人聖寶，非己智分。如鸚鵡學語，似客作數錢，不能開發自身寶藏。今欲論道前凡夫地之珍寶，即聞而修，故明觀心釋也。《淨名》曰「諸佛解脫當於眾生心行中求。」《釋論》云「有聞有智慧，是所說應受。」即此意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the name by contemplation of mind. Why is this needed? What has been said so far is entirely the sages' sacred jewel, not a share of one's own wisdom — like a parrot learning to talk, or a hired hand counting coins; it cannot open the jewel treasury of one's own self. Now we wish to discuss the treasure on the ground of ordinary beings before the path, so that one may cultivate as soon as one hears; hence we clarify the explanation by contemplation of mind. The Vimalakīrti says, "The liberation of the buddhas should be sought in the mental conduct of beings." The Great Treatise says, "One who has learning and has wisdom — what such a one says should be accepted." This is the intent.

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Mind has four aggregates; why discard three and contemplate one? Answer: Of the myriad things under heaven, only the human is most noble. Of the seven-foot frame, only the head is most noble. The head has seven openings, and the eyes are noble. Though the eyes are noble, they are not as noble as numinous wisdom. You should know that among the four aggregates, mind is noble; because it is noble, it is contemplated. Because mind is noble, mind is gold. The firefly illuminates only itself. Lamps, candles, jewels, and fire, though they illuminate others, do not cast their light far. The light of stars and moon dwells together with darkness. The light of the sun can illuminate all under heaven but cannot illuminate principle. The light of the mind's wisdom can give rise to wisdom and illuminate principle; hence mind is light. If the mind is deluded and dark, the body is haggard; if the mind has the light of wisdom, the skin is full and lustrous. Hence the Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] says, "Because prajñā is great, form is great; because prajñā is pure, form is pure; it can also fill and benefit feeling, perception, formations, and the rest." Mind is brightness. Further, knowing mind as no-mind is called light; knowing perception as no-perception and formations as no-formations is called brightness. Further, knowing the four aggregates as not the four aggregates is called light; knowing the aggregate of form as not the aggregate of form is called brightness. Further, knowing the five aggregates as not the five aggregates is called light; knowing the provisional person as not the provisional person is called brightness. Further, knowing the principal reward as not the principal reward is called light; knowing the dependent reward as not the dependent reward is called brightness. Further, knowing the dependent and the principal as not the dependent and the principal is called light; knowing all dharmas as without all dharmas is called brightness. One who grasps this intent has the Golden Light of contemplation of mind.

The Three Paths in Contemplation of Mind

Chinese Source

上約十種三法論金光明，今觀心王即觀苦道，觀慧數即煩惱道，觀諸數是業道。心王是金，慧數是光，餘數是明。如《淨名》曰「觀身實相觀佛亦然」者，若頭等六分各各是身，此即多身。若別有一身，則無是處。各各非身，合時亦無。若頭等六分求身叵得，現在不住故不可得，過去因滅亦不可得，未來未至亦不可得。如是橫豎求身畢竟不可得，即是無，此無亦不可得，亦有亦無亦不可得，非有非無亦不可得，但有名字名之為身。如是名字不在內，非四陰中故。不在外，非色陰中故。不在中間，非色心合故。亦不常自有，非離色心故。當知名無召物之功、物無應名之實，假實既空，名物安在？如此觀身是觀實相，實相即是金。實相觀智，即是光。緣身諸心心數寂不行者，即是明也。觀身是假名，假名既如此，觀色受想行識亦如是，即為苦道觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Above, the Golden Light was discussed in terms of the ten sets of three dharmas. Now, contemplating the mind-king is contemplating the path of suffering; contemplating the wisdom factor is the path of affliction; contemplating the [other] factors is the path of karma. The mind-king is gold, the wisdom factor is light, the remaining factors are brightness. As the Vimalakīrti says, "As one contemplates the true mark of one's own body, so one contemplates the Buddha." If the six parts — the head and the rest — were each a body, there would be many bodies. If there were a body separately, that is impossible. Each is not the body, and when they are combined it is not there either. If one seeks the body in the six parts, the head and the rest, it cannot be found: the present does not abide, so it cannot be found; the past cause has ceased, so it cannot be found; the future has not arrived, so it cannot be found. Seeking the body thus horizontally and vertically, it is ultimately not to be found — that is nonexistence; this nonexistence too cannot be found; both existent and nonexistent cannot be found; neither existent nor nonexistent cannot be found. There is only a name, called "body." Such a name is not inside, since it is not among the four aggregates; not outside, since it is not in the aggregate of form; not in between, since it is not the union of form and mind; nor does it always exist of itself, since it is not apart from form and mind. You should know that names have no power to summon things, and things have no reality that answers to names. Since the provisional and the real are both empty, where are name and thing? To contemplate the body thus is to contemplate the true mark; the true mark is gold. The wisdom contemplating the true mark is light. That the minds and mental factors taking the body as object are quiescent and do not move is brightness. The body is contemplated as a provisional name; since the provisional name is thus, contemplating form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness is likewise. This is the contemplation of the path of suffering.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, contemplating the path of affliction. Afflictions and karma are both causes of the body; now let us take afflictions as the cause of the body and raise the contemplation. The Vimalakīrti says, "Without destroying the cause of the body, still follow the single mark." One should distinguish four propositions: whose body has both cause and fruit destroyed? whose body has neither cause nor fruit destroyed? who destroys the fruit and not the cause? who destroys the cause and not the fruit? What is the fruit of the body? The six parts, the head and the rest, born of father and mother. What is the cause of the body? Greed, anger, and delusion, the actions of body, speech, and mind, and so on. Now, setting aside the three kinds of action, we contemplate greed, anger, delusion, and so on, and the four fruits. When the wisdom contemplating impermanence, suffering, and emptiness breaks greed, anger, and delusion, the bond of the seed is severed — this is called destroying the cause of the body; not receiving a later existence is called destroying the fruit of the body. Ordinary worldly people nourish the five aggregates with fine clothing and good food, indulge the mind and follow their nature, and give free rein to greed, anger, and delusion, afflicting themselves and afflicting others. When one body dies and is destroyed, they again receive a body; cause and fruit continue without end. This is called neither cause nor fruit destroyed. Like one who violates the king's law and is handed over to the caṇḍāla, or one who in enmity harms his own body: when the body has rotted away, the four aggregates are also exhausted — this is destroying the fruit. Yet greed, anger, and delusion, the cause of the body, blaze ever more fiercely, filling birth-and-death with no time of escape — this is the third proposition.

Ru-Yi Translation

The remaining three fruits likewise, with the wisdom contemplating impermanence, sever the bond of the cause of the five lower [fetters], while the fruit-body of the five lower [fetters] is not yet exhausted; this is called destroying the cause of the body without destroying the fruit of the body. These four propositions differ in what is preserved and destroyed, but none follows the single mark. Following the single mark is what is called contemplating together with the objects in cultivation. Contemplate one thought of a mind of greed, anger, or delusion: Does the mind arise of itself? Does it arise in response to objects? Does it arise from faculties [and objects] together? Does it arise apart from faculties and objects? None of these is so. Not from itself, not from another, not from both, not without cause; nor does it arise because the previous thought has ceased; neither arising nor not arising, neither ceasing nor not ceasing. Seeking the mind thus horizontally and vertically, it cannot be found. Since the mind is originally nonexistent, what destruction is there to speak of? This is called not destroying the cause of the body yet following the single mark. Following the single mark is following gold; the wisdom that follows the mark is following light; all factors quiescent and extinguished is following brightness. Having obtained the one proposition of not destroying, and following the single mark, one understands destroying the cause of the body; destroying the fruit of the body also follows the single mark, and not destroying the fruit of the body also follows the single mark — all are likewise (and so on).

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, contemplating the path of karma. As the Vimalakīrti says, "Raising the foot and lowering the foot are nowhere but the site of awakening, complete with all the buddha-dharmas." Contemplate the time of raising the foot: Is it karma that raises it? Is it the doer of karma that raises it? Do karma and the doer of karma raise it together? Is it raised apart from karma and the doer of karma? If karma raises it, it has nothing to do with the doer; if the doer raises it, it has nothing to do with karma. Since separately there is no raising, combined there is no raising; since combined there is no raising, how could there be raising apart? Since there is no raising of the foot, there is also no lowering of the foot. Since contemplating walking is so, standing, sitting, lying down, speaking, and working are likewise. This is contemplating the true mark of karma, called gold; this contemplating wisdom is called light; the mental factors in all deportments all quiescent is called brightness. This is discussing the Golden Light by the three paths. Whoever has mind possesses the Golden Light of the dharma-realm and the dharma-nature. One who can understand thus has only the Golden Light in name. Constantly relying on this contemplation, thought after thought without rest, mind after mind continuing, is the Golden Light in contemplative practice. If it is dim, like looking through gauze and not yet clear — seen with eyes closed, lost with eyes open — this is the Golden Light in resemblance. If it is clearly and distinctly seen with eyes closed or open, it is the Golden Light in a portion of the truth. If the fruit of wondrous awakening is perfect and ultimately clear, it is called the ultimate Golden Light.

The Three Consciousnesses and the Three Buddha-Natures

Chinese Source

次觀心明三識。論金光明者，諦觀一念心，即空即假即中，即是觀心識於三識。何者？意識託緣發意，本無識，緣何所發？又緣中為有識、為無識？若有識，緣即是識，何謂為緣？若無識，那能發識？若意緣合發，二俱無故，合不能發，離最不可。當知此識不在一處，從眾緣生。從緣生法，我說即是空。於此空中假作分別，是惡識、是善識、是非惡非善識，種種推畫強謂是非識。若定空不可作假識，若定假不可作空，當知空非空、假非假，非空非假雙亡二邊正顯中道，一念識中三觀具足。識於三識，亦不得三識觀。故《淨名》云「不觀色、不觀色如、不觀色性，乃至不觀識、不觀識如、不觀識性。」雖不得識、不得識如、不得識性，雙照識、識如、識性宛然無濫。以照識性故，是菴摩羅識。照識如故，是阿梨耶識。亦照亦滅故，是阿陀那識。是名觀心中三識金光明。六即位，如上說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying the three consciousnesses by contemplation of mind. In discussing the Golden Light: carefully contemplate a single thought of mind as identical to the empty, identical to the provisional, identical to the middle; this is contemplating the mind's consciousness as the three consciousnesses. Why? Mental consciousness relies on conditions and the mental faculty is aroused; if originally there is no consciousness, what would the conditions arouse? Further, within the conditions, is there consciousness, or is there no consciousness? If there is consciousness, the conditions are consciousness — why call them conditions? If there is no consciousness, how can they arouse consciousness? If the mental faculty and conditions combine to arouse it — since both lack it, combination cannot arouse it, and apart [from them] is least possible. You should know that this consciousness is not in one place; it arises from manifold conditions. "Dharmas arising from conditions I declare to be empty." Within this emptiness, discriminations are provisionally made — this is evil consciousness, this is good consciousness, this is neither evil nor good consciousness — and by all kinds of reckoning and sketching they are forcibly called right and wrong consciousness. If fixedly empty, one cannot make provisional consciousness; if fixedly provisional, one cannot make emptiness. You should know: the empty is not empty, the provisional is not provisional; neither empty nor provisional, both extremes vanish and the middle way is properly revealed; within a single thought of consciousness the three contemplations are complete. Consciousness [contemplated] as the three consciousnesses — yet one does not get hold of a contemplation of three consciousnesses either. Hence the Vimalakīrti says, "I do not contemplate form, nor the suchness of form, nor the nature of form, … nor do I contemplate consciousness, nor the suchness of consciousness, nor the nature of consciousness." Though one does not get hold of consciousness, the suchness of consciousness, or the nature of consciousness, one illuminates together consciousness, the suchness of consciousness, and the nature of consciousness, distinctly and without confusion. Because one illuminates the nature of consciousness, it is the amala consciousness; because one illuminates the suchness of consciousness, it is the ālaya consciousness; because one both illuminates and extinguishes, it is the ādāna consciousness. This is called the Golden Light of the three consciousnesses in contemplation of mind. The six identities as stages are as explained above.

次觀心明三佛性。金光明者，觀一念心起，即空即假即中，是見三佛性。何者？心從緣起是故即空，強謂有心是故即假，不出法性是故即中。此釋已顯，更引經證之。《淨名》云「何謂病本？所謂攀緣。何謂攀緣？謂緣三界。」證其假也。「何謂息攀緣？謂心無所得。」此證即空。「我及眾生病，皆非真非有。」此證即中。《華嚴》云「心佛及眾生，是三無差別。」此證觀心即三佛性也。又《般舟三昧經》云「我心如、佛心如。佛心如、我心如。不見我心為佛心、不見佛心為我心，而見阿彌陀佛。」如瑠璃中見像、如饑夢食、如夢婬從事、如觀骨光等喻，皆是證即空即假即中之文。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying the three buddha-natures by contemplation of mind. In the Golden Light: contemplate the arising of a single thought of mind as identical to the empty, identical to the provisional, identical to the middle; this is seeing the three buddha-natures. Why? Mind arises from conditions, hence it is identical to the empty; it is forcibly said that there is mind, hence it is identical to the provisional; it does not go beyond the dharma-nature, hence it is identical to the middle. This explanation is already clear; let us further cite the sūtras to prove it. The Vimalakīrti says, "What is the root of illness? Namely, clinging. What is clinging? It is clinging to the three realms." This proves the provisional. "How is clinging cut off? By the mind having nothing to attain." This proves identity with the empty. "My illness and the illness of beings are neither real nor existent." This proves identity with the middle. The Avataṃsaka says, "Mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without distinction." This proves that contemplating the mind is the three buddha-natures. Further, the Pratyutpanna Samādhi Sūtra says, "The suchness of my mind, the suchness of the Buddha's mind; the suchness of the Buddha's mind, the suchness of my mind. Not seeing my mind as the Buddha's mind, not seeing the Buddha's mind as my mind, yet seeing Amitābha Buddha." The similes — like seeing an image in lapis lazuli, like the hungry dreaming of food, like engaging in lust in a dream, like contemplating the light of bones, and so on — are all texts proving identity with the empty, the provisional, and the middle.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the Golden Light of the three virtues in contemplation of mind: carefully contemplate a single thought of mind as identical to the empty, identical to the provisional, identical to the middle. Because it is identical to the empty, one empty is all empty; there is no provisional and no middle that is not empty; emptiness has no accumulation and yet is called a treasury; because the treasury is complete, it is called a virtue. Because it is identical to the provisional, one provisional is all provisional; there is no empty and no middle that is not provisional; the provisional gathers in all dharmas and is also called a treasury; because the treasury is complete, it is called a virtue. Because it is identical to the middle, one middle is all middle; there is no empty and no provisional that is not the middle; the middle gathers in all dharmas and is also called a treasury; because the treasury is complete, it is termed a virtue. Although it says, "In one there is the immeasurable, in the immeasurable there is one; understanding their mutual arising, arising in turn is not real; the wise have nothing to fear," you should know that one is not too few and the many are not too many; neither one nor many, yet not losing one and many; inconceivable, neither longitudinal nor horizontal, neither side by side nor separate. The buddhas take identity with the middle as essence, hence it is called the Dharma-body; take identity with the empty as life, hence it is called prajñā; take identity with the provisional as power, hence it is called liberation. Each is permanence, bliss, self, and purity without lack; hence they are termed the three virtues. Each is the dharma-realm, containing and storing much; hence it is termed the secret treasury. Hence the Vimalakīrti says, "The liberation of the buddhas should be sought in the mental conduct of all beings." You should know that my mind is thus and beings are thus; since self and other are thus, the buddhas are thus. "Mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without distinction." One who grasps this intent [knows]: identity with the middle is gold, identity with the empty is light, identity with the provisional is brightness. This is the Golden Light of the three virtues in contemplation of mind. The six identities, as before.

"Emperor" is the noblest, most honored and most weighty; "wisdom" is divine planning and sagely strategy; "king" is the ten thousand states coming to court. Possessing these three meanings, it is called emperor-wisdom-king. This sūtra is likewise. "The Tathāgata roams in the immeasurable, very deep dharma-nature, beyond the pure [sphere] practiced by the bodhisattvas" — this is the meaning of the most honored and noblest. "Whoever hears it can reflect on the unsurpassed, very deep, subtle, and wondrous meaning"; "open the gate of sweet dew, enter the city of sweet dew, dwell in the chamber of sweet dew, and let all beings eat the taste of sweet dew"; with the sword of wisdom tear the net of afflictions — this is the meaning of sagely wisdom and heroic strategy. Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, Clarifying the Function. "Function" means power-in-action. Extinguishing evil and producing good is the power-in-action of the sūtra: because it extinguishes evil it is called power, and because it produces good it is called function; because it extinguishes evil it is called merit, and because it produces good it is called virtue. These are each said one-sidedly; discussed fully, all are complete. The wisdom knowing all modes is the virtue of the fruit; the wisdom of the fruit is adorned by immeasurable merits, wiping out all suffering and giving immeasurable happiness. Suffering is the fruit of evil karma; greed, anger, and delusion are the evil cause; if the evil cause is not removed, the fruit cannot end. The sage's intent is first to extinguish the evil cause; hence the "Repentance" chapter comes first. Happiness is the good fruit; repentance and praise are its cause; repenting offenses and praising the sages, evil is extinguished and good produced; hence the "Praise" chapter comes after. These too are mutual raisings. Using this supreme function to adorn the wisdom of the fruit, wisdom is complete and the essence revealed. The revealed essence is called gold; the completed fruit is called light; the accomplished power is called brightness; benefiting others is called teaching. But the Repentance chapter, in extinguishing evil, does not fail to produce good; the Praise chapter, in producing good, does not fail to extinguish evil; each speaks of one side. The "Emptiness" chapter guides both: if repentance does not attain emptiness, evil is not removed or extinguished; if praise does not attain emptiness, good is not pure. The text says, "the wisdom knowing all modes is their root" — this is its meaning. From the "Four Heavenly Kings" chapter on, protecting the sūtra so that it is proclaimed and spread is again producing good; warding off calamity so that it departs is again extinguishing evil. Gathering in all these texts, therefore we say that extinguishing evil and producing good is the function.

The Profound Meaning of the Golden Light Sūtra, Fascicle Two

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

The contemplation-of-mind section and the Song dispute. Units 4–20 (explaining the name by contemplation of mind) were missing from one circulating version of this text. In the early Song, Wu'en and other off-mountain masters held that the shorter version without this section was Zhiyi's, while Zhili of the home-mountain line defended the longer version and its contemplation of mind in his Supplementary Record (Shiyi ji, T1784) and related writings. The translation renders the section like the rest of the text and takes no position.

Golden Light by the six identities. The stages "the Golden Light in name," "in contemplative practice," "in resemblance," "in a portion of the truth," and "the ultimate Golden Light" (with "the Golden Light of the principle-nature" as identity in principle) apply the six identities of the site's Mohe Zhiguan. "The six identities, as before" is the text's refrain.

"Identical to the empty, identical to the provisional, identical to the middle." 即空即假即中 is rendered with "identical to" for 即 throughout, in keeping with the glossary's distinction of 即 from 具; the shorter forms 即空 / 即假 / 即中 become "identity with the empty / the provisional / the middle."

Mind-king and factors. 心王 is "the mind-king"; 心數 "mental factors" (the older term for caitasika); 慧數 "the wisdom factor." The "four aggregates" of mind are feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness.

Four movements (unit 13). 四運 (not yet thinking, about to think, thinking, having thought) uses 運 "move, convey," which is also the 運 of "conveying" in the definition of a vehicle. "This is called moving the movements by movement" (以運運運) keeps the triple wordplay; alternatively, "conveying by the conveyance of conveyance."

Sources located. "The mind is like a skilled painter…" and "Mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without distinction": Avataṃsaka (T278). "In one there is the immeasurable…; understanding their mutual arising…": Avataṃsaka (T279). "Rely on wisdom, not on consciousness": the four reliances of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T374); the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra has "rely on wisdom, not on ordinary consciousness." "Prajñā is a single dharma…" (English as in the site's Profound Meaning of the Lotus) and "If one contemplates the Buddha, prajñā, and nirvāṇa according to the Dharma…": Great Treatise (T1509). "Dharmas arising from conditions I declare to be empty": Madhyamaka-kārikā 24.18. The "new version" lines on the third body and the former two bodies: T664, "Chapter on Distinguishing the Three Bodies."

Sūtra quotations. "In the deep dharma-nature one dwells at peace…" and "If one enters this sūtra…" (Ghosts and Spirits, jinguangming-jing Part 3); "For the sake of those of dull faculties…," "Therefore this honored sūtra teaches it briefly," and "the wisdom knowing all modes is their root" (Emptiness and Introduction, Part 1); "Whoever hears it…" and "open the gate of sweet dew…" (Part 1); "The sphere of practice of the Two-Footed Honored One is also empty" (Praising the Buddha, Part 4); "I once heard that in the past, in a quiet place…" (Jalavāhana, Part 4, where the sūtra reads "a Great Vehicle Vaipulya scripture"). "Knowing existence as nonexistence, the original nature empty and still" is attributed to "Praising the Buddha," but the phrase "original nature empty and still" occurs in the "Emptiness" chapter (Part 1).

Vimalakīrti quotations. The site's Vimalakīrti Sūtra has "the mental conduct of every being," "the root of the illness… having something to cling to," "neither real nor truly existent," "without destroying the body, still follow the single mark," and "I do not contemplate his form, nor the suchness of his form, nor the nature of his form"; these are reused with small changes to fit Zhiyi's shorter wording. Zhiyi's text reads "without destroying the cause of the body" (不壞身因), not "the body" as in the sūtra, and his argument depends on it. The site renders 觀身實相 "the true reality of my own body"; here "the true mark" is kept because the next sentences discuss 實相.

Sources not located verbatim. The Pratyutpanna Samādhi Sūtra passage on "the suchness of my mind, the suchness of the Buddha's mind" (Zhiyi's own gloss reads 如 as "suchness"; alternatively, "my mind is like the Buddha's mind"); the long Great Sūtra passage "Not getting hold of the body…" (the short glosses inside it, such as "the eight-foot form," may be Zhiyi's own); "beings whose minds are confused by emptiness"; the Dhāraṇī [Sūtra] line on three places of prediction; the "new version" lines "Relics are bound to form…," "In the dharma-realm there is no different vehicle," and the repentance line on the four fruits.

Doubtful readings. Unit 7: 修塵共觀 is translated literally ("contemplating together with the objects in cultivation"); 為根大乘起 is read as 為根塵共起 ("from faculties [and objects] together"), since the fourfold argument needs "both" in third place. Unit 3: 利人懸解不，須株指 is read 不須株指. Unit 18: 朝會議 is read 朝會義. Unit 25 numbers sorting and sifting "second," though it is the third of the three parts announced in unit 22.

Unit 28, classification. 若安無相而時異，若入會三而未別案 is punctuated here as 若入會三而未。別案下文…, "it does not yet [fit]. Examining it separately: the text below says…" "Full-word [teaching]… shared and distinct, shared and perfect" (通別通圓) could alternatively mean "extends to the distinct and extends to the perfect."

Terms of classification. "The partial, indeterminate teaching" (偏方不定教), "the miscellaneous treasury" (雜藏), and the criticism of placing the Golden Light with the Śrīmālā and Aṅgulimālīya follow the site's Profound Meaning of the Lotus, fascicle 10. The five flavors follow the glossary (curdled butter for 生酥). "Accompanied by the distinct and clarifying the perfect" uses the glossary's 帶 "accompanied."

Lotus and Samantabhadra. "This vehicle is subtle and wondrous, pure and supreme" follows the site's Profound Meaning of the Lotus. "The Tathāgata will before long enter parinirvāṇa" corresponds to "The Jeweled Stūpa" chapter of the site's Lotus Sūtra, where the sūtra reads 入涅槃 rather than 般涅槃; "shall enter parinirvāṇa" is from the opening of the Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra (this round's draft: "Three months from now I shall enter parinirvāṇa").

Essence as rites. "Rites are essence" (禮者體也) is a standard gloss in Han and later commentaries on the Record of Rites; "the writers of the classics" (書家) is literal.

Other terms. 和光 "dims its light" alludes to Laozi 4 and 56 (和其光，同其塵); 諸使 "the drives" are the latent afflictions; 冤鳥 "enemy-bird of the Mahāyāna" is literal and its image is unclear; 四住 "the four abodes" are the four abodes of view and habituated thought; 三無為 are the three unconditioned dharmas of the Abhidharma (space and the two cessations).
