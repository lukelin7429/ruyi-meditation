Explaining the Name: The Ten Sets of Three Dharmas

The Profound Meaning of the Golden Light Sūtra (T1783) records Zhiyi's (538–597) lectures on the title of Dharmakṣema's four-fascicle Golden Light Sūtra, written down by his disciple Guanding. Like the Profound Meaning of the Lotus, it treats the title under five headings: the name, the essence, the cardinal doctrine, the function, and the characteristics of the teaching. This first fascicle, after an opening praise and outline, covers only the first heading, Explaining the Name, in five parts: the common and the particular, translation, simile, the name according to the sūtra text, and the name obtained from the thing itself. Zhiyi rejects earlier readings that match the three characters 金, 光, 明 (gold, light, brightness) to the three virtues, to essence and function, or, with Paramārtha, to three sets of three placed one after another. Instead he lets the three characters stand for ten sets of three dharmas, from the three virtues down to the three paths. Each set contains the others, and each fully possesses permanence, bliss, self, and purity. A section of sorting and sifting then measures other schools' versions of each set against this perfect reading. The fascicle closes by arguing that the name is grounded in the sūtra's own text and, finally, in the dharma-nature itself.

Opening Praise

Chinese Source

此金光明甚深無量，太虛空界尚不喻其高廣，況山斤海滴寧得盡其邊崖？日輪赫奕，非嬰兒之所瞻仰；大舶樓櫓，豈新產者之所執持？諸佛行處，過諸菩薩所行清淨，況二乘心口安可思說？凡夫徒欲言之，言則傷其實；徒欲不言，默則致其失，二俱不可。欲以言之，言亦不可；欲以默之，默亦不可。

Ru-Yi Translation

This Golden Light is very deep and immeasurable. Even the realm of great empty space cannot illustrate its height and breadth — how much less could the mountains weighed in pounds or the sea counted in drops ever reach its bounds? The blazing disk of the sun is not something an infant can gaze up at; the towers of a great ship — how could a newborn hold them? The sphere of practice of the buddhas is beyond the pure [sphere] practiced by the bodhisattvas; how much less can the minds and mouths of the two vehicles conceive of it or speak of it? Ordinary beings may wish in vain to speak of it, but speaking harms its reality; they may wish in vain not to speak of it, but silence brings about its loss. Neither of the two will do. If one would speak of it, speaking will not do; if one would be silent about it, silence will not do.

Chinese Source

故《大品》中梵志云「非內觀故得是菩提，非外觀故得是菩提，非亦內亦外觀故得是菩提。」經言「皆不可思說。又生生不可說，生不生不可說，不生生不可說，不生不生不可說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence in the Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] the brahmacārin says: "Not by inner contemplation is this bodhi attained; not by outer contemplation is this bodhi attained; not by contemplation both inner and outer is this bodhi attained." The sūtra says: "All are inconceivable and unspeakable. Again, arising-arising is unspeakable; arising-non-arising is unspeakable; non-arising-arising is unspeakable; non-arising-non-arising is unspeakable."

Chinese Source

有因緣故亦可得說者，以金為名，名蓋眾寶之上。以法性為體，義則如來所游莊嚴菩薩深妙功德以為宗，照曜諸天心生歡喜以為用。故文號經王、教攝眾典，故唯貴為名、唯極為體、唯深為宗、唯大為用、唯王為教。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet because there are causes and conditions, it can also be spoken of. "Gold" is taken as the name: the name stands above all jewels. The dharma-nature is taken as the essence — in meaning, that which the Tathāgata roams in. Adorning the deep and wondrous merit of the bodhisattvas is taken as the cardinal doctrine. Illuminating the gods so that joy arises in their minds is taken as the function. Hence the text calls itself the king of sūtras, and its teaching gathers in all scriptures. Therefore only the noble serves as the name, only the utmost as the essence, only the deep as the cardinal doctrine, only the great as the function, and only the king as the teaching.

Chinese Source

所以不二之體常為四佛世尊之所護持，三世十方亦復如是。一切菩薩，遍他方以遙禮；樹神善女，親雨淚以稱揚。諸天覆之以天威、地祇潤之以地肥，大辯加之以辯道、功德益之以財寶，諸有悉乾枯、三塗除熱惱。舉要言之，一切世間未曾有事悉皆出現。是以金龍尊王三世讚歎，地神發願以護說者，上聖既爾，豈況人乎？敢託斯義，輒欲興言，冀涓露入海、禽鳥向山，實藉片緣同均鹹色。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore this nondual essence is always protected and upheld by the four buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, and so it is too throughout the three times and the ten directions. All the bodhisattvas, throughout other regions, bow to it from afar; the tree spirit, the good goddess, herself rains tears in praise of it. The gods cover it with heavenly majesty; the earth deity moistens it with the richness of the earth; Sarasvatī adds to it the way of eloquence; Śrī increases it with wealth and treasure; all [the rivers of] existence dry up; and the three paths are rid of burning distress. To speak of the essentials, all the things never before seen in all the world appear. Thus King Golden Dragon Honored praised it through the three times, and the earth goddess vowed to protect the one who teaches it. If the highest sages are like this, how much more should human beings be? I venture to rely on this meaning and presume to speak, hoping that, as a trickle of dew enters the sea or birds fly toward the mountain, by truly relying on this slight connection [my words] may share alike its salt and its color.

Outline of the Five Sections

Chinese Source

將釋此經，大分為二：初釋題、二釋文。

Ru-Yi Translation

In explaining this sūtra there are two main divisions: first, explaining the title; second, explaining the text.

Chinese Source

釋題為五：一釋名、二辨體、三明宗、四論用、五教相。就此五章，大分為二：初、總釋。二、別釋。總釋又二，初、生起。二、簡別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the title has five [parts]: first, Explaining the Name; second, Clarifying the Essence; third, Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine; fourth, Discussing the Function; fifth, the Characteristics of the Teaching. These five sections fall broadly into two: first, the general explanation; second, the specific explanation. The general explanation again has two: first, arising in sequence; second, sifting and distinguishing.

Chinese Source

生起者，此娑婆國土音聲為佛事，或初從善知識所聞名、或從經卷中聞名，故名在初。以聞名故，次識法體。體顯次行，行即是宗。宗成則有力，力即是用。用能益物，益物故教他。聞名是自行之始，施教是化他之初，有始有終，其唯聖人乎。五章生起，次第如此。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for arising in sequence: in this Sahā land, voice and sound do the Buddha's work. Some first hear the name from a good friend; some hear the name in sūtra scrolls. Hence the name comes first. Because one has heard the name, one next recognizes the essence of the dharma. When the essence is revealed, next comes practice, and practice is the cardinal doctrine. When the cardinal doctrine is accomplished there is power, and power is the function. The function can benefit beings, and benefiting beings is teaching others. Hearing the name is the beginning of one's own practice; bestowing the teaching is the first step in transforming others. To have both a beginning and an end — is this not the sage alone? The arising in sequence of the five sections is in this order.

Chinese Source

簡別者，簡是料簡也。問：若略則唯一，若廣則無量。今此五章，進不是廣、退不成略，何故五耶？答：非略非廣，非略故不一、非廣故不多，廣則令智退、略則義不周。我今處中說，今義易明了。五章中當其義如此。別者分別也。前一章總三字共為名，次三章派三字以為別，後一章兼於總別而明教相也。又顯體一章明理，餘四章明事。又前三章是因，後二章是果。又前四章是行，後一章是教。又前四章是自利行，後一章是利他行。又前四章是聖默然，後一章是聖說法。如此等種種分別料簡。今顯譬中當，分明包富，如囊中有寶不探示人，人無知者。此皆為分明中作譬也。囊中有寶，為總三字作譬；探以示人，為別三字作譬。囊中有寶，為理一章作譬；探以示人，為明事章作譬。其餘例皆可知也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for sifting and distinguishing: "sifting" (簡) means sorting and sifting (料簡). Question: If brief, it would be only one; if extensive, it would be immeasurable. These five sections, going forward, are not extensive, and going back, do not make up the brief. Why five? Answer: They are neither brief nor extensive. Not brief, hence not one; not extensive, hence not many. The extensive would make wisdom retreat; the brief would leave the meaning incomplete. "Now I speak taking the middle, so that the present meaning may be easily understood." The meaning fits the five sections in this way. "Distinguishing" (別) means differentiating (分別). The first section takes the three characters as a whole for the name; the next three sections divide the three characters to make the particulars; the last section combines the whole and the particulars and clarifies the characteristics of the teaching. Again, the one section on revealing the essence clarifies principle; the other four sections clarify phenomena. Again, the first three sections are cause; the last two are fruit. Again, the first four sections are practice; the last is teaching. Again, the first four are the practice that benefits oneself; the last is the practice that benefits others. Again, the first four are the noble silence; the last is the noble teaching of the Dharma. All such kinds of differentiating are sorting and sifting. Now, to show this with a fitting simile, clear and comprehensive: it is like a jewel in a bag — if it is not taken out and shown to people, no one knows of it. This simile is made entirely for the sake of clarity. The jewel in the bag is a simile for the three characters as a whole; taking it out to show people is a simile for the three characters taken separately. The jewel in the bag is a simile for the one section on principle; taking it out to show people is a simile for the sections clarifying phenomena. The other cases can all be known by analogy.

Explaining the Name: Common and Particular, and the Translation

Chinese Source

二、別釋者，別釋五章也。今先解釋名章。若依四卷題，但作三字，無「帝王」兩字。若依經文，有經王之義。若說若不說，俱亦無妨。今釋名為五：一通別、二翻譯、三譬喻、四附文釋、五當體釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the specific explanation: explaining the five sections one by one. We first explain the section Explaining the Name. Following the title of the four-fascicle [version], it has only three characters, without the two characters "emperor-king" (帝王). Following the text of the sūtra, there is the meaning "king of sūtras." Whether one says it or not, there is no harm either way. Explaining the Name now has five [parts]: first, the common and the particular; second, translation; third, simile; fourth, explanation by attaching to the text; fifth, explanation by the thing itself.

Chinese Source

言通別者，夫教有通別，依教明行。行有通別，從行顯理。理有通別，且置行理，但明教通別者。夫理無名字，名字名理。如虛空無丈尺，丈尺約虛空。《天王般若》云「總持無文字，文字顯總持。」若從能顯之文字，是名則通。若從能顯之所以，此名則別。云何為通？如聖所說一經、一時、一處、一部、一偈、一句、一言皆是文字，從此文字通稱為經。云何為別？別則有四：一、令世諦不亂，歡心悅耳。二、逗化所宜，開發宿善。三、對其業障，令惡滅罪除。四、點示道理，霍然妙悟。悅宜對悟，各各所以，其致不同，稱之為別。譬如鹽梅相和成種種滋味，組織交橫成種種文繡。從別所以，故有「金光明」三字，標今教異於諸教。從通文言，故有「經」之一字，眾經通稱也。今經通別合標，故言金光明經。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the common and the particular: teachings have the common and the particular, and practice is clarified on the basis of the teaching. Practice has the common and the particular, and principle is revealed through practice. Principle has the common and the particular. Setting aside practice and principle for now, let us clarify only the common and the particular of the teaching. Principle has no names or words; names and words name principle — just as empty space has no feet or yards, yet feet and yards are measured against empty space. The Prajñā [Sūtra] of the Heavenly King says: "Total retention has no written words; written words reveal total retention." If one goes by the written words that reveal, the name is common; if one goes by that by virtue of which they reveal, the name is particular. What is the common? Everything the sage teaches — a sūtra, a time, a place, a collection, a verse, a phrase, a word — is written words, and from these written words all are commonly called sūtra. What is the particular? The particular has four: first, it keeps the worldly truth from confusion, delighting the mind and pleasing the ear; second, it fits what is suitable for transformation, opening up and developing past good; third, it counters their karmic obstructions, causing evil to be extinguished and offenses removed; fourth, it points out and shows the principle of the path, so that one suddenly awakens to the wondrous. Delighting, suiting, countering, awakening — each has its own reason and their aims are not the same; this is called the particular. It is like salt and plums blending to make all kinds of flavors, or warp and weft crossing to make all kinds of embroidery. From the particular reason come the three characters "Golden Light" (金光明), marking how the present teaching differs from the other teachings; from the common written words comes the one character "sūtra," the common designation of all sūtras. The present sūtra marks the common and the particular together; hence it is called the Golden Light Sūtra.

Chinese Source

二、翻譯者，真諦三藏云「具存外國音，應言修跋拏婆頗婆欝多摩因陀羅遮閱那修多羅。修跋拏，此言金。婆頗婆，此言光。欝多摩，此言明。因陀羅，此言帝。遮閱那，此言王。修多羅，此言經。外國又稱佛陀羅，此間所無。」又略「帝王」兩字，但存三字者，漢人好略，譯者省之，但翻為金光明經也。餘師翻不及此委悉也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, translation. Tripiṭaka Paramārtha says: "Keeping the full foreign sound, it should be called suvarṇa prabhāsa uttama indra [rāja] sūtra. Suvarṇa here means 'gold'; prabhāsa here means 'light'; uttama here means 'brightness'; indra here means 'emperor'; [rāja] here means 'king'; sūtra here means 'sūtra.' Foreign lands also call it fotuoluo, for which there is nothing here." Further, the two characters "emperor-king" were dropped and only three characters kept: the people of Han like brevity, and the translator omitted them, rendering it simply as Golden Light Sūtra. Other masters' translations are not as detailed as this.

The Simile: Earlier Interpretations Rejected

Chinese Source

三、譬喻者。舊經師以三字譬三德，金譬法身、光譬般若、明譬解脫。若大師云：數論但明真應二身，若以二釋三，於論不便。若取經文，經文無一處明三德。若別作義解，何義不通，而獨譬三德？既違己論，又不會經，非今所用。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, simile. The old sūtra masters took the three characters as similes for the three virtues: gold is a simile for the Dharma-body, light for prajñā, brightness for liberation. The Great Master says: The Abhidharma treatises clarify only two bodies, the true and the response [body]; to explain three by means of two is inconvenient for those treatises. If one takes the sūtra text, nowhere does the sūtra text clarify the three virtues. If one makes a separate explanation of meaning, what meaning could not be conveyed, that it should be a simile for the three virtues alone? It both contradicts their own treatises and fails to accord with the sūtra; it is not what is used here.

Chinese Source

地人云：金質之上自有光明之能，譬於法性從體起用自有般若解說之力。但作體用二義，不須分光明異也。若大師云：地論幸明三佛，三佛釋題於義自便。而棄三身從體用者，則非論意。若取經文新舊兩本，並說三身不道體用，亦違己論復不會經。進退何之？今所不用。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Dilun masters say: On the substance of gold there is of itself the capacity of light and brightness; this is a simile for the dharma-nature giving rise to function from essence, having of itself the power of prajñā and liberation. They make only the two meanings, essence and function; there is no need to distinguish light and brightness. The Great Master says: The Treatise on the Ten Grounds fortunately clarifies three buddhas; explaining the title by the three buddhas would be naturally convenient in meaning. To discard the three bodies and follow essence and function is not the intent of the treatise. If one takes the sūtra text, both the new and the old versions teach the three bodies and do not speak of essence and function; this too contradicts their own treatise and fails to accord with the sūtra. Going forward or back, where can they go? It is not used here.

Chinese Source

真諦三藏云「三字譬三種三法：一譬三身、二譬三德、三譬三位。」譬三身者，金體真實以譬法身，光用能照以譬應身，明能遍益以譬化身。次譬三德者，金有四義：一色無變、二體無染、三轉作無礙、四令人富，金以譬法身常淨我樂四德。光有二義：一能照了、二能除闇，以譬般若照境除惑。明有二義：一無闇、二廣遠，以譬解脫，眾累永盡溥益有緣。次三位者，金性先有，如道前正因位。光融體顯，如道中了因位。明無瑕垢，如道後緣因位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tripiṭaka Paramārtha says: "The three characters are similes for three sets of three dharmas: first, a simile for the three bodies; second, for the three virtues; third, for the three stages." As a simile for the three bodies: the essence of gold is true and real, a simile for the Dharma-body; the function of light can illuminate, a simile for the response-body; brightness can benefit everywhere, a simile for the transformation-body. Next, as a simile for the three virtues: gold has four meanings — first, its color does not change; second, its substance is not stained; third, it can be turned into [things] without hindrance; fourth, it makes people rich — and gold is a simile for the four virtues of the Dharma-body: permanence, purity, self, and bliss. Light has two meanings — first, it can illuminate; second, it can remove darkness — a simile for prajñā illuminating objects and removing delusion. Brightness has two meanings — first, absence of darkness; second, breadth and reach — a simile for liberation: all encumbrances forever exhausted and benefit extended universally to those with affinity. Next, the three stages: the nature of gold is there beforehand, like the stage of the correct cause before the path. When the light melts through, the essence is revealed, like the stage of the revealing cause on the path. Brightness without flaw or stain is like the stage of the conditioning cause after the path.

Chinese Source

彼家料簡云：法身是實，二身不實。法身具四德，般若、解脫各具二德。正因是本有，了因是現有，緣因是當有。大師謂三三之釋，三義不了：一、因果不通，二、乖圓別，三、不稱法性。云何因果不通？夫三身三德本是果上圓滿之名，而今分置，三德殘缺不足。何者？若法身是道前，為是果上之法身、為是性德之法身？若是果上之法身，不應在道前；若是性德之法身，性德何獨有法身？亦應有性德之般若、性德之解脫（云云）。若言般若是道中，為是何等之般若？若是果上之般若，不應在道中。若是分得之般若，何意無分得之法身、解脫（云云）。若解脫在道後，道後眾善溥會，何獨有解脫？以是觀之，因果不通。

Ru-Yi Translation

That school sorts it out thus: The Dharma-body is real; the two [other] bodies are not real. The Dharma-body possesses the four virtues; prajñā and liberation each possess two virtues. The correct cause is originally existent; the revealing cause is presently existent; the conditioning cause is to-be existent. The Great Master says that this explanation by three sets of three is not thorough in three senses: first, cause and fruit do not connect; second, it departs from both the perfect and the distinct; third, it does not accord with the dharma-nature. How do cause and fruit not connect? The three bodies and the three virtues are originally names for what is perfected in the fruit; now they are divided and placed apart, so the three virtues are broken and insufficient. Why? If the Dharma-body is before the path, is it the Dharma-body of the fruit, or the Dharma-body of inherent virtue? If it is the Dharma-body of the fruit, it should not be before the path; if it is the Dharma-body of inherent virtue, why should inherent virtue have only the Dharma-body? There should also be the prajñā of inherent virtue and the liberation of inherent virtue (and so on). If prajñā is on the path, what kind of prajñā is it? If it is the prajñā of the fruit, it should not be on the path; if it is partially attained prajñā, why is there no partially attained Dharma-body and liberation (and so on)? If liberation is after the path — after the path all goods come together universally; why should there be liberation alone? Seen in this way, cause and fruit do not connect.

Chinese Source

乖圓別者，若作圓說，法身常樂我淨此自可知（云云）。般若與法身相冥，法身既具四德，般若寧無四德耶？解脫脫果縛故樂、脫因縛故淨、無因果縛故我、非因非果故常。圓說圓滿，無有缺減。真諦若作別說，應依此經。經云「法身是常是實」，實即我德也，應身智慧清淨即淨德也，化身三昧清淨即樂德也。三藏說法身獨具四德，二身各具二德，故皆乖圓別也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for departing from the perfect and the distinct: if it is said in the perfect way, that the Dharma-body is permanence, bliss, self, and purity is self-evident (and so on). Prajñā is merged with the Dharma-body; since the Dharma-body possesses the four virtues, how could prajñā lack the four virtues? Liberation is freed from the bond of the fruit, hence bliss; freed from the bond of the cause, hence purity; without the bonds of cause and fruit, hence self; neither cause nor fruit, hence permanence. The perfect account is perfect and complete, with nothing lacking. If Paramārtha is giving a distinct account, he should follow this sūtra. The sūtra says, "The Dharma-body is permanent and real" — "real" is the virtue of self; the response-body's "purity of wisdom" is the virtue of purity; the transformation-body's "purity of samādhi" is the virtue of bliss. The Tripiṭaka [master] says that the Dharma-body alone possesses the four virtues and the two bodies each possess two virtues; hence all of it departs from both the perfect and the distinct.

Translator's notes

Versions of the sūtra. The "four-fascicle [version]" and the "old version" are Dharmakṣema's Golden Light Sūtra (T663), translated on this site as the Golden Light Sūtra (jinguangming-jing, Parts 1–4); every quotation from it reuses that English. The "Three Bodies" chapter quoted in unit 39 is in T664, where the order is reversed (同事, 同意, 同體 for the first, second, and third bodies).

Gold, light, brightness. As a title, 金光明 is Golden Light (as in the sūtra translation). When Zhiyi splits the three characters, 金 is "gold," 光 "light," and 明 "brightness" (the sūtra's "its brightness shines" in the "Praising the Buddha" chapter). When 金光明 names the dharma-gate itself ("the Dharma-gate of Golden Light"), it is "the Golden Light."

The ten sets of three dharmas. Three virtues (Dharma-body / prajñā / liberation); Three Jewels; three nirvāṇas (nature-pure / perfect-pure / expedient-pure); three bodies (Dharma-body / reward-body / response-body; 化身 transformation-body); three great vehicles (vehicle of principle / of according / of attainment, as in the site's Profound Meaning of the Lotus); three bodhis (of true nature / of real wisdom / expedient); three prajñās (of the true mark / of contemplative illumination / expedient); three buddha-natures (correct-cause / revealing-cause / conditioning-cause); three consciousnesses (amala / ālaya / ādāna); three paths (affliction / karma / suffering). Paramārtha's transcription (unit 10). 修跋拏 suvarṇa "gold," 婆頗婆 prabhāsa "light," 欝多摩 uttama (literally "supreme," here glossed as 明), 因陀羅 indra "emperor," 遮閱那 conventionally matched to rāja "king" though the sounds do not fit well, 修多羅 sūtra. 佛陀羅 (fotuoluo) is unidentified.

Abhidharma. 數論 and 數家 are rendered "the Abhidharma treatises" and "the Abhidharma school," the usual Tiantai name for the Sarvāstivāda (and Satyasiddhi) exegetes.

People and allusions. The brahmin 旃遮 (Zhanzhe) who points to his full belly is unidentified. "The utmost jewel takes non-greed as its gold" echoes Zihan in the Zuozhuan ("I take not being greedy as my jewel"); Yang Zhen of the Later Han refused a bribe offered at night, saying, "Heaven knows, the spirits know, I know, you know" — the "four who know."

Probable copying errors. 讚歡品 (unit 38) is read as 讚歎品, the "Praise" chapter. 未詳 at the end of unit 36 ("This is not yet clear") may be an editor's note rather than part of the lecture.

Later commentary. Zhili's Supplementary Record (Jinguangming jing xuanyi shiyi ji, T1784) comments on this whole text; none of its explanations have been put into the translation.
