The Seven Great Elements and the Ten Similes

This part covers the third, fourth, and fifth of Chuandeng's eight gates. For the third gate, "unchanging yet following conditions: without difference yet different," he cites at length the Śūraṅgama Sūtra's passage on the seven great elements — earth, fire, water, wind, space, seeing, and consciousness — and comments on each in the same pattern: the sūtra first shows the element in everyday experience, then shows that it is neither combined nor uncombined, then declares that within the tathāgatagarbha "the form of the nature is true emptiness," and finally that it "appears according to karma." Chuandeng reads the "nature" of each element as inherent good and inherent evil, and its appearance in the world as cultivated good and cultivated evil; along the way he answers Mencius's image of water flowing downward. The fourth gate, "following conditions yet unchanging," is taught through ten similes, from ice and water to the warmth of spring. The fifth gate turns to practice: the single thought of an ordinary person, taken as the object of contemplation, already includes the three thousand realms, so that seeing this is "seeing the nature and becoming a buddha." The part ends by citing Zhili and the Avataṃsaka on why the mind is singled out among mind, buddha, and beings.

On Inherent Good and Evil, Fascicle Two

Chinese Source

天台山幽谿沙門傳燈著

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Chuandeng, a śramaṇa of Youxi on Mount Tiantai

Chinese Source

客曰：十界隨緣差別之事，已聞教矣。云何名為不變隨緣無差而差門？余曰：上文所明十界隨緣差別門，能隨染淨緣，遂分十法界。此中宜確論性善性惡，與夫修善修惡之旨。性善性惡者，真如不變之體也；修善修惡者，隨緣差別之用也。正以真如不變體中，具善惡二性，故隨緣時，有善惡之用。是則能隨之性，名曰善惡；所隨之緣，名曰染淨。十界善惡之性，具足五陰實法，名為正報；具足國土實法，名為依報。天台大師於此具明十界十如，一界有十如，則十界有百如，十界各各互具，成百法界，百法界互具，則千如是。具論實法一千，國土一千，假名一千，如是三千，於真如性中，無不具足。惟其性本具足，故隨染淨緣時，有正報、依報三千之事，乃全真如不變之體，而為隨緣之用，以無差別而為差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: I have received your teaching on the differentiated phenomena of the ten realms following conditions. What is meant by the gate of unchanging yet following conditions: without difference yet different? I said: The gate of the differentiation of the ten realms following conditions, made clear above, is: "It can follow defiled and pure conditions, and thus the ten dharma-realms are divided." Here we should discuss definitively the purport of inherent good and inherent evil, and of cultivated good and cultivated evil. Inherent good and inherent evil are the essence of unchanging true suchness; cultivated good and cultivated evil are the function of differentiation in following conditions. It is precisely because the two natures, good and evil, are included within the essence of unchanging true suchness that, when it follows conditions, there is the function of good and evil. Thus the nature that follows is called good and evil, and the conditions that are followed are called defiled and pure. The nature of good and evil of the ten realms, fully including the real dharmas of the five aggregates, is called the principal reward; fully including the real dharmas of lands, it is called the dependent reward. The Great Master Tiantai here made clear in full the ten suchnesses of the ten realms: one realm has ten suchnesses, so ten realms have a hundred suchnesses; the ten realms each mutually include one another, making a hundred dharma-realms; when the hundred dharma-realms mutually include one another, there are a thousand suchnesses. Discussed fully, a thousand of real dharmas, a thousand of lands, and a thousand of provisional names — these three thousand are all fully included within the nature of true suchness. Precisely because the nature originally includes them fully, when it follows defiled and pure conditions there are the three thousand phenomena of the principal and dependent rewards; this is the whole essence of unchanging true suchness becoming the function of following conditions, the undifferentiated becoming the differentiated.

Chinese Source

如是等義，具在諸大乘圓頓經中，若顯示其相，則莫過楞嚴。彼於七大文中，而具明性色真空等義，并隨眾生心應所知量隨緣之義。地、水、火、風、空五大，乃十界之依報也；見、識二大，乃十界之正報也。今為具引以發明之，使深信此宗者，有所依憑，亦以知台宗所立，深契合乎佛旨也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Meanings such as these are fully present in the perfect-and-sudden sūtras of the Mahāyāna; but for displaying their marks, nothing surpasses the Śūraṅgama. In its passage on the seven great elements, it makes clear in full such meanings as "the form of the nature is true emptiness," together with the meaning of following conditions: "following the minds of beings, in accord with the measure of what they know." Earth, water, fire, wind, and space, the five great elements, are the dependent reward of the ten realms; seeing and consciousness, the two great elements, are the principal reward of the ten realms. Now I will cite them fully to bring this out, so that those who deeply believe in this school have something to rely on, and also so that they know that what the Tiantai school establishes accords deeply with the Buddha's purport.

The Great Element of Earth

Chinese Source

先明地大

Ru-Yi Translation

First, Making Clear the Great Element of Earth

Chinese Source

不變隨緣義中，如來先約現前可見易明之事而開示之，云：汝觀地性，粗為大地，細為微塵。至鄰虗塵，柝彼極微色邊際相，七分所成。更柝鄰虗，即實空性。次明隨緣體虗，云：阿難，若此鄰虗柝成虗空，當知虗空出生色相。汝今問言，由和合故，出生世間諸變化相。汝且觀此一鄰虗塵，用幾虗空和合而有？不應隣虗合成鄰虗。又鄰虗塵柝入空者，用幾色相合成虗空？若合色時，合色非空；若空合時，合空非色。色猶可柝，空云何合？

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the meaning of unchanging yet following conditions, the Tathāgata first opens and shows it in terms of present, visible, easily understood things, saying: "Observe the nature of earth: coarse, it is the great earth; fine, it is minute dust, down to the dust bordering on emptiness — split from that ultimate particle, the boundary mark of form, which is made of seven parts. Split further the [dust] bordering on emptiness, and it is truly the nature of emptiness." Next, making clear that in following conditions it is empty in essence, he says: "Ānanda, if this [dust] bordering on emptiness, when split, becomes empty space, know that empty space gives birth to the marks of form. You now ask whether, through combination, the various transforming marks of the world are produced. Look now at this one particle of dust bordering on emptiness: how much empty space is combined to make it? It should not be that [particles] bordering on emptiness combine to form [a particle] bordering on emptiness. Again, when a particle bordering on emptiness is split into emptiness, how many marks of form combine to make empty space? If form is combined, combined form is not emptiness; if emptiness is combined, combined emptiness is not form. Form can still be split, but how can emptiness be combined?"

Chinese Source

三明真如性具之理，云：汝元不知如來藏中，性色真空，性空真色，清淨本然，周遍法界。如來藏，即真如不變之體也。性色真空，性空真色，此指真如不變體中，所具一分地大，所對一分空大者為言。文言性者，對相而言。蓋有性色，有相色，有性空，有相空。相色相空，乃十法界隨染淨緣造成。色之與空，即修善修惡也。今指性善性惡之色空，而此色空在一性中，體恒不二，亦無差別，與所具三德之性，體亦不二，無有差別，故曰清淨本然，周遍法界。清淨即地大中般若德，亦名真諦；本然即地大中解脫德，亦名俗諦；周遍法界即地大中法身德，亦名中道第一義諦。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, making clear the principle of inherent inclusion in true suchness, he says: "You do not know at all that within the tathāgatagarbha, the form of the nature is true emptiness, the emptiness of the nature is true form, pure and originally so, pervading the dharma-realm." The tathāgatagarbha is the essence of unchanging true suchness. "The form of the nature is true emptiness; the emptiness of the nature is true form": this is said with reference to the portion of the great element of earth included in the essence of unchanging true suchness, and the portion of the great element of space set over against it. Where the text says "nature," it is said in contrast to marks. For there is form of the nature and form of marks, emptiness of the nature and emptiness of marks. Form of marks and emptiness of marks are created by the ten dharma-realms following defiled and pure conditions; this form and emptiness are cultivated good and cultivated evil. Here it refers to the form and emptiness of inherent good and inherent evil; and this form and emptiness, within the one nature, are in essence always nondual and without differentiation, and with the nature of the three virtues included [in it] they are in essence also nondual and without differentiation. Hence it says "pure and originally so, pervading the dharma-realm." "Pure" is the virtue of prajñā within the great element of earth, also called the truth of the real; "originally so" is the virtue of liberation within the great element of earth, also called the conventional truth; "pervading the dharma-realm" is the virtue of the Dharma-body within the great element of earth, also called the middle way, the truth of the supreme meaning.

Chinese Source

四明隨緣之義，云：隨眾生心，應所知量，循業發現。與華嚴能隨染淨緣，具造十法界義同。所隨之緣，有心有業。心即十法界染淨二心，業即十法界善惡二業。佛界三智之心名淨心，九界三惑之心名染心。佛界順性之行名善業，九界逆性之行名惡業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, making clear the meaning of following conditions, he says: "Following the minds of beings, in accord with the measure of what they know, it appears according to karma." This has the same meaning as the Avataṃsaka's "it can follow defiled and pure conditions, fully creating the ten dharma-realms." The conditions followed include mind and karma: the mind is the two minds, defiled and pure, of the ten dharma-realms; the karma is the two kinds of karma, good and evil, of the ten dharma-realms. The mind of the three wisdoms in the buddha-realm is called the pure mind; the mind of the three delusions in the nine realms is called the defiled mind. The conduct in the buddha-realm that accords with the nature is called good karma; the conduct in the nine realms that goes against the nature is called evil karma.

Chinese Source

謂真如不變佛界性善之體，能隨佛界淨心，造佛界修善之色。約其人，則持地菩薩是也。約正報，即法身、報身、應身、微塵相海、八萬四千相好、八十種好之色是也。約依報，即常寂光實報無障礙土之色是也。又真如不變九界性惡之體，能隨九界染心，造九界修惡之色。約正報，即三聖變易生死之色，六道分段生死之色是也。約依報，即寔報無障礙土、方便有餘土、凡聖同居土之色是也。至於極惡，則阿鼻地獄依報正報，莫非其相。是則今人道中所有正報六尺之軀，所稟地大之相依報山河國土，凡有質礙之色英，非從真如不變所具性惡之色，自無始隨無明塵沙見思三惑染心，造人天惡業之所招報。故曰：隨眾生心，應所知量，循業發現。心即華嚴所謂染心之緣，業即人天所造五戒十善之業。此之心業，比三途惡道，名為淨心善業。若望出世間四諦十二因緣，皆染心惡業，矧佛法界三智淨心六度善業哉。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, the essence of the inherent good of the buddha-realm in unchanging true suchness can follow the pure mind of the buddha-realm and create the form of the cultivated good of the buddha-realm. In terms of the person, it is Bodhisattva Dharaṇiṃdhara. In terms of the principal reward, it is the form of the Dharma-body, the reward-body, and the response-body, the ocean of marks as many as motes of dust, the eighty-four thousand marks and excellences, and the eighty minor features. In terms of the dependent reward, it is the form of the land of eternal tranquil light and the land of true reward free of obstruction. Again, the essence of the inherent evil of the nine realms in unchanging true suchness can follow the defiled mind of the nine realms and create the form of the cultivated evil of the nine realms. In terms of the principal reward, it is the form of the birth-and-death of change of the three [kinds of] sages, and the form of the segmented birth-and-death of the six paths. In terms of the dependent reward, it is the form of the land of true reward free of obstruction, the land of expedients with residue, and the land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together. As for the extreme of evil, the dependent and principal rewards of the Avīci hell are all its marks. Thus the six-foot body of the principal reward that people now have in the human path, the marks of the great element of earth they receive, and the mountains, rivers, and lands of the dependent reward — every form with solid obstruction — none is anything but the retribution brought about by the form of inherent evil included in unchanging true suchness, which from beginningless time has followed the defiled mind of the three delusions — ignorance, dust-and-sand, and views and habituated thought — to create the evil karma of humans and gods. Hence it says: "Following the minds of beings, in accord with the measure of what they know, it appears according to karma." The mind is the condition of the defiled mind spoken of in the Avataṃsaka; the karma is the karma of the five precepts and the ten good deeds created by humans and gods. This mind and karma, compared with the three evil paths, are called the pure mind and good karma; but viewed from the world-transcending four truths and twelve causes and conditions, they are all defiled mind and evil karma — how much more so [viewed from] the pure mind of the three wisdoms and the good karma of the six perfections in the buddha dharma-realm!

The Great Element of Fire

Chinese Source

次明火大

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, Making Clear the Great Element of Fire

Chinese Source

不變隨緣義中，如來先約現前可見易明之事而開示云：火性無我，寄於諸緣。汝觀城中未食之家，欲炊爨時，手執陽燧，日前求火。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the meaning of unchanging yet following conditions, the Tathāgata first opens and shows it in terms of present, visible, easily understood things, saying: "The nature of fire has no self; it lodges in various conditions. Observe the households in the city that have not yet eaten: when they want to cook, they hold up a burning-mirror before the sun to seek fire."

Chinese Source

次明隨緣其體本虗云：阿難，名和合者，如我與汝一千二百五十比丘，今為一眾。眾雖為一，詰其根本，各各有身，皆有所生氏族名字。如舍利弗，婆羅門種；優樓頻羅，迦葉波種；乃至阿難，瞿曇種姓。阿難，若此火性因和合有，彼手執鏡於日求火，此火為從鏡中而出？為從艾出？為從日來？阿難，若日來者，自能燒汝手中之艾，來處林木皆應受焚。若鏡中出，自能於鏡出然於艾，鏡何不鎔？紆汝手執，尚無熱相，云何融泮？若生於艾，何藉日鏡光明相接，然後火生？汝又諦觀，鏡因手執，日從天來，艾本地生，火從何方遊歷於此？日鏡相遠，非和非合，不應火光無從自有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear that in following conditions its essence is fundamentally empty, he says: "Ānanda, as for what is called combination: just as I and you and the twelve hundred fifty bhikṣus now form one assembly — though the assembly is one, if we inquire into its root, each has his own body, each has the clan and name into which he was born: Śāriputra of the brahmin caste, Uruvilvā of the Kāśyapa clan, and so on to Ānanda of the Gautama clan. Ānanda, if this nature of fire existed through combination, when that hand holds the mirror to seek fire from the sun, does this fire come out of the mirror? Does it come out of the mugwort? Or does it come from the sun? Ānanda, if it came from the sun, it would of itself burn the mugwort in your hand, and the forests of the places it came through should all be burned. If it came out of the mirror, it would of itself come out of the mirror and ignite the mugwort; why does the mirror not melt? Even your hand holding it has no mark of heat; how would [the mirror] melt? If it were born from the mugwort, why would it depend on the light of sun and mirror meeting before fire is born? Look carefully further: the mirror is held in the hand, the sun comes from the sky, the mugwort grows from the earth — from which direction does fire travel to arrive here? Sun and mirror are far apart, neither mixed nor combined; it should not be that the light of fire exists of itself without any source."

Chinese Source

三明真如性具之理云：汝猶不知如來藏中，性火真空，性空真火，清淨本然，周遍法界。如來藏，即真如不變之體也。性火真空，性空真火，此指真如不變體中，所具一分火大，所對一分空大者為言。文言性者，亦對相而說。盖有性火，有相火，有性空，有相空。相大相空，乃十法界隨染淨緣造成。空之與火，即修善修惡也。今指性善性惡，火之與空，此火即真空，此空即真火。是則性火即真空，性空即真火。故往復言之，而曰性火真空，性空真火。而此空火，在一性中，體恒不二，亦無差別。即與所具三德，體亦不二，亦無差別。故曰清淨本然，周遍法界。清淨即火大中般若德，亦名真諦。本然即火大中解脫德，亦名俗諦。周遍法界，即火大中法身德，亦名中道第一義諦。既曰清淨，則此火大，在一性中，本無汙染者也。既曰本然，則此火大，在一性中，本非造作者也。既曰周遍法界，則此火大，在一性中，本來絕待者也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, making clear the principle of inherent inclusion in true suchness, he says: "You still do not know that within the tathāgatagarbha, the fire of the nature is true emptiness, the emptiness of the nature is true fire, pure and originally so, pervading the dharma-realm." The tathāgatagarbha is the essence of unchanging true suchness. "The fire of the nature is true emptiness; the emptiness of the nature is true fire": this is said with reference to the portion of the great element of fire included in the essence of unchanging true suchness, and the portion of the great element of space set over against it. Where the text says "nature," this too is said in contrast to marks. For there is fire of the nature and fire of marks, emptiness of the nature and emptiness of marks. The great [element] of marks and emptiness of marks are created by the ten dharma-realms following defiled and pure conditions; this emptiness and fire are cultivated good and cultivated evil. Here it refers to inherent good and inherent evil, fire and emptiness: this fire is true emptiness, and this emptiness is true fire. Thus the fire of the nature is identical to true emptiness, and the emptiness of the nature is identical to true fire; hence it is stated back and forth: "the fire of the nature is true emptiness, the emptiness of the nature is true fire." And this emptiness and fire, within the one nature, are in essence always nondual and without differentiation; and with the three virtues included [in it] they are in essence also nondual and without differentiation. Hence it says "pure and originally so, pervading the dharma-realm." "Pure" is the virtue of prajñā within the great element of fire, also called the truth of the real. "Originally so" is the virtue of liberation within the great element of fire, also called the conventional truth. "Pervading the dharma-realm" is the virtue of the Dharma-body within the great element of fire, also called the middle way, the truth of the supreme meaning. Since it says "pure," this great element of fire, within the one nature, is fundamentally without defilement. Since it says "originally so," this great element of fire, within the one nature, is fundamentally not fabricated. Since it says "pervading the dharma-realm," this great element of fire, within the one nature, is originally absolute.

Chinese Source

四明不變隨緣之義，云隨眾生心，應所知量，循業發現。與華嚴能隨染淨緣，具造十法界義同。所隨有心有業，心即十法界染淨一心，業即十法界善惡二行。佛界三智之心名淨心，九界三惑之心名染心。佛界順性之行名善業，九界逆性之行名惡業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, making clear the meaning of unchanging yet following conditions, he says: "Following the minds of beings, in accord with the measure of what they know, it appears according to karma." This has the same meaning as the Avataṃsaka's "it can follow defiled and pure conditions, fully creating the ten dharma-realms." What is followed includes mind and karma: the mind is the single mind, defiled or pure, of the ten dharma-realms; the karma is the two kinds of conduct, good and evil, of the ten dharma-realms. The mind of the three wisdoms in the buddha-realm is called the pure mind; the mind of the three delusions in the nine realms is called the defiled mind. The conduct in the buddha-realm that accords with the nature is called good karma; the conduct in the nine realms that goes against the nature is called evil karma.

Chinese Source

謂真如不變，佛界性善，能隨淨心善業之緣，造佛界修善之火。約其人，則烏芻瑟摩是也。約正報，則佛之慧日光明圓照法界，日月諸天戢歛重曜。約依報，則無量香雲為光明臺，七寶樓閤常有光明，皆其事也。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, unchanging true suchness, the inherent good of the buddha-realm, can follow the conditions of pure mind and good karma and create the fire of the cultivated good of the buddha-realm. In terms of the person, it is Ucchuṣma. In terms of the principal reward, it is the sun of the Buddha's wisdom, whose light perfectly illuminates the dharma-realm, so that the sun, the moon, and the gods withdraw their manifold brilliance. In terms of the dependent reward, immeasurable clouds of incense form terraces of light, and the towers of the seven jewels are always luminous — these are such things.

Chinese Source

又真如不變，九界性惡，能隨染心惡業之緣，造九界修惡之火。約正報，則身中諸冷暖氣發而為煩惱火，氣息如烟，為障熱，為痟渴。約依報，則日月光明，林木烟燄。至於修惡之極，則火龍、火蛇、火麟、火鼠、諸餓鬼等，受氣猛火常被燒然。至於八熱地獄，上火徹下，下火徹上，東西交徹，歷劫燒然。依報，則極至於劫火所燒，上至初禪世界，所有悉為煨燼。莫非如來藏真如不變，性善性惡，能隨染淨緣所造之火，故曰隨眾生心應所知量。單心不立，獨業難成。既有染淨心，必有善惡業，故曰循業發現。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, unchanging true suchness, the inherent evil of the nine realms, can follow the conditions of defiled mind and evil karma and create the fire of the cultivated evil of the nine realms. In terms of the principal reward, the cold and warm vapors within the body arise as the fire of afflictions; the breath is like smoke, causing fevers and wasting thirst. Ru-Yi Translation

The guest asked further: The earlier gate of unchanging true suchness following conditions already discussed fully the two rewards, dependent and principal, of the ten realms. So the body, form, and the dependent and principal [rewards] are all inherently included, and in following conditions there is nothing not pervasively created, and the purport of following conditions yet unchanging can be discussed for all of them. Now you speak only of the causal mind originally including, discussing only the mind and not the body, form, and the dependent and principal [rewards]. Why? I replied: The perfect school of inherent inclusion of the Tiantai house holds precisely that form and mind, dependent and principal, are all inherently included; hence in following conditions there is nothing not pervasively created, and the form and mind, dependent and principal, that are created are all, in their very substance, following conditions yet unchanging. Hence the patriarch Siming says: "Further, you should understand: the inconceivable essence of the perfectly interfused dharma-realm makes up our single thought of mind, and likewise with its whole essence makes up beings and buddhas, the dependent and the principal, faculties and objects. A single mind, a single mote of dust, down to a single ultimate particle — none is other than made by the whole essence. Since each dharma is made by the whole dharma-realm, whichever one is raised is the whole portion of the perfectly interfused dharma-realm. Since it is the whole dharma-realm, what single dharma is there that does not include all dharmas?" Hence we know that the three virtues of the dharma-realm are not large, nor are the three virtues of a single thought small; the three virtues of the ocean of lands are not many, nor are the three virtues of a single mote of dust few.

Hence the Avataṃsaka says: "The mind is like a skilled painter, creating the various five aggregates; in all the world, there is nothing not created by the mind. As the mind, so the Buddha; as the Buddha, so beings; mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without distinction." Our present school explains the "wondrous Dharma" of the Lotus Sūtra's title precisely on this purport: "Dharma" is the three dharmas of mind, buddha, and beings; "wondrous" is that the three are without distinction. But although all three are without difference, in terms of what is near and essential, nothing is more essential than the mind. Hence the Great Master Tiantai said: "The Buddha-dharma is too lofty and [the dharma of] beings too vast; for the initial mind they are difficult. The three are without distinction; contemplating the mind is easy." Now, speaking in terms of what is near and essential for people of the world, I point solely to the mind-dharma and do not discuss the body, form, and the dependent and principal [rewards] in full. Hence we know that the form and mind, dependent and principal, the single thought and the single mote of dust of sentient beings lack not a hair of the essence of unchanging true suchness.

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

Śūraṅgama, the seven great elements (units 5–17): quoted from fascicle 3 of the Śūraṅgama Sūtra (T945); the site has no translation, so the English is new. Chuandeng splits each passage into four or five steps (showing the element, its emptiness in following conditions, inherent inclusion, following conditions). His readings 汝宛不知 (wind), 白月畫 (for 晝), and 鉢頭摩訶薩 (unit 29, dropping 多) differ slightly from T945; the English follows the sense of the sūtra.

"The form of the nature is true emptiness" (性色真空): translated as a statement ("the X of the nature is true emptiness, the emptiness of the nature is true X") because Chuandeng glosses it as 性火即真空 "the fire of the nature is identical to true emptiness." 相 in these comments is "marks" (form of marks, fire of marks).

Persons of each element (units 5–13): Dharaṇiṃdhara (持地), Ucchuṣma (烏芻瑟摩), the youth Moonlight and the Buddha Water-Deva (月光童子、水天), Ākāśagarbha (虛空藏) all come from the Śūraṅgama's twenty-five perfect penetrations (fasc. 5). 然燈如來流□元法王子 is corrupt; the Śūraṅgama's wind practitioner is Vaiḍūryaprabha (瑠璃光法王子, who learned from the Buddha Wuliangsheng), so 流□元 is probably 瑠璃光. The pairing with Dīpaṃkara is the text's own.

Pure Land descriptions (units 9, 11): the water rising "from below upward … among the flowers … fourteen channels" follows the site's Contemplation Sūtra (fifth contemplation); the text has 流淚 for 流注 and 一四 for 十四. "Like a hundred thousand kinds of music … mindful of the Saṅgha" follows the site's Amitābha Sūtra. 也流入德 is probably 八功德 "the eight virtues"; rendered "[the water of] the eight virtues, flowing in."

Mencius (unit 9): Mencius 6A2 (water tending downward; forced water staying on a mountain) and 2A2 (flood-like qi); translated here. In this book Mencius's 性善 is always "human nature is good," never "inherent good."

Seeing (unit 15): "His compassionate eyes gazing upon every being …" is the site's Lotus Sūtra, "Universal Gate" verses. The frightened dove at peace in the Buddha's [shadow] but trembling in Śāriputra's is a story from the Great Treatise (fascicle not checked); the text says 佛眼 "the Buddha's eye," probably for 佛影 "shadow."

Ten similes (units 19–39): ice and water, the non-combination passage — Śūraṅgama, fasc. 3 (the "ancient" commentator quoted twice is not located); vessels and space, bubble and ocean, "turning things, one is the same as the Tathāgata" — fasc. 2. "Merges the myriad things to make [them oneself]" (會萬物以為也) is probably Sengzhao's 會萬物以為己. "Still and unmoving, yet when stimulated it then penetrates" is the Book of Changes, "Appended Statements." "The gentleman is not a vessel" is Analects 2.12. 陽春 is rendered "the yang of spring" to keep the yin–yang contrast with 太陰 "the great yin."

Gate five (unit 40): the ten suchnesses of the human realm follow Tiantai usage (site Lotus list: mark, nature, embodiment, power, activity, cause, condition, effect, recompense, ultimate equality of beginning and end). 有界千如 is probably 百界千如; 非即性成性 probably 論性. 無諦／有諦 "the truth of nonexistence / of existence" are kept distinct from the usual 空諦／假諦.

Zhili and the Annotations (unit 41): "Further, you should understand…" is Zhili's Miaozong chao (T1751), English from the round-two Miaozong chao p1 (Chuandeng omits 法界 before 全體 and writes 一法 for 一物). The sentence on the three virtues of the dharma-realm "not large" is not in that passage; source not located. "The Buddha-dharma is too lofty…" follows the round-two Siming shiyi shu NOTES (Zhanran's Annotations, here credited to "the Great Master Tiantai").

Wuwei teaching (unit 13): 無為教 is most likely the popular Ming "Teaching of Non-Action" of Luo Qing, criticized by Buddhist monks of the period; it is not the Buddhist "unconditioned." 晦昧之空 "the emptiness of dim obscurity" echoes the Śūraṅgama (晦昧為空).

Copying errors and CBETA emendations: 相大 (unit 7) is evidently 相火 "fire of marks" (rendered "the great [element] of marks"); 染淨一心 (unit 7) where the parallel passages have 二心; unit 13 contains CBETA's bracketed emendations 〔立〕明〔下〕變 = 五明不變, translated "Fifth, making clear the meaning of unchanging…"; unit 17 「蓋有性識性，覺相識，相覺相，識相覺」 is garbled and translated by the parallel pattern; 滿世開起 (unit 19) for 滿世間起; 嚴塞 (unit 21) for 嚴寒; 空水之喻 (unit 29) for 月水之喻; 隨縱變造 (unit 31) for 隨緣變造; 比陽春 (unit 39) read as 此.
