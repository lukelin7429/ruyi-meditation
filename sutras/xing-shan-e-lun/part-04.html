Further Accounts of Inherent Evil and the Seventh Gate

This part continues the accounts of the "dharma-gate of inherent evil." Two more stories of advanced bodhisattvas complete the level of "a portion of the truth": Mañjuśrī binds Māra and makes him preach in the form of a buddha (Sūtra of Māra's Opposition), and the bodhisattva Unstained in Māra's Realm frees Māra and his heavenly maidens (Śūraṅgama Samādhi Sūtra), ending with the teaching that the suchness of Māra's realm is the suchness of the buddha-realm. The account of the ghost Burning Face is placed here too. The level of "resemblance" follows, with arhats and later masters who defeat opponents by superior power: Śāriputra and the six teachers, Maudgalyāyana and the two nāga kings, Upagupta and Māra, a seven-year-old novice before King Aśoka, the Han-dynasty contest between Buddhist monks and Daoists, Huisi's dealings with the Daoists who accused him, and Zhiyi's trials on Huading and Mount Yuquan, where Guan Yu takes the precepts. A guest then asks whether the punitive wars of the Confucian sage-kings are also inherent evil; Chuandeng answers that they are cultivated good, while inherent evil is something only the buddhas use. Fascicle Five then opens the seventh gate, perfect cultivation following pure conditions through Zhili, the Vimalakīrti, and the Perfect Awakening. The section on "contemplative practice" announced in Part 3 does not appear.

Ru-Yi Translation

The Sūtra of Māra's Opposition says: The bodhisattva Mañjuśrī expounded the great Dharma for the divine son Great Light. The divine son, having heard it, replied that it was hard to equal (and so on), up to: "Never before! Mañjuśrī, what you now teach is a subtle, lofty, limitless wisdom, surpassing in this way. And yet Māra Pāpīyas does not come here, wishing to disrupt it and act in opposition?" Hardly had he said this when, before long, Māra Pāpīyas raised great clouds and rain in the sky and sent forth a great sound, which was heard everywhere by all. Everyone in the assembly thought: "What sound is this, overflowing so?" At that time the World-Honored One said to Mañjuśrī: "Do you see the disturbance Māra has raised?" He answered: "Yes, God of gods." Mañjuśrī then reflected in a samādhi suited to the case, and caused Māra to be bound of himself in five places. He at once fell to the ground, crying out and complaining, raging and cursing: "Mañjuśrī, now you will put my body in fetters, shackles, and chains!" Mañjuśrī answered: "Tut, Māra Pāpīyas! There are other bonds, firm and hard to undo, that go beyond these; what you are now bound by — you seem not to be aware of it. What are the firm bonds? The inversions of I and self, the bonds of affection and wrong views, the bindings of causes and conditions. You are constantly bound by these cangues and chains and are not aware of it yourself."

魔又啟曰：惟見原赦，使得解脫。文殊答曰：汝當興造行作佛事，我能令卿從縛得脫。魔即對曰：我於佛法無所妨廢，亦無所壞。當以何因興作佛事？文殊答曰：波旬！欲知興作佛事，乃為菩薩智慧變化。假使如來興作佛事，不足為難。魔作佛事，斯乃為奇。於是文殊師利即如其像三昧思惟，使魔波旬變作佛像，三十二相莊嚴其身，坐師子床，智慧辨才，所說如佛，而宣此言：所欲問者，一切眾生諸所狐疑，自恣所啟，當為發遣。

Māra petitioned again: "Only let me be pardoned, so that I may be released." Mañjuśrī answered: "You should rise up and do the work of a buddha; then I can release you from your bonds." Māra replied: "I have not obstructed or abolished the Buddha-dharma, nor destroyed anything. For what reason should I do the work of a buddha?" Mañjuśrī answered: "Pāpīyas, if you wish to know about doing the work of a buddha: it is the transformation of a bodhisattva's wisdom. If a tathāgata does the work of a buddha, that is no hard thing; for Māra to do the work of a buddha — that is a wonder." Then Mañjuśrī reflected in a samādhi suited to the case, and caused Māra Pāpīyas to transform into the image of a buddha, his body adorned with the thirty-two marks, seated on a lion throne, with wisdom and eloquence, speaking like a buddha and declaring: "Whatever you wish to ask — all the doubts of all beings — ask freely, and I will resolve them for you."

時大迦葉問魔波旬：比丘修行，以何為縛？魔尋答曰：計我禪定而志寂然，則是有想。無想品第。想空為要，毀眾見想；想於要想；興於眾念，御無願想；壞諸所願，為泥洹想；而樂無為，毀生死想。是迦葉修行比丘之繫也。所以者何？迦葉當知，不當毀壞諸所獨見因而行空也，所謂空者諸見皆空。不當毀念未於無相。所以者何？敢可所念悉為無相。不當毀願而求無願，其所願者悉亦無願。不當毀生死而求泥洹，曉了生死不可得處則為泥洹。迦葉當知，其行泥洹不起思想，當於眾著令無所起，毀壞滅盡泥洹本淨，無所起生乃為無為。說是語時，五百比丘心逮清淨。如是乃至須菩提、舍利弗、目揵連，其尊弟子五百人等，各各自問己身所知，時魔波旬各各分別而發之。

Then the great Kāśyapa asked Māra Pāpīyas: "For a monk who cultivates practice, what is bondage?" Māra at once answered: "To reckon 'I am in meditative concentration' and set the will on stillness — this is having thought. The stages of no-thought: thinking emptiness the essential and destroying all views — thought; thinking of the essential — thought; raising many notions and controlling them — the thought of wishlessness; destroying all wishes — the thought of nirvāṇa; delighting in the unconditioned and destroying birth and death — thought. These, Kāśyapa, are the bonds of a monk who cultivates practice. Why? Kāśyapa, you should know: one should not destroy the views one has seen alone and on that basis practice emptiness; what is called emptiness is that all views are empty. One should not destroy thoughts in [seeking] signlessness. Why? Whatever is thought is entirely signless. One should not destroy wishes and seek wishlessness; whatever is wished is also wishless. One should not destroy birth and death and seek nirvāṇa; understanding that birth and death cannot be found — that is nirvāṇa. Kāśyapa, you should know: practicing nirvāṇa, one does not give rise to thoughts and notions; amid all attachments one should let nothing arise. Destroyed and extinguished completely, nirvāṇa is originally pure; when there is no arising, that is the unconditioned." When these words were spoken, the minds of five hundred monks attained purity. In the same way Subhūti, Śāriputra, Maudgalyāyana, and the other honored disciples, five hundred in all, each asked about what he himself knew, and Māra Pāpīyas distinguished and resolved each one.

Ru-Yi Translation

Then among the hosts of gods there was a divine son named Xushen, who asked Māra Pāpīyas: "Mañjuśrī has just spoken of the works of Māra; are you, sir, able to discuss them further? What are the Māra-works of a bodhisattva?" Māra answered: "Divine son, you should know that the Māra-works of a bodhisattva are twenty. What are the twenty? Fearing birth and death and wishing to obtain liberation; following, cultivating, and practicing the Buddha's true Dharma; bowing the head and taking refuge, yet with expectant thoughts — these are Māra-works." Thus the Māra-works of a bodhisattva are twenty in all, explained fully as in the sūtra. Then the World-Honored One praised Māra Pāpīyas: "Excellent, excellent! You have spoken well of these Māra-works of bodhisattvas. If anyone discusses these matters of the Dharma, listens, examines, and practices them, and does not follow Māra's teaching, that person establishes the great way of the Buddha-dharma, and in expounding the sūtras obtains twenty things. What are the twenty? Great loving-kindness and great compassion." Thus there are twenty items in all, fully as the sūtra says. Then the divine son Xushen asked Māra Pāpīyas: "How fine, what good benefit! You have been skillfully praised by the Tathāgata." Māra replied: "My own self is not at work. 'How fine, what good benefit' — it is like a man possessed by a ghost or spirit: what he says is in reality not that man's words, but what the ghost or spirit says. Just so, sir: what I now say is set in motion by Mañjuśrī; you should not spread it about as my words."

The divine son asked: "Now that you have been transformed into the bodily form of a buddha, are you not glad? And with your body adorned with the marks and excellences, seated on a lion throne expounding sūtras and the Dharma, are you not happy?" Māra replied again: "You see me adorned with the marks and excellences; I, looking at myself, [see only] cangues, chains, and bonds." The divine son said: "Pāpīyas, take refuge, repent, and confess your offense. Mañjuśrī! Mañjuśrī, by your majestic spiritual power pardon Pāpīyas." Pāpīyas answered: "One should not confess and repent to one who studies the Mahāyāna, a great bodhisattva. Why? Those who practice the bodhisattva way do not see a realm of flaws, leaks, blemishes, or defilements over which to give rise to anger; [only] if they had destroyed [someone] in resentment would one have to confess, repent, bow, and take refuge." The divine son further asked: "Of what kinds is a bodhisattva's patience?" Māra replied: "A bodhisattva's patience has twelve things. What are the twelve? Patience in one's will and nature, without flaw." Thus there are twelve kinds, as the sūtra explains in full. Then the divine son Xushen asked Māra Pāpīyas: "If you go on to speak of these twenty things up to the twelve kinds of patience, are you not elated?" He answered: "I rejoice."

Ru-Yi Translation

Xushen then petitioned Mañjuśrī: "Sir, pardon the offense of Māra Pāpīyas." Mañjuśrī asked Māra: "Who bound you?" He replied: "I do not know. Who bound me?" [Mañjuśrī] answered: "Pāpīyas, you are not bound; you think yourself bound. All foolish ordinary people are also like this: the mind is originally entirely pure, without thinking, yet their will is set on conceptual attachment. Not knowing, they conceive the impermanent as permanent, suffering as pleasure, what is not a self as a self, the impure as pure; where there is no form they conceive form; where there is no feeling, perception, formations, or consciousness, they conceive the five aggregates [of] birth and death. As now, Pāpīyas, you dread your bonds — how can you be released?" He said further: "Now can I not be released again?" [Mañjuśrī] answered: "Just so, Pāpīyas: one who is already released is not released again. How is one released? Bondage comes about through false thoughts; to remove this defilement is called liberation." Then Mañjuśrī withdrew the majestic stirring he had set up, and Māra Pāpīyas was at once as before. Mañjuśrī further taught the Dharma for the divine son Xushen, and when it was about to end, Māra Pāpīyas, his heart full of grief, wept like rain and said: "Wherever this sūtra circulates, the Māra Pāpīyases will find no opening; if anyone upholds it, the works of Māra will be cut off." Having said this, Māra at once vanished.

The Account of the Bodhisattva Unstained in Māra's Realm Transforming Māra

首楞嚴三昧經云：舍利弗白佛言：世尊，未曾有也。今說首楞嚴三昧，而是惡魔不來嬈亂。佛告舍利弗：汝欲見魔衰惱事不？唯然，已見。爾時佛放眉間白毫相光，一切眾會皆見惡魔被五繫縛，不能自解。佛告舍利弗：汝見惡魔被五縛不？唯然，已見。此惡魔者，為誰所縛？佛言：是首楞嚴三昧威神之力。爾時會中有一菩薩，名魔界行不汙，白佛言：唯然，世尊。我今當現於魔界中，自在神力，令魔得住首楞嚴三昧。佛言：隨意。時魔界行不汙菩薩即於會中忽然不現，現於魔宮，語惡魔言：汝寧不聞佛說首楞嚴三昧，無量眾生皆發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，出汝境界。亦復當度脫餘人，出汝境界。魔即報言：我聞佛說首楞嚴三昧名字，以被五縛，不能得往，所謂兩手、兩足及頭。又問惡魔：誰繫汝者？魔即答言：我適發心，欲往壞聽受首楞嚴三昧者，即被五縛。我適復念：諸佛菩薩大有威德，難可壞亂。我若往者，或當自壞，不如自住於此宮殿。作是念已，即於五縛而得解脫。菩薩答言：如是。一切凡夫憶想分別，顛倒取相，是故有縛。動念戲論，是故有縛。見聞覺知，是故有縛。此中實無縛者、解者。所以者何？諸法無縛，本解脫；諸法無縛，本無脫故，常解脫相。世問愚癡，如來以此法門說法。若有眾生得知此義，欲求解脫，勤心精進，則於諸縛而得解脫。

The Śūraṅgama Samādhi Sūtra says: Śāriputra said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, this has never been before! Now the Śūraṅgama samādhi is being taught, and yet the evil Māra does not come to disturb it." The Buddha said to Śāriputra: "Do you wish to see Māra's ruin and distress?" "Yes, I have seen it." At that time the Buddha emitted light from the white tuft between his eyebrows, and the whole assembly saw the evil Māra bound by five bonds, unable to free himself. The Buddha said to Śāriputra: "Do you see the evil Māra bound by five bonds?" "Yes, I have seen it. By whom is this evil Māra bound?" The Buddha said: "It is the majestic spiritual power of the Śūraṅgama samādhi." At that time there was in the assembly a bodhisattva named Unstained in Māra's Realm, who said to the Buddha: "Yes, World-Honored One. I will now appear in Māra's realm with sovereign spiritual power and cause Māra to abide in the Śūraṅgama samādhi." The Buddha said: "As you wish." Then the bodhisattva Unstained in Māra's Realm suddenly disappeared from the assembly and appeared in Māra's palace, and said to the evil Māra: "Have you not heard that the Buddha is teaching the Śūraṅgama samādhi, and countless beings have all given rise to the mind of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening and gone out of your realm? He will also deliver and free others, [so that they too go] out of your realm." Māra replied: "I heard the Buddha teach the name of the Śūraṅgama samādhi, and since I am bound by five bonds, I cannot go — namely, my two hands, my two feet, and my head." [The bodhisattva] further asked the evil Māra: "Who bound you?" Māra answered: "As soon as I resolved to go and ruin those who were listening to and receiving the Śūraṅgama samādhi, I was bound by five bonds. Then I thought again: 'The buddhas and bodhisattvas have great majestic virtue and are hard to disrupt. If I go, I may ruin myself; better to stay in this palace.' As soon as I had this thought, I was released from the five bonds." The bodhisattva answered: "Just so. All ordinary people have bonds because of recollection, thought, and discrimination, and inverted grasping at marks; they have bonds because of moving thoughts and frivolous discourse; they have bonds because of seeing, hearing, sensing, and knowing. In this there is really no one bound and no one released. Why? All dharmas are without bonds and originally liberated; all dharmas are without bonds and originally without release, and so are of the mark of constant liberation. The people of the world are foolish, and the Tathāgata teaches the Dharma by this dharma-gate. If any beings come to know this meaning and seek liberation with diligent mind and vigor, they will be released from all bonds."

客曰：誠如師引，則吾儒黃帝之戰蚩尤，成湯之放桀，伊尹之放太甲，禹王之殛防風氏，武王之伐紂，孔子之誅少正卯，獨不得稱性惡法門歟？予對曰：以逆犯順，以邪干正，謂之修惡；以順誅逆，以正袪邪，謂之修善。然則蚩尤、桀、太甲、紂、防風氏、少正卯，現世則造三途之因，以受征、放、殛、伐之華報；將來必墮三途，以受剉、燒、舂、磨之果報，皆修惡也。而聖人以大公至正（〔之道〕）征之、放之、殛之、伐之、誅之，皆人倫道中之修善也。至於歷代帝王，或以王師而破逆，或殺一夫以定天下，或殺千萬人以安百姓，皆修善也。其若中間容一毫私心以拂乎天理，或幸而有天下，或不幸而失天下，皆修惡也。或王道、伯道以兼行，仁義、詐力而雜用，則善惡為之混，邪正為之淆矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: If it is truly as you have cited, master, then in our Confucian [tradition] — the Yellow Emperor's war against Chiyou, Cheng Tang's banishment of Jie, Yi Yin's banishment of Taijia, King Yu's execution of the Fangfeng clan, King Wu's attack on Zhou, Confucius's execution of Shaozheng Mao — can these alone not be called the dharma-gate of inherent evil? I replied: To offend the compliant by the contrary and to violate the correct by the heterodox is called cultivated evil; to punish the contrary by the compliant and to expel the heterodox by the correct is called cultivated good. Thus Chiyou, Jie, Taijia, Zhou, the Fangfeng clan, and Shaozheng Mao, in their present lives, created the causes of the three [evil] paths and received the "flower retribution" of being attacked, banished, executed, and overthrown; in the future they will surely fall into the three [evil] paths and receive the fruit retribution of being chopped, burned, pounded, and ground. All this is cultivated evil. And the sages, by (the way of) great impartiality and utmost correctness, attacked them, banished them, executed them, overthrew them, and punished them — all of which is cultivated good within the way of human relations. As for the emperors and kings of successive ages, whether they defeated rebels with the royal army, or killed one man to settle the realm, or killed thousands and tens of thousands to give peace to the people, all this is cultivated good. But if in the midst they harbored a hair of selfish intent going against heavenly principle, whether they fortunately gained the realm or unfortunately lost it, all this is cultivated evil. Or if the kingly way and the hegemon's way are practiced together, and humaneness and rightness are used mixed with deceit and force, then good and evil are thereby mixed, and the heterodox and the correct are thereby confused.

Chinese Source

若夫性惡法門，譬如轉輪聖王能破一切怨敵，亦不有所破者。是故一切如來雖示現乎殺，而實未甞有所殺，不過無量劫來所修慈善根力，令一切眾生見如此事，而獲乎種種利益。矧此道理，在昔之世，如來之所未甞闡；方今之時，天台之所未甞弘，則此方儒聖烏足以知之？而況全性以行之乎？

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the dharma-gate of inherent evil, it is like a wheel-turning sage king, who can defeat all enemies yet has nothing that he defeats. Therefore all the tathāgatas, though they manifest killing, in reality have never killed anything; it is nothing but the power of the roots of goodness of loving-kindness cultivated through immeasurable eons, causing all beings to see such things and obtain benefits of every kind. Moreover, in the ages of old [when those sages lived], the Tathāgata had not yet expounded this principle, and in what was then the present, Tiantai had not yet propagated it; so how could the Confucian sages of this land have known it, much less practiced it with the whole of the nature?

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Chuandeng, a śramaṇa of Youxi on Mount Tiantai

Chinese Source

客曰：果地圓證，一無所改，已聞教矣。云何名為隨淨圓修全修在性門？余曰：前明真如隨緣十界差別門中，凡分兩義：一隨淨緣，一隨染緣。慈雲圖說，其義尚略。今欲廣明此義，故以因心本具，毫無虧欠者，重發明之。而云圓修者，對隨染九界之修，不得稱性圓修故也。又復應知九界之修，亦莫非全性起修，一一圓融。第迷了不同，故謂之圓不圓爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "The fruition ground perfectly realized, with not one thing altered" — I have heard your teaching. What is meant by the gate of perfect cultivation following pure [conditions]: the whole of cultivation lies in the nature? I said: In the gate of true suchness following conditions, with the ten realms differentiated, made clear earlier, there were two meanings in all: one, following pure conditions; the other, following defiled conditions. Ciyun's Explanation of the Diagram treats this meaning only briefly. Now, wishing to make this meaning clear at length, I bring it out once more by means of "the causal mind originally including [all], lacking not a hair." It is called perfect cultivation in contrast to the cultivation of the nine realms that follow defiled [conditions], which cannot be called perfect cultivation in accordance with the nature. Further, you should know that the cultivation of the nine realms too is nothing but cultivation arising from the whole of the nature, each fused and perfect; it is only that delusion and understanding differ, and so it is called perfect or not perfect.

Chinese Source

故四明尊者妙宗鈔釋心分垢淨，行開善惡云：心即迷了二心，行即違順二行。六道三教，迷三德性，為三惑染，故曰垢心。身口諸業，違理有作，皆名惡行。惟圓頓教，了三德性，離三惑染，方名淨心。身口諸業，順理無作，稱為善行。故知修之偏圓，行之逆順，全在了不了上分途。蓋事中九法界，乃全性惡以起修惡。事中佛法界，乃全性善以起修善。既全性起，有何一法不即佛法？猶如即波見水，即器見空，乃至即萬彚以見陽春。

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence the Venerable Siming's Wondrous Purport, explaining "the mind divides into defiled and pure; practice opens into good and evil," says: "'Mind' is the two minds of delusion and understanding; 'practice' is the two practices of going against and according with [principle]. The six paths and the three teachings are deluded about the nature of the three virtues and defiled by the three delusions; hence it says 'defiled mind.' All actions of body and speech that go against principle and are conditioned are called evil practice. Only the perfect-and-sudden teaching understands the nature of the three virtues and is free from the defilement of the three delusions; only this is called the pure mind. All actions of body and speech that accord with principle and are unconditioned are called good practice." Hence we know that the one-sidedness or perfection of cultivation, and the contrariness or compliance of practice, part their ways entirely on [the point of] understanding or not understanding. For the nine dharma-realms in phenomena give rise to cultivated evil with the whole of inherent evil; the buddha dharma-realm in phenomena gives rise to cultivated good with the whole of inherent good. Since they arise from the whole of the nature, what single dharma is not identical to the Buddha-dharma? It is like seeing water in the very waves, seeing space in the very vessels, and so on up to seeing the yang of spring in the very myriad kinds.

Chinese Source

是故諸圓頓大乘經典，即修善以觀性善者少，即修惡以觀性惡者多。如淨名云：生死即涅槃，煩惱即菩提。生死煩惱，修惡也。菩提涅槃，性惡也。修之善惡雖分，性之善惡無分。教門所謂性惡融通，無法不趣，任運攝得佛界性善是也。又云：淫怒癡即是解脫。又云：六十二見為如來種。又云：六根皆即淨土。又云：無離文字以說解脫。無行經云：貪欲即是道，瞋恚亦復然，如是三法中，具一切佛法。無非令修者不須轉側，達修惡以即性惡耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore in the perfect-and-sudden Mahāyāna sūtras, contemplating inherent good in terms of cultivated good is rare, and contemplating inherent evil in terms of cultivated evil is common. For example, the Vimalakīrti says: "Birth and death is identical to nirvāṇa; afflictions are identical to bodhi." Birth and death and afflictions are cultivated evil; bodhi and nirvāṇa are inherent evil. Although the good and evil of cultivation are divided, the good and evil of the nature are not divided. This is what the teaching calls "inherent evil fuses and penetrates, so that there is no dharma that does not tend toward it, and it effortlessly takes in the inherent good of the buddha-realm." It also says: "Lust, anger, and delusion are themselves liberation." It also says: "The sixty-two views are the seed of the Tathāgata." It also says: "The six faculties are all the pure land." It also says: "One cannot speak of liberation by departing from words." The Wuxing jing says: "Greed and desire are the path; anger is likewise; within these three dharmas all the buddha-dharmas are included." None of this is anything but enabling the practitioner, without needing to turn aside, to penetrate cultivated evil so that it is identical to inherent evil.

Chinese Source

又如圓覺楞嚴，教極圓頓，其中修門，亦無非令人即修惡以達性惡。文雖不言，旨極符契。今於圓覺，但舉綱要，而於楞嚴，乃廣引證以發明之。圓覺文殊章，明如來本起因地云：永斷無明，方成佛道。云何無明？一切眾生，從無始來，種種顛倒，妄認四大以為身相，六塵緣影為自心相。然後以奢摩他檢身歸於四大，達身無身相；檢心歸於六塵緣影，了心無心相；乃至（〔云知〕）是空華，即無輪轉。身心無明幻化，修惡也；達之本空觀，修惡即性惡也。故曰：知幻即離，不作方便；離幻即覺，亦非漸次。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, in the Perfect Awakening and the Śūraṅgama, the teaching is perfect-and-sudden to the utmost, and the gates of cultivation within them are nothing but enabling people to penetrate inherent evil in terms of cultivated evil. Though the text does not say so, the purport accords exactly. Now, for the Perfect Awakening I will only raise its main points, but for the Śūraṅgama I will cite proof at length to bring it out. The chapter of Mañjuśrī in the Perfect Awakening, making clear the Tathāgata's original causal ground, says: "Forever cutting off ignorance, one then accomplishes the way of the Buddha. What is ignorance? All beings, from beginningless time, with all kinds of inversion, mistakenly recognize the four great elements as the marks of their own bodies, and the conditioned shadows of the six sense-objects as the marks of their own minds." Then by śamatha one examines the body and returns it to the four great elements, penetrating that the body has no mark of body; one examines the mind and returns it to the conditioned shadows of the six sense-objects, understanding that the mind has no mark of mind; and so on up to "(knowing that) they are flowers in the sky, there is then no revolving [in birth and death]." The illusory transformations of body, mind, and ignorance are cultivated evil; penetrating them by the contemplation that they are originally empty is cultivated evil being identical to inherent evil. Hence it says: "Knowing illusion, one is free of it, without making expedients; free of illusion, one is awakened, also without gradual stages."

若楞嚴經二十五聖，各陳圓通，亦無非全修惡以即性惡。蓋六根、六塵、六識、七大，皆六道凡夫修惡邊事。惟三乘與偏教所觀，或即修惡分入空如來藏之性惡，以融通乎性善；或即修惡分入不空如來藏之性惡，以融通乎性善；或全入三種如來藏，即修惡觀性惡，以融通乎性善。其餘分入者，不能具引。今約烏芻瑟摩等七大菩薩全入者，以發明之，使從事圓頓者，有所適從。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the twenty-five sages in the Śūraṅgama Sūtra, each presenting his perfect penetration, these too are nothing but [taking] the whole of cultivated evil as identical to inherent evil. For the six faculties, the six sense-objects, the six consciousnesses, and the seven great elements are all matters on the side of the cultivated evil of ordinary beings of the six paths. Only, what is contemplated by the three vehicles and the one-sided teachings is either, from cultivated evil, a partial entry into the inherent evil of the empty tathāgatagarbha, so as to fuse and penetrate inherent good; or, from cultivated evil, a partial entry into the inherent evil of the not-empty tathāgatagarbha, so as to fuse and penetrate inherent good; or a whole entry into the three kinds of tathāgatagarbha, contemplating inherent evil in terms of cultivated evil, so as to fuse and penetrate inherent good. The other cases of partial entry cannot all be cited. Now, in terms of the bodhisattvas of the seven great elements, Ucchuṣma and the others, who enter wholly, I will bring it out, so that those who engage in the perfect-and-sudden have something to follow.

Translator's notes

Sūtra of Māra's Opposition (unit 4): Fo shuo moni jing (T589), Dharmarakṣa's archaic version; new English. 祖械 read as 杻械 "fetters"; 如其像三昧思惟 "reflected in a samādhi suited to the case"; Māra's answer to Kāśyapa ("To reckon 'I am in meditative concentration'…") is obscure and rendered phrase by phrase; 怯哉 read as 快哉 "how fine"; 未於無相 read as "in [seeking] signlessness"; 須深 is given in pinyin (Xushen).

Śūraṅgama Samādhi Sūtra (unit 6): Fo shuo shoulengyan sanmei jing (T642); new English. The first 唯然，已見 should be 唯然，欲見 ("Yes, I wish to see it"); the English keeps the text. 丘見 read as 正見, 世問 as 世間. 魔界行不汙 is "the bodhisattva Unstained in Māra's Realm," 堅意 "Firm Intent." "The suchness of Māra's realm is the suchness of the buddha-realm" is often cited in Tiantai (e.g., Mohe zhiguan); here it is the sūtra's own wording.
