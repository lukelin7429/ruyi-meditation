# On Inherent Good and Evil — Translation Notes

These notes record the main translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Xing shan e lun* (性善惡論, CBETA X57, no. 970 (Chuandeng)), so that its terms stay consistent across the whole text and with this site's other Tiantai translations.

## The text

- **Base text:** CBETA X0970, via the CBETA digital edition (new punctuation, 2026 release). Chuandeng died in 1628; the text is in the public domain.
- **Other English translations:** we have not used any published translation; this translation is made directly from the Chinese.
- **Coverage check:** the Chinese text on each page was compared character by character with the CBETA source to make sure nothing was skipped.

## Terms

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 性惡 | inherent evil | The Tiantai teaching that the nature includes evil as well as good; buddhas cut off cultivated evil, never inherent evil. |
| 性具善惡 | the nature includes good and evil | Chuandeng's thesis. |
| 修惡 / 修善 | cultivated evil / cultivated good | What practice cuts off or develops, as distinct from the nature. |
| 七大 | the seven great elements | From the Śūraṅgama; Parts 2 and 5. |
