Aṅgulimāla, Ajātaśatru, and the Guest's Vow

This final part gives the second half of the fifth reason for setting up "the dharma-gate of inherent evil": some beings enter the way through evil conditions, and the gate is needed to reach them. Chuandeng retells two stories at length. From the Aṅgulimāla Sūtra comes the young brahmin who, falsely accused by his teacher's wife, is told to kill a thousand people and wear their fingers as a garland; he meets the Buddha while about to kill his own mother, is converted, and then "receives" each precept by turning its meaning inside out (killing afflictions, drinking the wine of the Mahāyāna). The Buddha finally reveals him as a buddha of another world. From the Nirvāṇa Sūtra comes King Ajātaśatru, who killed his father, is cured by the moon-love samādhi, learns that his crime has no fixed nature, and gains "rootless faith," like sandalwood growing from an eraṇḍa seed. Chuandeng's comments stress that both men penetrated cultivated evil as inherent evil. The book closes with the guest summing up what he has learned, vowing to print and spread the treatise, and a one-line colophon from the disciples of Yutan Monastery who carved the blocks.

Beings Who Enter the Way through Evil Conditions

Chinese Source

二、明眾生機緣須此入道者

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, Making Clear That the Capacities and Conditions of Beings Need This to Enter the Way

Chinese Source

眾生機緣，入道不同。有從善緣入道者，此如常途所說。有從惡緣入道者，蓋由此人宿世雖有值三寶緣，而興道念，中間被惡緣牽引，而多造惡業，由此多劫墮苦，果報將盡，而宿根成熟。以惡緣故，今生仍復有種種惡事。以根熟故，得值三寶，翻邪歸正，速得悟入。今略引二緣，以證其義。

Ru-Yi Translation

The capacities and conditions by which beings enter the way are not the same. There are those who enter the way through good conditions; this is as commonly explained. There are those who enter the way through evil conditions: this is because such a person, though in past lives he had the conditions for meeting the Three Jewels and gave rise to thoughts of the way, in between was drawn along by evil conditions and committed much evil karma; because of this he fell into suffering for many eons, and when the retribution was about to be exhausted, his past roots matured. Because of the evil conditions, in this life he still has all kinds of evil deeds; because his roots have matured, he gets to meet the Three Jewels, turns from the wrong and returns to the right, and quickly attains awakening and entry. Now I briefly cite two accounts to prove the meaning.

Aṅgulimāla and His Teacher

Chinese Source

第一、央掘魔羅緣

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the Account of Aṅgulimāla

Chinese Source

央掘魔羅經云：舍衛城北去城不遠，彼處有林，名為薩那。有一貧窮婆羅門女，名䟦陀羅。女生一子，名一切世間現。少失其父，厥年十二，色力人相具足第一，聰明辨慧，微言善說。復有異村，名頗羅呵私。有一舊住婆羅門師，名摩尼跋陀羅，善能通達四毗陀經。時世間現從其受學，謙順恭敬，盡心供養，諸根純熟，所受奉持。爾時彼師暫受王請，留世間現守舍而去。婆羅門婦年少端正，見世間現即生染心，忽忘儀軌，前執其衣。時世間現白彼婦言：仁今便為是我之母，如何尊處而行非法？內懷愧悚，捨衣遠避。爾時彼婦欲心熾盛，泣淚念言：彼見斷絕，不隨我意。若不見從，要斷其命，不使是人更餘婚娶。即以指爪自攖其體，淫亂彌熾，自燒成病。行女人諂，莊嚴其身，以繩自縊，足不離地。時摩尼䟦陀事畢還家，以刀截繩，高聲大呌：誰為此事？時婦答言：是世間現欲行非法，強見陵遍，作如是事。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Aṅgulimāla Sūtra says: North of the city of Śrāvastī, not far from the city, there was a forest there named Sana. There was a poor brahmin woman named Bhadrā. The woman bore a son named All-World Appearance. He lost his father when young. At twelve years of age his complexion, strength, and human features were complete and foremost; he was intelligent, eloquent, and wise, and good at subtle speech. There was also another village, named Poluohesi. There was a long-resident brahmin teacher named Maṇibhadra, who thoroughly mastered the four Vedas. At that time World Appearance studied under him, humble, obedient, and respectful, making offerings with all his heart; his faculties were thoroughly ripe, and what he received he upheld. At that time the teacher, temporarily accepting the king's invitation, left World Appearance to guard the house and went off. The brahmin's wife was young and beautiful; seeing World Appearance, she at once gave rise to a defiled mind, suddenly forgot propriety, and came forward and seized his clothes. Then World Appearance said to the wife: "You are now as my mother; how can you, in so honored a position, do what is not right?" Inwardly ashamed and afraid, he left his clothes and fled far away. Then the wife's desire blazed; weeping, she thought: "He has seen [me] and broken off, and does not follow my wish. If he does not comply, I must cut off his life, so that this man will never marry anyone else." At once she scratched her own body with her nails, her lustful frenzy growing fiercer, burning herself into illness. Practicing a woman's wiles, she adorned her body and hanged herself with a rope, her feet not leaving the ground. When Maṇibhadra finished his business and returned home, he cut the rope with a knife and cried out loudly: "Who did this?" The wife answered: "It was World Appearance, who wished to do what is not right and forced himself upon me, causing this to happen."

Chinese Source

摩尼拔陀先知其人有大德力，即思惟言：彼初生日，一切剎利所有刀劒悉皆㧞出，利劍捲屈墮落於地，時諸剎利皆大恐怖。其生之日有如此相，當知是人有大德力。思惟是已語世間現：汝是惡人毀辱所尊，汝今非復真婆羅門，當殺千人可得除罪。世間現稟性恭順尊重師教，即白師言：嗚呼和尚！殺害千人非我所應。師即謂言：汝是惡人，不樂生天作婆羅門耶？答言：和尚！善哉奉命。即殺千人還禮師足。師聞見已生希有心：汝大惡人故不死耶？復作念言：今當令死。而告之言：殺一一人取一一指。殺千人已取指作鬘冠首而還，然後得成婆羅門耳。以是因緣名央掘魔羅。即白師言：善哉和尚！受教即殺千人少一。

Ru-Yi Translation

Maṇibhadra, already knowing that this person had great virtue and power, reflected: "On the day he was born, all the swords of the kṣatriyas were drawn out by themselves, and the sharp blades bent and fell to the ground, and the kṣatriyas were all greatly afraid. Since his birth had such signs, know that this person has great virtue and power." Having reflected thus, he said to World Appearance: "You are an evil man who has dishonored the one you should honor; you are now no longer a true brahmin. You must kill a thousand people; then you can be rid of your sin." World Appearance, by nature respectful and obedient and honoring his teacher's instruction, said to the teacher: "Alas, master! To kill a thousand people is not what I should do." The teacher then said: "You are an evil man; do you not wish to be born in heaven as a brahmin?" He answered: "Master, very well, I accept your command." He at once killed a thousand people and came back to bow at his teacher's feet. When the teacher heard and saw this, he gave rise to a sense of the extraordinary: "You great evil man, so you have not died?" He thought further: "Now I shall make him die." And he told him: "For each person you kill, take one finger. When you have killed a thousand people, take the fingers, make a garland of them, crown your head with it, and come back; only then can you become a brahmin." Because of this he was named Aṅgulimāla. He said to his teacher: "Very well, master." Receiving the teaching, he at once killed a thousand people less one.

Chinese Source

爾時央掘魔羅母念子當饑，自持四種美食送往與之。子見母已作是思惟：當令我母得生天上。即便執劍欲前斷命。去舍衛國十由延少一丈，於彼有樹名阿輪迦。爾時世尊以一切智，如是知時如鴈王來。央掘魔羅見世尊來，執劍疾往作是念言：我今復當殺是沙門瞿曇。爾時世尊示現避去。時央掘魔羅而說偈言：

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Aṅgulimāla's mother, thinking that her son must be hungry, herself brought four kinds of fine food to give him. When the son saw his mother, he thought: "I should cause my mother to be born in heaven." He at once grasped his sword and went forward to cut off her life. Ten yojanas less one zhang from the kingdom of Śrāvastī there was a tree named Aśoka. At that time the World-Honored One, with omniscience, knowing the time in this way, came like a king of geese. When Aṅgulimāla saw the World-Honored One coming, he grasped his sword, rushed toward him, and thought: "Now I shall further kill this śramaṇa Gautama." At that time the World-Honored One appeared to move away. Then Aṅgulimāla spoke a verse:

Stop, stop, great śramaṇa, / prince of King Śuddhodana! / I am Aṅgulimāla; / now I shall take one finger as toll. / Stop, stop, great śramaṇa, / gentleman in dyed robes, without greed! / I am Aṅgulimāla; / now I shall take one finger as toll.

Ru-Yi Translation

In all he spoke thirty-four verses; the other three lines are all the same, only the second line changes, saying: "gentleman of marred form and shaven head," "gentleman content, awaiting [food in] the bowl," "fearless, roaming like a lion," "heroic and strong, with a tiger's stride," "graceful in bearing, surpassing the king of geese," "calm and composed, walking like the dragon-elephant," "bright as the newly risen sun," "clear and pure as the full moon," "adorned like a mountain of true gold," "eyes like thousand-petaled lotuses," "white teeth like white lotuses," "tongue that speaks true words well," "mark of the white tuft between the eyebrows," "lustrous dark-blue hair," "slender long arms reaching past the knees," "free of desire, hidden like the king of horses," "knee bones close-knit, not showing," "nails of hands and feet like red copper," "feet lightly raised, treading the void," "voice like the kalaviṅka," "wondrous sound like the kokila," "brilliance surpassing a hundred koṭis," "faculties well tamed," "fully endowed with the ten powers," "upholding well the four true truths," "teaching the eightfold path for [beings'] benefit," "endowed with the thirty-two marks," "eighty kinds of wondrous excellences," "forever extinguishing all cravings," "do not make me angry," "a marvel never seen before." And so on up to asking: "What sort of person are you, walking so very fast? Before my knife comes down, know the time and quickly stop. You uphold the pure precepts well; you should quickly hand over a finger; do not cross out of my territory." At that time the World-Honored One, like a king of geese, walked calmly seven steps, looked around like a lion, and spoke a verse for Aṅgulimāla:

Stop, stop, Aṅgulimāla! / You should abide in the pure precepts. / I am the Perfectly Awakened One; / I pay you the toll of the sword of wisdom. / I abide at the limit of non-arising, / and you are not aware of it.

Ru-Yi Translation

Do not kill, do not cause killing; / do not take the form of a rākṣasa, / your body always smeared with human blood. / A sharp sword smeared with human blood / should not always be in your hand; / quickly cast off the finger garland, / leave these deeds of the twice-born; / the twice-born's non-Dharma and the like — / these are evil rākṣasas.

Ru-Yi Translation

The karma you now create, / evil karma beyond that of beasts, / in killing surpasses the rākṣasas; / this violence exceeds the asuras. / You will forever enter the band of base Māra, / forever parted from humankind.

The Mother's Plea and Aṅgulimāla's Turning

Chinese Source

爾時，彼母見佛世尊與央掘魔羅往返苦論，子心降伏，縱身垂臂，念其子故，說偈白佛：

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the mother, seeing the Buddha, the World-Honored One, and Aṅgulimāla going back and forth in this bitter debate, and her son's mind subdued, flung herself forward with arms hanging down, and, out of concern for her son, spoke a verse to the Buddha:

Chinese Source

久失寶藏今還得，塵穢壞眼令明淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Long lost, the treasure-store is now regained; / eyes ruined by dust are made bright and clear.

Chinese Source

哀哉我子心迷亂，常以人血自塗身，<br>極利刀劒恒在手，多殺人眾成尻聚，<br>當令此子隨順我，今敬稽首等二覺。

Ru-Yi Translation

Alas, my son's mind is confused; / he always smears his body with human blood, / an extremely sharp sword always in his hand, / killing many people and making heaps of corpses. / May this son be made to obey me! / Now I respectfully bow my head to the one equal to the two awakenings.

Chinese Source

多人見罵難聽聞，汝子如是切責我。

Ru-Yi Translation

Many people see him and revile him, [words] hard to hear: / "Your son is like this," they bitterly reproach me.

Chinese Source

爾時世尊告央掘魔羅：此樹下者是汝之母，生育之恩深重難報，云何欲害令其生天？如是乃至種種破其愚迷，復自稱讚種種功德，是所歸依處，令其捨劍出家飲甘露法水，乃至云：汝久遊惡道迷亂疲倦，今當休息。汝是稅主，我亦稅主，為守道王，於一切眾生常受其稅，令其超度生死有海。爾時央掘魔羅即捨利劒，如一歲嬰兒捉火即放振手啼泣，時央掘魔羅捨鬘振手發聲號呌亦復如是。如人熟眠蛇卒嚙脚，即時驚起振手遠擲，央掘魔羅速捨指鬘亦復如是。爾時央掘魔羅如離非人所持，自知慚愧，血出遍身淚流如雨。譬如有人為蛇所螫，良醫為呪令作蛇行，央掘魔羅宛轉腹行三十九旋亦復如是。然後進前頂禮佛足，而說偈言：

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One told Aṅgulimāla: "The one beneath this tree is your mother; the kindness of bearing and raising you is deep and hard to repay. How can you wish to harm her in order to have her born in heaven?" In this way and so on, he broke through his foolish delusion in all kinds of ways, and further praised his own various merits as the place of refuge, causing him to give up his sword, leave home, and drink the nectar water of the Dharma; and so on up to saying: "You have long wandered on evil paths, confused and weary; now you should rest. You are a toll-master; I too am a toll-master, a king guarding the way, always receiving toll from all beings and enabling them to cross beyond the ocean of birth-and-death existence." At that time Aṅgulimāla at once cast away his sharp sword. As a one-year-old infant who grasps fire at once lets go, shakes its hand, and cries, so Aṅgulimāla cast off the garland, shook his hand, and cried out wailing. As a person sound asleep, suddenly bitten on the foot by a snake, at once starts up, shakes his hand, and flings it far away, so Aṅgulimāla quickly cast off the finger garland. At that time Aṅgulimāla, as if freed from possession by a nonhuman, knew shame for himself; blood came out all over his body, and his tears flowed like rain. As when someone stung by a snake is made by a good physician's spell to move like a snake, so Aṅgulimāla writhed on his belly, turning thirty-nine times. Afterward he went forward, bowed his head at the Buddha's feet, and spoke verses:

奇哉正覺第一慈，調御人師為我來，<br>令我得度無如海，愚癡闇冥濤波惑，<br>奇哉正覺無上悲，調御人師為我來，<br>度我生死曠野難，種種煩惱棘刺林，

Ru-Yi Translation

Ru-Yi Translation

Ru-Yi Translation

Chinese Source

Reading Notes for Part 6

Translator's notes

Aṅgulimāla Sūtra (units 3–94): Yangjuemoluo jing (T120), abridged by Chuandeng with "and so on" and his own summaries (units 5, 9, 42, 85); new English, since the site has no translation. Readings from T120: 師予游 as 師子遊 "roaming like a lion"; 輪汝慧劍稅 as 輸汝 "I pay you" (units 6, 7, 10); 無如海 as 無知海 "ocean of unknowing"; 忍若穢 as 忍苦穢 "bearing hardship and filth"; 於波諸佛所 as 於彼; 興蕖 is asafoetida (hiṅgu).

Names: 一切世間現 All-World Appearance (short form 世間現 World Appearance); 䟦陀羅 Bhadrā; 摩尼跋陀羅 Maṇibhadra; 薩那 Sana and 頗羅呵私 Poluohesi (pinyin); 阿輪迦 Aśoka (tree); 尼陀沙 Nituosha (pinyin; not identified); 滿願子 Pūrṇa son of Maitrāyaṇī; 一切世間樂見上大精進如來 the Tathāgata Supreme Great Vigor Whom All the World Delights to See; 甘露鼓王佛 the Buddha King of the Nectar Drum (the site's Nirvāṇa has 甘露鼓如來 as "the Tathāgata Amṛtadundubhi ('the Drum of Nectar')"; the added 王 "King" is kept).

等二覺 (unit 25): T120 has 等正覺 "the Perfectly Awakened One"; the English keeps the text's "the one equal to the two awakenings."

如是為一 (unit 87): obscure in T120 as well; rendered "so be it: [he is] but one." Alternatively: "If he came, it would be [settled] in one [blow]."

童真 (units 50–57, 95): "pure youth"; the interlinear note in unit 50 identifies it with the śrāmaṇera. Aṅgulimāla's answers to each precept deliberately reverse its sense; Chuandeng's comment (unit 95) says "he reversed their names and broadened their meaning."

Unit 95: 未聞開權應是實殺 is read "we have not heard that this was opened as provisional; it should be real killing — [but of those who]…." Alternatively: "We have not heard [the sūtra] open the provisional; [those killed] should have been really killed." "Wishing to give… he first had to take away" echoes the Laozi (ch. 36) pattern 將欲…必固…. Xianyu and the five hundred brahmins: Nirvāṇa Sūtra (see NOTES, Part 3).

Nirvāṇa Sūtra (units 97–103): Chuandeng abridges the Ajātaśatru chapter (T374, fascicles 19–20). Site English (ref/mahaparinirvana-sutra.txt) is reused for every sentence the text keeps, with the cuts and variants followed: 似相為及 (site text 將為大王) as "a sign that reaches [you]"; 五譬 (the sūtra gives six); 吉辰 "auspicious hour" (site 吉星 "auspicious star"); 芭蕉葉 "plantain leaf." The verses of units 98–101 are not on the site page and are new.

Twenty observations (unit 102): items follow the site, except where the text differs from T374: (4) 無疑不畏 "without doubt and without fear" (T374 無處不畏); (7) the text lists 常、苦、無常、我、淨 as five words; (13) 五惡 (T374 五逆); (15) 不退果報 (T374 不得果報), rendered literally; (20) only "future and present"; the result is 止觀 "calming-and-contemplation" (T374 正觀).

Lotus (unit 105): "causing everyone to hear the voice of the buddha-path" and "worthy, everywhere among them, to receive offerings" reuse the site's Lotus (Belief and Understanding chapter) as already used in Part 1. 義天 "a god of the [supreme] meaning" recalls the Nirvāṇa's four kinds of gods (第一義天).

Śūraṅgama verse (unit 105): "Offering this deep mind to lands as many as motes of dust: this is called repaying the Buddha's kindness" (T945, fascicle 3); the guest changes 佛 "Buddha" to 師 "teacher."

Unit 105 readings: 出河 read as 山河 "mountains and rivers"; 全水以為水 read as 全水以為氷 "the whole of the water becoming ice"; 充櫎 as 充擴 "expanding"; 若天 as 若夫; the glyph 𮌎 in 未有其𮌎 is taken as "capacity" (probably 能); 槃譚 is read as 盤桓 "lingered." Each is a guess and is.

Colophon (unit 106): Yutan Monastery (漁潭寺) in Yongjia County (Wenzhou, Zhejiang); its disciples paid for the woodblocks. No date is given.
