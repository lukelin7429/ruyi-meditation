# Treatise on Buddha Nature — Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Foxing Lun* (佛性論, *Treatise on Buddha Nature*), Taishō T1610, attributed to Vasubandhu and translated into Chinese by Paramārtha (真諦) in the sixth century. They exist so that vocabulary stays consistent across all twenty-two parts.

## The text, and why there is no ancient commentary to cite

No Sanskrit or Tibetan version of this treatise has ever been located — it survives only in Paramārtha's Chinese translation. Unlike the *Awakening of Faith* (大乘起信論), which Fazang glossed line by line in a commentary six times the length of the root text, the *Foxing Lun* never attracted a dedicated exegetical tradition of its own in the Chinese canon or the Japanese *Zokuzōkyō*. Because there is no named ancient commentator to draw reading notes from, every reading note in this collection is original commentary from Ru-Yi Meditation Center, marked as such, and appears only where a passage seemed to call for one — not on every page, following the precedent already set on this site's *Buddhacarita* collection, which faced the same absence of a commentarial tradition.

The base text runs to roughly 37,000 Chinese characters across four fascicles (卷), not counting front matter or the CBETA colophon.

## Structure

The treatise names its own four major divisions in its closing lines: 緣起分 (the part on causes and conditions), 破執分 (the part refuting attachments), 顯體分 (the part revealing the essence), and 辯相分 (the part distinguishing the marks). The fourth division, by far the largest, is itself organized around ten named marks (十相) of buddha-nature.

The mark of total inclusion (總攝相, Parts 13–16) and the mark of non-change (無變異相, Parts 18–21) are the treatise's two longest chapters, each requiring four pages; both are split at natural argumentative turns rather than at even intervals.

## Core terminology

Renderings are matched first to this site's existing Yogācāra and tathāgatagarbha-line pages (*Cheng Weishi Lun*, *Abhidharmasamuccaya*, the *Awakening of Faith*, the *Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra*) wherever the same term already appears there; where this treatise's own vocabulary has no site precedent, a rendering is coined fresh and recorded here.

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 佛性 | buddha-nature | The treatise's central term throughout. |
| 如來藏 | tathāgatagarbha | Left untransliterated, matching this site's existing tathāgatagarbha-line pages. Part 8 translates the treatise's own three-meanings gloss of the term in full. |
| 真如 | suchness | Matches this site's existing Yogācāra pages. |
| 三性 | the three natures | Imagined (分別性), dependent (依他性), perfected (真實性) — matches this site's existing Yogācāra pages. |
| 轉依 | the transformed basis | Coined fresh for this collection. The treatise's own term for the dharmakāya considered specifically as the endpoint of a completed process of purification, upheld by eight dharmas (Part 13) and carrying seven further names (Part 14). |
| 一闡提 | icchantika | Kept as the Sanskrit loanword. The treatise resolves the apparent tension between sūtra passages that deny and affirm the icchantika's eventual liberation by reading the denial as a provisional teaching aimed at a reversible attitude, not a permanent metaphysical status — see Parts 2 and 12. |
| 常樂我淨 | permanence, bliss, self, purity | The dharmakāya's four pāramitās, reversing the four ordinary marks of impermanence, suffering, no-self, and impurity per the Śrīmālādevī Sūtra, quoted directly in Part 11. |
| 法身 / 應身 / 化身 | dharmakāya / sambhogakāya / nirmāṇakāya | Kept as Sanskrit loanwords per site convention. 應身 here corresponds to what some other site pages call the reward body; this treatise's own gloss (Part 19) ties it specifically to the simile of a wheel-turning king still in a poor woman's womb. |
| 二空 | the twofold emptiness | The emptiness of person (人空) and of dharma (法空), used throughout, especially in Fascicle One's refutation chapters. |
| 二諦 | the two truths | Ultimate (真諦) and conventional (俗諦), the subject of Part 6's refutation of a Mahāyāna misreading. |
| 無明住地 | the ground of ignorance | The subtlest affliction, present even in the arhat's continuum and broken only by the Tathāgata's own enlightenment — a recurring term from Part 11 onward. |
| 意生身 | the mind-made body | The subtle body of advanced sages (śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and bodhisattvas) still subject to the four kinds of birth-and-death described in Part 11. |
| 九喻 | the nine similes | Lotus and transformation-buddha, bee and honey, rice and husk, gold in filth, a poor woman's buried treasure, a mango seed, gold wrapped in rags, a poor woman carrying a future king, and a gold image still in its mold — unfolded in full across Parts 18–19, each paired with one of nine kinds of affliction and mapped onto the three buddha-bodies. |
| 六種二邊 | the six pairs of extremes | The dharmakāya's third mark (Part 19), each pair illustrated by one of the Buddha's own parables from the Ratnakūṭa Sūtra's Kāśyapa chapter (迦葉品): fearing empty space, a painter frightened by his own image, an illusionist devoured by his conjured tiger, fire from two rubbing sticks, and two lamp similes. |
| 唯識智 | the wisdom of consciousness-only | Matches this site's existing Yogācāra pages (vijñaptimātratā). |
| 如理修 / 如量修 | cultivation in accordance with principle / in accordance with measure | The treatise's own pairing (Part 13) of realizing emptiness and knowing the full particular extent of beings and dharmas — coined fresh for this collection. |

## A note on this treatise's place in the site's tathāgatagarbha line

This collection joins the *Awakening of Faith*, the *Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra*, and other tathāgatagarbha-line texts on this site. Several renderings here — "the transformed basis," "cultivation in accordance with principle / in accordance with measure" — have no prior site precedent and were coined with an eye toward reuse should this site later take up other texts in the same lineage, such as the *Ratnagotravibhāga* (寶性論). Future translators working on such texts should check this glossary before coining fresh renderings for the same terms.
