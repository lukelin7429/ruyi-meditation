The Cause Mark

This chapter takes up the second of the treatise's ten marks: the four causes that overcome four obstructions preventing four kinds of persons — the icchantika, the non-Buddhist, the śrāvaka, and the pratyekabuddha — from seeing their own pure dharmakāya. Along the way, the treatise sorts sentient beings into three groups by their relationship to saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, and closes with an account of what it actually means to be called a "child of the Buddha."

Four Causes, Four Obstructions

Chinese Source

復次有四種因，能除四障，得如來性義應知。四因者，一信樂大乘、二無分別般若、三破虛空三昧、四菩薩大悲。四障者，一憎背大乘、二身見計執、三怖畏生死、四不樂觀利益他事。初障闡提，二障外道，三障聲聞，四障獨覺。由此四惑，能令四人不能得見自性清淨法身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, there are four causes able to remove four obstructions and attain the Tathāgata's nature — this should be understood. The four causes are: first, faith and delight in the Mahāyāna; second, non-discriminating prajñā; third, the samādhi that breaks through attachment to emptiness; fourth, bodhisattva great compassion. The four obstructions are: first, hatred and turning one's back on the Mahāyāna; second, the view of self and its attendant attachments; third, dread of saṃsāra; fourth, an unwillingness to attend to benefiting others. The first obstructs the icchantika, the second obstructs the non-Buddhist, the third obstructs the śrāvaka, and the fourth obstructs the pratyekabuddha. It is by these four afflictions that these four kinds of persons are prevented from seeing their own inherently pure dharmakāya.

Three Kinds of Beings

Chinese Source

若略說世間有三種眾生：一樂生死恒有，二樂滅生死有，三兩俱不樂、有滅並忘。一樂生死有者，復有二種：一憎背解脫道、無涅槃性，決樂生死、不樂涅槃。二已墮定位。定位者，非聖非凡、進退無取，而是佛法內人，背大乘法。因此人故，佛說是言：我非是其師，其非我弟子。舍利弗！此人從輕暗入重暗，復從重暗入於盲暗。取暗為友，復取闡提為友，是故我說此人如是二樂。

滅生死有者，有二種：一墮非方便、二墮方便中。就墮非方便，復有二：一外道，謂九十六種。二是佛法內人，與外道同執，約正法起邪執我見故，於正教義不能了達。因此人故，佛說是言：若不信樂真空，則與外道無異。復次有增上慢人，取空為見，是真空實解脫門。約此空解脫門起於空執，謂一切有無並皆是空。此空執者即無所有，無所有故因果二諦道理並失，執此空過故即墮邪無。是等執者由空而起，故成邪執。一切邪執莫不由空故能滅除，此執既依空起故不可治。因此人故，故佛語迦葉：若人起我見執如須彌山大，我亦許之。何以故？以可滅故。若此增上慢人所起空執猶如髮端四分之一，我急呵責決定不許。

二墮方便中有二：一聲聞人，自利修行，但為自度，不為利他。二獨覺人，於利他心無樂無事，但起捨心。無樂者，不樂利他。無事者，了無度人之事，唯為自覺自利故。但起捨心者，捨是平等住心，不願利人亦無所損，獨自覺悟，故言獨覺。墮方便聲聞亦爾。如末田地及阿斯那二比丘，佛涅槃時其皆不往，後至迦葉集法藏時被召方出。迦葉呵責之言：汝為從佛得聖道不？答云實爾。又呵責言：汝大過失，今去當以佛法付汝任持。若不如法，罪失屬汝。其人甘失懺悔，受旨奉行。

三俱不樂者，謂修行大乘最利根人，既不同闡提樂生死有，亦不墮非方便中同外道執，亦不墮方便中如二乘人。是故此人具行生死涅槃平等之道，住無住處，雖行生死而不染、雖行涅槃亦非淨，但為大悲故不捨生死、為般若故不捨涅槃。不離涅槃者，異聲聞執永住無為。不捨生死者，異一闡提樂於生死。若樂著生死者，名一闡提。佛法內人墮定位者，亦同闡提。如是二人墮在邪定聚中。若樂滅生死有者，是人墮非方便中，則在不定聚。若人樂滅生死有，是人墮方便中及俱不樂。得前二者，修平等道，是人在正定聚中。

Ru-Yi Translation

To speak briefly, there are three kinds of beings in the world: first, those who delight in the continued existence of saṃsāra; second, those who delight in the extinguishing of the existence of saṃsāra; third, those who delight in neither, for whom both existence and extinguishing are set aside. As to the first, there are two kinds: first, those who hate and turn their backs on the path of liberation, having no nature for nirvāṇa at all, and definitely delight in saṃsāra and take no delight in nirvāṇa. Second, those who have already fallen into a fixed position — "fixed position" meaning neither sage nor ordinary person, neither advancing nor retreating, taking hold of nothing — yet who are, nominally, persons within the Buddhist fold, turning their backs on the Mahāyāna teaching. It is on account of such a person that the Buddha said: "I am not his teacher, and he is not my disciple. Śāriputra! This person passes from a lesser darkness into a greater darkness, and from that greater darkness into utter, blinding darkness. He takes darkness as his friend, and further takes the icchantika as his friend" — hence I say that this person has these two kinds of delight.

As to the second, those who delight in the extinguishing of the existence of saṃsāra, there are two kinds: first, those who fall into what is not skillful means; second, those who fall into what is skillful means. As to the first, there are, again, two kinds: non-Buddhists, of the ninety-six varieties, and those who, though nominally within the Buddhist fold, hold the very same attachment as the non-Buddhists — because, taking hold of the true teaching, they give rise to a mistaken attachment to a self, and so fail to penetrate the true doctrine's meaning. It is on account of such a person that the Buddha said: "If one does not have faith and delight in true emptiness, one is no different from a non-Buddhist." Furthermore, there are persons of excessive pride who take emptiness itself as a fixed view, as though it were the true gate of liberation, and, on this basis, give rise to an attachment to emptiness — holding that both existence and non-existence, in every case, are simply emptiness. This attachment amounts to sheer nothingness, and, being sheer nothingness, the entire structure of cause and effect and the two truths is thereby lost, and one falls into a wrongful nihilism. Every wrongful attachment can, in principle, be dissolved by means of emptiness — yet this particular attachment, having arisen precisely on the basis of emptiness itself, cannot be remedied at all. It is on account of such a person that the Buddha said to Kāśyapa: "If a person gives rise to a view of self as vast as Mount Sumeru, I would still permit it — why? Because it can be eliminated. But if a person of excessive pride gives rise to an attachment to emptiness no larger than a quarter of a hair's tip, I would sternly rebuke him and would never permit it at all."

As to the second, those who fall into skillful means, there are two kinds: first, śrāvakas, who cultivate practice for their own benefit alone, seeking only their own liberation and not the benefit of others. Second, pratyekabuddhas, who have neither the wish nor the deed of benefiting others, but merely give rise to an attitude of equanimity — "no wish" meaning taking no delight in benefiting others, "no deed" meaning having entirely no undertaking of saving others, seeking only their own awakening and benefit. Equanimity is a mind that abides in impartiality, wishing to benefit no one yet also causing no harm, awakened entirely alone — hence the name "solitary awakener." Those śrāvakas who fall into skillful means are exactly the same. It is like the two monks Madhyāntika and Aśvaka, who neither one attended when the Buddha entered parinirvāṇa, and only appeared, summoned, when Kāśyapa later convened the compiling of the Dharma treasury. Kāśyapa rebuked them, saying: "Did you attain the sagely path under the Buddha, or not?" They answered that they had indeed. He rebuked them further: "This is a grave fault of yours. From now on, the Buddhadharma shall be entrusted to you to uphold and maintain; should you fail to act in accord with the Dharma, the fault and the loss shall be yours alone." Those two men accepted this in shame, repented, and received his instruction, carrying it out with reverence.

As to the third, those who delight in neither: these are practitioners of the Mahāyāna of the very sharpest faculties, who neither resemble the icchantika in delighting in saṃsāra, nor fall into what is not skillful means, sharing the non-Buddhists' attachment, nor fall into skillful means, like followers of the two lesser vehicles. Such a person fully practices the path of equality between saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, abiding in the place of non-abiding: though engaging in saṃsāra, he is not defiled by it; though engaging in nirvāṇa, he is not attached to it as pure either — it is only that, for the sake of great compassion, he does not abandon saṃsāra, and, for the sake of prajñā, he does not abandon nirvāṇa. "Not departing from nirvāṇa" distinguishes him from the śrāvaka's attachment to permanently abiding in the unconditioned; "not abandoning saṃsāra" distinguishes him from the icchantika's delight in saṃsāra. One who delights in and clings to saṃsāra is called an icchantika. A person nominally within the Buddhist fold who has fallen into a fixed position is likewise counted as an icchantika. These two kinds of persons both fall within the assembly fixed toward wrong destiny. One who falls into what is not skillful means belongs to the assembly of undetermined destiny. One who falls into skillful means, and one who delights in neither, having attained the former two causes and cultivating the path of equality — such a person belongs to the assembly rightly fixed toward correct destiny.

The Four Obstructions Matched to Their Antidotes

Chinese Source

離發行大乘修習無障道人之外，所餘闡提、外道、聲聞、獨覺等四人，有四種障故不見佛性。何者四障？一憎背大乘，是闡提障。為對治此故，佛說菩薩修習信樂大乘之法。二於諸法中起我見執，是外道障。為對治此故，佛說菩薩修習般若波羅蜜。三於生死中定執苦想及厭怖心，是聲聞障。為對治此故，佛說菩薩修習破空三昧。空三昧者，從初地以上能得此三昧，則破虛空等執，入觀之時不即有無、不離有無，喻如八地真俗雙觀。而異八地者，八地以上無出入觀，初地入時則同、出時則異。四背眾生利益事、作捨眾生意，是獨覺障。為對治此故，佛說修習菩薩大悲。菩薩大悲利他為事。明獨覺人但自觀因緣，無度他意，故無大悲。聲聞亦爾。為滅此四障故，以信樂等四種為因，令諸菩薩修習此因，得至無上法身清淨波羅蜜，是名佛性清淨因。

Ru-Yi Translation

Apart from those who have given rise to the Mahāyāna practice free of obstruction, the remaining four kinds of persons — the icchantika, the non-Buddhist, the śrāvaka, and the pratyekabuddha — fail to see buddha-nature on account of four obstructions. What are the four? First, hatred and turning one's back on the Mahāyāna — the icchantika's obstruction. As its antidote, the Buddha taught bodhisattvas to cultivate faith and delight in the Mahāyāna teaching. Second, giving rise to attachment to a view of self among all dharmas — the non-Buddhist's obstruction. As its antidote, the Buddha taught bodhisattvas to cultivate the perfection of prajñā. Third, fixedly clinging to the notion of suffering and a mind of dread with respect to saṃsāra — the śrāvaka's obstruction. As its antidote, the Buddha taught bodhisattvas to cultivate the samādhi that breaks through emptiness. This samādhi can be attained from the first ground onward, breaking through attachment to a reified emptiness and the rest — upon entering it, one is neither caught in existence nor non-existence, and yet is not apart from them either, comparable to the eighth ground's simultaneous contemplation of the ultimate and the conventional. What distinguishes it from the eighth ground is that, from the eighth ground onward, there is no longer any entering or emerging from contemplation, whereas at the first ground, entering this contemplation is the same as the eighth ground's, but emerging from it is different. Fourth, turning away from the work of benefiting sentient beings and forming an intention to abandon them — the pratyekabuddha's obstruction. As its antidote, the Buddha taught the cultivation of bodhisattva great compassion. Bodhisattva great compassion takes benefiting others as its very work; this makes clear that the pratyekabuddha merely contemplates dependent origination for himself alone, having no intention of saving others, and so lacks great compassion — the śrāvaka is exactly the same. It is in order to extinguish these four obstructions that these four causes serve to let bodhisattvas cultivate their way to the unsurpassed, pure perfection of the dharmakāya; this is called the pure cause of buddha-nature.

Four Senses of Being a Child of the Buddha

Chinese Source

如是之人得名佛子，是故佛子有於四義：一因、二緣、三依止、四成就。初言因者，有二：一佛性、二信樂。此兩法，佛性是無為，信樂是有為。信樂約性得佛性為了因，能顯了正因性故。信樂約加行為生因，能生起眾行故。二緣者，謂般若波羅蜜能生菩薩身，是無為功德家緣故。三依止者，破空定等。樂有之人執斷，無處有樂淨等故。菩薩修破空三昧，能除彼執。由此定力，是故菩薩法身堅固則不羸弱。四成就者，菩薩大悲利益他事無盡故。由真如不盡，眾生無數故，利益事亦復無盡，是佛性為應得家因故。一因如父身分，二緣如母，三依止如胞胎，四成就如乳母故，諸菩薩由此四義名為佛子。

Ru-Yi Translation

A person such as this is called a child of the Buddha, and being a child of the Buddha carries four senses: first, cause; second, condition; third, support; fourth, fulfillment. As to the first, cause: there are two — buddha-nature, and faith and delight. Of these two, buddha-nature is unconditioned, while faith and delight are conditioned. Faith and delight, with respect to the nature, serve as the revealing cause of attaining buddha-nature, being able to manifest the true, causal nature; with respect to practice, they serve as the generative cause, being able to give rise to the whole of one's practice. Second, condition: this refers to the perfection of prajñā, which is able to give rise to the bodhisattva's own body, being the condition for the household of unconditioned virtue. Third, support: this refers to the samādhi that breaks through emptiness and the rest. Those who delight in mere conventional existence cling to a fixed notion of annihilation, supposing there is nowhere that bliss and purity ultimately exist; the bodhisattva's cultivation of this samādhi is able to remove this attachment, and by its strength, the bodhisattva's dharmakāya becomes firm and unwavering. Fourth, fulfillment: this refers to how the bodhisattva's great compassion, benefiting others, is inexhaustible — since suchness itself is inexhaustible and sentient beings are without number, the work of benefiting them is likewise inexhaustible; this is buddha-nature serving as the cause of the household of what is to be attained. The first, cause, is like the portion belonging to the father's own body; the second, condition, is like the mother; the third, support, is like the womb; and the fourth, fulfillment, is like the wet-nurse — and it is by these four senses that bodhisattvas are called children of the Buddha.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The story of Madhyāntika and Aśvaka is a small, sharp piece of narrative dropped into an otherwise abstract argument, and it is worth noticing what work it is doing. Both monks were, by the treatise's own account, genuine arhats — "did you attain the sagely path under the Buddha?" "We did indeed" — yet Kāśyapa still rebukes them for absenting themselves from both the Buddha's parinirvāṇa and the First Council. Their fault was not a failure of personal attainment; it was a failure to show up for the ongoing work of the community, which is exactly what the treatise has just finished calling the śrāvaka's structural obstruction: the wish and the deed to benefit others, missing even in someone who has otherwise reached the goal. The passage anticipates an objection a Western reader might raise here — if arhatship is possible, why insist that anything more is needed? — by showing, in miniature, what the "something more" actually looks like: not a higher metaphysical attainment, but showing up.
