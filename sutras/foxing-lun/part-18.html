Nine Similes of Non-Change

Fascicle Four opens with the ninth mark, non-change — the treatise's longest remaining chapter. This page covers its foundation: a detailed catalogue of nine kinds of affliction, from latent craving through the subtlest obstructions still facing an eighth-ground bodhisattva, shared in different combinations among the ordinary person, the arhat, the one still in training, and the bodhisattva — and the nine similes the treatise pairs with them, each showing buddha-nature concealed but never touched by what conceals it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 18 of 22 complete Source: Taishō T1610, 佛性論 (attributed to Vasubandhu, tr. Paramārtha)

Six Aspects, Reduced to Three

Chinese Source

復次無變異義應知。離有六義，合則成三。離有六種者，一無前後際變異、二無染淨異、三無生異、四無轉異、五無依住異、六無滅異。言無前後際變異者，如佛為海智菩薩說《解節經》偈言：「客塵相應故，有自性德故，如前後亦爾，是無變異相。」

復次釋不淨位中，有九種客塵，非所染污故不淨；淨位中，常樂我淨四德及如來恒沙功德，恒相應故，故說如來性前後無變異。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the meaning of non-change should be understood. Separated out, it has six aspects; combined, these reduce to three. The six separate aspects are: no change across former and latter times; no difference between defilement and purity; no difference of arising; no difference of transformation; no difference of dependence and abiding; and no difference of cessation. As to no change across former and latter times: as the Buddha said to Sāgaramati Bodhisattva in the Sandhinirmocana Sūtra, in verse: "Because it is conjoined with adventitious defilement, and because it possesses its own inherent virtue, it is, in the former time and in the latter alike, of this same mark of non-change."

Next, to explain this: within the impure stage, there are nine kinds of adventitious defilement — the nature is called impure not because it is itself stained, but on their account; within the pure stage, it is constantly conjoined with the four virtues of permanence, bliss, self, and purity, and with the Tathāgata's virtues beyond the sands of the Ganges. Hence it is said that the Tathāgata's nature undergoes no change across the former and latter times.

Nine Kinds of Affliction

Chinese Source

若略說一切煩惱客塵，凡有九種：一者隨眠貪欲煩惱、二隨眠瞋、三隨眠癡、四貪瞋癡等極重上心惑、五無明住地、六見諦所滅、七修習所滅、八不淨地、九淨地惑。若煩惱在世間離欲眾生相續中，為不動業增長家因，能生色無色界，為出世無分別智所滅，是名隨眠欲瞋癡等三毒。

釋曰：言煩惱在者，有二：一有體說在、二無體，約因在故說在。言體在者，即見諦。隨眠未得治道，故言在。約因在者，即思惟隨眠，已得治道故。以思惟用見諦為因，思惟雖滅，由有見諦為思惟因，故言因在。思惟必由見諦而生，若除思惟因，有根本見諦惑在，後更能生思惟之惑，故言思惟因在。不動業增長家因者，一思惟能成業、二見諦能得果。何以故？斷思惟盡，業不成故。斷見諦盡，不感果故，故說不動業。為增長家因，能生色無色界故，為出世無分別智所滅者，有二種滅：一者性滅、二治道滅。一性滅者有二，謂念念滅及相違滅。相違滅又有二種，一等類相違，如貪違瞋等；二不等類相違，如正思惟違欲瞋等，是名性滅。二治道滅者，有二種：一通、二別。通道者，謂觀真如，滅三界煩惱。二別道者，如不淨觀等能滅貪瞋等煩惱，如苦諦觀滅苦諦惑、不滅集諦惑故，名隨眠欲瞋癡等。

四三毒極重上心惑者，有諸煩惱在欲行眾生相續中，為罪福兩行增長家因，但生欲界，修不淨觀等所破，是名貪瞋癡極重上心煩惱。五無明住地惑者，在阿羅漢相續中，為無流業生家因，能生意所生身，為如來菩提所破，是名無明住地惑，故阿羅漢約安立諦觀能破諸煩惱。此無明住地，非安立諦觀所能破故，猶在羅漢相續中，為無流業生家因。無流業生家因者，流有三義：一者流入三界生死。二者退失，如失欲界流往色界，或失色界流下欲界，則隨生死不定一處故名為流。三者流脫功德善根失戒定慧，譬如破塘水則不住。無此三流，故名無流。業者，作意為義，此意業能生四種生死。如來菩提破者，如來菩提非安立諦觀，是名如來菩提。因此道故，能滅此惑故，名無明住地。

六見諦所滅惑者，有二種學人：一凡夫、二聖人。此惑在學道凡夫相續中，無始已來未曾見理，因初出世聖道所破，名為見諦。釋曰：學道凡夫相續中者，若小乘則從煖、頂、忍、世法，此四是學道凡夫位，見諦隨眠其未能滅，故言在中。無始時節未見者，從無始以來迄至此道所未曾見安立聖諦，故言未曾得見。初出世聖道所破者，始自苦法忍初念之道，能破煩惱，名為見諦。自此後去並屬思惟。平等觀者，有利有鈍。若利根人，於一念中則等觀四諦，八十八惑一時俱斷，皆名見諦。若鈍根人，於次第觀者，則初念觀苦、不見餘三諦，但斷苦下四諦，名為見諦，餘未斷者皆屬思惟，是名見諦所滅惑。若大乘則十信等諸位。聖人者，初地以上。

七修道所滅惑者，在學道聖人相續中，昔已曾見出世聖道所破，是名由修道所滅惑。八不淨地惑者，在未究竟行菩薩相續中，對障菩薩七地，為無相無功用道所破，對障故。釋曰：七地者，即前七地已還故。無相無功用道者，即八地以上。無相者，即真如境。無功用者，即自然昇進道。是名不淨地惑。九淨地惑者，在究竟行菩薩相續中，能障八地以上三地，譬金剛定慧所破。言究竟者，八地以上見境皆周，無更別境所未見者，故名究竟。

但方便由有淺深故、諸地為別故，分別煩惱有此九種。約此煩惱立眾生有四種：一凡夫、二羅漢、三有學、四菩薩。此四種眾生，依無流界，由四煩惱故不淨。第一眾生由四煩惱故不淨，第二眾生由一煩惱故不淨，第三眾生由兩煩惱故不淨，第四眾生由兩煩惱故不淨。第一凡夫由四惑者，前三是隨眠貪瞋癡等，後一即上心煩惱，由此四故不淨。二由一惑者，即羅漢由無明住地故不淨。三由二惑者，謂見諦思惟，即有學聖、凡夫學道。凡夫由見諦煩惱故不淨，有學聖人由思惟煩惱故不淨，是名由二惑。四由二惑者，謂淨不淨，即是從初地已上至七地菩薩，由不淨地惑故不淨；若從八地已上三地，由淨地惑故不淨，是名由二惑故言不淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Briefly stated, every kind of adventitious afflictive defilement numbers nine in all: the latent affliction of craving; latent aversion; latent delusion; the extremely heavy, active affliction of craving, aversion, and delusion; the ground of ignorance; what is eliminated by the path of insight; what is eliminated by the path of cultivation; the affliction of the impure grounds; and the affliction of the pure grounds. As to affliction present within the continuum of a worldly being who has become free of desire-realm desire: it serves as the cause for the growth of unshakable karma, able to give rise to rebirth in the form and formless realms, and is eliminated by supramundane, non-discriminating wisdom — this is the latent three poisons of craving, aversion, and delusion.

Explanation: as to affliction being "present," there are two senses — present in substance, and present in the sense that its cause is present. As to being present in substance: this refers to what is eliminated by insight — since the antidote has not yet been applied, it is said to be present in substance. As to being present in the sense that its cause is present: this refers to what is eliminated by cultivation, latent affliction pertaining to contemplative thought, since the antidote has already been applied to it — yet because this contemplative-thought affliction takes insight-level affliction as its cause, even once it has itself ceased, so long as insight-level affliction is still present, it can, later, give rise again to contemplative-thought affliction — hence it is said its cause is present. As to being the cause for the growth of unshakable karma: contemplative-thought affliction is able to accomplish karma, and insight-level affliction is able to obtain its fruit — for when contemplative-thought affliction is entirely cut off, that particular karma cannot be accomplished, and when insight-level affliction is entirely cut off, no fruit is invited. As to being eliminated by supramundane, non-discriminating wisdom: there are two kinds of elimination — elimination by nature, and elimination by the antidote of the path. Elimination by nature is of two kinds, perishing moment by moment, and elimination through mutual opposition — opposition between things of the same category, as craving opposes aversion, and opposition between differing categories, as correct thought opposes desire and aversion. Elimination by the antidote of the path is of two kinds, general and particular — the general path being the contemplation of suchness, which extinguishes the afflictions of all three realms, and the particular path being something like the contemplation of impurity, which extinguishes specific afflictions such as craving and aversion, just as the contemplation of the truth of suffering extinguishes affliction pertaining to suffering but not affliction pertaining to origination.

As to the fourth, the extremely heavy, active affliction of the three poisons: there are afflictions present within the continuum of beings still active within the desire realm, serving as the cause for the growth of both unwholesome and wholesome karmic action, giving rise only to rebirth within the desire realm, and broken by cultivating the contemplation of impurity and the like. As to the fifth, the affliction of the ground of ignorance: this is present within the continuum of the arhat, serving as the cause for the arising of outflow-free karma, able to give rise to the mind-made body, and broken only by the Tathāgata's own enlightenment. The arhat, by means of the contemplation of the established truths, is able to break every ordinary affliction, but this ground of ignorance cannot be broken by that same contemplation, and so remains present within the arhat's continuum. As to "outflow-free": "outflow" carries three senses — flowing into the birth-and-death of the three realms; retrogression, as when one loses rebirth in one realm and flows into another, following birth-and-death without any fixed place; and flowing away, losing one's virtuous roots, discipline, concentration, and wisdom, like a breached pond whose water no longer holds. Being free of these three kinds of flow is what is called "outflow-free." "Karma" here carries the sense of intentional action, this mental karma being able to give rise to four kinds of birth-and-death. It is by means of the Tathāgata's own enlightenment alone — not the ordinary contemplation of the established truths — that this affliction can be extinguished, and hence it is called the ground of ignorance.

As to the sixth, affliction eliminated by the path of insight: there are two kinds of persons still in training, the ordinary person and the sage. This affliction is present within the continuum of the ordinary person still on the path of training, who, since beginningless time, has never once seen the truth, and it is broken by the very first moment of the supramundane noble path. Explanation: in the Hīnayāna scheme, this spans the four stages of heat, summit, forbearance, and supreme worldly dharma, in which the latent affliction eliminated by insight has not yet been eliminated. As to the equal contemplation of the four truths: for a person of sharp faculties, within a single moment, all four truths are contemplated equally, and all eighty-eight afflictions are cut off at once — all of this is called insight into the truth. For a person of dull faculties, contemplating in sequence, the first moment contemplates suffering alone, cutting off only the afflictions pertaining to suffering — this alone is called insight into the truth, the rest belonging to contemplative thought. In the Mahāyāna scheme, this corresponds to the stages of the ten faiths and the like; "sage" refers to one at the first ground or above.

As to the seventh, affliction eliminated by the path of cultivation: this is present within the continuum of the sage still in training, already broken once before by the supramundane noble path. As to the eighth, affliction of the impure grounds: this is present within the continuum of a bodhisattva whose practice is not yet ultimate, obstructing the seven grounds of the bodhisattva, and is broken by the path free of signs and free of effort — "free of signs" meaning the object is suchness itself, "free of effort" meaning the path of advancement occurs spontaneously, this belonging to the eighth ground and above. As to the ninth, affliction of the pure grounds: this is present within the continuum of a bodhisattva whose practice has reached its ultimate, obstructing the three grounds from the eighth ground upward, and is broken only by the vajra-like concentration and wisdom — "ultimate" meaning that, from the eighth ground upward, every object to be seen has already been fully seen, with nothing left unseen.

It is simply because skillful means differ in depth, and the bodhisattva grounds are correspondingly distinguished, that affliction is differentiated into these nine kinds. With reference to these afflictions, sentient beings are established as four kinds: the ordinary person, the arhat, the one still in training, and the bodhisattva. The first is impure on account of four afflictions — the latent craving, aversion, and delusion, plus the active affliction. The second, the arhat, is impure on account of one affliction alone, the ground of ignorance. The third is impure on account of two — the ordinary person on the path of training, impure through affliction eliminated by insight, and the sage still in training, impure through affliction eliminated by contemplative thought. The fourth is impure on account of two as well — the bodhisattva from the first through the seventh ground, impure through the affliction of the impure grounds, and, from the eighth ground through the remaining three, impure through the affliction of the pure grounds.

Nine Similes for Nine Afflictions

Chinese Source

復次為現此九種煩惱故立九譬者，一為顯貪欲煩惱故，立蓮花化佛譬。譬如蓮花初開之時甚可愛樂，後時萎悴，人厭惡之。貪欲亦爾，初依塵成，後依塵壞，故以華譬貪。而華壞時，化佛出世，如貪覆法身。二為瞋煩惱故，以蜂為譬者，如蜂若為他所觸，放毒螫人。瞋亦如是，若心起瞋，即能自害復能害他。而有甘蜜，即譬法身，為瞋所覆故。三為無明惑故，立穀中粳糧譬。譬如白米，為糠所覆，不得受用。法身亦爾，為無明㲉所隱覆，故不得現。四為上心三種煩惱，立金墮不淨譬。譬如淨潔金寶為糞所塗，違逆人心。離欲之人亦復如是，為上心煩惱違逆其意，故說此譬。法身本淨，為上心惑所覆，故言不淨。

五為顯無明住地故，立貧女寶藏譬。譬如貧女宅中，地下有金寶藏，為地覆故，受貧窮苦。二乘亦爾，為無明所覆，不見佛果故，受四種生死之苦。六為顯見諦惑，立菴羅樹子譬。譬如菴羅子，生芽之時必破其皮，然後得出。皮譬見諦，芽譬法身。見諦亦爾，初見真理即破此惑，法身顯現故。七為顯思惟惑故，立弊帛裹金寶譬。譬如敗衣，不堪服用。身見真實先來已破，聖道對治數數習故。思惟煩惱無復勢力，譬彼敗衣；金如法身，為思惑所障。

八為顯不淨地惑，立貧女懷王子譬。譬如轉輪王子在貧女腹中，胎不能污。七地以還煩惱亦爾，雖名煩惱而有三德：一者無染濁，智慧慈悲所含養故；二者無過失，以不損自他故；三者無量功德，能成熟佛法及眾生故。若長煩惱即成凡夫，不能成熟佛法。若斷煩惱即成二乘，不能成熟眾生。九為顯淨地惑故，立摸中金像譬。譬如鑄金像，未開摸時像已成熟，水等諸物不能破，唯斧等乃能破故。八地以上惑亦如是，唯金剛心能破究竟故。因三種自性，為顯心清淨界名如來藏，故說九種如蓮花等譬。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, to illustrate these nine kinds of affliction, nine similes are established. First, to reveal the affliction of craving, the simile of the lotus and the transformation-buddha is given. It is like a lotus flower, which, when it first blooms, is utterly lovely and delightful, but which later withers, coming to be despised — craving is exactly the same, at first formed dependent on its object, and later decaying dependent on that same object. And at the very moment the flower decays, a transformation-buddha appears from within it, just as craving conceals the dharmakāya. Second, for the affliction of aversion, the bee is taken as the simile: just as a bee, when disturbed by another, releases its poison and stings, aversion is exactly the same, for when the mind gives rise to aversion, it is able to harm both oneself and others — yet there is sweet honey within, the simile for the dharmakāya, concealed by aversion. Third, for the affliction of ignorance, the simile of rice grain within its husk is given: it is like white rice, covered by its husk, unable to be put to use — the dharmakāya is exactly the same, concealed beneath the shell of ignorance, and hence unable to manifest. Fourth, for the three kinds of active affliction, the simile of gold fallen into filth is given: it is like a pure, clean gold treasure smeared with excrement, repugnant to the human mind — one who is free of ordinary desire is exactly the same, repelled by this active affliction. The dharmakāya is originally pure, yet concealed by active affliction, and hence called impure.

Fifth, to reveal the ground of ignorance, the simile of the poor woman's buried treasure is given: it is like a poor woman in whose house, underground, lies a treasure of gold, yet, covered by the earth, she suffers the pain of poverty — followers of the two vehicles are exactly the same, concealed by ignorance, unable to see the fruit of buddhahood, and hence suffering the pain of the four kinds of birth-and-death. Sixth, to reveal the affliction eliminated by insight, the simile of the mango seed is given: it is like a mango seed, which, when its sprout emerges, must first break through its husk before it can come out — the husk is the simile for the affliction eliminated by insight, the sprout the simile for the dharmakāya. Insight into the truth is exactly the same: at the very moment one first sees the truth, this affliction is broken, and the dharmakāya is thereby manifested. Seventh, to reveal the affliction eliminated by contemplative thought, the simile of gold wrapped in worn-out rags is given: it is like tattered clothing, no longer fit to be worn — the true view of a self having already been broken from the start, through the repeated cultivation of the noble path's antidote, the affliction of contemplative thought retains no further power, like those worn-out rags; the gold is like the dharmakāya, obstructed by this affliction.

Eighth, to reveal the affliction of the impure grounds, the simile of a poor woman carrying a future king in her womb is given: it is like a wheel-turning king's son within a poor woman's womb — the womb is unable to defile him. The affliction present through the first seven grounds is exactly the same: though called "affliction," it possesses three virtues — it is unstained, being nourished by wisdom and compassion; it causes no fault, since it harms neither oneself nor others; and it possesses immeasurable virtue, able to bring both the Buddhadharma and sentient beings to maturity. If one allowed this affliction to grow unchecked, one would become an ordinary being, unable to bring the Buddhadharma to maturity; if one simply cut it off entirely, one would become merely a follower of the two vehicles, unable to bring sentient beings to maturity. Ninth, to reveal the affliction of the pure grounds, the simile of a gold image still within its mold is given: it is like a cast gold image, already fully formed even before the mold has been opened, unable to be damaged by water, breakable only by something like an axe — the affliction present from the eighth ground onward is exactly the same, breakable, to its very end, only by the vajra-like mind. On account of the three kinds of self-nature, in order to reveal that the pure realm of mind is called the tathāgatagarbha, these nine similes, beginning with the lotus, are given.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The ninth simile carries a warning worth pausing on, since it cuts against a natural misreading of the whole chapter. A reader could take "affliction conceals but never touches buddha-nature" as license to leave affliction alone — if the gold inside is never actually dirtied, why bother refining it out? The eighth simile, the poor woman carrying a future king, heads this off directly: the treatise insists that affliction at this stage carries three genuine virtues — nourished by wisdom and compassion, harming no one, and actively maturing the Buddhadharma and sentient beings both — and then immediately warns that letting it grow unchecked produces an ordinary being, while simply cutting it off produces a mere follower of the two vehicles, neither outcome able to mature anything at all. The nine similes are not an argument for complacency; they are an argument for exactly calibrated, patient cultivation — treating what conceals the dharmakāya neither as filth to be violently scraped away nor as harmless decoration to be left in place.
