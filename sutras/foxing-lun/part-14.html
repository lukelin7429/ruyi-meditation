Seven Names of the Dharmakāya

The mark of total inclusion continues with seven names for the transformed dharmakāya, each one etymologized in turn: submergence of the aggregates of clinging, stillness of conditioned formations, relinquishment of every remaining companion the two vehicles still carry, transcendence of two kinds of suffering, extraction of the ālaya consciousness at its root, rescue from five ordinary fears, and severance of the six destinies of rebirth.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 14 of 22 complete Source: Taishō T1610, 佛性論 (attributed to Vasubandhu, tr. Paramārtha)

Seven Names, and the First: Submergence

Chinese Source

復次轉依法身有七種名應知。一沈沒，沈沒取陰故；二寂靜，諸行無生故；三棄捨，棄捨諸餘伴故；四過度，出二苦故；五拔除，拔除本識故；六濟度，濟度五怖畏故；七斷，斷於六道果報故。

釋曰：言沈沒取陰者，取名貪愛，有四種：一欲取、二見取、三戒取、四我語取。取有二義：一受取、二受資糧。受取者，如因受生愛。受資糧者，為貪此受故，取四種資糧。四資糧者，即四取也。

一欲取者，貪欲界六塵。二見取者，於欲界中唯除戒取與常見二種，所餘四見名之為見，貪愛此見，名為見取。三戒取者，於三界中取世間邪正二道為離苦得樂，是名為戒，貪著此戒故名為取。四我語取，我語者緣內身故，一切內法為我語，貪著內法名我語取。色無色界定緣內法成，故名我語，貪著此定名之為取。

此四取，前二屬斷見，但執現在謂無未來；後二屬常見，執有未來故。又前二是在家人起，後二是出家人執。又前二在家出家鬪諍因，後二為在家出家修行因。又前二欲取為所成、見取為能成，後二我語為能成、戒取為所成。

復次陰者，有眾多義，如別釋。今略明有二義：一能生取，凡夫五陰能為取因緣故；二從取生，即此五陰是取家果故，故言取陰。而言沈沒者，於法身中因果俱無，故稱沈沒。取為對治故沈，陰為報盡稱沒，故說法身約取陰永無，是名沈沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, it should be understood that the transformed dharmakāya has seven names. First, submergence, because the aggregates of clinging are submerged; second, stillness, because conditioned formations no longer arise; third, relinquishment, because every remaining companion is relinquished; fourth, transcendence, because the two kinds of suffering are transcended; fifth, extraction, because the root consciousness is extracted; sixth, rescue, because the five fears are rescued from; seventh, severance, because the retribution of the six destinies is severed.

Explanation: as to "the aggregates of clinging are submerged" — "clinging" here means craving, and is of four kinds: clinging to desire, clinging to views, clinging to precepts and vows, and clinging to the assertion of self. Clinging carries two senses: taking hold through feeling, and taking hold of the resources that sustain existence. Taking hold through feeling is, for instance, how craving arises dependent on feeling. Taking hold of the resources means that, out of craving for this feeling, one takes hold of four kinds of resources — these four resources being precisely the four kinds of clinging.

As to the first, clinging to desire: this is craving for the six sense-objects of the desire realm. As to the second, clinging to views: within the desire realm, apart from the two views of clinging to precepts and the view of eternalism, the remaining four views are called simply "view," and craving for these views is called clinging to views. As to the third, clinging to precepts and vows: within the three realms, taking hold of the world's mistaken and correct paths as a means to leave suffering and attain bliss is what is called "precept," and craving attachment to such precepts is called "clinging." As to the fourth, clinging to the assertion of self: "the assertion of self" refers to what is conditioned upon the body within — every internal dharma is "the assertion of self," and craving attachment to these internal dharmas is called clinging to the assertion of self. The concentrations of the form and formless realms are accomplished by conditioning upon internal dharmas, and are hence called "the assertion of self"; craving attachment to these concentrations is what is called "clinging."

Of these four kinds of clinging, the first two belong to the view of annihilation, holding to the present alone and denying any future; the latter two belong to the view of eternalism, holding that a future exists. The first two arise in householders, while the latter two are attachments of renunciants. The first two are the cause of strife among both householders and renunciants, while the latter two are the cause of religious practice among both. And of the first two, clinging to desire is what is accomplished and clinging to views is what accomplishes it; of the latter two, clinging to the assertion of self is what accomplishes it and clinging to precepts is what is accomplished.

Next, "aggregates" carries many senses, as explained elsewhere. Here, briefly, there are two senses: able to give rise to clinging, since the ordinary person's five aggregates are able to serve as the cause and condition of clinging; and arising from clinging, since these very five aggregates are themselves the fruit belonging to the household of clinging — hence they are called "the aggregates of clinging." As to why it is called "submergence": within the dharmakāya, neither this cause nor this fruit exists at all. "Clinging" is submerged because it has been counteracted; "aggregates" is submerged because their retribution is exhausted. Hence it is said that, with respect to the dharmakāya, the aggregates of clinging are permanently absent — this is called submergence.

Stillness and Relinquishment

Chinese Source

二寂靜諸行者，一切有為法名行，與四相相應故。四相者，一生、二異、三住、四滅。一切有為法，約前際與生相相應、約後際與滅相相應、約中與異住相相應，行役不息，故名為行。如來法身則不如是，約前無生、約後無滅、中無病老，湛然常住，無生故說寂、無滅故說靜。約前不更生，離意生身故；約後不更死，已過不可思惟退墮故。約前後際不被損污，過無明住地煩惱病故。

三棄捨諸餘，諸餘者，二乘人有三種餘：一煩惱餘，謂無明住地；二業餘，即無漏業；三果報餘，謂意生身。一煩惱餘應滅，二道餘應修，三虛妄餘應除。如來已離虛妄說名無餘，二乘未離故名為餘。如來轉依法身，已度四種生死故、一切煩惱虛妄已滅盡故、一切道已修故、棄生死捨道諦故。此二無四德故、唯法身獨住四德圓滿故，是名棄捨諸餘。

Ru-Yi Translation

As to the second, "conditioned formations no longer arise": every conditioned dharma is called a "formation," being associated with four marks — arising, change, abiding, and cessation. Every conditioned dharma is, at its earlier moment, associated with the mark of arising; at its later moment, associated with the mark of cessation; and in between, associated with the marks of change and abiding — laboring on without rest, and hence called a "formation." The Tathāgata's dharmakāya is not like this at all: at its earlier moment there is no arising, at its later moment no cessation, and in the middle no sickness or aging — it is limpid and permanently abiding. Because there is no arising, it is called "still"; because there is no cessation, it is called "quiescent." As to its earlier moment, it does not arise again, being free from the mind-made body; as to its later moment, it does not die again, having already passed beyond inconceivable falling back. As to both its earlier and later moments, it is never damaged or defiled, being beyond the sickness of affliction that is the ground of ignorance.

As to the third, "every remaining companion is relinquished": followers of the two vehicles have three kinds of remainder — the remainder of affliction, namely the ground of ignorance; the remainder of karma, namely outflow-free karma; and the remainder of retribution, namely the mind-made body. The remainder of affliction should be extinguished, the remainder of the path should be cultivated, and the remainder of falseness should be removed. The Tathāgata, having already left falseness behind, is said to be without remainder; the two vehicles, not yet having left it behind, are said to still have remainder. The Tathāgata's transformed dharmakāya has already transcended the four kinds of birth-and-death, has already utterly extinguished every false affliction, has already fully cultivated the path, and has left behind both saṃsāra and even the truth of the path itself. These two lack the four virtues, while the dharmakāya alone abides fully and completely in all four — hence it is called the relinquishment of every remaining companion.

Transcendence and Extraction

Chinese Source

四過度二苦者，苦，違逆為義。逆有二：一違聖人意，是聖人怨，能惱聖故；二聖意違逆，以聖能除之故。二苦者，一凡夫苦樂二受、二聖人行苦即捨受。又二者，一身苦、二心苦。又二者，一名苦、二色苦。又二者，一二乘界內苦、二菩薩界外苦。故法身地中無二乘麁苦，故名為過；無菩薩四種生死細苦，故名為度，是名過度二苦。

五拔除阿梨耶，阿梨耶者，依隱為義，是生死本，能生四種末故。四末者，煩惱有二、業一、果報一。初煩惱本二者，一者一切諸見以無明為本，無相解脫門為治道。二者離諸見外，一切煩惱以貪愛為本，無願解脫為對治道。次業本一者，以凡夫性為本，凡夫性者即是身見故。次果報本一者，一切生死果報依阿梨耶識為本故，以未離此識果報不斷，於法身中由兩道故二世滅盡故說拔除。言兩道者，一無分別智，能除拔現在虛妄、能清淨法身，即名盡智。二無分別後智，能令未來虛妄永不得起、圓滿法身，即無生智。拔者清淨，滅現在惑，除者圓滿，斷未來惑故名拔除。

Ru-Yi Translation

As to the fourth, "the two kinds of suffering are transcended": "suffering" carries the sense of what runs contrary. This contrariness is of two kinds: what runs contrary to the sage's own intention, being an enemy to the sage, able to trouble him; and what the sage's own intention runs contrary to, since the sage is able to remove it. As to the two kinds of suffering: the ordinary person's twin feelings of pleasure and pain, and the sage's suffering inherent in all conditioned activity, that is, the feeling of neutrality itself. There is, further, another pair: bodily suffering and mental suffering. And another: the suffering of name, and the suffering of form. And another: the suffering internal to the realm of the two vehicles, and the suffering external to that realm, belonging to the bodhisattva. Hence, at the stage of the dharmakāya, there is no coarse suffering of the two vehicles — hence it is called "transcendence" — and no subtle suffering of the bodhisattva's four kinds of birth-and-death either — hence it is called "passing beyond." This is what is meant by transcending the two kinds of suffering.

As to the fifth, "the ālaya consciousness is extracted": "ālaya" carries the sense of dependence and concealment, being the root of saṃsāra, able to give rise to four kinds of derivative. These four derivatives are: two of affliction, one of karma, one of retribution. As to the two roots of affliction: first, every view has ignorance as its root, and the gate of liberation through signlessness is the antidote; second, apart from views, every other affliction has craving as its root, and the gate of liberation through wishlessness is the antidote. As to the one root of karma: this has the nature of the ordinary person as its root, this nature being precisely the view of a bodily self. As to the one root of retribution: every fruit of saṃsāric retribution has the ālaya consciousness as its root — and it is because, so long as this consciousness is not left behind, its fruit of retribution never ceases, that, within the dharmakāya, by means of two paths, both these states are utterly extinguished, and this is called extraction. As to these two paths: first, non-discriminating wisdom, able to extract present falseness, purifying the dharmakāya — this is called the wisdom of exhaustion. Second, the subsequent non-discriminating wisdom, able to prevent future falseness from ever arising again, perfecting the dharmakāya — this is called the wisdom of non-arising. "Extraction" is purification, extinguishing present delusion; "removal" is perfection, cutting off future delusion — hence it is called extraction and removal.

Rescue and Severance

Chinese Source

六濟五怖畏，五怖畏者，一自責畏、二畏他責、三畏治罰、四畏惡道、五畏眾集。一自責畏者，如人作諸罪惡，晝夜怖畏。二他責畏者，既自作惡，恒恐他及冥中天神見之，而懷怖畏。三治罰畏者，身所作惡，恒懼王治。四惡道畏者，既有罪自隨，畏生惡道。五眾集畏者，三業不淨兼知解不深，恒怖畏德眾。若人已證見法身，則離此五畏，故說法身為濟五怖畏。

七斷六道果報者，道義眾多，略說二種：一行處故名為道，五陰為所行處、三世為能行，又以生老病死四苦所遊處，故名為道。二者六種同異故名為道，如人同人異於五道，餘亦如是。是同異類，云何名道？有二：一眾生所輪轉處、二業所行處故，以此二義立名為道。如來法身無復此道，若有餘涅槃業盡、眾生輪轉果未盡。無餘涅槃因果二種俱盡，故名斷滅六道。

若有處說如來法身，當知與此七名相應，是說名竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

As to the sixth, "rescue from the five fears": the five fears are fear of self-reproach, fear of others' reproach, fear of punishment, fear of the evil destinies, and fear of the assembly. Fear of self-reproach is as when a person, having committed various evils, is fearful day and night. Fear of others' reproach is when, having done evil oneself, one constantly dreads that others, or the unseen gods, may witness it. Fear of punishment is when, having done evil bodily deeds, one constantly dreads punishment by the ruler. Fear of the evil destinies is when, one's own transgressions following after oneself, one dreads being reborn into an evil destiny. Fear of the assembly is when, the three karmic actions being impure and one's own understanding not yet deep, one constantly fears the assembly of the virtuous. If a person has already realized and seen the dharmakāya, he is free from these five fears — hence the dharmakāya is said to be rescue from the five fears.

As to the seventh, "severance of the retribution of the six destinies": the meaning of "destiny" is manifold, but briefly there are two senses. First, it is called "destiny" as a place traversed: the five aggregates are that which is traversed, the three times are what does the traversing, and it is further the place where the four sufferings of birth, aging, sickness, and death roam. Second, it is called "destiny" on account of the six kinds of sameness-and-difference: as a human being is the same as other humans yet different from the other five destinies, and so on for the rest. As to this sameness-and-difference — why is it called "destiny"? There are two senses: the place where sentient beings revolve, and the place traversed by karma — it is by these two senses that the name "destiny" is established. The Tathāgata's dharmakāya no longer has any of these destinies at all. In the case of nirvāṇa with residue, karma is exhausted but the fruit of sentient beings' revolving has not yet been exhausted. In nirvāṇa without residue, both cause and effect are utterly exhausted — hence it is called the severance and extinction of the six destinies.

Wherever the Tathāgata's dharmakāya is spoken of, it should be understood as corresponding to these seven names. This concludes the explanation of the names.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Reading these seven names together, a pattern emerges that is easy to miss taking them one at a time: every single name is defined negatively, as the absence or reversal of something specific — clinging submerged, arising stilled, remainder relinquished, suffering transcended, the ālaya extracted, fear rescued from, destiny severed. Not one of the seven describes the dharmakāya by naming a positive attribute it possesses. This is not a limitation of the treatise's vocabulary; it is a deliberate discipline, consistent with how the treatise has handled buddha-nature since Fascicle One's long refutation of fixed self-natures. To describe the dharmakāya positively would risk making it sound like one more thing among things, findable the way a Vaiśeṣika substance was supposed to be findable. Naming it only by what has stopped happening to it is the treatise's way of pointing at something without turning it into an object.
