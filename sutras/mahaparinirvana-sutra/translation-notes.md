# Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra — Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*, Northern Edition (大般涅槃經, Taishō T0374), translated into Chinese by Dharmakṣema (曇無讖) at the court of the Northern Liang in 421 CE. They exist so that vocabulary and citation practice stay consistent across all forty parts.

## The text, and which edition this collection translates

Three distinct versions of this sūtra circulate in the Chinese canon. An earlier, incomplete translation in six fascicles (T0376, the "Six-Fascicle Text," tr. Faxian and Buddhabhadra, c. 418 CE) covers only material corresponding to roughly the first third of Dharmakṣema's text and ends before the sūtra's central teachings on buddha-nature are reached. Dharmakṣema's own translation (T0374), forty fascicles in thirteen chapters, is the **Northern Edition** — the version this collection translates in full. A later editorial team in the Liu Song, led by Huiyan, Huiguan, and the lay scholar Xie Lingyun, cross-checked Dharmakṣema's text against a Sanskrit manuscript Faxian had brought back and produced the **Southern Edition** (T0375), thirty-six fascicles reorganized into twenty-five chapters. This collection translates only the Northern Edition, and preserves its fascicle and chapter numbering throughout — part numbers in this collection correspond one-to-one with the Northern Edition's own forty fascicles (卷), not with the Southern Edition's different scheme.

The base text runs to roughly 335,000 Chinese characters. Fascicle length is unusually even for a text this size — every fascicle falls between about 6,200 and 10,400 characters, with no single outsized or truncated fascicle — which is why this collection follows the straightforward convention of one part per fascicle, forty parts in all, rather than subdividing chapters further the way some shorter treatises on this site do.

## Structure: thirteen chapters, several spanning many fascicles

The Northern Edition names thirteen chapters (品), several of which run six or seven fascicles and are themselves divided by the sūtra's own colophon into numbered sub-sections (之一, 之二, 之三…):

## Commentary sources, and a version caveat that matters throughout

This sūtra attracted an unusually rich commentarial tradition, but three of its major commentaries were not all written against the same edition, and citing them without noting which edition each assumes would misrepresent what they actually say:

- **Huiyuan's *Commentary on the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*** (大般涅槃經義記, T1764, 隋 慧遠述) is written directly against the Northern Edition translated here, fascicle for fascicle. It is this collection's primary commentarial source, cited by fascicle and section without any translation layer needed between his citations and this collection's own part numbering.
- **Bao Liang's *Compilation of Explanations of the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*** (大般涅槃經集解, T1763, 梁 寶亮等集), seventy-one fascicles compiling commentary from more than a dozen fifth- and sixth-century masters, is organized around the Southern Edition but is cited in this collection wherever it preserves material relevant to a Northern Edition passage — most importantly for the icchantika question in 如來性品 and 一切大眾所問品 (Parts 4–10), where it preserves some of the only surviving fragments of Zhu Daosheng's own reasoning for his famous, initially condemned claim that even the icchantika possesses buddha-nature.
- **Zhiyi and Guanding's *Profound Meaning of the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*** (大般涅槃經玄義, T1765) and ***Commentary on the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*** (大般涅槃經疏, T1767) are both written against the Southern Edition's twenty-five-chapter scheme, which does not map chapter-for-chapter or fascicle-for-fascicle onto the Northern Edition. Wherever this collection cites either work, the note identifies the corresponding Southern Edition chapter and fascicle explicitly, rather than asserting a direct correspondence to the Northern Edition part being discussed. Readers should not assume a citation from these two works to "fascicle N" means Northern Edition fascicle N.

Where no surviving ancient commentary treats a passage, reading notes are marked as original commentary from Ru-Yi Meditation Center, following this site's standing practice.

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 一闡提 | icchantika | Kept as the Sanskrit loanword, matching this site's *Foxing Lun* pages. This sūtra is the classical locus for the debate over whether the icchantika can attain buddhahood; Part 1 already excludes the icchantika from the assembly's universal loving-kindness, setting up a tension the sūtra's later chapters (especially 如來性品) address directly. |
| 佛性 | buddha-nature | Matched to this site's existing *Foxing Lun* and tathāgatagarbha-line pages. |
| 常樂我淨 | permanence, bliss, self, purity | The sūtra's reversal of the four ordinary marks (impermanence, suffering, no-self, impurity), applied to nirvāṇa and the Dharma-body — a central doctrinal thread from 壽命品 onward. |
| 大般涅槃 | Mahāparinirvāṇa | Kept as the Sanskrit title throughout rather than translated as "Great Final Nirvāṇa," matching how this sūtra is referred to in English-language scholarship generally. |
| 波羅奢花 | palāśa flower | The flame-of-the-forest tree (Butea monosperma), whose red-orange blossoms furnish this sūtra's recurring simile for blood appearing on skin in anguish. Distinguished from 波利質多 (pārijāta), a different tree named later in Part 1's list of heavenly flowers — the two are not interchangeable despite superficially similar transliteration patterns. |
| 力士 (力士生地) | the Mallas | The clan/people of Kuśinagara, not "strongmen" in a generic sense; 力士生地 is rendered "the land/birthplace of the Mallas." |
| 阿利羅跋提河 | the Ajitavatī River | Following the identification used in existing English-language scholarship on this sūtra, rather than coining a fresh transliteration. |
| 無邊身 (菩薩) | the bodhisattva Boundless Body | Translated rather than transliterated, since 無邊身 functions descriptively in the text (a body literally without bound, containing cities in every pore) and has no fixed independent Sanskrit-name convention on this site the way Mañjuśrī or Mahākāśyapa do. |
| 摩訶迦旃延 / 薄俱羅 / 優波難陀 | Mahākātyāyana / Bakkula / Upananda | Standard Sanskrit forms of well-attested disciples' names. |
| 娑婆世界 | the Sahā world | Kept as the standard transliteration (this world-system, the one Śākyamuni teaches in), matching general site convention for this term. |

## A note on Part 1's translation of proper names

Part 1's enormous assembly list names dozens of kings, nāga kings, spirit kings, and other beings by titles transliterated in the Chinese (e.g. 和修吉龍王, 毘沙門, 睒婆利王). Where a name has a well-attested Sanskrit or Pāli original independently identifiable from Buddhist literature generally (Vāsuki, Vaiśravaṇa, Śambara, and so on), this collection uses that reconstructed form. Where a name is a Chinese descriptive title without an independently attested Sanskrit original (e.g. 可畏王, "the fearsome king," or 妙音蜂王, "the bee-king of wondrous voice"), this collection translates the title's sense directly into English (e.g. "the rākṣasa king Bhīṣaṇa" glosses 可畏 rather than reconstructing an unattested Sanskrit proper name) or, where translation would misleadingly suggest independent Sanskrit attestation, renders the descriptive sense of the Chinese as an English epithet. Later parts translating similar lists of names will follow this same distinction and add entries here as new cases arise.
