Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra.

The Buddha's own account of his final day. At Kuśinagara, among the twin sāla trees, the whole of existence — monks and nuns, kings and queens, nāgas and gods up through the Brahmā heavens, even Māra — gathers to make a last offering, and every single one is refused, until the outcaste Cunda's own gift becomes the occasion for the sūtra's central teaching: that the Tathāgata's Dharma-body did not begin at Bodh Gayā and will not end tonight, that nirvāṇa itself is permanence, bliss, self, and purity rather than mere extinction, and that every being without exception — even the icchantika, cut off in every other text from ever attaining it — possesses buddha-nature and will one day become a buddha. Forty fascicles, translated by Dharmakṣema in 421 CE, structured around thirteen chapters that this collection presents across forty pages, one fascicle to a page.

How to read this collection

The sūtra runs to roughly 335,000 Chinese characters across forty fascicles (卷), each translated here as one page. This is the Northern Edition (T0374) that Dharmakṣema brought from Central Asia and translated at the court of the Northern Liang in 421 CE — the version thirteen fascicles longer than the fragmentary six-fascicle translation that preceded it, and the base text later editors expanded further, with the help of a Faxian manuscript, into the thirty-six-fascicle Southern Edition. This collection follows the Northern Edition's own fascicle and chapter divisions throughout, without re-mapping them onto the Southern Edition's different scheme.

The opening fascicles stage an enormous assembly and a chain of refused offerings before the sūtra's teaching proper begins with Cunda's own gift in fascicle 2. From there the sūtra unfolds across thirteen named chapters (品), several of them — 如來性品, 光明遍照高貴德王菩薩品, 師子吼菩薩品, 迦葉菩薩品 — running six or seven fascicles apiece, each divided by the sūtra's own colophon into numbered sub-sections (之一, 之二, 之三…) that this collection preserves in its part titles.

What each page includes

  • The Chinese source text beside our English translation, checked against the base text for coverage.
  • Reading notes drawn from Huiyuan's Commentary on the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra (大般涅槃經義記, T1764) wherever it treats the passage, and from Bao Liang's Compilation of Explanations (大般涅槃經集解, T1763) for the chapters on tathāgatagarbha and buddha-nature where it preserves otherwise-lost readings, including fragments of Zhu Daosheng's own reasoning on the icchantika question.
  • Where Zhiyi and Guanding's Profound Meaning (玄義, T1765) and Commentary (疏, T1767) are cited, an explicit note on how their chapter divisions — written against the differently-organized Southern Edition — map onto this Northern Edition's own fascicles, rather than a silent chapter-for-chapter substitution.
  • Original reading notes from Ru-Yi Meditation Center, clearly marked as such, wherever a passage raises a question no surviving ancient commentary settles.

The Sūtra's Thirteen Chapters

Several chapters run many fascicles long and are themselves divided by the sūtra's own colophon into numbered sub-sections (之一, 之二…), which this collection's part titles preserve rather than smooth over.

Fascicles 1–3 · 壽命品
The Chapter on the Buddha's Lifespan
The final assembly and its refused offerings, Cunda's own offering finally accepted, and the teaching — provoked by that offering — that the Tathāgata's lifespan and Dharma-body are without end.
Parts 1–3
Fascicle 3 · 金剛身品 / 名字功德品
The Vajra Body, and the Virtues of This Sūtra's Name
Why the Tathāgata's body cannot be destroyed, and what is gained simply by hearing this sūtra's title.
Part 3
Fascicles 4–10 · 如來性品
The Chapter on the Tathāgata's Nature
The sūtra's longest single chapter, and the classical source for its central claim: that every being possesses buddha-nature, examined here at length, including the icchantika question this sūtra made famous.
Parts 4–10
Fascicle 10 · 一切大眾所問品
Questions from the Whole Assembly
The assembly presses its remaining doubts before the sūtra turns to conduct.
Part 10
Fascicles 11 · 現病品
Manifesting Illness
Why the Tathāgata, already free of illness, manifests sickness for the sake of teaching beings.
Part 11
Fascicles 11–14 · 聖行品
Noble Conduct
The bodhisattva's practice examined through the five kinds of conduct: sacred, pure, heavenly, infant-like, and sick.
Parts 11–14
Fascicles 15–20 · 梵行品
Pure Conduct
The brahmic conduct of loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity, unfolded at length, including the famous simile of the seven stages in the growth of milk into ghee.
Parts 15–20
Fascicle 20 · 嬰兒行品
Infant-Like Conduct
Why the Tathāgata sometimes teaches as simply and unassumingly as an infant might act.
Part 20
Fascicles 21–26 · 光明遍照高貴德王菩薩品
The Bodhisattva King of Light
A single extended dialogue on the bodhisattva's ten powers, unfolded across six fascicles.
Parts 21–26
Fascicles 27–32 · 師子吼菩薩品
The Bodhisattva Lion's Roar
A second extended dialogue, on buddha-nature examined from every angle the bodhisattva Lion's Roar can press the question.
Parts 27–32
Fascicles 33–38 · 迦葉菩薩品
The Bodhisattva Kāśyapa
Kāśyapa's own extended questioning, closing the sūtra's doctrinal core before the narrative resumes.
Parts 33–38
Fascicles 39–40 · 憍陳如品
The Chapter on Kauṇḍinya
The sūtra's close: debates with rival ascetics and heterodox teachers, Subhadra's conversion as the Buddha's last disciple, and — in the Northern Edition translated here — the sūtra's own ending, reached before the cremation and relic-division narrated only in the Southern Edition's additional fascicles.
Parts 39–40

常樂我淨 — permanence, bliss, self, purityThe sūtra's own reversal of the four ordinary marks of impermanence, suffering, no-self, and impurity, applied specifically to nirvāṇa.
佛性 — buddha-natureMatched to this site's existing Foxing Lun and tathāgatagarbha-line pages.
北本 / 南本 — Northern Edition / Southern EditionThis collection translates only the Northern Edition (T0374, 40 fascicles, 13 chapters). The Southern Edition (T0375, 36 fascicles, 25 chapters) reorganizes the same material with the help of a Faxian manuscript; its chapter numbering does not match the Northern Edition's own.
Commentary version caveatZhiyi and Guanding's 玄義 (T1765) and 疏 (T1767) were written against the Southern Edition. Citations from them note the corresponding Southern Edition passage rather than assuming a direct fascicle match to this Northern Edition.
