Hearing His Very Name Ensures Non-Regression

"Good man, you should now observe my own pure Dharma-body, accomplished through an ocean of boundless kalpas carrying out the bodhisattva's own practice; across boundless kalpas, it is hard to hear of and hard to see, and one plants but a small root of goodness toward it even so. Even a śrāvaka-bodhisattva still cannot manage to hear my very name, still less behold my own body. Good man, should any sentient being hear my name, he will never again regress from unsurpassed, perfect awakening; whether he sees me, touches me, welcomes or escorts me, follows behind me, sees my own light, or sees a buddha-world tremble; and even seeing or hearing of me within a dream — it is likewise so. Should he be mindful of me in thought, whether for a single day and night, seven days and nights, half a month, a month, a year, a hundred years, a kalpa, a hundred kalpas, or even unspeakable, unspeakable numbers of world-dust-mote kalpas; should he be mindful of me for a single lifetime, a hundred lifetimes, or even unspeakable, unspeakable numbers of world-dust-mote lifetimes — it is likewise so. Through wondrous, skillful means as numerous as the motes of dust of these very worlds, I cause every sentient being to raise the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect awakening, and to abide without regression. Good man, should any sentient being hear that I cultivate a pure buddha-land, he will surely attain rebirth within a pure world; should any sentient being see or hear of my own body, he will surely be born within my own pure body. Good man, you should observe my pure Dharma-body once more."

Even a Dream of Him Ensures Non-Regression

Chinese Source

善男子！汝且觀我清淨法身，無量劫海行菩薩行之所成就；無量劫中，難聞、難見，種少善根。聲聞菩薩，猶尚不得聞我名字，況見我身。善男子！若有眾生聞我名者，於阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，不復退轉；若見，若觸，若迎送，若隨行，若見光明，若見震動諸佛世界；乃至夢中見聞我者，亦復如是。若思惟念我，若一日一夜，若七日七夜，若半月，若一月，若一歲，若百歲，若一劫，若百劫，乃至不可說不可說世界微塵等劫；若一生念我，若百生，乃至不可說不可說世界微塵等生念我，亦復如是。以如是等世界微塵等諸妙方便，令一切眾生，發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，住不退轉。善男子！若有眾生聞我修習淨佛剎者，必得往生清淨世界；若有眾生見聞我身，必得生我清淨身中。善男子！汝復觀我清淨法身。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good man, you should now observe my own pure Dharma-body, accomplished through an ocean of boundless kalpas carrying out the bodhisattva's own practice; across boundless kalpas, it is hard to hear of and hard to see, and one plants but a small root of goodness toward it even so. Even a śrāvaka-bodhisattva still cannot manage to hear my very name, still less behold my own body.

"Good man, should any sentient being hear my name, he will never again regress from unsurpassed, perfect awakening; whether he sees me, touches me, welcomes or escorts me, follows behind me, sees my own light, or sees a buddha-world tremble; and even seeing or hearing of me within a dream — it is likewise so. Through wondrous, skillful means as numerous as the motes of dust of these very worlds, I cause every sentient being to raise the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect awakening, and to abide without regression.

"Good man, should any sentient being hear that I cultivate a pure buddha-land, he will surely attain rebirth within a pure world; should any sentient being see or hear of my own body, he will surely be born within my own pure body. Good man, you should observe my pure Dharma-body once more."

Reading note — after Fazang

The escalating list of durations here — a single day and night, up through world-dust-mote-many kalpas and lifetimes — closely mirrors similar formulas used elsewhere in Mahāyāna literature for the benefits of mindfulness of a buddha's own name, and Fazang reads its application here to a bodhisattva rather than a buddha as itself remarkable: Samantabhadra is describing his own name and form as already carrying this same guaranteed, non-regressing efficacy, ordinarily reserved in other texts for buddha-recollection alone. The closing promise — hearing of his pure land ensures rebirth there, seeing his body ensures birth within it — anticipates, without yet naming it, the very kind of pure-land devotion the coming hymn will elaborate at length, where different beings behold different buddhas and their lands according to their own accumulated merit.
