Equal to Empty Space

Sudhana's own contemplation of the tower deepens into a series of equations: equal to empty space, equal to the dharma-realm without obstruction, equal to the reality-limit reaching every place, equal to a tathāgata free of falsehood and unattached to anything — like a reflection, a dream, lightning, an echo, arising entirely from conditions, neither existent nor non-existent.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 276 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四之十五 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

Like a Reflection, a Dream, Lightning, an Echo

Chinese Source

「作是念已，又復等觀，猶如虛空；等觀如法界，無有障礙；等觀如實際，至一切處；等觀如來，除諸虛妄，無所染著；等觀如影、如夢、如電、如響，悉從緣起，非有非無，深心信解。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Having formed this thought, he further contemplated it as equal to empty space; contemplated it as equal to the dharma-realm, without any obstruction; contemplated it as equal to the reality-limit, reaching every place; contemplated it as equal to the tathāgata, free of every falsehood and unattached to anything; contemplated it as like a reflection, like a dream, like lightning, like an echo, all arising entirely from conditions, neither existent nor non-existent, with deep faith and understanding.

Reading note — after Fazang

Four successive equations — empty space, dharma-realm, reality-limit, tathāgata — each name a different traditional designation for ultimate reality in Mahāyāna thought, and Sudhana contemplates the tower as identical to all four at once before the passage pivots to the four classic similes of conditioned appearance: reflection, dream, lightning, echo. Fazang reads this pairing as balancing two truths simultaneously — the tower's own ultimate nature is empty space itself, and yet, precisely because it arises from conditions like any dream or echo, it is neither simply existent nor simply non-existent.
