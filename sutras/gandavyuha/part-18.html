The Lotus from the Ocean Floor

Sāgaramegha's twelve years of ocean-gazing yield a vision: a jeweled lotus rising from the sea floor, a buddha seated upon it, and a sūtra so vast that all the ocean turned to ink could not exhaust a single chapter — followed, as always, by the teacher's own disclaimer and referral onward.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 18 of 454 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

The Lotus, and the Buddha Upon It

Chinese Source

我如是思惟時，復作是念：『世間頗更有法廣此大海，深此大海，莊嚴於此大海者不？』作是念已，即見海底水輪之際，妙寶蓮華自然涌出。伊那尼羅寶為莖，閻浮檀金為葉，沈水香寶為臺，碼碯寶為鬚，彌覆大海。百萬阿脩羅王悉共執持；百萬摩尼寶莊嚴網，羅覆其上；百萬龍王，雨以香水；百萬迦樓羅王，銜妙寶繒帶，垂下莊嚴；百萬羅剎王，慈心觀察；百萬夜叉王，恭敬禮拜；百萬乾闥婆王，讚歎供養；百萬天王，雨天香華、末香幢幡、妙寶衣雲；百萬梵王，稽首敬禮；百萬淨居天，各敬禮已，合掌而住；百萬轉輪王，七寶莊嚴；百萬海神王，從大海出，恭敬禮拜。百萬夜光寶光明網，普照一切；百萬淨寶，百萬明淨寶，以為莊嚴；百萬寶藏，出無量光明，普照一切；百萬閻浮檀寶，安住莊嚴；百萬金剛師子寶，不可沮壞，清淨莊嚴；百萬日藏寶，明淨光明，普照一切；百萬不可壞摩尼寶，出生長養一切善行；百萬如意寶珠，無盡莊嚴。

彼寶蓮華，如來無上善根所起，悉令一切菩薩諸願成滿，十方世界無不顯現；出生一切諸法如幻，從淨法生，無諍方便法之所莊嚴；行如夢法，無為法印，究竟到於無礙方便；普覆十方一切法界，唯佛境界，隨順世間無量阿僧祇劫，歎不可盡。見彼華上，有一如來結跏趺坐，彼佛淨身，上至非想非非想天，無不充滿。見彼如來坐此莊嚴寶蓮華座，不可思議大眾圍遶，見不可思議圓滿光明莊嚴；見不可思議相好莊嚴、見不可思議神力自在、見不可思議如來妙色、見不可思議無見頂相、見不可思議廣長舌相、念不可思議清淨音聲、思惟不可思議圓滿音聲、見不可思議如來諸力、解了不可思議清淨無畏、解了不可思議一切諸辯、憶念菩薩過去不可思議大劫本行、見不可思議菩提自在、見不可思議正法雲、見不可思議普門莊嚴身、見不可思議身左右端嚴、見辨一切不可思議事，饒益眾生。

Ru-Yi Translation

While I was contemplating in this way, I thought further: "Is there, in this world, any dharma broader than this great ocean, deeper than this great ocean, that could adorn this great ocean?" Having had this thought, I then saw, at the very base of the ocean's own water-wheel, a marvelous jeweled lotus spontaneously spring forth. Its stem was of indranīla gem, its leaves of Jambū-river gold, its calyx of aloeswood incense-jewel, its stamens of carnelian, and it covered the entire ocean. A million asura kings together held it up; a million net of maṇi jewels, adorning it, spread over its top; a million nāga-kings rained down fragrant water upon it; a million garuḍa-kings, holding marvelous jeweled ribbons in their beaks, let them hang down as adornment; a million rākṣasa kings gazed upon it with kindness; a million yakṣa kings reverently bowed before it; a million gandharva kings praised it and made offerings; a million heavenly kings rained down heavenly fragrant flowers, powdered incense, banners, and clouds of marvelous jeweled robes; a million Brahmā kings bowed their heads in reverence; a million pure-abode devas, each having bowed in reverence, stood with palms joined; a million wheel-turning kings adorned it with the seven treasures; a million ocean-deity kings came forth from the great ocean and bowed reverently. A million nets of light from night-luminous jewels universally illumined everything; a million pure jewels, a million pure and radiant jewels, adorned it further; a million jewel-treasuries released light without measure, universally illumining everything; a million Jambū-river gold jewels stood adorning it; a million indestructible vajra-lion jewels, which nothing could overturn, purely adorned it; a million sun-treasury jewels, pure and radiant, universally illumined everything; a million indestructible maṇi jewels gave rise to and nurtured every wholesome practice; and a million wish-fulfilling pearls adorned it inexhaustibly.

That jeweled lotus arose from the Tathāgata's own unsurpassed root of goodness, causing every bodhisattva's every vow to be fulfilled entirely, manifest in every world of the ten directions without exception; giving rise to every dharma as an illusion, born from the pure Dharma, adorned by the dharma of skillful means free of contention; practicing the dharma that is like a dream, the seal of the unconditioned, reaching at last the unobstructed skillful means; universally covering the entire Dharma-realm of the ten directions — this being the Buddha's own domain alone, according with the world through eons immeasurable and incalculable, praised without end. I saw, upon that lotus, a single tathāgata seated cross-legged, that buddha's own pure body reaching up to fill even the Heaven of Neither Perception Nor Non-Perception, leaving nothing at all unfilled. I beheld that tathāgata seated upon this adorned, jeweled lotus-throne, surrounded by an assembly beyond conceiving; I beheld an adornment of perfect light beyond conceiving; an adornment of excellent marks beyond conceiving; a sovereign spiritual power beyond conceiving; the Tathāgata's own sublime form beyond conceiving; the crown-mark none can see the top of, beyond conceiving; the long, broad tongue-mark beyond conceiving; I held in mind a pure voice beyond conceiving; I contemplated a perfect voice beyond conceiving; I beheld the Tathāgata's every power beyond conceiving; I understood a pure fearlessness beyond conceiving; I understood every eloquence beyond conceiving; I called to mind the bodhisattva's own past conduct through great eons beyond conceiving; I beheld a sovereign mastery of awakening beyond conceiving; a cloud of true Dharma beyond conceiving; a body adorned with the universal gate beyond conceiving; a body's own excellence, left and right, beyond conceiving; and I beheld every matter discerned beyond conceiving, bringing benefit to every sentient being.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang parses the catalogue of a million attendants and adornments into two groups of clauses, and the distinction matters: the first eleven — asura kings, nāga-kings, garuḍa-kings, and so on through the wheel-turning kings and ocean-deity kings — he reads as adornment of the world of sentient beings (眾生世間莊嚴); the remaining nine — the nets of light, the jewel-treasuries, the various named jewels — as adornment of the container world, the physical setting itself (器世間莊嚴). The lotus's own arising, described in the second paragraph, he reads as belonging to a third category again: the world of wisdom and true awakening (智正覺世間莊嚴), since the lotus is said to arise from the Tathāgata's own root of goodness rather than from any ordinary cause. The twenty clauses describing the buddha seated upon it split the same way once more: the first ten name what the Buddha's own fruit already fully possesses (his light, his marks, his voice, his power), the second ten name that fruit's own sovereign, wondrous function in action. Three separate adornments, at three separate scales, arranged in the same fixed order this collection has already seen at Jetavana itself in Part 2 — proof, on Fazang's reading, that the same fundamental pattern governs a scene this modest in scope (one monk, one shoreline, twelve years of quiet gazing) exactly as it governed the Buddha's own cosmic assembly.

The Universal Eye Sūtra, and the Referral Onward

Chinese Source

「時，彼如來即申右手，而摩我頂，說普眼經。唯是如來境界，出生一切菩薩淨行、普照一切法界、攝取圓滿一切法界、普照一切嚴淨佛剎、降伏一切眾魔外道、悉令一切眾生歡喜、普照一切眾生所行隨其所應無不顯現、普照一切眾生根輪。善男子！我從佛聞此普眼經，皆悉受持，讀誦通利，正念思惟。善男子！假使有人以大海等墨，須彌聚筆，書寫此經，一一品、一一法門、一一方便、一一生法門、一一句中義味，猶不能盡。善男子！我於佛所千二百歲，聞受此經；於一一日，受阿僧祇品，多聞陀羅尼光明力故；究竟阿僧祇品，百門陀羅尼光明力故；攝取阿僧祇品，無量旋陀羅尼光明力故；分別阿僧祇品，隨順分別諸地陀羅尼光明力故；淨阿僧祇品，嚴勝陀羅尼光明力故；出生阿僧祇品，隨諭莊嚴陀羅尼光明力故；說阿僧祇品，明淨音聲陀羅尼光明力故；照阿僧祇品，虛空藏陀羅尼光明力故；廣阿僧祇品，樹提沙陀羅尼光明力故；成阿僧祇品，海藏陀羅尼光明力故。其有十方諸天、天王；諸龍、龍王；夜叉、夜叉王；乾闥婆、乾闥婆王；阿修羅、阿修羅王；迦樓羅、迦樓羅王；緊那羅、緊那羅王；人、人王；梵天、梵天王；若來問我，我即為彼開發顯現，分別讚說，悉令安住此普眼經。善男子！我唯知此一法門，豈能盡知菩薩諸行；何以故？諸菩薩等，究竟一切行故；究竟大願海，一切劫海不斷絕故；入眾生海，應受化者悉隨順故；深入一切眾生心海，出生如來十力智光明故；悉知一切眾生諸根，隨所應化，不失時故；入一切佛剎海，出生佛剎堅固願故；究竟恭敬供養一切佛海，大願力故；度一切法海，解脫智故；深入功德海，如說修行故；度一切眾生語言海，於十方剎轉法輪故。善男子！汝詣南方六十由旬，有一國土，名曰海岸；彼有比丘，名曰善住；應往問彼云何菩薩修清淨行。」

時，善財童子頭面禮足，遶無數匝；眷仰無量，辭退南行。

Ru-Yi Translation

"At that time that tathāgata extended his right hand and placed it upon my crown, and expounded the Universal Eye Sūtra. This sūtra is the Tathāgata's own domain alone, giving rise to the pure practice of every bodhisattva; universally illumining the entire Dharma-realm; gathering in and perfecting the entire Dharma-realm; universally illumining every adorned, pure buddha-land; subduing every māra and heterodox path; causing every sentient being to rejoice entirely; universally illumining the practice of every sentient being, manifesting according to what each requires without exception; and universally illumining the wheel of every sentient being's own faculties. Good son, I heard this Universal Eye Sūtra from the Buddha, and upheld it entirely, reciting it fluently, rightly holding it in mind and contemplating it. Good son, suppose someone were to take the entire ocean as ink and gather Mount Sumeru itself as a brush, and write out this sūtra — even then, not a single chapter, not a single Dharma-gate, not a single skillful means, not a single gate of arising, not the meaning and flavor within a single phrase, could ever be exhausted. Good son, I spent twelve hundred years in the Buddha's own presence hearing and receiving this sūtra; in each and every day, I received chapters beyond counting through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī of vast learning; I brought chapters beyond counting to their very end through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī of a hundred gates; I gathered in chapters beyond counting through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī of boundless revolution; I discerned chapters beyond counting through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī that discerns, according with each, every ground; I purified chapters beyond counting through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī of adorned excellence; I gave rise to chapters beyond counting through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī of adornment according with analogy; I expounded chapters beyond counting through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī of a bright, pure voice; I illumined chapters beyond counting through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī of the treasury of space; I broadened chapters beyond counting through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī of Jyotiṣka; and I accomplished chapters beyond counting through the power of the light of the dhāraṇī of the ocean-treasury. Whatever devas or deva-kings, nāgas or nāga-kings, yakṣas or yakṣa-kings, gandharvas or gandharva-kings, asuras or asura-kings, garuḍas or garuḍa-kings, kiṃnaras or kiṃnara-kings, humans or human kings, or Brahmā gods or Brahmā-kings of the ten directions came and asked me about it, I would open and reveal it for their sake, discerning and extolling it, causing every one of them to abide firmly in this Universal Eye Sūtra.

"Good son, I know only this one Dharma-gate — how could I possibly know all of a bodhisattva's own practices in full? Why is this so? Because the bodhisattvas reach the very end of every practice; reach the very end of the ocean of the great vow, unbroken through the ocean of every eon; enter the ocean of sentient beings, according entirely with whoever is to be transformed; enter deeply into the ocean of every sentient being's own mind, giving rise to the light of the Tathāgata's own wisdom of the ten powers; know entirely every sentient being's own faculties, never once missing the right moment for whatever transformation is needed; enter the ocean of every buddha-land, giving rise to the firm vow of every buddha-land; reach the very end of reverently making offerings to the ocean of every buddha, through the power of the great vow; cross the ocean of every dharma, through the wisdom of liberation; enter deeply into the ocean of merit, practicing exactly as it is taught; and cross the ocean of every sentient being's own language, turning the Dharma-wheel in every land of the ten directions. Good son, sixty yojanas to the south of here, there is a land called Sea Shore; there is a monk there named Susthitamati. Go to him and ask how a bodhisattva cultivates pure practice."

At that time Sudhana bowed his head to his feet, circled him countless times, and, gazing up at him with boundless longing, took his leave and traveled onward to the south.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang glosses "Universal Eye" (普眼) as 從所詮為目 — "a name taken from what the sūtra itself expounds" — a general title, which the sūtra's own text then unpacks into a further set of specific names (universally illumining the Dharma-realm, subduing māras, and so on). The staggering image of the ocean as ink and Sumeru as a brush, still unable to exhaust a single chapter, he treats less as poetic exaggeration than as a direct claim about scale: this sūtra is, in Fazang's own words elsewhere in this chapter's opening, "the one-vehicle's own inexhaustible scripture" (一乘無盡修多羅) — inexhaustibility is not a feature added to it, but its actual nature.

Fazang closes with a small but telling observation about the refrain running through the entire second half of this teacher's speech: 皆云海者，以此比丘但知自一海，更有多海非己能知，故皆推也 — "everything is spoken of as an 'ocean' because this monk knows only his own single ocean; there are yet more oceans beyond what he himself can know, and it is for this reason that he defers in every case." Twelve years of contemplation, a vision vast enough to hold a buddha and a sūtra no amount of ink could exhaust, and still: one ocean, known; countless oceans, unknown. Meghaśrī's own disclaimer in Part 16 and Sāgaramegha's here are not false modesty repeating itself — they are the same structural honesty this pilgrimage will keep demonstrating, teacher after teacher, as what a genuine spiritual friend's self-knowledge actually looks like.
