The Buddha's Light, and What It Shows

The Buddha releases a ray of light from between his brows, and the assembly beholds every world at once — buddhas teaching, palaces of every kind of being, human cities — down to what appears within a single pore, and what it actually takes for a sentient being to perceive any of it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 7 of 454 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

The Light Between His Brows

Chinese Source

爾時，世尊欲令諸菩薩安住師子奮迅三昧故，放眉間白毫相光，名普照三世法界門；不可說世界微塵等光明，以為眷屬，普照十方一切世界海。時，祇洹林菩薩大眾，普雲集者，悉見一切法界、虛空界等一切佛剎種種色、種種清淨、種種安住、種種形；如是等一切世界諸大菩薩，現坐道場，菩薩圍遶，諸天供養，成等正覺。或見於不可說佛剎微塵等，諸眷屬中，出妙音聲，充滿法界，轉淨法輪；或見在天宮殿，在龍宮殿，夜叉、乾闥婆、阿修羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽，及人、非人等諸宮殿中；或見在人聚落城邑，大王京都，現種種身、種種姓名、種種色、種種圓光、種種光網、種種辯、種種眷屬、種種持、種種教持、種種音聲，而為說法。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to cause the bodhisattvas to abide firmly in the Lion's-Vigor Samādhi, released from between his brows a ray of white-curl light named the Gate Universally Illumining the Dharma-Realm of the Three Times; light as numerous as motes of dust in world-systems beyond telling served as its retinue, universally illumining the ocean of every world in the ten directions. At that time the great assembly of bodhisattvas gathered like clouds throughout Jeta's Grove all beheld the entire Dharma-realm and the realm of space, every buddha-land in every kind of color, every kind of purity, every kind of abiding, and every kind of shape; and, in every such world, the great bodhisattvas there, seated at the bodhimaṇḍa, surrounded by bodhisattvas, honored with the devas' offerings, attaining perfect awakening. Some beheld, among retinues as numerous as motes of dust in buddha-lands beyond telling, sublime voices coming forth, filling the Dharma-realm, turning the pure Dharma-wheel; some beheld palaces of the devas, palaces of the nāgas, palaces of yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, and both humans and non-humans; some beheld, in human villages, towns, and the capitals of great kings, manifestations of every kind of body, every kind of name and lineage, every kind of color, every kind of halo, every kind of net of light, every kind of eloquence, every kind of retinue, every kind of upholding, every kind of teaching upheld, and every kind of voice, expounding the Dharma.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang divides this whole light-emission section into three: first the occasion for the light (this passage), then the assembly's own vision depending on that light, then the benefit gained from truly seeing. He is explicit about why the light appears at all, rather than the teaching simply continuing in speech: 令菩薩因人隨分亦得此三昧故 — "so that the bodhisattvas, as individuals, might each attain some portion of this very samādhi for themselves" — the light does not merely illustrate what Samantabhadra's words have already described; it lets each member of the assembly personally verify it. He glosses the light's own name in two parts: 放白毫光者，顯中道平等白淨法界 — "releasing white-curl light makes manifest the pure, white, equal Dharma-realm of the middle way"; and "照法界" — its full name, the Gate Universally Illumining the Dharma-Realm of the Three Times — is 依義立名 — "a name given according to its own meaning: it illumines and makes present the Dharma-gate of the three times' own Dharma-realm, so that the bodhisattvas may enter through this very gate."

On the two kinds of vision this section shows — worlds, and then the palaces within them — Fazang notes that the palace-visions repeat the same world-scale scene at a smaller grain: 即於前世界中更取微細十種宮殿 — "taking up, within that very same world already described, ten further, finer palace-scenes." Both together, he says, are answering the assembly's own original sixty-clause question from Part 2 — what the Tathāgata's sovereign mastery, adornment, and so on actually consist of — not with a further explanation, but by simply letting the assembly watch it happen.

What Appears Within a Single Pore, and Who Can See It

Chinese Source

如此間，如來為諸菩薩現甚深三昧神力變化；一切法界、虛空界等，十方一切世界海中，現國土身及眾生身，諸業所起；乃至一毛孔中，一切悉現，亦復如是；而不壞三世，不壞眾生；普照一切諸眾生心，色身清淨，隨所應化；普現一切眾生類前，開示一切諸佛妙法；調伏眾生，顯現如來自在神力。其有眾生見聞念知如來自在神通力者，皆佛宿世善知識也；皆悉修習四攝善根，一向專求無上菩提，攝諸善根，成就方便，逮得如來不可思議自在三昧，悉與法界、虛空界等；或得法身；或得色身；或得菩薩具足諸行；或得清淨諸波羅蜜；或得菩薩圓滿淨行；或得菩薩諸地；或得菩提自在；或得如來不壞三昧；或得如來諸行智力；或得如來無礙辯才。

Ru-Yi Translation

Just as in this very place the Tathāgata manifests for the bodhisattvas the miraculous transformation of this profound samādhi's own spiritual power, so too, throughout the entire Dharma-realm and the entire realm of space, in the ocean of every world in the ten directions, he manifests the bodies of lands and the bodies of sentient beings, and every karma that arises from them; even within a single pore, everything appears in exactly the same way — without disturbing the three times, without disturbing any sentient being; universally illumining the mind of every sentient being, their form-bodies made pure, according to whatever transformation is needed; manifesting universally before every kind of sentient being, opening and revealing the sublime Dharma of every buddha; taming sentient beings, manifesting the Tathāgata's own sovereign spiritual power. Whatever sentient being sees, hears, reflects upon, or knows the Tathāgata's own sovereign spiritual power has been, in some past life, a good spiritual friend of some buddha; every one of them has cultivated the roots of goodness of the four means of attraction, single-mindedly seeking unsurpassed awakening alone, gathering in every root of goodness, accomplishing skillful means, and attaining the Tathāgata's own inconceivable, sovereign samādhi, equal entirely to the Dharma-realm and the realm of space; some attain the Dharma-body; some attain the form-body; some attain the bodhisattva's every practice fully complete; some attain the pure pāramitās; some attain the bodhisattva's own perfected, pure practice; some attain the bodhisattva grounds; some attain sovereign mastery of awakening; some attain the Tathāgata's own indestructible samādhi; some attain the Tathāgata's own wisdom-power in every practice; and some attain the Tathāgata's own unobstructed eloquence.

Reading note — after Fazang

On the scaling from world-systems down to "even within a single pore," Fazang reads this move as making a specific, careful claim rather than mere hyperbole: 雖在毛孔微細之中，而亦不壞三世九世廣大無邊眾生界等差別宛然 — "though present within something as fine as a single pore, this does not destroy the distinctions of the three times, the nine times, or the vast and boundless realm of sentient beings — every distinction remains perfectly intact." The point is not that scale collapses into meaninglessness at the smallest grain; it is that the entire, fully differentiated cosmos is genuinely present at every grain, large or small, without any of its own internal distinctions being lost in the compression. He glosses the passage's own function in three parts: illumining the mind of the one who sees, manifesting a body suited to what that mind needs, and opening the Dharma accordingly — three deliberate steps, not one blur of miraculous display.

The closing catalogue of ten attainments — Dharma-body, form-body, the bodhisattva's practices, the pāramitās, the grounds, and so on — Fazang reads as answering, this time in the register of actual benefit rather than mere vision, the same question this whole section opened with: what does it take to see any of this at all? His answer folds directly back to Part 4's contrast with the blind śrāvakas — 皆佛宿世善知識也 — "every one of them has, in some past life, already been a good spiritual friend to some buddha" — seeing this display is never a matter of simply being present in the hall, as the ten great śrāvakas' own failure to see it already proved; it is the fruit of a cause laid down long before this particular gathering ever convened.

Part 7 Complete

This closes Part 7: the Buddha's light from between his brows, the assembly's vision of every world and every palace at once, and the deeper claim beneath the spectacle — that what appears within a single pore loses none of the cosmos's own distinctions, and that truly seeing any of it depends on causes laid down long before this gathering. Part 8 turns to the catalogue of roughly a hundred and fifty named samādhis the assembly attains through this same vision — the last and largest block of front matter before Sudhana himself finally enters the story.
