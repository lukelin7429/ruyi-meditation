Ninety-Eight Buddhas

The kalpa of Stainless Light unfolds one buddha after another — ninety-eight named in unbroken sequence, each one met, each one reverently offered to, before the kalpa closes on its very last buddha. No name here is abbreviated or summarized; every one appears in full, exactly as the source names it.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang confirms the count exactly: 二次有佛下略列九十八佛。三佛子於離垢下總結須彌山塵等佛 — "second, beginning with 'next there was a buddha,' briefly lists ninety-eight buddhas; third, beginning with 'child of the Buddha, within Stainless Light,' concludes with buddhas as numerous as motes of dust on Mount Sumeru." Ninety-eight is the exact count this translation preserves, name for name, none abbreviated — a single, unbroken "next... next... next" that runs for dozens of lines before the account itself steps back to name what these ninety-eight actually stand in for: not the total number of buddhas of this kalpa, but a representative sample of a number the text can only gesture toward as "motes of dust on Mount Sumeru."

Read as a set rather than individually, these ninety-eight names recur around a small cluster of images — mountain, moon, cloud, lamp, lion, lotus, banner, treasury — recombined again and again into new compounds, "sublime virtue" (妙德) alone appearing in some form in more than a dozen of them. This is not an accident of translation; it is the source text's own pattern, a small vocabulary of cosmic imagery cycled through nearly a hundred permutations, each one naming a buddha this narrator claims to have personally met, offered to, and heard teach, within a single kalpa that is itself only one of countless kalpas her account has not yet even begun to describe.
