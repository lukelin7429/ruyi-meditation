"Knowing the Body to Be Like a Dream"

Sudhana thanks the rākṣasa king and asks a plain, practical question: in what direction, place, city, or village does one actually go to seek a good and wise friend? The answer names no location at all — only a manner of traveling: bowing throughout the ten directions, contemplating every state, ranging boldly, and knowing one's own body and conduct to be like a dream, like lightning.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 194 of 454 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四之十四 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

"In What Direction, What Place, Does One Seek?"

Chinese Source

「爾時，善財答羅剎言：「善哉！善哉！以哀愍故，方便教我見善知識。願為我說云何往詣善知識所？於何方處城邑、聚落求善知識？」

答言：「善男子！敬禮十方求善知識，正念思惟一切境界求善知識；勇猛自在，遍遊十方求善知識；知身、知行，如夢、如電，詣善知識。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Sudhana answered the rākṣasa, saying: "Excellent, excellent! Out of compassion, you have taught me, through skillful means, how to behold a good and wise friend. I beg you to further explain for me: how does one go to where a good and wise friend dwells? In what direction, place, city, or village, does one seek a good and wise friend?"

He answered, saying: "Good man, reverently bowing throughout the ten directions, one seeks a good and wise friend; rightly recollecting and contemplating every state, one seeks a good and wise friend; valiant and sovereign, ranging universally throughout the ten directions, one seeks a good and wise friend; knowing the body, knowing conduct, to be like a dream, like lightning, one goes to a good and wise friend."

Reading note — after Fazang

Sudhana's question is the most concrete one this whole approach sequence has raised — a direction, a place, a named city — and the rākṣasa king's answer deliberately refuses to give a concrete answer in kind, offering instead four postures of seeking that apply everywhere at once: reverence, right contemplation, valiant range, and the dissolving insight that "the body, and conduct, are like a dream, like lightning." Given that Sudhana already knows perfectly well where Māyā physically dwells — within this same city of Kapilavastu, as Gopā already told him — this answer is not geographic information he lacks, but the inner disposition the actual approach still requires.
