The Ordeal on the Mountain of Fire and Swords

Sudhana finds a brahmin about to throw himself from a mountain of knives into a mass of fire, and is told this is itself the teaching. His own twelvefold doubt — could this be a māra in disguise? — draws a chorus of testimony from across the entire cosmos, from Brahmā down to nāga kings and yakṣas.

A Mountain of Knives, a Mass of Fire

Chinese Source

時，善財童子歡喜無量，恭敬禮已，遶無數匝，瞻仰觀察，辭退南行。

爾時，善財童子為無壞幢智慧法門所照，決了諸佛不可思議自在神力，善知菩薩不可思議法門。又不可思議菩薩三昧智慧，以照其心，得一切時三昧光明，得一切相三昧境界光明，得明淨智。令一切眾生，得勝妙處；得一切至處道法門；隨順世間行，心無有二；以明淨智普照境界，得一切聲聞明淨忍藏；得無生忍，知法實相；常行菩薩行，不捨菩薩心，增長薩婆若心；得十力明，普照一切；樂妙法音，心無厭足；如說修行，住薩婆若，究竟一切智境界，出生無量菩薩莊嚴心，滿足菩薩清淨大願。於一念頃，遍至一切諸佛剎網，教化成熟無量眾生海，心無懈倦；悉見菩薩無量行境界，悉分別見一切世間，見諸佛剎種種莊嚴，於微細境界，悉能安置無量世界。又能見彼種種莊嚴，悉能分別無量世界，諸語言法。又知無量眾生欲樂，知諸眾生無量所行，以無量方便，教化眾生，善知殊方，隨其所應，化度眾生，念善知識。漸漸遊行，至進求國，周遍推求彼婆羅門。時，婆羅門修諸苦行，求一切智，四面火聚，猶如大山；中有刀山，高峻無極；從彼山上，自投火聚。

爾時，善財詣婆羅門，頭面禮足，合掌而立，白言：「大聖！我已先發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，而未知菩薩云何學菩薩行、修菩薩道，願為我說。」答言：「善男子！汝今若能登此刀山，投火聚者，菩薩諸行皆悉清淨。」爾時，善財作如是念：「得人身難、離諸難難、得無難難、得淨法難、值佛世難、具諸根難、聞佛法難、遇善知識難、得與同止難、得聞正教難、得正命難、順趣正法難。此將非魔魔所使耶？非善知識而現善知識相，將非惡菩薩耶？而今為我作壽命難、作善根難、薩婆若難，此非正教險惡道耳。遠離法門，薩婆若等一切佛法。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Sudhana the Boy, his joy beyond measure, having reverently paid homage, circled him countless times, gazing up at him, took his leave and traveled south.

At that time Sudhana the Boy, illumined by the Dharma-gate of the Indestructible Banner of Wisdom, resolved the inconceivable, sovereign spiritual power of every buddha, and skillfully knew the bodhisattva's own inconceivable Dharma-gate. Further, through the bodhisattva's own inconceivable samādhi and wisdom illumining his mind, he attained the light of samādhi at every time, attained the light of the domain of samādhi in every appearance, attained bright, pure wisdom. Causing every sentient being to attain a supreme, sublime place; attaining the Dharma-gate of every path that reaches its destination; according with worldly conduct, his mind never divided in two; through bright, pure wisdom universally illumining every domain, he attained the bright, pure treasury of patience belonging to every śrāvaka; attained the patience of non-arising, knowing the true mark of the Dharma; constantly practicing the bodhisattva's own practice, never abandoning the bodhisattva mind, increasing and growing the mind of omniscience; attained the brightness of the ten powers, universally illumining everything; delighting in the sublime sound of the Dharma, his mind never once wearying of it; cultivating practice exactly as taught, abiding in omniscience, reaching the very end of the domain of omniscience, giving rise to a bodhisattva's own boundless, adorned mind, fulfilling the bodhisattva's own pure, great vow. In a single thought-moment, reaching entirely the net of every buddha-land, teaching and ripening sentient beings beyond measure, his mind never once growing weary; beholding entirely the bodhisattva's own boundless domain of practice, discerning and beholding entirely every world, seeing every kind of adornment in every buddha-land, and, within the finest, most subtle domain, being able to establish worlds beyond measure. Further able to behold every kind of adornment, discerning entirely the languages of worlds beyond measure. Further knowing the desires and delights of sentient beings beyond measure, knowing the boundless conduct of every sentient being, through boundless skillful means teaching and transforming sentient beings, skillfully knowing what is exceptional, according with what each requires, transforming and delivering sentient beings, holding the good spiritual friend in mind. Gradually traveling onward, he arrived at the land Seeking Advancement, seeking everywhere for that brahmin. At that time this brahmin was cultivating every kind of ascetic practice, seeking omniscience: on all four sides, a mass of fire, like a great mountain; within it, a mountain of knives, towering to no limit; from the top of that mountain, he was about to throw himself into the mass of fire.

At that time Sudhana went before the brahmin, bowed his head at his feet, and, joining his palms, stood and said: "Great sage! I have already raised the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and I do not yet know how a bodhisattva learns the bodhisattva's own practice, and cultivates the bodhisattva path — I ask you to expound it for me." He answered: "Good son, if you are now able to climb this mountain of knives and throw yourself into this mass of fire, every bodhisattva practice will become entirely pure." At that time Sudhana thought thus: "Attaining a human body is difficult; being free of every difficulty is difficult; attaining freedom from difficulty is difficult; attaining the pure Dharma is difficult; encountering a buddha's own era is difficult; being endowed with every faculty is difficult; hearing the Buddha's own Dharma is difficult; encountering a good spiritual friend is difficult; dwelling together with them is difficult; hearing the true teaching is difficult; attaining right livelihood is difficult; according with and turning toward the true Dharma is difficult. Could this not be some māra, or someone possessed by māra? Could this not be one who is not truly a good spiritual friend, yet manifests the appearance of one — could this not be some evil bodhisattva? And now he makes for me a difficulty of my very life, a difficulty for my roots of goodness, a difficulty for omniscience itself — this is surely not the true teaching, but some perilous, evil path. I should stay far from this Dharma-gate — omniscience and every buddha-dharma are elsewhere."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang treats the fire and knife-mountain as the actual Dharma-gate itself, not mere symbolism pointing at something else, and gives two readings side by side. First: 四無礙智能燒惑薪 — "the fire on all four sides is the four unhindered wisdoms, able to burn away the firewood of affliction"; the mountain of knives is "true non-discrimination, emerging from false understanding"; throwing himself down is "preparatory practice according with itself, conceptual discrimination taking true reality as its object in hope of realization, so that truth is manifest and falsehood extinguished." Second, citing the *Great Treatise on the Perfection of Wisdom* directly: 般若波羅蜜猶如大火聚，四邊不可觸，遠離於四句 — "the perfection of wisdom is like a great mass of fire: it cannot be touched from any of its four sides, and stands far removed from the four propositions [of the tetralemma]." On this second reading, the fire itself is fundamental prajñā, carrying four distinct senses at once: burning the firewood of affliction, breaking the darkness of ignorance, ripening the roots of goodness, and illumining realized principle directly. Fazang states plainly why the teaching takes this exact, terrifying form rather than any gentler one: 此火聚刀山即法門，更無表示...即以此相即是法門甚難解者，是此文意 — "this mass of fire and mountain of knives simply *is* the Dharma-gate, with nothing further being merely represented... that this very appearance is itself a Dharma-gate extremely difficult to understand is exactly the point this passage is making."

A Cosmos of Testimony

Chinese Source

作是念時，十萬梵天在虛空中，作如是言：「善男子！莫作是念。莫作是念。此是大聖，具足金剛智慧光明，精進不退，悉已究竟一切境界。欲竭一切眾生貪愛大海；欲裂一切諸邪見網；欲燒一切眾生煩惱，除滅愚闇，普照一切；令一切眾生離生死險難，除滅三世愚癡闇冥，放淨光明普照一切。」時，諸梵天及自在天，眾生主天等，諸邪見天，作如是言：「我造眾生、我為一切世間最勝、我為最上、我為第一。」是諸天等，見婆羅門修大苦行，五熱炙身；見如是已，各於諸禪不得滋味，來詣其所。時，婆羅門以自在力而為說法，令滅邪見，捨離我心；發大慈悲，普覆眾生；長養菩提正直之心；開四種道，求佛法身；隨所應化，悉能示現佛微妙音，一切悉聞，無有障礙。復有一萬魔在虛空中，以種種摩尼寶華散婆羅門，告善財言：「善男子！此婆羅門，苦行力故，放大光明，令我宮殿諸莊嚴具，悉如聚墨。我不復樂，即與無量諸天天女，眷屬圍遶，來詣其所；為我說法，悉於阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，得不退轉。」復有一萬他化自在天在虛空中，各持天華恭敬供養，作如是言：「善男子！此婆羅門，苦行力故，放大光明，令我宮殿諸莊嚴具，悉如聚墨；我不復樂，即與眷屬，來詣其所，為我說法，令我於心而得自在、於煩惱中而得自在、於受生中而得自在、除滅障礙而得自在、於一切三昧而得自在、於莊嚴具而得自在、於壽命中而得自在、乃至令我於一切佛法而得自在。」復有一萬化自在天在虛空中，以天妓樂恭敬供養，作如是言：「善男子！此婆羅門，五熱炙身，放大光明，照我宮殿及莊嚴具。照已，悉令我等，不樂五欲，不求欲樂，身心柔軟，與眷屬俱，來詣其所；為我說法，淨心、明淨心、善奇特心、柔軟心、歡喜心；乃至逮得清淨十力，長養離生，出生無量清淨之身；乃至得佛清淨法身；得清淨口，微妙音聲，遍至一切，無所障礙；乃至得一切智。」復有一萬兜率陀天與其眷屬，在虛空中，雨一切末香雲，恭敬供養，作如是言：「善男子！此婆羅門，五熱炙身時，我自於宮殿，不樂須臾，來詣其所；為我解說無著之法，少欲知足，長養善根，發菩提心，乃至究竟一切佛法。」復有一萬諸天，三十三天及阿脩羅，與眷屬俱，在虛空中，雨曼陀羅華雲、摩訶曼陀羅華雲，恭敬供養，作如是言：「善男子！此婆羅門，五熱炙身時，我於天樂，不須臾樂著，來詣其所。為我說法，遠離欲樂；乃至為我說無常法，變易不住，斷除一切放逸高慢，長養發起菩提之心。又，善男子！我見此婆羅門時，須彌山頂六種震動；我於爾時，心大恐怖，專求一切智。」復有一萬大龍王，伊那槃那，難陀跋難陀等，興黑栴檀香雲；諸龍王女出妙樂音，雨天華雲、天香水雲，恭敬供養，作如是言：「善男子！此婆羅門，五熱炙身時，放大光明，普照一切龍王宮殿；令諸龍王，離熱沙苦，金翅鳥怖，滅瞋恚熱，身體清涼，發歡喜心。發喜心已，而為說法，厭惡龍趣，至誠悔過，除滅業障，發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，乃至住一切智。」復有一萬夜叉王，種種供養此婆羅門及以善財，作如是言：「善男子！此婆羅門，五熱炙身時，我及羅剎、鳩槃荼等，悉於眾生發大慈心，無所嬈害，慈心力故；不樂宮殿，與眷屬俱，來詣其所；彼婆羅門，以大慈心蔭覆我等，令我歡喜，身心柔軟，安隱快樂；為我說法，乃至令無量夜叉、羅剎、鳩槃荼等，發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。」

Ru-Yi Translation

As he thought this, a hundred thousand Brahmā deities, present in the open sky, spoke thus: "Good son, do not think this. Do not think this. This is a great sage, fully endowed with the light of vajra wisdom, his diligence never retreating, having already reached the very end of every domain. He wishes to exhaust the great ocean of every sentient being's own craving and desire; he wishes to tear apart the net of every wrong view; he wishes to burn away every sentient being's own affliction, extinguishing the darkness of folly, universally illumining everything; causing every sentient being to leave behind the peril of birth and death, extinguishing the dark folly of the three times, releasing pure light that illumines everything." At that time the Brahmā deities and the Sovereign Heaven, the Lord-of-Beings Heaven, and other heavens of wrong view, spoke thus: "I created sentient beings; I am supreme above every world; I am the highest; I am first." These devas, upon seeing the brahmin cultivating this great ascetic practice, scorching his body with the five fires, having seen this, found no more savor in any of their own meditations, and came before him. At that time the brahmin, through his own sovereign power, expounded the Dharma for them, causing them to extinguish their wrong views, relinquishing the mind of self; raising great kindness and compassion, universally covering sentient beings; nurturing the upright mind of awakening; opening the four paths, seeking the Buddha's own Dharma-body; manifesting, according to what was needed, the Buddha's own subtle voice, all of it heard entirely, without any obstruction.

There were, further, ten thousand māras present in the open sky, scattering every kind of maṇi-jeweled flower over the brahmin, and saying to Sudhana: "Good son, this brahmin, through the power of his ascetic practice, releases great light, causing the ornaments of our own palaces to appear like gathered ink. Finding no more joy in them, we, together with devas and heavenly maidens beyond measure, our retinues surrounding us, came before him; he expounded the Dharma for us, and all of us attained non-retrogression from unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening." There were, further, ten thousand devas of the Heaven of Free Enjoyment of Others' Emanations, present in the open sky, each holding heavenly flowers in reverent offering, who spoke thus: "Good son, this brahmin, through the power of his ascetic practice, releases great light, causing the ornaments of our own palaces to appear like gathered ink; finding no more joy in them, we, together with our retinues, came before him; he expounded the Dharma for us, causing us to attain sovereign mastery over our own minds, sovereign mastery amid affliction, sovereign mastery in taking birth, sovereign mastery in extinguishing every hindrance, sovereign mastery in every samādhi, sovereign mastery over our own ornaments, sovereign mastery over our own lifespan, even causing us to attain sovereign mastery over every buddha-dharma." There were, further, ten thousand devas of the Transformation-Delight Heaven, present in the open sky, reverently making offering with heavenly music, who spoke thus: "Good son, this brahmin, scorching his body with the five fires, releases great light, illumining our own palaces and their ornaments. Having illumined them, he caused all of us to no longer delight in the five desires, no longer seeking desire's own pleasure, our bodies and minds made supple; together with our retinues, we came before him; he expounded the Dharma for us — a pure mind, a bright, pure mind, a wondrously excellent mind, a supple mind, a joyful mind; even attaining the pure ten powers, nurturing freedom from birth, bringing forth bodies beyond measure, entirely pure; even attaining the Buddha's own pure Dharma-body; attaining pure speech, a subtle, marvelous voice, reaching everywhere without any obstruction; even attaining omniscience." There were, further, ten thousand devas of the Tuṣita Heaven, together with their retinues, present in the open sky, raining down clouds of every powdered incense, reverently making offering, who spoke thus: "Good son, when this brahmin scorched his body with the five fires, we ourselves, within our own palaces, found no joy even for a moment, and came before him; he explained for us the Dharma of non-attachment, having few desires and knowing contentment, nurturing roots of goodness, raising the mind of awakening, even reaching the very end of every buddha-dharma." There were, further, ten thousand devas — the Heaven of the Thirty-Three and the asuras — together with their retinues, present in the open sky, raining down clouds of mandārava flowers and great mandārava flowers, reverently making offering, who spoke thus: "Good son, when this brahmin scorched his body with the five fires, we found no attachment to heavenly pleasure even for a moment, and came before him. He expounded the Dharma for us, causing us to depart from the pleasure of desire; even expounding for us the Dharma of impermanence, of change and non-abiding, cutting off entirely every laxity and pride, nurturing and raising the mind of awakening. Further, good son, when we beheld this brahmin, the very peak of Mount Sumeru shook in six ways; at that time our minds were greatly terrified, and we single-mindedly sought omniscience." There were, further, ten thousand great nāga kings — Elāpattra, Nanda, Upananda, and others — raising clouds of black-sandalwood fragrance; the nāga kings' own daughters sent forth sublime music, raining clouds of heavenly flowers and heavenly, fragrant water, reverently making offering, who spoke thus: "Good son, when this brahmin scorched his body with the five fires, he released great light, universally illumining every nāga king's own palace; causing every nāga king to be freed from the suffering of hot sand, the terror of the garuḍa, extinguishing the heat of anger and hatred, their bodies made cool and clear, raising a mind of joy. Having raised this mind of joy, he expounded the Dharma for us, causing us to grow weary of and turn away from the nāga's own destiny, sincerely repenting our wrongs, extinguishing every karmic hindrance, raising the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, even abiding in omniscience." There were, further, ten thousand yakṣa kings, making every kind of offering to this brahmin and to Sudhana, who spoke thus: "Good son, when this brahmin scorched his body with the five fires, we, together with rākṣasas and kumbhāṇḍas, all raised a mind of great kindness toward sentient beings, harming none of them, through the power of that very mind of kindness; finding no joy in our own palaces, together with our retinues, we came before him; this brahmin, through his mind of great kindness, sheltered and covered us, causing us to rejoice, our bodies and minds made supple, at peace and joyful; he expounded the Dharma for us, even causing yakṣas, rākṣasas, and kumbhāṇḍas beyond measure to raise the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang treats Sudhana's doubt itself as necessary rather than a lapse: 善財是大人，何故疑者？以相是反道，理須疑也。若不爾者，無以甄別邪正 — "Sudhana is already a great being — why does he doubt at all? Because the outward appearance reverses the ordinary path, doubt is, in principle, the appropriate response. Were this not so, there would be no way to distinguish the false from the true." The celestial testimony exists for the same reason, in reverse: 顯示實德故。若不爾者，亦無以簡真偽 — "to make his real virtue manifest; without this, there would likewise be no way to discern the true from the false." He even raises the sharper worry directly — could a real māra fake this very chorus of praise? — and answers that the good friend before this one already pointed Sudhana here, that no māra could actually produce a teaching this genuine, and that Sudhana has himself already attained an eye that sees past Māra's own illusions.

Fazang identifies this scene as the first of three places in the pilgrimage where a figure's outward appearance matches one of the three poisons directly — this brahmin's ordeal resembling wrong view, a king still to come resembling wrath, and a courtesan still to come resembling craving — and lays out four distinct ways affliction can be said to relate to the Buddha-dharma, warning that only the last applies here. First, by nature every affliction is empty, and it is that emptiness, not the affliction as such, that counts as Dharma — citing the *Sūtra of the Non-Practice of All Dharmas*: "desire is itself the path; hatred and delusion are likewise so." Second, by skillful means: affliction is used as a hook to draw beings toward liberation, as in Vimalakīrti's "first bind them with desire, then lead them into buddha-wisdom" — the affliction is a means, not itself the Dharma. Third, by deliberate retention: a bodhisattva keeps a trace of affliction to moisten future rebirth and extend the path of service, as in Vimalakīrti's "every affliction is the seed of the Tathāgata" — again a use, not an identity. The fourth is different in kind from all three: 當相則是，不同前三...極難解，不思議故，如此文是也 — "taken directly, just as it appears, it simply is [the Dharma] — unlike the previous three... this is extremely difficult to understand, being inconceivable, and this very passage exemplifies it." The fire and the knives are not a device pointing at something else, nor affliction skillfully repurposed; on this fourth reading, they are already, exactly as they appear, the teaching itself.
