Blossoming Tree Flower, and a Light at Sunset

Within the same bodhimaṇḍa Sudhana has now occupied through three teachers running, he arrives at the night-deva Blossoming Tree Flower — seventh teacher of the Ten Grounds. She teaches first through a light released each evening as the lotus flowers close, guarding travelers on dangerous roads, then through a long list of afflictions each answered by its own exact remedy — stinginess with giving, broken precepts with purity, anger with patience.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 90 of 454 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

An Approach, and a Light Released at Sunset

Chinese Source

爾時，善財童子正念思惟增廣甚深妙德自在音聲法門，往詣開敷樹華夜天所；見彼夜天在眾寶香樹樓閣之內，處寶樹芽師子法座，百萬諸天眷屬圍遶。

爾時，善財頭面敬禮彼夜天足，遶無數匝恭敬合掌，於一面住，白言：「天神！我已先發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，云何菩薩學菩薩行，修菩薩道，趣薩婆若？唯願天神為我解說。」

答言：「善男子！我於日沒優鉢羅鉢曇摩華，皆悉還合；若諸人眾，遊園觀者，廢捨縱逸，歸其家時，為放光明；在險徑者，照示平路，令彼專求一切智道；若於山巖、深水、曠野，在如是等種種難處，悉放光照，令免眾苦，得安隱樂。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Sudhana the Boy rightly held in mind and contemplated the Dharma-gate of Profound Sublime Virtue, Sovereign Sound, expanding and increasing it, and journeyed onward to the place of the night-deva Blossoming Tree Flower. He beheld this night-deva within a pavilion of fragrant, jeweled trees, seated upon a lion-throne sprung from a jeweled tree's own bud, surrounded by a retinue of a million devas.

At that time Sudhana reverently bowed his head at this night-deva's feet, circled her countless times, respectfully joined his palms, and stood to one side, saying: "O deva! I have already raised the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. How does a bodhisattva learn the bodhisattva's own practice, cultivate the bodhisattva's own path, and tend toward all-wisdom? May the deva, out of compassion, expound this for me."

She answered: "Good son, when the sun sets, the utpala and the padma flowers all close up again; if there are people who, having wandered in gardens and parks, cast off restraint and indulged themselves, and, as they return to their homes, I release light for their sake; for those upon a perilous path, I illumine and reveal the level road, causing them to single-mindedly seek the path of every wisdom; if they are amid mountain cliffs, deep waters, or open wilderness, in every such difficult place as this, I release light to illumine it entirely, causing them to be freed from every suffering and attain peace and ease."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang glosses this teacher's name on two levels at once: 初舉法勸修中三：先指後位、次頌前法、三善財得利益。初中，開敷樹花者，若約位，此地是有行有開發無相住故名也；約事，此天在香樹樓閣中故立此名 — "first, raising the Dharma to urge cultivation, in three parts: first, indicating the next position; second, reciting the prior Dharma in verse; third, the benefit Sudhana gains from it. Within the first: 'Blossoming Tree Flower' — taken in terms of her own position, this ground has both active practice and an open development that has not yet settled into signlessness, hence the name; taken in terms of circumstance, this deva dwells within a pavilion of fragrant trees, hence the name is given this way as well." Notably, Fazang folds Sudhana's own closing hymn to the previous teacher — already translated at the close of this collection's Part 89 — into this teacher's own opening structure, exactly as he did at the boundary before it: the moment Sudhana turns to leave one teacher is already, in the commentary's own architecture, the beginning of the encounter with the next.

Of the light released at sunset, he writes plainly: 先明安樂眾生行。光救夜中眾生，謂日沒等夜中放光，是夜天之義，令免世嶮路，求一切智道 — "first, revealing the practice of bringing peace and happiness to sentient beings. Light rescuing beings in the night — releasing light as night falls at sunset — is the very meaning of 'night-deva,' causing beings to be freed from the world's own perilous roads and to seek the path of every wisdom." The image itself is exact and ordinary: flowers closing as the sun goes down, and a traveler, distracted by a day spent wandering gardens, suddenly needing light to find the way home before dark.

Every Affliction, Its Exact Remedy

Chinese Source

「又，善男子！若諸眾生，放逸五欲，為其顯現老病死苦，悉令覩見捨離放逸，修習善根；為慳貪者，讚歎布施；若犯戒者，安立淨戒；為瞋恚者，讚歎大慈，安立忍辱；若懈怠者，教令修行菩薩精進；若亂心者，教令修習諸禪三昧；若愚癡者，令其深入般若波羅蜜；樂小法者，教以大乘；著三界者，令住菩薩圓滿無著諸波羅蜜；若諸眾生，功德羸弱，為眾結業之所逼迫，令住菩薩力波羅蜜；順無智者，令住菩薩智波羅蜜，捨離癡闇。善男子！我已成就無量歡喜知足光明法門。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"And, good son, if there are sentient beings indulging in the five desires, I manifest for them the suffering of old age, sickness, and death, causing them all to behold it and relinquish their own indulgence, cultivating roots of goodness; for the stingy and greedy, I praise giving; for those who break the precepts, I establish pure precepts; for the wrathful, I praise great loving-kindness, establishing patience; for the indolent, I teach them to cultivate the bodhisattva's own diligence; for the disordered in mind, I teach them to cultivate every meditative samādhi; for the foolish, I cause them to enter deeply into the perfection of wisdom; for those who delight in the lesser Dharma, I teach them the Great Vehicle; for those attached to the three realms, I cause them to abide in the bodhisattva's own perfect, unattached perfections; if there are sentient beings whose merit is weak and frail, oppressed by the bonds of accumulated karma, I cause them to abide in the bodhisattva's own perfection of power; for those who accord with a lack of wisdom, I cause them to abide in the bodhisattva's own perfection of wisdom, relinquishing the darkness of folly. Good son, I have already accomplished the Dharma-gate of the Light of Boundless Joy and Contentment."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang divides this list in two, and then names the Dharma-gate that results: 二又善男子下明利益眾生行，初化放逸眾生令修知足行、次授與十波羅蜜令破十障，釋成歡喜行可知；二我已成下立法名，依上三義遂立此名 — "second, beginning with 'and, good son,' reveals the practice of benefiting sentient beings: first, transforming indolent beings, causing them to cultivate contentment; second, bestowing the ten pāramitās to break the ten obstructions, thereby accomplishing the practice of joy, which may be understood accordingly; second, beginning with 'I have already accomplished,' establishes the Dharma-gate's own name, founded on these three meanings above." Those three meanings — bringing peace to beings (the sunset light), benefiting beings (this list), and the contentment it produces — together give the Dharma-gate its full name, which Fazang also records in its Sanskrit-derived form: 天竺本名生廣喜處知足光明菩薩解脫 — "in the Indian original, the Bodhisattva Liberation of the Place Giving Rise to Vast Joy, the Light of Contentment."

The list itself maps each affliction to a precise counterpart drawn from the ten pāramitās — giving for stinginess, precepts for their breach, patience for anger, diligence for indolence, meditation for a disordered mind, wisdom for folly — before closing on three broader categories: those attached to the three realms, those whose merit has been worn thin by accumulated karma, and those simply lacking wisdom. Nothing in the list treats any affliction as disqualifying; each one is simply named, and immediately answered.
