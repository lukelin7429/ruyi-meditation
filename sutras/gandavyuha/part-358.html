Taking On the Color of Sumeru

The catalogue continues: it is like Sumeru — every class of being that draws near that mountain takes on its very color; the mountain of the mind of bodhi is likewise, and whoever draws near it likewise takes on the very color of all-wisdom. It is like the fragrance of the Pārijāta tree's own flower, which every jasmine and campaka blossom found within Jambudvīpa is unable to match; the fragrance of the mind of bodhi is likewise — its wondrous fragrance of merit is a fragrance the pure precepts, concentration, wisdom, liberation, and vision of the knowledge of liberation of every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha are unable to match.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 358 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四之十六 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

The Pārijāta Flower Surpasses Jasmine and Campaka

Chinese Source

「善男子！譬如須彌山眾生品類，近彼山者，悉同其色。菩提心山亦復如是，若有近者，皆得同彼薩婆若色。

「善男子！譬如波利質多樹華香，閻浮提中，諸婆師華、薝蔔華等所不能及。菩提心香亦復如是，妙功德香，聲聞、緣覺，無漏戒定，智慧解脫，解脫知見，所不能及。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good man, it is like Sumeru — every class of sentient being that draws near that mountain takes on its very color. The mountain of the mind of bodhi is likewise: whoever draws near it likewise takes on the very color of all-wisdom.

"Good man, it is like the fragrance of the Pārijāta tree's own flower, a fragrance that every jasmine and campaka blossom found within Jambudvīpa is unable to match. The fragrance of the mind of bodhi is likewise: its wondrous fragrance of merit is a fragrance that the pure precepts, concentration, wisdom, liberation, and vision of the knowledge of liberation of every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha are unable to match."

Reading note — after Fazang

Sumeru reappears here for a second time in this catalogue under a distinct justification: the first catalogue used it for unmoved equanimity before friend and foe, while here it illustrates a transformative property of proximity — a mountain of legendary color (traditionally described in Buddhist cosmology as composed of four different jeweled faces, one per cardinal direction) that colors whatever draws near it. "The five dharma-aggregates" of a liberated being — precepts, concentration, wisdom, liberation, and the knowledge and vision of liberation (戒定慧解脫解脫知見) — is a standard formula summarizing the complete attainment of an arhat; Fazang reads its citation here as asserting that bodhicitta's fragrance surpasses not a partial or preliminary attainment but the entire completed range of what a śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha can achieve.

Part 358 Complete

Two more similes are now given in full. Part 359 continues the Pārijāta flower theme with two further comparisons: its fragrance pervading Jambudvīpa even before the flower opens, and a single day's scenting of a garment outlasting a thousand years of ordinary blossoms.
