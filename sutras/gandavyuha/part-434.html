A True Child of the Buddha, Words Never False

The hymn continues: universally for the sake of every multitude of beings, enduring hardship across boundless kalpas, according with every world, his mind unstained by anything. At that time, the bodhisattvas, having heard Samantabhadra's own teaching, reverently listened with a mind of respect to the tathāgata's own sovereign, true meaning. Samantabhadra, a true child of the Buddha, has consummated every practice, constantly praised by the buddhas, his every word never once false. Samantabhadra's own flower of merit, unstained by any dharma of the three realms, urges the great assembly to listen to the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 434 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四之十七 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

The Bodhisattvas Listen With Reverent Minds

Chinese Source

「普為諸群萌，　苦行無量劫，
隨順諸世間，　其心無染著。

時諸菩薩眾，　既聞普賢教，
敬心聽如來，　自在真實義。

普賢真佛子，　究竟一切行，
常為佛所歎，　言必不虛妄。

普賢功德華，　不染三界法，
勸發大眾聽，　無盡智慧海。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Universally for the sake of every multitude of beings, enduring hardship across boundless kalpas,
According with every world, his mind unstained by anything.

At that time, the bodhisattvas, having heard Samantabhadra's own teaching,
Reverently listened with a mind of respect to the tathāgata's own sovereign, true meaning.

Samantabhadra, a true child of the Buddha, has consummated every practice,
Constantly praised by the buddhas, his every word never once false.

Samantabhadra's own flower of merit, unstained by any dharma of the three realms,
Urges the great assembly to listen to the inexhaustible ocean of wisdom."

Reading note — after Fazang

The hymn briefly shifts perspective within these four stanzas — from Samantabhadra's own first-person address to a third-person description of "the bodhisattvas... reverently listening" to his teaching — a shift Fazang reads as the text momentarily stepping back to frame its own reception, confirming to the reader that the assembly surrounding Samantabhadra receives this teaching exactly as it should be received, before the hymn itself resumes its direct address. "Never once false" (必不虛妄), applied here to Samantabhadra's own words, echoes the traditional formula used of a buddha's own speech throughout Buddhist literature — its application to a bodhisattva, even one as exalted as Samantabhadra, is a further instance of this final episode's consistent placement of Samantabhadra at the very threshold separating bodhisattva from buddha.

Part 434 Complete

Four more stanzas of the hymn are now given in full. Part 435 continues with praise of every buddha's own subtle wisdom, pure as space, comprehending every dharma of the three times in a single thought, and knowing precisely what every sentient being needs in order to be taught.
