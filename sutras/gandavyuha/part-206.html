Mother to the Tathāgata Vairocana

Māyā answers Sudhana by naming her own liberation — the Dharma-gate of the Vow's Own Wisdom-Illusion — and reveals what it made possible: becoming mother to the tathāgata Vairocana, giving birth to Prince Siddhārtha from her own right side, at Kapilavastu, in the palace of King Śuddhodana.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 206 of 454 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四之十四 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

The Dharma-Gate of the Vow's Own Wisdom-Illusion

Chinese Source

「答言：「佛子！我已成就大願智幻法門。得此法門故，為盧舍那如來母。於此閻浮提迦毘羅城淨飯王宮，從右脇生悉達太子，顯現不可思議自在神力。」

Ru-Yi Translation

She answered, saying: "Child of the Buddha, I have accomplished only the Dharma-gate of the Vow's Own Wisdom-Illusion. Because I attained this Dharma-gate, I became the mother of the tathāgata Vairocana. Within this Jambudvīpa, in the palace of King Śuddhodana at the city of Kapilavastu, I gave birth to Prince Siddhārtha from my own right side, manifesting inconceivable, sovereign, spiritual power."

Reading note — after Fazang

Māyā's own naming of her liberation — the Dharma-gate of the Vow's Own Wisdom-Illusion (大願智幻法門) — folds together the two threads that have run through this entire teacher's episode: 大願 (great vow), matching her own stated role as mother, and 智幻 (wisdom-illusion), matching the whole preceding catalogue of form-bodies each declared illusion-like even while fully functional. This is the first place in the account where Vairocana and Siddhārtha appear side by side as one and the same figure named twice — once by his cosmic buddha-name, once by his given human name — collapsing, in a single sentence, the distance this whole collection has traveled between the historical Buddha and the cosmic Buddha it has been describing throughout.
