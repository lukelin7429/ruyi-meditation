Maitrāyaṇī, and Every Buddha Reflected in a Single Jewel

In a hall of jewels at Lion's Vigor, Sudhana finds the girl Maitrāyaṇī, and within every single pillar, wall, mirror, bell, tree, and necklace beholds every buddha's entire career — from the first raising of the mind to entry into nirvāṇa — the way the moon's own reflection appears within clear water.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 30 of 454 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

The Hall of Jewels, and What Every Jewel Held

Chinese Source

爾時，善財童子起不可思議恭敬之心，好樂淨法，專向大乘，求諸佛智，親近如來，觀法境界無所障礙，決定實際，住實境界，至三世際，解了三世如虛空際，決定了知三世法際，不作法際，住無礙際，不違業際，決定了知佛際非際，住如來住；滅一切妄想，不著一切佛、一切眷屬、一切世界；知一切眾生非我無實，一切音聲離語言道，解一切色猶如電光。漸漸南行至彼城已，周遍推問彌多羅女為在何所。時，有人答：「今在師子幢王宮內。」聞已，即詣門下求見彼女。時，無量人眾悉入宮中，善財問言：「諸人今者為詣何所？」答言：「我等欲詣彌多羅女聽受正法。」

爾時，善財作如是念：「此王宮門自在出入無所障礙。」善財即入，見彼女人處在明淨寶藏法堂，地玻瓈色，瑠璃為柱，金剛為壁，閻浮檀金欄楯窻牖，光明普照，阿僧祇摩尼寶而莊校之；又千寶藏摩尼寶鏡，圓滿莊嚴，眾生所樂，明淨妙寶以為嚴飾；又阿僧祇摩尼寶網羅覆其上，百千金鈴出微妙音；有如是等不可思議眾寶校具，莊嚴講堂。見彼女人身如真金，目髮紺色，處淨水香寶師子座，覆以金網；敷眾寶衣，大眾圍遶，以梵音聲而為說法。見已，頭面禮足遶無數匝，合掌恭敬於一面住，白言：「大聖！我已先發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，而未知菩薩云何學菩薩行，修菩薩道？」答言：「善男子！汝諦觀此法堂莊嚴。」

爾時，善財見一一瑠璃柱中、一一金剛壁中、一一摩尼鏡中、一一形像中、一一寶中、一一莊嚴中，一一金鈴中、一一寶樹中、一一寶形像中、一一寶瓔珞中，悉見法界等一切如來，從初發心，修菩薩行，成滿大願，功德莊嚴，成等正覺，轉淨法輪，乃至示現無餘涅槃，如淨水中見月影像。善財童子於一切境界莊嚴具中，見一切佛從初發心，乃至示現無餘涅槃，亦復如是，皆是彼女過去善根依果力故。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Sudhana the Boy gave rise to a mind of reverence beyond conceiving, delighting in the pure Dharma, turning single-mindedly toward the Great Vehicle, seeking the wisdom of every buddha, drawing near to the Tathāgata, contemplating the Dharma's own domain without any obstruction, resolving upon the true limit, abiding in the true domain, reaching the very limit of the three times, understanding that the three times are like the limit of space itself, resolving and understanding entirely the limit of the Dharma of the three times, not fabricating any limit of the Dharma, abiding in the unobstructed limit, never opposing the limit of karma, resolving and understanding entirely that the Buddha's own limit is no limit at all, abiding in the Tathāgata's own abiding; extinguishing every false thought, attached to no buddha, no retinue, no world at all; knowing that no sentient being has any self or any true reality, that every sound and voice is apart from the path of language, understanding that every form is like a flash of lightning. Gradually traveling south, he arrived at that city, and, seeking everywhere, asked where the girl Maitrā might be found. At that time someone answered: "She is now within the palace of King Lion's Banner." Having heard this, he went to the gate seeking to see this girl. At that time countless people were entering the palace; Sudhana asked them: "Where are all of you going now?" They answered: "We wish to go before the girl Maitrā to hear and receive the true Dharma."

At that time Sudhana thought thus: "This palace gate opens and admits freely, without any obstruction." Sudhana then entered, and beheld this woman within a bright, pure, jeweled treasury Dharma-hall; its ground the color of crystal, its pillars of lapis lazuli, its walls of vajra, its balustrades and windows of Jambū-river gold, its light universally illumining everything, adorned with countless maṇi jewels; further, a thousand jeweled-treasury maṇi mirrors, perfectly round and adorned, delighting whatever being beheld them, adorned with bright, pure, sublime jewels; further, a net of countless maṇi jewels covered over it, a hundred thousand golden bells sending forth subtle, marvelous sounds — with every kind of treasure beyond conceiving such as these, the hall was adorned. He beheld this woman, her body like true gold, her eyes and hair a deep blue-black color, seated upon a lion-throne of pure, fragrant jewels amid water, covered with a net of gold; spread with every kind of jeweled robe, surrounded by a great assembly, expounding the Dharma in a voice like Brahmā's own. Having seen her, he bowed his head at her feet, circled her countless times, and, joining his palms in reverence, stood to one side and said: "Great sage! I have already raised the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and I do not yet know how a bodhisattva learns the bodhisattva's own practice, cultivates the bodhisattva path?" She answered: "Good son, look closely and contemplate the adornment of this very Dharma-hall."

At that time Sudhana beheld, within every single pillar of lapis lazuli, within every single wall of vajra, within every single maṇi mirror, within every single image, within every single jewel, within every single ornament, within every single golden bell, within every single jeweled tree, within every single jeweled image, within every single jeweled necklace — he beheld entirely, equal to the whole Dharma-realm, every tathāgata, from their very first raising of the mind, cultivating the bodhisattva's own practice, fulfilling the great vow, adorned with merit, attaining perfect awakening, turning the pure Dharma-wheel, even manifesting entry into nirvāṇa without remainder — just as one beholds the moon's own reflection within clear water. Within every single adorned object of every domain, Sudhana the Boy beheld every buddha, from their very first raising of the mind even to manifesting entry into nirvāṇa without remainder, in exactly the same way — all of it through the power of this woman's own roots of goodness planted in the past.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang glosses Maitrāyaṇī's own name plainly: 此云慈女，謂智德內圓、慈相外彰故名也 — "translated, this means 'Girl of Kindness': her wisdom-virtue perfectly complete within, her outward mark of kindness manifest without, and so she is named for both at once." He records the Sanskrit original, Maitrāyaṇā, as meaning "kindness that rescues beings," and notes that her father, King Lion's Banner, makes her a princess — fitting, on his reading, for the abode this teacher represents: the tenth and final of the ten abodes, the "abode of coronation," a position already crowned rather than still advancing toward one.

On the vision within every jewel, Fazang draws a precise contrast with a much later scene in this same chapter, where Samantabhadra's own pores will manifest lands directly: 下文普賢毛孔現塵剎等即正報現依，此中則依報現正 — "in that later passage, the personal retribution [Samantabhadra's own body] manifests the environmental retribution [lands]; here, the reverse: the environmental retribution [the hall's own jewels] manifests the personal retribution [every buddha's own career]." The same one-in-all principle runs through this whole chapter's use of vision, but the direction reverses depending on who is doing the manifesting — a detail worth watching for as more such scenes accumulate across the remaining teachers.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang counts a hundred and sixteen dhāraṇī-gates in this list and organizes them into ten groups by function, closing the entire "Ten Abodes" section of his commentary on exactly this teacher — Maitrāyaṇī is the last of the ten, matching the tenth and final abode, "coronation," her father's own kingship a fitting emblem of a position already crowned. His own note on why every one of her thirty-six-Ganges-River teachers taught her through a "different gate" leading to this same one Dharma-gate states the underlying principle directly: 攝別歸普故、一切成一故、顯一甚深故廣多故 — "gathering the particular back into the universal; everything becoming one; showing that this one is profoundly deep, and therefore vast."

Fazang draws one further connection worth keeping in mind for the rest of this pilgrimage: the tenth abode's own Dharma-gate here, "the Perfection of Wisdom's Universal Adornment," parallels — in function if not in name — the tenth and final bodhisattva ground, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud, which this site's Dasabhūmika-śāstra collection already treats at length. The ten abodes and the ten grounds are, on Fazang's own account, not simply two separate numbered lists that happen to share the number ten; the culmination of one anticipates the culmination of the other.
