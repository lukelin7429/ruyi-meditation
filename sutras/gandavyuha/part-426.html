None Can Fathom It, Save the Tathāgatas

At that time, the bodhisattva Samantabhadra said to Sudhana: "Good man, have you now seen my own sovereign spiritual power, this extraordinary matter, or not?" Sudhana answered: "Indeed, I have seen it. This inconceivable thing — none can fathom it, save the tathāgatas themselves." Samantabhadra said: "Good man, across unspeakable, unspeakable numbers of kalpas, as many as the motes of dust in an ocean of worlds, I have cultivated the bodhisattva's own practice, seeking only bodhi."

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, the bodhisattva Samantabhadra said to Sudhana: "Good man, have you now seen my own sovereign spiritual power, this extraordinary matter, or not?"

Sudhana answered: "Indeed, I have seen it. This inconceivable thing — none can fathom it, save the tathāgatas themselves."

Samantabhadra said: "Good man, across unspeakable, unspeakable numbers of kalpas, as many as the motes of dust in an ocean of worlds, I have cultivated the bodhisattva's own practice, seeking only bodhi—"

Reading note — after Fazang

Sudhana's own qualifying clause — "none can fathom it, save the tathāgatas themselves" — is a striking claim to make about a bodhisattva rather than a buddha, and Fazang reads it as this final episode's clearest statement yet of Samantabhadra's own status at the very threshold of buddhahood: his own domain of activity is placed here beyond the reach of any bodhisattva's own understanding, matched only by a buddha's. This same question-and-confirmation structure — "have you seen it?" followed by a brief affirmation — has now recurred with both Maitreya and Samantabhadra, each time marking the transition from vision into verbal teaching; here it opens directly into what "world-sea-dust-mote kalpas" (世界海微塵等劫), one of this literature's largest units of measure, will reveal about the true scope of Samantabhadra's own practice.
