The Girl's Own Verse, Final Five Stanzas

The girl's verse closes with a full catalogue of the prince's own body — golden, mountain-like, his eyes pure and broad, his chest like a lion's own, his tongue and teeth each carrying their own auspicious mark — declaring, mark by mark, that he shall surely become a wheel-turning king, fully endowed with all thirty-two.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 158 of 454 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四之十三 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

Five Stanzas: A Body Fully Endowed With Every Mark

Chinese Source

「『其身逾金山，　相好自嚴飾，
我今太子所，　合掌恭敬住。

其目淨修廣，　方臆如師子，
觀者無厭足，　妙音應納我。

舌相廣長妙，　猶如赤銅色，
演出梵音聲，　聞者踊躍喜。

口方牙深固，　齒白而齊密，
若有覩見者，　一切皆歡喜。

離垢清淨身，　具相三十二，
成就此妙相，　必為轉輪王。』」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Your body surpasses a mountain of gold, adorned of itself with every auspicious mark; I now, before the prince, stand with palms joined in reverence.

Your eyes, pure and broad, your chest square like a lion's own; those who behold you never grow weary of it — with a sublime voice, you ought to accept me.

The mark of your tongue, broad, long, and sublime, like the color of red copper; giving forth a Brahmā-like voice, those who hear it leap for joy.

Your mouth square, your teeth deep-set and firm, white and evenly arrayed; whoever beholds them, every one of them rejoices.

Your stainless, pure body, endowed with the thirty-two marks; having accomplished these sublime marks, you shall surely become a wheel-turning king."

Reading note — after Fazang

The girl's own catalogue moves through several of the thirty-two marks of a great being individually named elsewhere throughout Buddhist literature — the broad, long tongue; the even, white teeth; the lion's own chest — before closing on the number itself, all thirty-two, and the destiny they signify. Read alongside her opening refusal, the verse now on offer reads less as consent than as a formal recognition of what the prince's own body already declares about his future — a recognition that, notably, stops short of ever repeating her earlier acceptance, or withdrawing her earlier vow to die rather than marry him.
