Self-Identified, and the Dharma-Gate's Own Power

Four stanzas identify the deva directly with the prince of the story just told, and describe her own unbroken cultivation of this Dharma-gate since. Eight further stanzas, each built on the same refrain, then describe what the Dharma-gate itself makes possible — ranging every world in a single thought, appearing before every buddha, and pouring light and transformation-bodies from every single pore of the body.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 120 of 454 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

Four Stanzas: "Excellently Subdued Was None Other Than Myself"

Chinese Source

「善伏我身是，　修習大悲心，
不惜身壽命，　救護彼苦人。

逮得此法門，　劫海常修習，
念念悉增長，　無量諸功德。

所見諸最勝，　方便為我說，
聞已即修習，　此寂滅法門。

無量劫修此，　不思議法門，
佛雨甘露海，　我已悉飲之。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Excellently Subdued was none other than myself; I cultivated the mind of great compassion, and, not sparing my own body or life, rescued and protected those suffering people.

Having attained this very Dharma-gate, I constantly cultivated it throughout an ocean of kalpas; within every single thought-moment, boundless merit, every kind of it, increased.

Whatever supreme ones I beheld expounded it for me through skillful means; and having heard it, I at once cultivated this very tranquil Dharma-gate.

Throughout boundless kalpas I cultivated this inconceivable Dharma-gate; the buddhas' own rain, an ocean of ambrosia, I have drunk in its entirety."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang names this fifth movement: 次四頌結會得法 — "next, four stanzas concluding the identification and the attainment of the Dharma." This is the verse counterpart to the prose identification already given at the close of the previous teacher's account — the direct naming of the deva's own past self as the prince of the story — but the verse adds something the prose did not dwell on: an unbroken continuity of cultivation "throughout an ocean of kalpas," and a single striking image, "the buddhas' own rain, an ocean of ambrosia, I have drunk in its entirety," closing this movement on an image of a gift already fully received rather than a task still underway.

Eight Stanzas: "Relying on This Very Dharma-Gate"

Chinese Source

「依止此法門，　普遊十方界，
一念悉分別，　三世諸佛剎。

依此法門故，　見三世佛海，
於諸最勝所，　現身如電光。

依此法門故，　遍詣十方佛，
各現大神力，　勝妙威儀法。

依此法門故，　能為問難海，
不思議諸佛，　所說聞受持。

依此法門故，　於十方世界，
諸佛大眾中，　自在顯神變。

依此法門故，　種種現色身，
能於一身中，　顯現諸佛身。

依此法門故，　一一毛孔中，
放大光明海，　除眾生煩惱。

依此法門故，　一一毛孔中，
出化無量身，　法雨濟眾生。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Relying upon this very Dharma-gate, I universally range through the worlds of the ten directions; within a single thought-moment, I discern in full the buddha-lands of the three times.

Because of this very Dharma-gate, I behold the ocean of buddhas of the three times; at the presence of every supreme one, I manifest my body like a flash of lightning.

Because of this very Dharma-gate, I go universally to the buddhas of the ten directions, manifesting before each, in turn, great spiritual power and sublime, dignified conduct.

Because of this very Dharma-gate, I am able to pose an ocean of questions and challenges; whatever the inconceivable buddhas expound, I hear and uphold it.

Because of this very Dharma-gate, within the great assemblies of buddhas throughout the worlds of the ten directions, I manifest spiritual transformations with sovereign mastery.

Because of this very Dharma-gate, I manifest every kind of material body; within a single body, I am able to display the bodies of every buddha.

Because of this very Dharma-gate, from every single pore, I send forth a great ocean of light, extinguishing the afflictions of sentient beings.

Because of this very Dharma-gate, from every single pore, I give forth boundless transformation-bodies, raining down the Dharma to relieve sentient beings."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang names this sixth movement, the longest of the nine: 次八頌法門業用 — "next, eight stanzas on the Dharma-gate's own function and application." Seven of the eight stanzas open on the identical refrain, 依此法門故 — "because of this very Dharma-gate" — a repetition the translation preserves rather than varying for elegance, since the anaphora is itself the point: each stanza is not a new capacity so much as the same single gate opening onto a different vista each time, from ranging every world in a single thought to a light "extinguishing the afflictions of sentient beings" pouring from every pore of the body.
