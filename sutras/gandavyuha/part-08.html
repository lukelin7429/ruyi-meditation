A Hundred and Three Samādhis

Ten kinds of subtle merit, unfolded into a hundred and three named samādhis — the first naming the foundation every other one rests on, the last gathering every other one back into the Lion's-Vigor Samādhi that has run through this whole chapter's opening.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang reads the opening ten-term list — every kind of path, gate, entry, crossing-over, and so on — as a summary heading rather than ten separate topics, and offers a sharp observation about how it relates to the hundred-plus samādhis that follow: 又釋，此百但是前十之中種種門之一門也 — "on another reading, these hundred-odd samādhis are simply an unfolding of a single one of the ten terms — 'every kind of gate' — not of all ten at once." His evidence is grammatical: the passage's own closing line calls them collectively "不可說佛剎微塵等三昧門" — "samādhi-gates beyond telling" — using 門 ("gate"), the very same character as the second of the ten summary terms, not any of the other nine. A list this vast, on Fazang's reading, is not attempting to itemize the whole of "path, gate, entry, crossing-over" and so on exhaustively; it is a single, sustained illustration of what just one of those ten terms alone already contains without limit — which puts the other nine terms' own unstated scope in genuine perspective.

He gives the list's first and last entries special attention, reading them as bookends rather than as two names among equals. The first, the Bodhisattva Samādhi of Universally Adorning the Dharma-Realm, he glosses as 明所依之本 — "making manifest the fundamental basis every other samādhi in the list depends upon" — named, in his words, 以無盡行海用類平等法界，使出纏結究竟圓明具德 — "for using the inexhaustible ocean of practice to match the impartial Dharma-realm, causing one to emerge from every bond into ultimate, perfect clarity, complete with virtue." The last, the Bodhisattva Samādhi of Contemplating the Lion's Own Vigor, he glosses as 明攝歸其位 — "making manifest the gathering of everything back into its own proper place" — 上諸三昧皆為趣向此三昧，故云觀察 — "every samādhi listed above exists only as something oriented toward this very samādhi, which is why it is called 'contemplating.'" Fazang adds one careful qualification: 此不同佛地所得，故云菩薩也 — "this differs from what is attained at the very ground of buddhahood itself, and so it is still called a bodhisattva['s] samādhi" — the whole hundred-and-three-fold list, vast as it is, remains a bodhisattva's own attainment, one stage short of the Buddha's own. As for the roughly hundred names in between, Fazang offers no line-by-line gloss, noting only 隨義思釋 — "each should be understood according to its own name" — and leaves them, deliberately, as a single sustained enumeration rather than a hundred separate lessons: the sheer accumulation is itself the point, the same rhetorical strategy that produced the hundred-and-forty-one bodhisattva names of Part 1.

Part 8 Complete

This closes Part 8: a hundred and three named samādhis, bookended by a foundation every other one depends on and a final gathering-point every other one is oriented toward.
