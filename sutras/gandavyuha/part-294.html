Vows as Many as Every Drop of Water

Vows raised in number equal to every mote of dust in every land and every drop of water; boundless kalpas of cultivated practice, dhāraṇī, and meditative vow; every child of the Buddha giving rise to boundless virtue; and a closing stanza that departs from the hymn's own refrain — transformation-bodies filling the entire dharma-realm, manifesting sovereign power.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 294 of 454 complete Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四之十五 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

Five Stanzas, and a Refrain Broken

Chinese Source

「一切剎微塵，　眾生水渧等，
生此等願者，　安住此法堂。

無量劫修行，　總持禪定願，
解脫法門者，　安住此法堂。

一切諸佛子，　出生無量德，
饒益眾生者，　安住此法堂。

成就無礙智，　通明巧方便，
隨應現生者，　安住此法堂。

從初發道心，　究竟一切行，
化身滿法界，　顯現自在力。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Motes of dust in every land, drops of water equal in number to every sentient being —
One who has raised vows equal to these abides in this Dharma-hall.

Cultivating practice across boundless kalpas, dhāraṇī, meditative absorption, and vow,
One of the Dharma-gate of liberation abides in this Dharma-hall.

Every child of the Buddha, giving rise to boundless virtue,
One who benefits sentient beings abides in this Dharma-hall.

Accomplishing unobstructed wisdom, penetrating insight, and skillful means,
One who manifests rebirth according to what is fitting abides in this Dharma-hall.

From the very first raising of the mind set upon the path, consummating every practice —
His transformation-bodies fill the entire dharma-realm, manifesting his own sovereign power."

Reading note — after Fazang

The fifth stanza here breaks a pattern this hymn has held without exception across twenty-nine prior stanzas: rather than closing "abides in this Dharma-hall," it closes on transformation-bodies filling the dharma-realm and manifesting sovereign power. Fazang reads this deviation as deliberate rather than accidental — the hymn's own refrain has, until now, kept returning attention to a fixed location, and this single break widens the frame at exactly the point where the verse turns from Maitreya's own qualities to his activity throughout the whole dharma-realm, before the refrain itself resumes in the stanzas that follow.
