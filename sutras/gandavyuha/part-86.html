A King Who Became a Monk, and Samantabhadra Again

Asked how long she has held this Dharma-gate, the night-deva opens her account in a kalpa named Stainless Light. A wheel-turning king renounces the throne to become a monk and guard the true Dharma; when the sangha itself grows corrupt and indolent, he rises alone into the sky in radiant light to save it. His daughter, a bhikkhuṇī, witnesses this and attains the very Dharma-gate herself. The king who became a monk is revealed as none other than Samantabhadra Bodhisattva.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 86 in progress Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

A Kalpa Named Stainless Light

Chinese Source

爾時，善財白夜天言：「妙哉！天神！如此法門，最為甚深，得此法門，其已久如？」

答言：「佛子！乃往古世，過轉世界微塵等劫，有劫名離垢光明，時有世界，名法界妙德雲，有四天下微塵等香須彌山莊嚴。於蓮華中，出一切佛妙願音聲，一切眾生淨業所起，眾寶合成，形如蓮華，清淨無垢，有須彌山微塵等眾妙寶樹，周匝圍遶，有須彌山微塵等眾妙寶香，以為莊嚴；有須彌山微塵等諸四天下莊嚴世界，一一四天下，各有不可說不可說城。彼世界中，有四天下，名莊嚴幢；彼四天下，有王都城，名普寶華光；於彼城外，有道場名法王宮殿光明；其道場上，有須彌山微塵等佛，出興于世；其最初佛，號法海雷音光明王。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Sudhana said to the night-deva: "How wondrous, O deva! This Dharma-gate is utterly profound. How long has it been since you attained this Dharma-gate?"

She answered: "Child of the Buddha, in ages long past, beyond kalpas as numerous as motes of dust in a whirling world, there was a kalpa named Stainless Light. At that time there was a world, named the Cloud of Sublime Virtue of the Dharma-Realm, adorned with fragrant Mount Sumerus as numerous as motes of dust in four continents. From within a lotus flower there emerged the sublime sound of every buddha's own vow, arising from the pure karma of every sentient being, formed of every jewel, its shape like a lotus flower, pure and stainless; there were sublime jeweled trees as numerous as motes of dust on Mount Sumeru, encircling it all about, and sublime jeweled fragrances as numerous as motes of dust on Mount Sumeru adorning it. There were adorned worlds of four continents as numerous as motes of dust on Mount Sumeru, and within each and every one of these four continents there were unspeakable, unspeakable cities. Within that world there was a four-continent world, named the Banner of Adornment; within that four-continent world there was a royal capital city, named Universal Jeweled Flower-Light; outside that city there was a bodhimaṇḍa, named the Light of the Dharma-King's Own Palace; at that bodhimaṇḍa, buddhas as numerous as motes of dust on Mount Sumeru arose in the world, and the very first of these buddhas was named the King of the Light of the Thunder-Sound of the Ocean of Dharma."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang opens this deepest question with its own two-part answer structure: 答中二：初明離垢光明劫中供佛修行、二復次佛子下明於佛剎塵數劫中供佛修行 — "the answer has two parts: first, revealing cultivation and offering made to buddhas within the kalpa of Stainless Light; second, beginning with 'furthermore, child of the Buddha,' revealing cultivation and offering across kalpas as numerous as motes of dust in every buddha-land." Within the first of these, he counts six steps, the first three of which structure what this part translates: 一舉往時；二時有世界下顯往處；三於彼城外下明往佛興 — "first, citing the past time; second, beginning with 'at that time there was a world,' revealing the place; third, beginning with 'outside that city,' revealing the buddha's own arising." A kalpa, then a world located by its own lotus-born origin story, then a city, then a bodhimaṇḍa just outside its walls — the address narrows exactly as it did for the teacher before this one, ending on a single buddha's own name.

A King Becomes a Monk, and Saves a Corrupted Sangha

Chinese Source

「時，有轉輪王名離垢光明，於彼佛所，守護正法，聞持正法修多羅海。佛滅度後，出家學道。正法欲滅，於大劫中，有惡劫起，煩惱熾盛，眾生恚怒，忿毒交諍，諸比丘眾，背功德利，心樂放逸，常好王論、賊論、女論、國論、海論、世間之論，樂如是等種種諸論。

「時，王比丘作如是念：『如來無量阿僧祇劫，修集妙法，云何此諸比丘而共毀滅？』彼王比丘即昇虛空，放大光明雲，無量種色，普照十方一切世界，除滅一切眾生煩惱，立無量眾生無上菩提，復令正法，於六萬五千歲而得興盛。

「時，有比丘尼名法輪化光，是彼轉輪王女，十萬比丘尼以為眷屬。見父王比丘光明神變，即發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，得一切佛燈明三昧，甚深妙德自在音聲法門；得已，身心柔軟，法海雷音光明王佛神力自在，一切功德，悉現在前。

「佛子！時，轉輪王隨彼如來，轉正法輪，興隆法者，豈異人乎？今普賢菩薩摩訶薩是也；法輪化光比丘尼者，我身是也。我於爾時，守護佛法，建立十萬比丘尼眾，得不退轉地；又令攝取一切如來法門三昧，法輪光明三昧，又復建立入一切法海，方便般若波羅蜜。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"At that time, there was a wheel-turning king named Stainless Light, who, at that buddha's own presence, guarded the true Dharma, hearing and upholding the ocean of sūtras of the true Dharma. After the buddha's own passing into extinction, he left the household life to study the path. As the true Dharma was about to be extinguished, within this great kalpa, an evil kalpa arose: afflictions blazed fiercely, sentient beings grew wrathful and angry, resentment and poison contended with one another, and the assembly of monks turned their backs on the benefit of merit, their minds delighting in indolence, constantly favoring discussions of kings, discussions of thieves, discussions of women, discussions of nations, discussions of the sea, and discussions of worldly affairs, delighting in every kind of such discussion as this.

"At that time, the king who had become a monk thought to himself: 'The Tathāgata, throughout boundless asaṃkhyeya kalpas, cultivated and gathered this sublime Dharma — how can these monks together destroy it?' This monk who had been a king at once rose up into the sky, releasing a great cloud of light, of boundless colors, universally illumining every world of the ten directions, extinguishing the affliction of every sentient being, establishing boundless sentient beings in unsurpassed bodhi, and further causing the true Dharma to flourish for sixty-five thousand years.

"At that time, there was a bhikkhuṇī named Dharma-Wheel Transformation Light, who was that wheel-turning king's own daughter, with a hundred thousand bhikkhuṇīs as her retinue. Beholding her father, the monk-king's, own light and spiritual transformation, she at once raised the mind of unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and attained the samādhi of the Lamp-Light of Every Buddha, the Dharma-gate of Profound Sublime Virtue, Sovereign Sound; having attained it, her body and mind became soft and pliant, and the buddha King of the Light of the Thunder-Sound of the Ocean of Dharma's own sovereign spiritual power, and every merit, all appeared before her.

"Child of the Buddha, could that wheel-turning king who, following that tathāgata, turned the true Dharma-wheel and caused the Dharma to flourish, have been anyone other than himself? He is none other than Samantabhadra Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva, now; and the bhikkhuṇī Dharma-Wheel Transformation Light was I myself. At that time I guarded the buddha-Dharma, establishing an assembly of a hundred thousand bhikkhuṇīs, attaining the ground of non-retreat; and I further caused myself to gather in the samādhi of every tathāgata's own Dharma-gate, the samādhi of the Light of the Dharma-Wheel, and further established myself in entering the whole ocean of the Dharma, the perfection of wisdom through skillful means."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang divides the father-king's own cultivation into exactly three steps, matching the story's own turns: 四時有轉輪王下明父王修行，於中三：初王聞法出家、二見惡毀法、三護法令興 — "fourth, beginning with 'at that time, there was a wheel-turning king,' reveals the father-king's own cultivation; within this there are three parts: first, the king hearing the Dharma and leaving the household life; second, seeing evil destroy the Dharma; third, protecting the Dharma and causing it to flourish." What stands out in the middle step is exactly what is named as corrupting the sangha: not doctrinal error, but idle talk — "discussions of kings, discussions of thieves, discussions of women, discussions of nations, discussions of the sea, and discussions of worldly affairs" — the very same list of frivolous topics named elsewhere in early Buddhist literature as what a serious practitioner avoids. The threat to the true Dharma here is not a rival teaching; it is simply distraction, indolence, and gossip, spreading unchecked through a monastic community until nothing but a single monk's own radiant intervention can arrest it.

The fifth and sixth steps, Fazang notes, turn to the deva's own past self: 五時有比丘尼下明夜天本身發心得法；六佛子時輪王下結會古今及得法利益 — "fifth, beginning with 'at that time, there was a bhikkhuṇī,' reveals the night-deva's own past body raising the mind and attaining the Dharma; sixth, beginning with 'child of the Buddha, at that time, the wheel-turning king,' concludes the identification of past and present, and the benefit of attaining the Dharma." The identification lands a name this pilgrimage has now met at the origin of more than one teacher's own path: the king who became a monk and single-handedly saved a failing sangha was Samantabhadra Bodhisattva himself — the same bodhisattva who, in disguise, woke a sleeping princess with a single midnight voice at the origin of teacher thirty-three's own story, and who, as a night-deva, sent teacher thirty-four's princess-self toward her own awakening. Here he appears not as a hidden voice or a stranger at the gate, but as a king's own decision to leave the throne — and it is this decision, more than any miracle that follows it, that the account credits with saving the true Dharma from a sangha's own indolence.
