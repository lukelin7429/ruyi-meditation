The Assembly's Wish, and the Buddha's Samādhi

The whole assembly silently longs for the Buddha to reveal his own inconceivable domain — and he answers not with words but by entering the Lion's-Vigor Samādhi, which transforms the Hall of the Layered Pavilion, Jeta's Grove itself, and the space above it into an adornment beyond telling.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 2 in progress Source: Taishō T278, 入法界品第三十四 (Buddhabhadra, Eastern Jin) Reading notes after Fazang's 華嚴經探玄記 (T1733)

What the Assembly Silently Wishes

Chinese Source

爾時，諸菩薩、聲聞、天人、及其眷屬，咸作是念：「如來行、如來智境界、如來持、如來力、如來無畏、如來三昧、如來住、如來勝妙功德、如來身、如來智、如來法，一切天人無能知、無能度、無能得底、無能受、無能思惟、無能觀察、無能分別、無能開發、無能宣明、無能為人如實解說，除佛持力、自在力、威神力、如來本願力、過去善根力、親近善知識力、清淨信心方便力、樂求勝妙法力、清淨正直菩提心力、深心一切智願力。又諸大眾種種意、種種欲、種種解、種種語、種種地、種種根、種種方便、種種心境界、種種依如來功德、種種樂聞法；世尊往昔發一切智願，求一切智，菩薩諸願清淨波羅蜜、菩薩諸地、菩薩滿足行、菩薩莊嚴、菩薩方便莊嚴、菩薩道莊嚴、菩薩出生方便海莊嚴、菩薩自在莊嚴，菩薩本生海、菩提門自在海，如來自在轉法輪、如來剎清淨自在、如來方便莊嚴眾生界、如來法王法、如來道明普照一切、如來自在入一切眾生處、如來為一切眾生作最上福田、如來為一切眾生說功德達嚫，三輪化度一切群生。唯願世尊大悲慈愍，具足顯現。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time all the bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, gods and humans, together with their retinues, all thought thus: "The Tathāgata's conduct, the Tathāgata's domain of wisdom, the Tathāgata's sustaining power, the Tathāgata's strength, the Tathāgata's fearlessness, the Tathāgata's samādhis, the Tathāgata's abiding, the Tathāgata's sublime and excellent merit, the Tathāgata's body, the Tathāgata's wisdom, the Tathāgata's own Dharma — none among all gods and humans is able to know these, to fathom them, to reach their depths, to receive them, to reflect on them, to contemplate them, to discern them, to bring them forth, to proclaim them, or to explain them truly to another, except through the Buddha's own sustaining power, sovereign power, and majestic spiritual power; through the power of the Tathāgata's original vow, the power of roots of goodness planted in ages past, the power of having drawn near to good spiritual friends, the power of skillful means born of pure faith, the power of delighting in the pursuit of the sublime Dharma, the power of a pure and upright mind of awakening, and the power of a profound mind's vow for omniscience. Further, this great assembly holds every kind of intention, every kind of aspiration, every kind of understanding, every kind of language, every kind of ground, every kind of faculty, every kind of skillful means, every kind of mental state, every kind of reliance on the Tathāgata's merit, and every kind of delight in hearing the Dharma. The World-Honored One, having in ages past raised the vow of omniscience and sought omniscience — every vow of the bodhisattvas and their pure pāramitās, the bodhisattva grounds, the bodhisattva's practice brought to fulfillment, the bodhisattva's own adornment, the adornment of skillful means, the adornment of the path, the adornment of the ocean of skillful means from which it arises, the adornment of sovereign mastery, the ocean of the bodhisattva's own past lives, the ocean of sovereign mastery over the gate of awakening; the Tathāgata's sovereign turning of the Dharma-wheel, the Tathāgata's pure sovereignty over his own land, the Tathāgata's skillful adornment of the realm of sentient beings, the Tathāgata's own Dharma as king of the Dharma, the Tathāgata's own path of clarity universally illumining all things, the Tathāgata's sovereign entry into the place of every sentient being, the Tathāgata as the supreme field of merit for every sentient being, the Tathāgata expounding, for every sentient being, the fruit of merit given in offering — through the three wheels transforming and delivering every being. We can only wish that the World-Honored One, in great compassion and loving mercy, would fully manifest all of this."

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang reads this entire passage as one long unspoken request, and notes a real tension he takes care to resolve: the assembly includes those five hundred great śrāvakas already established, on his earlier reading, as being either genuinely deaf and blind to the Buddha's inconceivable domain, or great bodhisattvas merely appearing that way — so how can such figures be shown here silently longing for exactly what they supposedly cannot perceive? He offers two answers rather than choosing between them. The first, from an earlier master: 理處不隔，故得同疑；未積大心，故不應其次 — "in principle nothing here is closed off to them, so they are able to share in the same wondering; but not having yet accumulated the great mind, they cannot take their place among those who directly respond" — they can wonder, even if they cannot yet answer. The second is Fazang's own: these śrāvakas are present in the same assembly at Jetavana, so they share in the same collective aspiration by simple proximity, even while their outward, "blind and deaf" form continues to hold; or — reading them, as before, as bodhisattvas in disguise — the wondering is entirely genuine and the blindness only ever a trace (跡) laid over a reality that never stopped seeing.

Structurally, Fazang divides the sixty clauses of this unspoken request into two sets of thirty: the first thirty asking after the Buddha's own realized qualities — compassion inseparable from wisdom, omniscience, the great dhāraṇīs, the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses, inconceivable samādhis, and so on — the second thirty asking after the Buddha's power to bring others into the very same realization he asks about. He is careful to note that the request itself proceeds in three movements even within the first thirty: first naming what is asked for, then admitting its own inability to fathom what it is asking (too vast to know, too deep to reach bottom, too boundless to receive), and only then naming the countervailing causes — the Buddha's own empowering power, together with the assembly's accumulated causes of faith and aspiration — that make it possible, after all, for such a request to be answered. The wanting to know and the admission of not-knowing are not, on this reading, in tension; the second is what makes the first sincere.

The Lion's-Vigor Samādhi, and the Hall Transformed

Chinese Source

爾時，世尊知諸大眾心之所念，以大悲身、大悲門、大悲為首、大悲隨順方便法，入師子奮迅三昧，令一切眾生樂清淨法；入三昧已，時，大莊嚴重閣講堂忽然廣博，無量無邊不可破壞，金剛寶地清淨莊嚴，一切摩尼寶王遍布其地，散無量寶華，奇妙眾寶，瑠璃為柱，以明淨寶而莊嚴之，眾寶莊校微密無間，閻浮檀寶以為樓閣；眾寶欄楯，却敵竂向，阿僧祇欄楯而以莊飾；諸天王寶，堅固眾寶而莊嚴之；摩尼寶網，彌覆其上，建眾寶幢，懸諸幡蓋，放大光網，普照法界。又以不可說眾雜妙寶，莊嚴其外，四邊階道，眾寶合成。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, knowing what this great assembly held in their minds, through a body of great compassion, a gate of great compassion, great compassion as his foremost concern, and the skillful means that accord with great compassion, entered the Lion's-Vigor Samādhi, so that every sentient being might delight in the pure Dharma. Once he had entered this samādhi, the greatly adorned Hall of the Layered Pavilion suddenly grew vast and expansive — immeasurable, boundless, and indestructible. Its ground of vajra-jewel was purely adorned; every kind of maṇi jewel-king spread across its surface, scattering countless jeweled flowers and marvelous, rare treasures; its pillars were of lapis lazuli, adorned with pure, radiant jewels; every kind of jewel was set into it, close-fitted without any gap; its many-storied pavilion was formed of Jambū-river gold. Jeweled balustrades faced outward on every side, adorned with countless further balustrades; every treasure of the heavenly kings, solid and firm, adorned it further; a net of maṇi jewels spread over it entirely; jeweled banners were raised, streamers and canopies hung, and it released a great net of light, universally illumining the Dharma-realm. And with countless kinds of marvelous jewels beyond telling it was further adorned on the outside, its stairways on every side likewise formed entirely of jewels.

Reading note — after Fazang

On why the Buddha alone — not, as in some earlier assemblies of this sūtra, a bodhisattva — enters samādhi here, Fazang draws a contrast with how such scenes usually unfold: 前明從相入實，今辨依體起用 — "earlier [assemblies] made manifest a movement from the phenomenal into the real; here what is being shown is function arising directly from substance." Where a bodhisattva's samādhi typically marks an ascent from ordinary appearance toward realization, only a buddha's own samādhi can mark realization's own outward radiance — which is why, Fazang says, this scene belongs to the Buddha himself, showing that only the Buddha, having reached the very source, has this liberation fully at his own command.

The samādhi's name he unpacks through the lion simile directly: 如師子奮迅之時，諸根開張、身毛皆竪現其威勢，哮吼之相令餘獸類失威竄伏，令師子兒增其雄猛 — "just as when a lion is roused to vigor, every sense stands alert and every hair bristles, manifesting its full power; its roar causes every other beast to lose its own courage and flee for cover, while it causes the lion's own cubs to grow bolder and stronger in turn" — and reads the Buddha's samādhi as doing exactly this on four fronts at once, all rooted in great compassion: rousing the body of compassion itself, opening the gate of compassion, raising compassion as what leads every other quality, and manifesting the majesty that meets each being according to its own capacity — so that the "beasts" of the two lesser vehicles retreat in something like fear before what they cannot yet hold, while the bodhisattva-cubs of this assembly, witnessing the very same display, only grow more confident in the countless samādhis and dhāraṇīs still ahead of them.

The Grove, and the Sky Above It, Transformed

Chinese Source

爾時，佛神力故，令祇洹林忽然廣博，與不可說佛剎微塵數世界等，眾寶莊嚴，不可說寶遍布其地；阿僧祇寶以為垣牆；寶多羅樹列植道側；無量香河微流盈滿，一切寶華以為波浪，皆悉右旋，演說一切佛法，音聲不可思議。分陀利華皆悉開敷，彌布水上；眾寶華樹，高顯榮茂，列植其岸；不可思議樓閣，摩尼寶網羅覆其上。阿僧祇妙寶莊嚴，光明普照，阿僧祇摩尼寶王嚴飾其地，出眾妙香。建立無量摩尼王幢，香幢、衣幢、幡幢、繒幢、華幢、莊嚴具幢、鬘幢、寶垂帶幢、眾寶蓋幢、大摩尼幢、普照摩尼寶幢、出佛音幢、師子寶王幢、出一切佛本生海幢、一切法界幢、摩尼寶王幢，以為莊嚴。時，祇洹林上虛空中，有不可思議天寶宮殿雲、不可思議眾香樹雲、不可說須彌山雲，莊嚴虛空。不可說不可說眾寶樂器演妙法音，讚詠如來。不可說寶樹雲彌覆虛空，不可說眾寶座雲覆以寶衣，菩薩處上歎佛功德。不可說天寶像雲以為莊嚴，不可說白淨真珠網雲以為莊嚴，不可說解脫樓閣雲以為莊嚴，不可說妙解脫音樂雲雨以為莊嚴。何以故？如來善根不可思議故；如來白淨法不可思議故；如來威神不可思議故；如來一身，充滿一切法界自在，不可思議故；一切佛剎莊嚴入一佛身，不可思議故；一微塵中現一切佛，一切法界不可思議故；一毛孔中盡過去際，一切如來次第顯現不可思議故；放一光明照一切剎，不可思議故；如來一毛孔中出一切佛剎微塵等化身雲，充滿一切世界，不可思議故；如來一毛孔中現一切佛剎成壞不可思議故；如此，祇樹給孤獨園見嚴淨佛剎，一切法界、虛空界、一切世界，所見嚴淨，亦復如是。如來充滿來詣祇洹，菩薩充滿一切如來大眾海安住，普雨一切妙莊嚴雲；雨一切眾寶光明，普照一切摩尼王雲；雨一切蓋雲、莊嚴一切天身雲；雨一切華樹雲、莊嚴一切雜色衣雲；雨一切鬘雲、流注莊嚴一切摩尼寶王莊嚴雲；雨一切眾生身雜色香雲；雨寶華雲、諸天女雲；各持妙寶於虛空中迴轉，莊嚴一切眾寶，鉢曇摩華雜寶師子座莊嚴虛空。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, through the Buddha's spiritual power, Jeta's Grove itself suddenly grew vast and expansive, equal in extent to world-systems as numerous as motes of dust in buddha-lands beyond telling, adorned with every kind of jewel, treasures beyond telling spread across its ground; walls of countless jewels enclosed it; jeweled tāla-trees stood in rows along its paths; rivers of fragrance flowed in gentle currents, brimming full, every jeweled flower forming their waves, all turning to the right, expounding the entire Buddha-dharma in sounds beyond conceiving. White lotuses were everywhere in bloom, spreading across the water's surface; trees hung with jeweled flowers, tall and flourishing, stood in rows along the banks; pavilions beyond conceiving were covered over with nets of maṇi jewels. Countless marvelous jewels adorned it, their light universally illumining; countless maṇi jewel-kings adorned its ground, giving off every kind of marvelous fragrance. Immeasurable maṇi-king banners were raised — banners of fragrance, banners of robes, streamer-banners, silk banners, flower banners, banners hung with every kind of ornament, garland banners, banners with hanging jeweled sashes, banners of every kind of jeweled canopy, great maṇi banners, maṇi-jewel banners universally illumining, banners giving forth the Buddha's own voice, lion-jewel-king banners, banners giving forth the ocean of every buddha's past lives, banners of the entire Dharma-realm, and maṇi jewel-king banners — all these adorning it.

At that time, in the space above Jeta's Grove, there appeared clouds of heavenly jeweled palaces beyond conceiving, clouds of fragrant trees beyond conceiving, and clouds shaped like Mount Sumeru beyond telling, adorning all of space. Musical instruments of every jewel, beyond telling upon beyond telling, sounded forth the subtle Dharma, singing the Tathāgata's praise. Clouds of jeweled trees beyond telling spread over all of space; clouds of jeweled thrones beyond telling, covered with jeweled robes, on which bodhisattvas sat praising the Buddha's merit. Clouds of heavenly jeweled images beyond telling served as further adornment; clouds of pure white pearl-nets beyond telling served as further adornment; clouds of liberation-pavilions beyond telling served as further adornment; and rain-clouds of sublime, liberating music beyond telling served as further adornment.

Why is this so? Because the Tathāgata's roots of goodness are beyond conceiving; because the Tathāgata's pure white Dharma is beyond conceiving; because the Tathāgata's majestic spiritual power is beyond conceiving; because the Tathāgata's single body, filling the entire Dharma-realm in sovereign mastery, is beyond conceiving; because the adornments of every buddha-land entering into a single buddha's body are beyond conceiving; because, within a single mote of dust, every buddha appears, and the entire Dharma-realm is beyond conceiving; because, within a single pore, the entire past is exhausted and every tathāgata appears in due order, beyond conceiving; because a single ray of light illumines every land, beyond conceiving; because from a single pore of the Tathāgata come forth clouds of transformation-bodies as numerous as motes of dust in every buddha-land, filling every world, beyond conceiving; because within a single pore of the Tathāgata every buddha-land's formation and dissolution appears, beyond conceiving. And just as Jeta's Grove and Anāthapiṇḍada's Park are here seen as an adorned and pure buddha-land, so too, in exactly the same way, the entire Dharma-realm, the entire realm of space, and every world are likewise seen as adorned and pure.

Tathāgatas without number came, filling Jetavana; bodhisattvas without number abided, filling the ocean of every tathāgata's great assembly, everywhere raining down clouds of every kind of marvelous adornment: raining clouds of every jeweled light, universally illumining every maṇi-king; raining clouds of every canopy, adorning every heavenly body; raining clouds of every flowering tree, adorning every kind of many-colored robe; raining clouds of every garland, streaming forth to adorn every maṇi jewel-king's own adornment; raining clouds of fragrance in every color, matching every sentient being's own body; raining clouds of jeweled flowers, clouds of heavenly maidens — each holding some marvelous treasure, turning through the open sky, adorning it with every kind of jewel, lion-thrones of red lotus and every mingled jewel adorning all of space.

Reading note — after Fazang

Fazang divides this whole manifestation into three concentric adornments — the hall, the grove, and the space above — and preserves an older gloss, attributed to the Northern Qi master Guangtong, that gives each its own doctrinal register: 嚴空表無為緣起，嚴園表有為緣起，嚴閣顯自體緣起故也 — "adorning space signifies unconditioned dependent origination; adorning the grove signifies conditioned dependent origination; adorning the hall makes manifest the self-nature's own dependent origination." None of the three, on this reading, is decoration for its own sake: the fixed, solid hall stands for a nature that gives rise to everything from within itself; the grove, alive and growing, stands for the conditioned unfolding that nature gives rise to; and the open, unbounded space above stands for the unconditioned ground neither production nor destruction ever touches — the whole scene, taken together, showing all three at once as a single, unbroken field.

On the recurring "why is this so" — the ten inconceivable reasons the text itself gives for its own extravagance — Fazang reads the first two as naming cause and fruit directly (roots of goodness as cause, pure Dharma as its fruit), the third as a general summary of the Tathāgata's spiritual power, the fourth as that power specifically in the body, the fifth and sixth as the fine-grained mutual interpenetration of body and land (a buddha-land's whole adornment entering a single body; a single mote of dust holding every buddha and the entire Dharma-realm at once), the seventh and eighth as a past buddha's radiance made present and a single light reaching every land, and the ninth and tenth as the Tathāgata's transformation-bodies and his manifestation of every land's arising and passing. He is explicit that none of this is metaphorical excess: on the account this sūtra is building, Jetavana's transformation and the transformation of the entire Dharma-realm are the very same event, seen at two different scales — which is exactly the claim the chapter's own title, "entering the Dharma-realm," will spend its remaining fifty pages making good on, one encounter at a time.

Part 2 Complete

This closes Part 2: the assembly's silent, sixty-clause wish that the Buddha reveal his own inconceivable domain, and his wordless answer — the Lion's-Vigor Samādhi, which transforms the Hall of the Layered Pavilion, Jeta's Grove, and the sky above it into an adornment that Fazang reads as the entire Dharma-realm seen at the scale of a single monastery. Part 3 turns to what gathers into this transformed space next: bodhisattvas beyond number, arriving from all ten directions.
