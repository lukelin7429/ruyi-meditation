The Three Poisons, the Two Wishes, and the Thirty-Three Bodies

Part 2 is the second and last fascicle of The Commentary on the Meaning of Guanyin. It finishes the first question and answer with the capacity of mind and the capacity of body. For the mind, Zhiyi treats freedom from lust, anger, and delusion as right mindfulness that is itself wisdom, then reads the three poisons "in the contrary way": the bodhisattva's great greed, anger, and delusion become three Dharma-gates of taking up, letting go, and neither, illustrated by Vasumitrā and King Anala in the Avataṃsaka. For the body, the wish for a son or a daughter is read as wisdom and concentration, traced from the five precepts up to the twin birth of son and daughter at the first ground or first abode; he rejects the view that Avalokiteśvara is only a "matchmaker." After the comparison of merit, the second question — how Avalokiteśvara journeys through the sahā world and teaches — is answered through the thirty-three bodies, regrouped as the ten realms, four propositions on bodies and realms, the responsive buddha in each of the lands, and short glosses on each god, human, and spirit form. The fascicle ends with the necklace offered and divided between two buddhas, and with Dharaṇiṃdhara's praise of hearing the chapter, explained through three levels of arousing the mind. The commentary does not go on to the verse portion of the chapter.

The Capacity of Mind: The Three Poisons

Chinese Source

第二、從若有眾生多於婬欲去，是明意機也。釋此為二：初貼文，二、觀解。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Should there be beings much given to lustful desire" on, the capacity of mind is explained. This is explained in two parts: first, glossing the text; second, explanation by contemplation.

Chinese Source

貼文為二：初正明意機，次結意機。

Ru-Yi Translation

Glossing the text is in two parts: first, the capacity of mind proper; next, concluding the capacity of mind.

Chinese Source

意機約三毒，為三章，章各有三：一、明有苦，二、默念，此兩即是明機，三、明離，即是明其應三也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The capacity of mind is set out in terms of the three poisons, in three chapters, each chapter with three parts: first, explaining that there is suffering; second, silent mindfulness — these two explain the capacity; third, explaining becoming free, which explains his response. [These are the] three.

Chinese Source

通稱毒者，侵害行人喻之如毒。但名有單複，有人解云：「三毒多者，不知其是過，故不求觀音；少者，念觀音梵行之德，所以能感。」意謂此解乖文。文云：「若有眾生多於婬欲，念即得離。」云何對面違經耶？今明三毒多者，能念觀音，菩薩有力令多得離，何況少相？此則以多況少爾。《大論》云：「女人違戒垢，謗法餘殃，不擇禽獸、不避高牆廣塹之難、不計名聞德行，破家亡國、滅族傾宗，禍延其身，如術婆伽禍延其國、如周敗褒姒。」《淨住》及《禪經》明：「多欲人有欲蟲，男蟲淚出而青白，女蟲吐血而紅赤。」又言：「有欲鬼嬈動其心，令生倒惑。」如《大經》云：「若習近貪欲，是報熟時，此舉多欲相也。若少欲人，蟲鬼潛伏，無過狂醉，是少欲相。」

Ru-Yi Translation

They are called "poisons" in general because they invade and harm practitioners and are likened to poison. But the names are single or compound. Someone explains: "Those with much of the three poisons do not know their fault and so do not seek Avalokiteśvara; those with little are mindful of Avalokiteśvara's virtue of pure conduct and therefore can appeal [to him]." I think this explanation goes against the text. The text says: "Should there be beings much given to lustful desire, [who constantly hold him in mind,] they shall become free." How can one contradict the sūtra to its face? Here we make clear that those with much of the three poisons can be mindful of Avalokiteśvara, and the bodhisattva has the power to free even those with much; how much more those with little! This compares the much to the little. The Great Treatise says: "Women [lead men to] break the precepts and defile [them], and to slander the Dharma, with lingering calamities; [those in lust] do not choose between birds and beasts, do not avoid the danger of high walls and wide moats, do not reckon reputation or virtuous conduct; they ruin families and lose countries, exterminate clans and overturn lineages, the disaster reaching their own persons — as Shupojia's disaster reached his country, as the Zhou was ruined by Bao Si." The Jingzhu and the Dhyāna Sūtras explain: "Those of much desire have desire worms: the male worm exudes tears, bluish white; the female worm vomits blood, red." They also say: "There are desire ghosts that disturb the mind and make it give rise to inverted delusion." As the Great Sūtra says: "If one habitually draws near greed and desire, this is when the retribution ripens; this gives the marks of much desire. In people of little desire the worms and ghosts lie hidden, without the faults of madness and intoxication; this is the mark of little desire."

Chinese Source

瞋恚多者，今世人不喜見，如渴馬護水、如射師子母，故《遺教》云：「劫功德賊無過瞋恚。」《華嚴》云：「一念瞋起，障百法明門。」菩薩以瞋乖慈，障道事重。《大集》云：「一念起瞋，一切魔鬼得便。」《涅槃》云：「習近瞋恚」，若例婬恚亦應有鬼。如《柰女經》：「瞋則有蝎蟲，是名多瞋相，與上相違是瞋少相。」愚癡多者，邪畫諸見，撥無因果、謗毀大乘。如《大經》例前亦應有蟲鬼。三毒過患如此，欲離此故，至心存念觀音即得離也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for those with much anger: people in this life do not like to see them, as a thirsty horse guards water, as one dreads shooting at a lioness. Hence the Bequeathed Teaching says: "Of the thieves that rob merit, none surpasses anger." The Avataṃsaka says: "When one thought of anger arises, it obstructs the gate of the hundred bright dharmas." For a bodhisattva, because anger goes against loving-kindness, obstructing the path is a weighty matter. The Great Collection says: "When one thought of anger arises, all the māras and ghosts find their opportunity." The Nirvāṇa says, "habitually drawing near anger"; by analogy with lust, anger too should have ghosts. As the Nainü Sūtra says: "With anger there are scorpion worms; this is called the mark of much anger; the opposite of the above is the mark of little anger." Those with much delusion devise all kinds of wrong views, deny cause and effect, and slander the Great Vehicle. By analogy with the Great Sūtra above, they too should have worms and ghosts. Such are the faults of the three poisons. Wishing to be free of them, one holds Avalokiteśvara in mind with utmost sincerity and becomes free.

Chinese Source

有人解云：「起伏相違，稱之為離，非滅離也。」今謂經文說離，何意言非？若依《請觀音》者：「淨於三毒根，成佛道無疑。」今作十番明救三毒：三番是伏惑論離，七番是斷惑論離也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Someone explains: "Rising and being suppressed oppose each other; this is called 'becoming free,' not freedom by extinction." I say: the sūtra text says "free"; why say it is not? According to the Sūtra of Invoking Avalokiteśvara: "[He] purifies the roots of the three poisons, and the accomplishment of the buddha-path is beyond doubt." Here we explain the rescue from the three poisons in ten sets: three sets speak of freedom by subduing delusion, and seven sets speak of freedom by severing delusion.

Chinese Source

問：離煩惱須智慧，但念豈得離耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Being free of afflictions requires wisdom; how can mere mindfulness bring freedom?

Chinese Source

答：經稱常念即是正念，體達煩惱性無所有，住貪欲際即是實際，絕四句無能無所，念性清淨，如此正念非是智慧，更何處覓智慧？此慧不離煩惱，其誰能離耶？若如所難，必須別用。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The sūtra calls it "constantly holding in mind"; this is right mindfulness, which embodies and penetrates that the nature of afflictions is nonexistent. Abiding at the limit of greed and desire is the reality-limit; cutting off the four propositions, without subject or object, the nature of mindfulness is pure. If such right mindfulness is not wisdom, where else would one look for wisdom? This wisdom does not leave afflictions; who else could become free? If it were as your objection says, [wisdom] would have to be used separately.

Chinese Source

智慧破煩惱者，此則有惑可斷、有智能斷，非唯惑不可斷，慧還成惑，豈得名斷惑之慧耶？今此正念不以色念、不以非色念，如是四句；亦以色念、亦以非色念，如是四句；或次第論非念、或不次第論非念，或不次第論念、或次第論念，或次第論離、或不次第論離。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "wisdom breaks afflictions": this means there is delusion that can be severed and wisdom that can sever. Not only can the delusion not be severed, but the wisdom turns back into delusion; how can it be called the wisdom that severs delusion? This right mindfulness is not mindfulness by form and not mindfulness by non-form — such are the four propositions; it is also mindfulness by form and also mindfulness by non-form — such are the four propositions. Or it discusses non-mindfulness in sequence, or non-mindfulness not in sequence; or mindfulness not in sequence, or mindfulness in sequence; or freedom in sequence, or freedom not in sequence.

Chinese Source

次就觀解者，七番例上可解，今但順、逆兩意約界外作也，不取分段三毒相。今取善欲之心名貪，《大經》云：「一切善法，欲為其本，二乘欲樂涅槃名貪、厭生死名瞋、不達此理名癡，開三毒即有八萬四千宛然具足。」《淨名》云：「結習未盡，華則著身」，二乘未斷此三毒，即變易三毒相也。未斷別惑菩薩亦同有此三毒，故云菩薩貪求佛法，於恒沙劫未曾暫捨，多學問無厭足即貪相。惡賤二乘，不喜聞其名，故言「寧起惡癩野干心，不起二乘心。」如大樹折枝之譬，豈非瞋相？無明重數甚多，佛菩提智之所能斷，佛性未了了者皆是癡相。欲除此三煩惱故，常念觀音，隨機應赴即得永離。永離有兩種：若此菩薩於生身中全未除別惑，就變易論全未永離；若生身中已侵別惑，就變易中除殘論永離。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explanation by contemplation. The seven sets can be understood by analogy with the above; here only the two senses of conforming and contrary are worked out with regard to [what lies] beyond the realms, not taking the marks of the three poisons of the segmented [birth and death]. Here the mind of wholesome desire is taken as greed. The Great Sūtra says: "Of all good dharmas, desire is the root. The two vehicles' desire and delight in nirvāṇa is called greed, their loathing of birth and death is called anger, and their not penetrating this principle is called delusion. Opening out the three poisons, the eighty-four thousand are all clearly present." The Vimalakīrti says: "The habitual residue is not yet exhausted, so the flowers cling to the body." The two vehicles have not severed these three poisons; these are the marks of the three poisons of change. Bodhisattvas who have not severed the distinct delusions also have these three poisons. Hence it is said that the bodhisattva greedily seeks the buddha-dharmas and through eons as many as the Ganges sands never lets go even for a moment; learning much without satiety is the mark of greed. He detests and despises the two vehicles and does not like to hear their names; hence it is said: "Rather give rise to the mind of a mangy, leprous jackal than give rise to the mind of the two vehicles." Like the simile of the great tree breaking its branches — is this not the mark of anger? The layers of ignorance are very many, and only the Buddha's bodhi-wisdom can sever them; not yet seeing buddha-nature clearly is all the mark of delusion. Wishing to remove these three afflictions, one constantly holds Avalokiteśvara in mind, and he responds according to capacity, so that one becomes free forever. Becoming free forever is of two kinds: if this bodhisattva, in his body of birth, has not yet removed the distinct delusions at all, then in terms of change he has not yet become free forever at all; if in his body of birth he has already encroached upon the distinct delusions, then removing the remainder within change is spoken of as becoming free forever.

次明逆說三毒觀者，一切眾生名為少欲瞋癡。何以故？止瞋三途之苦，貪人天之樂。二乘只瞋生死，欲得涅槃樂，皆名為少。菩薩不爾，樂求佛法，非但求一佛法，遍求一切不可說佛法，如海吞眾流猶自不滿，非但不受生死，亦不受涅槃，故《大品》五不受，此即大瞋。無明力大，佛智能斷，菩薩於無明大力之惑尚在。又，癡如虛空不可盡，乃至老死如虛空不可盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the contemplation of the three poisons spoken in the contrary way. All beings are called [those of] little desire, anger, and delusion. Why? They are merely angry at the suffering of the three paths and greedy for the pleasures of humans and gods. The two vehicles are only angry at birth and death and wish for the bliss of nirvāṇa; all are called "little." The bodhisattva is not so: he delights in seeking the buddha-dharmas, not seeking just one buddha-dharma but seeking everywhere all the unspeakable buddha-dharmas, like the ocean swallowing all the streams and still not full. He not only does not accept birth and death but also does not accept nirvāṇa; hence the five non-acceptances of the Larger [Prajñāpāramitā]. This is great anger. The power of ignorance is great, and only the Buddha's wisdom can sever it; in the bodhisattva the delusion of ignorance's great power still remains. Again: "Delusion is like empty space and cannot be exhausted, and so on up to old age and death being like empty space and unable to be exhausted."

Chinese Source

如此三毒即為三法門：一、取，二、捨，三、不取不捨。大慈大悲，四攝、十力、無畏、三昧、解脫、無上菩提、淨佛國土、化度眾生，名為取門，即大貪也。一切法空無所有，不住不著。般若如大火炎，四邊不可取。大涅槃空、迦毘羅城空，言語道斷，心行處滅，不以四句得菩提，無得無證即是捨門，名為大瞋也。中道非取、非捨，不憎、不愛，不斷、不常，無去、無來，無生、無滅，如鏡中像不可見而見、見而不可見，非可見、非不可見，遮二邊故，不可言說，淨名杜口，名為中道，此即大癡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Such three poisons are three Dharma-gates: first, taking up; second, letting go; third, neither taking up nor letting go. Great loving-kindness and great compassion, the four means of attraction, the ten powers, the fearlessnesses, the samādhis, the liberations, unsurpassed bodhi, purifying buddha-lands, and transforming and delivering beings are called the gate of taking up; this is great greed. All dharmas are empty and nonexistent, not abiding, not attached; prajñā is like a great flame that cannot be taken hold of on any of its four sides; great nirvāṇa is empty, the city of Kapilavastu is empty; the path of words is cut off and the place of mental activity extinguished; bodhi is not attained by the four propositions; without attainment or realization — this is the gate of letting go, called great anger. The middle way is neither taking up nor letting go, neither hating nor loving, neither annihilation nor permanence, without going or coming, without arising or ceasing, like an image in a mirror, invisible yet seen, seen yet invisible, neither visible nor invisible. Because it negates the two extremes, it cannot be spoken — Vimalakīrti shut his mouth — and it is called the middle way; this is great delusion.

Chinese Source

故文殊云：「我是貪欲尸利、瞋恚尸利、邪見尸利。」此即其明證。欲滿此三法門，常念觀音即得滿願一切，聖人自行、化他，無不從此三門而入，離此更無有道。故《無行經》云：「貪欲即是道，恚、癡亦如是，如是三法中，具足一切佛法。」一切佛法不出萬行波羅蜜、不受三昧廣大之用、中道實相，此三法門不可宣示，愍眾生故，或作順說、或作逆說，互有去取，此即四悉檀意赴緣利益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence Mañjuśrī said: "I am Greed-Śrī, Anger-Śrī, Wrong-View-Śrī." This is its clear proof. Wishing to fulfill these three Dharma-gates, one constantly holds Avalokiteśvara in mind and obtains the fulfillment of all wishes. All the sages, in their own practice and in transforming others, enter through nothing but these three gates; apart from them there is no other path. Hence the Wuxing jing says: "Greed and desire are the path; anger and delusion are likewise; within these three dharmas all the buddha-dharmas are complete." All the buddha-dharmas do not go beyond the myriad practices and pāramitās, the vast function of the samādhi of non-acceptance, and the true mark of the middle way. These three Dharma-gates cannot be proclaimed; out of pity for beings, sometimes they are spoken in the conforming way, sometimes in the contrary way, each taking up and leaving aside. This is the sense of the four siddhāntas, meeting conditions to benefit.

Chinese Source

如《華嚴》四十二明：「險難國寶莊嚴城婆須密多女說離欲際法門：『一切眾生隨類見我，我皆為其女像，見我者得歡喜三昧、共我語得無礙妙音三昧、執我手得詣諸佛剎三昧、共我宿者得解脫光明三昧、目視我者得寂靜法門、見我嚬伸得壞散外道法門、阿𪏭宜我者得攝一切眾生三昧、阿眾鞞我者得諸功德密藏，住是離欲法門廣為利益。』」此豈非逆順欲法門導利群品耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

As the forty-second [fascicle] of the Avataṃsaka explains: "In the land of Xiannan, in the city of Baozhuangyan, the woman Vasumitrā taught the Dharma-gate of the limit of freedom from desire: 'All beings see me according to their kind; to all of them I appear in a woman's form. Those who see me attain the samādhi of joy; those who speak with me attain the samādhi of unobstructed wondrous sound; those who hold my hand attain the samādhi of going to all buddha-lands; those who stay the night with me attain the samādhi of the light of liberation; those who gaze at me attain the Dharma-gate of tranquility; those who see me frown and stretch attain the Dharma-gate of crushing the heterodox; those who embrace (阿𪏭宜) me attain the samādhi of gathering in all beings; those who kiss (阿眾鞞) me attain the secret treasury of all merits. Abiding in this Dharma-gate of freedom from desire, I benefit widely.'" Is this not the Dharma-gate of desire, contrary and conforming, guiding and benefiting the multitudes?

Chinese Source

又，四十一：「滿幢城滿足王於正殿行王法，其犯法者斬截、燒煮、劈裂、屠膾、嗔目、訶責，苦楚治罪。善財生疑。王斷事已，執善財手入其宮，見不可思議境界不可譬喻，語善財云：『我知幻化法門，化作眾生而苦治之，以調一切，其見聞者發菩提心。』」此豈非瞋法門？方便命婆羅門五熱炙身即是癡法門，如前說。次此應明、別圓兩觀觀三毒慈悲機，感例可知，不具記。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, the forty-first: "In the city of Tāladhvaja, King Anala carried out the king's law in the main hall; those who broke the law were cut down, burned and boiled, split apart, butchered, glared at, and rebuked — punished with pain. Sudhana grew doubtful. When the king had finished judging, he took Sudhana by the hand and led him into his palace, where he saw inconceivable realms beyond comparison. He said to Sudhana: 'I know the Dharma-gate of illusory transformation: I conjure beings and punish them harshly, in order to tame all; those who see and hear it arouse the bodhi-mind.'" Is this not the Dharma-gate of anger? The brahmin Jayoṣmāyatana scorching his body with the five heats is the Dharma-gate of delusion, as explained before. Next, one should here explain the loving-kindness, compassion, and capacity of contemplating the three poisons with the two contemplations, distinct and perfect; the appeal can be known by analogy and is not recorded in full.

The Capacity of Body: Seeking a Son or a Daughter

Chinese Source

第三、從若有女人去，明身業為機亦為三：一、貼文，二、引事證，三、觀解。貼文為二：一、求願滿，二、結歎求。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "Should there be a woman" on, bodily action is explained as the capacity, also in three: first, glossing the text; second, citing events as evidence; third, explanation by contemplation. Glossing the text is in two: first, the fulfillment of wishes sought; second, concluding and praising the seeking.

Chinese Source

又為二：一、求男，二、求女。文云：「女人求男，若是無子則絕嗣，有子則父母俱欣。」云何獨標女人求男耶？解者或云：「女厭女身，非求子也。」又解：「女性多愛，欣子偏重。」故標女人。今解女人以無子為苦，夫之所棄、並婦所輕、旁人所笑。又，婦有七失，六猶可忍，無子最劇：容惡、性妬、不能事公、姑、貪食、無子、拙。無子既苦，故以標女人求男也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This again is in two: first, seeking a son; second, seeking a daughter. The text says: "A woman [who wishes] to have a son." [Yet] if there is no son the line is cut off, and if there is a son father and mother both rejoice; why single out the woman as seeking a son? Some explain: "The woman loathes the woman's body; she is not seeking a son." Again it is explained: "Women by nature love much, and their joy in children is especially weighty"; hence it singles out the woman. Our explanation: a woman takes having no son as suffering: she is cast off by her husband, slighted by the co-wives, and laughed at by others. Again, a wife has seven failings; six can still be tolerated, but having no son is the worst: an ugly face, a jealous nature, inability to serve her father-in-law and mother-in-law, greed for food, having no son, and clumsiness. Since having no son is suffering, the woman is singled out as seeking a son.

Chinese Source

求男，文為三：一、立願，二、修行，三、德業。願與行如文。德業者，明士有百行，智居其首，若但智而無福則位卑而財貧，觸途壈坎；智與福合，彌相扶顯，福則財位高昇，慧則名聞博遠，故言便生福德智慧之男也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "has accomplished merit such as this" on, is the general answer. This concludes the specific and opens the general. "Has accomplished merit such as this" concludes the specific; "journeying through every land in every kind of form" is the general answer. "Every" names what is not one: horizontally it covers the ten directions everywhere; vertically it crowns and passes through the three lands. Changing and manifesting according to capacity, how could it stop at thirty-three bodies? Entrusting his transformation to whatever conditions, how could it be limited to the sahā world? "In every kind of form" explains generally that the manifestation of bodies is broad. "Journeying through every land" explains generally that the places transformed are broad. "Bringing beings across to liberation" explains generally that the benefit gained is broad. Though in words it is briefer than the above, in meaning it is far broader than before; hence it is called the general answer. The text of Sudhana entering the dharma-realm, though broad, does not necessarily cover the ten dharma-realms in meaning. The Daśabhūmika masters, seeing its broad text, classed it as the perfect doctrine, and seeing the Lotus's brief text, classed it as the doctrine that is not true. If one searches this meaning, there is no sense of "not true."

Dharaṇiṃdhara's Praise

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "Dharaṇiṃdhara said" on, is the praise of the merit of hearing the chapter. The text "who hears this . Avalokiteśvara" is hearing the question and answer above on hidden benefit; "Universal Gate chapter" is hearing the question and answer on manifest benefit. Here it explains "sovereign deeds" (自在業): the karma of ordinary people is moistened by craving, and its contaminated causes and conditions are not sovereign. Avalokiteśvara, to tame the ten dharma-realms, shows these three kinds of action moistened by the power of loving-kindness and compassion, receiving birth according to appeal and not burdened by afflictions; hence "sovereign deeds." They are included in the truth of the middle way of the first meaning and are sovereign within the two truths. "Unequaled" (無等等): the two vehicles, though they leave the three realms, still have higher dharmas above them; they are not "without equal" (無等). The Buddha is the ultimate ground; hence "without equal." Arousing the mind seeking buddhahood — hence "unequaled" (無等等, "equal to the unequaled"): equal to the Buddha. Again, in terms of mind: the mind contains completely the eighty-four thousand Dharma-gates; if one arouses the mind of the true mark, it is equal to the eighty-four thousand Dharma-gates; it is also the eighty-four thousand pāramitās and the eighty-four thousand gates of the toils of the senses, because they are the seeds of the Tathāgata. A sūtra says: "The arousing of the mind and the ultimate are not two; of these two minds, the former is the harder." Now the first mind aroused is equal to the later mind. The first mind is hard to arouse; hence "without equal"; being equal to the later mind is called "equal to the unequaled." This is the sense of the four siddhāntas, explaining the arousing of the mind. Arousing the mind is of three kinds: first, arousing in name, that is, the disciples of the five grades; second, arousing in resemblance, that is, the purification of the six faculties; third, arousing in a portion of the truth, that is, from the first abiding upward. This arousing of the mind is the true arousing of the mind.

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

Sūtra wording. Lemmas follow the site's Lotus Sūtra ("Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate"): "much given to lustful desire," "a son of merit, virtue, and wisdom," "a daughter upright and fine in form," "beloved and revered by all," "their good fortune shall not be spent in vain," "How does Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva journey through this sahā world?", "journeying through every land in every kind of form, bringing beings across to liberation," "grant freedom from fear," "Out of pity for us," and Dharaṇiṃdhara's "sovereign deeds" and "his universal gate's manifestation." Lines quoted from other Lotus chapters (units 51, 52, 53) also use the site's English.

Contrary and conforming. Unit 10 reverses the usual reading: ordinary beings have only "little" greed, anger, and delusion, while the bodhisattva's are great, and the three become Dharma-gates. The Wuxing jing line "Greed and desire are the path" is from the Zhufa wuxing jing (T650); Mañjuśrī's "I am Greed-Śrī ." is also from that sūtra.

Avataṃsaka episodes. Vasumitrā (Ch. 婆須蜜多) and King Anala (Ch. 滿足王) are in the sixty-fascicle Avataṃsaka (T278), around fascicles 49–50; as in Part 1, Zhiyi's fascicle numbers (41, 42) follow a different division. 阿𪏭宜 and 阿眾鞞 are transliterations, glossed here as "embrace" (āliṅgana) and "kiss" (paricumbana).

Son and daughter. From unit 20 on, son and daughter stand for wisdom and concentration (or wisdom and loving-kindness), worked through the five precepts, the dhyānas, the two vehicles, and the four teachings; the "twin birth" at the first ground (distinct) or first abiding (perfect) is the point where both are true. "Before this, all of us are called people of wrong views" (unit 26) echoes the Nirvāṇa Sūtra.

Thirty-three bodies. Zhiyi counts thirty-three bodies and nineteen teachings, regrouped as eight sets and as the ten realms; the missing bodhisattva and hell realms are supplied from the general answer, the Sūtra of Invoking Avalokiteśvara ("roams at play in the hells," quoted in this round's draft wording), and the Great Treatise. The "ancient version" (古本) is the Zheng fahua jing (T263).

Four lands. Units 44–58 place the responsive buddha in the land of true reward, the land of expedients (the land of change, with the five kinds of mind-made bodies), and the land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together, pure or defiled. The five flavors (milk, cream, curdled butter, clarified butter, ghee) follow the glossary.

Snow Mountain grass. Unit 52's "grass called forbearance" (忍辱) is from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra; the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra pages so far have only the parallel "grass called 'rich and unctuous'" passage, so the line is translated here directly.

Comparison of merit. Unit 34 cites the Treatise on the Lotus (Vasubandhu, T1519/T1520) on "knowing the Dharma ultimately and decisively." Unit 35's beggar equal to the Tathāgata Nanshang (難勝) is from the Vimalakīrti (Vimalakīrti divides his necklace between the lowliest beggar and that buddha).

Necklace. The Yingluo Sūtra scale of necklaces runs from copper at the first abiding to maṇi at equal awakening. Unit 96 reads the two halves as the Dharma-body and reward-body buddhas (Śākyamuni and Prabhūtaratna in the sūtra).

Arousing the mind. Unit 97 quotes the Nirvāṇa Sūtra verse "the arousing of the mind and the ultimate are not two" and grades arousing the mind by the six identities (in name, in resemblance, in a portion of the truth).

Eight classes. The glosses draw on Kumārajīva and Sengzhao's notes to the Vimalakīrti (Zhu Weimojie jing, T1775). Unit 80's "text above" on the asuras is not in the Universal Gate chapter; source not located.

Apparent copying errors. 四眾未 (unit 46) is read as 末; 不思義 (unit 38) as 不思議. The "seven failings" of unit 12 list only six. Unit 36's question "how does he obtain the name 'Buddha'?" is translated as written though the context is the name Avalokiteśvara.

Where the text ends. The commentary stops after Dharaṇiṃdhara's praise and does not comment on the verse section (偈) of the chapter, which was added to the Kumārajīva text later.
