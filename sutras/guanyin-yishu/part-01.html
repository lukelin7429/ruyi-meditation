Akṣayamati's Question and the Seven Calamities

The Commentary on the Meaning of Guanyin (T1728) records Zhiyi's phrase-by-phrase lectures on the Universal Gate chapter of the Lotus Sūtra, written down by his disciple Guanding; it is the companion of The Profound Meaning of Guanyin, which treats the same chapter through the five profound meanings. Part 1 is the first fascicle. After dividing the chapter, Zhiyi comments on the opening: the time, the name Akṣayamati ("inexhaustible" read through the three truths), and the gestures of reverence, each given a literal and a contemplative reading. He then takes up the Buddha's general answer — the capacity of beings who suffer and call the name, the "phenomenal" and "in principle" single mind, and the single response that frees all at once — and the seven calamities that make up the capacity of speech: fire, water, rākṣasas, swords and staffs, ghosts, shackles and chains, enemies and bandits. Each calamity is treated three ways: glossing the text, stories from the Records of Responsive Manifestations, and a contemplative explanation that reads the calamity across ten sets of beings, from the hells to bodhisattvas in the land of change. The bodhisattva's power to answer all at once is traced to the loving-kindness and compassion he aroused in the distinct and perfect contemplations, accomplished as the king samādhi.

Dividing the Chapter

Chinese Source

此文既別出大部，有人亦作三段分文，謂：初問去為序、佛答去為正、持地去為流通。復有云：「經家序者為序，無盡意白佛去為正，持地去為流通。」今師有時亦作三段，有時不作三段名，但分為三章：一、無盡意問，二、佛答，三、持地歎；或為四章，三如前，四者、聞品得益；或作二段，謂前後兩問答也，多種分章隨人意用也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This text has been issued separately from the great work. Some people also divide the text into three sections, saying: from the first question on is the preface, from the Buddha's answer on is the main teaching, and from Dharaṇiṃdhara on is the dissemination. Others say: "The compilers' preface is the preface; from 'Akṣayamati said to the Buddha' on is the main teaching; from Dharaṇiṃdhara on is the dissemination." Our master sometimes also makes three sections, and sometimes does not use the name "three sections" but simply divides it into three chapters: first, Akṣayamati's question; second, the Buddha's answer; third, Dharaṇiṃdhara's praise. Or he makes four chapters: three as before, and fourth, the benefit gained by hearing the chapter. Or he makes two sections, namely the earlier and the later question and answer. These many ways of dividing the chapters are used as one sees fit.

Chinese Source

若作問答分章，則有兩問答，初問答明觀音、樹王冥益等義，後問答明普門、珠王顯益等義。就前問答為二：一、問，二、答。就問為四：一、時節，二、標人，三、敬儀，四、正問。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the chapters are divided by question and answer, there are two questions and answers. The first question and answer explains the meanings of Avalokiteśvara, the [medicine-]tree king, hidden benefit, and so on; the later question and answer explains the meanings of the Universal Gate, the [wish-fulfilling] jewel king, manifest benefit, and so on. The first question and answer has two parts: first, the question; second, the answer. The question has four parts: first, the time; second, naming the person; third, the gestures of reverence; fourth, the question proper.

"At That Time" and the Name Akṣayamati

Chinese Source

一、爾時者，爾言即也，即是說東方妙音弘經已訖，次說西方觀音弘經之時，故言爾時。或可大眾已聞妙音弘經，歡喜已竟，宜聞觀音，發心生善之時，故言爾時。或可時眾疑於妙音，若為利益，上來說法，破眾疑情已竟，時眾有疑觀音之德，正破此疑之時，故言爾時。或可時眾機在妙音聞即得道，如二土菩薩得道已竟，八萬四千悟理之時須聞觀音，故言爾時。諸佛如來不空說法，有四悉檀因緣爾乃為說，正是敷演四悉檀時，故言爾時也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, "At that time" (爾時): 爾 means "just then." It is just when [the Buddha], having finished speaking of Gadgadasvara from the east spreading the sūtra, next speaks of Avalokiteśvara from the west spreading the sūtra; hence "at that time." Or perhaps: the great assembly had heard of Gadgadasvara spreading the sūtra and had finished rejoicing, and it was fitting that they hear of Avalokiteśvara — the time for arousing the mind and giving rise to good; hence "at that time." Or perhaps: the assembly had doubts about Gadgadasvara — how he could benefit beings — and the teaching above had finished breaking the assembly's doubts; now the assembly had doubts about Avalokiteśvara's virtue, and it was just the time to break this doubt; hence "at that time." Or perhaps: the capacities of the assembly were such that on hearing of Gadgadasvara they attained the path — as the bodhisattvas of the two lands had finished attaining the path — and it was the time when eighty-four thousand [beings] awakening to principle needed to hear of Avalokiteśvara; hence "at that time." The buddhas, the tathāgatas, do not teach the Dharma in vain: only when there are the causes and conditions of the four siddhāntas do they teach. This is precisely the time of expounding the four siddhāntas; hence "at that time."

Chinese Source

二、標人者，即是無盡意也。名無盡者，非盡、非無盡，為對小乘名盡，故言無盡。小乘明盡為對盡智、無生智滅色取空之盡，故名無盡也。又，云何無盡？所謂空不可盡、假不可盡、中不可盡，故言無盡。《大品經》云：「即色是空，非色滅空。」空故無盡也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, naming the person: this is Akṣayamati (無盡意, "Inexhaustible Intent"). As for the name "inexhaustible": it is neither exhaustion nor non-exhaustion; it is in contrast to the Lesser Vehicle, which is called "exhaustion," that he is called "inexhaustible." The Lesser Vehicle speaks of exhaustion in relation to the knowledge of exhaustion and the knowledge of non-arising — the exhaustion of extinguishing form to take up emptiness; hence [this one] is called "inexhaustible." Again, what is inexhaustible? Namely: the empty cannot be exhausted, the provisional cannot be exhausted, the middle cannot be exhausted; hence "inexhaustible." The Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] Sūtra says: "Form itself is emptiness; it is not that form is extinguished [to make] emptiness." Because it is empty, it is inexhaustible.

Chinese Source

又，《大集經》釋無盡意：「東方過十恒沙國微塵世界，國名不眴，佛號普賢，純諸菩薩，無二乘名，但修念佛三昧，不滅、不生、不出，心行平等，猶如虛空，是為念佛。即見佛時，即具六波羅蜜，得無生忍，所謂：不取色即檀、除色相即尸、觀色盡即羼提、觀色寂滅即毘黎耶、不行色即禪、不戲論色即般若也。身子問：『誰為汝作字，名無盡意？』答曰：『一切諸法因緣果報無盡，一切諸法不可盡，初發無上菩提心已不可盡，譬如虛空不可窮盡，為一切智發菩提心，豈可盡乎？諸佛戒定慧解脫、解脫知見、十力、無畏等無盡，因如是等發心故不可盡。眾生性無盡、教化眾生無盡、知一切法性無盡故無盡，是名菩薩發心無盡。又，檀波羅蜜無盡，乃至方便無盡，凡八十無盡，八十無盡悉能含受一切佛法，從是得名，名無盡意也。』」

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, the Great Collection Sūtra explains Akṣayamati: "To the east, past worlds as many as the dust-motes of lands as many as the sands of ten Ganges Rivers, there is a land named Unblinking, whose buddha is named Samantabhadra. It is made up purely of bodhisattvas, without even the name of the two vehicles. They cultivate only the samādhi of mindfulness of the Buddha: not extinguishing, not arising, not emerging, the activity of the mind even and equal, like empty space — this is mindfulness of the Buddha. Upon seeing the Buddha, they are at once complete in the six pāramitās and attain the forbearance of non-arising; namely: not grasping form is dāna; removing the mark of form is śīla; contemplating the exhaustion of form is kṣānti; contemplating the quiescence of form is vīrya; not acting on form is dhyāna; not making conceptual elaboration about form is prajñā. Śāriputra asked: 'Who gave you your name, Akṣayamati?' He answered: 'The causes, conditions, fruits, and retributions of all dharmas are inexhaustible; all dharmas cannot be exhausted; the unsurpassed bodhi-mind, once first aroused, cannot be exhausted — just as empty space cannot be exhausted to its end. Arousing the bodhi-mind for the sake of all-knowledge — how could it be exhausted? The buddhas' precepts, concentration, wisdom, liberation, and knowledge and vision of liberation, the ten powers, the fearlessnesses, and so on are inexhaustible; because the mind is aroused on account of such things, it cannot be exhausted. The nature of beings is inexhaustible, teaching beings is inexhaustible, knowing the nature of all dharmas is inexhaustible; therefore it is inexhaustible. This is called the inexhaustibility of the bodhisattva's arousing of the mind. Again, the dāna-pāramitā is inexhaustible, and so on up to skillful means being inexhaustible — eighty inexhaustibles in all. The eighty inexhaustibles can contain all the buddha-dharmas; from these I received my name, and am named Akṣayamati.'"

Chinese Source

又，《淨名》云：「何謂為盡？謂不盡有為。何謂無盡？不住無為。」《華嚴》有十無盡法門，如此等經皆就假名分別一切諸法因緣果報，以明無盡意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, the Vimalakīrti says: "What is meant by exhaustion? It means not exhausting the conditioned. What is meant by the inexhaustible? Not dwelling in the unconditioned." The Avataṃsaka has the Dharma-gate of the ten inexhaustibles. Sūtras such as these all distinguish the causes, conditions, fruits, and retributions of all dharmas in terms of provisional names, to explain Akṣayamati.

Chinese Source

又如《勝鬘經》云：「如來色無盡，智慧亦復然，一切法常住。」又，《大品經》云：「一切法趣意，是趣不過。」意為法界，意則非盡、非無盡。如是無盡，例如非常、非無常，是乃為常。又，《淨名》云：「法若盡、若不盡，皆是無盡相。無盡相即是空，空則無有盡與不盡，故知非盡、非無盡是真無盡義。」又，《大品經》云：「癡如虛空不可盡，乃至老、死如虛空不可盡，色不可盡，乃至識不可盡。」如此等經皆約中道之理以名無盡，通達空假中三諦之法不可盡，故名無盡意菩薩，亦名無盡心智識、色、受、想、行等義，不可說不可說，不能具載。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, as the Śrīmālā Sūtra says: "The Tathāgata's form is inexhaustible, and so too is his wisdom; all dharmas abide eternally." Again, the Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] Sūtra says: "All dharmas tend toward intent (意); this tending cannot be gone beyond." Intent is the dharma-realm; intent, then, is neither exhaustion nor non-exhaustion. Inexhaustibility of this kind is like "neither permanent nor impermanent — this, then, is permanence." Again, the Vimalakīrti says: "Whether dharmas are exhausted or not exhausted, all are the mark of the inexhaustible. The mark of the inexhaustible is emptiness; in emptiness there is neither exhaustion nor non-exhaustion. Hence we know that neither exhaustion nor non-exhaustion is the true meaning of the inexhaustible." Again, the Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] Sūtra says: "Delusion is like empty space and cannot be exhausted, and so on up to old age and death being like empty space and unable to be exhausted; form cannot be exhausted, and so on up to consciousness being unable to be exhausted." Sūtras such as these all name the inexhaustible in terms of the principle of the middle way. Because he penetrates the dharmas of the three truths — empty, provisional, and middle — which cannot be exhausted, he is called Akṣayamati Bodhisattva. He is also named by meanings such as the inexhaustible mind, wisdom, consciousness, form, feeling, perception, formations, and so on — unspeakable, unspeakable, and impossible to record in full.

Chinese Source

菩薩者，外國云摩訶菩提質多薩埵，此云大道心眾生。始心行者為煩惱所生、二乘為五分法身所生、六度菩薩為福德所生、別圓為中道所生，故《大品》云：「如來身者，不從一因一緣生如來身。」菩薩為眾行生，故言眾生發心求佛，故言為大道利益一切，以法道成他，或言成眾生，廣釋菩薩義如別記。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Bodhisattva": in the foreign land it is called mahābodhicittasattva, which here means "a being of the mind of the great path." Practitioners of the beginning mind are born of afflictions; the two vehicles are born of the fivefold Dharma-body; bodhisattvas of the six perfections are born of merit; the distinct and perfect [teachings] are born of the middle way. Hence the Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] says: "The Tathāgata's body is not born of one cause or one condition." The bodhisattva is born of many practices; hence "being" (眾生, "born of many"). He arouses the mind and seeks buddhahood; hence "for the great path." He benefits all, bringing others to completion by the path of the Dharma; or it is said, "bringing beings to completion." The meaning of "bodhisattva" is explained at length as in a separate record.

The Gestures of Reverence

Chinese Source

三、敬儀者，為三：一、起，二、袒，三、合掌。起者，《禮》云，請益起、請業起，菩薩於佛備其二儀，故言起也。觀釋者，菩薩常修遠離行故言起，亦是契諸法空，空即是座，於此空無所染著，故言起也。又，菩薩安住空理，理本無起，愍眾生故，乘機利益故言起。又，中道之寂，非起、非不起，而能起、能不起。無起之起，起即實相，亦起眾生實相，故言起也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the gestures of reverence, in three parts: first, rising; second, baring [the shoulder]; third, joining the palms. "Rose": the Record of Rites says one stands up when asking for further instruction and when asking about one's studies; the bodhisattva fulfills both courtesies before the Buddha; hence "rose." Contemplative explanation: the bodhisattva constantly cultivates the practice of detachment; hence "rose." It is also accord with the emptiness of all dharmas: emptiness is the seat, and toward this emptiness he has no defiled attachment; hence "rose." Again, the bodhisattva rests securely in the principle of emptiness; the principle originally has no rising, but out of pity for beings he avails himself of their capacities to benefit them; hence "rose." Again, the stillness of the middle way is neither rising nor not rising, yet can rise and can not rise. The rising of no-rising: the rising is the true mark, and it also raises the true mark of beings; hence "rose."

Chinese Source

偏袒右肩者，外國以袒為敬。露右者，示執奉為便，表弟子事師充役之儀，是故以袒為恭也。此方以袒為慢，然古有須賈肉袒謝於張儀，露兩髀也，此方亦不一向是慢也。觀解者，覆露表空、假二諦，又表權實，實不可說，如覆左表有冥益；權於化便，如露右表有顯益也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Bared his right shoulder": in foreign lands baring is a sign of respect. Exposing the right [shoulder] shows readiness to take things up and serve, expressing the manner of a disciple serving a teacher and doing his tasks; for this reason baring is taken as respect. In this land baring is regarded as insolence; yet in ancient times Xu Gu bared his flesh to apologize to Zhang Yi, exposing both thighs; so in this land too it is not always insolence. Explanation by contemplation: covering and exposing express the two truths of the empty and the provisional; they also express the provisional and the real. The real cannot be spoken, like covering the left, which expresses hidden benefit; the provisional is convenient for transforming [beings], like exposing the right, which expresses manifest benefit.

Chinese Source

合掌者，此方以拱手為恭，外國合掌為敬。手本二邊，今合為一，表不敢散誕，專至一心，一心相當，故以此表敬也。觀解者，昔權實不合而今得合。又，五指表陰。《仁王經》云：「法性，色、受、想、行、識。」此即實智真身亦有五陰也，應化因緣亦有五陰也，眾生性德之理亦有五陰也，眾生生死果報亦有五陰也。聖人為化眾生，示有應身五陰，是則權實陰殊。若眾生法性理顯，聖人亦息化歸真。權實不二，合掌表於返本還源，入非權、非實，事理契合，故合掌也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Joined his palms": in this land folding the hands is a sign of courtesy; in foreign lands joining the palms is a sign of respect. The hands are originally two sides; now they are joined into one, expressing that one does not dare to be scattered and careless but is wholly devoted with a single mind; the single mind fitting [its object], this is used to express respect. Explanation by contemplation: formerly the provisional and the real were not joined, and now they are joined. Again, the five fingers represent the aggregates. The Humane Kings Sūtra says: "The Dharma-nature is form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness." That is, the true body of real wisdom also has five aggregates; the causes and conditions of response and transformation also have five aggregates; the principle of beings' inherent virtue also has five aggregates; the fruits and retributions of beings' birth and death also have five aggregates. The sage, in order to transform beings, shows the five aggregates of a responsive body; thus the aggregates of the provisional and the real differ. If the principle of beings' Dharma-nature is revealed, the sage too ceases transforming and returns to the true. The provisional and the real are not two: joining the palms expresses returning to the root and going back to the source, entering what is neither provisional nor real, phenomena and principle in accord; hence "joined his palms."

Chinese Source

向佛者，表萬善之因向萬德之果也，亦是行人分證權實合，向於究竟權實合，故言向佛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Toward the Buddha" expresses the cause of the myriad good [practices] facing toward the fruit of the myriad virtues. It is also the practitioner's partial realization of the joining of the provisional and the real facing toward the ultimate joining of the provisional and the real; hence "toward the Buddha."

The Question

Chinese Source

四、發問者，此下有兩番問答，初番問觀世音，後番問普門。前問為三：一、稱歎，二、標所問人，三、正問。《大經》云：「汝具二莊嚴能問是義，我具二莊嚴能答是義。」今無盡意具二莊嚴，欲顯觀音二種莊嚴，諮發如來；如來究竟具二莊嚴，當答此義。《釋論》云：「問有多種，不解問、試問、赴機問，今無盡意即是赴機問也。」世尊者，即是稱歎尊號，十號具出。《釋論》：「用彼釋此觀世音菩薩，即是標所問之人也。」具如前釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, raising the question. Below there are two rounds of question and answer: the first round asks about Avalokiteśvara, the later round asks about the Universal Gate. The first question has three parts: first, praise; second, naming the person asked about; third, the question proper. The Great Sūtra says: "You are complete with the two adornments and can ask about this meaning; I am complete with the two adornments and can answer this meaning." Here Akṣayamati, complete with the two adornments, wishing to reveal Avalokiteśvara's two kinds of adornment, consults and draws out the Tathāgata; the Tathāgata, ultimately complete with the two adornments, will answer this meaning. The Great Treatise says: "There are many kinds of question: questions from not understanding, test questions, and questions that meet capacities." Here Akṣayamati's is a question that meets capacities. "World-Honored One" is praise by the honored title; the ten titles are given in full [in the Great Treatise]. The Great Treatise: "Use that to explain this." "Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva" is the naming of the person asked about; [it is] fully as explained before.

Chinese Source

何因緣者，因緣甚多，略言境智因緣。若就眾生，則以善、惡兩機為因，聖人靈智慈悲為緣；若就聖人，觀智慈悲為因，眾生機感為緣。以是因緣，名觀世音，如上釋也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"By what cause and condition": causes and conditions are very many; briefly, they are the causes and conditions of object and wisdom. In terms of beings, their good and evil capacities are the cause, and the sage's numinous wisdom, loving-kindness, and compassion are the condition. In terms of the sage, his contemplating wisdom, loving-kindness, and compassion are the cause, and beings' capacities and appeals are the condition. By these causes and conditions he is named Avalokiteśvara, as explained above.

The General Answer

Chinese Source

第二、佛答，即為三：初總答，次別答，三、勸持。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the Buddha's answer, in three parts: first, the general answer; next, the specific answers; third, the exhortation to uphold [his name].

Chinese Source

就初總答為二：一、明機，二、明應。就機為四：一、標人數，二、遭苦，三、聞名，四、稱號。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first, the general answer, has two parts: first, clarifying the capacity; second, clarifying the response. The capacity has four parts: first, giving the number of people; second, meeting with suffering; third, hearing the name; fourth, calling his title.

Chinese Source

數者，十法界機實自無量，而言百千萬億者，此乃通途商略。

Ru-Yi Translation

The number: the capacities of the ten dharma-realms are in fact immeasurable in themselves; saying "hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis" is a rough general estimate.

Chinese Source

業同者，如一地獄界大略是同，其間優降復有何量？如一獄復有百千萬億品格之殊，一一品格復有百千萬億罪人，是罪業正同，所以同受一品罪苦，將此意廣歷餓鬼、畜生、修羅、人、天皆亦如是，故知此數是標同業之意也。所以舉多數者，明百千萬億種業遭苦稱名，一時有機，一時能應，皆得解脫，何況一人、一業、一機獨來而不能救？此舉境、眾、機多，以顯觀深應大也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Same karma": for example, the realm of a single hell is broadly the same, yet within it how many gradations of better and worse there are! A single hell again has hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of different classes, and each class has hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of offenders whose karma of offense is exactly the same, so that they undergo the same class of punishment and suffering. Extend this sense widely through hungry ghosts, animals, asuras, humans, and gods: all are likewise. Hence we know that this number marks the sense of "same karma." The reason a large number is given is to show that when hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of kinds of karma meet with suffering and call the name, at one time there is the capacity and at one time he can respond, and all attain liberation — how much more when one person, one karma, one capacity comes alone: could he fail to save it? Translator's notes

Sūtra wording. Every phrase quoted from the Universal Gate chapter follows the site's Lotus Sūtra ("Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate"): Akṣayamati, "every kind of suffering and affliction," "swept away by a great flood," "shallow ground," "the peril of the rākṣasas," "with an evil eye," "grant freedom from fear," "Such is the majesty." Shared terms. Capacity / appeal / response (機／感／應), hidden and manifest benefit, the king samādhi, ten sets, and loving-kindness and compassion without object follow the site's Profound Meaning of Guanyin, which this commentary presupposes; it is cited at unit 25 as "the Profound Meaning of the great text" (i.e., the Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra).

Three aspects. For each calamity Zhiyi gives 貼文 "glossing the text," evidence from events, and a contemplative reading (觀解／觀釋／觀行解釋, kept distinct as "explanation by contemplation," "contemplative explanation," "explanation in terms of contemplative practice").
