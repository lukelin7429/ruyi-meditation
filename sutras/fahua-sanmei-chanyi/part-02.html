Worship, Repentance of the Six Faculties, Contemplation, and the Marks of Realization

This part completes Zhiyi's rite. The Sixth Method is a long sequence of single bows — to Śākyamuni, Prabhūtaratna, the buddhas of the ten directions, the buddhas past, present, and future named in the Lotus, the stūpas and the sūtra, the bodhisattvas of the Lotus, and finally Samantabhadra, called out three times as "the master of the Lotus repentance." The Seventh Method is the repentance of the six faculties, one faculty at a time, in wording adapted from the Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra, followed by short formulas for requesting, rejoicing, dedication, and making vows; the vow aims at birth in Amitābha's land. The Eighth Method is circumambulation of the Dharma seat with the three refuges, and the Ninth sets rules for reciting the Lotus, or only its "Peaceful Practices" chapter. The Tenth Method, seated contemplation of the true mark, investigates the present thought through four phrases until mind is found unobtainable; this is called the repentance of the mark of no offense. Section Five then sets out three grades each of purity in the precept, concentration, and wisdom faculties, warns against look-alike signs from Māra, and explains that the twenty-one-day schedule is a training for beginners.

Ru-Yi Translation

The Sixth Method: Clarifying the Method of Bowing to the Buddhas (Having finished praising, the practitioner should, with a single mind, setting the body straight in proper deportment, next bow to the buddhas in order. In the method of bowing to the buddhas, one should, according to the buddha one bows to, recollect with utmost mind: this buddha's Dharma-body is like empty space, manifesting form in response to beings as if before my eyes, accepting my bow; each of the other buddhas is also like this. In using the mind one must not be scattered. Further, when the practitioner bows to the buddhas, he knows for himself that body and mind are empty and still, without a mark of bowing; he also knows that this body, though like an illusion and not real, is yet not without its image appearing: before each and every buddha of the dharma-realm this body is present, bowing head and face to the ground. The method of bowing to the buddhas in the six periods during the three sets of seven days is as listed below, without difference.)

The Seventh Method: Repenting the Eye and Ear Faculties

Chinese Source

第七明懺悔六根及勸請隨喜迴向發願方法

Ru-Yi Translation

The Seventh Method: Clarifying the Methods of Repenting the Six Faculties and of Requesting, Rejoicing, Dedication, and Making Vows

Chinese Source

（行者既禮佛竟，即於法座前正身威儀，燒香散華，存想三寶畟塞虛空，普賢菩薩乘六牙白象，無量莊嚴眷屬圍遶，如對目前，一心一意，為一切眾生行懺悔法，生重慚愧發露無量劫來及至此生，與一切眾生，六根所造，一切惡業斷相續心，從於今時乃至盡未來際，終不更造一切惡業。所以者何？業性雖空，果報不失，知空之人尚不作善，況復作罪。若造惡不止，悉是顛倒因緣，則受妄果。是故行者以知空故生大慚愧，燒香散華發露懺悔。下所說懺悔章句，多用普賢觀經意，若欲廣知懺悔方法，讀經自見；若不能廣尋，今取意略說以成行法。）

Ru-Yi Translation

(Having finished bowing to the buddhas, the practitioner, before the Dharma seat, sets his body straight in proper deportment, burns incense and scatters flowers, and visualizes the Three Jewels crowding and filling empty space, and Samantabhadra Bodhisattva riding the six-tusked white elephant, surrounded by an immeasurably adorned retinue, as though before his eyes. With one mind and one intention, for the sake of all sentient beings, he carries out the method of repentance: giving rise to heavy shame, he reveals all the evil actions created through the six faculties together with all sentient beings from immeasurable kalpas past up to this life, and cuts off the mind of continuation, [resolving that] from this moment to the end of the future he will never again create any evil action. Why? Although the nature of action is empty, the fruit and retribution are not lost. A person who knows emptiness does not even create good — how much less would he create offenses? If one creates evil without stopping, it is all inverted causes and conditions, and one receives a false fruit. Therefore the practitioner, because he knows emptiness, gives rise to great shame, burns incense, scatters flowers, and reveals and repents. The repentance phrases given below mostly use the intent of the Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra; if one wishes to know the methods of repentance broadly, read the sūtra and see for oneself; if one cannot search it broadly, I now take its intent and state it briefly to make up the method of practice.)

Chinese Source

最初懺悔眼根法（行者一心胡跪正身威儀，燒香散華，心念改悔，我與眾生，眼根從昔已來，性常空寂，顛倒因緣起諸重罪，流淚悲泣，口宣懺悔。下五根懺悔威儀方法，例如今說。口即自言：）

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the Method of Repenting the Eye Faculty (The practitioner, with a single mind, kneels with one knee raised, setting the body straight in proper deportment, burns incense and scatters flowers, and thinks in his mind of correcting and repenting: "The eye faculty of myself and sentient beings has from the beginning been by nature always empty and still, [yet] through inverted causes and conditions has given rise to grave offenses." Shedding tears and weeping in sorrow, he proclaims the repentance aloud. The deportment and method for repenting the five faculties below follow what is said here. Then he says aloud himself:)

Chinese Source

「至心懺悔，比丘（某甲）與一切法界眾生，從無量世來，眼根因緣貪著諸色，以著色故貪愛諸塵，以愛塵故受女人身，世世生處惑著諸色，色壞我眼為恩愛奴，故色使我經歷三界，為此弊使盲無所見，眼根不善傷害我多，十方諸佛常在不滅，我濁惡眼障故不見。今誦大乘方等經典，歸向普賢菩薩及一切世尊，燒香散華，說眼過罪不敢覆藏，諸佛菩薩慧眼法水願與洗除，以是因緣令我與一切眾生眼根一切重罪畢竟清淨。」（懺悔已，禮三寶，說是語已，五體投地。普賢觀經中，明懺六根，悉須三說；若時久難行，一說亦得，但作是言：「第二第三亦如是說。」行者當自思憶經，於此身眼根所起重罪，對普賢發露懺悔，下五根皆有此意。）

Ru-Yi Translation

"With utmost mind I repent. I, the bhikṣu (so-and-so), together with all sentient beings of the dharma-realm, from countless lives past, because of the eye faculty, have been greedily attached to forms. Because of attachment to forms, I craved the objects; because of craving the objects, I received a woman's body, and in every life, wherever I was born, I was deluded and attached to forms. Forms have ruined my eyes, making me a slave of affection and love; therefore forms have driven me to pass through the three realms; because of this wretched driver I have been blind and seen nothing. The eye faculty is not good and has harmed me greatly. The buddhas of the ten directions constantly abide and do not perish, [but] because of the obstruction of my turbid, evil eyes I do not see them. Now I recite the Great Vehicle Vaipulya scriptures and turn to Samantabhadra Bodhisattva and all the World-Honored Ones; burning incense and scattering flowers, I tell the faults and offenses of the eyes and dare not conceal them. May the buddhas and bodhisattvas, with the Dharma-water of the wisdom-eye, wash them away. By these causes and conditions, may all the grave offenses of the eye faculty of myself and all sentient beings be ultimately purified." (Having repented, bow to the Three Jewels; having said these words, prostrate, casting the five limbs to the ground. The Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra makes clear that the repentance of each of the six faculties must be said three times; if time is long and it is hard to carry out, saying it once is also acceptable, but one then says only: "The second and third times are said likewise." The practitioner should himself reflect on and recollect the sūtra, and before Samantabhadra reveal and repent the grave offenses arising from the eye faculty of this body; the five faculties below all have this intent.)

The Method of Repenting the Ear Faculty

Chinese Source

「至心懺悔，比丘（某甲）與一切法界眾生從多劫來，耳根因緣隨逐外聲，聞妙音時心生惑著，聞惡聲時起百八種煩惱賊害，如此惡耳報得惡事，恒聞惡聲生諸攀緣，顛倒聽故當墮惡道邊地，邪見不聞正法，處處惑著無暫停時，坐此竅聲，勞我神識墜墮三塗，十方諸佛常在說法，我濁惡耳障故不聞。今始覺悟，誦持大乘功德海藏，歸向普賢菩薩及一切世尊，燒香散華，說耳過罪不敢覆藏，以是因緣令我與法界眾生，耳根所起一切重罪畢竟清淨。」（懺悔已禮三寶。）

Ru-Yi Translation

"With utmost mind I repent. I, the bhikṣu (so-and-so), together with all sentient beings of the dharma-realm, for many kalpas, because of the ear faculty, have chased after outer sounds. On hearing wondrous sounds, my mind became deluded and attached; on hearing evil sounds, a hundred and eight kinds of afflictions arose as robbers to harm me. With such evil ears I reap as retribution evil things, constantly hear evil sounds, and give rise to all kinds of clinging to conditions. Because of hearing in an inverted way, I shall fall into the evil paths and the borderlands, and with wrong views not hear the true Dharma; everywhere I was deluded and attached without a moment's rest. Because of these sounds of the openings, my spirit and consciousness have been wearied and I have fallen into the three evil paths. The buddhas of the ten directions constantly teach the Dharma, [but] because of the obstruction of my turbid, evil ears I do not hear it. Now for the first time I awaken; reciting and upholding the Great Vehicle, the ocean-store of merit, I turn to Samantabhadra Bodhisattva and all the World-Honored Ones; burning incense and scattering flowers, I tell the faults and offenses of the ears and dare not conceal them. By these causes and conditions, may all the grave offenses arising from the ear faculty of myself and the sentient beings of the dharma-realm be ultimately purified." (Having repented, bow to the Three Jewels.)

"With utmost mind I repent. Because of greed for fragrances, I discriminated with the consciousnesses and was defiled and attached everywhere, and fell into birth-and-death, undergoing painful retributions. The wondrous fragrance of the merits of the buddhas of the ten directions fills the dharma-realm, [but] because of the obstruction of my turbid, evil nose I do not smell it. Now I recite the pure and wondrous scripture of the Great Vehicle and turn to Samantabhadra Bodhisattva and all the World-Honored Ones; burning incense and scattering flowers, I tell the faults and offenses of the nose and dare not conceal them. By these causes and conditions, may all the faults and offenses of the nose faculty of myself and all sentient beings be ultimately purified." (Having repented, bow to the Three Jewels.)

Translator's notes

The buddhas of the ten directions (units 4–13) — Good Virtue, Sorrowless Virtue, Sandalwood Virtue, Jewel Giving, Immeasurable Light, Flower Virtue, Mark Virtue, Three-Vehicle Practice, Vast Assembly Virtue, and Bright Virtue are the ten-direction buddhas of the "Easy Practice" chapter of the Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā (T1521); the Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra names Good Virtue (east) and Sandalwood Virtue (south), whose renderings follow the round-two draft of that sūtra.

"Twenty thousand koṭis of Candrasūryapradīpa Buddhas" — The Lotus, ch. 1, speaks of twenty thousand buddhas of that name; the rite's 二萬億 is translated as written. The Siming zunzhe jiaoxing lu (T1937), a collection of Zhili's writings, quotes the same line from a "Nanyue Lotus repentance method."

Ratnatejo'bhyudgatarāja (寶威德上王) — Samantabhadra's home buddha in "Samantabhadra's Encouragement." Complete with a Thousand Myriad Marks of Light and Flower Light follow the site's Lotus.

"The master of the Lotus repentance" (法華懺悔主) — Samantabhadra is the one to whom offenses are told; the other buddhas and bodhisattvas are witnesses.

"A person who knows emptiness does not even create good" (知空之人尚不作善) — Translated literally. The point appears to be that one who knows emptiness does not even cling to creating good; alternatively 作 may mean "deliberately make," i.e., "does not even contrive good."

The six-faculty repentances — Units 35–45 rework passages of the Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra, and the English reuses the round-two draft of that sūtra, shifted from "you" to "I." Differences in the rite: "a hundred and eight kinds of afflictions" where the sūtra has "eight hundred"; "Dharma-water of the wisdom-eye" (慧眼) where the sūtra has "bright wisdom" (慧明); and added lines on the buddhas whom the faculty fails to see, hear, smell, taste, or feel.

"To see impurity within bodhi and to give rise to bondage within liberation" — The rite introduces it as part of "the sūtra" after the Vairocana line (which is in the Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra), but this sentence was not found in that sūtra; source not located.

Requesting, rejoicing, dedication, vows — Vocabulary follows the round-two drafts of the Golden Light Repentance Rite and Zhili's Essential Purport of Repentance Practice (repent, requesting, rejoicing, dedication, making vows). The vow for birth in "[the Land of] Peaceful Nourishment" uses the site's Amitābha Sūtra name for Sukhāvatī.

The three refuges — Wording follows the site's Great Compassion Repentance; this rite has "embody and understand the great path" (體解大道) where that page has "carry forward the seed of buddhahood."

"Without entering samādhi, simply through reciting and upholding, one sees supremely wondrous forms" — From the Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra, quoted with its order shifted; wording from the round-two draft.

Seated contemplation — "Not to sever the fetters and envoys and not to abide in the ocean of envoys," "contemplating mind, there is no mind … the truth of cessation is tranquil and still," and the four names of repentance are from the Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra (round-two draft wording); "contemplating all dharmas as empty, according to their true mark" is from "The Four Peaceful Practices" (the site has "real mark"; here the glossary's "true mark"). "The preliminary expedients for seated meditation" refers to Zhiyi's meditation manuals; which one is not stated.

The four phrases — "Is it mind because of a mind?" and the rest are the four phrases Zunshi defends in his preface (Part 1); "because of" renders 因.

"Seated meditation of the four periods" (四時坐禪) — Not explained in the rite; presumably four of the six periods of day and night.

Precept, concentration, and wisdom faculties (戒根／定根／慧根) — 根 is kept as "faculty" throughout, as in "lower / middling / superior faculties." The Brahmā's Net Sūtra (T1484) gives the good signs for receiving the precepts as "the Buddha comes and strokes one's head; one sees light, sees flowers, various extraordinary signs." "The precepts of the three sections" (三篇) is left literal.

The revolving dhāraṇī — The three dhāraṇīs and "a body pleasing for every being to behold" use the site's "Samantabhadra's Encouragement."

"Must not at once give rise to the thought that they are the same" (不得即生等心) — Alternatively: "must not at once think oneself equal [to the sages]."
