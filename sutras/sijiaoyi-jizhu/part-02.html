The Lotus and Nirvāṇa Periods and the Tripiṭaka Teaching's Truth of Suffering (I)

This part continues Mengrun's notes from the end of Chegwan's account of the Lotus period through the opening of the Tripiṭaka teaching. Mengrun first explains the four "ones" of the Lotus (teaching, practice, persons, principle), the noon sun that casts no shadow, and the scene in which the destitute son receives the family estate, drawing on the Textual Commentary for the stages of "opening, showing, awakening, and entering" and for the kinds of prediction. He then treats the Nirvāṇa Sūtra as the "gleaning" teaching and the teaching that "supports the precepts and discusses the eternal," compares its four teachings with those of the Vaipulya, and sets out two ways of matching the five flavors to the Five Periods. A long note distinguishes the Five Periods taken separately (in sequence) from the Five Periods taken in common (each reaching before and after). With the Four Teachings of Content, he gives sources for the names of the four teachings, defines the three piṭakas, defends the name "Tripiṭaka teaching" against Huiyuan's criticism, and begins the truth of suffering: the twenty-five existences and the first five of the six paths, with much cosmological detail from the Kośa and the Vibhāṣā. Fascicle three ends and fascicle four begins in this part.

Ru-Yi Translation

Because it is pure and single, it alone gets the name "wondrous"; hence it cites the ones to reveal the wondrous, for the one is the wondrous. "The buddha-lands of the ten directions" and so on is said according to what they share, and is also in terms of the Buddha's intent. "The Dharma of the one vehicle": because the division is perfect and the teaching is perfect. "There is no second, and no third": in terms of the teaching, there is no "second" of the shared teaching, where half-word and full-word are set against each other, and no "third," the three vehicles of the Tripiṭaka; "there is no other vehicle" means that there is no distinct teaching and no perfect entering the distinct. In terms of the division, there is no "second" accompanied in the Prajñā and no "third" contrasted in the Vaipulya; the Tripiṭaka of the Vaipulya embraces the Deer Park, and the distinct [teaching] of the two butters covers the Avataṃsaka. It is only the one buddha-vehicle; hence it says "the oneness of teaching." "I set aside skillful means directly, and teach only the unsurpassed path": Textual Commentary 5 (5) says: "The five vehicles are bent and roundabout rather than direct, and the shared and distinct teachings are one-sided and oblique rather than upright; now he sets aside all that is one-sided and bent, and teaches only the single, upright, and direct path" (end of quotation). The Commentary, taking the side of the teaching, assigns this to the oneness of teaching; here, taking "path" as named for its power to lead through, it is assigned to the oneness of practice. "Only for bodhisattvas": in terms of the Buddha's intent, it is only for bodhisattvas; according to the skillful means of former times, it was called teaching and transforming the three vehicles; now these are all the same bodhisattvas; hence it says "the oneness of persons." "The marks of the world are constantly abiding": the separated and differentiated marks of the dependent and principal [rewards] of the ten realms are called the marks of the world; because they are identical to principle, all are constantly abiding. As for deluded views, [these marks] arise and perish and flow on; when deluded feelings are cleared away and forgotten, all marks are constantly abiding. Since this "constant" is identical to the nature, it is neither constant nor impermanent: "the words are one-sided, the meaning is perfect" — this is what is meant. Students should understand and grasp this here.

Ru-Yi Translation

Those who spread the teaching in that age had not understood the subtle and wondrous purport of the Lotus, opening the provisional and the absolute; they saw only that the sūtra has similes such as the three carts, and said that it did not reach the Avataṃsaka and other sūtras. They did not know that the similes such as the three carts raise again the provisional of former days, with the intent of pointing to the provisional as identical to the real. Hence it raises the three carts to reveal the great cart, the destitute son to hand over the family estate, and the phantom city to reach the place of treasure. Not knowing this intent, they incurred the fault of slandering the Dharma. "The three carts": the goat cart is a simile for the śrāvaka vehicle, the deer cart a simile for the pratyekabuddha vehicle, and the water-buffalo cart a simile for the bodhisattva vehicle; these are the three vehicles of the Deer Park. "The phantom city": Textual Commentary 7 (18) says: "Because of spiritual power, what was not comes to be all at once; this is called 'phantom.' Guarding against wrongdoing and warding off the enemy is called 'city'" (end of quotation). It is a simile for the nirvāṇa of the truth of the real, which can guard against views and habituated thought. "The place of treasure" is a simile for [the land of] tranquil light; in the Great Sūtra it is called the island of treasure. "The provisional of the previous four periods": the three carts and so on point to the three vehicles of the former Tripiṭaka; yet it says "raises again the provisional of the previous four periods." You should know that in the opening and revealing of the three rounds, the Tripiṭaka and the perfect are set against each other; though it properly opens the small capacities, in raising the provisional of former times it covers the four periods. Further, these small capacities passed through the previous four periods, hence it is called the provisional of the four periods. Miaole 5 (13) says: "The words 'establishing the one and opening the provisional' now come to have two senses: first, recounting what was bestowed in former times; second, [serving] as the means of revealing the real. Without pointing to what is opened, there would be no way to teach the real; moreover, pointing to the provisional as provisional, one knows it is not ultimate; once the real has been revealed, the provisional is wholly the real" (end of quotation). "Slander": Annotations 10 (3) says: "You should know that the Lotus, in terms of division, still refutes the Avataṃsaka and the Prajñā, and in terms of teaching, still refutes the later mind of the distinct teaching" (end of quotation). People did not see this, and so incurred slander.

The Lotus as Noon and Ghee

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: In terms of period, it is the sun's disk at noon, casting no slanting shadow anywhere (the fifth period);

Chinese Source

十界咸開無不成佛如日方中無處不南周禮用一尺五寸土圭立八尺之表夏至午時以側日影求地之中以建國宋嚴觀二師與太史何承天用此法測日影以定中國表北得影一尺五寸與土圭等地上餘陰一寸天上萬里則知天竺方為地中今云罄無側影據天竺說。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten realms are all opened and none fails to become a buddha, as when the sun is at the zenith and there is no place not [lit from the] south. The Rites of Zhou uses an earth-gnomon of one foot five inches and sets up a pole of eight feet; at noon on the summer solstice it seeks the center of the earth by the slanting shadow of the sun, in order to found the capital. In the [Liu] Song, the two masters [Hui]yan and [Hui]guan, with the Grand Astrologer He Chengtian, used this method to measure the sun's shadow and determine the central land: north of the pole they obtained a shadow of one foot five inches, equal to the earth-gnomon. One inch of remaining shadow on earth [corresponds to] ten thousand li in the heavens; thus they knew that India is the center of the earth. That it now says "casting no slanting shadow anywhere" is said with reference to India.

Ru-Yi Translation

Annotations 1 (19): "Question: That sūtra itself uses ghee as a simile for the Nirvāṇa; how can it now be used as a simile for the Lotus? Answer: The meaning of our school is that the two divisions share the same flavor, yet the Nirvāṇa is still inferior. Why? The Lotus opening the provisional is like breaking a great battle formation; the remaining capacities that reach that [sūtra] are like remnants of the [defeated] party, not difficult [to deal with]. Hence the Lotus is taken as the great harvest and the Nirvāṇa as the gleaning. If it were not so, the Nirvāṇa should not point back far to the eight thousand śrāvakas who in the Lotus obtained predictions and saw the Tathāgata-nature, like the autumn harvest stored away in winter, with nothing further to be done" (end of quotation). Yet that sūtra originally has no words "comes the Lotus"; it is now said in terms of meaning; hence it says only, "This is the Lotus coming from the Mahāprajñā."

The Destitute Son Receives the Estate

Chinese Source

信解品云聚會親族即自宣言此實我子我實其父吾今所有皆是子有付與家業窮子歡喜得未曾有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: The "Faith and Understanding" chapter says: "He gathered together his relatives and himself declared: 'This is truly my own son, and I am truly his father. All that I now possess belongs to my son.' He handed over the family estate, and the destitute son rejoiced, gaining what he never had before."

Chinese Source

文句六三十一云十方法身菩薩影響者為親族影響之眾多是釋迦昔日同業並共如來於二萬億佛所共開化之於其即是伯叔之行故用此為親族文此實我子我實其父結會父子文句六二十一云實從我受學實是我子從我起解是我所生我實曾於二萬億佛所常教大法故我實是父文吾今所有皆是子有正付家業文句六三十一云一切大乘萬德萬行故云所有文又如來藏子性不殊故云皆是子有當知如來所有即子本有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Textual Commentary 6 (31) says: "The bodhisattvas of the Dharma-body of the ten directions, who are shadows and echoes, are the relatives. Most of this multitude of shadows and echoes shared the same work with Śākyamuni in the past, and together with the Tathāgata opened and transformed [these disciples] at the place of the twenty thousand million buddhas; toward them they stand in the rank of uncles; hence they are used as the relatives" (end of quotation). "This is truly my own son, and I am truly his father": settling father and son together. Textual Commentary 6 (21) says: "Truly, from me they received their learning — truly, they are my children; from me arose their understanding — they are what I brought forth. I truly once, at the assembly of the twenty thousand million buddhas, constantly taught them the great Dharma — hence I am truly their father" (end of quotation). "All that I now possess belongs to my son": the direct bestowal of the family estate. Textual Commentary 6 (31) says: "Every virtue and every practice of the Great Vehicle — hence it says 'all that I possess'" (end of quotation). Further, the Tathāgata-garbha is no different in nature in the son; hence it says "belongs to my son." You should know that what the Tathāgata possesses is what the son originally possesses.

臨命終時靈山唱入涅柈時也譬前轉教皆知法門文句六三十一云追指昔日大品領教所委有廣畧般若共不共法是汝所知即汝所有故法華但明佛之知見更不廣說一切行相也文開示悟入文句四十三約四意消之一約四位住行向地二約四智道慧道種慧一切智一切種智即上圓位能契之智也三約四門四約觀心妙樂云約智約位唯聖方開約觀約門乃通名字不妨高位不棄眾生文又二帋云若作餘釋為令之說徒施佛之知見安在文

Ru-Yi Translation

"At the time of approaching death": this is the time when, on the Vulture Peak, he announced his entry into nirvāṇa. "Earlier, in transferring the teaching, they all came to know the Dharma-gates": Textual Commentary 6 (31) says: "This reaches back to point to the days of old, the Larger Prajñā, when he was made to oversee the extensive and abbreviated forms of the Prajñā, the dharmas shared and unshared — 'this you already know' means precisely 'this you already possess' — and hence the Lotus makes clear only the knowledge and vision of the buddhas, without expounding at any greater length every aspect of practice" (end of quotation). "Opens, shows, awakens, enters": Textual Commentary 4 (13) interprets it by four senses: first, in terms of the four stages (the abidings, practices, dedications, and grounds); second, in terms of the four wisdoms (path-wisdom, path-kinds wisdom, the wisdom that knows all things, and the wisdom knowing all modes), which are the wisdoms that accord [with principle] in the perfect stages above; third, in terms of the four gates; fourth, in terms of contemplating the mind. Miaole says: "In terms of wisdom and of stage, only sages open [it]; in terms of contemplation and of gate, it extends to [identity in] name, without hindering the high stages and without abandoning sentient beings" (end of quotation). Again, two leaves later it says: "If one makes some other explanation, the teaching 'in order to cause' is given in vain; where then is the knowledge and vision of the buddhas?" (end of quotation).

Ru-Yi Translation

"The knowledge and vision of the buddhas": the Buddha's knowledge is the wisdom knowing all modes, complete with the three wisdoms; the Buddha's vision is the buddha-eye, complete with the five eyes. It is also called true knowledge and vision. If it is opened in the general way, those not present at the seat are taught it at second hand; some beyond the realms also get to hear it; some, after the Buddha's extinction, are urged and pressed to believe; and so on down to the far future, the coarse wisdom of the four evil [destinies] and the worldly wisdom of humans and gods: if these were not opened, the knowledge and vision of the buddhas would be forever buried in the four destinies and long sunk among humans and gods. If it is opened specifically, then those at the seat gain benefit and the capacities present there awaken wondrously. "Confers predictions of buddhahood": in conferring a prediction, the sage's words spoken and given are called "conferring," and the fruit promised to the mind is called "prediction." Predictions in the general way are like those at the beginning of the "Dharma Teacher" chapter: all those of the eight classes, the fourfold assembly, and the three vehicles who at the seat hear a single verse from the Buddha are given predictions that they will attain bodhi, and so on, so that even those who hear a single verse after his extinction are also given predictions. Specific predictions are like the trace gate's specific conferring of predictions of the response-body and the origin gate's conferring of predictions of the Dharma-body. Further, [predictions] are given in general to the seven hundred, and predictions specifying the eon, the land, the name, and so on are given specifically to the five hundred. Miaole 4 (26) says: "That the two vehicles are for now given predictions of the eight phases is to make them form connections of the pure land with beings. Bodhisattvas have already benefited beings through many eons, liberating them as they ripen, and do not need the shallow and near prediction of the eight phases. The two vehicles are not so; therefore they need it" (end of quotation).

The Nirvāṇa Sūtra: Gleaning and Supporting the Precepts

Chinese Source

次說大涅槃者有二義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, as for teaching the Great Nirvāṇa, there are two meanings.

Chinese Source

佛出淨土不說涅柈即以法華為後教後味如燈明迦葉等今佛熟前番人以法華為醍醐更熟後番人重將般若淘汰方入涅柈復以涅柈為後教後味。

Ru-Yi Translation

When a buddha appears in a pure land, he does not teach the Nirvāṇa, but takes the Lotus as the last teaching and the last flavor, as with [the buddhas] Candrasūryapradīpa, Kāśyapa, and others. The present Buddha ripened the earlier group of people, taking the Lotus as ghee; further ripening the later group of people, he again used the Prajñā to wash and sift [them], and only then entered the Nirvāṇa, again taking the Nirvāṇa as the last teaching and the last flavor.

Chinese Source

一為未熟者更說四教具談佛性令具真常入大涅槃故名捃拾教二為末代鈍根於佛法中起斷滅見夭傷慧命亡失法身設三種權扶一圓實故名扶律談常教。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: First, for those not yet ripe he again teaches the four teachings, discusses buddha-nature in full, makes them complete with the true and eternal, and has them enter great nirvāṇa; hence it is called the gleaning teaching. Second, for the dull faculties of the final age, who give rise to views of annihilation within the Buddhadharma, cut short the life of wisdom, and lose the Dharma-body, he sets up the three kinds of provisional to support the one perfect real; hence it is called the teaching that supports the precepts and discusses the eternal.

Chinese Source

一為未熟者即五千起去人天被移者更說四教法華廢竟今經復用故云更說而具追說追泯兩種四教妙玄二廿二云涅槃聖行品追分別眾經故具說四種四諦施權德王品追泯眾經俱寂四種四諦文開權即四不可說也釋籤三十三云追者退也却更分別前諸味也泯者會也自法華已前諸經皆泯此意則順法華部也至大經中更分別者為被末代故大經中具斯二說文具談佛性令具真常涅槃經首廣開常宗令一切眾生皆知常住佛性入祕密藏止觀云涅槃寄滅談常輔行云寄應迹滅度談法身圓常

Ru-Yi Translation

"First, for those not yet ripe": these are the five thousand who rose and left, and the humans and gods who were moved away. "He again teaches the four teachings": [the four teachings] had been set aside in the Lotus, and this sūtra uses them again; hence it says "again teaches." And it contains both kinds of four teachings, retrospective teaching and retrospective dissolution. Wondrous Profound [Meaning] 2 (22) says: "The 'Noble Practice' chapter of the Nirvāṇa retrospectively distinguishes the various sūtras, and so teaches the four kinds of four truths in full, setting up the provisional; the 'Virtuous King' chapter retrospectively dissolves the various sūtras, and the four kinds of four truths are all stilled" (end of quotation). Opening the provisional is the four "cannot be spoken." Annotations 3 (13) says: "'Retrospective' (追) means going back: it distinguishes the earlier flavors once more. 'Dissolving' (泯) means gathering: all the sūtras before the Lotus are dissolved. This meaning accords with the Lotus division. That it distinguishes them once more in the Great Sūtra is in order to address the final age; hence the Great Sūtra contains both these teachings" (end of quotation). "Discusses buddha-nature in full, makes them complete with the true and eternal": at the head of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra the doctrine of the eternal is broadly opened, causing all sentient beings to know the constantly abiding buddha-nature and enter the secret treasury. Calming and Contemplation says: "The Nirvāṇa relies on extinction to discuss the eternal." Ru-Yi Translation

Calming and Contemplation 4 (2) says: "As for the natural precepts, whether or not one has received them, breaking them is an offense; whether or not one has received them, keeping them is good. If one has received the precepts, keeping them produces merit and breaking them incurs an offense; if one has not received them, there is no merit, and if one has not received them, breaking them is no offense" (end of quotation). Fuxing 4A (3) says: "'Natural' means the old precepts: they do not wait for the Buddha's establishing them; their nature is good or evil; hence they are called natural." It also says: "Of the five precepts, four are natural and one is prohibitive; hence the Kośa says, 'Among the prohibitive, only abstaining from alcohol is [included], to protect the other disciplines.' Once the [Buddha's] rule is considered, an offense against the rule is added on top of the natural [offense]" (end of quotation). The natural precepts were also used by the wheel-turning kings; the prohibitive precepts were established by the Tathāgata. The difference between the five precepts and the ten wholesome deeds is one of opening and combining: the three of body can be seen; not speaking falsely includes the four kinds of action of speech; [abstaining from] alcohol guards the ground of mind and so includes the three of mind.
