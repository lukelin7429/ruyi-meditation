The Perfect Stages, the Six Identities, and the Expedients and Ten Modes of Contemplation

This final part covers the end of fascicle nine and all of fascicle ten. Mengrun explains why perfect practitioners lose the coarse delusions first, comparing the smelting of iron with the firing of gold, and tells how the quotation from Yongjia led Lord Qian of Wuyue to seek the lost Tiantai texts from Goryeo, which is how Chegwan came to China. He then annotates the perfect teaching's first abiding (the three virtues "neither vertical nor horizontal," the eight aspects, the arousing of the mind and the ultimate as not two), the forty-two stages measured against the distinct teaching, and the buddha of wondrous awakening seated on empty space. The six identities are glossed mainly from Calming and Contemplation, The Wondrous Purport, and the Fuxing. The second half follows the root text through the twenty-five expedients, with long citations from Calming and Contemplation 4 on the precepts, robes, food, dwelling places, involvements, good friends, the five desires and hindrances, and the regulation of body, breath, and mind, and then through the ten modes of contemplation, matched to the cart of the Lotus parable. A brief comment on Chegwan's closing lines ends the work.

Ru-Yi Translation

This makes clear the meaning of perfect severance. Fuxing 6A (9) says: "From the beginning the three truths are perfectly cultivated and have nothing whatever to do with the sequential meaning; this discusses that where coarse delusions are naturally severed, it is equal to the sequential" (end of quotation). Further, 7B (24) says: "The five grades can already perfectly subdue the five abodes; how should they at this stage separately sever views and habituated thought? It is just that with perfect cultivation coarse delusions are severed first, like smelting iron, where the coarse dross is removed first" (end of quotation). Notes on the Separately Circulated Profound Meaning, lower [fascicle] (6), says: "The perfect is compared to smelting iron to make a vessel; the distinct is compared to firing gold to make a vessel. Smelting means melting and casting: the plain material melts all at once, and the coarse dross naturally falls away first. Firing means forging and refining: the substance of the thing is still hard, so one must first remove the coarse dust and then melt the gold to remove the fine dross. The perfect contemplation at once exhausts the dharma-realm, without separately contemplating the two truths first, [yet] the two delusions naturally fall away first. The distinct contemplation reveals the middle sequentially, intentionally contemplating the two truths first; hence it causes the two delusions to be removed first" (end of quotation). Pointing to the Essentials, lower [fascicle] (5), says: "The perfect person from beginning to end uses the absolute wisdom, in which all dharmas vanish at once; even principle and fruit vanish — how could delusion have a sequence? Only because the power of this wisdom is slight or manifest, it becomes distant or close; because of distance and closeness, delusions fall away earlier or later, and are called thick or thin delusion. When wisdom is distant, delusion is thick; when wisdom is close, delusion is thin; it grows ever clearer. This is distinguishing delusion in terms of wisdom" (end of quotation). An earlier master (says): "In cultivating contemplation, delusion and wisdom are one suchness; as the work is accomplished, delusions fall away earlier or later."

永嘉大師云同除四住此處為齊若伏無明三藏則劣即此位也解曰四住者只是見思謂見為一名見一切處住地思惑分三一欲愛住地欲界九品思二色愛住地色界四地各九品思三無色愛住地無色界四地各九品思此之四住三藏佛與六根清淨人同斷故言同除四住也言若伏無明三藏則劣者無明即界外障中道之別惑三藏教止論界內通惑無明名字尚不能知况復伏斷故言三藏則劣也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Great Master Yongjia says: "Alike removing the four abodes, at this point they are equal; but in subduing ignorance, the Tripiṭaka falls short." This is this stage. Explanation: The four abodes are just views and habituated thought. Views count as one, called the abode-ground of views everywhere. The delusion of habituated thought is divided into three: first, the abode-ground of desire-craving, the nine grades of habituated thought of the desire realm; second, the abode-ground of form-craving, the nine grades of habituated thought in each of the four grounds of the form realm; third, the abode-ground of formless-craving, the nine grades of habituated thought in each of the four grounds of the formless realm. These four abodes are severed alike by the Tripiṭaka buddha and by the person of the purified six faculties; hence it says "alike removing the four abodes." As for "but in subduing ignorance, the Tripiṭaka falls short": ignorance is the particular delusion beyond the realms that obstructs the middle way; the Tripiṭaka teaching discusses only the common delusion within the realms and cannot even know the name of ignorance — how much less subdue or sever it? Hence it says "the Tripiṭaka falls short."

Ru-Yi Translation

The Yongjia Collection says: "Yet the buddha of the Tripiṭaka, compared with the stage of the purification of the six faculties, is in part equal and in part inferior: alike removing the four abodes, at this point they are equal; but in subduing ignorance, the Tripiṭaka falls short. The two vehicles can be known [from this]." This is originally a passage from [the section on] the wondrousness of stages in the Wondrous Profound [Meaning], quoted in the Yongjia Collection. It has been handed down that at the end of the Tang and in the Five Dynasties the Tiantai teaching sank into obscurity. Because Lord Qian, reading the Yongjia Collection, came to this point and did not understand it, he asked National Master [De]shao. The National Master pointed it out as words from the Tiantai teaching and [said] one should ask Dharma Master Yiji of Luoxi. The master memorialized that [these texts] flourished in Haedong, and so they were sought from Goryeo. Thereupon Master Chegwan brought the corpus of the teaching as envoy, and [the teaching] at last revived. That it is now called "Yongjia" has its reason. Annotations 6 (4) says: "'In part equal and in part inferior': where delusion is exhausted they are equal; in contemplative practice and the hearing of the teaching, the [Tripiṭaka] is inferior. Further, measuring it against the stage of buddha is because this accords with the path of the teaching" (end of quotation).

Chinese Source

次從八信至十信斷界內外塵沙惑盡假觀現前見俗諦理開法眼成道種智行四百由旬與別教八九十住及行向位齊行不退也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, from the eighth faith to the tenth faith, one severs the delusion of dust-and-sand within and beyond the realms completely; the contemplation of the provisional appears, one sees the principle of the conventional truth, opens the Dharma-eye, accomplishes the wisdom of the paths, and travels four hundred yojanas; equal to the distinct teaching's eighth, ninth, and tenth abidings and the stages of the practices and dedications; [this is] non-regression in practice.

Chinese Source

雖約位斷證格量似齊圓別即離不可一混又此六根明下根出假功逾十向此是相似圓融三諦不同次第出假之位又五品明中根出假五品之初為上根亦約觀行論坐道塲度眾生等又輔行五上云以初住為真出假位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Though in terms of the stages of severing and realizing they are measured as seemingly equal, the perfect and the distinct, identical and separate, cannot be mixed into one. Further, these six faculties make clear the going out into the provisional of those of inferior faculties, whose merit exceeds the ten dedications: this is the resembling perfect fusion of the three truths, unlike the stage of sequential going out into the provisional. Further, the five grades make clear the going out into the provisional of those of middling faculties, and the beginning of the five grades is for those of superior faculties; this too is discussed in terms of contemplative practice — sitting at the site of awakening, delivering sentient beings, and so on. Further, Fuxing 5A says that the first abiding is taken as the stage of truly going out into the provisional.

The Perfect Teaching: The First Abiding

Chinese Source

次入初住斷一品無明證一分三德謂解脫般若法身此之三德不縱不橫如世伊三點若天主三目現身百界八相成道廣濟群生。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next one enters the first abiding. One severs one grade of ignorance and realizes one portion of the three virtues, namely liberation, prajñā, and the Dharma-body. These three virtues are neither vertical nor horizontal, like the three dots of the Siddhaṃ letter i in the world, or like the three eyes of the Lord of Heaven. One manifests bodies in a hundred realms, accomplishes the path through the eight aspects, and widely saves the multitude of beings.

Ru-Yi Translation

This makes clear severing delusion, realizing principle, and the whole substance giving rise to function. The sequence of the three virtues is originally Dharma-body, prajñā, liberation; now it follows the order in which, at the first abiding, the minds of the conditioning, revealing, and correct [causes] unfold. "Neither vertical nor horizontal": it differs from the distinct teaching, which is either vertical or horizontal. Annotations 6 (20) says: "Though one dot is above, it is not like the vertical of the water-dots; the three virtues are likewise: though the Dharma-body is originally possessed, it is not like the distinct teaching, where it is covered by delusion. Though two dots are below, it is not like the horizontal of the blazing fire; the three virtues are likewise: because (in the Annotations it is written 'though'; The Wondrous Purport cites it as 'because,' which is correct) the two virtues are accomplished by cultivation, it is not like the distinct person's principle-substance, fully possessed but not including one another." As The Wondrous Purport says: "Though the three are inherent in the nature, conditioning and revealing are cultivation; though the two are cultivation, they are not just now existing. If the two were not cultivation, the three dharmas would be horizontal; if the two were not nature, the three dharmas would be vertical." The three dots and the three eyes come from the "Lamentation" chapter of the Great Sūtra. In the West there are a new and an old i: the old i is like the horizontal of [the character] 川, "running fire," and the vertical of the water-dots; the new i is like the cursive form of the character 下 in this land, its fine strokes linked, neither vertical nor horizontal. Maheśvara has three eyes and eight arms. "The eight aspects": the Avataṃsaka says, "Or [beings] see him enter the womb," and so on, each saying "or"; within each aspect there are all eight aspects; hence the text [says so].

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: The Avataṃsaka Sūtra says: "At the time of first arousing the mind one attains correct awakening; the body of wisdom one has is not awakened through another. The pure, wondrous Dharma-body, tranquil and clear, responds to everything." Explanation: "First arousing the mind" is the name of the first abiding. "Attains correct awakening" means becoming a buddha of the eight aspects; this is the fruit realized in part, which is the true cause of this teaching. To say that one becomes [the buddha of] wondrous awakening is a great error. If it were so, all the stages from the second abiding on would be set up in vain. If one says it is a restatement, the Buddha would be guilty of tedious repetition. Though there are statements that each stage includes all stages, and it is also said that the arousing of the mind and the ultimate are not two, one must know the grounds of this inclusion and recognize in detail the purport of nonduality. The dragon girl attaining correct awakening at once, and the śrāvakas receiving predictions of future buddhahood, are all marks of attaining buddhahood at this stage. "Body of wisdom" is the virtue of prajñā, the nature of the revealing cause unfolded; "wondrous Dharma-body" is the virtue of the Dharma-body, the nature of the correct cause unfolded; "responds to everything" is the virtue of liberation, the nature of the conditioning cause unfolded. These three bodies are unfolded from what is originally possessed; hence it says "not awakened through another." The contemplation of the middle appears; one opens the buddha-eye, accomplishes the wisdom knowing all modes, travels five hundred yojanas, and reaches the place of treasure. One first dwells in the land of true reward free of obstruction: [this is] the stage of non-regression in mindfulness.

Ru-Yi Translation

From here [the text] cites the sūtra to explain it, and then rebukes others' errors. "Though it says each includes all stages, one must know the grounds of this inclusion": it is because principle possesses them. "Though it says the arousing of the mind and the ultimate are not different, recognize in detail the purport of nonduality": the purport lies in identity, the principle of identity and possession. Though it is so, how could the phenomenal stages of shallow and deep not be divided? Because of identity, beginning and end are not two; because of six, beginning and end are not confused. "Each stage includes all stages": as in the Larger [Prajñā], the first is a and the last ḍha, with forty letters in between; the first, the letter-gate a, possesses the forty-two letters, and the last, the letter-gate ḍha, likewise. Also, as in the Avataṃsaka, one ground possesses the merits of all the grounds. The Great Sūtra says: "The arousing of the mind and the ultimate, the two are not different; of these two minds the former mind is harder." "The arousing of the mind" is the first abiding; "the ultimate" is wondrous awakening. The text on the dragon girl's attaining buddhahood speaks from the provisional, to prove that in the perfect sūtra buddhahood is attained swiftly. If the real practice were not swift, the provisional practice would be set up in vain; the provisional and the real being equal in meaning, the principle is not in vain — as in Miaole 8 (35, and so on). The śrāvakas are given predictions of eon, land, and name, forming connections with beings (end of quotation).

Ru-Yi Translation

"Unfolded from what is originally possessed": The Wondrous Purport, upper [fascicle] (22), says: "Now the three dharmas unfolded at the first abiding are all inherent in the nature; hence when they unfold they unfold together. Wisdom realizes the Dharma, and from the Dharma arises the response — this is not one time; the three bodies being attained at once, it is not before and after. Neither vertical nor horizontal is seen here again. From the beginning one perfectly cultivates the threefold contemplation in a single mind; now the three wisdoms are perfectly attained in a single mind. With this wisdom one realizes the Dharma-body; the nature of wisdom being form, three and one fused in substance, it is called the wondrous form-body. This body is tranquil and still like a mirror without feeling; when forms face it, images arise, without the slightest gap between mountain and hair — called 'responds to everything.' The three bodies and three virtues are in substance apart from vertical and horizontal" (end of quotation). "The contemplation of the middle appears": since the three causes unfold, the three wisdoms should be perfectly bright and the king's eye clearly illuminating. Now it says only "buddha-eye" and "wisdom knowing all modes," because the middle necessarily illuminates both, the three wisdoms are complete, and the four eyes enter the buddha-eye and are all called the buddha-eye. Fuxing 3A (25) says: "As a river entering the sea loses its original name of river. Why? The flesh and heavenly eyes are contaminated causes and conditions; the wisdom and Dharma eyes have habituation not yet exhausted; hence they relinquish their original positions and enter the buddha-eye" (end of quotation).

The Perfect Teaching: From the Second Abiding to Equal Awakening

Chinese Source

次從二住至十住各斷一品無明增一分中道與別教十地齊次入初行斷一品無明與別教等覺齊次入二行與別教妙覺齊從三行已去別教之人尚不知名字何况伏斷以別教但破十二品無明故故以我家之真因為汝家之極果只緣教彌權位彌高教彌實位彌下譬如邊方未靜借職則高定爵論勳其位實下故權教雖稱妙覺但是實教中第二行也次從三行已去至十地各斷一品無明增一分中道即斷四十品惑也更破一品無明入等覺位此是一生補處。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, from the second abiding to the tenth abiding, each severs one grade of ignorance and increases one portion of the middle way, equal to the distinct teaching's ten grounds. Next one enters the first practice, severs one grade of ignorance, and is equal to the distinct teaching's equal enlightenment. Next one enters the second practice, equal to the distinct teaching's wondrous awakening. From the third practice on, people of the distinct teaching do not even know the names, how much less subdue or sever [those delusions]? For the distinct teaching breaks only twelve grades of ignorance. Hence the true cause of our house is taken as the ultimate fruit of your house. It is simply that the more provisional the teaching, the higher the stage; the more real the teaching, the lower the stage. It is like a frontier not yet pacified, where borrowed offices are high; when ranks are fixed and merit assessed, the position is in fact low. Hence, though the provisional teaching speaks of "wondrous awakening," it is only the second practice in the real teaching. Next, from the third practice up to the tenth ground, each severs one grade of ignorance and increases one portion of the middle way; this is severing forty grades of delusion. Breaking one more grade of ignorance, one enters the stage of equal enlightenment; this is "one more birth to buddhahood."
