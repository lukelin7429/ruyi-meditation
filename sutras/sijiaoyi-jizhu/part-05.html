The Twelve Links, the Tripiṭaka Bodhisattva and Buddha, the Shared Teaching, and the Distinct Teaching's First Stages

This part covers the end of fascicle seven and the first half of fascicle eight. Mengrun finishes the twelve links, giving the ages of life that the Fuxing assigns to each link, and explains how the links map onto the four truths and how the pratyekabuddha's "residual habituation" differs from the śrāvaka's. For the Tripiṭaka bodhisattva he argues at length that this bodhisattva subdues but does not sever delusion, reconciling the Āgamas and Vibhāṣā, which teach this, with the Great Treatise, which rejects it. He then annotates the four universal vows, the three asaṃkhyeya eons and their buddhas (with the story of the offering to Dīpaṃkara), the hundred eons of planting the marks, the stories behind the fulfillment of the six pāramitās, and the eight phases of the Tripiṭaka buddha. Fascicle eight opens the shared teaching: the meanings of "shared," the ten grounds from dry wisdom to the buddha ground, the simile of three animals crossing a river, and the grafting of sharp bodhisattvas by the distinct and perfect teachings, including the readings of the character 被. The part ends with the opening of the distinct teaching: its eight kinds of distinctness, the two paths of teaching and realization borrowed from the Daśabhūmika masters, the ten faiths, and the ten abidings.

The Twelve Links: From the Six Entries to Aging-and-Death

Chinese Source

從名色後至第六七日名髮毛爪齒位七七日名具根位五根圓滿故六根成者輔行四下（廿一）云十九七日諸根具足（文）此胎中總有名色六入皆胎中位故輔行（八）云三十八箇七日皆胎中位。

Ru-Yi Translation

From [the stage of] name-and-form on, up to the sixth seven-day period, is called the stage of hair, nails, and teeth; the seventh seven-day period is called the stage of complete faculties, because the five faculties are complete. "The six faculties are formed": Fuxing 4B (21) says: "At the nineteenth seven-day period the faculties are complete" (end of quotation). In this womb there are in all name-and-form and the six entries, all being stages in the womb; hence the Fuxing (8) says: "The thirty-eight seven-day periods are all stages in the womb."

Chinese Source

六觸（出胎）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: sixth, contact (leaving the womb);

Chinese Source

出胎已後至三四嵗由根對塵情塵識合然於違順中庸差別境上未能了知生苦樂捨是名為觸。

Ru-Yi Translation

After leaving the womb, up to three or four years of age, the faculties face the objects, and feeling, object, and consciousness come together; yet regarding the different objects, adverse, agreeable, and neutral, one cannot yet discern them and give rise to suffering, pleasure, or equanimity; this is called contact.

Chinese Source

七受（領納前境好惡等事從識至受名現在五果）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: seventh, feeling (receiving such things as the good and bad of the objects before one; from consciousness to feeling are called the five present effects);

Chinese Source

從五六嵗至十三嵗因六塵觸六根即領納前境於三受違順中庸境上已能了別然未能起[婬-壬+(工/山)]貪之心故名受也。

Ru-Yi Translation

From five or six years to thirteen years, as the six objects contact the six faculties, one receives the objects before one, and regarding the three feelings and the adverse, agreeable, and neutral objects one can already discern them, yet one cannot yet give rise to a mind of lustful greed; hence it is called feeling.

Chinese Source

八愛（愛色男女金銀錢物等事）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: eighth, craving (craving for such things as forms, men and women, gold, silver, money, and goods);

Chinese Source

從十四五嵗至十八九嵗貪於種種勝妙資具及[婬-壬+(工/山)]欲等境然猶未能廣遍追求不名為取皆是愛支所攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

From fourteen or fifteen to eighteen or nineteen years, one is greedy for all kinds of excellent and wondrous necessities and for objects such as lustful desire, yet one cannot yet seek them widely everywhere; this is not called grasping and is all included in the link of craving.

Chinese Source

九取（凡見一切境皆生取着心此二未來因皆属煩惱如過去無明）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: ninth, grasping (whenever one sees any object, a mind of grasping and attachment arises; these two are causes for the future, both belonging to affliction, like ignorance in the past);

Chinese Source

即從二十嵗已後貪欲轉盛於五塵境四方馳求名之為取。

Ru-Yi Translation

From the twentieth year on, greed and desire grow stronger, and one runs about in the four directions seeking the objects of the five senses; this is called grasping.

Chinese Source

十有（業已成就是未來因属業道如過去行）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: tenth, existence (the karma is already accomplished, a cause for the future, belonging to the path of karma, like formations in the past);

Chinese Source

體即是業為馳求諸境起善惡業積集牽引當生三有果故名為有注云是未來因者雖属現在却為未來苦果之因也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Its substance is karma: running about seeking objects, one gives rise to wholesome and unwholesome karma, which accumulates and draws on the effect of the three existences in the life to come; hence it is called existence. The note says "a cause for the future": though it belongs to the present, it is the cause of future suffering effects.

Chinese Source

十一生（未來受生事）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: eleventh, birth (the matter of receiving birth in the future);

Chinese Source

從有還受後世五眾之身是名生所謂四生六道中受生也。

Ru-Yi Translation

From existence one again receives a body of the five aggregates in a later life; this is called birth, namely receiving birth among the four modes of birth and the six destinies.

Chinese Source

十二老死。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: twelfth, aging-and-death.

Chinese Source

從生五眾之身熟壞是名老死。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ripening and decay of the body of the five aggregates that has been born is called aging-and-death.

Chinese Source

此是所滅之境。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: This is the object to be extinguished;

Chinese Source

以能滅之觀順推此境故此十二即所滅境也不立病支者妙玄二（十六）云問何不說病為支荅一切時一切處𥁞有者立支自有人從生無病如薄拘羅生來不識頭痛况餘病是故不立問憂悲是支否荅非也以終顯始耳如老死必憂悲（文）釋籤三三云問愛取何別荅愛增廣名取（文）然上一徃似論三世在支佛逆順兩緣百千萬世觀因緣等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because the contemplation that extinguishes investigates this object in forward order, these twelve are the object to be extinguished. As for not setting up a link of sickness, Wondrous Profound [Meaning] 2 (16) says: "Question: Why is sickness not spoken of as a link? Answer: What exists at all times and in all places is set up as a link. There are people who from birth have no sickness, like Vakula, who from birth never knew a headache, let alone other sicknesses; therefore it is not set up. Question: Are sorrow and grief links? Answer: No; they only use the end to show the beginning, as aging-and-death is necessarily [accompanied by] sorrow and grief" (end of quotation). Annotations 3 (3) says: "Question: How do craving and grasping differ? Answer: Craving increased and widened is called grasping" (end of quotation). Yet the above at first glance seems to discuss the three times; the pratyekabuddha [contemplates] in both reverse and forward order the dependent origination of hundreds, thousands, and myriads of lifetimes, and so on.

Chinese Source

與前四諦開合之異耳云何開合謂無明行愛取有此之五支合為集諦餘七支為苦諦也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: it differs from the preceding four truths only in opening and combining. How do they open and combine? Ignorance, formations, craving, grasping, and existence — these five links combine to make the truth of accumulation; the other seven links make the truth of suffering.

Chinese Source

止觀一（十二）云總說名四諦別說名十二因緣苦是識名色六入觸受生老死七支集是無名行愛取有五支道是對治因緣方便滅是無明滅乃至老死滅（文）輔行一下（十）云離苦集為十二支觀因緣智以為道諦十二支滅以為滅諦（文）文句七（廿一）云十二因緣者還是別相細觀四諦耳約苦集即有無明老死約道滅即有無明滅乃至老死滅也（文）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Calming and Contemplation 1 (12) says: "Spoken of generally they are called the four truths; spoken of specifically they are called the twelve links of dependent origination. Suffering is the seven links of consciousness, name-and-form, the six entries, contact, feeling, birth, and aging-and-death; accumulation is the five links of ignorance, formations, craving, grasping, and existence; the path is the skillful means that remedy dependent origination; cessation is the extinction of ignorance, and so on up to the extinction of aging-and-death" (end of quotation). Fuxing 1B (10) says: "Apart from suffering and accumulation, which make the twelve links, the wisdom that contemplates dependent origination is taken as the truth of the path, and the extinction of the twelve links as the truth of cessation" (end of quotation). Textual Commentary 7 (21) says: "The twelve links of dependent origination are still just a specific and detailed contemplation of the four truths. In terms of suffering and accumulation there are ignorance [up to] aging-and-death; in terms of the path and cessation there is the extinction of ignorance up to the extinction of aging-and-death" (end of quotation).

The Pratyekabuddha's Contemplation and the Residual Habituation

Chinese Source

既名異義同何故重說為機宜不同故緣覺之人先觀集諦所謂無明緣行行緣識乃至生緣老死此則生起若滅觀者無明滅則行滅乃至生滅則老死滅因觀十二因緣覺真諦理故言緣覺。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Since the names differ but the meaning is the same, why is it taught again? Because capacities and what suits them differ. The person awakened through conditions first contemplates the truth of accumulation, namely, "ignorance conditions formations, formations condition consciousness," up to "birth conditions aging-and-death": this is [the order of] arising. In the contemplation of extinction: "when ignorance is extinguished, formations are extinguished," up to "when birth is extinguished, aging-and-death is extinguished." Because by contemplating the twelve links of dependent origination he awakens to the principle of the truth of the real, he is called "awakened through conditions."

Chinese Source

聲聞總觀四諦緣覺別觀十二因緣緣覺之人等者此觀十二因緣生若滅觀者等此觀十二因緣滅諸文更有逆順等異如阿含明始無明終老死名順觀始老死終無明名逆觀又止觀禪境以有支在初推因知果也釋籤先從受支起觀此推果知因也（此如輔行又四念處約十二支觀愛觀見明推尋觀破之義也文）因觀十二等以觀因緣生滅覺悟真空而結名也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The śrāvaka contemplates the four truths generally; the one awakened through conditions contemplates the twelve links of dependent origination specifically. "The person awakened through conditions" and so on: this contemplates the arising of the twelve links. "In the contemplation of extinction" and so on: this contemplates the extinction of the twelve links. The texts further have the differences of reverse and forward and so on: as the Āgamas make clear, beginning with ignorance and ending with aging-and-death is called the forward contemplation, and beginning with aging-and-death and ending with ignorance is called the reverse contemplation. Further, the dhyāna objects in Calming and Contemplation put the link of existence first, inferring the effect from the cause; the Annotations begins the contemplation from the link of feeling, which infers the cause from the effect (this is as in the Fuxing. Further, the Four Foundations of Mindfulness, in terms of the twelve links, contemplates craving and contemplates views, making clear the meaning of investigating, contemplating, and breaking them (end of quotation)). "By contemplating the twelve," and so on: by contemplating the arising and extinction of dependent origination he awakens to true emptiness, and [from this] the name is concluded.

Chinese Source

言獨覺者出無佛世獨宿孤峯觀物變易自覺無生故名獨覺。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: As for "solitary awakened one": appearing in an age without a buddha, dwelling alone on a solitary peak, contemplating the changes of things, he awakens by himself to non-arising; hence he is called the solitary awakened one.

Chinese Source

觀外因緣無師自悟未必一向獨宿孤峯如國王花飛釧動等（如釋籤七廿一云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Contemplating outer causes and conditions and awakening by himself without a teacher, he does not necessarily always dwell alone on a solitary peak: for example, the king [who awakened on seeing] flowers fly and bracelets move, and so on (as Annotations 7 (21) says).

Chinese Source

兩名不同行位無別此人斷三界見思與聲聞同更侵習氣故居聲聞上。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: The two names differ, but practice and stage are not distinct. This person severs the views and habituated thought of the three realms, the same as the śrāvaka, and further encroaches on the residual habituation; hence he ranks above the śrāvaka.

Chinese Source

雖緣覺獨覺之異而同修因緣之行同證侵習之果習氣者慣習氣分如噐中香其香雖𥁞餘氣尚存統論諸文有三家二即今是見思家習耳大經云我衣我鉢見習也舍利弗嗔畢陵伽慢思習也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Though there is the difference between the one awakened through conditions and the solitary awakened, they alike cultivate the practice of dependent origination and alike realize the fruit of encroaching on the habituation. "Residual habituation" (習氣) is the share of breath [left by] habituation, like the scent in a vessel: though the incense is gone, the remaining smell still lingers. Taking the texts as a whole, there are three [kinds of] habituation; here it is the habituation of views and habituated thought. The Great Sūtra's "my robe, my bowl" is the habituation of views; Śāriputra's anger and Pilindavatsa's arrogance are the habituation of habituated thought.

The Tripiṭaka Bodhisattva Does Not Sever Delusion

Chinese Source

次明菩薩位者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, making clear the stages of the bodhisattva:

Chinese Source

菩薩具云菩提薩埵摩訶薩埵舊翻大道心眾生亦大道成眾生新譯云覺有情以上求佛道下化眾生故此菩薩於當教內亦稱大乘然此菩薩全不斷惑三祗百刦伏惑行因四教義三（六）云三藏正化二乘傍化菩薩若說菩薩斷惑受生二乘即疑若結𥁞而得受生者諸聲聞人得羅漢果將不更受生耶是故不說菩薩斷結受生也（文）

Ru-Yi Translation

"Bodhisattva" in full is bodhisattva mahāsattva. The old rendering is "a being of the great mind of the path," also "a being accomplishing the great path"; the new translation says "awakening sentient being," because above he seeks the buddha-path and below he transforms sentient beings. This bodhisattva, within his own teaching, is also called the great vehicle. Yet this bodhisattva does not sever delusion at all: for three asaṃkhyeyas and a hundred eons he subdues delusion and practices the causes. Meaning of the Four Teachings 3 (6) says: "The Tripiṭaka transforms the two vehicles principally and bodhisattvas secondarily. If it taught that bodhisattvas sever delusion and receive birth, the two vehicles would doubt: if one can receive birth after the bonds are exhausted, will not the śrāvakas who attain the fruit of arhatship receive birth again? Therefore it does not teach that bodhisattvas sever the bonds and receive birth" (end of quotation).

Chinese Source

又妙玄五（十三）云今生事善故作是說欲求佛者改惡從善（文）又四教義（三二）云雖修性念處而不斷結為生三界度眾生故（文）由教不詮中道應本故留結惑受生利物故三藏菩薩不斷惑明矣（此乃五權則有）

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, Wondrous Profound [Meaning] 5 (13) says: "Now, because it arises from phenomenal good, it speaks thus: one who wishes to seek buddhahood should turn from evil to good" (end of quotation). Further, Meaning of the Four Teachings (3.2) says: "Though he cultivates the foundations of mindfulness by nature, he does not sever the bonds, so as to be born in the three realms to deliver sentient beings" (end of quotation). Because the teaching does not express the middle way as the origin that responds, he keeps the bonds of delusion in order to receive birth and benefit beings; hence it is clear that the Tripiṭaka bodhisattva does not sever delusion (this is "when the provisional is set up, it exists").

Chinese Source

若大論云聲聞人言菩薩不斷結使乃至坐道場然後斷者是為大錯又云豈有菩薩具足三毒能集佛法（文）此盖龍𣗳申通摩訶衍義以大破小故作此說當彼鹿苑稟教之時雖謂實歷三祗百刦伏惑不斷若方等般若轉入衍中來至法華會歸一實定無始終三祗伏惑故得大論約實斥權（此乃斥權則無）

Ru-Yi Translation

The Great Treatise says: "That the śrāvakas say the bodhisattva does not sever the bonds and agents until he sits at the site of awakening and only then severs them is a great error." It also says: "How could a bodhisattva fully possessed of the three poisons gather the Buddhadharma?" (end of quotation). This is because Nāgārjuna, expounding the meaning of the shared mahāyāna, used the great to break the small, and so spoke thus. At the time when [the bodhisattva] received the teaching in the Deer Park, though it was said that he really passed through three asaṃkhyeyas and a hundred eons subduing delusion without severing it, once he has turned and entered the mahāyāna in the Vaipulya and Prajñā and come to the Lotus assembly to return to the one real, there are certainly no three asaṃkhyeyas of subduing delusion from beginning to end; hence the Great Treatise can rebuke the provisional in terms of the real (this is "when the provisional is rebuked, it does not exist").

Chinese Source

若釋迦果後權示利生不妨自歷三祗百刦故有尸棄然燈等事今之所辨且順立權義邊故約鹿苑三藏明不斷惑如法華文句六（廿七）引阿含五佛子釋更與作字名之為兒四果支佛名佛真子菩薩不斷惑子義未成（文）妙樂七（十七）云阿含至子義未成者（疊䟽引經）既阿含中亦明不斷惑菩薩而大論斥權非謂全無（此會同經論明破立意謂非但婆沙明不斷惑菩薩而阿含亦明此對婆沙而云亦也以阿含會同婆沙而判大論則知大論斥權云無非謂全無乃立權則有）

Ru-Yi Translation

As for Śākyamuni, after the fruit, provisionally displaying [practice] to benefit beings, there is no obstacle to his himself passing through three asaṃkhyeyas and a hundred eons; hence there are the events with Śikhin, Dīpaṃkara, and so on. What is discussed now follows for the moment the side of the meaning of setting up the provisional; hence, in terms of the Tripiṭaka of the Deer Park, it makes clear that he does not sever delusion. As Textual Commentary on the Lotus 6 (27) cites the Āgama: "The five [kinds of] disciples of the Buddha: he further gives them a name and calls them sons. The four fruits and the pratyekabuddha are called true sons of the Buddha; the bodhisattva does not sever delusion, so the meaning of son is not yet accomplished" (end of quotation). Miaole 7 (17) says: "'The Āgama' up to 'the meaning of son is not yet accomplished'" (repeating the Commentary's citation of the sūtra) "since the Āgama too makes clear the bodhisattva who does not sever delusion, the Great Treatise's rebuking the provisional does not mean it does not exist at all" (this reconciles sūtra and treatise to make clear the intent of breaking and setting up. It says that not only the Vibhāṣā makes clear the bodhisattva who does not sever delusion, but the Āgama also makes it clear; it says "also" in contrast to the Vibhāṣā. Using the Āgama to agree with the Vibhāṣā and to classify the Great Treatise, one knows that the Great Treatise's rebuking the provisional and saying "does not exist" does not mean it does not exist at all; that is, when the provisional is set up, it exists).

Chinese Source

論云迦旃延造者從所造論及所計者說（此由上文阿含婆沙明不斷惑菩薩則經論相符而大論斥之者盖斥其執權之人耳故曰論云迦旃延造者從所造論及所計者說即出其斥權之意盖從其所造論及所計故大論謂是迦旃延造是則大論斥其計論執權之非也）豈以會二還歸阿含法華凖舊十二年前一何可笑（此由他師不分大小菩薩之殊及昧水牛白牛之異謂法華會二乘歸菩薩故此破云豈可會二乘斷惑之人還歸阿含不斷惑菩薩若以會二乘還歸阿含法華四十餘年之後開顯之教還復凖舊十二年前阿含之教故云一何可笑）

Ru-Yi Translation

"The treatise says it was composed by Kātyāyana: this is said from the treatise he composed and from what he reckoned" (because in the passage above the Āgama and the Vibhāṣā make clear the bodhisattva who does not sever delusion, sūtra and treatise agree, and the Great Treatise's rebuking it is a rebuke of the person who clings to the provisional. Hence it says, "the treatise says it was composed by Kātyāyana: this is said from the treatise he composed and from what he reckoned," which brings out its intent of rebuking the provisional: because, from the treatise he composed and from what he reckoned, the Great Treatise says it was composed by Kātyāyana; thus the Great Treatise rebukes the error of reckoning by that treatise and clinging to the provisional). "How could gathering the two return [them] to the Āgama? Taking the Lotus as the same as the old [teaching] of twelve years before — how laughable!" (This is because other masters did not distinguish the difference between small and great bodhisattvas and were unclear about the difference between the water buffalo and the white ox, saying that the Lotus gathers the two vehicles and returns them to the bodhisattva. Hence this breaks it, saying: how could the persons of the two vehicles who sever delusion be gathered and returned to the bodhisattva of the Āgama who does not sever delusion? If gathering the two vehicles returned them to the Āgama, then the Lotus, the teaching of opening and revealing after more than forty years, would revert to being the same as the old Āgama teaching of twelve years before; hence it says, "how laughable!")

Chinese Source

止觀三（十六）云煩惱脂消者名伏為消也故別行（下三）云但伏惑不斷如無脂肥羊取世智為般若即此悉也又俱舍婆沙意云下八地惑初修禪時先已斷竟此有漏斷亦是伏義（如輔行三下會釋云云）然不斷見思還斷塵沙否荅須知三祗百刦亦但伏而不斷故至𣗳王下斷見思時於塵沙法上證四真諦方斷塵沙也若止觀三（十六）云得法眼照俗諦（文）得相似法眼有漏智照耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Calming and Contemplation 3 (16) says: "The fat of affliction melts": subduing is called melting. You should know that though the Daśabhūmika Treatise has four kinds, the Profound [Meaning] text, borrowing them to prove the provisional and real divisions, makes only two intents, and the Fuxing, borrowing them, makes only three meanings. Why? Because in this teaching practice is divided into teaching and realization, while what is taught is only the path of the teaching; thus the expressing teaching is provisional from beginning to end, while for the capacities addressed, before the grounds is provisional and at the first ground the real is realized. The old [view], in borrowing to explain the gate of the distinct, also set up four kinds. But taking the path of realization as taught: if it is provisional, it contradicts the meaning of "the teaching provisional and realization real" and of borrowing to prove the provisional and real divisions; if it is real, it goes against the text "there is the teaching but no persons." Moreover, the Daśabhūmika Treatise properly expounds the ten grounds of the Avataṃsaka, and the treatise masters did not distinguish the difference of perfect and distinct, but made clear the expedient and the real only in terms of teaching and realization; as it says: "If the dharmas already realized in the ten grounds are taught, for them it is real realization"; how could one set up from them the teaching of realization of the distinct? Since it says "borrowing" realization, how could it be entirely the same?
