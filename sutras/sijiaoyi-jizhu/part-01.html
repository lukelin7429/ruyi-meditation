The Title, the Five Periods, and the Teachings of Method up to the Lotus

Mengrun (1275–1342), a Yuan-dynasty Tiantai master of the Southern Tianzhu monastery at Hangzhou, wrote these Collected Notes as a phrase-by-phrase commentary on Chegwan's Four Teachings of Tiantai. The CBETA text used here begins with fascicle two. This first part covers the opening of Chegwan's work: the title and author, Zhiyi's scheme of the Five Periods and Eight Teachings, the sudden teaching of the Avataṃsaka, the three gradual periods (Deer Park, Vaipulya, Prajñā) with their images from the parable of the destitute son, the secret and the indeterminate teachings, and the beginning of the Lotus section, where the provisional is opened to reveal the real. Mengrun explains terms and Sanskrit names, gives the sources behind Chegwan's summaries (mostly the Profound Meaning, the Textual Commentary, and Zhanran's sub-commentaries), and settles disputed points of Song-dynasty Tiantai, such as whether the Avataṃsaka has a "long-time" form, whether śrāvakas were present at its first assembly, and how the eighty thousand gods of the Deer Park belong to the explicit, indeterminate, or secret teaching. Each sentence of Chegwan's text is marked Root text and follows the English already published for the Four Teachings.

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: The Four Teachings of Tiantai

Chinese Source

天台山名也天者顛也元氣未分混而為一兩儀既判清而為天濁而為地此本俗名且依俗釋台者星名也其地分野應天三台故以名焉如輔行（一上十七）此山即大師棲身入寂之所盖以西方風俗稱名為尊此土避名為敬故以此處顯其人也復以人命家則天台為宗矣今題意在焉四教者別文明化儀化法有乎八教今但言四教者以通名立題義攝兩種蓋非化儀無以判非化法無以釋一書之旨莫越於斯教者聖人被下之言亦詮理化物為義

Ru-Yi Translation

"Tiantai" is the name of a mountain. Tian (天, heaven) means "the top" (顛). Before the primal breath divided, it was mixed into one; when the two modes had separated, the clear became heaven and the turbid became earth. This is originally a secular name, and for now it is explained in the secular way. Tai (台) is the name of stars: the region of this land corresponds to the Three Tai stars of heaven, hence the name, as in the Fuxing (1A.17). This mountain is the place where the Great Master dwelt and entered stillness. For in the customs of the West, calling by name is honor; in this land, avoiding the name is reverence; therefore the place is used to make the person known. Further, when the house is named after the person, Tiantai becomes [the name of] the school. The intent of the present title lies here. "Four Teachings": the text further on makes clear that method and content of transformation together make eight teachings; that it now says only "four teachings" is because the title is set up with a common name whose meaning embraces both kinds. For without method there is nothing to classify by, and without content there is nothing to explain by; the purport of the whole book does not go beyond this. "Teaching" is the words with which the sage addresses those below; its meaning is also to express principle and transform beings.

Chinese Source

問或約化儀立題乃據籤文化儀四教文義整足任運攝得三藏等四為證或謂頒宣藏等以為頓等謂化儀無体又謂頓等四教古師亦用藏等四教起自天台以此為化法立題今何以從通名耶荅化儀化法有体無体或彼此相攝文各有意皆不為此立題而設况古師所立頓等與今不同故妙樂以頓等藏等為天台一家判釋之綱目今此一書既明判釋立題四教豈偏屬乎儀者天台一家四教判釋儀式也文末既云自從此下畧明諸家判教儀式顯今一書明判明釋在乎天台豈可謂如來施化次第儀式邪。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Some take the title as set up in terms of method, relying on the Annotations, whose text on the four teachings of method is complete in meaning and naturally embraces the Tripiṭaka and the other three, as proof. Others say that the Tripiṭaka and the rest are proclaimed as the sudden and the rest, and that method has no substance; they further say that the four teachings of the sudden and the rest were also used by the ancient masters, while the four teachings of the Tripiṭaka and the rest arose with Tiantai, and so take this as a title set up by content. Why do you now follow the common name? Answer: Whether method and content have substance or lack it, or whether one embraces the other, the texts each have their own intent; none of them was written for setting up this title. Moreover, the sudden and the rest established by the ancient masters are not the same as ours now; therefore Miaole takes the sudden and the rest and the Tripiṭaka and the rest as the main headings and items of the classification and explanation of our Tiantai school. Since this book makes clear classification and explanation, how could the title "Four Teachings" belong to one side only? Yi (儀, form) is the form of the classification and explanation of the four teachings of our Tiantai school. Since the end of the text says, "From here on I briefly make clear the forms of the classification of the teachings by the various schools," it shows that the whole book makes clear that classifying and explaining lie in Tiantai. How could one say [that it means] the sequence and forms of the Tathāgata's bestowing of transformation?

Chinese Source

高麗東夷國名沙門此云勤息謂勤行眾善止息諸惡故又沙門復以釋為姓者始於𣈆安法師也後增一阿合來此土云四河入海同一醎味四姓出家皆名為釋（文）顯性錄以四句棟云一是沙門非釋子出家外道二是釋子非沙門在家釋種（此王種之釋也）三是釋子是沙門兩土之僧（此四姓出家通稱為釋）四非釋子非沙門兩土之俗四句揀之無遺矣或謂是釋子是沙門乃釋種出家且梵土餘種出家及此土之僧皆稱沙門釋子為何句收耶錄謂觀師抄錄台教綱要也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Goryeo" is the name of a country of the eastern barbarians. "Monk" (沙門, śramaṇa) is translated here as "diligent quieting," that is, diligently practicing all goods and stopping and quieting all evils. Further, the taking of "Śākya" (釋) as the surname of śramaṇas began with Dharma Master [Dao]an of the Jin. Later the Ekottarika Āgama came to this land, saying: "When the four rivers enter the ocean, they share the same salty taste; when the four castes leave home, all are called Śākya" (end of quotation). The Record Revealing the Nature sifts this with four alternatives, saying: first, a śramaṇa who is not a son of the Śākya: outsiders who have left home; second, a son of the Śākya who is not a śramaṇa: the Śākya clan at home (this is the Śākya of the royal clan); third, a son of the Śākya who is a śramaṇa: the monks of both lands (this is the four castes leaving home, commonly called Śākya); fourth, neither a son of the Śākya nor a śramaṇa: the laity of both lands. Sifted by the four alternatives, nothing is left out. Some say that "a son of the Śākya who is a śramaṇa" is the Śākya clan who have left home. But then the other castes of the Brahmā land who have left home, and the monks of this land, are all called śramaṇas and sons of the Śākya; under which alternative would they be included? "Recorded" means that Master [Che]gwan copied and recorded the main outline of the Tai teaching.

Zhiyi and the Five Periods

Chinese Source

天台智者大師

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai,

Chinese Source

拾遺記云天台棲真之處智者隋主所稱大師羣生模範亦帝王大臣所師也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gleanings says: "Tiantai is the place where he dwelt in the true. Zhizhe (智者, the Wise One) is what the ruler of the Sui called him. 'Great Master' [means that he is] the model for the multitude of beings, and also the one whom emperors, kings, and great ministers take as teacher."

Chinese Source

以五時八教判釋東流一代聖教罄無不盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: by means of the Five Periods and the Eight Teachings, classified and explained the sacred teaching of the Buddha's whole lifetime that had flowed to the East, leaving nothing out.

Chinese Source

五時八教本是如來所說之法大師依義立名用此判釋一代聖教故云以也然上天台智者乃能判能釋之人東流聖教乃所判所釋之法五時八教乃判釋之儀式也蓋天台凖法華意判釋諸經如籤文云判釋凖乎部教部教之義唯在法華判謂剖判釋謂解釋妙樂云頓等是此宗判教之大網藏等是一家釋義之綱目如以化儀判華嚴為頓以化法別圓解釋乃至判法華為非頓非漸以純圓獨妙解釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Five Periods and the Eight Teachings were originally the Dharma taught by the Tathāgata; the Great Master named them according to their meaning and used them to classify and explain the sacred teaching of the whole lifetime; hence it says "by means of." Thus "Zhiyi of Tiantai" above is the person who is able to classify and explain; "the sacred teaching that had flowed to the East" is the Dharma that is classified and explained; the Five Periods and the Eight Teachings are the forms of classifying and explaining. For Tiantai classified and explained the sūtras on the model of the intent of the Lotus, as the Annotations says: "Classification and explanation are modeled on the divisions and teachings; the meaning of the divisions and teachings lies only in the Lotus." "Classify" means to cut apart and judge; "explain" means to resolve and interpret. Miaole says: "The sudden and the rest are the main outline of this school's classification of the teachings; the Tripiṭaka and the rest are the headings and items of our school's explanation of meaning." For example, by method it classifies the Avataṃsaka as sudden, and explains it by the distinct and the perfect of content; and so on, up to classifying the Lotus as neither sudden nor gradual, and explaining it as purely perfect and uniquely wondrous.

Chinese Source

東流者佛法自西而流東也代者更也如來五十年說法為一代今以五時八教判釋無遺若爾妙玄何云柰苑之前不預小攝耶須知妙玄約時破古謂說提謂經時乃未轉法輪已前未有僧寶故破古師不應於鹿苑前別立提謂為人天小教若約法𭣣經則如四教義云三藏明世間布施持戒禪定即是人天之教並正因緣所生善法此已為三藏所攝故先達云約時破古不當五時所收約法收經義當三藏所攝也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Flowed to the East": the Buddha-dharma flowed from the west to the east. "Lifetime" (代): dai means "to succeed one another"; the Tathāgata's fifty years of teaching the Dharma make one lifetime. Now it is classified and explained by the Five Periods and the Eight Teachings without omission. If so, why does the Wondrous Profound [Meaning] say that what came before the Āmra Park does not take part in the small [teaching's] embrace? You should know that the Wondrous Profound [Meaning] refutes the ancients in terms of period: that is, the time of teaching the Trapuṣa Sūtra was before the Dharma-wheel had been turned, when there was not yet the Saṅgha Jewel; therefore it refutes the ancient masters, [saying] that they should not have set up the Trapuṣa separately before the Deer Park as a small teaching for humans and gods. If sūtras are gathered in terms of Dharma, then, as the Meaning of the Four Teachings says, the Tripiṭaka makes clear worldly giving, keeping the precepts, and dhyāna concentration, which are the teaching of humans and gods, together with the good dharmas produced by correct causes and conditions; this is already included in the Tripiṭaka. Therefore an earlier master said: "Refuting the ancients in terms of period, it is not included in the Five Periods; gathering sūtras in terms of Dharma, in meaning it is included in the Tripiṭaka."

從經題立時雖歷七處八會（新經九會）秖是一經因行如華莊嚴果德具云大方廣佛華嚴經此人法譬三具足立題更有單三復三。

The period is set up from the sūtra's title. Though it passed through seven places and eight assemblies (in the new sūtra, nine assemblies), it is just one sūtra. The practice of the cause, like flowers, adorns the virtue of the fruit. In full it is called the Great Expansive Buddha Flower-Adornment Sūtra. This title is set up with all three — person, Dharma, and simile — complete; besides this there are the three single and the three double [kinds of title].

舊經𣈆譯五十卷或六十卷成新經唐譯八十卷成若龍宮三本上本十三世界微塵數品中本四十九萬八千八百偈下本十萬偈四十八品今但有三十九品如釋籤十（十八）舊立四種華嚴祖無顯文考大師荊溪之意則有約時約處約理之不同約理則曰法界約處或曰寂塲約時曰三七日或時長盡未來際何得認此名言便謂華嚴有四種之別且其間於義有妨不應以後分時長華嚴而為寂塲又不應將通五時中通後之義為時長也問華嚴時長為至何時荅如妙樂云義當轉教時也經家取後分部類相從結歸前分華嚴部內此即通五時中文通之類也若般若明華嚴海空及日若垂沒餘輝峻嶺與夫蓮華藏海通至涅槃之後此於他部明華嚴義不可結歸本部乃通五時中義通之類非時長也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The old sūtra, translated in the Jin, is complete in fifty or sixty fascicles; the new sūtra, translated in the Tang, is complete in eighty fascicles. As for the three versions in the dragon palace: the upper version has as many chapters as the dust-motes of thirteen worlds; the middle version has 498,800 verses; the lower version has 100,000 verses in forty-eight chapters. Now we have only thirty-nine chapters, as in Annotations 10 (18). The old [masters] set up four kinds of Avataṃsaka, [but] the patriarchs have no explicit text for it. Examining the intent of the Great Master and Jingxi, there are the differences of [speaking] in terms of time, in terms of place, and in terms of principle: in terms of principle it is called the dharma-realm; in terms of place it is sometimes called the site of quiescence; in terms of time it is called three times seven days, or sometimes the long time to the end of the future. How can one take these words and then say that the Avataṃsaka has four distinct kinds? Moreover, there are problems of meaning in this: one should not take the long-time Avataṃsaka of the later portion as [the Avataṃsaka of] the site of quiescence; nor should one take the meaning of "pervading what comes after," [one of the ways of] pervading the Five Periods, as "long time." Question: How far does the long time of the Avataṃsaka extend? Answer: As Miaole says, "In meaning it corresponds to the time of transferring the teaching." The compilers took the later portion and, grouping it by category, concluded it and returned it to the Avataṃsaka division of the earlier portion; this is of the kind "pervading in text," [one of the ways of] pervading the Five Periods. As for the Prajñā making clear the ocean-like emptiness of the Avataṃsaka, and "the sun about to set, its remaining light on the high peaks," and the Lotus Treasury Ocean pervading to after the Nirvāṇa — these make clear the meaning of the Avataṃsaka in other divisions and cannot be concluded and returned to its own division; they are of the kind "pervading in meaning," [one of the ways of] pervading the Five Periods, not "long time."

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The period is set up from the place: though many sūtras were taught, they were in the same place. It was the land where the Tathāgata in a former life let down his transformation; the circumstances are as in the Fuxing. [According to] the Fuxing, a herd of deer lived there, hence the name Deer Park; named from its trees, it is also called the Āmra Park; since two seers lived there, it is also called the Seers' Park.

Root text: third, the Vaipulya period

Chinese Source

廣談四教均被眾機說經既多處亦不一故約法立時也若普賢觀稱方等者從理得名如釋籤六（十五）云此以理等名方等典若止觀二（六）云四門入清涼池曰方所契之理曰等此約行理合論今是生酥調斥之方等義應属事。

Ru-Yi Translation

It discusses the four teachings extensively, addressing all capacities equally; the sūtras taught are many and the places are not one; therefore the period is set up in terms of the Dharma. As for the Samantabhadra Contemplation being called vaipulya, it gets the name from principle, as Annotations 6 (15) says: "Here, by principle being equal (等), it is called a vaipulya (方等) scripture." Calming and Contemplation 2 (6) says: "Entering the cool pool by the four gates is called fang (方, square, method); the principle that is accorded with is called deng (等, equal)"; this discusses practice and principle together. The present one is the vaipulya of curdled butter, which rebukes and criticizes; its meaning should belong to phenomena.

Ru-Yi Translation
