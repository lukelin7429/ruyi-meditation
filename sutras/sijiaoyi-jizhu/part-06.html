The Distinct Teaching's Practices, Dedications, and Grounds, and the Perfect Teaching's Five Grades

This part covers the end of fascicle eight and most of fascicle nine. Mengrun first explains the six "lineage-natures" of the Yingluo Sūtra and their reduction to the two lineages of the Bodhisattvabhūmi, then annotates the distinct teaching's ten practices, ten dedications, and ten grounds, including the Yingluo definitions of each stage and the distances in yojanas. He discusses whether the "unconditioned" four truths studied in the ten practices are perfect or "exclusive," the two delusions that remain at equal awakening, and the reward-buddha on the lotus throne, and ends the distinct teaching with a summary of how it is vertical and horizontal in cause, fruit, nature, and cultivation. The perfect teaching opens with the meanings of "perfect," the scriptural evidence (including the Vimalakīrti's silent entry into nonduality), and the eight stages. The longest section concerns the first of the five grades: the wondrous mind complete with the three thousand, the five repentances, repentance in principle and in phenomena, and a rebuke of those who claim that the perfect-and-sudden needs no practice. The part ends with the other four grades, their relation to the three wisdoms and the four reliances, and the start of the ten faiths.

The Six Lineage-Natures of the Distinct Teaching

Chinese Source

習種性者纓絡經上卷（賢字函）明六種性以對別位（淨名䟽第九亦借別名以顯圓）今家玄籤四教義戒䟽等並依經列四念處中少有不次又地持論略明二種（亦名經弥勒造賢字函）如戒䟽列六種後復用二種及對教證前後生報佛法佛併圖示。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The nature attained through habituated practice": the Yingluo Sūtra, upper fascicle (case xian), makes clear six lineage-natures to match the stages of the distinct [teaching] (the Commentary on the Vimalakīrti, fascicle 9, also borrows the distinct names to reveal the perfect). The Profound [Meaning], the Annotations, the Meaning of the Four Teachings, the Commentary on the Precepts, and others of our school all list them following the sūtra; in the Four Foundations of Mindfulness they are slightly out of order. Further, the Bodhisattvabhūmi treatise briefly makes clear two kinds (it is also called a sūtra; composed by Maitreya; case xian). How the Commentary on the Precepts lists the six kinds and afterward again uses the two kinds, matching them to teaching and realization, to before and after, and to what gives rise to the reward-buddha and the Dharma-buddha, is shown together in a diagram.

Chinese Source

六種性者種別性分也地持第一云種性者名為種子名為界名為性（文）（種子不同有六界分）又性通六位種局在因故前四名種等覺雖因望前稱覺二種者地持經云略說二種性種性者是菩薩六入殊勝展轉相續無始法尓是名性種性習種性者若從先來修善所得是名習種性（文）

Ru-Yi Translation

"Six lineage-natures": "lineage" [種] means distinction; "nature" [性] means portion. Bodhisattvabhūmi 1 says: "Lineage-nature is called seed, is called element, is called nature" (end of quotation) (because the seeds are not the same, there are six divisions of element). Further, "nature" extends through all six stages, while "lineage" is confined to the cause; hence the first four are called "lineage," and equal awakening, though a cause, is called "awakening" in relation to what precedes it. "Two kinds": the Bodhisattvabhūmi Sūtra says: "In brief there are two kinds. The lineage of nature is this: the bodhisattva's six sense-bases are excellent, continuing one after another, beginningless, so by the nature of things; this is called the lineage of nature. The lineage of habituation is what is obtained from cultivating good from before; this is called the lineage of habituation" (end of quotation).

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Commentary on the Precepts matches the six kinds to the stages and afterward again uses the two kinds": because the six stages do not go beyond cultivation and habituation before the grounds and the realization of the nature on ascending the grounds, it uses the Bodhisattvabhūmi to gather up the six kinds. Though it does not state this explicitly, its intent must be so. Moreover, the Brahmā's Net is the concluding sūtra of the Avataṃsaka, and the Bodhisattvabhūmi takes the Avataṃsaka as its principal source; hence it is fitting to use that Bodhisattvabhūmi's two kinds to gather up the six kinds of the Yingluo. Further, their meaning is exactly in line with teaching and realization in terms of practice; hence [the commentary] also compares them. From of old the passage of the Commentary on the Precepts has been thought difficult; now I follow the old annotations. The Commentary on the Precepts says: "The two kinds, nature and habituation: if determined by stage, the lineage of habituation is first and the lineage of nature after; if discussed by practice, nature and habituation are simultaneous, and before and after are not fixed (this states the meaning in terms of practice). Relying on the substance, the function arises (relying on the principle of the middle, the function of contemplative practice arises): first making clear the lineage of nature, then the lineage of habituation. Tracing the function, one grasps the substance (from the function of one's own practice one grasps the substance of the middle way): first habituation, then nature (from 'relying on the substance' on, this explains the meaning of 'simultaneous, not fixed'). This resembles the two paths of teaching and realization: discussed in terms of stage, the path of the teaching is first and the path of realization after (teaching and realization in terms of practice); discussed in terms of practice, teaching and realization are simultaneous, and before and after are not fixed (this states [the point]). Relying on the substance, the function arises: first realization, then teaching (from the principle of the path of realization arises the function of the path of the teaching). Tracing the function, one grasps the substance: first teaching, then realization (tracing the function of the path of the teaching, one realizes the substance of the middle way). The lineage of habituation can give rise to the reward-buddha; the lineage of nature can give rise to the Dharma-buddha" (end of quotation. Since before the grounds one's own practice of cultivation and habituation is discussed, at the fruit it should give rise to the reward-buddha; on ascending the grounds one realizes the nature, so it should give rise to the Dharma-buddha. Wisdom and substance being merged, the function arises naturally; hence the response-buddha is not discussed. Differing explanations are given in full in the various commentaries).

Chinese Source

梵網經中更有六種一習種性二長養性（只是研習增長與習種性通對十住）三性種性四不可壞性（俗諦建立故不可壞與性種通對十行）五道種性（十向）六正法性（通𭣣聖種等妙三種）。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the Brahmā's Net Sūtra there are a further six kinds: first, the lineage of habituation; second, the nature of nurturing growth (it is just study and habituation increasing; together with the lineage of habituation it corresponds to the ten abidings); third, the lineage of nature; fourth, the indestructible nature (because the conventional truth is established, it is indestructible; together with the lineage of nature it corresponds to the ten practices); fifth, the lineage of the path (the ten dedications); sixth, the nature of the true Dharma (gathering in all three: the noble lineage, equal [awakening], and wondrous [awakening]).

Chinese Source

從假入空觀者次第三觀出纓絡經觀經䟽（三）云假是虛妄俗諦也空是審實真諦也今欲去俗歸真故言從假入空觀妙宗上（卄六）云見思取境無而謂有虛假凡俗知虛名諦二空之理是審實法知實名諦不究俗虛莫知真實要須照假方得入空是故名曰從假入空觀妙玄三（十一）云十住正修空傍修假中十行正修假旁修中淨名略記下之上（十五）若約別教為語正觀中道為慧眼者於十住中遠所期耳慧眼者古德頌云天眼通非礙肉眼礙非通法眼唯觀俗慧眼了知空佛眼如千日照異體還同一切智者觀音玄義下（初）云知一切內法內名一切能知能解一切外法外名能知能解但不能用一切道起一切種故名一切智玄記下具釋初住斷見即離四趣身子昔生至六住有退者此見思俱斷思既未盡見亦餘殘故有退墮如四明荅曰本難（教行錄）。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The contemplation of entering emptiness from the provisional": the sequential three contemplations come from the Yingluo Sūtra. Commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra (3) says: "The provisional is the false conventional truth; emptiness is the examined and real truth of the real. Now, wishing to leave the conventional and return to the real, it says 'the contemplation of entering emptiness from the provisional.'" The Wondrous Purport, upper [fascicle] (26), says: "Views and habituated thought grasp objects that do not exist and say they exist: this is the false, the ordinary and conventional; knowing it as false is called 'truth.' The principle of the two emptinesses is the examined and real dharma; knowing it as real is called 'truth.' Without investigating the falseness of the conventional, one cannot know the true and real; one must illuminate the provisional in order to enter emptiness. Therefore it is named 'the contemplation of entering emptiness from the provisional.'" Wondrous Profound [Meaning] 3 (11) says: "The ten abidings principally cultivate emptiness and secondarily cultivate the provisional and the middle; the ten practices principally cultivate the provisional and secondarily cultivate the middle." Abridged Notes on the Vimalakīrti, lower [fascicle], first part (15) [says]: "If one speaks in terms of the distinct teaching, taking the correct contemplation of the middle way as the wisdom-eye, then in the ten abidings it is only a distant aim." "Wisdom-eye": an ancient worthy's verse says: "The heavenly eye penetrates and is not obstructed; the flesh eye is obstructed and does not penetrate; the Dharma-eye contemplates only the conventional; the wisdom-eye understands emptiness; the buddha-eye is like a thousand suns shining, their bodies different yet returning to the same." "The wisdom that knows all things": Profound Meaning of Avalokiteśvara, lower [fascicle] (beginning), says: "Knowing all inner dharmas — 'inner' is called 'all'; being able to know and understand all outer dharmas — 'outer' is called 'able to know and understand'; but not being able to use all paths to give rise to all modes; hence it is called the wisdom that knows all things." The Notes on the Profound Meaning, lower [fascicle], explains it in full. In the first abiding one severs views and so leaves the four [evil] destinies. As for Śāriputra in the past reaching the sixth abiding and regressing: [at that stage] views and habituated thought are being severed together; since habituated thought was not yet exhausted, views too had remnants, hence he regressed and fell — as in Siming's Answers to the Questions from Japan (Records of Teaching and Practice).

The Distinct Teaching: The Ten Practices

Chinese Source

次明十行者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, making clear the ten practices:

Chinese Source

四教義四（六）云此十通名行者行以進趣為義前既發真悟理從此加修從空入假觀無量四諦。

Ru-Yi Translation

Meaning of the Four Teachings 4 (6) says: "These ten are all called 'practices' because practice means advancing. Having previously brought forth the true and awakened to the principle, from here one adds cultivation and, with the contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness, [contemplates] the four truths of the immeasurable."

Chinese Source

一歡喜二饒益三無違逆四無屈撓五無癡亂六善現七無着八難得九善法十真實（斷界外塵沙惑）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: first, joy; second, benefiting; third, no opposition; fourth, non-yielding; fifth, freedom from confused delusion; sixth, wholesome manifestation; seventh, non-attachment; eighth, hard to attain; ninth, good dharmas; tenth, the real (severing the delusion of dust-and-sand beyond the realms).

Chinese Source

始入法空不為邪動名歡喜行常化眾生使得法利名饒益行常修忍法謙下恭敬名無違逆行行大精進令一切至究竟涅槃名無屈撓行不為無明之所失亂名無癡亂行生生常在佛國中生名善現行於我我所一切皆空名無着行菩薩成就難得善根名難得行說法授人成物軌則名善法行二諦非如亦非非相名真實行。

Ru-Yi Translation

First entering the emptiness of dharmas and not moved by the wrong: this is called the practice of joy. Constantly transforming sentient beings so that they gain the benefit of the Dharma: called the practice of benefiting. Constantly cultivating the dharma of forbearance, humble and respectful: called the practice of no opposition. Practicing great effort so that all reach ultimate nirvāṇa: called the practice of non-yielding. Not being lost and confused by ignorance: called the practice of freedom from confused delusion. Being born life after life always in a buddha-land: called the practice of wholesome manifestation. Toward self and what belongs to self, all being empty: called the practice of non-attachment. The bodhisattva accomplishing wholesome roots hard to attain: called the practice of hard to attain. Expounding the Dharma and imparting it to people, becoming a model for beings: called the practice of good dharmas. The two truths being not suchness and also not not-mark: called the practice of the real.

Chinese Source

亦云性種性用從空入假觀見俗諦開法眼成道種智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: They are also called the nature attained through distinguishing; one uses the contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness, sees the conventional truth, opens the Dharma-eye, and accomplishes the wisdom of the paths.

Chinese Source

性種性者假觀分別十界差別種性也從空入假觀者觀經䟽（三）云若住於空與二乘何異不成佛法不益眾生是故觀空不住於空而入於假知病識藥應病授藥令得服行故名從空入假觀（文）道種智者觀音玄下（初）云能知一切道種差別則分別假名無謬故名道種智（文）

Ru-Yi Translation

"The nature attained through distinguishing": the contemplation of the provisional distinguishes the different lineage-natures of the ten realms. "The contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness": Commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra (3) says: "If one abides in emptiness, how is one different from the two vehicles? One does not accomplish the Buddhadharma and does not benefit sentient beings. Therefore one contemplates emptiness without abiding in emptiness and enters the provisional, knowing the illness, recognizing the medicine, giving medicine according to the illness, and getting [beings] to take it and practice; hence it is called the contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness" (end of quotation). "The wisdom of the paths": Profound Meaning of Avalokiteśvara, lower [fascicle] (beginning), says: "Being able to know the distinctions of all the kinds of paths, it distinguishes provisional names without error; hence it is called the wisdom of the paths" (end of quotation).

Chinese Source

諸文云十住修空斷見思十行修假破塵沙十向修中伏無明此以觀對位也若云初住斷見二住至七住斷思八九十住斷界內塵沙十行斷界外塵沙此斷惑分齊也四念處四云十住斷界外上品塵沙十行斷中品塵沙十向斷下品塵沙（文言三品若生無生八門漏上品無量四門為中品無作四門為下品）此以惑從教也以別是界外教或純用假觀攝故

Ru-Yi Translation

The various texts say: the ten abidings cultivate emptiness and sever views and habituated thought; the ten practices cultivate the provisional and break dust-and-sand; the ten dedications cultivate the middle and subdue ignorance — this matches the contemplations to the stages. If it is said that the first abiding severs views, the second to seventh abidings sever habituated thought, the eighth, ninth, and tenth abidings sever the dust-and-sand within the realms, and the ten practices sever the dust-and-sand beyond the realms — this is the extent of severing delusion. Four Foundations of Mindfulness 4 says: "The ten abidings sever the upper grade of dust-and-sand beyond the realms, the ten practices sever the middle grade of dust-and-sand, and the ten dedications sever the lower grade of dust-and-sand" (the text speaks of three grades: the eight gates of arising and non-arising are the upper grade, the four gates of the immeasurable are the middle grade, and the four gates of the unconditioned are the lower grade). This makes the delusion follow the teaching, because the distinct is a teaching beyond the realms, or because it is gathered entirely under the contemplation of the provisional.

又此十行明橫學四且無作四門為圓為但若圓無作十行位淺尚未修中如何能說以此化他耶若謂但中釋籤五（初）云各附彼教而為相狀（文）既附彼圓豈應是但雜編五（十四）云十向圓修可由實道（智轉行融乃修圓中）十行無作且順權方（只是但中無作）良以修中之位已深出假之位尚淺位深故知昔日化他無非妙行位淺故知將來自行亦是但中不可以實難權以他妨自（文）其說切當學者知之附彼圓教但為明於無作相狀能附豈可全同深位然此出假若果但中圓機起時何以赴之當知圓機自感圓應十行出假乃教道說無稽之問不足評矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, these ten practices make clear the horizontal study of the four [kinds of four truths]. Are the four gates of the unconditioned then perfect or exclusive? If they are the perfect unconditioned, the stage of the ten practices is shallow and has not yet cultivated the middle — how could it expound this to transform others? If one calls them the exclusive middle, Annotations 5 (beginning) says: "Each attaches to that teaching to form its features" (end of quotation); since they attach to that perfect [teaching], how should they be exclusive? Miscellany 5 (14) says: "The perfect cultivation of the ten dedications can come about through the real path (wisdom turns and practice is fused, and only then does one cultivate the perfect middle); the unconditioned of the ten practices for the time being follows the provisional method (it is only the unconditioned of the exclusive middle). Truly, because the stage of cultivating the middle is already deep while the stage of going out into the provisional is still shallow — since the stage is deep, one knows that the transforming of others in the past was none other than wondrous practice; since the stage is shallow, one knows that one's own practice in the future is also the exclusive middle. One must not use the real to challenge the provisional, nor use [practice for] others to obstruct [practice for] oneself" (end of quotation). This statement is apt; students should know it. Attaching to that perfect teaching is only to make clear the features of the unconditioned; how could what attaches be entirely the same as the deep stage? Yet if this going out into the provisional is really the exclusive middle, when a perfect capacity arises, how does it respond to it? One should know that a perfect capacity itself elicits a perfect response; the going out into the provisional of the ten practices is spoken from the path of the teaching. A baseless question is not worth evaluating.

The Distinct Teaching: The Ten Dedications

Chinese Source

次明十回向者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next, making clear the ten dedications:

Chinese Source

四教義四（七）云此十通名回向者回事向理回因向果回己功德普施眾生事理和融順入法界故名回向四念處三（十一）云別向圓修（文）霅川云可由實道（雜編但不合云只緣被接方曰圓修）此據得意者智轉行融及證道說也若三觀次第惟修但中據不得意者及教道說也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Meaning of the Four Teachings 4 (7) says: "These ten are all called 'dedications' because one turns phenomena toward principle, turns cause toward fruit, and turns one's own merit to bestow it universally on sentient beings; phenomena and principle harmoniously fused, one enters the dharma-realm in accord; hence they are called dedications." Four Foundations of Mindfulness 3 (11) says: "The distinct dedications are perfect cultivation" (end of quotation). Zhachuan says: "It can come about through the real path" (the Miscellany; but it should not say that it is called perfect cultivation only because of being grafted). This is said of one who has grasped the intent, wisdom turning and practice fusing, and from the path of realization. If the three contemplations are sequential and only the exclusive middle is cultivated, this is said of one who has not grasped the intent, and from the path of the teaching.

Chinese Source

一救護眾生離眾生相二不壞三等一切諸佛四至一切處五無盡功德藏六入一切平等善根七等隨順一切眾生八真如相九無縛無著解脫十入法界無量（伏無明習中觀）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: first, rescuing sentient beings while apart from the mark of sentient beings; second, indestructibility; third, equal to all buddhas; fourth, reaching every place; fifth, the inexhaustible treasury of merit; sixth, entering all the wholesome roots of equality; seventh, equally according with all sentient beings; eighth, the mark of suchness; ninth, unbound and unattached liberation; tenth, entering the immeasurable dharma-realm (subduing ignorance and practicing the contemplation of the middle).

Chinese Source

以無想心常行六道而入果報不受而受名救護眾生離眾生相觀一切法有受有用念念不住名為不壞三世佛法一切時行名等一切諸佛以大願力入一切佛土供養一切佛名至一切處以常住法授與前人名無盡功德藏行無漏善善而不二名入一切平等善根以觀善惡無二一相名等隨順一切眾生心得自在等三世佛常照有無名真如相以般若照三世諸法是一合相名無縛無著解脫覺一切法中道無相名入法界無量伏無明習中觀者集解云空假之心既已滿足正修中道第一義觀無明不起悉伏故也（文）。

Ru-Yi Translation

With a mind without notions, constantly going through the six destinies and entering [the realm of] karmic reward, receiving without receiving: this is called rescuing sentient beings while apart from the mark of sentient beings. Contemplating all dharmas as having reception and having use, thought after thought not abiding: called indestructibility. Practicing the Buddhadharma of the three times at all times: called equal to all buddhas. By the power of great vows entering all buddha-lands and making offerings to all buddhas: called reaching every place. Bestowing the eternally abiding Dharma on others: called the inexhaustible treasury of merit. Practicing uncontaminated good, good without duality: called entering all the wholesome roots of equality. Contemplating good and evil as not two, of a single mark: called equally according with all sentient beings. The mind attaining mastery, equal to the buddhas of the three times, constantly illuminating existence and nonexistence: called the mark of suchness. With prajñā illuminating all dharmas of the three times as a single combined mark: called unbound and unattached liberation. Awakening to the markless middle way in all dharmas: called entering the immeasurable dharma-realm. "Subduing ignorance and practicing the contemplation of the middle": the Collected Explanations says: "Since the mind of emptiness and the provisional is already fulfilled, one properly cultivates the contemplation of the middle way, the first principle; ignorance does not arise, because it is all subdued" (end of quotation).

Chinese Source

亦名道種性行四百由旬居方便有餘土（已上三十位為三賢亦名內凡從入住至此為行不退位）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: This stage of the five grades perfectly subdues the afflictions of the five abodes; it is the outer ordinary stage, the same as the distinct teaching's stage of the ten faiths.

Ru-Yi Translation

Wondrous Profound [Meaning] 5 (8) says: "The five grades have already perfectly understood the one real four truths; their mind, thought after thought, corresponds with the pāramitās of the dharma-realm; the whole substance is without the deviations of wrongness, crookedness, partiality, and inversion; they perfectly subdue the branch and guest [afflictions] and the fundamental delusions; hence it is called the forbearance of subduing. It also says on leaf 10: "In the stage of the five grades, though principle is not yet revealed, contemplative wisdom is already perfect; possessing the nature of affliction, one can know the Tathāgata's secret treasury and is fit to be the first reliance for the world" (end of quotation). If, as in the Samantabhadra Contemplation, the grades and the faiths are spoken of together, this is a different exposition according to capacity. Further, our patriarch's stage was in the five grades, and yet it is said that he obtained the revolving dhāraṇī: though the revolving of the provisional to enter emptiness, discussed vertically by stage, lies before the seventh faith of the six faculties, discussed horizontally by contemplation, there is no hindrance to its extending to the five grades.

The Perfect Teaching: The Ten Faiths

Chinese Source

次進六根清淨位即是十信初信斷見惑顯真理與藏教初果通教八人見地別教初住齊證位不退也次從二信至七信斷思惑盡與藏通二佛別教七住齊三界苦集斷盡無餘故仁王云十善菩薩發大心長別三界苦輪海解曰十善者各具十善也若別十信即伏而不斷故定屬圓信。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Next one advances to the stage of the purification of the six faculties, which is the ten faiths. The first faith severs the delusion of views and reveals the true principle, equal to the Tripiṭaka teaching's first fruit, the shared teaching's ground of the eighth-stage practitioner and ground of vision, and the distinct teaching's first abiding; one realizes non-regression in stage. Next, from the second faith to the seventh faith, one severs the delusion of habituated thought completely, equal to the buddhas of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings and the distinct teaching's seventh abiding; the suffering and accumulation of the three realms are severed completely without remainder. Hence the Humane Kings says: "The bodhisattva of the ten wholesome [deeds] arouses the great mind and parts forever from the sea of the wheel of suffering of the three realms." Explanation: "ten wholesome" means that each [faith] is complete with the ten wholesome [deeds]. If it were the distinct teaching's ten faiths, they would subdue but not sever; hence this surely belongs to the perfect faiths.

Chinese Source

Wondrous Profound [Meaning] 5 (4) says: "The stage of the ten faiths: at first, through perfect hearing, one can give rise to perfect faith and cultivate perfect practice; skillfully increasing it, this perfect practice grows five times deeper and clearer. Through this perfect practice one enters the perfect stage: by well cultivating the equal dharma-realm one enters the mind of faith, and so on up to well cultivating non-attachment, entering the mind of vows; this is called the stage of the ten faiths. The Yingluo (says): 'One faith has ten; the ten faiths have a hundred; the hundred dharmas are the root of all dharmas.' This is called the stage of the ten faiths of the iron wheel of the perfect teaching, which is the purification of the six faculties, the resembling understanding of the perfect teaching: warmth, summit, forbearance, and the supreme worldly dharma. The Samantabhadra Contemplation makes clear that before the acceptance of non-arising there are ten kinds of realms: this is this stage" (end of quotation) (Samantabhadra, Śākyamuni, the emanation bodies, and Prabhūtaratna — four sages — together with the purification of the six faculties make the ten kinds).

仁王云者波斯匿王所說偈也十善者仁王䟽中（十九）云十信善者有三品上品善鐵輪王化一天下中品善粟散王下品善人中王（文）妙樂十（八）云信信通皆具足十善非謂專以人天不殺盜等用對十信即云長別三界苦輪當知須是斷惑十信（文）釋籤五（卄七）云亦有人云六根清淨名為頓義十善菩薩此是漸義今（文）所引十善菩薩以證六根豈應引漸而證於頓故知二（文）俱頓明矣但仁王經語其初後法華經意論其中間人不見之徒生異見（文）。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Humane Kings says": this is a verse spoken by King Prasenajit. "Ten wholesome": Commentary on the Humane Kings Sūtra, middle [fascicle] (19), says: "The goods of the ten faiths have three grades: the upper grade of good is the iron-wheel king who transforms one continent; the middle grade of good is a millet-scattered king; the lower grade of good is a king among people" (end of quotation). Miaole 10 (8) says: "Each faith in common is fully complete with the ten wholesome [deeds]; it does not mean that the not killing, not stealing, and so on, of humans and gods alone are used to match the ten faiths. Since it says 'parts forever from the wheel of suffering of the three realms,' one should know they must be the ten faiths that sever delusion" (end of quotation). Annotations 5 (27) says: "Some people also say that the purification of the six faculties is the sudden meaning and the bodhisattva of the ten wholesome [deeds] is the gradual meaning. Now (the text) cites the bodhisattva of the ten wholesome [deeds] to prove the six faculties; how should it cite the gradual to prove the sudden? Hence one knows that both (texts) are clearly sudden. The Humane Kings Sūtra speaks of its beginning and end; the intent of the Lotus Sūtra discusses its middle. People who do not see this vainly give rise to different views" (end of quotation).

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Root text: Yet the perfect person originally does not aim at severing views, habituated thought, and dust-and-sand; his intent is to enter the abidings, sever ignorance, and see buddha-nature. It is like smelting iron: the coarse dross goes first, though that is not what was originally aimed at; the intent is to make a vessel. Before the vessel is made, [the dross] naturally falls away first; though views go first, that person has not a single thought of delight. Why? Because what he aimed at has not yet been fulfilled. The practitioner of the perfect teaching is likewise: though it is not what he originally hoped for, [these delusions] naturally fall away first.
