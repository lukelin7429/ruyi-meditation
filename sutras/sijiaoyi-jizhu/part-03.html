The Tripiṭaka Teaching: The Heavens, Accumulation, Cessation, the Path, and the Outer Ordinary

This part covers the rest of fascicle four and fascicle five. Mengrun finishes the path of gods with the cosmology of the great chiliocosm, the cycle of eons and the three calamities, the six desire heavens, the eighteen heavens of form, and the four formless heavens, including the debate over whether the formless realm has form. He then comments on the remaining three truths of the Tripiṭaka teaching. For accumulation he explains each of Chegwan's eight names for the delusion of views and habituated thought, the ten agents and how they are distributed among the four truths to make eighty-eight, and the eighty-one grades of habituated thought. For cessation he stresses that extinction only uncovers the real and is not itself the real. For the path he goes through all seven groups of the thirty-seven factors, mostly from Zhiyi's Four Foundations of Mindfulness and Sequential Gates of the Dharma-Realm, and then explains the four kinds of four truths. The part ends with the start of the śrāvaka's stages: why the Abhidharma "gate of existence" is used, and the first two of the five methods for stilling the mind, the contemplation of impurity and the contemplation of loving-kindness.

The Heavens and the Great Chiliocosm

Chinese Source

輔行二上（卄六）云今釋典中所言天者亦名最勝亦名光明（文）文句四（未）云天者天然自然樂勝身勝（文）二十八天不同舉竪包橫也若統論一佛化境則有三千大千世界（三千者小千中千大千也二禪統一小千三禪統一中千四禪統一大千三千是總大千是別總別雙舉以別顯總也）

Ru-Yi Translation

Fuxing 2A (26) says: "What the Buddhist scriptures now call 'heaven' (天) is also called 'most excellent' and also called 'light'" (end of quotation). Textual Commentary 4 (end) says: "'Heaven' means natural, spontaneous, superior in bliss and superior in body" (end of quotation). "The twenty-eight heavens differ": naming the vertical to include the horizontal. If one discusses the whole realm of a single buddha's transformation, there is the trichiliocosm (the three thousand are the small chiliocosm, the middle chiliocosm, and the great chiliocosm. The second dhyāna covers one small chiliocosm, the third dhyāna covers one middle chiliocosm, and the fourth dhyāna covers one great chiliocosm. "Three thousand" is the general term and "great chiliocosm" the specific; general and specific are named together, the specific being used to show the general).

Chinese Source

俱舍頌云四大洲日月蘇迷盧欲天梵世各一千名一小千界（謂一千四洲一千日月一千須彌一千六欲天一千初禪天總名小千世界）此小千千倍說名一中千（一千箇小千世界名中千世界）此千倍大千（一千箇中千世界名大千世界小千為一二禪所覆中千為一三禪所覆大千為一四禪所覆例此應云千箇初禪為小千千箇二禪為中千千箇三禪為大千小千如十錢中千如十貫大千如千箇千貫

Ru-Yi Translation

A verse of the Kośa says: "The four great continents, the sun and moon, Sumeru, the desire heavens, and the Brahmā worlds, each a thousand, are called one small chiliocosm" (that is, a thousand sets of four continents, a thousand suns and moons, a thousand Sumerus, a thousand sets of six desire heavens, and a thousand first-dhyāna heavens are together called a small chiliocosm). "A thousand times this small chiliocosm is called one middle chiliocosm" (a thousand small chiliocosms are called a middle chiliocosm). "A thousand times this is the great chiliocosm" (a thousand middle chiliocosms are called a great chiliocosm. A small chiliocosm is covered by one second dhyāna, a middle chiliocosm by one third dhyāna, and a great chiliocosm by one fourth dhyāna. By analogy one should say that a thousand first dhyānas make a small chiliocosm, a thousand second dhyānas a middle chiliocosm, and a thousand third dhyānas a great chiliocosm. A small chiliocosm is like ten coins, a middle chiliocosm like ten strings of coins, and a great chiliocosm like a thousand strings of a thousand.

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet if a hundred thousand is taken as a yi, a great chiliocosm has ten thousand yi of suns and moons; and that the Golden Light Sūtra says a hundred yi of suns and moons, and so on up to a hundred yi of heavens of Neither Perception nor Non-perception, is because there are four kinds of yi: first, a hundred thousand make a yi, so that a middle chiliocosm has ten yi and a great chiliocosm ten thousand yi; second, a million make a yi, so that a middle chiliocosm has one yi and a great chiliocosm a thousand yi; third, ten million make a yi, so that a great chiliocosm has a hundred yi; the Golden Light relies on this number; fourth, a hundred million make a yi, so that a great chiliocosm has only ten yi. Further, the fourth dhyāna and [the heaven of] Neither Perception are only one, yet it also says a hundred yi; Golden Light Notes 5 (7) says: "It is said looking from below to above." It may also be an error of translation, for in Yijing's new translation there are no words "a hundred yi of [heavens of] Neither Perception." Further, the Auspicious Response Sūtra says "the center of three thousand suns and moons and twelve thousand heavens and earths": the ten thousand is the great chiliocosm, that is, ten thousand yi; the two thousand are the middle and small chiliocosms. That is to say, the Buddha was born in the country of Kapilavastu, which is the center of the trichiliocosm.)

皆同一成壞（須知成住壞空各二十刦為一大刦一增一减為一小劫於住劫中二十增减每一增减有小三災壞於有情住刦𥁞時乃有大三災壞於世界經二十刦空刦成刦各二十刦並約住刦數量而論）

Ru-Yi Translation

"All alike share one formation and destruction" (you should know that formation, abiding, destruction, and emptiness, each of twenty eons, make one great eon, and one increase and one decrease make one small eon. Within the eon of abiding there are twenty increases and decreases; in each increase and decrease the three lesser calamities destroy sentient beings; when the eon of abiding is exhausted, the three great calamities destroy the world, lasting twenty eons; the eon of emptiness and the eon of formation are each twenty eons; all are reckoned by the measure of the eon of abiding).

Ru-Yi Translation

"The paths of [evil] karma increase, lifespan decreases down to ten, the three calamities appear: sword, sickness, and famine, in order, ending in seven days, months, and years" (according to the Yogācārabhūmi, when the human lifespan decreases to thirty years, the calamity of famine arises and ends after seven years, seven months, and seven days; when it decreases to twenty years, the calamity of pestilence arises and ends after seven months and seven days; when it decreases to ten years, the calamity of weapons arises and ends after seven days and seven nights. That the Kośa now says "down to ten, the three calamities appear" is said with reference to the extreme of the decreasing eon. Hence, counting back in order from ten years, the weapons at ten years correspond to seven days, the pestilence at twenty years to seven months, and the famine at thirty years to seven years; this is no different at all from the Yogācārabhūmi.

Ru-Yi Translation

Chuiyu Notes 10 (12) cites Master [Kumārajī]va: "Vasumitra descended from the Trāyastriṃśa heaven and was born in a royal house as crown prince. He transformed the people, saying: 'Our ancestors' lifespans were extremely long; because we now are angry and without kindness, we have come to such a short life. Therefore you should practice a mind of kindness.' The people followed his command, and their evil minds gradually thinned. After this their children lived twenty years, and so on in turn it increased to eighty-four thousand years" (end of quotation). In discussing increase, the children's years are double the fathers', so that it increases from ten years to eighty-four thousand; in discussing decrease, one year is lost every hundred years, so that it decreases from eighty-four thousand years to ten years. Hence one increase and one decrease make one small eon.)

三災水火風上三定為頂（二禪為火災頂三禪為水災頂四禪為風災頂）如次內災等四無不動故（初禪內有覺觀能燒惱心等外火災二禪內有喜受與輕安俱潤身如水故徧身觕重等外水災三禪內有動息息亦是風等外風災四禪無內患故亦無外災）然彼器非常情俱生滅故（彼器即依報雖云不動非是常住不壞之法情即有情正報彼天宮殿情生則生情滅則滅）

Ru-Yi Translation

"The three calamities, water, fire, and wind, have the upper three concentrations as their tops" (the second dhyāna is the top of the calamity of fire, the third dhyāna the top of the calamity of water, the fourth dhyāna the top of the calamity of wind). "In order they equal the inner calamities; the fourth has none, because it is unmoving" (the first dhyāna has within it initial and sustained thought, which can burn and trouble the mind, equal to the outer calamity of fire; the second dhyāna has within it the feeling of joy, which together with ease moistens the body like water, so that the whole body is coarse and heavy, equal to the outer calamity of water; the third dhyāna has within it moving breath, and breath too is wind, equal to the outer calamity of wind; the fourth dhyāna has no inner trouble, and so also has no outer calamity). "Yet those vessels are not permanent, because they arise and perish together with the sentient" (those vessels are the dependent reward; though it says "unmoving," they are not dharmas that abide constantly and do not decay. "The sentient" is the principal reward of sentient beings: the palaces of those heavens arise when the sentient beings arise and perish when the sentient beings perish).

Ru-Yi Translation

"There must be seven fires and one water; after seven waters and fires, wind" (below the first dhyāna there are seven calamities of fire, that is, seven rounds of formation, abiding, destruction, and emptiness, amounting to seven great eons; the eighth, the calamity of water, reaches the second dhyāna, again one round of formation and destruction, amounting to eight great eons. In this way, below the first dhyāna there are seven times seven, forty-nine fires, and below the second dhyāna seven rounds of the calamity of water, making fifty-six eons. Below the first dhyāna there are again seven calamities of fire, amounting to sixty-three eons. In the sixty-fourth eon the calamity of wind destroys up to the third dhyāna. Further, the calamity of fire arises from seven suns appearing together: while the eon is not yet destroyed, six suns are hidden below the Yugaṃdhara Mountains; when the world is destroyed, the latter six suns gradually come out. The calamity of water arises from rain pouring without end. The calamity of wind arises from winds striking one another: from the wind-wheel below a fierce wind arises; further, when the power of karma is exhausted, wind arises everywhere).

At first [they are] like five, up to ten [years old]; in [the realm of] form they are complete, and have clothes" (Fuxing 6A (2) says: "Those dwelling on the earth [unite] by bodily union; the Trāyastriṃśa [gods] do it by wind; the Yāma [gods] embrace; the Tuṣita [gods] hold hands; the [gods of] Delight in Transformation gaze and smile; the [gods of Mastery over] Others' Transformations only gaze" (end of quotation). When the six desire heavens are born by transformation, [those of] the heaven of the Four Heavenly Kings are like five-year-old children, and so on up to [those of Mastery over] Others' Transformations, who are like ten-year-old children; afterward they gradually grow to their full stature. In the form realm, at first birth the stature is already complete and they are fully clothed in heavenly garments; for example, [the gods of] the heaven of Brahmā's Retinue are half a yojana tall at first birth).

Ru-Yi Translation

"The heaven of the Four Heavenly Kings": in the east is the Heavenly King Dhṛtarāṣṭra, here called "Upholder of the Country," because he protects and upholds the land; he dwells on the golden terrace and leads the gandharvas and pūtanas. In the south is the Heavenly King Virūḍhaka, here called "Increase," because he causes the good roots of others to increase; he dwells on the beryl terrace and leads the kumbhāṇḍas and pretas. The king has ninety-one sons (as in the Record of Spiritual Responses). In the west is the Heavenly King Virūpākṣa, here called "Broad Eyes," also called "Not a Good Reward," also called "Mixed Speech," because he can make all kinds of speech; he dwells on the silver terrace and leads the piśācas, the poisonous dragons, and others. In the north is the Heavenly King Vaiśravaṇa, here called "Much Hearing," because the fame of his merit and virtue is heard in the four directions; he dwells on the crystal terrace and leads the yakṣas and rākṣasas. The establishment everywhere of [shrines to] the heavenly kings to take charge of affairs is seen in the first year of Tianbao of the Tang, as in the Brief History of the Saṅgha.

俱舍頌云妙高層有四相去各十千（梵語蘇迷盧亦云須彌此云妙高妙謂四實所成高謂出眾山表始從水際至山半腹分為四級相云各十千由旬）旁出十六千八四二千量（此橫廣也旁即是橫最下一層廣十六千踰𮜖那第二層八千第三層四千第四層二千也）堅首及持鬘桓憍大王眾如次居四級（堅首第三皆藥叉眾大王即四天王堅首最下級乃至四王居最上級）亦住餘七山（諸藥又眾亦住餘七金山是四王所部眾故）

Ru-Yi Translation

A verse of the Kośa says: "Wondrous Height has four tiers, each ten thousand apart" (the Sanskrit Sumeru, also called Xumi, is here called "Wondrous Height": "wondrous" means made of the four treasures; "high" means rising above all the mountains. From the water's edge to halfway up the mountain it is divided into four levels, each ten thousand yojanas apart). "Projecting sideways sixteen thousand, eight, four, and two thousand in measure" (this is the horizontal breadth; "sideways" means horizontal. The lowest tier is sixteen thousand yojanas broad, the second tier eight thousand, the third tier four thousand, the fourth tier two thousand). "The Firm Heads, the Garland Holders, the Ever Proud, and the hosts of the Great Kings dwell in order on the four levels" (the first three, the Firm Heads and the rest, are all hosts of yakṣas; the Great Kings are the Four Heavenly Kings. The Firm Heads are on the lowest level, and so on, the Four Kings dwelling on the highest level). "They also dwell on the other seven mountains" (the hosts of yakṣas also dwell on the other seven golden mountains, because they are the hosts under the Four Kings).

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the palace-cities of the sun and moon are held up by five winds (first, holding; second, nourishing; third, receiving; fourth, turning; fifth, regulating). At the level of the summit of the Yugaṃdhara Mountains, they circle around the slopes of the mountain and illumine the four continents. The Yugaṃdhara Mountains are forty-two thousand yojanas high. Sumeru, the seven golden mountains, and the Iron Ring Mountains all go down into the water eighty-four thousand yojanas; Sumeru also rises out of the water eighty-four thousand yojanas; the other eight mountains decrease by half each time, down to the Iron Ring Mountains, which are three hundred twenty-eight yojanas and two and a half krośas high. The breadth of the nine mountains is in each case equal to the height (krośa is here called two li). The sun's palace is fifty-one yojanas broad, and the moon's palace fifty yojanas broad. A verse of the Kośa says: "Midnight, sunset, noon, and sunrise are the same [moment] in the four continents" (the Fuxing cites Dīrgha Āgama 22: "When it is midnight in Uttarakuru, it is sunset in Pūrvavideha, noon in Jambudvīpa, and sunrise in Godānīya." The sūtra text speaks in order of all four directions. That sūtra also says: "Jambudvīpa is east, Pūrvavideha is west," and so on, "Uttarakuru is west, Pūrvavideha is east"; because the sun and moon revolve, everyone calls the place of sunrise east (end of quotation)). "Near the sun it is covered by its own shadow, hence one sees the disk of the moon deficient" (as with the new moon, the west is near the sun and its own shadow covers the east, so one sees the east deficient; with the waning moon, the east is near the sun and its own shadow covers the west, so one sees the west deficient).

Ru-Yi Translation

"Trāyastriṃśa" is here called "Thirty-Three." At the four corners of the mountain summit there are eight palaces each, with the palace of Śakra in the center. In a former life thirty-three people, with the heavenly emperor as their leader, cultivated superior karma in the country of Magadha, and so were born here together. The fourth [fascicle] of the Gates of Dhyāna makes clear three kinds of greed. First, outer greed: men and women greedily attached to each other's bodies; it is cured with the contemplation of the nine perceptions (contemplating others' bodies). The nine perceptions are (first, bloating; second, bluish discoloration; third, decay; fourth, smearing with blood; fifth, pus and rot; sixth, being eaten; seventh, scattering; eighth, white bones; ninth, burning).
