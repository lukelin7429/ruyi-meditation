Samantabhadra's Encouragement

This page completes Ru-Yi's own English translation of Zhiyi's Fahua Wenju (妙法蓮華經文句). Fascicle 10 is the most chapter-dense in the entire treatise: it closes 如來壽量品 and then moves through nine more chapters in succession — Discrimination of Merits, the Merit of Rejoicing, the Merits of a Teacher of the Dharma, Never Disparaging, the Tathāgata's Supernatural Power, Entrustment, Bhaiṣajyarāja, Gadgadasvara, Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate, Dhāraṇīs, King Wonderful Adornment, and finally Samantabhadra's Encouragement — closing out the entire Lotus Sūtra and Zhiyi's commentary on it together. We translate slowly, section by section, with the Chinese source beside the English so readers can study the meaning, not merely read around it.

The Remaining Sons Awaken, and the Verse Close of the Chapter 餘子醒悟 · 壽量品偈頌

Chinese Source

◎從「餘失心者」下，譬上非滅現滅。上有二：一、不久應死，譬上非滅現滅；二、諸子醒悟，譬上唱滅利益。又唱死之由，由子不服，譬上薄德見佛常在但增憍恣。上現滅中有二：一、本實不滅，二、非滅唱滅，不出現滅之由由，亦是唱滅利益中。今譬不譬第一，而明現滅之由，正由眾生薄德見佛不修行，即是不肯服藥也。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "as for the remaining sons who had lost their right mind" onward corresponds to what was said above about not truly ceasing yet manifesting cessation. What was said above fell into two: first, that he would soon have to die, corresponding to not truly ceasing yet manifesting cessation; second, the sons' awakening, corresponding to the benefit of proclaiming extinction. Moreover, the reason for proclaiming his death lies in the sons' refusal to take the medicine, corresponding to what was said above about beings of thin merit who, seeing the Buddha ever abiding, only grow the more prideful and self-indulgent. What was said above concerning manifesting cessation itself fell into two: first, that in reality he never truly ceases; second, not truly ceasing yet proclaiming cessation — this does not go beyond the reason for manifesting cessation, and is likewise included within the benefit of proclaiming extinction. Here, the parable does not represent the first of these, but instead makes clear the reason for manifesting cessation: precisely that beings of thin merit, seeing the Buddha, fail to cultivate practice — that is, they are simply unwilling to take the medicine.

「我今當設」下，正唱應死，譬非滅唱滅。此中明衰老為二：一、擬宜去住，譬上住有損、滅有益；二、「即作是言」下，唱應死，正譬現滅化期將竟也。「死時已至」者，當入涅槃也。留經教在，故云「是好良藥今留在此」。「復至他國」者，即是此方現滅、他方現生，上文云「願在他方遙見守護」，即其義也。「遣使」者，或取涅槃中大聲普告為使人，或用神通，或用舍利，或用經教等為使人，今用四依菩薩語眾生云佛已滅度，但留此法我今宣弘，汝當受行也。

From "I shall now devise" onward is the actual proclamation that he must die, corresponding to not truly ceasing yet proclaiming cessation. Within this, the account of his growing old and feeble falls into two: first, weighing what is fitting between remaining and departing, corresponding to what was said above about remaining bringing harm and ceasing bringing benefit; second, from "he then spoke these words" onward, the proclamation that he must die, corresponding precisely to the manifesting of cessation as the period of transformation draws to its close. "The time of death has now arrived" means that he is about to enter nirvāṇa. That he leaves the teaching of the sūtras behind is why it is said, "this good and efficacious medicine I now leave here." "He went again to another land" means precisely that, in this world, extinction is manifested, while, in another world, birth is manifested — as the text said above, "I vow to be in another region, watching over and protecting from afar," which is exactly this meaning. As for "he sent a messenger": this may be taken as the great proclamation made throughout at the time of the parinirvāṇa serving as the messenger, or the use of supernatural power, or the use of the relics, or the use of the sūtra teachings, and so on, each serving as the messenger. Here, it is the bodhisattvas of the four kinds of reliance who tell beings that the Buddha has already entered extinction, but that he has left this Dharma behind, and that they themselves now proclaim and spread it widely, and that beings should receive and practice it.

後時眾生若無四依傳述經法，豈能自知佛已滅度？故用四依是使人也。二、「是時諸子」下，諸子醒悟譬現滅利益。上文有二：一、明損益，二、釋損益，今但譬得益。此中「自惟孤露」下，明滅後得益，如優波笈多所化之流也。又為二：一、現滅利益，二、是未來機感。良由滅後眾生醒悟服藥修行以作因緣，能感未來應化，如《遺教》云「其未度者作得度因緣」；亦有現得感見，《普賢觀》云「精進苦到得見釋迦分身、多寶、東力、善德等及七佛世尊」。

For later beings, without the four kinds of reliance to transmit and expound the sūtra teaching, how could they know of themselves that the Buddha has already entered extinction? Hence it is the four kinds of reliance who serve as the messenger. Second, from "at that time, the sons" onward, the sons' awakening corresponds to the benefit of manifesting cessation. What was said above fell into two: first, making clear the harm and the benefit; second, explaining the harm and the benefit — here, however, the parable expresses only the attaining of benefit. Within this, from "reflecting on their own solitary and unprotected state" onward makes clear the benefit obtained after his extinction, as with the multitude transformed by Upagupta. This itself falls into two: first, the benefit of manifesting cessation; second, the stimulus for future ages. It is precisely because, after the extinction, beings awaken, take the medicine, and cultivate practice, thereby forming causes and conditions, that they are able to call forth a future response and transformation — as the Testament Sūtra says, "for those not yet delivered, this becomes the cause and condition for their deliverance." There are also those who, in the present, attain the stimulus and see the response directly — the Contemplation of Samantabhadra Sūtra says, "through diligent and arduous striving, they attain sight of Śākyamuni's emanation-bodies, of Prabhūtaratna, of Dongli and Shande" (unidentified buddha-names in the source text), "and of the buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, of the seven [past buddhas]."

第三，「其父聞子悉已得差」下，即是未來益物威猛之力。父聞子差即機咸使見之，即是起未來之應化，方將形聲兩益也，如文。從「諸善男子於意云何」下，牒不虛譬，明三世利益不虛也。從「佛言我亦如是」去，明合譬也。「成佛已來無量劫」者，合過去世也。「方便力言當滅度」，合現在世也。文略不合未來，亦無能說我虛妄，合益物不虛也。

Third, from "the father, hearing that his sons had all recovered" onward is precisely the mighty and forceful power of benefiting beings in the future. The father, hearing that his sons had recovered, at once causes their capacities collectively to see him — this is precisely the arising of the response and transformation of the future, about to bring the twofold benefit of form and sound together, exactly as the text has it. From "good sons, what do you think" onward restates the parable of never being false, making clear that the benefit across the three periods of time is never false. From "the Buddha said, I too am like this" onward makes clear the correlation of the parable. "Since I attained buddhahood, it has been immeasurable kalpas" corresponds to the past age. "Through the power of skillful means I speak of entering extinction" corresponds to the present age. The text, in brief, does not correlate the future, nor is there anyone able to say that I am false — this corresponds to the benefiting of beings being never false.

偈二十五行半，頌上法譬。初二十行半，頌法說；次五行，頌譬說。上法說有二，今頌亦二：初十九行半，頌三世益物；次，一行，頌皆實不虛。初四行，頌過去世益物為三：初一行，頌上成道已久；次「常說法」下，第二，一行，頌上中間益物；次「為度眾生」下，第三，二行，頌上住處。次「眾見我」下，第二，五行，頌現在。上文有二：初二行半，頌非生現生；次「我時語」下，第二，二行半，頌非滅現滅。次「我見諸」下，第三，十行半，頌上未來。上但寄「常住不滅」四字，今頌則廣。文為四：初「我見」下一行半，明未來機應。次「神通力如是」下，第二，四行，頌上常住不滅。「常在靈鷲山」，此謂實報土也，「及餘諸住處」者，謂方便有餘土也，即上餘國義也。「天人充滿」者，三十心是「人」，十地是「天」。「擊天鼓」者，無問自說也。「曼陀羅華」者，說賢聖位也。次「我淨土」下，第三，二行，明不見因緣。次「諸有修功德」下，第四，三行，明得見因緣。次「汝等有智」下，第二，一行，頌上利益不虛也。

The verse section, twenty-five and a half lines, restates the prose exposition and the parable set out above. The first twenty and a half lines restate the prose exposition; the next five lines restate the parable. The prose exposition above fell into two, and the verse likewise falls into two: the first nineteen and a half lines restate the benefiting of beings across the three periods of time; the next one line restates that everything is entirely true, never false. The first four lines, restating the benefiting of beings in the past age, fall into three: the first line restates the attaining of the path long ago; next, from "ever expounding the Dharma" onward, the second, one line, restates the benefiting of beings in between; next, from "in order to deliver beings" onward, the third, two lines, restate the dwelling-place spoken of above. Next, from "the assembly, seeing me" onward, the second part, five lines, restates the present age. What was said above fell into two: the first two and a half lines restate not truly being born yet manifesting birth; next, from "at that time I said" onward, the second, two and a half lines, restate not truly ceasing yet manifesting cessation. Next, from "I see all the" onward, the third, ten and a half lines, restate the future spoken of above. What was said above rested on only the four characters "ever abiding, never ceasing," but the verse here expands on this at length. The text falls into four parts: first, from "I see" onward, one and a half lines, making clear the future stimulus and response; next, from "such is the power of my supernatural faculties" onward, the second, four lines, restate the ever-abiding, never-ceasing spoken of above. "Ever dwelling on Vulture Peak" refers to the land of true reward; "and in all my other dwelling-places" refers to the land of skillful means with residue — this is precisely the meaning of "other lands" spoken of above. "Filled with gods and humans" — the thirty minds [of the ten faiths, ten abodes, and ten transferences] are the "humans," while the ten grounds are the "gods." "Beating the heavenly drum" means speaking without being asked. "Mandārava flowers" refers to expounding the ranks of the worthies and the saints. Next, from "my pure land" onward, the third, two lines, make clear the causes and conditions for not seeing him; next, from "all those who cultivate merit and virtue" onward, the fourth, three lines, make clear the causes and conditions for attaining sight of him. Next, from "you who have wisdom" onward, the second part, one line, restates that the benefit spoken of above is never false.

次，五行，頌譬，為二：一行頌開譬，為三：初一句，頌過去；「為治」下二句，頌現在，不頌未來也，「無能說」下，第三，一句，頌不虛。「後我亦為」下，第二，四行，頌合譬。上合中本不合未來，今初半行頌合過去；次「凡夫」下，第二，二行半，頌合現在；後「每自作」下，第三，一行，頌合不虛，開三顯一、開近顯遠，欲令眾生速入佛道，此事必得不虛也。

Next, the five lines restating the parable fall into two: one line restates the parable set out, and itself falls into three — the first phrase restates the past; the two phrases from "in order to cure" onward restate the present, without restating the future; the third, one phrase, from "none can say" onward, restates never being false. From "afterward, I too, for the sake of" onward, the second, four lines, restate the correlation of the parable. In the correlation above, the future was originally not correlated; here, the first half-line restates the correlation with the past; next, from "ordinary beings" onward, the second, two and a half lines, restate the correlation with the present; and finally, from "ever making myself out to be" onward, the third, one line, restates the correlation with never being false — opening the three to reveal the one, and opening the near to reveal the distant, all in order to cause beings swiftly to enter the buddha-path; this matter must indeed prove never false.

"Discrimination of Merits" Opens: Guangzhai's Scheme and the Fahua Lun 釋分別功德品 · 光宅與《法華論》之判

Chinese Source

釋分別功德品 佛說壽量，二世弟子得種種益，故言「功德」，淺深不同故，言「分別品」也。此文是本門第二授記段，論分此文，有法力、修行力。法力者有五：一證，二信，三供養，皆如今品，四聞法，如〈隨喜品〉，五讀誦持說，讀誦如〈法師功德品〉，持者追指〈法師〉、〈安樂行〉、〈勸持〉三品，說者如〈神力〉、〈囑累品〉也。修行力者，苦行力如〈藥王〉，教化如〈妙音〉，護難如〈觀音〉、〈陀羅尼〉，示功德如〈妙莊嚴王〉，護法如〈普賢品〉也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining "Discrimination of Merits." Once the Buddha has expounded his lifespan, disciples of both the present and the future ages obtain various kinds of benefit — hence it is called "merits"; and since these benefits are not uniform in depth, it is called the "Discrimination" chapter. This text is the second section of prediction within the origin gate. Discussing the divisions of this text, there is the power of the Dharma and the power of practice. The power of the Dharma has five aspects: first, realization; second, faith; third, offering — all three as in the present chapter; fourth, hearing the Dharma, as in "The Merit of Rejoicing"; fifth, reading, reciting, upholding, and expounding — reading and reciting as in "The Merits of a Teacher of the Dharma," upholding referring ahead to the three chapters "Teacher of the Dharma," "Peaceful Practices," and "Encouraging the Upholding of This Sūtra," and expounding as in "Supernatural Powers" and "Entrustment." The power of practice: the power of ascetic practice, as in "Medicine King"; teaching and transformation, as in "Wondrous Sound"; protecting against calamity, as in "Perceiver of the World's Sounds" and "Dhāraṇī"; demonstrating merit, as in "King Wonderful Adornment"; and protecting the Dharma, as in "Samantabhadra."

光宅云：「一約功德門，記其行進現在修因；二約智慧門，記其損生未來得果；三明外凡發心，夫授記通因果，此三通是授記耳。下八世界發心，應在初列，以外凡聞經發心住三十心；三十心聞經始出內凡登於初地得無生忍；初地聞經進入二地得聞持；二地聞經得入三地，名樂說辯；三地聞經入四地，得無量旋；四地聞經入五地，名不退；五地入六地，名清淨輪，小千已去約損生門授記；七地已斷無明惑，惑有九品，能為九生作因；從七地已上，果報無有期限，難可得判，但斷九品煩惱為言。煩惱品數百千萬種，今約一種九品作義。七地所斷，作下上二品，乃至十地所斷，亦作上下二品，金剛心所斷作一品，六地聞經登七地下忍斷一品，所餘八品在，為八生作因，故云八生當得菩提。七地上忍復斷一品，即損二品生，餘七品為七生作因，七生當得菩提。諸品例有上下，一品盡損一生，文略，故從八地乃至四生，餘有一生，即金剛心斷之法也。」

Guangzhai says: "First, in terms of the gate of merit, [the text] predicts their advance in practice and their present cultivation of causes; second, in terms of the gate of wisdom, it predicts their diminishing of birth and their future attainment of fruit; third, it makes clear the ordinary worldling's arousing of the aspiration [for awakening]. Now, prediction runs through both cause and fruit, and these three are, in every case, instances of prediction. The arousing of aspiration by beings of the eight worlds mentioned below ought properly to stand at the head of the list, since it is the ordinary worldling, upon hearing the sūtra, who arouses the aspiration and abides in the thirty minds; having heard the sūtra at the thirty minds, one first emerges from the ranks of the ordinary worldling and ascends to the first ground, attaining the forbearance of non-arising; hearing the sūtra at the first ground, one advances into the second ground and attains retention of what is heard; hearing the sūtra at the second ground, one is able to enter the third ground, named eloquence in preaching; hearing the sūtra at the third ground, one enters the fourth ground, attaining the immeasurable dhāraṇī; hearing the sūtra at the fourth ground, one enters the fifth ground, named non-retrogression; from the fifth ground one enters the sixth ground, named the pure wheel — from the 'small chiliocosm' onward, this is prediction in terms of the gate of the diminishing of birth. Once at the seventh ground, one has already cut off the affliction of fundamental ignorance; this affliction has nine grades, and is able to serve as the cause for nine further births. From the seventh ground upward, the fruit of reward has no fixed limit and is difficult to determine precisely, but one may speak simply in terms of cutting off the nine grades of affliction. The grades of affliction number in the hundreds of thousands upon thousands of kinds; here, we take the scheme of nine grades within a single kind as our basis. What is cut off at the seventh ground forms a lower and an upper grade, and so on up through what is cut off at the tenth ground, which likewise forms an upper and a lower grade; what is cut off by the vajra-mind forms a single grade. Hearing the sūtra at the sixth ground and ascending to the seventh ground's lower forbearance, one cuts off one grade, leaving the remaining eight grades to serve as the cause for eight further births — hence it is said that after eight births one will attain bodhi. At the seventh ground's upper forbearance, one cuts off yet another grade, thereby diminishing two grades of birth, and the remaining seven grades serve as the cause for seven further births, so that after seven births one will attain bodhi. Each of these grades, by analogy, has an upper and a lower division, and each single grade cut off entirely diminishes one birth; the text is abbreviated, and so, from the eighth ground onward through four births, down to the remaining one birth, this is precisely the method by which the vajra-mind cuts it off."

《法華論》云「得無生忍者，謂入初地證智，應知從八生乃至一生得菩提者，謂諸凡夫決定能證初地隨分生及一生則證菩提。菩提者，離三界分段生死，隨分能見真如佛性，名得菩提，非謂究竟滿足菩提。」

The Fahua Lun [Treatise on the Lotus Sūtra] says: "Attaining the forbearance of non-arising means entering the first ground and realizing that wisdom. It should be understood that 'from eight births down to one birth, attaining bodhi' means that ordinary worldlings, once determined, are able to realize the first ground, partially attaining birth after birth until, in one further birth, they realize bodhi. 'Bodhi' here means being freed from the fragmentary birth-and-death of the three realms and partially able to see the buddha-nature of true suchness — this is named 'attaining bodhi,' not the ultimate and complete fulfillment of bodhi."

今謂《論》前深後淺，光宅前淺後深，二彼相拒世孰判之？夫無生法忍經論不同，迦旃延子明五法成就獲不退轉，六度菩薩位也；須陀洹若智若斷是菩薩無生法忍者，三乘共位也；登初地得無生忍者，別菩薩位也；登初住得無生法忍者，圓菩薩位也。皆聖教明文，不可參濫。又《淨名》「近無等等得無生忍」，《仁王》云「至金剛頂皆名伏忍，亦名寂滅忍」，蓋是別、圓。地地，通途之意不可定用。即光宅以發心為內凡，三十心位為無生忍，是初地，皆別家名教非通家門戶；從初地至六地，呼為福德門，《大經》稱有為有漏，名聲聞僧，則通教意耳；七地已上斷九品生因者，小乘大乘得入位人，誰不斷惑未足定判，若言七地斷無明者，非通又非別，乃是別接通意耳。光宅游漾，不會今經。天親以發心為無生忍，從八生至一生，凡夫決定斷果報生盡得入初地，獲無生忍者，專據別義，亦不會經。

Now, as I would say: the Treatise moves from the deep to the shallow, while Guangzhai moves from the shallow to the deep — the two stand opposed to one another, and who in the world is to judge between them? As for the forbearance of non-arising, the sūtras and treatises are not in agreement on it. Kātyāyanīputra makes clear that, upon the completion of the five dharmas, one attains non-retrogression — this is the rank of the bodhisattva of the six perfections; the stream-enterer, whether in terms of wisdom or of severance, who is said to have the bodhisattva's forbearance of non-arising, is the rank shared in common by the three vehicles; ascending to the first ground and attaining the forbearance of non-arising is the rank of the bodhisattva of the distinct teaching; ascending to the initial abiding and attaining the forbearance of non-arising is the rank of the bodhisattva of the perfect teaching. All of these are set out explicitly in the sacred teachings, and must not be muddled together indiscriminately. Furthermore, the Vimalakīrti says, "drawing near to the unequaled equal, one attains the forbearance of non-arising," and the Humane Kings Sūtra says, "up to the vajra summit, all are named the subduing forbearance, also named the forbearance of quiescent extinction" — these, too, belong to the distinct and the perfect teachings. As for "ground by ground," this common way of speaking cannot be applied with fixed certainty. Now, Guangzhai takes the arousing of the aspiration as the rank of the inner worldling, and the position of the thirty minds as the forbearance of non-arising, identified with the first ground — these are all terms and doctrines proper to the distinct teaching, not the framework of the shared teaching. From the first ground to the sixth ground he calls the gate of merit and virtue; the Great Sūtra speaks of this as conditioned and subject to outflow, naming it the śrāvaka-saṃgha — this, then, is the sense proper to the shared teaching. As for cutting off, from the seventh ground upward, the nine grades of the cause of birth: among those who attain entry into rank, whether by the lesser or the greater vehicle, who does not cut off affliction? This alone is not enough to settle the matter with certainty. If one says that fundamental ignorance is cut off at the seventh ground, this belongs neither to the shared teaching nor to the distinct teaching, but is rather the sense of the distinct teaching grafted onto the shared. Guangzhai wanders and drifts, and does not meet the intent of the present sūtra. As for "discrimination": the Buddha's words are perfect and sublime, and cannot be explained through the ranks of the provisional teachings. Hence, in the passage above, "opening, showing, awakening to, and entering the buddha's knowledge and vision" [in "Skillful Means"], and here, in the origin gate, "increasing the path and diminishing birth" — both are to be explained solely in terms of the ranks of the perfect teaching. Three births, the ninth ground. Two births, the tenth ground. One birth, and one enters the vajra-mind of the stage of virtual enlightenment. Take, for example, the simile of the moon in the Great Sūtra: from the first day to the fifteenth, its light gradually waxes; from the sixteenth day to the thirtieth, its light gradually wanes — this is speaking of increase and decrease with respect to the single body of the moon, and the simile is speaking of wisdom and severance with respect to the single Dharma-body. It may be that, for one person, in a single lifetime, there are eight successive increases; or it may be one age, or eight ages, or immeasurable ages; it may be one thought-moment, or eight thought-moments, or immeasurable thought-moments; it may even be as numerous as fine motes of dust — and persons, too, are just the same. For this reason, one cannot confine the matter within a fixed scheme of the birth caused and the birth of fruit, nor can one explain it apart from the bow-drawn tension between wisdom and severance. And yet, in the origin gate, the attaining of the path occurs many times over the number found in all the other sūtras — not only is the number great, but the perfuming and cultivation has gone on for so very long: from the very root, the traces have been unfolded and beings guided in place after place, meeting one another again and again in between and ripening time after time; in the present age, through the five flavors [of the teaching], stage by stage they have been tamed and subdued, all gathered up and drawn together, brought at last into the Lotus Sūtra. It is like a farming family: in spring the crop sprouts, in summer it grows, and they plow, sow, weed, and tend it; in autumn they harvest, and in winter they store it away, reaping all at once. Those who have attained the path since the Lotus Sūtra was preached are like those who merely go gleaning afterward.

釋法師功德品 「法師」義，如上說。「功德」者，前謂初品之初功德，今五品之上，謂六根清淨，內外莊嚴五根清淨名外莊嚴；意根清淨名內莊嚴。又從地獄已上至佛而還，一切色像悉身中現者，名內莊嚴；從地獄已上佛已還，一切色像以普現三昧而外化者，名外莊嚴。身根既爾，餘五根亦然。讀誦既爾，四種亦然。初品既爾，四品加然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining "The Merits of a Teacher of the Dharma." The sense of "teacher of the Dharma" has been explained above. As for "merits": what was spoken of earlier was the merit of the very beginning of the first rank; here, it is that which stands above the five ranks, referring to the purification of the six sense-faculties, adorned both inwardly and outwardly — the purification of the five outer sense-faculties is named outward adornment, and the purification of the mind faculty is named inward adornment. Furthermore, that all forms and images, from the hells up through the Buddha and back again, manifest within one's own body, is named inward adornment; that all forms and images, from the hells up through the Buddha and back again, are transformed outwardly by means of the samādhi of universal manifestation, is named outward adornment. As it is for the body faculty, so it is for the remaining five faculties as well. As it is for reading and reciting, so it is for the other three of the four kinds of practice with the sūtra as well. As it is for the first rank, so it is, all the more, for the four later ranks as well.

相似既爾，分真倍然。行者聞說此功德利喜不自勝，勤求無厭信進倍增，明識大乘有大勢力決無疑網，似解之初初，過二乘之極極百千萬倍，指始顯終懸解究竟第一義諦不可思議。此品所明備斯四意，故言「法師功德品」也。六根功德者，光宅云：「三業合十善，一善具十為百，自行、化他、隨喜、讚歎合四百，約五種法師為二千，三品分之即六千功德，此土三根用弱奪言八百，三根用強與言千二，與奪合論還是六千也。」有人明數與光宅同，下品八百、中品一千、上品千二百。諸師偏釋未會今經，亦不合諸教。

As it is for the rank of approximation, so it is, twice over, for the rank of partial truth. The practitioner, hearing this merit expounded, is filled with joy beyond containing, seeking it diligently without ever tiring, his faith and his progress redoubled, clearly recognizing that the Great Vehicle possesses a mighty power, and resolutely free of the net of doubt; and, at the very outset of approximate understanding, he already surpasses the utmost limit of the two lesser vehicles by a hundred thousand myriad times over, pointing from the very beginning toward the final revelation, resolving from afar the ultimate and inconceivable truth of ultimate meaning. This chapter, in what it makes clear, is complete with these four senses, and hence it is named "The Merits of a Teacher of the Dharma." As for the merit of the six sense-faculties, Guangzhai says: "The three karmic activities together comprise the ten good deeds; one good deed, complete in its ten aspects, makes a hundred; self-practice, transforming others, rejoicing, and praising, taken together, make four hundred; taking this in terms of the five kinds of teacher of the Dharma gives two thousand; and dividing this among the three ranks gives, in all, six thousand meritorious qualities. In this world, since the use of the three lesser sense-faculties is weak, the number is diminished and it is said to be eight hundred; since the use of the three greater sense-faculties is strong, the number is augmented and it is said to be twelve hundred — taken together, augmented and diminished, it still comes to six thousand." There are those who explain the number in the same way as Guangzhai: the lower rank, eight hundred; the middle rank, one thousand; the upper rank, twelve hundred. The various masters' one-sided explanations fail to meet the present sūtra, and also do not accord with the other teachings.

《大品》云「色淨故般若淨，般若淨故色淨，色淨五根淨，般若淨意根淨」，若六根等，云何判上中下、強弱用耶？若一強一不強，一淨一不淨，一上品餘非上品(云云)。《正法華》整足具六千功德，不論上中下(云云)。《法華論》云「凡人以經力故得勝根用，雖未入初地，以父母所生肉眼，見大千內外也。」《大經》云「如來一根則能見色、聞聲、嗅香、別味、覺觸、知法」，今經六根清淨與《大品》同；以是功德莊嚴六根，與《正法華》同，鼻見色聞聲覺知與《涅槃》同，肉眼有天眼慧法佛眼等用與《論》同，文義如此，不可以偏見抑正經。今當說之，光宅數整足根不依文，今按三業安樂行即有十善，一善有十即百善，一善中有十如即千善，就化他為二千，約如來室、如來衣、如來座，即成六千，五種法師悉具六根清淨，一一根皆有一千功德也。

The Greater [Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra] says, "because form is pure, prajñā is pure; because prajñā is pure, form is pure; form being pure, the five outer sense-faculties are pure; prajñā being pure, the mind faculty is pure" — if the six sense-faculties are thus equal to one another, how, then, can one judge them as upper, middle, and lower, or as of strong and weak function? If one were strong and another not, one pure and another not, one of the upper rank and the rest not of the upper rank, this would not make sense (and so on). The Zhengfahua gives the full and complete six thousand meritorious qualities, without distinguishing upper, middle, and lower (and so on). The Fahua Lun says, "the ordinary person, through the power of the sūtra, attains a superior function of the sense-faculties; though not yet having entered the first ground, with the very physical eye born of father and mother, he sees within and beyond the great chiliocosm." The Great Sūtra says, "in the Tathāgata, a single sense-faculty is able to see form, hear sound, smell fragrance, distinguish flavor, feel touch, and know dharmas" — the present sūtra's purification of the six sense-faculties accords with the Greater [Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra]; that this merit adorns the six sense-faculties accords with the Zhengfahua; that the nose sees form, hears sound, and perceives and knows accords with the Nirvāṇa Sūtra; that the physical eye possesses the function of the heavenly eye, the wisdom eye, the Dharma eye, and the buddha eye accords with the Treatise. Since the text and its meaning are thus, one must not, through a one-sided view, force down the correct meaning of this sūtra. I shall now explain it: Guangzhai's number, though giving the sense-faculties in full, does not follow the text closely. Here, I take it that, within the peaceful practices, the three karmic activities already possess the ten good deeds; one good deed having ten aspects makes a hundred good deeds; and within one good deed there are the ten suchnesses, making a thousand good deeds; taking this in terms of transforming others gives two thousand; and, in terms of the Tathāgata's room, the Tathāgata's robe, and the Tathāgata's seat, this comes to six thousand — the five kinds of teacher of the Dharma each possess the purification of the six sense-faculties in full, and each and every sense-faculty possesses a full thousand meritorious qualities.

復次一心中具十法界，一一界皆有十如，即成一百，一根通取六塵，即有六百，約定慧二莊嚴，即是一千二百，根根悉用定慧莊嚴等千二百也。若論六根清淨，清淨則不言功德若少若多，若言莊嚴能盈能縮能等等莊嚴者，根根六千，若言千二顯其能盈，若言八百顯其能縮，若言清淨無盈無縮無等，六根互用根自在故、不可思議故。若偏判者則失旨也。相似之位，若依四輪即鐵輪位也；若依五十二位，即十信心也；若依《仁王》即十善大心也。今對常精進者，即十信之第三心也。諸經名目雖異，同是圓教相似位耳。

Furthermore, within a single mind are contained the ten dharma-realms, and each and every realm possesses the ten suchnesses, which comes to a hundred; a single sense-faculty, taking in all six sense-objects, gives six hundred; and, in terms of the twofold adornment of concentration and wisdom, this comes to twelve hundred — each and every sense-faculty, employing the adornment of concentration and wisdom equally, likewise has twelve hundred. If one discusses the purification of the six sense-faculties, purity itself does not speak of merit as being more or less; but if one speaks of adornment, which is able to expand, able to contract, and able to remain equal — equally adorning — then each and every sense-faculty has six thousand; if one says twelve hundred, this shows its capacity to expand; if one says eight hundred, this shows its capacity to contract; and if one says merely pure, this is without expansion, without contraction, and without any measure at all — for the six sense-faculties function reciprocally, since the sense-faculties are self-sovereign, and since this is inconceivable. Judged one-sidedly, one loses the point entirely. As for the rank of approximation: according to the fourfold wheel, it is the rank of the iron wheel; according to the fifty-two ranks, it is the mind of the ten stages of faith; according to the Humane Kings Sūtra, it is the great mind of the ten good deeds. Set against "ever-diligent [bodhisattva]" here, it is the third mind of the ten stages of faith. Though the various sūtras' designations differ, they are, alike, the rank of approximation within the perfect teaching.

The Six Sense-Faculty Chapters: Eye, Ear, Nose, Tongue, Body, and Mind 六根章：眼耳鼻舌身意

Chinese Source

文為二：初、總列六根盈縮功德數，次、別作六章解釋。各有長行、偈頌。眼根章明父母所生名肉眼，而所見過於天眼。梵王報得天眼，在己界遍見大千，大千外有風輪與眼作障不能見外，若在他界則不遍見大千，非所統故。小羅漢見小千，大羅漢見大千，辟支佛見百佛世界，不以風輪為礙，亦無己他界隔。今經論眼，能見大千內外應是天眼，那名肉眼？此是圓教似位。因經之力有勝根用，既未發真不得稱天眼，猶名肉眼，例如小乘方便未得神通則不稱天眼耳。猶是分段之身，故稱父母所生。雖稱肉眼，具五眼用：見大千內外，天眼用；見一切眾生及業因緣，法眼用；其目甚清淨，慧眼用；一時悉見大千內外見業見淨，又圓伏法界上惑，佛眼用。

Ru-Yi Translation

The text falls into two: first, a general listing of the numbers of merit, expandable and contractible, of the six sense-faculties; second, a separate explanation given in six sections, each with both a prose section and a verse. The section on the eye faculty makes clear that what is "born of father and mother" is named the physical eye, and yet what it sees surpasses the heavenly eye. The Brahmā king, through the fruit of his reward, obtains the heavenly eye, and, within his own realm, sees the great chiliocosm entirely; but outside the great chiliocosm there is the wheel of wind, which obstructs the eye so that he cannot see beyond, and were he in another realm he would not see the great chiliocosm entirely either, since it does not fall under his dominion. The lesser arhat sees the small chiliocosm; the greater arhat sees the great chiliocosm; the pratyekabuddha sees a hundred buddha-worlds, unobstructed by the wheel of wind and without any barrier between his own realm and another's. Now, the present sūtra, discussing the eye, says that it is able to see within and beyond the great chiliocosm — this ought properly to be the heavenly eye; how, then, can it be named the physical eye? This is because it is the rank of approximation within the perfect teaching: through the power of the sūtra, one has a superior function of the sense-faculty, yet, not having yet given rise to the true wisdom, one cannot properly be said to have the heavenly eye, and it is still named the physical eye — just as, in the lesser vehicle's stage of preparatory expedients, before one has attained the supernatural faculties, one is likewise not said to have the heavenly eye. It is still the body of fragmentary birth-and-death, and hence it is called "born of father and mother." Though it is called the physical eye, it possesses the function of all five eyes: seeing within and beyond the great chiliocosm is the function of the heavenly eye; seeing all beings and the causes and conditions of their karma is the function of the Dharma eye; its vision being exceedingly pure is the function of the wisdom eye; seeing, all at once, within and beyond the great chiliocosm, seeing karma and seeing purity, and, further, perfectly subduing the afflictions throughout the dharma-realm, is the function of the buddha eye.

《大經》云「雖有肉眼名為佛眼」，佛眼故名清淨，具五眼故故言莊嚴，能盈能縮名勝根用，名根自在，豈可祇作八百千二百解耶？

The Great Sūtra says, "though possessing the physical eye, it is named the buddha eye" — because it is the buddha eye, it is named pure; because it possesses all five eyes, it is called adorned; its capacity to expand and to contract is named the superior function of the sense-faculty, named the self-sovereignty of the sense-faculty — how, then, could it possibly be explained merely as eight hundred or twelve hundred?

耳根章遍聞大千內外十法界音聲，聞六道即肉天二耳，聞二乘即慧耳，聞菩薩即法耳，聞佛即佛耳。又父母所生肉耳，能聞內外即天耳，聽之不著即慧耳，不謬即法耳，一時互聞即佛耳。以耳例眼眼亦如是，見人天是二眼，見二乘是慧眼，見菩薩是法眼，見佛即佛眼(云云)。

The section on the ear faculty makes clear that it universally hears the sounds of the ten dharma-realms within and beyond the great chiliocosm: hearing the six destinies is the physical and heavenly ears together; hearing the two lesser vehicles is the wisdom ear; hearing the bodhisattvas is the Dharma ear; hearing the Buddha is the buddha ear. Furthermore, the physical ear born of father and mother, being able to hear within and beyond, is the heavenly ear; hearing without attachment is the wisdom ear; hearing without error is the Dharma ear; hearing all at once and reciprocally is the buddha ear. Taking the ear as a parallel for the eye, the eye is likewise so: seeing humans and gods is the twofold eye [physical and heavenly]; seeing the two lesser vehicles is the wisdom eye; seeing the bodhisattvas is the Dharma eye; seeing the Buddha is the buddha eye (and so on).

鼻根章亦如是，父母所生即肉鼻，大千內外即天鼻，不染不著即慧鼻，分別不謬即法鼻，一時互用即佛鼻。此章明互用者，鼻知好惡別貴賤，覩天宮莊嚴等，則鼻有眼用；讀經說法聞香能知，鼻有耳用；諸樹花果實及蘇油香氣，鼻有舌用；入禪出禪禪有八觸故，五欲嬉戲亦是觸法，鼻有身用；染欲癡恚心亦知修善者，鼻有意用。鼻根自在勝用若茲，例五根亦如是。

The section on the nose faculty is likewise so: what is born of father and mother is the physical nose; within and beyond the great chiliocosm is the heavenly nose; without defilement and without attachment is the wisdom nose; discerning without error is the Dharma nose; functioning all at once and reciprocally is the buddha nose. This section makes clear the reciprocal function: the nose, knowing what is good and what is repugnant, distinguishing the noble and the base, and even beholding the adornments of the heavenly palaces and the like — in this the nose has the function of the eye; reading the sūtras and expounding the Dharma while smelling a fragrance and knowing thereby — in this the nose has the function of the ear; the fragrance of every kind of tree, flower, and fruit, and of butter and oil — in this the nose has the function of the tongue; entering and emerging from meditative absorption, since absorption has its eight tactile sensations, and the sporting of the five desires likewise being a tactile phenomenon — in this the nose has the function of the body; and knowing, too, a mind defiled by desire, folly, or anger, as well as one that cultivates good — in this the nose has the function of the mind. The nose faculty's self-sovereign and superior function is thus; and, by analogy, the other five faculties are likewise so.

舌根章亦如是，父母所生即是肉舌，能作十法界語，約此即是五舌義明矣，能作十法界語即天舌，不壞即慧舌，不謬即法舌，一時互用即佛舌(云云)。問：苦澁惡味至舌皆變成上味，眾色到眼何不變成妙色？舊不例，味有損益，損者變，不損者不變，諸色不壞眼故不例。今解不爾，一切色同佛色，一切聲同佛聲，等皆清淨例則無妨。遍知一切色法聲法無亂無謬，分別亦無妨，自在之根那作頑礙之解耶？

The section on the tongue faculty is likewise so: what is born of father and mother is the physical tongue, able to produce the speech of the ten dharma-realms — taking this as the basis, the meaning of the fivefold tongue becomes clear: able to produce the speech of the ten dharma-realms is the heavenly tongue; unimpaired is the wisdom tongue; unerring is the Dharma tongue; functioning all at once and reciprocally is the buddha tongue (and so on). Question: bitter, astringent, and foul flavors, upon reaching the tongue, all transform into the finest flavor — why is it, then, that the various colors, upon reaching the eye, do not likewise transform into a sublime color? The old explanation held that these are not parallel, since flavor has both harm and benefit — what is harmful transforms, what is not harmful does not — whereas the various colors do not impair the eye, and hence they are not parallel. My own explanation is not so: all colors are identical with the Buddha's own color, and all sounds are identical with the Buddha's own sound, equally and entirely pure — taken as parallel, there is no difficulty. To know completely all dharmas of color and of sound, without confusion and without error, and to discern them, is likewise without any difficulty — how, then, could the self-sovereign sense-faculty be explained as though it were obtusely obstructed?

身根章亦如是，世間所有，皆於身中現肉身用也，上至有頂於身中現天身用也，二乘身中現慧身用也，菩薩於身中現法身用也，佛於身中現佛身用也，一時圓現一時互用，一時無謬一時無著(云云)。

The section on the body faculty is likewise so: all that exists in the world manifests, within the body, the function of the physical body; up to the peak of existence, it manifests, within the body, the function of the heavenly body; the two lesser vehicles manifest, within the body, the function of the wisdom body; the bodhisattvas manifest, within the body, the function of the Dharma body; the Buddha manifests, within the body, the function of the buddha body — all manifesting perfectly at once, all functioning reciprocally at once, all without error at once, all without attachment at once (and so on).

意根章亦如是，世間資生產業，皆順正法人意淨天心所行天所動作悉知天意淨。四月即四諦，一歲即十二月，是十二因緣，與實相不相違背，即慧意淨。一月即一乘菩薩意淨，有所思量皆是先佛經中所說即佛意淨。一時圓明一時圓互，一時無染一時無謬，根用自在能盈能縮能等能淨(云云)。

The section on the mind faculty is likewise so: all worldly livelihood and enterprise, when in accord with the true Dharma, are the purity of the human mind; the deeds and movements that the gods perform, all known, are the purity of the heavenly mind. The four months are the four noble truths; the single year of twelve months is the twelvefold chain of dependent origination — these, not contradicting the true mark of reality, are the purity of the wisdom mind. The single month is the purity of the mind of the bodhisattva of the one vehicle; whatever is thought and considered, all being what the buddhas of the past have already taught in their sūtras, is the purity of the buddha mind. All perfectly illumined at once, all perfectly reciprocal at once, all without defilement at once, all without error at once — the function of the sense-faculty, self-sovereign, is able to expand, able to contract, able to remain equal, and able to be pure (and so on).

Explaining "Never Disparaging": The Fourfold Oneness 釋常不輕菩薩品 · 開權開近之四一

Chinese Source

釋常不輕菩薩品 內懷不輕之解，外敬不輕之境，身立不輕之行，口宣不輕之教，人作不輕之目。不輕之解者，《法華論》云「此菩薩知眾生有佛性不敢輕之。佛性有五，正因佛性，通亘本當；緣了佛性，種子本有非適今也。果性果果性定當得之，決不虛也，是名不輕之解。」將解以歷人，彼亦如此，是名敬不輕之境；敬此境故名不輕之行；宣此語故名不輕之教。昔毀者以此目人，今經家以此目品。《見實三昧》云「佛為父王說一切皆是佛，王問：『一切眾生即是佛不？』佛答：『若如實見眾生，於其即是佛。』」私類此語，若不如實見佛，於其則非聖。譬初學射的多乖少當，以地為的無往不著。若分別賢聖孰是孰非，如實觀之即是佛也。初是因緣解，後是圓教解(云云)。此品引人為證，證五品功德深、六根報重，我昔隨喜獲現生後報，以募流通也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining "Never Disparaging." Inwardly he holds the understanding of never disparaging; outwardly he reveres the object of never disparaging; in his body he establishes the practice of never disparaging; with his mouth he proclaims the teaching of never disparaging; and people give him the name of Never Disparaging. As for the understanding of never disparaging, the Fahua Lun says: "This bodhisattva knows that beings possess buddha-nature, and dares not disparage them. Buddha-nature has five aspects: the correct-cause buddha-nature runs through both the origin and what is yet to come; the conditioning-cause and revealing-cause buddha-natures, as seeds, are inherently present, not something newly arisen now; and the fruit-nature and the fruit-of-fruit-nature are certain to be attained in the end, without fail — this is named the understanding of never disparaging." Taking this understanding and applying it to each person he meets, seeing that they too are just the same, is named revering the object of never disparaging; revering this object is named the practice of never disparaging; proclaiming this teaching is named the teaching of never disparaging. In the past, those who reviled him used this as a name for the person; now, the sūtra's compilers use it as a name for the chapter. The Bhūtatathatā-samādhi Sūtra says: "The Buddha, expounding for the sake of his father the king, said that all are precisely the Buddha. The king asked: 'Are all beings, then, precisely the Buddha?' The Buddha answered: 'If one sees beings exactly as they truly are, then, in them, this is precisely the Buddha.'" I would, on my own, put it in a similar way: if one does not see the Buddha exactly as he truly is, then, in him, this is not the sage. It is like a beginner learning archery: aiming at a mark, he misses far more often than he hits; but aiming at the ground itself, there is nowhere he does not strike it. If one distinguishes among the worthies and the sages which is right and which is wrong, one may miss; but seeing exactly as things truly are, this itself is the Buddha. The first of these is the understanding in terms of causes and conditions; the second is the understanding of the perfect teaching (and so on). This chapter cites a person as proof, proving that the merit of the five ranks is profound and the reward of the six sense-faculties weighty — that I myself, rejoicing in the past, obtained reward in this present life and in lives to come — so as to encourage its transmission.

The first line and a half set "all dharmas" to verse: upholding the Dharma is itself upholding the Buddha's own body (and so on).

The two verses beginning "may I, together with the emanation-buddhas" set the spiritual power to verse — the spiritual power urges the Buddha and brings him joy. The one verse beginning "the buddhas, seated at the place of enlightenment" sets the secret essential to verse, and may be understood as it stands. The four verses beginning "as for the meaning of all dharmas" set the profound matter to verse: expounding the Dharma to break through darkness and bring beings into the one vehicle is itself the Buddha's profound matter. The final line and a half sums up the whole in verse, as a closing.

Chinese Source

The first falls into three: first, the Tathagata's entrusting; second, the bodhisattvas' receiving; third, the matter's completion and the dispersal proclaimed. The first, again, falls into three: first, the entrustment proper; second, the explanation of the entrustment; third, the admonition accompanying the entrustment. As for the entrustment proper: the Buddha, with the skillful hand of his single provisional wisdom, laid it upon the crowns — the real wisdom — of the bodhisattvas who filled the empty sky of three thousand three hundred myriad kotis of nayutas of lands. Because the Tathagata bestows the path in order to convert others, this is called "the hand of provisional wisdom"; because the bodhisattvas, through their own practice, receive the path, this is called "the crown of real wisdom." His extending his hand and laying it upon their heads is precisely the entrustment through the body; the provisional wisdom's meeting the real wisdom is precisely the entrustment through the mind.

"And he spoke these words" is precisely the entrustment through speech. The text carries the meaning of the four siddhantas. "For immeasurable kalpas I have cultivated this Dharma so difficult to obtain" — this refers to his having received it in study from an earlier buddha, and his now entrusting it to them, so that they in turn should bestow it upon others, an unbroken succession across the three periods of time — this is the mundane siddhanta. "Propagate it with a single mind" is the individual siddhanta. "Cause it to spread and increase widely" is the therapeutic siddhanta. "Bring benefit" is the siddhanta of ultimate meaning. From "why is this?" onward is the explanation of the entrustment. "Possessing great compassion" is the Tathagata's room; "free of any stinginess" is the Tathagata's robe; "and also free of any fear" is the Tathagata's seat. "The Buddha's wisdom" is the wisdom of all things; "the Tathagata's wisdom" is the wisdom of the paths that lead beings; "spontaneous wisdom" is the wisdom of all modes. Within the Tathagata's room, he is able to bestow upon beings these three kinds of wisdom, and it is likewise so within his seat; being a benefactor of this kind, he is therefore free of stinginess, therefore free of fear — you, too, should study this Dharma of the Tathagata. This is what is meant by explaining the Buddha's intention in making the entrustment.

從「於未來世」下是誡付者，若根深智利直說佛慧，若不堪者，於餘深法中示教利喜。佛慧是深而非餘，六方便是餘而非深，別教次第是餘亦是深，汝能以餘深，助申佛慧者，即善巧報佛之恩，是名誡付囑也。

From "in ages to come" onward is the admonition accompanying the entrustment: if a being's roots are deep and his wisdom sharp, teach the buddha-wisdom directly; if he cannot bear it, instruct, teach, benefit, and delight him by means of some other profound Dharma. The buddha-wisdom is profound but not "other"; the six skillful means are "other" but not profound; the distinct teaching's step-by-step approach is both "other" and profound. If you are able, by means of this other profundity, to help set forth the buddha-wisdom, this is a skillful repayment of the Buddha's kindness — this is what is meant by the admonition accompanying the entrustment.

Bhaishajyaraja: The Chapter's Purpose, and the Question 釋藥王菩薩本事品：釋名與發問

Chinese Source

釋藥王菩薩本事品 《觀經》曰「昔名星光，從尊者日藏聞說佛慧，以雪山上藥供養眾僧，願我未來能治眾生身心兩病，舉世歡喜號曰藥王。」此文明一切眾生喜見，頓捨一身復燒兩臂，輕生重法命殞道存，舉昔顯今，故言「本事品」也。若推此義，星光應在喜見之後，從捨藥發誓已來名藥王故(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the chapter Bhaishajyaraja's Former Life. The Contemplation Sutra says: "In a former age he was named Starlight; hearing the venerable Sun-Store expound the buddha-wisdom, he made offerings to the assembly of monks with medicinal herbs from the Snow Mountains, vowing, 'In the future may I be able to heal beings' twin illnesses of body and mind' — and the whole world, rejoicing, called him Bhaishajyaraja, the King of Medicine." This passage of our sutra makes clear how Sarvasattvapriyadarshana, "Gladly Seen by All Beings," at once relinquished his own body, and further burned both his arms — making light of his life and holding the Dharma dear, his life perishing while the path endured — raising up the former deed to make manifest the present one, and hence it is called the chapter "Former Life." If we press this meaning further, Starlight should properly come after Sarvasattvapriyadarshana, since it is from the time he relinquished the medicine and made his vow onward that he was named Bhaishajyaraja (and so on).

此下五品皆是化他流通，今品明化他之師，唯願大法大得弘宣，大願眾生獲大饒益，所以竭其神力盡其形命，殷殷虔虔志猶未已，庶令弟子宗法如師，我傳爾明、爾復傳明，明明無已師之志也，故知此品勗弘法之師也。下如〈妙音〉、〈觀音〉兩品，明他方大士奉命弘經，普現色身形無定準，不可牛羊眼看，不可以凡庸識度，於所聞處勿生輕想，輕想則法不染心，故知下品勗受法弟子也。有人言，上諸品諸佛為佛事，此品下菩薩為佛事。

The five chapters that follow are all part of the propagation of the Dharma through converting others. The present chapter makes clear the teacher who converts others: wishing only that the great Dharma be widely proclaimed, vowing greatly that beings obtain great and abundant benefit, he therefore exhausts his spiritual power and spends his very life and body, earnest and reverent, his resolve never yet spent, hoping that his disciples will honor the Dharma as their teacher does — I transmit it and make it clear to you, and you in turn transmit it and make it clear to others, clarification upon clarification, the resolve of a teacher never exhausted — and hence we know that this chapter exhorts teachers who propagate the Dharma. Below, the two chapters "Gadgadasvara" and "Avalokiteshvara" make clear how great beings from other lands, carrying out the charge, propagate the sutra, manifesting bodily forms everywhere without any fixed pattern, forms that cannot be judged by the eyes of an ox or a sheep, nor measured by ordinary, mediocre understanding — wherever such a form is encountered, one must not give rise to any thought of contempt, for a thought of contempt keeps the Dharma from soaking into the mind — and hence we know that the chapters below exhort the disciples who receive the Dharma. Some say that, in the chapters above, the buddhas carry out the buddhas' work, while, from this chapter on, the bodhisattvas carry out the buddhas' work.

此一往耳，上品亦有菩薩，此下品亦有諸佛(云云)。今明〈方便品〉開三顯一圓因已竟，〈安樂行品〉明乘乘之法，〈壽量〉明乘果已竟，此品下明乘乘之人，故《十二門論》云「大乘者，普賢、文殊大人之所乘也」，〈藥王〉以苦行乘乘，〈妙音〉、〈觀音〉以三昧乘乘，〈陀羅尼〉以總持乘乘，〈妙莊嚴〉以誓願乘乘，〈普賢〉以神通乘乘，作此解者，於化他流通義便也。

This, however, is only a rough approximation, for the chapters above also have bodhisattvas carrying out the buddhas' work, and the chapters below also have buddhas doing so (and so on). Here it should be understood: "Skillful Means" makes clear the opening of the three to reveal the one, the perfect cause, and brings it to its conclusion; "Peaceful Practices" makes clear the Dharma of riding the vehicle; "The Tathagata's Lifespan" makes clear the fruit of riding the vehicle, and brings it to its conclusion; from this chapter on, what is made clear is the person who rides the vehicle. Hence the Twelve Gate Treatise says, "The Great Vehicle is that which is ridden by great persons such as Samantabhadra and Manjushri." "Bhaishajyaraja" rides the vehicle by means of ascetic practice; "Gadgadasvara" and "Avalokiteshvara" ride the vehicle by means of samadhi; "Dharanis" rides the vehicle by means of the retention of all teachings; "King Shubhavyuha" rides the vehicle by means of vows; "Samantabhadra" rides the vehicle by means of supernatural powers. Reading it in this way makes for a convenient sense of the propagation through converting others.

Bhaishajyaraja: The Ascetic Practice, and Self-Immolation 釋藥王菩薩本事品：苦行與燒身

Furthermore, contemplating that both the body and the fire, the one who makes the offering and that which is offered, are all alike true reality — who, then, is burned, and what burns? — the one who offers and that which is offered are both, in the end, unobtainable, and hence it is named "true Dharma."

As for the exhortation to practice: being able to burn a single finger surpasses relinquishing the outer body, since the outer is light and the inner is weighty, and so the merit and blessing differ accordingly. Where the text says "wife and children," this is the outer body; "kingdom and city," and the like, are outer wealth.

Reading note: Zhiyi never pauses here to caution against literal imitation of Bhaishajyaraja's self-immolation, and neither did any commentator we know of before the modern era — but readers today should understand this parable, as the whole passage's own logic insists, in terms of "making light of the outer, holding the inner dear," not as literal instruction. Zhiyi's own gloss treats the burning body and the burning offering alike as empty of any fixed self, "the one who offers and that which is offered both, in the end, unobtainable" — a call to relinquish attachment, not a model for bodily harm.

Offering the seven treasures to the four kinds of sages does not compare to upholding a single verse, for the Dharma is the sage's teacher, able to give birth, to nurture, to bring to completion, and to bring honor, as nothing else can — hence a person is light in weight compared to the Dharma, which is weighty. From "King of Constellations" onward is the second part, the praise of the Dharma that is upheld, which itself falls into two: first, praising the substance of the Dharma; next, praising its function. As for "among all the streams, rivers, and every kind of water, the sea is foremost": the Sutra of Infinite Meanings says, "the four kinds of water are a parable for the teachings; within the parable of the medicinal herbs, the single cloud able to send down rain is a parable for the preaching of the Dharma." Here, all the various waters together stand for the teachings as a whole, and the four specifically named are a parable for the four flavors of milk, cream, curdled, and refined butter as teachings. This Lotus teaching is a parable for the ocean of ghee: expounded to the very depths of the fundamental ground is called "profound"; pervading every place is called "great"; being purely the manifest buddha-dharma and expounding no other teaching is called "salty" — this is the meaning of "most profound and great."

The names of the ten treasure mountains are found in the Avatamsaka Sutra and various other sutras (and so on). This Lotus Sutra expounds the truth of permanence, bliss, self, and purity, like Sumeru formed of the four treasures, the very support of those who open, show, awaken to, and enter the Buddha's knowledge and vision — and hence, in this sense, it is the most exalted of all. The stars and the moon are alike essences of yin, both appearing at night, yet the stars, having no waxing or waning, do not compare to the moon — the other sutras' explanation of provisional wisdom cannot attain sovereign mastery, while this sutra makes clear that the provisional is itself the real and the real is itself the provisional, waxing and waning pointing to one another, not two yet two — and it is in this sense that the provisional wisdom spoken of here surpasses that of the other teachings. The sun, the essence of yang, alone is able to break through darkness — the other sutras' account of real wisdom breaking through delusion still does not compare to the real that is itself the provisional; how much less, then, could it be paired with the provisional that is itself the real? Hence we know that this sutra's account of real wisdom is foremost of all.

The other sutras require effort as a cause before one can enter the stream — as when those of the four fruits attain awakening only through hearing, reflection, and cultivation; this sutra makes clear that the uncontrived four noble truths, unmixed with any skillful means, flow spontaneously into the ocean of omniscience, like the great white ox, sturdy and full of strength, swift as the wind (and so on).

The line "foremost among shravakas, pratyekabuddhas, and bodhisattvas" makes clear that the cause is foremost: the other sutras' account of the cause consists in the seven skillful means, while this sutra's account of the cause lies beyond all skillful means, and hence its cause is foremost. From "if a person is able to hear" onward makes clear the depth of blessing in upholding the sutra: first, citing the blessing of hearing the entire sutra; next, citing the blessing of hearing even a single chapter — there is a comparative reckoning, and there is a final charge, as the text has it. The mouth emitting fragrance is a recompense in the present life; the rest are recompenses in a future life. As for "having heard this sutra, one neither ages nor dies" — this calls for a contemplative understanding: "not aging" is bliss, "not dying" is permanence; hearing this sutra, one attains an understanding of permanence and bliss, resting at ease within, with nothing to fear or dread.

The east is where the light begins, the west is where it ends — having both a beginning and an end, is this not the way of the sage alone?" Those who have not yet given rise to the resolve are thereby made to give rise to it; those who have not yet reached the ultimate are thereby made to reach it. As it is for one bodhisattva, so it is for the whole assembly; as it is for one direction, so it is for every direction — the sage, not wishing to burden the text, cites one to stand for all, and hence speaks only of east and west.

Setting aside the capacities behind the other sounds, only the capacity behind the buddha's sound is taken up, and a response set forth for it — and it is on account of this causal connection between capacity and response that he is named Avalokiteshvara, the "one who observes the sounds of the world."

"Universal" means pervading everywhere. The dharmas are immeasurable: failing to attain universality, they remain a partial dharma; attaining universality, they become the perfect dharma. Hence the Sutra of Resolving Doubts through Contemplation says, "all dharmas are false, all dharmas are correct." Here, in brief, ten dharmas are used to make clear this universality; once this sense is grasped, it may be extended by analogy to every dharma without exception being universal — namely, the universality of compassion, the universality of the great vow, the universality of practice, the universality of freedom from delusion, the universality of entering the dharma-gates, the universality of supernatural powers, the universality of skillful means, the universality of expounding the Dharma, the universality of bringing beings to maturity, and the universality of making offerings to the buddhas. From humans and gods up to bodhisattvas, all possess compassion, yet there is a compassion that is universal and one that is not. Where compassion's substance takes as its basis both beings and dharmas, it remains partial — the beings to whom it extends are not broad, and it cannot be called universal; where compassion has no such basis and shares the very substance of true reality, its principle is perfect, and there is nowhere its compassion does not reach — like a magnet drawing iron, responding spontaneously — such compassion pervades and suffuses all things alike, and is named the universality of compassion. As for the universality of the great vow: "great" means broad, "vow" means to bind oneself — binding the essential resolve broadly is called the great vow. The great vow arises in terms of the four noble truths: if it arises in terms of the contrived, the uncontrived, the boundless, or even the truly unarising four truths, it does not gather up every dharma without remainder, and cannot be called universal; only if it arises in terms of the uncontrived four truths is it named the universality of the great vow.

修行普者，例如佛未值定光佛前凡有所修不與理合，從得記已觸事即理，理智歷法而修行者，無行而不普也。斷惑普者，若用一切智、道種智，斷四住塵沙等惑，如却枝條，不名斷惑普；若用一切種智斷無明者，五住皆盡如除根本，名斷惑普。入法門普者，道前名修方便，道後所入名入法門。若二乘以一心入一定，一心作一不得眾多，又為定所縛，故不名普；若歷別諸地淺深階差，亦不名普；若入王三昧，一切三昧悉入其中，不起滅定現諸威儀，故名法門普。

As for the universality of practice: consider how, before the Buddha encountered Dipamkara Buddha, whatever he cultivated did not yet accord with principle; from the time he received his prediction onward, whatever he encountered was itself principle, and, principle and wisdom running through every dharma as he practiced, there was no practice that was not universal. As for the universality of cutting off delusion: if one employs the wisdom of all things and the wisdom of the paths to cut off the delusions of the four abodes and of dust and sand, this is like lopping off branches and twigs, and cannot be called the universality of cutting off delusion; only if one employs the wisdom of all modes to cut off ignorance, exhausting all five abodes of delusion as one removes the very root, is it named the universality of cutting off delusion. As for the universality of entering the dharma-gates: before the path, this is called cultivating skillful means; what is entered after the path is called entering the dharma-gates. If the two vehicles enter a single concentration with a single mind, this single mind produces only a single thing and cannot produce many, and is, moreover, bound by that concentration — and hence it cannot be called universal; if one proceeds step by step through the various grounds, shallow and deep, level by level, this, too, cannot be called universal; only if one enters the king of samadhis, in which every samadhi without exception is contained, so that, without rising from cessation-concentration, one manifests every manner of dignified conduct, is it named the universality of the dharma-gates.

As for the universality of supernatural powers: the great arhat's heavenly eye illuminates the great trichiliocosm, the pratyekabuddha's illuminates a hundred buddha-worlds, the bodhisattva's illuminates worlds as numerous as the sands of the Ganges — yet all of these, taking as their object a narrow domain, produce a supernatural power that is likewise partial; only if one's practice takes true reality as its object does a single arising of the power become the arising of all — a resembling supernatural power, as explained above — how much more so, then, must the true supernatural power be universal? As for the universality of skillful means, there are two kinds: skillful means before the path are subsumed under cultivation and practice; after the path, there are, again, two: first, the very substance of the Dharma, as explained under "entering the dharma-gates"; second, its transformative function, as explained here — meeting each capacity, benefiting beings, matching each condition as it is fitting, complete and pervasive all at once, and though carrying out one action after another in ever-varying ways, suffering no diminishment whatsoever with respect to the true nature of dharmas and the ultimate reality — this is named the universality of skillful means.

說法普者，能以一妙音，稱十法界機，隨其宜類俱令解脫，如修羅琴，故名說法普。成就眾生普者，一切世間及出世間所有事業，皆菩薩所為，鑿井造舟神農甞藥雲蔭日照利益眾生，乃至利益一切賢聖，示教利喜令入三菩提，是名成就眾生普。供養諸佛普者，若作外事供養，以一時一食一花一香普供養一切佛，無前無後一時等供，於一塵中出種種塵亦復如是；若作內觀者，圓智導眾行，圓智名為佛，眾行資圓智，即是供養佛，若行資餘智，不名供養普，眾行資圓智，是名供養普。

As for the universality of expounding the Dharma: being able, with a single wondrous voice, to match the capacities of the ten dharma-realms, causing each, according to its own fitting kind, to attain liberation, like the lute of the asuras that sounds differently to each hearer — hence it is named the universality of expounding the Dharma. As for the universality of bringing beings to maturity: every undertaking, whether mundane or transcending the mundane, is, in every case, the bodhisattva's own doing — digging wells, building boats, Shennong tasting the medicinal herbs, clouds giving shade, the sun giving light, all benefiting beings, extending even to benefiting every worthy and sage, instructing, teaching, benefiting, and delighting them, and bringing them into unsurpassed bodhi — this is named the universality of bringing beings to maturity. As for the universality of making offerings to the buddhas: if one performs an outward act of offering, offering, at a single time, a single meal, a single flower, or a single stick of incense universally to every buddha, with no "before" and no "after," all offered equally at one and the same time, and, from within a single speck of dust, bringing forth every manner of speck of dust in just the same way; or if one performs an inward contemplation, then perfect wisdom guides the whole host of practices — perfect wisdom is itself named "buddha," and the host of practices, providing resources for perfect wisdom, is itself the offering made to the Buddha; if one's practice provides resources for some other, partial, wisdom, this cannot be called a universal offering — only when the host of practices provides resources for perfect wisdom is it named a universal offering.

門者，從假入空空通而假壅，從空入假假通而空壅，偏通則非普，壅故非門；中道非空非假，正通實相雙照二諦故名普，正通故名門。普門圓通義則無量，略舉其十類則可知。此品猶是普現三昧化他流通也。

"Gate": entering emptiness from the provisional makes emptiness pass through, yet the provisional remains blocked; entering the provisional from emptiness makes the provisional pass through, yet emptiness remains blocked — such one-sided passing-through is not universal, and, being blocked, is not a gate. The middle way is neither empty nor provisional; its true passing-through doubly illuminates the two truths of true reality, and hence is called universal; its true passing-through is what is called a gate. The meaning of the perfect passing-through of the Universal Gate is thus immeasurable; here, in brief, ten categories have been set forth, from which it may be understood. This chapter, too, belongs to the propagation, through converting others, by means of the samadhi of manifesting everywhere.

Reading note: This chapter's fame as a stand-alone recitation text — the "Guanyin Sutra" chanted independently of the rest of the Lotus — is already presupposed here in Zhiyi's opening remark that "those who lecture on it are very numerous." His own reading insists on tracing every popular image (the wish-fulfilling jewel, the medicinal tree, the thirty-three forms) back to the single ground of perfect contemplation, so that devotion to Avalokiteshvara and the doctrinal architecture of the whole sutra are never treated as two different things.

從「成就」下，結別開總。別文廣意狹，總答文狹意廣(云云)。從「是故」下，勸供養。此中見形聞法，故勸供養也。初、勸，次、受旨。受旨為六：奉命、不受、重奉、佛勸、即受、結，皆如文。從「持地」下，是聞品功德(云云)。「無等等」者，九法界心不能等理，佛法界心能等此理，故無等而等也。又畢竟之理是無等，初緣畢竟理而發心，能等於理，故言無等等也。又心之與理俱不可得，將何物等何物，而言無等等耶？心之與理俱不可說，不可說而說，說此心等此理，故言無等等耳。初一是橫釋，次一是豎釋，次一非橫非豎釋也(云云)。

From "accomplishes" onward concludes the specific answer and opens into the general answer. The specific portion has broad wording but narrow meaning; the general answer has narrow wording but broad meaning (and so on). From "for this reason" onward is the urging to make offerings — here, having seen his form and heard the Dharma, an offering is therefore urged. First, the urging; next, the receiving of the instruction. The receiving of the instruction falls into six: offering it, declining it, offering it once more, the Buddha's urging, accepting it at once, and the conclusion — all as the text has it. From "Dharanindhara" onward is the merit obtained from hearing the chapter (and so on). As for "unequaled and equal": the minds of the nine dharma-realms cannot equal the principle, while the mind of the buddha-realm is able to equal this very principle — hence "unequaled, yet equal." Further, ultimate principle is unequaled; when one first takes the ultimate principle as an object and gives rise to the resolve, one becomes able to equal that principle — hence it is called "unequaled, yet equal." Further, mind and principle are both alike unobtainable — what thing, then, would equal what other thing, that one should speak of "unequaled, yet equal"? Mind and principle are both alike inexpressible, yet, being inexpressible, are nonetheless expressed — to say that this mind equals this principle is what is meant by "unequaled, yet equal." The first explanation is a horizontal one; the second is a vertical one; the third is neither horizontal nor vertical (and so on).

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the chapter Dharanis. This is translated "total retention," retaining evil so that it does not arise, and retaining good so that it is not lost (this is the first sense). It is also translated "able to block and able to retain": able to retain the good and able to block evil (this is the second and third sense) — this is also expressed as able to block partial evil and able to retain the middling good (this is the fourth sense). The various sutras open and restrict the use of dharanis differently: some use them solely to cure illness, as with the layman Nadi; some use them solely to protect the Dharma, as in the present text; some use them solely to eliminate offenses, as in the Vaipulya sutras; some use them broadly, to cure illness, eliminate offenses, and protect the sutra all at once, as in the Sutra of Requesting Avalokiteshvara; and some — the Great Bright Spell, the Unsurpassed Bright Spell, the Unequaled and Equal Bright Spell — are for none of these: neither curing illness, nor eliminating offenses, nor protecting the sutra. If one is speaking generally, all these uses should be combined; if one is discussing a particular case, one must be sure to follow the sutra and not depart from its own teaching (and so on).

諸師或說呪者，是鬼神王名，稱其王名，部落敬主不敢為非，故能降伏一切鬼魅(其一)。或云呪者如軍中之密號，唱號相應無所訶問，若不相應即執治罪，若不順呪者頭破七分，若順呪者則無過失(其二)。或云呪者，密默治惡惡自休息，譬如微賤，從此國逃彼國，訛稱王子，彼國以公主妻之，多瞋難事。

Various masters have said, variously: that a mantra is the name of a spirit-king, and, by invoking that king's name, his subjects, honoring their lord, dare not do wrong, and so the mantra is able to subdue every ghost and spirit (this is the first account). Or that a mantra is like a secret password within an army: chanting the password correctly meets with no challenge or interrogation, while, if it does not match, one is seized and punished — if one does not comply with the correct spell, one's head splits into seven pieces, while if one complies with the spell, no fault results (this is the second account). Or that a mantra secretly and silently overcomes evil, so that the evil of itself subsides — as when, for instance, a person of humble station fled from his own country to another, falsely claiming to be a prince, so that that country married its princess to him, a princess who was prone to anger and difficult to manage.

有一明人從其國來，主往說之，其人語主，若當瞋時說偈，偈云「無親遊他國，欺誑一切人，麁食是常事，何勞復作瞋。」說是偈時默然瞋歇後不復瞋，是主及一切人但聞斯偈，皆不知意。呪亦如是，密默遮惡餘無識者(其三)。或云呪者，是諸佛密語，如王索先陀婆，一切群下無有能識，唯有智臣乃能知之。呪亦如是，秖是一法遍有諸力，病愈罪除善生道合(其四)。為此義故皆存本音，譯人不翻意在此也。惡世弘經喜多惱難，以呪護之使道流通也。

There was a wise man who came from the impostor's own country; the husband went to consult him, and the man told him that, whenever she was about to grow angry, he should recite a verse. The verse ran: "Wandering to another land, with no kin of my own, I have deceived every person here; coarse fare has become my ordinary lot — why, then, should I trouble myself to grow angry as well?" As he recited this verse, she fell silent and her anger subsided, and afterward she was never angry again — yet the husband and everyone else, hearing only the verse, had no idea what it meant. A mantra is likewise so: secretly and silently blocking evil, its meaning unknown to anyone else (this is the third account). Or that a mantra is the buddhas' own secret speech, as when a king asks for saindhava — a word that can mean salt, water, a vessel, or a horse, depending on context — which none among his subjects can understand, save for a wise minister alone, who alone is able to know what is meant. A mantra is likewise so: it is a single dharma that pervasively holds every power at once, curing illness, removing offenses, giving rise to good, and according with the path (this is the fourth account). For this reason, mantras all retain their original sound, and it is for this very reason that translators do not translate them. In an evil age, propagating the sutra brings much vexation and difficulty, and it is by means of mantras that it is protected, so that the path may be propagated and spread.

King Shubhavyuha: The Story of the Four Monks 釋妙莊嚴王本事品：四比丘因緣

Chinese Source

釋妙莊嚴王本事品 此因緣出他經，昔佛末法有四比丘，於《法華經》極生殷重，雖卷舒祕教，甘露未霑，日夜翹誠，晷刻無忘，歎云：「苟非其人乎？地非其處乎？」世間紛愀，靜散相乖，直爾求閑尚須厭棄，況崇道乎？於是結契山林，志欣佛慧，幽居日積，衣糧單罄，有待多煩，無時不乏，一餐喀喀，廢萬里之行，十旬九飯，屈雲霄之志，可得言哉？

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the chapter King Shubhavyuha's Former Life. This causal story comes from another sutra. In the age of the Buddha's declining Dharma there were four monks who felt the utmost reverence and earnestness toward the Lotus Sutra; although they unrolled and studied this secret teaching, they had not yet tasted its ambrosia, and day and night, with upright sincerity, never for a single moment forgot it, lamenting, "Is it not, perhaps, that we are not the right persons? Is it not, perhaps, that this is not the right place?" The mundane world is a tangle of sorrow, its stillness and its distractions at odds with each other — merely to seek quiet already requires renouncing it, how much more so to honor the path! And so they bound themselves by an agreement to dwell together in the mountain forest, their resolve set upon the buddha-wisdom. As their secluded dwelling stretched on, day by day, their robes and provisions grew utterly bare; having needs, they suffered many troubles, and at no time were they without want — a single meal, hastily gulped down, was enough to abandon a journey of ten thousand li; nine meals in a hundred days bent low their sky-soaring resolve — how could this even be put into words?

其一人云：「吾等四窮尚不存身，法當安寄？君三人者但以命奉道，莫慮朝中，我一人者捨此身力，誓給所須。」於是振錫門閭以求供繼，自春至冬周而復始，如僕奉大家，甘苦無喜慍。

One of the four said: "We four, destitute as we are, cannot even sustain our own bodies — how, then, should the Dharma find its support? You three should simply devote your very lives to the path and set your minds at rest about our provisions; I alone will give up my own bodily strength and vow to supply whatever is needed." And so he shook his staff at the gates of the villages, seeking to sustain their support, from spring to winter, cycle after cycle, like a servant attending his master, enduring both the sweet and the bitter without either joy or resentment.

三人得展其誠，功圓事辦，一世之益，當無量生。其一人者數涉人間，屢逢聲色，坯器未火，難可護持。偶逢王出車，馬駢闐旌旗噏赫，生心動念愛彼光榮，功德薰修隨念受報，人中天上常得為王，福雖不貲亦有限也。三人得道會而議云：「我免籠樊功由此王，其耽果報增長有為，從此死已不復為王，方沈火坑良難可救，幸其未苦正可開化。」

The other three were thus able to unfold their sincerity fully, their achievement complete and their task accomplished — the benefit of a single lifetime, extending to lives without measure. As for the one man, going out among people time and again, he repeatedly encountered sound and beauty, and, being like an unfired clay vessel, found it hard to safeguard himself. He chanced once to meet a king setting out in his carriage, horses ranked close together, banners and pennants dazzling in profusion, and a thought arose in his mind, delighting in that royal splendor; his merit, thus perfumed and cultivated, brought its recompense in accordance with that very thought, and among humans and gods he thereafter constantly became a king — though the resulting blessing was beyond reckoning, it was, nonetheless, still finite. The other three, having attained the path, gathered and discussed the matter: "That we escaped the cage and trap of poverty and hunger was due to this man's merit — yet his indulgence in the recompense of that merit only increases what is conditioned, and, once he dies from this present state, he will no longer be a king, but will instead sink into a pit of fire, from which it will be truly hard to save him. While he has not yet suffered, this is precisely the moment to convert him."

其一人云：「此王著欲而復邪見，若非愛鈎無由可拔，一人可為端正婦，二作聰明兒，兒婦之言必當從順，如宜設化果獲改邪。」婦者，妙音菩薩是；昔二子者，今藥王、藥上二菩薩是，昔時王者，今華德菩薩是。所以白毫東召，升紫臺而西引，神呪護經，使流通而大益，說四聖之前緣故，名「妙莊嚴王本事品」。

One of the three said: "This king is attached to desire, and holds wrong views besides — unless we use the hook of love, there is no way to draw him out. One of us can become his upright and beautiful wife, and two can become his clever sons; the words of sons and wife will surely be heeded and followed, and, converting him in this fitting way, we may succeed in turning him from his wrongness." The wife was none other than the bodhisattva Gadgadasvara; the two sons of that former age are, in the present, the two bodhisattvas Bhaishajyaraja and Bhaishajyasamudgata; the king of that former age is, in the present, the bodhisattva Padmashri. This is why, in the preceding chapters, the white tuft of hair summoned Gadgadasvara from the east, and he ascended the purple platform and was led westward; and why the spirit-mantras protect the sutra, causing it to spread and be of great benefit — and it is in telling the prior karmic connection of these four sages that the chapter is named King Shubhavyuha's Former Life.

又「妙莊嚴」者，妙法功德莊嚴諸根也。此王往日於妙法有緣，道熏時熟，諸根應淨，生雖未獲，其理必臻，靈瑞感通，嘉名早立。例如善吉雖未無諍，已號空生，故下文云「得清淨功德莊嚴三昧」，以是義故名「妙莊嚴王」也。前品說呪護，今品說人護，人護尚爾，呪護彌良，普勸流通也。

Further, "Shubhavyuha," "Wonderful Adornment": the merit of the wondrous Dharma adorns all the sense-faculties. This king, in a former age, had a karmic connection to the wondrous Dharma; as the path perfumed him and the time ripened, his sense-faculties were fit to become pure — though not yet attained in that birth, its principle would inevitably arrive, and, moved and connected by this numinous omen, his fine name was established early on. This is like Subhuti, who, though not yet free of all contention, was already named "born of emptiness" — hence the text below says, "attaining the samadhi of adornment with pure merit" — and it is for this reason that he is named "King Wonderful Adornment." The preceding chapter told of protection by mantra; this chapter tells of protection by a person — if protection by a person is already so effective, how much better still is protection by mantra — and so, universally, the propagation of the sutra is urged.

Tenth, praising the Buddha and making his own vow. The Buddha's praise of the good spiritual friend carries great significance: the good spiritual friend is able to carry out the buddhas' work — this, then, is the good spiritual friend as outer protector. "Instructing, teaching, benefiting, and delighting" is, then, the good spiritual friend as teacher; "namely, guiding and converting so as to bring about the sight of the Buddha" is, then, the good spiritual friend as fellow practitioner; "causing entry into bodhi" is, then, the good spiritual friend of the ultimate reality itself. The Samyukta Agama says, "the good spiritual friend is like a chaste and virtuous wife" — this is the meaning of outer protector; further, the good spiritual friend is like the wealth of one's own clan — this is the meaning of fellow practitioner; further, the good spiritual friend is like the leader of a merchant caravan — this is the meaning of teacher; further, the good spiritual friend is like a child lying in its father's embrace — this is the meaning of the ultimate reality.

「佛告大眾」下，是結會古今，先、結會，次、結歎二菩薩也。 「說是」下，聞品得道，如文。

"The Buddha said to the great assembly" onward is the concluding identification of past and present: first, the identification itself; next, the concluding praise of the two bodhisattvas. "Having spoken this" onward is the attaining of the path upon hearing the chapter, as the text has it.

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the chapter Samantabhadra's Encouragement. The Great Treatise and the Contemplation Sutra both name him "Universally Auspicious"; this sutra calls him "Samantabhadra" — both are Chinese renderings of the Sanskrit sound Vishvabhadra, which means "universally worthy." The Sutra of Compassionate Flowers says, "'I vow that, in a defiled and evil world, I shall practice the bodhisattva path so as to make it adorned and pure, and my practice must surpass that of every other bodhisattva.' The Buddha Ratnagarbha said: 'On account of this causal vow, I now change your name to Samantabhadra.'" This carries the meaning of the three siddhantas, and is, further, an explanation in terms of causes and conditions, and also a name obtained through vow and practice. As it has come down, from the four foundations of mindfulness up through the four wholesome roots, all are together called "universally worthy"; taken specifically, the dharma called "supreme in the world," bordering on the true and near to sagehood, is what is called "worthy" — this, however, is spoken of within the Tripitaka teaching alone. Here, what is meant is the very summit of the stage of subduing affliction: its cause pervading everywhere is called "universal"; and, after the cutting-off of the path, bordering on the ultimate sage, is called "worthy." If the ten stages of faith are the beginning of the stage of subduing, they are neither the summit nor pervasive, bordering only on the very beginning of the initial sagehood, neither its later stage nor its ultimate; and even the tenth ground is likewise neither pervasive nor ultimate — how much less, then, the stages before it? Here what is discussed is the stage of Virtual Enlightenment, which occupies the very summit of every stage of subduing — since the stage of subduing is here pervasive, it is called "universal"; since the cutting-off of the path is all but complete, with scarcely anything left to compare, bordering on the very final limit, it is called "worthy." The Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom Sutra cites the moon of the fourteenth night as like the moon of the fifteenth — this meaning is thereby made plain.

此約圓教位，釋後位普賢也。「勸發」者，戀法之辭也，遙在彼國具聞此經始末既周，欲令自行化他永永無已，故自東自西而來勸發，具四悉檀意(云云)。文云「我為供養《法華經》故自現其身，若見我身甚大歡喜(其一)；已見我故轉復精進，即得三昧及陀羅尼(其二)；得是陀羅尼故，無有非人能破壞者，亦復不為女人之所惑亂(其三)；三千大千世界微塵菩薩具普賢道(其四)。」如此明文即四悉檀，而來勸發也。上判流通為三：從十九行偈已後三品半，舉經力大以勸流通；〈藥王品〉下五品，舉菩薩化道力大以勸流通；此一品舉普賢誓願力大以勸流通。

This, framed in terms of the perfect teaching's stages, explains Samantabhadra in his later stage. "Encouragement" is a phrase expressing longing for the Dharma: dwelling far off in his own land, having heard this sutra in full, from beginning to end, complete, he wishes his own practice and his converting of others to continue without ever ceasing, and hence he comes from the east, from the west, to give this encouragement — carrying, in full, the meaning of the four siddhantas (and so on). The text says: "Because I wished to make offerings to the Lotus Sutra, I manifested my own body; if beings see my body, they will feel great joy" (this is the first sense); "having already seen me, they will grow ever more diligent, and will at once attain samadhi and dharani" (this is the second sense); "having attained this dharani, there will be no non-human being able to destroy it, nor will they be led astray by any woman" (this is the third sense); "the bodhisattvas, as numerous as motes of dust in the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world, will all be endowed with the path of Samantabhadra" (this is the fourth sense). Such a manifestly plain text is precisely the four siddhantas, and it is with these that he comes to give his encouragement. What was set out above as the threefold propagation of the Dharma to later generations: from the nineteen-line verse onward, through the following three and a half chapters, the sutra's own power is cited as great, to urge its propagation; the five chapters following "Bhaishajyaraja" cite the bodhisattvas' power of converting and guiding as great, to urge its propagation; this one chapter cites Samantabhadra's power of vow as great, to urge its propagation.

Ru-Yi Translation

The text is divided into four parts: first, his coming; second, the encouragement; third, his account confirming it; fourth, the resulting benefit. The first, the sutra-compiler's own account of his coming, falls into three: first, offerings made above; second, converting below; third, paying reverent homage. "Sovereign freedom" is the oneness of principle; "supernatural power" is the oneness of practice; "awesome virtue" is the oneness of the person; "renown" is the oneness of the teaching. Further, "sovereign freedom" is permanence; "supernatural power" is bliss; "awesome virtue" is self; "renown" is purity — spoken of in this way, they are one yet four, virtue lacking nothing, this being the very meaning of sovereign freedom. By the power of purity, flowers rain down; by the power of bliss, music is played; by supernatural power, the earth quakes. By the power of sovereign freedom, he rains down flowers at will, raining wherever he goes, playing music wherever he moves — like a great dragon flying without cease, clouds and rain arising inexhaustibly all about its body.

普賢及眷屬，以菩薩身、用四德力來。勸發四：一、所逕歷處自行上供，其事如此。從「又與諸天龍」下，所逕歷處下化利益，隨他所宜現八部像，略用二力隨所堪任，其事如此。三者、修敬，身旋面禮，如文。

Samantabhadra and his retinue come in bodhisattva form, employing the power of these four virtues. As for the fourfold encouragement: first, wherever he passes, making his own offerings above — the matter is as described. From "and together with the various gods and dragons" onward is the benefit of converting below wherever he passes, manifesting the forms of the eight kinds of non-human beings as suited to each, employing, in brief, two of the powers according to what each is able to bear — the matter is as described. Third, paying reverent homage, circling his body and bowing his face to the ground, as the text has it.

勸發為二：一、請問勸發；二、誓願勸發。有問、有答。問者，遙聞經竟，戀法無已，遠來之志，志在勸發，是故更請。正說勸發自行，更請流通勸發化他，如來若許，二途再演光光無極，是故雙請也。佛答，先總、次別、三結。別列四法名，如文。其既雙請，如來巧答，略舉四以蔽諸。何者？四法之要該括正、通。何者？佛雖無偏，若能遠惡從善，反迷還正，開權知見，顯佛知見者，則稱可聖心，諸佛護念；若佛知見開，則般若照明，是植眾德本；亦是入正定聚，不亂、不味，不取、不捨；亦是發救眾生。當知此四與開權顯實，名異體同，無二無別。

The encouragement falls into two: first, the request that occasions the encouragement; second, the vow that constitutes the encouragement. There is a question, and there is an answer. As for the question: having heard the sutra to its conclusion from afar, his longing for the Dharma without end, his resolve in coming from so distant a place set upon giving encouragement, he therefore makes this further request. The direct request concerns the encouragement of his own practice; the further request concerns the encouragement of propagation, the converting of others — should the Tathagata grant it, both paths would then be expounded once more, their radiance without limit, and it is for this reason that he makes a double request. The Buddha's answer proceeds first with a general statement, then a specific one, and finally a conclusion. The specific portion lists the names of the four dharmas, as the text has it. Since Samantabhadra made a double request, the Tathagata's skillful answer, in brief, raises these four to cover them both. Why is this? Because the essence of the four dharmas embraces both the correct teaching and its propagation. Why is this? Although the Buddha shows no partiality, if one is able to turn from evil far away and follow the good, turning back from delusion to the correct, opening the provisional knowledge and vision to reveal the buddha's own knowledge and vision, this accords with the sage's own mind, and the buddhas guard and are mindful of it; if the buddha's knowledge and vision are opened, then prajna illuminates clearly, and this is the planting of every root of virtue; it is likewise entering the assembly certain of the correct path, neither confused nor deluded, neither grasping nor rejecting; and it is likewise the arousing of the vow to save beings. One should understand that these four are, in name, different from "opening the provisional to reveal the real," yet, in substance, the very same, without any duality or distinction.

又「佛護念」者，是開佛知見；「植眾德本」是示佛知見；「發救眾生」是悟佛知見；「入正定聚」是入佛知見。迹門之要，此四收矣。又迹則有本，從本開示悟入，故有迹中開示悟入。今開迹即顯本，本迹無二無別，以四法答其請正，於義明矣。

Further, "the buddhas guarding and being mindful of it" is the opening of the buddha's knowledge and vision; "planting every root of virtue" is the showing of the buddha's knowledge and vision; "arousing the vow to save beings" is the awakening to the buddha's knowledge and vision; "entering the assembly certain of the correct path" is the entering into the buddha's knowledge and vision. The essence of the trace gate is gathered up entirely in these four. Further, where there is a trace, there is an origin as well; from the origin come opening, showing, awakening, and entering, and hence there is likewise an opening, showing, awakening, and entering within the trace. Here, opening the trace is itself the revealing of the origin — origin and trace are without duality, without distinction — and it is by means of these four dharmas that the request concerning the correct teaching is answered; the meaning is thereby made plain.

以四法答請流通，流通之方，唯三唯四，「發救眾生」是入如來室；「入正定聚、佛所護念」是著如來衣；「植眾德本」是坐如來座。是弘宣之要，即四而三。「發救眾生」是誓願安樂行；「入正定聚」是意安樂行；「植眾德本」是口安樂行；「護念」是身安樂行，當知後四即前四也。一答酬其兩請，舉四冠罩一經，《法華》之重演，斯經之再宣，遠來之勸發，其義如此。

Answering with these four dharmas the request concerning propagation: the means of propagation are but three, yet also but four. "Arousing the vow to save beings" is entering the Tathagata's room; "entering the assembly certain of the correct path" and "the buddhas guarding and being mindful of it" is wearing the Tathagata's robe; "planting every root of virtue" is sitting in the Tathagata's seat — these are the essentials of propagation, four and yet three at once. "Arousing the vow to save beings" is the peaceful practice of vow; "entering the assembly certain of the correct path" is the peaceful practice of mind; "planting every root of virtue" is the peaceful practice of speech; "being guarded and held in mind" is the peaceful practice of body — one should understand that these latter four are none other than the former four. A single answer thus satisfies both requests, and these four raised up together crown the entire sutra — the Lotus repeated once more, this sutra proclaimed again, and the encouragement of one who has come from so far — such is the meaning of it.

三、結者，於如來滅後必得是經。舊云：「能行四法於未來世，常手得是經。」今謂不爾，上文云「諸法實相義，已為汝等說」，又云「咸令眾生開示悟入佛之知見」。蓋《法華》之正體，能行四法必得此解，名解為經，此結其請正之問，若能運此解，行傳與他人，他人得斯信解成初依人，能得真解成第二、第三、第四依人，此結其請流通之問，此意不見，浪作餘說耶？

Third, the conclusion: after the Tathagata's extinction, one will surely obtain this sutra. The older reading says: "One who is able to practice the four dharmas will, in ages to come, constantly hold this sutra in hand." Here it is held that this is not so: the passage above says, "the meaning of the true reality of all dharmas has already been expounded for your sake," and, further, "causing all beings, one and all, to open, show, awaken to, and enter the buddha's own knowledge and vision." This is, after all, the very substance itself of the Lotus — one who is able to practice the four dharmas will surely obtain this very understanding, and it is this understanding, named "sutra," that concludes the question concerning the request for the correct teaching. If one is able to carry this understanding and transmit its practice to others, and those others, obtaining this faith and understanding, become a first-level dependable person, while those able to obtain true understanding become a second-, third-, or fourth-level dependable person — this concludes the question concerning the request for propagation. If this intent goes unrecognized, is it not simply idle talk to say anything else?

Protecting the person falls into six: first, warding off outer difficulties — the first part, warding off difficulties in general, is the meaning of "so that none may find any opportunity to take advantage of him"; next, warding off difficulties specifically, listing the twelve non-human beings. If such a person is already praised by the Buddha's own mouth, has his head touched by the Buddha's hand, and is covered by the Buddha's robe, how much more so you, upon whose causal person the dharani itself provides covering? From "not covetously clinging to worldly pleasures" onward he confirms the raising up of the cause: setting forth at length that, within this cause, there is no fault or wrongdoing whatsoever, the practitioner wanting little, knowing contentment, and cultivating the practice of Samantabhadra — this confirms the superior cause. From "if, after the Tathagata's extinction" onward he confirms the raising up of the near fruit: such a person, bound for the place of enlightenment, will surely accomplish the distant fruit — how much more, then, the near fruit! He will, moreover, obtain this near fruit in the very present life, not merely by being born in the heavens. From "if a person makes light of and slanders one who upholds this sutra" onward he confirms his ability to ward off outer difficulty: the Buddha sets forth at length the offense of one who slanders, causing him, upon recognizing his fault, to surely correct it and cause no further vexation or disturbance to others — so that not only does the one who upholds the sutra escape harm, but even the one who wished to slander him gives rise to blessing instead; with neither slander nor difficulty, both sides find peace and ease — a boundless salvation, without partiality, the very height of compassion. From "one should rise to receive him from afar, as reverently as one would receive the Buddha himself" he confirms the merit of the one who places his faith in this practice.

Reading note: With this sentence Zhiyi's own words on the Lotus Sutra come to an end — the last line of the Fahua Wenju he delivered in his lectures and his disciple Guanding recorded. What follows in the Taishō text is the scribal repetition of the fascicle heading and, beyond it, the modern CBETA edition's own colophon; neither belongs to Zhiyi's commentary, and this translation stops here accordingly.

This closes fascicle ten, the final fascicle of the Fahua Wenju, and with it Ru-Yi's translation of the entire treatise — ten fascicles, moving line by line through the whole of the Lotus Sutra from its opening assembly to Samantabhadra's closing encouragement. It has been a long road from the fourfold exegesis of "如是我聞" in fascicle one to this final page; we are grateful to every reader who has studied it with us along the way.
