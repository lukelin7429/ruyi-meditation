Zhiyi · Tiantai · 妙法蓮華經文句

A Thousand Carts, One Rut.

The Fahua Wenju ("Words and Phrases of the Lotus Sūtra") is Zhiyi's line-by-line commentary on the Lotus Sūtra — not a system built from the sūtra's principles, as the Mohe Zhiguan is, but a phrase-by-phrase gloss of the sūtra's own words, worked through his fourfold interpretive method: causes and conditions, the four teachings, trace and origin, and the contemplation of mind. Ru-Yi's own translation of all ten fascicles is now complete — this page is the entrance for reading it fascicle by fascicle.

How to read this collection

Zhiyi delivered this commentary as oral lectures late in his life, recorded and edited by his disciple Guanding into the ten-fascicle text preserved in the Taishō canon. It goes through the Lotus Sūtra's own words — sometimes a single phrase, sometimes a whole passage — glossing each one through his fourfold method. There is no separate column for "sūtra quote" versus "commentary": Zhiyi cites a short phrase in quotation marks and glosses it in the same breath, and this translation preserves that same interleaved form.

This site already carries Ru-Yi's own translation of the Lotus Sūtra itself; this commentary is Zhiyi's own running gloss on that same text, and this translation follows this site's established English renderings for the sūtra's chapter titles and recurring phrases wherever the Wenju quotes them. Terminology is also kept consistent with this site's three other Zhiyi/Tiantai translations — the Mohe Zhiguan, the Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen, and the Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi — and with the Jiaoguan Gangzong, which quotes the Wenju itself repeatedly as an authority.

Read by Fascicle

All 10 fascicles are complete, linking to Ru-Yi's own translation. Use this as the table of contents for the whole commentary.

Fascicle 1 · The opening chapter begins

A Thousand Carts, One Rut

The exegesis of "prologue" and "chapter," a survey of five- and sixth-century masters' competing schemes for dividing the sūtra, Zhiyi's own threefold division and fourfold interpretive method, and the start of the disciple roll call — Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya's fourfold "non-arising" treatment, used as the template for every disciple that follows, through Mahākāśyapa, Śāriputra, Maudgalyāyana, Kātyāyana, Aniruddha, and Kapphiṇa.

Fascicle 2 · The six auspicious signs

The Six Auspicious Signs

Finishes the disciple roll call, moves into the bodhisattva assembly and its extended "non-retrogression" exegesis, works through the miscellaneous assembly of gods, dragon kings, and human rulers, and closes with the six auspicious signs that lead to Maitreya's question to Mañjuśrī.

Fascicle 3 · "Skillful Means" opens

Stop, Stop — No Need to Speak

Mañjuśrī's answer to Maitreya closes the opening chapter; then the bulk of 方便品 — the threefold etymology of "skillful means," the twin wisdoms of the provisional and the real, the fourfold interpretation of the ten suchnesses, and the doubt that builds to Śāriputra's three requests.

Fascicle 4 · The withdrawal, and the verses

Five Thousand Withdraw

The Buddha answers Śāriputra, five thousand arrogant monks, nuns, and laypeople rise and leave, and the five turbidities are explained — then the entire verse section of "Skillful Means," ending with the groundwork Zhiyi lays for the burning-house parable still to come.

Fascicle 5 · The burning house

The Great White-Ox Cart

Śāriputra's prediction of buddhahood, then the burning-house parable itself — the elder's vast, single-doored house, the sons absorbed in play, the promise of three carts, and their equal exchange for the one great white-ox cart, closed by its point-by-point correlation with the Buddha's teaching.

Fascicle 6 · The destitute son

The Destitute Son Returns

The burning-house parable's entire verse recap, then 信解品 opens with four senior disciples telling the parable of the destitute son — his terror and flight, his years clearing dung at his own father's estate, and the gradual, gentle entrusting of the household.

Fascicle 7 · The phantom city

The Conjured City

Closes "Faith and Understanding," covers the medicinal-herbs parable and the "Prediction" chapter, then gives the full phantom-city parable — a magically conjured city offered as rest on a long, fearful road — before opening the prediction for five hundred disciples.

Fascicle 8 · Five chapters in one

The Dragon Princess's Instant

The most chapter-dense fascicle in the treatise: "Teacher of the Dharma," the Jeweled Stūpa rising from the earth, Devadatta and the dragon princess's instant buddhahood, and "Encouraging the Upholding of This Sūtra" in full, before "Peaceful Practices" opens.

Fascicle 9 · The Lifespan chapter opens

Apparent Extinction, Never Extinct

Closes "Peaceful Practices," gives the countless bodhisattvas who spring from beneath the earth in full, and opens "The Tathāgata's Lifespan" — why the Buddha's teaching is entirely true, and why his apparent passing into extinction never touches his true, constant abiding.

Fascicle 10 · Where the sūtra ends

Samantabhadra's Encouragement

Closes "The Tathāgata's Lifespan," then carries the sūtra through nine more chapters in succession — Never Disparaging, the Tathāgata's Power, Bhaiṣajyarāja, Gadgadasvara, Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate, and more — to Samantabhadra's Encouragement, the sūtra's final chapter, completing both the sūtra and Zhiyi's commentary together.

Translation Notes

Recurring terms, kept consistent as the fascicles grow. See the full translation notes for the complete, fascicle-by-fascicle glossary, and also this site's note on translation method.

因緣 / 約教 / 本迹 / 觀心 — the fourfold interpretive methodCauses and conditions, in terms of the teachings, trace and origin, and the contemplation of mind — the four lenses Zhiyi applies to nearly every passage in the sūtra, kept visible throughout this translation.
四悉檀 — the four siddhāntasThe mundane, the individual, the therapeutic, and the ultimate-meaning siddhānta — a second recurring fourfold scheme, used constantly alongside the interpretive method above.
開三顯一 — opening the three to reveal the oneThe governing theme of "Skillful Means" onward: the three provisional vehicles opened to reveal the single real vehicle beneath them.
迹門 / 本門 — the trace gate and the origin gateThe sūtra's own two halves, as Zhiyi divides it: chapters 1–14 concern the Buddha's trace, his teaching within this one lifetime; chapters 15–28 concern his origin, awakened since a time immeasurably long past.
三車喻 / 大白牛車 — the three carts, and the great white-ox cartThe burning-house parable's central image: three provisional carts promised to draw the children out, exchanged in the end for one real cart, equally given to all.
窮子喻 — the parable of the destitute sonTold by four senior disciples in "Faith and Understanding": a son's flight from his own father's wealth, and the father's patient, gradual way of drawing him back to his inheritance.
化城喻 — the parable of the phantom cityA magically conjured city, offered as a resting-place partway along a long and fearful road — not the final destination, but a skillful means toward it.
皆實不虛 / 非滅現滅 — entirely true, never false / apparent parinirvāṇa, though not actually extinct"The Tathāgata's Lifespan" chapter's central doctrine: every teaching the Buddha gives is true, and his apparent passing into extinction is itself only a skillful means, never touching his true, constant abiding.
三身 — the three bodiesThe Dharma-body, the reward-body, and the response-body — the framework behind the Lifespan chapter's account of what, exactly, "abides" when the Buddha is said to be constant.
常不輕 — Never DisparagingThe bodhisattva who bows to every person he meets, telling each one, "I would never dare disparage you, for you are all certain to become buddhas."
