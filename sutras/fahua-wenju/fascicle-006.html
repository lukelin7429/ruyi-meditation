The Destitute Son Returns

This page continues Ru-Yi's own English translation of Zhiyi's Fahua Wenju (妙法蓮華經文句). Fascicle 6 closes 譬喻品 with its extended verse section, then opens 信解品 ("Faith and Understanding") with the parable of the destitute son — his terror and flight from his father's wealth, the years of menial work clearing dung, the gradual entrusting of the household, and the father's grief and long hope for his son's return, told by four disciples in response to the burning-house parable. We translate slowly, section by section, with the Chinese source beside the English so readers can study the meaning, not merely read around it.

Chinese Source

妙法蓮華經文句卷第六上

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary on the Words of the Sūtra of the Lotus Blossom of the Wondrous Dharma, Fascicle Six, Upper Part

天台智者大師說

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai

The Verses of the Parable Chapter: A Structural Overview 偈頌一百六十五行分科

Chinese Source

◎第二，偈，有一百六十五行，分為二：前有一百行，頌上長行，後有六十五行，明通經方法。上長行有開譬、合譬，偈頌亦二：初有六十五行半，頌開譬；次有三十四行半，頌合譬。初亦二：初有三十三行偈，頌總譬；次有三十二行半偈，頌別譬。總頌六意，六意中止頌其四，兼得其二，頌家宅兼得一門，頌五百人兼得三十子。初一句明長者，即頌上位號，即兼得名行歎德。既有長人之德，即知名行遍為國邑所崇，亦知內外年德俱高也，內合婆伽婆即位號，自知具足智斷慈悲萬德也。「有一大宅」下，第二，三行一句，頌上第二家宅譬，為二：初一句頌宅廣大；

Ru-Yi Translation

From here, second, the verses: there are one hundred sixty-five lines, falling into two parts — the first hundred lines versify the prose section above, and the remaining sixty-five lines set out the method for propagating the sūtra. The prose section above had the opening of the parable and the matching correlation, and the verses likewise fall into two: the first sixty-five-and-a-half lines versify the opening of the parable, and the next thirty-four-and-a-half lines versify the matching correlation. The first of these again falls into two: the first thirty-three lines of verse versify the general parable, and the next thirty-two-and-a-half lines of verse versify the specific parable. The general parable was set out under six points of sense, but of these six points only four are directly versified here, with the remaining two picked up along the way — the verses on the house and dwelling also pick up the one gate, and the verses on the five hundred people also pick up the thirty children. The first line makes plain the elder, versifying his rank and title above, and along with this picking up his name, conduct, and the praise of his virtue as well. Since he possesses the virtue of a person of standing, one thereby knows that his name and conduct are honored throughout the kingdom, city, and village, and also that, within and without, both his years and his virtue stand equally high; inwardly this matches Bhagavān as his rank and title, knowing of himself that he is fully possessed of the myriad virtues of wisdom, severance, loving-kindness, and compassion. From "there was a great house" onward is, second, three-and-a-half lines versifying the second point above, the parable of the house and dwelling; this falls into two: the first half-line versifies the house's vastness.

「其宅久故」下，第二，三行，廣出宅體，明所燒之相，故知此頌宅體也。三界無始為「久」，非今所造為「故」，無常卑鄙名「頓弊」。亦云頭殿、腹堂、背為舍，念念相續無常為「高危」。一云，色界為「堂」，欲界為「舍」，不免墮落名「高危」，命根支持如「柱」，過去行業為「基陛」也。亦云，兩足為「柱根」，三相所遷名「摧朽」也。意識綱維以為「梁棟」，諸苦所壞如「傾斜」。亦云脊骨為「梁棟」，[月*客]為「基陛」，衰老之時為「頹毀」。「牆壁」者，一云，四大為「牆壁」，皮膚為「泥塗」，四威儀不正為「亂墜」，五識不聰不相主境為「差脫」。亦云，「牆壁圮圻」如皮膚皴朽，壯色鮮淨如初泥塗，老色枯悴如後褫落，髮髭朽老則皆脫落如「覆苫亂墜」，筋骨老弱支節不援如「椽梠荖脫」。「周障屈曲」者，印師云：「三十六物更相隔障故云周障，猪腸盤迴故云屈曲，非但無常所遷，亦有不淨苦等，故云雜穢充遍也。」今云，「周障」是六識，「屈曲」是六根，六識緣六根取境，艱關故言「屈曲」，六塵遍染六根故言「雜穢充遍」。因緣、觀心兩番釋(云云)。

From "this house, being old" onward is, second, three lines setting out at length the substance of the house, making clear the marks of what is to be burned — one thereby knows that this versifies the substance of the house. The three realms, beginningless, are "old"; not built in the present is "worn"; and being impermanent and base is called "crumbling and decayed." It is also said that the head is the upper hall, the belly the central hall, and the back the outer chamber, and that being impermanent, ceasing moment after moment, is "towering and precarious." One account holds that the form realm is the "hall" and the desire realm the "chamber," and that being unable to escape falling is called "towering and precarious"; the life-faculty, sustaining the body, is like the "pillar," and past karmic action is the "foundation and steps." It is also said that the two feet are the "pillar's root," and being carried along by the three marks [of arising, abiding, and perishing] is "crumbling and rotten." Consciousness, binding all together, is the "beam and ridgepole," and being ruined by every suffering is "leaning and tilted." It is also said that the spine is the "beam and ridgepole," the flank is the "foundation and steps," and the time of aging and decline is "collapsing and ruined." As for the "walls": one account holds that the four great elements are the "walls," the skin is the "mud plaster," the four dignified postures gone awry are the "scattered and falling," and the five sense-consciousnesses, grown dull and no longer able to master their objects, are the "cracked and come away." It is also said that "walls crumbling and split" are like skin chapped and worn, that youthful color, fresh and clear, is like mud freshly plastered, that the withered, haggard color of old age is like this plaster later peeling away, that hair and beard, decayed with age, all falling out, are like "roof-thatch scattering and falling," and that sinews and bones, grown feeble, joints no longer supporting one another, are like "rafters and eaves rotting loose." As for the "encircling, tortuous windings": Master Yin says, "The thirty-six impure things, mutually screening one another, are called the 'encircling'; the pig's intestines, coiling round and about, are called the 'tortuous windings' — the body is not only carried along by impermanence, it also holds every impurity and suffering, and hence it is said to be 'filled throughout with filth.'" Here it is said instead that the "encircling" is the six consciousnesses and the "tortuous windings" are the six sense-faculties: the six consciousnesses, relying on the six sense-faculties, take hold of their objects, and, this being arduous and obstructed, it is called "tortuous windings"; the six sense-objects pervasively defile the six sense-faculties, and hence it is said to be "filled throughout with filth." Both the causal-condition reading and the contemplation-of-mind reading are given here (and so on, as before).

「有五百人」下，第三，半行，頌上第三，五百人譬。三乘根性為五道所攝，兼得三十子譬也。從「鴟梟」下，第四，有二十九行偈，正頌上第五火起。就此復四：初有二十二偈，明地上事，譬欲界火起；次第二有三偈半，明穴中事，譬色界火起；次第三有二偈半，明空中事，譬無色界火起；後第四，一偈，總結眾難非一。

From "there were five hundred people" onward is, third, half a line versifying the third point above, the parable of the five hundred people: the natures of the three vehicles, subsumed within the five destinies, also pick up along the way the parable of the thirty children. From "kites and owls" onward is, fourth, twenty-nine lines of verse directly versifying the fifth point above, the fire's outbreak. This in turn falls into four: the first twenty-two verses make clear what happens on the ground, a parable for the outbreak of fire in the desire realm; next, second, three-and-a-half verses make clear what happens within the caves, a parable for the outbreak of fire in the form realm; next, third, two-and-a-half verses make clear what happens in the open air, a parable for the outbreak of fire in the formless realm; and, fourth, one verse draws the general conclusion that these many hardships are not just one.

The Five Dull Hindrances: Pride, Anger, Delusion, Greed, and Doubt 五鈍使：慢、瞋、癡、貪、疑

Chinese Source

就欲界火起復為四：初十七偈半，明所燒之類，譬眾生十使；次第二有一偈半，明火起之由，譬起五濁所由；次第三，一行半，正明火起之勢，譬正起五濁；後第四，一行半，明被燒之相，譬受八苦五濁。就第一，十七行半復二：初十六行，正明所燒；後一行半，總結。就所燒中又二：初六行，明禽獸被燒，譬五鈍使眾生；後十行，明鬼神被燒，譬五利使眾生。第一五鈍使，為二：初五行半，明五鈍；第二，半行，結。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the outbreak of fire in the desire realm, this again falls into four: the first seventeen-and-a-half verses make clear the kinds of beings to be burned, a parable for the ten hindrances of sentient beings; next, second, one-and-a-half verses make clear the cause of the fire's outbreak, a parable for what gives rise to the five turbidities; next, third, one-and-a-half lines directly make clear the force of the fire's outbreak, a parable for the five turbidities actually arising; and, fourth, one-and-a-half lines make clear the marks of those being burned, a parable for undergoing the eight sufferings of the five turbidities. Within the first, the seventeen-and-a-half lines, this again falls into two: the first sixteen lines directly make clear what is to be burned, and the remaining one-and-a-half lines draw the general conclusion. Within what is to be burned there are again two: the first six lines make clear birds and beasts being burned, a parable for sentient beings under the five dull hindrances; and the remaining ten lines make clear spirits and demons being burned, a parable for sentient beings under the five sharp hindrances. The first, the five dull hindrances, falls into two: the first five-and-a-half lines make clear the five dull hindrances themselves, and the second, half a line, draws the conclusion.

今初五鈍為五：初半行譬慢使。眾生自舉輕他，如鳥為性陵高下視。八鳥譬八慢，《文殊問經》明八憍，今用配八鳥。盛壯憍如鴟，性憍如梟，富憍如雕，自在憍如鷲，壽命憍如烏，聰明憍如鵲，行善憍如鳩，色憍如鴿，陵他為憍、自貴為憍，自愛為貪、愛他為淫，自忿為恚、忿他為瞋，自惑為愚、惑他為癡(云云)。

Here, first, the five dull hindrances fall into five: the first half-line is a parable for the hindrance of pride. Sentient beings, exalting themselves and belittling others, are like birds, whose very nature is to soar aloft and look down upon what lies below. Eight birds are a parable for eight kinds of conceit; the Sūtra of Mañjuśrī's Questions sets out eight kinds of arrogance, here matched to the eight birds. Arrogance over one's vigor and strength is like the kite; arrogance over one's very nature is like the owl; arrogance over wealth is like the vulture; arrogance over one's freedom is like the osprey; arrogance over one's lifespan is like the crow; arrogance over one's cleverness is like the magpie; arrogance over one's good conduct is like the dove; and arrogance over one's beauty is like the pigeon. Belittling others is conceit, and exalting oneself is likewise conceit; cherishing oneself is greed, and cherishing another is lust; resenting oneself is indignation, and resenting another is anger; being deluded about oneself is folly, and being deluded about another is delusion (and so on, as before).

次「蚖蛇」下，第二，二句，譬瞋使。瞋有三：蚖毒盛，不觸而吸，譬非理生瞋；蝮蠍觸則螫，譬執理瞋；蜈蚣譬戲論瞋。世人云，赤頸者是蜈蚣，不赤者是蚰蜒。

Next, from "vipers and snakes" onward is, second, two lines, a parable for the hindrance of anger. Anger is of three kinds: the viper, its venom potent, draws in its prey without even touching it — a parable for anger that arises without reason; adders and scorpions sting the moment they are touched — a parable for anger that clings to reason; and the centipede is a parable for anger born of idle argument. People commonly say that the one with the red neck is the centipede, and the one without is the house-scolopendra.

「守宮」下，第三，二行，譬癡使。癡有獨起、相應起，守宮百足等兀然，譬獨頭無明，狖狸鼷鼠等譬相應也。

From "house lizards" onward is, third, two lines, a parable for the hindrance of delusion. Delusion arises either on its own or in conjunction with other afflictions: the house lizard, the millipede, and the like, sitting motionless, are a parable for delusion that is solitary and unaccompanied; while the weasel, the raccoon-dog, the shrew, and the like are a parable for delusion that arises in conjunction with other afflictions.

「諸惡蟲輩」下，從癡根本備起諸結也。明諸使相緣，或緣三界如交橫，起之速疾如馳走。「屎尿」下一行，明癡心所著之境，皆無常、苦、無我、不淨，由癡不了，於中計淨等而生染著，故云「蜣蜋諸蟲而集其上」。

From "every kind of evil vermin" onward, this shows every fetter fully arising from the root of delusion. It makes clear how the various hindrances condition one another — whether taking the three realms as their object, crisscrossing like these creatures, or arising with the swiftness of their scurrying about. The line following "dung and urine" makes clear the objects to which the deluded mind clings: these are, every one of them, impermanent, suffering, without self, and impure, but, because of delusion, this is not understood, and, amid them, purity and the rest are imagined, giving rise to defiled attachment — hence it says, "and dung beetles and every kind of vermin gather upon it."

「狐狼」下，第四，二行，明貪使。貪有二種：一有力，二無力。有力者，以威勢取，如狐狼等；無力者，但能從他乞索麁弊，如野干等。「咀嚼」下，明貪取境，引物向己如「咀嚼」，不以道理如「踐踏」。貪心取境，或取一城、或取一國，其有齊畔如「䶩囓」也。亦云，貪心取境有用不用，有用而取如「咀嚼」，不用而取如「踐踏」，又少則咀嚼，多則踐踏也。「骨肉狼藉」者，積聚五塵不知止足也。

From "foxes and wolves" onward is, fourth, two lines, making clear the hindrance of greed. Greed is of two kinds: one, forceful; and, two, without force. The forceful kind seizes by dint of power and menace, like the fox and wolf and the rest; the kind without force can only beg and scrounge coarse leavings from others, like the jackal and the rest. From "gnawing and chewing" onward makes clear greed's taking hold of its objects: drawing things toward oneself is "gnawing and chewing," and taking them without regard for what is proper is "trampling underfoot." When the greedy mind takes hold of its objects, whether seizing a single city or a single kingdom, its having some fixed boundary is "biting and gnashing." It is also said that when the greedy mind takes hold of its objects, there is what it makes use of and what it does not: what it makes use of and takes is "gnawing and chewing," and what it does not make use of yet still takes is "trampling underfoot" — or again, taking a little is "gnawing and chewing," and taking a great deal is "trampling underfoot." "Flesh and bone strewn in disorder" means hoarding up the five sense-objects without knowing when to stop.

「由是群狗競來搏撮」者，此有力貪，搏撮無力之者，謂王賊也。

As for "and because of this, packs of dogs come competing to snatch and seize": this is the forceful kind of greed snatching and seizing from the kind without force — meaning kings and brigands.

「饑羸慞惶」者，常不知足如饑，求不能得如羸，種種營覓如慞惶，多欲之人雖富而貧也。愛心貪，貪五塵之肉；見心貪，貪道理之骨，推求知見遂多所解即是多骨，須骨之狗競來撮之。諸見心中，未得正法之食名飢，不能伏斷見名羸，處處求解名為慞惶。一云，即是貪人希求念望也。「鬪諍摣掣」，第五，二句，譬疑使。

As for "starved, gaunt, and frantic": forever unsatisfied is like starvation; seeking and unable to obtain is like gauntness; casting about in every direction is like frantic distress — for one of great desire, though rich, remains poor. Greed of the affections craves the flesh of the five sense-objects; greed of views craves the bones of doctrine — probing and seeking out views and understanding, one comes to hold many opinions, and this is precisely "many bones," and dogs in need of bones come competing to seize them. Within every kind of view, not yet having obtained the food of the true Dharma is called hunger; being unable to subdue and cut off views is called gauntness; and seeking understanding everywhere, in every direction, is called frantic distress. One account holds that this is simply the greedy person's hoping and longing after something. "Quarreling, contending, grabbing, and clawing" is, fifth, two lines, a parable for the hindrance of doubt.

猶豫二邊名疑，未決是非鬪諍，意謂為是名掣，復謂為非為摣。

Wavering between two extremes is called doubt; not yet settled on right and wrong is quarreling and contending; the mind's supposing something to be right is "clawing," and its then supposing it to be wrong is "grabbing."

「啀喍嘷吠」者，發言論決是非之理也。「恐怖」兩句，第二，結上五鈍使也。

As for "snarling, snapping, howling, and barking": this is giving voice to argument, settling the principle of right and wrong. The two lines on "terror" are, second, the conclusion drawing together the five dull hindrances above.

The Five Sharp Hindrances: Wrong View, Precept-Attachment, Self-View, View-Attachment, and Extreme Views 五利使：邪見、戒取、身見、見取、邊見

Chinese Source

「處處皆有」下，第二，有十行，明五利使。為二：初半行，總明利使，利使遍緣五陰四諦，下故言「處處皆有」。夫鬼神有通有智，禽獸則無，故以利使譬鬼神，鈍使喻蟲獸。「夜叉」下，第二，九行半，別明五利使。為五：初三行，明夜叉，是捷疾鬼，譬邪見撥無因果，人是善報，譬出世因果不雜煩惱；撥無此理，如食人肉也。毒蟲之屬是惡報，如世間因果雜諸煩惱，撥無此理，如噉毒蟲之屬也。「孚乳產生」者，世間之法從自類因生自類果也。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "everywhere there are" onward is, second, ten lines making clear the five sharp hindrances. This falls into two: the first half-line generally makes clear the sharp hindrances — the sharp hindrances range pervasively over the five aggregates and the four truths, and hence it says below, "everywhere there are." Now, spirits and demons possess supernatural powers and intelligence, while birds and beasts have none — hence the sharp hindrances are likened to spirits and demons, and the dull hindrances to insects and beasts. From "yakṣas" onward is, second, nine-and-a-half lines making clear the five sharp hindrances individually. This falls into five: the first three lines make clear the yakṣa, a swift-moving demon, a parable for wrong view, which denies cause and effect. A human being is the fruit of good retribution — a parable for the causes and effects transcending the world, unmixed with any affliction; denying this principle is like devouring human flesh. Poisonous insects and the like are the fruit of evil retribution — like the causes and effects of the world, mixed with every affliction; denying this principle is like feeding on poisonous insects and the like. As for "hatching, suckling, and giving birth": this is the way of the world, whereby the fruit proper to a given kind is born from the cause proper to that same kind.

「各自藏護」者，因能有果名藏，必得不失名護也。又人肉是善，毒蟲是惡，邪見之心，撥無善惡因果，事如噉食也。「孚乳產生」，總說善惡並有因果相生之用也。「食之既飽」者，見心成就也。「惡心熾盛」者，見心增廣也。「鬪諍之聲」者，內心成就、外彰言教，宣於無因無果之法，能令聞者墮落三途，故言怖畏也。

As for "each hoards and guards its own": a cause being able to produce a fruit is called "hoarding," and being sure to obtain it without loss is called "guarding." Again, human flesh is good and poisonous insects are evil, and the mind of wrong view denies both the good and the evil causes and their effects — the matter is like feeding upon them. "Hatching, suckling, and giving birth" states in general that both good and evil possess this function of cause and effect giving rise to one another. As for "having eaten their fill": this is the view-mind reaching completion. As for "their evil minds blazing ever fiercer": this is the view-mind growing ever more extensive. As for "the sound of their quarreling and contending": this is the inward mind reaching completion and, outwardly, manifesting as spoken teaching, proclaiming a doctrine without cause and without effect, able to make whoever hears it fall into the three evil destinies — hence it is called "terrifying."

「鳩槃茶」下，第二，兩行二句，譬戒取。鳩槃茶是鬼勝者，如有漏善能勝諸蟲也。「蹲踞土埵」者，修十善戒能生六天，六天是欲界高處，事如土埵也。又外道持戒能修禪定，初得欲界定，或得未來定，未來定未脫欲界，欲界之頂如土埵也。「或離一尺二尺」者，得色界定如一尺，得無色處定如二尺，得升上界為「往」，退墮為「反」，起見蓋如「縱逸嬉戲」。「捉狗兩足」，一云，謗無苦因如捉狗足，撥無苦果如脚加頸，集本得果如狗之聲，利見撥言無集無得苦之理，令其失聲也。觀解者，修六行觀伏貪，貪不行似如被斷為失聲；狗是欲貪，兩足為覺觀，覺觀往還常在貪境，數息止心是能縛義，為捉覺觀也。撲者，貪覺若強向不淨境，作不淨觀伏貪覺，貪覺摧伏，如狗被撲因不能聲。又云，作不淨觀如撲狗，能生禪定如被撲失聲也。「脚加頸」者，如狗雖被撲，擾動不伏，更以脚加；貪雖知不淨止，貪猶未甚靜，更以無常觀脚，加保常之頸則生怖畏，則貪覺不起也。又云，一往制心如向地撲；常繫在緣，如脚加頸令不得起也；「怖狗自樂」者，以修無常覺悟貪心，如怖狗，因得禪味名自樂也。

From "kumbhāṇḍas" onward is, second, two-and-a-half lines, a parable for attachment to precepts. The kumbhāṇḍa is the more powerful kind of demon, just as tainted good is able to surpass every kind of insect. As for "crouching upon the earthen mound": cultivating the ten wholesome precepts is able to bring about birth among the six heavens, and, the six heavens being the higher regions of the desire realm, the matter is like an earthen mound. Again, non-Buddhist ascetics who uphold precepts are able to cultivate meditative absorption, first attaining the concentration of the desire realm, or perhaps attaining the "not-yet-attained" concentration — this "not-yet-attained" concentration has not yet freed them from the desire realm, and the summit of the desire realm is like an earthen mound. As for "leaving it a foot, or two feet": attaining the concentration of the form realm is like a foot, and attaining the concentration of the formless realms is like two feet; rising into the higher realm is "going," and falling back is "returning," and the arising of the hindrance of views is like "unrestrained frolic and play." As for "seizing the dog by both its legs": one account holds that slandering the truth that there is no cause of suffering is like seizing the dog's legs, denying the truth that there is a fruit of suffering is like putting a foot to its neck, and the fact that origination, taken as a cause, has a fruit is like the dog's cry — the sharp views deny that there is any principle of origination or of the attainment of the cessation of suffering, and this makes the dog lose its voice. According to the contemplative reading: cultivating the sixfold practice of contemplation to subdue greed, greed's ceasing to act is likened to its being cut off, and this is "losing its voice"; the dog is desirous greed, the two legs are discursive and reflective thought, discursive and reflective thought going back and forth, forever dwelling in the object of greed, and counting the breath to still the mind has the sense of being able to bind — this is "seizing" discursive and reflective thought. "Striking down" means that, when the greedy, discursive mind presses strongly toward an impure object, one cultivates the contemplation of impurity to subdue this greedy, discursive mind, and the greedy, discursive mind, being overcome and subdued, is like the dog, struck down, thereby unable to cry out. It is also said that cultivating the contemplation of impurity is like striking down the dog, and that being thereby able to give rise to meditative absorption is like the dog, struck down, losing its voice. As for "a foot placed at its neck": just as the dog, though struck down, still struggles and does not submit, so that a foot must further be placed upon it, so too greed, though it knows to cease at the impure, is still not entirely stilled, and one must further apply the contemplation of impermanence, placing this foot at the neck that clings to permanence, whereupon terror arises and greedy, discursive thought no longer arises. It is also said that subduing the mind at the outset is like striking it to the ground, and that keeping it forever bound to its object is like placing a foot at its neck so that it cannot rise. As for "the terrified dog, delighting in itself": by cultivating the contemplation of impermanence, awakening the greedy mind, this is like the terrified dog — and, thereby attaining the flavor of meditative absorption, this is called "delighting in itself."

「其身長大」下，第三，一行半偈，譬身見。豎入三世計我名「長」，橫遍五陰計我名「大」，計我自在不修善法，即無慚愧故言「裸形」，以惡莊嚴故言「黑」，無功德資故言「瘦」。計我者不出三界，故言「常住其中」。計我在心，發言宣說有我之相，故言「發大惡聲」。冀因此說望得道果，故言「叫呼求食」也。

From "its body long and huge" onward is, third, one-and-a-half lines of verse, a parable for the view of self. Reaching lengthwise into the three times and imagining a self there is called "long"; extending crosswise throughout the five aggregates and imagining a self there is called "huge." Imagining a self as free and autonomous, one does not cultivate wholesome dharmas, and this very absence of conscience and shame is called "naked"; adorning oneself with evil is called "black"; and lacking any provision of merit is called "emaciated." Those who imagine a self do not go beyond the three realms — hence it says, "forever dwelling within it." Imagining a self within the mind, one gives voice to it, proclaiming the mark of there being a self — hence it says, "uttering great and evil cries." Hoping by means of this proclamation to obtain the fruit of the path, it says, "shrieking and calling out for food."

「復有諸鬼」下，第四，半行，譬見取。咽細命危而保其壽，非想無常而計涅槃，故言「其咽如針」。

From "there were, further, other demons" onward is, fourth, half a line, a parable for view-attachment. Its throat being narrow, its life imperiled, and yet it clings to its own life — the peak of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, itself impermanent, is nevertheless imagined to be nirvāṇa, and hence it says, "its throat like a needle."

「首如牛頭」下，第五，兩行，譬邊見。推我斷常，斷常二邊如牛頭二角，為身是我、為我是身，依我見起邊見，如頭兩角也。計常斷之過，能斷出世善，如食人肉。能斷世善根，如「或時噉狗」。或時計常，或復計斷，前後迴轉如「頭髮蓬亂」。計常即破斷，計斷即破常，如「殘害兇險」。無有智定食飲自資，如「飢渴所逼」。

From "its head like an ox's head" onward is, fifth, two lines, a parable for extreme views. Reasoning from the self to permanence or annihilation, these two extremes of permanence and annihilation are like the ox-head's two horns; taking the body as the self, or taking the self as the body — relying on the view of self, extreme views arise, just as the two horns arise from the one head. The fault of imagining permanence or annihilation is able to cut off wholesome dharmas transcending the world — like devouring human flesh; and it is able to cut off wholesome roots within the world — like "at times devouring dogs." At times imagining permanence, and at other times imagining annihilation, turning back and forth between the two, is like "its hair disheveled and disordered." Imagining permanence overturns annihilation, and imagining annihilation overturns permanence — like "savage, cruel, and violent." Having no meditative concentration of wisdom to nourish itself is like "driven by hunger and thirst."

How the Fire Broke Out, and What It Consumed 結欲界煩惱、火起之由、被燒之相

Chinese Source

「夜叉餓鬼」下，第二，一行半，總結欲界煩惱之相，亦是結利純眾生之相，並是有漏之心，常無道味，故云「飢急」。「窺看牖」者，明其邪觀空理慕仰道味，雖復觀察而滯著，心多不會正理，如窺窓見空不得無礙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "yakṣas and hungry ghosts" onward is, second, one-and-a-half lines, drawing together the marks of the afflictions of the desire realm in general — this is likewise a conclusion to the marks of sentient beings under the sharp and dull hindrances together, all of them being minds with outflows, forever without the flavor of the path — hence it says, "hunger-driven and urgent." As for "peering in at the window": this makes clear their wrongly contemplating the principle of emptiness, longing after the flavor of the path — although they do go on contemplating, they remain fixed and attached, and their minds mostly fail to comprehend the true principle, like peering in at a window and glimpsing the emptiness beyond without attaining the unobstructed.

「是朽故宅屬于一人」下，第二，有一偈，明失火之由。三界是佛化應之處，發心已來誓願度脫，故云屬于一人。長者在宅能令慎火，由出去後諸子無知故令火起，內合正由如來大通佛時常教是等令伏五濁眾生，感盡如來捨應，此等於後便起五濁，他土赴緣非是永去，故言「近出」。又云，從得無生已，不生三界故名「出」，不久應來故言「近」。〈壽量品〉云「數現涅槃」，即是出宅意也。「於後宅舍」下，第三，二行，正明火起之勢。「四面」即是處所，身受心法等起四倒五濁八苦，故云「一時」，相續漸增為「熾」，命根斷為「爆」，風刀解體為「裂」。

From "this decrepit, old house belongs to a single person" onward is, second, one verse, making clear the cause of the fire's outbreak. The three realms are precisely the place where the Buddha's transformative response occurs, and, from the time he first set his mind on awakening, he has vowed to deliver and liberate all beings — hence it says, "belongs to a single person." While the elder remained within the house he was able to see that fire was guarded against, but, once he had gone out, his sons, in their ignorance, allowed the fire to break out — inwardly, this matches how, at the time of the Buddha of Great Universal Wisdom-Excellence, the Tathāgata constantly taught these beings, causing them to subdue the five turbidities, but, once their karmic affinity was exhausted, the Tathāgata set aside this response and, these beings afterward giving rise to the five turbidities once more, he responded to conditions in other lands rather than departing forever — hence it says, "recently gone out." It is also said that, from the attainment of non-arising onward, no longer being born within the three realms is called "gone out," while returning again before long is called "recently." The Chapter on the Life-Span says, "repeatedly manifesting nirvāṇa" — this is precisely the sense of going out from the house. From "afterward, within the dwelling" onward is, third, two lines, directly making clear the force of the fire's outbreak. "On every side" is precisely the place — the body, sensation, mind, and dharmas, and the rest, giving rise to the four inversions, the five turbidities, and the eight sufferings — hence it says, "at one and the same time"; continuing on and gradually increasing is "blazing"; the life-faculty being cut off is "bursting"; and the body dissolving as though cut by the knife-edged wind is "splitting."

又云，受苦悲痛呻吟聲名為「爆」，諸根破壞為「裂」，氣斷骨離筋絕，為「摧折墮落」，四大解散為「牆壁崩倒」也。「諸鬼神等」下，第四，一行半，明被燒之相。或云，親屬為「鬼神」，哭泣為「揚聲」，今例上利使以譬神鬼。利使之人或計斷常，若計常者謂法定空，已有還無，無即常；計斷之人謂法定斷，唯此一死更無復續，皆唱言定說其事已顯，故云「揚聲大叫」也。若是鈍使及諸戒取，本不計斷，今見無常但生疑怖，不知出離之方，故言「慞惶不能自出」。

It is also said that the sound of groaning and moaning under the pain of suffering is called "bursting"; the destruction of every faculty is "splitting"; the breath cut off, the bones parted, and the sinews severed is "shattering, breaking, and falling"; and the four great elements dissolving apart is "the walls collapsing and toppling." From "every kind of spirit and demon" onward is, fourth, one-and-a-half lines, making clear the marks of those being burned. One account holds that family members are the "spirits and demons," and their wailing is "raising their voices" — here, following the pattern above, the sharp hindrances are taken as a parable for spirits and demons. Those under the sharp hindrances reckon either annihilation or permanence: those who reckon permanence hold that dharmas are fixedly empty, so that, having existed, they return to non-existence, and this non-existence is itself permanence; those who reckon annihilation hold that dharmas are fixedly cut off, so that, once this one death has occurred, there is no further continuation — all of them proclaim this as settled, declaring the matter to be already manifest, and hence it says, "raising their voices in great cries." As for those under the dull hindrances, and those under attachment to precepts: they do not, to begin with, reckon annihilation, and, now seeing impermanence, they simply give rise to doubt and terror, not knowing any way of escape — hence it says, "frantic and unable to find their own way out."

The Fires of the Form and Formless Realms 色界、無色界火起，總結三界

Chinese Source

「惡獸毒蟲」下，第二，三行半，明穴中事，譬色界火起，諸部解義瞋通三界，即此文也。文為四：初一行，明所燒之類。四禪之定，譬如孔穴也，雖復不及門外敞豁，猶得免於猛炎，入禪定中猶得免於欲界麁惡也。利使眾生亦得禪定，如毘舍闍鬼亦住其中。「薄福德故」一句，是第二，火起之由，由少福故近惡遇苦。「為火所逼」一句，是第三，明火起之勢，孔穴之中雖無猛炎猶有熱惱，四禪雖無欲界惡，亦有愛味細苦，故言為火所逼。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "wild beasts and poisonous vermin" onward is, second, three-and-a-half lines, making clear what happens within the caves, a parable for the outbreak of fire in the form realm — the various schools' explanation, that anger extends through all three realms, is confirmed by this very text. The text falls into four: the first line makes clear the kind of beings to be burned. The concentrations of the four dhyānas are a parable for the caves: although not as open and spacious as what lies outside the gate, one nevertheless escapes the fierce flames there, just as, entering into meditative absorption, one nevertheless escapes the coarse evils of the desire realm. Sentient beings under the sharp hindrances also attain meditative absorption, just as the piśāca demon likewise dwells within these caves. The single line "because their merit is meager" is, second, the cause of the fire's outbreak — because their merit is slight, they draw near to evil and encounter suffering. The single line "pressed upon by the fire" is, third, making clear the force of the fire's outbreak: within the caves, although there are no fierce flames, there is still a burning distress, for the four dhyānas, though free of the evils of the desire realm, still harbor the subtle suffering of craving after their own flavor — hence it says, "pressed upon by the fire."

「共相殘害」下，第四，二行，明被燒之相，明利鈍相奪，諸使眾生得禪是同，所計各異，異故互相是非，如相殘害也。既於禪中起諸見，則不能生無漏定慧，但著默然如「飲血」，又著五支如「噉肉」，「野干」是欲界貪，未來定已斷，故言「並已前死」，亦名「食噉」，禪定之貪如大惡獸能吞欲界貪也。欲界四倒八苦如「猛炎」，色界四倒此苦如「臭煙」，亦通身受心法四大皮肉等，故言「四面充塞」也。

From "mutually preying upon and harming one another" onward is, fourth, two lines, making clear the marks of those being burned, making clear how the sharp and the dull hindrances contend against one another: the various beings under these hindrances are alike in having attained meditative absorption, but what they severally reckon differs, and, differing, they contend with and oppose one another, as though preying upon and harming one another. Once views have arisen within meditative absorption, one can no longer give rise to the concentration and wisdom free of outflows, but instead clings to silent stillness, as though "drinking blood," or clings to the five factors of dhyāna, as though "devouring flesh." The "jackal" is the greed of the desire realm — the "not-yet-attained" concentration having already cut this off, it is said to have "already died before" — this is also called "devouring," for the greed belonging to meditative absorption is like a great, fierce beast able to swallow up the greed of the desire realm. The four inversions and eight sufferings of the desire realm are like the "fierce flames," and this same suffering of the four inversions in the form realm is like the "foul smoke" — this too pervades the body, sensation, mind, and dharmas, the four great elements, the flesh, and the rest, and hence it says, "filling every side."

蜈蚣下，第三，二行半偈，明空中事，譬無色界火起。為二：初一行，明所燒之類；後一行半，明所燒之相。厭色界定出向無色，厭色麁境觀無色法，如毒蛇類火燒出穴，若爾瞋通三界也。若得無色定必滅下緣，故云「隨取而食」也。非想最頂，猶尚不免顛倒諸苦，如「頭上火然」。非想亦有八苦之火：心生異念名生苦；念念不住名老苦；行心擾擾妨定名病苦；退定是死苦；求定不得是求不得苦；求定不得必有於障，即怨憎會苦；四陰心即五盛陰苦。不能即斷有頂種，故頭上火然也。無無漏故飢渴所惱，猶是輪迴周慞悶走也。「其宅如是」下，第四，一行，總結三界眾難非一，頌總譬竟。

From "centipedes" onward is, third, two-and-a-half lines of verse, making clear what happens in the open air, a parable for the outbreak of fire in the formless realm. This falls into two: the first line makes clear the kinds of beings to be burned, and the remaining one-and-a-half lines make clear the marks of what is to be burned. Wearied of the concentration of the form realm, one emerges from it toward the formless; wearied of the coarse objects of form, one contemplates the formless dharmas — like a poisonous snake, sensing the fire, fleeing out of its den — and, if this is so, then anger indeed extends through all three realms. Once one attains the concentration of the formless realm, one is bound to extinguish objects of the lower realms, and hence it says, "seizing and devouring whatever they take hold of." Even at the very summit, the peak of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, one still cannot escape the various sufferings of inversion — like "fire blazing above their heads." The peak of neither-perception-nor-non-perception also has the fire of the eight sufferings: the mind's giving rise to a differing thought is the suffering of birth; thought after thought not abiding is the suffering of old age; the mind's activity, disturbed and agitated, obstructing concentration, is the suffering of sickness; falling back from concentration is the suffering of death; seeking concentration and not obtaining it is the suffering of not obtaining what one seeks; seeking concentration and not obtaining it must involve some obstruction, and this is precisely the suffering of encountering what one resents; and the mind of the four aggregates is itself the suffering of the five aggregates in their blazing fullness. Being unable to cut off at once the seed belonging to the peak of existence, the fire blazes above the head; and, lacking anything free of outflows, one is troubled by hunger and thirst, still caught in the cycle of rebirth, wandering about in frantic distress. From "this house, being such as it is" onward is, fourth, one line, drawing the general conclusion that the many hardships of the three realms are not just one — and with this, the versifying of the general parable is complete.

Verses on the Specific Parable: Seeing the Fire 頌別譬：長者見火

The first, two-and-a-half lines, versify the elder seeing the fire; the next, second, thirteen lines, versify the parable of setting aside the bench and using the cart, holding back the great teaching and bestowing the lesser; and the next, third, seventeen lines, versify the parable of bestowing the great cart. As for "standing": abiding in the ground of the dharma-body, forever cherishing great compassion, wishing to save sentient beings, he does not remain seated in the seat of ultimate emptiness. According to the old account, the buddhas of the ten directions spoke to Śākyamuni, saying, "You have sons bound to you by karmic affinity, now within the three realms, their wholesome roots about to be extinguished." It is also said that sentient beings' capacity to move the Buddha is itself "another person." Here it is said instead that the Dharma is the Buddha's own teacher — meaning the dharma of samādhi — and this dharma, serving as teacher, is precisely "another person": if one enters samādhi, one is thereby able to perceive the occasion, samādhi itself causing the Buddha to perceive it, and hence it says, "someone spoke, saying." 問：
子本未出，云何因戲來入？

Question:
If the sons had never yet come out, how could they, on account of their play, have come to enter in?

答：
或曾發心，名出三界，而復退還名之為入。如人舉足欲出門側而反，亦名為出亦名還入也。又理性本淨非三界法，因無明故而起戲論便有生死，故云「先因遊戲來入」也。

Answer:
It may be that they had once set their mind on awakening, and this is called coming out of the three realms, but then fell back and returned, which is called entering in — like a person who lifts his foot, meaning to go out through the gate, but then turns aside and goes back; this may equally be called "coming out" and "going back in." Again, the nature of principle is originally pure and is not itself a dharma of the three realms, but, because of ignorance, idle discourse arises, and birth-and-death then follows — hence it says, "at first, on account of their play, they came to enter in."

Great goodness not yet taken hold is "young and small"; being covered over by ignorance is "without knowledge." From "having heard this, he rushed in, alarmed" onward is, third, two lines, versifying the earlier "at once felt great terror and gave rise to a mind of great compassion."

「得如是乘」下，第二，三偈，頌得車歡喜。就此復二：初二偈，明各得大車；後「以是因緣」下，第二，一偈，結勸信也。今初二偈，「日夜」者，初得佛知見中道智光如日分，無明在如夜；自得中道智如日，慈悲入生死如夜。常行二法故言「遊戲」。三乘之人同入佛智，故云「與諸菩薩及聲聞眾」。又此明自行、化他，自獲是乘，故言「日夜遊戲」；以此化他，故言「與諸菩薩及聲聞眾」。能化三乘，「同乘寶乘」也。次一偈，所說一乘無三因緣，於十方土審實而求唯一無二，除佛方便則不在言耳。他云，菩薩若不索車，何因乘車歡喜？

From "having attained such a vehicle" onward is, second, three verses, versifying the joy at attaining the cart. This again falls into two: the first two verses make clear each one attaining the great cart, and, next, from "by this causal condition" onward, second, one verse draws the conclusion, exhorting to faith. As for the first two verses: "day and night" means that at first attaining the Buddha's knowledge and vision, the light of the wisdom of the middle way is like the portion belonging to day, while ignorance still present is like night; and that, having attained for oneself the wisdom of the middle way, this is like day, while entering birth-and-death through loving-kindness and compassion is like night. Constantly practicing both these dharmas is called "playing there." Practitioners of the three vehicles alike enter the Buddha's wisdom, and hence it says, "together with the bodhisattvas and the assembly of śrāvakas." This, further, makes clear both practice for oneself and the transformation of others: obtaining this vehicle for oneself is "playing there day and night," and using it to transform others is "together with the bodhisattvas and the assembly of śrāvakas" — being able to transform the three vehicles is "riding together upon the precious vehicle." In the next verse, the one vehicle that is taught has no threefold cause and condition; sought out and verified as true throughout the lands of the ten directions, it is one alone, without a second — except by the Buddha's own skillful means, it does not lie within the reach of words. Another account holds: if the bodhisattva does not ask for a cart, on what occasion could there be joy at riding in a cart?

「告舍利弗，汝諸人等」下，第四，有十五行半偈，頌上第四合不虛譬。上合有二，先舉二譬，後合不虛；今但頌合不虛，文為二：初三行半，正頌合不虛章門；次「若有菩薩」下，第二，十二偈，頌合釋不虛，身子作稱本心不虛譬。父本欲令子得出難故設三車，既得免難乃至不與小車，亦不違先心，是故不虛。佛頌其譬則明不虛，明佛本意即欲說一，但為五濁不肯信受，故說於三，濁障既除還說一大，即稱本心也。今初，章門為三：初一行，先定父子，明本欲與大，故文云皆是吾子，理應平等與大也。次「汝等」下，第二，一偈，明乃說三乘意為除障，故云汝等累劫眾苦所燒。次「我雖先」下，第三，一行半，既已障除，還遂本心與大乘法，故云「今所應作唯佛智慧」也。

From "Śāriputra, tell all these people" onward is, fourth, fifteen-and-a-half lines of verse, versifying the matching of the fourth point above, the parable of freedom from falsehood. The matching correlation above had two parts — first raising the two parables, and afterward matching the freedom from falsehood — but here only the matching of the freedom from falsehood is versified, the text falling into two: the first three-and-a-half lines directly versify the matching of the chapter-heading on freedom from falsehood; and next, from "if there are bodhisattvas" onward, second, twelve verses versify the matching of the explanation of freedom from falsehood, Śāriputra himself constructing the parable of this being in keeping with the original intention, and hence free of falsehood. The father, from the start, wished only to bring his sons safely out of danger, and so devised the three carts; once they had escaped the danger, even had the lesser carts not been given, this still would not have gone against his original intention, and hence there was no falsehood. The Buddha, in versifying this parable, thereby makes clear the freedom from falsehood — making clear that the Buddha's own original intention was, from the start, to teach the one, but, because those under the five turbidities would not believe and accept it, he taught the three instead, and, the obstruction of the turbidities now removed, he teaches once more the one great vehicle, this being exactly in keeping with his original intention. Here, first, the chapter-heading falls into three: the first line first fixes father and son together, making clear that he had, from the start, wished to give the great vehicle, and hence the text says, "all are my own children," it being proper, by principle, that he should give the great vehicle equally to them all. Next, from "you" onward, second, one verse makes clear that the reason he then taught the three vehicles was to remove this obstruction, and hence it says, "you, for many kalpas, have been consumed by every suffering." Next, from "though I formerly" onward, third, one-and-a-half lines make clear that, the obstruction now removed, he returns to his original intention and gives the Dharma of the great vehicle, and hence it says, "what ought now to be done is only the Buddha's own wisdom."

「若有菩薩」下，第二，十二行，頌合釋，還釋前三意。初二偈，釋同皆是子理應平等；次「若人小智」下，第二，七偈，釋不能受故乃說三乘；次「其實未」下，第三，有三偈，釋後若堪能還與其大。今初二行，「若有菩薩」者，方便三乘所化眾生，皆是昔日結緣佛子，亦皆同有真如佛性，故云皆是菩薩也。「若人小智」下，第二，七行，明小智障重不即信受，為是方便開三接引小智。「為說苦諦」者，聲聞於三乘中最小，復以苦諦為初門。「眾生心喜」者，稱其本習則喜，本厭生死自求涅槃，今聞出離即會宿習故歡喜。此中正明有作四諦。「但離虛妄」者，無明已是不實，通惑附無明起，故呼之為虛妄；有作四諦但除此惑，名為解脫，脫於分段未脫變易故非自在。

From "if there are bodhisattvas" onward is, second, twelve lines, versifying the matching explanation, once again explaining the three senses above. Here, first, the two lines: "if there are bodhisattvas" refers to the sentient beings transformed by the skillful means of the three vehicles, all of them being buddha's children who formed this karmic bond in former times, and all of them alike possessing the buddha-nature of true suchness — hence it says, "all are bodhisattvas." From "if a person of lesser wisdom" onward, second, seven lines make clear that, their obstruction of lesser wisdom being grave, they could not at once believe and accept the great vehicle, and, for this reason, by skillful means, the three were opened out to draw in and guide those of lesser wisdom. As for "taught the truth of suffering": the śrāvaka, being the smallest of the three vehicles, was taught by way of the truth of suffering as the initial gate. As for "the minds of these beings rejoiced": corresponding to what they had formerly practiced, they rejoiced — having, from the start, wearied of birth-and-death and sought nirvāṇa for themselves, now, hearing of release, they found this in keeping with their former practice, and so rejoiced. Here it directly makes clear the four truths that are constructed. As for "only leaving behind falsehood": ignorance is already itself unreal, and the pervasive afflictions, attaching themselves to ignorance, arise from it, and are hence called "falsehood"; the constructed four truths only remove this affliction, and this is called liberation — being freed from the fragmentary birth-and-death of the six destinies, but not yet freed from the birth-and-death of transformation, this liberation is not yet true freedom.

「其實未得」下，第三，三偈，釋障既除情根又利，還遂本心與大乘法。佛本欲與一切解脫，今汝始斷分段，非大涅槃，以其未得一切故，終是未稱本心，故言「我意不欲令至滅度」。今則還令得無上道入大涅槃，乃是究竟稱佛本心。一切解脫即是無作滅諦，無上道即是無作道諦，用二諦破無作苦習，昔欲說此而此眾生不堪，障既已除還說此也。

From "in reality, not yet attained" onward is, third, three verses, explaining that, the obstruction now removed and their faculties grown sharper, he returns and gives them, in accordance with his original intention, the Dharma of the great vehicle. The Buddha's original wish was to give complete liberation to all, but these beings have now, for the first time, only cut off the fragmentary birth-and-death — this is not the great nirvāṇa, and, because they have not yet attained the whole of liberation, this still does not, in the end, accord with his original intention, and hence it says, "it was not my intention to bring them only to this extinction." Now, instead, he causes them to attain the unsurpassed path and enter the great nirvāṇa, and this alone is what truly, ultimately accords with the Buddha's original intention. Complete liberation is precisely the uncreated truth of cessation, and the unsurpassed path is precisely the uncreated truth of the path — by means of these two truths he breaks through the uncreated truths of suffering and its origination; formerly he had wished to teach these very truths, but these beings were not yet capable of receiving them, and now, the obstruction having been removed, he teaches them after all.

佛為法王，於權實法已得自在，開三顯一實，豈當有虛也。

The Buddha, being the king of the Dharma, has already attained mastery over the provisional and the real alike; in opening the three to reveal the one reality, how could there ever be any falsehood in this?

Explaining the "Faith and Understanding" Chapter

有人言，信解三法，謂一往化、隨逐化、畢竟化，昔說大為一往，背大後為隨逐，父子相見為畢竟。又人天善為一往，說小乘齊法華為隨逐，說法華得記為畢竟。又初說二乘為一往，轉教為隨逐，法華為畢竟。又轉教為一往，歷方等為隨逐，悟一乘為畢竟。又說《法華》為一往，十地常教化為隨逐，至金剛心為畢竟。又結僧那為一往，中間為隨逐，得佛為畢竟。私謂諸解重疊，玉屑非寶，夫一往非本懷，畢竟是宗極，說人天、二乘為一往，可非本懷，昔為說大今說《法華》，箇是畢竟那稱一往？若《法華》畢竟而更成一往，人天一往還成畢竟，則大顛倒。又二乘是一往，草庵須破；昔大為一往，繫珠亦須破。若一破一不破，一是一往、一非一往。又父子相見是畢竟者，前畢竟應悟一，則後畢竟無復用；若後畢竟乃悟一，前畢竟非畢竟。節節有妨，今皆不用。有人言，此品是迹。

Some say that "faith and understanding" comprises three stages of transformation: the initial, the following, and the final. Formerly teaching the great vehicle was the initial stage; turning away from the great and coming after was the following stage; and father and son seeing one another again was the final stage. Others say that the goodness of humans and devas was the initial stage, that teaching the Lesser Vehicle up through the Lotus was the following stage, and that teaching the Lotus and granting the prediction was the final stage. Others say that first teaching the two lesser vehicles was the initial stage, that turning to teach others was the following stage, and that the Lotus was the final stage. Others say that turning to teach others was the initial stage, that passing through the Vaipulya teachings was the following stage, and that awakening to the one vehicle was the final stage. Others say that teaching the Lotus itself was the initial stage, that the constant teaching and transformation through the ten grounds was the following stage, and that reaching the vajra-like mind was the final stage. Others say that forming the great vow was the initial stage, that the interval between was the following stage, and that attaining buddhahood was the final stage. In my own view, these various explanations pile up upon one another, jade shavings rather than the jewel itself. Now, the initial stage is not the original intention, and the final stage is the ultimate purport; teaching humans, devas, and the two lesser vehicles as the initial stage may indeed not be the original intention — but if what was formerly taught was the great vehicle, and what is now taught is the Lotus, how can this, being final, be called merely initial? If the Lotus, though final, is turned once more into something merely initial, and the initial stage of humans and devas is turned instead into something final, this is a great inversion. Again, if the two lesser vehicles are the initial stage, then the thatched hut must be broken down; if the great vehicle formerly taught is the initial stage, then the pearl sewn into the garment must likewise be broken down. If one is broken down and the other is not, then one is truly initial and the other is not. Again, if father and son seeing one another is the final stage, then, if the earlier "final" ought already to have brought about awakening to the one, the later "final" would have no further use; but if it is the later "final" that brings about this awakening, then the earlier "final" was not truly final. At every turn there is some difficulty, and none of these accounts will do here. Some say that this chapter belongs to the trace gate.

何者？如來成道已久，乃至中間中止亦是迹耳。私謂義理乃然，在文不便。何者？佛未說本迹，那忽豫領？若未會三已應悟一(云云)。

Why is this? Because the Tathāgata's attainment of the path lies already so long in the past, and even the intervening pause belongs to the trace as well. In my own view, this is indeed so in principle, but it is inconvenient with respect to the actual text. Why is this? The Buddha has not yet taught the trace and the origin — how, then, could this chapter suddenly anticipate and take hold of that teaching in advance? If the three had not yet been brought together, awakening to the one ought already to have occurred (and so on, as before).

First, as for their standing at the head of the assembly: their years in the Dharma being already so many, those who came later took them as a model, and, were they now suddenly to change course and abandon the lesser to seek the greater, this would draw the disapproval of those who followed after — guarding this position of their own accord, they therefore did not seek it. Second, their years as laypeople being already advanced: were they to act as bodhisattvas, they ought to devote themselves wholly to the great path and broadly deliver sentient beings, but, being now aged and decrepit, they were fit for no such task, and therefore did not seek it. Third, having already attained nirvāṇa, the unconditioned, correct position, they could not give rise to the great mind — just as the lotus does not grow on high, dry ground — their wisdom of exhaustion and of non-arising being already established, they had nothing further to rely upon or seek. Why is this? In explaining these three reasons for not seeking, the text does not proceed in strict order: it first explains not seeking because of having attained nirvāṇa, and next explains their advanced years, which also takes in their standing at the head of the assembly, or perhaps points to their having, for so long a time, taken no delight in the Dharma being taught. In explaining not seeking because of their standing at the head of the assembly, having already spoken of being seated in the assembly and further spoken of their advanced years, one thereby knows this to be the explanation of their standing at the head.

釋不求如文。「我等今於佛前」下，陳得解之由，由遠聞五章略廣開三顯實，是故慶幸。「獲大善利」者，正陳得解，是近聞四番譬喻希有之法而獲開悟，開悟善利也。「無量珍寶」下，第二是略舉譬，譬昔不求而今自得希有法寶也。

The explanation of not seeking is as the text has it. From "we, today, before the Buddha" onward, they set forth the reason for their attaining understanding: at a distance, they had heard the five sections opening the three, briefly and then extensively, to reveal the real, and it is for this reason that they now rejoice. As for "obtained this great and good benefit": this directly sets forth their attaining understanding, this being the awakening they gained, nearby, from hearing the fourfold telling of the rare Dharma through parable — the good benefit of this awakening. From "immeasurable precious treasure" onward is, second, a brief citing of the parable, a parable for their formerly not seeking and yet, now, obtaining of themselves this rare treasure of the Dharma.

從「世尊我等樂說譬喻」下，是廣領解，有開譬、合譬，欲開先諮發(云云)。譬為五：一、從「捨父逃逝」下，名父子相失譬，近領火宅總譬，遠領方便略頌；二、從「窮子傭賃」下，名父子相見譬，近領火宅見火，遠領方便我以佛眼觀見；三、從「即遣傍人急追將還」下，名追誘譬，近領火宅捨几用車，遠領方便寢大施小；四、從「過是已後心相體信」下，委知家業譬，此非領上近遠，乃追取方等彈呵、《大品》轉教意耳；五、從「復經少時父知子意」下，名付家業譬，近領火宅賜一大車，遠領法說正直捨方便。又合第四、第五共為一領付譬，在下更明其意也。

From "World-Honored One, we take joy in speaking through parable" onward is the extensive expression of their understanding, comprising both the opening of the parable and the matching correlation, and, wishing to open it, they first ask leave to speak (and so on, as before). The parable falls into five: first, from "abandoned his father and fled" onward, is named the parable of father and son becoming lost to one another, receiving, nearby, the general parable of the burning house, and, at a distance, the brief verses of "Skillful Means"; second, from "the destitute son, hiring himself out for wages" onward, is named the parable of father and son seeing one another again, receiving, nearby, the seeing of the fire in the burning house, and, at a distance, "I, with the Buddha's own eye, observed and saw"; third, from "at once sent men nearby to pursue and bring him back urgently" onward, is named the parable of pursuit and enticement, receiving, nearby, the setting aside of the bench and the use of the cart, and, at a distance, holding back the great and bestowing the lesser; fourth, from "after this, their minds coming to trust one another" onward, is the parable of coming to know the household estate — this does not receive anything from the near or the distant sections above, but rather takes up the sense of the reproof at the Vaipulya assemblies and the turning to teach others in the Larger Prajñāpāramitā; and fifth, from "after some time had further passed, the father, knowing his son's mind" onward, is named the parable of bestowing the household estate, receiving, nearby, the bestowal of the one great cart, and, at a distance, the dharma-exposition's directly setting aside skillful means. The fourth and fifth may also be taken together as a single parable of bestowal, and their sense will be made clearer still below.

舊以西方無量壽佛以合長者，今不用之。西方佛別、緣異，佛別故隱顯義不成，緣異故子父義不成；又此經首末全無此旨，閉眼穿鑿。今依文附義，若釋窮子，取二乘人半字法銷文，若釋長者，取盧舍那佛滿字法門銷文。何者？宅內長者，脫纓珞著垢衣，衣纓有異，人秖是一，譬盧舍那佛隱無量神德示丈六金輝，執持糞器設三乘教，隱顯有殊何關體別，舍那著脫近尚不知，彌陀在遠何甞變換(云云)？

The old accounts match the elder to Amitābha Buddha of the western Pure Land, but this is not adopted here. Amitābha is a distinct buddha, and his karmic affinity is different: because the buddha is distinct, the sense of concealment and manifestation cannot be established, and because the karmic affinity is different, the sense of father and son cannot be established; moreover, this sūtra, from beginning to end, carries not the slightest trace of this meaning, and it is a forced interpretation, argued with eyes shut. Here, instead, the parable is worked through according to the text and in keeping with its own sense: in explaining the destitute son, the dharma of the half-word teaching, belonging to the two lesser vehicles, is taken up to work through the text; and in explaining the elder, the gate of the full-word teaching, belonging to Vairocana Buddha, is taken up to work through the text. Why is this? Within the house, the elder removes his necklace and dons coarse, soiled clothing — the necklace and the clothing differ, yet the person remains one and the same — a parable for Vairocana Buddha concealing his immeasurable spiritual virtues and manifesting instead the golden radiance of the sixteen-foot body, taking up the dung-basket and establishing the teaching of the three vehicles: concealment and manifestation differ, but how does this bear upon any distinction in essence? If even Vairocana's own donning and removing of these forms, so near at hand, is not yet understood, how, then, could Amitābha, so distant, ever have undergone any such transformation (and so on, as before)?

父子相失譬又為四：一、子背父去，二、父求子中止，三、子遇到本，四、其父憂念。四段各兩。初兩者：一、背父而去，領總譬中五百人。昔結大乘子父，尋復失解流浪五道，故言「或十二十至五十歲」，通是佛子，子義微弱故言「幼稚」，非結緣己界，故言「久住他國」；二者向本而還，領總譬三十子。此緣有微著之義故言「長大」，緣既經苦關佛大悲，故言「遇到本國」。父求子而止為兩者：一、父求子不得，領總譬中長者。從眾生退大之後，伺其大機未得其會，故言「不得」；二、中止一城，「其家」者，領總譬中宅，「大富」者，領總譬中長者德業內外財富意耳。子到父城為二：一、到城之由，領火起苦惱之相。從退大已後，處處遊歷備嬰辛苦；二遂到父城者，以苦為機扣於大悲，故言遂到父城。其父憂喜即是兩者：一、念失子苦，二、念得子樂，領總譬中一門。子既幼稚取門不當動父之憂，元以此門通之，故動父之喜。分章竟。

The parable of father and son becoming lost to one another again falls into four: first, the son turning his back on his father and going away; second, the father seeking his son and then pausing; third, the son happening upon his native land; and fourth, the father's longing thoughts. Each of these four sections has two parts. The first two: first, turning his back and going away, receives the five hundred people of the general parable — having formerly formed the karmic bond of children to a Mahāyāna father, they soon afterward lost this understanding and drifted through the five destinies, and hence it says, "some ten, some twenty, up to fifty years" — being, throughout, buddha's children, yet this sense of "child" having grown so faint, it says "young"; and, not having remained within the realm of their own karmic bond, it says "dwelling long in another land." Second, turning back toward their native land receives the thirty children of the general parable — this karmic affinity now having some slight sense of taking hold, it says "grown up"; and this affinity, having passed through suffering, comes to touch the Buddha's great compassion, and hence it says, "chanced upon their native land." The father seeking his son and then pausing has two parts: first, the father seeking his son and not finding him, receiving the elder of the general parable — from the time sentient beings turned back from the great vehicle, he watched for the occasion of their great capacity, but it had not yet come, and hence it says, "did not find him"; and second, pausing within a single city — "his household" receives the house of the general parable, and "of great wealth" receives the sense of the elder's virtuous deeds and his wealth, within and without, in the general parable. The son's arriving at his father's city falls into two: first, the reason for his arrival at the city, receiving the marks of suffering under the fire's outbreak — from the time he turned back from the great vehicle, he wandered from place to place, fully undergoing every hardship; and second, his finally arriving at his father's city, taking this suffering as the occasion by which he touches the Buddha's great compassion, and hence it says, "finally arrived at his father's city." The father's longing and his joy are likewise two: first, his grief at the thought of his lost son, and second, his joy at the thought of regaining him, receiving the one gate of the general parable — the son, being yet young, would not, by taking this gate, stir his father's grief, but it is by this very gate that he was, from the start, to be brought across, and so it stirs, instead, his father's joy. The analysis of the chapters is now complete.

Ru-Yi Translation

In working through the text: first, the son turning his back on his father and going away falls into two. First, "suppose there is a person" receives the twenty children as a parable for the two lesser vehicles — the bodhisattvas' own stages and practice being difficult to know, this receives only what corresponds to them and no further. As for "his years already young": the old account holds that having heard little of the Dharma is called "young," but, if this were so, would not the text below, speaking of "grown up," mean having heard much of the Dharma? Here, instead, it is because the thick weight of ignorance covers over the understanding mind, and this understanding mind, being without force, is called "young"; while the gradual permeating and covering by the roots of goodness, coming little by little to take hold, is called "grown up." As for "abandoned his father and fled": turning back from the great vehicle is "abandoning"; ignorance covering oneself over is called "fleeing"; and heading toward birth-and-death is "going away."

問：
佛捨應後眾生起惑，是父離子，非是子捨父。

Question:
After the Buddha sets aside his response, sentient beings give rise to affliction — this is the father leaving the son, not the son abandoning the father.

答：
由眾生不感佛則去世，還成子捨父義。

Answer:
It is because sentient beings no longer move the Buddha that he then departs from the world — and this, after all, amounts to the sense of the son abandoning the father.

「久住他國」者，涅槃法界是佛自國，生死五欲是為他國，本求出離而退墮不反，故云「久住」。「或十」是天道，「二十」人道，「五十」是五道，約於一人備輪諸道。

As for "dwelling long in another land": the dharma-realm of nirvāṇa is the Buddha's own native land, and birth-and-death, together with the five desires, is the "other land." Formerly having sought release, he instead turned back and fell away without returning, and hence it says "dwelling long." "Some ten" is the destiny of the devas; "twenty" is the destiny of humans; and "fifty" is all five destinies together — this speaks, in terms of a single person, of that person's passing fully through the whole cycle of every destiny.

「年既長大」下，二、向國而還者，幼有二義，一癡小故；二未遭苦故，則不知還，譬結緣已後，大解未濃如癡不反，尚有殘福耽迷不反，今習業冥薰微知向道，遭苦失樂思求出要，此二為機扣佛，名為漸向父國。上文云「若人遭苦為說涅槃」，若以人天二善非感佛緣，在三界中不見佛父為「窮」，不得出要之術又為「窮」，八苦火燒故為「困」。「馳騁四方以求衣食」者，舊云，人天五戒十善各有因果，以為四方用自資給；又於四生營生以求衣食。下文云「一百三十劫今乃得一見」，彼之劫中非無人天因果不能感佛，故知此善非見佛機。今佛既未出，諸凡夫人身受心法起於四見，於中求正道如求食，求助道如求衣，以厭苦求理為可化之緣，佛初出時諸外道等皆先得度，即此意也。大經云「諦觀四方喻於四諦」，準此可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "his years now grown up" onward is, second, the parable of turning back toward his native land. "Young" has two senses: first, being foolish and small; and second, not yet having encountered suffering, and so not knowing to turn back — a parable for how, after forming this karmic bond, before the great understanding has ripened, one is like a fool who does not turn back, still clinging to some remaining merit, indulging in confusion, and not returning. Now, however, habituated karma subtly permeates him, and he comes faintly to know the direction of the path; encountering suffering and losing his happiness, he thinks to seek release — these two conditions become the occasion by which he touches the Buddha, and this is called gradually turning toward his father's native land. The text above says, "if a person encounters suffering, nirvāṇa is taught for their sake" — if the two forms of goodness belonging to humans and devas do not themselves constitute the karmic affinity for touching the Buddha, then not seeing the buddha-father while yet within the three realms is "destitution," not attaining the art of release is likewise "destitution," and being consumed by the fire of the eight sufferings is "distress." As for "racing about in the four directions in search of food and clothing": the old account holds that the causes and effects belonging to the five precepts and the ten wholesome deeds of humans and devas each serve as the "four directions" by which one provides for oneself, or that this refers to seeking food and clothing amid the toil of the four modes of birth. The text below says, "only after a hundred and thirty kalpas do we now, for the first time, gain sight of him" — throughout those kalpas it was not that there was no causality belonging to humans and devas, and yet it could not touch the Buddha, and one thereby knows that this goodness is not itself the occasion for seeing the Buddha. Here, the Buddha not yet having appeared in the world, every ordinary person, in body, sensation, mind, and dharmas, gives rise to the four views; within these, seeking the correct path is like seeking food, and seeking what aids the path is like seeking clothing — taking weariness of suffering and the search for the principle as the karmic condition fit for transformation, and, when the Buddha first appeared in the world, the various non-Buddhist teachers were all delivered first — this is exactly this same sense. The Great Sūtra says, "contemplating closely the four directions is a parable for the four truths" — and this may be understood accordingly.

「漸漸遊行遇向本國」者，明其厭苦希脫邪求涅槃，雖非本意亦蒙值佛，故云「遇向」也，「本國」如上說。下文明城舍云何分別。一切佛法為國，此義則寬；城語小密，以斷德涅槃防非禦惡為城；舍語又親，同體大悲為舍也。「其父先來求子不得」下，第二，求子中止，譬亦為二：初、從退大已後求機不會，不會故名「不得」。二、「中止一城」者，不為一子而廢家業，譬佛不以一處無機而廢餘方施化。舊云，二萬佛後、釋迦佛前，兩楹間為中止。今謂中義可然，止國城家皆不可用。今取方便有餘土為「國」，在同居、實報兩間為「中」，有餘涅槃為「城」，住此涅槃名「止」。處此為家，起勝劣兩應，劣應應聲聞，勝應應菩薩，五人斷通惑者，同生其土皆為菩薩，佛以勝應應之，純以大乘家業訓令修學，中止於此伺覓同居子機，非但中義得合，國城家業皆悉分明。「大富」者，實相境為「家」，具足萬德名為「富」，五度福德名為「財」，般若智慧名為「寶」，導一切悉摩訶衍名「無量」。金銀珠等，是大乘三十七道品也。此即領上長者大富義也。

As for "gradually wandering along, he chanced to turn toward his native land": this makes clear his weariness of suffering and his wish for release, seeking nirvāṇa though by a mistaken path — although this was not his original intention, he nevertheless comes to encounter the Buddha, and hence it says "chanced toward," and "native land" is as explained above. The text below makes clear how "city" and "house" are to be distinguished. All the Buddha's Dharma taken as the "country" — this sense is the broadest; "city" carries a slightly more restricted sense, taking nirvāṇa, the virtue of severance guarding against wrong and warding off evil, as the "city"; and "house" is closer still, taking great compassion, one in essence with all, as the "house." From "his father, who had earlier gone in search of his son and had not found him" onward is, second, the parable of the father seeking his son and then pausing, this too falling into two: first, from the time sentient beings turned back from the great vehicle, he sought the occasion but it did not come together, and, not coming together, it is called "not finding him." Second, as for "paused within a single city": he did not, for the sake of one son alone, set aside his household affairs — a parable for the Buddha's not setting aside his teaching and transformation in other quarters merely because one place lacked the capacity for it. The old account holds that the interval between the twenty thousand buddhas of the past and Śākyamuni Buddha, or the space between the two pillars, constitutes this "pausing." Here it is held that the sense of "pausing between" may indeed be so, but that "pausing," taken as country, city, or house, cannot be used in this literal sense. Here, instead, the land of skillful means with residue is taken as the "country"; the interval between the realm shared in common and the realm of the fruit of reward is taken as the "between"; the nirvāṇa with residue is taken as the "city"; and dwelling in this nirvāṇa is called "pausing." Abiding here as his household, he gives rise to both the superior and the inferior forms of responsive manifestation — the inferior response corresponding to the śrāvakas, and the superior response corresponding to the bodhisattvas. The five men who have cut off the shared afflictions are born together in this same land, all of them as bodhisattvas, and the Buddha responds to them with his superior manifestation, instructing and causing them to study and practice purely the household estate of the great vehicle — pausing here, he watches for the capacity of the sons who dwell there in the realm shared in common. It is not only the sense of "between" that is thereby made to fit, but "country," "city," and "household estate" are all, every one of them, made plain. As for "of great wealth": the domain of true reality is the "household," being fully possessed of the myriad virtues is called "wealth," the merit of the five pāramitās is called "riches," the wisdom of prajñā is called "treasure," and guiding all beings entirely by means of the Mahāyāna is called "immeasurable." Gold, silver, pearls, and the rest are the thirty-seven factors of the path belonging to the great vehicle. This is precisely what receives the sense of the elder's great wealth above.

As for "storehouses and treasuries filled to overflowing": being full within is "filled," and overflowing without is "overflowing"; that which holds grain is the "storehouse," and that which holds other goods is the "treasury." As for "countless": every dharma, provisional and real alike, is called a "cart" or "vehicle," and provisional and real wisdom and contemplation are called "elephant, horse, ox, and goat" — not only are the teachings themselves exceedingly many, the modes of contemplation and wisdom are likewise countless. As for "going out and coming in": the two, being not-two, is "coming in," and the not-two, being two, is "going out"; or, again, the not-two, being two, is "coming in," and the two, being not-two, is "going out"; the immeasurable returning to the one is "coming in," and the one, containing the immeasurable, is "going out"; the function of transforming others is "going out," and the function of practicing for oneself is "coming in." Going forth with the Dharma to benefit sentient beings is "yielding growth," and the merit of this transformation returning to oneself is "profit." As for "and reached even into other lands": pervading the three buddha-lands and, walking through what is not the path, penetrating through to the buddha-path — this is exactly its sense, for only the dharma-nature is truly one's own native land. As for "merchants and traders, too, were exceedingly numerous": every bodhisattva is such a merchant, pervading, moreover, the three lands in search of the profit of the Dharma, and hence it says they were numerous. Bodhisattvas of this land go to other quarters to hear the Dharma, and great beings of other quarters come to this one to hear the sūtra taught — going back and forth, gathering this profit. Again, the responsive body and the transformation body are like traders, carrying the true Dharma pervasively into the three lands, transforming and benefiting sentient beings, and then returning to the dharma-body — hence it says they were exceedingly numerous, just as, among people of the world, one may set another to hold one's wealth and increase it, while thereby increasing one's own wealth as well.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "at that time the destitute son wandered through the villages" onward is, third, the parable of the son drawing near again to his father. This too falls into two: first, seeking food and clothing, and second, arriving at his father's city. The first, inwardly, matches how, once he had turned back from the great vehicle, fully undergoing every suffering, a deep weariness and disgust arose within him, and, wishing to seek release, his grasp of the principle went astray, coming to form a wrong and deviant view. Yet, through this wrong wisdom passing through his mind, he could readily be brought into the correct path, and seeking what transcends the world became the occasion for touching the Buddha. Contemplating the five aggregates is the "villages"; the twelve sense-fields are the "settlements"; and the eighteen elements are the "country" — passing through these in search of the principle is called "seeking food and clothing." Second, from "at last arrived at the place where his father dwelt" onward, this is his turning directly toward it. As for "the city where his father dwelt": the condition of suffering becomes the occasion, and touching the Buddha's great compassion is called arriving at the city — the city being precisely nirvāṇa. Nirvāṇa spans both the partial and the full, and sentient beings' habituated understanding may indeed carry the sense of attaining nirvāṇa — hence it says he arrived at the city.

「父每念子」下，第四，即是父憂念子譬。此中亦二：一、念失子之苦。如來自昔至今，恒思子大機，故言「每念」。「五十餘年」者，五道也。開鬼出修羅，故言「餘」也。「未曾說」者，未曾向方便有餘土中臣佐吏人說有此子機緣也。又應世已來自昔《華嚴》方等《大品》諸座，未曾向諸大士說此聲聞本是大乘之子。既非佛子，不解佛法，或如聾啞，或花著拜座，或棄鉢茫然，種種不逮也。「心懷悔恨」，悔昔不勤教詔致令無訓逃逝，恨子不惟恩義疎我親他，內合如來悔不殷勤令入內凡，遂使退失。本解恨其無心不能精進固志逃迷不返，故言悔恨也。「自念老朽」者，化期將畢無傳大法之人，如老朽而無子也。

From "the father, forever thinking of his son" onward is, fourth, precisely the parable of the father's longing thoughts for his son. This too falls into two: first, his grief at the thought of his lost son. The Tathāgata, from of old until now, has forever thought of his son's great capacity, and hence it says, "forever thinking." As for "more than fifty years": this is the five destinies; opening out the hungry ghosts and adding the asuras is what "more" refers to. As for "had never yet spoken of him": he had never yet, among the ministers, assistants, and officials of the land of skillful means with residue, spoken of there being this son, of this karmic occasion. Again, from the time of his response to the world, from of old, throughout the assemblies of the Avataṃsaka, the Vaipulya, and the Larger Prajñāpāramitā, he had never yet told the great beings there that these śrāvakas were, from the outset, children of the great vehicle. Being, apparently, not the Buddha's children, they did not understand the Buddha's own Dharma — some as though deaf and mute, some clinging to their flowers and bowing at their seats, some abandoning their alms-bowls, at a loss — falling short in every manner of way. As for "his heart harbored regret and remorse": regret that, formerly, he had not diligently taught and instructed them, thereby causing them, untaught, to flee; and remorse that his son gave no thought to the bond of kindness owed him, keeping distant from him and drawing close to another instead — inwardly, this matches the Tathāgata's own regret at not having urged them on more earnestly to enter the ranks of the inner ordinary worldling, so that they thereupon fell back and were lost. According to the original explanation, this is remorse that the son, having no such thought, was unable to press forward with diligence, held fast in his confusion and fled without returning — hence it speaks of regret and remorse. As for "reflected to himself on his own old age and decline": the period of his transforming activity now nearing its end, there being no one to whom the great Dharma might be transmitted, this is like being old and decrepit and without a son.

問：
法身所化諸菩薩等悉堪補處，何遽此憂？

Question:
The bodhisattvas transformed by the dharma-body are all fit to succeed to Buddhahood — why, then, this sudden anxiety?

答：
法身所化本無興廢，誰談老朽？此非所論。今明化身眷屬則有二種：一、法身大士，其相影嚮，迹雖弟子本或是師，亦不約此自念老朽也；二者同居凡夫，始從化佛初發道心者，名此為子也。

Answer:
Those transformed by the dharma-body know, from the start, no rising or falling — who, then, speaks of old age and decline? This is not what is under discussion here. Here, rather, it is made clear that the retinue of the transformation body is of two kinds: first, the great beings of the dharma-body, whose forms are but echoes and reflections — although in their traces they appear as disciples, in their origin they may in fact be teachers, and it is not with reference to these that he reflects upon his own old age and decline; and second, the ordinary worldlings dwelling in the realm shared in common, who, from the very moment they first set their minds upon the path under the transformation body, are called his children.

子繼父業令胤族不斷，若身子受決作華光佛，則一方佛種相續不斷，大乘家業遞相傳付；若身子無可化之機，則大乘法財現無付囑，後來眾生佛種安寄？老朽興歎正為此也。「復作是念我若得子」下，二、念得子為樂。可度之機名為「得子」，與受佛記名付法財，稱於本心復言「快樂」。領上總譬竟。◎

It is the son who carries on the father's estate, so that the family line is not cut off — if Śāriputra receives the prediction and becomes Padmaprabha Buddha, then the buddha-seed of that one quarter continues on unbroken, the household estate of the great vehicle handed down from one to the next in succession; but if Śāriputra had no capacity fit for this transformation, then the Dharma-wealth of the great vehicle would, even now, have no one to whom it might be entrusted — and where, then, would the buddha-seed for beings yet to come find its resting place? His sigh over his own old age and decline is precisely for this reason. From "and had, further, this thought: if I could obtain a son" onward is, second, his thought of the joy of regaining a son. The capacity fit to be delivered is called "obtaining a son"; granting the prediction of buddhahood is called "entrusting the Dharma-wealth"; and, this according exactly with his original intention, it is further called "joy." With this, the reception of the general parable above is complete.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "at that time the destitute son, hired out to labor" onward is, second, the parable of father and son seeing one another — near at hand, matching the seeing of the fire within the burning house; far off, matching the sense of the five turbidities within "Skillful Means." This falls into three: first, making clear the destitute son's being hired out to labor, matching the fire seen within the burning house, and, in the dharma-teaching, the five turbidities that are seen; second, the father seeing the son, matching the elder's seeing within the burning house, and, in the dharma-teaching, the Buddha's own eye that sees; third, joy at having his wish fulfilled, matching the terror within the burning house, and, in the dharma-teaching, the great compassion that thereby arises. Both the parable and the dharma-teaching alike make clear that the father sees the son first; here, however, it is made clear that the son sees the father first. From the standpoint of the Buddha, his numinous wisdom knows first, and his response arises only afterward — hence it is said that the father sees the son first. From the standpoint of sentient beings, the capacity must come first, and the response comes only afterward — hence it is said that the son sees the father first. Capacity and response are inconceivable, neither before nor after one another, and so the order is stated now one way, now the other, for convenience. For convenience of exposition, the text is now taken as falling into only two sections: first, the son seeing the father; second, the father seeing the son. Within each of these two sections there are, again, four points. The four points of the son first seeing the father are: first, the reason for seeing the father, the reason being his seeking clothing and food; second, the place where he sees the father, this place being at the side of the gate; third, the appearance in which he sees the father, seated upon the lion's throne; and fourth, his fear and avoidance, regretting having come to this place.

見父之由，由厭苦欣樂推求理味，漸漸積習，遂成出世善根，故言「傭賃展轉」。以此善根能扣佛慈悲，故言「遇到父舍」。父喻道後法身，舍喻無緣慈悲。大小二機雙扣此舍，有大機故，故言「遙見其父」，有小機故「住在門側」。若唯小無大則應不見尊特之身，父不應言「我財物庫藏今有所付」；若唯大無小，不應住立門側，子不應言「非我傭力得物之處」也。

As for the reason for seeing the father: it is by being wearied of suffering and delighting in joy, seeking after the flavor of principle, that, little by little, through accumulated practice, the wholesome roots of transcending the world come at last to be formed — hence it is said, "drifting from one hired labor to another." Because these wholesome roots are able to move the Buddha's compassion, it is said, "happened at last to arrive at his father's house." The father is a metaphor for the dharma-body attained after the path; the house is a metaphor for compassion that arises without any object. Both the greater and the lesser capacities together move this house at once: because there is the greater capacity, it is said, "saw his father from a distance"; because there is the lesser capacity, it is said, "stood by the side of the gate." If there were only the lesser capacity and not the greater, then he ought not to have seen the honored, exalted body at all, and the father ought not to have said, "now I have someone to entrust my wealth and storehouses to"; and if there were only the greater capacity and not the lesser, he ought not to have stood at the side of the gate, and the son ought not to have said, "this is no place where I could earn anything by my own labor."

見父之處者即是門側，二觀為方便即門二邊，圓中之機當門正見，二乘偏真故言門側，但空三昧偏真慧眼，傍窺法身耳。「遙見其父」，正見有二種，一近見、二遠見，今言大機始發扣召事遠，是故言「遙」；又機微非應赴，名之為「遙」也。「踞師子床」者，圓報法身安處空理，無復通別二惑、八魔等畏，故云踞師子床也。《華嚴》說第一義空，四無所畏為床也。「寶几承足」者，定慧為足，實諦為几，無生定慧依真如境也。「婆羅門」，舊云，高良大姓，八地已上也。「剎利」者，七地已還也。「居士」，內凡夫等。舊云，此經中明法身，非常住法身也，乃是他方應身將應此土，即為此間之法身，故有內凡諸人圍遶。今謂不爾，若作他方佛者，子父機應體用著脫，皆不成，如前說。又不容小機扣此、大機扣彼，亦不應結大緣於彼、結小緣於此，亦不應雙結在此、雙應在彼，如是大惑亂。

As for the place where he sees the father, this is precisely the side of the gate: the two partial contemplations, taken as skillful means, are precisely the two sides of the gate, while the capacity within the perfect teaching sees the father directly, facing the gate itself; the two lesser vehicles, being of the one-sided real, are therefore said to stand at the side of the gate — for it is only the eye of wisdom of the one-sided real, the samādhi of emptiness alone, that peers sideways upon the dharma-body. As for "saw his father from a distance": true seeing is of two kinds, seeing near at hand and seeing from afar. Here it is said that, the greater capacity only now beginning to stir, the matter of being called to it lies still distant — hence it is said "from a distance"; and, further, because the capacity is still faint and unfit for the response to reach it, this too is called "from a distance." As for "seated upon the lion's throne": this means that the perfectly recompensed dharma-body abides at rest within the principle of emptiness, no longer subject to any fear of the two delusions, the shared and the distinct, or of the eight kinds of demon — hence it is said, "seated upon the lion's throne." The Avataṃsaka Sūtra speaks of the emptiness of ultimate meaning, with the four fearlessnesses forming the throne. As for "his feet resting on the jeweled bench": concentration and wisdom form the feet, and the truth of reality forms the bench — the concentration and wisdom of non-arising rest upon the state of true suchness. "Brahmins," according to the ancients, means those of noble and excellent great lineage, from the eighth ground and above. "Warrior-nobles" means those from the seventh ground and below. "Householders" means the ordinary worldlings of the inner stage, and the like. The ancients said that what this sūtra makes clear here is the dharma-body, but not the ever-abiding dharma-body — rather, that this is a response-body from another region about to respond within this land, and so is treated, for the occasion, as the dharma-body of this place, and hence there are ordinary worldlings of the inner stage of every kind surrounding him. Here, however, it is held that this is not so: if one takes this to be a buddha from another region, then the correspondence of capacity and response between father and son, and the taking on and putting off of substance and function, none of it can be made to work, as explained before. Nor would it allow for the lesser capacity to move this buddha while the greater capacity moves that other one, nor ought the great affinity be formed with that one and the lesser affinity with this one, nor ought both affinities be formed here while both responses occur there — such a reading would be a great confusion indeed.

Here it is made clear that the superior response-body responds to bodhisattvas — this is precisely the honored, exalted body of Vairocana, moved by the greater capacity; while the inferior response-body responds to the Lesser Vehicle — the worn robes of the sixteen-foot body, moved by the lesser capacity. The present sūtra makes clear the ever-abiding ghee, equal to what the Nirvāṇa Sūtra teaches, and the perfect and sudden dharma-body, equal to what the Avataṃsaka Sūtra teaches; the majesty and virtue of the elder here likened, his attendants, and the warrior-nobles and brahmins reverently surrounding him, all point to nothing other than the retinue within the Avataṃsaka Sūtra itself, without the slightest difference, and the characteristics of the dharma he expounds are, just as that sūtra makes clear, likewise without any distinction. "Brahmin" is named for pure conduct — noble in lineage and lofty in purity, this is precisely the bodhisattva of equal awakening, utterly free of defilement. "Warrior-noble" is precisely the royal lineage — from the ninth ground down through the first ground. "Householder" is wealthy but not noble — this is precisely the thirty preliminary stages of mind. As for "a necklace of pearls": this is precisely morality, concentration, wisdom, and dhāraṇī-samādhi — the four adornments. As for "worth a thousand myriad pieces": this is precisely the merit of the forty bodhisattva stages, adorning the dharma-body with it. As for "officials, servants, and attendants": read through one gate of meaning, these are those who, receiving the provisional teaching, sever the afflictions shared with the other vehicles, named the "servants"; and those who, receiving the distinct teaching, sever the afflictions shared with the other vehicles, named the "officials." Read through the other, identical gate of meaning, these are simply the pāramitā of skillful means once more — inwardly the same as true wisdom, outwardly the same as the capacities and conditions of beings — a metaphor, as when officials and servants have both an inward duty of service and an outward function of labor.

"The white fly-whisk" is precisely the function of provisional wisdom in use. "Left and right": the right is the function of the wisdom that enters emptiness, sweeping away the dust of the four abodes of affliction; the left is the function of the wisdom that enters the provisional, sweeping away the dust of ignorance — these two together form the skillful means of the middle way, and hence it is said, "attending on either side" (and so on). As for "draped with a jeweled canopy": this is true and real compassion. As for "hung with flowered banners": the flowers are precisely the four means of attraction, and the banners are precisely the spiritual powers. As for "scented water sprinkled on the ground": this is the raining down of the water of the Dharma, sprinkled upon the ground of every bodhisattva's mind so as to submerge the dust of delusion — and is also the water of concentration, sprinkled to disperse the scattered mind. As for "rare flowers scattered about": these are spread as the seven pure flowers, namely morality, concentration, wisdom, the severing of doubt, purity in distinguishing the path from what is not the path, purity of knowledge and vision, and purity through severance and knowledge. Morality is the threefold morality that gathers in the precepts and the rest; concentration is the Śūraṅgama samādhi and the like; wisdom is true and real wisdom itself; the severing of doubt is having already crossed beyond doubt regarding the two truths; purity in distinguishing the path from what is not the path is penetrating to the buddha-path by way of what is not the path; purity of knowledge and vision is the perfection of the virtue of wisdom, seeing the buddha-nature with utter clarity; and purity through severance is the accomplishment of the virtue of severance, ignorance forever exhausted. As for "the treasures arrayed about": this is the true and real merit of every ground, arrayed in order. Taking in and giving out is as explained before (and so on). As for "his majesty and virtue were singularly exalted": his light is boundless and his bodily forms are boundless, the ocean of his marks towering and majestic — this sense can only be made of the Buddha Rocana; how could any other explanation possibly be made of it?

「窮子見父有大力勢」下，是第四，見父畏避。「大力勢」者，智大故名大力，神通大故名大勢，如上身手有力義也。「恐怖」者，小機劣弱怯懼大道也。「悔來至此」者，佛本欲以大法擬之，應不稱機，但有退大之意，故言悔來至此也。「竊作是念」者，機中潛密冥有此事，非是顯對見勝應身也。「或是王王等」者，波旬是王，徒輩為等，小機灰斷無言說道絕於色像，既見勝應之像，非天人所及，所說法相逈異二乘，小智薄德未曾見聞，便謂是魔是魔所說。略開三顯一，身子狐疑將非魔作佛惱亂我心耶？若初用大逗小疑佛為魔，有過今日也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The destitute son, seeing his father possessed of such great power" onward is, fourth, seeing the father and being seized with fear and avoidance. As for "great power": because his wisdom is great, it is called "great strength"; because his spiritual powers are great, it is called "great might" — the same sense as "strength in his body and hands" above. As for "terror": the lesser capacity, weak and feeble, dreads the great path. As for "regretted having come to this place": the Buddha originally wished to gauge him by the great Dharma, but the response did not fit his capacity, and he had only the intention of retreating from the great — hence it is said, "regretted having come to this place." As for "he thought to himself in secret": this means that, hidden and unseen within his own capacity, this matter dimly existed — it does not mean that he openly and directly beheld the superior response-body. As for "this must be some king, or someone equal to a king": Māra is the king, and his followers make "or equal to a king." The lesser capacity, its ashes of affliction extinguished, has no way of speaking that could reach so far as this — the road of words is cut off before such a form; having now seen the image of the superior response-body, a thing beyond the reach of any deva or human, and hearing a description of the dharma utterly unlike anything of the two lesser vehicles, one of shallow wisdom and thin virtue, never having seen or heard the like before, at once supposes it must be some demon, or something spoken by a demon. In outline, this already opens the three to reveal the one; and Śāriputra's own doubt — could this not be some demon, posing as the Buddha, come to trouble my mind? — if, at the very first use of the great to draw out the small, one doubts the Buddha to be a demon, this fault would already have occurred that very day.

復次勝應譬長者，長者即表報身佛，故是王等，法身是報師，師即如王，諸經多名是經王，智契於法即是智與法等，故名報佛為等，此乃大乘法報，非是小乘得益之處，故或是王王等也。「非我傭力得物之處」者，小機不能受大化也。「不如往至貧里」乃至「衣食易得」者，《淨名》云「能以貧所得法，度斯下劣」也。但空之理不含萬德非如來藏，故言貧里；偏空稱於小智，故言「肆力有地」也。「衣食易得」者，能得有餘涅槃無漏衣食，行行衣惠行食也。「若久住此或見逼迫強使我作」者，行大乘道經無量劫，故言久住。我本厭怖生死，若修大乘必入生死。廣學萬行，故言逼迫。我本樂小，而今令我發大乘菩提心，是為強使。捨大取小故言「疾走」也。

Furthermore, the superior response-body is likened here to the elder — the elder standing precisely for the reward-body Buddha, and hence "a king, or equal to a king": the dharma-body is the reward-body's teacher, and the teacher is like a king — most sūtras, indeed, name this very sūtra the "king of sūtras" — while wisdom, in according with the Dharma, is thus wisdom made equal to the Dharma, and hence the reward-body Buddha is named "equal to" — this being precisely the reward of the Great Vehicle's own Dharma, not a place where the Lesser Vehicle gains any benefit at all; hence, "this must be some king, or someone equal to a king." As for "this is no place where I could earn anything by my own labor": the lesser capacity is unable to receive the great transformation. As for "better to go to some poor quarter instead" through "clothing and food would be easy to come by": the Vimalakīrti Sūtra says, "able, by the Dharma attained through poverty, to bring across those of this base and lowly kind." The principle of mere emptiness does not contain the myriad virtues and is not the tathāgatagarbha — hence it is called "the poor quarter"; the one-sided emptiness suits the lesser wisdom — hence it is called, "my labor would have a place." As for "clothing and food would be easy to come by": this means being able to attain the undefiled clothing and food of nirvāṇa-with-remainder — practice upon practice serving as clothing, and the bestowal of practice serving as food. As for "if I stay here long, I may be pressed and forced to work against my will": practicing the path of the Great Vehicle passes through immeasurable kalpas — hence it is said, "stay here long." I originally dreaded and feared birth-and-death, but were I to cultivate the Great Vehicle, I would necessarily enter birth-and-death; studying the myriad practices at large — hence it is said, "pressed." I originally delighted in the lesser, and now, were I made to give rise to the bodhicitta of the Great Vehicle, this would be "forced to work." Casting aside the great and taking up the lesser — hence it is said, "fled away."

Ru-Yi Translation

"At that time the wealthy elder" onward is, second, the parable of the father seeing the son. As for the place of seeing the son, this is precisely the lion's throne — the Tathāgata's own dharma-body, dwelling within the fearless state of the emptiness of ultimate meaning, clearly illuminating beings' capacities. As for seeing the son and recognizing him at once, this means knowing that this is a being with whom karmic affinity was formed in days gone by. As for his heart being filled with great joy: the Buddha forever watches for his children's capacity to ripen, and now, this capacity having come and matched his compassion, he is therefore filled with joy — this is precisely matching the great compassion that arises within the dharma-teaching; within the burning house it is instead great terror, since there it makes clear the removing of suffering, and hence "terror" is spoken of, while here it makes clear the bestowal of joy, and hence "joy" is spoken of. In the past he saw sentient beings retreat from the great and take up the lesser, seeking food in the poor quarter, their means of livelihood beset with hardship; he had constantly wished to give them his wealth, but, lacking any capacity for it, could not do so — and now, this day, the capacity has come and matched his great compassionate mind, and hence it is said, "now I have someone to entrust my wealth and storehouses to." As for "I have constantly thought of this son of mine": this makes clear that he was not merely poor and without great wealth, but was also pressed by every suffering of drifting through birth-and-death, and was thereby afflicted with great compassion — hence it is said, "constantly thought of him." Though he wished to rescue and deliver him, lacking the capacity there was no way to save him — hence it is said, "with no way to see him." Now that a capacity fit to be delivered has arisen, it is said, "and now, suddenly, he has come of himself." Matching his great compassionate mind, it is said, "this is exactly what I have wished for." As for "though I am old and worn, I still hold to this desire": this explains the reason for his wish being fulfilled. Because this one lifetime's transforming activity is nearing its end, it is said, "old and worn"; not yet having seen the great capacity, the Dharma has no one to entrust it to — through whom, then, could those who come after find liberation? Because of those who might yet be delivered, it is said, "still hold to this desire." Now that the capacity has come of itself, he has no more of this anxious care, and so my wish comes to be fulfilled.

As for "cried out in protest": the Lesser Vehicle takes affliction to be its enemy and birth-and-death to be suffering — if persuaded that affliction is itself bodhi, he cries out loudly in protest at this wrong; if he hears that birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa, he cries out loudly in pain. This — the absence of capacity, unable to accept it — belongs to the gate of persuasion. As for "I have done nothing against you": I have sought nothing from you — why, then, use the great to transform me against my will? Knowing for certain that, at the assembly of the twenty thousand million buddhas, he had once given rise to the mind of the path, it is not that he altogether lacks the great capacity — it is only that, for the time being, the great transformation is set to rest, the Buddha's own intention not yet finished, but awaiting some later occasion still. As for "did not tell anyone else": within the Lesser Vehicle teaching of the past, speaking according to others' own intent, he covered and protected this by skillful means, calling him a śrāvaka, and did not speak according to his own intent, saying that he was a bodhisattva. From "the messengers said to him" onward is, third, directly making clear the resting of the transformation. "I now release you" is precisely knowing that the great capacity is weak; "go wherever you wish" is precisely knowing that the lesser good is strong — it is by these two conditions that the great transformation is made to rest. From "the destitute son, overjoyed" onward is, fourth, precisely the resting of the transformation proving fitting to his capacity: not being pressed by the great teaching, he is therefore "overjoyed"; free from the grave offense of slandering the great, and thereby spared the three lower paths, it is said, "from the ground"; and a lesser good having arisen within him, it is said, "and rose up." Furthermore, earlier, contemplating the fitness of the great Dharma, he lay confused and uncomprehending upon the ground of ignorance; now, met instead with the lesser, he is able to come to his senses — hence it is said, "rose up from the ground." Wishing to seek the path within the four noble truths, it is said, "went off to a poor quarter to seek clothing and food"; or, seeking the path amid the four mistaken views, it is said, "poor quarter."

This falls into two: first, matching what is common to the teaching — near at hand, matching the three carts that save the children; far off, matching the offering of the provisional at Vārāṇasī; next, from "and on another day" onward, matching by way of intent — matching the dharma-body's stage, which has long since illuminated skillful means, and was not first awakened to the use of the lesser beneath the tree of enlightenment, but had discerned sentient beings long before, bringing about the difficulty of the honored and exalted one drawing close in soiled clothing — hence this reaches back to match what came before, so as to bring the present understanding to completion.

問：
四大弟子，何因能知法身久照？

Question: by what means were the four great disciples able to know that the dharma-body had illuminated beings for so long?

答：
推近知遠，若始道樹知無大機，不應兜率降神正慧託胎，乃至現有煩惱納妃生子，三十四心後身斷結，驗知脫大小相海微妙瓔珞，更著麁弊丈六垢衣，其已久矣。

Answer: one infers the distant from the near. If it were only beneath the tree of enlightenment that he first came to know there was no great capacity for it, then he ought not to have descended in spirit from Tuṣita, entrusted himself in the womb with correct wisdom, and even manifested as having affliction, taken a consort, and had a son, only severing every fetter in the thirty-fourth thought-moment of his final life — from this one can verify that his casting off the ocean of marks, great and small, subtle and wondrous, and putting on instead the coarse, worn, soiled robes of the sixteen-foot body, must already have been of long standing.

今初且釋齊教。領者，〈譬喻品〉文有四：一、用方便擬宜，二、知先心，三、歎三車，四、適其所願，〈方便品〉亦四。今領亦四：從「將欲誘引」下，是領上擬宜。「時二使人即求窮子，既已得之」，領上知子先心有機也。「具陳上事」，領上歎三車希有。「窮子先取其價」下，領上適願爭出火宅也。初、「將欲誘引」者，既息大化不容孤棄，欲設方便，故言將欲。「密遣二人」者，四弟子齊己分領，不涉菩薩，故言二人。約法是因緣四諦，約理是有作真俗，約人是聲聞緣覺。初擬大乘云密遣傍人，表一實諦、一大乘教、一菩薩人，今明方便，隱實為密，指偏真為遣。約教，隱滿字為密，指半字言遣。約人，內祕菩薩行故言密，外現是聲聞故言遣。「形」者，二乘教中不修相好，但說苦、無常、不淨，即是「形色憔悴」也。約人，則諸菩薩隱其本色示以迹形。非了義說，無有十力無畏，名「無威德」也。「汝可詣彼」者，即以小教擬小機也。大教明理直實，故言「疾走往捉」；小教明理迂隱，故云「徐語」。「此有作處」者，見修兩道是斷惑作處也。

Here, first, the matching of what is common to the teaching is explained. As for the matching: the text of the "Parable" chapter has four parts — first, using skillful means to contemplate fitness; second, knowing his prior intent; third, praising the three carts; and fourth, having his own wish fulfilled — and "Skillful Means" likewise has four. The matching here, too, has four parts: from "wishing to draw his son onward" onward matches the contemplating of fitness above. "The two messengers then went to find the destitute son, and, having found him," matches knowing the son's prior intent, that he had a capacity for it, above. "Told him everything just as instructed" matches praising the rarity of the three carts above. From "the destitute son first took his wage" onward matches having his wish fulfilled and racing out of the burning house, above. First, as for "wishing to draw his son onward": since the great transformation had already been set to rest, yet it was unbearable simply to abandon him, he wished to devise a skillful means — hence it is said, "wishing to." As for "secretly sent two men": the four disciples, each receiving a share of the matching in common with themselves, without touching upon the bodhisattvas, and hence it is said, "two men." Read in terms of the Dharma, this is the four noble truths of causes and conditions; read in terms of principle, this is the conventional and the ultimate as constructed things; read in terms of persons, this is the śrāvaka and the pratyekabuddha. Earlier, in contemplating the Great Vehicle, it was said, "secretly sent his attendants," standing for the one truth of reality, the one Mahāyāna teaching, and the one bodhisattva person; here, making clear the skillful means, concealing the real is "secretly," and pointing to the one-sided real is "sent." Read in terms of the teaching, concealing the full-word teaching is "secretly," and pointing to the half-word teaching is "sent." Read in terms of persons, inwardly concealing the bodhisattva practice is "secretly," and outwardly manifesting as a śrāvaka is "sent." As for "appearance": within the teaching of the two lesser vehicles, the excellent marks are not cultivated, but only suffering, impermanence, and impurity are taught — this is precisely "haggard in appearance." Read in terms of persons, this means the bodhisattvas conceal their own true color and display instead a trace-form. This is not the teaching of ultimate meaning, and is without the ten powers or the fearlessnesses — hence it is named "without any air of authority." As for "go to him": this is precisely gauging the lesser capacity by the lesser teaching. The Great Teaching makes its principle plain and direct — hence it is said, "ran quickly and seized him"; the Lesser Teaching makes its principle roundabout and hidden — hence it is said, "spoke gently." As for "there is work to be had here": the paths of seeing and of cultivation are precisely the place where affliction is severed and work is done.

「倍與汝直」者，五戒十善止出三途，今四諦十二因緣能出生死，是為一倍；又外道六行但能伏惑，今修四諦則能斷惑得至涅槃，是為二倍也。「窮子若許」者，有機是許，即設教；無機是不許，不設教。「欲何所作」者，二乘唯欲除惑取證，不論淨佛國土成就眾生。所以言「雇汝除糞，我等二人亦共作」者，二乘鈍根憑教行行方能修業，約理者即是智諦相資也，約人即權人共實人修行也。「時二使人即求窮子」者，第二，審知有機，故言「已得」，領上知先心也。「具陳上事」下，第三，陳說雇作，領上歎三車也。除苦集之糞，取道滅之價。「窮子先取其價尋與除糞」下，第四，尋即為作，領上適願爭出火宅也。二乘慕果行因，所以先取也。「其父見子愍而怪之」者，怪不求佛道，愍其取阿羅漢，所失者大、所得者寡，故言怪也。齊此領法譬中意，其文竟。從「又以他日」下，第二，是取意領，靈智先照久設權謀崎嶇隨逐，非止樹下始見因緣，已如上說。此文為四：一、「又以他日」取意領先以權智久欲擬宜；二、見子憔悴，是久知方便是其玩好；三、脫妙著麁，領久知須歎三車；四、親教子作，久知適願受行今初。「又」者，鄭重辭也。將欲取意領法身之地久知大小之機(一三)施化，重述佛意故標章稱「又」也。

As for "paying double your usual wage": the five precepts and the ten wholesome deeds only halt one from the three lower paths, while the four noble truths and the twelve links of dependent origination, now taught, are able to bring one out of birth-and-death altogether — this is the "one" doubling; and, further, the six practices of the non-Buddhist paths are able only to suppress affliction, while now cultivating the four noble truths is able to sever affliction and reach nirvāṇa — this is the "second" doubling. As for "if the destitute son agrees": having the capacity is "agreeing," whereupon the teaching is set forth; lacking the capacity is "not agreeing," whereupon the teaching is not set forth. As for "what work is it": the two lesser vehicles wish only to remove affliction and attain realization, and do not speak of purifying buddha-lands and bringing beings to maturity. This is why it is said, "we would hire you to clear away dung; the two of us will work alongside you as well" — the two lesser vehicles, being of dull faculties, must rely on the teaching and practice upon practice before they are able to cultivate their task; read in terms of principle, this is precisely wisdom and truth mutually sustaining one another; read in terms of persons, this is precisely the provisional person cultivating practice together with the real person. As for "the two messengers then went to find the destitute son": this is, second, ascertaining for certain that there is a capacity for it — hence it is said, "having found him," matching knowing his prior intent, above. From "told him everything just as instructed" onward is, third, telling him of the hire — matching praising the three carts, above: clearing away the dung of suffering and its origin, and receiving the wage of cessation and the path. From "the destitute son first took his wage, and set at once to clearing away dung" onward is, fourth — setting at once to the work — matching having his wish fulfilled and racing out of the burning house, above; the two lesser vehicles, longing for the fruit, practice the cause, and this is why they take the wage first. As for "his father, seeing his son, felt pity for him, though also puzzlement": the puzzlement is at his not seeking the buddha-path, and the pity is at his taking up arhatship instead — what he loses is great, and what he gains is slight, and hence it is said, "puzzlement." Reaching this point, the matching of the sense within the dharma-teaching and the parable comes to its end. From "and on another day" onward is, second, matching by way of intent: his numinous wisdom, illuminating things beforehand, had long since devised this provisional stratagem, pursuing him through every difficulty — it was not only beneath the tree that he first came to see the causes and conditions — as already explained above. This text falls into four: first, "and on another day" matches, by way of intent, his having long used provisional wisdom, wishing all along to contemplate fitness; second, seeing the son thin and haggard is his having long known that skillful means were what the son delighted in; third, removing his fine robes and putting on coarse ones matches his having long known that the three carts must be praised; and fourth, personally teaching the son to work matches his having long known that the wish would be fulfilled and accepted in practice — here, first. As for "and": this is a word of solemn emphasis. Wishing to match, by way of intent, the dharma-body's own stage, which had long known the great and lesser capacities and their transformation, restating the Buddha's own intention, the section heading is therefore marked "and."

「他日」者，二乘自謂方便為己，非二乘法為他，即擬法身也。

As for "another day": the two lesser vehicles themselves suppose that skillful means belong to themselves, while what is not the dharma of the two lesser vehicles is "other" — this refers precisely to what is contemplated at the level of the dharma-body.

「日」者時也，亦智也，依法身之時，用智照機，故言「他日」。若從此義，實智照實為自，權智照方便為他。齊教領，領化身用事為己日，非化身用事為他日。若就如來，自行權實之智皆名為己，如來化他權實之照，皆名為他。如來自他權實之照，照實為己，照權為他。此之探領法身之時，用化他之權智，照於權機若有若無，照用權事若可若否，皆是權智所照，故言他日。

As for "day": this means time, and also means wisdom — depending on the time proper to the dharma-body, wisdom is used to illuminate the capacity, and hence it is called "another day." Taken in this sense, true wisdom illuminating the true is "self," and provisional wisdom illuminating the provisional means is "other." Matching what is common to the teaching, matching the transformation-body's exercise of its function is "his own day," while what is not the transformation-body's exercise of its function is "another day." Taken from the standpoint of the Tathāgata, the wisdom of the provisional and the real that he practices for himself is all named "self," while the illumination of the provisional and the real by which the Tathāgata transforms others is all named "other." Within the Tathāgata's own illumination of the provisional and the real, for self and for other alike, illuminating the real is "self," and illuminating the provisional is "other." This present exploration, matching the time proper to the dharma-body, uses the provisional wisdom that transforms others, illuminating whether the provisional capacity exists or not, and illuminating whether the provisional matter is fitting or not — all of this is illuminated by provisional wisdom, and hence it is called "another day."

若從此義，齊教領，領化他之權事，故二乘稱己事，探領領自他之權，此權非二乘事，故稱為他。雖有兩意，他日俱成，今依二乘所領，又逐他日之文，以探領領法身中照機也。「牖窓」者，偏見則小，表權智照彼偏機也。「遙」者，小去大懸，故名為遙。「見子」者，昔曾種大稱之為子，以大擬之故言為見。窓牖偏狹未宜大化，故息大而施小也。「羸瘦」下，第二，是領先知有小玩好也。修因智力少為「羸」，修因福力少為「瘦」，內怖無常為「憔」，外遭八苦為「悴」。四住為「糞土」，無知為「塵坌」也。「即脫瓔珞」下，第三，是領先知須歎三車希有也。脫妙服，譬隱報身無量功德。四十二地戒定慧陀羅尼等「瓔珞」，寂滅忍「細軟上服」，大小相海「嚴飾之具」。容服若盛子則驚畏，二乘不宜見此相好，是故脫之。「更著麁弊」者，現丈六形是麁，生忍、法忍是弊也。「塵土坌身」者，現有煩惱有為有漏也。「執除糞」下，但治見思有漏之法，不論諸地清淨智慧也。左手喻實，右手喻權，權用便易，自以此法斷結成佛，又用此化人。「狀有所畏」者，示同怖生死，又有寒風馬麥之報也。「語諸作人」下，第四，親教子作譬也，即是道品中七科法門，以顯除糞之相，領上諸子心各勇銳互相推排競共馳走爭出火宅也。一者語作人譬，譬四念處是外凡位；二、令勤作勿得懈息譬，譬四正勤；三、「咄男子」，勿復餘去譬，譬四如意足；四、「好自安意」下，名安慰譬，譬五根；五、「所以者何」下，名無五過譬，譬五力。此前四句是第二內凡位。六、即時長者字以為子譬，譬八正。七、「雖欣此遇」下，名教常令除糞譬，譬七覺。此二句是第三聖位也。

Taken in this sense, matching what is common to the teaching matches the provisional matter of transforming others, and so the two lesser vehicles call this their own matter; matching by way of intent matches the provisional wisdom belonging both to self and to other, and this provisional wisdom is not a matter of the two lesser vehicles, and so it is called "other." Though there are these two senses, in either case "another day" is established; here, following what the two lesser vehicles themselves match, and, further, following the text's own wording of "another day," this is taken as matching by way of intent, matching the illumination of capacity within the dharma-body. As for "window": a one-sided view is small, standing for provisional wisdom illuminating that one-sided capacity. As for "from a distance": the lesser is far removed from the great, and hence it is called "from a distance." As for "seeing his son": having in the past planted the seed of the great, he is called "son," and, gauging him by the great, it is said, "seeing." The window being narrow and one-sided, it was not yet fitting to the great transformation — hence the great was set to rest and the lesser offered instead. From "thin and haggard" onward is, second, matching his having long known that the son delighted in something lesser. Little strength of wisdom in cultivating the cause is "thin"; little strength of merit in cultivating the cause is "haggard"; inward dread of impermanence is "gaunt"; and outward encounter with the eight sufferings is "wasted." The four abodes of affliction are "dung and filth," and ignorance is "clinging dust." From "he at once removed his own necklace" onward is, third, matching his having long known that the three carts must be praised for their rarity. Removing his fine robes is a metaphor for concealing the immeasurable merit of the reward-body. The morality, concentration, wisdom, and dhāraṇī of the forty-two grounds are the "necklace"; the forbearance of quiescent cessation is the "fine, soft upper robe"; and the ocean of marks, great and small, is "every adornment." Were his countenance and dress too resplendent, the son would be startled and afraid — the two lesser vehicles are not fit to see these excellent marks, and this is why he removes them. As for "put on instead coarse, worn clothing": manifesting the sixteen-foot form is "coarse," and the forbearance regarding sentient beings and the forbearance regarding the Dharma are "worn." As for "covering his body with dust": manifesting as though possessed of affliction is conditioned and contaminated. From "took up an implement for clearing dung" onward treats only of the contaminated dharmas of views and thought, and does not speak of the pure wisdom of the various grounds. The left hand is a metaphor for the real, and the right hand for the provisional — the provisional being convenient and easy to use, he himself, by this same dharma, severed every fetter and accomplished buddhahood, and now uses this same dharma to transform others. As for "appearing as one who might be feared": this shows him sharing the same dread of birth-and-death, and also the retributions of the cold wind and the horse-fodder. From "he said to the workers" onward is, fourth, the parable of personally teaching the son to work — this is precisely the seven groups of the factors of the path, used to display the mark of clearing away dung, matching "each of the children grew bold and eager, pushing past one another, competing, together, racing to run, competing to be first out of the burning house," above. First, the parable of speaking to the workers is a parable for the four foundations of mindfulness, the stage of the ordinary worldling at the outer stage; second, the parable of urging diligent work, without growing lax, is a parable for the four right exertions; third, "ah, good man," and not going elsewhere, is a parable for the four bases of supernormal power; fourth, from "set your mind at ease" onward, named the parable of consolation, is a parable for the five faculties; fifth, from "why is this" onward, named the parable of being without the five faults, is a parable for the five powers. These first four sections are the second, the stage of the ordinary worldling at the inner stage. Sixth, the elder at once giving him a new name and calling him his own child is a parable for the eightfold path. Seventh, from "though he rejoiced at this treatment" onward, named the parable of teaching him to be constantly set to clearing dung, is a parable for the seven factors of enlightenment. These last two sections are the third, the stage of the noble one.

The Seven Groups of the Path Matched to the Work of Clearing Dung 道品七科：語作人、勤作、咄男子等

Chinese Source

今初語諸作人者，即是說三藏示四念處，是除糞之器，斷結之境故。《遺教》云「常依念處行道能破四倒」，領火宅中適願勇銳，即是聞慧也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, first, "he said to the workers" is precisely teaching the Tripiṭaka teaching and showing the four foundations of mindfulness, these being the implement for clearing away dung, the very object upon which affliction is severed. The Sūtra of the Buddha's Final Teaching says, "constantly relying on the practice of the foundations of mindfulness in walking the path, one is able to break the four inversions" — this matches "his wish fulfilled, each grew bold and eager," within the burning house, above, this being precisely the wisdom born of hearing.

第二，「勿得懈息」者，即是令勤修四念處也，若起懈息不能滅二惡，不能生二善，以二勤故能發煖火，對火宅互相推排，入修慧煖位也。「以方便故得近其子」者，念處未得理火溫心，猶為疎外不可附近，以初得煖方便則可附近也。

Second, as for "do not grow lax": this is precisely making him diligently cultivate the four foundations of mindfulness — should laxness arise, he could neither extinguish the two evils nor give rise to the two goods, and it is by these two forms of diligence that the fire of heat is kindled; this matches "pushing past one another," within the burning house, and is precisely entering the stage of heat within the wisdom born of cultivation. As for "by this skillful means he was able to draw near to his son": before the foundations of mindfulness have attained the principle's fire to warm the mind, one remains too distant and outside to draw near; only once heat is first attained by skillful means can one draw near.

第三，「咄男子」者，咄是驚覺亦是責數，上正勤中紛動即是智法，如男子是陽性，如意足是定法，如女人是陰性，良以正勤策動不得與真相應，故咄驚責數，令捨散入靜，故咄男子也。「汝常此作勿復餘去」者，念處、正勤動不專一，不名為常，四如意中定不異緣，思惟則定、思惟則斷，定斷專一故常，不紛動故勿復餘去。此猶在互相推排中，即是頂法之位也。

Third, as for "ah, good man": "ah" is an exclamation of startled awakening, and also a word of reproach and reckoning. Within the right exertions above, the agitated stirring is precisely the dharma of wisdom, likened to the male, of yang nature; the bases of supernormal power are the dharma of concentration, likened to the female, of yin nature. It is precisely because the striving stirred up by the right exertions cannot yet accord with the truth that this "ah" of startled reproach and reckoning is spoken, so as to make him set aside agitation and enter stillness — hence, "ah, good man." As for "you may work here always; there is no need to go elsewhere": the stirring of the foundations of mindfulness and the right exertions is not single-minded, and hence is not named "constant"; but within the four bases of supernormal power, concentration does not vary its object — where there is reflection there is concentration, and where there is reflection there is severance — and, concentration and severance being single-minded, this is "constant"; and, being free of agitated stirring, this is "there is no need to go elsewhere." This still lies within "pushing past one another," and is precisely the stage of summit.

「當加汝價」者，煖法意觀中不能發真，如意觀中能發無漏故言加價。若有所須者，漏無漏善助道正道，皆從如意觀求，欲須即得。四禪體含支林如盆器，生空麁如米，法空細如麵，此即正道。四諦下十六諦觀，無常如鹽、苦如醋，此即助道。如米麪難食須鹽醋和之，正道難顯須助道助之。「莫自疑難」者，結上正助，審在如意觀中故令勿疑，決定可辦如己物想故言勿難。「亦有老弊使人」者，若欲直取通以代手足如使人驅役者，如意觀中亦有此通，但通劣弱事同老弊，雖不丁壯亦堪運役；又以正道求理，正道弱未能發真，欲須助道，九想、十想、八背捨等。助道使人者，如意觀中亦有此法，若得助助正，即成共解脫人也。

As for "I shall raise your wage": within the contemplation of the meditative object at the stage of heat, the true cannot yet be brought forth, while within the contemplation of the bases of supernormal power, the undefiled can be brought forth — hence it is said, "raise your wage." As for "whatever you need": both contaminated and uncontaminated wholesome dharmas, whether aiding the path or the correct path itself, are all to be sought from the contemplation of the bases of supernormal power — whatever is wanted is at once obtained. The four dhyānas, their substance containing every branch and limb, are like the basin and the vessel; the emptiness of the person, coarse, is like rice; the emptiness of dharmas, fine, is like flour — this is precisely the correct path. The sixteen forms of contemplation beneath the four noble truths — impermanence like salt, suffering like vinegar — this is precisely the aiding path. Just as rice and flour are hard to eat without being seasoned with salt and vinegar, so the correct path is hard to make manifest without the aiding path to assist it. As for "do not hesitate to ask": this concludes the correct path and the aiding path together, above — since this is certainly to be found within the contemplation of the bases of supernormal power, he is made not to doubt it; being certain that it can be accomplished, he is to regard it as though it were already his own — hence, "do not hesitate." As for "there are old and worn-out servants here as well": if one wished to draw directly on the spiritual powers to stand in place of hand and foot, as one might drive and employ a servant, then, within the contemplation of the bases of supernormal power, this same power exists as well, only weak and feeble, its case resembling that of the old and worn-out — though not young and vigorous, it can still be put to use; and, further, seeking the principle by the correct path, when the correct path is weak and not yet able to bring forth the true, one must have recourse to the aiding path — the nine contemplations, the ten contemplations, the eight forms of liberation through renunciation, and the rest. As for "servants to aid the work": within the contemplation of the bases of supernormal power, this same dharma exists as well; once the aiding path is able to assist the correct path, this becomes what is called "one who is freed together with another."

第四，「好自安意」者，得五根安固難壞也。「我如汝父」者，忍解隣真似像未實，故言如父，亦是如子。「勿復憂慮」者，令其安意破壞見思也。

Fourth, as for "set your mind at ease": this is attaining the five faculties, settled firm and hard to destroy. As for "I am like a father to you": forbearance and understanding, drawing near to the true, resemble it in likeness but are not yet real — hence it is said, "like a father," and also "like a son." As for "have no more worry": this is making his mind settled, breaking through views and thought.

第五，「我老汝少」者，佛居道終已具智斷，故言老大，汝居道始未有智斷，故名為少壯，此即忍法位也。無五過者，得五力離五惡法也。得信力故不欺，精進力故不怠，念力故不瞋，定力故不恨，慧力故不怨言。「餘作人」者，遠指外道，諸見求理名餘作人，近指煖等四位未免五過，亦名餘作人。此文無五過，即五力也。「自今已後如所生子」者，下忍十六剎那時節猶長，中忍雖復縮觀亦未是一剎那，若上忍世間最後一剎那心隣真逼聖，故名此位為如所生子，即世第一法位也。

Fifth, as for "I am old and advanced in years, and you are young and strong": the Buddha, having reached the end of the path, already possesses wisdom and severance — hence, "old and advanced"; you, standing only at the beginning of the path, do not yet possess wisdom and severance — hence, "young and strong." This is precisely the stage of forbearance. As for being "without the five faults": this is attaining the five powers and thereby being free of the five evil dharmas. Through the power of faith, one does not deceive; through the power of diligence, one does not grow lazy; through the power of mindfulness, one does not harbor anger; through the power of concentration, one does not resent; and through the power of wisdom, one does not speak with ill will. As for "my other workers": at the furthest remove, this points to the followers of the outside paths, who seek the principle through every kind of view, and are named "other workers"; at the nearer remove, it points to the four stages of heat and the rest, not yet free of the five faults, likewise named "other workers." This present text, being without the five faults, is precisely the five powers. As for "from now on, you shall be to me like my own son": within the lower grade of forbearance, the sixteen instants still stretch out a long while; within the middle grade of forbearance, though the contemplation is compressed, it is not yet a single instant; but within the highest grade of forbearance, the very last single instant of the mundane world, the mind draws near to the true and presses close upon the noble — hence this stage is named "like my own son," this being precisely the stage of supreme mundane dharma.

第六，「即時長者更與作字名之為兒」者，得八正入見道中競共馳走，故言名之為兒。世第一法與真不久故言即時，《阿含》說五種佛子，四果及辟支佛名佛真子，菩薩不斷結子義未成。「爾時窮子雖欣此遇」下，第七，常令傭作譬。譬雖為子，思惟未盡猶居學位未得無難，故二十年常令除糞，亦復自知不任紹大，正是依教修行盡苦耳，故云「猶故自謂客作賤人」。若得初果厭小樂大，大乘機發者，即應授以大乘，又不須進斷其餘殘結，正由不捨小志大機不發，以是且令依教盡漏，故言「由是之故二十年中常令除糞」。「二十年」者，見諦，一解脫一無礙；思惟，九無礙九解脫，故言二十年。又云見思二道中斷結，名二十年。又云五下分、五上分，為二十年也。又云猶於二乘法中斷思惑故，名二十年。又云依二使人，共斷餘結故，名二十年也。從有二乘之機而來感佛，故云自見子來已二十年，若住二乘位轉大乘教，名為於二十年中執作家事也。二十語同各有所以，指此一句，即是爭出三界火宅位也。

Sixth, as for "the elder at once gave him a new name, calling him his own child": this is attaining the eightfold path and entering the path of seeing, competing, together, racing to run — hence it is said, "calling him his own child." Supreme mundane dharma is not far removed from the true — hence, "at once." The Āgamas speak of five kinds of children of the Buddha: the four fruits and the pratyekabuddha are named the true children of the Buddha, while the bodhisattva, not having severed every fetter, has not yet fully accomplished the sense of "child." From "at that time, though the destitute son rejoiced at this treatment" onward is, seventh, the parable of being constantly set to hired labor. Though the parable calls him a "child," his reflection is not yet complete, and he still dwells at the stage of the trainee, not yet free of all difficulty — hence, for twenty years, he is constantly set to clearing away dung, and, moreover, knows of himself that he is not fit to inherit the great — this is precisely practicing according to the teaching so as to exhaust suffering, and nothing more — hence it is said, "he still thought of himself as a lowly hired hand." Were he, having attained the first fruit, to grow weary of the lesser and delight in the great, the great capacity thereby stirred, he ought at once to be given the Great Vehicle, with no further need to sever his remaining fetters one by one — but it is precisely because he has not relinquished his lesser resolve that the great capacity does not stir, and this is why he is, for the time being, made to exhaust the contaminants according to the teaching — hence it is said, "and this is why, for twenty years, he was constantly set to clearing away dung." As for "twenty years": the seeing of the truths is one path of liberation and one of no obstruction; reflection is nine paths of no obstruction and nine of liberation — hence it is said, "twenty years." Some also say that severing the fetters within the two paths of seeing and of thought is named "twenty years." Some say the five lower fetters and the five higher fetters make "twenty years." Some say that this is named for severing the afflictions of thought still remaining within the teaching of the two lesser vehicles. And some say that, in dependence on the two messengers, severing the remaining fetters together with them, this is named "twenty years." From having the capacity for the two lesser vehicles onward, coming to be moved by the Buddha, it is said, "it has been twenty years since I first saw my son"; and if he dwells within the station of the two lesser vehicles while the Mahāyāna teaching is being relayed to him, this is named "managing the affairs of the household for twenty years." Though "twenty" is spoken of in the same way each time, each carries its own particular sense; and this single phrase points precisely to the stage of racing to be first out of the burning house of the three realms.

Ru-Yi Translation

"After this had passed" onward is, fourth, the parable of entrusting him with the household, matching, near at hand, the equal bestowal of the great cart within the burning house; and, far off, matching the unsurpassed path within the dharma-teaching. This falls into two: first, entrusting; second, bestowing. Each, again, falls into two, together matching the four senses within the burning house's equal bestowal of the cart — which are also the four senses of revealing the real, within "Skillful Means." The first section's two points: first, mutual trust of heart, matching escape from danger above; second, entrusting him with the household, gradually opening him into ease and bringing his great resolve to completion, matching asking for the cart above. The second section's two points: first, bestowing the household, matching the equal bestowal of the great cart above; second, joy at receiving the bestowal, matching the joy at receiving the cart above. Because there was mutual trust of heart, he came to be entrusted with the household; the household having been made thoroughly familiar, his knowledge and vision complete, his great resolve thereby came to completion; because his intention had opened into ease, he came to be bestowed with the household; and because he was bestowed with the household, he was thereby joyful. Since this involves both the distant and the near — had he not first been taught to labor for hire and given a single day's wage, how could mutual trust, entrustment with the household, and the bestowal of wealth ever have come about? Read within, this matches his severing the fetters through the Tripiṭaka teaching, being fit to hear the Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra alongside the rest, receiving the rebuke of the Vimalakīrti Sūtra, and having the Prajñāpāramitā relayed to him — and it is by this that the bestowal of wealth comes about. One should understand that laboring for hire and taking the wage is precisely the distant cause, while mutual trust and being entrusted with the household is precisely the near cause. Furthermore, within the earlier parable of enticement, there was both the matching of what is common to the teaching, running from beneath the tree of enlightenment through to coming out of the house, and the matching by way of intent, running from the dharma-body through to the exhaustion of reflection. Here, too, the matching is twofold: beginning with matching by way of intent, the four stages of compassion ripening the son, and ending with matching the final bestowal of wealth, the ultimate single flavor — near and far, beginning and end together, discussed in terms of the five flavors. Why is this?

即遣傍人，傍人所說乃譬《華嚴》圓頓，此教最初，傍人譬牛，所說譬乳，內合從佛出十二部經，即初味也，以此擬二乘人，無機不受迷悶躃地，於其全生如乳味也。次明密遣二人說除糞法，此譬息大之後鹿苑說三，於小即信革凡成聖，如轉乳為酪，內合從十二部出修多羅，即第二味也。次明心相體信入出無難，譬三藏之後說方等淨名揚大折小，二乘聞大不謗、折小不退，良以三藏斷結取一日價故，得恣其褒貶，若未斷結不堪聞揚大，如前不受勸門，亦不堪聞折小，如前不受誡門，而今不謗不退者，心相體信故也。親既證小則信大不虛，得涅槃價故體折不瞋，雖非己事而不疑謗，此心淳熟如從酪出生蘇，內合從修多羅出方等經，第三味也。次明「長者自知將死不久」下，譬方等心相體信入出無難已後委以家業，使其領教為大菩薩說摩訶般若，既領知眾物貫統法門，心明口辯彌益慕樂，但恨住小非是己分，脫更開許豈不樂哉，於是心漸通泰成就大志，如似生蘇出於熟蘇，是從方等出摩訶般若，第四味也。次「臨欲終時而命其子」者，此譬般若之後判天性定父子，會三歸一付財與記說《法華》之教，開佛知見示真實相，菩薩疑除、聲聞作佛，悉以如來滅度而滅度之，如從熟蘇出於醍醐，是從摩訶般若出大涅槃，即第五味也。四大弟子深得佛意，探領一化五味之教，始終次第其文出此也。

He first sent his attendants — what the attendants expound is likened to the perfect and sudden teaching of the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, this being the very first of the teachings; the attendant is likened to the cow, and what he expounds is likened to milk. Read within, this matches the twelve divisions of scripture issuing from the Buddha — this being precisely the first flavor; gauging the two lesser vehicles by this, lacking the capacity, they do not receive it, and are confused and distressed, falling to the ground — for them, this is entirely like the flavor of raw milk. Next, making clear the secret sending of the two men to teach the dharma of clearing dung — this is likened to, after the great is set to rest, the teaching of the three at the Deer Park, where, for the lesser, one is transformed from an ordinary being into a noble one through faith, like the turning of milk into cream. Read within, this matches the sūtras issuing from the twelve divisions of scripture — this being precisely the second flavor. Next, making clear mutual trust of heart, entering and leaving without difficulty — this is likened to, after the Tripiṭaka teaching, the teaching of the Vaipulya period, the Vimalakīrti Sūtra extolling the great and rebuking the small, where the two lesser vehicles, hearing the great, do not slander it, and, rebuked for the small, do not retreat from it — precisely because, having severed their fetters through the Tripiṭaka teaching and taken the wage of a single day, they can now be indulged in this praise and rebuke; had they not yet severed their fetters, they could not have borne to hear the great extolled, just as, before, they could not accept the gate of persuasion, nor could they have borne to hear the small rebuked, just as, before, they could not accept the gate of admonition — but that they now neither slander nor retreat is precisely because of this mutual trust of heart. Having personally realized the lesser, they trust that the great is not false; having attained the wage of nirvāṇa, they accept the rebuke without resentment; and, though this is not their own affair, they harbor no doubt or slander regarding it — this mind having grown ripe and mellow, like fresh butter coming forth from cream. Read within, this matches the Vaipulya sūtras issuing from the sūtras — this being precisely the third flavor. Next, making clear, from "the elder, knowing for himself that he would not live much longer" onward, that this is likened to, after the Vaipulya period's mutual trust of heart, entering and leaving without difficulty, his being entrusted with the household — having him receive the teaching and expound the Great Perfection of Wisdom for the great bodhisattvas; once he has received and come to know all these things, threading through and mastering every gate of the Dharma, his mind grown clear and his speech eloquent, his own longing and delight only increase, though he still regrets that dwelling in the lesser is not his own proper portion — were this restriction only to be lifted and permission granted, would this not be a joy indeed? And so his mind gradually opens into ease, and his great resolve comes to completion, like refined butter coming forth from fresh butter — this being the Great Perfection of Wisdom issuing from the Vaipulya period, precisely the fourth flavor. Next, as for "when the time of his death drew near, he commanded his son": this is likened to, after the Prajñāpāramitā period, the settling once for all of the natural bond of father and son, the merging of the three into the one, the bestowal of wealth and the conferral of the prediction, the teaching of the Lotus Sūtra — opening the Buddha's own knowledge and vision, and showing the mark of true reality, the bodhisattvas' doubt removed, the śrāvakas becoming buddhas, all alike brought to the very extinction of the Tathāgata himself, like ghee coming forth from refined butter — this being the great nirvāṇa issuing from the Great Perfection of Wisdom, precisely the fifth flavor. The four great disciples, having deeply grasped the Buddha's own intention, reach by way of intent for the teaching of the five flavors within this one process of transformation, in their proper order from beginning to end — this text emerges from just that.

The text on matching the entrustment of the household falls into two: first, mutual trust of heart; second, being commanded to oversee the household affairs. Within mutual trust of heart there are, again, two: first, making clear this mutual trust; second, that he still remains in his former station. First, here, "trust" means mutual trust on both sides: within the Tripiṭaka teaching, having attained the wage of nirvāṇa, this was already no falsehood, and now, having the Great Vehicle expounded for the bodhisattvas, this likewise is no falsehood — this is precisely the son trusting the father. The Buddha, knowing that these disciples have already severed views and thought, knows they will surely not slander what they hear; and, their undefiled faculties being sharp, hearing even a little gives rise to faith — this is precisely the father trusting the son. By this, seeing the honored, exalted body and hearing the Mahāyāna teaching is named "entering"; and being further rebuked and corrected, still seeing the sixteen-foot body expound the Lesser Vehicle teaching, is named "leaving" — entering and leaving, great and small, without any doubt or difficulty. Second, as for "yet he still remained dwelling in the same place as before": though he now enters and leaves without difficulty and hears the Great Vehicle, he still supposes this to be the bodhisattvas' own affair, no part of his own portion of wisdom, and is unwilling to turn from the lesser toward the great, still dwelling as an arhat, not yet saying that he shall attain buddhahood in the future — this matches the teaching of the Mahāsaṃnipāta and Vimalakīrti sūtras, the flavor of fresh butter. From "World-Honored One, at that time the elder fell ill" onward is, second, entrusting him with the household — this matches the teaching of the Great Perfection of Wisdom, where the Buddha commands the relay-teaching to be given, the flavor of refined butter. This falls into two: first, commanding him to know the affairs of the household; second, receiving the command and coming to oversee it. Each of these two sections has, again, four parts.

初四者，一、明時節，二、正命知家事，三、誡令體我心，四、勅無令漏失。初，「將死不久」者，有機則應為生，機盡應謝為死，今化機將畢，應謝非久也。「語窮子言我今多有」下，第二，命知家事。「金」即別教理，「銀」即通教理，《大品》所明真諦不出此二，而云「多有」者，理則非多，約種種門亦得言多，例如空非十八，約破十八法名十八空也。勸學中明一切法門皆是珍寶也。

The first four are: first, making clear the season; second, directly commanding him to know the affairs of the household; third, admonishing him to embody the Buddha's own mind; and fourth, commanding him to let nothing be lost or wasted. First, as for "he would not live much longer": where there is capacity, a response ought to arise for the sake of life; once the capacity is exhausted, the response ought to withdraw, and this is "death." Now, the capacity for transformation being nearly exhausted, the withdrawal of the response will not be long delayed. From "he said to the destitute son, 'I now have great stores'" onward is, second, commanding him to know the affairs of the household. "Gold" is precisely the principle of the distinct teaching, and "silver" is precisely the principle of the shared teaching — the ultimate truth made clear in the Great Perfection of Wisdom does not go beyond these two; and as for saying "great stores," the principle itself is not many, but, considered through its various approaches, it too may be called "many" — just as emptiness is not eighteenfold in itself, yet, considered in terms of the eighteen dharmas it breaks through, it is named "the eighteen emptinesses." Within "Encouragement to Study" it is made clear that every gate of the Dharma is itself a treasure.

「倉」是定門，即百八三昧；「庫」是慧門，十八空境也。通別兩種定慧倉庫，包藏一切禪定智慧無所闕少，內充外溢故云「盈溢」。「其中多少」者，說於般若，則有廣、略二門。菩薩行般若，應知略、廣相，略則為少、廣則為多，自行為取、化他為與。

The "granary" is the gate of concentration, that is, the hundred and eight samādhis; the "storehouse" is the gate of wisdom, the domain of the eighteen emptinesses. The two kinds of concentration and wisdom, shared and distinct, granary and storehouse, contain within them every meditative concentration and wisdom, wanting for nothing, filled within and overflowing without — hence it is said, "filled to overflowing." As for "how much there is": in expounding the Prajñāpāramitā, there are the two approaches, the extensive and the abbreviated. The bodhisattva, practicing the Prajñāpāramitā, ought to know both the abbreviated and the extensive aspects — the abbreviated being "little," and the extensive being "much"; practicing for himself is "taking," and transforming others is "giving."

《大品》中云「汝當為菩薩說」，故云「汝悉知之」。「我心如是」下，第三，誡體。「我心」者，佛以般若為心，汝今傳燈當隨佛意說也。又二乘人本解是析法空，命當體此意者，命轉教用誡令同我體法空也。昔時被命謂傳燈與他，今乃知佛令我識體之門，故言「當體此意」。「今我與汝便為不異」者，釋此有三：一、被加令說與佛不異；二、就理以諸法皆如故得不異，善吉如、如來如，一如無二如，故言便為不異；三、就今時，始悟父子天性本來不異，而二乘人自謂被加異耳。「宜加用心」下，第四，勅無令漏失也。汝為菩薩說般若教無令漏失，二者就理此即汝法後時當用，是故無令漏失也。「即受教勅」下，第二，受命。又為四：一、正受命領知。二、無悕取，善吉雖說般若，自謂我無其分也。三、未捨劣心猶居本處者，住羅漢位，雖復慕大亦未定言欲作菩薩也；未捨下劣心者，雖復恥小，亦未定言捨於小證也。四「復經少時父知子意」下，即是領上索車譬，鄙棄先心欲求大道大機發也。

Within the Great Perfection of Wisdom it is said, "you should expound this for the bodhisattvas" — hence it is said, "you now know all of this in full." From "understand well what is in my heart" onward is, third, the admonition to embody it. As for "my heart": the Buddha takes the Prajñāpāramitā as his heart — you, now transmitting the lamp, should speak in accordance with the Buddha's own intent. Furthermore, the two lesser vehicles originally understand emptiness only as the emptiness reached through analysis; commanding him to embody this very intent means commanding him to relay the teaching, using this admonition to make him identical with my own embodiment of the emptiness inherent in things themselves. In times past, when commanded, he supposed this to be transmitting the lamp on another's behalf; now, at last, he comes to know that the Buddha is making him recognize the very gate of embodiment itself — hence it is said, "understand well what is in my heart." As for "now you and I are truly no different": this explanation has three parts: first, being empowered to speak, he is made no different from the Buddha; second, in terms of principle, since every dharma is thus-suchness, no difference is thereby obtained — Subhūti's thus-suchness and the Tathāgata's thus-suchness are one single thus-suchness, with no second thus-suchness — hence it is said, "truly no different"; and third, in terms of the present moment, he first awakens to the fact that the natural bond of father and son was never, from the start, any different at all, though the practitioners of the two lesser vehicles themselves had supposed that this sameness came only through being empowered. From "you should apply your mind carefully" onward is, fourth, the command that nothing be lost or wasted. You are to expound the Prajñāpāramitā teaching for the bodhisattvas and let nothing be lost; and, second, in terms of principle, this is precisely your own dharma, to be put to use in time to come — hence, let nothing be lost or wasted. From "he at once received this instruction" onward is, second, receiving the command. This, too, falls into four: first, directly receiving the command and coming to oversee it; second, having no desire to take anything for himself — Subhūti, though expounding the Prajñāpāramitā, considers himself to have no portion in it; third, "not yet having set aside his lowly mind, he still remained in his former station" — dwelling in the station of the arhat, though he now longs for the great, he does not yet say with certainty that he wishes to become a bodhisattva; "not yet having set aside his base and lowly mind" means that, though he is now ashamed of the lesser, he does not yet say with certainty that he has relinquished his realization of the lesser; and fourth, from "after some further time had passed, the father saw that his son's mind" onward, this matches asking for the cart above, despising and casting off his former mind and wishing to seek the great path, the great capacity now stirred.

問：何時名少時？

Question: what time is meant by "some further time"?

答：一云說《般若》竟，於異處遊觀，尋思所領大乘法門生心貪樂，為失為不失，如此等尋思，即是大乘機發時也。此時去《法華》未遠故言少時。又當說無量義時大乘機發。何以知然？無量義中明七種方便，無量漸頓從一法生。既聞此說，思惟昔之三藏三乘悉從一法生，如是三乘亦應入一，如是思時漸已通泰大心即發，故言成就大志也。

Answer: one view says that, once the exposition of the Prajñāpāramitā was complete, wandering and observing in some other place, he reflected on the Mahāyāna teaching he had come to oversee, and a mind of longing and delight arose within him — whether this would be lost, or not lost — and it is this very reflection that is precisely the moment the great capacity stirs. This time is not far removed from the Lotus Sūtra — hence it is called "some further time." Another view holds that the great capacity stirred at the time of the expounding of the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings. How can this be known? Within the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings it is made clear that the seven kinds of skillful means, immeasurable and gradual or sudden, all issue from the one dharma. Having heard this teaching, he reflected that the three vehicles of the Tripiṭaka teaching in times past had likewise all issued from the one dharma, and that these same three vehicles ought likewise to enter into the one — and, reflecting in this way, his mind gradually opened into ease, and the great mind at once stirred — hence it is said, "his great resolve came to completion."

Third, when sentient beings meet with suffering, they look only to their father — the Buddha watches for what is fitting for them, as a calf follows its mother, and fully practices the six perfections to benefit sentient beings — this is the kindness of the Tathāgata's room, sheltering from the cold and warding off the heat, the great compassion that removes suffering. Fourth, the Buddha, having accomplished the path, ought to have received the joy of unconditioned quiescent cessation, and yet concealed his own numinous virtue, and, by means of the dharma the poor delight in, the five precepts and the ten wholesome deeds, sprinkled cold water on their faces to bring them to their senses — this is the kindness of the Buddha's robe, sheltering them from the heat of greed and desire. Fifth, manifesting the form of an old bhikṣu, drawing near by skillful means and giving a single day's wage — this is the kindness of the Buddha's robe, removing the heat of views and craving. Sixth, after this, mutual trust of heart, rebuking and disparaging them so as to make them ashamed of the lesser and long for the great — this is the kindness of the Buddha's robe, sheltering them from ugliness and shame. Seventh, commanding him to oversee the household, letting him know all the gold, silver, and storehouses — this is the kindness of the Buddha's robe, adorning me. Eighth, gathering together the relatives, settling father and son, and bestowing the household — the unsurpassed treasure, sought by us not at all, yet attained — this is the kindness of the Tathāgata's seat. Ninth and tenth: having now taken this seat, body and mind at ease, gaining swift and blessed peace, causing the sound of the buddha-path to be heard by all, every deva and human universally receiving offerings within it — this is the kindness of the Tathāgata's seat, causing me to be complete, practicing for myself and transforming others. This is what is meant by "the World-Honored One's great kindness, which the two shoulders, though they bear its weight, could never repay."
