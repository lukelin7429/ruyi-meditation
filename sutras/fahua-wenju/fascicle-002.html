The Six Auspicious Signs

This page continues Ru-Yi's own English translation of Zhiyi's Fahua Wenju (妙法蓮華經文句). Fascicle 2 finishes the disciple roll call begun in fascicle 1, moves into the bodhisattva assembly and its extended "non-retrogression" exegesis, works through the miscellaneous assembly of gods, dragon kings, and human rulers, and closes with the six auspicious signs (六瑞) that lead to Maitreya's question to Mañjuśrī. We translate slowly, section by section, with the Chinese source beside the English so readers can study the meaning, not merely read around it.

Chinese Source

妙法蓮華經文句卷第二上

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary on the Words of the Sūtra of the Lotus Blossom of the Wondrous Dharma, Fascicle Two, Upper Part

天台智者大師說

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai

Gavāṃpati 憍梵波提

Chinese Source

◎「憍梵波提」，此翻牛呞，《無量壽》稱牛王，《增一》云：「牛跡，昔五百世曾為牛王，牛若食後，恒事虛哨；餘報未夷，唼唼常嚼，時人稱為牛呞。」昔五百雁，一雁常得花果，供於雁王，佛一夏受阿耆達王請，五百比丘皆噉馬麥，而憍梵獨在天上尸利沙園，受天王供養。《增一》云：「樂在天上，不樂人間者，牛跡比丘第一。」樂在天上者，是隨樂欲，世界悉檀也；供雁王福所致者，為人也；避人笑者，對治也；天不笑者，第一義也(云云)。又云：「人但觀形不知有德，若笑羅漢即得罪，避人笑故常居天上，天知有德不笑其形，故居天也。」佛滅度後，迦葉集千大羅漢，遣下座僧使追憍梵，憍梵問：「佛及和尚。」答言：「皆滅。」即言：「佛出我出，佛住我住，佛滅我滅。」四道流注大迦葉所，水說偈(云云)：「大象既去象子隨，世尊和尚既滅度，我今在此復何為？」斯亦第一義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Gavāṃpati" is translated "Bull-Chewer," literally "cow's cud." The Sūtra of Immeasurable Life calls him "Bull-King." The Ekottarika Āgama says: "Bull-Tracks — long ago, for five hundred lifetimes he was a bull-king, and whenever a bull has eaten, it habitually works its jaw uselessly afterward; that karmic residue not yet exhausted, he still constantly chews and champs, and people of his time therefore called him 'the Bull-Chewer.'" Long ago, among five hundred wild geese, one goose regularly obtained flowers and fruit to offer to the goose-king; when the Buddha, during one summer retreat, accepted the invitation of King Agnidatta, and the five hundred bhikṣus all had to subsist on horse-fodder barley, Gavāṃpati alone remained in the Śirīṣa Grove in the heavens, receiving the offerings of the heavenly king. The Ekottarika says: "Among those who delight to dwell in the heavens and take no delight in the human world, the bhikṣu Bull-Tracks is foremost." Delighting to dwell in the heavens is a matter of following his own inclination and desire — this is the mundane siddhānta; that this resulted from the merit of offering to the goose-king is the individual siddhānta; that he avoided the mockery of human beings is the therapeutic siddhānta; that the heavenly beings did not mock him is the siddhānta of ultimate meaning (and so on). It is also said: "People, seeing only outward form, do not perceive inner virtue; were they to mock an arhat, they would incur an offense by it — so, to avoid human mockery, he dwelt constantly in the heavens, where the gods, knowing his inner virtue, did not mock his outward form; hence he dwelt in the heavens." After the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, when Kāśyapa gathered the thousand great arhats [for the First Council], he sent a junior monk as messenger to summon Gavāṃpati. Gavāṃpati asked him: "How are the Buddha and my preceptor?" The messenger answered: "Both have entered extinction." Gavāṃpati at once said: "When the Buddha arose, I arose; when the Buddha abided, I abided; when the Buddha entered extinction, I too enter extinction." He then, by his spiritual power, flowed as water into the presence of Mahākāśyapa, and from within the water spoke this verse (and so on): "The great elephant having already departed, the young elephant follows after; now that the World-Honored One and my preceptor have both entered extinction, what further purpose have I in remaining here?" This too is the siddhānta of ultimate meaning.

約教者，住天園是示善，有牛嚼是示惡，三藏意也。以牛嚼身得道，此示惡非惡也，居天園而嚼，示善非善，通教意。示界內外善惡者，別教意。示善惡實相者，圓教意。

As for Gavāṃpati in terms of the teachings: his dwelling in the heavenly grove displays good, and his bovine chewing displays evil — this is the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching. That he attained the path even in a body given to bovine chewing shows that this "evil" is not truly evil; that he dwelt in the heavenly grove and yet still chewed shows that this "good" is not truly good — this is the sense of the shared teaching. To display good and evil both within and beyond the three realms is the sense of the distinct teaching. To display good and evil as themselves true reality is the sense of the perfect teaching.

本迹者，本住四無所畏，安住聖主如牛王第一義天；迹示牛呞樂居天上也。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Gavāṃpati abides in the four fearlessnesses, dwelling securely as a sagely lord, like a bull-king in the heaven of ultimate meaning; in trace he manifests as the Bull-Chewer who delights to dwell among the gods.

觀心者，觀於心性中道之理，安步平正其疾如風，即牛王觀也。

As for the contemplation of mind: to contemplate the principle of the middle way in the nature of mind, advancing steadily and evenly, swift as the wind — this is the contemplation of the bull-king.

Revata 離婆多

Chinese Source

「離婆多」，亦云離越，此翻星宿，或室宿，或假和合，《文殊問經》稱常作聲。父母從星辰乞子，既其感獲因星作名，雖得出家猶隨本字。假和合者，有人引《釋論》，空亭中宿，見二鬼爭屍，告其分判。設依理、枉理俱不免害，故隨實而答。大鬼拔其手足，小鬼取屍補之，食竟，拭口而去。其因煩惱不測誰身故，言假和合。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Revata," also rendered "Raivata," is translated "Constellation," or also "Lodging-Constellation," or also "Provisional Aggregate." The Questions of Mañjuśrī Sūtra calls him "Ever Speaking." His parents prayed to the stars and constellations for a son, and since he was thus obtained through a response to the stars, they named him after a star; though he later went forth as a monk, he still kept his original name. As for "Provisional Aggregate": someone has cited the Treatise [on the Great Perfection of Wisdom], telling how he once lodged for the night in an empty pavilion and saw two demons quarreling over a corpse, and was asked to settle their dispute. He reasoned that whether he answered truthfully or falsely, he could not escape harm either way, and so he answered according to the facts. The greater demon then tore off his hands and feet, and the lesser demon took flesh from the corpse and fitted it onto him in their place; when they had finished eating and wiped their mouths, they departed. Because of this, he could no longer be certain whose body — his own original one, ridden with affliction, or this replaced one — was truly his, and hence he is called "Provisional Aggregate."

常作聲者，其疑此事，若我本身眼見拔去，若是他身復隨我行住。

As for "Ever Speaking": doubting this experience, he wondered — if this is truly my original body, I myself saw it torn away and taken; and if this is another's body, it nevertheless now follows me in every movement and rest.

疑惑猶豫逢人即問：「汝見我身不？」故言常作聲。眾僧云：「此人易度。」語云：「汝身本是他遺體，非己有也。」即得道也(云云)。《增一》云：「坐禪入定，心不倒亂者，離越比丘第一。」

Doubting and hesitating, he would ask everyone he met: "Have you seen my body?" — hence he is called "Ever Speaking." The assembly of monks said: "This man is easy to lead to liberation," and told him: "Your body was originally another's remains; it was never truly your own." At this he immediately attained the path (and so on). The Ekottarika says: "Among those who sit in meditation and enter concentration without their minds becoming inverted or confused, the bhikṣu Revata is foremost."

約教言，析破五陰非我所有，三藏意。體達五陰本非我有，通意。

As for Revata in terms of the teachings: to analytically break apart the five aggregates and thereby see that they are not one's own possession is the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching. To comprehend directly that the five aggregates were never truly one's own possession is the sense of the shared teaching.

分別十法界、五陰皆非己有，別意。達五陰非我有、非他有，見陰實相，即圓意。

To distinguish that, throughout the ten dharma-realms, the five aggregates are never anyone's own possession is the sense of the distinct teaching. To comprehend that the five aggregates are neither one's own nor another's, and thereby to see the true reality of the aggregates, is the sense of the perfect teaching.

本迹者，本住日星宿三昧，迹示此名。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Revata abides in the samādhi of the sun and the constellations; in trace he manifests under this name.

觀心者，觀心念佛，見十方佛多，如夜觀星(云云)。

As for the contemplation of mind: to contemplate the mind while being mindful of the Buddha, and thereby to see the many buddhas of the ten directions, is like gazing at the stars by night (and so on).

Piṇḍola-Bharadvāja 畢陵伽婆蹉

Chinese Source

「畢陵伽婆蹉」，此翻餘習，五百世為婆羅門，餘氣猶高，過恒水：「咄！小婢駐流。」恒神為之兩派。神往訴佛，佛令懺謝，即合手：「小婢莫瞋。」大眾笑之，懺而更罵。佛言：「本習如此，實無高心。」《增一》云：「樹下苦坐，不避風雨者，婆蹉比丘第一。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Piṇḍola-Bharadvāja" is translated "Residual Habit." For five hundred lifetimes he was born a brahmin, and the residual arrogance of that habit still lingered in him. Once, crossing the Ganges, he called out: "Halt, little slave-girl, and let the current stop!" — and the river-spirit parted the waters in two for him. The river-spirit went and complained of this to the Buddha, who directed him to apologize; he pressed his palms together and said: "Little slave-girl, do not be angry." The great assembly laughed at him for this, and even as he apologized he went on scolding her. The Buddha said: "This is merely his habitual manner from long ago; there is truly no arrogance in his heart." The Ekottarika says: "Among those who sit in austere practice beneath trees, undeterred by wind or rain, Bhikṣu Vatsa is foremost."

約教者，滅慢無慢，三藏意也。即慢無慢，通意也。分別十法界高下，別意也。八自在我具足佛法，圓意也。

As for Piṇḍola-Bharadvāja in terms of the teachings: to extinguish arrogance and thereby be without arrogance is the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching. To see that arrogance is itself without arrogance is the sense of the shared teaching. To distinguish the higher and lower positions throughout the ten dharma-realms is the sense of the distinct teaching. To be fully endowed with the buddha-dharma through the eight sovereign selves is the sense of the perfect teaching.

本迹者，本住常樂我淨八自在我，微妙梵聲；迹示慢心惡口耳。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Piṇḍola-Bharadvāja abides in the eight sovereign selves of permanence, bliss, true self, and purity, possessed of a subtle and wondrous Brahmā-voice; in trace he merely manifests an arrogant mind and harsh speech.

觀心者，觀麁言、軟語皆歸第一義(云云)。

As for the contemplation of mind: to contemplate that both coarse speech and gentle speech alike return to the ultimate meaning (and so on).

Vakkula 薄拘羅

Chinese Source

「薄拘羅」者，此翻善容，或偉形，或大肥盛，或腬囊，或楞鄧，或賣性，然而色貌端正，故言善容也。年一百六十歲，無病無夭，有五不死報。後母置熬槃釜中、水中，魚食、刀破皆不死，昔持不殺戒故，九十一劫命不中夭。昔施僧一訶梨勒果故，身常無病，能持一戒、四戒莊嚴，堅持不犯不避火水，餘人雖持五戒，多毀犯也(云云)。身樂寂靜，常處閑居，不樂眾中，眼不樂玄黃等色，耳不樂聞世間之聲，鼻不嗅世間香臭，舌不曾為人說一兩句語，意常在禪定不散亂，乃至舍利塔亦樂閑靜。阿育王禮諸羅漢塔，次至其塔而說偈言：「雖自練無明，於世少利益。」供二十貝子。《增一》云：「施一錢而貝子從塔飛出，來著王足。諸臣驚怪，閑靜少欲乃至其塔猶有是力故。」《增一》云：「壽命極長終不中夭，常樂閑居，不處眾中，薄拘羅第一。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Vakkula" is translated "Excellent Countenance," though it is also rendered "Robust in Form," "Great and Corpulent," "Fleshy Sack," "Gaunt-Browed," or "Merchant by Nature" — his features and countenance being handsome and dignified, he is called "Excellent Countenance." He lived to a hundred and sixty years of age, without illness and without dying prematurely, having received the karmic reward of five escapes from death. His stepmother once placed him in a heated pan and in boiling water, and fish devoured him and blades cut him, yet in every case he did not die — this was the reward for his having, in a past life, upheld the precept against killing, so that for ninety-one kalpas his life was never cut short. And because he had once offered a single myrobalan fruit to the saṅgha, his body was forever free of illness; he was able to keep a single precept adorned by the fourfold precepts, holding to it firmly without transgression, undeterred by fire or water — while other people, though they kept the five precepts, mostly violated them in one way or another (and so on). He delighted in bodily stillness, dwelling always in quiet solitude, taking no pleasure in crowds; his eyes took no delight in colors of black and yellow and the like, his ears took no delight in hearing the sounds of the world, his nose did not smell the world's fragrances and stenches, his tongue never spoke so much as a phrase or two to another person, and his mind constantly abided in meditative concentration without distraction — and even the stūpa built over his relics remained a place of quiet solitude. When King Aśoka went to pay homage at the stūpas of the various arhats, when he came in turn to Vakkula's stūpa he spoke this verse: "Though he cultivated only for himself, without knowledge, he brought little benefit to the world" — and he offered only twenty cowrie shells. The Ekottarika says: "Yet when he offered a single coin, the cowrie shells flew forth from the stūpa of their own accord and came to rest at the king's feet. The ministers, astonished, marveled that even now, his quiet solitude and few desires still carried this kind of power at his very stūpa." The Ekottarika also says: "Among those of the longest life who never die prematurely, who delight always in quiet solitude and do not dwell among crowds, the bhikṣu Vakkula is foremost."

約教者，滅喧入真，三藏寂靜。即喧而真，通寂靜。離二邊入中，別寂靜。即邊而中，圓寂靜。

As for Vakkula in terms of the teachings: to extinguish clamor and thereby enter the truth is the stillness of the Tripiṭaka teaching. To see that clamor is itself the truth is the stillness of the shared teaching. To depart from both extremes and enter the middle is the stillness of the distinct teaching. To see that the extremes are themselves the middle is the stillness of the perfect teaching.

本者，本住大寂滅定，長壽是常，無病是樂，不夭是我，寂靜是淨；居此四德之本，迹示六根寂靜耳。

As for his origin: Vakkula abides in the great concentration of quiescent extinction — his long life is permanence, his freedom from illness is bliss, his not dying prematurely is true self, and his stillness is purity; dwelling in this origin of the four virtues, he merely manifests in trace as one whose six sense-faculties are still.

觀心者，心性中道，即空、即假、即中，常、樂、我、淨觀也。

As for the contemplation of mind: the middle way of the nature of mind — being at once empty, provisional, and the middle — is the contemplation of permanence, bliss, true self, and purity.

Mahākauṣṭhila 摩訶拘絺羅

Chinese Source

「摩訶拘絺羅」，此翻大膝，舍利弗舅，由來論勝姊。姊孕，論則不勝，知所懷者，智寄辯尚爾，何況出胎？(云云)即棄家往南天竺，讀十八經。時人笑之：「累世難通，一生非冀。」喟然歎曰：「在家為姊所勝，出路為他所輕。」誓讀不休，無暇剪爪，時人呼為長爪梵志。學訖還家，問甥所在，人云：「為佛弟子。」即大憍慢：「我甥八歲聲震五竺，彼沙門者有何道術誘我姊子？」徑往佛所，思惟良久，不得一法入心，語佛言：「一切法不忍，忍即安義，此言一切法我皆能破，使不得安故，言一切法不忍。」佛問：「汝見是忍不？」此墮兩負處：若我見忍，前已云：「一切不忍。」若我見不忍，無以勝佛，即低頭，得法眼淨。身子扇佛聞舅論，得阿羅漢果。《增一》云：「得四辯才，觸難能答，拘絺羅第一。」南方天王毘留匐叉常來隨侍。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Mahākauṣṭhila" is translated "Great Knee." He was Śāriputra's maternal uncle, and had always been bested by his sister in debate. When his sister became pregnant, he was defeated in debate even more thoroughly, and understood that the wisdom borrowed by the child she carried was already remarkable even before birth — how much more so, then, once the child had actually come forth from the womb? (and so on) He therefore abandoned his household and went to South India to study the eighteen canons. People of his time mocked him: "Even a lifetime spent across many generations could scarcely master them all — a single life is no hope at all." He sighed and said: "At home I was bested by my sister; abroad I am belittled by others." He vowed to read without ceasing, having no time even to trim his nails — and so people of the time called him "the Long-Nailed Brahmacārin." When his studies were complete and he returned home, he asked after his nephew's whereabouts, and was told: "He has become a disciple of the Buddha." At this he grew greatly arrogant, saying: "My nephew's fame shook the five regions of India by the age of eight — what manner of arts does this ascetic possess, that he could lure away my sister's son?" He went directly to where the Buddha was, and after pondering at great length he could find not a single teaching that took root in his mind, and said to the Buddha: "I do not accept any doctrine" — "accept" here meaning "to rest in" — that is, he meant: "I am able to refute every single doctrine, allowing none to stand at rest; hence I say I do not accept any doctrine." The Buddha asked him: "Do you accept this view of yours?" This placed him in a position where he could not win either way: if he answered that he did accept this view, then it contradicted what he had just said — that he accepted no doctrine at all; and if he answered that he did not accept this view either, then he had no way to defeat the Buddha. He bowed his head at once, and thereby attained the purity of the Dharma-eye. Śāriputra, who had been fanning the Buddha, overheard his uncle's debate and attained the fruit of arhatship as well. The Ekottarika says: "Among those who possess the four unhindered eloquences and can answer any challenge put to them, Kauṣṭhila is foremost." Virūḍhaka, the heavenly king of the south, constantly comes to attend upon him.

約教者，外通四韋陀，內通三藏，三藏四辯也。我無所得，辯乃如是，通教辯也。若名、若義遍十法界，別教辯也。依於實相遍一切辯，圓教辯也。

As for Mahākauṣṭhila in terms of the teachings: to be versed outwardly in the four Vedas and inwardly in the Tripiṭaka is the fourfold eloquence of the Tripiṭaka teaching. To have obtained no fixed self, and yet to speak with just such eloquence, is the eloquence of the shared teaching. To have both name and meaning pervade the ten dharma-realms is the eloquence of the distinct teaching. To speak with eloquence that pervades everything, grounded in true reality, is the eloquence of the perfect teaching.

本者，本住口密、口輪不思議化，大定、大慧；迹示大膝也。

As for his origin: Mahākauṣṭhila abides in the secret of speech and the inconceivable transformative power of the wheel of speech, in great concentration and great wisdom; in trace he merely manifests as "Great Knee."

觀心者，觀心即空即定、即假即慧，以嚴其心(云云)。

As for the contemplation of mind: to contemplate that mind, being empty, is itself concentration, and, being provisional, is itself wisdom — thereby adorning the mind (and so on).

Nanda 難陀

Chinese Source

「難陀」，亦云放牛難陀，此翻善歡喜，亦翻欣樂。淨飯王偪十萬釋出家，即一人也。有師言：「是律中跋難陀。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Nanda," also called "Cowherd Nanda," is translated "Excellent Joy," also rendered "Delight." He was one of the hundred thousand Śākyas whom King Śuddhodana compelled to go forth. Some teachers say he is the same as the Upananda who appears in the Vinaya.

約教者，事歡喜，理無歡喜，三藏意也。即事歡喜是理歡喜，是通教意也。歡喜地，即別教也。歡喜住，即圓教意也。

As for Nanda in terms of the teachings: joy as a phenomenal matter, with no principle of joy behind it, is the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching. That phenomenal joy is itself the joy of principle is the sense of the shared teaching. The ground of joy [the first of the ten bodhisattva grounds] is the sense of the distinct teaching. The abode of joy [the first of the ten abodes] is the sense of the perfect teaching.

本者，本住實際，非喜非不喜；迹名歡喜。

As for his origin: Nanda abides in ultimate reality itself, which is neither joy nor the absence of joy; in trace he is named "Joy."

觀心者，觀心與理相似相應，故名歡喜觀也。

As for the contemplation of mind: to contemplate mind as corresponding in resemblance to principle is called the contemplation of joy.

Sundarananda 孫陀羅難陀

Chinese Source

「孫陀羅難陀」，孫陀羅，此翻好愛，亦端正，難陀如前，種姓如那律中說。四月九日生，短佛四指，容儀挺特與世殊異，若入眾中有不識者，謂言佛來。《彌沙塞律》云：「摩竭有裸形外道，大聰明，國人號為智者、見者，共身子論議，結舌，善心生，欲於佛法出家。見難陀色貌姝偉，歎云：『短小比丘智慧難概，況堂堂者乎？』難陀即度出家。婦即孫陀利，極端正，食息不相離。佛與阿難途行乞食到其門，正共婦在高樓食，即起迎佛，婦言：『須君還乃共食耳。』白佛言：『轉輪王種云何自辱？』持佛鉢取飯。佛即還尼俱類園，語阿難：『令難陀送食來。』阿難宣佛旨，令其送飯奉佛，佛令剃頭，握拳語剃者：『勿持刀臨閻浮提王頂。』佛偪不得止，乃剃頭。明日佛與五百比丘應請，求住守寺，意欲逃去。佛令關房掃地，關南、北開，掃此、彼污。復懼佛歸，即逃走歸去。於路值佛，屏身隱樹，樹逈升空，佛見即喚將還。問：『何故去？』即答：『昨與婦別，待還乃食。憶婦去耳。』佛將遊天堂、地獄(云云)。」故以婦字標之。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Sundarananda" — "Sundarī" is translated "Beloved," also "Handsome," while "Nanda" is as explained above; his clan is as described in the account of Aniruddha. He was born on the ninth day of the fourth month, and stood four fingers' breadth shorter than the Buddha; his bearing and countenance were so extraordinary and unlike the rest of the world's that, should someone unfamiliar with him enter the assembly, they would take him for the Buddha himself. The Mahīśāsaka Vinaya says: "In Magadha there was a naked ascetic of great intelligence, whom the people of the kingdom called 'the wise one' and 'the seer.' He debated with Śāriputra and was reduced to silence, whereupon a good mind arose in him, and he wished to go forth within the Buddha's Dharma. Seeing Nanda's handsome and imposing countenance, he exclaimed: 'Even this bhikṣu of short stature is one whose wisdom is difficult to fathom — how much more so, then, one so magnificent as this!' Nanda was thereupon led forth to ordination. His wife was Sundarī, a woman of the utmost beauty, who was never apart from him whether eating or resting. Once, when the Buddha and Ānanda were passing on their alms round and came to his door, Nanda was just then eating with his wife in an upper chamber; he rose at once to greet the Buddha, but his wife said: 'Wait until you return before we eat together' — and then said to the Buddha: 'How is it that one of the royal lineage of a wheel-turning king should so demean himself?' — and took the Buddha's bowl herself to fill it with food. The Buddha then returned to the Nyagrodha Grove and told Ānanda: 'Have Nanda bring the food here.' Ānanda conveyed the Buddha's instruction, and had Nanda deliver the meal to offer to the Buddha. The Buddha then ordered his head to be shaved; Nanda clenched his fist and said to the one who was to shave him: 'Do not bring a blade near the head of a king of Jambudvīpa!' — but, pressed by the Buddha, he could not refuse, and so his head was shaved. The next day, when the Buddha and five hundred bhikṣus went out to accept an invitation, Nanda asked to remain behind to watch over the monastery, intending to slip away. The Buddha had him close the gates and sweep the grounds — closing the south gate while the north stood open, sweeping one side while the other grew soiled — and, still fearing the Buddha would return, Nanda fled and made his way home. On the road he met the Buddha, and hid himself behind a tree; but the tree rose far up into the sky, and the Buddha, seeing him, called out and had him brought back. The Buddha asked: 'Why were you fleeing?' He answered: 'I parted from my wife only yesterday, and promised to eat together with her upon my return — I was merely thinking of my wife, and so I went.' The Buddha then took him to see the heavens and the hells (and so on)." Hence he is designated by the word "wife" [in his very name, Sundarī-Nanda].

約教者，俗諦有法喜，真諦無喜，三藏教也。即俗喜是真喜，通教也。從通法喜，有俗法喜、中法喜，別教也。即通喜具一切法喜，圓教也。

As for Sundarananda in terms of the teachings: that there is Dharma-joy at the level of conventional truth, but no joy at the level of ultimate truth, is the Tripiṭaka teaching. That conventional joy is itself ultimate joy is the shared teaching. That, proceeding from the shared teaching's Dharma-joy, there is further the Dharma-joy of the conventional and the Dharma-joy of the middle, is the distinct teaching. That the joy of the shared teaching already contains every form of Dharma-joy is the perfect teaching.

本迹觀心如前(云云)。

His trace and origin, and the contemplation of mind, are as explained above [for Nanda] (and so on).

Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇīputra 富樓那

Chinese Source

「富樓那」，翻滿願；彌多羅，翻慈；尼，女也。父於滿江禱梵天求子，正值江滿，又夢七寶器，盛滿中寶入母懷，母懷子，父願獲滿，從諸遂願，故言滿願。母名彌多羅尼，此翻慈行，亦云知識。四韋陀有此品，其母誦之，以此為名。尼者女也，通稱女為尼，通稱男為那，既是慈之所生故言慈子。《增一》云：「我父名滿，我母名慈，諸梵行人呼我為滿慈子。」此從父母兩緣得名，故云滿慈子。是人善知，內外經書靡所不知，就知滿故復名滿。《增一》云：「善能廣說分別義理，滿願子最第一。」下文云：「於說法人中最為第一。」第一者，說滿字也。欲還本國利益，佛言：「彼國弊惡，汝云何？」答：「我當修忍，若毀辱我，我當自幸不得拳歐，拳歐時自幸不得木杖，木杖時自幸不得刀刃，刀刃時自幸離五陰毒器。」是為行忍滿，故名滿。《七車喻經》中說：「為大智舍利弗所稱歎，一切梵行人皆當縈衣頂戴於汝，若見汝者得大利益。」是為歎滿，故名滿。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Pūrṇa" is translated "Fulfilled Vow"; "Maitrāyaṇī" is translated "Loving-Kindness"; "putra" means "son." His father prayed to Brahmā at the Full River for a son, and it happened that the river was at that very moment full to overflowing; he also dreamed of a vessel of the seven treasures, brimming with treasure, entering his wife's bosom. When his wife then conceived this child, the father's prayer had been fulfilled, his wish granted in every particular — hence the name "Fulfilled Vow." His mother was named Maitrāyaṇī, translated "Conduct of Loving-Kindness," also called "Knowledge" [one versed in learning]. The four Vedas contain a section by this name, which his mother had memorized and recited, and from this he took his name. "Nī" means "female" — it is the general term for a woman, just as "na" is the general term for a man — and since he was born of this "Loving-Kindness," he is called "the Son of Loving-Kindness." The Ekottarika says: "My father is named Fulfilled, and my mother is named Loving-Kindness; hence all my fellow practitioners of the holy life call me the Son of Fulfilled Loving-Kindness." Since he received his name from these two causes, both father and mother, he is called "the Son of Fulfilled Loving-Kindness." This man was well versed in learning, leaving nothing unknown among the scriptures both within and beyond the Buddhist canon; and because of this thorough — literally "full" — knowledge, he is also named "Fulfilled" on that separate ground. The Ekottarika says: "Among those able to expound the Dharma broadly and to distinguish its meaning with skill, the son of Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇī is foremost." The text below likewise says: "Among those who teach the Dharma, he is foremost" — this "foremost" here plays on the character "full" as well. Wishing to return to his native country to benefit its people, he was asked by the Buddha: "That land is coarse and violent — what will you do?" He answered: "I will cultivate forbearance. If they should revile and insult me, I will consider myself fortunate that they have not struck me with their fists; if they strike me with their fists, I will consider myself fortunate that they have not beaten me with a rod; if they beat me with a rod, I will consider myself fortunate that they have not cut me with a blade; and if they cut me with a blade, I will consider myself fortunate to be freed at last from this poisonous vessel of the five aggregates." This is the fulfillment of the practice of forbearance, and hence he is called "Fulfilled." The Sūtra of the Simile of the Seven Chariots says: "He was praised by the great and wise Śāriputra, who said: 'All practitioners of the holy life ought to wrap up their robes and carry them upon their heads in his honor — whoever meets him gains great benefit.'" This is the fulfillment of praise, and hence he is called "Fulfilled."

約教者，殷勤析法，所作已辦，三藏願滿；體達即空，於空法得證，通教願滿；法眼具足，別教願滿；住祕密藏，圓教願滿。

As for Pūrṇa in terms of the teachings: to labor diligently in analytical dharma-practice until one's task is accomplished is the fulfillment of the vow according to the Tripiṭaka teaching. To comprehend directly that all is empty, and to attain realization within the dharma of emptiness, is the fulfillment of the vow according to the shared teaching. To have the Dharma-eye fully complete is the fulfillment of the vow according to the distinct teaching. To abide in the secret treasury is the fulfillment of the vow according to the perfect teaching.

本迹者，本願久滿，迹為說法第一，示眾生知識也。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Pūrṇa's vow was fulfilled long ago; in trace he becomes foremost in teaching the Dharma, showing himself as a spiritual friend to sentient beings.

觀心者，如止觀中人行理等善知識觀也。

As for the contemplation of mind: this is as explained in the Mohe Zhiguan concerning the contemplation of the good spiritual friend — the person, the practice, and the principle alike.

Subhūti 須菩提

Chinese Source

「須菩提」，此翻空生，生時家中倉庫筐篋器皿一切皆空。問占者，占者言吉。因空而生，字曰空生。從依報器皿瑞空以名正報，依正俱吉，故言空生也。常修空行故言善業。若供養者得現報故，故言善吉。常樂遊止閑林石窟寂靜之處，所修行業以空為本，常入空定住無諍三昧，喜說空法，有所宣辯皆分別空。將護眾生不令起礙，嫌行即住，嫌住即行。佛忉利下，率土輻湊爭前頂禮，端坐石室念諸法空，色非佛乃至識非佛，眼非佛乃至意非佛，豁然悟道。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Subhūti" is translated "Born of Emptiness." At the time of his birth, every storehouse, chest, basket, and vessel in his household became empty. When a diviner was consulted, the diviner pronounced this auspicious. Because he was born amid this emptiness, he was named "Born of Emptiness" — his name for his dependent retribution [environment] taken from the auspicious emptiness of the household vessels, and applied by extension to his direct retribution [person]; since both dependent and direct retribution were auspicious, he is called "Born of Emptiness." Because he constantly cultivated the practice of emptiness, he is also called "Good Karma"; and because those who made offerings to him obtained visible rewards in this very life, he is also called "Excellently Auspicious." He always delighted to dwell and wander in quiet forests and stone caves, places of stillness and solitude; the practice he cultivated took emptiness as its root, and he constantly entered the concentration of emptiness, abiding in the samādhi of non-contention, delighting to expound the Dharma of emptiness — whatever he proclaimed or debated, all of it distinguished emptiness. Guarding and protecting sentient beings so as not to give rise to any obstruction in them, he would rest when they disliked his moving about, and move about when they disliked his resting. When the Buddha descended from the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, the whole land converged upon him, each vying to be first to bow at his feet; Subhūti alone sat upright in a stone cave, contemplating that all dharmas are empty — that form is not the Buddha, and so on up to consciousness not being the Buddha; that the eye is not the Buddha, and so on up to the mind not being the Buddha — and thereby suddenly awakened to the path.

佛告蓮華比丘尼：「非汝前禮，汝禮色身；須菩提前見法身。」

The Buddha said to the bhikṣuṇī Padmā: "You are not truly the first to bow before me — you have only bowed before my physical body; it is Subhūti who was truly first, for he has seen my dharma-body."

約教者，自有滅色空智生，體色空智生，從有智生空智，從空智生俗智，從俗智生中智，空生即有智，是圓空智生，而今是圓空智生也。

As for Subhūti in terms of the teachings: there is the wisdom of emptiness that arises from extinguishing form [the Tripiṭaka teaching]; the wisdom of emptiness that arises from directly comprehending form [the shared teaching]; the wisdom of emptiness that arises from the wisdom of existence, from which the wisdom of the conventional then arises, and from which in turn the wisdom of the middle arises [the distinct teaching]; and the wisdom by which "Born of Emptiness" is himself the wisdom of existence — this is the wisdom of emptiness born perfect and complete, and this present instance is precisely that perfect wisdom of emptiness [the perfect teaching].

本者，本住實相法身，迹示見空而生也。

As for his origin: Subhūti abides in the dharma-body of true reality; in trace he manifests as one born through the seeing of emptiness.

觀心者，不在內外中間，非自有，是為觀心法身也。

As for the contemplation of mind: to see that mind is neither inside, nor outside, nor in between, and does not exist of itself, is the contemplation of the dharma-body of mind.

Ānanda 阿難

Chinese Source

「阿難」，此云歡喜，或無染，淨飯王冀太子為金輪霸其宗社，忽棄國捐王，憂惱殆絕，魔來誑之：「汝子已死。」王哭云：「阿夷語既虛，瑞相亦無驗。」復有天來云：「汝子成佛。」王疑未決，須臾信報：「昨夜天地大動，太子成佛。」王大歡喜，白飯王奏云：「生兒。」舉國欣欣，因名歡喜，是為父母作字。阿難端正，人見皆悅，佛使著覆肩衣。有一女人將兒詣井，見阿難目視不眴，不覺以綆繫其兒頸。《中阿含》云：「四眾若聞阿難所說，若多若少無不歡喜，欲發問時先為謦咳，大眾皆歡喜。四眾若觀其默，行住坐臥，指撝處分，進止動轉皆歡喜。」阿難四月八日佛成道日生，侍佛得二十五年，推此佛年五十五，阿難年二十五。佛時求侍，五百請為如前說，眾勸阿難，阿難順從，五百皆歡喜。目連騰阿難三願，佛言：「預知譏嫌，求不受故衣食，欲自利益求出入無時。」佛印而許。佛言：「阿難勝過去侍，過去侍聞說乃解，今佛未發言，阿難已解如來意，須是、不須是皆悉能知。」故以法付阿難，如來歡喜。四天王各奉佛鉢，佛累而按之合成一鉢，四緣宛然而此鉢大重，阿難歡喜，荷持無倦。《中阿含》第七云：「阿難侍佛二十五年，所聞八十千揵度皆誦不遺，不重問一句。」念力歡喜。阿難隨佛入天人龍宮，見天人龍女，心無染著，雖未盡殘思而能不染，一切天人龍神無不歡喜。佛滅度後在師子床，迦葉大眾讚曰：「面如淨滿月，眼若青蓮華，佛法大海水，流入阿難心。」自誓坐入涅槃，住恐離車有怨，進恐闍王有怨。於恒河中，入風奮迅三昧，分身為四分：一與天，一與龍，一毘舍離，一阿闍世。阿育王禮阿難塔，奉千萬兩金，偈歎白：「能攝持法身，法燈故法住，念盛佛智海，故設上供養，念持多所聞，口出微妙語，世尊所讚歎，天人之所愛。」《增一》云：「知時明物，所至無疑，所憶不忘，多聞廣達，堪任奉持，阿難第一。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Ānanda" is translated "Joy," also rendered "Undefiled." King Śuddhodana had hoped that the crown prince would become a wheel-turning king and rule over his clan and its ancestral shrine; when the prince suddenly abandoned the kingdom and renounced the throne, the king's grief and distress very nearly overwhelmed him. Māra then came to deceive him, saying: "Your son is already dead." The king wept, saying: "If the seer's prophecy proves false, then the auspicious signs too are unconfirmed." Then a deva came and told him: "Your son has become a buddha" — but the king remained uncertain, still undecided, until in a short while a messenger brought confirming word: "Last night heaven and earth shook greatly; the crown prince has become a buddha." The king rejoiced greatly, and at that same time word was brought to King Śuklodana: "A son has been born to you." The whole kingdom rejoiced together, and for this reason the child was named "Joy" — this being the name given him by his parents. Ānanda's countenance was handsome and dignified, and all who saw him were delighted; the Buddha had him wear an outer shoulder-robe. Once a woman, bringing her child to a well, saw Ānanda and gazed at him without blinking, and, without noticing what she did, tied the rope to her child's neck instead of the bucket. The Madhyama Āgama says: "Whenever the fourfold assembly heard Ānanda speak, whether much or little, there was none who was not delighted; when he was about to raise a question, he would first clear his throat, and the whole assembly would already be delighted. And whenever the fourfold assembly merely observed him in silence — in his walking, standing, sitting, and lying down, in his gestures and bearing, in his every movement and pause — they were delighted as well." Ānanda was born on the eighth day of the fourth month, the very day the Buddha attained enlightenment, and he attended upon the Buddha for twenty-five years; reckoning from this, when the Buddha was fifty-five years old, Ānanda was twenty-five. At that time the Buddha sought an attendant, and the five hundred [disciples present] each requested the position, as explained above; the assembly then urged Ānanda to accept it, and Ānanda complied, to the joy of all five hundred. Maudgalyāyana conveyed Ānanda's three requests to the Buddha: that he be told in advance of anything that might give rise to criticism or suspicion; that he not be required to accept the Buddha's used robes and food; and that, for his own convenience, he be permitted to come and go without fixed times. The Buddha sealed his approval and granted these requests. The Buddha said: "Ānanda surpasses my former attendants — my former attendants understood only after I had spoken, but now, before I have even opened my mouth, Ānanda has already grasped the Tathāgata's intent, knowing at once whether a thing is needed or not needed." For this reason the Buddha entrusted the Dharma to Ānanda, and the Tathāgata himself rejoiced in this. Once, when the Four Heavenly Kings each offered the Buddha an alms bowl, the Buddha stacked all four and pressed them together into a single bowl, so that the four separate bowls remained distinctly visible within it, yet this bowl was extremely heavy — and Ānanda joyfully carried it without ever tiring. The seventh fascicle of the Madhyama Āgama says: "Ānanda attended the Buddha for twenty-five years, and of the eighty thousand teaching-divisions he heard, he recited every one without omitting anything, never needing to ask a single question twice" — this is the joy of the power of memory. Ānanda accompanied the Buddha into the palaces of the gods, men, and nāgas, and, seeing the daughters of gods and nāgas, his mind remained free of any defiling attachment; though he had not yet exhausted every residual thought, he was nonetheless able to remain undefiled — and all the gods, men, nāgas, and spirits alike rejoiced at this. After the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, as Ānanda sat upon the lion throne, Kāśyapa and the great assembly praised him, saying: "His face is like the pure and full moon, his eyes like the blue lotus flower; the great ocean of the Buddha-dharma has flowed entirely into Ānanda's mind." Ānanda then vowed to sit and enter nirvāṇa himself: were he to remain, he feared the Licchavis would bear a grudge; were he to depart, he feared King Ajātaśatru would bear a grudge. So, in the midst of the Ganges River, he entered the samādhi of the swiftly-rising wind, and divided his body into four parts — one for the gods, one for the nāgas, one for Vaiśālī, and one for Ajātaśatru. When King Aśoka paid homage at Ānanda's stūpa, he offered ten million taels of gold, and praised him in verse, saying: "Able to uphold and preserve the dharma-body, the lamp of the Dharma, so that the Dharma endures; mindful of the vast ocean of the Buddha's wisdom, I therefore make this supreme offering. Mindful and retentive, of vast learning, from whose mouth came forth subtle and wondrous words, praised by the World-Honored One himself, beloved of gods and men." The Ekottarika says: "Among those who know the proper time and understand all things clearly, who go wherever needed without hesitation, who remember what they have heard without forgetting, who are of vast learning and broad understanding, and who are fit to receive and uphold the Dharma, Ānanda is foremost."

約教者，歡喜阿難，三藏也；賢阿難，通也；典藏阿難，別也；海阿難，圓也。

As for Ānanda in terms of the teachings: "Joyful Ānanda" belongs to the Tripiṭaka teaching; "Worthy Ānanda" belongs to the shared teaching; "Treasury-Keeper Ānanda" belongs to the distinct teaching; "Ocean Ānanda" belongs to the perfect teaching.

本迹者，本住非歡喜非不歡喜，法身如虛空，智慧如雲雨，能持能受。迹為歡喜也。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Ānanda abides in that which is neither joy nor the absence of joy; his dharma-body is like empty space, his wisdom like clouds and rain, able both to uphold and to receive [the Dharma]. In trace he becomes "Joy."

觀心與相似即空、即假、即中相應，是觀心歡喜，乃至真觀相應(云云)。

As for the contemplation of mind: when the mind corresponds, in resemblance, to being empty, provisional, and the middle, this is the joy of the contemplation of mind; and it extends up to correspondence with the true contemplation itself (and so on).

Rāhula 羅睺羅

Chinese Source

「羅睺羅」，此言覆障，往昔塞鼠穴，又不看婆羅門六日，由是緣故故言覆障。太子求出家，父王不許，殷勤不已，王言：「若汝有子，聽汝出家。」菩薩指指妃腹：「却後六年，汝當生男。」在胎六年，故言覆障。真諦三藏云：「羅睺，本名修羅，能手障日月，翻此應言障月。」佛言：「我法如月，此兒障我不即出家，世世障我，我世世能捨，故言覆障。」佛出家後耶輸有娠，諸釋咸瞋，何因有此？欲治、欲殺，惡聲盈路，寶女劬毘羅證之小差。因焚火坑，發大誓願：「我若為非，子母俱滅，若真遺體，天當為證！」因抱子投坑，坑變為池，蓮華捧體，王及國人，始復不疑。後佛還國，耶輸令羅睺奉佛歡喜丸，羅云以幼稚之年，於大眾中徑持上佛，耶輸以此息謗。謗由有子，故言覆障。祖王歡喜：「雖失其父，而獲其子，孫為金輪，吾亦何恨？」想其長大，冀神寶至。而佛索令出家，父王不許，耶輸將上高樓，目連飛空來取，佛度出家，付舍利弗為弟子。既出家已，王位亦失，故言覆障。羅睺以沙彌之年喜多妄語，國王、大臣、婆羅門、居士來求見佛，羅云答云：「不在。」令無量人不得見佛，是為障他。由是妄語，佛即訶責。行還使羅云洗足，脚挑澡盆三覆三仰，然後覆地命令注水，羅睺云：「盆覆注水不立。」佛言：「汝如覆盆，於佛法中法水不立，今當實語，勿妄語也。」後時修道殷勤不獲，以問佛，佛言：「汝為人說五陰未？」答言：「未。」「當為他說。」說竟又問，「汝說十二入未？說十八界未？說法是得道之門，若欲得道當為他說法。」因廣說法竟然後得道，是為覆障。既已得道，見愛皆除，三界生盡，故言覆障。三界生盡，願不能牽，故言覆障。佛勅四大羅漢不得滅度，待我法滅盡。由是住持于今，未得入無餘涅槃，故言覆障(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Rāhula" means "Obstruction." In a past life he had blocked up a mouse's burrow, and on another occasion had refused to look upon a brahmin for six days; because of these causes, he is called "Obstruction." When the crown prince sought to go forth, his father the king would not permit it; when the prince pressed his request again and again, the king said: "If you have a son, I will allow you to go forth." The bodhisattva then pointed at his consort's womb and said: "After six years have passed, you shall bear a son" — and the child indeed remained in the womb for six years, hence he is called "Obstruction." The Tripiṭaka Master Paramārtha says: "Rāhu was originally the name of an asura who could block the sun and moon with his hand; translated accordingly, the name ought to mean 'Moon-Obstructor.'" The Buddha said: "My Dharma is like the moon; this child obstructed my going forth without delay, obstructing me life after life — and life after life I have been able to renounce him regardless — hence he is called 'Obstruction.'" After the Buddha had gone forth, Yaśodharā was found to be pregnant, and all the Śākyas were enraged, demanding to know how this could be; some wished to punish her, others to put her to death, and evil rumors filled every road. The jewel-maiden Kubjottarā testified on her behalf, which brought about only a slight easing of the matter. Yaśodharā therefore had a fire-pit built, and made a great vow before it: "If I have done wrong, let my child and I both perish in these flames; but if this is truly [the Buddha's] own child, let the heavens themselves bear witness!" — and, holding her child, she leapt into the pit. The pit at once transformed into a pool, and a lotus flower rose to support her body, and only then did the king and the people of the country cease to doubt her. Later, when the Buddha returned to his home country, Yaśodharā had Rāhula present the Buddha with a ball of sweetened dough as an offering; though still a young child, Rāhula carried it forward directly before the whole assembly and presented it to the Buddha, and by this Yaśodharā finally put the slander to rest. Since the slander had arisen on account of this child, he is again called "Obstruction." His grandfather the king rejoiced, saying: "Though I have lost my son, I have gained my grandson — my grandson will become a wheel-turning king; what regret have I left?" — and he looked forward to the boy's growing to manhood, hoping the seven divine treasures would then appear. But the Buddha sought to have him go forth as well; the king would not permit it, and Yaśodharā took the boy up to a high tower to keep him from the Buddha, but Maudgalyāyana flew up through the air and brought him down, and the Buddha had him ordained, entrusting him to Śāriputra as his teacher. Once he had gone forth, the throne too was lost to him — hence he is again called "Obstruction." In his years as a novice, Rāhula was much given to false speech; when kings, ministers, brahmins, and householders came seeking to see the Buddha, Rāhula would tell them: "He is not here" — thereby preventing countless people from seeing the Buddha, and this too counts as "obstructing others." Because of this habit of false speech, the Buddha rebuked him. Returning from a journey, the Buddha had Rāhula wash his feet, and had him overturn the washbasin three times and turn it upright three times; the Buddha then had it turned upside-down on the ground and ordered water poured over it. Rāhula said: "The basin is upside-down; the poured water cannot stand within it." The Buddha said: "You are like this overturned basin — the water of the Dharma cannot stand within you either. From now on you must speak truthfully, and never speak falsely again." Later, though he applied himself diligently to cultivating the path, he made no progress, and he asked the Buddha about this; the Buddha said: "Have you taught others about the five aggregates?" He answered: "Not yet." "You should teach this to others." When he had done so, the Buddha asked again: "Have you taught the twelve sense-fields? Have you taught the eighteen elements? Teaching the Dharma is itself the gateway to attaining the path — if you wish to attain the path, you must teach the Dharma to others." He then expounded the Dharma at length, and only afterward attained the path — this too is an instance of "Obstruction." Having attained the path, he had eliminated views and craving alike, and his rebirths within the three realms were exhausted — hence he is again called "Obstruction." With his rebirths within the three realms exhausted, no vow could any longer draw him back into them — hence he is again called "Obstruction." The Buddha decreed that Rāhula, together with three other great arhats, should not enter final extinction, but should remain until the Buddha's Dharma itself had entirely disappeared. For this reason he continues to abide and uphold the Dharma even to this day, not yet having entered the nirvāṇa without remainder — hence he is called "Obstruction" (and so on).

約教者，析法道諦障四住，三藏意也；體法道諦障四住，通教也；次第三智障五住，別教也；一心三智障五住，圓教也。

As for Rāhula in terms of the teachings: the path-truth of analytical dharma-practice obstructing the four kinds of affliction-abiding is the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching; the path-truth of directly comprehended dharma-practice obstructing the four kinds of affliction-abiding is the sense of the shared teaching; the three kinds of wisdom cultivated in sequence obstructing the five kinds of affliction-abiding is the sense of the distinct teaching; the three kinds of wisdom within a single mind obstructing the five kinds of affliction-abiding is the sense of the perfect teaching.

本迹者，本住中道，障塞二邊，八種障，障涅槃邊，一種障，障生死邊，一種非障生死、非障涅槃，障無餘也。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Rāhula abides in the middle way, blocking off both extremes. Of the various "obstructions" recounted above, eight kinds obstruct the extreme of nirvāṇa, one kind obstructs the extreme of birth-and-death, and one kind neither obstructs birth-and-death nor obstructs nirvāṇa, but obstructs his entry into the nirvāṇa without remainder.

觀心例前可解(云云)。

The contemplation of mind may be understood by analogy with what has come before (and so on).

Reading note: Zhiyi builds this entire biography around a single pun: "覆障," Obstruction, is glossed nine separate times across Rāhula's story, each time isolating a different sense in which something was blocked or covered over — his conception hidden for six years, a slander he unwittingly provoked, the throne he never inherited, his own habit of lying as a novice, and finally his own vow to remain in the world rather than pass into final nirvāṇa. Ru-Yi Meditation Center has kept every occurrence of the word in place rather than varying the translation for elegance, since the accumulation is itself Zhiyi's argument: a name given in blame turns out, read patiently enough, to describe several entirely different kinds of restraint, more than one of which is a virtue rather than a fault.

Concluding the Roll Call: "Known to the Whole Assembly" 六、結：如是等眾所知識

Chinese Source

六、結。「如是等眾所知識」，或言知秖是識，或言聞名為知、見形為識，見形為知、見心為識。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, the conclusion. As for "such great arhats as these, known to the whole assembly": some say that "knowing" and "recognizing" here mean the same thing; others say that hearing a name constitutes "knowing" while seeing the form constitutes "recognizing"; still others say that seeing the form constitutes "knowing" while seeing the mind constitutes "recognizing."

本者，本為眾生作滿字知識，迹為半字知識(云云)。

As for their origin: these great arhats, in origin, serve as spiritual friends to sentient beings through the "full letters" of the complete teaching; in trace they appear as spiritual friends through the "half letters" of the partial teaching — an image, drawn from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, of a child first taught simple half-syllables before whole words, applied here to the lesser teachings as against the complete Mahāyāna (and so on).

觀行知識如止觀。

The contemplative practice of the spiritual friend is as explained in the Mohe Zhiguan.

The Two Thousand in Training and Beyond Training 學無學二千人

Chinese Source

多知識眾竟，次列少知識眾者。「復有學無學二千人俱」，但舉位明數而不歎德，呼此為少知識眾耳。聖與凡絕交，亦不分別多識少識，特以希高慕遠者以多識引之，藏名隱德退讓者以少識引之，隨順眾生故有若干，不可以多少之迹失其本。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having concluded the assembly of the great, well-known disciples, the text next lists the lesser-known assembly. "There were also two thousand more, some still in training and some beyond training" — this merely states their rank and number without praising their virtues, and so this is called the "lesser-known assembly." In truth, sages hold no such distinctions from ordinary beings, nor do they themselves distinguish between the well-known and the lesser-known; it is only that those who admire and aspire to the lofty and the distant are drawn onward by the well-known, while those who conceal their names and hide their virtues in modest reticence are drawn onward by the lesser-known. Following the inclinations of sentient beings in this way, there arise these various distinctions — but one must not, on account of the trace of "many" or "few," lose sight of their common origin.

「學無學」者，三藏中十八種學人，九種無學人；通教五地皆名學，六地名無學，又通教九地名為學，佛地為無學；別圓中，或就功用、無功用，或就具足、未具足，明學無學。《阿含》云：「外道問佛：『羅漢更學不？』佛言：『羅漢不作惡法，住於善法，學其無學即名為學。』」若爾，學人亦稱無學。學人齊其所斷，不復更斷，即是無學，是為四句。就五方便，非學非無學，便是五句。

As for "those in training and those beyond training": in the Tripiṭaka teaching there are eighteen kinds of persons still in training and nine kinds beyond training. In the shared teaching, the first five [common] grounds are all called "in training," while the sixth ground is called "beyond training" — or, alternatively, the ninth ground of the shared teaching is called "in training" and the buddha-ground is called "beyond training." In the distinct and perfect teachings, "in training" and "beyond training" are distinguished either in terms of effortful versus effortless practice, or in terms of incomplete versus complete attainment. The Āgama says: "A follower of another path asked the Buddha: 'Does an arhat still undergo training?' The Buddha answered: 'An arhat commits no evil deed, and abides in what is good; his training in that which requires no further training is itself what may be called "training."'" If this is so, then one who is beyond training may also be called one in training. And one in training, having reached the limit of what is to be cut off for the time being, and not yet cutting off any further, may in that respect be called "beyond training" as well — hence there are these four possible statements. Taking into account as well the five expedient stages that precede stream-entry, which are neither in training nor beyond training, this makes five statements in all.

約四教中例亦應爾，四五二十句。

Applying this pattern across all four teachings likewise, four times five makes twenty statements in all.

本迹者，本法身大士，居滿字學無學位，眾生應以半字學無學人，莊嚴雙樹也。

As for trace and origin: in origin, these are great dharma-body bodhisattvas dwelling in the "full-letter" ranks of training and beyond training; for the sake of sentient beings they appear as the "half-letter" persons in training and beyond training, adorning the twin śāla trees.

觀者正觀中道，不緣二邊，中間即是無學，能如是觀是名為學。若就觀門明數者，觀色心具十法界、十如，界如互論即具二千，舉迹故標本法，迹即是本迹也。

As for contemplation: to contemplate the middle way directly, without dwelling on either extreme — that very middle is "beyond training"; and to be able to contemplate in just this way is called "training." If one accounts for these numbers in terms of the gate of contemplation: to contemplate that form and mind are each endowed with the ten dharma-realms and the ten suchnesses, and that, cross-multiplying realms and suchnesses, this comes to two thousand factors — this is to raise up the trace in order to signify the origin-dharma; the trace is itself the trace of the origin.

Introducing the Nuns' Assembly; Mahāprajāpatī 列尼眾；波闍波提

Chinese Source

次列尼眾者，舊以此例前為二眾，今不用。若例前為多識、少識二眾者，又復無文，義亦不可。但是舉兩眾主，何須苦名為大小、多少耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

The text next lists the assembly of nuns. Some earlier commentators, following the pattern of what came before, treated this as forming two further assemblies; this reading is not adopted here. To follow the earlier pattern and divide this into a well-known assembly and a lesser-known assembly again finds no support in the text, nor does the sense of the passage allow it. This is simply the naming of the heads of two assemblies — why should one insist on forcing them into categories of "great" and "small," "many" and "few"?

先列「波闍波提」，此翻大愛道，亦云憍曇彌，此翻眾主，尼者天竺女人通名也。

The text first lists "Mahāprajāpatī," translated "Great Way of Love," also called "Gautamī," translated "Lord of the Assembly" — "nī" being the general term in India for a woman.

本住智度法門，迹為千佛之母，生育導師。

In origin, Mahāprajāpatī abides in the Dharma-gate of the perfection of wisdom; in trace she becomes the mother of a thousand buddhas, the one who nurtures and raises the Guide.

觀釋者，中觀廣博名大；無緣慈名愛；中理虛通名道。大即自行；愛即化他，如以愛故受生，慈故涉有；道即通自行、化他也。

As for the contemplative explanation: the breadth and vastness of the contemplation of the middle is called "great"; unconditioned loving-kindness is called "love"; the empty and pervasive principle of the middle is called "way." "Great" pertains to practice for oneself; "love" pertains to the transformation of others — just as it is through love that one takes on rebirth, and through loving-kindness that one enters into existence to save beings; "way" pervades both practice for oneself and the transformation of others together.

「六千」者，數也。

"Six thousand" is simply a number [of her retinue].

觀門者，觀六根清淨具千功德，雖眼有八百，耳千二百，以多足少，數滿六千，表本法門，亦是觀行意也。

As for the gate of contemplation: to contemplate that each of the six sense-faculties, when purified, is endowed with a thousand merits — though the eye has only eight hundred and the ear has twelve hundred, the greater numbers making up for the lesser — the total comes to six thousand in full; this signifies the Dharma-gate of the origin, and is likewise the meaning of contemplative practice.

Yaśodharā 耶輸陀羅

Chinese Source

「羅睺羅母耶輸陀羅」者，以子標母，此翻花色，亦曰名聞，或云無翻，溫良恭儉德齊太子，然在家為菩薩之妻、天人知識，出家為尼眾之主、位居無學，豈是無名聞眾耶？《十二遊經》出三夫人：第一、瞿夷，二、耶輸，三、鹿野。《未曾有》及《瑞應》皆云，羅睺是瞿夷子。《涅槃》及《法華》皆云，是耶輸子。二義云何通？或可彼經舉大母，此處舉所生。《釋論》瞿毘陀是寶女不孕。即是瞿夷，此翻明女，故知定是耶輸子也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Rāhula's mother, Yaśodharā" is identified here by her son's name. "Yaśodharā" is translated "Flower-Colored," also rendered "Renowned," or, some say, left untranslated. Gentle, good, reverent, and modest, her virtue matched the crown prince's own; while a laywoman, she was the bodhisattva's wife and a spiritual friend to gods and men, and once gone forth, she became the head of the assembly of nuns, ranked among those beyond training — how, then, could she be reckoned among the unrenowned? The Sūtra of Twelve Wanderings names three consorts: first Gopī, second Yaśodharā, third Mṛgajā. The Sūtra of Wondrous Occurrences and the Sūtra of Auspicious Response both say that Rāhula was Gopī's son; the Nirvāṇa Sūtra and the Lotus Sūtra both say he was Yaśodharā's son. How can these two accounts be reconciled? It may be that those other sūtras name the principal consort as his mother by rank, while the present passage names the one who actually bore him. The Treatise [on the Great Perfection of Wisdom] says that Gopī, the jewel-maiden, was barren — which shows that this same Gopī is elsewhere translated "Bright Woman," and confirms that Rāhula was indeed Yaśodharā's own son.

本迹者，妻則齊也，豈有博地為太子妻？故知本住寂定微妙法喜，迹為佛妻。《悲花》云：「寶藏佛所，誓願為妻耳。」

As for trace and origin: a wife is, by definition, one's equal — how could an ordinary being of the lowest ground be consort to the crown prince? One thus knows that, in origin, Yaśodharā abides in tranquil concentration and subtle, wondrous Dharma-joy; in trace she becomes the Buddha's wife. The Sūtra of Compassionate Flowers says: "In the presence of the Buddha Ratnagarbha, she vowed to become his wife."

觀空無漏法喜，即以鹿野表妻；觀假道種智法喜，即以耶輸表妻；觀中法喜，即以瞿夷表妻。

The Dharma-joy of contemplating emptiness, free of all outflow, is represented by "wife" in the figure of Mṛgajā; the Dharma-joy of contemplating the provisional, the wisdom of the paths, is represented by "wife" in the figure of Yaśodharā; and the Dharma-joy of contemplating the middle is represented by "wife" in the figure of Gopī.

A Summary Reflection: Trace and Origin Across the Whole Assembly 總論本迹

Chinese Source

上當分明本迹、觀心，今更總論，顯善權曲巧，明觀行精微。夫首楞嚴種種示現，稱適根性靡所不為，今且近論，託迹王宮降神聖后，法身菩薩，皆輔佛行化，散影餘家，若三十二瑞，金姿誕應，諸大士各各出生，或空室雨寶，寄辯通夢，若皇皇太子，捨國捐王，踰城學道，諸大士悉從師請業，才藝兼通，為彼宗匠，若法輪初啟甘露門開，聞諸大士化緣未熟，示同不受，分庭抗禮，崇我道真，能化所化全生如乳；若所化緣熟，則素絲易染，池花早開，革凡成聖轉乳成酪；師宗為佛上首弟子，或智慧、神通、辯才、三昧各各第一，共輔法王更度未度，重熟已熟，於方等座席聞菩薩不可思議功德，耻小慕大，耻小則嗚呼自責，失於如來無量知見，慕大則不知當云何得佛無上慧，如轉酪為生蘇；次聞般若摩訶衍門，初歷色心終于種智，含挾小大，出內取與，或共或別或偏或圓，奉命領知而無希取，雖未頓捨已漸通泰，如轉生蘇為熟蘇；次聞《法華》會天性，定父子、授記莂、付大乘，廢三歸一，如餘四味，同一醍醐，不令一人獨得滅度，皆以如來滅度而滅度之。法王、法臣大事出世巧用方便，初用半字法，破二十五有之繁芿，成四枯雙樹利益眾生。次用半滿法，破二乘之獨善，成菩薩之廣大，成四榮雙樹利益聖人。後用常住滿字，破二邊之前後，成非枯非榮佛祕密藏，究竟利益。主將之功畢，大誓之願滿，故身子、目連於《法華》而息化，聖主贖命，斯亦不久，文云：「如我本誓願，今者已滿足，如來不久當入涅槃。」唱滅之言起自於此，二萬燈明迦葉佛等，皆於《法華》究竟，今以師弟皆於此經發迹，內祕菩薩道，外現作聲聞。「我實成佛已來無量億劫」，以此推之，諸大羅漢從法身地，俯影隨緣迹臨萬水，為學無學，作男作女，示道示俗，首楞嚴力靡所不現，方便善權為若此(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Above, the trace and origin, and the contemplation of mind, have been explained one by one for each disciple; here they will be surveyed together as a whole, to display the Buddha's skillful means in their full subtlety and to make plain the fine workings of contemplative practice. The Śūraṅgama power manifests in every conceivable form, matching itself to the capacities of beings, leaving nothing undone; let us here take up only what lies nearest at hand. When the Buddha entrusted his trace to a royal palace and descended into the womb of a sacred queen, these dharma-body bodhisattvas all assisted him in carrying out his transformative teaching, scattering their own reflections into other households as well — as with the thirty-two auspicious signs, the golden form's manifested birth: each of these great beings was likewise born at that time, some appearing amid showers of jeweled rain from an empty chamber, some through eloquence lent in dreams. And when the resplendent crown prince abandoned his kingdom and renounced the throne, crossing the city wall by night to seek the path, all these great beings likewise sought instruction from teachers, mastering every art and accomplishment, and became themselves eminent masters in their own right. Then, when the wheel of the Dharma was first set turning and the gate of the nectar of immortality first opened, the Buddha, perceiving that the karmic conditions ripening these great beings toward transformation were not yet mature, displayed himself as their equal rather than accepting their homage, standing on the same footing with them so as to honor the truth of his own path — thus both the one who transforms and those transformed were, at that stage, entirely as raw milk. When the karmic conditions of those to be transformed had at last ripened, then — as raw silk takes dye easily, and the lotus in the pond opens early — the ordinary was transformed into the sagely, and the milk was turned into curds; the masters of these teachers-turned-disciples became the Buddha's foremost disciples, each foremost in his own right in wisdom, spiritual powers, eloquence, or samādhi, together assisting the Dharma-king in delivering those not yet delivered, and in ripening further those already ripened once. At the Vaipulya assemblies, hearing of the bodhisattvas' inconceivable merits, they grew ashamed of the lesser and aspired to the greater — ashamed of the lesser, they lamented and reproached themselves for having lost the Tathāgata's boundless knowledge and vision; aspiring to the greater, they did not yet know how they might attain the Buddha's unsurpassed wisdom — this was the turning of curds into fresh butter. Next, hearing the gate of the Great Vehicle in the Prajñāpāramitā, ranging at first through form and mind and reaching at last the wisdom of all modes, embracing both the lesser and the greater, going out and coming in, taking and giving — whether shared or distinct, partial or perfect — they received and comprehended this teaching under instruction, without any further grasping of their own; though not yet suddenly relinquishing everything, they had already gradually attained a thoroughgoing ease — this was the turning of fresh butter into refined butter. Next, hearing the Lotus Sūtra, the heavenly nature was united, father and child were confirmed as one, the prediction of buddhahood was bestowed, and the Great Vehicle was entrusted to them; the three vehicles were set aside and merged into the one, just as the remaining four flavors are alike united in the single flavor of ghee — no one was to attain extinction alone, but all were to be brought to extinction by means of the Tathāgata's own extinction. The Dharma-king and his Dharma-ministers, in the great matter of their appearance in the world, skillfully employed expedient means: at first they used the "half-letter" teaching to break apart the tangled thicket of the twenty-five states of existence, forming the four withered trees for the benefit of ordinary beings; next they used the teaching that joins "half-letters" and "full-letters," breaking through the solitary self-benefit of the two vehicles and bringing to completion the vast greatness of the bodhisattva, forming the four flourishing trees for the benefit of the sagely; and finally they used the eternally abiding "full-letter" teaching, breaking through the sequence of the two extremes, bringing to completion the Buddha's secret treasury that is neither withered nor flourishing, the ultimate benefit of all. Once the labors of these commanders-in-chief were complete and the vows of their great oath fulfilled, Śāriputra and Maudgalyāyana therefore ceased their transformative activity within the Lotus Sūtra itself; the sagely lord's own ransomed life, too, would not be long thereafter. The text says: "As I originally vowed, so it is now fulfilled; the Tathāgata will, before long, enter nirvāṇa" — the proclamation of his coming extinction arises from precisely this. The twenty thousand buddhas named Candrasūryapradīpa, the Buddha Kāśyapa, and others besides — all reached their culmination within their own Lotus Sūtras; and in the present case, both teacher and disciples alike put forth their traces within this very sūtra, secretly holding the bodhisattva path within while outwardly manifesting as śrāvakas. "In truth I attained buddhahood immeasurable koṭis of kalpas ago" — reasoning from this, one sees that all these great arhats, proceeding from the ground of the dharma-body, cast down their reflections in accordance with conditions, manifesting their traces across the ten thousand streams — becoming those in training or beyond training, appearing as men or as women, displaying the sagely or the worldly — there is nothing the power of the Śūraṅgama does not manifest; such is the nature of skillful means (and so on).

Ru-Yi Translation

To speak generally of contemplation: everything the teacher and his disciples have done above in carrying out the dharma-body's transformative work is of no benefit to the practitioner unless it is turned into the expedient means of contemplation — like a poor man counting another's treasure, or a blind man holding a candle. Now, the mental factors are exceedingly numerous, but let us confine ourselves here to the wholesome factors, taking the disciples as an example, mentioning only ten in number. The ten wholesome factors are: faith, diligence, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom, joy, pliancy, equanimity, discernment, and morality. These ten factors assist the sovereign mind, able to transform evil into good, to change the ordinary into the sagely, and to accomplish every Dharma-gate; taking these ten mental states as the foundation is like the ten disciples assisting the Buddha in carrying out his transformative teaching, jointly ripening sentient beings and establishing the Buddha-dharma. The factor of faith corresponds to Aniruddha, foremost in the divine eye — the eye being chief among the five sense-faculties, just as every direction takes the east as its head, so faith is the very first of the mental factors by which one enters the Buddha-dharma. The factor of diligence corresponds to Kāśyapa, foremost in the dhūta austerities — the vigorous exertion of shaking off defilements corresponds to the factor of diligence. The factor of mindfulness corresponds to Upāli, foremost in upholding the vinaya — the power of mindfulness is firm and strong, retaining what is recollected without forgetting. The factor of concentration corresponds to Maudgalyāyana, foremost in spiritual powers; the factor of wisdom corresponds to Śāriputra, foremost in wisdom — both of these are self-evident. The factor of joy corresponds to Ānanda, foremost in learning — one of vast learning takes joy in discernment, and joy is itself this factor of joy. The factor of pliancy corresponds to Kātyāyana, whose debates fathomed the subtlest points and exhausted every principle, dispelling error and revealing the truth — just as pliancy departs from evil and attains the good, releasing suffering and entering into ease. The factor of equanimity corresponds to Subhūti, foremost in the understanding of emptiness — one who abides in the equality of emptiness corresponds to the factor of equanimity. The factor of discernment corresponds to Pūrṇa, foremost in teaching the Dharma — discernment is the root of speech, and once this root is established, eloquent exposition becomes inexhaustible. The factor of morality corresponds to Rāhula, foremost in upholding the precepts — this is self-evident. The ten factors, assisting the sovereign mind, are able to accomplish contemplative practice; entering deeply into the wholesome dharmas within a single thought, the Three Jewels stand complete: the sovereign mind is itself the Buddha-jewel; the factors are the Saṅgha-jewel; and their common object, ultimate reality — where there is no sovereign mind and no factors at all — is the Dharma-jewel. Once one has entered ultimate reality, the merit and function of the sovereign mind and its factors are complete and sufficient.

又取通大地十數與心王俱起，入善、入惡遍通一切，謂：想、欲、觸、慧、念、思、解脫、憶、定、受也。想對富樓那，想得假名，其人善達假名，辯才無滯。欲對迦葉，迦葉無世間欲而欲於無為。觸對旃延，觸入二事更相涉入，旃延善論義，能窮往復。慧即身子，可解。念對波離，念持律之上也。思對羅云，思是行陰，此人實行持戒也。解脫對善吉，脫名無累，此人解空於有得脫。憶對那律，憶動發取境修天眼，易三摩提。定數對目連，可解。受對阿難，多聞領持無謬也。十人各備眾德，為引專門，宣示佛道，隨眾生欲。欲慧者師身子，乃至欲多聞者師阿難，共輔法王，各掌一職。

Again, taking up the ten factors of the great mental ground shared by every mental state, which arise together with the sovereign mind and pervade every wholesome and unwholesome state alike — namely: perception, intention, contact, wisdom, mindfulness, volition, liberation, recollection, concentration, and feeling. Perception corresponds to Pūrṇa: perception grasps conventional designations, and this man was well versed in conventional designations, his eloquence unobstructed. Intention corresponds to Kāśyapa: Kāśyapa had no worldly desire, yet he had, so to speak, an intention toward the unconditioned. Contact corresponds to Kātyāyana: contact is the mutual interpenetration of two things, and Kātyāyana excelled in debate, able to trace every argument back and forth to its conclusion. Wisdom corresponds simply to Śāriputra, which is self-evident. Mindfulness corresponds to Upāli, the very height of mindful upholding of the vinaya. Volition corresponds to Rāhula: volition is the aggregate of formations, and this man truly put his precept-keeping into actual practice. Liberation corresponds to Subhūti: liberation means being free of entanglement, and this man, understanding emptiness, attained liberation within existence itself. Recollection corresponds to Aniruddha: recollection stirs and draws forth its object, cultivating the divine eye, and readily attains samādhi. The factor of concentration corresponds to Maudgalyāyana, which is self-evident. Feeling corresponds to Ānanda: his vast learning received and retained everything without error. Each of these ten men was fully endowed with every virtue, yet each is singled out here for the one specialty by which he proclaimed the Buddha's path, according to what beings desired: those who desired wisdom took Śāriputra as their teacher, and so on up to those who desired vast learning, who took Ānanda as their teacher — together they assisted the Dharma-king, each holding his own office.

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, the contemplation of mind is just the same: within every single thought, the sovereign mind and its factors are both fully present; to accomplish contemplation, the sovereign mind and its factors support one another in order to attain awakening — one may enter the path through the factor of perception, or enter through the factor of intention, whichever is most suited; the sovereign mind and its factors work upon this together, transforming and taking hold of every mind mired in the dust of affliction, and thereby carrying out the Buddha's work. At the stage of ghee, the sovereign mind and its factors are both brought to completion, and thought after thought, no further mental factors operate — hence this is called "practicing the perfection of wisdom." The Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra says: "Contemplating the mind, one finds no mind; dharmas do not abide in dharmas; my own mind is of itself empty, and offense and merit have no possessor." This — being without mind and without mental factors — is called correct contemplation. If the dust of affliction in the mind and its factors is not yet exhausted, contemplation is not yet complete; hence the sūtra says: "Until sentient beings are delivered, I myself will not attain perfect awakening" — this is precisely the meaning intended here (and so on).

Chinese Source

第二列菩薩眾者，《釋論》云：「菩薩為出家、在家四眾攝，何故別列？答：『有菩薩墮四眾中，有四眾不墮菩薩中，為其不發心作佛，故今別列。』」同發心求作佛者名菩薩眾。文為六：一、氣類；二、大數；三、階位；四、歎德；五、列名；六、結句。

The text next lists the second assembly, that of the bodhisattvas. The Treatise [on the Great Perfection of Wisdom] says: "Bodhisattvas are already included among the fourfold assembly of monastics and laypeople — why, then, are they listed separately? "Bodhi" means "the path"; "sattva" means "mind"; "mahā" means "great." All such persons seek the vast and expansive path, and, moreover, bring sentient beings to maturity — hence they are of the kind possessing "the mind set on the path" and "the mind set on the great path." Bodhisattvas are of many kinds, corresponding to the partial, shared, distinct, and perfect teachings. As when the Treatise cites Kātyāyanīputra's account of the six perfections fulfilled up to a fixed limit — this is meant to temper the blood of sentient beings into milk. Or as the Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra explains, when there are bodhisattvas who, upon first arousing the resolve, immediately correspond with omniscience — this is meant to temper milk into curds. As for trace and origin: the origin-ground of these bodhisattvas is difficult to fathom — some dwell at the stage of equal enlightenment, some stand equal to the Dharma-king himself; just as, when Sudhana entered the Dharma-realm, he beheld Mañjuśrī's form as boundless and his Dharma-teachings as profound and far-reaching — in origin these bodhisattvas are neighbors to the buddhas themselves, and in trace they come to assist Śākyamuni, appearing as bodhisattvas, manifesting their bodies everywhere through the power of samādhi, scattering their reflections and lowering their countenances, expounding at length through the inconceivable transformative power of the wheel of speech as circumstances require — this can be known in the mind, but cannot be fully expressed in words. Hence, in trace, they draw beings through the four flavors toward the single true reality — like the hammer and anvil that shape all manner of raw ore into a single form; once ghee is achieved, the work of this particular age of transformation comes to rest. Yet how could their origin-ground's ultimate accomplishment have only begun with entering the Great Vehicle today? Nor is it the case that they were first illuminated, as the highest peak is first touched by the rising sun, at the site of quiescent enlightenment — whether sudden or gradual, all of this is merely what their trace has done.

"All irreversible from unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening" clarifies their rank. The Avataṃsaka Sūtra, however, explains that at the first of the ten abodes one attains the Tathāgata's single body and his immeasurable bodies, complete with all three kinds of non-retrogression — this is non-retrogression according to the perfect teaching, and this is the single true reality; it is this reading that is used here to determine their rank.

As for trace and origin: in their origin-ground of quiescent extinction, these bodhisattvas are not even properly spoken of as being at the ten grounds — how much less at the first abode? Not even properly at the non-retrogression of the first abode — how much less within the distinct or shared teachings? The ranks of the distinct and shared teachings are appropriate for interpreting the assemblies listed in other sūtras; the rank of the perfect teaching belongs properly to the present sūtra alone. If the various sūtra-commentators have failed even to recognize the trace, how could they possibly know the origin? Once their praise has gone astray in this way, disparagement lies hidden within it as well, amounting to the two faults of both adding to and subtracting from the truth at once — how, then, could this be called a true praising of virtue?

觀心者，三觀即三不退，又一心三觀，即一心三不退(云云)。舊云：「皆得陀羅尼」去，始是歎德。今取不退轉即具兩意，成上屬明位，起後屬歎德。舊云：「歎德作十二句，分為四意：初三句歎現德，次三句歎往行，次四句歎內體，後兩句歎外名，四意不同而德居於初，故稱歎德。歎現又兩：初一句歎自行，後二句歎化他。歎行為三：初句歎行本，本從諸佛得般若；次句歎本行，行福德也，既有福德能資於慧；次句為佛所歎。體又三：初慈悲歎應身，中間兩句歎心慧報身，後一句歎法身。歎名為二：初句歎名普聞，次句歎能度眾生。」此之分文極有眉眼，覈論宗體，殊無趣向。若歎通教，通教無三身，又非入佛慧，名不普聞，種種義不成。若歎別教，別教初地已過二乘，云何七地更起聲聞、支佛之念？若歎圓教，不應言七地已下無不退之德，進退無當，竟知歎誰？是所不用。

As for the contemplation of mind: the threefold contemplation is itself the threefold non-retrogression, and, further, the threefold contemplation within a single mind is itself threefold non-retrogression within a single mind (and so on). Earlier commentators held that the praise of their virtues begins only from the phrase "they had all attained dhāraṇī" onward. Here, however, "irreversible" is taken to carry both senses at once: read with what precedes, it belongs to the clarification of their rank; read as the start of what follows, it belongs to the praise of their virtues. Earlier commentators say: "The praise of virtue forms twelve phrases, divided into four groups of meaning: the first three phrases praise their present virtue; the next three praise their past practice; the next four praise their inner substance; and the last two praise their outward renown. Though these four groups differ, virtue stands foremost among them, and so this whole passage is called 'the praise of virtue.' The praise of their present virtue further divides in two: the first phrase praises their own practice, the next two praise their transformation of others. The praise of their practice forms three: the first phrase praises the root of their practice, this root being the prajñā they obtained from the buddhas; the next phrase praises the practice rooted in this, namely the practice of merit — for, having merit, one is thereby able to nourish wisdom; the next phrase concerns their being praised by the buddhas. The praise of their substance likewise forms three: the first praises the response-body through compassion; the middle two phrases praise the recompense-body of mind and wisdom; the last phrase praises the dharma-body. The praise of their renown forms two: the first phrase praises the universal spread of their fame, the next praises their ability to deliver sentient beings." This division of the text is, on its face, very neatly reasoned — but examined against the actual substance of the teaching, it points nowhere in particular. If this is taken as praise according to the shared teaching, the shared teaching has no three bodies, nor does it enter the Buddha's wisdom, so "renown not universally spread" makes no sense, and every such point fails to hold together. If taken as praise according to the distinct teaching, in the distinct teaching the first ground has already surpassed the two lesser vehicles — how, then, could thoughts of the śrāvaka or the pratyekabuddha still arise at the seventh ground? And if taken as praise according to the perfect teaching, it ought not to be said that there is no virtue of non-retrogression below the seventh ground — in either direction, the reading fits nowhere. In the end, whom is this praise even meant to describe? Hence this scheme is not adopted here.

The Vertical Reading: Thirteen Phrases Mapped to the Ten Grounds 竪消文：十三句對十地

Chinese Source

今以十三句作橫竪消文，一、竪，約十地義便；二、橫，約初住義便。「不退轉」者成前，即是明位，起後即是歎德，以對初地。初地名歡喜，喜其不退墮二邊，入中道獲三不退，故知歎初歡喜地也。「皆得陀羅尼」歎二地，二地名離垢，亦名離達，離遮諸惡、達持眾善，即陀羅尼義，故知歎離垢地也。「樂說辯才」歎三地，三地名明地，內智明、外說辯，欲知智在說，說有種種樂，說最勝故，故知歎第三明地也。「轉不退轉法輪」歎四地，四地名焰，焰能破闇，又能焦炷，轉法輪自害己惑如焦炷，破他迷如除闇，故知歎第四焰地也。「供養百千諸佛」歎五地，五地名難勝地，此地得深禪定，用神通力難勝難及，於一念頃遍至十方供養諸佛，故知歎第五地也。「於諸佛所植眾德本」歎六地，六地名現前，由得禪能供養諸佛，福資種智，種智現前，智是德本，如植種於地，故知歎第六地也。「常為諸佛之所稱歎」歎第七遠行地，此地二智方便出過一切，廣修利益稱會佛心，故知歎第七地。「以慈修身」歎第八不動地，正智不動不出三界，但以慈董身應入五道，董口為說法，董心為設方便，正法華具董三業，故知歎第八地也。「善入佛慧」歎第九地，九地名善慧，深入實際妙徹本源，此名義最合，故知歎第九地。「通達大智」歎第十地，十地名法雲，法身如虛空，禪定如大雲，智慧如大雨，善入佛法名慧，巧用佛法名智，互舉耳。「到於彼岸」歎十地內德，到三諦之彼岸因中說果，又到在不久也。「名稱普聞」歎十地外德，由內德深廣，致令聲名普聞，內外相稱。若開等覺位者，此二句擬之。「能度百千眾生」者，餘地度人，或一界至九界，不名能度，十地勝前故稱能度。諸地悉具眾功德，而今出沒釋者，為人情好異故，依十地名便故，又豎義易解故，作此一途消文耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

The present thirteen phrases of praise may now be read both vertically and horizontally: first, vertically, matching them conveniently to the ten grounds; second, horizontally, matching them conveniently to the first abode. "Irreversible" completes what precedes — that is, it clarifies their rank — and, as the start of what follows, belongs to the praise of their virtue, corresponding to the first ground. The first ground is named "Joy," rejoicing that one no longer retrogresses into either extreme, having entered the middle way and attained the threefold non-retrogression — hence one knows this phrase praises the first ground, the Ground of Joy. "They had all attained dhāraṇī" praises the second ground. The second ground is named "Free of Defilement," also "Freed and Comprehending" — freed from every obstruction of evil, and comprehending and upholding every form of good: this is precisely the meaning of dhāraṇī — hence one knows this praises the Ground of Freedom from Defilement. "They delighted in eloquent speech" praises the third ground. The third ground is named "Illumination" — wisdom illumined within, eloquence expressed without; wishing to know where wisdom resides, look to its speech, and speech has many kinds of delight, this being the most excellent form of speech — hence one knows this praises the third ground, the Ground of Illumination. "They turned the wheel of the Dharma that never turns back" praises the fourth ground. The fourth ground is named "Blazing" — the blaze is able to break through darkness, and also able to scorch the wick; turning the wheel of the Dharma harms one's own remaining delusions as the flame scorches the wick, and breaks through others' confusion as it dispels darkness — hence one knows this praises the fourth ground, the Ground of the Blazing Flame. "They had made offerings to immeasurable hundreds of thousands of buddhas" praises the fifth ground. The fifth ground is named "Difficult to Surpass" — at this ground one attains profound meditative concentration, and, by the power of spiritual penetration difficult to surpass and difficult to equal, one is able within a single thought-moment to reach every direction and make offerings to the buddhas — hence one knows this praises the fifth ground. "They planted every root of merit in the presence of those buddhas" praises the sixth ground. The sixth ground is named "Manifestation" — through attaining meditative concentration one is able to make offerings to the buddhas, and merit nourishes the wisdom of all modes, which then manifests before one; wisdom is the root of merit, as a seed planted in the ground — hence one knows this praises the sixth ground. "They were constantly praised by them" praises the seventh ground, the Ground of Going Far. At this ground the twofold wisdom and its expedient means surpass all that came before, broadly cultivating benefit in accord with the Buddha's own mind — hence one knows this praises the seventh ground. "They cultivated their own bodies with loving-kindness" praises the eighth ground, the Ground of Immovability. True wisdom, unmoving, does not depart from the three realms, but through loving-kindness alone governs the body so as to respond within the five destinies, governs speech in order to teach the Dharma, and governs mind in order to establish expedient means — the Orthodox Lotus Sūtra [an earlier translation of this sūtra] fully states this governance of the three modes of karma — hence one knows this praises the eighth ground. "They had entered well into the Buddha's wisdom" praises the ninth ground. The ninth ground is named "Excellent Wisdom" — penetrating deeply into ultimate reality, reaching subtly through to its very source; this name and its meaning correspond most closely of all — hence one knows this praises the ninth ground. "They had penetrated great wisdom" praises the tenth ground. The tenth ground is named "Dharma Cloud" — the dharma-body is like empty space, meditative concentration is like the great cloud, and wisdom is like the great rain; entering skillfully into the Buddha-dharma is called "wisdom," while employing the Buddha-dharma skillfully is called "great wisdom" — the two terms are used interchangeably here. "They had reached its far shore" praises the inward virtue of the tenth ground — reaching the far shore of the three truths, speaking of the fruit already present within the cause; it also means that their arrival there is not long delayed. "Their renown had spread through immeasurable worlds" praises the outward virtue of the tenth ground — it is because their inward virtue is so deep and vast that their fame spreads universally, inward and outward corresponding to one another; and if the stage of equal enlightenment is to be accounted for separately, these two phrases would apply to it as well. As for "they were able to bring countless beings across": at the other grounds, one delivers beings — perhaps from the first dharma-realm up through the ninth — without yet being called "able to bring across," but the tenth ground surpasses what came before, and so is called "able to bring across." Every ground is in fact fully endowed with the whole range of merits, and the reason these are singled out and matched here in this fashion is simply that human sentiment favors variety, that following the names of the ten grounds is convenient, and that the vertical reading is easier to grasp — this is the one approach taken in unpacking the text here.

Reading note: This thirteen-phrase passage of praise is one of the most technically dense stretches in the fascicle, and Zhiyi works through it twice — once "vertically," assigning each phrase to one of the ten bodhisattva grounds in sequence, and once "horizontally" in the section that follows, reading all thirteen phrases as different facets of a single stage, the first of the ten abodes. His own conclusion, stated plainly at the end of this section, is that neither reading is more correct than the other; the vertical reading is offered only because it is easier to follow. Ru-Yi Meditation Center has translated both readings in full rather than summarizing the second as a repetition of the first, since Zhiyi's underlying point — that the perfect teaching's first abode already contains every virtue elsewhere spread across ten stages — is only visible if both accounts are read side by side.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, as for the horizontal reading: this speaks directly in terms of the first abode, and every other rank may be understood by the same pattern. At the first abode of the arousal of the resolve, one arousal is an arousal of all — passing beyond both extremes, transforming the ordinary and surpassing the sagely, entering the middle way; the mind is quiescent and extinguished, flowing thought after thought into the ocean of omniscience — hence it is said they attained irreversibility. At the first abode one screens off and departs from the obstructions of grasping at characteristics, ignorance, and fundamental delusion, upholding and penetrating the virtues of prajñā, liberation, and the dharma-body — hence it is said they attained dhāraṇī. Even the semblance understanding of the ten degrees of faith is already able to fill the trichiliocosm with a wondrous voice — how much more so, then, the first abode? The true understanding and merit of the secret of speech is meant by "they delighted in eloquent speech." At the first abode one is able to divide one's body and become a buddha in a hundred worlds — and in truth this reaches immeasurable, boundless worlds; since one is able to become a buddha, teaching the Dharma and transforming beings, this is what is meant by "they were able to turn the wheel of the Dharma that never turns back." At the first abode one attains inconceivable spiritual power, able to serve every buddha throughout the dharma-realm — hence "they made offerings to immeasurable hundreds of thousands of buddhas." At the first abode one attains the root of true reality, able to plant every root of merit. At the first abode one opens the Buddha's knowledge and vision, and, this knowledge and vision now being one with that of every buddha, one is thereby praised by the buddhas. At the first abode, unconditioned loving-kindness manifests one's body everywhere, responding throughout the entire dharma-realm — hence "they cultivated their own bodies with loving-kindness." At the first abode one enters the secret treasury — hence "they had entered well into the Buddha's wisdom." At the first abode the threefold wisdom within a single mind meets with no obstruction whatsoever — hence "they had penetrated great wisdom." At the first abode phenomena and principle are, in their respective portions, already ultimate — hence "they had reached the far shore." At the first abode the perfect virtue is genuinely true, matching its very name — hence "their renown had spread through immeasurable worlds." At the first abode one is able to serve as the support and refuge of the ten dharma-realms, establishing and rescuing beings — hence "they were able to bring countless hundreds of thousands of beings across." The first abode possesses, beyond this, immeasurable, boundless, and inconceivable further merits of every kind, of which only these thirteen phrases have been stated in outline. From the second abode onward, up through the stage of equal enlightenment, it is likewise so in every case. Hence the Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra says: "The gate of the initial letter A is already endowed with the merit of all forty-one letters; the final letter TA is likewise endowed with the merit of every letter; and it is the same in every letter between — the letters are equal, the words are equal, and the merits are equal as well."

Four Questions and Answers on This Exegesis 問答四番

Chinese Source

問：此中歎斷惑德，三藏不斷惑，可不被歎；聲聞尚被歎，迹為通、別，何不歎德？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: If this passage praises the virtue of having cut off delusion, then the Tripiṭaka teaching, which does not cut off delusion in this sense, ought not to be praised by it — yet even the śrāvakas are praised here; if their trace belongs to the shared or distinct teaching, why is their virtue not praised accordingly?

Answer: To praise them in a way that corresponds to their trace would indeed carry this sense — but the present sūtra is concerned precisely with praising those of the perfect teaching, and does not praise them according to the expedient means of their trace.

問：云何諸句功德皆歎初住耶？

Question: Why, then, are all these phrases of merit taken to praise the first abode alone?

答曰：餘位亦如是，何獨初住。舊云「八地有諸功德」，不以為疑；今圓歎初住，何德不攝？初住尚爾，何況後位耶？《法華論》云：「上支、下支門，總相、別相應知。」初得不退轉一句是總。此不退有十種示現：聞法不退轉，即是陀羅尼；樂說不退轉，即是樂說辯才；說不退轉，即是轉不退法輪；依善知識不退轉，即是供養百千諸佛植眾德本；斷疑不退轉，即是為諸佛稱歎；入事不退轉，即是以慈修身；入一切智如實境不退轉，即是善入佛慧；依我空法空不退轉，即是通達大智；入如實境不退轉，即是到於彼岸；應作所作不退轉，即是度百千眾生。故初總句即是上支，次諸別句即是下支。記中橫歎初住德，即與此意同也。論云：「二者攝取事門者，示現諸菩薩住何等清淨地中，因何等方便、何等境界、何等應作所作故。」若從此義，作豎歎菩薩德亦無妨。

Answer: Every other rank is just the same — why should this apply to the first abode alone? Earlier commentators held that "the eighth ground possesses these merits," without any hesitation in saying so; here, when the perfect teaching's praise is applied to the first abode, what merit is not already subsumed within it? If this holds even at the first abode, how much more so at every later rank? The Fahua Lun [the Treatise on the Lotus Sūtra] says: "One should understand the 'upper branch' and 'lower branch' gates, the general characteristic and the particular characteristics." The single opening phrase, "they had attained irreversibility," is the general statement. This non-retrogression is manifested in ten particular forms: non-retrogression in hearing the Dharma, which is dhāraṇī; non-retrogression in delighting to speak, which is delighting in eloquent speech; non-retrogression in preaching, which is turning the wheel of the Dharma that never turns back; non-retrogression in relying on good spiritual friends, which is making offerings to immeasurable hundreds of thousands of buddhas and planting every root of merit; non-retrogression in the severing of doubt, which is being praised by the buddhas; non-retrogression in entering into phenomenal activity, which is cultivating one's own body with loving-kindness; non-retrogression in entering the true object of omniscience, which is entering well into the Buddha's wisdom; non-retrogression relying on the emptiness of self and of dharmas, which is penetrating great wisdom; non-retrogression in entering the true object itself, which is reaching the far shore; and non-retrogression in what ought to be done being done, which is bringing countless hundreds of thousands of beings across. Hence the first, general phrase is the "upper branch," and the several particular phrases that follow are the "lower branch." That the [Fahua] Ji [commentary] gives this horizontal praise of the virtue of the first abode accords with just this same meaning. The Treatise also says: "Second, as for the gate of comprehending the matter: this manifests in which pure ground the various bodhisattvas dwell, by what expedient means, within what objective sphere, and what they ought to do and accomplish there." If one follows this sense, then to give the vertical praise of the bodhisattvas' virtue presents no difficulty either.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the contemplative explanation of this praise of virtue: "irreversible" is as already explained above. As for "dhāraṇī": the contemplation of emptiness is the revolving dhāraṇī; the contemplation of the provisional is the dhāraṇī of a hundred thousand revolutions; the contemplation of the middle is the dhāraṇī of the Dharma-sound and its expedient means. Further: when the contemplation of emptiness contemplates that mind has only a name, this is the dhāraṇī of retaining what is heard; when the contemplation of the provisional contemplates that mind gives rise to immeasurable minds, and that every mind and mental factor is itself a Dharma-gate, this is the dhāraṇī of retaining what is practiced; when the contemplation of the middle contemplates that mind is itself true reality, this is the dhāraṇī of retaining the meaning. When the contemplation of the provisional contemplates that mind is endowed with the dharmas of the ten dharma-realms, this is unhindered eloquence in the Dharma; when the contemplation of the middle contemplates that the ten dharma-realms all enter into true reality, this is unhindered eloquence in meaning; when the contemplation of emptiness contemplates that the ten dharma-realms have only names and words, this is unhindered eloquence in language. To contemplate that the one mind is the three minds, and the three minds are the one mind, that one realm is every realm, revolving without any obstruction whatsoever — this is unhindered eloquence in joyful exposition. The contemplation of emptiness is the turning of the wheel of the Dharma that never retrogresses in rank; the contemplation of the provisional is the turning of the wheel that never retrogresses in practice; the contemplation of the middle is the turning of the wheel that never retrogresses in mindfulness. "Making offerings to the buddhas" means simply according with the Buddha's own words; now, to accord with the Buddha's teaching and cultivate the threefold contemplation of mind is itself making offerings to the Buddha; to do so for the sake of breaking through the five kinds of affliction-abiding and attaining liberation is making offerings to the Dharma; and the harmony of the principle of the three truths is making offerings to the Saṅgha. Again: the mind of the manifold practices nourishing the mind of contemplative wisdom is making offerings to the Buddha; the mind of contemplative wisdom opening forth its objective sphere is making offerings to the Dharma; and the harmony of the mind of the object and the mind of wisdom is making offerings to the Saṅgha. The mind of true reality is the root of the mind of contemplative wisdom, and the mind of contemplative wisdom is the root of the mind of the manifold practices; once this root is attained and planted, it becomes firmly established — hence "they planted every root of merit." The mind of contemplative wisdom merges with its objective sphere, and that objective sphere seals itself upon contemplative wisdom in turn; wisdom, illuminating its object, is constantly united with it — this is what is meant by "being praised by the buddhas." The contemplation of emptiness is suffused by loving-kindness conditioned upon dharmas; the contemplation of the provisional is suffused by loving-kindness conditioned upon sentient beings; the contemplation of the middle is suffused by unconditioned loving-kindness. The contemplation of emptiness enters the Buddha-wisdom of the shared teaching; the contemplation of the provisional enters the Buddha-wisdom of the distinct teaching; the contemplation of the middle enters the Buddha-wisdom of the perfect teaching. The contemplation of emptiness reaches the far shore of the wisdom of all things; the contemplation of the provisional reaches the far shore of the wisdom of the paths; the contemplation of the middle reaches the far shore of the wisdom of all modes. The contemplation of emptiness hears the ultimate truth; the contemplation of the provisional hears the conventional truth; the contemplation of the middle hears universally the ultimate truth of the middle way, and likewise hears universally all three truths at once. The contemplation of emptiness delivers the hundreds of thousands of beings caught in the four kinds of affliction-abiding; the contemplation of the provisional delivers the hundreds of thousands of beings caught in delusions numerous as dust and sand; the contemplation of the middle delivers the hundreds of thousands of beings caught in fundamental ignorance. The threefold contemplation within a single mind possesses immeasurable virtues, which no praise could ever exhaust — this account has only touched upon them in brief outline.

As for the contemplation of mind: the principle of the nature of mind, the secret of the three virtues, neither vertical nor horizontal, yet perfectly united — this is why he is named Wondrous Virtue.

The Miscellaneous Assembly 雜眾

Chinese Source

第三列雜眾者，舊云凡夫眾，此中有聖；舊云俗眾，此中有道；舊云天人眾，此中有龍鬼；皆不便。今呼為雜眾，意則兼矣，所謂五道、二界、八番，是故言雜。方等經亦列地獄，《中陰經》亦化無色，此皆隨機適現，不可一例作並，復不可定其次第。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the third listing, that of "the miscellaneous assembly": the old commentators called it "the assembly of ordinary beings," yet among them are the noble ones; they called it "the assembly of laypeople," yet among them are those of the path; they called it "the assembly of gods and humans," yet among them are dragons and spirits — none of these names fit well. Now I call it "the miscellaneous assembly," a designation that comprehends them all: what is meant is the five destinies, the two realms, and the eight groupings — and it is for this reason that it is called "miscellaneous." The Vaipulya sūtras likewise list the hell-realm among the assembly, and the Sūtra of the Intermediate State likewise brings forth transformation-born beings of the formless realm; all of this manifests appropriately according to the capacity of the assembly in question, and cannot be forced into a single uniform pattern, nor can any fixed sequence among them be established.

舊云人是土主，讓諸客在前，《無量義經》秖與此經同席，明國王、國臣，國士、國女，不論賓主相讓。出經家趣列在文，或有別意未詳。今觀此文有八番，先標帝釋，次列四王，前龍後鳥，鬼神重出，為此義故呼為雜眾，不可言其次第。

The old commentators said that human beings, being the lords of this land, yield precedence to their guests and so are listed first; yet the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings shares the very same seating with this sūtra, and in it kings, ministers, gentlemen, and gentlewomen of the realm are named without regard to any yielding of precedence between host and guest. The compiler of this sūtra simply followed his own inclination in arranging the text, and there may be some further intention behind it that remains unclear. Examining this text now, I find eight groupings: first Śakra is set forth, then the Four Kings are listed, then the dragons before the birds, with the spirits and demons appearing more than once — it is for this reason that I call it "the miscellaneous assembly," and no fixed sequence among them can be asserted.

又雜眾者，此中有得道、未得道者，雜果報與形服雜，故言雜。其中得二乘道者，無漏智與無明煩惱雜，故言雜；其中得菩薩道者，漚和與眾機雜，故言雜；其中得佛道者，一法具一切法，故言雜。雜義如是，豈可以凡夫形俗判之，復不可以五道人天等判之，故言雜也。此是約教釋(云云)。

Furthermore, as "the miscellaneous assembly": among them are those who have attained the path and those who have not yet attained it, their rewards and their outward forms and garments all intermingled — hence it is called "miscellaneous." Among them, those who have attained the path of the two vehicles have unafflicted wisdom intermingled with the afflictions of ignorance — hence it is called "miscellaneous"; those who have attained the bodhisattva path have skillful means intermingled with the capacities of the many beings they teach — hence it is called "miscellaneous"; and those who have attained buddhahood have the single dharma endowed with the whole of every dharma — hence it, too, is called "miscellaneous." Such is the meaning of "miscellaneous" — how, then, could one judge it merely by the outward forms of ordinary, worldly beings, or fix it merely according to the five destinies of gods and humans? Hence it is called "miscellaneous." This is the interpretation in terms of the teachings (and so on).

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "Śakra Devānām Indra": this renders Indra, also transliterated Śatakratu, and is translated "the One Able to Act," the lord of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven. "Trāyastriṃśa" is translated "the Thirty-Three": on its four sides there are eight cities each, and together with the city of Sudarśana at the center they total thirty-three, all dwelling together upon the summit of Mount Sumeru. "Sumeru" is translated "Well-Illumined," formed of the four precious substances, three million three hundred and sixty thousand li in height and breadth; he is the lord of the desire-realm gods, and hence is listed first. The Saṃyukta Āgama, fascicle forty, says: "A certain bhikṣu asked the Buddha: 'Why is he named Śakra Devānām Indra?' The Buddha answered: 'When he was formerly a human being, he practiced sudden and complete giving, and was thereby capable of becoming a lord — hence he is named Śakra Devānām Indra.' 'Why is he named Puraṃdara?' 'Because when he was a human being he practiced giving again and again.' 'Why is he named Maghavan?' 'Because that was his name when he was formerly a human being.' 'Why is he named Śabala?' 'Because when he was formerly a human being he gave away his own robe in charity.' 'Why is he named Kauśika?' 'Because that was his clan-name when he was formerly a human being.' 'Why is he named Śacīpati?' 'Śacī is his wife, and pati means husband.' 'Why is he named Sahasranetra, Thousand-Eyed?' 'Because when he was formerly a human being he was wise and intelligent, and once, sitting and reflecting on a thousand different matters, he examined and weighed them all — hence he is named Thousand-Eyed.' 'Why is he named Indrali?' 'Because he is the lord of the thirty-two heavens.'" The Yingluo Sūtra, fascicle three, says, "The lord of the gods is named Kauśika."

教門者，《阿含》中帝釋是阿那含，般若明十方，難問般若者，皆名釋提桓因。別、圓中明釋提桓因得首楞嚴三昧，內證不同，過賢劫二千二十四劫作佛，號無著世尊(云云)。

As for the teachings: in the Āgamas, Śakra is described as an anāgāmin; the Prajñāpāramitā literature sets forth Śakras in the ten directions, and all those who press their questions upon the Perfection of Wisdom in that context are named Śakra Devānām Indra. In the distinct and perfect teachings, it is explained that Śakra Devānām Indra has attained the Śūraṅgama-samādhi; his inward realization thus differs according to teaching, and after two thousand and twenty-four kalpas beyond the Bhadrakalpa he will become a buddha, titled the World-Honored One Asaṅga, "the Unattached" (and so on).

本迹者，十住、行、向即三十，十地為一，等覺為二，妙覺為主，同棲第一義天，共服實相甘露即本也，居須彌頂迹也。

As for trace and origin: the ten abidings, the ten practices, and the ten transferences of merit together make thirty; the ten grounds make one further rank; equal enlightenment makes two; and sublime enlightenment is the master of them all, making thirty-three in all — dwelling together in the heaven of ultimate truth and partaking together of the ambrosia of true reality: this is his origin. His dwelling upon the summit of Mount Sumeru is his trace.

觀心解者，自行十善，勸他隨喜，此三十善皆空、皆假、皆中，即是三十三觀門也。

As for the interpretation through the contemplation of mind: practicing the ten wholesome actions oneself, and encouraging others to rejoice in and follow them, makes a total of thirty wholesome acts; and since each of these thirty is at once empty, provisional, and the middle, this is itself the thirty-three gates of contemplation.

Reading note: The number thirty-three runs through this entire passage on three different registers at once — the thirty-three cities of Trāyastriṃśa, the thirty-three bodhisattva ranks Zhiyi maps onto Śakra's own trace and origin, and finally the thirty-three "gates of contemplation" produced by tripling the ten wholesome actions through the three truths. Ru-Yi Meditation Center has kept all three intact rather than flattening the wordplay, since Zhiyi is visibly building toward the pun.

The Three Luminary Deities and the Four Heavenly Kings 名月等三天子與四大天王

Chinese Source

「名月」等三天子，是內臣如卿相，或云是三光天子耳。名月是寶吉祥月天子，大勢至應作；普香是明星天子，虛空藏應作；寶光是寶意日天子，觀世音應作。此即本迹釋也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three celestial sons "Bright Moon" and the others are Śakra's inner ministers, comparable to high officials of state — or, it is said, they are simply the celestial sons of the three luminaries, sun, moon, and stars. "Bright Moon" is the moon-deity Ratnaśrī, "Jewel-Auspicious Moon," whose role is filled by Mahāsthāmaprāpta; "Universal Fragrance" is the star-deity, whose role is filled by Ākāśagarbha; and "Jeweled Light" is the sun-deity Ratnamati, whose role is filled by Avalokiteśvara. This is the interpretation through trace and origin.

觀解者，三觀即二智，三智即三光，從三諦生三智，諦即天、智即子(云云)。

As for the interpretation through contemplation: the three contemplations are the two forms of wisdom, and the three forms of wisdom are the three luminaries; from the three truths arise the three forms of wisdom — the truths are "the heavens," and the wisdom is "the sons" (and so on).

「四大天王」者，帝釋外臣，如武將也，居四寶山，高半須彌，廣二十四萬里。東提頭賴吒，此云持國，亦言安民，居黃金山，領二鬼：揵闥婆、富單那。南毘留勒叉，此云增長，亦云免離，居瑠璃山，領二鬼：薜荔多、鳩槃茶。西毘留博叉，此云非好報，亦云惡眼，亦云雜語，居白銀山，領二鬼：毒龍、毘舍闍。北毘沙門，此云種種聞，亦云多聞，居水精山，領二鬼：羅剎、夜叉。各領二鬼不令惱人，故稱護世。

As for "the Four Great Heavenly Kings": these are Śakra's outer ministers, comparable to military generals. They dwell upon four mountains of precious substance, half the height of Mount Sumeru, and two hundred and forty thousand li in breadth. In the east, Dhṛtarāṣṭra, translated "Upholder of the Nation," also "Pacifier of the People," dwells upon the Golden Mountain and governs two classes of spirit: gandharvas and pūtanas. In the south, Virūḍhaka, translated "Increase and Growth," also "Freedom from Separation," dwells upon the Lapis Lazuli Mountain and governs two classes of spirit: pretas and kumbhāṇḍas. In the west, Virūpākṣa, translated "Not of Good Recompense," also "Evil Eye," also "Mixed Speech," dwells upon the Silver Mountain and governs two classes of spirit: poisonous dragons and piśācas. In the north, Vaiśravaṇa, translated "Hearer of All Manner of Things," also "Much-Hearing," dwells upon the Crystal Mountain and governs two classes of spirit: rākṣasas and yakṣas. Each governs his two classes of spirit so that they do not trouble human beings — hence they are called "the Protectors of the World."

本迹者，本為常樂我淨四王護持佛法，不令外人取其枝葉斫截破壞，常王護東方常、無常雙樹，樂王護南方樂、無樂雙樹，我王護西方我、無我雙樹，淨王護北方淨、不淨雙樹。枝榦喻常，華喻於我，果喻於樂，茂葉喻淨，護此華果，常能利益一切眾生故，迹為四王而護世也。

As for trace and origin: in origin, these are the four kings of permanence, bliss, self, and purity, who uphold and protect the Buddha-dharma, not permitting outsiders to seize its branches and leaves, to hew them down or destroy them. The King of Permanence protects the twin trees of the permanent and impermanent in the east; the King of Bliss protects the twin trees of the blissful and the blissless in the south; the King of Self protects the twin trees of the self and no-self in the west; and the King of Purity protects the twin trees of the pure and the impure in the north. The branches and trunk are likened to permanence, the flower to self, the fruit to bliss, and the luxuriant leaves to purity; protecting these flowers and fruits, they are ever able to benefit all sentient beings — this is their origin — while their trace is to become the Four Kings who protect the world.

觀解者，觀四諦智即是四王，一諦下除愛見二惑，即是護八愛見也。次忉利上有焰摩，此翻善時，《大論》云妙善，去忉利三百三十六萬里。善時上有兜率陀，此翻妙足，去焰摩如地遠，而不列者，略耳。何者？下天鈍、上天著樂，尚知來集，況不著、不鈍而不來耶？

As for the interpretation through contemplation: the wisdom that contemplates the four truths is itself the four kings, and beneath each single truth, dispelling the two afflictions of craving and views is itself the protecting of the eight forms of craving and views. Next, above the Trāyastriṃśa heaven is the Yāma heaven, translated "Good Season" — the Great Treatise calls it "Sublimely Good" — three million three hundred and sixty thousand li above Trāyastriṃśa. Above the Good Season heaven is Tuṣita, translated "Sublimely Contented," as far above Yāma as Yāma is above the earth; that it is not listed here is simply an omission for brevity. Why so? If even the lower heavens, dull of faculty, and the upper heavens, attached to pleasure, are nonetheless known to have come and assembled, how much more should those neither attached nor dull fail to come?

The Sovereign and the Great Sovereign 自在與大自在

Chinese Source

「自在」即第五，「大自在」即第六，自化五欲，他化五欲(云云)。有人言，是色界頂大自在。此不應超至彼也。

"The Sovereign" is the fifth desire-realm heaven, and "the Great Sovereign" is the sixth: in the fifth, one transforms the five desires for oneself; in the sixth, others transform the five desires for one (and so on). As for the contemplation of mind: entering emptiness is the contemplation of the Sovereign; entering the middle is the contemplation of the Great Sovereign (and so on).

Brahmā, Śikhin, and the Heavens of Form 娑婆、梵、尸棄與色界諸天

Chinese Source

次列色界天。「娑婆」，此翻忍，其土眾生安於十惡不肯出離，從人名土，故稱為忍。《悲花經》云：「云何名娑婆？是諸眾生忍受三毒及諸煩惱，故名忍土。」亦名雜，雜九道共居(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next the text lists the heavens of the form realm. "Sahā" is translated "Endurance": the beings of this land are content in the ten evils and unwilling to depart from them, and the land is named after its people — hence it is called "the Land of Endurance." The Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka Sūtra says, "Why is it named Sahā? Because these sentient beings endure the three poisons and the other afflictions — hence it is named the Land of Endurance." It is also named "the mixed land," mixed because the nine destinies dwell there together (and so on).

「梵」者，此翻離欲，除下地繫，上升色界，故名離欲，亦稱高淨。「尸棄」者，此翻為頂髻，又外國喚火為樹提尸棄，此王本修火光定，破欲界惑，從德立名。然經標梵王，復舉尸棄，似如兩人，依《釋論》正以尸棄為王，今經舉位顯名，恐目一人耳。住禪中間，內有覺觀，外有言說，得主領為王，單修禪為梵民，加四無量心為王也。初禪有梵眾、梵輔、大梵，今舉王攝諸也。

"Brahmā" is translated "Freed from Desire": having removed the fetters of the lower stage and ascended into the form realm, he is named "Freed from Desire," also called "Lofty and Pure." "Śikhin" is translated "Crest," or, in a foreign tongue, "fire" is called Jyotiṣka-śikhin; this king originally cultivated the samādhi of fiery radiance, thereby destroying the afflictions of the desire realm, and his name derives from this virtue. Now, the sūtra names King Brahmā and also names Śikhin, as though these were two different persons; but according to the Great Treatise, Śikhin is properly the same king as Brahmā — this sūtra merely brings up his rank to display his name, and it is likely that only a single person is meant. Dwelling in the intermediate stage of dhyāna, with inward investigation and reflection and outward speech, and having attained the position of ruler, he is "king"; those who merely cultivate dhyāna without ruling are "the citizens of Brahmā," while adding the four immeasurable minds to dhyāna makes one "king." The first dhyāna has three heavens — the multitude of Brahmā, the ministers of Brahmā, and Great Brahmā — and here the king is named to encompass them all.

「光明」者，二禪也，此有少光、無量光、光音；三禪有少淨、無量淨、遍淨；四禪有密身亦無挂礙、無量密亦受福、密果亦廣果、無想密亦無想。又有五那含：不煩、不熱、善見、善現、色究竟亦大自在即摩醯首羅，經文存略不具出，但等等此諸天也。例有教門、本迹、觀心，自思之。

"Light" is the second dhyāna, which has three heavens: Parīttābha, "Lesser Light"; Apramāṇābha, "Immeasurable Light"; and Ābhāsvara, "Light-Sound." The third dhyāna has Parīttaśubha, "Lesser Purity"; Apramāṇaśubha, "Immeasurable Purity"; and Śubhakṛtsna, "All-Pervading Purity." The fourth dhyāna has Anabhraka, "Concealed Body," also "Without Obstruction"; Puṇyaprasava, "Immeasurably Concealed," also "Receiving Blessings"; Bṛhatphala, "Concealed Fruit," also "Extensive Fruit"; and Asaṃjñisattva, "Without Thought," also "No-Thought." There are also the five Anāgāmin heavens: Avṛha, "Without Vexation"; Atapa, "Without Heat"; Sudṛśa, "Well-Seen"; Sudarśana, "Well-Manifest"; and Akaniṣṭha, "Ultimate Form," also the heaven of Maheśvara. The sūtra's text preserves only an abbreviated list and does not enumerate them all in full, but simply gathers all these heavens together under "and so on." There would in each case be interpretations in terms of the teachings, of trace and origin, and of the contemplation of mind, following the same pattern — the reader may work these out for himself.

The Eight Dragon Kings 八龍

Chinese Source

次列八龍者，「難陀」名歡喜，「跋」名善，兄弟常護摩竭提，雨澤以時，國無饑年，瓶沙王年為一會，百姓聞皆歡喜，從此得名，即目連所降者也，居海中。

Next the text lists the eight dragon kings. As for the interpretation through contemplation: the three contemplations are themselves the middle way, giving rise to the joy of the Dharma.

"Sāgara" receives his name from his dwelling in the ocean, as he is called in the Avataṃsaka Sūtra; the old commentators say he received his name from a kingdom. In origin he abides in the great ocean of the perfection of wisdom; in trace he dwells in the blue-green sea.

"Vāsuki" is translated "Many-Headed," also "Jewel-Renowned," and he dwells in the water. In origin he abides in the samādhi of universally manifesting physical bodies; in trace he displays many heads. As for contemplation: this is the contemplation of entering the provisional, distinguishing countless dharma-gates (and so on).

「德叉迦」，此云現毒，亦云多舌，或云兩舌，本住樂說無礙辯法門，迹示多舌。「阿那婆達多」，從池得名，此云無熱。

"Takṣaka" is translated "Manifesting Poison," also "Many-Tongued," or "Double-Tongued"; in origin he abides in the dharma-gate of unobstructed eloquence in joyful preaching; in trace he displays many tongues. "Anavatapta" receives his name from a lake, and is translated "Without Heat."

無熱池，《長阿含》十八云：「雪山頂有池，名阿耨達池，中有五柱堂，從池為名，龍王常處其中。閻浮提諸龍有三患：一、熱風、熱沙著身，燒皮肉及骨髓以為苦惱；二、惡風暴起吹其宮殿，失寶飾衣等，龍身自現以為苦惱；三、諸龍娛樂時，金翅鳥入宮，搏撮始生龍子食之，怖懼熱惱。此池無三患，若鳥起心欲往即便命終，故名無熱惱池也。」本住清涼常樂我淨，迹處涼池。

The dragons of Jambudvīpa suffer three afflictions: first, hot winds and hot sand adhere to their bodies, burning their skin, flesh, and marrow, causing them suffering; second, evil winds arise violently and blow upon their palaces, so that they lose their bejeweled ornaments and garments, and their dragon-bodies are exposed, causing them suffering; and third, when the dragons are at ease and enjoying themselves, garuḍas enter their palaces, seize their newly-born dragon offspring, and devour them, filling them with dread and burning anguish. 觀者，三觀妙慧，淨五住之煩唼，免二死之熱沙(云云)。

As for contemplation: the sublime wisdom of the three contemplations purifies the vexation of the five levels of afflictive abiding, and delivers one from the burning sand of the two kinds of death (and so on).

"Manasvin" is translated "Great Body," or also "Great Mind," "Great Strength," and so on. When the asuras once churned the sea and threatened to flood the city of Sudarśana, this dragon coiled his body about to hold back the sea-waters. In origin he abides in the dharma-gate of the boundless body; in trace he becomes this great body.

觀者，中道正觀其性廣博(云云)。

As for contemplation: the true contemplation of the middle way, whose nature is vast and expansive (and so on).

「漚鉢羅」，此云黛色蓮華池，龍依住，從池得名。本住法華三昧，迹居此池。觀者，三觀即是修因，因即蓮華也。

"Utpalaka" is translated "the Dark-Blue Lotus Pool," in which the dragon dwells, and from which he receives his name. In origin he abides in the Lotus Samādhi; in trace he dwells in this pool. As for contemplation: the three contemplations are themselves the causal practice, and this cause is itself the lotus flower.

《正法念經》云：「龍為諸天保境，修羅興兵前與龍鬪。」故知為天所管也。

"Kharaskandha" is translated "Broad Shoulders," also "Evil Aggregate" — he who churns up the sea; the Zhengfahua text calls him "Jeweled Brocade." In origin he abides in the twofold wisdom of the provisional and the real, bearing sentient beings compassionately upon his shoulders; in trace he becomes "Broad Shoulders." As for contemplation: the three contemplations are able to churn up and overturn the great ocean of birth-and-death of the five levels of abiding.

「毘摩質多」此云淨心，亦云種種疑，波海水出聲，名毘摩質多，即舍脂父也。《觀佛三昧》云：「光音天生此地，地使有欲，入海洗不淨，墮泥變為卵，八千歲生一女，千頭少一，二十四手。此女戲于水，水精入身，八千歲生一男，二十四頭，千手少一，海水波音名為毘摩質多。索乾闥婆女，生舍脂，帝釋業力令其父居七寶殿，納為妻，後讒其父，遂交兵，脚波海水、手攻喜見，帝釋以般若呪力，不能為害。」《正》本云「燕居」。本者色心本淨，迹為此名。觀者，正觀中道即是淨心。

"Vemacitra" is translated "Pure Mind," also "Various Doubts" — he who churns the sea-waters until they resound, and hence is named Vemacitra; he is the father of Śacī. The Sūtra on the Samādhi of Buddha-Visualization says: "Beings of the Ābhāsvara heaven were born in this land; the land caused desire to arise in them, and they entered the sea to wash away impurity, whereupon they fell into the mud and were transformed into eggs. After eight thousand years, one such being gave birth to a daughter with a thousand heads less one and twenty-four arms. This girl played in the water, and the essence of the water entered her body; after eight thousand years she gave birth to a son with twenty-four heads and a thousand arms less one — and because the sound of the sea's waves gave rise to him, he was named Vemacitra. He sought the daughter of a gandharva and by her begot Śacī; through Śakra's karmic power her father came to dwell in a palace of the seven treasures, and Śakra took her as his wife. Afterward, slandered by her against her father, he went to war with Śakra: with his feet he churned the sea-waters, and with his hands he assaulted the city of Sudarśana, but Śakra, by the power of the Prajñāpāramitā mantra, could not be harmed by him." The Zhengfahua text calls him "Dwelling in Peace." In origin, form and mind are fundamentally pure; in trace he bears this name. As for contemplation: the true contemplation of the middle way is itself "the pure mind."

「羅睺羅」，此云障持，障持日月者也，是畜生種，身長八萬四千由旬，口廣千由旬，寶珠嚴身，觀天女天園林，若四天下人，孝養父母、供養沙門者，諸天有威力，上空雨刀；若不爾，諸天入宮不出。又日放光照其眼，不能得見，舉手掌障日，世人咸言日蝕怪險，種種邪說；掩月亦如是。或作大聲，世人言天獸吼，險亂王衰，種種邪說。怖日月時，倍大其身氣呵日月，日月失光。來訴佛，佛告羅睺：「莫吞日月。」羅睺支節戰動，身流白汗，即放日月，日月力、眾生力、佛力，眾因緣故不能為害。

"Rāhu" is translated "Obstructing and Seizing," for he obstructs and seizes the sun and moon. He belongs to the animal species, his body eighty-four thousand yojanas in length, his mouth a thousand yojanas across, his body adorned with jeweled pearls; he gazes upon the gardens and groves of the celestial maidens, and if the people of the four continents are filial in caring for their parents and make offerings to the śramaṇas, the gods possess awesome power and rain down knives from the sky against Rāhu when he threatens the sun and moon; but if not, the gods retreat into their palaces and do not come forth to protect him from Rāhu. Moreover, when the sun casts its light into his eyes, he cannot bear to look upon it, and raises his hand to shield the sun with his palm — and the people of the world all say that this is the strange and ominous portent of a solar eclipse, and put forward all manner of mistaken explanations; and it is the same when he covers the moon. Or he lets out a great roar, and the people of the world say that this is the roaring of a heavenly beast, an omen of turmoil and the decline of kings, again with all manner of mistaken explanations. When he thus threatens the sun and moon, he doubles the size of his body and breathes out upon them, so that the sun and moon lose their light. They come to complain to the Buddha, and the Buddha tells Rāhu: "Do not swallow the sun and moon." At this, every joint of Rāhu's body trembles, and his body streams with white sweat, and he at once releases the sun and moon — for by the combined power of the sun and moon, the power of sentient beings, and the power of the Buddha, working together through many causes and conditions, he is unable to do them harm.

昔有婆羅門聰明廣施，四千車載食，於曠野施。有一佛塔，惡人所燒，即以四千車載水滅火救塔，歡喜發願，願得大身，欲界第一。既無正信、好鬪愛戰、喜施，故生光明城，作羅睺羅修羅主也。《正》本云「吸氣」。本、觀(云云)。

Long ago there was a brahmin, wise and generous in giving, who loaded four thousand carts with food and distributed it in the wilderness. There was a stūpa of the Buddha there, which had been burned by evil men; the brahmin thereupon loaded four thousand carts with water, extinguished the fire, and rescued the stūpa, and, filled with joy, made a vow, wishing to obtain a great body and become foremost in the desire realm. Yet, lacking true faith, and fond of fighting and warfare and delighting in giving of a kind, he was thereby born in the City of Light, becoming Rāhu, lord of the asuras. The Zhengfahua text calls him "Breath-Drawer." The interpretations through origin and contemplation are omitted here (and so on).

Next the text lists the four garuḍas, translated "Golden Wings," their wing-feathers golden in color, dwelling in the great trees above the four continents, their two wings spanning three million three hundred and sixty thousand li apart. Some say that the Zhuangzi's "peng" bird refers to this, since when the peng travels, all the other birds shelter beneath its wings, and it is also called the phoenix. In my own view, the phoenix does not tread upon living grass, feeds on bamboo seeds, and roosts in the wutong tree that yields milk-sap — while the golden-winged bird devours dragons; how, then, could they be the same kind? "Great Awesome Virtue": his awesome power surpasses that of his fellows, and his awesome power also subdues the dragons; the Zhengfahua text calls him "Fully Endowed." "Great Body": great among his fellows. "Great Fullness": the dragons ever fulfill his every wish.

"Wish-Fulfilling": he bears this jewel upon his neck; the Zhengfahua text calls him "Immovable." The garuḍa birds possess supernatural power: the males can transform into celestial youths, and the females into celestial maidens, and where they dwell by transformation there stand jeweled palaces furnished with a hundred flavors of food — yet their karmic recompense still requires them to feed upon dragons. A womb-born garuḍa can devour womb-born dragons but not the other three kinds; an egg-born garuḍa can devour egg-born and womb-born dragons; a moisture-born garuḍa can devour three kinds; and a transformation-born garuḍa can devour all four kinds.

《觀佛三昧經》云：「正音迦樓，一日山東噉一龍王，五百小龍，三方亦爾，周而復始壽八千年。臨終失勢欲噉龍子，龍母[口*熬]嘇之不得食，即嗔從金剛山透海，穿地輪過，不能過風輪，風彈之，從故孔湧到金剛山，如是七返，還山頂命終，肉裂火起，將燒寶山，難陀雨雨滅之，肉爛心衝風輪亦七返，墮山上成如意珠，龍得之即為王，人王亦感此珠者也。」

The Sūtra on the Samādhi of Buddha-Visualization says: "The garuḍa, properly pronounced, each day devours, on the eastern side of the mountain, one dragon king and five hundred lesser dragons, and likewise on each of the other three sides, cycling through in this way, for a lifespan of eight thousand years. As his life draws to its end and his strength fails, he seeks to devour dragon offspring, but the mother dragon shrieks at him and he cannot obtain his food; enraged, he plunges from the Diamond Mountain into the sea, bores through the wheel of earth and passes beyond it, but cannot pass beyond the wheel of wind, which flings him back, and he gushes back up through his own former hole to the Diamond Mountain — and thus seven times over, until at last, back at the mountain's summit, his life comes to its end: his flesh splits open and fire breaks forth, threatening to burn the jeweled mountain, until Nanda sends down rain to extinguish it. His flesh decays, and his heart, driven by the wheel of wind, likewise rebounds seven times, and falls upon the mountain, becoming the wish-fulfilling jewel; whatever dragon obtains it at once becomes king among dragons, and human kings, too, are those who have gained merit worthy of this very jewel."

Next the text lists the human beings. "Vaidehī" is the mother, translated "Thinking." Bimbisāra, translated "Model of Reality," is the father. "Ajātaśatru" means "Enmity Before Birth"; he is also called Balaruci, translated "Fingerless" — his attendants, wishing to shield him from this ominous name, called him instead "Well-Seen." "Well-Seen" is his original name, and "Fingerless" is a name expressing his trace. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says, "'Ajā' means 'not born,' and 'śatru' means 'enmity.'" Because beings fail to give birth to the buddha-nature, the enmity of affliction is thereby born; and because the enmity of affliction is born, the buddha-nature is not seen; but where affliction is not born, the buddha-nature is thereby seen. It is also said that "ajā" means "not born," and "śatru" means "worldly dharma," and because he was unstained by the eight worldly dharmas he was named Ajātaśatru — this is his original meaning.

《普超經》云：「阿闍世從文殊懺悔，得柔順忍，命終入賓吒羅地獄，即入、即出，生上方佛土得無生忍，彌勒出時復來此界，名不動菩薩，後當作佛，號淨界如來。」其迹既爾，本豈可量？說《法華》時預清淨眾，至《涅槃》時引逆罪者，何異迦葉於《法華》受記，於《涅槃》不堪付囑？不可迷迹而惑其本也。

The Sūtra of Universal Transcendence says, "Ajātaśatru, through repentance before Mañjuśrī, attained the receptivity to the truth of non-arising that comes through gentle compliance; at the end of his life he entered the Prativi hell, but no sooner had he entered than he emerged again, and was born in a buddha-land above, where he attained the receptivity to the truth of non-arising itself; when Maitreya appears in this world, he will come again to this realm, named the Bodhisattva Acala, 'Unmoving,' and thereafter will become a buddha, titled the Tathāgata Pure-Realm." Since his trace was thus, how could his origin possibly be fathomed? At the time of the Lotus Sūtra he took part among the assembly of the pure, while at the time of the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra he is cited as one guilty of the five heinous transgressions — how does this differ from Mahākāśyapa, who received the prediction of buddhahood at the Lotus Sūtra yet, at the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, appears unfit to receive the Buddha's final entrustment? One must not be deceived by the trace and thereby be led into doubt about the origin.

觀解者，貪愛母、無明父，害此故稱逆，逆即順也，行於非道，通達佛道。

As for the interpretation through contemplation: craving is "the mother," and ignorance is "the father"; to slay these is called "transgression" — yet this very transgression is itself compliance, for it is by walking the improper path that one penetrates through to the buddha-path.

Two Questions on the Assembly's Composition 列眾二問

Chinese Source

問：
佛在人中說法，列人眾何少？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The Buddha preached the Dharma among human beings — why, then, is the human assembly listed so briefly?

答：
文略不載，人實不少，文云：「及諸小王、轉輪聖王等。」《無量義》中列四輪王，國王、國臣，國民、士女，其眾則廣。

Answer: The text is abbreviated and does not record them all; the number of human beings present was in fact not small. The text says, "and the various minor kings and wheel-turning sage kings, and so on." In the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings, the four kinds of wheel-turning kings are listed, along with kings, ministers, subjects, gentlemen and gentlewomen of the realm — there the assembly is set forth at much greater length.

問：
天、人、龍、鬼皆見佛聞法，地獄一道，無色一界，何意不列？

Question: The gods, humans, dragons, and spirits all see the Buddha and hear the Dharma — why, then, are the single destiny of hell and the single realm of the formless not listed?

答：
此義今當辯。夫諸道升沈，由戒有持、毀；見佛、不見佛，由乘有緩、急。然持戒有麁細，故報有優、劣；持乘有小、大，見佛有權、實，且略判戒乘各為三品，依《涅槃》一句開為四句釋之，其義則顯：一、戒乘俱急，二、戒緩乘急，三、戒急乘緩，四、戒乘俱緩。若通論戒乘，一切善法，一切觀慧，皆得稱戒，亦皆是乘，人天五乘即是其義，道、共等戒悉是通意也。

Answer: This meaning must now be explained. The rising and falling among the various destinies depends upon whether the precepts are upheld or violated; whether one sees the Buddha or does not see him depends upon whether the vehicle is slack or urgent. Now, the upholding of precepts may be coarse or fine, and so karmic recompense is superior or inferior; the upholding of the vehicle may be lesser or greater, and the seeing of the Buddha may be provisional or real. Let us then, in brief, divide both precepts and vehicle each into three grades, and, following a single phrase from the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, open this into four propositions to explain the matter, whereby its meaning becomes clear: first, both precepts and vehicle urgent; second, precepts slack but vehicle urgent; third, precepts urgent but vehicle slack; and fourth, both precepts and vehicle slack. If we speak of precepts and vehicle in their broadest sense, then every wholesome dharma and every contemplative wisdom may be called "precepts," and may likewise all be called "vehicle" — the five vehicles of gods and humans are precisely this meaning, and the precepts of the path and of concentration and the like are all included in this broad sense.

今就別判，三歸、五戒、十善、八齋、出家律儀，乃至定共、能防身口、遮惡道果、得人天報者，名之為戒。若聞經生解，觀智推尋四諦、十二緣、六度、生滅無生滅等，智能破煩惱，運出三界者，名之為乘。故《大品》云：「有相之善不動、不出，無相之善能動、能出。」即此義也。

Now, speaking in the narrower, particular sense: the three refuges, the five precepts, the ten wholesome actions, the eight precepts of a single day and night, the monastic disciplinary codes, and even the precepts arising together with concentration — whatever is able to guard body and speech, to bar the fruits of the evil destinies, and to obtain the rewards of gods and humans — this is called "precepts." If, on hearing the sūtras, one gives rise to understanding, and with contemplative wisdom investigates the four truths, the twelve links of dependent origination, the six perfections, arising-and-ceasing and non-arising-and-non-ceasing, and so forth, such that this wisdom is able to destroy the afflictions and carry one out beyond the three realms — this is called "vehicle." Hence the Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra says, "Wholesome conduct that bears characteristics neither moves nor escapes the three realms; wholesome conduct without characteristics is able to move and able to escape" — this is precisely this meaning.

若戒乘俱急者，持下品戒，戒急報在人中，持小乘乘急，以人中身，於三藏教時見佛聞法；持中乘乘急，以人報身，於通教大乘乃至帶方便諸大乘經時，見佛聞法；持上乘乘急，以人報身於華嚴、法華等教及諸教中圓，見佛聞法，預列為同聞眾者是也。若持中品戒急，報在欲界天，持小乘乘急，以欲界天身，於三藏時見佛聞法，餘如上說。若持上品戒急，加修禪定報在色、無色天等，持小乘乘急，以色、無色天身，於三藏中見佛聞法，餘如上說。釋第一句竟。

If both precepts and vehicle are urgent: one who upholds the lowest grade of precepts urgently receives recompense as a human being; if he also upholds the lesser vehicle urgently, then, in a human body, he sees the Buddha and hears the Dharma at the time of the Tripiṭaka teaching; if he upholds the middling vehicle urgently, then, in a human body of recompense, he sees the Buddha and hears the Dharma at the time of the shared teaching's Great Vehicle, or even amid the various Great Vehicle sūtras that carry an admixture of skillful means; if he upholds the supreme vehicle urgently, then, in a human body of recompense, he sees the Buddha and hears the Dharma at the Avataṃsaka, the Lotus, and the other teachings, and within the perfect teaching found in every teaching — and it is precisely such beings who are listed among this assembly of those hearing together with the Buddha. If he upholds the middling grade of precepts urgently, he receives recompense as a desire-realm god; if he also upholds the lesser vehicle urgently, then, in the body of a desire-realm god, he sees the Buddha and hears the Dharma at the time of the Tripiṭaka teaching, and the rest follows as explained above. If he upholds the highest grade of precepts urgently, and in addition cultivates meditative concentration, he receives recompense as a form-realm or formless-realm god; if he also upholds the lesser vehicle urgently, then, in the body of a form-realm or formless-realm god, he sees the Buddha and hears the Dharma amid the Tripiṭaka teaching, and the rest follows as explained above. This concludes the explanation of the first proposition.

若戒緩乘急者，三品戒皆緩，報墮三途，持小乘乘急，以三途身，於三藏中見佛聞法，餘如上說。釋第二句竟。若戒急乘緩者，三戒急故，受欲界人天及色、無色天身，三乘緩故，佛雖出世說三乘法，愛著樂報耽荒五欲，不見佛、不聞法，舍衛三億家，及諸不見聞者，三界著樂諸天等是也。釋第三句竟。若戒乘俱緩者，受三途報，不見佛、不聞法也，釋第四句竟。

If precepts are slack but vehicle urgent: all three grades of precepts are slack, and he falls into recompense among the three evil destinies; but if he upholds the lesser vehicle urgently, then, in the body of one of the three evil destinies, he sees the Buddha and hears the Dharma amid the Tripiṭaka teaching, and the rest follows as explained above. This concludes the explanation of the second proposition. If precepts are urgent but vehicle slack: because all three grades of precepts are urgent, he receives a body among the desire-realm gods and humans, or the form- and formless-realm gods; but because all three vehicles are slack, then, even though a buddha appears in the world and preaches the teachings of the three vehicles, he remains attached to the pleasures of his recompense, indulging and immersed in the five desires, and neither sees the Buddha nor hears the Dharma — such were the three hundred million households of Śrāvastī who neither saw nor heard the Buddha, and such are the gods of the three realms attached to pleasure. This concludes the explanation of the third proposition. If both precepts and vehicle are slack: he receives recompense among the three evil destinies, and neither sees the Buddha nor hears the Dharma. This concludes the explanation of the fourth proposition.

此文不列地獄者，以其戒緩，苦重報隔；上乘又緩，不能於法華見佛聞法，餘經有列者，餘乘急耳。又不列無色天者，上戒急故受天身著定味，上乘緩故，不能於法華見佛聞法；餘經有列者，有餘乘急耳。若得此意，一一勘天龍八部，皆識本緣緩急，來、不來義悉可解，廣釋如《淨名疏》。又識權者引實，本迹義轉明，將此勘已觀行，三世因果朗然可識。

This text does not list the hell-realm because, its precepts being slack, its suffering is heavy and its recompense cut off from the possibility of encounter; and because even its highest vehicle is slack, it is unable to see the Buddha or hear the Dharma at the Lotus assembly. Where other sūtras do list beings of hell, it is because in those instances some vehicle among them was urgent. Nor does this text list the formless-realm gods, because, their highest grade of precepts being urgent, they receive a divine body attached to the savor of concentration, while their highest vehicle, being slack, is unable to see the Buddha or hear the Dharma at the Lotus assembly; where other sūtras do list them, it is because some vehicle among them was urgent. If one grasps this meaning, and examines each of the eight kinds of beings among the gods, dragons, and the rest in turn, one may fully understand the urgency or slackness of their original causal conditions, and thereby grasp entirely the meaning of why they come or do not come — a fuller explanation may be found in the Commentary on the Vimalakīrti Sūtra. Furthermore, recognizing how the provisional draws forth the real, the meaning of trace and origin becomes ever clearer; and applying this to one's own contemplative practice, the causes and effects of the three periods of time become brilliantly clear and knowable.

「各禮佛足」者，總結眾集也。

"Each bowed at the Buddha's feet" is the summary conclusion of the gathering of the assembly.

Reading note: This fourfold grid of "precepts urgent or slack" crossed with "vehicle urgent or slack" is one of the more systematic pieces of scholastic reasoning in this fascicle — Zhiyi's answer to a real exegetical problem, namely why the sūtra's opening assembly lists gods, humans, dragons, and spirits but conspicuously omits beings from hell and from the formless realm. Ru-Yi Meditation Center has kept the full four-part working-out intact rather than summarizing the pattern, since Zhiyi explicitly numbers and closes each of the four cases in turn.

The Distinct Prologue: Five Parts 別序五意

Chinese Source

◎「爾時世尊」下，訖品，名別序。文為五：一、眾集，二、現瑞，三、疑念，四、發問，五、答問。光宅逆順生起，由眾集故現瑞，乃至由問故答，答由於問，乃至瑞由眾集，此乃翻覆緣起，鉤鎖相連，序於正意，竟自未顯，直是因緣一釋，尚自不明，況二三四緣，了無趣向。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "At that time, the World-Honored One" onward, to the end of the chapter, is named "the distinct prologue." The text falls into five parts: first, the gathering of the assembly; second, the manifestation of the auspicious signs; third, the arising of doubt; fourth, the posing of the question; and fifth, the answering of the question. Guangzhai traces their arising forward and backward: because the assembly gathers, the signs are therefore manifested; and so on, up to: because there is a question, there is therefore an answer — the answer arising from the question, and the signs, in turn, arising from the gathering of the assembly. But this is merely a reciprocal chain of causal arising, each link hooked to the next, and it never actually manages to show how the prologue relates to the principal meaning that follows it — it amounts to no more than a single reading through causes and conditions, and even this single reading remains unclear, let alone the second, third, and fourth readings, which are given no direction at all.

今明五序，序正中四一，集眾敘人一，現瑞敘理一，疑念敘行一，問答敘教一，此則因緣釋也。約教者，此序序正，非三藏，非通、非別，乃是序於圓正耳。約本迹者，若以序序〈壽量〉中本地四一者，此義自可知，不復記。觀心可解(云云)。

Now I explain that these five parts of the prologue serve as a prologue to the "four ones" within the main body of the teaching to come: the gathering of the assembly narrates the "one" of persons; the manifestation of the signs narrates the "one" of principle; the arising of doubt narrates the "one" of practice; and the question and answer narrate the "one" of the teaching. This is the interpretation in terms of causes and conditions. As for the teachings: this prologue serves as prologue to a main teaching that is neither the Tripiṭaka teaching, nor the shared, nor the distinct teaching, but is rather the prologue to the perfect and true teaching alone. As for trace and origin: if this prologue is taken as prologue to the "four ones" of the original ground set forth in the "Chapter on the Lifespan," this meaning may be understood of itself, and need not be recorded again here. The interpretation through the contemplation of mind may likewise be understood by extension (and so on).

The Assembly's Deportment: Four Kinds of Hearers 四眾威儀

Chinese Source

就眾集又二，初眾集威儀，次眾集供養，《法華論》目此為威儀如法住。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the gathering of the assembly there are again two parts: first, the deportment of the assembly's gathering; and second, the offerings made by the assembly's gathering. The Fahua Lun designates this as "abiding in proper deportment, in accordance with the Dharma."

「四眾」者，舊云：「出家、在家各二，合為四眾。」此名局，意不周。今約一眾，更開為四：謂發起眾、當機眾、影響眾、結緣眾。發起者，權謀智鑒，知機、知時，擊揚發動，成辦利益，如大象躄樹使象子得飽，所謂發起令集，發起瑞相，乃至發起問答等，皆名發起眾。當機者，宿植德本，緣合時熟，如癰欲潰，不起于座，聞即得道，此名當機眾。影嚮者，古往諸佛、法身菩薩，隱其圓極，匡輔法王，如眾星繞月，雖無為作而有巨益，此名影嚮眾。

As for "the fourfold assembly": the old commentators said, "Those who have gone forth and those who remain in the household, each of two kinds, male and female, make the fourfold assembly." But this designation is too narrow, and its meaning does not fully cover the case. Now, taking any single assembly, I open it further into four: namely, the assembly of those who set things in motion, the assembly of those whose capacity has ripened, the assembly of those who lend resonant support, and the assembly of those forming karmic affinity. "Those who set things in motion": with skillful counsel and discerning wisdom, knowing the right capacity and the right time, they strike and set forth the occasion, bringing benefit to completion — like a great elephant that shakes a tree so that its young may eat their fill; that is, those who set in motion the gathering of the assembly, who set in motion the auspicious signs, and even those who set in motion the question and answer, are all named "the assembly of those who set things in motion." "Those whose capacity has ripened": having long ago planted roots of virtue, their conditions now come together and their time has ripened — like a boil about to burst — they need not even rise from their seats before, upon hearing the teaching, they attain the path at once; this is named "the assembly of those whose capacity has ripened." "Those who lend resonant support": buddhas of ages past, and bodhisattvas of the dharma-body, concealing their perfect and ultimate attainment, support and assist the Dharma-king — like the multitude of stars circling the moon, which, though they do nothing themselves, nonetheless provide immense benefit; this is named "the assembly of those who lend resonant support."

結緣者，力無引導擊動之能，德非伏物鎮嚴之用，而過去根淺，覆漏污雜，三慧不生，現世雖見佛、聞法，無四悉檀益，但作未來得度因緣，此名結緣眾。比丘眾既爾，餘三眾亦然，合十六眾。類如大通智勝佛時，王子覆講即彼時發起眾，聞法得道即彼時當機眾，聞法未度而世世相值，于今有住聲聞地者，即彼時結緣眾。彼佛世時尚有四四十六眾，今佛道同，寧得無耶？此是圓教十六眾，約三教亦例可知。

"Those forming karmic affinity": their power lacks the capacity to lead or set things in motion, and their virtue lacks the power to subdue and adorn things; their roots from past lives are shallow, obscured, outflowing, and defiled, so that the three kinds of wisdom do not arise in them; though in this present life they see the Buddha and hear the Dharma, they gain no benefit from the four siddhāntas, but merely form a karmic affinity toward liberation in some future life — this is named "the assembly of those forming karmic affinity." As it is for the assembly of bhikṣus, so it is for the other three assemblies as well, making sixteen assemblies in all. This is comparable to the time of the Buddha Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū: the princes who resumed his preaching were, at that time, the assembly of those who set things in motion; those who heard the Dharma and attained the path were, at that time, the assembly of those whose capacity had ripened; and those who heard the Dharma but were not yet liberated, meeting him again life after life, some of whom even now abide at the level of the śrāvaka, were, at that time, the assembly of those forming karmic affinity. If even in that buddha's age there were already these four sets of sixteen assemblies, how, now that the present Buddha's path is the same, could there fail to be the same? This is the sixteen assemblies of the perfect teaching; the pattern for the other three teachings may likewise be inferred by analogy.

本迹可解。

The interpretation through trace and origin may be understood by extension.

觀心者，研境作觀，在名字觀行位中，即成結緣眾，入相似位即成當機眾，入分真位即成發起、影嚮眾(云云)。

As for the contemplation of mind: when one investigates one's object and undertakes contemplation while still at the stage of verbal understanding and contemplative practice, one thereby becomes "the assembly of those forming karmic affinity"; on entering the stage of resemblance, one becomes "the assembly of those whose capacity has ripened"; and on entering the stage of partial truth, one becomes "the assembly of those who set things in motion" and "of those who lend resonant support" (and so on).

Reading note: Zhiyi's fourfold recasting of "the fourfold assembly" — trading a literal headcount of monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen for a functional typology of who each hearer is *for* the sūtra's unfolding — is one of his more original exegetical moves in this fascicle, and he takes care to show it working at the level of an individual practitioner's own stages of insight, not just as a taxonomy of the historical crowd at Vulture Peak.

Circumambulation and Offerings 圍遶供養

Chinese Source

「圍遶」者，佛初出世，人未知法，淨居天下化為人像，到已右旋，旋已敬禮，禮已却坐聽法。因於天敬，人以為楷，此因緣解也。圍遶者，行旋威儀也，表四門機動，俱見圓理，以圓對偏，例有四義，即教門解也。又佛身周匝相好莊嚴，四旋瞻仰，增念佛定，即觀心解也。若觀佛色身，得見法身，即本迹解。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "circumambulating": when the Buddha first appeared in the world, human beings did not yet know the Dharma, and so the gods of the Pure Abodes descended and transformed themselves into human forms; having arrived, they circled to the right, and having circled, they paid reverent homage, and having paid homage, they withdrew and sat down to hear the Dharma. Because of this reverence shown by the gods, human beings took it as their model — this is the interpretation through causes and conditions. "Circumambulating" is the deportment of walking in a circle, signifying that the capacities of beings at the four gates of the four directions are all set in motion and all behold the perfect principle; taking the perfect as set against the partial, there are, by analogy, four meanings for the four teachings — this is the interpretation in terms of the teachings. Furthermore, the Buddha's body, adorned all around with its major and minor marks, is gazed upon from all four sides as the assembly circles him, deepening their samādhi of buddha-recollection — this is the interpretation through the contemplation of mind. And if, by contemplating the Buddha's physical body, one comes to behold the dharma-body, this is the interpretation through trace and origin.

「供養」者，通三業皆是供養，別論卑謹虔禮名恭敬，至念專注名尊重，發言稱美名讚歎，施其依報名供養。此中文略，具辯應如《無量義經》廣說。天厨、天香、天鉢器等即是供養。大莊嚴菩薩及八萬大士，合掌叉手即是恭敬，一心瞻仰即是尊重，說七言偈即是讚歎。今論眾集，指彼文者，彼經眾集，說法竟，儼然不散，即彼座席仍說《法華》，故知三業供養不得有異，用彼廣釋，此略，於義無咎。

As for "making offerings": in general, all acts of the three karmic modes of body, speech, and mind are offerings; but, spoken of individually, humble and reverent obeisance is called "reverence"; focused and single-minded mindfulness is called "esteem"; speaking words of praise is called "eulogy"; and bestowing material support is called "offering" in the narrow sense. Here the text is abbreviated; a full account should be sought at length in the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings. Heavenly delicacies, heavenly incense, and heavenly bowls and vessels are "offerings" in this narrow sense; the Bodhisattva Mahāvyūha and the eighty thousand great beings, with palms joined and hands clasped, display "reverence"; gazing up single-mindedly is "esteem"; and reciting verses of seven characters is "eulogy." Now, in discussing the gathering of the assembly, I point to that text because, in that sūtra, after the assembly had gathered and the preaching of the Dharma was complete, the assembly remained composed and did not disperse, and it was in that very seat that the Lotus Sūtra was then preached — hence it is known that the threefold karmic offering cannot be any different between the two sūtras; that sūtra's fuller explanation may be used to supplement this sūtra's brevity, without fault to the meaning.

The Prologue of Manifesting Signs 現相序

Chinese Source

從「為諸菩薩說大乘經」下，訖「以佛舍利起七寶塔」，是現相序。瑤師明七瑞，此土開六，他土總一；光宅此彼各六瑞。此六者，動則說法度人，靜則入定觀理，動靜為一雙；上天雨四花，下地六種動，上下為一雙；大眾內懷歡喜，如來外放光明，內外為一雙。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "he taught, for the sake of the bodhisattvas, a Great Vehicle sūtra" down to "raised up stūpas of the seven treasures from the Buddha's relics," this is the prologue of manifesting signs. Master Yao explains seven auspicious signs, opening six in this world and gathering the rest into a single sign in other worlds; Guangzhai takes six signs in this world and six in other worlds, respectively. These six are: motion, in preaching the Dharma to liberate beings, and stillness, in entering samādhi to contemplate principle — motion and stillness make one pair; above, the heavens rain down the four flowers, and below, the earth quakes in six ways — above and below make one pair; and within, the great assembly harbors joy, while without, the Tathāgata emits radiant light — inner and outer make one pair.

今謂尋文起盡如光宅，若取名義便易，表報之意並自未彰。今明智定、因果、感應為三雙：智則指一說多，定則諦緣義處；因則四位天花，果則六處地動；感則大乘機發，應則圓毫照之。此六皆稱瑞相者，文云：「今相如本瑞」，瑞秖是相耳，人情分別，以密報為瑞，奇異為相。相何所報？妙理玄賾說之至難，人情悠悠不能尊重，先以異相駭變常情，常情既變而生欽渴，故以異釋相，以報釋瑞。略明六瑞，表報十妙，感應妙中已說，今更道說法瑞，表報說法妙、智妙；入定瑞，表報行妙；雨花瑞，表報位妙；地動瑞，表報境妙、乘妙；眾喜瑞，表報眷屬妙、利益妙；放光瑞，表報感應妙、神通妙，是故六種俱名現相序。

Now, in my own view, tracing where the text begins and ends accords with Guangzhai's scheme; but if one merely takes up names and their easy meanings, the sense of what these signs "signify" and "recompense" still remains unrevealed. I now explain them instead as three pairs — wisdom and concentration, cause and effect, and stimulus and response: wisdom points to the "one" truth while speaking of "the many" expedients; concentration is the samādhi of the truths, the conditions, the meaning, and the object; cause is the heavenly flowers of the four ranks; effect is the six-fold quaking of the earth; stimulus is the arousal of the Great Vehicle capacity in the assembly; and response is the perfect buddha's radiant tuft of hair illuminating them. As for why all six are called "auspicious signs": the text says, "the signs now are as the signs of old" — a "sign" is simply a "mark"; it is merely popular usage that distinguishes a hidden recompense as "auspicious" and an unusual appearance as a "mark." But what, then, does the mark recompense? The sublime principle is so profound and subtle that it is exceedingly difficult to express in words, and ordinary sentiment is too idle and inattentive to hold it in due esteem; hence the Buddha first employs unusual marks to startle ordinary sentiment out of its habitual state, and once ordinary sentiment has been so transformed, reverent longing is thereby aroused — hence "the unusual" is used to explain "the mark," and "recompense" is used to explain "the auspicious." Briefly explaining the six auspicious signs as signifying and recompensing the ten wondrous aspects of the Dharma: the wonder of stimulus and response has already been discussed elsewhere; I will now further say that the sign of preaching the Dharma signifies and recompenses the wonder of preaching the Dharma and the wonder of wisdom; the sign of entering samādhi signifies and recompenses the wonder of practice; the sign of raining down flowers signifies and recompenses the wonder of rank; the sign of the earth's quaking signifies and recompenses the wonder of the object and the wonder of the vehicle; the sign of the assembly's joy signifies and recompenses the wonder of the retinue and the wonder of benefit; and the sign of emitting light signifies and recompenses the wonder of stimulus-and-response and the wonder of supernatural power — and it is for this reason that all six are together named "the prologue of manifesting signs."

Reading note: Zhiyi here explicitly sets aside two earlier commentators' schemes for organizing the six auspicious signs — reciprocal cause-and-effect chains that, as he says of Guangzhai's version a few lines earlier, never actually show how the prologue connects to the sūtra's main teaching — and proposes his own three-pair structure (wisdom/concentration, cause/effect, stimulus/response) mapped onto his ten wondrous aspects from the Fahua Xuanyi. Ru-Yi Meditation Center has kept the full list of six signify/recompense pairings intact, since this passage is essentially a table of contents for everything that follows in this fascicle.

Debating the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings as Prologue 無量義經為序辨

Chinese Source

生師云：「無相空理，大乘之本，封三來久，頓說無三，不能取信，故說無相為《法華》序。」觀師意同。若爾，《般若》、《淨名》皆應是序，何獨《無量義》耶？彼釋云：「如此由五時故後教得起。」更問：「若爾，《無量義》與諸經皆通途相生，非關別序。」基師云：「空理無形故云無量，序意同前，難亦如是。」印師云：「無相善有成佛義，故言無量。」又云：「彼經不說有三、無三有異，《大品》非《法華》所指，指者不來秦地。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Master Sheng says: "The principle of the emptiness of characteristics is the root of the Great Vehicle; beings have long been bound to the three vehicles, and to suddenly declare that there is no distinction of three would not win their trust — hence the doctrine of no characteristics is preached as the prologue to the Lotus." Master Guan's view agrees with this. But if this is so, then the Prajñāpāramitā and the Vimalakīrti Sūtras should equally count as this prologue — why should only the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings be singled out? He explains this by saying, "It is because of the five periods of teaching that the later teaching is thereby able to arise." One may further ask: "If so, then the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings merely gives rise to the other sūtras in the ordinary, general way that any earlier teaching gives rise to a later one, and has nothing to do with serving as this sūtra's own distinct prologue." Master Ji says: "The principle of emptiness is without form, hence it is called 'immeasurable' — his account of the prologue's meaning agrees with the one above, and the same objection applies to it as well." Master Yin says: "The good of no characteristics carries the meaning of attaining buddhahood, hence it is called 'immeasurable.'" He further says: "That sūtra does not speak of any difference between there being three vehicles and there being no three; the Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra is not what the Lotus points to, for the sūtra it points to had not yet arrived in the land of Qin at the time in question."

今謂此經是宋元嘉三年慧表比丘，於南海郡朝廷寺，遇曇摩耶舍受此本，還武當山，永明三年始傳於世。經既已來，豈可送還天竺？光宅云：「《無量義》以萬善同歸能成佛道，《法華》正明無二、無三，破三與一為異，故即為序。」若言萬善同歸，二三何不同歸？二三若歸，序正不異；若言破二、破三，何不破萬？破二、破三，則無二無三，既其破萬，是則無序。取經互舉意為異者，不成異也，異意不顯，序義亦不成也。劉虬注云：「無相為本，無相一法含義不貲。」若含義不貲，即是有相，何謂無相？

Now, in my own view: this sūtra was received by the bhikṣu Huibiao in the third year of the Yuanjia era of the Liu Song [426 CE], at Chaoting Monastery in Nanhai commandery, from Dharmayaśas, and brought back to Mount Wudang, where it first began to circulate in the world in the third year of the Yongming era [485 CE]. Since the sūtra had thus already arrived in China long before the debate in question, how could it possibly have been "sent back to India"? Guangzhai says: "The Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings teaches that the ten thousand forms of goodness converge together and are able to accomplish the buddha-path, while the Lotus specifically clarifies that there is neither two nor three vehicles, breaking apart the difference between three and one — hence it serves as the prologue." But if the ten thousand forms of goodness converge together, why should the two and three vehicles not likewise converge? If the two and three do converge, then the prologue and the main teaching are no different in this respect; and if it is said that the Lotus breaks apart the two and the three, why does it not likewise break apart the ten thousand? If it broke apart the two and the three, then there would be neither two nor three; and since it has already, in this account, broken apart the ten thousand, then there would be no prologue at all distinguishing it from the main teaching. To take up the sūtras and cite them against one another, treating their meanings as different, does not in fact establish any real difference; and since the difference in meaning is not made evident, the meaning of "prologue" is likewise not established. Liu Qiu's commentary says: "No characteristics is the root; the single dharma of no characteristics contains meaning beyond measure." But if it contains meaning beyond measure, this is itself a kind of having characteristics — how, then, can it be called "no characteristics"?

尋諸師各偏一種，若言有相之善有成佛義，此三藏意耳；若言無相之善有成佛義，此通教意耳；若言含法不貲，此別教意耳。並他經所明，皆非序《法華》意耳。若《法華論》列十七種，皆《法華》之異名，無量義者，即《法華》之一名也。今申論意，佛直說此名而入此定，故得為序。

Examining these various masters, each favors a single, partial view: to say that the good bearing characteristics carries the meaning of attaining buddhahood is merely the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching; to say that the good without characteristics carries the meaning of attaining buddhahood is merely the sense of the shared teaching; and to say that it contains dharmas beyond measure is merely the sense of the distinct teaching. All of these are what other sūtras set forth, and none of them is truly the meaning of the prologue to the Lotus. The Fahua Lun lists seventeen alternate names, all of which are other names for the Lotus itself, and "Immeasurable Meanings" is simply one such name for the Lotus. I now set forth the treatise's true intent: the Buddha directly spoke this very name of the sūtra to come and thereupon entered this samādhi — and it is on this basis that it can serve as a prologue.

《大品》、《金光明》、《涅槃》，皆先唱名，於序無妨。今經文殊引古佛，亦名無量義；又云：「當說大乘經名妙法蓮華」，此亦序中唱名，與論意同也。今按彼經釋無量義者從一法生，其一法者所謂無相，無相、不相名為實相，從此實相生無量法，所謂二法、三道、四果。今釋此文，無相者，無生死相也；不相者，不涅槃相也；涅槃亦無，故言不相無相，指中道為實相也。二法即頓、漸，頓謂《華嚴》頓中一切法也，漸謂三藏、方等、般若一切法也。三道即三乘，四果即羅漢、支佛、菩薩、佛，此等諸法名為無量。實相為義處，從一義處出無量法，得為無量法入一義處作序。譬如算師，從一算下諸算，除諸算歸一算，由下故除，下為除序。從一派諸，收諸歸一，開為合序，亦復如是。如此消釋，不違彼經論，亦與此經合(云云)。

The Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra, the Suvarṇaprabhāsa, and the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra all first announce their title, and this presents no difficulty for the notion of a prologue. In the present sūtra, Mañjuśrī cites an ancient buddha who likewise named his preliminary sūtra "Immeasurable Meanings"; and our sūtra also says, "he is about to preach a Great Vehicle sūtra named the Wondrous Dharma Lotus Flower" — this, too, is the announcing of a title within the prologue, in agreement with the treatise's intent. Now, examining that sūtra's own explanation, "immeasurable meanings" arise from a single dharma, and this single dharma is what is called "no characteristic"; "no characteristic" and "not any fixed characteristic" are named "true reality," and from this true reality arise immeasurable dharmas, namely the two dharmas, the three paths, and the four fruits. Explaining this text now: "no characteristic" means without the characteristic of birth-and-death; "not a fixed characteristic" means without the characteristic of nirvāṇa either — nirvāṇa, too, being absent as a fixed characteristic — hence it is said "not a fixed characteristic, no characteristic," pointing to the middle way as true reality. "The two dharmas" are the sudden and the gradual: "the sudden" refers to all the dharmas within the sudden teaching of the Avataṃsaka; "the gradual" refers to all the dharmas of the Tripiṭaka, Vaipulya, and Prajñāpāramitā periods. "The three paths" are the three vehicles, and "the four fruits" are the arhat, the pratyekabuddha, the bodhisattva, and the buddha — all these various dharmas are called "immeasurable." True reality is the locus of meaning; from this one locus of meaning issue immeasurable dharmas, and thus the sūtra can serve, through its immeasurable dharmas entering into this one locus of meaning, as a prologue. It is like a master reckoner: from a single reckoning-unit he sets down all the other reckoning-counters, and by removing these counters he returns them to the single unit — because he sets them down from the one, he can likewise remove them back into the one; the setting-down is the prologue to the removing. From the one, he branches out into the many; gathering the many, he returns them to the one; the opening-out is the prologue to the closing-together — the meaning is just the same here. Explained in this way, this reading does not contradict that sūtra's own text and commentary, and it also accords with the present sūtra (and so on).

復次《無量義》讚偈，明法身百非洞遣，應為丈六紫金輝。《普賢觀》明常樂我淨四波羅蜜住處。前後兩文皆明常，豈有中間壽量而是無常耶？他難云：「序已說常，正何所道？」今反難之，《涅槃》以純陀是序，已開常宗，正何所道？他又例《淨名》序金剛無為無數，而正說不明常，《法華》亦應爾。今還反難之，純陀序常，《涅槃》正應無常，今論序常，正常何疑也？

Furthermore, the verses of praise in the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings make clear that the dharma-body, having thoroughly swept away the hundred negations, manifests as the sixteen-foot form radiant with purple-gold light. The Contemplation of Samantabhadra Sūtra makes clear the abode of the four pāramitās of permanence, bliss, self, and purity. Both the preceding and following texts make permanence clear — how, then, could the "Chapter on the Lifespan" in between possibly teach impermanence? Someone objects: "If the prologue has already spoken of permanence, what is left for the main teaching to say?" I now turn this objection back upon him: in the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, the story of Cunda serves as its prologue, and this has already opened up the doctrine of permanence — what, then, is left for its main teaching to say, by this same logic? He further cites, as a parallel, the prologue of the Vimalakīrti Sūtra, which speaks of the indestructible and the numberless, while its main teaching does not clarify permanence — and says the Lotus should be the same. I now turn this back upon him as well: if Cunda's prologue speaks of permanence, then, by his own logic, the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's main teaching ought to speak of impermanence — yet now this very prologue in our sūtra discusses permanence, and its main teaching too speaks of permanence — what doubt, then, can remain?

Reading note: This is one of the longest sustained scholarly debates in the fascicle — Zhiyi working through five or six named predecessors' rival explanations for why the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings, rather than any other earlier teaching, serves as the Lotus's own prologue, before offering his own reading rooted in that sūtra's account of "the one dharma" generating "immeasurable dharmas." Along the way he corrects the historical record on the sūtra's transmission to China — dating its arrival to 426 CE against an opponent's claim that its source text never reached "the land of Qin" — a rare moment where Zhiyi argues from textual history rather than doctrine alone. Ru-Yi Meditation Center has kept every named master's position in full rather than compressing them into a summary of "some say," since Zhiyi is answering each one specifically in turn.

「教菩薩法」者，無量義處用教菩薩也，義處即諦理也。下文：「普令一切眾，亦同得此道」又云：「若我遇眾生，盡教以佛道」即此意焉。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "the Dharma that teaches the bodhisattvas": it is by means of the locus of immeasurable meanings that the bodhisattvas are taught — "the locus of meaning" being the principle of the truths. Later in the text: "universally causing all the assembly likewise to attain this path," and again, "whenever I encounter sentient beings, I teach them all the way of the Buddha" — this is precisely this meaning.

「佛所護念」者，無量義處是佛自所證得，是故如來之所護念。下文云：「佛自住大乘也，雖欲開示，眾生根鈍，久默斯要，不務速說。」故言護念。

As for "protected and kept in mind by the Buddha": the locus of immeasurable meanings is precisely what the Buddha himself has realized, and it is for this reason that the Tathāgata protects and keeps it in mind. Later in the text it says: "The Buddha himself abides in the Great Vehicle... though wishing to open and reveal it, since the faculties of sentient beings are dull, he has long kept silent about this essential truth, and has not striven to preach it quickly" — hence it is called "protected and kept in mind."

Entering the Samādhi of Immeasurable Meanings 入無量義處三昧

Chinese Source

「佛說經已，入無量義處三昧」者，慧定相成，非禪不智，須先入定，非智不禪，故先說法。即智而定，即定而智，先後入出，無有隔礙。疑者云：「若未說無量義可入斯定，說此經已何故入定？」釋言：「先入此定，後說此經，可解。說此經竟而更入者，是為《法華》作序耳。何者？若不先開，則後無所合，先入開定為合定作序，稱為瑞相，即此義焉。若作次第者，先入無量義三昧已，應入法華三昧，若明文彰顯時眾則知，何俟彌勒殷勤、文殊靳固，故知作序其義轉明。」

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "having finished preaching the sūtra, the Buddha entered the samādhi of the locus of immeasurable meanings": wisdom and concentration bring one another to completion — without meditative concentration there is no wisdom, and one must first enter concentration; yet equally, without wisdom there is no true meditative concentration, and so the Buddha first preaches the Dharma. Wisdom is itself concentration, and concentration is itself wisdom; whether entered first or later, entering or emerging, there is no obstruction between them. One might doubt this, saying: "It would make sense to enter this samādhi before the Immeasurable Meanings had been preached — but having already finished preaching this sūtra, why does he then enter samādhi again?" The explanation is: "That he first enters this samādhi and only afterward preaches this sūtra is readily understood. But that, having finished preaching this sūtra, he enters samādhi once more — this is precisely what makes it the prologue to the Lotus. Why so? If the teaching of the three vehicles is not first opened up, there is nothing afterward for it to be merged back into; his first entering of the 'opening' samādhi serves as the prologue to the later 'merging' samādhi before the Lotus proper, and this is called an auspicious sign — this is precisely the meaning. If we speak in terms of sequence: having first entered the samādhi of Immeasurable Meanings, he should next enter the samādhi of the Lotus of the True Dharma; and were this made plainly evident in the text, the assembly at the time would simply have known it directly — what need, then, would there be for Maitreya's earnest inquiry and Mañjuśrī's firm reticence before answering? Hence it is known that, read as a prologue, its meaning becomes all the clearer."

「身心不動」者，與所緣之處相應也。身之本源湛若虛空，心之理性畢竟常寂，大通智勝身體及手足，寂然安不動，其心常憺怕，未曾有散亂。身若金剛不可動轉，心若虛空無有分別，無量義處三昧法持於身心故不動也。稱為無量者，此定寂而常，照能知世間，從此一法出無量法也。若作序義，身法體動運，今令不動運；心法體分別，今令不分別，序義明矣。

As for "body and mind unmoving": this accords with the object upon which the mind rests. The fundamental source of the body is limpid as empty space; the principle-nature of the mind is, in the end, ever tranquil. As with the body, torso, hands, and feet of the Buddha Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū, still and at peace, unmoving, his mind ever calm and unagitated, never once scattered or confused — so too here: the body is like vajra, incapable of being moved or altered, and the mind is like empty space, without any discriminating distinction; it is the dharma of the samādhi of the locus of immeasurable meanings that sustains body and mind, and hence they are unmoving. As for why it is called "immeasurable": this samādhi is both tranquil and constant, illuminating and able to know the world, and from this single dharma issue immeasurable dharmas. If we take up its meaning as prologue: the substance of the body's dharma is ordinarily to move and act, but now it is made unmoving and unacting; the substance of the mind's dharma is ordinarily to discriminate, but now it is made free of discrimination — and thus the meaning of "prologue" becomes clear.

問：
瑞相本論奇異，說法入定，佛之恒儀，何得為瑞？

Question: An auspicious sign, by its very definition, must be something unusual and extraordinary — but preaching the Dharma and entering samādhi are the Buddha's ordinary practice. How, then, can they count as signs?

答：
說法雖竟，時眾不散，肅有所待，故知前之說法，舉眾來集，待於後聞；此事奇特與常說異，何意非瑞？雖入開定，意在合定，與常入定有異，何意非瑞相耶？又文殊引古佛六瑞皆有此事，若昔非瑞相，何以證今？今古同然，豈可以凡情而非之耶？

Answer: Although the preaching of the Dharma had come to its end, the assembly of the time did not disperse, but remained solemnly waiting for something further — and from this it is known that the earlier preaching, and the assembly's gathering together, were themselves in expectation of what was to be heard afterward; this circumstance is extraordinary and differs from an ordinary preaching — how, then, could it fail to be a sign? Although the Buddha enters the "opening" samādhi, his intent lies in the "merging" samādhi that is to follow — this differs from an ordinary entering of samādhi — how, then, could it fail to be an auspicious sign? Moreover, when Mañjuśrī cites the six signs of the ancient buddha, this very circumstance is included among them; if it were not an auspicious sign in that ancient case, how could it serve as evidence for the present one? Since past and present accord with one another in this way, how could one possibly reject it merely on the basis of ordinary, unreflective sentiment?

The Rain of Four Flowers 天雨四花

Chinese Source

「天雨四花」者，舊云：「小、大白，小、大赤。」《正法華》云：「意花、大意花，浦嚮花、大浦嚮花。」《釋論》九十九云：「天花妙者，名曼陀羅。」又七十九云：「八百比丘成佛國土，常雨五色曼陀羅花。」舊雨小、大白，表在家二眾，小、大赤表出家二眾，表其昔來因而未果。今謂此解狹而不當，直論四眾，收三藏中十六眾尚不盡，況復四十八眾？是故為狹。夫花相密報其因，四眾昔來已是因，何俟花報？若報其果，天應雨實，何故雨花？故云不當。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "the heavens rained down four kinds of flowers": the old commentators say these are "the lesser and greater white, and the lesser and greater red" flowers. The Zhengfahua translation calls them "the mandārava and great mandārava, the mañjūṣaka and great mañjūṣaka." The Great Treatise, fascicle ninety-nine, says, "The wondrous heavenly flower is named mandārava." And fascicle seventy-nine says, "In the buddha-land where the eight hundred bhikṣus in question will become buddhas, it ever rains down mandārava flowers of five colors." The old commentators hold that the lesser and greater white flowers signify the two lay assemblies, and the lesser and greater red flowers signify the two monastic assemblies, signifying that their past causal practice has not yet borne fruit. Now, in my own view, this reading is too narrow and does not fit the case: to speak simply of "the fourfold assembly" does not even exhaust the sixteen assemblies within the Tripiṭaka teaching alone, let alone the forty-eight assemblies of all four teachings together — hence it is too narrow. Moreover, the mark of the flowers secretly recompenses their cause; but the fourfold assembly's conduct in the past was already itself the cause — what need, then, is there for the flowers to recompense it further? And if the flowers were to recompense their fruit instead, the heavens ought to rain down actual fruit — why, then, rain down flowers? Hence this reading, too, does not fit.

今言雨花，明其昔因非佛因，三藏中因是二乘因，通中因是共因，別中是菩薩因，皆非佛因。今天雨花，報其當獲佛因。佛因者，即四輪因也，小白表銅輪，習種性開佛知見也；大白表銀輪，性種性十行，示佛知見也；小赤表迦輪，道種性十迴向，悟佛知見也；大赤表琉璃輪，聖種性十地，入佛知見也。四輪皆同是因，是因由中而生，故從天而雨，由是因位故，以花表之。但因有趣果之義，故而散佛上。如此因果，誰當感剋？秖是此會時眾，故言「及諸大眾」也。下文殊釋疑，吹大法䗍等四句，又正說中開示悟入，又與大車中遊於四方，節節相承皆是位義，故知花表因位也。

Now I say that the raining of flowers makes clear that their past causes were not yet buddha-causes: within the Tripiṭaka teaching, the cause is the cause of the two lesser vehicles; within the shared teaching, it is a cause held in common with the two vehicles; within the distinct teaching, it is the bodhisattva's cause — none of these is yet the buddha-cause. Now the heavens rain down flowers to signify that they are about to obtain the buddha-cause. "The buddha-cause" is the cause of the four wheels of the cakravartin: the lesser white signifies the copper wheel, the nature cultivated through practice, opening the Buddha's knowledge and vision; the greater white signifies the silver wheel, the nature inherent in the ten practices, showing the Buddha's knowledge and vision; the lesser red signifies the golden wheel, the nature of the path in the ten transferences of merit, awakening to the Buddha's knowledge and vision; and the greater red signifies the crystal wheel, the noble nature of the ten grounds, entering the Buddha's knowledge and vision. All four wheels are equally causes, and, being causes, they arise from within — hence they rain down from the heavens; and because they belong to the stage of cause, they are signified by flowers. But since a cause carries within it the tendency toward its fruit, they are also scattered upon the Buddha himself. As for who among those present is capable of realizing such a cause and effect — it is simply this assembly gathered at this time, hence the text says "and the great assembly." Below, when Mañjuśrī resolves the doubt, he speaks of "blowing the great conch of the Dharma" and the other three phrases in his verse; and again, in the main teaching, "opening, showing, awakening, and entering"; and again, in the parable of the great cart traveling in the four directions — each of these, link by link, carries forward the same meaning of rank and stage, and hence it is known that the flowers signify the stage of cause.

問：
四輪是別位義，那得釋圓位耶？

Question: The four wheels belong to the ranks of the distinct teaching — how, then, can they be used to explain the ranks of the perfect teaching?

答：
名通義圓尚無所失，況名別義圓而不得用耶？

Answer: Even where the name is shared with another teaching but the meaning is perfect, there is still no fault in using it — how much less fault, then, when the name is merely distinct but the meaning is perfect, and yet may not be used at all?

問：
別義賢聖，圓亦有耶？

Question: Does the perfect teaching, too, have its own worthies and sages, corresponding to the meaning of the distinct teaching?

答：
已如《玄義》，若言四花俱從天雨，表四眾當同成一因，如此釋者，出三藏義未出通義；若言四眾同是菩薩因者，此釋出通義，未出別義；並非佛因，皆非《法華》意也。《法華》意如前說。

Answer: This has already been explained in the Xuanyi. If it is said that all four flowers rain down from heaven to signify that the fourfold assembly is about to together accomplish a single cause, such an explanation transcends the meaning of the Tripiṭaka teaching but does not yet transcend the meaning of the shared teaching; if it is said that the fourfold assembly's cause is alike the bodhisattva's cause, this explanation transcends the meaning of the shared teaching but does not yet transcend the meaning of the distinct teaching — and neither is yet the buddha-cause, so neither is the true intent of the Lotus. The true intent of the Lotus is as explained above.

The Sixfold Quaking of the Earth 地六種動

Chinese Source

「普佛世界地六種動」者，舊云動三乘人因果決定六執者，此破三藏家三乘六執，未破通教三乘六執。通教約法，三人因果同，若約人，三人因果異，此之同異俱被破，而舊家破意不破此也。別教無三乘名則無六執，舊所不破。今明別家因時三法縱橫，果時三法亦縱橫，此則須破。今釋地六種動，表圓家六番破無明，無明磬礴未曾侵毀，方將破壞，故動地以表之，無明若轉即變為明，故普佛世界六種震動也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "the universal buddha-worlds quaked in six ways": the old commentators say this shakes loose the six kinds of attachment to the fixed and determinate causes and effects of the practitioners of the three vehicles — but this only refutes the Tripiṭaka teaching's six attachments regarding the three vehicles, and does not yet refute the shared teaching's six attachments regarding the three vehicles. In the shared teaching, considered in terms of the dharma itself, the causes and effects of the three kinds of persons are the same; but considered in terms of the persons themselves, the causes and effects of the three kinds of persons differ — and both this sameness and this difference must be refuted, yet the old commentators' intent in refuting does not reach this far. The distinct teaching has no separate name for "three vehicles," and hence, in that scheme, there are no corresponding six attachments — this the old commentators leave unrefuted as well. I now explain that, within the distinct teaching, the three dharmas of cause and effect are both vertically and horizontally interrelated at the stage of cause, and likewise vertically and horizontally interrelated at the stage of effect — and this, too, must be refuted. I now explain that the earth's six-fold quaking signifies the perfect teaching's six-fold breaking through of ignorance: ignorance, massed and solid, has never yet been breached or destroyed, and is now about to be broken apart — hence the earth is made to quake, to signify this; and once ignorance is overturned, it is at once transformed into illumination — hence "the universal buddha-worlds quaked in six ways."

六種表住、行、向、地、等、妙六番也。《優婆塞清淨行經》云：「菩薩生時動地，示此生已盡無復煩惱，一切眾生應得道者，煩惱將滅故動。」即此義也。

The six kinds of quaking signify the six ranks of abiding, practice, transference of merit, ground, equal enlightenment, and sublime enlightenment. The Upāsaka-śīla Sūtra says, "When the bodhisattva was born, the earth quaked, showing that his births had come to their end and that no further affliction remained; for all sentient beings who are to attain the path, it is because their afflictions are about to be extinguished that the earth quakes" — this is precisely this meaning.

本迹解者，如文殊釋疑，引古佛為答，密得此意即是識本，非謂他佛昔現斯瑞，而我世尊本亦斯瑞，非今一反也(云云)。

As for the interpretation through trace and origin: just as Mañjuśrī, in resolving the doubt, cites the ancient buddha in answer, so one who secretly grasps this meaning thereby recognizes the origin — it is not that some other buddha once manifested this sign long ago while our own World-Honored One's origin likewise manifests this same sign only now, as a single unprecedented occurrence (and so on).

觀行者，動六根也，地相堅固如六根水執，未曾入大乘之道，動難動之地，表淨未淨之根。東涌西沒者，東方青主肝，肝主眼；西方白主肺，肺主鼻，此表眼根功德生、鼻根煩惱互滅，鼻根功德生、眼中煩惱互滅，餘方涌沒表餘根生滅亦復如是。六動者，動、起、涌、震、吼、覺，一一中又有三：謂動、遍動、等遍動。直動為動，四天下動為遍動，大千動為等遍動。餘五亦如是，合十八種動，此即表淨十八界也(云云)。

As for the interpretation through contemplative practice: this is the moving of the six sense-faculties. The characteristic of the earth's solidity is like the fixed attachment of the six faculties, which have never yet entered upon the path of the Great Vehicle; the moving of this hard-to-move earth signifies the purifying of the as-yet-unpurified faculties. As for "rising in the east and sinking in the west": in the east, the color blue governs the liver, and the liver governs the eye; in the west, the color white governs the lungs, and the lungs govern the nose — this signifies that as the merit of the eye-faculty arises, the affliction of the nose-faculty is thereby extinguished, and as the merit of the nose-faculty arises, the affliction within the eye is thereby extinguished; and the rising and sinking in the other directions likewise signify the arising and ceasing of the other faculties in just the same way. As for the six modes of quaking — moving, rising, surging, shaking, roaring, and awakening — each of these again has three degrees: simple motion, universal motion, and equally universal motion. A direct, simple motion is "moving"; motion throughout the four continents is "universal motion"; and motion throughout the great trichiliocosm is "equally universal motion." The other five modes are likewise each threefold, making eighteen kinds of motion in all — and this signifies the purifying of the eighteen sense-realms (and so on).

The Great Assembly's Joy 大眾心喜

Chinese Source

次明大眾心喜瑞者，眾見雨花地動，知甘露將降欣躍內充，表大機當發感於勝應。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the sign of the great assembly's inward joy: seeing the raining of flowers and the quaking of the earth, the assembly knows that the ambrosia of the Dharma is about to descend, and joy and exultation fill them within — signifying that their great capacity is about to be aroused, stirred by this sublime response.

問：
喜怒人之常情，何得為瑞？

Question: Joy and anger are ordinary human sentiments — how, then, can they count as a sign?

答：
天花悅眼，地動震心，《大經》云：「動時能令眾生心動。」花地是外瑞，心喜是內瑞，非常之喜，昔雖曾有，而不為喜所動，而能一心觀佛，何得非瑞？若言歡喜動陰心者，人天義也；若喜動真諦無漏心者，藏通義也；若喜動即假心者，別義也；喜動實相心者，圓義也。

Answer: The heavenly flowers delight the eye, and the quaking earth stirs the heart; the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says, "When the earth quakes, it is able to make the minds of sentient beings quake as well." The flowers and the earth's motion are external signs, while the heart's joy is an internal sign; this is no ordinary joy — though joy of some kind may have occurred before, in that past case the assembly was not moved by such joy as this while yet remaining single-mindedly focused on beholding the Buddha — how, then, could this fail to be a sign? If the joy that stirs is a joy that stirs the ordinary mind of the five aggregates, this is the meaning of gods and humans; if it is a joy that stirs the unafflicted mind of ultimate truth, this is the meaning of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings; if it is a joy that stirs the mind of the provisional itself, this is the meaning of the distinct teaching; and if it is a joy that stirs the mind of true reality, this is the meaning of the perfect teaching.

The Buddha's Emission of Light 佛放光瑞

Chinese Source

次明佛放光瑞，即表應機設教，破惑除疑。白毫具種種功德，《觀佛海三昧經》云：「佛初生時，牽長五尺，苦行時長一丈四尺，得佛時長一丈五尺，其毫中表俱空，如白琉璃筒，內外清淨。從初發心，中間行行種種相貌，乃至入涅槃，一切功德皆現毫中。」毫在二眉之間，即表中道常也，其相柔軟表樂，卷舒自在表我，白即表淨，放光破闇表中道生智慧，光照此土、他土表自覺、覺他。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the sign of the Buddha's emitting light: this signifies his responding to capacities by establishing teachings, breaking through delusion and dispelling doubt. The white tuft of hair between his brows is endowed with all manner of merit. The Sūtra on the Ocean-like Samādhi of Buddha-Visualization says: "When the Buddha was first born, this tuft, drawn out, measured five feet in length; during his ascetic practices it measured one zhang four feet; and upon attaining buddhahood it measured one zhang five feet. From his first arousing of the awakening mind, through all the various forms of practice along the way, down to his entering nirvāṇa, every merit of his entire path appears within this tuft of hair." The tuft lies between his two brows, signifying the constancy of the middle way; its softness of form signifies bliss; its freedom to coil and unfurl signifies self; its whiteness signifies purity; and its emitting of light to break through darkness signifies the middle way giving rise to wisdom, while its light illuminating this world and other worlds signifies awakening himself and awakening others.

復次二乘雖達二諦不知中道，如有二眉而無白毫，別教雖知三諦，不能毫中具一切法，當知從初至後，法界中事悉現毫內者，即表圓教之意。

Furthermore: the two vehicles, though they penetrate the two truths, do not know the middle way — like having two brows but no white tuft between them; the distinct teaching, though it knows the three truths, is not able to show every dharma contained within the tuft itself. One should understand that the fact that everything within the dharma-realm, from beginning to end, appears entirely within this single tuft of hair, is precisely what signifies the meaning of the perfect teaching.

復次，眾經明放光不同，《大品》從足下千輻輪相，乃至頂髻，一一各放六萬億光明，如彼廣說。《大經》面門放光，此經白毫放光，緣宜不同耳。又收光不同，《育王經》云：「收從背入，欲記過去事；收從前入，欲記未來事。」而不見記現在事，私謂脇入應記現在事也。足入記地獄，踝入記畜生，脚指入記鬼，膝入記人，左掌入記鐵輪王，右掌入記金輪王及記諸天，臍入記聲聞，口入記緣覺，白毫入記菩薩，肉髻入記佛。而今經放白毫光，而未見收光之文者，略耳。又解云，放光照現在事，收光明將來事，此經正論此土、他土諸佛道同，故正論放光。若解諸佛道同，即開示悟入，任運獲記則放光為正，收光是傍，故略而不說耳。若丈六佛放光者，三藏義也；若尊特佛與丈六佛共放光者，通義也；若尊特佛獨放光者，別義也；若丈六佛即毘盧遮那法身放光者，圓義也。

Furthermore, the various sūtras differ in how they describe the emitting of light. The Larger Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra has the Buddha emit light from the thousand-spoked wheel marks beneath his feet, and on up to his cranial protuberance, each place emitting sixty thousand koṭis of rays of light, as that sūtra explains at length. The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra has him emit light from between his brows, while this sūtra has him emit light from the white tuft — the circumstances calling for each simply differ. There is also a difference in how the light is drawn back in: the Aśokāvadāna says, "When the light is drawn back in through the back, this signifies matters of the past; when it is drawn back in through the front, this signifies matters of the future" — yet no mention is made of how matters of the present are signified; in my own view, drawing it in through the ribs ought to signify matters of the present. In that scheme, drawing it in through the feet signifies the hell-realm; through the ankles, the animal realm; through the toes, the hungry ghosts; through the knees, human beings; through the left palm, the iron-wheel kings; through the right palm, the golden-wheel kings and the gods; through the navel, the śrāvakas; through the mouth, the pratyekabuddhas; through the white tuft, the bodhisattvas; and through the fleshy cranial protuberance, the buddhas. Now, in the present sūtra, the Buddha emits light from the white tuft, but no text describing the light being drawn back in appears — this is simply an omission for brevity. There is also an explanation that says: the emitting of light illuminates matters of the present, while the drawing-in of light signifies matters yet to come; and since this sūtra is specifically concerned with showing that the buddhas of this world and of other worlds share the same path, it specifically discusses the emitting of light rather than its withdrawal. If one understands that the buddhas share the same path — that is, that all alike proceed through opening, showing, awakening, and entering, and spontaneously receive the same prediction — then the emitting of light is the primary matter and the drawing-in of light is secondary, and hence the text is abbreviated and does not speak of it. If it is the sixteen-foot manifested Buddha who emits the light, this is the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching; if it is the venerable and sublime Buddha together with the sixteen-foot Buddha who together emit the light, this is the sense of the shared teaching; if it is the venerable and sublime Buddha alone who emits the light, this is the sense of the distinct teaching; and if it is the sixteen-foot Buddha who is himself Vairocana, the dharma-body, emitting the light, this is the sense of the perfect teaching.

Reading note: The proposed system for "drawing the light back in" through different parts of the Buddha's body, each recording a different destiny or class of being, is offered by Zhiyi as his own private conjecture ("私謂") to fill a gap he notices in the Aśokāvadāna's scheme, which accounts for past and future but never says how the present would be signified. Ru-Yi Meditation Center has kept his hedge intact — he proposes it, then immediately concedes that this sūtra never actually uses it, since no text describing the light's withdrawal appears here at all.

Light Illuminating the Eastern Lands 照東方萬八千土

Chinese Source

舊云：「此土六瑞訖至膩吒天。」今尋文從照東方萬八千土下，即是他土六瑞之文，蓋斟酌由人耳。舊云：「實照十方，照東方者，表一乘因果是諸因果之上，萬是數圓表果位滿，八千數缺表因果未足，若照東方義已足，更照九方復何所表？」今明東是方始，表十住是位始，迹門說法，生身菩薩朗然見理，入於十住開佛知見。舉初即知中後，故云：「靡不周遍者」。當知諸方亦然，諸位亦然，若就本門說法，四方佛集即表本門說法，法身菩薩增道損生，四位增長也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The old commentators say, "The six signs of this world extend up through the Akaniṣṭha heaven." Now, examining the text, from "illuminating eighteen thousand lands to the east" onward is the text of the six signs of other worlds — though where exactly this boundary falls is a matter of individual judgment. The old commentators say: "In fact the light illuminates the ten directions; that it illuminates the east specifically, in the text, signifies that the one-vehicle cause and effect stands above all other causes and effects; 'ten thousand' is a round, complete number, signifying that the rank of effect is fulfilled, while 'eight thousand' falls short of a round number, signifying that cause and effect are not yet complete. If the illumination of the east already fully expresses this meaning, what further would illuminating the other nine directions signify?" I now explain that east is where direction itself begins, signifying that the ten abidings are the beginning of rank: in the trace-gate's preaching, physically-born bodhisattvas clearly behold the principle and enter the ten abidings, opening the Buddha's knowledge and vision. By citing the beginning, the middle and end are thereby known as well — hence it is said, "there was nowhere it did not pervade." One should understand that it is likewise so for the other directions, and likewise so for the other ranks. If, on the other hand, this is taken as the preaching of the origin-gate, then the gathering of buddhas from the four directions signifies the preaching of the origin-gate, in which dharma-body bodhisattvas increase the path and diminish further birth, growing through the four ranks.

觀解「萬八千」者，約十八界，論百法界、千性相，即有一萬八千，此等境界佛慧未開，今應當開，故以數表之耳。文云：「從阿鼻獄上至有頂」即六法界也。又見諸佛、菩薩、比丘等，十界具足，故文云：「靡不周遍」，即此意也。若分文屬此土第六相，若屬他土即是總相照他土文。

As for the interpretation through contemplation of "eighteen thousand": in terms of the eighteen sense-realms, and speaking of the hundred dharma-realms and thousand qualities-and-characteristics that each realm contains, one arrives at exactly eighteen thousand — these are the objects for which the Buddha's wisdom has not yet been opened, and which must now be opened, and hence they are signified by this number. The text says, "from the Avīci hell up to the Akaniṣṭha heaven" — this is the six dharma-realms; and it also sees buddhas, bodhisattvas, bhikṣus, and the rest — the full ten dharma-realms — hence the text says "there was nowhere it did not pervade," which is precisely this meaning. If this passage is assigned to this world, it forms the sixth sign of this world; if assigned to other worlds, it is the general statement heading the text on illuminating other worlds.

The Six Signs in Other Worlds 照他土六瑞

Chinese Source

次明光照他土六瑞者，一見六趣，二見諸佛，即是上聖下凡為一雙，三聞佛說法，四見四眾得道，即是人法一雙，五見菩薩行行，六見佛涅槃，即是始終一雙。既有可化眾生，即有能化之佛，有佛即有說法，說法即有弟子，弟子即是行始，行始必致終也。若此土六瑞，總報眾生當獲自覺；彼土六瑞，總報眾生當獲覺他。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the six signs of the light illuminating other worlds: first, seeing the six destinies; second, seeing the buddhas — these two together form the pair of the noble ones above and ordinary beings below. Third, hearing the buddhas preach the Dharma; fourth, seeing the fourfold assembly attain the path — these two together form the pair of persons and Dharma. Fifth, seeing the bodhisattvas' practice; sixth, seeing the buddhas enter nirvāṇa — these two together form the pair of beginning and end. Wherever there are sentient beings capable of being transformed by the teaching, there is a buddha capable of transforming them; where there is a buddha, there is the preaching of the Dharma; where there is the preaching of the Dharma, there are disciples; and disciples are the beginning of practice, and the beginning of practice must in the end arrive at its completion. If the six signs of this world, taken together, recompense sentient beings with the attainment of self-awakening, then the six signs of other worlds, taken together, recompense sentient beings with the attainment of awakening others.

又此彼六瑞，表此彼諸佛道同。從「盡見彼土六趣眾生」下，至「行菩薩道」者，是現彼土已與此同。從「復見諸佛」下，至「七寶塔」者，是現此土當與彼同。略說竟。

Moreover, the six signs here and there together signify that the buddhas here and there share the same path. From "beholding entirely the sentient beings of the six destinies in that other land" down to "practicing the bodhisattva path" shows that other land already matching this one; from "and further beholding the buddhas" down to "the stūpas of the seven treasures" shows this land as about to match that one. This concludes the brief explanation.

更廣說者，從又見六趣眾生，是現彼佛為五濁故出現於世，此佛亦然，二土出世意同也。及「聞諸佛所說經」者，是現彼佛初從無相一法非頓而頓，與此土初說《華嚴》意同也。從「并見諸比丘」下，是現彼佛非漸而漸，與此土佛次說三藏意同也。從「復見諸菩薩」下，是彼佛三藏之後，說方等、《般若》眾經，與此土佛三藏之後意同也。從「復見諸佛」下，至「起七寶塔」，是現彼佛般若之後開權顯實，收無量法還入一法，唱入涅槃息化起塔，光照彼土始終究竟炳然在目。當知此土從一出無量，非頓而頓、非漸而漸，其事已竟，必當收無量法還入一法，開權顯實、息化歸真，與彼土同也。

To explain this more fully: from "and further beholding the sentient beings of the six destinies" onward, this shows that buddha appearing in the world on account of the five turbidities, just as our own Buddha does — the intent behind both buddhas' appearance in their respective worlds being the same. As for "hearing the sūtras preached by the buddhas," this shows that buddha, at the outset, teaching from the single dharma of no characteristics, in a manner neither sudden nor yet sudden — the same in intent as this world's first preaching of the Avataṃsaka. From "and also seeing the bhikṣus" onward, this shows that buddha's teaching as neither gradual nor yet gradual — the same in intent as this world's Buddha next preaching the Tripiṭaka teaching. From "and further seeing the bodhisattvas" onward, this shows that buddha, after his Tripiṭaka period, preaching the many sūtras of the Vaipulya and Prajñāpāramitā periods — the same in intent as this world's Buddha after his own Tripiṭaka period. From "and further seeing the buddhas" down to "raising up stūpas of the seven treasures," this shows that buddha, after his Prajñāpāramitā period, opening the provisional to reveal the real, gathering the immeasurable dharmas back into the one dharma, and proclaiming his entry into nirvāṇa, ceasing his transformative activity and having stūpas raised — the light illuminating that other land shows its beginning and end, in their ultimate completeness, brilliantly manifest before the eyes. One should understand that this world, too, proceeds from the one to issue forth the immeasurable, neither sudden nor yet sudden, neither gradual nor yet gradual; and when its own course has run to completion, it too must gather the immeasurable dharmas back into the one dharma, opening the provisional to reveal the real, ceasing its transformative activity and returning to the true — the same as that other land.

Various Causes and Conditions 種種因緣

Chinese Source

復次種種因緣者，昔善為因，今教為緣。又別說者，正是三藏之後，明共不共般若為因，助道戒定慧等為緣。約三人即有種種因緣。又就共不共人種種因緣、種種相貌者，共不共各四門，一一門復有無量相貌，五百比丘各說身因，即其義也。不共四門亦如是，故知因緣、相貌種別無量，皆是彼與此同，彼明此相因緣相貌，還入一因、一緣、一相、一貌，當知此土亦與彼同。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, as for "various causes and conditions": past goodness serves as the cause, and the present teaching serves as the condition. Speaking of this more specifically: this refers precisely to the period after the Tripiṭaka teaching, where the shared and unshared teachings of the Prajñāpāramitā serve as cause, and the auxiliary practices of precepts, concentration, and wisdom serve as condition. Considered in terms of the three kinds of persons, there are accordingly various causes and conditions. And as for the various causes, conditions, and forms belonging to the shared and unshared persons: the shared and unshared teachings each have four gates, and within each gate there are again immeasurable forms — the five hundred bhikṣus each explaining the cause of their own present bodies is precisely an instance of this meaning. The four gates of the unshared teaching are likewise so. Hence it is known that causes, conditions, and forms are of countless different kinds, and yet all of these, there and here, are the same: that world makes clear this world's own forms of cause and condition, and these in turn are gathered back into a single cause, a single condition, a single form, and a single mark — and one should understand that this world, too, is the same as that one.

Maitreya's Doubt and the Question to Mañjuśrī 彌勒疑念與發問

Chinese Source

「爾時彌勒作是念」訖「今當問誰」，是疑念序，文為兩：一、彌勒疑念，二、大眾疑念。彌勒有三念：一、正念六瑞，二、念問誰，三、念文殊。文殊念起，第二念除，唯初念在，但成一疑也。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "At that time Maitreya conceived this thought" down to "whom shall I now ask" is the prologue of the arising of doubt. This text falls into two parts: first, Maitreya's doubt, and second, the great assembly's doubt. Maitreya has three thoughts: first, reflecting directly on the six signs; second, wondering whom to ask; and third, thinking of Mañjuśrī. Once the thought of Mañjuśrī arises, the second thought of whom to ask is thereby dispelled, leaving only the first thought remaining — and so only a single doubt is finally formed.

「神變」者，神，內也；變，外也。神名天心，即是天然內慧；變名變動，即是六瑞外彰。《首楞嚴》云：佛住不二法，能作神通，法王法力超蓋一切。彌勒不測外變，亦不知內慧，故興念至此，若夫庸人不知術者，散人不知定者，凡人不知聖者，小聖不知身子，身子不知菩薩，菩薩不知補處，補處不知尊極，此就極處亦不知也。又彌勒值佛植善既多，何容不髣髴知？應須隱明示闇，權言不知也。

As for "supernatural transformation": "supernatural" refers to the inward, and "transformation" refers to the outward. "Supernatural" is named for the heavenly mind, that is, the innate inward wisdom; "transformation" is named for change and motion, that is, the outward manifestation of the six signs. The Śūraṅgama Sūtra says, "The Buddha abides in the dharma of non-duality, and is thereby able to work supernatural powers; the power of the Dharma-king's Dharma surpasses and covers all things." Maitreya, unable to fathom the outward transformation nor to know the inward wisdom, is therefore moved to this thought. Just as an ordinary, mediocre person does not know the arts of one skilled in them, a scattered person does not know the state of one in concentration, an ordinary person does not know a noble one, a lesser noble one does not know Śāriputra, Śāriputra does not know a bodhisattva, a bodhisattva does not know one at the stage just prior to buddhahood, and one at the stage just prior to buddhahood does not know the venerable and ultimate Buddha — this pattern holds even at the most ultimate stage, where even that one does not know what lies beyond. Yet since Maitreya has, in meeting the Buddha, already planted a great abundance of wholesome roots, how could he possibly fail to have some faint intuition of this? He must, then, be concealing his clarity and displaying seeming obscurity, provisionally professing not to know.

大眾有兩念：一、正念六瑞，二、念問誰。若將下偈望此，亦得有三念，偈云：「四眾欣仰，瞻仁及我。」無第三念，何事瞻仁？而此中無者，欲推補處居先也。舊解先有三意：一、是補處，二、有三念，三、能發問。為此義故大眾闕一念也。

The great assembly has two thoughts: first, reflecting directly on the six signs, and second, wondering whom to ask. If we compare this with the verse that follows below, the assembly might also be said to have three thoughts, for the verse says, "The fourfold assembly gazes up in joyful reverence at both you and me" — suggesting a third thought, of Mañjuśrī, for otherwise, why would they gaze up at "you"? That this third thought is absent here, in the prose account of the assembly, is because the text wishes to give Maitreya, the one at the stage just prior to buddhahood, precedence in raising the question. The old commentators explain that there are, in advance, three considerations favoring Maitreya as questioner: first, that he is at the stage just prior to buddhahood; second, that he has all three thoughts; and third, that he alone is capable of posing the question — and it is for this reason that the great assembly is left lacking this one, third, thought in the narrative's own account of them.

問：
文殊、彌勒德位相亞，何故一問一答？

Question: Mañjuśrī and Maitreya are close to one another in virtue and rank — why, then, does one ask and the other answer?

答：
夫機有在無，位雖齊等，賓主異宜，聖人承機非問者不能答也。又法門有權實，權補處須問，實者須答。又迹有久近，近問久答。又名有便易，彌勒名慈，慈為眾生應須問；文殊名妙德，德應須答。此即四種消文意。◎

Answer: Now, capacity to grasp the teaching, in a given assembly, may be present or absent; though their ranks are on a par, host and guest have different fitting roles, and the noble one who receives and answers the assembly's capacity cannot do so except through one who poses the question. Furthermore, the dharma-gates have both the provisional and the real: the provisional one at the stage just prior to buddhahood must be the one to ask, and the real one must be the one to answer. Furthermore, traces may be of long or recent standing: the more recent one asks, and the more long-standing one answers. Furthermore, their names carry their own fittingness: Maitreya's name means "Compassionate," or "Loving-Kindness," and it is fitting that compassion, for the sake of sentient beings, should be the one to ask; Mañjuśrī's name means "Sublime Virtue," and it is fitting that virtue should be the one to answer. These are the four ways of resolving the meaning of the text.

Reading note: Zhiyi's closing move here is characteristic of his method throughout this fascicle: rather than settling on a single reason why Maitreya asks and Mañjuśrī answers, he offers all four of his interpretive lenses at once — capacity, the provisional-versus-real distinction, seniority of trace, and even simple etymology — as complementary rather than competing explanations. This passage brings the fascicle's coverage of the opening assembly and its six auspicious signs to a close; the text continues from here into "約教者" (an examination in terms of the teachings) of this same prologue material, which lies beyond the range of this translation.
