A Thousand Carts, One Rut

This page begins Ru-Yi's own English translation of Zhiyi's Fahua Wenju (妙法蓮華經文句, "Words and Phrases of the Lotus Sūtra"), his line-by-line commentary on the Lotus Sūtra — one of the three great Tiantai works on this single scripture, alongside the Fahua Xuanyi (妙法蓮華經玄義) and the Mohe Zhiguan already translated on this site. We translate slowly, section by section, with the Chinese source beside the English so readers can study the meaning, not merely read around it.

Status: fascicle 1 complete Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T34, no. 1718 Covers: the meaning of "prologue" and "chapter," earlier masters' schemes for dividing the sūtra, Zhiyi's own division and fourfold method, and the opening roll call of the assembly from Kauṇḍinya through Aniruddha and Kapphiṇa

Project Background

Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597 CE), the founder of the Tiantai school, delivered the Fahua Wenju as oral lectures late in his life; his disciple Guanding (灌頂) recorded and edited them into the ten-fascicle text preserved in the Taishō canon. Where the Fahua Xuanyi sets out the sūtra's overall doctrinal architecture and the Mohe Zhiguan builds an entire system of practice from principles the Lotus Sūtra supplies, the Wenju does something different and, in its own way, more demanding: it goes through the sūtra's actual words, phrase by phrase, glossing each one through Zhiyi's own fourfold method of interpretation — by causes and conditions, by the four teachings, by trace and origin, and by the contemplation of mind. This site already carries Ru-Yi's own translation of the Lotus Sūtra itself; the Wenju is Zhiyi's own running commentary on that same text, and this translation follows this site's established English renderings for the sūtra's chapter titles and recurring phrases wherever the Wenju quotes them.

This translation is made independently, directly from the Chinese source, without reference to any published English translation — the same principle already followed for Zhiyi's other treatises on this site, the Mohe Zhiguan and the Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen. Terminology is kept consistent with those two works and with the Xiao Zhiguan Jiangyi and Jiaoguan Gangzong — the latter of which quotes the Wenju repeatedly as an authority, making the two projects close companions.

Translation Method

  • Made directly from the Chinese source text preserved by CBETA, which is in the public domain — Zhiyi died in 597 CE, well over a millennium ago.
  • CBETA's edition splits each fascicle into "upper" and "lower" halves (之上 / 之下) for print-volume reasons; we treat both halves as one continuous fascicle, matching this site's existing convention for the Mohe Zhiguan and the Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen.
  • Zhiyi's own fourfold interpretive method — by causes and conditions (因緣), by the four teachings (約教), by trace and origin (本迹), and by the contemplation of mind (觀心) — recurs constantly throughout the Wenju and is kept visible in the translation exactly as Zhiyi presents it, term by term, rather than smoothed over.
  • The Wenju quotes short phrases from the sūtra directly, marked in the Chinese only by quotation marks, with Zhiyi's own gloss following immediately in the same breath. The translation preserves this same interleaved form rather than separating "sūtra text" from "commentary" into different columns.
  • Every completed section can be revised after review by Dharma teachers and readers familiar with the Chinese text.

Title Page and the Meaning of "Prologue" and "Chapter" 釋"序"、"品"二字

Chinese Source

妙法蓮華經文句卷第一上

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary on the Words of the Sūtra of the Lotus Blossom of the Wondrous Dharma, Fascicle One, Upper Part

天台智者大師說

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai

序品第一(佛出世難，佛說是難，傳譯此難，自開悟難，聞師講難，一遍記難。余二十七於金陵聽受，六十九於丹丘添削，留贈後賢，共期佛慧)

Chapter One: The Prologue. (For a Buddha to appear in the world is difficult; for the Buddha to preach is difficult; to transmit and translate this teaching is difficult; to awaken to it oneself is difficult; to hear a master expound it is difficult; to record it correctly after a single hearing is difficult. At twenty-seven I received this teaching by ear in Jinling; at sixty-nine I revised and polished it in Danqiu. I leave it as a gift to worthies of later ages, that together we may await the Buddha's wisdom.)

委釋經題已如上說。「序」者，訓庠序，謂階位、賓主、問答悉庠序也。經家從義，謂次、由、述也。如是等五事冠於經首，次序也；放光六瑞起發之端，由序也；問答釋疑，正說弄引，敘述也。

A detailed exegesis of the sūtra's title has already been given above. As for "prologue," it is glossed as "orderly sequence" — meaning that ranks and positions, host and guest, question and answer, are all set forth in proper order. The sūtra's compilers follow this sense, taking it to mean "sequence," "occasion," and "narration." The five items — such as the words "Thus have I heard" — placed at the head of the sūtra constitute its "sequence"; the emitting of light and the six auspicious signs that set the exposition in motion constitute its "occasion"; the questions and answers that resolve doubt, and the introductory passages that lead into the main teaching, constitute its "narration."

具此三義，故稱為序。「品」者，《中阿含》云跋渠，此翻為品。品者，義類同者聚在一段，故名品也。或佛自唱品，如《梵網》；或結集所置，如《大論》；或譯人添足，如羅什。今〈藥王本事〉是佛唱。〈妙音〉、〈觀音〉等是經家、譯人未聞。諸品之始，故言第一。

Having all three of these senses, it is called "prologue." As for "chapter," the Madhyama Āgama calls it baqu, which is translated as "chapter." A chapter is a passage in which items of the same category and meaning are gathered together — hence it is called a chapter. Sometimes the Buddha himself announced the chapter division, as in the Brahmajāla Sūtra; sometimes it was set by the compilers, as in the "Great Treatise" (the Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra); sometimes it was supplied by the translator, as with Kumārajīva. Here, the chapter "The Past Deeds of the Medicine King" was announced by the Buddha himself; whether chapters such as "Wonderful Sound" and "Guanyin" were named by the sūtra's compilers or by the translator is not known. Since this is the first of all the chapters, it is called "Chapter One."

How Earlier Masters Divided the Sūtra 古德科經之異說

Chinese Source

佛赴緣作散花、貫花兩說，結集者按說傳之，論者依經申之，皆不節目。古講師但敷弘義理、不分章段，若純用此意，後生殆不識起盡。又佛說貫、散，集者隨義立品。《增一》云：契經一分，律一分，阿毘曇一分。契經更開四，謂《增一》、《長》、《中》、《雜》。《增一阿含》明人天因果，《長阿含》破邪見，《中阿含》明深義，《雜阿含》明禪定。律開五部及八十誦。阿毘曇開《六足》、《八犍度》等。《阿含》謂：「施、戒、慧、六度皆足也，謂根、性、道、定等八種聚也。」天親作論，以七功德分〈序品〉，五示現分〈方便品〉，其餘品各有處分。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the Buddha, responding to conditions, preached, he did so in two modes — "scattered blossoms" and "strung blossoms" — and those who compiled the canon transmitted his words accordingly, while the treatise-masters expounded them following the sūtra text; in neither case were section divisions originally marked. The lecture-masters of old simply expounded the meaning broadly without dividing the text into chapters and sections; if we relied solely on that approach, students of later generations would scarcely be able to tell where a passage began and ended. Moreover, since the Buddha preached in both the "strung" and "scattered" modes, those who compiled the texts established chapter divisions according to the sense of the content. The Ekottarika [Āgama] says: one part is the sūtra proper, one part is the vinaya, one part is the abhidharma. The sūtra portion is further divided into four: the Ekottarika, the Dīrgha, the Madhyama, and the Saṃyukta. The Ekottarika Āgama expounds the causes and effects governing rebirth among humans and gods; the Dīrgha Āgama refutes false views; the Madhyama Āgama expounds profound principles; the Saṃyukta Āgama expounds meditative concentration. The vinaya is divided into the five schools' recensions and the eighty recitations. The abhidharma is divided into the Six Feet, the Eight Categories, and so forth. The Āgamas hold that "giving, precepts, and wisdom, and the six perfections, are all complete within them" — meaning the eight groupings such as faculties, natures, paths, and concentrations. Vasubandhu, in composing his treatise on the Lotus Sūtra, divided the "Prologue" chapter according to seven merits, and the "Skillful Means" chapter according to five manifestations, with each of the remaining chapters likewise assigned its own disposition.

昔河西憑、江東瑤，取此意節目經文，末代尤煩。光宅轉細，重雰翳於太清，三光為之戢耀，問津者所不貴。曇鸞云：「細科煙颺，雜礪塵飛。」蓋若過、若不及也。

In the past, Feng of Hexi and Yao of Jiangdong, following this approach, marked off sections in the sūtra text, and by the end of that age their schemes had grown especially elaborate. Guangzhai [Fayun] made the divisions even finer still — like a heavy mist obscuring the great clear sky, so that the light of the sun, moon, and stars was dimmed by it — something those who sought the ford did not prize. Tanluan said: "Fine sub-sections rise like smoke, mixed grindings scatter like dust" — meaning that such schemes err either by excess or by deficiency.

廬山龍師，分文為序、正、流通，二十七品統唯兩種：從〈序〉至〈法師〉，言方便、言真實，理一說三故；〈寶塔〉下，身方便、身真實，實遠唱近故。又從〈方便〉至〈安樂行〉是因門，從〈踊出〉下是果門。齊中興印、小山瑤，從龍受經，分文同。

Elder Long of Mount Lu divided the text into prologue, main teaching, and dissemination, and held that the sūtra's twenty-seven chapters fall under just two categories overall: from the "Prologue" chapter through the "Teacher of the Dharma" chapter, the Buddha speaks of the provisional and speaks of the true, because though the principle is one, it is taught as three vehicles; from "The Jeweled Stūpa" chapter onward, his body is provisional and his body is true, because the Buddha who is actually distant proclaims himself as near. He also held that from "Skillful Means" through "Peaceful Practices" constitutes the "cause gate," and from "Emerging from the Earth" onward constitutes the "effect gate." Yin of Zhongxing in Qi and Yao of Xiaoshan, having received the sūtra from [Huiyuan of Mount] Lu's lineage, divided the text the same way.

玄暢從〈序〉至〈多寶〉為因分，從〈勸持〉至〈神力〉為果分，從〈囑累〉盡經為護持分。又有師云：「從〈序〉至〈學無學人記〉，是《法華》體；從〈法師〉至〈囑累〉，明受持功德；從〈藥王〉盡經，美諸菩薩本願。」

Xuanchang held that from "Prologue" through "Many Jewels" forms the "cause section," from "Encouraging Upholders" through "Supernatural Powers" forms the "effect section," and from "Entrustment" to the end of the sūtra forms the "section on upholding and protecting the Dharma." Another master held: "From 'Prologue' through 'Prediction for Those Still Learning and Beyond Learning' constitutes the essential body of the Lotus; from 'Teacher of the Dharma' through 'Entrustment' expounds the merit of upholding the sūtra; from 'Medicine King' to the end of the sūtra extols the bodhisattvas' original vows."

有師作四段：初品為序段；從〈方便〉至〈安樂行〉，開三顯一段；從〈踊出〉訖〈分別功德〉，開近顯遠段；後去餘勢，流通段。光宅雲從印受經，初三段次各開二，謂通序、別序，正謂因門、果門，流通謂化他、自行。二序各五，二正各四，二流通各三，合二十四段(云云)。

Another master made a fourfold division: the opening chapter is the "prologue section"; from "Skillful Means" through "Peaceful Practices" is the section of "opening the three to reveal the one"; from "Emerging from the Earth" to "Discrimination of Merits" is the section of "opening the near to reveal the far"; and everything thereafter, the remaining momentum of the teaching, is the "dissemination section." Guangzhai [Fayun], having received the sūtra from Yin, divided it into these same first three sections, then further subdivided each into two: the prologue into a "general prologue" and a "specific prologue"; the main teaching into a "cause gate" and an "effect gate"; and the dissemination into "converting others" and "one's own practice." The two prologues have five sub-divisions each, the two main-teaching sections have four each, and the two dissemination sections have three each, making twenty-four sections in all (and so on).

夫分節經文悉是人情，蘭菊各擅其美，後生不應是非諍競，無三益、喪一道。三益者，世界等三悉檀也；一道者，第一義悉檀也。

Now, the parsing and sectioning of a sūtra's text is entirely a matter of human judgment — the orchid and the chrysanthemum each excel in their own beauty. Students of later generations ought not to wrangle over which is right and which is wrong, lest they gain none of the "three benefits" and lose the "one path." The three benefits are the three siddhāntas beginning with the mundane siddhānta; the one path is the siddhānta of ultimate meaning.

Reading note: The teachers surveyed in this passage are fifth- and sixth-century exegetes of the Lotus Sūtra whose competing schemes for dividing the text Zhiyi is here cataloguing before setting out his own. Feng of Hexi and Yao of Jiangdong are early, now largely lost, lecture-masters. Guangzhai refers to Fayun (467–529), the celebrated Liang-dynasty commentator based at Guangzhai Monastery in the capital, whose Lotus Sūtra commentary was hugely influential in the south. Tanluan (476–542), later revered as a Pure Land patriarch, is quoted here only for a passing critical remark about over-fine sectioning. Elder Long of Mount Lu represents the exegetical line descending from Huiyuan of Mount Lu (334–416); Yin of Zhongxing and Yao of Xiaoshan were later Qi-dynasty lecturers within that same lineage. Xuanchang (416–484) was a prominent Liu Song and Southern Qi–era vinaya and Lotus Sūtra scholar. Zhiyi surveys all of them only to set them aside — his own threefold and twofold schemes follow in the next section.

Zhiyi's Own Division and His Fourfold Interpretive Method 智者三分二門與四意消文

Chinese Source

天台智者分文為三：初品為序；〈方便品〉訖〈分別功德〉十九行偈，凡十五品半，名正；從偈後盡經，凡十一品半，名流通。又一時分為二：從序至〈安樂行〉十四品，約迹開權顯實；從〈踊出〉訖經十四品，約本開權顯實。本迹各序、正、流通。初品為序，〈方便〉訖〈授學無學人記品〉為正，〈法師〉訖〈安樂行〉為流通。〈踊出〉訖「彌勒已問斯事佛今答之」半品，名序；從「佛告阿逸多」下，訖〈分別功德品〉偈，名為正；此後盡經為流通。今記從前三段消文也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai divided the text into three: the opening chapter is the prologue; from the "Skillful Means" chapter through the nineteen verses of "Discrimination of Merits" — fifteen and a half chapters in all — is called the main teaching; from after those verses to the end of the sūtra — eleven and a half chapters in all — is called the dissemination. At the same time, he also divided the sūtra into two: the fourteen chapters from the Prologue through "Peaceful Practices" expound, in terms of the trace gate, the opening of the provisional to reveal the true; the fourteen chapters from "Emerging from the Earth" to the end of the sūtra expound, in terms of the origin gate, the opening of the provisional to reveal the true. Each of the trace gate and the origin gate has its own prologue, main teaching, and dissemination. Within the trace gate: the opening chapter is the prologue; from "Skillful Means" through "Prediction for Those Still Learning and Beyond Learning" is the main teaching; from "Teacher of the Dharma" through "Peaceful Practices" is the dissemination. Within the origin gate: from "Emerging from the Earth" to the half-chapter ending at "Maitreya has already asked about this matter and the Buddha now answers it" is called the prologue; from "The Buddha said to Ajita" onward, through the verses of "Discrimination of Merits," is called the main teaching; everything after this to the end of the sūtra is the dissemination. This present commentary follows these three initial sections in construing the text.

問：一經云何二序？

答：《華嚴》處處集眾，《阿含》篇篇如是，《大品》前後付囑，皆不乖一部，兩序阿妨？

今不安五義者，本門非次首故也。迹門但單流通者，說法未竟也，有無之意云爾。今帖文為四：一、列數；二、所以；三、引證；四、示相。

Question: How can a single sūtra have two prologues?

Answer: The Avataṃsaka Sūtra assembles its audience anew in place after place; the Āgamas do likewise, chapter by chapter; the "Great Perfection of Wisdom" entrusts its teaching repeatedly from beginning to end — in none of these cases does this conflict with their being a single text, so what objection could there be to two prologues within one sūtra?

As for why the origin gate's prologue does not include all five standard items, it is because it does not stand at the very head of the sūtra. As for why the trace gate has only a dissemination section by itself, it is because the exposition of the Dharma is not yet complete there — this is the meaning of "having" and "not having" such elements. The present commentary now glosses the text under four headings: first, listing the number of readings; second, the reason for using four; third, textual proof; fourth, demonstrating their application.

列數者：一因緣、二約教、三本迹、四觀心。始從「如是」終于「而退」，皆以四意消文，而今略書，或三、二、一，貴在得意不煩筆墨。

As for listing the number: first, causes and conditions; second, in terms of the teachings; third, trace and origin; fourth, contemplation of mind. From the opening words "Thus have I heard" to the closing words "and withdrew," the text will be construed throughout by these four approaches, though for brevity I will sometimes give only three, two, or even one of them — what matters is grasping the meaning, not burdening the reader with excess ink.

二、所以者，問：若略則一，若廣匪四，所以云何？

答：廣則令智退，略則意不周，我今處中說，令義易明了。因緣亦名感應，眾生無機雖近不見，慈善根力遠而自通，感應道交故，用因緣釋也。夫眾生求脫，此機眾矣；聖人起應，應亦眾矣。此義更廣，處中在何？然《大經》云：「慈善根力有無量門，略則神通，若十方機感，曠若虛空。」今論娑婆國土音聲佛事則甘露門開，依教釋者中說明矣。若應機設教，教有權實、淺深不同，須置指存月、亡迹尋本。故肇師云：「非本無以垂迹，非迹無以顯本。」故用本迹釋也。若尋迹，迹廣徒自疲勞，若尋本，本高高不可極，日夜數他寶，自無半錢分，但觀己心之高廣，扣無窮之聖應，機成致感，逮得己利，故用觀心釋也。

Second, as to the reason: Question: If brevity means using just one approach, and thoroughness would require more than four, why settle on four?

Answer: Too much detail causes the understanding to falter; too little leaves the meaning incomplete. I now speak from a middle course, so that the meaning may be easily grasped. Causes and conditions are also called "stimulus and response": though sentient beings without the capacity to receive the teaching may be near yet fail to see it, the power of the Buddha's compassionate roots of goodness reaches even the far and spontaneously connects with them — because stimulus and response intersect on the same path, this reading uses "causes and conditions." Now, the beings who seek liberation are this "capacity," and they are many; the sage who rouses himself to respond does so in manifold ways as well — this topic could be expanded much further, so where does the middle course lie? Yet the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The power of the compassionate roots of goodness has innumerable gateways; in brief, it is the Buddha's supernatural powers; as for the capacities and responses throughout the ten directions, they are as boundless as empty space." Here, in discussing how, in this Sahā world, the Buddha's work is carried out through sound, the gate of ambrosia opens — this reading, following the teaching, is made clear in the section "in terms of the teachings." As for adapting the teaching to the capacity of the hearer, the teachings differ in being provisional or true, shallow or deep, and one must therefore set aside the pointing finger to keep the moon, and forget the trace in order to seek the origin. Hence Sengzhao says: "Without the origin there would be nothing to manifest as trace; without the trace there would be no way to reveal the origin" — and so this reading uses "trace and origin." If one merely pursues the trace, the trace is vast and one only wearies oneself in vain; if one pursues the origin, the origin is lofty beyond all reckoning — like counting another's treasure day and night while possessing not half a coin oneself. One need only contemplate the loftiness and vastness of one's own mind, and strike upon the sage's boundless response; when the capacity matures it draws forth the response, and one attains one's own true benefit — hence this reading uses "contemplation of mind."

三、引證。〈方便品〉云：「十方諸佛為一大事因緣故出現於世。」若人天、小乘，非一、非大，又非佛事，不成機感。實相名一，廣博名大，佛指此為事，出現於世，是名一大事因緣也。又云：「以種種法門，宣示於佛道。」當知種種聲教，若微若著、若權若實，皆為佛道而作筌罤，《大經》云：「麁言及軟語，皆歸第一義。」此之謂也。〈壽量品〉云：「今天人阿修羅，皆謂我少出家，出釋氏宮，去伽耶城不遠，得三菩提。然我實成佛已來，無量無邊阿僧祇劫，以斯方便導利眾生。」〈方便品〉又云：「我本立誓願，普令一切眾，亦同得此道，如我等無異。」又〈五百受記品〉云：「內祕菩薩行，外現是聲聞，實自淨佛土，示眾有三毒，又現邪見相；我弟子如是，方便度眾生。」此則師弟皆明本迹(云云)。〈譬喻品〉云：「若人信汝所說，即為見我亦見於汝，及比丘僧并諸菩薩。」當知隨有所聞，諦心觀察，於信心中得見三寶：聞說是法寶，見我是佛寶，見汝等是僧寶(云云)。

Third, textual proof. The "Skillful Means" chapter says: "The buddhas of the ten directions appear in the world for the sake of the one great cause and condition." Were this concerned with humans and gods, or with the Hīnayāna, it would be neither "one" nor "great," nor would it be the Buddha's own work — it would not constitute the meeting of capacity and response. "One" names ultimate reality; "great" names its vastness; the Buddha points to this as the "cause," and appears in the world for its sake — this is called "the one great cause and condition." It further says: "Through various dharma-gates, I proclaim and reveal the Buddha path." One should understand that the various forms of vocal teaching — whether subtle or overt, provisional or true — all serve as a fish-trap or rabbit-snare for the sake of the Buddha path; the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Coarse words and gentle words alike return to the ultimate meaning" — this is what is meant. The "Lifespan" chapter says: "Now the gods, humans, and asuras all say that I left my household recently, went forth from the palace of the Śākya clan, and, not far from the city of Gayā, attained complete awakening. Yet in truth, since I attained buddhahood, immeasurable, boundless asaṃkhyeya kalpas have passed, and it is by this skillful means that I have been guiding and benefiting sentient beings." The "Skillful Means" chapter also says: "I originally made a vow that I would cause all beings universally to attain this same path, equal to my own, without difference." The "Prediction for the Five Hundred" chapter also says: "Secretly practicing the bodhisattva conduct while outwardly manifesting as a śrāvaka, in reality purifying their own buddha-lands, they show to the assembly the appearance of the three poisons, and even manifest wrong views; my disciples are like this — it is skillful means for saving beings." This shows both teacher and disciples alike making clear their origin and trace (and so on). The "Parable" chapter says: "If a person believes what you say, this is the same as seeing me, as well as seeing you and the assembly of monks and the bodhisattvas." One should understand that whenever something is heard, if one examines it with a sincere mind, one attains, within that faith, a vision of the Three Jewels: hearing the teaching expounded is seeing the Dharma Jewel; seeing me is seeing the Buddha Jewel; seeing you disciples is seeing the Saṃgha Jewel (and so on).

四、示相者，且約三段示因緣相。眾生久遠蒙佛善巧，令種佛道因緣，中間相值，更以異方便助顯第一義而成熟之。今日雨花動地，以如來滅度而滅度之。

復次，久遠為種，過去為熟，近世為脫，地涌等是也。

復次，中間為種，四味為熟，王城為脫，今之開示悟入者也。復次，今世為種，次世為熟，後世為脫，未來得度者是也。雖未是本門，取意說耳。其間節節作三世九世，為種、為熟、為脫，亦應無妨。何以故？如來自在神通之力，師子奮迅大勢威猛之力，自在說也。以如是等故，有序分也。眾見希有瑞，顒顒欽渴，欲聞具足道，佛乘機設化，開示悟入佛之知見，故有正說分也。非但當時獲大利益，後五百歲遠沾妙道，故有流通分也。

Fourth, as to demonstrating their application: let us first, in terms of the three sections, demonstrate the application of the "causes and conditions" reading. Sentient beings, in the far distant past, received the Buddha's skillful guidance, which caused the seed of the cause and condition for the Buddha path to be planted in them; meeting the Buddha again in the intervening time, he further employed various skillful means to help reveal the ultimate meaning and bring them to maturity. Today, as flowers rain down and the earth trembles, he liberates them through his own parinirvāṇa, causing them likewise to attain liberation.

Furthermore: the distant past is the "planting," the more recent past is the "ripening," and the present age is the "harvesting" — this is what is meant by the bodhisattvas "emerging from the earth" and the like.

Furthermore: the intervening time is the "planting," the four flavors of the teaching are the "ripening," and what occurs at Rājagṛha is the "harvesting" — this is what is meant, in the present sūtra, by "opening, showing, awakening to, and entering" the Buddha's knowledge and vision. Furthermore: the present age is the "planting," the next age is the "ripening," and the age after that is the "harvesting" — this refers to those who will attain liberation in the future. Although this is not yet an application to the origin gate, it is offered here to convey the general sense. Within this span, section by section, one may equally speak of three ages or nine ages as the "planting," "ripening," and "harvesting," and there should be no objection to this. Why is this so? Because it is spoken freely by virtue of the Tathāgata's power of unimpeded spiritual mastery, and the awesome, forceful power of his lion's vigorous stride. Because of all this, there is a prologue section. The assembly, seeing the rare and auspicious signs, gazed up in eager anticipation, longing to hear the complete path; the Buddha, according to the capacity of his listeners, set forth his teaching, opening, showing, awakening to, and causing entry into the Buddha's knowledge and vision — hence there is a main-teaching section. And since the benefit gained was not confined to that time alone, but would reach, five hundred years later, those far removed who would still be touched by this wondrous path, there is a dissemination section.

又示教相者，此序非為人天清升作序，非為二乘小道作序，不為即空通三作序，不為獨菩薩法作序，乃為正直捨方便、但說無上佛道作序耳。此正不指世間為正，不指螢光析智為正，不指燈炬體法智為正，不指星月道種智為正，乃指日光一切種智為正。此流通非為楊葉木牛木馬而作流通，非流通半字，非流通共字，非流通別字，純是流通圓滿修多羅滿字法也。

Further, to demonstrate the "teachings" aspect: this prologue is not a prologue for the pure ascent of humans and gods; it is not a prologue for the lesser path of the two vehicles; it is not a prologue for the shared teaching of emptiness held in common with the three vehicles; nor is it a prologue solely for the bodhisattva's own dharma — rather, it is a prologue for the Buddha's act of rightly and directly setting aside skillful means and preaching only the unsurpassed Buddha path. This "main teaching" does not point to the mundane world as its content; it does not point to the fragmentary wisdom likened to a firefly's glow; it does not point to the wisdom that comprehends dharmas, likened to a torch's flame; it does not point to the wisdom of the paths and their seeds, likened to the stars and moon — rather, it points to the wisdom of all modes, likened to the light of the sun. This "dissemination" is not the dissemination of a willow leaf, a wooden ox, or a wooden horse; it is not the dissemination of half-words, nor of words shared in common with the three vehicles, nor of separate words alone — it is purely the dissemination of the perfect and complete sūtra, the teaching in its full and complete words.

次示本迹者，久遠行菩薩道時，宣揚先佛《法華經》，亦有三分上中下語，亦有本迹，但佛佛相望是則無窮，別取最初成佛時所說《法華》三分上中下語，專名為上，名之為本。何以故？最初成佛，初說法故，為上為本，此意可知；中間行化，助大通智勝、然燈等佛，宣揚《法華》三分者，但名為中，但名為迹。何以故？前有上故，前有本故。今日王城所說三分，但名為下，但名為迹，乃至師子奮迅之力，未來永永所說三分，亦指最初為上為本。譬如大樹雖有千枝萬葉，論其根本，不得傳傳相指同宗一根，此喻可解(云云)。

Next, to demonstrate the "trace and origin" reading: in the distant past, when the one who is now Śākyamuni was practicing the bodhisattva path, he proclaimed the Lotus Sūtra of earlier buddhas, and that too had three sections — upper, middle, and lower — and likewise had its own origin and trace. But since buddha looks back to buddha without end, if we single out the three sections of the Lotus Sūtra as preached at the time of his very first attainment of buddhahood, these alone are properly called "upper" and are called the "origin." Why is this? Because it was at his very first attainment of buddhahood, his very first preaching of the Dharma, that it is "upper" and "origin" — this meaning may be understood. As for the intervening ages, when he practiced and taught, assisting buddhas such as Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū and Dīpaṃkara, proclaiming the three sections of the Lotus, these are called only "middle," called only "trace." Why is this? Because there was an "upper" before them, because there was an "origin" before them. As for the three sections preached today at Rājagṛha, these are called only "lower," called only "trace" — and indeed, however far into the endless future the three sections may be preached by the power of the lion's vigorous stride, these too point back to that very first attainment as the "upper" and the "origin." It is like a great tree: though it has a thousand branches and ten thousand leaves, when one traces its root, one cannot go on tracing from branch to branch without eventually pointing to a single common root of one ancestral trunk. This analogy may be understood (and so on).

次示觀心相者，當約己心論戒、定、慧為三分，修行以戒初、定中、慧後；若法門以慧為本，定、戒為迹。又戒、定、慧各各作三分，前方便白四羯磨結竟，為戒三分；二十五方便正觀歷緣，又善入出住百千三昧等為定三分；因緣所生法，即空、即假、即中為慧三分。

Next, to demonstrate the "contemplation of mind" reading: one should, with reference to one's own mind, discuss precepts, concentration, and wisdom as the three sections — in the order of practice, precepts come first, concentration in the middle, and wisdom last; but if one speaks in terms of the dharma-gate itself, wisdom is the origin, and concentration and precepts are the trace. Furthermore, precepts, concentration, and wisdom can each be divided into their own three sections: the preliminary expedients through the completion of the fourfold announcement and motion form the three sections of precepts; the twenty-five preliminary expedients, correct contemplation applied to one's circumstances, and skill in entering, emerging from, and abiding in the hundreds and thousands of samādhis, form the three sections of concentration; and dharmas arising from causes and conditions, contemplated as empty, as provisional, and as the middle, form the three sections of wisdom.

已約三分示四種相，當用此義。從「如是」去、至「作禮而退」已還，悉作四意消文。但準望此義比知則易，分別顯示其辭則難，行者善思量之，語異意同，千車共轍、萬流醎會者也。

Having, in terms of the three sections, demonstrated the four modes of application, this is the sense that should be employed throughout. From "Thus" onward, all the way to "made obeisance and withdrew," the text will everywhere be construed by these four approaches. If one measures against this general sense, comparison and understanding come easily; but to articulate and display it in words is difficult — let the practitioner reflect well upon it. Though the phrasing may differ, the meaning is the same, like a thousand carts sharing one rut, or ten thousand streams alike returning to the salt sea.

The Fourfold Exegesis of the Opening Words "如是我聞"等四意消文

Chinese Source

「如是」者，舉所聞之法體。「我聞」者，能持之人也。「一時」者，聞持和合，非異時也。「佛」者，時從佛聞也。「王城耆山」，聞持之所也。「與大比丘」者，是聞持之伴也。此皆因緣和合，次第相生。

又「如是」者，三世佛經初皆安「如是」，諸佛道同不與世諍，世界悉檀也；《大論》云：「舉時方令人生信者」，為人悉檀也；又對破外道阿歐二字不如、不是，對治悉檀也；又如是者，信順之辭，信則所聞之理會，順則師資之道成，即第一義悉檀也。因緣釋甚廣，不能具載(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Thus" points to the substance of the Dharma that was heard. "I heard" names the person who retained it. "At one time" means that the hearing and the retaining occurred together in harmony, not at different times. "The Buddha" means that it was from the Buddha, at that time, that it was heard. "On Vulture Peak at Rājagṛha" is the place of hearing and retaining. "Together with the great bhikṣus" names the companions in hearing and retaining. All of these are the harmonious coming together of causes and conditions, arising one after another in sequence.

Furthermore, as for "thus": the sūtras of the buddhas of the three times all place "thus" at their opening, showing that the buddhas' path is the same and does not contend with the world — this is the mundane siddhānta. The "Great Treatise" (the Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra) says, "citing the time and place so as to cause people to give rise to faith" — this is the individual siddhānta. Furthermore, it counters and refutes the non-Buddhists' two syllables "a" and "ou," used to mean "not-thus" and "not-so" — this is the therapeutic siddhānta. And again, "thus" is a term of faith and compliance: through faith, the principle that was heard is comprehended; through compliance, the way between teacher and disciple is accomplished — this is the siddhānta of ultimate meaning. The "causes and conditions" exegesis is extremely extensive and cannot be given in full here (and so on).

約教釋者，經稱三世佛法初皆如是，先佛有漸、頓、祕密、不定等經，漸又三藏、通、別、圓；今佛亦爾，諸經不同，如是亦異，不應一匙開於眾戶。又佛、阿難二文不異為如，能詮詮所詮為是。今阿難傳佛何等文、詮何等是？不可以漸文傳頓是、以偏文詮圓是，傳詮若謬則文不如，文不如則理不是。此義難明，須加意詳審。

As for the reading "in terms of the teachings": the sūtras hold that the dharma of the buddhas of the three times all begins with "thus," yet earlier buddhas had gradual, sudden, secret, and indeterminate sūtras, and the gradual teachings were further of the Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect types; the present Buddha is likewise so — the sūtras differ from one another, and their "thus" differs as well; one should not expect a single key to open every door. Furthermore, "as" means that the Buddha's text and Ānanda's text do not differ; "is" means that the expounding words match what is expounded. Now, which text did Ānanda transmit from the Buddha, and which "is" did he expound? One cannot transmit a gradual text as expounding a "sudden is," nor use a partial text to expound a "perfect is" — if the transmission and the exposition go astray, then the text is not "as," and if the text is not "as," then the principle is not "is." This point is difficult to clarify and demands careful, close attention.

且依漸教分別，佛明俗有文字，真無文字。阿難傳佛俗諦文字，與佛說不異，故名如，因此俗文會真諦理，故名為是，此則三藏經初明如是也。佛明即色是空，空即是色，色空空色，無二無別，空色不異為如，即事而真為是。阿難傳佛文不異為如，能詮即所詮為是，此則通教經初如是也。佛明生死是有邊，涅槃是無邊，出生死有邊，入涅槃無邊，出涅槃無邊，入於中道。阿難傳此出有入無，出無入中，與佛說無異為如，從淺至深，無非曰是，此則別教經初如是也。佛明生死即涅槃，亦即中道，況復涅槃寧非中道？真如法界實性實際，徧一切處無非佛法。阿難傳此，與佛說無異，故名為如，如如不動故名為是，是則圓經初如是也。若動俗入如，三藏義耳；不動俗即是如，通教義耳；動如入如，別教義耳；不動如而是如，圓教義也(云云)。若頓如是與圓同，不定如是前後更互，祕密者隱而不傳。敷八教網亘法界海，懼其有漏，況羅之一目若為獨張？又一時接四箭，不令墮地，未敢稱捷；策鈍驢、驅跛鼈尚不得一，何況四耶(云云)？

Let us first analyze this in terms of the gradual teaching. The Buddha taught that the conventional has the reality of words and letters, while the ultimate is without words and letters. Ānanda transmitted the Buddha's conventional-truth words and letters without differing from what the Buddha said — hence this is called "as"; and through these conventional words, one comes to understand the principle of ultimate truth — hence this is called "is." This, then, is what "thus" means at the opening of a Tripiṭaka-teaching sūtra. The Buddha taught that form itself is emptiness, and emptiness itself is form — form and emptiness, emptiness and form, without duality, without distinction: that emptiness and form do not differ is "as"; that very phenomena are themselves true is "is." Ānanda transmitted the Buddha's text without differing — this is "as"; the expounding words being identical with what is expounded is "is." This, then, is what "thus" means at the opening of a shared-teaching sūtra. The Buddha taught that saṃsāra is the bounded extreme and nirvāṇa is the unbounded extreme; that going out from the bounded extreme of saṃsāra one enters the unbounded extreme, and going out from the unbounded extreme of nirvāṇa one enters the middle way. Ānanda transmitted this going-out-from-being and entering-into-nothingness, going-out-from-nothingness and entering-into-the-middle, without differing from what the Buddha said — this is "as"; proceeding from the shallow to the deep, all of it without exception is called "is." This, then, is what "thus" means at the opening of a distinct-teaching sūtra. The Buddha taught that saṃsāra is itself nirvāṇa, and is itself the middle way — how much more, then, is nirvāṇa itself not other than the middle way? Suchness, the dharma-realm, true nature, and ultimate reality pervade every place and are nowhere other than the Buddha-dharma. Ānanda transmitted this without differing from what the Buddha said — hence it is called "as"; being thus-as-thus, unmoving, it is called "is." This, then, is what "thus" means at the opening of a perfect-teaching sūtra. If one moves from the conventional to enter the "as," this is the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching; if without moving the conventional is itself the "as," this is the sense of the shared teaching; if one moves through the "as" to enter a higher "as," this is the sense of the distinct teaching; if without moving the "as" is nonetheless the "as," this is the sense of the perfect teaching (and so on). As for the sudden teaching, its "thus" is the same as that of the perfect teaching; the indeterminate teaching's "thus" alternates between earlier and later teachings; the secret teaching's "thus" is hidden and not transmitted openly. To spread the net of the eight teachings across the ocean of the dharma-realm, fearing some gap might be left uncovered — how, then, could a single mesh of that net be stretched out alone and expected to catch everything? Furthermore, to catch four arrows at once and let none fall to the ground cannot yet be called skillful; when even urging on a dull donkey or driving a lame turtle one may fail to manage even one task, how much more so four at once (and so on)?

約本迹釋如是者，三世十方橫豎皆爾，過去遠遠，現在漫漫，未來永永，皆悉如是，何處是本？何處是迹？且約釋尊最初成道，經初如是者，是本也；中間作佛說經，今日所說經初如是者，皆迹也。

又阿難所傳如是者迹也，佛所說如是者本也。又師弟通達如是，非始今日、亦非中間者，本也；而中間而今日者，迹也。

As for expounding "thus" in terms of "trace and origin": throughout the three times and the ten directions, both across and along, it is everywhere so — in the far distant past, in the boundless present, in the endless future, it is everywhere "thus." Where, then, is the origin, and where is the trace? Let us take, in terms of Śākyamuni's very first attainment of the path, the "thus" at the opening of that sūtra as the origin; the "thus" at the opening of the sūtras he preached in the intervening ages and preaches today are all trace.

Furthermore, the "thus" that Ānanda transmitted is trace; the "thus" that the Buddha himself spoke is origin. Furthermore, the "thus" by which teacher and disciple penetrate to full understanding — not beginning today, nor only in the intervening ages — is the origin; while its occurring in the intervening ages and today is the trace.

觀心釋者，觀前悉檀教迹等諸如是義，悉是因緣生法，即通觀也；因緣即空即假者，別觀也；二觀為方便道，得入中道第一義，雙照二諦者，亦通亦別觀也；上來悉是中道者，非通非別觀也。下文云：「若人信汝所說，即得見我亦見於汝及比丘僧并諸菩薩。」即觀行之明文也。信則論機，見則是應，即因緣也。又信有淺深、見有權實，種種分別不同者，即分別教。又信《法華》之文，則見實相之本，若見身子之化，則見龍陀之本；若見始成釋尊，亦見久成先佛；若見千二百比丘、八萬菩薩者，亦見其本也。又聞經心信無疑，覺此信心明淨，即是見佛；慧數分明，是見身子；諸數分明，是眾比丘；慈悲心淨，是見諸菩薩。約心為四帖釋轉明，若釋他經但用三意，為未發本顯迹故。當知今經三釋與他同，一釋與彼異，四番釋如是竟(云云)。

As for the "contemplation of mind" reading: to contemplate all the foregoing meanings of "thus" — the siddhāntas, the teachings, the trace, and so on — as themselves dharmas arising from causes and conditions, is the "shared" contemplation; that causes and conditions are themselves empty and themselves provisional is the "distinct" contemplation; that the two contemplations serve as expedient paths by which one enters the ultimate meaning of the middle way, illuminating the two truths simultaneously, is the contemplation that is both shared and distinct; and that all the above is, in its entirety, the middle way, is the contemplation that is neither shared nor distinct. The text below says: "If a person believes what you say, he thereby comes to see me, and also sees you, and the assembly of monks, and the bodhisattvas" — this is the very text that makes plain the contemplative practice. Faith is a matter of capacity; seeing is a matter of response — this is the "causes and conditions" aspect. Furthermore, that faith has shallow and deep degrees, and seeing has provisional and true modes, with various distinctions among them, is the "differentiating the teachings" aspect. Furthermore, to have faith in the text of the Lotus is to see the origin of ultimate reality; to see the transformation-body of Śāriputra is to see the origin of the nāga-king's daughter; to see Śākyamuni's recent attainment of buddhahood is also to see the buddhas of the distant past whom he attained long ago; to see the twelve hundred bhikṣus and eighty thousand bodhisattvas is likewise to see their origin. Furthermore, when hearing the sūtra one's mind believes without doubt, and one becomes aware that this faithful mind is bright and pure — this is "seeing the Buddha"; when the faculty of wisdom is distinctly clear, this is "seeing Śāriputra"; when all the faculties are distinctly clear, this is "seeing the assembly of bhikṣus"; when the mind of compassion is pure, this is "seeing the bodhisattvas." Explained in terms of the mind, these four readings become ever clearer; when expounding other sūtras, only three approaches are used, because in them the origin has not yet been disclosed to reveal the trace. One should understand that, for the present sūtra, three of the readings are the same as for other sūtras, while one reading differs from them. Here ends the fourfold exegesis of "thus" (and so on).

「我聞」者，或「聞如是」，蓋經本不同，前後互舉耳。今例為四釋，《大論》云：「耳根不壞聲在可聞處，作心欲聞，眾緣和合，故言我聞。」

問：應言耳聞，那云我聞？

答：我是耳主，舉我攝眾緣。此世界釋也。阿難登高稱我聞，大眾應悲號，適見如來今稱我聞，無學飛騰說偈。《佛話經》明文殊結集，先唱題、次稱如是我聞，時眾悲號。此為人釋也。阿難登高稱我聞遣眾疑，阿難身與佛相似，短佛三指，眾疑釋尊重出，或他方佛來，或阿難成佛；若唱我聞，三疑即遣。此對治釋也。阿難學人，隨俗稱我聞，第一義中無我無聞，古來眾釋同是因緣一意耳。

As for "I heard": some versions say "heard thus," since the sūtra exemplars differ, the two phrases being cited in reverse order of one another. Here again I give the four readings. The "Great Treatise" says: "When the ear faculty is unimpaired, and sound is present within its range, and the mind is set on hearing, then the various conditions come together in harmony — hence it is said, 'I heard.'"

Question: One ought to say "the ear heard" — why say "I heard"?

Answer: The "I" is the master of the ear, and citing the "I" subsumes all the other conditions. This is the mundane-siddhānta reading. When Ānanda ascended the high seat and proclaimed "I heard," the great assembly ought to have wailed in grief, for they had just witnessed the Tathāgata's parinirvāṇa, yet now hearing "I heard," those beyond learning leapt up and spoke verses of joy. The Sūtra on the Buddha's Discourse records that when Mañjuśrī presided over the compilation, he first announced the title and then proclaimed "Thus have I heard," and the assembled multitude wailed in grief at that time. This is the individual-siddhānta reading. Ānanda ascended the high seat and proclaimed "I heard" in order to dispel the assembly's doubts — Ānanda's body resembled the Buddha's, being shorter than the Buddha's by only three finger-widths, and the assembly feared that the World-Honored One had appeared once more, or that a buddha had come from another world, or that Ānanda himself had become a buddha; but once he proclaimed "I heard," these three doubts were at once dispelled. This is the therapeutic-siddhānta reading. Ānanda, a disciple still in training, proclaimed "I heard" in accordance with worldly convention, though in terms of ultimate meaning there is neither "I" nor "hearing" in any substantial sense. The various exegeses handed down from of old are, in this, all of a single sense — that of causes and conditions.

約教解釋者，《釋論》云：「凡夫三種我，謂見、慢、名字，學人二種，無學一種。」阿難是學人，無邪我、能伏慢我，隨世名字稱我無咎，此用三藏意釋我也。《十住毘婆沙》云：「四句稱我皆墮邪見，佛正法中無我，誰聞？」此用通教意也。《大經》云：「阿難多聞士，知我無我而不二，雙分別我、無我。」此用別教意也。又阿難知我無我而不二，方便為侍者，傳持如來無礙智慧，以自在音聲傳權傳實，有何不可？此用圓教釋我也。又《正法念經》，明三阿難：阿難陀此云歡喜，持小乘藏；阿難跋陀此云歡喜賢，受持雜藏；阿難娑伽此云歡喜海，持佛藏。《阿含經》有典藏阿難，持菩薩藏。蓋指一人具於四德，傳持四法門，其義自顯(云云)。

As for the reading "in terms of the teachings," the Śāstra says: "Ordinary beings have three kinds of 'self' — the self of erroneous views, the self of pride, and the self as a mere name; the disciple in training has two kinds; the one beyond training has one kind." Ānanda is a disciple in training: he is without the false self of erroneous view, and is able to subdue the self of pride, so that using "I" in accordance with worldly convention involves no fault — this employs the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching to explain "I." The Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā [Śāstra] says: "To assert 'I' in any of the four propositions is to fall into wrong view; within the Buddha's true Dharma there is no self — who, then, is hearing?" — this employs the sense of the shared teaching. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Ānanda, the one of great learning, knows that self and no-self are not two, yet distinguishes both self and no-self" — this employs the sense of the distinct teaching. Furthermore, Ānanda, knowing that self and no-self are not two, became, as an expedient, the Buddha's attendant, transmitting and upholding the Tathāgata's unobstructed wisdom, and with a freely responsive voice transmitting both the provisional and the true — what fault could there be in this? This employs the sense of the perfect teaching to explain "I." Furthermore, the Sūtra of the True Dharma's Mindfulness speaks of three Ānandas: Ānandaḥ, meaning "Joy," who upheld the Hīnayāna piṭaka; Ānandabhadra, meaning "Joyful Worthy," who received and upheld the miscellaneous piṭaka; and Ānandasāgara, meaning "Joyful Ocean," who upheld the Buddha's own piṭaka. The Āgama Sūtra also speaks of an Ānanda who was "Keeper of the Canon," who upheld the bodhisattva piṭaka. This no doubt indicates that a single person was endowed with all four virtues, transmitting and upholding four dharma-gates — its meaning is self-evident (and so on).

本迹釋者，若未會入，可言阿難隨世名我；若發迹顯本，空王佛所同時發心，方便示為傳法之人，何所不能？

As for the "trace and origin" reading: if Ānanda has not yet fully entered into the ultimate, one may say that Ānanda simply used "I" in accordance with worldly convention; but if the trace is disclosed to reveal the origin, then — having, together with Śākyamuni, first given rise to the resolve for awakening in the presence of King of Emptiness Buddha, and now, as an expedient, manifesting as the one who transmits the Dharma — what could he not do?

觀心釋者，觀因緣所生法，即空、即假、即中，即空者我無我也，即假者分別我也，即中者真妙我也(云云)。

As for the "contemplation of mind" reading: to contemplate dharmas arisen from causes and conditions as empty, as provisional, and as the middle — the "empty" aspect is the self that is no-self; the "provisional" aspect is the distinguishing of self; the "middle" aspect is the true and wondrous self (and so on).

釋「聞」者，阿難，佛得道夜生，侍佛二十餘年，未侍佛時應是不聞。《大論》云：「阿難集法時自云：『佛初轉法輪，我爾時不見，如是展轉聞。』」當知不悉聞也。舊解云：「阿難得佛覺三昧，力自能聞。」《報恩經》云：「阿難求四願，所未聞經願佛重說。」又云：「佛口密為說也。」《胎經》云：「佛從金棺出金臂，重為阿難現入胎之相，諸經皆聞。」況餘處說耶？此文云：「阿難得記，即憶本願，持先佛法皆如今也。」此因緣釋也。

As for explaining "heard": Ānanda was born on the very night the Buddha attained the path, and attended the Buddha for over twenty years — before he became the Buddha's attendant, he ought not to have heard the earlier teachings. The "Great Treatise" says: "When Ānanda helped compile the Dharma, he himself said: 'When the Buddha first turned the Dharma wheel, I was not present at that time; I heard of it only through successive transmission.'" One should understand from this that he did not hear everything directly. An old interpretation holds: "Ānanda attained the Buddha's samādhi of awakening, and by that power was able to hear even what he had not attended." The Sūtra of Repaying Kindness says: "Ānanda sought four boons, one being that for sūtras he had not heard, he asked the Buddha to preach them again"; it also says, "the Buddha, by secret means of his mouth, preached them for him." The Garbha [Sūtra] says: "The Buddha, from within his golden coffin, put forth a golden arm, and once more, for Ānanda's sake, manifested the sign of entering the womb, so that he might hear all the sūtras" — how much more so, then, might he have heard sermons given elsewhere? The present text says: "Ānanda, having received his prediction of future buddhahood, thereupon recalled his original vow, and upheld the Dharma of the buddhas of the past exactly as though hearing it now." This is the "causes and conditions" reading.

若約教者，歡喜阿難，面如淨滿月，眼若青蓮華，親承佛旨如仰完器，傳以化人如瀉異瓶，此傳聞、聞法也。歡喜賢，住學地得空無相願，眼、耳、鼻、舌諸根不漏，傳持聞不聞法也。典藏阿難多所含受，如大雲持雨，此傳持不聞聞法也。阿難海，是多聞士，自然能解了是常與無常，若知如來常不說法，是名菩薩具足多聞，佛法大海水，流入阿難心，此傳持不聞不聞法也。今經是海阿難，持不聞不聞之妙法也。

As for the reading in terms of the teachings: Ānanda "Joy," whose face was like the pure full moon and whose eyes were like blue lotus flowers, personally received the Buddha's teaching as water poured into an unbroken vessel, and transmitted it to convert others as though pouring it into a different vessel — this is "transmitting what was heard, hearing the Dharma." Ānanda "Joyful Worthy," abiding at the stage of a disciple in training, attained the aspirationless samādhi of emptiness and signlessness, so that his eyes, ears, nose, and tongue and other faculties were free of outflow — this is "transmitting and upholding what was heard-yet-not-heard." Ānanda "Keeper of the Canon," embracing and receiving all things, was like a great cloud holding rain — this is "transmitting and upholding what was not heard, yet heard." Ānanda "Ocean," the one of vast learning, was naturally able to understand what is permanent and what is impermanent; knowing that the Tathāgata, in his permanent aspect, preaches no Dharma at all, is what is meant by a bodhisattva fully endowed with vast learning — the great ocean-waters of the Buddha-dharma flowed into Ānanda's mind: this is "transmitting and upholding what was not heard, and was not heard." The present sūtra is upheld by Ānanda the Ocean, who holds the wondrous Dharma that is "not heard, and not heard."

本迹解者，如上四聞皆迹引，而本地不可思議(云云)。

As for the "trace and origin" interpretation: the four kinds of "hearing" described above are all traces used to draw beings in, while his fundamental ground is inconceivable (and so on).

觀心釋者，觀因緣法是觀聞聞，觀空是觀聞不聞，觀假是觀不聞聞，觀中是觀不聞不聞(云云)。一念觀者，妙觀也(云云)。

As for the "contemplation of mind" reading: to contemplate dharmas of causes and conditions is to contemplate "hearing what is heard"; to contemplate emptiness is to contemplate "hearing what is not heard"; to contemplate the provisional is to contemplate "not hearing what is heard"; to contemplate the middle is to contemplate "not hearing what is not heard" (and so on). To contemplate all this in a single thought-moment is the wondrous contemplation (and so on).

「一時」者，肇師云：「法王啟運嘉會之時」者，世界也。論云：「迦羅是實時」，示內弟子時；食時著衣者，為人也。「三摩耶是假時」，破外道邪見者，對治也。若時與道合者，第一義也(云云)。若見諦已上、無學已下，名下一時；若三人同入第一義，名中一時；若登地已上，名上一時；若初住已上，名上上一時。今經是上上一時，此約教分別也。

本迹者，前諸一時，迹也；久遠實得之一時，本也。

觀心釋者，觀心先空、次假、後中，次第觀心也。觀心即空、即假、即中者，圓妙觀心也。

As for "at one time": Sengzhao says, "the time when the Dharma King set his teaching into motion, the occasion of the auspicious gathering" — this is the mundane-siddhānta reading. The Śāstra says, "kāla is 'real time,'" indicating the time proper to the disciples within the community — such as mealtime, the time for putting on robes — this is the individual-siddhānta reading. "The word samaya is 'conventional time,'" used to refute the false views of non-Buddhists — this is the therapeutic-siddhānta reading. When the time coincides with the attainment of the path, this is the ultimate-meaning siddhānta (and so on). If the assembly ranges from the seeing of the truths up through those still below the stage beyond learning, this is called the "lower one time"; if the three kinds of persons of the three vehicles together enter the ultimate meaning, this is called the "middle one time"; if they have ascended to the bodhisattva grounds and above, this is called the "upper one time"; if they have reached the first abiding and above, this is called the "upper-upper one time." The present sūtra is an instance of the "upper-upper one time" — this is the analysis in terms of the teachings.

As for trace and origin: all the aforementioned "one times" are trace; the "one time" of the truly attained, distant past is origin.

As for the "contemplation of mind" reading: to contemplate the mind first as empty, then as provisional, and finally as the middle, is sequential contemplation of mind; to contemplate the mind as simultaneously empty, provisional, and the middle, is the perfect and wondrous contemplation of mind.

「佛」者，劫初無病，劫盡多病；長壽時樂，短壽時苦。東天下富而壽，西天下多珠寶多牛羊，北天下無我、無臣屬，如此時處不感佛出。八萬歲時百年時，南天下未見果而修因，故佛出其地。離車子云：「摩竭提國如大池，佛出其國如大蓮華。」無勝云：「佛於眾生平等無二，汝等耽荒五欲不見佛耳，非佛棄汝出摩竭提。」此皆世界釋也。日若不出，池中未生、生已等華，翳死無疑；佛若出世則有剎利、婆羅門、居士、四天王乃至有頂，此就為人釋也。三乘根性感佛出世，餘不能感，善斷有頂種，永度生死流，此就對治說也。佛於法性，無動、無出，能令眾生感見動出，而於如來實無動出，此就第一義說也。皆因緣釋耳。

As for "Buddha": at the beginning of an eon there is no illness, while at an eon's end illness abounds; in ages of long life there is happiness, in ages of short life there is suffering. The eastern continent is wealthy and long-lived; the western continent abounds in pearls, jewels, cattle, and sheep; the northern continent is without a sense of self and without ministers or subjects — in such times and places, the conditions do not draw forth a buddha's appearance. It is at the time of an eighty-thousand-year lifespan, or a hundred-year lifespan, in the southern continent, where the fruit is not yet seen and the causes must still be cultivated, that a buddha appears in that land. The Licchavi said: "The land of Magadha is like a great pool, and the Buddha's appearing in that land is like a great lotus flower." Anuttara said: "The Buddha regards all sentient beings equally, without distinction; it is you who, indulging and drowning in the five desires, fail to see the Buddha — it is not that the Buddha abandons you and leaves Magadha." These are all mundane-siddhānta readings. If the sun does not rise, then the lotuses in the pool — whether not yet sprouted or already sprouted — will alike wither and die without doubt; if the Buddha appears in the world, then there will be kṣatriyas, brāhmaṇas, householders, the Four Heavenly Kings, and on up to the gods of the Peak of Existence who benefit — this is the individual-siddhānta reading. Those of the three vehicles' capacities draw forth the Buddha's appearance in the world, while others cannot draw it forth; through it, beings skillfully sever the seeds of rebirth at the Peak of Existence and forever cross beyond the stream of birth and death — this is the therapeutic-siddhānta reading. The Buddha, with respect to the nature of dharmas, neither moves nor emerges, yet he is able to make sentient beings perceive him as though moving and emerging, while in the Tathāgata himself there is truly no movement or emergence — this is the ultimate-meaning siddhānta reading. All of these are the "causes and conditions" exegesis.

佛名覺者、知者，於道場樹下，知覺世間、出世間，總相、別相，覺世即苦、集，覺出世即道、滅；亦能覺他。身長丈六，壽八十，老比丘像。菩提樹下三十四心正、習俱盡者，即三藏佛自覺覺他。

帶比丘像現尊特身，樹下一念相應，斷餘殘習者，即通佛自覺覺他。單現尊特相，坐蓮花臺受佛職者，即別佛自覺覺他。隱前三相，唯示不可思議如虛空相，即圓佛自覺覺他。故經云：「或見如來丈六之身，或見小身、大身，或見坐花臺為百千釋迦說心地法門，或見身同虛空遍於法界無有分別。」即此義也。是為約教分別也。

"Buddha" means "the awakened one," "the one who knows": beneath the bodhi tree, he knew and was awakened to the mundane and the supramundane, to their general and particular characteristics — his awakening to the mundane is awakening to suffering and its arising, his awakening to the supramundane is awakening to the path and cessation; and he is able, moreover, to awaken others. He whose body is sixteen feet tall and lives to eighty years, in the appearance of an aged bhikṣu, and who, beneath the bodhi tree, in thirty-four mind-moments extinguishes both the fundamental afflictions and their residual habits — this is the Tripiṭaka-teaching buddha awakening himself and awakening others.

He who bears the appearance of a bhikṣu yet manifests the sublime and distinguished body, and who, beneath the tree, in a single thought-moment corresponding to reality, severs the remaining habitual traces — this is the shared-teaching buddha awakening himself and awakening others. He who manifests solely the sublime and distinguished mark, seated on a lotus-blossom throne receiving the office of buddhahood — this is the distinct-teaching buddha awakening himself and awakening others. He who conceals the aforementioned three marks and shows only the inconceivable mark like empty space — this is the perfect-teaching buddha awakening himself and awakening others. Hence the sūtra says: "Some see the Tathāgata's sixteen-foot body; some see a small body, or a great body; some see him seated on a lotus throne, expounding the mind-ground Dharma to hundreds and thousands of Śākyamunis; some see his body as identical to empty space, pervading the dharma-realm without distinction" — this is precisely that meaning. This constitutes the analysis in terms of the teachings.

本迹釋者，一佛為本，三佛為迹，中間示現數數唱生、數數唱滅，皆是迹也；唯本地四佛皆是本也。

觀心釋者，觀因緣所生心，先空、次假、後中，皆偏覺也；觀心即空、即假、即中，是圓覺也(云云)。

As for the "trace and origin" reading: the one ultimate buddha is the origin, and the other three buddhas described above are trace; in the interval, the repeated proclamations of birth and repeated proclamations of extinction are all trace — only the fundamental ground is origin, and there all four buddhas described are origin.

As for the "contemplation of mind" reading: to contemplate the mind that arises from causes and conditions first as empty, then as provisional, and finally as the middle, is in every case a partial awakening; to contemplate the mind as simultaneously empty, provisional, and the middle, is the perfect awakening (and so on).

「住」者，能住住所住，所住即是忍土王城，能住即是四威儀，住世未滅，此則世界因緣釋住也。又住者，住十善道、住四禪中，此即為人因緣釋住也。又住者，住三三昧，對治因緣釋住。又住者，住首楞嚴，即是第一義因緣釋住(云云)。

約教者，三藏佛從析門發真無漏，住有餘、無餘涅槃。通佛從體門發真，住有餘、無餘涅槃。別佛從次第門入，住祕密藏。圓佛從不次第門入，住祕密藏。前三佛住，能所皆麁；後一佛住，能所俱妙。今經則是圓佛住於妙住也。

本迹解者，三藏佛應涅槃，慈悲垂迹生身住世。通佛誓願慈悲，扶餘習度眾生作佛事。別、圓佛皆慈悲薰法性，愍眾生故垂應法界。

當知四佛住本佛住，以慈悲故住於忍土王城，威儀住世，是名迹住。

觀解者，觀住於境，或住無常境，即空、即假、即中等境，以無住法住於境中故名為住。

As for "abiding": there is the subject that abides and the object in which it abides — the object abided in is this land of endurance (Sahā), namely Rājagṛha; the subject that abides is manifest through the four bodily postures, abiding in the world without yet passing into extinction — this is the mundane-siddhānta "causes and conditions" reading of "abiding." Furthermore, "abiding" can mean abiding in the ten good paths of action, abiding in the four dhyānas — this is the individual-siddhānta "causes and conditions" reading of "abiding." Furthermore, "abiding" can mean abiding in the three samādhis — the therapeutic-siddhānta "causes and conditions" reading of "abiding." Furthermore, "abiding" can mean abiding in the Śūraṅgama samādhi — this is the ultimate-meaning-siddhānta "causes and conditions" reading of "abiding" (and so on).

As for the teachings: the Tripiṭaka-teaching buddha, having given rise to true, untainted wisdom through the gate of analytic contemplation, abides in nirvāṇa with and without remainder. The shared-teaching buddha, having given rise to true wisdom through the gate of directly comprehending emptiness in phenomena, likewise abides in nirvāṇa with and without remainder. The distinct-teaching buddha enters through the sequential gate, abiding in the secret treasury. The perfect-teaching buddha enters through the non-sequential gate, abiding in the secret treasury. For the first three buddhas' abiding, both the subject and the object are coarse; for the last buddha's abiding, both the subject and the object are wondrous. The present sūtra is precisely the perfect-teaching buddha abiding in this wondrous abiding.

As for the "trace and origin" interpretation: the Tripiṭaka-teaching buddha, responding to the need to demonstrate nirvāṇa, out of compassion manifests the trace of a born body abiding in the world. The shared-teaching buddha, through the compassion of his vow, sustains residual habits in order to save sentient beings and carry out the Buddha's work. The distinct- and perfect-teaching buddhas both, through compassion, are infused with dharma-nature, and out of pity for sentient beings manifest their response throughout the dharma-realm.

One should understand that the abiding of these four buddhas is grounded in the original buddha's abiding: it is out of compassion that he abides in this land of endurance, at Rājagṛha, manifest in bodily deportment abiding in the world — this is called the trace of "abiding."

As for the contemplative interpretation: to contemplate "abiding" with respect to its object — whether abiding in the object of impermanence, or in objects that are empty, provisional, and the middle — since it is by the dharma of non-abiding that one abides within the object, this is called "abiding."

「王舍城」者，天竺稱羅閱祇伽羅，羅閱祇此云王舍，伽羅此云城，國名摩伽陀，此云不害，無刑殺法也；亦云摩竭提，此云天羅，天羅者王名也，以王名國，此王即駁足之父。昔久遠劫，此王主千小國，王巡山值牸師子，眾人迸散，仍共王交。後月滿來殿上生，王知是己子，訛言我既無兒，此乃天賜，養為太子，足上斑駁，時人號為駁足。後紹王位，喜噉肉，勅厨人無令肉少，一時遽闕，乃取城西新死小兒為膳。王言大美，勅之常辦此肉，厨人日捕一人，舉國愁恐，千小國興兵廢王，置耆闍山中，諸羅剎輔之為鬼王，因與山神誓，誓取千王祭山。捕得九百九十九，唯少普明王，後時伺執得之。大啼哭，恨生來實語，而今乖信。駁足放之還國，作大施，立太子，仍就死，形悅心安。駁足問之，答：「得聞聖法。」因令說之，廣讚慈心，毀呰殺害，仍說四非常偈(云云)。駁足聞法，得空平等地，即初地也。千王各取一渧血、三條髮，賽山神願。駁足與千王共立舍城，都五山中為大國，各以千小國付子胤，千王更迭知大國事。又百姓在五山內，七遍作舍，七度被燒，百姓議云：「由我薄福數致煨燼，王有福力其舍不燒，自今已後皆排我屋為王舍。」由是免燒，故稱王舍城。又駁足共千王立舍於其地，故稱王舍。又駁足得道放赦千王，千王被赦於其地故，名地為王赦，而經家借音為屋舍字耳。因緣出《大論》及諸經(云云)。

As for "Rājagṛha": in India it is called Rājagṛhapura — "Rājagṛha" translates as "king's house," and "pura" translates as "city." The kingdom's name, Magadha, translates as "no harm," a land without punitive execution; it is also called Mo-jie-ti, which translates as "heaven-net" — "heaven-net" being a king's name, the kingdom having been named for this king, who was the father of Brāhmadatta "Spotted-Foot." Long ago, in a distant kalpa, this king ruled over a thousand lesser kingdoms. Once, while touring the mountains, the king encountered a female lion; his attendants scattered in fright, and he then coupled with her. Later, when the months were full, a child was born and left on the palace steps; the king, knowing it was his own son, falsely declared: "Since I have had no son, this must be a gift from heaven" — and he raised the child as crown prince. Because the child's feet were mottled, people of the time called him "Spotted-Foot." When he later succeeded to the throne, he was fond of eating meat, and ordered the kitchen never to let the meat run short; when supplies once suddenly ran out, the cooks took the flesh of a small child newly dead west of the city and served it as a dish. The king pronounced it delicious and ordered that this meat be provided regularly; the kitchen staff seized one person each day, and the whole kingdom fell into terror. The thousand lesser kingdoms raised armies and deposed the king, exiling him to Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, where various rākṣasas served him, making him their demon king; he thereupon made a vow with the mountain god, pledging to capture a thousand kings to sacrifice to the mountain. He seized nine hundred and ninety-nine, lacking only King Universal Light; he later lay in wait and captured him as well. The captured king wept bitterly, regretting that although he had always spoken the truth in life, he had now betrayed his own word. Spotted-Foot released him to return to his kingdom; there he made a great act of giving, installed his crown prince, and then came back to face death, his form at ease and his mind at peace. When Spotted-Foot questioned him about this composure, he answered: "I was able to hear the sacred Dharma." Spotted-Foot thereupon had him expound it; the king broadly praised a compassionate mind and condemned killing and harm, and then spoke the verses on the four kinds of impermanence (and so on). Spotted-Foot, upon hearing the Dharma, attained the ground of empty equanimity — that is, the first bodhisattva ground. The thousand kings each took a drop of blood and three strands of hair to fulfill the vow made to the mountain god. Spotted-Foot and the thousand kings together built a city among the five mountains, making it their capital and a great kingdom; each entrusted his thousand lesser kingdoms to his own heirs, and the thousand kings took turns administering the affairs of the great kingdom. Furthermore, the common people, dwelling within the five mountains, built their houses seven times, and seven times these were burned down; the people deliberated, saying: "Because of our meager merit, our dwellings are repeatedly reduced to ash, while the king, having merit and power, has a dwelling that is never burned — from now on, let us all call our houses 'the king's dwellings.'" By this, they were spared from further burning, and hence the place came to be called "Rājagṛha." Another account holds that Spotted-Foot and the thousand kings together built their dwellings on this ground, hence it is called "King's House." Yet another holds that when Spotted-Foot attained the path he pardoned the thousand kings, and because the thousand kings were pardoned on this ground, the place was named "King's Pardon," and the sūtra-compilers borrowed the sound to write it with the characters for "house" and "dwelling." This account is drawn from the "Great Treatise" and various other sūtras (and so on).

約教者，《像法決疑經》云：「一切大眾所見不同，或見娑羅林地，悉是土砂、草木、石壁，或見七寶清淨莊嚴，或見此林是三世諸佛所遊行處，或見此林即是不可思議諸佛境界真實法體。」例知此義，四見不同，所住既然，能住亦爾。此則約教分別也。本迹、觀心在後說。

As for the teachings: the Sūtra Resolving Doubts about the Semblance Dharma says: "What the whole great assembly sees differs — some see the ground of the sāla grove as nothing but earth, sand, grasses, trees, and stone walls; some see it adorned in pure splendor with the seven treasures; some see this grove as the place where the buddhas of the three times have traveled and dwelt; some see this grove as the inconceivable true dharma-body that is the very domain of the buddhas." By analogy, one may understand this same principle: the four ways of seeing differ, and just as it is with the object abided in, so it is also with the subject that abides. This constitutes the analysis in terms of the teachings. The trace-and-origin and contemplation-of-mind readings will be given later.

「耆闍崛山」者，此翻靈鷲，亦云鷲頭，亦云狼跡，梁武云：「王鴡引詩人所詠關睢是也。」《爾雅》云：「似鵄。」又解，山峯似鷲，將峯名山。又云，山南有尸陀林，鷲食尸竟棲其山，時人呼為鷲山。又解，前佛今佛皆居此山，若佛滅後羅漢住，法滅支佛住，無支佛鬼神住。既是聖靈所居，總有三事，因呼為靈鷲山。有五精舍：鞞婆羅跋恕，此云天主穴；薩多般那求訶，此云七葉穴；因陀世羅求訶，此云蛇神山；薩簸恕魂直迦鉢婆羅，此云少獨力山；五是耆闍崛山。

問：劫火洞然，天地廓清，云何前佛後佛同居此山？

答：後劫立，本相還現，得神通人，知昔名以名今耳。例如先劫姓瞿曇，將本姓以姓今也。

約教釋「山」，例如城義說(云云)。

As for "Gṛdhrakūṭa": this translates as "Sacred Vulture Peak," also called "Vulture Head," also called "Wolf's Track." Emperor Wu of Liang said: "It is the 'royal osprey' that the poet invoked in the line 'the ospreys cry' in the Book of Odes." The Erya says it resembles a kite. Another explanation holds that the mountain's peak resembles a vulture, and the peak's shape gave the mountain its name. Yet another says that south of the mountain there is a charnel forest, and vultures, having eaten the corpses there, would then roost on this mountain, so that people of the time called it "Vulture Mountain." Another explanation holds that buddhas past and present have all dwelt on this mountain; after a buddha's parinirvāṇa, arhats dwell there, and after the Dharma's disappearance, pratyekabuddhas dwell there, and where there are no pratyekabuddhas, spirits dwell there. Since it is the dwelling of sacred and numinous beings — all told, these three kinds — it is accordingly called "Sacred Vulture Peak." There are five hermitages on it: Vaihārabṛṣa, meaning "Cave of the Lord of Heaven"; Saptaparṇaguhā, meaning "Seven-Leaf Cave"; Indraśailaguhā, meaning "Serpent-Spirit Mountain"; Śarpaśuṇḍikāprāgbhāra, meaning "Mountain of Solitary Strength"; the fifth is Gṛdhrakūṭa itself.

Question: When the kalpa-fire blazes forth and heaven and earth are utterly swept clean, how can buddhas of the past and buddhas of the present dwell together on this same mountain?

Answer: When the following kalpa is established, the original features reappear, and those who have attained spiritual powers, knowing the former name, apply it to the present mountain — just as, in an earlier kalpa, the surname of the Buddha's clan was Gautama, and this original surname was likewise applied in the present.

The exegesis of "mountain" in terms of the teachings follows the same pattern as the meaning given for "city" above (and so on).

觀釋者，王即心王，舍即五陰，心王造此舍，若析五陰舍空，空為涅槃城，此觀既淺如見土木；若體五陰舍即空，空為涅槃城，即通教也；若觀五陰舍，因滅是色獲得常色，受、想、行、識亦復如是，此之四德常為諸佛之所遊處；若觀五陰即法性，法性無受、想、行、識，一切眾生即是涅槃，不可復滅，畢竟空寂舍，如是涅槃，即是真如實體(云云)。

As for the contemplative interpretation: the "king" is the king-mind, and the "house" is the five aggregates — the king-mind constructs this house. If one analytically breaks down the house of the five aggregates into emptiness, taking this emptiness as the city of nirvāṇa, this contemplation is still shallow, like merely seeing earth and wood rather than what they compose. If one comprehends that the house of the five aggregates is itself empty, taking this emptiness as the city of nirvāṇa, this is the shared teaching. If one contemplates the house of the five aggregates and sees that by the extinguishing of impermanent form one attains permanent form, and that feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness are likewise so, then these four virtues are forever the domain in which the buddhas dwell and roam. If one contemplates that the five aggregates are themselves dharma-nature, and that dharma-nature is without separately existing feeling, perception, mental formations, or consciousness, one sees that all sentient beings are themselves nirvāṇa, incapable of further extinction — this house, ultimately empty and still, this nirvāṇa, is itself the true and real substance of suchness (and so on).

觀心山者，若觀色陰，無知如山，識陰如靈，三陰如鷲，觀此靈鷲無常，即析觀也；觀此靈鷲即空，體觀也；觀靈即智性了因，智慧莊嚴也，鷲即聚集緣因，福德莊嚴也，山即法性正因，不動三法，名祕密藏；自住其中亦用度人，下文云：「佛自住大乘」，即別、圓二觀(云云)。

As for the contemplation-of-mind reading of "mountain": if one contemplates the aggregate of form as insentient, like the mountain; the aggregate of consciousness as numinous, like the vulture's spirit; and the remaining three aggregates as the vulture's body — then to contemplate this "sacred vulture" as impermanent is the analytical contemplation; to contemplate this "sacred vulture" as itself empty is the comprehending contemplation. The numinous spirit corresponds to the illuminating cause, the adornment of wisdom; the vulture corresponds to the accumulating conditioning cause, the adornment of merit; and the mountain corresponds to the direct cause of dharma-nature — these three unmoving factors are called the "secret treasury." To abide within it oneself, and also to use it to save others — the text below says, "the Buddha himself abides in the Great Vehicle" — this is the twofold contemplation of the distinct and perfect teachings (and so on).

「中」者，佛好中道，升中天、中日、降中國、中夜滅，皆表中道；今處山中，說中道也。

As for "amid" (i.e., "in the midst of Vulture Peak"): the Buddha favors the middle way — he descended to be born amid the heavens, at midday, into the Middle Kingdom, and passed into extinction at midnight — all of these signify the middle way; and now, dwelling amid the mountain, he preaches the middle way.

釋同聞眾為三：初聲聞、次菩薩、後雜眾，諸經多爾。舊云有事、有義。事者，逐形迹親疎，聲聞形出俗網，迹近如來，證經為親，故前列也；天人形乖服異，迹非侍奉，證經為疎，故後列也；菩薩形不檢節、迹無定處，既不同俗，復異於僧，處季孟之間，故居中仲也。有義者，聲聞欣涅槃，天人著生死，各有所偏，菩薩不欣、不著，居中求宗，故在兩間。《釋論》意亦爾，此一解似兩釋，事解似因緣，義解似約教(云云)。

Ru-Yi Translation

The exegesis of the co-assembled listeners falls into three groups: first the śrāvakas, next the bodhisattvas, and last the miscellaneous assembly — most sūtras follow this pattern. Older explanations give both a "factual" and a "doctrinal" account. In the factual account: the order follows outward forms and traces, near or distant. The śrāvakas' outward form has left the net of worldly life, and their trace is close to the Tathāgata, so that in bearing witness to the sūtra they are "close," and hence are listed first; the gods and humans differ in form and dress, and their trace is not one of attending as monastics, so that in bearing witness to the sūtra they are "distant," and hence are listed last; the bodhisattvas' outward form is not bound by monastic regulation, and their trace has no fixed place — being neither identical to the laity nor different from the monastic assembly, they occupy a position between the two, like a middle sibling between elder and younger, and hence are placed in the middle. In the doctrinal account: the śrāvakas delight in nirvāṇa, while gods and humans are attached to saṃsāra — each has its own bias; the bodhisattvas neither delight in nirvāṇa nor are attached to saṃsāra, but dwell in the middle seeking the true source, and hence stand between the two. The sense of the Śāstra is likewise so. This single explanation resembles two readings: the "factual" explanation resembles the "causes and conditions" reading, and the "doctrinal" explanation resembles the reading "in terms of the teachings" (and so on).

本迹解者，聲聞內祕外現，何甞保證涅槃；天人皆大薩埵，豈復耽染生死？皆是迹引二邊，而本常中道也。

觀心釋者，從假入空觀，即偏破生死；從空入假觀，即偏破涅槃，中道正觀無復前後(云云)。

As for the "trace and origin" interpretation: the śrāvakas, secretly bodhisattvas within while outwardly manifesting as śrāvakas, have never truly clung to and confirmed nirvāṇa; the gods and humans, in reality all great bodhisattvas, are hardly indulging in and attached to saṃsāra — all of this is the trace drawing forth an appearance at the two extremes, while their origin ever abides in the middle way.

As for the "contemplation of mind" reading: the contemplation of entering emptiness from the provisional is the one-sided breaking of attachment to saṃsāra; the contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness is the one-sided breaking of attachment to nirvāṇa; the correct contemplation of the middle way admits no such sequence of before and after (and so on).

列聲聞為二：先比丘、次比丘尼。比丘又二：先列多知識，次列少知識；舊呼為大名聞、小名聞。雖然無據，今依文判如此。就多知識眾為六：一類、二數、三位、四歎、五列名、六結。

The listing of the śrāvakas falls into two: first the bhikṣus, next the bhikṣuṇīs. The bhikṣus are again divided into two: first those listed as widely renowned, next those listed as less renowned — traditionally called "greater renown" and "lesser renown." Although there is no firm basis for this, the present commentary follows the text and makes this division accordingly. With regard to the assembly of the widely renowned, the exposition proceeds under six headings: first, their category; second, their number; third, their rank; fourth, praise of their virtues; fifth, the listing of their names; sixth, the conclusion.

一、類者，皆是大比丘氣類也。譬群方貴賤，各有班輩。今諸比丘，皆眾所知識、高譽大德也。

First, as to category: they are all of the class of "great bhikṣus." It is like how, among the various regions, the noble and the humble each have their own ranks and cohorts. Here, all these bhikṣus are ones widely known to the assembly, of lofty renown and great virtue.

《釋論》明「與」者，共義，舉七一解共，謂一時、一處、一戒、一心、一見、一道、一解脫也。若歷教，應各明七一：三藏一七一，通教二七一，別教無量七一，圓教一七一。若未發迹，正是三藏、通教中七一，直明兩意幾異，時、處、戒、解脫是同，心、見、道三種則異；若至開三顯一，即得入圓教七一也。

The Śāstra explains "together with" (in "together with the great bhikṣus") as meaning "in common," and sets forth "seven ones" to explain this commonality: one time, one place, one set of precepts, one mind, one view, one path, and one liberation. If one runs through the four teachings, each should have its own "seven ones": the Tripiṭaka teaching has one set of the seven ones, the shared teaching has two, the distinct teaching has innumerable, and the perfect teaching has one. If the origin has not yet been disclosed, then this passage properly refers to the "seven ones" shared by the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings, and one need only clarify how these two senses differ slightly: time, place, precepts, and liberation are the same between them, while mind, view, and path differ among the three; once one reaches the "opening of the three to reveal the one," one then gains entry into the perfect teaching's "seven ones."

《法華》論四種聲聞，今開住果者為兩：析法住果是三藏聲聞，體法住果是通教聲聞。開應化者為兩：登地應化別教聲聞，登住應化圓教聲聞。開佛道聲聞亦為兩：令他次第聞佛道是別教聲聞，令他不次第聞佛道即圓聲聞。聲聞義浩然，云何以證涅槃者判之(云云)。

The Treatise on the Lotus Sūtra speaks of four kinds of śrāvakas. Here, "those abiding in the fruit" are opened into two: those abiding in the fruit through the analytical method are the śrāvakas of the Tripiṭaka teaching, while those abiding in the fruit through the comprehending method are the śrāvakas of the shared teaching. "Those manifesting a response" are opened into two: those who, having ascended to the bodhisattva grounds, manifest a response, are the śrāvakas of the distinct teaching; those who, having ascended to the ten abidings, manifest a response, are the śrāvakas of the perfect teaching. "Śrāvakas of the Buddha path" are likewise opened into two: those who cause others to hear the Buddha path in sequence are the śrāvakas of the distinct teaching; those who cause others to hear the Buddha path without sequence are the śrāvakas of the perfect teaching. The meaning of "śrāvaka" is vast and manifold — how, then, could one judge it solely by those who merely bear witness to nirvāṇa (and so on)?

「大」者，《釋論》明「大」者，亦言多，亦言勝，器量尊重為天王等大人所敬故言大，升出九十五種道外故言勝，遍知內外經書故言多，又數至一萬二千故言多。今明有大道故、有大用故、有大知故，故言大。勝者，道勝、用勝、知勝，故言勝。多者，道多、用多、知多，故言多。道即性念處，大於一切智外道；用即共念處，勝神通外道；知即緣念處，多四韋陀外道也。約教釋大、多、勝者，大人所敬等，是三藏中釋耳。大者，大力羅漢所敬也；多者，遍知生滅即無生滅法也；勝者，勝三藏四門也，此通教釋也。又大者，體法大力羅漢所敬也；多者，恒沙佛法皆知也；勝者，勝二乘人，此別教釋也。又大者，諸大菩薩所敬也；多者，法界不可量法悉知也；勝者，勝諸菩薩也，此圓教釋也。

As for "great": the Śāstra, in explaining "great," also speaks of "many" and "superior." The bhikṣus' capacity and stature are held in such high regard that they are revered by heavenly kings and other great persons — hence called "great"; they have risen above and gone beyond the ninety-five heterodox paths — hence called "superior"; they comprehensively know the scriptures both within and outside the Buddhist canon — hence called "many"; and, further, their number reaches twelve thousand — hence also called "many." Here it is explained that they possess the great path, the great function, and the great knowledge — hence called "great." As "superior": their path is superior, their function is superior, their knowledge is superior — hence called "superior." As "many": their path is manifold, their function is manifold, their knowledge is manifold — hence called "many." The "path" corresponds to the mindfulness of true nature, greater than the omniscience claimed by the non-Buddhists; the "function" corresponds to the shared mindfulness, superior to the mere supernatural powers of the non-Buddhists; the "knowledge" corresponds to the conditioned mindfulness, exceeding the four Vedas of the non-Buddhists. As for explaining "great," "many," and "superior" in terms of the teachings: being "revered by great persons" and the rest is simply the exegesis within the Tripiṭaka teaching. In the shared teaching: "great" means being revered by arhats of great power; "many" means comprehensively knowing that arising-and-ceasing is itself the dharma of no-arising-and-no-ceasing; "superior" means surpassing the four approaches of the Tripiṭaka teaching. Furthermore, in the distinct teaching: "great" means being revered by arhats of great power who comprehend dharmas; "many" means knowing all the Buddha-dharmas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges; "superior" means surpassing the practitioners of the two lesser vehicles. Furthermore, in the perfect teaching: "great" means being revered by all the great bodhisattvas; "many" means knowing all the immeasurable dharmas of the dharma-realm; "superior" means surpassing all the other bodhisattvas.

本迹者，此諸大德久為諸佛之所咨嗟，本得勝幢三昧超諸外道，先已成就種智遍知，迹來輔佛行化，示作愛見中大、多、勝，欲引乳入酪；又作三藏中大、多、勝，欲引酪入生蘇；示方等中大、多、勝，欲引生蘇入熟蘇；示轉教作《般若》中大、多、勝；欲引熟蘇入醍醐故作《法華》中大、多、勝也，然其本地大、多、勝又矣(云云)。

觀心者，空觀為大，假觀為多，中觀為勝。又直就中觀心性廣博猶若虛空，故名大；雙遮二邊入寂滅海，故名勝；雙照二諦多所含容，一心一切心，故名多也。

As for the "trace and origin" reading: these great worthies have long been praised by the buddhas; in their origin they attained the samādhi of the Victory Banner, surpassing all the non-Buddhists, and had already accomplished the omniscience of the wisdom of all modes — as trace, they have come to assist the Buddha in carrying out his teaching, showing themselves as "great," "many," and "superior" within the affliction of love and views, in order to lead beings, as milk, into curds; then showing themselves as "great," "many," and "superior" within the Tripiṭaka teaching, in order to lead the curds into fresh cream; showing themselves as "great," "many," and "superior" within the Vaipulya teachings, in order to lead the fresh cream into refined cream; showing themselves, in the shift to the teaching of the Prajñāpāramitā, as "great," "many," and "superior" there — in order to lead the refined cream into ghee, they show themselves in the Lotus Sūtra as "great," "many," and "superior." Yet their fundamental, original "greatness," "manyness," and "superiority" exceed even this (and so on).

As for the contemplation-of-mind reading: the emptiness contemplation is "great"; the provisional contemplation is "many"; the middle contemplation is "superior." Furthermore, directly in terms of the middle contemplation: the nature of mind, vast and broad like empty space, is therefore called "great"; simultaneously negating both extremes and entering the sea of quiescent cessation is therefore called "superior"; simultaneously illuminating the two truths and embracing much within, one mind containing all minds, is therefore called "many."

「比丘」者，肇師云：「秦言淨命，乞食、破煩惱、能持戒、怖魔等，天竺一名含此四義，秦無以翻，故存本稱。」什師云：「始出妻子家，應以乞食自資，清淨活命；終出三界家，必須破煩惱，持戒自守。具此二義，天魔怖其出境也。」《釋論》云：「怖魔、破惡、乞士，魔樂生死，其既出家復化餘人，俱離三界乖於魔意，魔用力制翻被五繫，但愁懼而已，故名怖魔。出家人必破身、口七惡，故言破惡。夫在家三種如法，一田、二商、三仕，用養身命；出家人佛不許此，唯乞自濟，身安道存，福利檀越，三義相成即比丘義也。」《涅槃》、《寶梁》皆舉破惡名比丘者，不具說也。今明此三義，應通初、後，如初出家時白四羯磨，無作戒力遍一切境，翻無作惡；初修禪定發定共戒，防伏意地貪、瞋不起；初修觀慧發相似道共戒，能伏煩惱。初心亦稱破惡，何獨後心耶？怖魔者，初剃髮、稟戒，已令魔愁，修定欲伏惱煩，修慧欲破煩惱，初心亦令魔怖，何獨後心耶？乞士者，初離邪命以乞自活、修禪歷境、求定修慧緣理求無漏，皆是乞士，何況相應而非乞士？具此義故通名比丘。依經家皆歎後心比丘耳，此皆三藏意。若歷緣求真名乞士，破障理之惑名破惡，修此行怖四魔，即通教義。若歷三諦求理名乞士，除通、別惑名破惡，怖八魔十魔者，即別義。若即生死求實相味名乞士，達煩惱即菩提名破惡，魔界即佛界者，是圓教義。若未發迹但明前二義，若已顯本，具後意也。

As for "bhikṣu": Sengzhao says, "In Chinese it means 'pure livelihood,' 'one who begs for food,' 'one who destroys the afflictions,' 'one who is able to uphold the precepts,' 'one who frightens Māra,' and so forth — the single Indian term contains all these four senses, and since there is no single Chinese word for it, the original term is kept." Kumārajīva says: "At the outset, having left behind wife and children and household, one ought to sustain oneself by begging for food, purely maintaining one's life; ultimately, having left the household of the three realms, one must destroy the afflictions and uphold the precepts to guard oneself. Having both these senses complete, the heavenly Māra is frightened by the monk's having gone beyond his domain." The Śāstra says: "'Frightening Māra,' 'destroying evil,' and 'mendicant' — Māra delights in saṃsāra; when a monk has left the household and, moreover, converts others to do likewise, all of them leave the three realms, going against Māra's wishes; though Māra tries to use his power to restrain them, he instead finds himself bound by the five fetters, left only with sorrow and fear — hence 'one who frightens Māra.' One who leaves the household must destroy the seven evils of body and speech — hence 'one who destroys evil.' Now, there are three proper occupations for a householder — farming, trade, and government service — by which to sustain body and life; the Buddha does not permit these to one who has left the household, who relies solely on begging to sustain himself, so that his body is at ease and the path is preserved, and merit and benefit accrue to his donors — these three senses, taken together, constitute the meaning of 'bhikṣu.'" The Nirvāṇa Sūtra and the Ratnakūṭa both cite "destroying evil" as the meaning of "bhikṣu," without giving the full explanation. Here it is made clear that these three senses should apply throughout, both at the beginning and at the end of practice: just as, when one first leaves the household, through the fourfold announcement-and-motion ordination procedure, the power of the unconditioned precept pervades every domain, thereby countering unconditioned evil; when one first cultivates meditative concentration, giving rise to the precept coextensive with concentration, one guards against and subdues the arising of greed and anger in the mental sphere; when one first cultivates contemplative wisdom, giving rise to the precept coextensive with the semblance path, one is able to subdue the afflictions. The initial resolve, too, may be called "destroying evil" — why should this be reserved solely for the final stage? As for "frightening Māra": from the very moment of shaving one's head and receiving the precepts, Māra is already made anxious; cultivating concentration aims to subdue the afflictions, cultivating wisdom aims to destroy the afflictions — the initial resolve, too, makes Māra afraid; why should this be reserved solely for the final stage? As for "mendicant": from the outset, leaving wrong livelihood to sustain oneself by begging, cultivating meditation amid varied circumstances, seeking concentration, cultivating wisdom in reliance on principle, and seeking the untainted — all of this is "mendicancy"; how much more so, then, once practice and principle correspond, would one not be a true mendicant? Having all these senses, one is generally called "bhikṣu." The sūtra-compilers, in praising the assembly, praise only bhikṣus of the final stage — but this is entirely the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching. If one seeks the truth by moving through varied circumstances, this is called "mendicant"; destroying the delusion that obstructs principle is called "destroying evil"; cultivating this practice frightens the four Māras — this is the sense of the shared teaching. If one seeks principle by moving through the three truths, this is called "mendicant"; removing the delusions addressed by the shared and distinct teachings is called "destroying evil"; frightening the eight Māras or ten Māras — this is the sense of the distinct teaching. If, directly within saṃsāra, one seeks the flavor of ultimate reality, this is called "mendicant"; comprehending that the afflictions are themselves bodhi is called "destroying evil"; understanding that the domain of Māra is itself the domain of the Buddha — this is the sense of the perfect teaching. If the origin has not yet been disclosed, then only the first two senses apply; once the origin has been revealed, the latter senses are fully present as well.

本迹者，本登涅槃山頂，與無明癡愛父母、結業妻子別，出分段、變易家，久除五住，何惡不破？獲真法喜，如食乳糜更無所須。持中道道共尸波羅蜜攝眾生戒度，魔界降伏，即佛界如。堪任乘御，本地功德久已成就，為調眾生，迹示五味比丘傳引眾生，例如前釋。

觀心者，觀一念心淨若虛空，不為二邊桎梏所礙，平等大慧無住、無著，即名出家；以中觀自資、活法身慧命，名為乞士；觀五住煩惱即是菩提，是名破惡；一切諸邊顛倒無非中道，即是怖魔(云云)。

As for "trace and origin": in origin, these great bhikṣus have already ascended the peak of the mountain of nirvāṇa, taking leave of ignorance, delusion, and craving as their "parents," and of karmic bonds as their "spouse and children," going forth from the household of both segmental and transformational rebirth, having long ago eliminated the five categories of affliction — what evil remains undestroyed? Having attained true dharma-joy, they are like one who, having eaten the milk-rice gruel that ended the Buddha's asceticism, needs nothing further. Upholding the precept-pāramitā coextensive with the middle way, gathering sentient beings through the perfection of morality, they have subdued the domain of Māra, so that it is identical with the domain of the Buddha. Fit to guide and be guided, their fundamental merit has long since been accomplished; yet, in order to tame sentient beings, as trace they display themselves as bhikṣus across the Buddha's teaching in the five flavors, drawing sentient beings onward — this follows the same pattern as explained above.

As for the contemplation of mind: to contemplate the single thought-moment of mind as pure like empty space, unobstructed by the shackles of the two extremes, its impartial great wisdom without abiding, without attachment — this is called "leaving the household"; to sustain oneself by the middle-way contemplation, nourishing the life and wisdom of the dharma-body — this is called "mendicant"; to contemplate the afflictions of the five categories as themselves bodhi — this is called "destroying evil"; to see that all the distorted views at every extreme are nothing other than the middle way — this is "frightening Māra" (and so on).

「眾」者，天竺云僧伽，此翻和合眾，一人不名和合，四人已上乃名和合，事和無別眾，法和無別理。佛常與千二百五十人俱，三迦葉千人，身子、目連二百五十。又云，耶舍五十。《雜阿含》四十五云：「五百比丘中，九十人三明，九十人俱解脫，餘但慧解脫。」《釋論》明四種僧：不依淨命名破戒僧，不解法律名愚癡僧，五方便名慚愧僧，苦法忍去名真實僧。此中非三種，但是真實僧。若依四教者，此僧歷偏、圓五味座，作同聞人，今正是圓教中證信也。

本迹釋者，本與實相理和，又與法界眾生機緣和，而迹為半字事理之僧，歷五味中引諸眾生(云云)。

觀解者，初學中觀入相似觀，既未發真，慚第一義天、愧諸聖人，即是有羞僧；觀慧若發即真實僧。若異此者，即前兩僧：不依觀行名破戒僧，不解觀相名愚癡僧。舉類義竟。

As for "assembly": in India it is called saṃgha, translated as "harmonious assembly" — a single person cannot be called "harmonious," but from four persons upward it may be so called. Harmony in practice means there is no assembly apart from this harmony; harmony in Dharma means there is no principle apart from this harmony. The Buddha was constantly accompanied by twelve hundred and fifty persons: the three Kāśyapa brothers accounted for a thousand, and Śāriputra and Maudgalyāyana together for two hundred and fifty. Another account gives Yaśas's fifty disciples, making up the difference. The Saṃyukta Āgama, fascicle forty-five, says: "Among the five hundred bhikṣus, ninety possessed the three insights, ninety were fully liberated in both respects, and the rest were liberated through wisdom alone." The Śāstra distinguishes four kinds of saṃgha: those who do not rely on pure livelihood are called the "precept-breaking saṃgha"; those who do not understand the Dharma and Vinaya are called the "ignorant saṃgha"; those still practicing the five expedient means are called the "saṃgha with a sense of shame"; those from the patient acceptance of the truth of suffering onward are called the "true saṃgha." Among these, the assembly here is not of the first three kinds, but is solely the "true saṃgha." If one follows the four teachings, this saṃgha, having passed through the seats of the partial and perfect teachings across the five flavors, serves as the co-assembled witnesses of this sūtra — and here it is precisely the witnessing and confirming saṃgha of the perfect teaching.

As for the "trace and origin" reading: in origin, they are in harmony with the principle of ultimate reality, and further in harmony with the capacities and conditions of the sentient beings of the dharma-realm; as trace, they appear as the saṃgha of the "half-word," provisional, practice and principle, passing through the five flavors to draw forth the various sentient beings (and so on).

As for the contemplative interpretation: the beginning student, within the middle contemplation, enters the semblance contemplation; not having yet given rise to the true wisdom, he feels shame before the heaven of ultimate meaning and remorse before the sages — this is the "saṃgha possessing a sense of shame"; once contemplative wisdom arises, this is the "true saṃgha." Apart from these, there are the earlier two kinds of saṃgha: those who do not rely on contemplative practice are the "precept-breaking saṃgha," and those who do not understand the marks of contemplation are the "ignorant saṃgha." Here ends the topic of "category."

二、明數者，即是一萬二千人也。本迹者，本是一萬二千菩薩，迹為萬二千聲聞也。觀者，觀十二入，一入具十法界，一界又十界，界界各十如是，即是一千，一入既一千，十二入即是萬二千法門也。

Second, as to the number: this is the twelve thousand persons. As for "trace and origin": in origin they are twelve thousand bodhisattvas; as trace they are twelve thousand śrāvakas. As for the contemplative reading: to contemplate the twelve sense-bases — each base contains the ten dharma-realms, each realm in turn containing the other ten realms, and each realm-within-a-realm possessing the ten "suchnesses" — yields one thousand; since each base already yields a thousand, the twelve bases together yield the twelve thousand dharma-gates.

三、明位者，皆是阿羅漢也。《阿颰經》云應真；《瑞應》云真人，悉是無生釋羅漢也。依舊翻云無著、不生、應供。或言無翻，名含三義。無明糠脫，後世田中不受生死果報，故云不生；九十八使煩惱盡，故名殺賊；具智斷功德，堪為人天福田，故言應供。含此三義釋阿羅漢也。或言初始學無生，生未無生，初雖怖魔，魔未大怖，初雖乞士，未是灼然應供；今獲無生忍，破煩惱賊盡，是好良田，以果對因釋羅漢三義。若論成就應取果三義，若通於初，亦取因三義，如此釋者，皆三藏通中意耳。若別、圓者義則不然，非但殺賊，亦殺不賊，不賊者涅槃是，是亦須破故，是殺賊義。不生於生，亦不生不生，無漏是不生，非但應供亦是供應，一切眾生是供應，皆歎初地、初住德也。

本迹者，本得不受三昧，於二邊無所著，故名不生；斷五住惑，故名殺賊；能福九道饒益眾生，故有應供。本義也。方便度眾生，歷五味傳傳作不生，迹也。又本是法身，迹示己利；本是般若，迹示不生；本是解脫，迹示殺賊(云云)。

Third, as to rank: these bhikṣus are all arhats. The Ābhāsa Sūtra calls this "the worthy one"; the Ruiying [Sūtra] calls it "the true person" — both are exegeses of "arhat" in the sense of "no-arising." In the traditional translation, the term is rendered as "without attachment," "no-arising," and "worthy of offerings" — or it is said to admit of no single translation, the name containing all three senses. Freed from the husk of ignorance, in future ages he no longer undergoes the fruits and effects of birth and death in the karmic field — hence "no-arising"; the ninety-eight proclivities of affliction being exhausted, he is called "killer of the thief" of affliction; endowed with the merit of wisdom and its severing of delusion, fit to be a field of merit for humans and gods, he is called "worthy of offerings." Containing these three senses, the term explains "arhat." Another account holds that at the very outset of learning "no-arising," arising is not yet no-arising; though at first one "frightens Māra," Māra is not yet greatly frightened; though at first one is a "mendicant," one is not yet manifestly "worthy of offerings"; only now, having attained the patient acceptance of no-arising and utterly destroyed the thief of affliction, does one become a good field of merit — this explains the three senses of "arhat" by pairing effect against cause. If discussing accomplishment, one should take the three senses in terms of the effect; if extending this to the beginning of practice as well, one may also take the three senses in terms of the cause — but such explanations belong entirely to the sense of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings. In the distinct and perfect teachings, the meaning is otherwise: it is not only "killing the thief" of affliction, but also "killing the non-thief" — the "non-thief" being nirvāṇa itself, which, too, must be transcended, and this too is the sense of "killing the thief." It is "not arising with respect to arising," and also "not arising with respect to non-arising" — the untainted itself is "no-arising"; and it is not only "worthy of offerings," but also itself "offering in response" — all sentient beings being worthy of offering-and-response. All of this praises the virtue of the first bodhisattva ground or the first abiding.

As for "trace and origin": in origin, these arhats have attained the samādhi of non-reception, unattached to either extreme — hence called "no-arising"; having severed the delusions of the five categories, they are called "killer of the thief"; being able to bring benefit and blessing to sentient beings across the nine realms, they are "worthy of offerings" — this is the meaning in terms of origin. As trace, through skillful means they save sentient beings, passing through the five flavors, appearing again and again as "no-arising" arhats — this is the meaning in terms of trace. Furthermore: in origin they are the dharma-body, as trace they display "one's own benefit"; in origin they are prajñā, as trace they display "no-arising"; in origin they are liberation, as trace they display "killing the thief" (and so on).

觀心者，空觀是般若，假觀是解脫，中觀是法身。又觀心者，從假入空，觀亦有三義，乃至中道觀，殺無明賊，不生二乘心，供養此人如供養世尊。《方等》云：「供佛及文殊，不如施行方等者一食充軀。」下文云：「毀讚佛罪福輕，毀讚持經者罪福重。」何者？

佛無食想，久離八風不為損益；施持經者，全肉身、續報命、生法身、增慧命，故有益；毀之，憂惱退悔，若失好時則不可救，故大損(云云)。

As for the contemplation of mind: the emptiness contemplation is prajñā, the provisional contemplation is liberation, the middle contemplation is the dharma-body. Further, as for the contemplation of mind: from entering emptiness through the provisional, the contemplation likewise has three senses, up to and including the contemplation of the middle way — it slays the thief of ignorance and does not give rise to the mind of the two lesser vehicles; to make offerings to such a person is to make offerings to the World-Honored One himself. The Vaipulya [sūtras] say: "To make offerings to the Buddha and to Mañjuśrī is not equal to sustaining, with a single meal, one who practices the Vaipulya teaching." The text below says: "The offense or merit of slandering or praising the Buddha is light; the offense or merit of slandering or praising one who upholds this sūtra is grave." Why is this?

The Buddha has no thought of food, having long since transcended the eight winds, unaffected by loss or gain; but one who sustains an upholder of the sūtra thereby preserves that person's very flesh, continues the span of his karmic life, nourishes his dharma-body, and increases his life of wisdom — hence there is merit in it. To slander such a person brings anguish and regret, and, if the right occasion is once lost, cannot be remedied — hence there is grave loss in it (and so on).

四、歎德，文有五句歎上三德。《法華論》云：「初句總，後句別。」當知諸句皆歎羅漢句耳。

「諸漏已盡，無復煩惱」，此兩句歎上殺賊。漏者三漏也，《成論》云：「失道故名漏。」律云：「癡人造業，開諸漏門。」毘曇云：「漏落生死。」論、律語異而同明漏義。良由賊誑失於理寶，貧窮孤露造諸惡業、致生死苦，亡法身、失慧命、喪重寶，皆是賊義。不應謂是不生義歎德也。煩惱者，即九十八使，流扼纏蓋等逼惱行人。煩惱是能潤，漏業是所潤，能所既盡，正是殺賊義，那得作不生歎耶？

「逮得己利」一句，是歎應供，三界因果皆名為他，智斷功德皆名己利，己利具足故成應供。

「盡諸有結，心得自在」兩句，是歎不生。諸有即二十五有，生處也；結即二十五有，生因也。因盡果亡，歎不生明矣！不應作殺賊歎也。羅漢但應結盡，未應有盡；有盡者，因中說果，又盡在不久也。

「心得自在」者，定具足名心自在，慧具足名慧自在，慧自在未必心自在，心自在必慧自在。今言心自在，即是定、慧具足俱解脫人，俱解脫人生決定盡，驗知歎不生德也。若依《法華論》者，呼為上上起門，則是以後釋前也。論云：「以諸漏盡故名羅漢，以心得自在故名有結盡。」如是傳傳釋上也。

本迹者，不生不生名大涅槃，煩惱漏流其源久竭，不復墮落二乘及凡夫地，即本不生。法身智斷實相功德，名本己利；得王三昧破二十五有，顯出我性具八自在我，名本殺賊。迹示二乘功德耳。

觀心者，中道正觀不漏落空、假二邊，二邊煩惱滅也，能觀心性名為上定，衣珠祕藏是己之物即己利也，正觀中道結賊則斷，無結故有亦斷，二邊不能縛心故名自在，雖有煩惱如無煩惱，不斷煩惱而入涅槃即其義也。

Fourth, as to the praise of their virtues: the text gives five phrases praising the three virtues described above. The Treatise on the Lotus Sūtra says: "The first phrase is general, the latter phrases are particular" — one should understand that all these phrases are, in fact, phrases praising the meaning of "arhat."

"All outflows exhausted, no more affliction" — these two phrases praise "killing the thief" above. "Outflow" refers to the three outflows. The Chengshi lun says: "It is called 'outflow' because it causes one to lose the path." The Vinaya says: "The deluded person, in creating karma, opens the gates of the outflows." The Abhidharma says: "Outflow means falling into saṃsāra." Though the Śāstra and the Vinaya use different words, they equally clarify the meaning of "outflow." It is precisely because the thief deceives and causes one to lose the treasure of principle, so that, impoverished and destitute, one creates all manner of evil karma and brings on the suffering of saṃsāra, losing the dharma-body, losing the life of wisdom, and losing this great treasure — all of this is the sense of "thief." One should not take this instead as praise in the sense of "no-arising." "Affliction" refers to the ninety-eight proclivities — the floods, the yokes, the entanglements, the hindrances, and the rest, which oppress and afflict the practitioner. Affliction is that which moistens karma into further rebirth; the karma of outflow is that which is moistened; once both the moistener and the moistened are exhausted, this is precisely the sense of "killing the thief" — how, then, could it be taken as praise in the sense of "no-arising"?

The single phrase "having attained one's own benefit" praises the virtue of "worthy of offerings": all the causes and effects of the three realms are called "other," while the merit of wisdom and its severing of delusion are called "one's own benefit" — since one's own benefit is fully complete, one becomes worthy of offerings.

"All the bonds of existence exhausted, the mind attaining mastery" — these two phrases praise "no-arising." The "existences" are the twenty-five realms of existence, the places of rebirth; the "bonds" are the causes of rebirth within these twenty-five existences. With the cause exhausted, the effect vanishes — this plainly praises "no-arising"; it should not be taken as praise for "killing the thief." Strictly speaking, an arhat should only have the bonds exhausted, not yet have existence itself exhausted; the phrase "existence exhausted" speaks of the effect in terms of the cause, meaning that its exhaustion is imminent.

As for "the mind attaining mastery": when concentration is complete, this is called mastery of mind; when wisdom is complete, this is called mastery of wisdom. Mastery of wisdom does not necessarily entail mastery of mind, but mastery of mind necessarily entails mastery of wisdom. Here, "mastery of mind" refers to one who is complete in both concentration and wisdom, one liberated in both respects; for such a person, rebirth is certainly exhausted — this confirms that this phrase praises the virtue of "no-arising." According to the Treatise on the Lotus Sūtra, this is called the "gate of arising from the higher into the higher," which is to explain the earlier phrase by means of the later. The Treatise says: "Because all outflows are exhausted, one is called an arhat; because the mind attains mastery, one is said to have the bonds of existence exhausted" — thus each phrase is explained, one by the next, in succession.

As for "trace and origin": "no-arising with respect to no-arising" is called great nirvāṇa — the source of the outflowing stream of affliction having long since dried up, such a one no longer falls back into the two lesser vehicles or the stage of an ordinary being — this is fundamental "no-arising." The merit of ultimate reality, the dharma-body and the wisdom that severs delusion, is called fundamental "own-benefit"; attaining the kingly samādhi, destroying the twenty-five realms of existence, and manifesting the true nature of self endowed with the eight masteries of self, is called fundamental "killing the thief." As trace, these arhats merely display the merit of the two lesser vehicles.

As for the contemplation of mind: the correct contemplation of the middle way does not fall into the outflow of either the empty or the provisional extreme, and the afflictions of both extremes are thereby extinguished; the mind able to perform this contemplation is called the "supreme concentration"; the jewel sewn into one's robe, the secret treasury, being one's own possession, is "one's own benefit"; when the correct contemplation of the middle way is achieved, the thief of the karmic bonds is severed, and since there are no bonds, "existence" too is severed; since neither extreme can bind the mind, this is called "mastery"; though affliction is present, it is as though there were none — this is the very meaning of "not severing affliction, yet entering nirvāṇa."

五、列名，略舉二十一尊者，佛諸弟子皆備眾行，而隱其圓能各從一德標名者，欲引偏好故。《增一阿含》云：「憍陳如比丘，皆共上座名者，有德大人相隨，舍利弗共智慧深利者相隨，目連共神通大力者相隨。」皆掌一法，引諸偏好意也。若欲消名須識其行，從德立號無往不通也。一一羅漢例作四釋(云云)。

Fifth, as to the listing of names: the text briefly cites twenty-one venerable ones. All of the Buddha's disciples are complete in every practice, yet, concealing their perfect capability, each is named for a single virtue, in order to draw in beings of particular inclinations. The Ekottarika Āgama says: "Bhikṣu Kauṇḍinya was accompanied by those who shared the name 'elder'; those of great virtue kept company with one another; Śāriputra was accompanied by those of deep and keen wisdom; Maudgalyāyana was accompanied by those of great supernatural power." Each held sway in a single dharma, drawing in beings according to their particular inclinations. If one wishes to explain each name, one must know the corresponding practice; naming by virtue, the explanation will go through without obstruction in every case. Each and every arhat, in turn, will be given the fourfold exegesis (and so on).

「憍陳如」，姓也，此翻火器，婆羅門種，其先事火，從此命族。

火有二義：照也、燒也。照則闇不生，燒則物不生，此以不生為姓。「阿若」者，名也，此翻已知，或言無知，無知者非無所知也，乃是知無耳，若依二諦即是知真，以無生智為名也。《無量壽》、《文殊問》、《阿毘曇》、《婆沙》皆稱為了本際、知本際，若依四諦即是知滅，而諸經多名為無知，或翻為得道。《增一阿含》云：「我佛法中寬仁博識，初受法味者，拘隣如比丘第一。」故以阿若為名也。願者，佛昔於饑世，化為赤目大魚，閉氣不喘示為死相，木工五人先斧斫魚肉，佛時誓言：「於當來世先度此等。」先願與其無生，故云阿若。又迦葉佛時，九人學道，五人未得果，誓於釋迦法中最先開悟，本願所牽，前得無生，故名阿若。行者，智生惑滅智斷行也。夫巨夜長寢無人能覺，日光未出明星前現，憍陳如比丘，初得無生智，譬若明星在眾明之始，一切人智明無前陳如，故名阿若。最先破闇莫過明星，陳如亦爾，一切人闇滅無前陳如，故名阿若。前者，太子棄國捐王入山學道，父王思念遣五人追侍，所謂拘隣；頞鞞，亦云濕鞞，亦阿說示，亦馬星跋提，亦摩訶男，十力迦葉，拘利太子，二是母親，三是父親。二人以欲為淨，三人以苦行為淨。太子勤行苦行，二人便捨之去，三人猶侍；太子捨苦行，還受飲食蘇油、煖水，三人又捨去。太子得道，先為五人說四諦，初教二人，拘隣法眼淨，四人未得，三人乞食，六人共噉；次教三人，三人法眼淨，二人乞食六人共噉；第三說法時，拘隣五人、八萬諸天遠塵離垢，五人得無生，佛三問：「知法未？」即三答云：「已如。」地神唱，空神傳，乃至梵世。

咸稱已知，拘隣最前，初見佛道相，初聞法鼓，初服道香，初甞甘露，初入法流，初登真諦，閻浮提得道，最在一切人、一切天、一切羅漢前，故《十二遊經》云：「佛成道第一年度五人，第二年度三迦葉，第五年度身子、目連。」當知阿若在前明矣。此因緣釋也。

"Kauṇḍinya" is his family name, translated as "Fire Vessel" — he was of the brahmin caste, whose ancestors served the fire-god, and from this his lineage took its name.

"Fire" carries two senses: illumination and burning. Where there is illumination, darkness does not arise; where there is burning, things do not arise — the family name is taken from this sense of "non-arising." "Ājñāta" is his given name, translated as "already known," or also rendered "without knowledge" — "without knowledge" does not mean without any knowledge at all, but rather knowing "nothingness"; in terms of the two truths, this is knowing the truth, taking the wisdom of no-arising as his name. The Sūtra of Immeasurable Life, the Questions of Mañjuśrī, the Abhidharma, and the Vibhāṣā all render it as "comprehending the original limit" or "knowing the original limit"; in terms of the four noble truths, this is knowing cessation, though most sūtras name it "no-knowledge," or else translate it as "attaining the path." The Ekottarika Āgama says: "Among those in my Buddha-Dharma of broad benevolence and vast learning, the first to receive the flavor of the Dharma is Bhikṣu Kauṇḍinya." Hence he was named "Ājñāta." As for his vow: long ago, in an age of famine, the Buddha, in a former life, transformed himself into a great fish with red eyes, holding his breath so as not to gasp, showing the appearance of death; five woodcutters were the first to hack at the fish's flesh with their axes. The Buddha then vowed: "In a future age, I shall save these five first." Having first vowed to bring them to no-arising, he is therefore called Ājñāta. Again, in the time of Kāśyapa Buddha, nine people were studying the path; five of them had not yet attained the fruit, and vowed to be the very first to awaken within Śākyamuni's teaching. Drawn by this original vow, he attained no-arising ahead of the others, and hence is named Ājñāta. As for "conduct": it is the conduct of wisdom arising and delusion being extinguished, the severing accomplished by wisdom. Now, through the long night of deep sleep, no one is able to awaken; before the sun's light rises, the morning star appears first. Bhikṣu Kauṇḍinya, the first to attain the wisdom of no-arising, is likened to the morning star at the very beginning of all lights — the wisdom of all people that shines forth has none before Kauṇḍinya, hence he is called Ājñāta. The very first to break the darkness is none other than the morning star; Kauṇḍinya is likewise so — the darkness of all people is dispelled with none preceding Kauṇḍinya, hence he is called Ājñāta. As to "preceding": when the crown prince abandoned his kingdom and renounced his throne to enter the mountains and study the path, his father the king, in longing concern, sent five men to follow and attend him — namely, Kauṇḍinya; Aśvajit, also called Vāṣpa, also Aśvaka; Bhadrika, also Mahānāman; Daśabala-Kāśyapa; and the prince of the Koliya clan — two of these were relatives of his mother, three were relatives of his father. The two maternal kin held that indulgence in desire was purity; the three paternal kin held that ascetic practice was purity. When the crown prince diligently practiced asceticism, the two maternal kin abandoned him and left, while the three paternal kin continued to attend him; when the crown prince gave up asceticism and resumed taking food, ghee, and warm water, the three paternal kin in turn abandoned him and left. When the crown prince attained the path, he first preached the four noble truths to the five men: he first taught two of them, and Kauṇḍinya's Dharma-eye became pure, while the other four had not yet attained it; the three as yet untaught went out to beg for food, and all six shared the meal together. He then taught the remaining three, and their Dharma-eyes became pure, while the first two went to beg for food, and again all six shared the meal together. At the third preaching, the five including Kauṇḍinya, together with eighty thousand devas, became free of dust and defilement; the five attained no-arising. The Buddha asked three times, "Do you now know the Dharma?" and they answered three times, "We have already thus understood." The earth-spirits proclaimed it, and the sky-spirits transmitted the news, on up to the Brahma heaven.

All declared it "already known," with Kauṇḍinya foremost among them: he was the first to see the mark of the Buddha's path, the first to hear the drum of the Dharma, the first to taste the fragrance of the path, the first to savor the ambrosia of the Dharma, the first to enter the stream of the Dharma, the first to ascend to the ultimate truth — attaining the path in Jambudvīpa before all other people, all other devas, and all other arhats. Hence the Sūtra of the Twelve Roamings says: "In the first year after the Buddha's attainment of the path, he saved the five men; in the second year, he saved the three Kāśyapa brothers; in the fifth year, he saved Śāriputra and Maudgalyāyana." One should understand from this that Ājñāta's precedence is thereby made clear. This is the "causes and conditions" exegesis.

三藏教者，盲譬無生智，鏡譬無生境，陰、入、界也，頭等六分譬現在因也，像譬未來果也。若開眼取鏡，形對像生，愚故不斷絕；若閉眼如盲，則無所見。不見六分是因不生，不見鏡像是果不生，故《阿含經》云：「若謂有色，色是淨，淨即生，非不生；若謂有受想行識，識是淨，淨即生，非不生；若謂有受，受是樂，樂即生，非不生；乃至色，色是樂，樂是生，非不生；若計有想行，行是我，我是生，非不生；乃至色，色是我，我是生，非不生；若計有識，識是常，常是生，非不生，乃至色，色是常，常是生非不生。」譬如執鏡見面，面是生，非不生，若謂有五陰，悉是生，非不生，若能知色非淨乃至識非常，又能知色無常、苦、空、不淨，乃至識無常、苦、無我、不淨者，是為不生，非是生。如盲執鏡不見像生，是為不生，非是生。既知不生，寧復於中計我是色，計我異色，我在色中，色在我中？乃至識亦如是。如是觀者，現因來果俱皆不生，如盲對鏡不見形像，是名觀陰無生觀智也。

As for the Tripiṭaka teaching's reading of "no-arising": blindness is likened to the wisdom of no-arising, and the mirror to the object contemplated in no-arising — that is, the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements. The head and the other five parts of the body reflected are likened to present causes, and the reflected image is likened to the future effect. If one opens the eyes and picks up the mirror, a form arises corresponding to the reflected image, and, being deluded, one does not sever attachment to it; if one closes the eyes as though blind, then nothing is seen. Not seeing the six parts is the "non-arising" of the cause; not seeing the mirror-image is the "non-arising" of the effect. Hence the Āgama Sūtra says: "If one holds that form exists, that form is pure, then this 'purity' is itself arising, not non-arising; if one holds that feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness exist, that consciousness is pure, then this 'purity' is itself arising, not non-arising; if one holds that feeling exists, that feeling is pleasure, then this 'pleasure' is itself arising, not non-arising — and so on up to form, holding that form is pleasure, then this 'pleasure' is arising, not non-arising; if one supposes that perception and mental formations exist, that mental formations are the self, then this 'self' is arising, not non-arising — and so on up to form, supposing that form is the self, then this 'self' is arising, not non-arising; if one supposes that consciousness exists, that consciousness is permanent, then this 'permanence' is arising, not non-arising — and so on up to form, supposing that form is permanent, then this 'permanence' is arising, not non-arising." It is like grasping a mirror and seeing one's face: the face reflected is arising, not non-arising; so too, if one holds that the five aggregates exist, this is entirely arising, not non-arising. But if one is able to know that form is not pure, and so on up to that consciousness is not permanent, and further is able to know that form is impermanent, suffering, empty, and impure, and so on up to that consciousness is impermanent, suffering, without self, and impure — this is "non-arising," not "arising." It is like the blind man holding the mirror and not seeing the image arise — this is "non-arising," not "arising." Having thus known non-arising, how could one still, within this, suppose that the self is form, that the self is different from form, that the self is within form, or that form is within the self? And so it is likewise for consciousness. Contemplating in this way, the present cause and the future effect are both, entirely, non-arising — like the blind man before the mirror, not seeing any image — this is called the contemplative wisdom of the non-arising of the aggregates.

觀入、界者，凡言海者，雖復深廣亦有此彼岸，蓋小水耳；若眼見色已，愛念染著貪樂，起身、口、意業者，是為大海，沈沒一切世間天、人、修羅。當知眼是大海，色是濤波，愛此色故是洄澓，於中起不善覺是惡魚龍，起妬害是男羅剎，起染愛是女鬼，起身、口、意是飲醎自沒，是為眼色無知而生無明愛，愛生故名為行；行生故名為業，業縛識入中陰，是為識生；所受胞胎五疱未成，是為名色生；五疱成已名六入生；六入未能別苦樂，名為觸生；別苦樂名受生；於塵起染名愛生；四方馳求名取生；造身、口、意名有生；應受未來五陰，名生生；未來陰變名老生，未來陰壞名死生，心中內熱名憂生，發聲大喚名悲生，身心顦悸名苦惱生。是名眼見色時即有三世十二因緣大苦聚生，非不生。耳、鼻、舌、身、意，眼界乃至法界亦如是，是為入界生，非不生。

As for the contemplation of the sense-bases and elements: whatever is called an "ocean," however deep and vast, still has a near shore and a far shore — it is, after all, but a small body of water by comparison. But when the eye, having seen form, gives rise to craving, attachment, and delight, and thereby generates karma of body, speech, and mind, this is the true great ocean, in which all the worldly devas, humans, and asuras are drowned. One should understand that the eye is the great ocean, form is its surging waves, craving for this form is its whirling eddies; within it, the arising of unwholesome thoughts is the ocean's fierce fish and dragons, the arising of jealousy and malice is its male rākṣasas, the arising of defiled craving is its female demons, and the resulting karma of body, speech, and mind is like drinking the brine and drowning oneself. This is how, from the eye's ignorance regarding form, ignorance and craving arise; from craving's arising, there is what is called "volitional formation"; from formation's arising, there is what is called "karma"; karma binds consciousness, which then enters the intermediate state — this is the arising of "consciousness"; the receiving of the fetal state, before the five swellings of the embryo are formed, is the arising of "name-and-form"; once the five swellings are formed, this is the arising of the "six sense-bases"; when the six sense-bases are not yet able to distinguish suffering from pleasure, this is called the arising of "contact"; distinguishing suffering from pleasure is called the arising of "feeling"; giving rise to defilement toward sense-objects is called the arising of "craving"; seeking about in the four directions is called the arising of "grasping"; creating karma of body, speech, and mind is called the arising of "becoming"; being due to receive the five aggregates in the future is called the arising of "birth"; the future aggregates' changing is called the arising of "old age," and the future aggregates' disintegration is called the arising of "death"; inward heat of mind is called the arising of "sorrow"; crying out aloud is called the arising of "lamentation"; and the body and mind being distressed and trembling is called the arising of "suffering and vexation." This is what is meant by saying that, at the very moment the eye sees form, the great mass of suffering of the twelve links of dependent origination across the three times arises — it is not "non-arising." The ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind, and correspondingly the eye-element up through the mind-object-element, are likewise so — this is the arising of the sense-bases and elements, not their non-arising.

云何不生？觀眼色時，不種苦種，不生苦芽，不漏臭汁，不集蛆蠅，若種不生則芽不生，則臭汁不生，則蛆蠅不生，故名不生。云何苦種？眼見色時起貪恚覺是為苦種，念於五欲法是生苦芽，六根取六塵是名臭汁流出，於六塵中善惡競起是名蛆蠅，若知眼色無常、苦、空、無我，則貪恚不生、念欲不生、取境不生、善惡行不生，是為不生；耳、鼻、舌、身、意亦如是，是眼界乃至法界亦如是。阿若最初得此三藏不生智故，名阿若憍陳如。

How, then, is there non-arising? When contemplating the eye and form, one does not plant the seed of suffering, does not give rise to the sprout of suffering, does not exude the foul fluid, and does not draw the maggots and flies; if the seed does not arise, then the sprout does not arise, the foul fluid does not arise, and the maggots and flies do not arise — hence this is called "non-arising." What is the "seed of suffering"? When the eye sees form and gives rise to thoughts of craving and aversion, this is the seed of suffering; dwelling on the dharmas of the five desires is the arising of the sprout of suffering; the six faculties grasping at the six sense-objects is called the flowing-out of the foul fluid; the competing arising of good and evil within the six sense-objects is called the maggots and flies. If one knows that the eye and form are impermanent, suffering, empty, and without self, then craving and aversion do not arise, dwelling on desire does not arise, grasping at objects does not arise, and good-and-evil conduct does not arise — this is "non-arising"; and it is likewise so for the ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind — and likewise for the eye-element up through the mind-object-element. Because Ājñāta was the very first to attain this Tripiṭaka-teaching wisdom of non-arising, he is named Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya.

通教無生觀，譬如幻人執幻鏡，以幻六分臨幻鏡、覩幻像，像非鏡生、非面生，非鏡面合生，非離鏡面生，既不從四句生，則非內外中間，不常自有亦無滅處，去不至東西南北方，性本無生，非滅生無生，性本無滅，非滅滅無滅，無生無滅故曰無生；受想行識亦復如是。又觀幻色如幻鏡像、觀受如泡、觀想如炎、觀行如芭蕉、觀識如幻，幻不從幻物生，不從幻師生，非物、師合生，非離物、師生，四句求幻生，生無從來，四方求幻滅，滅無去處，性本無生，非滅生無生，性本無滅，非滅滅無滅，無生無滅故曰無生。觀根塵村落結賊所止，從本已來一一不實，妄想故起，業力機關假為空聚，無明體性本自不有，妄想因緣和合而有，有本自無，因緣成諸，煩惱業苦如旋火輪，觀其本無，皆如上說，此通意(云云)。◎

As for the shared teaching's contemplation of no-arising: it is like an illusory person holding an illusory mirror, bringing illusory bodily parts before the illusory mirror and beholding an illusory image. The image does not arise from the mirror, nor from the face, nor from the mirror and face combined, nor apart from the mirror and face; since it does not arise from any of the four propositions, it is neither internal, nor external, nor in between; it is not permanently self-existent, nor does it have any place of extinction; it does not go to the east, west, south, or north — its nature is fundamentally without arising, and this is not an arising that has been extinguished into non-arising, but rather non-arising from the very start; its nature is fundamentally without cessation, and this is not a cessation that has been extinguished into non-cessation. Being without arising and without cessation, it is therefore called "no-arising"; and feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness are likewise so. Furthermore, contemplate illusory form as like the image in an illusory mirror; contemplate feeling as like a bubble; contemplate perception as like a heat-mirage; contemplate mental formations as like a plantain trunk; contemplate consciousness as like an illusion. The illusion does not arise from the illusory object, nor from the illusionist, nor from the object and illusionist combined, nor apart from the object and illusionist; seeking the arising of the illusion through the four propositions, its arising has no origin; seeking the extinction of the illusion in the four directions, its extinction has no destination; its nature is fundamentally without arising — not an arising extinguished into non-arising; its nature is fundamentally without cessation — not a cessation extinguished into non-cessation. Being without arising and without cessation, it is therefore called "no-arising." Contemplate the faculties and sense-objects — the village where the bandit of the karmic bond lodges — as, from the very beginning, one and all unreal, arising only because of false imagining; the mechanism of karmic power is provisionally taken as an empty gathering-place; the very substance and nature of ignorance is fundamentally non-existent, existing only through the coming together of false imagining and conditions — its existence being fundamentally non-existence, conditions bring about all things, and the suffering of karmic affliction is like a whirling wheel of fire. To contemplate its fundamental non-existence, in every respect as explained above, is the sense of the shared teaching (and so on). ◎

Chinese Source

◎別觀無生智者，鏡譬法界，眼譬觀智，青黃赤白、小大長短譬十法界，青譬地獄因果，黃譬餓鬼因果，赤譬畜生因果，白譬人天因果，小色像譬二乘因果，大色像譬通菩薩因果，短色像譬別菩薩因果，長色像譬佛因果，皆於鏡中分別無謬。若欲自正，令九因果不生、一因果生；若欲正他，令他九因果不生、一因果生。依於法界行菩提行，次第用析體觀智，斷四住生令不生；次用恒沙佛法斷客塵煩惱，令無知不生；後用實相智慧斷無明，令根本不生。若無四住則分段不生，若無無知則方便不生，若無無明則實報不生，生亦不生，不生亦不生，故名不生，是名別教無生智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the wisdom of non-arising according to the distinct teaching: the mirror is a metaphor for the dharma-realm, and the eye a metaphor for the wisdom that contemplates it. Blue, yellow, red, white, and small, large, short, long are metaphors for the ten dharma-realms: blue is a metaphor for the causes and effects of the hells, yellow for those of the hungry ghosts, red for those of the animals, white for those of humans and gods; the small form-image is a metaphor for the causes and effects of the two vehicles, the large form-image for the causes and effects of bodhisattvas of the shared teaching, the short form-image for the causes and effects of bodhisattvas of the distinct teaching, and the long form-image for the causes and effects of the buddhas — all distinguished within the mirror without error. If one wishes to rectify oneself, one causes the nine lesser causes-and-effects not to arise and the one true cause-and-effect to arise; if one wishes to rectify others, one likewise causes their nine causes-and-effects not to arise and their one cause-and-effect to arise. Relying on the dharma-realm, one carries out the conduct of bodhi: in due order, one first uses the analytical contemplation of substance to cut off the arising born of the four abodes of affliction, causing it not to arise; next one uses Buddha-dharmas numberless as the sands of the Ganges to cut off the afflictions of adventitious dust, causing nescience not to arise; and finally one uses the wisdom of ultimate reality to cut off fundamental ignorance, causing the root itself not to arise. Where the four abodes of affliction are absent, fragmentary birth-and-death does not arise; where nescience is absent, the birth-and-death of skillful means does not arise; where fundamental ignorance is absent, the birth-and-death of true recompense does not arise — arising itself does not arise, and non-arising, too, does not arise; hence it is called non-arising. This is what is meant by the wisdom of non-arising according to the distinct teaching.

約圓教觀無生智者，觀鏡團圓，不觀背面、不觀形像，非背非闇、非面非明，不取種種形容、不取種種檠像，但觀團圓，無際畔、無始終、無明闇、無一異差別者，譬於圓觀，不取十法界相貌，無善惡、無邪正、無小大等，一切皆泯，但緣諸法實相，法性佛法，若色若香無非實相，觀煩惱業生即無生，無生不生，故曰無生，陰、入、界苦即是法身，非顯現故名為法身，障即法身，貪、恚、癡即般若，非能明故名為般若，無所可照性自明了，業行繫縛皆名解脫，非斷縛得脫，亦無體可繫，亦無能繫，故稱解脫。解脫即業不生，般若即煩惱不生，法身即苦不生，是三不生即一不生，是一不生即三不生，非三非一故言不生，況變易煩惱業苦而非不生？此即圓無生觀智(云云)。

As for the wisdom of non-arising according to the perfect teaching: one contemplates the roundness of the mirror itself, without regard to its back or its face, without regard to the images it reflects — neither back nor darkness, neither face nor brightness; one does not seize upon its various contours, nor upon the various images set within its frame, but contemplates only its roundness, which has no boundary or edge, no beginning or end, no brightness or darkness, and no distinction of sameness or difference. This is a metaphor for the perfect contemplation, which does not seize upon the characteristics of the ten dharma-realms — there is no good or evil, no false or true, no small or large, and so forth; all such distinctions are effaced. One instead takes as one's object the true reality of all dharmas, the dharma-nature that is itself the Buddha-dharma: whether it be form or fragrance, none of it is other than true reality. Contemplating that the arising of afflictions and karma is itself non-arising — non-arising itself does not arise — this is called non-arising. The suffering of the aggregates, sense-fields, and elements is itself the dharma-body; it is called the dharma-body not because it is manifest, but because obstruction itself is the dharma-body. Greed, hatred, and delusion are themselves prajñā; it is called prajñā not because it illuminates, but because, having nothing that it need illuminate, its nature is of itself luminous. The bondage of karmic action is itself called liberation, not because bondage is severed and liberation thereby attained, nor because there is any substance that binds or any agent that binds — hence it is called liberation. Liberation is itself the non-arising of karma; prajñā is itself the non-arising of affliction; the dharma-body is itself the non-arising of suffering. These three non-arisings are a single non-arising, and this single non-arising is the three non-arisings; being neither three nor one, it is called non-arising. How much more so, then, is this true of the afflictions, karma, and suffering of transformational birth-and-death, which are likewise nothing other than non-arising? This is the contemplative wisdom of non-arising according to the perfect teaching (and so on).

本迹者，是憍陳如本自不生，非始不生；欲引乳為酪故，迹為初教不生；引酪為生蘇故，迹為通不生；引生為熟故，迹為別不生；引熟為醍醐故，迹為圓不生。而其本地住阿字門，謂一切法初不生故。若聞阿字門，則解一切義皆非生非不生；垂迹引化，能為生不生。眾生若能會圓不生，則同阿若，非本非迹、非生非不生，大事因緣於茲畢矣。故下文云：「富樓那，種種變化事，我若具足說，眾生聞是者，心則懷疑惑。」即其義也。《阿含》云：「阿難持傘蓋燈隨佛後，大梵王持傘蓋燈隨陳如後。」斯皆示迹而欲顯本也。

As for trace and origin: Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya is originally, from the outset, non-arising — his is not a non-arising that has a beginning. In order to lead beings from the taste of milk to that of curdled milk, his trace was the non-arising of the initial teaching; in order to lead them from curdled milk to fresh butter, his trace was the non-arising of the shared teaching; in order to lead them from fresh butter to clarified butter, his trace was the non-arising of the distinct teaching; and in order to lead them from clarified butter to ghee, his trace was the non-arising of the perfect teaching. Yet in his true origin he abides in the gate of the letter A, which is to say, in the truth that all dharmas are from the outset non-arising. If one hears the gate of the letter A, one then understands that the meaning of everything is neither arising nor non-arising; and it is by manifesting traces to guide and transform beings that he can appear as either arising or non-arising. If sentient beings are able to comprehend the perfect non-arising, they become identical to Ājñāta himself — neither origin nor trace, neither arising nor non-arising — and the great matter of causes and conditions is thereby brought to completion. Hence the text below says: "Pūrṇa, were I to explain in full the various transformations, sentient beings who heard it would harbor doubt and confusion in their hearts" — this is precisely that meaning. The Āgama says: "Ānanda, holding a parasol and a lamp, followed behind the Buddha; Mahābrahmā, holding a parasol and a lamp, followed behind Kauṇḍinya." All of this is a manifestation of trace meant to reveal origin.

觀心不生者，約三觀不生可知，不煩更說。

As for the non-arising of the contemplation of mind: it may be understood in terms of the threefold contemplation and its non-arising; there is no need to say more.

Reading note: This passage closes out Zhiyi's treatment of Kauṇḍinya begun earlier in the fascicle: the mirror simile works through all Four Teachings before arriving at the "gate of the letter A" (阿字門), a Siddham-script convention in which the first letter of the Sanskrit syllabary stands for the primordial, unarisen nature of all dharmas. Mahākāśyapa and the Three Kāśyapa Brothers 摩訶迦葉與三迦葉兄弟

Chinese Source

「摩訶迦葉」，此翻大龜氏，其先代學道，靈龜負仙圖而應，從德命族故言龜氏。真諦三藏翻光波，古仙人身光炎踊，能映餘光使不現，故言光波。亦云飲光，迦葉身光亦能映物。名畢鉢羅，或畢鉢波羅延，或梯毘犁。畢鉢羅樹也，父母禱樹神求得此子，以樹名之。跋耆子生此聚落，人以為號，其家大富。《增一阿含》云：「羅閱祇大富長者，名迦毘羅，婦名檀那，子名畢鉢羅，子婦名婆陀，其家千倍勝瓶沙王，十六大國無以為隣。」《付法藏》言：「毘婆尸佛滅後，塔像金色缺壞，時有貧女匃得金珠，倩匠為薄。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Mahākāśyapa" is rendered "Great Tortoise Clan." In an earlier generation, an ancestor of his who was cultivating the way was answered by a numinous tortoise bearing a chart of the immortals on its back, and the clan took its name from this virtuous sign — hence "Tortoise Clan." The Tripiṭaka Master Paramārtha renders it "Light-Wave": an ancient sage's bodily radiance blazed so brightly that it could outshine all other light and make it disappear, hence "Light-Wave." He is also called "Drinker of Light," since Kāśyapa's own bodily radiance likewise outshone all things. His personal name was Pippala, or Pippalāyana, or Ti-pi-li — that is, the pippala tree; his parents prayed to the tree spirit and thereby obtained this son, and so named him after the tree. He was born in a village of the Vṛji people, and the villagers took this as his epithet; his family was extremely wealthy. The Ekottarika Āgama says: "In Rājagṛha there was a very wealthy elder named Kapila, whose wife was named Dhanā, whose son was named Pippala, and whose daughter-in-law was named Bhadrā; his household was a thousand times greater than King Bimbisāra's, and among the sixteen great kingdoms none could rival it as a neighbor." The Fu fa zang [zhuan yin yuan jing] says: "After the parinirvāṇa of the Buddha Vipaśyin, the golden luster of a stūpa image had worn away and been damaged. At that time a poor woman begged for a gold bead and hired a craftsman to beat it into gold leaf."

金師歡喜治瑩佛畢，立誓為夫婦。九十一劫人中天上身恒金色、心恒受樂，最後託摩竭提國尼拘律陀婆羅門家生，畏勝王得罪，減一耕犁，但用九百九十九雙牛金犁。」又經云：「其家有㲲，最下品者，直百千兩金，以釘釘入地七尺，㲲不穿破如本不異。六十庫金粟，一庫容三百四十斛。」庫，倉類也。又經云：「以麥飯供養支佛，怛越忉利各千反受樂，身有三十相。」直論金色，剡浮那陀金，在濁水底光徹水上，在闇闇滅，迦葉身光勝於此金，身光照一由旬。闕二相，應是無白毫、肉髻也。故諸天請結集時讚言：「耆年欲恚慢已除，其形譬如紫金柱，上下端嚴妙無比，目明清淨如蓮花。」捨此家業，又納金色婦迭臥無欲，捨而出家，身披無價寶衣，截為僧伽梨，四疊奉佛為座，如是三捨世無倫匹，是為捨大。

The goldsmith was delighted, and when he had finished burnishing and restoring the Buddha-image, the two took a vow to become husband and wife. For ninety-one kalpas, whether born among humans or among the gods, their bodies were forever the color of gold and their minds forever experienced bliss. In his final birth he was conceived in the household of a Brahmin of the Nyagrodha clan in the kingdom of Magadha; fearing to offend the reigning king by outdoing him, the family reduced the customary offering by one plow's worth, using only nine hundred and ninety-nine pairs of oxen with golden plows. The sūtra further says: "In that household there was a length of fine cloth, the very lowest grade of which was worth a hundred thousand taels of gold; when a nail was driven seven feet into the ground to test it, the cloth remained unpierced and undamaged, exactly as before. There were sixty storehouses of gold grain, each storehouse holding three hundred and forty bushels." A ku is a kind of granary. The sūtra also says: "Because in a past life they offered wheat-gruel to a pratyekabuddha, they experienced bliss a thousand times over as far as the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, and their bodies bore thirty of the Buddha's major marks." Speaking strictly of golden color: even Jāmbūnada gold, lying at the bottom of murky water, shines through to the water's surface, yet in darkness its light is extinguished — whereas Kāśyapa's own bodily radiance surpassed even this gold, his light illuminating a full yojana. He lacked two of the marks — presumably the white ūrṇā curl and the uṣṇīṣa crown-protuberance. Hence, when the devas entreated him at the time of the First Council, they praised him, saying: "Elder in years, his anger and pride now gone, his form is like a pillar of purple gold; from head to foot dignified and unmatched in splendor, his eyes are bright and pure as the lotus flower." He gave up his family estate; and though he had taken a wife whose complexion, too, was of gold, they lay side by side without desire between them, and he renounced the household life to go forth. His body was clothed in a priceless jeweled robe, which he cut and made into a saṃghāṭī, folded fourfold and offered to the Buddha as a seat. To renounce in this threefold way is without equal in the world — this is called the greatness of renunciation.

於跋耆聚落，值佛奉寶衣，佛授糞掃大衣，此衣是大聖大衣，又不麁重，故迦葉云：「我受佛衣，師想、塔想，未曾頭枕，況以覆臥？如此大衣，大進我行。」故言受大。

In the village of the Vṛjis, he encountered the Buddha and offered his jeweled robe; the Buddha in turn gave him a great robe of discarded rags. This robe was the great robe of the great sage, and moreover it was neither coarse nor heavy; hence Kāśyapa said: "Having received the Buddha's robe, I regard it with the thought 'teacher,' with the thought 'stūpa' — never have I used it as a pillow for my head, still less have I lain covered by it. Such a great robe greatly advances my practice." Hence this is called the greatness of receiving.

佛弟子中多名迦葉，如十力、三迦葉等，皆是大人；於諸同名中最長，故標大迦葉也。於跋耆聚落，初從佛聞增上戒、定、慧，即得無漏，受乞食法，行十二頭陀，逾老不捨，後時佛語：「汝年高，可捨乞食，歸眾受食，可捨麁重糞掃衣，受壞色居士輕衣。」迦葉白佛：「佛不出世我當為辟支佛，終身行頭陀，我今不敢放所習更學餘者，又為當來世作明。未來世言：『上座迦葉為佛所歎，我亦當學難行苦行。』」佛言：「善哉！」是為行大。

Among the Buddha's disciples there were many named Kāśyapa — such as the ten-powers Kāśyapa, and the three Kāśyapa brothers — all of them great men; but among all those who shared this name he was the most senior, and so he alone is designated "Mahā," Great, Kāśyapa. In the village of the Vṛjis, he first heard from the Buddha the teachings of the higher morality, concentration, and wisdom, and thereupon attained the state without outflows. He undertook the practice of alms-begging and carried out the twelve dhūta ascetic practices, and even as he grew old he would not give them up. Later the Buddha said to him: "You are advanced in years; you may now give up begging for alms and return to the assembly to receive food, and you may give up the coarse and heavy robe of discarded rags for the plain-colored, light robe of a householder." Kāśyapa replied to the Buddha: "Had the Buddha not appeared in the world, I would myself have become a pratyekabuddha, practicing the dhūta austerities to the end of my life. I dare not now abandon what I have long practiced in favor of something else — and moreover, I do this to serve as a beacon for ages to come, so that people of the future may say: 'The elder Kāśyapa, praised by the Buddha — I too should learn such difficult and arduous practice.'" The Buddha said: "Excellent!" This is called the greatness of practice.

《增一阿含》：「佛法中行十二頭陀，難行苦行，大迦葉第一。頭陀既久，鬚髮長、衣服弊，來詣佛所，諸比丘起慢。佛命令就佛半座共坐，迦葉不肯。佛言：『吾有四禪，禪定息心從始至終無有耗損，迦葉亦然；吾有大慈仁覆一切，汝亦如此體性亦慈；吾有大悲濟度眾生，汝亦如是；吾有四神三昧：一無形、二無量意、三清淨積、四不退轉，汝亦如是；吾有六通，汝亦如是；吾有四定：一禪定、二智定、三慧定、四戒定，汝亦如是。』」《增一阿含》云：「一婆羅門白佛：『昨有婆羅門至我家，何者是？』佛指迦葉。又問：『此沙門，非婆羅門。』佛言：『沙門法律，婆羅門法律，我皆知，迦葉亦爾。迦葉功德與我不異，何故不坐？』諸比丘聞佛所讚，心驚毛竪。」

The Ekottarika Āgama says: "Among those in the Buddha's teaching who practice the twelve dhūta austerities, difficult and arduous practices, Mahākāśyapa is foremost. Having long practiced the dhūta austerities, his hair and beard had grown long and his robes were worn, and when he came before the Buddha the other bhikṣus felt disdain toward him. The Buddha bade him come and share his own half of the seat, but Kāśyapa would not consent. The Buddha said: 'I possess the four dhyānas, in which the concentrated stilling of mind, from beginning to end, never diminishes — and Kāśyapa is just the same. I possess great loving-kindness that covers all beings, and your very nature, too, is compassionate in just this way. I possess great compassion that saves and delivers sentient beings, and you are just the same. I possess the four divine samādhis — first, formlessness; second, boundless intent; third, pure accumulation; and fourth, non-regression — and you are just the same. I possess the six superknowledges, and you are just the same. I possess the four concentrations — first, the concentration of dhyāna; second, the concentration of wisdom; third, the concentration of insight; and fourth, the concentration of morality — and you are just the same.'" The Ekottarika Āgama also says: "A certain Brahmin said to the Buddha, 'Yesterday a Brahmin came to my house — who was it?' The Buddha pointed to Kāśyapa. The Brahmin asked again, 'But this is a monk, not a Brahmin.' The Buddha said, 'The discipline of monks and the discipline of Brahmins — I know them both, and Kāśyapa knows them both as well. Kāśyapa's merit is no different from my own; why then should he not sit beside me?' When the assembled bhikṣus heard the Buddha's praise, their hair stood on end in astonishment."

佛引本因緣，昔有聖王號文陀竭，高才絕倫，天帝欽德，遣千馬車造闕迎王，天帝出候與王同坐，相娛樂已，送王還宮。昔迦葉以生死座命吾同坐，吾今成佛以正法座報其往勳，對佛坐時天人咸謂佛師。又迦葉共阿難為比丘尼說法，有一比丘尼不喜云：「販針兒在針師前賣針。」迦葉語阿難言：「此比丘尼以汝為針師，我為販針兒。」迦葉語尼言：「佛說月喻經，日日增長常如新學者，唯大迦葉，汝聞不？於大眾中分半座，汝聞不？於大眾中讚同佛廣大功德，汝聞不？云何此人是販針兒？」如此等是被佛印可大也。

The Buddha then recounted the causal circumstance behind this: long ago there was a sage-king named Mandhātṛ, whose talent and ability had no peer, and whose virtue Śakra, Lord of the Gods, himself admired. Śakra sent a thousand chariots to build a gate-tower and welcome the king, coming out himself to receive him and sit together with him as an equal; and after they had enjoyed each other's company, he sent the king back to his own palace. "Long ago Kāśyapa, using the seat of birth-and-death, bade me sit beside him as an equal; now that I have become a Buddha, I repay his former merit by using the seat of the true Dharma" — and when Kāśyapa sat beside the Buddha, all the gods and men alike called him the Buddha's teacher. On another occasion, Kāśyapa together with Ānanda taught the Dharma to the bhikṣuṇīs. One bhikṣuṇī, displeased, said: "A needle-peddler's child is selling needles in front of the needle-maker!" Kāśyapa said to Ānanda: "This bhikṣuṇī takes you for the needle-maker, and me for the needle-peddler's child." Kāśyapa then said to the nun: "The Buddha taught the Sūtra of the Moon Simile, saying that one who daily grows and increases, ever as if newly learning, is none other than Mahākāśyapa — have you heard this? That in the great assembly he was given half the seat — have you heard this? That in the great assembly the Buddha praised him as equal to the Buddha's own vast and great merit — have you heard this? How, then, can this man be a needle-peddler's child?" Instances such as these are cases in which he was affirmed and sealed by the Buddha as great.

位大者，於大眾中為大，於千二百五十中為大，於五百中為大，於四大弟子中為大，為五山寺主，作閻浮提知事上座，故言位大。

As for the greatness of rank: he was great among the great assembly, great among the twelve hundred and fifty, great among the five hundred, and great among the four great disciples; he served as abbot over five mountain monasteries and as the presiding elder in charge of affairs throughout Jambudvīpa. Hence this is called the greatness of rank.

佛燒身後，灰場生四鉢多羅樹，此表迦葉集三僧祇劫法為三藏四阿含。僧肇序云：「宗極絕於稱謂，賢聖以之冲默，玄旨非言不傳，釋迦以之致教。約身、口防之以禁律，明善惡則導之以契經，演幽微辨之以法相。」此即明戒、定、慧三藏也。《增一》明人天因果，《長》破邪見，《中》明深義，《雜》明禪定，皆大迦葉之功也。若別論集者，阿難誦出修多羅，優波離誦出毘尼，迦葉誦出阿毘曇，故言結集大也。

After the Buddha's body was cremated, four Pāṭala trees grew up on the site of the pyre — this signifies that Kāśyapa compiled the Dharma accumulated over three asaṃkhyeya kalpas into the Tripiṭaka and the four Āgamas. The preface by Sengzhao says: "The ultimate source is beyond all names and designations; sages and worthies abide in it through profound silence. Yet its mysterious purport cannot be transmitted without words, and so Śākyamuni established his teaching through them: with regard to body and speech he guarded them by the disciplinary precepts; to clarify good and evil he guided beings by the discourse-sūtras; and to expound the subtle and profound he distinguished them through the treatises on dharma-characteristics." This is precisely an explanation of the Tripiṭaka of morality, concentration, and wisdom. The Ekottarika clarifies the causes and effects of humans and gods; the Dīrgha refutes false views; the Madhyama clarifies the profound meaning; and the Saṃyukta clarifies meditative concentration — all of these are the achievement of Mahākāśyapa. If one speaks of the compilation according to its several parts, Ānanda recited and brought forth the Sūtra-piṭaka, Upāli recited and brought forth the Vinaya-piṭaka, and Kāśyapa recited and brought forth the Abhidharma-piṭaka. Hence this is called the greatness of the Council compilation.

如來去後法付迦葉，能為一切而作依止，猶如如來。何者？若有頭陀苦行人，我法則存；若無此人，我法則不存。迦葉能荷負佛法令得久住，至未來佛，付法、授衣竟，然後入滅，故言持法大。

After the Tathāgata's departure, the Dharma was entrusted to Kāśyapa, so that he could serve as a refuge for all beings, just as the Tathāgata himself had done. Why is this? As long as there are dhūta ascetics practicing austerities, the Buddha's Dharma will endure; if there are no such persons, it will not endure. Kāśyapa was able to bear the burden of the Buddha-dharma and cause it to abide long in the world; and it is only after he has, at the coming of the future Buddha, completed the transmission of the Dharma and the robe, that he will then enter extinction. Hence this is called the greatness of upholding the Dharma.

而迦葉將隱密上天禮佛髮，為諸天說法云：「為善生天，為惡入淵，五欲無常，如花上露，見陽則晞。」於是別去。諸天泣歎曰：「里巷窮酸，苦厄羸劣，貧窮孤露，彼恒矜愍，今捨滅度，誰復覆護？(云云)」

When Kāśyapa was about to withdraw into concealment, he first ascended to the heavens to pay reverence to the Buddha's hair-relic, and there taught the Dharma to the gods, saying: "Doing good, one is born in the heavens; doing evil, one sinks into the abyss. The five desires are impermanent, like dew upon a flower, which vanishes the moment the sun appears." With this he took his leave. The gods, weeping, lamented: "Those of the back alleys, poor and wretched, suffering hardship and infirmity, destitute and unsheltered — he was ever compassionate toward them. Now that he has left us and entered extinction, who will shelter and protect them again?" (and so on)

約教明抖擻者，抖擻十二種過，謂好衣求時苦，得時多怖畏，失時生懊惱。糞掃衣無水、火、盜、賊、王難五怖；若多畜者，縫治浣負其勞亦多，故但三衣；若僧中食則營佐僧事，故乞食；若受殘食、小食，擾動喪時，故一坐食；多食難消、生睡懈怠，少食饑縣乏力故節量食；多器洗持多妨，故一鉢食；須漿勞動故不飲漿；房舍生著故樹下；樹下又著故塚間；冢間憂悲妨故露地；若臥消功增懶故常坐。二是衣法，六是食法，四是住處法。

As for explaining the dhūta austerities in terms of the teachings — beginning with the Tripiṭaka teaching — the dhūta practices shake off twelve kinds of faults. Fine clothing brings the suffering of seeking it while one lacks it, great anxiety while one possesses it, and vexation when one loses it. The robe of discarded rags is free of the five fears of water, fire, theft, robbery, and royal exaction; and were one to keep many such robes, the labor of sewing, mending, washing, and carrying them would likewise be great — hence one keeps only three robes. Eating within the community of monks involves attending to and assisting in the community's affairs, and so one instead begs for food. Accepting leftover food or a light meal disturbs one and wastes one's time, and so one eats only once, sitting. Eating too much is hard to digest and induces drowsiness and sloth, while eating too little leaves one hungry and weak, and so one measures and limits one's food. Using many vessels for washing and carrying is a great hindrance, and so one eats from a single bowl. Because seeking out beverages involves exertion, one does not drink fruit juices. Because a dwelling gives rise to attachment, one dwells beneath a tree; and because even beneath a tree attachment may arise, one dwells instead among the graves; because dwelling among the graves is hindered by grief and sorrow, one dwells in the open; and because lying down dissolves one's diligence and increases indolence, one always sits rather than lies down. Of these, two concern the rule of robes, six concern the rule of food, and four concern the rule of dwelling.

且約乞食明抖擻者，乞易得生喜、難得生瞋，得好則愛、得惡則憂，憂喜依色而起即色陰，受此憂喜即受陰，取憂喜相即想陰，憂喜即是行陰，分別憂喜即識陰。憂喜即意法二入三界，界入陰即苦諦，我能乞食，計有我、無我，以乞為道以乞為實，如是諦當。讚喜毀瞋我能被呵即疑不了為癡，是為十使，歷三界四諦，即八十八使名集諦。若識乞食中四倒相似相續覆故謂常，適意謂樂，動轉所作覆故謂我，薄皮覆故謂淨，識四覆、無四倒，勤遮二惡、生二善，修四定根力覺道，是為道諦。

Let us take, for instance, the explanation of the dhūta austerities with regard to alms-begging: when alms are easily obtained one feels joy, and when they are hard to obtain one feels anger; when one obtains good food one feels attachment, and when one obtains poor food one feels distress — such distress and joy arise dependent on form, and this is the aggregate of form; the experience of this distress and joy is the aggregate of feeling; grasping at the marks of distress and joy is the aggregate of perception; distress and joy themselves are the aggregate of mental formations; and distinguishing between distress and joy is the aggregate of consciousness. Distress and joy, as mental objects, enter through the mind sense-base into the three realms — and realms, sense-bases, and aggregates are themselves the truth of suffering. Reflecting, "I am able to beg for alms" — calculating a self or no-self, taking begging as the path or taking begging as real — such reckoning gives rise to affliction: praise brings joy and blame brings anger; that one can be reproached yet still feel this is doubt; not to understand is delusion. These are the ten fundamental afflictions, which, distributed through the three realms and the four truths, make up the eighty-eight proclivities constituting the truth of accumulation. If, within the practice of begging, one recognizes the four inversions — mistaking what merely seems continuous and unbroken for permanence, mistaking what is merely agreeable for happiness, mistaking what is merely the covering activity of the body for a self, and mistaking what is merely covered by thin skin for purity — then, recognizing these four coverings, one is free of the four inversions; diligently one wards off the two kinds of evil and cultivates the two kinds of good, and cultivates the four foundations of mindfulness, powers, faculties, factors of enlightenment, and the path — this is the truth of the path.

於乞食中不計我則癡滅，癡滅故愛滅，愛滅故瞋滅，瞋滅故不自舉則慢滅，慢滅故被呵則無疑，無我故我見滅，我見滅故邊見滅，不執是道則戒取滅，不計為實故見取滅，不邪執故邪見滅，此十滅故則八十八滅，八十八滅故子縛滅，子縛滅故果縛滅，果縛滅故二十五有滅，是為滅諦。若於乞食中，不見四真諦，是故久流轉生死大苦海；若能見四諦，則得斷生死。生死既盡已，更不受諸有，是為乞食中抖擻觀慧；衣法、住處法亦復如是。是三藏頭陀也。

Within the practice of begging, if one does not reckon a self, then delusion is extinguished; because delusion is extinguished, craving is extinguished; because craving is extinguished, anger is extinguished; because anger is extinguished, one does not exalt oneself, and so pride is extinguished; because pride is extinguished, one feels no doubt when reproached; because there is no self, the view of self is extinguished; because the view of self is extinguished, extreme views are extinguished; not clinging to wrong practice as the path, attachment to precepts and vows is extinguished; not reckoning it as real, attachment to views is extinguished; not clinging wrongly, wrong views are extinguished. Because these ten are extinguished, the eighty-eight proclivities are extinguished; because the eighty-eight are extinguished, the bondage of the seed is extinguished; because the bondage of the seed is extinguished, the bondage of the fruit is extinguished; because the bondage of the fruit is extinguished, the twenty-five states of existence are extinguished — this is the truth of cessation. If, within the practice of begging, one does not see the four noble truths, one will for long ages circulate in the great ocean of suffering of birth-and-death; but if one is able to see the four truths, one attains the cutting off of birth-and-death. When birth-and-death is exhausted, one no longer receives any further existence — this is the contemplative wisdom of the dhūta austerities within the practice of begging; and the rules of robes and of dwelling are to be understood in just the same way. This is the dhūta practice of the Tripiṭaka teaching.

通教抖擻者，緣真證寂則是住處，空慧為食，空心行諸行為衣，常性空無不性空時，空慧抖擻皆如幻化，妄想諸惡寂滅不起，心心數法不行故，以不可得故，諸相應中空相應最為第一，諸苦行中空行第一，諸抖擻中空慧抖擻最為第一。略說竟。

As for the dhūta austerities according to the shared teaching: taking as one's object true reality and realizing quiescence is itself one's dwelling; the wisdom of emptiness is one's food; performing every act with a mind that understands emptiness is one's robe. Since the nature of all things is constantly empty, there is never a time when it is not empty by nature; the dhūta austerities practiced through the wisdom of emptiness are all understood as illusory transformations, and deluded thoughts and every evil are stilled and no longer arise, since the mind and its mental factors no longer operate — because all this is unobtainable. Among all forms of accordance, accordance with emptiness is foremost; among all austerities, the practice of emptiness is foremost; among all dhūta practices, the dhūta of the wisdom of emptiness is foremost. This concludes the brief explanation.

別教抖擻者，依於法身以為住處，般若智慧以為食，一切諸行莊嚴遮覆，遮覆抖擻黑業之惡，般若抖擻煩惱之惡，法身抖擻生死苦惡，前抖擻分段煩惱業苦，次抖擻變易煩惱業苦，是為中道正觀頭陀，出過二乘所行苦行(云云)。

As for the dhūta austerities according to the distinct teaching: one relies on the dharma-body as one's dwelling, and on the wisdom of prajñā as one's food; every act of adornment and covering becomes one's robe. This covering shakes off the evil of dark karma, prajñā shakes off the evil of affliction, and the dharma-body shakes off the evil of the suffering of birth-and-death. First one shakes off the affliction, karma, and suffering of fragmentary birth-and-death, and next one shakes off the affliction, karma, and suffering of transformational birth-and-death — this is the dhūta austerity of the correct contemplation of the middle way, which surpasses the austerities practiced by the two vehicles (and so on).

圓教抖擻者，住處即衣即食，但是一法分別說三，一抖擻一切抖擻，一切抖擻一抖擻，非一非一切，於一切抖擻無非實相，諸佛所行是如來行，過諸菩薩所行清淨(云云)。

As for the dhūta austerities according to the perfect teaching: dwelling, robe, and food are but a single dharma, distinguished in speech as three. To shake off one thing is to shake off all things; to shake off all things is to shake off one thing; it is neither one nor all — and in every act of shaking off, there is nothing that is not true reality. This is the practice carried out by all buddhas, the practice of the Tathāgata himself, surpassing in purity the practices carried out by all bodhisattvas (and so on).

本迹者，本與如來同坐畢竟空理、同得廣大法身、同得無礙智慧、同得無量功德，內捨法愛、外無垢染，內外抖擻本已清淨，欲引乳味事中抖擻，次引酪味空中抖擻，次引生蘇別中抖擻，次引熟蘇圓中抖擻。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Kāśyapa sits together with the Tathāgata in the principle of ultimate emptiness, has together attained the vast and great dharma-body, has together attained unobstructed wisdom, and has together attained immeasurable merit; inwardly he has abandoned attachment to the Dharma, and outwardly he is free of all defilement — inwardly and outwardly, his dhūta practice was from the outset already pure. Yet in order to lead beings, he manifests, at the taste of milk, the dhūta austerities within phenomenal practice; next, at the taste of curdled milk, the dhūta austerities within emptiness; next, at the taste of fresh butter, the dhūta austerities of the distinct teaching; and next, at the taste of clarified butter, the dhūta austerities of the perfect teaching.

觀心者，即空抖擻取相，即假抖擻塵沙，即中抖擻無明，一心中抖擻五住(云云)。

As for the contemplation of mind: contemplating emptiness, one shakes off the grasping at marks; contemplating the provisional, one shakes off the afflictions numberless as dust and sand; contemplating the middle, one shakes off fundamental ignorance — within a single mind, one shakes off the five abodes of affliction all at once (and so on).

三迦葉，迦葉如前釋。「優樓頻蠡」，亦優樓毘，亦優為，此翻木瓜林。「那提」，此翻河，亦江。「伽耶」，亦竭夷亦象，此翻城家。在王舍城南七由旬，毘婆尸佛時共樹剎柱，緣是為兄弟。兄為瓶沙王師，五百弟子；兩弟各二百五十，行兄法。佛作十種變：謂龍毒不中，龍火不燒，恒水不溺，三方取果，北取粳糧，忉利甘露，知嫌隱去，知念現來，火滅不然，斧舉不下，廣出瑞應，雖覩眾變邪執未改，故言：「瞿曇雖神，不如我道真。」佛即語云：「汝非羅漢，亦不得道。」霍然開悟，師徒皆伏；二弟見相亦隨歸佛，是則一千比丘。

As for the three Kāśyapas — "Kāśyapa" is to be explained as before. "Uruvilvā," also rendered Urubilva or Uruvela, is translated "Wood-Apple Grove." "Nadī" is translated "River," or "Stream." "Gayā," also rendered Gayā or "Elephant," is translated "City-Home." This place lies seven yojanas south of Rājagṛha; at the time of the Buddha Vipaśyin, these three together erected a commemorative pillar there, and by this bond they became brothers. The eldest brother was teacher to King Bimbisāra and had five hundred disciples of his own; the two younger brothers each had two hundred and fifty disciples, following their elder brother's practice. The Buddha performed ten kinds of miraculous transformation for their sake: the venom of the dragon that dwelt in Uruvilvā's fire-hall could not harm him; the dragon's fire could not burn him; the waters of the Ganges could not drown him; he gathered fruit from the three other directions; he gathered rice from the north; he received sweet dew from the Trāyastriṃśa heaven; he knew when he was resented and would withdraw from sight; he knew when he was thought of and would appear; fire that had gone out would not rekindle at Uruvilvā's command, and an axe once raised would not fall. Many such auspicious signs were manifested at length; yet although he witnessed all these transformations, his false attachment remained unchanged, and he still said: "Though Gautama has spiritual power, it is not equal to the truth of my own path." The Buddha then said to him: "You are not an arhat, nor have you attained the path." At this he suddenly awakened, and both master and disciples submitted; his two younger brothers, seeing these signs, likewise took refuge in the Buddha — and so there came to be a thousand bhikṣus in all.

約教者，如《增一阿含》云：「優留毘能將護四眾，供給四事令無所乏，最為第一；那提比丘心意寂然，降伏諸結，精進最第一；伽耶比丘觀了諸法，都無所著，善能教化，為最第一。」是為酪教中意。若轉入生蘇，即應耻小慕大，例則可知；若轉入熟蘇，即應委業領教；若轉入醍醐，如此經中得記作佛也。

As for these three in terms of the teachings: the Ekottarika Āgama says: "Uruvilvā is foremost in being able to care for and protect the fourfold assembly, supplying the four necessities so that nothing is lacking; the bhikṣu Nadī is foremost in tranquility of mind, having subdued all the fetters, and in diligence; the bhikṣu Gayā is foremost in contemplating and understanding all dharmas without any attachment whatsoever, and in his skill at teaching and transforming others." This represents their significance within the teaching of the taste of curdled milk. Should one turn and enter the teaching of fresh butter, they would then be ashamed of the lesser vehicle and aspire to the greater — the pattern may be inferred. Should one turn and enter the teaching of clarified butter, they would then devote themselves fully to their task and receive the teaching. Should one turn and enter the teaching of ghee, then, as in this present sūtra, they receive the prediction of buddhahood.

本迹者，住於三德，林即般若、城即法身、水即解脫，是為祕密本藏；而迹依林城水以度眾生也。

As for trace and origin: these three abide in the three virtues — the grove signifies prajñā, the city signifies the dharma-body, and the water signifies liberation; this is their hidden, original treasury. Yet in trace, they rely upon grove, city, and water in order to deliver sentient beings.

觀心者，正觀心性，中道不動如城防敵，不動而動如水淨諸邊顛倒，雙照枯榮如林蓊欝，三法相資即是連枝兄弟也。

As for the contemplation of mind: the correct contemplation of the nature of mind — the middle way, unmoving, is like the city that wards off enemies; unmoving yet moving, it is like the water that cleanses the inverted views of all extremes; illuminating both the withered and the flourishing at once, it is like the lushness of the grove. These three dharmas, sustaining one another, are precisely the brothers of a single, connected branch.

胎者，父名優波提舍，學通典籍，鐵鍱其腹、頭戴火冠，獨步王舍打論議鼓，國師陀羅自知陳故兼則相不祥，義屈奪封以女妻之。妻夢見人身被甲冑，手執金剛杵碎一切山，後立一山邊。夢覺體重以問其夫，夫云：「汝所懷者，破一切論師，唯不勝一人，當為弟子。」舅名拘絺羅，論常勝姊，既懷智人論則勝弟。弟自念言：「此非姊力，必懷智人寄辯母口，在胎尚爾，何況出生耶？」委家更廣遊學，不暇剪爪，時人呼為長爪梵志(云云)。

As for his time in the womb: his father, Upatiṣya, whose learning encompassed all the classic treatises, once bound an iron plate about his belly and wore a crown of fire upon his head, and walked alone through Rājagṛha beating the drum of debate. The kingdom's preceptor Dāra, recognizing on his own that his former standing was now inauspicious by comparison, conceded the argument and, yielding, gave Upatiṣya his own daughter, together with a fief, in marriage. His wife dreamed that a man clad in armor, wielding a vajra club, shattered every mountain, and then stood beside a single remaining mountain. Waking, she found her body heavy with child, and asked her husband what this meant; he said: "The one you carry will defeat every master of debate, save one alone, whom he cannot surpass, and to whom he will become a disciple." Her brother was named Kauṣṭhila; he had always defeated his sister in debate, but now that she carried within her a man of wisdom, in debate she surpassed her own brother. The brother thought to himself: "This cannot be my sister's own power — she must be carrying within her a man of wisdom, who lends eloquence to his mother's very mouth. If this is so while he is still in the womb, how much more will it be once he is born!" He then left his household to travel widely in search of learning, with no time even to cut his nails, so that people of the time called him "the long-nailed brahmacārin" (and so on).

難陀、跋難陀二龍，護王舍城，雨澤以時，國無饑年。王及臣民，歲設大會置三高座，王、太子、論師，身子以八歲之年身到會所，問人三座，人具答之，即越眾，登論床，群儒皆耻不肯論議，勝此小兒，無足顯譽；脫其不如，屈辱大矣！皆遣侍者，傳語問之。答過問表，盡墮諸幢，無敢當者。王及臣民稱慶無極，國將太平智人出世。及年十六，究盡閻浮典籍，無事不閑，博古覽今，演暢幽奧，十六大國論議無雙，五天竺地最為第一。師事沙然梵志，梵志道術身子皆得，師有二百五十弟子，悉附身子而成就之。沙然臨死欣然而笑，身子問故，答：「世俗無眼為恩愛所親，我見金地國王死，夫人投火聚，願同生一處。」言已命終。後見金地商人，問之果然。身子追悔：「我未盡師術，而不授此法，為我非其人，師祕乎！」自知未達，更求勝法，而無師可事。雖不逮此一法，餘法皆通，於外道眾中最為第一。於道見頞䫌威儀庠序，因問師法，頞䫌答云：「諸法從緣生，是故說因緣，是法緣及盡，我師如是說。」一聞即得須陀洹果，來至佛所，七日遍達佛法淵海，又云：「十五日後得阿羅漢，為羅云和尚，憍梵作師，聲聞眾中右面弟子。調達破僧，引五百比丘去，身子往化五百人歸(云云)。」勞度差捔力，度差為花池，身子為象拔花蹋池；度差為夜叉鬼，身子為毘沙門王；種種皆勝，度差降伏。《中阿含》云：「身子是四眾所生母，目連是所養母(云云)。」

The two nāga kings Nanda and Upananda protected the city of Rājagṛha, sending rain in its proper season, so that the kingdom never knew famine. The king and his subjects held a great assembly each year, setting out three high seats for the king, the crown prince, and the master of debate. When Śāriputra, at the age of eight, arrived in person at the assembly hall, he asked the attendants about the three seats, and when they had answered him in full, he stepped past the assembly and mounted the seat of debate. The assembled scholars were all ashamed and unwilling to debate with him — to defeat so small a child would bring no glory, while to fail to surpass him would be a humiliation indeed! They therefore all sent their attendants to relay questions to him in their stead. His answers surpassed every question put to him, and one by one every opposing banner fell, with none daring to stand against him. The king and his subjects rejoiced without end, saying that the kingdom was about to know great peace, now that a man of wisdom had appeared in the world. By the age of sixteen he had mastered every text of Jambudvīpa, being versed in all things, learned in both the old and the new, and able to expound the deep and subtle with eloquence; in debate he was without peer among the sixteen great kingdoms, foremost throughout the five regions of India. He studied under the brahmacārin Sañjaya, and mastered every one of his teacher's arts and methods. His teacher had two hundred and fifty disciples, all of whom, through Śāriputra, were brought to accomplishment. As Sañjaya was dying he smiled with delight; when Śāriputra asked him why, he answered: "Ordinary people, lacking true insight, cling to what they love. I have seen that when a king of the Land of Gold dies, his consort casts herself into the funeral pyre, wishing to be reborn together with him in the same place." With these words he passed away. Later, Śāriputra met a merchant from the Land of Gold and, on inquiring, found this to be exactly true. Śāriputra, filled with regret, thought: "I had not yet exhausted my teacher's art, and he did not impart this teaching to me — was it because I was not fit to receive it, that my teacher kept it secret from me?" Knowing that he himself had not yet reached the ultimate, he sought out a still higher teaching, yet found no teacher fit to serve. Though he had not attained this one particular art, he was versed in every other, and among the followers of other paths he was foremost. On the road he saw Aśvajit, whose bearing and deportment were composed and dignified, and so questioned him about his teacher's Dharma. Aśvajit answered, saying: "All dharmas arise from conditions; it is for this reason that the Buddha speaks of causes and conditions. These dharmas, too, have conditions for their ending — thus does my teacher teach." At these words alone Śāriputra at once attained the fruit of stream-entry. He then came to the Buddha, and within seven days had fully penetrated the entire ocean of the Buddha-dharma. It is also said that after fifteen days he attained arhatship, and served as preceptor to Rāhula, with Gavāṃpati as the ordaining teacher, and became the disciple on the Buddha's right hand among the assembly of śrāvakas. When Devadatta caused a schism in the saṅgha and led five hundred bhikṣus away, Śāriputra went and converted the five hundred, bringing them back (and so on). In the contest of strength with Rāja-pralamba, when Rāja-pralamba became a lotus pond, Śāriputra became an elephant that plucked lotus blossoms and trampled the pond; when Rāja-pralamba became a yakṣa demon, Śāriputra became King Vaiśravaṇa — in every such transformation Śāriputra prevailed, and Rāja-pralamba was subdued. The Madhyama Āgama says: "Śāriputra is the mother who bears the fourfold assembly, and Maudgalyāyana is the mother who nurtures it (and so on)."

《中阿含》第二云：「生處安居比丘，稱歎滿慈子，少欲知足精進閑居，一心正念智慧無漏，勸發亦稱說此等法。時身子聞，念：『我何時得見此人？此人何時到佛所？』他示云：『白哲隆鼻鸚鵡嘴者，是其形相。』後於安陀林——此云勝林——相見，身子問：『賢者於瞿曇所修梵行耶？』答：『如是。』又問：『為戒淨修梵行耶？』答：『不也。』『為心淨、見淨、度疑淨、知道非道淨、道迹知見淨、道迹智斷淨修梵行耶？』答：『不也。』又問：『向言如是，今言不也。此義云何？』答：『為無餘涅槃故修梵行。』又問：『以戒淨故，設無餘涅槃？』答：『不也。』乃至『道迹智斷淨故，設無餘涅槃？』答：『不也。』又問：『此義云何？』答：『若以戒淨設無餘者，此以有餘稱無餘，乃至道迹智斷淨設無餘者，亦是有餘稱無餘。若離此七者，凡夫人當般涅槃，凡夫離七故。以不離故，從戒淨至心淨，乃至道迹智斷淨。仁者聽我說喻。

The Madhyama Āgama, fascicle two, says: "The bhikṣus keeping the rains retreat in that place praised Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇīputra, saying that he had few desires and was content, diligent and given to solitude, single-minded in right mindfulness, and possessed of the unafflicted wisdom, and they urged one another on by speaking of these same qualities. At that time Śāriputra, hearing this, thought: 'When shall I be able to see this man? When will he come to where the Buddha is?' Others told him: 'One who is fair of complexion, with a high nose and a beak like a parrot's — that is his likeness.' Later they met each other in the Andha Grove — meaning 'Grove of Victory' — and Śāriputra asked him: 'Worthy one, do you practice the holy life under Gautama?' He answered: 'I do.' Śāriputra asked further: 'Do you practice the holy life for the purity of morality?' He answered: 'No.' 'Do you practice the holy life for purity of mind, purity of view, purity in crossing over doubt, purity in knowing the path from the not-path, purity in the knowledge and vision of the path and its traces, or purity in the wisdom and severance of the path and its traces?' He answered: 'No.' Śāriputra asked again: 'A moment ago you said "I do," and now you say "No" — what is the meaning of this?' He answered: 'It is for the sake of nirvāṇa without remainder that I practice the holy life.' Śāriputra asked further: 'Is it, then, for the sake of purity of morality that you posit nirvāṇa without remainder?' He answered: 'No.' — and so on, up to: 'Is it for the sake of purity in the wisdom and severance of the path and its traces that you posit nirvāṇa without remainder?' He answered: 'No.' Śāriputra asked: 'What, then, is the meaning of this?' He answered: 'If one posits nirvāṇa without remainder on the basis of purity of morality, this is to call what still has remainder "without remainder"; and so too, if one posits nirvāṇa without remainder on the basis of purity in the wisdom and severance of the path and its traces, this too is to call what still has remainder "without remainder." If one is free of these seven, then an ordinary person would attain parinirvāṇa, since an ordinary person is already free of these seven. But because one is not yet free of them, one must pass from purity of morality to purity of mind, and on up to purity in the wisdom and severance of the path and its traces. Sir, hear me offer a simile.

如波斯匿王欲從拘薩羅至婆雞帝，中間布七車，捨初乘二，乃至捨六乘七。婆雞帝人問：「為乘初車？」答：「不也。」「乃至乘第七車。」答：「不也。」問：「離此七車？」答：「不也。」此喻問可知。』身子問：『賢名何等，梵行人云何稱汝？』答：『我父名滿，我母名慈，梵行人稱我為滿慈子。』身子稱嗟：『善哉！賢者滿慈子，為如來弟子，智辯聰明決定安隱無畏，逮大辯才得甘露幢，於甘露自作證，值汝者得大饒益，諸梵行人應縈衣頂載。』滿慈子問：『賢者何名，梵行人云何稱？』答：『我父字優波提舍，我母名舍利，故稱我為舍利子。』滿慈子嗟曰：『今與世尊等弟子共論而不知，與第二世尊共論而不知，與法將共論而不知，與轉法輪復轉弟子共論而不知。若我知尊者，不能答一句，況復深論？善哉！善哉！為如來弟子，乃至縈衣頂戴。』(云云)」佛說一句，身子以一句為本，七日七夜作師子吼，更出異句、異味，使無窮盡，況佛多說，而身子智辯寧可盡耶？

It is like King Prasenajit, wishing to travel from Kośala to Sāketa, who set out seven relay chariots along the way: he left behind the first to mount the second, and so on until he left behind the sixth to mount the seventh. If a man of Sāketa were asked, "Did he ride in the first chariot?" the answer would be "No." "Did he ride, then, in the seventh chariot?" the answer would again be "No." "Was he then apart from these seven chariots?" the answer would again be "No." This simile answers your question.' Śāriputra then asked: 'Worthy one, what is your name — how do the practitioners of the holy life address you?' He answered: 'My father's name is Maitrī, and my mother's name is Karuṇā; the practitioners of the holy life call me Maitrāyaṇīputra.' Śāriputra exclaimed in admiration: 'Excellent! Worthy Maitrāyaṇīputra, a disciple of the Tathāgata, wise and eloquent, resolute, at peace and fearless, possessed of great eloquence and the banner of the ambrosial Dharma, having himself borne witness to the ambrosial — those who meet you gain great benefit, and every practitioner of the holy life should wrap you in robes and bear you upon their heads.' Maitrāyaṇīputra then asked: 'Worthy one, what is your name, and how do the practitioners of the holy life address you?' He answered: 'My father is named Upatiṣya, my mother is named Śāri; hence I am called Śāriputra.' Maitrāyaṇīputra exclaimed: 'Just now I was debating with one equal to the World-Honored One's own disciples and did not know it — debating with a second World-Honored One and did not know it — debating with the very general of the Dharma and did not know it — debating with a disciple who himself turns the wheel of the Dharma once turned by the Buddha and did not know it! Had I known who you were, Venerable One, I could not have answered even a single phrase, still less have engaged in such deep debate. Excellent! Excellent! A true disciple of the Tathāgata — I too should wrap you in robes and bear you upon my head' (and so on)." From a single phrase spoken by the Buddha, Śāriputra, taking that one phrase as his basis, roared the lion's roar for seven days and seven nights, bringing forth ever new phrases and new flavors of meaning without end or exhaustion — how much more so, then, given how much the Buddha himself expounded, could Śāriputra's wisdom and eloquence ever be exhausted?

《中阿含》第二十云：「佛在阿耨達池，龍王云：『此眾不見舍利弗，願佛召之。』佛命目連往祇洹呼，身子正縫五納衣。答云：『汝但前去，我在後來。』目連云：『我為佛使人，云何前去？』目連以手摩衣，衣即成。身子念：『目連弄試我，我亦試之。』即以衣繩擲地，『汝能舉此耶？』目連念：『身子弄試我。』即盡力舉不起。身子于時以繩繫閻浮樹，一天下動；繫二三四，四天下亦不立；又繫小千、中千、大千亦不立，又繫他方佛座脚，十方佛世界皆鎮鎮不動。目連自念：『我神力第一，今不能動，將不失神力？』因催促令去，答：『汝前去。』目連還佛所，已見身子在佛前。龍王見地動問佛，佛答：『二人之力。』龍王及五百比丘，於目連生輕心，佛言：『舍利弗於四神力得自在，目連亦自在，而不能拔者，佛力耳。』語目連云：『現汝神力。』目連以鉢絡盛五百比丘，舉著梵宮，一足躡須彌，一足至梵宮，身在彼方而說偈滿大千國，五百心伏(云云)。」

The Madhyama Āgama, fascicle twenty, says: "The Buddha was at Lake Anavatapta, and the nāga king said: 'This assembly does not see Śāriputra here — may the Buddha summon him.' The Buddha ordered Maudgalyāyana to go to Jetavana and call him. Śāriputra was at that moment mending his patched robe of five pieces, and answered: 'You go on ahead; I will come after.' Maudgalyāyana said: 'I am the Buddha's own messenger — how can I go on ahead without you?' Maudgalyāyana then stroked the robe with his hand, and the mending was at once complete. Śāriputra thought: 'Maudgalyāyana is testing me for sport — I will test him in return.' He then threw the robe's cord upon the ground and said: 'Can you lift this?' Maudgalyāyana thought: 'Śāriputra is testing me for sport,' and exerted all his strength, yet could not lift it. Śāriputra then tied the cord to the jambu tree, and the entire single world-system trembled; he tied it to two, three, and four such systems, and the four-fold world-system likewise would not stand firm; he tied it further to a chiliocosm, a dichiliocosm, and a trichiliomegachiliocosm, and still it would not stand firm; and he tied it further to the very legs of the seats of buddhas in other directions, and the world-realms of the ten directions all shook without settling. Maudgalyāyana thought to himself: 'I am foremost in spiritual power — how is it that I cannot move this now? Can I have lost my spiritual power?' And so he urged Śāriputra to go on. Śāriputra answered: 'You go ahead.' Maudgalyāyana returned to where the Buddha was, and found that Śāriputra was already there before the Buddha. The nāga king, seeing the earth tremble, asked the Buddha the cause, and the Buddha answered: 'It was the power of these two.' The nāga king and the five hundred bhikṣus present felt a slight disdain toward Maudgalyāyana on account of this; the Buddha said: 'Śāriputra has mastery over the four bases of spiritual power, and Maudgalyāyana too has such mastery — that he could not pull it free was due only to the Buddha's own power.' He then said to Maudgalyāyana: 'Show your spiritual power.' Maudgalyāyana then gathered the five hundred bhikṣus within his begging bowl and lifted them up to the Brahmā heaven, with one foot planted on Mount Sumeru and the other reaching to the Brahmā palace; and remaining bodily in that place, he spoke verses that filled the great trichiliocosm, and the hearts of the five hundred were subdued (and so on).'"

約教者，若三藏智慧即是無學十智，斷結證真輔佛揚化。《釋論》四十稱為右面大將，即其義也。通教智慧者，如《般若》中自說所以為摩訶薩，謂我見、眾生見、佛見、菩提見、轉法輪見，破如此等見故，名摩訶薩。諸賢聖自說己法，不如即今人妄有所說，當知身子非但破生死見，亦破佛見、菩提、法輪、涅槃等見，此慧異初教也。別教智慧者，當約五味分別，若從元初但聞乳酪不聞餘味，發心修行但行乳酪者，此是初教智慧也；若但聞酪，酪不由乳，善惡之性性本自空，不由修善破惡、滅色取空，但修即空者，是通教智慧；若從元初得聞醍醐，為醍醐故，[穀-禾+牛]牛求乳、烹乳為酪，轉酪為生蘇，轉生為熟蘇，方得醍醐，修如此行者即是別教智慧也。若從元初但聞牛食忍草即出醍醐，若能服者眾病皆除，一切諸藥悉入其中。為此修行，即是圓教智慧也。

As for Śāriputra in terms of the teachings: according to the Tripiṭaka teaching, his wisdom is precisely the ten kinds of knowledge of the one beyond training, cutting off the fetters and realizing the truth in order to assist the Buddha in spreading his transformative teaching — the Treatise, fascicle forty, calls him "the great general on the right," referring to exactly this. As for his wisdom according to the shared teaching: as the Perfection of Wisdom sūtras themselves explain the reason he is called a mahāsattva — namely, that he breaks through the view of self, the view of sentient beings, the view of Buddha, the view of bodhi, and the view of the turning of the Dharma-wheel — it is by breaking through views such as these that he is called a mahāsattva; and all the sages and worthies who declare their own attainments are unlike present-day people who make vain and idle claims. One should understand that Śāriputra breaks through not only the view of birth-and-death, but also the view of Buddha, of bodhi, of the Dharma-wheel, of nirvāṇa, and the like — and this wisdom differs from that of the initial teaching. As for his wisdom according to the distinct teaching: this should be distinguished with reference to the five flavors. If, from the very outset, one hears only of the flavors of milk and curdled milk and no other flavor, and, arousing the resolve, cultivates practice only within the framework of milk and curdled milk, this is the wisdom of the initial teaching. If one hears only of curdled milk, understanding that curdled milk does not depend on milk — that the nature of good and evil is itself from the outset empty, and that one need not, by cultivating good to destroy evil, extinguish form in order to grasp emptiness, but rather cultivates directly the very emptiness of all dharmas — this is the wisdom of the shared teaching. If, from the very outset, one has heard of ghee, and, for the sake of that ghee, milks the cow to obtain milk, boils the milk into curdled milk, turns the curdled milk into fresh butter, and turns the fresh butter into clarified butter, only thereby at last obtaining ghee, then the practice cultivated in this manner is precisely the wisdom of the distinct teaching. And if, from the very outset, one hears only that the cow feeds on forbearance-grass and directly produces ghee, and that whoever partakes of it is cured of every illness, all other medicines being subsumed within it, and cultivates practice on this basis — this is precisely the wisdom of the perfect teaching.

本迹者，本住實相智度為母，從境生智慧，境即是身，智慧即是子；悲愍眾生，迹為五味。身子欲轉煩惱惡血令成善乳，示為外道智慧，作大論師；欲烹乳為酪，示三藏智慧，為第二世尊；欲引酪為生蘇，訥大現小，受淨名之屈；欲引生蘇為熟蘇，安慰饒益同梵行者，於般若領教；欲引熟蘇為醍醐，於法華初悟，斯皆迹中外現，而本地內祕其實久矣。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Śāriputra abides in the mother that is prajñāpāramitā, the wisdom that fathoms true reality; wisdom is born from its object, and the object is itself the "body" while wisdom is itself the "son" of the name Śāriputra. Out of compassion for sentient beings, in trace he manifests through the five flavors. Wishing to transform the evil blood of affliction into the good milk of the teaching, he manifested as a master of debate among the heterodox teachings, becoming a great treatise-master; wishing to lead beings from boiling milk into curdled milk, he manifested the wisdom of the Tripiṭaka teaching, becoming a second World-Honored One; wishing to lead beings from curdled milk into fresh butter, though inwardly vast he outwardly appeared small, and accepted rebuke from Vimalakīrti; wishing to lead beings from fresh butter into clarified butter, he comforted and benefited his fellow practitioners of the holy life, receiving the teaching within the Prajñāpāramitā assembly; and wishing to lead beings from clarified butter into ghee, he attained his first awakening within the Lotus Sūtra itself. All of this is his outward manifestation within trace, while in his true origin he has, deep within, long kept secret his true reality.

觀心者，一心三觀，攝得一切智慧，觀心即空故，攝得酪智慧；觀心即假故，攝得兩蘇智慧及世智慧；觀心即中故，攝得醍醐智慧，是名觀心中一慧一切慧，一切慧一慧，非一慧非一切慧(云云)。◎

As for the contemplation of mind: the threefold contemplation within a single mind subsumes every form of wisdom. In a past life, Maudgalyāyana once assisted a pratyekabuddha, shaving his head, washing and dyeing his robes, and sewing his kāṣāya, and, on the strength of this, made the vow that won him his spiritual powers (and so on).

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Maudgalyāyana abides in the Śūraṅgama samādhi of ultimate truth, able within a single thought-moment to respond throughout the ten directions, manifesting in every manner to carry out the Buddha's work. As the Dīrgha Āgama relates: there was a heterodox teacher who held to the view of annihilationism, maintaining that there is no world beyond this one; there follow in all ten rounds of question and answer. The heterodox teacher said: "There is no other world." Kātyāyana answered: "Are the sun and moon of the present day, then, of the heavens, or of men? Do they belong to this world, or to another? The heterodox teacher asked further: "I have observed that the dead do not return — how, then, can one say they suffer in another world? Hence I know there is no other world." Kātyāyana answered: "It is like a condemned criminal placed under guard — can he simply return home?" The heterodox teacher asked further: "If he is reborn in heaven, why does he not return? Hence I know there is no other world." Kātyāyana answered: "It is like a man who has fallen into a latrine and managed to climb out — would he willingly go back into the latrine again? Moreover, a single day in the heavens is equal to a hundred years here; one born there, after only three or five of their days, would have no time yet to think of returning — and even if he wished to return, you yourself would already have died and been transformed by then; how could you ever know of it?" A person, while alive, possesses spirit, and so is lighter; when he dies and loses his spirit, he becomes heavier." The heterodox teacher asked further: "I have observed the dying turn and toss about, seeking their spirit and never finding it — hence I know there is no other world." Thus, refuting him point by point, Kātyāyana expounded these many meanings at length, until the heterodox teacher at last submitted and praised him, saying: "Venerable One, when you first spoke of the sun and moon, I had already understood — but wishing to hear more of your wisdom and eloquence, I kept raising objection after objection. Excellent! A wondrous exposition." Kātyāyana's skill in the marks of debate was of just this kind in every case.

律中云：「善能教化歸戒，令屠受夜戒，婬者受晝戒，後受報時各於晝夜見前樂相(云云)。」又世典婆羅門，語五百釋：「能與我論不？」五百釋言：「有瞿蜜釋，國中無黠無聞言語醜拙；有周利槃特，於出家中亦為下者。汝能與此二人論勝者，我與汝能名。」世典思惟：「勝此二人無足可尚，脫不如者甚為屈辱。」後時於路遇槃特，問：「何名？」答：「汝當問義，何勞問名？」又問：「汝能與我論義耶？」答：「我能與梵王論，況汝盲無目者乎？」又問：「盲即無目，無目即盲，豈非煩重？」周利作十八變，即云：「此人但能飛變，更不解義。」迦旃延天耳遙聞，即隱槃特，示身如彼，從空而下問：「汝字何等？」答：「字男丈夫。」又問：「男即丈夫，丈夫即男，豈非煩重？」世典答：「止！止！置此雜論，可論深義。」問：「頗不依法，得涅槃耶？」答：「不依五陰法，能得涅槃。」又問：「五陰依何生？」答：「因愛生。」又問：「云何斷愛？」答：「依八正道即能斷愛。」世典聞此遠塵離垢。例皆如此。

The Vinaya says: "He was skilled at converting people to take up the precepts, causing a butcher to accept the precept for the night and a fornicator to accept the precept for the day, so that when each later received their retribution, they would each see, by day and by night, the pleasant sign befitting the time they had kept the precept (and so on)." Also, a Brahmin master of the secular treatises said to five hundred Śākyas: "Can any of you debate with me?" The five hundred Śākyas said: "There is Gavāṃpati, but in our country he is reckoned neither clever nor learned, and his speech is coarse and unrefined; there is also Cūḍapanthaka, who among the monks is likewise reckoned among the lowest. If you can defeat these two in debate, we will grant you renown." The Brahmin thought to himself: "To defeat these two is nothing to be proud of, but should I fail to match even them, it would be a humiliation indeed!" Later, on the road, he met Cūḍapanthaka and asked: "What is your name?" Cūḍapanthaka answered: "You ought to ask about the meaning of the teaching — why trouble to ask my name?" The Brahmin asked further: "Can you debate with me on the meaning?" He answered: "I could debate even with Brahmā himself — how much more so with a blind man such as you, who has no eyes?" The Brahmin asked: "Blind is the same as eyeless, and eyeless the same as blind — is this not needless repetition?" Cūḍapanthaka then performed the eighteen transformations, at which the Brahmin said: "This man can only fly about and transform himself — he does not understand the meaning at all." Kātyāyana, hearing this from afar with his divine ear, concealed Cūḍapanthaka and manifested himself in that same form, descending from the sky and asking: "What is your name?" The Brahmin answered: "My name is 'Male, a Grown Man.'" Kātyāyana asked further: "Male is the same as a grown man, and a grown man the same as male — is this not needless repetition?" The Brahmin answered: "Enough, enough! Set aside such trivial disputation — let us discuss a deeper meaning." He asked: "Can one, perhaps, attain nirvāṇa without relying on any dharma at all?" Kātyāyana answered: "Without relying on the dharmas of the five aggregates, one is able to attain nirvāṇa." He asked further: "On what do the five aggregates depend for their arising?" Kātyāyana answered: "They arise dependent on craving." He asked further: "How, then, does one cut off craving?" Kātyāyana answered: "By relying on the eightfold noble path, one is able to cut off craving." Hearing this, the Brahmin became far removed from dust and free of defilement. Other such instances all follow this same pattern.

約教論義者，依無常、苦、空、無我，破斷常見等，是初教論義。

As for debate in terms of the teachings: relying on impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and no-self to refute the views of annihilationism and eternalism and the like — this is debate according to the initial teaching.

依空、無所有、不可得，破斷常愛見者，通教論義，故天女云：「我無所得故辯如此。」依總持四辯，觀機照假以藥逗病，破斷常見者，是別教論義相。依實相畢竟不有不無，破斷常見者，是圓教論義。

Relying on emptiness, on the absence of anything to be possessed, and on the unobtainable, to refute the afflicted views of annihilationism and eternalism — this is debate according to the shared teaching; hence the heavenly maiden says: "It is because I have nothing to obtain that I can speak in this way." Relying on the dhāraṇīs and the four unhindered forms of eloquence, observing the capacities of one's listeners and illuminating the provisional in order to apply the right medicine to the right ailment, and thereby refuting the views of annihilationism and eternalism — this is the mark of debate according to the distinct teaching. Relying on true reality, which is ultimately neither existent nor nonexistent, to refute the views of annihilationism and eternalism — this is debate according to the perfect teaching.

約本迹者，本住福德、智慧二種莊嚴，能問能答；為愍眾生，迹為五味論義師耳。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Kātyāyana abides in the twofold adornment of merit and wisdom, able both to question and to answer; out of pity for sentient beings, in trace he becomes merely a master of debate within the five flavors.

觀心者，觀智研境，境發於智，智境往復，即觀心論義也。

As for the contemplation of mind: contemplative wisdom investigates its object, and the object gives rise to wisdom in turn — wisdom and object move back and forth between one another: this is the debate of the contemplation of mind.

Reading note: The ten-round debate with the annihilationist is one of the longest sustained dialogues Zhiyi quotes anywhere in this fascicle, and its logic — analogy after analogy dismantling a materialist "no afterlife" position — reads remarkably close to arguments still made today. Aniruddha and Kapphiṇa 阿那律與劫賓那

Chinese Source

「阿㝹樓馱」，亦云阿那律，亦阿泥盧豆，皆梵音奢切耳，此翻無貧，亦如意，亦無獵，名也。昔於饑世，贈辟支佛稗飯，獲九十一劫果報充足，故名無貪。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Aniruddha," also rendered Anuruddha or Aniruddha in other transliterations, are all merely variant renderings of the same Sanskrit sound. It is translated "Without Want," also "As One Wishes," also "Without Hunting" — these are all his personal name. Long ago, in a time of famine, he offered a pratyekabuddha a meal of coarse millet, and for ninety-one kalpas thereafter received abundant reward as recompense; hence he is named "Without Craving."

姓者，劫初大水風吹結構以成世界，光音天命盡，化生為人，身有光、飛而行，歡喜為食，無男女尊卑，眾共生中呼為眾生。自然地味，味如醍醐，色如生蘇，甜如蜜。多食失光，憔悴不能飛，少食者猶光澤，便有勝負，遂相是非，致失地味，食自然地皮。

As for his clan-name: at the beginning of the kalpa, when the great waters were blown by the wind and congealed to form the world, the lifespan of the beings of the Ābhāsvara heaven came to an end, and they were reborn by transformation as human beings, their bodies possessed of their own light, moving by flight, taking joy itself as their food; there was as yet no male or female, no noble or base, and since they were born together in common they were called "the multitude born," that is, sentient beings. There was then a naturally occurring earth-essence, its flavor like ghee, its color like fresh butter, and its sweetness like honey. Those who ate much of it lost their light and grew wan, unable to fly; those who ate little retained their radiance. Thus arose distinctions of superior and inferior, and mutual disputes over right and wrong followed, until the earth-essence was lost altogether, and they ate instead the natural earth-skin that arose next.

轉相輕慢，失皮食地膚。轉生諸惡，失膚食自然粳米。食米則男女根生，遂為夫婦。羞故造舍多儲取米，後米生糠[禾*會]，刈已不生，枯株現，更相盜奪，遂立一平能者，為田主，理諍訟，是為民主。民主有子名珍寶，珍寶有子名好味。始自民主草創之後，金輪相繼，迄至善思，從懿摩至淨飯，四世是鐵輪，合有八萬四千二百一十王。

Growing contemptuous of one another in turn, they lost the earth-skin as well, and ate the earth's natural rind. As further evils arose, they lost the rind too, and ate the naturally occurring unhusked rice. Once they ate rice, male and female organs arose, and they became husbands and wives. Ashamed of this, they built dwellings and stored up large quantities of rice; but later the rice began to grow husks, and once cut it would not grow again, and withered stumps appeared, and people began to steal and plunder from one another. They therefore established one capable and impartial person as lord of the fields, to govern disputes and litigation — this was the first "lord of the people." The lord of the people had a son named Jewel-Treasure, and Jewel-Treasure had a son named Good-Flavor. From this original founding by the lord of the people onward, the golden-wheel kings succeeded one another in unbroken line down to Sujāta; and from Ikṣvāku down to Śuddhodana, four generations were iron-wheel kings, making in all eighty-four thousand two hundred and ten kings.

《十二遊經》云：「久遠劫有王，早失父母，以國付弟，事一婆羅門。婆羅門言：『汝當解王衣體瞿曇姓。』因而從之，時人號為小瞿曇，住甘蔗園。賊盜他物從園過，捕賊尋迹執小瞿曇，木貫射之，血流污地。大瞿曇悲哀，收血土，還園器盛，置左右呪之：『此瞿曇若誠心，天神變血為人。』逕十月，左為男，右為女，從是姓瞿曇。」瞿曇此言純淑，亦名舍夷，舍夷者，貴姓也。

The Sūtra of Twelve Wanderings says: "In a distant kalpa there was a king who lost his parents young, and, entrusting his kingdom to his younger brother, went to serve a certain Brahmin. The Brahmin said to him: 'You should set aside your royal garb and take up the clan-name of Gautama.' He accordingly did so, and people of the time called him Little Gautama; he dwelt in a sugarcane grove. A thief, having stolen another's goods, passed through this grove; those pursuing the thief, tracking his footprints, seized Little Gautama instead, and shot him through with a wooden stake, so that his blood flowed and stained the ground. The elder Gautama, grief-stricken, gathered up the blood-soaked earth, carried it back to the grove, placed it in a vessel to either side, and invoked it, saying: 'If this Gautama's heart was sincere, may the gods transform this blood into human form.' After ten months, from the left arose a boy, and from the right a girl — and from this the clan-name Gautama was continued." Gautama is translated "Pure and Good"; this clan is also called Śākya — Śākya being a name of noble lineage.

仁賢劫初，當寶如來出世時，瞿曇識神始託生。若尋此意，民主已來即姓瞿曇，從懿摩王四子，一面光、二象食、三路指、四莊嚴，被猜徙雪山北直樹林中，國人樂從者如市，欝為彊國，父王歎曰：「我子有能。」四子因此為姓。又其地釋迦樹甚茂，此翻直林，既於林立國，即以林為姓。外國語多含，釋迦亦直、亦能，今淨飯所承，承莊嚴王後，莊嚴即是烏頭，烏頭生烏頭羅，烏頭羅生尼求羅，尼求羅生尸休羅，尸休羅即師子頰，師子頰生三飯，斛飯二子，長名摩訶男，季阿那律，乃是淨飯王之姪兒，斛飯王之次子，世尊之堂弟，阿難之從兄，羅云之叔，非聊爾人也。故周公歎曰：「我是文王之子，武王之弟，成王之叔，於天下非賤人也，而沐三握，餐三吐，禮賢尚爾，況餘人乎？」

At the beginning of the Bhadrakalpa, at the time when the Tathāgata Ratna appeared in the world, the consciousness of Gautama first took birth by transmigration. If one traces this meaning, then from the lord of the people onward the clan-name was already Gautama. From King Ikṣvāku's four sons — first Face-Light, second Elephant-Food, third Road-Finger, fourth Adornment — these four, having fallen under suspicion, were exiled to a forest of straight trees north of the Himalayas; the people of the kingdom who gladly followed them were as numerous as a marketplace crowd, and they flourished into a mighty kingdom. Their father the king exclaimed in wonder: "My sons have such ability!" — and it was from this that the four sons took their clan-name. Moreover, in that land the Śāka tree grew luxuriantly, translated "Straight Grove," and since they founded their kingdom within that grove, they took the grove as their clan-name. In foreign speech the term carries several senses at once, and Śākya likewise means both "straight" and "able." The line that Śuddhodana now continues descends from King Adornment, Adornment being the same as Udaka; Udaka begot Udakara; Udakara begot Nikṣura; Nikṣura begot Siṃhahanu — that is, "Lion-Jaw"; Siṃhahanu begot the three -bhāta brothers, and Droṇodana, one of these three, had two sons: the elder named Mahānāman, and the younger Aniruddha. Aniruddha was thus the nephew of King Śuddhodana, the second son of King Droṇodana, first cousin to the World-Honored One, elder cousin to Ānanda, and uncle to Rāhula — by no means an ordinary man. Hence the Duke of Zhou himself exclaimed: "I am the son of King Wen, the younger brother of King Wu, and the uncle of King Cheng — by no means a man of low station in the realm — and yet, when a worthy man called, I would three times spit out the food in my mouth and three times wring out my wet hair to receive him at once. If I showed such reverence to the worthy, how much more should others do the same!"

《賢愚經》云：「弗沙佛末法時世饑饉，有支佛名利吒，行乞空鉢無獲，有一貧人見而悲悼，白言：『勝士能受稗不？』即以所噉奉之。食已，作十八變。後更採稗，有兔跳抱其背，變為死人，無伴得脫。待闇還家，委地即成金人，拔指隨生，用脚更出，取之無盡。惡人、惡王欲來奪之，但見死尸，而其所覩純是金寶，九十一劫果報充足，故號無貧。其生已後，家業豐溢，日夜增益，父母欲試之，蓋空器皿往送，發看百味具足。而其門下日日常有一萬二千人，六千取債，六千還直。出家已後，隨所至處人見歡喜，欲有所須，如己家無異。」

The Sūtra of the Wise and the Foolish says: "In the age of the Final Dharma of the Buddha Puṣya, when the world suffered famine, there was a pratyekabuddha named Ilāta who went begging and returned with an empty bowl, having received nothing. A certain poor man, seeing this, was moved to sorrow, and said: 'Noble one, would you accept some coarse millet?' and offered him what he himself had to eat. Having eaten it, the pratyekabuddha performed the eighteen transformations. Later, when the poor man went out again to gather millet, a rabbit leapt up and clung to his back, transforming into a corpse; alone, with no companion, he could not free himself of it. Waiting until dark, he returned home, and when he set the body down upon the ground it turned at once into a golden man — whatever finger was plucked from it would immediately grow back, and whatever was taken from its foot would likewise reappear, so that it could be taken from without end. Evil men and an evil king who came to seize it found only a corpse, while what he himself perceived was pure gold and treasure; for ninety-one kalpas his reward was abundant and sufficient, and hence he was called 'Without Want.' After this birth, his household's fortune overflowed, increasing day and night; when his parents wished to test this, they sent him covered, empty vessels, and on opening them found them filled with a hundred flavors. At his gate there were every day some twelve thousand people — six thousand coming to borrow, and six thousand coming to repay their debts. After he went forth as a monk, wherever he went people rejoiced to see him, and whatever he needed was provided as though it were his own household."

阿那律精進，七日七夜眼睫不交，眠是眼食，既七日不眠，眼則喪睛。失肉眼已，佛令求天眼，繫念在緣，四大淨色半頭而發，徹障內外，明闇悉覩，對梵王曰：「吾見釋迦大千世界如覩掌果。」《增一》云：「我佛法中，天眼徹視者，阿那律比丘第一。」那律既失肉眼，佛與諸比丘恒為裁縫，佛在舍衛拘薩羅窟，佛與八百比丘集，為阿那律作三衣，佛自為舒張，諸比丘截者縫者，一日即成。佛廣為說出家受衣，進止共俱，無量人得道。

Aniruddha, striving in diligence, went seven days and seven nights without closing his eyes; since sleep is the food of the eyes, having gone seven days without sleep, he lost his physical sight. Having lost the eye of flesh, the Buddha bade him seek the divine eye; fixing his mindfulness upon its object, the pure form of the four great elements arose from half his head, penetrating every obstruction within and without, so that he perceived all things bright and dark alike, and could say to Brahmā: "I behold Śākyamuni's great trichiliocosm as though gazing at a fruit in the palm of my hand." The Ekottarika says: "Among those in my Dharma who see with perfect clarity by the divine eye, the bhikṣu Aniruddha is foremost." Once Aniruddha had lost his eye of flesh, the Buddha and the other bhikṣus would regularly sew and mend garments for him. When the Buddha was at the Kosala cave near Śrāvastī, he gathered together with eight hundred bhikṣus to make three robes for Aniruddha; the Buddha himself stretched out the cloth, while the other bhikṣus cut and sewed it, and in a single day the robes were completed. The Buddha then expounded at length on going forth and receiving the robe, and on advancing and resting together in harmony, and countless people thereby attained the path.

約教者，依禪定發天眼，凡夫、外道也；依無漏事禪發天眼，三藏義。依體法無漏慧發諸行，依諸行發天眼，通教意。依散善發肉眼，依定發天眼，依真發慧眼，依俗發法眼，依中發佛眼，別教意。依實相發天眼，天眼即佛眼，圓教意。又依散善修肉眼，依定修天眼，三藏意。依空修肉眼天眼，是通意。次第修五眼，是別意。不次第修五眼，是圓意。

As for the divine eye in terms of the teachings: to develop the divine eye by relying on meditative concentration is the way of the ordinary person or the follower of other paths. To develop the divine eye by relying on the unafflicted phenomenal meditations is the meaning of the Tripiṭaka teaching. To develop the various practices by relying on the unafflicted wisdom that comprehends dharmas directly, and to develop the divine eye by relying on these practices, is the sense of the shared teaching. To develop the physical eye by relying on scattered good, the divine eye by relying on concentration, the wisdom-eye by relying on the truth, the dharma-eye by relying on the conventional, and the buddha-eye by relying on the middle, is the sense of the distinct teaching. To develop the divine eye by relying on true reality — where the divine eye is itself the buddha-eye — is the sense of the perfect teaching. Again: to cultivate the physical eye by relying on scattered good, and the divine eye by relying on concentration, is the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching. To cultivate both the physical and divine eyes by relying on emptiness is the sense of the shared teaching. To cultivate the five eyes in sequence, one after another, is the sense of the distinct teaching. To cultivate the five eyes not in sequence, all at once, is the sense of the perfect teaching.

本迹者，本住實相真天眼，不以二相見諸佛國；迹示半頭天眼。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Aniruddha abides in the true divine eye of true reality, seeing all buddha-lands without recourse to any duality of characteristics; in trace he manifests the divine eye that arose from half his head.

觀心者，觀因緣生善心，即肉眼；觀因緣生心空，即天眼；觀因緣生心假，即法眼；即中即佛眼(云云)。

As for the contemplation of mind: to contemplate the good mind born of causes and conditions is the physical eye; to contemplate that the mind born of causes and conditions is empty is the divine eye; to contemplate that the mind born of causes and conditions is provisional is the dharma-eye; and to contemplate that it is the middle is the buddha-eye (and so on).

「劫賓那」者，此翻房宿(音秀)，父母禱房星感子故，用房星以名生身也。是比丘初出家未見佛，始向佛所，夜值雨，寄宿陶師房中，以草為座。晚又一比丘亦寄宿，隨後而來，前比丘即推草與之，在地而坐。中夜相問：「欲何所之？」答：「覓佛。」後比丘即為說法，辭在《阿含》可撿取，豁然得道。後比丘即是佛也。共佛房宿(音夙)，得見法身，從得道處為名，故言劫賓那。毘沙門持蓋隨賓那後，毘沙門是宿主，主既侍奉星宿亦然。此比丘善占星宿，明識圖像，從解得名，名劫賓那。《增一阿含》云：「我佛法中，善知星宿日月者，劫賓那比丘第一。」

"Kapphiṇa" is translated "Lodging in the Room-Constellation" — his parents prayed to the Room constellation and were thereby blessed with this son, and so his name came from the star under which he was born. When this bhikṣu had newly gone forth but had not yet seen the Buddha, he set out for where the Buddha was; caught in rain by night, he took shelter in a potter's hut, using straw for a seat. Later that evening another bhikṣu likewise came seeking shelter there, and the first bhikṣu made room, pushing the straw over to him, and himself sat upon the bare ground. In the middle of the night they asked each other where they were bound; Kapphiṇa answered, "I seek the Buddha." The other bhikṣu then taught him the Dharma — the words of which may be found in the Āgama — and he suddenly attained the path. That other bhikṣu was in fact the Buddha himself. Having thus lodged together with the Buddha, he came to behold the dharma-body, and so, from the place of his attainment of the path, he was named — hence he is called Kapphiṇa, "Lodging." Vaiśravaṇa carries a parasol and follows behind Kapphiṇa, since Vaiśravaṇa is the lord of the constellations, and, being himself in attendance upon the constellations, he attends upon Kapphiṇa in just the same way. This bhikṣu was also skilled at divining by the stars and constellations, clearly versed in their configurations, and so, from this understanding, he received the name Kapphiṇa. The Ekottarika Āgama says: "Among those in my Dharma who are skilled in knowledge of the stars, constellations, sun, and moon, the bhikṣu Kapphiṇa is foremost."

約教者，析破根塵之舍，同佛棲真諦之房，是三藏意。體達根塵，即共如來同宿真諦之房，是通教意。分別十法界根塵房舍，悉得見佛，是別教意。於一根塵房舍，即見一切房舍，見一切佛，即圓教意。

As for Kapphiṇa in terms of the teachings: to analytically break apart the dwelling of the sense-faculties and their objects, and thereby lodge together with the Buddha in the room of ultimate truth, is the sense of the Tripiṭaka teaching. To comprehend the sense-faculties and their objects directly, without analysis, and thereby lodge together with the Tathāgata in the room of ultimate truth, is the sense of the shared teaching. To distinguish the dwellings of the sense-faculties and objects throughout the ten dharma-realms, and thereby attain the vision of every buddha, is the sense of the distinct teaching. Within a single dwelling of sense-faculty and object, to see at once every dwelling and every buddha, is the sense of the perfect teaching.

約本迹者，本與如來同棲實相，迹示諸房宿耳。

As for trace and origin: in his origin, Kapphiṇa dwells together with the Tathāgata in true reality itself; in trace he merely manifests his various lodgings under the constellations.

觀心者，觀五陰舍析空即空，與化佛同宿；觀五陰舍即假，與報佛同宿；觀五陰舍即中，與法佛同宿(云云)。◎

As for the contemplation of mind: to contemplate that the dwelling of the five aggregates is, through analysis, empty, is to lodge together with the transformation-body Buddha; to contemplate that the dwelling of the five aggregates is itself provisional is to lodge together with the recompense-body Buddha; to contemplate that the dwelling of the five aggregates is itself the middle is to lodge together with the dharma-body Buddha (and so on).
