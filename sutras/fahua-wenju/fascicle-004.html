Chinese Source

妙法蓮華經文句卷第四上

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary on the Words of the Sūtra of the Lotus Blossom of the Wondrous Dharma, Fascicle Four, Upper Part

天台智者大師說

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai

The Ten Points of Analysis: An Overview 十義料揀

Chinese Source

○從「爾時世尊告舍利弗，汝已殷懃三請，豈得不說」下，廣明開三顯一，凡七品半，文為三：一、為上根人法說；二、為中根人譬說；三、為下根人宿世因緣說，亦名理事行，例如《大品》亦為三根(云云)。今以十義料揀：一、有通有別，二、有聲聞、無聲聞，三、惑有厚薄，四、根轉不轉，五、根有悟不悟，六、領解無領解，七、得記不得記，八、悟有淺深，九、益有權實，十、待時不待時。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "at that time the World-Honored One told Śāriputra, since you have now three times earnestly requested it, how could I fail to speak" onward is the extensive clarification of opening the three to reveal the one, spanning in all seven and a half chapters. The text falls into three: first, the dharma-teaching for persons of superior faculties; second, the parable-teaching for persons of middling faculties; third, the teaching of past-life causal conditions for persons of inferior faculties — also called the teaching through principle, phenomena, and practice, following the precedent of the Da Pin [the Larger Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra], which likewise teaches in three for the three kinds of faculties, and so on. Here, ten points of analysis are used to sort this out: first, the general and the specific; second, whether there are real śrāvakas or none at all; third, whether the delusion is thick or thin; fourth, whether the faculties are transformed or not transformed; fifth, whether the faculties attain realization or fail to; sixth, whether there is comprehension in response or none; seventh, whether a prediction is received or not; eighth, whether the depth of realization is shallow or deep; ninth, whether the benefit gained is provisional or real; tenth, whether it awaits its proper time or does not.

First: The General and the Specific 一、明通別

Chinese Source

一、明通別者，初周別名法說，通則具三；「如優曇花時一現耳」，即譬說；「若我遇眾生，盡教以佛道」，即因緣說也。中周別名譬說，通則亦三，「我先不言，皆為化菩薩故」。又合譬於一佛乘分別說三，即是法說；「於二萬億佛所常教化汝」，即因緣說。若謂此文屬法說者，可取長者聞已驚入火宅方宜救濟，即因緣說。下周別名宿世因緣，通亦具三，「涅槃時到眾又清淨令入佛慧」，是法說；「有一導師」是譬說，而作三周者，從多從正，從略從傍，欲令名字不濫，各據一意耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying the general and the specific: the first round, taken specifically, is named the dharma-teaching, but taken generally it comprises all three — "like the udumbara flower, which blooms but once in its season" is the parable-teaching; "if I should encounter sentient beings, I teach them all, without exception, the buddha path" is the causal-condition teaching. The middle round, taken specifically, is named the parable-teaching, but taken generally it, too, comprises all three: "I did not speak of this before, since it was all for the sake of converting bodhisattvas" — and, further, combining the parable with the distinguishing, within the one buddha vehicle, of the teaching of three — this is the dharma-teaching; "at the abodes of twenty thousand koṭis of buddhas he constantly taught and transformed you" is the causal-condition teaching. If one holds that this passage belongs to the dharma-teaching, one may instead take the passage where the elder, on hearing this, is startled and enters the burning house so as to rescue his son, as the causal-condition teaching. The last round, taken specifically, is named the teaching of past-life causal conditions, but taken generally it, too, comprises all three: "when the time for nirvāṇa arrived, and the assembly was also purified, he caused them to enter the Buddha's wisdom" is the dharma-teaching; "there was a single guide" is the parable-teaching. As for why it is made into three rounds at all: it follows the majority and follows what is primary, and sets aside the minority and what is secondary, wishing that the names should not become confused, each round being fixed according to a single governing sense.

問：
三周為三根人，一周通有三說者，一說應具三根？

Question: If the three rounds are for the three kinds of faculties, and yet each single round, taken generally, comprises all three teachings, then should not a single teaching accommodate all three kinds of faculties?

答：
法說非止逗上中之上，又有中下，從正略傍，故言逗上根人耳，餘二周亦如是。

Answer: The dharma-teaching does not accommodate only the very highest of the superior faculties — it also reaches the middling and the inferior; but since what is primary is taken and what is secondary is set aside, it is said to accommodate persons of superior faculties. The same holds for the remaining two rounds.

Second: Are There Real Śrāvakas, or None at All? 二、明有聲聞、無聲聞

Chinese Source

二、明有聲聞、無聲聞者，光宅定有實行聲聞，若言無實，權何所應。開善解無實行聲聞，引《勝鬘》「三乘初業不愚於法」，外凡已知一乘，寧有二乘猶執小果？經明有者權也，此二家偏執，乖經失義；若定有者，經那言無聲聞弟子但化諸菩薩？若定無者，誰入化城？亦無三可會，權何所引？若言實有為權所引者，亦應實有三藏佛，復為權三藏佛所引；若實無此佛，但有權佛者，何意不許但有權聲聞、無實聲聞耶？此義不例，實有斷界內惑者，呼此為實，而權者應之。何處有斷界內惑佛，而有權佛應此佛？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying whether there are real śrāvakas or none: Guangzhai holds definitely that there are śrāvakas who really practice [the small vehicle], for if he said there were none real, then what would the provisional be responding to? Kaishan, another contemporary lecture-master, explains that there are no śrāvakas who really practice, citing the Śrīmālā [Sūtra]'s "in their initial undertakings, the three vehicles are not ignorant of the Dharma" — since even ordinary worldlings already know the one vehicle, how could there still be adherents of the two vehicles clinging to the fruits of the small [vehicle]? The sūtra's clarification that there are [real śrāvakas] is itself the provisional. Both these schools cling one-sidedly, straying from the sūtra and losing its meaning. If one holds definitely that there are [real śrāvakas], then how could the sūtra say that there are no śrāvaka disciples, and that he only converted bodhisattvas? If one holds definitely that there are none, then who is it that enters the phantom city? Nor would there then be any three vehicles to be reconciled into one, and what would the provisional be drawing in? If one says that there really are such beings drawn in by the provisional, then there ought equally to be a real Tripiṭaka buddha, who is in turn drawn in by a provisional Tripiṭaka buddha; but if there really is no such buddha, and there is only a provisional buddha, then why would one refuse to allow that there is only a provisional śrāvaka, and no real śrāvaka? This comparison does not hold: it is only where there really is one who has cut off the delusions within the threefold world that this is called "real," and it is the provisional that responds to him. Where is there a buddha who has cut off the delusions within the threefold world, such that there is a provisional buddha responding to this buddha?

今明有無不可偏執，若從長者實智往觀則無客作人，若就窮子根性則便自謂作人。《法華論》有四種聲聞：一、決定；二、上慢；三、退大；四、應化。前二未熟不與授記，後二與記。若依今經應有五：一、久習小，今世道熟，聞小教證果，如《論》是決定聲聞；二、本是菩薩積劫修道，中間疲厭生死，退大取小，《大品》稱為別異善根，佛且成其小道為說小教，齊教斷結取果，是退大未久習小來近，理應易悟，如《論》是退菩提聲聞；三、以此二故，諸佛、菩薩內祕外現，成就引接令入大道，如《論》是應化聲聞；四、若見權實兩種能出生死，欣樂涅槃，修戒定慧，微有觀慧未入似位薄有所得，謂是證果，此名未得謂得、未證謂證，如《論》即是增上慢聲聞；

Here it is made clear that "existing" and "not existing" cannot be clung to one-sidedly: viewed from the elder's real wisdom, there is no such thing as a servant hired to do menial work; but from the standpoint of the impoverished son's own capacity, he himself calls himself just such a servant. The Fahua Lun [Vasubandhu's treatise on the Lotus Sūtra] speaks of four kinds of śrāvakas: first, those of decisive resolve; second, those of overweening pride; third, those who have retreated from the great [vehicle]; fourth, those manifested through response. The first two, not yet ripened, are not given a prediction; the latter two are given one. According to the present sūtra there ought to be five: first, one who has long practiced the small [vehicle] and whose path has, in this present life, come to ripeness, and who, on hearing the small teaching, attains its fruit — this corresponds, in the treatise, to the śrāvaka of decisive resolve; second, one who was originally a bodhisattva cultivating the path over accumulated eons, but who, midway, grew weary of birth-and-death, retreated from the great and took up the small — the Da Pin calls this a "differing wholesome root"; the Buddha, for the time being, brings his small path to completion by teaching him the small teaching, and, in accordance with that teaching, cuts off the fetters and takes its fruit — since his retreat from the great is not long past and his practice of the small is recent, he ought reasonably to awaken with ease — this corresponds, in the treatise, to the śrāvaka who has retreated from bodhi; third, on account of these two, the buddhas and bodhisattvas, secretly [being what they are] within while outwardly manifesting [as śrāvakas], accomplish the drawing-in of beings so as to bring them into the great path — this corresponds, in the treatise, to the śrāvaka manifested through response; fourth, one who, seeing that the two kinds, the provisional and the real, are both able to bring one out of birth-and-death, delights in nirvāṇa and cultivates precepts, concentration, and wisdom, and who, having some slight contemplative wisdom but not yet having entered the semblance stage, attains something slight and calls this the realization of the fruit — this is called "not yet attained yet calling it attained, not yet realized yet calling it realized" — this corresponds, in the treatise, precisely to the śrāvaka of overweening pride;

五者、大乘聲聞，以佛道聲令一切聞，若從決定退菩提兩種，即有聲聞，若從大乘理無灰斷永住化城，終歸寶所。實者既爾，則無有權，故無聲聞。若增上慢者，既未入位則非實，又非應化，則非權。若得此意，有無冷然，何須苦諍？復次秪就大乘聲聞復論有無，若權作應化，外現小迹內隱大德，則謂無大乘聲聞；若從自行發迹顯本，則言有大乘聲聞。今開三顯一正意，為決定退大聲聞令成大乘聲聞，自行既立即能化應聲聞，若得此意則達有無也。

fifth, the śrāvaka of the great vehicle, who causes everyone to hear the sound of the buddha path — [this fifth kind] does have the character of a śrāvaka if reckoned from the two kinds, the decisively resolved and the one who has retreated from bodhi, but if reckoned from the principle of the great vehicle, there is in truth no extinction into ashes at all, but only an eternal dwelling in the phantom city, ultimately to return to the place of treasure. Given that this is how the real stands, then there is no provisional at all, and hence no [provisional] śrāvaka. As for the one of overweening pride, since he has not yet entered the [true] stage, he is not real; and since he is not one manifested through response, he is not provisional either. If one grasps this point, then "existing" and "not existing" become perfectly clear — why exert oneself in bitter dispute over it? Moreover, taking up specifically the question of the great-vehicle śrāvaka, one may again discuss whether it exists or not: if, as the provisional, he manifests as one responsively transformed, outwardly showing the small trace while inwardly concealing his great virtue, then one says there is no great-vehicle śrāvaka; but if, from his own practice, the trace is given rise to and the origin is revealed, then one says there is a great-vehicle śrāvaka. The true intent of the present opening of the three to reveal the one is precisely to cause the śrāvaka of decisive resolve and the one who has retreated from the great to become a great-vehicle śrāvaka: once his own practice is established, he is then able himself to convert and respond to [other] śrāvakas. If one grasps this point, then one penetrates to the meaning of "existing" and "not existing."

Third: Thick and Thin Delusion 三、惑有厚薄

Chinese Source

第三、惑有厚薄者，瑤師云：「三根得果已後遊觀無生。無生之理是一，及其出觀，緣三教則異，將必異之三教，惑於無生之一理，謂教既三，理豈容一？又將一理惑於三教，理既是一，教寧得三？踟蹰理教之間迴遑得失，以理惑教，此有得義；以教惑理，此有失義。上根以理惑教情多，初聞法說順情即悟；下根以教惑理情多，聞法說無三，逆其計謂故三聞乃解；中根處二楹之際，法說不悟，譬說便了。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying whether the delusion is thick or thin: Master Yao says, "After the three kinds of faculties have attained the fruit, they wander in the contemplation of non-arising. The principle of non-arising is one; yet when they emerge from this contemplation and take the three teachings as their object, these differ. Taking up the three teachings, which must differ, they become confused about the single principle of non-arising — thinking, since the teachings are indeed three, how could the principle possibly be one? And, conversely, taking up the one principle, they become confused about the three teachings — thinking, since the principle is indeed one, how could the teachings possibly be three? Hesitating between principle and teaching, wavering between gain and loss — where the principle confuses the teaching, this has the sense of a gain; where the teaching confuses the principle, this has the sense of a loss. Those of superior faculties, in whom the principle confuses the teaching more strongly, on first hearing the dharma-teaching, find it accords with their disposition and awaken at once; those of inferior faculties, in whom the teaching confuses the principle more strongly, on hearing the dharma-teaching that there are not three [vehicles], find this runs contrary to their settled view, and so only after hearing all three [teachings] do they come to understand; those of middling faculties stand between these two pillars — they do not awaken at the dharma-teaching, but come to understanding readily at the parable-teaching."

今謂此釋三根未必應爾，三人踟蹰何等理教？若迴遑小乘理教則疑惑未盡，尚非初果斷結之人；若迴遑大乘理教，大乘條然永異，何曾與小乘相濫而言踟蹰耶？若以小惑大、以大惑小，爾前未斥方便，那忽遊觀出入預有踟蹰？既預踟蹰即已疑生執動，非始今日，若先動執生疑，聞開三顯一即應領解，那忽猶有驚疑？

Now, it must be said that this explanation of the three kinds of faculties need not necessarily be so. Between what principle and what teaching do these three kinds of persons hesitate? If they waver between the principle and teaching of the small vehicle, then their doubt and confusion are not yet exhausted, and they are not even persons who have cut off the fetters at the first fruit; if they waver between the principle and teaching of the great vehicle, the great vehicle is utterly and permanently distinct [from the small] — how could it ever be so mixed up with the small vehicle that one could speak of "hesitating" between them? If it is a matter of the small confusing the great, or the great confusing the small, then, prior to this point, the expedient means had not yet been refuted — how, then, could there suddenly be, in their coming and going through contemplation, any prior hesitation at all? And if there were already such prior hesitation, then doubt would already have arisen and attachment already been stirred, and this would not be something beginning only today — but if the attachment were already stirred and the doubt already arisen beforehand, then on hearing the opening of the three to reveal the one they ought to comprehend it at once — how, then, could there still, all of a sudden, be alarm and doubt?

進退無據，故不用此解。今明根有利鈍者，皆論大乘根性，惑有厚薄者，約別惑為言耳，即為四句：一、惑輕根利；二、惑重根利；三、惑輕根鈍；四、惑重根鈍。若別惑輕、大根利，初聞即悟；若惑重根利，再聞方曉；若惑輕根鈍，三聞乃決；第四句雖復三聞不能得悟，止為結緣眾耳。或可初兩句根利同為上根，或可中間兩句為中下根(云云)。

Advance or retreat, this account has no foundation, and so it is not adopted. It is now made clear that the sharpness or dullness of faculties is, in every case, discussed in terms of great-vehicle capacity, while the thickness or thinness of delusion is spoken of in terms of the delusion of the distinct teaching — this then makes four propositions: first, light delusion and sharp faculties; second, heavy delusion and sharp faculties; third, light delusion and dull faculties; fourth, heavy delusion and dull faculties. If the delusion of the distinct teaching is light and the great-vehicle faculty is sharp, one awakens at the very first hearing; if the delusion is heavy but the faculty sharp, one comes to understand only at the second hearing; if the delusion is light but the faculties dull, one is only resolved at the third hearing; as for the fourth proposition, even after three hearings one is unable to attain awakening, and this serves only to form a karmic connection within the assembly. It may also be that the first two propositions, both being of sharp faculties, together constitute the superior faculties, and the middle two propositions constitute the middling and inferior faculties, and so on.

復次約初品無明三重，覆初住中道，若初法說，上根之人三重無明一時俱盡，開佛知見入菩薩位，得菩提記；中根斷二重無明；下根斷一重。次譬說時，中根斷第三重盡，開佛知見入菩薩位得授記莂；下根進斷二重。次聞因緣說，下根斷三重盡，開佛知見入菩薩位也。例如小乘十六心未滿不得名初果，十六心滿名須陀洹也。

Moreover, in terms of the three layers of ignorance in the initial stage, which cover over the middle way of the first abode: at the initial dharma-teaching, persons of superior faculties exhaust all three layers of ignorance at once, open the Buddha's knowledge and vision, enter the stage of bodhisattva, and receive the prediction of bodhi; those of middling faculties cut off two of the three layers; those of inferior faculties cut off one layer. Then, at the time of the parable-teaching, those of middling faculties exhaust the third layer, open the Buddha's knowledge and vision, enter the stage of bodhisattva, and receive the prediction; those of inferior faculties advance and cut off a second layer. Then, on hearing the causal-condition teaching, those of inferior faculties exhaust all three layers, open the Buddha's knowledge and vision, and enter the stage of bodhisattva. This is comparable to how, in the small vehicle, so long as the sixteen mind-moments are not yet complete one cannot be called one who has attained the first fruit, and it is only when the sixteen mind-moments are complete that one is called a srota-āpanna.

Fourth: Transformed and Untransformed Faculties 四、明轉根不轉根

Chinese Source

四、明轉根不轉根者，舊云：「上根初聞法說即悟，而中根轉同上根，下根進同中根；若譬說時，中根前已成上，即能得悟，下根成上；次因緣說時，下根已同於上故即得悟。」若爾轉下成上，因緣說時皆悉是上，為利則均，那得猶稱鈍者待因緣說耶？若轉成上即同上悟，若其未悟猶受鈍名，則無轉根之義。例如身子一聞，目連再聽，同得初果，若二皆利則無復優劣，若猶稱利鈍，轉根義不成。夫眾生心神不定，遇惡緣轉利為鈍，遇善緣轉鈍為利，先世值佛聞法，自有轉下中為上，俱於法說得悟；自有轉下為中，聞譬說得解；下者不轉，三周乃了。如此轉根不同舊釋。譬三刀斫木，利一、中二、鈍者三下，利鈍之名不失，木斷之處是同。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying whether the faculties are transformed or not transformed: the old explanation says, "Those of superior faculties awaken at once on first hearing the dharma-teaching, while those of middling faculties are transformed to become the same as the superior, and those of inferior faculties advance to become the same as the middling; then, at the time of the parable-teaching, those of middling faculties, having already become superior, are then able to awaken, and those of inferior faculties become superior; then, at the time of the causal-condition teaching, those of inferior faculties, having already become the same as the superior, thereupon attain awakening." But if this is so, and the inferior are transformed into the superior, then by the time of the causal-condition teaching all would be superior, and, being equally sharp, how could any still be called dull and made to wait for the causal-condition teaching? If, once transformed into the superior, one awakens together with the superior — but if one has not yet awakened and still bears the name "dull" — then there is no meaning at all to "transformation of faculties." Take, for instance, Śāriputra, who awakened at a single hearing, and Maudgalyāyana, who required a second hearing, and yet both alike attained the first fruit: if both were equally sharp there would be no difference of superior and inferior between them, but if they are still called, respectively, sharp and dull, then the meaning of "transformation of faculties" does not hold up. Now, the minds of sentient beings are unsettled: meeting adverse conditions, the sharp is turned into the dull; meeting favorable conditions, the dull is turned into the sharp. Having encountered buddhas and heard the Dharma in former lives, some have had their inferior and middling faculties transformed into superior ones, and awaken, all alike, at the dharma-teaching; some have had their inferior transformed into middling, and come to understanding on hearing the parable-teaching; those of the inferior who are not transformed only come to full understanding after all three rounds. Transformation of faculties, understood in this way, differs from the old explanation. It is comparable to three blows of an axe cutting wood: the sharp axe fells it in one stroke, the middling in two, and the dull in three — the names "sharp" and "dull" are not thereby lost, even though the place where the wood is severed is the same.

問：
三根入初住位，猶有利鈍不？

Question: Once the three kinds of faculties enter the stage of the first abode, is there still sharpness or dullness among them?

答：
真修體顯則無差降。

Answer: Once the essence of true cultivation is manifested, there is no longer any difference of higher or lower.

問：
若爾，初住已上更起緣修，有優劣不？

Question: If so, then above the first abode, when conditioned cultivation is again given rise to, is there superiority or inferiority?

答：
此同位人無復勝負，真修體融寧得有異耶？

Answer: Among persons of this same stage there is no longer any superiority or inferiority — once the essence of true cultivation is fused, how could there be any difference?

Fifth: Realization and Non-Realization 五、明有悟不悟

Chinese Source

五、明有悟不悟者，經中多明菩薩為上根、緣覺中根、聲聞下根，若言菩薩上根，應併在法說中得悟，緣覺併在譬說中得解，聲聞併在於因緣中得悟耶？然經中一往判出三根，至於悟解義未必然。今經但見聲聞得解、不見支佛者，支佛是中根，既值佛出世入聲聞數，隨根得悟故，不別標緣覺耳。故身子請偈云：「其求緣覺者，比丘、比丘尼。」依此文即知緣覺入四眾中攝也。又〈法師品〉云：「比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷，求聲聞者、求支佛者」，豈無緣覺得解耶？舊云：「菩薩是上根，不必皆利，從多為上，而執心易轉，原其域懷求佛，但執過三百已即求近果，此疑易悟。三根菩薩同在法說得解，上者或在略說，中者或在廣說之初，下者與身子齊。」今明菩薩語通，但使發大心悉是菩薩，何必併是利根？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, clarifying whether there is realization or no realization: the sūtra frequently makes clear that bodhisattvas are of superior faculties, pratyekabuddhas of middling faculties, and śrāvakas of inferior faculties — but does this mean that bodhisattvas, as a whole, ought to attain realization at the dharma-teaching, pratyekabuddhas, as a whole, come to understanding at the parable-teaching, and śrāvakas, as a whole, attain realization at the causal-condition teaching? In fact, the sūtra, in a single general pass, distinguishes the three kinds of faculties, but when it comes to the actual meaning of realization and understanding, this need not hold so strictly. In the present sūtra one sees only śrāvakas attaining understanding, and does not see pratyekabuddhas mentioned, because the pratyekabuddha, being of middling faculties, having encountered the Buddha's appearance in the world, is included among the ranks of śrāvakas, and, attaining realization according to his faculties, is not separately singled out as "pratyekabuddha." Hence Śāriputra's verse of request says that "those who seek to be pratyekabuddhas" are among "the bhikṣus and bhikṣuṇīs" — from this passage one knows that pratyekabuddhas are included and gathered within the fourfold assembly. Also, the Dharma-Teacher Chapter says, "bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, and upāsikās, those who seek to be śrāvakas, those who seek to be pratyekabuddhas" — how could there be no pratyekabuddhas attaining understanding? The old explanation says, "Bodhisattvas are of superior faculties, though not necessarily all sharp — they are classed as superior because they are the majority — and their attached mind is easily transformed; tracing their original aspiration, they seek buddhahood, but, clinging [to the small], once they have passed three hundred yojanas, they then seek the nearby fruit — this doubt is easily awakened from. The three kinds of faculties among bodhisattvas alike come to understanding at the dharma-teaching: the superior perhaps at the abbreviated teaching, the middling perhaps at the beginning of the extended teaching, and the inferior on a par with Śāriputra." It is now made clear that the term "bodhisattva" applies broadly — anyone at all who has given rise to the great resolve is a bodhisattva; why must they all, as a whole, be of sharp faculties?

及身子尚少，豈得初周之前已併得悟？若爾，流通壽量，何意有諸菩薩節節得悟無生忍者、發菩提心者。舊云：「壽量中悟，皆是法身增道損生。」今言不爾，有六百八十萬億那由他恒河沙人得無生法忍，此人始得此忍，當知〈壽量〉之前未是法身，故知菩薩得悟，不可局在初周之初也。

And even Śāriputra himself was still a lesser figure among them — how, then, could they all, as a whole, have already attained realization before the first round even began? If it were so, then in the transmission section of the Life Span Chapter, what would be the point of the various bodhisattvas attaining, stage by stage, the acceptance of non-arising, or giving rise to the resolve for bodhi? The old explanation says, "The realization within the Life Span Chapter is, in every case, the dharma-body advancing the path and diminishing further birth." It is now said that this is not so: there are six hundred and eighty myriad koṭis of nayutas of Ganges-sands' worth of people who attain the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas — these people are only now, for the first time, attaining this acceptance, and one should know that, prior to the Life Span Chapter, they were not yet the dharma-body. Hence it is known that a bodhisattva's attainment of realization cannot be confined to the beginning of the first round alone.

問：
菩薩得悟通於始終，二乘得悟亦應至後。

Question: If a bodhisattva's attainment of realization extends throughout, from beginning to end, then the attainment of realization by the two vehicles ought likewise to extend to the end.

答：
三周定父子天性已竟，則皆名菩薩，設在後悟，同名菩薩悟也。

Answer: Once the three rounds have finished fixing the natural bond of father and son, all are then called bodhisattvas — even supposing realization comes later, it is alike called a bodhisattva's realization.

Sixth: Comprehension in Response 六、明有領解無領解

Chinese Source

六、明有領解無領解者，若三乘同悟，何意但見聲聞領解，其二則無？今明無佛出世名獨覺，聞佛說十二因緣法名緣覺，既入聲聞數中得悟領解，皆不別出，大意可見。身子、迦葉等，悉是中乘根性，故聲聞領解兼得緣覺，無勞別出也。又四眾中，有發緣覺心者，其人得悟即不一也，〈信解品〉云：「密遣二人追捉將還」即是其義。菩薩不領解者，聲聞之教不明得佛，今經開其歸大之路，自恐解謬故對佛述解，菩薩不爾故無領解。又其意有三：一、菩薩本意求佛，設有異執而執輕終歸取佛，無有不得之慮，今聞三周之說，但是正其觀慧，故不須領解。二、菩薩悟大，處處有文，二乘作佛始自今教，逐要流傳，故略菩薩領解，胡文或有，漢略不書耳。三、菩薩位行深絕，諸新小菩薩不敢領解，說〈壽量〉竟，彌勒總都領解，初從無生法忍，終訖餘有一生在，則是具足領解，更求何物(云云)？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, clarifying whether there is comprehension in response or none: if the three vehicles alike attain realization, why is it that one sees only śrāvakas offering their comprehension in response, and finds none in the other two? It is now made clear: one who lives where no buddha has appeared in the world is called a solitary-awakened pratyekabuddha; one who hears the Buddha teach the twelve links of dependent origination is called a pratyekabuddha in the fuller sense — since these latter are already included among the ranks of śrāvakas when they attain realization and comprehension, they are, in every case, not separately singled out, and the general purport may be seen from this. Śāriputra, Kāśyapa, and the others are all, in nature, of the capacity of the middle vehicle, and so the śrāvakas' comprehension in response also includes that of the pratyekabuddhas, and there is no need to single them out separately. Moreover, among the fourfold assembly there are some who have given rise to the pratyekabuddha resolve, and the realization such people attain is not uniform — the Chapter on Belief and Understanding says, "he secretly sent two men to pursue and seize him and bring him back," which is precisely this meaning. As for why bodhisattvas offer no comprehension in response: the śrāvaka teaching had not made clear the attainment of buddhahood, and the present sūtra opens up their path of returning to the great, so that, fearing their own understanding might be mistaken, they set it forth before the Buddha in response — bodhisattvas are not in this position, and hence offer no comprehension in response. There are, moreover, three further senses to this: first, a bodhisattva's original intent is to seek buddhahood, and though he may have some divergent attachment, this attachment is light, and he ultimately turns to take up buddhahood — he has no anxiety about failing to attain it; hearing the teaching of the three rounds now only sets his contemplative wisdom in order, and so he need not offer any comprehension in response. Second, a bodhisattva's realization of the great is attested everywhere throughout the text, whereas the two vehicles' becoming buddhas begins only with the present teaching — the text, following what is essential, was transmitted accordingly, and so the bodhisattvas' comprehension in response was abbreviated; the Indic text perhaps had it, but the Chinese, abbreviating, simply did not write it down. Third, the bodhisattvas' stage and practice are profound and far surpassing, and the various newly arisen minor bodhisattvas dare not offer their own comprehension in response; only once the Life Span Chapter has been preached does Maitreya offer a comprehensive comprehension on behalf of all, beginning from the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas and concluding with those who have but one more birth remaining — this then constitutes a complete comprehension in response, and what more could be sought, and so on?

Seventh: Receiving the Prediction 七、得記不得記

Chinese Source

七、得記不得記者，若同皆領解，何故聲聞得記，不見緣覺、菩薩受記？此亦三意：一者、昔明二乘入正位不能發心，何由得記？今既悟大，欣斯別決，故為記劫國也。菩薩發心求佛行成自滿，故不欣急求佛亦不促授，又前教處處授菩薩記，此是恒說逐要傳譯如前(云云)。二、菩薩亦有別記，調達、龍女豈非記耶？又〈法師品〉云：「求聲聞者、求辟支佛者、求佛道者，如是等類，咸於佛前聞法華經，我皆與授記，當得三菩提。」此豈非皆記耶？三、二乘昔來未曾得八相記，故記其劫國；菩薩先已曾記，故不重明耳。淺近之記初住已得，非菩薩所欣，菩薩所欣乃是圓極妙覺遠記耳。故〈壽量品〉中，始從發心訖一生得，妙因斯滿、極果頓圓，此乃授法身記莂，何謂無記耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, clarifying whether a prediction is received or not: if all alike offer comprehension in response, why is it that śrāvakas receive a prediction, while pratyekabuddhas and bodhisattvas are not seen to receive one? This too has three senses. First: formerly it was taught that the two vehicles, having entered the correct stage, were unable to give rise to the resolve — how, then, could they receive a prediction? Now that they have awakened to the great, they delight in this special resolution, and so predictions of eon and land are given to them. Bodhisattvas, having given rise to the resolve to seek buddhahood, have their practice already fulfilled and self-complete, and so they do not delight in urgently seeking buddhahood, nor is it urgent to bestow such a prediction upon them; moreover, in the earlier teachings bodhisattvas were given predictions everywhere throughout — this too is a constant teaching, transmitted and translated according to what was essential, as before, and so on. Second: bodhisattvas also do have distinct predictions — are not Devadatta and the Nāga Girl given predictions? And the Dharma-Teacher Chapter says, "those who seek to be śrāvakas, those who seek to be pratyekabuddhas, those who seek the buddha path — all such kinds as these, once they have heard the Lotus Sūtra in the Buddha's presence, I give every one of them a prediction that they shall attain complete awakening" — is this not, then, a universal prediction for all? Third: the two vehicles have, until now, never yet received the prediction of the eight aspects, and so a prediction of their eon and land is given them; bodhisattvas have already, previously, received their prediction, and so this is not made clear a second time. A shallow, immediate prediction is already obtained at the first abode, and this is not what bodhisattvas delight in — what bodhisattvas delight in is precisely the distant prediction of the perfect, ultimate, sublime enlightenment. Hence, in the Life Span Chapter, from the initial giving rise of the resolve down to the attainment with but one birth remaining, the sublime cause is thereby fulfilled and the ultimate fruit suddenly made perfect — this is precisely the bestowal of the prediction of the dharma-body — how could this be called "no prediction"?

問：
若小悟大，應同授法身記，那得授八相記耶？

Question: If those of the small vehicle awaken to the great, they ought to receive, in like manner, the prediction of the dharma-body — how, then, do they receive the prediction of the eight aspects?

答：
八相是應記，既得應記知必有本，欲使物知聞共結來緣，故與應記耳。又此二乘若聞〈壽量〉，即同損生得法身記也。

Answer: The eight aspects are the response-body prediction; and once one receives the response-body prediction, one knows there must be an underlying source — this is so that beings may know of it and, hearing it, together form a bond for the future, and so the response-body prediction is given. Moreover, if these two vehicles should hear the Life Span Chapter, they would likewise, alike diminishing further birth, receive the prediction of the dharma-body.

Eighth Through Tenth: Depth of Realization, Differing Benefit, and Timing 八悟淺深、九權實得益、十待時不待時

Chinese Source

八、明悟有淺深者，一往同破無明入證初住，細尋必應明晦。初聞法說尚入佛慧，更聞譬說豈不重明？又聞因緣，理自增進，更聞〈壽量〉彌復優深，如聽法人重聞勝前，單複厚薄方之可知也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, clarifying whether the depth of realization is shallow or deep: in a single general pass, they all alike break through ignorance and realize the first abode, but on close examination there must be, in fact, a difference of brightness and dimness. On first hearing the dharma-teaching one already enters the Buddha's wisdom — hearing the parable-teaching further, how could this not brighten it yet again? And on hearing the causal-condition teaching, the principle naturally advances further still; hearing the Life Span Chapter further, it becomes yet more excellent and profound — just as one who listens to the Dharma, hearing it again, surpasses what came before, so the single and the compound, the thick and the thin, may be understood by comparison to this.

九、明權實得益不同者，一云實行得益，權行正為接引影響不論其益。今明不爾，若至〈壽量〉權實悉得益，增道彌高、損生彌盡，隣圓際極，唯一生在，豈非權者益耶？所以初為影響共熟實行，後說極果則自道明，文云：「出入息利乃遍他國」，息利在他即是己利，實行得益由於權引，化功歸己權亦得益，故一音演說法，眾生隨類各得解，何必須待〈壽量〉耶(云云)？又我自欲得此真淨大法即是自益也。

Ninth, clarifying that the benefit gained by the provisional and by the real is not the same: one view holds that only the real practitioner gains benefit, while the provisional practitioner exists purely to draw others in as an echoing presence, and his own benefit is not discussed. It is now made clear that this is not so: by the time one reaches the Life Span Chapter, both the provisional and the real alike gain benefit — as the path advances ever higher and further birth is ever more diminished, drawing near the ultimate limit of the perfect, with but a single birth remaining — is this not the benefit of the provisional practitioner as well? Hence, at the outset, the provisional practitioners serve as an echoing presence, ripening the real practitioners together with them, and afterward, when the ultimate fruit is preached, their own path is likewise made bright. The text says, "the benefit of his breathing in and out extends even to other lands" — the benefit of his breath resting in another is itself his own benefit as well; the real practitioner's gaining of benefit comes about through the provisional's drawing-in, and the merit of transforming others returns to oneself, so the provisional too gains benefit. Hence, with a single voice he expounds the Dharma, and sentient beings, each according to their kind, attain understanding accordingly — why must one wait for the Life Span Chapter, and so on? Moreover, one's own wish to attain this true and pure great Dharma is itself a benefit to oneself.

十、明待時不待時，爾前不悟必待《法華》悟者，名為待時；《法華》前教已解者，名不待時。何故爾？佛有顯密二說，若顯說為論，《法華》之前二乘未悟大道，要須五味調熟會在法華，故云：「說時未至故，今正是其時，決定說大乘。」此即待時也。若密教為論，未必具待五味在法華方會，爾前密有入者故名不待時，此乃大判時不時。若就三周，亦是待時、不待時，迹本二門，亦是待時、不待時，致有前後悟入，即此意也。

Tenth, clarifying whether it awaits its proper time or does not: those who, prior to this, had not awakened, and must necessarily wait for the Lotus Sūtra to awaken, are called those who await their time; those who had already come to understanding in the teachings prior to the Lotus Sūtra are called those who do not await their time. Why is this so? The Buddha has both an overt and a covert manner of teaching. If one argues from the overt teaching, then, prior to the Lotus Sūtra, the two vehicles had not yet awakened to the great path, and it was necessary that the five flavors ripen them gradually before they could be brought together in the Lotus — hence it says, "because the time for preaching it had not yet arrived, but now is precisely its time, and I shall decisively preach the great vehicle." This is precisely awaiting one's time. If one argues from the covert teaching, then it is not necessarily the case that one must wait for all five flavors to be complete before being brought together in the Lotus — there were, prior to this, those who covertly entered the great vehicle, and hence they are called those who do not await their time; this is the great distinction between awaiting and not awaiting one's time. Applied to the three rounds, this too is a matter of awaiting or not awaiting one's time; applied to the two gates of trace and origin, this too is a matter of awaiting or not awaiting one's time — that there should be earlier and later awakenings and entries is precisely this meaning.

Further Questions: Lost Time and the Manner of the Request 待時問答、宿世望譬

Chinese Source

問：
有一種根性非密、非顯二時不攝者，應是失時永不得悟耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: If there is a kind of capacity not included in either the covert or the overt time, ought this to mean that its time has been lost, and it will never be able to awaken?

答：
餘經或謂此為失時，今經不爾。此人雖於密、顯兩時不悟，雖生滅度之想，而於彼土得聞是經，故無失時，乃是待彼土之時耳。

Answer: Other sūtras may hold this to be a lost time, but the present sūtra does not. Though such a person does not awaken during either the covert or overt time, and though he gives rise to the notion of final extinction, he nonetheless comes to hear this sūtra in another land — hence there is no lost time at all; it is simply that he awaits the time proper to that other land.

問：
五千起去，應是失時？

Question: When the five thousand rise and depart, is this not a lost time?

答：
此等應以如來滅度後，弘經人受益也。

Answer: These people are to gain benefit, after the Tathāgata's parinirvāṇa, through those who propagate the sūtra.

問：
身子初周為四眾三根請，譬周為中下請，云何言佛各為三根人三周說法耶？

Question: In the first round, Śāriputra requests on behalf of the fourfold assembly and the three kinds of faculties, while in the parable round he requests on behalf of the middling and inferior — how, then, can it be said that the Buddha preaches each of the three rounds for each of the three kinds of faculties respectively?

答：
此語不便，請則普請、說亦普說，但上根智利聞法得悟，中根處中聞譬得悟，下根居下聞三得悟，汝當隨義云何隨語(云云)？

Answer: This phrasing is not quite apt. The request is a request made on behalf of all, and the preaching, too, is preaching for the sake of all — it is simply that those of superior faculties, being sharp of wisdom, attain realization on hearing the first teaching; those of middling faculties, occupying the middle, attain realization on hearing the parable; and those of inferior faculties, occupying the lowest place, attain realization only on hearing all three. You ought to follow the meaning — why cling so to the wording, and so on?

問：
宿世是過去事法，譬是當、現事不？

Question: "Past-life causal conditions" is a matter belonging to the past — is the parable, then, a matter belonging to the future, or to the present?

答：
經無文，義推應爾。引三歸一，三望一，一則是當；舉事為譬，譬即是現。準後望前，應如所問。

Answer: The sūtra has no explicit text on this, but reasoning from its meaning it ought to be so: drawing the three back into the one, when the three look toward the one, the one belongs to the future; citing an event to serve as a parable, the parable belongs to the present. Measuring what comes later against what comes before, it ought to be as the question supposes.

Further Questions: Obstruction, Prior Knowledge, and the Pratyekabuddha 五濁障大、知不知義、緣覺出無佛世

Chinese Source

問：
舊以五濁障大，四句料揀如前說，有人斷見與無明，合共為障，指《法華論》云「無煩惱人有染慢，不知一乘法身常住」者是也。若博地不執涅槃而不聞法者，即是無明獨為障，若爾為當三周聞法已破無明？為當未聞法破無明？若聞法已破，則無明非是障；若未聞法而能破無明者，都慮無有障。是義云何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The old explanation holds that the five turbidities obstruct the great vehicle, sorted into four propositions as discussed above; there are those who say that the view of extinction and ignorance together combine to form the obstruction, pointing to the Fahua Lun's statement, "those without afflictions have a defiled pride, and do not know that the one vehicle's dharma-body abides permanently," as evidence for this. But if an ordinary worldling neither clings to nirvāṇa nor has heard the Dharma, then ignorance alone forms the obstruction — if so, is it that ignorance is broken only after hearing the Dharma over the course of the three rounds, or is it broken before ever having heard the Dharma? If it is broken after hearing the Dharma, then ignorance is not truly the obstruction; but if one is able to break ignorance without ever having heard the Dharma, then it would seem, in every respect, that there is no obstruction at all. What is the meaning of this?

答：
是他人立義，今為其通。譬如燈生闇滅，不可定其前後，雖不前後，闇定是障(云云)。

Answer: This is a thesis established by others, and it shall now be reconciled for their sake. It is comparable to the lamp arising and the darkness vanishing — one cannot fix a definite order of before and after between them; yet even though there is no such order, the darkness is nonetheless, without question, the obstruction, and so on.

問：
《勝鬘》云：「三乘初業不愚於法，自知當覺。」《優婆塞戒經》第十四云：「二乘自知得菩提，且取小乘果。」又十三云：「知之者易、行之者難，雖知一乘而取羅漢。」彼兩經皆言知，今經云何三根之後猶自不知，初疑後悟，此義云何？

Question: The Śrīmālā [Sūtra] says, "in their initial undertakings, the three vehicles are not ignorant of the Dharma, and know of themselves that they shall awaken." The Upāsaka Precepts Sūtra, fascicle fourteen, says, "those of the two vehicles know of themselves that they will attain bodhi, and for the present take up the fruit of the small vehicle." And fascicle thirteen says, "knowing it is easy, practicing it is hard — though one knows the one vehicle, one nonetheless takes up the fruit of the arhat." Both these sūtras speak of "knowing" — why, then, does the present sūtra say that, even after the three kinds of faculties have heard the teaching, they still, of themselves, do not know, doubting at first and awakening only afterward? What is the meaning of this?

答：
此經亦云知。文云「若實得羅漢，不信此法無有是處，除佛滅後現前無佛，此人雖生滅度之想，若遇餘佛便得決了。」凡有三意，前明知，次明不知，後會歸知，非永不知。又身子云：「今於佛前皆墮疑惑，我今不知是義所趣。」又大通佛時，聲聞多生疑惑，彼見佛、聞法尚疑不知，況不見聞那忽得知？若執二文更相矛盾，秖增諍競於道何益？論者止可論餘事，聲聞成聖能知、不能知，唯佛境界非爾所諳，今試融之。

Answer: This sūtra, too, speaks of knowing. The text says, "if one has truly attained arhatship and does not believe this Dharma, there is no such possibility — except after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, when no buddha is present before one; such a person, though he gives rise to the notion of final extinction, will, upon encountering another buddha, then attain resolution." There are, in all, three senses here: first, making clear knowing; next, making clear not-knowing; and finally, reconciling them by returning to knowing — it is not that they never know at all. Moreover, Śāriputra himself says, "now, in the Buddha's presence, we have all fallen into doubt and confusion — I now do not know to what this meaning tends." And at the time of the Buddha Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū, the śrāvakas, too, largely gave rise to doubt and confusion — those who saw the Buddha and heard the Dharma still doubted and did not know, how much more so those who neither saw nor heard, that they should suddenly come to know? If one clings to these two passages as mutually contradicting each other, this only increases contention, to no benefit to the path. Those who debate this ought properly only to debate other matters — whether śrāvakas who have become sages can know or cannot know is purely the Buddha's own domain, not something for you to be so familiar with. Let it now be reconciled, tentatively, as follows.

三乘初業，初業為二，若久遠為初業，曾聞於大則不愚於法；若取中忘，今日學小始修念處，為初業者，是則不知。其義如此。若得此意，權為初業是則能知，實是初業則不能知。有人言「利者能知，鈍者不能知。」此應四句：權為利鈍，示俱不能知；權為利鈍，示俱能知；權為利鈍，聞則能知，不聞不知；權為利鈍，俱示非知、非不知。今不取此判，但取權者內心了了久知，實行者未得入大是故不知，於義自顯(云云)。

"The initial undertaking of the three vehicles" — "initial undertaking" admits of two senses. If "initial undertaking" refers to the distant past, then, having once heard the great teaching, one is indeed not ignorant of the Dharma; but if it is taken in the sense of the intervening forgetting, where, in this present life, one has studied the small and only now begins the cultivation of mindfulness, then, as an "initial undertaking" in this sense, one does not know. Such is the meaning of it. If one grasps this point: where "initial undertaking" refers to the provisional, one does indeed know; where "initial undertaking" refers to the real, one does not know. There are those who say, "the sharp are able to know, the dull are unable to know" — this would then require four propositions: whether provisional or real, whether sharp or dull, none are able to know; whether provisional or real, whether sharp or dull, all alike are able to know; whether provisional or real, whether sharp or dull, on hearing they know, without hearing they do not know; whether provisional or real, whether sharp or dull, all alike are shown to be neither knowing nor not-knowing. This judgment is not adopted here; rather, it is held only that the provisional practitioner, within his own mind, has long known this quite clearly, while the real practitioner, not yet having entered the great, therefore does not know — the meaning is self-evident from this, and so on.

問：

Answer: The Shi Lun [the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom, attributed to Nāgārjuna] says, "as for pratyekabuddha and solitary-awakened one: the solitary-awakened one arises in an age without a buddha, while the pratyekabuddha-proper vows to be born in an age with a buddha." The Huayan [Sūtra, the Avataṃsaka] says, "when a bodhisattva descends from Tuṣita and emits light to illumine such a one, if he awakens he casts off his body at once; if he does not awaken, he is transferred elsewhere." The Da Jing [the Nirvāṇa Sūtra] speaks of the comet. The Zhong Lun [the Middle Treatise] says, "the pratyekabuddha who arises after the buddha-dharma has already vanished from the world is the solitary-awakened one. As for one who vows to be born in an age with a buddha: he has previously attained the first fruit, and if, before his fourteen remaining births are complete, he encounters a buddha, he becomes an arhat; if he does not encounter a buddha, he becomes a solitary-awakened one; but if he does encounter a buddha, he neither casts off his lifespan nor is transferred elsewhere, since it was precisely his vow to see a buddha that brought him this far. The same holds, by analogy, for the second and third fruits." There are, moreover, community-dwelling pratyekabuddhas, who, in an age without a buddha, teach and transform others as master and disciples. There ought to be two kinds of these: after the Buddha has departed the world, when there are no written teachings and sentient beings' faculties have grown dull, the pratyekabuddha does not preach the Dharma — this is not the community-dwelling type, for the community-dwelling type is one who is able to preach the Dharma. There are, further, transformation-manifested pratyekabuddhas, who, wherever it is fitting that they be seen, manifest in the form of a pratyekabuddha. That there should be pratyekabuddhas present in the assembly at the three rounds may thus be understood.

As the showing forth of the real, this belongs to the text that follows, which falls into five parts: first, setting forth the supreme person and dharma; second, setting forth the intent behind appearing in the world; third, restating it; fourth, the direct explanation; fifth, the concluding summary. Setting forth the person and dharma means: it raises the non-discriminating dharma as something only the Buddha knows — the Buddha, with his non-discriminating wisdom, comprehends and knows the non-discriminating dharma, and this is precisely the showing forth of the real dharma.

「所以者何」下，二、標出世意者，為兩：初、總，次、分字。總者，諸佛覺如實之相，乘此實道出應於世，秖令眾生得此實相，唯為此事出現於世，曾無他事，除諸法實相，餘皆名魔事。分字釋者，一則一實相也，非五、非三、非七、非九，故言「一」也。其性廣博，博五、三、七、九，故名為「大」。諸佛出世之儀式，故名為「事」。眾生有此機感佛，故名為「因」，佛乘機而應，故名為「緣」。是為出世之本意，而今開三者，為一弄引耳，如人欲取，先當與之，雖說種種道，其實為一乘，即此義也。「舍利弗云何」下，三、重標者，將欲分別，更重提起為解釋之端。又此大事佛所尊重，如《釋論》中明，父王欲多聞太子名，數數說之無有厭足(云云)。

From "why is this?" onward is, second, the setting forth of the intent behind appearing in the world, which falls into two: first, the general statement; next, the breakdown by character. The general statement is: the buddhas awaken to the mark of things as they really are, and, riding upon this real path, appear in response within the world, solely so as to cause sentient beings to attain this mark of reality — it is only for this one matter that they appear in the world, and there is, in truth, never any other matter; apart from the true mark of all dharmas, everything else is called a demonic affair. The breakdown by character is as follows: "one" is the one true mark, which is neither five, nor three, nor seven, nor nine, and hence it is called "one." Its nature is vast and broad, encompassing the five, the three, the seven, and the nine, and hence it is called "great." The buddhas' ceremonial manner of appearing in the world is hence called the "affair." That sentient beings possess this capacity to move the Buddha is hence called the "cause," and that the Buddha, riding upon this capacity, responds to it, is hence called the "condition." This is the original intent behind appearing in the world; and as for the present opening of the three, this is simply a device to draw beings in — just as, when a person wishes to take something from another's hand, he must first give something to him — though he speaks of paths of various kinds, in reality it is all for the sake of the one vehicle; this is precisely this meaning. From "Śāriputra, how" onward is, third, the restating: about to make the distinctions, he once more raises the matter again so as to serve as the starting point of the explanation. This great cause, moreover, is something the Buddha himself holds in reverence — as the Shi Lun makes clear, a king-father, wishing to hear his crown prince's name spoken often, has it spoken again and again without ever growing weary of it, and so on.

「我以無數方便、種種因緣，演說諸法」，此自是開權之文耳。從「是法非思量分別之所能解」顯理一；「唯有諸佛乃能知之」顯智一；「唯以一大事」者，小須分別，一則是理，大則是智，事則是行，理發智、智導行，逐此義便是顯行一；知見者，智知於理，眼見諦法，諦法無為則無分別，以無為故而有差別，約此知見論開示悟入，以略擬廣則有四十位，是顯位一。又取結四句文明一，一即法身，大即般若，事即解脫，是祕密藏即顯三法一；出現於世顯感應一；但教化菩薩顯眷屬一；諸有所作顯神通一；唯以佛之知見示悟眾生，顯利益一；但以一佛乘故，為眾生說法，顯說法一。經文印義信如符契。若略和舊作四一者，數同義異。舊云果一，今言理一，依義、依文。依義者，若無理一，眾事顛倒悉是魔說，非復佛經，故須理一；依文者，文稱佛知見，今取所知見，所見即諦、所知即境，境諦即實相之理，故名理一。舊云因一，今云行一，因語單義別，行一語通收得因果，故言行一。人一、教一與彼同。

"By countless expedient means and various causal conditions I expound all dharmas" — this itself is the text of the opening of the provisional. From "this Dharma is not something that thought and discrimination can comprehend" is shown the one-in-principle; "only the buddhas are able to know it" is shown the one-in-wisdom; as for "solely by means of the one great matter" — this requires a little distinguishing: "one" is the principle, "great" is the wisdom, and "matter" is the practice — the principle gives rise to wisdom, and wisdom guides the practice, and following this sense is precisely the showing of the one-in-practice. "Knowledge and vision": wisdom knows the principle, and the eye sees the dharma of the truth — the dharma of the truth, being unconditioned, is without distinction, yet, because it is unconditioned, it also has distinctions — and, in terms of this knowledge and vision, discussing "opening, showing, awakening, and entering," taking the brief as a model for the extended, there are forty stages, and this is the showing of the one-in-stage. Taking, further, the text of the concluding four phrases as making clear the "one": "one" is precisely the dharma-body, "great" is precisely prajñā, and "matter" is precisely liberation, and this is the secret treasury — this shows the one-in-the-three-dharmas; "appearing in the world" shows the one-in-response; "only teaching and transforming bodhisattvas" shows the one-in-retinue; "all that he does" shows the one-in-spiritual-powers; "solely by means of the Buddha's knowledge and vision showing and awakening sentient beings" shows the one-in-benefit; "solely by means of the one buddha vehicle he preaches the Dharma for sentient beings" shows the one-in-preaching. The sūtra's text and its meaning correspond exactly, as a tally matches its counterpart. If one reconciles this briefly with the old explanation of the four ones, the numbering is the same but the meaning differs. The old explanation calls it the one-in-fruit; here it is called the one-in-principle, whether reckoned by meaning or by the text itself. By meaning: if there were no one-in-principle, then all affairs would be topsy-turvy and every one of them would be the Māra's own teaching, no longer the Buddha's sūtra at all — hence the one-in-principle is necessary. By the text itself: the text speaks of "the Buddha's knowledge and vision," and here what is taken up is what is known and seen — what is seen is precisely the truth, and what is known is precisely its object, and the object and the truth are precisely the principle of the reality-mark, and hence it is named the one-in-principle. The old explanation calls it the one-in-cause; here it is called the one-in-practice — the word "cause" is narrow and its meaning specific, whereas the term "one-in-practice" broadly gathers in both cause and fruit together, and hence it is called the one-in-practice. The one-in-person and the one-in-teaching are the same as in the old explanation.

The One-in-Principle: The Four Stages 理一之一：約四位

Chinese Source

今且從略說，以四一消文，先釋理一，復為四意：一、約四位，二、約四智，三、約四門，四、約觀心。一、約四位者，諦境不可知見，約於智眼乃能知見；二智四眼不能知見，唯一切種智佛眼則能知見。經云「為令眾生開佛知見」，不論佛果自知自見，若偏語佛果即失眾生，若語眾生則無佛知見，故不可偏取。三教行人雖是眾生，未有佛眼、佛智故，不能知見實相；圓教四位亦是眾生，又分得佛眼、佛智，則眾生義成，知見義亦成，故寄此四位以釋理一，如瑞相中天雨四花，表萬善同歸得入四位，乘四位華以趣佛果，故約位顯理也。

Ru-Yi Translation

For the present, then, speaking in brief, the four ones are used to interpret the text, beginning with the explanation of the one-in-principle, which again has four senses: first, in terms of the four stages; second, in terms of the four kinds of wisdom; third, in terms of the four gates; fourth, in terms of the contemplation of mind. First, in terms of the four stages: the object that is the truth cannot, in itself, be known or seen — it is only in terms of the eye of wisdom that it can be known and seen; the two lesser kinds of wisdom and the four lesser eyes are unable to know and see it, and it is only the buddha-eye, together with the wisdom of all modes of all things, that is able to know and see it. The sūtra says, "so as to cause sentient beings to open the Buddha's knowledge and vision" — it does not speak simply of the buddha-fruit's own self-knowing and self-seeing; for if one speaks one-sidedly of the buddha-fruit alone, one loses sight of sentient beings, and if one speaks of sentient beings alone, then there is no Buddha's knowledge and vision at all — hence one cannot cling one-sidedly to either. Practitioners of the three lesser teachings, though they are sentient beings, do not yet possess the buddha-eye or buddha-wisdom, and hence are unable to know and see the reality-mark; the four stages of the perfect teaching are likewise sentient beings, yet they have, in part, attained the buddha-eye and buddha-wisdom, and so both the sense of "sentient beings" and the sense of "knowledge and vision" are thereby fulfilled — hence the exposition relies on these four stages to explain the one-in-principle. Just as, among the auspicious signs, the heavens rained down the four kinds of flowers, representing that the ten thousand forms of goodness alike return and enter the four stages, riding upon the flowers of the four stages so as to advance toward buddhahood — it is for this reason that the principle is shown in terms of the stages.

開者，即是十住初破無明，開如來藏見實相理。何者？性德之理而為通別兩惑之所染著，難可了知，初心能圓信、圓受、圓伏，而未能斷，不名為開；內加觀行，外藉法雨助，破通別惑藏，顯出真修性，知見朗然開發，如日出闇滅眼目有用，故名為開。緣修破惑故名「使得清淨」。《仁王》云：「入理般若名為住」，住於十住小白花位也。

"Opening" is precisely the initial breaking of ignorance at the ten abodes, opening the tathāgatagarbha and seeing the principle of the reality-mark. Why is this? The principle of the nature's virtue is defiled and attached to by the two delusions, the shared and the distinct, and is hard to fully comprehend; the initial mind is able to have perfect faith, perfect acceptance, and perfect suppression of these delusions, but is not yet able to cut them off, and so this is not yet called "opening"; only when, inwardly, contemplative practice is added, and, outwardly, the aid of the rain of the Dharma is relied upon, breaking through the store of shared and distinct delusion and revealing the nature of true cultivation, does the knowledge and vision become radiantly opened and manifest — like the sun rising and the darkness vanishing, so that the eyes may then be put to use — and hence this is called "opening." Since conditioned cultivation breaks through delusion, this is called "attaining purity." The Renwang [Sūtra, the Humane Kings Sūtra] says, "entering the prajñā of principle is called abiding" — abiding in the small white-flower stage of the ten abodes.

示者，惑障既除知見體顯，體備萬德，法界眾德顯示分明，故名為示，即是十行大白位也。悟者，障除體顯法界行明，事理融通更無二趣，攝大乘師云：「如理智、如量智。」今理量不二故名為悟，即十迴向小赤位也。入者，事理既融自在無礙，自在流注任運，從阿到茶入薩婆若海，如攝大乘師云：「如理、如量通達自在。」如量知見能持眾德，如理知見能遮諸惑，即是十地大赤位也。

"Showing" means: once the obstruction of delusion is removed, the essence of knowledge and vision is manifested; this essence, complete with the myriad virtues, displays the manifold virtues of the dharma-realm clearly and distinctly, and hence this is called "showing" — this is precisely the great white stage of the ten practices. "Awakening" means: once the obstruction is removed and the essence manifested, the practice of the dharma-realm becomes bright, and phenomena and principle are fused together with no longer any duality of direction — as the masters of the She Dacheng [the Mahāyānasaṃgraha school] say, "the wisdom that accords with principle, and the wisdom that accords with measure." Here, principle and measure being non-dual, this is called "awakening" — this is precisely the small red stage of the ten dedications. "Entering" means: once phenomena and principle are fused, one is at ease and unobstructed, flowing at ease and spontaneously, from the letter A to the letter Ca — the first letter of the Siddham syllabary to its last — entering the ocean of sarvajña, the all-knowing wisdom, as the masters of the She Dacheng say, "the wisdom that accords with principle and the wisdom that accords with measure, penetrating freely." The wisdom that accords with measure, in its knowledge and vision, is able to sustain the myriad virtues; the wisdom that accords with principle, in its knowledge and vision, is able to screen off every delusion — this is precisely the great red stage of the ten grounds.

然圓道妙位一位之中，即具四十一地功德，秖開即具示悟入等，更非異心，但如理知見，無有分別淺深之相；欲顯如量知見故，分別四位耳。發心、畢竟二不別，如是二心前心難，既云難易，即知初心與畢竟心，應有明晦淺深之別，猶如月體初後俱圓而有朔望之殊，四位知見皆明照實相而說開入之異耳(云云)。

Yet within a single stage of the sublime perfect path, all forty-one stages' merits and virtues are already fully contained — the mere "opening" already includes "showing," "awakening," and "entering" within it, and there is, in truth, no other mind involved; it is only that, in the wisdom that accords with principle in its knowledge and vision, there is no mark of shallow or deep to be distinguished at all — it is only in order to display the wisdom that accords with measure that the four stages are distinguished. The initial resolve and the ultimate consummation are not, in themselves, two different things — yet it is said that, between these two states of mind, the earlier is more difficult — and since it speaks of "difficult" and "easy," one thereby knows that between the initial mind and the ultimate mind there must be some distinction of brightness and dimness, of shallowness and depth — just as the disk of the moon, whether at its beginning or its end, is equally round, and yet there is the distinction of new moon and full moon. The knowledge and vision at all four stages equally illumine the reality-mark, and it is only in speaking of them that the distinction of opening and entering is made, and so on.

The One-in-Principle: Four Wisdoms, Four Gates, and the Contemplation of Mind 理一之二三四：約四智、四門、觀心

Chinese Source

二、約四智者，今欲以圓教四智對於四位，不如《般若》中通教釋也。一、道慧見道實性，實性中得開佛知見也；二、道種慧，知十法界諸道種別解惑之相，一一皆示佛知見也；三、一切智，知一切法一相寂滅，寂滅即悟佛知見也；四、一切種智，知一切法一相寂滅相，種種行類相貌皆識，即入佛知見也。又道慧如理名開，道種慧如量名示，一切智理量不二稱悟，一切種智理量雙照為入，此亦約實理無淺深中，而淺深分別也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, in terms of the four kinds of wisdom: here it is intended to correlate the perfect teaching's four kinds of wisdom with the four stages, and this is not the same as the explanation in terms of the shared teaching found in the Prajñā scriptures. First, the wisdom of the path sees the real nature of the path, and, within this real nature, opens the Buddha's knowledge and vision; second, the wisdom of the varieties of the path knows the various marks of understanding and delusion, distinguished path by path, throughout the ten dharma-realms, and each and every one of these shows forth the Buddha's knowledge and vision; third, the wisdom of all things knows that all dharmas share the single mark of quiescent extinction, and this quiescent extinction is precisely the awakening to the Buddha's knowledge and vision; fourth, the wisdom of all modes of all things knows that all dharmas share the single mark of quiescent extinction, while also discerning the marks and appearances of every kind and category of activity, and this is precisely the entering into the Buddha's knowledge and vision. Moreover, the wisdom of the path, according with principle, is called "opening"; the wisdom of the varieties of the path, according with measure, is called "showing"; the wisdom of all things, in which principle and measure are non-dual, is termed "awakening"; and the wisdom of all modes of all things, in which principle and measure are illumined together, is "entering" — this, too, is a distinction of shallow and deep drawn within the real principle, which is, in itself, without any such distinction.

三、約圓教四門橫釋四句者，空門，一空一切空，即開佛知見；有門，一有一切有，即示佛知見也；亦空亦有門，一切亦空亦有，即悟佛知見也；非空非有門，一切非空非有，即入佛知見。能通則四，所通則一，開示悟入是能通之門，所知所見是所通之理也。

Third, in terms of a horizontal explanation of the four phrases through the perfect teaching's four gates: the gate of emptiness — where one thing is empty, all things are empty — is precisely the opening of the Buddha's knowledge and vision; the gate of existence — where one thing exists, all things exist — is precisely the showing of the Buddha's knowledge and vision; the gate of both emptiness and existence — where all things are both empty and existent — is precisely the awakening to the Buddha's knowledge and vision; the gate of neither emptiness nor existence — where all things are neither empty nor existent — is precisely the entering into the Buddha's knowledge and vision. That which does the penetrating is fourfold, while that which is penetrated is one: opening, showing, awakening, and entering are the gates that do the penetrating, while what is known and what is seen is the principle that is penetrated.

四、約觀心釋者，觀於心性三諦之理不可思議，此觀明淨，名為開；雖不可思議，而能分別空、假、中心，宛然無濫，名為示；空、假、中心，即三而一、即一而三，名為悟；空、假、中心，非空、假、中，而齊照空、假、中，名為入。是為一心三觀而分開示悟入之殊也。

Fourth, in terms of the explanation through the contemplation of mind: to contemplate that the principle of the three truths within the nature of the mind is inconceivable, and for this contemplation to become bright and pure, is called "opening"; though inconceivable, to be able still to distinguish the empty mind, the provisional mind, and the mind of the middle, each vividly distinct and unconfused, is called "showing"; that the empty, the provisional, and the middle mind are, precisely as three, one, and, precisely as one, three, is called "awakening"; that the empty, the provisional, and the middle mind are neither empty, provisional, nor middle, and yet illumine the empty, the provisional, and the middle equally together, is called "entering." This is the distinction of opening, showing, awakening, and entering drawn within the single contemplation of mind that sees the three truths at once.

所以四種釋者，見理由位，位立由智，智發由門，門通由觀；觀故則門通，門通故智成，智成故位立，位立故見理，見理故名為理一也。

As for why there are these four kinds of explanation: seeing the principle depends on the stage; the stage is established through wisdom; wisdom arises through the gate; and the gate is penetrated through contemplation. Because there is contemplation, the gate is penetrated; because the gate is penetrated, wisdom is accomplished; because wisdom is accomplished, the stage is established; because the stage is established, the principle is seen; and because the principle is seen, it is called the one-in-principle.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "Śāriputra, it is for this reason that all buddhas, through the one great cause" onward is precisely the concluding summary of the meaning of the one-in-principle. The provisional teachings of former times could, in a certain sense, also be discussed in terms of opening, showing, awakening, and entering, yet they were not the Buddha's knowledge and vision, and hence they were provisional; what is made clear here is the Buddha's knowledge and vision, and hence it is real — and the real is precisely the one-in-principle. From "he told Śāriputra: the Tathāgata teaches and transforms only bodhisattvas" onward makes clear the one-in-person: in terms of the provisional teaching of former times, it was said that he taught and transformed the three vehicles, but, in principle and in reality, he only ever transformed bodhisattvas — just as the impoverished son himself called himself a lowly hired servant, while, as the elder regarded him, he was in reality his own son all along — this is precisely the one-in-person. From "all that he does is always for the sake of the one matter" — Guangzhai calls this the one-in-teaching, but here it is called the one-in-practice. The manifold practices of the three vehicles are called "all"; because they are perfect, they are "all," and this is precisely "the one matter." Where does this practice arrive at, in the end? It tends solely toward the Buddha's knowledge and vision, and this is precisely the meaning of the one-in-practice. It may also be taken as the one-in-teaching: if one speaks in terms of the teaching's master, then "all that he does" takes only teaching and transforming as its business, and here "one-in-teaching" is the more fitting term; if one speaks in terms of the person who practices, then the matter that is done, in its very doing, is precisely practice, and here it is fitting to call this the one-in-practice. Yet each of the four phrases admits of two senses: within the one-in-principle, if one takes the capacity to know and see, then "one-in-stage" is the more fitting term; if the object known and seen, then "one-in-principle" is the more fitting term. Within the phrase for the one-in-person, if one takes teaching and transforming, then "one-in-teaching" is more fitting; if one takes the bodhisattvas who are its object, then "one-in-person" is more fitting. Within the phrase for the one-in-teaching, if one takes the preaching of the Dharma to sentient beings by means of the one buddha vehicle, then "one-in-teaching" is more fitting; if one takes the sense of riding and conveying, then "one-in-practice" is more fitting. All four phrases work this way in common — the explanation simply follows whichever is more fitting in each case.

Chinese Source

從「但以一佛乘」者，光宅為因一，今言教一。圓頓之教，名一佛乘，故〈序品〉云「說大乘經」即是教義也，自別教已去，皆名有餘之說，即不了義非佛一乘。光宅云：「無緣覺、聲聞之二，無偏行菩薩之三。」又有人云：「無菩薩、緣覺為無二，無聲聞為無三。」若作此解，秖是無三藏諸乘、存於通乘，何關一佛乘耶？有人言：「無緣覺為無二，無聲聞為無三，存於菩薩大乘。」若爾秖無三藏中二乘，不無三藏中菩薩，此存有餘，何關佛乘？何處經論以聲聞為第三？既無此次第，都是妄說。若依汝解，無二是無緣覺，無三是無菩薩，第一是聲聞應不被無；若如此者則大倒亂。今言「但以一佛乘」者，純說佛法之圓教乘也。「無餘乘」者，無別教帶方便有餘之說。「無二」者，無《般若》中之帶二。「無三」者，無方等中所對之三也。如此二三皆無，況三藏中三耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

From "but with the one buddha vehicle" onward — Guangzhai takes this as the one-in-cause, but here it is called the one-in-teaching. The perfect and sudden teaching is named the one buddha vehicle; hence the Assembly and the Six Signs chapter says, "preaching the great vehicle sūtra," which is precisely this sense of "teaching." From the distinct teaching onward, all are called teachings "with remainder" — that is, teachings of incomplete meaning, not the buddhas' one vehicle. Guangzhai says, "the one buddha vehicle means without the two of pratyekabuddha and śrāvaka, and without the three, including the bodhisattva of partial practice." There are, further, those who say, "without bodhisattva and pratyekabuddha is 'without the two'; without śrāvaka is 'without the three.'" If one adopts this explanation, this would simply mean without the various vehicles of the Tripiṭaka teaching, while retaining the shared-teaching vehicle — what has this to do with the one buddha vehicle at all? There are those who say, "without pratyekabuddha is 'without the two'; without śrāvaka is 'without the three'; retaining the bodhisattva great vehicle." If this were so, it would simply be without the two vehicles within the Tripiṭaka teaching, while not being without the bodhisattva within the Tripiṭaka teaching — this retains a teaching with remainder — what has this to do with the buddha vehicle? In what sūtra or treatise is the śrāvaka ever placed as the third? Since there is no such sequence anywhere, this is, in every respect, an unfounded assertion. If one follows your explanation, "without the two" means without pratyekabuddha, and "without the three" means without bodhisattva — then the first, the śrāvaka, ought not to be excluded at all — and if this were so, it would produce utter confusion and disorder. What is meant here by "but with the one buddha vehicle" is the purely preached vehicle of the perfect teaching of the buddha-dharma. "Without any remaining vehicle" means without the distinct teaching's expedient teaching that carries a remainder. "Without the two" means without the pairing of two found within the Prajñā scriptures. "Without the three" means without the trio set in opposition within the Vaipulya scriptures. Given that these twos and threes are, every one, absent, how much more so the three of the Tripiṭaka teaching?

The chapters on the buddhas of the three time-periods each make clear the one-in-teaching and the one-in-practice, and afterward generally discuss the one-in-person and the one-in-principle, as may be seen in the text. It would also be acceptable for each chapter to independently work out its own four ones, though this would not be as clear as the general treatment of the text. The Pusa Yingluo Jing [the Sūtra of the Bodhisattva's Necklace of Jewels], fascicle thirteen, makes clear the nine time-periods of buddhas: the past has its own three sub-periods, and the present and future likewise each have three. As for the three sub-periods of the future buddhas: the past-of-the-future is where an ancient buddha, out of compassion, enters the future and takes on various forms to save sentient beings; the present-of-the-future is one who is due to receive a prediction in time to come; the future-of-the-future is a buddha who is, in turn, next in line to receive a prediction. The past may be understood by analogy with this, and so on. As for the present: the present-of-the-present is the present teaching-master; the future-of-the-present is the one next in succession to him; the past-of-the-present is an ancient buddha manifesting a trace in the present.

從過去諸佛章，此中應具六義，但出二種：一、開方便，二、顯真實。兩則指上，兩則指下。「以無量無數方便」者明開權也，「是法皆為一佛乘故」明顯實也，例上一佛乘即是教一；「從諸佛聞法」，是雖聞於法，法被眾生兼得人一；「究竟皆得一切種智」，種智所知即是理一，能知即是行一。雖不次第，四一兼足也。

From the chapter on the buddhas of the past, this section ought properly to be furnished with all six meanings, but only two are set forth here: first, the opening of the expedient means; second, the showing forth of the real. Of the passage's parts, two point to what precedes, and two point to what follows. "By immeasurable, countless expedient means" makes clear the opening of the provisional; "this Dharma is, every bit of it, for the sake of the one buddha vehicle" makes clear the showing forth of the real — following the pattern above, the one buddha vehicle is precisely the one-in-teaching. "Having heard the Dharma from the buddhas" — though this speaks of hearing the Dharma, the Dharma is bestowed upon sentient beings, and this also attains the one-in-person. "Ultimately, all attain the wisdom of all modes of all things" — what this wisdom knows is precisely the one-in-principle, and the capacity that knows it is precisely the one-in-practice. Though not set out in strict sequence, the four ones are here all fully present together.

從未來佛章亦有二義，指上指下，兼即具六(云云)。從現在佛門正是化主，初標佛出之意，如諸佛章中唯以大事因緣出現於世，此亦如是，唯為饒益安樂眾生而出於世也，次開權、次顯實，又具四一也。◎

From the chapter on the future buddhas there are, likewise, the same two senses, pointing to what precedes and to what follows, and, taken together, all six meanings are present, and so on. From the gate of the present buddhas — this is precisely the present teaching-master — the passage first sets forth the intent behind the Buddha's appearance in the world: just as, in the chapter on the buddhas in general, it is solely through the one great cause that he appears in the world, so too is it here — it is solely for the sake of benefiting and bringing peace and happiness to sentient beings that he appears in the world. Next comes the opening of the provisional, and next the showing forth of the real — here, too, all four ones are present.

Question: If the five turbidities obstruct the Great Vehicle, then how is it that in the Avataṃsaka Sūtra there are those who hear the teaching without having yet removed the turbidities?

Answer: This should be distinguished by a fourfold formula: first, sharp faculties for the Great Vehicle but heavy obstruction — because the faculties are sharp, the heavy obstruction is unable to obstruct; those in this land who first heard the Great Vehicle at the Avataṃsaka assembly are of this kind. Second, sharp faculties and light obstruction. Third, dull faculties and light obstruction — those who heard the Great Vehicle in the pure lands of other regions are of this kind. The four gates of two of the teachings are distinguished with respect to the lesser vehicle, and the four gates of the other two teachings are distinguished with respect to the Great Vehicle — considering this closely, it may be understood, and so on.

Answer: The turbidity of sentient beings being heavy, one falsely calculates the five aggregates to be the four virtues, and on hearing of the permanent self one grasps at what is not so as though it were so — like an old physician, dull and inexperienced, who does not know good milk from bad, does not know the root cause from which the illness arose, and does not know when a remedy should be given or withheld: knowing nothing at all, the turbidities become a heavy obstruction to him. The text says, "if I were to praise the buddha-vehicle, sentient beings would sink and drown in suffering" — this expresses precisely this meaning. But on hearing of impermanence, suffering, and emptiness, one at once wearies of birth-and-death and delights in nirvāṇa, breaking one's own false calculation and grasping, and hence the five turbidities do not obstruct the lesser vehicle. The text says, "as I was pondering in this way, the buddhas of the ten directions all appeared, and with their brahma-voices comforted and encouraged me" — this expresses precisely this meaning.

The eon is a turbidity shared in common; the other four are turbidities each distinct in itself. From "if my disciples themselves declare" onward is, fourth, the distinguishing of the false in order to press home the true. If the Buddha's disciples are themselves able to have faith and understanding, they are genuine; if they lack faith and understanding, they are neither true disciples nor true arhats — this presses and urges the assembly of the time to accept the teaching with faith and understanding. With respect to the text, this falls into two: first, distinguishing the true from the false; second, exempting certain cases and resolving doubts. Just as, in the world, a disciple follows and accords with his teacher's dharma, inheriting and transmitting the lamp — if he has not heard and does not know, then he has no dharma to accord with, so how could he be called a disciple? From "except after the Buddha has entered extinction" onward is, second, exempting certain cases and resolving doubts. First, the exemption: after the Buddha's extinction, this is not counted as overweening pride. Next, from "why is this? After the Buddha's extinction" onward makes clear that a good person is hard to find and a profound sūtra hard to understand, and this too is not counted as overweening pride. If, while the Buddha is in the world personally teaching this sūtra, one fails to believe and accept it, this is indeed not a true arhat and becomes overweening pride; but if one attains arhatship only after the Buddha's extinction, clinging one-sidedly to the provisional sūtras and not believing the perfect Dharma, this is permitted and not counted as overweening pride. But then, since the Buddha has entered extinction and yet this sūtra still remains, should not failing to believe and accept it still count as overweening pride? Here too an exemption is granted: after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, although this sūtra still exists, one who can explain its text and meaning is hard to encounter, and this causes arhats to fail to believe and understand — this too is permitted and not counted as overweening pride.

次、釋疑。若佛滅後解經人難遇，得羅漢者即永入涅槃耶？即釋云，是人雖生滅度之想，捨命已後便生界外有餘之國，值遇餘佛得聞此經，即便決了。《釋論》第九十三釋〈畢定品〉云：「羅漢受先世身，身必應滅，住在何處而具足佛道？答：『羅漢三界漏因緣盡，更不復生三界，出三界外有淨佛土，無煩惱名，於是國土佛所，聞《法華經》具足佛道。』」即引《法華》云：「有羅漢若不聞《法華》，自謂得滅度，我於餘國為說是事，汝皆作佛。」《論》既引經為證，今釋經還將《論》解。南岳師云：「餘佛者，四依也，羅漢遇之聞經決了。又羅漢修念佛定見十方佛，為說此經便得決了。又凡夫行人，苦到懺悔見十方佛為說，亦得決了。」瑤師云：「實羅漢必自知《法華》志求於大，利根則自知，中、下根須聞而知，故言聞知。何容於佛滅後不聞《法華》？

Next, the resolving of doubts. If, after the Buddha's extinction, one who can explain the sūtra is hard to encounter, then does one who attains arhatship in that time enter final nirvāṇa forever? The explanation given is this: although such a person gives rise to the notion of having attained extinction, after relinquishing this life he is born into a land beyond the threefold world where a remainder still persists, and there, encountering another buddha, he comes to hear this sūtra and is at once brought to full resolution. The Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra, fascicle ninety-three, in its explanation of the "Chapter on What Is Ultimately Fixed," says: "An arhat still bears the body of his former life, and this body must certainly perish — in what place, then, does he come to complete the buddha-path? Answer: 'Once the causes and conditions of the arhat's contaminations within the threefold world are exhausted, he is never again born within the threefold world; beyond the threefold world there is a pure buddha-land free of the name of affliction, and there, in the presence of the buddha of that land, he hears the Lotus Sūtra and completes the buddha-path.'" This in turn cites the Lotus Sūtra, saying: "If there is an arhat who has not heard the Lotus Sūtra and supposes himself to have attained extinction, I will, in another land, teach this very matter to him, that he too shall become a buddha." Since the Śāstra itself cites the sūtra as its proof, the present commentary, in turn, uses the Śāstra to explain the sūtra. Master Nanyue says: "'Another buddha' refers to the four reliances — an arhat who encounters one of them hears the sūtra and is brought to full resolution. Moreover, an arhat who cultivates the samādhi of buddha-recollection sees the buddhas of the ten directions, who teach him this sūtra, whereupon he too attains full resolution. And an ordinary practitioner who, in the depths of suffering, repents and sees the buddhas of the ten directions teaching him, likewise attains full resolution." Master Yao says: "A true arhat must of himself know that the Lotus Sūtra's intent is to seek the Great Vehicle — one of sharp faculties knows this of himself, while one of middling or dull faculties must hear it in order to know it, and hence it is said, 'having heard and having known.' How, then, could it be permitted that after the Buddha's extinction one should not hear the Lotus Sūtra at all?

或聞而不信，遇餘佛方解耶？末法凡夫猶尚能信，況聖人乎？除佛滅後者，指凡夫也。」有人言：「凡夫未證法相，所見不明、執心不固，所以易信；羅漢證法相，所見分明、執心牢固，忽聞異說未便信受，故云不信，其義必然。故身子云：『將非魔作佛惱亂我心耶？』」若從此義，指羅漢不指凡夫(云云)。此直異解，不用此義也。

Or does he hear it and yet not believe, coming to understand it only upon encountering another buddha? Even ordinary beings in the age of the Dharma's decline are still able to believe it — how much more so a sage? 'Except after the Buddha's extinction' therefore refers to ordinary beings." There are those who say: "Ordinary beings have not yet realized the mark of the dharmas; their perception is unclear and their grasping mind is not fixed, and this is why they believe easily. Arhats have realized the mark of the dharmas; their perception is clear and their grasping mind is firmly fixed, and so, on suddenly hearing an unfamiliar teaching, they do not straightaway accept it with faith — hence it is said that they 'do not believe,' and this meaning must necessarily be so. Hence Śāriputra says, 'Might this not be some demon impersonating the Buddha, come to trouble and confuse my mind?'" If one follows this interpretation, then the phrase refers to arhats and not to ordinary beings, and so on. This is simply a differing interpretation, and this present commentary does not adopt it.

Now, first: from "if there are classes of sentient beings" onward, two lines open the bodhisattva vehicle. If one interprets this in terms of the five vehicles, it refers only to the bodhisattva vehicle of the six pāramitās; but if one interprets it in terms of the seven kinds of expedient means, it also encompasses the bodhisattvas of the shared and distinct teachings. Why is this? The Great Vehicle of all three teachings practices the six pāramitās, yet the operation of mind involved differs. Practicing the six pāramitās with a mind that grasps at marks is the Tripiṭaka-teaching bodhisattva; practicing them without any mark at all is the shared-teaching bodhisattva; and practicing them progressing in sequence from neither-mark to no-mark is the distinct-teaching bodhisattva. Here the text simply lists "the six pāramitās" without specifying to which of these it definitely belongs. Considering the phrase above, "furthermore, by means of differing skillful means," this is not the bodhisattva of the six pāramitās alone, but the skillful means of the bodhisattvas of all three teachings. From "offering to the relics" onward is, third, twenty lines opening the vehicles of gods and humans. The text does not explicitly announce that this is the vehicle of gods and humans, but simply describes the making of images and the raising of stūpas, whether done with utmost devotion or with a distracted mind, and from this it may be known that this is the karma of gods and humans. A Dilun master explains: "The child here stands for the ground of childlike purity, having a mind free of the extremes of desire belonging to either the two lesser vehicles or ordinary beings. Gathering sand to build a stūpa: the sand is non-attachment, and the stūpa is the accumulation of the many practices, containing within it the mind of true awakening." That master takes this as a meeting with non-arising and considers it a profound attainment, but the present commentary considers this to depart from the text and to be forced and narrow. Why is this? One who has ascended to the bodhisattva grounds should naturally become a buddha — how could this be remarkable, any more than an asura crossing the sea? Here, rather, it is precisely because a mere child at play with sand, with a distracted mind singing snatches of song, points to something so trifling and yet it still takes hold, that it is like an ordinary being crossing the sea — utterly inconceivable. The Buddha here clearly and broadly gathers together the five vehicles, letting not the tiniest good deed go uncollected, and it would be a mistake to abandon this broad, all-gathering intent and take instead only the narrow reading of non-arising. If one follows that Dilun interpretation, it can hardly even encompass the two lesser vehicles, let alone ordinary beings. Discussed at the level of profundity, both readings come to the same point; but fixed at the level of breadth, that interpretation departs from the text of the sūtra.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

"Namo" carries a rich range of meanings. Some say it means, "save me, save me — may this be given to sentient beings." If the Buddha's answer were, "the buddhas save me," the meaning would not fit properly. The Sūtra on the Five Precepts speaks of it in terms of terror — it is terror above all that may properly be offered up to the Buddha. Birth-and-death, perilous and difficult, is truly terrifying; being unable to save oneself by the Great Vehicle, one now, in common with all buddhas, is ferried across by the lesser — offering up one's terror to the Buddha is fitting, and hence the text says, "joyfully proclaiming, 'Namo Buddha.'" "Joy" is the joy of the manner in which one is saved. The Sūtra on the Five Precepts also says: "'Taking refuge with one's life' means offering everything up to sentient beings." When Devadatta, at the point of death, proclaimed "Namo," he had not yet managed to say "Buddha" before he fell into hell; yet the Buddha predicted that, upon emerging from hell, he would become a pratyekabuddha named Namo.

「世間相常住」者，出世正覺以如為位，亦以如為相，位相常住；世間眾生亦以如為位，亦以如為相，豈不常住？世間相既常住，豈非理一？又釋世間者，即是陰、界、入也。常住者即正因也。然此正因不即六法，緣了不離六法，正因常故緣了亦常，故言「世間相常住」也。「於道場知已」，此舉果釋成開權顯實，道場朗然斯理久暢，物情障重方便施三(云云)。

"The mark of the world is ever abiding" — the transmundane true awakening takes suchness as its rank, and also takes suchness as its mark, and this rank and mark are ever abiding; the sentient beings of the mundane world likewise take suchness as their rank, and likewise take suchness as their mark — how could this not be ever abiding? Since the mark of the world is ever abiding, is this not precisely the oneness of principle? Furthermore, explained otherwise, "the world" here refers to the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields, and "ever abiding" refers precisely to the correct cause. Now, this correct cause is not identical with the six ordinary mundane dharmas, yet the conditioning and revealing causes are never apart from the six dharmas; since the correct cause is ever abiding, the conditioning and revealing causes are likewise ever abiding, and hence it says, "the mark of the world is ever abiding." "Having known this at the site of enlightenment" — this cites the fruit to explain and complete the opening of the provisional to reveal the real: at the site of enlightenment this principle became luminously clear and, long dwelling within it, unfolded outward, but because the sentiment of beings was heavily obstructed, the three vehicles were bestowed as skillful means, and so on.

從「天人所供養」下，第四，有四行半，頌現在佛章，上文有四，今頌三，不頌後結。初一行半，頌為化之意，正為安隱眾生。次「知第一寂滅」下一行，頌上顯實，知第一寂滅即頌理一，「其實為佛乘」，或頌教一，或頌行一。後「知眾生諸行」下二行，頌開權，如文。

From "revered by gods and men" onward is, fourth, four-and-a-half lines versifying the section on present buddhas; the prose above had four parts, but the verse here versifies only three, not versifying the final conclusion. The first one-and-a-half lines versify the intent behind the transforming teaching, which is precisely to bring peace and security to sentient beings. Next, from "knowing the foremost stillness and extinction" onward, one line versifies the earlier revealing of the real: "knowing the foremost stillness and extinction" versifies the oneness of principle, while "in reality this is the buddha-vehicle" versifies either the oneness of the teaching or the oneness of practice. Last, from "knowing the various practices of sentient beings" onward, two lines versify the opening of the provisional, as the text has it.

This private record therefore first sets forth the parable correspondences in advance, to be matched again against the text when it is reached, in the hope that this may serve as its own mirror.

然釋迦章偈凡兩意：一頌上，二本下。上根已悟、中根未了，故須作諭還譬上法，譬不孤起承躡有由，故言譬本也。口舊為五譬，一、長者譬，二、思濟譬，三、權誘譬，四、平等譬，五、不虛譬。然初是總譬非獨長者，思濟是救子不得義耳，猶少見火譬故不用。

Now, the verse of the Śākyamuni section has, in all, two aspects of meaning: first, versifying what came above; and second, forming the basis for what lies below. Those of superior faculties have already awakened, but those of middling faculties have not yet understood, and hence a parable must be made, returning to give an analogy for the dharma taught above — a parable does not arise on its own, but follows upon and continues from what precedes it for a reason, and hence it is called "the basis of the parable." The old oral tradition gave five parables: first, the parable of the elder; second, the parable of pondering how to save the children; third, the parable of luring with the provisional; fourth, the parable of equality; and fifth, the parable of the absence of falsehood. Yet the first of these is the general parable, not the elder alone, and "pondering how to save" refers merely to the meaning of being unable to save the children — this scheme still lacks the parable of seeing the fire, and so it is not adopted here.

Evaluating the Schemes for the Parable 有人評之：六義、本迹

Chinese Source

有人評之，若以句判應有十九句，若以義判則有六義：一、總，二、見火，三、一乘化不得，四、三乘化得，五、還說一乘，六、不虛，自餘攝入六義之內。

Ru-Yi Translation

There are those who evaluate this scheme, saying that, if judged by phrase, there ought to be nineteen phrases, but if judged by meaning, there are six meanings: first, the general; second, seeing the fire; third, transformation by the one vehicle being unable to be achieved; fourth, transformation by the three vehicles being achieved; fifth, teaching the one vehicle once again; and sixth, the absence of falsehood — the remaining items are all gathered within these six meanings.

又十譬則法譬參差，法說中索車在前、父喜在後；譬說中父喜在前、索車在後，雖欲會通，終成迂迴。又大小相違，法說見大機動故喜，譬說見小緣免難故喜；法說明大因，譬說敘小果；法說大障將傾，譬說小難已離，義勢乖各。又有無異故，法說中敘上根易悟，故無索車；譬說明中根猶惑，故有索車。

Moreover, with these ten parables, the direct teaching and the parable-teaching fall out of step with one another: in the direct teaching the seeking of the cart comes first and the father's joy comes after, while in the parable-teaching the father's joy comes first and the seeking of the cart comes after — though one might wish to reconcile the two, in the end this only becomes circuitous. Moreover, the great and the small are set at odds: in the direct teaching, seeing the capacity for the Great Vehicle stirred is the cause of joy, while in the parable-teaching, seeing the lesser conditions escape danger is the cause of joy; the direct teaching makes clear the great cause, while the parable-teaching narrates the lesser fruit; in the direct teaching the great obstruction is on the verge of collapsing, while in the parable-teaching the lesser difficulty has already been escaped — the sense and thrust of the two diverge from each other at every point. Moreover, because of a difference in presence and absence: in the direct teaching, it narrates that those of superior faculties awaken easily, and hence there is no seeking of a cart; in the parable-teaching, it makes clear that those of middling faculties are still confused, and hence there is a seeking of a cart.

If one were to cite "with a reverent mind" as the seeking of a cart, this would utterly fail to grasp the intent of the text — the present scheme is free of these four faults. As for the presence and absence of items: the prose section has five parts — first, opening the three; second, revealing the one; third, the five turbidities; fourth, the true and the false; and fifth, the absence of falsehood. The verse likewise has five, but where the prose has the true and the false, the verse lacks this, and where the verse has the praise of the Dharma, the prose lacks this — the two simply appear alternately in each other's place. As for their sequence: the prose first opens the three and only afterward reveals the one, while the verse first reveals the one and only afterward opens the three. As for opening and combining: opening the three to reveal the one forms the basis of the general parable, and the two verses on this are combined rather than kept separate; next, the passage setting the five turbidities apart forms the basis of four of the parables, and here they are kept separate rather than combined; the absence of falsehood forms the basis of the parable of the absence of falsehood, and here it is neither combined nor separated. As for what is taken up and what is set aside: the four sections of the sūtra text form the basis of the six parables, and these are taken up rather than set aside; the one section on the praise of the Dharma does not belong among the six parables, and hence it is set aside rather than taken up. As for discussing the general and the particular: the opening section, opening the three to reveal the one, generally narrates the whole of Śākyamuni's single transforming teaching-gate; from the five turbidities onward, all of this belongs to the particular parables.

次本迹者，總敘佛教總含本迹，從五濁去別明本迹。五濁一章正明居法身本，見眾生苦起大悲；從一乘化不得者，垂迹(云云)。今謂迹門大意，正是開三顯一，前直法說上根即悟解，中、下未悟更為作譬，譬於三一令得曉了。前法說中既略廣開三顯一，後譬說中亦應略廣許三賜一，因緣中亦應引三入一，若作三譬、六譬、十譬，於三周之文不合，於四人信解乖離，是所不用。

Next, as to the trace and the origin: the general narration of the Buddha's teaching generally encompasses both the origin and the trace, while from the five turbidities onward, the trace and the origin are made clear individually. The one section on the five turbidities directly makes clear the Buddha's dwelling in the origin that is the dharma-body, seeing the suffering of sentient beings and giving rise to great compassion; from the passage on the inability to achieve transformation by the one vehicle onward, this is the manifesting of the trace, and so on. The present view, however, is that the essential intent of the trace gate is precisely the opening of the three to reveal the one: earlier, the direct teaching in prose was enough for those of superior faculties to awaken and understand at once, but those of middling and lesser faculties had not yet awakened, and so a parable had to be made besides, giving an analogy for the three and the one so that they might be brought to understanding. Since the earlier direct teaching in prose already, in both brief and extended form, opened the three to reveal the one, the parable-teaching that follows ought likewise, in both brief and extended form, to grant the three and bestow the one, and the causal-condition teaching ought likewise to bring the three into the one — but if one were to construct three parables, six parables, or ten parables as fixed schemes, this would fail to accord with the text across the three rounds of teaching, and would diverge from the faith and understanding of the four kinds of persons — and hence such schemes are not adopted here.

三頌上顯實中，更開四意：初「舍利弗當知，我見佛子」下二行，明大乘機動，為後索車譬作本；次「我即作」下，第二有兩行一句，明佛歡喜，為後見子免難譬作本；次「於諸菩薩」下，第三三句，正顯實，為後等賜一大車譬作本；次「菩薩聞是」下第四一行，明受行悟入，為後諸子得一大車歡喜譬作本。

Third, within the versifying of the earlier revealing of the real, this opens further into four points of meaning: first, from "Śāriputra, you should know, I saw the buddha's disciples" onward, two lines make clear that the capacity for the Great Vehicle has been stirred, forming the basis for the later parable of seeking the cart; next, from "I then" onward, second, two lines and one phrase make clear the Buddha's joy, forming the basis for the later parable of seeing the children escape the danger; next, from "before all the bodhisattvas" onward, third, three phrases directly reveal the real, forming the basis for the later parable of equally bestowing the single great cart; and next, from "the bodhisattvas, having heard this" onward, fourth, one line makes clear the accepting, practicing, and awakened entering, forming the basis for the later parable of the children's joy at receiving the single great cart.

頌上不虛直為下不虛譬作本，不論開也。又一時大開為三譬：初「今我亦如是」兩行，合而不離，為下總譬作本；二、從「我以佛眼觀」下，離而不合，為下別譬作本；三、不虛譬不離不合，為不虛譬作本。若承上本下略廣二頌，則通三周及信解中，文之與義悉皆不闕。若約廣頌，更開四意頌上四義，為下四譬作本，此亦通三周及信解中文義不闕。

The versifying of the absence of falsehood above directly forms the basis for the parable of the absence of falsehood below, without any further division being discussed. Furthermore, taken together all at once, this opens broadly into three parables: first, "I too am now like this," two lines, combined rather than kept separate, forming the basis for the general parable below; second, from "I, with the buddha-eye, observe" onward, kept separate rather than combined, forming the basis for the particular parables below; and third, the parable of the absence of falsehood, neither separated nor combined, forming the basis for the parable of the absence of falsehood. If one follows this scheme of the brief and the extended versifying, corresponding to what comes above and what lies below, then it holds good throughout the three rounds of teaching and the section on faith and understanding as well, with neither the text nor the meaning ever lacking. And if, with respect to the extensive versifying, one opens this further into four points of meaning versifying the four meanings above, forming the basis for the four parables below, this too holds good throughout the three rounds of teaching and the section on faith and understanding, with the text and meaning never lacking.

若更子派，開頌五濁中為三，開頌方便中為二，開頌顯實中為四，不虛中但一，合成十意，作下十譬之本。此之十意但在法、譬兩周，信解及因緣中其文則闕，故作三節開章承上本下，非是無趣漫作。

And if one divides this further still, opening the versifying of the five turbidities into three, the versifying of the skillful means into two, the versifying of the revealing of the real into four, and the absence of falsehood remaining as one, this together makes ten points of meaning, forming the basis for the ten parables below. But these ten points of meaning belong only to the direct-teaching and parable rounds, and are lacking in the text of the faith-and-understanding round and the causal-condition round — hence the present commentary constructs these three tiers of division, corresponding to what comes above and what lies below, and this is not done aimlessly or at random.

The Brief Versifying: Revealing the Real 頌略：今我亦如是

Chinese Source

頌略中初一行，頌上顯實，後一行頌上開權，此文雖窄，具頌四一。「今我亦如是」，如於諸佛之是，同以一實教化眾生，此是總頌顯實也。「安隱」者，涅槃祕藏是安隱處，佛自住其中，亦安置眾生入祕密藏。安隱處即頌理一，眾生即頌人一，種種法門入於佛道即頌行一，宣示即教一。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the brief versifying, the first line versifies the earlier revealing of the real, and the second line versifies the earlier opening of the provisional; though this passage is narrow, it versifies the fourfold oneness in full. "I too am now like this" — "like this" as with the buddhas, equally teaching and transforming sentient beings by means of the single reality — this is the general versifying of the revealing of the real. "Peace and security" — the secret treasury of nirvāṇa is the place of peace and security; the Buddha himself dwells within it, and also settles sentient beings within this secret treasury. The place of peace and security versifies the oneness of principle; sentient beings versifies the oneness of persons; the various dharma-gates entering into the buddha-path versifies the oneness of practice; and "proclaiming and showing" versifies the oneness of the teaching.

「智慧力」者，即權智力也。「知眾生性欲」者，鑒小機也。「方便說諸法」者，正施權也。「皆令得歡喜」者，隨宜稱機也。二偈雖略，收佛一化開權顯實，原始要終罄無不盡，故稱略頌為下總譬本也。

"The power of wisdom" refers precisely to the power of provisional wisdom. "Knowing the nature and desire of sentient beings" refers to discerning the lesser capacities. "Teaching all dharmas by skillful means" refers directly to the bestowal of the provisional. "Causing them all to attain joy" refers to according with what is fitting to match their capacity. Though these two verses are brief, they gather in the whole of the Buddha's single transforming teaching, the opening of the provisional and the revealing of the real, tracing it from beginning to end and exhausting it completely without remainder — hence this is called the brief versifying that forms the basis of the general parable below.

The Extensive Versifying of the Five Turbidities 我以佛眼觀：廣頌五濁

Chinese Source

二、從「我以佛眼觀」下，四十一行半，廣頌上六義。舊以最後七行，是法說流通，今不用，用頌歎法敦信耳。初四行，頌上五濁開三；次第二，十七行半，頌施方便化；次第三，六行，頌上顯實；次第四，五行半，頌上歎法希有，釋迦章雖無，指諸佛章中也；次第五，二行半，頌上不虛；次第六，六行，頌上敦信。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "I, with the buddha-eye, observe" onward, forty-one-and-a-half lines extensively versify the six meanings above. The old interpretation took the final seven lines to be the dissemination of the direct teaching, but this is not adopted here; instead, they are taken as versifying the praise of the Dharma and the pressing home of faith. The first four lines versify the earlier opening of the three on account of the five turbidities; the second, seventeen-and-a-half lines, versify the bestowing of the transforming teaching by skillful means; the third, six lines, versify the earlier revealing of the real; the fourth, five-and-a-half lines, versify the earlier praise of the rarity of the Dharma — though the Śākyamuni section lacks this, it points back to what is found within the section on the buddhas in general; the fifth, two-and-a-half lines, versify the earlier absence of falsehood; and the sixth, six lines, versify the earlier pressing home of faith.

初四行頌五濁，上文有四：唱數、列名、出體、結釋，今但頌數、名、體三也。上云為五濁故說三，今云為五濁故出世，出世本應說大，障不獲已，故前說小。此又為三：初十一字，明佛有能見之眼；次第二、「六道」下二行三句四字，明所見五濁；次第三、「為是眾生」下有半行，明起大悲應赴。

The first four lines versify the five turbidities; the prose above had four parts — stating the number, listing the names, setting forth the essence, and explaining and concluding — but the verse here versifies only three of these: number, name, and essence. The prose above said that the three vehicles are taught on account of the five turbidities; the verse here says that the Buddha comes forth into the world on account of the five turbidities — coming forth into the world, he should properly teach the Great Vehicle, but being obstructed and unable to do so, he first teaches the lesser. This again falls into three: first, eleven characters make clear that the Buddha possesses the eye capable of seeing; second, from "the six destinies" onward, two lines, three phrases, and four characters make clear the five turbidities that are seen; and third, from "for the sake of these sentient beings" onward, half a line makes clear the arising of great compassion, responding to what is seen.

初十一字「我以佛眼觀見」者，下文云「長者在門外立」，舉下證上，知佛在法身之地，以常寂佛眼圓照群機，若根利濁輕，則以盧舍那像說一乘法；若根鈍濁重，則脫瓔珞以老比丘像，驚入火宅方便開三，秖是于時鑒機，故言「我以佛眼觀見」也。若觀色法應用天眼，若分別根機應用法眼，云何言以佛眼見耶？佛眼圓通舉勝兼劣，又四眼入佛眼皆名佛眼(云云)。

As for the first eleven characters, "I, with the buddha-eye, observe and see": the text below says, "the elder stood outside the gate" — citing what lies below to confirm what stands above, one knows that the Buddha, dwelling in the ground of the dharma-body, with the ever-still buddha-eye perfectly illuminates the capacities of the many, and that, if the faculties are sharp and the turbidities light, he then teaches the one-vehicle dharma in the form of Vairocana, while if the faculties are dull and the turbidities heavy, he then removes his jeweled ornaments, taking the form of an old bhikṣu, and, startled, enters the burning house, opening the three vehicles as a skillful means — this is simply discerning the capacity of the moment, and hence it says, "I, with the buddha-eye, observe and see." Now, if one is observing physical form, one should properly employ the heavenly eye, and if one is discerning capacities, one should properly employ the dharma-eye — why, then, does it speak of seeing "with the buddha-eye"? The buddha-eye is perfectly all-encompassing, raising up the superior while also including the inferior, and moreover, when the other four eyes enter into the buddha-eye, they are all likewise called the buddha-eye, and so on.

「六道眾生」下，第二，有二行三句四字，明所見五濁。「貧窮無福慧」半行，頌眾生濁；「入生死險道相續苦不斷」，此頌命濁；「深著於五欲」一行，頌煩惱濁；「不求大勢佛及與斷苦法」，此頌劫濁；「深入諸邪見以苦欲捨苦」，此頌見濁。或云五熱炙身，欲望捨苦反得苦報，或云諸見即是受，受即是苦，行此苦因望欲捨苦，豈可得耶？

From "the sentient beings of the six destinies" onward is, second, two lines, three phrases, and four characters making clear the five turbidities that are seen. "Poor and destitute, without merit or wisdom" — half a line — versifies the turbidity of sentient beings; "entering the perilous path of birth-and-death, its sufferings continuing without break" versifies the turbidity of lifespan; "deeply attached to the five desires," one line, versifies the turbidity of affliction; "not seeking the buddha of great power, nor the dharma that cuts off suffering" versifies the turbidity of the eon; and "deeply entering into every wrong view, wishing by suffering to escape suffering" versifies the turbidity of views. Some say this refers to scorching the body with the five kinds of heat, wishing thereby to escape suffering but instead receiving only suffering as recompense; others say that these views are themselves a form of feeling, and feeling is itself suffering — practicing this very cause of suffering while wishing to escape suffering, how could this ever be attained?

The Origin of the Five Destinies 《普曜經》：五道源來

Chinese Source

《普曜》曰：「五道源來，五戒為人、十善生天、慳貪墮餓鬼、觝突墮畜生、十惡墮地獄。」無五趣五陰六衰，則是泥洹，不處生死、不住泥洹，便受菩提決，毘曇《毘婆沙》第七云：「地獄中人初生時念云，昔聞沙門說，貪欲是地獄過惡大可畏處，我昔不斷貪欲，今受此劇惱。」此舉貪欲是地獄因也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Lalitavistara says: "As for the origin of the five destinies: the five precepts make one human; the ten wholesome deeds bring birth among the gods; stinginess and greed bring a fall into the realm of hungry ghosts; butting and clashing bring a fall into the animal realm; and the ten evil deeds bring a fall into hell." To be without the five destinies, the five aggregates, and the six forms of decay is itself nirvāṇa; yet neither dwelling in birth-and-death nor abiding in nirvāṇa, one then receives the prediction of bodhi. The Abhidharma Vibhāṣā, fascicle seven, says: "When a person is first born in hell, he thinks: 'I formerly heard a śramaṇa teach that greed and desire are the transgression that leads to this greatly fearsome place, hell; because I formerly did not cut off greed and desire, I now undergo this intense affliction.'" This cites greed and desire as the cause of hell.

又云：「五道各有自爾法，地獄色斷還續；畜生能飛虛空；餓鬼施摶食時能來到人中；人中有勇健、念力、梵行，勇健者，不見果而廣能修因，念力者，久遠所作而能憶，梵行者，能得解脫達分得正決定；天中有自然隨意，所須即得(云云)。」地獄中，成就他化自在天煩惱業及善，而不現前行；他化自在成就地獄煩惱業及不善，而不現前行，舉上、舉下，中間可知。

It also says: "Each of the five destinies has its own natural characteristic: in hell, form is cut off and yet continues again; among animals, there are those able to fly through the sky; among hungry ghosts, when food is offered up in a handful, they are able to come to the midst of humans; among humans there is courage and vigor, the power of recollection, and the holy life — courage and vigor meaning that, without yet seeing the fruit, one is broadly able to cultivate the cause; the power of recollection meaning that deeds done long ago can still be remembered; and the holy life meaning that one is able to attain liberation, reach the portion leading to it, and attain right and certain determination; and among the gods there is the natural following of one's own wishes, so that whatever is needed is obtained at once," and so on. In hell, one still possesses the afflictions, karma, and wholesome deeds belonging to the Paranirmita-vaśavartin heaven, though these do not manifest and operate there; and in the Paranirmita-vaśavartin heaven, one still possesses the afflictions, karma, and unwholesome deeds belonging to hell, though these likewise do not manifest and operate there — citing the highest and citing the lowest in this way, what lies in between may be inferred accordingly.

地獄此方名，胡稱泥犁者，秦言無有，無有喜樂、無氣味、無歡、無利，故云無有。或言卑下，或言墮落，中陰倒懸諸根皆毀壞故。或言無者，更無赦處。

"Hell" is the name for this in our own land; in the languages of foreign lands it is called niraya, which, translated into Chinese, means "without": without joy or happiness, without any pleasant flavor, without delight, without benefit — hence it is called "without." Some also render it as "the lowly and base," or as "the fallen," because in the intermediate state one hangs upside down and every faculty is destroyed. Some render it as "without" in the sense that there is, moreover, no place of pardon there.

Etymologies of the Six Destinies, and the Buddha's Great Compassion 畜生、鬼、人、修羅、天；起大悲心

Chinese Source

獄卒是變化令見，非眾生數，初將罪人縛至閻王所者，是眾生數，若受苦時非眾生數，如此解者初皆正語，若受苦痛聲不復可分別。畜生者形傍，行傍故名畜生。又畜生者名遍有，遍有五道中，四天三十三天悉有，而上天所乘象馬等，是福業化作，非眾生數也。又畜生者名盲冥，盲冥者，無明多故名畜生。劫初時皆解聖語，後飲食異，諂心而語皆變，或不復能語。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the wardens of hell, these are conjured apparitions made to be seen, and are not counted among sentient beings; when a guilty person is first bound and led to the presence of King Yama, this is counted among sentient beings, but at the time of undergoing suffering, this is not counted among sentient beings — those who explain it this way take the initial cries all to be coherent speech, while the cries uttered during the actual suffering can no longer be distinguished as such. As for animals: their bodies lie sideways, and their movement is sideways, and hence they are called "animals." Animals are also called "the pervasively present," because they are found pervasively throughout the five destinies — even within the four heavens and the thirty-three heavens they are all present, though the elephants, horses, and the like on which the gods above ride are conjured by the power of merit, and are not counted among sentient beings. Animals are also called "the blind and benighted," because it is on account of their abundant ignorance that they are called animals. At the start of the eon they were all able to understand the language of the sages; afterward, as food and drink came to differ, and flattering minds arose, their speech was entirely transformed, and some lost the ability to speak at all.

鬼者胡言闍梨多，秦言祖父，眾生最初生彼道名祖父，後生者亦名祖父；又慳貪墮此趣，此趣多饑渴，故名餓鬼；亦被諸天驅使，亦希望飲食故名餓鬼。

As for hungry ghosts: in a foreign tongue they are called preta, which, translated into Chinese, means "ancestor" — the sentient being first born into that destiny is called "the ancestor," and those born there afterward are likewise called "ancestors"; moreover, it is stinginess and greed that cause a fall into this destiny, and this destiny is marked by great hunger and thirst, and hence they are called "hungry ghosts"; they are also driven and made use of by the gods, and constantly long for food and drink, and this too is why they are called "hungry ghosts."

人者胡言摩㝹奢，此云意，昔頂生王初化，諸有所作，當善思惟、善籌量、善憶念，即如王教諸有所作，先思量憶念，故名人為意；又人能息意、能修道得達分；又云：人名慢，五道中多慢者稱人趣也。

As for humans: in a foreign tongue they are called manuṣya, which means "mind" — formerly, when King Mūrdhaja first transformed the people, teaching that in whatever they undertook they should think well, deliberate well, and recollect well, the people, following the king's teaching in whatever they undertook, first deliberated and recollected — and hence humans are called after "mind." Moreover, humans are able to still the mind and are able to cultivate the path and reach the portion leading to it. It is also said that "human" means "pride," since, among the five destinies, it is among humans that pride is most abundant, and hence the human destiny is so named.

阿修羅者，修羅名天，阿言非，非天故稱阿修羅；又修羅名端正，彼不端正故言阿修羅；修羅名酒，阿之言無，彼無酒故言阿修羅也。天者，天然自然勝、樂勝、身勝故天名勝，眾事悉勝餘趣，常以光自照故名為天；又天者天然自然，《阿含》云：「眾生是假名，界是法，五趣眾生與法界和合，若眾生行不善心時與不善界俱，行善心時與善界俱，行勝心時與勝界俱，行鄙心時與鄙界俱，是故比丘當作是學善種種界。」前是因緣釋六趣，後似觀心釋六趣也。

As for the asuras: "sura" means "god," and "a" means "not" — being not gods, they are called "asura"; or "sura" means "upright and fine-featured," and, they being not upright and fine-featured, they are called "asura"; or "sura" means "wine," and "a" means "without," and, they being without wine, they are called "asura." As for the gods: "heaven" means naturally and spontaneously superior — superior in happiness, superior in body — and hence "heaven" means "superior," every matter there being superior to that of the other destinies, and they constantly illuminate themselves with their own light, and hence they are called "gods"; the gods are also naturally and spontaneously so. An Āgama sūtra says: "Sentient being is a provisional designation; the dharma-realm is the true dharma. The sentient beings of the five destinies are united together with the dharma-realm: when sentient beings act with an unwholesome mind, they are united together with the unwholesome realm; when they act with a wholesome mind, they are united together with the wholesome realm; when they act with a superior mind, they are united together with the superior realm; and when they act with a base mind, they are united together with the base realm — for this reason, monks, you should train yourselves in this way, skillfully understanding these various realms." What has come before explains the six destinies in terms of causes and conditions; what follows resembles an explanation of the six destinies in terms of the contemplation of mind.

「為是眾生故」下，第三，有半行，明起大悲。「而起大悲心」者，上舉能見，次明所見，今明大悲熏心，應入三界施設方便，引趣佛慧也。◎

From "for the sake of these sentient beings" onward is, third, half a line making clear the arising of great compassion. "And so he gave rise to a mind of great compassion" — above, the ability to see was cited, and next, what is seen was made clear; now it is made clear that great compassion suffuses the mind, and that he ought to enter the threefold world and set forth skillful means, drawing beings toward the buddha's wisdom.
