Stop, Stop — No Need to Speak

This page continues Ru-Yi's own English translation of Zhiyi's Fahua Wenju (妙法蓮華經文句). Fascicle 3 closes out the sutra's opening chapter with Mañjuśrī's answer to Maitreya's question, then moves into the bulk of the "Skillful Means" chapter (方便品) — the exegesis of the chapter's own title, the Buddha's praise of the buddhas' twin wisdoms, the fourfold interpretation of the ten suchnesses, and the doubt that stirs the assembly, building to Śāriputra's three requests that the Buddha speak. We translate slowly, section by section, with the Chinese source beside the English so readers can study the meaning, not merely read around it.

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary on the Words of the Sūtra of the Lotus Blossom of the Wondrous Dharma, Fascicle Three, Upper Part

天台智者大師說

Expounded by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai

Why the Question Has Both Prose and Verse 長行與偈頌

Chinese Source

◎從「爾時彌勒欲自決疑」下訖偈，即是發問序，文為二：長行、偈頌。長行中經家述自疑、他疑發問。問中此土、他土如文，何意有偈頌耶？龍樹《毘婆沙》云：「一、隨國土天竺有散花、貫花之說，如此間序、後銘也；二、隨樂欲不同，有樂散說，或樂章句；三、隨生解不同，或於散說得解，或於章句得解；四、隨利鈍，利者一聞即悟，鈍者再說方悟。」又表佛殷勤重說，又為眾集前後故有偈也。偈有六十二行，文為兩：初五十四行頌上問，後八行請答。就問為兩：前四行，問此土；後五十行，問他土。長行總問此土六瑞，偈中長有香風地淨，無說法入定。觀文謂言盈縮，尋義不然。說法是慧性，入定是天心，由天心慧性，能作動地放光，舉末即能知本故，縮非縮也。他不見此意，謂彌勒不問兩事，便不以為瑞。今反難之，若彌勒不問，文殊何故而答？又，問指何處為問？

Ru-Yi Translation

From "At that time Maitreya, wishing to resolve his own doubt" down through the verses is the prologue of the posing of the question. This text falls into two parts: the prose section and the verse section. In the prose section, the sūtra's compiler narrates the arising of Maitreya's own doubt and of the assembly's doubt, and the question itself. The question covers this world and other worlds, just as the text shows — so why is there a verse section as well? Nāgārjuna's Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra says: "First, according to local custom: in India there is the practice of scattering loose flowers and of stringing flowers into garlands, corresponding to prose and to the trailing inscriptions of verse used here. Second, according to differing inclinations: some delight in loose prose, others delight in versified lines. Third, according to differing capacities for understanding: some gain understanding through the prose, others gain understanding through the verse. Fourth, according to sharpness or dullness of faculty: the sharp awaken on a single hearing, while the dull awaken only once the matter is stated a second time." It also signifies the Buddha's earnest and repeated teaching, and it exists so that those who gathered before and after the prose account might still hear the matter — for these reasons there are verses. The verses run to sixty-two lines, and the text divides into two: the first fifty-four lines versify the foregoing question, and the last eight lines request an answer. Within the question itself there are two parts: the first four lines question this world, and the last fifty lines question other worlds. The prose section, in questioning this world, poses the six signs as a whole; the verse section additionally mentions the fragrant wind and the purified ground, but omits the preaching of the Dharma and the entering of samādhi. Looking only at the wording, one might call this an uneven surplus and deficiency, but examined for its meaning, this is not so. The preaching of the Dharma belongs to the nature of wisdom, and the entering of samādhi belongs to the heavenly mind; and it is by means of this heavenly mind and this nature of wisdom that the Buddha is able to shake the earth and emit light — citing the branch-tip thereby makes the root itself known, so this is an omission that is not truly an omission. Others, not seeing this point, say that because Maitreya does not ask about these two matters, they are therefore not to be counted among the signs. I now turn this argument back on them: if Maitreya had not asked about them, on what basis would Mañjuśrī answer concerning them? And besides, where exactly does the question itself point?

今指長行，總問是也，若更顯其別問，秖導師兩字是也。良以說法、入定能導於人，既稱導師，即是問說法、入定也，是故非縮。

The answer now is: it points to the prose section's general question. And if one wishes to identify still more specifically where the particular question lies, it is simply in the two words "guiding teacher." For it is precisely by preaching the Dharma and entering samādhi that one is able to guide others — and since the Buddha is here called "guiding teacher," this is itself a question about the preaching of the Dharma and the entering of samādhi. Hence this is not truly an omission.

他云：「風由檀林故香，地加之嚴淨，盈長兩事。」今謂非盈，風本無香而香，為奇特故以成瑞，夫天花至妙，豈有色而無香？此表因運至果，如花有香風，花既集地，地則嚴淨，因若趣果，果則嚴淨。《金光明》云：「聚集功德莊嚴佛身。」故以二事顯成四花，盈非盈也。

Others say: "The wind is fragrant on account of the sandalwood grove, and the earth, in addition, is adorned and purified — these two matters are an added surplus." I now say that this is not truly a surplus. The wind, though ordinarily without fragrance, here becomes fragrant, and it is precisely this extraordinary quality that makes it a sign; for the heavenly flowers are utterly sublime — how could they possess color and yet lack fragrance? This signifies the cause carrying through to the effect: just as the flowers give rise to a fragrant wind, and once the flowers have gathered upon the ground the ground itself becomes adorned and purified, so too, if the cause proceeds toward the effect, the effect is thereby adorned and purified. The Golden Light Sūtra says: "The gathering together of merit adorns the Buddha's body." Hence these two matters — the fragrant wind and the purified ground — serve to bring the significance of the four flowers to completion; this is not truly a surplus.

The Structure of the Fifty Verses on Other Worlds 頌問他土六瑞

Chinese Source

「眉間光」下，次有五十行，頌問他土六瑞，舊云：「頌中不問三乘四眾，不問佛涅槃。今教廢三，那忽問三？方說壽量，那問滅度？於義不便故不問也。」嗚呼！不解消文，抑經就情。今明頌中具問他土六瑞，文為六：初、三行問六趣眾生；二、四行問見彼佛及說法；三、三行問他土四眾；次、一行半結前開後；次、三十一行半，問他土修菩薩行；次、七行問供養舍利，即是問佛涅槃也。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "the light between the brows" onward, the next fifty lines versify the question about the six signs of other worlds. The old commentators say: "The verses do not ask about the three vehicles and the fourfold assembly, and do not ask about the Buddha's parinirvāṇa. Since the present teaching does away with the three vehicles, why would it suddenly ask about the three? And since the sūtra is just about to expound the Buddha's lifespan, why would it ask about his entry into extinction? Since this would not fit the sense of the text, these matters are not asked about." Alas — this fails to properly work through the text, and instead forces the sūtra to conform to a preconceived feeling. I now show that the verses do in fact ask fully about the six signs of other worlds. The text divides into six: first, three lines ask about the sentient beings of the six destinies; second, four lines ask about seeing that buddha and his preaching of the Dharma; third, three lines ask about the fourfold assembly of that other world; next, one and a half lines conclude what precedes and open what follows; next, thirty-one and a half lines ask about the bodhisattva practices cultivated in that other world; and next, seven lines ask about the making of offerings to the relics, which is itself the question concerning the Buddha's parinirvāṇa.

The Six Destinies and the Opening of the Three Vehicles 六趣與三乘之開

Chinese Source

初三行問六趣，驗此頌，知上文光照東方是總照他土意也，此頌頌上總問。六趣眾生是能趣之人，生死是所趣之處，善惡業緣是趣因，好醜是趣果也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first three lines question the six destinies. Examining these verses confirms that the earlier text on the light illuminating the east signifies, as a whole, the illuminating of other worlds; these verses versify the general question given above. The sentient beings of the six destinies are the persons who proceed toward their destinies; birth-and-death is the place toward which they proceed; the causes and conditions of good and evil karma are the cause of their proceeding; and beauty or ugliness is the effect of their proceeding.

從「又覩諸佛」下，第二四行問見彼佛土，直見佛說法，此廣明說法之相，謂說頓教逗大根性，聖主師子即如此土現盧舍那像也。

From "and also beholding the buddhas" onward, the second unit of four lines questions the seeing of that buddha-land — directly, the seeing of that buddha preaching the Dharma. This more fully clarifies the character of that preaching: preaching the sudden teaching to match beings of great capacity, where "the sagely lord, the lion" corresponds, in our own world, to the manifestation of the form of Vairocana.

「演說經法微妙第一」者，即如此土先照高山演華嚴教也。「教諸菩薩」者，即如此土七處等會，無聲聞人也。「照明佛法，開悟眾生」者，即如此土始見佛身，入如來慧也。

"Expounding sūtra-teachings sublime and foremost" corresponds, in our own world, to the light first illuminating the high mountains and to the expounding of the Avataṃsaka teaching. "Teaching the bodhisattvas" corresponds, in our own world, to the seven assemblies at their various places, in which there are no śrāvakas present. "Illuminating the Buddha-dharma and awakening sentient beings" corresponds, in our own world, to beings first beholding the Buddha's body and entering the Tathāgata's wisdom.

「若人遭苦」下，第三三行問彼土四眾，即是頓說之後，次明三藏教也。「若人遭苦」者，開聲聞乘也，此頌具明四諦，在文分明(云云)。若人遭苦而造惡業，苦不得盡，底下眾生是也；若人遭苦而造善業，苦亦不盡，厭下攀上，如難陀為欲故持戒等是也；若人遭苦，於外道法中求解脫，增見長非者，苦亦不盡；若人遭苦厭集，復厭依果，感佛說涅槃者，此人能盡諸苦際也。他土亦開此乘也。

From "if a person meets with suffering" onward, the third unit of three lines questions the fourfold assembly of that other world — that is, following the sudden teaching, it next clarifies the Tripiṭaka teaching. "If a person meets with suffering" opens the śrāvaka vehicle; these verses fully set out the Four Noble Truths, as is plain in the text itself (and so on). If a person, meeting with suffering, commits evil karma, his suffering is never exhausted — this describes beings at the very bottom. If a person, meeting with suffering, performs good karma, his suffering is likewise not exhausted, though he thereby wearies of the lower state and reaches upward toward the higher, as when Nanda kept the precepts for the sake of desire, and the like. If a person, meeting with suffering, seeks liberation within the teachings of outsiders, he only increases wrong views and lengthens his faults, and his suffering, too, is not exhausted. But if a person, meeting with suffering, wearies of its origin in accumulation, and further wearies of the resultant existence he depends upon, and is thereby moved to have the Buddha preach nirvāṇa to him, this person is able to exhaust the very bounds of all suffering. That other world likewise opens this vehicle.

「若人有福」下一行，是開中乘也，若供養佛少，遭苦致惱；若供佛多，雖遭苦而福，故云聲聞三生種福，支佛百劫種福，形彼聲聞故言有福。「志求勝法」者，聲聞厭苦而修行，支佛求道故修行，深求緣起勝妙之理，即是他土開中乘也。「若有佛子」下，是開六度大乘也，真慈悲能紹佛種故言佛子，修於六度故言種種行，志求故言無上慧，六度中無六蔽，如藥中無病故言淨道，非畢竟淨也。

From "if a person possesses merit" onward is one line opening the middle vehicle: if one's offerings to the buddhas are few, one meets with suffering and vexation; if one's offerings to the buddhas are many, though one meets with suffering, one nonetheless has merit — hence it is said that the śrāvaka plants merit over three lifetimes, while the pratyekabuddha plants merit over a hundred kalpas, and, set against the śrāvaka, it is therefore said that the pratyekabuddha "possesses merit." "Aspiring to seek the supreme Dharma" means that whereas the śrāvaka wearies of suffering and thereby cultivates practice, the pratyekabuddha seeks the path and thereby cultivates practice, deeply seeking the sublime principle of dependent origination — this is that other world's opening of the middle vehicle. From "if there are sons of the Buddha" onward is the opening of the great vehicle of the six perfections: because true compassion is able to carry on the Buddha's lineage, they are called "sons of the Buddha"; because they cultivate the six perfections, it speaks of their "various practices"; because they aspire and seek, it speaks of "unsurpassed wisdom"; and because, within the six perfections, the six obscurations are absent — as when a medicine contains no trace of the illness it cures — it speaks of the "pure path," though this is not yet the ultimate purity.

又聲聞苦諦為觀門，緣覺集諦為門，六度菩薩道諦為門，故言淨道。

Furthermore: for the śrāvaka the truth of suffering is the gate of contemplation; for the pratyekabuddha the truth of accumulation is the gate; and for the bodhisattva of the six perfections the truth of the path is the gate — hence it speaks of the "pure path."

「文殊我住」下，第四有一行半結前開後。「見聞若斯」，即是結前，「如是眾多」，即是開後。

From "Mañjuśrī, as I dwell" onward, the fourth unit of one and a half lines concludes what precedes and opens what follows. "What I have seen and heard is thus" concludes what precedes; "and things of this kind, many in number" opens what follows.

The Six Perfections in Sequence 次第問六度

Chinese Source

「我見彼土」下，第五有三十一行半，問他土菩薩種種修行。就此為三：初、一行總問，次、十五行次第問，次、十五行半雜問。初總問可解。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "I see, in that other land" onward, the fifth unit, of thirty-one and a half lines, questions the various practices cultivated by the bodhisattvas of that other world. This divides into three: first, one line asks in general; next, fifteen lines ask in sequence; and next, fifteen and a half lines ask without regard to sequence, following whatever is seen. The first, the general question, needs no further explanation.

「或有行施」下，第二有十五行次第問，中為六：初、六行問檀，二、二行問尸，三、一行問忍，四、一行問進，五、二行問禪，六、三行問慧。就問檀有三意：初四行問捨財，一行問捨身，一行問捨命。珍寶奴婢，貴賤共能此施；駟馬寶車，豪俠者所施；妻子等是外身，身肉等是內身，捨頭目即捨命，而不言法施者，讓後般若也。又約身、命、財與生死後際等得不壞常住，即是法施，故不別說也。

From "there are some who practice giving" onward, the second unit, of fifteen lines, questions in sequence, and within it there are six topics: first, six lines question giving (dāna); second, two lines question moral precepts (śīla); third, one line questions patience (kṣānti); fourth, one line questions vigor (vīrya); fifth, two lines question meditative concentration (dhyāna); and sixth, three lines question wisdom (prajñā). As for the question on giving, it carries three senses: the first four lines question the giving of wealth, one line questions the giving of one's own body, and one line questions the giving of one's own life. Treasures, servants, and the like are things that both the humble and the noble alike are able to give; teams of horses and jeweled carriages are given by those of noble and heroic bearing; wife and children and the like are one's "outer body," while one's own flesh and the like are one's "inner body"; and giving up one's head or eyes is itself the giving up of one's life. That the giving of the Dharma is not mentioned here is because this is reserved for the Prajñāpāramitā perfection discussed later. Furthermore, when body, life, and wealth are taken together with the final limit of birth-and-death and understood as attaining the indestructible and ever-abiding, this is itself the giving of the Dharma, and so it is not separately discussed here.

「文殊師利見王」下，第二二行問戒。約比丘論持戒者，在家施易戒難，出家施難戒易，故約比丘明戒，此中引《五王經》(云云)。「或見菩薩」下，第三一行問忍。忍有三種：

From "Mañjuśrī, I see kings" onward, the second topic, of two lines, questions moral precepts. Discussing the upholding of precepts in terms of the bhikṣu: for a householder, giving is easy and upholding precepts is difficult, while for one gone forth from home, giving is difficult and upholding precepts is easy — hence precepts are clarified here in terms of the bhikṣu, and this passage cites the Sūtra of the Five Kings (and so on). From "or one sees a bodhisattva" onward, the third topic, of one line, questions patience. Patience is of three kinds:

閑林邃谷，惡人惡獸，忍耐無瞋即生忍；自節守志即苦行忍；為求佛道即第一義忍。又「而作比丘」即苦行忍，「獨處閑靜」即生忍，「樂誦經典」即第一義忍。「又見菩薩勇」下，第四一行問精進者，夫深山可畏，非窳怯者所居，勇進者能安之，傍若無物，思修實相念念不休，進求佛道也。「又見離」下，第五兩行問禪，前一行問修根本禪，後一行問修出世上上禪，通途皆得有根本之修也。

amid secluded forests and deep valleys, enduring evil persons and fierce beasts without anger, is the patience of endurance with sentient beings; disciplining oneself and holding fast to one's resolve is the patience of ascetic practice; and seeking the buddha-path is the patience of ultimate meaning. Furthermore, "and becomes a bhikṣu" corresponds to the patience of ascetic practice, "dwelling alone in quiet solitude" corresponds to the patience of endurance with sentient beings, and "delighting to recite the sūtra-texts" corresponds to the patience of ultimate meaning. From "and further sees a bodhisattva, bold and" onward, the fourth topic, of one line, questions vigor: deep mountains are fearsome places, not fit to be dwelt in by the cowardly and timid, yet one bold in advancing is able to remain secure there, as if nothing at all were beside him, contemplating and cultivating true reality, thought after thought without ceasing, advancing in the seeking of the buddha-path. From "and further sees one who has left" onward, the fifth topic, of two lines, questions meditative concentration — the first line questions the cultivation of the root dhyānas, and the second questions the cultivation of the supreme, world-transcending dhyānas; in the general course, both may include a cultivation grounded in the root dhyānas.

離欲者，若離欲得五通，通教定也。又根本本離欲，背捨亦修不淨等離欲，別教兼離二乘欲，中道又離順道法愛欲(云云)。深修禪定者，發初禪一品此定未深，乃至九品傳傳為深，又背捨、九定、八勝、十一切入等傳傳為深，此定轉變自在，能發諸通。凡夫但五通，二乘具六，別教菩薩讓佛分有無漏，亦但稱五通也，圓教初後皆具六通。

As for "freedom from desire": if freedom from desire yields the five supernormal powers, this is the concentration of the shared teaching. Furthermore, the root dhyānas are themselves rooted in freedom from desire; the eight liberations likewise cultivate freedom from desire through such practices as the contemplation of impurity; the distinct teaching adds to this a freedom from the desires of the two vehicles; and the middle way further frees one from attachment to the dharma-love that accompanies compliance with the path (and so on). As for "deeply cultivating meditative concentration": when the first dhyāna first arises, at its lowest grade, this concentration is not yet deep, and it deepens progressively up through its ninth grade; and likewise the eight liberations, the nine successive concentrations, the eight forms of mastery, and the ten totalities all deepen progressively one upon another. This concentration, once fully mastered and freely transformable, is able to give rise to the various supernormal powers. Ordinary worldlings possess only five such powers, while the two vehicles possess all six; bodhisattvas of the distinct teaching, deferring to the Buddha's exclusive share of the undefiled power, are also said to possess only five, while in the perfect teaching all six powers are possessed from beginning to end.

「安禪萬偈」下，第二一行明上上禪，此是別、圓之禪，靜散不相妨，不起滅定現諸威儀，如修羅琴不拊而韻，無緣無念有感則形，故能安禪讚佛也。「復見智深」下，第六三行問般若，為二：初一行是自行，「智深」者，慧窮理本也；「志固」者，誓願廣大也，此即二種莊嚴，能問、能持也。「又見佛子定慧」下，次兩行是化他也，未到慧多，無色定多，四禪等。又背捨慧多，九定定多，十一切中等。又二乘定多，菩薩慧多，佛則等。

From "settled in dhyāna, reciting ten thousand verses" onward, the second unit, of one line, clarifies the supreme dhyāna — this is the dhyāna of the distinct and perfect teachings, in which stillness and diffusion do not obstruct one another; without rising from cessation-concentration, one manifests all manner of dignified deportment, like the asura's lute, which resonates of itself without being plucked — with no conditioned object and no discriminating thought, yet responsive whenever there is an appeal, and thus able, while settled in dhyāna, to praise the Buddha. From "and further sees one deep in wisdom" onward, the sixth topic, of three lines, questions prajñā, which divides into two: the first line concerns cultivation for oneself — "deep in wisdom" means that his understanding fathoms the very root of principle, and "firm in resolve" means that his vow is vast and expansive; these are the two kinds of adornment, wisdom and merit, by which one is able both to question and to uphold the Dharma. From "and further sees sons of the Buddha, in concentration and" onward, the next two lines concern the transforming of others: those not yet arrived at the fourth dhyāna have more wisdom, while those in the formless concentrations have more concentration, as with the four dhyānas and the rest; and again, those cultivating the eight liberations have more wisdom, while those cultivating the nine successive concentrations have more concentration, as with the ten totalities and the rest; and again, the two vehicles have more concentration while bodhisattvas have more wisdom, while in a buddha the two are equal.

又空觀定多，假觀慧多，中觀則等。「無量喻」即是種種方便，諸教之中引無量譬類，助顯第一義也。「破魔兵」者，空觀破四魔，假觀次第破八魔，中觀圓破八魔、十魔、一切魔。「擊鼓」者，初發心住便成正覺，百佛世界作佛，圓擊梵輪法鼓。從「又見菩薩寂然宴默」下，第三有十五行半，不歷次第隨見而問。

Furthermore, the contemplation of emptiness has more concentration, the contemplation of the provisional has more wisdom, and the contemplation of the middle has the two equal. "Immeasurable analogies" refers to all manner of skillful means: within the various teachings, immeasurable similes and comparisons are cited to help reveal the meaning of ultimate truth. "Defeating the armies of Māra": the contemplation of emptiness defeats the four Māras; the contemplation of the provisional defeats, in sequence, the eight Māras; and the contemplation of the middle perfectly defeats the eight Māras, the ten Māras, and all Māras whatsoever. "Beating the drum": from the very abode of the first arising of the bodhi-mind one already attains true awakening and becomes a buddha in a hundred buddha-worlds, perfectly beating the Dharma-drum of the wheel of Brahmā. From "and further sees bodhisattvas silent and at rest in meditation" onward, the third unit, of fifteen and a half lines, questions without regard to sequence, following whatever is seen.

問：
上六度自收得萬行，何須更問，太煩雜耶？

Question: Since the six perfections given above already comprehensively gather in the ten thousand practices, what need is there to question further? Is this not excessively elaborate and disorderly?

答：
上問次第者，自漸一途；非次第者，不定一途。既言種種相貌，何啻兩途而言是煩耶？此次第雜亂，兩番六度，擬他土開三藏，後說方等十二部經，辯六度相貌，具如此間不異。

Answer: The sequential questioning given above follows a single, gradual course; the non-sequential questioning does not follow any single fixed course. Since the text itself speaks of "various forms and appearances," how could this be merely two courses, that one should call it excessive? These two rounds of the six perfections, sequential and non-sequential, are meant to parallel that other world's opening of the Tripiṭaka teaching and its subsequent preaching of the twelve divisions of scripture of the Vaipulya period, in which the forms and appearances of the six perfections are distinguished — fully as in our own world, no different.

The Request for an Answer: Mañjuśrī's Four Concealed Objections 請答與伏難

Chinese Source

從「佛放」下，第二有八行請答，為二：初三行舉疑事述請，後五行釋伏難。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "the Buddha emits" onward, the second unit, of eight lines, is the request for an answer, and divides into two: the first three lines raise the matter of doubt and set forth the request, and the last five lines resolve the concealed objections.

初三行為三：初一行舉見此土事，白毫為本故先舉，及諸事故言「種種」；次「諸佛」下，第二一行舉見他土事，諸佛為本即總攝餘五也；「我等」下，第三一行請答。

The first three lines divide into three: the first line raises the matter seen in this world — since the white tuft is the basis of all the signs, it is cited first, and, because it involves all the other matters as well, the text says "various kinds of things." Next, from "the buddhas" onward, the second line raises the matter seen in other worlds — since the buddhas are the basis there, this single mention comprehensively gathers in the remaining five signs. From "we" onward, the third line requests an answer.

第二就釋伏難為二：初四行正釋伏難，次一行結請。言伏難者，文殊內心搆難不肯時答，其意有三：一、此瑞希奇，不可倉卒輕爾有判；二、智眾如海謙光推高；三、靳固前却生眾渴仰，故以伏難潛而拒之。彌勒彰灼，釋難意亦有三：一、瑞大疑大，若不為釋，憂兕在懷妨聞正說；二、眾海乃多機在仁者；三、闔眾瞻仁，故知注誠殷重，所以彰言釋難，請令時答。

The second part, resolving the concealed objections, itself divides into two: the first four lines directly resolve the concealed objections, and the last one line concludes the request. As for these "concealed objections": in his inmost mind Mañjuśrī constructs objections and is unwilling to answer at once, and his intent in this has three aspects: first, that these signs are so rare and extraordinary that no hasty, casual judgment about them should be made; second, that the wise ones in the assembly are as vast as the sea, and out of humility he defers to those of higher standing; and third, that by holding back and hesitating he causes the assembly's yearning and admiration to grow all the greater — and it is for these reasons that he uses concealed objections to quietly decline. Maitreya, speaking openly and clearly, resolves these objections, and his own intent likewise has three aspects: first, that the sign is great and the doubt is great, and that if it is not resolved, an anxious rhinoceros-like unease will remain lodged in the heart, obstructing the hearing of the true teaching to come; second, that the assembly, vast as the sea, has its many capacities resting upon you, the Worthy One; and third, that the whole assembly gazes up at you, and hence one may know that their earnest sincerity is deep and weighty — and for this reason he speaks openly to resolve the objections, requesting that an answer be given without delay.

初伏難者，因正請生，請云「佛子文殊願決眾疑」，文殊仍此起初伏難，汝云眾疑，眾未曾疑，若疑應問；眾既不疑，我何所決？彌勒即以第一偈釋云「四眾欣仰瞻仁及我」。及我欲令我問，瞻仁欲得仁答。文殊因此起第二難，眾同有疑，不易可答，待佛出定，然後決疑。彌勒即用第二偈釋，若有疑在，懷憂兕不泰，應以時答。復知如來何時起定，故言「佛子時答，決疑令喜」。文殊因此起第三難，我與仁者同居學地，欲測佛意微共籌量，獨令我答，於理不可。彌勒即以第三偈釋，我亦微心下思踟蹰兩楹，為說妙法？為當授記？故言「佛坐道場，所得妙法，為欲說此，為當授記。」文殊因此起第四難，若如汝說即是釋疑，何煩我答？彌勒即以第四偈釋，安得以我猶豫之心而判大事？故言「示諸佛土，此非小緣」。文殊伏難既窮，謙光亦止，後一偈結請答也。此四伏難，光宅受於次師，次師受於江北釗師；既是先賢文外巧思，今用之。

As for the first concealed objection: it arises from the direct request itself, which says, "O son of the Buddha, Mañjuśrī, we beg you to resolve the assembly's doubts." Mañjuśrī, on this basis, raises his first concealed objection: "You say the assembly has doubts, yet the assembly has never actually doubted; if they had a doubt, they themselves would ask it — and since the assembly has not doubted, what is it that I am to resolve?" Maitreya then resolves this with his first verse, saying, "The fourfold assembly, joyful and reverent, gazes up at both you and me" — that is, "at me" because he wishes Maitreya himself to be the one questioning, and "at you" because they wish for Mañjuśrī himself to be the one answering. Mañjuśrī, on this basis, raises a second objection: "The assembly all alike has this doubt, yet it is not easily answered — we should wait for the Buddha to rise from samādhi, and only then resolve the doubt." Maitreya then resolves this with his second verse: if the doubt remains unresolved, the anxious, rhinoceros-like unease in their hearts will not be put at ease, and the matter should therefore be answered without delay; moreover, who can know when the Tathāgata will rise from his samādhi? — hence it says, "O son of the Buddha, answer at once, and resolve our doubt, bringing us joy." Mañjuśrī, on this basis, raises a third objection: "I dwell together with you, Worthy One, both still at the stage of learners; to fathom the Buddha's intent requires careful, joint deliberation, and it would not accord with reason for me alone to be made to answer." Maitreya then resolves this with his third verse: "I too, in my own humble mind, ponder and hesitate between the two pillars — is he about to preach the sublime Dharma, or is he about to bestow a prediction?" — hence it says, "The Buddha, seated on the platform of enlightenment, holds the sublime Dharma he has attained; is he now about to expound it, or is he about to bestow a prediction?" Mañjuśrī, on this basis, raises a fourth objection: "If it is as you say, then this is already itself a resolving of the doubt — what further need is there to trouble me to answer?" Maitreya then resolves this with his fourth verse: "How could a matter of such magnitude be judged merely by my own hesitant mind?" — hence it says, "He displays the buddha-lands in every direction; this is no small matter." With this, Mañjuśrī's concealed objections are exhausted, and his humility, too, comes to rest; the final verse then concludes the request for an answer. These four concealed objections were received by Guangzhai from his own teacher, and that teacher in turn received them from Master Zhao of the region north of the Yangtze; since this is an ingenious insight of former worthies, beyond what the text itself states, I make use of it here.

From "At that time Mañjuśrī said to Maitreya" down through the verses is called the prologue of the answer to the question, comprising both prose and verse. The prose text divides into four: first, from "said to Maitreya" onward, is called the answer by conjecture; second, from "Good sons, in the past I" onward, is called the brief answer from things formerly seen; third, from "Good sons, just as in the past" onward, is called the extended answer from things formerly seen; and fourth, from "now, seeing these signs no different from before" onward, is called the answer of clear judgment. Now, when what is below seeks to fathom what is above, one can only grope after images and reckon by conjecture, comparing the past with the present, and cannot arrive at a sudden, outright decision — hence Mañjuśrī begins from a faint impression, then cites what he saw in brief; and since what he saw in brief is not yet complete, he further cites what he saw at length, using the many instances to corroborate the one, and only then does he render his judgment. The answer by conjecture answers the question about this world above; the brief answer from things formerly seen answers the question about other worlds above; the extended answer from things formerly seen answers both the questions about this world and about other worlds together; and the answer of clear judgment renders judgment on both this world and other worlds together.

惟忖答為二：初、標章；次、正惟忖。惟者思惟也，忖者忖量，惟今如昔、忖昔如今，然文殊古佛豈應不知？迹示思惟也。光宅以初後兩句是法說，表因果廣略，中間三句是譬說，「欲說大法」是略開三顯一、略開近顯遠；演大法義，是廣開三顯一、廣開近顯遠。

The answer by conjecture divides into two: first, the heading of the section, and next, the conjecture proper. "Weigh" means to reflect, and "reckon" means to estimate: he reflects that the present is like the past, and reckons that the past is like the present. Yet how could Mañjuśrī, himself an ancient buddha, truly not know? His trace merely displays the act of reflecting. Guangzhai takes the first and last phrases as a direct statement of the Dharma, signifying cause and effect in their broad and abbreviated forms, and the three phrases in between as a statement by simile: "about to preach the great Dharma" is the abbreviated opening of the three to reveal the one, the abbreviated opening of the near to reveal the distant; and "expounding the meaning of the great Dharma" is the extended opening of the three to reveal the one, the extended opening of the near to reveal the distant.

「大法雨」者，譬得記作佛，昔因果定執不得作佛，是枯涸義；今皆作佛，是被雨潤義。吹䗍是改三乘之號，嚴鼓誡兵譬破無明。今明其法，說不用。何者？迹本兩門由籍各異，迹由籍起彌勒生疑，文殊為釋；本由籍未起，彌勒何所疑？文殊何所釋？若於此中，已是釋於開近顯遠之疑者，後地裂眾涌，彌勒何故更疑？更疑則浪疑、浪釋，釋後既虛，釋前亦謬，此大有所妨，故不用也。

"The great rain of Dharma" is a simile for receiving the prediction of buddhahood: formerly, cause and effect were fixedly grasped, and one was held not to be able to become a buddha — this is the sense of being parched and withered; now, all alike become buddhas — this is the sense of being moistened by the rain. "Blowing the conch" is the changing of the names of the three vehicles; "sounding the drum to array the troops" is a simile for the destroying of ignorance. I now clarify this teaching, though I do not adopt this particular explanation. Why is this? Because the trace gate and the origin gate each arise from a different occasion: the occasion of the trace gate has already arisen, and hence Maitreya's doubt arises and Mañjuśrī resolves it; but the occasion of the origin gate has not yet arisen — so what would Maitreya have to doubt, and what would Mañjuśrī have to resolve? If what is given here were already a resolving of the doubt over opening the near to reveal the distant, then, later, when the earth splits open and the bodhisattvas spring forth, why would Maitreya doubt yet again? A further doubt at that point would make both this doubt and this resolution empty gestures — if the later resolution turns out to be groundless, then the earlier resolution, too, is mistaken; this presents a very great difficulty, and so I do not adopt this explanation.

今明彌勒但問迹中此彼二土等瑞，文殊以惟忖答，答迹中事，不關〈壽量〉本中事也。「欲說大法」者，答說法瑞；「雨大法雨」，答雨花瑞；「吹大法䗍」，答大眾心喜瑞；「擊大法鼓」，答地動瑞；「演大法義」，答放光瑞。

I now clarify that Maitreya's question only concerns, within the trace gate, the signs of this world and the other worlds; and Mañjuśrī's conjectural answer answers a matter within the trace gate, and has nothing to do with the matter within the origin gate that belongs to the later chapter on the Buddha's lifespan. "About to preach the great Dharma" answers the sign of preaching the Dharma; "raining the great rain of Dharma" answers the sign of the rain of flowers; "blowing the great conch of Dharma" answers the sign of the great assembly's joy of heart; "sounding the great drum of Dharma" answers the sign of the earth's quaking; and "expounding the great meaning of Dharma" answers the sign of the emitting of light.

「欲說大法」者，惟昔諸佛說無量義後，則開權顯實，收無量歸一；忖於今佛既說法已，亦應開權顯，實會無量以歸一，一者即大法也。「雨大法雨」者，惟昔諸佛天雨四花之後，普入圓因住、行、向、地；忖於今佛雨花之後皆成佛因住、行、向、地，故言雨大法雨也。「吹大法䗍」者，惟昔四眾見瑞歡喜，得未曾有，障除機動，即改人教行理；忖今眾喜，亦應障除機動改人教行理，所改既深，故言吹大法䗍也。「擊大法鼓」者，惟昔地動已後即有六番破無明賊；忖於今佛地動已後亦應六番破無明惑，聲教極妙，故言擊大法鼓。「演大法義」者，惟昔諸佛放白毫光後說《法華》彼此道同；忖於今佛放光已後廣明五佛道同，既是佛道，故言演大法義。如是五句悉是惟昔判今，忖今類昔，會文附義唯少入定一瑞，而雨花、動地、放光等，皆由入定故爾，意則兼具，無勞疑也，闕此一條故稱略答耳。

"About to preach the great Dharma": reflecting that, in the past, the buddhas, after preaching the sūtra of immeasurable meanings, opened the provisional to reveal the real, gathering the immeasurable back into the one, he reckons that the present buddha, having likewise already preached the Dharma, must also open the provisional to reveal the real, gathering the immeasurable to return it to the one — and this "one" is precisely the great Dharma. "Raining the great rain of Dharma": reflecting that, in the past, after the buddhas rained down the four kinds of heavenly flowers, beings universally entered the causes of the perfect teaching — abiding, practice, dedication, and the ten grounds — he reckons that, after the present buddha rains down flowers, beings will all likewise become the causes of buddhahood — abiding, practice, dedication, and the grounds — hence it says "raining the great rain of Dharma." "Blowing the great conch of Dharma": reflecting that, in the past, the fourfold assembly, seeing the signs, rejoiced and gained what they had never had before, their hindrances removed and their capacities stirred, and thereupon changed their persons, their teaching, their practice, and their principle, he reckons that the present assembly's joy, too, should likewise remove their hindrances, stir their capacities, and change their persons, teaching, practice, and principle — and since the change wrought is so profound, it says "blowing the great conch of Dharma." "Sounding the great drum of Dharma": reflecting that, in the past, after the earth quaked, there followed six successive routings of the bandit of ignorance, he reckons that, after the present buddha's earth-quaking, there should likewise be six successive routings of the confusion of ignorance, the vocal teaching being utterly sublime — hence it says "sounding the great drum of Dharma." "Expounding the great meaning of Dharma": reflecting that, in the past, after the buddhas emitted the light from the white tuft, they preached the Lotus Sūtra, this world and that world sharing the same path, he reckons that, after the present buddha emits his light, he will broadly clarify that the paths of the five kinds of buddhas are the same — and since this is precisely the buddha-path, it says "expounding the great meaning of Dharma." These five phrases are, in every case, a matter of reflecting on the past to judge the present, reckoning the present after the pattern of the past; and matching the text to its meaning, only the single sign of entering samādhi is missing — yet the raining of flowers, the quaking of the earth, the emitting of light, and the rest, all come about precisely because of the entering of samādhi, so its sense is already comprehended within them, and no doubt need be raised over this. It is on account of this one item's omission that this is called "the brief answer."

今更別解，初一句總，後四句別。總者大法是也，別者雨、吹、擊、演開示悟入是也，如天非小大、非赤白，而雨赤白之花。

I now offer a further, separate explanation: the first phrase is general, and the last four phrases are particular. The general term is "the great Dharma" itself; the particular terms are "raining," "blowing," "sounding," and "expounding," corresponding to opening, showing, awakening, and entering — just as the heavens themselves are neither small nor great, neither red nor white, and yet rain down flowers that are red and white.

如第一義非開示悟入，見此理時即證開示悟入，譬如種子得雨萌開，今聞大法雨，潤法性種，破無明糠，開於十住佛知見也，譬如吹䗍知是改號。今之與先已得十住，今從十住聞法更改入十行，示佛知見也，譬如擊鼓知是誡兵。今之與先已在十行，今從十行聞法誡入迴向，悟佛知見也。演之言布橫闊豎深，乃是演義。今之與先已在十向，今從十迴向入於十地，入佛知見，窮源盡邊，深廣備足也。惟昔六瑞已後即開示悟入，忖今瑞後亦應如是，橫豎釋惟忖答竟。

Just as ultimate truth itself is neither opening, showing, awakening, nor entering, yet when this principle is seen, opening, showing, awakening, and entering are thereby realized — like a seed that, upon receiving rain, sprouts and opens: now, hearing the great rain of Dharma, moistening the seed of the dharma-nature and breaking through the husk of ignorance, is the opening of the knowledge and vision of the Buddha at the ten abidings; like hearing the conch blown and thereby knowing that the name is to be changed — what one has now, compared with before, is that having already attained the ten abidings, one now, from the ten abidings, hears the Dharma and is further changed to enter the ten practices — this is the showing of the knowledge and vision of the Buddha; like hearing the drum sounded and thereby knowing that the troops are being roused — what one has now, compared with before, is that already being at the ten practices, one now, from the ten practices, hears the Dharma and is roused to enter the ten dedications — this is the awakening to the knowledge and vision of the Buddha. The word "expound," meaning to spread horizontally in breadth and to extend vertically in depth, is precisely the expounding of the meaning: what one has now, compared with before, is that already being at the ten dedications, one now, from the ten dedications, enters the ten grounds, entering the knowledge and vision of the Buddha, exhausting the source and reaching every limit, complete in both depth and breadth. Reflecting that, in the past, after the six signs, opening, showing, awakening, and entering followed, he reckons that, after the present signs, the same should likewise follow. With this, the horizontal and vertical explanations of the answer by conjecture are complete.

The Extended Answer: The First Ancient Buddha 廣曾見答・初引一佛

Chinese Source

「如過去」下，三引廣曾見答，更分明於略，此廣答此土、他土之問，彌勒因光橫見東方以為問，文殊引昔豎見而為答，橫豎顯諸佛道同也。文為三：初、引一佛同，次、引二萬佛同，後、引最後一佛同。就前一佛又為三：一、明時節；二、標名；三、說法時節，如文。「有佛號日月標名同」者，通號與今佛可同，別名云何同？

Ru-Yi Translation

From "just as in the past" onward, the third answer, citing what was extensively formerly seen, makes matters clearer still than the brief answer; this broadly answers the questions about both this world and other worlds. Maitreya, by means of the light spreading horizontally to the east, poses his question, while Mañjuśrī cites what he saw vertically, in the past, as his answer — horizontal and vertical together revealing that the buddhas share the same path. The text divides into three: first, he cites the sameness of a single buddha; next, he cites the sameness of twenty thousand buddhas; and last, he cites the sameness of the final buddha in that lineage. Regarding the first, the single buddha, this again divides into three: first, clarifying the time and season; second, indicating his name; and third, the season of his preaching the Dharma, as the text shows. As for "there was a buddha named Candrasūryapradīpa, sharing the same designation," the general epithet may indeed be shared with the present buddha — but how could his particular, personal name likewise be the same?

此當以名別義同為釋。何者？日是慧，月是定，定慧是自行德，燈明是化他德，能仁能定，慧能自他。又日月燈是三智，今佛亦三智，隨緣稱別，義則不殊，故言名同。

This should be explained as: the names differ, but the meaning is the same. Why is this? The sun signifies wisdom, and the moon signifies concentration; concentration and wisdom are the virtues of practice for oneself, while the added epithet "lamp" signifies the virtue of transforming others; being capable in benevolence and capable in concentration, wisdom is able to serve both self and other. Moreover, sun, moon, and lamp are the three kinds of wisdom, and the present buddha likewise possesses these three kinds of wisdom; the names differ according to circumstance, but the meaning does not differ — hence it is said their names are the same.

「演說法」下，第三說法同者，昔佛先頓後漸，與今佛初頓後漸同也。「演說正法初、中、後善」者，即是頓教也。夫七善之語乃通大小，尋文是大乘七善，初、中、後善者，即是頓教序、正、流通，名為時節善；「其義深遠」，即是頓教了義之理，二乘不測其邊底，故言深遠，是名義善。「其語巧妙」即是頓教八音所吐，會理直說悅菩薩心，即頓教之文，名為語善。「純一無雜」不與二乘共，即是頓教獨一善。

From "expounding the Dharma" onward, the third topic, the sameness of preaching: the ancient buddha preached the sudden teaching first and the gradual teaching after, the same as the present buddha's preaching the sudden teaching first and the gradual teaching after. "Expounding the true Dharma, excellent in its beginning, middle, and end" refers precisely to the sudden teaching. Now, the term "seven excellences" applies broadly to both the greater and lesser vehicles, but examining the text here, these are the seven excellences of the great vehicle. "Excellent in beginning, middle, and end" corresponds to the prologue, the main teaching, and the transmission of the sudden teaching — this is called the excellence of season. "Its meaning profound and far-reaching" is the principle of the definitive meaning of the sudden teaching, whose limits the two vehicles cannot fathom — hence it is called "profound and far-reaching"; this is the excellence of meaning. "Its words skillful and sublime" refers to the eight kinds of sound uttered in the sudden teaching, according with principle and speaking directly, delighting the minds of the bodhisattvas — this is the very text of the sudden teaching, called the excellence of language. "Pure and unmixed," not shared with the two vehicles, is the sudden teaching's excellence of being unique and singular.

「具足」者，具明界內、界外滿字之法，即是頓教圓滿善。「清白」，無二邊瑕穢，即是頓教調柔善，師云行善。「梵行之相」者，梵即頓教無緣慈善。又初、中、後善，解者不同，今且依一途，若小乘以戒、定、慧為三善，大乘以初、中、後心為三善。

"Complete and sufficient" means fully clarifying the complete-letter teaching both within and beyond the three realms — this is the sudden teaching's excellence of perfect completeness. "Pure and unsullied," free of the taints of either extreme, is the sudden teaching's excellence of being gentle and pliant, which the master calls the excellence of conduct. "The mark of pure, Brahma-like conduct" means that this "Brahma" is the sudden teaching's excellence of unconditioned compassion. As for "excellent in beginning, middle, and end," interpreters differ; I follow, for the present, a single approach: in the lesser vehicle, precepts, concentration, and wisdom are taken as the three excellences, while in the great vehicle, the mind at the beginning, the middle, and the end are taken as the three excellences.

《金光明》云：「前心如來不可思議，中心如來種種莊嚴，後心如來不可破壞。」此亦三善之意也。文殊引古佛頓教七善，與今佛頓說七善同，亦與他土初頓說同。

The Golden Light Sūtra says: "The Tathāgata of the initial mind is inconceivable; the Tathāgata of the middle mind is adorned in every manner; and the Tathāgata of the final mind is indestructible." This, too, is the sense of the three excellences. Mañjuśrī cites the ancient buddha's seven excellences of the sudden teaching as the same as the present buddha's seven excellences of the sudden preaching, and likewise the same as that other world's initial sudden preaching.

所以用此為答者，酬上彌勒據光橫問他土佛云聖主師子，演說經法微妙第一，文殊豎引昔舉此為答，即是初佛說頓法同也。「為求聲聞人，說應四諦法」者，即是古佛次頓之後，開漸教法同也。上問若人遭苦為說《涅槃》，今引古佛亦開此漸，以答斯問也。「為求辟支佛，說應十二因緣法」，答上若人有福志求勝法之問也。「為諸菩薩，說應六波羅蜜」，答上佛子修種種行之問也。皆引古佛開漸教同也，廣引曾見佛，答他土之問也。「令得三菩提成一切種智」者，此明古佛開頓漸後，即顯實之說始終究竟，此答彌勒見他土佛般涅槃，涅槃後起塔之問也。若引古佛說法至六波羅蜜者，明今佛已與昔同。從「令得三菩提」去，明今佛當與昔同(云云)。

The reason for using this as the answer is to respond to Maitreya's earlier question, based on the light spreading horizontally, about that other world's buddha called "the sagely lord, the lion," expounding sūtra-teachings sublime and foremost; Mañjuśrī cites, vertically, what he saw in the past, offering this as the answer — this is the sameness of the initial buddha's preaching of the sudden teaching. "For those who seek to be śrāvakas, he preached the Dharma corresponding to the Four Noble Truths" is the sameness of the ancient buddha's opening of the gradual teaching, following the sudden teaching. The earlier question asked about a person who, meeting with suffering, is preached nirvāṇa; the citing of the ancient buddha now, likewise opening this gradual teaching, answers this very question. "For those who seek to be pratyekabuddhas, he preached the Dharma corresponding to the twelve links of dependent origination" answers the earlier question about a person who possesses merit and aspires to seek the supreme Dharma. "For the bodhisattvas, he preached the Dharma corresponding to the six pāramitās" answers the earlier question about the sons of the Buddha cultivating their various practices. All of these cite the ancient buddha's opening of the gradual teaching as the same, extensively citing what was formerly seen of that buddha to answer the question about other worlds. "Causing them to attain the unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening and to accomplish the wisdom of all modes" clarifies that, after the ancient buddha opened both the sudden and gradual teachings, he then revealed the real teaching to its very completion — this answers Maitreya's question about seeing that other world's buddha enter parinirvāṇa, and the raising of stūpas after his parinirvāṇa. Insofar as this cites the ancient buddha's preaching of the Dharma up through the six pāramitās, it clarifies that the present buddha has already been the same as the ancient one; from "causing them to attain the unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening" onward, it clarifies that the present buddha is about to be the same as the ancient one as well (and so on).

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Next he cites the twenty thousand buddhas, whose names and preaching are all the same. In citing the first, single buddha, he sets out fully both the sameness of the sudden and of the gradual preaching; in the middle, citing the twenty thousand buddhas, he sets out only the sameness of the sudden preaching, hence speaking of "excellent in beginning, middle, and end"; and in citing the last, single buddha, he sets out only the sameness of opening the gradual teaching. The reason for this is simply that each citation supplies what the others omit, and, pointing to what precedes, the rest may be understood. That he does not cite the buddha immediately preceding the twenty thousand is precisely because their names and preaching are all identical, and, considered in terms of the meaning, this simply proves the more convenient course. "Of the clan Bhāradvāja" is translated "swift and quick," also called "sharp of faculty," and also called "full of speech."

From "the last" onward, the third citation, of a single buddha, establishes sameness. The text divides into three: first, clarifying that what was formerly seen is the same as what has now already occurred; second, clarifying that what was formerly seen is the same as what is now, presently, occurring; and third, clarifying that what was formerly seen is the same as what will now, in future, occur. "Formerly" refers to what was experienced long ago, and "already" refers to what has passed into the past — both "formerly" and "already" equally denote things past and equally denote things experienced; here, what is more distant in time is taken as "formerly," and what is somewhat nearer is called "already"; the six signs and the like are taken as "now, presently," and the Buddha's rising from samādhi, still to come, is taken as "now, in future."

第一，從「其最後佛有八子」者，是曾與已同。昔佛八子今佛一子，數雖不等，並出同居之土，土有見思俱示有子，有子事同，一八赴緣別有所表，生一子總表一道清淨，生八子表八正道，數異義同，今取有子義同也。又昔佛子出家發大乘意，今佛子住小乘果，此云何同？昔化道已竟，顯本事彰，故言發大乘意；今未發迹猶言羅漢，至下文發本，即是菩薩，其義則同。

First, from "that last buddha had eight sons" — this is the sameness of the formerly-seen with the already-past. The ancient buddha had eight sons, while the present buddha has one son; though the numbers are unequal, both alike issue forth into a land where the afflictions of views and thought are still shared in common, a land where beings dwell together with the buddha's presence, and in each case a son, or sons, is shown, and this having-a-son is the same in both cases; the difference between one and eight responds to different circumstances and each signifies something distinct — the birth of a single son signifies, altogether, the purity of the one path, while the birth of eight sons signifies the eightfold noble path; the numbers differ but the meaning is the same, and here it is precisely this sameness in the meaning of having a son that is being taken. Moreover, the ancient buddha's sons went forth from home and gave rise to the great-vehicle resolve, while the present buddha's son abides in the fruit of the lesser vehicle — how can these be called the same? In the ancient buddha's case, his work of transformation was already complete, and his origin was thereupon made manifest — hence it speaks of his sons giving rise to the great-vehicle resolve; in the present case, the trace has not yet been opened, and Rāhula is still spoken of as an arhat, but by the time we reach the text below where the origin is revealed, he too will be a bodhisattva — and so their meaning is, in the end, the same.

From "At that time the Buddha Candrasūryapradīpa preached the great-vehicle sūtra" onward, the second section clarifies the sameness of the formerly-seen with the present. The ancient buddha's own six signs in his own world are, in every respect, the same as the present, in the same sequence as the text shows. As for the ancient buddha's six signs in other worlds, the text simply says, "the same as what is seen now" — from which one knows that the ancient buddha's six signs in other worlds are likewise the same as the present. In the ancient buddha's case, the specific prologue is clarified as having the two of the five parts — the manifestation of the signs, and the arising of doubt — explicitly given as the same, but lacking the remaining three parts — the gathering of the assembly, the posing of the question, and the answering of the question. This is simply Mañjuśrī's own skillful telling, a matter of skillful means concealing and revealing in turn.

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Chapter "Skillful Means"

○釋此有略、廣。略為二：先、略，次、料簡。「方」者法也，「便」者用也；法有方圓，用有差會；三權是矩、是方，一實是規、是圓。若智詣於矩，則善用偏法逗會眾生；若智詣於規，則善用圓法逗會眾生。譬如偏舉指以目偏處，是舉偏法以目智，宜用法以釋方，將用以釋便。若總舉指以目圓處，宜將祕以釋方，妙以釋便也。舉偏法釋方便，蓋隨眾生欲，非佛本懷，如經令離諸著出三界苦，是故如來殷勤稱歎方便。此義可釋他經，非今品意。又方便者，門也，門名能通，通於所通，方便權略皆是哢引，為真實作門，真實得顯，功由方便，從能顯得名，故以門釋方便，如經：「開方便門示真實相」，此義可釋他經，非今品意。又方者祕也，便者妙也，妙達於方即是真祕，點內衣裏無價之珠，與王頂上唯有一珠，無二無別；指客作人是長者子亦無二無別，如斯之言，是祕、是妙，如經：「唯我知是相，十方佛亦然」「止止不須說，我法妙難思。」故以祕釋方，以妙釋便，正是今品之意，故言方便品也。

Explaining this chapter has a brief treatment and a broad treatment. The brief treatment has two parts: first, the brief exegesis itself, and next, a critical sifting of other interpretations. "Fang" means dharma, and "bian" means function. Dharma has the square and the round; function has differentiation and convergence. The three expedient vehicles are the carpenter's square, the "square"; the one real vehicle is the compass, the "round." If wisdom arrives at the square, one skillfully uses the partial dharma to meet and accommodate sentient beings; if wisdom arrives at the compass, one skillfully uses the perfect dharma to meet and accommodate sentient beings. It is like pointing with a single finger to indicate a particular, partial place — this is citing the partial dharma to indicate wisdom, and it is fitting to use "dharma" to explain "fang," and to use "function" to explain "bian." If one instead raises the whole hand to indicate the perfect place, it is fitting to use "secret" to explain "fang," and "sublime" to explain "bian." To cite the partial dharma in explaining "skillful means" accords with sentient beings' own desires, and is not the Buddha's own original intent — as the sūtra says, causing beings to leave behind all their attachments and escape the suffering of the three realms, and it is for this reason that the Tathāgata earnestly and repeatedly praises "skillful means." This meaning may be used to explain other sūtras, but it is not the intent of the present chapter. Furthermore, "skillful means" is a gate: a gate is so called because it is able to lead through, leading through to that to which it leads. Skillful means, expedients, and provisional summaries are all a beckoning and a leading, made into a gate for the sake of the real and true; and it is by the merit of skillful means that the real and true is made manifest — taking its name from its ability to reveal, it is on this basis that "gate" explains "skillful means," as the sūtra says, "opening the gate of skillful means, he shows the mark of the real and true." This meaning, too, may be used to explain other sūtras, but it is not the intent of the present chapter. Furthermore, "fang" is the secret, and "bian" is the sublime: the sublime penetration of the "fang" is itself the true secret — like the priceless pearl sewn inside the garment, and the single pearl atop the king's own crown, without duality, without distinction; or like pointing to the hired laborer and saying that he is in fact the elder's own son, likewise without duality, without distinction — words such as these are themselves the secret, are themselves the sublime, as the sūtra says: "Only I know this mark, and the buddhas of the ten directions are likewise so," and "Stop, stop — there is no need to speak further; my Dharma is sublime and hard to conceive." Hence it is that "secret" explains "fang," and "sublime" explains "bian" — and this is precisely the intent of the present chapter, and hence it is called the chapter of "Skillful Means."

As for the critical sifting: the first interpretation given is "skillful means external to the essence" — the provisional teaching used to transform beings, spoken according to others' understanding. The second interpretation is likewise "skillful means external to the essence," but is the provisional teaching of both practice for oneself and transforming others, spoken according to both one's own and others' understanding. There are, further, those who explain that skillful means is simply the provisional, while the real is the carefully examined and true; or that skillful means is provisional cleverness, while the real is wisdom itself; or that skillful means is provisional borrowing, as in the borrowing of the three carts outside the gate; or that skillful means is provisional expediency, since it is fitting to preach the three vehicles; or that the provisional is a term of simile, as with a steelyard's weight, which, moved forward, makes the load heavier, and, drawn back, makes it lighter, and which, set in the middle, achieves balance — corresponding to the Buddha's wisdom in its illuminating examination and weighing. The first pair of wisdoms causes faith to arise; the second pair causes understanding to arise; the third pair causes the transforming of others; and the fourth pair is the resultant fruit. All of these pairs of wisdom, moreover, pass through three turnings: first, existence is taken as the conventional and emptiness as the ultimate; next, emptiness and existence together are taken as the conventional, and neither-emptiness-nor-existence as the ultimate; and next, emptiness and existence are taken as duality, and neither-emptiness-nor-existence as non-duality, with both duality and non-duality together taken as the conventional, and neither-duality-nor-non-duality as the ultimate — and the teaching's own wisdom is likewise so structured. Furthermore, since the Tathāgata always preaches the Dharma by relying on the two truths, the two truths accordingly have three approaches. And though the Buddha's teachings are many, they do not go beyond these three approaches. Furthermore, because beings are led gradually: the ordinary worldling reckons mind and form to be real, though this is in fact not real at all, and only the emptiness of the nature of dharmas is truly real; the ordinary worldling therefore casts aside existence and takes hold of emptiness, and so it is said that both emptiness and existence are the conventional, and neither-emptiness-nor-existence is the real. Or, again, one who has cast aside the two extremes may become fixed, in turn, upon the middle way, and so a third approach — being far removed from both extremes and yet not attached even to the middle way — is what is truly real. This scheme is meant for the five vehicles of persons: the first leads the ordinary worldling to give rise to faith and escape existence; the next leads the two vehicles to enter the middle; and the next leads the bodhisattva to cast aside both the middle and the partial together. There are also those who take this in terms of one still training in the middle way, holding that the three kinds of provisional designation are the conventional, and the emptiness of the three provisional designations is the real — but this attains only the first of these senses. The next holds that it is not the three provisional designations, but rather that emptiness and existence together are the conventional, and neither-emptiness-nor-existence is the real (and so on).

今詳彼釋，乃是傍五時顯己意，却漸次梯隥之非耳，可釋他經，非今品意。經云：「咸令眾生皆得覩見」，何時前後開悟不同？又云：「正直捨方便」，那用漸次會於圓妙？又初引生信解化果等，何關今經悟入之意耶？如天親列十七名，第十三名大巧方便。又《大乘方便經》明方便十種，第九名善巧，移二乘令入大乘方便波羅蜜，當知今品乃是如來方便攝一切法，如空包色、若海納流，豈可以諸師一枝、一派釋法界之大都耶？

Examining these interpretations closely, I find them to be nothing more than leaning upon the five periods to display their own private views, and merely stepping back into the error of a gradual, staircase-like scheme; they may serve to explain other sūtras, but they are not the intent of the present chapter. The sūtra says, "causing all sentient beings alike to attain sight and vision" — how, then, could there be an earlier and a later, an unequal awakening at different times? And again it says, "directly and straightforwardly casting aside skillful means" — how, then, could one make use of a gradual, step-by-step scheme to merge into the perfect and sublime? Moreover, the earlier scheme of leading beings to faith, to understanding, to being transformed, to the resultant fruit, and the rest — what has this to do with the present sūtra's own intent of awakening and entering? Just as Vasubandhu, in listing seventeen names for skillful means, gives as the thirteenth "great and skillful means"; and the Mahāyāna Sūtra on Skillful Means sets out ten kinds of skillful means, the ninth of which is named "skillful cleverness," by which the two vehicles are shifted so as to enter the great vehicle's pāramitā of skillful means — one should therefore understand that the present chapter is precisely the Tathāgata's own skillful means, gathering in the whole of all dharmas, like empty space containing all form, like the sea receiving every stream — how, then, could one possibly use a single branch or a single tributary offered by these various masters to explain the great totality of the dharma-realm?

"Only this one matter is real; the other two are not true" — there is but a single ultimate path, so how could there be many ultimate paths? All the masters set forth above are, in being refuted, brought into the real — how could they still hold on to their own nests and lairs?

If all dharmas are both provisional and real, what again would this thesis fail to refute? Since everything has both a provisional and a real aspect, how could one, taking one's own single approach to be sole and correct, deny every other differing interpretation? In each and every dharma there is both the provisional and the real; one cannot insist exclusively on the provisional or exclusively on the real.

If all dharmas are neither provisional nor real, what again would this thesis fail to refute? Why, then, should one still contend and forcibly establish one's own position? Merely to list the bare names is already like this; how much more so when one surveys it from afar, contemplates it in its mysterious depth, and takes in its vast and lofty brilliance — how much more, then, when one comes to discuss its actual purport?

今就有權有實句更開十法，就十法中，為八番解釋：一、列十名，二、生起，三、解釋，四、引證，五、結十為三種權實，六、分別三種權實照三種二諦，七、約諸經判權實，八、約本迹判權實。

Now, taking up the thesis that dharmas have both a provisional and a real aspect, I open this out further into ten topics, and within these ten topics I offer an eightfold explanation: first, listing the ten names; second, their arising in sequence; third, explaining them; fourth, citing proof texts; fifth, uniting the ten into three kinds of the provisional-and-real; sixth, distinguishing how the three kinds of the provisional-and-real illumine the three kinds of the two truths; seventh, judging the provisional and the real according to the various sūtras; and eighth, judging the provisional and the real in terms of trace and origin.

一、列名者，謂事理、理教、教行、縛脫、因果、體用、漸頓、開合、通別、悉檀，即是十種名也。

First, listing the names: fact-and-principle, principle-and-teaching, teaching-and-practice, bondage-and-liberation, cause-and-effect, essence-and-function, gradual-and-sudden, opening-and-uniting, shared-and-distinct, and the siddhāntas — these are the ten names.

二、生起者，從無住本立一切法。無住者，理也；一切法者，事也。理事故有教，由教故有行，由行故有縛脫，由脫故成因果，由果故體顯能用，故有漸頓之化，由開漸頓故有於開合，開合故有通別之益，分別兩益故有四悉檀，是為十章次第(云云)。

Second, their arising in sequence: all dharmas are established from the rootless root. The rootless itself is principle; all dharmas are fact. Because there is principle and fact, there is teaching; because of teaching, there is practice; because of practice, there is bondage and liberation; because of liberation, cause and effect are accomplished; because of effect, essence is manifested and function made able; hence there is the transforming activity of the gradual and the sudden; because the gradual and the sudden are opened, there comes to be opening and uniting; because of opening and uniting, there is the benefit of the shared and the distinct; and because these two kinds of benefit are distinguished, there are the four siddhāntas. This is the sequential order of the ten topics, and so on.

Explaining the Ten: Principle, Teaching, Practice, Bondage 釋十義：理事、教行、縛脫、因果

Chinese Source

三、解釋者，理是真如，真如本淨，有佛無佛常不變易故，名理為實；事是心意識等，起淨、不淨業，改動不定故，名事為權。若非理無以立事，非事不能顯理，事有顯理之功，是故殷勤稱歎方便。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, explaining them: principle is suchness (tathatā); suchness is originally pure, and whether or not a buddha appears in the world, it is forever unchanging — hence principle is named the real. Fact is mind, intellect, and consciousness and the like, giving rise to pure and impure karma, shifting and never fixed — hence fact is named the provisional. Without principle there would be no basis on which to establish fact, yet without fact, principle could not be manifested; fact has the merit of manifesting principle, and it is for this reason that the sūtra earnestly praises skillful means.

理教者，總前理事皆名為理，例如真俗俱稱為諦，諸佛體之而得成聖，聖者正實也，欲以己法下被眾生，因理而設教，教即權也，非教無以顯理，顯理由教，是故如來稱歎方便(云云)。

As for principle-and-teaching: taking the preceding principle and fact together, both are here named principle — just as, for example, the ultimate and the conventional are together called truths. The buddhas embody this principle and thereby achieve sagehood; sagehood is precisely the real and true. Wishing to bestow their own realized dharma upon sentient beings below them, they establish teaching by means of principle, and this teaching is precisely the provisional. Without teaching there would be no way to manifest principle, yet the manifesting of principle depends upon teaching — and it is for this reason that the Tathāgata praises skillful means, and so on.

教行者，依教求理則生正行，行有進趣深淺之殊，故行名權也；教無進趣深淺之異，故教名實也。非教無以立行，非行無以會教，會教由行，是故如來稱歎方便(云云)。

As for teaching-and-practice: relying on teaching to seek principle gives rise to right practice; practice has differences of shallow and deep advancement, and so practice is named the provisional, while teaching has no such differences of shallow and deep advancement, and so teaching is named the real. Without teaching there would be no way to establish practice, yet without practice there is no way to bring teaching to fruition — the fruition of teaching depends upon practice, and it is for this reason that the Tathāgata praises skillful means, and so on.

縛脫者，為行違理則縛，縛是虛妄故稱權；為行順理則生解，解冥於理故稱實。非縛無由求脫，得脫由縛，如因屍渡海，屍有濟岸之力，故稱歎方便。

As for bondage-and-liberation: when practice runs contrary to principle, this is bondage, and since bondage is false and empty, it is called the provisional; when practice accords with principle, understanding is thereby produced, and since this understanding merges with principle, it is called the real. Without bondage there would be no occasion to seek liberation, yet liberation is attained by means of bondage — just as one crosses the sea by clinging to a corpse, and the corpse itself has the power to bring one to the farther shore. Hence the sūtra praises skillful means.

因果者，因有進趣暫用故名權，果有剋終永證故為實。無果，因無所望；無因，果不自顯。

As for cause-and-effect: cause has the temporary function of advancing forward, and hence is named the provisional; effect has final, everlasting attainment, and hence is the real. Without effect, cause would have nothing to look toward; without cause, effect could not manifest of itself.

Explaining the Ten: From Cause-and-Effect to the Four Siddhāntas 釋十義：體用、漸頓、開合、通別、悉檀

Chinese Source

是以二觀為方便道，斷惑成因得入中道解脫之果，若非二觀豈契中道？果由因剋，故稱歎方便。

Ru-Yi Translation

This is why the two contemplations serve as the expedient path: by cutting off delusion and accomplishing the cause, one is able to enter the fruit of liberation in the middle way — for without the two contemplations, how could one ever accord with the middle way? The fruit is accomplished by the cause, and hence the sūtra praises skillful means.

體用者，前方便為因，正觀入住為果，住出為體用，體即實相無有分別，用即立一切法差降不同，如大地一生種種芽，非地無以生，非生無以顯，尋流得源推用識體，用有顯體之功，故稱歎方便。

As for essence-and-function: the preceding expedients serve as cause, and correct contemplation entering and abiding serves as effect; abiding and emerging constitute essence-and-function. Essence is precisely the mark of reality, without any differentiation; function is precisely the establishing of all dharmas in their differing gradations. It is like the great earth giving rise to shoots of every kind: without the earth there would be no way for them to grow, yet without their growing there would be no way for the earth to be revealed. By tracing the stream one arrives at the source, and by inferring from function one comes to know essence — function has the merit of revealing essence, and hence the sūtra praises skillful means.

漸頓者，修因證果從體起用，俱有漸頓，今明起用，用漸為權、用頓為實，若非漸引無由入頓，從漸得實，故稱歎方便。

As for gradual-and-sudden: cultivating the cause and realizing the effect, arising in function from essence, each involves both the gradual and the sudden. Here I am explaining the arising of function: function exercised gradually is the provisional, function exercised suddenly is the real. Without gradual guidance there would be no way to enter the sudden, and it is from the gradual that the real is attained — hence the sūtra praises skillful means.

開合者，從頓開漸，漸自不合亦不合頓，故名為權；漸令究竟還合於頓，故名為實。由開故合，開有合力從開受名，故稱歎方便。

As for opening-and-uniting: opening the gradual out from the sudden, the gradual, of itself, neither unites with itself nor unites with the sudden, and hence it is named the provisional; bringing the gradual to its ultimate conclusion, one returns it to union with the sudden, and hence this is named the real. It is because of the opening that there can be a uniting — the opening carries within it the power of uniting, and takes its name from the opening. Hence the sūtra praises skillful means.

通別益者，通則半字無常之益，別即滿字常住之益。然常益道長，喜生退沒故，以化城接引，生安隱想，然後息化引至寶所，若無半益不得會常，半有顯滿之功，故稱歎方便。

As for the benefit of the shared and the distinct: the shared teaching gives the benefit of the "half-letter" teaching of impermanence, while the distinct teaching gives the benefit of the "full-letter" teaching of the eternal. But since the path to the benefit of the eternal is long, and beings are prone to grow weary and regress, the Buddha uses the phantom city to receive and guide them, producing in them a sense of security, and only afterward ceases this transformative device and leads them on to the treasure-place. Without the half-letter benefit, one could not come to meet the eternal; the half-letter benefit carries within it the merit of manifesting the full. Hence the sūtra praises skillful means.

四悉檀者，三是世間是故為權，第一義是出世是故為實。非世不得出世，由三悉檀得第一義，是故如來稱歎方便。當用四句釋十番權實，三番是他經意，一番是此品意(云云)。

As for the four siddhāntas: the first three pertain to the mundane, and hence are the provisional; the siddhānta of ultimate meaning pertains to what transcends the mundane, and hence is the real. Without the mundane, one could not attain what transcends the mundane; it is by means of the three mundane siddhāntas that the siddhānta of ultimate meaning is attained, and it is for this reason that the Tathāgata praises skillful means. Each of these ten topics of the provisional-and-real should now be explained by means of the fourfold thesis above; three of these explanations belong to the intent of other sūtras, while one belongs to the intent of this chapter, and so on.

And why is this so? "The Tathāgata's skillful means and knowledge and vision are all already complete" is the discussion of the provisional-and-real in terms of opening-and-uniting. "To state the essential matter, the Buddha has accomplished all of this" is the accomplishment of the first three siddhāntas, while "stop, stop — there is no need to speak further" is the siddhānta of ultimate meaning — together these form the discussion of the provisional-and-real in terms of the four siddhāntas. From "the Buddha has accomplished the foremost and rarest" down to "the true mark of the dharmas" is principle, while "what is called the suchness of characteristics of the dharmas" is fact — this is the discussion of the provisional-and-real in terms of fact-and-principle.

Now, the Saddharmapuṇḍarīka-upadeśa, in explaining the buddhas' profound wisdom, treats it as proof of profundity, and finds profundity to be fivefold: the profundity of meaning, the profundity of the real substance, the profundity of inner realization, the profundity of what is relied upon, and the unsurpassed profundity. The unsurpassed profundity refers to the realization of great awakening; the naming of it as the gate of wisdom refers to the profundity of the meaning of the āgama teaching — this accords in sense with the provisional-and-real of principle-and-teaching. The Upadeśa explains that the Buddha has drawn near a hundred thousand buddhas as the profundity of cultivated practice, and that his vigorous diligence and universally heard renown are the profundity of increased merit — this accords in sense with the provisional-and-real of teaching-and-practice.

《論》解成就甚深未曾有法，為微妙事甚深；意趣難解等，為無上甚深、入甚深，此與體用權實意同。《論》解吾從成佛已來，為說如來功德成就法，此與因果權實意同。《論》解無數方便者即是教化成就、說法成就，此與漸頓、權實意同。《論》解如來方便知見，乃至深入無際等，是自身成就不可思議境，勝餘一切菩薩，此是明利也。《論》解能種種分別，悅可眾心等，是言語成就，此是益也，與利益權實意同。

The Upadeśa explains "accomplishing what is profound and unprecedented" as the profundity of the sublime matter, and "whose purport is hard to understand" and the like as the unsurpassed profundity and the profundity of entry — this accords in sense with the provisional-and-real of essence-and-function. The Upadeśa explains "since I attained buddhahood" as speaking of the dharma of the Tathāgata's accomplished merit — this accords in sense with the provisional-and-real of cause-and-effect. The Upadeśa explains "countless skillful means" as the accomplishment of transformative teaching and the accomplishment of preaching the Dharma — this accords in sense with the provisional-and-real of gradual-and-sudden. The Upadeśa explains "the Tathāgata's skillful knowledge and vision" and, further, "penetrating deeply without limit" and the like, as the Buddha's own accomplishment of an inconceivable state surpassing all other bodhisattvas — this makes clear the category of benefit-to-self. The Upadeśa explains "able to make various discriminations" and "gladdening the minds of the assembly" and the like as the accomplishment of speech — this is the category of benefit-to-others, according in sense with the provisional-and-real of benefit.

《論》解取要言之止不須說等，為可化眾生成就，此與四悉檀分別可化、不可化意同。《論》解唯佛與佛乃能究盡，為無量福成就諸佛能知，謂如來法身之體不變故覺，能自證成就，能隨順眾生，說一切諸法相等，此與理事權實意同。彼論解佛經，今疏冥符二聖，可謂與修多羅、優波提舍皆合也。

The Upadeśa explains "to state the essential matter... stop, there is no need to speak further" and the like as the accomplishment of the class of sentient beings capable of being transformed — this accords in sense with the four siddhāntas' distinguishing of those who can and cannot be transformed. The Upadeśa explains "only a buddha together with a buddha is able to exhaust it" as the accomplishment, through immeasurable merit, of what only the buddhas are able to know — meaning that, since the substance of the Tathāgata's dharma-body is unchanging, it is thereby awakened, able to realize itself in accomplishment, and able to accord with sentient beings in expounding the characteristics of all dharmas — this accords in sense with the provisional-and-real of fact-and-principle. That treatise's explanation of the Buddha's sūtra, and this commentary here, silently concur, sage with sage — one might say that they are in full accord, both with the sūtra itself and with its upadeśa.

Uniting the Ten into Three Kinds of the Provisional-and-Real 五、結十為三種權實

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, uniting the ten into three kinds of the provisional-and-real: these ten topics run through all Four Teachings, making forty instances of the provisional-and-real in total. Within the Tripiṭaka teaching, for instance, the ten dharmas one realizes for oneself are named the provisional-and-real of self-cultivation; the ten dharmas one expounds for the benefit of others are named the provisional-and-real of transforming others. The ten of transforming others are together united as the provisional, and the ten of self-cultivation are together united as the real — together these are named the provisional-and-real of self-and-other. The ten dharmas of each of the remaining three teachings are likewise bundled into these three kinds of the provisional-and-real.

又當教各以事理、教行、縛脫、因果四種，為自行權實；各以理教、開合二種，是化他權實；各以體用、漸頓、通別、悉檀四種，為自他權實。其名雖同，其義各異也。別結者，三教若通、若別，當分皆是化他權實，隨他意語故；圓教若通、若別，當分皆是自行權實，隨自意語故。化他之三皆名為權，自行皆名為實。

Furthermore, within each teaching, the four topics of fact-and-principle, teaching-and-practice, bondage-and-liberation, and cause-and-effect serve as the provisional-and-real of self-cultivation; the two topics of principle-and-teaching and opening-and-uniting serve as the provisional-and-real of transforming others; and the four topics of essence-and-function, gradual-and-sudden, shared-and-distinct, and the siddhāntas serve as the provisional-and-real of self-and-other. Though the names are the same across the teachings, their meanings differ in each case. To conclude this separately: whether the Tripiṭaka, the shared, or the distinct teaching, each, taken on its own terms, belongs entirely to the provisional-and-real of transforming others, since each speaks according to the intent of others; the perfect teaching, whether shared or distinct in aspect, belongs entirely, on its own terms, to the provisional-and-real of self-cultivation, since it speaks according to its own intent. The three teachings of transforming others are all together named the provisional, and self-cultivation alone is named the real.

次、結成四句，隨他意語者，即一切法權；隨自意語者，即一切法實；雙取，即一切法亦權亦實；雙非，即一切法非權非實。

Next, uniting these into the fourfold thesis: speaking according to the intent of others is precisely all dharmas as the provisional; speaking according to one's own intent is precisely all dharmas as the real; taking both together is precisely all dharmas as both provisional and real; and negating both together is precisely all dharmas as neither provisional nor real.

Chinese Source

次、結成三番釋品者，若自行自意者，此文稱道場所得法，《大經》云：「修道得故」，《攝大乘》稱如理如量智，皆是圓教自行權實，隨自意語。佛雖能於此不可說法，方便能說，而眾生不堪，若發軫單說此法取眾生者，即不能得也，故言不可說、不可說也。

Next, uniting these into three explanations of the chapter title: as for self-cultivation and one's own intent — this passage speaks of the dharma attained at the site of enlightenment; the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra calls it "what is attained through the cultivation of the path," and the Mahāyānasaṃgraha calls it the wisdom that accords with principle and the wisdom that accords with quantity — all of these are the provisional-and-real of self-cultivation in the perfect teaching, spoken according to one's own intent. Though the Buddha is able, with regard to this inexpressible dharma, to speak of it by skillful means, sentient beings are not able to bear it; were he, from the very outset, to expound this dharma alone in order to draw beings in, they simply would not be able to attain it — hence it is said, "inexpressible, inexpressible."

復置此事，單用三藏權實取眾生者，大機利鈍者，密得、顯不得；小機利鈍者，但保於證，取亦不得，蓋三藏意也。

Setting this matter aside again: when the provisional-and-real of the Tripiṭaka teaching alone is used to draw in sentient beings, those of great capacity, whether sharp or dull, attain it secretly but not openly; those of lesser capacity, whether sharp or dull, merely hold fast to their own attainment, and even this drawing-in does not fully succeed — this is the intent of the Tripiṭaka teaching.

復置是事，合用四種權實，共取眾生者，大機利鈍者，曲直俱得；小機利鈍者，保證俱不得，蓋《方等》意也。

Setting this matter aside once more: when the four kinds of the provisional-and-real are used together to draw in sentient beings, those of great capacity, whether sharp or dull, attain it whether directly or indirectly; those of lesser capacity, whether sharp or dull, neither attain it nor even hold fast to their own attainment — this is the intent of the Vaipulya teachings.

復置是事，捨三藏權實，用三種權實，共取眾生者，大機利鈍俱得；小機利鈍保證俱不得，蓋《般若》意也。

Setting this matter aside yet again: when the provisional-and-real of the Tripiṭaka is set aside and the remaining three kinds of the provisional-and-real are used together to draw in sentient beings, those of great capacity, whether sharp or dull, all attain it; those of lesser capacity, whether sharp or dull, neither attain it nor hold fast to their own attainment — this is the intent of the Prajñāpāramitā teachings.

Setting this matter aside one final time: when the three kinds of the provisional-and-real are set aside and the provisional-and-real of self-cultivation in the perfect teaching alone is used to draw in sentient beings, those of great and lesser capacity, sharp or dull alike, all attain it — this is the intent of the Lotus Sūtra. The Tathāgata's wisdom leaves nothing unpenetrated; it clearly discerns, in each season, what is fitting to use and what is not, and hence, in explaining the chapter title, it is said: "skillful" refers to the various methods, and "means" refers to their skillful application; by skillfully applying these methods, sentient beings are drawn in and attain the path — and it is for this reason that the sūtra earnestly praises skillful means.

復次如來自證權實俱不可說，愍念眾生說自證之權為門，於物非宜，眾生不能得入，故自證亦不可說。說別權實為門，利者得入，鈍者不入，於物非宜，別權實亦不可說。說三藏權實為門，利者密入，鈍者亦不入，於物非宜，亦不可說。說三種化他權實為門，利者得入、鈍亦不入，於物非宜，亦不可說。說二種化他權實為門，於利者得入，鈍亦不入，亦不可說，於物非宜。

Furthermore, both the provisional and the real of the Tathāgata's own self-realization are alike inexpressible. Out of compassionate regard for sentient beings, were he to speak of the provisional aspect of his own realization as a gate, this would not suit their circumstances, and sentient beings would be unable to enter through it — hence self-realization, too, is inexpressible. Were he to speak of the provisional-and-real of the distinct teaching as a gate, those of sharp faculties would enter but those of dull faculties would not, which likewise does not suit their circumstances, and so the provisional-and-real of the distinct teaching, too, is inexpressible. Were he to speak of the provisional-and-real of the Tripiṭaka teaching as a gate, those of sharp faculties would enter secretly while those of dull faculties would still not enter, which likewise does not suit their circumstances, and so this too is inexpressible. Were he to speak of the three kinds of the provisional-and-real of transforming others as a gate, those of sharp faculties would enter while those of dull faculties would still not enter, which likewise does not suit their circumstances, and so this too is inexpressible. Were he to speak of the two kinds of the provisional-and-real of transforming others as a gate, those of sharp faculties would enter while those of dull faculties would still not enter — this too is inexpressible, and does not suit their circumstances.

捨三種化他權實，但說自行之權，於利者鈍者俱得入，從始至終以方便為門，是故如來稱歎方便。釋品云，方便為入實之門，即此意也。前一番，明如來能知方便、能用方便，此一番明行者能隨順方便(云云)。

Setting aside these three kinds of the provisional-and-real of transforming others, and speaking only of the provisional aspect of self-cultivation, both those of sharp and of dull faculties are able to enter through it; from beginning to end, skillful means itself serves as the gate — and it is for this reason that the Tathāgata praises skillful means. In explaining the chapter title, it is said that skillful means is the gate that leads into the real — this is precisely that meaning. The preceding explanation made clear that the Tathāgata is able to know skillful means and able to employ skillful means; this present explanation makes clear that the practitioner is able to follow along in accord with skillful means, and so on.

復次，如來自證修道所得，於一切方便即是真實，而此真實不可得說；雖能說之，眾生不能即實，以方便力，帶不即說一即，利者能即、鈍不能即；又純說一不即，利者密即、鈍者不即；又帶三不即說一即，利者能即、鈍者不即；又帶二不即說一即，利者能即、鈍者不即；又廢三不即，純說一切即，利鈍者俱能即，於方便得見真實。

Furthermore, what the Tathāgata realizes for himself through the cultivation of the path is, within every skillful means, precisely the real and true; yet this real and true cannot be spoken. Though he is able to speak of it, sentient beings are not able to grasp it as the real directly. By the power of skillful means: speaking of the one immediacy alongside three non-immediacies, those of sharp faculties are able to grasp the immediacy while those of dull faculties are not; speaking purely of the one non-immediacy, those of sharp faculties grasp it secretly while those of dull faculties do not; speaking of the one immediacy alongside three non-immediacies in another arrangement, those of sharp faculties are able to grasp it while those of dull faculties are not; speaking of the one immediacy alongside two non-immediacies, those of sharp faculties are able to grasp it while those of dull faculties are not; and finally, setting aside the three expedient vehicles as non-immediacy and speaking purely of the immediacy of all things, both those of sharp and of dull faculties alike are able to grasp it, and through skillful means come to behold the real and true.

上兩意用方便從方便，此一意即方便即真實，真實即圓因，圓因即自行之方便，如此自行方便今始證入。上釋品云，方便者即是真實，從自行方便得名，故言方便品。

In the two explanations above, skillful means is used by proceeding through skillful means; in this present explanation, skillful means is itself precisely the real and true — the real and true is precisely the perfect cause, and the perfect cause is precisely the skillful means of self-cultivation. It is this skillful means of self-cultivation that is only now, in this chapter, truly realized and entered. As explained above regarding the chapter title: skillful means is itself precisely the real and true, and the chapter takes its name from this skillful means of self-cultivation — hence it is called the chapter of "Skillful Means."

As for the Lotus Sūtra — in terms of teaching, the half-letter is set aside for the full-letter alone; in terms of period, it is purely the ghee; in terms of dharma, there is only self-cultivation. According to the present text, this is the opening of the provisional to reveal the real: "this is truly my son, truly born of me, and I am truly his father," entrusting him with the family estate and predicting his attainment of buddhahood. What the earlier teachings did not disclose is here disclosed entirely; "directly setting aside skillful means, he preaches only the unsurpassed path" — hence this is the provisional aspect of self-cultivation, and hence it is called the chapter of "Skillful Means." All that remains is either the twin wisdom of self-and-other, or the twin wisdom of transforming others.

The Tripiṭaka teaching, addressing itself to the two vehicles, expounds a single kind of transforming others, without intending bodhisattvas, and hence has no self-cultivation. The Vaipulya teachings address themselves fully to both the lesser and the greater: to the two vehicles they expound two kinds of transforming others, and to bodhisattvas they expound one part self-cultivation and one part transforming others. The Prajñāpāramitā likewise addresses all three: it expounds one part self-cultivation and two parts transforming others, expounding to the two vehicles one part transforming others and to bodhisattvas one part self-cultivation and one part transforming others. The Lotus Sūtra addresses universally those whose capacity has ripened, and expounds only the single self-cultivation, without discussing transforming others any further. The text says, "the bodhisattvas, on hearing this Dharma, have had the net of their doubt entirely removed; the twelve hundred arhats shall likewise all become buddhas." All sentient beings alike enter the skillful means of self-cultivation — hence it is called the chapter of "Skillful Means," and so on.

"Real" refers to the wisdom of the buddhas; since it is not any of the three kinds of the provisional-and-real of transforming others, it is called "the buddhas," and since it manifests the real aspect of self-cultivation, it is called "wisdom" — the substance of this wisdom is precisely the single mind endowed with the three kinds of cognition. "Hard to understand and hard to enter" is the language of praising the provisional: understood without deliberation, its great function has no fixed direction, and the seven kinds of expedient means are unable to fathom it; the ten abidings mark the beginning of understanding, and the ten grounds mark entry into it — citing the beginning and the end, the middle stages are difficult to indicate, though their difficulty of realization may thereby be inferred. That the text singles out the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas as those unable to know it is simply because their attachment is heaviest, and so they are singled out for refutation.

In the Vaipulya period, the two vehicles were rebuked and reproached for still clinging to their thatched hermitage, and hence the text says they "do not know." In the Prajñāpāramitā, the two vehicles, relaying the teaching to bodhisattvas with no thought of seeking so much as a single meal for themselves, and hence the text says they "do not know." Now, in this present chapter, their great capacity is opened forth, the light is emitted and the earth quakes, and the buddhas of here and there, present and past, are shown to share the same path — yet because doubt still lingers in their hearts, the text still says they "do not know." Bodhisattvas of sharp faculties are able to know it stage by stage, but those of dull faculties, being on a par with the two vehicles, likewise do not know it.

Granting his point for a moment: this would be the shallowest of abilities, permanently unable to recognize its own object, and so on. I now explain that there exists a gate of expedient wisdom by which one enters the Buddha's wisdom, just as the Yingluo Sūtra says, "the two contemplations serve as the expedient path, by which one enters the middle way, the truth of ultimate meaning" — and likewise, each of the three non-perfect teachings has its own set of four gates serving as the expedient means by which one enters the middle way. Moreover, expedient wisdom itself can serve as a gate by which one enters expedient cognition — this is the entry into realization proper to each of the three teachings, through their respective four gates. There is also the Buddha's own wisdom serving as a gate by which one enters the Buddha's wisdom — as explained above, this is called the perfect cause, named the chapter of "Skillful Means"; this is precisely the gate of the contemplative wisdom of self-cultivation, and it is precisely the gate to wisdom praised in the present sūtra, of which the perfect teaching's four gates form but one instance. There is, further, the real itself serving as a gate by which one enters expedient cognition, doubly illuminating the two truths — this too is of the same meaning. An explanation such as this is rich and expansive — how could it compare with Guangzhai's single, narrow reading? If, following the Upadeśa, one takes the āgama teaching as the gate, this too must be opened out to encompass all the teachings, as may be understood by the same standard of contemplation, and so on.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "and why is this so" onward, Guangzhai calls this "the chapter praising Śākyamuni." Examining the sense of the text, however, I take this to be the twofold explanation of the twin wisdoms of all buddhas. From "the Buddha has drawn near" down to "practiced the path in full" is the explanation of the buddhas' real wisdom: precisely because, outwardly, they encountered many buddhas and received the essentials from them, their real wisdom is profound; and precisely because, inwardly, their practice was pure and full, practicing the path in its entirety, their real wisdom is immeasurable. "Immeasurable" thus explains its horizontal vastness, and "profound" explains its vertical loftiness.

"Vigorous, diligent, their renown heard everywhere" explains the buddhas' expedient wisdom. As for "the gate to their wisdom, hard to understand and hard to enter" — it is precisely through vigorous diligence that they are able to enter this gate so hard to enter, and once they have entered this gate, their beneficent influence spreads without bound, and all beings revere their supreme virtue, and hence their renown is heard everywhere. One may also divide this into separate clauses: "vigorous and diligent" refers to being able to enter the dharma-gate, explaining the profundity of expedient wisdom, while "renown heard everywhere" explains the vastness of expedient wisdom. Though, on inspection, the text on the provisional lacks any explicit words of "profound" and "vast," this meaning nonetheless holds by analogy with real wisdom, and so on.

Being called "according with what is fitting," because it accords appropriately with capacity, and being called "hard to understand," because it is not known by the seven kinds of expedient means — this concludes the explanation of expedient wisdom. When one accords with sentiment, principle is thereby obscured, and hence it is called "hard to understand"; when the meaning is fully drawn out, its intent becomes manifest, and hence it is called "easy to know." The Mahāyānasaṃgraha says, "sūtras of fully drawn-out meaning are to be judged by their explicit meaning based on the text, while sūtras of meaning not fully drawn out are to be judged in their text based on the meaning" — this is precisely that same principle. There is also an interpretation that takes "accomplished what is profound and unprecedented" as concluding the provisional-and-real of self-cultivation, and "what is preached according to what is fitting, whose purport is hard to understand" as concluding the provisional-and-real of transforming others, and so on.

Praising Śākyamuni's Two Wisdoms 就釋迦文：吾從成佛已來

Chinese Source

從「吾從成佛已來」者，是歎釋迦權實。舊云釋迦權實各各歎，謂「吾從成佛」下，是歎權，「所以者何」是釋權；「如來知見廣大」下，是歎實，從「無量無礙」下，是釋實；從「如來能種種分別」下，是結歎實文。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "since I attained buddhahood" onward is the praise of Śākyamuni's provisional-and-real. The old commentators say that Śākyamuni's provisional and real are praised each separately: that from "since I attained buddhahood" onward is the praise of the provisional, and "and why is this so" its explanation; that from "the Tathāgata's knowledge and vision are vast and great" onward is the praise of the real, and from "immeasurable and unobstructed" onward its explanation; and that from "the Tathāgata is able to make various discriminations" onward is the concluding praise of the real.

舊料揀前後有三意：一、合歎諸佛二智者，明二智體同，開歎釋迦二智者，明二智功用有異；二、明垂迹之本，故諸佛先歎實，明顯本之能，故釋迦先歎權；三、諸佛顯自行，先須得實，釋迦明化他，先以權引童蒙，而互現出沒者，將明體圓不可偏存，存則失旨也。今謂不爾，但依文次第於義易解，不須曲辯。又汝云諸佛道同，云何異解？如人善讚孝順而打擲父母(云云)。

The old commentators, distinguishing the reasoning behind this ordering, give three considerations: first, that the joint praise of the twin wisdoms of all buddhas makes clear that the substance of the two wisdoms is the same, while the separate praise of Śākyamuni's twin wisdoms makes clear that the function of the two wisdoms differs; second, that, in making clear the origin from which his trace is manifested, the buddhas are praised for their real wisdom first, while, in making clear the capacity by which the origin is manifested, Śākyamuni is praised for his expedient wisdom first; and third, that the buddhas manifest self-cultivation, which must first attain the real, while Śākyamuni makes clear transforming others, which first uses the provisional to guide the young and ignorant — and that this alternating pattern of manifestation is meant to make clear that their essence is perfectly complete and cannot be one-sidedly fixed upon, since to fix upon either one alone would lose the true purport. I now hold this not to be so: one need only follow the sequence of the text itself, by which the meaning is easily understood, without needing to strain for such elaborate distinctions. Moreover, if you say that the buddhas' paths are the same, why then propose differing interpretations for them? This is like a person who praises filial devotion eloquently and yet strikes and beats his own parents, and so on.

就釋迦文亦為三：初、雙歎；次、雙釋；後、雙結。「吾從成佛已來」歎實智。若實智不圓佛道不成，既云成佛，一成一切成，即是歎實智也。「種種因緣」下，是歎權智。四十餘年以三種化他權實逗會眾生，故言種種因緣也。「譬喻」者，小乘中以芭蕉、水沫為譬，大乘中以乾城、鏡幻等譬。依諸論者以小乘譬乳，大乘譬醍醐也。

The text concerning Śākyamuni likewise falls into three: first, the twofold praise; next, the twofold explanation; last, the twofold conclusion. "Since I attained buddhahood" praises real wisdom: were real wisdom not perfectly complete, the buddha-path could not be accomplished; and since it says he "attained buddhahood," once this one thing is accomplished, all things are thereby accomplished — this, then, is the praise of real wisdom. From "various causes and conditions" onward is the praise of expedient wisdom: for more than forty years he responded to and gathered in sentient beings by means of the three kinds of the provisional-and-real of transforming others, and hence it is said, "various causes and conditions." As for "parables": in the Lesser Vehicle, the plantain tree and water-foam serve as parables, while in the Greater Vehicle, the phantom city and the mirror-reflection and the like serve as parables. According to the various treatises, the Lesser Vehicle is likened to milk, and the Greater Vehicle is likened to ghee.

「廣演」者，能於一法出無量義也。「無數方便」者，即七種方便也。「引導眾生令離諸著」者，說散十善離三途著，說淨十善離欲界著，說三藏離見思著，說菩薩法離涅槃著，說佛法離順道法愛著。

As for "expounding it widely": he is able to draw forth immeasurable meanings from a single dharma. As for "countless skillful means": these are precisely the seven kinds of expedient means. As for "guiding sentient beings to depart from every attachment": he preached the scattered practice of the ten wholesome deeds so as to free them from attachment to the three lower destinies; he preached the pure practice of the ten wholesome deeds so as to free them from attachment to the desire realm; he preached the Tripiṭaka teaching so as to free them from attachment to views and thought; he preached the bodhisattva dharma so as to free them from attachment to nirvāṇa; and he preached the buddha-dharma so as to free them from attachment even to the dharma of love that accords with the path.

Explaining and Concluding Śākyamuni's Two Wisdoms 雙釋、雙結：如來知見廣大深遠

Chinese Source

從「所以者何」，是雙釋二智也。如來半句即是釋實智，從真如實相中來而得成佛道，故名如來，即釋成實智也；方便即是釋權智；由於方便善巧故能種種因緣知見波羅蜜者，即是雙舉權實知見也。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "and why is this so" is the twofold explanation of the two wisdoms. The half-phrase "Tathāgata" is precisely the explanation of real wisdom: coming from the very midst of suchness, the mark of reality, and thereby attaining the buddha-path — hence he is named "Tathāgata," and this is precisely the explanation of the accomplishment of real wisdom. "Skillful means" is precisely the explanation of expedient wisdom: it is by means of skillful and adept expedients that he is able to bring about the pāramitā of knowledge and vision through various causes and conditions — this is precisely the joint presentation of the knowledge and vision of both the provisional and the real.

一切種智名實知，佛眼名實見；道種智名權知，法眼名權見，悉到事理邊故，悉名波羅蜜。「皆已具足」者，權實悉究竟也。若不作雙釋之意，那忽言皆已？皆已者雙釋意顯也。

The wisdom that knows all modes is called real knowledge, and the buddha-eye is called real vision; the wisdom that knows the paths of all beings is called expedient knowledge, and the dharma-eye is called expedient vision — since both reach the very limit of fact and principle, both alike are called pāramitās. "All already complete" means that both the provisional and the real are entirely brought to their ultimate conclusion. Were this not intended as a twofold explanation, why would the text suddenly say "all already"? "All already" is precisely what makes the sense of this twofold explanation manifest.

從「如來知見廣大深遠」，即是雙結釋迦二智也。如來知見如前說，廣大明橫、深遠明豎，如此實智非橫非豎，寄言往歎論其橫豎照無限極，如函大、蓋大也。

From "the Tathāgata's knowledge and vision are vast, great, deep, and far-reaching" is precisely the twofold conclusion of Śākyamuni's two wisdoms. "The Tathāgata's knowledge and vision" is as explained above; "vast and great" clarifies its horizontal breadth, and "deep and far-reaching" clarifies its vertical depth. Though this real wisdom is in itself neither horizontal nor vertical, in resorting to words in order to praise it, one speaks of its illumination as limitless in both the horizontal and the vertical, like a container matched exactly to its contents, or a lid matched exactly to its vessel.

「無量無礙」下，即是結權智也。自行之權，道前方便，約諸法門，故知是結權智明矣；實智無若干也。光宅以此釋實智，非但光宅不識實智，梁代皆不知其無礙慧無若干也(云云)。「無量」即佛地四等也，「無礙」即佛地四辯也。

From "immeasurable and unobstructed" onward is precisely the conclusion of expedient wisdom. The provisional aspect of self-cultivation, as an expedient means preceding the path, is considered in terms of the various dharma-gates, and hence it is clear that this is the conclusion of expedient wisdom; real wisdom admits of no such gradation. Guangzhai uses this passage to explain real wisdom — but it is not only Guangzhai who fails to recognize real wisdom; the entire Liang dynasty likewise failed to know that this unobstructed wisdom admits of no gradation, and so on. "Immeasurable" refers to the buddha-stage's four immeasurable minds, and "unobstructed" refers to the buddha-stage's four unobstructed eloquences.

能於一辭一義，旋出無量樂說不窮，比於別通菩薩，如甲上土方地。「力」即十力，「畏」即四無所畏，「禪」盡禪之實相，「定」即首楞嚴定，「三昧」即王三昧，「深入無際」者，結成豎深，「成就一切未曾有法」，結成橫廣。

Able, from a single word or a single meaning, to bring forth immeasurable eloquent expositions without exhaustion — compared to this, the bodhisattvas of the shared and distinct teachings are like the dirt beneath a fingernail set against the whole earth. "Powers" refers to the ten powers; "fearlessnesses" refers to the four kinds of fearlessness; "dhyāna" refers to the very reality-mark of all dhyānas; "concentration" refers to the Śūraṅgama-samādhi; "samādhi" refers to the king of samādhis; "penetrating deeply without limit" concludes the explanation of vertical depth, and "accomplishing every unprecedented dharma" concludes the explanation of horizontal breadth.

The Reason for Cutting Off Speech 絕言歎之由：舉權實為由

Chinese Source

從「舍利弗如來能種種分別」下，舊將結成前權實；今用起後將欲絕言，更舉權實為絕歎之由。文為二：初、舉絕歎之由；次、指絕言之境。鄭重者表殷勤也，「如來能善分別、巧說諸法」者，即舉權也。「言辭柔軟悅可眾心」者，舉實也。何以得知？上見他土說頓云：「其聲清淨出柔軟音」下，身子領解云：「聞佛柔軟音深遠甚微妙」，據前後兩文，知是舉實智也。前歎中，前實後權，今何意前權後實？明前欲寄言故從實而舒權，今欲絕言須卷權歸實耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "Śāriputra, the Tathāgata is able to make various discriminations" onward, the old commentators take this as concluding the provisional-and-real explained above; I now take it instead as opening what follows, about to cut off speech entirely, once again raising the provisional-and-real as the occasion for the praise that cuts off speech. The text falls into two: first, raising the occasion for the praise that cuts off speech; next, pointing to the object of this cutting-off of speech. "Earnestly" signifies deep sincerity. "The Tathāgata is able skillfully to discriminate and adeptly expound the dharmas" raises the provisional; "his words are gentle and soft, gladdening the hearts of the assembly" raises the real. How can this be known? Above, where the light was seen preaching the sudden teaching in other lands, it says, "his voice, pure and clear, gave forth a gentle and soft sound," and below, where Śāriputra expresses his understanding, he says, "hearing the Buddha's gentle and soft voice, profound, far-reaching, and most subtle" — on the evidence of these two passages, before and after, it is known that this passage raises real wisdom. In the earlier praise, the real came first and the provisional after; why, here, does the provisional come first and the real after? Because, earlier, wishing to resort to words, the exposition proceeded from the real and unfolded outward into the provisional; here, wishing to cut off words entirely, it must instead fold the provisional back and gather it into the real.

從「取要言之」，是指實境，要者莫過于實也；「無量無邊未曾有法」是指權境。又「舉要」是創指之端，「無量無邊」是指權，「未曾有法」是指實，言此二法佛悉成就，修道得故，此那可說？

From "to state the essential matter" points to the object that is the real, since nothing surpasses the real in essentiality; "the immeasurable, boundless, unprecedented dharmas" points to the object that is the provisional. Moreover, "stating the essential matter" is the initial point of this pointing; "immeasurable and boundless" points to the provisional, and "unprecedented dharmas" points to the real; the passage says that the Buddha has accomplished both of these dharmas fully through the cultivation of the path — how, then, could this possibly be spoken?

若單明一事，不應言悉，既雙指權實，其意明矣。

Were only a single matter being made clear, the text ought not to say "entirely"; since it points jointly to both the provisional and the real, its intent is thereby made clear.

Stopping the Explanation: Only a Buddha Together with a Buddha 止不須說：唯佛與佛乃能究盡

Chinese Source

「止」者下，第二，即絕言歎也。印師云：「欲因止生其疑請之心也。」觀師云：「實法難知，故先抑止，驚其常情。」今明此法深寂言語道斷，體不可說，故止而歎之。設慈悲為說，聞不能解，傷其善根，是故止也。從「所以者何」下，是釋止歎之意。意為兩：一、就佛是最上人成就，修得最上法，故不可說；次、明甚深境界不可思議，故不可說。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "stop" onward is, second, the praise that cuts off speech entirely. Master Yin says, "This is meant, by means of the stopping, to give rise in the assembly to the mind that doubts and requests further teaching." Master Guan says, "The real dharma is hard to know, and so the Buddha first restrains and stops himself, so as to startle the assembly's ordinary expectations." I now explain that this dharma is so profoundly still that the path of words is cut off, and its substance cannot be spoken — hence he stops himself and yet still praises it. Were he, out of compassion, to speak of it regardless, those who heard it would be unable to understand, and this would injure their wholesome roots — and it is for this reason that he stops himself. From "and why is this so" onward is the explanation of the intent behind this stopping and praising. This intent is twofold: first, in terms of the Buddha being the supreme person who has accomplished the supreme dharma through cultivation, this dharma cannot be spoken; and second, in making clear that this profound state is inconceivable, it cannot be spoken.

就「佛成就」下，明上人權實橫滿不可說；從「唯佛與佛」下，明上人權實豎深不可說。成就對不成就，乃至難解對不難解，即是橫明成就，修道得故，故不可說。「唯佛與佛乃能究盡」者，初中分獲，未盡其源，如十四日月，光用未普；獨佛與佛究竟邊底，如十五日之月，體無不圓、光無不遍，如此豎深，修道得故，故不可說。從「諸法實相」下，即是甚深境界不可思議，故不可說。

From "the Buddha has accomplished" onward makes clear that the horizontal fullness of the supreme person's provisional-and-real cannot be spoken; from "only a buddha together with a buddha" onward makes clear that the vertical depth of the supreme person's provisional-and-real cannot be spoken. "Accomplished" set against "not accomplished," and further, "hard to understand" set against "not hard to understand" — this is the horizontal clarification of what is accomplished through the cultivation of the path, and hence it cannot be spoken. As for "only a buddha together with a buddha is able to exhaust it": those at the initial and middling stages attain it only in part, not yet exhausting its source, like the moon on the fourteenth day, whose light and function are not yet complete; only buddha together with buddha reaches its utmost depths, like the moon on the fifteenth day, whose substance is nowhere incomplete and whose light is nowhere unspread — this is the vertical depth of what is accomplished through the cultivation of the path, and hence it cannot be spoken. From "the true mark of the dharmas" onward is precisely the inconceivability of this profound state, and hence it cannot be spoken.

光宅云：「初一句標二智章，諸法標權智境。」三三非一故言諸法，三法之中其教最顯，教必逗機仍有其人，故知三三是權也。實相者是實智境，一理非虛故言實相，四一之中遍舉一理，理是本故，故是實也。中有九句，還釋上兩章耳。前五句釋權章。「如是相」者，三乘言教攬而可別也；「如是性」者，三乘教性分不可移易也；「如是體」者，三乘之教八音章句各各有體，菩薩教以六度為體(云云)；「如是力」者，三乘教用訓導之力也；「如是作」者，三乘教被前人有造作也，廣實智境，略不牒章。

Guangzhai says: "The first single phrase marks out the section on the twin wisdoms, and 'the dharmas' marks out the object of expedient wisdom." Since the three vehicles, taken thrice-over as three separate things, are not a single thing, the text says "the dharmas"; and among these three teachings, the teaching itself is most evident, and a teaching must respond to capacity and thus have a person to whom it is addressed — hence it is known that this threefold-times-three is the provisional. "The mark of reality" is the object of real wisdom; since it is a single principle, not empty, it is called "the mark of reality," and among the fourfold "suchness" terms, it universally cites this single principle, and since this principle is the root, it is therefore the real. Within this passage there are nine phrases, which in turn explain the two sections above. The first five phrases explain the section on the provisional. "Suchness of characteristics" means that the verbal teachings of the three vehicles can be grasped and differentiated; "suchness of nature" means that the natures proper to the teachings of the three vehicles cannot be interchanged; "suchness of substance" means that the eight-toned phrases and passages of the three vehicles' teachings each have their own substance, the bodhisattva teaching taking the six perfections as its substance, and so on; "suchness of power" means the power of the three vehicles' teachings to instruct and guide; "suchness of activity" means that the three vehicles' teachings, once received by their hearers, produce some activity in them — this broadens the object of real wisdom, and for brevity is not cited again as a separate heading.

實境有四一，以四廣其一理，「如是因」者，境生真解為因，萬善望果也；「如是緣」者，境發實智為緣，以因所望處為果，果起酬因為報。後二句雙結，初句結權，「本」即舉相，「末」即舉作，次句結實，「究竟」即結因，「等」即結報也。北地師云：「三乘法皆有相、性、果、報、本末也。」

The object that is the real has four "suchness" terms, broadening this single principle into four: "suchness of cause" means that the object gives rise to true understanding, which serves as the cause, the myriad wholesome deeds looking forward to their fruit; "suchness of condition" means that the object gives rise to real wisdom, which serves as the condition; taking the place toward which the cause looks forward as the fruit, the fruit that arises in requital of the cause is the recompense. The final two phrases jointly conclude both sections: the first phrase concludes the provisional, "root" citing "characteristics" and "branch" citing "activity"; the second phrase concludes the real, "ultimate" concluding "cause" and "equally so" concluding "recompense." The Northern masters say: "The dharmas of the three vehicles all possess characteristics, nature, fruit, recompense, root, and branch."

瑤師云：「如是相、性，此釋智慧照用三乘萌異，為相必爾成三為性；發心為體；隨心所堪為力；力有所造為作；作行招果為因；因者語其已分，所由為緣；緣者語其外力，遂剋為果；酬因為報；相為本；報為末；終同一致為究竟等也。如是者，其事不差也。」

Master Yao says: "As for suchness of characteristics and suchness of nature: this explains how wisdom's illuminative function shows the three vehicles budding forth differently — the characteristics necessarily becoming, in this way, three distinct natures. The arousal of the resolve is the substance; what the mind is able to bear is the power; the power that produces something is the activity; the activity that invites a fruit is the cause; 'cause' speaks of what already belongs to oneself, while 'condition' speaks of that from which it arises; 'condition' speaks of the external power that finally brings the fruit to completion, hence 'fruit'; requiting the cause is the recompense; characteristics are the root; recompense is the branch; and their ultimate arrival at one and the same outcome is 'ultimate and equally so.' 'Suchness' means that the matter is exactly thus, without discrepancy."

暢師但約佛上作，相者，十力各有相貌也；性者，從根各有所習，所習不改謂之為性，是性力境也；體者，根性不同所欲亦異，言其心用縛著故以體為名，此欲力境也；力者，定別名也，神通變動非定不運，鎮心靜亂非定不寂，故力為禪定境也；作者，是業，即業力境也；因者，道為因能至涅槃，即至處道力境也；緣者，緣宿命力境也；果者，據今因，所召果在未來，是天眼力境也；報者，今報以望往因，據即漏盡力境也，故語報是漏盡也；本者是相，末是報，總而望之都是處非處力境也。

Master Chang, however, explains this solely in terms of what pertains to the Buddha himself: "characteristics" means that each of the ten powers has its own particular mark; "nature" means that, according to root faculties, each being has what it has become habituated to, and this unchanging habituation is called "nature" — this is the object of the power of knowing others' faculties. "Substance" means that, since root faculties differ, desires likewise differ; speaking of the mind's function of attachment, this is named "substance" — this is the object of the power of knowing others' desires. "Power" is another name for concentration: supernormal powers and transformative movement cannot function without concentration, and the stilling of the mind and quieting of disturbance cannot become tranquil without concentration — hence "power" is the object of the power of knowing the dhyānas and concentrations. "Activity" is karma — this is the object of the power of knowing karma. "Cause" means that the path serves as the cause able to reach nirvāṇa — this is the object of the power of knowing the destination reached by every path. "Condition" is the object of the power of knowing former lives. "Fruit" means that, based on a present cause, the fruit it summons lies in the future — this is the object of the divine eye power. "Recompense" means that the present recompense is viewed in light of a past cause — this is based on the object of the power of the exhaustion of outflows, and hence "recompense" here refers to the exhaustion of outflows. "Root" is "characteristics," and "branch" is "recompense"; taken together and viewed as a whole, all of this is the object of the power of knowing what is possible and what is not.

Faulting the Old Interpretations; the Nine Categories of the Śāstra 斥古釋、《釋論》九種

Chinese Source

上來諸釋非不一途，然於理不通，於文不允。文不允者經云「諸法」，何法不收？豈止三乘耶？理不通者，經云「實相」，何所不在，而但在因果體。若實獨在於佛，佛則不權；權獨在三乘，三乘則永無實；若三乘但為五，則權法不足，復全無實；若四句但在佛，佛全無權，實亦不足；義不涉於凡夫，則諸法之文，便是無用。實相不遍，實相外別更有法。如此等過故，皆不用也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The various explanations set forth above are none of them without their own approach, and yet they do not accord with principle, nor do they fit the text. As for not fitting the text: the sūtra says "the dharmas" — what dharma would this fail to encompass? How could it be limited to the three vehicles alone? As for not according with principle: the sūtra says "the mark of reality" — where is this not present, and yet these interpretations confine it to cause, effect, and substance alone. If the real belongs solely to the Buddha, then the Buddha would have no provisional; if the provisional belongs solely to the three vehicles, then the three vehicles would forever lack the real. If the three vehicles are taken as merely five of the ten suchness terms, then the dharma of the provisional would be insufficient, and there would be no real at all. If the fourfold thesis pertains only to the Buddha, then the Buddha would be entirely without the provisional, and the real, too, would be insufficient. If the meaning does not extend to ordinary beings at all, then the text's phrase "the dharmas" would simply be useless. If the mark of reality is not universal, there would have to be some further dharma existing outside the mark of reality. Because of faults such as these, none of these interpretations should be adopted.

The Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra, fascicle thirty-one, explains that each and every dharma has nine aspects: first, each has its own substance; second, each has its own function — for example, though the eye and the ear are alike composed of the four great elements, the eye has the function of seeing while the ear has no such function of seeing, just as fire has heat as its function but is unable to moisten; third, each has its own power, as fire has burning as its power and water has moistening as its power; fourth, each has its own cause; fifth, each has its own condition; sixth, each has its own fruit; seventh, each has its own nature; eighth, each has its own limiting bounds; and ninth, each has its own expedient means of opening and penetrating. Dharmottara took these nine categories and correlated them with the ten suchness terms in the Lotus Sūtra: "each has its own function" corresponds to the Lotus Sūtra's "suchness of activity"; "each has its own limiting bounds" corresponds to the Lotus Sūtra's "suchness of characteristics"; "each has its own fruit" corresponds to the Lotus Sūtra's "suchness of fruit" and "suchness of recompense"; and "each has its own expedient means of opening and penetrating" corresponds to the Lotus Sūtra's "suchness of root, branch, and their ultimate equality"; the remaining terms share the same names and may be understood accordingly.

From "the uncontaminated" onward is, third, three lines making clear that the fourfold assembly of the three vehicles has this doubt — the first line makes clear the arhats, and the following two lines make clear the pratyekabuddhas, while the phrase in between, "and those who seek nirvāṇa," makes clear the bodhisattvas of the six perfections. How can this be known? Above it says "those who have attained nirvāṇa," whereas here it says "and" — this "and" indicates that these bodhisattvas seek nirvāṇa for themselves and, in addition, for others as well, and hence, differing from the two vehicles, it is known that these are bodhisattvas.

「於諸」下，第四，有一行半，明身子疑。「佛口所生」下，第五、有一行，明佛子疑。「諸天龍」下，第六，二行，總明同疑請也。夫偈頌、長行，可以意推，如其非頌即是長出，於義非急者，不能煩文分擘故略耳。從「爾時佛告」下，是二止，更牒疑為請，悉如文(云云)。

From "with regard to the" onward is, fourth, one-and-a-half lines making clear Śāriputra's own doubt. From "born from the Buddha's mouth" onward is, fifth, one line making clear the doubt of the buddhas' disciples. From "the gods and dragons" onward is, sixth, two lines generally making clear the shared doubt and request. As for the verse and the prose, their correspondence may generally be inferred; where a passage is not versified, it is simply carried over from the prose, and where the meaning is not urgent, the text need not be laboriously divided and analyzed point by point, and so it is left brief here. From "at that time the Buddha told" onward is the second refusal, once again restating the doubt in order to prompt the request — all of this is as the text itself has it, and so on.
