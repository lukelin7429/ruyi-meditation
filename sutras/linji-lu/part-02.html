The Fourfold Scheme, and Seeking Nothing Outside

示眾（一）

The first third of Linji's great evening instruction to the assembly — the fourfold scheme of "taking away," the three bodies of buddhahood relocated into your own house as three kinds of light in a single thought, a warning against mistaking Buddha and Māra for two different things, and the person of no reliance who rides every circumstance without being carried away by any of them.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1985, recorded by Huiran Status: Part 2 of 7 complete Reading notes: after Zhizhao's 人天眼目 (T2006) where noted; otherwise Ru-Yi Meditation Center
This part covers
The fourfold scheme of taking away the person and not the circumstance, the circumstance and not the person, both, or neither; the three bodies of buddhahood relocated as three kinds of light within a single thought; the warning that Buddha and Māra are two states rather than two beings; and the person of no reliance who manifests whatever circumstance a question calls for, like the moon in water.
Genre note
This is continuous "instructions to the assembly" (示眾) rather than the short question-and-answer exchanges of Part 1 — a sustained sermon interrupted only by occasional questions, closer in form to a homily than to the rapid dialogues that dominate this text's later parts.

1. Taking Away the Person, Taking Away the Circumstance

Chinese Source

師晚參示眾云：「有時奪人不奪境、有時奪境不奪人、有時人境俱奪、有時人境俱不奪。」時有僧問：「如何是奪人不奪境？」師云：「煦日發生鋪地錦，瓔孩垂髮白如絲。」僧云：「如何是奪境不奪人？」師云：「王令已行天下遍，將軍塞外絕烟塵。」僧云：「如何是人境兩俱奪？」師云：「并汾絕信，獨處一方。」僧云：「如何是人境俱不奪？」師云：「王登寶殿，野老謳歌。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Giving evening instructions to the assembly, the master said: "Sometimes I take away the person but not the surrounding circumstance; sometimes I take away the circumstance but not the person; sometimes I take away both person and circumstance; sometimes I take away neither." At that time a monk asked: "What is taking away the person but not the circumstance?" The master said: "The warm sun rises and spreads its brocade across the earth; a babe's hanging hair is already white as silk." The monk asked: "What is taking away the circumstance but not the person?" The master said: "The king's command has already gone out through all the world; the general beyond the frontier has cut off every trace of smoke and dust." The monk asked: "What is taking away both person and circumstance together?" The master said: "Bing and Fen have severed all word from each other, each standing alone in its own quarter." The monk asked: "What is taking away neither person nor circumstance?" The master said: "The king ascends his jeweled hall; the old rustic sings his song."

Reading note — after Zhizhao's 人天眼目 (T2006)

This "fourfold scheme" (later systematized as 四料揀, the "four discernments") became one of the most heavily annotated formulas in the entire Linji-house tradition — Zhizhao's Song-dynasty compilation Rentian Yanmu (人天眼目, T2006) records generations of masters, from Fengxue Yanzhao to Shoushan Shengnian to Dahui Zonggao, each supplying their own verse gloss on all four positions. Dahui's own note there identifies "Bing and Fen" (并汾) as the names of two Tang-dynasty prefectures in Linji's own region, and glosses "cut off all word" (絕信) as these two neighboring territories losing all communication with each other — an image, Dahui says, that several generations of commentators had garbled before the actual place-names in Linji's own text resolved the confusion. The four positions describe not fixed teaching styles but a diagnostic Linji applies according to the student before him, made explicit later in this same part when he pairs the scheme with a threefold ranking of student capacity.

2. True Understanding, and the Three Bodies of Light in Your Own House

Chinese Source

師乃云：「今時學佛法者，且要求真正見解。若得真正見解，生死不染、去住自由，不要求殊勝，殊勝自至。道流！秖如自古先德，皆有出人底路。如山僧指示人處，秖要爾不受人惑，要用便用，更莫遲疑。如今學者不得，病在甚處？病在不自信處。爾若自信不及，即便忙忙地徇一切境轉，被他萬境回換，不得自由。爾若能歇得念念馳求心，便與祖佛不別。爾欲得識祖佛麼？秖爾面前聽法底。是學人信不及，便向外馳求。設求得者，皆是文字勝相，終不得他活祖意。莫錯諸禪德，此時不遇，萬劫千生輪回三界，徇好境掇去，驢牛肚裏生。道流！約山僧見處，與釋迦不別。今日多般用處，欠少什麼？六道神光未曾間歇，若能如是見得，秖是一生無事人。

「大德！三界無安，猶如火宅，此不是爾久停住處。無常殺鬼一剎那間，不揀貴、賤、老、少。爾要與祖佛不別，但莫外求。爾一念心上清淨光，是爾屋裏法身佛；爾一念心上無分別光，是爾屋裏報身佛；爾一念心上無差別光，是爾屋裏化身佛。此三種身是爾即今目前聽法底人，秖為不向外馳求，有此功用。據經論家取三種身為極則，約山僧見處不然，此三種身是名言、亦是三種依。古人云：『身依義立，土據體論。』法性身、法性土，明知是光影。大德！

Ru-Yi Translation

The master then said: "Students of the Buddhadharma today need above all to seek a true and correct understanding. Once you have it, birth and death cannot stain you, and you come and go in complete freedom — you need not seek anything extraordinary, for the extraordinary arrives of itself. Followers of the Way! Every eminent teacher since ancient times has had some way of leading people out. As for what this mountain monk points out, I want only that you not be deceived by others: use it the moment you need it, and hesitate no further. Students today cannot manage this — where does the sickness lie? It lies in not believing in yourselves. If you cannot trust yourselves, you rush about, turned this way and that by every circumstance you meet, exchanged and switched about by ten thousand circumstances, never free. But if you can bring to rest this mind that runs after one thing after another, moment by moment, you will be no different from the patriarchs and buddhas. Do you want to recognize the patriarchs and buddhas? It is simply you, right in front of me, listening to this Dharma. Because the student cannot believe this, he runs after something outside himself. Even if what he sought were found, it would be nothing but words and superior-seeming forms — never the living intent of the patriarchs. Do not make this mistake, Chan worthies: miss this chance now, and you will spin through ten thousand kalpas and a thousand lifetimes in the three realms, dragged off by whatever pleasant circumstance you chase, to be born in the belly of a donkey or an ox. Followers of the Way! As I see it, I am no different from Śākyamuni. Today, in all my various activities, what am I lacking? The spiritual light of the six paths has never once ceased. See it this way, and you are simply a person with nothing to do for the rest of your life.

"Worthies! The three realms have no peace — they are like a burning house; this is no place for you to stay for long. The killer-demon of impermanence arrives in a single instant, choosing neither the noble nor the lowly, neither the old nor the young. If you want to be no different from patriarchs and buddhas, simply stop seeking outside yourself. The pure light in a single thought of your mind is the dharma-body buddha in your own house; the light of non-discrimination in a single thought of your mind is the reward-body buddha in your own house; the light of non-differentiation in a single thought of your mind is the transformation-body buddha in your own house. These three bodies are you yourself, right now, in front of me, listening to this Dharma; only because you do not run after something outside yourself do they have this function at all. The scholars of the sūtras and treatises take these three bodies as the ultimate standard, but as I see it this is not so — these three bodies are mere names and phrases, and also three kinds of reliance. The ancients said: 'The body is established by way of meaning; the land is discussed by way of substance.' The dharma-nature body and the dharma-nature land are clearly known to be nothing but reflections of light. Worthies!

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This passage relocates the standard Mahāyāna doctrine of a buddha's "three bodies" (dharma-body, reward-body, transformation-body) entirely inside a single present thought, calling each one merely "a name and a phrase" the moment it is taken as an external fact about buddhahood rather than a description of your own mind functioning right now. The same insistence that the sought-after reality is never elsewhere recalls this site's Xinxin Ming, whose opening lines already warn against any preference that splits a single undivided mind into things to seek and things to avoid.

3. What Truly Hears the Dharma

Chinese Source

爾且識取弄光影底人是諸佛之本源，一切處是道流歸舍處。是爾四大色身不解說法聽法、脾胃肝膽不解說法聽法、虛空不解說法聽法，是什麼解說法聽法？是爾目前歷歷底、勿一箇形段孤明，是這箇解說法聽法。若如是見得，便與祖佛不別。但一切時中更莫間斷，觸目皆是，秖為情生智隔想變體殊，所以輪回三界受種種苦。

Ru-Yi Translation

You should recognize that the one who plays with these reflections of light is the very source of all buddhas, and that every single place is where you, followers of the Way, truly belong. It is not your four great elements, this physical body, that can expound or hear the Dharma; not your spleen, stomach, liver, or gallbladder; not empty space either. What is it, then, that expounds and hears the Dharma? It is you yourself, standing vividly clear before me right now, without a single shred of form, solitary and bright — this is what expounds and hears the Dharma. See it this way, and you are no different from patriarchs and buddhas. Only, in every moment, do not let this break off; whatever meets your eye is exactly this. It is only because feeling arises that wisdom becomes obstructed, and thought shifts so that substance seems to differ, that you spin through the three realms and undergo every kind of suffering.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This "one who expounds and hears the Dharma" — never the physical body, never any organ, never empty space — is this text's most repeated identification of what is actually present in every exchange, restated with slight variation more than a dozen times across the remaining parts. It anticipates the same move this site's Platform Sūtra makes with self-nature: not a further thing to locate, but the very activity of locating anything at all.

4. Hitching Posts and Latrines: The Cost of Not Believing Yourself

Chinese Source

若約山僧見處，無不甚深、無不解脫。道流！心法無形，通貫十方，在眼曰見、在耳曰聞、在鼻嗅香、在口談論、在手執捉、在足運奔。本是一精明，分為六和合。一心既無，隨處解脫。山僧與麼說，意在什麼處？秖為道流一切馳求，心不能歇，上他古人閑機境。道流取山僧見處，坐斷報化佛頭，十地滿心猶如客作兒，等妙二覺擔枷鎖漢，羅漢、辟支猶如廁穢，菩提、涅槃如繫驢橛。何以如此？秖為道流不達三祇劫空，所以有此障礙。若是真正道人，終不如是，但能隨緣消舊業，任運著衣裳，要行即行、要坐即坐，無一念心希求佛果。緣何如此？古人云：『若欲作業求佛，佛是生死大兆。』

「大德！時光可惜，秖擬傍家波波地學禪、學道，認名、認句，求佛、求祖、求善知識，意度莫錯。道流！爾秖有一箇父母，更求何物？爾自返照看。古人云：『演若達多失却頭，求心歇處即無事。』大德！且要平常莫作模樣，有一般不識好惡禿奴，便即見神、見鬼，指東劃西，好晴、好雨。如是之流盡須抵債，向閻老前吞熱鐵丸有日。好人家男女被這一般野狐精魅所著，便即揑怪瞎屢生！索飯錢有日在。」

Ru-Yi Translation

As I see it, there is nothing that is not utterly profound, nothing that is not already liberated. Followers of the Way! The dharma of mind has no form, and runs through all ten directions: in the eye it is called seeing, in the ear it is called hearing, in the nose it smells fragrance, in the mouth it converses, in the hand it grasps, in the foot it walks and runs. Fundamentally it is a single luminous essence, only divided into six harmoniously functioning gates. Once you know that this one mind does not exist as a fixed thing, you are liberated wherever you stand. Why do I put it this way? Only because you, followers of the Way, run after everything without your minds ever coming to rest, and so climb onto the idle devices of the ancients. Followers of the Way, take up my own way of seeing, and cut clean through even the heads of the reward-body and transformation-body buddhas: the bodhisattva whose mind is full at the tenth ground is like a hired hand; one at the stage of Equal or Wondrous Enlightenment wears a cangue and chains; arhats and pratyekabuddhas are like filth in a latrine; bodhi and nirvāṇa are like a hitching post for tethering a donkey. Why is this so? Only because you, followers of the Way, have not penetrated the emptiness of the three incalculable kalpas, and so this obstruction remains. If one is a truly genuine person of the Way, it is never like this: he simply exhausts his old karma as circumstances bring it, puts on his clothes as they come to hand, walks when he needs to walk, sits when he needs to sit, without a single thought that hopes for the fruit of buddhahood. Why is this so? The ancients said: "If you wish to perform deeds in order to seek the Buddha, the Buddha is the great omen of birth and death."

"Worthies! Time is precious. Yet you insist on running about from house to house learning Chan, learning the Way, recognizing names, recognizing phrases, seeking a buddha, seeking a patriarch, seeking a good teacher — do not misjudge your own intent. Followers of the Way! You have only the one set of parents — what more do you seek? Turn the light back upon yourself and look. The ancients said: 'When Yajñadatta thought he had lost his head, it was only in the place where the seeking mind came to rest that there was nothing the matter.' Worthies! You need above all to be ordinary — do not put on airs and postures. There is a type of shave-pated fool who cannot tell good from bad, and who goes about seeing spirits and seeing ghosts, pointing east and gesturing west, delighting in fine weather, delighting in rain. People of this kind will all have to pay their debts one day, swallowing red-hot iron pellets before old King Yama. Sons and daughters of good families, taken in by this kind of wild-fox spirit, go about play-acting strange states — blind fools! — and the day will come when they are asked to pay for the rice they have eaten."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The deliberately degrading images here — arhats as latrine filth, nirvāṇa as a hitching post — are aimed not at the goals themselves but at treating them as external prizes still to be won by "one who has not yet penetrated the emptiness of the three incalculable kalpas." Yajñadatta (演若達多), the man in the Śūraṅgama Sūtra who wakes one morning convinced he has lost his own head because he cannot see his own face in a mirror and runs through the streets in a panic, is one of Chan literature's most quoted images for a search that only needs to stop, since the very thing sought was never actually missing.

5. A True Home-Leaver Discerns Buddha from Māra

Chinese Source

師示眾云：「道流！切要求取真正見解，向天下橫行，免被這一般精魅惑亂。無事是貴人，但莫造作，秖是平常。爾擬向外傍家求過覓脚手，錯了也。秖擬求佛，佛是名句。爾還識馳求底麼？三世十方佛祖出來也秖為求法，如今參學道流也秖為求法。得法始了，未得依前輪回五道。云何是法？法者是心法，心法無形，通貫十方，目前現用；人信不及，便乃認名認句，向文字中求意度佛法，天地懸殊。道流！山僧說法，說什麼法？說心地法，便能入凡、入聖，入淨、入穢，入真、入俗。要且不是爾真俗凡聖能與一切真俗凡聖安著名字，真俗凡聖與此人安著名字不得。道流！把得便用，更不著名字，號之為玄旨。山僧說法與天下人別，秖如有箇文殊、普賢出來，目前各現一身問法，纔道咨和尚，我早辨了也。老僧穩坐，更有道流來相見時，我盡辨了也。何以如此？秖為我見處別，外不取凡聖、內不住根本，見徹更不疑謬。」

師示眾云：「道流！佛法無用功處，秖是平常無事——屙屎、送尿、著衣、喫飯、困來即臥……。愚人笑我，智乃知焉。古人云：『向外作工夫，總是癡頑漢。』爾且隨處作主，立處皆真，境來回換不得，縱有從來習氣、五無間業，自為解脫大海。今時學者總不識法，猶如觸鼻羊逢著物安在口裏，奴郎不辨、賓主不分，如是之流邪心入道，鬧處即入不得，名為真出家人，正是真俗家人。夫出家者，須辨得平常真正見解——辨佛、辨魔，辨真、辨偽，辨凡、辨聖……。若如是辨得，名真出家。若魔、佛不辨，正是出一家入一家，喚作造業眾生，未得名為真出家。秖如今有一箇佛魔同體不分，如水乳合鵝王喫乳。如明眼道流，魔、佛俱打。爾若愛聖憎凡，生死海裏浮沈。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The master gave instructions to the assembly, saying: "Followers of the Way! You must urgently seek a true and correct understanding, and walk boldly across the whole world, so as to avoid being confused and led astray by this kind of spirit-possession. To have nothing to do is to be a person of true worth — only do not manufacture anything further; simply be ordinary. You intend to go seeking outside, from house to house, looking for hands and feet to use — this is already a mistake. You merely intend to seek the Buddha, but "Buddha" is only a name and a phrase. Do you even recognize the one who is doing the running and seeking? The buddhas and patriarchs of the three times and ten directions appear in the world only for the sake of seeking the Dharma; you, followers of the Way, studying here today, likewise study only for the sake of seeking the Dharma. Only once you have attained the Dharma are you truly finished; until then, you spin through the five paths as before. What, then, is the Dharma? The Dharma is the dharma of mind, which has no form, and runs through the ten directions, manifesting its function right before your eyes; but people cannot believe this, and so they go recognizing names and phrases, seeking the meaning of the Buddhadharma inside written words — as far removed from it as heaven from earth. Followers of the Way! When this mountain monk expounds the Dharma, what dharma do I expound? I expound the dharma of the ground of mind, which enters the ordinary and enters the sage, enters the pure and enters the impure, enters the true and enters the conventional. But none of these — the true, the conventional, the ordinary, the sage — can fix a name upon this person; rather, they are quite unable to fix any name upon him at all. Followers of the Way! Seize it and use it at once, without attaching any name to it — call this the mysterious purport. This mountain monk expounds the Dharma differently from everyone else in the world: if Mañjuśrī and Samantabhadra themselves came forward, each manifesting a body to ask about the Dharma, the moment either said "I address the master," I would already have taken his measure. This old monk sits here at ease, and whenever another follower of the Way comes to meet me, I have already taken his measure entirely. Why is this so? Simply because my own way of seeing is different: outwardly I do not grasp at the ordinary or the sage, inwardly I do not dwell in any root or foundation — I see all the way through, and am no longer subject to doubt or error."

The master gave instructions to the assembly, saying: "Followers of the Way! In the Buddhadharma there is no place to apply effort — it is simply being ordinary, with nothing to do: relieving yourself, urinating, putting on clothes, eating food, lying down when tired. The foolish laugh at me for saying this, but the wise understand. The ancients said: 'One who makes an effort looking outward is nothing but a foolish, stubborn fellow.' You need only become master of yourself wherever you stand, and every place you stand becomes true; then no circumstance that comes your way can turn you around, and even the habitual tendencies accumulated from the past, even the five deeds of unbroken retribution, become of themselves a great ocean of liberation. Students today altogether fail to recognize the Dharma — they are like a goat nosing about, taking whatever it meets into its mouth, unable to distinguish master from servant, guest from host. People of this kind, entering the Way with a crooked mind, cannot enter wherever there is noise and confusion; called "true home-leavers," they are in fact true householders still at home. Now, one who has truly left home must be able to discern an ordinary, true and correct understanding — discerning Buddha from Māra, the genuine from the false, the ordinary from the sage. Discern this way, and it is called truly leaving home. Fail to discern Māra from Buddha, and it is exactly leaving one household only to enter another — called a being who creates karma, not yet worthy of the name "true home-leaver." Suppose, right now, there were a single Buddha-and-Māra sharing one body, indistinguishable — like water mixed with milk, which only the king goose can drink apart from the water. A follower of the Way with clear eyes strikes down Buddha and Māra alike. If you love the sage and hate the ordinary, you will still float and sink in the sea of birth and death."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The claim that even Mañjuśrī and Samantabhadra, appearing in person, could not surprise Linji is not a boast about his own attainment so much as a statement about what any encounter actually is in his account: never a matter of who the visitor claims to be, but of catching the "person of no reliance" riding whatever circumstance the visitor brings. The image of the king goose (鵝王) able to drink milk from a mixture of milk and water — a stock simile from Indian Buddhist literature for a discernment fine enough to separate what looks inseparable — sets up the pointed instruction that follows: a true home-leaver "strikes down Buddha and Māra alike" rather than clinging to one and rejecting the other, since clinging itself, whichever side it favors, is the very thing that keeps one "floating and sinking in the sea of birth and death."

6. What Is Buddha? What Is Māra?

Chinese Source

問：「如何是佛？魔？」師云：「爾一念心疑處是魔；爾若達得萬法無生，心如幻化，更無一塵、一法，處處清淨是佛。然佛與魔是染、淨二境，約山僧見處，無佛、無眾生，無古、無今，得者便得，不歷時節，無修、無證，無得、無失，一切時中更無別法。設有一法過此者，我說如夢、如化。山僧所說皆是。道流！即今目前孤明歷歷地聽者，此人處處不滯，通貫十方，三界自在，入一切境差別不能回換，一剎那間透入法界，逢佛說佛、逢祖說祖、逢羅漢說羅漢、逢餓鬼說餓鬼，向一切處游履國土教化眾生未曾離一念，隨處清淨，光透十方，萬法一如。道流！大丈夫兒今日方知本來無事，秖為爾信不及，念念馳求，捨頭覓頭，自不能歇。如圓頓菩薩入法界現身，向淨土中厭凡忻聖，如此之流取捨，未忘染淨心在。

「如禪宗見解，又且不然，直是現今更無時節。山僧說處皆是一期藥病相治，總無實法。若如是見得，是真出家，日消萬兩黃金。道流！莫取次被諸方老師印破面門道，我解禪、解道，辯似懸河，皆是造地獄業。若是真正學道人，不求世間過，切急要求真正見解。若達真正見，解圓明方始了畢。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Someone asked: "What is Buddha? What is Māra?" The master said: "The place where a single thought of doubt arises in your mind — that is Māra. If you can penetrate the fact that the ten thousand dharmas are unborn, that mind is like an illusion, with not one speck of dust, not one dharma remaining, and that everywhere is already pure — that is Buddha. Yet Buddha and Māra are simply two states, defiled and pure. As I see it, there is no Buddha, no sentient being, no past, no present; whoever attains, attains at once, without passing through any period of time — no cultivation, no realization, no gain, no loss; in every moment there is no other dharma whatsoever. Were there even one dharma surpassing this, I would say it too is like a dream, like a transformation. Everything this mountain monk has said amounts to exactly this. Followers of the Way! The one who, right now, right in front of me, listens to this teaching — solitary, bright, vividly distinct — this person is unobstructed wherever he goes, passes freely through all ten directions, is at ease in the three realms, enters every differentiated circumstance without being turned by any of them, and in a single instant penetrates the entire dharma-realm: meeting a buddha, he speaks of buddha; meeting a patriarch, he speaks of patriarch; meeting an arhat, he speaks of arhat; meeting a hungry ghost, he speaks of hungry ghost. Traveling everywhere, teaching sentient beings, he never once departs from this single thought; wherever he stands he is pure, his light penetrating the ten directions, the ten thousand dharmas all one and the same. Followers of the Way! Only today does the great man come to know that fundamentally there has never been anything the matter — it is only because you cannot believe this that you run about moment after moment, throwing away your own head to go searching for your head, unable of yourselves to come to rest. Like the bodhisattva of the perfect and sudden teaching, who manifests a body entering the dharma-realm and, in the Pure Land, wearies of the ordinary and delights in the sagely — people of this kind still have a mind of taking and rejecting, and have not yet forgotten the distinction between defiled and pure.

"The understanding of the Chan school, however, is not like this at all — it is simply the present moment, with no other time involved whatsoever. Everything this mountain monk says is only medicine matched for the moment to a particular sickness; there is no real, fixed dharma in any of it. See it this way, and this is true home-leaving, worth ten thousand taels of gold spent in a single day. Followers of the Way! Do not go about carelessly having your face stamped with approval by teachers everywhere, saying 'I understand Chan, I understand the Way,' debating as fluently as a river in flood — all of this only creates the karma of hell. If you are a person truly studying the Way, you do not go looking for faults in the world; what matters urgently is to seek a true and correct understanding. Only once you have attained a true and correct view, with understanding perfect and complete, are you at last finished."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Linji's answer relocates "Buddha" and "Māra" from persons or external forces into two states of a single mind — doubt on one side, unobstructed clarity on the other — then immediately withdraws even that distinction ("no Buddha, no sentient being"), on the grounds that naming any fixed dharma at all, even "purity," reintroduces the very duality the teaching is meant to dissolve. "Medicine matched for the moment to a particular sickness" (一期藥病相治) is this text's own recurring description of its method: every formula Linji offers, including this one, is a temporary remedy for a specific listener's specific confusion, never a fixed doctrine to be carried forward and reapplied unchanged.

7. True Understanding, and the Person Who Rides the Circumstance

Chinese Source

問：「如何是真正見解？」師云：「爾但一切入凡、入聖，入染、入淨，入諸佛國土、入彌勒樓閣、入毘盧遮那法界，處處皆現國土成、住、壞、空，佛出于世，轉大法輪，却入涅槃，不見有去來相貌，求其生死了不可得，便入無生法界，處處游履國土，入華藏世界。盡見諸法空相，皆無實法，唯有聽法無依道人，是諸佛之母，所以佛從無依生。若悟無依，佛亦無得。若如是見得者，是真正見解。學人不了，為執名句，被他凡聖名礙，所以障其道眼不得分明。秖如十二分教皆是表顯之說，學者不會，便向表顯名句上生解，皆是依倚落在因果，未免三界生死。爾若欲得生死去住脫著自由，即今識取聽法底人：無形、無相、無根、無本、無住處。活撥撥地，應是萬種施設，用處秖是無處，所以覓著轉遠、求之轉乖，號之為祕密。道流！爾莫認著箇夢幻伴子，遲晚中間便歸無常。爾向此世界中覓箇什麼物作解脫，覓取一口飯喫補毳過時，且要訪尋知識，莫因循逐樂。光陰可惜，念念無常，麁則被地、水、火、風，細則被生、住、異、滅四相所逼。道流！今時且要識取四種無相境，免被境擺撲。」

問：「如何是四種無相境？」師云：「爾一念心疑，被地來礙；爾一念心愛，被水來溺；爾一念心嗔，被火來燒；爾一念心喜，被風來飄。若能如是辨得，不被境轉，處處用境，東涌西沒、南涌北沒、中涌邊沒、邊涌中沒，履水如地、履地如水。緣何如此？為達四大如夢如幻故。道流！爾秖今聽法者，不是爾四大能用。爾四大若能如是見得，便乃去住自由。約山僧見處，勿嫌底法。爾若愛聖，聖者聖之名，有一般學人向五臺山裏求文殊，早錯了也，五臺山無文殊。爾欲識文殊麼？秖爾目前用處，始終不異，處處不疑，此箇是活文殊。爾一念心無差別光，處處總是真普賢。儞一念心自能解縛，隨處解脫，此是觀音。三昧法互為主伴，出則一時出，一即三、三即一，如是解得，始好看教。」

師示眾云：「如今學道人且要自信，莫向外覓，總上他閑塵境，都不辨邪正。秖如有祖、有佛，皆是教迹中事。有人拈起一句子語，或隱顯中出，便即疑生，照天、照地，傍家尋問，也大忙然。大丈夫兒莫秖麼論主、論賊，論是、論非，論色、論財，論說閑話過日。山僧此間不論僧俗，但有來者盡識得伊，任伊向甚處出來，但有聲名文句，皆是夢幻。却見乘境底人是諸佛之玄旨，佛境不能自稱我是佛境，還是這箇無依道人乘境出來。若有人出來問我求佛，我即應清淨境出；有人問我菩薩，我即應慈悲境出；有人問我菩提，我即應淨妙境出；有人問我涅槃，我即應寂靜境出。境即萬般差別，人即不別，所以應物現形，如水中月。道流！爾若欲得如法，直須是大丈夫兒始得。若萎萎隨隨地，則不得也。夫如㽄嗄之器不堪貯醍醐。如大器者，直要不受人惑，隨處作主，立處皆真，但有來者皆不得受。爾一念疑即魔入心，如菩薩疑時，生死魔得便。但能息念，更莫外求，物來則照。爾但信現今用底，一箇事也無。爾一念心生三界，隨緣被境分為六塵。儞如今應用處欠少什麼？一剎那間便入淨、入穢，入彌勒樓閣、入三眼國土，處處遊履，唯見空名。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Someone asked: "What is a true and correct understanding?" The master said: "You need only enter everywhere — into the ordinary, into the sage, into the defiled, into the pure, into every buddha-land, into Maitreya's tower, into Vairocana's dharma-realm — and everywhere see lands arise, abide, decay, and become empty; buddhas appearing in the world, turning the great wheel of the Dharma, and then entering nirvāṇa, with no appearance of coming or going to be seen anywhere; seeking their birth and death, you will find them ultimately unobtainable, and so you enter the unborn dharma-realm, traveling everywhere, entering the Flower Treasury World. You will see that all dharmas are marked with emptiness, that none of them is a real, fixed thing — there is only the person of no reliance who listens to the Dharma, who is the mother of all buddhas; and so the Buddha is born from no reliance. Awaken to no reliance, and even "Buddha" is nothing to be attained. See it this way, and this is a true and correct understanding. Students fail to grasp this because they cling to names and phrases, obstructed by the very names "ordinary" and "sage," so their eye of the Way is blocked and cannot see clearly. Even the twelve divisions of the teachings are nothing but statements that point things out; students who fail to understand this form their view right on top of these pointing-out phrases, which is nothing but reliance and dependence, falling into cause and effect, never escaping birth and death in the three realms. If you want to be free to leave and stay, to put on and take off birth and death, then recognize right now the person who is listening to this Dharma: without shape, without form, without root, without foundation, without any dwelling place — vividly alive, responding with ten thousand kinds of activity, yet its very functioning is precisely nowhere at all. That is why the more you seek it, the further it recedes, and the more you pursue it, the more it eludes you; hence it is called 'the secret.' Followers of the Way! Do not mistake this dream-and-illusion companion of yours for what is real — sooner or later, it returns to impermanence. What is it you are looking for in this world to serve as liberation? You seek only a mouthful of food, a robe to patch out your days, while what you truly need is to go seeking out a good teacher, not drifting along chasing pleasure out of habit. Time is precious; every thought is impermanent — in its coarse aspect you are pressed by earth, water, fire, and wind; in its subtle aspect you are pressed by the four marks of arising, abiding, changing, and ceasing. Followers of the Way! What you need right now is to recognize the four kinds of formless realm, so as to avoid being tossed about by circumstance."

Someone asked: "What are the four kinds of formless realm?" The master said: "When a single thought of doubt arises in your mind, you are obstructed by earth; when a single thought of craving arises, you are drowned by water; when a single thought of anger arises, you are burned by fire; when a single thought of joy arises, you are blown about by wind. Discern things this way, and you will not be turned by circumstance, but will put circumstance itself to use everywhere — rising in the east and vanishing in the west, rising in the south and vanishing in the north, rising in the center and vanishing at the edge, rising at the edge and vanishing in the center, walking on water as if it were solid ground, walking on solid ground as if it were water. Why can this be so? Because you have penetrated the fact that the four great elements are like a dream, like an illusion. Followers of the Way! You who are listening to the Dharma right now are not something your four great elements are able to make use of. See your own four great elements this way, and you will come and go in complete freedom. As I see it, there is not a single dharma to be rejected. If you love the sage — 'sage' is merely the name of a sage. There is a type of student who goes to Mount Wutai to seek Mañjuśrī — already a mistake, for there is no Mañjuśrī on Mount Wutai. Do you want to recognize Mañjuśrī? It is simply your own activity right in front of you now, never different from beginning to end, doubted nowhere at all — this is the living Mañjuśrī. The light of non-differentiation in a single thought of your mind — that, everywhere, is the true Samantabhadra. Your own mind's ability, in a single thought, to untie its own bonds, liberated wherever it stands — this is Avalokiteśvara. These three act as host and guest for one another: when they appear, they appear all at once; one is three, and three are one. Only once you understand this in this way are you fit to study the scriptures at all."

The master gave instructions to the assembly, saying: "Students of the Way today need above all to trust themselves, and not go seeking outside, climbing up onto every idle speck of dust and circumstance, unable to distinguish the crooked from the correct at all. Even talk of 'there is a patriarch, there is a buddha' is only something within the traces of the teaching. The moment someone raises up a single phrase, whether it emerges veiled or plain, doubt springs up at once — casting about to heaven, casting about to earth, running from house to house asking questions, in a great fluster. A great man should not spend his days merely debating host and thief, right and wrong, form and wealth, and idle chatter. In this place of mine, I make no distinction between monastic and layperson — whoever comes, I recognize him completely, no matter where he comes from; whatever reputation or words of praise he carries are nothing but a dream and an illusion. What I do see is that the person who rides upon circumstance is himself the mysterious purport of all buddhas — a buddha-realm cannot itself proclaim 'I am a buddha-realm'; it is only this very person of no reliance who rides forth upon the circumstance. If someone comes forward and asks me to seek the Buddha, I respond by manifesting the circumstance of purity; asks about the bodhisattva, I respond by manifesting the circumstance of compassion; asks about bodhi, I respond by manifesting the circumstance of pure subtlety; asks about nirvāṇa, I respond by manifesting the circumstance of stillness. The circumstances themselves are ten thousand kinds of difference, but the person is not different at all — hence he responds to things and manifests form, like the moon reflected in water. Followers of the Way! If you want to attain what accords with the Dharma, you must be a great man to do it — go about limp and hesitant, and it cannot be done. A cracked vessel is not fit to hold ghee. One who is truly a great vessel need only refuse to be deceived, becoming master of himself wherever he stands, so that every place he stands becomes true; then whatever comes cannot be accepted by him at all. The instant a single doubt arises in your mind, Māra has entered your heart — just as, when a bodhisattva doubts, the demon of birth and death seizes the opportunity. You need only bring your thoughts to rest, and seek nothing further outside yourself; when something comes, simply illuminate it. Only trust in what is functioning right now — there is not even a single thing besides this to trust in. A single thought of your mind gives rise to the three realms, and, following conditions, is divided by circumstance into the six sense-objects. What is lacking in your own present activity, right now? In a single instant you enter the pure and enter the impure, enter Maitreya's tower and enter the land of the three eyes, traveling everywhere — yet you see only empty names."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This closing stretch performs its own argument: it names Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, and Avalokiteśvara — three of the most venerated bodhisattvas in the entire Mahāyāna pantheon — and immediately empties each name of any location outside "your own activity right in front of you now," going so far as to state flatly that there is no Mañjuśrī to be found even at Mount Wutai, his own most famous pilgrimage site. The chapter's final image, "the person who rides upon circumstance" answering "purity" to one questioner and "stillness" to another purely because each word was asked for, previews the fuller catalogue of "three eyes" and "three statements" Linji develops at length in Part 3, where the same insistence — that the response is generated by the question rather than fixed in advance — becomes explicit doctrine.
