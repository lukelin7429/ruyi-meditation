The Robe Is Not the Wearer

示眾（三）

The evening instruction closes: the fish hidden in a spring that strikes its own waves, Linji's own line of teachers across five masters who were each disbelieved and slandered in their own time, his practice of changing robes to teach a confused student that the wearer is never the robe, and the five deeds of unbroken retribution — killing one's own father and mother — turned entirely inward, ending in his one piece of first-person testimony about the years before he found a genuine teacher.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1985, recorded by Huiran Status: Part 4 of 7 complete Reading notes: Ru-Yi Meditation Center
This part covers
The corpse-carrier's wasted life; the fish hidden in a spring, and a threefold ranking of student capacity; Linji's own teaching lineage across five Tang masters; his practice of putting on and removing robes to teach that the wearer is not the robe; true buddha, true dharma, and true way as empty names; the five deeds of unbroken retribution reinterpreted entirely as inward events; and Linji's closing first-person testimony on the years before he found a genuine teacher.
Genre note
This part closes the long evening instruction (示眾) begun in Part 2. Part 5 shifts to a new genre — 勘辨, "testing and discernment" — short, often wordless contests between Linji and his fellow masters and students.

1. A Corpse-Carrier's Life, and the Fish Hidden in a Spring

Chinese Source

「大德！山僧如是所舉，皆是辨魔揀異，知其邪正。道流！寔情大難，佛法幽玄，解得可可地。山僧竟日與他說破，學者總不在意，千遍萬遍脚底踏過，黑沒焌地，無一箇形段，歷歷孤明。學人信不及，便向名句上生解，年登半百，秖管傍家負死屍行，檐却檐子天下走，索草鞋錢有日在。

「大德！山僧說向外無法。學人不會，便即向裏作解，便即倚壁坐，舌拄上齶，湛然不動，取此為是祖門佛法也。大錯，是爾若取不動清淨境為是，爾即認他無明為郎主。古人云：『湛湛黑暗深坑寔可怖畏。』此之是也。爾若認他動者是，一切草木皆解動，應可是道也。所以，動者是風大、不動者是地大，動與不動俱無自性。爾若向動處捉他，他向不動處立；爾若向不動處捉他，他向動處立。譬如潛泉魚鼓波而自躍。大德！動與不動是二種境，還是無依，道人用動、用不動。如諸方學人來，山僧此間作三種根器斷：如中下根器來，我便奪其境，而不除其法；或中上根器來，我便境法俱奪；如上上根器來，我便境法人俱不奪；如有出格見解人來，山僧此間便全體作用不歷根器。

「大德！到這裏學人著力處不通風，石火電光即過了也。學人若眼定動，即沒交涉，擬心即差，動念即乖。有人解者，不離目前。大德！爾檐鉢囊屎檐子，傍家走求佛求法，即今與麼馳求底，爾還識渠麼？活撥撥地，秖是勿根株，擁不聚、撥不散，求著即轉遠、不求還在目前。靈音屬耳，若人不信，徒勞百年。

「道流！一剎那間便入華藏世界、入毘盧遮那國土、入解脫國土、入神通國土、入清淨國土、入法界、入穢入淨、入凡入聖、入餓鬼畜生，處處討覓尋皆，不見有生、有死，唯有空名。幻化空花不勞把捉，得失是非一時放却。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Worthies! Everything this mountain monk has set out is for the sake of discerning Māra and telling apart what differs, so as to know the crooked from the correct. Followers of the Way! Genuine feeling is exceedingly hard to come by; the Buddhadharma is deep and mysterious, and to understand it is no small matter. This mountain monk explains it thoroughly all day long, yet students pay no real attention — trampling over it a thousand, ten thousand times, and still it remains dark and blank to them, with not one recognizable form, though it is vividly present and solitary and bright the whole time. Because the student cannot believe this, he forms his understanding right on top of names and phrases; by the time he reaches fifty, all he has done is carry a corpse from house to house, shouldering his own baggage as he runs about the world, and the day will come when he is asked to pay for the straw sandals he has worn out.

"Worthies! This mountain monk says there is no dharma to be found outside. Students, failing to understand this, go and form an understanding inside instead — sitting leaning against a wall, tongue pressed against the roof of the mouth, perfectly still and unmoving — and take this for the very Dharma of the patriarchal gate. A great mistake: take this unmoving, pure state to be correct, and you are simply recognizing ignorance itself as your true master. The ancients said: 'That vast, murky, dark pit is truly a fearful thing' — this is exactly it. Take instead what moves to be correct, and every plant and tree knows how to move, and so they too would have to be the Way. Hence: what moves is the element of wind, and what does not move is the element of earth, and neither moving nor not-moving has any nature of its own. Try to catch it where it moves, and it stands where it does not move; try to catch it where it does not move, and it stands where it moves — like a fish hidden in a spring, striking the waves and leaping up of itself. Worthies! Moving and not-moving are simply two kinds of circumstance; it is the person of no reliance who makes use of both. When students come to me, this mountain monk judges them by three kinds of capacity: for one of middling-to-lower capacity, I take away his circumstance but not his dharma; for one of middling-to-higher capacity, I take away both circumstance and dharma; for one of the very highest capacity, I take away neither circumstance, dharma, nor person; and for someone of truly exceptional understanding, this mountain monk simply acts with the whole body at once, without regard to capacity at all.

"Worthies! When you reach this point, there is no room at all for the student to apply effort — the spark from flint, the flash of lightning, and it is already past. If the student's eyes so much as flicker, it has nothing to do with the matter any longer; the instant a thought forms, it is already wrong. Anyone who understands this does not depart from what is right in front of him. Worthies! You go about shouldering your begging bowl and your bag of filth, running from house to house seeking the Buddha, seeking the Dharma — but this very act of running and seeking right now, do you recognize the one who is doing it? Vividly alive, it simply has no root or stem; gather it, and it will not collect; scatter it, and it will not disperse. Seek it, and it only recedes further; stop seeking, and it remains right in front of you. Its numinous sound reaches the ear on its own — if a person does not believe this, he will labor in vain for a hundred years.

"Followers of the Way! In a single instant you enter the Flower Treasury World, enter Vairocana's land, enter the land of liberation, the land of supernatural power, the land of purity, the dharma-realm, the impure and the pure, the ordinary and the sage, the realms of hungry ghosts and animals — searching everywhere for them, you will find no birth and no death anywhere, only empty names. Illusory transformations, flowers in the sky — do not trouble yourself to grasp at them; gain and loss, right and wrong — let them all go at once."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The image of "the fish hidden in a spring, striking the waves and leaping up of itself" is Linji's answer to a trap he names explicitly: whichever side of "moving" and "not-moving" a student fixes on as the correct one, the thing sought simply relocates to the other side — a structural point rather than advice about meditation posture, aimed squarely at students who had turned wall-gazing itself into a doctrine, "recognizing ignorance as their true master." The threefold ranking of student capacity that follows makes explicit what Part 2's fourfold "taking away" scheme implied all along: it is a diagnostic tool applied differently to different listeners, never a single fixed teaching delivered the same way twice.

2. Linji's Own Lineage

Chinese Source

「道流！山僧佛法的的相承，從麻谷和尚、丹霞和尚、道一和尚、廬山拽石頭和尚，一路行遍天下，無人信得，盡皆起謗。如道一和尚用處純一無雜，學人三百、五百盡皆不見他意。如廬山和尚自在真正順逆用處，學人不測涯際，悉皆忙然。如丹霞和尚翫珠隱顯，學人來者皆悉被罵。如麻谷用處苦如黃蘗，近皆不得。如石鞏用處向箭頭上覓人，來者皆懼。如山僧今日用處真正成壞，翫弄神變，入一切境，隨處無事，境不能換。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Followers of the Way! This mountain monk's Buddhadharma has been genuinely transmitted, one lineage traveling the whole world — from Reverend Magu, Reverend Danxia, Reverend Daoyi, and Reverend Shitou of Mount Lu who dragged stones — yet nowhere has anyone truly believed it, and everywhere slander has arisen instead. Reverend Daoyi's own activity was pure and unmixed, yet three hundred, five hundred students never once grasped his meaning. The Reverend of Mount Lu's activity was free and genuine, going with or against as needed, yet students could never fathom its limits, and were all left at a loss. Reverend Danxia's playing with the pearl, now hidden, now shown, left every student who came to him thoroughly scolded. Reverend Magu's activity was as bitter as Huangbo's, and hardly anyone could get close to it. Reverend Shigong's activity sought out a person at the very point of an arrow, and everyone who came to him was afraid. As for this mountain monk's own activity today, it is genuinely both building up and breaking down, playing freely with supernatural transformation, entering every circumstance, at ease wherever I stand, never to be exchanged by any circumstance at all."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This is Linji's own account of the teaching line running through him — Mazu Daoyi (道一和尚), his disciples Shitou-lineage master "the Reverend of Mount Lu" and Danxia Tianran, and Magu Baoche and Shigong Huizang, two more of Mazu's direct heirs, though Linji's actual dharma-transmission runs instead through Huangbo Xiyun, mentioned here only in passing ("as bitter as Huangbo's"). The insistence that every single one of these masters "was disbelieved" and met with slander in his own lifetime reframes rejection itself as a mark of authenticity, echoing the same claim made earlier in Part 3's teaching on good teachers no one at first believes.

3. Changing Robes

Chinese Source

「但有來求者，我即便出看渠，渠不識我，我便著數般衣，學人生解一向入我言句：『苦哉，瞎禿子無眼人把我著底衣認青、黃、赤、白，我脫却入清淨境中。』學人一見便生忻欲。我又脫却，學人失心，忙然狂走，言：『我無衣。』我即向渠道：『爾識我著衣底人否？』忽爾回頭，認我了也。『大德！爾莫認衣，衣不能動，人能著衣，有箇清淨衣、有箇無生衣、菩提衣、涅槃衣、有祖衣、有佛衣。大德！但有聲名文句，皆悉是衣變，從臍輪氣海中鼓激、牙齒敲磕成其句義，明知是幻化。大德！外發聲語業、內表心所法，以思有念，皆悉是衣。爾秖麼認他著底衣為寔解，縱經塵劫秖是衣通，三界循還輪回生死；不如無事，相逢不相識、共語不知名。今時學人不得，蓋為認名字為解，大策子上抄死老漢語，三重五重複子裹，不教人見，道是玄旨，以為保重。大錯，瞎屢生！爾向枯骨上覓什麼汁？有一般不識好惡，向教中取意度商量成於句義，如把屎塊子向口裏含了吐過與別人。猶如俗人打傳口令相似，一生虛過也。道我出家，被他問著佛法，便即杜口無詞，眼似漆突、口如楄檐，如此之類逢彌勒出世，移置他方世界寄地獄受苦。大德！爾波波地往諸方覓什麼物？踏爾脚板闊，無佛可求、無道可成、無法可得。外求有相佛，與汝不相似，欲識汝本心，非合亦非離。』」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Whenever someone comes seeking me, I go out to see him, and since he does not recognize me, I put on various kinds of robes. The student, forming his own understanding, plunges straight into my words, thinking: 'How pitiful — this blind, shave-pated, eyeless man takes the robe I am wearing for blue, yellow, red, or white; I will take it off and enter a pure state instead.' The moment he sees this, he grows delighted and eager. I then take that robe off as well, and the student, losing his composure, runs about in a frenzy, saying: 'I have no robe!' I then say to him: 'Do you recognize the person who wears the robe?' All at once he turns his head, and recognizes me at last. 'Worthies! Do not mistake the robe for the wearer — a robe cannot move on its own; it is the person who wears a robe. There is a robe of purity, a robe of no-birth, a robe of bodhi, a robe of nirvāṇa, a robe of the patriarchs, a robe of the buddhas. Worthies! Any reputation, any name, any phrase whatsoever is nothing but a change of robe, stirred up from the sea of breath at the navel-wheel, struck into shape by the clashing of the teeth — clearly nothing but an illusory transformation. Worthies! What is expressed outwardly as speech, and manifested inwardly as a mental factor — thought accompanied by consciousness — all of this alike is simply a robe. You go and mistake the very robe someone is wearing for the real understanding itself; even after kalpas as numerous as dust motes, this is nothing but mastery of robes, circling endlessly through birth and death in the three realms. Better to have nothing to do — to meet without recognizing, to converse without knowing each other's name. Students today cannot manage this, precisely because they take recognizing names for understanding, copying the words of dead old men into a great notebook, wrapping it in three or five layers of cloth so that no one else may see it, calling it the mysterious purport, and treasuring it as something precious. A great mistake, blind fools! What juice do you expect to find by squeezing dry bones? There is a type who cannot tell good from bad, who take up the scriptures, work out some meaning by discussion, and form it into a phrase — exactly like taking a lump of excrement into one's mouth and spitting it out for someone else to eat. It is exactly like an ordinary person passing along a rumor, wasting an entire lifetime on it. They call themselves home-leavers, yet the moment someone questions them about the Buddhadharma, their mouths clamp shut with nothing to say, their eyes as dark as lacquer, their mouths hanging like a plank across a door — people of this kind, when Maitreya appears in the world, will be moved off to some other world entirely and consigned to suffering in hell. Worthies! Why do you run about everywhere in such a fluster looking for something? You wear the soles of your feet flat with walking, yet there is no Buddha to seek, no Way to accomplish, no Dharma to attain. Seeking outwardly for a buddha with marks, you will find nothing resembling your own self at all; if you want to recognize your own original mind, it is neither united with anything nor separate from anything.'"

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This is one of only two moments in the entire text where Linji describes staging a deliberate teaching device rather than simply reacting to whoever walks in — putting on and removing robes until a confused student finally asks the right question, "do you recognize the person who wears the robe?" The extended catalogue of robes named afterward (of purity, of no-birth, of bodhi, of nirvāṇa, of the patriarchs, of the buddhas) turns nearly every honorific term in the Buddhist vocabulary into "a change of robe" rather than a fixed attainment — the same deflationary move Part 3 applied to the six supernatural powers, here extended to the entire vocabulary of praise itself.

4. True Buddha, True Dharma, True Way

Chinese Source

「道流！真佛無形、真道無體、真法無相，三法混融和合一處，辨既不得，喚作忙忙業識眾生。」

問：「如何是真佛？真法？真道？乞垂開示。」師云：「佛者，心清淨是；法者，心光明是；道者，處處無礙淨光是。三即一，皆是空名，而無寔有，如真正學道人念念心不間斷。自達磨大師從西土來，秖是覓箇不受人惑底人。後遇二祖，一言便了，始知從前虛用功夫。山僧今日見處與祖佛不別，若第一句中得，與祖佛為師；若第二句中得，與人天為師；若第三句中得，自救不了。」

問：「如何是西來意？」師云：「若有意，自救不了。」云：「既無意，云何二祖得法？」師云：「得者是不得。」云：「既若不得，云何是不得底意？」師云：「為爾向一切處馳求，心不能歇。所以，祖師言：『咄哉，丈夫！將頭覓頭。』儞言下便自回光返照，更不別求。知身心與祖佛不別，當下無事，方名得法。

「大德！山僧今時事不獲已，話度說出許多不才淨。爾且莫錯，據我見處，寔無許多般道理，要用便用、不用便休。秖如諸方說六度萬行以為佛法，我道是莊嚴門、佛事門，非是佛法，乃至持齋、持戒，擎油不㴸，道眼不明，盡須抵債，索飯錢有日在。何故如此？入道不通理，復身還信施。長者八十一，其樹不生耳，乃至孤峯獨宿，一食卯齋，長坐不臥，六時行道皆是造業底人，乃至頭、目、髓、腦、國城、妻子、象、馬、七珍盡皆捨施，如是等見皆是苦身心故，還招苦果，不如無事純一無雜。乃至十地滿心菩薩皆求此道流蹤跡了不可得，所以諸天歡喜，地神捧足，十方諸佛無不稱歎。緣何如此？為今聽法道人用處無蹤跡。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Followers of the Way! The true buddha has no form; the true way has no substance; the true dharma has no mark. These three dharmas mingle and merge into a single place, and once you can no longer even discern them apart, this is called a sentient being of confused, busy consciousness."

Someone asked: "What are the true buddha, the true dharma, and the true way? Please instruct us." The master said: "'Buddha' is simply a pure mind; 'dharma' is simply a luminous mind; 'way' is simply pure light unobstructed everywhere. The three are really one, all of them empty names with nothing real to correspond to them — this is how it is for a person truly studying the Way, whose mind does not break off moment after moment. When the great teacher Bodhidharma came from the Western lands, he sought only a person who would not be deceived by others. Later he met the Second Patriarch, and a single word was enough — only then did the Second Patriarch know that all his earlier efforts had been wasted. This mountain monk's own understanding today is no different from the patriarchs and buddhas: grasp it at the level of the first statement, and you become teacher to patriarchs and buddhas themselves; grasp it at the second, and you become teacher to gods and men; grasp it at the third, and you cannot even save yourself."

Someone asked: "What was the intent of the Patriarch's coming from the West?" The master said: "If there were any intent at all, he could not even save himself." The questioner said: "If there was no intent, then how did the Second Patriarch attain the Dharma?" The master said: "'Attaining' is precisely 'not attaining.'" The questioner said: "If it is truly not attaining, then what is the meaning of 'not attaining'?" The master said: "It is because you go running after everything everywhere, and your mind cannot come to rest. That is why the Patriarch said: 'Ah, you fool! Carrying your own head, still searching for your head!' The instant you hear these words, turn your own light back upon yourself, and seek nothing further elsewhere. Know that body and mind are no different from the patriarchs and buddhas, and that right now, this instant, there is nothing the matter — only then is it called attaining the Dharma.

"Worthies! This mountain monk, having no choice in the matter, has spoken at such length about so many things not truly worth mentioning. Do not mistake this for something elaborate — as I see it, there is truly no such array of principles: use it when you need it, and let it be when you do not. As for what people everywhere call the six perfections and the ten thousand practices, taking them for the Buddhadharma, I say these are the gate of adornment, the gate of buddha-works, not the Buddhadharma itself; even keeping the fast, keeping the precepts, carrying a bowl of oil without spilling a drop — if the eye of the Way is not clear, all of it must be repaid one day, and the day will come to pay for the rice one has eaten. Why is this so? Because entering the Way without penetrating its principle only turns the body back to living off the alms of the faithful. An old man of eighty-one whose tree no longer grows leaves; solitary dwelling on a lonely peak, one meal at dawn, sitting for long stretches without ever lying down, performing the six periods of walking practice — all such people are simply creating karma; even giving away one's own head, eyes, marrow, brain, kingdom, city, wife, children, elephants, horses, and the seven treasures — views of this kind are all merely afflicting body and mind, and will only bring suffering as their fruit. Better to have nothing to do, pure and unmixed with anything else. Even a bodhisattva whose mind is full at the tenth ground, searching for the very tracks of this follower of the Way, could never find them — and so the gods rejoice, the earth-spirits hold up his feet, and the buddhas of the ten directions all without exception praise him. Why is this so? Because the activity of this person of the Way, listening to the Dharma right now, leaves no track at all."

Reading note — after Zhizhao's 人天眼目 (T2006)

This is the second time this text states the "three statements" ranking first stated in Part 1 (Section 10) — here in prose rather than verse, confirming that grasping the teaching at its deepest level makes one "teacher to patriarchs and buddhas," at its middle level "teacher to gods and men," and at its shallowest level unable even to save oneself. Bodhidharma's exchange with Huike, the Second Patriarch — reduced here to "attaining is precisely not attaining" — is the same founding transmission story this site's Platform Sūtra and Song of Enlightenment both take for granted as the root of the entire Chan lineage Linji claims to inherit.

5. The Five Deeds of Unbroken Retribution, Reinterpreted

Chinese Source

問：「大通智勝佛十劫坐道場，佛法不現前，不得成佛道。未審此意如何？乞師指示。」師云：「大通者，是自己，於處處達其萬法無性、無相，名為大通。智勝者，於一切處不疑、不得一法，名為智勝。佛者，心清淨，光明透徹法界，得名為佛。十劫坐道場者，十波羅密是。佛法不現前者，佛本不生、法本不滅，云何更有現前？不得成佛道者，佛不應更作佛。古人云：『佛常在世間，而不染世間法。』道流！爾欲得作佛，莫隨萬物。心生種種法生、心滅種種法滅，一心不生，萬法無咎。世與出世，無佛、無法，亦不現前、亦不曾失。設有者，皆是名言章句，接引小兒施設藥病，表顯名句。且名句不自名句，還是爾目前昭昭靈靈鑒覺聞知照燭底，安一切名句。大德！造五無間業方得解脫。」

問：「如何是五無間業？」師云：「殺父、害母、出佛身血、破和合僧、焚燒經像等，此是五無間業。」云：「如何是父？」師云：「無明是父，爾一念心求起滅處不得，如響應空，隨處無事，名為殺父。」云：「如何是母？」師云：「貪愛為母，爾一念心入欲界中求其貪愛，唯見諸法空相，處處無著，名為害母。」云：「如何是出佛身血？」師云：「爾向清淨法界中無一念心生解，便處處黑暗，是出佛身血。」云：「如何是破和合僧？」師云：「爾一念心正達煩惱結使，如空無所依，是破和合僧。」云：「如何是焚燒經像？」師云：「見因緣空、心空、法空，一念決定斷，逈然無事，便是焚燒經像。大德！若如是達得，免被他凡聖名礙。爾一念心秖向空拳指上生寔解，根境法中虛揑怪，自輕而退屈，言：『我是凡夫，他是聖人。』禿屢生！有甚死急？披他師子皮，却作野干鳴。大丈夫漢不作丈夫氣息，自家屋裏物不肯信，秖麼向外覓，上他古人閑名句，倚陰博陽，不能特達，逢境便緣、逢塵便執，觸處惑起，自無准定。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Someone asked: "The Buddha of Great Penetration and Supreme Victory sat in the place of enlightenment for ten kalpas, yet the Buddhadharma did not manifest before him, and he could not accomplish the buddha-way. I am not clear what this means — please, master, instruct me." The master said: "'Great Penetration' is simply yourself, penetrating everywhere the fact that the ten thousand dharmas have no nature and no mark. 'Supreme Victory' is having no doubt anywhere, and attaining not a single dharma. 'Buddha' means a pure mind whose light penetrates the entire dharma-realm. 'Sitting in the place of enlightenment for ten kalpas' means the ten perfections. 'The Buddhadharma did not manifest before him' means the Buddha was never born to begin with, and the Dharma never ceases to begin with — so how could there be anything further to manifest? 'He could not accomplish the buddha-way' means the Buddha ought not become a buddha all over again. The ancients said: 'The Buddha is always present in the world, yet is not stained by any worldly dharma.' Followers of the Way! If you want to become a buddha, do not follow after the ten thousand things. When mind arises, every kind of dharma arises; when mind ceases, every kind of dharma ceases. Let the one mind not arise, and the ten thousand dharmas are without fault. Whether worldly or world-transcending, there is no Buddha and no Dharma; nothing ever manifests, and nothing has ever been lost. Even supposing there were something, it would only be names, words, and phrases, set up as medicine to lead children along, statements that merely point things out. Yet even these names and phrases do not name themselves — it is you, right in front of me, brilliantly, luminously perceiving, aware, hearing, knowing, and illuminating, who fix all these names and phrases in place. Worthies! Only by committing the five deeds of unbroken retribution do you attain liberation."

Someone asked: "What are the five deeds of unbroken retribution?" The master said: "Killing one's father, harming one's mother, drawing a buddha's blood, destroying the harmony of the saṅgha, and burning the scriptures and images — these are the five deeds of unbroken retribution." The questioner said: "What is 'father'?" The master said: "Ignorance is the father. The instant a single thought of your mind seeks the place where arising and ceasing occur and cannot find it — like an echo answering into empty space, with nothing the matter wherever it goes — this is called 'killing the father.'" The questioner said: "What is 'mother'?" The master said: "Craving and attachment are the mother. The instant a single thought of your mind enters the realm of desire seeking after this craving, and sees only that all dharmas are marked with emptiness, clinging to nothing anywhere — this is called 'harming the mother.'" The questioner said: "What is 'drawing a buddha's blood'?" The master said: "The instant a single thought forms any understanding at all within the pure dharma-realm, everywhere at once turns to darkness — this is 'drawing a buddha's blood.'" The questioner said: "What is 'destroying the harmony of the saṅgha'?" The master said: "The instant a single thought of your mind truly penetrates the bonds and fetters of affliction, finding them like empty space with nothing to rely on — this is 'destroying the harmony of the saṅgha.'" The questioner said: "What is 'burning the scriptures and images'?" The master said: "Seeing that causes and conditions are empty, that mind is empty, that dharmas are empty, and with a single thought cutting this off decisively, utterly free with nothing the matter — this is 'burning the scriptures and images.' Worthies! Penetrate it this way, and you will be free of the obstruction of the very names 'ordinary' and 'sage.' Yet you go on forming a real understanding out of a mere empty fist or pointing finger, conjuring up strange notions among roots, objects, and dharmas, belittling yourself and shrinking back, saying: 'I am only an ordinary person; he is a sage.' Blind fools! What is the desperate hurry? You put on the lion's own skin, only to yelp like a jackal. A great man does not carry himself like a great man — unwilling to trust the very thing in his own house, he goes seeking outside instead, climbing onto the idle names and phrases of the ancients, leaning on shade to bargain for sunlight, never able to reach through to anything, seizing on whatever circumstance he meets, clinging to whatever speck of dust he encounters, confusion arising at every contact, with no fixed ground of his own anywhere."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The five deeds of unbroken retribution (五無間業) are, in standard Buddhist doctrine, the five most severe acts a person can commit, each said to guarantee immediate rebirth in the Avīci hell with no interval (無間) of relief. Linji takes the most horrifying list available in the entire tradition and reinterprets every item as an inward event with no victim at all — "killing the father" is dissolving ignorance, "burning the scriptures" is cutting off attachment to emptiness itself — the same rhetorical strategy as "kill the Buddha, kill the patriarch" in Part 3, now applied to acts a listener could not possibly hear as anything but atrocity on first mention.

6. Mañjuśrī's Sword, and Linji's Own Testimony

Chinese Source

「道流！莫取山僧說處。何故？說無憑據。一期間圖畫虛空，如彩畫像等喻。道流！莫將佛為究竟，我見猶如廁孔。菩薩、羅漢盡是枷鎖、縛人底物。所以，文殊仗劍殺於瞿曇、鴦掘持刀害於釋氏。道流！無佛可得，乃至三乘、五性、圓頓教迹皆是一期藥病相治，並無實法。設有，皆是相似，表顯路布，文字差排，且如是說。

「道流！有一般禿子便向裏許著功，擬求出世之法，錯了也。若人求佛，是人失佛；若人求道，是人失道；若人求祖，是人失祖。大德莫錯，我且不取爾解經論、我亦不取爾國王大臣、我亦不取爾辯似懸河、我亦不取爾聰明智慧，唯要爾真正見解。

「道流！設解得百本經論，不如一箇無事底阿師。爾解得——即輕蔑他人、勝負修羅、人我無明、長地獄業，如善星比丘解十二分教，生身陷地獄，大地不容。不如無事休歇去，飢來喫飯、睡來合眼，愚人笑我、智乃知焉。道流！莫向文字中求，心動疲勞，吸冷氣無益，不如一念緣起無生，超出三乘權學菩薩。

「大德！莫因循過日。山僧往日未有見處時，黑漫漫地，光陰不可空過，腹熱心忙，奔波訪道，後還得力，始到今日共道流如是話度。勸諸道流莫為衣食，看世界易過，善知識難遇，如優曇花時一現耳。

「儞諸方聞道有箇臨濟老漢出來，便擬問難，教語不得，被山僧全體作用。學人空開得眼，口總動不得，懵然不知以何答我。我向伊道：『龍象蹴踏，非驢所堪。』爾諸處秖指胸點肋，道我解禪、解道，三箇兩箇到這裏不奈何。咄哉，爾將這箇身心到處簸兩片皮誑謼閭閻，喫鐵棒有日在，非出家兒，盡向阿修羅界攝。

「夫如至理之道，非諍論而求激揚，鏗鏘以摧外道，至於佛祖相承更無別意。設有言教，落在化儀三乘、五性、人天因果，如圓頓之教，又且不然，童子善財皆不求過。大德！莫錯用心，如大海不停死屍，秖麼擔却擬天下走，自起見障以礙於心。日上無雲，麗天普照，眼中無翳、空裏無花。道流！爾欲得如法，但莫生疑，展則彌綸法界，收則絲髮不立，歷歷孤明未曾欠少。眼不見、耳不聞，喚作什麼物？古人云：『說似一物則不中。』爾但自家看，更有什麼。說亦無盡，各自著力。珍重。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Followers of the Way! Do not cling to what this mountain monk says. Why not? Because speech has no basis to stand on — it is only a temporary sketch drawn in empty space, like a painted likeness used for illustration. Followers of the Way! Do not take the Buddha as the ultimate — as I see it, he is no different from a latrine hole. Bodhisattvas and arhats are all no more than cangues and chains, things that bind people. That is why Mañjuśrī took up his sword to kill Gautama, and Aṅgulimāla took up his blade to harm Śākyamuni himself. Followers of the Way! There is no Buddha to be attained; even the three vehicles, the five natures, and the traces of the perfect and sudden teaching are all only medicine matched for the moment to a particular sickness, with no real, fixed dharma among them. Even supposing there were something, it would only be an approximation, a mere pointing-out, a laying-out of written words — that is simply how it is put into words.

"Followers of the Way! There is a type of shave-pated fool who applies himself intently on the inside, seeking a method for transcending the world — a mistake. If a person seeks the Buddha, that very person loses the Buddha; if a person seeks the Way, that very person loses the Way; if a person seeks a patriarch, that very person loses the patriarch. Worthies, do not make this mistake — I set no store at all by your understanding of the sūtras and treatises, nor by your being a king or a minister, nor by your debating as fluently as a river in flood, nor even by your cleverness and intelligence; the only thing I require of you is a true and correct understanding.

"Followers of the Way! Even if you could master a hundred volumes of sūtras and treatises, it would not equal being one ordinary monk with nothing to do. Master them, and you only end up despising others, competing for victory like the fighting asuras, caught in the ignorance of self and other, and lengthening the karma that leads to hell — like the monk Sunakṣatra, who understood the twelve divisions of the teachings perfectly well and yet fell alive into hell, with the whole earth itself unable to hold him. Better to have nothing to do and simply rest — eat when hungry, close your eyes when sleepy. The foolish laugh at me for saying this, but the wise understand. Followers of the Way! Do not go seeking inside written words — it only stirs the mind and wearies it, like sucking in cold air to no benefit at all. Better to see, in a single thought, that dependent arising is itself unborn, and so surpass even the provisional bodhisattvas of the three vehicles.

"Worthies! Do not idle your days away out of habit. In the days before this mountain monk had any understanding at all, everything was a vast, dark blank; I could not bear to let the time pass empty, and so, with a burning stomach and an anxious heart, I ran about seeking the Way — and only afterward did I gain any real strength from it, arriving at last at today, when I can speak with you, followers of the Way, in this manner. I urge every one of you not to labor merely for food and clothing: see how easily the world passes by, and how hard it is to meet a genuinely good teacher — as rare as the udumbara flower blooming for a single instant.

"You all, everywhere, have heard there is some old fellow called Linji, and so you come intending to press him with difficult questions, to make his teaching fail — only to find yourselves caught by this mountain monk's whole being acting at once. The student's eyes open uselessly, his mouth cannot move at all, and he is left utterly at a loss, not knowing how to answer me. I say to him: 'Only a dragon or an elephant can trample like this — no donkey could bear it.' Everywhere you go, you merely point at your own chest and tap your ribs, saying you understand Chan, you understand the Way — but two or three of you, brought right here, can do nothing at all. Ah, you carry this body and mind of yours everywhere, flapping two pieces of skin, deceiving the whole village — the day will come when you must eat the iron staff for it. You are no true home-leavers at all; every one of you belongs entirely to the realm of the asuras.

"As for the way of ultimate principle, it is not to be sought through argument and disputation, through clashing words meant to crush rival teachings — the transmission from patriarch to patriarch and from buddha to buddha has never had any other intent than this. Where there is verbal teaching at all, it falls within the provisional forms of the three vehicles, the five natures, and the causes and effects of gods and men; even the perfect and sudden teaching is no different — the boy Sudhana himself never went seeking beyond this. Worthies! Do not misapply your minds — like the great ocean, which does not harbor a single corpse, you go on carrying your own corpse about, trying to run across the whole world with it, only raising up your own obstruction of views to block your own mind. When the sun rises with no cloud in the sky, its bright light shines on everything equally; there is no film in the eye, and no flower in the empty sky. Followers of the Way! If you want to attain what accords with the Dharma, simply give rise to no doubt: unfurled, it fills the entire dharma-realm; gathered in, not even a single hair's breadth remains standing — vividly present, solitary, and bright, never lacking in anything at all. The eye does not see it, the ear does not hear it — what should we call it? The ancients said: 'To liken it to any one thing at all is already to miss the mark.' Simply look for yourself — what more is there? Speaking of it could never be exhausted. Each of you must make your own effort. Take care."

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Mañjuśrī's sword "killing Gautama" and Aṅgulimāla's blade "harming Śākyamuni" both refer to stories from the wider Mahāyāna canon in which extreme, even violent-sounding acts turn out, on closer reading, to be liberative rather than harmful — a precedent Linji invokes to license his own "kill the Buddha" language from Part 3. This closing testimony is one of only two first-person passages in the entire evening instruction (with Part 3's "twelve years" remark) where Linji speaks about his own history rather than issuing instruction, and its account of "a vast, dark blank" before he found a genuine teacher previews the fuller narrative told in Part 6's biographical record of his training under Huangbo. The instruction ends, appropriately, by refusing its own closing word: "to liken it to any one thing at all is already to miss the mark."

Part 4 Complete

This closes Part 4, and with it the long evening instruction to the assembly begun in Part 2. Part 5 shifts to an entirely different genre — 勘辨, "testing and discernment" — short, often wordless contests between Linji and his fellow Chan masters, his own students, and the eccentric wandering monk Puhua, ending with Puhua's own extraordinary death.
