The Preface and the Ten Meanings

Siming Zhili (960–1028), the leading Tiantai master of the Northern Song, wrote these Notes on the Profound Meaning of Guanyin, Zhiyi's lecture on the "Universal Gate" chapter of the Lotus Sūtra as recorded by Guanding. In a short preface dated 1021, Zhili says he is writing down what he heard from his teacher Baoyun. This first fascicle begins with the title and authorship of the Profound Meaning and then reads Zhiyi's preface phrase by phrase. Zhili explains Avalokiteśvara's true and responsive bodies through the perfectly merged dharma-realm, the inherent three thousand, and the meeting of capacity and response. He then covers the bodhisattva's past and future names and reads "contemplating the sounds of the world" as object and wisdom. The rest of the fascicle covers the general explanation of the name. It explains why ten paired meanings are listed and how they follow one another, first in contemplation and then through the Five Periods and four teachings. The first two pairs are then explained in full. Under person and dharma, Zhili sets out the four gates of the lesser vehicle, Mahāyāna emptiness, and the three causes of buddha-nature, "neither identical to the six dharmas nor apart from them." Under loving-kindness and compassion, he explains the four universal vows in each of the four teachings.

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Zhili, a śramaṇa of Siming, of the Song

Chinese Source

知禮俯伏惟念，早年慕學投跡寶雲，遇授法師講說此品，神根既鈍遂數諮疑。先師念我學勤，不辭提耳，故所說義麁記在心。昔同聞人今各衰朽，慮乎先見不益後昆，共勉不才抄錄於世。但疑識暗謬有所傳，圓宗哲人刊正是望。時天禧五年歲在辛酉八月一日絕筆故序。

Ru-Yi Translation

I, Zhili, prostrate and reflect: in my early years, longing to learn, I entrusted myself to Baoyun, and there met the master who conferred [the teaching on me] lecturing on this chapter. My spiritual faculties being dull, I questioned him many times about my doubts. My late teacher, mindful of my diligence in learning, did not decline to pull me by the ear, so I have kept the meaning he taught roughly recorded in my mind. Those who heard it with me in the past are now each worn with age; concerned that what was formerly seen would not benefit later generations, we encouraged one another, and I, lacking talent, have copied it out for the world. Only I fear that, my understanding being dim, I may have transmitted errors; I hope the wise ones of the perfect school will correct them. Written to the last stroke of the brush on the first day of the eighth month of the fifth year of Tianxi, the year being xinyou; hence this preface.

The Title, the Speaker, and the Recorder

Chinese Source

觀音玄義，從略標之，具存應云觀世音菩薩普門品玄義。以其序中及以正文具明人法，故且略標。言玄義者，能釋之義門也。玄者，幽微難見之稱。義者，深有所以也。斯蓋大師以三昧力徹法性際，深見今品人法之意也。應知名等五義皆悉幽微，七方便人智莫能見。

Ru-Yi Translation

Profound Meaning of Guanyin: it is titled in abbreviated form; set out in full it should be called Profound Meaning of the Universal Gate Chapter of Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva. Because both its preface and its main text fully explain person and dharma, it is titled in brief for now. "Profound meaning" refers to the gates of meaning that do the explaining. "Profound" is a term for what is hidden, subtle, and hard to see. "Meaning" is what has deep reasons. This is because the Great Master, by the power of samādhi, penetrated to the limit of the Dharma-nature and saw deeply the intent of the person and dharma of this chapter. One should know that the five meanings, the name and the rest, are all hidden and subtle; the wisdom of people of the seven expedient [stages] cannot see them.

Chinese Source

卷上者既有兩軸，乃以上下而甄別之。次示能說之人，即天台智者。既是門人記錄所說，故不敢正斥其法諱也。天台山者，即大師棲身入寂之所，故以此處顯其人也。若山之得名、居之所自，入滅相狀、滅後靈異，具於《大本》及《輔行》、《別傳》等文，今不備述。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The upper fascicle": since there are two scrolls, they are distinguished as upper and lower. Next it shows the one who spoke it, namely Zhizhe of Tiantai. Since this is a record made by his disciple of what he spoke, [the disciple] did not dare to state his Dharma-name directly. Mount Tiantai is the place where the Great Master dwelt and entered quiescence; hence this place is used to make the person known. As for how the mountain got its name, how he came to dwell there, the manner of his entering extinction, and the marvels after his extinction, they are fully given in the Larger Version, the Fuxing, the Separate Biography, and other texts; they are not fully related here.

Ru-Yi Translation

"Zhizhe": when the Sui emperor had sought and received the great bodhisattva precepts, the master said, "Great King, you have condescended to follow the sacred prohibitions; you should be named Zongchi (Upholding All)." The king said, "The Bodhisattvabhūmi Sūtra says, 'One who transmits the lamp of the Buddha-Dharma is a wise one (zhizhe).' Since the master transmits the lamp, he may be named Zhizhe." From then on, in every letter he submitted, he wrote, "The disciple Zongchi pays homage to Zhizhe." "Great Master" is said because he was the teacher of emperors, kings, and great men; it is not the same as the titles conferred by appointment nowadays. "Spoken" (shuo) means "delighted" (yue): giving free rein to the eloquence of delight in speaking and to a heart delighted in wondrous awakening, unlike the various masters who gathered from sūtras and treatises to compose commentaries and explain the sūtra text. Hence Dharma Master Daren, seeing Zhizhe expound the Dharma, sighed before the assembly: "This is not what written commentaries contain; it is eloquence given free rein by contemplating capacities. Prajñā is neither dull nor sharp; sharp and dull depend on conditions. Its richness suiting the occasion — this is its sharp aspect; the pool is deep and the flowers large — its dull aspect can be grasped by the mind." The recorder was the Venerable Zhang'an; his understanding and practice, his marvels, and the events of his life from beginning to end are fully displayed in his own biography.

The Preface: The True and Responsive Bodies

Chinese Source

釋文為二：初、釋序文，二：初、敘真應益物，二：初、正明真應，二：初、示二身妙用，三：初、明體妙故二用泯亡，二：初、法融應泯。「法界圓融」者，色心依正以即性故，趣指一法遍攝一切。諸法遍攝亦復如是，法法互遍皆無際畔，乃以無界而為其界。此之法界無不圓融，即百界千如、百如千界也，是故得云唯色唯心、唯依唯正；若不爾者即非圓融。觀音證此以為本體，全此妙體而起應像，以法界應赴法界機，亦是以法界機感法界應。法界無二能所自忘，感應尚忘，體用寧異？故雖設應，無應可存，故云「像無所像」。

Ru-Yi Translation

The explanation of the text has two parts: first, explaining the preface, in two: first, narrating how the true and the responsive benefit beings, in two: first, explaining the true and the responsive directly, in two: first, showing the wondrous function of the two bodies, in three: first, explaining that because the essence is wondrous the two functions dissolve and vanish, in two: first, the dharma merged and the response dissolved. "The dharma-realm is perfectly merged": because form and mind, the dependent and the principal, are identical to the nature, any one dharma one points to universally includes all. All dharmas universally include in just the same way; dharma after dharma mutually pervade, all without boundary, so that the boundless is taken as its realm. This dharma-realm is in every respect perfectly merged; that is, the hundred realms and thousand suchnesses, the hundred suchnesses and thousand realms. Therefore one can say: only form, only mind, only the dependent, only the principal. If it were not so, it would not be perfectly merged. Avalokiteśvara realizes this and takes it as the fundamental essence; with the whole of this wondrous essence he gives rise to responsive images, responding with the dharma-realm to the capacities of the dharma-realm; this is also the capacities of the dharma-realm appealing to the response of the dharma-realm. The dharma-realm being nondual, subject and object are forgotten of themselves; when even appeal and response are forgotten, how could essence and function differ? Hence, though a response is set up, no response remains; hence it says, "it images, yet there is nothing imaged."

二、「真如」下，性淨真忘。「真如清淨」者，《起信論》云「真如者，所謂心性不生不滅。是故一切法從本已來，離言說相、離名字相、離心緣相，畢竟平等，無有變異、不可破壞，唯是一心，故名真如。」又云「此真如體無有可遣，以一切法悉皆真故；亦無可立，以一切法皆同如故。既不可破立、自絕言想，則與河沙煩惱本不相應，故曰清淨。」觀音證此而為本體，即以此體示諸眾生，令觀行知或真似見，此知見者成伏斷益。若其未有此知見者，但能三業精進成機，亦離眾苦，悉得名化。此皆真身益物相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "True suchness" on: the nature pure, the true forgotten. "True suchness is pure": the Awakening of Faith says, "True suchness is the nature of mind, which neither arises nor ceases. Therefore all dharmas from the very beginning are apart from the mark of words, apart from the mark of names, apart from the mark of the mind's objects; they are ultimately equal, without change, indestructible; they are only the one mind, hence called true suchness." It also says, "This essence of true suchness has nothing to be removed, because all dharmas are true; nor is there anything to be established, because all dharmas are equally such. Since it can be neither refuted nor established, it is cut off of itself from words and thoughts, and so it is originally not in accord with afflictions as numerous as the sands of the river; hence it is called pure." Avalokiteśvara realizes this and takes it as the fundamental essence, and with this very essence shows beings, letting them know it in contemplative practice or see it in the true and resemblance [stages]; this knowing and seeing accomplishes the benefits of subduing and severing [delusion]. Those who do not yet have this knowing and seeing, simply by forming a capacity through diligence in the three kinds of action, are also freed from the many sufferings; all of this can be called transformation. These are all marks of the true body benefiting beings.

問：同緣曰應，欻有名化。此二種身皆非智德，今何以化而為真身？答：欻有之化即化現化也。今對像論化，取化轉化也。所以者何？上言於像則應化皆像，自實報下至地獄身皆已攝盡。若欲化轉凡賢入聖，須示真智，若非真身不能化轉。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: what accords with conditions is called response; what suddenly exists is called transformation. Neither of these two kinds of body is the virtue of wisdom; why now is transformation taken as the true body? Answer: transformation that suddenly exists is transformation as manifestation. Here transformation is discussed in contrast to images, taking transformation as converting [beings]. Why so? Above, speaking of images, both response and transformation are images; from the [land of] true reward down to the bodies in the hells, all are already included. If one wishes to transform and convert ordinary people and worthies into noble ones, one must show true wisdom; without the true body one cannot transform and convert them.

Ru-Yi Translation

"It transforms, yet there is nothing transformed": in terms of the nature being equal, he forgets the merit of transforming. Though he causes the nine destinies all to head for nirvāṇa, yet no being attains extinction. "In the equal, true dharma-realm, the Buddha does not save beings"; all day he transforms beings, all day there is no transforming.

二、「雖像」下，明用忘故二益周遍，二：初、顯益周。色心諸法雖無生性，因緣和合法爾而生。觀音妙證同諸法性，雖無形相，眾機扣之無像不現。此由絕於垂應之念，故能遍應法界群機。其猶明鑒無念而現，故云無所而不像。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Though it images" on: explaining that because the functions are forgotten the two benefits are complete and pervasive, in two: first, the manifest benefit complete. Dharmas of form and mind, though without a nature of arising, arise by their very nature when causes and conditions come together. Avalokiteśvara's wondrous realization is the same as the nature of dharmas; though without form or mark, when the many capacities knock on it, there is no image it does not manifest. This is because he is cut off from any thought of letting down a response, so he can respond universally to the many capacities of the dharma-realm. It is like a bright mirror that manifests [images] without thought; hence it says, "there is nothing it does not image."

Chinese Source

二、「化無」下，冥益遍。以上「雖」字貫此句初。雖中實性不可變化，不變而變，迷悟宛然。觀音順理雖知不變，常以真智化一切凡成不二聖，此則由無化物之念故，遍令他革迷成悟，其猶磁石無念而吸，故云無所而不化。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "though it transforms with nothing transformed" on: the hidden benefit pervasive. The word "though" above extends to the beginning of this phrase. Though the real nature of the middle cannot be changed or transformed, unchanging it changes, and delusion and awakening are plainly there. Avalokiteśvara, according with principle, though he knows it does not change, constantly with true wisdom transforms all ordinary beings into nondual sages. This is because he has no thought of transforming beings, so he universally causes others to reform delusion into awakening — like a lodestone that attracts without thought; hence it says, "there is nothing it does not transform."

Chinese Source

三、「故無」下，遮照相即結二身德相，二：初、應身相。中道法界雙遮二邊故無所在，當體雙照故無不在。「化應九道之身」者，此中云化，作欻有釋，并應成二，顯益相足也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "So it is nowhere" on: concluding the virtuous marks of the two bodies, negating and illuminating being identical, in two: first, the mark of the responsive body. The middle-way dharma-realm negates both extremes, hence it is "nowhere"; in its very essence it illuminates both, hence it is "nowhere absent." "It transforms in response into bodies in the nine destinies": "transformation" here is to be explained as sudden existence; together with response it makes two, showing that the marks of benefit are complete.

Chinese Source

問：經云「應以佛身得度，即現佛身。」今那云九？答：佛界身者有通有局，局在妙覺智相之身，三千實相以究盡故。尚非等覺心眼觀見，況乎下地及凡小耶？通則三教果頭之相，及以圓教凡聖所見，雖分麁妙皆名佛身。然是隨機應現之相，是其事識或是業識之所見故，雖是佛身而通二乘菩薩界攝。經文從通，故云現十；今文從局，故云九道。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: the sūtra says, "Should one be brought across by the form of a buddha, he at once manifests the form of a buddha." Why does it now say nine? Answer: the body of the buddha-realm has a general and a restricted [sense]. In the restricted sense, it lies in the body of the wisdom-mark of wondrous awakening, because the true mark of the three thousand is exhausted [there]. It is not seen even by the mind's eye of equal awakening, how much less by those of lower grounds, ordinary people, and the lesser [vehicles]! In the general sense, it is the mark at the fruition of the three teachings, as well as what the ordinary and noble of the perfect teaching see; though divided into coarse and wondrous, all are called buddha-bodies. But since these are marks manifested in response according to capacities, seen by the consciousness of phenomena or by the karmic consciousness, though they are buddha-bodies they are included in the realms of the two vehicles and bodhisattvas. The sūtra text follows the general [sense], so it speaks of manifesting ten; the present text follows the restricted, so it says the nine destinies.

Chinese Source

二、「處有」下，真身得。通以九界名之為有，以其皆有業報故也。應身雖乃處在其中，而其真智自冥極理，故云「寂入不二之旨」。前即真身而垂應相，此即應相而示真身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "it dwells in existence" on: the true body attained. The nine realms are generally called "existence" because they all have karmic retribution. Though the responsive body dwells among them, its true wisdom is of itself merged with the ultimate principle; hence it says, "[still,] it enters the purport of nonduality." The former [phrase] was the true body letting down responsive marks; this one is the responsive marks showing the true body.

Chinese Source

二、「是以」下，明兩用攝生。上明真應兩用既然，今示與拔攝生之相。初二句明真身拔苦，次二句示應身與樂。佛答前問，三業顯機感乎冥應，七難二求及以三毒盡諸苦際，故云「蒙脫苦崖」。佛答後問，三業顯應赴其冥機，三土眾生十重獲益，終歸祕藏，故云「使霑上樂」。然其四誓非專與樂，雖在此明，實通上句。以上三業即能感之因，此明四誓是能應之本，上下互顯，彼此無虧。應知三業亦通冥機，現在雖無，宿生須具。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "That is why" on: explaining the two functions gathering in beings. Above it explained that the two functions, true and responsive, are thus; now it shows the marks of giving and removing that gather in beings. The first two phrases explain the true body removing suffering; the next two show the responsive body giving happiness. In the Buddha's answer to the first question, the manifest capacity of the three kinds of action appeals to the hidden response; the seven calamities, the two wishes, and the three poisons exhaust the bounds of all suffering; hence it says, "they are freed from the shore of suffering." In the Buddha's answer to the second question, the manifest response of the three kinds of action goes to meet the hidden capacity; beings of the three lands obtain benefit in ten degrees and finally return to the secret treasury; hence it says, "it lets them be steeped in the highest bliss." Yet the four vows are not exclusively for giving happiness; though explained here, they actually extend to the preceding phrase. The three kinds of action above are the cause that appeals; the four vows explained here are the root that responds. Above and below reveal each other, and neither lacks anything. One should know that the three kinds of action also extend to hidden capacity: though absent in the present, they must be present from past lives.

Origin and Trace: The Names of Avalokiteśvara

Chinese Source

二、「故娑」下，兼明本跡，二：初、示諸名，二：初、今昔因名。今堪忍土稱無畏者，此經兩出。怨賊難中，一人唱言：「諸善男子！勿得恐怖。汝等應當一心稱觀世音名號，是菩薩能以無畏施於眾生。」乃至云「稱其名故即得解脫。」又勸供養中，佛自歎云：「是觀世音菩薩摩訶薩，於怖畏急難之中能施無畏。」是故此娑婆世界皆號之為施無畏者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "So in the Sahā world" on: explaining origin and trace as well, in two: first, showing the names, in two: first, the names in the cause, present and past. That in the present land of endurance he is called "Bestower of Fearlessness" — this sūtra gives it twice. In the peril of bandits, one person cries out: "Good sirs, do not be afraid! You should call, with a single mind, upon Avalokiteśvara's name — this bodhisattva is able to grant beings freedom from fear," and further, "Because they call his name, they are at once delivered." Again, in [the passage] exhorting offerings, the Buddha himself praises him: "This Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva, amid fear and urgent peril, is able to grant freedom from fear — for this reason, in this sahā world, he is called everywhere the Bestower of Fearlessness."

「寶藏」等者，《悲華經》云「過去散提嵐界善持劫中，時有佛出名曰寶藏。有轉輪王名無量淨，第一太子三月供佛及比丘僧，發菩提心：『若有眾生受三途等一切苦惱，若能念我稱我名字，為我天耳天眼聞見，不免苦者，我終不成無上菩提。』寶藏佛云：『汝觀一切眾生欲斷眾苦，故今字汝為觀世音。』」

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Buddha Jewel Treasury" and so on: the Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka Sūtra says: "In the past, in the world Santilan, in the eon Good Holding, a buddha appeared named Jewel Treasury. There was a wheel-turning king named Immeasurable Purity, whose eldest prince for three months made offerings to the Buddha and the saṅgha of monks and aroused the mind of bodhi: 'If there are beings who undergo all the sufferings and afflictions of the three [evil] paths and the like, and if they can think of me and call my name, and are heard and seen by my heavenly ear and heavenly eye, and yet are not spared suffering, I will never attain unsurpassed bodhi.' The Buddha Jewel Treasury said: 'You contemplate all beings and wish to cut off their many sufferings; therefore I now name you Avalokiteśvara.'"

二、「已成」下，過未果號。「已成」等者，《千手眼大悲經》云「此菩薩不可思議威神之力，已於過去無量劫中已作佛竟，號正法明如來，大悲願力安樂眾生故現作菩薩。」又《觀音三昧經》云「先已成佛，號正法明如來。釋迦為彼佛作苦行弟子。」「次當」等者，《觀音授記經》云「觀世音菩薩，次阿彌陀後當成正覺，名普光功德山王如來。」「補處」者，猶儲君之義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "it has already attained" on: the names of the fruit, past and future. "It has already attained" and so on: the Thousand-Handed and Thousand-Eyed Great Compassion Sūtra says: "This bodhisattva's inconceivable majestic spiritual power: already in immeasurable eons past he had become a buddha, named the Tathāgata Right Dharma Illumination; by the power of his great compassionate vow, to bring peace and bliss to beings, he now appears as a bodhisattva." Again, the Avalokiteśvara Samādhi Sūtra says: "He had already become a buddha earlier, named the Tathāgata Right Dharma Illumination; Śākyamuni was his disciple practicing austerities under that buddha." "Next it will" and so on: the Sūtra of Avalokiteśvara's Prediction says: "Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, next after Amitābha, will attain right awakening, named the Tathāgata Universal Light Merit Mountain King."
