The Ten Pairs Concluded, Resolving Doubts, and Inherent Good and Evil

The second fascicle of Zhili's Notes continues his line-by-line reading of the general explanation of the name in Zhiyi's Profound Meaning of Guanyin. It first finishes the ten paired meanings: merit and wisdom, the true and responsive bodies, the medicine tree and the jewel, hidden and manifest benefit, the provisional and the real, origin and trace, the conditioning and revealing causes, and wisdom and severance. Zhili then shows how each pair is matched to phrases of the Lotus "Universal Gate" chapter. Next comes the section on resolving doubts, where the same ten pairs are tested by questions. Here, under the conditioning and the revealing, stands the passage this commentary is best known for. The icchantika has cut off cultivated good and the Buddha has cut off cultivated evil, but neither can cut off inherent good or inherent evil. Zhili uses this to reject the Dilun and Shelun view that the Buddha shows evil only through deliberate supernatural display. The fascicle closes with the start of the specific explanation of "Avalokiteśvara." It covers conceivable and inconceivable object and wisdom, the three kinds of world and the three thousand, and why form as well as mind contains all dharmas. Last, it explains why the name speaks of "sounds" when all three kinds of action are capacities.

Merit and Wisdom

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, merit and wisdom, in three: first, marking the different names. The foremost of merits is none other than concentration; raising the superior includes the inferior, so the five perfections are complete.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Concentration means" on: explaining the meaning according to the names, in two: first, explaining the powers of the two dharmas, in four: first, the effect of concentration and wisdom. "Quiet attachment" and "contemplative urging": because of quietude, it can hold all practices with attachment; because of contemplative illumination, it can urge all practices forward. Attachment without urging gives rise to a stagnant mind; urging without attachment becomes a scattered and overreaching wisdom; only when attachment and urging are both complete is there the effect of heading toward the fruit.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "the wisdom of still illumination" on: the virtues of merit and wisdom. "The wisdom of still illumination" is the two wisdoms, the provisional and the real. "Leaves nothing dark unlit" means being without the darkness of the three delusions. Merit and dhyāna-concentration necessarily include all the perfections and all the dhyānas, great and lesser; merit supports wisdom as oil helps a lamp. Third, from "They are also called eyes" on: the designation of eyes and feet. "The cool pond … the pond is nirvāṇa": nirvāṇa must have the three virtues complete; its ultimate lies at wondrous awakening, and in part it extends to the first abiding. Fourth, from "nirvāṇa is called" on: the name of adornment. The two adornments belong to cultivation; the Dharma-body is the nature. Because the nature may be lacking or complete, the two cultivations come to differ as true or conditioned, as discussed below.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "There are many ways of explaining this" on: explaining in terms of the four teachings, in two: first, explaining the four teachings. Although the bodhisattva of the Tripiṭaka [teaching] is said to contemplate principle, he subdues [delusion] without cutting it off. [The text] raises only the various dhyānas, but they actually include the other perfections. Bringing forth the true must lie at the thirty-four minds. As for the bodhisattva of the shared [teaching], the skillful wisdom of embodying dharmas is helped by the perfections of principle; in the cause he brings forth the true, and only at buddhahood is it complete. Although the person of the distinct [teaching] believes that the mind that creates is itself buddha-nature, [for him] the nature does not contain the nine [realms] and is covered by delusion; so he must separately take as object the two principles of the true and the middle to break the shared and distinct delusions. Therefore it is called "wisdom cultivated through conditions," and he uses the various dhyānas and samādhis of the conventional truth to help reveal the Dharma-body. The perfect [teaching] speaks of inherent evil: realizing that delusion is the true mark is itself the contemplating, called "the contemplation of the real mark"; concentration is likewise, called "the concentration of the real mark"; and further, the body revealed is named by the real mark. Identical to the one yet three, they are called the body of concentration and wisdom; identical to the three yet one, they are together called the real mark. If one is in the dark about inherent evil, what would it have to do with the beginning mind?

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Here the bodhisattva of the perfect teaching" on: showing the six identities of the perfect [teaching]; as in the text. Third, from "Avalokiteśvara uses" on: concluding by pointing to the sūtra title, in two: first, pointing to the sūtra text. "Uses the light of wisdom to illuminate suffering": the sūtra does not have these words, but it has the meaning. Immeasurable beings meeting suffering call his name, and the bodhisattva at once perceives the sound of their voices, and they all attain liberation. "Perceives" (觀, contemplating) is the illumination of wisdom, and illumination is light. Avalokiteśvara's wondrous wisdom is itself the essence of the three paths of beings; because beings are deluded, inversion arises; when Avalokiteśvara illuminates them, liberation is there. As for repeatedly citing the passages of three sūtras on emitting light: if it were not form, how could it be said to be emitted? If it were definitely form, how could it be called wisdom? So we know that form and mind are nondual in essence: the nature of form is wisdom, and the nature of wisdom is form. How is this so only of light? All forms are so. "Manifesting form-bodies everywhere" can be understood by the same principle. Moreover, how could it be only a matter of the fruit? It truly exists in the principle of the cause: truly, only because it is inherently contained in principle is there phenomenal function. Second, from "Truly because" on: concluding by returning to the title.

The True and the Responsive

Chinese Source

四、真應，三：初、標名示義。二身皆有集藏之義。真集一切，智慧藏於一心；應集一切，神通藏於一色。色心不二、通慧一如，唯色唯心，斯之謂也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the true and the responsive, in three: first, marking the names and showing the meaning. Both bodies have the meaning of "accumulation and storehouse." The true accumulates everything: wisdom is stored in one mind. The responsive accumulates everything: spiritual powers are stored in one form. Form and mind are nondual, powers and wisdom are one suchness: "only form, only mind" refers to this.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "If one accords with" on: distinguishing right and wrong by contrast, in two: first, shown in terms of the dharma, in three: first, the dharma. The essence of the true mark is the Dharma-body; the wisdom that accords with it is the reward-body of self-enjoyment; both of these are here called the true body. Once the Dharma[-body] and the reward[-body] are merged, one can give rise to superior and inferior responses and the like, in accord with capacities. Second, from "like taking up a mirror" on: the simile. Taking up a mirror is a simile for realizing the true; the form appearing at once is a simile for giving rise to response. Third, from "This true and responsive" on: the conclusion. The three thousand together as essence is the true; the three thousand together as function is the responsive; only this true and responsive are inseparable. The teaching of [response] without planning makes plain the meaning of our school; what the various [other] schools discuss cannot escape deliberate effort.

Their fundamental contaminated dhyāna does not clarify the object; even if they manifest some powers, they cannot benefit beings. This sifts out what is not response. From "They have not even" on: sifting out what is not true. "As for the two vehicles" and so on: it raises only the two vehicles for now, but necessarily includes the two teachings and the two [kinds of] bodhisattvas. According to the intent of the Wondrous Profound [Meaning], the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings both have spiritual powers by deliberate effort; because they must be reduced to ashes and extinguished, without an abiding origin, they cannot give rise to response. If the distinct is grafted onto the shared, the distinct delusions are not yet cut off, and they too do not obtain response; even if they go to beings, it is all called coarse response. Those of the beginning mind of the distinct [teaching] also cannot respond; at the first ground they first attain the three contemplations appearing before them. Realizing the twenty-five king samādhis, the Dharma-body is pure, without thought or reflection; according to capacities it at once faces them. This is the inconceivable wondrous response.

Having already realized beings' nature of original awakening, he uses the hundred realms of the capacity to respond to the capacities of the hundred realms; in essence originally nondual, how could there be the slightest difference? The Medicine Tree and the Jewel

Chinese Source

五、藥珠：三：初、標名示教。「柰女經」者，具云佛說柰女祇域經，一卷。柰女即維耶離國梵志家柰樹所生，顏色端正，宣聞遠國。因蓱沙王往娉，後生一男名曰祇域，生時手把針筒藥囊。至年八歲，廣通醫術，遍行治病。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, medicine and jewel, in three: first, marking the names and showing the teaching. "The Āmrapālī Sūtra": in full it is called the Sūtra Spoken by the Buddha on Āmrapālī and Jīvaka, in one fascicle. Āmrapālī was born from a mango tree in the house of a brahmin in the country of Vaiśālī; her features were upright and fine, and her fame was heard in distant countries. Because of this King Bimbisāra went to take her as his consort, and afterward she bore a son named Jīvaka, who at birth held a needle case and a medicine bag in his hands. By the age of eight he was widely versed in the art of medicine and went everywhere treating illness.

Ru-Yi Translation

Later he came upon a boy carrying firewood. Jīvaka looked at him and saw all of the boy's five organs, intestines, and stomach. Jīvaka thought: "The Materia Medica says there is a medicine-king tree that, from the outside, illuminates the inside, letting one see a person's abdominal organs. Could there be a medicine-king tree in this boy's firewood?" He went and asked the boy, "How much is the firewood?" The boy said, "Ten coins." So he paid the money and took the firewood. When the firewood was set down on the ground, it was dark, and he could not see inside the abdomen. Jīvaka thought, "I do not know which in the bundles is the medicine king." So he untied the two bundles, took the pieces one by one, and placed them on the boy's abdomen; whenever nothing was illuminated, he took another. In this way he went through both bundles of firewood. At the last there was one small branch, just over a foot long; he tried it to illuminate, and saw the inside of the abdomen completely. Jīvaka was overjoyed, knowing that this small branch was surely the medicine king, and returned all the firewood to the boy. The boy, having got the money with his firewood as before, went away happy. Jīvaka treated the sick throughout the country, always examining them with the medicine king; he saw the root of every illness and then treated it, and none failed to recover. Here it is taken as a simile for the true body removing suffering, like the medicine king curing illness.

Second, from "One could go through all the teachings at length" on: discussing the powers in terms of the teachings, in two: first, briefly indicating the three [teachings]: according to the depth of each teaching, its benefit to beings is broad or narrow; this explains the marks of curing illness and obtaining treasure. Second, from "here it is explained in terms of the perfect teaching" on: explaining the perfect at length, in two: first, explaining the two bodies, in two: first, the medicine-tree body, in two: first, the simile. Deep roots are a simile for the true being wondrous; spreading in all directions, for the response being broad. It shows principle, practice, fruit, and teaching in order, like the root and the rest. Those who practice by faith cultivate the four as obtaining benefit by smelling (聞, also "hearing"); those who practice by Dharma cultivate the four as obtaining benefit by touch. The first section of this chapter explains only removing suffering; this is great compassion perfuming the true body, the same in meaning as curing illness. "With form and voice he benefits beings" speaks for now in general terms; if based on the sūtra text, it must lie in hidden benefit. The reason form and voice are not matched to the smelling and touching above also lies here. Ru-Yi Translation

Question: great compassion perfumes the true [body]; what is its mark?

Chinese Source

答：真是妙智，能破妄惑。悲名愍傷，能拔他苦。同是法身一清淨用耳。欲彰照理有利他益，故立拔苦之悲熏於破惑之智，即顯有悲之智；普除眾生妄惑之苦，例於大慈熏應與樂。同是法身一自在用，一用二能故有能熏所熏之義。良由應身本是自行證得之法，以慈熏故方遍益他。然則慈心非不熏真、悲心非不熏應、真身非不與樂、應身非不拔苦，欲令易解，是故經文寄兩問答分別說也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: the true is wondrous wisdom, able to break false delusions. Compassion means pity and sorrow, able to remove the suffering of others. Both are just one pure function of the Dharma-body. Wishing to show that illuminating principle has the benefit of benefiting others, it sets up the compassion that removes suffering as perfuming the wisdom that breaks delusion; this shows a wisdom that has compassion, universally removing the suffering of beings' false delusions. In the same way great loving-kindness perfumes the responsive [body] to give happiness. Both are one sovereign function of the Dharma-body; one function has two powers, hence the meaning of perfuming and perfumed. Truly because the responsive body is originally a dharma realized through one's own practice, only through the perfuming of loving-kindness does it pervasively benefit others. Thus the mind of loving-kindness does not fail to perfume the true, the mind of compassion does not fail to perfume the responsive, the true body does not fail to give happiness, and the responsive body does not fail to remove suffering; to make it easy to understand, the sūtra text uses two questions and answers to explain them separately.

Hidden and Manifest Benefit

Chinese Source

六、冥顯，三，初、釋名。二、「大聖」下，辯相，二：初、明二益，三：初、示相。大聖常以真智冥熏、妙色顯被，無明隔故益而不知。二、「譬如」下，舉譬。兩曜喻二益，盲者喻無明。凡小全在下地分隔，眼膜既有厚薄之殊，故不見之相不可一揆。三、「故藥」下，引證。三草二木而皆不知一地一雨。下不測上，亦通圓人，故引妙德不知妙音。言「以不知故名為冥益」者，此明二身於不知者皆稱冥益，即彰真應於其知者皆稱顯益。發智見理於真顯益，見身不識但荷冥利，真冥應顯可以意思。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, hidden and manifest, in three: first, explaining the names. Second, from "The great sage always" on: discussing the marks, in two: first, explaining the two benefits, in three: first, showing the marks. The great sage constantly perfumes [beings] hiddenly with true wisdom and covers them manifestly with wondrous form; because ignorance separates them, they are benefited without knowing it. Second, from "It is like the sun and moon" on: raising a simile. The two luminaries are a simile for the two benefits; the blind, for ignorance. Ordinary people and the lesser are entirely within the lower grounds and are partitioned off; since the film over the eye differs in thickness, the ways of not seeing cannot be measured by one standard. Third, from "So the [parable of the] Medicinal Herbs" on: citing proof. The three plants and two trees all do not know the one ground and the one rain. Translator's notes

Lemmas from the Profound Meaning. Every phrase Zhili quotes from Zhiyi's text uses the site's English of the Profound Meaning of Guanyin (Part 1 end and Part 2). Where Zhili's lemma differs slightly from the site's Chinese, the site's wording is kept. Examples: 「若外」 (site 苦外道, a copying error there), 「此且」 (site 此具), 「然通」 and 「問下」 (site 通論皆得 and 問眾生各有三業).

Inherent good and inherent evil (unit 32). Zhili's key terms follow the shared notes: 性善／性惡 inherent good / inherent evil; 修善／修惡 cultivated good / cultivated evil; 性具 contained in the nature. 法門 is "dharma-gate," explained by Zhili as "norm" (軌) and "passage" (通). "The limit of evil is the limit of reality" and "what is not the path is the Buddha path" echo the Vimalakīrti (T475). "Before the Nirvāṇa, all are called wrong views" (涅槃已前皆名邪見) was not found in this form. Similar wording appears in Calming and Contemplation (T1911) and the Meaning of the Four Teachings (T1929).

Dilun and Shelun masters (unit 32). The Northern and Southern Paths of Xiangzhou (相州北道／南道) are the two branches of the Dilun school. Zhili's account of them follows Zhanran's Annotations (T1717) and Notes on the Textual Commentary (T1719).

The Āmrapālī Sūtra (unit 8). Zhili's full title 佛說柰女祇域經 matches T553 (㮈女祇域因緣經) and T554 (奈女耆婆經). His retelling of the medicine-king branch is condensed. 柰 is rendered "mango" to match the name Āmrapālī.

Two kinds of bird (unit 27). The Nirvāṇa passage follows the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, with "kāraṇḍava" for 迦隣提. The question "How does one share in the noble conduct?" is from the site's verse in the same sūtra. Zhili splits 娑羅 / 隣提 differently, so those two words are left as sāra / lindi. Zhang'an's remark is from his Nirvāṇa commentary (T1767).

Other sūtra quotations. The Lotus lines "This Dharma abides in its own Dharma-position…" (unit 32) and "at once perceives the sound of their voices" (unit 4) follow the site's Lotus Sūtra. The Nirvāṇa simile of the hundred blind people (unit 35) follows the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra. The Vimalakīrti lines on the mark of nirvāṇa (unit 17) adapt the site's Vimalakīrti; Zhili paraphrases them. "The twelve links … are not made by buddhas…" (unit 32) is quoted the same way in Calming and Contemplation (T1911). The Avataṃsaka verse (unit 42) is from T278, spoken by 如來林菩薩. The Golden Light line "Within all dharmas, it contains all" (unit 39) was not found in T663/T665. It appears in Zhiyi's Textual Commentary on the Golden Light (T1785).

Jingxi quotations. Jingxi's sayings in units 37 and 39 were not located: "The two emptinesses as originally so…" and "The three virtues, the three truths, and the three thousand are all cut off from words and thoughts…" (source not located). "Without understanding this text, how can one digest the verse…" (unit 42) is quoted in the Zongjing lu (T2016); its original source was not located. The Main Points (大意, unit 40) is Zhanran's Main Points of Calming and Contemplation (T1914), which has "Form is created by mind; its whole essence is mind." The Examples of Meaning (義例) is his Examples of the Meaning of Calming and Contemplation (T1913).

Zhiyi's Four Foundations of Mindfulness (units 39, 41). "It is only one consciousness; it is only one form" and the two kinds of form (discriminating / non-discriminating) are from T1918.

The Great Treatise (units 26, 34, 39). The three kinds of world under the samādhi "that can illuminate all worlds" and the three kinds of self (views, conceit, names) are from T1509. Zhili's "heroic march" (健相) for Śūraṅgama follows the site's English of the Profound Meaning.

King Aśoka's brother (unit 23). 阿輸加 and 育 are both transcriptions of Aśoka. Zhili identifies this "King Ashujia" as the younger brother of King Ayu (Aśoka); the brother is usually named Vītāśoka. The site's lemma "[the brother of] King Aśoka" is kept.

Possible copying errors. Unit 9: 略指，三：隨教… is read as "briefly indicating the three [teachings]." Unit 15: 故覩判為權 is read as 故都判為權. Unit 35: 今文但喻抉見思之。膜示真諦之指 is read as 抉見思之膜、示真諦之指. Unit 36: 名識道。滅四諦既明 is read as 名識道滅。四諦既明. Unit 42: 今載難 is read as 今再難.

Alternative readings. Unit 32: 斷常名通別人 is taken as "the names 'annihilation' and 'permanence' apply to people of the distinct [teaching]." Alternatively: "annihilation and permanence name people of the shared and distinct [teachings]." Unit 34: 與彼外外輪迴一等 is rendered "just like those outsiders beyond"; 外外 may mean "those outside even the outer [path]."
