Contemplating Wisdom through the Four Teachings, the Contemplation of Mind, and the Universal Gate

The third fascicle of Zhili's Notes comments on the second half of Zhiyi's specific explanation of "Avalokiteśvara" and the start of his explanation of "the Universal Gate." Zhiyi first gathers the "sounds of the world" into objects of the truths and then sets out the contemplating wisdom. Zhili matches the three contemplations of the Yingluo Sūtra and the three wisdoms of the Large Prajñāpāramitā with the four wisdoms of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra and the five eyes. He then follows Zhiyi through the four teachings. For the Tripiṭaka bodhisattva he retells the stories of the six perfections and the three asaṃkhyeya eons; for the shared, distinct, and perfect teachings he explains how delusion is severed and why only the perfect contemplation is "universal." A set of questions shows that the middle way does not destroy cause and effect but establishes the Three Jewels of every teaching, using the Nirvāṇa simile of the waxing and waning moon. In the contemplation of mind, Zhili reads the three truths, three contemplations, and all dharmas as the three causes contained in one thought, and lists the ten modes of contemplation as unfoldings of the three thousand. The fascicle ends with the four gates of each teaching, the gates of nonduality in the Vimalakīrti, and the opening of the ten universal gates.

Contemplating: Gathering Up the Object

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the contemplating wisdom, in two: first, marking and listing. Second, from "Gathering up the object" on: the explanation, in two: first, gathering up the object, "the sounds of the world." Wishing to clarify the contemplating wisdom, one first gathers up the objects. The kinds of worldly sounds are boundless; even dust and sand cannot compare to them. One must rely on the noble teaching to gather them up and show the object of the truths; only then can contemplation be clarified. If contemplation does not rely on the truths, how could one doubt that it is false and mistaken? "Gathering the ten dharma-realms as the object of causes and conditions": because the ten suchnesses are of a kind with the twelve links, there is no difference in meaning. Second, from "Second, clarifying the contemplating wisdom" on: clarifying properly the wisdom that contemplates, in two: first, clarifying broadly the contemplations of the various objects. The truths and causes and conditions extend through the four teachings, so [there are] four [sets each]; the three truths belong only to the distinct and the perfect, so two; the two truths, adding the three graftings, make seven; the one real [truth] belongs only to the perfect ultimate, so one. With no truth, the essence is forgotten, and contemplation too is not established.

Embodying the non-generation of the three kinds of provisional reality under the four propositions, one sees the real right in the conventional; this is called the contemplation of entering the empty from the provisional. Contemplating the conventional of the three kinds of provisional reality, one enters the real that is identical to emptiness; because one enters the real through the conventional, it is further called [the contemplation of] the two truths. Not realizing [and resting in] emptiness, one distinguishes all the medicines and illnesses of the three kinds of provisional reality and gives medicine according to the illness; so it is called entering the provisional from the empty. Before, the real was used to break the conventional; now the conventional is used to break the real. Since the breaking and the using of the conventional and the real are balanced, it is further called [the contemplation of] equality. The two breakings before serve as the expedient for negating both; the two usings serve as the expedient for illuminating both. Once the sequential breaking and using are established, negating and illuminating in a single mind can be cultivated; hence it says, "The two contemplations are the skillful means," and so on. The three contemplations are all used; it is named "the middle" after the superior. Since the mind is identical to the middle, conceiving and deliberating are forgotten and dissolved; it is called the contemplation of the truth of the first meaning.

Second, from "These three contemplations" on: clarifying the three wisdoms according to the Large [Prajñāpāramitā], in three: first, clarifying properly the marks of the three wisdoms. "Inner dharmas and inner names" means the dharma-marks expressed and the names and words that express them within the principle. "Outer dharmas and outer names" means the dharma-marks expressed and the names that express them outside the principle. If the contemplation of emptiness is accomplished, one can thoroughly realize in all these names and marks that there is no self and nothing belonging to self. Hence the Buddha said: "Mahākāśyapa, the dharmas of the brahmins he knows completely; the dharmas of the śramaṇas he knows completely." Hence it says that inner and outer it can know and can understand. Yet this wisdom of emptiness can only reach in general the non-generation of dharmas; it cannot know in particular the conditioned arising of dharmas, and so it cannot use the dharmas of the path of the buddhas to give rise to all the good seeds of beings. The provisional contemplation can do so; therefore its wisdom is named by "path" and "seed" (道種).

"Within a single wisdom of modes" and so on: as for the wisdom of the middle contemplation, it understands that everything is the middle way. The middle is not one-sided; its meaning is the absolute. If one dharma is the middle, then the causal seeds of all beings and the dharmas of the path of all buddhas all, without exception, tend toward it. If there were a dharma outside the one, it would not be called the middle. As one dharma is, so are all; hence it says that within a single wisdom of modes it knows all paths and knows all modes. "The single mark" and so on concludes what was said before and completes negating and illuminating. Negating both, there is the single mark, quiescent extinction; illuminating both, all the various [kinds] are known. Negating and illuminating are simultaneous, so it is called the wisdom knowing all modes.

The Four Wisdoms, the Five Eyes, and the Masters of the Teachings

Chinese Source

三、「此三」下，對《大經》四智，二：初、略示四智相。《大經》二十五云「觀因緣智，凡有四種：謂下、中、上、上上。下智觀者不見佛性，以不見故得聲聞菩提。中智觀者不見佛性，不見佛性故得緣覺菩提。上智觀者見不了了，不了了故住十住地。上上智觀者見則了了，得阿耨菩提。」《輔行》釋云「因緣不殊、四觀不等，對別教中云住十住地者，以次第行從住入空，乃至十地方入中道。次第住三，故名為住，住及不了並約教道。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "These three contemplating wisdoms" on: matching them with the four wisdoms of the Great Sūtra, in two: first, briefly showing the marks of the four wisdoms. The Great Sūtra, [fascicle] twenty-five, says: "The wisdom that contemplates causes and conditions is, in all, of four kinds: namely lower, middle, upper, and upper-upper. One who contemplates with lower wisdom does not see buddha-nature, and, because of not seeing it, attains the bodhi of the śrāvaka. One who contemplates with middle wisdom does not see buddha-nature, and, not seeing buddha-nature, attains the bodhi of the pratyekabuddha. One who contemplates with upper wisdom sees it, but not with complete clarity, and, lacking complete clarity, abides at the ground of the ten abidings. One who contemplates with upper-upper wisdom sees it with complete clarity, and attains anuttara bodhi." The Fuxing explains: "The causes and conditions do not differ, but the four contemplations are not equal. As for matching it with the distinct teaching and saying 'abides at the ground of the ten abidings': by sequential practice one enters the empty from the abidings, and only at the ten grounds does one enter the middle way. Abiding in the three in sequence, it is called 'abiding.' 'Abiding' and 'not with complete clarity' are both in terms of the path of teaching."

「初為陶師」者，合云陶師之子。因遇彼佛入城乞食，相好巍巍，乃發善心而興供養。遂對彼佛發於誓言：「願我當成佛，一如今世尊。」故今釋迦法住之時、度人多少等、皆同往佛。言「即起慈悲」者，發心拔苦、欲與其樂。若不諦審非想結集及輪迴苦，又不諦審三無為滅及盡苦道，則不拔苦際非與真樂。凡外不諦、二乘無誓，菩薩雙非依諦立誓。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Śākyamuni was first a potter": it should say "a potter's son." Because he met that buddha entering the city to beg for food, with marks and signs majestic, he aroused a good mind and made offerings. Then before that buddha he made the vow: "May I become a buddha just like the present World-Honored One." Hence the present Śākyamuni's duration of the Dharma, the number of people he delivers, and so on are all the same as the former buddha's. As for "he gives rise to a mind of loving-kindness and compassion": he arouses the mind to remove suffering and wishes to give happiness. If one does not truly examine the bonds and origin up to [the heaven of] Neither-Perception [nor Non-Perception] and the suffering of transmigration, and does not truly examine the cessation of the three unconditioned [dharmas] and the path that ends suffering, then one does not remove suffering to its limit and does not give true happiness. Ordinary people and non-Buddhists do not [examine] the truths; the two vehicles have no vows; the bodhisattva is neither, and establishes vows based on the truths.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "He practices the practices of the six perfections" on: giving rise to practice based on the vows, in two: first, the six perfections filling out the aspirations. In what the text clarifies, the six coverings are origin and the six paths are suffering; the six perfections are the path, and the ceasing of the coverings is cessation. It raises briefly the first and the last; the middle four can be known by analogy. The bodhisattva of this teaching himself subdues the six coverings and correspondingly breaks the six paths, in order to let others sever origin and leave suffering. The Commentary on the Bodhisattva Precepts says: "Dāna breaks [the realm of] the hungry ghosts; śīla rescues the hells; patience saves the animals; vigor pulls out the asuras; dhyāna [brings] stillness among humans; wisdom illuminates the hosts of gods."

二、「行此」下，六度滿時。如尸毘王，遍割身肉就鷹貿鴿，至盡一身不惱不沒；自誓真實，感身平復，是檀滿相。如須摩提王，以身就死持不妄戒，是尸滿相。如忍辱仙人，被歌利王割截身體，慈忍不動，作誓即感血化為乳，是羼提滿相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "In practicing these six perfections" on: the time when the six perfections are fulfilled. Like King Śibi, who cut the flesh all over his body to trade with the hawk for the dove, until his whole body was used up, without vexation or faltering; by his vow of truthfulness he caused his body to be restored: this is the mark of the fulfillment of dāna. Like King Sumati, who went to his death keeping the precept against lying: this is the mark of the fulfillment of śīla. Like the seer of patience, whose body was cut apart by King Kali, unmoved in loving patience; he made a vow, and at once the blood turned into milk: this is the mark of the fulfillment of kṣānti.

Chinese Source

如好施太子，求如意珠雨寶濟貧，得珠墜海抒海取之，筋骨斷壞終不懈廢。諸天問之，云生生不休。故助抒海。海水減半，龍恐海乾送珠與之，是毘離邪滿相。如尚闍梨得第四禪出入息斷，鳥謂為木於髻生卵，定起欲行恐鳥母不來，即更入禪，鳥飛方起，是禪滿相。如劬嬪大臣，分閻浮提地為七分，城邑山川均故息諍，是般若滿相。所言滿者，度本治蔽，行期滿願。今蔽已離，與拔遂心，即知六度其功剋滿。

Ru-Yi Translation

Like Prince Fond-of-Giving, who sought the wish-fulfilling jewel to rain treasure and relieve the poor; having obtained the jewel, he let it fall into the sea, and he bailed out the sea to get it back; though his sinews and bones broke, he never slackened or gave up. The gods asked him, and he said: "Birth after birth I will not stop." So they helped him bail out the sea. When the seawater was halved, the dragons, fearing the sea would dry up, sent him the jewel: this is the mark of the fulfillment of vīrya. Like Shangsheli, who attained the fourth dhyāna with his breathing cut off; a bird took him for a tree and laid eggs in his topknot; rising from concentration, he wished to go, but fearing that the mother bird would not come back, he entered dhyāna again and rose only when the birds had flown: this is the mark of the fulfillment of dhyāna. Like the great minister Govinda, who divided the land of Jambudvīpa into seven parts, with cities, towns, mountains, and rivers evenly [shared], and so ended the disputes: this is the mark of the fulfillment of prajñā. As for what is called "fulfillment": the perfections originally treat the coverings, and the practice aims at fulfilling the vows. Now that the coverings are left behind and giving and removing accord with his mind, one knows that the work of the six perfections is fulfilled.

Times and Stages of the Tripiṭaka Bodhisattva

Chinese Source

二、「如此」下，明時位，三：初約時明行相。從古釋迦至罽那尸棄佛名初僧祇，準望聲聞位。在五停心及別相總相念處也，觀力既微故不知作佛。從罽那尸棄至然燈佛時名第二僧祇，位當煖法。既有證法之信必知作佛，心未分明故不向他說。從然燈至毘婆尸佛名第三僧祇，位在頂法，內心了了自知作佛，口自發言無所畏難也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Practicing thus" on: clarifying the times and stages, in three: first, showing the marks of practice in terms of time. From the ancient Śākyamuni to the Buddha Ratnaśikhin is called the first asaṃkhyeya; compared with the stages of the śrāvakas, it lies at the five stilling-minds and the particular and general foundations of mindfulness. Since the power of contemplation is still weak, he does not know that he will become a buddha. From Ratnaśikhin to the time of the Buddha Dīpaṃkara is called the second asaṃkhyeya; the stage corresponds to the dharma of warmth. "Mahākāśyapa, the dharmas of the brahmins he knows completely…" (unit 2); "Within the bodies of beings there is already a Tathāgata seated cross-legged," which Zhili assigns to the Buddha Treasury (unit 15); and Jingxi's "The three thousand identical to the empty are the revealing cause of the nature…" (unit 20). For the last, the Ten Gates of Nonduality (T1927) has related wording. The Buddha Treasury's "ten similes" (unit 22) may refer to the similes of the Tathāgatagarbha Sūtra (T666); this is not certain.

Inherent inclusion. 具 is "contain" throughout (性具 inherent inclusion); 理具 in unit 20 is "containment in principle." 理造／事造 are "creation in principle / creation in phenomena," as in Part 2.

Outline formulas. 「二：初、……」 is "in two: first, …," following the instruction for this round. Parts 1–2 of this text use "in two parts: first"; see decisions.

Names. 罽那尸棄 is taken as Ratnaśikhin, the buddha who ends the first asaṃkhyeya in the Abhidharma account. 好施太子 is "Prince Fond-of-Giving"; the site's Profound Meaning calls the same figure Mahādāna (大施). 尚闍梨 follows the site's "Shangsheli." 弗沙／底沙 are Puṣya and Tiṣya; Zhili treats them as one name.

Alternative readings. Unit 5: 主對已齊 is read as "already evenly matched"; 主 may be a copying error for 生 or 互. Unit 13: 故未別後不名不共 is read as "since [being unshared] does not yet distinguish it from the later [teaching], it is not named 'the unshared.'" Unit 18: 彊覺 is "forced awareness"; alternatively, "strong awareness." Unit 20: the lemma 「此下」 is taken as 「此之」 (the site's "This mind-nature").
