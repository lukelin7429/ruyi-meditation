Preface, the Place of Practice, Offerings, Invitations, and Praise

Ciyun Zunshi (964–1032), the Tiantai master of Hangzhou who also edited Zhiyi's Lotus Samādhi Repentance, compiled this rite for practitioners who seek rebirth in Amitābha's Pure Land; its postface dates the revised version to 1015. Part 1 contains the preface and the first six of its ten sections. The preface lists what the practice is said to bring: the removal of grave offenses, purity in the precepts, the samādhi of mindfulness of the Buddha, a peaceful death with Amitābha and the two bodhisattvas coming to receive the practitioner, and the powers of those born in Sukhāvatī. The first three sections are preparatory: how to prepare the room and the images (Amitābha in the west, facing east), how to spend seven days rehearsing the texts and giving up other work, and how long the retreat lasts. Section Three takes the five gates of mindfulness from Vasubandhu's Treatise on the Pure Land as the core of the rite, adds repentance to remove obstacles to rebirth, and distinguishes the single mind in principle from the single mind in phenomena. Sections Four to Six are the opening liturgy: offering incense and flowers with a visualization, inviting the buddhas, the Dharma, the bodhisattvas, the saṅgha, and the guardian gods one by one (each with a short verse to visualize), and a verse in praise of Amitābha followed by a dedication of its merit.

Title and Compiler's Note

Chinese Source

往生淨土懺願儀

Ru-Yi Translation

The Rite of Repentance and Vows for Rebirth in the Pure Land

Chinese Source

（沙門遵式輒采大本《無量壽經》，及《稱讚淨土》等諸大乘經，集此方法，流布諸後，普結淨緣。）

Ru-Yi Translation

(The śramaṇa Zunshi has ventured to take from the larger Sūtra of Immeasurable Life, the Sūtra in Praise of the Pure Land, and other Mahāyāna sūtras, and has compiled this method, to circulate it to those who come after and universally form pure connections.)

Preface: Why This Practice

Chinese Source

原其諸佛憫物迷盲，設多方便而引取之，但誰安養淨業捷直可修，諸大乘經皆啟斯要，十方諸佛無不稱美者也。若比丘四眾及善男女，諸根缺具者，欲得速破無明諸闇，欲得永滅五逆十惡，犯禁重罪及餘輕過，當修此法；欲得還復清淨，大、小戒律現前，得念佛三昧，及能具足一切菩薩諸波羅密門者，當學此法；

Ru-Yi Translation

As to its origin: the buddhas, pitying beings in their blindness, set up many expedients to draw them in; but only the pure karma of [the Land of] Peaceful Nourishment is quick and direct to cultivate. The Mahāyāna sūtras all open up this essential point, and among the buddhas of the ten directions there is none who does not praise it. If bhikṣus, the fourfold assembly, and good men and women, whether their faculties are impaired or whole, wish quickly to break the darkness of ignorance, and wish to extinguish forever the five heinous offenses and the ten evils, the grave offenses of breaking the prohibitions, and other light faults, they should cultivate this method. If they wish to regain purity, so that the greater and lesser precepts and discipline appear before them, to attain the samādhi of mindfulness of the Buddha, and to be able to complete all the gates of the bodhisattvas' pāramitās, they should study this method.

Chinese Source

欲得臨終離諸怖畏，身心安快，喜悅如歸，光照室宅，異香音樂，阿彌陀佛、觀音、勢至現在其前，送紫金臺，授手接引，五道橫截，九品長騖，謝去熱惱，安息清涼，初離塵勞便至不退，不歷長劫即得無生者，當學是法；欲修少法而感妙報，十方諸佛俱時稱讚，現前授記，一念供養無央數佛，即還本國與彌陀坐食、觀音議論、勢至行步，眼耳洞視徹聞，身量無際，飛空自在，宿命了了，遍見五道如鏡面像，念念證入無盡三昧，如是稱述不可窮盡，應當修習此之勝法。

Ru-Yi Translation

If they wish, at the end of life, to be free of all fear, with body and mind at ease and glad, joyful as if returning home, with light shining through the room and house, rare fragrance and music, and Amitābha Buddha, Avalokiteśvara, and Mahāsthāmaprāpta appearing before them, presenting the purple-gold dais and extending their hands to receive and guide them; to cut straight across the five paths and race far through the nine grades; to take leave of feverish torment and rest in cool clarity; on first leaving the dust and toil, to reach non-retrogression at once, and without passing through long eons to attain non-arising at once — they should study this method. If they wish, by cultivating a small practice, to receive a wondrous reward — the buddhas of the ten directions praising them all at once and giving them predictions face to face; in a single thought making offerings to countless buddhas and at once returning to their own land; sitting and eating with Amitābha, discussing with Avalokiteśvara, and walking with Mahāsthāmaprāpta; with eyes and ears seeing and hearing all the way through; with bodies of boundless size; flying freely through the sky; remembering past lives clearly; seeing all of the five paths like images on the face of a mirror; and in thought after thought realizing and entering inexhaustible samādhis — such praise could never be exhausted — they should cultivate and practice this excellent method.

Chinese Source

如所說者，皆實不虛，十方諸佛出廣長舌，稱美此事以示不妄。我等云何敢不信佛！今取淨土眾經，立此行法，若欲廣知，尋經補益。且聊為十科說之：一、嚴淨道場；二、明方便法；三、明正修意；四、燒香散華；五、禮請法；六、讚歎法；七、禮佛法；八、懺願法；九、旋誦法；十、坐禪法。

Ru-Yi Translation

All that has been said is true and not false; the buddhas of the ten directions put forth the broad and long tongue to praise this matter, to show that it is not false. How could we dare not believe the Buddha! Now I take the many Pure Land sūtras and set up this method of practice; whoever wishes to know it more broadly should search the sūtras to supplement it. For now I briefly explain it in ten sections: first, adorning and purifying the place of practice; second, clarifying the method of expedients; third, clarifying the intent of the main cultivation; fourth, burning incense and scattering flowers; fifth, the method of bowing and inviting; sixth, the method of praise; seventh, the method of bowing to the buddhas; eighth, the method of repentance and vows; ninth, the method of circumambulation and recitation; tenth, the method of seated meditation.

Section One: Adorning and Purifying the Place of Practice

Chinese Source

第一嚴淨道場

Ru-Yi Translation

First: Adorning and Purifying the Place of Practice

Chinese Source

當選閑靜堂室，先去舊土，後於靜處取新土，須地無瓦石及先非穢染，用填其地，以香和塗，極令清淨。次於其上懸新寶蓋，蓋中懸五雜旛，及遍室懸諸繒綵旛華，取好莊嚴。安佛像西坐東向，觀音侍左，勢至侍右，像前列眾好華及蓮華等，若安九往生像最好，無亦無妨。餘者嚴事，隨力安之。次周設薦席，慮地卑濕。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should select a quiet, secluded hall or room. First remove the old earth; then take new earth from a quiet place — ground that is free of tiles and stones and has not previously been soiled — and use it to fill the floor; mix it with fragrance and plaster it, making it utterly pure. Next, above it hang a new jeweled canopy; within the canopy hang banners of the five mixed [colors], and throughout the room hang silk banners and flowers, adorning it finely. Place the Buddha image seated in the west, facing east, with Avalokiteśvara attending on the left and Mahāsthāmaprāpta attending on the right. Before the image arrange many fine flowers, lotus flowers, and so on. If images of the nine [grades of] rebirth can be set up, that is best; if there are none, there is no harm. The other adornments are set up according to one's means. Next, lay mats all around, since the ground may be low and damp.

Chinese Source

行人須新淨衣，如絕無新淨，即浣染身中上者，浴後披著，方入道場，應從門頰左右出入，鞋履齊正，不得雜亂。其所往時須換故衣，沐浴後却著淨衣，日日如是。次於道場，當自傾竭種種供養三寶，若不盡其所有供養，行法不專，必無感降，如絕無己物，方可外求。行者十人已還，多則不可，宜於六齋日建首。

Ru-Yi Translation

The practitioners need new, clean robes; if there are no new, clean ones at all, then wash the best of their own clothes and put them on after bathing, and only then enter the place of practice. They should go out and in by the left and right sides of the door; shoes should be set straight and neat, not in disorder. When going out [to relieve themselves], they must change into old clothes, and after bathing put the clean robes back on. Do so every day. Next, in the place of practice, one should exhaust one's means in making all kinds of offerings to the Three Jewels; if one does not make offerings with all that one has, the practice of the method is not wholehearted, and there will certainly be no responsive descent. Only if one has no possessions at all may one seek from outside. The practitioners should be ten or fewer; more is not permitted. It is fitting to begin on one of the six days of abstinence.

Section Two: The Method of Expedients

Chinese Source

第二明方便法

Ru-Yi Translation

Second: Clarifying the Method of Expedients

Chinese Source

行者欲入道場，身心散亂，須預行方便。當於七日，營理別房，不得與道場同處；如無別屋，亦許共室。應日夜調習案試，及預誦下五悔等文，極令精熟即通。染浣、紉縫及中辦事餘，治生雜務即時併息。但念不久，定生淨土，一心求懺，無有留難，各自剋期，不惜身命，定取淨業，即時成就。不得一念思憶五塵，訶去愛欲，勤息恚癡。行人各有無始惡習，速求捨離不為，正懺障礙，自當觀察何習偏重，訶棄調停，取令平復，勿使行法唐喪其功，可以意解。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the practitioners wish to enter the place of practice, since body and mind are scattered and confused, they must first carry out expedients. For seven days they should prepare a separate room, not in the same place as the place of practice; if there is no separate building, sharing a room is also permitted. Day and night they should train and rehearse, and recite beforehand the texts of the five repentances and so on below, making them thoroughly familiar so that they flow. Dyeing and washing, sewing, and the rest of the affairs to be managed, as well as the miscellaneous tasks of livelihood, should all be stopped at once. Only think: "Before long I shall certainly be born in the Pure Land; with a single mind I seek repentance, and there will be no obstacles." Each sets a fixed term, not sparing body or life, determined to obtain pure karma, which will then be accomplished at once. Not for a single thought may one recollect the five sense-objects; rebuke and drive away love and desire, and diligently stop anger and delusion. Each practitioner has beginningless evil habits; quickly seek to give them up and not act on them, [for they are] obstacles to true repentance. One should oneself contemplate and examine which habit is especially heavy, rebuke and discard it, adjust and settle it, and bring oneself back to evenness, so that the practice of the method does not waste its effort. One can understand this for oneself.

Section Three: The Intent of the Main Cultivation

Chinese Source

第三明正修意

Ru-Yi Translation

Third: Clarifying the Intent of the Main Cultivation

Chinese Source

《大集》明七七日，《鼓音王》及《大彌陀經》十日十夜，《十六觀經》及《小彌陀經》明七日七夜，取此三等為期，決不可減。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Great Collection specifies seven times seven days; the Drum-Sound King and the Larger Amitābha Sūtra, ten days and ten nights; the Sixteen Contemplations Sūtra and the Smaller Amitābha Sūtra specify seven days and seven nights. Take these three as the term; it must never be shortened.

Chinese Source

言正修意者，《天親論》曰：「明何義觀安樂世界、見阿彌陀佛、願生彼國土。云何觀？云何生信心？修五念門成就者，畢竟得生安樂國土，見阿彌陀佛。何等為五？一者、禮拜門；二者、讚歎門；三者、作願門；四者、觀察門；五者、迴向門。……乃至菩薩巧方便迴向者，謂說禮拜等五種修行，所集一切功德善根，不求自身住持之樂，欲拔一切眾生苦故，作願攝取一切眾生，共生彼安樂佛國，是名菩薩巧方便迴向。如是善知迴向，……得三種順菩提門：一者、無染清淨心，不為自身求諸樂故；二者、安清淨心，以拔一切眾生苦故；三者、樂清淨心，令一切眾生得大菩提，以攝取眾生生彼國土故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the intent of the main cultivation, Vasubandhu's Treatise says: "What meaning is clarified by contemplating the world of Peace and Bliss, seeing Amitābha Buddha, and vowing to be born in that land? How does one contemplate? How does one give rise to the mind of faith? One who cultivates and accomplishes the five gates of mindfulness will in the end be born in the land of Peace and Bliss and see Amitābha Buddha. What are the five? First, the gate of bowing; second, the gate of praise; third, the gate of making vows; fourth, the gate of contemplation and examination; fifth, the gate of dedication. … Up to: the bodhisattva's dedication through skillful expedients means this: all the merit and wholesome roots gathered by the five kinds of practice — bowing and the rest — just described, he does not seek for his own sustained bliss; but because he wishes to remove the suffering of all sentient beings, he makes vows to gather in all sentient beings to be born together in that buddha-land of Peace and Bliss. This is called the bodhisattva's dedication through skillful expedients. One who thus knows dedication well … attains three gates that accord with bodhi: first, the pure mind without defilement, because he does not seek pleasures for himself; second, the pure mind of peace, because he removes the suffering of all sentient beings; third, the pure mind of joy, because he causes all sentient beings to attain great bodhi, by gathering in sentient beings to be born in that land."

Chinese Source

故全用論文，為今正意。但加懺悔者，為令除滅往生障故，為順佛慈速度生故，當須一心一意，滿七七日，乃至七日晝夜六時，禮十方佛及彌陀世尊。若坐、若行，皆勿散亂，不得如彈指頃念世五欲，及接對外人，語論戲笑，亦不得託事延緩，放逸睡眠。當於瞬息俯仰，繫念不斷，為求往生，一心精進。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore I use the treatise text in full as the main intent here. Repentance is added only in order to remove the obstacles to rebirth and to accord with the Buddha's compassion in swiftly delivering beings. One must, with a single mind and a single intent, for a full seven times seven days, or even seven days, in the six periods of day and night, bow to the buddhas of the ten directions and the World-Honored Amitābha. Whether sitting or walking, never be scattered; one may not, even for the space of a finger-snap, think of the five worldly desires, nor receive outsiders, chatting, discussing, joking, and laughing; nor may one use some matter as an excuse to delay, be heedless, or sleep. In every blink and breath, bowing and raising the head, keep the thought bound without interruption, and for the sake of seeking rebirth be vigorous with a single mind.

Chinese Source

問：「行法既多，云何一心？」答：「有理、有事。一者、理一心，謂初入道場，乃至畢竟，雖涉眾事，皆是無性，不生不滅，法界一相，如法界緣，名理一心。二者、事一心，謂若禮佛時，不念餘事，但專禮佛，誦經行道，亦復如是，是名事一心也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "The practices of the method are many; how can there be a single mind?" Answer: "There is [the single mind] in principle and [the single mind] in phenomena. First, the single mind in principle: from first entering the place of practice until the very end, though one is engaged in many matters, all of them are without nature, neither born nor perishing, the one mark of the dharma-realm; taking the dharma-realm as one's object in accordance with the dharma-realm — this is called the single mind in principle. Second, the single mind in phenomena: when bowing to the Buddha, one does not think of other matters but bows to the Buddha exclusively; reciting the sūtra and circumambulating are likewise. This is called the single mind in phenomena."

Section Four: Burning Incense and Scattering Flowers

Chinese Source

第四燒香散華

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth: Burning Incense and Scattering Flowers

Chinese Source

（行者已淨三業，初入道場時，先當正立思惟，我為眾生發菩提心，願求淨土，故總禮三寶，廣修供養。三寶受供，必當念我，隨我請求，證知我願。此燒香散華，亦名三業供養。初總禮三寶，未須一一隨方想念，但想己身及十方諸佛，實相理體，本無能、所，故無能禮、所禮，想無能、所，名法界海。願諸眾生同見此理，名為同入。首者先當唱云：）

Ru-Yi Translation

(The practitioners, having purified the three kinds of action, on first entering the place of practice should first stand upright and reflect: "For the sake of sentient beings I arouse the bodhi-mind and vow to seek the Pure Land; therefore I bow to the Three Jewels all together and broadly cultivate offerings. The Three Jewels, receiving the offerings, will surely be mindful of me, follow my requests, and attest to my vows." This burning of incense and scattering of flowers is also called offering with the three kinds of action. In first bowing to the Three Jewels all together, there is no need yet to visualize each one according to its direction; only visualize that one's own body and the buddhas of the ten directions are the principle-essence of the true mark, originally without agent or object, and therefore there is no one who bows and no one bowed to. Visualizing that there is no agent or object is called the ocean of the dharma-realm. Wishing that all sentient beings together see this principle is called entering together. The leader should first chant:)

Chinese Source

一切恭敬，一心敬禮十方法界常住佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Let all be reverent! With a single mind I bow in reverence to the buddhas forever abiding in the dharma-realm of the ten directions."

Chinese Source

一心敬禮十方法界常住法。

Ru-Yi Translation

"With a single mind I bow in reverence to the Dharma forever abiding in the dharma-realm of the ten directions."

Chinese Source

一心敬禮十方法界常住僧。（唱已，各執香爐，䠒跪復云：）

Ru-Yi Translation

"With a single mind I bow in reverence to the Saṅgha forever abiding in the dharma-realm of the ten directions." (Having chanted, each holds a censer, kneels with one knee raised, and again says:)

Chinese Source

是諸眾等各各䠒跪，嚴持香華，如法供養，供養十方法界三寶。（至此輟唱，各放香爐，手捧散華，廣作供養。想云：「願此香華遍十方，以為微妙光明臺，諸天音樂天寶香，諸天餚饍天寶衣，不可思議妙法塵，一一塵出一切塵，一一塵出一切法，旋轉無礙互莊嚴，遍至十方三寶前。十方法界三寶前，皆有我身修供養，一一皆悉遍法界，彼彼無雜無障礙，盡未來際作佛事，普熏法界諸眾生，蒙熏皆發菩提心，同入無生證佛智。」想已，散華，執手爐，口發是言：）

Ru-Yi Translation

"Let this whole assembly, each and every one, kneel with one knee raised, holding incense and flowers reverently, and make offering as the Dharma directs, making offering to the Three Jewels of the dharma-realm of the ten directions." (At this point stop chanting; each puts down the censer, takes the flowers for scattering in the hands, and makes offerings broadly. Visualize, saying: "May this incense and these flowers fill the ten directions, / becoming a subtle and wondrous terrace of light, / the music of the heavens, the incense of heavenly treasures, / the delicacies of the heavens, the garments of heavenly treasures, / inconceivable, wondrous motes of Dharma, / each mote giving rise to every mote, / each mote giving rise to every dharma, / turning without hindrance and adorning one another, / reaching everywhere before the Three Jewels of the ten directions. / Before the Three Jewels of the dharma-realm of the ten directions / my own body is present everywhere, making offering, / each and every one filling the whole dharma-realm, / each unmixed and unobstructed, / doing the work of the Buddha to the limit of the future, / universally perfuming all sentient beings of the dharma-realm, / so that, touched by this perfume, they all arouse the bodhi-mind, / together entering non-arising and realizing the Buddha's wisdom." Having visualized, scatter the flowers, hold the hand censer, and say aloud these words:)

Chinese Source

願此香華雲，遍滿十方界，供養一切佛，尊法諸菩薩，無量聲聞眾，以起光明臺，過於無邊界，無邊佛土中，受用作佛事，普熏諸眾生，皆發菩提心。（「皆發」下一句接和「供養」下。獨唱：）

Ru-Yi Translation

"May this cloud of incense and flowers / fill the realms of the ten directions, / making offering to all the buddhas, / the honored Dharma, and the bodhisattvas, / and the immeasurable assembly of śrāvakas, / raising a terrace of light / that passes beyond boundless realms; / in boundless buddha-lands / may they receive and use it to do the work of the Buddha, / universally perfuming all sentient beings, / so that all arouse the bodhi-mind." (The line beginning "all arouse" is joined in chorus, [as] after "making offering." Chanted alone:)

Chinese Source

供養已，一切恭敬。（起已，一拜。）

Ru-Yi Translation

"Having made offering, let all be reverent." (Having risen, bow once.)

Section Five: Bowing and Inviting the Buddhas

Chinese Source

第五禮請法

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth: The Method of Bowing and Inviting

Chinese Source

（當更添香，如前䠒跪，執香爐，端意勤重，遍請三寶來入道場，不可輕率延屈至尊，當，須三業併切，一心奉請。若不爾者，虛請無益。各想一一如來，隨其方面，領諸眷屬，入我道場，如住目前，畟塞虛空，不得剎那起於雜念。但初八日迎請，餘時不用。首人唱云：）

Ru-Yi Translation

(One should add incense again, kneel with one knee raised as before, and hold the censer, with intention upright and earnest, and invite the Three Jewels everywhere to come into the place of practice. One must not be casual in summoning the Most Honored Ones; one must bring the three kinds of action together in earnest and respectfully invite with a single mind. If not, the invitation is empty and of no benefit. Each should visualize every tathāgata, from his own direction, leading his retinue into our place of practice, as if present before one's eyes, crowding and filling the empty space; not for an instant may one give rise to mixed thoughts. This welcoming invitation is made only on the first, eighth day; at other times it is not used. The leader chants:)

Chinese Source

一心奉請南無本師釋迦牟尼佛。（釋迦是我等師，說諸大乘，令我修淨土業，故須初請。當各運心，感此恩德。如是三請，每一遍請時，想云：「我三業性如虛空，釋迦如來亦如是，不起真際為眾生，與眾俱來受供養。」下共例為三請。）

Ru-Yi Translation

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Sources named (unit 1). "The larger Sūtra of Immeasurable Life" is the Sūtra of Immeasurable Life (T0360), quoted on the site as the Immeasurable Life Sūtra; the Sūtra in Praise of the Pure Land is Xuanzang's version of the Amitābha Sūtra (稱讚淨土佛攝受經, T0367), which is the source of the ten-direction buddha names in Part 2.

Peaceful Nourishment / Peace and Bliss. 安養 is rendered "[the Land of] Peaceful Nourishment," as in the round-2 Fahua Sanmei Chanyi draft; 安樂 (in the Treatise quotation) is "Peace and Bliss." 極樂 is Sukhāvatī; 樂國 "the Land of Bliss."

Broad and long tongue. As in the site's Amitābha Sūtra: the buddhas of the six directions "put forth the sign of the broad and long tongue."

Terms of the term (unit 8). Seven times seven days: cf. the Great Collection Sūtra (T0397), 七七日中晝夜六時. Ten days and ten nights: the Drum-Sound King Dhāraṇī Sūtra (T0370) and the Larger Amitābha Sūtra (T0362, T0361). Seven days: the Contemplation Sūtra (called here the Sixteen Contemplations Sūtra) and the Amitābha Sūtra.

Vasubandhu's Treatise (unit 8). The Upadeśa on the Sūtra of Immeasurable Life (無量壽經優波提舍, T1524). Zunshi quotes with two ellipses (……). The wording 乃至菩薩巧方便迴向者，謂說禮拜等… matches Tanluan's commentary (T1819) more closely than T1524, which reads 何者菩薩巧方便迴向？. Not translated on the site; translated here directly.

Single mind in principle (unit 8). 如法界緣 is read as "taking the dharma-realm as one's object in accordance with the dharma-realm." Alternatively: "it is like taking the dharma-realm as object."

Offering verse (unit 14). The visualization verse 願此香華遍十方… is the same as in the site's Great Compassion Repentance; that page's wording is followed, except "bodhi-mind" (for 菩提心) and "terrace of light" (光明臺, kept the same in unit 15).

Chorus note (unit 15). 「皆發」下一句接和「供養」下 is unclear; read as "the line beginning 'all arouse' is joined in chorus, [as] after 'making offering.'"

Fifty-three buddhas (unit 20). The list in the Immeasurable Life Sūtra begins with Dīpaṃkara (錠光, here 定光) and Far-Reaching Light; Dragon's Voice is the fifty-second. Names follow the site's Immeasurable Life Sūtra, as does Lokeśvararāja.

Bowing and inviting (units 11–33). 一心敬禮 / 一心奉請 / 一切恭敬 / 胡跪 (䠒跪) follow the fixed ritual terms; "Let all be reverent" and "Having made offering, let all be reverent" follow the round-2 Fahua Sanmei Chanyi. 想云 is "Visualize, saying," as on the site's Great Compassion Repentance.

Samantabhadra repentance (unit 24). 普賢懺法 refers to the Lotus Samādhi Repentance, in which the last invitation is to Samantabhadra.

Invincible and Unresting (unit 26). 無能勝 is Ajita (usually Maitreya); 不休息 is a bodhisattva named in some versions of the Immeasurable Life Sūtra opening. The Sūtra of the King of Vows is the Samantabhadra vows chapter of the Avataṃsaka (T0293).

Bound for buddhahood after one more birth (unit 30). 一生補處 as in the site's Amitābha Sūtra.

"[Rewards] good and punishes evil" (unit 32). The text has 善罰惡; a verb such as 賞 seems to have dropped out.

Praise verse (units 36–38). The verse 色如閻浮金… also appears in the Guoqing Bailu (T1934); its last line 聞名得不退 recalls Nāgārjuna's praise of Amitābha in the Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā (T1521): 聞名得不退，是故稽首禮.
