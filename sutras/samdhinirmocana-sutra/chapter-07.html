Chapter 7 · 地波羅蜜多品第七

The Grounds and Perfections

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva asks how the bodhisattva grounds are included, how their obstructions are counteracted, and how the perfections are cultivated, purified, and brought to completion on the path to buddhahood.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: chapter 7 complete Source: Taishō T16, no. 676 Chinese translator: Xuanzang

Four Purities and Eleven Factors 四清淨十一分

Chinese Source

爾時，觀自在菩薩白佛言：「世尊！如佛所說菩薩十地，所謂極喜地、離垢地、發光地、焰慧地、極難勝地、現前地、遠行地、不動地、善慧地、法雲地；復說佛地為第十一。如是諸地，幾種清淨？幾分所攝？」

爾時，世尊告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！當知諸地四種清淨、十一分攝。

「云何名為四種清淨能攝諸地？謂增上意樂清淨攝於初地；增上戒清淨攝第二地；增上心清淨攝第三地；增上慧清淨於後後地轉勝妙故，當知能攝從第四地乃至佛地。善男子！當知如是四種清淨普攝諸地。

「云何名為十一種分能攝諸地？謂諸菩薩先於勝解行地，依十法行，極善修習勝解忍故，超過彼地，證入菩薩正性離生。彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能於微細毀犯誤現行中正知而行，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能得世間圓滿、等持、等至及圓滿聞持陀羅尼，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能令隨所獲得菩提分法，多修習住，心未能捨諸等至愛及與法愛，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能於諸諦道理如實觀察，又未能於生死涅槃棄捨一向背趣作意，又未能修方便所攝菩提分法，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能於生死流轉如實觀察，又由於彼多生厭故，未能多住無相作意，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能令無相作意、無缺、無間、多修習住，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能於無相住中捨離功用，又未能得於相自在，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能於異名眾相、訓詞差別、一切品類宣說法中，得大自在，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能得圓滿法身現前證受，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，彼諸菩薩由是因緣，此分圓滿。而未能得遍於一切所知境界無著、無礙、妙智、妙見，由是因緣，於此分中猶未圓滿。為令此分得圓滿故，精勤修習便能證得，由是因緣，此分圓滿。此分滿故，於一切分皆得圓滿。善男子！當知如是十一種分普攝諸地。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, as the Buddha has taught the ten bodhisattva grounds, namely the Ground of Great Joy, the Stainless Ground, the Luminous Ground, the Blazing Wisdom Ground, the Ground Difficult to Conquer, the Manifest Ground, the Far-Reaching Ground, the Immovable Ground, the Good Wisdom Ground, and the Dharma Cloud Ground, and has further taught the Buddha ground as the eleventh, by how many purities and how many factors are these grounds included?"

At that time, the World-Honored One told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, you should understand that the grounds are included by four purities and eleven factors.

"What are the four purities that include the grounds? The purity of superior intention includes the first ground. The purity of superior discipline includes the second ground. The purity of superior mind includes the third ground. Because the purity of superior wisdom becomes increasingly excellent and wondrous on the later grounds, you should understand that it includes the fourth ground up to the Buddha ground. Good son, you should understand that these four purities universally include all the grounds.

"What are the eleven factors that include the grounds? First, while still on the ground of conduct through resolute understanding, bodhisattvas rely on the ten Dharma practices and cultivate the forbearance of resolute understanding extremely well. Therefore they pass beyond that ground and enter the bodhisattvas' fixed rightness beyond ordinary birth. Through this condition, this factor is complete. Yet they are still unable to act with right knowing amid the mistaken manifest activity of subtle transgressions; for this reason, within this factor, they are not yet complete. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it. Through this condition, this factor is complete.

"Yet they are still unable to obtain the worldly completions of equipoise, meditative attainments, and perfect dhāraṇī of retention through hearing. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it. Yet they cannot cause the factors of awakening they have obtained to be cultivated often and made to abide, and their minds cannot yet abandon attachment to meditative attainments and attachment to Dharma. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it. Yet they cannot observe the principles of the truths exactly as they are, cannot abandon one-sided attention that turns away from saṃsāra and toward nirvāṇa, and cannot cultivate factors of awakening included by skillful means. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it.

"Yet they cannot observe the flowing turning of saṃsāra exactly as it is, and because they give rise to much disenchantment toward it, they cannot often abide in signless attention. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it. Yet they cannot make signless attention abide without deficiency, without interruption, and through much cultivation. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it. Yet, while abiding in the signless, they cannot abandon exertion, nor can they obtain mastery over characteristics. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it.

"Yet they cannot gain great mastery in proclaiming Dharma through different names, many characteristics, distinctions in explanations, and all categories. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it. Yet they cannot directly realize and receive the complete dharma-body. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it. Yet they cannot obtain wondrous knowledge and wondrous vision, unattached and unobstructed throughout every object-field of what can be known. To make this factor complete, they cultivate diligently and then realize it. Through this condition, this factor is complete. Because this factor is complete, all factors are complete. Good son, you should understand that these eleven factors universally include all the grounds."

Names, Obstructions, and Excellence of the Grounds 地名與所治

Chinese Source

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！何緣最初名極喜地？乃至何緣說名佛地？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！成就大義，得未曾得出世間心，生大歡喜，是故最初名極喜地。遠離一切微細犯戒，是故第二名離垢地。由彼所得三摩地及聞持陀羅尼，能為無量智光依止，是故第三名發光地。由彼所得菩提分法，燒諸煩惱，智如火焰，是故第四名焰慧地。由即於彼菩提分法，方便修習最極艱難，方得自在，是故第五名極難勝地。現前觀察諸行流轉，又於無相多修作意方現在前，是故第六名現前地。能遠證入無缺無間無相作意，與清淨地共相隣接，是故第七名遠行地。由於無相得無功用，於諸相中不為現行煩惱所動，是故第八名不動地。於一切種說法自在，獲得無罪廣大智慧，是故第九名善慧地。麁重之身，廣如虛空，法身圓滿，譬如大雲，皆能遍覆，是故第十名法雲地。永斷最極微細煩惱及所知障，無著無礙，於一切種所知境界，現正等覺，故第十一說名佛地。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「於此諸地有幾愚癡、有幾麁重為所對治？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！此諸地中有二十二種愚癡、十一種麁重為所對治。謂於初地有二愚癡：一者、執著補特伽羅及法愚癡，二者、惡趣雜染愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於第二地有二愚癡：一者、微細誤犯愚癡，二者、種種業趣愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於第三地有二愚癡：一者、欲貪愚癡，二者、圓滿聞持陀羅尼愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於第四地有二愚癡：一者、等至愛愚癡，二者、法愛愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於第五地有二愚癡：一者、一向作意棄背生死愚癡，二者、一向作意趣向涅槃愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於第六地有二愚癡：一者、現前觀察諸行流轉愚癡，二者、相多現行愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於第七地有二愚癡：一者、微細相現行愚癡，二者一向無相作意方便愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於第八地有二愚癡：一者、於無相作功用愚癡，二者、於相自在愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於第九地有二愚癡：一者、於無量說法、無量法句文字、後後慧辯陀羅尼自在愚癡，二者辯才自在愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於第十地有二愚癡：一者、大神通愚癡，二者、悟入微細祕密愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。於如來地有二愚癡：一者、於一切所知境界極微細著愚癡，二者、極微細礙愚癡；及彼麁重為所對治。

「善男子！由此二十二種愚癡及十一種麁重故，安立諸地；而阿耨多羅三藐三菩提離彼繫縛。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！阿耨多羅三藐三菩提甚奇希有，乃至成就大利大果，令諸菩薩能破如是大愚癡羅網，能越如是大麁重稠林，現前證得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, for what reason is the first called the Ground of Great Joy, and so on up to the reason it is called the Buddha ground?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, because one accomplishes the great meaning, obtains a world-transcending mind never obtained before, and gives rise to great joy, the first is called the Ground of Great Joy. Because one is far removed from all subtle breaches of discipline, the second is called the Stainless Ground. Because the samādhi and hearing-retention dhāraṇī obtained there can serve as the support for immeasurable light of wisdom, the third is called the Luminous Ground. Because the factors of awakening obtained there burn up afflictions, and wisdom is like a flame, the fourth is called the Blazing Wisdom Ground. Because only through extremely difficult cultivation of skillful means with regard to those very factors of awakening does one gain mastery, the fifth is called the Ground Difficult to Conquer. Because one directly observes the flowing turning of conditioned practices, and only then, through much cultivation of signless attention, does it become directly present, the sixth is called the Manifest Ground. Because one can enter far into signless attention that is without deficiency and without interruption, and because this borders the purified ground, the seventh is called the Far-Reaching Ground. Because one attains effortlessness in the signless and is not moved among characteristics by manifest afflictions, the eighth is called the Immovable Ground. Because one has mastery in teaching Dharma in every way and obtains vast faultless wisdom, the ninth is called the Good Wisdom Ground. Because the body of coarseness and heaviness becomes vast like empty space, and the complete dharma-body is like a great cloud covering all, the tenth is called the Dharma Cloud Ground. Because the most extremely subtle afflictions and cognitive obstruction are permanently severed, and because, unattached and unobstructed, one directly and perfectly awakens to every kind of knowable object-field, the eleventh is called the Buddha ground."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "In these grounds, how many foolishnesses and how many coarsenesses and heavinesses are counteracted?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, in these grounds, twenty-two kinds of foolishness and eleven kinds of coarseness and heaviness are counteracted. On the first ground there are two foolishnesses: foolishness of attachment to persons and dharmas, and foolishness of defilement in the lower destinies; together with the coarseness and heaviness belonging to them. On the second ground there are two: foolishness of subtle mistaken transgression and foolishness regarding the destinies of various karmas; together with their coarseness and heaviness. On the third ground there are two: foolishness of desire-greed and foolishness regarding the complete dhāraṇī of retention through hearing. On the fourth there are attachment to meditative attainments and attachment to Dharma. On the fifth there are one-sided attention that turns away from saṃsāra and one-sided attention that tends toward nirvāṇa. On the sixth there are foolishness regarding direct observation of the flowing of conditioned practices and foolishness of the frequent manifest activity of characteristics. On the seventh there are foolishness of the manifest activity of subtle characteristics and foolishness regarding the skillful means of single-minded signless attention. On the eighth there are foolishness of exerting effort toward the signless and foolishness regarding mastery over characteristics. On the ninth there are foolishness regarding mastery in boundless Dharma teaching, boundless Dharma phrases and words, later and still later wisdom, eloquence, and dhāraṇī, and foolishness regarding mastery in eloquence. On the tenth there are foolishness regarding great superknowledges and foolishness regarding entry into subtle secrets. On the Tathāgata ground there are foolishness of extremely subtle attachment to every knowable object-field and foolishness of extremely subtle obstruction. Their coarseness and heaviness are also counteracted.

"Good son, because of these twenty-two kinds of foolishness and eleven kinds of coarseness and heaviness, the grounds are established. Yet unsurpassed, complete, perfect awakening is free from their bondage."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, unsurpassed, complete, perfect awakening is marvelous and rare, accomplishing such great benefit and great fruit, enabling bodhisattvas to break through this great net of foolishness, pass beyond this dense forest of coarseness and heaviness, and directly realize unsurpassed, complete, perfect awakening."

Eight Excellences, Birth, Vows, and Training 八種殊勝與學事

Chinese Source

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！如是諸地，幾種殊勝之所安立？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！略有八種：一者、增上意樂清淨；二者、心清淨；三者、悲清淨；四者、到彼岸清淨；五者、見佛供養承事清淨；六者、成熟有情清淨；七者、生清淨；八者、威德清淨。

「善男子！於初地中，所有增上意樂清淨，乃至威德清淨；後後諸地乃至佛地，所有增上意樂清淨，乃至威德清淨，當知彼諸清淨展轉增勝，唯於佛地除生清淨。又初地中所有功德，於上諸地平等皆有，當知自地功德殊勝。一切菩薩十地功德皆是有上，佛地功德當知無上。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！何因緣故說菩薩生，於諸有生最為殊勝？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！四因緣故：一者、極淨善根所集起故；二者、故意思擇力所取故；三者、悲愍濟度諸眾生故；四者、自能無染除他染故。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！何因緣故說諸菩薩行廣大願、妙願、勝願？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！四因緣故：謂諸菩薩，能善了知涅槃樂住，堪能速證；而復棄捨速證樂住；無緣無待發大願心；為欲利益諸有情故，處多種種長時大苦。是故我說彼諸菩薩行廣大願、妙願、勝願。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！是諸菩薩凡有幾種所應學事？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！菩薩學事略有六種：所謂布施、持戒、忍辱、精進、靜慮、慧到彼岸。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！如是六種所應學事，幾是增上戒學所攝？幾是增上心學所攝？幾是增上慧學所攝？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！當知初三，但是增上戒學所攝；靜慮一種，但是增上心學所攝；慧是增上慧學所攝；我說精進遍於一切。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！如是六種所應學事，幾是福德資糧所攝？幾是智慧資糧所攝？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！若增上戒學所攝者，是名福德資糧所攝；若增上慧學所攝者，是名智慧資糧所攝；我說精進、靜慮二種遍於一切。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！於此六種所學事中，菩薩云何應當修學？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！由五種相應當修學：一者、最初於菩薩藏波羅蜜多相應微妙正法教中，猛利信解；二者、次於十種法行，以聞、思、修所成妙智，精進修行；三者、隨護菩提之心；四者、親近真善知識；五者、無間勤修善品。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, by how many kinds of excellence are these grounds established?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, in brief there are eight: purity of superior intention, purity of mind, purity of compassion, purity of the perfections, purity in seeing buddhas and making offerings and service to them, purity in maturing sentient beings, purity of birth, and purity of majestic virtue.

"Good son, on the first ground, there is purity of superior intention up through purity of majestic virtue. On each later ground up to the Buddha ground, there is likewise purity of superior intention up through purity of majestic virtue. You should understand that these purities become progressively more excellent, except that on the Buddha ground there is no purity of birth. Moreover, all the virtues present on the first ground are equally present on the higher grounds, while the virtues proper to each ground are especially excellent. The virtues of all ten bodhisattva grounds are still surpassed by something higher. You should understand that the virtues of the Buddha ground are unsurpassed."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, for what reason is the birth of bodhisattvas said to be most excellent among all births in existence?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, for four reasons: it is gathered and arisen from supremely pure roots of goodness; it is taken up by the power of deliberate discernment; it is for compassionately rescuing and liberating sentient beings; and one can remain undefiled oneself while removing the defilement of others."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, for what reason are bodhisattvas said to practice vast vows, wondrous vows, and excellent vows?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, for four reasons. Bodhisattvas can well understand the joyful abiding of nirvāṇa and are able to realize it quickly. Yet they abandon quick realization of that joyful abiding. Without condition and without waiting, they give rise to the mind of great vow. For the sake of benefiting sentient beings, they remain for a long time amid many kinds of great suffering. Therefore I say those bodhisattvas practice vast vows, wondrous vows, and excellent vows."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, how many matters of training do these bodhisattvas have?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, bodhisattva training matters are, in brief, six: the perfections of generosity, discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, and wisdom."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, of these six matters to be trained in, how many are included in the training of superior discipline? How many in the training of superior mind? How many in the training of superior wisdom?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, you should understand that the first three are included only in the training of superior discipline. The single perfection of meditative concentration is included only in the training of superior mind. Wisdom is included in the training of superior wisdom. I say that diligence pervades them all."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, of these six matters to be trained in, how many are included in the provision of merit? How many are included in the provision of wisdom?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, what is included in the training of superior discipline is included in the provision of merit. What is included in the training of superior wisdom is included in the provision of wisdom. I say that diligence and meditative concentration pervade them all."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, how should bodhisattvas train in these six matters of training?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, they should train through five characteristics. First, from the beginning, they have sharp trust and understanding toward the wondrous true Dharma teaching in the bodhisattva collection that corresponds to the perfections. Second, with wondrous wisdom produced by hearing, thinking, and cultivation, they diligently cultivate the ten Dharma practices. Third, they protect the mind of bodhi. Fourth, they draw near to true good spiritual friends. Fifth, without interruption, they diligently cultivate wholesome qualities."

The Six and Ten Perfections 六度十度

Chinese Source

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！何因緣故，施設如是所應學事，但有六數？」

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, for what reason are the matters to be trained in established as only six in number?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, for two reasons: to benefit sentient beings and to counteract afflictions. You should understand that the first three benefit sentient beings, while the latter three counteract all afflictions. The first three benefit sentient beings in this way: by generosity, bodhisattvas take up supports and supplies and benefit sentient beings; by discipline, they do not harm, oppress, or disturb sentient beings, and so benefit them; by patience, they are able to bear harm, oppression, and disturbance from them, and so benefit sentient beings. The latter three counteract all afflictions in this way: through diligence, although bodhisattvas have not yet permanently subdued all afflictions or permanently destroyed all latent tendencies, they can courageously cultivate wholesome qualities, so that those afflictions cannot shake their applied practice in wholesome qualities; through meditative concentration, they permanently subdue afflictions; through prajñā, they permanently destroy latent tendencies."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, for what reason are the remaining perfections established as only four in number?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, because they serve as companions to the previous six perfections. Bodhisattvas take in sentient beings included by the first three perfections through the means of gathering, by skillful means, and establish them in wholesome qualities. Therefore I say that the perfection of skillful means is the companion of the first three. If bodhisattvas, because they have many afflictions in the present life, are unable to cultivate without interruption; because of weak intention and lower-realm understanding, are unable to abide inwardly in mind; and because they cannot hear, take as object, and well cultivate the bodhisattva collection, their meditative concentrations cannot give rise to world-transcending wisdom. They then gather a small and narrow provision of merit and give rise to right vows so that in a future life their afflictions will be light. This is called the perfection of vow. Because of this vow, their afflictions become slight and they can cultivate diligence. Therefore I say that the perfection of vow is the companion of the perfection of diligence.

"If bodhisattvas draw near to good persons, hear the true Dharma, and attend according to the principle, then by these conditions they transform inferior intention into excellent intention and can also obtain higher-realm understanding. This is called the perfection of power. Because of this power, they become able to abide inwardly in mind. Therefore I say that the perfection of power is the companion of the perfection of meditative concentration. If bodhisattvas are already able to hear, take as object, and well cultivate the bodhisattva collection, and can give rise to meditative concentration, this is called the perfection of wisdom. Because of this wisdom, they are able to give rise to world-transcending wisdom. Therefore I say that the perfection of wisdom is the companion of the perfection of prajñā."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, for what reason are the six perfections taught in this order?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, because each later one is brought forth depending on the previous one. If bodhisattvas have no clinging or stinginess toward body and possessions, they can receive and uphold pure precepts. To guard the precepts, they cultivate patience. Having cultivated patience, they can arouse diligence. Having aroused diligence, they can accomplish meditative concentration. Having meditative concentration, they can obtain world-transcending wisdom. Therefore I teach the perfections in this order."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, how many categories and distinctions does each of these six perfections have?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, each has three kinds. Generosity has three: giving Dharma, giving material goods, and giving fearlessness. Discipline has three: turning away from unwholesome discipline, giving rise to wholesome discipline, and giving rise to the discipline of benefiting sentient beings. Patience has three: patience in enduring enemies' harm, patience in accepting suffering, and patience in carefully examining dharmas. Diligence has three: armor-like diligence, applied diligence that gives rise to wholesome dharmas, and applied diligence that benefits sentient beings. Meditative concentration has three: meditative concentration as joyful abiding, which is non-discriminating, quiescent, extremely quiescent, faultless, and counteracts afflictions and many sufferings; meditative concentration that brings forth virtues; and meditative concentration that brings forth benefit for sentient beings. Wisdom has three: wisdom taking conventional truth as its object, wisdom taking ultimate truth as its object, and wisdom taking the benefit of sentient beings as its object."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, for what reason are the perfections called perfections?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, for five reasons: because there is no attachment, no clinging, no fault, no discrimination, and right dedication. No attachment means not being attached to things opposed to the perfections. No clinging means that the mind is not bound to any fruit and maturation of the perfections or to being repaid for kindness. No fault means that in these perfections, one has no uninterruptedly mixed defiled dharmas and is free from unskillful conduct. No discrimination means that, in these perfections, one does not cling to own-characteristics according to words. Right dedication means turning the perfections one has performed and accumulated toward the fruit of unsurpassed great bodhi."

"World-Honored One, what are called things opposed to the perfections?"

"Good son, you should understand that, in brief, there are six. First, deeply seeing virtues and advantages in pleasures of desire such as delight in desire, wealth, and freedom. Second, deeply seeing virtues and advantages in letting body, speech, and mind act as one likes. Third, deeply seeing virtues and advantages in being unable to bear others' contempt. Fourth, deeply seeing virtues and advantages in not cultivating diligently and being attached to pleasures of desire. Fifth, deeply seeing virtues and advantages in dwelling amid noise, busyness, worldly confusion, and mixed activity. Sixth, deeply seeing virtues and advantages in conceptual play based on seeing, hearing, sensing, knowing, and speaking."

"World-Honored One, what are the fruits and maturations of all these perfections?"

"Good son, you should understand that these too are, in brief, six: obtaining great wealth, being reborn in good destinies, having no enemies and no harm with many kinds of joy and happiness, becoming a leader of beings, having a body free from distress and harm, and having a great lineage."

"World-Honored One, what are called defiled dharmas mixed into the perfections?"

"Good son, you should understand them briefly through four kinds of applied practice: applied practice without compassion, applied practice not according to the principle, inconstant applied practice, and applied practice that is not earnest. Applied practice not according to the principle means that when cultivating one perfection, one is far removed from and damages the other perfections."

"World-Honored One, what is called unskillful conduct?"

"Good son, when bodhisattvas benefit sentient beings through the perfections, if they are satisfied merely by gathering material goods and benefiting sentient beings in that way, without causing them to leave unwholesome places and establishing them in wholesome places, this is called unskillful conduct. Why? Good son, merely doing this for sentient beings is not called true benefit. It is like filth: whether much or little, no one can make it fragrant and clean. In the same way, because sentient beings are conditioned by the suffering of formations, their nature is suffering. There is no skillful means by which merely giving material goods for temporary benefit could make them happy. Only establishing them in wondrous wholesome Dharma can be called the highest benefit."

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！何因緣故，是諸菩薩深信愛樂波羅蜜多，非於如是波羅蜜多所得可愛諸果異熟？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！五因緣故：一者、波羅蜜多是最增上喜樂因故；二者、波羅蜜多是其究竟饒益一切自他因故；三者、波羅蜜多是當來世彼可愛果異熟因故；四者、波羅蜜多非諸雜染所依事故；五者、波羅蜜多非是畢竟變壞法故。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！一切波羅蜜多，各有幾種最勝威德？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！當知一切波羅蜜多，各有四種最勝威德：一者、於此波羅蜜多正修行時，能捨慳悋、犯戒、心憤、懈怠、散亂、見趣所治；二者、於此正修行時，能為無上正等菩提、真實資糧；三者、於此正修行時，於現法中能自攝受饒益有情；四者、於此正修行時，於未來世能得廣大無盡可愛諸果異熟。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！如是一切波羅蜜多，何因？何果？有何義利？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！如是一切波羅蜜多，大悲為因；微妙可愛諸果異熟，饒益一切有情為果；圓滿無上廣大菩提為大義利。」

觀自在菩薩白佛言：「世尊！若諸菩薩具足一切無盡財寶成就大悲，何緣世間現有眾生貧窮可得？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！是諸眾生自業過失！若不爾者，菩薩常懷饒益他心，又常具足無盡財寶，若諸眾生無自惡業能為障礙，何有世間貧窮可得？譬如餓鬼為大熱渴逼迫其身，見大海水悉皆涸竭；非大海過，是諸餓鬼自業過耳。如是菩薩所施財寶猶如大海無有過失，是諸眾生自業過耳，猶如餓鬼自惡業力令無有果。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, what activity does each of these five characteristics have?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, you should understand that those characteristics have five activities. Because bodhisattvas have no attachment, in the present Dharma they always and earnestly apply themselves diligently and without carelessness to the perfections they cultivate. Because they have no clinging, they take up causes for future freedom from carelessness. Because they have no fault, they can rightly cultivate perfections that are perfectly complete, perfectly pure, and perfectly bright. Because they have no discrimination, the perfection of skillful means is quickly completed. Because they dedicate rightly, the perfections in every birthplace and their lovely fruits and maturations are all inexhaustible, up to unsurpassed, complete, perfect awakening."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, among the perfections just taught, which are most vast, which are undefiled, which are most bright, which are immovable, and which are most pure?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, the nature of no attachment, the nature of no clinging, and the nature of right dedication are most vast. The nature of no fault and the nature of no discrimination are undefiled. What is done through discernment is most bright. Those who have entered the ground of irreversible dharmas are called immovable. What is included by the ten grounds and by the Buddha ground is called most pure."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, for what reason are the lovely fruits and maturations of the perfections obtained by bodhisattvas always inexhaustible, and why are the perfections themselves inexhaustible?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, because they arise and are cultivated in uninterrupted succession, depending on one another."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, for what reason do these bodhisattvas deeply trust in and delight in the perfections, rather than in the lovely fruits and maturations obtained from those perfections?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, for five reasons: the perfections are the supreme cause of joy and happiness; the perfections are the cause of the ultimate benefit of all, self and others; the perfections are the cause of those lovely fruits and maturations in future lives; the perfections are not a basis for defilements; and the perfections are not dharmas that finally change and decay."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, how many supreme majestic virtues does each perfection have?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, you should understand that each perfection has four supreme majestic virtues. First, while one is rightly practicing that perfection, one can abandon the counteractives of stinginess, breach of discipline, anger in the mind, laziness, distraction, and tendencies of view. Second, while one is rightly practicing it, it can become a true provision for unsurpassed, complete, perfect awakening. Third, while one is rightly practicing it, in the present Dharma one can gather and benefit sentient beings by oneself. Fourth, while one is rightly practicing it, in future lives one can obtain vast, inexhaustible, lovely fruits and maturations."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, what is the cause of all these perfections, what is their fruit, and what is their great benefit?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, all these perfections have great compassion as their cause; their fruits are subtle, wondrous, and lovely maturations, and the benefiting of all sentient beings; and their great benefit is the completion of unsurpassed, vast bodhi."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, if bodhisattvas possess all inexhaustible treasures and accomplish great compassion, for what reason can poverty still be found among beings in the world?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, this is the fault of those sentient beings' own karma. If it were not so, bodhisattvas always cherish the mind of benefiting others and always possess inexhaustible treasures. If sentient beings had no bad karma of their own that could obstruct them, how could poverty be found in the world? It is like hungry ghosts tormented in body by great heat and thirst: when they see the water of the great ocean, it all appears dried up. This is not the fault of the ocean; it is only the fault of the hungry ghosts' own karma. In the same way, the treasures given by bodhisattvas are like a great ocean and have no fault. It is only the fault of sentient beings' own karma, like the evil karmic power of hungry ghosts that causes there to be no fruit."

Naturelessness, Grades of Perfection, and Latent Tendencies 無自性與隨眠

Chinese Source

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！菩薩以何等波羅蜜多，取一切法無自性性？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！以般若波羅蜜多能取諸法無自性性。」

「世尊！若般若波羅蜜多能取諸法無自性性，何故不取有自性性？」

「善男子！我終不說以無自性性取無自性性。然無自性性，離諸文字自內所證；不可捨於言說文字而能宣說。是故我說般若波羅蜜多能取諸法無自性性。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！如佛所說波羅蜜多，近波羅蜜多，大波羅蜜多。云何波羅蜜多？云何近波羅蜜多？云何大波羅蜜多？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！若諸菩薩經無量時修行施等成就善法，而諸煩惱猶故現行，未能制伏然為彼伏，謂於勝解行地軟中勝解轉時，是名波羅蜜多。復於無量時修行施等，漸復增上，成就善法，而諸煩惱猶故現行，然能制伏非彼所伏，謂從初地已上，是名近波羅蜜多。復於無量時修行布施等，轉復增上，成就善法，一切煩惱皆不現行，謂從八地已上，是名大波羅蜜多。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！此諸地中煩惱隨眠可有幾種？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！略有三種：一者、害伴隨眠，謂於前五地。何以故？善男子！諸不俱生現行煩惱，是俱生煩惱現行助伴，彼於爾時永無復有，是故說名害伴隨眠；二者、羸劣隨眠，謂於第六、第七地中微細現行，若修所伏不現行故；三者、微細隨眠，謂於第八地已上，從此已去，一切煩惱不復現行，唯有所知障為依止故。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！此諸隨眠，幾種麁重斷所顯示？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！但由二種：謂由在皮麁重斷故，顯彼初二，復由在膚麁重斷故，顯彼第三。若在於骨麁重斷者，我說永離一切隨眠，位在佛地。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！經幾不可數劫能斷如是麁重？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！經於三大不可數劫、或無量劫，所謂年、月、半月、晝夜、一時、半時、須臾、瞬息、剎那量劫不可數故。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！是諸菩薩於諸地中所生煩惱，當知何相？何失？何德？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！無染污相。何以故？是諸菩薩於初地中，定於一切諸法法界已善通達，由此因緣，菩薩要知方起煩惱非為不知，是故說名無染污相。於自身中不能生苦，故無過失。菩薩生起如是煩惱，於有情界能斷苦因，是故彼有無量功德。」

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「甚奇！世尊！無上菩提乃有如是大功德利，令諸菩薩生起煩惱，尚勝一切有情、聲聞、獨覺善根，何況其餘無量功德？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, by which perfection do bodhisattvas take up the naturelessness of all dharmas?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, by the perfection of prajñā they can take up the naturelessness of dharmas."

"World-Honored One, if the perfection of prajñā can take up the naturelessness of dharmas, why does it not take up their nature of having own-nature?"

"Good son, I never say that naturelessness takes up naturelessness. Yet naturelessness is separated from all words and letters and is realized within oneself. It cannot be proclaimed while abandoning spoken words and letters. Therefore I say that the perfection of prajñā can take up the naturelessness of dharmas."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, the Buddha has taught perfection, near perfection, and great perfection. What is perfection, what is near perfection, and what is great perfection?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, if bodhisattvas cultivate generosity and the others through boundless time and accomplish wholesome dharmas, yet afflictions still manifest as before, and they cannot subdue them but are subdued by them, namely while weak or middling resolute understanding turns on the ground of conduct through resolute understanding, this is called perfection. Again, if through boundless time they cultivate generosity and the others, gradually increase further, and accomplish wholesome dharmas, while afflictions still manifest as before, yet they can subdue them and are not subdued by them, namely from the first ground upward, this is called near perfection. Again, if through boundless time they cultivate generosity and the others, increase still further, and accomplish wholesome dharmas, so that no afflictions manifest at all, namely from the eighth ground upward, this is called great perfection."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, how many kinds of latent afflictive tendencies can there be on these grounds?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, in brief there are three. First are latent tendencies whose companions have been harmed, found on the first five grounds. Why? Good son, manifest afflictions that are not innate are companions supporting the manifest activity of innate afflictions. At that time they permanently no longer exist, and therefore they are called latent tendencies whose companions have been harmed. Second are weak latent tendencies, found on the sixth and seventh grounds, because subtle manifest activity is subdued by cultivation and does not manifest. Third are subtle latent tendencies, found from the eighth ground upward. From then on, all afflictions no longer manifest, and only the cognitive obstruction remains as a basis."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, by how many kinds of severing of coarseness and heaviness are these latent tendencies indicated?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, only by two kinds. The first two are indicated by severing coarseness and heaviness that is in the skin. The third is indicated by severing coarseness and heaviness that is in the flesh. When coarseness and heaviness in the bones is severed, I say one is permanently separated from all latent tendencies, and that stage is the Buddha ground."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, through how many incalculable eons can such coarseness and heaviness be severed?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, through three great incalculable eons, or through boundless eons, because the eons measured by years, months, half-months, days and nights, hours, half-hours, brief moments, instants, and kṣaṇas are incalculable."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, regarding afflictions that arise for bodhisattvas on the grounds, what characteristic, what fault, and what virtue should be understood?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, they have the characteristic of not being defiled. Why? On the first ground, these bodhisattvas have definitely penetrated well the dharma-realm of all dharmas. Through this condition, bodhisattvas give rise to afflictions only knowingly, not unknowingly. Therefore they are called undefiled in characteristic. They cannot produce suffering in their own bodies, so they are without fault. When bodhisattvas give rise to such afflictions, they can sever causes of suffering in the realm of sentient beings. Therefore they have immeasurable virtue."

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "How marvelous, World-Honored One. Unsurpassed bodhi has such great virtuous benefit that even the afflictions bodhisattvas give rise to surpass all roots of goodness of sentient beings, śrāvakas, and solitary buddhas, to say nothing of its other immeasurable virtues."

The Hidden Intent of One Vehicle 一乘密意

Chinese Source

觀自在菩薩復白佛言：「世尊！如世尊說：『若聲聞乘、若復大乘，唯是一乘。』此何密意？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！如我於彼聲聞乘中，宣說種種諸法自性，所謂五蘊、或內六處、或外六處，如是等類；於大乘中，即說彼法同一法界、同一理趣，故我不說乘差別性。於中或有如言於義妄起分別，一類增益，一類損減。又於諸乘差別道理，謂互相違，如是展轉遞興諍論。如是名為此中密意。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, as the World-Honored One has said, 'Whether the śrāvaka vehicle or the Great Vehicle, there is only one vehicle.' What hidden intent does this have?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, in the śrāvaka vehicle I proclaim the various own-natures of dharmas, such as the five aggregates, the six inner sense-fields, the six outer sense-fields, and so on. In the Great Vehicle, I teach that those very dharmas share one and the same dharma-realm and one and the same principle. Therefore I do not teach a nature of difference among the vehicles. Within this, some people follow the words and wrongly discriminate about the meaning: one group superimposes, another group denies. Again, with regard to the principles by which the vehicles differ, they say these contradict one another, and in this way, back and forth, disputes arise. This is called the hidden intent here."

The Teaching's Title

Chinese Source

爾時，觀自在菩薩摩訶薩復白佛言：「世尊！於是解深密法門中，此名何教？我當云何奉持？」

佛告觀自在菩薩曰：「善男子！此名諸地波羅蜜多了義之教，於此諸地波羅蜜多了義之教，汝當奉持！」

說此諸地波羅蜜多了義教時，於大會中有七十五千菩薩，皆得菩薩大乘光明三摩地。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Bodhisattva Mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara again said to the Buddha, "World-Honored One, within this Dharma-gateway of the Saṃdhinirmocana, what is this teaching called? How should I uphold it?"

The Buddha told Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, "Good son, this is called the definitive teaching of the grounds and perfections. You should uphold it as the definitive teaching of the grounds and perfections."

When this definitive teaching of the grounds and perfections was spoken, within the great assembly seventy-five thousand bodhisattvas all obtained the bodhisattva Great Vehicle Light Samādhi.

Reading note: This chapter continues the previous chapter's meditation teaching by mapping its results across the grounds. It keeps two truths together: the vehicles are taught with different analyses and capacities, yet the dharmas they disclose share one dharma-realm and one principle.
