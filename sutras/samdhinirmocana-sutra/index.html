Unraveling the Hidden Intent.

The Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra is one of the central scriptures of Yogācāra Buddhism. It explains why the Buddha sometimes speaks in hidden intent, how ultimate truth stands beyond language, how the Three Natures and three naturelessnesses should be understood, and how bodhisattvas cultivate meditation, the grounds, and buddhahood.

How to read this project

This translation is being built directly from Xuanzang's Chinese text, with the Chinese source beside Ru-Yi's English translation. Because this sūtra is the scriptural root of many later Yogācāra doctrines, terminology follows the site's completed Cheng Weishi Lun translation wherever the two works overlap.

The project has begun with chapters 1 through 5. Later chapters will be added in order, with reading notes where a modern English reader needs the doctrinal thread made visible.

What each chapter page includes

  • The Chinese source text from CBETA's Taishō T676 edition.
  • Ru-Yi's plain-English translation, kept close to Xuanzang's Chinese.
  • Reading notes for Yogācāra doctrine, key terms, and the sūtra's structure.
  • Honest status labels so incomplete chapters are not mistaken for finished work.

Read by Chapter

The Chinese text has five fascicles and eight chapters. This reading path follows the chapters, since each one is framed by a bodhisattva's question and a distinct doctrinal theme.

Chapter 1 Introductory Chapter 序品第一 The pure Buddha-field, the assembly of śrāvakas and bodhisattvas, and the cast of questioners who will unfold the whole sūtra. Chapter 2 The Characteristic of the Ultimate Truth 勝義諦相品第二 Why ultimate truth is beyond language, thought, sameness, and difference, and is one taste throughout all dharmas. Chapter 3 Mind, Intellect, and Consciousness 心意識相品第三 The scriptural basis for the ādāna-consciousness, ālayavijñāna, and the six active consciousnesses. Chapter 4 The Characteristics of All Dharmas 一切法相品第四 The Three Natures as the structure beneath naming, dependent arising, and realized purity. Chapter 5 The Characteristic of Naturelessness 無自性相品第五 The three naturelessnesses, hidden intent, the one vehicle, and the definitive third turning of the Dharma wheel. Chapter 6 Analyzing Yogic Practice 分別瑜伽品第六 Maitreya's questions on śamatha, vipaśyanā, images, attention, and the bodhisattva's meditative path. Chapter 7 The Grounds and Perfections 地波羅蜜多品第七 The bodhisattva grounds, the perfections, and the path's progressive purification. Chapter 8 What the Tathāgata Accomplishes 如來成所作事品第八 Mañjuśrī's questions on the Buddha's body, speech, activity, and final accomplishment.

Translation Notes

Recurring decisions are recorded in this project's translation-notes.md, and shared Yogācāra terminology follows the Cheng Weishi Lun glossary unless this sūtra requires a special distinction.

解深密經 — Saṃdhinirmocana SūtraThe title means a sūtra that unravels or discloses the Buddha's hidden intent.
勝義諦 — the ultimate truthThe reality directly realized by noble wisdom, not reached by ordinary language or conceptual debate.
離言法性 — the dharma-nature apart from languageA central phrase for what the awakened ones know directly and name only provisionally for the sake of others.
三性 — the Three NaturesThe imagined or discriminated nature, the dependent nature, and the perfectly accomplished nature.
