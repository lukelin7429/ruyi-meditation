The Buddha-Ground's Eight Terms

Having explained the tathāgatagarbha as it appears wrapped in affliction, the treatise now turns to explain it free of every taint — buddhahood itself, analyzed through eight terms: essence, cause, fruit, function, correspondence, manifestation, constancy, and inconceivability. Purity here divides into two kinds — the purity a being's mind already has by nature, and the purity that results from actually removing what obscured it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 24 complete Source: Taishō T1611 (tr. Ratnamati, 511 CE)

Chapter Eight: The Buddha-Ground's Eight Terms

Chinese Source

論曰：已說有垢如，自此以下說無垢如應知。無垢如者，謂諸佛如來於無漏法界中遠離一切種種諸垢，轉雜穢身得淨妙身，依八句義略差別說彼真如性無漏法身應知。何等為八？偈言：

淨得及遠離，　自他利相應，
依止深快大，　時數如彼法。

是名八種句義次第。一偈示現八種義者，何謂八種？一者實體、二者因、三者果、四者業、五者相應、六者行、七者常、八者不可思議。

實體者，向說如來藏不離煩惱藏所纏，以遠離諸煩惱轉身得清淨，是名為實體應知。偈言：淨故。是故《聖者勝鬘經》言：世尊！若於無量煩惱藏所纏如來藏不疑惑者，於出無量煩惱藏法身亦無疑惑故。

因者，有二種無分別智：一者出世間無分別智、二者依出世間智得世間出世間依止行智，是名為因。偈言：得故。

果者：即依此得，得證智果，是名為果。偈言：遠離故。

業者，有二種遠離：一者遠離煩惱障、二者遠離智障，如是次第故名遠離。如是遠離自利利他成就，是名為業。偈言：自他利故。

相應者，自利利他得無量功德，常畢竟住持，是名相應。偈言：相應故。

行常不思議者，謂三種佛法身，無始世界來作眾生利益常不休息，不可思議。偈言：依止深快大故。

以是義故，略說偈言：

實體因果業，　及以相應行，
常不可思議，　名佛地應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

The treatise says: suchness accompanied by taint has already been explained; from here on, suchness free of taint should be explained. Suchness free of taint means that the buddhas, the tathāgatas, within the uncontaminated dharma-realm, are apart from every kind of taint, and, transforming the impure body, attain the pure, wondrous body. It should be understood that this nature of true suchness, the uncontaminated dharmakāya, is briefly distinguished by way of eight terms. What are the eight? A verse says:

Purity and its attainment, and freedom from taint; benefiting self and others, and its correspondence;
resting on the profound, the joyous, and the vast; and its constancy and its inconceivability, matching that same dharma.

This names, in order, the eight terms. This one verse reveals eight meanings — what are the eight? First, essence; second, cause; third, fruit; fourth, function; fifth, correspondence; sixth, manifestation; seventh, constancy; eighth, inconceivability.

As for essence: it was explained above that the tathāgatagarbha is not separate from being wrapped in the storehouse of affliction; it should be understood that, by becoming apart from every affliction, the transformed body attains purity — this is called essence. The verse says "purity." Hence the Noble Śrīmālā Sūtra says: "World-Honored One! If one has no doubt regarding the tathāgatagarbha wrapped in the storehouse of immeasurable affliction, one likewise has no doubt regarding the dharmakāya that has emerged from the storehouse of immeasurable affliction."

As for cause: there are two kinds of non-discriminating wisdom: first, the transcendent non-discriminating wisdom; second, the wisdom of activity resting on both the worldly and the transcendent, attained in dependence on that transcendent wisdom — this is called cause. The verse says "attainment."

As for fruit: it is precisely in dependence on this attainment that the fruit of realizing wisdom is attained — this is called fruit. The verse says "freedom from taint."

As for function: there are two kinds of freedom from taint: first, freedom from the afflictive obstruction; second, freedom from the cognitive obstruction — named, in this order, freedom from taint. Such freedom accomplishes both self-benefit and the benefit of others — this is called function. The verse says "benefiting self and others."

As for correspondence: through benefiting self and others, one attains immeasurable qualities, ever and ultimately sustained — this is called correspondence. The verse says "correspondence."

As for manifestation, constancy, and inconceivability: these refer to how the three kinds of buddha-dharmakāya, from beginningless time, work benefit for sentient beings ceaselessly, without ever resting, inconceivably. The verse says "resting on the profound, the joyous, and the vast."

For this reason, briefly stated, a verse says:

Essence, cause, fruit, and function, and its correspondence, manifestation,
constancy, and inconceivability — it should be understood that these are called the buddha-ground.

The Sun and Moon Behind the Cloud

Chinese Source

又依實體、依因於佛地中，及得彼方便因故，說三偈：

向說佛法身，　自性清淨體，
為諸煩惱垢，　客塵所染污。

譬如虛空中，　離垢淨日月，
為彼厚密雲，　羅網之所覆。

佛功德無垢，　常恒及不變，
不分別諸法，　得無漏真智。

此三行偈，以四行偈略釋應知。偈言：

佛身不捨離，　清淨真妙法，
如虛空日月，　智離染不二。

過恒沙佛法，　明淨諸功德，
非作法相應，　不離彼實體。

煩惱及智障，　彼法實無體，
常為客塵染，　是故說雲喻。

遠離彼二因，　向二無分別，
無分別真智，　及依彼所得。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, in dependence on the essence and the cause within the buddha-ground, and the attainment of the skillful cause for that, three verses are spoken:

It was explained above that the buddha's dharmakāya, the essence pure by its own nature,
is stained by the taint of every affliction, by adventitious dust.

It is like how, in the sky, the sun and moon, pure and free of stain,
are covered by the thick, dense net of cloud.

The Buddha's qualities are stainless, ever-constant, everlasting, and unchanging —
not discriminating among dharmas, having attained uncontaminated, true wisdom.

It should be understood that these three verses are briefly explained by four further verses. A verse says:

The buddha-body is never separate from the pure, true, wondrous Dharma —
like the sun and moon in the sky, wisdom, apart from taint, is non-dual.

The buddha-dharmas, surpassing the sands of the Ganges, the bright and pure qualities,
are not the correspondence of contrived dharmas, yet are never separate from that essence.

Affliction and the cognitive obstruction — these dharmas really have no essence,
yet ever stain it adventitiously — hence the simile of cloud is spoken.

Freedom from these two obstructions has two causes: the wisdom non-discriminating with regard to them,
the true, non-discriminating wisdom, together with what is attained in dependence on it.

Two Kinds of Purity

Chinese Source

此偈明何義？向說轉身實體清淨。又清淨者，略有二種。何等為二？一者自性清淨、二者離垢清淨。自性清淨者，謂性解脫無所捨離，以彼自性清淨心體不捨一切客塵煩惱，以彼本來不相應故。離垢清淨者，謂得解脫。又彼解脫不離一切法，如水不離諸塵垢等而言清淨，以自性清淨心遠離客塵諸煩惱垢更無餘故。

Ru-Yi Translation

What meaning does this verse make clear? It was explained above that the transformed body's essence is pure. Furthermore, purity is, briefly, of two kinds. What are the two? First, purity by nature; second, purity through freedom from taint. Purity by nature means liberation by nature, with nothing to be abandoned — because the essence of the mind pure by its own nature never actually held onto any adventitious affliction in the first place, these never truly corresponded to it. Purity through freedom from taint means the attainment of liberation. And that liberation is not separate from any dharma — just as water is called pure though not literally separate, prior to purification, from the dust and taint within it — because the mind pure by its own nature, once free of every adventitious affliction and taint, has nothing further remaining to remove.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This distinction resolves a question a careful reader might already be asking: if the mind was pure by nature all along, what does the path actually accomplish? The treatise's answer is that "purity by nature" and "purity through freedom from taint" are not two different purities but two descriptions of the identical purity from two different vantage points — one describing what was always true of the essence itself, the other describing what becomes true, practically, once the obstruction actually clears. The water simile is exact: water was never really made of mud, yet it is entirely correct to say muddy water "becomes clean" once the mud settles out — nothing false is asserted at either stage, only two different, equally accurate descriptions of the same water.

The Pond, the Moon, and the Honey

Chinese Source

又依彼果離垢清淨故，說四偈：

如清淨池水，　無有諸塵濁，
種種雜花樹，　周匝常圍遶。

如月離羅睺，　日無雲翳等，
無垢功德具，　顯現即彼體。

蜂王美味蜜，　堅實淨真金，
寶藏大果樹，　無垢真金像，
轉輪聖王身，　妙寶如來像，
如是等諸法，　即是如來身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, in dependence on this fruit, purity through freedom from taint, four verses are spoken:

It is like a pure pond of water, free of every speck of dust and turbidity,
forever surrounded on every side by trees of every kind of blossom.

It is like the moon free of Rāhu, the sun without cloud or covering —
every stainless quality is complete, and manifests as that very essence.

The queen bee's sweet honey, the firm and pure true gold, the treasure-store, the great fruit-bearing tree,
the stainless golden image, the universal monarch's body, the wondrous jeweled image of the Tathāgata —
such dharmas as these are precisely the Tathāgata's body.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Every one of the nine similes from Chapter Six's account of concealment now returns, unwrapped: the honey no longer amid threatening bees, the gold no longer buried in filth, the jeweled image no longer sealed in its clay mold. The treatise is making the point visually rather than argumentatively — nothing here is a new image; each is the identical picture from the earlier chapter with only the obstruction erased, demonstrating that buddhahood adds nothing to what was already latently present, and removes only what was never truly part of it.
