Thirty-Two Marks, and the Vajra Scepter

The thirty-two marks of a great being complete the sixty-four qualities of buddhahood, from the wheel-marks beneath the feet to eyes the color of deep blue lotus petals. The treatise then begins unpacking its four governing similes one by one, opening with the vajra scepter — the Tathāgata's ten powers pictured as a weapon able to shatter the armor, the mountain wall, and the tree that ignorance builds around a mind.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 28 complete Source: Taishō T1611 (tr. Ratnamati, 511 CE)

The Thirty-Two Marks of a Great Being

Chinese Source

又依三十二大人相故，說十一偈：

足下相平滿，　具足千輻輪，
跟傭趺上隆，　伊尼鹿王[跳-兆+專]，
手足悉柔軟，　諸指皆纖長，
鵝王網縵指，　臂肘上下傭，
兩肩前後平，　左右俱圓滿，
立能手過膝，　馬王陰藏相，
身傭相洪雅，　如尼拘樹王，
體相七處滿，　上半如師子，
威德勢堅固，　猶如那羅延，
身色新淨妙，　柔軟金色皮，
淨軟細平密，　一孔一毛生，
毛柔軟上靡，　微細輪右旋，
身淨光圓匝，　頂上相高顯，
項如孔雀王，　頤方若師子，
髮淨金精色，　喻如因陀羅，
額上白毫相，　通面淨光明，
口含四十齒，　二牙白踰雪，
深密內外明，　上下齒平齊，
迦陵頻伽聲，　妙音深遠聲，
所食至喉現，　得味中上味，
細薄廣長舌，　二目淳紺色，
瞬眼若牛王，　功德如蓮華。

如是說人尊，　妙相三十二，
一一不雜亂，　普身不可嫌。

此佛十力、四無所畏、十八不共法、三十二大人相略集一處，是名六十四種功德應知。偈言：

六十四功德，　修因及果報，
一一各差別，　寶經次第說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, in dependence on the thirty-two marks of a great being, eleven verses are spoken:

The soles of the feet are flat and full, complete with the thousand-spoked wheel;
the heels are broad, the insteps arched, the calves like the antelope-king's.
Hands and feet are entirely soft and supple, every finger slender and long,
the fingers webbed like a royal goose's; the arms and elbows, above and below, are full.
Both shoulders, front and back, are level, left and right equally rounded;
standing, the hands reach past the knees; the mark of a sheathed organ, like a stallion's.
The body's proportion is grand and graceful, like the king of banyan trees;
the body is full in seven places, the upper body like a lion's.
Majestic power, firm, like Nārāyaṇa; the body's color fresh, pure, and wondrous,
soft, golden-colored skin, pure, soft, fine, smooth, and dense — one hair grows from each pore.
The hairs are soft, curling upward, each fine whorl turning to the right;
the body's pure light surrounds it all around, the crown-mark rising high and visible.
The neck is like a peacock king's, the jaw square, like a lion's;
the hair is pure, the color of refined gold, likened to Indra's.
The mark of a white curl on the forehead, the whole face pure and radiant;
the mouth holds forty teeth, the two eyeteeth whiter than snow,
deep-rooted, closely set, bright within and without, the upper and lower teeth even.
A kalaviṅka's voice, a wondrous, deep, far-reaching voice;
whatever is eaten, upon reaching the throat, manifests as the supreme among flavors.
A fine, thin, broad, and long tongue; both eyes a pure, deep-blue color,
blinking like a bull-king, their beauty like a lotus flower.

Thus are told the honored one's thirty-two wondrous marks —
each one distinct, without any confusion, and, in every part, without a single flaw.

These — the Buddha's ten powers, four fearlessnesses, eighteen unshared dharmas, and thirty-two marks of a great being — briefly gathered together in one place, should be understood as the sixty-four kinds of qualities. A verse says:

The sixty-four qualities, their cultivated causes and their resulting fruits,
each one distinct in turn, are explained in the Ratnadārikā Sūtra.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The thirty-two marks belong to a much older layer of Buddhist tradition than the treatise's own philosophical vocabulary — the same list appears attached to the legendary body of a universal monarch in the Diamond Sūtra citation from Part 25, which is precisely why that citation insists the marks alone cannot be what identifies a buddha. Their inclusion here is not a contradiction of that earlier point but its completion: the marks are real and are genuinely among a buddha's sixty-four qualities, but they belong to the visible, reward-body register the treatise assigned in Part 27, never to the formless dharmakāya that the marks alone could never disclose.

Four Similes for Sixty-Four Qualities

Chinese Source

此偈明何義？向說諸佛如來六十四種功德因果差別，依此次第《寶女經》中廣說應知。又復依此四處，次第有四種喻，謂金剛杵及師子王、虛空譬喻、水中月等，有九行偈。依彼九偈，略說偈言：

衝過無慈心，　不共他無心，
故說杵師子，　空水中月喻。

Ru-Yi Translation

What meaning does this verse make clear? It was explained above that the buddhas, the tathāgatas, have sixty-four kinds of qualities, distinct in cause and fruit; it should be understood that this is explained at length, in this same order, in the Ratnadārikā Sūtra. Furthermore, in dependence on these four categories, there are, in due order, four similes — namely, the vajra scepter, the lion king, the simile of space, and the moon in water — given in nine verses. In dependence on those nine verses, it is briefly stated in a verse:

Breaking through, without a mind of ill will; unshared by others, without a limiting mind —
hence the similes of the scepter, the lion, space, and the moon in water are spoken.

The Ten Powers as a Vajra Scepter

Chinese Source

又依十力金剛杵喻故，說二偈：

處非處果性，　眾生諸信根，
種種道修地，　過宿命差別。

天眼漏盡等，　佛力金剛杵，
能刺碎散斫，　癡鎧山牆樹。

此偈示現何義？略說偈言：

諸如來六力，　次第三及一，
所知境界中，　離三昧諸障，
及離餘垢障，　譬如破散截，
鎧牆及樹等，　亦重亦堅固，
亦不可破壞。　如來十種力，
猶如彼金剛，　故說金剛杵。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, in dependence on the simile of the vajra scepter for the ten powers, two verses are spoken:

The fruit-nature of what is and is not the case; the faculties and dispositions of sentient beings;
the various paths and grounds of cultivation; the distinctions beyond ordinary knowledge of past lives.

The heavenly eye, the exhaustion of contaminants, and the rest — the Buddha's powers, like a vajra scepter,
are able to pierce, shatter, scatter, and cut down the armor, the mountain, the wall, and the tree of delusion.

What meaning do these verses reveal? Briefly stated, a verse says:

Of the Tathāgata's ten powers, six, then three, then one, in that order, act within the domain to be known,
apart from every obstruction of samādhi and apart from every remaining taint — just as one breaks, scatters,
and cuts through armor, walls, and trees, though these be heavy and firm and cannot ordinarily be broken —
the Tathāgata's ten powers are just like that vajra, and hence the simile of the vajra scepter is spoken.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The three obstacles named — armor, mountain wall, and tree — are not interchangeable images for one single obstruction but a graded scale: armor is worn and can be struck through directly, a wall must be breached, but a tree has grown into its obstruction from the inside, its roots and the ground no longer clearly separable. Ignorance, on this reading, is not always a simple barrier standing between a person and the truth; it sometimes has to be recognized as something that has grown, over time, into the very structure of how a mind has learned to see.

Part 28 Complete

Part 29 completes Chapter Nine with the remaining three similes — the four fearlessnesses as a lion unafraid amid any herd, the eighteen unshared dharmas as space itself, containing nothing that can be shared with earth, water, fire, or wind, and the thirty-two marks as the moon reflected in still water, seen by ordinary beings and awakened bodhisattvas in two entirely different ways.
