The Gist of Calming and Contemplation

Zhanran (711–782) wrote this short work at the request of the official Li Hua (d. c. 766), who wanted to know what Zhiyi's Mohe Zhiguan (Calming and Contemplation) was about; it is translated here in full as a single part. Zhanran first places the Tiantai school in its lineage (Nāgārjuna, Huiwen, Nanyue Huisi, Zhiyi) and names its two tools: the fivefold profound meaning and ten interpretive principles for reading sūtras, and the five groups of expedients with the ten modes of contemplation for practice. He then walks through Calming and Contemplation fascicle by fascicle: the five brief points of the first two fascicles (arousing the perfect mind, the four kinds of samādhi, the fruit, the eight teachings, the three virtues), the twenty-five preliminary methods of fascicle four, the ten modes of contemplation of fascicles five to seven, and the ten objects of fascicles eight to ten, noting that Zhiyi never lectured on the last three. Each of the ten modes is tied to an image from the parable of the great cart in the Lotus Sūtra, and each is presented as the remedy for a practitioner who has not yet "entered" through the one before. The first mode, the inconceivable object, receives the longest treatment and includes Zhanran's own verse-like lines on mind, Buddha, and sentient beings. The work closes with Zhanran's apology for the brevity of his summary.

Ru-Yi Translation

Because the supernumerary official Li Hua wished to know the gist of Calming and Contemplation, I report its main outline in brief.

The Teaching and Contemplation of the School

Chinese Source

略述教觀門戶大概。

Ru-Yi Translation

A brief account of the general shape of the gates of teaching and contemplation.

Chinese Source

今家教門以龍樹為始祖，慧文但列內觀視聽而已。洎乎南嶽天台，復因法華三昧發陀羅尼，開拓義門，觀法周備，消釋諸經皆以五重玄解、十義融通，觀法乃用五科方便、十乘軌行。言五重者，一切經前五義玄釋名通義異，以總冠別，謂釋名、出體、明宗、辨用、判教。自《法華》前，諸教未合五重皆麁，來至《法華》，名等俱妙，廣如玄文十卷委釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

The teaching-gate of our school takes Nāgārjuna as its first ancestor. Huiwen only set out inner contemplation as seen and heard, and nothing more. When it came to Nanyue and Tiantai, through the Lotus samādhi they further brought forth dhāraṇī, opened up the gates of meaning, and made the methods of contemplation complete. In explaining all the sūtras, they used the fivefold profound [meaning] to interpret them and the ten meanings to harmonize and penetrate them; for the methods of contemplation, they used the five groups of expedients and the ten-vehicle track of practice. As for the fivefold: at the head of every sūtra the five meanings give a profound explanation; the names are common and the meanings differ, and the general caps the particular. They are: explaining the name, setting forth the essence, clarifying the cardinal doctrine, distinguishing the function, and classifying the teachings. Before the Lotus, the teachings had not yet been unified, and in the fivefold [analysis] they are all coarse; coming to the Lotus, the name and the rest are all wondrous. This is explained in detail at length in the ten fascicles of the Profound Meaning.

Chinese Source

言十義者：一、先明道理寂絕亡離、不可思議；於一寂理不分而分，離開諸諦，謂四四諦、七二諦、五三諦等。若開若合，權實道理冷然可見。二、能詮教門，綱格槃峙，包括祕露，謂漸、頓、不定、祕密、藏、通、別、圓，得此八意，一代聲教化道可知。三、經論矛盾、言義相乖，不可以情通、不可以博解。古來執諍連代不休，今用四悉檀意，無滯不融，拔擲自在。四、者巧破執著，善用諸句破能著心，如所破惑單複具足，無言窮逐。五、結正法門，對當行位使依教修，有方便依行，證有階差、賢聖不濫，免增上慢。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the ten meanings: First, it first makes clear that the principle of the path is quiescent and cut off, vanished and departed, inconceivable; within the one quiescent principle it divides without dividing, separating out and opening up the truths: namely, the four [kinds of] fourfold truth, the seven [kinds of] twofold truth, the five [kinds of] threefold truth, and so on. Whether opened or combined, the principle of the provisional and the real can be seen with clarity. Second, the gate of teaching that expresses it: its framework stands firm, encompassing the secret and the revealed — namely, the gradual, the sudden, the indeterminate, the secret, the Tripiṭaka, the shared, the distinct, and the perfect. Gaining these eight intents, the voiced teaching and the way of transformation of a whole lifetime can be known. Third, where sūtras and treatises contradict one another and words and meanings conflict, they cannot be reconciled by feeling or resolved by broad learning; from ancient times people have held to their views and disputed generation after generation without ceasing. Now, using the intent of the four siddhāntas, there is no obstruction that is not dissolved, and one is free to draw out and cast aside. Fourth, skillfully breaking attachment: it makes good use of every form of statement to break the mind that attaches, matching the delusions to be broken, single, compound, and complete, pursuing them until no words remain. Fifth, it concludes and corrects the Dharma-gates, matching them to practice and stage so that one cultivates according to the teaching: there are expedients to rely on in practice, and realization has its grades, so that worthies and sages are not confused and overweening pride is avoided.

Chinese Source

六、隨以一句縱橫無礙，而綸緒次第宛然成章。七、開章科段鉤銷相承，決疏文勢生起冠帶。八、帖釋經文，須義順理當。九、翻譯方言，令名義不壅。十、一一句下理觀消通，觀與經合，印心成行，非數他寶。若釋《法華》，彌須曉了權實本迹，方可立行。此經獨得稱妙，方可依此以立觀意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, following any single phrase, it is unobstructed in every direction, and yet its threads and sequence clearly form a pattern. Seventh, in opening chapters and dividing sections, the linking and explaining follow on from one another; in settling the flow of the commentary's text, its arising and its capping and belting [are made clear]. Eighth, in explaining the sūtra text phrase by phrase, the meaning must accord and the principle must fit. Ninth, translating local speech, so that names and meanings are not blocked. Tenth, under each and every phrase, principle and contemplation are dissolved and penetrated, so that contemplation accords with the sūtra and, sealing the mind, becomes practice — not counting another's treasure. If one explains the Lotus, it is all the more necessary to understand clearly the provisional and the real, the origin and the trace; only then can practice be established. This sūtra alone gets to be called wondrous; only then can one rely on it to establish the intent of contemplation.

Chinese Source

言五方便及十乘軌行者，即圓、頓、止、觀，全依法華圓頓止觀，即法華三昧之異名耳。若欲修此圓頓三昧，具圓十乘方名圓行。〈方便品〉法文雖略，〈譬諭品〉大車諭足。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the five expedients and the ten-vehicle track of practice: these are the perfect, the sudden, calming, and contemplation, wholly relying on the perfect-and-sudden calming-and-contemplation of the Lotus, which is just another name for the Lotus samādhi. If one wishes to cultivate this perfect-and-sudden samādhi, only when one is complete with the perfect ten vehicles is it called perfect practice. The Dharma-text of the "Skillful Means" chapter is brief, but the simile of the great cart in the "Parable" chapter is complete.

The Brief Account: The First Two Fascicles

Chinese Source

然《止觀》十卷，大分為二：初之二卷略釋綱紀；後之八卷廣明行相。初略明中又開為五：謂發圓心、修圓行、感圓果、起八教、歸三德。初發圓心在第一卷，謂約四弘、四諦、六即，以簡偏圓發心之相。四弘是能發之誓，四諦是所依之境，六即是所歷之位。誓若無境名為狂願，境不辨位凡聖不分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Calming and Contemplation, in ten fascicles, is broadly divided into two: the first two fascicles explain its main framework in brief; the latter eight fascicles clarify the features of practice at length. The first, the brief clarification, is further opened into five: arousing the perfect mind, cultivating perfect practice, evoking the perfect fruit, giving rise to the eight teachings, and returning to the three virtues. The first, arousing the perfect mind, is in the first fascicle: by means of the four universal vows, the four truths, and the six identities, it sifts the features of arousing the mind in the partial and the perfect. The four universal vows are the vows that arouse; the four truths are the object relied upon; the six identities are the stages passed through. A vow without an object is called a mad wish; if the stages of the object are not distinguished, ordinary beings and sages are not told apart.

Chinese Source

言依境發誓者：謂眾生無邊誓願度，依苦諦境；煩惱無數誓願斷，依集諦境；法門無盡誓願知，依道諦境；佛道無上誓願成，依滅諦境。《涅槃經》中四諦開為四重，故使弘誓亦有四番。今簡偏從圓，以此圓四願融前三願無非法界，故依法界起於妙願。初心遍攝觀惑法界，遍習佛法三身等證。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for making vows in reliance on the object: "Sentient beings are boundless; I vow to deliver them" relies on the object of the truth of suffering. "Afflictions are countless; I vow to sever them" relies on the object of the truth of accumulation. "Dharma-gates are inexhaustible; I vow to know them" relies on the object of the truth of the path. "The buddha-path is unsurpassed; I vow to accomplish it" relies on the object of the truth of cessation. In the Nirvāṇa Sūtra the four truths are opened into four levels, and so the universal vows also come in four sets. Now, setting aside the partial and following the perfect, these perfect four vows fuse the previous three [sets of] vows so that none is not the dharma-realm; thus, relying on the dharma-realm, one gives rise to the wondrous vow. The initial mind universally gathers in the dharma-realm of contemplation and delusion, universally practices the Buddhadharma, and is equal in realization to the three bodies and the rest.

Chinese Source

已發圓心、未知圓心，為初心是、為後心是，為初即後、為初異後，若初非後是、若初心異後，俱非圓融，故辨六即而判是非，謂理即、名字即、觀行即、相似即、分真即、究竟即。即故初後俱是、六故初後不濫，理同故即、事異故六。凡諸經中有即名者，如生死即涅槃之流，皆以六位甄之，使始終理同而初後無濫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having aroused the perfect mind but not yet knowing the perfect mind: is it the initial mind that is right, or the later mind? Is the initial identical to the later, or is the initial different from the later? If the initial is not [right] and the later is, or if the initial mind differs from the later, neither is perfect fusion. Therefore the six identities are distinguished to judge right and wrong: namely, identity in principle, identity in name, identity in contemplative practice, identity in resemblance, identity in a portion of the truth, and identity in the ultimate. Because of "identity," initial and later are both right; because of "six," initial and later are not confused. Because the principle is the same, there is identity; because phenomena differ, there are six. Wherever the word "identity" occurs in the sūtras, as in "birth-and-death is identical to nirvāṇa" and the like, all are to be sifted by the six stages, so that from beginning to end the principle is the same while initial and later are not confused.

Chinese Source

次修圓行等四文，並在第二卷中。初圓行者，謂四種三昧遍攝眾行，若無勝行，勝果難階：一常坐，出《文殊問》、《文殊說》兩般若經，亦名一行三昧，唯專念法界故也，以九十日為一期。二常行，出《般舟三昧經》，亦名佛立三昧，成時見十方佛在室中立，亦以九十日為一期。三半行半坐，出《法華》、《方等》二經；《法華》三七日為一期，《方等》不限時節。四非行非坐，亦名隨自意，意起即觀故也。方法出《請觀音》等諸大乘經，通於四儀及諸作務公私忽遽，亦可修也。是四三昧行異理同，是故同用十乘之法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the four sections beginning with cultivating perfect practice are all in the second fascicle. First, perfect practice: the four kinds of samādhi universally gather in the many practices; without superior practice, a superior fruit is hard to reach. First, constant sitting, from the two prajñā sūtras, Mañjuśrī's Questions and Mañjuśrī's Teaching; it is also called the one-practice samādhi, because one is mindful solely of the dharma-realm; ninety days make one period. Second, constant walking, from the Pratyutpanna Samādhi Sūtra; it is also called the samādhi of the Buddha standing present, because when it is accomplished one sees the buddhas of the ten directions standing in the room; it too takes ninety days as one period. Third, half walking and half sitting, from the two sūtras, the Lotus and the Vaipulya: for the Lotus, three weeks make one period; the Vaipulya is not limited in time. Fourth, neither walking nor sitting, also called following one's own intent, because as soon as an intent arises one contemplates it. Its methods come from the Invoking Avalokiteśvara and other Mahāyāna sūtras; it extends to the four postures and to all kinds of work, public and private, even in haste — these too can be cultivated. These four samādhis differ in practice but are the same in principle; therefore all alike use the method of the ten vehicles.

Chinese Source

二感圓果者，由諸行故得入圓位，近在初住名無生忍，遠期妙覺名寂滅忍。初住功能具如《華嚴》歎初住文，即其相也，豈可造次自謂證真？乃至妙覺，廣如經說。三起八教者，既入位已八相成道，現十界身，能隨順物機，用三藏等四及漸等四，五時利物，一代始終。四歸三德者，機緣息已宜歸三德。三德者何？謂祕密藏。故《涅槃》云「安置諸子祕密藏中，我亦不久自住其中。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, evoking the perfect fruit: through these practices one is able to enter the perfect stages. Near at hand, at the first abiding, it is called the acceptance of non-arising; looking far ahead to wondrous awakening, it is called the acceptance of quiescent extinction. The capacities of the first abiding are fully as in the passage of the Avataṃsaka praising the first abiding; that is its feature. How could one rashly claim for oneself to have realized the true? And so on up to wondrous awakening, as the sūtras explain at length. Third, giving rise to the eight teachings: having entered the stages, one accomplishes the path through the eight aspects and manifests bodies in the ten realms, able to accord with the capacities of beings, using the four — the Tripiṭaka and the rest — and the four — the gradual and the rest — to benefit beings in the Five Periods, from the beginning to the end of a lifetime. Fourth, returning to the three virtues: once capacities and conditions have ceased, it is fitting to return to the three virtues. What are the three virtues? They are the hidden treasury. Hence the Nirvāṇa says: "I have placed all my children within the hidden treasury, and before long I too shall dwell within it myself."

The Extended Account: Fascicles Three and Four

Chinese Source

次第三卷去，廣釋行相，開演前五，令易行故，謂釋止觀名、辨止觀體，明體攝法、判法偏圓，此四並在第三卷中。次為正修作前方便，並在第四卷中。謂二十五法，總為五科，初具五緣：一衣食具足，離希望緣故。二持戒清淨，離惡道因故。三閑居靜處，離憒鬧事故。四息諸緣務，棄猥雜業故。五須善知識，有諮疑地故。文中各有事、理二具。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, from the third fascicle on, the features of practice are explained at length, unfolding the previous five so that they are easy to practice: namely, explaining the name of calming-and-contemplation, distinguishing the essence of calming-and-contemplation, clarifying how the essence gathers in the dharmas, and judging the dharmas as partial or perfect; these four are all in the third fascicle. Next come the preliminary expedients for correct cultivation, all in the fourth fascicle: namely, the twenty-five methods, in five groups altogether. First, fulfilling the five conditions: first, being sufficient in clothing and food, in order to be free of the condition of longing; second, keeping the precepts pure, in order to be free of the causes of the evil destinies; third, dwelling at leisure in a quiet place, in order to be free of noisy affairs; fourth, resting from all involvements and tasks, in order to set aside miscellaneous work; fifth, one needs a good friend, in order to have a place to ask about doubts. In the text each has two kinds of fulfillment, phenomenal and in principle.

Chinese Source

二訶五欲，謂色、聲、香、味、觸，正報、依報各具此五，並能生於行者須欲心故，故須訶滅。文中自有事、理二訶。三棄五蓋者，緣具無欲方堪入觀，觀未相應五法覆心，謂貪欲、瞋恚、睡眠、掉悔、狐疑。由觀所起倍異於常，損於寂照，覺已須棄。文中各有事、理二棄。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, rebuking the five desires, namely form, sound, smell, taste, and touch. The principal and the dependent [rewards] each have these five, and both can give rise in the practitioner to a mind that needs desire; therefore they must be rebuked and extinguished. The text itself has two rebukes, phenomenal and in principle. Third, abandoning the five hindrances: when the conditions are complete and there is no desire, one is fit to enter contemplation; but while contemplation has not yet corresponded, five dharmas cover the mind, namely greed-and-desire, anger, sleep, restlessness-and-regret, and doubt. Arising from contemplation, they are twice as strong as usual and harm stillness and illumination; once one is aware of them, they must be abandoned. In the text each has two abandonings, phenomenal and in principle.

Chinese Source

四調五事者，蓋去不入，當是身等五法不調，謂身、息、心三定，內合調令身不寬急、心無澁滑、心無浮沈。眠、食二法定外各調，眠應不節不恣，食使不饑不飽。五行五法者，四科雖具，必須此五方成行首：一樂欲，須希慕故。二專念，須憶持故。三精進，須相續故。四巧慧，須迴轉故。五一心，無他求故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, regulating the five things: if the hindrances are gone and still one does not enter, it must be that the five dharmas, the body and the rest, are not regulated. Namely, body, breath, and mind, the three [regulated] in concentration, are harmonized together inwardly, so that the body is neither slack nor tense, the breath is neither rough nor slippery, and the mind neither floats nor sinks. Sleep and food, the two dharmas, are each regulated outside concentration: sleep should be neither restricted nor indulged; food should leave one neither hungry nor full. Fifth, practicing the five methods: though the four groups are complete, these five are needed before one becomes the head of practice: first, aspiration, because longing is needed; second, single-minded mindfulness, because recollection is needed; third, effort, because continuity is needed; fourth, skillful wisdom, because turning [the mind] is needed; fifth, one-mindedness, because there is no seeking elsewhere.

The Ten Modes: The Inconceivable Object

Chinese Source

具此方便，正觀可獲。正觀者何？所謂十法，若無此十，名壞驢車。又此十法雖俱圓常圓，人復有三根不等，上根唯一法、中根二或七、下根方具十。

Ru-Yi Translation

Complete with these expedients, correct contemplation can be obtained. What is correct contemplation? It is the ten methods; without these ten, it is called a broken donkey cart. Moreover, although these ten methods are all perfect and constantly perfect, people again have three kinds of faculties, which are unequal: those of superior faculties [need] only one method; those of middling faculties two or seven; only those of inferior faculties [need] all ten.

Chinese Source

上根一法者，謂觀不思議境，境為所觀、觀為能觀。所觀者何？謂陰、界、入，不出色、心。色從心造，全體是心，故經云「三界無別法，唯是一心作。」此之能造具足諸法，若漏、無漏、非漏、非無漏等；若因、若果、非因、非果等。故經云「心佛及眾生，是三無差別。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The one method for those of superior faculties is contemplating the inconceivable object. The object is what is contemplated; the contemplation is what contemplates. What is contemplated? The aggregates, the elements, and the entries, which do not go beyond form and mind. Form is made by mind; its whole essence is mind. Hence the sūtra says: "The three realms have no separate dharma of their own — they are made by the one mind alone." This that makes is complete with all dharmas: whether contaminated, uncontaminated, neither contaminated nor uncontaminated, and so on; whether cause, fruit, neither cause nor fruit, and so on. Hence the sūtra says: "Mind, Buddha, and sentient beings — these three are without any difference."

Chinese Source

眾生理具、諸佛已成，成之與理莫不性等，謂一一心中一切心、一一塵中一切塵、一一心中一切塵、一一塵中一切心、一一塵中一切剎、一切剎塵亦復然。諸法、諸塵、諸剎身，其體宛然無自性，無性本來隨物變，所以相入事恒分。故我身心剎塵遍，諸佛、眾生亦復然。一一身土體恒同，何妨心、佛、眾生異，異故分於染淨緣，緣體本空空不空，三諦三觀三非三，三一一三無所寄，諦觀名別體復同。是故能所二非二（三觀名義在《纓絡》等經，三諦名義在《王仁》等經）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sentient beings possess it in principle; the buddhas have already accomplished it. Accomplishment and principle are, without exception, equal in nature: namely, in each and every mind all minds, in each and every mote all motes, in each and every mind all motes, in each and every mote all minds, in each and every mote all lands — and all lands and motes are likewise so. All dharmas, all motes, the bodies of all lands — their essence is plainly without self-nature; being without nature, they from the beginning change according to things; therefore they enter one another, while as phenomena they remain always distinct. Thus my body and mind pervade lands and motes, and all buddhas and sentient beings are likewise so. The essence of each body and land is always the same — what prevents mind, Buddha, and sentient beings from differing? Because they differ, they are divided into conditions of defilement and purity; the essence of conditions is originally empty, and the empty is not empty. The three truths and three contemplations are three and not three; three is one and one is three, with nothing to rest upon; truths and contemplations differ in name, yet their essence is again the same. Therefore what contemplates and what is contemplated are two and not two. (The names and meanings of the three contemplations are in the Yingluo and other sūtras; the names and meanings of the three truths are in the Humane Kings and other sūtras.)

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

If one says that knowing the principle does not hinder evil, one should also [say that] knowing about wealth escapes poverty and knowing about medicine escapes illness. If phenomenal evil is removed, the wholesome in principle is easily made clear; one should still ask for the sages' aid to help me reveal the principle. For those of middling faculties, the use of contemplation reaches its utmost here.

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, knowing the stages: those of inferior faculties have heavy obstructions; not only are they unclear about the correct and the auxiliary, they even give rise to overweening pride, calling themselves equal to the Buddha, claiming to have attained what they have not attained and to have realized what they have not realized. This is the māra of the divine sons among the four māras; down to the duiti, the yakṣas, the shimei, and other ghosts in the human world, who are subordinate to the heavenly Māra, patrol for him, and prevent the practitioner from leaving his realm. Hence the Larger [Sūtra] says: "A bodhisattva who does not speak of Māra is called a bodhisattva caṇḍāla."

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Li Hua 李華. A Tang official and writer (員外, a "supernumerary" post in a ministry), a lay student of Zhanran's circle. In unit 2 CBETA prints 《止觀大意》 as a book title; the translation reads it as "the gist of Calming and Contemplation," since Li Hua is asking about the larger work.

Section names matched to the site's Mohe Zhiguan. The ten modes of contemplation follow Mohe Zhiguan (contemplating the inconceivable object, giving rise to the mind of compassion, … free of attachment to the Dharma), adjusted only where Zhanran's labels differ (安心 "settling the mind" for 巧安止觀; 道品調適 "adjusting the branches of the path"; 助道對治 "the auxiliary path and remedies"; 安忍 "bearing with adversity"; 離法愛 "freedom from attachment to the Dharma"). The twenty-five methods and their five groups (fulfilling the five conditions, rebuking the five desires, abandoning the five hindrances, regulating the five things, practicing the five methods), the four kinds of samādhi, and the ten objects (afflictions, illness, the marks of karma, demonic affairs, meditative states, views, overweening pride, the two vehicles, the bodhisattvas) use the site's Mohe Zhiguan English.

The five brief points. Mohe Zhiguan names them 發大心, 修大行, 感大果, 裂大網, 歸大處 ("giving rise to the great mind … returning to the great abode" on the site). Zhanran restates them as 發圓心, 修圓行, 感圓果, 起八教, 歸三德; the translation follows his wording ("arousing the perfect mind … returning to the three virtues") rather than the site's labels for Zhiyi's.

The fivefold profound meaning (unit 4). Zhanran writes 釋名、出體、明宗、辨用、判教; the glossary's names (from the Profound Meaning) use 辨體／論用. To keep different characters distinct, 出體 is "setting forth the essence" and 辨用 "distinguishing the function."

Lotus quotations. The images for the ten modes come from the "Parable" chapter (site: "The Parable of the Burning House" and "The Burning House in Verse"): "high and broad," "spread above with a canopy," "fitted with a crimson pillow," "swift as the wind," "great white oxen," "attended besides by many servants for its escort," and "roamed" are taken from the site's English. 其事高廣 in unit 5 is probably an error for the sūtra's 其車高廣 ("its cart is high and broad"); "the site of awakening" is the verse line 直至道場. CBETA closes the quotation after 更修下法, which cannot be part of the sūtra; the translation closes it after "the site of awakening."

"Roamed in the four directions." The site's Lotus has "roamed in every direction" for 遊於四方; here "four directions" is kept because Zhanran reads the four as the four stages (abidings, practices, dedications, grounds).

Other quotations. "The three realms have no separate dharma…" uses the site's Profound Meaning wording (there attributed to the Great Treatise); "Mind, Buddha, and sentient beings…" (Avataṃsaka) as in the site's Mohe Zhiguan; "To be able to contemplate the nature of mind…" (Nirvāṇa) as in the site's Profound Meaning; "I have placed all my children within the hidden treasury…" exactly as in the site's Mohe Zhiguan ("returning to the great abode"). "The afflictions of sentient beings are not of one kind…": source not located in the Taishō files consulted. "A bodhisattva who does not speak of Māra is called a bodhisattva caṇḍāla": not located verbatim; the Larger Prajñāpāramitā (T223) has the phrase 是名菩薩旃陀羅 in a related passage. "Putting on the saṃnāha…" (發僧那於始心，終大悲以赴難) is also quoted in Zhanran's Zhiguan fuxing (T1912); its earlier source was not located.

隨緣不變／不變隨緣. Rendered in two directions ("follows conditions yet does not change" / "does not change yet follows conditions"), as required for Zhanran's Diamond Scalpel.

The verse-like lines (眾生理具… 能所二非二). Printed as prose by CBETA but written in seven-character lines; translated as prose following the punctuation. (王仁) in the interlinear note is a transposition of 仁王, the Humane Kings Sūtra; 纓絡 is the Yingluo (Bodhisattva Necklace) Sūtra.

Apparent copying errors. 心無澁滑 in the regulation of the five things is translated as "the breath" (息), since body, breath, and mind are the three named and "mind" is treated in the next clause; 尚夫人天 is read as 尚失人天 ("still lose humans and gods"); 由慈習生 is read as 由茲習生 ("through this habituation"). Alternatively, 慈 could be kept: "through the habituation of kindness."

Ambiguous lines. 慧文但列內觀視聽而已: Alternatively, "Huiwen only set out inner contemplation; [what he taught was] what he saw and heard." 即圓、頓、止、觀 follows CBETA's four-way split; Alternatively, "this is the perfect-and-sudden calming-and-contemplation." 初心遍攝觀惑法界，遍習佛法三身等證: Alternatively, "the initial mind universally embraces contemplation, delusion, and the dharma-realm, and universally practices the Buddhadharma until the three bodies and the rest are realized."

One hundred forty views. 百四十見 is translated as written; the usual counts in Mohe Zhiguan are sixty-two views and the like, and this figure may be a copying error.
