The Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings.

Tradition places this short sūtra just before the Lotus Sūtra: the Lotus itself opens with the Buddha entering the samādhi of the abode of immeasurable meanings after teaching a sūtra of that name. In three chapters it praises the Buddha, teaches that immeasurable meanings arise from a single dharma — the markless — and lists ten inconceivable merits of the sūtra. Its line that for more than forty years the Buddha had not yet revealed the real became a cornerstone of the Tiantai classification of the teachings.

How to read this collection

Part 1 holds Liu Qiu's preface (a later author) and the chapter on virtuous conduct; Part 2 the chapter on teaching the Dharma; Part 3 the ten merits.

Read it as the prelude to the Lotus Sūtra and the Sūtra on Contemplating the Practice of Samantabhadra.

The Text's Structure

Three chapters, one part each.

Part 1
Liu Qiu's Preface and Virtuous Conduct
The Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings (T0276) was translated by Dharmāgatayaśas in the Southern Qi; Tiantai reads it as the "opening sūtra" of the Lotus trilogy, because the Lotus itself says that the Buddha taught a Great Vehicle sūtra called Immeasurable Meanings just before he entered samādhi.
