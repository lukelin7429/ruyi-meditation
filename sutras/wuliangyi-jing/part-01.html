Liu Qiu's Preface and Virtuous Conduct

The Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings (T0276) was translated by Dharmāgatayaśas in the Southern Qi; Tiantai reads it as the "opening sūtra" of the Lotus trilogy, because the Lotus itself says that the Buddha taught a Great Vehicle sūtra called Immeasurable Meanings just before he entered samādhi. This Part has two pieces by two different hands. The first is a preface by Liu Qiu (438–495), a lay recluse of Jingzhou, written in 485; it is not part of the sūtra. Liu Qiu arranges the Buddha's teaching in seven stages, with this sūtra as the fifth, tells how the monk Huibiao obtained the text from Dharmāgatayaśas in Guangzhou, and then weighs the debate of his time on whether awakening comes gradually or suddenly, siding with the sudden. The second piece is the sūtra's first chapter, "Virtuous Conduct." It sets the assembly on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, lists the bodhisattvas and monks, describes the qualities of the eighty thousand bodhisattvas, and closes with the long verses of Great Adornment Bodhisattva praising the Buddha's body — "neither existent nor nonexistent" yet with the thirty-two marks — his wheel of the Dharma, and his practices in past kalpas.

Liu Qiu's Preface: The Name and the Seven Stages of Teaching

Chinese Source

無量義經序

Ru-Yi Translation

Preface to the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings

Chinese Source

蕭齊荊州隱士劉虬作

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Liu Qiu, a recluse of Jingzhou under the Xiao Qi

Chinese Source

無量義經者，取其無相一灋，廣生眾教，含義不貲，故曰無量。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings: it takes the single markless dharma and from it broadly gives rise to the many teachings; the meanings it contains cannot be reckoned, and so it is called "immeasurable."

Chinese Source

夫三界群生，隨業而轉；一極正覺，任機而通。流轉起滅者，必在苦而希樂，此叩聖之感也；順通示現者，亦施悲而用慈，即救世之應也。根異教殊，其階成七。先為波利等說五戒，所謂人天善根一也；次為拘隣等轉四諦，所謂授聲聞乘二也；次為中根演十二因緣，所謂授緣覺乘三也；次為上根舉六波羅蜜，所謂授以大乘四也。眾教宣融，群疑須導，次說無量義經。既稱得道差品，復云未顯真實，使發求實之冥機，用開一極之由緒五也。故法華接唱顯一除三，順彼求實之心，去此施權之名六也。雖權開而實現，猶掩常住之正義；在雙樹而臨崖，乃暢我淨之玄音七也。過此以往法門雖多，撮其大歸數盡於此；亦由眾聲不出五音之表，百氏竝在六家之內。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now the multitudes of beings in the three realms turn according to their actions; the singular ultimate, right awakening, penetrates according to their capacities. Those who flow on and turn, arising and ceasing, being in suffering, necessarily hope for happiness: this is the stimulus that knocks at the sage. The one who accords, penetrates, and manifests bestows compassion and uses kindness: this is the response that saves the world. As the roots differ, the teachings vary, and their stages make seven. First, for Bhallika and the others, he taught the five precepts — what is called the good roots of humans and gods: this is the first. Next, for Kauṇḍinya and the others, he turned the four truths — what is called conferring the śrāvaka vehicle: the second. Next, for those of middling faculties, he expounded the twelve links of dependent origination — what is called conferring the pratyekabuddha vehicle: the third. Next, for those of superior faculties, he raised up the six pāramitās — what is called conferring the Great Vehicle: the fourth. When the many teachings had been proclaimed and blended, the crowd of doubts needed guiding, so next he taught the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings. Having stated that the attainment of the path differs in grade, it says further that "the true and real has not yet been revealed," so as to stir the hidden capacity that seeks the real and to open the thread that leads to the singular ultimate: the fifth. Then the Lotus takes up the song, revealing the one and removing the three, according with their mind that seeks the real and removing the name of the provisional that had been bestowed: the sixth. Although the provisional was opened and the real appeared, it still concealed the correct meaning of eternal abiding; at the twin trees, on the brink [of passing], he then fully set forth the profound sound of self and purity: the seventh. Beyond this, though the Dharma gates are many, if one gathers up their great return, the count ends here — just as the many sounds do not go outside the five notes, and the hundred schools all lie within the six families.

Liu Qiu's Preface: How the Sūtra Reached China

Chinese Source

其無量義經，雖法華首戴其目，而中夏未覩其說，每臨講肆，未嘗不廢談而歎想見斯文。忽有武當山比丘慧表，生自羌胄，偽帝姚略從子，國破之日，為晉軍何澹之所得。數歲聰黠，澹之字曰螟蛉，養為假子。俄放出家，便勤苦求道，南北遊尋，不擇夷險。以齊建元三年，復訪奇搜祕遠至嶺南，於廣州朝廷寺，遇中天竺沙門曇摩伽陀耶舍，手能隸書，口解齊言，欲傳此經未知所授。表便殷勤致請，心形俱至，淹歷旬朔僅得一本，仍還嶠北齎入武當。以今永明三年九月十八日，頂戴出山見校弘通，奉覿真文欣敬兼誠，詠歌不足手舞莫宣。輒虔訪宿解抽刷庸思，謹立序注云。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for this Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings: although the Lotus bears its title at its head, the Central Xia had never seen its text. Each time I came to the lecture hall, I never failed to break off the discussion and sigh, longing to see this text. Then suddenly there was a bhikṣu of Mount Wudang, Huibiao, born of Qiang descent, the nephew of the usurping emperor Yao Lüe. On the day his state was destroyed, he was taken by He Danzhi of the Jin army. At a few years of age he was bright and clever; Danzhi gave him the name "Mingling" and raised him as an adopted son. Before long he let him leave home, and he sought the path with diligence and hardship, traveling and searching north and south, not choosing between the easy and the perilous. In the third year of the Jianyuan era of the Qi, seeking out the rare and searching for the secret, he went far to Lingnan, and at the Chaoting Monastery in Guangzhou he met the śramaṇa of Central India Dharmāgatayaśas, whose hand could write clerical script and whose mouth understood the speech of Qi. [Dharmāgatayaśas] wished to transmit this sūtra but did not yet know to whom to give it. Biao then earnestly made his request, present in heart and in person; after lingering for some ten days and months, he obtained just a single copy, and then returned north of the passes and brought it into Wudang. In this present third year of Yongming, on the eighteenth day of the ninth month, bearing it on his head he came out of the mountains and showed it [to me] to be collated and widely circulated. Respectfully beholding the true text, my joy and reverence were alike sincere; singing and chanting were not enough, and dancing hands could not express it. I then reverently sought earlier interpretations, drew out and brushed up my ordinary thoughts, and respectfully set up this preface and notes, as follows.

Liu Qiu's Preface: The Gradual and the Sudden

Chinese Source

自極教應世與俗而差，神道救物稱感成異。玄圃已東，號曰太一；罽賓以西，字為正學。東國明殃，慶於百年；西域辯休，咎於三世。希無之與修空，其揆一也。有欲於無者，既無得無之分；施心於空者，豈有入空之照。而講求釋教者，或謂會理可漸，或謂入空必頓，請試言之，以筌幽寄。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the ultimate teaching responds to the world, it differs with the customs; since the spirit-way saves beings, it becomes different according to the stimulus. East of the Xuanpu it is called the Great One; west of Jibin it is named the Correct Learning. The Eastern lands make clear calamity and blessing within a hundred years; the Western Regions discern fortune and blame across the three times. Aspiring to nonbeing and cultivating emptiness — their measure is one. One who has desire toward nonbeing has no share in attaining nonbeing; one who applies the mind to emptiness — how could he have the illumination of entering emptiness? Yet among those who lecture on and seek the Buddha's teaching, some say that union with principle can be gradual, and some say that entering emptiness must be sudden. Let me try to speak of it, using a fish-trap for the hidden import.

Chinese Source

立漸者，以萬事之成，莫不有漸。堅氷基於履霜，九仞成於累土。學人之入空也，雖未圓符；譬如斬木，去寸無寸，去尺無尺，三空稍登，寧非漸耶？立頓者，以希善之功，莫過觀法性。法性從緣，非有非無。忘慮於非有非無，理照斯一者，乃曰解空；存心於非有非無，境智猶二者，未免於有。有中伏結，非無日損之驗；空上論心，未有入理之効。而言納羅漢於一聽，判無生於終朝，是接誘之言，非稱實之說。妙得非漸，理固必然。既二談分路，兩意爭途；一去一取，莫之或正。

Ru-Yi Translation

Those who establish the gradual hold that among the accomplishments of the myriad affairs, none is without gradualness: hard ice is founded on treading frost; nine fathoms are made by piling earth. When a student enters emptiness, though not yet in perfect accord, it is like cutting wood: remove an inch and there is no inch; remove a foot and there is no foot. Ascending the three emptinesses by degrees — how is that not gradual? Those who establish the sudden hold that among the efforts toward goodness none surpasses contemplating dharma-nature. Dharma-nature comes from conditions and is neither existent nor nonexistent. Forgetting deliberation in what is neither existent nor nonexistent, so that principle and illumination are one — this is called understanding emptiness. Keeping the mind on what is neither existent nor nonexistent, so that object and wisdom are still two — this does not escape existence. Subduing the fetters within existence is not without the proof of daily diminishing; but discussing the mind upon emptiness, there has never been the effect of entering principle. And to speak of admitting arhats at a single hearing, or of deciding non-arising in a single morning — these are words of receiving and enticing, not statements that accord with the real. Wondrous attainment is not gradual: in principle this is certainly so. So the two discussions part ways and the two views contend on the road; one rejects, one takes, and no one has set it right.

Chinese Source

尋得旨之匠，起自支安。支公之論無生，以七住為道慧陰足，十住則群方與能。在迹斯異，語照則一。安公之辯異觀，三乘者，始簣之日稱；定慧者，終成之實錄。此謂始求可隨根而三，入解則其慧不二。譬喻亦云，大難既夷，乃無有三；險路既息，其化即亡。此則名一為三，非有三悟明矣。生公云，道品可以泥洹，非羅漢之名；六度可以至佛，非樹王之謂。斬木之喻，木存故尺寸可漸；無生之證，生盡故其照必頓。案三乘名教，皆以生盡照息，去有入空，以此為道，不得取像於形器也。今無量義亦以無相為本，若所證實異，豈曰無相；若入照必同，寧曰有漸。非漸而云漸，密筌之虛教耳。如來亦云：「空拳誑小兒，以此度眾生。」微文接麁，漸說或允；忘象得意，頓義為長。聊舉大較，談者擇焉。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seeking out the masters who grasped the purport, [the line] begins with Zhi and An. Master Zhi, discussing no-birth, took the seventh abiding as where the wisdom of the path is hiddenly sufficient, and the tenth abiding as where all quarters are given ability. In the traces these differ; speaking of illumination, they are one. Master An, distinguishing the different contemplations, [held that] "three vehicles" is the name for the day of the first basket [of earth], while "concentration and wisdom" is the true record of final completion. This means that in first seeking one may follow the faculties and be three, but in entering understanding the wisdom is not two. The similes, too, say: once the great danger has been leveled, there are no longer three; once the perilous road is at an end, the transformation vanishes. This makes it clear that the one is named as three, not that there are three awakenings. Master Sheng said: the factors of the path can [reach] nirvāṇa, but that is not the name of arhat; the six perfections can reach buddhahood, but that is not what is meant by the king of trees. In the simile of cutting wood, because the wood remains, foot and inch can be gradual; in the realization of no-birth, because birth is exhausted, its illumination must be sudden. Considering the names and teachings of the three vehicles: all take the exhaustion of birth and the cessation of illumination, leaving existence and entering emptiness, as the path; one cannot take an image from shaped vessels. Now the Immeasurable Meanings too takes the markless as its root. If what is realized were really different, how could it be called markless? If entering illumination is necessarily the same, how could it be called gradual? To call what is not gradual "gradual" is an empty teaching, a hidden fish-trap. The Tathāgata too said: "With an empty fist I deceive small children; with this I carry beings across." Where a subtle text reaches down to the coarse, the gradual explanation may be fitting; forgetting the image and grasping the meaning, the sudden meaning is superior. I have raised the general outline; let those who discuss it choose.

The Sūtra Opens: The Assembly on Gṛdhrakūṭa

無量義經

The Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings

德行品第一

Ru-Yi Translation

Chapter One: Virtuous Conduct

Chinese Source

蕭齊天竺三藏曇摩伽陀耶舍譯

Ru-Yi Translation

Translated by Dharmāgatayaśas, Tripiṭaka master from India, under the Xiao Qi

Chinese Source

如是我聞：

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus have I heard:

Chinese Source

一時，佛在王舍城耆闍崛山中，與大比丘眾萬二千人俱。菩薩摩訶薩八萬人，天、龍、夜叉、乾闥婆、阿修羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽，諸比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷俱。大轉輪王、小轉輪王，金輪、銀輪、諸轉輪王，國王、王子、國臣、國民、國士、國女、國大長者，各與眷屬百千萬數而自圍遶，來詣佛所，頭面禮足，遶百千匝，燒香、散華，種種供養；供養佛已，退一面坐。

Ru-Yi Translation

At one time the Buddha was on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa near Rājagṛha, together with a great assembly of twelve thousand monks, eighty thousand bodhisattva-mahāsattvas, gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, and monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen. Great wheel-turning kings, lesser wheel-turning kings, the golden-wheel, silver-wheel, and other wheel-turning kings, kings, princes, ministers of state, people of the realm, gentlemen of the realm, women of the realm, and the great elders of the realm, each surrounded by a retinue numbering hundreds of thousands of myriads, came to where the Buddha was, bowed their heads at his feet, circled him hundreds of thousands of times, burned incense, scattered flowers, and made every kind of offering; having made offerings to the Buddha, they withdrew and sat to one side.

The Eighty Thousand Bodhisattvas

Chinese Source

其菩薩名曰文殊師利法王子、大威德藏法王子、無憂藏法王子、大辯藏法王子、彌勒菩薩、導首菩薩、藥王菩薩、藥上菩薩、花幢菩薩、花光幢菩薩、陀羅尼自在王菩薩、觀世音菩薩、大勢至菩薩、常精進菩薩、寶印手菩薩、寶積菩薩、寶杖菩薩、越三界菩薩、毘摩跋羅菩薩、香象菩薩、大香象菩薩、師子吼王菩薩、師子遊戲世菩薩、師子奮迅菩薩、師子精進菩薩、勇銳力菩薩、師子威猛伏菩薩、莊嚴菩薩、大莊嚴菩薩，如是等菩薩摩訶薩八萬人俱。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bodhisattvas were named: Mañjuśrī, Prince of the Dharma; Great-Majestic-Virtue-Treasury, Prince of the Dharma; Sorrowless-Treasury, Prince of the Dharma; Great-Eloquence-Treasury, Prince of the Dharma; Maitreya Bodhisattva; Leading-Head Bodhisattva; Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva; Bhaiṣajyasamudgata Bodhisattva; Flower-Banner Bodhisattva; Flower-Light-Banner Bodhisattva; Dhāraṇī-Sovereign-King Bodhisattva; Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva; Mahāsthāmaprāpta Bodhisattva; Constant-Diligence Bodhisattva; Jewel-Seal-Hand Bodhisattva; Ratnākara Bodhisattva; Jeweled-Staff Bodhisattva; Beyond-the-Three-Realms Bodhisattva; Pimobaluo Bodhisattva; Gandhahastin Bodhisattva; Great-Gandhahastin Bodhisattva; Lion's-Roar-King Bodhisattva; Lion-at-Play-in-the-World Bodhisattva; Lion-Swift-Advance Bodhisattva; Lion-Diligence Bodhisattva; Brave-Keen-Strength Bodhisattva; Lion-Fierce-Subduer Bodhisattva; Adornment Bodhisattva; and Great-Adornment Bodhisattva — bodhisattva-mahāsattvas such as these, eighty thousand in all.

Chinese Source

是諸菩薩，莫不皆是法身大士，戒、定、慧、解脫、解脫知見之所成就。其心禪寂，常在三昧，恬安惔怕，無為無欲，顛倒、亂想不復得入，靜寂清澄，志玄虛漠，守之不動億百千劫，無量法門悉現在前；得大智慧，通達諸法，曉了分別性相真實、有無長短，明現顯白；又能善知諸根性欲，以陀羅尼無礙辯才，請佛轉法輪，隨順能轉，微渧先墮，以淹欲塵，開涅槃門，扇解脫風，除世熱惱，致法清涼；次降甚深十二因緣，用灑無明老病死等，猛盛熾然苦聚日光。爾乃洪注無上大乘，潤漬眾生諸有善根，布善種子遍功德田，普令一切發菩提萌，智慧日月，方便時節，扶踈增長大乘事業，令眾疾成阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，常住快樂微妙真實。

Ru-Yi Translation

These bodhisattvas were without exception great beings of the Dharma-body, accomplished in precepts, concentration, wisdom, liberation, and the knowledge and vision of liberation. Their minds were in meditative stillness, constantly in samādhi, calm, peaceful, and tranquil, without conditioned action and without desire; inversions and confused thoughts could no longer enter. Still, tranquil, clear, and pure, their resolve profound, empty, and vast, they held to it unmoving for hundreds of thousands of koṭis of kalpas, and immeasurable Dharma gates all appeared before them. They had attained great wisdom and penetrated all dharmas; they understood and distinguished nature and marks, the true and real, existence and nonexistence, long and short, clearly, manifestly, and plainly. They were also well able to know the faculties, natures, and desires [of beings]. With dhāraṇī and unhindered eloquence they requested the Buddha to turn the wheel of the Dharma, and, following him, were able to turn it. Fine drops fall first, to settle the dust of desire; they open the gate of nirvāṇa, fan the wind of liberation, remove the feverish torments of the world, and bring the coolness of the Dharma. Next they rain down the very profound twelve links of dependent origination, to sprinkle the fierce, blazing sunlight of the mass of suffering — ignorance, old age, sickness, death, and the rest. Then they pour out in flood the unsurpassed Great Vehicle, soaking the good roots of beings in all existences; they spread good seeds throughout the field of merit, causing all everywhere to put forth the sprouts of bodhi. With wisdom as sun and moon and skillful means as the seasons, they make the work of the Great Vehicle spread, flourish, and grow, causing the multitude swiftly to attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, eternally abiding bliss, subtle, wondrous, true and real.

Chinese Source

無量大悲救苦眾生，是諸眾生真善知識，是諸眾生大良福田，是諸眾生不請之師，是諸眾生安隱樂處，救處、護處、大依止處，處處為眾作大導師；能為生盲而作眼目，聾劓啞者作耳鼻舌，諸根毀缺能令具足，顛狂荒亂作大正念；船師、大船師，運載群生，渡生死河，置涅槃岸；醫王、大醫王，分別病相，曉了藥性，隨病授藥，令眾樂服；調御、大調御，無諸放逸行，猶如象馬師，能調無不調，師子勇猛威伏眾獸，難可沮壞。遊戲菩薩諸波羅蜜，於如來地堅固不動，安住願力，廣淨佛國，不久得成阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。是諸菩薩摩訶薩，皆有如是不思議功德。

Ru-Yi Translation

With immeasurable great compassion they rescue suffering beings. They are true good friends to these beings, a great and good field of merit for these beings, uninvited teachers of these beings, a place of peace and ease for these beings, a place of rescue, a place of protection, a great place of reliance; everywhere they act as great guides for the multitude. For those born blind they can act as eyes; for the deaf, the noseless, and the mute they act as ears, nose, and tongue; where the faculties are damaged and lacking they can make them complete; for the deranged, wild, and confused they act as great right mindfulness. Ship-masters, great ship-masters, they carry the multitudes of beings across the river of birth-and-death and set them on the shore of nirvāṇa. Kings of physicians, great kings of physicians, they distinguish the marks of illness, understand the nature of medicines, give medicine according to the illness, and cause the multitude to take it gladly. Tamers, great tamers, free of all heedless conduct, like trainers of elephants and horses, they can tame what has never been tamed; lions, brave and fierce, overawing the beasts, hard to obstruct or destroy. Sporting in the pāramitās of the bodhisattva, firm and unmoving in the ground of the Tathāgata, abiding securely in the power of their vows, broadly purifying buddha-lands, before long they will attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. These bodhisattva-mahāsattvas all had such inconceivable merit as this.

The Twelve Thousand Monks

Chinese Source

其比丘名曰：大智舍利弗、神通目揵連、慧命須菩提、摩訶迦旃延、彌多羅尼子富樓那、阿若憍陳如等，天眼阿那律、持律憂波離、侍者阿難、佛子羅云、憂波難陀、離婆多、劫賓那、薄拘羅、阿周陀、莎伽陀、頭陀大迦葉、憂樓頻螺迦葉、伽耶迦葉、那提迦葉。如是等比丘萬二千人，皆阿羅漢，盡諸結漏，無復縛著，真正解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Liu Qiu's preface (units 0–6). Liu Qiu 劉虬 (438–495) was a lay recluse of Jingzhou under the Southern Qi; the preface is his own composition, written in the third year of Yongming (485), not part of the sūtra. The opening of the sūtra itself begins at unit 7.

The seven stages (unit 3). 波利 is read as Bhallika (of the merchants Trapuṣa and Bhallika, 提謂波利, who received the five precepts after the awakening); 拘隣 as Kauṇḍinya. The "fifth" stage quotes this sūtra's claim that "the true and real has not yet been revealed" (未顯真實); the sūtra's own wording in Chapter 2 is 四十餘年未曾顯實 (see Part 2 notes). "Self and purity" (我淨) at the twin trees refers to the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's permanence, bliss, self, and purity. Liu Qiu's scheme is often cited as a forerunner of later classifications of the teachings, including Tiantai's Five Periods.

"Five notes" and "six families" (unit 3). The five notes of Chinese music; the six schools of pre-Han thought (Yin-yang, Confucian, Mohist, Legalist, Names, Daoist) in Sima Tan's list.

Huibiao (unit 4). 偽帝姚略 is read as Yao Lüe, i.e. the Later Qin ruler Yao Xing, whose style name was Zilüe; 從子 "nephew." 螟蛉 ("mulberry caterpillar," from the Book of Odes) is a stock word for an adopted son; it is given as the name He Danzhi gave him. Jianyuan 3 of the Qi = 481; Yongming 3 = 485. 見校弘通 is read "showed it [to me] to be collated and widely circulated"; alternatively, "had it collated and spread."

Xuanpu and Jibin (unit 5). Xuanpu 玄圃 is the mythical garden on Mount Kunlun; Jibin 罽賓 is Kashmir. "The Great One" (太一) and "the Correct Learning" (正學) stand for the Chinese and the Buddhist teaching. "Aspiring to nonbeing" (希無) is the Daoist side, "cultivating emptiness" (修空) the Buddhist.

Sayings in unit 5. "Hard ice is founded on treading frost" echoes the Book of Changes (hexagram Kun); "nine fathoms made by piling earth" echoes the Book of Documents and Laozi 64; "daily diminishing" (日損) echoes Laozi 48.

Zhi, An, Sheng (unit 6). Zhi Dun 支遁 (314–366), Dao'an 道安 (312–385), and Zhu Daosheng 竺道生 (d. 434), the great advocate of sudden awakening. 始簣 "the first basket [of earth]" alludes to Analects 9.19. "The similes also say" (譬喻亦云) points to the Lotus parables of the burning house and the phantom city.

"With an empty fist I deceive small children" (unit 6). The same line is quoted in Mohe Zhiguan (T1911, fascicle 5, on the antidote siddhānta) as 「我坐道場時，不得一切法；空拳誑小兒，誘度於一切。」; its sūtra source was not located in the vault's Taishō files.

Opening assembly (units 11–13). Names shared with the site's Lotus Sūtra, Chapter 1 ("The Assembly and the Six Signs") follow that page: Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa near Rājagṛha, Constant-Diligence, Ratnākara, Beyond-the-Three-Realms, Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya, Mahākātyāyana, Kapphiṇa, Revata, Vakkula, and the three Kāśyapa brothers. Other bodhisattva names are rendered by meaning in the same hyphenated style. 毘摩跋羅 and 阿周陀 are given in pinyin (Pimobaluo, Azhoutuo) because their Sanskrit forms are uncertain.

䠒跪 (unit 14). A variant of 胡跪, "kneeling with one knee raised."

The marks (units 20–21). 腋掌合縵內外握 is obscure; rendered literally ("armpits and palms full, webbed, clasping within and without"). 陰馬藏 is the mark of the private part sheathed like a horse's; 鹿𨄔腸 "calves like a deer's."

Eight qualities of the Brahmā voice (unit 28). 八種 is read as the eight qualities of the Buddha's voice; the text names only the number.
