Beyond Every Comparison

非說所說分第二十一 至 福智無比分第二十四

Why calling this teaching "spoken" at all risks slandering it, the systematic clearing of body, speech, and mind, and why a teaching level with no higher and no lower still outweighs a mountain of treasure.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T235 · Kumārajīva's translation, c. 402 CE Commentary guide: Grand Master Taixu's 金剛般若波羅蜜經講錄 (1927)

21. Not Speaking, Yet Spoken

Chinese Source · 非說所說分第二十一

「須菩提！汝勿謂如來作是念：『我當有所說法。』莫作是念。何以故？若人言：如來有所說法，即為謗佛，不能解我所說故。須菩提！說法者，無法可說，是名說法。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Subhūti, do not say that the Tathāgata has the thought, 'I should teach a teaching.' Do not think this way. Why is this? If someone says the Tathāgata has taught a teaching, they slander the Buddha, because they cannot understand what I have taught. Subhūti, one who teaches, teaches no teaching at all — that is what is called teaching."

爾時，慧命須菩提白佛言：「世尊！頗有眾生，於未來世，聞說是法，生信心不？」佛言：「須菩提！彼非眾生，非不眾生。何以故？須菩提！眾生眾生者，如來說非眾生，是名眾生。」

At that time the venerable Subhūti said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, in ages to come, will there be beings who hear this teaching and give rise to faith?" The Buddha said: "Subhūti, they are neither beings nor non-beings. Why is this? Subhūti, what are called 'beings,' the Tathāgata says, are no beings at all — that is why they are called beings."

Reading note — after Taixu

Taixu takes the word "slander" seriously here, not as rhetorical overstatement: to say the Tathāgata "has taught a teaching," treated as a fixed thing handed from a fixed teacher, misunderstands the Tathāgata so completely that it counts as a kind of defamation, however devout the intention behind it. Teaching, genuinely done, teaches nothing that could be picked up and carried away as an object — "one who teaches, teaches no teaching at all" is not a riddle but a description of what actually happens when a mind that dwells nowhere speaks.

Subhūti's title here — 慧命須菩提, "wisdom-life Subhūti" — is one Taixu pauses on: Subhūti has made the wisdom that sees beings as empty his very life, which is why his question about future faith carries real weight. The answer he receives collapses the usual distinction between buddha and being: every being already carries the same vajra-wisdom seed a buddha has fully uncovered — "beings" only in the sense that the seed has not yet flowered, "no beings" in the sense that nothing about their nature actually differs from a buddha's. Faith in a future age is possible for exactly this reason: there is no gulf to cross, only a covering to be worn through.

22. Nothing Whatsoever to Attain

Chinese Source · 無法可得分第二十二

須菩提白佛言：「世尊！佛得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。為無所得耶？」佛言：「如是，如是。須菩提！我於阿耨多羅三藐三菩提乃至無有少法可得，是名阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Subhūti said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, when the Buddha attained unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening — did he attain nothing at all?" The Buddha said: "So it is, so it is, Subhūti. In unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, I did not attain the least thing whatsoever — that is exactly why it is called unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening."

Reading note — after Taixu

Taixu reads Sections 20 through 22 as one deliberate sequence, methodically clearing the three traditional avenues of karma — body, speech, and mind — of anything fixed to hold onto. The Buddha teaches through these three: walking his alms round is teaching through the body; entering concentration and speaking the dharma is teaching through speech; guiding beings to practise toward awakening is teaching through the mind. Section 20 already showed that neither the radiant reward-body nor the countless response-bodies are simply the dharma-body itself — clearing the body. Section 21 showed that no fixed teaching is ever really spoken — clearing speech. This section clears the last and subtlest of the three: even the Buddha's own awakening, the mind's own attainment, is not the least fixed thing gotten.

Subhūti's question shows he has followed the argument but still wants final confirmation, and the Buddha's double "so it is, so it is" gives it without qualification. Taixu's phrasing is blunt: if there were even a hair's breadth of something actually gained, that gain itself would disqualify the attainment from being unsurpassed — because anything gained is, by definition, a fixed possession sitting apart from everything else, and "unsurpassed" cannot mean "one fixed thing among others." It is precisely the absence of anything held onto that lets the attainment be called complete.

23. Pure Mind, Wholesome Action

Chinese Source · 淨心行善分第二十三

「復次，須菩提！是法平等，無有高下，是名『阿耨多羅三藐三菩提』；以無我、無人、無眾生、無壽者，修一切善法，則得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。須菩提！所言善法者，如來說即非善法，是名善法。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Subhūti, this teaching is level and equal, with nothing higher and nothing lower — that is why it is called unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. Free of self, free of person, free of being, free of lifespan, cultivate every wholesome teaching, and unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening is attained. Subhūti, what is called a wholesome teaching, the Tathāgata says, is no wholesome teaching at all — that is why it is called a wholesome teaching."

Reading note — after Taixu

Having shown that nothing is gained, Taixu says the sūtra now has to answer the question that naturally follows: if nothing is gained, why practise at all? "Level, with nothing higher and nothing lower" is the hinge — the awakened nature is not increased in a sage or diminished in an ordinary being; it is the identical ground under both. Because it is level in this way, every wholesome action — the six pāramitās and everything built on them — genuinely leads toward it, provided it is practised free of the four notions that would otherwise turn the practice back into more self-building.

The closing "wholesome teaching, no wholesome teaching, that is why it is called wholesome" is not a hedge, Taixu insists, but the same discipline applied to virtue itself: goodness has no fixed essence sitting behind the label either, only the name given to conduct that helps rather than harms. Treating "wholesome" as a fixed prize to be collected would recreate, in the register of ethics, the exact same grasping the sūtra has spent twenty-two sections dismantling everywhere else.

24. Merit and Wisdom Beyond Compare

Chinese Source · 福智無比分第二十四

「須菩提！若三千大千世界中，所有諸須彌山王，如是等七寶聚，有人持用布施；若人以此《般若波羅蜜經》，乃至四句偈等，受持讀誦，為他人說，於前福德百分不及一，百千萬億分、乃至算數譬喻所不能及。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Subhūti, if someone gathered up every Mount Sumeru, king of mountains, in a billion-world universe, made them into a mass of the seven treasures, and gave it all away; and if someone else took up even a single four-line verse of this Perfection of Wisdom Sūtra, upheld it, recited it, and explained it to others — the first person's merit would not be a hundredth part of the second, not a fraction in a hundred thousand million, not any fraction that number or likeness could express."

Reading note — after Taixu

Taixu treats the scale of this comparison — not one treasure-filled cosmos but every Sumeru, king of mountains, across an entire billion-world universe turned into treasure — as making the same point already made twice before, in Sections 8 and 11, but sharpens exactly where the two kinds of giving actually diverge. Giving away treasure, however vast, is material giving: it plants merit alone. Taking up and passing on even four lines of this sūtra is giving of the teaching: it cultivates merit and wisdom together in the same act — 福慧雙修, as he puts it — because the giver's own understanding deepens in the explaining, and the listener receives not wealth that will be spent but a seed that grows into their own seeing. Two kinds of giving that look comparable from a distance turn out, on inspection, to belong to entirely different orders of consequence — which is why no fraction, however small, can properly express the difference between them.
