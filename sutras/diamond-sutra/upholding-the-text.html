A Mind That Dwells Nowhere

如法受持分第十三 至 能淨業障分第十六

The sūtra names itself, Subhūti weeps at how deep the teaching runs, King Kalī's cruelty meets a patience with no self left to resent it, and karmic obstruction burns off the way frost burns off in sunlight.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Kumārajīva's translation, c. 402 CE (Taishō T235), in the received text chanted today Commentary guide: Grand Master Taixu's 金剛般若波羅蜜經講錄 (1927)

13. Upholding It As Taught

Chinese Source · 如法受持分第十三

爾時，須菩提白佛言：「世尊！當何名此經？我等云何奉持？」佛告須菩提：「是經名為金剛般若波羅蜜，以是名字，汝當奉持。所以者何？須菩提！佛說般若波羅蜜，即非般若波羅蜜，是名般若波羅蜜。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Subhūti said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, what should this sūtra be called? How should we hold and carry it?" The Buddha told Subhūti: "This sūtra is named the Diamond Perfection of Wisdom. By this name you should hold and carry it. Why is that? Subhūti, what the Buddha calls the Perfection of Wisdom is no Perfection of Wisdom — that is why it is called the Perfection of Wisdom.

須菩提！於意云何？如來有所說法不？」須菩提白佛言：「世尊！如來無所說。」「須菩提！於意云何？三千大千世界所有微塵，是為多不？」須菩提言：「甚多，世尊！」「須菩提！諸微塵，如來說非微塵，是名微塵；如來說世界，非世界，是名世界。須菩提！於意云何？可以三十二相見如來不？」「不也，世尊！不可以三十二相得見如來。何以故？如來說：三十二相，即是非相，是名三十二相。」「須菩提！若有善男子、善女人，以恆河沙等身命布施；若復有人，於此經中，乃至受持四句偈等，為他人說，其福甚多！」

Subhūti, what do you think — has the Tathāgata taught any teaching?" Subhūti said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, the Tathāgata has taught nothing." "Subhūti, what do you think — are the particles of dust in a billion-world universe many?" Subhūti said: "Very many, World-Honored One." "Subhūti, these particles of dust the Tathāgata calls no particles of dust — that is why they are called particles of dust; the world the Tathāgata calls no world — that is why it is called a world. Subhūti, what do you think — can the Tathāgata be seen by his thirty-two marks?" "No, World-Honored One, the Tathāgata cannot be seen by his thirty-two marks. Why is this? The Tathāgata says the thirty-two marks are no marks at all — that is why they are called the thirty-two marks." "Subhūti, if a good son or good daughter gave up as many bodies and lives as there are grains of sand in the Ganges; and if someone else took up even a single four-line verse of this sūtra and explained it to others — that person's merit would be far, far greater."

Reading note — after Taixu

Halfway through, the sūtra finally names itself — and Taixu makes sure the name is not mistaken for a fixed label. "Diamond" is not literally a thing, yet functions as an image; "Perfection of Wisdom" is not literally a fixed teaching, yet functions as one. Hold the name, he says, without clinging either to a self doing the holding or to a teaching being held — the name itself has no essence of its own, only a provisional label offered for beings' sake. This is the sūtra's clearest statement yet of its own recurring pattern: what the Buddha calls the Perfection of Wisdom is no Perfection of Wisdom — that is exactly why it is called the Perfection of Wisdom.

Taixu distinguishes three kinds of giving up one's body and life: giving the body itself, giving the life itself, and giving both together — a practice, he says, that only makes sense once the giver has actually realized this teaching, because only then are self and other no longer two. At that point, he writes, "I am the mountains, rivers, grass, and leaves of the whole earth, and the mountains, rivers, grass, and leaves of the whole earth are me" — and he adds a sobering count: across beginningless lives already spent cycling through birth and death, a being has already given up more bodies than there are grains of sand in the Ganges, involuntarily, to no purpose at all. A single body given consciously, in service of the teaching, is a small thing beside that — and still, he says, nothing beside holding four lines of this sūtra and passing them on.

14. Beyond All Marks, Utter Stillness

Chinese Source · 離相寂滅分第十四

爾時，須菩提聞說是經，深解義趣，涕淚悲泣，而白佛言：「希有世尊！佛說如是甚深經典，我從昔來所得慧眼，未曾得聞如是之經。世尊！若復有人，得聞是經，信心清淨，則生實相，當知是人，成就第一希有功德。世尊！是實相者，則是非相，是故如來說名實相。世尊！我今得聞如是經典，信解受持，不足為難；若當來世，後五百歲，其有眾生，得聞是經，信解受持，是人則為第一希有。何以故？此人無我相、人相、眾生相、壽者相。所以者何？我相即是非相，人相、眾生相、壽者相即是非相。何以故？離一切諸相，則名諸佛。」佛告須菩提：「如是！如是！若復有人，得聞是經，不驚、不怖、不畏，當知是人甚為希有。何以故？須菩提！如來說第一波羅蜜，非第一波羅蜜，是名第一波羅蜜。須菩提！忍辱波羅蜜，如來說非忍辱波羅蜜。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, hearing this sūtra taught, Subhūti understood its meaning deeply, and, weeping, said to the Buddha: "How rare, World-Honored One! Since I first gained the eye of wisdom, I have never before heard a sūtra as profound as this. World-Honored One, if someone hears this sūtra with a pure and believing mind, true reality will arise in them, and know that this person accomplishes the rarest merit. World-Honored One, this true reality is no mark at all; that is why the Tathāgata calls it true reality. World-Honored One, now that I have heard a sūtra such as this, to believe, understand, receive, and uphold it is not hard for me. But if in an age to come, in the last five hundred years, there are beings who hear this sūtra and believe, understand, receive, and uphold it, such people will be the rarest of all. Why is this? Such people hold no notion of a self, a person, a being, a lifespan. And why is that? The notion of a self is no notion at all, and the notions of a person, a being, a lifespan are no notions at all. Why is this? To be free of every notion is what is called being a buddha." The Buddha told Subhūti: "So it is, so it is. If someone hears this sūtra and is not alarmed, not frightened, not afraid, know that such a person is rare indeed. Why is this? Subhūti, what the Tathāgata calls the foremost perfection is no foremost perfection — that is why it is called the foremost perfection. Subhūti, the perfection of patience the Tathāgata calls no perfection of patience."

「何以故？須菩提！如我昔為歌利王割截身體，我於爾時，無我相、無人相、無眾生相、無壽者相。何以故？我於往昔，節節支解時，若有我相、人相、眾生相、壽者相，應生瞋恨。須菩提！又念過去，於五百世，作忍辱仙人，於爾所世，無我相、無人相、無眾生相、無壽者相。」

"Why is this? Subhūti, long ago when King Kalī cut my body limb from limb, I held no notion of a self, no notion of a person, no notion of a being, no notion of a lifespan. Why do I say this? Because if, as my limbs were cut away piece by piece, I had held the notion of a self, a person, a being, a lifespan, I would surely have given rise to anger and resentment. Subhūti, I also recall that in ages past, for five hundred lives, I was a sage who practiced patience — and through all those lives I held no notion of a self, a person, a being, a lifespan."

「是故，須菩提！菩薩應離一切相，發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，不應住色生心，不應住聲、香、味、觸、法生心，應生無所住心。若心有住，則為非住。是故佛說：菩薩心不應住色布施。須菩提！菩薩為利益一切眾生，應如是布施。如來說：一切諸相，即是非相；又說：一切眾生，則非眾生。須菩提！如來是真語者、實語者、如語者、不誑語者、不異語者。須菩提！如來所得法，此法無實無虛。須菩提！若菩薩心住於法而行布施，如人入闇，則無所見；若菩薩心不住法而行布施，如人有目，日光明照，見種種色。須菩提！當來之世，若有善男子、善女人，能於此經受持讀誦，則為如來以佛智慧，悉知是人，悉見是人，皆得成就無量無邊功德。」

"Therefore, Subhūti, a bodhisattva should be free of every notion in setting out for unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. They should not give rise to a mind dwelling on form, nor give rise to a mind dwelling on sound, smell, taste, touch, or mental object; they should give rise to a mind that dwells nowhere. If the mind dwells on anything, that is no true dwelling. That is why the Buddha says a bodhisattva's mind should not dwell on form in giving. Subhūti, for the benefit of all beings, a bodhisattva should give in just this way. The Tathāgata says that all marks are no marks, and says too that all beings are no beings. Subhūti, the Tathāgata speaks what is true, speaks what is real, speaks what is so; he does not deceive and does not speak otherwise. Subhūti, the teaching the Tathāgata has attained is neither substantial nor empty. Subhūti, a bodhisattva who gives while the mind dwells on things is like a person who has entered the dark and sees nothing; a bodhisattva who gives while the mind does not dwell on things is like a person with good eyes in bright sunlight, seeing every kind of form. Subhūti, in an age to come, if a good son or good daughter can uphold and recite this sūtra, the Tathāgata, with a buddha's wisdom, fully knows that person and fully sees that person: all such people will accomplish measureless, boundless merit."

Reading note — after Taixu

Taixu reads Subhūti's tears as the mark of genuine understanding finally landing, not mere emotion: he had already realized, through the wisdom-eye of his own path, that beings are empty — but hearing this teaching in full is something he says even he had never encountered before. "True reality," Taixu is careful to add, is not some further reality hiding behind appearances; it is exactly how things already are, seen without distortion — which is why the sūtra immediately calls it "no mark" as well, so that reality itself does not become one more thing to grasp.

On King Kalī's cruelty, Taixu adds a detail easy to miss on a fast read: in that past life as the patient sage, endlessly wronged, the bodhisattva held not only no anger but made a private vow toward his tormentor — "when I attain the path, I will be the first to liberate you." The absence of resentment was never numbness; it was patience with nowhere left for anger to grab hold, because there was no fixed self left standing behind the pain to defend. Taixu also glosses the sūtra's later line that the Tathāgata's realization is "neither substantial nor empty" (無實無虛): to treat it as substantial is to fall into the fixed view of permanence; to treat it as empty is to fall into the fixed view of annihilation. Genuine patience, like genuine realization, stands clear of both edges at once.

15. The Merit of Upholding This Sūtra

Chinese Source · 持經功德分第十五

「須菩提！若有善男子、善女人，初日分以恆河沙等身布施，中日分復以恆河沙等身布施，後日分亦以恆河沙等身布施，如是無量百千萬億劫，以身布施；若復有人，聞此經典，信心不逆，其福勝彼，何況書寫、受持、讀誦、為人解說。」「須菩提！以要言之，是經有不可思議、不可稱量、無邊功德。如來為發大乘者說，為發最上乘者說。若有人能受持讀誦，廣為人說，如來悉知是人，悉見是人，皆得成就不可量、不可稱、無有邊、不可思議功德。如是人等，則為荷擔如來阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。何以故？須菩提！若樂小法者，著我見、人見、眾生見、壽者見，則於此經，不能聽受讀誦、為人解說。須菩提！在在處處，若有此經，一切世間天、人、阿修羅，所應供養；當知此處，則為是塔，皆應恭敬，作禮圍繞，以諸華香而散其處。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Subhūti, if a good son or good daughter gave up as many bodies as there are grains of sand in the Ganges in the morning, gave up as many bodies again at midday, and gave up as many bodies again in the evening, and kept giving up bodies like this for countless hundreds of thousands of millions of eons; and if someone else, on hearing this sūtra, believed it without resistance — this person's merit would surpass the first, how much more so one who copies it, upholds it, recites it, and explains it to others." "Subhūti, to put it briefly, this sūtra has merit beyond thought, beyond measure, without limit. The Tathāgata teaches it for those who set out on the Great Vehicle, for those who set out on the supreme vehicle. If someone can uphold it, recite it, and explain it widely to others, the Tathāgata fully knows that person and fully sees that person: all such people will accomplish merit that cannot be measured, cannot be weighed, has no limit, and is beyond thought. Such people carry on their shoulders the Tathāgata's unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. Why is this? Subhūti, those who delight in lesser teachings cling to the view of a self, a person, a being, a lifespan, and so they cannot listen to this sūtra, receive it, recite it, or explain it to others. Subhūti, wherever this sūtra is found, it should be honored with offerings by every being in every world — gods, humans, and asuras. Know that such a place is itself a stūpa: all should revere it, bow to it, walk around it, and scatter flowers and incense there."

Reading note — after Taixu

Taixu's image for why this merit resists measurement is a mirror: a mirror that holds nothing of its own reflects whatever comes before it completely, without limit; a mirror clouded by its own fixed image reflects only within the narrow bounds of what it already clings to. Merit tied to a mark — however vast the mark, even bodies given up morning, noon, and night across countless eons — is a clouded mirror, bounded by exactly what it holds onto. Merit that arises without dwelling on anything is the clear mirror: unlimited, because there was never a fixed edge to it in the first place.

He is equally direct about who, in his experience, tends to miss this teaching even with real devotion: those content with the "lesser vehicle's" residual nirvāṇa and unwilling to turn toward the greater path; and sincere practitioners whose motives, examined closely, are still aimed elsewhere — seeking worldly wealth and long life, seeking a quick escape from present affliction through some fixed absorption, seeking rebirth in the heavens, or hoping only for a favorable next life rather than awakening itself. None of these are condemned outright, but Taixu is clear that a mind still aimed at any of them cannot yet receive this sūtra as it is meant to be received — which is exactly why he calls one who genuinely can "a bearer of the Tathāgata's own unsurpassed awakening," carrying the family's whole estate on their own shoulders.

16. Purifying Karmic Obstruction

Chinese Source · 能淨業障分第十六

「復次，須菩提！善男子、善女人，受持讀誦此經，若為人輕賤，是人先世罪業，應墮惡道，以今世人輕賤故，先世罪業則為消滅，當得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。須菩提！我念過去無量阿僧祇劫，於燃燈佛前，得值八百四千萬億那由他諸佛，悉皆供養承事，無空過者。若復有人，於後末世，能受持讀誦此經，所得功德，於我所供養諸佛功德，百分不及一，千萬億分，乃至算數譬喻所不能及。須菩提！若善男子、善女人，於後末世，有受持讀誦此經，所得功德，我若具說者，或有人聞，心則狂亂，狐疑不信。須菩提！當知是經義不可思議，果報亦不可思議。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Subhūti, if a good son or good daughter upholds and recites this sūtra and is treated with contempt, that is because of wrongdoing from a former life which should have brought them down into a lower realm; but because they are treated with contempt in this present life, the wrongdoing of that former life is thereby dissolved, and they will attain unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. Subhūti, I remember that countless incalculable eons ago, before the time of Dīpaṃkara Buddha, I met eighty-four thousand myriad millions of nayutas of buddhas, and made offerings to and served every one of them, letting not one pass by. Yet if someone, in the final age to come, can uphold and recite this sūtra, the merit they gain is such that my merit from making offerings to all those buddhas does not equal a hundredth of it, nor a millionth, nor any fraction that number or comparison could express. Subhūti, if I were to describe in full the merit gained by a good son or good daughter who upholds and recites this sūtra in the final age, some who heard it would be thrown into confusion, doubtful and unbelieving. Subhūti, know that the meaning of this sūtra is beyond thought, and its fruit, too, is beyond thought."

Reading note — after Taixu

Taixu names three layers of obstruction this teaching works through: the obstruction of affliction — greed, anger, delusion, pride, doubt; the obstruction of karma itself — a mind turned upside down and driven by habit rather than freedom; and the obstruction of consequence — the way a bird's own past causes confine it to the sky and never the water, a fish's confine it to the water and never the sky, a human's confine it to neither. His image for how the sūtra dissolves these: karmic obstruction is like frost or morning dew, and this teaching is like the sun of wisdom — the sun does not fight the frost or negotiate with it; it simply rises, and the frost is gone.

The Buddha offers his own case as the clearest evidence of scale: across countless incalculable eons, before even Dīpaṃkara Buddha, the Buddha served eighty-four thousand myriad millions of buddhas without ever once missing an occasion to make offerings — merit built up across more lifetimes than can be counted. And still, he says, holding this sūtra sincerely in the degenerate age to come outweighs all of that accumulated offering-merit by a margin no fraction or comparison could express. The reason, Taixu explains, is the same distinction running through the whole fascicle: offerings made to buddhas, however vast, remain "conditioned" merit, bounded by what was given; holding this teaching is "unconditioned" merit, and unconditioned merit reaches awakening directly, by a different order of speed entirely.
