What Generosity Is Actually Worth

Part 5 carries the sūtra's tenth and eleventh chapters. Kṣitigarbha asks the Buddha a question anyone who has ever given anything has probably wondered about privately: why does the same act of generosity seem to carry such different weight from one case to the next? The Buddha's answer works through giving to the destitute, giving to images and monasteries, giving to the elderly and the sick, and giving within the Buddha-dharma itself, before the earth-spirit Dṛḍha — who has protected the whole of Jambudvīpa's soil since long before this sūtra existed — steps forward with her own account of what this teaching is worth to her, and what she will do for anyone who keeps it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 5 of 6 complete Source: Taishō T0412, 地藏菩薩本願經 (tr. Śikṣānanda) Reading notes after Lingcheng's 地藏菩薩本願經科注 (X0384)

Comparing the Weight of Generosity

Chinese Source

爾時，地藏菩薩摩訶薩承佛威神，從座而起，胡跪合掌，白佛言：「世尊！我觀業道眾生，校量布施，有輕有重：有一生受福，有十生受福，有百生千生受大福利者。是事云何？唯願世尊，為我說之。」
爾時，佛告地藏菩薩：「吾今於忉利天宮，一切眾會，說閻浮提布施，校量功德輕重。汝當諦聽，吾為汝說。」
地藏白佛言：「我疑是事，願樂欲聞。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva, by the Buddha's spiritual power, rose from his seat, knelt with one knee to the ground, joined his palms, and said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, I observe that among beings on the path of karma, the weighing of generosity carries different weights — some receive blessing for one lifetime, some for ten lifetimes, some receive great blessing and benefit for a hundred or a thousand lifetimes. What is the truth of this? I beg only that the World-Honored One explain it to me."

At that time the Buddha said to Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva: "I will now, before this whole assembly in the Trāyastriṃśa Heaven, explain the comparative weight of generosity's merit in Jambudvīpa. Listen carefully — I will explain it for you."

Kṣitigarbha said to the Buddha: "I am uncertain of this matter, and wish gladly to hear it."

Reading note — after Lingcheng

Lingcheng raises, and sets aside, the obvious question this opening exchange invites: does Kṣitigarbha genuinely not know the answer, or is he asking on behalf of an audience that does not? He cites a story from the Miscellaneous Storehouse Sūtra in which a devotee offers a single golden flower to a Buddha-image and, hoping to learn the exact merit of the act, is passed from an ordinary monk, to a sūtra-reciter, to an arhat with clairvoyant powers, all the way up to Maitreya himself — and even Maitreya answers only that a being as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, each already destined for buddhahood in one more life, still could not calculate it. Lingcheng's conclusion is that Kṣitigarbha's question is genuine rather than rhetorical: the exact weight of a single act of generosity is, on this sūtra's own admission, beyond even a bodhisattva of his rank to compute unaided — which is precisely why the answer that follows works by comparison rather than by exact sum.

Giving to the Poor, and Giving to Images

Chinese Source

佛告地藏菩薩：「南閻浮提，有諸國王、宰輔大臣、大長者、大剎利、大婆羅門等，若遇最下貧窮，乃至癃殘瘖瘂，聾癡無目，如是種種不完具者。是大國王等，欲布施時，若能具大慈悲，下心含笑，親手遍布施，或使人施，軟言慰喻。是國王等，所獲福利，如布施百恒河沙佛功德之利。何以故？緣是國王等，於是最貧賤輩，及不完具者，發大慈心，是故福利，有如此報：百千生中，常得七寶具足，何況衣食受用？

「復次，地藏！若未來世，有諸國王，至婆羅門等，遇佛塔寺，或佛形像，乃至菩薩、聲聞、辟支佛像，躬自營辦，供養布施。是國王等，當得三劫為帝釋身，受勝妙樂。若能以此布施福利，迴向法界，是大國王等，於十劫中，常為大梵天王。

「復次，地藏！若未來世，有諸國王，至婆羅門等，遇先佛塔廟，或至經像，毀壞破落，乃能發心修補。是國王等，或自營辦，或勸他人，乃至百千人等，布施結緣。是國王等，百千生中，常為轉輪王身。如是他人同布施者，百千生中，常為小國王身。更能於塔廟前，發迴向心，如是國王，乃及諸人，盡成佛道，以此果報，無量無邊。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said to Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva: "In southern Jambudvīpa, there are kings, ministers, great officials, great elders, great kṣatriyas, and great brāhmaṇas, who, when they encounter the lowest and most destitute, or those who are crippled, mute, deaf, simple-minded, or blind, incomplete in every such way — when these great kings and others wish to give to them, if they can do so with great compassion, humbling themselves, smiling, giving with their own hands to every one of them, or having others give on their behalf, with gentle words of comfort, then the blessing and benefit such kings gain is equal to the merit of giving to as many buddhas as the sands of a hundred Ganges rivers. Why is this? Because these kings, toward the lowest, most destitute, and most incomplete of beings, give rise to a mind of great compassion — and it is for this reason that such blessing and benefit brings this result: for a hundred thousand lifetimes, they will always have the seven treasures in abundance — how much more so, then, food and clothing to use as they please?

"Furthermore, Kṣitigarbha, if in ages to come there are kings or brāhmaṇas who, encountering a buddha's stūpa or monastery, or an image of a buddha, or even images of bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, or pratyekabuddhas, personally see to making offerings to them, such kings will, for three kalpas, become Śakra himself, enjoying supreme and wondrous bliss. And if they can dedicate the blessing of this giving to the whole Dharma-realm, such great kings will, for ten kalpas, always be a Great Brahmā King.

"Furthermore, Kṣitigarbha, if in ages to come there are kings or brāhmaṇas who, encountering the stūpas, monasteries, sūtras, or images of buddhas of the past fallen into ruin and disrepair, are moved to repair them — whether they see to this themselves, or urge others to do so, even a hundred or a thousand people together, forming a bond of giving — such kings will, for a hundred thousand lifetimes, always be a wheel-turning king; and those others who joined in the giving will, for a hundred thousand lifetimes, always be minor kings. And if they can, further, before that stūpa or monastery, give rise to a mind that dedicates this merit, then such kings, and all those others, will attain buddhahood entirely — and the fruit of this is measureless, boundless."

Reading note — after Lingcheng

Lingcheng draws a distinction the sūtra itself leaves implicit: giving to the destitute is giving to a "field of compassion" (悲田), while giving to a buddha's image is giving to a "field of reverence" (敬田) — and his point is that the sūtra ranks the first, not the second, as equal to giving to a hundred Ganges' worth of buddhas. He grounds this in a pointed passage from the Sūtra Resolving Doubts in the Semblance Dharma: "there are beings who see others gathering to perform merit, and, seeking only reputation, empty their household's wealth into it — and who then see the poor and destitute nearby, and drive them off with curses, giving not the smallest coin. This is called performing goodness upside-down — not true merit-making at all. Such people spend a great deal and gain very little." On Lingcheng's reading, the sūtra is not ranking a poor beggar above the Buddha in some absolute sense; it is insisting that the mind doing the giving, not the rank of the recipient, is what actually determines the field's weight — a lesser field met with genuine compassion outweighs a greater field met with performance.

Giving to the Old, the Sick, and Women in Labor

Chinese Source

「復次，地藏！未來世中，有諸國王，及婆羅門等，見諸老病，及生產婦女，若一念間，具大慈心，布施醫藥、飲食、臥具，使令安樂。如是福利，最不思議：一百劫中，常為淨居天主；二百劫中，常為六欲天主；畢竟成佛，永不墮惡道，乃至百千生中，耳不聞苦聲。

「復次，地藏！若未來世中，有諸國王，及婆羅門等，能作如是布施，獲福無量，更能迴向，不問多少，畢竟成佛，何況釋梵轉輪之報？是故，地藏！普勸眾生，當如是學。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Kṣitigarbha, in ages to come, if there are kings or brāhmaṇas who, seeing the aged, the sick, or women in labor, give rise, even for the space of a single thought, to a mind of great compassion, and give medicine, food, or bedding, bringing them ease — the blessing and benefit of this is most inconceivable: for a hundred kalpas, they will always be lord of the Pure Abodes; for two hundred kalpas, they will always be lord of the six heavens of desire; and in the end they will attain buddhahood, never falling into an evil destiny, and for a hundred thousand lifetimes their ears will never so much as hear a cry of suffering.

"Furthermore, Kṣitigarbha, if in ages to come there are kings or brāhmaṇas who can give in this way, and gain measureless blessing, and can further dedicate it — whether much or little — they will attain buddhahood in the end, how much more so, then, the lesser reward of becoming a Śakra, a Brahmā, or a wheel-turning king? For this reason, Kṣitigarbha, broadly urge beings to learn to give in just this way."

Reading note — after Lingcheng

Lingcheng lingers on why this particular kind of giving — a single thought of compassion toward the old, the sick, and women in labor — is called "most inconceivable" even though the actual gifts described (medicine, food, bedding) are the plainest and least glamorous in the whole chapter. His answer is that the three recipients named here share something the earlier categories do not: each is suffering in a way no image, monastery, or ruined sūtra ever does, which means the giving cannot be even partly motivated by reverence for what is being given to — it can only be compassion for what is actually being felt. A gift to a suffering stranger, offered for no reason but that they are suffering, is, on this reading, the least performable and therefore the most purely tested form of generosity the sūtra describes, which is exactly why its reward outranks even giving to a buddha's own image.

What Giving Within the Buddha-Dharma Is Worth

Chinese Source

「復次，地藏！未來世中，若善男子、善女人，於佛法中，種少善根，毛髮沙塵等許，所受福利，不可為喻。復次，地藏！未來世中，若有善男子、善女人，遇佛形像、菩薩形像、辟支佛形像、轉輪王形像，布施供養，得無量福，常在人天，受勝妙樂。若能迴向法界，是人福利，不可為喻。

「復次，地藏！未來世中，若有善男子、善女人，遇大乘經典，或聽聞一偈一句，發殷重心，讚歎恭敬，布施供養。是人獲大果報，無量無邊。若能迴向法界，其福不可為喻。

「復次，地藏！若未來世中，有善男子、善女人，遇佛塔寺、大乘經典新者，布施供養，瞻禮讚歎，恭敬合掌；若遇故者或毀壞者，修補營理，或獨發心，或勸多人同共發心。如是等輩，三十生中，常為諸小國王。檀越之人，常為輪王，還以善法，教化諸小國王。

「復次，地藏！未來世中，若有善男子、善女人，於佛法中，所種善根，或布施供養，或修補塔寺，或裝理經典，乃至一毛一塵，一沙一渧。如是善事，但能迴向法界，是人功德，百千生中，受上妙樂。如但迴向自家眷屬，或自身利益，如是之果，即三生受樂，捨一得萬報。是故，地藏！布施因緣，其事如是。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Kṣitigarbha, in ages to come, if there are good sons or good daughters who plant even the smallest wholesome root within the Buddha-dharma — no bigger than a strand of hair, a grain of sand, or a mote of dust — the blessing and benefit they receive cannot be compared to anything. Furthermore, Kṣitigarbha, in ages to come, if there are good sons or good daughters who, encountering an image of a buddha, a bodhisattva, a pratyekabuddha, or a wheel-turning king, give offerings to it, they will gain measureless blessing, and will forever dwell among gods and humans, enjoying supreme and wondrous bliss. And if they can dedicate this to the whole Dharma-realm, that person's blessing and benefit cannot be compared to anything.

"Furthermore, Kṣitigarbha, in ages to come, if there are good sons or good daughters who, encountering a Mahāyāna sūtra, hear even a single verse or phrase of it, and give rise to a mind of earnest reverence, praising and honoring it, and giving offerings to it, that person will obtain a great and measureless, boundless result. And if they can dedicate this to the whole Dharma-realm, their blessing cannot be compared to anything.

"Furthermore, Kṣitigarbha, in ages to come, if there are good sons or good daughters who, encountering a buddha's stūpa or monastery, or a new copy of a Mahāyāna sūtra, give offerings to it, gaze on it and bow, praise it and join their palms in reverence — or, encountering an old or damaged one, repair and restore it, whether alone or urging many others to join together in doing so — such people will, for thirty lifetimes, always be minor kings; and the one who led the giving will always be a wheel-turning king, who will in turn use wholesome teaching to guide those minor kings.

"Furthermore, Kṣitigarbha, in ages to come, if there are good sons or good daughters who plant a wholesome root within the Buddha-dharma — whether by giving offerings, repairing a stūpa or monastery, or restoring a sūtra — even so small a thing as a single hair, a mote of dust, a grain of sand, or a single drop — if such a good deed can be dedicated to the whole Dharma-realm, that person's merit will, for a hundred thousand lifetimes, bring them the finest and most wondrous bliss. But if it is dedicated only to their own family, or their own benefit, the result will last only three lifetimes of enjoyment — giving up the whole for a ten-thousandfold return in exchange for merely a fraction of it. For this reason, Kṣitigarbha, the causes and conditions of giving are just as I have said."

Reading note — after Lingcheng

Lingcheng treats the "three lifetimes versus a hundred thousand" comparison as the chapter's real thesis, everything before it a set of worked examples building toward this one point about dedication itself. He reaches for a verse from Yongjia's Song of Enlightenment to characterize giving that stops at one's own family or one's own benefit: "generosity that clings to appearances breeds only heavenly merit — like shooting an arrow straight up into the empty sky; once its force is spent, it falls back down again, and what it brings you next life is exactly the disappointment you'd expect." The scriptural line he sets beside it is blunter still: giving that clings to appearances is called "the third life's enemy" — the first life enjoys the merit, the second life is still coasting on what remains of it, and the third life arrives to find the debt exhausted and nothing left to show for it. Dedicating the same small act to the whole Dharma-realm instead, on Lingcheng's reading, is not a more generous version of the same gift; it is the difference between an arrow that must eventually come down and a river that empties into an ocean it can never leave again.

Dṛḍha, the Earth-Spirit Who Has Watched Since the Beginning

Chinese Source

爾時，堅牢地神白佛言：「世尊！我從昔來，瞻視頂禮無量菩薩摩訶薩，皆是大不可思議神通智慧，廣度眾生。是地藏菩薩摩訶薩，於諸菩薩，誓願深重。世尊！是地藏菩薩，於閻浮提，有大因緣。如文殊、普賢、觀音、彌勒，亦化百千身形，度於六道，其願尚有畢竟。是地藏菩薩，教化六道一切眾生，所發誓願劫數，如千百億恒河沙。

「世尊！我觀未來及現在眾生，於所住處，於南方清潔之地，以土石竹木，作其龕室。是中能塑畫，乃至金銀銅鐵，作地藏形像，燒香供養，瞻禮讚歎。是人居處，即得十種利益。何等為十？一者，土地豐壤。二者，家宅永安。三者，先亡生天。四者，現存益壽。五者，所求遂意。六者，無水火災。七者，虛耗辟除。八者，杜絕惡夢。九者，出入神護。十者，多遇聖因。「世尊！未來世中，及現在眾生，若能於所住處方面，作如是供養，得如是利益。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the earth-spirit Dṛḍha said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, since long ago I have gazed upon and bowed before measureless bodhisattva-mahāsattvas, every one of them possessed of great, inconceivable spiritual powers and wisdom, broadly liberating beings. This Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva's vow, among all bodhisattvas, is especially profound and weighty. World-Honored One, Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva has a great karmic affinity with Jambudvīpa. Even Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Avalokiteśvara, and Maitreya, who likewise transform into hundreds of thousands of bodily forms to liberate beings throughout the six destinies, still have vows that reach an end. But Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva's vow, made to teach and transform every being of the six destinies, spans as many kalpas as the sands of a thousand hundred million Ganges rivers.

"World-Honored One, I observe that if beings, present and future, in the place where they dwell, on a clean patch of ground to the south, build a shrine of earth, stone, bamboo, or wood, and within it paint or sculpt an image of Kṣitigarbha — even one made of gold, silver, bronze, or iron — and burn incense in offering to it, gazing on it and bowing, praising it in worship, then that person's dwelling will gain ten kinds of benefit. What are these ten? First, the land will be fertile and abundant. Second, the household will be forever at peace. Third, those who have already died will be reborn in the heavens. Fourth, those still living will have their lives extended. Fifth, whatever they seek will come to pass. Sixth, there will be no disaster by water or fire. Seventh, every waste and loss will be warded off. Eighth, nightmares will cease entirely. Ninth, spirits will guard their every coming and going. Tenth, they will often encounter the causes of holiness.

"World-Honored One, if beings present and future can, wherever they dwell, make offerings such as these, they will gain benefits such as these."

Reading note — after Lingcheng

Lingcheng addresses a question this passage never asks outright but that a reader coming from other traditions might: why is the earth itself gendered female in Buddhist cosmology? He answers with a citation from the Longer Āgama: when the Buddha first taught the Four Great Spirits — of earth, water, wind, and fire — the earth-spirit initially insisted no water, fire, or wind existed within earth at all, and, corrected and taught the four noble truths in turn, became the first of the four to attain the pure Dharma-eye and take the lay precepts, "and thus," Lingcheng writes, simply reporting the source rather than arguing the point himself, "it is known that this is a female spirit" — the water, fire, and wind spirits, taught the same way immediately afterward in the same text, are recorded the same way. On the instruction to build the shrine specifically on clean ground to the south, he reads it through the compass symbolism this commentary has used since Part 1's cosmology of hell to the east: south corresponds to fire and to clarity, "so that a person may rise from the buried, shadowed path into the bright, sunlit ground" — a spatial echo of the sūtra's own governing movement from hell toward light.

Protection for Anyone Who Reads This Sūtra

Chinese Source

復白佛言：「世尊！未來世中，若有善男子、善女人，於所住處，有此經典及菩薩像，是人更能轉讀經典，供養菩薩。我常日夜，以本神力，衛護是人，乃至水火盜賊，大橫小橫，一切惡事，悉皆銷滅。」
佛告堅牢地神：「汝大神力，諸神少及。何以故？閻浮土地，悉蒙汝護。乃至草木沙石，稻麻竹葦，穀米寶貝，從地而有，皆因汝力。又常稱揚地藏菩薩利益之事，汝之功德，及以神通，百千倍於常分地神。若未來世中，有善男子、善女人，供養菩薩，及轉讀是經，但依地藏本願經一事修行者，汝以本神力，而擁護之。勿令一切災害，及不如意事，輒聞於耳，何況令受？非但汝獨護是人故，亦有釋梵眷屬、諸天眷屬，擁護是人。何故得如是聖賢擁護？皆由瞻禮地藏形像，及轉讀是本願經故。自然畢竟出離苦海，證涅槃樂。以是之故，得大擁護。」

Ru-Yi Translation

She said again to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, if in ages to come there are good sons or good daughters who, in the place where they dwell, keep this sūtra and an image of the bodhisattva, and who, further, recite this sūtra aloud and make offerings to the bodhisattva, I will, day and night, by my own spiritual power, guard and protect that person, so that flood, fire, thieves, every great and small mishap, and every evil thing, will be entirely dissolved."

The Buddha said to the earth-spirit Dṛḍha: "Your great spiritual power is something few other spirits can match. Why is this? Because the very earth of Jambudvīpa is entirely under your protection — even its grass and trees, sand and stone, rice and hemp, bamboo and reeds, grain and treasure, everything that comes from the earth, exists through your power. And you constantly extol Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva's work of benefit — your merit, and your spiritual power, is a hundred thousand times that of an ordinary earth-spirit. If in ages to come there are good sons or good daughters who make offerings to the bodhisattva, and recite this sūtra aloud, and simply practice according to this one thing, the Sūtra of Kṣitigarbha's Fundamental Vows, you should guard and protect them with your own spiritual power, and not let any disaster, or anything unwanted, so much as reach their ears — how much less befall them. Nor are you the only one who guards such a person — the retinues of Brahmā and Śakra, and the retinues of every heaven, will guard them as well. Why do they gain the protection of such holy and worthy beings? It is entirely because of gazing on and bowing before an image of Kṣitigarbha, and reciting this Sūtra of Fundamental Vows, that they will naturally, in the end, escape the sea of suffering, and realize the bliss of nirvāṇa. It is for this reason that they gain such great protection."

Reading note — after Lingcheng

Lingcheng reads the Buddha's answer to Dṛḍha as doing something structurally unusual for this sūtra: rather than promising a reward for a devotional act, it explains why the promise is credible in the first place. Dṛḍha's own power already runs beneath every field, every grain of rice, every scrap of building material in Jambudvīpa — so her promise of protection is not a supernatural favor layered on top of ordinary reality, but a claim that ordinary reality, understood rightly, was always working this way. He connects this back to the sūtra's opening chapters in Part 1, where earth-spirits and grain-spirits are named among the very first beings gathering at Trāyastriṃśa to hear this teaching, and reads the closing formula — "guarded by the retinues of Brahmā and Śakra" as well — as marking a threshold within the sūtra itself: from this point forward, protection is no longer described only as coming from Kṣitigarbha directly, but from the entire cosmology of spirits and deities this text has spent four chapters cataloguing by name, now acting in concert around anyone who keeps it.

Part 5 Complete

This closes Part 5: the Buddha's own comparison of what different kinds of giving are worth, from a coin pressed into a beggar's hand to the dedication of merit to the entire Dharma-realm, and the earth-spirit Dṛḍha's testimony that her own vast power has always run in service of exactly this teaching. Part 6 turns to the sūtra's final two chapters: Avalokiteśvara's own question about what seeing, hearing, and even briefly touching this teaching is worth to ordinary people in ordinary trouble, and the Buddha's last, direct entrustment of every being not yet free to Kṣitigarbha's care.
