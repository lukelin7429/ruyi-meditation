The Last Five Calamities and the Three Poisons

Part 2 is the second fascicle of Siming Zhili's Notes on the Commentary on the Meaning of Guanyin (T1729). It follows Zhiyi's Commentary through the rest of the seven calamities — rākṣasas, swords and staffs, ghosts, shackles and chains, enemies and bandits — and then into the capacity of mind, the sūtra's promise that those "much given to lustful desire," anger, or folly become free by constantly holding Avalokiteśvara in mind. As in Part 1, most of the text is an outline that divides the Commentary sentence by sentence, with Zhili's explanations set between the headings. His longer additions include the story of the bhikṣu Saṃgharakṣita and the sea-mountain hells, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra parable of the four snakes, the distinction between karma and retribution in one's own family, the four practices that "meet bandits," and the stories of Bao Si and of the Great Treatise's dove. The center of the fascicle is his defense of the claim that mindfulness is itself wisdom, set out in six phrases and in four phrases of "forgetting" and "illuminating." The fascicle ends with the three poisons read "in the contrary way" as three Dharma-gates — taking up, letting go, and neither — which Zhili says cannot be understood without the doctrine of inherent evil.

Lemmas. Lemmas reuse this round's draft of the Commentary (`guanyin-yishu/p1_body.md` for the calamities, `p2_body.md` for the three poisons). The sūtra's 婬欲／瞋恚／愚癡 follow the site's Lotus ("lustful desire," "anger," "folly"); the Commentary's single terms 貪／瞋／癡 stay "greed / anger / delusion."

Variant lemmas. A few lemmas do not match the Commentary text exactly as we have it: 「今入」 (unit 2; the Commentary has 令入), 「論其」 (unit 5), and "Know that a single mote of dust is complete with the three truths" (unit 3), which is not in the received Commentary. Zhili's copy of the Commentary evidently differed in places.

Saṃgharakṣita (unit 2). The story of the bhikṣu who saw monks suffering in hells disguised as monasteries is from the Sūtra on the Causes and Conditions of Saṃgharakṣita (T749, located). The Commentary draft writes the title as "Sengzhu"; 僧護 is Senghu.

Great Sūtra (unit 3). The four snakes in a basket, five outcastes, the false friend, the empty village, the six thieves, and the river are from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T374/T375); the wording follows the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, Part 23, abridged as Zhili abridges.

Zengzi (unit 5). "When a bird is about to die…" is Analects 8.4. Zhili's "commandant of justice" (廷尉) glosses the Commentary's story of Zhang Chang. King Śubhavyūha (Lotus, ch. 27) is named as on the site.

Vimalakīrti (units 5, 6). Bright Appearance Bodhisattva's words follow the site's chapter "Entering the Gate of Nonduality" ("the element of emptiness"; the Commentary draft says "space element," kept in Zhili's own sentences). The goddess and the flowers follow the site's chapter "The Goddess and the Flowers" (Zhili's 觀眾生品, "Contemplating Beings"). "That commentary" is Zhiyi's commentary on the Vimalakīrti; source not located in the vault.

Bao Si (unit 6). Abridged from the Shiji, "Basic Annals of Zhou." Shupojia and Kapilavastu are referred to Zhili's Notes on the Profound [Meaning] (T1727).

The poisons named (unit 6). "Loving oneself is desire, loving others is lust…" is attributed to "the commentary on the great text"; source not located. The Chinese pairs 恚／瞋 and 愚／癡 are kept in parentheses because English has no matching pairs.

Hundred bright dharmas (unit 6). "On the first ground one attains the gate of the hundred bright dharmas" is found in Amoghavajra's Humane Kings (T246), not in Kumārajīva's (T245). The Treatise on the Ten Grounds (T1522) sentence was not located in this wording.

Great Treatise (units 7, 8). The dove that breaks the śālmali branch is in T1509 (located), around fascicle 26–27, not the thirtieth as Zhili says. "In the śrāvaka sūtras it is called habitual residue; in the Mahāyāna, the main fetters" was not located in T1509 in this wording.

Larger Prajñā (unit 8). Subhūti's answer is in T223 (located) as "because the own-nature of prajñāpāramitā cannot be obtained, it does not accept"; Zhili's wording differs. The fifth non-acceptance repeats the second (不受亦不受) in the text; translated as written.

Non-Action Sūtra (unit 8). Mañjuśrī as "Greed-Śrī, Anger-Śrī, Folly-Śrī" is from the Zhufa wuxing jing (T650, located). The pun on 外道 ("outside the path") is kept in English.

Six phrases and four phrases (unit 6). 念 is "mindfulness," 想 "perception," 觀 "contemplation," 智 "wisdom" (慧 also "wisdom," as in the Commentary draft). 忘／照 are "forget / illuminate"; 雙非／雙亦 "double negation / double affirmation"; 雙遮中／雙照中 "the middle of double negation / of double illumination."

Obscure readings. Unit 2 證風非證難 is read as 證風非正難 ("not a calamity proper"). Unit 2 而示欲於六種明別圓觀: alternatively, "and it shows the desire [element] among the six…." Unit 8 德過一際 ("a virtue beyond a single limit") and 三相圓觀 ("the perfect contemplation of the three marks") are translated literally.
