Akṣayamati's Question, the General Answer, and the Calamities of Fire and Water

Notes on the Commentary on the Meaning of Guanyin (T1729) is Siming Zhili's (960–1028) sub-commentary on Zhiyi's Commentary on the Meaning of Guanyin (T1728), itself a phrase-by-phrase reading of the Universal Gate chapter of the Lotus Sūtra. Part 1 is Zhili's first fascicle. It follows the Commentary's first fascicle from the title to the calamity of water. Most of the text is a running outline ("in two: first … second, from '…' on …"), which tells the reader how each sentence of the Commentary is to be divided; between the outline headings Zhili adds his own explanations. Several are substantial: the name Akṣayamati read through the empty, the provisional, and the middle, ending with the claim that cultivated evil is wholly inherent evil; the phenomenal and principle readings of the "single mind"; why the seven calamities stand for the six elements; why retribution belongs in a contemplative reading at all (units 8–9); the three paths of afflictions, karma, and suffering in each land; the phenomenal dhyānas that bear the name "fire" or "water"; and a short exchange on whether Sudhana's fire-mountain gate is distinct or perfect. He also tells the full story of Fan Ju to correct a slip in the Commentary.

Second, from "Again, the Vimalakīrti" on, the Vimalakīrti clarifies the provisional in terms of the two truths. The conditioned is the conventional, a dharma that can be exhausted; the unconditioned is the real, a dharma that cannot be exhausted. The wisdom of the Lesser Vehicle is shallow: it exhausts the conditioned and dwells in the unconditioned, and so returns to ashes and annihilation. Entering the empty in the four teachings, leaving the flood of existence, is equal to [leaving] the two floods of views and desire. "In the provisional, within the flood of existence, neither stained nor wet": the provisional, though [coming from] that emptiness, also does not attach to existence, because of the twofold flow. One should know that as the provisional becomes manifest, emptiness too becomes all the more evident; this is called the contemplation of equality, and the meaning lies in this. The middle breaks ignorance, as is usually explained.

Reading Notes for Fascicle 1

Translator's notes

Lemmas. Every quoted lemma ("from '…' on," 「……」下) reuses the English of this round's draft of the Commentary on the Meaning of Guanyin (`guanyin-yishu/p1_body.md`). Quotations of the Universal Gate chapter follow the site's Lotus Sūtra ("Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate"): "at once perceives the sound of their voices, and every one of them attains liberation," "every kind of suffering and affliction."

Outline formula. 二：初、…… is "in two: first, …"; 「X」下 is "from 'X' on." These outline phrases are Zhili's divisions of the Commentary, not of the sūtra.

Dharmakṣema and Juqu Mengxun (unit 2). Zhili repeats the tradition that the Universal Gate chapter circulated as a separate sūtra because Dharmakṣema (讖師) recommended it to the Northern Liang ruler Juqu Mengxun during an illness.

The two lands (unit 3). CBETA punctuates 二、土者; the sense requires 「二土」者, glossing the Commentary's "the bodhisattvas of the two lands": the land Adorned with Pure Light (Gadgadasvara's eighty-four thousand) and this land (Padmaśrī and the forty-two thousand sons of the gods, as in the site's Lotus, "Gadgadasvara").

Record of Rites (unit 5). "Summary of the Rules of Propriety" (曲禮上), with Zheng Xuan's note; not a Buddhist source. The Fan Ju story is abridged from the Shiji, biography of Fan Ju and Cai Ze. Zhili corrects the Commentary's "Zhang Yi" to "Zhang Lu"; the text writes 范睢 for 范雎 (Fan Ju).

Avataṃsaka (unit 4). The "new sūtra" is the eighty-fascicle version (T279); the chapter "The Ten Inexhaustible Treasuries" (十無盡藏品, ch. 22) begins in fascicle 21 of the Taishō, not 20 as Zhili says. Zhili abridges the passage.

Wondrous Profound Meaning (unit 6). "From the icchantika's first arising of a mind of repentance…" is from T1716 (located), worded after the site's Fahua Xuanyi, which has "equal enlightenment"; "equal awakening" follows the BRIEF. The reference "第四十，益在第六" is read as 第四，十益在第六: the king samādhi is in fascicle 4 of T1716 and the ten benefits in fascicle 6 (both located). Alternatively, following CBETA's punctuation: "in the fortieth … the benefits in the sixth," which does not fit the work.

Commentary on the great text (unit 6). 大本疏 is taken as the Textual Commentary on the Lotus, where 如是我聞 is explained with the four phrases "hearing hearing … not-hearing not-hearing" from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra.

Great Treatise (unit 7). "Breathing in and out is bodily formation; initial and sustained thought is verbal formation…": not located in T1509 in this wording; the same line appears in T1726 (Profound Meaning of Guanyin).

Fuxing (unit 10). The three paths in each of the three lands are quoted from Zhanran's Zhiguan fuxing zhuanhong jue (T1912, located).

Great Sūtra (unit 16). The float-bag simile is Zhili's abridgment of T374/T375; the wording follows the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, Part 11 ("floating bag," "a single mote of dust," "offense requiring temporary suspension / forfeiture," "simple expiation," "wrongful conduct"). The Commentary's own phrase keeps "float-bag." "Loving-kindness that is existent, nonexistent…" is in T375 (located).

Other sources. Master Zhao's "He puts on the armor at the beginning of the mind…" is from the Zhaolun (T1858, located). The Annotations passage on Sudhana (unit 15) paraphrases Zhanran's Shiqian (T1717); "the subtle ignorance that obstructs the middle" is located there. Maitrakanyaka (慈童女): Zabao zang jing (T203). Shupojia's lust: told in T1916 and T1728; the Great Treatise version was not located in the vault's T1509 file.

Readings. Unit 6 「聞有」下，四、聞三慧俱能感 is read as 四聞、三慧俱能感 ("the four kinds of hearing and the three wisdoms"). Unit 7 亦為所觀二識 is read as 亦為所觀。二、識 ("it too is what is contemplated. Second, consciousness"). Unit 11 三內無色相內觀色少 has 內觀 where the pattern requires 外觀 ("seeing forms outside"); translated as written.

Obscure phrases. Unit 5 權實䐇然: the character 䐇 is rare; translated "fused together" by context. Unit 10 直就果報地上清涼: alternatively, "merely in terms of retribution on the ground above [the hells], [making the fire] cool." 因華已去 ("from the flower of causes on") seems to mean the sets from cultivated causes onward. 四心流動 ("the four [kinds of] mind flowing") is unclear; the Commentary has "the four inversions" at this point.

Inherent evil (unit 4). "Cultivated evil is in its whole essence inherent evil" (修惡全體是性惡) is Zhili's own gloss on the Larger Prajñāpāramitā line; the terms follow the site's Profound Meaning of Guanyin.
