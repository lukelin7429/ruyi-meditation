The Bodies, the Offering, and the Verses (with Zunshi's Explaining the Verses)

Part 4 is the fourth and last fascicle of Siming Zhili's Notes on the Commentary on the Meaning of Guanyin (T1729), followed by an appendix by a different author. Zhili's fascicle (units 0–10) finishes the Commentary on the second question: the small capacities ripened through the five flavors, the names and essence of the lands, the bodhisattva, pratyekabuddha, and śrāvaka bodies, the gods, humans, fourfold assembly, and eight classes, the general answer, Akṣayamati's necklace as a "gift of the Dharma," and Dharaṇiṃdhara's praise. Because the chapter's verse section was translated later by Jñānagupta and Zhiyi did not comment on it, Zhili adds a brief explanation of the verses that follows the outline of "Dharma master Shi of Tianzhu Monastery," that is, Ciyun Zunshi. The appendix (units 11–24) is Zunshi's own short commentary, Explaining the Verses (〈釋重頌〉), which reads the twenty-six stanzas as a restatement of the two prose questions and answers, followed by a colophon of 1302 by the Yuan monk Xingcheng, who recovered Zunshi's text in the north and had it printed. The chapter's verses are quoted throughout in the site's Lotus English.

二、「若小」下，小機益相，四：初、酪益，二：初、明小機應。即是小種先熟之者，初感劣應，始從入胎至于成佛其相皆劣。拘隣，或隣兒、或憍陳如，此五人首也。其四人者，即阿鞞、跋提、摩訶男、拘利。太子初於鹿園證四諦理，名得甘露，此乃佛日次照幽谷。二、「既非」下，對大甄揀，二：初、進對《法華》揀悟。初教得道雖曰甘露，既非第五醍醐之味，豈得度於二種生死？故未名得度。故云等者，引此經也。但用一門解脫虛妄見思之縛，其實未得一切境界解脫塵沙無明惑累，其至靈山方證斯脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "If people of small capacity appeal" on, the marks of benefit for small capacities, in four: first, the benefit of cream, in two: first, clarifying the response to small capacities. These are those whose small seeds ripened first: they first appeal to the inferior response, and from entering the womb to attaining buddhahood its marks are all inferior. Kauṇḍinya (拘隣), also called Linyi (隣兒) or Kauṇḍinya (憍陳如), is the head of these five; the other four are Aśvajit (阿鞞), Bhadrika (跋提), Mahānāman (摩訶男), and Kulika (拘利). The prince first realized the principle of the four truths in the Deer Park; this is called obtaining the sweet dew. This is the buddha-sun next shining on the deep valleys. Second, from "Since it is not ghee" on, sifting by contrast with the great, in two: first, going forward to sift their awakening against the Lotus. Attaining the path by the first teaching, though called sweet dew, is not the fifth flavor, ghee; how could it bring them across the two kinds of birth and death? Hence they are "not yet called delivered." "Hence it says" and so on cites this sūtra. With only one gate they are liberated from the bondage of false views and thought; "in truth" they have "not yet attained" liberation from the delusive burdens of dust-and-sand and ignorance in all realms; only when they reach Vulture Peak do they realize this liberation.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Not yet able to bear the great teaching" on, going back to the Avataṃsaka to discuss capacities, in two: first, in relation to the great it is called milk. Here it uses [their] not hearing when they met the great after realizing the small to verify that, as ordinary capacities, they did not receive the great; for the text on "deaf and mute" is in the latter part of the sūtra. That period is still long; in meaning it corresponds to the time of the Vaipulya and the Prajñā, and it can also extend to before the Deer Park. Therefore Kāśyapa, recounting how the small capacities, receiving the great at the time of testing [what was suitable], "fainted, falling to the ground," uses the later to show the earlier: the meaning that the capacities could not yet bear the great is plain. Though there was hidden benefit, views and craving were blazing and active; hence the capacities at the Avataṃsaka were wholly raw, like milk. Second, from "When they hear the expedient teaching" on, in relation to the small it is called cream. When chased urgently and [about to be] given the wealth, [the son] cried out loudly that he was wronged; when spoken to gently [of] clearing away dung, he followed gladly. So [the Buddha] gave skillful means and taught the suffering of the three realms; fearing suffering, they severed the accumulation of views and thought, and having changed from ordinary to holy, this is called the milk turning into cream.

Ru-Yi Translation

[Second,] from "Next they hear the four kinds of four truths of the Vaipulya" on, curdled butter. The four teachings are all expounded, horizontally gathering the many capacities; the small, hearing rebuke and reproach, gradually come to long for the great and secretly obtain the benefit of the shared [teaching]; bodhisattvas of dull faculties benefit the same as the two vehicles; taming such capacities yields the flavor of curdled butter. One should know that explaining the five flavors in terms of the teachings does not take thick or thin, but speaks only of their arising from one another, because the sudden milk is ghee. In terms of capacities there is the meaning of thick and thin; but this is said of the dullest of the three vehicles: for this one kind there was no manifest benefit at all in the Avataṃsaka, like milk still rank with blood; when the Tripiṭaka was taught these capacities became cream, growing gradually thicker in sequence up to the ultimate flavor. [Third,] from "Next they hear the three kinds of four truths of the Prajñā" on, clarified butter. It does not speak of the Tripiṭaka but fully shows the three [teachings] of the Mahāyāna (衍); many people of sharp faculties enter the perfect, and the śrāvakas at this point are given the added [task of] turning over the teaching. Since within true emptiness it speaks fully of the myriad practices, it lets the dull secretly obtain the benefit of the distinct; in terms of taming gradual capacities it is called the flavor of clarified butter.

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, from "Next they hear the Lotus" on, ghee, in two: first, the Lotus, in two: first, clarifying that the three vehicles all attain buddhahood. Setting aside the skillful-means four truths of the three earlier teachings, it teaches only the one real, unsurpassed path; and further it opens the gates of skillful means of the three teachings as all the true reality of the One Vehicle: these are this sūtra's two [readings of] "wondrous," relative and absolute. Because it speaks of this wondrousness, it makes the scorched grain of the two vehicles grow again and the provisional doubts of the bodhisattvas of the three teachings cease forever; therefore there is not one who does not attain buddhahood. Second, from "hence it says" on, verifying that the whole career all enters ghee. If great capacities ripened first, at the first seeing in the Avataṃsaka they entered the buddha-wisdom; if small capacities ripened first, they had to be led gradually, and now, hearing the opening and the setting aside, they obtain the buddha-wisdom. Obtaining it at first and obtaining it now are both the buddha-wisdom, both likened to ghee; but that included the distinct, while this is purely perfect.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "If there are further those of dull faculties" on, the Nirvāṇa. The intent of opening and revealing is fully displayed in the Lotus; the great battle formation of capacities clinging to the provisional has already been broken, and the Nirvāṇa is further needed to gather in the remnants. Hence after the Lotus it again speaks of prajñā, taming and ripening their minds so that in the Nirvāṇa they obtain the flavor of ghee. Therefore that sūtra, taking the latter part of the Prajñā corpus, sums up the sequence of the five flavors, saying: "From the Mahāprajñā comes the Great Nirvāṇa." "Teaches the superior three cultivations": that sūtra explains three kinds of threefold cultivation: first, wrong; second, inferior; third, superior. The wrong is the permanence, bliss, and self taught by the wrong teachers of the world. The inferior relies on the Buddha's half teaching to break wrong attachment, namely impermanence, no bliss, and no self. The superior relies on the Buddha's superior teaching to break inferior cultivation, namely permanence, bliss, and self: the Dharma-body is permanent and constant, without change; roaming among the flowers of awakening, it enjoys bliss; it has the eight sovereignties that none can obstruct. One who cultivates thus enters the secret treasury; this is called the superior threefold cultivation.

Second, from "Here one should explain the loving-kindness and compassion arising from the original" on, the loving-kindness and compassion of the original contemplation. As explained above, he extends forms in the three lands and transforms beings in the Five Periods; the defiled points to Śākyamuni and the pure is in terms of Amitābha; the texts of the teaching fully display the work of transformation of the two buddhas, to show that Avalokiteśvara's manifesting the buddha-body is no different from this, because [the stages of] partial truth and the ultimate are the same in essence and function. 三、「問經」下，簡土名體，二、初辯土名，二：初、問。娑婆之名翻為堪忍，於同居中尚不通淨，那得具約三土釋邪？二、答。菩薩舉一以為問端，如來稱法周遍為答，故云以種種形遊諸國土。橫亘十方竪徹三土，故言諸也，皆是觀音應身遊處。此約如來答過於問，據文釋也。若更約義，其相宛然。何者？經示方便及實報土不離娑婆，故云「若聞長壽深心信解，則為見佛常在耆闍崛山，共大菩薩諸聲聞眾圍繞說法。」既云常在耆山，則劫火洞然此土安隱。復以菩薩共諸聲聞而為聽眾，豈非娑婆即方便土？復云「又見娑婆世界其地瑠璃，乃至樓觀皆悉寶成，其菩薩眾咸處其中。」既云又見，即非前處唯有菩薩不共聲聞，即純菩薩而為僧也。驗知娑婆即是實報。此文皆是四信妙觀，即於堪忍而見二土。觀音深智遊於娑婆，豈容獨應同居穢邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "Question: The sūtra only says" on, sifting the names and essence of the lands, in two: first, discussing the names of the lands, in two: first, the question. The name sahā translates as "endurance"; within [the land of] dwelling together it does not even extend to the pure; how can it be explained in terms of all three lands? Second, the answer. The bodhisattva raised one [land] as the starting point of his question; the Tathāgata answered universally in accord with the Dharma; hence he says "journeying through every land in every kind of form." Horizontally spanning the ten directions and vertically penetrating the three lands, hence it says "every"; all are places where Avalokiteśvara's responsive body journeys. This is in terms of the Tathāgata's answer exceeding the question, explaining by the text. If further in terms of meaning, its marks are plain. Why? The sūtra shows that the land of expedients and the land of true reward are not apart from the sahā; hence it says: "[Should one,] hearing of the long lifespan, believe and understand it with a deep heart, it is as if they see the Buddha dwelling always on Vulture Peak, surrounded by great bodhisattvas and the assembly of śrāvakas, teaching the Dharma." Since it says "dwelling always on Vulture Peak," then when the fire of the eon blazes, this land is safe and at peace; further, since bodhisattvas together with śrāvakas form the audience, is this not the sahā being the land of expedients? It further says: "They see, too, this very sahā world, its ground made of lapis lazuli . every terrace and tower made entirely of jewels, and the assembly of bodhisattvas dwelling everywhere within it." Since it says "see, too," it is not the former place; there are only bodhisattvas, not together with śrāvakas: that is, purely bodhisattvas form the saṅgha. From this we verify that the sahā is itself [the land of] true reward. These texts are all the wondrous contemplation of the four [kinds of] faith, seeing the two lands right within [the land of] endurance. Avalokiteśvara journeys through the sahā with deep wisdom; how could he respond only to the defiled [land] of dwelling together?

二、「問二」下，明土體，二：初：問。《大論》云「出三界外有淨土，聲聞辟支佛出生其中，受法性身非分段生」，即方便土也。《大品》云「法身佛為法性身菩薩說法」，其聽法眾非生死人，但云菩薩不共二乘，即實報土也。二土不同皆稱法性，云何分別？二、答。小乘灰斷無界外生，論云出界復云受身，此據大說。大乘法性體本常住，即是一切色心之源。何者？小謂色心因見思有，故因縛斷，其果永忘。大說色心因惑生滅，不因惑有，體是法性，見思若盡，無明全在，則當真諦法性色心。方便生滅無明分破，本性分顯，義當中道法性色心。實報生滅，無明究盡，則復本性常住色心離生滅相，常寂光也。今明方便及實報土，法性名同，約斷惑論，真中大異。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Question: Both lands are called the Dharma-nature" on, clarifying the essence of the lands, in two: first, the question. The Great Treatise says: "Outside the three realms there are pure lands, in which śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas are born, receiving bodies of the Dharma-nature, not segmented birth": this is the land of expedients. The Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] says: "The Dharma-body buddha teaches the Dharma for bodhisattvas with bodies of the Dharma-nature": its audience are not people of birth and death, and it speaks only of bodhisattvas, not together with the two vehicles: this is the land of true reward. The two lands differ yet both are called the Dharma-nature; how are they distinguished? Second, the answer. In the Lesser Vehicle [the body] is reduced to ashes and annihilated, and there is no birth outside the realms; that the treatise says "outside the realms" and also "receiving bodies" is said on the basis of the Great [Vehicle]. In the Great Vehicle the essence of the Dharma-nature is originally eternally abiding; it is the source of all form and mind. Why? The Lesser [Vehicle] says that form and mind exist because of views and thought; therefore, when the cause, the bondage, is severed, the fruit is forever gone. The Great [Vehicle] says that form and mind arise and cease because of delusion but do not exist because of delusion; their essence is the Dharma-nature. If views and thought are exhausted while ignorance is wholly present, this corresponds to form and mind of the Dharma-nature of the real truth. When the arising and ceasing of [the land of] expedients [ends], ignorance is partially broken and the original nature partially manifest; in meaning this corresponds to form and mind of the Dharma-nature of the middle way. When the arising and ceasing of true reward [ends], ignorance is completely exhausted, and one returns to the original nature, the eternally abiding form and mind free of the marks of arising and ceasing: this is eternal tranquil light. Here it clarifies that the lands of expedients and of true reward share the name Dharma-nature, but discussed in terms of the severing of delusion, the real [truth] and the middle differ greatly.

Discussed horizontally, there are the four teachings; vertically, the three lands. If discussed in detail, within the sudden there are distinct [people] who are dull, and within the gradual perfect [people] who are sharp. The dharmas taught rise and fall with the capacities, and the bodhisattvas assisting the Buddha change along with them; the text cannot give everything, and one should consider it according to the teachings. Here it says "teaches the Dharma," so it is speaking of those who in the Buddha's time received the teaching of causes and conditions. The Śūraṅgama's fourteen kinds of fearlessness (T945); the Continued Biographies of Eminent Monks on Jñānagupta; the Records of Spiritual Responses (Daoxuan's conversations with a god) on Kumārajīva omitting the verses; Zhiyi's "divine medicine of the gods that prevents death"; the Commentary on the Larger Version on the ten virtues of elders (a parallel list is in the Fahua wenju); the Nirvāṇa's three kinds of threefold cultivation; the old monk of Donglin; the four creatures (dove, snake …) each naming the worst suffering.

Terms. 超頌／加頌／追頌 are "restating out of order / adding in the verses / restating retrospectively." 敬田／恩田／悲田 "field of reverence / of gratitude / of compassion." 相待妙／絕待妙 follows the glossary ("relative / absolute [reading of 'wondrous']"). 一切智／道種智 follow the glossary. 宗 is "cardinal doctrine" (glossary, five profound meanings).

Numbering and copying. Unit 2 numbers the pratyekabuddha "second" where "third" is expected; translated as written. Unit 2's "curdled butter" and "clarified butter" lack their numbers; supplied in brackets. Unit 16 has 金山 for the verse's 金剛山. Unit 22's 三無緣 may be an error for 三無失 ("three non-failings"). Unit 8's 疑兕 ("rhinoceros of doubt") may be an error for 疑兆.

Obscure readings. Unit 14's quoted 「依本觀慈悲，汝聽觀音行」 is CBETA punctuation; the first four characters are read as Zunshi's description ("one stanza based on the loving-kindness and compassion of the original contemplation") and only the second half as the verse. Unit 23's last sentence is repunctuated (身業勤則增長福德供養應身；口業勤…) to match the three bodies. In the colophon, 大德 is read as the era name Dade (1302), not "great worthies"; 興元住山 is translated literally ("dwelling on the mountain at Xingyuan").
