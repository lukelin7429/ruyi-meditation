The Fourth and Fifth Principles: Sections, Objects, and Modes; Twenty Doubts on Mind and Object

This part completes the first fascicle with the fourth and fifth principles. The fourth principle explains why the Mohe Zhiguan is laid out in tens. Zhanran first shows how its ten major sections could be opened or combined differently — set beside the fivefold profound meaning of the Profound Meaning, they collapse into five — and why ten is kept. He then takes the ten objects of contemplation, showing that they could be reduced to nine, eight, or even one, but that their different ways of arising justify ten. For the ten modes of contemplation he gives five intents for each mode, then ten general points and four particular points for each mode that set the Tiantai reading apart from other schools: buddha-nature in the insentient, the three causes as inherent virtue, inherent evil not severed in the Buddha, the six identities, and the five repentances as the entry to the perfect stages. The fifth principle is a series of twenty questions and answers on passages about mind and object: the four phases of a thought, how colors can be the dharma-realm, why great compassion comes first, how "only believe in the Dharma-nature" should be read, how an object can illuminate wisdom, and how the three contemplations relate to an earlier Chinese lineage. It ends with a verse from Zhiyi's Oral Decisions and Zhanran's comment on it.

The Fourth Principle: The Ten Major Sections

Chinese Source

第四大章總別例者，十章、十境、十乘，離合、同異、立意等別。初十立大章。言大章者，準分別中十門不同，具如大意，與九而辨同異。初釋大意，既云五略，對下即是廣略二門。若爾，亦可「釋名」已去更開十章，如《法華玄》釋「十妙」文，今此為對，旨歸非廣非略，文勢便故，故以「大意」共為十章。《禪門》亦爾，若欲開者則開顯體，出眼智教相，則中間八章自離為十。如「五重玄義」亦是合於中八而為五章，合攝法入體相中，義當顯體，合方便、正觀、果報共為一宗。起教，義當於用；偏圓，義當判教。彼為釋經，此為成觀，故有少別爾。今存十數，故離五為八，更加初後，則數整足。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Fourth Principle, the General and the Particular in the Major Sections: the ten sections, the ten objects, and the ten modes differ in their separating and combining, their sameness and difference, and the intent of their establishment. First, the ten established major sections. As for "major sections": according to the ten different gates in the [passage on] distinguishing, as fully [set out] in "the great intent," [each] is distinguished as the same as and different from the [other] nine. First, explaining "the great intent": since it is called the five brief points, in relation to what follows these are the two gates of the extensive and the brief. If so, one could also open ten further sections from "explaining the name" on, like the passage explaining "the ten wondrous" in the Profound [Meaning] of the Lotus; here, for the sake of correspondence — "where it ultimately leads" being neither extensive nor brief — and for the convenience of the momentum of the text, "the great intent" is included to make up ten sections. The Dharma Gate of Dhyāna is also like this. If one wished to open them, one would open "revealing the essence," bringing out "the eye and wisdom" and "the features of the teachings," and then the eight middle sections would of themselves separate into ten. The "fivefold profound meaning" likewise combines the middle eight into five sections: "gathering in the teachings" is combined into "the essence and its marks," and in meaning corresponds to "revealing the essence"; "the preliminary expedients," "the actual practice," and "fruits and rewards" are combined together as one "cardinal doctrine"; "the teachings that arise" in meaning corresponds to "function"; "partial and complete" in meaning corresponds to "classifying the teachings." That [work] is for explaining a sūtra, this is for completing contemplation; hence there is a slight difference. Now, to keep the number ten, the five are separated into eight, and with the first and the last added, the number is complete.

The Ten Objects: Separating and Combining

Chinese Source

十境離合者，互發正意只有九雙，亦為成十數故加三障四魔一雙，故此十境若非三障即是四魔。又十境者，亦是為成十數故具示爾。故下章安問云：「法若塵沙，境何定十」？今於十內若更合者，二乘、菩薩但合為一方便境則但有九，若以煩惱入於六蔽習因相中則但有八，若以煩惱及業并陰入境為三道境則但有七，或依前八復以慢入煩惱境中亦但成七，若以見慢入世禪攝則但有六，又以病患入陰入攝則但有五，四三者如四魔三障，若但以發不發相對則但成二，若但以一所觀為言則但有一。開合雖爾，今明發相，氣類不同，是故為十。以陰對九，陰非發得，是故別立二乘、菩薩，雖同方便，發心異故。煩惱起重，習因輕微，展轉互通，故名為道。此諸發得，三皆現起。慢與煩惱濫別，禪中未必一向發見。病雖如陰，陰不必病；病雖有業，非業相現；病雖有魔，已屬魔境。二及三四發相頗分，況但為一最為通漫，是故隨相必須開十。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the separating and combining of the ten objects: in the correct sense of their mutual arousing, there are only nine pairs; also, to make up the number ten, the pair of the three obstructions and the four māras is added; hence of these ten objects, any that is not the three obstructions is the four māras. Again, the ten objects are also shown in full in order to make up the number ten. Hence below, Zhang'an asks: "Dharmas are like dust and sand; why are the objects fixed at ten?" Now, if within the ten they were further combined: if the two vehicles and the bodhisattva were simply combined as one object of expedients, there would be only nine; if affliction were put into the features of the habitual causes of the six coverings, there would be only eight; if affliction and karma, together with the object of the aggregates and entries, were made the object of the three paths, there would be only seven; or, following the preceding eight, if pride were further put into the object of affliction, there would also be only seven; if views and pride were included under worldly dhyāna, there would be only six; and if illness were further included under the aggregates and entries, there would be only five. Four and three are like the four māras and the three obstructions. If only arising and not arising were paired, there would be only two; if one spoke only of the single object contemplated, there would be only one. Though opening and combining are thus, now, in clarifying the features of their arising, their kinds differ; therefore they are ten. The aggregates are set against the nine because the aggregates are not something that arises [later]. Therefore the two vehicles and the bodhisattva are established separately: though both are expedients, their arousing of the mind differs. Affliction arises heavily, habitual causes are light and subtle, and they pass back and forth into each other; hence they are called paths. In all these that arise, the three all manifestly arise. Pride and affliction are confused [yet] distinct; within dhyāna, views do not necessarily always arise. Illness is like the aggregates, yet the aggregates are not necessarily illness; illness has karma, yet it is not the manifestation of the features of karma; illness has māra, yet that already belongs to the object of māra. The features of arising for two, three, and four are fairly distinct; how much more general and diffuse is merely one! Therefore, following their features, they must be opened into ten.

The Ten Modes: Five Intents for Each

Chinese Source

次明十乘立意者，下文云「橫竪該羅，十觀具足」。今探文意，總為五釋：一者總釋、二者別釋、三者橫竪四句釋、四者附文來意法相釋、五者與他所立永異釋。總別二釋，橫竪四句，具如記中。附文者，十法生起又名為竪，一一法中各含多意又名為橫。生起如文，各有多意，具如本文，隨義別釋。今搜文意覽略知廣，雖有小小多少不同，今於十法各例為五，故立斯十。妙境五者，一為示三千在一念故，二為示極理異後乘故，三為欲開顯思議境故，四為利根者開悟理故，五為下九法作所依故。發心五者，一為解理者仍須願故，二為明發心攝法遍故，三為欲辨異諸偏小故，四為明中根發方悟故，五為下八法作行始故。安心五者，一為明有願仍須行故，二為辨中根難安故，三為示法同隨人異故，四為示凡夫自他安故，五為示開總出別安故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying the intent of establishing the ten modes. The text below says: "Encompassing horizontally and vertically, the ten contemplations are complete." Now, searching out the intent of the text, there are five explanations in all: first, the general explanation; second, the particular explanation; third, the explanation by the four phrases of horizontal and vertical; fourth, the explanation of the intent and dharma features attached to the text; fifth, the explanation [showing that it is] forever different from what others establish. The general and particular explanations and the four phrases of horizontal and vertical are fully as in my Record. As for "attached to the text": the generative order of the ten modes is also called the vertical; that each mode contains many intents is also called the horizontal. The generative order is as in the text; that each has many intents is fully as in the original text, explained separately according to the meaning. Now, searching out the intent of the text, surveying the brief to know the extensive — though there are minor differences of more and less — here five [intents] are set out by the pattern for each of the ten modes; hence these ten are established. The five of the wondrous object: first, to show that the three thousand are in a single thought; second, to show that the ultimate principle differs from the later vehicles; third, to open and reveal the conceivable object; fourth, so that those of sharp faculties awaken to principle; fifth, to serve as the basis for the nine modes below. The five of arousing the mind: first, because those who understand principle still need the vow; second, to clarify that arousing the mind gathers in dharmas universally; third, to distinguish it from the partial and small; fourth, to clarify that those of middling faculties awaken only on arousing [the mind]; fifth, to serve as the beginning of practice for the eight modes below. The five of settling the mind: first, to clarify that having the vow one still needs practice; second, to make clear that those of middling faculties are hard to settle; third, to show that the dharma is the same while persons differ; fourth, to show the settling of ordinary beings, oneself and others; fifth, to show settling by opening the general and bringing out the particular.

Chinese Source

破法遍五者，一為辨此門徧用慧故，二隨用一門橫竪遍故，三為初心者依教門故，四示初心者依無生故，五三諦圓融破方遍故。此中五意，後之三意別在今文，前之二意通在初後。通塞五者，一為示檢校非一節故，二為示橫竪通仍塞故，三為示一心仍有塞故，四為示寶渚是所通故，五兼消經文過五百故。道品五者，一為示須用道品調故，二為示調停異偏小故，三為示念處是陰境故，四為示道品攝諸行故，五為示品後必有門故。正助五者，一為重蔽者必須助故，二為示事度能治蔽故，三為示助道攝法遍故，四為示正助合行相故，五為示三教俱是助故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The five of breaking through every dharma: first, to make clear that this gate uses wisdom universally; second, that following any one gate one is universal horizontally and vertically; third, so that beginners rely on the gates of the teaching; fourth, to show that beginners rely on non-arising; fifth, that only when the three truths are perfectly fused is the breaking through universal. Of these five intents, the latter three are particular to the present text, while the former two are common to the first and the later [teachings]. The five of passage and blockage: first, to show that examination is not a single step; second, to show that passing through horizontally and vertically there is still blockage; third, to show that within the single mind there is still blockage; fourth, to show that the treasure-isle is what one passes through to; fifth, at the same time to interpret the sūtra's text on "passing beyond five hundred." The five of the branches of the path: first, to show that one must use the branches of the path to adjust; second, to show that this adjustment differs from the partial and small; third, to show that the foundations of mindfulness are the object of the aggregates; fourth, to show that the branches of the path gather in all practices; fifth, to show that after the branches there must be the gates. The five of the principal and auxiliary: first, because those with heavy coverings must have the auxiliary; second, to show that the perfections as phenomena can treat the coverings; third, to show that the auxiliary path gathers in dharmas universally; fourth, to show the features of practicing the principal and auxiliary together; fifth, to show that the three teachings are all auxiliary.

Chinese Source

次位五者，一為示妙位使不濫故，二為示妙位德難思故，三為示慕果令思齊故，四為增上慢知非聖故，五為逗眾生宜樂者故。安忍五者，一示內外障須安忍故，二為斥鄙夫擅師位故，三為示行者內外術故，四為示先賢安忍軌故，五為令策進相似位故。離愛五者，一為令離於頂墮位故，二為示大小乘頂墮別故，三為示似愛非真愛故，四為示功用異偏小故，五為令策進入初住故。此一一五並以初文而為正意，下四並是文中兼具，若預了此五，則對文可識。

Ru-Yi Translation

The five of the stages: first, to show the wondrous stages so as to prevent confusion; second, to show that the virtues of the wondrous stages are hard to conceive; third, to show [them] so that those who long for the fruit will think of equaling it; fourth, so that those of overweening pride will know they are not sages; fifth, to accommodate beings who delight in what suits them. The five of bearing with adversity: first, to show that inner and outer obstacles must be borne with; second, to rebuke base men who usurp the teacher's seat; third, to show the practitioner the techniques for the inner and the outer; fourth, to show the model of bearing with adversity left by former worthies; fifth, to spur advance toward the stage of resemblance. The five of freedom from attachment: first, to cause one to leave the stage of falling from the summit; second, to show the difference between falling from the summit in the Great Vehicle and in the Lesser Vehicle; third, to show that resembling attachment is not true attachment; fourth, to show that its effort differs from the partial and small; fifth, to spur advance into the first abiding. In each of these fives, the first item is the principal intent, and the four below are all included in the text alongside it. If one understands these five in advance, one can recognize them when facing the text.

How the Ten Modes Differ from Other Schools: Ten General Points

Chinese Source

次明所立異於諸家，及今學者讀文昧旨，不知所立唯順圓融，若不了之，修習無分。於中又二，先總，次別。所言總者略列十條：一須知乘體無發無到，二須知乘體通因通果，三須知圓乘具於十法，四須知大車唯喻十法，五須知諸法皆具十乘，六須知諸教門門具十，七須知開顯唯妙十法，八須知簡體與具度別，九須知觀心立十法義，十須知白牛異黑牛故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying that what is established differs from the other schools — and that students today read the text but are in the dark about its purport, not knowing that what is established accords solely with perfect fusion; if one does not understand this, one has no share in cultivation. This is again twofold: first general, then particular. The general, briefly listed, is in ten items: first, one must know that the essence of the vehicle neither sets out nor arrives; second, one must know that the essence of the vehicle extends through cause and through fruit; third, one must know that the perfect vehicle possesses the ten modes; fourth, one must know that the great carriage illustrates only the ten modes; fifth, one must know that all dharmas possess the ten modes; sixth, one must know that in each teaching every gate possesses the ten; seventh, one must know that opening and revealing is only the wondrous ten modes; eighth, one must know that sifting out the essence is different from the accompanying perfections; ninth, one must know the meaning of establishing the ten modes in contemplating the mind; tenth, one must know that the white ox differs from the black ox.

Four Points for Each Mode

Chinese Source

所言別者，於一一法各具四意，縱有一兩似前附文，為欲辨異他所立故。妙境四者，一於無情境立佛乘故。若無佛乘，佛法身體為遍不遍？亦不應云「佛法身體同於無情及以不同」，是故應云「法名不覺，佛名為覺，佛即是法，法即是眾，豈可條然」。二眾生性德具三因故。若無三因，則緣、了始有無常，如何無常而立常果？《大經》破外，用別教意，非此所論。三依正二報在一念故。他人咸知一切唯識，不知身土居乎一心，故知心體即常寂光，寂光、諸土無二無別，遮那之身與土相稱，法與報、應一體無差。四佛本不斷性惡法故。性惡若斷，普現色身從何而立？但使分得常住法身，不動而動，遍應身土，具如《觀音玄文》及「第五記」。

Ru-Yi Translation

The particular: each mode has four intents; even if one or two resemble the earlier "attached to the text," [they are given] in order to distinguish [this] from what others establish. The four of the wondrous object: first, because the buddha-vehicle is established in the insentient object. If there were no buddha-vehicle [there], would the essence of the Buddha's Dharma-body be universal or not universal? Nor should one say "the essence of the Buddha's Dharma-body is the same as the insentient, or is not the same"; therefore one should say: "Dharma is called non-awakened, Buddha is called awakened; Buddha is Dharma, Dharma is beings — how could they be separate?" Second, because the inherent virtue of beings possesses the three causes. If there were not the three causes, then the conditioning and the revealing [causes] would have a beginning and be impermanent; how could the impermanent establish an eternal fruit? The Great Sūtra's refutation of the outsiders uses the intent of the distinct teaching; that is not what is discussed here. Third, because the dependent and the principal rewards are in a single thought. Others all know that everything is consciousness-only, but do not know that body and land dwell in a single mind; hence one knows that the essence of mind is the eternal tranquil light, that tranquil light and the various lands are not two and not separate, that the body of Vairocana matches its land, and that the Dharma[-body], the reward[-body], and the response[-body] are one body without difference. Fourth, because the Buddha originally does not sever the dharmas of inherent evil. If inherent evil were severed, from where would the universally manifested form-bodies be established? It need only be that one partially attains the eternally abiding Dharma-body, moving without moving, responding universally in body and land — fully as in the text of the Profound Meaning of Avalokiteśvara and in "the fifth [fascicle of my] Record."

Chinese Source

發心四者，一發心先思所託之境，如十種發心各四解不同，乃至當分跨節不同；二念念具足四弘誓故，以總冠別，一一行願從茲而立，故知無作四諦只一念心，為依此境遍於多念；三於一念心以辨能所，以此能所悲己悲他，他一念心生佛理等，是故菩薩依斯起誓；四圓發仍須遍立遍破，離能著已，方契所依。安心四者，一圓頓止觀據行仍開六十四番，不立多番，逗會不足；二一念止觀須了能所，定慧諦境諸行宛然，所以離總出別，別皆有總，雖異而同，雖同而異；三寂照相即初心可修，他以用權在於極果，初後不二，其教徒施；四凡師為他仍須問彼，同設一位未見益方，縱轉弄好異，尚新而已，隨順己見，何關適他？受者非機，語見增長，所以同宗，枝派乖各，理觀既薄，矛盾遂滋。今家辨師先分凡聖，六根淨位尚曰凡流，五品弟子理非真應，問他設教，依病立方，四悉便宜、二行互益，何須固執終朝守株？

Ru-Yi Translation

The four of arousing the mind: first, in arousing the mind one first considers the object relied on — as with the ten kinds of arousing the mind, each with four different interpretations, up to the difference between "its own portion" and "crossing the joint"; second, because every thought fully possesses the four universal vows — crowning the particular with the general, each practice and vow is established from this; hence one knows that the unconditioned four truths are just a single thought of mind, and in reliance on this object they pervade many thoughts; third, in a single thought of mind, the agent and its object are distinguished; with this agent and object one has compassion for oneself and compassion for others, [knowing that] others' single thought of mind is equal to the Buddha in principle, and so on; therefore bodhisattvas make their vows on this basis; fourth, perfect arousing still requires establishing universally and refuting universally; only after leaving the attachment of the agent does one accord with what is relied on. The four of settling the mind: first, the perfect-and-sudden calming-and-contemplation, in terms of practice, still opens into sixty-four rounds; if many rounds were not established, accommodating and fitting [capacities] would be insufficient; second, the calming-and-contemplation of a single thought must understand agent and object: concentration and wisdom, truth and object, and all the practices are distinct; therefore, leaving the general to bring out the particular, each particular has the general — though different, the same; though the same, different; third, the mutual identity of stillness and illumination can be cultivated by the beginner; others place the use of the provisional in the ultimate fruit; [if] beginning and end [were not] nondual, that teaching would be bestowed in vain; fourth, an ordinary teacher, for the sake of others, must still question them; setting up one and the same stage, one does not see a beneficial remedy; even if one turns and plays with fine novelties, it is only esteeming the new; following one's own views, what has that to do with suiting others? The recipients are not the [right] capacity, and words and views grow; thus, [though] of the same school, the branches and offshoots go each their own way, and with contemplation of principle thin, contradictions then multiply. Our school, in discerning teachers, first separates the ordinary and the sage: those at the stage of the purity of the six faculties are still called of the ordinary stream, and disciples of the five grades are in principle not true responses. Questioning others and setting up teachings, establishing remedies according to the illness, the four siddhāntas as suitable, the two practices mutually benefiting — why cling stubbornly all day long, waiting by the tree stump?

Chinese Source

破遍四者，一初無生心橫竪雖遍，復須後位六即竪窮，若橫若竪無不即理，橫門一一無不具竪及非橫竪；二須知他門、他門理等；三度入他門諸法無差，若用一門諸門融入，況《涅槃》釋義、《佛藏》示相、《棱伽》釋成、《地持》對教，咸隨法相度入諸門，又以一念心該冠兩門，高廣大車不動而運；四入住應遍方名假遍及以真遍，爾前雖觀圓融三諦，但是自行觀行相似，約位仍在若俗若真，是故雖圓未名破遍，住後尚須節節離愛，方能令淨餘位無明。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Fifth Principle, Resolving Doubts concerning Mind and Object, is in brief twenty rounds. Question: In the first fascicle, under "the universal vows," it says "the dharma-realm one is faced with, the dharma-realm that arises." How can the dharma-realm have arising and facing? Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: In relation to the later object, knowing that the mind has not yet arisen is called "not yet arisen"; the features of mind being about to be produced is "about to arise." Moreover, discussing their essential nature, it is only in terms of the Dharma-nature's stillness and illumination being free and usable; hence there are so many.

十三問：即此文中或云諸法即是法性，或云四運四性推檢，何者為要？答：夫觀心法有事有理，從理唯達法性更不餘塗，從事則專照起心四性叵得，亦名本末本末相映、事理不二。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirteenth question: Within this very text, it sometimes says the dharmas are the Dharma-nature, and sometimes says to examine by the four phases and the four natures. Which is essential? Answer: The method of contemplating mind has phenomena and has principle. From principle, one simply comprehends the Dharma-nature and takes no other route; from phenomena, one exclusively illuminates the arising mind, which cannot be found under the four natures. This is also called root and branch, root and branch reflecting each other, phenomena and principle nondual.

Chinese Source

十四問：《法華玄文》「境能照智」，雖引誠證，理亦難明。答：順方便教，理不可會，若從極說，於理易融。以心為境，心亦能照，能所俱心，心體俱遍，心心相照，於理甚明。故「不可思議境」初云「不可思議境即是觀」。以是得為四句分別：境照於境，境照於智，智照於境，智照於智。照者方照非說可窮，照者應說非照可了；說者方說非照可窮，說者應照非說可了。是故不同世謂頑境以為所照，又亦不同偏小妄心以為所照，又亦不同假立真如以為所照。所照既爾，能照亦然，不可率爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourteenth question: In the text of the Profound Meaning of the Lotus, "the object can illuminate wisdom" — though it cites a reliable proof, the principle is hard to clarify. Answer: Following the expedient teachings, the principle cannot be fitted together; if one speaks from the ultimate, it is easily fused in principle. Taking mind as the object, mind too can illuminate; agent and object are both mind, and the essence of mind is universal in both; mind and mind illuminate each other — in principle this is very clear. Hence at the beginning of "the inconceivable object" it says, "The inconceivable object is itself contemplation." On this basis four phrases can be distinguished: the object illuminating the object, the object illuminating wisdom, wisdom illuminating the object, wisdom illuminating wisdom. Illumination is properly illumination, not something speech can exhaust; illumination ought to be spoken, not something illumination can make clear. Speech is properly speech, not something illumination can exhaust; speech ought to be illuminated, not something speech can make clear. Therefore it is unlike the world's taking an inert object as what is illuminated; again, unlike the partial and small taking the deluded mind as what is illuminated; again, unlike taking a provisionally established suchness as what is illuminated. What is illuminated being so, what illuminates is likewise; one cannot be hasty about it.

Chinese Source

十五問：「破法遍」中云「須先用無生為首」者，門後料簡云何復云「無生是智，無滅是斷，智則是觀，斷則是止」？應無生門唯觀無止。答：破遍門意從事偏說，故文中云「有定之慧而盡淨之」，具如記中廣分別說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifteenth question: In "breaking through every dharma" it says "one must first take non-arising as the head." Why then does the sifting after the gates say, "Non-arising is wisdom, non-ceasing is severance; wisdom is contemplation, severance is calming"? It would seem the gate of non-arising has only contemplation and no calming. Answer: The intent of the gate of breaking through is spoken partially from the side of phenomena; hence the text says "one applies the wisdom present in concentration to exhaust and purify it," as distinguished at length in my Record.

Chinese Source

十六問：禪境初「十二因緣觀不思議境」初云「不同世人取著一念能具三千」，為唯此中諸境皆然？答：一切皆爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixteenth question: At the beginning of the object of dhyāna, at the start of "contemplating the twelve links as the inconceivable object," it says, "unlike worldly people grasping a single thought as able to possess the three thousand." Is it only here, or is it so for all the objects? Answer: It is so for all.

Chinese Source

十七問：若爾，應當取著心中不具三千？答：此準用觀觀境而說。取著之心本是諸法，照此著心緣生虛假，假中三千自體性空，即是心性不可思議圓妙三諦。譬如空華，華與空體無一無別，此空不當華之與空，對華說空，空無名字。以此細推，諸法皆爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventeenth question: If so, should one grasp that the mind does not possess the three thousand? Answer: This is said by analogy with using contemplation to contemplate the object. The grasping mind is originally the dharmas; illuminating this grasping mind as arisen from conditions, unreal and provisional, the three thousand within the provisional are empty in their own nature — this is the nature of mind, the inconceivable, perfect, wondrous three truths. It is like flowers in the sky: the flowers and the essence of space are neither one nor separate; this space is not [a matter of] the flowers or of space; against the flowers one speaks of space, [but] space has no name. Examining finely in this way, all dharmas are like this.

十八問：「十二因緣妙境」文中束為三道以對三德，苦身質閡，那對法身？答：此約理說，的相翻對，以身對身故作此說，實論三體更無前後。且論無始苦輪無際，與業煩惱不並不別，況今文中約於一念十界百界以論因緣，約此因緣以論三道，約此三道以論三德，是故三道及以三德並無前後，性德三因無時不具，豈更問質閡等耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighteenth question: In the text on "the twelve links as the wondrous object," they are bundled into the three paths to match the three virtues. The suffering body is material and obstructive; how can it match the Dharma-body? Answer: This is said in terms of principle, an exact matching by inversion; body is matched with body, hence it is put this way; in reality the three essences have no before or after. Moreover, the wheel of suffering without beginning has no limit, and with karma and affliction it is neither side by side nor separate. How much more, since the present text discusses causes and conditions in terms of the ten realms and the hundred realms in a single thought, discusses the three paths in terms of these causes and conditions, and discusses the three virtues in terms of these three paths — therefore the three paths and the three virtues alike have no before or after, and the three causes of inherent virtue are never not possessed. Why further ask about materiality and obstruction?

Ru-Yi Translation

Nineteenth question: Someone asked: "The true teachings of this land have a lineage of transmission; though the divisions of the teaching are opened broadly, the original flavor still remains; seeking the source of the school, one can of oneself meet the root. Why further set up the threefold contemplation in a single mind and examination by the four phases, muddying our clear stream?" Answer: The stream of the Jing is originally clear; stirred, it is not muddied. The true source is pure in essence; what harm is there in mixing? Even if a sage from India came in person, it would not equal one descending from Tuṣita in a second birth. Hence the Great Being of Dongyang, whose stage was virtual awakening, still took the three contemplations and the four phases as the essentials of mind; so his poem "Alone" says: "Alone, refined — / in reality apart from sound and name; / the three contemplations in one mind fuse the myriad kinds — / where could thorny thickets grow? / Alone, I act — / ask what my mind is attached to: / examining the four phases, all is non-arising — / how can a thousand ends and ten thousand ties bind?"

問：《大師口決》純為治病，為復更有餘心要耶？答：諸皆治病，唯有一偈，云師嘗教誡言：「實心繫實境，實緣次第生，實實迭相注，自然入實理。」釋曰：「心若繫境，境必繫心，心境相繫，名為實緣。復由後心心心相續、心心相繫名迭相注，即是心注於境、境注於境，境注於心，心心境境念念相注，如是次第剎那無間，自然從於觀行、相似以入分證，故云入實。」◎

Question: Are the Great Master's Oral Decisions purely for treating illness, or do they also have other essentials of mind? Answer: All are for treating illness; there is only one verse, which says that the Master once taught and admonished: "A real mind tied to a real object, / real conditions arise in sequence; / the real and the real flow into each other in turn, / and naturally one enters the real principle." Explanation: "If the mind is tied to the object, the object must be tied to the mind; mind and object tied to each other is called real conditions. Further, by later minds, mind after mind continuing, mind and mind tied to each other, is called flowing into each other in turn: that is, the mind flows into the object, the object flows into the object, the object flows into the mind; mind and mind, object and object, thought after thought flowing into each other; in such sequence, instant after instant without interval, one naturally passes from contemplative practice and resemblance to enter the portion of realization; hence it says 'enters the real.'"

Fivefold profound meaning. Zhang'an's question. "Dharmas are like dust and sand; why are the objects fixed at ten?" refers to a question in Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 5, in the discussion of the ten objects.
