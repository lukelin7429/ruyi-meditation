The First Three Principles: The Work, Its Scriptural Basis, and How to Read It

Zhanran (711–782) wrote the Guiding Principles of Calming and Contemplation as a reader's guide to Zhiyi's Mohe Zhiguan, arranged under seven "principles" (例) that he lists at the outset. This part covers the first fascicle up to the end of the third principle. The first principle states that the whole work, despite its references to three kinds of calming-and-contemplation, is a single treatise on the perfect-and-sudden practice; the other teachings it discusses serve as "attendants" to the great carriage of the true mark. The second principle names the two scriptures on which it rests: the Lotus, which supplies the true mark, the ten modes, and the image of the great carriage, and the Nirvāṇa, which supports precepts and the eternal for practitioners of the latter age. The third principle, much the longest, gives forty short rules for interpreting the text, in four groups of ten: on the meaning of passages (how quotations, similes, and borrowed terms work), on the features of the text (where things are concluded, what is principal and what auxiliary), on the structure of the wording (dharma and simile, question and answer, the note "and so on"), and on the structure of the meaning (how teaching and contemplation, practice and principle relate). Readers will find it easier with the ten sections, the ten objects, and the ten modes of the Mohe Zhiguan in mind.

The First Principle: The Division of the Work Transmitted

Chinese Source

第一所傳部別例者，總指一部以為圓頓佛乘正行之大體也。「大意」文初雖有數處「三止觀結」，但汎爾借名結義，非為即為三種行相。以「大意」中文略意廣，故用三、一收束結撮，應知部內意唯在頓。故序中云「大意在一頓，以略冠廣，不可差殊」，又示處中云「圓頓文者，如玉泉寺所記」。下文雖有四教八教、思議不思議、相待絕待，皆為顯於圓頓一實。故大車文中以思議、相待等而為僕從，實相妙理以為車體，無漏妙觀以為白牛，自餘諸法皆莊嚴具。故知此部更無他趣。

Ru-Yi Translation

The First Principle, the Division of the Work Transmitted: it points to the work as a whole as the main body of the correct practice of the perfect-and-sudden buddha-vehicle. Although at the beginning of the text of "the great intent" there are several places that conclude with "the three [kinds of] calming-and-contemplation," these only borrow the names in a general way to conclude the meaning; they are not thereby the three kinds of practice. Because in "the great intent" the text is brief and the meaning broad, it uses the three and the one to gather and sum it up; one should know that the intent within the work lies solely in the sudden. Hence the preface says, "The great intent rests in the one sudden [approach]; with the brief it crowns the extensive, and there can be no discrepancy"; and again, where it indicates [the three texts], it says, "The text for the perfect-and-sudden is as recorded at Yuquan Monastery." Although the text below has the four teachings and the eight teachings, the conceivable and the inconceivable, the relative and the absolute, all of these serve to reveal the one reality of the perfect-and-sudden. Hence in the passage on the great carriage, the conceivable, the relative, and so on are made its attendants, the wondrous principle of the true mark is made the body of the carriage, the wondrous uncontaminated contemplation is made the white ox, and all the other dharmas are its adornments. Hence one knows that this work has no other aim.

The Second Principle: The Correct Teachings Relied On

Chinese Source

第二所依正教例者，散引諸文該乎一代，文體正意唯歸二經：一依《法華》本迹顯實、二依《涅槃》扶律顯常，以此二經同醍醐故。所以釋名論待論絕，乃至偏圓文中具引「蓮華三喻」，釋名顯體具用「光宅四一」，即是實相為行正體也，況諸境十乘皆以「大車」為喻。故生起文末總稱歎云「積劫勤求，道場證得，身子三請，法譬三說」，正在茲乎！是知四種三昧皆依實相，實相是安樂之法，四緣是安樂之行。證實相已，所獲依報名為大果，起教只是為令眾生開示悟入，旨歸只是歸於三軌妙法祕藏，所以始末皆依《法華》，此即法華三昧之妙行也。次用《涅槃》者，雖依《法華》咸歸一實，末代根鈍若無扶助則正行傾覆，正助相添方能遠運。佛化尚以涅槃為壽，況末代修行非助不前，故扶律說常以顯實相，推功在彼；故正用《法華》，意顯圓常。二經齊等。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Second Principle, the Correct Teachings Relied On: though [the work] cites scattered passages that encompass [the Buddha's] whole lifetime, the correct intent of the text's structure returns solely to two sūtras: first, it relies on the Lotus for origin and trace and for revealing the real; second, it relies on the Nirvāṇa for supporting the precepts and revealing the eternal — because these two sūtras are alike the ghee. That is why "explaining the name" discusses the relative and discusses the absolute, and why the passage on partial and complete cites in full "the three similes of the lotus"; "explaining the name" and "revealing the essence" use in full "Guangzhai's four onenesses" — this is the true mark as the correct essence of practice. How much more do the various objects and the ten modes all take "the great carriage" as their simile! Hence at the end of the passage on the sequence of arising, it sums up in praise: "What [the Tathāgata] sought with diligence across accumulated kalpas, what he realized at the site of awakening, what Śāriputra begged three times to hear, what was taught three times through Dharma and simile — it lies precisely here!" Thus one knows that the four samādhis all rely on the true mark: the true mark is the peaceful dharma, and the four conditions are the peaceful practice. Once the true mark is realized, the dependent reward obtained is called the great fruit; "the teachings that arise" serve only to cause beings to open, display, awaken to, and enter [the Buddha's knowledge and vision]; "where it ultimately leads" simply returns to the wondrous dharma of the three tracks, the secret treasury. Therefore from beginning to end it relies entirely on the Lotus; this is the wondrous practice of the Lotus samādhi. Next, as for using the Nirvāṇa: although, relying on the Lotus, all return to the one reality, in the latter age faculties are dull, and without support the correct practice would topple; only when the correct and the auxiliary are added together can one travel far. The Buddha's teaching itself takes nirvāṇa as its life-span; how much less can practice in the latter age advance without help! Therefore it supports the precepts and speaks of the eternal so as to reveal the true mark, attributing the achievement to that. Hence it uses the Lotus as the correct [basis], intending to reveal the perfect and the eternal. The two sūtras stand equal.

The Third Principle: Interpreting Text and Meaning — Detailed Investigation of Textual Meaning

Chinese Source

第三文義消釋例者，又更為二：一者詳究文義、二者消釋體勢。初文為二：一者詳究文義，二者詳究文相。初為十例：一者引證通局。如引法華部唯一實文敘昔教以為所開，故部中之文有權有實，若局證一實則引實文，若通證方便則兼引昔義，如引《法華》證漸、不定所引四味文之與部，通局亦然。二者況引流類。謂引教證觀等，如引《華嚴》「先照高山」、《淨名》「始坐佛樹」、《大經》「從牛出乳」、《法華》「以異方便」等，以證三止觀義。故知教觀漸等名同，其義永異。彼文判教，今以類同，是故借用。三者借名申義。謂借權名申於實義，如引《方等》斥奪之名，申今開權絕待之義，引餘三時類此可知。於彼即是兼、但、對等，於今即成開、廢、會等。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Third Principle, Interpreting Text and Meaning, is further twofold: first, a detailed investigation of text and meaning; second, the structure and momentum of interpretation. The first part is twofold: first, a detailed investigation of textual meaning; second, a detailed investigation of textual features. The first consists of ten principles. First: citations as proof, general and restricted. For example, in citing the Lotus as a work: [it teaches] only the one reality, yet its text narrates the earlier teachings as what is opened up; hence the text within the work has both the provisional and the real. If [the citation] proves the one reality in a restricted way, it cites texts on the real; if it proves the expedients in a general way, it also cites the meanings of the earlier [teachings]. In citing the Lotus to prove the gradual and the indeterminate, the texts and works of the four flavors that are cited are general and restricted in the same way. Second: citing things of the same kind by analogy. That is, citing teachings to prove contemplation and so on — for example, citing the Avataṃsaka's "it shines first on the high mountains," the Vimalakīrti's "when first seated beneath the buddha-tree," the Great Sūtra's "from the cow comes milk," and the Lotus's "with different skillful means," and so on, in order to prove the meaning of the three [kinds of] calming-and-contemplation. Hence one knows that though the names "gradual" and so on are the same in teaching and in contemplation, their meanings are forever different. Those texts classify the teachings; here, because they are alike in kind, they are borrowed. Third: borrowing names to set forth meanings. That is, borrowing provisional names to set forth the real meaning — for example, citing the names of rebuking and taking away in the Vaipulya to set forth the present meaning of opening the provisional and of the absolute; citing the other three periods can be understood in the same way. There they are "combined," "exclusive," "contrasted," and so on; here they become "opening," "discarding," "merging," and so on.

Chinese Source

四者借喻轉譬。如猪揩金山等論喻忍等，今借譬止等喻是世間物類而以隨義轉用，何局本文。如火一物，諸經或時譬瞋譬智，或用照用燒，以形以性、若體若用，地水風等為喻亦然，是故不應局文為定。五者旁引辨異。如諸文用《毘曇》、《成實》若證漸初及偏小等，則名義兼借，如信、法二行文初「五陰、王數，同時、異時」等，但為辨異，非借名義。六者開總出別。如四悉、五味、三假、二空，本文義含開合，義遍諸門諸教，莫不咸然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth: borrowing similes and turning their application. Such as the hog rubbing against the golden mountain and so on, which the Treatise uses as similes for forbearance and so on: here these similes are borrowed to illustrate calming and so on. They are things of the world, used with their application turned according to the meaning — why restrict them to the original text? Take fire, a single thing: the sūtras sometimes use it as a simile for anger, sometimes for wisdom; sometimes using its illuminating, sometimes its burning; by its form or by its nature, as essence or as function. Using earth, water, wind, and so on as similes is likewise. Therefore one should not restrict [them] to the text as fixed. Fifth: citing alongside to distinguish differences. As when various passages use the Abhidharma and the Satyasiddhi: if they prove the beginning of the gradual, the partial and small, and so on, then both name and meaning are borrowed; [but] as at the start of the passage on the two practices of faith and Dharma — "the five aggregates, the mind-king and mental factors, simultaneous or at different times," and so on — these serve only to distinguish differences, not to borrow name and meaning. Sixth: opening the general to bring out the particular. Such as the four siddhāntas, the five flavors, the three kinds of provisional reality, the two emptinesses: in the original texts their meaning includes opening and combining, and the meaning pervades all the gates and all the teachings — none is otherwise.

Chinese Source

七者引用宗要。如《法華》權、實、本、迹，《般若》加說共不共等，《方等》彈斥、神力不共等，隨引一句兩句得彼文心，若破若立不失部音。八者引用儒、道。若破若立不違本宗，略辨異同不在委細，不以名似將為義同，是故所引粗爾存略。九者借名略義。如「攝法」中及「識藥」等，但借其名以示相狀，若更委釋太成繁廣。略指上、下，準例可知。十者準例用意。如教證二道本在別教，今則通用，乃分兩意——約證、約說，準望三觀立三止等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh: citing the cardinal essentials. Such as the provisional, the real, origin, and trace in the Lotus; the shared and unshared spoken further in the Prajñā; the rebuking and refuting, and the unshared supernatural powers, in the Vaipulya, and so on: whichever one or two phrases are cited capture the heart of that text, and whether refuting or establishing, they do not lose the tone of the work. Eighth: citing Confucian and Daoist [texts]. Whether refuting or establishing, they do not contradict our own school; similarities and differences are distinguished briefly, not in detail; names that look alike are not taken to mean the same; hence what is cited is kept rough and brief. Ninth: borrowing names with the meaning abbreviated. As in "gathering in the teachings" and in "recognizing the medicine" and so on: only the names are borrowed to show the features; explaining them further in detail would become too complicated and broad. They are briefly referred to above and below, and can be understood by analogy with the pattern. Tenth: applying the intent by analogy with the pattern. Such as the two paths of teaching and realization, which originally belong to the distinct teaching but here are used in general, divided into two senses — in terms of realization and in terms of exposition; by analogy with the three contemplations, the three calmings are established, and so on.

Detailed Investigation of Textual Features

Chinese Source

二者詳究文相，亦為十例：一者隨相開合。如三觀、四教、四悉、五味、諦、緣、度等，一家立義，文相皆然，寬廣無窮，隨事、隨理、隨法、隨名、隨行、隨證、隨自、隨他無不通用，然須結撮勿使浮濫。若不爾者，徵文靡託、立行莫施。若得今意，存本文則淺深有則，演義理則縱廣無涯，示一心則卷權歸實，從被物則開實出權，立行儀則以智為通，蕩相者則纖毫不遺，存諸教則因果歷然，顯一理則始終無二。二者結示處所及立本文意。如例餘陰入在破遍文末、竪破法遍有六處示妙、妙境中明說教大體、發心中則約圓斥偏、安心中義開三種、道品中不出念處、通塞中元治能執、次位中勸修五悔、安忍無著但在進功，若搜得宗源則諸文可識，大綱既整網日易存。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the detailed investigation of textual features, also in ten principles. First: opening and combining according to features. Such as the three contemplations, the four teachings, the four siddhāntas, the five flavors, the truths, the [twelve] links, the perfections, and so on: in the meanings our school establishes, the textual features are all like this, broad without end — according to phenomena, according to principle, according to the dharma, according to the name, according to practice, according to realization, according to oneself, according to others, there is nothing to which they do not apply; yet they must be gathered and summed up, not allowed to drift and overflow. If not, a search of the text will find nothing to rely on, and establishing practice will have nothing to apply. If one grasps the present intent: in keeping to the original text, depth and shallowness have their rule; in expanding the meaning and principle, length and breadth have no limit; in showing the single mind, one rolls up the provisional and returns to the real; in accommodating beings, one opens the real and brings out the provisional; in establishing the forms of practice, wisdom is taken as the passage; in sweeping away features, not a hair is left; in preserving the teachings, cause and fruit are distinct; in revealing the one principle, beginning and end are without two. Second: indicating the places [where things are concluded] and the intent of establishing the original text. For example, applying [the pattern] to the remaining aggregates and entries comes at the end of the passage on breaking through every dharma; the vertical breaking through every dharma shows the wondrous in six places; within the wondrous object, the great body of the exposition of the teachings is clarified; within arousing the mind, the partial is rebuked in terms of the perfect; within settling the mind, the meaning opens into three kinds; within the branches of the path, it does not go beyond the foundations of mindfulness; within passage and blockage, it originally treats the mind that grasps; within the stages, it urges cultivating the five repentances; bearing with adversity and freedom from attachment lie only in advancing effort. If one searches out the source of the school, the various passages can be recognized; once the main cord is in order, the meshes are easily kept.

Chinese Source

三者事理旁正。如四三昧正為顯理、旁兼治重，如十法界正示理具、旁識淺深，如識次位正為簡濫、旁為通經。又諸文中一切皆以破古為旁，意雖旁正，文不可廢。四者文偏意圓。如以三止觀結於諸文，及五略中所用三教諸境十乘縱橫偏小等，三種止觀皆緣實相等。五者廣略有無。如發心中顯數則廣而文相略，後十法中文相則廣而顯數略，修大行中事儀則廣而十法略，修正觀中十法則廣而事儀略，後三大章大意中有、廣解中無，十種境界正觀中有、四三昧無，雖互略無，義必通具。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third: phenomena and principle, auxiliary and principal. For example, the four samādhis are principally for revealing principle and auxiliarily also treat grave [offenses]; the ten dharma-realms principally show what is inherently possessed in principle and auxiliarily recognize shallow and deep; recognizing the stages is principally for sifting out confusion and auxiliarily for penetrating the sūtras. Again, in all the passages, refuting the ancients is always auxiliary; though the intent has auxiliary and principal, the text cannot be discarded. Fourth: the text partial but the intent perfect. Such as concluding the various passages with the three [kinds of] calming-and-contemplation, and the three teachings, the various objects, the ten modes, the horizontal and vertical, the partial and small, and so on used in the five brief points; the three kinds of calming-and-contemplation all take the true mark as their object, and so on. Fifth: extensive and brief, present and absent. For example, in arousing the mind, the enumeration is extensive while the textual features are brief; in the later ten modes, the textual features are extensive while the enumeration is brief; in cultivating the great practice, the forms of the practices are extensive while the ten modes are brief; in cultivating the actual contemplation, the ten modes are extensive while the forms of the practices are brief; the last three major sections are present in the great intent but absent in the extensive explanation; the ten kinds of objects are present in the actual contemplation but absent in the four samādhis. Though each abbreviates or omits [something], in meaning they must be complete throughout.

Chinese Source

六者文行不同。如十境十乘生起次第、十禪淺深，竪破法偏文雖次第，行必隨人。十禪何必自淺階深？竪破元為顯於不二，或隨竪次入，何障於理？自淺階深妙觀斯在。七者待絕前後。若約教相必先待後絕，若論道理待絕俱時。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth: text and practice are not the same. Such as the order of arising of the ten objects and the ten modes, the shallow and deep of the ten dhyānas, and the vertical breaking through every dharma: though the text is sequential, practice must follow the person. Why must the ten dhyānas ascend from shallow to deep? The vertical breaking through was originally for revealing the nondual; and if one enters following the vertical sequence, what obstacle is that to principle? Ascending from shallow to deep, the wondrous contemplation is right there. Seventh: relative and absolute, earlier and later. In terms of the features of the teachings, the relative must come first and the absolute after; in terms of the principle of the path, relative and absolute are simultaneous.

Chinese Source

八者破會不同。若依化儀必先破次會，如先斥小、後方會圓，故方等、般若廣破偏小，次至法華方會入實；約人約行破會同時，如照權了實——照權名破、了實名會。是故今文為顯理故，不同《玄文》專在判教，凡有釋義，破必居先，故今偏圓借彼釋名，次第則別。九者行解不同。如五略生起、分別十章、引證破古、問答料簡等多為生解，若十法總別、十境互發、十禪離合、十境發相專在於行，是故先識，方可造修。十者舉例從略。如道品、攝法、假中空例，破餘陰餘使餘品以例初品，相續相待以例因成，則細尋廣意以申餘文，歷法先思方不昧旨。若十法成觀，但於陰入委識根由，餘九待發方可設觀，故九境中但分別境，境下十觀不細委分，但隨境轉照，非關廣說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth: refuting and merging are not the same. According to the teachings of method, refuting must come first and merging next — as first rebuking the small and only afterward merging into the perfect; hence the Vaipulya and the Prajñā widely refute the partial and small, and only on reaching the Lotus are they merged and entered into the real. In terms of the person and of practice, refuting and merging are simultaneous — as in illuminating the provisional and comprehending the real: illuminating the provisional is called refuting, and comprehending the real is called merging. Therefore the present text, because it is for revealing principle, differs from the text of the Profound Meaning, which is devoted to classifying the teachings: wherever it explains a meaning, refuting always comes first. Hence the present [section on] partial and complete borrows that [work's] "explaining the name," but the order is different. Ninth: practice and understanding are not the same. For example, the generative order of the five brief points, the distinctions among the ten sections, citing proofs and refuting the ancients, the questions and answers that sift [points], and so on, are mostly for producing understanding; whereas the general and particular of the ten modes, the mutual arousing of the ten objects, the separating and combining of the ten dhyānas, and the features of the arising of the ten objects lie wholly in practice. Therefore one first recognizes [them], and only then can undertake cultivation. Tenth: citing an example and abbreviating the rest. Such as the branches of the path, gathering in the teachings, and the provisional and the middle exemplified by the empty; breaking through the remaining aggregates, the remaining fetters, and the remaining grades is exemplified by the first grade; continuity and mutual dependence are exemplified by [the provisional reality] established by cause. Then one investigates the broad intent carefully in order to extend it to the other passages; in going through the dharmas one first reflects, and only then is not in the dark about the purport. As for the ten modes making up contemplation: only in the aggregates and entries are the root causes recognized in detail; the other nine must wait until they arise, and only then can contemplation be set up. Hence in the nine objects only the objects are distinguished, and under each object the ten contemplations are not divided in detail; one simply turns the illumination according to the object, which has nothing to do with extended exposition.

The Structure and Momentum of the Text

Chinese Source

次消釋體勢，又更為二：一文體勢、二義體勢。初文體勢亦為十例：一者法喻廣略。若法略喻廣，則展法文與喻相稱，不專守略法以擁喻文；或法廣喻略則攢法對喻；或開喻對法，如如意珠喻不思議境等；或法別喻總，如以大車喻於十法則開彼總喻以對十法，若以善畫勝堂為喻，不須開對，但略合而已；若法喻俱總，如以虛空喻於法界；若法喻俱別，則隨文對消，可以意得。二者法喻合三互有互無，隨宜設用，存沒適時。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the structure and momentum of interpretation is further twofold: first, the structure and momentum of the text; second, the structure and momentum of the meaning. The first, the structure and momentum of the text, is also in ten principles. First: extensive and brief in dharma and simile. If the dharma is brief and the simile extensive, then unfold the text of the dharma to match the simile, not keeping strictly to the brief dharma so as to hem in the text of the simile. Or, if the dharma is extensive and the simile brief, then gather the dharma to match the simile. Or open the simile to match the dharma, as with the wish-fulfilling jewel as a simile for the inconceivable object and so on. Or the dharma is particular and the simile general: when the great carriage is used as a simile for the ten modes, open that general simile to match the ten modes; if the skilled painter or the finest hall is the simile, there is no need to open and match, only to apply it briefly. If both dharma and simile are general, as with empty space as a simile for the dharma-realm, [or] if both dharma and simile are particular, then interpret them in correspondence following the text; this can be grasped by the mind. Second: dharma, simile, and application, the three, are each sometimes present and sometimes absent; they are set up as suitable, kept or dropped as the moment requires.

Chinese Source

三者開合自他。凡列章門，有對自開合，如五略對十廣；有對他開合，如以十廣對五重玄義；有相攝開合，如開上合下；有義立開合，如攝法等。開六開四等不出自他因果及文四義二，意唯在一。文相若盈若縮多少適時，後數必使至十，從義則賒促隨宜。四者注云云者。若上文已具，或餘部廣存，重展成繁，或廣文非要。若消釋者，須委的處所撮略指示；若傳寫者，有闕須填。五者破立存沒。如破古師及破邪僻，其義壞已不須更存，縱存其名不用其義，或小有不當則有去有取。若破偏破小，破己必立，為成一家不思議理、為逗一代不思議化等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third: opening and combining with respect to self and other. Wherever lists of sections are given, there is opening and combining with respect to itself, as the five brief points against the ten extensive [sections]; there is opening and combining with respect to another, as the ten extensive [sections] against the fivefold profound meaning; there is opening and combining by mutual inclusion, as opening the above and combining the below; there is opening and combining established by meaning, as with gathering in the teachings and so on. Opening into six, opening into four, and so on, do not go beyond self and other, cause and fruit, and the four of the text and the two of the meaning; the intent lies only in the one. The textual features, whether full or contracted, many or few, are as suitable to the moment; the latter count must be made to reach ten, [while] following the meaning they are loose or tight as appropriate. Fourth: on the note "and so on" (云云). [It is used] where the text above has already covered [the matter], or another work keeps it in full, so that unfolding it again would be tedious; or where the extensive text is not essential. As for the interpreter, he must point out in detail the exact place and indicate it in summary; as for the copyist, where something is missing it must be filled in. Fifth: refuting and establishing, keeping and dropping. As in refuting the ancient masters and refuting the deviant: once their meaning has been destroyed it need not be kept; even if the name is kept, its meaning is not used; or, where something is slightly inappropriate, some is discarded and some taken. If it refutes the partial and refutes the small, having refuted, it must establish — in order to complete our school's inconceivable principle, to accord with the inconceivable teaching of [the Buddha's] whole lifetime, and so on.

六者長短不同。若大小法相問答研覈，法喻對當章門開合，則隨文消釋無俟遠求。如不思議境等及破法遍，必觀歷初後尋求中間，或結長就短、或演短令長，是故皆須遠騰文勢方可碎釋，碎釋文顯，必融碎令全，使文理通暢，令一家行門歸趣有在。或總別二釋則以總冠別，別釋義長則攬別歸總。七者法喻隱顯。若法隱喻顯則求喻意以消法，若法顯喻隱則求法意以消喻，喻望於合顯隱例然，是則不失文旨上下相承。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth: long and short are not the same. Where the dharma features of the Mahāyāna and the Hīnayāna are investigated by question and answer, dharma and simile are matched, and section headings are opened and combined, interpret following the text without needing to look far. As for the inconceivable object and the like, and the breaking through every dharma: one must survey from beginning to end and search out the middle, sometimes gathering the long into the short, sometimes expanding the short into the long; hence one must in all cases follow the momentum of the text from afar before one can interpret it in detail; once the detailed interpretation makes the text clear, one must fuse the details to make them whole, so that text and principle flow freely and our school's gates of practice have a place to return to. Or, where there are both a general and a particular explanation, crown the particular with the general; if the particular explanation runs long in meaning, gather the particular back into the general. Seventh: dharma and simile, hidden and evident. If the dharma is hidden and the simile evident, seek the intent of the simile to interpret the dharma; if the dharma is evident and the simile hidden, seek the intent of the dharma to interpret the simile; the simile in relation to its application, hidden and evident, is likewise. In this way one does not lose the purport of the text, and above and below continue one another.

Chinese Source

八者問答迷解。若迷問而不迷答則求答意以設問，若迷答而不迷問則研問文以成答，或問從答生、或孤然釋妨、或因答作並、或從答設難、或答順於問、或答違於問、或答杜於問、或答開問端。九者舉例消文。如六度之文，或語勢兼含，應以教定之，使六文一類，若諦若緣諸法皆爾。十者以教定法，皆隨其教體以立義宗，或名偏意圓從體以定。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth: confusion and understanding in question and answer. If one is confused by the question but not by the answer, seek the intent of the answer to frame the question; if one is confused by the answer but not by the question, study the text of the question to complete the answer. Sometimes the question arises from the answer; sometimes it stands alone, resolving an objection; sometimes a parallel objection is made on account of the answer; sometimes a difficulty is posed from the answer; sometimes the answer accords with the question; sometimes the answer goes against the question; sometimes the answer shuts off the question; sometimes the answer opens the start of a question. Ninth: citing an example to interpret the text. Such as the text on the six perfections: where the phrasing combines and contains [several things], one should settle it by the teaching, making the six passages of one kind; whether the truths or the [twelve] links, all dharmas are like this. Tenth: settling the dharma by the teaching. In each case, establish the cardinal meaning according to the essence of the teaching; where the name is partial but the intent perfect, settle it by the essence.

The Structure and Momentum of the Meaning

Chinese Source

二者義體勢，亦為十例：一者部體本意。凡欲釋義，先思部類。如《法華玄》雖諸義之下皆立觀心，然文本意明五重玄義出諸教上，則教正觀旁，託事興觀、義立觀心。教中則以權實本迹為主，常以五味八教以簡於權，並以世界塵數以簡於迹。若本迹交雜、教味疏遺，無以顯於待絕二妙，餘味餘部以類求之則可知矣。若今止觀縱用諸教，意在十法以成妙觀，則觀正教旁，為顯實理旁通諸教，復為生信旁引諸經。二者觀教同異。既約法華應須八教，教雖有八，頓等四教是佛化儀，藏等四教是佛化法。依法起觀，觀則有四，漸既異別，更加不定，故觀則有六。開權顯實會藏等三，漸及不定元知圓極，故佛本意唯佛乘是，如今文隨教雖復若八若四，本意唯為成一佛乘。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the structure and momentum of the meaning, also in ten principles. First: the essence of the work and its original intent. Whenever one wishes to explain the meaning, first consider the kind of work. For example, although the Profound [Meaning] of the Lotus sets up contemplation of mind under each of its meanings, the original intent of its text is to clarify that the fivefold profound meaning stands above all the teachings; thus teaching is principal and contemplation auxiliary — contemplation is raised by relying on matters, and contemplation of mind is set up by the meaning. Within the teaching, the provisional and the real, origin and trace, are the main thing; it always uses the five flavors and eight teachings to sift out the provisional, and uses the number of dust-motes of the worlds to sift out the trace. If origin and trace were mixed together and teachings and flavors left loose, there would be no way to reveal the two wondrous [readings], relative and absolute. The other flavors and other works can be understood by seeking them by analogy. As for the present Calming and Contemplation, even though it uses the various teachings, its intent lies in the ten modes to complete the wondrous contemplation; thus contemplation is principal and teaching auxiliary — to reveal the real principle it auxiliarily draws on the various teachings, and again, to produce faith, it auxiliarily cites the various sūtras. Second: contemplation and teaching, same and different. Since [this is] in terms of the Lotus, there must be the eight teachings. Though the teachings are eight, the four teachings of the sudden and so on are the Buddha's methods of teaching, and the four teachings of the Tripiṭaka and so on are the Buddha's content of teaching. Raising contemplation on the basis of the content, the contemplations are four; since the gradual is different and separate, and the indeterminate is further added, the contemplations are six. Opening the provisional to reveal the real merges the three, the Tripiṭaka and the rest; the gradual and the indeterminate are originally known to be the perfect ultimate. Hence the Buddha's original intent is solely the buddha-vehicle. So in the present text: following the teachings, though there are eight or four, the original intent is only to complete the one buddha-vehicle.

三者觀門準則。隨機逗物雖立四門，教法體度無生為首。若消法相為成行解，斥奪比決上下交映，皆悉結撮歸於一乘。四者會異考同。若一切異名皆入一實名為通會，如會隨入悉、止觀異名等名為別會，通別二會攝法罄盡，則一家義勢攝法無窮，乃至法門亦具通別二會之意。又如破偏度入名為通會，當教當門名為別會。言考同者，如大小經論凡所立名，部帙雖殊，名相不別，豈為名同令法一概？必以理簡而使甄分。如諸外人尚以大自在天立三身名，豈此三名體同宗極？故云不可尋通名而求別體，故用相簡之。是以，考同出異、會異令同，若異若同同入一極。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third: the standard for the gates of contemplation. In responding to capacities and accommodating beings, although four gates are set up, in the essential measure of the teaching, non-arising comes first. When interpreting dharma features in order to complete practice and understanding — rebuking and taking away, comparing and judging, above and below reflecting each other — all are gathered, summed up, and returned to the one vehicle. Fourth: merging differences and examining sameness. If all different names enter the one reality, this is called general merging; merging such as that of "according" and "entering" with siddhānta, or of the different names of calming-and-contemplation, and so on, is called particular merging. With the two merges, general and particular, the gathering in of dharmas is complete; thus the structure and momentum of our school's meaning gathers in dharmas without end, and even the Dharma-gates possess the sense of the two merges, general and particular. Again, refuting the partial and crossing over to enter is called general merging; [each in] its own teaching and its own gate is called particular merging. As for "examining sameness": in the sūtras and treatises of the Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna, whatever names are set up, though the works differ, the names are not distinct — how could the sameness of names make the dharmas uniform? One must sift them by principle so that they are distinguished. For example, outsiders even set up the names of the three bodies with Maheśvara; how could the essence of these three names be the same as the ultimate of our school? Hence it says, "One cannot pursue a shared name to seek a distinct essence"; therefore they are used to sift one another. Thus examining sameness brings out difference, and merging difference makes it the same; whether different or the same, all alike enter the one ultimate.

五者以一例諸。如教證二道及一心具法，不同世人取著一念思議境立佛法界，前明可思議，後明不可思議等。部內唯有一文說之，以此一文而均上下，使處處文義通徹昭然。六者名義通局。如置毒譬，經中唯譬五道不同、佛性不變，五味唯喻一代五時濃淡，濃淡雖殊皆從牛出。今文從義，處處遍入，或定不定、或行或人、或教或位、或時或部，不可壅義而守其名，故用置毒則有兩種醍醐殺人，若用五味則有兩種乳不等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth: using one to exemplify all. Such as the two paths of teaching and realization, and the single mind's possessing all dharmas — unlike worldly people grasping a single thought as the conceivable object and setting up the buddha dharma-realm — first clarifying the conceivable and afterward clarifying the inconceivable, and so on. Within the work there is only one passage that speaks of it; with this one passage one levels above and below, so that everywhere text and meaning are penetrated through and clear. Sixth: names and meanings, general and restricted. Such as the simile of placing poison: in the sūtra it illustrates only that the five destinies differ while buddha-nature does not change; the five flavors illustrate only the thickness and thinness of the five periods of [the Buddha's] whole lifetime — though thick and thin differ, all come from the cow. The present text follows the meaning and enters everywhere, whether the fixed or the indeterminate, whether practice or person, whether teaching or stage, whether period or work; one cannot block the meaning to keep to its name. Hence in using the placing of poison there are two kinds of ghee that kill people; in using the five flavors there are two kinds of milk that are not equal.

七者開拓句法。或四或六或三十六乃至百千，隨其文意應多應少，皆使遍在一代教門，一一皆令有名有義，無得輒爾無義立名。八者束散前後。凡諸寬文文中無結，或隨文勢、若逐意便、或前結而後開、或前散而後束，所以釋者先須撿括語意寬急、所詣歸宗，使觀行有在。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh: expanding the patterns of phrases. Whether four, six, or thirty-six, up to a hundred or a thousand, according to the intent of the text, as many or as few as appropriate — all are made to pervade the teaching gates of [the Buddha's] whole lifetime, each one having both name and meaning; one must not casually set up a name without a meaning. Eighth: gathering and scattering, before and after. In all the loose passages, there is no conclusion within the text; sometimes following the momentum of the text, sometimes following the convenience of the meaning; sometimes concluding first and opening afterward, sometimes scattering first and gathering afterward. Hence the interpreter must first examine whether the language and intent are loose or tight, and where it arrives and returns to its source, so that contemplative practice has its place.

Chinese Source

九者行理交映。理有權實，行有親疏，親正疏旁、廢權入實，理無種種，行有淺深。說理則泯彼階差，談行必積功方達，以理融行，以行綜理，諸位無濫，方可免失。豈可尚深偏求一句！況以二十五法而為前導、十乘十境以為正修，故知行理相融方有所至。故第五云「照潤導達交絡瑩飾，一體二手更互揩摩」。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth: practice and principle reflecting each other. Principle has the provisional and the real; practice has the close and the distant; the close is principal and the distant auxiliary, discarding the provisional to enter the real. Principle has no variety; practice has shallow and deep. In speaking of principle, one obliterates those gradations; in discussing practice, one must accumulate effort to arrive. With principle, fuse practice; with practice, organize principle; only when the stages are not confused can one avoid error. How could one prize the deep and one-sidedly seek a single phrase! Moreover, the twenty-five methods are taken as the forerunners, and the ten modes and ten objects as the actual cultivation; hence one knows that only when practice and principle fuse with each other does one get anywhere. Hence the fifth [fascicle] says: "Each illuminates, moistens, guides, and reaches the other, interwoven and burnished bright. One body, two hands, mutually rubbing and polishing each other."

然義解者非消文時欲出文意，必須出沒開合，先令妙境周圓，依境立願、願行相稱、正助無闕、不失次位，是則可以斥偏邪、可以進圓行、可以顯異意、可以立宗徒。若消文時，先鉤鎖文勢一道竪進；次洮鍊前後以顯行相，以一觀境冠於下九，以一弘誓通及諸行，以一安心遍該始末，破遍只是安心加行，通塞只是上二細門，以一道品調停陰入，正助只是助開前四，次位通為上之七門以除濫過，安忍、離愛上八功能，入初住時轉名十大，良有以也。若不得意徒勞生起，說行說理未足可師。何者？越次則傷文，專文則損理。

Ru-Yi Translation

However, as for those who understand the meaning, when not interpreting the text but wishing to bring out the intent of the text, they must bring forth and drop, open and combine: first making the wondrous object complete all around; relying on the object, establishing the vow; vow and practice matching each other; the principal and the auxiliary lacking nothing; not losing the order of the stages. Then one can rebuke the partial and deviant, advance the perfect practice, reveal the distinctive intent, and establish followers of the school. When interpreting the text: first link the momentum of the text like a chain, advancing vertically along one path; next sift and refine before and after to reveal the features of practice — with the one contemplation of the object, crown the nine below; with the one universal vow, pervade all the practices; with the one settling of the mind, encompass beginning and end throughout; breaking through every dharma is just the added practice of settling the mind; passage and blockage are just the finer gates of the above two; with the one [mode of] the branches of the path, adjust the aggregates and entries; principal and auxiliary are just auxiliary [remedies] that open the former four; the stages serve in general the seven gates above, to remove the fault of confusion; bearing with adversity and freedom from attachment are the functions of the eight above; on entering the first abiding they change their name to the ten great — there is good reason for this. If one does not grasp the intent, setting out the generative order is wasted effort, and speaking of practice and speaking of principle, one is not fit to be a teacher. Why? Skipping the order harms the text; keeping only to the text damages principle.

十者教觀折攝。教折攝者，若權若實適時而用，或攝權折實、或歎實折權，理事因果、四門四悉，隨教隨機、約人約部，若密若顯、若現若當，為熟為種、逆化順化，不出一折一攝意也。觀折攝者，觀一境歷一心，或折為無常，或融為理性，宜治、樂厭、止觀、智斷、苦樂、縱奪、順逆、體折，如是等相皆亦不出折攝二意。故此教觀折攝二者，以理觀之，使元意有在。此四十條略知大概，餘不盡者準此可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth: subduing and embracing in teaching and contemplation. As for subduing and embracing in teaching: whether provisional or real, it is used as the time fits — sometimes embracing the provisional and subduing the real, sometimes praising the real and subduing the provisional; principle and phenomena, cause and fruit, the four gates and the four siddhāntas, according to the teaching and according to capacity, in terms of the person and in terms of the work, secret or manifest, present or future, for ripening or for planting, teaching by opposing or teaching by according — none goes beyond the intent of one subduing and one embracing. As for subduing and embracing in contemplation: contemplating one object and passing through one mind, sometimes subduing it as impermanent, sometimes fusing it as principle-nature — suited treatment, delight and disgust, calming and contemplation, wisdom and severance, suffering and joy, granting and taking away, according and opposing, embodying and subduing — all such features likewise do not go beyond the two intents of subduing and embracing. Hence, as for these two, subduing and embracing in teaching and contemplation, one views them by principle so that the original intent has its place. These forty items let one know the general outline in brief; what is not exhausted can be understood by analogy with these.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

The seven principles (例). The list in unit 2 gives the third principle as 依正消釋例, while its own opening (unit 5) calls it 文義消釋例; both are translated as written. The interlinear note 有題云：破迷者請改 is obscure. Alternatively: "Some [copies] title this [principle] 'Refuting Delusion'; please change it [to 'Clarifying Doubts']."

"The great intent rests in the one sudden [approach]." The first half is from the Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 1, in the clarifying questions after the brief pointer (site: Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 1); the site renders it "the great intent rests wholly in the one, sudden approach." The following words 以略冠廣，不可差殊 were not found in the T1911 text; source not located.

"The text for the perfect-and-sudden." Paraphrase of Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 1 (site, fascicle 1: "the very ten fascicles recorded by Guanding at Yuquan Monastery in Jingzhou").

The great carriage. The Lotus parable of the great white-ox carriage, which the Mohe Zhiguan applies to the ten modes; attendants, ox, and adornments follow the parable's wording.

"What [the Tathāgata] sought with diligence across accumulated kalpas." Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 5, end of the list of the ten modes (site, fascicle 5). Zhanran calls this "the passage on the sequence of arising" (生起文).

Guangzhai's four onenesses (光宅四一). Fayun of Guangzhai's scheme of the oneness of teaching, practice, person, and principle, discussed at length in the Profound Meaning (site: "the four onenesses").

Peaceful dharma, four conditions. 安樂 echoes the Lotus chapter the site titles "The Four Peaceful Practices." 四緣 is translated literally; it may refer to those four practices, but the text does not say so.

Scripture quotations in the second interpretive principle. "It shines first on the high mountains" (Avataṃsaka) and "from the cow comes milk" (Nirvāṇa) are the passages the Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 1, uses to prove the three kinds of calming-and-contemplation (site, fascicle 1). "With different skillful means" is the Lotus, "Skillful Means" chapter. "When first seated beneath the buddha-tree" is from the Vimalakīrti, chapter 1 verses.

Hog and golden mountain. The simile appears in the Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 5 (site: "like a hog rubbing itself against a golden mountain"); the Great Treatise passage Zhanran has in mind was not located by text search.

Interpretive vocabulary. 體勢 is rendered "structure and momentum," 文相 "textual features," 合 (in 法喻合) "application" of a simile, 並 "parallel objection," 折攝 "subduing and embracing."

Apparent copying errors. 網日易存 is read as 網目易存 ("the meshes are easily kept"); 竪破法偏 as 竪破法遍. In the final list, 體折 is translated "embodying and subduing"; alternatively 折 is an error for 析, giving the familiar pair "embodying and analyzing."

Placing poison. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra simile, cited in Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 1, to prove the indeterminate (site: "Place poison in milk, and the milk alone can kill a person.").

"Each illuminates, moistens, guides, and reaches the other." Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 5, opening of the actual practice; quoted in the site's wording.

"One cannot pursue a shared name to seek a distinct essence." Introduced with "hence it says," but not found elsewhere by text search; source not located.

The ten great (十大). On entering the first abiding the ten modes "change their name to the ten great"; the text does not supply a noun, and none is added.
