The Sixth and Seventh Principles: Understanding and Practice; Against the "Sudden-Sudden"

The second fascicle holds the last two principles. The sixth, short and constructive, shows how the ten sections of the Mohe Zhiguan divide into understanding (the first five) and practice (the preliminary expedients and the actual practice), and why no section can be dropped; it compares the ten objects and ten modes to the ground, seed, rain, sprout, trunk, leaves, flowers, and fruit of a tree, and the layers of the text to marrow, bones, flesh, and skin. The seventh is a long refutation, in thirty-seven rounds of question, answer, and "Clarification," of a fellow Tiantai student who divided the perfect teaching into a "sudden-sudden" (identified with the Avataṃsaka) and a lesser "gradual-sudden" or "gradual-perfect" (to which he assigned the Lotus and even the perfect-and-sudden calming-and-contemplation). Zhanran answers point by point from the Profound Meaning's eight teachings and five flavors, from the wording of the Mohe Zhiguan and its preface, and from the stages of the six identities, insisting that the Lotus is the one fully revealed perfect teaching and that contemplation cannot depart from the teaching. Readers will meet many technical pairs here — relative and absolute, the mere middle and the perfect middle, the four abodes of affliction — that the earlier parts introduce. The work closes with a four-line verse of dedication.

The Sixth Principle: Understanding and Practice Supporting Each Other

Chinese Source

◎第六行解相資例者，如「分別」中總以十義分別十章，於中且約自行化他則前八自行，於中去果論因則果報為果，非今正意。前七為因，正明修相。於七因中，前五生解，後二為行。分別文中雖以起教取譬於自，起教化他義當於解，越次取文兼化他故，故自行解唯前五章是也。「大意」雖有行及果報等文，但是示行及果報等令知始末，非謂即是修行相也。何者？修行俱須二十五法以為方便，十乘、十境以為正修。所以者何？若無十境乘則無體，若無十法名壞驢車，故知必須五章以生妙解。於生解中，「大意」則略解始終、自他因果，則文略而意寬；次四專在名體，則文理俱廣。故以廣解導於行始，使二十五法隨教甄分，會開廢麁，方可得名妙行之首也。是故五章，一不可廢。若用此解而修十法，則但釋十法名宗，次第於理自足。而今文中相猶廣者，為鈍根者仍恐不曉觀法次第，故引前解入觀委論。又恐繁文，故於「陰入」具釋十法，九境比知，是故諸文不無旁正。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Sixth Principle, Understanding and Practice Supporting Each Other: as in "distinguishing," where the ten sections are distinguished in general by ten meanings. Among them, taking self-practice and teaching others, the first eight are self-practice. Among these, setting aside the fruit to discuss the cause, "fruits and rewards" is the fruit, which is not the present principal intent. The first seven are the cause, directly clarifying the features of cultivation. Among the seven causes, the first five produce understanding and the last two are practice. Although in the "distinguishing" passage "the teachings that arise" is taken as a simile for oneself, the teaching of others in "the teachings that arise" corresponds in meaning to understanding; it takes the text out of order because it includes teaching others. Hence understanding for self-practice is only the first five sections. Though "the great intent" has passages on practice, on fruits and rewards, and so on, these only show practice, fruits and rewards, and so on so that one knows beginning and end; it is not that they are themselves the features of practice. Why? Practice in every case needs the twenty-five methods as the expedients and the ten modes and ten objects as the actual cultivation. Why so? Without the ten objects, the vehicle has no essence; without the ten modes, it is called a broken donkey cart. Hence one knows that the five sections are needed to produce wondrous understanding. Within producing understanding, "the great intent" briefly explains beginning and end, self and other, cause and fruit, so its text is brief and its meaning broad; the next four are wholly about name and essence, so both text and principle are extensive. Hence extensive understanding is used to guide the beginning of practice, so that the twenty-five methods are distinguished according to the teachings; merging, opening, and discarding the coarse, only then can they be called the head of wondrous practice. Therefore of the five sections not one can be discarded. If one used this understanding to cultivate the ten modes, one would only explain the names and purport of the ten modes, and the order would be sufficient in principle of itself. Yet the features in the present text are still extensive because, for those of dull faculties, it still fears they will not understand the order of the method of contemplation, so it draws the earlier understanding into contemplation and discusses it in detail. Again, fearing a cumbersome text, it explains the ten modes in full under "the aggregates and entries," and the nine objects are known by comparison; therefore the passages are not without auxiliary and principal.

Ru-Yi Translation

Take the ten objects: they are simply the ground on which a single thought of mind practices. Each one showing the different features of the object is the seed of practice. Each one giving rise to the ten modes of contemplation is the rain of practice. Each one turning into the inconceivable object is the sprout of practice. Each arousing of the mind is the trunk of practice. Each settling of the mind is the leaves of practice. Each breaking through every dharma, up to the principal and auxiliary, is the flowers of practice. Each of the stages, up to freedom from attachment, is the fruit of practice. Without these six things, the tree of the path does not stand straight. Though the order is so, speaking in terms of the person, those of superior faculties produce the fruit right from the seed of the object; hence the text says, "merely on hearing these words, the illness is cured." For those of middling and lesser faculties, the later methods are needed; therefore the text says, "they go to the elder, and he compounds the many medicines."

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, in the ten modes, each one must again be understood as to the purport of the text: that each relies entirely on the inconceivable calming-and-contemplation of stillness and illumination is the marrow of the text; the order of arising of the features of each mode is the bones of the text; that each draws on matters to help complete the features of practice is the flesh of the text; the broad refutation of the ancients and the old [views], and the questions and answers resolving doubts, are the skin of the text. Again, the four passages of "explaining the name" and so on combine the sense of skin and the intent of flesh, and are the bones; where the intent leads below is the marrow. Without these four things, the Dharma-body is not complete. Therefore readers and practitioners must know what is slack and what is urgent, and must not wrongly point to partial words and one-sided intents, making practice incomplete. How could the course of practice go beyond gathering understanding and raising expedients? Practicing the cause, one obtains the fruit; the fruit full, one teaches others; others' capacities [appeal], and I respond; when sympathetic response ceases, self and other return together to the principle of extinction, the true nature. The intent of this one work is solely this. Hence these ten sections gather in everything without exception.

The Seventh Principle: Clarifying Doubts and Revealing the Correct

The Seventh Principle, Clarifying Doubts and Revealing the Correct: those who study this school all received from one teacher, and text and principle have been handed down without any differing interpretation. Two Kinds of Sudden?

Chinese Source

問：頓教有幾種？答：有漸頓及頓頓。喻曰：夫講貫之法先觀本文，本文立名不可取異，求異會釋仍須體同，頓頓之名經論不出、一家著述諸部所無。若名、體俱無，修行何託？若以頓頓是圓，如圓圓等，此義可爾。既分二頓，漸頓為圓，更加頓頓為何所擬？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: How many kinds of sudden teaching are there? Answer: There are the gradual-sudden and the sudden-sudden. Clarification: The method of lecturing and expounding is first to look at the original text. The names the original text sets up cannot be taken differently, and if one seeks something different in harmonizing interpretations, the essence must still be the same. The name "sudden-sudden" does not come out of the sūtras and treatises, and is absent from all the works our school has composed. If both name and essence are lacking, on what does practice rely? If "sudden-sudden" means the perfect, like "perfect-perfect" and so on, this meaning might be acceptable. But since you divide two suddens and make the gradual-sudden the perfect, what is the further addition of the sudden-sudden meant to represent?

Chinese Source

問：此二位者斷惑何殊？答：二位不同。若漸頓者，初住已前四住先除。若頓頓者，初住已前圓伏五住，登住已去圓破五住。喻曰：初住已前四住先除，引證屬圓，處處皆爾，故圓教四念處云「如冶鐵作器，本為成器非為除垢，麁垢先除非關漸次」。頓頓既云登住圓破，即顯住前五住全在，當知此義非別非圓。圓則初住唯破無明，不應入住五住俱斷。別須住前五住全在，住破四住，行破塵沙，登地方破一品無明，故知非別。離二別立，無教可憑。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: How do these two stages differ in severing delusion? Answer: The two stages are not the same. In the gradual-sudden, before the first abiding the four abodes are removed first. In the sudden-sudden, before the first abiding the five abodes are perfectly subdued, and from ascending to the abidings on, the five abodes are perfectly broken. Clarification: That the four abodes are removed first before the first abiding is cited everywhere as proof belonging to the perfect; hence the perfect-teaching [section] of the Four Foundations of Mindfulness says: "It is like smelting iron to make a vessel: the aim is to make the vessel, not to remove the dross; the coarse dross is removed first, [but] this has nothing to do with gradualness." Since the sudden-sudden is said to break [the five abodes] perfectly on ascending to the abidings, this shows that before the abidings the five abodes are wholly present; one should know this meaning is neither the distinct nor the perfect. In the perfect, the first abiding breaks only ignorance; it should not be that on entering the abidings all five abodes are severed. In the distinct, before the abidings the five abodes must be wholly present; the abidings break the four abodes, the practices break dust-and-sand, and only on ascending to the grounds is one grade of ignorance broken; hence one knows it is not the distinct. Setting up something separate apart from these two has no teaching to rely on.

問：二頓修成，其相何別？答：漸頓觀者空觀先成，頓頓觀者三觀俱證。喻曰：此甚違背一家教文。既云漸圓是四教中圓，應依六即判此圓位，則不應云空觀先成。何者？五品即是觀行三觀，六根即是相似三觀，初住已去分證三觀，如何乃云空觀先成？又復不識見思先落似位之意，若先成者，何名似即？頓頓既云三觀俱證，為是何位？若在初住，與漸頓何別？若在住前，都無此理。若云住前但伏、初住俱斷，諸教無文，方成邪說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: In the cultivation and accomplishment of the two suddens, how do their features differ? Answer: In the contemplation of the gradual-sudden, the contemplation of emptiness is accomplished first; in the contemplation of the sudden-sudden, the three contemplations are realized together. Clarification: This goes far against the teaching texts of our school. Since you say the gradual-perfect is the perfect of the four teachings, its perfect stages should be judged by the six identities; then one should not say the contemplation of emptiness is accomplished first. Why? The five grades are the three contemplations of contemplative practice, the six faculties are the three contemplations of resemblance, and from the first abiding on are the three contemplations of partial realization; how can one say the contemplation of emptiness is accomplished first? Moreover, you do not recognize the intent of views and habituated thought falling away first at the stage of resemblance; if [emptiness] were accomplished first, why call it identity in resemblance? Since the sudden-sudden is said to realize the three contemplations together, which stage is this? If at the first abiding, how does it differ from the gradual-sudden? If before the abidings, there is no such principle at all. If you say that before the abidings they are only subdued and at the first abiding all are severed, there is no text for it in any teaching, and it becomes a deviant teaching.

Chinese Source

問：據何得知有二種頓？答：準《玄文》「八教」，謂漸、頓、祕密、不定。漸又四，謂藏、通、別、圓，此四兼前名為八教。漸中既有最後一圓，漸外又復更立一頓，故知前圓但是漸圓，別立一頓即是頓頓。頻將此義以難他人，他無對者唯我獨知。喻曰：依此所判則有多妨。一者不識教名之妨。別立一頓乃是華嚴最初頓部，佛初成道未游諸會，不從漸來直說於大，大部在初故名為頓，部仍兼別，不得妙名，豈以兼別之經翻為頓頓，《法華》獨顯却號漸圓？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: On what basis do you know there are two kinds of sudden? Answer: According to the "eight teachings" of the Profound Text, namely gradual, sudden, secret, and indeterminate. The gradual is again four, namely Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect; these four together with the former are called the eight teachings. Since within the gradual there is the final perfect, and outside the gradual a sudden is further set up, one knows that the former perfect is only the gradual-perfect, and the separately established sudden is the sudden-sudden. I have repeatedly used this meaning to challenge others; none of them could answer — I alone know it. Clarification: According to this judgment there are many faults. First, the fault of not recognizing the names of the teachings. The separately established sudden is the sudden work of the Avataṃsaka at the very beginning: when the Buddha first attained the path, before he had traveled to the various assemblies, not coming by way of the gradual, he directly taught the great; the great work being at the beginning, it is called sudden. The work still combines the distinct and does not get the name "wondrous." How could a sūtra that combines the distinct be turned into the sudden-sudden, while the Lotus, which alone reveals [the perfect], is on the contrary called the gradual-perfect?

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the fault of not recognizing the opening of the gradual. As for "opening the gradual," it follows the Profound [Meaning] of the Lotus: after the sudden of the Avataṃsaka, separately for those of small capacity, without moving and descending, [the Buddha] bestowed the gradual teaching; at the beginning of the gradual teaching he first taught the Tripiṭaka; after the Tripiṭaka teaching, rebuking and refining, he then used all four; hence it says they are "opened out." Hence in the Profound Text, everything from the Deer Park up to the Prajñā assembly is called gradual; how could the perfect teaching within this gradual therefore be called the gradual-perfect? Moreover, in the tenth [fascicle] of the Profound [Meaning], in classifying the teachings by gradual and sudden, everything from the Avataṃsaka up to the Prajñā assembly has gradual and sudden. The perfect teaching of the Avataṃsaka and the perfect within the Vaipulya and the Prajñā — the perfect being no different, they should all alike be called sudden-sudden; why only the Avataṃsaka? If the perfect within the Vaipulya and the Prajñā is called gradual-perfect, then the perfect teaching of the Avataṃsaka is also called gradual-sudden; what has it to do with the other works? Third, the fault of not recognizing the essence of the teachings. If the gradual opens out into four, it is like opening a fist into fingers: there are only fingers and no fist; combining the four into the gradual is like combining fingers into a fist: there is only the fist and no fingers. Keeping the gradual, the teachings are only four; dropping the gradual, the teachings are only seven; keeping both, one side must lack an essence; setting up eight, the essence is narrow and the name broad.

Chinese Source

四者抑挫《法華》之妨。近代判教多以《華嚴》為根本法輪，以《法華》為枝末法輪，唯天台大師靈鷲親承，大蘇妙悟，自著章疏以十義比之，迹門尚殊，本門永異。故《玄文》中凡諸解釋，皆先約教判則三麁一妙，次約味判則四麁一妙，如何以麁稱為頓頓、以妙翻作漸圓？五者不識頓名之妨。若從行為名，圓只是頓，是故舊題「圓頓止觀」。若從味立稱，則頓異於圓，故判初味云「高山頓說」。若將判味兼帶之頓以斥判教獨顯之圓，一何誤哉，一何誤哉！六者違拒本宗之妨。本師贊為獨妙，學者毀為漸圓，抑實揚權，有何利益？七者違文背義之妨。經云「已說今說當說，而於其中《法華》第一」。《華嚴》至《般若》名已說，《無量義經》名今說，《大般涅槃經》名當說，依彼所判則已說第一，何關法華？如此獨知，聞者掩耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the fault of suppressing the Lotus. Recent classifications of the teachings mostly take the Avataṃsaka as the root Dharma-wheel and the Lotus as the branch Dharma-wheel. Only the Great Master of Tiantai, who received it in person at Vulture Peak and awakened wondrously at Dasu, in composing his own commentaries compared them by ten meanings: even the trace gate is distinct, and the origin gate forever different. Hence throughout the explanations in the Profound Text, first judging in terms of the teachings, three are coarse and one wondrous; next judging in terms of the flavors, four are coarse and one wondrous. How can the coarse be called sudden-sudden and the wondrous be turned into the gradual-perfect? Fifth, the fault of not recognizing the name "sudden." If it is named from practice, the perfect is just the sudden; hence the old title "Perfect-and-Sudden Calming-and-Contemplation." If it is named from the flavors, then the sudden differs from the perfect; hence in judging the first flavor it says "the sudden teaching on the high mountains." To take the sudden that combines and accompanies, from the judging of the flavors, and use it to rebuke the perfect that alone is revealed, from the classifying of the teachings — how mistaken, how mistaken! Sixth, the fault of defying our own school. Our original master praised it as uniquely wondrous; a student disparages it as gradual-perfect. Suppressing the real and exalting the provisional — what benefit is there? Seventh, the fault of going against the text and turning from the meaning. The sūtra says: "Already taught, now taught, and to be taught — and among them the Lotus is foremost." The Avataṃsaka up to the Prajñā is called "already taught," the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings is called "now taught," and the Great Parinirvāṇa Sūtra is called "to be taught." According to your judgment, the "already taught" would be foremost; what has it to do with the Lotus? Such "knowing alone" — those who hear it cover their ears.

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Opening four from the gradual, together with the former there are only seven; how do they make eight teachings? Answer: After the four are opened out, there is still one gradual. Clarification: As in the third fault above, the delusion and error can be fully known. Since he does not know that the gradual runs from the Deer Park up to the Prajñā, how will he set up a separate gradual teaching? If he knew that from the Deer Park up to the Prajñā it is called gradual in terms of time, he would never casually judge the perfect of the four teachings to be the gradual-perfect — how could he judge the Lotus inferior to the milk teaching?

問：法華經部為是何頓？知非頓頓。喻曰：此師非但不識頓漸之名，亦乃不曉結文之意。《玄文》釋前四味教竟，次以漸等結釋《法華》云「非頓、漸、祕密、不定」。初云「今《法華》是顯露非祕密，是定非不定」，結非祕密、不定，祕密、不定通前四時。次云「是漸頓非漸漸」，結非前頓後漸教。言漸頓者，約前四時，漸中有頓、頓中有漸。今《法華經》迹門圓說，與漸頓中其義不殊，但異漸中漸耳。言漸漸者，鹿苑一，方等三，般若二，頓中之漸，即是別教，與漸中漸其義不殊，故不須簡。頓中之頓同漸中之頓，亦同《法華》，是故頓教不須別簡。故《玄》後文「今《法華》迹門與諸經有同有異」。本門與諸經一向異同者，謂諸部中圓異者，謂諸部中兼於三教。不見此意，望聲釋義，便謂法華但是漸頓非頓頓也。文中只云「非是漸漸」，何曾復云不是頓頓？

Ru-Yi Translation

Here "general cultivation" means that the four samādhis gather in all practices; hence it is called general, [but] he on the contrary takes it as particular. Recollection of the Buddha, in gathering in practices in general, is not universal; it is one within the general, hence it is called particular, [but] he on the contrary calls it general — deeply unacceptable.

Translator's notes

The opponent. Zhanran does not name the "deviant" student; he says only that he received the teaching from "our mountain gate." Later Tiantai writers discuss who he was; that question is left to them.

"Merely on hearing these words, the illness is cured" / "they go to the elder." Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 8, the object of illness, quoting the Golden Light Sūtra; wording follows the site (site: Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 8).

Six things. The sixth principle names eight parts of the tree (ground, seed, rain, sprout, trunk, leaves, flowers, fruit) but then says "without these six things"; perhaps the six from seed to flowers are meant. Translated as written.

The three similes and the preface. "One who has mastered flight rising into the sky," "Setting the gradual and the indeterminate aside . we turn to the sūtras themselves to clarify the perfect-and-sudden further," and "a single color, a single scent — none of it is anything other than the middle way" are from Guanding's preface to the Mohe Zhiguan and follow the site's fascicle 1. "Again, resolving it through a single calming-and-contemplation" is the site's rendering of fasc. 1 (the conclusion of arousing the mind).

Walking, horse, supernatural power. Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 7, distinguishing blockage from passage: "Emptiness identical with the three contemplations breaks . walking; the provisional . riding a horse; the middle . supernatural power" (site, fascicle 7).

Father and son as strangers. Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 1, on the four kinds of four truths; the site's wording ("a father and son who each mistake the other for a stranger on the road"; "two people who are not truly kin") is reused. The opponent's quotation 實非父子 conflates the two halves of Zhiyi's simile.

Lotus quotations. "Already taught, now taught, and to be taught" condenses the "Dharma Teacher" chapter (site: ".among all the sūtras I have taught, this Lotus Sūtra is foremost of all"); "The bodhisattvas hearing this Dharma have all removed their nets of doubt" is from "Skillful Means"; the dust-mote figures are from "Discerning Merit." Wording here is the translator's, as the site's verse renderings are not lined up with the Chinese.

Humane Kings. "Forever parted from the wheel (or sea) of suffering" and "the three worthies and ten sages dwell in [the land of] fruit and reward" are from the Renwang Jing; the latter line was not verified against T245/T246 by text search.

Four Foundations of Mindfulness. Zhiyi's Si Nianchu (T1918); the smelting-iron simile and "not only consciousness-only, but also form-only." are cited from its perfect-teaching section.

"Not abiding in the subdued." Vimalakīrti, chapter 5; cited in Mohe Zhiguan, fasc. 8, the object of affliction. 斥失玄 is translated "rebuking a loss of the profound"; alternatively 玄 names a person or text, which could not be identified.

Guanding's Commentary on the Nirvāṇa. The Da Banniepan Jing Shu (T1767). The sentence on the "non-sequential five practices" the opponent cites is, Zhanran says, not in it.

Mouse and bird. 鼠即鳥空 is read as 鼠唧鳥空, the stock image (also in the Mohe Zhiguan) of a mouse's squeak "ji" and a bird's cry "kong" mimicking "realization" and "emptiness" without meaning.

Apparent copying errors. 喻四 for 喻曰; 若破光宅 probably 苦破 ("refute painstakingly"); 大異 for 大意; 聲問 for 聲聞; the gap 餘□則無 is marked. 頓及頓頓 in the question on the three dharmas probably means 漸頓及頓頓.

Obscure sentences. 實得說尚招愆違想，故當重釁: alternatively, "even one who has truly attained invites blame by speaking of it; going against [that] thought, [a false claim] is a grave offense." 無將此異質竅他人: alternatively, "do not use such strange stuff to trouble others." 頑境體心、踏心 is translated word for word.

Closing verse. Four five-character lines; 貶量失 is read as "belittle [others] or measure [their] faults."
