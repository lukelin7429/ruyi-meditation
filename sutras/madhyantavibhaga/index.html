Maitreya · Vasubandhu · Xuánzàng · 辯中邊論

Distinguishing the Middle from Every Extreme.

The Madhyāntavibhāga — "Distinguishing the Middle from the Extremes" — opens with a single verse naming its entire program: characteristics, obstructions, reality, the cultivation of remedies, the stages of that cultivation, the fruit attained, and the unsurpassed vehicle. Maitreya's root verses, expounded by Vasubandhu's prose commentary, work through false discrimination and the three natures, a full catalogue of what obstructs the path, ten kinds of reality, the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment, eighteen stages from the spiritual lineage to buddhahood, five fruits of practice, and — in the treatise's longest chapter by far — the unsurpassed vehicle itself, closing with the treatise's own explanation of its name. This page is the entrance for reading it part by part.

How to read this collection

Unlike this site's Viṃśatikā, the Madhyāntavibhāga's root verses and prose commentary belong to two different authors: Maitreya's verses, and Vasubandhu's own long-form exposition beneath them. The treatise's own seven-part structure already gives it a natural shape, so this site follows it directly — one page per topic, except the seventh and by far the longest, "the unsurpassed vehicle," which is split across two pages at a seam in Vasubandhu's own argument.

For a second layer of reading, we follow Kuījī, Xuánzàng's direct disciple, whose commentary (辯中邊論述記, T1835) preserves the same "Xuánzàng system" of terminology already running through this site's Cheng Weishi Lun, Mahāyānasaṃgraha, and Viṃśatikā.

Read by Part

The treatise's own seven topics, worked out across eight pages, from false discrimination's own middle way to the unsurpassed vehicle's three kinds of supremacy.

Parts 1–3 ·境

Characteristics, Obstructions, Reality

False discrimination and the three natures, the sixteen emptinesses, and the mind's original purity; the five kinds of obstruction and the thirty obstacles blocking the path; and ten kinds of reality, from the four noble truths to a closing set of ten skillful means.

Parts 4–6 · 行

Cultivation, Stages, Fruit

The thirty-seven factors of enlightenment as the direct remedy for every obstruction catalogued; the entire path compressed into eighteen named stages; and the five classical fruits of practice, renamed ten further ways.

Parts 7–8 · 果

The Unsurpassed Vehicle

Six correct practices — including the treatise's famous ten inversions and ten vajra-verses, and a Middle Way threading fifteen extremes from the Ratnakūṭa Sūtra — the object relied on, realization through cultivation, and the treatise's own account of its name.

Part 1 · 辯相品第一 Distinguishing the Characteristics False discrimination, the three natures, the sixteen emptinesses, and the mind's original purity. Complete. Part 2 · 辯障品第二 Distinguishing the Obstructions Nine fetters, thirty obstacles to ten good qualities, and the obstructions blocking every factor of enlightenment, perfection, and ground. Complete. Part 3 · 辯真實品第三 Distinguishing Reality Ten kinds of reality, all built from the three natures, including the four noble truths and ten skillful means. Complete. Part 4 · 辯修對治品第四 Distinguishing the Cultivation of the Remedies The thirty-seven factors of enlightenment as one continuous progression, from mindfulness to the eightfold noble path. Complete. Part 5 · 辯修分位品第五 Distinguishing the Stages of Cultivation The entire path in eighteen named stages, folded into three: impure, partly pure, and fully pure. Complete. Part 6 · 辯得果品第六 Distinguishing the Fruit Attained Five classical fruits of cultivation, renamed ten further ways along the path. Complete. Part 7 · 辯無上乘品第七 (一) The Unsurpassed Vehicle, Part One Twelve excellences shared by all ten perfections, and the practice of intention sustained by three kinds of wisdom. Complete. Part 8 · 辯無上乘品第七 (二) The Unsurpassed Vehicle, Part Two Ten inversions, ten vajra-verses, a Middle Way among fifteen extremes, and the treatise's own name explained. Complete.

Translation Notes

Recurring terms, kept consistent from Part 1 through Part 8, and matched to the site's other Yogācāra pages where the same term appears. See also this site's note on translation method.

虛妄分別 — false discriminationAbhūtaparikalpa; matches the Mahāyānasaṃgraha's rendering of the same term. The treatise's entire subject: consciousness constructing an apparent object and subject where neither truly exists.
所取、能取 — the grasped and the grasperThe apparent object and apparent subject false discrimination projects — matches the site's Viṃśatikā usage.
遍計所執 / 依他起 / 圓成實(自性) — the discriminated, attached-to nature / the dependent nature / the perfectly accomplished natureKept identical to the Cheng Weishi Lun and Mahāyānasaṃgraha glossaries.
煩惱障 / 所知障 — the afflictive obstruction / the cognitive obstructionMatches Cheng Weishi Lun usage — what blocks liberation from suffering, and what blocks full awakening.
波羅蜜多 — pāramitā, perfectionThe ten-perfection scheme (generosity, morality, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, wisdom, skillful means, the vow, power, and gnosis) matches the site's Mahāyānasaṃgraha usage.
金剛句 — vajra wordsTen terms, traced to the Ratnakūṭa Sūtra, that this treatise identifies one-to-one with its own ten inversions — named for meaning as dense and unbreakable as a diamond.
