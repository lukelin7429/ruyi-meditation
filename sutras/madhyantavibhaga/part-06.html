Distinguishing the Fruit Attained — Five Fruits, Renamed Ten Ways

This page carries Ru-Yi's translation of Part Six of the Madhyāntavibhāga. Having traced the cultivation of the path through Parts Four and Five, the treatise now asks what all that cultivation actually yields — five classical Abhidharma fruits (the ripened fruit, the dominant fruit, the outflow fruit, the fruit of endeavor, and the fruit of freedom from bondage), which the chapter's second half then renames ten further ways, showing that every one of these further terms is simply one of the original five fruits, described from the angle of where it falls along the path.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 6 of 8 complete Source: Taishō T31, no. 1600, 辯中邊論 (Maitreya, comm. Vasubandhu, tr. Xuánzàng) Commentary guide: Kuījī's 辯中邊論述記 (T1835)

Translation Method

As in Parts 1 through 5, the Chinese source is Xuánzàng's translation of Maitreya's root verses with Vasubandhu's prose commentary beneath each, read with Kuījī's 辯中邊論述記 (T1835). Like Part 5, this is a short chapter — two verses and a closing summary — so it is translated here in full within a small number of sections. See also this site's note on translation method.

1. The Five Fruits

The chapter opens with the classical Abhidharma scheme of five kinds of fruit, each named here by a single, almost homely image — a vessel, a strength, a delight, a growth, and a purity.

Chinese Source

器說為異熟，　力是彼增上，

愛樂增長淨，　如次即五果。

Ru-Yi Translation

The vessel is the ripened fruit;

the strength is its dominant fruit;

delight, growth, and purity —

these, in order, complete the five fruits.

論曰：器謂隨順善法異熟。力謂由彼器增上力，令諸善法成上品性。愛樂謂先世數修善力，今世於善法深生愛樂。增長謂現在數修善力，令所修善根速得圓滿。淨謂障斷，得永離繫。此五如次即是五果：一異熟果、二增上果、三等流果、四士用果、五離繫果。

What does this mean? "The vessel" means the karmically ripened result that accords with wholesome dharmas. "The strength" means: through that vessel's dominant power, the wholesome dharmas come to have a superior nature. "Delight" means: through the power of having repeatedly cultivated the good in a former life, one now, in this life, comes to feel deep delight in wholesome dharmas. "Growth" means: through the power of repeatedly cultivating the good in the present, the good roots one cultivates swiftly reach completion. "Purity" means: the obstruction is cut off, and one attains permanent freedom from bondage. These five, in order, are precisely the five fruits: first, the ripened fruit; second, the dominant fruit; third, the outflow fruit; fourth, the fruit of endeavor; fifth, the fruit of freedom from bondage.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī reads "the vessel" as the ordinary five aggregates a practitioner is born with, karmically shaped to be a fit container for the noble path — the ripened fruit of prior action, ethically neutral in itself. "The strength" is that same vessel's power to make one's ongoing wholesome practice more excellent — a dominant condition, not the practice's own direct result. "Delight" and "growth" are the felt, self-reinforcing side of practice: pleasure in the good from habits already built, and the actual completion of good roots being cultivated now. "Purity," finally, is unconditioned — the one fruit, Kuījī notes, that only ever arises from an unconditioned path, since anything still conditioned could never be permanently free of bondage.

2. Ten Further Names for the Same Fruits

The chapter's second verse insists these ten further terms are not a sixth category of fruit standing beside the first five, but simply the same fruits, renamed according to their position along the unfolding path — a point the prose commentary makes explicit before working through all ten.

Chinese Source

復略說餘果，　後後初數習，

究竟順障滅，　離勝上無上。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, in brief, the remaining fruits:

the subsequent, the initial, the repeatedly-practiced,

the final; the accordant, the obstruction-ending;

freedom, the excellent, the higher, and the unsurpassed.

論曰：略說餘果差別有十。一後後果，謂因種性得發心果，如是等果展轉應知。二最初果，謂最初證出世間法。三數習果，謂從此後諸有學位。四究竟果，謂無學法。五隨順果，謂因漸次應知即是後後果攝。六障滅果，謂能斷道即最初果，能滅障故說為障滅。七離繫果，謂即數習及究竟果，學無學位如次遠離煩惱繫故。八殊勝果，謂神通等殊勝功德。九有上果，謂菩薩地，超出餘乘未成佛故。十無上果，謂如來地，此上更無餘勝法故。此中所說後六種果，即究竟等前四差別。如是諸果但是略說，若廣說即無量果。

What does this mean? In brief, the remaining fruits are, in distinction, ten. First, the subsequent fruit — the fruit of arousing bodhicitta from the cause, the spiritual lineage, and so on, each fruit understood as leading, in turn, to the next. Second, the initial fruit — the very first realization of the supramundane dharma. Third, the repeatedly-practiced fruit — every stage of the trainee from this point onward. Fourth, the final fruit — the dharma of one beyond training. Fifth, the accordant fruit — the gradual progression of the cause, subsumed under the subsequent fruit. Sixth, the obstruction-ending fruit — the path capable of severance, which is precisely the initial fruit, called "obstruction-ending" because it eliminates the obstruction. Seventh, the fruit of freedom — precisely the repeatedly-practiced fruit and the final fruit, since the trainee's stage and the stage beyond training are, in order, freed from the bondage of affliction. Eighth, the excellent fruit — the excellent virtues such as the superknowledges. Ninth, the higher fruit — the bodhisattva grounds, since these surpass the other vehicles though buddhahood is not yet attained. Tenth, the unsurpassed fruit — the tathāgata ground, since beyond this there is no further, more excellent dharma. Here, the last six fruits spoken of are simply distinctions within the first four — the final fruit, and the rest. These fruits are, in this way, only briefly described; explained in full, the fruits would be without limit.

A Summary of the Whole Chapter

Vasubandhu closes the chapter by sorting its fifteen named fruits — five, then ten — under six final headings, each explaining why a given fruit needed its own name at all.

論曰：總義者，謂攝受故、差別故、宿習故、後後引發故、標故、釋故。此中攝受者，謂五果。差別者，謂餘果。宿習者，謂異熟果。後後引發者，謂餘四果。標者，謂後後等四果。釋者，謂隨順等六果，分別前四果故。

The general meaning: through embracing; through distinction; through prior habituation; through drawing forth what follows in turn; through naming; and through explaining. Here, "embracing" means the five fruits. "Distinction" means the remaining ten fruits. "Prior habituation" means the ripened fruit specifically. "Drawing forth what follows in turn" means the remaining four of the five fruits. "Naming" means the subsequent fruit and its following three — the initial, the repeatedly-practiced, and the final. "Explaining" means the accordant fruit and the remaining five, since these further distinguish the first four.

Part Six Complete

This closes Part Six of Ru-Yi's translation: the five classical fruits of cultivation, and the ten further names the treatise shows to be nothing more than these same five fruits, seen from the angle of where each falls along the path from an ordinary being's first arousing of bodhicitta to a buddha's own unsurpassed ground.
