Distinguishing Reality — Ten Truths, All Built from Three Natures

This page carries Ru-Yi's translation of Part Three of the Madhyāntavibhāga. Where Part One distinguished the three natures in the abstract, this chapter puts them to work: ten different kinds of "reality," from the four noble truths to the conventional and ultimate truths to a closing set of ten skillful means — aggregates, elements, sense-fields, dependent origination, and more — each one shown to be nothing but the three natures examined from a different angle, and all of it aimed, the chapter insists at both its opening and its close, at removing ten specific mistaken views of a self.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 3 of 8 complete Source: Taishō T31, no. 1600, 辯中邊論 (Maitreya, comm. Vasubandhu, tr. Xuánzàng) Commentary guide: Kuījī's 辯中邊論述記 (T1835)

Translation Method

As in Parts 1 and 2, the Chinese source is Xuánzàng's translation of Maitreya's root verses with Vasubandhu's prose commentary beneath each, read with Kuījī's 辯中邊論述記 (T1835). This chapter's ten kinds of reality are, in Kuījī's own reading, ultimately just one idea — the three natures — worked out ten different ways, so this page follows the treatise's own order through all ten. See also this site's note on translation method.

1. Ten Truths, Named

The chapter opens, as Part Two did, by naming its entire subject in advance — ten kinds of reality, the last of them itself tenfold, all of it aimed at a single therapeutic purpose.

Chinese Source

真實唯有十，　謂根本與相、

無顛倒、因果，　及麁細真實、

極成、淨所行、　攝受并差別，

十善巧真實，　皆為除我見。

Ru-Yi Translation

Reality is only tenfold: the root, and the characteristic;

the non-inverted, and cause-and-effect;

and the coarse-and-subtle truth; the universally established, the purified object's domain,

the embraced, and the differentiated;

the truth of the ten skillful means —

all for the sake of removing views of self.

論曰：應知真實唯有十種，一根本真實、二相真實、三無顛倒真實、四因果真實、五麁細真實、六極成真實、七淨所行真實、八攝受真實、九差別真實、十善巧真實。此復十種，為欲除遣十我見故。十善巧者，一蘊善巧、二界善巧、三處善巧、四緣起善巧、五處非處善巧、六根善巧、七世善巧、八諦善巧、九乘善巧、十有為無為法善巧。

What does this mean? Reality should be known as only tenfold: first, the root truth; second, the characteristic truth; third, the non-inverted truth; fourth, the truth of cause and effect; fifth, the coarse-and-subtle truth; sixth, the universally established truth; seventh, the truth of the purified object's domain; eighth, the embraced truth; ninth, the differentiated truth; tenth, the truth of the ten skillful means. This last is itself tenfold, in order to remove ten [mistaken] views of self: first, skill in the aggregates; second, skill in the elements; third, skill in the sense-fields; fourth, skill in dependent origination; fifth, skill in the possible and the impossible; sixth, skill in the faculties; seventh, skill in time; eighth, skill in the [four noble] truths; ninth, skill in the vehicles; tenth, skill in the conditioned and unconditioned dharmas.

2. The Root Truth: The Three Natures

The first and most basic of the ten is simply the three natures themselves — the foundation every other kind of truth in this chapter is built on.

論曰：此中云何根本真實？謂三自性，一遍計所執自性、二依他起自性、三圓成實自性，依此建立餘真實故。

What, here, is the root truth? It is the three natures: first, the discriminated, attached-to nature; second, the dependent nature; third, the perfectly accomplished nature — since every other truth is established in dependence on these.

Chinese Source

許於三自性，　唯一常非有，

一有而不真，　一有無真實。

Ru-Yi Translation

Of the three natures accepted:

one is always non-existent;

one exists but is not real;

one both exists and does not exist — [each in this way] is truth.

論曰：即於如是三自性中：遍計所執相常非有。唯常非有，於此性中許為真實，無顛倒故。依他起相有而不真。唯有非真，於依他起許為真實，有亂性故。圓成實相亦有非有。唯有非有，於此性中許為真實，有空性故。

What does this mean? Within these three natures: the character of the discriminated, attached-to nature is always non-existent — and precisely because it is always non-existent, it is accepted as truth within this nature, since seeing it so is without inversion. The character of the dependent nature exists but is not real — and precisely because it exists but is not real, it is accepted as truth within the dependent nature, since it has the nature of confusion. The character of the perfectly accomplished nature both exists and does not exist — and precisely because it both exists and does not exist, it is accepted as truth within this nature, since it has the nature of emptiness.

3. The Characteristic Truth

The second truth names what, specifically, one comes to see correctly about the person and about dharmas once the three natures are understood: neither the exaggerated reality a naïve view assigns them, nor the flat non-existence a nihilistic denial assigns them, but exactly what each nature's own character actually is.

Chinese Source

於法數取趣，　及所取能取，

有非有性中，　增益損減見；

知此故不轉，　是名真實相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Regarding dharmas and the person who takes rebirth,

and regarding the grasped and the grasper,

and regarding what exists and does not exist,

there are views that exaggerate and views that deny;

knowing this, such views do not arise —

this is called the characteristic of truth.

論曰：於一切法補特伽羅所有增益及損減見，若知此故彼便不轉，是遍計所執自性真實相。於諸所取能取法中所有增益及損減見，若知此故彼便不轉，是名依他起自性真實相。於有非有所有增益及損減見，若知此故彼便不轉，是名圓成實自性真實相。此於根本真實相中無顛倒故，名相真實。

What does this mean? Regarding all dharmas and the person, there are views that exaggerate their reality and views that deny them; knowing this, such views do not arise — this is the characteristic truth of the discriminated, attached-to nature. Regarding all grasped and grasping dharmas, there are views that exaggerate and views that deny; knowing this, such views do not arise — this is the characteristic truth of the dependent nature. Regarding what exists and does not exist, there are views that exaggerate and views that deny; knowing this, such views do not arise — this is the characteristic truth of the perfectly accomplished nature. Since this is free of inversion within the character of the root truth, it is called the characteristic truth.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī flags the obvious question this raises: the root truth is already the three natures, so how does the characteristic truth differ from it? His answer is that the root truth simply states what the three natures are, in general, while the characteristic truth specifically isolates the two opposite errors — exaggeration and denial — that a mistaken view of each nature falls into, and shows how correctly knowing each nature's own character prevents both. The two truths look at the same three natures from two different angles: what they are, and what goes wrong when they are misperceived.

4. The Non-Inverted Truth: Four Inversions, Each Threefold

The classical four inversions — mistaking what is impermanent for permanent, suffering for pleasant, empty for substantial, and non-self for a self — are remedied by impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self themselves. This chapter's distinctive move is to show that each of these four remedies is, in turn, threefold, one aspect per nature.

論曰：無顛倒真實者，謂無常、苦、空、無我性，由此治彼常等四倒。

The non-inverted truth is the nature of impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self, by which the four inversions — the belief in permanence and the rest — are remedied.

Chinese Source

無性與生滅、　垢淨三無常；

所取及事相、　和合苦三種；

空亦有三種，　謂無、異、自性；

無相及異相、　自相三無我。

如次四三種，　依根本真實。

Ru-Yi Translation

No-nature, arising-and-ceasing, and defilement-and-purity —

the three kinds of impermanence;

the grasped, the thing's own character, and combination —

the three kinds of suffering;

emptiness likewise has three kinds:

non-existence, difference, and own-nature;

signlessness, difference of character, and own-character —

the three kinds of non-self.

In this order, each of the four has three kinds,

according to the root truth.

論曰：無常三者，一無性無常，謂遍計所執，此常無故；二生滅無常，謂依他起，有起盡故；三垢淨無常，謂圓成實，位轉變故。苦三種者，一所取苦，謂遍計所執，是補特伽羅法執所取故；二事相苦，謂依他起，三苦相故；三和合苦，謂圓成實，苦相合故。

What does this mean? The three kinds of impermanence: first, no-nature impermanence — the discriminated, attached-to nature, since it is always non-existent; second, arising-and-ceasing impermanence — the dependent nature, since it has arising and cessation; third, defilement-and-purity impermanence — the perfectly accomplished nature, since its stage undergoes transformation. The three kinds of suffering: first, the suffering of the grasped — the discriminated, attached-to nature, since it is what the mistaken grasping at a person and at dharmas takes hold of; second, the suffering of the thing's own character — the dependent nature, since it bears the character of the three kinds of suffering; third, the suffering of combination — the perfectly accomplished nature, since it stands paired with the character of suffering.

論曰：空有三者，一無性空，謂遍計所執，此無理趣可說為有，由此非有說為空故；二異性空，謂依他起，如妄所執不如是有，非一切種性全無故；三自性空，謂圓成實，二空所顯為自性故。無我三者，一無相無我，謂遍計所執，此相本無故名無相，即此無相說為無我；二異相無我，謂依他起，此相雖有而不如彼遍計所執故名異相，即此異相說為無我；三自相無我，謂圓成實，無我所顯以為自相，即此自相說為無我。

The three kinds of emptiness: first, the emptiness of no-nature — the discriminated, attached-to nature, since there is no rational way it could be said to exist, and this very non-existence is called emptiness; second, the emptiness of difference-of-nature — the dependent nature, since it does not exist in the way falsely attached to, though it is not utterly non-existent in every respect; third, the emptiness of own-nature — the perfectly accomplished nature, since it takes as its own nature exactly what the twofold emptiness reveals. The three kinds of non-self: first, signless non-self — the discriminated, attached-to nature, since this character is originally non-existent and so is called signless, and this very signlessness is called non-self; second, non-self of a different character — the dependent nature, since this character, though it exists, is not as the discriminated, attached-to nature takes it to be, and so is called a different character, and this very difference is called non-self; third, non-self of one's own character — the perfectly accomplished nature, since what non-self reveals is taken as its own character, and this very own character is called non-self.

5. The Truth of Cause and Effect: The Four Noble Truths, Each Threefold

The same pattern — one nature-based aspect per triad — is now applied to the four noble truths, with the suffering already analyzed in the previous section simply carried over.

Chinese Source

苦三相已說，　集亦有三種，

謂習氣、等起，　及相未離繫。

自性、二不生、　垢寂二三滅。

遍知及永斷、　證得三道諦。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three characteristics of suffering have already been explained;

the origin, too, has three kinds:

habituated tendency, active arising,

and what is not yet freed from bondage to its own character.

Own-nature, the non-arising of the two, and the twofold defilement-and-quiescence —

[these are] the three kinds of cessation.

Full understanding, permanent severance,

and realization — the threefold truth of the path.

論曰：苦諦有三，謂無常等四各三相，如前已說。集諦三者，一習氣集，謂遍計所執自性執習氣；二等起集，謂業煩惱；三未離繫集，謂未離障真如。滅諦三者，一、自性滅，謂自性不生故；二、二取滅，謂所取能取二不生故；三、本性滅，謂垢寂二，即擇滅及真如。道諦三者，一遍知道、二永斷道、三證得道。應知此中於遍計所執唯有遍知，於依他起有遍知及永斷，於圓成實有遍知及證得，故依此三建立道諦。

What does this mean? The truth of suffering has three aspects — the three characteristics of each of the four [impermanence, and the rest] already explained above. The truth of origin has three kinds: first, the origin of habituated tendency — the habituated tendency of attachment to the discriminated, attached-to nature; second, the origin of active arising — karma and affliction; third, the origin of what is not yet freed from bondage — suchness not yet freed of its obstruction. The truth of cessation has three kinds: first, cessation through own-nature — since the discriminated, attached-to nature never arises; second, cessation of the two graspings — since the grasped and the grasper do not arise; third, cessation of the fundamental nature — the twofold defilement-and-quiescence, namely analytical cessation and suchness. The truth of the path has three kinds: first, the path of full understanding; second, the path of permanent severance; third, the path of realization. It should be understood that, regarding the discriminated, attached-to nature, there is only full understanding; regarding the dependent nature, there is full understanding and permanent severance; regarding the perfectly accomplished nature, there is full understanding and realization — and so the truth of the path is established in dependence on these three.

6. The Coarse-and-Subtle Truth: Conventional and Ultimate

"Coarse and subtle" names the familiar pair of conventional and ultimate truth — each, once again, resolved into three kinds along the lines of the three natures, with the ultimate truth traced entirely to the one nature that is neither purely conditioned nor purely unconditioned: the perfectly accomplished.

Chinese Source

應知世俗諦，　差別有三種，

謂假、行、顯了，　如次依本三。

勝義諦亦三，　謂義、得、正行，

依本一無變，　無倒二圓實。

Ru-Yi Translation

The conventional truth should be known

as having three distinct kinds —

the merely designated, the functioning, and the manifest —

corresponding, in order, to the three root natures.

The ultimate truth, too, has three kinds:

the meaning, the attainment, and correct practice,

depending on the single unchanging root —

the perfectly accomplished nature, without inversion, in its two [conditioned and unconditioned] aspects.

論曰：世俗諦有三種，一假世俗、二行世俗、三顯了世俗。此三世俗如其次第，依三根本真實建立。勝義諦亦三種，一義勝義，謂真如勝智之境名勝義故；二得勝義，謂涅槃，此是勝果亦義利故；三正行勝義，謂聖道，以勝法為義故。此三勝義，應知但依三根本中圓成實立。此圓成實總有二種，無為、有為有差別故。無為總攝真如涅槃，無變異故，名圓成實。有為總攝一切聖道，於境無倒故，亦名圓成實。

What does this mean? The conventional truth has three kinds: first, the merely designated conventional; second, the functioning conventional; third, the manifest conventional. These three, in order, are established in dependence on the three root truths. The ultimate truth likewise has three kinds: first, the ultimate as meaning — suchness, the object of supreme wisdom, called the ultimate as meaning; second, the ultimate as attainment — nirvāṇa, the supreme fruit and also a genuine benefit; third, the ultimate as correct practice — the noble path, since it takes the supreme dharma as its meaning. These three should be understood as established only in dependence on the perfectly accomplished nature among the three roots. This perfectly accomplished nature has, in total, two kinds, distinguished as unconditioned and conditioned. The unconditioned wholly comprises suchness and nirvāṇa, since these are without change — hence called the perfectly accomplished nature. The conditioned wholly comprises the entire noble path, since it is without inversion regarding its object — hence it, too, is called the perfectly accomplished nature.

7. Two Short Truths: Universally Established, and the Purified Object's Domain

Two brief truths follow: what "everyone agrees on" actually rests on, and what remains as an object once a mind has been purified of both hindrances.

Chinese Source

世極成依一，　理極成依三。

Ru-Yi Translation

The worldly-established depends on one [root nature];

the rationally-established depends on all three.

論曰：若事世間共所安立，串習隨入覺慧所取，一切世間同執此事是地非火、色非聲等，是名世間極成真實。此於根本三真實中，但依遍計所執而立。若有理義聰叡賢善能尋思者，依止三量證成道理施設建立，是名道理極成真實。此依根本三真實立。

What does this mean? Where a matter is commonly established by worldly convention, entered into through habitual practice, and grasped by ordinary understanding — where all the world alike holds this thing to be earth and not fire, form and not sound, and so on — this is called worldly-established truth. Among the three root truths, this is established only in dependence on the discriminated, attached-to nature. Where there is a rational principle that the wise and learned, capable of careful investigation, establish and set forth by relying on the three valid means of knowledge in accordance with reasoning — this is called rationally-established truth. This is established in dependence on all three root truths.

Chinese Source

淨所行有二，　依一圓成實。

Ru-Yi Translation

The purified object's domain has two kinds,

each depending on the one perfectly accomplished nature.

論曰：煩惱、所知二障，淨智所行真實，唯依根本三真實中圓成實立，餘二非此淨智境故。

The truth that is the domain of wisdom purified of the two obstructions — the afflictive and the cognitive — is established only in dependence on the perfectly accomplished nature among the three root truths, since the other two natures are not the object of this purified wisdom.

8. The Embraced Truth, and the Differentiated Truth

Two more short truths close out the first nine. The embraced truth sorts a classical set of five categories — name, characteristic, discrimination, suchness, and correct wisdom — into the three natures; the differentiated truth does the same for seven further terms scattered across the sūtras: transmigration, true character, Consciousness-Only, establishment, wrong practice, purity, and correct practice.

Chinese Source

名遍計所執，　相分別依他，

真如及正智，　圓成實所攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Name is subsumed under the discriminated, attached-to nature;

characteristic and discrimination, under the dependent nature;

suchness and correct wisdom,

under the perfectly accomplished nature.

論曰：相等五事，隨其所應，攝在根本三種真實，謂名攝在遍計所執，相及分別攝在依他，圓成實攝真如正智。

What does this mean? The five things — characteristic and the rest — are, each as appropriate, subsumed under the three root truths: name is subsumed under the discriminated, attached-to nature; characteristic and discrimination are subsumed under the dependent nature; the perfectly accomplished nature subsumes suchness and correct wisdom.

Chinese Source

流轉與安立，　邪行依初二，

實相唯識淨，　正行依後一。

Ru-Yi Translation

Transmigration and establishment, and wrong practice,

depend on the first two natures;

true character, Consciousness-Only, and purity,

and correct practice, depend on the last.

論曰：流轉等七，隨其所應，攝在根本三種真實，謂彼流轉安立邪行依根本中遍計所執及依他起，實相唯識清淨正行依根本中圓成實立。

What does this mean? The seven — transmigration, and the rest — are, each as appropriate, subsumed under the three root truths: transmigration, establishment, and wrong practice depend, among the roots, on the discriminated, attached-to nature and the dependent nature; true character, Consciousness-Only, purity, and correct practice are established, among the roots, in dependence on the perfectly accomplished nature.

9. The Tenth Truth: Ten Skillful Means

The chapter's final and largest truth returns to the opening chapter's promise: ten specific "skillful means," each one aimed at removing a specific mistaken view of self projected onto a familiar Buddhist category — the aggregates, elements, sense-fields, dependent origination, the possible and impossible, the faculties, time, the four noble truths, the three vehicles, and the conditioned and unconditioned.

Chinese Source

非一及總略、　分段義名蘊。

Ru-Yi Translation

Non-singularity, total collection,

and division into parts — these are the meaning of "aggregate."

論曰：應知蘊義，略有三種：一、非一義，如契經言：諸所有色等，若過去若未來若現在、若內若外、若麁若細、若劣若勝、若遠若近。二、總略義，如契經言：如是一切略為一聚。三、分段義，如契經言：說名色蘊等，各別安立色等相故，由斯聚義蘊義得成。

What does this mean? The meaning of "aggregate" should be known as, in brief, threefold: first, the meaning of non-singularity, as scripture says, "all form, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, coarse or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near"; second, the meaning of total collection, as scripture says, "all this is, in brief, gathered into a single mass"; third, the meaning of division into parts, as scripture says, "this is called the aggregate of form," and so on, each characteristic separately established. By this meaning of a gathered mass, the meaning of "aggregate" is established.

Chinese Source

能所取、彼取，　種子義名界。

Ru-Yi Translation

The seed-nature of the grasper, the grasped,

and that which takes hold of both —

this is the meaning of "element."

論曰：能取種子義，謂眼等六內界。所取種子義，謂色等六外界。彼取種子義，謂眼識等六識界。

The seed-nature of the grasper means the six inner elements — the eye and the rest. The seed-nature of the grasped means the six outer elements — form and the rest. The seed-nature of that which takes hold of both means the six consciousness-elements — eye-consciousness and the rest.

Chinese Source

能受、所了境，　用門義名處。

Ru-Yi Translation

The gateway through which the receiver, and the cognized object,

[each] function —

this is the meaning of "sense-field."

論曰：此中能受受用門義，謂六內處。若所了境受用門義，是六外處。

Here, the gateway of function for the receiver is the six inner sense-fields; the gateway of function for the cognized object is the six outer sense-fields.

Chinese Source

緣起義於因、　果、用無增減。

Ru-Yi Translation

Dependent origination means:

regarding cause, effect, and function,

there is neither exaggeration nor denial.

論曰：於因、果、用若無增益及無損減，是緣起義。應知此中增益因者，執行等有不平等因。損減因者，執彼無因。增益果者，執有我行等緣無明等生。損減果者，執無明等無行等果。增益用者，執無明等於生行等有別作用。損減用者，執無明等於生行等全無功能。若無如是三增減執，應知彼於緣起善巧。

Where there is neither exaggeration nor denial regarding cause, effect, and function, this is the meaning of dependent origination. Exaggerating the cause means holding that the formations and the rest have some unequal, independent cause; denying the cause means holding they have no cause at all. Exaggerating the effect means holding that a real self, formations, and the rest arise conditioned by ignorance; denying the effect means holding that ignorance and the rest have no such effect. Exaggerating the function means holding that ignorance and the rest have some separate, real function in generating the formations; denying the function means holding they have no capacity whatsoever to do so. Where one is free of these three kinds of exaggerating and denying misapprehension, one should be known as skilled in dependent origination.

Chinese Source

於非愛、愛、淨、　俱生及勝主、

得、行不自在，　是處非處義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Regarding the undesirable, the desirable, purity,

co-arising, and supreme mastery,

attainment, and function — [each] not subject to free control —

this is the meaning of the possible and the impossible.

論曰：處非處義略由七種不得自在，應知其相。一於非愛不得自在，謂由惡行雖無愛欲而墮惡趣。二於可愛不得自在，謂由妙行雖無愛欲而昇善趣。三於清淨不得自在，謂不斷五蓋、不修七覺支，決定不能作苦邊際。四於俱生不得自在，謂一世界無二如來、二轉輪王俱時出現。五於勝主不得自在，謂女不作轉輪王等。六於證得不得自在，謂女不證獨覺無上正等菩提。七於現行不得自在，謂見諦者必不現行害生等事。

The meaning of the possible and the impossible should be known through seven ways in which one is not subject to free control. First, not free regarding the undesirable: through bad conduct, even without any wish for it, one falls into an unfortunate destiny. Second, not free regarding the desirable: through excellent conduct, even without any wish for it, one rises to a fortunate destiny. Third, not free regarding purity: without cutting off the five hindrances and cultivating the seven factors of enlightenment, one cannot possibly reach the very limit of suffering. Fourth, not free regarding co-arising: in one world-system, two tathāgatas, or two wheel-turning kings, do not appear at the same time. Fifth, not free regarding supreme mastery: a woman does not become a wheel-turning king, and so on. Sixth, not free regarding attainment: a woman does not realize solitary buddhahood or unsurpassed, perfect bodhi [in that very body]. Seventh, not free regarding function: one who has seen the truths will never actually commit an act such as killing a living being.

Feeling, and the provisions for feeling;

Ru-Yi Translation

The meaning of the conditioned and the unconditioned:

the designated, the cause, and the characteristic;

論曰：應知此中假謂名等；因謂種子所攝藏識；相謂器身并受用具，及轉識攝意取思惟；意謂恒時思量性識；取謂五識取現境故；思惟即是第六意識，以能分別一切境故。如是若假、若因、若相及相應法，總名有為。若寂靜者，謂所證滅及能證道，能寂靜故。彼所觀義，謂即真如，是寂靜道所緣境故。如是所說若諸寂靜、若所觀義，總名無為。

Here, the designated means name and the rest; the cause means the storehouse consciousness, comprising the seeds; the characteristic means the vessel-world and the body, together with the requisites of experience, and also the evolving consciousnesses, comprising manas, the grasping [five sense-consciousnesses], and thinking. "Manas" means the consciousness whose nature is constant deliberation; "that which grasps" means the five sense-consciousnesses, since they grasp their present objects; "thinking" is the sixth, mental consciousness, since it can discriminate every object. Thus the designated, the cause, the characteristic, and their conjoined dharmas are together called the conditioned. As for quiescence, it means the cessation realized and the path that realizes it, since both are capable of quiescence. Its own observed object means suchness itself, since it is the object the quiescent path takes as its condition. What has been described — quiescence, and its own observed object — is together called the unconditioned.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī notes that these ten skillful means are not an arbitrary miscellany, but exactly the ten categories a mistaken view most readily projects a "self" onto: a single owner behind the aggregates, a causal agent within the elements, an experiencer behind the sense-fields, a maker within dependent origination, a free agent behind what is really only possible or impossible, a controlling power within the faculties, a persisting entity through time, a single subject underlying the four truths, one fixed path for every practitioner regardless of vehicle, and a bound-and-freed self within the conditioned and unconditioned. Each skillful means dismantles its corresponding self-view not by denying the category outright, but by showing precisely what it is — a threefold structure resolving, in every case, into the three natures.

10. How the Ten Skillful Means Map onto the Three Natures

Having defined all ten skillful means, the treatise closes the chapter's central argument by showing, in general terms, that each of the ten aggregates, elements, sense-fields, and the rest itself has three aspects — one falsely attached to, one merely dependent, and one truly perfected — exactly paralleling the three root truths from Section 2.

Chinese Source

此所執分別，　法性義在彼。

Ru-Yi Translation

[Each of] these has an attached-to meaning, a discriminated meaning,

and a meaning that is dharma-nature itself —

[each] resides within that [same threefold root].

論曰：此蘊等十各有三義。且色蘊中有三義者，一所執義色，謂色之遍計所執性；二分別義色，謂色之依他起性，此中分別以為色故；三法性義色，謂色之圓成實性。如色蘊中有此三義，受等四蘊、界等九法各有三義，隨應當知。如是蘊等由三義別，無不攝入彼三性中，是故當知十善巧真實皆依根本三真實而立。

What does this mean? Each of these ten — the aggregates and the rest — has three meanings. Take the aggregate of form: it has three meanings. First, form as the attached-to meaning — the discriminated, attached-to nature of form. Second, form as the discriminated meaning — the dependent nature of form, since here it is discrimination itself that constitutes form. Third, form as the dharma-nature meaning — the perfectly accomplished nature of form. Just as the aggregate of form has these three meanings, so too do the remaining four aggregates, and the nine further categories — the elements and the rest — each as appropriate. In this way, the aggregates and the rest, through this threefold distinction of meaning, are entirely subsumed within the three natures — and so it should be known that the truth of the ten skillful means is, in every case, established in dependence on the three root truths.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī closes the chapter's main argument by naming what the whole ten-truth structure has been building toward: everything this chapter calls "reality" — from the four noble truths to a single sense-faculty — reduces, without remainder, to the three natures first set out in Part One. He then reads the treatise's own closing summary as a final accounting of the chapter: of the ten truths, the first three — root, characteristic, and non-inverted — are called "revealing" truths, since they directly display the three natures themselves; the remaining seven are called "revealed" truths, since each is simply the first three, applied to a further specific purpose — countering the arrogance of thinking oneself already free of self-view, treating the four inversions, distinguishing the śrāvaka's coarser liberation from the unsurpassed vehicle's, refuting rival schools, displaying the Great Vehicle's own distinctive purity, gathering all knowable things under the three natures, showing that suchness itself is never false, and — the final purpose named in this chapter's own opening verse — entering every hidden aspect of how a mistaken grasp at self actually operates, category by category.

Part Three Complete

This closes Part Three of Ru-Yi's translation: ten kinds of reality, from the three natures themselves through the four noble truths, the conventional and ultimate truths, and a closing set of ten skillful means covering the aggregates, elements, sense-fields, dependent origination, the possible and impossible, the faculties, time, the truths, the vehicles, and the conditioned and unconditioned — every one of them, in the end, nothing more than the three natures viewed from a different angle, aimed at removing ten specific ways a mistaken mind projects a self.
