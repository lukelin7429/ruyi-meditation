# Madhyāntavibhāga Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Madhyāntavibhāga* (辯中邊論, Taishō T31, no. 1600), verses attributed to Maitreya with prose commentary by Vasubandhu, translated into Chinese by Xuánzàng. They exist so that the vocabulary of this treatise stays consistent with the site's larger Yogācāra corpus, especially the *Cheng Weishi Lun*, the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*, and the *Viṃśatikā*.

## Source and independence from prior translations

- Root text and commentary: Taishō T1600, 辯中邊論 — root verses attributed to Maitreya, prose commentary (長行) by Vasubandhu, translated by Xuánzàng. Unlike the *Viṃśatikā*, where Vasubandhu is both root-verse author and commentator, here the verses and the commentary belong to two different authors — closer in structure to the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*'s root-text-plus-commentary pairing than to the *Viṃśatikā*'s single-author treatise.
- Commentary guide: Taishō T1835, 辯中邊論述記, Kuījī (窺基), Xuánzàng's direct disciple — chosen for the same reason as on this site's other Xuánzàng-lineage pages: a single consistent "Xuánzàng system" of terminology running from the root translation into the exegesis.
- This page translates directly from Xuánzàng's Chinese. Existing English translations and scholarly studies may be consulted privately to check difficult passages, but the English wording, sentence structure, and section framing on this site must be Ru-Yi's own.
- The title is given as *Madhyāntavibhāga*, "Distinguishing the Middle from the Extremes."

## Page structure

The treatise's seven topics — characteristics, obstructions, reality, the cultivation of remedies, the stages of that cultivation, the fruit attained, and the unsurpassed vehicle — already correspond to its own seven chapters (品), and both the root text and Kuījī's commentary align chapter for chapter. This site follows that structure directly, one page per chapter, except the seventh — "the unsurpassed vehicle" — which runs to nearly 40% of the entire treatise (both root text and commentary) and is split across two pages (Parts 7 and 8) at the seam between the third and fourth of its six "correct practices."

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 辯中邊論 | *Madhyāntavibhāga* | Use the full Sanskrit title on first appearance per page; "the treatise" thereafter. |
| 虛妄分別 | false discrimination | Abhūtaparikalpa. Matches the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*'s rendering of the same term (not "false imagination," despite that being more common in Western academic translations of this specific treatise) — chosen for cross-site consistency, since this treatise's opening verse is directly quoted in *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* Part 3. Part 1, section 2. |
| 所取 / 能取 | the grasped / the grasper | Matches the *Viṃśatikā* glossary. The apparent object and apparent subject false discrimination projects, neither truly existing. |
| 遍計所執(自性) / 依他起(自性) / 圓成實(自性) | the discriminated, attached-to nature / the dependent nature / the perfectly accomplished nature | Kept identical to the *Cheng Weishi Lun* and *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* glossaries throughout. |
| 空性 | emptiness | Śūnyatā; Part 1 gives its character (neither existent nor non-existent, neither identical to nor different from false discrimination), five synonyms (suchness, the reality-limit, signlessness, the ultimate nature, the dharma-realm), and sixteen distinct kinds. |
| 心性本淨，由客塵所染 | the mind's nature is originally pure, [yet] tainted by adventitious dust | The treatise's single most quoted line, closing Part 1 — read by Kuījī as strictly compatible with the three-natures framework: what is "originally pure" is the perfectly accomplished nature, never separate from false discrimination itself; what is "adventitious" is only the discriminated, attached-to nature a mistaken mind projects on top of it. |
| 煩惱障 / 所知障 | the afflictive obstruction / the cognitive obstruction | Matches *Cheng Weishi Lun* usage — what blocks liberation from suffering (which an arhat can eliminate) versus what blocks full awakening (which only a bodhisattva's path addresses). Central to Part 2. |
| 波羅蜜多 | pāramitā, "perfection" | The ten-perfection scheme — generosity, morality, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, wisdom (prajñā), skillful means, the vow, power, and gnosis (jñāna) — matches the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* glossary. Central to Parts 2 and 7. |
| 十地 | the ten (bodhisattva) grounds | Matches *Cheng Weishi Lun* and *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* usage. Part 2's dharma-realm meanings per ground directly parallel the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*'s own ten-ground account. |
| 三十七菩提分法 | the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment | The four foundations of mindfulness, four right exertions, four bases of supernormal power, five faculties, five powers, seven factors of enlightenment, and eightfold noble path — treated in Part 4 as one continuous progression rather than seven independent lists. |
| 十八分位 | the eighteen stages | Part 5's compressed map of the entire path, from a being who merely possesses the spiritual lineage to a buddha's three bodies. |
| 正行、所緣、修證(無上) | correct practice, the object relied on, and realization through cultivation | The three kinds of supremacy that together define "the unsurpassed vehicle," Parts 7–8. |
| 十無倒 / 十金剛句 | the ten inversions / the ten vajra words | Part 8's central passage: ten specific non-inverted forms of knowledge and vision, identified one-to-one with ten terms this treatise traces to the *Ratnakūṭa Sūtra* (寶積經), named for meaning as dense and unbreakable as a diamond (vajra). |
| 中道 | the Middle Way | Threaded, in Part 8, through fifteen distinct extremes drawn from the *Ratnakūṭa Sūtra* — not a single pair of opposites, but eight extremes concerning the person, dharmas, and dependent origination, plus seven further extremes concerning emptiness itself, each answered with its own scriptural simile (space, a painter, a magician, two fire-sticks, two lamps). |
