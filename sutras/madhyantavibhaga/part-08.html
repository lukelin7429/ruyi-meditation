Distinguishing the Unsurpassed Vehicle — Ten Vajra-Verses, and the Treatise's Own Name

This page carries the second half of Ru-Yi's translation of Part Seven of the Madhyāntavibhāga, and completes the treatise. It covers the remaining four of six correct practices — the practice according with the Dharma, built on the treatise's famous ten inversions and ten vajra-words; the practice free of the two extremes, a Middle Way threading fifteen distinct mistaken views drawn from the Ratnakūṭa Sūtra; and differentiated and undifferentiated practice — before turning to the chapter's remaining two supremacies, the object relied on and realization through cultivation, and closing with the treatise's own explanation of why it bears the name "Distinguishing the Middle from the Extremes."

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 8 of 8 complete — treatise complete Source: Taishō T31, no. 1600, 辯中邊論 (Maitreya, comm. Vasubandhu, tr. Xuánzàng) Commentary guide: Kuījī's 辯中邊論述記 (T1835)

Translation Method

As throughout this series, the Chinese source is Xuánzàng's translation of Maitreya's root verses with Vasubandhu's prose commentary beneath each, read with Kuījī's 辯中邊論述記 (T1835). This page picks up directly where Part 7 left off, within the treatise's third correct practice. See also this site's note on translation method.

1. The Practice According with the Dharma: Freedom from Six Kinds of Distraction

The third correct practice has two aspects: freedom from distraction, which sustains calm-abiding, and freedom from inversion, which sustains insight. This section names the six kinds of distraction a bodhisattva must recognize and dispel; the next several sections take up the ten inversions in full.

Chinese Source

隨法行二種，　謂諸無散亂、

無顛倒轉變，　諸菩薩應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

The practice according with the Dharma has two kinds:

the transformation into freedom from distraction,

and the transformation into freedom from inversion —

bodhisattvas should know these.

論曰：隨法正行略有二種，一無散亂轉變、二無顛倒轉變，菩薩於此應正了知。此中六種散亂無故，名無散亂。六散亂者，一自性散亂、二外散亂、三內散亂、四相散亂、五麁重散亂、六作意散亂。

What does this mean? The practice according with the Dharma is, in brief, of two kinds: first, the transformation into freedom from distraction; second, the transformation into freedom from inversion. Bodhisattvas should correctly understand these. Here, being free of six kinds of distraction is called freedom from distraction. The six distractions are: first, distraction of own-nature; second, external distraction; third, internal distraction; fourth, distraction of characteristic; fifth, distraction of heaviness; sixth, distraction of attention.

Chinese Source

出定、於境流、　味沈掉、矯示、

我執、心下劣，　諸智者應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Leaving concentration; flowing out toward objects;

relishing torpor and agitation; feigning [attainment];

self-attachment; a base-minded [aspiration] —

the wise should recognize these.

論曰：此中出定由五識身，當知即是自性散亂。於境流者，馳散外緣，即外散亂。味沈掉者，味著等持惛沈掉舉，即內散亂。矯示者，即相散亂，矯現相已修定加行故。我執者，即麁重散亂，由麁重力我慢現行故。心下劣者，即作意散亂，依下劣乘起作意故。菩薩於此六散亂相，應遍了知當速除滅。

What does this mean? Here, "leaving concentration" occurs through the five sense-consciousnesses — this should be known as distraction of own-nature. "Flowing out toward objects" means scattering out toward external conditions — this is external distraction. "Relishing torpor and agitation" means relishing concentration itself while torpor and agitation are present — this is internal distraction. "Feigning" is distraction of characteristic, since one feigns an outward sign [of attainment] before actually cultivating the practice of concentration. "Self-attachment" is distraction of heaviness, since, through the power of this heaviness, self-conceit becomes active. "A base-minded aspiration" is distraction of attention, since one's attention arises in dependence on an inferior vehicle. Bodhisattvas should fully understand these six characteristics of distraction, and should swiftly eliminate them.

2. Ten Inversions, Named

The chapter's most celebrated passage now begins: ten specific inversions a bodhisattva must recognize and correct, each defined by true knowledge and vision regarding one of ten matters — from the plainest question of what makes a written text meaningful, to the most difficult question of why neither fear nor pride is appropriate before emptiness itself.

Chinese Source

智見於文義，　作意及不動，

二相染淨客，　無怖高無倒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Knowledge and vision regarding the text, the meaning,

attention, and non-agitation,

the two [further] characters, defilement-and-purity, and the adventitious,

and freedom from fear and from pride — [these are] non-inversion.

論曰：依十事中如實智見，應知建立十無倒名。

What does this mean? In dependence on true knowledge and vision regarding these ten matters, the ten names of non-inversion should be understood as established.

3. The Ten Inversions, One by One

The treatise now takes up each of the ten in turn, from the most basic — what makes a written text meaningful at all — to the most demanding, regarding fear and pride before emptiness itself.

Chinese Source

知但由相應、　串習或翻此，

有義及非有，　是於文無倒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Knowing that it is only through concordance and habituation,

or their opposites,

that a text comes to have meaning, or lacks it —

this is non-inversion regarding the text.

論曰：若於諸文能無間斷次第宣唱，說名相應。共許此名唯目此事，展轉憶念名為串習。但由此二，成有義文；與此相違，文成無義。如實知見此二文者，應知是名於文無倒。

What does this mean? If, regarding any text, one is able to recite it in unbroken succession and in proper order, this is called concordance. That this name is commonly accepted to designate only this one thing, remembered again and again from time immemorial, is called habituation. Only through these two does a text come to have meaning; where they are contravened, a text comes to lack meaning. Truly knowing and seeing these two kinds of text should be understood as non-inversion regarding the text.

Chinese Source

似二性顯現，　如現實非有，

知離有非有，　是於義無倒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Appearing as if it had a twofold nature,

[yet] not truly existing as it appears —

knowing it is free of both existence and non-existence —

this is non-inversion regarding meaning.

論曰：似二性顯現者，謂似所取能取性現，亂識似彼行相生故。如現實非有者，謂如所顯現，實不如是有。離有者，謂此義所取能取性非有故。離非有者，謂彼亂識現似有故。如實知見此中義者，應知是名於義無倒。

"Appearing as if it had a twofold nature" means it appears as if it had the nature of the grasped and the grasper, since the confused consciousness arises with an aspect resembling them. "Not truly existing as it appears" means it does not truly exist in the way it appears. "Free of existence" means this meaning's nature as grasped and grasper does not exist. "Free of non-existence" means that confused consciousness appears as if it existed. Truly knowing and seeing the meaning here should be understood as non-inversion regarding meaning.

Chinese Source

於作意無倒，　知彼言熏習，

言作意彼依，　現似二因故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Non-inversion regarding attention:

knowing that the habituation of that language —

the attention rooted in language — is its basis,

since it is the cause making the two [grasped and grasper] appear.

論曰：所取能取言所熏習名言作意，即此作意是所能取分別所依，是能現似二取因故。由此作意是戲論想之所熏習名言作意，如實知見此作意者，應知是於作意無倒。

The habituation of the language of "grasped" and "grasper" is called the attention rooted in language; this very attention is the basis for the discrimination of the grasped and the grasper, since it is the cause capable of making the two graspings appear. Since this attention is habituated by the conceptual proliferation of linguistic ideation, it is called the attention rooted in language. Truly knowing and seeing this attention should be understood as non-inversion regarding attention.

Chinese Source

於不動無倒，　謂知義非有，

非無如幻等，　有無不動故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Non-inversion regarding non-agitation:

knowing that this meaning is not existent,

yet not non-existent, like an illusion and the rest —

[the mind] unmoved by either existence or non-existence.

論曰：前說諸義離有非有。此如幻等非有無故。謂如幻作諸象馬等彼非實有，象馬等性亦非全無，亂識似彼諸象馬等而顯現故。如是諸義無如現似所取能取定實有性亦非全無，亂識似彼所取能取而顯現，故等聲顯示陽焰夢境及水月等，如應當知。以能諦觀義如幻等，於有無品心不動散，如實知見此不動者，應知是於不動無倒。

It was said above that these meanings are free of both existence and non-existence, since, like an illusion and the rest, they are neither existent nor non-existent. Just as an illusion conjures elephants, horses, and the like that are not truly real, yet the nature of these elephants and horses is not utterly non-existent either — since the confused consciousness arises appearing as those elephants and horses — so too these meanings have no fixed, real nature as the apparent grasped and grasper, yet are not utterly non-existent either, since the confused consciousness appears as that grasped and grasper. The word "and the rest" indicates a mirage, a dream-object, the moon reflected in water, and the like, as should be understood accordingly. Through the ability to truly contemplate these meanings as like an illusion, the mind is unmoved and undistracted with respect to either existence or non-existence. Truly knowing and seeing this non-agitation should be understood as non-inversion regarding non-agitation.

Chinese Source

於自相無倒，　知一切唯名，

離一切分別，　依勝義自相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Non-inversion regarding own-character:

knowing that all [dharmas] are only names,

free of every discrimination —

[spoken of] in dependence on the own-character of the ultimate.

論曰：如實知見一切眼色乃至意法皆唯有名，即能對治一切分別，應知是於自相無倒。此依勝義自相而說。若依世俗非但有名可取，種種差別相故。

Truly knowing and seeing that the eye, form, and so forth, up to mind and dharmas, are all only names, one is thereby able to counteract every discrimination — this should be understood as non-inversion regarding own-character. This is spoken of in dependence on the own-character of the ultimate; with respect to the conventional, it is not the case that only a name can be apprehended, since there are various distinct characters conventionally.

Chinese Source

以離真法界，　無別有一法，

故通達此者，　於共相無倒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since, apart from the true dharma-realm,

there is not a single separate dharma,

one who penetrates this

has non-inversion regarding the universal character.

論曰：以無一法離法無我者故，真法界諸法共相攝。如實知見此共相者，應知是於共相無倒。

Since there is not a single dharma apart from the selflessness of dharmas, the true dharma-realm is subsumed under the universal character of all dharmas. Truly knowing and seeing this universal character should be understood as non-inversion regarding the universal character.

Chinese Source

知顛倒作意，　未滅及已滅，

於法界雜染，　清淨無顛倒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Knowing that, where the inverted attention

is not yet extinguished, or has already been extinguished,

the dharma-realm is [respectively called] defiled,

or pure — this is non-inversion.

論曰：若未斷滅顛倒作意，爾時法界說為雜染，已斷滅時說為清淨。如實知見此染淨者，如次是於染淨無倒。

Where the inverted attention has not yet been cut off, at that time the dharma-realm is called defiled; when it has already been cut off, it is called pure. Truly knowing and seeing this defilement and purity, in order, is non-inversion regarding defilement and purity.

Chinese Source

知法界本性，　清淨如虛空，

故染淨非主，　是於客無倒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Knowing that the dharma-realm's own nature

is pure, like space —

hence defilement and purity are not its master —

this is non-inversion regarding the adventitious.

論曰：法界本性淨若虛空，由此應知先染後淨二差別相是客非主。如實知見此客相者，應知是名於客無倒。

The dharma-realm's own nature is pure like space; by this, it should be known that the two distinct characters — first defiled, later pure — are adventitious, not its master. Truly knowing and seeing this adventitious character should be understood as non-inversion regarding the adventitious.

Chinese Source

有情法無故，　染淨性俱無，

知此無怖高，　是於二無倒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the person and dharmas do not exist,

the natures of defilement and purity likewise do not exist;

knowing this, one is without fear and without pride —

this is non-inversion regarding these two.

論曰：有情及法俱非有故，彼染淨性亦俱非有。以染淨義俱不可得，故染淨品無減無增，由此於中無怖無慢。如實知見無怖高者，應知是名於二無倒。

Since the person and dharmas alike do not exist, their natures as defiled and pure likewise do not exist. Since the meanings of defilement and purity are both unattainable, the defiled and pure classes neither decrease nor increase; hence, within this, there is neither fear nor pride. Truly knowing and seeing this freedom from fear and pride should be understood as non-inversion regarding these two.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī maps these ten onto a single path: the first three — text, meaning, and attention — concern what a mistaken mind is caught by before any real practice begins; non-agitation belongs to the level of preparation, just before the path of seeing; own-character belongs to the path of seeing itself, the first direct breakthrough; the universal character is what that breakthrough directly realizes; defilement-and-purity and the adventitious both belong to the path of cultivation that follows, watching what remains to be removed; and the final two, freedom from fear and from pride, belong only to the path beyond training, since only there is the equanimity before emptiness — neither dreading its implications nor taking pride in having understood it — finally complete and unshakeable.

4. A Summary of the Ten, and the Ten Vajra Words

Vasubandhu closes the ten inversions with his own summary of what each one accomplishes, then identifies all ten with a set of ten terms — the "vajra words" — that this treatise traces to the Ratnakūṭa Sūtra, so called because their meaning is said to be as dense and unbreakable as a diamond.

論曰：無倒行總義者，謂由文無倒，能正通達止觀二相；由義無倒，能正通達諸顛倒相；由作意無倒，於倒因緣能正遠離；由不動無倒，善取彼相；由自相無倒，修彼對治無分別道；由共相無倒，能正通達本性清淨；由染淨無倒，了知未斷及已斷障；由客無倒，如實了知染淨二相；由無怖無高二種無倒，諸障斷滅得永出離。

What does this mean? The general meaning of non-inverted practice: through non-inversion regarding the text, one correctly penetrates the two aspects of calm-abiding and insight; through non-inversion regarding meaning, one correctly penetrates every inverted character; through non-inversion regarding attention, one correctly abandons the causes and conditions of inversion; through non-inversion regarding non-agitation, one correctly grasps their character; through non-inversion regarding own-character, one cultivates the non-discriminating path that remedies them; through non-inversion regarding the universal character, one correctly penetrates original purity; through non-inversion regarding defilement and purity, one knows the obstruction not yet cut off, and already cut off; through non-inversion regarding the adventitious, one truly knows the two characters of defilement and purity; and through the two non-inversions of freedom from fear and pride, every obstruction is severed and one attains permanent liberation.

論曰：此十無倒如次安立於彼十種金剛句中。何等名為十金剛句？謂有非有無顛倒。所依幻等喻無分別。本性清淨雜染清淨。虛空喻無減無增。為攝如是十金剛句，有二頌言：

「應知有非有，　無顛倒所依，

幻等無分別，　本性常清淨，

及雜染清淨，　性淨喻虛空，

無減亦無增，　是十金剛句。」

These ten non-inversions are, in order, established within those ten vajra words. What are the ten vajra words? Existence and non-existence; non-inversion; the basis; the illusion and other similes; non-discrimination; original purity; defilement and purity; the simile of space; no decrease; and no increase. To gather these ten vajra words, there are two verses: "One should know existence and non-existence, non-inversion, the basis, the illusion and the rest, non-discrimination, original constant purity, and defilement and purity, whose purity of nature is likened to space, and no decrease and no increase — these are the ten vajra words."

論曰：且初安立十金剛句。自性者，謂自性故、所緣故，無分別故、釋難故。自性故者，謂三自性，即圓成實、遍計所執及依他起。是初三句，如次應知。所緣故者，即三自性。無分別故者，謂由此無分別，即無分別智；及於此無分別，即本性清淨。如次應知安立境智，謂三自性及無分別。釋難故者，謂所餘句。

Now, first, we explain the establishment of these ten vajra words. Their own nature comes about through four reasons: through own-nature; through being an object; through non-discrimination; and through resolving objections. "Through own-nature" means the three natures — the perfectly accomplished, the discriminated and attached-to, and the dependent — corresponding, in order, to the first three words. "Through being an object" means these same three natures. "Through non-discrimination" means: through this, non-discrimination — namely, non-discriminating wisdom; and with respect to this non-discrimination, original purity. In this order, it should be understood that object and wisdom are established — namely, the three natures and non-discrimination. "Through resolving objections" means the remaining words.

論曰：且有難言：遍計所執、依他起相，若實是無，云何可得？若實是有，不應諸法本性清淨。為釋此難，說幻等喻，如幻事等雖實是無而現可得。復有難言：若一切法本性清淨，如何得有先染後淨？為釋此難，說有染淨及虛空喻，謂如虛空雖本性淨，而有雜染及清淨時。復有難言：有無量佛出現於世，一一能度無量有情，令出生死入於涅槃。云何生死無斷滅失、涅槃界中無增益過？為釋此難，說染及淨無減無增。又有情界及清淨品俱無量故。

Now, an objection may be raised: if the characters of the discriminated, attached-to nature and the dependent nature are truly non-existent, how can they be apprehended at all? And if they are truly existent, then it should not be said that all dharmas are originally pure by nature. To resolve this objection, the simile of illusion and the rest is spoken of: just as an illusory thing, though truly non-existent, can nevertheless be apprehended as appearing. A further objection may be raised: if all dharmas are originally pure by nature, how can there be first defilement and then purity? To resolve this objection, defilement-and-purity and the simile of space are spoken of: just as space, though pure by nature, nevertheless has times of being defiled and times of being purified. A further objection may be raised: there are immeasurable buddhas appearing in the world, each one liberating immeasurable sentient beings, causing them to leave saṃsāra and enter nirvāṇa — how, then, can saṃsāra suffer no diminishment, and the realm of nirvāṇa incur no increase? To resolve this objection, no decrease and no increase in defilement and purity are spoken of — and moreover because the realm of sentient beings and the class of the purified are both without limit.

論曰：第二安立彼自性者，如有頌言：

「亂境自性因，　無亂自性境，

亂無亂二果，　及彼二邊際。」

Second, we explain the establishment of their own nature, as a verse says: "The object, own-nature, and cause of confusion; the own-nature and object of non-confusion; the fruits of confusion and non-confusion; and the limit of these two."

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī reads the three objections behind these vajra words as a single escalating challenge to the mind-is-originally-pure teaching from Part One: first, how can a purely negative fact — that the discriminated, attached-to nature is unreal — still be experienced as vividly as it is? (Answered by illusion: unreal things can still appear.) Second, if purity was always already there, how can defilement and purification be real events rather than illusions themselves? (Answered by space, which can genuinely gather dust and genuinely be swept clean without its own nature ever changing.) Third, if countless buddhas have already emptied saṃsāra of countless beings, why is saṃsāra not visibly shrinking, nor nirvāṇa visibly growing? (Answered by pointing out that both the realm of sentient beings and the class of the purified are, from the start, without any finite measure to begin with.)

5. The Practice Free of the Two Extremes: Eight Extremes from the Ratnakūṭa Sūtra

The fourth correct practice is the Middle Way itself, as the Ratnakūṭa Sūtra teaches it — not one single pair of extremes, but fifteen, worked through here in two batches. The first eight concern the person, dharmas, and the nature of dependent origination itself.

Chinese Source

異性與一性，　外道及聲聞，

增益損減邊，　有情法各二。

Ru-Yi Translation

Being different, and being one;

the extremist and the śrāvaka;

the extremes of exaggeration and denial —

each, for the person and for dharmas, twofold.

Chinese Source

所治及能治，　常住與斷滅，

所取能取邊，　染淨二三種。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is to be treated, and what treats it;

permanence, and annihilation;

the grasped and the grasper —

[and] defilement and purity, each threefold.

論曰：若於色等執我有異、或執是一，各為一邊；為離此執說中道行，謂觀無我乃至儒童，見有我者定起此執，我異於身、或即身故。

What does this mean? If, regarding form and the rest, one holds that the self is different from them, or holds that it is identical to them, each is an extreme; to depart from this attachment, the Middle Way is taught — the contemplation of non-self, up through "the young nurtured one," since one who sees a self necessarily falls into this attachment, holding the self either different from the body or identical to it.

論曰：若於色等執為常住是外道邊，執無常者是聲聞邊；為離此執說中道行，謂觀色等非常無常。

If, regarding form and the rest, one holds them to be permanent, this is the extremist's extreme; holding them to be impermanent is the śrāvaka's extreme; to depart from this attachment, the Middle Way is taught — the contemplation of form and the rest as neither permanent nor impermanent.

論曰：定執有我是增益有情邊，定執無我是損減有情邊，彼亦撥無假有情故；為離此執說中道行，謂我無我二邊中智。

Definitely holding there is a self is the extreme of exaggerating the person; definitely holding there is no self is the extreme of denying the person, since this also denies the conventionally existing person; to depart from this attachment, the Middle Way is taught — wisdom situated within the middle of the two extremes of self and non-self.

論曰：定執心有實是增益法邊，定執心無實是損減法邊；為離此執說中道行，謂於是處無心無思無意無識。

Definitely holding that mind truly exists is the extreme of exaggerating dharmas; definitely holding that mind does not truly exist is the extreme of denying dharmas; to depart from this attachment, the Middle Way is taught — that here there is no mind, no thought, no intellect, and no consciousness [as ultimately real entities, though conventionally functioning].

論曰：執有不善等諸雜染法是所治邊，執有善等諸清淨法是能治邊；為離此執說中道行，謂於二邊不隨觀說。

Holding that the unwholesome and other defiled dharmas [truly] exist as what is to be treated is one extreme; holding that the wholesome and other pure dharmas [truly] exist as what treats them is the other extreme; to depart from this attachment, the Middle Way is taught — not following either extreme in one's contemplation or one's speech.

論曰：於有情法定執為有是常住邊，定執非有是斷滅邊；為離此執說中道行，謂即於此二邊中智。

Regarding the person and dharmas, definitely holding they exist is the extreme of permanence; definitely holding they do not exist is the extreme of annihilation; to depart from this attachment, the Middle Way is taught — wisdom situated within the middle of these two extremes.

論曰：執有無明所取能取各為一邊，若執有明所取能取各為一邊。如是執有所治諸行、能治無為，乃至老死及能滅彼諸對治道所取能取各為一邊。此所能治所取能取，即是黑品白品差別。為離此執說中道行，謂明與無明無二無二分，乃至廣說，明無明等所取能取皆非有故。

Holding that there is a grasped and a grasper with respect to ignorance is each an extreme; holding that there is a grasped and a grasper with respect to [true] knowledge is likewise each an extreme. In this way, holding that there is a grasped and a grasper with respect to the formations and the rest that are to be treated, and the unconditioned that treats them, up through old age and death and the very path that extinguishes them, is each an extreme. This "what is to be treated" and "what treats it," this "grasped" and "grasper," are precisely the distinction between the black class [of defilement] and the white class [of purification]. To depart from this attachment, the Middle Way is taught — that knowledge and ignorance are neither two nor divided into two, and so on as fully explained, since a grasped and a grasper with respect to knowledge, ignorance, and the rest are none of them real.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī groups these eight into four pairs, each addressing a different rival misreading of selflessness: whether the self is one with or different from the body (targeting the substantialist views of non-Buddhist schools); whether form and the rest are permanent or merely impermanent (targeting, on one side, eternalist extremists, and on the other, a śrāvaka reading that stops short at bare impermanence); whether the person and dharmas are exaggerated into full reality or denied into bare nothing; and finally, whether the twelve links of dependent origination themselves — ignorance and knowledge, the defiled and the purifying — are treated as a real grasped object opposed to a real grasping subject, rather than as the empty, dependently arisen process Part One of this treatise has already described.

6. Seven More Extremes, and the Threefold Purification

A second set of seven extremes follows, each illustrated with its own simile from the sūtra — a lamp, two rubbing sticks, a frightened traveler mistaking empty space for a threat, a foolish painter startled by his own picture, and a magician startled by his own conjured beast.

Chinese Source

分別二邊性，　應知復有七，

謂有非有邊，　所能寂怖畏，

所能取正邪，　有用并無用，

不起及時等，　是分別二邊。

Ru-Yi Translation

The nature of the discriminated two extremes

should be known to be seven more:

the extremes of existence and non-existence; of what pacifies and what is pacified; of dread;

of the grasped and grasper; of correct and mistaken;

of having use and having no use;

of not arising, and of equal duration —

these are the discriminated two extremes.

論曰：分別有、分別非有各為一邊。彼執實有補特伽羅以為壞滅，立空性故。或於無我分別為無，為離如是二邊分別說中道行，謂不為滅補特伽羅方立空性，然彼空性本性自空，前際亦空、後際亦空、中際亦空，乃至廣說。

What does this mean? Discriminating existence and discriminating non-existence are each an extreme, since one holds that emptiness is established in order to annihilate a truly existing person — or, regarding non-self, discriminates it as bare nothing. To depart from this twofold discrimination, the Middle Way is taught: emptiness is not established in order to annihilate the person, but rather that very emptiness is, by its own nature, empty — empty in its former limit, empty in its later limit, and empty in its middle — and so on, as fully explained.

論曰：分別所寂、分別能寂各為一邊。執有所斷及有能斷，怖畏空故。為離如是二邊分別，說虛空喻。

Discriminating what is pacified and discriminating what pacifies it are each an extreme, since one holds there is a real thing relinquished and a real relinquishing agent, dreading emptiness. To depart from this twofold discrimination, the simile of space is taught.

論曰：分別所怖、分別從彼所生可畏各為一邊。執有遍計所執色等可生怖故，執有從彼所生苦法可生畏故。為離如是二邊分別，說畫師喻。前虛空喻為聲聞說，今畫師喻為菩薩說。

Discriminating what is dreaded and discriminating the fearsome thing arising from it are each an extreme, since one holds that the discriminated, attached-to form and the rest can arise as an object of dread, and holds that the suffering dharma arising from it can arise as an object of fear. To depart from this twofold discrimination, the simile of the painter is taught. The earlier simile of space was spoken for śrāvakas; this simile of the painter is spoken for bodhisattvas.

論曰：分別所取、分別能取各為一邊。為離如是二邊分別，說幻師喻。由唯識智無境智生，由無境智生復捨唯識。智境既非有，識亦是無，要託所緣識方生故。由斯所喻與喻同法。

Discriminating the grasped and discriminating the grasper are each an extreme. To depart from this twofold discrimination, the simile of the magician is taught. From the wisdom of Consciousness-Only arises the wisdom that there is no [external] object; from the arising of the wisdom that there is no object, one further relinquishes even Consciousness-Only itself. Since the object of this wisdom does not exist, consciousness too does not exist [as an object-grasping reality], for consciousness necessarily arises only in dependence on an object it takes as condition. In this way, the thing illustrated and the illustration share the same principle.

論曰：分別正性、分別邪性各為一邊。執如實觀為正為邪，二種性故。為離如是二邊分別，說兩木生火喻。謂如兩木雖無火相，由相鑽截而能生火，火既生已還燒兩木。此如實觀亦復如是，雖無聖道正性之相，而能發生正性聖慧。如是正性聖慧生已，復能除遣此如實觀。由斯所喻與喻同法。然如實觀雖無正性相，順正性故亦無邪性相。

Discriminating a correct nature and discriminating a mistaken nature are each an extreme, since one holds that authentic contemplation must be either correct or mistaken in nature. To depart from this twofold discrimination, the simile of two sticks generating fire is taught: just as two sticks, though bearing no trace of fire, can generate fire through rubbing, and the fire, once generated, then burns up the two sticks — so too this authentic contemplation, though it bears no trace of the noble path's true nature, is nonetheless able to give rise to the true, noble wisdom; and once this true, noble wisdom has arisen, it is able, in turn, to dispel this very authentic contemplation. In this way, the thing illustrated and the illustration share the same principle. Yet, although authentic contemplation bears no trace of a correct nature, since it accords with that correct nature, it likewise bears no trace of a mistaken nature.

論曰：分別有用、分別無用各為一邊。彼執聖智要先分別方能除染，或全無用。為離如是二邊分別，說初燈喻。

Discriminating that it has use and discriminating that it has no use are each an extreme, since one holds either that noble wisdom must first involve discrimination in order to remove defilement, or that it has no use whatsoever. To depart from this twofold discrimination, the first simile of a lamp is taught.

論曰：分別不起、分別時等各為一邊。彼執能治畢竟不起，或執與染應等時長。為離如是二邊分別，說後燈喻。

Discriminating that it will never arise and discriminating that it requires an equal duration are each an extreme, since one holds either that the remedy will ultimately never arise at all, or that, if it does arise, it must require as long a duration as the defilement it remedies took to accumulate. To depart from this twofold discrimination, the second simile of a lamp is taught.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī draws out what the two lamp similes actually answer: a lamp brought into a room does not need to deliberate before dispelling darkness, so wisdom's removal of defilement needs no prior act of discrimination — yet a lamp is not therefore useless, so wisdom is not without effect either. And a room darkened since beginningless time is lit the instant a lamp enters it, not gradually over an equally long duration — so an unarisen wisdom does not need eons to arise once conditions are met, any more than it needed eons of darkness to justify the darkness in the first place. Between the two sets of similes, Kuījī notes a deliberate audience distinction: the space-simile answering fear of emptiness is given for śrāvakas, prone to clinging too tightly to real dharmas, while the painter-simile answering fear of one's own projections is given for bodhisattvas, whose risk runs the other way — mistaking their own compassionate engagement with a seemingly real world for something to be afraid of.

7. Threefold Defilement and Its Remedy, and Why Neither Extreme Survives

Woven into the treatment of the fourth and fifth extremes above, the treatise pauses to catalogue three kinds of defilement — afflictive, karmic, and of birth — each further threefold, together with the specific wisdom that remedies each, closing with the insight that neither "defiled" nor "pure" was ever more than a label for something that was, from the very start, without any nature of its own to defile or purify.

論曰：雜染有三，謂煩惱雜染、業雜染、生雜染。煩惱雜染復有三種：一諸見、二貪瞋癡相、三後有願。此能對治，謂空智、無相智、無願智。業雜染，謂所作善惡業。此能對治，謂不作智。生雜染有三種：一後有生、二生已心心所念念起、三後有相續。此能對治，謂無生智、無起智、無自性智。

What does this mean? Defilement is threefold: afflictive defilement, karmic defilement, and defilement of birth. Afflictive defilement is further threefold: first, [wrong] views; second, the character of greed, hatred, and delusion; third, craving for future existence. The remedy for this is: the wisdom of emptiness, the wisdom of signlessness, and the wisdom of wishlessness. Karmic defilement means wholesome and unwholesome deeds performed. The remedy for this is the wisdom of non-doing. Defilement of birth is threefold: first, future birth itself; second, mind and mental factors arising moment to moment once born; third, the continuity of future existence. The remedy for this is: the wisdom of non-arising, the wisdom of non-origination, and the wisdom of no [inherent] nature.

論曰：如是三種雜染除滅，說為清淨。空等智境，謂空等法。三種雜染隨其所應，非空等智令作空等，由彼本性是空性等，法界本來性無染故。若於法界或執雜染或執清淨各為一邊，本性無染，非染淨故。為離此執說中道行，謂不由空能空於法，法性自空，乃至廣說。

The removal of these three kinds of defilement is called purity. The object of the wisdom of emptiness and the rest is emptiness and the corresponding dharmas. It is not that the wisdom of emptiness and the rest, respectively, makes the three kinds of defilement into emptiness and the rest — rather, it is because their own nature already is emptiness and the rest, since the dharma-realm is, from the outset, by nature free of defilement. If, regarding the dharma-realm, one holds either that it is defiled or that it is pure, each is an extreme, since its own nature is free of defilement, being neither defiled nor pure. To depart from this attachment, the Middle Way is taught: it is not that emptiness empties a dharma — rather, the dharma's own nature is, itself, already empty; and so on, as fully explained.

8. Differentiated and Undifferentiated Practice, and a Summary of All Six

The chapter's fifth and sixth correct practices are given together, in a single verse: whether a bodhisattva emphasizes one particular perfection at a particular ground, or cultivates all ten evenly across every ground. Vasubandhu then closes correct practice as a whole with a summary of all six practices at once.

Chinese Source

差別無差別，　應知於十地，

十波羅蜜多，　增上等修集。

Ru-Yi Translation

Differentiated and undifferentiated —

it should be known that, across the ten grounds,

the ten perfections are cultivated

either with one predominant, or equally.

論曰：於十地中十到彼岸隨一增上而修集者，應知說為差別正行。於一切地皆等修集布施等十波羅蜜多，如是正行名無差別。

What does this mean? Where, across the ten grounds, the ten perfections are cultivated with any one of them predominant, this should be known as differentiated practice. Where generosity and the other ten perfections are cultivated equally across every ground, such practice is called undifferentiated practice.

論曰：六正行總義者，謂即如是品類最勝，由此思惟如所施設大乘法等，由如是品無亂轉變修奢摩他，及無倒轉變修毘鉢舍那，為如是義修中道行而求出離，於十地中修習差別無差別行。如是已說正行無上。

The general meaning of the six correct practices: it is precisely this class of things that is supreme [supreme practice]; by this, one reflects on the Great Vehicle's dharmas as they have been set forth [the practice of intention]; by this same class, one cultivates calm-abiding through the transformation into freedom from distraction, and cultivates insight through the transformation into freedom from inversion [the practice according with the Dharma]; for the sake of this same meaning, one cultivates the Middle Way and seeks liberation [the practice free of the two extremes]; and, across the ten grounds, one cultivates differentiated and undifferentiated practice. This concludes the supremacy of correct practice.

9. The Supremacy of the Object Relied On

The chapter's second supremacy names twelve objects a bodhisattva relies on across the entire path — from the Dharma teaching first heard, through suchness itself, to the object a buddha alone relies on.

Chinese Source

所緣謂安、界、　所能立、任持、

印內持、通達，　增、證、運、最勝。

Ru-Yi Translation

The object relied on: the established, the realm,

what is established and what establishes it, holding,

affirming, inward holding, and penetrating,

increasing, partial realization, equanimous carrying, and the supreme.

論曰：如是所緣有十二種，一安立法施設所緣、二法界所緣、三所立所緣、四能立所緣、五任持所緣、六印持所緣、七內持所緣、八通達所緣、九增長所緣、十分證所緣、十一等運所緣、十二最勝所緣。

What does this mean? The object relied on has twelve kinds: first, the object of the established Dharma as expounded; second, the object of the dharma-realm; third, the object that is established; fourth, the object that establishes; fifth, the object of holding; sixth, the object of affirming; seventh, the object of inward holding; eighth, the object of penetration; ninth, the object of increase; tenth, the object of partial realization; eleventh, the object of equanimous carrying; twelfth, the supreme object.

論曰：此中最初謂所安立到彼岸等差別法門；第二謂真如；第三第四如次應知，即前二種到彼岸等差別法門，要由通達法界成故；第五謂聞所成慧境，任持文故；第六謂思所成慧境，印持義故；第七謂修所成慧境，內別持故；第八謂初地中見道境；第九謂修道中乃至七地境；第十謂即七地中世出世道品類差別分分證境；第十一謂第八地境；第十二謂第九第十如來地境。應知此中即初第二，隨諸義位得彼彼名。

Here, the first means the various established Dharma-gateways, such as the perfections and the rest, as expounded. The second means suchness. The third and fourth, in order, are these same two — the established Dharma-gateways such as the perfections and the rest — since they are accomplished only through penetrating the dharma-realm. The fifth means the object of the wisdom accomplished through hearing, since it holds the text. The sixth means the object of the wisdom accomplished through reflection, since it affirms the meaning. The seventh means the object of the wisdom accomplished through meditative cultivation, since it holds it inwardly and distinctly. The eighth means the object of the path of seeing in the first ground. The ninth means the object of the path of cultivation, up through the seventh ground. The tenth means the object of the partial realization, ground by ground, of the many kinds of mundane and supramundane path within the seventh ground. The eleventh means the object of the eighth ground. The twelfth means the object of the ninth and tenth grounds and the tathāgata-ground. It should be understood that these are, in every case, simply the first [the Dharma] and the second [suchness], each receiving its own name according to the specific meaning and stage involved.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī stresses the closing line as the key to the whole list: there are, strictly, only two objects in the entire Buddhist path — the Dharma as taught, and suchness itself — and every one of these twelve names is simply one of those two, seen at a different stage. Hearing, reflecting, and meditative cultivation each "hold" the same Dharma differently; the path of seeing, the path of cultivation, and the grounds that follow each "penetrate" suchness a little more completely, right up through the tathāgata-ground, where the object relied on and the wisdom that relies on it, this treatise has already said elsewhere, are ultimately not even two different things.

10. The Supremacy of Realization Through Cultivation

The chapter's third and final supremacy names ten kinds of realization a bodhisattva's cultivation produces, from first entering the spiritual lineage to the final, ceaseless activity of manifesting enlightenment for others' benefit.

Chinese Source

修證謂無闕、　不毀動、圓滿、

起、堅固、調柔、　不住、無障息。

Ru-Yi Translation

Realization through cultivation: without deficiency,

without slander, without being shaken, complete,

arising, firm, pliant,

not abiding, without obstruction, and without rest.

論曰：如是修證總有十種，一種性修證，緣無闕故。二信解修證，不謗毀大乘故。三發心修證，非下劣乘所擾動故。四正行修證，波羅蜜多得圓滿故。五入離生修證，起聖道故。六成熟有情修證，堅固善根長時集故。七淨土修證，心調柔故。八得不退地受記修證，以不住著生死涅槃，非此二種所退轉故。九佛地修證，無二障故。十示現菩提修證，無休息故。

What does this mean? Realization through cultivation is, in total, of ten kinds: first, the realization of the spiritual lineage, since its conditions are without deficiency. Second, the realization of confident understanding, since one does not slander the Great Vehicle. Third, the realization of arousing the mind of bodhi, since one is not shaken by an inferior vehicle. Fourth, the realization of correct practice, since the perfections are brought to completion. Fifth, the realization of entering freedom from birth, since the noble path arises. Sixth, the realization of maturing sentient beings, since firm good roots are accumulated over a long time. Seventh, the realization of a pure land, since the mind becomes pliant. Eighth, the realization of attaining the stage of non-regression and receiving the prediction [of future buddhahood], since one is not attached to either saṃsāra or nirvāṇa, and so is turned back by neither. Ninth, the realization of the buddha-ground, since one is free of the two obstructions. Tenth, the realization of manifesting bodhi, since this activity is without rest.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī locates these ten precisely along the path already mapped in earlier chapters: the first is simply possessing the spiritual lineage; the second and third belong to the first incalculable eon; the fourth spans the path of practice generally; the fifth is the path of seeing itself; the sixth runs from the path of cultivation through the seventh ground; the seventh belongs specifically to the eighth ground, where mastery over pure lands first becomes possible; the eighth spans the ninth and tenth grounds, closest to buddhahood; and the ninth and tenth belong to buddhahood itself — the ninth naming the dharma body and self-enjoyment body freed of both obstructions, the tenth naming the enjoyment-for-others body and transformation body's unceasing activity on behalf of beings who have not yet awakened.

11. Three Kinds of Supremacy, Summarized

A brief closing line folds all twenty-eight verses of Part Seven back into the three supremacies named at the very start of the chapter.

論曰：無上乘總義者，略有三種無上乘義，謂正行無上故、正行持無上故、正行果無上故。

What does this mean? The general meaning of the unsurpassed vehicle is, in brief, threefold: the supremacy of correct practice itself; the supremacy of what correct practice relies on and holds in mind; and the supremacy of correct practice's fruit.

12. Why This Treatise Is Named "Distinguishing the Middle from the Extremes"

Having completed all seven topics, the treatise closes by explaining its own title — a title that, Vasubandhu shows, can be read in three complementary ways, before naming four further qualities of what the treatise as a whole has accomplished.

Chinese Source

此論辯中邊，　深密堅實義，

廣大一切義，　除諸不吉祥。

Ru-Yi Translation

This treatise distinguishes the middle from the extremes;

its meaning is profound, and firm and true;

its meaning is vast, and universal;

and it removes every inauspicious thing.

論曰：此論能辯中邊行故，名辯中邊，即是顯了處中二邊能緣行義。又此能辯中邊境故，名辯中邊，即是顯了處中二邊所緣境義。或此正辯離初後邊中道法故，名辯中邊。

What does this mean? This treatise is able to distinguish the conduct that is central and the conduct that is extreme — hence it is named "Distinguishing the Middle from the Extremes," which is to say, it clearly displays the sense of central and extreme conduct as it operates on its objects. Again, this treatise is able to distinguish the objects that are central and the objects that are extreme — hence it is likewise so named, displaying the sense of central and extreme objects as they are relied on. Or again, it correctly distinguishes the Middle Way dharma, free of both the earlier and the later extremes — hence it is named "Distinguishing the Middle from the Extremes" for this reason too.

論曰：此論所辯是深密義，非諸尋思所行處故。是堅實義，能摧他辯非彼伏故。是廣大義，能辯利樂自他事故。是一切義，普能決了三乘法故。又能除滅諸不吉祥，永斷煩惱所知障故。

What this treatise distinguishes is a profound and hidden meaning, since it is not the domain of ordinary reasoning. It is a firm and true meaning, since it is able to overcome others' arguments and is never overcome by them. It is a vast meaning, since it is able to distinguish the affairs of benefiting and delighting oneself and others. It is a universal meaning, since it is able to fully resolve the teachings of all three vehicles. And it is further able to remove every inauspicious thing, since it permanently severs both the afflictive obstruction and the cognitive obstruction.

Reading note — after Kuījī

Kuījī reads the three explanations of the title as not competing but cumulative: the treatise distinguishes central from extreme conduct — false discrimination's own middle path between "everything is empty" and "everything is real," worked out across every chapter of this site's translation; it distinguishes central from extreme objects — the very same three natures examined as what a bodhisattva actually takes as their object of practice; and it distinguishes, most directly, the Middle Way itself as a teaching, free of every extreme this treatise has catalogued, from the fourfold challenge of Part One's opening pages to the fifteen extremes of this chapter's own fourth correct practice. All three readings, Kuījī notes, point to the same single achievement: a treatise whose entire content, from its first verse to its last, has never once said "only this side" or "only that side," but has instead shown, patiently and by name, exactly what lies in the middle.

Closing Dedication

Vasubandhu closes the treatise, as he opened it, in his own voice — dedicating whatever merit belongs to this work of composition to every being, in the hope that they too might swiftly reach what this treatise has spent seven chapters describing.

Chinese Source

我辯此論諸功德，　咸持普施群生類，

令獲勝生增福慧，　疾證廣大三菩提。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whatever virtue there is in my having expounded this treatise,

I take up entirely and offer it universally to every kind of being —

may they obtain an excellent rebirth, and increase their merit and their wisdom,

and swiftly realize the vast, unsurpassed awakening.

The Treatise Complete

This closes Ru-Yi's translation of the Madhyāntavibhāga — Maitreya's opening verse naming seven topics, worked out across eight pages: false discrimination and the sixteen emptinesses in Part 1; the obstructions blocking the bodhisattva and śrāvaka lineages in Part 2; the ten kinds of reality in Part 3; the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment as their direct remedy in Part 4; the eighteen stages of the path in Part 5; the five fruits of cultivation in Part 6; and, across Parts 7 and 8, the unsurpassed vehicle itself — its supreme practice, its object relied on, and its realization through cultivation — closing on the treatise's own name: a teaching that, from its first line to its last, distinguishes the middle from every extreme.
