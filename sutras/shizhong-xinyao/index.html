Zhanran · Tang dynasty · 始終心要

The Essentials of Beginning and End.

In about three hundred characters, Zhanran (湛然, 711–782) lays out the Tiantai path from its beginning in the nature to its end in realization. The three truths are the nature as it is; delusion covers them as three delusions; three contemplations break the delusions, three wisdoms are realized, and three virtues are accomplished — and none of these are separate or come at different times. Tiantai students have long learned it by heart.

How to read this collection

The text is short enough to memorize, and that is how it has been used. Read it slowly, noticing how each set of three matches the others.

For the fuller picture behind it, see the Jiaoguan Gangzong and the Mohe Zhiguan.

Translation Notes

Key terms, kept consistent across the whole text and with this site's other Tiantai translations. See the full translation notes, and also this site's note on translation method.

三諦 — the three truthsEmpty, provisional, and the middle.
三惑 — the three delusionsViews and habituated thought, dust-and-sand, and ignorance.
三觀 — the three contemplationsOf emptiness, of the provisional, and of the middle.
三智 — the three wisdomsThe wisdom that knows all things, the wisdom of the paths, and the wisdom knowing all modes.
三德 — the three virtuesThe Dharma-body, prajñā, and liberation.
