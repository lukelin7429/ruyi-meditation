The Essentials of Beginning and End

This short piece, about three hundred characters long, is attributed to Zhanran (711–782), the sixth patriarch of the Tiantai school, and is translated here in full as a single part. It sets out the whole of Tiantai practice as five sets of three that move in strict parallel: the three truths (the middle, the real, the conventional), the three delusions that cover them (ignorance, dust-and-sand, views and habituated thought), the three contemplations that break those delusions (emptiness, the provisional, the middle), the three wisdoms they bring about, and the three virtues in which they are fulfilled (prajñā, liberation, the Dharma-body). The three truths are called an "inherent virtue" that all living beings possess by nature rather than produce. The text then insists that the sets are not separate and do not follow one another in time: the order belongs only to the explanation, not to the principle itself. The title, "beginning and end," points to this movement from cause to fruit. The text is not in the Taishō canon; it is transmitted in later collections, and readers who know the Profound Meaning and Calming and Contemplation will recognize it as a compact summary of their teaching on the threefold truth.

Chinese Source

始終心要

Ru-Yi Translation

The Essentials of Beginning and End

Chinese Source

唐天台沙門釋湛然述

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Zhanran, a monk of Tiantai, Tang dynasty.

The Three Truths and the Three Delusions

Chinese Source

夫三諦者。天然之性德也。中諦者統一切法。真諦者泯一切法。俗諦者立一切法。舉一即三非前後也。含生本具非造作之所得也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three truths are the inherent virtue that is so by nature. The truth of the middle unifies all dharmas; the truth of the real dissolves all dharmas; the conventional truth establishes all dharmas. Raise one and it is the three; they are not earlier and later. Beings that hold life possess them originally; they are not obtained by fabrication.

Chinese Source

悲夫祕藏不顯。葢三惑之所覆也。故無明翳乎法性。塵沙障乎化導。見思阻乎空寂。

Ru-Yi Translation

Alas! That the secret store does not appear is because it is covered by the three delusions. Thus ignorance clouds the dharma-nature, dust-and-sand obstructs transforming guidance, and views and habituated thought block the empty stillness.

Chinese Source

然茲三惑。乃體上之虛妄也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet these three delusions are falsehoods upon the essence.

Chinese Source

於是大覺慈尊。喟然嘆曰。真如界內。絕生佛之假名。平等慧中。無自他之形相。但以眾生妄想不自證得。莫之能返也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thereupon the Greatly Awakened, the compassionate Honored One, sighed and said: "Within the realm of suchness, the provisional names of beings and buddhas are cut off; in equal wisdom there are no forms of self and other. It is only that sentient beings, through deluded thinking, do not realize it for themselves, and none of them is able to return to it."

The Three Contemplations, Wisdoms and Virtues

Chinese Source

由是立乎三觀。破乎三惑。證乎三智。成乎三德。

Ru-Yi Translation

For this reason the three contemplations are established, the three delusions are broken, the three wisdoms are realized, and the three virtues are accomplished.

Chinese Source

空觀者。破見思惑。證一切智。成般若德。假觀者。破塵沙惑。證道種智。成解脫德。中觀者。破無明惑。證一切種智。成法身德。

Ru-Yi Translation

The contemplation of emptiness breaks the delusion of views and habituated thought, realizes the wisdom that knows all things, and accomplishes the virtue of prajñā. The contemplation of the provisional breaks the delusion of dust-and-sand, realizes the wisdom of the paths, and accomplishes the virtue of liberation. The contemplation of the middle breaks the delusion of ignorance, realizes the wisdom knowing all modes, and accomplishes the virtue of the Dharma-body.

Chinese Source

然茲三惑三觀三智三德。非各別也。非異時也。天然之理。具諸法故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet these three delusions, three contemplations, three wisdoms, and three virtues are not separate from one another, and not at different times, because the principle that is so by nature possesses all dharmas.

Conclusion

Chinese Source

然此三諦性之自爾。迷茲三諦轉成三惑。惑破藉乎三觀。觀成證乎三智。智成成乎三德。從因至果。非漸修也。說之次第。理非次第。大綱如此。綱目可尋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet these three truths are so of their own nature. Deluded about these three truths, [beings] turn them into the three delusions. Breaking delusion relies on the three contemplations; when contemplation is accomplished, one realizes the three wisdoms; when wisdom is accomplished, one accomplishes the three virtues. From cause to fruit, this is not gradual cultivation. Speaking of it has a sequence; the principle has no sequence. The main cord is like this; the cord and its meshes can be traced.

Chinese Source

始終心要（竟）

Ru-Yi Translation

The Essentials of Beginning and End (end)

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Source and attribution. The text is cited by the editors as G1977 (outside the Taishō canon); it is not among the CBETA Taishō files used for this project, so its wording could not be compared with another edition here. The attribution to Zhanran rests on the heading of unit 1.

性德 inherent virtue. Rendered as in the glossary (inherent virtue / cultivated virtue). Here the three truths themselves are called the inherent virtue, while the three virtues of unit 7 are what is "accomplished" (成); the text thus moves from inherent virtue to its fulfillment.

天然 "so by nature." The same phrase is used in units 2 and 8 (天然之性德, 天然之理); it is rendered the same way both times. 性之自爾 in unit 9 is rendered "so of their own nature."

The five sets of three. 三諦 the three truths (the truth of the middle / of the real / the conventional truth, as on the site's Jiaoguan Gangzong); 三惑 the three delusions (views and habituated thought / dust-and-sand / ignorance, as in Mohe Zhiguan); 三觀 the contemplations of emptiness / of the provisional / of the middle; 三智 the wisdom that knows all things / the wisdom of the paths / the wisdom knowing all modes (glossary, from the Profound Meaning); 三德 the virtue of prajñā / of liberation / of the Dharma-body (as in Jiaoguan Gangzong Part 1). The English for each member is kept identical wherever it recurs.

統／泯／立. "Unifies," "dissolves," and "establishes" (unit 2). 泯 is rendered "dissolve" in line with the glossary's candidate list (遮／照；亡；泯；絕).

The order of the three delusions in unit 3. Here ignorance is paired with the dharma-nature, dust-and-sand with transforming guidance (the bodhisattva's work of teaching), and views and habituated thought with "the empty stillness" (空寂), i.e., the emptiness realized by the first contemplation. The order runs from the deepest delusion to the shallowest, the reverse of unit 7.

體上之虛妄 "falsehoods upon the essence." Alternatively: "false [things arising] on the essence [itself]," i.e., the delusions have no being apart from the very nature they cover.

The extent of the Buddha's words (unit 5). The quotation could end after 無自他之形相 ("no forms of self and other"), with "It is only that sentient beings…" as the author's own comment. The translation keeps the whole as speech, which the Chinese allows; Alternatively: close the quotation after "self and other." Source of the saying not located; it reads as Zhanran's own wording placed in the Buddha's mouth rather than a sūtra quotation.

Subject of unit 6. 立乎三觀… has no stated subject. It can be read as continuing the Buddha's action ("he established the three contemplations…"); the translation uses the passive to keep the subject open.

非漸修 "not gradual cultivation." The text distinguishes the sequence of explanation (說之次第) from the principle, which is without sequence (理非次第); this is the standard perfect-teaching position on the three contemplations in a single mind.

綱目 "the cord and its meshes." A net image: the main cord (綱) of the net is given, and the meshes (目) can be followed from it. Rendered literally so the image is kept.
