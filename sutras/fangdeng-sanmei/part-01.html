Zunshi's Preface and the Six Conditions

The Practice of the Vaipulya Samādhi is Zhiyi's manual, recorded by Guanding, for the seven-day rite based on the Great Vaipulya Dhāraṇī Sūtra (T1339), the "half walking, half sitting" samādhi of Calming and Contemplation. The text was lost in China and came back from Japan in 1003; this part opens with the preface by Zunshi (964–1032), who explains its return, warns against improvising the rite, and notes that only four of its six sections survive. The first section sets out six conditions for practice. The condition of the method distinguishes the "general" seven-day rite of the Leader, open to all seven assemblies, from the "particular" repentances of the sūtra's fourth fascicle for those who have broken grave precepts: eighty-seven days for bhikṣus, ninety-seven for bhikṣuṇīs, sixty-seven for those with bodhisattva precepts, forty-seven for novices and lay followers, each with its own dhāraṇī and number of recitations. The remaining conditions cover the three kinds of good friend, the preliminary week of seeking one of the twelve dream kings, the three sets of clean garments, the round altar with twenty-four images, and the offerings.

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Zunshi, a śramaṇa of the Song

Chinese Source

山門教卷自唐季多流外國，或尚存目錄而莫見其文。學者思之，渺隔滄海。方等三昧行法者，皇宋咸平六祀，日本僧寂照等齎至。雖東國重來，若西乾新譯，載披載沃，適奉醒醐。竊念方等至尊禁法嚴密，苟不克由聖範，事出師心，豈唯招無益之訶，抑亦負餘殃之責。南嶽師躬行七載，理洞其微，位淨六根，言符先佛。大師咨詢有在，況發總持，垂此典謨，足可繩準。今時或壇場延袤、形像巍峨，行法則半任臆裁，律範則全由心匠。縱謂七眾階節，寧逾上首之科；雖曰像多無妙，要符表法之便。將恐未除故業、更貽新戚，染衣增垢，良用悲夫。然此行法六篇，後二不載者，修行備《百錄》、《止觀》，受戒具出本經。存篇目者，令知法有始終也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the end of the Tang, the teaching texts of our mountain school have mostly drifted to foreign countries; some still survive as titles in the catalogs, but their texts are not to be seen. Students long for them, separated by the vast sea. The Practice of the Vaipulya Samādhi was brought here in the sixth year of Xianping of the Imperial Song by the Japanese monk Jakushō and others. Though it has come back again from the eastern country, it is as if newly translated in the western land; I open it again and again and drink it in, as one who has just received ghee. I humbly reflect that the Vaipulya is most exalted, and its prohibitions and methods are strict and secret. If one does not follow the sages' model, and matters come from taking one's own mind as teacher, one will not only invite the rebuke of uselessness but also bear the blame of lingering calamity. Master Nanyue practiced it himself for seven years; his [grasp of] principle penetrated its subtleties, his stage was the purity of the six faculties, and his words accorded with the earlier buddhas. The Great Master had one to consult, and moreover he brought forth total retention and handed down this canon and plan, fully adequate as a measuring line and standard. In the present day, some make the altar-ground vast and extended and the images towering and lofty, yet in the method of practice they leave half to conjecture, and in the norms of discipline everything comes from the craft of their own minds. Even if they speak of the grades and stages of the seven assemblies, how could they go beyond the Leader's rules? Though it is said that many images are no wonder, what matters is that they accord with what is fitting for expressing the Dharma by symbols. I fear that before old karma is removed, new sorrow will be added, and the dyed robe will gather more stains. Truly this is lamentable. Now, of the six sections of this method of practice, the last two are not included: [the section on] practice is fully given in the Hundred Records and Calming and Contemplation, and [that on] receiving the precepts is fully set out in the sūtra itself. The section titles are kept so that it may be known that the method has a beginning and an end.

The First Condition: The Method, General and Particular

Chinese Source

第一具六緣者（一法緣、二善知識、三前方便、四辦衣、五行法、六供養）

Ru-Yi Translation

First, Being Equipped with the Six Conditions (first, the condition of the method; second, good friends; third, preliminary expedients; fourth, preparing robes; fifth, the method of practice; sixth, offerings)

Chinese Source

一法緣者，依經有總有別。總法者，如第一卷末，七眾通行。七日要心行法，誦三篇呪。經云：爾時上首告恒伽言：「若善男子善女人願欲聞者，汝當夢中住其人前，當現汝身。是人若見汝身，當教行如是實法。若欲行時，七日長齋，日三時洗浴，著淨潔衣，坐佛形像，作五色蓋。用月八日十五日行此法時，若眾生犯五逆罪，身有白癩，若不除差，無有是處。若菩薩二十四戒、沙彌十戒、式叉摩那、沙彌尼戒、比丘、比丘尼戒，如是諸戒，若犯一一諸戒，當一心懺悔。若不還生，無有是處，除不至心。」是名總相法也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the condition of the method. According to the sūtra there is a general [method] and a particular [method]. The general method is as at the end of the first fascicle, practiced in common by the seven assemblies: the seven-day method of practice of the pledged mind, reciting the three sections of mantra. The sūtra says: "Then the Leader said to Gaṅgā: 'If good men or good women wish to hear it, you should stand before them in a dream and manifest your body. If these people see your body, you should teach them to practice this real Dharma. When they wish to practice it, for seven days they keep a long fast, bathe three times a day, put on clean robes, seat the Buddha's image, and make a five-colored canopy. Use the eighth and fifteenth days of the month to practice this method. If beings have committed the five heinous offenses and their bodies have white leprosy, that it should not be cured — there is no such possibility. The bodhisattva's twenty-four precepts, the śrāmaṇera's ten precepts, the śikṣamāṇā['s precepts], the śrāmaṇerikā's precepts, the bhikṣu's and bhikṣuṇī's precepts — if one has broken any one of these precepts, one should repent with a single mind. That [the precepts] should not grow back — there is no such possibility, except [for one who] is not of utmost mind.'" This is called the method of the general mark.

Chinese Source

言別相法者，如第四卷初，七眾行法階節各別，誦呪亦別。如經「若有比丘毀四重禁。至心憶念此陀羅尼呪，請一比丘為懺悔主，如經誦呪一千四百遍已，乃一懺悔。經八十七日懺悔已，是諸戒根若不還生，無有是處。若見比丘尼毀八重禁者，若欲滅八重禁罪，請一比丘為懺悔主，修行九十七日，誦呪四十九遍乃一懺悔。隨師修行，是諸惡業若不除滅，無有是處。若有菩薩受八重禁然後毀德者，誦呪六百遍乃一懺悔。當懺悔時，應請一比丘在前立，口自陳罪，經六十七日，於其夢想如上所說。若下劣沙彌、沙彌尼、優婆塞、優婆夷，亦請一比丘，誦呪四百遍乃一懺悔。如是次第四十七日已，如上所說，夢中得見二事者，當知是沙彌、沙彌尼、優婆塞、優婆夷住清淨戒。」是名別相行法。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the method of the particular mark: as at the beginning of the fourth fascicle, the stages of practice for the seven assemblies each differ, and the mantras recited also differ. As the sūtra says: "If there is a bhikṣu who has violated the four grave prohibitions, let him with utmost mind keep this dhāraṇī mantra in mind and invite one bhikṣu to be the master of repentance. As the sūtra [directs], after he has recited the mantra one thousand four hundred times, he repents once. When he has passed eighty-seven days repenting, that these roots of the precepts should not grow back — there is no such possibility. If a bhikṣuṇī is seen to have violated the eight grave prohibitions, and she wishes to extinguish the offense of the eight grave prohibitions, let her invite one bhikṣu to be the master of repentance and practice for ninety-seven days, repenting once for every forty-nine recitations of the mantra. Following the teacher in practice, that these evil karmas should not be removed and extinguished — there is no such possibility. If there is a bodhisattva who has received the eight grave prohibitions and afterward damages his virtue, let him repent once for every six hundred recitations of the mantra. When repenting, he should invite one bhikṣu to stand before him and state his offenses with his own mouth; through sixty-seven days, [he examines] his dreams as described above. If they are inferior śrāmaṇeras, śrāmaṇerikās, upāsakas, or upāsikās, they too invite one bhikṣu and repent once for every four hundred recitations of the mantra. When, after forty-seven days in this order, they see in dreams the two things described above, know that these śrāmaṇeras, śrāmaṇerikās, upāsakas, and upāsikās abide in pure precepts." This is called the method of practice of the particular mark.

Chinese Source

問：第一卷末，上首七日要心通於七眾，十惡五逆並得消除。何故第四卷初乃各別行法，日有延促，誦呪亦別？答：文殊大悲重請世尊：「佛去世後，若有比丘犯於四重、比丘尼犯八重乃至菩薩戒。又沙彌、沙彌尼、優婆塞、優婆夷犯重禁者，如是等罪當云何滅？」佛言：「因汝問故，我今當說。汝若不問，我終不說。」今既明懺法各異，日數不同，故知上首七日要心，不可令犯重過者修行此實法也。何以故？現生所犯業障尤重，若不加其功行，滅罪無由。是以世尊哀愍，文殊致問，濁惡世時救於七眾地獄衰惱，故立別相懺悔。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: At the end of the first fascicle, the Leader's seven-day pledged mind is common to the seven assemblies, and the ten evils and the five heinous offenses are all removed. Why then, at the beginning of the fourth fascicle, are there separate methods of practice, with the days longer or shorter and the mantras recited also different? Answer: Mañjuśrī, out of great compassion, again asked the World-Honored One: "After the Buddha has left the world, if bhikṣus violate the four grave [prohibitions], or bhikṣuṇīs violate the eight grave [prohibitions], and so on up to the bodhisattva precepts; and if śrāmaṇeras, śrāmaṇerikās, upāsakas, and upāsikās violate the grave prohibitions — how are such offenses to be extinguished?" The Buddha said: "Because you ask, I shall now explain. If you did not ask, I would never explain." Now, since it is made clear that the methods of repentance each differ and the numbers of days are not the same, we know that the Leader's seven-day pledged mind cannot be used to have those who have committed grave faults practice this real Dharma. Why? The karmic hindrances of offenses committed in the present life are especially heavy; if one does not add to one's effort in practice, there is no way to extinguish the offenses. For this reason the World-Honored One had pity, and Mañjuśrī made his request, and, to rescue the seven assemblies from the decline and distress of hell in the time of the turbid and evil world, the repentance of the particular mark was established.

The Second Condition: Good Friends

Chinese Source

二者善知識緣，有其三種：一者外護善知識，所謂能荷負眾事、供給所須，將護行人加以善事，猶如慈母養護嬰兒，勿令行人心有所念。二者同行善知識，謂是舊行道人，同行一道，互相勸發離彼我人。若見同行者有情念過生，即應當起悲愍之心如法教導，如人被火燒頭燒衣，救令速滅，火若未滅心不得安。亦如自身為毒所害，更無餘念。但為利益安樂行人，資長法身策勤修進，善和諍訟如水乳合，如同一船得失共之。行者亦爾，勸發未聞同得甘露，乘方便船至薩婆若海。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the condition of good friends, of which there are three kinds. First, the good friend who is an external protector: one who is able to shoulder all the affairs and supply what is needed, protecting the practitioner and adding good deeds, like a loving mother raising and protecting her infant, not letting the practitioner's mind have anything to worry about. Second, the good friend who is a fellow practitioner: an experienced practitioner of the path who practices the same path, each encouraging and arousing the other, free of [the distinctions of] that and I, self and person. If he sees the fault of emotional thoughts arising in a fellow practitioner, he should arouse a mind of compassion and guide him according to the Dharma, as when someone's head or clothes are on fire, one rescues him and makes it go out quickly, and while the fire is not out one's mind cannot be at ease. It is also like one's own body being harmed by poison: there are no other thoughts. It is only to benefit and bring ease and happiness to the practitioner, to nourish and grow the Dharma-body and spur on diligent cultivation and progress, skillfully harmonizing disputes like water and milk mixed, sharing gain and loss as in the same boat. Practitioners are also like this: encouraging and arousing [one another] in what they have not yet heard, together obtaining the nectar, and riding the boat of expedients to reach the ocean of sarvajña.

Chinese Source

三教授善知識者，謂行道日久親行分明，勝人所印，解內外律相，識遮障、知通塞，奉請為道場主，迎來送去，日三時禮拜，百味飲食而供養之。當於此人如醫王想，於自己身作癕瘡想；當於此人作天人想，於自己身如三惡道想；當於此人如橋梁想，於自己身如墮溺想；當於此人如正路想，於自己身如迷路想；當於此人生安樂國想，於自己身如牢獄人想。以生重心故，令一切障道罪滅。若無如上之人，但使戒根清淨，浮囊所護，雖不能言詞辯了廣明法相，亦得為次善知識也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the good friend who is an instructor: one who has practiced the path for a long time and whose own practice is clear, who has been certified by superior people, understands the marks of the inner and outer vinaya, recognizes hindrances, and knows what opens and what blocks. Respectfully invite him to be the master of the place of practice; welcome him on arrival and see him off on departure, bow to him three times a day, and make offerings to him of food and drink of a hundred flavors. Toward this person, think of him as a king of physicians, and think of your own body as an abscess; toward this person, think of him as a god, and think of your own body as the three evil paths; toward this person, think of him as a bridge, and of your own body as one sinking and drowning; toward this person, think of him as the right road, and of your own body as one who has lost the road; toward this person, give rise to the thought of a land of ease and happiness, and of your own body as a prisoner in jail. Because a weighty mind is produced, all offenses that obstruct the path are extinguished. If there is no such person as above, but only one whose precept roots are pure, protected as by a float, then though he cannot speak eloquently and clearly and expound the marks of dharmas broadly, he may still serve as a good friend of the second rank.

The Third Condition: Preliminary Expedients and the Twelve Dream Kings

Chinese Source

If a man or woman in a dream cultivates powers and is able to fly, with silk banners and canopies hanging behind this person — one who sees this is called Zuchaluo.

If a man or woman in a dream sees images, relic stūpas and shrines, or a great assembly of monks gathered — one who sees this is Jinchiluo.

Ru-Yi Translation

If a man or woman in a dream sees a king or a great minister wearing clean robes and riding alone on a white horse — one who sees this is Maochiluo.

Chinese Source

若有男女，於其夢中若見乘象渡於大江。如是見者，即是乾基羅。

Ru-Yi Translation

If a man or woman in a dream sees [oneself] riding an elephant across a great river — one who sees this is Qianjiluo.

Chinese Source

If a man or woman in a dream rides a camel up a high mountain — one who sees this is Duolinluo (the five signs above are the signs for householders).

若有比丘，求於此法，於其夢中上於高座轉于般若。如是見者，即是波林羅。

Ru-Yi Translation

If a bhikṣu seeking this method in a dream ascends a high seat and turns the prajñā [scripture] — one who sees this is Bolinluo.

Chinese Source

若有比丘，於其夢中到一樹下，上於戒壇授具足戒。如是見者，即是檀林羅。

If a bhikṣu in a dream goes to the foot of a tree, ascends the precept platform, and is given the full precepts — one who sees this is Tanlinluo.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

If a bhikṣu in a dream seats the Buddha's image, invites the assembly of monks, and sets out offerings — one who sees this is Chanduolinluo.

Chinese Source

若有比丘，於其夢中見有一樹華果茂盛，於其樹下入禪三昧。如是見者，即是窮伽林羅（此上四相，即是出家比丘相）。

Ru-Yi Translation

If a bhikṣu in a dream sees a tree with luxuriant flowers and fruit, and beneath that tree enters the samādhi of dhyāna — one who sees this is Qiongqielinluo (the four signs above are the signs for monastic bhikṣus).

Chinese Source

若有大王，於其夢中帶持刀劍遊行四方。見如是者，即是迦林羅（此是國王相）。

Ru-Yi Translation

If a great king in a dream carries a sword and travels in the four directions — one who sees this is Jialinluo (this is the sign for a king).

Chinese Source

若有大臣，於其夢中見有諸人，持諸水瓶洗浴其身，坌種種香著淨潔衣。見如是者，即是窮伽林羅（此是大臣相）。

Ru-Yi Translation

If a great minister in a dream sees people holding water jars and bathing his body, sprinkling him with various incenses, and [he] puts on clean robes — one who sees this is Qiongqielinluo (this is the sign for a great minister).

Chinese Source

若有夫人，於其夢中乘於白羊入於深水，於其水中有諸毒蛇。見如是者，即是波林羅（此一國王大臣夫人之相也。問曰：經中但言是夫人相，據何得知是王大臣夫人之相？答曰：上初五相，但言男子女人，即攝一切俗人盡。然經中更別說國王大臣之相，故知非前男子攝。今經既別言夫人，非前女人所攝，理是國王大臣夫人也。又夫既別舉，妻亦應爾也。又此祖茶羅等十二夢，即魔王發心，護助受持大方等陀羅尼者，是大菩薩也）。

Ru-Yi Translation

If a lady in a dream rides a white sheep into deep water, and in the water there are poisonous snakes — one who sees this is Bolinluo (this one is the sign for the wives of kings and great ministers. Question: The sūtra only says that this is the sign for a lady; on what basis do we know that it is the sign for the wives of kings and great ministers? Answer: The first five signs above speak only of men and women, which covers all lay people entirely. Yet the sūtra separately speaks further of the signs for a king and a great minister, so we know these are not covered by the "men" before. Now since the sūtra separately speaks of a lady, she is not covered by the "women" before; by reason, she is the wife of a king or great minister. Further, since the husbands are mentioned separately, the wives should be likewise. Further, these twelve dreams of Zuchaluo and the others are the Māra kings who have aroused the [bodhi] mind and protect and help those who receive and keep the Great Vaipulya Dhāraṇī; they are great bodhisattvas).

Chinese Source

如是見者，乃可為說七日行法。

Ru-Yi Translation

When they have seen this, only then may the seven-day method of practice be explained to them.

The Fourth Condition: Preparing Robes

Chinese Source

四辦衣緣者，出家在家皆須具備三種衣服，悉須新淨。若無新者，浣故令淨，以香湯渡之。亦得以布衣為上服。若三衣不具足者，應請一知法比丘作捨墮懺悔，如法受持三衣六物，乃至突吉羅罪皆須發露，則行成就。如上三種淨衣，一最上淨者，擬入道場中著。一衣次淨，擬從浴處趣道場時著。一衣擬常坐起時著。問：在家人三衣，為是俗衣？為是出家衣耶？答：經云：一是出家衣（但單縫者為異），此衣擬入道場時著。作三世諸佛法式，雖著出家衣服，然不應剃髮。亦須具楊枝澡豆水瓶食器坐具，如比丘法，將至道場。餘二是俗衣，用同前法。又須辦革屣兩量軟細者，又要須新作大小兩廁，與舊令別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the condition of preparing robes: monastics and householders alike must all be equipped with three kinds of garments, all of which must be new and clean. If there are no new ones, wash old ones until clean and pass them through fragrant water. Cloth garments may also be used as the upper garment. If one's three robes are not complete, one should invite a bhikṣu who knows the Dharma to perform the repentance for [an offense of] forfeiture-and-expiation, and receive and keep the three robes and six requisites according to the Dharma; even duṣkṛta offenses must all be revealed; then the practice will be accomplished. Of the three kinds of clean garments above: one, the most pure, is to be worn inside the place of practice; one garment, the next pure, is to be worn when going from the bathing place to the place of practice; one garment is to be worn when ordinarily sitting and rising. Question: Are the three garments of householders lay garments, or are they monastic robes? Answer: The sūtra says one is a monastic robe (differing only in being single-stitched); this robe is to be worn on entering the place of practice. Following the model of the buddhas of the three times, though they wear monastic robes, they should not shave their heads. They must also have willow twigs, washing powder, a water jar, an eating vessel, and a sitting cloth, as in the bhikṣu's method, and bring them to the place of practice. The other two are lay garments, used in the same way as before. One must also prepare two pairs of soft, fine leather sandals, and must also newly build two latrines, large and small, kept separate from the old ones.

The Fifth and Sixth Conditions: The Method of Practice and Offerings

Chinese Source

五行法緣者，月八日十五日可入道場。行人極多數可至十人已還，不應過數，則違教法。行者若多，即須別作道場。應作圓壇，縱廣一丈六尺，以香泥塗地，高座置上。請二十四像并座，各高一尺。繒旛二十四口。古鏡一面，以鎮道場。作五色圓蓋懸於壇上。行人可作五寸下脚床，面向佛坐。氈褥薦席皆須新淨。盡世珍妙嚴飾道場，燒香散華如法供養。日日掃灑，以種種香熏陸棧沈塗末海渚岸香。及以香湯常置一盆，於其板上洗穢入淨。脫故衣及革屣遠置於外，必以新淨之衣入於內淨，無令淨觸混雜。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the condition of the method of practice: one may enter the place of practice on the eighth or fifteenth day of the month. Practitioners, at the most numerous, may be up to ten people; one should not exceed the number, or it goes against the teaching. If the practitioners are many, a separate place of practice must be made. One should make a round altar, one zhang six chi across in length and breadth, smear the ground with fragrant paste, and place a high seat upon it. Invite twenty-four images, together with their seats, each one chi high. Twenty-four silk banners. One ancient mirror, to secure the place of practice. Make a round five-colored canopy and hang it above the altar. Practitioners may make couches with legs under five inches, and sit facing the Buddha. Felt mats, cushions, and straw mats must all be new and clean. Adorn the place of practice with the rarest treasures of the world, burn incense and scatter flowers, and make offerings according to the Dharma. Sweep and sprinkle every day, and perfume [it] with various incenses — frankincense, zhan, aloeswood, paste and powder, and the incense of the shores of ocean isles. Also always keep a basin of fragrant water, [where,] on a board, one washes off the impure and enters the pure. Take off old garments and leather sandals and leave them far outside; always enter the inner pure [area] in new, clean garments, and do not let the pure and the touched mix together.

Chinese Source

六供養緣者，隨力所辦，種種飲食一切器物皆以香湯淨之。若如山間不可得處故，須初日一供養，後七日滿一供養。解道場日，請眾僧不限多少，隨力堪能並無妨也。若能廣設悲敬二田，最為勝益。若有施主每日供養，故須別設，隨有充供。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, the condition of offerings: according to what one's means can provide, all kinds of food and drink and all vessels are purified with fragrant water. If, as in the mountains, these cannot be obtained, then there must be one offering on the first day and one offering at the end of the seven days. On the day the place of practice is dismantled, invite the assembly of monks without limiting the number; according to one's ability, there is no harm. If one can broadly set up the two fields of compassion and reverence, that is of the greatest benefit. If there is a donor who makes offerings every day, separate arrangements must be made, providing offerings according to what there is.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Zunshi's preface — Ciyun Zunshi (964–1032) wrote this preface when the text, brought from Japan by Jakushō (寂照, d. 1034) in Xianping 6 (1003), was printed. "The Hundred Records" is Guanding's Guoqing Bailu (T1934); the missing fifth section (practice) is covered there and in Calming and Contemplation, fascicle 2.

醒醐 — read as 醍醐 "ghee," the last of the Five Flavors; 醒 is probably a copying error.

上首 the Leader — in T1339 上首 is the name of a figure who teaches 恒伽 (Gaṅgā). Rendered as a name, "the Leader," throughout; "the Leader's seven-day pledged mind" is the general rite of the sūtra's first fascicle.

Sūtra quotations (unit 13) — the general method paraphrases T1339 fascicle 1 (上首告恒伽言… 若欲行時，七日長齋…); the particular methods paraphrase fascicle 4 (護戒分), where the counts and day numbers match those given here (1,400 / 87; 49 / 97; 600 / 67; 400 / 47). The sūtra says that novices and lay followers who see "each of these things" (一一事) in dreams are pure; Zhiyi's "two things" (二事) is translated as written. Source checked in the vault's T1339.

Mañjuśrī's question (unit 14) — paraphrases the opening of T1339 fascicle 4 (爾時文殊師利即從座起…). The words "If you did not ask, I would never explain" were not located verbatim in the vault's T1339.

Dream kings (units 34–45) — the twelve dream kings are from T1339 fascicle 2. Their names are transliterations; they are given here in pinyin, since no Sanskrit forms are attested. Variants against T1339: 祖茶羅 (sūtra 袒荼羅), 䈥持羅 (sūtra 斤提羅; the reading of 䈥 is uncertain, given as "Jinchiluo"), 骼駝 (sūtra 駱駝, "camel"), 白羊 (sūtra 羊車, "sheep cart"). 窮伽林羅 and 波林羅 each occur twice, as in the sūtra. Compare the site's Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 2, which renders 夢王 "the King of dreams."

The twelve dream kings as Māra kings (unit 45) — the interlinear note says these twelve are Māra kings who aroused the bodhi mind; in T1339 the twelve kings appear with the bodhisattva 華聚 after their conversion.

式又 (unit 24) — read as 式叉 (śikṣamāṇā).

曰力辦即作 (unit 27) — 曰 makes no sense here; translated as if 但 or 日 ("when one's means suffice"). Alternatively: "on the day one's means suffice, one does it."

悔懺 (unit 28) — inverted for 懺悔, translated as "repentance."

Three good friends (unit 32) — the same three kinds (external protector, fellow practitioner, instructor) appear in the site's Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 4 (Fifth, the good friend), whose terms are reused here. 浮囊 "float" alludes to the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's parable of the traveler crossing the sea with a float, cited in the site's Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 4. 安樂國 "land of ease and happiness" may mean Sukhāvatī; it is translated literally.

Robes and the altar (units 47–48) — compare the site's Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 2 (half walking, half sitting), which gives a shorter version of the same arrangements (fragrant paste, round altar, five-colored banners, twenty-four images, new clean robes, no more than ten people, lay practitioners with single-layered robes). 一丈六尺 is about 16 Chinese feet; measures are left in Chinese units. 捨墮 (naiḥsargika-pāyattika) is rendered "forfeiture-and-expiation"; 突吉羅 is duṣkṛta.

Incenses (unit 48) — 熏陸 frankincense; 棧 (棧香) a grade of aloeswood, left as "zhan"; 沈 aloeswood; 海渚岸香 "incense of the shores of ocean isles" follows T1339's 海渚岸香.

淨觸 (unit 48) — 觸 here means "touched," i.e., ritually contaminated, in contrast to 淨 "pure."
