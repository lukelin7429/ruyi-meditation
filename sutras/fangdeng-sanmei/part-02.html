Hindrances, Prohibitions, and the Inner Vinaya

This part contains the three surviving sections that follow the six conditions. The second section, on hindrances, is practical: it gives four adjustments for a week of intensive practice — bathing (with a sequence of four connected rooms for passing from impure to pure), food, walking, and sitting — and ends the account of sitting with a short passage on the real nature of mind, neither defiled nor pure. The third section sets the prohibitions: once the seven-day period has begun it may not be broken off; circumambulation and mantra are counted together, one hundred twenty circuits and recitations to a round, twenty-one rounds for the strongest practitioners; offenses must be revealed to the master of repentance; and the visions that arise, including the ten Dharma-princes, may be told only to the teacher. Along the way Zhiyi surveys the signs of evil and good karma that practice can bring up, from debts owed to the Three Jewels to signs of meditative attainment in the Tripiṭaka and bodhisattva teachings. The fourth section, the "inner vinaya," lists the dream signs that show violations of each class of precepts and of each of the ten evil actions, and the signs that show them purified, and closes by warning against taking up or rejecting any sign. The text ends here; its fifth and sixth sections were lost.

The Four Adjustments: Bathing

Chinese Source

第二識遮障，有四調適（一者洗浴調適、二者飲食調適、三者行道調適、四者坐禪調適）

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, Recognizing Hindrances, with Four Adjustments (first, adjusting bathing; second, adjusting food and drink; third, adjusting circumambulation; fourth, adjusting seated meditation)

Chinese Source

一洗浴調適者，三時行法調。秋夏內既熱，於洗浴非妨。春冬二時既寒，善須調適。若行人身羸，多以水澆腹，則發痢疾，妨廢行道。若能將慎調適得所，則無患難，不妨行道。若上廁，別著不淨衣，宜以灰汁香湯熟洗三洗，浴時以手薄拭令淨。其浴室極須如法。若有力能辦者，當造四間好舍悉令相連，間間密隔其內差互，皆安小門悉令相通。莊嚴一間以為道場，其次一間香泥塗地以為淨室，擬安上淨衣服，及供養灰火。其次一間亦以香泥塗治，擬安香湯火爐。一間作浴室及安次衣。行者若欲入道場時，先於浴室淨澡浴，以淨板承足，赤體入次淨室。入已却閉門，當以香湯灑身已，香烟熏身之足，然後入上淨室。入已還却閉門，當著衣服入於道場。若欲出道場時，先入淨室脫去衣服，赤身入於浴室，著次淨衣然後而出。每常如此。若行者要須行道，急時力未能辦如上室者，當近道場之側作一室，與道場相通。若猶不辦者，當以淨席淨縵幕等權時遮障作室，亦得通道場。結淨皆以香泥塗治，如道場無別，擬安上淨衣及以次淨衣。此二衣雖同一室，然須別處，莫令相觸。又浴室中安新淨鞋履一緉。澡浴已香湯灌之，并須以楊枝淨口，著淨鞋履，赤身入次淨室，乃至上淨，如上所說。雖不及前法護淨，亦得行道。若不如是護淨，則不如法，徒行無益，乃更招罪。是故行者努力護之，日三時浴者不可闕也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, adjusting bathing: in practicing the method at the three times [of day], adjust it. In autumn and summer, since it is hot, bathing does no harm. In spring and winter, since it is cold, one must adjust it well. If the practitioner's body is weak and he pours much water over the belly, it brings on dysentery and hinders and interrupts the circumambulation. If one can take care and adjust it properly, there will be no trouble, and circumambulation is not hindered. If one goes to the latrine, one wears separate impure clothes, and should wash thoroughly three times with lye water and fragrant water; when bathing, wipe lightly with the hand to make clean. The bathhouse must be entirely in accord with the Dharma. If one has the means to provide it, one should build four good rooms, all connected, each room closely partitioned and staggered within, each fitted with a small door so that all communicate. Adorn one room as the place of practice. Next, one room with the floor smeared with fragrant paste as the pure room, to hold the upper pure garments and the ash and fire for offerings. Next, one room also plastered with fragrant paste, to hold the fragrant water and the brazier. One room is made the bathroom, and also holds the next garments. When the practitioner wishes to enter the place of practice, he first bathes clean in the bathroom, sets his feet on a clean board, and enters the next pure room naked. Having entered, he closes the door behind him, sprinkles his body with fragrant water, and perfumes his body and feet with incense smoke; then he enters the upper pure room. Having entered, he again closes the door behind him, puts on his garments, and enters the place of practice. When he wishes to leave the place of practice, he first enters the pure room and takes off his garments, enters the bathroom naked, puts on the next pure garments, and then goes out. Always do it this way. If the practitioner urgently needs to practice and his means cannot yet provide rooms as above, he should make one room beside the place of practice, communicating with the place of practice. If even this cannot be provided, he should use clean mats, clean curtains, and the like as a temporary screen to make a room, which may also communicate with the place of practice. For marking off purity, all is plastered with fragrant paste, no different from the place of practice, to hold the upper pure garments and the next pure garments. Though these two garments are in the same room, they must be in separate places, not letting them touch. Also place in the bathroom one pair of new, clean shoes. After bathing, pour fragrant water over oneself, also clean the mouth with a willow twig, put on the clean shoes, and enter the next pure room naked, and so on up to the upper pure [room], as described above. Though this does not equal the former method in guarding purity, one may still practice. If one does not guard purity in this way, it is not according to the Dharma; practicing in vain is useless and instead invites offense. Therefore practitioners should strive to guard it; bathing three times a day must not be omitted.

Food, Circumambulation, and Seated Meditation

Chinese Source

二飲食調適者，行者以食為命，憑之進道。若過飽則身急，百脈不通，好多沈睡。若過饑則心懸不能觀行，身弱不能行道。若能調適饑飽得所，則身能行道。經云：有命有食，有身有道，依色報命而得法身慧命。若不宜身食，不得食之，若強食則發宿病。若識其性，即知此性熱此性冷，此能發病則不可食，此能資補應可食之。若調適得所，七日行法得成。若先來腹內病患，護行法故，忍食白飲白餅即差，勝餘治也。是故行者應善知節度，勿令因食為患閡也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, adjusting food and drink: the practitioner takes food as his life, relying on it to advance on the path. If he is overfull, the body is tight, the hundred channels do not flow, and he is much given to heavy sleep. If he is overhungry, the mind is in suspense and cannot contemplate and practice, and the body is weak and cannot circumambulate. If he can adjust hunger and fullness properly, the body is able to circumambulate. The sūtra says: "There being life, there is food; there being a body, there is the path; relying on the life of the form-retribution, one attains the wisdom-life of the Dharma-body." If a food does not suit the body, one must not eat it; if one forces oneself to eat it, it brings on old illnesses. If one recognizes its nature, one knows that this is hot in nature and this is cold in nature; this can bring on illness and so must not be eaten, this can nourish and replenish and so should be eaten. If one adjusts properly, the seven-day method of practice can be accomplished. If one previously had an illness in the belly, then for the sake of protecting the method of practice, bear with eating plain gruel and plain cakes, and it will be cured; this is better than other treatments. Therefore the practitioner should know moderation well, and not let food become a trouble or obstacle.

Chinese Source

三行道調適者，行道必藉脚以進步，善須將護也。可作氈鞋蒲革，務令寬大細軟，勿使研脚生瘡。行若過急，脚即楚痛。行若過緩，法復不成。若先太急，後則致患，故須初行道時三日徐行，漸調適已疾行非妨。若是寒時行疾，風扇下分冷，則腹內脹滿發痢，即宜加坐令下分暖，治之即差。行者當須善識對治，為之利益。若對治睡眠，可加行。行若散動，可加坐為對治也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, adjusting circumambulation: circumambulation necessarily relies on the feet to advance step by step, and they must be well cared for. One may make shoes of felt or of rush and leather, making sure they are roomy, fine, and soft, not letting them rub the feet and cause sores. If one walks too fast, the feet ache. If one walks too slowly, the method again is not accomplished. If one is too fast at first, trouble results later; therefore, when first circumambulating, one must walk slowly for three days, and after gradually adjusting, walking quickly does no harm. If in cold weather one walks fast, the wind fans the lower part and chills it, so that the belly swells and dysentery breaks out; then one should add sitting to warm the lower part, and treating it this way, it is cured. The practitioner must recognize the antidotes well, so that they are of benefit. To counter drowsiness, one may add walking. If the walking is scattered and agitated, one may add sitting as the antidote.

Chinese Source

四坐禪調適者，加趺正坐，以左脚置右脚上，以左手置右手上，牽衣近身對臍，開口三吐胸中穢氣。開口吐熱氣，閉口內冷氣，然後閉口齒才相拄，閉眼才斷外光，然後平面而住。以要言之，令身不寬不急，若寬則頭低垂，若急則胸背煩痛，故不寬不急是身調相。當調息令不澁不滑，若出入有聲及不細即是滑相，若結滯不通則是澁相。若隨息連綿微微，然遍諸毛孔出入，則資補四大易得禪定。取要言之，可自耳聽不聞聲者是息調相。當調心令不浮不沈，若覺觀攀緣則是浮相，若無所記錄即是沈相。浮則可以止攝歸心性，知性不動。沈則可以觀察起，令念慮明白。雖無能觀所觀，而法性平等非垢非淨即是實性。然此實性，不為二十五有生死所垢，亦不為萬行所淨，是則垢淨雙泯，無垢無淨猶如虛空，名為畢竟清淨。亦名心性真如、心性法界，諸佛之本源，一切眾生之實際。正觀了達無間現前，是名思惟諸佛一實。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, adjusting seated meditation: sit upright in the cross-legged posture, placing the left foot on the right foot and the left hand on the right hand; draw the robe close to the body, facing the navel; open the mouth and breathe out the foul air in the chest three times. Open the mouth to breathe out hot air and close the mouth to take in cool air; then close the mouth with the teeth just touching, close the eyes just enough to cut off outer light, and then keep the face level and remain so. To put it in brief, make the body neither slack nor tense: if slack, the head droops; if tense, the chest and back ache; therefore "neither slack nor tense" is the mark of the body adjusted. One should adjust the breath to be neither rough nor slippery: if, going out and in, it makes sound and is not fine, that is the mark of slipperiness; if it is knotted and stagnant and does not flow, that is the mark of roughness. If it follows the breath continuously and very faintly, going out and in through all the pores, it nourishes and replenishes the four elements, and dhyāna concentration is easily attained. To put it in brief, when one cannot hear it with one's own ears, that is the mark of the breath adjusted. One should adjust the mind to be neither floating nor sinking: if there is initial and sustained thought clinging to objects, that is the mark of floating; if there is nothing registered, that is the mark of sinking. When floating, one may use calming to gather [it] back to the nature of mind, knowing that the nature does not move. When sinking, one may use contemplation to examine and raise [it], making thought clear. Though there is no contemplating and nothing contemplated, the Dharma-nature is equal, neither defiled nor pure; this is the real nature. Yet this real nature is not defiled by the birth and death of the twenty-five existences, nor purified by the myriad practices; thus defilement and purity both dissolve, and without defilement and without purity it is like empty space; this is called ultimate purity. It is also called the suchness of the mind-nature, the dharma-realm of the mind-nature, the original source of all buddhas, the reality-limit of all sentient beings. Right contemplation penetrates it and it is present without interval; this is called "reflecting on the one reality of all buddhas."

The Prohibitions: The Pledged Mind and the Count of Mantras

Chinese Source

第三禁法者（一者七日要心及誦呪，二者請師受戒及發露，三者見善惡業相及法王子不得向他人說也）

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the Prohibitions (first, the seven-day pledged mind and the reciting of the mantra; second, inviting the teacher, receiving the precepts, and revealing; third, the signs of good and evil karma that are seen, and the Dharma-princes, must not be spoken of to others)

Chinese Source

一者七日要心及誦呪，遍奉請三尊在道場內，則有七日要期。無作戒法生起，更不得餘事中途有廢，一則違前要心，作禁法不成，則令善心有間，不得相續。二則行法不成，於後若更行道，還有障閡事起。行者必當慎之。行道法者有其三種，謂上中下。良為行人強羸不等、行有遲速，上品行者二十一周，中品行者十六周，下品行者十二周。一周有一百二十匝，誦呪一百二十遍為一周，取角為誌，若始此角還終此角。行道及誦呪，齊始齊終，不得賸一步少一字，不得欠一步賸一字，常須行與呪俱以為數定。若唯數行道，法亦不成。若唯數呪為遍，法亦不成，壞於禁法。若同行道人俱行俱誦，是則齊好。其中若遲駛不等，禁法不成。若人人捉珠，各自數遍，復不齊好。可預調習令熟、遲駛齊等，從容共行一百二十遍，不高不下不緩不急。誦呪令自耳聽分明，不得錯謬。但令用心為正，則作法成就。必不得生雜念間斷，遲亦無妨，但取二十一周為上（次十六，下十二）。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the seven-day pledged mind and the reciting of the mantra: once the three honored ones have been respectfully invited everywhere within the place of practice, there is a seven-day pledged period. The unmanifest precept-dharma arises, and one must not abandon it midway for other matters. First, this would go against the earlier pledged mind, the prohibitions would not be fulfilled, and the good mind would be interrupted and could not continue. Second, the method of practice would not be accomplished, and if one later practiced again, obstructions would still arise. Practitioners must be careful about this. The method of circumambulation is of three kinds, namely superior, middle, and inferior, because practitioners differ in strength and weakness and practice fast or slow. The superior practitioner does twenty-one rounds, the middle practitioner sixteen rounds, the inferior practitioner twelve rounds. One round has one hundred twenty circuits; reciting the mantra one hundred twenty times makes one round. Take a corner as the marker: if one begins at this corner, one also ends at this corner. Circumambulation and reciting the mantra begin together and end together; one must not have one step extra and one word short, nor one step short and one word extra; walking must always go with the mantra, so that the count is fixed. If one counts only circumambulation, the method again is not accomplished. If one counts only the mantra as recitations, the method again is not accomplished, and the prohibitions are broken. If fellow practitioners walk together and recite together, that is well matched. If among them some are slow and some fast, unequal, the prohibitions are not fulfilled. If each person holds beads and counts recitations separately, again it is not well matched. One may practice in advance until familiar, so that slow and fast are equal, and calmly walk together through one hundred twenty recitations, neither high nor low, neither slow nor rushed. Recite the mantra so that one's own ears hear it distinctly, without error. Simply make the use of the mind correct, and the method will be accomplished. One absolutely must not give rise to mixed thoughts and interruptions; slowness is no harm, but take twenty-one rounds as superior (next, sixteen; inferior, twelve).

The Prohibitions: Receiving the Precepts and Revealing Offenses

Chinese Source

二奉請懺悔主受戒發露者，請一比丘解內外律者為懺悔主。若受戒時師將至，大眾於佛像前，為受二十四重戒。行者起殷重心，如渴思飲、如饑遇食、如死得脫，若生此心，必能發方等淨戒，無作戒法任運增長。自要其心七日行法，受此戒時隨心遠志，乃至菩提受持此戒，皆須發究竟不退轉心。若欲行此法者，須識真偽可同行不。必是道伴，應當三呼共行此法，發大精進，勇猛不怯，能建大志，有大忍力，乃可共行。從今發心至妙菩提，修學此法。如不能發弘誓心，但為隨時小善，終不能以此實法相應。若於道場三尊前，不得委臥勒拄著地，不得語言調戲及睡，並作法不成。唯除道場主說法誠勸也。發露者，可人各就師盡所憶者，急皆發露可條疏。依此判相，知罪滅盡不盡。故經云，懺悔有二種，一者真實懺悔，所謂發露向人；二者虛妄懺悔，謂不發露，覆藏眾罪。罪轉益深，禁法不成。如欲染衣不却垢膩，雖加水色外增其垢。如欲治癕不令決破，終身抱疾。如人掘樹，更以糞壤壅根，終年茂盛。如是懺悔，罪終不滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, respectfully inviting the master of repentance, receiving the precepts, and revealing: invite one bhikṣu who understands the inner and outer vinaya to be the master of repentance. When receiving the precepts, the teacher having come, the great assembly, before the Buddha image, is given the twenty-four grave precepts. The practitioner arouses an earnest mind, like the thirsty longing for drink, like the hungry meeting food, like the dying obtaining release; if this mind arises, he will surely be able to bring forth the pure Vaipulya precepts, and the unmanifest precept-dharma will increase spontaneously. Pledging his own mind to the seven-day method of practice, when receiving these precepts he follows his mind's far-reaching aspiration, receiving and keeping these precepts up to bodhi; all must arouse the mind of ultimate non-retrogression. If one wishes to practice this method, one must recognize the true and the false — whether [another] can practice together or not. If he is surely a companion on the path, one should call three times to practice this method together: arousing great vigor, courageous and unafraid, able to establish a great aspiration and having great patience — only then may one practice together. "From the arousal of the mind now until wondrous bodhi, I will cultivate and learn this method." If one cannot arouse the mind of the vast vows, but does it only as a small good of the moment, one will never be able to correspond to this real Dharma. Before the three honored ones in the place of practice, one must not lie down slackly or prop oneself on the ground, nor talk, jest, or sleep; all these make the method not accomplished — except when the master of the place of practice speaks the Dharma and earnestly exhorts. As for revealing: each person may go to the teacher and urgently reveal everything he remembers, and may list them item by item. On this basis the signs are judged, to know whether the offenses are fully extinguished or not. Therefore the sūtra says: Repentance is of two kinds: first, true repentance, which is revealing to others; second, false repentance, which is not revealing and concealing one's many offenses. The offenses become ever deeper, and the prohibitions are not fulfilled. It is like wishing to dye cloth without removing the grime: though water and color are added, outwardly the grime only increases. It is like wishing to treat an abscess without letting it burst: one carries the disease for life. It is like a man digging up a tree who instead banks the roots with manure and soil: it flourishes year after year. Repentance like this — the offenses are never extinguished.

The Prohibitions: Signs of Evil Karma and Debts to the Three Jewels

Chinese Source

三者見善惡業相及十法王子等，不得向人說，唯得向師一人說決疑。行者過去今生，雖造種種眾行，不出總別二業，一總相業現、二別相業現。總相業者，不出善惡兩業。若從來不行道，覺觀覆蔽心目，善惡業皆不現。經云：一切眾生如大富盲人，雖有種種法寶而不得見。行者不可以過去習因難知，若行道功成，行者罪垢除滅，心路清淨善惡皆現，喻如水鏡澄明眾像皆現。若善惡業現者，不出四恩，所謂過去今生經負貸師僧、父、母、國王，信施財物相現。或五逆相現，所謂殺父母、殺和尚阿闍梨、出佛身血、殺阿羅漢、破轉法輪僧，現身白癩。若能至心懺悔，業轉病除相現。或可侵犯三寶及經知事，互用三寶物相現。或可作三寶田不還稅直，或用三寶淨人及牛車力，或偷盜三寶園果等，如是等相現時。師自當別，不可文載。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the signs of good and evil karma that are seen, and the ten Dharma-princes and so on, must not be spoken of to others; one may speak of them only to the teacher alone, to resolve doubts. Though the practitioner in past and present lives has done all kinds of deeds, they do not go beyond two kinds of karma, general and particular: first, the manifesting of general-mark karma; second, the manifesting of particular-mark karma. General-mark karma does not go beyond the two kinds of karma, good and evil. If one has never practiced the path, initial and sustained thought cover the mind's eye, and neither good nor evil karma manifests. The sūtra says: "All sentient beings are like a very rich blind man: though he has all kinds of Dharma-treasures, he cannot see them." The practitioner cannot [see them], because past habitual causes are hard to know; if the practice of the path succeeds, the practitioner's stains of offense are removed and extinguished, the road of the mind becomes pure, and good and evil all manifest, just as in a clear water-mirror all images appear. When good and evil karma manifest, they do not go beyond the four [to whom one owes] kindness: namely, signs appear of debts, in past and present lives, of goods and wealth borrowed from teachers and monks, father and mother, the king, or the faithful donors. Or signs of the five heinous offenses appear — namely killing father or mother, killing one's upādhyāya or ācārya, drawing blood from the Buddha's body, killing an arhat, or splitting the saṅgha of the turning Dharma-wheel — and white leprosy appears on the body. If one can repent with utmost mind, signs appear of the karma turning and the illness being removed. Or signs may appear of having infringed on the Three Jewels, and of having served as an administrator and used the property of the Three Jewels interchangeably. Or of having worked the fields of the Three Jewels without returning the rent, or of having used the Three Jewels' attendants and the strength of their oxen and carts, or of having stolen the orchard fruit of the Three Jewels, and so on. When such signs appear, the teacher will discern them himself; they cannot be set down in writing.

If the practitioner, in dreams or while walking or sitting, sees great mountain cliffs, blue-green in color, collapsing and falling, cutting off and blocking the road, or sees great waters flooding vastly, or sees defiling objects heaped up, blocking and cutting off [the way] so that he cannot get through, or on facing an object at once gives rise to a defiled mind, or sees his family behaving against the Dharma, know that this is the sign of sexual desire manifesting. If the practitioner, in dreams or while walking or sitting, sees coarse and evil matters, people slandering one another, or is deceived and misled by others and loses the right road, or though himself in the right is wronged and cannot get redress, know that this is the sign of the karma of false speech manifesting. If the practitioner, in dreams or while walking or sitting, sees two mountains blocking [the way] so that messages cannot pass, or sees relatives and friends separated and family members not in harmony, know that this is the sign of double-tongued speech manifesting. If the practitioner, in dreams or while walking or sitting, hears evil sounds roaring and quaking, his heart filled with alarm and fear and no place to hide himself, or sees someone cursing, saying, "You cheated me; now I repay you," or sees people fighting and quarreling with one another, know that this is the sign of harsh speech manifesting. If the practitioner, in dreams or while walking or sitting, sees the breath he exhales come out of his mouth and fall like dregs, or sees his own mouth speaking haltingly so that words do not come out, or if there are words they are not clear, and when he tells others they do not believe or accept them, know that this is the sign of the karma of frivolous speech manifesting. If the practitioner, in dreams or while walking or sitting, sees clouds rising like a black wind, or sees people of much desire greedily attached to their rewards, or sees the objects of the five sense-fields in confusion before him, or sees himself sinking and unable to get out, know that this is the sign of greed manifesting. If the practitioner, in dreams or while walking or sitting, sees a mountain of the seven treasures covered by clouds and mist and turned into earth and stone, and fear arises in his mind, or sees someone waving his fist and beating [him] in rage and fury, or sees people coming to harass and disturb him, or sees someone come and drag him off by his clothes, not letting go despite his apologies, or sees poisonous snakes covering the ground, spitting poison at people, know that this is the sign of anger manifesting. If the practitioner, in dreams or while walking or sitting, sees mixed sand, gravel, and stones soiling the practitioner, or sees the state before him covered as by broken clouds that come to darken the practitioner, so that the road of his mind is dark and blocked and he does not know which way to go, or sees black elephants crisscrossing, or sees people of the border lands who do not believe in the Three Jewels, with flattering, crooked, and hesitant minds, or sees [himself] abandoning the precepts and returning to lay life, know that this is the sign of wrong views manifesting.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

This briefly sets out ten kinds of coarse matters, the signs of the ten evil karmas manifesting. When the practitioner has seen such evils, if he can repent with utmost mind, the ten evil karmas are extinguished, and then good signs appear of themselves. If the practitioner sees black change into white and yellow, and beings looking upon one another with gladness of mind, with respect and love that never tire, know that this is the sign that the karma of killing has been repented and extinguished. If he sees jeweled trees with flowers and fruit complete, or sees all kinds of wealth that people give one another as gifts, know that this is the sign that the karma of stealing has been repented and extinguished. If he sees lotuses filling Jambudvīpa, or sees people all born by transformation from flowers, or sees someone before him explaining the pure and spotless Dharma, know that this is the sign that the karma of sexual misconduct has been repented and extinguished. If he sees someone showing a city of the seven treasures, and the people within the city show its fine and wondrous halls and dwellings, and his mind is joyful, and turning his face to other directions he sees them too, know that this is the sign that the karma of false speech has been repented and extinguished. If the practitioner sees an assembly gathered speaking the Dharma of harmony, meeting with joy, celebration, and happiness, know that this is the sign that the offense-karma of double-tongued speech has been repented and extinguished. If he sees a great road clean and pure, and a traveler returning from afar greeted with a kind heart as if by father and mother, or hears voices praising the Three Jewels, know that this is the sign that the karma of harsh speech has been extinguished. If the practitioner sees a good friend admonishing and encouraging him to be diligent, speaking the true essentials of the Dharma, so that faith and delight arise in his mind and he cultivates as taught, know that this is the sign that the karma of frivolous speech has been extinguished. If the practitioner sees [himself] undergoing great suffering and distress, and suddenly awakens [to see that] all of it comes from reckoning that body and mind exist, the root of all suffering — the bones and joints false and borrowed, [full of] all kinds of worms — and sees his own body as a great hell in which everything is burning with fire and consumed entirely, know that this is the sign that the karma of greed has been extinguished. If the practitioner sees all haggard beings change into handsome ones, facing one another with friendly faces, the sky opening and the sun appearing, everything bright, or sees withered trees flourish, know that this is the sign that the karma of anger has been extinguished. If the practitioner sees a wish-fulfilling jewel raining down all treasures, which beings gather and use without exhausting, or sees bhikṣus holding their alms bowls, so that love and delight arise in his mind, or sees bhikṣus who explain the excellent Dharma for him and call him to enter samādhi together, know that this is the sign that the karma of wrong views has been extinguished.

The Inner Vinaya: Questions on Signs and the True Mark

Chinese Source

問曰：相貌無量，行者修行各別、得法亦異，何一類耶？答曰：初云略示行者令知行道有此境界，直明五篇中一戒七支犯相，或緣貪起、或緣瞋起、或緣癡起。三七二十一犯相，有二十一持相，亦二十一初受戒時，始從阿鼻獄上至佛身，遍三千大千世界有情無情皆發無作戒。約一戒明持犯眾多，乃至大小乘不可說不可計微塵無邊故，持犯相亦無邊。或標相、或現相，不可文載。行者若自久行、或教人行，如上方法一一成就，自他既明，與經中皆合。佛不虛說，唯除不至心及緣不具，非可如何。若此救急之法，定能滅罪、增壽長福。若無如是知識，及新學三昧心強行者，此則如經中明得遮道法，如不善呪捉於毒蛇。十惡十善及障道法亦不可計，行者爾時自以智力斟酌，或他為決疑，莫見一相應生邪見。何以故？相相不同，隱顯輕重有異，心若明了，如鏡淨好醜自現。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The signs and appearances are immeasurable; practitioners cultivate differently and attain the Dharma differently; why [give] a single category? Answer: At the beginning it was said that this briefly shows the practitioner, so that he knows that in practicing the path there are such states. It directly clarifies the signs of violating the seven branches of a single precept among the five classes, arising either from greed, or from anger, or from delusion. Three times seven is twenty-one signs of violation; there are twenty-one signs of keeping, likewise twenty-one. At the time of first receiving the precepts, from Avīci hell up to the Buddha's body, throughout the trichiliocosm, the sentient and the insentient all bring forth the unmanifest precept. Taking one precept to clarify it, keeping and violating are many; and so on, since in the Great and Small Vehicles they are inexpressible, incalculable, boundless as dust motes, the signs of keeping and violating are also boundless. Some are indicative signs and some manifest signs; they cannot be set down in writing. If the practitioner has practiced long himself, or teaches others to practice, and accomplishes the methods above one by one, both self and others are clear, and all agrees with the sūtra. The Buddha does not speak falsely; only for one who is not of utmost mind, or whose conditions are not complete, can nothing be done. This emergency method can surely extinguish offenses, increase lifespan, and enlarge merit. If there is no such good friend, and for those new to studying samādhi who practice with forced minds, this is like what the sūtra explains as obtaining dharmas that obstruct the path, like one unskilled in spells catching a poisonous snake. The ten evils, the ten goods, and the dharmas that obstruct the path are also incalculable; at that time the practitioner should weigh them with the power of his own wisdom, or have another resolve his doubts; let him not, on seeing one sign, give rise to wrong views. Why? Each sign differs, hidden or apparent, light or heavy; if the mind is clear, then, as in a clean mirror, beauty and ugliness appear of themselves.

Question: The true mark of all dharmas has no marks of good or evil; how is it that in practicing the real Dharma of all buddhas there are marks that appear? Answer: The true mark of all dharmas is without marks, [yet] it can show the marks of the world. In practicing the path, the practitioner does not think of marks and does not think of no-marks, but only contemplates the real nature of the mind; when the mind is pure, all dharmas are pure; when the mind is exhausted, all dharmas are exhausted. Only, as mind after mind increases and the effort succeeds, marks appear, like a basin of water placed in a closed room: though it has no mind that discriminates, the many images appear of themselves. Question: When marks appear, the true and the false are hard to know; how can one distinguish them so as to take or reject? Answer: The true and the false are indeed hard to recognize; a good friend will decide for one. Even so, the practitioner, in all four postures, must not take up any mark and must not reject [any]. If thoughts, ideas, and contemplations are removed, one can see prajñā; to take is like a man taking hold of empty space; to reject is like a man rejecting empty space. Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

Four adjustments (units 0–4) — the adjustments of body, breath, and mind in unit 4 closely parallel Zhiyi's treatment of 調身、調息、調心 in his other meditation manuals (e.g., Xiao Zhiguan). 覺觀 is rendered "initial and sustained thought" (vitarka and vicāra).

"There being life, there is food" (unit 2) — source not located in the vault's Taishō files.

Rounds and circuits (unit 6) — 周 "round" = 120 circuits (匝) with 120 recitations; superior 21 rounds, middle 16, inferior 12. The site's Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 2, gives the same "one mantra for each circuit, neither too slow nor too fast, neither too loud nor too soft."

無作戒 "the unmanifest precept" — the avijñapti of the precepts, the "body" of the precepts said to arise at ordination; here it arises when the seven-day period is pledged.

二十四重戒 (unit 7) — the twenty-four precepts of T1339, fascicle 1 (是名菩薩摩訶薩二十四戒), conferred within the rite; compare the site's Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 2 ("receive from them the twenty-four precepts").

委臥勒拄著地 (unit 7) — the sense of 勒拄 is unclear; translated "prop oneself on the ground." Alternatively: "lean on a staff planted on the ground."

Two kinds of repentance and the three similes (unit 7) — introduced as "the sūtra says"; source not located in the vault's T1339.

"A very rich blind man" (unit 8) — similar wording is in the Great Treatise (T1509, 譬如大富盲人，多有伏藏); the site's Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 1, has "like a wealthy blind child sitting in the midst of a treasure-store."

花聚 (unit 8) — 華聚 is the bodhisattva Flower Cluster of T1339, to whom the dhāraṇī is entrusted; "not included in Flower Cluster" seems to refer to the sūtra (or its lists of signs). Translated "the Flower Cluster [text]."

救疾經 (unit 8) — the Sūtra on Rescuing from Illness (救護眾生惡疾經) is listed in catalogs such as Lidai Sanbao Ji (T2034), but its text is not in the Taishō; the quotation's source was not located. The same rates are repeated in later vinaya works.

依報 (unit 8) — "dependent reward" is the glossary term; here it means one's material circumstances, i.e., the means to repay.

Debts to the Three Jewels — the site's Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 8 (the object of the marks of karma), discusses the same question, citing the Vaipulya masters and Nanyue.

□諦 (unit 8) — one character is missing in CBETA. With 中道第一義諦 and 真諦 named, the missing one is probably 俗 (the conventional truth); the gap is kept in the translation.

相 "sign" and "mark" — 相 is rendered "sign" for the karmic signs throughout, but "mark" in the general mark / particular mark and in the closing question on the true mark (實相), where the argument turns on the word. 標相／現相 are "indicative signs" / "manifest signs."

Five classes of precepts (unit 11) — 五篇: pārājika, saṅghāvaśeṣa, pāyattika (with the naiḥsargika), pratideśanīya, duṣkṛta. Here the "third class" and "the ninety matters within the third class" are treated separately, and 六聚／七聚 "the six groups and the seven groups" cover the remaining minor classes. 內律要決 in the heading (unit 9) is written 要訣 in the table of contents (Part 1, unit 9).

"Second" (unit 10) — the unit begins 第二別明善惡業現者, though it introduces the first point of the fourth section; translated as written.

掫臆 (unit 12) — the meaning is unclear; translated "heaped up." Alternatively 掫 may be a copying error.
