Zhili's Preface, the Commentary's Preface, and the Buddha in Principle and in Name

This part is the first fascicle of Siming Zhili's Wondrous Purport (T1751), written in 1021 as a line-by-line sub-commentary on Zhiyi's Commentary on the Sūtra on the Contemplation of Amitāyus Buddha. Zhili's own short preface says why he wrote: earlier commentators, beginning with his teacher Baoyun, stressed phenomenal practice for ordinary people, and he wants to show the gate of contemplation, so that one recollects the Buddha "right in this mind." He then explains the Commentary's title and reads its preface phrase by phrase. The contrast of the land of bliss and the realm of suffering is extended to all four lands, and tranquil light too is said to have its "ultimate gold and jewels." The six phrases of mutual refutation, cultivation, and identity settle how a teaching that takes up the pure and rejects the defiled can still be perfect. The core of the fascicle is the cardinal doctrine, "the contemplation of mind": the Buddha is contemplated in terms of the mind, because the mind inherently contains and creates the dependent and principal [rewards]. The fascicle ends inside the first profound meaning, Explaining the Name. There Zhili reads common and particular as teaching, practice, and principle, and treats the first two of the Buddha's six identities, in principle (grounded in inherent evil) and in name.

Title and Zhili's Preface

Chinese Source

觀無量壽佛經疏妙宗鈔卷第一

Ru-Yi Translation

The Wondrous Purport: Sub-commentary on the Commentary on the Sūtra on the Contemplation of Amitāyus Buddha, Fascicle One

Chinese Source

宋四明沙門知禮述

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Zhili, a śramaṇa of Siming, of the Song

Chinese Source

此經義疏，人悕淨報，故說聽者多矣。所稟寶雲師首製記文，相沿至今著述不絕，皆宗智者，豈有不知修心妙觀、感四淨土文義者耶？良以愍物情深、適時智巧，故多談事相、少示觀門，務在下凡普霑緣種。方今嘉運，盛演圓乘，慕學之徒皆欲得旨而修證矣。故竭鄙思，鈔數千言，上順妙宗，略消此疏。適時之巧，非我所能，願共有情即心念佛，乃此鈔所以作也。天禧五年歲在辛酉，重陽日下筆故序。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because people long for the pure reward, those who have lectured on and listened to the commentary on this sūtra have been many. The master from whom I received [the teaching], Baoyun, was the first to compose a record on it, and works on it have followed one another down to the present without a break; all take Zhizhe as their source. How could any of them fail to know the text and meaning of the wondrous contemplation of cultivating the mind and of eliciting the four pure lands? It is truly because their pity for beings was deep and their wisdom was skillful in suiting the times that they mostly discussed phenomenal marks and seldom showed the gate of contemplation, intent on letting ordinary people of the lower [capacities] all be moistened with seeds of connection. Now, in this auspicious age, the perfect vehicle is expounded abundantly, and those who aspire to learn all wish to attain its purport and to cultivate and realize it. Therefore I have exhausted my humble thought and set down several thousand words of sub-commentary, above following the wondrous purport and briefly explaining this commentary. The skill of suiting the times is not something I am capable of; I wish, together with all sentient beings, to recollect the Buddha right in this mind — this is why this sub-commentary was made. In the fifth year of Tianxi, the year being xinyou, on the day of the Double Ninth, I set brush to paper; hence this preface.

The Title of the Commentary: Mind and the Pure Land

Chinese Source

此之疏題，佛等八字備舉經目，皆是所釋，唯疏一字是能釋也。今之五章，釋其八字，義稍委悉，入文自見。若欲預知，可陳梗概。經是通號，餘是別名。今且明別。「佛說」者，釋迦化主，四辯宣演也。「觀」者，總舉能觀，即十六觀也。「無量壽佛」者，舉所觀要攝十五境也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the title of this commentary, the eight characters from "Buddha" on fully present the title of the sūtra, and all of them are what is explained; only the one word "commentary" is that which explains. The five sections [of the Commentary] explain those eight characters; their meaning is fairly detailed, as will be seen on entering the text. If one wishes to know in advance, an outline may be set out. "Sūtra" is the common designation; the rest is the particular name. Let us now make clear the particular. "The Buddha speaks": Śākyamuni, the lord of the teaching, proclaims and expounds with the four kinds of eloquence. "Contemplation" raises in general that which contemplates, that is, the sixteen contemplations. "Amitāyus Buddha" raises the essential [object] contemplated, which includes the fifteen [other] objects.

Chinese Source

且置能說，略明所說。能觀皆是一心三觀，所觀皆是三諦一境。毘盧遮那遍一切處，一切諸法皆是佛法。所謂眾生，性德之佛，非自非他非因非果，即是圓常大覺之體。故《起信論》云「所言覺義者，謂心體離念。離念相者，等虛空界無所不遍。法界一相即是如來常住法身，依此法身說名本覺。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Setting aside for now the one who speaks, let us briefly make clear what is spoken. What contemplates is in every case the threefold contemplation in a single mind; what is contemplated is in every case the single object of the three truths. Vairocana pervades all places; all dharmas are the Buddha-Dharma. The so-called beings are the buddha of inherent virtue, neither self nor other, neither cause nor fruit; this is the essence of perfect, eternal great awakening. Hence the Awakening of Faith says, "What is called the meaning of awakening is that the essence of mind is free of thought. The mark of being free of thought is equal to the realm of empty space, pervading everywhere. The single mark of the dharma-realm is the eternally abiding Dharma-body of the Tathāgata; on the basis of this Dharma-body it is called original awakening."

Chinese Source

故知果佛圓明之體，是我凡夫本具性德。故一切教所談行法，無不為顯此之覺體，故四三昧通名念佛，但其觀法為門不同。如一行三昧，直觀三道顯本性佛；方等三昧，觀袒持顯；法華兼誦經，觀音兼數息，覺意歷三性。此等三昧，歷事雖異、念佛是同，俱為顯於大覺體故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence we know that the perfect, bright essence of the buddha of the fruit is the inherent virtue originally possessed by us ordinary beings. Therefore the practices discussed in all the teachings are without exception for revealing this essence of awakening; hence the four samādhis are all commonly called buddha-recollection, only their methods of contemplation differ as gates. For example, the single-practice samādhi directly contemplates the three paths to reveal the buddha of original nature; the Vaipulya samādhi reveals it through contemplation and holding [the dhāraṇī]; the Lotus [samādhi] combines [contemplation] with reciting the sūtra; the Avalokiteśvara [samādhi] combines it with counting the breath; and the awareness of intention passes through the three natures. Though these samādhis differ in the phenomena they pass through, they are the same in being buddha-recollection, for all of them are for revealing the essence of great awakening.

Chinese Source

雖俱念佛，而是通途，顯諸佛體。若此觀門及般舟三昧，託彼安養依正之境，用微妙觀，專就彌陀顯真佛體。雖託彼境，須知依正同居一心。心性遍周，無法不造、無法不具。若一毫法從心外生，則不名為大乘觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Though all are buddha-recollection, they are the general route, revealing the essence of all buddhas. As for this gate of contemplation and the pratyutpanna samādhi, they rely on the object of the dependent and principal [rewards] of that [land of] Peaceful Nourishment and, using wondrous contemplation, reveal the essence of the true buddha specifically in terms of Amitābha. Though relying on that object, one must know that the dependent and principal dwell together in a single mind. The nature of mind pervades everywhere: there is no dharma it does not create, no dharma it does not inherently contain. If a single hair's worth of dharma were born from outside the mind, it would not be called Mahāyāna contemplation.

Chinese Source

行者應知，據乎心性觀彼依正，依正可彰；託彼依正觀於心性，心性易發。所言心性具一切法造一切法者，實無能具所具、能造所造，即心是法、即法是心，能造因緣及所造法皆悉當處全是心性。是故今觀若依若正，乃法界心觀法界境，生於法界依正色心。是則名為唯依唯正、唯色唯心、唯觀唯境。

Ru-Yi Translation

Practitioners should know: if one contemplates those dependent and principal [rewards] on the basis of the nature of mind, the dependent and principal can be manifested; if one contemplates the nature of mind by relying on those dependent and principal [rewards], the nature of mind is easily disclosed. As for saying that the nature of mind inherently contains all dharmas and creates all dharmas: in truth there is no containing and contained, no creating and created; the mind is identical to dharmas and dharmas are identical to the mind, and the causes and conditions that create and the dharmas created are all, right where they are, wholly the nature of mind. Therefore in the present contemplation, whether of the dependent or of the principal, it is the mind of the dharma-realm contemplating the object of the dharma-realm, giving birth to the dependent and principal, form and mind, of the dharma-realm. This is then called "only dependent, only principal; only form, only mind; only contemplation, only object."

Chinese Source

故釋觀字，用一心三觀。釋無量壽，用一體三身。體宗力用義並從圓，判教屬頓。五重玄義本是經中所詮觀法，大師預取解釋經題，欲令行者用此視法入十六門而為修證，故於序文以主包眾、以正收依。觀佛既即三身，觀餘豈非三諦？

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore in explaining the word "contemplation," [the Commentary] uses the threefold contemplation in a single mind; in explaining "Amitāyus," it uses the three bodies in a single essence. Essence, cardinal doctrine, and power-and-function all follow the perfect in meaning, and in classifying the teaching it belongs to the sudden. The fivefold profound meaning is originally the method of contemplation expressed in the sūtra; the Great Master took it in advance to explain the sūtra's title, wishing to let practitioners use this method of seeing to enter the sixteen gates and cultivate and realize them. Hence in the preface he encompasses the assembly by the lord and includes the dependent by the principal. Since contemplating the Buddha is identical to the three bodies, how could contemplating the rest not be the three truths?

Chinese Source

寄語行者，觀雖深妙，本被初心。若能進功，何憂不就？縱未入品，為因亦強。生至彼邦，得預大會，所見依正微妙難思，速入聖階、度生亦廣，永異事善及小乘行。得往生者，如此土人宿圓修者，於諸座席見相殊常、聞法易悟。以此類彼，功在妙宗。

Ru-Yi Translation

I send word to practitioners: though the contemplation is deep and wondrous, it is originally meant for the beginning mind. If you can advance in effort, why worry that it will not be accomplished? Even if you have not yet entered the grades, as a cause it is still strong. Born in that land, you get to join the great assembly; the dependent and principal you see there are subtle and wondrous beyond thought; you swiftly enter the stages of the sages and deliver beings widely as well — forever different from phenomenal good and the practice of the lesser vehicle. Those who attain rebirth are like people of this land who cultivated the perfect in past lives: in every seat [of teaching] they see the marks as extraordinary and, hearing the Dharma, easily awaken. Comparing that with this, the merit lies in the wondrous purport.

Chinese Source

但為戒福不精、無往生願，故在穢土聞法入真。須懼娑婆不常值佛，縱遇善友，色心不勝，難發我心，況塵境麁強，誠為險處。故須外加事懺、內勤理觀，正助雙行，加願要制，必於寶剎速證無生。今解觀門，其意在此。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is only because their precepts and merit were not refined and they had no vow of rebirth that they heard the Dharma and entered the real in the defiled land. One must fear that in Sahā one does not always meet a buddha; even meeting a good friend, one's form and mind are not equal to it, and it is hard to arouse one's own mind; how much more, with the dust-objects coarse and strong, is it truly a perilous place. Therefore one must externally add repentance through phenomena and internally be diligent in the contemplation of principle, practicing principal and auxiliary together and adding vows as a firm restraint; then one will certainly realize non-arising swiftly in the jeweled land. The present explanation of the gate of contemplation has its intent in this.

Chinese Source

「疏」者，疎也、決也。疎通決擇上之義趣通而不壅，令其行者得意修之故也。次能說人號，備於別傳及諸章記，有未知者須尋彼文。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Commentary" (疏) means to open up (疎) and to decide (決): opening up and deciding the meaning and import above so that it flows through without obstruction, letting practitioners obtain the meaning and cultivate it. Next, the designation of the person who speaks is fully given in the Separate Biography and the various records; those who do not yet know it should seek out those texts.

The Preface: Pure and Defiled Lands

Chinese Source

二、釋文。初、釋序，三：初、敘經觀意，二：初、正明觀行，二：初、敘意，二：初、對垢立淨，二：初、法，二：初、明二報苦樂。欲論觀行，先示二報苦樂之相。文有四句，一一皆論淨穢相對。初句以所成國土苦樂相對。安養淨國但受諸樂，故名樂邦；堪忍穢土多受眾苦，義言苦域。次句以能成物體貴賤相對。彼純七珍，略言金寶；此多眾穢，略語泥沙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the text. First, explaining the preface, in three: first, setting out the intent of the sūtra's contemplation, in two: first, directly making clear contemplative practice, in two: first, setting out the intent, in two: first, establishing the pure in contrast to the defiled, in two: first, the dharma, in two: first, making clear the suffering and bliss of the two rewards. Before discussing contemplative practice, [the preface] first shows the marks of suffering and bliss of the two rewards. The text has four phrases, each of which discusses pure and defiled in contrast. The first phrase contrasts suffering and bliss in terms of the lands that are formed. The pure land of Peaceful Nourishment receives only bliss, hence it is called the land of bliss; the defiled land of Endurance mostly receives sufferings, and in meaning is called the realm of suffering. The next phrase contrasts noble and base in terms of the substance of things that form [the lands]. That [land] is purely the seven treasures, briefly called gold and jewels; this [land] has many defilements, briefly called mud and sand.

Chinese Source

次句以初生受質垢淨相對。此土六道具有四生，今就人中多從胎藏，母食冷熱及飢飽時，兒在胎中如處寒熱倒懸山壓地獄之苦，故云胎獄；彼土九品，八從蓮生，下品之人雖經多劫，大本中說，疑心修善生彼胎宮樂同忉利，況八九品不生疑惑，豈有苦耶，是故華池受生即樂。次句以生後遊處麁好相對。此則荊棘叢林，彼則金渠玉樹。

Ru-Yi Translation

The next phrase contrasts defiled and pure in terms of first receiving a body at birth. In this land the six paths all have the four modes of birth; now, among humans, most come from the womb. When the mother eats cold or hot [food], or is hungry or full, the child in the womb is as if in the hells of cold and heat, of hanging upside down, and of being crushed by mountains; hence it says "womb-prison." In that land of the nine grades, eight are born from lotuses; though people of the lower grade pass many eons [in the lotus], the larger version says that those who cultivate good with a doubting mind and are born in its womb-palace have bliss equal to that of the Trāyastriṃśa [heaven]; how much more the eight or nine grades, who do not give rise to doubt — how could there be suffering? Therefore receiving birth in the flower-pond is bliss itself. The next phrase contrasts coarse and fine in terms of the places one roams after birth. This [land] has thorny thickets and forests; that [land] has golden channels and jade trees.

Chinese Source

然此四句，雖一一句苦樂相對，意則對穢顯彼淨相。又復應知，四句之文似唯顯示同居二土。據下明宗具論四土淨穢之相，以後驗此，不專同居。當知四句一一通於四種淨穢：見思輕重，則感同居樂邦苦域；體析巧拙，則感方便樂邦苦域；次第頓入，則感實報樂邦苦域；分證究竟，則感寂光樂邦苦域。以例金寶泥沙、胎獄華池、棘林瓊樹，亦復如是。一家制立，正文與序必不相違；但序總示、文宗別說，是故似異。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet in these four phrases, although each phrase contrasts suffering and bliss, the intent is to reveal the pure marks of that [land] in contrast to the defiled. Further, you should know that the text of the four phrases seems to show only the two [kinds of] land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together. But [the section on] Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine below discusses in full the pure and defiled marks of the four lands; verifying this by what follows, [the four phrases] are not limited to [the land of] dwelling together. You should know that each of the four phrases extends to all four kinds of pure and defiled: views and habituated thought, light or heavy, elicit the land of bliss and the realm of suffering of [the land of] dwelling together; the skill or clumsiness of embodying or analysis elicits the land of bliss and the realm of suffering of the land of expedients; gradual or sudden entry elicits the land of bliss and the realm of suffering of the land of true reward; partial or ultimate realization elicits the land of bliss and the realm of suffering of tranquil light. Gold and jewels and mud and sand, the womb-prison and the flower-pond, the thorny forest and the jade trees are likewise by analogy. In what our school establishes, the main text and the preface certainly do not contradict each other; but the preface shows things in general, while the text and [the section on] the cardinal doctrine explain them separately, and therefore they seem different.

Chinese Source

問：下三淨土既皆有相，則可論於金寶等事；寂光之淨已全無相，如何可說金寶華池及以瓊樹？答：經論中言寂光無相，乃是已盡染礙之相，非如太虛空無一物。良由三惑究竟清淨，則依正色心究竟明顯。故《大經》云「因滅是色獲得常色，受想行識亦復如是。」《仁王》稱為法性五陰，亦是《法華》世間相常，《大品》色香無非中道，是則名為究竟樂邦、究竟金寶、究竟華池、究竟瓊樹。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since the three lower pure lands all have marks, one can discuss such things as gold and jewels; but the purity of tranquil light is already wholly without marks — how can one speak of gold and jewels, flower-ponds, and jade trees? Answer: When the sūtras and treatises say that tranquil light is without marks, it is the marks of defilement and obstruction that are already exhausted; it is not like the great void, empty of any single thing. It is truly because the three delusions are ultimately purified that the dependent and principal, form and mind, are ultimately clear and manifest. Hence the Great Sūtra says, "Through the cessation of this very cause, one attains a permanently abiding form; and feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness are exactly the same." The Humane Kings calls them the five aggregates of dharma-nature; this is also the Lotus's "the marks of the world are constantly abiding" and the Great [Perfection of Wisdom]'s "form and fragrance are none other than the middle way." This then is called the ultimate land of bliss, ultimate gold and jewels, ultimate flower-ponds, ultimate jade trees.

Chinese Source

又復此就捨穢究盡、取淨窮源，故苦域等判屬三障、樂邦金寶以為寂光。若就淨穢平等而談，則以究竟苦域泥沙而為寂光。此之二說，但順悉檀無不圓極。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, this speaks in terms of abandoning the defiled to the very end and taking up the pure to its very source; hence the realm of suffering and the rest are assigned to the three obstructions, and the land of bliss and the gold and jewels are taken as tranquil light. If one speaks in terms of the equality of pure and defiled, then the ultimate realm of suffering and mud and sand are taken as tranquil light. These two explanations, simply following the siddhāntas, are none other than perfect and ultimate.

Chinese Source

問：佛無上報是即理之事，可論金等；究竟寂光是即事之理，豈有金等？若其同有，事理既混，如何分於二土義耶？答：佛無上報是究竟始覺，上品寂光是究竟本覺，始本既極，豈分二體？應知二土縱分事理，實非有無，豈真善妙有而非理邪？祕藏之理豈同小空？故此事理二名一體，以復本故名無上報事也，以復本故名上寂光理也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The Buddha's unsurpassed reward is phenomena identical to principle, so one can discuss gold and so on; the ultimate tranquil light is principle identical to phenomena — how can there be gold and so on? If both alike have them, phenomena and principle are confused; how then can the meaning of two lands be distinguished? Answer: The Buddha's unsurpassed reward is ultimate initial awakening; tranquil light of the highest grade is ultimate original awakening. Once initial and original are both at their limit, how could they be divided into two essences? You should know that, though the two lands are distinguished as phenomena and principle, in reality they are neither existent nor nonexistent. How could true, good, wondrous existence not be principle? How could the principle of the secret treasury be the same as the small emptiness? Hence these two names, phenomena and principle, are one essence: because of returning to the origin it is called the phenomena of the unsurpassed reward; because of returning to the origin it is called the principle of the highest tranquil light.

Chinese Source

故妙樂云：修得四德、本有四德，二義齊等，方是遮那身土之相。況《淨名疏》顯將寂光為佛依報，故知定執報土有金寶等、寂光定無，斯乃迷名全不知義矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence Miaole says: "The four virtues attained through cultivation and the four virtues originally present — only when the two meanings are equal is this the mark of Vairocana's body and land." Moreover, the Commentary on the Vimalakīrti explicitly takes tranquil light as the Buddha's dependent reward. Hence we know that rigidly holding that the land of reward has gold and jewels while tranquil light definitely does not is to be confused by the names and wholly fail to know the meaning.

The Two Causes: Mind and Practice

Chinese Source

二、「誠由」下，明二因心行。誠，實也。由，從也。報之淨穢，實從心行二因致感。心即迷了二心，行即違順二行。六道三教，迷三德性、為三惑染，故曰垢心。身口諸業違理有作，皆名惡行。此之心行感四穢土，沈下麁淺也。唯圓頓教，了三德性、離三惑染，方名淨心。身口諸業順理無作，稱為善行。此之心行感四淨土，高升深妙也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "truly it is because" on, making clear the two causes, mind and practice. "Truly" (誠) means "really" (實); "because" (由) means "from" (從). The purity or defilement of the reward really comes about from the stimulus of the two causes, mind and practice. "Mind" is the two minds of delusion and understanding; "practice" is the two practices of going against and according with [principle]. The six paths and the three teachings are deluded about the nature of the three virtues and defiled by the three delusions; hence it says "defiled mind." All actions of body and speech that go against principle and are conditioned are called evil practice. This mind and practice elicit the four defiled lands, sinking, coarse, and shallow. Only the perfect-and-sudden teaching understands the nature of the three virtues and is free from the defilement of the three delusions; only this is called the pure mind. Awakening of Faith. The three quotations are from T1666. The first reads 如來平等法身 there, where Zhili has 常住法身. The "ultimate awakening" passage is abridged (it omits 得見心性). The last line, 心體離念…本性明了, is a paraphrase; 本性明了 was not found in T1666.

Sources Zhili names. The deviant master and Zhanran's reply are in the Meaning and Examples of Calming and Contemplation (止觀義例, T1913). Zhili paraphrases its 喻曰 passage on 頓頓 and 漸圓. Miaole's two sayings (修得四德…; 顯露彰灼…) are in the Textual Commentary Record (法華文句記, T1719), fascicles 9 and 3. The Golden Light line 十方諸佛常念是經 is a verse of T0663, "Four Heavenly Kings" chapter. 一中解無量 is in both Avataṃsaka translations (T0278, T0279). The wax-seal simile is in T0374, the "Siṃhanāda Chapter," and so is the Sudatta story, which Zhili retells in brief.

Sources not located. The "Kāśyapa Chapter" line "Beings are identical to buddha … apart from beings one cannot attain saṃbodhi" was not located verbatim. T0374 has 眾生即佛性 in that chapter. The Precious Casket verse of Shengzhi (己界及法界…) was not located in T0462. 仁王's 法性五陰 was not located verbatim in T0245 or T0246. 縱觀他境亦須約心 was not located in T1716, and the Satyasiddhi point on the intermediate state was not located verbatim.

Contemplating the Buddha in terms of the mind (約心觀佛). The sūtra's title says "contemplation of the Buddha" and the Commentary's cardinal doctrine says "contemplation of mind." Zhili joins them: this is "outer" contemplation that relies on Amitābha's land, yet never leaves the nature of mind, because the mind inherently contains (具) and creates (造) all dharmas. The renderings keep 具 "contain," 造 "create," 作 "make up," and 即 "identical to" apart throughout.

The six identities. 理是、名字是… is rendered "being it in principle, being it in name …," because Zhili glosses 即 as 是 ("'identity' means 'is'"). 理即佛 and the Commentary's 理佛 are both "the buddha in principle." The argument that the buddha in principle rests on 性染性惡 (inherent defilement, inherent evil) is Zhili's own signature teaching.

"Now our Zhizhe" (今我智者). This explains why the Commentary says the principle is "evident" (灼然). The subject is Zhiyi, whose physical eye is called a buddha-eye. It could also be read "we who are wise," but the following lines (祕妙觀, 灼然) favor Zhiyi.

"Because of returning to the origin" (以復本故). The same phrase is given for both the phenomenal reward and principled tranquil light. Alternatively, Zhili means initial awakening "returns to" the origin while original awakening is the origin "returned to." The literal repetition is kept.

Leaping over the divisions (跨節). This term recurs twice. It means reading a pre-Lotus text with the Lotus's opened-up meaning, skipping over the period in which it was taught.

Other terms. 二喻 "two similes" (form/shadow, source/stream) reverse into contrary and compliant cultivation (逆修／順修). Zhili's 鴻漸 "gradual advance like the wild goose" echoes the Yijing hexagram Jian. 兩楹之際 "between the two pillars" echoes the Liji (Confucius's dream). 自非道場得入三昧發旋總持 alludes to Zhiyi's awakening in the Lotus samādhi under Huisi, when he gained the revolving dhāraṇī.
