The Two Bodhisattvas, the Nine Grades, and the Transmission

The last fascicle finishes Zhili's defense of the ninth contemplation's body as the specially honored body, answering objections drawn from the Commentary on the Lotus, the larger Sūtra of Immeasurable Life, and the Pratyutpanna: the thirty-two marks are the constant body of Sukhāvatī seen by all, while the eighty-four thousand marks are manifested for those of great capacity. He then moves quickly through the contemplations of Avalokiteśvara, Mahāsthāmaprāpta, the universal contemplation, and the miscellaneous contemplation, repeating that each is cultivated with "the mind makes" and "the mind is." The longest section treats the last three contemplations, the nine grades of rebirth. Zhili reconciles the Treatise on Rebirth (no women or followers of the two vehicles in that land) with the sūtra, explains why those with heinous offenses can be reborn, and argues that the Commentary's matching of the nine grades with three stages of the distinct teaching lets every good and every evil, dedicated or repented, extend through all nine grades. He also matches the sixteen contemplations, one by one, with grades and stages. The fascicle closes with Vaidehī's awakening, which he places in the first abiding of the perfect teaching, and the transmission at the palace and at Vulture Peak.

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence Jingxi says, "The excellent covers both places; the inferior is only at the Deer Park." If the resemblance stage did not see it at all, why do the four [kinds of] faith of the Lotus see the land of true reward? How could those in [the land] with residue see the perfect ocean of marks? What marks do people of the shared teaching who are received while at their stage see? If it were not the specially honored [body], the combined body would not be established. This sūtra clearly says that Amitāyus Buddha's bodily measure is boundless; how does it differ from the Great Treatise's "form and image are boundless"? Since that "boundless" is called specially honored, how is this alone not? Moreover, the Commentary specifically quotes this text of that treatise to prove the meaning of the bodily measure being boundless; this verifies that the present Buddha's body is definitely the specially honored [body]. There is no need to doubt it.

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: If it is the specially honored [body], it should be the constant body. Why does the Commentary on the Lotus classify the Sūtra on the Contemplation of Amitāyus Buddha as "really measured yet called immeasurable"? Answer: This is the Corrective Notes misquoting that commentary. That commentary says in full, "Really measured yet called immeasurable, as in the Amitābha, together with the Commentary on the Golden Light and this commentary." For the smaller and larger Amitābha sūtras are not exclusively the specially honored [body] reaching sudden capacities; hence that Buddha manifests the thirty-two marks, reaching all capacities in common. Though those of great capacity see the specially honored constant body, the people accustomed to the lesser and the ordinary beings, though they attain birth in that land through dedication, are not yet suited to the specially honored [body] teaching the principle of eternal abiding; hence he teaches the dharma of impermanence through response and transformation, accomplishing their lesser fruit. Therefore, though the Buddha's lifespan cannot be counted, in the end it comes back to the measured. Those born there from the sahā [world] are mostly of this capacity, since the resemblance-stage people of the distinct and perfect are hard to reach. The three commentaries are in terms of this; hence they classify Amitābha among the measured. As for the Sūtra on the Contemplation of Amitāyus Buddha, it reaches purely perfect people and clearly says that the Buddha's body arises from the whole dharma-realm. Since the response has a root, birth is the same as Dharma, exactly of the same kind as the dharma-nature specially honored [body] of the Great Treatise; it is precisely the immeasurable of the immeasurable. Hence the Annotations says, "The teaching distinguishes two bodies; because capacities are inferior, it temporarily manifests the birth-body." Now the capacity is not inferior; why set it against the birth-body?

Question: The larger version says, "Those born in my land will all be complete with the thirty-two marks of the body." Since the people of that land have these marks, the Buddha's body should by principle surpass the people; hence we know that the constant body has eighty-four thousand marks. The Pratyutpanna Sūtra's "thirty-two marks" merely borrows Śākyamuni as the object of contemplation for the beginning mind. Answer: The Pratyutpanna Sūtra says, "Because the bodhisattva uses this buddha-recollection, he will be born in the land of Amitābha Buddha. He should recollect such a buddha-body, complete with the thirty-two marks, its light shining through, upright beyond compare, teaching the sūtras among the saṅgha of bhikṣus." The sūtra points to Amitābha having thirty-two marks; what text says it borrows Śākyamuni as the object? Moreover, Calming and Contemplation has no such text and the Fuxing does not say it; how can you assert it yourself and make your own view? Further, that since the people there have thirty-two marks the Buddha's constant marks must be eighty-four thousand is not so. Because in [a land where sages and ordinary beings] dwell together the Buddha responds in the same way as the people: only because, by the purity of the land, the people all have thirty-two marks, the Buddha's constant body must manifest these marks, but within the sameness each mark is superior. The Buddha's body in a defiled land, though different from the ordinary and base, is also the same as superior people; hence it responds with the dharma of marks found in this region, so the thirty-two are the same as the marks of a wheel-turning king, and within the sameness there are clarity and obscurity. Since the thirty-two marks are the same as those people's, this verifies that they are the constant body and constant marks of that land. Hence we know that the eighty-four thousand are the specially honored marks manifested separately for those of great capacity; what more is there to doubt?

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The specially honored [body] being one and the same, by what causes and conditions are its marks divided into three grades? Answer: For the reasons of the siddhāntas. For there is a kind of capacity that should gain the four benefits through the specially honored marks [as numerous as] the treasury's dust-motes, and so the Buddha manifests them in keeping with the capacity. For those who should gain the four benefits through the specially honored marks of eighty-four thousand, or through the specially honored marks of thirty-two, the Buddha manifests them in keeping with each capacity. Yet one must understand that, of this ocean of marks, the distinct teaching uses particular cultivation of the conditioning and revealing [causes] to accomplish these marks; this is the specially honored [body] accomplished by cultivation, hence it is called the reward-body. The perfect teaching can understand that the two cultivations are identical to the nature, and cultivated virtue is without effort; this is the specially honored [body] inherently contained in the nature, hence it is called the Dharma-body. This was already discussed at the opening of this contemplation. One must know that though the marks [as numerous as] the dust-motes of the Lotus Treasury in the Avataṃsaka are many, because that [sūtra] is combined with the distinct, it still carries [the sense of] accomplishment by cultivation. The eighty-four thousand discussed here, being only perfect and sudden, are nothing but what the nature inherently contains; hence the Commentary on the contemplations of the three sages shows each one as "contemplating the Dharma-body." Practitioners should settle principle by the teaching, make clear the contemplation by principle, and reveal the marks in the contemplation, and must not merely use the many to fault the few, making the excellent into the inferior. Only after precise study does one cultivate diligently and find one cannot stop; hence I make this analysis.

"Carrying the fruit while practicing the cause": the Avalokiteśvara Samādhi Sūtra says, "Avalokiteśvara long ago became a buddha, named Right Dharma Illumination. Now as a bodhisattva he cultivates the practice of the pure land." This is carrying the virtue of the former fruit while practicing the present practice of the cause. The transformation-buddha on his head symbolizes carrying the fruit. Second, listing the sūtra according to the outline, in three: first, concluding the above. Second, directly contemplating the bodhisattva's body, in three: first, directly contemplating the bodily marks. The sub-outline is eleven: first, from "you should next contemplate" on, the bodily measure. It should say eighteen koṭis; that it now says "eighty" is a reversal, exceeding the Buddha's body by twenty koṭis; hence we know it is an error. Question: Śākyamuni is sixteen feet, while a human body is eight feet. Now the Buddha's body is sixty koṭis and the bodhisattva's eighteen; why is the bodhisattva's body so much lower? Answer: The excellent response of a pure land cannot be compared with the inferior response of a defiled land. It is also like Gadgadasvara, whose bodily measure is only forty-two thousand yojanas while [his] buddha's body is six million eight hundred thousand yojanas; the Buddha's bodily measure exceeds the bodhisattva's by even more.

Ru-Yi Translation

When the practitioner contemplates the crown, the protuberance, the tuft, the face, the body's color, and the light, in every case he must use "the mind makes" and "the mind is" as the contemplating. It was explained at the image [contemplation] and is used here: since one says "makes the buddha" and "is the buddha," how could one not make Avalokiteśvara and be Avalokiteśvara? Making the protuberance, making the crown, being the protuberance, being the crown: all can be taken by example. One not only applies the Buddha as an example to contemplating the bodhisattvas, but must also apply it to the universal, the miscellaneous, and the three classes. Not only does the earlier serve as an example for the later; the later should also serve as an example for the earlier. Because practitioners now survey the sūtra from beginning to end before cultivating contemplation, the great master, grasping the meaning, showed the threefold contemplation generally in explaining the title. If it were not used in the sixteen places, the great master would have spoken in vain and practitioners would be seen to cultivate carelessly. One should follow the Buddha's words and not turn against the methods of the patriarchs, using only the wondrous contemplation to reveal the excellent marks; taking this wondrous contemplation as the root of seeing the Buddha, which far surpasses other causes, one quickly realizes the acceptance of dharmas on arriving in that land. Third, from "To contemplate in this way" on, concluding contemplation as right or wrong.

Second, setting out the intent, in two: first, from "The method of contemplation is omitted" on, making clear the lack of a true contemplation in terms of this gate. For contemplating the Buddha's true body, the contemplation is set up as "directly contemplating the Buddha's body" and so on, and in the Avalokiteśvara [contemplation] it says "directly making clear the contemplation of the bodhisattva's body"; but the present Mahāsthāmaprāpta contemplation only says "the two kinds of name set up on account of his light and spiritual power," and says "how he is like and unlike Avalokiteśvara." The Twelfth Contemplation: The Universal Contemplation

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, the twelfth, the contemplation of universal rebirth, in two: first, the Commentary's outline, in two: first, distinguishing it from the miscellaneous; second, from "Within the universal" on, dividing the sections of the universal. Second, the sūtra text, in two: first, making the visualization of oneself going [there]. All the contemplations above went first in order through the dependent and the principal, first the lord and then his followers; though all were accomplished, they were not yet universal and general, nor yet visualizing oneself born there and seeing in person. Hence now it has one visualize oneself, at life's end, born there and seeing all universally at one time; not only are the realms contemplated suddenly complete, but the mind's visualization of rebirth is also accomplished. It can be compared with the earlier contemplations of the dependent reward: first the particular contemplations of the ground, trees, ponds, and so on, up to the accomplishment of the tower contemplation, where the four matters are seen together, called the general contemplation, which however can only make general the four matters of the dependent reward. Now one visualizes being born there and universally seeing and universally hearing all the marks of the dependent and the principal; hence it is called the universal contemplation.

Question: Upper-upper birth rides a vajra platform, upper-middle birth rides a platform of purple gold, upper-lower birth enters a golden lotus flower. Now, the universal contemplation is cultivated only after the contemplations of the three sages are accomplished; it should be those of upper-upper birth. Why is it the same as upper-lower birth? Answer: The matching of the people of the sixteen contemplations with the stages of the nine grades has many approaches in meaning. Now, taking the first approach, when the contemplations of the three sages and the universal contemplation are accomplished, it corresponds to the upper-middle grade; only when the four contemplations of the miscellaneous and the three classes are accomplished is it upper-upper. Hence, though those of upper-middle grade at life's end see themselves seated on a platform of purple gold, this platform on arriving there becomes a great jeweled flower and opens after a night has passed. This text also says, "born in Sukhāvatī, seated in a lotus flower, picture the lotus closing and opening; as the lotus opens, see the buddhas filling the whole sky and proclaiming the wondrous Dharma"; this exactly matches the marks of upper-middle birth. In upper-lower birth, the flower opens and only in seven days does one see the Buddha, and still the mind is not clear about the many marks; hence we know that this text is exactly level with the marks of upper-middle birth. As for matching the grades with the stages of the distinct and perfect, that can be discussed in detail only at the contemplations of the three grades. Second, from "Amitāyus Buddha's countless" on, making clear the three sages coming and appearing. Above, after the end of the visualization, one is born in that land and sees the Buddha and bodhisattvas; now, before the visualization ends, the three sages constantly come and enter my mind's visualization. Truly, because the present thought is identical to the time to come, one can visualize in advance what will happen at birth. Further, because beings and buddha are not different in essence, the three sages come without coming. This is the threefold contemplation in a single mind, "makes" and "is" running together, which brings it about that mind and buddha go there and come here; hence we know that the essence of contemplation cannot be spoken of or thought.

Since the person is the Buddha, the Buddha is surely complete in the four unobstructed wisdoms, namely of dharmas, of meanings, of words, and of eloquence. In teaching the dharma of contemplating the Buddha he is free of error; hence he is called pure. Meeting such a person now, how could one not rejoice? The dharma is the dharma of contemplation; its sixteen gates exhaust its wonders in every detail and can let the ordinary mind enter deep samādhi; being free of empty talk, it is called pure. Hearing such a dharma, how could one not rejoice? The fruit is the fruit gained by cultivating contemplation. Vaidehī and the others, hearing the Dharma and at once cultivating it, ascended to the fruit of the portion of the true; the attendant women and the devas attained the fruit of resemblance; Maudgalyāyana and Ānanda, sharing in the Buddha's teaching activity, may have increased in the path to a depth that cannot be measured; each, being free of delusion, is called pure. Attaining such a fruit, how could one not rejoice? These three depend on one another: attaining the fruit depends on the Dharma, and the Dharma is spoken by the person; the joy of that assembly was complete in these three meanings. Today, though I do not see the golden face directly, I am reached by the wondrous wisdom and further have heard this subtle and wondrous dharma of contemplation, but have not yet attained the fruit; therefore I lack only the third joy.

Second, the transmission at Vulture Peak, in two: first, from "In the transmission at Vulture Peak" on, the Buddha walks through the sky and returns. Earlier, in going in response to the request, he disappeared from Vulture Peak and emerged in the royal palace; now he walks through the open air back to Vulture Peak. Both are spiritual powers; that the earlier is hidden and the later evident is because earlier, when he was about to transform [beings] and the teaching had not yet been accomplished, he went only secretly. Now, having proclaimed the wondrous contemplation and those present as its proper capacity having already benefited, wishing to let all alike follow this Dharma, he displayed the transformation and returned conspicuously. Second, from "Next, Ānanda" on, Ānanda relates it again. The capacities at the royal palace awakened, while [those at] Vulture Peak did not yet know; hence he was sent to proclaim it again, letting all believe and accept it. What Ānanda related is the Buddha's own words; hence the text says, "hearing what the Buddha had said, all rejoiced greatly"; by principle they rejoiced for the same three reasons as before.
