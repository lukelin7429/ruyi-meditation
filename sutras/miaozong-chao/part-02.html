The Buddha in Contemplative Practice through the Ultimate; "Speaks," "Contemplation," and "Immeasurable Life"

Fascicle two continues Zhili's line-by-line sub-commentary on the Explanation of the Name in Zhiyi's Commentary. It first finishes the six identities: the buddha in contemplative practice, where the practitioner, relying on Amitābha's golden form, finds the wondrous form to be mind and sees the Buddha with eyes open or closed; the buddha in resemblance, with the four similes and the purity of the six faculties; the buddha in a portion of the truth, where Zhili uses the three dots of the letter i to argue that only a teaching in which original awakening inherently contains defilement and evil makes the three virtues neither vertical nor horizontal; and the ultimate buddha, with the forty-two letters and the fifteen-day moon. Along the way he reads "identity" (即) as "the essence being not two" rather than the union of two things. He then explains the title's words "speaks" (the four gates and four siddhāntas), "contemplation" (the sequential threefold contemplation and the threefold contemplation in a single mind, defended against an objection about ignorance), and "Immeasurable Life" (the three bodies, each with a body, life, and measure, finally interfused in principle). Lemmas from the Commentary follow the site's English of that work.

Identity in Contemplative Practice: The Wondrous Form

Chinese Source

◎三、觀行即。此中觀法，異常坐等直觀心性，故託他佛而為所緣，然是大乘知心作佛、佛即是心，其觀未成為塵所動。始自圓聞觀佛妙境，至識次位勤行五悔。若未發品，此等行人皆屬名字，故知名字其位甚長。今明對塵即成佛觀，其中念念覺知之心，名觀行佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, identity in contemplative practice. The method of contemplation here differs from sitting constantly and the like, which contemplate the mind-nature directly; therefore it relies on another buddha as its object. Yet it is the Mahāyāna [contemplation] that knows the mind makes up the Buddha and the Buddha is this mind; [only,] while the contemplation is not yet accomplished, it is moved by sense-objects. From first hearing perfectly of the wondrous object of contemplating the Buddha up to recognizing the sequence of stages and diligently practicing the five repentances — if they have not yet aroused [the first of the five] grades, such practitioners all belong to [identity in] name; hence one knows that the stage of name is very long. Now it is made clear that, facing sense-objects, one accomplishes the contemplation of the Buddha; the mind that is aware and knowing in thought after thought within it is called the buddha in contemplative practice.

Chinese Source

此自分二：初、觀妙色即真法。以大小相皆悉周遍，故此色身即是真法。又二；初、約一佛，二：初、示始習即心觀相。此是名字昇進之位，不獨解名，能修觀故；但未入品，非觀行位觀佛相者。若此經中八萬相好，非羸劣想而可繫緣，故須初心先觀落日，漸觀地樹及以座像，觀既深著方觀勝身。

Ru-Yi Translation

This in turn is in two: first, contemplating the wondrous form as the true Dharma. Since the major and minor marks are all pervasive, this form-body is the true Dharma. Again in two: first, in terms of one buddha, in two: first, showing the marks of the contemplation of mind right in this mind at the beginning of practice. This is the stage of advancing within [identity in] name: one does not merely understand the name, because one is able to cultivate contemplation; but one has not yet entered the grades, so it is not [the same as] contemplating the Buddha's marks at the stage of contemplative practice. As for the eighty-four thousand marks and features in this sūtra, they are not something that a feeble and weak visualization can fix upon as an object; hence the beginning mind must first contemplate the setting sun, gradually contemplate the ground, the trees, the seat, and the image, and only when the contemplation has become deep and firm does it contemplate the superior body.

Chinese Source

今依《般舟》，初心先觀千輻輪相，次第逆想至肉髻三十二種，是下品相。復從足起，可作始行繫心之境，不須更以落日為緣。若相若好皆依於身，身唯金色，故云「如鑄金像」。此之色相雖從心想如在目前，故云「與眼作對」。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, following the Pratyutpanna, the beginning mind first contemplates the mark of the thousand-spoked wheel, then visualizes in reverse order up to the fleshy topknot, the thirty-two kinds, which are the marks [taken in order] from below. Then, starting again from the feet, this can serve as the object on which the beginning practitioner fixes the mind, and there is no need to take the setting sun as an object as well. Whether marks or features, all rest on the body, and the body is only golden in color; hence it says "as if [beholding] an image cast in gold." Though this form and these marks come from the mind's visualization, they are as if before one's eyes; hence it says "set face to face with the eye."

Chinese Source

言「妙色」者，即是不可思議色也。所以者何？由此行人已圓聞故，知色唯心、知心唯色。五根所對尚體唯心，況想成色豈在心外？此色非色、非色非非色，而能雙照色與非色。既離情想，故名妙色。非由三觀莫見妙色、非由妙色莫成三觀，境觀相資塵念靡間，方能得入觀行位也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The wondrous form" is inconceivable form. Why? Because this practitioner has already heard perfectly, he knows that form is only mind and knows that mind is only form. Even what the five faculties face is in essence only mind; how much less could a form accomplished by visualization lie outside the mind? This form is not form, and is neither not-form nor not not-form, yet it can illuminate both form and not-form. Since it is apart from feeling and visualization, it is called the wondrous form. Without the threefold contemplation one cannot see the wondrous form; without the wondrous form one cannot accomplish the threefold contemplation. Only when object and contemplation aid each other, and sense-objects and thought leave no gap, can one enter the stage of contemplative practice.

Chinese Source

二、「開眼」下，明觀成稱性周遍。妙心作相、妙相發心，心心不休成觀入品，塵緣莫動佛常現前，閉目了然、開眼不失，在明見佛、處暗不忘，性無間然，佛豈暫闕？一一相海莊嚴法身，相為大相、好為小相，觀大發小名為流出。劫水雖大，止劑二禪，佛相遍周稱於法界，且以分喻顯於周遍。觀行佛眼名曰周眸，此眼所觀何處非佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "with eyes open" on, making clear that when the contemplation is accomplished it accords with the nature and pervades everywhere. The wondrous mind makes the marks, and the wondrous marks arouse the mind; mind after mind without ceasing, the contemplation is accomplished and one enters the grades. Sense-conditions do not move one, and the Buddha is constantly present: with eyes closed [he is seen] clearly, with eyes open he is not lost; in brightness one sees the Buddha, in darkness one does not forget him. The nature has no gaps; how could the Buddha be absent even for a moment? Each ocean of marks adorns the Dharma-body: the marks are the major marks, the features are the minor marks; contemplating the major gives rise to the minor, which is called "flowing forth." Though the waters of an eon are great, they reach only up to the second dhyāna; the Buddha's marks pervade everywhere, in accord with the dharma-realm; [the commentary] uses a partial simile to show their pervasiveness. The eye of the buddha in contemplative practice is called "the eye turning all around"; wherever this eye contemplates, what place is not the Buddha?

Chinese Source

問：《金光明玄義》觀於三道顯金光明，似位尚云閉目則見、開目則失。今觀三身位在觀行，因何開閉俱得見邪？答：彼明性德金光明理，此理初住方任運見，故於似位猶論得失。今帶事定，託彼應色觀於三身，以其應相凡心可見，故理三身雖乃未顯，不妨應色先與定合，故令開閉皆見佛身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The Profound Meaning of the Golden Light contemplates the three paths to reveal the golden light, and at the stage of resemblance it still says, "With eyes closed one sees; with eyes open one loses it." Now the contemplation of the three bodies is at the stage of contemplative practice; why can one see with eyes both open and closed? Answer: That [text] makes clear the principle of the golden light of inherent virtue; this principle is seen effortlessly only at the first abiding, hence at the stage of resemblance it still discusses gaining and losing. Now [this contemplation] involves a concentration on phenomena: relying on that responsive form, one contemplates the three bodies. Since the responsive marks can be seen by the ordinary mind, though the three bodies of principle are not yet revealed, this does not prevent the responsive form from first uniting with concentration; hence one sees the Buddha's body with eyes both open and closed.

Chinese Source

如以三觀觀彼落日，三觀未成而能開閉皆見於日。故雖事理一念同修，而理難事易，事易故先現、理難故後發。故般舟三昧以三十二相為事境，以即空假中為理觀。境觀雖乃同時而修，境必先成；託境進觀、藉觀顯境，更進更顯，從凡入聖。故知彼就趣真無住，對似愛頂墮為開閉得失。此就應色，得成觀行。為開閉俱見，不知事理難易淺深，此相違文何能銷釋？

Ru-Yi Translation

It is as when one contemplates the setting sun with the threefold contemplation: though the threefold contemplation is not yet accomplished, one can see the sun with eyes both open and closed. Thus, although phenomena and principle are cultivated together in a single thought, principle is hard and phenomena are easy; because phenomena are easy they appear first, because principle is hard it arises later. Hence the pratyutpanna samādhi takes the thirty-two marks as the phenomenal object and takes identity with the empty, the provisional, and the middle as the contemplation of principle. Though object and contemplation are cultivated at the same time, the object is always accomplished first; relying on the object one advances the contemplation, and by means of the contemplation one reveals the object; advancing further and revealing further, one passes from the ordinary into the sage. Hence one knows that that [text], dealing with the heading toward the true without abiding, regards gaining and losing with eyes open and closed [in terms of] attachment in resemblance and falling from the summit. This [text], dealing with the responsive form, takes being able to see with eyes both open and closed as accomplishing contemplative practice. If one does not know the difficulty and ease, depth and shallowness of phenomena and principle, how could one explain these mutually contradictory texts?

Equal to All Buddhas; Mindful of the Three Bodies

Chinese Source

二、「念一」下，等諸佛，二：初、約一佛等諸佛。為成觀故不可散緣，故以彌陀一佛為境。雖觀一佛，何異十方；雖照現今，何殊過未？此彰一切不離彌陀，良以彌陀是無量之一，故能等於一中無量。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "One is mindful of one buddha" on, being equal to all buddhas, in two: first, in terms of one buddha being equal to all buddhas. In order to accomplish the contemplation one cannot take scattered objects; hence the one buddha Amitābha is taken as the object. Though one contemplates one buddha, how is this different from [those of] the ten directions? Though one illuminates the present, how is this different from past and future? This makes clear that all are not apart from Amitābha: truly because Amitābha is the one of the immeasurable, he can be equal to the immeasurable within the one.

Chinese Source

二、「一身」下，明諸佛同三法。「一佛等彼一切佛」者，以由佛佛同得三法：身是法身、智慧是般若、十力四無所畏是解脫，亦是三身三涅槃等。身智言一者，顯於諸佛法報不別，應用亦同，故力無畏結云亦然，同身智一也。菩薩因中分破無明，分同妙覺所證三法，無明破盡，則究竟同諸佛三法。諸佛三法既其不二，是故彌陀三法不少、一切諸佛三法不多，故言等也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "one body" on, making clear that all buddhas share the three dharmas. "One buddha is equal to all those buddhas": this is because buddha after buddha equally attains the three dharmas. The body is the Dharma-body, wisdom is prajñā, and the ten powers and four fearlessnesses are liberation; these are also the three bodies, the three nirvāṇas, and so on. Saying "one" of the body and wisdom shows that the Dharma[-body] and reward[-body] of all buddhas are not different, and the responsive function is also the same; hence of the powers and fearlessnesses it concludes "so too," the same as the oneness of body and wisdom. Bodhisattvas at the causal [stage] break ignorance in portions and share in portions the three dharmas realized at wondrous awakening; when ignorance is broken entirely, they ultimately share the three dharmas of all buddhas. Since the three dharmas of all buddhas are not two, the three dharmas of Amitābha are not fewer and the three dharmas of all buddhas are not more; hence it says "equal."

Chinese Source

二、「念色」下，念三身以結示。色是應身，通於勝劣及他受用。法門是報身，以諸法門聚而為身，即八萬四千陀羅尼為髮、第一義諦為髻、種智為頭、慈悲為眼、無漏為鼻、四辯為口、四十不共為齒、二智為手、如來藏為腹、三三昧為腰、定慧為足等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "mindful of the form-body" on, being mindful of the three bodies, to conclude and show. Form is the response-body, covering the superior and inferior [response-bodies] and the other-enjoyment [body]. The Dharma-gates are the reward-body, since the Dharma-gates gathered together make up the body: the eighty-four thousand dhāraṇīs are the hair, the truth of the first principle is the topknot, the wisdom of [all] modes is the head, loving-kindness and compassion are the eyes, the undefiled is the nose, the four kinds of eloquence are the mouth, the forty exclusive [qualities] are the teeth, the two wisdoms are the hands, the tathāgatagarbha is the belly, the three samādhis are the waist, concentration and wisdom are the feet, and so on.

Chinese Source

此諸法門，若從所證名為法身，今從能證名為報身，自受用也。實相是法身，非不具於一切法門及諸色相，讓於能證及垂應故。今是所證及以能垂，但名實相；前論觀法，文中但言相好周遍，次文乃約三法論等。至今結示云念三身，應知法門及以實相不離色身，舉一即三、全三是一，法爾相即，非縱非橫。是故此經第九佛觀，經示相好，疏名真法。不知圓觀，此名莫消？若觀佛身，不涉後二，便同小外，何預妙宗？須知此文，是結前觀色相周遍，已具三身。

Ru-Yi Translation

These Dharma-gates, if named from what is realized, are the Dharma-body; now, named from what realizes, they are the reward-body, that is, the self-enjoyment [body]. The true mark is the Dharma-body; it is not that it does not contain all the Dharma-gates and all the forms and marks, [but it gives place to them] because it yields [the names] to what realizes and to what is manifested in response. Now, being what is realized and what is able to manifest, it is called only "the true mark." Earlier, in discussing the method of contemplation, the text spoke only of the marks and features pervading everywhere; the next passage then discussed equality in terms of the three dharmas; now, in concluding and showing, it says to be mindful of the three bodies. One should know that the Dharma-gates and the true mark are not apart from the form-body: raise one and it is three, the whole three are one; they are by their very nature identical to one another, neither vertical nor horizontal. Therefore, in the ninth, the contemplation of the Buddha, of this sūtra, the sūtra shows the marks and features while the commentary calls it the true Dharma. Without knowing the perfect contemplation, how could this name be explained? If, in contemplating the Buddha's body, one does not touch on the latter two, it is the same as the lesser [vehicle] and the outsiders; what has it to do with the wondrous purport? One must know that this passage concludes the preceding contemplation of the forms and marks pervading everywhere, which already contains the three bodies.

Identity and the Meaning of "Identity"

Chinese Source

二、「常運」下，明即佛。觀行位人，一切時處，念佛三觀常得現前，故云「無不念時」。言「念念皆覺」者，示即佛義。雖是始覺，即同本覺，非全本覺，觀不名中。亦得義論始本一合，雖非究竟及真似合，而亦得是觀行合也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "constantly keeping mindfulness at work" on, making clear identity with buddha. A person at the stage of contemplative practice, at all times and in all places, constantly has the threefold contemplation of buddha-recollection present; hence it says "there is no time without mindfulness." Saying "in thought after thought there is awakening" shows the meaning of identity with buddha. Though it is initial awakening, it is the same as original awakening; [if it] were not wholly original awakening, the contemplation would not be called the middle. One may also speak in terms of meaning of initial and original being united as one: though it is not the ultimate, nor the uniting of the true or of resemblance, it can still be the uniting of contemplative practice.

Chinese Source

若論即字，《廣雅》訓合，荊谿云「依訓乃成二物相合」，於理猶疏。今以義求，其體不二方名為即。然其始覺與本覺合，雖名為合，非二物合，正是荊谿體不二義。良以始本覺體是一，故知六即得名六合。理即乃以逆修之覺與本覺合，五皆順覺與本覺合，六合無非體不二也。荊谿有時亦以合名明不二體，故不二門云「復由緣了與性一合，方能稱性施設萬端」。緣了是始，性豈非本？修性體一，復名為合。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the word "identity" (即), the Guangya glosses it as "uniting" (合). Jingxi says, "Following the gloss, it becomes the uniting of two things," which is still remote from principle. Now, seeking it by meaning, only when their essence is not two is it called identity. Yet when initial awakening unites with original awakening, though it is called uniting, it is not the uniting of two things; this is precisely Jingxi's meaning of the essence being not two. Truly because the essence of initial and original awakening is one, one knows that the six identities may be called the six unitings. Identity in principle is the awakening of contrary cultivation uniting with original awakening; the [other] five are all compliant awakening uniting with original awakening; of the six unitings, none is other than the essence being not two. Jingxi at times also uses the name "uniting" to make clear the nondual essence; thus the Ten Gates of Nonduality says, "Only when the conditioning and revealing [causes] are united as one with the nature can one, in accord with the nature, set up the myriad means." The conditioning and revealing are the initial; how is the nature not the original? Cultivation and nature being one in essence is again called uniting.

Identity in Resemblance

Chinese Source

四、相似即。今既釋佛，乃似本覺，良以此位始覺之功尚伏無明，全未破故，非真本覺，唯得名為相似即佛。若四十一位分破無明，故得分分是真本覺，名分真佛。至極果位，無明既盡，本覺全彰，故得名為究竟是佛。即究竟本覺，亦究竟始覺，亦是究竟始本一合，亦是究竟始本俱忘。例前五即，皆有四義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, identity in resemblance. Now, since it explains the Buddha, it resembles original awakening: truly because at this stage the work of initial awakening still only subdues ignorance and has not yet broken it at all, it is not the true original awakening and can only be called the buddha identical in resemblance. At the forty-one stages, ignorance is broken in portions, so one can be portion by portion the true original awakening, called the buddha true in portions. On reaching the stage of the ultimate fruit, ignorance being exhausted and original awakening wholly manifest, one can be called the buddha in the ultimate. This is ultimate original awakening, also ultimate initial awakening, also the ultimate uniting of initial and original as one, and also the ultimate forgetting of both initial and original. By analogy, the preceding five identities all have these four meanings.

Chinese Source

問：名字等五，以始對本，論合及忘，四義稍可。唯初理即，既全在迷，豈有始覺及二義邪？答：理雖全迷，而具三因及五佛性，緣了二性豈非本有？修因始覺，果及果果二種佛性，豈非理中究竟始覺？理若不具，此等始覺，名字等五便須別修，復何得云全修在性？但有即名無即義也，自非山教圓位徒施。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: For the five [identities] from name on, setting the initial against the original and discussing uniting and forgetting, the four meanings are somewhat acceptable. But identity in principle, the first, being wholly in delusion — how could it have initial awakening and the two meanings? Answer: Though principle is wholly deluded, it contains the three causes and the five buddha-natures; how are the two natures of the conditioning and the revealing not originally present? The initial awakening of the cultivated causes, and the two buddha-natures of the fruit and the fruit of the fruit — are they not the ultimate initial awakening within principle? If principle did not contain them, then these initial awakenings, [the identities] from name on, would have to be cultivated separately; how then could one say that the whole of cultivation lies in the nature? There would be only the name of identity without the meaning of identity. Unless it is the teaching of the Mountain, the perfect stages are set up in vain.

Chinese Source

今當相似位中四義，文自分二：初、標釋，二：初、約三身明即佛。前觀行位，常用三觀念佛三身，觀覺雖成，似覺未發，加功不已。今本覺三身相似而發，與始覺三觀相似相應，應是合義，合而不忘，非妙覺也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, as for the four meanings within the stage of resemblance, the text is divided in two: first, marking and explaining, in two: first, making clear identity with buddha in terms of the three bodies. At the preceding stage of contemplative practice, one constantly used the threefold contemplation to be mindful of the Buddha's three bodies; though contemplative awakening was accomplished, the resembling awakening had not yet arisen, and one added effort without ceasing. Now the three bodies of original awakening arise in resemblance and accord in resemblance with the threefold contemplation of initial awakening; "accord" is the meaning of uniting. Uniting without forgetting — it is not wondrous awakening.

Chinese Source

問：於一本覺，約何要義，顯示三身令人可見？答：本覺諸法即空假中，覺諸法假即相好身，覺諸法空即法門身，覺諸法中即實相身。如此論之，其義宛爾。更於一覺約寂照說，照而常寂，自在神通，即相好身；寂而常照，清淨智慧，即法門身；非寂非照，而寂而照，即實相身。此之二三，皆非縱橫，不可思議。乃是寂覺照覺，雙遮照覺，全本成始，即是相應及俱忘義。此位三身，即佛義顯，是故文中不特言覺示於即佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: In one original awakening, by what essential meaning are the three bodies shown so that people can see them? Answer: In original awakening all dharmas are identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle. Awakening to all dharmas as provisional is the body of marks and features; awakening to all dharmas as empty is the body of Dharma-gates; awakening to all dharmas as the middle is the body of the true mark. Discussed in this way, the meaning is clear. Further, speaking of the one awakening in terms of tranquility and illumination: illuminating yet ever tranquil, with sovereign spiritual powers — this is the body of marks and features; tranquil yet ever illuminating, with pure wisdom — this is the body of Dharma-gates; neither tranquil nor illuminating, yet tranquil and illuminating — this is the body of the true mark. These two [sets of] three are neither vertical nor horizontal, inconceivable. They are tranquil awakening and illuminating awakening, negating both and illuminating awakening, the whole of the original forming the initial — this is the meaning of according and of both being forgotten. At this stage the meaning of the three bodies being identical to buddha is evident; therefore the text does not specially speak of awakening to show identity with buddha.
