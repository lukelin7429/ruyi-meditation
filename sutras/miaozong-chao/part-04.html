Pure Karma and the Contemplations from the Sun to the Image

Fascicle four finishes the preface of occasion (the Buddha's coming to Vaidehī's palace, compared with the Śrīmālā, and her plea) and follows the main teaching of the sūtra from the light between the Buddha's brows through the eighth contemplation, the image of the Buddha and the two bodhisattvas. Zhili reads the three blessings as the auxiliary path of the perfect teaching, and he reads the sūtra's promise of the acceptance of non-arising as the first abiding of the perfect teaching. He argues at length, against the view that only the image contemplation is "contemplation of principle," that all sixteen contemplations are the threefold contemplation "following practice" in the sense of Zhanran's Meaning and Examples: they go through phenomena while contemplating principle, as the four samādhis do. The contemplation of the sun is thus "the sun of the dharma-realm" seen by "the mind of the dharma-realm," though for most beginners it stands only at identity in name. The fascicle closes with Zhili's key reading of "This mind makes the buddha; this mind is the buddha," which he takes as the inconceivable threefold contemplation and "the wondrous purport of the whole sūtra," and with his defense of using the threefold contemplation from the first contemplation onward.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Not far from here" on, raising the fruit to encourage cultivating the cause, in two: first, the question. The larger version and the smaller version both say, "Sukhāvatī is a hundred thousand million fields from here." A field is a great trichiliocosm; hence it says "Ganges-sands [of worlds]; why say 'not far'?" Second, from "Explanation" on, the answer, which has two meanings. The first: through the Buddha's power, those who cultivate contemplation see as soon as they wish to see. Hence this text says, "You should fix your thoughts, and contemplate carefully that land"; so we know that the Buddha's power is added to those who wish to see, making their contemplation become seeing. A later text says, "[all beings] contemplate the western land of Sukhāvatī. Through the Buddha's power, one will come to see that pure land." Hence the Pratyutpanna, on seeing the Buddha, discusses three powers: first, the Buddha's majestic power; second, the power of samādhi; third, the power of the practitioner's original merit. The second meaning is the land appearing in the light, that is, seen with one's own eyes. Both kinds of seeing come about through eliciting and response; though far, [the land] is near. But if the nature of mind did not inherently contain lands as numerous as dust motes, the Buddha would have no principle by which to respond and appear, and beings would have no capacity to elicit and see; hence this sūtra speaks of "this mind is the buddha." A contemplation deluded about this intent is not the wondrous purport.

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "First" on, directly showing the cause of rebirth, in two: first, the direct showing, in three: first, the karma shared with ordinary beings. This sūtra is properly for those with the capacity for sudden cultivation. Though they cultivate the practice of the buddhas, how could they not care for and serve their fathers, mothers, teachers, and elders, and how could they not keep the practice of restraint in the ten precepts of the wheel-turning king? Only, the mind that cultivates accords with the nature at every point; what harm is there in the good of loving-kindness and filial care that it cultivates being shared with ordinary beings? Second, from "Second" on, the karma shared with the two vehicles. How could the practitioner of the perfect and sudden go against the Lesser Vehicle's rules for leaving the household? The Three Refuges, the many precepts, proper conduct, and the like: only, the mind that receives and upholds them unites with the one essence and relies on the ultimate, while the dharmas practiced are shared with the two vehicles. Third, from "Third" on, the unshared karma of the Great Vehicle. Raising the vow without condition on the basis of the unconditioned object is called giving rise to the mind of awakening. The true mark, not two yet two, establishes the difference of cause and fruit; two yet not two, beginning and end are one in principle. Only believing in this cause and fruit is called "deeply." Reading and reciting the Mahāyāna scriptures, cultivating the understanding of the three wisdoms, and setting in motion the practice of the perfect vehicle, and with this understanding and practice teaching others who practice, is called "encouraging." Of these three kinds of karma, one who obtains the earlier ones does not obtain the later ones, while one who obtains the later ones necessarily obtains the earlier ones. Hence practitioners now can cultivate the first two, but the first two cannot cultivate the Great Vehicle; hence it says "the other two do not reach it; this is called the Great Vehicle."

"Listen closely" and so on: in all the sūtras the admonition to listen has these words, all to make people give rise to the three wisdoms. But one must, according to the teaching, make clear whether the wisdom is partial or perfect: that which listens and what is listened to, that which reflects and what is reflected on, that which keeps in mind and what is kept in mind. If one understands them as generation-and-extinction, these are the three wisdoms of the Tripiṭaka; if one understands them as no generation, the three wisdoms of the shared teaching; the immeasurable and the unconditioned, [for] the distinct and the perfect, can be known. Now he makes Vaidehī and the others give rise to the three wisdoms of the perfect [teaching]; if it were not so, how could they realize the acceptance of dharmas right in this seat?

The Acceptance of Non-Arising and Vaidehī's Seeing

三、「即得」下，略付阿難。經「如執明鏡」等者，觀法如鏡、修之如執，觀成土現，如見面像。是知外有三種淨業、內備十六妙觀，乃得見也。此雖略付淨業，意說妙觀。「初住初地」者，圓住別地俱破無明，是無生忍位。《妙玄》一實位云「若入初住，正破無明，是明圓教無生忍位」，今意在圓。引仁王五種忍位者，用顯無生居三忍上。若依別教，十信伏忍、十住信忍、十行去順忍、十地無生忍、妙覺寂滅忍。若約圓位五品伏忍，六根清淨信順二忍，初住至等覺名無生忍，妙覺名寂滅忍。然別初地即圓初住，故引《仁王》以證今位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "At once attain" on, briefly entrusting it to Ānanda. The sūtra's "as if holding a bright mirror" and so on: the contemplative method is like the mirror, and cultivating it is like holding it; when the contemplation is accomplished and the land appears, it is like seeing the image of one's face. Thus we know that only with the three kinds of pure karma outwardly and the sixteen wondrous contemplations complete inwardly does one see. Though this briefly entrusts pure karma, the intent speaks of the wondrous contemplations. "The first abiding or the first ground": the abiding of the perfect [teaching] and the ground of the distinct both break ignorance; this is the stage of the acceptance of non-arising. The Wondrous Profound [Meaning], on the stages of the one reality, says, "If one enters the first abiding, one directly breaks ignorance; this makes clear the stage of the acceptance of non-arising of the perfect teaching." The present intent lies in the perfect. [The Commentary] cites the five stages of acceptance of the Humane Kings to display that non-arising stands above three acceptances. According to the distinct teaching, the ten faiths are the acceptance of subduing, the ten abidings the acceptance of faith, the ten practices onward the acceptance of compliance, the ten grounds the acceptance of non-arising, and wondrous awakening the acceptance of tranquil extinction. In terms of the stages of the perfect, the five grades are the acceptance of subduing; the purity of the six faculties is the two acceptances of faith and compliance; from the first abiding to equal awakening is called the acceptance of non-arising; and wondrous awakening is called the acceptance of tranquil extinction.
