Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching; the Preface of the Sūtra

Fascicle three completes Zhili's sub-commentary on the five profound meanings of Zhiyi's Commentary and begins the line-by-line reading of the sūtra. Under the Essence, Zhili argues that the true mark, free of the marks of words, names, and the mind's objects, is the one seal of the perfect teaching, and that it is "not apart from the present discriminating thought." He reads the Commentary's three glosses of 體 (rites, bottom, penetrating) as the Dharma-body, prajñā, and liberation within the essence. Under the Cardinal Doctrine he rejects both the identity and the separation of cardinal doctrine and essence, and insists that the perfect threefold contemplation in a single mind is the cause of all four pure lands, including the land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together that Vaidehī sought; the four lands are explained with the image of "horizontal" and "vertical" seeing in one place. The Function chapter ties the sūtra's power over the five heinous transgressions to inherent evil; the Classification places the sūtra in the vaipulya period and calls it sudden by content. The fascicle then covers the six phrases of the common preface, the assembly, and the opening of the preface of occasion, through Vaidehī's request for Maudgalyāyana and Ānanda.

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Zhili, a śramaṇa of Siming, of the Song

Clarifying the Essence: The Essence and the Name

Chinese Source

◎二、辯體。前文解釋能說所說、能觀所觀皆能詮名，今辯此名所詮之體，欲令學者因筌得魚、尋名顯體。尋名意在忘名、顯體知無別體，此乃今師釋名辯體之妙意也。復應了知，釋名是總，總三法故；三章是別，別三法故。是故解釋通別二名，無不義具教行理三。能說之佛既具三身，所說觀境各具於三，故云釋名總於三法，體章別在法身，宗用別當餘二，教相一章分別總別。今之辯體雖在一法，一必具三，故明體禮、體底、體達三種之義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, Clarifying the Essence. The preceding text explained the one who speaks and what is spoken, what contemplates and what is contemplated, all of which are names that express; now we clarify the essence that these names express, wishing to let students obtain the fish by means of the trap and, following the name, reveal the essence. The intent of following the name lies in forgetting the name; in revealing the essence one knows there is no separate essence. This is the wondrous intent of our master in explaining the name and clarifying the essence. One should further understand that explaining the name is general, because it covers the three dharmas generally; the three [following] chapters are particular, because they treat the three dharmas particularly. Therefore, in explaining the two names, common and particular, there is nothing whose meaning does not contain the three: teaching, practice, and principle. Since the Buddha who speaks contains the three bodies, and the objects of contemplation that are spoken each contain the three, we say that explaining the name covers the three dharmas generally, the chapter on the essence is particularly on the Dharma-body, the cardinal doctrine and the function particularly correspond to the other two, and the one chapter on the characteristics of the teaching distinguishes the general and the particular. Though the present clarifying of the essence lies in one dharma, the one necessarily contains three; hence it makes clear three meanings: essence as rites, essence as bottom, and essence as penetrating.

Chinese Source

雖論三義但是法身中三，未明餘二各三，故《涅槃玄》云「總唱祕藏以當其名。法身攝一切法不縱不橫以當其體，般若攝一切法如面三目以當其宗，解脫攝一切法如三點伊以當其用。如此敷演即是其教，非但經體義成，餘義亦顯。（文畢）」

Ru-Yi Translation

Though three meanings are discussed, they are only the three within the Dharma-body; it has not made clear the three of each of the other two. Hence the Profound Meaning of the Nirvāṇa says, "Proclaiming the secret treasury as a whole corresponds to its name. The Dharma-body gathering all dharmas, neither vertical nor horizontal, corresponds to its essence; prajñā gathering all dharmas, like the three eyes on a face, corresponds to its cardinal doctrine; liberation gathering all dharmas, like the three dots of [the letter] i, corresponds to its function. Expounding it in this way is its teaching. Not only is the meaning of the sūtra's essence accomplished; the other meanings are revealed as well." (End of quotation.)

Chinese Source

今出其意，空假皆中故三屬體，假中皆空故三屬宗，中空皆假故三屬用。用是解脫，特喻三點，點是文字故；宗當般若，特喻三目，目能照明故；法身之三，特泯縱橫，彰離念故。故知釋名總於九法，辯體別在法身中三。然九不多、三不為少，方是圓教總別之義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now to bring out its intent: because the empty and the provisional are both the middle, the three belong to the essence; because the provisional and the middle are both empty, the three belong to the cardinal doctrine; because the middle and the empty are both provisional, the three belong to the function. The function is liberation, compared specially to the three dots, because dots are letters; the cardinal doctrine corresponds to prajñā, compared specially to the three eyes, because eyes can illuminate; the three of the Dharma-body specially dissolve the vertical and the horizontal, to display freedom from thought. Hence we know that explaining the name covers nine dharmas generally, and clarifying the essence is particularly the three within the Dharma-body. Yet the nine are not many and the three are not few; only this is the meaning of general and particular in the perfect teaching.

The Seal of the True Mark

Chinese Source

此自分二：初、牒起略示。名傍是賓、體正是主，名是假名、體是實質，一切名下皆有其質。二、「釋論」下，正釋主質，四：初、據二文定體。諸法當處不生不滅、非有非空、無能無所，離言說相、離名字相、離心緣相。離此等相，名為實相，無相之相也。誰人不具？何法不然？若論證知，唯有諸佛。故《法華》云「唯佛與佛乃能究盡諸法實相」。

Ru-Yi Translation

This in turn is in two: first, citing [the topic] and briefly showing it. The name is secondary, the guest; the essence is primary, the host. The name is a provisional name; the essence is the real substance. Beneath every name there is its substance. Second, from "The Great Treatise" on, directly explaining the principal substance, in four: first, settling the essence on the basis of two texts. All dharmas, right where they are, neither arise nor cease, are neither existent nor empty, and are without subject or object; they are free of the mark of words, free of the mark of names, free of the mark of the mind's objects. Being free of such marks is called the true mark: the mark of no mark. What person does not contain it? What dharma is not so? But if we speak of knowing it by realization, there are only the buddhas. Hence the Lotus says, "Only a buddha, together with another buddha, can fathom the true mark of all dharmas to its very limit."

Chinese Source

稟圓說者，初心即用佛智照境，故能信解諸法實相。既解實相，亦解諸法實性、實體、實力、實作、實因、實緣、實果、實報、實本末究竟等。十法既實，即是實生實佛、實依實正，一色一香無非中道、一切諸法皆是佛法。既一切皆實，實外無餘，復何得云餘皆魔事？

Ru-Yi Translation

Those who receive the perfect teaching use the Buddha's wisdom to illuminate the object from the very first mind, and so can believe and understand the true mark of all dharmas. Having understood the true mark, they also understand the real nature, real essence, real power, real activity, real cause, real condition, real effect, real retribution, and real ultimate equality from beginning to end of all dharmas. Since the ten dharmas are real, there are real beings and real buddhas, the real dependent and the real principal; a single form or a single fragrance is nothing but the middle way, and all dharmas are the Buddha-dharma. Since everything is real and there is nothing outside the real, how can it then be said that everything else is the work of Māra?

Chinese Source

應知此說以理簡情。若離心緣能所等相名為實相，介爾有相即為魔事，故別教已下至六道法，皆有能所心緣等相，魔能說之悉名魔事。故知一切皆魔、一切皆佛，以情分別一切皆邪，離情分別一切皆正。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that this statement uses principle to sift out feeling. If being free of the marks of the mind's objects, of subject and object, and so on is called the true mark, then the slightest mark is the work of Māra. Hence the dharmas from the distinct teaching down to the six paths all have marks of subject and object, of the mind's objects, and so on; Māra can speak them, and all are called the work of Māra. Hence we know that everything is Māra and everything is buddha: discriminated with feeling, everything is deviant; discriminated apart from feeling, everything is correct.

Chinese Source

今簡情取理而為經體，應知實相全體照明，稱為真心亦名本覺，覺體遍故諸法皆實。若指其要，不離現前分別之念，念即本覺，覺即經體，無別經體以為所詮。以此覺心觀於依正，能所即絕、待對斯忘，妙觀之宗自茲而立。若不爾者，何須得體方立經宗？

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, sifting out feeling and taking principle as the essence of the sūtra, one should know that the whole essence of the true mark illuminates and is bright; it is called the true mind and also original awakening. Because the essence of awakening pervades, all dharmas are real. To point to its crux: it is not apart from the present discriminating thought. Thought is original awakening, and awakening is the essence of the sūtra; there is no separate essence of the sūtra to be what is expressed. When one contemplates the dependent and the principal with this awakened mind, subject and object are at once cut off and relativity and opposition are forgotten; the cardinal doctrine of the wondrous contemplation is established from this. If it were not so, why would one need to obtain the essence before establishing the cardinal doctrine of the sūtra?

Chinese Source

實相印者，印即符印，亦信也，亦印定義。乃以所詮定其大小及以邪正，理符佛旨方可信從。小乘三印，無常、無我此之二印印於生死，寂滅一印印於涅槃。小乘涅槃與生死異，故各印之。所詮符此，則可信受是小乘經非魔外說。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "the true mark as seal": a seal is a tally-seal; it also means trust, and it also means to seal and settle. That is, by what is expressed one settles whether [a sūtra] is great or small, deviant or correct; only when its principle tallies with the Buddha's purport can it be believed and followed. The Lesser Vehicle has three seals: impermanence and no-self, these two seals, seal birth-and-death; the one seal of tranquil extinction seals nirvāṇa. In the Lesser Vehicle nirvāṇa differs from birth-and-death, so each is sealed separately. If what is expressed tallies with these, one can believe and accept that it is a sūtra of the Lesser Vehicle and not the teaching of Māra or of non-Buddhists.

Chinese Source

大乘一印即一實相，二種生死、三德涅槃其體是一，究竟真實。義符於此，可以信受是大乘經，非小非外。今據此經，圓實為大。若從彼論三藏對衍，通別二教亦名一印。今不取二，唯圓實相名一印也，則能說之人、所觀依正、四種淨穢、五逆罪等其性不二。以此一印，為經正體。

Ru-Yi Translation

The one seal of the Great Vehicle is the single true mark: the two kinds of birth-and-death and the nirvāṇa of the three virtues are one in essence, ultimate and true. If the meaning tallies with this, one can believe and accept that it is a sūtra of the Great Vehicle, neither small nor non-Buddhist. Now, according to this sūtra, the perfect and real is the great. If, following that treatise, the Tripiṭaka is contrasted with the Mahāyāna, then the shared and distinct teachings are also called the one seal. Here we do not take those two; only the true mark of the perfect [teaching] is called the one seal. Then the person who speaks, the dependent and principal that are contemplated, the pure and defiled of the four kinds [of land], the offenses of the five heinous transgressions, and so on, are not two in nature. This one seal is taken as the proper essence of the sūtra.

That to Which the Four Chapters Return

Chinese Source

二、「無量」下，為四章所歸。「無量功德」等者，經用歸也。經之力用亦名功德，力有滅惡之功、用有生善之德。滅一切惡、生一切善，是故功德受無量名。如此功德共嚴實體，其猶帝王治亂育民。以此功德莊嚴聖躬，種種眾行即經宗也。從理起行，全理成修，如水為波、波還歸水。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "immeasurable" on, [the essence] as that to which the four chapters return. "Immeasurable merits" and so on: this is the sūtra's function returning [to it]. The sūtra's power and function are also called merit and virtue: power has the merit of extinguishing evil, and function has the virtue of producing good. It extinguishes all evil and produces all good; therefore its merit and virtue receive the name "immeasurable." Such merits and virtues together adorn the real essence, just as an emperor quells disorder, nurtures the people, and with such merit and virtue adorns his sacred person. "All kinds of practices" is the sūtra's cardinal doctrine. Practice arises from principle, and the whole of principle becomes cultivation, as water becomes waves and the waves return to water.

Chinese Source

宗必會體，故云「歸趣」。通則萬行、別觀十六，故名「眾行」。「言說問答」，即經名也，能詮之名在於言說。言義幽奧，復須問答種種詮辯，以立經名而彰實體。

Ru-Yi Translation

The cardinal doctrine necessarily meets the essence; hence it says "converge on." In common terms they are the myriad practices; in particular terms, the sixteen contemplations; hence they are called "all kinds of practices." "Words, questions, and answers" is the sūtra's name: the name that expresses lies in words. Since the meaning of the words is deep and recondite, there must also be questions and answers, all kinds of expression and discussion, to establish the sūtra's name and display the real essence.

Chinese Source

問：題目為名，何嘗問答等邪？答：名能詮體，一部言句皆能詮名。如《法華經》本迹十妙以為其名，但題是總，故就題釋名；餘之四義皆遍始終，故一經之名。「問答詮辯」等，從後向前，示能歸法。不別云教，兼在名中。自稟曰名、化他為教，自他雖異俱是能詮，故知四章同歸一體。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The title is the name; how could it be questions and answers and so on? Answer: The name can express the essence, and all the words and phrases of the whole scripture are the name that expresses. Thus the Lotus Sūtra takes the ten wonders of origin and trace as its name; only, the title is the general [form], so the name is explained in terms of the title. The other four meanings all pervade from beginning to end; hence [the words of] the whole sūtra are its name. "Questions, and answers express and discuss" and so on: going from the last back to the first, it shows the dharmas that return. It does not mention the teaching separately, because [the teaching] is included in the name. What one receives oneself is called the name; [what serves] to transform others is the teaching. Though self and other differ, both are what expresses; hence we know that the four chapters return alike to one essence.

Chinese Source

三、「譬眾」下，約二喻顯尊。體於四章，猶如北辰，眾星環拱；又似東海，萬水潮宗。以其四章不暫離體，一切諸法無理不成。經體既然，安得不辯？四、「故以」下，以一印結示。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "like the stars" on, revealing [its] honor by two similes. The essence is to the four chapters like the North Star, around which the stars circle in homage, or like the Eastern Sea, to which the ten thousand waters flow in tribute. Because the four chapters are never for a moment apart from the essence, no dharma is accomplished without principle. Since the essence of the sūtra is so, how could it not be clarified? Fourth, from "Therefore" on, concluding and showing by the one seal.

The Virtues of the Essence: Rites, Bottom, Penetrating

Chinese Source

三、「書家」下，具明體德。體對釋名，但在一德，所謂法身。蓋釋名中總示三法，利根雖解，鈍者未明。何者？以總示文帶於宗用，體混其中，情想叵忘，本性難顯。故於總後，別示靈源，永異四魔，諸法皆實。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "Literary scholars" on, fully making clear the virtues of the essence. The essence, as against the explanation of the name, lies in only one virtue, namely the Dharma-body. For in explaining the name the three dharmas were shown generally; though those of sharp faculties understood, the dull did not yet understand. Why? Because the text that shows them generally carries the cardinal doctrine and the function along with it, the essence is mixed among them; thoughts of feeling are hard to forget, and the original nature is hard to reveal. Hence, after the general [showing], the numinous source is shown particularly, forever different from the four Māras, and all dharmas are real.

Chinese Source

於彼圓伊當上一點，絕思絕議、非用非宗。而其性融，一不定一，如伊一點，點不孤然，故直法身非法身，法身必具般若、解脫，故別顯體而談三義。雖彰三德，意在法身，以空假皆中，是故明三名為體德。

Ru-Yi Translation

In that perfect i it corresponds to the upper dot, beyond thought and beyond discussion, neither function nor cardinal doctrine. Yet its nature is fused: the one is not fixed as one. Like the one dot of the i, the dot is not solitary; hence a bare Dharma-body is not the Dharma-body, for the Dharma-body necessarily contains prajñā and liberation. Hence, in revealing the essence particularly, it discusses three meanings. Though it displays the three virtues, the intent is on the Dharma-body: because the empty and the provisional are both the middle, the three that are made clear are called the virtues of the essence.

Chinese Source

中三：初、約禮義明法身。書既以體而釋於禮，故今以禮而釋於體。禮別尊卑，意崇君父。前明魔事已揀偏邪，今之臣子唯揀宗用，故君父體即是法身。諸佛所師萬法朝會，體非修證、理絕言思。欲使標心，強稱貴極。斯是本覺，非寂非照；亦是法性，非深非廣。第一義諦名為本性法身德也。尋能詮名，欲識此體，體顯故行，令修觀者以此體德體彼依正，一一貴極，成妙宗矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within this, three: first, making clear the Dharma-body in terms of the meaning of rites. Since the literary scholars explained 禮 (rites) by 體, now 體 (essence) is explained by 禮. Rites distinguish the honored and the humble; their intent is to revere lord and father. Above, in making clear the work of Māra, the one-sided and deviant were already sifted out; the ministers and sons here sift out only the cardinal doctrine and the function, so the body of lord and father is the Dharma-body. That which all buddhas take as teacher, to which the myriad dharmas pay court: its essence is not cultivated or realized, and its principle is beyond words and thought. To let [people] fix their minds on it, it is by force called utmost nobility. This is original awakening, neither tranquil nor illuminating; it is also dharma-nature, neither deep nor broad. The truth of the first principle is called the virtue of the Dharma-body of the original nature. Following the name that expresses, one wishes to know this essence; because the essence is revealed, one practices, letting the one who cultivates contemplation, with this virtue of the essence, embody that dependent and principal [reward], each one of utmost nobility. Thus the wondrous cardinal doctrine is accomplished.

Chinese Source

二、「復次」下，約底義明般若。德空即中故，故般若德是諸法底，亦名本源、淵府、實際。若得中體，則能窮暢也。論云「智度」，即實相般若。佛以觀照般若於諸法中證此智體，故云「窮底」。然法性甚深無有底際，云窮底者，良由佛以無底際智稱性而證，義言窮底。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Further" on, making clear prajñā in terms of the meaning of bottom. Because the virtue of emptiness is identical to the middle, the virtue of prajñā is the bottom of all dharmas, also called the original source, the depths of the abyss, the limit of reality. If one obtains the essence of the middle, one can reach it thoroughly. The treatise's "perfection of wisdom" is the prajñā of the true mark. The Buddha, by the prajñā of contemplative illumination, realizes this essence of wisdom within all dharmas; hence it says "reaches the bottom." Yet dharma-nature is extremely deep and has no bottom or limit; it says "reaches the bottom" because the Buddha, with a wisdom without bottom or limit, realizes it in accord with the nature; in meaning this is called reaching the bottom.

Chinese Source

七方便人以有底智，故不能到諸法源底。若圓教人，從名字即，以信解心窮智度底；五品觀行窮底；十信相似窮底；四十一位分證窮底；唯佛與佛究竟窮底。以此底義辯於經體，則彰法性甚深第一義空，名般若德也。尋名識體，體顯故行，令修觀者以此體德，窮彼依正一一到底，成妙宗矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

The persons of the seven expedient [stages], having a wisdom with a bottom, cannot reach the source and bottom of dharmas. Persons of the perfect teaching, from identity in name, reach the bottom of the perfection of wisdom with a mind of faith and understanding; at the five grades they reach the bottom in contemplative practice; at the ten faiths they reach the bottom in resemblance; at the forty-one stages they reach the bottom in the portion realized; only a buddha together with a buddha reaches the bottom ultimately. When the essence of the sūtra is clarified by this meaning of bottom, it displays the extremely deep emptiness of the first principle of dharma-nature, called the virtue of prajñā. Following the name, one recognizes the essence; because the essence is revealed, one practices, letting the one who cultivates contemplation, with this virtue of the essence, reach to the bottom of that dependent and principal, each one. Thus the wondrous cardinal doctrine is accomplished.

Chinese Source

三、「復次」下，約達義明解脫德。假即中故，故解脫德是一切法自在之體，復具一切真實名義。若識此體，則於諸法通達自在，復於世間及出世間一切異名，一中解多、多中解一。論云「般若」，亦實相般若。般若、解脫名殊義一，故互舉也。前明底義，以觀照般若窮實相底；今明達義，以文字般若說實相般若種種名也。七方便人迷此體故，於諸異名壅塞障礙。圓教行人名字體達、觀行體達、相似體達、分真體達。論今舉佛究竟體達，達義辯體，則彰法性無量如來藏義，名為真性解脫德也。令修觀者以此體德，達彼依正一一無壅，成妙宗矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "Further" on, making clear the virtue of liberation in terms of the meaning of penetrating. Because the provisional is identical to the middle, the virtue of liberation is the essence of the freedom of all dharmas, and further contains all true and real names and meanings. If one recognizes this essence, one penetrates all dharmas freely, and further, among all the different names of the worldly and the world-transcending, understands the many in the one and the one in the many. Copying errors? Several readings may be copying errors: 正信序 ("the preface of correct faith") for 證信序; 圓人初從 for 圓人初住 (rendered "from the first [abiding]"); 此乃諸佛乃大菩薩, where the second 乃 is probably 及; and "三、覺" / "四、種分別," read as "the three awakenings" / "four kinds of distinction." All are translated as they stand.

Horizontal and vertical lands. Zhili's 橫／豎 here is distinct from 縱／橫 (vertical / horizontal) in the three-virtues discussion. 豎 is rendered "vertically" (entering a land by transformation of body), 橫 "horizontally" (seeing a higher land from where one is).

Alternatively. 如果報修因二種行人 is rendered "practitioners of the two kinds, [relying on] the fruit of reward and cultivating causes." It could also be read "those who cultivate causes for a fruit of reward, [and the like]."
