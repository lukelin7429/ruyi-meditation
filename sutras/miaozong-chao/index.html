The Wondrous Purport.

Siming Zhili (四明知禮, 960–1028), the great systematizer of Song Tiantai, was also a lifelong Pure Land practitioner. In this sub-commentary he explains the Commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra phrase by phrase and makes it the foundation of a Pure Land practice rooted in Tiantai doctrine: to contemplate the Buddha is to contemplate the mind, because the mind inherently contains the three thousand realms — the Buddha and his land included. Contemplating Amitābha is therefore at once the contemplation of mind and a real seeing of the Buddha who will receive the practitioner at death. The work became the classic of Tiantai Pure Land.

How to read this collection

Each fascicle is a page. Quotations of the Commentary use this site's translation of it, and quotations of the sūtra the site's Contemplation Sūtra; read the three together.

Fascicles 1–3 cover the five profound meanings, with Zhili's central arguments on contemplating the Buddha in terms of the mind and on the four lands; fascicles 4–6 go through the sixteen contemplations.

What each page includes

  • The Chinese source text beside our English translation, paragraph by paragraph, checked character by character against the CBETA edition so that nothing is skipped.
  • A short introduction to each fascicle, and reading notes on allusions, technical terms, and textual variants.
  • Terminology matched to this site's existing Tiantai translations.

The Text's Structure

Six fascicles, one page each.

Fascicle 1
Zhili's Preface, the Commentary's Preface, and the Buddha in Principle and in Name
This part is the first fascicle of Siming Zhili's Wondrous Purport (T1751), written in 1021 as a line-by-line sub-commentary on Zhiyi's Commentary on the Sūtra on the Contemplation of Amitāyus Buddha.
Translated
Fascicle 2
The Buddha in Contemplative Practice through the Ultimate; "Speaks," "Contemplation," and "Immeasurable Life"
Fascicle two continues Zhili's line-by-line sub-commentary on the Explanation of the Name in Zhiyi's Commentary.
Translated
Fascicle 3
Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching; the Preface of the Sūtra
Fascicle three completes Zhili's sub-commentary on the five profound meanings of Zhiyi's Commentary and begins the line-by-line reading of the sūtra.
Translated
Fascicle 4
Pure Karma and the Contemplations from the Sun to the Image
Fascicle four finishes the preface of occasion (the Buddha's coming to Vaidehī's palace, compared with the Śrīmālā, and her plea) and follows the main teaching of the sūtra from the light between the Buddha's brows through the eighth contemplation, the image of the Buddha and the two bodhisattvas.
Translated
Fascicle 5
The Ninth Contemplation: The Buddha's True Dharma-Body
Fascicle five is given wholly to the ninth contemplation, the Buddha's body, which the Commentary calls "the Buddha's true Dharma-body." Zhili first explains why a body with form and marks, the excellent response of the reward-body, is named the Dharma-body: in the sudden teaching reward and response are what the nature inherently contains, so the three bodies are "identical to one." Fascicle 6
The Two Bodhisattvas, the Nine Grades, and the Transmission
The last fascicle finishes Zhili's defense of the ninth contemplation's body as the specially honored body, answering objections drawn from the Commentary on the Lotus, the larger Sūtra of Immeasurable Life, and the Pratyutpanna: the thirty-two marks are the constant body of Sukhāvatī seen by all, while the eighty-four thousand marks are manifested for those of great capacity.
