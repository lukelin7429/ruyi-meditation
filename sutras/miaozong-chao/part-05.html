The Ninth Contemplation: The Buddha's True Dharma-Body

Fascicle five is given wholly to the ninth contemplation, the Buddha's body, which the Commentary calls "the Buddha's true Dharma-body." Zhili first explains why a body with form and marks, the excellent response of the reward-body, is named the Dharma-body: in the sudden teaching reward and response are what the nature inherently contains, so the three bodies are "identical to one." He then follows the sūtra mark by mark, insisting that each is contemplated with "this mind makes the buddha, this mind is the buddha," and explains the Commentary's three kinds of loving-kindness (conditioned by beings, by dharmas, and without condition) through the three truths, quoting the Nirvāṇa Sūtra story of Mahāsenadattā. On the white tuft he explains why beginners are told to picture Śākyamuni's smaller tuft. The second half is a long series of questions and answers arguing against the view that the body of eighty-four thousand marks is only the birth-body. Zhili holds that the birth-body and the specially honored body are distinguished not by the number of marks but by the provisional and real principles, the contemplations of emptiness and the middle, and the two consciousnesses of the Awakening of Faith. The argument continues into fascicle six.

The Ninth Contemplation: Why the "True Dharma-Body"

Chinese Source

◎三第九、佛身觀，二：初、分科。「真法身」者，前觀寶像則似佛身，今對彼似故名為真。然此色相是實報身應同居土，亦名尊特亦名勝應，而特名法身者，為成行人圓妙觀也。良以報應屬修、法身是性，若漸教說，別起報應二修、莊嚴法身一性；若頓教詮，報應二修全是性具、法身一性舉體起修，故得全性成修、全修在性，三身融妙、指一即三。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the ninth, the contemplation of the Buddha's body, in two: first, dividing the sections. "True Dharma-body": the jeweled image contemplated before resembled the Buddha's body; now, set against that resemblance, it is called true. Yet these forms and marks are the reward-body of true reward responding in the land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together; they are also called the specially honored [body] and also the excellent response[-body]. That they are specially named the Dharma-body is in order to accomplish the practitioner's perfect and wondrous contemplation. Truly, because reward and response belong to cultivation while the Dharma-body is the nature: if the gradual teaching speaks of it, the two cultivations of reward and response are separately raised up to adorn the single nature of the Dharma-body; if the sudden teaching expresses it, the two cultivations of reward and response are wholly what the nature inherently contains, and the single nature of the Dharma-body in its whole essence gives rise to cultivation. Hence one obtains the whole nature becoming cultivation and the whole of cultivation lying in the nature; the three bodies are fused and wondrous, and pointing to one is identical to the three.

問：既言指一即三，但名為應自攝二身，何故疏文立法身稱？答：若言報應，恐濫別修歸於別教。今以報應名為法身，即顯三身皆非修得。故今家生身、應身、報身、法身，對藏、通、別、圓。行者應知，圓宗大體非唯報應稱為法身，亦乃業惑名為理毒，三觀十乘名性德行，慈悲與拔性德苦樂。今之勝應稱為法身，顯示妙宗其旨非淺，須袪滯想方見旨歸。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Since it says that pointing to one is identical to the three, merely calling it response would of itself include the two [other] bodies; why does the Commentary set up the designation "Dharma-body"? Answer: If one spoke of reward and response, one might confuse them with particular cultivation and assign them to the distinct teaching. Now, calling reward and response the Dharma-body displays that none of the three bodies is attained by cultivation. Hence our school matches the birth-body, the response-body, the reward-body, and the Dharma-body with the Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect [teachings]. Practitioners should know that in the overall essence of the perfect doctrine, not only are reward and response called the Dharma-body; karma and delusion too are named the poison of principle, the threefold contemplation and the ten modes [of contemplation] are named the practice of inherent virtue, and the giving and removing of loving-kindness and compassion are the suffering and happiness of inherent virtue. That the present excellent response is called the Dharma-body displays the wondrous purport, whose meaning is not shallow; one must dispel stagnant thinking to see where its meaning leads.

The Contemplating Mind and the Buddha's Body

Chinese Source

二、隨釋，五：初、明結上。二、正觀佛身。既指報應名為法身，即顯彌陀三身具足。既為妙境，但是法身行人心觀。即空假中，空假是二修，中觀是一性。修性冥妙三觀圓融既為能觀，但是般若境觀相契見尊特身，雖具三身但名解脫。此則以三照三故發現三，合此三三只是一三，三不定三同在一念，一念無念三三宛然，如此方名修心妙觀，此觀能令四土皆淨。若不爾者，非是頓教所詮妙觀。當以此觀觀彌陀身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining in order, in five: first, making clear the conclusion of the above. Second, directly contemplating the Buddha's body. Since reward and response are pointed to and named the Dharma-body, this displays that Amitābha is complete in the three bodies. Since this is the wondrous object, the Dharma-body alone is [what] the practitioner's mind contemplates. [The contemplation] is identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle: the empty and the provisional are the two cultivations, the contemplation of the middle is the single nature. Cultivation and nature merge wondrously, and the threefold contemplation is perfectly fused; since this is what contemplates, it is prajñā alone. Object and contemplation tally, and one sees the specially honored body; though it is complete in the three bodies, it is called only liberation. This is illuminating the three with the three, hence the three are revealed; uniting these three threes, they are only one three. The three are not fixed as three, and are all in a single thought; a single thought is without thought, yet the three threes are distinctly present. Only this is called the wondrous contemplation of cultivating the mind, and this contemplation can make the four lands all pure. If it is not so, it is not the wondrous contemplation expressed by the sudden teaching. With this contemplation one should contemplate Amitābha's body.

Ru-Yi Translation

The sub-outline divides the sūtra into three: first, from "next contemplate" on, the general heading and brief listing. Second, directly contemplating the marks of the Buddha's body, in four: first, from "Ānanda" on, contemplating the body's color. Second, from "Buddha's body" on, contemplating the body's measure. The Commentary's explanation divides into two: first, briefly unpacking the sūtra text. Second, from "The eyes are like" on, weighing the measures, in two: first, gauging the body by the eyes. Second, settling the sūtra and faulting the translation. Third, contemplating the body's light. Yet in contemplating the color and measure, and the marks, features, and light, one must in every case use the earlier "this mind makes the buddha, this mind is the buddha" as the method of contemplation. Because the mind makes the buddha, the contemplating and the contemplated break and establish distinctly: breaking, the three delusions and the three wisdoms are all swept away; establishing, the three truths and the threefold contemplation are all accomplished. Without this breaking and establishing, it is not the meaning of the pure mind making the buddha. Because the mind is the buddha, one forgets the subject and forgets the object, neither breaking nor establishing; making it "is" in a single thought, negating and illuminating are simultaneous. This is contemplation identical to no contemplation, using unconditioned practice to cultivate the concentration of buddha-recollection. This method is the essential art of contemplating the Buddha; if it were not used now, why proclaim it? If this art is not applied, the excellent marks do not appear.

Contemplating the light divides into four: first, from "Every pore of his body" on, the light of the pores; second, from "That Buddha's halo" on, contemplating the halo; third, from "within this halo" on, the transformation-buddhas in the light; fourth, from "each transformation-buddha" on, the attendants of the transformation-buddhas. Fourth, contemplating the marks and features, in two: first, from "eighty-four thousand marks" on, directly showing the body of marks and features. The general marks and the particular marks, the general features and the particular features, the general light and the particular light: that these three [pairs of] general and particular are all said to be eighty-four thousand is because, the obstructions being identical [to virtue], virtue is revealed, and so this number is formed. The Buddha, while in the ordinary ground, possessed eighty-four thousand toils of dust, and in these toils of dust he saw the true mark in every case. Since principle and wisdom have united, he can manifest marks, features, and light; hence at each stage it says eighty-four thousand. The practitioner now contemplates and knows that the mind is it, and can take all the toils of dust as identical to the Buddha's marks.

Second, from "each ray of light" on, the light embracing beings. Beings and buddha are the same in essence; though the land is wide and beings many, in embracing them not one is lost. In the place on contemplating the Buddha's mind, [the Commentary] again explains why this text must embrace [beings]. Third, making clear that when the contemplation is accomplished one can see, in two: first, from "Whoever sees this" on, seeing one buddha. Second, from "sees all buddhas" on, seeing all buddhas. When one sees the Buddha by the contemplation of the middle, the Buddha's essence is perfectly fused, one identical to all, the same as the specially honored body; hence by contemplating one buddha one can see all buddhas.

Seeing the Buddha's Mind: The Three Kinds of Loving-Kindness

Chinese Source

三、正觀佛心。疏三；初、「眼見」下，因身見心。疏有二釋：初、約如來由大悲心起勝應身，故令行者觀身見心。「由見身」下，二、約行者觀想明故得見佛。心所以明者，由觀佛身。是故二意皆是由色而見於心，以心無形由色表故。以圓人所觀色心不二，既見微妙色，豈隔大悲心？故《勝鬘》云「如來色無盡，智慧亦復然」。既三種慈體是三諦，今三觀明故三慈顯，以用果法為觀行故，故於位位見佛色心。二、「佛心」下，正示心體。若匪無緣，慈悲不大。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, directly contemplating the Buddha's mind. The Commentary has three [parts]. First, from "When the eye sees" on, seeing the mind through the body. The Commentary has two explanations. First, in terms of the Tathāgata: because he raises the excellent response-body from the mind of great compassion, he lets the practitioner contemplate the body and see the mind. From "Through seeing the body" on, second, in terms of the practitioner: because the visualization becomes clear, one is able to see the Buddha. The reason the mind becomes clear is the contemplation of the Buddha's body. Therefore both meanings are seeing the mind by way of form, because the mind has no shape and is expressed through form. Because what the perfect person contemplates is form and mind not two, once he sees the subtle and wondrous form, how could he be cut off from the mind of great compassion? Hence the Śrīmālā says, "The Tathāgata's form is inexhaustible, and so too is his wisdom." Since the essence of the three kinds of loving-kindness is the three truths, now, because the threefold contemplation is clear, the three kinds of loving-kindness are revealed; because the dharma of the fruit is used as contemplative practice, at stage after stage one sees the Buddha's form and mind. Second, from "A buddha's mind" on, directly showing the essence of the mind. If it were not without condition, the loving-kindness and compassion would not be great.

三、「以無緣」下，引文廣釋，三：初、牒經引論以明文意。問：經文但云「以無緣慈攝諸眾生」，疏中何故兼明生法皆云無心？答：起三慈者，由三觀智照三諦也。照真即起法緣之慈，照俗即起眾生緣慈，照中即起無緣之慈。此三諦慈，淺不具深、深必具淺，故照真俗未必照中，若能照中必具真俗，故次第生法不即無緣。今無緣慈合具生法，豈但具二亦乃俱深，故今生法皆云無心。故《涅槃》云「慈若有無非有非無，如是之慈非諸聲聞辟支佛等所能思議」。當知三慈其體本一，非三非一而三而一，如是方名佛心慈也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "with loving-kindness without condition" on, quoting texts to explain at length, in three: first, citing the sūtra and quoting the treatise to make clear the meaning of the text. Question: The sūtra text only says "embracing all beings with loving-kindness without condition"; why does the Commentary also make clear [the other two], beings and dharmas, all said to be "without the mind"? Answer: The three kinds of loving-kindness arise because the wisdom of the threefold contemplation illuminates the three truths. Illuminating the real gives rise to loving-kindness conditioned by dharmas; illuminating the conventional gives rise to loving-kindness conditioned by beings; illuminating the middle gives rise to loving-kindness without condition. Of the loving-kindness of these three truths, the shallow does not contain the deep, while the deep must contain the shallow; hence illuminating the real and the conventional does not necessarily illuminate the middle, but if one can illuminate the middle one must contain the real and the conventional. Hence in the gradual [teaching], [loving-kindness conditioned by] beings and dharmas is not identical to [loving-kindness] without condition. Now loving-kindness without condition should contain beings and dharmas, and not only contain the two but both are deep; hence here [those conditioned by] beings and dharmas are both said to be "without the mind." Hence the Nirvāṇa says, "Loving-kindness may be existent, nonexistent, neither existent nor nonexistent; such loving-kindness cannot be conceived or discussed by the śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and the like." One should know that the three kinds of loving-kindness are originally one in essence, neither three nor one, yet three and yet one; only so is it called the loving-kindness of the Buddha's mind.

此自分三：初、眾生緣慈三無差別。今盡現前心與眾生，能所既絕無我心想，緣他眾生而一切眾生與我同體，十界因果不離一心，而此一心是慈體故，十界苦集四種道滅，能於一時任運與拔，故云無心攀緣自然現益。「如涅槃」下，〈梵行品〉文也。然彼經如來凡說八事：一伏醉象、二降力士、三化盧至、四度女人、五塗割瘡、六摩調達、七救群賊、八醫釋女。一一皆結云：慈善根力，見如是事。今文云「我實不往」者，即引第五塗割瘡文。文現一處，意通諸緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

This divides of itself into three. First, loving-kindness conditioned by beings: the three are without difference. Now, the mind exhausted [of distinctions] and beings present before one, subject and object being cut off and without the thought of a self in the mind, it takes other beings as conditions, and all beings are of the same essence as oneself. The causes and fruits of the ten realms do not depart from a single mind, and this single mind is the essence of loving-kindness; hence the suffering and origination of the ten realms and the four kinds of path and cessation can, at one time and effortlessly, be given and removed. Hence it says, "without the mind clinging to objects, it naturally manifests benefit." From "as the Nirvāṇa" on, this is the text of the "Chapter on Pure Practice." In that sūtra the Tathāgata tells of eight matters in all: first, subduing the drunken elephant; second, overcoming the strongmen; third, converting Luzhi (盧至); fourth, delivering the woman; fifth, anointing the cut wound; sixth, stroking Devadatta; seventh, rescuing the band of thieves; eighth, healing the Śākya women. Each one concludes, "Through the power of loving-kindness's own wholesome root, they saw such things." Here, the text's "I did not, in reality, go" quotes the fifth, on anointing the cut wound. The text appears in one place, but the meaning extends to all the stories.

言割瘡者，經云「波羅柰城有優婆夷，名摩訶斯那達多。夏九十日，屈請眾僧，奉施醫藥。有一比丘身嬰重病，良醫診之當須肉藥，若不得者命將不全。是優婆夷尋自取刀，割其股肉切以為羹，施病比丘，服已病差。女人患瘡苦惱，發聲稱佛。我在舍衛聞其音聲，於是女人起大悲心。是女尋見我持良藥塗其瘡上，還復如本。善男子！我於爾時實不往至波羅柰城持藥塗彼。當知皆是慈善根力，令彼女人見如是事。」

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the cut wound, the sūtra says: "In the city of Vārāṇasī there was a laywoman named Mahāsenadattā. During the ninety days of summer she invited the whole assembly of monks and offered them medicine. A monk was afflicted with a grave illness; a skilled physician examined him and [said] he required medicinal meat, and should he fail to obtain it his life could not be preserved. This laywoman then took a knife herself, cut flesh from her own thigh, sliced it and prepared it as a broth, and gave it to the sick monk; having taken it, he was cured of his illness. The woman, suffering the pain of her wound, cried out the Buddha's name. I was in Śrāvastī and heard her voice, and gave rise to a mind of great compassion toward this woman. This woman at once saw me bring good medicine and apply it to her wound, which was restored as it was before. Good son! I, at that time, did not, in reality, go to the city of Vārāṇasī and apply medicine to her. One should know that it was entirely through the power of loving-kindness's own wholesome root that this woman came to see things in this way."

今云「我實不往」者，正引此緣。不言女人而言眾生者，通收十界眾生，不以文害意也，即俗諦慈也。《涅槃》云「慈之所緣一切眾生，如緣父母妻子親屬。以是義故，名眾生緣。」以緣十界同在一心，故非次第生緣慈也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, "I did not, in reality, go" quotes exactly this story. That it says "beings" rather than "the woman" is in order to take in the beings of the ten realms generally; one must not let the wording harm the meaning. This is the loving-kindness of the conventional truth. The Nirvāṇa says, "The object of loving-kindness is all beings, as one takes as objects one's father and mother, wife and children, and relatives. For this reason it is called conditioned by beings." Because the ten realms it takes as objects are all within a single mind, it is not the gradual loving-kindness conditioned by beings.

Chinese Source

二、法緣慈十界緣起。是三諦法不離一心，唯佛究盡。境相既寂，能觀亦忘，是故得云無心觀法。而畢竟空智照此三諦不受一塵，此智自然照破眾生三諦惑著，或為眾生說斯空慧，皆令得離有相之苦、證真實樂。此即不思議真諦慈悲，名為法緣。故《涅槃》云「不見父母妻子親屬，見一切法皆從緣生，是名法緣。」不見之言，須忘十界，是佛法緣也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, loving-kindness conditioned by dharmas: the conditioned arising of the ten realms. These dharmas of the three truths do not depart from a single mind, and only a buddha fathoms them fully. Since the marks of the object are tranquil, the contemplating too is forgotten; therefore one can say "without the mind contemplating dharmas." Yet the wisdom of ultimate emptiness illuminates these three truths without admitting a single mote of dust, and this wisdom naturally illuminates and breaks beings' delusive attachment to the three truths; or it explains this wisdom of emptiness for beings, letting them all leave the suffering of marks of existence and realize true happiness. This is the inconceivable loving-kindness and compassion of the truth of the real, called conditioned by dharmas. Hence the Nirvāṇa says, "Not seeing father and mother, wife and children, and relatives, one sees that all dharmas arise from conditions; this is called conditioned by dharmas." The words "not seeing" require forgetting the ten realms; this is the Buddha's [loving-kindness] conditioned by dharmas.

Chinese Source

三、無緣慈，以佛性中成究竟智，有何別理為心所緣？故云無心觀理。境智既泯、空有又忘，無住無依絕思絕議，此名安住第一義中。心既無緣，慈乃周遍入眾生性，稱為內熏；或為現身說第一義，稱為外熏。以此攝生，名無緣慈。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, loving-kindness without condition: since in buddha-nature he has accomplished ultimate wisdom, what separate principle is there to be the mind's object? Hence it says "without the mind contemplating principle." Object and wisdom having vanished, and emptiness and existence forgotten as well, without abiding and without reliance, beyond conceiving and beyond discussion: this is called abiding in the first principle. Since the mind is without condition, loving-kindness pervades everywhere and enters the nature of beings; this is called inner perfuming. Or he manifests a body and explains the first principle; this is called outer perfuming. Embracing beings in this way is called loving-kindness without condition.

Chinese Source

二、「念佛」下，却牒前經以對初慈。即前正觀佛身光明攝生之文也。雖與無緣慈體不別，若約義辯為門不同，是故此慈念佛眾生攝取不捨，終令離苦永得安樂。此從感應生佛相關，順於俗諦名生緣慈，故舉魚母念子不失，喻此慈相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "being who is mindful of the Buddha" on, going back to cite the earlier sūtra text to match it with the first [kind of] loving-kindness. This is the earlier text, in directly contemplating the Buddha's body, of the light embracing beings. Though it is not different in essence from loving-kindness without condition, if we discuss it in terms of meaning, the gates differ. Therefore this loving-kindness embraces and never abandons beings who are mindful of the Buddha, and in the end lets them leave suffering and forever attain peace and bliss. Ru-Yi Translation

Although in this contemplation the sūtra text does not yet permit contemplating the Buddha's body, it has one first visualize a great jeweled image seated in proportion to the throne, and visualize the two bodhisattvas as well, each seated on a throne. All the more, one uses throughout the wondrous contemplation of "makes" and "is" not two to contemplate them, letting the mind become fluent, and only then has one contemplate the Buddha. Students should know that the threefold contemplation cultivated from the sun contemplation onward is shared with phenomenal meditation, truly because all of them require the visualization to be accomplished and the marks to arise. Once phenomenal meditation is excellent, the delusions of [habituated] thought of the three realms are all subdued; when the wondrous contemplation contemplating the image breaks views, one ascends to the stage of the seventh faith. Only after attaining this stage does one contemplate the Buddha's true Dharma-body, whose eighty-four thousand marks appear; only then can it be called the samādhi of buddha-recollection, and one at once elicits the buddhas appearing before one to confer the prediction, and on being born there one realizes the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas. The sūtra text in this way makes clear the marks revealed by the perfect and deep contemplation; truly they are extraordinary, and are certainly not the marks of the birth-body that ordinary beings and the lesser vehicle constantly see.

The Object Follows Understanding

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The preface explaining the title says, "Amitāyus Buddha is the excellent object of contemplation." Is this not relying on the dependent and principal [rewards], form and mind of that [Buddha], to cultivate the threefold contemplation and reveal the principle of the three truths? Now since the eighty-four thousand marks are the principal reward, their meaning corresponds to the birth-body; relying on them one cultivates contemplation, and when the contemplation is accomplished and principle revealed, one sees marks as numerous as the dust-motes of the treasury-ocean, and only that is called specially honored. How could the segmented birth-body be the specially honored [body]?

Chinese Source

答：前正釋題，以妙三身解所觀境。今至經文，以八萬相為所觀境。信八萬相與妙三身無二無別，二處皆用不思議境而為所觀。故八萬相觀之令顯，顯名觀成，無別所顯。且行人念佛，誰不託佛正報修觀？但境隨解，名生名法。小機不解所觀佛身是法界用，謂正習生，故曰生身。大機能解所觀之佛是法界用，應既有本，生即同法，是故受於法身之稱，故見佛相若多若少皆稱法身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Earlier, directly explaining the title, [the Commentary] interpreted the object of contemplation as the wondrous three bodies; now, coming to the sūtra text, it takes the eighty-four thousand marks as the object of contemplation. Clearly, the eighty-four thousand marks and the wondrous three bodies are not two and not different; both places use the inconceivable object as what is contemplated. Hence contemplating the eighty-four thousand marks makes them revealed; being revealed is called the contemplation being accomplished; there is nothing separate that is revealed. Moreover, when practitioners recollect the Buddha, who does not rely on the Buddha's principal reward to cultivate contemplation? But the object follows the understanding, and is named birth or named Dharma. Those of lesser capacity do not understand that the Buddha's body they contemplate is the function of the dharma-realm, and take it as born of correct habits; hence it is called the birth-body. Those of great capacity can understand that the Buddha they contemplate is the function of the dharma-realm; since the response has a root, birth is the same as Dharma; therefore it receives the designation "Dharma-body," and so whether the Buddha's marks they see are many or few, all are called the Dharma-body.

Ru-Yi Translation

This sūtra clearly shows that the Buddha's dharma-realm body enters the mind's visualization; hence the Commentary heads it "contemplating the Buddha's Dharma-body"; this is the Dharma-body that is the three identical to one. Moreover, this is not the object contemplated by the beginning mind but the object contemplated at the seventh faith of the perfect. Once the perfect contemplation of the throne and image has been accomplished, how could one turn back to rely on the birth-body of the Tripiṭaka and shared [teachings] to cultivate contemplation? Further, contemplating the birth-body to reveal the treasury-dust marks is a person of the shared [teaching] being received into the distinct and perfect, not at all the perfect contemplation of the sudden teaching from beginning to end. Just as the thirty-two marks of the Pratyutpanna, once known to be manifested by the mind, are each the middle: to call the excellent object of contemplation the birth-body is deeply unacceptable.

Translator's notes

Lemmas from the Commentary. Cited phrases ("next contemplate," "The eyes are like," "When the eye sees," "Through seeing the body," "with loving-kindness without condition," "Now, making clear," "like a person," "Because one follows," "Enter through a single," "One may only," "he manifests an extraordinary body," and the others) follow the English of the Commentary (guanjing-shu Part 2, "The Ninth Contemplation"). Where the sūtra lemma is two characters (「佛身」, 「一一」) the English gives the nearest words of the site's sentence.

Mahāsenadattā. The story is in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T374/T375, 梵行品). Zhili abridges it (the search through the markets is left out), so the English follows the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra wording only where the Chinese matches. The definitions of loving-kindness conditioned by beings and by dharmas match T374/T375 word for word; "Loving-kindness may be existent, nonexistent…" is T375.

Śrīmālā. "The Tathāgata's form is inexhaustible…" is from Śrīmālā's verse of praise in T353.

Commentary on the Golden Light. The passage on the four buddhas abiding in the real, the middle, and so on was not found in that wording in T1785; T1785 has the related scheme of the four Ānandas who "receive" the sixteen-foot buddha, the sixteen-foot specially honored buddha of the combined body, the specially honored buddha, and [the Dharma-body]. Zhili seems to summarize. Dragon-Honored's verse "The buddhas are pure, subtle, wondrous, tranquil, and extinct" was not located in T663 in that wording.

Awakening of Faith. The two functions of the buddhas (according to the consciousness that discriminates phenomena and the karmic consciousness) match T1666.

Commentary on the Invitation to Avalokiteśvara. "The immeasurable of the measured / of the immeasurable" is in T1800.

Notes on the Textual Commentary. Miaole's "If the earlier three are hidden…" and the Prabhūtaratna passage are in T1719 (法華文句記). "Each mark is the ocean of the dharma-realm" is in T1718.

Other quotations. "Towering and majestic, like the moon among the stars" is found in Calming and Contemplation (T1911), not in the Medicine Master Sūtra (T450), so Zhili's attribution could not be confirmed.
