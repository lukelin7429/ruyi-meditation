Fascicle 6 · 攝淨義品第二之二 · Complete

Ultimate Truth, the Three Natures, and Taking Refuge

Fascicle 5 gave conventional truth; this one gives the ultimate. It arrives as the five items and the three natures, then as four kinds of reality, and then as the four investigations and the four knowledges they yield — a method for taking apart the relation between a name and a thing. The three natures are then examined from seven angles, including their use as a master key for reading every scripture whose meaning is not explicit. The second half turns to what one does with all this: the twelve genres of scripture, the six approaches to reading them, and finally the refuges — with an argument, unusually direct, for why the gods cannot serve as one.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Fascicle 6 complete Source: Taishō T1602, 顯揚聖教論卷第六, 攝淨義品第二之二 Verse companion: 顯揚聖教論頌 (T1603)

Where This Fascicle Picks Up

Fascicle 5 opened the chapter on Gathering the Pure Meaning and expounded conventional truth at length through the five aggregates. It closed having said what conventional truth asserts and why it is spoken at all. This fascicle takes up the other half of the pair.

Classical text is set in the left-hand column below, our English in the right. See also this site's note on translation method, and the note on this treatise's method in fascicle 1.

1. Ultimate Truth: The Five Items

Ultimate truth is set out under five headings that between them cover the whole relation of language to reality — the thing spoken of, the word, the mind that discriminates, what is actually there, and the knowledge that sees it.

Chinese Source

論曰：如是廣說世俗諦已，勝義諦云何？頌曰：

五三法、真實，　彼復四應知，

及四種尋思，　四種如實智。

Ru-Yi Translation

The exposition: conventional truth having been expounded at length, what is ultimate truth?

The five items, the three natures, the real —
which is further to be known as fourfold —
and the four investigations,
and the four knowledges in accordance with reality.

Chinese Source

論曰：五法者，一相、二名、三分別、四真如、五正智。相者，若略說，謂一切言說所依處。名者，謂於諸相中依增語。分別者，謂三界所攝諸心心法。真如者，謂法無我所顯、聖智所行、一切言說所不依處。正智者，略有二種：一唯出世間、二世間出世間。

唯出世間正智者，謂由正智聲聞獨覺諸菩薩等通達真如。又諸菩薩以世出世智，於五明處精勤學時，由遍滿真如智多現在前故，速疾證得所知障淨。世間出世間正智者，謂諸聲聞及獨覺等初通達真如已，由初一向出世間正智力，後所得世間出世間正智故，於諸安立諦中起厭怖三界心，及愛味三界寂靜處，又由彼正智多現在前故，速疾證得煩惱障淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

The exposition: the five items are mark, name, discrimination, suchness, and correct knowledge.

Mark: in brief, that on which all speech rests. Name: the designation applied to marks. Discrimination: all the mind and mental factors comprised in the three realms. Suchness: what is displayed by the selflessness of phenomena, the range of noble knowledge, and that on which no speech whatever rests. Correct knowledge: of two kinds in brief — the purely transcendent, and that which is both worldly and transcendent.

Purely transcendent correct knowledge: that by which śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and bodhisattvas penetrate suchness. And when bodhisattvas, with knowledge both worldly and transcendent, train diligently in the five sciences, then because the knowledge of all-pervading suchness is frequently manifest, they swiftly realize purity from the obstruction to what is to be known. Correct knowledge that is both worldly and transcendent: when śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas have first penetrated suchness, then by the power of that first, purely transcendent correct knowledge and by the subsequently attained knowledge that is both worldly and transcendent, they give rise, with respect to the established truths, to a mind that is weary and fearful of the three realms and that relishes the still places beyond them; and because that correct knowledge is frequently manifest, they swiftly realize purity from the obstruction of the afflictions.

Reading note

The five items are arranged so that suchness is defined by exactly the negation of what defines mark. A mark is 一切言說所依處, that on which all speech rests; suchness is 一切言說所不依處, that on which no speech rests. Everything sayable attaches to marks; suchness is what is left when nothing attaches. Between them stand name and discrimination, which are the two halves of how speech gets its purchase: a word applied to a mark, and a mind that sorts.

The two kinds of correct knowledge are distinguished by which obstruction they remove, and the difference is a Mahāyāna claim about the value of ordinary learning. The bodhisattva's knowledge is worldly and transcendent because he trains in the five sciences — logic, grammar, medicine, crafts, and the inner science — and that training is what removes the obstruction to what is to be known. The śrāvaka's subsequent knowledge removes the obstruction of the afflictions instead, and its mark is given without flattery: weariness and fear of the three realms, and a relish for the still places. The two paths are here distinguished by what their post-realization knowledge is turned toward.

2. The Three Natures, and Four Kinds of Reality

Chinese Source

三法者，謂三自體，一遍計所執自體、二依他起自體、三圓成實自體。遍計所執自體者，謂依名言假立自體，為欲隨順世間言說故。依他起自體者，謂從緣所生法自體。圓成實自體者，謂諸法真如聖智所行聖智境界，聖智所緣，為欲證得極清淨故、為令一切相及麁重二縛得解脫故、為欲引發諸功德故。

彼復四應知者，彼真實復有四種：一世間真實、二道理真實、三煩惱障淨智所行真實、四所知障淨智所行真實。

世間真實者，謂一切世間於諸事中，由串習所得悟入智見，共施設世俗性，如於地謂唯是地非火等。如是於水火風、色聲香味觸、飲食服乘、諸莊嚴具，及諸什物香鬘塗飾、歌舞音樂、眾朋男女、威儀諸行、田宅財物及苦樂等，於苦謂苦非樂，於樂謂樂非苦。又若略說者，謂此是此非彼，如是謂彼是彼非餘。若事世間有情決定勝解所行，一切世間自昔傳來名言決定自他分別，共為真實，非邪思搆觀察所取，是名世間真實。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three natures are the three own-natures: the imagined, the dependent, and the perfectly accomplished. The imagined own-nature: an own-nature nominally established in dependence on names and words, in order to accord with worldly speech. The dependent own-nature: the own-nature of things produced from conditions. The perfectly accomplished own-nature: the suchness of things, the range of noble knowledge, the domain of noble knowledge and what noble knowledge takes as its object — in order to realize utter purity, in order that one may be liberated from the two bondages of signs and of coarse heaviness, and in order to bring forth the good qualities.

Which is further to be known as fourfold: the real is of four kinds — the real as the world has it; the real as reasoning has it; the real that is the range of knowledge purified of the obstruction of the afflictions; and the real that is the range of knowledge purified of the obstruction to what is to be known.

The real as the world has it: that understanding, knowledge, and vision which all the world arrives at through habituation in the various matters, and jointly sets up as conventional — as with earth, that it is just earth and not fire, and so on. So too with water, fire, and wind; with visible form, sound, smell, taste, and touch; with food, clothing, and conveyances; with ornaments, household goods, incense, garlands, and cosmetics, song, dance, and music, companions, men and women, the postures and activities, fields, houses, property, and pleasure and pain — that pain is pain and not pleasure, that pleasure is pleasure and not pain. Or, stated briefly: that this is this and not that, and that that is that and not something else. Whatever matters are the range of a settled conviction among the beings of the world, established by names handed down from of old, distinguished as self and other, and held in common to be real — not what is seized by false conjecture and speculation — this is called the real as the world has it.

道理真實者，謂正智者，有道理義。諸聰叡者、諸黠慧者、諸推求者、諸審察者、住尋思地者、具自辯才者、處異生位者、隨觀察行者，依現、比、至教三量，極善決擇智所行所知事。以證成道理所建立故，是名道理真實。

煩惱障淨智所行真實者，謂一切聲聞獨覺無漏方便智、無漏正智、無漏後所得世間智等所行境界，是名煩惱障淨智所行真實。由緣此故，於煩惱障智得清淨，及後證住無障礙性，是故說為煩惱障淨智所行真實。問：此中何者是真實？答：謂苦集滅道名之所顯四種聖諦，由簡擇如是四聖諦故得入現觀位，於現觀位中真實智生。

所知障淨智所行真實者，謂於所知中能礙智故，名所知障。若真實性，是解脫所知障智所行境界，是名所知障淨智所行真實應知。此復云何？謂諸菩薩佛薄伽梵為入法無我及已入極清淨者，依一切法離言說自性假說自性無分別平等智所行境界，謂最勝真如無上所知究竟性。此性一切正法簡擇，不能迴轉、不能過越，是名所知障淨智所行真實。

The real as reasoning has it: that which has the sense of reasoning for those of correct knowledge. It is the knowable matter that is the range of the utterly well-discerning knowledge of the clever, the sharp-witted, the inquiring, the examining, those abiding on the level of investigation, those possessed of their own eloquence, those in the position of ordinary beings, and those who proceed by observation — relying on the three means of knowledge: direct perception, inference, and authoritative testimony. Because it is established by the reasoning of demonstration, it is called the real as reasoning has it.

The real that is the range of knowledge purified of the obstruction of the afflictions: the objective domain of all the uncontaminated knowledge of means, uncontaminated correct knowledge, and uncontaminated subsequently attained worldly knowledge of śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas. By taking this as object, knowledge becomes pure of the obstruction of the afflictions, and afterward one realizes and abides in unobstructedness. Question: what here is the real? Answer: the four noble truths displayed by the names suffering, origin, cessation, and path; for by discerning these four one enters the stage of direct realization, and in that stage the knowledge of the real arises.

The real that is the range of knowledge purified of the obstruction to what is to be known: what obstructs knowledge with respect to the knowable is called the obstruction to what is to be known; and the real nature that is the objective domain of the knowledge liberated from it is so called. And what is it? For the bodhisattvas and the buddhas — for those entering the selflessness of phenomena, and for those already entered and utterly pure — it is the objective domain of the non-discriminating knowledge of sameness, which rests on the ineffable own-nature of all things beneath their nominally spoken own-nature: namely, supreme suchness, the ultimate nature of what is unsurpassedly to be known. This nature no discernment of any true teaching can turn back or pass beyond. This is the real that is the range of knowledge purified of the obstruction to what is to be known.

Reading note

The four kinds of reality form a ladder, and the first two rungs are treated with a respect that is easy to underestimate. The real as the world has it — that earth is earth and not fire, that pain is pain — is explicitly distinguished from what is "seized by false conjecture." Ordinary consensus is not error; it is a genuine kind of reality, held in common, arrived at by habituation, and reliable within its range. The second kind is what careful reasoning establishes by the three means of knowledge, and the list of who does this reasoning pointedly includes 處異生位者, those in the position of ordinary beings. Neither of the first two levels requires any attainment.

The list of what the world takes as real is worth reading as the small anthology it is: earth and fire, food and clothing, garlands and cosmetics, song and dance, companions, men and women, fields and houses, pleasure and pain. Then the whole thing is compressed into one clause that could serve as a definition of conventional truth itself — 此是此非彼, this is this and not that. All worldly reality reduces to the operation of distinguishing.

3. The Four Investigations

A method rather than a doctrine: four things to look at until one sees them as no more than what they are.

Chinese Source

四種尋思者，一名尋思、二事尋思、三自體假立尋思、四差別假立尋思。名尋思者，謂諸菩薩於名唯見名。事尋思者，謂諸菩薩於事唯見事。自體假立尋思者，謂諸菩薩於假立自體唯見假立自體。差別假立尋思者，謂諸菩薩於差別假立唯見差別假立，是名差別假立尋思。此諸菩薩於名事二種，或離相觀，或合相觀，依名事合觀故，通達自體假立差別假立。

Ru-Yi Translation

The four investigations are: investigation of the name, of the thing, of the nominally established own-nature, and of the nominally established distinctions.

Investigation of the name: bodhisattvas see, in a name, nothing but a name. Investigation of the thing: they see, in a thing, nothing but a thing. Investigation of the nominally established own-nature: they see, in a nominally established own-nature, nothing but a nominal establishment. Investigation of the nominally established distinctions: they see, in nominally established distinctions, nothing but nominal establishment.

These bodhisattvas observe the two, name and thing, sometimes with their aspects apart and sometimes with their aspects joined; and it is by observing name and thing joined that they penetrate the nominally established own-nature and the nominally established distinctions.

Reading note

The formula is the same four times — 唯見, sees nothing but — and its force is entirely in what it withholds. To see in a name nothing but a name is to stop seeing the thing through it. To see in a thing nothing but a thing is to stop seeing its name in it. The first two investigations take apart a fusion so habitual that it is normally invisible: we do not experience a word and an object, we experience a named object.

The last sentence explains why there are four and not two. Own-nature and distinctions are what arise from name and thing being taken together — that this thing is by nature such-and-such, and that it differs thus from others. So the method requires observing the pair both separated and joined: separated, to see that neither carries the other; joined, to catch the moment where an own-nature and its distinctions get manufactured.

4. The Four Knowledges in Accordance with Reality

What each investigation yields when carried through. The first contains a compact argument for why nothing can be clung to without first being named.

Chinese Source

四種如實智者，一名尋思所引如實智、二事尋思所引如實智、三自體假立尋思所引如實智、四差別假立尋思所引如實智。

名尋思所引如實智者，由諸菩薩於名尋思唯有名已，於名如實了知，謂此名為此義故於此事中建立，為令世間起想見言說故。若於色等所想事中不為建立色等名者，一切世間無有能想此事；是色等若無想者，無有能起增益執著；若不執著則無言說。若如是如實了知，是名第一名尋思所引如實智。

事尋思所引如實智者，由諸菩薩於事尋思唯有事已，如實了知色等所想事性，離一切言說不可言說，是名第二事尋思所引如實智。

Ru-Yi Translation

The four knowledges in accordance with reality are those drawn forth by each of the four investigations in turn.

The knowledge drawn forth by investigating the name: having investigated the name and found nothing but a name, bodhisattvas know the name as it really is — that this name is set up with respect to this thing for the sake of this meaning, in order that the world may form conceptions, views, and speech. If, with respect to the conceived thing that is form and the rest, the name "form" and the rest were not set up, then no one in all the world could conceive of this thing; and if there were no conceiving of form and the rest, none could give rise to superimposition and clinging; and if there were no clinging, there would be no speech. Knowing this as it really is, this is the first knowledge, drawn forth by investigating the name.

The knowledge drawn forth by investigating the thing: having investigated the thing and found nothing but a thing, bodhisattvas know as it really is that the nature of the conceived thing that is form and the rest is apart from all speech and cannot be spoken. This is the second.

自體假立尋思所引如實智者，由諸菩薩於色等所想事假立自體中尋思唯假立，故如實通達假立自體，非實彼事自體而似彼事自體顯現。又能了知彼事自體，猶如變化影像響應光影水月焰水夢幻似有體性，是名第三自體假立尋思所引甚深義所行境如實智。

差別假立尋思所引如實智者，由諸菩薩於差別假立尋思唯假立性故，於色等所想事差別假立中善能通達不二之義。謂彼諸事非有性非無性，由可言說自體不成就故，非有性；由不可言說自體成就故，非無性。如是非有色，猶勝義諦故；非無色，由世俗諦中假立色故。如有性無性、有色無色，如是有見無見等諸差別假立法門，彼一切由是理趣盡應知。若能如實了知差別假立如是不二之義，是名第四差別假立尋思所引如實智。如是顯了所入事、能入因及能入已。

The knowledge drawn forth by investigating the nominally established own-nature: having investigated the nominally established own-nature of the conceived thing that is form and the rest, and found it to be nothing but nominal establishment, bodhisattvas penetrate as it really is that the nominally established own-nature is not the actual own-nature of that thing, yet appears as though it were the own-nature of that thing. And they can know that the own-nature of that thing is like a conjuring, a reflected image, an echo, a light-shadow, the moon in water, a mirage, a dream, an illusion — having the semblance of a substantial nature. This is the third, the knowledge whose range is the profound meaning.

The knowledge drawn forth by investigating the nominally established distinctions: having investigated the nominally established distinctions and found them to be nothing but nominal establishment, bodhisattvas are well able to penetrate the sense of non-duality within the nominally established distinctions of the conceived thing that is form and the rest. That is, those things are neither existent in nature nor non-existent in nature: because the own-nature that can be spoken is not accomplished, they are not existent in nature; and because the own-nature that cannot be spoken is accomplished, they are not non-existent in nature. Just so, there is no form, since it is by ultimate truth; and there is not no form, since form is nominally established within conventional truth. As with existent and non-existent, and with form and no form, so with visible and invisible and every other approach through nominally established distinctions — all of them should be exhaustively understood by this same principle. To know as it really is this non-dual sense within the nominally established distinctions is the fourth. Thus have been displayed the matter to be entered, the cause by which one enters, and the state of having entered.

Reading note

The argument inside the first knowledge is a small chain worth setting out on its own: no name, no conceiving; no conceiving, no superimposition and clinging; no clinging, no speech. Naming makes conception possible, conception makes clinging possible, and clinging is what generates speech in turn. The chain is circular by design — speech both begins and ends it — and it explains why the investigation of names is placed first among the four. It is the point where the loop can be cut.

The fourth knowledge gives the treatise's most careful formulation of neither-existent-nor-non-existent, and the two halves are asymmetric on purpose. Things are not existent because the speakable own-nature is not accomplished; they are not non-existent because the unspeakable own-nature is accomplished. Something really is there. What is not there is the version of it that words deliver. And the closing instruction is important for reading the rest of the treatise: this same principle exhausts every pair of distinctions whatever — visible and invisible, and all the rest of the long lists in fascicle 5. One argument dissolves all of them at once.

5. The Three Natures Under Seven Headings

A new verse takes the three natures apart from seven angles. The most consequential is the fourth: the three natures are the key by which every scripture of non-explicit meaning is to be read.

Chinese Source

復次頌曰：

三自體成立、　差別、業、隱密、

方便、攝、別異，　是各有多種。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore:

The three natures: their establishment,
their differences, function, hidden sense,
their use as means, their subsumption, their distinctness —
each of these is of many kinds.

Chinese Source

論曰：彼三種自體，成立、差別、業用、隱密、方便、攝別異，應知各有多種。成立多種者，如成無性品中當廣說。

差別者，問：遍計所執自體有幾種？答：如依他起自體中所有假立自體差別，如是遍計所執自體，是故遍計所執自體無有限量應知。復次於依他起自體中有二種遍計所執自體分別，謂隨勝覺及隨數習習氣隨眠。問：依他起自體有幾種？答：即如諸相多種差別應知。謂色相、心相、心法相、心不相應相如是等。復次若略說，有二種依他起自體，謂遍計所執自體分別所起及非分別所起。問：圓成實自體有幾種？答：圓成實自體於一切處一味故，不可建立差別。

業者，問：遍計所執自體能作幾業？答：有五種，一能生依他起自體、二即於是中起諸言說、三能生眾生執、四能生法執、五能攝受二執習氣麁重。問：依他起自體能作幾業？答：有五種，一能生諸雜染體、二能為遍計所執自體及圓成實自體所依、三能為眾生執所依、四能為法執所依、五能為二執習氣麁重所依。問：圓成實自體能作幾業？答：有五種，謂能為二種五業對治生起所緣性故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The exposition: the establishment, differences, function, hidden sense, use as means, subsumption, and distinctness of those three natures should each be understood as of many kinds. As for the many kinds of establishment, this will be expounded at length in the chapter on Establishing Naturelessness.

Differences. Question: how many kinds has the imagined own-nature? Answer: as many as the differences of nominally established own-nature found within the dependent own-nature — and therefore the imagined own-nature should be known to be without limit. Further, within the dependent own-nature there are two kinds of discrimination of an imagined own-nature: that which follows a dominant awareness, and that which follows the latent tendencies perfumed by repeated habituation. Question: how many kinds has the dependent own-nature? Answer: as many as the many differences of the marks — the mark of form, the mark of mind, the mark of mental factors, the mark of what is dissociated from mind, and so on. Further, in brief, the dependent own-nature is of two kinds: that arisen from the discrimination of an imagined own-nature, and that not so arisen. Question: how many kinds has the perfectly accomplished own-nature? Answer: since the perfectly accomplished own-nature is of one taste everywhere, no differences can be established in it.

Function. Question: how many functions can the imagined own-nature perform? Answer: five — it can produce the dependent own-nature; it gives rise to all speech within it; it can produce the clinging to persons; it can produce the clinging to phenomena; and it takes up the coarse heaviness perfumed by those two clingings. Question: how many can the dependent own-nature perform? Answer: five — it can produce the substance of all defilement; it can serve as the support of both the imagined and the perfectly accomplished own-natures; it can be the support of clinging to persons; the support of clinging to phenomena; and the support of the coarse heaviness perfumed by those two clingings. Question: how many can the perfectly accomplished own-nature perform? Answer: five — since it serves as the object by which the counteragents to those two sets of five functions arise.

Reading note

The most startling line here is the first function of the imagined nature: it 能生依他起自體, can produce the dependent own-nature. One might expect the dependent to be the ground and the imagined a mere overlay on it — and indeed the dependent is immediately listed as the support of the imagined. Both are said, and the circularity is deliberate. Conditioned arising throws up what gets misread, and the misreading in turn lays down the seeds from which further conditioned arising comes. This is the same point fascicle 1 made when it named the dependent nature "the mark of what arises from superimposition."

The asymmetry in the counts is also worth noting. The imagined nature is 無有限量, without limit — as many kinds as there are ways of nominally establishing anything. The dependent has as many kinds as there are marks. The perfectly accomplished has none at all, being 一味, of one taste everywhere. The three natures are not three parallel categories; they differ even in whether they admit of internal variety.

6. The Three Natures as a Key to Hidden Meaning

Chinese Source

隱密者，謂當隨三種自體義解釋一切不了義經，由無量經中一切如來隱密語言，及一切菩薩隱密語言，皆隨三種自體方可悟入彼義故。問：如經中說三解脫門，彼云何建立？答：由三自體故，謂由遍計所執自體故建立空解脫門，由依他起自體故建立無願解脫門，由圓成實自體故建立無相解脫門。問：如經中說無生法忍，彼云何建立？答：由三自體故，謂由遍計所執自體故說本來無生忍，由依他起自體故說自然無生忍，由圓成實自體故說煩惱苦垢無生忍。此三種忍在不退轉地應知。由如是等差別義故，於餘一切隱密語言皆應隨三自體解釋應知。

方便者，謂了知如是三種自體，能作一切聲聞獨覺無上正等菩提方便。

攝者，謂三種自體及相名分別等五事相攝。問：如是五事，初自體幾事攝？答：無。問：第二自體幾事攝？答：四。問：第三自體幾事攝？答：一。

別異者，謂遍計所執自體，唯正應知。依他起自體，應知及應斷。圓成實自體，應知及應作證。如是悟入俗諦勝義諦已。

Ru-Yi Translation

Hidden sense: one should interpret every scripture of non-explicit meaning according to the sense of the three natures, since all the hidden speech of all the Tathāgatas in measureless scriptures, and all the hidden speech of all the bodhisattvas, can only be entered into by way of the three natures.

Question: the scriptures speak of the three doors of liberation — how are they established? Answer: by the three natures. By the imagined own-nature the door of emptiness is established; by the dependent own-nature the door of wishlessness; by the perfectly accomplished own-nature the door of signlessness. Question: the scriptures speak of the forbearance for the non-arising of things — how is that established? Answer: by the three natures. By the imagined own-nature is taught the forbearance for what is originally unarisen; by the dependent own-nature, the forbearance for what is spontaneously unarisen; by the perfectly accomplished own-nature, the forbearance for the non-arising of the stains of affliction and suffering. These three forbearances should be understood as belonging to the stage of non-retrogression. By such differences of sense, all other hidden speech likewise should be interpreted according to the three natures.

Use as means: understanding these three natures can serve as the means to all that śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas attain, and to unsurpassed perfect enlightenment.

Subsumption: the mutual subsumption of the three natures and the five items — mark, name, discrimination, and the rest. Question: how many of the five items does the first nature subsume? Answer: none. Question: the second? Answer: four. Question: the third? Answer: one.

Distinctness: the imagined own-nature is only to be known. The dependent own-nature is to be known and to be cut off. The perfectly accomplished own-nature is to be known and to be realized. Thus one enters into both conventional and ultimate truth.

Reading note

The subsumption answers are the densest four words in the fascicle, and they are worth unpacking. Of the five items — mark, name, discrimination, suchness, correct knowledge — the imagined nature subsumes none. Not one of the five is the imagined nature, because the imagined nature is precisely what is not there. The dependent nature subsumes four: mark, name, discrimination, and correct knowledge, all of them conditioned occurrences. The perfectly accomplished subsumes one: suchness. That the imagined nature gets zero is the whole Yogācāra position stated arithmetically.

The reading of the three doors of liberation shows the key in use, and it is not the obvious mapping. One might expect emptiness to pair with the perfectly accomplished; instead emptiness answers to the imagined nature, because what is empty is precisely what was imagined. Wishlessness answers to the dependent, because conditioned things are not worth wishing for. Signlessness answers to the perfectly accomplished, because there suchness bears no signs. Compare the treatment of the same three doors in fascicle 2, which approached them through what is known and the knowing of it.

The final heading is the practical summary and repays memorizing: the imagined is only to be known; the dependent is to be known and cut off; the perfectly accomplished is to be known and realized. Nothing is done to the imagined nature except understanding it, because there is nothing there to do anything to.

7. The Twelve Genres of Scripture

Having said how scripture is to be read, the chapter turns to what scripture is. Twelve genres, each defined by what distinguishes it, and then sorted into the three baskets.

Chinese Source

復次頌曰：

聞十二分教，　三最勝歸依，

三學三菩提，　為有情淨說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore:

Hearing the twelvefold teaching,
the three supreme refuges,
the three trainings, the three enlightenments —
taught for the purification of beings.

Chinese Source

論曰：聞十二分教者，謂聞契經、應頌、記別、諷頌、自說、緣起、譬喻、本事、本生、方廣、未曾有法、論議聖教。

契經者，謂諸經中佛薄伽梵於種種時處、依種種所化有情調伏行差別，或說蘊所攝法、界所攝法、處所攝法，或說緣起所攝法，或說食所攝法、諦所攝法，或說聲聞、獨覺、如來所攝法，或說念住、正斷、神足、根、力、覺支、道支所攝法，或說不淨、息、念學、證淨等所攝法。如來說是語已，諸結集者歡喜敬受，為令聖教得久住故，以諸善妙名句字身，如其所應次第結集、次第安置，以能綴緝引諸義利、引諸梵行種種善義，故名契經。

應頌者，謂諸經中，或於中間、或於最後，以頌重顯，及諸經中不了義說，是為應頌。記別者，謂諸經中記諸弟子命終之後生處差別，及諸經中顯了義說，是為記別。諷頌者，謂諸經中非長行直說，然以句結成，或二句、或三句、或四句、或五句、或六句，是為諷頌。自說者，謂諸經中不列請者姓名，為令正法久住故及為聖教久住故，自然宣說，是為自說。緣起者，謂諸經中列請者姓名已而為宣說，及諸所有毘柰耶攝有緣起教別解脫戒經等，是為緣起。

Ru-Yi Translation

The exposition: hearing the twelvefold teaching means hearing the sacred teaching as discourse, mingled verse, prediction, verse, spontaneous utterance, occasioned teaching, simile, past events, birth stories, extensive teachings, marvels, and exposition.

Discourse: those scriptures in which the Buddha, the Blessed One, at various times and places and according to the differing disciplines of the various beings to be trained, teaches the things comprised in the aggregates, in the elements, in the sense fields, in dependent arising, in the nutriments, in the truths, in what belongs to śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and Tathāgatas; or the things comprised in the establishments of mindfulness, the right exertions, the bases of supernormal power, the faculties, the powers, the factors of awakening, and the limbs of the path; or the things comprised in the contemplation of impurity, mindfulness of breathing, the trainings, the confirmed faiths, and the rest. When the Tathāgata had spoken thus, those who compiled the canon received it with joy and reverence and, in order that the sacred teaching might long endure, compiled and arranged it in order with fine words, phrases, and syllables as was fitting. Because it strings together and draws forth benefit and the various good senses of the holy life, it is called discourse.

Mingled verse: where in a scripture, whether in the middle or at the end, verses restate what was said; and also the statements of non-explicit meaning in the scriptures. Prediction: where a scripture records the differing places of rebirth of disciples after death; and also the statements of explicit meaning. Verse: where a scripture speaks not in direct prose but in composed lines — two, three, four, five, or six lines. Spontaneous utterance: where a scripture does not name any petitioner, and the teaching is delivered of itself, so that the true Dharma and the sacred teaching may long endure. Occasioned teaching: where a scripture names the petitioner before the teaching is delivered; and also the teachings comprised in the Vinaya that have an occasion, such as the Prātimokṣa precept scriptures.

譬喻者，謂諸經中有譬喻說，由譬喻故本義明白，是為譬喻。本事者，謂宣說前世諸相應事，是為本事。本生者，謂諸經中宣說如來於過去世處種種生死行菩薩行，是為本生。方廣者，謂諸經中宣說能證無上菩提諸菩薩道，令彼證得十力無障智等，是為方廣。未曾有法者，謂諸經中宣說諸佛及諸弟子，比丘、比丘尼、式叉摩那、沙彌、沙彌尼、鄔波索迦、鄔波私迦等共不共功德，及餘最勝殊特驚異甚深之法，是為未曾有法。論議者，謂一切摩怛履迦、阿毘達磨，研究解釋諸經中義，是為論議。

如是十二分教中具有經、律、阿毘達磨藏。此中所說契經、應頌、記別、諷頌、自說、譬喻、本事、本生、方廣、未曾有法，是為經藏。此中所說緣起，是為律藏。此中所說論議，是為阿毘達磨藏。

三最勝歸依者，謂佛、法、僧三種歸趣。三學者，謂增上戒學、增上心學、增上慧學。三菩提者、謂聲聞菩提、獨覺菩提、無上正等菩提。

為有情淨說者，為令有情得清淨故，次第宣說是三種法，謂能持方便果。能持者，謂聞及歸依。方便者，謂三學。果者，謂三菩提。

Simile: where a scripture speaks by simile, so that the underlying sense becomes plain. Past events: the recounting of matters belonging to former lives. Birth stories: where a scripture recounts the Tathāgata's practice of the bodhisattva conduct amid the births and deaths of past ages. Extensive teachings: where a scripture sets forth the bodhisattva paths by which unsurpassed enlightenment is realized, causing them to attain the ten powers, unobstructed knowledge, and the rest. Marvels: where a scripture sets forth the qualities, shared and unshared, of the buddhas and their disciples — monks, nuns, probationers, novices of both kinds, and laymen and laywomen — and other supreme, extraordinary, astonishing, and profound teachings. Exposition: all the mātṛkās and Abhidharma, investigating and explaining the meaning of the scriptures.

These twelve genres contain the three baskets of Sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma. Of these, discourse, mingled verse, prediction, verse, spontaneous utterance, simile, past events, birth stories, extensive teachings, and marvels are the Sūtra basket. Occasioned teaching is the Vinaya basket. Exposition is the Abhidharma basket.

The three supreme refuges are the three places of resort: the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. The three trainings are the higher discipline, the higher mind, and the higher wisdom. The three enlightenments are those of the śrāvaka, of the pratyekabuddha, and unsurpassed perfect enlightenment.

Taught for the purification of beings: in order that beings may attain purity, these three kinds of teaching are set forth in order — that which sustains, the means, and the fruit. That which sustains: hearing, and taking refuge. The means: the three trainings. The fruit: the three enlightenments.

Reading note

Two of the twelve definitions carry a doctrinal payload that the others do not. Mingled verse is defined not only by form — verses restating prose — but also as "the statements of non-explicit meaning"; and prediction, defined by content as records of where disciples will be reborn, is also "the statements of explicit meaning." Asaṅga has folded the provisional-and-definitive distinction into two genre labels. Given the preceding section's instruction to read all non-explicit scripture by way of the three natures, this quietly tells the reader which parts of the canon that instruction applies to.

The mapping of twelve genres onto three baskets is unbalanced in a way worth noticing: ten genres make up the Sūtra basket, and the Vinaya and Abhidharma get one each. The Vinaya is identified with 緣起, occasioned teaching, and the identification is exact — a rule exists because something happened that called for it, and the Vinaya records the occasion along with the rule. The Abhidharma is identified with exposition and the mātṛkās, the classificatory lists from which Abhidharma grew. This treatise is itself, by its own classification, an instance of the twelfth genre.

8. Hearing, Analyzed Six Ways

A further verse turns each of the four just named — hearing, refuge, training, enlightenment — into a numbered analysis. Hearing gets six headings, and the third of them holds a checklist for both sides of a teaching.

Chinese Source

復次如是聞等，云何分別應知。頌曰：

聞、歸、學、菩提，　六、三、十二、五，

隨名數次第，　如應廣分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, how are hearing and the rest to be distinguished?

Hearing, refuge, training, enlightenment:
six, three, twelve, five —
each according to the order of its name and number,
to be distinguished at length as is fitting.

Chinese Source

論曰：聞六種分別者，一依處、二依攝、三依清淨、四依行、五依理趣、六依義。

依處者，謂依五明處：一內明處、二因明處、三聲明處、四醫方明處、五工業明處。

依攝者，有二種，謂聲聞藏攝、菩薩藏攝。

依清淨者，謂十種清淨。說清淨有五：一善說者說故、二顯了文句說故、三盡所知義如所知義說故、四易方便修行說故、五能出離一切苦說故。聽清淨有五：一不以求過意聽故、二以求涅槃意聽故、三極善諦聽故、四依名句字身義極善分別聽故、五以正修行意聽故。

依行者，謂十種法行。

Ru-Yi Translation

The exposition: hearing is distinguished six ways — by its field, by its collection, by its purity, by its practice, by its approach, and by its meaning.

By its field: the five sciences — the inner science, the science of reasoning, the science of language, the science of medicine, and the science of crafts.

By its collection: two — what is comprised in the śrāvaka canon, and what is comprised in the bodhisattva canon.

By its purity: ten kinds of purity. There are five purities of teaching: that it is spoken by one who speaks well; that it is spoken with plain words and phrases; that it is spoken exhausting the knowable sense and according with the knowable sense; that it is spoken so that practice is easy of means; and that it is spoken so as to deliver from all suffering. And five purities of listening: that one listens without a mind that seeks faults; that one listens with a mind that seeks nirvāṇa; that one listens with utmost attention; that one listens discerning most carefully by way of words, phrases, syllables, and meaning; and that one listens with a mind intent on right practice.

By its practice: the ten Dharma practices.

Reading note

The ten purities are the most immediately usable passage in this fascicle, because they make hearing a joint achievement with obligations on both sides. The teacher must speak plainly, exhaustively, practicably, and to the point of liberation. The listener must not come hunting for faults, must want liberation rather than information, must attend closely, must discern at the level of words and of meaning both, and must intend to practice. A teaching goes wrong if any of the ten fails, and five of the ten are the listener's.

Note that the first heading places the field of hearing in the five sciences — not only the inner science of the Dharma but logic, language, medicine, and crafts. This is the same claim made at the opening of this fascicle, where the bodhisattva's training in the five sciences was what removes the obstruction to what is to be known. Learning grammar or medicine is here classified as a form of hearing the Dharma.

9. The Six Approaches, and the Real as Established

The fifth heading opens the six approaches named at the close of fascicle 4 — and now they are explained, including a careful account of what it means for a truth to be "established."

Chinese Source

依理趣者，有六種理趣，謂真義理趣乃至意樂理趣。此中前三理趣由後三理趣隨釋應知。謂由離二邊理趣隨釋真義理趣，由不思議理趣隨釋證得理趣，由意樂理趣隨釋教導理趣。此中真義即是理趣，故名真義理趣。乃至意樂即是理趣，故名意樂理趣。於彼彼處無顛倒性，是理趣義。

真義理趣者，略有六種應知。謂世間真實，乃至所知障淨智所行真實，及安立真實非安立真實。此中前四種真實，如前分別應知。安立真實者，謂四聖諦。苦真苦故安立為苦，乃至道真道故安立為道。問：何因緣故名為安立？答：由三種俗所安立故，一由世間俗、二由道理俗、三由證得俗。世間俗者，謂安立田宅瓶盆軍林數等，及安立我有情等。道理俗者，謂安立蘊界處等。證得俗者，謂安立預流果等，及安立彼所依住法。復有四種安立，謂前三種，及由勝義俗安立勝義諦性不可安立，由內自所證故，為欲隨順引生彼智依俗安立。非安立真實者，謂一切法真如實性。

Ru-Yi Translation

By its approach: there are six approaches — the approach of the truly real, and so on down to the approach of intent. Of these, the first three are to be understood as explained by the latter three: the approach that departs from the two extremes explains the approach of the truly real; the approach of the inconceivable explains the approach of realization; and the approach of intent explains the approach of guidance. Here, the truly real is itself the approach, and so it is called the approach of the truly real; and so on down to intent. Being without distortion in each respective place is the sense of "approach."

The approach of the truly real is, in brief, of six kinds: the real as the world has it, and so on down to the real that is the range of knowledge purified of the obstruction to what is to be known — and then the established real and the non-established real. The first four have been distinguished above.

The established real: the four noble truths. Because suffering is truly suffering it is established as suffering, and so on down to: because the path is truly the path it is established as the path. Question: for what reason is it called "established"? Answer: because it is established by three kinds of convention — worldly convention, conventions of reasoning, and conventions of realization. Worldly convention: establishing fields, houses, pots, basins, armies, forests, numbers, and the rest, and establishing a self, a being, and so on. Convention of reasoning: establishing the aggregates, elements, sense fields, and the rest. Convention of realization: establishing the fruit of stream-entry and the rest, and the states on which they rest. There is also a fourth kind of establishment beyond these three: by the convention of the ultimate, one establishes that the nature of ultimate truth cannot be established — since it is inwardly realized for oneself; and in order to accord with and draw forth that knowledge, one establishes it in dependence on convention. The non-established real: the true nature, suchness, of all things.

Reading note

The fourth kind of establishment is a small logical knot handled with care. Ultimate truth cannot be established, since it is realized inwardly and not by any convention. But that very unestablishability has to be said, and saying it is itself an act of convention. Asaṅga names this 由勝義俗, the convention of the ultimate: a conventional establishment whose content is that no establishment reaches here. Its purpose is stated plainly — 為欲隨順引生彼智, to accord with and draw forth that knowledge. The same justification given in fascicle 5 for conventional truth as such.

The three conventions are also a neat ranking of vocabularies by who uses them. Fields, pots, armies, and selves belong to worldly convention. Aggregates, elements, and sense fields — the Abhidharma vocabulary — belong to the conventions of reasoning. And the fruits of the path belong to the conventions of realization. All three are conventions. The technical vocabulary of Buddhism is not thereby exempted.

10. The Approach of Realization, and the Approach of Guidance

Chinese Source

證得理趣者，略有四種：一一切有情業報證得、二聲聞乘證得、三獨覺乘證得、四大乘證得。

一切有情業報證得者，謂一切有情造作淨不淨業，依自業故，於五趣流轉中感種種異熟、受種種異熟。

聲聞乘證得者，謂初受三歸，乃至依止聞莊嚴故，得五證得，一地證得、二智證得、三淨證得、四果證得、五功德證得。地證得者，謂得三地：一見地、二修地、三究竟地。智證得者、謂得九智：一法智、二種類智、三苦智、四集智、五滅智、六道智、七此後所得俗智、八盡智、九無生智。淨證得者，謂四證淨。果證得者，謂四沙門果。功德證得者，謂無量解脫、勝處、遍處、無諍、願智、無礙解、神通等功德。復次聲聞乘證得中證得依止者，謂先修世間道離欲，次修順解脫分善根，後修順決擇分善根。

獨覺乘證得者，略有三種：一由先已得順決擇分善根故、二由先已得無漏真證故、三由次第得故。此中由前二證得者，名非獨勝覺；由後證得者，名犀角喻覺。

大乘證得者，謂大悲證得、發心證得、波羅蜜多證得、攝事證得、地證得、於五無量中隨至真如證得、不思議威德證得、不共佛法證得。彼一切如前分別應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

The approach of realization is, in brief, of four kinds: the realization that is the karmic recompense of all beings; the realization of the śrāvaka vehicle; of the pratyekabuddha vehicle; and of the Great Vehicle.

The karmic recompense of all beings: all beings perform pure and impure karma, and in dependence on their own karma they bring about and undergo the various ripened results as they flow on through the five destinies.

The realization of the śrāvaka vehicle: from first taking the three refuges, down to the adornment that rests on hearing, one obtains five realizations — of stage, of knowledge, of confirmed faith, of fruit, and of good qualities. Of stage: three stages, the stage of seeing, of cultivation, and the final stage. Of knowledge: nine knowledges — knowledge of things, inferential knowledge, knowledge of suffering, of origin, of cessation, of the path, the conventional knowledge subsequently attained, knowledge of exhaustion, and knowledge of non-arising. Of confirmed faith: the four confirmed faiths. Of fruit: the four fruits of the renunciant. Of good qualities: the measureless minds, liberations, bases of mastery, totalities, non-contention, the knowledge of vows, the analytic knowledges, the supernormal powers, and the rest. Further, the support of realization within the śrāvaka vehicle is this: first one cultivates the worldly path and becomes free of craving; next one cultivates the wholesome roots conducive to liberation; and last the wholesome roots conducive to penetration.

The realization of the pratyekabuddha vehicle is, in brief, of three kinds: by having previously obtained the wholesome roots conducive to penetration; by having previously obtained genuine uncontaminated realization; and by obtaining it in due sequence. Of these, one who realizes by the first two is called a solitary who is not alone; one who realizes by the last is called an awakened one like the rhinoceros horn.

The realization of the Great Vehicle: the realization of great compassion, of generating the aspiration, of the perfections, of the means of attraction, of the grounds, of reaching through to suchness within the five measureless attentions, of inconceivable majesty, and of the qualities a buddha holds in common with none. All of these should be understood as distinguished above.

教導理趣者，謂略有三處所攝：一藏所攝、二摩怛履迦所攝、三彼俱所攝。藏所攝者，謂聲聞乘藏及大乘藏。摩怛履迦所攝者，謂十七本地及四種攝。彼俱所攝者，謂略有十種，如前分別義中十種義應知。此為攝前一切藏所攝，及摩怛履迦所攝，故名為總略摩怛履迦。

The approach of guidance is comprised, in brief, in three places: what is comprised in the baskets; what is comprised in the mātṛkās; and what is comprised in both. Comprised in the baskets: the śrāvaka-vehicle canon and the Great Vehicle canon. Comprised in the mātṛkās: the seventeen grounds and the four compilations. Comprised in both: in brief, ten kinds — the ten meanings distinguished above. Because this subsumes everything comprised in the baskets and everything comprised in the mātṛkās, it is called the summary mātṛkā.

Reading note

The classification of pratyekabuddhas by how they got there resolves a puzzle in the term itself. If a pratyekabuddha awakens without a teacher, how do some of them come in groups? The answer given is that those who had already obtained the wholesome roots conducive to penetration, or genuine realization, in a previous life are 非獨勝覺 — solitary yet not alone, since their preparation was received before. Only the one who does the whole thing in sequence within a single life is 犀角喻, like the rhinoceros horn, the old Indian image for what has exactly one of something.

The account of the approach of guidance identifies the mātṛkās with 十七本地及四種攝 — the seventeen grounds and the four compilations, which is the structure of the Treatise on the Stages of Yogic Practice itself. Asaṅga is naming his own source as one of the two great divisions of what guides a reader, alongside the canon proper, and then offering the ten meanings as the summary that subsumes both. The claim being made for the present treatise is not modest.

11. Twelve Kinds of Teaching, and What the Buddha Would Not Answer

The approach of guidance continues with a twelvefold typology of teachings — and the last of the twelve, the questions the Buddha set aside, receives the fullest treatment, with four reasons and then four more.

Chinese Source

復有十二種教。一事教，謂宣說各別色等、眼等一切法教。二想差別教，謂宣說蘊、界、處、緣起、是處非處、諸根、諸諦、念住、有色無色、有見無見、有對無對等，如是廣說無量佛薄伽梵想差別教。三自宗觀察教，謂契經、應頌、記別等教，依攝釋中之所顯示。四他宗觀察教，謂依七種因明摧伏他論成立自論教。七種因明者，謂論體、論處所等，後當分別。五不了義教，謂契經、應頌、記別等中，薄伽梵略摽其義未廣分別，應更開示教。六了義教，謂翻前應知。七俗諦教，謂諸所有言路顯示，彼一切皆名俗諦。又依名想言說增上所起相名分別，亦是俗諦。八勝義諦教，謂四聖諦教，及真如實際法性教。九隱密教，謂多分聲聞藏教。十顯了教，謂多分大乘藏教。十一可記事教，如四種法嗢拕南教，謂一切行無常乃至涅槃寂靜，如是等教。

Ru-Yi Translation

There are further twelve kinds of teaching. First, teaching by matter: setting forth all things individually — form and the rest, eye and the rest. Second, teaching by difference of concept: setting forth the aggregates, elements, sense fields, dependent arising, what is possible and impossible, the faculties, the truths, the establishments of mindfulness, having form and formless, visible and invisible, resistant and unresistant, and so on at length — the Blessed One's measureless teachings by difference of concept. Third, teaching that examines one's own school: the teachings of discourse, mingled verse, prediction, and the rest, as displayed in the summaries and commentaries. Fourth, teaching that examines other schools: the teaching that, relying on the seven topics of logic, overthrows others' doctrines and establishes one's own; the seven topics of logic being the substance of a debate, the setting of a debate, and the rest, to be distinguished later. Fifth, teaching of non-explicit meaning: where in discourse, mingled verse, prediction, and the rest, the Blessed One marks out the sense only briefly without distinguishing it at length, so that it requires further disclosure. Sixth, teaching of explicit meaning: understood as the reverse.

Seventh, teaching of conventional truth: whatever is displayed by any avenue of speech is all called conventional truth; and the marks, names, and discriminations that arise through the dominance of name, conception, and speech are also conventional truth. Eighth, teaching of ultimate truth: the teaching of the four noble truths, and the teaching of suchness, the true limit, and the nature of things. Ninth, hidden teaching: for the most part, the teachings of the śrāvaka canon. Tenth, plain teaching: for the most part, the teachings of the Great Vehicle canon. Eleventh, teaching on what can be declared: as in the teaching of the four summary marks of the Dharma — that all formations are impermanent, and so on down to: nirvāṇa is peace.

十二不可記事教，謂有問言：世間為常、為無常耶？如來爾時默然不記，但告彼言：我說此事不可記別。乃至問言：如來滅後為非有非無耶？如來爾時默然不記，但告彼言：我說此事不可記別。

此中四因緣故宣說不可記事應知。一無體性故不可記別，如有問言：我與諸蘊為異不異、為常無常？如是等。二能引無義利故不可記別，如《升攝波葉經》說：有無量法，我已證覺而不宣說。何以故？彼法能引無義利故。三甚深故不可記別，謂有問言：我為有為無耶？此不可記別。何以故？若如來記別我有者，彼人或執蘊中有我、或執離蘊有我；若記別我無者，彼人或謗世俗言說我亦是無。乃至有問：如來滅後為有、為無、亦有亦無、非有非無等。由甚深故皆不記別。四彼相法爾故不可記別，謂諸法真如與彼諸法若一若異不可記別，由彼如相法爾不可安立若異性若不異性故。

復有四種因緣如來宣說不可記別事應知，一由此事外道所說故、二不如理故、三不引義利故、四唯能發起諍論纏故。有二因緣不引義利應知，一遠離因果思惟故、二遠離雜染清淨思惟故。

Twelfth, teaching on what cannot be declared: someone asks, "Is the world permanent, or impermanent?" and the Tathāgata is then silent and does not declare, but tells him only: I say that this matter cannot be declared. And so on down to: "After the Tathāgata's passing, is he neither existent nor non-existent?" — and again he is silent and does not declare, but says only: I say that this matter cannot be declared.

Four reasons should be understood for teaching that a matter cannot be declared. First, because it has no substance. As when someone asks: is the self the same as the aggregates or different, permanent or impermanent? And the like. Second, because it would lead to no benefit. As the Siṃśapā Leaves Sūtra says: there are measureless things that I have realized and awakened to, and have not proclaimed. Why? Because those things would lead to no benefit. Third, because it is profound. As when someone asks: does a self exist or not? This cannot be declared. Why? If the Tathāgata declared that a self exists, that person would grasp either a self within the aggregates or a self apart from them; and if he declared that a self does not exist, that person might deny even the conventional speech of "I." And so with the questions whether after the Tathāgata's passing he exists, does not exist, both, or neither — by reason of profundity none of them is declared. Fourth, because such is the nature of the mark itself. Whether the suchness of things is the same as those things or different cannot be declared, since it is simply the nature of that mark of suchness that neither difference nor non-difference can be established of it.

There are four further reasons why the Tathāgata declares a matter undeclarable: because such a matter is what the outsiders speak of; because it is not in accord with reason; because it leads to no benefit; and because it can only give rise to the entanglement of disputation. And two reasons should be understood for why it leads to no benefit: because it is remote from any thinking about cause and effect, and because it is remote from any thinking about defilement and purity.

Reading note

The third reason for silence is the most illuminating, because it explains the Buddha's refusal without making it a refusal to know. The question "does a self exist?" is not left unanswered because the answer is unknown but because either answer misleads. Say yes, and the questioner posits a self inside or outside the aggregates. Say no, and the questioner may go on to deny even the ordinary use of the word "I" — 謗世俗言說, disparaging conventional speech. Silence here is not evasion; it is the recognition that a question can be so framed that no true sentence answers it.

The ninth and tenth items in the typology deserve a note for their candor. The śrāvaka canon is classified as, for the most part, hidden teaching, and the Mahāyāna canon as, for the most part, plain. This is a sectarian claim, and Asaṅga makes it without argument here — though the two qualifiers 多分, "for the most part," in both entries keep it from being absolute. Read together with the earlier assignment of non-explicit meaning to mingled verse, this is the treatise setting out its hermeneutic position in the middle of what looks like a neutral catalogue.

12. The Last Three Approaches

Chinese Source

離二邊理趣者，略有六種應知，一遠離於不實有增益邊、二遠離於真實有損減邊、三遠離執常邊、四遠離執斷邊、五遠離受用欲樂邊、六遠離受用自苦邊。

不可思議理趣者，略有六種不可思議事：一我不可思議、二有情不可思議、三世間不可思議、四一切有情業報不可思議、五證靜慮者及靜慮境界不可思議、六諸佛及諸佛境界不可思議。

意樂理趣者，略有十六種意樂：一開示意樂、二離欲意樂、三勸導意樂、四獎勵意樂、五讚悅意樂、六令入意樂、七除疑意樂、八成熟意樂、九安定意樂、十解脫意樂、十一依別義意樂、十二發證行者無過歡喜意樂、十三令聞行者於說法師起尊重意樂、十四法眼流布意樂、十五善增廣意樂、十六摧壞一切相意樂。

依義者，謂不了義及了義。

Ru-Yi Translation

The approach that departs from the two extremes is, in brief, of six kinds: keeping far from the extreme of superimposing existence on what does not really exist; from the extreme of denying what really does exist; from the extreme of grasping permanence; from the extreme of grasping annihilation; from the extreme of enjoying sensual pleasure; and from the extreme of enjoying self-mortification.

The approach of the inconceivable: in brief, six inconceivable matters — the self is inconceivable; beings are inconceivable; the world is inconceivable; the karmic recompense of all beings is inconceivable; those who attain the absorptions and the domain of the absorptions are inconceivable; and the buddhas and the domain of the buddhas are inconceivable.

The approach of intent: in brief, sixteen intents — the intent to disclose; to bring about dispassion; to exhort; to encourage; to praise and gladden; to bring one in; to remove doubt; to ripen; to settle; to liberate; the intent that rests on a distinct sense; the intent to give faultless joy to one who has aroused realization in practice; the intent to make a practitioner who hears give rise to reverence toward the teacher of the Dharma; the intent that the eye of Dharma should spread; the intent that the wholesome should increase and grow broad; and the intent to demolish all signs.

By its meaning: non-explicit meaning, and explicit meaning.

Reading note

The six extremes pair off into three couples, and the first couple is the one that distinguishes this school. Superimposition and denial — asserting what is not there, denying what is — are placed ahead of permanence and annihilation, and ahead of indulgence and mortification. The middle way named first is not the ethical one but the ontological one: neither adding nor subtracting. The famous middle between luxury and asceticism comes sixth on the list.

The sixteen intents are a working account of why a teacher says any given thing, and the range is wide: to disclose, to exhort, to gladden, to remove doubt, to settle, to liberate. Three of them are about the social fabric of teaching rather than its content — giving joy to someone who has genuinely realized something, causing a hearer to respect the teacher, and spreading the eye of Dharma. And the last is of a different order altogether: 摧壞一切相, the intent to demolish all signs. Some things are said only to destroy the frame in which the question arose.

13. Refuge: Why Only the Three Jewels

The treatise now argues, at more length than it gives to almost anything else, that the gods cannot be a refuge. The case is made twice over — first by two reasons, then by five.

Chinese Source

歸三種分別者，一成就、二建立、三差別。成就者，唯佛法僧是真歸依，非餘天等。何以故？由二因故，一無所能為故、二不現見故。

云何無所能為？謂諸天神不能為諸眾生作利益事，此諸天神或無能故、或待敬事故、或不忍疲苦故、或無慈悲故、或有障礙故。如是一切非真歸處，謂無能故、墮偏黨故、避自疲苦無自在故、無哀愍故、德微劣故。

云何不現見？謂諸天神非現證見。世間未見不現見主能為所依，除可依信現攝受他，餘現見依所不見故。問：夢中見故應是歸依？答：欲想所見或實不實。又復覺時何不現見？雖於夢中少見實相，此亦欲想所作。又眾緣現前令處夢者少有所見，此亦多虛。

Ru-Yi Translation

Refuge is distinguished three ways: by its being accomplished, by its establishment, and by its differences.

Its being accomplished: only the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha are a true refuge, and not the gods or others. Why? For two reasons: because they can do nothing, and because they are not seen.

How can they do nothing? The gods cannot perform what benefits beings — whether because they lack the capacity, or because they wait to be honored with rites, or because they will not endure the fatigue, or because they lack compassion, or because they are themselves obstructed. All such are not a true place of refuge: they lack capacity; they fall into partiality; they avoid their own fatigue and are not free; they have no pity; and their virtue is slight and inferior.

How are they not seen? The gods are not directly witnessed. The world has never seen an unseen master serve as a support — apart from what can be relied on by faith when someone present takes another under his care, every support one relies on is one that is seen. Question: since they are seen in dreams, should they not be a refuge? Answer: what is seen by the mind's wishing may or may not be real. And besides, why are they not seen while awake? Though in a dream one may see a little of the real, that too is made by the mind's wishing; and though various conditions coming together may let a dreamer see a little, most of that is false.

復次由五種因，諸天神等非歸依處。何等為五？一由相故、二由體故、三由業故、四由法爾故、五由因果故。

由相故者，謂諸天神世不現見，無談論故、容色奮發有怖畏故、染習放逸有貪愛故、捨他利益無悲愍故、不能解了作與不作不達實義故，不可歸依。云何相故佛可歸依？謂世間現見，有談論故、容色和靜無怖畏故、遠離放逸無貪愛故、不捨利他有大悲故、善能解了作與不作通達實義故。復由五相佛可歸依。何等為五？為利有情證大菩提故、現處大眾開正法眼故、怨親有情平等利益故、於諸家室攝受捨離貪著諸根寂靜故、善除一切眾生疑網故。

由體故者，謂由如來永斷諸漏，自既調御亦調御他，故可歸依。諸天神等具諸漏故，尚不自調御，況調御他，故非歸處。

由業故者，謂如來安住廣大無垢靜慮等業，又復能為利眾生業，故可歸依。諸天神等安住穢下受用欲業，又有殺害諸眾生業，故非歸處。

Further, by five reasons the gods are not a place of refuge: by their marks, by their substance, by their activity, by the nature of things, and by cause and effect.

By their marks: the gods are not seen in the world, for there is no conversing with them; their countenance is roused and fearsome; they are habituated to defilement and heedlessness and have craving; they abandon others' benefit and have no pity; and they cannot make out what is to be done and not done, not having penetrated the real meaning. Therefore they cannot be a refuge. And how, by marks, can the Buddha be a refuge? Because he is seen in the world, for there is conversing with him; his countenance is harmonious and still and not fearsome; he is far from heedlessness and without craving; he does not abandon the benefit of others and has great compassion; and he can well make out what is to be done and not done, having penetrated the real meaning. And by five further marks the Buddha can be a refuge: because he realized great enlightenment for the benefit of beings; because he appears amid great assemblies and opens the eye of the true Dharma; because he benefits equally those hostile and those dear; because, with respect to households, whether taking them up or leaving them, his faculties are still; and because he can well remove the net of doubt in all beings.

By their substance: because the Tathāgata has permanently cut off the contaminants and, having tamed himself, also tames others, he can be a refuge. The gods, being possessed of the contaminants, do not even tame themselves, still less others; therefore they are not a place of refuge.

By their activity: because the Tathāgata abides in the vast and stainless activity of the absorptions and the rest, and can moreover act for the benefit of beings, he can be a refuge. The gods abide in the base activity of enjoying desire, and moreover have the activity of killing beings; therefore they are not a place of refuge.

Reading note

The first argument — that the gods "can do nothing" — is a disjunction rather than a flat denial, and it is more devastating for that. Either they lack the power, or they demand to be propitiated first, or they will not put themselves out, or they do not care, or they are themselves hindered. Any one of these disqualifies. The second item is the sharpest: a being that must be honored with rites before it will help is described as 墮偏黨, fallen into partiality — it helps those who court it, which is precisely not what a refuge does.

The point about visibility rests on a general observation about how reliance works: apart from cases where one trusts a present person who takes another under his care, everything one actually relies on is something one can see. The rejoinder about dreams is handled briskly and without mockery — what is seen in a dream is made by the mind's wishing, and the obvious question is asked: why then not while awake? The Buddha's counter-qualification under "marks" begins with the same criterion turned around: 世間現見，有談論故, he is seen in the world, for there is conversing with him. The first credential of the Buddha as refuge is that one could talk to him.

14. Merit Cannot Be Purchased

The last two of the five reasons, and the second is a piece of close argument — a dilemma put to anyone who serves a god in hope of reward.

Chinese Source

由法爾故者，謂一切世間及出世間功德勝利，皆依自己功用所得。若離自己功用，雖於天神起深敬信，亦不能證；設於天神不生敬信，但自用功必能證得，是故天神非歸依處。

由因果故者，今問事天神者：天神體性，為由天業感得？為由供養天得？為無因得？若天業得者，即應歸業，非天。若無因得者，應歸無因，非天。若供養天神得者，為唯因供養感天神體、為唯因天神為因二種。若唯因供養者，即徒事天神，隨處供養皆應能感天報。若唯因天神者，即徒設供養；雖不供養，但由天神應得天報。若俱因二種者，但設供養，天神攝受，諸所祈願悉應果遂。又於七種所祈願事不定果遂，是故不然。一於供養緣攝受、二於信解緣攝受、三於信解彼者發起信解能感最勝天神自體、四於能感最勝所受富樂、五於摧壞阿素洛等怨敵、六於出生、七於終沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

By the nature of things: all the good qualities and benefits of the world and beyond it are obtained in dependence on one's own exertion. Apart from one's own exertion, though one may generate deep reverence and faith toward a god, one still cannot realize them; and though one generate no reverence or faith toward a god, if one exerts oneself one will assuredly realize them. Therefore the gods are not a place of refuge.

By cause and effect: now let us ask one who serves the gods. Is the very substance of a god brought about by heavenly karma, obtained by making offerings to the god, or obtained without a cause? If it is obtained by heavenly karma, then one should take refuge in karma and not in the god. If it is obtained without a cause, then one should take refuge in causelessness and not in the god. If it is obtained by making offerings to the god, then is it caused by the offering alone, by the god alone, or by both together?

If by the offering alone, then serving the god is useless, and offerings made anywhere at all should bring about a heavenly recompense. If by the god alone, then making offerings is useless, and even without offerings one should obtain a heavenly recompense simply by the god. If by both together, then merely by making offerings and being taken up by the god, everything one prayed for ought to come to pass. But in seven kinds of prayed-for matter the result is not settled, and therefore this is not so: being taken up on the strength of one's offering; being taken up on the strength of one's faith; the arousing of faith in one who has faith in the god so as to bring about the most excellent divine body; the most excellent wealth and pleasure that one might receive; the crushing of enemies such as the asuras; birth; and death.

Reading note

The dilemma is constructed with some care and is worth following as a piece of reasoning. The question is not whether gods exist — the treatise assumes they do — but what accounts for a god's status, and therefore what one would actually be relying on in relying on a god. Every answer redirects the refuge elsewhere: if karma made the god, take refuge in karma; if nothing made it, take refuge in nothing. The remaining branch, that offerings make it, then splits three ways, and each branch produces an absurdity: offerings alone would make the god superfluous, the god alone would make offerings superfluous, and both together would make prayer infallible.

The refutation of the last branch is empirical rather than logical, and that is its strength. Prayer is not reliably answered — and the seven items name exactly the things people pray for: to have their offering accepted, to have their faith accepted, to be reborn among the gods, for wealth and pleasure, for victory over enemies, for a birth, and about a death. The argument does not sneer at any of these. It observes that the results are 不定, not settled, and lets that do the work.

The fourth reason, "by the nature of things," is the one that generalizes beyond gods. Good qualities are obtained by one's own exertion; devotion without exertion does not produce them, and exertion without devotion does. This is stated of worldly benefits as well as transcendent ones, and it is the ground on which the whole argument stands.

15. Establishing the Refuges, and What Follows from Taking Them

Chinese Source

建立者，問：有幾種歸趣？答：三種歸趣，謂佛、法、僧。問：何因唯有三種歸趣？答：由四因故，唯有如來是可歸趣，謂善自調故、善解一切種調伏方便故、以財供養不悅意故、以行供養悅可意故。由具此德，彼所說法及弟子眾亦可歸依。

問：齊何當名能歸趣耶？答：具四因故，名能歸趣，一善知有德故、二善知差別故、三自誓受故、四更不餘歸趣故。

問：歸趣正行云何？答：有四種歸趣正行應知，一親近善人、二聽聞正法、三如理作意、四法隨法行。復有四種正行應知，一善攝諸根令不掉動、二受正學處、三悲愍眾生、四時時如法供養三寶。

問：歸趣三寶有何利益？答：有四種利益，一得廣大功德、二得廣大歡喜、三得勝等持、四得善清淨。復有四種利益，一大護具足；二一切邪解障礙漸得微薄遍盡滅沒；三得入聰慧正至善人眾中，所謂大師同梵行眾；四為信聖教諸天之所愛樂，彼諸天等若見有受三歸趣者，生大歡喜展轉相告：我等往昔皆由成就三歸趣故，從彼命終來生此間。是善男子等，今亦成就此三歸趣多住不捨，不久當來為我等伴。

Ru-Yi Translation

Establishment. Question: how many refuges are there? Answer: three — the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. Question: why are there only three? Answer: for four reasons only the Tathāgata can be resorted to — because he has well tamed himself; because he well understands every kind of means of taming; because he takes no pleasure in offerings of goods; and because offerings of practice are pleasing to him. Because he possesses these qualities, the Dharma he taught and the community of his disciples can also be a refuge.

Question: at what point is one called one who takes refuge? Answer: one is so called by four causes — because one well knows that there is virtue there; because one well knows the differences among the three; because one undertakes it by one's own vow; and because one resorts to nothing else.

Question: what is the right practice of taking refuge? Answer: four — drawing near to good people; hearing the true Dharma; attending in accordance with principle; and practicing the Dharma in accordance with the Dharma. And four further right practices: gathering the faculties well so that they are not agitated; undertaking the training rules; having compassion for beings; and from time to time making offerings to the Three Jewels as the Dharma requires.

Question: what benefit is there in taking refuge in the Three Jewels? Answer: four benefits — obtaining vast merit; obtaining vast gladness; obtaining excellent concentration; and obtaining good purity. And four further benefits: first, one is fully protected; second, all the obstructions of wrong understanding gradually grow slight and are wholly extinguished; third, one enters among the wise, the rightly arrived, and the good — that is, the great teacher and the community of fellow practitioners of the holy life; fourth, one is delighted in by the gods who have faith in the sacred teaching. For when those gods see someone taking the three refuges, they rejoice greatly and tell one another: it was by accomplishing the three refuges in the past that we all came to be born here when we died there. And this good son too now accomplishes these three refuges and abides in them without abandoning them; before long he will come to be our companion.

Reading note

Two of the four qualifications of the Buddha as refuge are about offerings, and they answer the objection raised against the gods a moment earlier. The gods were disqualified in part because they 待敬事, wait to be honored with rites. The Buddha, by contrast, 以財供養不悅意, takes no pleasure in offerings of goods, and is pleased only by offerings of practice. He cannot be propitiated, which is exactly what makes him reliable.

The four causes by which one becomes someone who has taken refuge are worth attention, because none of them is a ceremony. One must know there is virtue there, know how the three differ from each other, undertake it by one's own vow, and resort to nothing else. The third makes refuge an act rather than a status conferred; the fourth is exclusivity, and it is the practical consequence of the whole preceding argument against the gods.

The passage about the gods rejoicing is a striking way to end an argument that spent several pages demonstrating that gods cannot help anyone. They cannot serve as refuge — and they are, on this account, present, watching, and pleased, because they got where they are the same way. The gods are not denied. They are relocated: fellow travelers rather than protectors.

16. How the Three Jewels Differ

Chinese Source

差別者，謂由六種因故，三寶差別應知。一由相故、二由業故、三由信解故、四由行故、五由隨念故、六由生福故。

由相故差別者，自證覺相是佛寶，證覺果相是法寶，由隨他教正修行相是僧寶。由業故差別者，轉正說業是佛寶，煩惱苦斷所緣境業是法寶，增勤勇業是僧寶。由信解故差別者，謂於佛寶應親近敬事，於正法寶應信敬作證，於諸僧寶應同法共住敬信親近。由行故差別者，謂於佛寶應起延請迎接承事供養行，於正法寶應起如理方便修習行，於諸僧寶應起互共受用財法行。由隨念故差別者，於三寶所應各起別行隨念。如經中說：此薄伽梵、如來、應、正等覺，乃至廣說。由生福故差別者，謂於佛寶依一有情生最勝福，於正法寶依增上法生最勝福，於諸僧寶依多有情生最勝福。

Ru-Yi Translation

Differences. By six causes the differences among the Three Jewels should be understood: by mark, by activity, by confident understanding, by practice, by recollection, and by the merit produced.

By mark: the mark of self-realized awakening is the Buddha Jewel; the mark of the fruit of that awakening is the Dharma Jewel; the mark of rightly practicing according to another's teaching is the Saṅgha Jewel. By activity: the activity of turning the true teaching is the Buddha Jewel; the activity of being the object by which the suffering of affliction is cut off is the Dharma Jewel; the activity of increasing diligence and courage is the Saṅgha Jewel. By confident understanding: toward the Buddha Jewel one should draw near and serve with reverence; toward the Dharma Jewel one should have faith and reverence and realize it; toward the Saṅgha Jewel one should dwell together in the same Dharma, revering, trusting, and drawing near. By practice: toward the Buddha Jewel one should undertake the practice of inviting, welcoming, attending upon, and making offerings; toward the Dharma Jewel, the practice of cultivating by means in accordance with principle; toward the Saṅgha Jewel, the practice of sharing goods and Dharma with one another. By recollection: toward each of the Three Jewels one should undertake its own distinct recollection, as the scripture says: "This Blessed One is the Tathāgata, the Worthy One, the Perfectly Enlightened One," and so on at length. By the merit produced: with respect to the Buddha Jewel, the most excellent merit is produced in dependence on a single being; with respect to the Dharma Jewel, in dependence on a superior teaching; with respect to the Saṅgha Jewel, in dependence on many beings.

Reading note

The distinction by mark is exact and generative: the Buddha is awakening self-realized; the Dharma is the fruit of that awakening; the Saṅgha is right practice according to another's teaching. What separates the first from the third is not attainment but derivation — the Buddha found it, the Saṅgha received it. And the practices assigned to each follow from that. One invites and attends upon the Buddha; one cultivates the Dharma; and with the Saṅgha one does something reciprocal — 互共受用財法, sharing goods and Dharma with one another. Only the third relation runs in both directions.

The last distinction is the most quietly interesting: merit arises toward the Buddha in dependence on a single being, and toward the Saṅgha in dependence on many. It is a note about how the field of merit is constituted in each case, and it explains why offerings to the community are treated differently in the Vinaya from offerings to an individual, however exalted.
