How to Explain a Scripture

The last topic of the verse that opened fascicle 8 is interpretation, and it turns out to be a complete manual for the person who has to stand up and explain a sūtra. A scripture has two parts, the letter and the meaning, and each is taken apart — the letter into six headings that run from kinds of name down to the twenty-seven kinds of person who might be asking, and the meaning into ten. Then the method itself: five steps for expounding any scripture, five kinds of objection with the manner of answering each, and the Buddha's four ways of responding to a question, one of which is silence. It closes with the ten things a teacher of the Dharma must have — the last being that when reviled he does not answer back — and with eight kinds of speech matched to five kinds of audience.

1. The Two Parts of a Scripture, and Twelve Kinds of Name

Chinese Source

釋應知多種者，嗢柁南曰：

體釋文義法，　起義難次師，

說眾聽讚佛，　略廣學勝利。

Ru-Yi Translation

Interpretation is to be known as of many kinds. Summary verse:

Substance, interpretation, letter, meaning, teaching;
occasion, meaning, objection, sequence, the teacher;
speaking, the audience, listening, praising the Buddha;
brief and extensive, and the benefits of training.

Chinese Source

論曰：體者，諸經體性略有二種，謂文及義。當知文是所依，義是能依。如是二種，總名所知境界。

釋者，略有五種應知：一法、二等起、三義、四釋難、五次第。

文者，略有六種：一名身、二句身、三字身、四語、五相、六機請。

名身者，謂共了增語。此復略說有十二種：一假名、二實名、三總名、四別名、五隨義名、六戲論名、七易名、八難名、九顯名、十隱名、十一略名、十二廣名。

假名者，謂於內諸蘊立我有情命者等名，於外諸色立瓶衣車乘等名。實名者，謂於眼等色等立根義等名。總名者，謂有情色受大種等名。別名者，謂佛友德友青黃等名。隨義名者，謂質礙故名色、領納故名受、能照能燒故名為日，如是等名。戲論名者，如呼貧名富如是等類，不觀於義施設彼名。易名者，謂共所知想。難名者，謂翻於彼。顯名者，謂其義易曉。隱名者，謂其義難曉，如達羅弭荼明呪。略名者，謂一字名。廣名者，謂多字名。

Ru-Yi Translation

The exposition: substance — the substance of any scripture is, in brief, of two kinds: the letter and the meaning. Know that the letter is what is depended on and the meaning is what depends on it. These two together are called the objective domain of what is to be known.

Interpretation is of five kinds: the teaching; the occasion of its arising; the meaning; answering objections; and sequence.

The letter is of six kinds: word-groups; phrase-groups; syllable-groups; speech; topic; and the questioner's request.

Word-groups: designations commonly understood. These are, in brief, of twelve kinds: nominal names; real names; general names; particular names; names that follow the meaning; names that are mere proliferation; easy names; difficult names; plain names; obscure names; brief names; and extended names.

Nominal names: applying to the inner aggregates the names "self," "being," "living one," and the like; and applying to outer forms the names "jar," "cloth," "carriage," and the like. Real names: applying to the eye and the rest, and to form and the rest, the names "faculty," "object," and the like. General names: names such as "being," "form," "feeling," "great element." Particular names: names such as Buddhamitra, Guṇamitra, blue, yellow. Names that follow the meaning: called "form" because it obstructs; called "feeling" because it registers; called "the sun" because it illumines and burns — names of that kind. Names that are mere proliferation: as when a poor man is called rich, and the like — the name is applied without regard to the meaning. Easy names: those whose notion is commonly known. Difficult names: the reverse. Plain names: those whose meaning is easily understood. Obscure names: those whose meaning is hard to understand, like a Draviḍian spell. Brief names: names of one syllable. Extended names: names of many syllables.

Reading note

The first distinction is the one everything rests on: the letter is 所依, what is depended on, and the meaning is 能依, what depends on it. Meaning does not float free of words. But the relation is asymmetric — one supports and the other is supported — which is why the fourth reliance in fascicle 2 could tell a reader to rely on the meaning and not the letter without thereby licensing anyone to ignore what a text says.

Two of the twelve kinds of name carry doctrinal weight. Nominal names are the terms applied to aggregates — "self," "being" — and to assemblages of form — "jar," "cloth." Real names are the technical terms of the analysis itself: faculty, object. This is exactly the distinction that fascicle 5 drew when it said that a being is nominally designated upon things, and things upon marks. And the sixth kind, names that are mere proliferation, is defined with unusual bite: a name applied 不觀於義, without regard to the meaning, as when a poor man is addressed as rich.

2. Phrases, Syllables, and Why the Three Are Needed

Chinese Source

句身者，謂依名字釋義滿足。此復六種：一不滿句、二滿句、三所成句、四能成句、五序句、六釋句。

不滿句者，謂文不究竟、義不究竟，更加餘句方得成滿。如說諸惡者莫作，諸善者奉行，善調伏自心，是諸佛聖教。若唯言諸惡則於文未足，若復言諸惡者又於義未足，若具言諸惡者莫作則二俱滿足，是則名為第二滿句。所成句者，所謂前句待後句成。如說諸行無常，有起盡法，生必滅故，彼寂為樂。此中諸行無常是所成句，由有起盡法句之所成立。能成句者，謂第二句以能成立第一句故。序句者，如言善人。釋句者，如言謂趣正丈夫。

字身者，謂若究竟若不究竟名之與句，二種所依四十九字。此中欲為名首名、為句首句必有名，若唯一字則不成句。又若有字名所不攝，則唯字無名。

問：何因緣故，建立名等三種身耶？答：為領增語觸所生受故。問：名者何義？答：目種種事令世共知故，又能令意作種種相故，又由語言所傳述故，謂之為名。問：句者何義？答：攝受於名，究竟顯了不現見義，故名為句。問：文者何義？答：顯發名句，故謂之文。

Ru-Yi Translation

Phrase-groups: the complete expression of a meaning in dependence on names and syllables. These are of six kinds: incomplete phrases; complete phrases; phrases that are established; phrases that establish; introductory phrases; and explanatory phrases.

Incomplete phrases: where neither the letter nor the meaning is finished, and further phrases must be added to complete it. As it is said: "Do no evil; practice all good; well tame your own mind — this is the teaching of the buddhas." If one says only "evil," the letter is not yet sufficient; if one says "all evils," the meaning is not yet sufficient; if one says in full "do no evil," then both are sufficient — and that is the second kind, a complete phrase. Phrases that are established: an earlier phrase awaits a later one to establish it. As it is said: "All formations are impermanent; they are things that arise and perish; since what arises must cease, their stilling is bliss." Here "all formations are impermanent" is the phrase established, being established by the phrase "they are things that arise and perish." Phrases that establish: the second phrase, because it establishes the first. Introductory phrases: as when one says "a good person." Explanatory phrases: as when one says "that is, one who proceeds as a true man."

Syllable-groups: the forty-nine letters on which both names and phrases depend, whether finished or unfinished. Here a syllable is the head of a name and a name is the head of a phrase; a phrase must have a name, and a single syllable does not make a phrase. And if there are syllables not comprised in a name, there are syllables only and no name.

Question: for what reason are the three groups — names and the rest — established? Answer: in order to register the feeling produced by the contact of designation. Question: what does "name" mean? Answer: it points out the various matters so that the world may commonly know them; it also causes the mind to form various images; and it is what is conveyed by speech. Question: what does "phrase" mean? Answer: it takes up names and completely displays a meaning not presently visible; hence it is called a phrase. Question: what does "syllable" mean? Answer: it brings names and phrases to light; hence it is called a syllable.

Reading note

The worked example is the most famous verse in Buddhism, and the analysis of it is a small lesson in reading. "Do no evil" is complete; "evil," or even "all evils," is not — and the treatise distinguishes the two failures, one of grammar and one of sense. Then the second example separates a claim from its ground: "all formations are impermanent" is established by "they are things that arise and perish." Both halves look like assertions; only one is doing the proving. Sorting a text into phrases that establish and phrases established is the exegete's first task.

The answer to why names, phrases, and syllables are needed at all is unexpectedly psychological rather than grammatical: 為領增語觸所生受, in order to register the feeling produced by the contact of designation. Fascicle 5 listed 增語觸, contact by designation, among the sixteen contacts from which feeling arises. Language is set up here as a species of sense contact — something that reaches us and produces feeling — and its grammar is analyzed for that reason.

3. Designation, and the Eight Marks of Speech

Chinese Source

如薄伽梵說：增語、增語路，如是廣說。增語者，謂一切眾類共所立名增語。路者，謂眾類之欲能起彼故。詞者，謂彼相應語及與各別彼彼方言，彼所依故，名為彼路。施設者，謂分析一法建立多種彼所依處，名為彼路。欲即是詞，無有別欲，此詞即是增語施設之路。彼名身等略有六種依處：一法、二義、三補特伽羅、四時、五數、六處所。彼廣分別，如〈聞所成地〉。

語者，當知略有八分，謂上首美妙等由彼語言具足相應，乃至常委分資糧等德故能說正法。上首語者，趣涅槃宮為先首故。美妙語者，清美音故。顯了語者，文辭善故。易解語者，巧辯說故。樂聞語者，引法義故。無依語者，不依希望他信己故。不逆語者，言知量故。無邊語者，善巧多故。是八種語當知略具三德：一趣處德，謂初一種；二自體德，謂次二種；三加行德，謂所餘種。

相應者，謂名句文身次第善安立故，又依四種道理共相應故。助伴者，能成次第故。隨順者，解釋次第故。清亮者，文句顯了清淨故。有用者，善入眾心故。相稱者，如眾會故。應順者，稱法故。引義故，順時故。常委分資糧者，常修委修故名常委。彼分者，謂正見等。資糧者，是彼資糧故。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the Blessed One said: designation, and the path of designation, and so on at length. Designation: the designating names commonly established by all classes of beings. Path: because the wish of those classes gives rise to them. Word: the speech corresponding to them, and the several regional tongues; because designation rests on these, they are called its path. Setting up: analyzing one thing and establishing many; the places on which this rests are called its path. The wish just is the word, there being no separate wish; and this word is the path of designation and setting up. Word-groups and the rest have, in brief, six supports: teaching, meaning, person, time, number, and place. These are distinguished at length in the Ground of What Is Attained by Hearing.

Speech should be known to have eight parts — beginning with foremost and beautiful — such that by speech complete and consistent, down to the qualities of the provisions belonging to constant and thorough practice, one can teach the true Dharma. Foremost speech: because it takes as its head the going to the palace of nirvāṇa. Beautiful speech: because its sound is clear and pleasing. Plain speech: because its wording is good. Easily understood speech: because it is skillfully expressed. Speech one is glad to hear: because it draws forth the Dharma and its meaning. Speech that rests on nothing: because it does not rest on hoping that others will believe in oneself. Speech that does not go against: because it speaks knowing the measure. Boundless speech: because its skill is abundant. These eight kinds of speech have, in brief, three qualities: the quality of destination, which is the first; the quality of its own substance, which is the next two; and the quality of application, which is the rest.

Consistent: because word-groups, phrase-groups, and syllable-groups are well arranged in order, and because they agree with the four principles of reasoning. With助伴 support: because it accomplishes the sequence. Concordant: because it explains the sequence. Clear: because the wording and phrasing are plain and pure. Useful: because it enters the minds of the assembly well. Fitting: because it suits the assembly. Suitable: because it accords with the Dharma, because it draws forth the meaning, and because it accords with the time. The provisions belonging to constant and thorough practice: because one practices constantly and thoroughly it is called constant and thorough; its parts are right view and the rest; and the provisions are the provisions for those.

Reading note

Two of the eight marks of speech are about the speaker rather than the speech. Speech that rests on nothing is glossed as speech that does not rest on 希望他信己, hoping that others will believe in oneself — the absence of any stake in being believed. And speech that does not go against is glossed as 言知量, speaking knowing the measure: knowing when enough has been said. Between them they name the two commonest ways a teacher's speech goes wrong, and neither is a fault of content.

The account of designation contains a striking identification: 欲即是詞，無有別欲 — the wish just is the word, there being no separate wish. One might expect a wish to precede its expression and cause it. The treatise denies the gap. What we call the intention behind a word is not a separate item standing behind it. This is consistent with the treatment of mind and mental factors in fascicle 1, where attention and discrimination were shown to be inseparable rather than sequential.

4. Topic, the Questioner, and the Four Things the Letter Displays

Chinese Source

相者，謂諸蘊相應、諸界相應、諸處相應、緣起相應、處非處相應、念住相應，如是等相應言說。或聲聞說、或菩薩說、或如來說，是名為相。

機請者，謂因機請問起於言說。此復根等差別，有二十七種補特伽羅應知。此中由根差別故分成二種，謂鈍根、利根。行差別有七種，謂貪行等，如〈聲聞地〉說。品類差別有二種，謂在家、出家。願差別有三種，謂聲聞、獨覺、菩薩。可救不可救差別有二種，謂涅槃法、不涅槃法。方便差別有九種，謂已入正法、未入正法、有障礙、無障礙、已熟、未熟、具縛、不具縛、無縛。種類差別有二種，謂人及非人。

如是六文總攝為四：一所說，謂名身等及相一分。二所為，謂機請攝二十七種補特伽羅。三能說，謂語言。四說者，謂佛菩薩及與聲聞。如是一切六種相貌總顯於文，若減一種義不顯了，由能顯義是故名文。

Ru-Yi Translation

Topic: speech pertaining to the aggregates, to the elements, to the sense fields, to dependent arising, to what is possible and impossible, to the establishments of mindfulness, and the like — whether spoken by a śrāvaka, by a bodhisattva, or by a Tathāgata. This is called topic.

The questioner's request: speech arising because someone with the capacity asks a question. By differences of faculty and so on, twenty-seven kinds of person should be known. By difference of faculty, two: dull and keen. By difference of conduct, seven: the conduct of craving and the rest, as stated in the Śrāvaka Ground. By difference of class, two: householder and renunciant. By difference of vow, three: śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, bodhisattva. By difference of whether one can be rescued, two: those with the nature for nirvāṇa and those without. By difference of means, nine: having entered the true Dharma, not yet entered; having obstructions, without obstructions; ripened, not ripened; fully bound, not fully bound, unbound. By difference of kind, two: human and non-human.

These six aspects of the letter are together subsumed under four: what is spoken, namely word-groups and the rest, and part of topic; for whom, namely the twenty-seven kinds of person comprised in the questioner's request; the means of speaking, namely speech; and the speaker, namely buddhas, bodhisattvas, and śrāvakas. All six aspects together display the letter, and if even one is lacking the meaning is not made plain — and because they display the meaning, they are called the letter.

Reading note

The most consequential move in this section is including the questioner among the six components of the letter. A scripture's text, on this analysis, is not just its words; it includes who asked. The twenty-seven kinds of person are given by seven cross-cutting divisions, and the sum is a fair description of an audience: dull and keen, seven temperaments, householder and renunciant, three vehicles, those who can and cannot be rescued, nine states of readiness, human and non-human.

The fourfold summary is the practical yield. Any scripture can be read by asking four questions: what is being said, to whom, by what means, and by whom. And the treatise insists that all four are needed — 若減一種義不顯了, if even one is lacking the meaning is not made plain. A passage read without knowing who was asking is a passage whose meaning is not yet visible.

5. The Ten Meanings: Ground and Mark

Chinese Source

義者略有十種：一地義、二相義、三作意義、四處所義、五過患義、六勝利義、七所治義、八能治義、九略義、十廣義。

地義者，略有五地，謂資糧地、方便地、見地、修地、究竟地。又廣分別有十七地，謂五識身相應地、意地、有尋有伺地、無尋唯伺地、無尋無伺地、三摩呬多地、非三摩呬多地、有心地、無心地、聞所成地、思所成地、修所成地、聲聞地、獨覺地、菩薩地、有餘依地、無餘依地。

相義者，當知五種：一自相、二共相、三假立相、四因相、五果相。如是五相，若廣分別如〈思所成地〉。復有五相：一差別相、二瑜伽相、三轉異相、四染污相、五清淨相，此五種相當知如前處處分別。復有五相：一所詮相、二能詮相、三此二相屬相、四執著相、五不執著相。

所詮相者，謂相等五法，如五法藏說。能詮相者，謂即於彼法依止名等，為詮諸法自體差別所有言說，此亦能顯遍計所執自體應知。此遍計所執自體有多種名，所謂亦名遍分別所計、亦名和合所成、亦名所增益相、亦名虛妄所執、亦名言說所顯、亦名文字方便、亦名唯有音聲、亦名無有體相，如是等類差別應知。此二相屬相者，謂能詮所詮互相屬著，遍計所執自體執所依止。執著相者，謂無始流轉一切愚夫遍計所執自體執及此隨眠。不執著相者，謂已見諦者如實了知遍計所執相及彼習氣，解脫正分別知，隨其所應分別，如〈思所成地〉等。

Ru-Yi Translation

The meaning is of ten kinds: the meaning of ground; of mark; of attention; of support; of fault; of benefit; of what is to be counteracted; of what counteracts; the brief meaning; and the extensive meaning.

The meaning of ground: in brief, five grounds — the ground of the provisions, of means, of seeing, of cultivation, and the final ground. Distinguished at length, seventeen grounds: the ground associated with the five bodies of consciousness; the ground of mentation; the ground with coarse and subtle discursive thought; with subtle only; with neither; the ground of meditative equipoise; not of meditative equipoise; the ground with mind; without mind; the ground of what is attained by hearing; by reflection; by cultivation; the śrāvaka ground; the pratyekabuddha ground; the bodhisattva ground; the ground with a remainder of support; and the ground without remainder.

The meaning of mark: five kinds — the particular mark, the general mark, the nominally established mark, the causal mark, and the resultant mark. And five more: the mark of distinction, of yoga, of alteration, of defilement, and of purity. And five more: the mark of what is expressed, of what expresses, of the relation between the two, of clinging, and of not clinging.

What is expressed: the five items, mark and the rest, as stated in the collection of the five items. What expresses: with respect to those very things, resting on names and the rest, all the speech that expresses the own-natures and distinctions of things — and this too should be understood as displaying the imagined own-nature. This imagined own-nature has many names: it is called what is reckoned by pervasive discrimination; what is produced by combination; the mark of superimposition; what is falsely grasped; what is displayed by speech; a device of letters; mere sound; and having no substance or mark. The relation between the two: what expresses and what is expressed attaching to one another, which is the support of the grasping at an imagined own-nature. The mark of clinging: the grasping at an imagined own-nature by all foolish beings flowing on from beginningless time, and its latent tendency. The mark of not clinging: one who has seen the truths knows as it really is the mark of the imagined nature and its habitual traces, and is liberated, discerning rightly; distinguished as is fitting in the Ground of What Is Attained by Reflection and elsewhere.

Reading note

The eight names for the imagined nature are the most valuable thing in this section, because each is a different angle on the same error. What pervasive discrimination reckons; what is produced by combination; the mark of superimposition; what is falsely grasped; what is displayed by speech; a device of letters; mere sound; and having no substance or mark. Three of the eight — displayed by speech, a device of letters, mere sound — locate the imagined nature squarely in language, which is why a chapter on how to interpret texts is where they appear.

The seventeen grounds are the table of contents of the Treatise on the Stages of Yogic Practice, cited here as one of the ten headings under which a scripture's meaning may be sought. Together with the repeated cross-references in this fascicle to the Ground of What Is Attained by Hearing, the Ground of What Is Attained by Reflection, and the Śrāvaka Ground, this is Asaṅga openly working as a condenser: the reader who wants more is sent back to the source.

6. The Meaning of Attention, and of Support

Chinese Source

作意義者，有七種作意，謂了相等，如前說。若廣分別，如〈聲聞地〉。復有十智，謂苦智、集智、滅智、道智、法智、種類智、他心智、世俗智、盡智、無生智，若廣分別，如〈聲聞地〉。復有六識身，所謂眼識乃至意識，如前略釋。若廣分別，如〈五識身相應地〉及〈意地〉。復有九種遍知，謂欲繫見苦集所斷斷初遍知、色無色繫見苦集所斷斷二遍知、欲繫見滅所斷斷三遍知、色無色繫見滅所斷斷四遍知、欲繫見道所斷斷五遍知、色無色繫見道所斷斷六遍知、順下分結斷七遍知、色愛盡八遍知、無色愛盡九遍知。若廣分別，如〈三摩呬多地〉。又有三解脫門，謂空、無願、無相，此亦如〈三摩呬多地〉廣說。是中諸法應當觀察，幾種作意之所思惟？幾智所知？幾識所識？幾種遍知之所遍知？幾解脫門之所解脫？以如是等無量觀門應觀諸法。

處所義者，略有三種：一事依處、二時依處、三補特伽羅依處。

事依處者，復有三種：一根本事依處、二得方便事依處、三愍他事依處。根本事依處有六種：一善趣、二惡趣、三退墮、四升進、五流轉、六寂滅。得方便事依處者，有十二種，謂十二種行：一欲行、二離行、三善行、四不善行、五苦行、六非苦行、七順退分行、八順進分行、九染污行、十清淨行、十一自義行、十二他義行。愍他事依處者，有五種：一令離欲、二示現、三教導、四讚勵、五慶慰。

Ru-Yi Translation

The meaning of attention: the seven attentions, beginning with the attention that understands the mark, as stated above; distinguished at length in the Śrāvaka Ground. And the ten knowledges: of suffering, of origin, of cessation, of the path, of things, inferential, of others' minds, conventional, of exhaustion, of non-arising — distinguished at length in the Śrāvaka Ground. And the six bodies of consciousness, from eye-consciousness to mental consciousness, briefly explained above and distinguished at length in the Ground Associated with the Five Bodies of Consciousness and the Ground of Mentation. And the nine full knowledges: the first, of the abandoning of the afflictions bound to the realm of desire abandoned by seeing suffering and origin; the second, of those bound to form and formlessness so abandoned; the third and fourth, of those abandoned by seeing cessation, in the realm of desire and in form and formlessness; the fifth and sixth, of those abandoned by seeing the path, likewise; the seventh, of the abandoning of the five lower fetters; the eighth, of the exhaustion of craving for form; the ninth, of the exhaustion of craving for the formless — distinguished at length in the Ground of Meditative Equipoise. And the three doors of liberation: emptiness, wishlessness, signlessness — these too expounded at length in the Ground of Meditative Equipoise.

Here things should be examined thus: by how many kinds of attention is this contemplated? By how many knowledges known? By how many consciousnesses cognized? By how many full knowledges fully known? By how many doors of liberation liberated? By such measureless approaches of examination should things be examined.

The meaning of support: three kinds — the support of the matter, of the time, and of the person.

The support of the matter is of three kinds: the support of the root matter; the support of the matter of obtaining the means; and the support of the matter of compassion for others. The root matter is of six kinds: the good destinies; the evil destinies; falling back; advancing; flowing on; and quiescence. The matter of obtaining the means is of twelve kinds, namely twelve courses of conduct: the conduct of desire; of renunciation; wholesome; unwholesome; austerity; non-austerity; conducing to falling back; conducing to advance; defiled; pure; for one's own benefit; and for the benefit of others. The matter of compassion for others is of five kinds: causing to be free of craving; displaying; instructing; encouraging; and gladdening.

Reading note

The five questions in the middle of this section are the most usable thing in the fascicle, and they amount to a portable method. Confronted with any teaching, ask: which of the seven attentions contemplates this? Which of the ten knowledges knows it? Which consciousnesses cognize it? Which of the nine full knowledges fully knows it? Which door of liberation liberates it? The apparatus assembled over eleven fascicles is here converted into a set of questions to put to a passage.

The twelve courses of conduct are a compact map of what any scripture is likely to be about — desire and renunciation, wholesome and unwholesome, austerity and its opposite, decline and advance, defilement and purity, self and other. Six pairs, and every pair is an opposition. Note that austerity and non-austerity are treated as a pair like the others, with no assumption that either is the right one; fascicle 10 had already refuted self-mortification as one of the two extremes.

7. Rebirth, Decline, and Advance

Chinese Source

此中善趣者，謂人天。惡趣者，謂那洛迦等。退墮者，復有二種，謂不方他及方他。不方他者，所謂自然壽命退減。如壽命，當知色、力、安樂、辯等亦爾。方他者，謂族姓退減、自在增上退減，少枝屬言，不肅弊惡慧，不能證獲微妙廣大色香味觸，於諸勝妙所受用具心不憙樂。如是等類名為退墮。翻此退墮，如其所應即名昇進。流轉者，謂即此善趣、惡趣、退墮、昇進。寂滅者，謂有餘依、無餘依寂滅界。

欲行者，如十種欲所引中說。離行者，謂即於彼所受用事，知無常已厭欲出家，受持禁戒守護根門等。善行者，謂施戒修有漏善行。不善行者，謂三種惡行。苦行者，謂露形無衣，如是等乃至廣說。非苦行者，謂受用如法所得資具，棄捨樂行遠離二邊，所謂受用欲樂行邊及與受用自苦行邊，勤行中道依止於法，求衣鉢等及正受用。順退分行者，謂若行能障昇進分等。順進分行者，謂與上相違。

染污行者，如《鸚鵡經》說略有三種，謂業雜染、煩惱雜染、流轉雜染。當知此等有九根本句，謂業雜染有三句，一貪、二瞋、三癡。煩惱雜染有四句，謂四顛倒。流轉雜染有二句，謂無明及有愛。所以者何？由三不善根生起種種業雜染故，由四顛倒能發種種煩惱雜染故，由無明門引生種種諸出家者流轉雜染故，由有愛門引生種種諸在家者流轉雜染故。清淨行者，略有三學五地。此三學等，當知亦有九根本句，謂增上戒學、增上心學所攝無貪無瞋無癡，在資糧地及方便地；增上慧學所攝四無顛倒明及解脫，在見地修地及究竟地。自義行者，謂自利行，如聲聞獨覺，彼雖或時起利他行，然本期願為自利故，亦名自義。他義行者，謂利他行，如佛菩薩，究竟利益無量眾生，乃至廣說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, the good destinies are humans and gods. The evil destinies are the hells and the rest. Falling back is of two kinds: not in comparison with others, and in comparison with others. Not in comparison: the natural decline of lifespan; and as with lifespan, so with form, strength, ease, eloquence, and the rest. In comparison with others: decline of family standing; decline of authority and influence; having few dependents; being coarse, mean, and ill-witted; being unable to obtain subtle and abundant forms, smells, tastes, and touches; and taking no delight in the excellent things one might enjoy. Such things are called falling back; and the reverse, as is fitting, is called advancing. Flowing on: these very things — the good destinies, the evil destinies, falling back, and advancing. Quiescence: the realm of quiescence with a remainder of support and without remainder.

The conduct of desire: as stated under the ten kinds of desire. The conduct of renunciation: with respect to those very things one enjoys, having known them to be impermanent, one wearies of desire and goes forth, undertaking the precepts and guarding the doors of the faculties. Wholesome conduct: contaminated wholesome conduct of giving, discipline, and cultivation. Unwholesome conduct: the three kinds of evil conduct. Austerity: going naked without clothing, and so on at length. Non-austerity: using requisites obtained in accordance with the Dharma, casting off the conduct of pleasure and keeping far from the two extremes — the extreme of enjoying the pleasure of desire and the extreme of enjoying self-mortification — diligently practicing the middle way in dependence on the Dharma, seeking robes and bowl and using them rightly. Conduct conducing to falling back: conduct that obstructs the factors of advance. Conducing to advance: the reverse.

Defiled conduct: as stated in the Parrot Sūtra, of three kinds — the defilement of karma, of affliction, and of flowing on. These have nine root clauses: the defilement of karma has three, craving, hatred, and delusion; the defilement of affliction has four, the four distortions; the defilement of flowing on has two, ignorance and craving for existence. Why? Because the three unwholesome roots give rise to the various defilements of karma; because the four distortions set going the various defilements of affliction; because through the gate of ignorance the various defilements of flowing on are produced in those who have gone forth; and because through the gate of craving for existence they are produced in householders. Pure conduct: in brief, the three trainings and the five grounds. These likewise have nine root clauses: non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion comprised in the higher trainings in discipline and in mind, on the ground of the provisions and the ground of means; and the four undistorted knowledges and liberation comprised in the higher training in wisdom, on the grounds of seeing, of cultivation, and the final ground. Conduct for one's own benefit: as with śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas — though they sometimes undertake conduct benefiting others, their original vow was for their own benefit, and so it is called conduct for one's own benefit. Conduct for the benefit of others: as with buddhas and bodhisattvas, who ultimately benefit measureless beings.

Reading note

The two kinds of falling back are worth distinguishing carefully. One is simply what happens to everyone — lifespan, strength, beauty, ease, and eloquence all decline with time, and the treatise calls this 不方他, not in comparison with others. The other is entirely comparative: standing, influence, dependents, and the quality of what one can enjoy, all measured against other people. Only the second is described at length, and reading the list — few dependents, coarse and ill-witted, unable to obtain fine things, taking no pleasure in what one has — one recognizes a description of resentment rather than of any objective loss.

The analysis of defiled conduct includes one asymmetry that reveals the audience. Defilement of flowing on has two gates: ignorance, which produces it in 出家者, those who have gone forth, and craving for existence, which produces it in householders. Monastics do not typically stay in the round through craving for a next life; they stay in it through not understanding. The distinction is a small piece of pastoral observation set inside a doctrinal list.

8. Five Ways of Helping Others

Chinese Source

令離欲者，訶毀六種黑品諸行，示現過患令離愛欲。示現者，為令受學白品行故，示現四種真實道理。教導者，謂示現已得信解者，令於學處正受正行。由已於彼得自在故，而告之曰：汝等今者於如是如是事，應正作、應隨學。讚勵者，謂彼有情若於所知所行所得中心生退屈，爾時稱讚策發彼心，令於所知所行所得中堪有勢力。慶慰者，謂彼有情於法隨法勇猛正行，即應如實讚說令其欣喜。

復次令離欲、示現者，或有令離欲而不示現。如教導他令其離欲，而謂彼曰：如某所言不應作者，汝今必定不應復作。或怖彼言：汝若作者，我當解作。或復求彼：汝若是我親愛友者，必不應作。或復示現不令離欲，如處中者示現有情功德過失，而不遮彼令不造過。或有亦示現亦離欲，如示彼過令其遠離。教導者，初未受學令其受學。讚勵者，學已未進令其昇進。慶慰者，有五種勝利：一令彼於已所證心得決定、二令餘於彼所證勝德起心趣求、三令誹謗者心處中住、四令不信者心生淨信、五令已信者倍復增長。若有慶慰他人善事，當知是人造作增長能感悅意眾天生業；若命終已，彼彼所生常聞悅意美妙音聲無不悅意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Causing to be free of craving: censuring the six kinds of dark conduct and displaying their faults, so as to free them from craving. Displaying: in order that they may take up bright conduct, one displays the four principles of what is truly real. Instructing: to those who have been shown and have gained confident understanding, causing them to undertake and rightly practice the training rules; because one has attained mastery in them, one tells them: you should now do rightly, and should train in, such and such a matter. Encouraging: if a being's mind shrinks back with respect to what is to be known, practiced, or attained, one then praises and rouses his mind so that he has strength in what is to be known, practiced, and attained. Gladdening: when a being practices the Dharma in accordance with the Dharma with courage and rightness, one should praise it truthfully so as to make him rejoice.

Further, as to causing to be free of craving and displaying: there is causing to be free of craving without displaying — as when one instructs another to be free of craving and says to him: what so-and-so said should not be done, you now must certainly not do again. Or one frightens him: if you do it, I will deal with you. Or one appeals to him: if you are my dear friend, you certainly must not do it. And there is displaying without causing to be free of craving — as when an impartial person displays the merits and faults of beings without restraining them from committing faults. And there is both displaying and causing to be free of craving — as when one shows someone his fault so that he keeps away from it.

Instructing is causing one who has not yet undertaken the training to undertake it. Encouraging is causing one who has trained but not advanced to advance. Gladdening has five benefits: it makes him settled in mind about what he has realized; it makes others aspire toward the excellent qualities he has realized; it makes those who disparage him become neutral; it makes those without faith give rise to pure faith; and it makes those who already have faith increase twofold. Know that whoever gladdens another's good deeds performs and reinforces karma that brings about birth among the delightful classes of gods; and when he dies, wherever he is born he will constantly hear delightful and beautiful sounds, with nothing that is not delightful.

Reading note

The three ways of trying to stop someone doing wrong are observed with disconcerting accuracy, and none of them involves displaying anything: citing an authority — so-and-so said not to; threatening — if you do it, I will deal with you; and appealing to the relationship — if you are my friend, you must not. All three are classified as causing freedom from craving without displaying, which is to say they may work and they teach nothing. The alternative named at the end of the paragraph is the one the treatise prefers: showing someone the fault itself so that they keep away from it.

The five benefits of gladdening are almost entirely social, and only the first concerns the person praised. The other four concern bystanders: those who were disparaging him fall silent, the faithless gain faith, the faithful gain more, and others begin to want what he has. Praising someone's practice truthfully is here treated as a form of teaching aimed at everyone in earshot — and the karmic result promised is fitting to the deed: one who gladdens others with words will hear delightful sounds wherever he is born.

9. What Each Conduct Leads To, and the Remaining Meanings

Chinese Source

復次欲行，或有能感善趣，如為欲故造後善業；或有能感惡趣，如以非法攝受諸欲。離行，行若毀犯能感惡趣，若無毀犯能感善趣及作寂滅資糧。善行，能感善趣及為寂滅資糧。不善行，能感惡趣。苦行，由依邪見苦自身故能感惡趣。非苦行，能為寂滅資糧及感善趣。順退分行，能感退墮。順進分行，能感昇進。染污行，能感流轉。清淨行，能證寂滅。自義行，唯令自己感善趣引昇進證寂滅。他義行，俱令自他生善趣引昇進證寂滅。

如是三事，根本事有六種，所謂善趣乃至涅槃。得方便事有十二種，謂十二行。悲愍他事有五種，所謂五種悲愍眾生。此中由根本事增上力故，依十二行如其所應令他離欲，乃至慶慰時。

依處者，略有三種：一過去言依、二未來言依、三現在言依，如經廣說。補特伽羅依處者，謂鈍根等二十七種數取趣應知。如上所說，事、時、補特伽羅，佛薄伽梵依此三處流布聖教，故名依處。

過患義者，謂於應毀厭義而起毀厭，或法或眾生。勝利義者，略而言之，於可稱讚義而起稱讚，或法或眾生。所治義者，略而言之，一切雜染行。能治義者，謂一切清淨行。如貪是所治、不淨是能治，瞋是所治、慈為能治，如是等盡當知。略義者，謂說諸法通種類義。廣義者，謂說諸法別種類義。復次說不了義經故、說了義經故。復次略義有二種：一名略、二義略。廣義亦二種，謂名廣及義廣。如薄伽梵說：舍利子！我能廣略宣說正法，然悟解者甚難可得。於彼經中，長行文廣義略，伽他義廣文略。

為攝十義，故說中間。嗢柁南曰：

諸地相作意，　依處德非德，

所對治能治，　略廣義應知。

復次如是略說佛教體性十種義已，諸說法者應依聖教尋求十種若具不具，既自求已應為他說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further: the conduct of desire may bring about a good destiny, as when for the sake of desire one performs later wholesome karma; or an evil destiny, as when one takes up desires unlawfully. The conduct of renunciation: if one transgresses in it, it brings about an evil destiny; if one does not, a good destiny and the provisions for quiescence. Wholesome conduct brings about a good destiny and serves as provisions for quiescence. Unwholesome conduct brings about an evil destiny. Austerity, since one afflicts one's own body in dependence on wrong view, brings about an evil destiny. Non-austerity serves as provisions for quiescence and brings about a good destiny. Conduct conducing to falling back brings about decline; conducing to advance, advancement. Defiled conduct brings about flowing on; pure conduct realizes quiescence. Conduct for one's own benefit causes only oneself to attain a good destiny, to advance, and to realize quiescence. Conduct for the benefit of others causes both oneself and others to be born in a good destiny, to advance, and to realize quiescence.

Of these three matters: the root matter is of six kinds, the good destinies down to nirvāṇa; the matter of obtaining the means is of twelve, the twelve conducts; and the matter of compassion for others is of five. Here, through the dominant force of the root matter, one relies on the twelve conducts as is fitting to free others from craving, down to gladdening them.

The support of time is of three kinds: speech resting on the past, on the future, and on the present, as expounded at length in the scriptures. The support of the person: the twenty-seven kinds of person, dull faculties and the rest. Upon these three — matter, time, and person — the Buddha, the Blessed One, spreads the sacred teaching; hence they are called supports.

The meaning of fault: giving rise to censure and weariness toward what should be censured and wearied of, whether a teaching or a being. The meaning of benefit: in brief, giving rise to praise toward what is praiseworthy, whether a teaching or a being. The meaning of what is to be counteracted: in brief, all defiled conduct. The meaning of what counteracts: all pure conduct — as craving is to be counteracted and impurity counteracts it, hatred is to be counteracted and loving-kindness counteracts it, and so on. The brief meaning: stating what is common to the kinds of things. The extensive meaning: stating what is particular to the kinds. Again: the one is stating a scripture of non-explicit meaning, the other of explicit meaning. Again, the brief meaning is of two kinds, brevity of name and brevity of sense; and the extensive likewise. As the Blessed One said: "Śāriputra, I can proclaim the true Dharma both extensively and briefly, but those who understand are very hard to find." In those scriptures, the prose is extensive in letter and brief in sense, while the verses are extensive in sense and brief in letter.

To gather the ten meanings, a middle verse is stated:

The grounds, marks, attention,
supports, merit and fault,
what is to be counteracted and what counteracts,
the brief and extensive meanings — these are to be known.

Further, having thus briefly stated the ten meanings that are the substance of the Buddha's teaching, those who teach the Dharma should seek out in the sacred teaching whether these ten are present or absent; and having sought them out for themselves, should then teach others.

Reading note

The observation about prose and verse is one of the most useful remarks about scripture in the whole treatise: 長行文廣義略，伽他義廣文略 — the prose is extensive in letter and brief in sense, the verses are extensive in sense and brief in letter. Anyone who has read a sūtra where a long prose passage is followed by a short verse restating it will recognize what is being described. The prose spends many words on a little; the verse compresses much into few. Neither is the "real" version.

The quotation that precedes it is the more sobering half. The Buddha can teach at length or in brief — 然悟解者甚難可得, but those who understand are very hard to find. The problem was never the mode of exposition. And the closing instruction turns the ten meanings into a working checklist: a teacher should first go through a text asking which of the ten are present and which absent, and only then teach it.

10. The Five-Part Method, and the Twelve Genres Again

Now the method itself: five steps for expounding any scripture. The first step requires knowing which of the twelve genres one is dealing with — and the definitions given here differ instructively from those in fascicle 6.

Chinese Source

如是建立文義體性已，諸說法者應以五相隨順解釋一切佛經，謂初應略說法要、次應說等起、次應釋義、次應釋難、後應辯次第。

法者，略有十二種，謂契經等十二分教。契經者，謂縫綴義，多分長行直說攝諸法體。應頌者，謂長行後諷頌及略舉所說不了義經。記別者，謂廣分別略所舉義，及記命過弟子生處。諷頌者，謂以一句說、或二句說、或三四五六句等說。自說者，謂不請而說，為令弟子得勝解故，為令上品所化眾生安住勝理，自然而說，名為自說。如經言：世尊今者自然宣說。因緣者，謂因請問說。如經言：世尊於一時中，因沒力伽羅子，為諸苾芻宣說法要。又依別解脫增上道毘奈耶所有言說，謂依如是如是因緣、依如是如是事，世尊說如是如是語。是名因緣。譬喻者，謂有譬喻經，由譬喻故隱義明了。本事者，謂除本生，宣說前際諸所有事。本生者，謂宣說己身於過去世行菩薩行時諸本生事。

方廣者，謂說菩薩道，如說十地四菩薩行等，及說如來百四十不共佛法，謂四一切種清淨乃至一切種妙智，如〈菩薩地〉廣說。又法無量故、義深廣故、時長遠故，謂極勇猛，經三大劫阿僧企耶方得成滿，故名方廣。希法者，謂佛及諸弟子說希奇法。如諸經中因希有事故起於言說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having thus established the substance of the letter and the meaning, those who teach the Dharma should explain any scripture of the Buddha by following five aspects: first state the essentials of the teaching in brief; next state the occasion of its arising; next explain the meaning; next answer objections; and last set out the sequence.

The teaching is of twelve kinds, the twelvefold teaching beginning with discourse. Discourse: it has the sense of stitching together; for the most part it is prose directly stating and gathering the substance of things. Mingled verse: verses following prose, and also scriptures of non-explicit meaning that state a thing only in brief. Prediction: distinguishing at length what was stated only in brief, and recording the places of rebirth of disciples who have died. Verse: stated in one line, or two, or three, four, five, six, and so on. Spontaneous utterance: spoken without being asked, in order that disciples may gain resolve and that beings of the highest capacity may be settled in the excellent principle — spoken of itself. As a scripture says: "The Blessed One now spoke of himself." Occasioned teaching: spoken because of a request. As a scripture says: "The Blessed One at one time, on account of Maudgalyāyana's son, proclaimed the essentials of the Dharma to the monks." And also all the speech in the Vinaya of the higher path of individual liberation: relying on such and such a cause, and on such and such a matter, the Blessed One spoke such and such words. Simile: scriptures containing similes, by which a hidden meaning becomes plain. Past events: excluding the birth stories, the recounting of all matters of the former limit. Birth stories: recounting the matters of his own former lives when he practiced the bodhisattva conduct in past ages.

Extensive teachings: teaching the bodhisattva path — as in the teaching of the ten grounds and the four bodhisattva practices, and the teaching of the Tathāgata's hundred and forty qualities held in common with none, namely the fourfold purity in every aspect down to the knowledge of all aspects, as expounded at length in the Bodhisattva Grounds. And because the teaching is measureless, the meaning deep and broad, and the time long — being accomplished only after three incalculable great aeons of the utmost courage — it is called extensive. Marvels: the buddhas and their disciples speaking of marvelous things, as when in the scriptures speech arises on account of some rare event.

Reading note

Comparing this list with the one in fascicle 6 is instructive, because the two are not identical and the differences are not errors. There the genres were being sorted into the three baskets, and the definitions were framed to make that sorting work. Here they are being defined for a person about to expound a text, and so the emphasis shifts to what each genre requires of an interpreter — that mingled verse contains non-explicit statements, that prediction distinguishes at length what was stated in brief, that a simile is what makes a hidden meaning plain.

The etymology given for discourse is worth keeping: 縫綴義, it has the sense of stitching together. A sūtra is a thread, and the prose of a discourse stitches the substance of things into a continuous fabric. And the definition of extensive teachings gives three quite different reasons for the name — the teaching is measureless, the meaning deep and broad, and the time long. A genre named for its scale in aeons is unusual, and it is characteristic of a Mahāyāna reading of the canon.

11. Exposition, the Occasion, and the Two Kinds of Meaning

Chinese Source

論議者，謂諸經所攝摩怛履迦。且如諸了義經皆名摩怛履迦，所謂如來自廣分別諸法體相；又諸弟子已見聖迹，依自所證無倒顯示諸法體相，亦名摩怛履迦。摩怛履迦亦名阿毘達磨，猶如世間一切書算詩論皆有本母，當知經中研究法相所有言說亦復如是。又如世間若無諸字本母，字不顯了。如是十二分教中若不建立諸法體相，法不明了。若建立已，諸法自相共相皆得顯現。又復能顯無雜法相故。即此摩怛履迦名阿毘達磨，依此摩怛履迦所餘解釋諸經義者，亦名鄔波第鑠。

等起者，略有三種，謂事、時、補特伽羅依處別故。如經中說：於如是時，為如是補特伽羅，依如是行，令彼離欲乃至慶慰。

義者，略有二種：一總義、二別義。當知總義復有四種：一引了義經、二分別事究竟、三行、四果。行復二種，謂邪行、正行。果亦二種，謂邪行果、正行果。當知別義亦有四種，一分別差別名、二分別自體相、三訓釋名言、四義門差別。訓釋名言復由五種方便，一由相、二由自體、三由業、四由法、五由因果。義門差別亦有五種應知：一自體差別、二界差別、三時差別、四位差別、五補特伽羅差別。

自體差別者，謂色自體有十色處差別，受自體有三受差別，想自體有六想差別，行自體有三行差別，識自體有六識差別，如是等類自體差別應知。界差別者，謂欲界、色界、無色界。時差別者，謂去、來、今。位差別者，當知差別二十五種，謂下、中、上三位，苦、樂、不苦不樂三位，善、不善、無記三位，聞、思、修三位，增上戒、增上心、增上慧三位，內、外二位，所取、能取二位，所治、能治二位，現前、不現前二位，及因、果二位。補特伽羅差別者，如前所說二十七種應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Exposition: the mātṛkās comprised in the scriptures. Thus all scriptures of explicit meaning are called mātṛkās — namely, where the Tathāgata himself distinguishes at length the substance and marks of things; and where disciples who have seen the noble path display, without distortion and in dependence on their own realization, the substance and marks of things, this too is called a mātṛkā. A mātṛkā is also called Abhidharma. Just as in the world all writing, arithmetic, and poetry have their mother-texts, so it is with all the speech in the scriptures that investigates the marks of things. And just as in the world, without the mother-letters, letters are not made plain, so within the twelvefold teaching, if the substance and marks of things are not established, the teaching is not clear; and once established, the particular and general marks of all things become manifest — and further, the unmixed marks of things can be displayed. This very mātṛkā is called Abhidharma; and the remaining explanations of scriptural meaning that rest on this mātṛkā are called upadeśa.

The occasion of arising is of three kinds, by the difference of the supports of matter, time, and person. As a scripture says: at such a time, for such a person, relying on such conduct, to free him from craving, down to gladdening him.

The meaning is of two kinds: the general meaning and the particular meaning. The general meaning is of four kinds: adducing scriptures of explicit meaning; distinguishing the matter to completion; conduct; and fruit. Conduct is of two kinds, wrong and right; and fruit likewise, the fruit of wrong conduct and of right. The particular meaning is also of four kinds: distinguishing the differences of name; distinguishing the marks of own-substance; glossing the terms; and the distinctions of the approaches to the meaning. Glossing the terms proceeds by five means: by mark, by own-substance, by activity, by property, and by cause and effect. The distinctions of approach are of five kinds: distinction of own-substance; of realm; of time; of stage; and of person.

Distinction of own-substance: the own-substance of form has the distinction of the ten form sense fields; of feeling, three feelings; of perception, six perceptions; of formations, three formations; of consciousness, six consciousnesses. Distinction of realm: the realms of desire, form, and formlessness. Distinction of time: past, future, present. Distinction of stage: twenty-five — lesser, middling, greater; painful, pleasant, neither; wholesome, unwholesome, neutral; hearing, reflection, cultivation; higher discipline, higher mind, higher wisdom; inner and outer; grasped and grasper; to be counteracted and counteracting; present and not present; and cause and effect. Distinction of person: the twenty-seven kinds stated above.

Reading note

The image of the mother-letters is the key to what Abhidharma is for. Just as the alphabet is what makes any particular letter legible, the mātṛkā — the list of categories — is what makes any particular teaching legible. Without it, 法不明了, the teaching is not clear. This is a defense of scholasticism against the charge that it adds nothing: the categories are not additional doctrines but the conditions under which doctrines can be read at all. And the term is generous — a disciple who has seen the path and sets out the marks of things from his own realization is producing a mātṛkā, not merely commenting on one.

The five means of glossing a term — by mark, own-substance, activity, property, and cause and effect — are the same five that fascicle 11 gave as the five kinds of inference. This is not coincidence. Explaining what a word means and inferring what a thing is turn out to be the same operation performed on different material.

12. Five Kinds of Objection, and Four Ways to Answer

Chinese Source

釋難者，若自設難、若他設難，皆應解釋。當知設難略有五因：一、為未了義得顯了故，如言：此文有何義耶？二、語相違故，如言：何故薄伽梵前後說異？三、理相違故，如有顯示與四道理相違之義。四、不定顯示故，如言：何故薄伽梵於一種義種種異門差別顯示？五、究竟不可見故，如言：內我之體有何相貌，而常恒不變自性正住？如是等類。

於此五難，如其次第應當解釋。謂於不了義難，方便顯了；於語相違難，隨順會通；如於語相違難，隨順會通；如是於不定顯示難、究竟不可見難亦爾。於理相違難，或以黑教道理而判決之，或復顯示四種道理，或顯因果相應道理，謂此言顯果或復顯因。

又於問難應設四記：決定記者，謂為如理問者無倒建立諸法體相故；分別記者，謂為如理不如理問者開示差別相故；反詰記者，謂為止息戲諍論故；默置記者，有四種因，謂無體性故乃至彼相法爾故，如前已說。若廣分別，如〈思所成地〉。又如有問如來滅後為有為無等，此於世俗諦及勝義諦理趣皆不可記，是故默置。以約勝義無如來故，不可記別；若約世俗所依能依，道理違故，及斷滅果非真實故，亦不可記如來滅後是有無等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answering objections: whether one raises the objection oneself or another raises it, it should be answered. Objections arise, in brief, from five causes. First, so that a meaning not yet understood may be made plain — as when one asks: what does this passage mean? Second, because of a contradiction in wording — as when one asks: why did the Blessed One say different things at different times? Third, because of a contradiction in principle — as when someone displays a meaning contrary to the four principles of reasoning. Fourth, because of indeterminate presentation — as when one asks: why did the Blessed One display one and the same meaning through many different approaches? Fifth, because something is finally invisible — as when one asks: what marks does the substance of an inner self have, such that it abides permanently, unchanging, in its own nature?

These five should be answered in order. For an objection about a meaning not understood, one makes it plain by skillful means. For an objection about contradictory wording, one reconciles them by according with both; and likewise for objections about indeterminate presentation and about what is finally invisible. For an objection about contradiction in principle, one settles it either by the principle of the scriptural teaching, or by displaying the four principles of reasoning, or by displaying the principle of the correspondence of cause and effect — that is, whether this statement displays the effect or displays the cause.

And to a question four kinds of answer should be given. The categorical answer: for one who asks in accordance with principle, establishing the substance and marks of things without distortion. The answer with a distinction: for one who asks whether in accordance with principle or not, disclosing the differences. The answer by counter-question: in order to put a stop to frivolous disputation. The answer of setting aside: for four reasons — because it has no substance, and so on down to because such is simply the nature of that mark, as stated above; distinguished at length in the Ground of What Is Attained by Reflection. Thus when someone asks whether the Tathāgata exists after his passing or not, this cannot be declared in the way of either conventional or ultimate truth, and so it is set aside. In the ultimate sense there is no Tathāgata, and so no declaration can be made; and conventionally, since the reasoning of what supports and what is supported is contradicted, and since an annihilated result is not real, neither can it be declared that the Tathāgata after his passing exists or does not.

Reading note

The five causes of objection form a useful diagnostic, because they distinguish problems that need entirely different treatments. Not understanding a passage needs clarification. Two passages contradicting each other needs reconciliation. A passage contradicting reason needs argument. A single point taught many ways needs explanation of why. And a question about something inherently invisible — the example given is the self — needs something else again. Note that four of the five get the same remedy, 隨順會通, reconciling by according with both; only the third calls for the machinery of proof.

The four ways of answering include one that is not an answer: 反詰記, the counter-question, whose stated purpose is 止息戲諍論, to put a stop to frivolous disputation. Asking a question back is here a recognized technique, and its function is to end an exchange rather than advance it. The fourth, silence, is given the fullest treatment, and the analysis of the question about the Tathāgata after death is exact: ultimately there is no Tathāgata to be the subject of the question; conventionally, the framing itself is incoherent. Silence is not a refusal to know but a recognition that no true sentence answers the question as put — the same conclusion reached in fascicle 6.

13. Sequence: Three Kinds

Chinese Source

次第者，略有三種：一圓滿次第、二解釋次第、三能成次第。為顯此三次第，略引聖教。如世尊言：我出家時盛美、第一盛美、最極盛美。此言即顯盛美圓滿次第。又復說言：曾處我父淨飯王宮，顏容端正，乃至廣說。此言即顯盛美解釋次第。又言：為何義故盛美出家？由見老病死等法故。此言即顯能成次第。

又復經中略說諸法，如言三受，苦、樂、不苦不樂如是等。此中唯顯圓滿次第。由所餘句圓滿此受，故名圓滿。如受，應知四聖諦中先說初句，後後次第隨順分析亦爾。能成次第，有二種，或以前句成立後句，或以後句成立前句。當知解釋次第亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sequence is of three kinds: the sequence of completion; the sequence of explanation; and the sequence of establishing. To display these three, a passage of the sacred teaching is briefly adduced. As the Blessed One said: "When I went forth I was in my prime, in the first flush of my prime, in the very height of my prime." These words display the sequence of completion with respect to being in one's prime. And again he said: "I had dwelt in the palace of my father King Śuddhodana, comely of face," and so on at length. These words display the sequence of explanation with respect to being in one's prime. And again: "For what purpose did one in his prime go forth? Because he saw old age, sickness, death, and the rest." These words display the sequence of establishing.

And again, when a scripture states things briefly — as in saying "three feelings: painful, pleasant, and neither painful nor pleasant" — this displays only the sequence of completion, since the remaining phrases complete that feeling, and hence it is called completion. As with feeling, so it should be understood that in the four noble truths the first phrase is stated first and each subsequent phrase is analyzed in accordance with the sequence. The sequence of establishing is of two kinds: either an earlier phrase establishes a later, or a later establishes an earlier; and the sequence of explanation should be understood likewise.

Reading note

The illustration is chosen with care, because all three sequences are drawn from one short passage of the Buddha's own account of his going forth. "In my prime, in the first flush of my prime, in the very height of my prime" is completion — three phrases building one idea to its full extent. "I had dwelt in my father's palace, comely of face" is explanation — unpacking what being in one's prime consisted of. And "why did one in his prime go forth? Because he saw old age, sickness, and death" is establishing — giving the reason. Three different jobs done by three consecutive stretches of the same narrative.

This is the last of the five steps for expounding a scripture, and it is the one an expositor is likeliest to skip. Knowing what a passage means is not the same as knowing why its phrases are in the order they are in, and the three sequences give a reader three distinct questions to ask of any list, any repetition, and any string of clauses.

14. The Ten Qualifications of a Teacher

Chinese Source

師者，謂成就十法，名說法師眾相圓滿。一善於法義，謂於六種法十種義善能解了。二能廣宣說，謂多聞聞持其聞積集。三具足無畏，謂於剎帝利等勝大眾中宣說正法無有怯懼。又因此故，聲不嘶破、腋不流汗、念無忘失。四言詞善巧，謂語工圓滿、八分成就、言詞具足、處眾說法。語工圓滿者，謂文句相應助伴等，乃至廣說。八分成就者，謂上首美妙等，乃至廣說。五善方便說，謂二十種善方便說，如以時殷重等相。六具足成就法隨法行，謂不唯聽聞語言為極，要如說行。七威儀具足，謂說正法時，手足不亂、不搖頭動眉，口面支節無有改變，進止去來端嚴庠序。八勇猛精勤，謂常樂聽聞所未聞法，於已聞法轉令明淨，不捨瑜伽不捨作意，心不捨離內奢摩他。九無有疲厭，謂為四眾廣宣妙法身心無倦。十具足忍力，謂罵弄訶責終不反報，若被輕蔑不生忿慼，乃至廣說。

Ru-Yi Translation

The teacher: one who accomplishes ten things is called a teacher of the Dharma complete in every aspect.

First, good at the teaching and its meaning: able to understand well the six kinds of letter and the ten meanings. Second, able to expound broadly: having heard much, retained what he heard, and accumulated his learning. Third, fully fearless: proclaiming the true Dharma in the great assemblies of kṣatriyas and the rest without timidity — and on that account his voice does not crack, his armpits do not sweat, and his mindfulness does not lapse. Fourth, skillful in wording: complete in the craft of speech, accomplished in the eight parts, complete in wording, teaching the Dharma in an assembly. Fifth, skillful in the means of speaking: the twenty kinds of skillful means of speaking, such as timeliness and earnestness. Sixth, fully accomplished in practicing the Dharma in accordance with the Dharma: not taking the hearing of words as the end, but practicing as it is spoken. Seventh, complete in deportment: when teaching the true Dharma, his hands and feet are not restless, he does not shake his head or move his brows, his mouth, face, and limbs do not change, and his going and coming, stopping and starting, are dignified and unhurried. Eighth, courageous and diligent: constantly delighting to hear teachings he has not heard, making what he has heard ever clearer, not abandoning yoga, not abandoning attention, his mind not departing from inner calm abiding. Ninth, without weariness: proclaiming the marvelous Dharma at length to the fourfold assembly, tireless in body and mind. Tenth, complete in the power of patience: when reviled, mocked, or berated, he never answers back; when slighted, he gives rise to no anger or resentment.

Reading note

Only the first two of the ten concern knowledge. Everything after that is about the person: nerve in front of a hostile crowd, craft of language, whether he practices what he teaches, how he holds his body while speaking, whether he is still learning, whether he tires, and — last — whether he answers back when insulted. The sixth is stated with particular sharpness: 不唯聽聞語言為極, not taking the hearing of words as the end. A teacher who has only heard is not qualified.

The seventh is the most concrete instruction in the fascicle and reads like a note from an experienced observer: hands and feet still, head and eyebrows not moving, face and limbs unchanging, and one's coming and going 端嚴庠序, dignified and unhurried. And the eighth quietly requires that a teacher still be a practitioner — 不捨瑜伽不捨作意, not abandoning yoga, not abandoning attention. The tenth is the hardest and is placed last, where the culminating item goes.

15. Eight Kinds of Speech, and Five Kinds of Audience

Chinese Source

說眾者，謂處於五眾宣八種言。何等為八？一可憙樂言、二善開發言、三善釋難言、四善分析言、五善順入言、六引餘證言、七勝辯才言、八隨宗趣言。五眾者，一在家眾、二出家眾、三清淨信眾、四邪怨眾、五中平眾。

可憙樂言者，有五種相應知，一有證因、二有譬喻、三語業具足、四文字句美、五言詞顯了。善開發言者，開深隱義令顯現故，辯麁顯義令深邃故。善釋難言者，能善解釋五種難故，如前應知。善分析言者，析一一法，依增一道理乃至十種，或復過此，善分別故。如依一法建立二種三種四念住等，乃至廣說。善順入言者，唯善顯釋契經、應頌等十二分教，終不引攝邪道異論。引餘證言者，謂引餘經成立所說。勝辯才言者，隨自所忍，善分別義。隨宗趣言者，謂依摩怛履迦分別顯示，或依餘無倒說者所說言教如理解釋。

復次處在家眾，應依毀諸惡行、稱讚善行現前說法，令其止息及進修故。處出家眾，應依增上戒等三學現前說法，令彼速疾修圓滿故。處淨等眾，應依聖教廣大威德現前說法，如其次第令倍增長、令處中住、令生淨信故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Speaking to an audience: in the midst of five kinds of assembly one proclaims eight kinds of speech. What eight? Speech one is glad to hear; speech that opens things up well; speech that answers objections well; speech that analyzes well; speech that leads in well; speech that adduces further proof; speech of surpassing eloquence; and speech that follows the school's purport. The five assemblies are: householders; renunciants; the assembly of pure faith; the hostile assembly; and the neutral assembly.

Speech one is glad to hear has five marks: it has a supporting reason; it has a simile; its verbal action is complete; its letters and phrases are beautiful; and its wording is plain. Speech that opens things up well: it opens a deep and hidden meaning so that it appears, and it treats a coarse and obvious meaning so that it becomes profound. Speech that answers objections well: it can answer the five kinds of objection, as stated above. Speech that analyzes well: it analyzes each thing, by the principle of ascending number up to ten or beyond, distinguishing well — as from one thing one establishes two, three, the four establishments of mindfulness, and so on. Speech that leads in well: it explains only the twelvefold teaching, discourse and mingled verse and the rest, and never draws in the doctrines of wrong paths. Speech that adduces further proof: it adduces other scriptures to establish what is said. Speech of surpassing eloquence: it distinguishes the meaning well according to what one has oneself accepted. Speech that follows the school's purport: it distinguishes and displays in dependence on the mātṛkā, or explains in accordance with principle the teaching stated by others who speak without distortion.

Further: in the midst of an assembly of householders, one should teach by censuring evil conduct and praising good conduct, so as to make them desist and go on to cultivate. In the midst of an assembly of renunciants, one should teach on the three trainings, higher discipline and the rest, so that they may swiftly cultivate them to fullness. In the midst of the assemblies of pure faith and the rest, one should teach on the vast majesty of the sacred teaching — respectively, so as to make it increase twofold, so as to make them remain neutral, and so as to make them give rise to pure faith.

Reading note

The second kind of speech states a two-way obligation that is easy to miss and worth taking seriously: it opens a deep meaning so that it appears, and it treats an obvious meaning so that it becomes profound. Good exposition does not only make hard things easy. It also makes easy things hard again — recovering the depth in what has become familiar. Both failures are failures of the same skill.

The three instructions at the close match content to audience with some realism. To householders one talks about conduct, not the three trainings. To renunciants one talks about the trainings. And to the three remaining assemblies — the faithful, the hostile, and the neutral — one teaches the same thing, the vast majesty of the teaching, with three different aims: the faithful should increase, the hostile should be brought to neutrality, and the neutral to faith. Note the modesty of the goal for the hostile assembly. One does not try to convert them; one tries to get them to stop opposing.
