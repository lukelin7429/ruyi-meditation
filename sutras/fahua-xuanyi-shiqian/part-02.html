Explanations and Proof-Texts: The Five Flavors, the Indeterminate and the Secret, and the Rest of the Sevenfold Exposition

Fascicle two continues Zhanran's comments on the first fascicle of the Profound Meaning (fascicle-001 on this site), from the simile of the sun shining on the high mountains to the end of the sevenfold common exposition. He works through the five flavors and their matching similes in the Avataṃsaka and the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, quoting at length the Nirvāṇa's three sons, three fields, three vessels, three patients, and three horses; then the indeterminate and secret teachings, where he criticizes Fazang's four explanations of how the Buddha could "not move" from the seat of awakening and yet ascend to the heavens; then why the Lotus alone is "open and manifest," "gradual-sudden," "united," and "ghee." He insists more than once that the Avataṃsaka is not "more sudden" than the Lotus. The rest of the fascicle covers the other six of the seven aspects: the proof-texts from the "Introduction," "Supernatural Power," and "Bhaiṣajyarāja" chapters; the order of the five sections; their opening and combining; twelve rounds of resolving doubts; the contemplation of mind, with its warning against both mere learning and "blind realization"; and the matching of the five sections with the four siddhāntas and with the title of the Middle Treatise.

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Zhanran, a monk of Tiantai.

Marking the Teaching: The Sun on the Mountains and the Valleys

Chinese Source

初文「云何」下至「出乳味相」者，此文以華嚴說大，未遊鹿苑，詺之為頓。此是頓部，非是頓教，以彼部中兼一別故，人不見者便謂華嚴頓於法華者誤矣。下去準說。初文牒譬，「厚殖」下以感應合譬，一往總以別圓為厚。「頓說」下明說頓意，良由下說頓之由由相隔故。言「大隔小」者，小人未轉，為大所隔。「此如華嚴」下結《華嚴》譬以同涅槃，故云「如」也。下去例爾。「約法」去明機感相應得益以結部名。「約說」下結說，次第同《涅槃》譬。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first passage, from "How is this differentiated?" to "the flavor of milk drawn straight from the cow": this passage calls the Avataṃsaka's teaching of the great, before he went to the Deer Park, "sudden." This is the sudden collection, not the sudden teaching, since that collection also includes one [other teaching], the distinct. Those who fail to see this and say the Avataṃsaka is more sudden than the Lotus are mistaken. What follows is to be read the same way. The first passage takes up the simile; from "those whose good roots are deeply planted" on, it applies the simile through appeal and response, for now taking the distinct and the perfect together as "deeply." From "The sudden teaching" on makes clear the purpose of teaching the sudden, and from "truly because" on the reason for teaching it: because they were kept apart. "The great is kept apart from the lesser": the lesser people had not yet turned, and were kept apart by the great. From "this is like the Avataṃsaka" on, it concludes the Avataṃsaka simile so as to make it the same as the Nirvāṇa; hence "like." What follows is likewise. From "In terms of the dharma reaching its conditions" on, it makes clear the benefit gained when capacity and appeal correspond, so as to conclude the name of the collection. From "in terms of the sequence of its telling" on, it concludes the telling: the sequence is the same as the Nirvāṇa simile.

Chinese Source

次照幽谷，亦先提《華嚴》譬，「淺行」下亦舉感應以合譬，乃至《法華》文中皆有結譬同法得益。結名結說次第，尋初頓教意以對下四味，比說可知。唯三藏結部名在前，餘文或闕說意等，尋之可見。「大雖在座」等者，大人示迹隱在小中，「多跢」是學行之相，「嘙啝」是習語之聲，示為三藏始行初教，而三藏實行者謂之為實，故云「不識」。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, "shines into the dark valleys": again it first presents the Avataṃsaka simile; from "those of shallow practice" on, it again applies the simile through appeal and response; and so on through the Lotus, each passage concludes the simile, makes it the same as the dharma, and states the benefit gained. As for concluding the name and concluding the sequence of telling: seek out the purpose of the first, the sudden teaching, and set it against the four flavors below; they can be understood by comparison. Only in the Tripiṭaka does the conclusion of the collection's name come first; the other passages sometimes lack the purpose of telling and so on, as can be seen by looking. "Though the great may be present in the assembly" and so on: great people showed traces, hidden among the small. 多跢 is the look of learning to walk, and 嘙啝 the sound of learning to speak: they appeared as beginners practicing the first teaching, the Tripiṭaka; and those who truly practiced the Tripiṭaka took them for real; hence "fail to recognize them."

「影臨萬水」至「方等」者，至方等中具說四教，以未融故，故見不同。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "its reflection falling upon the ten thousand waters" to "the Vaipulya": in the Vaipulya all four teachings are taught, and since they are not yet fused, what is seen differs.

Chinese Source

問：華嚴鹿苑大小永融，纔說方等，則同座並聞者何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: In the Avataṃsaka and the Deer Park, the great and the small were forever separate; how is it that as soon as the Vaipulya is taught, they sit together and hear together?

Chinese Source

答：若以祕密橫被無時不遍，若約橫論豎則隱顯在機，佛本意在大故遂本居初，然由一分漸機致使聖慈未暢，前已專大、次復專小，今雖同座大小仍隔，但小被大彈為成生蘇，以此為次耳。故知於創稟者，仍互不相知，故有此諸喻意不同。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: If [we speak of] the secret, extending horizontally, there is no time it does not pervade. If, within the horizontal, we discuss the vertical, then hidden and manifest lie in the capacities. The Buddha's original intent was in the great, so following the origin he placed it first; but because of a portion of gradual capacities, the sage's compassion could not yet be freely expressed. First it was wholly great, next wholly small; now, though they sit together, great and small are still kept apart, only the small are rebuked by the great so as to form the fresh curdled butter — this is taken as the sequence. So one knows that those first receiving it still did not know of one another; hence these similes differ in meaning.

Chinese Source

「影臨萬水」譬現身不同，「逐器方圓」譬示土不同，「隨波動靜」譬說法不同，「示一佛土」至「不共」合譬也。從「故見有」下明機見不同，對前三相，初見淨如梵王、見穢如身子，開彼三相，總引經文，兼約六根明斥小也。「見有淨穢」對前現土眼根也。亦應更云覩相優劣，已舉見土身必稱土，故不繁文。「聞有褒貶」，耳根也。「嗅有薝蔔」，鼻根也。「華有著身」，身根也。「慧有若干」，意根也。唯闕舌根，以褒貶兼之。若語身者，應云如須彌山巨身也；始坐佛樹，細身也。一一根中並一褒一貶，尋之可見。又佛歎文殊、淨名，褒也。聲聞菩薩被折，貶也。其例蓋多不能具記。隨其心淨即佛土淨，入此室者不嗅餘香，結習盡者華不著也。聲聞有若干，其無礙慧無若干也。即是褒貶之意。世人判《楞伽》，或同《華嚴》，或同《法華》，具如《止觀》記已有二處引《楞伽》文判屬方等，以彼經文具四教故、有彈訶故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Its reflection falling upon the ten thousand waters" likens the bodies manifested differing; "following each vessel's shape whether round or square" likens the lands shown differing; "moving with each wave whether stirring or still" likens the teaching differing; from "he shows a single buddha-land" to "his spiritual power appears differently to each" applies the simile. From "Hence some see" on makes clear that capacities see differently, matching the three marks above: first, seeing purity like the Brahmā king and seeing defilement like Śāriputra. Opening those three marks, it cites the sūtra text in general and, by way of the six faculties, makes clear the rebuke of the small. "Some see purity, others defilement" matches the land shown above: the eye faculty. One should also add seeing the marks [of the body] as superior or inferior; but since the land has been raised, and the body must fit the land, it is not spelled out. "Some hear praise, others blame" is the ear faculty. "Some smell the fragrance of the campaka flower" is the nose. "Some have flowers cling to their bodies" is the body. "Some have wisdom in full measure" is the mind. Only the tongue is missing, included in praise and blame. Were the body spoken of, it would say: like the huge body of Mount Sumeru; or, first sitting under the Buddha-tree, the small body. In each faculty there is one praise and one blame, as can be seen by looking. Again, the Buddha's praise of Mañjuśrī and Vimalakīrti is praise; the śrāvakas and bodhisattvas being rebuked is blame. The examples are many and cannot all be recorded. "As the mind is pure, the buddha-land is pure"; "those who enter this room smell no other fragrance"; "for those whose bonds and habits are exhausted, flowers do not cling." The śrāvakas' [wisdom] has a measure; unobstructed wisdom has no measure. This is the meaning of praise and blame. People of the world classify the Laṅkāvatāra either with the Avataṃsaka or with the Lotus; but in my Record on Calming and Contemplation there are already two places that cite the Laṅkāvatāra and assign it to the Vaipulya, because that sūtra's text contains all four teachings and contains rebukes.

Ru-Yi Translation

Here the purpose of telling is missing; it should say: the Vaipulya fundamentally reproves the small and rebukes the partial, while to the perfect nothing stands between. The reason for telling is also missing; it should say: truly because the small had not fully turned, the five hundred heard of the great and were rebuked.

Chinese Source

「復有義」者，《華嚴經》譬但云平地，今離彼平地，以譬方等般若法華，方等如食時，般若如禺中，法華如正中，於彼義上更加二義，故云「復有」。言「大人蒙其光用」等者，菩薩大人蒙般若光諸法之用，二乘之人既無此用，是故譬之如七日嬰兒若視日輪令眼失光，故名為「喪」，外人暗證譬如夜遊，菩薩利他譬如作務。

Ru-Yi Translation

"There is, moreover, this further sense": the Avataṃsaka simile speaks only of level ground; here that level ground is divided to liken the Vaipulya, Prajñā, and Lotus — the Vaipulya like mealtime, the Prajñā like mid-morning, the Lotus like noon. To that meaning two more senses are added; hence "moreover." "Grown persons receive the benefit of its light" and so on: bodhisattvas, the grown persons, receive the function of all dharmas through the light of prajñā; people of the two vehicles have no such function, so they are likened to an infant seven days old who, looking at the sun's disk, loses the light of its eyes; hence "lose." Outsiders with blind realization are likened to night travelers; bodhisattvas benefiting others are likened to those engaged in tasks.

Chinese Source

「務」者運役也。故「文云」下引信解聲聞自述以合嬰兒喪其睛明，一切智明於般若光，無明全在義之如喪。「雖三人俱學」下說意也。仍闕說由，亦應云良由小稍通泰，致使被加令說。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Tasks" (務) means labor. So from "Hence the text says" on, it cites the śrāvakas' own account in "Belief and Understanding" to apply "infants lose the clarity of their sight": the clarity of all-knowledge lies in the light of prajñā, and with ignorance wholly remaining, in meaning it is as if lost. From "Though the three kinds of persons study together" on is the purpose of telling. The reason for telling is still missing; it should also say: truly because the small were becoming somewhat receptive, they were empowered and made to teach.

Chinese Source

次譬法華中「土圭」等者，「圭」者累土也，故字從重土謂之為圭。如宋嚴觀法師與此太史官何承天，共論此土是邊是中，觀乃引周公測影之法，以一尺二寸土圭用測日影，夏至之日猶有餘陰，天竺此日則無餘陰。故叡法師云「如日方中無處不南」，此指中方無餘陰處。準此算法，地上寸影天上萬里。「若低頭若微善」者，總結散亂、小善之類無不開之，以成佛因，以用合譬，同入一實，故無盈縮。「不令」下說意。亦闕說由，應云良由大機已熟，眾無枝葉，致使一切佛知見開。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, in the simile for the Lotus, "the gnomon" and so on: 圭 is piled earth, so the character is made of doubled 土 and called 圭. For example, Dharma Master Yan Guan of the Song discussed with He Chengtian, Grand Astrologer of this land, whether this land is at the edge or the center. Guan cited the Duke of Zhou's method of measuring shadows: using a gnomon of one foot two inches to measure the sun's shadow, on the day of the summer solstice there is still some shade left over, whereas in India on that day there is none. So Dharma Master Rui said, "As when the sun is at noon, there is no place that is not south," referring to the central land where no shade is left over. By this reckoning, an inch of shadow on earth is ten thousand li in the sky. "Whether one merely bows one's head … or performs only the slightest good": this concludes in general that the scattered, the small good, and the like are all opened to become causes of buddhahood; using them to apply the simile, all enter the one reality together; hence "neither shrinks nor overflows." From "He does not let" on is the purpose of telling. The reason for telling is also missing; it should say: truly because the great capacities were already ripe and the assembly had no branches and leaves, the Buddha's knowledge and vision was opened for all.

Chinese Source

「若約法被緣名漸圓教」者，此文語略，具足應云鹿苑漸後會漸歸圓，故云漸圓。人不見之，便謂法華為漸圓，華嚴為頓圓，不知華嚴部中有別，乃至般若中方便二教皆從法華一乘開出，故云「於一佛乘分別說三」。故疏云「於一佛乘開出，帶二帶三」。今法華部無彼二三，故云無二亦無三。又上結云華嚴兼等，此經無復兼但對帶，此非難見，如何固迷？又今文諸義凡一一科皆先約四教，以約麁妙，則前三為麁、後一為妙。次約五味以判麁妙，則前四味為麁、醍醐為妙，全不推求上下文意，直指一語便謂法華劣於華嚴，幾許誤哉！幾許誤哉！「約說次第名醍醐味相」者，此五味教相生之文，在第十三〈聖行品〉末，佛印無垢藏王菩薩竟，云譬如從牛出乳乃至醍醐，譬從佛出十二部經乃至涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

"In terms of the dharma reaching its conditions, this is named the gradual-and-perfect teaching": the wording here is brief; fully stated it should be: after the gradual of the Deer Park, it merges the gradual and returns it to the perfect; hence "gradual-and-perfect." People who fail to see this say the Lotus is the gradual-perfect and the Avataṃsaka the sudden-perfect, not knowing that within the Avataṃsaka collection there is the distinct, and that even the two expedient teachings in the Prajñā all came forth from the one vehicle of the Lotus; hence "within the one Buddha-vehicle he distinguishes and teaches three." So the commentary says, "Coming forth from the one Buddha-vehicle, accompanied by two and accompanied by three." The present Lotus collection has none of those twos and threes; hence "there is no second, nor yet a third." Again, the conclusion above said the Avataṃsaka is combined and so on, while this sūtra no longer has any combining, exclusiveness, contrasting, or accompanying. This is not hard to see; how can they cling so to their confusion? Again, in the present text, every section first takes each meaning by the four teachings to judge coarse and wondrous, the first three being coarse and the last wondrous; next it judges coarse and wondrous by the five flavors, the first four flavors being coarse and ghee wondrous. To point straight at one phrase without ever examining the meaning of the text above and below, and say the Lotus is inferior to the Avataṃsaka — how great an error! How great an error! "In terms of the sequence of its telling, this is the mark of the flavor of ghee": this passage on the five flavors arising one from another is at the end of the thirteenth [fascicle], in the chapter on Noble Practice: after the Buddha confirmed the Bodhisattva Stainless Treasury King, he said that, just as from the cow comes milk and so on up to ghee, so from the Buddha come the twelve divisions of scripture and so on up to nirvāṇa.

Chinese Source

問：彼經自以醍醐譬於涅槃，今何得以譬於法華？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: That sūtra itself likens ghee to nirvāṇa; how can it be used here to liken the Lotus?

Chinese Source

答：一家義意謂二部同味，然《涅槃》尚劣。何者？《法華》開權如已破大陣，餘機至彼如殘黨不難，故以《法華》為大收，《涅槃》為捃拾。若不爾者，《涅槃》不應遙指八千聲聞於法華中得授記莂，見如來性，如秋收冬藏更無所作。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: In the understanding of our school, the two collections are of the same flavor, yet the Nirvāṇa is still inferior. Why? The Lotus's opening of the provisional is like having already broken the main battle line; the remaining capacities that reach that [sūtra] are like scattered remnants, not difficult. So the Lotus is the great harvest, and the Nirvāṇa the gleaning. Were it not so, the Nirvāṇa would not point back to the eight thousand śrāvakas who received their predictions and saw the Tathāgata-nature in the Lotus, like the autumn harvest stored for winter, with nothing more to be done.

Chinese Source

次引同中二：初總結同，次引文別釋。初言「當知華嚴之譬與涅槃義同」者，《華嚴》日出等譬與《涅槃》五味義同，但有廣略之殊，故廣開平地以為三譬，譬三味也。次引文證同中言「三子三田三馬」等者，《涅槃》「迦葉白佛：如來憐愍一切眾生，不調能調、未脫能脫，善星是佛菩薩時子，斷欲界結，證得四禪，如來何故記說是一闡提，地獄劫住不可治人？何不先為演說正法後為菩薩？若不能救善星比丘，云何得名有大慈悲有大方便？佛言：善男子！譬如有人唯有三子，一者有信順心，恭敬父母；利根智慧，於世間事能悉了知。其第二子無信順心，不敬父母；利根智慧，於世間事能悉了知。其第三子不敬父母，無信順心；鈍根無智。父母教告應先教誰？迦葉白佛：應先初、次及第二、後及第三。而彼二子雖無信順恭敬之心，以慈念故次復教之。如來亦爾，其三子者先譬菩薩、次譬聲聞、後譬闡提，如脩多羅中微細之義，我先已為諸菩薩說，淺近之義為聲聞說，世間之義為闡提說。今雖無益，作後世因。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, citing the parallel, in two: first concluding in general that they are the same; then citing texts to explain one by one. First, "One should know that the Avataṃsaka simile accords in meaning with the Nirvāṇa Sūtra": the Avataṃsaka similes of the sun rising and so on accord in meaning with the five flavors of the Nirvāṇa, differing only in breadth and brevity; so level ground is opened broadly into three similes, likening the three flavors. Next, in citing texts to prove sameness, "the three kinds of sons, the three kinds of fields, the three kinds of horses" and so on: the Nirvāṇa says: "Kāśyapa said to the Buddha: The Tathāgata has compassion for all beings; he can tame the untamed and liberate the unliberated. Sunakṣatra was the Buddha's son when he was a bodhisattva; he severed the bonds of the desire realm and realized the four dhyānas. Why did the Tathāgata predict that he is an icchantika, an incurable person who will remain in hell for a kalpa? Why did he not first teach him the true Dharma, and only afterward the bodhisattvas? If he could not save the bhikṣu Sunakṣatra, how can he be said to have great kindness and great compassion, and great skillful means? The Buddha said: Good man, it is as if a man had only three sons. The first has a mind of faith and compliance and respects his parents; he has sharp faculties and wisdom, and understands all worldly affairs. The second son has no mind of faith or compliance and does not respect his parents; he has sharp faculties and wisdom, and understands all worldly affairs. The third son does not respect his parents and has no mind of faith or compliance; he has dull faculties and no wisdom. When the parents give instruction, whom should they teach first? Kāśyapa said to the Buddha: They should teach the first first, next the second, and last the third. Though those two sons have no mind of faith, compliance, or respect, out of loving concern they teach them next. The Tathāgata is likewise. Of the three sons, the first likens bodhisattvas, the next śrāvakas, the last icchantikas. The subtle meanings in the sūtras I have already taught first to the bodhisattvas; the shallow and near meanings I teach to the śrāvakas; the worldly meanings I teach to the icchantikas. Though it is of no benefit now, it becomes a cause for later lives.

Chinese Source

善男子！譬如三田：一者渠流便易，無諸沙鹵瓦石棘刺，種一得百；二者雖無沙鹵瓦石棘刺，渠流嶮難，收實減半；三者渠流嶮難，多諸沙鹵，種一得一。又有三器：一者完，二者漏，三者破。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good man, it is like three fields. The first has channels that flow easily and no sand, salt, tiles, stones, or thorns; sow one and reap a hundred. The second, though it has no sand, salt, tiles, stones, or thorns, has channels that are hard to manage; the harvest is cut by half. Reading Notes for Fascicle 2

Translator's notes

嘙啝 / 婆和. Zhanran's lemma writes 嘙啝 where the Taishō Profound Meaning has 婆和; both are sound-words for a child's babbling. The site's Profound Meaning already cites Zhanran's gloss of this phrase in a reading note.

"Forever separate." The question in paragraph 4 reads 「華嚴鹿苑大小永融」, literally "the great and small were forever fused." The sense of the question requires "forever separate" (隔), and 融 is probably a copying error; translated by the sense.

The gnomon story. "Dharma Master Yan Guan" (嚴觀) is probably Huiyan and Huiguan (慧嚴、慧觀), the Song-dynasty pair often named together; the conversation with He Chengtian (何承天) about the summer-solstice shadow is told in Huiyan's biography in the Biographies of Eminent Monks (T2059). The line "As when the sun is at noon, there is no place that is not south," attributed to Dharma Master Rui (叡), was not located.

Where the five flavors are in the Nirvāṇa. Zhanran places the milk-to-ghee passage at the end of the "Noble Practice" chapter in the thirteenth fascicle. That matches the southern edition (T375, fascicle 13); in the northern edition (T374) it falls in fascicle 14.

The three sons, fields, vessels, patients, and horses. The long quotation in paragraphs 12–13 is from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's chapter on Kāśyapa; Zhanran condenses it.

Fazang's four explanations. "Dharma Master Zang of recent times" is Fazang (法藏, 643–712) of the Huayan school; the four explanations (place, buddha, time, dharma-realm) are from his commentary on the Avataṃsaka. Zhanran's objection is that all four speak only from the Buddha's side and ignore the capacities of beings.

The Great Treatise quotations. The passages on the manifest and secret Dharma-wheels (fascicle 65) and on the secret and non-secret voice (fascicle 31) are condensed paraphrases of the Dazhidu lun (T1509).

The blind man and the lame man. The Śata-śāstra (T1569) passage is quoted closely: 「如盲跛，二觸二思惟故法應能去。身神無二……」. Zhanran borrows only the image, not the outsider's argument about a self.

"As will be explained" (云云). In paragraph 62 Zhanran says that wherever the Profound Meaning writes 云云, it is to be read as explained in his Record on fascicle one of Calming and Contemplation. The site's Profound Meaning renders 云云 as "as will be explained"; that rendering is kept here.

"Transforming others takes the virtue of severance as the cardinal doctrine." In paragraph 74 Zhanran glosses the Profound Meaning's "wisdom and severance together are the cardinal doctrine" this way. Since the Profound Meaning itself pairs the virtue of severance with the function, 宗 here may be a slip for 用; translated as written.

Eye and wisdom. In paragraph 85 Zhanran explains the four characters 眼慧明聞 as two pairs read across one character: 眼…明 ("eye … clear") and 慧…聞 ("wisdom … learning"). The site's Profound Meaning renders the phrase as "the eye of wisdom and clear learning."
