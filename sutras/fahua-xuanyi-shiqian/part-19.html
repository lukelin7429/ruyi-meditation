Classifying the Teachings: The General Sense, the Southern Three and Northern Seven, and Clarifying the Difficulties

Fascicle nineteen opens the fifth and last of the five profound meanings, Classifying the Teachings, and covers the first three of its five items on the site's fascicle-010: the general sense, setting forth the differences, and most of clarifying the difficulties. Zhanran first explains why the Lotus, unlike other sūtras, cannot be expounded without the marks of the teaching, and reads the "general sense" as a survey of how each earlier sūtra leaves three intents unfinished: it does not take in every capacity, does not open the provisional, and does not bring forth the trace. He sets out the threefold and fourfold entreaties of the Lotus against the entreaty to Vajragarbha in the Avataṃsaka, insisting that the likeness is in the entreaty, not in the dharma taught. On the "Southern three and Northern seven" he gives a short history of the Three Treatises and the Chengshi in the south and denies that Tiantai is simply a "southern school." The rest of the fascicle is a close commentary on the refutations: the five periods of the south and the misuse of the Nirvāṇa's five flavors, the Trapuṣa Sūtra as a "teaching for humans and devas," Bodhiruci's half and full teaching, and the four doctrines, parsed character by character. It breaks off in the refutation of the five doctrines.

Ru-Yi Translation

The fifth, the marks of the teaching, in two: first making clear the reason for coming; next, setting out the sections and explaining them. The teaching is what expresses; why set up [the claim] that the true doctrine has no teaching? Since it also reaches the small, the ignorant and untutored have a teaching; why set up [the claim] that there is no doctrine but only teaching? Those people do not know that the true of the three vehicles includes both the empty and the not-empty, and that the not-empty again has the two differences of teaching and realization. Not seeing this, they explain the meaning by the sound of the words, dividing doctrine and teaching in vain, and lose the purport all the more. Fourth, from "If this is so" on, the challenge by example: following their setup for now, it challenges the two doctrines of causes and conditions and provisional names, [showing that] they equally become untrue; if one further compares and decides, one should follow the preceding; so it says "inconvenient."

Reading Notes for Fascicle 19

Translator's notes

The "newly translated" Avataṃsaka. In paragraph 4 Zhanran outlines the eighty-fascicle Avataṃsaka (Śikṣānanda's "new translation"). His own chapter counts add up to 39 (6+6+6+4+3+1+11+1+1), and the sūtra in fact has 39 chapters in nine assemblies, but he says "thirty-seven chapters." Translated as written.

"Three refusals and four entreaties." Paragraph 14 numbers the refusals and entreaties of the "Skillful Means" chapter in a way that is hard to follow (two items are both called "fourth"). The numbering is kept exactly as written.

"Second, concluding with an exhortation" (paragraph 18). Paragraph 3 had called this the third part ("third, from 'If one is able' on, concluding with an exhortation"). The "second" may be a copying error, or Zhanran may be counting differently here. Translated as written.

The four disciples of Sengquan (paragraph 19). The saying lists the four students of Sengquan of Zhiguan Temple, usually identified as Falang of Xinghuang, Huibu of Qixia, Zhibian of Changgan, and Huiyong of Chanzhong. The "Master Lang of Goguryeo" earlier in the paragraph is Senglang, a different person. The identifications come from the later tradition and are not in Zhanran's text.

"As for the marks of dharmas, the followers of [our] school…" (paragraph 19). 若法相宗徒多依《大論》 could also be read "the followers of the Faxiang school mostly rely on…." The translation takes 宗徒 as "the followers of [our] school," because the sentence goes on to explain what Tiantai itself relies on. The other reading should be weighed.

The sandalwood ear (paragraph 20). 食檀耳 refers to Cunda's last meal, which included the "sandalwood-tree ear" (a kind of mushroom). Zhanran's point is that an event of the Nirvāṇa period also appears in the Āgamas.

The unmanifest karma (paragraph 25). 無作之業 here is the Abhidharma's "unmanifest" (avijñapti) karma of the precepts, which is said to continue into the future. It is not the 無作 ("without fabrication") of the four kinds of four truths, so the settled rendering is not used here.

The dog and the elephant (paragraph 27). Both similes are in the Larger Prajñā (T223), fascicle 13, as Zhanran says: the dog that "does not seek food from its master but begs from the servants," and the man who "wishes to see an elephant, and having seen it, looks back at its tracks." Zhanran's 見象 ("seeing the elephant") follows the sūtra. The site's Profound Meaning has 見像 and "seeing only the image".

Punctuation of 《淨名》、《金剛》 (paragraph 30). The CBETA punctuation makes 金剛 a sūtra title. In context Zhanran seems to mean the "vajra [body]" spoken of in the Vimalakīrti (the passage on Ānanda begging for milk), set against the "seven hundred [asaṃkhyeyas]" of the Śūraṅgama-samādhi. The translation follows that reading.

The Milk-Light Sūtra (paragraph 30). The long quotation is an abridgment of T809 (佛說乳光佛經). For example, the sūtra gives the brahman five hundred close disciples but fifty thousand followers in all; 醒穢 is read as 腥穢 ("foul filth"). The verse line 與乳作乳種 is translated literally.

Fascicle numbers for the Nirvāṇa Sūtra. The forbearance-grass passage (paragraph 36, "fascicle twenty-five") is in fascicle 27 of the southern text (T375) and fascicle 29 of the northern text (T374). The "seventh fascicle" on the nine divisions (paragraph 36) and the "third fascicle" on the bodhisattvas' questions (paragraph 42) match T375. The fingernail-soil passage (paragraph 31, "fascicle thirty-two") is in T375 fascicle 31 and T374 fascicle 33. These numbers have been left as Zhanran gives them.

Śrāvastī and Ajātaśatru (paragraph 35). In the summary of the Sūtra of the Girl Vimaladattā, the king is Ajātaśatru, but Śāriputra wishes the beings of "Śrāvastī" to hear the four truths. Translated as written; the extant versions (T338, T339, and the Ratnakūṭa) were not compared in detail.

The Chengshi three causes (paragraph 43). "The object-condition is as when consciousness gives rise to eye-consciousness" (緣緣者如識生眼識) is odd; one would expect form (色) giving rise to eye-consciousness. It may be a copying error. Translated as written.

The character-count parsing (paragraphs 46–47). Zhanran counts the characters of each clause of the Profound Meaning's argument. The quoted English is the site's, but its clause boundaries do not always match his counts. In particular, his sixteen-character clause ends with 通不真宗教、通真宗教, which the site splits differently. The character counts are his and refer to the Chinese.

說通 / 宗通 (paragraph 47). Zhanran's quotation of the Laṅkāvatāra has 說通兼教童蒙 (with 兼), and he explains it as "the explanation-penetration" and "the doctrine-penetration" (deśanā-naya and siddhānta-naya). "Penetration" is used for 通 here, to keep these terms apart from "the shared teaching" (通教).
