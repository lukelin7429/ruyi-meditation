The Two Truths, the Compendium of the Mahāyāna, and the Three, One, and No Truth

Fascicle six comments on the last part of the Profound Meaning's second fascicle and the opening of its third (fascicle-002 and fascicle-003 on this site), all within the wondrous of the object. Zhanran first finishes the three ways of teaching the two truths — according with feeling, feeling-and-wisdom, and wisdom — and then explains the seven kinds of two truths: why seven, how Zhang'an matched them to the eight pairs of two truths in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, the three readings of "neither tainted nor taintless," and the doctrine of "receiving" (接), by which a hearer of a lower teaching is drawn into a higher one. He then judges them coarse and wondrous by the seven levels and the five flavors, and comments at length on the "lead-in" (弄引) to the Lotus, which he extends back past the site of awakening to the Buddha's first vow. Two polemical passages follow: against those who say the Lotus only predicts the śrāvakas' buddhahood, and against the Compendium of the Mahāyāna's "ten supreme marks," which Zhanran measures against the ten wondrous aspects. The fascicle ends with the three truths, the one truth (with the Nirvāṇa's drunken man who sees the sun turning), "no truth," and the opening and combining of all six objects.

The Two Truths: According with Wisdom

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "according with wisdom": setting aside what the minds of worldly people perceive, it makes clear solely the two truths perceived by the sages, as a round of its own.

Ru-Yi Translation

Within this, five parts: first, setting up its features; next, from "It is like an eye" on, a simile; third, from "Again, it is like" on, citing an event; fourth, from "The Abhidharma" on, revealing again by simile that the two truths are deep and superior; fifth, from "The sūtra says" on, citing the teaching and concluding the name. The Buddha dwells at the highest stage, so the highest stage is raised to prove that wisdom is superior and to conclude how it gets its name. In the first, "awakened to principle" is seeing the real, and further being able to understand the conventional truth; so one knows that according with wisdom fully has the two truths. In the simile, "form and emptiness" likens the two truths. In citing the event, a near event is cited to compare a far principle: entering dhyāna is like "awakening to the real," and body and mind open and empty is like "understanding the conventional." In revealing by simile, the delusions of views are like the "small cloud" and the delusions of thought like the "large cloud"; "taintless" means seeing the real, and "worldly wisdom" means understanding the conventional. In concluding the name, the ordinary person's loss is raised to reveal the sage's gain; so one knows the sage has both truths. The fourth, the concluding passage, can be seen.

The Two Truths Themselves: Why Seven Kinds

Chinese Source

次正明二諦者，於中為四：先明來意，次明功能，三「所言」下廣釋，四「問答」下問答料簡。初文者，凡諸釋義或從廣之略、或先略後廣，從略即預用跨節，從廣則教門當分，故略言之唯一法性以對無明，無明是迷真之始，法性則全指無明無始時來奚甞非真，未發心前無真不俗，秖點一法，二諦宛然。俗即百界千如，真即同居一念，仍顯相異，義理雖足其如麁淺，聞之墮苦，故佛於一代曲開七重、二十一重以赴物情，使佛本懷暢、使物宿種遂故。以下二門判之開之，意如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the two truths themselves, in four parts: first, making clear the purpose of its coming; next, making clear its capability; third, from "As for what is called" on, explaining at length; fourth, from "Question" on, sifting by question and answer. In the first: in all explanations, one either goes from the broad to the brief or first gives the brief and then the broad; going from the brief, one uses in advance what skips over the sections; going from the broad, the teaching gates each stay in their own portion. So, speaking briefly, there is only the one dharma-nature set against ignorance. Ignorance is the beginning of confusion about the real; dharma-nature points wholly to ignorance — from beginningless time, when was it ever not real? Before arousing the mind, there is no real that is not the conventional; pointing to just one dharma, the two truths are distinctly there. The conventional is the hundred realms and thousand suchnesses; the real is the same single thought, still revealing their difference. Though the meaning is sufficient, what of the coarse and shallow? On hearing it they would fall into suffering. So the Buddha, over a lifetime, carefully opened seven levels and twenty-one levels to meet beings' feelings, so that the Buddha's original intent might be freely expressed and the old seeds of beings brought to fruition. The judging and opening by the two gates below follow the meaning stated above.

Third, the explanation proper, in two: first listing, then explaining. The meaning of the first: one, the Tripiṭaka; two, the shared; three, the distinct received into the shared; four, the perfect received into the shared; five, the distinct; six, the perfect received into the distinct; seven, the perfect. In Calming and Contemplation, to complete the contemplation of principle, only the principle outside the realms is used to receive the principle within the realms; so the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings make clear the principle within the realms, the distinct and perfect make clear the principle outside the realms, and the shared and distinct make clear where the two principles meet; therefore it makes clear only the distinct received into the shared. Here the first six levels still keep the teaching-path, meeting those provisional capacities before the Lotus; so there are the two senses of the perfect received into the shared and into the distinct. In the real path, only the perfect principle should receive the provisional; so in explaining the present text one should follow the teaching-path, and again take the perfect middle as receiving the mere middle. Again, though these seven names establish two truths, the meanings of the latter five already contain three: illusory existence is the conventional, emptiness is the real, not-empty is the middle. Just look at the names: into which truth is emptiness combined? If combined into the conventional, it is like the distinct teaching's name, two truths in which the real is contained and enters the conventional; if combined into the real, it is like the distinct and the perfect entering the shared, two truths whose name contains the middle entering the real. The Tripiṭaka and the shared are named a single conventional and a single real; the perfect teaching is named the inconceivable real and conventional.

The Eight Pairs in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra

Chinese Source

次解釋中文自為七，若欲憑教者，然此七文散在諸經，無一處具出，唯《大經》十二，四諦文後列八二諦，章安作七二諦銷之，初一是總，餘七是別。初云「如出世人心所見者，名第一義諦，世人心所見者，名為世諦。」疏云總冠諸諦，世情多種束為世諦，聖智多知束為第一義諦，即是諸教隨情智也。經云「五陰和合稱名某甲，是名世諦；解陰無陰亦無名字，離陰亦無是名第一義諦。」陰是實法、某甲是假名，即實有俗諦也。無陰無名即假實空，若離陰者名太虛空，是故離陰亦無某甲及二諦名。疏云名無名二諦也。世諦有名、真諦無名，即生滅二諦也。經云「或有法有名有實是名第一義諦，或有法有名無實是名世諦。」幻化假名即空，故實即真諦也。秖指幻化，但有假名，故名世諦，故疏云實不實二諦也。真實幻化不實也，即無生二諦也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the explanation falls into seven by itself. If one wishes to rely on the teaching: these seven passages are scattered through the sūtras, and nowhere are they given all together. Only in the twelfth [fascicle] of the Great Sūtra, after the passage on the four truths, are eight [kinds of] two truths listed, and Zhang'an digested them as seven two truths, the first being general and the other seven specific. First it says: "What the minds of those who have transcended the world perceive is named the truth of ultimate meaning; what worldly people's minds perceive is named the conventional truth." The commentary says this crowns all the truths in general: the many kinds of worldly feeling are bundled together as the conventional truth, and the many kinds of sagely knowing as the truth of ultimate meaning — that is, the teachings' according with feeling and with wisdom. The sūtra says: "The five aggregates combined are called so-and-so; this is called the conventional truth. Understanding that in the aggregates there are no aggregates, and no name either, and that apart from the aggregates there is nothing either — this is called the truth of ultimate meaning." The aggregates are real dharmas and "so-and-so" is a provisional name; this is the really existent conventional truth. No aggregates and no name is the emptiness of the provisional and the real; if apart from the aggregates, it would be called great empty space; therefore apart from the aggregates there is also no so-and-so, nor the names of the two truths. The commentary calls this the two truths of name and no-name. The conventional truth has a name and the real truth has no name — these are the two truths of arising and ceasing. The sūtra says: "Some dharmas have a name and have reality; this is called the truth of ultimate meaning. Some dharmas have a name but no reality; this is called the conventional truth." Illusory transformations and provisional names are precisely empty, so their "reality" is the real truth. Pointing only to illusory transformations, which have only provisional names, is called the conventional truth; so the commentary calls them the two truths of real and unreal. The true is real, and illusory transformation unreal — these are the two truths of non-arising.

經云「如我人眾生壽命知見，乃至如龜毛兔角等，陰界入是名世諦，苦集滅道是名真諦。」兔角之俗與前不殊，故成單俗，四諦義含共為真諦，即是含中真諦也。故疏云定不定二諦也。即單俗、複真，俗是不定、中道名定。經云「世法有五種：謂名世、句世、縛世、法世、執著世，是名世諦。經文廣解，於此五法心無顛倒，名第一義諦。」五種世法名與前異，大意不別亦是單俗，於世無倒謂見實相，教道但中雖未究竟，此中究竟望前故實，故疏云法不法二諦也。法謂實相，不法謂俗，亦是含中二諦也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The sūtra says: "Self, person, sentient being, life, knower, seer, and so on to tortoise hair and rabbit horns, and the aggregates, sense-fields, and elements — this is called the conventional truth; suffering, its origin, cessation, and the path are called the real truth." The convention of rabbit horns is no different from before, so it forms a single conventional; the four truths contain [the middle] in meaning and together are the real truth — this is the real truth that contains the middle. So the commentary calls them the two truths of fixed and unfixed: that is, a single conventional and a compound real, the conventional being unfixed and the middle way being called fixed. The sūtra says: "Worldly dharmas are of five kinds: namely the world of names, the world of phrases, the world of bonds, the world of dharmas, and the world of attachment; this is called the conventional truth." The sūtra explains these at length. "Having no inverted mind toward these five dharmas is called the truth of ultimate meaning." The names of the five kinds of worldly dharmas differ from before, but the main meaning is no different; it is also a single conventional. Not being inverted toward the world means seeing the true mark; the mere middle of the teaching-path, though not yet ultimate, is ultimate here compared with what precedes, hence real. So the commentary calls them the two truths of dharma and non-dharma: "dharma" means the true mark and "non-dharma" the conventional — these too are the two truths containing the middle.

經云「燒割死壞是名世諦，無燒割死等是名第一義諦。」地前方便皆屬無常故云可燒，登地見常故無燒等，故疏云燒不燒二諦也。無常可燒，常不可燒，複俗單中二諦也。經云「有八種苦是名世諦，無八種苦故是第一義。」八苦無常同前燒義，無八苦真即是實相，故疏云苦不苦二諦，亦是複俗單中。教道有苦，圓中無苦。經云「譬如一人有多名字，依父母生是名世諦，依十二緣和合生者名第一義諦。」依父母生即十二緣，而分二者，以《大經》中明十二緣即佛性故，且據顯說，即以佛性而為真諦，依父母生即是無明名為世諦。故疏云和合二諦，真俗不二故名和合，複俗複中二諦也。後乃結云今七二諦來銷此文，佛旨難知且用一師意耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

The sūtra says: "Burning, cutting, dying, breaking — this is called the conventional truth; no burning, cutting, dying, and so on is called the truth of ultimate meaning." The skillful means before the grounds all belong to impermanence, hence "can be burned"; on reaching the grounds one sees the eternal, hence no burning and so on. So the commentary calls them the two truths of burning and not burning: the impermanent can be burned and the eternal cannot — these are the two truths of a compound conventional and a single middle. The sūtra says: "Having the eight kinds of suffering is called the conventional truth; having no eight kinds of suffering is the ultimate meaning." The impermanence of the eight sufferings is the same as the meaning of burning above, and the real without the eight sufferings is the true mark; so the commentary calls them the two truths of suffering and non-suffering — also a compound conventional and a single middle. The teaching-path has suffering; the perfect middle has no suffering. The sūtra says: "It is like one person having many names: being born of father and mother is called the conventional truth; being born of the combination of the twelve links is called the truth of ultimate meaning." Being born of father and mother is the twelve links; yet they are divided into two because the Great Sūtra makes clear that the twelve links are buddha-nature. Speaking for now manifestly, buddha-nature is taken as the real truth, and being born of father and mother is ignorance, called the conventional truth. So the commentary calls them the two truths of combination: the real and the conventional being nondual is called combination — the two truths of a compound conventional and a compound middle. Afterward it concludes: "Now the seven two truths are used to digest this text; the Buddha's intent is hard to know, and for now the view of one master is used."

The Three Readings of "Neither Tainted nor Taintless"

Chinese Source

次釋接義者，即含中入真也。於中為三：初以一法標，次「俗不」下略以三法示，三「其相」下辨相，通寄三法以辨其相。漏無漏本是通法，為成接義故立雙非。空不空本是別法，一切法趣本是圓法，於一一法，各有三人取解不同者，良由機發故所聞不同。又通教菩薩由根利鈍發習不同，故鈍同二乘，直至法華方乃被會；利者爾前接入中道，故使同觀幻有之俗而契真各異。所以別圓機發對鈍住空，致成三別。是以釋後二接須對通鈍，共成三人同聞異聽，故約漏等以示解源。若得此意，於一切法無礙自在。於中為四：初正釋三相，次「無量」下明三意，三「何故」下釋疑，四「大品」下引證三人。初文又二：初正釋，次「是故」下結。初文自有三別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the meaning of "receiving": this is the real that contains the middle. Within this three: first, marking it by one dharma; next, from "the conventional does not" on, showing it briefly by three dharmas; third, from "What is the mark" on, distinguishing the features, using the three dharmas in general to distinguish their marks. Tainted and taintless are originally a dharma of the shared teaching; to form the meaning of receiving, the double negation is set up. Empty and not-empty are originally a dharma of the distinct teaching; "all dharmas converge" is originally a dharma of the perfect teaching. In each dharma the three persons understand differently, truly because capacities arise and so what is heard differs. Again, the bodhisattvas of the shared teaching differ in sharp and dull faculties and in the habits aroused; so the dull are the same as the two vehicles, and only at the Lotus are they merged; the sharp are received into the middle way before that. Explanatory Notes on the Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra, Fascicle Six [ends]

Reading Notes for Fascicle 6

Translator's notes

"From Kakuda to the attachment to the perfect." The phrase 「始自迦羅、終至圓著」 refers to Calming and Contemplation's survey of mistaken views, from those of the non-Buddhist teachers (迦羅, probably Kakuda Kātyāyana) to attachment to the perfect teaching itself. The identification of 迦羅 is uncertain.

The eight pairs in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra. Zhanran's long paragraph 7 follows the northern Nirvāṇa (T374), fascicle 12, and Guanding's commentary on it (T1767), which reads the eight pairs as one general and seven specific kinds of two truths. The sūtra quotations are condensed.

"Much is missing." The commentary's answer 「天台師云多有所關」 is read with 關 as a slip for 闕 ("missing"): the sūtras that would record the occasion have not all reached China.

The school that holds some lack the nature (無性宗家). This refers to the Yogācāra position, associated with Xuanzang's school, that some beings lack the nature for buddhahood; Zhanran answers it with the Lotus's promise that even those who have entered extinction "can hear this sūtra" in another land.

Song of Pengcheng. "Dharma Master Song of Pengcheng Monastery" is Sengsong (僧嵩), remembered in Buddhist histories for holding that the Buddha was impermanent; the story that his tongue rotted before death is told of him in the Biographies of Eminent Monks.

The precious ransom for life. The quotation is from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, fascicle 14 (northern edition, "Noble Practice" chapter), condensed. Zhanran's own explanation distinguishes a "compound" reading (the Nirvāṇa protects both the vehicle and the precepts) and a "single" reading (it protects the precepts).

The ten supreme marks. The opening of the Compendium of the Mahāyāna is quoted from Paramārtha's translation (T1593/T1595 tradition). "Paramārtha relies on the amala, the later translations on the ālaya" refers to the debate over whether the ninth (amala) or eighth (ālaya) consciousness is the "basis."

The Vibhāṣā on "one truth." The long quotation in paragraph 42 is from the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā (T1545); the bracketed sentence "Combining the four truths into two above uses precisely this meaning" is Zhanran's own interjection in the middle of the quotation.

無作: "uncreated" or "without fabrication." The site's Profound Meaning renders 無作 as "uncreated" in fascicle-003 but "without fabrication" elsewhere; this translation keeps the site's word inside quoted lemmas and uses "without fabrication" (the term already chosen for this project) in Zhanran's own sentences. The site may want to make these consistent.
