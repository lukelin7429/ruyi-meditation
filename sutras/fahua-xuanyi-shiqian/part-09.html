The Wondrous of Practice: The Wisdom Holy Practice, the Twenty-Five Samādhis, and the Five Practices

Fascicle nine comments on most of the wondrous of practice, the third of the ten wondrous aspects (fascicle-004 on this site), which the Profound Meaning builds on the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's five practices: the holy practice (precepts, concentration, wisdom), the brahma practice, the heavenly practice, the infant practice, and the sick practice. Zhanran begins with the wisdom holy practice — the four kinds of four truths as the bodhisattva of the distinct teaching practices them — explaining, for example, why the Profound Meaning's text should read without the character 不, how its list of "never" clauses gives the ten wholesome deeds, and the Nirvāṇa's parable of the strongman whose jewel sank into his forehead. He then explains the "ground of fearlessness" and the five fears through the Bodhisattva-bhūmi and the Ten Grounds, and the twenty-five samādhis that break the twenty-five states of existence, with long glosses on Vasu and Devadatta in hell and on the bodhisattva who takes the form of an animal or a hungry ghost. The last third covers the brahma, heavenly, infant, and sick practices — including a gloss on "three in the morning, four in the evening" — and the perfect five practices, where "one practice is all practices." The fascicle closes with a warning against practitioners who "wholly esteem principle" and those who "wholly esteem phenomena."

The Wisdom Holy Practice

◎次明慧聖行，中二：先標列，次釋。釋中但釋四種四諦、不復判開者，前之兩行並在外凡，雖辨淺深，初心立信須明濃淡以標遠志。今慧聖行始從初住終至初地，行自親證故不勞判，況歷位竪深義同四教已當於判，初地證同義當於開，是故此行不復辨開。若欲判之，住前如乳，生滅等四如後四味。所言開者，至初地時，地前諸行皆入初地薩婆若海，變為無緣一切法相，具如後辨。今此四四諦雖並在別教，既約生滅等四竪判一教，別教菩薩即此自行而用化他，故十行中橫辨此四全為利物，所以四相各附彼教而為相狀，故初用生滅教慈悲六度在道諦中明，次無生無量中慈悲六度在滅諦後明，次無作中於四諦後略言無緣慈悲及明攝諸度及離怖畏得二十五三昧。又復四中皆以苦集二諦並苦前集後，道滅並以道前滅後。所以爾者，生滅等四智雖巧拙，莫不發足皆知苦斷集，先廣習道法後取涅槃，故與常儀所列稍異。生滅菩薩既不斷惑，故道諦中具諸願行無生無量，並斷惑已方乃赴難。無作既在初地已證，以願滿故且略不論。真如分滿分具諸行，是故於此但明廣攝。若通論願始並在戒聖行初，此中分進，分論故爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the wisdom holy practice, in two: first the heading and list, then the explanation. The explanation explains only the four kinds of four truths and does not further judge or open, because the two preceding practices are both at the outer ordinary stages; though their depth was distinguished, for the initial mind establishing faith one must make clear the dense and the thin so as to mark out the far aspiration. Now the wisdom holy practice runs from the first abiding to the first ground; the practice is realized personally, so there is no need to judge — all the more since, going vertically through the stages, its meaning is like the four teachings and already amounts to judging, and since the first ground's realization is the same, its meaning amounts to opening; therefore this practice does not further distinguish opening. If one wished to judge it, before the abidings is like milk, and generation-and-extinction and the other three are like the later four flavors. As for opening: on reaching the first ground, all the practices before the grounds enter the first ground's ocean of sarvajña and turn into the objectless mark of all dharmas, fully as explained later. These four kinds of four truths, though all in the distinct teaching, judge one teaching vertically by generation-and-extinction and the other four; the distinct bodhisattva uses this very self-practice to transform others; so in the ten practices the four are distinguished horizontally wholly to benefit beings; hence the four marks each follow their own teaching for their features. So first, the kindness, compassion, and six perfections of the generation-and-extinction teaching are made clear within the truth of the path; next, in no generation and the immeasurable, kindness, compassion, and the six perfections are made clear after the truth of cessation; next, in the unconditioned, after the four truths it speaks briefly of objectless kindness and compassion, and makes clear the including of the perfections, freedom from fears, and attaining the twenty-five samādhis. Again, in all four, of suffering and its arising, suffering comes first and arising after; of the path and cessation, the path comes first and cessation after. The reason: though the four wisdoms of generation-and-extinction and the rest are skillful or clumsy, none, on setting out, fails to know suffering and sever its arising, first broadly practicing the dharmas of the path and afterward taking nirvāṇa; so it differs somewhat from the usual listing. Since the bodhisattva of generation-and-extinction does not sever delusion, within the truth of the path he has all the vows and practices; [those of] no generation and the immeasurable go to meet [beings'] difficulties only after severing delusion. The unconditioned, already realized at the first ground, its vow fulfilled, is for now not discussed. Suchness, partially full, partially possesses all practices; therefore here it only makes clear the broad including. Speaking generally, the start of the vows lies wholly at the beginning of the precept holy practice; here it advances in portions, so it is discussed in portions.

The three sufferings are as in my Record on the seventh fascicle of Calming and Contemplation.

「以不起迷著依正」者，此中恐剩「不」字。此集諦慧中先明三惡由迷依正，次明三善由達世間因果過故不行十惡及以十善，善之與惡俱是生長之相，故知剩「不」字。「終不」下至「邪僻失真」，明十善業道，以一「終不」字冠下諸句，終不殺他活己，不殺也。終不奪彼潤身，不盜也。終不耽湎不淨，不婬也。終不隱曲求直，不妄也。終不離合至榮辱，不兩舌也，謂離親合怨間榮搆辱。終不內諂外佞，不綺語也。闕不惡口，以兩舌兼之。終不引納無度，不貪也。終不縱毒傷道，不瞋也。終不邪僻失真，不邪見也。「生長等相」者，以由集故復至來世，故名為「長」。由集輪迴，故名為「轉」。遍起有因，故「云二十五有相」。道滅諦慧中俱云「除相」者，除是滅家之相，苦集是所除，道是能除，滅是除已，故無能字。從所滅為名，故云「除相」。

Ru-Yi Translation

"By failing to refrain from (不起) delusion and attachment to the dependent and direct retributions": here I suspect the character 不 is superfluous. In this wisdom of the truth of arising, it first makes clear that the three evil destinies come from delusion about the dependent and the direct; next it makes clear that the three good destinies come from penetrating the faults of worldly cause and effect, so not doing the ten evils and practicing the ten goods; good and evil are both marks of arising and growth; so one knows the character 不 is superfluous. From "In the end, one no longer" to "strays into deviance and loses the real" makes clear the ten wholesome paths of action, with the one word "never" (終不) crowning all the following phrases. "Never kills another to sustain oneself" is not killing. "Never seizes another's [goods] to enrich one's body" is not stealing. "Never indulges immersed in impurity" is not sexual misconduct. "Never twists the crooked in search of the straight" is not lying. "Never separates and reunites … honor and disgrace" is not divisive speech, that is, separating those who are close and joining enemies, scheming between honor and disgrace. "Never flatters within while cheating without" is not frivolous speech. Harsh speech is missing, included in divisive speech. "Never indulges without restraint" is not greed. "Never unleashes poison to injure the path" is not anger. "Never strays into deviance and loses the real" is not wrong view. "The mark of arising and growth" and so on: because of arising one reaches the next life again, hence "growth"; through arising one cycles, hence "turning"; arising everywhere as causes of existence, hence "the mark of the twenty-five states of existence." In the wisdom of the truths of the path and cessation, both say "the mark of removal": removal is the mark of cessation; suffering and arising are what is removed, the path is what removes, and cessation is removal completed; so the word "able" is absent. Named from what is extinguished, it says "the mark of removal."

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, no generation; the text has two: first the four truths, then the vows and practices. The first, the four truths, falls into four by itself, each with marking and conclusion; the text's features are easy to know. From here on it is not divided further; each has dharma, simile, and so on, which can be seen. In cessation it says, "were there even a single dharma that surpassed nirvāṇa itself, I would say that this, too, is like an illusion or a conjuring": the fiftieth [fascicle] of the Great Treatise first cites the sūtra: "The sons of the gods thought: What sort of person should hear what Subhūti teaches? Subhūti, knowing their thoughts, said: Illusory persons hear the Dharma; I would have such persons hear — no hearing, no listening, no knowing, no realizing. Subhūti said: I am like an illusion, a conjuring, and so on to the knower and seer, like an illusion, a conjuring; going through all dharmas up to the Buddha, also like an illusion, a conjuring; the dharma of nirvāṇa is also like an illusion, a conjuring." The treatise explains: "Among all beings the Buddha is foremost, and among all dharmas nirvāṇa is foremost; hearing these two things called like an illusion, they were startled and doubtful, thinking Subhūti had misspoken or the hearers had erred; therefore they asked Subhūti again. Subhūti said: Because both dharmas arise from false dharmas, dharmas belong to causes and conditions and have no fixed reality. Subhūti thought: Even if there were a dharma surpassing nirvāṇa, it could be made like an illusion — how much more nirvāṇa? Nirvāṇa is the cessation of all dharmas; therefore there is no dharma that surpasses nirvāṇa." Question: If there is no dharma surpassing nirvāṇa, why say that if there were a dharma surpassing nirvāṇa, it too would be like an illusion?

答：凡譬喻法，或以實事、或以假設。如佛說言：若令樹木解我語者，我亦說令得須陀洹。但樹木無解語者，佛為解語人引此喻耳。又須菩提欲引般若之力大故，故云設有法過涅槃者，以智慧力亦能破之，故云如幻。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Similes use either real matters or hypothetical ones. As the Buddha said: if trees could understand my words, I would say they could attain stream-entry. But trees cannot understand words; the Buddha gave this simile for those who do. Again, Subhūti wished to draw on the great power of prajñā; so he says that, supposing there were a dharma surpassing nirvāṇa, the power of wisdom could also break it; hence "like an illusion."

Ru-Yi Translation

In the vows, in terms of the path, it says "plant trees within empty space": the Great Treatise cites the sūtra: "Śāriputra said to the Buddha: What these bodhisattvas teach — whoever can understand it gains great merit. Why? Even hearing these bodhisattvas' names brings great benefit, how much more hearing what they teach? World-Honored One, as when a person plants a tree not relying on the ground and yet wishes it to get roots, stalk, branches, and leaves and to form flowers and fruit — this is hard to achieve. The nature and marks of all practices are likewise: not abiding in any dharma, yet abiding in birth-and-death; not abiding is like space, abiding in birth-and-death is like planting." The present text is likewise: though one knows the path has no curer and nothing cured, no saver and no one saved, one constantly cultivates the path to cure suffering and its arising and to benefit beings; subject and object both empty, yet curing and saving are both like illusions; hence nothing is attained. Therefore the bodhisattva uses the illusory dharma-gate to break the illusory delusion and illusory great compassion to benefit illusory sentient beings; the work for self and others complete, in illusory nirvāṇa, he attains without attaining anything.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the immeasurable, in two: first the four truths, then the vows. The four truths have three: first distinguishing against the small; next, from "This wisdom" on, the explanation proper; third, from "The two vehicles" on, rebuking the two vehicles to reveal the true and correct. The first has two: first citing the sūtra; next, from "If this were" on, explaining the sūtra's meaning. The first: first Kāśyapa's challenge, then the Buddha's answer. The challenge says "what remains untaught is as vast as the lands of the ten directions": as in my Record on the seventh fascicle of Calming and Contemplation. Next, the four truths, as the text says. In revealing the correct: the "pulse" is suffering, the "sickness" arising, the "medicine" the path, and the "cure" cessation. Again, "diagnose … pulse" raises in general the method of curing illness. "Recognize … sickness" is like knowing the illness in Calming and Contemplation; "refine … medicine" like recognizing the medicine there; "achieve … cure" like giving the medicine there. Next, among the vows and practices, "brings every manner of sentient being to maturity, and purifies every manner of buddha-land," as in my Record on the second fascicle of Calming and Contemplation. In sum, the wisdom of the immeasurable four truths is fully as in the pervasive refutation of dharmas in going from emptiness into the provisional.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, in the unconditioned it says only that suffering and its arising are the path and cessation: since arousing the mind, who does not, for the sake of severing afflictions and birth-and-death, broadly gather the Buddha-dharmas and seek the unsurpassed path? But the meaning of this teaching penetrates the essence of the path and cessation right within birth-and-death and afflictions; hence "sought within the mental activity of sentient beings." This is true knowing, true severing, wondrous cultivation, realizing principle; so one knows that in vertical practice, the abidings and practices are still confused, the dedications know a little, and the first ground realizes a little — this is its meaning. Within this two: first explaining the wisdom of the four truths itself; next, from "Contemplating in this way" on, making clear the completion of contemplation, with practices, vows, and so on. The first two: first establishing in general; next, from "The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra" on, explaining. The explanation again three: first explaining the causes and conditions of understanding and delusion; next, from "If this is so" on, explaining how the four are formed; third, from "The Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra says" on, explaining "unconditioned." The first two: first explaining, then identity. The first again two: first delusion, then understanding. The first is suffering and arising. "A jewel is present within one's own body" and so on: the eighth [fascicle] of the Nirvāṇa, the "Tathāgata-Nature" chapter, says: "It is like a strongman of the royal house who had a vajra jewel between his brows. Wrestling with another strongman, the jewel was struck and sank into his skin, and he did not know where the jewel was. There was a sore there, and he had a physician treat it. The physician, skilled in remedies, knew the sore had been caused by the jewel entering, and that the jewel, having entered the skin, had stayed there. The physician asked the strongman: Where is the jewel on your forehead? The strongman, startled, answered: Is the jewel on my forehead gone? Could it have been an illusion? And he wept. The physician comforted him: You should not grieve; it entered your body during the fight and is now in your skin; its reflection shows outside; in the fight the poison was strong, and you did not feel the jewel go in. The strongman did not believe him, and instead suspected the physician of deceiving him. The physician had him hold a mirror, and he saw the jewel himself." The sore from fighting is like arising, the jewel entering the body like suffering; the mirror likens the path, and the jewel cessation. "Only sorrow and grief" is like having only the three sufferings; "joy no longer" is like having no perfect understanding. If one understands that the body of the sore is the body of the jewel, one knows that suffering and its arising are the dharma-nature; because one does not know this, there are only suffering and arising. From "the path and cessation" on, identity can be understood. Delusion and understanding lie in persons; the essence is always identical.

The perfectly fused four truths are only one reality. Translator's notes

"I suspect the character 不 is superfluous." Zhanran reads the Profound Meaning's 「以不起迷著依正」 as 「以起迷著依正」 ("by giving rise to delusion and attachment"). The site's Profound Meaning keeps 不 and renders it "By failing to refrain from delusion," which yields Zhanran's sense in another way.

The ten wholesome deeds. Zhanran shows that each "never …" clause names one of the ten wholesome deeds; by his reading, 「奪彼閏身」 is "seizing another's [goods] to enrich one's own body," that is, stealing.

The strongman's jewel. The parable is quoted, condensed, from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, northern edition, fascicle 8 ("Tathāgata-Nature").

Planting trees in space. The Great Treatise passage and the sūtra it cites are condensed; Zhanran's own sentence "uses the illusory dharma-gate to break the illusory delusion" is his gloss.

The eight spiritual transformations. The two lists (Great Treatise; Nirvāṇa Sūtra, northern edition, fascicle 20 or 21 depending on edition) are given as Zhanran summarizes them.

Vasu. The Fangdeng Dhāraṇī Sūtra (大方等陀羅尼經, T1339), fascicle 1, is quoted at length; the syllable-glosses of va and su are the sūtra's.

The ten disturbances. The list in paragraph 33 is the Lotus Sūtra's "Peaceful Practices" chapter as summarized in Tiantai commentary; "Lokāyata and anti-Lokāyata" renders 路逆路.
