Trace and Origin; the Origin's Ten Wondrous Aspects; the Lotus Flower and Its Seventeen Names

Fascicle fifteen covers the origin gate's own ten wondrous aspects, fascicle-007 on this site, from "Explaining Trace and Origin" through the opening of the lotus-flower exposition and the Fahua Lun's seventeen names. Zhanran first comments on the six senses of trace and origin: he explains "the non-abiding root" as ignorance and the dharma-nature, each rootless yet the root of all dharmas; traces "inconceivably one" to Sengzhao's preface on the Vimalakīrti; and shows why only already-and-now is properly the Lotus's own origin. He then follows the ten headings of the origin's ten wondrous aspects, supplying sources for the trace accounts (the potter, the young brahmin, Samudrareṇu in the Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka, Cunda's broth, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's calf and its assembly of beasts), and repeatedly insists that origin and trace are judged only by long-ago and recent, never by coarse and wondrous or broad and narrow. Notable moves include his suspicion that the Maitreya clause in the brief explanation is misplaced, his relocation of "inferring the moon above from what is seen below," the four propositions of origin high and trace low, the answer to why the origin gate judges without a separate opening, the "awaiting the time" and "awaiting companions" of propagation, and a line-by-line reading of the six propositions on great and wondrous. He closes by measuring the prior lotus interpretations against the wondrous of practice and of teaching, and by reading the seventeen names through origin and trace.

The Origin Gate: Trace and Origin in Common

This explains the ten wondrous aspects of the origin gate, and within it there are of itself two parts: first trace and origin, next the ten wondrous aspects. The reason trace and origin are made clear first is that the names "trace" and "origin" are common [to many senses], so they must be distinguished in common terms. Sifting out the common in order to follow the particular makes the present trace and origin plainly visible; therefore within this, trace and origin are first discussed in common, and then discussed in particular.

Ru-Yi Translation

The explanation next is, for now, in two: first the explanation; next, from "As for discussing trace and origin in terms of" on, concluding by gathering in. The first passage has six of itself, and each passage sifts the common to bring out the particular. Again, each passage has three: first explaining the marks of trace and origin; next making clear that trace and origin reveal each other and are fused in principle; third citing texts as proof. In the first, principle and phenomena, it says "from the rootless root, every dharma is established": ignorance acts as the root of every dharma; ignorance is the dharma-nature, and ignorance in turn takes the dharma-nature as its root; one should know that all dharmas likewise take the dharma-nature as their root. The dharma-nature is ignorance, and the dharma-nature in turn takes ignorance as its root. The dharma-nature is ignorance, so the dharma-nature has no place of abiding; ignorance is the dharma-nature, so ignorance has no place of abiding. Though ignorance and the dharma-nature are both without abiding, they act as the root of all dharmas; hence it says "from the rootless root, every dharma is established." Since the root without abiding is common [to all], the real truth points to principle; all dharmas are phenomena, and so the three thousand are pointed to as its manifold array. As for "From the origin descending into the trace": this is the trace and origin of principle-nature; only through this are there the trace and origin of outward function. Therefore it begins from principle and phenomena and ends with already and now. Of the six layers of trace and origin, the first five layers are common to already and to now, common to origin and to trace; lest they be confused with the trace, in each layer it explains explicitly, from "the original true mark" down to "genuinely attained at the very outset." Again, the sixth layer, already and now, judges the preceding five layers as having origin and having trace, which verifies that the preceding five all belong to the origin. Again, at the end of each of the six layers, in [the part on] mutual revelation and fusion in principle, each says "Trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one and the same": this borrows the words of Master Zhao to praise the principle of trace and origin. Though Master Zhao has the words "origin" and "trace," both his origin and his trace belong to the trace. Now, in saying "inconceivable": in terms of phenomena, the origin is past and the trace present; in terms of principle there is no longer present or past; hence one knows that the essence of principle is entirely inconceivable.

Ru-Yi Translation

If one speaks by the sense of the present five layers of trace and origin: in terms of phenomena there are the phenomena and principle within the origin and the phenomena and principle within the trace; in terms of principle there is no longer any distinction of origin and trace, phenomena and principle; therefore each says "inconceivably one and the same." So too down through the other four. In the sixth, already and now, "already" is the trace, pointing to the trace gate and the various trace teachings; "now" is the origin, pointing to the origin gate. Everything before the origin gate is called "already"; only from the emerging [of the bodhisattvas from the earth] onward is it called "now." Hence it says that the phenomena and principle down through the provisional and the real already taught are called the trace, and the phenomena and principle down through the provisional and the real taught now are all called the origin. Hence one knows: without the phenomena and principle down through the provisional and the real within the trace, how could the long-distant origin be revealed? Again, though the words "already and now" differ from the first five, they too, in a first approximation, point to the "Life Span" chapter, calling it the present origin. Measured against the very first origin, one should further sift out the difference between already and now: the already and now of the sūtras before the Lotus still belong to the trace; what the present sūtra makes clear truly makes clear the long-distant origin. That is, the already and now already taught are the trace, and the already and now taught now are the origin — only this is the real account. Why is it that, of the texts cited as proof in the six gates, the first three cite the trace and the last three cite the origin? The first three are again common to origin and trace: both have phenomena and principle down through teaching and practice. Essence and function and the other two of the three are particular within the common: since they speak of the original function and the original provisional, a text from the trace cannot reveal them; still less, when the origin gate is taken as "now," can one plainly cite the trace. For this reason, three cite trace texts and three cite origin texts. Again, since the first three texts concern the cause, and [the text on] the original cause is narrow, the trace is simply used to reflect back to the origin; hence only the trace gate is cited.

Again, measuring the six layers against the ten wondrous aspects, some are complete and some lacking, some many and some few; therefore it is made clear again. Within this, three: first properly making clear the mutual gathering-in; next, from "The very original moment's own origin" on, sifting and showing; third, from "Without the origin belonging to the original moment" on, making clear in general mutual revelation and fusion in principle. The first passage, as is convenient, recognizes the six pairs in reverse, from the last: if one recognizes already and now, one knows the other five; hence it explains them in reverse order. The reason the six gates make clear the gathering-in differently is that, among the six gates, three are cause and two are fruit, while already and now is again the two gates of the present sūtra; therefore what is gathered in differs according to the sense.

In the sifting and showing it says "The very original moment's own origin is origin alone, never trace," and so on: since the very first real accomplishment had not yet descended into a trace, it belongs only to the origin. In the interval, measured against one another, they are mutually origin and trace. Again, what is accomplished in the interval is called "also trace" at the time of descending into a trace, and "also origin" at the time of revealing the origin. "What was already spoken of at the very end" is the trace gate already spoken today, at a time when the origin is not yet revealed; hence it belongs only to the trace. "Without the already-spoken trace" refers to today's trace gate; "the origin spoken of in the present" refers to today's origin gate.

Ru-Yi Translation

The first, the brief explanation, is of itself in ten layers; the first passage is somewhat broad, the remaining nine brief. The first passage, three: first raising the origin; next, from "As for the sixteen princes" on, raising the trace to compare and decide; third, from "If the cause belonging to the interval were left as it stands" on, wiping away the trace to reveal the origin. In the trace passage it says, from "taking the sahā world's own worlds as ink" to "how, then, could this possibly be exhausted?": this cites the text of the trace gate's chapter "The Parable of the Conjured City," and it is not fitting to cite the words about Maitreya and the rest; the text on Maitreya's not knowing is in the origin gate. Perhaps it borrows a text from within the origin and brings it here as an analogy: even the three thousand dust-dots of the trace could not be known by the wisdom of the one who is to succeed to buddhahood. If the trace text is to be used strictly, then the twenty-five characters about Maitreya and the rest should be removed, and the sense would then run smoothly. My teacher said, "I fear there is some other intent." Yet although it cites Maitreya, the tone of the words differs somewhat: that origin-gate text says only, "We abide in the stage of avaivartika, yet in this matter we too do not comprehend," whereas this text says that with the wisdom that emerges into the provisional he could not know even by counting the worlds, still less the number of their dust-motes. If it is taken as knowing the dust, then only the wisdom that emerges into the provisional should be used. Therefore it ought to be explained by the earlier text. As for "wiping away the trace to reveal the origin": simply by pointing to the attainment of buddhahood before so many dust-motes [of eons], the interval and today are wiped away as a matter of course, and the long-distant origin can be seen as a matter of course. The nine wondrous aspects below all follow this pattern. Below this, the two wondrous aspects of the original fruit and the original land have only two passages, establishing the wondrous and wiping away the trace to reveal the origin; the seven wondrous aspects after that point directly to the origin. Since they are explained more broadly below, they are not discussed here. The texts can all be seen.

The Broad Explanation: The Wondrous of the Original Cause

"The Secret Script of Every Buddha": the provisional and the real are identical, and so too origin and trace, yet no one knows it; hence it is called "secret." Since the secret is made the name, it says "secret script." As for "What Gives Birth to Every Buddha": opening the provisional to reveal the real, one recognizes where the trace buddha is born from; opening the trace to reveal the origin, one recognizes where the original buddha is born from. As for "the Place of Enlightenment of Every Buddha": the true mark is the place where the trace buddha and the original buddha attained the path. As for "the Wheel of the Dharma Turned by Every Buddha": the wheel of the Dharma turned by [the buddhas of] the three times does not go beyond the two gates of provisional and real, origin and trace. As for "the Firm and Solid Relics of Every Buddha": this sūtra is the firm and solid relic of the whole body of the Dharma-body, unlike the relics of the shattered body of the birth-body; the real origin does not move, hence it is called "firm and solid." As for "the Great and Skillful Expedient Means of Every Buddha": since expedient means is another name for the lotus flower, why did the ancients judge that the "Skillful Means" chapter takes its title from the past? Reading Notes for Fascicle 15

Translator's notes

"Inconceivably one." In paragraph 3 Zhanran says the refrain "trace and origin, though differing, are inconceivably one" is borrowed from Master Zhao; the line is in Sengzhao's preface to the Annotated Vimalakīrti (T1775, fascicle 1: 非本無以垂跡、非跡無以顯本，本跡雖殊而不思議一也).

The sixth layer's proof-texts (paragraph 4). The sentence 俱有事理乃至教行、體用等三，通中復別 can be punctuated more than one way; it is read here as "both have phenomena and principle down through teaching and practice; essence-and-function and the other two are particular within the common." 以迹倒本 ("the trace is used to reflect back to the origin") is uncertain.

The Maitreya clause (paragraph 8). Zhanran holds that the Profound Meaning's brief explanation mixes the "Conjured City" dust-dot simile with the origin gate's words on Maitreya; the Maitreya line he quotes is Lotus, chapter 15 (T262, fascicle 5: 我等住阿惟越致地，於是事中亦所不達). "My teacher" (師云) is not named; Zhanran's teacher was presumably Zuoxi Xuanlang, but the text does not say so.

The potter and Samudrareṇu (paragraph 12). The potter story is cited as Great Treatise fascicle 4; in the CBETA text it is in fascicle 3 (T1509: 釋迦文佛先世作瓦師，名大光明). The Śāntimati / Saṃdilya / Araṇemin / Samudrareṇu account is Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka fascicle 2 (T157), summarized freely. In the "Bestowal of Predictions" summary Zhanran has Samudrareṇu giving the predictions to Amitābha and Avalokiteśvara; it is translated as written.

"Inferring the moon above from what is seen below." Zhanran says these four characters belong after "the disk is a simile for the wondrous of stage" and may be a copying error; the site follows the received order (see fx issues).

Sources in paragraphs 13 and 15. The Great Treatise passage on the grass seat and heavenly cloth, cited as fascicle 38, is in fascicle 33 of the CBETA text. The Nirvāṇa land "Unsurpassed" is in fascicle 24 of the northern text, where the distance is thirty-two Ganges-sands, not thirty-six. "One Lamp Light" is in the Śūraṅgamasamādhi Sūtra (T642). In paragraph 13, 三秖四階 is read as 三祇 (three asaṃkhyeya eons).

Lemmas not in the received Profound Meaning. Paragraph 10 "separating two" (離二), paragraph 16 "the Buddha enters the city to beg for food" (with its ten causes, not located in the Great Treatise), paragraph 29 "the first ground fuses them, as before," and paragraph 42 "if now one wishes to know briefly the intent among the seventeen names" are not in T1716 or in the site English. Zhanran's text of the Profound Meaning may have differed; these were translated directly.

The calf (paragraph 18). The quotation is Nirvāṇa fascicle 2 (T374), which reads 牸牛 (cow) where Zhanran writes 乳牛; it also lists 滑草、麥䴬, which Zhanran drops while keeping the interlinear glosses on wine, wheat, dregs, and bran. The glosses are uncertain.

Sāla grove assembly and Cunda (paragraph 21). The assembly list and Boundless Body come from Nirvāṇa fascicle 1; Cunda's broth from the Dīrgha Āgama fascicle 3 (T1: 別煮栴檀樹耳). The Erya gloss "a locust larva without wings" does not fit the viper and looks like a confused entry; translated as written.

Lost gold and the vajra body (paragraph 22). Kāśyapa's verse question (云何得長壽，金剛不壞身) is Nirvāṇa fascicle 3. In 長者行還索金無所, 無所 is read as "nothing is lost"; uncertain.

The four propositions of origin and trace (paragraph 25). Zhanran cites "the Commentary on the Vimalakīrti" (Zhiyi's) for the four propositions; the matching of the initial abiding, second abiding, and wondrous enlightenment is his own.

"Coarse" and "wondrous" accompanied (paragraphs 26–27). The glosses on the four propositions are very terse; the bracketed additions (which teaching each refers to) follow the Profound Meaning's own list.

Great and wondrous (paragraphs 39–40). The citation of the Ratnagotravibhāga (two vehicles still holding "impermanence and so on") matches T1611 fascicle 4 only loosely. Zhanran's division of question and answer differs from the site's (see fx issues).

The Treatise on Diverse Things (paragraph 41). Several inventors' names differ from the usual forms (維父 for 雍父, 正首 for 隸首, 稽仲 for 奚仲); they are romanized as written. "Twelve characters" for the lotus is unexplained.

Small copying errors (paragraph 43). 三世諸法所轉法輪 is read as 諸佛 ("[the buddhas of] the three times"); 今此》法華》 is a punctuation slip. In paragraph 44, "the third fascicle" is ambiguous (it may refer to Zhanran's own earlier fascicle).
