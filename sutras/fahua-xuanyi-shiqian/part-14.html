The Wondrous of Merit and Benefit; the Provisional and the Real; the Ten Gates of Nonduality

Fascicle fourteen closes the trace gate. Zhanran first comments on the wondrous of merit and benefit, the tenth of the ten wondrous aspects (the end of fascicle-006 and the start of fascicle-007 on this site): the seven benefits and their opening into ten rounds — fruit, cause, śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, six pāramitās, shared, distinct, perfect, transformational birth and death, and real reward — with the Kośa's list of hot and cold hells, the five signs of a god's decline, and the stories of flying flowers and jingling bracelets that awaken pratyekabuddhas; then distant, near, and present benefit, benefit in the propagation section, and benefit in the contemplation of mind. He next comments on "closing by resolving into the provisional and the real," Zhiyi's refutation of Guangzhai's "threefold-threefold" and "fourfold-one." The fascicle ends with the passage later circulated by itself as The Ten Gates of Nonduality (十不二門, T1927), in which Zhanran gathers the ten wondrous aspects into ten gates for contemplation. For that passage this translation reuses the English of the separate translation The Ten Gates of Nonduality, adjusted wherever the Shiqian text differs from T1927 (see the notes).

What is said cannot all be spread out at once, yet the pattern cannot be entirely lacking; each one gives its mark of suffering as the capacity, and then triggering and fitting and the rest can be known. First, in hell, "eight great hells" are the eight hot hells. "Hell" is in Sanskrit niraya, here rendered "instrument of suffering"; "hell" (地獄, "earth-prison") is named from the place. Each of the eight has sixteen lesser hells as its retinue; thus eight sixteens make one hundred twenty-eight, together with the eight root hells, one hundred thirty-six hells in all. So the Kośa says: "Below this, beyond twenty thousand, is Avīci, equal in depth and breadth; above it the seven naraka; the eight each have sixteen additions, namely the embers, the excrement, the blades, and the burning river, each placed on its four sides." Since the four gates of Avīci each have four, the upper seven likewise. "The eight cold hells": for now following other texts it says one hundred thirty-six; if one follows the text listing eight: first arbuda, here rendered "blister," meaning the cold wind presses the body and raises blisters; second nirarbuda, here rendered "bursting blister"; third aṭaṭa; fourth hahava; fifth huhuva — these are named from the sounds; sixth utpala, here rendered "blue lotus"; seventh padma, here rendered "red lotus"; eighth mahāpadma, here rendered "great red lotus" — these are named from the color of the body. All are beside the hells spoken of before; their base place is below, their branches unfixed, sometimes near mountains, rivers, and seas, in the empty space under the earth, or elsewhere. The ghosts' base place is in the realm of King Yama; thus five hundred yojanas beneath this continent is Yama's realm, and its breadth is likewise. From there they spread to dwell in other places; some are handsome and enjoy pleasures, just as among the gods there are still ugly ones; the present text is somewhat brief. The various marks of hell are as broadly in the Contemplation of the Buddha Samādhi, the Saddharmasmṛtyupasthāna, the Āgamas, the Vibhāṣā, the Kośa, and so on. "Of water, of land, and of air": these three are the places animals depend on. The Great Treatise also has three classes that include all animals, namely those that go by day, by night, and by day and night.

Translator's notes

The Viśeṣacinti light. Paragraph 3 refers to the second fascicle of the Viśeṣacintibrahma-paripṛcchā (思益經), where Bodhisattva Net of Light (網明) emits light; the summary is Zhanran's.

The hells. Paragraph 7 quotes the Kośa verse on the sixteen additional hells and transliterates the eight cold hells; the Sanskrit names follow the usual Kośa forms. The distance to Yama's realm (five hundred yojanas) is as written.

The eight noble lineages. 「八聖種」 in paragraph 8 does not appear in the Profound Meaning as printed on the site; it is translated as Zhanran gives it and referred to his Record.

"The fourth samādhi" and "the seventeenth samādhi." Zhanran explains (paragraph 15) that these mean the four samādhis that break the four continents and the seventeen that break the remaining heavens.

The Dhyāna Sūtra stories. The king who reduced his consorts' bracelets and the monkey who taught non-Buddhists to sit are quoted from a "Dhyāna Sūtra"; the exact source has not been located.

The two persons in real reward. In paragraph 22 Zhanran reads the Profound Meaning's 「八番中有兩人……又二人」 as two different pairs: those in the land where ordinary beings and sages dwell together, and those already in the land of expedients. "Ignorance [of the lesser kind]" renders 無知, supplied by sense.

Polishing the mirror. Paragraph 26 expands the Profound Meaning's brief answer about the response-body and the Dharma-body into an argument; "[Vimalakīrti]" is supplied for 方丈現病.

The seven pairs of propositions. Paragraph 26 mentions "two sets of four propositions and seven pairs"; only the first sets are explained in detail, as in the text.

Guangzhai and "the Great Master never met him." Paragraph 33 notes that Zhiyi could not have heard Guangzhai's defense himself, so the defense must have been reported by an earlier critic.

The Shiqian text of the Ten Gates. Paragraphs 37–53 follow T1717 rather than T1927.
