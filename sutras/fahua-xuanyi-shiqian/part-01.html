Prefaces, the Five Sections, and the Marking of Name, Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching

The Explanatory Notes on the Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra is Zhanran's (湛然, 711–782) line-by-line commentary on Zhiyi's Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra (Fahua xuanyi), already translated in full on this site. Zhanran explains in his opening note how it came about: on Mount Tiantai he wrote answers on slips (籤) for students who brought him questions, and late in life, back in Piling, he revised and filled them out. The work quotes the Profound Meaning phrase by phrase — usually as "'…' and so on" or "from '…' on" — and then explains, divides, and sometimes corrects it. In this translation every quoted phrase of the Profound Meaning follows the English already published on this site, so that a reader can move between the two; where Zhanran reads a word differently from that English, his own reading is given in the explanation that follows and noted below. This first part covers a preface by Zhanran's contemporary Pumen, dated 764; Zhanran's own opening note; and his comments on the first portion of fascicle one of the Profound Meaning (fascicle-001 on this site): Guanding's account of the ten virtues of Zhiyi and of why he wrote the work down; Zhiyi's preface with its six similes of the lotus; Guanding's private preface; the common and particular senses of the five sections and the sevenfold common exposition; and the "marking" of the name, the essence, the cardinal doctrine, the function, and the teaching, up to the opening simile of the sun shining on the high mountains. Throughout, Zhanran keeps pressing two points that run through the whole work: that the five sections go no further than origin and trace, and that the Lotus alone is "wondrous" because it opens and sets aside every earlier teaching.

Preface on the Origins of the Explanatory Notes

Chinese Source

君山除饉男普門子屬辭

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Pumen, a bhikṣu of Junshan.

Chinese Source

四教成列，開合之旨，蘊乎其中，十子既往，幽贊之功，在人方絕，惟三轉遂周，一乘載導，經文顯而約，玄記博而深，後進難窺，蒙求尚壅，不遠而復，存乎其時，我哲匠湛然公當之矣。公孩提秀發，志學名成，淵解得於自心，博贍振於先達，無適不可，以虛受人，洎毘壇以至於國清，其從如雲矣。間者島夷作難，海山不寧，徇法之多，仄身巖宇，或謂身危法喪，莫如奉法全身，僶俛遂行，暴露原野，是樂法者請益悅隨，且法實無邊，身則有待，弘敷未暇，籤訪有憑，因籤以釋，思逸功倍，美哉洋洋乎！登門者肯綮未嘗，望涯者恥躬不逮，乘是以訓文，其可廢耶？先德既詳，雖大科不舉，諸生未達，在小疑必疏。凡十卷，不忘於本，以天台命家，善繼其宗，以釋籤順學，信所謂觀象得意、俾昏作明，永代不朽者也。普早歲在塵，後時從道，徒欲擊其大節，獨不愧於心乎！

Ru-Yi Translation

The four teachings stand in their ranks, and the purport of opening and combining them lies stored within. Since the ten masters passed away, the work of profoundly assisting the Dharma had all but been cut off among people. Only when the three turnings [of the wheel] had come full circle did the one vehicle take up the lead. The sūtra's text is plain and concise; the Profound Meaning and its record are broad and deep. Later students find it hard to see into, and those seeking instruction are still blocked. "Returning before going far astray" depends on the moment, and our wise master-craftsman, Master Zhanran, was the one who met it. From infancy the master showed outstanding gifts; by the age for study his name was established. His deep understanding was gained from his own mind, and his broad learning stirred even the earlier worthies. There was nothing he could not do, and he received others with an open heart. From Piling to Guoqing, his followers gathered like clouds. Recently, when the island barbarians rose in revolt and the seas and mountains were not at peace, many who followed the Dharma took shelter, bodies bent low, in cliff dwellings. Some said that if the body is in danger the Dharma is lost, and that nothing is better than upholding the Dharma by keeping the body whole. So, with great effort, he set out, exposed on the open plain; and those who delighted in the Dharma asked for further teaching and gladly followed. Moreover, the Dharma is truly boundless while the body depends on conditions; there was no leisure to expound it broadly, but the questions on slips gave him something to rely on. Explaining by way of the slips, his thought ran free and the result was doubled. How fine, how vast! Those who had reached the gate had never yet tasted its joints and sinews; those who gazed from the shore were ashamed that they fell short. Using this to teach the text — how could it be set aside? Where earlier worthies had already been detailed, even the major divisions are not raised; where students had not yet understood, even small doubts are always cleared away. In ten fascicles, not forgetting the root, he named his house after Tiantai; well continuing its lineage, he called it the Explanatory Notes to suit the learners. Truly this is what is called "contemplating the image and grasping the meaning," "causing the dark to become bright" — a work that will not decay for ages. I, Pu, spent my early years in the dust of the world and only later followed the path. If I tried merely to strike at its great joints, would I not be ashamed in my own heart?

Chinese Source

天台沙門湛然述

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Zhanran, a monk of Tiantai.

Chinese Source

昔於台嶺，隨諸問者籤下所錄，不暇尋究文勢生起，亦未委細分節句逗，晚還毘壇，輒添膚飾裨以管見，然所記者莫非述聞兼尋經論，但識用暗短而繁略頗馴，呈露後賢敢悕添削。

Ru-Yi Translation

Long ago, on the Tiantai peaks, I recorded under the slips whatever those who questioned me asked about, without leisure to look into how the text unfolds or to divide it in detail into sections and phrases. Late in life, back in Piling, I added some outer ornament and supplemented it with my narrow views. Yet everything recorded is nothing but what I relate from hearing, together with what I sought in the sūtras and treatises. My understanding is dim and short, though the fuller and briefer parts are fairly well balanced. I lay it open to later worthies and dare to hope they will add to it and cut it down.

Guanding's Account: The Ten Virtues of the Master

Chinese Source

「私記」者，章安尊者於江陵自記大師所說，不與他共，故名為私。「緣起」者，述所記之興致，序教法之所由，此中先序師德及傳述之意，名為總序。次正序玄文，不過五義及本迹奧旨。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Private record" (私記): the Venerable Zhang'an, in Jiangling, recorded for himself what the Great Master taught, not sharing it with others; hence it is called "private." "Account of origins" (緣起): it relates how the record came to be made and sets out how the teaching came about. Here he first narrates the master's virtues and his own intent in transmitting the account; this is called the general preface. Next comes the preface proper to the profound text, which goes no further than the five meanings and the deep purport of origin and trace.

Chinese Source

初序分二：初序師德，次「幸哉」下明傳述之意。初文又二：初列十德，次「唯我」下結歸我師。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first, the preface, has two parts: first, it narrates the master's virtues; second, from "How fortunate" on, it makes clear his intent in transmitting the account. The first again has two: first it lists ten virtues; second, from "Only our" on, it concludes by returning them to our master.

Chinese Source

初十德中初云「大法」者，通指佛教以為大法。「東漸」者，自漢明夜夢迦竺初臨，洎乎隋文御㝢台衡誕應，諸有習禪、義解、翻譯之徒，漸被此方弘宣教法，僧史所錄未有不曾聽講自解佛乘。「佛乘」者即是今典，永異餘教，不同三五七九等乘，仍開會之使歸乎一極，故云佛乘。言「僧史」者，如宋文宣王記室王簡栖所集百卷，如俗史書，左史記事、右史記言。《禮》云：「動則左史書之，言則右史書之。」凡所集者不出言之與事，今亦例之，故云僧史。又嘉祥皎法師所集《高僧傳》十三卷，開為十科，終無不聽自解如天台大師，此不聽自解即是今文十德中之一也。及從「縱令」已下九德並在大師《別傳》，章安撮之用序師德。初德之中「詎」者正云無也。下之九德，德德之中皆云「縱」者，謂與而言之。縱有第一終無第二乃至第十，故下結云「唯我智者具諸功德」。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the ten virtues, the first speaks of "the great Dharma": this refers in general to the Buddha's teaching as the great Dharma. "Spread eastward": from Emperor Ming of the Han dreaming at night, when Kāśyapa [Mātaṅga] and [Zhu Fa]lan first arrived, down to Emperor Wen of the Sui ruling the realm, when [the master of] Tiantai and Heng[shan] was born in response — all who practiced meditation, explained doctrine, or translated came gradually to this land to spread and proclaim the teaching. Among those the monks' histories record, there was none who understood the Buddha-vehicle by himself without having heard it lectured on. "Buddha-vehicle" is this present scripture: forever different from the other teachings, not like the vehicles counted as three, five, seven, nine and so on, yet it opens and merges them so that they return to the one ultimate; hence "Buddha-vehicle." "Monks' Histories": for example, the hundred fascicles compiled by Wang Jianqi, recording secretary to Prince Wenxuan of the Song, modeled on the secular histories, where the Left Historian recorded deeds and the Right Historian recorded words. The Record of Rites says: "When he acted, the Left Historian wrote it down; when he spoke, the Right Historian wrote it down." All that is compiled goes no further than words and deeds; this is taken as the model here, hence "monks' histories." Again, the Biographies of Eminent Monks in thirteen fascicles compiled by Dharma Master [Hui]jiao of Jiaxiang is opened into ten categories, and in the end it holds no one who understood by himself without hearing, as the Great Master of Tiantai did. This "understanding by himself without hearing" is one of the ten virtues in the present text. The nine virtues from "And even granting" on are all in the Great Master's Separate Biography; Zhang'an gathered them up to narrate the master's virtues. In the first virtue, 詎 properly means "none." In each of the nine virtues below, "even granting" (縱) means speaking by way of concession: even granting there is the first, in the end there is no second, and so on up to the tenth. Hence the conclusion below says, "Only our [Great Master] Zhiyi possessed every one of these."

Chinese Source

「發悟」者，謂於大蘇法華道場三昧開發悟解一乘，代受法師，講金字《大品》。「陀羅尼」者，此云總持。從三昧起以證白師，師云此是法華前方便陀羅尼。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Awakened" (發悟): in the Lotus samādhi of the practice hall at Dasu, an awakened understanding of the one vehicle opened in him, and he lectured on the gold-lettered Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] in his teacher's place. "Dhāraṇī" means "total retention." Arising from samādhi, he reported his realization to his teacher, and the teacher said, "This is the preparatory dhāraṇī of the Lotus."

Chinese Source

「縱具定慧」者，發悟屬慧，入定是定。「盛席」者，盛弘定慧二法。「謝遣」等者，自省不能益他為謝，推眾不受曰遣。隱居華頂佛壟唐溪。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Possessed both samādhi and wisdom": awakening belongs to wisdom; entering samādhi is samādhi. "Flourishing assembly": he greatly spread the two dharmas of samādhi and wisdom. "Take leave" and so on: reflecting on himself that he could not benefit others is "taking leave" (謝); declining the assembly and not accepting them is "sending away" (遣). He lived in seclusion at Huading, Fo[long] ridge, and Tang creek.

Chinese Source

「避世」等者，捨名利為「避世」，進己道為「守玄」，陳少主再敕頻迎，隋文帝有敕請住，故云「被徵為二國師」。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Shunned the world" and so on: giving up fame and gain is "shunning the world"; advancing one's own path is "guarding what is profound." The Last Ruler of Chen issued edict after edict to welcome him, and Emperor Wen of the Sui issued an edict inviting him to stay; hence "summoned to serve as preceptor to two kingdoms."

Chinese Source

「太極殿」者，陳朝正殿名為太極。「三禮」者，陳少主敕云，國家一年舊有仁王兩集，仰屈於太極殿開講，法式處分一聽指揮。初開法筵，主上親於眾中三禮。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Great Ultimate Hall": the main hall of the Chen court was called Taiji. "Threefold homage": the Last Ruler of Chen's edict said that the state had of old held two assemblies on the Humane Kings [Sūtra] each year; he humbly asked the master to lecture in the Taiji Hall, with the ceremonial arrangements entirely under his direction. When the Dharma-feast first opened, the ruler himself made threefold homage among the assembly.

Chinese Source

天子為萬乘之主，故云「萬乘屈膝」。言「萬乘」者，《論語》包氏引王制云：「古者井田，方里為井，十井為乘，百里之國適千乘也。諸侯之國千乘，天子之國萬乘，故云萬乘之主。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Son of Heaven is the lord of ten thousand chariots; hence "ten thousand chariots bowed their knees." As for "ten thousand chariots": in the Analects, Master Bao cites the "Royal Regulations": "In ancient times, under the well-field system, a square li made a well, and ten wells made a chariot; a state of a hundred li came to a thousand chariots. The state of a feudal lord had a thousand chariots, and the state of the Son of Heaven ten thousand; hence 'the lord of ten thousand chariots.'"

Chinese Source

「道俗」等者，百高座為道，百官為俗。「玄悟法華圓意」者，五義釋經，統收五味，故名為玄。非兼非帶開廢諸典，名為圓意。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Monastics and laity" and so on: the hundred high seats are the monastics; the hundred officials are the laity. "Penetrated the perfect meaning of the Lotus Sūtra in its full profundity": he explained the sūtra by the five meanings, gathering together all five flavors; hence it is called "profound" (玄). Neither combined nor accompanied, opening and setting aside all the scriptures — this is called "the perfect meaning."

Chinese Source

「樂說辯」如《止觀》第一記。「晝夜」等者，舉譬也。如懸河流瀉晝夜不竭。此之十德通舉悟解弘經之相，故初德云「自解佛乘」，最後德云「晝夜流瀉」。諸有託胎靈瑞誕育徵祥，髫齓精誠從師訪道，臣主珍敬緇素歸心，臨終示相滅後應驗，具如《別傳》。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Eloquence of unhindered exposition" is as in my Record on the first fascicle of Calming and Contemplation. "Day and night" and so on gives a simile: like a river hanging down, pouring forth day and night without running dry. These ten virtues raise in general the marks of awakened understanding and of spreading the sūtra; hence the first virtue says "understood the Buddha-vehicle by himself," and the last says "pour it forth day and night." The auspicious signs at his conception and the omens at his birth, his sincerity as a child, his following teachers in search of the path, the honor paid him by ministers and rulers, the devotion of monastics and laity, the signs shown at his death and the confirmations after his passing — all are as in the Separate Biography.

Guanding's Account: Why He Recorded It

Chinese Source

次傳述中分三：初述歎所受功微，次「並復」下正明傳述以益當，三「或以」下述己添助以勸信。初自斤己名謙恭所學。

Ru-Yi Translation

The account of the transmission comes next, in three parts: first, he narrates and laments how slight was what he received; second, from "And I could not help but think" on, he makes clear that he transmits it for the benefit of those to come; third, from "In places" on, he relates what he himself added in support, to encourage faith. First he names himself, humble toward what he learned.

Chinese Source

「幸」者，自省之詞。「建業」亦云建康，即晉宋等稱為業都，今江寧是也。舊稱揚州，隋滅陳後移揚州名額過京江北，江陵即荊州也。故下句云「荊揚往復途將萬里」，「將」猶當也，謂依俙髣髴將一萬里。漢南曰荊，自漢南至衡山之南。江南曰揚，自京江至南海已來。「台嶺」「鶴林」，具如《止觀》第一記。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Fortunate" is a word of self-reflection. "Jianye" is also called Jiankang; it is what the Jin, the Song, and other dynasties called the capital of Ye, present-day Jiangning. It was formerly called Yangzhou; after the Sui destroyed the Chen, the name Yangzhou was moved across the river north of Jingjiang. Jiangling is Jingzhou. Hence the following line says "traveling back and forth between Jing and Yang, a journey of nearly ten thousand li." "Nearly" (將) is like "about": roughly, approximately ten thousand li. South of the Han river is called Jing, from south of the Han to south of Mount Heng. South of the Yangtze is called Yang, from Jingjiang to the Southern Sea. "The Tiantai mountains" and "the grove where the cranes gathered" are as in my Record on the first fascicle of Calming and Contemplation.

Chinese Source

「非但未聞不聞」等者，歎恨不聞者絕分，如《止觀》、《禪門》、《淨名疏》等，各有餘分說未終者，名為不聞。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Not only did I fail to hear what I never had the chance to hear" and so on: he laments that what he did not hear was cut off from him entirely — for example the Calming and Contemplation, the Dharma Gate of Dhyāna, and the Commentary on the Vimalakīrti, each of which has a remaining portion whose teaching was never finished. These are called "not heard."

Chinese Source

「亦乃」已去，謙己聞者未能盡意。「卷舒鑽仰」者，章安總歎再治再讀理堅意高，如孔子諸弟子歎孔子智深，如窺其宮牆，鑽之彌覺其堅，仰之彌覺其高，如飲大河水飽腹而歸不測深淺。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "even what I did hear" on, he humbly says that what he heard he could not grasp in full. "Turned it over and pressed into it with reverent effort": Zhang'an laments in general that, having worked over it and read it again and again, its principle is firm and its meaning high — as Confucius's disciples lamented the depth of Confucius's wisdom: like peering over the walls of his palace; boring into it, one feels it ever firmer; looking up at it, one feels it ever higher; like drinking from a great river, one returns with a full belly without having fathomed its depth.

Chinese Source

「猶恨緣淺」下明私記緣起。今雖欲記，但恨一聞而已，屬大師滅度不獲重聞，諮決無從如犢思乳。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "I could only regret that my connection to it had been too slight" on, he makes clear the origin of the private record. Though he now wished to record it, he regretted having heard it only once; with the Great Master's passing he could not hear it again, and had no way to consult him and settle his doubts, "like a calf longing for its mother's milk."

Chinese Source

「並復惟念」者，自惟及念彼，故云「並」也。自惟不足、念彼未聞，若不記其所聞，念彼當來有不聞佛乘之苦，故云「可悲」。

Ru-Yi Translation

"And I could not help but think" (並復惟念): he reflected on himself and thought of others, hence "and" (並). Reflecting that he himself fell short, and thinking of those who had not yet heard: if he did not record what he had heard, he thought of the suffering of those to come who would never hear the Buddha-vehicle; hence "a sorrow."

Chinese Source

「若樹若石」等者，如《涅槃》雪山童子，聞半偈已傳於石壁。又如今經〈隨喜品〉初，隨其所聞，聚落田里為父母宗親隨力演說。

Ru-Yi Translation

"What a tree or a stone" and so on: as in the Nirvāṇa [Sūtra], the youth of the Snow Mountains, having heard the half verse, passed it on by writing it on rock walls. Again, as at the start of this sūtra's chapter on "Rejoicing in Accord": according to what one has heard, one expounds it as best one can to parents and kin in the villages, fields, and hamlets.

Chinese Source

「聿」循也。「聖典」者，依今經及《大經》等皆令傳授。

Ru-Yi Translation

聿 means "following." "The sacred canon": relying on this sūtra, the Great Sūtra, and others, all of which bid it be transmitted.

Chinese Source

「甘露門」者，實相常住如天甘露，是不死之藥。今釋妙法能通實相，故名為門。初總序竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The gate of sweet dew": the true mark, eternally abiding, is like the sweet dew of the heavens, the medicine of deathlessness. Now the explanation of the wondrous dharma can lead through to the true mark; hence it is called a gate. The general preface ends here.

Zhiyi's Preface: "Wondrous" and "Dharma"

Chinese Source

次正序玄文，總有三序：初一是大師別行經序，次「私序王」去是記者所序，三「此妙法」去玄文本序。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the preface proper to the profound text has three prefaces in all: the first is the Great Master's preface to the sūtra, which circulated separately; the second, from "Private Preface" on, is the recorder's own preface; the third, from "This [Wondrous] Dharma" on, is the original preface of the profound text.

Chinese Source

初中云「王」字，去聲，謂起也、初也。序起眾文之始，故云「序王」。就初序為二：初總攬五義以釋名，次「記者」下就總名以別解。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the first, the character 王 is read in the departing tone, meaning "to rise," "beginning." It prefaces the rise of all the texts; hence "Preface" (序王). The first preface has two parts: first, it gathers up the five meanings as a whole to explain the name; second, from "The recorder" on, it explains them one by one on the basis of the overall name.

Chinese Source

初文又三：初釋妙法，次釋蓮華，三釋經。初文又二：先妙、次法。下文廣釋先法次妙者，從義便故，今既略解且從名便。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first again has three: first, explaining "wondrous dharma"; second, explaining "lotus flower"; third, explaining "sūtra." The first again has two: first "wondrous," then "dharma." Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining one by one on the basis of the whole: from "The recorder" on, Zhang'an explains the purport of the Great Master's preface. The Great Master's preface seems only to explain the name, but its meaning contains the particulars. Zhang'an's explanation sets out the essence, the cardinal doctrine, and the function, because explaining the name is the whole and essence and the rest are the particulars; the particulars are particulars of the whole, and the whole is the whole of the particulars. So what is explained within the whole includes all five sections. One should know that the three sections of essence and the rest are just the three virtues, and indeed just every set of three dharmas; hence the text below says, "Explaining the Name discusses the three dharmas in general," and "the essence and the rest open and match the three dharmas." So the overall name crowns the whole work, and the whole work, from beginning to end, goes no further than the two gates; therefore the two gates are given the designation "the heart of the text," as at the start of arousing the mind in Calming and Contemplation: "Gathering the essentials is called mind." In the present gathering of dharmas, origin and trace are the essentials. Again, essence and the rest in the origin do not differ from those in the trace; so only the name is divided into origin and trace, and the others — essence, cardinal doctrine, and function — are explained straightforwardly. Thus Zhang'an relates the Great Master's purport and grasps the heart of the sūtra's text; so the five sections of the Profound Meaning go no further than origin and trace — as in explaining the character "wondrous," there are ten for the origin and ten for the trace. The two essences of origin and trace do not differ in principle. The cause and fruit of long ago are called the cardinal doctrine of the origin; the cause and fruit discussed for the middle period and today are called the cardinal doctrine of the trace. The two functions of origin and trace are judged not by coarse and wondrous or broad and narrow, but only by near and far. The mark of the teaching in origin and trace is simply the mark that distinguishes provisional and real, long ago and recent. So one knows that the heart of the sūtra goes no further than origin and trace. "Looking up and taking in" the purport of origin and trace, the text and meaning of the teaching given in the three times are "clear and distinct" (冷然). 冷 means "clear and cool": on surveying it, one can tell things apart.

Seeing their old seeds, he had them take on the business and handed over the wealth; to restore their wondrous practice to its beginning, he displayed portents to startle them. So in this preface the words "taking pity on this" have a comprehensive meaning.

"Perfect" is another name for wondrous, and "ultimate" is the fruit of the wondrous dharma. Here he relies on the dharma of the fruit to praise the wondrous, as the sūtra says, "only buddhas can know"; hence "narrates the name." It is written 敘, "narrate," meaning to set out the author's intent. Were it written 序, "prelude," it would mean an orderly opening, as the six portents are the orderly opening of the main teaching; that is not the meaning here, so it is not written that way. "Merging with the hidden" means attaining the essence of the sūtra. In the general sense, this is the essence of all dharmas, all sūtras, all practices, and so on; in the particular sense, only seeing the true mark, as grasped in cause and fruit, is called attaining the essence. Hearing the Dharma, the pearl is tied into the robe: this is the perfect cause; receiving the prediction, the pearl is shown: this is called the perfect fruit; so the pearl narrates the cardinal doctrine. Again, the pearl's own essence is neither tied nor shown; taking up the pearl again to discuss tying and showing, it becomes the cardinal doctrine that belongs to the essence. Transformation completed and principle revealed is the power of the Lotus; hence "both being quiescent narrates the function." Truly because the provisional and the real operate together, taming capacities and bringing them into quiescence becomes the function that belongs to the cardinal doctrine. The four flowers and six tremors stand at the head of the whole sūtra; hence "narrates the teaching." The general prologue and the particular prologue both narrate the teaching; since the general is diffuse, the general is set aside for the particular. Of the particular prologue's five [portents], the other four are still loose; the portent displayed, indicating the reward, has the most fitting mark. Among the six portents the other four are still loose — none matches the heavenly flowers of the four stages and the earth-tremors of the six rounds of breaking delusion; the purport of the teaching lies here, so he cites these briefly. Above, "opening the gate of skillful means" draws on that purport; hence the wording. Origin and trace are only a difference of far and near, and the Great Master's explanation has already made the meaning clear; hence "may be understood accordingly."

Speaking in general, origin and trace each have cutting off doubt and giving rise to faith; speaking in particular, increasing the path and diminishing birth lie only in the origin gate. Here, for brevity, giving rise to faith and increasing the path are taken as the trace gate, and cutting off doubt and diminishing birth as the origin gate. "The very limit of the Buddha's own state" is just the provisional and the real of the ten suchnesses; hence "only a buddha together with a buddha." Next, in the benefit, "those still in a born body, and those in a born body who have attained forbearance" and so on: before the grounds and before the abidings is "born body"; reaching the grounds and reaching the abidings is "born body that has attained forbearance," meaning that within the born body one can break ignorance and attain the forbearance of non-arising. If the trace gate benefits only the born body and the born body that has attained forbearance, the origin gate advances to the Dharma-body and the Dharma-body's final stage; the benefit covers both; hence "not only." Since the trace was let down, those receiving the teaching have gradually broadened, and that they gain the benefit of long ago and of the recent is the work of the Lotus.

Reading Notes for Fascicle 1

Translator's notes

Pumen's preface. 除饉男 ("man who removes famine") is an old rendering of bhikṣu. "The island barbarians rose in revolt" refers to the uprising of Yuan Chao (袁晁) on the Zhejiang coast in 762–763, which drove monks inland; Piling (毘壇, modern Changzhou) and Guoqing (國清寺, on Tiantai) are the places of Zhanran's teaching. The date line refers to Emperor Daizong's flight to Shan (陜) when Tibetan forces took Chang'an in 763; "the next year, jiachen" is 764, and 相 is the old name of the seventh month (Erya). "Returning before going far astray" (不遠而復) is from the Book of Changes, hexagram Fu; "contemplating the image and grasping the meaning" (觀象得意) echoes Wang Bi. "Ten fascicles" is the work's original division; the Taishō edition splits each into two, giving twenty. Who "the ten masters" (十子) are is not clear; the phrase is translated literally.

"My Record on … Calming and Contemplation." 「如《止觀》第一記」 refers to Zhanran's own commentary on the Mohe zhiguan, the Zhiguan fuxing chuanhong jue (T1912), fascicle one, where the "eloquence" of Zhiyi, the Tiantai mountains, and the crane grove are discussed. Zhanran cites it this way throughout the work.

Monks' histories. Zhanran writes "Prince Wenxuan of the Song," but the prince who sponsored such a compilation was Xiao Ziliang, Prince Wenxuan of the Southern Qi (齊), whose secretary was Wang Jin (王巾, style Jianqi 簡棲). "Dharma Master Jiao of Jiaxiang" is Huijiao (慧皎) of Jiaxiang monastery in Kuaiji; the Taishō text of his Biographies of Eminent Monks (T2059) has fourteen fascicles (thirteen plus a preface fascicle). Both are kept as written.

"Under the well-field system …" The Analects passage is 1.5, "a state of a thousand chariots"; the comment is that of Bao Xian (包咸). The Record of Rites quotations on the Left and Right Historians come from the "Jade Bead-Pendants" (玉藻) chapter, and "When the Great Way prevailed" from the "Conveyance of Rites" (禮運) chapter, which Zhanran counts as the seventh.

"Leaving the city at nineteen." The source reads 十九喻城; 喻 is almost certainly a copying error for 踰 ("cross over [the city wall]"), the usual phrase for the Buddha's departure. Translated as "leaving the city."

"Speaking of the pearl" (譚記). The site's Profound Meaning renders Guanding's 「談記是敘名」 as "speaking of the pearl narrates the name." Zhanran says 記 is probably an error for 託 ("relying"), so that the phrase picks up 「寄圓珠而談理……託寶所而論極」 at the start of Guanding's preface. The lemma keeps the site's English;

Zhanran's readings of single characters. In several places Zhanran glosses a character differently from how the site's Profound Meaning translates it: 況 as "likeness" (what a simile reveals) rather than "moreover"; 例 as "following the pattern [of the dharma section]" rather than "in general"; 詎 as "none"; and the two 體 in 「體化他之權實」 as "embody, penetrate" rather than "essence." The translation follows Zhanran in his explanations and keeps the site's English in the quoted lemmas.

"'Casting aside' is another name for setting aside." This sentence is set in quotation marks in the source, as if quoting the Profound Meaning; the same thought appears later in that work, where opening and setting aside are compared. The exact source line was not located.

The line break inside 「從迹 / 說」. In the CBETA Markdown file, the character 說 (line 425) was mistakenly turned into a heading ("### 說"). It is treated here as ordinary text, continuing the sentence 「且從迹說」.

The contest for Gopī. The story is from the Abhiniṣkramaṇa Sūtra (佛本行集經, T190), chapter on the contest of skills for a bride; Zhanran's quotation is a condensed paraphrase. The site's Profound Meaning calls the prince by his full name Sarvārthasiddha; Zhanran's text uses the short form 悉達, translated Siddhārtha.

The du tree. The Records of Wu (吳錄) with its "Geographical Treatise" is a lost Jin-dynasty work; this passage survives only in quotations like this one.

Nanyue's Four Peaceful Practices. This is Huisi's Meaning of the Peaceful Practices of the Lotus Sūtra (法華經安樂行義, T1926). The two sentences Zhanran quotes are close to, but not identical with, the Taishō text.

Born body and Dharma-body. Zhanran glosses the Profound Meaning's four kinds of bodhisattva benefited by the Lotus (生身／生身得忍／法身／法身後心) as stages: before the grounds or abidings; at the grounds or abidings with the forbearance of non-arising; dwelling in the land of real reward after giving up the born body; and virtual awakening. "Grounds" (地) belong to the distinct teaching and "abidings" (住) to the perfect teaching, so each stage is named twice.

The five flavors. In this part the stages of milk are named as on the site's Profound Meaning: milk, cream, fresh curdled butter, ripened butter, ghee (see decision 3).
