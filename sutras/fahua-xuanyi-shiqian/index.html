Explanatory Notes on the Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra.

Zhanran (湛然, 711–782), the sixth patriarch of Tiantai, wrote these twenty fascicles of notes on Zhiyi's Fahua Xuanyi, the great treatise on the five profound meanings of the Lotus Sūtra. He works through it passage by passage, quoting a phrase and then explaining its structure, its sources, and its meaning: he traces Zhiyi's citations to the sūtras and treatises, fills in the lists and numbers Zhiyi leaves out, answers objections, and defends the Tiantai reading against other schools. At the end of fascicle 14 stands the passage later circulated on its own as the Ten Gates of Nonduality.

How to read this collection

The notes are meant to be read with the Fahua Xuanyi. Wherever Zhanran quotes the Fahua Xuanyi, the English of this site's translation of it is used, so the two can be read side by side; where Zhanran reads a phrase differently, his explanation is followed and the difference is noted.

Each of the twenty pages is one fascicle of the Chinese. Fascicles 1–4 cover the opening sections; fascicles 5–14 the ten wondrous aspects of the trace; fascicle 15 the origin; fascicles 16–18 the essence, cardinal doctrine, and function; and fascicles 19–20 the classification of the teachings.

What each page includes

  • The Chinese source text beside our English translation, paragraph by paragraph, checked character by character against the CBETA edition so that nothing is skipped.
  • A short introduction to each fascicle, and reading notes on allusions, technical terms, and textual variants.
  • Terminology matched to this site's existing Tiantai translations.

The Text's Structure

Twenty fascicles, each following the matching portion of the Fahua Xuanyi.

Fascicle 1
Prefaces, the Five Sections, and the Marking of Name, Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching
The Explanatory Notes on the Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra is Zhanran's (湛然, 711–782) line-by-line commentary on Zhiyi's Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra (Fahua xuanyi), already translated in full on this site.
Translated
Fascicle 2
Explanations and Proof-Texts: The Five Flavors, the Indeterminate and the Secret, and the Rest of the Sevenfold Exposition
Fascicle two continues Zhanran's comments on the first fascicle of the Profound Meaning (fascicle-001 on this site), from the simile of the sun shining on the high mountains to the end of the sevenfold common exposition.
Translated
Fascicle 3
The Four Siddhāntas and the Refutation of Guangzhai
The third fascicle of the Explanatory Notes comments on the last part of the Profound Meaning's first fascicle (site page fascicle-001), from "Next, the four siddhāntas are explained under ten headings" to the end of the twenty-four objections against Guangzhai.
Translated
Fascicle 4
Broad, High, and Long; the Ten Suchnesses; Relative and Absolute Wondrous
Fascicle four of the Explanatory Notes covers the end of the Profound Meaning's first fascicle and most of its second (fascicle-001 to fascicle-002 on this site).
Translated
Fascicle 5
The Specific Explanation Begins: The Ten Wondrous Aspects and the Wondrous of the Object
The fifth fascicle of the Explanatory Notes begins the specific explanation of "wondrous" in the Profound Meaning.
Translated
Fascicle 6
The Two Truths, the Compendium of the Mahāyāna, and the Three, One, and No Truth
Fascicle six comments on the last part of the Profound Meaning's second fascicle and the opening of its third (fascicle-002 and fascicle-003 on this site), all within the wondrous of the object.
Translated
Fascicle 7
The Wondrous of Wisdom: The Twenty Wisdoms, the Four Good Roots, and Wisdom in Relation to Its Objects
Fascicle seven comments on the wondrous of wisdom, the second of the ten wondrous aspects of the trace gate (fascicle-003 on this site).
Translated
Fascicle 8
The End of the Wondrous of Wisdom and the Wondrous of Practice: Precepts and Concentration
Fascicle eight comments on the end of the Profound Meaning's third fascicle and the first part of its fourth (fascicle-003 and fascicle-004 on this site).
Translated
Fascicle 9
The Wondrous of Practice: The Wisdom Holy Practice, the Twenty-Five Samādhis, and the Five Practices
Fascicle nine comments on most of the wondrous of practice, the third of the ten wondrous aspects (fascicle-004 on this site), which the Profound Meaning builds on the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's five practices: the holy practice (precepts, concentration, wisdom), the brahma practice, the heavenly practice, the infant practice, and the sick practice.
Translated
Fascicle 10
The Wondrous of Stage: From the Small Herb to the Most Real Stage
Fascicle ten comments on the wondrous of stage, the fourth of the ten wondrous aspects (the end of fascicle-004 and the start of fascicle-005 on this site).
Translated
Fascicle 11
The Wondrous of Stage Concluded; the Three Dharmas by the Four Teachings
Fascicle eleven finishes Zhanran's comments on the wondrous of stage and begins the wondrous of the three tracks, all within fascicle-005 on this site.
Translated
Fascicle 12
The Three Dharmas Judged and Correlated; the Wondrous of Sympathetic Response
Fascicle twelve finishes Zhanran's comments on the wondrous of the three dharmas (the end of fascicle-005 on this site) and then covers the wondrous of sympathetic response (the opening of fascicle-006).
Translated
Fascicle 13
The Wondrous of Spiritual Powers, of Teaching the Dharma, and of the Retinue
Fascicle thirteen covers three of the ten wondrous aspects in the Profound Meaning's fascicle-006 on this site: spiritual powers (the seventh), teaching the Dharma (the eighth), and the retinue (the ninth).
Translated
Fascicle 14
The Wondrous of Merit and Benefit; the Provisional and the Real; the Ten Gates of Nonduality
Fascicle fourteen closes the trace gate.
Translated
Fascicle 15
Trace and Origin; the Origin's Ten Wondrous Aspects; the Lotus Flower and Its Seventeen Names
Fascicle fifteen covers the origin gate's own ten wondrous aspects, fascicle-007 on this site, from "Explaining Trace and Origin" through the opening of the lotus-flower exposition and the Fahua Lun's seventeen names.
Translated
Fascicle 16
The Lotus Illustration Concluded; the Common Name "Sūtra"; Clarifying the Essence
Fascicle sixteen spans the end of fascicle-007 and the first part of fascicle-008 on this site.
Translated
Fascicle 17
Illustration and Awakening; One Dharma Under Many Names; the Gates That Enter the True Mark and Their Contemplations
Fascicle seventeen comments on the second half of the Profound Meaning's fascicle-008 on this site and the opening of fascicle-009.
Translated
Fascicle 18
Judging the Gates; the True Mark as the Essence of All; Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine and the Function
Fascicle eighteen covers the end of fascicle-009 and the first part of fascicle-010 on this site.
Translated
Fascicle 19
Classifying the Teachings: The General Sense, the Southern Three and Northern Seven, and Clarifying the Difficulties
Fascicle nineteen opens the fifth and last of the five profound meanings, Classifying the Teachings, and covers the first three of its five items on the site's fascicle-010: the general sense, setting forth the differences, and most of clarifying the difficulties.
Translated
Fascicle 20
Classifying the Teachings: What to Accept, the Great Outline, and Zhang'an's Private Notes
The last fascicle comments on the second half of the Profound Meaning's fifth profound meaning, classifying the teachings (the later part of fascicle-010 on this site).

Translation Notes

Key terms, kept consistent across the whole text and with this site's other Tiantai translations. See the full translation notes, and also this site's note on translation method.

迹 / 本 — the trace / the originThe two gates of the Lotus Sūtra.
十妙 — the ten wondrous aspectsObject, wisdom, practice, stage, the three tracks, sympathetic response, spiritual powers, teaching the Dharma, the retinue, and merit and benefit.
三軌 — the three tracksReal nature, contemplative illumination, and resource-and-accomplishment.
實相 — the true markThe site's Fahua Xuanyi sometimes has "real mark"; it is kept inside quotations.
麁 / 妙 — coarse / wondrousWith 相待 / 絕待, the relative and the absolute wondrous.
五味 — milk, cream, fresh curdled butter, ripened butter, gheeIn quotations, the site's Fahua Xuanyi wording is kept.
正因 / 緣因 / 了因 — the correct cause / the conditioning cause / the revealing causeThe three causes of buddha-nature, matched to this site's Fahua Xuanyi and Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra.
《止觀》第N記 — my Record on the Nth fascicle of Calming and ContemplationZhanran's own commentary on the Mohe Zhiguan.
