Illustration and Awakening; One Dharma Under Many Names; the Gates That Enter the True Mark and Their Contemplations

Fascicle seventeen comments on the second half of the Profound Meaning's fascicle-008 on this site and the opening of fascicle-009. Zhanran first finishes the sifting of the essence: the three illustrations (three beasts, two gems, gold in a stone), which he reads as sifting out the one-sided real and the mere middle when the distinct and perfect middles are folded into the shared teaching's real truth, and then matches with the three virtues; and the distinguishing by awakening, which he maps onto the stages from the five ranks through the purity of the six faculties to the first abiding. On "one dharma under many names" he explains the twelve names as three per gate, the disputes in which great and small vehicles wrest gates from one another, and the man of many names, with long excerpts on Zhang Yi and Fan Li from the Records of the Grand Historian. On "the gates that enter the true mark" he outlines the sixteen gates, then follows the ten modes of contemplation through the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct gates of existence, with glosses on Devadatta and the Viśeṣacinti's thirty-two kinds of compassion. Finally he divides the ten distinctions between the distinct and perfect gates, comments on the perfect contemplation in ten modes, and closes with the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's parable of the stolen cows.

Distinguishing by Way of Illustration

◎五約譬中二：初敘傍正意，次正明用譬。初言「兼明」等者，三獸二珠約開合為喻，黃石中金約破會為譬；雖此二意，正為顯體。

Ru-Yi Translation

◎Fifth, in terms of illustration, two: first setting out the meaning of the incidental and the principal; next, the use of illustration proper. First, "while also clarifying" and so on: the three beasts and the two gems serve as illustrations in terms of opening and closing, and the gold within the yellow stone serves as an illustration in terms of breaking through and gathering together; though there are these two meanings, the principal purpose is to reveal the essence.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the use of illustration proper, two: first explaining the three illustrations proper; next, from "Citing these three illustrations" on, making clear the intent of using the three illustrations. The explanation is itself in three. First, borrowing the three beasts, three: first the explanation proper; next, from "An illustration of this kind" on, showing the essence; third, from "This distinguishing is made" on, the concluding indication. The first passage again two: first, the illustration of the three beasts originally illustrates the shared [teaching]: within the real truth of the shared [teaching] there is emptiness and there is the middle, and the elephant's non-emptiness is taken as the essence of the present sūtra. Next, from "Even reaching it has two further kinds" on, it sifts again in terms of the teaching-path: the mere middle is still the small elephant, and only the non-emptiness of the perfect teaching is called the great elephant. Next, from "An illustration of this kind" on, it shows that within the perfect it differs from the shared and the distinct, directly revealing the inconceivable essence of the present sūtra. Third, from "This distinguishing is made" on, it concludes the original intent of the illustration. The three beasts were originally borrowed to illustrate the shared teaching, so it concludes again by saying "in terms of emptiness and the middle taken jointly as the real truth." Since the real truth contains one real and two middles, one must sift out the one-sided real and the mere middle as not the essence of this sūtra. This sifts the essence of the sūtra in terms of the perfect and the distinct entering the shared. Hence we know that the explanations of others do not even recognize the non-emptiness of the small elephant — how much less could they discern the non-emptiness of the great elephant? How much less could they know that the two middles lie together within the real truth? They only know the great elephant as the Great Vehicle in general.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, further borrowing the illustration of the two gems, again two: first in terms of the two gems proper; next, again in terms of the single wish-fulfilling jewel alone. The first passage again two: first the explanation proper; next the concluding indication of the sūtra's essence. First, it says that what cannot rain down treasures is called mere emptiness, and what can rain down treasures is called the emptiness of all dharmas. Emptiness contains two meanings, so it must be sifted. Since the name "emptiness" is no different, it says they "resemble one another"; since mere emptiness is only empty, it says "merely empty" — this is the one-sided real, the mere emptiness, of the shared teaching. Set against the conventional truth of the distinct teaching, the emptiness of the middle there does not contain all dharmas, so it says "merely empty and unable to rain down treasures"; being both empty and raining is because it is at once the middle and at once the provisional. Mere emptiness and non-emptiness are provisional statements of the teaching-path, so the ability to rain directly illustrates the real middle. This is the account of the perfect teaching entering the distinct: the distinct teaching takes existence and non-existence together as the conventional truth, and the perfect, entering it, takes the two middles as the real — hence "the real middle." It sifts out the mere middle as not the essence of this sūtra; therefore it again uses the illustration of the wish-fulfilling maṇi, to discern gain and loss. Thus the preceding illustration of the three beasts was in terms of the distinct and the perfect entering the shared, so within what enters it must sift out the sequential middle. Because it speaks of what enters and what is entered together, it says the sifting is made in terms of "emptiness and the middle taken jointly as the real truth." Here it is likewise: on the side of what is entered, it is the middle way of the distinct teaching; it only sifts what is entered, and what enters is wholly wondrous — this is exactly the sūtra's essence. Here too it discusses what enters and what is entered together; that the side entered is still spoken of as the conventional is in order to distinguish it from the preceding, so it says the sifting is made in terms of "existence and non-existence taken together as the conventional."

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, further using the wish-fulfilling jewel as an illustration, also two: first explaining, next concluding. In the explanation, "wish-fulfilling" is likewise the same name, just as the two emptinesses share the same name; hence it says the sifting is made in terms of "the real truth that contains the middle within it." Its main purport is no different from the two elephants above; here it is shown again. The two illustrations borrowed above were both in terms of the teaching-path, so they divided two elephants and two gems; here it is in terms of principle, and principle is originally not two. It is because capacities take it up according to conditions that there come to be the real and the middle. Hence we know that the real and the middle are originally the same single real, and so the illustration says the substance of the wish-fulfilling jewel is no different. This again takes up the preceding distinct and perfect entering the shared: that within the real truth there are two middles means that persons have gain and loss, not that principle is so. Though it again uses the wish-fulfilling jewel as an illustration, it goes together with it to make up the meaning of the second, the illustration of the gems.

Ru-Yi Translation

The third illustration discusses teaching and principle together, so it applies generally to all ordinary beings and sages and to the teaching-dharmas. Within it, two: first in terms of differing capacities; next judging sameness and difference. The first, differing capacities, again three: first the illustration, next the application, third the concluding indication of the sūtra's essence. Of these four persons, the second includes the two vehicles of the two teachings and the bodhisattvas of the shared teaching, because the bodhisattvas of the Tripiṭaka are the same as ordinary beings. The next passage, two: first judging, next concluding the intent. As for "granting and withdrawing, breaking through and gathering together": the substance of the gold is no different, so it says "granting." Ordinary beings in fact have not yet attained the principle of the fruit; speaking in the mode of granting, one says the principle is not two — hence "granting." Though the principle is not two, ordinary beings in fact do not yet have the function that belongs to the fruit — hence "withdrawing." The merchant and the goldsmith are spoken of by this same standard. As for "breaking through": if the provisional is set aside and the real established, the elixir is still a different vessel — how much more gold and stone? If the provisional is merged and returned to the real, the substance being no different, why discriminate gold from stone? Ordinary beings are likewise; speaking by this standard, it can be seen.

The first passage, three. The first intent, "the first illustrates faculties" and so on: the two vehicles are shallow and the bodhisattvas deep, and those of sharp faculty among them further attain the middle. Next, "three kinds of disposition" likewise concerns three persons whose three dispositions each differ: so the first person enters emptiness from the provisional, seeking only to escape suffering; the next, the person of the distinct teaching, proceeds sequentially before the grounds; only the last person is able, beyond the mere middle of the distinct [teaching], to see the middle in all dharmas — hence it says "the last disposition seeks vastly and broadly." The last illustrates the three kinds of skillful means; ordinary beings have none at all, so they are set aside and not discussed.

Next, in concluding them as the three dharmas — Question: In the three illustrations above, each said "the essence of the present sūtra"; moreover, the three meanings were originally for revealing the essence. How can it here say that the three illustrations correspond to the three dharmas?

In the third intent it says "These three illustrations together are precisely the three virtues": the elephant illustrates the Dharma-body, because it reaches the bottom; the jewel illustrates liberation, because it can rain [treasures]; the immortal illustrates prajñā, because he abides in wondrous emptiness. This is speaking of them separately, so it makes this matching. If each illustration is taken as having all three, then "reaching the firm" illustrates the Dharma-body, "by the water" illustrates prajñā, and "arriving at the shore" illustrates liberation; the substance of the jewel illustrates the Dharma-body, the emptiness of the jewel illustrates prajñā, and raining treasures illustrates liberation; "refining" illustrates prajñā, "the substance of the gold" illustrates the Dharma-body, and "becoming an immortal" illustrates liberation. All take the Dharma-body as the sūtra's essence. Lest the essence be confused, it sifts out two virtues in terms of the three virtues and selects the Dharma-body. If one goes by the revealing of the essence, it too should be only the virtue of the Dharma-body. That it now speaks of the three virtues — the intent is again as before; reflect on it and it can be seen.

Distinguishing in Terms of Awakening

Chinese Source

約悟簡者，所以須此悟簡者，如釋十妙自有從因至果自行化他，故不須論悟，尚一一文下皆須觀心，及至釋經亦立觀心一門，經是教法故也。今明體中須約悟者，體通凡鄙，其名猶通是故須簡。下文明用，用是果上之法，宗是因果，不悟無果，故並不須約悟簡也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Distinguishing in terms of awakening: why this distinguishing in terms of awakening is needed. In explaining the ten wondrous aspects there is of itself [a course] from cause to fruit, one's own practice and transforming others, so there is no need to discuss awakening; even so, under each passage the contemplation of mind is required, and when it comes to explaining the sūtra a gate of contemplating the mind is also established, because the sūtra is a teaching-dharma. Now, in clarifying the essence, one must use awakening, because the essence is shared even by the ordinary and lowly, and its name, too, is shared, so it must be sifted. The text below clarifies the function, and the function is a dharma on the level of the fruit; the cardinal doctrine is cause and fruit, and without awakening there is no fruit; so neither of them needs to be sifted in terms of awakening.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, in revealing the right, two: first making clear that in the stages of the ordinary one is not yet awakened; next making clear that only in the stages of the sages is one awakened. The first passage, two: dharma and simile. The first passage runs from the beginning of the stage of contemplative practice to entering the purity of the six faculties, hence it says "clear and pure" and so on. "Bitter repentance to its very limit" is cultivating the fivefold repentance before the five ranks; the phrase "open and unfold" is for now spoken generally. Because practicing the fivefold repentance can draw the empowering support of the buddhas, it causes one to arouse the five ranks and on up to the first abiding. "The contemplation of mind becomes clear and pure" is entering the five ranks; "faith and understanding become empty and unobstructed" is the six faculties. In the simile it says "a person from a piece of wood" and so on: the preceding phrase is abbreviated and should further say "watching dust in water"; below, it concludes both together, saying that person, wood, and the rest are not clearly made out. Next, from "If one is able" on, making clear the stage of the sages: from the stage of resemblance one enters the stage of the first abiding. Within this, four: first generally making clear wisdom and severance; next, from "the pure mind" on, making clear object and wisdom; third, from "The Treatise says" on, citing proof; fourth, from "Stated briefly" on, showing the sūtra's essence. The first again two: first dharma, next simile. At the start of the first abiding one attains a portion of the sūtra's essence: the bright mirror and the clear water illustrate the extinction of ignorance; the forms of fish appearing of themselves illustrate the manifesting of the Dharma-body. The next passage again three: first wisdom, next the object, third the efficacy — that is, the mutual accord of object and wisdom. The essence revealed depends on the wisdom that accords with it, and the wisdom depends on the person; hence it says "Such a person, honored and wondrous, is then able to see prajñā itself." Next, in the object seen it says "one finger, two fingers" and so on, as in my Record on the fifth fascicle of Calming and Contemplation. The three fingers clearly distinct — this is truly the sūtra's essence.

One Dharma Under Many Names

Chinese Source

○三明一法異名中，文開四意，初出異名中為三：初來意，次列，三「無量」下破計。初如文。次列名中，始從妙有，終至寂滅，合十二名。所言「等」者，此十二名覽諸經論，今略列之，以例可知。初三是有門，次「畢竟」下三是空門，次「虛空」下三是空有門，次「非有非無」下三是非空非有門，為欲分對名義體故，每一門中且引三名以之為式。今文意者，此之四門，圓別教共若不簡之名體不分。圓人初心即觀四門，四門相攝體同名異正顯經體；別則遠期果地，仰信居初，先用方便一十二門，向後方觀此之四意，既存教道，諍訟易生，縱當門體與名義同，然與他門互相吞噉，故此學者非法毀人，良由不知體同名異，故云不識天主千名，而謂憍尸不是帝釋，故弘教者失旨於茲，將恐弘法利他之功不補非法毀人之失。三破計中二：初總破，次「小乘」下示偏計之失。又二：初斥大小兩乘各於其教法門互非，次「皆不」下以失佛法大意斥之。如天帝釋有千種名，此中即是千中之四，雖黨其門，所斥失大。「實相」下合譬，可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

○Third, in making clear one dharma under many names, the text opens four senses. First, in listing the differing names, three: first the reason it comes; next the list; third, from "Every one of these immeasurable, differing names" on, refuting the conceptions. Translator's notes

"The distinct and perfect entering the shared." In paragraphs 2–3 Zhanran reads the three-beasts and wish-fulfilling-jewel illustrations as cases where the middle of the distinct and perfect teachings is "entered into" (入) the shared teaching's real truth (別圓入通), and the perfect into the distinct (圓入別). "What enters" (能入) and "what is entered" (所入) are rendered literally. The sentence 「對別教俗諦中空不具諸法」 is difficult; it is taken to mean that, against the distinct teaching's conventional truth, the "emptiness" in question does not contain all dharmas.

The three-in-one matching of the illustrations. The tags Zhanran quotes in paragraph 8 (「得堅」「以水」「至岸」「脩治」「金體」「成仙」) do not appear word for word in the site's Chinese of the Profound Meaning. They are translated literally ("reaching the firm," "by the water," "arriving at the shore," "refining," "the substance of the gold," "becoming an immortal") rather than forced into the site's English.

「信解虛隔」. Paragraph 10 reads 「信解虛隔是六根」, while the Profound Meaning has 「信解虛融」. The English follows the site's wording in the quotation; 隔 may be a copying error for 融.

The five wondrous aspects and five identities. In paragraph 13, 「三總指廣，乃至五妙五即」 is translated literally. It seems to mean that the Profound Meaning's "and so on" after the three kinds of "nothing further to do" extends to further sets (perhaps five of the wondrous aspects and the five identities above identity in principle). The reading is uncertain.

Zhang Yi and Fan Li. The excerpts in paragraph 18 are close paraphrases of the Records of the Grand Historian. Zhanran labels the Zhang Yi passage "Hereditary Houses" (世家) and the Fan Li passage "Arrayed Traditions" (列傳), but Zhang Yi's biography is in the Arrayed Traditions, and the Fan Li story is told at the end of the Hereditary House of Goujian of Yue (with a further account in the "Biographies of Money-makers"). Translated as written. 鴟夷子 is left as "Master Chiyi" (literally "wine-skin").

The twelve names of the man of many names. Paragraph 18 says the Profound Meaning's list has only eleven items and proposes reading 「中庶人，信直人」 as two. On Zhanran's count, 「遍通達人」 and 「能破敵人」 must also be two items. The site's English joins both pairs.

雄渠 / 熊渠. Zhanran writes 「雄渠」 where the site's Chinese has 熊渠. The site's English romanization (Xiong Qu) fits both, so the quotation is unchanged.

Devadatta and Xiutuo. Paragraph 33 identifies 達兜 in the Profound Meaning as Devadatta (調達), who could not get the five hundred to teach him the supernormal powers. Zhanran's Record (T1912) likewise says, following the Great Treatise, that Devadatta obtained tainted powers 「依於修陀」. 脩陀 is left as "Xiutuo"; the Indic name has not been confirmed.

The Viśeṣacinti quotation. Paragraph 42 is close to the Viśeṣacintibrahma-paripṛcchā (T586), fascicle 2 (the five powers by which the Tathāgata teaches the Dharma). The sūtra's first power is 語說 ("speaking"); Zhanran has 一說.

The ten marks. Paragraph 44 cites "the twenty-third [fascicle] of the Great Sūtra." This matches the southern text (T375, fascicle 23; northern T374, fascicle 25): 色、聲、香、味、觸、生、住、滅、男、女. Zhanran's list omits sound and adds "change" (異) to arising, abiding, and extinction; it is translated as written.

King of Spotless Fame. The upāsaka 威德無垢稱王 heads the lay assembly in the opening of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T374, fascicle 1). The sūtra's list of antidote-gates is 苦樂、常無常…; Zhang'an's gloss on 恒/常 is translated as Zhanran quotes it.

Kāśyapa's "persons of deviant view." Paragraph 57 paraphrases the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, fascicle 7 (T374: 「自是之前，我等悉名邪見之人」).

Śāriputra's verse. The verse in paragraph 58 is the Lotus Sūtra, "Parable" chapter (T262), quoted exactly.

The stolen cows. Paragraph 81 quotes the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, fascicle 3 (T374), with small variants (鑽搖 for 攢搖; 生蘇 for 生酥; "even if we got it" for "even if we got the milk"). The note 「(止觀記具注解)」 inside the quotation is Zhanran's own cross-reference and is given in brackets. "The end of the fifth fascicle" refers to the close of the wondrous of the three tracks, where the site's fascicle-005 English has "close out the first half of the wish-fulfilling jewel." Zhanran's 「半如意珠則以迹中五妙為半」 is a paraphrase, not a verbatim quotation.

Cross-references. "The ninth fascicle" (paragraphs 22, 26) is the ninth fascicle of the Profound Meaning (site fascicle-009). "The fourth fascicle above" (paragraph 70) is unclear; it probably means the five practices in the wondrous of practice. The eighth mode (stages) in paragraph 74 has no number of its own; the sentence on the six identities follows the seventh directly.
