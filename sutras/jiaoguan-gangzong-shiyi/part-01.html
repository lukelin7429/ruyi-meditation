Glosses on the Opening Section, the Tripiṭaka Teaching, and the Shared Teaching

Ouyi Zhixu (1599–1655) wrote the Outline of the Teachings and Their Contemplations (Jiaoguan gangzong) as a compact chart of Tiantai doctrinal classification, and then added this short companion, Explaining the Outline of the Teachings and Their Contemplations (Jiaoguan gangzong shiyi, X0974). It is not a running commentary. Zhixu picks out phrases from the Outline — each one stands here as a heading, given in the wording of the site's translation of the Outline — and glosses only those he thinks need it. Part 1 begins with the table-of-contents line and an opening gloss on the words "teaching" and "contemplation" and on the "outline" (the headrope of a net) that holds the many teachings together. He then takes up terms from the Outline's account of the Five Periods: half-word and full-word, the gates of generation-and-extinction and of no generation, the Vaipulya's contrast of half and full, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's retrospective teaching, the Prajñā's mixture of teachings, and the secret and indeterminate methods. The rest of the part follows the Outline through the Tripiṭaka teaching (the canon, the four truths, the twelve links read through Consciousness-Only, the verse "All conditioned things are impermanent," and the path stages) and the first terms of the shared teaching, ending with its two kinds of "two truths containing the middle" and the three truths into which the distinct and perfect teachings enter.

Title and Opening Gloss: Teaching and Contemplation

Chinese Source

教觀綱宗釋義

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Outline of the Teachings and Their Contemplations

Chinese Source

北天目蕅益沙門智旭述

Ru-Yi Translation

Set forth by the monk Ouyi Zhixu of Beitianmu

Chinese Source

教者，聖人被下之言也。觀者，稟教修行之法也。教網萬殊，大綱唯八，而化儀無體，全攬化法為體，則藏、通、別、圓四教，乃教之綱也。依教設觀，亦復萬殊，而析空、體空、次第、一心四觀，收無不盡，則析體等四，乃觀之綱也。教觀雖各有四，而前三是權，後一是實，為實施權，開權顯實，乃佛法之宗要也。臨濟云：識取綱宗，本無實法。夫四教四觀，總為對治眾生見思、無明、重輕諸病而設，藥不執方，合宜而用，所以施此四教，須有頓、漸、秘密、不定四種之殊，豈容執著？又為實施權，則不可執實而廢權，開權顯實，則不可執權定異實，故云無實法也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Teaching" (教) is the words of a sage bestowed on those below. "Contemplation" (觀) is the method of cultivating practice in accord with the teaching. The net of the teaching has ten thousand differences, but its great outline (大綱) is only eight; and since the methods have no essence of their own but take the [teachings of] content wholly as their essence, the four teachings — Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect — are the outline of the teaching. The contemplations set up in dependence on the teaching likewise have ten thousand differences, yet the four contemplations — analytical emptiness, embodied emptiness, the sequential, and the one mind — gather them in with nothing left over; so the four, analytical, embodied, and the rest, are the outline of contemplation. Though teaching and contemplation each have four, the first three are provisional and the last one is real; establishing the provisional for the sake of the real, and opening the provisional to reveal the real, is the cardinal essential (宗要) of the Buddha-dharma. Linji says: "Grasp the guiding thread of the teaching (綱宗) — there was never any real dharma to begin with." Now the four teachings and four contemplations were all set up to counteract the illnesses of sentient beings — views and habituated thought, and ignorance — heavy and light. Medicine is not tied to one prescription; it is used as is fitting. Therefore, in bestowing these four teachings, there must be the differences of the four kinds — sudden, gradual, secret, and indeterminate; how could attachment be allowed? Further, since the provisional is established for the sake of the real, one may not cling to the real and discard the provisional; since the provisional is opened to reveal the real, one may not cling to the provisional and fix it as different from the real. Hence it says "never any real dharma."

Glosses on the Opening Section

Chinese Source

觀非教不正四句

Ru-Yi Translation

The Four Lines Beginning "Contemplation without Teaching Is Not Correct"

Chinese Source

問：教觀止約自行化他，本無二理，何得云有教無觀、有觀無教耶？答：得意之人，舉一教字，教為法界，便具觀法，不必更別言觀；舉一觀字，觀為法界，便具教法，不必更別言教。只因眾生但認語言為教，不能與觀相應；但認工夫為觀，不能與教相應。故設做工夫，不以教印，則盲修瞎鍊，未免行邪險徑，名之為殆，猶所云思而不學也；設學文字，不解觀心，則說食數寶，究竟茫無受用，名之為罔，猶所云學而不思也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Teaching and contemplation concern only practice for oneself and transforming others; fundamentally there are not two principles. How can it say there is "teaching without contemplation" and "contemplation without teaching"? Answer: For a person who has grasped the intent, raising the single word "teaching," the teaching is the dharma-realm and already possesses the method of contemplation, and there is no need to speak of contemplation separately; raising the single word "contemplation," the contemplation is the dharma-realm and already possesses the dharma of teaching, and there is no need to speak of teaching separately. It is only because sentient beings take mere words and speech to be teaching and cannot accord with contemplation, and take mere effort to be contemplation and cannot accord with teaching. So if one makes effort without sealing it with the teaching, it is blind cultivation and blind refining, and one cannot avoid walking wrong and dangerous byways; this is called "peril" (殆), like what is said: "thinking without learning." If one studies the written word without understanding the contemplation of mind, it is talking of food and counting [another's] treasure; in the end one is at a loss, with no benefit received; this is called "being lost" (罔), like what is said: "learning without thinking."

Chinese Source

依教設觀，數亦略同

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Contemplations Set Up in Dependence on These Teachings Likewise Number, in Brief, Roughly the Same"

Chinese Source

化法四教則有四觀，化儀四教但有三觀，無秘密觀，故云略同。又頓、漸、不定三教統該化法四教，而頓、漸、不定三觀唯約圓觀，故云略同，明其不盡同也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The four teachings of content have four contemplations; the four teachings of method have only three contemplations, with no secret contemplation; hence it says "roughly the same." Further, the three teachings — sudden, gradual, and indeterminate — comprise all four teachings of content, while the three contemplations — sudden, gradual, and indeterminate — pertain only to the perfect contemplation; hence it says "roughly the same," making clear that they are not entirely the same.

Chinese Source

半字滿字

Ru-Yi Translation

"Half-Word" and "Full-Word"

Chinese Source

喻出大涅槃經。例如此，方小學、大學也。三藏正化二乘，傍化菩薩，哆哆和和，漸誘初學，故如半字。通教正化菩薩，傍化二乘，名為大乘初門。別教獨菩薩法，圓教純明佛法，故如滿字。此略判也。細而論之，藏、通詮真，名為半字；別、圓詮中，名為滿字。又藏教不能通至別、圓，故但是半字；通教能通別、圓，是半而含滿；別教須用藏、通方便，是滿而帶半；唯圓教始終皆以佛知佛見而為修行，是滿字法門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The simile comes from the Great Nirvāṇa Sūtra. An example is the "lesser learning" and "greater learning" of this land. The Tripiṭaka [teaching] directly converts the two vehicles and incidentally converts bodhisattvas; with "ta-ta" and "he-he" it gradually entices beginning learners; hence it is like the half-word. The shared teaching directly converts bodhisattvas and incidentally converts the two vehicles, and is called the first gate of the Great Vehicle. The distinct teaching is a dharma of bodhisattvas alone, and the perfect teaching purely makes clear the Buddha-dharma; hence they are like the full-word. This is a brief classification. Discussed in detail: the Tripiṭaka and shared expound the real and are called half-word; the distinct and perfect expound the middle and are called full-word. Further, the Tripiṭaka teaching cannot lead through (通) to the distinct and perfect, so it is only half-word; the shared teaching can lead through to the distinct and perfect, so it is half yet containing the full; the distinct teaching must use the expedients of the Tripiṭaka and shared, so it is full yet accompanied by the half; only the perfect teaching, from beginning to end, takes the buddha's knowing and the buddha's seeing as its cultivation, and this is the dharma-gate of the full-word.

Chinese Source

生滅門、不生不滅門

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Gate of Generation-and-Extinction" and "the Gate of No Generation and No Extinction"

Chinese Source

三藏詮生滅，四諦由之證道，故名為生滅門。通教詮無生，四諦由之證道，故名為不生不滅門。亦一往略判也。細而論之，三藏或界內生滅門，通教是界內不生滅門，別教是界外生滅門，圓教是界外不生滅門。又通教約界內論，雖不生滅，約界外觀，仍屬生滅。以其但能體分段空，不能體變易空故。別教約界外論，雖云生滅，約界內觀，亦不生滅。以其雖不體變易空，亦能體分段空故。○言界內界外者，三界之內，見思為因，所感分段生死為果。藏、通二教，正治此病，名界內教。三界之外，方便、實報二土，無明為因，所感變易生死為果。別、圓二教，正治此病，名界外教。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Tripiṭaka expounds generation-and-extinction, and by its four truths one realizes the path; hence it is called the gate of generation-and-extinction. The shared teaching expounds no-generation, and by its four truths one realizes the path; hence it is called the gate of no generation and no extinction. This too is a first, brief classification. Discussed in detail: the Tripiṭaka may be called the gate of generation-and-extinction within the realms; the shared teaching is the gate of no generation-and-extinction within the realms; the distinct teaching is the gate of generation-and-extinction beyond the realms; the perfect teaching is the gate of no generation-and-extinction beyond the realms. Further, the shared teaching, discussed in terms of within the realms, is no generation-and-extinction, but viewed in terms of beyond the realms it still belongs to generation-and-extinction, because it can only embody the segmented [birth-and-death] as empty and cannot embody change as empty. The distinct teaching, discussed in terms of beyond the realms, is said to be generation-and-extinction, but viewed in terms of within the realms it is also no generation-and-extinction, because, although it does not embody change as empty, it can still embody the segmented as empty. ○ As for "within the realms" and "beyond the realms": within the three realms, views and habituated thought are the cause, and the segmented birth-and-death they bring about is the effect; the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings directly cure this illness and are called teachings within the realms. Beyond the three realms, in the two lands of expedients and of true reward, ignorance is the cause, and the birth-and-death of change it brings about is the effect; the distinct and perfect teachings directly cure this illness and are called teachings beyond the realms.

Chinese Source

對半明滿

Ru-Yi Translation

Contrasting with the Half to Clarify the Full

Chinese Source

諸方等經，彈偏斥小，歎大褒圓，故云對半明滿。然細論之，或以通別圓對破三藏，如維摩經五百弟子品是也。或以別圓對破藏通，如佛與彌勒論說俗諦，諸大弟子謂說真諦，真俗俱不解是也。或唯以圓對破藏通別教，如大佛頂首楞嚴經，訶斥不知常住真心性淨，明體用諸妄想，不知二種根本，錯亂修習，別成三乘魔外者是也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The various Vaipulya sūtras rebuke the partial and censure the small, praise the great and extol the perfect; hence it says "contrasting with the half to clarify the full." Discussed in detail, however: sometimes the shared, distinct, and perfect are used to contrast with and refute the Tripiṭaka, as in the chapter of the five hundred disciples in the Vimalakīrti Sūtra. Sometimes the distinct and perfect are used to contrast with and refute the Tripiṭaka and shared, as when the Buddha and Maitreya discussed the conventional truth, the great disciples thought they were discussing the truth of the real, and they understood neither the real nor the conventional. Sometimes the perfect alone is used to contrast with and refute the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct teachings, as in the Great Buddha's Crown Śūraṅgama Sūtra, which rebukes [those who] "do not know the eternally abiding true mind, the pure and bright essence of its nature, [and] use all kinds of deluded thoughts," and who, "not knowing the two kinds of root, cultivate in confusion" and "separately become" the three vehicles, demons, and outsiders.

Chinese Source

涅槃追說四教、追泯四教

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Nirvāṇa Retrospectively Teaches the Four Teachings and Retrospectively Dissolves the Four Teachings"

Chinese Source

重為未入實者廣談常住，是追說圓教也。又為末世鈍根重扶三權，是追說藏通別教也。雖復四教並談，而與方等四教不同。方等中之四教，藏通初後並不知常，別教初不知後方知（初心雖知中道，是但中理不具諸法，不同涅槃佛性），唯圓教初後俱知。今涅槃中之四教同知常住，故不同也。既前三教亦皆知常，不同方等中隔異之三，是追泯藏通別也。既扶三權以助一實，不同方等中對三之一，是追泯圓教也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Once more for the sake of those who had not yet entered the real, it broadly expounds the eternally abiding": this is retrospectively teaching the perfect teaching. "And again, for the dull faculties of the degenerate age, it once more upholds the three provisional teachings": this is retrospectively teaching the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct teachings. Although all four teachings are discussed together again, they are not the same as the four teachings of the Vaipulya. Of the four teachings in the Vaipulya, the Tripiṭaka and shared do not know the eternal at either beginning or end; the distinct teaching does not know it at the beginning and only knows it at the end (although the initial mind knows the middle way, it is the principle of the mere middle, which does not contain all dharmas, unlike the buddha-nature of the Nirvāṇa); only the perfect teaching knows it at both beginning and end. The four teachings in the present Nirvāṇa all alike know the eternally abiding; hence they are not the same. Since the first three teachings also all know the eternal, unlike the separated and different three in the Vaipulya, this is retrospectively dissolving the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct. Since the three provisional are upheld to assist the one real, unlike the one contrasted with the three in the Vaipulya, this is retrospectively dissolving the perfect teaching.

Chinese Source

般若帶通別，正明圓教

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Prajñā Is Accompanied by the Shared and Distinct, and Directly Clarifies the Perfect Teaching"

Chinese Source

會通一切世出世法，皆摩訶衍，互具互融，是圓教也。或說法性離一切相，非生死非涅槃，非有為非無為等，則是帶別教義。或說一切法如幻如夢，或說諸法實相，三乘同證，則是帶通教義。蓋般若明空有共不共，共即諸法本空，三乘同證，出三界四果幻縛，是通教義。不共即第一義空，菩薩獨入，斷三土二死因果，是別圓義。若云依第一義空得成諸法，猶是別義。若云即第一義空頓具諸法，諸法無非第一義空，乃是圓義也。細玩大部般若，顯發圓義為多，為鈍根人略帶通別方便耳。後人判般若為空宗者，但得共意，尚未知別教義，何況知有圓教義耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

Bringing together and passing through all worldly and world-transcending dharmas as all being the Mahāyāna, mutually containing and mutually fusing: this is the perfect teaching. Sometimes it says that the dharma-nature is apart from all marks, neither birth-and-death nor nirvāṇa, neither conditioned nor unconditioned, and so on: this is the meaning of the distinct teaching accompanying it. Sometimes it says that all dharmas are like illusions and dreams, or says that the three vehicles alike realize the true mark of dharmas: this is the meaning of the shared teaching accompanying it. For the Prajñā makes clear emptiness and existence as shared and unshared. The shared is that dharmas are originally empty, the three vehicles alike realize it, and they leave the illusory bonds of the three realms [in] the four fruits: this is the meaning of the shared teaching. The unshared is the emptiness of the ultimate meaning, which bodhisattvas alone enter, severing the causes and effects of the three lands and the two deaths: this is the meaning of the distinct and perfect. If one says that all dharmas are formed in dependence on the emptiness of the ultimate meaning, it is still the distinct meaning. If one says that the emptiness of the ultimate meaning itself suddenly contains all dharmas, and that all dharmas are nothing other than the emptiness of the ultimate meaning, this is the perfect meaning. Examining the great Prajñā corpus closely, it mostly reveals and brings out the perfect meaning, and only for people of dull faculties is it briefly accompanied by the expedients of the shared and distinct. Later people who classify the Prajñā as the school of emptiness have grasped only the shared intent; they do not yet know the meaning of the distinct teaching, still less know that there is the meaning of the perfect teaching.

Chinese Source

秘密不定

Ru-Yi Translation

The Secret and the Indeterminate

Chinese Source

具足，應云秘密不定，顯露不定。蓋一音說法，隨類異聞異解，就相知邊，則名顯露不定；就不相知邊，則名秘密不定也。秘密故無可傳，可傳便屬顯露不定。又或一座說法，兩人所聞所解不同，若互相知，則皆名顯露不定；若互不知，則皆名秘密不定。若此知彼，彼不知此，或彼知此，此不知彼，則約不知邊，便名秘密；若約知邊，便名不定也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In full, one should say "the secret indeterminate" and "the open-and-manifest indeterminate." For when the Dharma is taught with a single sound, each kind hears differently and understands differently; on the side where they know of each other, it is called the open-and-manifest indeterminate; on the side where they do not know of each other, it is called the secret indeterminate. Being secret, there is nothing that can be transmitted; what can be transmitted belongs to the open-and-manifest indeterminate. Further, sometimes the Dharma is taught at a single sitting and two people differ in what they hear and understand: if they know of each other, both are called the open-and-manifest indeterminate; if neither knows of the other, both are called the secret indeterminate. If this one knows of that one but that one does not know of this one, or that one knows of this one but this one does not know of that one, then on the side of not knowing it is called secret, and on the side of knowing it is called indeterminate.

Chinese Source

法尚無一，云何有四？

Ru-Yi Translation

"Even a Single Dharma Does Not Exist — How, Then, Could There Be Four?"

Chinese Source

諸法寂滅相，不可以言宣。又諸法從本來，常自寂滅相，寂滅則何一何四？此所謂識取綱宗，本無實法也。因病設藥，大綱唯四；開權顯實，究竟歸一。一為實，三為權，權實相對，皆是不得已而有言。若論本體，不但不可名四，亦復不可名一，故云非權非實也。然所謂非權非實者，非謂出權實外，別有一法，名為非權非實。但以權即實家之權，故即非權；實即權家之實，故即非實。猶云波即水家之波，故即非波；水即波家之水，故即非水。究竟同一濕性耳，濕性豈在波水外哉？

Ru-Yi Translation

"The quiescent, ceased mark of all phenomena cannot be conveyed in words." Again: "All phenomena, from the very beginning, are always themselves marked by quiescent cessation." In stillness-and-extinction, what one is there, and what four? This is what is meant by "Grasp the guiding thread of the teaching — there was never any real dharma to begin with." Medicine is set up according to the illness, and the great outline is only four; opening the provisional to reveal the real, in the end they return to one. The one is real, the three are provisional; the provisional and the real set against each other — all of this is words spoken only because there was no other way. If one discusses the original essence, not only can it not be named four, it cannot even be named one; hence it says "neither provisional nor real." Yet what is called "neither provisional nor real" does not mean that outside the provisional and the real there is separately one dharma named "neither provisional nor real." It is only that, since the provisional is the provisional of the real, it is not provisional; and since the real is the real of the provisional, it is not real. It is like saying: the wave is the wave of the water, so it is not a wave; the water is the water of the wave, so it is not water. In the end it is the same single wetness; how could wetness be outside wave and water?

The Tripiṭaka Teaching: Canon, Truths, and Links

Chinese Source

四阿含、毗尼、阿毗曇

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Four Āgamas," "the Vinaya," and "the Abhidharma"

Chinese Source

阿含，亦云阿笈多，亦云阿笈摩，此翻教，又翻法歸，又翻傳所說義，又翻無比法。通則大、小二教皆名阿含，別則增一阿含約數明法，長阿含明世界生起等事，中阿含明諸深義，雜阿含明諸禪法。乃摩訶迦葉請阿難陀結集，故云四阿含也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Āgama (ahan) is also transcribed ajiduo and ajimo; here it is translated "teaching," also translated "return of dharmas," also translated "transmitting the meaning of what was taught," also translated "incomparable dharma." In the general sense, both the great and the small teachings are called Āgama; in the specific sense, the Ekottarika Āgama makes clear dharmas by numbers, the Dīrgha Āgama makes clear such matters as the arising of worlds, the Madhyama Āgama makes clear the various deep meanings, and the Saṃyukta Āgama makes clear the various dharmas of dhyāna. It was Mahākāśyapa who asked Ānanda to compile them; hence they are called the four Āgamas.

Chinese Source

毗尼，亦云毗柰耶，亦云鼻柰耶，此翻善治，亦翻調伏，亦翻滅，亦翻律。通則佛所說教皆名正法毗尼，別俱因事所制五篇戒相。摩訶迦葉請優波離結集成毗尼藏，初唯一部，後因諍故，分為大眾、上座兩部，乃至分為十八部等，久後流傳，力留五部也。阿毗曇，亦云阿毗達磨，此翻無比法，又翻對法。通則佛所說法亦皆名阿毗曇，別則摩訶迦葉自結佛所說論及阿羅漢所造諸論，名為阿毗曇也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Vinaya (pini) is also transcribed pinaiye and binaiye; here it is translated "good governing," also translated "taming," also translated "extinction," also translated "discipline." On the path of liberation, contemplative wisdom realizes principle, and this is called knowledge: it is wisdom that is itself concentration.

Chinese Source

真無漏三十四心

Ru-Yi Translation

"The True, Uncontaminated Thirty-Four Minds"

Chinese Source

見道八忍、八智，名十六心。修道約三界、九地，各有一無礙、一解脫，名十八心。見、修合論，共成三十四心。此之定、慧，並從無始本具無漏種子所發現行，由此現行能證真諦我空真如，故云發真無漏三十四心也。三大阿僧祗劫所修福、智為增上緣，無始法爾無漏種子為親因緣，故得頓發、頓斷、頓證。然約所斷惑品，故分三十四心。若約能斷、能證，唯是無漏定、慧而已，所證秪是我空真如，亦名擇滅無為。無為真如不墮諸數，以無為法而有差別，故名三十四心。復分漸、頓，種種不同。

Ru-Yi Translation

The eight forbearances and eight kinds of knowledge of the path of vision are called the sixteen minds. The path of cultivation, in terms of the three realms and nine grounds, has for each one unobstructed [path] and one [path of] liberation, called the eighteen minds. Taking vision and cultivation together, there are thirty-four minds in all. This concentration and wisdom are both manifest activity brought forth from the uncontaminated seeds innately possessed from beginningless time; by this manifest activity one can realize the suchness of the emptiness of self of the truth of the real; hence it says "bringing forth the true, uncontaminated thirty-four minds." The merit and wisdom cultivated through three great asaṃkhyeya kalpas are the dominant condition, and the uncontaminated seeds, so by nature from beginningless time, are the direct causal condition; hence one can bring forth suddenly, sever suddenly, and realize suddenly. Yet in terms of the grades of delusion severed, it is divided into thirty-four minds. In terms of what severs and what realizes, it is only uncontaminated concentration and wisdom, and what is realized is only the suchness of the emptiness of self, also called the unconditioned of cessation through analysis. The unconditioned, suchness, does not fall into numbers; [but] "there are distinctions through the unconditioned dharma," hence it is called thirty-four minds. It is further divided into gradual and sudden, with all kinds of differences.

The Shared Teaching

Chinese Source

苦無逼近相四句

Ru-Yi Translation

The Four Lines "Suffering Has No Mark of Pressing Close"

Chinese Source

三界色心，依正諸果，名為苦諦。如幻如夢，當體全空，所以無逼近相，所謂生死即涅槃也。見思煩惱，有漏行業，名為集諦。不自他生，不共無因，所以無和合相，所謂煩惱即菩提也。既體達煩惱即是菩提，故道不二相，非別有法為能治也。既體達生死即是涅槃，故滅無生相，非別有法可證得也。然此但是即空，故與圓教即中不同。

Ru-Yi Translation

The form and mind of the three realms, the various fruits dependent and principal, are called the truth of suffering. Like illusions and dreams, in their very substance wholly empty, they therefore have no mark of pressing close; this is what is called "birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa." The afflictions of views and habituated thought, and contaminated action, are called the truth of accumulation. They arise neither from themselves nor from another, neither jointly nor without cause; they therefore have no mark of coming together; this is what is called "afflictions are themselves bodhi." Since one embodies and penetrates that afflictions are themselves bodhi, the path has no mark of duality: there is not separately a dharma that does the curing. Since one embodies and penetrates that birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa, cessation has no mark of generation: there is not separately a dharma to be realized. Yet this is only identity with emptiness, and so differs from the perfect teaching's identity with the middle.

Chinese Source

癡如虗空，乃至老死如虗空，無明如幻化不可得故，乃至老死如幻化不可得

Ru-Yi Translation

"Delusion Is Like Space, Down to Aging-and-Death, Which Is Like Space; Because Ignorance Is Like an Illusion, Unobtainable, Down to Aging-and-Death, Which Is Like an Illusion, Unobtainable"

Chinese Source

癡即無明也。十二因緣，流轉還滅，名相與前藏教不殊。但以體空智慧，了達生即非生，滅即非滅耳。癡如虗空等者，諦觀無明，不在內，不在外，不在中間，猶如虗空，但有名字，毫無實體也。行等例知。無明如幻化等者，無明不自生，不他生，不共生，不無因生，以當體不可得故。喻如幻化，本不可得，不可妄謂自他共離生也。行等亦然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Delusion is ignorance. The twelve links of dependent origination, in flowing on and in returning to cessation, are no different in name and mark from the preceding Tripiṭaka teaching. Only, with the wisdom of embodied emptiness, one thoroughly understands that generation is not generation and extinction is not extinction. "Delusion is like space" and so on: contemplating ignorance carefully, it is not inside, not outside, not in between, like empty space; it has only a name and no real substance at all. Volitional formations and the rest can be known by analogy. "Ignorance is like an illusion" and so on: ignorance does not arise from itself, does not arise from another, does not arise jointly, does not arise without cause, because in its very substance it is unobtainable. It is like an illusion, fundamentally unobtainable; one may not falsely say that it arises from itself, another, both, or neither. Volitional formations and the rest are likewise.

Chinese Source

幻有空二諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Two Truths of Illusory Existence and Emptiness"

Chinese Source

三界因果，色心依正，並是非有似有，猶如幻事，指此幻有以為俗諦。有既是幻，則當體全空，非滅故空，指此即空以為真諦。此則真俗不二，不同藏教真居事外。

Ru-Yi Translation

The causes and effects of the three realms, form and mind, dependent and principal, are all nonexistent yet seemingly existent, like illusory things; pointing to this illusory existence is taken as the conventional truth. Since existence is illusory, in its very substance it is wholly empty, empty not because of extinction; pointing to this identity with emptiness is taken as the truth of the real. Here the real and the conventional are not two, unlike the Tripiṭaka teaching, where the real lies outside phenomena.

Chinese Source

兩種含中二諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"Two Kinds of Two Truths That Contain the Middle"

Chinese Source

通含別者，幻有為俗，如前說幻有即空為真，而此空理即是真如，其體不空，故能為迷悟依。是則真諦之中含有別教中道理體，故名通含別二諦也。通含圓者，幻有為俗，亦如前說幻有即空為真，而此空理即如來藏，亦名為空，亦名不空。名空之時，空即具一切法，一切法皆趣此空；言不空時，不空亦即具一切法，一切法皆趣不空。是則真諦之中含有圓教、圓空、圓中道理，故名通含圓二諦也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The shared containing the distinct": illusory existence is the conventional, as said before; illusory existence being itself empty is the real; yet this principle of emptiness is suchness, whose essence is not empty, and so it can be the basis of delusion and awakening. Thus within the truth of the real is contained the principle-essence of the middle way of the distinct teaching; hence it is called the two truths of the shared containing the distinct. "The shared containing the perfect": illusory existence is the conventional, again as said before; illusory existence being itself empty is the real; yet this principle of emptiness is the tathāgatagarbha, which is named empty and also named not empty. When it is named empty, emptiness itself contains all dharmas, and all dharmas converge in this emptiness; when it is called not empty, the not-empty likewise itself contains all dharmas, and all dharmas converge in the not-empty. Thus within the truth of the real is contained the principle of the perfect teaching, the perfect emptiness, and the perfect middle way; hence it is called the two truths of the shared containing the perfect.

Chinese Source

別入通三諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Distinct Teaching Entering into the Shared Teaching's Three Truths"

Chinese Source

通教止云：有漏是俗，無漏是真。今立非漏非無漏句，以顯中道，則三諦義成。蓋由前通含別二諦，故成此別入通三諦也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The shared teaching says only: "The contaminated is the conventional; the uncontaminated is the real." Now the line "neither contaminated nor uncontaminated" is set up to reveal the middle way, and so the meaning of three truths is completed. For it is because of the preceding two truths of the shared containing the distinct that these three truths of the distinct entering the shared are formed.

Chinese Source

圓入通三諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Perfect Teaching Entering into the Shared Teaching's Three Truths"

Chinese Source

二諦同前。今立非漏非無漏句，以顯中道一切法皆趣非漏無漏，則此非漏無漏具一切法，圓中義成。蓋由前通含圓二諦，故成此圓入通三諦也。

The two truths are as before. Now the line "neither contaminated nor uncontaminated" is set up to reveal the middle way: all dharmas converge in the neither contaminated nor uncontaminated, and so this neither contaminated nor uncontaminated contains all dharmas, and the meaning of the perfect middle is completed. For it is because of the preceding two truths of the shared containing the perfect that these three truths of the perfect entering the shared are formed.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Headings are lemmas from the Outline. Every heading unit quotes a phrase of the Jiaoguan Gangzong; the English gives the site's wording for that phrase (site Jiaoguan Gangzong Parts 1–3), except where the glossary overrides it (see the decisions file). Three headings have no exact match in the site's Chinese: "Contrasting with the Half to Clarify the Full" (對半明滿; the site's text has the fuller 對三藏教半字生滅門說通別圓教滿字不生不滅門, Part 1), "The Secret and the Indeterminate," and "Suffering has no mark of pressing close" (see below).

Outline (綱). 綱 is the headrope of a fishing net, which is why unit 4 sets it against "the net of the teaching" (教網). Linji's line 識取綱宗，本無實法 is quoted as the Outline itself quotes it at its close (site Part 5: "grasp the guiding thread of the teaching — there was never any real dharma to begin with"). The line was not located in the CBETA files in the vault apart from the Outline (T1939).

Analects. "Thinking without learning" and "learning without thinking" (unit 6) are from Analects 2.15, which the Outline's opening adapts ("to have teaching without contemplation is to be lost; to have contemplation without teaching is to be in peril," site Part 1). "Talking of food and counting [another's] treasure" (說食數寶) is a stock image for learning without practice (e.g. T1966, T1850).

Half-word (unit 10). The simile is from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's chapter on letters, where the elder teaches his son the half-letters before the full grammar (site Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra Part 5 renders 半字 "the half-letters"; the glossary and the Outline use "half-word"). "Ta-ta and he-he" (哆哆和和) is infant babble, recalling the sūtra's "Infant Conduct" chapter (T374). The CBETA punctuation 例如此，方小學 should read 例如此方小學, "like this land's lesser learning"; the English follows that reading.

Lead through (通, unit 10). Zhixu plays on 通, the name of the shared teaching (通教), in the sense "lead through to." The two senses are kept apart in English: "shared" for the teaching, "lead through" for the verb.

"The Tripiṭaka may be called" (unit 12). The text has 三藏或界內生滅門, with 或 ("or, perhaps") where the parallel clauses have 是 ("is"). Alternatively: 或 is a copying error for 是.

Vimalakīrti and the Buddha with Maitreya (unit 14). "The chapter of the five hundred disciples" is Zhixu's name for the Vimalakīrti's "Disciples" chapter (弟子品), where the great disciples decline to visit Vimalakīrti (site Vimalakīrti Sūtra, "The Disciples Decline"). The story of the Buddha discussing the conventional truth with Maitreya while the śrāvakas thought it was the ultimate truth is from the Northern Nirvāṇa Sūtra, fascicle 32 (cited in Mohe zhiguan fuxing, T1912, and Zhiyi's Weimo jing xuanshu, T1777).

Śūraṅgama (unit 14). The phrases are from T945 fascicle 1: 由不知常住真心性淨明體，用諸妄想 and 皆由不知二種根本錯亂修習 … 別成聲聞、緣覺，及成外道、諸天魔王. CBETA's punctuation in this text (性淨，明體用諸妄想) splits the first phrase differently; the English follows the sūtra.

Quotations of the Outline in units 16 and 22. "Once more for the sake of those who had not yet entered the real…" and "the dull faculties of the degenerate age" follow the site's Part 1 (Zhixu here writes 末世鈍根 for the Outline's 末世根鈍). The two Lotus verses in unit 22 (and again in unit 32) follow the site's Lotus Sūtra, "The Buddha's Hesitation and the Turning of the Wheel" ("quiescent cessation"); elsewhere 寂滅 is "stillness-and-extinction," as in the site's Outline. "Neither provisional nor real" (非權非實) is not in the site's text of the Outline.

Lotus on the Tripiṭaka (unit 24). "Scholars of the Tripiṭaka, greedily attached to the lesser vehicle" is from the "Peaceful Practices" chapter (T262; site Lotus Sūtra, "The Four Peaceful Practices").

Ten agents and Consciousness-Only (unit 28). The two delusions (異熟果愚, 真實義愚) are Yogācāra terms (e.g. Abhidharmasamuccaya, T1605). The fourfold grouping of the links (two drawing, five drawn, three generating, two generated) is the Cheng weishi lun scheme Zhixu names as his source. 十使 "the ten agents" follows the round-2 Four Teachings draft.

All conditioned things are impermanent (unit 32). The verse is the Himalayan youth's verse of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (site Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra Part 14); its wording here follows the site's Outline Part 2. "Without severing delusion and craving, still giving rise to clarity and liberation" is the Vimalakīrti, "Disciples" chapter (site: "without extinguishing delusion and craving…"); Zhixu has 斷 "sever" for the sūtra's 滅.

"There are distinctions through the unconditioned dharma" (unit 40). From the Diamond Sūtra (T235): 一切賢聖皆以無為法而有差別.

Pressing close (逼近, units 41–42). The Outline reads 苦無逼迫相, "suffering has no mark of oppression" (site Part 3); this text has 逼近 in both heading and gloss, translated literally. Alternatively: 近 is a copying error for 迫.

Two truths containing the middle (units 47–52). The terms "the shared containing the distinct / the perfect," "grafted," and "the distinct / perfect teaching entering into the shared teaching's three truths" follow the site's Outline Part 3.
