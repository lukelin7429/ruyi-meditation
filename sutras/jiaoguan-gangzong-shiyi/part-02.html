Glosses on the Distinct and Perfect Teachings and the Ten Modes of Contemplation

Part 2 completes Ouyi Zhixu's glosses on his Outline of the Teachings and Their Contemplations. It opens with the longest gloss in the work, on the four lines "Dharmas do not arise from themselves…": Zhixu shows how each of the four teachings reads them, running the shared teaching's refutation of self-, other-, joint, and causeless arising first through the mind and then through its objects, and closing with the perfect teaching's "effort without effort." Shorter glosses follow on the shared teaching's truth of the real and the bodhisattva who keeps residual habit to be reborn; on the distinct teaching's five practices, single cause and fruit, the twelve links beyond the three realms, and its two and three truths; and on the perfect teaching's way of establishing beings, its inconceivable two truths, and the nirvāṇa of the three virtues. The perfect teaching's ten modes of contemplation, which the Outline only lists, receive a full account: each mode is offered to a grade of practitioner who did not enter through the one before, from the person of highest faculties, for whom the first mode alone contains all ten, down to the person of lower faculties, who needs all ten. An appendix sets out three kinds of loving-kindness — with beings as object, with dharmas as object, and without object — across the four teachings.

The Shared Teaching: The Four Lines on No-Generation

Chinese Source

諸法不自生四句

Ru-Yi Translation

The Four Lines "Dharmas Do Not Arise from Themselves"

Chinese Source

四性推撿，通乎四教。無生之義，局在後三。藏通二教，指六凡為諸法。別圓二教，指十界為諸法。藏教明六凡諸法，自種有故不從他，待眾緣故非自作，無作用故非共生，有功能故非無因（此四句出集論）。則顯緣起正理，不墮四執也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Investigating by the four natures runs through all four teachings; the meaning of no-generation is confined to the latter three. The Tripiṭaka and shared teachings point to the six ordinary [realms] as "dharmas"; the distinct and perfect teachings point to the ten realms as "dharmas." The Tripiṭaka teaching makes clear that the dharmas of the six ordinary [realms] do not come from another, because they exist from their own seeds; are not self-made, because they depend on many conditions; are not jointly generated, because they have no agency; and are not without cause, because they have capacity (these four lines come from the Abhidharmasamuccaya). This reveals the correct principle of conditioned arising, without falling into the four attachments.

Chinese Source

通教明六凡諸法，如幻如夢，如水中月，如空中花，非有似有，有即非有，不可說從自他共離而生。且如妄情所計，三界有為生法，雖復萬品差殊，略而言之，心境二字，收無不盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

The shared teaching makes clear that the dharmas of the six ordinary [realms] are like illusions and dreams, like the moon in water, like flowers in the sky, nonexistent yet seemingly existent, existent yet not existent; one cannot say that they arise from themselves, from another, jointly, or apart [from both]. Take, for instance, the conditioned, generated dharmas of the three realms as imagined by deluded feeling: though they differ in ten thousand kinds, put briefly, the two words "mind" and "object" gather them all in with nothing left over.

Chinese Source

先約心法以推四性。若自生者，從心生心，應有二心。又不對境時，心應常生，而實不生，故非自生。若他生者，從境生心，于我何與？又聖者對境，亦應生心，而聖不生，故非他生。若共生者，為心境各有生性，故共生耶？為心境各無生性，故共生耶？若心境各有生性，何須待共方生？又設待共方生，應有二心並生，謂一從心生，一從境生故。而實不然。若心境各無生性，共亦何能有生？如一砂無油，眾砂共壓，亦豈有油？故非共生。若云無因生者，既不因心，又不因境，心境尚無，云何能生于心？不應虗空突出心識，故非無因。如是四性，檢實不得心之生處，則知心本無生也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the four natures are investigated in terms of mind-dharmas. If [mind] arises from itself, mind arising from mind, there should be two minds. Further, when not facing an object, mind should constantly arise; but in fact it does not arise; hence it does not arise from itself. If it arises from another, mind arising from an object, what has that to do with me? Further, when a sage faces an object, mind should also arise; but the sage does not [give rise to it]; hence it does not arise from another. If it arises jointly: is it because mind and object each have the nature of arising that they arise jointly? Or is it because mind and object each lack the nature of arising that they arise jointly? If mind and object each have the nature of arising, why must they wait to be joined before arising? Further, supposing they must wait to be joined before arising, two minds should arise together, one arising from the mind and one arising from the object. But in fact it is not so. If mind and object each lack the nature of arising, how could they jointly have arising? Just as one grain of sand has no oil, how could many grains of sand pressed together have oil? Hence it does not arise jointly. If one says it arises without cause: since it depends neither on mind nor on object, and mind and object do not even exist, how could it give rise to mind? Mental consciousness should not burst forth from empty space; hence it is not without cause. Investigating the four natures in this way, one truly cannot find the place where mind arises; then one knows that mind fundamentally has no generation.

Chinese Source

次約境法以推四性。若自生者，從境生境，應有二境。又心不緣時，境應常現，而實不現，故非自生。若他生者，從心生境，境還屬心，何得名境？又心念兔角，兔應生角，而角不生，故非他生。若共生者，例如前破，但以二心並生，改作二境並生為異。若云無因生者，不應日中忽覩明月。如是四性，檢實不得境之生處，則知境亦無生也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the four natures are investigated in terms of object-dharmas. If [an object] arises from itself, object arising from object, there should be two objects. Further, when the mind is not taking it as object, the object should constantly appear; but in fact it does not appear; hence it does not arise from itself. If it arises from another, object arising from mind, the object would still belong to the mind; how could it be called an object? Further, when the mind thinks of a hare's horns, the hare should grow horns; but the horns do not grow; hence it does not arise from another. If it arises jointly, it is refuted as before, only changing "two minds arising together" to "two objects arising together." If one says it arises without cause, one should not suddenly see a bright moon at midday. Investigating the four natures in this way, one truly cannot find the place where the object arises; then one knows that the object too has no generation.

Chinese Source

是以若心若境，俱如幻夢，求其生性，了不可得，當體無生。此無生理，今古常然，始終不改，佛不能增，生不能減，但約種性差別，有三乘人，約悟入淺深，分十地位耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus whether mind or object, both are like illusions and dreams; seeking their nature of generation, it is utterly unobtainable; in their very substance they have no generation. This principle of no-generation is constant now and in the past, unchanged from beginning to end; the buddhas cannot increase it and beings cannot diminish it. It is only in terms of differences of lineage that there are people of the three vehicles, and in terms of the depth of awakening and entry that the ten grounds are distinguished.

Chinese Source

別教明十界諸法，自種有故不從他，待眾緣故非自作，無作用故非共生，有功能故非無因，則顯不思議緣起正理，皆以真如為迷悟依，藏識為持種依，以先知有真如藏識，而用四性推簡，入無生門，故為別教工夫也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The distinct teaching makes clear that the dharmas of the ten realms do not come from another, because they exist from their own seeds; are not self-made, because they depend on many conditions; are not jointly generated, because they have no agency; and are not without cause, because they have capacity. This reveals the correct principle of inconceivable conditioned arising: all take suchness as the basis of delusion and awakening and the storehouse consciousness as the basis that holds the seeds. Because one first knows that there are suchness and the storehouse consciousness, and then uses the four natures to investigate and sift, entering the gate of no-generation, it is the effort of the distinct teaching.

Chinese Source

圓教明十界諸法，非法性生，故非自生；非無明生，故非他生；非法性無明合生，故非共生；非離法性無明別有諸法，故非無因而生。隨一一法，體即法界，當體無生，無生而生，三千宛然，生即無生，三千無性，非生非無生，唯一實相，實相非生，生亦實相，實相非無生，無生亦實相，法住法位，世間相常，因緣即中，雙照空假，此是圓教初無生門。一門一切門，阿字即具四十二字功德，餘字亦然，故為圓妙無工夫之工夫。

Ru-Yi Translation

The perfect teaching makes clear that the dharmas of the ten realms are not generated by the dharma-nature, so they do not arise from themselves; are not generated by ignorance, so they do not arise from another; are not generated by the union of the dharma-nature and ignorance, so they do not arise jointly; and there are no dharmas separately apart from the dharma-nature and ignorance, so they do not arise without cause. In each and every dharma, its essence is the dharma-realm; in its very substance there is no generation; no generation, yet generated, the three thousand are clearly present; generation is itself no generation, the three thousand are without nature; neither generation nor no generation, there is only the single true mark. The true mark is not generation, [yet] generation too is the true mark; the true mark is not no-generation, [yet] no-generation too is the true mark. "Dharma abides in its own Dharma-position; the marks of the world are constant." Causes and conditions are themselves the middle, illuminating both emptiness and the provisional. This is the first gate of no-generation of the perfect teaching. One gate is all gates; the letter a contains the merits of the forty-two letters, and the other letters are likewise. Hence it is the effort without effort of the perfect and wondrous.

Chinese Source

解苦無苦而有真諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"Understanding Suffering as without Suffering, One Has the Truth of the Real"

Chinese Source

三界果報，名為三苦，解之則皆如空華，無逼近相，但是真空法性而已。真諦即在虗妄苦諦之上，如空處即在華處，無二處也。于此真諦，假立四名，四並即真，真非事外，乃是通教所詮之理。今人談玄說妙，逈超情見，壁立萬仞，總皆不能出此。

Ru-Yi Translation

The retribution of the three realms is called the three kinds of suffering; when understood, they are all like flowers in the sky, without any mark of pressing close; they are only true emptiness, the dharma-nature. The truth of the real is right on the false truth of suffering, as the place of emptiness is right where the flowers are; there are not two places. In this truth of the real, four names are provisionally set up; all four are the real, and the real is not outside phenomena; this is the principle expounded by the shared teaching. People today who talk of the profound and speak of the wondrous, far surpassing feelings and views, sheer as a wall ten thousand fathoms high, all cannot go beyond this.

Chinese Source

扶習潤生

Ru-Yi Translation

"Upholding Residual Habituation to Nourish Rebirth"

Chinese Source

別圓二教證中道理，則以中道法身為應化本，譬如月印萬川，不須惑業受生也。藏教說惑是實有，故菩薩不可斷惑，斷則不能三僧祗受生行道矣。通教說惑是幻有，故菩薩體幻而斷正使，正使既斷，設欲涅槃即便能入，但由本願力故不取涅槃，以神通力扶起三界思惑餘習，資于故業種子而得受生。所謂思惑餘習者，非貪似貪、非慢似慢、非癡似癡等是也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The distinct and perfect teachings realize the principle of the middle way, and so take the Dharma-body of the middle way as the root of response and transformation, like the moon imprinted in ten thousand rivers; there is no need of delusion and action to take birth. The Tripiṭaka teaching says delusion truly exists, so the bodhisattva may not sever delusion; if he severed it, he could not take birth through three asaṃkhyeyas to practice the path. The shared teaching says delusion is illusory existence, so the bodhisattva embodies the illusion and severs the fundamental afflictions; once the fundamental afflictions are severed, if he wished for nirvāṇa he could enter it at once; but by the power of his original vow he does not take nirvāṇa, and by the power of spiritual powers he upholds the residual habituation of the delusions of habituated thought of the three realms, assisting the seeds of former action, and so can take birth. What are called the residual habituations of the delusions of habituated thought are: not greed yet resembling greed, not pride yet resembling pride, not delusion yet resembling delusion, and so on.

The Distinct Teaching

Chinese Source

行則五行差別

Ru-Yi Translation

"As Practice, Its Five Practices Are Each Distinct from the Others"

Chinese Source

戒定慧三名為聖行，十住入空行也。慈悲喜捨名為梵行，十行十向入假行也。依理成行名為天行，初地已上中道行也。從天行體起化他用，示同小善名嬰兒行，即慈用也。示同煩惱名為病行，即悲用也。藏通二教但有聖行及少分梵行耳，今有五行故不同前。圓教則一行一切行，今有次第故不同後。

Ru-Yi Translation

Precepts, concentration, and wisdom are called the noble practice: the practice of entering emptiness of the ten abidings. Loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity are called the pure practice: the practice of entering the provisional of the ten practices and ten dedications. Practice accomplished in dependence on principle is called the heavenly practice: the practice of the middle way from the first ground upward. From the essence of the heavenly practice arises the function of transforming others: showing oneself the same as small goodness is called the infant practice, which is the function of loving-kindness; showing oneself the same as afflictions is called the sick practice, which is the function of compassion. The Tripiṭaka and shared teachings have only the noble practice and a small portion of the pure practice; this [teaching] has five practices, hence it differs from the preceding. In the perfect teaching one practice is all practices; this [teaching] has sequence, hence it differs from the following.

Chinese Source

一因逈出，不即二邊

Ru-Yi Translation

"As Cause, Its Single Cause Stands Apart, Not Identical with the Two Extremes"

Chinese Source

正因佛性，即指中道理體，非是生死，亦非涅槃，藏、通二教之所不詮。又復不具緣、了二因，故不同圓。

Ru-Yi Translation

The correct cause, buddha-nature, points to the principle-essence of the middle way; it is not birth-and-death, nor is it nirvāṇa; it is not expounded by the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings. Further, it does not contain the conditioning and revealing causes; hence it differs from the perfect.

Chinese Source

一果不融，諸位差別

Ru-Yi Translation

"As Fruit, Its Single Fruit Does Not Fuse the Distinctions among All the Stages"

Chinese Source

妙覺極果，方證法身，不云初發心時便成正覺也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Only at the ultimate fruit of wondrous awakening is the Dharma-body realized; it does not say that "at the very moment the resolve is first aroused, one attains correct enlightenment."

Chinese Source

不思議生滅十二因緣

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Inconceivable Twelve Links of Dependent Origination, with Generation-and-Extinction"

Chinese Source

枝末無明為分段生因，已如藏教中釋。根本無明者，不了心外無法，厭苦斷集，證滅修道，即是迷于中道真實義諦，發起界外偏真無漏行也。由此引生方便變易生死識種，復緣界外無漏正受而起涅槃法愛，深生取著，潤彼變易識種，令生有芽，招感方便不思議變易生死。又不了心外無法，訶棄真空，別修萬行，亦是迷于中道真實義諦，發起界外入假神通行也。由此引生實報變易生死識種，復緣界外勝妙境界而起神通法愛，深生取著，潤彼變易識種，令生有芽，招感實報不思議變易生死。直至佛果，則十二品無明盡滅，方得二死永亡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Branch-and-leaf ignorance is the cause of segmented birth, as already explained in the Tripiṭaka teaching. Root ignorance is this: not clearly knowing that there are no dharmas outside the mind, one loathes suffering, severs accumulation, realizes cessation, and cultivates the path; this is being deluded about the truth of the true meaning of the middle way, and initiating the one-sided real, uncontaminated practice beyond the realms. From this are drawn forth the consciousness-seeds of the birth-and-death of change [in the land] of expedients; further, taking the uncontaminated right reception beyond the realms as object, Dharma-attachment to nirvāṇa arises, and one deeply grasps and clings to it, moistening those consciousness-seeds of change and causing the sprout of becoming to be generated, bringing about the inconceivable birth-and-death of change [in the land] of expedients. Further, not clearly knowing that there are no dharmas outside the mind, one rebukes and discards true emptiness and separately cultivates the myriad practices; this too is being deluded about the truth of the true meaning of the middle way, and initiating the practice of spiritual powers that enters the provisional beyond the realms. From this are drawn forth the consciousness-seeds of the birth-and-death of change [in the land] of true reward; further, taking the excellent and wondrous objects beyond the realms as object, Dharma-attachment to spiritual powers arises, and one deeply grasps and clings to it, moistening those consciousness-seeds of change and causing the sprout of becoming to be generated, bringing about the inconceivable birth-and-death of change [in the land] of true reward. Only at the fruit of buddhahood are the twelve grades of ignorance completely extinguished and the two deaths forever gone.

Chinese Source

顯中二諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Two Truths That Reveal the Middle"

Chinese Source

束通教之二諦以為俗諦，則十界皆為俗也。以不有不空為真，則中道為十界迷悟所依，乃為真也。明言中道為真，故稱顯中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Bundling the two truths of the shared teaching together as the conventional truth, all ten realms are the conventional. Taking the neither existent nor empty as the real, the middle way is the basis of delusion and awakening for the ten realms, and so is the real. Since it says clearly that the middle way is the real, it is called "revealing the middle."

Chinese Source

圓入別二諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Perfect Teaching Entering into the Distinct Teaching's Two Truths"

Chinese Source

當教以真諦顯中，中但不有不空法性而已。今明法性具一切法，一切法皆趣不有不空。則不有不空之外，更無一法可得。是十界之俗，猶屬別義。不有不空之真，已成圓義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This teaching itself uses the truth of the real to reveal the middle, and the middle is only the dharma-nature that is neither existent nor empty. Now it makes clear that the dharma-nature contains all dharmas, and all dharmas converge in the neither existent nor empty. Then outside the neither existent nor empty there is no further dharma to be found. Here the conventional of the ten realms still belongs to the distinct meaning, [but] the real of the neither existent nor empty has already become the perfect meaning.

Chinese Source

別三諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Distinct Teaching's Own Three Truths"

Chinese Source

顯中二諦，則合有之與空為俗，不有不空為真。今分此俗諦為二，謂有即是俗，空即是真。取真諦之不有不空，名為中諦。則二諦三諦，但有開合之殊，義無增減。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the two truths that reveal the middle, existence and emptiness are combined as the conventional, and the neither existent nor empty is the real. Now this conventional truth is divided into two: that is, existence is the conventional and emptiness is the real. Taking the real truth's neither existent nor empty, it is named the truth of the middle. So between two truths and three truths there is only the difference of opening and combining; in meaning there is no increase or decrease.

Chinese Source

圓入別三諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Perfect Teaching Entering into the Distinct Teaching's Three Truths"

Chinese Source

有即是俗，空即是真，不改別義，不空不有，名為中諦。中諦具一切法，則為圓中矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Existence is the conventional and emptiness is the real, not altering the distinct meaning; the neither empty nor existent is named the truth of the middle. [When] the truth of the middle contains all dharmas, it is the perfect middle.

The Perfect Teaching

Chinese Source

圓建立眾生

Ru-Yi Translation

"Perfectly Establishing Sentient Beings"

Chinese Source

藏通別教，雖亦各論四悉檀益，而收機未普。今圓教人，窮心性源，徹法界底，自行既圓，故四益亦普。所謂為實施權，開權顯實，權實不二，用與適宜。如一雨所潤，大地普洽也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Although the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct teachings each also discuss the benefits of the four siddhāntas, their gathering in of capacities is not yet universal. Now the person of the perfect teaching exhausts the source of the mind-nature and penetrates to the bottom of the dharma-realm; since his own practice is perfect, the four benefits too are universal. That is to say: establishing the provisional for the sake of the real, opening the provisional to reveal the real, the provisional and the real not two, he uses them as is fitting — as with what one rain moistens, the whole great earth is universally soaked.

Chinese Source

不思議二諦

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Inconceivable Two Truths"

Chinese Source

二千性相，皆名為俗，一一無非實相，故名為真，三千之外無實相，則即俗恒真，事造即理具也，實相之外無三千，則即真恒俗，理具即事造也，故云理具事造。兩重三千，同居一念，亦可云同居一塵，同居一名，如一念，一切諸念亦然，如一塵，一切諸塵亦然，如一名，一切諸名亦然，真俗不二，真俗宛然。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three thousand natures-and-characteristics are all named the conventional; each and every one is nothing other than the true mark, hence it is named the real. Outside the three thousand there is no true mark: so, identical with the conventional, it is always the real — what is brought about in actual phenomena is itself inherently possessed in principle. Outside the true mark there are no three thousand: so, identical with the real, it is always the conventional — what is inherently possessed in principle is itself brought about in actual phenomena. Hence it says "inherently possessed in principle, and brought about in actual phenomena." The two aspects of the three thousand are together present in a single thought; one may also say they are together present in a single mote of dust, together present in a single name. As with a single thought, so with every thought; as with a single mote of dust, so with every mote of dust; as with a single name, so with every name. The real and the conventional are not two, [yet] the real and the conventional are clearly present.

Chinese Source

圓證三德涅槃

Ru-Yi Translation

"Perfectly Realizing the Nirvāṇa of the Three Virtues"

Chinese Source

法身德，名性淨涅槃。般若德，名圓淨涅槃。解脫德，名方便淨涅槃。唯是一心，更不縱橫並別，故名圓證。藏通二教，不知法身但有修得生空，般若僅能淨于見思，未得一切解脫。別教謂法身本有，般若修成，解脫始滿。則先後歷別，不名圓證。

Ru-Yi Translation

The virtue of the Dharma-body is called the nirvāṇa pure by nature. The virtue of prajñā is called the nirvāṇa perfectly pure. The virtue of liberation is called the nirvāṇa pure through expedients. It is only one mind, not further vertical or horizontal, side by side or separate; hence it is called "perfectly realizing." The Tripiṭaka and shared teachings do not know the Dharma-body and have only the emptiness of beings attained through cultivation; their prajñā can only purify views and habituated thought, and they have not attained liberation from everything. The distinct teaching says the Dharma-body exists originally, prajñā is accomplished through cultivation, and liberation is fulfilled only at the end. Then, being successive and separate, it is not called "perfectly realizing."

Chinese Source

十乘觀法

Ru-Yi Translation

The Ten Modes of Contemplation

First Mode: Contemplating the Inconceivable Object

Chinese Source

第一、觀不思議境者，心、佛、眾生三無差別。佛法、眾生法，一一皆不思議，皆得為所觀境。但初機之人則謂佛法太高，生法太廣，故但就現在自己陰、界、入法以為所觀。又捨界、入，但觀于陰；又捨前四陰，但觀識陰。又七、八二識微細難觀，前五根識現起時少，故但以現前一念第六意識為所觀境，近而復要也。現前意識不起則已，起則千十界中必落一界；若落一界，必具百界千如。以此隨落一界之心，非是心之少分，必是心之全體，心外更無百界千如故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, as for contemplating the inconceivable object: mind, buddha, and sentient beings — the three are without distinction. The dharmas of buddhas and the dharmas of sentient beings are each and every one inconceivable, and all can be the object contemplated. But a person of beginning capacity considers the dharmas of buddhas too high and the dharmas of beings too broad, and so takes only his own present dharmas of aggregates, elements, and sense-bases as what is contemplated. Further, setting aside the elements and sense-bases, he contemplates only the aggregates; further, setting aside the first four aggregates, he contemplates only the consciousness aggregate. Further, since the seventh and eighth consciousnesses are subtle and hard to contemplate, and the first five sense-consciousnesses seldom arise, he takes only the present single thought, the sixth, mental consciousness, as the object contemplated, which is near and also essential. If the present mental consciousness does not arise, that is all; if it arises, it must fall into one realm among the ten realms; if it falls into one realm, it must contain the hundred realms and thousand suchnesses. For this mind that falls into one realm is not a small portion of the mind; it must be the whole essence of the mind, since outside the mind there are no further hundred realms and thousand suchnesses.

Chinese Source

若頓了此現前一念全具百界千如、三千性相，無自性、無他性、無共性、無無因性、無性亦無性，則能頓證三德秘藏，則為初發心時便成正覺，則是定慧平等莊嚴，則已徧破三惑，則已了知一切諸法中皆有安樂性，則已具足圓妙道品，則已到于事理彼岸，則為登于菩薩正位，則為永超十魔、八魔，則已心心流注薩婆若海，是謂上上根人秪千一法具足十法乘也。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one suddenly understands that this present single thought wholly contains the hundred realms and thousand suchnesses, the three thousand realms of nature-and-characteristics, without self-nature, without other-nature, without joint nature, without causeless nature, and that being without nature is also without nature, then one can suddenly realize the secret storehouse of the three virtues; then "at the very moment the resolve is first aroused, one attains correct enlightenment"; then concentration and wisdom are equally adorned; then the three delusions are already comprehensively broken; then one already clearly knows that in all dharmas there is the nature of peace and happiness; then one already fully possesses the perfect and wondrous factors of the path; then one has already reached the other shore of phenomena and principle; then one has ascended to the correct stage of the bodhisattva; then one forever transcends the ten māras and the eight māras; then already, mind after mind, one flows into the ocean of omniscience (sarvajña). This is what is called the person of upper-upper faculties fully possessing the vehicle of the ten modes in just one mode.

Second Mode: Truly and Correctly Arousing the Bodhi-Mind

Chinese Source

若雖觀心，未能頓入，應念心、佛、眾生三無差別，云何諸佛已悟，我及眾生猶滯迷情？由是緣無作四諦，殷重發起四弘誓願。因發心故，一發一切發，登發心住，則是定慧平等莊嚴，徧破三惑，知一切法皆安樂性，具圓妙道品，到事理彼岸，登菩薩正位，超八魔、十魔，心心流入薩婆若海，知一切法本不思議，是謂上中根人于第二真正發菩提心，具足十法乘也。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, though contemplating the mind, one cannot enter suddenly, one should reflect: mind, buddha, and sentient beings — the three are without distinction; how is it that the buddhas have already awakened, while I and sentient beings are still stuck in deluded feelings? Therefore, taking the Four Noble Truths of the unconditioned as object, one earnestly and deeply arouses the four universal vows. Because of arousing the resolve, one arousal is all arousals; one ascends to the abiding of arousing the resolve; then concentration and wisdom are equally adorned, the three delusions are comprehensively broken, one knows that all dharmas are the nature of peace and happiness, fully possesses the perfect and wondrous factors of the path, reaches the other shore of phenomena and principle, ascends to the correct stage of the bodhisattva, transcends the eight māras and ten māras, mind after mind flows into the ocean of omniscience, and knows that all dharmas are fundamentally inconceivable. This is what is called the person of upper-middle faculties fully possessing the vehicle of the ten modes in the second, truly and correctly arousing the bodhi-mind.

Third Mode: Skillfully Settling the Mind through Calming-and-Contemplation

Chinese Source

若雖發心，心仍散動，未能登位，應念心體本來寂照，善巧調試，或以即寂之照令不沉沒，或以即照之寂令不浮散，浮沉病除，心體明淨，則能徧破三惑，證安樂性，具圓道品，到事理岸，登菩薩位，超越魔境，入薩婆若，知一切法本不思議，初發心時便成正覺，是謂上下根人於第三善巧安心止觀，具足十法乘也。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, though one has aroused the resolve, the mind is still scattered and moving and cannot ascend to the stage, one should reflect that the essence of the mind is originally still and illuminating, and skillfully regulate and test it: sometimes with the illumination that is itself stillness, so that it does not sink, sometimes with the stillness that is itself illumination, so that it does not float and scatter. When the illnesses of floating and sinking are removed and the essence of the mind is bright and pure, then one can comprehensively break the three delusions, realize the nature of peace and happiness, fully possess the perfect factors of the path, reach the shore of phenomena and principle, ascend to the bodhisattva stage, transcend the realm of māras, enter omniscience, know that all dharmas are fundamentally inconceivable, and "at the very moment the resolve is first aroused, attain correct enlightenment." This is what is called the person of upper-lower faculties fully possessing the vehicle of the ten modes in the third, skillfully settling the mind through calming-and-contemplation.

Fourth Mode: Comprehensively Breaking through Every Dharma

Chinese Source

若雖以止觀安心，心仍未安，未得即證寂照本體，必於所觀一念三千之境猶存意解，未知當下即空、假、中，應以四性而簡責之。其根利者，秪觀一念三千無自生性，即當悟入無生，無生則無不生，三諦圓顯，十乘具足。若根鈍者，破自則必計他，破他則必計共，破共必計無因，展轉破盡，方悟無生，具足十乘。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, though one settles the mind with calming-and-contemplation, the mind is still not settled and one has not attained immediate realization of the original essence of stillness and illumination, there must still remain some conceptual understanding about the object contemplated, three thousand realms in a single thought, and one does not yet know that right here it is itself empty, provisional, and the middle; one should sift and press it with the four natures. One of sharp faculties need only contemplate that three thousand realms in a single thought has no nature of self-generation, and he will at once awaken and enter no-generation; with no-generation there is nothing not generated, the three truths are perfectly revealed, and the ten modes are fully possessed. If one's faculties are dull, refuting self, he will certainly reckon other; refuting other, he will certainly reckon joint; refuting joint, he will certainly reckon causeless; only after refuting them all in turn does he awaken to no-generation and fully possess the ten modes.

Chinese Source

若猶未悟，必當度入相續假中，應觀此一念三千，為前念不滅後念續耶？為前念滅後念續耶？為前念亦滅亦不滅後念續耶？為前念非滅非不滅後念續耶？若仍不悟，必當度入待待假中，應觀此一念三千，為待有念而立耶？為待無念而立耶？為待亦有念亦無念而立耶？為待非有念非無念而立耶？如此展轉簡責，若能悟入無生無不生者，則知一切諸法中悉有安樂性，具圓道品，到事理岸，登菩薩位，超越魔境，入薩婆若，知一切法本不思議，便成正覺，定慧莊嚴，是謂中上根人於第四破法徧具足十法乘也。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one still has not awakened, one must cross into the provisional reality established by continuity, and should contemplate this three thousand realms in a single thought: does the following thought continue with the preceding thought not extinguished? Does the following thought continue with the preceding thought extinguished? Does the following thought continue with the preceding thought both extinguished and not extinguished? Does the following thought continue with the preceding thought neither extinguished nor not extinguished? If one still does not awaken, one must cross into the provisional reality established by mutual dependence, and should contemplate this three thousand realms in a single thought: is it established in dependence on having a thought? Is it established in dependence on having no thought? Is it established in dependence on both having and not having a thought? Is it established in dependence on neither having nor not having a thought? Sifting and pressing in turn in this way, if one can awaken and enter no-generation and nothing not generated, then one knows that in all dharmas there is the nature of peace and happiness, fully possesses the perfect factors of the path, reaches the shore of phenomena and principle, ascends to the bodhisattva stage, transcends the realm of māras, enters omniscience, knows that all dharmas are fundamentally inconceivable, attains correct enlightenment, and is adorned with concentration and wisdom. This is what is called the person of middle-upper faculties fully possessing the vehicle of the ten modes in the fourth, comprehensively breaking through every dharma.

Fifth Mode: Distinguishing Passage from Blockage

Chinese Source

若雖約因成相續相待，徧破諸惑，仍未入者，應思一切諸法中悉有安樂性，但除其病，不除其法，如金錍去膜養珠，如郢匠去堊存鼻，那得一向破法，則破却成塞。今須善識通塞，若塞須破，若通須護，如聖王輪寶，能破能安。由此識通塞故，即塞成通，煩惱即菩提，菩提通達，無復煩惱，生死即涅槃，涅槃寂滅，無復生死，則能具足圓妙道品，到事理岸，登菩薩位，超越魔境，入薩婆若，知一切法本不思議，便成正覺，定慧莊嚴，徧破三惑，是謂中中根人於第五識通塞具足十法乘也。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, though by way of [the provisional realities established] by cause, by continuity, and by mutual dependence one has comprehensively broken through the delusions, one still has not entered, one should reflect that in all dharmas there is the nature of peace and happiness: one removes only the illness, not the dharma, like the golden probe removing the film and nourishing the pearl, like the craftsman of Ying removing the plaster and keeping the nose. How could one simply break through dharmas all the way? Then the breaking would instead become blockage. Now one must skillfully distinguish passage from blockage: if blocked, it must be broken; if passable, it must be protected — like the wheel-treasure of the sage king, which can break and can pacify. Through thus distinguishing passage from blockage, blockage itself becomes passage: afflictions are themselves bodhi, and bodhi penetrates through, with no further affliction; birth-and-death is itself nirvāṇa, and nirvāṇa is stillness-and-extinction, with no further birth-and-death. Then one can fully possess the perfect and wondrous factors of the path, reach the shore of phenomena and principle, ascend to the bodhisattva stage, transcend the realm of māras, enter omniscience, know that all dharmas are fundamentally inconceivable, attain correct enlightenment, be adorned with concentration and wisdom, and comprehensively break the three delusions. This is what is called the person of middle-middle faculties fully possessing the vehicle of the ten modes in the fifth, distinguishing passage from blockage.

Sixth Mode: Harmonizing the Factors of the Path

Chinese Source

若雖識通塞，仍未能即塞成通，應觀現前一念三千性相不可思議，即是圓心念處、一心念處、一切心念處，正勤䇿發，緣如意定而生五根，令其增長而成五力，調停七覺，趣八正道，開圓三解脫門而入秘藏，則為到於事理彼岸，登菩薩位，超越魔境，入薩婆若，知一切法本不思議，便成正覺，定慧莊嚴，徧破三惑，證得諸法皆安樂性，是謂中下根人，於第六調適道品，具足十法乘也。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, though one distinguishes passage from blockage, one still cannot turn blockage itself into passage, one should contemplate that the present single thought, the three thousand natures-and-characteristics, is inconceivable: it is itself the perfect mind's foundation of mindfulness, the one mind's foundation of mindfulness, the foundation of mindfulness of all minds. With right effort one spurs it on, takes the concentration of the bases of spiritual power as object and generates the five faculties, makes them grow into the five powers, regulates the seven factors of awakening, proceeds on the eightfold right path, and opens the perfect three gates of liberation to enter the secret storehouse. Then one reaches the other shore of phenomena and principle, ascends to the bodhisattva stage, transcends the realm of māras, enters omniscience, knows that all dharmas are fundamentally inconceivable, attains correct enlightenment, is adorned with concentration and wisdom, comprehensively breaks the three delusions, and realizes that all dharmas are the nature of peace and happiness. This is what is called the person of middle-lower faculties fully possessing the vehicle of the ten modes in the sixth, harmonizing the factors of the path.

Seventh Mode: Counteracting and Helping to Open

Chinese Source

若雖調練無作道品，而觀慧力弱，蓋障偏起，不能入位，必有無始事障未除，應審觀察，何障偏重，數數現起，兼以事行而對治之，理觀為主，事行為助，正助合行，不惜身命，誓當尅證，終弗懈息，由事理二治，能斷無始事理二幻障故，豁然證入，位相分明，則為永超魔網，入薩婆若，知一切法本不思議，便成正覺，定慧莊嚴，徧破三惑，證安樂性，具足圓妙道品，是謂下上根人，於第七對治助開，具足十法乘也。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, though one has regulated and trained in the unconditioned factors of the path, the power of contemplative wisdom is weak, the hindrances and obstructions arise one-sidedly, and one cannot enter the stage, there must be beginningless phenomenal obstructions not yet removed. One should examine carefully which obstruction is especially heavy and arises again and again, and also counteract it with phenomenal practice, taking the contemplation of principle as the main thing and phenomenal practice as the aid, practicing the main and the auxiliary together, not sparing body and life, vowing to attain realization, never slacking or resting. Because the two remedies of phenomena and principle can sever the two illusory obstructions of phenomena and principle that are beginningless, one suddenly realizes and enters, and the marks of the stages are clear. Then one forever transcends the net of māras, enters omniscience, knows that all dharmas are fundamentally inconceivable, attains correct enlightenment, is adorned with concentration and wisdom, comprehensively breaks the three delusions, realizes the nature of peace and happiness, and fully possesses the perfect and wondrous factors of the path. This is what is called the person of lower-upper faculties fully possessing the vehicle of the ten modes in the seventh, counteracting and helping to open.

Eighth Mode: Knowing the Sequence of Stages

Chinese Source

然對治助開之時，縱令鈍根，必皆有益，倘不知次位，起增上慢，以凡濫聖，招過不輕，故須深自簡察，為究竟耶，為分證耶，為相似耶，抑亦僅僅小輕安耶，

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet when counteracting and helping to open, even one of dull faculties will certainly gain benefit. If one does not know the sequence of stages and gives rise to overweening pride, passing off the ordinary as the noble, the fault incurred is not light. Therefore one must deeply examine oneself: is it the ultimate? Is it a portion of realization? Is it resemblance? Or is it merely a slight lightness and ease?

Ninth Mode: Being Able to Bear Patiently

Chinese Source

既知位次，不起上慢，必有強軟諸魔，惱亂真修，須加安忍，不動不退，䇿進五品，而階十信，

Ru-Yi Translation

Once one knows the sequence of stages and does not give rise to overweening pride, there will certainly be harsh and gentle māras disturbing true cultivation; one must add patient endurance, neither moving nor regressing, spurring on through the five grades and climbing to the ten faiths.

Tenth Mode: Departing from Attachment to the Dharma

Chinese Source

既階十信，六根清淨，得順道法，易生法愛，須離法愛，而入分真，入分真已，分得大理，大誓願，大莊嚴，大智斷，大徧知，大道，大用，大權實，大利益，大無住，是謂下根人，具修十法，而成大乘，乘是大乘，遊于四方，直至道場，自運功畢，運他不休，故此十乘妙觀，全性起修，全修顯性，非橫非豎，橫豎該徹也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once one has climbed to the ten faiths, the six faculties are purified and one attains the dharma that accords with the path, and Dharma-attachment easily arises; one must depart from Dharma-attachment and enter [identity in] a portion of the truth. Having entered a portion of the truth, one attains in part the great principle, the great vow, the great adornment, the great wisdom and severance, the great universal knowledge, the great path, the great function, the great provisional and real, the great benefit, and the great non-abiding. This is what is called the person of lower faculties fully cultivating the ten modes and so accomplishing the Great Vehicle. Riding this great vehicle, one travels to the four directions, arriving directly at the site of awakening; one's own carrying is completed, and the carrying of others never rests. Hence these wondrous contemplations of the ten modes — the whole of the nature arising as cultivation, the whole of cultivation revealing the nature — are neither horizontal nor vertical, yet pervade both the horizontal and the vertical.

Ru-Yi Translation

Appendix: The Essence and Characteristics of the Three Kinds of Loving-Kindness

Chinese Source

生緣慈有六種差別：一、凡夫修生緣慈，成梵王福，以愛為體。二、外道修生緣慈，取梵天果，以見為體。三、藏教二乘修生緣慈，對治瞋恚，作父母親屬想，以世諦為體。菩薩修生緣慈，廣行濟度，亦以世諦為體。然皆不起我人等執，依折空觀得成。四、通教三乘修生緣慈，皆了生空，依體空觀得成。緣六凡境，亦得以世諦為體。五、別教次第先修生緣慈，亦以世諦為體，不起我人等妄執，亦是依析體二空觀成（十信析空，十住體空）。六、圓教一心中修生緣慈，知十法界即空假中，緣此即空假中之十界有情，一一皆作父母等想，故以妙有為門，得論六即。

Ru-Yi Translation

Loving-kindness with beings as object has six kinds of difference. First, ordinary people cultivate loving-kindness with beings as object and accomplish the merit of a Brahmā king, with craving as its essence. Second, outsiders cultivate loving-kindness with beings as object and attain the fruit of the Brahmā heaven, with views as its essence. Third, the two vehicles of the Tripiṭaka teaching cultivate loving-kindness with beings as object to counteract anger, thinking of [beings] as parents and kin, with the conventional truth as its essence; bodhisattvas cultivate loving-kindness with beings as object, broadly practicing rescue and deliverance, also with the conventional truth as its essence. Yet none of them gives rise to attachments to self, person, and the rest; it is accomplished in dependence on the contemplation of analytical emptiness. Fourth, the three vehicles of the shared teaching cultivate loving-kindness with beings as object, all clearly understanding the emptiness of beings; it is accomplished in dependence on the contemplation of embodied emptiness. Taking the objects of the six ordinary [realms] as object, it too can have the conventional truth as its essence. Fifth, the distinct teaching, in sequence, first cultivates loving-kindness with beings as object, also with the conventional truth as its essence, not giving rise to deluded attachments to self, person, and the rest; it too is accomplished in dependence on the contemplations of the two emptinesses, analytical and embodied (the ten faiths, analytical emptiness; the ten abidings, embodied emptiness). Sixth, the perfect teaching cultivates loving-kindness with beings as object within the one mind, knowing that the ten dharma-realms are themselves empty, provisional, and the middle; taking as object the sentient beings of the ten realms that are themselves empty, provisional, and the middle, it thinks of each and every one as parents and so on; hence it takes wondrous existence as its gate, and the six identities can be discussed.

Chinese Source

法緣慈有四種差別：一、藏教，二乘了生空故，觀諸眾生唯是四大五蘊，不生瞋惱，隨力濟度，以生空觀為體（菩薩雖未證空，亦得修空）。二、通教，三乘了生本空，法亦如幻，二乘隨力濟度，菩薩弘誓徧周，同以空觀為體。三、別教，次第修法緣慈，知空而不住空，悟緣生法，廣度九界，以從空入假觀為體。四、圓教，一心中修法緣慈，知十界緣生之法即空、假、中，緣此即空、假、中之十界法，知其無性，故以妙無為門，得論六即。

Ru-Yi Translation

Loving-kindness with dharmas as object has four kinds of difference. First, the Tripiṭaka teaching: because the two vehicles clearly understand the emptiness of beings, they contemplate all sentient beings as only the four great elements and the five aggregates, do not give rise to anger and vexation, and rescue and deliver according to their strength, with the contemplation of the emptiness of beings as its essence (though bodhisattvas have not realized emptiness, they too can cultivate emptiness). Second, the shared teaching: the three vehicles clearly understand that beings are originally empty and that dharmas too are like illusions; the two vehicles rescue and deliver according to their strength, and the bodhisattvas' universal vows extend everywhere, alike with the contemplation of emptiness as its essence. Third, the distinct teaching cultivates loving-kindness with dharmas as object in sequence, knowing emptiness yet not abiding in emptiness, awakening to conditionally generated dharmas, and broadly delivering the nine realms, with the contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness as its essence. Fourth, the perfect teaching cultivates loving-kindness with dharmas as object within the one mind, knowing that the conditionally generated dharmas of the ten realms are themselves empty, provisional, and the middle; taking as object the dharmas of the ten realms that are themselves empty, provisional, and the middle, it knows them to be without nature; hence it takes wondrous nonexistence as its gate, and the six identities can be discussed.

Chinese Source

無緣慈有二種差別：一、別教十回向，不緣十界假名之眾生，不緣十界五蘊之法相，惟緣中道佛性，故名無緣，以相以中道為體（若登初地後，則與圓慈同）。二、圓人初心，即了三千諸法，一一當體即中，中道之外，更無別法，能所頓絕，與拔難思，得論六即，故有觀行無緣慈，乃至究竟無緣慈義。只一慈體而論三慈，故三慈皆妙，三慈皆論六即。如慈心三昧，有此種種差別，悲、喜、捨亦如是。但約與樂名慈，拔苦名悲，見他得樂，心生適悅名喜，怨親平等一相，心無繫著名捨也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Loving-kindness without object has two kinds of difference. First, the ten dedications of the distinct teaching do not take as object the provisionally named sentient beings of the ten realms, nor take as object the dharma-characteristics of the five aggregates of the ten realms, but take as object only the middle way, buddha-nature; hence it is called "without object," with the mark of the middle way as its essence (once one has ascended to the first ground, it is the same as the perfect loving-kindness). Second, the person of the perfect [teaching], from the initial mind, clearly understands the three thousand dharmas, each and every one in its very substance being the middle; outside the middle way there is no separate dharma; subject and object are suddenly cut off, and the giving [of happiness] and the relieving [of suffering] are hard to conceive; the six identities can be discussed; hence there is the meaning of loving-kindness without object in contemplative practice, up to loving-kindness without object in the ultimate. Just one essence of loving-kindness is discussed as three kinds of loving-kindness; hence the three kinds of loving-kindness are all wondrous, and the six identities are discussed for all three kinds of loving-kindness. Just as the samādhi of the mind of loving-kindness has these various differences, so too do compassion, joy, and equanimity. Only, in terms of giving happiness it is named loving-kindness; relieving suffering is named compassion; seeing others attain happiness and the mind feeling delight is named joy; enemies and kin being equal, of a single mark, the mind without attachment, is named equanimity.

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

Lemmas. As in Part 1, the headings quote the Outline, given in the site's wording (site Jiaoguan Gangzong Part 3 for the shared teaching, Part 4 for the distinct teaching, Part 5 for the perfect teaching), with glossary terms substituted where the decisions file says so.

The four lines (unit 1). The Outline's verse 諸法不自生，亦不從他生，不共不無因，是故知無生 is from the opening of the Middle Treatise (site Outline Part 3: "Dharmas do not arise from themselves, nor do they arise from another…"). The Tripiṭaka and distinct teachings' four reasons (自種有故不從他…) are, as Zhixu says, from the Abhidharmasamuccaya: the wording is that of the Abhidharmasamuccaya-vyākhyā (雜集論, T1606), also cited as 對法論 by Fazang and Chengguan.

Lotus and the forty-two letters (unit 1). "Dharma abides in its own Dharma-position; the marks of the world are constant" adapts the site's Lotus Sūtra ("The Buddha's Hesitation and the Turning of the Wheel": "This Dharma abides in its own Dharma-position; the marks of the world are constantly abiding"); Zhixu writes 法住法位，世間相常 without 是 and 住. The letter a containing the merit of all forty-two letters refers to the forty-two letter gates of the Larger Prajñāpāramitā (site Mohe Zhiguan renders 四十二字門 "the forty-two letter gates").

Five practices (unit 7). Noble, pure, heavenly, infant, and sick practice (聖行、梵行、天行、嬰兒行、病行) are the five practices of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra; the chapter titles of the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra should be checked when it is published, so the names match.

Root ignorance (unit 13). "Right reception" renders 正受 (samāpatti). The two kinds of Dharma-attachment, to nirvāṇa and to spiritual powers, correspond to the two lands beyond the three realms, the land of expedients and the land of true reward.

"Three thousand" (unit 25). The text reads 二千性相, "two thousand natures-and-characteristics," almost certainly a copying error for 三千; the English follows 三千. "Inherently possessed in principle, and brought about in actual phenomena" and "together present in a single thought" follow the site's Outline Part 5.

The ten modes (units 28–29). The ten names follow the site's Outline Part 5 and Mohe Zhiguan fascicle 5 as far as the wording of this text allows: here 對治助開 is "counteracting and helping to open" (MZ: "auxiliary remedies to open the way"), 破法徧 "comprehensively breaking through every dharma" (MZ), 識通塞 "distinguishing passage from blockage" (Outline). Each mode is assigned to one of eight grades of faculty (上上, 上中, 上下, 中上, 中中, 中下, 下上, 下), translated literally ("upper-upper" and so on). Modes 8–10 are run together in the Chinese without the closing formula; the subheadings mark where each begins.

Copying errors in unit 29. 千十界中 ("among the ten realms") and 秪千一法 ("in just one mode") read 千 as 于/於; 待待假 is read as 相待假, "the provisional reality established by mutual dependence" (glossary).

Golden probe and the craftsman of Ying (unit 29). The golden probe that removes the film from the eye is the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's simile (glossary: "golden probe"); "removing the film and nourishing the pearl" (除膜養珠) is from Mohe Zhiguan's own section on distinguishing passage and blockage ("break only the blockage and keep the passage"), which Zhixu is following here. The craftsman of Ying who swings his axe to take a fleck of plaster off a man's nose without harming it is from the Zhuangzi, "Xu Wugui." The sage king's wheel-treasure that "can break and can pacify": source not located.

Omniscience (薩婆若). "The ocean of omniscience (sarvajña)" follows the site's Mohe Zhiguan.

Ten great [attainments] (unit 29). The list "the great principle, the great vow … the great non-abiding" is a list of ten "great" attainments of the Great Vehicle; source not located. "Riding this great vehicle, one travels to the four directions" echoes the Lotus parable of the burning house, as in the Outline Part 5.

Analytical emptiness (unit 31). The text has 折空觀 for 析空觀; translated as "analytical emptiness."

"With the mark of the middle way as its essence" (unit 33). The text reads 以相以中道為體, which looks corrupt. Alternatively: 以但中道為體, "with the mere middle way as its essence" (the distinct teaching's middle), or 以相似中道為體, "with the resembling middle way," since the ten dedications are the distinct teaching's stage of resemblance.

Three kinds of loving-kindness (units 30–33). Loving-kindness with beings as object, with dharmas as object, and without object (生緣慈、法緣慈、無緣慈) are the three kinds named in the Great Treatise (眾生緣、法緣、無緣); Zhixu distributes them across the four teachings.
