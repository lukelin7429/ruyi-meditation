Belief and Understanding: The Hired Son, the Household, and the Ten Kindnesses

This part continues Zhanran's commentary on the Belief and Understanding chapter, following the second half of the Textual Commentary's sixth fascicle (the site's fascicle-006). It takes up the parable of the destitute son from the father's sending of attendants to the closing verses of thanks. Zhanran reads the attendants both as the teaching sent out from principle and as the four great bodhisattvas who expound the forty stages in the Avataṃsaka, and explains why the second pair of messengers is sent "secretly." A long section works through the phrase "on another day," distinguishing what the disciples received within the limits of the teaching they actually heard from what they received by anticipation, reaching back to the dharma-body's illumination before the bodhi tree. He then matches the son's labor to the seven groups of the factors of the path, sorts out the readings of "twenty years," lines up the entrustment of the household with the five flavors, sets out sixteen differences between the Lotus and the Nirvāṇa, and refutes the Dilun masters on the "relatives" and Jizang's five pairs. The part ends with the verse application and the ten kindnesses of the Tathāgata's room, robe, and seat.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, as for "sent his attendants": [it is explained] in terms of the teaching and in terms of persons. In terms of the teaching, principle and teaching are set against each other; in terms of persons, teacher and disciple are set against each other. Persons necessarily point to the teaching, and the teaching necessarily depends on persons; only with both explanations is it complete, and neither may be shown alone. First, in terms of the teaching: principle is the dharma-body, and wisdom is the reward-body. Because of wisdom there is speaking, so wisdom is what sends; though wisdom sends, what it relies on is principle. What sends is like a minister, and what it relies on is like a king; what is sent is the teaching, so principle is primary and the teaching is attendant (旁). Hence the reward[-body], set against the teaching, is also called primary; yet the reward is accomplished through principle, principle is close and the reward distant, so of the two primaries it speaks from the close one. As for the explanation in terms of persons, from "Furthermore, 'attendants' means" on: after the Bhadraśrī chapter [of the Avataṃsaka], the Ten Abidings chapter takes place in the Trāyastriṃśa heaven; of the ten "Wisdom" bodhisattvas Dharmamati is the head, the other nine "Wisdoms" each praise in verse, and then Bodhisattva Dharmamati expounds the ten abidings at length. Next, in the chapter on the Sovereignty in the Yāma Heaven there are ten "Forest" bodhisattvas; when the nine "Forests" have each praised in verse, Guṇavana expounds the ten practices at length. Next, in the chapter on Ascending to Tuṣita there are ten "Banner" bodhisattvas; when the nine "Banners" have each praised in verse, Vajradhvaja then expounds the ten dedications at length. Next, in the chapter on Ascending to the Paranirmitavaśavartin [Heaven] there are thirty-six "Treasury" bodhisattvas with Vajragarbha at the head, and a bodhisattva named Vimukticandra requests the exposition of the ten grounds. When these four bodhisattvas expound these stages, all say that they speak through the Buddha's power; hence they are called "that which is sent."

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the explanation of "ran quickly" is also in terms of persons and the teaching. First, in terms of the teaching, only being manifest is taken as "quick." From "if the bodhisattvas are taken as the attendants" on, it restates [the explanation] in terms of persons: the bodhisattvas have spiritual powers of their own and are moreover empowered by the Buddha; hence they are called "quick." As for "goes against his own mind" and so on: going against [his mind], he certainly does not recognize it; being alarmed, he is certainly startled. As when one sees something unexpected, it goes against the mind and one does not recognize it, so too was this teaching for them. As for "cried out in protest" (稱怨大喚): the sūtra says only "protest" (怨, also "enemy") and not "pain," yet in the commentary's explanation the loud cry is taken as [a cry of] pain. The reason it has both senses is that meeting an enemy is certainly painful, so the loud cry is likened to it; in meaning and principle this fits. As for "if persuaded that affliction is itself bodhi": this means making him not sever affliction; if affliction is not severed, it necessarily brings painful retribution; how could birth-and-death be nirvāṇa? Therefore "cried out in protest" in meaning corresponds to cause and effect, and the text sets out both intents. As for "thinking himself innocent of any wrong" and so on: examining himself, he has not been able to join beings in entering evil and giving rise to greed, desire, and so on; if he did so now, it would be called sinful conduct. Bodhisattvas necessarily enter the prison of birth-and-death; hence it says "seized and held captive." As for "without the great skillful means": without the wisdom of entering the provisional, to make him give rise to greed and desire and enter birth-and-death is called "loss of the life-breath of wisdom." From "he must fall" on there are two explanations: falling into the suffering of the three lower paths, both great and lesser are lost; sinking in the ground of ignorance, the "ground" must in meaning include both within and beyond the [three] realms. As for "Read in terms of persons, the 'messenger' is this": the method of taking hold of capacity requires a body of the same kind.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "as in the Vimalakīrti Sūtra" and so on: [Vimalakīrti] sent a conjured bodhisattva to fetch the fragrant rice; the bodhisattvas there saw him and questioned the conjured bodhisattva, and the conjured bodhisattva answered. Because the bodhisattvas there heard it told that the buddha and bodhisattvas of the defiled land were capable of such toil and humility, on hearing it they wished to come, so that both [lands] would spread [this practice] together. That buddha admonished them: "Gather in your own bodily fragrance," and so on. Their coming here also caused those of this land who had not aroused the aspiration to arouse it, and those who had aroused it to cultivate practice; hence he had them gather in the superior and follow the inferior, appearing as the same kind. As for "as when Samantabhadra entered this Sahā world" and so on: Bodhisattva Samantabhadra's body is boundless in size, his voice is boundless, and his form is boundless; wishing to come to this land, he contracted his body by the power of his sovereign spiritual powers to make it small, and, because the people of Jambudvīpa have heavy threefold obstructions, by the power of wisdom he conjured [himself] riding a white elephant. It was also for the sake of those not fit to see the superior response: the superior reward of a bodhisattva too may not be shown to the two vehicles.

Ru-Yi Translation

The words "do not force him" explain and complete the preceding phrase. From "My own view" on: although here there are no words of "reflecting," in the parable text both admonition and persuasion have these words. In gauging what is fitting in the gate of persuasion it says, "This elder thought further: 'My own body and hands have strength,'" and in gauging what is fitting in the gate of admonition it says, "as he had thought, he told the children everything"; hence "my own view" quotes them. "Need" (須) is the arising of good, so "no need" is the resting of the persuasion to good; "force" (彊) is admonishing against evil, so "do not force" is the resting of the admonition against evil. As for from "it is fitting to use" up to "is like 'the face'": observing that they were fit only to hear the teaching of arising and ceasing, he took the principle of ashes-and-cessation and taught a teaching that has principle; hence it says "the water of the principle." "Face" is explained as "turning toward": having the capacity that turns its back on birth-and-death and turns toward nirvāṇa is called "face"; it does not mean that sprinkling that nirvāṇa is called "face." In "say nothing further to him," "saying" is just the teaching.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for from "did not tell anyone else" up to "that he was a bodhisattva": here a first approximation speaks only of the lesser of the past; yet the Mahāyāna sūtras before the Lotus do not say that the two vehicles are bodhisattvas. That is protecting the two vehicles; how does it concern the bodhisattvas? Covering the real and protecting the provisional, it says "covered and protected." It is also covering, shading, and protecting without displaying the real; hence before the Lotus everything outside the one real is called "according to others," and, concealing the words of the four flavors, they are generally called the Lesser Vehicle teaching. Because in the two flavors of the past there was still the lesser teaching and definitely no words of the Lesser Vehicle becoming buddhas, the milk and the two butters are all said, with respect to the lesser capacity, "not to tell"; the two flavors get their names only from the denunciation and from the speaking under empowerment. As for from "precisely the resting of the transformation" up to "rose up from the ground": here there are two explanations. The two "grounds" differ, but the two "risings" do not differ. Hence, in resting the one great transformation, he leaves two kinds of ground: [when] the great was praised he fell into suffering, hence the first explanation; using the lesser, he obtained [it], hence the second explanation. As for "the ground of ignorance" in the second explanation: if one says "lay on the ground," it is a metaphor for the two ignorances; if one says "and rose up," it is for the time being [rising] from within the realms. Hence one knows that not understanding the great and the lesser, possessing both ignorances, is all called "lying." Now, fitting him with the lesser dharma, it first cures [what is] within the realms; hence for the time being it says "rose up." As for from "within the four noble truths" up to "clothing and food": it is fitting to go toward the object that is faced, hence "went off to"; the lesser is still in the capacity, so "seek" is explained as "wishing," for only the following phrase says "the elder, wishing to." The object takes in dharmas narrowly, hence "poor quarter"; wishing to go toward the lesser fruit, one still needs the principal and the auxiliary, hence "clothing and food." From "and on another day" on, as for "matching by way of intent — matching the dharma-body's stage": the words say only "before the tree of enlightenment," but the intent points altogether to [the time] before the gradual and sudden teachings; since it says "bringing about the difficulty of the honored and exalted one," one knows it reaches back to before the sudden.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "Question: by what means were the four great disciples" and so on: the intent of the answer is that, having heard the parable, they fully received both rounds, and their entry into the great was not empty; hence they inferred it for themselves, saying "If it were only" and so on. Having taken in the Dharma deeply, they must have this knowledge; speaking in terms of the manner of transformation, advancing and retreating at leisure, they first [receive] within the limits of the teaching and afterward by anticipation. How could it be that, in the fifty years since they attained the lesser, having fully seen the inconceivable realms of the bodhisattvas and heard all dharmas wholly interfused, they were entirely without knowledge? Yet they had not fathomed the source, and, examining themselves, cut off [any] share. Now, receiving the dharma[-teaching] and parable, they partly took on the perfect and permanent, and inferred that the Tathāgata had illuminated [them] beforehand in past days; hence below the Buddha praises them as "most rare." Hence by what they saw in the two flavors they verified that what was seen before was not the true. As for "his casting off the ocean of marks, great and small": the marks are the great ocean of marks, and the minor features the lesser ocean of marks; wholly concealing the body of the ocean of immeasurable marks and features, the parable says "cast off." Marks and features are hard to measure, so they are likened to an ocean. As for "secretly sent two men": within this, first "two men" is explained; next, from "Earlier, in contemplating the Great Vehicle" on, "secretly sent" is explained. The men are what is sent; "secretly" is the intent of the sending. The first passage first makes clear the intent of there being only two; hence, set against the bodhisattvas, it points generally to two men. Next, from "Read in terms of the Dharma" on, it divides the two in terms of dharma and of persons. Next, explaining "secretly sent," it first distinguishes the difference from the attendants: "at once" and "secretly" differ as revealing and covering. Now, in making clear "secretly sent," covering the real is called "secret," and using the lesser is "sending"; covering the full to make clear the half can be understood by analogy. This is an explanation by dividing the characters. It is also that, from the great, he bestows the lesser, hence "sent"; the lesser does not fathom the great, hence "secretly." Yet the half-word and full-word [teachings] here differ from the ancient explanations; now they must be within the perfect, as fully in the Profound Meaning. [The explanation] in terms of persons can be understood by analogy.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "The Great Teaching" and so on: earlier the great teaching was quick, hence "at once sent"; protecting the lesser aspiration, hence "spoke gently." As for "paying double your usual wage" and so on: within it there are two explanations. First, morality-and-good set against the truths and conditions make one doubling: morality-and-good lies in the desire [realm], while the truths and conditions go out of the realms. From "and, further, the six practices of the non-Buddhist paths" on, the contaminated and the uncontaminated set against each other make one doubling; it is also that the two cures, inner and outer, differ, which is called one doubling. Quoted phrases. All lemmas come from the latter half of the Belief and Understanding chapter in the site's fascicle-006 of the Textual Commentary and follow its English.
