The Assembly of Śrāvakas and Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya

This part is the lower third of the first fascicle of Zhanran's Notes on the Textual Commentary on the Lotus. It follows Zhiyi's Textual Commentary (site fascicle one) through the opening of the sūtra's list of hearers: why śrāvakas are listed before bodhisattvas and the miscellaneous assembly, the "seven ones" that make up "together with," the four kinds of śrāvakas named in Vasubandhu's Treatise on the Lotus Sūtra, and the words "great," "bhikṣu," "assembly," "twelve thousand," and "arhat," with the five phrases that praise the arhats' virtues. Zhanran checks each of Zhiyi's four modes of explanation (causes and conditions, by the teachings, origin and trace, contemplating the mind) against the four siddhāntas, corrects older readings by Sengzhao, Kumārajīva, Guangzhai, and the "ancients," and quotes the Āgamas, the Great Treatise, and the Abhidharmakośa in support. The longest section treats the first disciple named, Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya: Zhanran retells the Buddha's search under Ārāḍa and Udraka from the Madhyama Āgama, then lays out the structure of Zhiyi's four contemplations of non-arising (mirror and blindness, the illusory mirror, the colored images, the round mirror), and closes by explaining why each disciple receives all four explanations. Phrases quoted from the Textual Commentary use the site's published English.

The Order of the Assemblies

Chinese Source

釋列眾中，初辨次第。言「多爾」者，亦有經中菩薩後列，各有所表。如《華嚴經》不列聲聞，純無雜故。舊解者多是光宅，與《大論》意同故無別破，但總結云似兩解耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

In explaining the listing of the assemblies, it first distinguishes their order. As for "most sūtras follow this pattern": there are also sūtras in which the bodhisattvas are listed last; each order has what it signifies. For example, the Avataṃsaka Sūtra does not list the śrāvakas, because it is pure and unmixed. The "older explanations" are mostly Guangzhai's; since they agree with the intent of the Great Treatise, there is no separate refutation, and [the commentary] merely sums up, saying that it "resembles two readings."

注「云云」者，事似因緣須具四悉，義似約教復須論八。事即身也，故云親疎，義即諦理，故云涅槃等，形服異故即世界。親者生善，疎者破惡，不親不疎即第一義。於有義中既以三諦，以諦對教則四可識，於藏等四辨漸等四，其義可知。又兩二義並欣涅槃，及四菩薩並不欣不著故皆居中，唯詣實理兼能利人，故居中求宗超彼凡聖。若以入中為菩薩，即指別教地上、圓教始終。注家云：聲聞學疎敦之於內，菩薩道親忘之於外，此但得事而失義，似迹而迷本。況復觀心、因緣耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the note "(and so on)": since the "factual" account resembles [the reading by] causes and conditions, it must include the four siddhāntas; since the "doctrinal" account resembles [the reading] in terms of the teachings, it must further discuss the eight [teachings]. The factual is the body, hence it speaks of "close" and "distant"; the doctrinal is the principle of the truths, hence it speaks of "nirvāṇa" and so on; because outward form and dress differ, this is the mundane [siddhānta]. "Close" generates good; "distant" destroys evil; neither close nor distant is the ultimate meaning. Since the doctrinal account uses the three truths, when the truths are paired with the teachings the four can be recognized; within the Tripiṭaka and the other three [teachings] one distinguishes the gradual and the other three [teachings of method] — the meaning can be understood. Further, [the śrāvakas of] the two [teachings], in both senses, delight in nirvāṇa, and the bodhisattvas of the four [teachings] all neither delight nor are attached, so all "dwell in the middle"; only those who reach the real principle and are also able to benefit others therefore "dwell in the middle seeking the true source," surpassing those ordinary beings and sages. If entering the middle is taken as [the mark of] the bodhisattva, this refers to the distinct teaching from the grounds upward and to the perfect teaching from beginning to end. A commentator says: "The śrāvakas' learning is distant, yet they hold it earnestly within; the bodhisattvas' path is close, yet they forget it without." This only grasps the factual and loses the doctrinal; it resembles the trace yet is confused about the origin — how much less does it reach contemplating the mind or causes and conditions!

約本迹中，此經列眾超出群經，故人天二乘能引之人本非下地，所引之眾堪通別記，語通意兼，故云內祕。皆大薩埵，既能迹引二邊判非凡小，故云薩埵。菩薩濫本故且不論，應知亦且約體用論也。觀解後「云云」者，亦應更約因緣明觀諸教開顯及本迹觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

In [the reading by] origin and trace: the assembly listed in this sūtra surpasses that of all other sūtras. Hence the humans, gods, and those of the two vehicles who are able to lead are in origin not of the lower stages, and the assembly that is led is fit for both general and particular predictions; the words are general but the intent includes [both], hence "secretly bodhisattvas within." As for "all great bodhisattvas": since they are able, as trace, to draw forth [beings at] the two extremes, they are judged to be neither ordinary nor lesser, hence "bodhisattvas." Bodhisattvas [as such] would be confused with the origin, so for now they are not discussed; one should know that this too is for now discussed in terms of essence and function. As for the "(and so on)" after the contemplative interpretation: one should further, in terms of causes and conditions, clarify the contemplation of the teachings, of opening and revealing, and of origin and trace.

Why the Śrāvakas Are Listed First

Chinese Source

釋列聲聞，準《法華論》以八義故，先列聲聞：一、為顯親聞後不謗故；二、攝不定性迴心入大故；三、除尊貴慢非究竟故；四、常隨佛故；五、形儀同故；六、令內眷捨欲故；七、令菩薩敬故；八、令眾生信故。

Ru-Yi Translation

In explaining the listing of the śrāvakas: according to the Treatise on the Lotus Sūtra, the śrāvakas are listed first for eight reasons: first, to show that, having heard in person, they will not slander later; second, to gather in those of indeterminate nature who turn their minds and enter the Great [Vehicle]; third, to remove the pride of the honored, [showing that their state] is not ultimate; fourth, because they constantly follow the Buddha; fifth, because their form and deportment are the same [as the Buddha's]; sixth, to make the inner retinue abandon desire; seventh, to make the bodhisattvas respect them; eighth, to make sentient beings believe.

Chinese Source

然《論》中八義唯第二一半獨屬今經，以不定性此土得故，仍少一半，若定性者彼土得故、未來得故；餘之七義論眾前後，通諸經故。以第二義入餘七中，使七一一皆有第二，方令八義全在今經。若依今意更有三義：欲別記故、別開權故、先顯本故，故令餘義永殊昔教。故論文意諸經多爾，所以通釋。今使諸教雖共復殊，方等先列，以四義故：欲斥奪故、欲密引故、令體信故，令味變故。般若亦四：欲洮汰故、欲委業故、密引進故、成熟酥故。故知《論》文通前二味，如向各四，又異前後。

Ru-Yi Translation

However, of the eight reasons in the Treatise, only half of the second belongs solely to the present sūtra, because those of indeterminate nature attain [the fruit] in this land; half is still lacking, for those of determinate nature attain it in that [other] land, that is, attain it in the future. The other seven reasons discuss the order of the assemblies and apply to all sūtras. Only when the second reason is put into the other seven, so that each of the seven contains the second, are all eight reasons wholly within the present sūtra. According to the present intent there are three further reasons: in order to give particular predictions; in order to open the provisional in particular; in order first to reveal the origin. Hence the other reasons are made wholly different from the former teachings. So the intent of the Treatise's text is that "most sūtras follow this pattern," and therefore it explains generally. Now we make the teachings, though shared, again distinct. In the Vaipulya [period] they are listed first for four reasons: in order to rebuke and strip them; in order to draw them secretly; in order to make them believe in essence; in order to make the flavor change. In the Prajñā [period] there are likewise four: in order to wash and sift them; in order to entrust [them with] the work; to draw them secretly forward; to bring about ripened butter. Hence we know that the Treatise's text applies to the two preceding flavors, each with four as just stated, and further differs as to before and after.

Ru-Yi Translation

The Treatise also says that monks come first and nuns next, likewise for eight reasons: first, men are honored and women lowly; second, earlier and later entry into the path; third, teacher and disciple are not the same; fourth, ability or inability to transmit the Dharma; fifth, advancing or withdrawing at the compilation; sixth, whether or not they may dwell together; seventh, deference according to number; eighth, having or lacking praise. These eight apply wholly to the three preceding flavors, and mostly to the teaching of cream. If judged by their being equally śrāvakas, the second sense is still lacking. In the present particular [listing], only entrusting the work is excluded, yet by inference it is still present, because [the nuns], together with the monks, heard the transferred teaching. Hence at the time of entrusting the work they are not wholly without a share; all the other reasons are the same as for the monks. Within the five flavors, for the assemblies of the gradual teaching, the meaning is no different.

Category: "Together with" and the Seven Ones

Chinese Source

次出舊解，云大小名聞者，意云：名即己名、聞即他聞，所以名大故聞大、名小故聞小。今但依文故，破其無據。言「依文」者，但約所列多少以明所識多少。前列萬二千，又云眾所知識；後云二千，又無知識，縱以名聞用釋知識，後文全無，何小之有？況復大小一向無憑。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next it cites the older explanation, "greater renown" and "lesser renown." The idea is this: the name is one's own name and the renown is others' hearing; hence a great name is heard greatly and a small name is heard slightly. Now, simply because it "follows the text," [the commentary] refutes this as having "no firm basis." As for "follows the text": it simply uses the number listed to clarify how many are known. The first list is twelve thousand and also says "known to the assembly"; the latter says two thousand and has no "known." Even if one uses renown to explain "known," the later text lacks it entirely — what "lesser" is there? Moreover, "greater" and "lesser" have no basis at all.

In the first, explaining the category, it first explains the five characters in general; next, from "The Śāstra" on, it explains the four meanings separately, since "bhikṣu" [two characters] is combined [into one]. In the first, the general explanation, the five characters gather in all those of virtue; since the company with whom [the Buddha was] is marked "great," how could it merely differ from the lower assemblies? Among bhikṣus the types are again many — the types of the four gates, the three liberations, the analytical and the embodying, shared wisdom, and discernment of emptiness — so one can only say for now "of lofty renown and great virtue"; how could they be discussed in full? Here it says it is like "the ranks and cohorts" of the noble class.

Next, in explaining the four meanings separately: first, from "The Śāstra" on, the word "together with" is explained separately; the first passage is the explanation by causes and conditions. If these seven are made into the four siddhāntas: time and place are the mundane; precepts are for the individual; mind and view are the therapeutic; path and liberation are the ultimate meaning. If judged by the first three [teachings], in the former teachings the seven meanings are only three [siddhāntas]: path, liberation, and the rest all belong to the therapeutic sense. The seven ones of the perfect teaching all belong to the perfect four [siddhāntas]; though by the teachings they differ, ultimately they are only perfect. Though seven, they are the same, hence the seven are called "one," and "one" means "in common."

四教不同通在五味，具如諸經不可遍述，且準歎德在三藏教者，同感佛時、同鹿苑處、同別脫戒、同一切智心、同無漏正見、同三十七道、同有餘脫、為同聞人，入通序時已得記者，全成圓人七一故也。同妙感應時、同見妙依處、同得究竟戒、同證種智心、同無作正見、同圓實道品、同不思議脫，得授記已即同菩薩，安得復以聲聞歎德？豈結集者謬抑德耶？故知在四味時隨味而變，經家從本列在聲聞，故依本歎。

Ru-Yi Translation

The difference of the four teachings runs through all five flavors, fully as in the sūtras, and cannot be described exhaustively. Taking for now the praise of virtues as within the Tripiṭaka teaching: the same time of encountering the Buddha, the same place, the Deer Park, the same prātimokṣa precepts, the same mind of all-knowledge, the same untainted right view, the same thirty-seven [factors of the] path, the same liberation with remainder — [so] they become co-listeners. Those who by the time of the general introduction have already received predictions have wholly become persons of the perfect [teaching's] seven ones: the same time of wondrous resonance and response, the same place of seeing the wondrous dependent [land], the same ultimate precepts attained, the same mind realizing the wisdom knowing all modes, the same right view without fabrication, the same perfect and real factors of the path, the same inconceivable liberation. Once they have received predictions they are the same as bodhisattvas; how then could their virtues again be praised as those of śrāvakas? Were the compilers mistakenly suppressing their virtue? Hence we know that in the times of the four flavors they changed with the flavor; the sūtra-compilers, following their original [status], list them among the śrāvakas, and so praise them according to the original.

Ru-Yi Translation

If it did not retain the original, how would we know that śrāvakas include the provisional and the real? The real ones receive predictions, which shows that they come from somewhere, because at the time of hearing the Lotus great and small are distinguished. Hence judging by the teachings must cover beginning and end; hence the sevens of the three teachings are either originally of the three, or transferred in. First, "the Tripiṭaka teaching has one set of the seven ones," because arising-and-ceasing is the same. "The shared teaching has two," because it divides the sharp and the dull; the sharp include the perfect and the distinct, so one should say three sevens, but for now the shared [teaching] is spoken of generally as one case. "The distinct teaching has innumerable," because in both one's own practice and transforming others, horizontally and vertically, there are four gates, and at each gate those who enter through the four siddhāntas differ. "The perfect teaching has one," because the initial resolve and the ultimate are not two and not different. The expounder should clarify this in detail for the four teachings, up to judging the teachings and flavors first and opening them afterward. From "If the origin has not yet been disclosed" on, the one sentence is the explanation by origin and trace; it too discusses origin and trace in terms of essence and function. The distinct [teaching] has no śrāvakas, so it speaks only of the Tripiṭaka and the shared; if the shared includes the distinct, that too could be discussed — this then is an abbreviation. Further, the two matters of time and place are for now discussed in terms of the teachings, hence it says that within the Tripiṭaka time and place are one. If taken from the time and place of first attaining the path, either many persons share one place, roughly as in the present text; or many persons are in many places; or one person is in many places, as with the many times and places in the Āgamas.

Opening and Revealing: The Four Kinds of Śrāvakas

Chinese Source

「直明兩意」下，欲明開顯，先更辨異。所言異者，且置七同，更於藏通七中辨異，則三異四同。何者？時處等四不可不同，如所會、所依、所稟、所證；若心等三，安能不別？別修、別見、別行故也。此仍一往，亦可戒法從別各各得故，解脫從人各各證故，是則五別二同。又若從人亦可俱別，今釋共義不合論之。義分三四乃成旁耳，是故且從七同以說，別教同異準說可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "one need only clarify how these two senses differ" on: wishing to clarify opening and revealing, it first further distinguishes the differences. As for "differ": setting aside for now the sameness of the seven, it further distinguishes the differences within the sevens of the Tripiṭaka and the shared [teaching]; then three differ and four are the same. Why? Time, place, and the other two cannot but be the same — namely, what is met, what is relied on, what is received, and what is realized. As for mind and the other two, how could they not differ? Because cultivation differs, view differs, and practice differs. This is still one way of putting it; one could also say that the precepts are obtained by each separately and liberation is realized by each person separately; then five differ and two are the same. Further, if taken from the person, all could differ; but now, in explaining the meaning of "in common," it is not fitting to discuss this. Dividing the meaning into three and four is then secondary; therefore it is for now explained from the sameness of the seven. The sameness and difference of the distinct teaching can be understood by analogy.

次「若至」下正明開顯。「法華論」者，明被開者有權有實，未開具四、開已唯一，今加佛道據新入者。《論》中四者，決定、增上、退大、應化。《論》自釋云：「後二與記，前兩不記，根鈍未熟故」。且約此會，即經中云「生滅度想」，決定性也。若彼得聞、《論》中未說，天親豈可迷經文耶？經云：「而於彼土得聞是經」，《論》且一往據現說耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, from "once one reaches" on, opening and revealing are properly clarified. As for "the Treatise on the Lotus Sūtra": it clarifies that those who are opened include the provisional and the real; before the opening all four are present, after the opening there is only one; here "the Buddha path" is added, on the basis of those newly entered. The four in the Treatise are: the determinate, the overweening, those who retreated from the Great [Vehicle], and those manifesting a response. The Treatise itself explains: "The latter two are given predictions; the former two are not given predictions, because their faculties are dull and not yet ripe." Taking this assembly for now, this is what the sūtra calls "[they] form the thought that . is final extinction" — the determinate nature. That they get to hear [it] in that [other land] is not mentioned in the Treatise; but how could Vasubandhu be confused about the sūtra's text? The sūtra says: "[he] will in that land . come to hear this very sūtra"; the Treatise is for now speaking one-sidedly of the present.

是故今師但除上慢，即五千起去者是。雖從座去仍判於涅槃中。若四依邊得聞，故上慢者亦非不聞，已聞略開及在後故，但不可云此會得記，種、熟、脫三始終無廢，故準今文，遠近相望四種俱得。今云「住果」，兼於決定及退菩提，住果變異故分二教，是故二種總立住果。又「佛道」者，準經義立。若爾。佛道應化各有別圓。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore our master excludes only the overweening — namely the five thousand who rose and left. Though they left their seats, they are still assigned to the Nirvāṇa [assembly]. If they get to hear it from the four reliances, then the overweening too are not without hearing, because they have already heard the brief opening and [will hear] later; one simply cannot say that they receive predictions in this assembly. Sowing, ripening, and liberating, the three, are never abandoned from beginning to end. Hence, judging by the present text and viewing far and near together, all four kinds attain [it]. Here "abiding in the fruit" includes both the determinate and those who retreated from bodhi; because abiding in the fruit varies, it is divided into two teachings, and therefore the two kinds are together set up as "abiding in the fruit." Further, "the Buddha path" is established according to the meaning of the sūtra. If so, the Buddha path and manifesting a response each have distinct and perfect.

列名中初總為五：初、現數，次、立名意，三、證立意，四、消釋意，五、用義意。引證中多引《阿含》者，如《增一》中列四眾名，各有偏好以引同類。「一一四釋」下「云云」者，即因緣等四不復更列，然諸聖因緣多寄初教，以是感應之始故也。故諸聖初因並在外道，為破外故，在釋種中為調伏故，為導為主而將引之，是故具明入胎立行眷屬師友。若逆若順能化所化；縱有始終計不轉者，亦為後來得破之由，顯諸聖者迹不徒設，一一無非感應故也。故四悉義一一應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

In listing the names, it first [proceeds] in general in five parts: first, presenting the number; second, the intent of naming; third, proving the intent; fourth, the intent of explaining; fifth, the intent of applying the meanings. In citing proof, it often cites the Āgamas, as the Ekottarika lists the names of the four assemblies, each with particular inclinations, in order to draw those of the same kind. As for the "(and so on)" after "Each and every arhat, in turn, will be given the fourfold exegesis": the four, causes and conditions and the rest, are not listed again. However, the causes and conditions of the sages are mostly lodged in the initial teaching, because it is the beginning of resonance and response. Hence the first causes of the sages all lie among the non-Buddhists, in order to refute the outsiders; or among the Śākya clan, in order to tame them; as guides and as leaders they lead them on. Therefore it fully clarifies entering the womb, establishing practice, kin, teachers, and friends; whether adverse or favorable, transformer and transformed. Even those who to the end held their views without turning become the occasion for later ones to be refuted. This shows that the traces of the sages are not set up in vain, each being nothing other than resonance and response. Hence the meaning of the four siddhāntas should be known for each one.

Ru-Yi Translation

In "Kauṇḍinya," the first is the mundane; from "As for his vow" on is for the individual; from "As for 'conduct'" on is the therapeutic; from "the crown prince" on is the ultimate meaning. This ultimate meaning is still within the former teachings, but since it is the teaching that sifts whether one advances or not, this does not matter; according to the matter it explains in turn so as to accord with the four siddhāntas, and all that follows is likewise. As for "whose ancestors served the fire-god": though those who serve fire are many, without being the first to attain the path, fire does not become a virtue. Hence the first to attain the path receives the names "illumination" and "burning": darkness dispelled, the cause is extinguished; things extinguished, the effect vanishes — though at first not yet vanished, later it surely vanishes, like a person falling from a cliff. As for "'without knowledge' . but rather knowing 'nothingness'": the Sanskrit sound is inverted. The nothingness that is known is the truth of the real; hence it cites the real within the two truths. Next, as for citing two sūtras and one treatise: "the original limit" is simply the truth of the real that is known. The vow comes from the Sūtra of Causes and Effects: it is the original vow of the Buddha, when he was a bodhisattva, to save them first; next, from "Again, in the time of Kāśyapa Buddha" on, is [Kauṇḍinya's] own vow. From "Now, through the long night" on, it draws a comparison for Kauṇḍinya. Saṃsāra is like the night of the world, and the rising of the sun makes one awaken. "The sun's light" is the great arhats and the bodhisattvas; hence the morning star and the sunlight both dispel the great darkness of their own persons.

Translator's notes

Quoted lemmas: Zhanran comments phrase by phrase on the site's Textual Commentary, fascicle one, from "The exegesis of the co-assembled listeners" to the trace-and-origin reading of Kauṇḍinya. Words in quotation marks that come from that text use the site's English; where the site's phrasing does not show the word Zhanran is glossing, the Chinese sense is given in brackets.

The notes on Calming and Contemplation: "the notes on fascicle five of Calming and Contemplation" (《止觀》第五記) and similar references point to Zhanran's own commentary on the Mohe Zhiguan, the Fuxing (T1912).

The four siddhāntas: Zhanran repeatedly assigns sections of the Textual Commentary to the mundane, the individual, the therapeutic, and the ultimate-meaning siddhāntas. "For the individual" corresponds to "generating good" (生善) and "the therapeutic" to "destroying evil" (破惡).

Treatise on the Lotus Sūtra: Vasubandhu's treatise (T1519/T1520) names four kinds of śrāvakas and says that the determinate and the overweening are not given predictions "because their faculties are not yet ripe" (T1519). Its three gates (上上起門, 總別相門, 攝取事門) for the sixteen phrases on the arhats are also in T1519. The two sets of eight reasons Zhanran cites (for listing śrāvakas first, and monks before nuns) were not located in either version; source not located.

"It censures people" (unit 14): the Chinese reads 貴人、非論 ("it values people"); the context ("therefore it censures, saying…") suggests 貴 is a scribal error for 責 "censure," and the translation follows that reading. Alternatively, with 貴 as written: "it values the person, not the treatise."

The round number (unit 27): the Great Treatise (T1509, fascicle 3) explains 大數 as "slightly more or slightly less is called the round number"; Zhanran's wording is a paraphrase.

Three insights (unit 21): the dialogue of Śāriputra and the Buddha on the five hundred bhikṣus is in both the Madhyama Āgama (T26) and the Saṃyukta Āgama (T99); the site's Textual Commentary cites the Saṃyukta, Zhanran the Madhyama.

Entanglements (unit 43): the verse is from the Abhidharmakośa (T1558).

The Rāma Sūtra (unit 47): Madhyama Āgama no. 204, fascicle 56 (T26). Zhanran's citation is abridged and in places paraphrased. "Non-perception" renders his 非相定, a short form of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. "Fourteen days" renders 二七. The speaker of "If you wish to sit, then sit" is not named in the Chinese.

Treatise on Distinguishing Merits (unit 47): T1507 names Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya as the eldest and Subhadra as the youngest disciple; the wording is paraphrased.

The four poisonous snakes (unit 50): the parable is in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (the site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra has it in full); Zhanran gives a condensed version followed by a paraphrase of the sūtra's own application, so the site's wording is not reused verbatim.

"Mechanism" (unit 50): Zhanran glosses the two characters of 機關 separately, ji "trigger" and guan "joint."

The parasol and lamp (unit 54): Saṃyukta Āgama no. 872 (T99). Zhanran's "fascicle thirty-one" may reflect a different fascicle division.

Bhikṣus at the five flavors (unit 3): "the work" (委業) is the task of transmitting the teaching that the śrāvakas received in the Prajñā period ("the transferred teaching," 轉教).

"Have long been so" (unit 18): some texts of the Textual Commentary read 又矣; Zhanran's lemma reads 久矣, "has long been so," and his comment ("each has a long-standing origin") follows that reading.

"One who accords with the true" (unit 38): Zhanran analyzes the two characters of 應真 as "the wisdom that accords" and "the true principle accorded with."
