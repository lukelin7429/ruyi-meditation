The Medicinal Herbs Chapter: One Ground, One Rain, and the Three Herbs and Two Trees

This part covers Zhanran's commentary on the Medicinal Herbs chapter, following the opening of the Textual Commentary's seventh fascicle (the site's fascicle-007). It begins by measuring Vasubandhu's scheme of seven parables and seven remedies against the Lotus itself, rejecting Jizang's distinction of auspicious and inauspicious plants, and then reads the chapter title through the four siddhāntas. The core of the part is the Buddha's confirmation of Kāśyapa: what Kāśyapa received, and what he "did not reach" horizontally, vertically, both, and neither. Zhanran then follows the parable of the cloud and rain point by point: the earth as the aggregates rather than the habitual cause, the dense cloud as the three mysteries, the two causes and two fruits of growth, and the grounds before and after the path. In the passage on "one mark, one flavor" he insists that nature and cultivation must both be understood, and asks readers directly to reflect on it; he also works out the seed, mark, essence, and nature that only the Tathāgata knows. He refutes Guangzhai's reading of "returning to emptiness," explains the three herbs and two trees by the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct bodhisattvas, compares the cloud and rain with the "single voice," and ends with the passage Jñānagupta added in the Supplemented Lotus.

Ru-Yi Translation

The Treatise on the Lotus sets up seven remedies with the seven parables. First, for the affliction of overweening pride in inverted merit, blazing, which seeks the reward of humans and gods, it teaches the burning house as the remedy. For śrāvakas who [think themselves] equal to the Tathāgata Vehicle, it teaches the destitute son as the remedy. For overweening Mahāyāna people who say there are no two vehicles, it teaches the parable of the cloud and rain as the remedy. For those who in fact have no nirvāṇa yet form the notion of nirvāṇa, it teaches the phantom city as the remedy. For those who do not seek the Mahāyāna and take a false liberation as the ultimate meaning, it teaches the jewel sewn [in the robe] as the remedy. For Mahāyāna people who take what is not Mahāyāna, it teaches the jewel in the topknot as the remedy. For people without merit who do not take up the foremost vehicle, it teaches the physician as the remedy. If one relies only on these seven, each with its remedy, then the principle of the dharma[-teaching] being made into a parable, the parable-teaching being received with understanding, and the Buddha confirming with a parable, and so on, seems not to hold. One should know the treatise's intent: the burning house does not point to the side of the great cart; the destitute son properly uses the intent of receiving and entrusting; the cloud and rain cause the opening of the provisional two vehicles; the phantom city draws them on with the place of treasure; the rest can be understood. If others say that the present chapter only brings out the producing [of the plants], they wholly miss the purport of the sūtra. Moreover, the treatise names [the parable] "rain" from what does the moistening, while the sūtra, from what is moistened, says "medicinal herbs." Among the medicinal herbs the two vehicles are not absent, and the single rain confirms their returning to the great. Jiaxiang says: "Plants and trees are of two kinds: first, those that do not know they are the same; second, those that do not know they are different. If there are auspicious plants, they can know the sameness, as a metaphor for Kāśyapa." This would be acceptable only from what Kāśyapa received. If it is from the Buddha's confirmation, how could all the other medicinal herbs be said not to know? Also they do not know whether the three herbs and two trees are auspicious or not, so it must be said that the past and present reveal whether they are auspicious or not. I say: having reached this Lotus, how could there still be any inauspicious plant? It should be said that, though these plants and trees originally grow from one ground with one flavor and one moisture, they do not know it themselves; suddenly receiving the opening and revealing, none is not auspicious, so that snapping the fingers and joining the palms all become wondrous causes, and the wisdom of arising and of non-arising all becomes the wisdom of all modes.

In the parable, as for "cultivated in their clump" and so on: it is a metaphor for each having accumulated habits in the past, hence it is called "clump"; "育" means nurturing. As for "over many days": through twenty thousand koṭis [of buddhas] they were still in the skillful means; now for the first time [the real] is opened and revealed, hence it says "no sooner meet with cloud and rain." From here it next likens the present; from "cloud and rain, like" on it explains. As for saying "spread" (扶蔬): "扶" means assisting. The Erya says: "A grove with plants and trees is called 蔬." By the past assisting the present, they are fit to be given the prediction. As for saying "radiant and bright": it describes brightness and abundance. Once they meet with the cloud and rain, the plants and trees spread forth and flourish. The beginningless inherent virtue is like the ground; arousing the Mahāyāna mind is like the seed; arousing the mind of the two vehicles is like the sprouts and stems of plants and trees; now entering the first abiding is like the sprouts and stems of the same accomplished buddha vehicle, and so on. In the first passage: once [the real] has been opened and revealed, they benefit themselves and also beings; named from the benefit received by self and others, it is called the individual [siddhānta]. Next, in the therapeutic and the ultimate meaning, there are two, parable and application. In the parable too it first likens the past, and from "here, once it meets with" on likens the present. As for saying "the four great elements" and so on: the three grades of worldly medicine are none of them the king of medicines: the lower cures the four elements, the middle benefits the five viscera, and the upper can renew one's years. As for saying "the wind and cold": it briefly marks two elements. As for origin and trace: in the origin, dwelling in the ground of wisdom, he once sent down cloud and rain; in the trace he becomes plants and trees to draw them toward growth. Contemplating the mind is fully as in the Profound Meaning, after the wondrousness of benefit. Further, taking medicine in terms of contemplation is as in the tenth [fascicle] of Calming and Contemplation, where the four medicines curing views fully relate the provisional and the real; thought likewise follows the same pattern. Although the old masters say that it confirms the depth of the Buddha's kindness, without the intent of the ten kindnesses the confirmation too is not complete. The ancient masters also did not take "teaching to work" as receiving by anticipation; even if they set up receiving by anticipation, it would still be only the provisional merit as judged by Nanyue in the text below. As for "Our teacher says": it points precisely to Nanyue. Although the two vehicles receive their own realm, since it says "well expounded," they receive fully the provisional and real of a whole lifetime; now the Buddha confirms in detail what they received. "Another day" is the meaning of the two "others"; "other" is the provisional; it should say, "this is truly my son"; how could it be only the man taught to work?

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "taking hold of what has not yet been reached": raising what Kāśyapa did not reach shows that the other kinds have their share. Again, bringing out what Kāśyapa did not reach shows Kāśyapa's full receiving. Again, showing Kāśyapa's full receiving, one knows that one takes in all. Within it, first the listing; next, from "Why is this so?" on, the explanation. The intent of the explanation: if only the two vehicles were confirmed, [the chapter] should only be titled from the middle herb; beyond what they received, it further confirms what they did not reach, to make known the intent of the examples just given. Again, the words "could not exhaust" are used to make clear that it is not exhausted: if the Buddha's teaching is not exhausted, how much less Kāśyapa's! Here it first relates the Buddha's intent: since the Buddha's intent is universal, it verifies that the receiving has limits. Next, from "How, then, could" on, it censures [the confirmation] within the limits of the teaching, running through all the phrases; from "It says nothing" on, it properly relates what was not reached. As for "the lesser and the greater" (退進) and so on: from the stage of the two vehicles, looking upward is "advancing" and looking downward "retreating." The ten realms each have their own cause and effect, not by sequence, hence they are called horizontal; one person gradually rising can also be vertical. Now, taking the side of mixed arising and of possessing [all], it can only be horizontal. Again, many persons regarded in sequence, and dharmas as shallow and deep, can also be vertical; each person dwelling in his own [station] can also be horizontal. As for "the seven skillful means . vertical": whether the seven persons, each with seven, are regarded as earlier and later; or the seven persons, each one, are regarded as greater and lesser; or the seven persons passing into [one another] and the seven persons entering the real — all can be called vertical. Translator's notes

Quoted phrases. All lemmas come from the Medicinal Herbs chapter in the site's fascicle-007 of the Textual Commentary and follow its English. In Zhanran's own sentences 習因／報因 are "habitual cause / reward-cause" and 草木 "plants and trees," where the site's lemmas have "causal factor of habituation," "causal factor of retribution," and "grasses and trees." Where the site has "abodes" for 住, "abidings" is written.

The Lotus. Sūtra lines follow the site's Lotus: "said to Mahākāśyapa and the other great disciples" (unit 9), "You, Kāśyapa, are exceedingly rare" (unit 25), "If those with wisdom hear it" (unit 75), and from the verses "practice diligence and concentration," "constantly practicing loving-kindness and compassion, who know for themselves," "turning the wheel that never turns back," "seek the World-Honored One's own place," "single-minded in the buddha-path," and "delivering across immeasurable koṭis" (unit 83).

Seven parables, seven remedies. The list in unit 3 is from Vasubandhu's Treatise on the Lotus (T1519), which pairs each of the seven parables with a kind of affliction it cures, including the cloud and rain as a remedy for those who say there are no two vehicles.

Returning to the theme. Zhanran reads 復宗 as "returning to the theme," that is, coming back to the confirmation of Kāśyapa after the long praise of the Tathāgata (unit 25). The site's lemma "the further sectarian confirmation" is kept inside quotations.

"Fits the subtle." Zhanran reads 稱 in 嘉著而稱微 in the departing tone, "to fit": hearing accords with principle and so "fits the subtle" (unit 10). He glosses 著 with 漏, probably a copyist's error for 露, "manifest."

Sources. The Saṃyukta Āgama passage on the Wind-and-Cloud gods (unit 32) is in T99. Kāśyapa's confession that before hearing the Nirvāṇa "we were all people of wrong views" (unit 78) is in the northern Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T374). Pan Yue's Record of the Land within the Passes (unit 30) survives only in quotations. Jizang's saying on the two kinds of plants (unit 3), the Erya gloss (unit 10), Sengzhao's saying on the four vows (unit 42), the treatise on the arhat's boundary concentration (unit 84), and Daoxuan's (Nanshan's) remark on Jñānagupta (unit 95) were not located.

Rocana. Zhanran explains 舍那 (Rocana) as the foreign sound for "pure and perfect" (淨滿), the land of true reward (unit 43). The site's fascicle-007 renders 盧舍那佛 as "Vairocana Buddha." In unit 78 the "new sūtra" is the eighty-fascicle Avataṃsaka, whose 遮那 is Vairocana.

The single voice. Zhanran glosses 報 in 一音報眾聲 as "responding" (unit 94); the site has "gives rise, in retribution, to every sound."

Seed, mark, essence, nature. Units 48–54 work through the Wenju's four ways the Tathāgata knows. The seed "by correlation" (相對) is the three paths as the very seed of the three virtues; the seed "by category" (類) is the good already present in kind, such as bowing the head or raising a hand. The "ten gates" in unit 46 are probably Zhanran's own ten gates of non-duality.

The Supplemented Lotus. Jñānagupta's Supplemented Lotus (T264) adds, after the Medicinal Herbs verses, the parables of the clay vessels and of the man born blind, which are also in Dharmarakṣa's Correct Dharma Flower (T263), and moves the Entrustment chapter to the end (unit 95).
