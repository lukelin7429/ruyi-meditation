The Bestowal of Predictions and the Phantom City: Five Hundred Yojanas, the Cart, and the City

This part, the first section of Zhanran's eighth fascicle, comments on the end of the Textual Commentary's seventh fascicle (the site's fascicle-007): the Bestowal of Predictions chapter and the Phantom City chapter. Zhanran first asks why the śrāvakas, and not bodhisattvas in general, need predictions here, and why sūtras such as the Vimalakīrti refuse predictions while the Lotus gives them. He sets the Lotus's predictions against the eight kinds in the Necklace and against those who say the "fixed" śrāvakas are never predicted, and he arranges the four siddhāntas in ten layers. In the Phantom City chapter he reads the conjured city by the four teachings, discusses the three turnings and twelve aspects of the Dharma wheel, and explains why this parable has no losing of father and son and no "not false." The longest discussion concerns the five hundred yojanas. Zhanran rejects in turn the explanations of Ji, the Saṃgraha masters, and others, insists that only the Lotus opens the provisional, and holds that in the perfect teaching one reaches the place of treasure at the first abiding. He closes with the old question of why the cart does not exist while the city does, and with the guide and the matching verses.

Explaining the Bestowal of Predictions Chapter

Chinese Source

注家云：業似先違，心符後順，既拂殊音之異，寧爽一味之果哉！故與記也。今云：事似先違，心機本順，然諸菩薩豈無先違後順之人，故知今記聲聞須除通釋。釋此品題，先翻譯，次料簡。於中初「諸經」下先引經問中，初文總舉問意，次「淨名」下別引三經，初引《淨名》者，彌勒得記既為補處，必生兜率為彼天主，彼諸天子預來修敬，彌勒因為說得記由，由不退位；廣為天子說不退行，即不退因也。乃被呵云「眾生諸法、賢聖與彌勒如同記同，何獨彌勒？況如無生滅，何以得記？」次「思益云」者，以記虛假願不聞名。《大品》亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

The commentator says: "Their deeds seemed at first to go against [the one vehicle], but their minds later accorded and complied; since the difference of the distinct sounds has been brushed away, how could they miss the fruit of the single flavor? Hence the predictions are given." Now I say: their affairs seemed at first to go against it, but their minds and capacities were originally compliant. Yet among the bodhisattvas, how could there be none who first went against and later complied? Hence one knows that the present predictions for the śrāvakas must set aside the general explanation. In explaining this chapter title, first the translation [of the name], next the examination. Within it, first, from "Various sūtras" on, it first cites sūtras within the question. In this, the first passage raises the intent of the question generally; next, from "The Vimalakīrti [Sūtra]" on, it cites three sūtras separately. First, as for citing the Vimalakīrti: since Maitreya, having obtained the prediction, is the one in the place of succession, he must be born in Tuṣita as lord of that heaven; the gods' sons of that heaven came in advance to pay him reverence, and Maitreya therefore told them the reason for his obtaining the prediction, namely the stage of non-retreat; he broadly expounded to the gods' sons the practice of non-retreat, which is the cause of non-retreat. Then he was rebuked: "Beings, all dharmas, the worthies and sages, and Maitreya are alike in suchness and alike in prediction; why Maitreya alone? Moreover, suchness is without arising and ceasing; how can a prediction be obtained?" Next, as for "The Viśeṣacintin Sūtra says": because a prediction is empty and false, he wishes not to hear its name. The Larger [Prajñā] is the same.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the first passage, as for saying "this view must be broken through" and so on: the meaning is that where there is a view it must be broken, and where there is a vowed prediction it must be given; how could one cite only the Vimalakīrti and the like? In terms of the present sūtra, five intents must be complete: first, to break the near predictions obtained in the teachings of expedients; second, to break the defiled attachment arising in those first predicted; third, to reveal the marks of prediction and non-prediction in the Mahāyāna gate; fourth, to stop the hoping mind of those not yet fit to be predicted; fifth, for those fit to hear the breaking of attachment and gain benefit. The Mahāyāna gate's breaking of the lesser includes in meaning the marks of prediction in the three teachings and four gates, and so on.

What Other Sūtras Lack: The Eight Predictions and the Fixed Nature

Chinese Source

五對他經辨有無者，又二，初明今有，次「瓔珞」下辨無明有。又諸經為對菩薩，多授法身究竟果記，今此品中秖記八相，如前後說。「瓔珞八記」者，今經已眾無不知者，故前四句中，今經唯有第三句，純顯露故，於中不必在第七地，如不輕中一句通記，故他經記深。後四句中，初句雖云遠處不覺，化道同故亦非不覺，故今經有第三句義同初三二句也。故知諸經實義未暢。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, as for distinguishing presence and absence by setting it against other sūtras, again two: first it makes clear what the present [sūtra] has; next, from "The Yingluo Sūtra" on, it distinguishes the absence and makes clear the presence. Moreover, the various sūtras, being addressed to bodhisattvas, mostly bestow predictions of the dharma-body's ultimate fruit; in this chapter it predicts only the eight aspects [of a buddha's life], as said before and after. As for "eight kinds of conferring the prediction" of the Yingluo: in the present sūtra the assembly already all know; hence of the first four phrases, the present sūtra has only the third phrase, because it is purely open. Within it, one need not be at the seventh ground, as in [the chapter on] Never Disparaging a single phrase predicts generally; hence the predictions of the other sūtras are deep. Of the latter four phrases, though the first phrase says the distant one is unaware, because the path of transformation is the same, he too is not unaware; hence the present sūtra has the third phrase, and its meaning is the same as the third of the first [four]: the two phrases [coincide]. Hence one knows that the real meaning of the various sūtras is not yet fully expressed.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "has not yet attained the practice free of attachment" together with "the seventh ground": the words "free of attachment" should be judged by the shared [teaching]. In the distinct and perfect teachings, one who first enters the grounds or the abidings is already called free of attachment; or else it is in the distinct teaching, in terms of the path of teaching only. Since one is free of attachment only on entering [the stage] without effort, judged by the accomplishment of the contemplation of emptiness it fits only the shared. As for "the seventh ground": it is in the shared teaching, because, having passed the two vehicles, one is fit to be given a prediction. Yet the treatise first lists four kinds of śrāvakas: those who retreated from the great and those [appearing] in response-transformation are given predictions; the overweening and the fixed are not given them. Someone defends it, saying that the fixed are also predicted. This too is not so. Their position takes the fixed to be the fixed nature that never arouses the mind. One need only point to the sūtra text: though they have the notion of extinction, they get to hear [this sūtra] in that land; their meaning collapses by itself; why seek further? They only explain those with the notion of extinction as ordinary beings, twisting the sūtra text to make it fit their own meaning. Also, the Ratnagotra Treatise only says that śrāvakas who have left the realms have dull faculties; it does not say their faculties are ruined. As for "ruined faculties": Kāśyapa in the Vaipulya is just such a person. On reaching the Lotus the ruined faculties are restored; those who entered extinction are born [there] only after leaving the realms. Master Sheng says: "Having merged with principle and being free of burdens, how could he admit a land? Yet though he is said to be without a land, there is nowhere that is not his land; without body and without name, yet body and name surpass existence. Hence land and name arise in response to beings; [predicting them] only draws on those who are not yet sufficient. If one grasps the present intent, one only says that the eight aspects lure beings." These words sum it up. The Treatise on the Lotus says: "Because the two vehicles have buddha-nature and the dharma-body, they are given predictions; it is not that their practice is complete." What the Treatise calls "not complete" is with respect to the ultimate fruit only. How could they not cultivate the practices of the pure land? By this it is clear that they must further make offerings to buddhas. If pratyekabuddhas enter the number of śrāvakas, they are the same as śrāvakas; those who appear in an age without a buddha are the same as the fixed nature.

Ten Layers of the Four Siddhāntas

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, from "Originally, every buddha" on, it treats the four siddhāntas broadly; in all there are ten layers making up the four siddhāntas. As for the two within the mundane: the first is in terms of capacity and response set against each other; the next is in terms of beings' capacities alone. Though in meaning it is called single, in the end it forms capacity and response: capacity and appeal fit each other, as with [the benefit of] joy. As for the two within the individual: the first passage, changing from the lesser and entering the great, already produces one's own good. As for the assembly's vows, from "The assembly of that time" on, there is further the good of benefiting others. As for the four of the therapeutic: first, breaking the evil of bodhisattvas retreating to the lesser; next, breaking the evil of wishing to arouse the lesser mind; next, directly breaking the lesser evil; next, breaking the evil of being about to realize the lesser. The third has already realized; the fourth has already entered the stages of the worthies; hence they differ from the second. If it were a matter of striking at those lesser people by addressing bodhisattvas, other sūtras also have it; now it makes clear that predicting the lesser further draws on lesser people, hence it is only the present sūtra. As for the two of the ultimate meaning: first the explanation proper, next resolving a doubt; the passage on the doubt can be seen. As for the explanation from "Yet sentient beings" on: it is in order to complete the intent of the ultimate meaning; the preceding three siddhāntas need not be non-arising. Moreover, within the ultimate meaning it is only in terms of predicting oneself, whereas the preceding three siddhāntas sometimes include both self and other: thus in the therapeutic it is wholly with respect to others, and in the individual the first includes self and other while the latter is only others. In the first, the mundane, if one sets the lord of transformation against those predicted, it too is only in others. Though there are these ten, they are also spoken of for the time being only in terms of predicting the two vehicles. The single phrase "the Buddha's own prediction," the bodhisattva's prediction, and so on are not discussed here.

Explaining the Parable of the Phantom City Chapter

Chinese Source

因緣釋中為四：初約喻釋名，次以法合，三「蘇息」下說化意，四「權假」下總結。初文者以大涅槃非化作故不專禦敵，理性即故、具眾德故。次合中初總標，「權智」下合神力所為，「以權」下合無而歘有，「用教」下合化，「防思」下合城。教無實故，故名為「歘」。言非敵譬見思者，非寬敵急、見逼思遙，見親礙故，思不障果。三說意者，蘇息施小引入顯大故，二酥名引醍醐方入，是教道故，「故而言」下郤釋城也。「權假」去結意者，辨異實故。於此四中立四悉者，若通方義立從於權智，若從機說無而欲見，見已生喜即世界益，得入蘇息即為人益，防非禦敵即對治益，而言滅度第一義益。若從能引權立，此城即世界化，為生小善即為人化，且除見思即對治化，終引入大即第一義化。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the explanation by causes and conditions there are four [parts]: first, explaining the name in terms of the metaphor; next, matching it with the dharma; third, from "revives" on, stating the intent of the phantom; fourth, from "a provisional, borrowed device" on, the general conclusion. In the first passage: because great nirvāṇa is not something conjured, it does not only ward off enemies, since it is identical to the principle-nature and contains the multitude of virtues. Next, within the matching, first comes the general heading; from "provisional wisdom" on, it matches "what spiritual power brings about"; from "By the power of provisional wisdom" on, it matches "something that was not comes to be all at once"; from "the teaching is used" on, it matches "conjured"; from "guarding against wrong thought" on, it matches "city." Because the teaching has no reality, it is called "all at once" (歘). As for "wrongdoing" and "the enemy" being metaphors for thought and views: wrongdoing is lax and the enemy urgent; views press close and thought is distant; because views are close they obstruct, while thought does not hinder the fruit. Third, as for stating the intent: "revives" is bestowing the lesser to lead into revealing the great; hence the two butters are named "leading," and only with ghee does one enter, because it is the path of teaching. From "and so it is called" on, it goes back to explain "city." As for the conclusion of the intent from "a provisional, borrowed device" on: it distinguishes it from the real. Setting up the four siddhāntas within these four: if according to the general meaning, they are set up from provisional wisdom. If spoken from the capacity: it was not, and they wished to see it; seeing it, they produce joy, which is the mundane benefit; entering revival is the individual benefit; guarding against wrongdoing and warding off the enemy is the therapeutic benefit; and calling it "cessation and deliverance" is the benefit of the ultimate meaning. If set up from the provisional that can lead: this city is the mundane transformation; producing lesser good is the individual transformation; removing views and thought for the time being is the therapeutic transformation; leading into the great in the end is the transformation of the ultimate meaning.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, in terms of the teachings: the Tripiṭaka bodhisattva has not yet set out at all, so he is not discussed. The bodhisattva of the shared teaching, though he reaches the city together [with the others], enters it and yet can come out, unlike the lesser: because he originally leaves the realms, one foot enters the city; because of great compassion and because he does not realize [nirvāṇa] with residue, one foot does not enter. The capacities and conditions of the three realms are called the "children"; those who aroused the mind long ago are by meaning like the "wife." The shared teaching takes the two vehicles as the evil path; the distinct teaching takes birth-and-death as perilous, reaching nirvāṇa without entering it; hence it is called "passes directly through." The words "not . the ultimate" are spoken with respect to the lesser. As for the perfect teaching's speaking of "conjured": in the past [the lesser] was rebuked and stripped, it being said only that they were unfit, yet it was never said that nirvāṇa is conjured. Hence only when the present teaching stirs attachment and opens the provisional does it say it is conjured, up to revealing the real, when the conjured becomes the true, because it is the treasure isle. Hence one knows: the Tripiṭaka and shared regard it as ultimate and not conjured; the distinct as not ultimate and not conjured; the perfect as not ultimate and conjured, or also as ultimate and conjured, or also as ultimate and not conjured, which uses the same words as the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings with a different meaning. As for "The present passage" and so on: it too says "conjured" for the time being from the breaking of attachment. Once opened and revealed, nothing is not true and real. As for "The trace-and-origin reading and the contemplation of mind are not recorded": they can be understood by comparison. Four kinds of śrāvakas. Vasubandhu's Treatise on the Lotus (T1519) lists fixed, overweening, retreated, and transformation śrāvakas, and gives predictions only to the last two (unit 9). The three wheels. In unit 44 意輪／口輪／身輪 are the wheels of mind, speech, and body.
