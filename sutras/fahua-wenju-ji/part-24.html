The Jeweled Stūpa, Devadatta, and the Upholding Chapter

This part is the fourth and last section of Zhanran's eighth fascicle (the Taishō's 八之四). It comments on the later portion of the Textual Commentary's eighth fascicle (the site's fascicle-008): the Jeweled Stūpa, Devadatta, and Upholding chapters. On the Jeweled Stūpa, Zhanran sorts the Commentary's four accounts of the chapter title into the four siddhāntas, rejects an earlier commentator's reading of the stūpa as a sign that the Buddha at the twin trees was not extinguished, and explains how the stūpa's emergence attests what came before and initiates the revealing of the origin. He then works through the stūpa's dimensions and ornaments as the virtues of the dharma body, and answers questions about Prabhūtaratna's vow, the threefold transformation of the land, and the emanation buddhas' consent. On Devadatta, he reviews the textual history of the chapter, disputes Jiaxiang's three arguments against its authenticity, lists the early translations of the Lotus and Sengrui's nine tracks, and discusses the five grave transgressions, the six perfections under the four teachings, the thirty-two marks, Mañjuśrī's coming from the ocean, and the Dragon Princess's buddhahood. On the Upholding chapter, he explains the araṇya dweller's proper conduct and the "armor" of forbearance through the Madhyama Āgama and the Vibhāṣā.

Explaining the Jeweled Stūpa Chapter

Chinese Source

此中四度云「見寶塔」，下文云「四番」即四悉也。初世界中翻名有無三世不同，覩必歡喜故是世界。言「經云佛三種身」者，借《普賢觀經》也，既結此經故可證同。「當佛亦然」者，未來有說《法華經》處，此塔還現、彼佛亦坐。本論亦云「此經為三身菩提故。此經已說師弟因果，故古佛證、集分身佛。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Here it says "this is why it is called the chapter 'The Jeweled Stūpa'" four times, and the text below says "these four accounts," which are the four siddhāntas. In the first, the mundane [siddhānta], there are the translation of the name, the presence or absence [of bodily relics], and the difference of the three ages; seeing [the stūpa], one is sure to rejoice, hence it is the mundane. As for "The sūtra says that the Buddha's three bodies": this borrows from the Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra; since that is the concluding sūtra of this sūtra, it can serve as proof of the same. As for "buddhas yet to come will do likewise": in the future, wherever there is a place where the Lotus Sūtra is taught, this stūpa will appear again, and that buddha too will sit [in it]. The treatise on this [sūtra] also says: "This sūtra is for the sake of the awakening of the three bodies. This sūtra has already taught the cause and effect of teacher and disciple; hence the ancient buddha bears witness, and the emanation buddhas are gathered."

Chinese Source

彼《華嚴經》加四菩薩說菩薩因果，能加但是迹佛主伴，故不假集佛，但云十方互為主伴，仍不云伴是佛分身，但云眷屬而已。文中諸品皆云「集諸菩薩」，注家云：道非存亡，古今一揆。然則裂地踊塔以表雙林不滅，更延接影微顯丈六非生，故云「見寶塔品」。今謂古佛塔今佛坐，則表古今實道不生，未必預表雙林不滅。一往觀之，謂今現佛入古滅佛之塔，表滅不滅。今謂不然，古佛塔現示滅而不滅，釋迦入塔示生而不生，不生不滅故並坐表之。集分身召本屬，乃顯舍那非成，豈唯丈六示滅他人？

Ru-Yi Translation

In the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, [the Buddha] empowers four bodhisattvas to teach the cause and effect of bodhisattvas; the one who empowers is only the trace buddha with his host and companions; hence it has no need to gather buddhas, and says only that [those of] the ten directions are host and companions to one another. Still, it does not say that the companions are the Buddha's emanations, but only calls them [his] retinue. In that text every chapter says "gathering all the bodhisattvas." A commentator says: "The Way is neither preserved nor lost; past and present are of one measure. Thus the splitting of the earth and the welling up of the stūpa signify that [the Buddha at] the twin [śāla] trees is not extinguished, and the further invitation and reception of [his] image subtly shows that the sixteen-foot [body] is not born; hence it says 'the Jeweled Stūpa Chapter.'" I say: the ancient buddha's stūpa with the present buddha seated [in it] signifies that the real Way of past and present is unborn; it need not signify in advance that [the Buddha at] the twin trees is not extinguished. Looked at as a first approximation, [he] says that the present living buddha's entering the stūpa of the ancient extinguished buddha signifies extinction that is not extinction. I say it is not so: the appearing of the ancient buddha's stūpa shows extinction that is not extinction, and Śākyamuni's entering the stūpa shows birth that is not birth; it is because [they are] neither born nor extinguished that their sitting side by side signifies it. Gathering the emanations and summoning the original retinue reveals that Rocana was not [newly] attained; how would it be merely the sixteen-foot [body] showing extinction to others?

Ru-Yi Translation

[That commentator] asks: When the great assembly sees the stūpa, is it the faculty, or is it the consciousness? Or is it the stūpa that makes them see? He uses analogies broadly to refute [arising from] self and other. This deeply fails to understand the great purport of the sūtra. After the three rounds, even if there were ordinary beings, all differed from ordinary views; moreover, the contemplative method of the Middle Treatise is meant for the dull faculties of the last age; why trouble to set this up for those matching the occasion in the Buddha's time? In digesting the sūtra, contemplative practice by principle ought to be like this [Commentary]. In all explanations of sūtras, if there is no comprehensive view, later students are left unversed in the path of the school; this [text] is precisely for attesting the sūtra, and incidentally discusses what is signified.

有人問：召古佛、集分身，身何故多，塔何故一？今為答之，證經義足，故寶塔不多，合多寶願，故分身不一。假令證經須集多塔，塔豈不多？既能應現十方，信知塔亦非一，故分身是化、塔亦宜權。今從所表，今佛一身而集多身，密表迹用之非一；古佛多塔但示一塔，顯表實理之不二，況從地在空及諸校飾，悉非徒爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Someone asks: In summoning the ancient buddha and gathering the emanations, why are the bodies many and the stūpa one? I now answer: for attesting the sūtra the meaning is sufficient, hence the jeweled stūpa is not many; to fulfill Prabhūtaratna's vow, hence the emanations are not one. Supposing that attesting the sūtra required gathering many stūpas, would the stūpas not be many? Since [he] can appear in response throughout the ten directions, one knows with confidence that the stūpa too is not one; hence, as the emanations are transformations, the stūpa too should be provisional. Now, following what is signified: the present buddha, one body, gathers many bodies, secretly signifying that the function of the trace is not one; the ancient buddha, [having] many stūpas, shows only one stūpa, openly signifying that the real principle is not two. Let alone [its rising] from the earth, [standing] in the air, and all its adornments: none of it is for nothing.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "makes this fact manifest": the turning of the Dharma wheel and so on by the buddhas of the three ages all reach the Lotus, where the matter for which they appear in the world is taught; hence the present text applies the four relic-sites [to it]. From "The Yingluo Sūtra" on is the individual [siddhānta], because comparing merit is for producing good. As for "records Subhūti asking" and so on: the ninth chapter, "Offering to Relics," says: "Because Subhūti asked whether making offerings to whole relics, fragmented relics, the living body, and the dharma body is the same or different, the Buddha compared them broadly, saying: Making offerings to the relics of one buddha by raising stūpas filling the four continents is not as good as making offerings to the living body, because it is by the physical body that there are relics. Again, raising stūpas filling the great thousandfold world and making offerings to the physical body is not as good as making offerings to the dharma body, because it is by the dharma body that there is the physical body." One should know that seeing the physical [body] does not match hearing the sūtra, because it is by hearing the sūtra that there is the dharma body; hence the partial and the perfect [teachings] of the sūtras are the whole and fragmented [relics] of the dharma body, and their merits are not equal. As for "the Tathāgata Crown-King" and so on: I once examined the Mahāyāna Crown-King Sūtra and the Vaipulya Crown-King Sūtra, one fascicle each, and did not find this in either. The point of the objection: the teaching and the relics are the same in duration and the same in benefit; how can they be unequal? The point of the answer: that the relics are equal in duration is also the power of the living body.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "A Northern master says" on: refuting the error and rejecting the wrong, which in meaning amounts to breaking evil; this is the therapeutic [siddhānta]. As for "once the teaching of the sūtra was finished": the meaning is that the main sūtra was already complete. From "A Northern master says that Prabhūtaratna is the dharma-body buddha" (地師, the Dilun master) on: the bodies are signified as not two; because this accords with principle, it belongs to the ultimate meaning. From "the Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra says" on: proving the Dilun master wrong. "My own teacher says": [this is] Nanyue. Though it speaks of three bodies, the intent is to use the dharma and reward [bodies] to reject the Dilun master. As for "neither comes": [then] it ought not to come from the east. As for "nor goes" (無出, does not come forth): it ought not to well up. As for "towering and majestic": it ought not to be inside a stūpa. As for "the response-body": it ought not to be only this [one]. As for "to say simply that Prabhūtaratna is any one of these three bodies" and so on: as with the superior marks of the three bodies described by Nanyue, if one says outright that Prabhūtaratna is the dharma buddha, that certainly will not do. He is not even the response body; how could he contain the three bodies? Hence one knows that [saying] Prabhūtaratna is the dharma body does not exhaust the sūtra's purport. As for "This is, rather, only a matter of symbolic representation" and so on: it is acceptable only to use matters such as Śākyamuni to signify principle. Hence one knows that [he] not only comes to attest but signifies that the sūtra is eternal; hence Prabhūtaratna signifies the Dharma and also contains the three bodies. Prabhūtaratna, long extinguished, now comes forth to attest the sūtra: birth that is not birth. Having passed into extinction long ago, he still shows his whole body: extinction that is not extinction. Since it is neither birth nor extinction, eternally abiding and unmoving, it can signify the dharma body. Śākyamuni enters the stūpa and the two bodies match each other, as wisdom matches its object; hence it can signify the reward [body]. That the emanations signify the response [body] is self-evident in text and principle: as when object and wisdom merge, a response can arise.

「三佛」至「而不一異」者，能表三中各三故不一，所表三中皆一故不異。又能表中三相別故不一，法體同故不異。又所表中亦以體同故不異，以身別故不一。又化道別故不一，共成此故不異。總而言之，即三而一故不異，即一而三故不一。「見寶塔」下「云云」者，於前四文明示四相以消四文，略如前釋，四皆屬圓。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "Though the three buddha-bodies" through "neither strictly one nor strictly different": in the three that signify, each is three, hence not one; in the three that are signified, all are one, hence not different. Again, within what signifies, the three marks are distinct, hence not one; the Dharma-essence is the same, hence not different. Again, within what is signified, too, because the essence is the same, not different; because the bodies are distinct, not one. Again, because the paths of transformation are distinct, not one; because together they accomplish this [sūtra], not different. Speaking in sum: being three yet one, hence not different; being one yet three, hence not one. As for the "and so on" after "the jeweled stūpa's appearance": in the four passages above, the four marks [of the siddhāntas] are clearly shown so as to digest the four passages, roughly as explained before; all four belong to the perfect [teaching].

Attesting What Came Before and Initiating What Follows

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

From "The stūpa's emergence serves two purposes" on is, next, [the explanation] by the teachings and by origin and trace. First it lists both meanings together; next it explains the two intents of [the stūpa's] emergence. In the explanation there are again four: first, the explanation proper; next, from "And if the stūpa is taken as rising from the earth" on, distinguishing same and different; third, from "Where the stūpa's coming bears witness" on, judging open and secret; fourth, from "and to take up this latter sense here" on, resolving an objection. The first passage again has two: first it makes clear the stūpa's emergence, attesting what came before and initiating what follows; next it makes clear [its standing] in the air, so as to signify the two intents. Hence in both passages, attesting what came before corresponds to [the explanation by] the teachings, and initiating what follows corresponds to origin and trace. In the first, attesting what came before, there are two: first in terms of "true and real," next in terms of "great wisdom," because these two are Prabhūtaratna's words of attestation. The first passage has three: first it shows that the three rounds are all perfect and real; next it distinguishes brief, broad, and so on; third it shows the dissemination. The first is as the text. In the next passage, first brief, then broad. In the first [of these]: since [Prabhūtaratna] restates and attests, it can only be brief; hence it says only "true and real." Moreover, the middle and the broad [statements] are both in terms of what is negated; what is negated must be many, while what negates is only one; hence in the three rounds, whether it is the four kinds of oneness, or ten, or many, none goes beyond the true mark. Next, as for "the eight negations" in the middle [statement]: fully as in the notes on fascicle six of Calming and Contemplation, which cite the text of the Middle Treatise. The Middle Treatise reveals principle, hence it praises the Buddha with the eight negations; in the stūpa's attestation the sūtra too praises the real with the eight negations; hence the event of the stūpa's welling up again signifies the eight negations. Because the meaning is the same, it says "The stūpa's rising from the earth" and so on. From "Further, it bears witness" on, it shows that attesting the dissemination is also true and real.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, in terms of great wisdom. As for "true and real": in general it covers the four kinds of oneness, but specifically it lies in what is revealed; hence first it praises great wisdom, and next it praises the Dharma that is taught and revealed. Great wisdom is simply the wisdom of [all] modes, already explained fully before; here it only further distinguishes how it is the same as and different from the unshared prajñā. Within this, first the question; next, from "The Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra, fascicle seventy-nine, says" on, the answer; third, from "like a single city gate" on, the simile. Because that prajñā and this great wisdom both can enter, the word "wondrous Dharma" in one respect extends to wisdom. Fourth, from "from which it may be known" on, it concludes that the meaning of the two texts is the same. Next, as for "initiating what is to follow, making clear the origin": making clear the origin in contrast with the trace can be called origin and trace. Within it there are two; first it makes clear the reason for what is initiated. Because the stūpa came forth, [they] request its opening; because the stūpa opens, [they] see the buddha; because they see the buddha, they request empowerment; because they obtain empowerment, they are in the air; because they are in the air, [he] commands the assembly; because he commands the assembly, the voice penetrates; because the voice penetrates, the assembly arrives; because the assembly arrives, doubt arises; because doubt arises, [the origin] comes to be taught. As for "setting forth the profound entrustment" and so on: briefly raising the sūtra's title profoundly gathers the whole collection; hence it says "The Buddha wishes to [entrust] this wondrous Dharma" and so on. From "the vast, ancient stūpa" on, it makes clear what initiates and what is signified: it signifies the benefit of the original ground in the three ages. Long extinguished and now coming forth, hence "spiritual penetration"; present benefit lies in teaching, hence "sound"; the vow does not cease, hence to the end of the future; by this initiating that, hence it is called "opens."

The three provisionalities. In unit 72 Zhanran maps the Vibhāṣā's arguments onto the provisionality of causal composition ("this accomplishment belongs to him"), of continuity ("a single syllable"), and of mutual dependence ("the one who insults and the one insulted").

Sound element. The eightfold classification of sound (unit 72) follows the Abhidharmakośa: sounds produced by appropriated or unappropriated great elements, each either conveying meaning to sentient beings or not, and each agreeable or disagreeable. Read by the Kośa, "wind, forests, rivers … are called appropriated" should be "not appropriated."
