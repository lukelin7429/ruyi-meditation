Belief and Understanding: The Lost Son and the Lion's Throne

This part opens Zhanran's commentary on the Belief and Understanding chapter, following the Textual Commentary's sixth fascicle (the site's fascicle-006). He first rejects several earlier readings of the chapter: faith and understanding as four alternatives of sharp and dull, right and wrong view; faith and understanding as two mental factors; and a list of seven differences between Śāriputra and the four great disciples. He then sorts the six masters who read the chapter through "initial, following, and final" stages of transformation, and explains the title through the four siddhāntas and the paths of seeing and cultivation. The rest of the part follows the parable of the destitute son as far as the father's first pursuit. Zhanran argues against identifying the elder with Amitābha, reads the father's "pausing in a city" as the Buddha dwelling in the land of expedients with residue, matches the father's wealth, servants, ministers, and officials to the stages of the distinct and perfect teachings, and explains why the son "sees the father" before the father sees him. The lion's throne, the jeweled bench, the canopy, banners, and scattered flowers are read as the adornments of the reward body, and the father's sending of attendants is aligned, gate by gate, with persuasion and admonition in the parable of the burning house.

Explaining the Belief and Understanding Chapter

Chinese Source

有人以信解相對為四句，鈍根正見信而不解，利根邪見解而不信，利根正見有信有解，鈍根邪見無信無解。初二二句依何得名？若第三句當此品者，若約小乘得作此說，不合釋此。若法執中見等，聲聞領時合入七地，云何猶在初信心耶？有人以通大地信數慧數為信解體。今問，體是何義？指心所耶？如此心所聖位攝耶？凡位攝耶？若聖位者大小聖耶？小非今意大深淺耶？況今聞實而領解耶？故信解今經已入初住，則非小乘心所所攝，舉一例諸餘皆準此。

Ru-Yi Translation

Someone sets faith and understanding against each other as four alternatives: the dull with right view have faith but no understanding; the sharp with wrong view have understanding but no faith; the sharp with right view have both faith and understanding; the dull with wrong view have neither faith nor understanding. On what basis do the first two alternatives get their names? If the third alternative corresponds to this chapter, such an account could be made in terms of the Lesser Vehicle, but it does not fit the explanation of this [chapter]. If [they mean] views and so on within attachment to dharmas, then at the time the śrāvakas received [understanding] they should have entered the seventh ground; how could they still be at the initial mind of faith? Someone takes the mental factors of faith and of wisdom among the generally present great-ground [factors] as the essence of faith and understanding. Now I ask: what does "essence" mean? Does it point to mental associates? Are such mental associates included in the sage stages, or in the ordinary stages? If in the sage stages, are they the sages of the great or of the lesser? The lesser is not the present intent; if the great, are they deep or shallow? How much more so, since now they hear the real and receive understanding? Hence faith and understanding in the present sūtra have already entered the first abiding, and are not included among the mental associates of the Lesser Vehicle. Raise one as an example, and all the rest follow it.

Ru-Yi Translation

Someone says that, compared with before, there are seven differences; [but] the differences do not hold. First, a difference in what they proceed from. I say this is not so: the three faculties, hearing the brief [teaching], all gave rise to doubt and had their attachment stirred; only their faith and understanding come earlier or later. How can one say that Śāriputra proceeds from doubt? Here, proceeding from attachment: where there is doubt there must be attachment, and where there is attachment there must be doubt. Moreover, the names "doubt" and "attachment" are general and must be distinguished from the lesser; in the Lesser Vehicle, doubt and attachment are already removed at the path of seeing; how, separated by the two butters, must they still be set up against each other? Second, a difference of broad and brief: this too is not so; only the text differs, while the meaning is the same. Thus Śāriputra says, "could this not be Māra," and so on, and relates the five buddhas; the words are brief but the meaning broad; how could it be called brief? Third, a difference of distant and near: this too is not so; text and meaning are both the same. Not only does this text relate [the time] before the bodhi tree; Śāriputra too, in relating the time when he originally clung to wrong views, goes back beyond the bodhi tree; hence there is no difference. Fourth, a difference of general and specific: this too is not so; the text differs but the intent is the same. Śāriputra says, "while we ourselves had no part in this," and also, "We too have entered equally into the nature of all things," and also, "if we had waited for him to teach the causes," and also, "we did not understand that he was teaching according to what was fitting by skillful means." In the passage relating the loss he says "we" four times; how could the relating of the attainment be specific to one person? Though four are listed here, [as] "the head of the saṅgha" they are again restricted. Thus Śāriputra extends to all, while Kāśyapa is specific to the four persons.

The Six Masters on the Three Stages

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Before explaining the name of the chapter, it cites the ancients' judgment of the chapter's intent, because the failure in explaining the title stems from failing the intent of the text. Within it, first, generally listing the three times as "the initial" and so on: since within this chapter the beginning and the end are received in full, starting again with the lesser after retreating from the great is taken as the initial, the interval as the following, and the last as the final. The ancients got only the names of the three times and did not understand their intent. Now, with the five periods, and adding the dharma-body's transformation received by anticipation, one still fears not understanding the feelings of the four sages; merely making names such as "initial," how could one parse this chapter? Hence they must be cited and refuted; only then is the correct principle recognized. In all there are six masters. Saṃnāha (僧那) is here called "the great vow." Next, from "In my own view" on, is the refutation. First, the general rebuke. A book says: "Cut silk filling a basket cannot be woven into a figured sash; jade shavings filling a box cannot be carved into a scepter." The various explanations are fragmentary and confused and cannot be relied on. From "Now, the initial stage" on is the specific refutation; it first states the initial and the final, briefly bringing out the main point, and the middle can be known by analogy.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, within the specific refutation proper, the first two phrases concede and then refute: granting the "initial" of the second and third [masters], it uses their "initial" to refute the first master and the fifth master. Next, from "If the Lotus, though final" on, it again sets the fifth master's "initial" against the second's "initial" as a parallel. Next, from "Again, if the two lesser vehicles" on, it uses the third master's "initial" to refute the first master's "initial"; within this it first refutes them side by side. Next, from "If one is broken down and the other is not" on, it sets up the analogy. Next, from "Again, if father and son seeing one another" on, it refutes the first master's "final" alone: seeing his father from a distance is called "seeing one another"; if that is called final, then at the time of the Avataṃsaka teaching they should already have received prediction, and the Lotus would be useless. From "but if it is the later 'final'" on, it reverses the parallel: if the later is right, the earlier has no further use; if it has no further use, the forming of affinity by hearing the great in the past is not established. Moreover, the "final" set up by the second, third, and fourth masters lies in every case in the Lotus, and that intent is acceptable. [But their] "initial" and "following," near or distant, are hard to rely on. The fifth master's "initial" has already been refuted as above; his "following" and "final" do not explain the Lotus: how could the present single transformation necessarily bring them to the vajra? For the fourth master, Zhang'an has no refutation; [the text] is only brief. He says the "initial" lies in the transferred teaching; then what precedes the transferred teaching is not "initial"; what is it called? He takes attaining awakening as "final"; then the five grades and the six faculties are again neither "initial" nor "following"; what are they named? Yet the masters' "final" is in no case refuted, because the first four and the fifth for the most part all reach the Lotus, or say "reaching the buddha-fruit"; even if there are small faults, there is no great error; only one cannot definitely take the vajra mind as the "final." Our master's intent is that words such as "initial" can only be weighed when one comes to discuss the past lives for the lower faculties, because the vehicles of humans and gods and the teaching of the great lie in the distant past, and everything after the distant past is called "following." How can the second, third, and fourth masters take the three flavors of this life as "following"? Hence they are not adopted. Next, from "In my own view, this is indeed so in principle" on, it refutes [the view of] origin and trace: if this is taken as trace, then it would already be the origin gate stirring attachment; if it anticipates the later chapter, then here the revealing of the origin would already be completed, and the later text would be useless. Hence it is not acceptable.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "Now, in explaining this chapter" on: the present intent for the moment discusses the faith and understanding of the middling faculties; hence it first judges its intent by five of the ten meanings at the beginning of the three rounds. "Faculties are sharp or dull" is the turning of faculties before. Not turning the faculties [corresponds to] the appeal being thick or thin, that is, "afflictions are thick or thin" before. "Teaching proceeds through dharma-exposition and through parable" is the general and the specific before. "Awakening comes sooner for some and later for others" is awakening or not awakening, and awakening being shallow or deep, before. The other meanings are not principal. For this reason the three faculties receive understanding earlier or later; what need is there to set up "initial" and so on here? As for "pea" (豌豆): if it is written 剜, it is just the character for cutting with a knife. Those of the two vehicles before the Lotus are like raw peas that a drill or awl cannot enter. As for "could only reflect upon emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness": this is the gate by which their own teaching enters the undefiled; hence they constantly think of it. As for "they are joyful and exultant" and so on: though in the text the four characters beginning with "faith" are used to match faith and understanding as the individual [siddhānta], this good must rely on the first, joy. Moreover, with principle [made clear], good arises, evil is broken, and realization is gained: the four siddhāntas, perfectly fused, are obtained all at once; that is the ultimate meaning of partial realization. The other three siddhāntas are named by their own positions and, following [it], are said to be partial. Hence these four siddhāntas necessarily arise at the same time; only because matching faith and understanding to the individual [siddhānta] is convenient, it speaks of them separately for the moment; this is the specific within the general. Why are all four siddhāntas real here? Because understanding is attained through hearing the Dharma. It is unlike the earlier text listing the assemblies, which, accompanied by the lesser, has three siddhāntas in phenomena, while the siddhānta of ultimate meaning must still be distinguished by teaching; only at the end of the five periods is the present intent distinguished.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, from "Those receiving either the Lesser or the Greater teaching" on is the explanation in terms of the teachings. This wishes to explain faith and understanding in terms of stages, and should list all four teachings, but it states generally "receiving either the Lesser or the Greater teaching." The lesser is the Tripiṭaka, and the greater must point to the perfect; for the moment it omits the middle two, because the great and lesser teachings without exception set up the two practices and the two paths. Hence it first makes clear the lesser, and next, from "Measuring the Lesser Vehicle against the Greater" on, looks at it from the great, dividing the two characters and assigning them to the two paths: breaking doubt is therefore faith, and advancing to enter is called understanding. Faith extends to both paths, and understanding lies only in cultivation; hence it says, "one enters the path of cultivation . and this is called understanding." By this intent, only the first abiding should be taken as the path of seeing, because principle is seen for the first time; adding effort at the first abiding is called the path of cultivation, because one cultivates in reliance on principle. The text does not say "from the second abiding on" but only "advancing further"; that is, from the first abiding there is necessarily increase. Hence the śrāvakas, after hearing the Dharma, mostly enter the path of cultivation; now it speaks from the side of increase after hearing the Dharma; hence "faith and understanding." It is also like the sixteenth mind being called the path of cultivation: if they entered secretly in the past, here it is plainly the full path of cultivation; speaking from the manifest, faith and understanding are simultaneous. Next, from "The text says" on, for the moment it generally proves the entry into the stage, without dividing faith and understanding.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "receiving, nearby, the burning-house parable" and so on: having heard the parable and understood it, they necessarily understand the dharma; how could they, after awakening, again be confused about what came before? Hence all must receive both dharma and parable. As for the two explanations of "Wisdom-Life": the first is causes and conditions; the next, from "Among all who possess wisdom" on, is in terms of the teachings. The three and the one are distinguished against each other by the difference of practice in phenomena; hence it belongs to causes and conditions. As for "the Buddha . charged Subhūti with the task of turning to teach others" belonging to [the explanation] in terms of the teachings: there are two intents here. Belonging to the teaching of ripened butter, it is already called "in terms of the teachings"; at the time of the ripened butter they secretly became people of the distinct [teaching], and it is again called "in terms of the teachings." One should also match it fully against the five flavors and the four teachings; reflect on it and it can be understood. From "The other three disciples" on, it resolves a doubt: it is not that they did not transfer [the teaching] at all; it is only a matter of more or less. The new [translations] name it "endowed with life" (具壽): "endowed" does not reach "wisdom," and how could "lifespan" surpass "life"? As for "the fourfold telling of the parable": prose and verse each have a general and a specific [parable]; or each has the opening of the parable and its application; two ways, four rounds, each with opening and revealing. Yet the former explanation is superior; then it would correspond to the twenty-two rounds of the later text. As for "this mind having arisen" up to "the sense of 'Faith and Understanding'": by this, only by hearing the parable does faith arise and they enter the stage, that is, from the first abiding on, without discussing seeing and cultivation. As for "Following the pattern of Śāriputra" and so on: "gave rise to a mind of wonder" is the mind's receiving of understanding; "at once rose from their seats" is the body's receiving of understanding; "and . said to him" is speech's receiving of understanding. It should have noted "and so on," to have one match them as just now, fully as in Śāriputra's verses of receiving; that the text lacks it is abbreviation.

Ru-Yi Translation

Relating the three [reasons for] not seeking, standing at the head of the saṅgha and the rest, relates the loss in the past. Translator's notes

Quoted phrases. This part comments on the opening of the Belief and Understanding chapter in the site's fascicle-006 of the Textual Commentary; one phrase (unit 13) is from fascicle-005. Quoted phrases follow the site's English, which uses the chapter name "Faith and Understanding"; Zhanran's own sentences use "Belief and Understanding." Where the site has "abodes" for 住, "abidings" is written; "kalpas" is written "eons."

The Lotus. Lines quoted directly from the sūtra follow the site's Lotus: "while we ourselves had no part in this," "We too have entered equally into the nature of all things" (unit 3); "he had never spoken of this to anyone," "when he came at last to die" (units 31–32); the Skillful Means verses "for three periods of seven days," "Beings' faculties are dull," "People of this kind," "I went at once to Vārāṇasī" (unit 57); and the burning-house passages in unit 58. The Brahmās' verses in the Phantom City chapter (unit 16) follow the site's wording, with "eons" for "kalpas."

The Correct Dharma Flower. Dharmarakṣa's translation (T263) titles the chapter "Faith and Delight" (信樂品), which Zhanran rejects because delight falls short of understanding (unit 8).

The six masters. The six schemes of "initial, following, and final" are listed in the site's fascicle-006. The saying about cut silk and jade shavings (unit 9) is quoted from an unnamed "book"; its source was not located.

The Vibhāṣā. The ranking "Śāriputra superior, Maudgalyāyana middling, the rest lesser" (unit 8) was not located in the Mahāvibhāṣā (T1545).

Wisdom-Life. The "new" rendering 具壽 ("endowed with life," āyuṣmat) is Xuanzang's; Zhanran prefers the older 慧命 ("wisdom-life") (unit 11).

The seven kinds of beings in the Ganges. Zhanran's summary (unit 16) condenses the Kāśyapa chapter of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, which is in fascicle thirty-six of the northern text (T374) and thirty-two of the southern (T375); he cites it as fascicle thirty. It is a paraphrase, not a quotation.

The lion's seat. "The Buddha is the lion among humans ." (unit 35) is in the Great Treatise (T1509), fascicle seven.

The jeweled-torch dhāraṇī. The Great Collection (T397) names a dhāraṇī "Jeweled Torch" (寶炬) (unit 27).

The Avataṃsaka. The chapter on the Ocean of the Tathāgata's Marks and the chapter on the Light of the Minor Features (unit 44) are chapters of the eighty-fascicle Avataṃsaka (T279); the sixty-fascicle version (T278) has corresponding chapters.

Rocana and Vairocana. Zhanran writes 舍那 (Rocana), the reward body, for the elder; the site's fascicle-006 has "Vairocana" in two places and "Rocana" in one. Zhanran's own sentences use Rocana; in unit 44, where he describes the Avataṃsaka's 毘盧遮那, the name is Vairocana.

Persuasion and admonition. Units 57–58 line up the pursuit of the destitute son with the two gates of the burning-house parable: in each gate, gauging what is fitting, the absence of capacity, and letting go (or resting the transformation). The site's fascicle-006 attributes the prose application to "Skillful Means"; in context it is the application of the burning-house parable.

Textual difficulties. Some sentences are compressed or possibly corrupt: "Not turning the faculties" (unit 10); "the eighth, the sixth, and so on" (unit 26), which may refer to the eighth-person and sixth grounds of the shared teaching; "the forty-one minds" (unit 27), probably for "forty-one stages"; "views, as they arise, overcome dullness" (unit 34); and the "reckoning and detailed items" (unit 38). They are translated literally.
