Welling Up from the Earth and the Name of the Lifespan Chapter

This part is the middle section of Zhanran's ninth fascicle. It comments on the site's fascicle-009 of the Textual Commentary, from the explanation of the Welling Up chapter to the opening of the Lifespan chapter. Zhanran first reads the five glosses on the Welling Up title through the four siddhāntas, and shows why the bodhisattvas of other lands are stopped while those beneath the earth are summoned. He then takes up the fifty small eons that seemed like half a day, the four leading bodhisattvas, and the ten senses in which the first hearers of the Huayan and the present hearers of the Lotus differ in their conditions while entering the same buddha-wisdom. After Maitreya's doubt and the parable of the young father and the aged son, he turns to the Lifespan chapter. There he reviews the earlier masters (Sengrui, Daolang, Huiguan, an annotator, and Daosheng) and faults them for explaining the lifespan only by the dharma-body. He defends Zhiyi's four readings of "lifespan and measure," the claim that the Lotus teaches permanence, and the explanation of the twofold and threefold Tathāgata.

Explaining the Welling Up Chapter

Chinese Source

先以四悉通釋，於世界中初明命赴之由，次「如來」下正明命赴。「師嚴」等者，二義互明，道在師故道尊，師有道故師嚴，師嚴故命不可違，道尊故有命必赴，由師具二故「鞠躬衹奉」。次正明命赴。所言「命」者，一由〈寶塔品〉末云「佛欲以此妙法華經付屬有在」，此命猶通。二由下文他方菩薩八恒沙眾請他土弘經，佛止之曰：我娑婆世界自有六萬恒河沙眾。即別命也。故經家敘之。諸菩薩聞釋迦牟尼佛所發音聲從下發來，「四方奔踊」者，「奔」疾也，恭之至也。自在升此故是世界，下三共成初感應也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, [the title] is explained in general by the four siddhāntas. Within the mundane [siddhānta], it first makes clear the reason for answering the summons; next, from "at the Tathāgata's single summons" on, it makes clear the answering of the summons proper. As for "The teacher stern" and so on: the two meanings clarify each other. Because the path is in the teacher, the path is revered; because the teacher has the path, the teacher is stern. Because the teacher is stern, his summons cannot be disobeyed; because the path is revered, whenever there is a summons it must be answered. Because the teacher possesses both, "disciples bow low in reverent service." Next, the answering of the summons proper. As for what is called the "summons": first, it derives from the end of the Jeweled Stūpa chapter, which says, "the Buddha wishes to entrust this Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra to one who will carry it onward"; this summons is still general. Second, it derives from the text below, where the bodhisattvas of other directions, an assembly as many as the sands of eight Ganges, ask to propagate the sūtra in this land not their own, and the Buddha stops them, saying: In my own sahā world there are already sixty thousand Ganges-sands [of bodhisattvas]. This is the specific summons. Hence the compilers of the sūtra narrate it. The bodhisattvas, hearing the voice uttered by Śākyamuni Buddha, come forth from below. As for "they come rushing up from every direction": "rushing" (奔) means swift; it is the utmost of reverence. Their rising up here at will is therefore the mundane [siddhānta]; the three below together complete this first appeal and response.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "Across the three times" on is the individual [siddhānta]; it has dharma, metaphor, and application. First, the dharma: from his original attainment on, he has benefited beings through the three ages; hence these three ages all belong to the past. Now the Buddha himself is to benefit beings in the present, and the bodhisattvas' propagation of the sūtra lies for now in the future. Since the benefit of wisdom is so broad, it is not what the mind can fathom. As for raising the metaphor of the moon: the one moon is the origin; the myriad reflections are the traces. As long as there is water, it responds without tiring; how could one conceive of it by the three ages? If one does not brush aside the reflections, how will one know the moon in the sky? This makes clear that the bodhisattvas' real origin is hard to fathom, and silently reveals that the Tathāgata's traces cannot be measured. As for "Summoning the past" and so on: if the trace were not opened, no one from the Buddha downward could know it. Now, wishing to reveal the origin, he first brings out his original retinue, which possesses good roots that grow in two ages; hence he summons the original people to show the present people, causing the present people to produce present good, and makes the original people propagate the present sūtra, causing the people to come to produce future good.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "Empty space" and so on, as the therapeutic [siddhānta]: this is in terms of what is signified. Empty space is the principle; origin and trace are phenomena. Phenomena have origin and trace; the principle has no early or late. The deluded are confused about the principle and in the dark about origin and trace; hence they cling to the near trace and lose the distant origin. If they are confused even about origin and trace, how much more about the inconceivable oneness! Hence the disciples of the origin dwell in the empty space below; the teacher of the original ground passes through the empty space of long ago; the teacher and disciples of today are in today's empty space. Though the space of long ago and the space of today, the space below and the space above, are one in substance, the original disciples have always known the near trace, while today's disciples are still confused about the distant origin. To break the clinging to the near, he summons the past to show the present; today's disciples, through their doubt, make a request, and only on hearing the teaching is [the clinging] broken. Because it breaks the evil of clinging to the near, it is called therapeutic.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "At the quiescent site of awakening" on is the ultimate meaning: at the quiescent site Rocana shows his first attainment, hence "a father seemingly young"; in the quiescent light the bodhisattvas' practice has long been manifest, hence "a child seemingly old." As for the metaphor of "the medicine's own power": why is the father young and the son old? Following the sense of the metaphor of father and son below, the father long ago first took the medicine of the wisdom of all modes that restores youth, so the father is old yet seems young; the son, too, long ago received the method of eternal abiding and deathlessness, so the son is young yet seems old. Though each has the merit of taking medicine, father and son have long been settled. Though these four siddhāntas explain the present text in general, all their meanings also extend to the later chapters: the text of the first siddhānta is in the present chapter; the meaning of the second extends to the later chapters; the third and fourth siddhāntas draw on the later chapters in advance. All help the later [chapters] to accomplish the revealing of the distant, because good arises, evil is broken, and the origin is seen. Hence know that the mundane is the reason for the other three siddhāntas; therefore the Welling Up chapter lies wholly in the mundane.

From "The text itself says" on is the citation of proof, proving all four siddhāntas together: the four siddhāntas are the causes and conditions for which [they] gathered. As for the "and so on" after "the section of the teaching's transmission to the future": it ought to describe all the chapters in full, as discussed in detail below. Further, of the twenty-eight chapters, only eleven and a half are the dissemination of origin and trace; in sixteen and a half chapters, by the power of the sūtra, it greatly cites dharmas and cites persons, cites the present and cites the past, east and west, the revealed and the secret, the general body and particular bodies, the adverse and the compliant, past buddhas and the present Buddha, the subtle of themselves and the manifest of themselves, present benefit and future benefit, benefit to animals, benefit to humans, benefit to men, benefit to women, close benefit and distant benefit, benefit in phenomena and benefit in principle, and so on, praising it without end. Moreover, the text of the four kinds of faith in the half chapter is uncertain as to whether it is included or excluded; how much more do the intents of dissemination in the two places of origin and trace differ! Hence the note "and so on." Therefore the dissemination of the origin gate is forever different from all other collections.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "The bodhisattvas from other lands, hearing that the merit of proclaiming this sūtra is great": having already heard the trace gate's teaching on dissemination, they wished to disseminate it, recruited by the superior merit. As for "the Tathāgata declined their offer": above, he broadly recruited [people] to propagate the sūtra; now those of other directions ask to propagate it; why does he not permit it? Hence it explains this with three meanings: first, since their charges are separate, there is no benefit for the two ages, so there is no mundane benefit; second, as for those of other directions bringing "no great benefit" here, there is no individual [benefit]; the third has two meanings: if the doubt about the trace is not broken, there is no therapeutic [benefit]; if the distant origin is not revealed, there is no ultimate meaning.

Buddhas and bodhisattvas truly have no "this" and "that"; but capacities have their particular place, and none of this is other than naturally so from beginningless time.

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence by means of the second meaning it reveals the first, saying "their karmic connection with this land, formed only shallowly": one first forms a karmic connection with particular buddhas and bodhisattvas, and is ripened again by those same buddhas and bodhisattvas. The meaning of "the intention of equality" is as said earlier. Because of these two meanings it is necessary to summon those below; hence the single summoning of those below also accomplishes three meanings, and this is why he stops the others. Without the three meanings there is no benefit of the four siddhāntas; the three meanings of summoning those below contain the four benefits. First, sons propagate the father's Dharma, so there is the mundane benefit; next, from "their karmic connection is deep and broad" on, the connection is deep and the benefit much, so there is the individual benefit; third, from "and, again, only through them" on, the doubt about the near is broken, so there is the therapeutic benefit, and the distant origin is revealed, so there is the benefit of the ultimate meaning. This is the benefit of summoning the original [disciples] to propagate the sūtra. As for the preceding four siddhāntas, [they apply] only in the mundane and the individual; the severing of doubt is [a matter of] the origin, which is why it is not described there.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "their dwelling place" on explains the original abode. First it gives the name of the land; next it explains it by the four virtues; third, from "this is the secret treasury" on, it concludes the name. The originally existing four virtues are what is relied on; the four virtues attained through cultivation are what relies. With both the relying and the relied-on there is a body that relies, and it relies on the land relied on by both; only when the two meanings are equal is it the mark of Vairocana's body and land. If one says that the dust-mote lands entering one another layer upon layer, containing one another layer upon layer, their phenomena layer upon layer and their teachings layer upon layer, are not yet final, [the answer is that] they reveal the principle through phenomena. If one does not understand this one purport, who would understand that throughout the dharma-realms of the ten directions there is only one buddha, while also allowing other buddhas? If other buddhas are allowed, those others too, in body and land, layer upon layer manifest one another, enter one another, and merge with one another; know that this is simply speaking of pervasion in terms of one. Fourth, from "by means of a dharma that dwells nowhere at all" on, it explains the intent of dwelling.

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, from "As for 'beneath'" on, it explains "beneath" (下方). "The very depth of the abyss of the dharma-nature" explains "below" (下); "the ultimate ground of the mysterious teaching" explains "direction" (方). Because it is the cardinal [aim] of the mysterious principle, it is called "mysterious teaching" (玄宗); 宗 means the cardinal aim, what one expects to take as the fruit. These bodhisattvas have partially reached what they expect, so for now it is called "the ultimate ground." Further, as for the splitting of the earth: the earth covering what originally belongs [to him] is like the trace concealing the origin; now he opens the trace to reveal the origin, hence the splitting of the earth signifies it. Yet these bodhisattvas had traces before this too; though only the origin is revealed, it is not yet manifest in principle. But the disciples being covered amounts in meaning to the teacher being covered; if the disciples are revealed, the teacher is revealed without fail; hence revealing the disciples amounts in meaning to revealing the teacher.

Beneath, in Space, and the Retinues

Chinese Source

言「在下不屬此空中不屬彼」者，在下空故不屬此界，住於空故不屬彼界，以彼表無，以此表有，以空表中。「出此不在上」者非上界也。「不在此下」者，此界在上空之下，此即是下，故云「此下」。復以上界表無，此界表有，空亦表中，故上下二空俱表中理，理即寂光。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "Being beneath does not belong to this world above, and being in the sky does not belong to that world beneath": being in the space below, they do not belong to this world; dwelling in space, they do not belong to that world. "That" signifies non-existence, "this" signifies existence, and space signifies the middle. "Emerging from beneath, they are neither above" means it is not the realm above. As for "nor still beneath" (此下): this world lies beneath the space above; this is "below," hence it says "this below." Again, the realm above signifies non-existence, this world signifies existence, and space too signifies the middle; hence both spaces, above and below, signify the principle of the middle, and the principle is the tranquil light.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "As for the reason for their coming" up to "all exactly as explained above": they come because of hearing the summons and the four phrases that follow; this concludes the meaning of the four siddhāntas in explaining the chapter. It is also the three meanings of stopping those of other directions and summoning those below; hence it says "as above." As for "if one seeks this out through the Dharma-gate": these five characters ought to be attached after "nor the sixty thousand truly many"; [what comes] before "truly many" still belongs to causes and conditions. Yet there is also no need to move them. As for "One is the purity of the single path" and so on: since it speaks of "Dharma-gate," it must extend to cause and fruit. In phenomena one goes from many to few; in the Dharma-gate one goes from few to many, because in meaning one should rely on the principle to give rise to practice. Increasing up to the six perfections, it concludes in sixty thousand to complete the contemplative practice of the perfect [teaching]. Since the practice relies on the wondrous object, the perfections become ten thousand perfections, yet in reality there are not ten thousand: the ten thousand in each of the six is just one ten thousand, with no separate ten thousand; the six are wholly the ten thousand, with no separate six. Every practice of the perfections is the dharma-realm; the realm has no mark of a realm; just a single instant is the single path. Since one and six are so, two, three, four, and five can be seen by analogy. Thus among the six, none is not complete with one, many, and all; know that from the single path to the five eyes, each eye and each truth is complete with ten thousand, none other than the three contemplations contemplating the dharma-realm. Everywhere it speaks of "goodness" (善), because [this] does not extend to delusion. "Many is not truly many" is the empty; "one is not truly few" is the provisional; "neither many nor few, and yet both many and few at once" is the middle. As for "and so on": one must discuss the wondrous object and the wondrous contemplation fully, as above and below, without saying more; these are the virtues of practice of those bodhisattvas.

The Fifty Small Eons and the Fourfold Assembly's Seeing

Chinese Source

「就初三業供養」至「遍見」者，此乃感應道交。於五十劫令如半日，此明如來不可思議延促之事，顯於如來自在之力，則顯無機雖借雖隱而亦不能於長見短於狹見廣。鏡豈惜妍？由形故耳。有人云：聽法之志而忘其長。或云：長短斯亡、長短斯在。此並得感而失應，何以抑於如來神力耶？借（子夜切）。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for from "As to the first, the offering made through the three karmic actions" up to "the fourfold assembly seeing this throughout": this is the mutual resonance between response and appeal. Making fifty eons seem like half a day makes clear the Tathāgata's inconceivable lengthening and shortening and reveals the Tathāgata's sovereign power; it reveals that without the capacity, though he borrows [his power] and conceals, he still could not make them see the short within the long and the broad within the narrow. Does a mirror begrudge beauty? It is because of the form. Someone says: in their resolve to hear the Dharma they forgot how long it was. Translator's notes

Fascicle and site pages. This is the middle section of Zhanran's ninth fascicle. All of its lemmas comment on the site's fascicle-009 of the Textual Commentary: the Welling Up chapter and the first part of the Lifespan chapter (up to the threefold Tathāgata). Inside quoted lemmas "kalpa" is written "eon" and "abode" (住) is written "abiding."

The four siddhāntas. In units 3, 4, and 9 Zhanran assigns the Commentary's five glosses on the chapter title, and its three reasons for stopping the bodhisattvas of other lands, to the mundane, individual, therapeutic, and ultimate-meaning siddhāntas. In unit 4 the Taishō reads 無臣益; it is translated with the Commentary's own wording 必無巨益, "no great benefit," taking 臣 as a copying error for 巨.

Chapter count. Unit 3 says that "only eleven and a half" of the twenty-eight chapters are the dissemination of origin and trace and that in "sixteen and a half" the sūtra praises itself without end. The numbers are translated as they stand; how Zhanran divides them is not clear.

Above and below. In unit 10 Zhanran reads 在下不屬此，空中不屬彼 as "being in the space below, they do not belong to this world; dwelling in space, they do not belong to that [realm above]," with "that" (the realm above) signifying non-existence, "this" (our world) existence, and space the middle. The site's "this world above . that world beneath" is kept inside the quotation.

Weaning and the cold compress. Unit 17 explains 薄須塗熨 by two images: a sick infant weaned for a time with poison smeared on the breast, and an abscess cooled with a compress until the heat subsides. The three middle flavors (cream and the two butters) are the smearing and compressing.

The ten senses. Units 18–28 comment on the Commentary's ten ways the first hearers of the Huayan and the hearers of the Lotus differ in conditions while entering the same buddha-wisdom. The three Huayan chapters that say "first attained right awakening" (unit 19) are the chapter on the World-Rulers, the chapter on Names, and the chapter on the Ten Concentrations of the eighty-fascicle Avataṃsaka (T279); the sixty-fascicle version (T278) opens with the same words.

Eight thousand, not eighty thousand. In unit 34 Zhanran notes that the Commentary's "Maitreya, together with eighty thousand great masters" should follow the sūtra, which has "Maitreya Bodhisattva and the bodhisattvas, as many as the sands of eight thousand Ganges" (T262).

Persons in the Lifespan chapter. 叡師 is Sengrui; 河西 is Daolang of Hexi; 道場觀 is Huiguan of Daochang Temple (the site renders it as a book title, "The Daochang Contemplation"); 注家 is "the annotator," unnamed; 生公 is Daosheng. 光宅 is Fayun of Guangzhai Temple.

期頤 and 脩夭. In unit 47 Zhanran explains 期頤 as in the Book of Rites ("a hundred years is called 期頤"): a person of a hundred years must "require nourishment" from filial children. He reads 脩夭 as "living long and dying young." The site's English ("a hundred years as the fixed span of a lifetime," "beyond the bounds of cultivation") is kept in the quotations.

Snipe and clam. Zhanran ascribes the story to the Spring and Autumn; it is told in the Intrigues of the Warring States (Yan section), where Su Dai warns Zhao that while it fights Yan, mighty Qin will take both. He refers to his own notes on fascicle five of Calming and Contemplation for it.

The Golden Light passage. Unit 49 quotes the Lifespan chapter of the Golden Light Sūtra: Ruciraketu's question why Śākyamuni lived only eighty years, and the verse of the four buddhas. The Taishō text writes the bodhisattva's name 相信 for 信相. The wording is closest to the composite edition (T664).

Nirvāṇa quotations. "How does one attain long life?" (unit 34) is Kāśyapa's verse question in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T374); "because all beings are of the one vehicle" (unit 50) is also in T374. "Only a buddha and a buddha have an immeasurable lifespan; because it is immeasurable, it is permanent" was not located in this wording. The chapter on the Deviant and the True (unit 60) is fascicle seven of the southern edition (T375).

Lotus quotations. "The Buddha wishes to entrust this Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra to one who will carry it onward" (Jeweled Stūpa), "sixty thousand Ganges-sands," and "the pure land is never destroyed, yet the multitude see it burned" follow the site's Lotus pages.

Uncertain readings. In unit 47, "the first explanation [concerns] only the five phrases within the first" and "what of the snapping of the fingers?" are translated literally; the second may refer to the Lotus's statement that even small acts lead to buddhahood.
