The Three Bodies, the Six Clauses, and the Lifespan Not Exhausted

This part is the lower section of Zhanran's ninth fascicle and completes his notes on the Lifespan chapter as explained in the site's fascicle-009 of the Textual Commentary. It opens with the response-body Tathāgata, the names Vairocana, Rocana, and Śākyamuni, and the merging of the three bodies, then follows the Commentary's seven steps on "lifespan and measure": three propositions, the four propositions of permanent and impermanent, the merging, following conditions, the emphasis on the reward body, origin and trace, and a question on why a sūtra with few lists of stages can be called unsurpassed. Zhanran sets out his own outline of the chapter's answer, then comments phrase by phrase on the dust-mote worlds, the dispelling of the doubt about Dīpaṃkara, the faculties seen by the buddha eye, those who "delight in the lesser," and the two responses with their births of body and dharma. A long section explains the six clauses on how the Tathāgata sees the three realms, warning against concluding double negations as partial propositions. The part ends with nature, desire, conduct, and thought, and with the refutation of those who read the lifespan as only prolonged and impermanent.

In the first text, it first makes clear the reason for responding: because wisdom accords with the object, he shows a body able to teach; teaching is response. The reward [of self-enjoyment] has no teaching; hence it says "because the objective realm of thusness and the wisdom of thusness are joined together." As for its saying only "thus" (以如) and not "the thusness of dharmas" (法如): since "dharma" belongs to object and wisdom, using this object and wisdom one can give rise to response; since understanding belongs to wisdom, it is matched specifically to the reward body. The response is not without wisdom, but for now, matching capacities, its function is compared to teaching; hence the [body of] other-enjoyment can be called reward and can also be called response, because superior and inferior are both called response. Hence know that the great master used the Treatise's text skillfully, wondrous to this degree.

Next, the translation of the names contains the three bodies. Translators of recent times do not separate dharma and reward and cannot tell three from two. Since ancient times, sūtras and treatises have allowed three bodies. If one says that Vairocana and Rocana are not distinct, then the dharma-body is the reward body; if they are identical, all beings would be nothing but perfect and full; if the dharma-body teaches, beings would teach too. If one teaches only when the fruit is full, the fullness is established from the reward. If one says they are inseparable, all three bodies are so; why only dharma and reward? Beings and buddhas are not two; why only the three bodies? Hence one keeps the three bodies: the dharma[-body] definitely does not teach; the reward has both meanings; the response and transformation definitely teach. If they are mutually identical, all teach and all do not teach; if one goes only by principle, it is neither teaching nor not teaching; with phenomena and principle set against each other, not teaching is teaching and teaching is not teaching. When feelings are penetrated and one accords wondrously, the disputed reckonings are all lost.

Translator's notes

Fascicle and site pages. This is the lower section of Zhanran's ninth fascicle. All its lemmas comment on the Lifespan chapter in the site's fascicle-009 of the Textual Commentary, from the response-body Tathāgata to the passage on the lifespan "not yet exhausted." Inside quoted lemmas "kalpa" is written "eon" and "abode" (住) "abiding."

Vairocana and Rocana. In unit 2 Zhanran rejects recent translators who make Vairocana (the dharma-body) and Rocana (the reward body) the same. The site's Textual Commentary spells 盧舍那 "Locana"; Zhanran's own sentences use "Rocana."

The Tiantai commentary on the precepts. "The flower-dais and the flower-petals are the distinction of origin and trace" (unit 2) refers to the Commentary on the Meaning of the Bodhisattva Precepts (T1811), which reads Rocana on the dais as the original body and Śākyamuni on the petals as the trace buddha.

Huayan quotations. "The dharma-realm is not finite, nor is it infinite" (unit 10) was not located in the Taishō Avataṃsaka; Yanshou's Zongjing lu (T2016) quotes it as a sūtra verse. The passage on the four continents and ten thousand names (unit 39) is from the chapter on Names. The words that Vajragarbha used to stop Vimukticandra (unit 42) are in the Ten Grounds chapter of the sixty-fascicle Avataṃsaka (T278).

The box and its lid. In unit 11 Zhanran explains 函大蓋大 as "the box is large, the lid is large": the box is the object, the lid is wisdom. The site renders it "the capacity and what it covers are equally vast." In the same unit, the four phrases of the Lotus verse are matched to the substance, cause, function, and power of the reward body in the order of the Commentary's Chinese; the site's English gives the phrases in a different order.

The ten kinds of the unsurpassed. The list in unit 26 is from the Treatise on the Lotus (T1520). Its third item is the parable of the caravan leader (the phantom city), and its seventh "transforming beings," which Zhanran abbreviates as 化生.

The strap of a clog. In unit 30 Zhanran glosses 蒵 in 喉襟目蒵 as the strap of a clog (屐之蒵), citing the Fengsu tong. The site's "the throat, the collar, the eye, and the sash" is kept inside the quotation.

The missing phrase. The story of Tanfu (unit 34), who dreamed that a phrase was missing from Kumārajīva's text, was not located in the Biographies of Eminent Monks (T2059).

Cunda. The Buddha asking Cunda whether his preceptor Śāriputra's body of precepts and the rest had perished (unit 48) refers to the Āgama account of Śāriputra's death; it was not located in this wording.

The five thousand. In unit 48 the Taishō reads 若五十起去; it is read as 五千起去, the five thousand who rose and left the assembly in the Skillful Means chapter.

The Treatise on the six clauses. The four quotations in unit 49 are from the Treatise on the Lotus (T1520), which reads 常恒清涼 where Zhanran has 常恒清淨.

Double negation and concluding clauses. Units 50–51 explain why the sūtra's double negations ("neither real nor unreal," and so on) must not be "concluded" in one extreme, using Zhiyi's Commentary on the Vimalakīrti on Vimalakīrti's rebuke of Kātyāyana. The site's Vimalakīrti has the passage in the Disciples chapter ("the five aggregates are thoroughly understood as empty and without arising: this is the meaning of suffering").

Nature and desire. The Nirvāṇa passage on body and afflictions arising "without before and after" (unit 53) was not located in this wording. The Calming and Contemplation passage on the dhyāna sūtras and the Great Treatise is in its first fascicle.

Uncertain readings. In unit 42, 相聖 is read as 相望, "set against each other." In unit 60 one character is missing in the Taishō (對□人財) and is marked as missing. In unit 40, "it simply sets the distinct alongside its negation" is a literal rendering of 秖是並別及非.
