The Five Turbidities, the Five Thousand, and the Small Goods of Humans and Gods

This part completes Zhanran's notes on the prose of the Skillful Means chapter and follows the Textual Commentary well into the verses. He first lays out the many "gates" by which the commentary sorts the five turbidities: cause and fruit, person and dharma, the three obstructions, the provisional designations of the Middle Treatise and the Great Treatise, ordinary and sage, long and short, and inner and outer. On the passage that distinguishes the false to press home the true, he argues against Master Yao and another interpreter that the arhats who "encounter another buddha" after the Buddha's extinction are true arhats reborn in a land beyond the three realms. In the verses he explains the three failings of the five thousand who withdrew, the commentary's comparison of verse and prose, and the sentient beings of the Sūtra of the True Dharma's Mindfulness. The longest section concerns the opening of the vehicles of humans and gods: against the Dilun reading of the child playing with sand he insists that the smallest good becomes the conditioning cause of buddhahood, and he adds material on stūpas, images, the Upāsaka-śīla Sūtra, music, and the word "namo." The part ends with the buddha-seed that arises from conditions and the dharma that abides in its rank.

The Five Turbidities by the Four Siddhāntas and Other Gates

Chinese Source

次「約五濁論四悉」中劫命對世界者，劫命秖是依正二報即世界也。眾生即是所為之人。見是能計見者，與眾生同。由此二故有所為機，則見滅善生。煩惱是對治者，如五停中具治三毒，正用小乘能治濁，故屬第三悉。亦準論意以第一義用對衍門。此四悉門通酬五濁障大不障小之問也。以四悉文所被多，故著料簡門首。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, in "Considering the five turbidities in relation to the four siddhāntas," that the eon and lifespan correspond to the mundane [siddhānta]: the eon and lifespan are simply the two rewards, dependent and principal, that is, the world. Sentient beings are the persons for whom [it is done]. Views: the one who reckons views is the same as sentient beings. Because of these two there is the capacity for which [it is done], so that views are extinguished and good arises. That affliction corresponds to the therapeutic: as in the five [methods of] stilling [the mind], which fully treat the three poisons, it properly uses the Lesser Vehicle that can treat the turbidities, hence it belongs to the third siddhānta. Also, following the meaning of the treatise, it matches the ultimate meaning with the gate of the Mahāyāna. This gate of the four siddhāntas answers in general the question of the five turbidities obstructing the great and not the lesser. Because what the text on the four siddhāntas covers is much, it is placed at the head of the gate of analysis.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "If one considers this in terms of cause and fruit" on, it further distinguishes the five turbidities by many gates. The first is the gate of cause and fruit. The two causes are affliction and views; the other three are fruits, the meaning including dependent and principal. Next, from "one person" on, the gate of person and dharma: the person is sentient beings, the dharmas are the other four; set against beings they all become dharmas, all being what beings reckon. From "the four dharmas" on, the gate of dharma and time: it should say "the four dharmas, one time"; the text is abbreviated. The time is the eon; the other four belong to dharma; if spoken against time, beings too belong to dharma. From "the two obstructions of recompense" on, the gate of the three obstructions. The two rewards are sentient beings and lifespan. As for "the two obstructions of affliction": because views too can in general be called afflictions. As for "with karma lying in between": affliction moistens karma and karma can bring recompense, hence it says "in between." From "Sentient beings" on, the gate of the three provisional designations of the Middle Treatise. As for "a provisional designation of mutual dependence may likewise be understood": these are the eon, affliction, and views. The eon is long and short in mutual dependence; affliction is adverse and agreeable and so on in mutual dependence; views are existence and nonexistence and so on in mutual dependence. Speaking generally, each contains the three provisional designations; the two rewards, dependent and principal, are without exception formed by the three provisional designations; now, following distinction, it speaks of them separately for the present, hence it matches them separately. From "Sentient beings" on, the gate of the three provisional designations set up in the Great Treatise: beings in combination receive the name "being"; lifespan and the rest are all dharmas reckoned by beings. Again, there is the provisional name of what is reckoned, set against the name "being"; hence it says "the provisional designation of name applies to both." A detailed explanation is fully as in my notes on fascicle five of Calming and Contemplation.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "Affliction" on, the gate of ordinary and sage. "Affliction . belong[s] to ordinary beings": meaning that ordinary beings definitely have them. Yet views are necessarily in ordinary beings, while affliction extends to sages, such as sages fully bound and those on the way to arhatship. As for "the remaining three turbidities apply to both": in a decreasing eon there are buddhas, in an increasing eon only ordinary beings. Again, in decrease there are buddhas, while increase extends to the other sages. Again, increase and decrease each extend to having and not having [buddhas], because the great and lesser vehicles differ. Does the pure and full Lotus Treasury [World] wait for a decreasing eon? "I dwell always on Vulture Peak" means just this. The name "sentient beings" extends fully to ordinary and sage; lifespan [too], the recompense-lifespans of ordinary and sage differing. From "Lifespan is short" on, the gate of long and short: because within one eon one is born again and again, long and short differ. This is spoken of humans and the desire gods, the places where buddhas are seen; if one discusses the first dhyāna, then eon and lifespan are equal; from the second dhyāna on, the eon is short and lifespan long; up to the formless [realm] it can be understood by analogy. As for "the remaining three apply to both long and short": affliction is long among ordinary beings and short among sages; views are long among other ordinary beings and short among non-Buddhists of sharp faculties; or short among other ordinary beings and long among non-Buddhists of dull faculties; or short among the lesser, because cut off in one life, and long among the great, because they retain delusion to moisten birth and enter birth and death. Again, views ending first is short, and affliction ending later is long. Now, distinguishing, [those of] the turbidities who enter birth and death [by intention] are not called turbid; sentient beings are spoken of likewise.

The others all belong to dharma and are not unbound from time; not being directly bound up, they lie only in dharma. From "The eon extends" on, the gate of inner and outer: the great eon harms the vessel [world], hence it is called outer; the lesser eon harms persons, hence it is called inner. Or it may be that when the four turbidities gather, the time extends to both inner and outer. From "The three lesser" on, the gate of harming or not: "things" are the outer vessel; when the great eon arises, persons have already been born above, according to the three [calamities] born in different places up to the third dhyāna. From "The lesser eon" on, the gate of the five destinies and three realms: by rights they should be discussed separately, but three and five intermingle, hence they are made clear together. "Extends to the form realm" is as before. Distinguishing the False to Press Home the True

Chinese Source

言「正三毒」者，他方淨土如阿閦國，亦有女人無邪欲故，舉一準餘諸可例識，廣歷諸土分別不同，故注「云云」。「敦真」者，敦實得者故斥言非，上慢亦然，終無實得而不信者。「嗣」亦繼也。「身尚無量」者，小乘教中不說更有界外生處，有計變易在界內者，亦名生處；大乘教中既其變易生處多故，故云無量。此述逼意，言中但云「名增上慢」，意指羅漢。若實得者豈有不發大心者耶？乃以大乘密而斥之。若謂究竟應變易盡，若未盡者何以不信？

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "the correct forms of the three poisons": in the pure lands of other regions, such as the land of Akṣobhya, there are also women, because there is no deviant desire; raising one, the rest can be recognized by analogy; going broadly through the lands, the distinctions differ, hence the note "(and so on)." As for "to press home the true": it presses those who have truly attained, hence it rebukes [the others] as wrong; the overweening likewise; in the end there is no one who has truly attained and does not believe. "Inheriting" (嗣) also means continuing. As for "your bodies still have no measure": in the Lesser Vehicle teaching it is not said that there are further places of birth beyond the realms; some reckon transformational [birth and death] as within the realms, and also call it a place of birth; in the Mahāyāna teaching, since the places of transformational birth are many, it says "no measure." This states the intent of pressing; the words only say "overweening pride," but the intent points to arhats. If they had truly attained, how could any fail to arouse the great resolve? Thus it censures them covertly with the Mahāyāna. If it is said that they are ultimate, the transformational [birth] should be exhausted; if it is not exhausted, why do they not believe?

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "encountering another buddha": the first passage takes the buddha of the land with remainder as "another buddha." As for "An arhat still bears the body of his former life": because it requites former karma it is called the body of the former life. Because it is the fruit of affliction, it must perish. If what is deluded by affliction could avoid perishing, there would be the fault of cause and fruit not matching. Even if it is said that the power of the samādhi of the furthest limit sustains it so that it does not perish, I now ask: is the samādhi of the furthest limit great or lesser? If lesser, the lesser has neither the name of transformational [birth] nor the doctrine of eternal permanence. If great, in the great the name "furthest limit" applies only to equal awakening; how could one be at equal awakening right after the prediction? If the great enters the lesser samādhi, it shows that the great has no use for samādhi. Some cite Rāhula and others; this should not be so. Only after the Buddha's extinction the arhats were hidden or manifest differently, all according to capacity and conditions, let alone those who were originally great bodhisattvas. This is Nāgārjuna Bodhisattva in the Great Treatise posing this question hypothetically, wishing to answer it fully according to the great teaching. Hence the answer says, "he is never again born within the threefold world," and moreover the treatise in turn cites the Lotus as proof. Hence we know that the words "ordinary beings" have no teaching to rely on, and the meaning of "another land" comes from another [author]. Hence the ancients said, "If learning does not take [Dao]an as its teacher, its meaning will not withstand challenge." Hence this Lotus is not understood without this. Again, if transformational [birth] did not go beyond the realms, then the words "born in the five destinies," the teaching of re-entering birth and death, the parable of entering the burning house in alarm, and the text [saying it is] "not nirvāṇa" for a single day would all be fabrications. From "Master Nanyue says" on, three explanations, all of them Nanyue's explanations.

次釋中云「羅漢若修念佛三昧」等者，若言念十方佛則已發大心，若唯念釋尊乃因小感大，亦是機發使之然耳。然小乘之中諸部不同，亦有信有十方佛者，即小乘人修第四禪邊際定力，見十方佛等，念佛觀成感佛為說。第三舉凡夫者，舉凡夫況聖耳。是故羅漢必無不聞之理。瑤師意云：實得羅漢無不信者，不須餘佛；遇餘佛者，則指凡夫。南岳豈不知凡夫於佛滅後能生信耶？故值餘佛必指羅漢。故《淨名》云：「凡夫有反復而聲聞無也」。故知聲聞難迴而須商議，凡夫易受何須引例？

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, in the explanation it says "an arhat who cultivates the samādhi of buddha-recollection" and so on: if [the arhat] recollects the buddhas of the ten directions, he has already aroused the great resolve; if he recollects only Śākyamuni, he moves the great through the lesser, and this too is the arising of capacity that makes it so. Yet within the Lesser Vehicle the schools differ, and some also believe that there are buddhas of the ten directions; that is, persons of the Lesser Vehicle cultivate the power of the samādhi of the furthest limit of the fourth dhyāna and see the buddhas of the ten directions and so on; when recollection of the Buddha is accomplished they move the Buddha to preach for them. Third, raising ordinary beings: raising ordinary beings to imply a fortiori the sages. Therefore there is certainly no way for the arhat not to hear. Master Yao's meaning is: a true arhat believes without exception and does not need another buddha; "encountering another buddha" points to ordinary beings. Would Nanyue not know that ordinary beings can give rise to faith after the Buddha's extinction? Hence encountering another buddha must point to arhats. Hence the Vimalakīrti says, "an ordinary, unenlightened being can still turn back toward the buddha-dharma, but a śrāvaka cannot." Hence we know that śrāvakas are hard to turn and need deliberation; ordinary beings accept easily, so why need they be cited as an example?

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "There are those who say" up to "must necessarily be so": the intent is to refute Master Yao, establishing the meaning of those who do not believe, which is also not so. A firmly fixed grasping mind must belong to the former teachings; it should not prove the text about after the extinction now. This master also cites Śāriputra's [response] on first hearing the brief opening in the Lotus to prove the words that arhats after the extinction do not accept. His meaning is: arhats who meet the Buddha still suspect a demon; how much less would they believe and accept after the Buddha's extinction? Now it is not so. The Buddha pressed them beforehand so that none would fail to believe; hence after the extinction clinging to the provisional teachings of the sūtras may in meaning be allowed. "And so on" (云云): one should explain at length the intent of belief and disbelief of arhats and of ordinary beings, to refute this master and Master Yao. The nine characters "This is simply a differing interpretation" and so on should perhaps be written as main text. They indicate that the two masters before are not wholly wrong, but are not wholly in accord with the sūtra's intent; not being able to refute them wholly, it says "differing interpretation." As for Master Yao, his view goes against the sūtra's text: the sūtra [says] that an arhat encountering another buddha gives rise to faith, yet he says it is not needed. The sūtra's intent in saying "except after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa" is to show the power of the four reliances to propagate the sūtra; but Yao does not take the four reliances as "another buddha," saying that arhats of the three faculties, whether they hear or not, do not depend on hearing from the four reliances; this is deeply wrong. Again, using ordinary beings for an a fortiori is acceptable, only one must say that they meet the four reliances. The later master's intent is: ordinary beings are easy and arhats difficult. This too goes against the sūtra's words of resolution, hence it says "does not adopt it."

The Verses: The Three Failings of the Five Thousand

Chinese Source

次釋偈頌。上慢等三四眾通有者，又凡夫上慢有五不同，謂四善根及以四果，通義易知且後別說從多分故，故經云「比丘等四有此三失」，於三別中仍從拂席者說，故云也。「藏玼」等者，釋三失也。「藏玼揚德」釋上慢，「不能自省」釋我慢，「無慚人者」釋不信，若自見過無此三失，雖未證果且名有羞。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the verses. That overweening pride and the other two are found in common among the fourfold assembly: again, the overweening pride of ordinary beings has five kinds, namely [claiming] the four roots of goodness and the four fruits. The common meaning is easy to know; for now it speaks of them separately afterward, following the majority; hence the sūtra [means that] monks and the other three have these three failings; and among the three separate [failings] it still speaks of those who swept from their seats, hence it says [so]. As for "concealing their blemishes" and so on: explaining the three failings. "Concealing their blemishes while flaunting their supposed virtues" explains overweening pride; "unable to examine themselves" explains self-conceit; "persons without shame" explains lack of faith.
