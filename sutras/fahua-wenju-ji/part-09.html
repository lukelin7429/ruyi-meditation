The Three Explanations of "Skillful Means" and the Refutation of Earlier Interpretations

With this part Zhanran reaches the Skillful Means chapter. He follows the Textual Commentary's three explanations of the chapter title: "skillful means" as dharma and function, as a gate, and as the secret and sublime. The first two describe skillful means external to the essence, the expedient teachings of the past; only the third, in which the provisional is identical with the real, is the intent of this chapter. He then works through the critical sifting by the three kinds of speech, by what is able to enter and what is entered, and by the four propositions on skillful means and the provisional, and criticizes Master Daosheng for saying the chapter is titled from the past. The longest passages answer earlier interpreters: the five-period schemes of the provisional and the real, glosses of the word "provisional" as temporary, borrowed, or a steelyard's weight, and above all the "three turnings" of the two truths that Zhanran traces to Jiaxiang (Jizang), whose four propositions on the vehicle and three wheels of the Dharma he questions point by point. The part ends with the four propositions that all dharmas are provisional, real, both, or neither, the reason the chapter is named from half of the third proposition, and the eight gates and ten pairs that follow.

Explaining the Skillful Means Chapter

Chinese Source

《正法華》題稱「善權品」，及至釋文皆云「善權方便」，當知法護亦以善權而釋方便，善是巧之異名耳。文自分二，初略中自二，初正釋中文自為三，初一從字訓，後二從意義。又初二從昔教，後一屬今經。雖有三釋，並以三教而為方便，但有能通非能通及以即不即異，致成三釋，然須略譚三種大旨，方可消文。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Zhengfahua titles it "Chapter of Skillful Expedients" (善權品), and when it comes to explaining the text it always says "skillful expedients and skillful means" (善權方便). One should know that Dharmarakṣa too explained "skillful means" by "skillful expedient"; "skillful" (善) is simply another name for "adept" (巧). The text naturally divides into two. The first, the brief treatment, is itself in two. In the first, the explanation proper, the text is in three: the first follows the gloss of the characters, the latter two follow the sense. Again, the first two follow the teachings of the past, and the last belongs to the present sūtra. Though there are three explanations, all take the three teachings as skillful means; only, there are the differences of being able to lead through or not able to lead through, and of being identical or not identical, and these produce the three explanations. One must first briefly discuss the main purport of the three before one can digest the text.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the three, the first takes its name from the three teachings as what employs: dharma is what is employed, function is what employs. Though dharma and function both run through the four teachings, there is the distinction of the square and the round, of differentiation and convergence; hence the name "skillful means" is set up from the provisional. Since the provisional is not identical with the real, it is discussed with reference to the past; it forms the provisional external to the essence and is not the intent of the present chapter. That the text raises the perfect, which then belongs to the real, is only by way of contrast. Hence one knows that in the past one should not explain "skillful means" by "secret" and "sublime"; rather, it was kept secret and not spoken, and that is called skillful means. Moreover, the perfect in the past was a perfect that was combined and accompanied; therefore both the partial and the perfect are not the present intent. Next, in the second explanation the provisional belongs to the three teachings that are able to lead through, and these too may be called skillful means. Though they are not identical [with the real], because they can serve as a distant expression for the perfect, and because the perfect that is expressed also carries what expresses it as skillful means, one knows that this too is not the intent of the present chapter. The reason the former explanation does not call the three "what is able [to lead through]" is that the provisional and the real, in meeting [beings], each reach their own extreme, and so the methods differ. Only with the third explanation are the three provisional [teachings] identical with the one real; pointing to this provisional that is identical with the real is what is called the skillful means of the present sūtra.

Next, digesting the text. The first, in terms of dharma and function, is in five: first the dharma; next, the simile; third, making clear the intent of using the provisional; fourth, citing proof; fifth, from "This meaning" on, the conclusion that rejects it. The first is again in three. First, the explanation of the gloss. As for ""Fang" means dharma": the Shuowen says, "Dharma (法) is technique (術)." This fits the present text exactly. The Erya says, "It is a rule (則)," that is, the rule of the Legalists. It also says, "It is the correct (正)." The present [text] is likewise: because its dharma is correct, it is able to meet capacities; though not yet opened and revealed, it cannot but be correct.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, from "Dharma has" on, the explanation of the meaning. Though both are dharma and function, when the partial is viewed against the perfect, the partial is differentiation and the perfect convergence; though convergence surpasses differentiation, convergence is not [yet] free of differentiation, and the function that appears is not the wondrous. Third, from "The three expedient vehicles" on, the explanation of the marks, again in two. First, the explanation of the dharma as square and round: by matching them with the carpenter's square and the compass it divides the partial and the perfect; the function that appears is not the wondrous. As for "three square and one round": though all four teachings are called dharma and function, it is properly the partial dharma that is named skillful means. Next, from "If wisdom" on, the explanation of function as differentiation and convergence. The reason it sets out the words "dharma" and "function" for both is that each fits capacities. Since not both converge on the perfect, it says "meet" (逗) of both. Again, as for "dharma and function": though the name "dharma" is general, since the function suits the occasion it is not yet purely one; with what dharma does it meet what kind of capacity? Hence the four dharmas respond to capacities with unequal differentiation and convergence; the provisional and the real are mutually dependent, and right and wrong are both not [final]. As in the preceding explanation of dharma, the intent is not yet fused; in meeting beings it is not yet unimpeded, and the square and the compass are still separate; for the time being it is called "skillfully uses." "Arrives" (詣) means where one goes, and this is precisely function. As wisdom arrives differently, the use of dharma is unequal.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the intent of the simile: it is a simile precisely for the difference of dharma and function. There are two layers of dharma and simile. Here it sets aside the name "dharma" for the perfect and borrows for the time being the title "secret" and "sublime"; hence even the perfect that is separated from the partial has skillful means internal to the essence, and so it is named secret and sublime. The name "secret and sublime" looks the same as in the third [explanation], but its intent is different. Why? The third takes opening and revealing as the sublime, while here the perfect alone is taken as the sublime. Hence in this passage all four are methods, and the first three teachings are called only methods, neither secret nor sublime, while the later perfect teaching is secret and sublime; hence the later teaching receives the name secret and sublime, which has nothing to do with opening and revealing. Hence using the partial dharma is like using one finger to point partially to one direction; using the perfect dharma is like using five fingers to show all directions everywhere.

Third, making clear the intent of using the provisional: before [the Lotus], when there is not yet convergence, the provisional dharma is called following desires. Fourth, citing proof: one should take the three provisional [teachings] as the dharmas that lead out; since the text relies on the Lesser [Vehicle] teaching it says "the three realms." Moreover, though what is left behind differs, the three realms are no different. If, in the Tathāgata, skillful means is originally one, [but] this meaning has not yet been proclaimed, it belongs to the past teachings. Though it is skillful means external to the essence, in principle it is never anything but internal to the essence; yet beings do not yet know this, and it is spoken according to the Buddha's intent; hence it says "praises 'skillful means.'" Because it is not yet opened, it is not the intent of the present chapter.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the second explanation, in terms of what expresses. If the principle and the teaching are viewed against each other, then in each of the four teachings there is nothing that is not what expresses. Now, viewing the three against the one, the three serve as expression for the one real, and so the three are called what expresses; thus the teaching, practice, person, and principle of the first three teachings are all what expresses. This is in seven. First, it directly sets up the three teachings as gates; this explains from the meaning and has nothing to do with the gloss of the characters. Next, from "a gate is so called" on, the explanation of the meaning of gate: just as a worldly gate is originally what leads through, all three enter the real and so are named gates. Third, from "Skillful means, expedients" on, making clear the intent of the gate: beings do not understand that this is originally what is led through to; relying on what they cling to, it becomes "a beckoning and a leading" (弄引). Only one who is not skilled in music takes the lead-in (引) for the tune (弄). Fourth, from "the real and true is made manifest" on, making clear the function of the gate: though it is not identical with what is reached, entry is gained through it. Fifth, from "taking its name from its ability" on, making clear how it gets its name: the provisional and the real are still separated, because the capacities of beings differ; hence the first two explanations, on the side of the explicit [teaching] and [in the distinct teaching] before the grounds, are not the intent of the present chapter. Sixth, the intent of the citation of proof: it makes clear that the gate of the past was called only what leads through, and in the present must be opened; hence it says "opening the gate of skillful means." It does not mean that the opening of the gate was already made clear there. Seventh, from "This meaning" on, the conclusion that rejects it.

Ru-Yi Translation

The third explanation is the intent of the present chapter. Only, in the first two explanations the past received merely the name "partial" and the name "gate," kept secret and not spoken. Now the partial gate is opened, and it is itself where the perfect is reached; hence it says "the secret" and "the sublime." Because it is explicit and luminous, it says "the true secret." This is again in six. First, the direct setting up: in the past it was secret; that secret is opened, and in the present it becomes the sublime. Next, from "the sublime penetration" on, the explanation of the efficacy: "penetration" is opening, the convenience (便) of using the sublime; because it opens the secret method (方), and outside the sublime there is no dharma, it says "is itself." Third, from "like the priceless pearl sewn inside the garment" on, the showing of the marks in terms of persons and teachings: in the bodies of beings there are seeds and conditions from the past, called the pearl in the garment. Ever since that drunken guest withdrew, he did not even have the name of the partial gate, let alone the perfect that is reached. If it is not opened, the three provisional [teachings] are not yet revealed, like the garment covering the pearl; the present sūtra opens it, and it is one with the wisdom of the fruit. The hired laborer is likewise; reflect on it and it can be understood. Fourth, from "words such as these" on, the conclusion of the name: the method and the gate are themselves the secret and the sublime, and so it says "such as these." Fifth, from "as the sūtra says" on, citing proof. Sixth, from "Hence it is that" on, the concluding name, showing that this is right.

The Critical Sifting

Chinese Source

次料簡中三：初約自他三語，寄前初釋以簡於三，故初被開即第三也。次約能所寄第二釋，以簡三文，故第二被開亦即第三。三約四句共簡三釋。初釋者，自有三文，簡初文者，三教一向名他名權，權隔實故。釋次文者，以三教之他與圓自對辨。釋第三文者，三俱體內無非真實，但名為自自外無他，三語亦然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the critical sifting, in three. First, in terms of the three kinds of speech of self and other: relying on the first explanation above, it sifts the three; hence the first, once opened, is the third. Next, in terms of what is able and what is entered, relying on the second explanation, it sifts the three passages; hence the second, once opened, is likewise the third. Third, in terms of the four propositions, it sifts the three explanations together. As for the first, it has three passages of its own. Sifting the first passage: the three teachings are entirely called "other" and "provisional," because the provisional is separated from the real. Explaining the next passage: the "other" of the three teachings is set against the "self" of the perfect. Explaining the third passage: all three are internal to the essence and nothing but the real and true; it is simply called "self," and outside self there is no other. The three kinds of speech are likewise.

次約能所者，亦自有三文，並將能所兩字以簡能所是非。釋初文者，且指三教，但是法用尚非能通，況是所通？是故三教非能非所，故三方便悉皆為麁。釋次意者，門是三教得是能通，不云三教即是圓故，故非所通，既其不即故猶是麁。釋第三文者，亦開前二，非能非所及以能通並開成所，所中善巧名為方便，故妙方便異於方法及能通門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, in terms of what is able and what is entered: this too has three passages of its own, and all of them use the two words "able" and "entered" to sift right and wrong in what is able and what is entered. Explaining the first passage: it points for the time being to the three teachings; being only dharma and function, they are not even what is able to lead through, let alone what is led through to. Therefore the three teachings are neither what is able nor what is entered, and so the three skillful means are all coarse. Explaining the next meaning: the gate is the three teachings and is gained as what is able to lead through; since it does not say that the three teachings are themselves the perfect, it is not what is led through to; since they are not identical, it is still coarse. Explaining the third passage: it also opens the first two; what is neither able nor entered, and what is able to lead through, are both opened to become what is entered. The adeptness within what is entered is called skillful means; hence wondrous skillful means differs from method and from the gate that is able to lead through.

「故知」下結斥，先結名體若同若異，雖俱名方便有此三異，豈但聞名便解其義？故三釋皆有方便之言，二非今品，故云「義異」。「世人」下總斥世人，豈大師帶偏情有阿黨耶？其如理何？其如文何？其如行何？其如證何？生公天真獨秀，尚云「從昔題品」，若從昔題，何故稱歎？若稱歎昔，豈非毀今？若以昔歎今又失於昔，則使體內之權全闕，體外之用又虧，乃成內外俱亡，安得歎佛權智？五佛開顯便濫初施，三周善巧仍為徒設，若言品雖題昔品內在今，縣額牓州惑亂行者。又初釋中，既以隨他等三通後二釋，次釋復以門通初後，理應亦以妙通前二，文無者略，但注「云云」，應云初釋方便是祕而非妙，次釋方便祕堪入妙，後釋方便祕即是妙，此乃從佛內解以說，今成顯露故闕不論。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "Hence one should know" on, the concluding censure. First it concludes [whether] name and substance are the same or different: though all are called skillful means, there are these three differences; how could one understand the meaning merely on hearing the name? Hence all three explanations have the words "skillful means," and two are not [the intent of] the present chapter; hence it says "their meanings differ." From "people of the world" on, the general censure of the people of the world. Would the Great Master carry a partial sentiment and be partisan? What of the principle? What of the text? What of practice? What of realization? Master [Dao]sheng, uniquely brilliant by heavenly endowment, still said, "The chapter is titled from the past." If it is titled from the past, why does it praise [skillful means]? If it praises the past, is that not to disparage the present? If one praises the present by means of the past, one also loses the past. Then the provisional internal to the essence would be entirely lacking, and the function external to the essence would also be deficient, so that inner and outer would both be lost; how then could one praise the Buddha's provisional wisdom? The opening and revealing of the five buddhas would then be confused with the first setting out, and the adeptness of the three rounds would be set up in vain. If one says that the chapter, though titled from the past, has its content in the present, that is hanging a county's plaque on a prefecture and confusing practitioners. Again, in the first explanation, since speech according to others' intent and the rest, the three, run through the latter two explanations, and the next explanation again uses "gate" to run through the first and the last, by principle the "sublime" should also run through the first two. That the text lacks it is an abbreviation; it only notes "(and so on)." It should say: the skillful means of the first explanation is secret but not sublime; the skillful means of the next explanation is secret and able to enter the sublime; the skillful means of the last explanation is secret and is itself sublime. This is spoken from the Buddha's inner understanding; since it has now become explicit, it is omitted and not discussed.

The Four Propositions on Skillful Means and the Provisional

Chinese Source

三約四句中初問意者，《正》本既云善權，當知權是方便異名，欲約異名料簡同異故先問起。答中四，先標列，次釋，三「三句」下判，四「故正法華」下引證。釋中相破一對分為兩句，相修相即各為一句，合四句也。他文或以此為三句，或為六句，隨其法相立意不同，句法常定。今立三對已乃開為四句者，欲以四句用對三釋，以前三句屬初二釋。若但為三句，以前二句判屬前二，有何不可？但相破中文相別故。又準相破，相修亦可分為兩句，四句屬前亦無大失，名句隨時不可一準。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, in terms of the four propositions. The intent of the first question: since the Zheng[fahua] version says "skillful expedients," one should know that the provisional (權) is another name for skillful means; wishing to sift sameness and difference by way of the other name, it first raises the question. The answer is in four: first, the heading and list; next, the explanation; third, from "The first three of these propositions" on, the judgment; fourth, from "hence the Zhengfahua" on, citing proof. In the explanation, the pair of mutual breaking is divided into two propositions, and mutual cultivation and mutual identity each make one proposition, together four. Other texts sometimes treat these as three propositions, sometimes as six; the intent set up differs according to the dharma-marks, but the form of the propositions is always fixed. The reason it sets up three pairs and then opens them into four propositions is that it wishes to match the four propositions with the three explanations, assigning the first three propositions to the first two explanations. If there were only three propositions, assigning the first two propositions to the first two [explanations], what would be wrong? Only that the textual features of mutual breaking are different. Reading Notes for Part 9

Translator's notes

Quoted phrases. Zhanran comments phrase by phrase on the site's third fascicle of the Textual Commentary, from the title of the Skillful Means chapter through the four propositions on the provisional and the real and the opening of the ten pairs. Quoted phrases keep the site's English; where he paraphrases rather than quotes ("three square and one round," "further divided"), the words are translated directly.

The Zhengfahua. Dharmarakṣa's translation (T263) titles the second chapter 善權品, "Chapter of Skillful Expedients," as the Textual Commentary says; Zhanran uses this to argue that 權 ("the provisional") and 方便 ("skillful means") are two names for one thing.

Glosses on fang. The Shuowen defines 法 as 術 ("technique"); the Erya glosses it as 則 ("rule") and 正 ("correct"). Zhanran uses these to show that even the partial teachings are "correct" methods.

"A beckoning and a leading." The site renders 哢引 (Zhanran: 弄引) as "a beckoning and a leading." Zhanran's remark in unit 5 plays on the musical sense: 引 is a lead-in or prelude and 弄 the tune itself, and only someone who does not know music mistakes the prelude for the piece.

A variant in the Textual Commentary. Zhanran quotes "the four kinds are all secret and sublime and identical with the essence" (四種皆是祕妙及同體); the Taishō text of the Textual Commentary reads "the secret, sublime skillful means" (祕妙之方便), which the site follows.

Master Daosheng. The saying "the chapter is titled from the past" (從昔題品), attributed to Daosheng in unit 8, was not located in the Taishō canon; Daosheng's Lotus commentary is outside it.

"For persons." In unit 20 Zhanran says that 為人 in "Because it accords with the individual siddhānta" means the three kinds of persons whose capacities the two truths meet, not the individual siddhānta of the four siddhāntas. The site's English follows the more usual sense.

Jiaxiang (Jizang). Zhanran presents the "attaching to the five periods" and the "three turnings" as Jiaxiang's earlier positions, refuted because Jiaxiang later "gave his heart" to Zhiyi. The four propositions on the vehicle and the body that he quotes in unit 21 were not located in the Taishō works of Jizang checked (Fahua yishu T1721, Fahua xuanlun T1720). Jiaxiang's three wheels of the Dharma are the root wheel (the Avataṃsaka), the branch wheel (the teachings in between), and the wheel that gathers the branches back to the root (the Lotus).

An odd quotation. In unit 21 the CBETA punctuation puts the whole of 皆得覩見汝乃至果方常 inside quotation marks; only the first words are from the Lotus ("causing all sentient beings alike to attain sight and vision"), and the rest is Zhanran's summary of his opponent.

The Upadeśa. "From here on it displays the marks of cause and fruit of this sūtra" (unit 29) condenses the Upadeśa on the Lotus (T1519/T1520): "From here on, the marks of cause and fruit of the Dharma that is spoken should be known."

The ten gates of nonduality. "The ten gates of nonduality" (不二十門, unit 23) are the ten gates that Zhanran sets out in his Explanatory Notes, later circulated separately as the Ten Gates of Nonduality.

Characters and readings. For "nests and lairs" Zhanran writes 巢窟, where the site's text has 樔窟. In unit 23 he allows 懸 ("remote") as an alternative reading of 玄 in "contemplates it in its mysterious depth." The two pronunciation notes in unit 16 are for 錘 and its variant 鎚 ("weight").

The Lotus line. "Is my own skillful means, and every buddha does likewise" (unit 29) is quoted in the site's Lotus wording, from the Medicinal Herbs chapter.

The closing title. The Taishō text closes this fascicle with 法華文句記卷第三（下）, the same as the opening title.
