The Jewel in the Robe, Learners and Those Beyond Learning, and the Teacher of the Dharma

This part contains two sections of Zhanran's eighth fascicle (the Taishō's 八之二 and 八之三). It comments on the close of the Textual Commentary's seventh fascicle and the first half of its eighth (the site's fascicle-007 and fascicle-008): the Prediction for the Five Hundred Disciples, the Prediction for Learners and Those Beyond Learning, and the Teacher of the Dharma chapters. Zhanran first reads Pūrṇa's silent receiving through the four siddhāntas and asks how far his trace and origin reach. He explains the "food of the Dharma" with a long quotation from the Candragarbha, and reads the parable of the jewel sewn in the robe: the two kinds of drunkenness, the outer and inner robes, and trading the jewel. In the Teacher of the Dharma chapter he compares lists of teachers of the Dharma and works through the room, robe, and seat as the three virtues. He treats the single thought of rejoicing as vertical and horizontal, the envoy of the Tathāgata, and the relics of the physical and dharma bodies. The last third concerns "opening the gate of skillful means." Zhanran weighs Guangzhai, Jiaxiang, Daolang, Master Yin, and Liu Qiu, and develops four propositions on name and meaning and on the three revealing the three and the one revealing the one, which he grounds in the mutual revealing of inherent and cultivated virtue.

Explaining the Prediction for the Five Hundred Disciples Chapter

Chinese Source

先標五百，故須作「受」字。「五百是數」等，非要。「上二周」下問也。次「上為」下答也。亦具四悉，初文世界三品異故，又「上來」下對治除嫌惡故，又「默念」下為人生大善故，又「權實」下第一義理非言念故。「上來何意」下但是釋疑，非四悉數。「我等於佛」下述領不及，上中根聞譬，具領五時及法身地，中止方便見勝應身，如來猶斥領所不及；我今聞此雖欲領會，豈能逾於四大弟子，當知所領之外不及處多，是故亦云「所不能宣」。然聞迦葉、身子具領，如來委述非全昧旨，但仰佛法高深，未敢逾於先悟耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because [the title] first marks "five hundred," the character must be written 受 ("receive"). [As for] "five hundred is a number" and so on: it is not essential. From "In the two periods above" on is the question; next, from "Above, it was for the sake of" on is the answer. It also has the four siddhāntas: the first passage is the mundane, because the three chapters differ; also, from "Moreover, in what came before" on is the therapeutic, because it removes the evil of giving offense; also, from "Moreover, silent contemplation" on is the individual, because it produces great good; also, from "Again, the provisional and the real" on is the ultimate meaning, because principle is neither words nor thought. From "Why, above, was there no seeking" on is only resolving a doubt, not counted among the four siddhāntas. From "We could never express in words the Buddha's own merit" on, they state that their receiving does not reach. Above, those of middling faculties, hearing the parable, received in full the five periods and the ground of the dharma-body, and, having stopped midway at the expedients, they saw the superior response-body; and yet the Tathāgata still censured what their receiving did not reach. "Now that we hear this, though we wish to receive and understand it, how could we surpass the four great disciples?" One should know that outside what they receive, what they do not reach is much; therefore they also say "could never express." Yet having heard that Kāśyapa and Śāriputra received fully and that the Tathāgata confirmed it in detail, they are not wholly ignorant of the purport; they only look up to the height and depth of the Buddha's Dharma and dare not surpass those who awakened before.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "proclaiming my Dharma" and so on: if the trace is viewed from the origin, did he not formerly, under past buddhas, assist with the partial alone, the complete alone, and the like? Why must it be the complete with the [partial] accompanying it, and the opened, and the rest? Facing the present buddha, who appears in the world of the five turbidities, it is fitting to lead with transformations such as opening the provisional, setting aside, and merging; therefore all are spoken of in terms of the same kind, just as Mañjuśrī cited the light that shone in the past toward the east. How could there be no other ways? He cites the like example. As for "apart from the Tathāgata himself" through "refers to the seven kinds of skillful means": if one goes by the words "apart from the Buddha," even the one in the place of succession should be unable to fathom him; since he is unfathomable, his origin and trace are hard to measure. Why only formerly with past buddhas? In origin he could also be equal to past buddhas; and with respect to past buddhas the origin is again hard to measure; or perhaps he is even the teacher of past buddhas; why only equal? As for "the seven kinds of skillful means": it is discussed for the time being by setting the partial against the perfect. Hence one knows that the distant origin is dim and truly hard [to fathom]; if so, this is the origin within the trace. The six pāramitās include one another, fully as in my notes on the second [fascicle] of Calming and Contemplation; in the Pūrṇa chapter of the Larger [Prajñā] the six perfections adorn one another. As for "can be understood from the text itself (and so on)": the aspects in the sūtra text correspond clearly to the meanings. Also, as in the five [parts] (五時) listed in the text, [a land that is] purely the good destinies still has differing grades: if there are no women, there are certainly no evil destinies; or at times there are women and still no evil destinies, as in the land of Akṣobhya Buddha, where, though there are women, there are no women's matters; in the land of Amitāyus both are absent. The aspects of difference cannot be listed in full; but since vehicle and precepts each have three grades interwoven, they can be roughly understood by analogy.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "the ninth fascicle of the Candragarbha speaks of the food of the Dharma" and so on: the food of the Dharma is hearing the Dharma, as with those born in the lower grades in Sukhāvatī (安養), who in the lotus constantly hear Amitābha and Avalokiteśvara expound the Dharma. The food of Dharma-joy is rejoicing on hearing the Dharma: the hearing itself belongs to the food of the Dharma, and having heard, it becomes the food of joy. The food of dhyāna means nourishing oneself with the dharma of dhyāna, not needing morsel food; or else the Dharma is itself the joy. The fifth [fascicle] of the Candragarbha has ten merits for each of the ten good [deeds], and it agrees in intent with the Vimalakīrti's "the ten good [deeds] are the bodhisattva's pure land"; hence the end of every passage says "afterward becomes a buddha" and so on. Its fifth fascicle, the chapter on Faith and Reverence, says: "The precepts pure and equal, namely the ceasing of the ten wholesome courses of action. Ceasing killing gains ten merits. What are the ten? First, gaining fearlessness toward all beings; second, gaining a mind of great kindness toward all beings; third, cutting off evil habitual karma; fourth, little illness and decisiveness; fifth, long life; sixth, protection by nonhumans; seventh, no bad dreams; eighth, no enemies; ninth, no fear of the evil destinies; tenth, at the end of life, birth in a good destiny. If one can dedicate this good root of ceasing killing to awakening, one will certainly reach awakening and accomplish unsurpassed wisdom; when one reaches awakening, beings who have left all harmful weapons and live long will come to be born in that land." The following nine are each also ten, and from "If one can" on they are all the same. Hence the bodhisattva in the causal stage practices non-killing as the cause of a pure land: not killing himself, teaching others not to kill, and so on, the four dharmas complete; afterward, when he becomes a buddha, the ten kinds of beings are born together in his land. Hence the Vimalakīrti says: "The ten good [deeds] are the bodhisattva's pure land; when the bodhisattva becomes a buddha, beings whose lives are not cut short and so on come to be born in his land." Hence in the Great Treatise, for the bodhisattva practicing a single practice, the four dharmas must all be complete before it becomes a pure cause.

The Jewel in the Robe

Chinese Source

「譬說有二」至「顯實」者，皆云領法譬者，故前開譬本中直作開權顯實，則應三周及信解五百領解文也，三節開文意在於此。言「譬如有人即二乘人」者，二乘機耳。小機當起爾時猶大。醉有二義，與前墮落中二義意同，初是幼稚譬著五欲，但如法師常不輕等，或一句結緣，次是善弱或五品初未入相似，故云弱耳。以由結緣厚薄不同，遂名無明以為輕重，故云「醉有二種」，當知貧人本來先醉，如蒙肴膳受已而臥，三教助道猶如肴膳，更以異方便助顯第一義也。肴膳食已便消，如方便教非究竟益，往在大通佛所，未結大緣已前歷諸味中並聞三教，及至法華雖聞圓頓，但成結緣如繫珠也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "The parable-teaching has two parts" through "revealing of the real in ages past": both say they receive the dharma-parable, because the earlier opening of the parable was itself made directly into opening the provisional and revealing the real; hence it should apply to the three cycles and to the texts of receiving and understanding in Belief and Understanding and the Five Hundred; the intent of the opening texts of the three sections lies in this. As for "suppose there were a man: this is precisely a person of the two lesser vehicles": [it means] the capacity of the two vehicles. When the lesser capacity was about to arise, at that time it was still great. Drunkenness has two meanings, the same in intent as the two meanings in "falling" earlier: the first is the metaphor of the "young children" attached to the five desires, but like [those in] the Teacher of the Dharma [chapter] and Never Disparaging, forming affinity through a single phrase; the next is good that is weak, or the first of the five grades not yet entering resemblance; hence "weak." Because the forming of affinity is thick or thin, ignorance is named light or heavy; hence "Drunkenness carries two senses." One should know that the poor man was originally drunk first, like one who receives food and delicacies and, having received them, lies down; the aids to the path of the three teachings are like food and delicacies, further using different expedients to help reveal the ultimate meaning. Food and delicacies, once eaten, are digested, like the expedient teachings being not the ultimate benefit. Formerly, at the place of the Buddha Mahābhijñā, before forming the great affinity, they passed through the flavors and heard the three teachings; on reaching the Lotus, though they heard the perfect and sudden, it only formed affinity, like the sewing of the jewel.

「無價」至「真如智寶也」者，此是約教，乃以了因而為繫珠。教中詮理故云「真如」，教是智用故云「智寶」。約受化者，教能生智亦名為智，寶有二義，俱是智家之寶。「繫其衣裏」者，初結緣時具足二衣，具慚愧故、有信樂故，方能結緣；退大墮惡則無外衣，若約現無信樂乃似內衣亦無，且據當時所繫內種仍存與本信俱，義如內衣猶在，但是衣弊非全無衣，故親友示還示衣裏，即是示本慚信時也。據此而言，無衣繫身理亦無失。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "a priceless jewel" through "the jewel of the wisdom of the one-vehicle real mark, true suchness": this is in terms of the teaching, taking the revealing cause as the sewn jewel. The teaching expresses principle, hence "true suchness"; the teaching is the function of wisdom, hence "wisdom-jewel." In terms of those who receive the transformation, the teaching can produce wisdom and is also called wisdom; "jewel" has two meanings, both being jewels belonging to wisdom. As for "sewed it into the lining of his robe": at the first forming of affinity they had both robes complete; because they had shame, and because they had faith and delight, only then could they form affinity. Having retreated from the great and fallen into evil, they have no outer robe; in terms of presently having no faith and delight, it seems the inner robe too is absent; but according to the inner seed sewn at that time, which still remains together with the original faith, by meaning the inner robe is still there; only the robe is worn, not wholly without a robe. Hence the close friend shows him back to the lining of the robe, that is, shows him the time of his original shame and faith. On this basis, [to say that] there is no robe and [the jewel] is tied to the body is also no fault in principle.

「起已遊行」至「求於小乘衣食」者，應更求於寶衣天饌，而但求於纔蔽身之衣、劣充軀之食者，由向他國故也。文中二釋他義皆成，若論有求今日稍切，故云「厭苦」等。「若魔佛相望」等者，今日初得小乘之他，且從大小以說，故知若往他國求濟，非但衣食不充，亦迷所繫之寶，示珠之友居本土故。「勸貿譬得記作佛意」者，珠雖價直無數眾寶，必須貿易方有濟用，了因內解雖復究竟，必以種易現，以昔一解一切解而貿一行一切行，珠體不竭貿亦無窮，故須更聽更修方顯寶之功用。如《華嚴》中得摩尼珠，十種瑩治方能雨寶，解行相稱方堪佛記，從是已後則具有寂滅忍衣，首楞嚴食自行化他無量眾寶，無功用位彼此不窮。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "rising, he wandered off to another country" through "the clothing and food of the lesser vehicle": he ought to seek further for jeweled clothing and heavenly fare, but he only seeks clothing that barely covers the body and food that scarcely fills the frame, because he is heading for another country. The text's two explanations of "another" both hold. If one discusses seeking, today it is somewhat urgent; hence "weary of suffering" and so on. As for "If Māra and the Buddha are set against each other" and so on: today's first obtaining of the Lesser Vehicle's "another" is spoken of for the time being from great and lesser; hence one knows that if he goes to another country to seek relief, not only are clothing and food insufficient, but he is also deluded about the jewel sewn [in his robe], because the friend who shows the jewel dwells in the home country. As for "urging him to trade it in illustrates receiving the prediction of buddhahood": though the jewel is worth countless treasures, it must be traded before it is of use; though the inner understanding of the revealing cause is ultimate, one must trade the seed for the manifest, trading the past "one understanding is all understanding" for "one practice is all practices." The jewel's substance is inexhaustible and the trading too is endless; hence one must further listen and further cultivate to reveal the function of the treasure. As in the Avataṃsaka, the maṇi jewel obtained can rain treasures only after ten kinds of polishing; when understanding and practice match, one is fit for the Buddha's prediction. From then on one has the robe of the forbearance of quiescent cessation, the food of Śūraṅgama, and the countless treasures of one's own practice and transforming others; in the stage without effort, both are inexhaustible.

「三周皆有此意」者，若以繫珠望上二周，法說但在佛樹者，初坐道樹思用大時，以法說時未論往古；且據現文，若譬周中在二萬億佛，彼亦未論塵點界故。然上中二周豈不亦有於大通佛所曾繫珠耶？如探領中尚領法身，豈止道樹？且約現文耳。但由根利聞便信解，不假指昔，是故未論。故前文中以發軫學小為中間，故不唯在道樹時也。「某年」等言，亦唯在穢不通二周。「無量佛寶」者，寶由貿得故，亦可云得佛之寶，即利他也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "all three of the periods of teaching carry these same three senses": if the sewn jewel is viewed against the two cycles above, in the dharma-teaching it is only at the Buddha's tree, when he first sat at the tree of the path and thought of using the great, because at the time of the dharma-teaching the ancient past was not yet discussed; and it goes for the time being by the manifest text. In the parable cycle it is at the twenty thousand koṭis of buddhas, because there too the dust-mote realms were not yet discussed. Yet in the upper and middle cycles, how could the jewel not also have been sewn at the place of the Buddha Mahābhijñā? As in receiving by anticipation they even received the dharma-body; how could it stop at the tree of the path? For now it goes by the manifest text. Only because their faculties were sharp, and on hearing they believed and understood without needing to be shown the past, it was not discussed. Hence the earlier text takes setting out to study the lesser as the interval; hence it is not only at the time of the tree of the path. The words "in such-and-such a year" and so on are also only in the defiled [land] and do not extend to the two cycles. As for "the measureless treasure of the buddhas": the treasure is obtained by trading; it can also be called the treasure of obtaining buddhahood, which is benefiting others.

Explaining the Prediction for Learners and Those Beyond Learning Chapter

Chinese Source

因緣初文應具四悉，學無學別即世界，見道位即為人，脩道位即對治，無學位即第一義。又得記即第一義。約教者，三教如文，「研如來藏」去圓教。又如通序中釋若約觀心者，六即之中究竟即為無學位，餘四名為學，理即非學非無學。又六即者，通皆非學非無學，分真已去而學而無學。「云云」者，如向略舉。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first passage of the explanation by causes and conditions should include the four siddhāntas: the distinction of learning and beyond learning is the mundane; the stage of the path of seeing is the individual; the stage of the path of cultivation is the therapeutic; the stage beyond learning is the ultimate meaning. Also, obtaining the prediction is the ultimate meaning. In terms of the teachings, the three teachings are as in the text; from "To study the tathāgata-garbha" on is the perfect teaching. Also, as in the explanation in the general prologue, if in terms of contemplating the mind: among the six identities, ultimate identity is the stage beyond learning, the other four are called learning, and identity in principle is neither learning nor beyond learning. Also, generally, the six identities are all neither learning nor beyond learning, and from partial truth on they are both learning and beyond learning. As for "(and so on)": as just briefly raised.

「二人在上數中」者，在多知識中列，今之得記何為在此？答中總論得記，在千二百中仍是下根中之上流耳。雖有多人所識，意為引下根故也。若爾不答上問，問意何不同上周，今答中但云下中之上，如何稱問？然非上者，為引實故、四悉檀故。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "these two were among the foremost number": they were listed among the many well-known ones; why does their present prediction come here? The answer discusses obtaining the prediction generally: among the twelve hundred they are still the upper stream among those of lower faculties. Though known to many, the intent is to draw those of lower faculties. If so, it does not answer the question above: the intent of the question is why they are not the same as the upper cycle, and now the answer only says the upper of the lower; how does it fit the question? Yet they are not upper in order to draw to the real, and because of the four siddhāntas.

The Five Teachers of the Dharma and Reducing the Number

Chinese Source

釋法師品

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Teacher of the Dharma Chapter

Chinese Source

釋此品者為二：先總釋，次別釋。初文二，先釋五法師，次減數釋。初又二：初出經論以辨離合有無，次判通別以結品名。初中二：初依今經判五，次「大論」下出異釋名。對今以辨離合有無，乃至如下減數以釋，故若多若小俱名法師。初五法師者，未有一文著一二等言，古今共立稱為五種法師故。經論所立多少不同，所以此品但通名法師。《天王般若》云：「受持此經有十種法：一書寫，二供養，三流傳，四諦聽，五自讀，六憶持，七廣說，八誦，九思惟，十修行。」十中第一是今文第五，第四第六是今文第一，第五是第二，第八是第三，第七是第四，流傳攝在廣說文中，餘三並攝在憶持文中。彼經憶秖是受，《大論》中分受持為二，則應不得單云憶持。此「別論」下判通別以結品名，於中先分自他以別師位。言「大經分九品」等者，元是別義，且借以證五種法師。九品秖是熙連并八恒，第四恒廣說，於十六分中解一分義。云「前四無解」者，三恒并熙連，五恒八分，六恒十二分，七恒十四分，八恒具足盡解其義，故須應從能解一分義去，能為他說即是師位。次「通論」下捨別從通，自他俱得受此品名，從通義邊無有一句不任為師。

Ru-Yi Translation

In explaining this chapter there are two [parts]: first the general explanation, next the specific explanation. The first passage has two: first explaining the five teachers of the Dharma, next the explanation by reducing the number. The first again has two: first it brings out sūtras and treatises to distinguish combining and separating, presence and absence; next it judges general and specific to conclude the chapter name. The first has two: first, according to the present sūtra, it judges five; next, from "The Great Treatise" on, it brings out different explanations of the names. Distinguishing combining and separating, presence and absence relative to the present [sūtra], up to the explanation by reducing the number below: hence, whether many or few, all are called teachers of the Dharma. As for the first, the five teachers of the Dharma: no text marks them with "one," "two," and so on; past and present together set them up and call them the five kinds of teachers of the Dharma. Sūtras and treatises set up more or fewer, differently; therefore this chapter only names them generally "teacher of the Dharma." The Heavenly King Prajñā says: "Receiving and upholding this sūtra has ten dharmas: first, copying; second, making offerings; third, circulating; fourth, listening attentively; fifth, reading oneself; sixth, remembering and holding; seventh, expounding widely; eighth, reciting; ninth, reflecting; tenth, cultivating." Of the ten, the first is the present text's fifth; the fourth and sixth are the present text's first; the fifth is the second; the eighth is the third; the seventh is the fourth; circulating is included in the text on expounding widely; the remaining three are all included in the text on remembering and holding. In that sūtra "remembering" is only receiving; the Great Treatise divides receiving and upholding into two, so one should not say simply "remembering and holding." From "Distinguished separately" on, it judges general and specific to conclude the chapter name; within this, it first divides self and other to distinguish the stage of the teacher. As for "The Nirvāṇa Sūtra divides these into nine grades" and so on: originally it is a specific meaning, borrowed for the time being to prove the five kinds of teachers of the Dharma. The nine grades are only the Hiraṇyavatī together with the eight Ganges. At the fourth Ganges one expounds widely, understanding one portion of the meaning out of sixteen portions. As for saying "the first four kinds, without understanding": the three Ganges and the Hiraṇyavatī. At the fifth Ganges, eight portions; at the sixth Ganges, twelve portions; at the seventh Ganges, fourteen portions; at the eighth Ganges one understands its meaning fully. Hence it must be from understanding one portion of the meaning on that one is able to expound for others, which is the stage of the teacher. Next, from "Taken in general" on, it leaves the specific for the general: self and other both can receive this chapter name; from the side of the general meaning, there is not a single phrase for which one is unfit to be a teacher.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the explanation by reducing the number again has four: four, three, two, and one; all successively bundle the many into the few, and none is not a teacher of the Dharma. As for saying "as explained later": fully as in the explanation of the four practices at the beginning of the Peaceful Practices chapter. Since the four point there, here one should use the four to conclude the chapter name; the text lacks it because it is abbreviated; since there are examples before, after, and in between, it can be understood. If one wishes to know it: just first judge general and specific, and next, from the general four, conclude the chapter name. Bundling the four into three again has three intents: first, the three kinds of action; next, the three gates; third, the three dharmas; each is first explained, next judged general and specific to conclude the chapter name. Next, bundling the three into two: one's own practice and transforming others. As for saying "need not be further recorded": it can be understood from the preceding; it too first explains, next judges general and specific, and concludes the chapter name. Since self and other pervade the three above, one knows that self and other also have general and specific: specifically each is separate, generally they interpenetrate, because self and other are both mutually capable. Now, generally from transforming others, it is named the Teacher of the Dharma chapter.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, bundling the two into one: as for "the Tathāgata's own practice, which is complete with every practice": this relies on the Noble Practice chapter of the Great Sūtra: "There is again one practice, the Tathāgata's practice, namely the Mahāyāna Great Parinirvāṇa." Nirvāṇa is only the secret treasury of the three virtues; hence here it in turn uses room, robe, and seat to explain the three virtues; the three virtues are only one great nirvāṇa; this great nirvāṇa pervades all places and all dharmas, and is generally named "one." First it explains, next it points to the full [treatment]. In the first explanation there are two: first general, next specific. The general: the one practice generally includes the three dharmas, the room and the rest. The specific correlation has two: first correlating, next the examination. The first passage has nine, all correlating the three dharmas with various dharmas. First, correlating them with good and evil, and with sweeping away marks. Next, from "Again, compassion and forbearance establish" on, merit and wisdom. As for "wisdom is the eyes" and so on: it should also say that one whose eyes and feet are complete must have something to rely on, and what is relied on contains the two. Third, from "Again, compassion surpasses" on, correlating them with censuring the partial and the wrong, also first explaining, next citing the Vimalakīrti as proof: surpassing the partial and the wrong and boldly cultivating the perfect. Fourth, from "Again, compassion breaks" on, breaking the four māras, also first explaining (in the explanation all must be explained by the perfect beyond the realms), next citing proof of emptiness. As for "(and so on)": it explains its intent. Fifth, from "Again, because of compassion" (慈悲) on, making clear that the two adornments are complete, again with two explanations, which only reverse the preceding explanation; judging the former from the latter, the former should say "compassion and forbearance." Sixth, from "through compassion and forbearance" on, in terms of metaphors: breaking and establishing and so on, both [like] the preceding example. Seventh, from "Again, because of compassion, what is there" on, in terms of essence and function: the function is departing from faults. Eighth, from "Rising above the three truths" on, in terms of names: unlike the sequential three truths, the room of compassion embraces the robe of forbearance and the samādhi. Ninth, from "The sūtra says" on, citing the sūtra: the markless is the seat. Next, pointing to the full [treatment], as for saying "it could never be exhausted (and so on)": fully as in the Profound Meaning's making clear that the five practices of the perfect include all practices, and as in Calming and Contemplation's ten vehicles and ten objects, including all horizontally and vertically, and the horizontal and vertical dharma-gates in the pervasive breaking, up to the four samādhis including all practices. All practices are only one practice; hence it is explained in nine passages.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, from "Question" on, the examination. As for the first question: according to the first explanation of the chapter, it should say five kinds; according to the later reduction of the number, it should say one dharma; why does it correlate only the three, robe, seat, and room, with the various dharmas? As for the answer: matters revealing principle must have the three dharmas; moreover, according to this text there should be the three dharmas, so as to expound for the assembly; hence only with both threes, of matters and of principle, can one expound the Dharma. Hence first the explanation by matters, next the combination of matters and principle, third principle alone. The explanation by matters is as the text has it.

合釋中言「三門」者，初二屬事，後一屬理，事理並是所迷之境。初「約苦果」者，故用慈悲門，苦須拔故。次「約結業」者，故用忍門，依理離故。三「約諦境」，故用空門，善住空故。第三單「約理」者，理即諦理，故約三諦，即向第三具三諦故。言「云云」者，應以多種三法對釋此三，令成圓融通暢自在，乃識此文攝教遍相，故下總約教云「凡多種釋品者皆約圓教」。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the combined explanation, as for saying "three gates": the first two belong to matters, the last to principle; matters and principle are both objects about which one is deluded. First, "with regard to the fruit of suffering": hence it uses the gate of compassion, because suffering must be removed. Next, "with regard to karmic bondage": hence it uses the gate of forbearance, because one departs [from it] by principle. Third, "with regard to the object of the truths": hence it uses the gate of emptiness, because one dwells well in emptiness. Third, solely "in terms of the principle": the principle is the principle of the truths, hence it is in terms of the three truths, namely the preceding third, which contains the three truths. As for saying "(and so on)": one should explain these three by correlating many kinds of three dharmas, making them perfectly fused, penetrating, and free; then one recognizes that this text includes the aspect of pervading the teachings. Hence below, speaking generally in terms of the teachings, it says, "As for the many kinds of explanation generally, all explain the chapter in terms of the dharma-gate of the perfect teaching."

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, from "As for 'dharma'" on, the specific explanation. The two words "teacher of the Dharma" run through all the explanations above, because the dharmas above are the teacher's dharmas; hence now in explaining the name it also speaks in terms of two kinds up to five kinds. As for "Whether one takes the Dharma as one's own standard" and so on, the explanation by causes and conditions: first, the difference of self and other is the mundane, that is, the standard for oneself and the training of others. Accomplishing one's own practice is producing good; transforming others and removing evil is the therapeutic; self and other both being called teachers of the Dharma is the ultimate meaning. From "As for the many kinds" on, in terms of the teachings: as with the many kinds just now, there can be no other explanation. "Moisten" (洽) means to soak.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "Those still outside the ranks of the sages" through "without fear of danger or hardship": those outside the ranks, in the five grades, delight in the blessing of propagation; śrāvakas wholly lack the mind of benefiting beings. Hearing that the great beings are cast out by the multitude, and seeing that their own power of contemplation is not yet deep, if they did not rely on it, they would within defile pure contemplation and without invite criticism, losing the way of benefiting people. Hence the Buddha teaches the four peaceful practices so that they have no such trouble and are far from the ten vexations; hence "without fear." As for "at once bear witness and assist": the jeweled stūpa comes because it bears witness to the sūtra, and the emanation-bodies gather because they assist the opening; through the witnessing and the assisting, [beings] aspire to transmission and flow. As for "It also shows the method and standard for proclaiming the sūtra": putting on the robe and the other two, fully as said later. As for "on account of this he told" and so on: originally entrusting it to Bhaiṣajyarāja, on that account he tells the others; this is transmission and flow. As for the first, first telling the eighty thousand great beings: the Great Treatise says, "The Lotus is the secret, entrusted to the bodhisattvas"; as in the text below he summons those from below, still waiting for the original retinue, verifying that the rest are not yet fit. As for "the general and the separate predictions": the general is given to the seven hundred, the separate with eon, land, and so on. "Bound" (絓; pronunciation note) means to hinder and to take part in.

「乃至一念」等者，明乘此念必得菩提。遠因不亡藉茲而發，不同餘善是故簡之。究而論之，必須盡行諸佛道法，二乘記已尚經劫數，然曉其經旨與具足何殊？但自行化他令始未耳。「聞一句一偈」者，通論但云聞極少法，舉經功深，部內隨取一句一偈；別論但今義合權實本迹十妙四一之流，功福皆爾。然義通大小，故下引《增一》集云「如四諦之流」，及下引四安樂行等，今亦準之。言「皆與授記」者，必由此經成菩提故，雖無劫國當得理同，此中容用別時意趣，是故應須以聞約行廣明供養，宣通益他內觀具足，具如《止觀》。豈可端拱唯仰初心？「中上亦然」者，若不爾者，豈可上周唯在一人，中周四人得記者耶？說寄在此、事乃通彼，故云「亦然」。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "even a single thought-moment" and so on: it makes clear that riding on this thought one necessarily attains awakening. The distant cause is not lost and by this is aroused; it is unlike other good, therefore it is singled out. Investigating it thoroughly, one must fully practice all the path-dharmas of the buddhas; even the two vehicles, after their predictions, still pass through eons. Yet understanding the purport of the sūtra, how does it differ from completeness? It only causes one's own practice and transforming others to have beginning and end (始未). As for "hearing a single phrase, a single verse": speaking generally, it only says hearing a very little dharma, raising the depth of the sūtra's merit: any single phrase or verse taken from within the collection. Speaking specifically, only the present meaning, such as the provisional and the real, origin and trace, the ten wonders, and the four onenesses: the merit and blessing are all so. Yet the meaning covers great and lesser; hence below it cites the Ekottara Compilation, saying "of the kind such as the four truths," and below it cites the four peaceful practices and so on; now take it accordingly. As for saying "all receive the prediction": because by this sūtra one necessarily achieves awakening; though without eon and land, the future attainment is the same in principle. Here one may apply the intention of another time; therefore one must, by hearing, broadly make clear practice and offerings, proclaiming and benefiting others, and complete inner contemplation, fully as in Calming and Contemplation. How could one sit with folded hands and rely only on the beginning mind? As for "the middling and highest are likewise so": if not so, how could the upper cycle be only one person and the middle cycle four persons obtaining prediction? The speaking is lodged here, but the matter extends to those; hence "likewise."

Next, in the chapter of the Prediction for the Thirty-Three Gods, eight koṭis of gods of the Trāyastriṃśa [heaven], seeing the preceding gods make offerings and obtain predictions, also all transformed [themselves] into implements of offering and at the same time received predictions named Indra's Banner, and expounded the dharma of illusion. Next, in the chapter of the Yāma Gods, four koṭis of Yāma gods, also seeing the gods make offerings and obtain predictions, likewise made offerings to the Buddha with transformations and with verses, and at the same time received predictions, all named Pure Wisdom. As for saying "Kauśika": that sūtra does not have it; I fear it is an error.
