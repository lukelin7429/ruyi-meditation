Predictions, the Five Thousand, and Opening the Buddha's Knowledge and Vision

This part completes Zhanran's notes on the Textual Commentary's ten points of analysis for the three rounds of teaching in the Skillful Means chapter: why bodhisattvas offer no comprehension and receive no new predictions, why awakening deepens from round to round, how provisional and real hearers both benefit, and whether a hearer must "await the time." A series of questions follows: how Śāriputra's requests relate to the three faculties, whether ignorance can be called an obstruction, how the Lotus squares with sūtras that say the two vehicles already "know," and why pratyekabuddhas can be present in the Buddha's assembly. Zhanran then comments on the Buddha's granting of permission and the departure of the five thousand, citing the Necklace Sūtra, the Avataṃsaka, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, and the Golden Light Sūtra, and on the six sections of the extended teaching and the udumbara flower. The last unit, the longest of the part, defends the Textual Commentary's rejection of older readings of "opening, showing, awakening, and entering" the Buddha's knowledge and vision, and lists ten pairs of features that set the Lotus apart from the other sūtras.

Comprehension and Predictions for Bodhisattvas

Chinese Source

次菩薩中二：先總述意，次「又其意」下別出所以。其意有三：初明無，次意或有或無，易故則無故云「處處有文」，準理合有故云「梵文」等也。三明有中云「菩薩位行」等者，菩薩實位極至妙覺，仍異於別故名為深。新入實者猶非所及，故名為「絕」。始從權來，故名為「新」。昔權位下故名為「小」。「說壽量」等者，據理迹中具合有領，新小自不敢耳。故下總領頌云：「佛說希有法，昔所未曾聞，世尊有大力，壽命不可量，無數諸佛子，聞世尊分別，說得法利者」，乃至「或無礙樂說，餘有一生在」等，即是補處總為領解，八相易領故聲聞領之，法身記難非淺所領。言「更求何物云云」者，應更責問者，汝唯知聲聞別領，而不見菩薩通領耶？故此三釋其義猶通，初意通指三教菩薩，通皆求佛，雖是權人執易轉故；第二三意雖云處處有文，及小菩薩等，故應分別，小在三權、悟大語略，故注「云云」良由於此。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, concerning the bodhisattvas, two: first, stating the intent in general; next, from "There are, moreover, three further senses" (又其意) on, setting out the reasons separately. The intent has three: first, it makes clear that [their comprehension] is absent; next, the intent is that it is either present or absent: because [their awakening] is easy, it is absent, hence it says "attested everywhere throughout the text"; judging by principle it should be present, hence it says "the Indic text" and so on. Third, in making clear that it is present, it says "the bodhisattvas' stage and practice" and so on: the bodhisattva's real rank reaches up to wondrous awakening and still differs from the distinct [teaching], hence it is called "profound." Those newly entering the real still do not reach it, hence it is called "far surpassing" (絕). They have just come from the provisional, hence "newly arisen" (新). Their former provisional rank was low, hence "minor" (小). As for "once the Life Span Chapter has been preached" and so on: according to principle, within the trace there should fully be comprehension; it is only that the new and minor do not dare. Hence below, the general verse of comprehension says: "The Buddha has taught a rare Dharma, never heard until now; the World-Honored One possesses great power, his lifespan beyond all measure. Countless children of the Buddha, hearing the World-Honored One's own distinctions, of those who have gained this benefit of the Dharma," up to "some have unobstructed eloquence," and "with only a single lifetime remaining," and so on. This is the one in the place of succession comprehending on behalf of all. The eight aspects are easy to comprehend, so the śrāvakas comprehend them; the prediction of the dharma-body is difficult and is not comprehended by the shallow. As for saying "what more could be sought, and so on": one should further question the questioner: "You know only that the śrāvakas comprehend separately; do you not see that the bodhisattvas comprehend generally?" Hence the meaning of these three explanations is still general. The first intent points generally to the bodhisattvas of the three teachings, all of whom seek buddhahood, because, though they are provisional persons, their attachment is easily turned. The second and third intents, though they say "attested everywhere throughout the text" and "minor bodhisattvas" and so on, should therefore be distinguished: the minor are within the three provisional [teachings], and [the text on] their awakening to the great is terse; the note "and so on" (云云) is precisely for this reason.

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, receiving or not receiving predictions: this too is a reckoning of the ancients. First it raises the challenge. Next, in the explanation proper, the pratyekabuddhas, as before, are included among the śrāvakas, so it addresses only the bodhisattvas, again in three. The first text fully uses the first intent of the preceding [gate on] the presence or absence of comprehension, with the meaning also including the second; if it is the same as the first two, it is the same as the third; that is, the first text uses all three preceding meanings, because where there is a prediction there is comprehension. As for saying "as before, and so on": the first is as with comprehension before; in the second, the words "the Indic text perhaps had it" are missing, and it says only "according to what was essential." The second intent is the same in meaning as the third before; but the former text was general, while now it has both general and specific: the Nāga Girl is specific and the Teacher of the Dharma [chapter] is general. Since the predictions are clearly manifest, what prevents comprehension? The third intent is based on the difference between near and far: the near is not what they delight in, and the distant prediction is also in Distinguishing Merits. As for "the Life Span Chapter": they receive the prediction because they hear again in the Lifespan [chapter]. Compared with the third before, only what is addressed differs: the former addressed the minor bodhisattvas, this the persons of the Lesser Vehicle. The intent of the next question: why not say directly that after so many eons they will obtain the dharma-body of wondrous awakening, instead of speaking only of the eight aspects at the initial abiding? This is the main text of our school's teaching intent, yet many people do not awaken to it. The intent of the answer has two: one, making clear that the eight aspects are necessary; two, the dharma-body is given afterward; using the later to shape the earlier, one knows it is the initial abiding, pointing horizontally for now to the origin of the dharma-body. Hence one knows that the two vehicles gain benefit in two places; giving them the prediction of the eight aspects further makes them form with beings the causes of a pure land. Bodhisattvas have already benefited beings for many eons, liberating them as they ripen, and do not need the shallow and near prediction of the eight aspects; the two vehicles are not so, therefore they need it.

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, awakening shallow or deep: here there is no ancient master. Within it again four: first, briefly making clear the reason: they are distinguished as shallow and deep because there is brightness and dimness. Next, from "first opening" (初開) on, explaining the meaning of shallow and deep. Since it says that on first hearing the dharma-teaching one already enters the Buddha's wisdom, the words "the Buddha's wisdom" are not confined to one grade within the abidings: perhaps only the initial abiding, or the second, third, or fourth, up to the tenth ground. That the three stages advance is truly so in principle. As in the explanation of "pondering" it says "entered before" and "entering now," so the words "entering now" cover four stages, namely the three rounds and the Lifespan [chapter]. But "those who entered before" differ in manifest and hidden; fearing the preceding intent would not be understood, it repeats it. Third, from "just as one who listens to the Dharma" on, citing the ordinary to argue a fortiori for the sage, citing the coarse to argue for the fine: if hearing again had no benefit, the first hearing too would have been in vain; with effort added at four stages, in principle there should be advance, and that some reach [the last] single birth is precisely because of this. Fourth, from "the single and the compound" on, citing a matter: like getting clothes when cold, layer upon layer gradually better. "The thick and the thin" explains "the single and the compound."

Benefit, Awaiting the Time, and Miscellaneous Questions

Chinese Source

第九明權實得益者，為六：初出舊解，言「一云」者，舊應多釋，其一違文故且云一。次「今明」下正釋。言「隣圓」者，「圓」謂圓滿，近於滿位隣妙覺也。言「際極」者，顯隣圓耳。三「所以」下明得益由國。「文云」下引證。言「化功」者，影響迹時已有化益，前後增道即是深利，權人處處得益深故。五「故一音」下立理，不必併須待至〈壽量〉，故云「一音」。又密益者宜聞長遠，時處不同顯密各異，故注「云云」。六「又我」下正證影響得益之文，若於己無益何謂欲得？

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, making clear the benefits gained by the provisional and the real, in six: first, setting out the old explanation. It says "one view holds" (一云) because the old [masters] must have had many explanations; one of them contradicts the text, so for now it says "one." Next, from "It is now made clear" on, the explanation proper. As for "drawing near . the perfect" (隣圓): "perfect" means perfect fulfillment; being near the stage of fulfillment is neighboring wondrous awakening. As for "the ultimate limit" (際極): it shows "drawing near the perfect." Third, from "Hence" on, it makes clear that the benefit is gained by way of [other] lands. [Fourth,] from "The text says" on, citing proof. As for "the merit of transforming others": at the time of the echoing trace there is already the benefit of transforming; advancing the path before and after is the deep gain, because provisional persons gain deep benefit everywhere. Fifth, from "Hence, with a single voice" on, establishing the principle: it is not necessary to wait until the Lifespan [chapter]; hence "a single voice." Moreover, those who benefit hiddenly should hear of the long and distant [lifespan]; times and places differ, and manifest and hidden are each different; hence the note "and so on." Sixth, from "Moreover, one's own wish" (又我) on, properly proving by the text that the echoing assembly gain benefit: if there were no benefit to oneself, why would it speak of wishing to attain?

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, making clear awaiting or not awaiting the time: first, in the explanation proper, first the great distinction; next, from "Applied to" (若就) on, it discusses in detail awaiting and the rest with respect to the three rounds and the origin. Within the three rounds there are of themselves hidden [benefits]: as when, at the time of the dharma-teaching, some covertly hear of the great carriage and the matter of Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū and gain benefit; this is not awaiting the time. Hidden hearing in the middle round can be seen by analogy with this account.

Ru-Yi Translation

The question says "not included in either the covert or the overt time" (非顯非密): this means those of fixed nature. In the preceding four periods they had no hidden benefit, and not reaching the Lotus they had no manifest gain either; with no benefit in either place, is it called a lost time? The answer, "Other sūtras" and so on, means: since the preceding four periods speak of eternal extinction, the śrāvakas and others do not know the transformational [birth-and-death]. Hence in the Vimalakīrti Kāśyapa says of himself that they "ought to weep aloud, with a cry that shakes the whole trichiliocosm," and "with respect to the Great Vehicle, no better than scorched seed." Judged by that sūtra, how could scorched seeds sprout and come to the Lotus all to receive the Buddha's prediction? If so, why did the Buddha not predict them right then, but let them be delayed until they came to the Lotus? Manifest and hidden differ, as already explained. If one uses the provisional treatises of eternal extinction to explain the real sūtra of opening and merging, then, since the sūtra has already given them life, how can a treatise extinguish them? It is only that those who are extinguished get to hear it in that [other land], which the other sūtras do not speak of, let alone the general sūtras and treatises. Hence they lose the manifest and the hidden, yet this is not losing the time; it is only that the propagators of the teaching twist [these texts] to explain this. It is the fault of the explainers, not the blame of the treatises.

問：身子初周為三根請者，此下雜料簡不關十門。言為四眾三根者，初周普請佛亦普說，餘未悟者還為普請，既普請普說云何言佛各為說耶？此引三抑俟其三周，佛既權抑預表身子權能預謀，何以不各各請之？而三周之首通為三根，譬周之初通為中下，故諸天領解。文後云「爾時舍利弗白佛言：我今無復疑悔，是諸千二百等」，故知是為中根請也。乃至文云「願為四眾說其因緣」，即為下根請也。答意可知。然身子設使預知在三，終須普請。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the question "In the first round, Śāriputra requests on behalf of the . three kinds of faculties": from here on there is miscellaneous sorting that does not concern the ten gates. As for saying "on behalf of the fourfold assembly and the three kinds of faculties": in the first round he requested for all and the Buddha also taught for all; for the rest who had not yet awakened, he again requested for all. Since the request was for all and the teaching for all, how can one say the Buddha taught for each? This cites [the view that] the three restraints awaited the three rounds: since the Buddha restrained them provisionally as a foreshadowing, Śāriputra could provisionally plan in advance; why did he not request for each separately? Yet the head of the three rounds is generally for the three faculties, and the beginning of the parable round is generally for the middling and inferior; hence the gods comprehend. After the text it says, "At that time Śāriputra said to the Buddha: 'World-Honored One! I now have no more doubt or regret . But these twelve hundred,'" hence one knows that he requests for the middling faculties. Further on, the text says, "I beg you, World-Honored One, to explain for the fourfold assembly the cause and condition of this," which is requesting for the inferior faculties. The intent of the answer can be understood. Yet even if Śāriputra had known in advance that there would be three, in the end he must request for all.

The middling and inferior were still in the dark, therefore he requested again for all the middling and inferior, and the Buddha also gave the parable-teaching for all the middling and inferior. Hence, of the twelve hundred, those apart from the four belong to the inferior faculties; so the Buddha, discerning the capacity, did not need another request and at once granted it, saying, "The causal connections between myself and all of you, from ages past, I shall now speak of."

"And so on" (云云): how could the middling and inferior not hear the dharma-teaching? How could the first request be solely for the sharpest? It rebukes at length the old views that do not understand the great plan.
