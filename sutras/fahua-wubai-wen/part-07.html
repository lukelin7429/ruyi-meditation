From the Peaceful Practices to the Tathāgata's Lifespan

Part 7 continues Fascicle Three through the "Peaceful Practices," "Welling Up," and "Lifespan" chapters. Ji treats the seven chapters from "Peaceful Practices" on as dissemination by study and practice, with the peaceful practices as the training to be learned by the bodhisattvas who well up from the earth. Zhanran answers that the peaceful practices are a beginner's practice in the trace gate, and that bodhisattvas who have long practiced and stand at the stage of succession cannot be sent back to study them. He also objects to Ji's glosses on the conduct to be avoided, on the "fifty small eons" that seemed like half a day, and on the young father with the old son. The longest dispute concerns the "Lifespan" chapter. Ji reads it through the three bodies and the Yogācāra "secret intentions," which allow the Buddha to speak of other buddhas as himself, and he still counts the chapter as dissemination. Zhanran insists that the chapter reveals what no earlier teaching revealed: that Śākyamuni attained buddhahood in the distant past. Therefore the origin gate is the core of the sūtra, and the Treatise on the Lotus itself points to the long-ago accomplishment. The part ends with the parable of the physician.

○安樂行品

Ru-Yi Translation

The "Peaceful Practices" Chapter

Is This the Practice That the Six Chapters Study?

Chinese Source

問：此品下文何義理耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is the meaning and principle of this chapter and the text below it?

Chinese Source

答：三流通中，上四品讚重流通，此下七品學行流通。今問：學行屬能，如何屬所？又曰：雖復前已讚重，若不說學能行弘經之行，末代多難，頻生傷毀，不能弘通。準此釋，豈判屬所？又曰：於七品中，安樂一品是所學弘經之行，誦出等六能持經之人。人由法以成德，法藉人以弘宣。若無所學之行，其人何由建德？故安樂為所學之行。若無能行之人，其法何由廣布？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Among the three [kinds of] dissemination, the four chapters above are the dissemination by praise and honoring; the seven chapters from here on are the dissemination by study and practice." Now I ask: study and practice belong to the side of the agent; how can they belong to the side of the object? It further says: "Although [the sūtra] has already praised and honored [it] before, if it did not teach the practice of propagating the sūtra that one studies and is able to practice, then in the last age, with its many difficulties, [the Dharma] would often suffer injury and could not be propagated." According to this explanation, how could [this chapter] be judged to belong to the object? It further says: "Among the seven chapters, the single 'Peaceful [Practices]' chapter is the practice of propagating the sūtra that is studied; the six, 'Welling Up' and the rest, are the persons who are able to uphold the sūtra. Persons accomplish virtue by means of the Dharma; the Dharma relies on persons to be propagated and proclaimed. If there were no practice to be studied, how could those persons establish virtue? Hence the 'Peaceful [Practices]' is the practice to be studied. If there were no persons able to practice, how could that Dharma be spread widely?"

Chinese Source

今謂若此品為弘經之行，此則可然。如前所釋云：身子、文殊、彌勒為三處請主。此則不爾。何者？凡有請者，皆是請正。此中自判以為流通，文殊但云：於後惡世，菩薩摩訶薩云何能說是經？故知不得為經緣起。緣起在於初中，流通居後，故不例也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now I say: if [you say that] this chapter is the practice of propagating the sūtra, that may be so. But as explained earlier, it said: "Śāriputra, Mañjuśrī, and Maitreya are the hosts of the requests in three places." This is not so. Why? Wherever there is a request, it is always a request for the main [section]. Here you yourself judge [this chapter] to be dissemination, and Mañjuśrī only says: "In the evil age to come, how is a bodhisattva-mahāsattva able to teach this sūtra?" Hence we know it cannot be the occasion of the sūtra. The occasion lies at the beginning and in the middle; the dissemination stands at the end; hence they are not analogous.

Chinese Source

故知安樂行是迹門，始行弘於迹經。云何釋言云是涌出菩薩之行，安令本地久成，反學迹中始行？若實學於迹中始行，如何彌勒不識一人，乃問受持誰經、集何佛法、從誰發心？因如來答說醫師喻，父少子老乃至住入出於百千三昧，如何得云伽耶佛化？集此佛法始離惡緣，不說他人好惡長短等，方始夢見當入諸位，故壽量品唯說如來本地久成久遠眷屬，豈應反學迹中始行？如八十老人為教玄孫令讀孝經，如何令子反讀此典？

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence we know that the peaceful practices are the beginning practice of the trace gate, propagating the sūtra of the trace. How can you explain them as the practice of the bodhisattvas who welled up, making those whose original ground was accomplished long ago turn back and study the beginning practice within the trace? If they really studied the beginning practice within the trace, how is it that Maitreya did not recognize a single one of them, and even asked which sūtra they upheld, whose buddha-Dharma they had gathered, and from whom they had aroused the mind? Because of this the Tathāgata answered by telling the parable of the physician. [As for] "the father young and the son old," up to "entering, emerging from, and abiding in hundreds of thousands of samādhis," how can it be said that they were converted by the Buddha at Gayā? Gathering this buddha-Dharma, one only begins to leave evil conditions, does not speak of others' good and bad, strengths and faults, and so on, and only then sees in a dream that one will enter the various stages. Hence the "Lifespan" chapter speaks only of the Tathāgata's original ground accomplished long ago and of his retinue from the distant past; how should they turn back and study the beginning practice within the trace? It is like an old man of eighty who, to teach his great-great-grandson, has him read the Classic of Filial Piety; how could he make his son turn back and read this book?

Chinese Source

又分別功德但論聞於長壽獲益經文，何曾令學四種之行？縱八界塵是初發心亦是聞遠，非聞安樂行而發心也。隨喜功德彼自挍量，第五十人以淺況深，淺法以為法師品本，不輕自明往弘一句得淨六根，亦非專修四安樂行。若通方云引於法華，此乃可爾。又學無學已前諸品，既是一實唯為聲聞，豈令地踊久行菩薩反學聲聞入實行耶？究竟不知何為六品所學行耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Moreover, the "Distinguishing Merits" [chapter] discusses only the sūtra text on obtaining benefit by hearing of the long lifespan; when did it ever make them study the four kinds of practice? Even [the beings equal to] the dust of eight worlds who first aroused the mind did so on hearing of the distant [past]; they did not arouse the mind by hearing of the peaceful practices. The "Merits of Rejoicing" [chapter] makes its own comparison: it uses the fiftieth person, the shallow, to illustrate the deep, and the shallow dharma is taken as the basis for the "Teacher of the Dharma" chapter. "Never Disparaging" itself makes clear that by propagating a single phrase in the past he attained the purification of the six faculties; this too was not exclusively cultivating the four peaceful practices. If you say in a general way that [these chapters] are drawn from the Lotus, that may be so. Moreover, the chapters before "Learners and Those Beyond Learning" are the one real [vehicle] solely for the śrāvakas; how could you make the bodhisattvas who welled up from the earth, long in practice, turn back and study the practice by which śrāvakas enter the real? In the end I do not know: what is the practice that the six chapters study?

Chinese Source

又曰：論云：為七慢人中第六集功德人，謂有功德人說大乘法，而取非大乘增上慢，為此人說髻中明珠。釋云：求三乘果者，名集功德人。昔日總相說於大乘，彼心未定，退取二乘，取非大乘，得功勳小，但賜餘物。今謂論中直云非大乘，不云小者，非大之言，義兼大中漸次等也。兼取安樂弘經之行，是非大乘之對治也。應取此行，退取於小，即非大乘也。又論但云：說大乘法，以此法門同十地滿，諸佛如來密與授記。乃釋云：令二乘同十地行，佛密授記。今謂論說初心四安樂行同十地滿，即是發心、畢竟二不別也。義同賜珠，如十地滿，初心一句尚堪記故，況弘經者而不記耶？此經二乘顯露得記，是微密之密，非祕密也。

Ru-Yi Translation

It further says: "The Treatise says: 'For the sixth of the seven [kinds of] persons with overweening pride, the persons who gather merit — that is, persons with merit to whom the Great Vehicle Dharma is taught, but who take what is not the Great Vehicle, with overweening pride — for these persons the bright jewel in the topknot is told.' The explanation says: 'Those who seek the fruits of the three vehicles are called persons who gather merit. In the past [the Buddha] taught the Great Vehicle in general marks; their minds were not settled, they retreated and took the two vehicles, took what is not the Great Vehicle; their meritorious achievement was small, and they were given only other things.'" Now I say: the Treatise says directly "not the Great Vehicle" and does not say "the small"; the words "not the great" in meaning include the great, the middle, the gradual, and so on. It also includes taking the practice of propagating the sūtra of the peaceful [practices]; this is the remedy for [taking] what is not the Great Vehicle. They ought to take this practice; retreating to take the small is precisely "not the Great Vehicle." Moreover, the Treatise says only: "The Great Vehicle Dharma is taught; by this Dharma gate they are the same as the completion of the ten grounds, and the buddha-tathāgatas secretly give them predictions." Yet [the other] explains: "It makes those of the two vehicles the same as the practice of the ten grounds, and the Buddha secretly gives predictions." Now I say: the Treatise says that the four peaceful practices of the initial mind are the same as the completion of the ten grounds; this is [the teaching that] the arousing of the mind and the ultimate are not two and not separate. The meaning is the same as bestowing the jewel: since [it is] the same as the completion of the ten grounds, even a single phrase in the initial mind is worthy of a prediction — how much more those who propagate the sūtra, are they not to be predicted? In this sūtra those of the two vehicles receive predictions openly; this is the secret of subtlety, not the secret of concealment.

問：四安樂中為有幾行？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: How many practices are there among the four peaceful [practices]?

Chinese Source

答曰：四行之中具有二行，但在初行中說之耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Within the four practices there are two practices, but they are taught only in the first practice."

Chinese Source

又云有二行：一妄，二真。應無所行，捨諸妄想。今謂初行觀於實相，實相不當空有真妄。四行既其總名安樂，安樂即是四行之果。一一行中具足四句，不當四句，以實相果非四故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

It also says: "There are two practices: first, the false; second, the true. One should practice nothing and abandon all false thoughts." Now I say: the first practice contemplates the true mark, and the true mark is not [a matter of] emptiness and existence, the true and the false. Since the four practices are collectively named "peace and happiness," peace and happiness is the fruit of the four practices. Each practice is complete with the four propositions yet is not [bound by] the four propositions, because the fruit of the true mark is not four.

Proper Conduct and Proper Company

問：經從菩薩至變現之戲者，何耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is [the sūtra] from "a bodhisattva" to "shape-shifting performances"?

Chinese Source

答曰：此中有三：一、損害緣；二、惡見緣；三、壞亂緣。菩薩雖為普化之緣，始行之人應為簡擇。今謂此中既云始行之人，何以分品云踊出修學？又云：律中入王宮者有十種過失：吳主同輦一代為榮，秦王讓妻千齡受恥。今謂吳主同輦永代光榮，秦王讓妻千齡莫測，何不自誡譏斥先賢？尼乾子離繫外道，既云是後六品之行，豈地踊眾須此誡耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Here there are three: first, conditions of harm; second, conditions of evil views; third, conditions of disruption. Though a bodhisattva is a condition for universal conversion, a person beginning practice should be selective." Now I say: since here you speak of persons beginning practice, why in dividing the chapters do you say [it is studied by those who] welled up? It also says: "In the Vinaya, entering the king's palace has ten faults: the lord of Wu sharing his carriage was an honor for one generation; the king of Qin yielding his wife was a shame for a thousand years." Now I say: the lord of Wu sharing his carriage is a glory for all ages; the king of Qin yielding his wife is unfathomable for a thousand years; why not caution yourself, instead of ridiculing and rebuking the former worthies? The Nirgranthas are the non-Buddhists of freedom from bonds. Since you say this is the practice of the latter six chapters, how would the host who welled up from the earth need this caution?

Chinese Source

言文筆者，謂詩歌之類為文，碑誌之屬為筆。言路伽耶陀者，先言惡答對人，正言路迦也，底迦云順世外道。逆路伽耶陀者，惡徵問人，正云縛摩路迦，夜底迦云左順世外道。執與前乖者名左順世也者，言貫上二處。那羅者，文畫其身變現。獻者，作幻術等。阿含云：智常懷憂如似獄囚，愚常喜悅猶如光音天。今謂此不許觀，何論作耶？扠，刃佳反，以拳加人。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "writings": poems and songs and the like are called 文 (literary writing); steles, epitaphs, and the like are called 筆 (prose writing). As for Lokāyata: formerly said to mean answering people with evil [words]; correctly it is lokāyatika, translated as the non-Buddhists who follow the world. Counter-Lokāyata: questioning people with evil challenges; correctly it is vāmalokāyatika, translated as the non-Buddhists who follow the world on the left. Those who hold [views] contrary to the former are called "left world-followers"; the word runs through the two places above. Nara: those who paint their bodies with designs and perform transformations. 戲 (performances): doing magic tricks and the like. The Āgama says: "The wise always harbor sorrow like prisoners in jail; the foolish are always delighted like the gods of Ābhāsvara." Now I say: here even watching is not permitted; how much less performing? 扠 (grappling): (pronunciation note); striking a person with the fist.

Chinese Source

經文云不親近至律儀者，旃陀羅，此云殺者，不律儀也。正云旃荼羅，此云嚴飾惡事，自嚴行持以為標幟，搖鈴持竹為自標故。此中舉六雜心十二，謂殺羊、養鷄、養猪、捕魚、捕鳥、獵師、作賊、魁膾、守獄、呪龍、屠牛犬、伺獵。涅槃十六，牛羊猪鷄為利故買肥已轉賣，為利故買已屠殺，即為八九釣魚、十獵師、十一劫奪、十二魁膾、十三捕鳥、十四兩舌、十五獄卒、十六呪龍。對法第八有十四：屠羊、養猪、捕鳥、捕魚、獵鹿、罝兔、劫盜、魁膾、害牛、縛象、立檀、呪龍、守獄、讒構、好為損等。正言意思廣重損害寔深。𩵾亦捕也，亦可作漁。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the sūtra text from "does not keep close company" to "evil observances": caṇḍāla, here translated "killer," is [one of] the non-restraints. Correctly it is caṇḍāla [in another transcription], here translated "adorned with evil deeds": they adorn themselves in conduct as a mark, ringing a bell and holding a bamboo [stick] as their own sign. Here six are listed. The Miscellaneous Heart has twelve, namely: killing sheep, raising chickens, raising pigs, catching fish, catching birds, hunters, thieves, executioners, prison guards, dragon charmers, butchering cattle and dogs, and lying in wait to hunt. The Nirvāṇa has sixteen: buying cattle, sheep, pigs, and chickens for profit, fattening them and reselling them, and buying them for profit and slaughtering them, making eight; ninth, catching fish; tenth, hunters; eleventh, robbers; twelfth, executioners; thirteenth, catching birds; fourteenth, double-tongued speech; fifteenth, jailers; sixteenth, dragon charmers. The Abhidharma, [fascicle] eight, has fourteen: slaughtering sheep, raising pigs, catching birds, catching fish, hunting deer, snaring rabbits, robbery, executioners, killing cattle, binding elephants, setting up altars, charming dragons, guarding prisons, slandering and plotting, delighting in doing harm, and so on. The correct sense is that [their] intention is broad and heavy and the harm is truly deep. 𩵾 also means to catch; it may also be written 漁 (to fish).

問：經從又親近至無所希求者，何耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is [the sūtra] from "Nor does he keep close company" to "expects nothing"?

Chinese Source

答曰：如大經云：菩薩怖畏二乘，如惜命人。如寂調音天子所問經云：佛告文殊：譬如有飢渴羸瘦，寧忍飢渴，不食雜毒之食。菩薩亦爾，寧慳嫉破戒，惡口懶墯，妄念無智，終不悕求二乘果地。佛告天子：如貧人食，是輪王毒。故二乘者，持戒精進，即是菩薩破戒懶墯，故不親近來者說法，不願親狹利養恭敬。今謂彼經譏刺彈斥，不同此經，不共住止，亦不捨離，故於是人不讚其美，不說其惡，故知與彼一向不同。於此二乘皆當作佛，故不與彼滅種經同。人不知之，徒引何益？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "As the Great Sūtra says: 'Bodhisattvas fear the two vehicles as a person who cherishes life [fears death].' As the Sūtra Asked by the God Calm-Tuned Sound says: 'The Buddha said to Mañjuśrī: "It is like someone hungry, thirsty, emaciated, and weak, who would rather endure hunger and thirst than eat food mixed with poison. Bodhisattvas are likewise: they would rather be stingy and jealous, break the precepts, use harsh speech, be lazy, have confused thoughts and no wisdom, than ever seek the ground of the fruits of the two vehicles." The Buddha said to the god: "As the food of a poor man is poison to a wheel-turning king."' Hence the precept-keeping and diligence of those of the two vehicles are precisely precept-breaking and laziness for a bodhisattva. Hence he does not keep close company, teaches the Dharma to those who come, and does not wish for intimacy, gain, offerings, or reverence." Now I say: that sūtra satirizes, censures, and rebukes [them], unlike this sūtra, [in which one] does not dwell together with them yet does not abandon them either; hence toward these persons one neither praises their merits nor speaks of their faults. Hence we know that it is entirely different from that one. Here those of the two vehicles will all become buddhas; hence it is not the same as those sūtras [that declare their] seeds destroyed. People do not know this; what is the benefit of citing it in vain?

問：經從文殊師利至以為親厚者，何耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is [the sūtra] from "Mañjuśrī" to "treating them as intimates"?

Chinese Source

答曰：有二：一、長染緣，二、非器緣。十誦云：譬如熟食，人所愛食。一切女人亦復如是，令人愛著，不能捨故。智論云：清風無形猶可捉，毒蛇含毒猶可觸，女人之心難可近，捉劒向歒猶可勝，女賊害人難可禁。四分律：數往俗家有五過：一、數見女人，二、數相親，三、轉相親狹，四、便生欲想，五、或時致死，次死苦惱。今謂大小乃殊，所防可爾。然須分別二教意殊：小為自行，此為弘經；彼為成小，此為棄小；彼唯制無開，此亡犯興利。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "There are two: first, conditions that prolong defilement; second, conditions of [those who are] not vessels. The Ten Recitations says: 'It is like cooked food, which people love to eat. All women are likewise: they make people love and cling to them, unable to let go.' The Great Treatise says: 'The pure wind has no form, yet it can still be caught; the venomous snake holds poison, yet it can still be touched; a woman's heart is hard to approach. Grasping a sword against an enemy, one can still win; the female thief harms people and is hard to restrain.' The Four-Part Vinaya: 'Frequently going to lay households has five faults: first, frequently seeing women; second, frequent mutual intimacy; third, growing ever more intimate; fourth, then giving rise to thoughts of desire; fifth, sometimes leading to death, or to the suffering and vexation next to death.'" Now I say: the great and the small are indeed different, though what they guard against may be [alike]. Yet one must distinguish that the intents of the two teachings differ: the small is for one's own practice, this is for propagating the sūtra; that is to accomplish the small, this is to abandon the small; that only prohibits and does not permit, this forgets [the matter of] offense and brings about benefit.

問：從不獨入他家至不樂與同師者，何耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is [the sūtra] from "He does not enter another's house alone" to "nor in sharing a teacher with them"?

Chinese Source

答曰：此有三：一、譏呵緣；二、非軌緣；三、散亂緣。瑜伽論云：應離十四垢業，藏隱六方，遠四惡友，攝四善友。言十四者，長含云：身語惡業有四，謂婬、殺、盜、妄；惡因有四，謂貪、瞋、癡、慢。離六損財法：一、躭酒；二、愽戲；三、放蕩；四、迷著伎樂；五、惡友相損；六、漸隨彼說。今謂此乃常誡於安樂行，其事太麤，豈安樂行者有酒愽耶？藏隱六方者，如長含云：羅閱城中有長者子，名善生，積世相傳，清旦禮六方，令六神常護。父死後，忽然值佛，問：有法義不？佛言：有，與汝不同。父母為東方，師長為南方，妻子為西方，親友為北方，僕使為下方，沙門高德為上方。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Here there are three: first, conditions of reproach; second, conditions of improper conduct; third, conditions of distraction. The Yogācāra Treatise says: 'One should leave the fourteen defiling actions, conceal and protect the six directions, stay far from the four evil friends, and gather the four good friends.' As for the fourteen, the Long Āgama says: 'Bad actions of body and speech are four, namely lust, killing, stealing, and lying; bad causes are four, namely greed, anger, delusion, and pride. Leave the six ways of losing wealth: first, indulging in liquor; second, gambling; third, dissipation; fourth, infatuation with music and entertainers; fifth, evil friends who do harm; sixth, gradually following what they say.'" Question: The sūtra says "Good sons" up to "never false"; what is its meaning?

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Now I say: first [the sūtra] speaks of the long-ago accomplishment; after the long-ago accomplishment, what manifested in between is all Śākyamuni manifesting as himself and as others; why must "another's body" point to Mahābhijñā? Moreover, Mahābhijñā is a near matter, and what he manifested was not much; it is not enough to fully reveal the traces in between. If his own and others' bodies were only like this, why not continue directly from before and say it? Why first raise doubt by means of the retinue, withholding [it] back and forth, before relating the original matter? Hence his own and others' affairs, whether bodies or teachings, do not go beyond the three bodies; then every buddha is so, and in principle it is easy to show; why point only to the response-body's own and other? It is also like an infant who does not yet know who is honored and who is lowly, and says his parents are the same age as himself; whenever he speaks, he talks of what he himself has seen. Later the child relays his father's words, and again takes [what happened] before he was born to be an account of [what happened] after his birth. Would not those who know laugh at it, and the neighbors argue hotly? If the Buddha himself had no distant lifespan, he might point to others as fitting; how can the ultimate teaching of the whole lifetime instead be made into a teaching that fits capacities?

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The sūtra says from "Why is this so?" to "without the slightest error"; what is it?

Chinese Source

答曰：證法身故，然真理本無此事也。今謂如此等文，須附先德，改頭換後，何益者耶？但依論文，堪為準的。論云：三界相者，說眾生界即涅槃界，不離眾生界有如來藏。第二句，論云：無有生死若退若出，謂常恒清淨不變故也。第三句者，論云：亦無在世及滅度者者，謂如來藏真如之體，不即眾生界，不離眾生界，非實非虗，非如非異者，論云：謂離四種相故，四種相者是常住故。不如三界見於三界者，論云：如來能證能見真如常住，法身凡夫不能見故。如來明見，無有錯謬。但依此解，其理自深。若信餘談法華非妙，仍須簡云：此是本地，實成如來所證所知故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Because he realizes the Dharma-body; yet true principle originally has no such affairs." Now I say: texts like this must rely on the former worthies; changing the head and replacing the tail, what benefit is there? Simply relying on the Treatise text is adequate as a standard. The Treatise says: "The mark of the three realms: it teaches that the realm of beings is the realm of nirvāṇa; the tathāgatagarbha is not apart from the realm of beings." The second phrase — the Treatise says: "There is no birth and death, no retreating and no emerging: that is, because it is eternal, constant, pure, and unchanging." The third phrase — the Treatise says: "Nor dwelling in the world nor attaining extinction: that is, the substance of the tathāgatagarbha, suchness, is not identical to the realm of beings and not apart from the realm of beings." "Not real and not unreal, not thus and not otherwise" — the Treatise says: "That is, because it is apart from the four kinds of marks; [apart from] the four kinds of marks because it is eternally abiding." "Not as the three realms see the three realms" — the Treatise says: "The Tathāgata can realize and see suchness eternally abiding; the Dharma-body cannot be seen by ordinary beings." The Tathāgata sees clearly, without error. Simply relying on this explanation, its principle is deep of itself. If one believes other talk, the Lotus is not wondrous; still one must specify: this is what is realized and known by the Tathāgata of the original ground who really accomplished [buddhahood].

For example, a recent explanation of the first phrase says: "Like the 'Skillful Means' chapter's 'the marks of the world abide constantly.'" This may be so. Explaining the second phrase it says: "Retreating means transmigration; emerging means getting out; 'birth and death' means that the dharma of birth and death has retreating and emerging." Now I say: this explanation [makes] the Tathāgata only now emerge from segmented birth-and-death, entirely the same as the two vehicles; what is there to praise? Explaining the fourth phrase it says: "The mark of arising is really existent, the mark of ceasing is nonexistent, the mark of abiding is 'thus,' the mark of changing is 'otherwise'; the Dharma-body has none of the four, hence all four are negated." Now I say: that the Dharma-body is not the four is truly so in principle, but what is negated is not deep; how can what negates reach far? Only if the four marks are made to extend to change [birth-and-death] does the Dharma-body accord with eternal abiding.

問：經云諸善男子至復倍上數，其義何耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The sūtra says "Good sons" up to "twice the number already given"; what is its meaning?

Chinese Source

答：本立誓願，度眾生盡，方入無餘。生無盡期，故壽不盡，復倍上數。今謂論釋三身具足，如何唯說化身？經意唯論過去，何得輙指未來？況中間示生，凡經幾度，示入無餘？只緣眾生尚多，所以數唱生滅。豈獨應身不滅，與生俱窮？今現告終，應當生盡。若然，又復言其入於涅槃，則是眾生已盡不耶？論文但云我本行菩薩道，今猶未滿者，以本願故，生界未盡，願非究竟。言未滿者，非謂菩提。是故論云所成壽命今未盡者，示現如來常住壽命，不云應身住世不盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Originally he made a vow to deliver all beings and only then enter [nirvāṇa] without remainder. Beings have no end, hence his lifespan does not end — 'twice the number above.'" Now I say: the Treatise explains [it] as the three bodies complete; how can you speak only of the transformation-body? The sūtra's intent discusses only the past; how can you point to the future? Moreover, in between, showing birth, how many times did he show entering [nirvāṇa] without remainder? Only because beings are still many does he repeatedly proclaim birth and extinction. How could only the response-body not become extinct, ending together with beings? Now he announces his end; then beings ought to be exhausted. If so, when it further says he enters nirvāṇa, are beings then already exhausted or not? The Treatise text says only: "I originally practiced the bodhisattva path, and even now it is not yet fulfilled" — because of the original vow, the realm of beings is not exhausted, and the vow is not ultimate. "Not fulfilled" does not refer to bodhi. Therefore the Treatise says: "The lifespan attained is not exhausted even now" — showing the Tathāgata's eternally abiding lifespan; it does not say the response-body dwells in the world without end.

Proclaiming Extinction and the Physician

Chinese Source

問：經云所以者何至網中者，其文如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The sūtra says from "Why is this so?" to "net"; what is that text?

Chinese Source

答：此二，初明本性惡，後明更生惡。此初惡有六：一者薄德，離勝人法；二不種善根，無因感果；三貧無財法，志意下劣；四者下賤，自輕劣故；五者著欲，煩惱昏醉；六入憶想見網，邪詐聰明，錯行亂學。今謂若云性者，應一切皆然，豈一切人並具斯六？故與後六不須別分。次若見如來至恭敬之心。即更生惡，復有五也：一憍恣放逸；二懷厭，不樂親近；三懷懈怠，不能自察；四不生難遭想，見佛不生悕遇之心；五不生恭敬心，不生渴慕心。今謂此段由不唱滅，於前六上更生此五。若唱滅已，前六存耶？經文並是唱滅之由，何須分擗性、生二種？以後望前，後五亦性，性憍恣故，性不敬故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "This is two: first making clear evils by nature, then making clear evils newly arising. The first evils are six: first, thin merit, apart from the dharma of superior persons; second, not planting good roots, no cause to bring about the fruit; third, poor, without the wealth of the Dharma, inferior in will; fourth, lowly, despising themselves; fifth, attached to desire, drunk and dazed by afflictions; sixth, entering the net of vain thoughts and false views, crafty and deceitfully clever, practicing wrongly and studying in confusion." Now I say: if you say "by nature," it should be so for everyone; how could everyone have all six? Hence there is no need to separate these from the latter six. "Next, from 'should they see that the Tathāgata' to 'a reverent heart' are evils newly arising, again five: first, arrogance and self-indulgence; second, harboring weariness, not delighting in drawing near; third, harboring indolence, unable to examine themselves; fourth, not giving rise to a sense of how rare an encounter is, seeing the Buddha without giving rise to a mind longing to meet him; fifth, not giving rise to a reverent heart, not giving rise to a thirsting heart." Now I say: [you say] this section [describes how], because he does not proclaim extinction, these five further arise on top of the former six. Once extinction has been proclaimed, do the former six remain? The sūtra text is all the reason for proclaiming extinction; why divide the two kinds, by nature and arising? Viewing the former from the latter, the latter five are also by nature, because by nature they are arrogant and self-indulgent, because by nature they are irreverent.

Reading Notes for Part 7

Translator's notes

Voices. As in earlier parts, "Answer" reports Ji (Fahua xuanzan, T1723, fascicles 9–10) and "Now I say / Now I ask" is Zhanran. Where Ji's text continues after one of Zhanran's objections without a marker, it is put in double quotation marks (units 65, 67, 79). In unit 53 a "Question:" inside Zhanran's discussion introduces a Treatise passage raised as a question; it is kept as "Question:". In unit 67 the sentence "Examine this division by the grounds…" has no 今謂 but is clearly Zhanran's comment and stands outside the quotation marks.

The Treatise on the Lotus. The passages quoted (units 25, 53, 67, 85, 87, 95) match Bodhiruci's version, T1520 (e.g., "Ninth, showing that nirvāṇa is unsurpassed…"; "Tenth… the remaining sūtra"), more closely than T1519. The five sentences in unit 85 explaining "the mark of the three realms" follow the Treatise line by line.

Lotus quotations. The wording follows the site's Lotus Sūtra: The Four Peaceful Practices ("in the evil age to come," "keep close company," "treating them as intimates," "nor in sharing a teacher with them"); Bodhisattvas from Beneath the Earth ("felt that no more than half a day had passed," "more than forty years," the man of twenty-five with a son of a hundred); The Tathāgata's Lifespan ("truthful words," "secret power of spiritual penetration," "a distinction made by skillful means," "Throughout this span of time, I have spoken of Dīpaṃkara Buddha," "the mark of the three realms," "without the slightest error," "twice the number already given," "the net of vain thoughts and false views"). Where Zhanran or Ji quotes loosely (unit 33 "like the space of a meal"; unit 42 "one does not age and does not die"), the quoted wording is translated.

Kings of Wu and Qin (unit 8). T1723 reads "the king of Wu (吳王) sharing his carriage … the lord of Qin (秦主) yielding his wife," as an illustration of the ten faults of entering a king's palace; Zhanran's copy swaps 主 and 王. The historical allusions were not identified.

Lokāyata (unit 8). The glosses 路迦也底迦 and 縛摩路迦夜底迦 are transcriptions of lokāyatika and vāmalokāyatika; CBETA's punctuation splits them. The translation reads them as single words.

Sources located. The Sūtra Asked by Calm-Tuned Sound (unit 10) is T1490 (寂調音所問經), where the "hungry and thirsty" passage is found. The four wheel-turning kings (unit 20) are quoted as in T1723. Bodhiruci's verse on the Buddha's ages (unit 40) is in T1723 fascicle 9. The Long Āgama story of Sujāta (Siṅgālaka) bowing to the six directions (unit 14) is the Shansheng jing (善生經) in the Dīrgha Āgama (T1, sūtra 16); Zhanran's summary was not checked against it. The "wise always harbor sorrow" saying (unit 8) was not located.

Dates of the Buddha (unit 40). Ji gathers conflicting figures for leaving home (19 or 29) and awakening (30 or 35). Zhanran's answer is that the schools differ because of their views, and that this is why the Tiantai scheme adds the indeterminate and secret teachings to the gradual one.

The four secrets and four intentions (units 65, 79). Ji cites the Abhidharmasamuccaya (對法) and the Mahāyānasaṃgraha scheme of four secrets (令入／相／對治／轉變) and four intentions (平等／別時／別義／眾生意樂). On this reading, the Buddha's naming of Dīpaṃkara and others could be "the intention of equality," speaking of other buddhas as himself. "I was once named Vipaśyin" (unit 65, 我曾名勝) is the standard example of the intention of equality; the Chinese gives only 勝.

Three thousand and five hundred dust-motes (unit 75). Zhanran refers to the two dust-mote measures of the Lotus: the three-thousand-dust-mote eons of the "Phantom City" chapter and the five-hundred-dust-mote eons of the "Lifespan."

The physician's twelve phrases (unit 95). "The remaining sūtra" (餘殘修多羅) is the Treatise's tenth unsurpassed item. Zhanran reads "remaining" as the rest of the main teaching, not as dissemination.

Probable copying errors, translated as corrected. Unit 55: 呪復不云 is read as 況復 ("still more"). Unit 53: 今古誦迷津 ("ancients and moderns recite while lost at the ford") is translated literally. Unit 69 ends in mid-sentence with a comma ("the three rounds would be set out in vain,"), as in CBETA.
