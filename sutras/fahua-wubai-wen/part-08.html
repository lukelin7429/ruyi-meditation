From Distinguishing Merits to Samantabhadra's Encouragement

Part 8 completes the Treatise of Five Hundred Questions. It runs from the "Distinguishing Merits" chapter to "Samantabhadra's Encouragement." It ends with two colophons and a later postscript. Zhanran continues to reject Ji's placing of the origin gate in the dissemination section. He argues that the benefits listed after the "Lifespan" chapter are part of the main teaching, and that Ji's contrast between predictions for śrāvakas and the realizations of bodhisattvas rests on a misreading of origin and trace. Shorter disputes follow: the acceptance of non-arising, the twelve hundred and eight hundred merits of the six faculties, and Never Disparaging's bowing. For the bowing he rejects Ji's appeal to lineage and to the rules for monks bowing to laypeople. He also discusses the spiritual powers of the "Spiritual Power" chapter, stinginess in the "Entrustment," and the comparison of Avalokiteśvara's merit with that of the buddhas. The treatise closes with Zhanran's complaint that Ji's commentary nowhere establishes a contemplation based on the sūtra. The colophons record the copying of the text at Guoqing Monastery in 839, its transmission to Japan, a recopying in 1272, and an Edo-period edition of 1795.

Auxiliary or Principal: The Intent of the Chapter

Chinese Source

問：品初來意，其相云何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What are the features of the intent of [its] coming, at the beginning of the chapter?

Chinese Source

答：初意者，一者二品傍釋踊出疑。今謂踊出乃是開壽量端，當知為壽量故踊出，踊出故生疑。不應却以踊出為正，亦由不了長遠特出，故使分文詺章前却。若爾，亦應身子懷疑為正，三周與記為傍。涅槃經初二十六問為正，佛答迦葉為傍。淨名長者獻葢為正，佛答淨土因果為傍。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "As for the first intent: first, [these] two chapters explain, as auxiliary, the doubt of 'Welling Up.'" Now I say: "Welling Up" is the starting point that opens the "Lifespan"; know that the welling up was for the sake of the "Lifespan," and because of the welling up doubt arose. One should not instead take "Welling Up" as principal; this too is because you do not understand that the long and distant [lifespan] stands out uniquely, so that in dividing the text you name the chapters back and forth. If so, then Śāriputra's harboring doubt should also be principal and the three rounds of giving predictions auxiliary; the twenty-six questions at the beginning of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra principal, and the Buddha's answers to Kāśyapa auxiliary; in the Vimalakīrti, the elder's offering of the canopies principal, and the Buddha's answer on the causes and fruits of pure lands auxiliary.

Chinese Source

問：此中正因地踊出疑，佛為釋疑，豈得為正？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Here, properly because of the doubt over those who welled up from the earth, the Buddha explains the doubt; how can it be principal?

Chinese Source

答：此中前說權實兩佛、理智二身，今謂應當涅槃，純陀論議因茲發問，承此佛答亦不名正。淨名合葢亦復如是，分別功德因聞長壽辨得益異，故亦屬正，故知他判傍正翻倒。故論云：一者證，如經我說如來壽命長遠時等，前為發起後辨得益，故並為正，壽量定非流通故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Here, earlier it taught the two buddhas of the provisional and the real, the two bodies of principle and wisdom." Now I say: [then] it should be that in the Nirvāṇa, [where] Cunda's discussion raised questions on this account, the Buddha's answers in response to this would also not be called principal; the joining of the canopies in the Vimalakīrti is likewise. "Distinguishing Merits," on hearing of the long lifespan, distinguishes the differences in benefit obtained; hence it too belongs to the principal. Hence we know his judgment of auxiliary and principal is inverted. Hence the Treatise says: "First, realization, as in the sūtra: 'When I taught the Tathāgata's lifespan to be long and distant,'" and so on. The earlier [chapter] initiates and the later distinguishes the benefit obtained; hence both are principal, because the "Lifespan" is definitely not dissemination.

Ru-Yi Translation

That master also says: "It gathers the vast foundation of the myriad virtues and binds the ultimate substance of the three clarities; those who hear it awaken in accord, those who fuse with it realize the path." Now I say: how could the vast foundation of the myriad virtues and the ultimate substance of the three clarities surpass the conversion of the ten directions and three times and so on? Hence the "Skillful Means" chapter has already made it clear extensively. The parable and the causes and conditions [went on] a second and a third time; in the "Herbs" chapter the Buddha had already related and confirmed it. This [chapter] only makes clear the distant; it is not exclusively the vast foundation; though it says "originally practiced the bodhisattva path" and so on, how could it surpass the skillful means, the pāramitās, and so on? How is it only here that the vast foundation is praised? The stages of benefit obtained are many times those before; people do not see this and tire themselves in vain.

又論云：此品已去法力有五門：一證、二信、三供養、四聞法、五讀誦持說。證謂菩薩聞於壽量，有十一位證聖不同。信謂八界塵數發心信解。供養謂天雨華等。上之三門在此品中。後二長行偈頌并隨喜品，聞法歡喜讀誦持說謂法師功德品。論自解云：初四門彌勒品示現，皆告彌勒故；後一門常精進品示現，唯告常精進故。今謂由證信之功大，故獻供之事廣。然三周中唯法說末八部四眾散衣奏樂，諸天雨華其事猶微，此中所供養橫至八方豎極梵世，故知本門事重報德不輕，判為流通全不可也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, the Treatise says: "From this chapter on, the power of the Dharma has five gates: first, realization; second, faith; third, offering; fourth, hearing the Dharma; fifth, reading, reciting, upholding, and teaching." Realization means that the bodhisattvas, hearing of the lifespan, realized sagehood at eleven different stages. Faith means that [beings equal to] the dust of eight worlds aroused the mind and believed and understood. Offering means the gods raining flowers and so on. The three gates above are within this chapter. As for the latter two, the prose and verses and the "Rejoicing" chapter [are] hearing the Dharma with joy; reading, reciting, upholding, and teaching is the "Merits of the Dharma Teacher" chapter. The Treatise itself explains: "The first four gates are shown in the Maitreya chapters, because they are all addressed to Maitreya; the last gate is shown in the Constantly-Diligent chapter, because it is addressed only to Constantly-Diligent." Now I say: because the merit of realization and faith is great, the matter of making offerings is broad. Yet in the three rounds, only at the end of the teaching by Dharma did the eight classes and the fourfold assembly scatter robes and play music and the gods rain flowers; the matter was still slight. Here the offerings extend horizontally to the eight directions and vertically to the Brahmā world; hence we know that the matter of the origin gate is weighty and the repaying of virtue is not light; judging it as dissemination is entirely impossible.

又論云：次修行力攝十一品：一法師、二持、三安樂行、四神力、五藥王、六妙音、七觀音、八陀羅尼、九嚴王、十普賢、十一囑累，皆屬第十勝力無上。而勝妙力無上中乃兼本正，故論不立流通之言，等用論文何不盡依？故知論文勝妙之言兼於二段，是故無上勝妙等言並是歎於經部故也。故別簡三品餘名修行，故不應以壽量之文而為流通，故修行言屬在利他弘經之相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, the Treatise says: "Next, the power of practice includes eleven chapters: first, the Teacher of the Dharma; second, Upholding; third, Peaceful Practices; fourth, Spiritual Power; fifth, Bhaiṣajyarāja; sixth, Wondrous Sound; seventh, Avalokiteśvara; eighth, Dhāraṇī; ninth, King [Wondrous] Adornment; tenth, Samantabhadra; eleventh, Entrustment — all belonging to the tenth, the unsurpassed excellent power." Yet the unsurpassed excellent and wondrous power includes both the origin and the main [section]; hence the Treatise does not set up the word "dissemination." Since you equally use the Treatise text, why not follow it fully? Hence we know the Treatise text's words "excellent and wondrous" include both sections; therefore words such as "unsurpassed" and "excellent and wondrous" all praise the sūtra as a whole. Hence it separately specifies three chapters and names the rest "practice"; hence one should not take the text of the "Lifespan" as dissemination; hence the word "practice" belongs to the mark of propagating the sūtra to benefit others.

Predictions for Śrāvakas and Bodhisattvas

Chinese Source

問：何名功德？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is meant by "merit" (功德)?

Chinese Source

答：功謂功勞聞法力用，德謂道德分別可知。今謂雖作此釋，不辨與前功勞共別，將何以簡與三周異？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "功 means meritorious effort, the power and function of hearing the Dharma; 德 means the virtue of the path, which can be known by distinguishing." Now I say: though you make this explanation, you do not distinguish how it is common with or different from the meritorious effort earlier; by what will you distinguish it from the three rounds?

問：有人問云：云何聲聞聞實權而領悟乃為授記，菩薩聞真化而解知不為授記？有人

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Someone asked: "Why is it that śrāvakas, hearing of the real and the provisional and understanding, are then given predictions, while bodhisattvas, hearing of the true and the transformation and understanding, are not given predictions?" Someone

Chinese Source

答云：菩薩之記佛已數彰，但說證因不說當果。今謂何以不明所聞之法與迹不同，而但辨能聞得益差別？況辨差別但論因果，何故不云法記應記二相不同，不辨淺深與平等異？又亦不明生數所以，況辨能聞豈專因果？前聲聞中供佛為因，安得獨果？後菩薩中當得是果，豈但專因？況聲聞領悟俱成菩薩，此更獲益其位彌深，況壽量中亦明中間處處權實，前三周中佛乘即是真化故也。亦由不了本迹之旨，故二處領悟俱屬正宗咸名授記，亦不應判記不記別。故知若不了於本門深遠，則真化何謝於實權？小悟何劣於大解？若解本妙與迹永殊，則真化之言有歸、權實之語有據。又若云聲聞與菩薩得記，但言有劫國無劫國等異者，此則可然，以聲聞人爾前未曾與其授記，故以劫定之。

Ru-Yi Translation

answered: "The bodhisattvas' predictions the Buddha has made manifest many times; [here] he speaks only of the realized cause, not of the future fruit." Now I say: why do you not make clear that the dharma heard differs from the trace, but only distinguish the differences in benefit obtained by those who hear? Moreover, in distinguishing the differences you discuss only cause and fruit; why not say that the two marks, the prediction of the Dharma [body] and the prediction of the response [body], differ, and why not distinguish deep and shallow as different from equality? Nor do you make clear the reason for the number of lives. Moreover, in distinguishing the hearers, how could it be only cause and fruit? Earlier, among the śrāvakas, making offerings to buddhas is the cause; how could it be only fruit? Later, among the bodhisattvas, what they will attain is the fruit; how could it be only cause? Moreover, the śrāvakas' understanding made them all bodhisattvas; here, further gaining benefit, their stage is ever deeper. Moreover, in the "Lifespan" too it makes clear the provisional and the real everywhere in between; in the three rounds earlier, the buddha vehicle is precisely the true and the transformation. This too is because you do not understand the purport of origin and trace; hence the understandings in both places belong to the main section and are all called predictions; one should not judge prediction and non-prediction to be different. Hence we know: if one does not understand the depth and distance of the origin gate, how would the true and the transformation differ from the real and the provisional? How would small awakening be inferior to great understanding? If one understands that the wonder of the origin is forever different from the trace, the words "true and transformation" have their home, and the words "provisional and real" have their basis. Again, if you say that the predictions of śrāvakas and bodhisattvas differ only in that some have eons and lands named and some do not, that may be so, because the śrāvakas had never been given predictions before this; hence they are fixed by eons.

問：有人問：云何因說乘權實，菩薩記證雙無；說身真化，聲聞記證雙闕？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Someone asked: "Why is it that, when the provisional and the real of the vehicles were taught, the bodhisattvas lacked both prediction and realization, [and] when the true and the transformation of the body were taught, the śrāvakas lacked both prediction and realization?"

Chinese Source

乃答云：菩薩無取捨，聲聞未證故。今謂若以佛果為證，菩薩亦無；若以小果為證，則聲聞已得；若以期佛而為取捨，菩薩反有；若以見取為取，二乘先無；若以證位為證，菩薩先有；若以佛果為證，菩薩亦無。是故問答俱非，徒費言論。然教內之事，何以存而不言？說身真化，聲聞已為菩薩，菩薩得益，何殊聲聞益耶？說乘權實，菩薩非無記證，故法師品首云皆與記。與記必證，不得云無。故知三周雖語聲聞，義沾菩薩。所以至法師品重述向因，以少況多，令多可識。聲聞根鈍，須委言之。菩薩先聞，未假劫國。若云兩雙互闕，乃是迷旨惑名。故知菩薩先知權實，聞身何妨更證？聲聞未聞本化，聞已理合道增。若菩薩取捨先無，聞身何得生損？何以不問經首但列八萬人俱，至此聞法益中而數倍千萬？

Ru-Yi Translation

[He] then answers: "Bodhisattvas have no taking and rejecting; the śrāvakas have not yet realized." Now I say: if buddhahood is taken as realization, the bodhisattvas do not have it either; if the small fruit is taken as realization, the śrāvakas have already obtained it; if anticipating buddhahood is taken as taking and rejecting, the bodhisattvas on the contrary have it; if the grasping of views is taken as grasping, those of the two vehicles lacked it before; if the stage of realization is taken as realization, the bodhisattvas had it before; if buddhahood is taken as realization, the bodhisattvas do not have it either. Therefore question and answer are both wrong, wasting words in vain. Yet why are matters within the teaching kept back and not spoken of? When the true and the transformation of the body were taught, the śrāvakas had already become bodhisattvas; how does the bodhisattvas' gaining of benefit differ from the śrāvakas' benefit? When the provisional and the real of the vehicles were taught, the bodhisattvas were not without prediction and realization; hence the beginning of the "Teacher of the Dharma" chapter says that all are given predictions. Being given a prediction necessarily [implies] realization; one cannot say there was none. Hence we know that although the three rounds speak of śrāvakas, in meaning they moisten the bodhisattvas too. Therefore in the "Teacher of the Dharma" chapter [the Buddha] again relates the former cause, using the few to illustrate the many, making the many recognizable. The śrāvakas' faculties are dull, so they must be told in detail; the bodhisattvas heard earlier and did not need eons and lands. If you say the two pairs mutually lack [something], this is being deluded about the purport and confused by names. Hence we know that the bodhisattvas knew the provisional and the real beforehand; on hearing of the body, why could they not further realize? The śrāvakas had not yet heard of the original conversion; having heard it, in principle their path should increase. If the bodhisattvas had no taking and rejecting beforehand, how could hearing of the body give rise to the reducing [of birth]? Why do you not ask why at the beginning of the sūtra only eighty thousand persons are listed, yet here, in the benefit of hearing the Dharma, they are many times thousands of myriads?

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Someone asked: "Why is teaching the provisional and the real to predict the śrāvakas the main section, [while] teaching the true and the transformation to predict the bodhisattvas belongs to dissemination?"

Chinese Source

乃答云：法華開秀，正對權實陳宗，不說真應，豈說身為宗旨？今謂大事因緣出現於世，豈非真應？三祇菩薩被會權開，豈非華秀？故知別說則迹華本蓮，總論乃二文咸具，雙題二字，豈可分張？深鑒之流，於茲取信。故知本迹雙美，華實敷榮，顯一代都無，總斯經咸妙。

[He] then answers: "The Lotus, opening its blossoms, properly sets forth its tenet in terms of the provisional and the real, and does not speak of the true and the response; how would it speak of the body as its cardinal purport?" Now I say: "The one great cause [for which buddhas] appear in the world" — is it not the true and the response? The bodhisattvas of the three asaṃkhyeyas were merged and the provisional opened; is this not the flower blossoming? Hence we know that, speaking separately, the trace is the flower and the origin the lotus; speaking generally, both texts have both. The two characters of the title together — how could they be split? Those of deep discernment may take faith from this. Hence we know that origin and trace are both excellent, flower and fruit flourish together; manifesting [what] the whole lifetime entirely lacked, the whole of this sūtra is wondrous.

The Acceptance of Non-Arising and the Seven Sections

Chinese Source

問：經云爾時至法輪者，何耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The sūtra says from "At that time" to "wheel of the Dharma"; what is it?

Chinese Source

答：初明證得，後明信發。論云：無生忍者，謂初地八生至一生者，諸凡夫決定能證初地，隨力隨分，八生乃至一生。證初地故者，前六門依位證，後五門依生證。而云無生者是境，無所執生故；法者是教，詮無生教。地前昔聞，未能智證，今始智證，名無生法忍。忍者，證義。今謂若準智論及大品云：於聲聞名果，於菩薩名忍。故忍者是因，讓佛從果，聲聞果促，處處有之。若立證名，與果何別？故生是所無，法是所證，證無生之法，位猶在因，故名為忍。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "First it makes clear realization and attainment, then the arising of faith. The Treatise says: 'The acceptance of non-arising: that is, the first ground, from eight lives up to one life — ordinary beings definitely able to realize the first ground, according to their strength and share, from eight lives up to one life. Because they realize the first ground': the first six gates realize according to stage, the latter five realize according to lives. 'Non-arising' is the object, because there is no grasped arising; 'dharmas' is the teaching, the teaching that explains non-arising. Before the grounds they heard it in the past but could not realize it with wisdom; now for the first time they realize it with wisdom; this is called the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas. 'Acceptance' means realization." Now I say: according to the Great Treatise and the Larger [Prajñā]: "For śrāvakas it is called fruit; for bodhisattvas it is called acceptance." Hence acceptance is the cause, yielding the fruit to the Buddha; the śrāvakas' fruit is short, and appears everywhere. If you set up the name "realization," how does it differ from fruit? Hence arising is what is negated, and dharmas are what is realized; realizing the dharma of non-arising, the stage is still in the cause; hence it is called acceptance.

Chinese Source

又曰：即三無生，名無生忍。徧計所執，名相無生；依他起生，名自然無生；圓成實性，惑苦無生。今謂若相無生，未除惑苦，何曰無生？依他未證圓成，則無自然之稱。若以淨法緣起為自然者，灼然須證圓理，方號自然。今謂應云：無遍計者，是見修無生；無依他者，是無知無生；證圓成者，是無明無生。次與不次，隨義準知。初地、初住，分得、全得，例餘可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

It further says: "This is the three [kinds of] non-arising, called the acceptance of non-arising: the imagined nature is the non-arising of marks; the dependent nature is the non-arising of self-so; the perfected nature is the non-arising of delusion and suffering." Now I say: if the marks are not arising but delusion and suffering are not removed, how can it be called non-arising? If the dependent has not realized the perfected, then there is no title "self-so." If the arising of pure dharmas from conditions is taken as self-so, clearly one must realize the perfect principle before it is called self-so. Now I say one should say: [realizing that there is] no imagined [nature] is the non-arising of [the delusions of] views and cultivation; no dependent [nature] is the non-arising of non-knowing; realizing the perfected is the non-arising of ignorance. Whether sequential or not can be known according to the meaning. The first ground and the first abiding, partial attainment and full attainment, can be known by analogy with the rest.

Tenth, the roars of lions and the like cannot startle it; bodhisattvas are likewise: non-Buddhists and others cannot startle them.'" It is not without a way, but it lacks [analysis] in terms of the teachings.

The ancients said: "The whole canon of the [Buddha's] lifetime is but a commentary on the single word 'mind.'" How much more [is this so of] the Lotus, the final and ultimate teaching: if not by contemplating the mind, by what can one enter the profound pass of origin and trace? Hence our school sets up four [kinds of] explanation, and further says that the first three, compared with contemplation, belong to events. My late teacher, the Venerable Tokuō, exhausted his strength in collating and correcting it, and where it was difficult and deep, he added brief annotations for the convenience of beginners. Translator's notes

Voices. "Answer" is Ji (T1723, fascicle 10); "Now I say / Now I ask" is Zhanran. In units 7–12 and 41–42, "Someone asked … [he] answers" reports questions Ji raises and answers in his own commentary. In unit 26 the closing sentences after Ji's second explanation ("Hence the original treatise says 'ordinary beings'; these words are most wondrous…") have no marker; they are left outside the quotation marks as Zhanran's, though they may continue Ji.

The Treatise on the Lotus. The five gates of the power of the Dharma (unit 4), "eight lives up to one life" (unit 14), the seven or ten spiritual powers (units 46–48), and the comparison of Avalokiteśvara with sixty-two koṭis of buddhas (unit 68) are in T1520. The phrase "seeking that my body be like Avalokiteśvara" is T1520's wording.

Never Disparaging (units 31–35). Ji's text (T1723) reads: "If a bodhisattva monk does not make this bow, he has an offense… bodhisattvas must keep [the precepts against] natural offenses, [but] may offend against prohibited offenses… bowing to the four assemblies is no offense, bowing to only one is deficient… he reveres the buddha-nature, not the body… newly trained monks bowed at Vimalakīrti's feet." Lotus quotations. The wording follows the site's Lotus Sūtra: The Merit of Believing (Ajita, "the wisdom knowing all modes"); Never Disparaging ("Every one of you practices the bodhisattva path, and shall attain buddhahood," "did not devote himself to reading and reciting the scriptures … but only to bowing," "a false prediction"); the Universal Gate (Akṣayamati, Dharaṇiṃdhara).
