The Preface and the Preliminary Gates: Occasion, Cardinal Purport, and the Opening of the Introduction

The Treatise of Five Hundred Questions on the Lotus (X0939) is Zhanran's (711–782) critique of the Profound Praise of the Lotus (Fahua xuanzan, T1723) by Ji (632–682), the Faxiang master of Ci'en Monastery. Its form is fixed: a question names a point of the Profound Praise; the "answer" reports Ji's position, usually in his own words; and Zhanran then objects ("Now I say," "Now I ask"), often several times as further passages of Ji are cited ("It further says"). This part opens with Rikunyo Jishū's preface to a Japanese reprint (1794), which notes that the work survived only in Japan and sums up its three charges: Ji reads the Lotus through the Yogācāra doctrine of five lineages, buries its purpose under classifications, and says nothing about practice. Zhanran's text then begins with Ji's preliminary gates: the occasion of the sūtra, the three periods of the Saṃdhinirmocana and the claim that the one vehicle is provisional, the one capacity versus the five natures, the three teachings and eight tenets, the names of the chapters, and the division of the text. The part ends inside the "Introduction" chapter, with "Thus I have heard," the assembly of arhats and bodhisattvas, and why the Immeasurable Meanings is taught first.

Chinese Source

五百問：論何為而造耶？曰：為俾破權執，悟實道也。然篤而論之，如來一代說大小權實教，每會必各有囑累。諸大羅漢弘演小宗，彌勒以權教為職，文殊大乘為任，等是荷擔菩提，莊嚴雙樹，其間豈容優劣哉？及至滅後，諸大祖師後先𮞏出，分宗立家，偏弘一門，葢亦各受密囑，乘願轂而來者也。若慈恩基師專據深密，主張瑜伽，以三時教該括一化，三無二有統轄眾機，其極遂至乎以一乘為權，三乘為實矣。斯旨也，始與我所弘法華之者戞戞乎不相容焉，則不得不作為玄贊，別立一家之說也。然竊忖其意，豈非木牛楊葉俯提弱喪，以餘深法示教利喜者耶？所謂世界故實、第一義故實，權誘方便皆無唐捐。但稟今宗教眼未明者，或眩其說，將泣兩岐。又彼宗徒牢執一途，或恐馴致蹋順璟之禍，又復焉知無或不幸而寶器受粗食者乎？是論主之所以深憂遠慮而有此造也歟？

Ru-Yi Translation

The Five Hundred Questions: for what purpose was this treatise composed? I answer: to cause [people] to break their attachment to the provisional and awaken to the real path. Yet, to discuss it earnestly: the Tathāgata, in his whole lifetime, taught the great and the small, the provisional and the real teachings, and at every assembly there was always an entrustment. The great arhats spread and expounded the small tradition; Maitreya took the provisional teaching as his charge, and Mañjuśrī the Great Vehicle as his responsibility. All alike shouldered bodhi and adorned the twin trees; how could there be any superior or inferior among them? After [the Buddha's] extinction, the great patriarchs burst forth one after another, dividing into traditions and founding houses, each spreading one gate exclusively; for each had received a secret entrustment and came riding on the hub of his vows. Master Ji of Ci'en relied solely on the Saṃdhinirmocana and championed the Yogācāra; with the teaching of the three periods he encompassed the whole [career of] transformation, and with "three without, two with" he governed all capacities; at its extreme this went so far as to take the one vehicle as provisional and the three vehicles as real. This purport, from the start, grates against the Lotus that we spread and cannot be reconciled with it; so he could not but compose the Profound Praise, separately setting up the doctrine of one house. Yet, when I privately weigh his intent, was it not [like] the wooden ox and the willow leaves, bending down to lift up the weak and the lost, and showing, teaching, benefiting, and gladdening them with the remaining deep dharmas? This is what is called "real by way of the worldly, real by way of the ultimate meaning": the provisional enticements and skillful means are none of them in vain. But those who receive the present school's teaching and whose eye is not yet clear may be dazzled by his doctrine and weep at the forked road. Moreover, followers of that school cling firmly to one track, and it is to be feared that they may gradually bring on the calamity of Shunjing, who trod [into the earth]; and further, how can one know that someone will not, by misfortune, be a jeweled vessel receiving coarse food? Is this not why the author of the treatise had deep worries and far-reaching concerns, and so composed this?

Ru-Yi Translation

Though its articles and chapters are many, its main essentials are three. First: the correct intent of opening and revealing is for the śrāvakas of fixed determination, yet [Ji] turns it around and assigns it to those with the nature. Using a treatise on rotten seeds to interpret a sūtra on reviving [them] — now that they have already been born, how can one cite a treatise to extinguish them? This is cutting the foot to fit the shoe, touching gold and turning it into iron; I do not know how it could be acceptable. Second: the essential point of the present sūtra lies in revealing the intent of the whole [career's] work of transformation; the outlines of the various dharma-gates are all yielded to the earlier sūtras. Yet he makes an empty display of names and marks, opens chapters and sections broadly, mixes the great and the small, and does not distinguish the present from the former, while the cause and condition of the great matter he sets aside without regard — buying the casket and returning the pearl; is this not perverse? Third: spreading [the teaching] in the final age is solely for practitioners; yet his tangled dharma-marks are as vast as a sea of mist, and not one word is seen to touch on the essentials of practice — asking about the bridge and debating the arrow, emptily wasting time. One morning, when he closes his eyes, on what will form and mind rely? This is abandoning what is urgent and not knowing what must be done. As for the rest — choices about matters and meanings, agreements and differences in glosses — these are of the secondary kind. With the three essentials laid out, it is hoped that followers of the [Tian]tai teaching will have something firm to hold, that those who study that school will be freed of their sticky attachments, and that some with the perfect seed will give rise to longing and turn toward it. It does not necessarily aim to clamor at everyone, making them all give up that and follow this. The main intention of the composition is simply thus.

此論智證大師之所將來，趙宋時彼方已失傳，最宜須寶護。獨奈舊刻鹵莾，訛謬相望，而復靡別本可讐校，慧心致疑，其來也久矣。每一披卷，未曾不憮然而增慨也。山門碩匠有守篤和尚，恒惋惜不置，乃據昔人所考覈及玄贊等，默索精思，嚴訂其譌，疑者不敢輒改，別為箋註三卷，論定是非，且略釋肯綮難通者。於是從來以為不可讀，委諸高閣者，皆喜而讀之，研之得益者寢多。其孫昌宗上人，銳志進學，稱不愧堂搆之任，比以和尚校本，壽之梨棗，仍以箋註分會入本論各章之下合刻之，將欲以公于大方，而揭論主之志於幾墜也。其為功為惠，亦豈小哉？

Ru-Yi Translation

This treatise was brought [to Japan] by the Great Master Chishō; in the time of the Zhao Song it had already been lost in that land, so it most needs to be treasured and protected. Unfortunately the old cutting was careless, with errors following one upon another, and there was no other copy against which to collate it; the wise have long had their doubts. Each time I opened the scroll, I never failed to grow sad and sigh the more. Among the great craftsmen of our mountain gate was the Venerable Shutoku, who constantly regretted this without cease. So, relying on what earlier people had examined and on the Profound Praise and so on, he searched silently and thought deeply, strictly corrected its errors — not daring to change rashly what was doubtful — and separately made annotations in three fascicles, settling right and wrong and briefly explaining the crucial points that were hard to understand. Thereupon those who had always thought it unreadable and left it on the high shelf all read it gladly, and those who studied it and gained benefit grew more and more numerous. His grandson, the Shōnin Shōshū, keen in his resolve to advance in learning, is said to be not unworthy of the task of building on the hall; recently he took the Venerable's collated text and had it carved on pear and jujube [blocks], further dividing the annotations and inserting them under each chapter of the treatise, cutting them together, intending to make it public to all and to raise up the author's intent, which had nearly fallen. Its merit and kindness — how could they be small?

Ru-Yi Translation

When the cutting was done, he asked me for a preface. Hearing this, I was very pleased; without measuring myself, I dare to set out my humble views thus, and to raise its essentials for it. The National Teacher Qingliang said: "The Lotus says: 'This sūtra is hard to believe and hard to understand; even while the Buddha is present in the world it meets with much enmity and jealousy — how much more so after his parinirvāṇa?' True indeed are these words! Those who hold to the three vehicles and the five natures and do not believe in the one vehicle and the one nature are deeply to be pitied." This sifts the provisional and the real without the slightest indulgence. It is what is called: on a mountain of treasure everything is treasure, but in taking it one first chooses the maṇi.

Ru-Yi Translation

The Great Master Chishō said: "Those who explain the Lotus — the three houses, the Three Treatises, the Dharma-characteristics, and Tiantai — are all one office of the Dharma-king and one gate of the dharma-realm; they can be wondrous medicine for beings, and fords and bridges for all the destinies." This is the four siddhāntas fitting the occasion, all the patriarchs being of one measure. It is what is called: on the Snow Mountains everything is medicine, but the gatherer aims at what matches the illness. If one makes different what is different and does not penetrate the great sameness, then in the ocean of harmony one divides the rivers to drink the water, only increasing views and pride; how is that different from the disputes of non-Buddhists? Even so, this is not yet [all]. The Great Master Zhizhe said: "[All this] is a skillful means of speech, calling something wondrous or not wondrous. At the moment principle is truly seen, there is no longer any provisional or real, neither provisional nor real, nor is there any wondrous or not-wondrous; it is for this reason that it is called the wondrous." Ah! What kind of realm is this! How may I, together with the sentient beings of the dharma-realm, reach this ground and receive the joy of the vast wondrous Dharma? If readers are fortunate enough to come to know this well, then the pains the Venerable Shutoku took and the diligence the Shōnin Shōshū applied will both not have been spent in vain. Then may this treatise circulate, flourishing by the day and by the month; this is my hope. How could I not rejoice greatly!

問：釋一經文前幾門？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Before explaining the text of the sūtra, how many gates [are set up]?

Chinese Source

答：開為六門：一、敘經緣起之意；二、明宗旨；三、明經品得名；四、明品廢立；五、彰品次第；六、釋經本文。今意謂之不然，除釋本文，餘之五門，初文略明緣起可爾，餘三明品在無，非經骨格。第二宗旨未曉如何，容子陳疑，方知遠旨。曾聞有用名、體宗、用教相等五稍，似得於此經旨歸。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: It is opened into six gates: first, narrating the intent of the sūtra's occasion; second, clarifying the cardinal purport; third, clarifying how the sūtra's chapters got their names; fourth, clarifying the establishing and abolishing of chapters; fifth, displaying the order of the chapters; sixth, explaining the sūtra's text proper. My present intent is to say that this is not so. Apart from explaining the text proper, of the remaining five gates the first, briefly clarifying the occasion, may be acceptable; the other three, which clarify whether chapters exist or not, are not the skeleton of the sūtra. As for the second, the cardinal purport, I do not yet understand how it is; allow me to set out my doubts, and then you will know the distant purport. I have heard that some use the five — name, essence, cardinal doctrine, function, and the marks of the teaching — which seems somewhat to grasp the ultimate purport of this sūtra.

The Occasion: Responding to Causes and Requests

Chinese Source

問：何謂緣起？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is meant by the occasion?

Chinese Source

答：緣起為五：一、酬因請，二、破疑執，三、為彰記行，四、為利今後，五、顯時機。初文有二：一、酬因，二、酬請。酬因又六：一、酬行因，即方便品初云佛曾至未曾有法。二、酬願因，亦方便品云我本立誓願及壽量中每自作是意等。三、酬求因，即達多品。四、酬持因，論云佛曾已下為讀誦甚深及後之四恒，即四依是，及不輕品受持之文。五、酬相因，謂雨華等彌勒疑問等。六、酬說因，三世諸佛成佛已後必說故也。今謂雖列六因，竝今昔大途之通因，而非出世本意之別因。經云若但讚佛乘，眾生沒在苦等四十餘秊不顯真實，今始得暢，而不為酬因，徒說久遠，何益於今？故今別因大唯二種：一、為迹門開權顯實，二、為本門開迹顯遠。此則正當一代別因，不同諸教。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The occasion is fivefold: first, responding to causes and requests; second, breaking doubts and attachments; third, displaying prediction and practice; fourth, benefiting the present and the later; fifth, revealing time and capacity. The first has two: first, responding to causes; second, responding to requests. Responding to causes is again sixfold. First, responding to the cause of practice: the passage at the beginning of the "Skillful Means" chapter from "The Buddha once" to "unprecedented dharmas." Second, responding to the cause of vows: again in the "Skillful Means" chapter, "I originally made a vow," and in the [chapter on the] Lifespan, "I always think this thought," and so on. Third, responding to the cause of seeking: the Devadatta chapter. Fourth, responding to the cause of upholding: the Treatise says that from "The Buddha once" on concerns reading and reciting the very profound, and the later four Ganges [sands], that is, the four reliances; together with the passage on receiving and upholding in the "Never Disparaging" chapter. Fifth, responding to the cause of signs: the raining of flowers and so on, Maitreya's doubting questions, and so on. Sixth, responding to the cause of teaching: because the buddhas of the three times, after attaining buddhahood, always teach [it]. Now I say: although six causes are listed, they are all general causes of the broad course of past and present, not the specific cause of the original intent in appearing in the world. The sūtra says, "If I only praise the buddha vehicle, beings sunk in suffering," and so on: for more than forty years the real was not revealed, and only now could it be fully expressed. Yet [you] do not take this as the cause responded to, but merely speak of the distant past; what benefit is that for the present? Therefore the present specific causes are, broadly, only two: first, in the trace gate, opening the provisional to reveal the real; second, in the origin gate, opening the trace to reveal the distant. These are precisely the specific causes of the whole [career], unlike the other teachings.

問曰：何謂酬請？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is meant by responding to requests?

Chinese Source

答曰：釋迦初成，梵王請說但說方便，今酬身子請說權實境，酬文殊請說安樂行，酬彌勒請說真應果。今謂一代大小皆酬梵請，身子所請通為三周，三周所陳豈獨於境？權在於昔安合重求？況法說周三世十方，皆令眾生得一切種智，乘是寶乘遊於四方，此豈非行？直至道場此豈非果？滅化寶所行果亦然，所談者教何以獨境？餘之二酬至文方決。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: When Śākya first attained buddhahood, King Brahmā requested [him to teach], yet he taught only skillful means. Now, responding to Śāriputra's request, he teaches the object of the provisional and the real; responding to Mañjuśrī's request, he teaches the peaceful practices; responding to Maitreya's request, he teaches the fruit of the true and responsive [bodies]. Now I say: the great and the small [teachings] of the whole career all respond to Brahmā's request. What Śāriputra requested extends to all three rounds; how could what is set out in the three rounds be only the object? The provisional lay in the past; why should it be sought again? Moreover, in the round of teaching by Dharma, the [buddhas of the] three times and ten directions all cause beings to attain the wisdom knowing all modes; "riding this jeweled vehicle, they roam in the four directions" — how is this not practice? "Straight to the seat of enlightenment" — how is this not the fruit? Extinguishing the phantom [city] at the place of treasure — practice and fruit are likewise. What is discussed is the teaching; why only the object? The other two responses will be settled when we reach their text.

Breaking Doubts and Attachments

Chinese Source

問：云何謂破疑？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is meant by breaking doubts?

Chinese Source

答曰：一、破疑；二、破執。初破疑者，聲聞疑謂永不成佛，故云感傷。諸小菩薩亦疑獨得，又疑菩提己亦無分。經云菩薩聞是法等，文兼破悔，疑小不得作佛。今聞作佛，悔所以除，知為大因，故疑通三乘、悔唯小有，以寬攝狹但說破疑。今謂疑通悔局，為出何文？文中既云諸求三乘若有疑悔，豈從一句便判疑寬？以寬攝狹應俱有悔。又諸小菩薩身住何位？初疑獨得、後疑無分，獨得與無分相違，疑悔焉分於大小？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: First, breaking doubts; second, breaking attachments. First, breaking doubts: the śrāvakas doubted, thinking they would never become buddhas; hence it says "grieved." The lesser bodhisattvas also doubted whether [the śrāvakas] alone would attain [it], and further doubted whether they themselves had no share in bodhi. The sūtra says, "The bodhisattvas, hearing this Dharma," and so on. The text also breaks regret: [those of] the small doubted that they could become buddhas; now, hearing that they will become buddhas, their regret is thereby removed, knowing [this] to be the great cause. Hence doubt extends to the three vehicles, while regret exists only in the small; using the broad to include the narrow, it speaks only of breaking doubt. Now I say: "doubt extends and regret is limited" — what text does this come from? Since the text says, "For all who seek the three vehicles, if any harbor doubt or regret," how can one judge from a single line that doubt is broader? If the broad includes the narrow, all should have regret. Moreover, in what stage do these lesser bodhisattvas abide? First doubting that [the śrāvakas] alone attain, then doubting that they themselves have no share: "alone attaining" and "having no share" contradict each other; how can doubt and regret be divided between the great and the small?

問曰：何名破執？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is meant by breaking attachments?

Chinese Source

答曰：法華論云：決定、退大二種聲聞。今經唯為退大一種。經云：我昔教汝志願佛道，及明大通十六王子。今經正破退大者執，四中非三。今謂論語現在且置未來，若弘教者欲達經旨，須遠尋始達末，謂於彼土生滅想時得聞是經。滅想乃順現在情執，得聞則順化儀大體。既云得聞是經，何以蔽之？論云：決定增上慢未熟故。釋云：其趣寂者元無大性，亦非不輕與記之限。乃引例云：如般若經皆令入於無餘涅槃，非皆盡入。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The Treatise on the Lotus says: "Two kinds of śrāvakas: those of fixed [nature] and those who retreated from the great." The present sūtra is only for one kind, those who retreated from the great. The sūtra says, "I once taught you to aspire to the buddha-path," and it clarifies the sixteen princes of Mahābhijñā. Question: From "taught a Great Vehicle sūtra" to "guarded and held in mind by the Buddha" — what is the meaning?

Chinese Source

答曰：論云：欲說法時成就。今謂論云：如來欲說法時至成就，為諸菩薩說大乘經，名無量義乃至法門故。既云欲說，即指法華名為正說，說無量義猶名為欲。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Why does he first teach the Immeasurable Meanings?

Chinese Source

Answer: There are three meanings. First, by person: first he teaches the Immeasurable Meanings for bodhisattvas, next he teaches the one vehicle for śrāvakas. Second, by benefit: first, with the teaching and principle of the one vehicle that benefit others, he transforms bodhisattvas whose faculties are ripe; next, with the practice and fruit of the one vehicle that benefit oneself, he transforms the śrāvakas who retreated from the great. Third, by Dharma: first he discusses the substance of the dharma of immeasurable meanings, next he teaches the function of the dharma of the Lotus. Now I say: each of the three meanings has great difficulties. If he teaches the Immeasurable Meanings for bodhisattvas, I do not know to which vehicle the Immeasurable Meanings belongs. If you say "first for bodhisattvas," why does that sūtra's listing of the assembly fully list both the śrāvakas and the bodhisattvas, in numbers no different from this sūtra? Hence in the "Ten Merits" chapter, after broadly teaching the ten great inconceivable powers of merit, all the bodhisattvas attain the samādhi of immeasurable meanings, or attain hundreds of thousands of dhāraṇī-gates, or attain the bodhisattva grounds and acceptances, or attain [the fruits of] pratyekabuddha and the four fruits up to arhat. Further, in the "Teaching the Dharma" chapter, thirty-two thousand bodhisattvas attain the samādhi of immeasurable meanings, thirty-four thousand bodhisattvas attain immeasurable dhāraṇī-gates, and the four assemblies, the eight classes, the kings, and the men and women, hundreds of thousands together, all hearing what the Buddha taught, some attain warmth up to the supreme worldly dharma, stream-entry up to arhat and pratyekabuddha, the acceptance of non-arising, and the dhāraṇīs. How are these not benefits to śrāvakas? For the bodhisattvas numbers are given, for the śrāvakas it says "the various"; we can verify that the word "various" expresses many. How can you say it is only for bodhisattvas? If you say this sūtra is for śrāvakas, [there are] "the bodhisattvas, hearing this Dharma" and the "Distinguishing Merits," where bodhisattvas gain benefit. Moreover, in the chapters below, the benefit gained on hearing each chapter exceeds that sūtra.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, by benefit: the words "self" and "other" should not be matched separately. As for the one who transforms, both are present, the two virtues of self and other fully possessed; as for those transformed, they must first benefit themselves. The śrāvakas, having received predictions, vow to spread the sūtra; if, having received predictions, they are again made to benefit only themselves, how does this practice and fruit differ from the small tradition? Moreover, the one vehicle must contain both self and other; how can it be divided into two sections? Moreover, [you make] teaching and principle reach only the bodhisattvas and practice and fruit reach only the śrāvakas; yet it is precisely because the śrāvakas were formerly confused about the great teaching and lost the great principle [that they need it]. Now to give those śrāvakas a practice and fruit [cut off from] the teaching and principle they were confused about — why not simply rely on the small teachings before the Lotus that contain the four dharmas? As for "substance and function": now I say: both are the one vehicle, and both have substance and function. Function is benefiting beings, and benefiting beings requires substance; how could one transform those bodhisattvas with a substance that has no function, and teach these śrāvakas with a function that lacks substance? A one vehicle with substance and no function is possessed by all sentient beings; a one vehicle without substance and with function is the spiritual powers of non-Buddhists. You should know that that [sūtra's] transforming the three vehicles is called function, while in this sūtra the wondrous substance of the true mark is evident. Thus that sūtra is the one vehicle that opens function from substance, and so the three vehicles receive benefit; this sūtra is the one vehicle that gathers function into substance, and so it makes the three return to the one. Hence the Immeasurable Meanings says: "Immeasurable meanings arise from a single dharma." This sūtra says: "By all kinds of causes and conditions all accomplish the wisdom of the modes." Moreover, the substance is the root of dharmas and of no benefit to people [by itself]; how can you divide it into three rounds and explain them differently?

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Who is speaking. In each exchange, "Question" is the framing question, "Answer" reports the position of Ji's Profound Praise (Fahua xuanzan, T1723), and "Now I say" (今謂) / "Now I ask" (今問) introduce Zhanran's objections; "It further says" (又曰／又云) introduces further passages of Ji. The answers are often close to Ji's wording: for example, "Those bent on quiescence originally have no great nature" (unit 16) and "opening is the meaning of unsurpassed" can be found in T1723. Zhanran addresses Ji as "you" (子), "the Venerable" (大德), and "the other" (他).

The preface. Written by the Japanese Tendai monk Rikunyo Jishū in Kansei 6 (1794). "Great Master Chishō" is Enchin (814–891), who brought the text to Japan; Shutoku and his "grandson" Shōshū are Hiei monks who collated and printed it (their dates have not been checked). The "Editorial Conventions" heading (unit 3) is followed in CBETA only by Shōshū's signature; the conventions themselves are not in the input.

"Three without, two with" (三無二有). Most likely the five lineages: three that never reach buddhahood (fixed śrāvakas, fixed pratyekabuddhas, those without the nature) and two that do (the bodhisattva lineage and the indeterminate). Alternatively: the three non-natures and the two existent natures (dependent and perfected) of Yogācāra three-nature theory.

Allusions in the preface. The wooden ox and the yellow willow leaves are the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's images of toys and false gold used to stop a child crying (skillful means for the weak). Shunjing (順璟) is the Silla monk who, by tradition, fell alive into hell for rejecting the Avataṃsaka. "A jeweled vessel receiving coarse food" echoes the Vimalakīrti's "do not put foul food in a jeweled vessel" (quoted in T1775, T1717). "Cutting the foot to fit the shoe" and "buying the casket and returning the pearl" are proverbial (Huainanzi; Han Feizi). "Asking about the bridge and debating the arrow": source not located; possibly an echo of the poisoned-arrow parable.

Quotations in the preface. The Lotus passage quoted through Qingliang (Chengguan) is from the "Teacher of the Dharma" chapter, in the site's wording; the source of Chengguan's own remark was not located, and how far his words run is uncertain. "Real by way of the worldly, real by way of the ultimate meaning" is the Great Treatise's four-siddhānta formula, also in the Fahua xuanyi (T1716). Zhiyi's sentence on "wondrous" is from the Fahua xuanyi (T1716); the English follows the site's Profound Meaning.

Lotus quotations. Wording follows the site's Lotus Sūtra where the passage is on the site: "If I only praise the buddha vehicle…" and "Directly setting aside skillful means…" (The Buddha's Hesitation and the Turning of the Wheel); "teaches and transforms only bodhisattvas," "only a buddha together with a buddha" (Skillful Means: The Buddha's Wisdom); "For all who seek the three vehicles, if any harbor doubt or regret" (The Assembly and the Six Signs); "contemplates all things as empty, according to their true mark" and "How is a bodhisattva-mahāsattva, in the evil age to come, able to teach this sūtra?" (The Four Peaceful Practices); "riding this jeweled vehicle straight to the seat of enlightenment" (The Parable of the Burning House); "whose minds had attained mastery" (The Assembly and the Six Signs). The string of "Why is it called…" phrases in unit 22 comes mostly from the "Belief and Understanding" chapter (the site's "The Parable of the Destitute Son"); these were translated directly.

The Treatise on the Lotus. Vasubandhu's Saddharmapuṇḍarīka-upadeśa (T1519). "Those of fixed [nature] and of overweening pride, because they are not yet ripe" is located there; it distinguishes four kinds of śrāvakas (fixed, overweening, retreated and returned, and transformation), hence "[one] of the four, not the [other] three" in unit 16.

The three periods. The Saṃdhinirmocana's three turnings of the wheel (T676, "Paramārthasamudgata" chapter); the formula 有上有容 ("had something above it, allowed [rebuttal]") is also in Ji's Dacheng fayuan yilin zhang (T1861). The "old" five periods (Trapuṣa; three vehicles with existence; with emptiness; the one vehicle; the Nirvāṇa) are the southern scheme that Zhiyi refutes in the Fahua xuanyi.

Compound types (六釋). The six kinds of Sanskrit compound analysis used by Ji: 持業 karmadhāraya, 依主 tatpuruṣa, 有財 bahuvrīhi, 隣近 avyayībhāva, 相違 dvandva, 帶數 dvigu.

Persons. Jiaxiang is Jizang (549–623); Guangzhai is Fayun (467–529); "Master Zun of the Sui" and "Dharma Master Bao" are not identified; "the Tripiṭaka [Master]" is probably Xuanzang. "[Jñāna]gupta" (岌, read as 笈多) produced the Tianpin (Supplemented) Lotus (T264) with Dharmagupta.

Sūtras of the assembly order (unit 72). The Luomojia (T294) is a partial Gaṇḍavyūha; the Julouwang, Hexiu, and Great Five Turbidities sūtras are not identified (source not located). The Sūtra of the Twelve Journeys is T195.

Probable copying errors, translated as corrected. Unit 30: 三昧 read as 三時 ("three periods"). Unit 50: 末 read twice as 未 ("not yet sifted," "not yet acceptable"). Unit 78: 十二由 read as 十二遊 (Twelve Journeys). Unit 46: the punctuation 果寧是從顛？倒生故說 is read as 果寧是？從顛倒生故說, a quotation from the "Peaceful Practices." Unit 90: the punctuation is read as 歡喜藏摩尼寶積佛。聞名… Unit 38: 現出世部 is taken as the Lokottaravāda (說出世部).

Doubtful passage. Unit 61, 未若鈴場之下流, "it does not match even the lower stream of the bell-ground," is translated literally; the sense is unclear.
