Five Hundred Questions on the Lotus.

Zhanran (湛然, 711–782) wrote this treatise against the Profound Praise of the Lotus by Ji (窺基, 632–682), the great Faxiang master. Going through the Lotus Sūtra chapter by chapter, each question names a point of Ji's commentary; the "answer" reports Ji's position, usually in his own words; and Zhanran objects, often several times. His charges are three: Ji reads the sūtra through the Yogācāra doctrine of five distinct lineages, buries its purpose under classifications, and says nothing about practice. The work survived only in Japan.

How to read this collection

"Answer:" gives Ji's position; "Now I say" and "Now I ask" are Zhanran. Keep the two voices apart.

Lotus quotations use this site's Lotus Sūtra; Zhanran's own Tiantai reading is in the Fahua Xuanyi and Fahua Wenju.

The Text's Structure

Three fascicles in eight parts, following the chapters of the Lotus.

Part 1
The Preface and the Preliminary Gates: Occasion, Cardinal Purport, and the Opening of the Introduction
The Treatise of Five Hundred Questions on the Lotus (X0939) is Zhanran's (711–782) critique of the Profound Praise of the Lotus (Fahua xuanzan, T1723) by Ji (632–682), the Faxiang master of Ci'en Monastery.
Translated
Part 2
The Portents of the Introduction and the Opening of the "Skillful Means" Chapter
This part continues Zhanran's questions on Ji's Profound Praise of the Lotus through the rest of the "Introduction" and into the "Skillful Means" chapter.
Translated
Part 3
The One Vehicle, the Wheel of the Dharma, and Śāriputra's Prediction
This part finishes the first fascicle of the Five Hundred Questions and opens the second.
Translated
Part 4
The Burning House and the Destitute Son
This part follows Ji's commentary through the parable of the burning house and into the "Belief and Understanding" chapter, where the four great disciples tell the parable of the destitute son.
Translated
Part 5
The Herbs, the Predictions, and the Phantom City
This part covers three chapters of the Lotus: "Herbs," "Bestowal of Predictions," and "Phantom City." As before, each "Answer" reports Ji's Profound Praise, and "Now I say" is Zhanran's reply.
Translated
Part 6
From the Sewn Jewel to the Dragon Girl
Part 6 closes Fascicle Two and opens Fascicle Three.
Translated
Part 7
From the Peaceful Practices to the Tathāgata's Lifespan
Part 7 continues Fascicle Three through the "Peaceful Practices," "Welling Up," and "Lifespan" chapters.
Translated
Part 8
From Distinguishing Merits to Samantabhadra's Encouragement
Part 8 completes the Treatise of Five Hundred Questions.
Translated
