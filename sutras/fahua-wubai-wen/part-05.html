The Herbs, the Predictions, and the Phantom City

This part covers three chapters of the Lotus: "Herbs," "Bestowal of Predictions," and "Phantom City." As before, each "Answer" reports Ji's Profound Praise, and "Now I say" is Zhanran's reply. In the "Herbs" chapter, Ji reads the grasses and trees as fixed differences among beings, so that the teaching is one while the capacities stay different. Zhanran answers that the chapter confirms the disciples' understanding, and that the one rain and the one ground show the past differences merged into one. He also rejects Ji's many schemes matching the trees to bodhisattva stages. In the "Bestowal of Predictions" chapter he takes up the order in which the four great disciples are predicted, the meaning of their future buddha-names, and the famished man who waits for the king's command before eating. The long "Phantom City" section deals with the Treatise on the Lotus's "settled persons," the four intentions Ji uses to explain the sixteen princes, the parents of an other-enjoyment body, and the four truths. At its center is Ji's claim that arhats who enter nirvāṇa without remainder have no body or consciousness left with which to turn to the great. Zhanran replies that the rebukes of śrāvakas in earlier sūtras were provisional, and that the Nirvāṇa and the Lotus leave no one in final extinction.

The Intent of the "Herbs" Chapter

Chinese Source

問：此品來意如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is the intent of this chapter's coming?

Chinese Source

答曰：佛法雖同，機修有異，故說此品。今謂此品述成信解，信解領譬始終不殊，何以將異而述於同？只緣昔異令同，反更別同令異，非但領述差𤘆，亦乃開廢乖張。論中上慢言無二者，恐人一向撥無所會，所會即是能會之本，於一佛乘分別說三，三外無一、一外無三，今會三歸一、三一不二，若撥無三還成撥一。如眾藥為丸，丸即是藥，若不信藥豈信於丸？存實捨權過亦如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "Though the Buddha's Dharma is the same, capacities and cultivation differ; hence he teaches this chapter." Now I say: this chapter relates and confirms the "Belief and Understanding," and the belief and understanding gave their understanding of the parable, the same from beginning to end. Why use difference to relate sameness? It is only because the former difference has been made the same that you turn around and separate the same to make it different; not only are the understanding and the relating at odds, but the opening and the discarding also run contrary. When the Treatise speaks of those of overweening pride who say there are not two, it fears that people will flatly deny what is merged; what is merged is the basis of what merges. Within the one buddha vehicle the three are taught by distinction: outside the three there is no one, outside the one there are no three. Now the three are merged and return to the one; the three and the one are not two. If one denies the three, that turns into denying the one. It is like making many medicines into a pill: the pill is the medicines. If one does not believe in the medicines, how would one believe in the pill? Keeping the real and abandoning the provisional is a fault of the same kind.

Question: Is there yet another approach?

Chinese Source

答曰：譬喻品說乘三體一，四人領解實一假三。今此會門，門即阿含，教同機異，名會其門。如說四諦，三根俱聞，依陰、處、界、緣起三性，觀修有異，名曰三乘。故曰教同而機異也。今問：教為能詮？體為所詮？若教體一，昔教三異，已論一乘，何須法華若破若會？況論體教，能所不成。故乘三體一之言，在昔三乘共行，或在三祇百劫。教、理對言之，若彼兩三對於圓極，大小永隔，能所天殊。如大經云：我與彌勒共論世諦，五百聲聞謂說真諦。故知二乘但知其名而不知義，螢光之智無照大千，共學之乘預圓妙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "The 'Parable' chapter taught that the vehicles are three while the substance is one; the four persons, in giving their understanding, [took it that] the real is one and the three are provisional. Now this [chapter] merges the gate. The gate is the Āgamas: the teaching is the same while the capacities differ; this is called merging the gate. As when the four truths are taught, [those of] the three faculties all hear them; relying on the aggregates, the sense-fields, the elements, dependent arising, and the three natures, their contemplation and cultivation differ, and these are called the three vehicles. Hence it says that the teaching is the same and the capacities differ." Now I ask: is the teaching what expresses, and the substance what is expressed? If the substance of the teaching is one, and the former teachings, differing as three, already discussed the one vehicle, why would the Lotus need either to break or to merge? Moreover, when you discuss substance and teaching, the expressing and the expressed do not hold. Hence the words "the vehicles are three while the substance is one" refer to the shared practice of the three vehicles in the past, or to the three asaṃkhyeyas and a hundred eons. Speaking of teaching and principle in contrast: if those two threes are set against the perfect ultimate, the great and the small are forever apart, and the expressing and the expressed are as far apart as heaven [from earth]. As the Great Sūtra says: "When Maitreya and I discussed the worldly truth, the five hundred śrāvakas thought we were speaking of the real truth." Hence we know that those of the two vehicles know only the name and not the meaning; the wisdom of a firefly's glow does not illuminate the great chiliocosm; [how does] the vehicle of shared study [have] a share in the perfect and wondrous?

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, since you merge the teaching but not the capacities — further, are the words "the capacities differ" only for the Herbs, or are they also in [the rounds of] Dharma, parable, and former lives? If only in the Herbs, the relating and the understanding do not hold; since the relating does not hold, the three rounds are as far apart as heaven [from earth]. Moreover, the teaching is basically applied to beings; what would an empty teaching be for? Then the Buddha himself would teach sameness while beings are always different; what would be the use of merging the teaching? If you take as your example the hearing of the four truths together by the three faculties, which differ, a teaching of difference is not the Mahāyāna. As the Aṅgulimāla says: "What is called four? Namely the four truths. This is the tenet of the śrāvakas, not the Mahāyāna." How can one take [it as] merging the teaching but not the capacities, and use not merging the capacities to prove the merging of the teaching? How much less so, since the original Treatise speaks only of the parable of the rain and not of grasses and trees: the grasses and trees indicate difference, the one rain reveals sameness — and, moreover, the sameness is sameness identical with difference. Thus the remedy for slander has no distinction, and the sūtra and the Treatise blend together; your explanation forces them apart. Further, the Treatise says: "'Not apart from my body' is the meaning of unsurpassed." Hence we know that not being apart from the grasses and trees is precisely the one ground; for this reason it is called unsurpassed.

Medicines, Grasses, and the Title of the Chapter

Chinese Source

問：何名藥艸？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is meant by "medicinal herbs"?

Chinese Source

答曰：治疾神功名藥，受潤之形名艸。有藥是艸，有藥非艸，有艸非藥，非艸非藥，所喻亦爾。理、教、行、果俱名為藥，今取行、藥稟教修生，不取餘名，不能生長世、出世種。能生長者俱名為艸，但取五乘，不取生死惡道種艸。今謂大小各有理、教、行、果，若以大治小，則四俱非藥；轉小為大，則四俱生長。若行獨長而三不長，應當小行轉成大行，而人仍住小理、小果。若本已得大理、大果，則教、行二種亦不須長，況言行長而稟於教，還稟小教，行亦無長？故知教轉成大，其行方長，況言長者小轉成大，迷轉成悟，既存差別，當其自位，何須增長？況復本立經唯為小，安得更存人、天差別？應當亦是為人、天耶？況復今經云為二乘令其轉小，豈直令其但得小耶？故知此經唯獨佛乘，不須餘釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "What has the spiritual power of curing illness is called a medicine; the form that receives moisture is called a grass. There are medicines that are grasses, medicines that are not grasses, grasses that are not medicines, and things that are neither grasses nor medicines; what they symbolize is likewise. Principle, teaching, practice, and fruit are all called medicines; here [the chapter] takes practice, the medicine that arises from cultivation on receiving the teaching, and does not name the others, [since they] cannot make the seeds of the worldly and the transworldly grow. What can grow are all called grasses; it takes only the five vehicles, not the grasses of the seeds of birth-and-death and the evil paths." Now I say: the great and the small each have principle, teaching, practice, and fruit. If the great is used to cure the small, then all four [of the small] are not medicine; if the small is turned into the great, then all four grow. If practice alone grows and the other three do not, then small practice should turn into great practice while the person still abides in the small principle and the small fruit. If one has originally already attained the great principle and the great fruit, then teaching and practice too need not grow; how much less can you say that practice grows by receiving the teaching, when it again receives the small teaching, so that practice does not grow either? Hence we know that only when the teaching turns into the great does practice grow. Moreover, to speak of growth is [to say that] the small turns into the great and delusion turns into awakening; if distinctions are kept, each in its own position, what need is there for growth? Moreover, since you originally set up the sūtra as being only for the small, how could you further keep the distinctions of humans and gods? Should it also be for humans and gods? Moreover, the present sūtra says that it is for the two vehicles, to make them turn [from] the small; how would it merely let them attain the small? Hence we know that this sūtra is solely the buddha vehicle and needs no other explanation.

Question: I once heard someone ask: according to the Treatise, this sūtra uses rain as its parable; why is the title "the Parable of the Medicinal Herbs"?

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "The Treatise takes what moistens; the sūtra takes the substance that serves as the parable, in order to break that doubt." Now I say: what moistens is the Buddha's great teaching; what is symbolized is the differing capacities of beings in the past. Why not say that the differing capacities are what the one teaching rains upon? What is symbolized basically lies in the sameness of the moistening. If the differing capacities receive the same and yet remain forever separate, then those who have not received the great conversion are not what is taken here; how could the difference of those who do not receive it be used as the title of this chapter? If this chapter is about distinction, then the skillful means set against the past [remain], and the past teaching still stands: the three carts and the one cart are still there, father and son and the hired laborer in [the parable of] the destitute son do not change, and the śrāvakas given predictions are rotten seeds from the beginning. Hence we know that what the destitute son understood and what the Buddha related were given in vain.

Answer: "By principle it is truly so." Now I ask: even if the two trees are taken as medicines and it becomes the "Grasses and Trees" chapter, are these grasses and trees what cures, or do they become what is cured? If what is cured, they accord with the nature of distinction; if they become what cures, they accord with the one rain. Since distinctions are kept, should [beings] in this sūtra newly attain the small fruit and [the states of] humans and gods? If there are none, the distinctions do not hold. If it merely restates that the distinctions differ, which sūtra lacks that, that this teaching should further be needed? It also says: "Grasses are broad and trees narrow; grasses are small and trees great. [The title] shows that it covers the three vehicles, not only the great; it shows that the moistening makes [them] grow, not only brings [them] to completion." Now I say: if all alike are made distinctions, the grasses are humans, gods, and the two vehicles, and the great grass is still the bodhisattvas. Though the two trees come later, the later include the earlier; what includes is broad and what is included is narrow. One should say: the three grasses are broad in name and narrow in substance; the two trees are narrow in name and broad in substance. Again, the three grasses are broad in name and short in stage; the two trees are narrow in name and long in stage. If the grasses cover the great and the small, humans and gods should not be only small; the trees cover the great and the small, yet the name "bodhisattva" is only great. Hence what is moistened here cannot show distinction. If you say it does not show completion, then it cannot [show] growth either. Basically it is for bringing to maturity; how could anything grow without being completed? Therefore the text says: "Flowers and grasses spread and bear fruit, all born of the one ground." Hence we know that all take the ground as their maturing. If the ground does not complete them, the predictions too are not maturity.

It also says: "The earlier parable had the burning house that is loathed and the three carts that are delighted in, and the matching of the parable could only speak generally; here, by the difference in the capacities moistened, it breaks their sickness of clinging to oneness; hence it uses only the medicine as the parable." Now I say: if you grant the ancient explanation, why go against it again? In the parable the house is set against the carts; the carts and the house both relate the past; hence it says that the three carts lead out of the burning house. Now these herbs are set against the ground and the rain: the grasses and trees relate the past, the ground and the rain reveal the present; merging the past to follow the present, the merit returns to the ground and the rain. The meaning corresponds to the three and the one set against each other in the parable: now one, formerly three; merging the past to follow the present, all are given equally. Hence we know only that one cannot take the loathing and delight [concerning] the burning house as an analogy for the bestowing and opening here; hence the ancient's words exactly fit the principle and obtain the names of what [moistens] and what [is moistened], fully as said just above.

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is the principle of the sūtra from "At that time the World-Honored One" to "could not exhaust them in speaking"?

Chinese Source

答曰：品文分三：初讚印，迦葉當知下陳述，三品末二頌結成說實。今問：若其讚印會實及以陳述廢權，品後偈文結成說實，何以品內專辨不同？科文與義其相既殊，故知判釋未能稱理，所以不可以差而述於會。又曰：顯彼所言尚未窮德，故更自歎佛同長者、汝同窮子。今謂若以窮子、長者顯同，理須彼此會實，故彼會窮子、此存出生，水火既殊安可異述？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "The text of the chapter divides into three: first, praise and sealing; [second,] from 'Kāśyapa, you should know' on, the setting forth and relating; third, the two verses at the end of the chapter conclude by teaching the real." Now I ask: if the praise and sealing merge into the real, the setting forth and relating discard the provisional, and the verses at the end of the chapter conclude by teaching the real, why does the body of the chapter discuss only differences? Since the outline of the text and the meaning differ in their marks, we know that the judgment and explanation do not fit the principle; hence one cannot use difference to relate merging. It further says: "It shows that what they said has not yet exhausted [the Buddha's] virtues; hence he further praises himself: the Buddha is like the elder, you are like the destitute son." Now I say: if the destitute son and the elder are used to show sameness, in principle both that and this must merge into the real. Yet there you merge the destitute son, and here you keep [the distinctions of] coming forth and growing. Since water and fire differ, how can [one] be related by [the other,] different [one]?

Though I am not clever, what I have received has its source.

In the Nirvāṇa it is said: [to those who say that] all śrāvakas can become buddhas, [and those who say they] cannot become buddhas, the Buddha says to all of them, 'You do not understand my intent'; hence one who can thus understand the sūtra's intent can also accord well with the minds of the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Yet in the present assembly most are of retreating nature; hence it teaches that the one is real and the two are provisional. The names of merging the provisional and the real cover both persons and dharmas: if one speaks in terms of dharmas, one must definitely take the one as real and the two or three as provisional; if one speaks in terms of persons, then great and small are not fixed: what is fitting is real, what is not fitting is provisional."

Five Hundred Yojanas and the Phantom City

Chinese Source

問：經從五百至珍寶處，其義何也？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is the meaning of the sūtra from "five hundred [yojanas]" to "the place of jewels"?

Chinese Source

答曰：正法華云：五道為嶮難，五怖畏為怖畏處。釋曰：正法華中出嶮道體，非釋五百數。言五百者，分段生死惑業苦為三百，變易生死無明苦為二百。今問：若五道是體，體皆嶮惡，嶮惡過已即珍寶處，應過五道即是寶所，何妨正法華五即是百，乃至自釋亦五無百？若先知變易在分段外有無明苦以為二百，何以前諸釋義並只但云離生死耶？又分段生死何故三？變易生死何故二？分段之惑亦無明，無明對苦亦為二，分段之惑開於業變易，故知不但二。變易無明對於苦，分段無明應亦然，故知他釋不曉開合。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "The Correct [Dharma Flower] says: 'The five paths are the perilous difficulty; the five fears are the fearful place.'" The explanation says: "The Correct sets out the substance of the perilous road; it does not explain the number five hundred. As for 'five hundred': the delusion, karma, and suffering of segmented birth-and-death are three hundred; the ignorance and suffering of change birth-and-death are two hundred." Now I ask: if the five paths are the substance, the substance being entirely perilous and evil, and once the perilous and evil is passed it is the place of jewels, then passing the five paths should be the place of treasure; what prevents the Correct's five from being the hundreds, and even your own explanation from having five without hundreds? If you knew beforehand that change lies outside the segmented and has ignorance and suffering as two hundred, why did all the earlier explanations say only "leaving birth-and-death"? Further, why is segmented birth-and-death three? Why is change birth-and-death two? The delusion of the segmented is also ignorance; ignorance set against suffering is also two; the delusion of the segmented opens out into karma; [so with] change, we know it is not only two. If ignorance in change is set against suffering, ignorance in the segmented should be likewise; hence we know the other's explanation does not understand opening and combining.

問：經從導師多諸方便至一城者，此義云何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is the meaning of the sūtra from "The guide, with many skillful means" to "a city"?

Chinese Source

答曰：攝大乘云：如末尼天皷，無思成自事。釋曰：雖無勉勵思，亦有任運思，實無思假稱思。今謂等是無思，何故苦以有思釋之，復云實無等耶？思非任運，任運非思，乃以凡心測於聖境，故使此論便成小教，豈法華中三佛慧耶？又曰：一切諸佛晝三夜三觀眾生機。今謂此正當於婆沙中義，尚不可以釋餘大乘，況法華耶？又曰：過三百者，或云過五趣為五百，此但過分段三百而已。或令出三惡為三百，實論初已斷於分段，雖亦出於人天變易，猶人天攝不名無出。若依實說，過分段為三百由旬，涅槃在於無學位故。今謂無學在三百由旬外，即成四百立化城也，是則違經。經意只出分段入變易，出變易入佛地，佛地即是寶所果也。若也變易却在人天，佛果即却在於生死，生死亦應却在三惡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: "The Mahāyānasaṃgraha says: 'Like the maṇi jewel and the heavenly drum, without thought it accomplishes its own work.'" The explanation says: "Though there is no thought of effort, there is spontaneous thought; in reality there is no thought, but it is provisionally called thought." Now I say: since it is equally without thought, why take pains to explain it as having thought, and then say "in reality there is none," and so on? Thought is not spontaneous, and the spontaneous is not thought; this measures the realm of the sages with an ordinary mind, thus turning this treatise into a small teaching; how could it be the buddha-wisdom of the Lotus? It further says: "All buddhas, three times by day and three times by night, observe the capacities of beings." Now I say: this corresponds exactly to the meaning in the Vibhāṣā; it cannot even explain other Great Vehicle [sūtras], how much less the Lotus? It further says: "As for 'passing three hundred': some say that passing the five destinies is the five hundred; this only passes the three hundred of the segmented. Or [some] make leaving the three evil [paths] the three hundred; really speaking, at the first [fruit] one has already cut off [this part of] the segmented; though one also leaves humans and gods, [in] change one is still included among humans and gods and is not called leaving. If one speaks according to the real, passing the segmented is the three hundred yojanas, because nirvāṇa lies in the stage beyond learning." Now I say: if those beyond learning are outside the three hundred yojanas, then the phantom city is set up at four hundred; this contradicts the sūtra. The sūtra's intent is only: leaving the segmented and entering change, leaving change and entering the buddha ground; the buddha ground is the fruit of the place of treasure. If change were back among humans and gods, the buddha-fruit would be back in birth-and-death, and birth-and-death too would have to be back in the three evil [paths].
