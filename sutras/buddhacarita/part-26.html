The Great Disciples Go Forth

King Bimbisāra formally gives the Bamboo Grove to the growing saṅgha, and the poem turns to how its two greatest disciples were won: Śāriputra, stopped in the street by the mere dignity of the monk Aśvajit's bearing, receives in reply a single compressed verse on dependent origination and attains the Dharma-eye on the spot; his friend Maudgalyāyana reads the change in his face before a word is spoken. The chapter closes with the wealthy Mahākāśyapa's own renunciation and a dense catalogue of the ascetic virtues that would make him, in later tradition, foremost among the disciples in strict practice.

The Bamboo Grove, and Śāriputra's Conversion

Chinese Source

佛所行讚大弟子出家品第十七

爾時瓶沙王，　稽首請世尊，
遷住於竹林；　哀受故默然。

王已見真諦，　奉拜而還宮，
世尊與大眾，　徙居安竹園。

為度眾生故，　建立慧燈明，
以梵住天住，　賢聖住而住。

時阿濕波誓，　調心御諸根，
時至行乞食，　入於王舍城。

容貌世挺特，　威儀安序庠，
城中諸士女，　見者莫不歡，
行者為住步，　前迎後風馳。

迦毘羅仙人，　廣度諸弟子，
第一勝多聞，　其名舍利弗。

見比丘庠序，　閑雅靜諸根，
蹰路而待至，　舉手請問言：
「年少靜儀容，　我所未曾見。

得何勝妙法？　為宗事何師？

師教何所說？　願告決所疑。」

比丘欣彼問，　和顏遜辭答：
「一切智具足，　甘蔗勝族生，
天人中最尊，　是則我大師。

我年既幼稚，　學日又初淺，
豈能宣大師，　甚深微妙義？

今當以淺智，　略說師教法。

一切有法生，　皆從因緣起，
生滅法悉滅，　說道為方便。」

二生憂波提，　隨聽心內融，
遠離諸塵垢，　清淨法眼生。

先所脩決定，　知因及無因，
一切無所作，　皆由自在天。

今聞因緣法，　無我智開明，
增微諸煩惱，　無能究竟除。

唯有如來教，　永盡而無遺，
非攝受我所，　而能離吾我。

明因日燈興，　孰能令無光？

如斷蓮花莖，　微絲猶連綿；
佛教除煩惱，　猶斷石無餘。

敬禮比丘足，　退辭而還家；
比丘乞食已，　亦還歸竹園。

Ru-Yi Translation

Buddhacarita, Chapter Seventeen: The Great Disciples Go Forth

At that time King Bimbisāra bowed his head and asked the World-Honored One to settle in the Bamboo Grove; and the Buddha, out of compassion, accepted this offer in silence.

The king, having already beheld the ultimate truth, paid his reverent farewell and returned to his palace; and the World-Honored One, together with his great assembly, moved to settle at ease in the Bamboo Grove.

For the sake of delivering sentient beings, he raised up there the very lamp of wisdom, dwelling in the dwelling of Brahmā, the dwelling of the gods, and the dwelling of the sages and the wise alike.

At that time Aśvajit, his mind well-tamed and every one of his faculties mastered, went, when the proper hour arrived, to beg for alms, entering the city of Rājagṛha.

His countenance stood out among the people of the world, his dignified bearing calm and composed; and among the men and women of the city, there was none who saw him and did not rejoice — those walking would stop in their tracks, and those already ahead would hurry back like the wind to greet him.

Among the many disciples widely converted by the seer of the Kapila lineage was one foremost in learning, named Śāriputra.

Seeing this monk's own calm and dignified bearing, at ease and with every faculty stilled, he waited along the road for him to draw near, and, raising his hand in greeting, asked him: "Such youthful yet composed bearing as this, I have never before seen.

What excellent and wondrous Dharma have you attained? Under what tradition, and serving what teacher?

What does your teacher's instruction actually say? I beg you, tell me, and resolve this doubt of mine."

The monk, delighted at this question, answered him with a gentle countenance and humble words: "Complete in all-encompassing wisdom, born of the excellent sugarcane lineage, most honored among gods and men — this is my great teacher.

Yet I myself am still young in years, and my own days of study are still shallow — how could I possibly proclaim my great teacher's profound and subtle meaning in full?

Still, let me now, with what little wisdom I have, explain my teacher's instruction in brief.

Every conditioned dharma that arises does so entirely from causes and conditions; and when these very dharmas of arising and cessation are themselves brought to cessation, this is what my teacher calls the path, taught as the skillful means to that end."

Śāriputra, the twice-born, listened to this, and his heart melted open within him; far removed at once from every trace of defilement, the pure Dharma-eye arose in him.

He had, until then, held firmly to the view that things arise either from a fixed cause or from no cause at all, or that nothing at all is truly done, but that everything comes about entirely through the agency of Īśvara, the Sovereign God.

But now, hearing this Dharma of dependent arising, the wisdom of no-self opened clear within him — for even the very subtlest of the afflictions, once increased, could never truly be eradicated by any of those views at all.

Only the Tathāgata's own teaching brings them to a final and complete end, with nothing left over — for it is not by clinging to a self, or to what belongs to a self, that one is able to depart from "I" and "mine" at all.

Once the sun of clear causation has risen like a lamp, who could possibly cause its light to fail?

It is like cutting the stalk of a lotus flower — its finest threads still cling on, unbroken; but the Buddha's own teaching removes the afflictions the way one might sever solid stone, with nothing left over at all.

He bowed reverently at the monk's own feet, and took his leave to return home; and the monk, having finished begging for alms, likewise returned to the Bamboo Grove.

Maudgalyāyana, and the Chief Disciples

Chinese Source

舍利弗還家，　貌色甚和雅，
善友大目連，　同體聞才均，
遙見舍利弗，　顏儀甚熙怡，
告言：「今見汝，　而有異常容，
素性至沈隱，　歡相見於今。

必得甘露法，　此相非無因。」

答言：「如來告，　實獲未曾法。」

即請而為說，　聞則心開解，
諸塵垢亦除，　隨生正法眼。

久殖妙因果，　如觀掌中燈，
得佛不動信，　俱行詣佛所。

與徒眾弟子，　二百五十人，
佛遙見二賢，　而告諸眾言：
「彼來者二人，　吾上首弟子，
一智慧無雙，　二神足第一。」

以深淨梵音，　即命汝善來，
此有清涼法，　出家究竟道。

手執三掎杖，　縈髮持澡瓶，
聞佛善來聲，　即變成沙門。

二師及弟子，　悉成比丘儀，
稽首世尊足，　却坐於一面，
隨順為說法，　皆得羅漢道。

Ru-Yi Translation

When Śāriputra returned home, his countenance was gentle and composed; and his close friend Mahāmaudgalyāyana, his equal in both understanding and talent, seeing Śāriputra's own face radiant with such joy from a distance, said to him: "Seeing you now, I notice something quite different about your appearance — your nature has always been deeply reserved, and yet you seem filled with joy today.

You must surely have attained the Dharma of immortality — such a mark as this could hardly arise without some real cause behind it."

Śāriputra answered: "The Tathāgata's own teaching — I have truly obtained a Dharma never known before."

Maudgalyāyana at once asked him to explain it, and, hearing it, his own heart opened and understood; every trace of his own defilement was likewise removed, and the true Dharma-eye arose in him as well.

Having long ago planted the wondrous causes for this very fruit, it was as clear to him now as gazing at a lamp held in his own palm; and, attaining an unshakable faith in the Buddha, the two of them set out together for where the Buddha was.

They came together with their own community of disciples, two hundred and fifty in number; and the Buddha, seeing these two worthy men approach from a distance, said to the whole assembly: "Those two who now approach shall be my foremost disciples — the first unequaled in wisdom, the second foremost in spiritual powers."

With a deep and pure, Brahmā-like voice, he called out to them at once, "Come, and welcome" — for here was found the very Dharma of true coolness and peace, the ultimate path for one gone forth from home.

Though they still held in their hands the triple staff of the wandering ascetic, their hair still coiled and their water-vessels still in hand, the moment they heard the Buddha's call of welcome, they were transformed at once into true śramaṇas.

Both teacher and their whole company of disciples became complete, all at once, in the full deportment of monks; they bowed their heads at the World-Honored One's own feet, and withdrew to sit to one side, and, receiving his teaching accordingly, every one of them attained the way of arhatship.

Mahākāśyapa Goes Forth

Chinese Source

爾時有二生，　迦葉族明燈，
多聞身相具，　財盈妻極賢，
厭捨而出家，　志求解脫道。

路由多子塔，　忽遇釋迦文，
光儀顯明耀，　猶若祠天幢。

肅然舉身敬，　稽首頂禮足：
「尊為我大師，　我是尊弟子。

久遠積癡冥，　願為作燈明。」

佛知彼二生，　心樂崇解脫，
清淨軟和音，　命之以善來。

聞命心融泰，　形神疲勞息，
心栖勝解脫，　寂靜離諸塵。

大悲隨所應，　略為其解說，
領解諸深法，　成四無礙辯，
大德普流聞，　故名大迦葉。

本見身我異，　或見我即身，
有我及我所，　斯見已永除，
唯見眾苦聚，　離苦則無餘。

持戒修苦行，　非因而見因，
平等見苦性，　永無他聚心。

若有若見無，　二見生猶豫，
平等見真諦，　決定無復疑。

染著於財色，　迷醉貪欲生，
無常不淨想，　貪愛永已乖。

慈心平等念，　怨親無異想，
哀愍於一切，　則消瞋恚毒。

依色諸有對，　種種雜想生，
思惟壞色想，　則斷色於愛。

雖生無色天，　命亦要之盡，
愚於四正受，　而生解脫想，
寂滅離諸想，　無色貪永除。

動亂心變逆，　猶狂風鼓浪，
深入堅固定，　寂止掉亂心。

觀法無我所，　生滅不堅固，
不見軟中上，　我慢心自忘。

熾然智慧燈，　離諸癡冥闇，
見盡無盡法，　無明悉無餘。

思惟十功德，　十種煩惱滅，
甦息作已作，　深感仰尊顏。

離三而得三，　三弟子除三，
猶三星布列，　三十三司弟，
列侍於三五，　三侍佛亦然。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time there lived one of the twice-born, a shining lamp of the Kāśyapa clan — a man of wide learning, of complete and excellent bearing, of abundant wealth, and married to a most virtuous wife — who, weary of it all, renounced everything to go forth from home, his heart set on seeking the path of liberation.

On the road, by the shrine of Bahuputraka, he suddenly encountered Śākyamuni, his radiant bearing shining brilliantly, like a banner raised in offering to the gods.

Overcome with reverence, he prostrated his whole body, bowing his head to the very feet of the Buddha: "You are my great teacher, and I am your own disciple.

I have accumulated ignorance and darkness across ages beyond counting — I beg you now to be my very lamp of light."

The Buddha, knowing that this twice-born man's heart delighted in and honored liberation, called out to him in a pure and gentle voice, "Come, and welcome."

Hearing this call, his heart melted into ease and peace, and every weariness of body and spirit came to rest; his mind settled into the most excellent liberation, quiescent and free of every trace of defilement.

The Buddha, out of great compassion, explained the Dharma to him in brief, according to what was fitting, and he came to understand its profound teachings fully, attaining the four unobstructed forms of eloquence; and because his great virtue spread far and wide, he came to be called Mahākāśyapa, "Kāśyapa the Great."

Whatever view he had once held — that the body and the self are different, or that the self is identical with the body, holding to a self and to what belongs to a self — every such view was now permanently removed; he saw only the accumulation of suffering itself, and, once free of suffering, nothing at all remained beyond it.

Keeping the precepts and cultivating ascetic practice without mistaking it for the true cause of liberation, he saw the very nature of suffering with perfect equanimity, and no thought of gathering anything else to himself ever arose in him again.

Whether holding that something exists or that it does not, both such views give rise only to doubt and hesitation; but seeing the ultimate truth with perfect equanimity, he remained settled and certain, free of doubt forever after.

Attachment to wealth and beauty arises only from the intoxication of delusion and the craving of desire; but through the perception of impermanence and impurity, craving and attachment were, for him, permanently dispelled.

Through a heart of loving-kindness, holding all beings with perfect equanimity, making no distinction at all between enemy and kin, and extending compassion to every being alike, the poison of anger and hatred was thereby dissolved in him.

Every manner of confused and mixed perception arises through attachment to form and to whatever stands in relation to it; but through reflecting on the disintegration of form itself, he cut off craving for form entirely.

And even one reborn in the formless heavens must still see that very life come to its end in time; the foolish, mistaking the four formless absorptions themselves for liberation, remain trapped — but he, in quiescent stillness, free of every such perception, permanently removed even craving for the formless realms.

Restless and disordered movements of mind turn against themselves like waves whipped up by a wild wind; but entering deeply into firm and steady concentration, he stilled every such agitated and disordered state of mind.

Contemplating that all dharmas are without self and without anything belonging to a self, arising and ceasing without any true solidity at all, he no longer perceived any distinction of lesser, middling, or superior standing among beings, and the very thought of pride in himself was forgotten of its own accord.

With the blazing lamp of his own wisdom, he departed from every trace of ignorant darkness; seeing the exhaustible and the inexhaustible Dharma both clearly, ignorance itself was left with nothing remaining of it at all.

Reflecting on the ten virtues and extinguishing the ten kinds of affliction, he found rest and repose, what needed to be done now done, and looked up at the World-Honored One's own countenance with the deepest gratitude.

Departing from three things and attaining three others in their place, these three disciples each removed their own three faults — like three stars arrayed together in the sky, or like the thirty-three gods each attending their own retinue, arrayed in threes and fives — so, too, did these three attend upon the Buddha.
