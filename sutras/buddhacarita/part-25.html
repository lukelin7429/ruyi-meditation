King Bimbisāra's Disciples (II)

The fire sermon lands: a thousand ascetics who had spent their lives serving flame recognize, in the Buddha's image of the fires of greed, hatred, and delusion, exactly what their own practice had never addressed, and every one of them attains liberation on the spot. At Rājagṛha, King Bimbisāra's unspoken question — has the great Kāśyapa himself really become a disciple? — is answered publicly, with a display of psychic power followed by Kāśyapa's own plain statement of who his teacher now is. The Buddha then teaches the assembly directly: a close argument, addressed to a king, on why no fixed and permanent self can be found anywhere in a being at all.

The Fire Sermon, and a Thousand Arahants

Chinese Source

亂想鑽燧生，　貪欲瞋恚火，
焚燒於眾生。

如是煩惱火，　熾然不休息，
彌淪於生死，　苦火亦常然。

能見二種火，　熾然無依怙，
云何有心人，　而不生厭離？

厭離除貪欲，　貪盡得解脫，
若已得解脫，　解脫知見生。

觀察生死流，　而舉於梵行，
一切作已作，　更不受後有。」

如是千比丘，　聞世尊說法，
諸漏永不起，　一切心解脫。

佛為迦葉等，　千比丘說法：
「所作者已作，　淨慧妙莊嚴，
諸功德眷屬，　施戒淨諸根。

大德仙從道，　苦行林失榮，
如人捨戒德，　空身而徒生。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"and the friction of confused and disordered thought kindles it further; the fires of craving, desire, and hatred then burn sentient beings to their very core.

The fire of these afflictions, once ablaze, never rests — submerging beings deep in birth and death, this fire of suffering, too, burns on forever.

Having seen these two kinds of fire, ablaze with nothing at all to shelter one from them — how could any person of true feeling fail to give rise to weariness and renunciation at the sight?

Weariness and renunciation remove craving and desire; once craving is exhausted, liberation is attained; and once liberation is attained, the very knowledge and vision of that liberation arises.

Observing closely the current of birth and death, and taking up instead the holy life — once all of this has been accomplished, one receives no further existence to come."

Hearing the World-Honored One teach the Dharma in this way, these thousand monks found that not a single defiling outflow arose in them ever again, and every one of their minds attained liberation.

The Buddha then taught Kāśyapa and these thousand monks further still: "What needed to be done has now been done; pure wisdom stands as your own wondrous adornment, and the whole retinue of your virtues — generosity, moral discipline, and purified faculties — is now complete.

A great and virtuous sage who abandons the true path, losing the very glory found in the forest of ascetic practice, is no different from a man who casts aside moral virtue altogether — his body left empty, living out his life in vain."

Arrival at Rājagṛha, and Kāśyapa's Testimony

Chinese Source

世尊大眷屬，　進詣王舍城，
憶念摩竭王，　先所修要誓。

世尊既至已，　止住於杖林，
瓶沙王聞之，　與大眷屬俱，
舉國士女從，　往詣世尊所。

遠見如來坐，　降心伏諸根，
除去諸俗容，　下車而步進，
猶如天帝釋，　往詣梵天王。

前頂禮佛足，　敬問體和安，
佛還慰勞畢，　命令一面坐。

時王心默念：　「釋迦大威力，
勝德迦葉等，　今皆為弟子？」

佛知眾心念，　而問於迦葉：
「汝見何福利，　而棄事火法？」

迦葉聞佛命，　驚起大眾前，
胡跪而合掌，　高聲白佛言：
「修福事火神，　果報悉輪迴，
生死煩惱增，　是故我棄捨。

精勤奉事火，　為求五欲境，
愛欲增無窮，　是故我棄捨。

事火修呪術，　離解脫受生，
受生為苦本，　故捨更求安。

我本謂苦行，　祠祀設大會，
為最第一勝，　而更違正道。

是故今棄捨，　更求勝寂滅，
離生老病死，　無盡清涼處。

以知此義故，　放捨事火法。」

世尊聞迦葉，　說自知見事，
欲令諸世間，　普生淨信故，
而告迦葉言：　「汝大士善來，
分別種種法，　而從於勝道。

今於大眾前，　顯汝勝功德，
如巨富長者，　開現於寶藏，
令貧苦眾生，　增其厭離心。」

「善哉奉尊教。」　即於大眾前，
斂身入正受，　飄然昇虛空，
經行住坐臥，　或舉身洞然，
左右出水火，　不燒亦不濡。

從身出雲雨，　雷電動天地，
舉世悉瞻仰，　縱目觀無厭。

異口而同音，　稱歎未曾有。

然後攝神通，　敬禮世尊足。

「佛為我大師，　我為尊弟子，
奉教聞斯行，　所作已畢竟。」

舉世普見彼，　迦葉為弟子，
決定知世尊，　真實一切智。

Ru-Yi Translation

The World-Honored One, together with his great company of followers, then made his way to Rājagṛha, recalling the solemn vow he had once made to the king of Magadha.

Once the World-Honored One had arrived, he settled at the Bamboo Grove; and King Bimbisāra, hearing of this, came with his own great retinue, the whole kingdom's men and women following behind him, to where the World-Honored One sat.

Seeing the Tathāgata seated there from a distance, he humbled his own heart and mastered every one of his faculties, setting aside all worldly show, and descended from his carriage to approach on foot — just as Śakra, lord of the gods, once went on foot to visit Brahmā himself.

He came forward and bowed his head at the Buddha's own feet, respectfully asking after his health and well-being; and the Buddha, having offered him the same courteous greeting in return, bid him be seated to one side.

The king thought silently to himself: "Could it truly be that Kāśyapa, of such great power and excellent virtue, is now a disciple of this Śākya sage?"

The Buddha, knowing the thought in the assembly's own hearts, asked Kāśyapa directly: "What benefit did you see, that led you to abandon the practice of fire-worship?"

Kāśyapa, receiving the Buddha's own question, rose in astonishment before the whole assembly, and, kneeling with palms joined, declared in a loud voice: "In cultivating merit through the worship of the fire-god, the fruit obtained was, in every case, only further circling through birth and death, only a deepening of the afflictions of birth and death — and it was for this reason that I cast it aside.

Diligently serving the fire in pursuit of the realm of the five desires only deepened my own craving and desire without end — and it was for this reason, too, that I cast it aside.

Fire-worship and the cultivation of ritual incantations only lead one further from liberation and toward yet further rebirth — and since rebirth is itself the very root of suffering, I abandoned it, and sought instead a true peace.

I had once believed that ascetic austerity and the holding of great sacrificial assemblies were themselves the very finest and most excellent path — only to find that they, too, run contrary to the true path.

And so I have now cast them aside, seeking instead the far more excellent quiescent extinction, free of birth, old age, sickness, and death, the inexhaustible place of true coolness and peace.

It was through coming to understand this very meaning that I set aside the practice of fire-worship entirely."

The World-Honored One, hearing Kāśyapa recount this understanding he had reached for himself, and wishing that the whole world might come, through this, to give rise to a pure and genuine faith, said to Kāśyapa: "You are most welcome, great one, having discerned so many different teachings and now following the most excellent path of all.

Now, before this whole assembly, display your own excellent spiritual powers — as a great and wealthy elder might open up his own treasury to reveal its contents — so that these poor and suffering beings here might have their own weariness with the world deepened all the more."

"How excellent — I shall obey your honored instruction." And so, right there before the whole assembly, he composed his own body and entered true absorption, rising lightly into the empty sky, walking, standing, sitting, and lying down there in the air; at times his whole body blazed with fire, and water and fire poured forth from his left and right sides at once, and yet he was neither burned nor drenched by either one.

Clouds and rain issued forth from his own body, and thunder and lightning shook heaven and earth; the whole assembly gazed up at him in wonder, their eyes never once growing weary of the sight.

With one voice, though from many mouths, they cried out in praise of this wonder such as had never before been seen.

Only then did he withdraw his own spiritual powers, and bow in reverence at the World-Honored One's own feet.

"The Buddha is my great teacher, and I am his honored disciple; having received his teaching and heard and practiced accordingly, what needed to be done has now, for me, been fully accomplished."

The whole assembly, seeing with their own eyes that Kāśyapa was truly the Buddha's disciple, came to know with certainty that the World-Honored One was indeed possessed of true and complete omniscience.

The Teaching on Non-Self

Chinese Source

佛知諸會眾，　堪為受法器，
而告瓶沙王：　「汝今善諦聽！

心意及諸根，　斯皆生滅法，
了知生滅過，　是則平等觀。

如是平等觀，　是則為知身，
知身生滅法，　無取亦無受。

如身諸根覺，　無我無我所，
純一苦積聚，　苦生而苦滅。

已知諸身相，　無我無我所，
是則之第一，　無盡清涼處。

我見等煩惱，　繫縛諸世間，
既見無我所，　諸縛悉解脫。

不實見所縛，　見實則解脫，
世間攝受我，　則為邪攝受。

若彼有我者，　或常或無常，
生死二邊見，　其過最尤甚。

若使無常者，　修行則無果，
亦不受後身，　無功而解脫。

若使有常者，　無死生中間，
則應同虛空，　無生亦無滅。

若使有我者，　則應一切同，
一切皆有我，　無業果自成。

若有我作者，　不應苦修行，
彼有自在主，　何須造作為？

若我則有常，　理不容變異，
見有苦樂相，　云何言有常？

知生則解脫，　遠離諸塵垢，
一切悉有常，　何用解脫為？

無我不唯言，　理實無實性，
不見我作事，　云何說我作？

我既無所作，　亦無作我者，
無此二事故，　真實無有我。

無作者知者，　無主而常遷，
生死日夜流，　汝今聽我說。

六根六境界，　因緣六識生，
三事會生觸，　心念業隨轉。

陽珠遇乾草，　緣日火隨生，
諸根境界識，　士夫生亦然。

芽因種子生，　種非即是芽，
不即亦不異，　眾生生亦然。」

世尊說真實，　平等第一義，
瓶沙王歡喜，　離垢法眼生。

王眷屬人民，　百千諸鬼神，
聞說甘露法，　亦隨離諸塵。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha, knowing that the whole assembly was now a fit vessel to receive the Dharma, said to King Bimbisāra: "Listen now, and consider carefully what I say!

Mind and intention, and every one of the faculties, are all subject to arising and cessation — and to fully understand the fault inherent in this arising and cessation is itself what is called perfectly equal contemplation.

Such perfectly equal contemplation is itself what it means to truly know the body; and to know the body as subject to arising and cessation is to hold to it neither by grasping nor by any sense of ownership.

Just as the body and its faculties of awareness are themselves without any self and without anything belonging to a self, so, too, is this entire aggregation nothing but suffering in its purest form, arising and ceasing as suffering alone.

Having thus known that every mark of the body is without any self and without anything belonging to a self, this alone is the very foremost and inexhaustible place of true coolness and peace.

The view that clings to a self, and every affliction like it, binds fast the whole of this world — but once one sees that there is no self and nothing belonging to a self, every one of these bonds is entirely loosened.

It is a false view that binds a person fast; seeing the truth of the matter is itself liberation — and the world's own clinging to a self is nothing but a mistaken and false form of clinging.

Should there indeed be a self, whether one holds it to be permanent or impermanent, both of these views fall to the two extremes concerning birth and death, and the fault in either case is exceedingly grave.

Should the self be impermanent, then spiritual practice would bear no fruit at all, since one would receive no future body to inherit its result, and liberation itself would be attained without any true merit behind it.

Should the self instead be permanent, then there would be no interval at all between death and birth, and it would have to be exactly like empty space, neither arising nor ceasing at all.

Should there indeed be a self, then it ought to be exactly the same in every being — every being would possess a self in precisely the same way, and the fruits of karma would then arise of themselves, without any actual karmic action behind them at all.

Should there be a self that is itself the doer of actions, then there would be no need to labor at spiritual practice at all — for if that self already possessed complete sovereignty over itself, what need would there be for any further striving or effort?

And should this self be permanent, then by its very nature it could admit of no change or alteration at all — yet we plainly observe the marks of suffering and pleasure arising and changing constantly; how, then, could one possibly call it permanent?

To know birth truly is itself to attain liberation, far removed from every trace of defilement — yet if everything were entirely permanent already, what use could liberation possibly serve at all?

To say there is no self is not merely an assertion made in words — the very nature of reality itself holds no such fixed, real self at all; since we never actually observe any self performing any action, how could one possibly speak of "the self acting" at all?

Since the self performs no action of its own, and there is likewise no agent that produces this self, and since neither of these two things can be found at all, it follows that, in ultimate truth, there is no self whatsoever.

There is no doer and no knower, no master presiding over anything, and yet everything is in constant flux, the very stream of birth and death flowing on day and night without pause — now listen closely as I explain this to you.

The six sense-faculties and their six corresponding objects give rise, through the aggregation of conditions, to the six consciousnesses; when these three things come together, contact arises, and thought and karmic action follow along in its wake.

Just as a burning-glass, meeting dry grass, produces fire through the condition of the sun's own light, so too do the faculties, their objects, and consciousness give rise, in this same way, to the very arising of a person.

A sprout arises from its seed, and yet the seed itself is not identical to the sprout — neither the same as it nor entirely different from it either; and the arising of a sentient being is, in this same way, exactly the same."

As the World-Honored One spoke this truth, this perfectly equal and ultimate meaning, King Bimbisāra rejoiced greatly, and the pure Dharma-eye, free of every defilement, arose within him.

The king's own household and people, and hundreds and thousands of spirits and demons besides, hearing this teaching of the Dharma of immortality, likewise attained freedom from every trace of defilement along with him.
