The Courtesan Āmrapālī Sees the Buddha

As the thought of final nirvāṇa begins to form in him, the Buddha crosses the Ganges by his own spiritual power rather than by boat, giving the ford its lasting name, and blesses the fortified town that will grow into Pāṭaliputra. At Vaiśālī, the wealthy courtesan Āmrapālī comes to see him in her own mango grove — and before she arrives, he turns to his monks with one of the poem's most direct warnings: that a woman's beauty, examined honestly rather than through the distortions of desire, has no more substance than any other compounded, impermanent thing, and that guarding the mind against it matters more than any external circumstance.

Crossing the Ganges

Chinese Source

佛所行讚菴摩羅女見佛品第二十二

世尊廣化畢，　而生涅槃心，
發於王舍城，　詣巴連弗邑。

到已住於彼，　婆吒利支提，
彼是摩竭提，　邊邑附庸國。

國主婆羅門，　多聞明經典，
瞻相土安危，　國之仰觀師。

摩竭王遣使，　勅告彼仰觀，
命起於牢城，　以備於強隣。

世尊記彼地，　天神所保持，
於中起城郭，　永固不危亡。

仰觀心歡喜，　共養佛法僧，
佛出彼城門，　往詣恒河濱。

仰觀深敬佛，　名為瞿曇門，
恒河側人民，　皆出迎世尊，
興種種供養，　各嚴船令渡。

世尊以船多，　偏受違眾心，
即以神通力，　隱身及大眾，
忽從此岸沒，　而出於彼岸。

以乘智慧船，　廣濟於眾生，
緣斯德力故，　濟河不憑舟。

恒河側人民，　同聲唱奇哉，
咸言名此津，　名為瞿曇津。

城門瞿曇門，　津名瞿曇津，
斯名流於世，　歷代共稱傳。

如來復前行，　至彼鳩梨村，
說法多所化，　復至那提村。

人民多疫死，　親戚悉來問：
「諸親疫死者，　命終生何所？」

佛善知業報，　悉隨問記說。

前至鞞舍離，　住於菴羅林。

Ru-Yi Translation

Buddhacarita, Chapter Twenty-Two: The Courtesan Āmrapālī Sees the Buddha

The World-Honored One, having now completed his wide-ranging work of teaching and conversion, felt the thought of final nirvāṇa begin to arise in him; and he set out from Rājagṛha toward the town of Pāṭaliputra.

Arriving there, he settled at the shrine of Pāṭali, in that town — itself a border settlement dependent on the kingdom of Magadha.

The lord of that town was a brahmin of wide learning, versed in scripture, who read the auspicious and inauspicious signs of the land, serving as the country's own official diviner.

The king of Magadha had sent an envoy with orders to this diviner, commanding him to raise a fortified city there, in preparation against powerful neighboring states.

The World-Honored One declared of that place that it was guarded and protected by the gods themselves, and that a city built there would remain secure forever, never falling into ruin or danger.

The diviner, filled with joy at this, made offerings to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the saṅgha together; and the Buddha, leaving through the gate of that town, made his way toward the bank of the Ganges.

The diviner, in his deep reverence for the Buddha, named that very gate "Gautama's Gate"; and the people who dwelt along the Ganges all came out to greet the World-Honored One, making every kind of offering and preparing boats to carry him across.

The World-Honored One, seeing that there were so many boats offered that accepting only one would go against the wishes of the rest, used his own spiritual power instead to make himself and his whole assembly vanish from this shore and appear at once upon the far shore.

Riding, as it were, the very boat of wisdom, he carries sentient beings across on a vast scale — and it was through the power of this same virtue that he crossed the river now without relying on any boat at all.

The people dwelling along the Ganges cried out together in wonder, and all agreed to name that very ford "Gautama's Ford."

The city gate called "Gautama's Gate," and the ford called "Gautama's Ford" — these very names have been passed down in the world, remembered and repeated across the generations ever since.

The Tathāgata continued on his way, arriving at the village of Koṭigrāma, where he taught the Dharma and converted many there, and then went on further to the village of Nādikā.

The people there had suffered many deaths from plague, and their kinsmen all came to ask him: "Where have our relatives who died of this plague now been reborn?"

The Buddha, knowing well the workings of karmic retribution, answered every one of their questions in full, according to what they asked.

He then went on further to Vaiśālī, and settled there in the Āmra grove.

A Warning About Desire

Chinese Source

彼菴摩羅女，　承佛詣其園，
侍女眾隨從，　庠序出奉迎。

善執諸情根，　身服輕素衣，
捨離莊嚴服，　自沐浴香花。

猶世貞賢女，　潔素以祠天，
端正妙容姿，　猶天玉女形。

佛遙見女來，　告諸比丘眾：
「此女極端正，　能留行者情，
汝等當正念，　以慧鎮其心。

寧在暴虎口，　狂夫利劍下，
不於女人所，　而起愛欲情。

女人顯恣態，　若行住坐臥，
乃至畫像形，　悉表妖姿容，
劫奪人善心，　如何不自防？

現啼笑憙怒，　縱體而垂肩，
或散髮髻傾，　猶尚亂人心。

況復飾容儀？　以顯妙姿顏，
莊嚴隱陋形，　誘誑於愚夫。

迷亂生德想，　不覺醜穢形，
當觀無常苦，　不淨無我所。

諦見其真實，　滅除貪欲想，
正觀於自境，　天女尚不樂。

況復人間欲，　而能留人心？

當執精進弓，　智慧鋒利箭，
被正念重鎧，　決戰於五欲。

寧以熱鐵槍，　貫徹於雙目，
不以愛欲心，　而觀於女色。

愛欲迷其心，　炫惑於女色，
亂想而命終，　必墮三惡道，
畏彼惡道苦，　不受女人欺。

根不繫境界，　境界不繫根，
於中貪欲想，　由根繫境界。

猶如二耕牛，　同一軛一鞅，
牛不轉相縛，　根境界亦然。

是故當制心，　勿令其放逸。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Āmrapālī, learning that the Buddha had come to her own grove, came out with her whole retinue of attendant women, in dignified and orderly procession, to receive him.

Mastering well her own senses and faculties, she wore only plain, light garments, having set aside every fine ornament, and had bathed herself simply with fragrant water and flowers.

Like a chaste and virtuous woman of the world, pure and unadorned as she goes to make offering to the gods, her form was dignified and her countenance wondrously fine, like the very form of a heavenly maiden.

The Buddha, seeing this woman approach from a distance, said to the assembly of monks: "This woman is exceedingly beautiful, capable of ensnaring the heart of any practitioner on the path — you must all hold to right mindfulness, and use wisdom to steady your own hearts against her.

Better to be caught in the jaws of a raging tiger, or beneath the sharp blade of a madman, than to give rise to a heart of craving and desire in the presence of a woman.

A woman's every unguarded gesture — whether walking, standing, sitting, or lying down, even down to a mere painted image of her form — displays some seductive charm; how could one fail to guard oneself against something that plunders a person's own good heart in this way?

Even her weeping, her laughter, her delight, her anger, the careless slump of her shoulders, her hair falling loose or her topknot tilted askew — even these alone are enough to throw a person's heart into confusion.

How much more so, then, when she deliberately adorns her own appearance, displaying every wondrous grace and charm, using such adornment to conceal what is in truth an unlovely form, and thereby deceiving and beguiling the foolish?

Confused and deluded, they conceive of it as something virtuous and fine, never perceiving the hideous impurity actually there — one ought instead to contemplate it as impermanent and suffering, impure, and without self or anything belonging to a self.

Seeing its true nature clearly, and thereby extinguishing every thought of craving and desire — for even a heavenly maiden herself, rightly examined as she truly is, brings no true delight at all.

How much less, then, could mere human desire possibly hold any person's heart captive?

You ought to take up the bow of diligent effort, and the sharp arrow of wisdom, and, clad in the heavy armor of right mindfulness, wage a decisive battle against the five desires.

Better to have a burning iron spike driven straight through both one's own eyes, than to gaze upon a woman's beauty with a heart full of craving and desire.

Craving and desire cloud the heart, dazzled and bewildered by a woman's beauty; and should one's thoughts remain thus disordered at the moment of death, one is certain to fall into one of the three evil destinies — fearing that very suffering of the evil destinies, do not let yourselves be deceived by women.

The sense-faculty is not, in itself, bound to its object, nor is the object bound to the faculty — it is only the thought of craving and desire arising between them that binds the faculty to the object at all.

It is like two plow-oxen yoked together beneath the same harness and collar: the oxen themselves are not, by nature, bound to one another — and the faculty and its object are exactly the same.

And so you must master your own minds, and never let them fall into careless self-indulgence."

Āmrapālī's Offering

Chinese Source

佛為諸比丘，　種種說法已，
彼菴摩羅女，　漸至世尊前。

見佛坐樹下，　禪定靜思惟，
念佛大悲心，　哀受我樹林。

端心斂儀容，　止素妖冶情，
恭形心純至，　稽首接足禮。

世尊命令坐，　隨心為說法：
「汝心已純靜，　表徹外德容，
壯年豐財寶，　備德兼姿顏，
能信樂正法，　是則世之難。

丈夫宿智慧，　樂法非為奇，
女人情志弱，　智淺愛欲深，
而能樂正法，　此亦為甚難。

人生於世間，　唯應法自娛，
財色非常寶，　唯正法為珍。

強良病所壞，　少壯老所遷，
命為死所困，　行法無能侵，
所愛莫不離，　不愛而強隣，
所求不隨意，　唯法為從心，
他力為大苦，　自在力為歡。

女人悉由他，　兼懷他子苦，
是故當思惟，　厭離於女身。」

彼菴摩羅女，　聞法心歡喜，
堅固智增明，　能斷於愛欲，
即自厭女身，　不染於境界。

雖耻於陋形，　法力勸其心，
稽首而白佛：　「已蒙尊攝受，
哀受明供養，　令滿其志願。」

佛知彼誠心，　兼利諸群生，
默然受其請。　令即隨歡喜，
視聽轉增明，　作禮而還家。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having taught the monks this teaching, in its various parts, the Buddha then received Āmrapālī, who gradually approached and came before the World-Honored One.

Seeing the Buddha seated beneath a tree in meditative absorption, quietly deep in thought, she thought to herself: may the Buddha, in his great compassion, graciously accept this grove of mine as an offering.

Composing her own heart and gathering in her outward bearing, she set aside every trace of her former seductive manner; with a reverent form and a heart of true sincerity, she bowed her head and touched her lips to his feet in homage.

The World-Honored One bid her be seated, and taught her the Dharma according to what her heart required: "Your heart is already pure and still, and this shows clearly through your own outward and excellent bearing; to be in the fullness of youth, rich in wealth, complete in both virtue and beauty, and yet still able to have faith and delight in the true Dharma — this is a rare thing indeed, even in this world.

For a man of long-cultivated wisdom to delight in the Dharma is no great wonder; but for a woman, whose feelings and resolve tend toward weakness, whose wisdom often runs shallow while her craving and desire run deep, to be able to delight in the true Dharma as you do — this, too, is an exceedingly rare thing.

A person born into this world ought to take the Dharma alone as their own true source of delight — for wealth and beauty are no lasting treasure; only the true Dharma is a genuine treasure.

The strong and healthy are broken down by illness; the young and vigorous are worn away by old age; life itself is beset by death on every side — and yet the practice of the Dharma cannot be touched by any of these things. What one loves is never left unparted from; what one does not love still forces itself close regardless; what one seeks does not follow one's own wishes — only the Dharma follows the heart exactly as one wishes it to. To depend on something outside oneself is itself a great suffering; to hold true sovereignty within oneself is itself true joy.

A woman's whole existence depends entirely on others, and she must besides bear the pains of carrying another's child within her own body — and so you ought to reflect on this, and grow weary of and renounce the very state of being a woman."

Āmrapālī, hearing this teaching, felt her heart fill with joy; her firm wisdom grew ever more radiant, able now to cut off craving and desire entirely, and she came at once to feel a genuine weariness with her own womanhood, no longer tainted by attachment to the objects of the senses.

Though she felt shame at her own unlovely condition, the power of the Dharma moved her heart, and she bowed her head and said to the Buddha: "I have already been graciously accepted by you, the Honored One — I beg you, out of compassion, to accept as well this clear and simple offering of mine, that my own wish might be fulfilled."

The Buddha, knowing the sincerity of her heart, and seeing besides the benefit it would bring to sentient beings everywhere, silently accepted her request. She, filled with joy at this, found her own sight and hearing grow only more radiant still, and, having paid him homage, returned to her own home.
