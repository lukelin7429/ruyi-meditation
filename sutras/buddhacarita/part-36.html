Nirvāṇa (II)

The elder's son Cunda offers the Buddha his very last meal, and the journey continues across two rivers to Kuśinagara, where the Buddha bathes one final time in the Hiraṇyavatī and lies down between a pair of sal trees, on his right side facing north, in the very posture he will hold until the end. Word reaches the Malla people of the city, who arrive in a state of open grief and disarray — and the Buddha's own response to their sorrow is neither distant nor cold, but a direct argument that what he is about to attain is a release worth rejoicing in, not a loss worth mourning.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 36 of 43 complete Source: Taishō T0192, 佛所行讚 (tr. Dharmakṣema, early 5th c.)

Cunda's Last Offering

Chinese Source

說斯教誡已，　至於波婆城，
彼諸力士眾，　設種種供養。

時有長者子，　其名曰純陀，
請佛至其舍，　供設最後飯。

飯食說法畢，　行詣鳩夷城，
度於蕨蕨河，　及熙連二河。

彼有堅固林，　安隱閑靜處，
入金河洗浴，　身若真金山。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having given this instruction, he arrived at the city of Pāvā, where the Malla people there made every kind of offering to him.

At that time there was an elder's son named Cunda, who invited the Buddha to his own house, and there offered him his very last meal.

Having eaten this meal and taught the Dharma, he went on toward the city of Kuśinagara, crossing two rivers, the Kakutthā and the Hiraṇyavatī.

There he found a grove of sal trees, a peaceful and quiet place, and entered the Hiraṇyavatī, the "Golden River," to bathe, his body shining there like a very mountain of true gold.

Lying Down Between the Twin Trees

Chinese Source

告勅阿難陀：　「於彼雙樹間，
掃灑令清淨，　安置於繩床。

吾今中夜時，　當入於涅槃。」

阿難聞佛教，　氣塞而心悲，
行泣而奉教，　布置訖還白。

如來就繩床，　北首右脇臥，
枕手累雙足，　猶如師子王，
畢苦後邊身，　一臥永不起。

弟子眾圍遶，　哀歎世眼滅，
風止林流靜，　鳥獸寂無聲。

樹木汁淚流，　華葉非時零，
未離欲人天，　悉皆大惶怖。

如人遊曠澤，　道險未至村，
但恐行不至，　心懼形怱怱。

如來畢竟臥，　而告阿難陀：
「往告諸力士，　我涅槃時至。

彼若不見我，　永恨生大苦。」

Ru-Yi Translation

He instructed Ānanda: "Between those twin trees over there, sweep and cleanse the ground thoroughly, and set out a rope-strung couch for me.

This very night, at its middle watch, I shall enter final nirvāṇa."

Ānanda, hearing the Buddha's own command, felt his breath catch and his heart fill with grief; weeping as he went, he carried out the instruction, and, once the couch was arranged, returned to report that it was ready.

The Tathāgata went to the rope-strung couch and lay down upon it, his head to the north, on his right side, resting his head upon his own hand with his feet placed one upon the other, just as a lion-king reclines — his very last body, having now finished with suffering, lying down there this once, never to rise again.

His disciples gathered around him in mournful lament, grieving that the very eye of the world was about to close; the wind fell still, the streams in the grove grew silent, and every bird and beast fell hushed without a single sound.

The very trees wept sap like tears, and their flowers and leaves fell out of season; every god and human being not yet free of desire was thrown into the greatest dread and alarm.

It was like a traveler crossing a vast marshland, the road perilous and the village not yet reached, fearing only that he might never arrive at all, his heart full of dread and his steps hurried and anxious.

The Tathāgata, having now lain down for the last time, said to Ānanda: "Go and tell the Malla people that the time of my nirvāṇa has come.

Should they fail to see me one last time, they will carry a lasting regret, and suffer greatly for it."

The Mallas' Grief

Chinese Source

阿難受佛教，　悲泣而隨路，
告彼諸力士：　「世尊已畢竟。」

諸力士聞之，　極生大恐怖，
士女奔馳出，　號泣至佛所，
弊衣而散髮，　蒙塵身流汗，
號慟詣彼林，　猶如天福盡，
垂淚禮佛足，　憂悲身萎熟。

如來安慰說：　「汝等勿憂悴，
今應隨喜時，　不宜生憂慼。

長劫之所規，　我今始獲得，
已度根境界，　無盡清涼處。

離地水火風，　寂靜不生滅，
永除於憂患，　云何為我憂？

我昔伽闍山，　欲捨於此身，
以本因緣故，　存世至於今。

守斯危脆身，　如毒蛇同居，
今入於大寂，　眾苦緣已畢。

不復更受身，　未來苦長息，
汝等不復應，　為我生恐怖。」

力士聞佛說，　入於大寂靜，
心亂而目冥，　如覩大黑闇。

合掌白佛言：　「佛離生死苦，
永之寂滅樂，　我等實欣慶。

猶如被燒舍，　親從盛火出，
諸天猶歡喜，　何況於世人？

如來既滅後，　群生無所覩，
永違於救護，　是故生憂悲。

譬如商人眾，　遠涉於曠野，
唯有一導師，　忽然中道亡，
大眾無所怙，　云何不憂悲？

現世自證知，　覩一切知見，
而不獲勝利，　舉世所應笑。

譬如經寶山，　愚癡守貧苦。」

如是諸力士，　向佛而悲訴，
猶如人一子，　悲訴於慈父。

佛以善誘辭，　顯示第一義，
告諸力士眾：　「誠如汝所言，
求道須精勤；　非但見我得。

如我所說行，　得離眾苦網。

行道存於心，　不必由見我。

猶如疾病人，　依方服良藥，
眾病自然除，　不待見醫師。

不如我說行，　空見我無益。

雖與我相遠，　行法為近我；
同止不隨法，　當知去我遠。

攝心莫放逸，　精勤修正業。

人生於世間，　長夜眾苦迫，
擾動不自安，　猶若風中燈。」

時諸力士眾，　聞佛慈悲教，
內感而收淚，　強自抑止歸。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ānanda, receiving the Buddha's command, wept as he went along the road, and told the Malla people there: "The World-Honored One's time has come."

The Mallas, hearing this, were seized with the greatest dread; men and women rushed out at once, weeping and crying aloud as they made their way to the Buddha, their clothes disheveled, their hair unbound, their bodies covered in dust and streaming with sweat. Wailing as they went, they arrived at the grove as though the very fortune of heaven itself had run out, and, weeping, bowed at the Buddha's feet, their bodies wasted with grief and sorrow.

The Tathāgata comforted them, saying: "Do not let yourselves waste away in grief — this is a moment that calls, rather, for shared rejoicing; it is not fitting to give way to sorrow now.

What I have planned across such long ages, I am only now, at last, about to attain — having already crossed beyond the very realm of the faculties and their objects, into the inexhaustible place of true coolness and peace.

Departing from earth, water, fire, and wind, in quiescent stillness beyond all arising and ceasing, forever free of every affliction — why, then, should you grieve on my account?

Long ago, at Mount Gayā, I had already resolved to relinquish this very body — yet, for the sake of certain causes and conditions, I have remained in the world until this very day.

To guard this fragile and perilous body has been like sharing my own dwelling with a venomous serpent — and now, entering this great quiescence, every condition tied to such suffering is finally brought to its end.

Never again to take up another body, every future suffering now brought to its long and final rest — you, then, ought no longer to feel any dread on my account at all."

The Mallas, hearing the Buddha speak of entering this great and final stillness, felt their own minds thrown into confusion, their eyes darkened as though beholding a vast and impenetrable darkness.

Joining their palms, they said to the Buddha: "That you, Buddha, are departing from the suffering of birth and death, into the eternal bliss of quiescent extinction — in this alone we truly do rejoice with you.

It is like a man escaping from his own burning house, safely emerging from the raging flames — even the gods would rejoice at such an escape; how much more so, then, should we, mere mortals, rejoice for you?

Yet once the Tathāgata has passed away, sentient beings will have nothing left to look upon, forever deprived of your protection and care — and it is for this reason that grief and sorrow arise in us.

It is like a company of merchants traveling far across a vast wilderness, with only a single guide to lead them, who suddenly dies partway along the road — the whole company left with nothing to rely upon; how could they possibly fail to grieve?

To have realized, in this very life, direct knowledge of all things, and yet to fail to secure some lasting benefit from it for those left behind, would be a thing the whole world would rightly laugh at —

like passing through a mountain full of treasure, and yet, in one's own foolishness, guarding nothing but poverty and hardship instead."

In this way the Malla people poured out their grief before the Buddha, just as an only child might pour out his sorrow before his own loving father.

The Buddha, with well-chosen and skillful words, revealed to them the ultimate truth of the matter, saying to the assembled Mallas: "It is truly as you say — the seeking of the path requires diligent effort; it is not attained merely by seeing me in person.

Whoever practices in accordance with what I have taught will attain freedom from the whole net of suffering.

The practice of the path rests in the heart alone — it does not depend on actually seeing me at all.

It is like a sick person who, relying on a proper prescription, takes the right medicine — every illness is thereby naturally cured, without needing to see the physician who prescribed it in person.

To fail to practice as I have taught, while merely gazing upon me in person, brings no benefit at all.

Even one far removed from me in distance, if he practices the Dharma, remains close to me indeed; while one who dwells right alongside me, yet fails to follow the Dharma, ought to be understood as being, in truth, far removed from me.

Master your own minds, and do not fall into carelessness — apply yourselves diligently to the cultivation of right conduct.

A person born into this world is beset, through the long night of existence, by every kind of suffering, disturbed and unable to find any peace within himself, like a lamp flickering in the wind."

Hearing the Buddha's own compassionate teaching, the Malla people felt moved within, and, wiping away their tears, forced themselves to master their own grief and returned to their homes.
