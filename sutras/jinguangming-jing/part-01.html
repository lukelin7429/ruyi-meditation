Fascicle One: The Golden Drum and the Repentance

This part gives the first fascicle of the Golden Light Sūtra (T0663) in Dharmakṣema's four-fascicle translation of the early fifth century, the version on which Zhiyi lectured. It opens with a preface by the Song Chan master Zongze, dated 1081, which reads the sūtra through the pairing of nature and marks, emptiness and practice. The first chapter announces the sūtra as "king of sūtras," protected by the four buddhas of the four directions and by a long list of gods and spirits. In the second, the bodhisattva Faith-Mark wonders why Śākyamuni lived only eighty years; the four buddhas appear in his chamber and answer in verse that the Buddha's lifespan cannot be measured. The third chapter is the heart of the fascicle: that night Faith-Mark dreams of a golden drum struck by a brahmin, and the drum's sound becomes a long verse of repentance, vows, praise of the Buddha, and rejoicing. This is the text that Zhiyi's and Zhili's Golden Light repentance rites draw on. The fourth chapter tells of a past king, Golden Dragon Honored, who praised the buddhas and vowed to hear the golden drum in his dreams; he is revealed as Faith-Mark himself. The fifth chapter teaches emptiness briefly in verse: the body as an empty village of six bandits, the four elements as snakes in one basket, and the twelve links as arising from ignorance that has no substance.

Preface to the Golden Light Sūtra

真定府十方洪濟禪院住持傳法慈覺大師宗賾述

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Zongze, Great Master Cijue, Dharma-transmitting abbot of the Hongji Chan Monastery of the Ten Directions, Zhending Prefecture

Chinese Source

夫靈心絕待，泯萬物而獨存。妙行難思，諸緣普現，則實際理地雖不受於一毫，而事相門中不捨於一法。故如來出世，說《金光明經》，依清淨心，建解脫行，顯出塵之經卷，永廓迷情；示伏藏之寶王，頓圓大用。夫以信相為起教之人，則菩薩無滯空之行；於堅牢有贊空之頌，則法身非有相之求。性相該通，有無互建，則一經之要義在於斯。

Ru-Yi Translation

The numinous mind is cut off from all dependence: it dissolves the myriad things and alone remains. Its wondrous practice is hard to conceive: all conditions appear everywhere. Thus, although on the ground of principle, the reality-limit, not a single hair is received, within the gate of phenomenal marks not a single dharma is discarded. Therefore the Tathāgata appeared in the world and taught the Golden Light Sūtra: relying on the pure mind, he established the practice of liberation; he revealed the sūtra-scroll beyond the dust, forever opening up deluded feelings; he showed the jewel-king of the hidden treasury, suddenly perfecting the great function. Since Faith-Mark is taken as the person who occasions the teaching, the bodhisattva has no practice that stagnates in emptiness; since in [the address to] Dṛḍhā there are verses praising emptiness, the Dharma-body is not something sought through marks. Nature and marks pervade each other; existence and nonexistence establish each other: the essential meaning of the whole sūtra lies in this.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

As for clarifying the source of the dharma-nature of the buddhas and probing the root of the Tathāgata's lifespan — the golden light illuminating everywhere and opening up the gate of the wisdom that knows all things, the golden drum sending forth its sound and displaying boundless wondrous function — these are the source from which the bodhisattva sets out in practice. Repenting and removing the obstruction of offenses is at root the Dharma-gate of [all things being] like a dream; praising the Tathāgata holds up the wondrous fruit of cultivation. Mahāsattva's feeding of the tiger — able to give up, able to carry it out; the elder Jalavāhana's rescuing of the fish — having a beginning and an end: these are the bodhisattva's practices that rely on the true.

Chinese Source

譬之明月當天，影沈眾水，真金作器，體應萬形，主伴圓融，一多自在，能於一法，受一切法，則信如來之祕藏，實為三界之寶乘，莊嚴性海之門，趣向覺王之本。是以十千天子，聞而頓證於菩提；一切眾生，入者皆同於甘露，則見聞隨喜，讀誦受持，於一念具了無上法門者，豈小緣哉。凡我同儔，勉思佛意。元豐四年三月十五日序。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is like the bright moon in the sky, whose reflection sinks into all waters; like true gold made into vessels, whose substance responds to the myriad forms. Principal and companion are perfectly interfused; one and many are freely sovereign; within one dharma it can receive all dharmas. Truly, then, it is the secret treasury of the Tathāgata, indeed the jewel vehicle of the three realms, the gate that adorns the ocean of nature, the root that leads toward the King of Awakening. Thus ten thousand sons of the gods, on hearing it, suddenly realized bodhi; all sentient beings who enter it become one with the sweet dew. So those who see and hear it and rejoice, who read, recite, receive, and uphold it, and in a single thought fully possess the unsurpassed Dharma-gate — how could theirs be a small affinity? All you my companions, strive to reflect on the Buddha's intent. Preface written on the fifteenth day of the third month of the fourth year of Yuanfeng [1081].

Chapter 2: Lifespan

Chinese Source

金光明經壽量品第二

Ru-Yi Translation

The Golden Light Sūtra, Chapter 2: Lifespan

Chinese Source

爾時王舍城中，有菩薩摩訶薩名曰信相，已曾供養過去無量億那由他百千諸佛，種諸善根。是信相菩薩作是思惟：「何因何緣，釋迦如來壽命短促方八十年？」復更念言：「如佛所說，有二因緣壽命得長。何等為二？一者不殺、二者施食。而我世尊，於無量百千億那由他阿僧祇劫，修不殺戒、具足十善，飲食惠施不可限量，乃至己身骨髓肉血，充足飽滿飢餓眾生，況餘飲食。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time in the city of Rājagṛha there was a bodhisattva-mahāsattva named Faith-Mark, who had already made offerings to immeasurable koṭis of nayutas of hundreds of thousands of buddhas of the past and planted wholesome roots. This Faith-Mark Bodhisattva thought thus: "For what cause and what condition is the lifespan of Śākyamuni Tathāgata so short, only eighty years?" He further thought: "As the Buddha has taught, there are two causes and conditions by which one's lifespan becomes long. What are the two? First, not killing; second, giving food. Yet our World-Honored One, for immeasurable hundreds of thousands of koṭis of nayutas of asaṃkhyeya eons, cultivated the precept of not killing and fully practiced the ten wholesome [deeds], and his giving of food and drink was beyond measure — even his own body, bone marrow, flesh, and blood he gave to fill and satisfy hungry beings, let alone other food and drink."

Chinese Source

大士如是至心念佛，思是義時，其室自然廣博嚴事，天紺琉璃種種眾寶，雜廁間錯以成其地，猶如如來所居淨土，有妙香氣過諸天香，煙雲垂布遍滿其室。其室四面，各有四寶上妙高座自然而出，純以天衣而為敷具，是妙座上各有諸佛所受用華，眾寶合成。於蓮華上有四如來：東方名阿閦、南方名寶相、西方名無量壽、北方名微妙聲，是四如來自然而坐師子座上，放大光明照王舍城，及此三千大千世界，乃至十方恒河沙等諸佛世界，雨諸天華作天妓樂。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the great being thus with utmost mind recollected the Buddha and pondered this matter, his chamber spontaneously became broad and vast, adorned [with] heavenly dark-blue lapis lazuli and all kinds of jewels, mixed and interspersed to form its floor, like the pure land in which the Tathāgata dwells. There was a wondrous fragrance surpassing the fragrances of the gods, whose clouds of smoke hung down and filled his chamber. On the four sides of the chamber, high seats of the four jewels, supremely wondrous, spontaneously appeared, spread entirely with heavenly garments; upon each of these wondrous seats were flowers used by the buddhas, made of many jewels. On the lotus flowers were four Tathāgatas: in the east, named Akṣobhya; in the south, named Jewel Mark; in the west, named Amitāyus; in the north, named Wondrous Voice. These four Tathāgatas spontaneously sat on lion thrones and emitted a great light that illuminated the city of Rājagṛha and this trichiliocosm, and even buddha-worlds of the ten directions as many as the sands of the Ganges; they rained heavenly flowers and made heavenly music.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time all the sentient beings of the trichiliocosm, through the Buddha's spiritual power, received heavenly happiness; those whose faculties were incomplete at once had them made complete. To speak of the essentials, all the benefits in the world, things never before seen, all appeared.

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Faith-Mark Bodhisattva, seeing these buddhas and these rare events, was overjoyed and leaped with delight; reverently joining his palms, he turned to the World-Honored Ones, recollected the Buddha with utmost mind, and thought thus: "The immeasurable merit of Śākyamuni Tathāgata — only concerning his lifespan does doubt arise in my mind. How is it that the Tathāgata's lifespan is like this, only eighty years?"

When the four Tathāgatas were about to proclaim the lifespan attained by Śākyamuni Buddha, the gods of the desire and form realms, the dragons, ghosts and spirits, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, and immeasurable hundreds of thousands of koṭis of nayutas of bodhisattva-mahāsattvas, through the Buddha's spiritual power, all came and gathered in the chamber of Faith-Mark Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva.

Reading Notes for Fascicle 1

Translator's notes

Preface. The preface by Zongze 宗賾 (Great Master Cijue 慈覺大師), dated Yuanfeng 4 (1081), is printed before the sūtra in the Taishō edition (T16, 335a). It is not part of the sūtra and refers ahead to later chapters: the tiger story (Chapter 17, Giving Up the Body), Jalavāhana and the fish (Chapter 16), and the ten thousand sons of the gods (Chapter 14).

Names fixed for the series. Names follow Zhili's Golden Light Repentance Rite (`./jinguangming-chanyi/p1_body.md`): Faith-Mark 信相, the four buddhas Akṣobhya / Jewel Mark / Amitāyus / Wondrous Voice, Dṛḍhā the earth goddess, Hārītī. New names (Golden Dragon Honored 金龍尊, Silver Mark 銀相, the chapter names) are listed under 後續新增 in NOTES_FOR_TRANSLATORS.md.

Lotus wording reused. "Thus have I heard … on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa near the great city of Rājagṛha," the eight classes (gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas), "trichiliocosm," "lion throne," "lapis lazuli," "sweet dew," "brought forth the resolve for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening," and koṭi / nayuta / asaṃkhyeya follow the site's Lotus Sūtra (`ref/lotus-sutra.txt`). 劫 is "eon" per the BRIEF (the Lotus page has "kalpa").

嚴事 (unit 20). "廣博嚴事" is odd; Yijing's later version (T0665, 金光明最勝王經) has 廣博嚴淨 at the same place, so 事 is probably a copying error for 淨 or 飾. Translated as "adorned."

Verse format. The sūtra's four-character verse is given about two Chinese phrases per English line; each CBETA verse unit is one stanza. Quotation levels: 「 = double quotes, 『 = single quotes.

Who speaks the repentance. In Chapter 3 the long verse (units 33–89) is the speech heard in the drum's sound, quoted by Faith-Mark; from unit 41 it turns into first-person repentance and vows ("I now repent"). This "I" is the voice later taken up by the practitioner in the repentance rites.

Addressee in Chapter 4. The Buddha begins by addressing Dṛḍhā the earth goddess ("Good goddess," unit 91) but ends by addressing Faith-Mark ("Faith-Mark, you should know," unit 113). Both are in the Chinese; not harmonized.

銀相等 (unit 114). "Silver Mark and the other": the second son is named Silver Light 銀光 in Chapter 14 (授記品).

善女 in Chapter 5 (unit 125). "Good woman" is not identified in the chapter; Zongze's preface links the emptiness verses with Dṛḍhā the earth goddess, the addressee of Chapter 4.

Four snakes in one basket (unit 121). The image of the body as four poisonous snakes appears also in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374, e.g. 自觀己身如四毒蛇); the wording here is the sūtra's own.

六情 / 六根 / 六入. Kept distinct: the six senses / the six faculties / the six entrances. 空聚 = "empty village."

意 in units 17 and 56. 身意 and 意思 are rendered with "intention"; the fixed triad 身口意 stays "body, speech, and mind."
