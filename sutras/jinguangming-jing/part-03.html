Fascicle Three: The Right Treatise and the Healer's Son

This part gives the third fascicle of the Golden Light Sūtra (T0663, tr. Dharmakṣema), Chapters 10 to 15. In Chapter 10 the great spirit general Saṃjñeya pledges, like the deities of the previous fascicle, to guard the one teaching the sūtra, and explains his name by his right understanding of all dharmas. Chapter 11 is a verse treatise on kingship that a past king, Power-Honored Mark, hands down to his son: a king is called "son of the gods" because the gods protect him, and if he lets evil go unpunished the gods leave and the country falls into disorder. Chapter 12 tells how Śākyamuni, as the wheel-turning king Good Gathering, heard the Golden Light from the bhikṣu Jewel Profound, now the buddha Akṣobhya. Chapter 13 praises the merit of going to hear the sūtra and lists the many gods, spirits, dragons, and asuras who guard its hearers. In Chapter 14 Faith-Mark and his two sons receive predictions of buddhahood, and so do ten thousand sons of the gods who have only come to listen; the spirit of the bodhi tree asks why. The Buddha's answer begins in Chapter 15, the story of Jalavāhana, a physician's son who learns the seasonal medicine of the four elements from his father and heals a whole country during an epidemic. The story continues in the fourth fascicle.

佛告道場菩提樹神：「善女天！諦聽，諦聽！善持憶念，我當為汝演說往昔誓願因緣。過去無量不可思議阿僧祇劫，爾時有佛出現於世，名曰寶勝如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊。善女天！爾時是佛般涅槃後，正法滅已，於像法中，有王名曰天自在光王，修行正法如法治世，人民和順孝養父母。是王國中有一長者名曰持水，善知醫方救諸病苦，方便巧知四大增損。善女天！爾時持水長者家中，後生一子名曰流水，體貌殊勝端正第一，形色微妙威德具足，受性聰敏善解諸論，種種技藝書疏算計無不通達。是時國內天降疫病，有無量百千諸眾生等，皆無免者，為諸苦惱之所逼切。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha told the spirit of the bodhi tree at the site of awakening: "Good goddess! Listen closely, listen closely! Hold it well in recollection: I will expound for you the causes and conditions of vows of the past. Immeasurable, inconceivable asaṃkhyeya eons ago, there was then a buddha who appeared in the world, named Jewel Victory Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One. Good goddess! At that time, after this buddha attained parinirvāṇa and the true Dharma had disappeared, in the semblance Dharma there was a king named King Heavenly Sovereign Light, who cultivated the true Dharma and governed the world according to the Dharma; the people were harmonious and obedient and cared for their fathers and mothers with filial piety. In this king's country there was an elder named Water-Holder, who knew medicine well and rescued [people] from the sufferings of illness, and by skillful means knew well the increase and decrease of the four great elements. Good goddess! At that time in the household of the elder Water-Holder there was later born a son named Jalavāhana, outstanding in body and appearance, foremost in dignity, subtle and wondrous in form and color, complete in majestic virtue; by nature intelligent and quick, he understood the treatises well, and there was none of the various arts and skills, writing, letters, and calculation, that he did not master. At this time in the country the heavens sent down an epidemic, and immeasurable hundreds of thousands of beings, none escaping it, were pressed by sufferings and distress.

Chinese Source

「善女天！爾時流水長者子，見是無量百千眾生受諸苦惱故，為是眾生生大悲心，作是思惟：『如是無量百千眾生受諸苦惱。我父長者，雖善醫方能救諸苦，方便巧知四太增損，年已衰邁老耄枯悴，皮緩面皺羸瘦顫掉，行來往反要因几杖，困頓疲乏不能至彼城邑聚落；而是無量百千眾生，復遇重病無能救者，我今當至大醫父所諮問治病醫方祕法，諮稟知已，當至城邑聚落村舍治諸眾生種種重病，悉令得脫無量諸苦。』

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good goddess! At that time Jalavāhana, the elder's son, seeing these immeasurable hundreds of thousands of beings undergoing sufferings and distress, gave rise to a mind of great compassion for these beings and thought thus: 'Such immeasurable hundreds of thousands of beings are undergoing sufferings and distress. My father the elder, although he knows medicine well and can rescue them from sufferings, and by skillful means knows well the increase and decrease of the four great elements, is already aged and decrepit, withered and worn, his skin slack and his face wrinkled, thin and trembling; going and coming he must rely on a stool and staff; exhausted and weary, he cannot reach those cities, towns, and settlements. Yet these immeasurable hundreds of thousands of beings have met with grave illness and there is no one who can rescue them. I should now go to my father, the great physician, and consult him about the medical methods and secret arts for curing illness; having consulted him and learned them, I will go to the cities, towns, settlements, villages, and dwellings and cure the various grave illnesses of beings, so that all are freed from immeasurable sufferings.'

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Jalavāhana Heals the Country

Chinese Source

「善女天！爾時流水長者子，問其父醫四大增損，因是得了一切醫方。時長者子知醫方已，遍至國內城邑聚落，在在處處隨有眾生病苦者所，軟言慰喻作如是言：『我是醫師，我是醫師，善知方藥，今當為汝療治救濟悉令除愈。』

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good goddess! At that time Jalavāhana, the elder's son, asked his father the physician about the increase and decrease of the four great elements, and through this came to understand all medical methods. When the elder's son knew the medical methods, he went everywhere throughout the country, to cities, towns, and settlements, in every place, to wherever there were beings suffering from illness, and with gentle words comforted them, speaking thus: 'I am a physician, I am a physician; I know prescriptions and medicines well. I will now treat and rescue you and cure you all.'

Chinese Source

「善女天！爾時眾生聞長者子軟言慰喻許為治病，心生歡喜踊躍無量。時有百千無量眾生，遇極重病，直聞是言，心歡喜故，種種所患即得除差，平復如本氣力充實。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good goddess! At that time the beings, hearing the elder's son comfort them with gentle words and promise to treat their illnesses, gave rise to joy, leaping with boundless joy. At that time there were hundreds of thousands of immeasurable beings who had met with extremely grave illnesses; merely by hearing these words, because their minds rejoiced, the various ailments they suffered were at once cured, and they recovered as before, their energy and strength full.

Chinese Source

「善女天！復有無量百千眾生，病苦深重難除差者，即共來至長者子所，時長者子，即以妙藥授之令服，服已除差亦得平復。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good goddess! There were further immeasurable hundreds of thousands of beings whose sufferings of illness were deep and grave and hard to cure; they came together to the elder's son, and the elder's son at once gave them wondrous medicines to take; having taken them, they were cured and also recovered.

Chinese Source

「善女天！是長者子，於其國內治諸眾生所有病苦悉得除差。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good goddess! This elder's son cured all the sufferings of illness of the beings in his country, and all were healed."

Reading Notes for Fascicle 3

Translator's notes

Chapter names. The provisional names in NOTES_FOR_TRANSLATORS were kept, except 正論品 "The Right Treatise" (正 as in "right mindfulness," "govern rightly" in Parts 1–2; "correct" is reserved for 正因 the correct cause) and 善集品 "Good Gathering" (善集 is the king's name; 集 is "gather" elsewhere in this sūtra, e.g. "gather an immeasurable heap of blessings").

Saṃjñeya's name (unit 4). The chapter explains the name through knowing and right understanding (正解正觀); Sanskrit saṃjñeya is usually connected with saṃjñā, "perception, understanding." The explanation is the sūtra's own.

Buddha name in unit 7. Here the buddha of Śrī's past is 寶華功德海琉璃金山光照如來 (光照, not 照明 as in Part 2, unit 52); rendered "Light-Illumination" to keep the difference.

Power-Honored Mark and Faith-Mark (unit 9). The prince has the same name, 信相, as the bodhisattva Faith-Mark of Chapters 2–4 and 14; the sūtra does not identify them. Yijing's version (T0665, 王法正論品) calls the king 力尊幢 and the prince 妙幢, the same name as its bodhisattva.

Hard lines in Chapter 11. 半名人王，亦名執樂 (unit 16) is rendered literally ("Half [of him] is called king of humans; he is also called holder of happiness"); 羅剎魁膾 (unit 17) "a rākṣasa, a chief executioner" — the king as the one who punishes. 三異 (unit 26) "three anomalies," explained by the following lines. Alternatively, 半名人王 may mean "he is half [human], called king of humans."

Jewel Profound 寶冥 (Chapter 12). Yijing's version has 寶積 "Jewel Heap" (Ratnoccaya) and the city 妙音聲 for 水音尊 "Water-Sound Honored." 冥 is kept as "Profound." The ending (unit 63) identifies the bhikṣu with Akṣobhya and King Good Gathering with Śākyamuni (釋迦文).

Names in Chapter 13 (units 80–97). These long lists of spirits are transliterations. Well-known forms are given in Sanskrit (Pūrṇabhadra, Kumbhīra, Pāñcika, Citrasena, Mucilinda, Elāpatra, Vemacitra, Kharaskandha, Aṅgulimāla, Hārītī). Others are tentative reconstructions (Āṭavaka, Piṅgala, Nararāja, Jinarṣabha, Maṇikaṇṭha, Nikaṇṭha, Mahāghaṭa, Pañcāla, Dharmapāla, Makarapāla, Sūryamitra, Ratnakeśa, Mahāpana, Kuṇḍala, Kāmaśreṣṭha, Śaramitra, Haimavata, Sātāgiri, Agrapāla, Caṇḍā, Caṇḍālī, Kulā, Kuladantī). Those with no plausible reconstruction are in pinyin (Cheboluopo, Ponali, Duoxiboxi, Zhiluomojia, Shanmoli, Boheli, Qiantuo). 𫁸脂 is given as "Zhezhi," reading the rare character as a form of 遮; uncertain. 禪那英鬼 "the heroic ghost Channa": source of the name not located.

違馱 (unit 87). "The god Weituo," as in Zhili's rite (韋馱), though the sūtra writes 違馱. 自在天 Īśvara and 摩醯首羅 Maheśvara are kept apart.

Line breaks in the spirit lists. The four-character rhythm cuts some names (旃陀旃陀、利大鬼神、女等鳩羅). Read as Caṇḍā, Caṇḍālī, "the great ghost-spirits," "the women Kulā and the rest"; other readings are possible.

Predictions (Chapter 14). "Prediction," "true Dharma and semblance Dharma," and the ten epithets follow the site's Lotus Sūtra. 忉利 is "Trāyastriṃśa [Heaven]," kept distinct from 三十三天 "the Thirty-three Heavens."

The bodhi-tree spirit (units 113–116). 道場菩提樹神 "the spirit of the bodhi tree at the site of awakening," named 等增益 "Equal Increase"; addressed as 善女天 "Good goddess!" as with Dṛḍhā. Zhili's rite has "the spirit of the bodhi tree."

Jalavāhana's family (unit 116). 天自在光王 "King Heavenly Sovereign Light"; the father 持水 "Water-Holder." The Sanskrit text gives the father as Jaṭiṃdhara, which does not match the Chinese meaning, so the Chinese meaning is translated.

Medicine verses (units 119–126). 四大 the four great elements; 風／熱／肺病／等分 wind / heat / lung illness / the balanced [illness] (等分, "equal portions," the disorder of all humors together). 胏 in unit 119 is read as 肺 "lung." 水過 "water excess." 六大 "the six great elements" and 六時 "six seasons" are literal. 訶梨勒 harītakī (myrobalan). 四太 in unit 117 is a copying error for 四大.
