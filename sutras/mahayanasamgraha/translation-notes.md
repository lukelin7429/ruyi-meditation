# Mahāyānasaṃgraha Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* (攝大乘論, Taishō T31, no. 1594), composed by Asaṅga and translated into Chinese by Xuánzàng. They exist so that the vocabulary of this treatise stays consistent with the site's larger Yogācāra corpus, especially the *Cheng Weishi Lun* and the *Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra*.

## Source and independence from prior translations

- Root text: Taishō T1594, 攝大乘論本, Asaṅga, translated by Xuánzàng.
- Primary commentary: Taishō T1597, 攝大乘論釋, Vasubandhu (Asaṅga's own younger brother), also translated by Xuánzàng — chosen because root text and commentary share one translator and one vocabulary.
- Second-voice commentary: Taishō T1598, 攝大乘論釋, Asvabhāva (無性), also translated by Xuánzàng — brought in on a passage-by-passage basis where his reading adds something the primary commentary does not, in the manner already used elsewhere on this site for a second commentator (e.g. Shandao/Huiyuan on the Contemplation Sūtra).
- This page translates directly from Xuánzàng's Chinese. Existing English translations and scholarly studies may be consulted privately to check difficult passages, but the English wording, sentence structure, and section framing on this site must be Ru-Yi's own.
- The title is given as *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*, "Summary of the Great Vehicle."

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 攝大乘論 | *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* | Use the full Sanskrit title on first appearance per page; "the treatise" thereafter. |
| 薄伽梵 | Bhagavat | Left transliterated, matching the site's Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra convention, where the Chinese keeps the Indic honorific rather than simply saying 佛. |
| 阿毘達磨大乘經 | the *Abhidharma-mahāyāna-sūtra* | The (largely lost) scripture Part 1 quotes as its own source; italicized as a proper title. |
| 殊勝殊勝語 | an excellent point, excellently taught | The doubled 殊勝 in each of the ten items of Part 1: the topic itself excels, and so the teaching about it excels. Render each item as "the excellent point of X, excellently taught," not simply "the excellent teaching on X" — the doubling is deliberate (see Part 1, note after Vasubandhu). |
| 所知依 | the basis of the knowable | Identified in Part 1 as the ālayavijñāna; title of Part 2. |
| 所知相 | the character of the knowable | Identified in Part 1 as the three natures; title of Part 3. |
| 入所知相 | entering the character of the knowable | Identified in Part 1 as the nature of Consciousness-Only; title of Part 4. |
| 彼入因果 | the cause and effect of that entry | The six perfections before and after realization; title of Part 5. |
| 彼修差別 (also 彼因果修差別) | the distinctions in cultivating that (cause and effect) | The ten grounds; title of Part 6. |
| 增上戒(學) | the higher training in morality | Title of Part 7. |
| 增上心(學) | the higher training in concentration | Title of Part 8. |
| 增上慧(學) | the higher training in wisdom | Title of Part 9. |
| 彼果斷 | the fruit of relinquishment | Non-abiding nirvāṇa; title of Part 10. |
| 彼果智 | the fruit of wisdom | The three bodies of a buddha; title of Part 11. |
| 阿賴耶識 | the ālayavijñāna | Matches Cheng Weishi Lun and Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra usage. Give the Sanskrit on first use per part, "storehouse consciousness" as an occasional gloss. |
| 三種自性 / 三自性 | the three natures | 依他起自性 the dependent nature, 遍計所執自性 the discriminated, attached-to nature, 圓成實自性 the perfectly accomplished nature — kept identical to the Cheng Weishi Lun glossary. |
| 唯識性 | the nature of Consciousness-Only | vijñaptimātratā-dharmatā; matches Cheng Weishi Lun's "the nature of Consciousness-Only." |
| 六波羅蜜多 | the six perfections | Generosity, morality, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, wisdom (施, 戒, 忍, 精進, 靜慮, 般若). |
| 十地 | the ten (bodhisattva) grounds | Matches Cheng Weishi Lun usage. |
| 菩薩律儀 | the bodhisattva's moral discipline | The substance of the higher training in morality (Part 7). |
| 首楞伽摩三摩地 | the Heroic Stride absorption | Śūraṅgama-samādhi — an absorption, distinct from the *Śūraṅgama Sūtra* already on this site; disambiguate on first use. |
| 虛空藏三摩地 | the Space-Treasury absorption | Named for Ākāśagarbha Bodhisattva. |
| 無分別智 | non-discriminating wisdom | nirvikalpajñāna; the substance of the higher training in wisdom (Part 9). |
| 無住涅槃 | non-abiding nirvāṇa | apratiṣṭhita-nirvāṇa; the fruit of relinquishment (Part 10) — neither abiding in saṃsāra nor in the cessation of the two vehicles. |
| 二障 (煩惱障 / 所知障) | the two obstructions (the afflictive obstruction / the cognitive obstruction) | Matches Cheng Weishi Lun glossary exactly. |
| 三種佛身 / 三身 | the three bodies | 自性身 the intrinsic-nature body, 受用身 the enjoyment body, 變化身 the transformation body — kept identical to the Cheng Weishi Lun glossary. |
| 一切智智 | the wisdom that knows all things in every mode | sarvākārajñatā; the omniscient wisdom of a buddha, distinct from 一切智 (the more limited "knowledge of all [dharmas]" attributed to the two vehicles in some contexts). |
| 三無數大劫 | three incalculable great eons | The traditional length of the bodhisattva path from the first arousing of bodhicitta to buddhahood, spanning the ten grounds. |
| 阿陀那識 | the ādāna-consciousness | Another scriptural name for the ālayavijñāna, from the *Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra* quotation in Part 2 — "the appropriator," so named for its role in appropriating and sustaining the physical faculties. |
| 染污意 | the defiled manas | The self-grasping mental faculty proven to exist in Part 2, section 4 — constantly associated with the view of self, self-conceit, self-love, and ignorance. Not yet numbered as a "seventh consciousness" in this treatise (that systematization comes later in the tradition, e.g. Cheng Weishi Lun), but functionally identical. |
| 薰習 / 熏習 | habituation | vāsanā; the process by which an experience leaves behind, in the ālayavijñāna, not a stored copy of itself but a functional capacity (a seed) to reproduce something like it later. |
| 種子 | seed(s) | bīja; matches Cheng Weishi Lun usage. Part 2 gives the classical six characteristics of seeds (momentary cessation, co-existence with their result, unbroken continuation, fixed to their own kind, dependent on conditions, drawing only their own result). |
| 異熟識 / 異熟果識 | the ripening consciousness | vipāka; the ālayavijñāna named in its role as what ripens karmic seeds into their result — matches Cheng Weishi Lun's distinction between 阿賴耶識 and 異熟(識) as the same consciousness under different functional names. |
| 聞熏習 | hearing-habituation | The seed, distinct from the ālayavijñāna's own defiled continuity, planted by hearing and correctly reflecting on the Buddha's teaching — identified in Part 2 as "the seed of the dharma-body." Central to how the treatise explains liberation as possible at all. |
| 轉依 | the transformation of the basis | āśraya-parāvṛtti; the gradual process, introduced in Part 2, by which pure seeds increase and defiled seeds correspondingly diminish within the same continuum, until defilement is permanently exhausted. Not a sudden swap of one consciousness for another. |
| 法身 / 解脫身 | the dharma-body / the body of liberation | What a bodhisattva's practice of hearing-habituation is said to belong to (the dharma-body, en route to full buddhahood) versus what a śrāvaka's or solitary buddha's belongs to (the body of liberation alone). Distinguish from the "three bodies" (三種佛身) of Part 11, where 自性身 plays a related but not identical role. |
| 有覆無記 / 無覆無記 | obscured-but-neutral / unobscured-and-neutral | Two of the four moral qualities (wholesome, unwholesome, obscured-neutral, unobscured-neutral) used throughout Part 2 — the defiled manas is obscured-but-neutral; the ripening consciousness itself is always unobscured-and-neutral. |
| 依他起(自性/相) | the dependent nature | paratantra; kept identical to the Cheng Weishi Lun glossary. Part 3 gives its full definition: the ten kinds of consciousness having the ālayavijñāna as seed. |
| 遍計所執(自性/相) | the discriminated, attached-to nature | parikalpita; kept identical to the Cheng Weishi Lun glossary — "the appearance of what seems to be an object within what is, in truth, nothing but consciousness." |
| 圓成實(自性/相) | the perfectly accomplished nature | pariniṣpanna; kept identical to the Cheng Weishi Lun glossary — the dependent nature's permanent freedom from the discriminated, attached-to appearance. |
| 能遍計 / 所遍計 | the universally-imagining subject / the universally-imagined object | Part 3's own vocabulary for how the discriminated nature arises: the mental consciousness (the imagining subject) projects a fixed object onto the dependent nature (the imagined object). |
| 十地經 | the *Daśabhūmika Sūtra* | Cited in Part 3 for "these three realms are nothing but mind" — one of the two scriptural proofs of Consciousness-Only, alongside the *Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra*. |
| 梵問經 | the *Brahma-Questions Sūtra* | Cited in Part 3 for "the Tathāgata neither attains birth-and-death nor attains nirvāṇa," resolved via the three natures. |
| 四意趣 | the four intentions | Equality, a different time, a different meaning, and accommodating an individual's disposition — four reasons a Buddha saying may need reading with a hidden intent rather than at face value. |
| 四祕密 | the four hidden meanings | Leading one to enter, characteristics, counteraction, and transformation — distinguished from the four intentions by the specific interpretive key each requires. |
| 意言 | mental discourse | The conceptual, verbal-feeling activity of thinking through the teaching before it deepens into direct, non-conceptual realization — central to Part 4's account of how a practitioner actually enters Consciousness-Only. |
| 四尋思 / 四如實遍智 | the four investigations / the four kinds of thorough knowledge of things as they really are | Part 4's two-stage technique: investigating whether name, meaning, own-nature, and differentiating quality are real, then thoroughly knowing that none of them are. |
| 勝解行地 | the stage of resolute conduct | The long preparatory stage before the path of seeing, where entry into Consciousness-Only happens through conviction rather than direct sight. |
| 見道 / 修道 / 究竟道 | the path of seeing / the path of cultivation / the ultimate path | Matches Cheng Weishi Lun's five-stage path (accumulation, preparatory effort, seeing, cultivation, ultimate). |
| 順決擇分 (煖 / 頂 / 忍 / 世第一法) | the aids to penetration (heat / summit / acceptance / the highest worldly dharma) | The four-stage threshold immediately before the path of seeing; matches Cheng Weishi Lun's terminology for the same four stages. |
| 極喜地 | the Ground of Utmost Joy | Pramuditā, the first of the ten bodhisattva grounds, entered at the path of seeing. |
| 轉依 | see Part 2 entry | Cross-reference: the transformation of the basis, introduced in Part 2, is the goal of the path of cultivation described in Part 4. |
| 六波羅蜜多 | the six perfections | see Part 1 entry. Part 5 gives the full treatment: generosity 施, moral discipline 戒, patience 忍, diligence 精進, meditative concentration 靜慮, wisdom 慧 — each with three grades, matching the Cheng Weishi Lun glossary's threefold breakdown of moral discipline in particular. |
| 三品 (施/戒/忍/精進/靜慮/慧) | three grades | Each perfection's own threefold distinction, given in full in Part 5, section 7 — e.g. discipline's restraint / gathering wholesome dharmas / benefiting beings, matching Cheng Weishi Lun's threefold precepts scheme exactly. |
| 增上意樂 | superior resolve | The pure resolve that is both what makes entry into Consciousness-Only possible and what the six perfections become, as fruit, once entry has occurred (Part 5). Not to be confused with 增上戒/心/慧學, the "higher trainings" of Parts 7–9, which use 增上 in a related but distinct sense. |
| 十地 | the ten grounds | see Part 1 entry. Part 6 gives the full names: 極喜地 the Ground of Utmost Joy, 離垢地 Free of Defilement, 發光地 Emitted Light, 焰慧地 Blazing Wisdom, 極難勝地 Difficult to Conquer, 現前地 Direct Presence, 遠行地 Far-Reaching, 不動地 Immovable, 善慧地 Excellent Wisdom, 法雲地 the Dharma-Cloud. |
| 方便善巧 / 願 / 力 / 智 波羅蜜多 | skillful means / vows / power / wisdom (the four additional perfections) | Matched to grounds 7–10 in Part 6; all four are subsumed within the wisdom-perfection's subsequently-attained wisdom, not separate from the original six. |
| 三無數大劫 | see Part 1 entry | Part 6 maps the three eons onto five successive stages of one practitioner (資糧行/勝解行, 清淨增上意樂行, 有相行, 無相行, 無功用行), not five different people. |
| 律儀戒 / 攝善法戒 / 饒益有情戒 | the discipline of restraint / of gathering wholesome dharmas / of benefiting sentient beings | Matches Cheng Weishi Lun glossary exactly. Part 7 gives the fullest treatment: restraint as the basis the other two build on. |
| 性罪 / 遮罪 | inherently wrong action / merely prohibited action | Part 7's distinction for what a bodhisattva shares with a śrāvaka's code (性罪, wrong regardless of any rule) versus what may diverge (遮罪, wrong only because a specific rule forbids it). |
| 十種作業 | the ten courses of unwholesome action | The classical list (killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, and so on) — cited in Part 7 as the extreme case a bodhisattva's "excellence of profundity" may exceptionally, compassionately override; reread riddle-style in Part 8, section 4. |
| 十難行 | the ten difficult practices | Part 8's list, culminating in "neither leaving nor being stained" (不離不染難行) — remaining fully engaged in saṃsāra without withdrawing into private nirvāṇa or being tainted by worldly contact. |
| 甚深祕密言詞 | the Buddha's profound, hidden words | Part 8's riddle-style sayings (e.g. "true giving is giving nothing") where a surface reading and a hidden, technical reading both apply — resolved by reading at two levels at once, not by picking one over the other. |
| 甚深佛法 | the profound buddha-dharmas | Part 8's eight paired sayings (permanent yet arising, having craving yet a buddha-dharma) — each resolved by identifying which of the three bodies of a buddha (Part 11) the statement actually describes. |
| 無分別智 | non-discriminating wisdom | see Part 1 entry. Part 9 gives the exhaustive treatment: self-nature free of five characteristics, support, cause, object, aspect, sustaining function, companion-path, ripening-fruit, outflow-fruit, emergence, ultimate point, and similes. |
| 加行無分別智 / 根本無分別智 / 後得無分別智 | preparatory / fundamental / subsequently-attained non-discriminating wisdom | The three-stage structure first introduced via Part 4's illusionist simile, given its fullest treatment in Part 9 — effortful approach, direct wordless realization, and skillful post-realization engagement. |
| 般若波羅蜜多 | the perfection of wisdom | Identified in Part 9 as without difference from non-discriminating wisdom itself, "abiding without being fixed to any particular ground" — free of five specific traps a less complete practice falls into. |
| 轉依 | the transformation of the basis | see Part 2 entry. Part 10 gives the full six-fold treatment (損力益能轉 through 廣大轉), and identifies it with non-abiding nirvāṇa: relinquishing saṃsāra's defiled portion of the dependent nature without relinquishing saṃsāra itself. |
| 下劣轉 / 廣大轉 | the lesser transformation / the vast transformation | Part 10's fifth and sixth kinds of transformation of the basis — a śrāvaka's turning wholly away from saṃsāra versus a bodhisattva's remaining within it, seeing it as already quiescent. |
| 自性身 / 受用身 / 變化身 | the intrinsic-nature body / the enjoyment body / the transformation body | see Part 1 entry. Part 11 gives the exhaustive treatment — the three bodies' characteristics, realization, mastery, basis, sustaining dharmas, and why neither the enjoyment nor transformation body is simply identical to the intrinsic-nature body (six and eight reasons respectively). |
| 金剛喻定 | the vajra-like concentration | vajropamasamādhi; the final moment of practice, in Part 11, that shatters the last, most subtle obstruction and brings about the transformation of the basis into the dharma-body. |
| 十自在 | the ten masteries | Part 11's fivefold-perfection-to-mastery mapping: lifespan/mind/necessities (generosity), karmic action/rebirth (discipline), resolve (patience), vows (diligence), supernormal power (concentration), wisdom/Dharma (wisdom). |
| 十八不共佛法 | the eighteen unique buddha-dharmas | Named among the qualities accompanying the dharma-body in Part 11, alongside the ten powers, four fearlessnesses, and the rest of the standard Mahāyāna catalogue of a buddha's qualities. |
| 十二種甚深 | the twelve kinds of profundity | Part 11's twelve verses on paradoxical-sounding truths about a buddha (non-arising as arising, retained-but-not-destroyed affliction, and so on), each resolved by precision about which level or aspect is meant. |
| 七種念 | the seven kinds of mindfulness (of the buddha's dharma-body) | Part 11's structured recollection practice: mastery (with its own limit), permanence, faultlessness, effortlessness, great wealth, freedom from defilement, and the accomplishing of great deeds. |
| 五業 | the five activities | Part 11's five constant buddha-activities: rescuing from calamity, from unfortunate rebirth, from wrong means, from self-view, and from a lesser vehicle. |
| 十八圓滿 | the eighteen perfections (of a pure buddha-land) | Part 11's analysis of the Bodhisattva Piṭaka's opening description of a buddha-land — color, shape, extent, location, cause, fruit, lord, attendants, retinue, sustenance, activity, benefit, fearlessness, dwelling-place, path, vehicle, gateway, support. |
