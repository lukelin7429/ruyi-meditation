Asaṅga · Xuánzàng · 攝大乘論

The Treatise That Gathers the Whole Great Vehicle.

The Mahāyānasaṃgraha — "Summary of the Great Vehicle" — is Asaṅga's own synthesis of Yogācāra philosophy into a single treatise: the storehouse consciousness that carries every seed of experience, the three natures that explain how a constructed world is mistaken for a real one, the path of the six perfections through the ten bodhisattva grounds, and the three bodies of a buddha at the path's end. This page is the entrance for reading it part by part, following the treatise's own eleven-part outline.

How to read this collection

This treatise is dense and systematic — Asaṅga wrote it to gather the entire Great Vehicle into eleven parts, each building on the one before. We keep the treatise's own structure rather than smoothing it away, and add reading notes wherever a modern English reader would otherwise lose the thread.

Our primary commentary guide is Vasubandhu, Asaṅga's own younger brother, whose commentary shares Xuánzàng as translator with the root text, so terminology stays aligned. Where Asvabhāva's independent commentary on the same root text adds something worth hearing, we bring it in as a second voice.

What each part page includes

Read by Part

Asaṅga's own eleven-part outline, named in Part 1 itself. Use this as the table of contents for the treatise, from the proof that the Great Vehicle is Buddha-word through to the three bodies of buddhahood.

Parts 1–3

The Ten Points, the Storehouse Consciousness, and the Three Natures

Asaṅga's proof that the Great Vehicle is Buddha-word, the full account of the ālayavijñāna as the basis of everything knowable, and the three natures — dependent, discriminated, and perfectly accomplished — that are its character.

Parts 4–9

Entering the Path, Cultivating the Grounds, the Three Trainings

Realizing the nature of Consciousness-Only, the six perfections as the entry's cause and effect, the ten bodhisattva grounds, and the higher trainings in morality, concentration, and non-discriminating wisdom.

Translation Notes

Recurring terms, kept consistent from Part 1 through Part 11, and matched to the site's other Yogācāra pages where the same term appears. See also this site's note on translation method.

所知依 — the basis of the knowableWhat everything knowable depends on and arises from — identified in Part 1 as the ālayavijñāna.
阿賴耶識 — ālayavijñānaThe storehouse consciousness, which holds the seeds of every past action and experience.
三自性 — the three naturesThe dependent, discriminated-and-attached-to, and perfectly accomplished natures — the same terms used across the site's Cheng Weishi Lun pages.
唯識性 — the nature of Consciousness-OnlyVijñaptimātratā: what is realized when the three natures are directly understood.
十地 — the ten groundsThe ten bodhisattva stages, each pairing one of the six perfections with a ground to be traversed.
三種佛身 — the three bodiesThe intrinsic-nature body, the enjoyment body, and the transformation body — the treatise's account of buddhahood.
