The Fruit of Wisdom: The Three Bodies of a Buddha

The treatise's closing part, and its longest sustained meditation on a single subject: the three bodies of a buddha — the intrinsic-nature body, the enjoyment body, and the transformation body — examined through ten topics named in Asaṅga's own summary verse. This part carries the dharma-body's characteristics, how it is realized, its masteries, its qualities in a long sequence of verses of homage, twelve kinds of profundity, seven ways to hold it in mindfulness, the pure buddha-land it manifests, its five constant activities, and a series of hard questions — one buddha or many, permanent or impermanent, does a buddha enter nirvāṇa — before the treatise closes with Asaṅga's own colophon.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 11 of 11 complete Source: Taishō T31, no. 1594, 彼果智分第十一 Commentary guide: Vasubandhu's 攝大乘論釋 (T1597)

Translation Method

As throughout this project, the Chinese source is Xuánzàng's translation of Asaṅga's root text (T1594), read with Vasubandhu's commentary (T1597), also translated by Xuánzàng. "The three bodies" — the intrinsic-nature body, the enjoyment body, and the transformation body — are kept identical to the site's Cheng Weishi Lun glossary. Given the length of this closing part, several long verse sequences are presented with a single synthesizing reading note rather than a note for every individual verse. See also this site's note on translation method.

1. The Three Bodies Introduced; Ten Topics for This Part

Chinese Source

如是已說彼果斷殊勝，彼果智殊勝云何可見？謂由三種佛身，應知彼果智殊勝：一、由自性身，二、由受用身，三、由變化身。此中自性身者，謂諸如來法身，一切法自在轉所依止故。受用身者，謂依法身，種種諸佛眾會所顯清淨佛土，大乘法樂為所受故。變化身者，亦依法身，從覩史多天宮現沒、受生、受欲、踰城出家、往外道所修諸苦行、證大菩提、轉大法輪、入大涅槃故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having thus spoken of the excellence of the fruit of relinquishment, how may the excellence of the fruit of wisdom be seen? It should be understood through three bodies of a buddha: first, the intrinsic-nature body; second, the enjoyment body; third, the transformation body. Here, the intrinsic-nature body is the dharma-body of the tathāgatas, the very basis on which mastery over all dharmas depends. The enjoyment body, resting on the dharma-body, is what is manifested amid the various assemblies gathered around a buddha, within a pure buddha-land, where the joy of the Great Vehicle's Dharma is enjoyed. The transformation body, likewise resting on the dharma-body, is what appears descending from the Tuṣita heaven, taking birth, enjoying sensual pleasures, leaving the city to renounce the world, going to the non-Buddhist teachers to practice austerities, realizing great awakening, turning the great wheel of the Dharma, and entering great nirvāṇa.

此中說一嗢拕南頌：

「相、證得、自在、　依止、及攝持、

差別、德、甚深、　念、業：明諸佛。」

Here there is one summary verse:

"Characteristic, realization, mastery, basis, and what sustains it;

distinction, virtue, profundity, mindfulness, and activity: these illuminate the buddhas."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

The three bodies name three genuinely different things, not three degrees of the same thing: the intrinsic-nature body is suchness itself, realized without remainder — not an object among other objects, but the very basis on which everything else depends. The enjoyment body is what appears within pure buddha-lands specifically for the benefit of bodhisattvas already on the grounds, who directly enjoy the Great Vehicle's own teaching in a form ordinary beings cannot perceive. And the transformation body is the historically visible pattern — descent, birth, renunciation, austerity, awakening, teaching, death — repeated for the sake of beings who need a teacher who looks and lives as they do. Asaṅga's own summary verse then functions as this part's table of contents: ten topics, worked through in the order named, that structure everything that follows.

2. Five Characteristics of the Dharma-Body: Transformed Basis, and Made of Wholesome Qualities

Chinese Source

諸佛法身以何為相？應知法身略有五相：一、轉依為相，謂轉滅一切障雜染分依他起性故，轉得解脫一切障於法自在轉現前清淨分依他起性故。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the characteristic of the dharma-body of the buddhas? It should be understood that the dharma-body has, briefly, five characteristics. First, the transformation of the basis as its characteristic: through eliminating the defiled portion of the dependent nature, together with every obstruction, one attains liberation from every obstruction and mastery over all dharmas, causing the purified portion of the dependent nature to become directly present.

二、白法所成為相，謂六波羅蜜多圓滿得十自在故。此中壽自在，心自在，眾具自在，由施波羅蜜多圓滿故。業自在，生自在，由戒波羅蜜多圓滿故。勝解自在，由忍波羅蜜多圓滿故。願自在，由精進波羅蜜多圓滿故。神力自在五通所攝，由靜慮波羅蜜多圓滿故。智自在，法自在，由般若波羅蜜多圓滿故。

Second, being made of wholesome dharmas as its characteristic: through the full completion of the six perfections, one attains ten kinds of mastery. Here, mastery over one's own lifespan, over one's own mind, and over the necessities of life come from the full completion of the perfection of generosity. Mastery over karmic action and over rebirth come from the full completion of the perfection of moral discipline. Mastery over resolve comes from the full completion of the perfection of patience. Mastery over vows comes from the full completion of the perfection of diligence. Mastery over supernormal power, comprising the five kinds of higher knowledge, comes from the full completion of the perfection of meditative concentration. Mastery over wisdom and over the Dharma come from the full completion of the perfection of wisdom.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

The first characteristic simply restates, at the level of the dharma-body itself, the transformation of the basis explained in Part 10: the dharma-body just is the dependent nature with its defiled portion eliminated and its purified portion made fully present, nothing further added. The ten masteries under the second characteristic map each perfection onto specific fruits: complete generosity yields mastery over how long one lives, an untroubled mind regardless of circumstance, and effortless access to whatever is needed; complete discipline yields mastery over one's own karmic action and over where and how one is reborn; complete patience yields the power to make reality conform to resolve — Vasubandhu gives the example of transforming earth into gold purely through the strength of what was endured; complete diligence yields the power to bring every vow to fulfillment; complete meditative concentration yields the five supernormal powers; and complete wisdom yields full mastery over both dharmas' own nature and the ability to teach them skillfully. Every quality traditionally associated with an awakened being, in other words, is shown here to be the direct, traceable fruit of one specific perfection carried to its completion.

3. Three More Characteristics: Non-Dual, Permanent, Inconceivable

Chinese Source

三、無二為相，謂有無無二為相，由一切法無所有故，空所顯相是實有故。有為無為無二為相，由業煩惱非所為故，自在示現有為相故。異性一性無二為相，由一切佛所依無差別故，無量相續現等覺故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, being non-dual as its characteristic: non-dual as neither existence nor nonexistence, since all dharmas [as discriminated and attached to] have no real existence, while the characteristic disclosed by emptiness is genuinely real. Non-dual as neither conditioned nor unconditioned, since it is not produced by karma or affliction, and yet, with complete mastery, manifests conditioned characteristics. Non-dual as neither different nor identical, since the basis on which all buddhas depend is without differentiation, while, at the same time, countless individual continua manifest full awakening.

此中有二頌：

「我執不有故，　於中無別依；

隨前能證別，　故施設有異。

種姓異、非虛、　圓滿、無初故，

無垢依無別，　故非一、非多。」

Here there are two verses:

"Because self-grasping does not exist, there is no separate basis within it;

yet, according to each one's own prior realization, distinctions are provisionally established.

Because spiritual dispositions differ, because effort would not be in vain, because fulfillment [must be complete], and because there is no first beginning,

the stainless basis, being without distinction, is therefore neither one nor many."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Each of the three "non-dualities" resolves an apparent contradiction the same way the three natures resolved them in Part 3. The dharma-body is neither simply existent (since the discriminated, attached-to appearance projected onto ordinary experience has no reality) nor simply nonexistent (since what emptiness discloses — the perfectly accomplished nature itself — is genuinely, fully real). It is neither conditioned, in the ordinary sense of being produced by karma and affliction, nor strictly unconditioned in a way that would make it inert, since it still manifests conditioned-seeming activity with complete freedom. And it is neither straightforwardly one nor straightforwardly many: the underlying basis every buddha rests on is entirely without differentiation, and yet countless individual streams of practice each culminate in their own full awakening. The two verses defend this last point specifically against the objection that if the basis is truly undifferentiated, there should logically be only one buddha: because beings have genuinely different spiritual dispositions requiring different specific realizations, because it would be absurd for countless bodhisattvas' efforts to be wasted if only one could actually become a buddha, because a buddha's work of benefiting all beings would remain forever incomplete if there were only one, and because saṃsāra itself has no discoverable first beginning — for all these reasons, "one buddha only" cannot be right, even though the underlying basis they all share is, itself, without any real distinction at all.

4. Permanent, and Inconceivable

Chinese Source

四、常住為相，謂真如清淨相故，本願所引故，所應作事無竟期故。五、不可思議為相，謂真如清淨自內證故，無有世間喻能喻故，非諸尋思所行處故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, being permanent as its characteristic: because the purified aspect of suchness is itself permanent; because it is carried forward by original vows; and because the work it must accomplish has no final endpoint. Fifth, being inconceivable as its characteristic: because it is directly, personally realized within the purified aspect of suchness itself; because no worldly analogy can adequately capture it; and because it lies beyond the reach of ordinary conceptual thought altogether.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Permanence here rests on three independent grounds, any one of which would suffice: suchness itself, once purified, simply does not change; the original vows made long ago to benefit all beings were never empty promises, so their fruit cannot simply cease; and since sentient beings' need for benefit never actually runs out, a buddha's work correspondingly never reaches a final stopping point. "Inconceivable" then closes this section by pointing beyond what any further definition could capture: the dharma-body is known only by direct, personal realization, admits no adequate worldly comparison, and cannot be reached by ordinary discursive reasoning at all — a limit the treatise will return to explicitly under "profundity" later in this part.

5. How the Dharma-Body Is First Realized; Five Kinds of Mastery

Chinese Source

復次，云何如是法身最初證得？謂緣總相大乘法境無分別智及後得智，五相善修，於一切地善集資糧，金剛喻定破滅微細難破障故，此定無間離一切障故得轉依。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, how is this dharma-body first realized? Through non-discriminating wisdom and subsequently-attained wisdom, taking the general character of the Great Vehicle's own teaching as their object, well cultivated through the five modes of practice, well provisioned with resources across every ground — the vajra-like concentration finally shatters even the subtlest, most difficult-to-break obstruction, and, immediately following this concentration, freed of every obstruction without exception, the transformation of the basis is attained.

復次，法身由幾自在而得自在？略由五種：一、由佛土、自身、相好、無邊音聲、無見頂相自在，由轉色蘊依故。二、由無罪無量廣大樂住自在，由轉受蘊依故。三、由辯說一切名身句身文身自在，由轉想蘊依故。四、由現化、變易、引攝大眾、引攝白法自在，由轉行蘊依故。五、由圓鏡、平等、觀察、成所作智自在，由轉識蘊依故。

Furthermore, through how many kinds of mastery does the dharma-body attain mastery? Briefly, through five. First, mastery over one's buddha-land, one's own body, one's excellent marks, boundless voice, and the unseen crown-mark, through transforming the basis of the aggregate of form. Second, mastery over a faultless, immeasurable, vast abiding in joy, through transforming the basis of the aggregate of feeling. Third, mastery over eloquent explanation through every collection of names, phrases, and syllables, through transforming the basis of the aggregate of perception. Fourth, mastery over manifesting transformations, transmuting substances, drawing in great assemblies, and drawing in wholesome dharmas, through transforming the basis of the aggregate of volitional formations. Fifth, mastery through the mirror-like, the equality, the discerning, and the accomplishing wisdoms, through transforming the basis of the aggregate of consciousness.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

The vajra-like concentration (金剛喻定, vajropamasamādhi) names the final, single moment of practice, traditionally likened to a diamond precisely because nothing else can withstand it: it alone is strong enough to shatter the last, most stubborn residue of obstruction that has survived every previous stage of the path, and the transformation of the basis follows immediately, without any further gap. The five masteries that follow then map directly onto the five aggregates (form, feeling, perception, volitional formations, and consciousness) that make up an ordinary being's experience, showing that buddhahood is not escape from those five categories but their complete transformation: what was once a body subject to birth and decay becomes mastery over embodiment itself; what was once ordinary feeling becomes untroubled, boundless joy; what was once perception structured by language becomes perfect, effortless teaching; what was once conditioned mental activity becomes mastery over transformation and the drawing-in of both beings and wholesome qualities; and what was once eight vulnerable, defiled consciousnesses becomes the four wisdoms of buddhahood — a scheme this treatise introduces here for the first time and which becomes standard in later Yogācāra literature, most fully developed in the site's own Cheng Weishi Lun pages.

6. Three Bases the Dharma-Body Depends On

Chinese Source

復次，法身由幾種處應知依止？略由三處：一由種種佛住依止，此中有二頌：

「諸佛證得五性喜，　皆由等證自界故，

離喜都由不證此，　故求喜者應等證。

由能無量及事成，　法味義德俱圓滿，

得喜最勝無過失，　諸佛見常無盡故。」

二由種種受用身依止，但為成熟諸菩薩故。三由種種變化身依止，多為成熟聲聞等故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, through how many bases should the dharma-body's dependence be understood? Briefly, through three. First, through its being the basis for the buddhas' own manifold abidings. Here there are two verses:

"The buddhas attain five kinds of joy from realization, all through equally realizing their own [shared] realm;

being without this joy comes entirely from not realizing this — therefore whoever seeks this joy should equally realize it.

Through boundless capacity, and accomplished activity, the taste of the Dharma, and both meaning and virtue fully complete,

the most excellent, faultless joy is attained — for the buddhas see it as constant, without end."

Second, through its being the basis for the buddhas' manifold enjoyment bodies, solely in order to bring bodhisattvas to maturity. Third, through its being the basis for the buddhas' manifold transformation bodies, largely in order to bring śrāvakas and others to maturity.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu explains that the two verses answer an unspoken objection: if śrāvakas, too, attain liberation, what makes a buddha's own realization actually superior? His answer is five specific kinds of joy unique to full buddhahood — arising from boundless capacity (since countless buddhas share exactly the same realization without limit), from the actual, effective accomplishment of benefiting beings, from the Dharma's own inherent savor, and from the complete fulfillment of both its meaning and its qualities — all of it recognized by the buddhas themselves as continuing endlessly, unlike a śrāvaka's own more limited attainment. The three bases then distinguish the dharma-body's role behind each of the other two bodies: it underlies the buddhas' own direct abiding in realization, it underlies the enjoyment body manifested specifically for bodhisattvas already advanced enough to perceive it, and it underlies the transformation body manifested for those — chiefly śrāvakas and beings not yet ready for the Great Vehicle directly — who need a visible, historical teacher to guide them first.

7. Six Buddha-Dharmas That Sustain the Dharma-Body; Same or Different Across Buddhas

Chinese Source

應知法身由幾佛法之所攝持？略由六種：一、由清淨，謂轉阿賴耶識得法身故。二、由異熟，謂轉色根得異熟智故。三、由安住，謂轉欲行等住得無量智住故。四、由自在，謂轉種種攝受業自在，得一切世界無礙神通智自在故。五、由言說，謂轉一切見聞覺知言說戲論，得令一切有情心喜辯說智自在故。六、由拔濟，謂轉拔濟一切災橫過失，得拔濟一切有情一切災橫過失智故。應知法身由此所說六種佛法之所攝持。

Ru-Yi Translation

By how many buddha-dharmas should the dharma-body's being sustained be understood? Briefly, by six. First, by purity: through transforming the ālayavijñāna, the dharma-body is attained. Second, by ripening: through transforming the physical sense-faculties, the wisdom of ripening is attained. Third, by abiding: through transforming ordinary abiding in sensual and other activities, an abiding in immeasurable wisdom is attained. Fourth, by mastery: through transforming manifold mastery over engaging in worldly action, unobstructed mastery of supernormal wisdom throughout every world is attained. Fifth, by speech: through transforming all discursive speech built from seeing, hearing, sensing, and knowing, mastery of the wisdom of eloquence that delights every sentient being's mind is attained. Sixth, by rescue: through transforming the rescuing of beings from ordinary calamity and fault, the wisdom to rescue every sentient being from every calamity and fault is attained. It should be understood that the dharma-body is sustained by these six buddha-dharmas.

諸佛法身當言有異？當言無異？依止、意樂、業無別故，當言無異。無量依身現等覺故，當言有異。如說佛法身，受用身亦爾，意樂及業無差別故當言無異；不由依止無差別故，無量依止差別轉故。應知變化身如受用身說。

Should the dharma-body of the buddhas be said to differ, or be said not to differ? Because their basis, their resolve, and their activity are without difference, it should be said they do not differ. Because countless individual bodies manifest full awakening, it should be said they do differ. As is said of the dharma-body of the buddhas, so too the enjoyment body: because resolve and activity are without difference, it should be said they do not differ — but not because their basis is without difference, since manifold, differentiated bases are what actually operate. It should be understood that the transformation body is spoken of in just the same way as the enjoyment body.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Each of the six buddha-dharmas names a transformation already established earlier in the treatise, now gathered together as what actively sustains the dharma-body: purity is the transformation of the ālayavijñāna itself, the central subject of Part 2; ripening is the transformation of the physical senses into a wisdom that ripens correctly; abiding, mastery, speech, and rescue each transform an ordinary, limited human capacity — where to dwell, what one can influence, how one communicates, whom one can help — into its unlimited buddha counterpart. The "same or different" question is then resolved with the same precision used throughout this part: every buddha shares one undifferentiated basis, resolve, and activity — which is why, in that sense, "the buddhas" are not really many things — while each still manifests through its own distinct body, which is why, in another sense, they plainly are many. The enjoyment and transformation bodies follow the same logic but with one difference Vasubandhu is careful to flag: unlike the dharma-body, whose basis is genuinely undifferentiated, the enjoyment and transformation bodies do rest on differentiated individual bases, even while their underlying resolve and activity remain shared.

8. The Qualities That Accompany the Dharma-Body: Sixteen Verses of Homage

The treatise now lists the full catalogue of qualities that accompany the dharma-body, then unfolds sixteen verses of homage — each naming a further constellation of qualities and closing with the refrain "homage" (歸禮), in the manner of a devotional hymn rather than a technical enumeration.

Chinese Source

應知法身幾德相應？謂最清淨四無量，解脫，勝處，遍處，無諍，願智，四無礙解，六神通，三十二大士相，八十隨好，四一切相清淨，十力，四無畏，三不護，三念住，拔除習氣，無忘失法，大悲，十八不共佛法，一切相妙智等功德相應。

Ru-Yi Translation

With how many virtues should the dharma-body be understood to be accompanied? With the utterly pure four immeasurables, the eight liberations, the eight bases of mastery, the ten totalities, freedom from contention, the wisdom of aspiration, the four unobstructed forms of understanding, the six supernormal powers, the thirty-two marks of a great being, the eighty minor marks, the four kinds of complete purity in every aspect, the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses, the three things a buddha need never guard, the three foundations of mindfulness, the uprooting of habitual traces, the dharma of never forgetting, great compassion, the eighteen unique buddha-dharmas, and the sublime wisdom of every aspect — with all these qualities the dharma-body is accompanied.

此中有多頌：

「憐愍諸有情，　起和合遠離，

常不捨利樂，　四意樂，歸禮。

解脫一切障，　牟尼勝世間，

智周遍所知，　心解脫，歸禮。

能滅諸有情，　一切惑無餘，

害煩惱、有染　常哀愍，歸禮。

無功用，無著，　無礙，常寂定，

於一切問難　能解釋，歸禮。

於所依，能依，　所說；言及智，

能說；無礙慧　常善說，歸禮。

為彼諸有情，　故現、知言、行、

往、來、及出離，　善教者，歸禮。

諸眾生見尊，　皆審知善士，

暫見便深信，　開導者，歸禮。

攝受住持捨，　現化及變易，

等持，智自在，　隨證得，歸禮。」

Here there are several verses:

"Compassion for all sentient beings, bringing about union with benefit and separation from harm,

never abandoning their welfare and happiness — homage to these four resolves.

Liberated from every obstruction, the Sage surpassing the world,

wisdom pervading everything knowable — homage to this liberated mind.

Able to extinguish, without remainder, every affliction of every being,

destroying the afflictions and their taint, ever compassionate — homage.

Effortless, unattached, unobstructed, ever in quiescent absorption,

able to resolve every question and challenge — homage.

Toward the basis and what depends on it, and toward what is taught — word and wisdom together,

able to teach with unobstructed wisdom, always teaching well — homage.

For the sake of all sentient beings, manifesting, knowing their speech, conduct,

coming, going, and their path of release — the excellent teacher — homage.

All beings who behold the Honored One at once recognize a true, excellent being;

even a single glimpse gives rise to deep faith — the guide — homage.

Gathering in, sustaining, and releasing; manifesting transformation and transmutation;

mastery of concentration and wisdom, attained according to realization — homage."

「方便，歸依，淨，　及大乘出離；

於此誑眾生，　摧魔者，歸禮。

能說智，及斷，　出離，能障礙，

自他利，非餘　外道伏，歸禮。

處眾能伏說；　遠離二雜染；

無護無忘失，　攝御眾，歸禮。

遍一切行住，　無非圓智事，

一切時遍知，　實義者，歸禮。

諸有情利樂，　所作不過時，

所作常無虛，　無忘失，歸禮。

晝夜常六返，　觀一切世間，

與大悲相應，　利樂意，歸禮。

由行及由證，　由智及由業，

於一切二乘，　最勝者，歸禮。

由三身至得，　具相大菩提，

一切處他疑　皆能斷，歸禮。」

"Skillful means, refuge, purity, and release through the Great Vehicle —

the vanquisher of the māras who would deceive beings regarding these — homage.

Able to teach wisdom, relinquishment, and release, and to overcome every obstacle,

benefiting both self and other in a way no other teaching can — subduing all non-Buddhist rivals — homage.

Able, in any assembly, to teach and subdue; free of both kinds of defilement;

needing no guarding, never forgetting, skillfully governing the assembly — homage.

In every activity and every state of abiding, nothing that is not the work of perfect wisdom;

knowing all things at all times — the one who knows true reality — homage.

Benefit and happiness for sentient beings, accomplished always at the right time,

never once in vain — the one who never forgets — homage.

Six times, constantly, day and night, surveying every world,

ever joined with great compassion — the mind set on benefit and happiness — homage.

Through conduct, and through realization, through wisdom, and through activity,

surpassing both of the lesser vehicles entirely — the most excellent one — homage.

Through the attainment of the three bodies, possessing every mark of great awakening,

able to resolve every doubt others may raise, anywhere at all — homage."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu glosses this sequence verse by verse, but its overall shape is more important than any single line: sixteen verses, each closing on the same word of homage, moving through compassion, liberated wisdom, the extinguishing of affliction, effortless activity, skillful teaching, the sheer magnetism of a buddha's presence, mastery over manifestation itself, victory over every rival teaching, unfailing mindfulness, ceaseless compassionate vigilance ("six times, day and night" — the traditional watches into which a buddha's constant surveying of the world is divided), and finally the full possession of all three bodies at once. Two details are worth pausing on: the seventh verse's claim that a single glimpse of a buddha gives rise to deep faith is, Vasubandhu notes, meant literally as a description of the transformation body's actual visible effect on ordinary beings; and the tenth verse's "subduing all non-Buddhist rivals" is explained not as sectarian triumphalism but as the simple observation that no other teaching accomplishes both the teacher's own liberation and the liberation of others at once, which is the specific claim being defended. Read as a whole, the sequence functions exactly as its refrain suggests — not a technical list to be memorized, but a hymn of praise, structured so that reciting it is itself a form of practice.

9. Two Closing Verses on the Dharma-Body's Unsurpassed Virtue

Chinese Source

諸佛法身與如是等功德相應，復與所餘自性、因、果、業、相應、轉功德相應，是故應知諸佛法身無上功德。

Ru-Yi Translation

The dharma-body of the buddhas is accompanied by all these qualities just described, and is further accompanied by the remaining qualities of self-nature, cause, fruit, activity, correspondence, and transformation — and so it should be understood that the dharma-body of the buddhas possesses unsurpassed virtue.

此中有二頌：

「尊成實勝義；　一切地皆出；

至諸眾生上；　解脫諸有情；

無盡無等德　相應；現世間

及眾會可見，　非見人天等。」

Here there are two verses:

"The Honored One is genuinely accomplished ultimate truth; emerging from every ground;

reaching above all sentient beings; liberating every sentient being;

accompanied by inexhaustible, incomparable virtue — appearing within the world

and visible within assemblies, yet not seen by ordinary gods and humans."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu reads this final couplet as a compressed summary of everything the preceding sections established, with each phrase naming one of six further qualities: "genuinely accomplished ultimate truth" names the dharma-body's own self-nature — pure suchness itself; "emerging from every ground" names its cause, the completed cultivation of all ten grounds; "reaching above all sentient beings" names its fruit, since nothing surpasses it; "liberating every sentient being" names its activity; "accompanied by inexhaustible, incomparable virtue" names its correspondence with every quality already listed; and the final line distinguishes the three bodies one last time — "appearing within the world" is the transformation body, "visible within assemblies" is the enjoyment body, and "not seen by ordinary gods and humans" is the intrinsic-nature body itself, which no unawakened being can perceive directly at all.

10. Twelve Kinds of Profundity, Verses 1–6

The treatise now turns to the dharma-body's profundity directly, in twelve verses, each naming one specific way ordinary logic breaks down when applied to a buddha — and each resolved, not by abandoning logic, but by seeing more precisely what is actually being said.

Chinese Source

「佛無生為生，　亦無住為住，

諸事無功用，　第四食為食。

無異亦無量；　無數量一業；

不堅業堅業，　諸佛具三身。

現等覺非有，　一切覺非無，

一一念無量，　有非有所顯。

非染非離染，　由欲得出離，

了知欲無欲，　悟入欲法性。

諸佛過諸蘊，　安住諸蘊中，

與彼非一異，　不捨而善寂。

諸佛事相雜，　猶如大海水，

我已現當作，　他利無是思。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"The buddha's non-arising is what is meant by arising; likewise his non-abiding is what is meant by abiding;

every activity is effortless; the fourth kind of food is what he eats.

Without difference, and yet without measure; without number, yet a single activity;

activity that is not fixed, and activity that is fixed — the buddhas are endowed with three bodies.

The manifestation of full awakening is not truly existent; yet that all beings can awaken is not nonexistent;

in every single moment there are countless such awakenings — this is what is disclosed by what is existent and yet not existent.

Neither defiled nor free of defilement; through desire itself, release is attained;

knowing that desire has no real nature of its own, one thereby enters desire's own true nature.

The buddhas have gone beyond the aggregates, yet abide within the aggregates;

neither identical with nor different from them; not abandoning them, and yet perfectly at peace.

The buddhas' activities intermingle, just as the waters of the great ocean do;

accomplished in past, present, and future alike, no thought ever arises of 'I am benefiting another.'"

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Each verse names a different specific profundity, and Vasubandhu resolves each the same way, by distinguishing two levels at once. The first (arising and abiding) means that a buddha is never born the way an ordinary being is, driven by karma and affliction — and yet something does occur that is properly called "arising," just not that kind; likewise his "abiding" is specifically non-abiding nirvāṇa, and his eating is a fourth, purely demonstrative kind of food, taken only to let generous donors accumulate merit, never out of actual need. The second (number) means that, while every buddha shares one undifferentiated dharma-body, they remain countless in number, and their enjoyment bodies persist steadily while their transformation bodies flicker in and out like flame — three bodies, three different relationships to constancy. The third addresses how full awakening can be said to occur "in every single thought-moment," countless times over, without there being any single, fixed entity called "awakening" undergoing it — awakening is real without being a real, fixed thing. The fourth resolves an apparent contradiction about desire itself: a buddha is not simply free of desire the way that phrase usually suggests, but retains its subtlest trace deliberately (a point developed further in verse eleven below) until the discriminated, attached-to nature of desire is seen through and its true, empty nature directly entered. The fifth applies the same three-natures logic to the five aggregates: a buddha has transcended the aggregates as ordinarily, delusively grasped, while still abiding within them as their own true nature — neither identical with the aggregates nor separate from them, exactly as Part 3 explained the three natures are neither the same nor different. And the sixth uses the ocean simile to describe how countless buddhas' activities for beings blend into one another without any sense of separate agency or self-congratulation — helping without ever thinking "I have helped."

11. Twelve Kinds of Profundity, Verses 7–12

Chinese Source

「眾生罪不現，　如月於破器；

遍滿諸世間，　由法光如日。

或現等正覺，　或涅槃，如火；

此未曾非有，　諸佛身常故。

佛於非聖法，　人趣及惡趣，

非梵行法中，　最勝自體住。

佛一切處行，　亦不行一處，

於一切身現，　非六根所行。

煩惱伏不滅，　如毒呪所害，

由惑至惑盡，　證佛一切智。

煩惱成覺分，　生死為涅槃，

具大方便故，　諸佛不思議。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Through beings' own faults, he does not appear to them, like the moon unreflected in a broken vessel;

yet he pervades every world, through the Dharma's own light, like the sun.

Now manifesting full awakening, now manifesting nirvāṇa, like fire now blazing, now extinguished;

yet this has never once been truly absent, since the buddha's body is permanent.

The buddha, amid non-noble dharmas, amid the human and the evil destinies,

amid what is not conduct of purity, abides in his own most excellent abiding.

The buddha acts everywhere, and yet acts nowhere in particular;

he appears in every body, yet is not the object reached by any ordinary sense-faculty.

The afflictions are subdued but not destroyed, like poison rendered harmless by a spell;

by retaining affliction until affliction is finally, fully exhausted, the buddha's all-knowing wisdom is realized.

The afflictions become factors of awakening; birth-and-death becomes nirvāṇa itself;

because the buddhas are endowed with great skillful means, the buddhas are inconceivable."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

The seventh continues a theme from earlier parts: a buddha's apparent absence from an unfortunate being's experience is never a failure on the buddha's side, but is like a broken vessel that simply cannot hold the moon's reflection no matter how bright the moon — the Dharma's teaching still pervades every world like sunlight, available to anyone whose own vessel is intact enough to receive it. The eighth resolves the seeming inconsistency between a buddha's historically bounded life and his declared permanence: birth and parinirvāṇa are like a fire's blazing and going out — real events, but never implying the underlying capacity for fire itself has ceased, since the dharma-body beneath both events never wavers. The ninth describes a buddha's ability to dwell, in a certain sense, even amid unwholesome states, ordinary or unfortunate rebirths, and impure conduct — always from within his own most excellent, uncompromised abiding, the way one can visit a place without being altered by it. The tenth again applies three-natures logic to activity itself: post-attainment wisdom operates everywhere in ordinary, differentiated engagement, while fundamental wisdom operates nowhere in particular, resting in the undifferentiated; and it is specifically the transformation body that appears everywhere yet remains, in its true nature, beyond what an ordinary being's eye, ear, or mind could actually grasp. The eleventh makes explicit something the fourth verse implied: a bodhisattva deliberately retains the subtlest trace of affliction, unlike a śrāvaka who extinguishes it immediately, precisely because that retained trace is what allows continued engagement with saṃsāra for the sake of others, until it is finally, completely exhausted only at full buddhahood. And the twelfth closes the whole sequence with the treatise's most compressed statement of its central insight: given sufficiently great skillful means, affliction itself becomes a factor of awakening rather than its opposite, and birth-and-death simply is nirvāṇa — not two things reconciled, but one thing rightly seen, which is precisely why the buddhas, and this teaching about them, remain genuinely inconceivable to ordinary calculation.

12. The Twelve Kinds of Profundity, Named

Chinese Source

應知如是所說甚深有十二種：謂生住業住甚深，安立數業甚深，現等覺甚深，離欲甚深，斷蘊甚深，成熟甚深，顯現甚深，示現等覺涅槃甚深，住甚深，顯示自體甚深，斷煩惱甚深，不可思議甚深。

Ru-Yi Translation

It should be understood that the profundity spoken of here is of twelve kinds: the profundity of arising and abiding-in-activity; the profundity of establishing number and activity; the profundity of manifesting full awakening; the profundity of freedom from desire; the profundity of transcending the aggregates; the profundity of bringing beings to maturity; the profundity of appearing and not appearing; the profundity of manifesting awakening and nirvāṇa; the profundity of abiding; the profundity of manifesting one's own essence; the profundity of relinquishing affliction; and the profundity of being inconceivable.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu simply notes that these twelve names correspond, one by one and in order, to the twelve verses just given — a labeled index provided after the fact, matching the treatise's own working method throughout the whole of Part 3 and beyond: state the paradox in verse first, so its force is felt directly, and only then supply the technical name that anchors it back into the larger system.

13. Seven Kinds of Mindfulness of the Buddha's Dharma-Body

Chinese Source

若諸菩薩念佛法身，由幾種念應修此念？略說菩薩念佛法身，由七種念應修此念：一者、諸佛於一切法得自在轉，應修此念，於一切世界得無礙通故。此中有頌：

「有情界周遍，　具障而闕因，

二種決定轉，　諸佛無自在。」

二者、如來其身常住，應修此念，真如無間解脫垢故。三者、如來最勝無罪，應修此念，一切煩惱及所知障並離繫故。四者、如來無有功用，應修此念，不作功用一切佛事無休息故。五者、如來受大富樂，應修此念，清淨佛土大富樂故。六者、如來離諸染污，應修此念，生在世間一切世法不能染故。七者、如來能成大事，應修此念，示現等覺般涅槃等，一切有情未成熟者能令成熟，已成熟者令解脫故。

Ru-Yi Translation

If bodhisattvas hold the buddha's dharma-body in mindfulness, by how many kinds of mindfulness should this mindfulness be cultivated? Briefly, this should be cultivated through seven kinds. First: the buddhas have attained mastery over all dharmas — this should be held in mind, since they attain unobstructed penetration throughout every world. Here there is a verse:

"Though the realm of sentient beings is all-pervading, some are obstructed and some simply lack the cause for liberation;

with regard to these two fixed conditions, even the buddhas have no mastery."

Second: the tathāgatas' own body is permanent — this should be held in mind, since suchness is, without interruption, free of every stain. Third: the tathāgatas are utterly excellent and faultless — this should be held in mind, since they are entirely free from the bondage of both the afflictive and cognitive obstructions. Fourth: the tathāgatas act without any effort — this should be held in mind, since, without exertion, every buddha-activity proceeds without ever ceasing. Fifth: the tathāgatas enjoy great wealth and happiness — this should be held in mind, since their pure buddha-lands possess vast wealth and happiness. Sixth: the tathāgatas are free of every defilement — this should be held in mind, since, though born into the world, they are unstained by any worldly thing. Seventh: the tathāgatas are able to accomplish great deeds — this should be held in mind, since, by manifesting full awakening and final nirvāṇa and the rest, they bring every being not yet matured to maturity, and liberate every being already matured.

此中有二頌：

「圓滿屬自心，　具常住，清淨，

無功用，能施　有情大法樂，

遍行無依止，　平等利多生：

一切佛，智者　應修一切念。」

Here there are two verses:

"Completion belonging entirely to his own mind; endowed with permanence and with purity;

effortless, able to bestow upon beings the great joy of the Dharma;

pervading everywhere without any fixed dependence; benefiting countless lives equally —

toward all the buddhas, the wise should cultivate mindfulness of every one of these."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu treats the first mindfulness as forestalling a possible misunderstanding of everything that follows: a buddha's mastery, however vast, does not extend to overriding a being's own karmic obstruction or the plain absence, in some beings, of the very spiritual disposition liberation requires — even buddhas cannot make the causeless caused. The remaining six then form a coherent portrait to actually hold in mind during practice: permanent, utterly faultless, effortlessly active, abundantly resourced, untouched by worldly contact, and constantly, effectively working for the maturation and liberation of others. The closing couplet reduces this sevenfold portrait to a single instruction: hold all seven together, at once, as one unified object of recollection, rather than cycling through them one at a time.

14. The Pure Buddha-Land Described

The treatise now quotes, at length, the opening description of a pure buddha-land from the Bodhisattva Piṭaka's hundred-thousand-verse sūtra — one continuous, richly layered sentence, followed by an analysis into eighteen distinct perfections it displays.

Chinese Source

復次，諸佛清淨佛土相，云何應知？如菩薩藏百千契經序品中說，謂：薄伽梵住最勝光曜七寶莊嚴放大光明普照一切無邊世界，無量方所妙飾間列，周圓無際其量難測，超過三界所行之處，勝出世間善根所起，最極自在淨識為相，如來所都，諸大菩薩眾所雲集，無量天、龍、藥叉、健達縛、阿素洛、揭路荼、緊捺洛、莫呼洛伽、人、非人等常所翼從，廣大法味喜樂所持，作諸眾生一切義利，蠲除一切煩惱災橫，遠離眾魔，過諸莊嚴如來莊嚴之所依處，大念慧行以為遊路，大止妙觀以為所乘，大空無相無願解脫為所入門，無量功德眾所莊嚴，大寶花王之所建立大宮殿中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, how should the characteristics of the buddhas' pure buddha-lands be understood? As is said in the opening chapter of the Bodhisattva Piṭaka's hundred-thousand-verse sūtra: the Bhagavat dwells within a great palace built upon the king of great jeweled lotuses — adorned with the seven treasures of utterly excellent radiance, emitting great light that universally illuminates every boundless world; adorned throughout with wondrous ornaments across countless directions and places, its full extent without any boundary, its measure impossible to fathom; surpassing the domain traversed by the three realms, arisen from wholesome roots that transcend the world; having, as its very character, consciousness purified to the utmost mastery; the very capital of the tathāgatas, where great assemblies of great bodhisattvas gather like clouds; constantly attended by countless gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, and non-humans; sustained by the joy of the Great Vehicle's own vast savor of Dharma; accomplishing every benefit for all beings; eliminating every calamity born of affliction; far removed from every māra; itself the very basis of the tathāgatas' own adornment, surpassing every other adornment; having great mindfulness and wisdom's own activity as its path of approach; having great calming and sublime insight as its vehicle; and having the great gateways of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness as its entrance.

如是現示清淨佛土顯色圓滿，形色圓滿，分量圓滿，方所圓滿，因圓滿，果圓滿，主圓滿，輔翼圓滿，眷屬圓滿，住持圓滿，事業圓滿，攝益圓滿，無畏圓滿，住處圓滿，路圓滿，乘圓滿，門圓滿，依持圓滿。復次，受用如是清淨佛土，一向淨妙，一向安樂，一向無罪，一向自在。

In this way, the pure buddha-land is shown to display eighteen perfections: perfection of color, perfection of shape, perfection of extent, perfection of location, perfection of cause, perfection of fruit, perfection of its lord, perfection of its attendants, perfection of its retinue, perfection of its sustenance, perfection of its activity, perfection of its benefit, perfection of its fearlessness, perfection of its dwelling-place, perfection of its path, perfection of its vehicle, perfection of its gateway, and perfection of its support. Furthermore, the enjoyment of this pure buddha-land is, throughout, purely excellent; throughout, purely blissful; throughout, purely faultless; and throughout, purely self-mastered.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu works through the long opening sentence phrase by phrase, matching each clause to one of the eighteen perfections named afterward: the seven treasures and their radiance show perfection of color; the boundless, unmeasurable extent shows perfection of shape, extent, and location together; arising from world-transcending wholesome roots shows perfection of cause — specifically the roots produced by non-discriminating wisdom and its subsequently-attained counterpart; consciousness purified to its utmost shows perfection of fruit; and so on through attendants, retinue (the various classes of beings named, drawn from across the Buddhist cosmological hierarchy), sustenance (food and drink), activity, benefit, freedom from the four māras (afflictive, aggregate-bound, heavenly, and death) as fearlessness, dwelling-place, and finally path, vehicle, gateway, and support — mindfulness-and-wisdom as the path walked to reach it, calming-and-insight as the vehicle that carries one there, and the three gateways to liberation (emptiness, signlessness, wishlessness) as the entrance itself. The land's fourfold pure enjoyment — excellent, blissful, faultless, self-mastered — then closes the description by contrasting it directly with any ordinary realm, however pleasant, which always mixes some measure of impurity, suffering, fault, or constraint into its pleasures.

15. Five Constant Activities; Why the Buddha Teaches a Single Vehicle

Chinese Source

復次，應知如是諸佛法界，於一切時能作五業：一者、救濟一切有情災橫為業，於暫見時便能救濟盲聾狂等諸災橫故。二者、救濟惡趣為業，拔諸有情出不善處置善處故。三者、救濟非方便為業，令諸外道捨非方便求解脫行，置於如來聖教中故。四者、救濟薩迦耶為業，授與能超三界道故。五者、救濟乘為業，拯拔欲趣餘乘菩薩，及不定種性諸聲聞等，安處令修大乘行故。於此五業，應知諸佛業用平等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, it should be understood that this dharma-realm of the buddhas is, at all times, able to carry out five activities. First, the activity of rescuing all sentient beings from calamity: even a momentary glimpse of a buddha is enough to rescue beings from the calamities of blindness, deafness, madness, and the like. Second, the activity of rescuing beings from the unfortunate destinies: drawing beings out of unwholesome states and placing them in wholesome ones. Third, the activity of rescuing beings from wrong means: causing non-Buddhist teachers to abandon their pursuit of liberation through wrong means, and placing them within the tathāgatas' own noble teaching instead. Fourth, the activity of rescuing beings from the view of a substantial self: bestowing upon them the path capable of transcending the three realms. Fifth, the activity of rescuing beings regarding their vehicle: drawing out bodhisattvas inclined toward a lesser vehicle, and śrāvakas of indeterminate spiritual disposition, and establishing them in the practice of the Great Vehicle instead. Regarding these five activities, it should be understood that the buddhas' exercise of activity is entirely equal.

此中有頌：

「因、依、事、性、行，　別故許業異；

世間此力別，　無故非導師。」

Here there is a verse:

"Through difference in cause, basis, undertaking, nature, and conduct, ordinary beings' activities are said to differ;

since the buddhas lack any such differentiating force found in the world, they are, for this very reason, its true guides."

若此功德圓滿相應諸佛法身，不與聲聞獨覺乘共，以何意趣佛說一乘？

If the dharma-body of the buddhas, accompanied by such perfect qualities, is not shared with the Śrāvaka or Solitary-Buddha vehicles, with what intent do the buddhas nonetheless teach a single vehicle?

此中有二頌：

「為引攝一類，　及任持所餘，

由不定種性，　諸佛說一乘。

法，無我，解脫　等故，性不同，

得二意樂，化，　究竟：說一乘。」

Here there are two verses:

"In order to draw in one kind of being, and to sustain the rest,

because of beings of indeterminate spiritual disposition, the buddhas teach a single vehicle.

Because dharma, selflessness, and liberation are all equal, though spiritual dispositions differ;

through gaining two kinds of resolve, through transformation, and because it is ultimate — a single vehicle is taught."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

The five activities move from the most immediate to the most far-reaching: relieving physical affliction with a mere glimpse, rescuing from unfortunate rebirth, correcting misdirected spiritual effort, providing the actual path beyond the three realms, and finally redirecting those capable of the Great Vehicle away from a lesser one. Vasubandhu stresses that these operate with perfect equality across all buddhas precisely because none of the ordinary causes of unequal treatment — differing motive, differing resource, differing disposition — apply to beings whose activity is entirely effortless and whose basis is entirely shared. The one-vehicle teaching is then explained as serving two distinct audiences at once: it draws śrāvakas of indeterminate disposition — those not yet permanently fixed on the lesser path — toward the Great Vehicle, while sustaining bodhisattvas of similarly indeterminate disposition who might otherwise drift toward a lesser goal. Vasubandhu adds a further, striking reason among the second verse's five: śrāvakas who received predictions of future buddhahood in scriptures such as the Lotus Sūtra are said to gain a shared "resolve of equality with the Dharma's own nature," recognizing that a buddha's own nature and their own are, at that level, the very same nature — which is itself sufficient grounds for teaching that, in the end, only one vehicle truly exists.

16. One Dharma-Body, Yet Many Buddhas; Does a Buddha Enter Nirvāṇa?

Chinese Source

如是諸佛同一法身，而佛有多，何緣可見？

Ru-Yi Translation

Given that all buddhas share one and the same dharma-body, by what reasoning can it be seen that there are, nonetheless, many buddhas?

此中有頌：

「一界中無二，　同時無量圓，

次第轉非理，　故成有多佛。」

Here there is a verse:

"Within one world-realm there cannot be two buddhas at once; yet countless bodhisattvas' provisions reach completion simultaneously;

their becoming buddhas one after another, in strict sequence, makes no sense — therefore there must be many buddhas."

云何應知於法身中佛非畢竟入於涅槃，亦非畢竟不入涅槃？

How should it be understood that, with regard to the dharma-body, a buddha neither finally enters nirvāṇa nor finally fails to enter it?

此中有頌：

「一切障脫故，　所作無竟故，

佛畢竟涅槃，　畢竟不涅槃。」

Here there is a verse:

"Because he is freed of every obstruction, a buddha finally enters nirvāṇa;

because his work of benefiting beings has no final end, a buddha finally does not enter nirvāṇa."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu resolves "one dharma-body, yet many buddhas" with a simple observation about timing: since no two buddhas ever appear within the same world-system at once, there is a clear sense in which "buddha" names one thing — but since countless bodhisattvas across countless world-systems complete their provisioning for buddhahood at genuinely the same time, and it would be absurd to suppose their attainment must somehow queue up and occur one at a time in sequence, there must, in fact, be many buddhas manifesting simultaneously across different world-systems. This directly answers a real historical debate the commentary elsewhere notes: some schools held there could be only one buddha ever, others that buddhas succeed one another strictly in turn — this treatise's answer is that both are half right, since the dharma-body genuinely is one, while its manifestations are genuinely many. The nirvāṇa question receives an equally balanced answer: in the sense that every obstruction, without exception, has already been removed, a buddha has, in that specific sense, already finally entered nirvāṇa; but in the sense that a buddha's work benefiting beings can never be said to be complete — since beings in need of benefit never run out — a buddha, in that equally valid sense, never finally enters it at all. Both statements are true at once, describing two different aspects of the very same reality, in the same way saṃsāra and nirvāṇa were shown, in Part 10, to be one reality seen two ways.

17. Why the Enjoyment Body Is Not Simply the Intrinsic-Nature Body

Chinese Source

何故受用身非即自性身？由六因故：一、色身可見故，二、無量佛眾會差別可見故，三、隨勝解見自性不定可見故，四、別別而見自性變動可見故，五、菩薩聲聞及諸天等種種眾會間雜可見故，六、阿賴耶識與諸轉識轉依非理可見故。佛受用身即自性身，不應道理。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is the enjoyment body not simply identical to the intrinsic-nature body? For six reasons. First, because it is seen as a body possessing form. Second, because countless distinct assemblies, gathered around different buddhas, are seen. Third, because its own nature is seen to vary according to each perceiver's own resolve. Fourth, because it is seen, by beings individually, to undergo change over time in its own nature. Fifth, because it is seen intermingled with manifold assemblies at once — bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, gods, and others. Sixth, because it would make no sense for the transformation of the basis, from the ālayavijñāna and the other consciousnesses into the dharma-body, to apply to a body that already possessed form in this way. For these reasons, it is not correct to say that a buddha's enjoyment body is simply identical to the intrinsic-nature body.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Each of the six reasons points to a feature the intrinsic-nature body, by its very definition, cannot have. The intrinsic-nature body is suchness itself — formless, singular, undifferentiated, unchanging, and utterly beyond the reach of ordinary perception; the enjoyment body has visible form, appears differently within different assemblies gathered around different buddhas, is perceived differently depending on each perceiver's own capacity and resolve (a scriptural example already cited elsewhere in the tradition: some perceive a buddha's body as golden, others as blue), changes over the course of a single being's own experience of it, and appears surrounded by a genuinely mixed audience of bodhisattvas, śrāvakas, and gods together. The sixth reason is the most technical: the dharma-body is specifically what results from transforming the ālayavijñāna itself, and it would be incoherent to say the very same transformation somehow also produces a form-possessing, visually variable body — the two are simply different kinds of thing, arrived at through different processes.

18. Why the Transformation Body Is Not Simply the Intrinsic-Nature Body

Chinese Source

何因變化身非即自性身？由八因故，謂諸菩薩從久遠來，得不退定，於覩史多及人中生不應道理。又諸菩薩從久遠來，常憶宿住，書算數印工巧論中及於受用欲塵行中不能正知，不應道理。又諸菩薩從久遠來，已知惡說、善說法教，往外道所不應道理。又諸菩薩從久遠來，已能善知三乘正道，修邪苦行不應道理。

Ru-Yi Translation

For what reason is the transformation body not simply identical to the intrinsic-nature body? For eight reasons. Bodhisattvas who have, since far distant time, already attained unwavering concentration — it would make no sense for them still to need to take birth in the Tuṣita heaven and among humans. Bodhisattvas who have, since far distant time, always remembered their past lives — it would make no sense for them to fail to properly understand writing, arithmetic, seals, and the treatises of craft and skill, or the conduct of enjoying sensual pleasures. Bodhisattvas who have, since far distant time, already distinguished mistaken teachings from correct ones — it would make no sense for them to go to non-Buddhist teachers to learn from them. Bodhisattvas who have, since far distant time, already thoroughly known the correct path of all three vehicles — it would make no sense for them to practice mistaken austerities.

又諸菩薩捨百拘胝諸贍部洲，但於一處成等正覺，轉正法輪，不應道理。若離示現成等正覺，唯以化身於所餘處施作佛事，即應但於覩史多天成等正覺，何不施設遍於一切贍部洲中同時佛出？既不施設，無教無理。雖有多化，而不違彼無二如來出現世言；由一四洲攝世界故，如二輪王不同出世。此中有頌：

「佛微細化身，　多處胎平等，

為顯一切種，　成等覺而轉。」

Bodhisattvas who have relinquished a hundred koṭis of Jambudvīpa continents — it would make no sense for them to achieve full awakening and turn the wheel of the true Dharma in only a single place. If, apart from actually manifesting full awakening, buddha-activity elsewhere were carried out solely through a transformation body, full awakening ought to occur only within the Tuṣita heaven — why, then, do buddhas not appear simultaneously throughout every Jambudvīpa continent? Since this is not so, there is neither scriptural authority nor logical reason to think it. Though there are, in fact, many transformation bodies, this does not contradict the saying that "no two tathāgatas appear in the world at once," since one set of four continents constitutes a single world-system — just as two wheel-turning kings never appear in the world together. Here there is a verse:

"The buddha's subtle transformation bodies, entering the womb equally in many places at once,

in order to display every kind of teaching, proceed together to full awakening."

為欲利樂一切有情，發願修行證大菩提，畢竟涅槃不應道理，願行無果成過失故。

Having made the vow and cultivated the practice, for the sake of benefiting and bringing happiness to all sentient beings, to realize great awakening — it would make no sense to then enter final nirvāṇa immediately, since this would mean the vow and its practice bore no fruit, which would be a genuine fault.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu reads all eight reasons as variations on a single observation: the historical Buddha's biography — needing to be born, forgetting nothing yet still learning worldly skills, visiting non-Buddhist teachers, practicing austerities that (as this treatise elsewhere makes clear) do not actually lead to awakening, and achieving realization in only one place at one time — makes sense only as a deliberate, skillful performance for the benefit of onlookers, never as the literal biography of a being who, as a bodhisattva of countless eons' standing, had already resolved every one of these questions long before. The eighth reason then closes with the ninth root's underlying logic made explicit: if a bodhisattva's entire vow and practice were aimed at benefiting beings, an abrupt, final exit immediately upon awakening would render that whole vow pointless — which is itself sufficient reason to know that what looks like "the historical Buddha's one life" cannot be the intrinsic-nature body's own true, complete story.

19. Permanence Despite Impermanent Bodies; Six Reasons the Transformation Body Doesn't Stay Forever

Chinese Source

佛受用身及變化身，既是無常，云何經說如來身常？此二所依法身常故。又等流身及變化身，以恒受用無休廢故，數數現化不永絕故；如常受樂，如常施食，如來身常應知亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Given that the buddha's enjoyment body and transformation body are both impermanent, why do the scriptures say the tathāgata's body is permanent? Because these two bodies both rest upon the dharma-body, which is itself permanent. Moreover, since the enjoyment body is constantly enjoyed without interruption, and the transformation body manifests again and again without ever finally ceasing, they too may be spoken of as permanent — just as one might say "constantly experiencing happiness" or "constantly giving food" without meaning this literally without a single break. The tathāgata's body's permanence should be understood in just the same way.

由六因故，諸佛世尊所現化身非畢竟住：一、所作究竟，成熟有情已解脫故；二、為令捨離不樂涅槃，為求如來常住身故；三、為令捨離輕毀諸佛，令悟甚深正法教故；四、為令於佛深生渴仰，恐數見者生厭怠故；五、令於自身發勤精進，知正說者難可得故；六、為諸有情極速成熟，令自精進不捨軛故。

For six reasons, the transformation body the buddhas, the world-honored ones, manifest does not abide forever. First, because its work is already accomplished, once beings have been matured and liberated. Second, in order that beings might abandon any distaste for nirvāṇa, and instead seek the tathāgata's own permanently abiding body. Third, in order that beings might abandon any tendency to disparage the buddhas, and instead awaken to the profound, true teaching. Fourth, in order that beings might give rise to deep, heartfelt yearning for the buddha, out of fear that seeing him too often would breed weariness and complacency instead. Fifth, in order that beings might arouse diligent effort in themselves, knowing how rare it is to encounter one who teaches correctly. Sixth, in order that beings might be brought to maturity with the utmost speed, so that they themselves would not abandon effort under the yoke of practice.

此中有二頌：

「由所作究竟，　捨不樂涅槃，

離輕毀諸佛，　深生於渴仰，

內自發正勤，　為極速成熟：

故許佛化身，　而非畢竟住。」

Here there are two verses:

"Because the work is accomplished; because distaste for nirvāṇa is abandoned;

because disparaging the buddhas is left behind; because deep yearning is given rise to;

because inward, correct diligence is aroused; and because beings are brought to maturity with the utmost speed —

for these reasons it is accepted that a buddha's transformation body does not abide forever."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

The permanence question closes the same way the "one buddha or many" question did: by carefully distinguishing what is literally, unconditionally permanent (only the dharma-body itself) from what is permanent only in an extended, functional sense — an unbroken pattern of activity, like a habit of "always giving food" that plainly involves gaps, still meaningfully called constant because the pattern itself never lapses. The six reasons the transformation body deliberately does not remain forever then read, together, as a single psychological insight about how beings actually relate to a teacher: permanence, paradoxically, would undermine the very urgency, gratitude, and effort that a visible teacher's eventual departure is specifically designed to cultivate. A teacher who never left would, Vasubandhu implies, eventually be taken for granted; one whose visible presence is finite creates exactly the yearning, respect, and motivated diligence that make beings actually walk the path rather than merely admire it from a comfortable distance.

20. Why the Effort Must Continue — and the Treatise's Own Close

Chinese Source

諸佛法身，無始時來無別無量，不應為得更作功用？

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the dharma-body of the buddhas has, since beginningless time, always been without differentiation and without measure, should further diligent effort still be exerted in order to attain it?

此中有頌：

「佛得無別無量因，　有情若捨勤功用，

證得恒時不成因，　斷如是因不應理。」

Here there is a verse:

"The buddhas' own undifferentiated, unmeasured attainment has always itself served as the cause;

were sentient beings to abandon diligent effort, this attainment would, for all time, fail to serve as that cause —

to cut off such a cause makes no sense at all."

《阿毘達磨大乘經》中〈攝大乘品〉，我阿僧伽略釋究竟。

The chapter "Compendium of the Great Vehicle," within the Abhidharma-mahāyāna-sūtra — I, Asaṅga, have here brought this brief exposition to its completion.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

This closing question could easily be mistaken for an argument against practice altogether: if buddhahood's own attainment has always, beginninglessly, already been present as an undifferentiated, unlimited reality, why bother exerting further effort to reach it? Vasubandhu's answer reframes the entire relationship between cause and effort: the buddhas' own attainment functions as a cause specifically by continuing to inspire and sustain sentient beings' effort — it is not a substitute for that effort, but the very thing that makes the effort meaningful and effective in the first place. Should beings actually abandon effort, that same beginningless attainment would simply fail to do the causal work it has always done; the chain from cause to effect runs through sustained effort, not around it. To imagine "giving up effort because the goal is already, eternally there" is therefore not a subtle insight but a straightforward confusion about what the word "cause" means here — which is why the treatise says, in its very last technical point, that cutting off effort on these grounds simply does not make sense.

With this, Vasubandhu's commentary — and Asaṅga's own root text — reach their close. The treatise's final line is Asaṅga's own colophon: a plain statement, in his own voice, that this brief exposition of the Compendium of the Great Vehicle chapter is complete.

Part Eleven Complete — and the Treatise Entire

This closes Part Eleven, and with it, Ru-Yi's translation of the complete Mahāyānasaṃgraha: the three bodies of a buddha in every dimension the treatise brings to bear — characteristic, realization, mastery, basis, sustaining dharmas, distinction across buddhas, accompanying virtue in a full hymn of homage, twelve kinds of profundity, sevenfold mindfulness, the pure buddha-land, five constant activities, the reason for teaching one vehicle, and the careful reasoning by which one dharma-body, many buddhas, permanence amid impermanence, and a teacher's necessary departure are all shown to be, in the end, consistent. Ten parts ago, Part One opened by naming ten points that prove the Great Vehicle is truly the Buddha's own word; this closing part completes the last of those ten — the fruit of wisdom — bringing Asaṅga's own outline, given at the very start, to its own promised end.
