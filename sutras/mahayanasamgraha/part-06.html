The Distinctions in Cultivating That: The Ten Grounds

Part Five described the six perfections; Part Six describes the ten bodhisattva grounds (bhūmi) across which they are actually cultivated, ground by ground, to completion. Asaṅga explains why there are exactly ten, why each carries the name it does, how each is attained, and how the whole path — from an ordinary being's first resolve to the very threshold of buddhahood — spans three incalculable great eons.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 6 of 11 complete Source: Taishō T31, no. 1594, 彼修差別分第六 Commentary guide: Vasubandhu's 攝大乘論釋 (T1597)

Translation Method

As in Parts 1 through 5, the Chinese source is Xuánzàng's translation of Asaṅga's root text (T1594), read with Vasubandhu's commentary (T1597), also translated by Xuánzàng. The ten grounds' names are kept aligned with the site's Cheng Weishi Lun pages. See also this site's note on translation method.

1. The Ten Grounds, and the Ten Kinds of Ignorance They Counteract

Chinese Source

如是已說彼入因果，彼修差別云何可見？由菩薩十地。何等為十？一、極喜地，二、離垢地，三、發光地，四、焰慧地，五、極難勝地，六、現前地，七、遠行地，八、不動地，九、善慧地，十、法雲地。如是諸地安立為十，云何可見？為欲對治十種無明所治障故。所以者何？以於十相所知法界，有十無明所治障住。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having thus spoken of that entry's cause and effect, how may the distinctions in cultivating it be seen? By means of the bodhisattva's ten grounds. What are these ten? First, the Ground of Utmost Joy; second, the Ground Free of Defilement; third, the Ground of Emitted Light; fourth, the Ground of Blazing Wisdom; fifth, the Ground Difficult to Conquer; sixth, the Ground of Direct Presence; seventh, the Far-Reaching Ground; eighth, the Immovable Ground; ninth, the Ground of Excellent Wisdom; tenth, the Ground of the Dharma-Cloud. How may it be seen that these grounds are established as exactly ten? Because this is in order to counteract ten kinds of ignorance to be counteracted. Why is this? Because, within the dharma-realm known through ten aspects, ten kinds of ignorance stand as what is to be counteracted.

云何十相所知法界？謂初地中由遍行義；第二地中由最勝義；第三地中由勝流義；第四地中由無攝受義；第五地中由相續無差別義；第六地中由無雜染清淨義；第七地中由種種法無差別義；第八地中由不增不減義，相自在依止義，土自在依止義；第九地中由智自在依止義；第十地中由業自在依止義，陀羅尼門，三摩地門自在依止義。

What are the ten aspects of the dharma-realm that is known? In the first ground, through the aspect of pervasiveness. In the second ground, through the aspect of surpassing excellence. In the third ground, through the aspect of surpassing outflow. In the fourth ground, through the aspect of being without anything to appropriate. In the fifth ground, through the aspect of the continuum's non-differentiation. In the sixth ground, through the aspect of being without defilement or purification. In the seventh ground, through the aspect of the non-differentiation of manifold dharmas. In the eighth ground, through the aspect of neither increasing nor decreasing, the aspect of the basis for mastery over characteristics, and the aspect of the basis for mastery over buddha-lands. In the ninth ground, through the aspect of the basis for mastery over wisdom. In the tenth ground, through the aspect of the basis for mastery over activity, and the basis for mastery over the gateways of retention and of meditative absorption.

此中有三頌：

「遍行，最勝義，　及與勝流義，

如是無攝義，　相續無別義，

無雜染淨義，　種種無別義，

不增不減義，　四自在依義。

法界中有十　不染污無明，

治此所治障，　故安立十地。」

Here there are three verses:

"The aspect of pervasiveness, the aspect of surpassing excellence, together with the aspect of surpassing outflow,

the aspect of being without anything to appropriate, the aspect of the continuum's non-differentiation,

the aspect of being without defilement or purification, the aspect of the non-differentiation of manifold dharmas,

the aspect of neither increasing nor decreasing, and the aspect of the basis for the four masteries.

In the dharma-realm there are ten kinds of non-defiling ignorance;

in order to counteract these obstructions to be counteracted, the ten grounds are therefore established."

復次，應知如是無明，於聲聞等非染污，於諸菩薩是染污。

Furthermore, it should be understood that this same ignorance, for śrāvakas and the rest, is non-defiling, while for bodhisattvas, it is defiling.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu lists the ten specific obstructions counteracted at each ground: at the first, the very condition of being an unenlightened, ordinary being; at the second, subtle wrongdoing toward other beings in body, speech, and mind; at the third, a certain sluggishness in retaining what has been heard, reflected on, and cultivated; at the fourth, the most subtle residue of afflictions bound up with an innate sense of self; at the fifth, still wishing for one's own nirvāṇa in a lesser sense; at the sixth, gross characteristics still appearing to consciousness; at the seventh, even subtle characteristics still appearing; at the eighth, effortful engagement with the signless; at the ninth, insufficient spontaneous engagement in benefiting beings; and at the tenth, not yet having complete mastery over all dharmas. He also explains why the same ignorance is called "non-defiling" for śrāvakas but "defiling" for bodhisattvas: śrāvakas, who have no wish to enter these ten grounds in the first place, are simply unaffected by not knowing what a bodhisattva needs to know — it obstructs nothing they are trying to do. For a bodhisattva, whose entire path runs directly through this specific knowledge, the very same ignorance is a genuine defilement, actively blocking progress. And though the dharma-realm's full nature is, in principle, already glimpsed at the first ground, ten distinct grounds are still needed because that first glimpse must be stabilized and deepened through ten successive levels of settled attainment before it becomes fully one's own.

2. Why Each Ground Carries the Name It Does

Chinese Source

復次，何故初地說名極喜？由此最初得能成辦自他義利勝功能故。何故二地說名離垢？由極遠離犯戒垢故。何故三地說名發光？由無退轉等持，等至所依止故，大法光明所依止故。何故四地說名焰慧？由諸菩提分法焚滅一切障故。何故五地名極難勝？由真諦智與世間智，更互相違，合此難合令相應故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, why is the first ground named "Utmost Joy"? Because it is here that one first attains the excellent capacity to accomplish benefit for both oneself and others. Why is the second ground named "Free of Defilement"? Because one is here utterly free from the stain of violating moral discipline. Why is the third ground named "Emitted Light"? Because it is the basis for unwavering concentration and meditative attainment, and the basis for the great light of the Dharma. Why is the fourth ground named "Blazing Wisdom"? Because the factors of enlightenment here burn away every obstruction. Why is the fifth ground named "Difficult to Conquer"? Because the wisdom of ultimate truth and the wisdom of the world, mutually opposed as they are, are here made to accord — a difficult union brought into harmony.

何故六地說名現前？由緣起智為所依止，能令般若波羅蜜多現在前故。何故七地說名遠行？至功用行最後邊故。何故八地說名不動？由一切相有功用行不能動故。何故九地說名善慧？由得最勝無礙智故。何故十地說名法雲？由得總緣一切法智含藏一切陀羅尼門，三摩地門，譬如大雲能覆如空廣大障故，又於法身能圓滿故。

Why is the sixth ground named "Direct Presence"? Because, resting on the wisdom of dependent origination, the perfection of wisdom is here made directly present. Why is the seventh ground named "Far-Reaching"? Because it reaches the very furthest limit of effortful practice. Why is the eighth ground named "Immovable"? Because no characteristic-bound, effortful activity can any longer move it. Why is the ninth ground named "Excellent Wisdom"? Because one here attains the most excellent, unobstructed wisdom. Why is the tenth ground named "the Dharma-Cloud"? Because one here attains wisdom that takes all dharmas together as its object, holding within it every gateway of retention and of meditative absorption, like a great cloud able to cover obstructions as vast as space itself — and because it brings the dharma-body to full completion.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu adds a telling detail to several of these names. The first ground brings a joy śrāvakas simply do not experience in the same way, since their own direct realization accomplishes only their personal benefit, not the shared benefit of self and others that produces a bodhisattva's distinctive rejoicing. The second ground's freedom from defilement is not merely careful rule-keeping, as at the first ground, but discipline that has become the practitioner's actual nature, requiring no deliberate restraint at all. The fifth ground's "difficult union" names something genuinely hard: holding, at once, both the ultimate wisdom that sees no fixed characteristics anywhere and the ordinary, discriminating wisdom needed to function skillfully in the world — two ways of knowing that pull against each other until this ground finally reconciles them. And the tenth ground's cloud image works two ways at once, Vasubandhu notes: the great mass of retention and absorption gateways gathered "within" this wisdom is like water held inside a cloud, while that same wisdom's capacity to cover and dissolve every remaining vast obstruction is like a cloud spreading to cover the whole sky.

3. Four Ways the Grounds Are Attained

Chinese Source

得此諸地云何可見？由四種相：一、得勝解，謂得諸地深信解故。二、得正行，謂得諸地相應十種正法行故。三、得通達，謂於初地達法界時，遍能通達一切地故。四、得成滿，謂修諸地到究竟故。

Ru-Yi Translation

How may the attainment of these grounds be seen? By four aspects: first, attainment through resolute conviction — that is, gaining deep, resolute conviction in the grounds; second, attainment through correct practice — that is, gaining the ten kinds of correct dharma-practice appropriate to the grounds; third, attainment through penetration — that is, when the dharma-realm is penetrated at the first ground, thereby penetrating every ground at once; fourth, attainment through complete fulfillment — that is, cultivating the grounds all the way to their ultimate completion.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

These four aspects trace the same overall arc already familiar from Part 4's account of the path: conviction, corresponding to the earlier stage of resolute conduct; penetration, corresponding to the path of seeing, where the dharma-realm's single nature is glimpsed all at once, in a way that already implicitly reaches every one of the ten grounds even though each still needs to be separately stabilized; correct practice, corresponding to the sustained work of the path of cultivation; and complete fulfillment, corresponding to the path's ultimate completion. The same underlying reality is engaged differently at each stage — first believed, then directly seen, then practiced, then finally brought to its own completion.

4. Five Modes of Cultivation, Five Resulting Fruits

Chinese Source

修此諸地，云何可見？謂諸菩薩於地地中，修奢摩他，毘鉢舍那，由五相修。何等為五？謂集總修，無相修，無功用修，熾盛修，無喜足修。如是五修，令諸菩薩成辦五果：謂念念中銷融一切麁重依止，離種種相得法苑樂，能正了知周遍無量無分限相大法光明，順清淨分無所分別無相現行，為令法身圓滿成辦，能正攝受後後勝因。

Ru-Yi Translation

How may the cultivation of these grounds be seen? At each successive ground, bodhisattvas cultivate calming and insight through five modes. What are these five? Cultivation that gathers everything together; cultivation free of characteristics; cultivation free of effort; cultivation that blazes vigorously; and cultivation free of easy satisfaction. These five modes of cultivation bring about five fruits for bodhisattvas: dissolving, moment by moment, the basis of every coarse and heavy burden; gaining the joy of the Dharma's own garden, free of every manifold conception; correctly comprehending the great light of the Dharma, pervading without limit or boundary; engaging, in accordance with the portion of purity, without discrimination and without characteristics; and, in order to bring the dharma-body to full completion, correctly gathering in ever more excellent causes, one after another.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu pairs the five modes of cultivation with their five specific fruits. "Gathering everything together" and "free of characteristics" together dissolve, moment by moment, the deep karmic burden of habituated obstructions built up since beginningless time — the same "coarse and heavy" (麁重) seeds discussed at length in Part 2. "Free of effort" produces a joy that comes from no longer needing to strain against the manifold conceptions ordinary experience is built from — a genuine rest, likened to a garden one can simply dwell in. "Blazing vigorously" produces an ever-widening comprehension of the Dharma's own light, without any sense of limit. And "free of easy satisfaction" — refusing to settle for what has already been attained — produces both the effortless, non-discriminating engagement of a mind approaching full purity, and the continued gathering of ever-better causes all the way to the dharma-body's final completion at buddhahood, glossed by Vasubandhu as "completion" proper belonging fully only to the eleventh, buddha ground beyond the tenth.

5. Ten Perfections Matched to the Ten Grounds

Chinese Source

由增勝故，說十地中別修十種波羅蜜多。於前六地所修六種波羅蜜多，如先已說。後四地中所修四者：一、方便善巧波羅蜜多，謂以前六波羅蜜多所集善根，共諸有情迴求無上正等菩提故。二、願波羅蜜多，謂發種種微妙大願，引攝當來波羅蜜多殊勝眾緣故。三、力波羅蜜多，謂由思擇、修習二力，令前六種波羅蜜多無間現行故。四、智波羅蜜多，謂由前六波羅蜜多成立妙智，受用法樂，成熟有情故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because of their surpassing predominance, it is said that ten distinct perfections are cultivated across the ten grounds. The six perfections cultivated in the first six grounds are as already explained. The four cultivated in the latter four grounds are these: first, the perfection of skillful means — dedicating the wholesome roots gathered through the first six perfections, together with all sentient beings, toward unsurpassed, perfect awakening. Second, the perfection of vows — making manifold, sublime, great vows that draw in the surpassing conditions needed for the perfections yet to come. Third, the perfection of power — through the twin powers of deliberation and cultivation, causing the first six perfections to operate without interruption. Fourth, the perfection of wisdom [in its fullest sense] — through the first six perfections, establishing sublime wisdom, enjoying the joy of the Dharma, and bringing sentient beings to maturity.

又此四種波羅蜜多，應知般若波羅蜜多無分別智後得智攝。又於一切地中，非不修習一切波羅蜜多。如是法門，是波羅蜜多藏之所攝。

It should further be understood that these four perfections are all included within the wisdom-perfection's own subsequently-attained wisdom, following its non-discriminating wisdom. And it is not that any single perfection is left uncultivated at any of the ten grounds — every perfection is cultivated at every ground. Such a teaching is included within the treasury of the perfections.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu is careful to head off a possible misunderstanding: saying that "generosity is cultivated at the first ground" does not mean the other five perfections are absent there, only that generosity is the predominant, most emphasized practice at that particular stage — every perfection, in fact, is practiced at every ground, "according to one's capacity and portion." The four additional perfections named for the latter four grounds — skillful means, vows, power, and wisdom in its fullest sense — are not really separate from the original six, but are the wisdom-perfection's own subsequently-attained wisdom (introduced in Part 4's illusionist simile) unpacked into four more specific functions: dedicating merit skillfully, sustaining aspiration through vows, ensuring the six perfections never lapse through the joint power of reflection and habituated practice, and finally using accumulated wisdom to benefit others directly. Vasubandhu closes by noting that the entire ten-ground teaching belongs to what he calls "the treasury of the perfections" — the Great Vehicle's own body of teaching, as distinct from the Śrāvaka canon — and that it is taught identically across every buddha-land by every buddha, which is why it holds the most authoritative possible place within Mahāyāna scripture.

6. Three Incalculable Great Eons, Across Five Kinds of Practitioners

Chinese Source

復次，凡經幾時修行諸地可得圓滿？有五補特伽羅，經三無數大劫：謂勝解行補特伽羅，經初無數大劫修行圓滿；清淨增上意樂行補特伽羅及有相行，無相行補特伽羅，於前六地及第七地，經第二無數大劫修行圓滿；即此無功用行補特伽羅，從此已上至第十地，經第三無數大劫修行圓滿。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, altogether how much time is needed for cultivating the grounds to reach completion? Five kinds of practitioners pass through three incalculable great eons: the practitioner of resolute conduct completes practice over the first incalculable great eon; the practitioner of pure, superior resolve, together with the practitioner of practice-with-characteristics and the practitioner of practice-without-characteristics, complete practice, across the first six grounds and the seventh, over the second incalculable great eon; and this same practitioner of effortless practice, from that point up through the tenth ground, completes practice over the third incalculable great eon.

此中有頌：

「清淨，增上力，　堅固心，昇進，

名菩薩初修　無數三大劫。」

Here there is a verse:

"By the power of purity and superior resolve, by a firm mind, and by continued advancement —

this is named a bodhisattva's initial cultivation across the three incalculable great eons."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu clarifies that these are not five different people but five successive stages of the very same practitioner, named the way a single person is called a stream-enterer, then a once-returner, then a non-returner at different points along the śrāvaka path. The practitioner of resolute conduct completes the first incalculable eon during the stage of resolute conduct explained in Part 4; upon directly penetrating suchness, the same practitioner becomes the practitioner of pure, superior resolve, known as the practitioner "with characteristics" through the first six grounds and "without characteristics, yet still effortful" at the seventh, completing the second incalculable eon; entering the eighth ground, the same practitioner becomes the practitioner of effortless practice, whose effortlessness only reaches full maturity at the ninth and tenth grounds, completing the third incalculable eon there. He closes by explaining the verse's terms: "the power of purity" is the strength of one's own accumulated wholesome roots, ensuring what is to be counteracted cannot overpower it; "the power of superior resolve" is the strength of one's own great vows, ensuring one continually meets good spiritual friends; and "a firm mind" that "continues to advance" names bodhicitta so settled that no negative influence can dislodge it, sustaining wholesome growth, without exception, across every single lifetime until buddhahood is reached.
