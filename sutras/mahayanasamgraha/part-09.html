The Higher Training in Wisdom

Parts Seven and Eight covered the higher trainings in morality and concentration; Part Nine covers the third and final one — non-discriminating wisdom — examined from every angle the treatise can bring to bear: its own nature, its support, its causes, what it takes as object, its many fruits, and a long sequence of similes that carry more of the actual teaching than the prose around them. This is the treatise's most sustained run of verse, since much of what can be said about wisdom beyond all discrimination is best said in the compressed, suggestive form a verse allows.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 9 of 11 complete Source: Taishō T31, no. 1594, 增上慧學分第九 Commentary guide: Vasubandhu's 攝大乘論釋 (T1597)

Translation Method

As in Parts 1 through 8, the Chinese source is Xuánzàng's translation of Asaṅga's root text (T1594), read with Vasubandhu's commentary (T1597), also translated by Xuánzàng. "Non-discriminating wisdom" (無分別智) is kept consistent with the site's Cheng Weishi Lun pages throughout. See also this site's note on translation method.

1. An Overview: Wisdom Examined from Every Angle

Chinese Source

如是已說增上心殊勝，增上慧殊勝云何可見？謂無分別智，若自性，若所依，若因緣，若所緣，若行相，若任持，若助伴，若異熟，若等流，若出離，若至究竟，若加行、無分別、後得勝利，若差別，若無分別、後得譬喻，若無功用作事，若甚深。應知無分別智，名增上慧殊勝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having thus spoken of the excellence of the higher training in concentration, how may the excellence of the higher training in wisdom be seen? It is by examining non-discriminating wisdom under its self-nature, its support, its causes and conditions, its object, its aspect, its sustaining function, its companion-path, its ripening-fruit, its outflow-fruit, its emergence, its ultimate point, the benefits of preparatory, fundamental, and subsequently-attained wisdom, its own distinctions, similes for the fundamental and subsequently-attained wisdoms, its effortless activity, and its profundity. It should be understood that this non-discriminating wisdom is what is named the excellence of the higher training in wisdom.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu identifies non-discriminating wisdom as, in effect, another name for the wisdom-perfection at its fullest — the same perfection named in Part 5, now examined in exhaustive detail because it is what the entire path has been building toward. He also introduces here a threefold division that structures much of what follows: preparatory non-discriminating wisdom (加行), the effortful, still-discriminating approach toward realization; fundamental non-discriminating wisdom (根本), the direct realization itself, utterly free of discrimination; and subsequently-attained wisdom (後得), the wisdom that engages the world skillfully once realization has occurred — the same three-stage structure already met in Part 4's illusionist simile.

2. Self-Nature: Free of Five Characteristics

Chinese Source

此中無分別智，離五種相以為自性：一、離無作意故，二、離過有尋有伺地故，三、離想受滅寂靜故，四、離色自性故，五、離於真義異計度故。離此五相，應知是名無分別智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, non-discriminating wisdom has, as its self-nature, freedom from five characteristics: first, freedom from simply lacking any attention at all; second, freedom from merely having passed beyond the stage of applied and sustained thought; third, freedom from the mere stillness of the cessation of perception and feeling; fourth, freedom from having the nature of physical form; fifth, freedom from discriminating and calculating something other than the real meaning itself. Free of these five characteristics — this should be understood as non-discriminating wisdom.

於如所說無分別智成立相中，復說多頌：

「諸菩薩自性，　遠離五種相，

是無分別智，　不異計於真。」

In establishing the characteristics of non-discriminating wisdom as just described, many further verses are spoken:

"A bodhisattva's self-nature, free of these five characteristics —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, which discriminates and calculates nothing other than the real."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Each of the five denials rules out a plausible but mistaken candidate for what "non-discriminating" could mean. It is not simply the absence of attention, or sleep, drunkenness, or fainting would count as non-discriminating wisdom. It is not simply any meditative state beyond applied and sustained thought (the second absorption and above), or ordinary deep meditation would already be it. It is not simply the stillness of the cessation of perception and feeling, since that attainment has no mind present at all, and wisdom requires a mind. It is not like physical form, which is simply inert and incapable of any cognition whatsoever. And it does not discriminate something other than the true, real object it takes — its freedom from discrimination applies specifically to how it engages reality, not to being blank or disengaged. What remains, once all five wrong candidates are excluded, is wisdom that engages the real directly, without ever superimposing anything extra onto it.

3. Four Verses: Support, Cause and Condition, Object, and Aspect

Chinese Source

「諸菩薩所依，　非心而是心，

是無分別智，　非思義種類。

諸菩薩因緣，　有言聞熏習，

是無分別智，　及如理作意。

諸菩薩所緣，　不可言法性，

是無分別智，　無我性真如。

諸菩薩行相，　復於所緣中，

是無分別智，　彼所知無相。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"A bodhisattva's support: not mind, and yet also mind —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, not of the same kind as any deliberating thought.

A bodhisattva's cause and condition: verbal hearing-habituation —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, together with correct attention.

A bodhisattva's object: the unspeakable nature of dharmas —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, the selfless nature that is suchness.

A bodhisattva's aspect, in turn, within its very object —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, whose known object is without any mark."

「相應自性義，　所分別非餘，

字展轉相應，　是謂相應義。

非離彼能詮，　智於所詮轉，

非詮不同故，　一切不可言。」

"The essential meaning of correspondence — this and nothing else — is what is discriminated;

syllables, linked one after another in succession, are what is meant by 'correspondence.'

It is not that this wisdom operates on what is expressed apart from the very word that expresses it;

yet, because what expresses and what is expressed do not match, all things remain, in the end, unspeakable."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu resolves the first verse's apparent contradiction — "not mind, and yet also mind" — by distinguishing two senses of "mind": non-discriminating wisdom does not itself deliberate or reason the way ordinary discursive thought does, so it is "not mind" in that sense; but it does arise from and depend on mind as its causal basis, so it is still "mind" in that broader sense. The second verse traces its actual cause: hearing and reflecting on the Great Vehicle's teaching (the same "hearing-habituation" introduced in Part 2 as the seed of the dharma-body) combined with correctly directed attention. The third names its object as suchness itself — "unspeakable" not because nothing can be said about it, but because no word actually captures its own-nature the way the word seems to promise. The fourth states something genuinely subtle: this wisdom's own aspect and its object are not, in the end, two separate things standing apart from one another, the way an ordinary perceiver and a perceived thing normally are. The two further verses on "unspeakability" work through why: words function by linking syllables together in a fixed, conventional correspondence with meaning, but that correspondence is itself only ever a convention, never a genuine identity between the word and the thing — which is why, however precisely language is used, ultimate reality itself remains, strictly, beyond what any word can fully capture.

4. Four More Verses: Sustaining Function, Companion-Path, and Two Kinds of Fruit

Chinese Source

「諸菩薩任持，　是無分別智，

後所得諸行，　為進趣增長。

諸菩薩助伴，　說為二種道，

是無分別智，　五到彼岸性。

諸菩薩異熟，　於佛二會中，

是無分別智，　由加行證得。

諸菩薩等流，　於後後生中，

是無分別智，　自體轉增勝。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"A bodhisattva's sustaining function: this is non-discriminating wisdom,

sustaining every subsequently-attained activity, so that it may advance and grow.

A bodhisattva's companion is said to be twofold in path —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, the nature of the [other] five perfections that reach the far shore.

A bodhisattva's ripening: within the two assemblies of a buddha —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, attained through preparatory effort.

A bodhisattva's outflow, in life after life that follows —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, its own essence growing ever more excellent."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu explains "companion" as the other five perfections divided into two supporting paths: generosity, discipline, patience, and diligence form the "provisioning path," while meditative concentration forms the "supporting path" — together, these are what non-discriminating wisdom grows out of and depends on, which is why wisdom itself is sometimes simply called "the perfection" without further qualification. "Ripening," he explains, names where this wisdom actually bears its karmic fruit: in an assembly gathered around a buddha's transformation body, for a bodhisattva still exerting preparatory effort; in an assembly gathered around the enjoyment body, for one who has already attained the wisdom directly. And "outflow" names the simple, cumulative fact that, once this wisdom has been genuinely attained, it continues to grow more excellent from one lifetime to the next, its own essence never regressing even as the bodhisattva is reborn again and again across the ten grounds.

5. Emergence Across the Ten Grounds, and the Ultimate Fruit

Chinese Source

「諸菩薩出離，　得成辦相應，

是無分別智，　應知於十地。

諸菩薩究竟，　得清淨三身，

是無分別智，　得最上自在。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"A bodhisattva's emergence: attaining full accomplishment in correspondence with it —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, understood across the ten grounds.

A bodhisattva's ultimate point: attaining the three purified bodies —

this is non-discriminating wisdom, attaining the highest mastery."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

"Emergence" describes the long process by which non-discriminating wisdom, first genuinely attained at the first ground, is not yet fully accomplished there — it takes the entire span of the ten grounds, across countless further eons, for that initial attainment to mature into full accomplishment. "The ultimate point" names where this entire process actually ends: the three purified bodies of a buddha (the subject of Part 11) together with ten kinds of complete mastery Vasubandhu says will be detailed later in the treatise. Non-discriminating wisdom, in other words, is not a single achievement reached once and then simply held — it is a seed planted at the very first ground that only fully ripens at buddhahood itself.

6. Three Similes of Space; Similes Distinguishing the Three Wisdoms

The treatise now turns to a run of similes, first illustrating how each of the three wisdoms — preparatory, fundamental, and subsequently-attained — remains untainted, and then distinguishing all three from one another through four further comparisons.

Chinese Source

「如虛空無染，　是無分別智，

種種極重惡，　由唯信勝解。

如虛空無染，　是無分別智，

解脫一切障，　得成辦相應。

如虛空無染，　是無分別智，

常行於世間，　非世法所染。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Like space, untainted — this is non-discriminating wisdom;

every manner of grave evil, through faith and resolute conviction alone, cannot stain it.

Like space, untainted — this is non-discriminating wisdom;

liberated from every obstruction, its accomplishment fully in correspondence.

Like space, untainted — this is non-discriminating wisdom;

constantly active within the world, yet unstained by any worldly thing."

「如啞求受義，　如啞正受義，

如非啞受義，　三智譬如是。

如愚求受義，　如愚正受義，

如非愚受義，　三智譬如是。

如五求受義，　如五正受義，

如末那受義，　三智譬如是。

如未解於論，　求論，受法，義，

次第譬三智，　應知加行等。」

"As a mute person seeks to grasp meaning, as a mute person directly grasps it,

as one who is not mute then expresses what is grasped — such are the similes for the three wisdoms.

As a simple person seeks to grasp meaning, as a simple person directly grasps it,

as one who is not simple then expresses what is grasped — such are the similes for the three wisdoms.

As the five senses grasp their object, as the five senses directly grasp it,

as the mind then grasps that same object — such are the similes for the three wisdoms.

As one who has not yet understood a treatise seeks it out, then receives its content, then its meaning —

in this order, the three wisdoms are compared: preparatory, and the rest, should thus be understood."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

The three space-verses map directly onto the three wisdoms in order: preparatory wisdom, sustained by mere faith and resolute conviction, is already untouched by even grave evil, the way space is untouched by anything within it; fundamental wisdom, having actually liberated the practitioner from every obstruction, is untainted in a fuller sense; and subsequently-attained wisdom remains untainted even while fully, constantly active in the ordinary world, the way space remains itself no matter what passes through it. The four comparison-verses that follow then distinguish the three wisdoms from each other rather than from taint: a mute person seeking to communicate (preparatory wisdom, still striving, still without words), a mute person's silent direct grasp (fundamental wisdom, direct but wordless), and a person who can actually speak once meaning is grasped (subsequently-attained wisdom, which alone can teach). The same pattern repeats through "simple versus wise," "the five physical senses versus the coordinating mind," and finally a scholar's progression from seeking out an unfamiliar treatise, to absorbing its content, to finally grasping its full meaning — four different everyday activities, Vasubandhu notes, each illuminating the same underlying three-stage structure from a different angle.

7. Closed and Open Eyes; the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel; the Deepest Point

Chinese Source

「如人正閉目，　是無分別智；

即彼復開目，　後得智亦爾。

應知如虛空，　是無分別智；

於中現色像，　後得智亦爾。

如末尼，天樂，　無思成自事，

種種佛事成，　常離思亦爾。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"As a person's eyes are simply closed — this is non-discriminating wisdom;

as that same person then opens their eyes — subsequently-attained wisdom is just so.

It should be understood as like space — this is non-discriminating wisdom;

images of form appearing within it — subsequently-attained wisdom is just so.

Like a wish-fulfilling jewel, or heavenly music, accomplishing their own function without any thought —

the manifold activities of a buddha are accomplished, constantly free of thought, in just this way."

「非於此、非餘，　非智、而是智，

與境無有異，　智成無分別。

應知一切法，　本性無分別，

所分別無故，　無分別智無。」

"Not toward this, and not toward anything else — not wisdom, and yet wisdom —

without any difference from its object, wisdom becomes non-discriminating.

It should be known that all dharmas are, by their very own nature, without discrimination;

because what would be discriminated does not exist, [beings remain unliberated only] where non-discriminating wisdom is itself absent."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

The closed-and-open-eyes image is deliberately simple: fundamental wisdom, resting in direct, wordless realization, is like eyes closed to any distinguishable object; subsequently-attained wisdom, engaging particulars again afterward, is the very same person opening their eyes. The sky-and-images verse makes a related point from the other direction: fundamental wisdom is like empty space itself, all-pervading and never stained by anything; subsequently-attained wisdom is like the images that appear within that same space, distinguishable and various, yet never separate from the space they appear in. The wish-fulfilling jewel and heavenly music — both traditional images for spontaneous, effortless accomplishment — describe how a buddha's countless activities for the benefit of beings proceed without any deliberate, self-conscious intention driving them, the way a wish-jewel simply grants wishes and a certain kind of heavenly instrument is said to produce music without anyone playing it.

The final two verses reach the deepest point of the entire discussion. Fundamental wisdom is described as taking neither its own apparent object (since that would still be a kind of discrimination) nor anything else as object — and yet it is still, genuinely, wisdom, since it clearly does something other than mere blankness. Vasubandhu resolves this by saying the knower and the known, here, are simply not two separate things standing apart — wisdom "becomes non-discriminating" precisely by no longer differing from its own object at all. And the closing verse answers an obvious challenge: if all dharmas are, by their very nature, already free of discrimination, why isn't every being already liberated? Because, Vasubandhu explains, the mere abstract fact that reality has no fixed characteristics does not itself liberate anyone — only the actual, direct realization of that fact, which is exactly what non-discriminating wisdom is, does. Its absence, not any defect in reality itself, is the only thing standing between an ordinary being and freedom.

8. Three Kinds Each of Preparatory, Fundamental, and Subsequently-Attained Wisdom

Chinese Source

此中加行無分別智有三種，謂因緣、引發、數習生差別故。根本無分別智亦有三種，謂喜足、無顛倒、無戲論無分別差別故。後得無分別智有五種，謂通達、隨念、安立、和合、如意思擇差別故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, preparatory non-discriminating wisdom is of three kinds, distinguished as arising through causal condition, through prior induction, and through repeated practice. Fundamental non-discriminating wisdom is likewise of three kinds, distinguished as the non-discrimination of easy satisfaction, the non-discrimination free of distorted views, and the non-discrimination free of conceptual proliferation. Subsequently-attained wisdom has five kinds, distinguished as reflective consideration through direct realization, through recollection, through explaining to others, through unifying comprehension, and through wish-fulfilling discernment.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu glosses each triad concretely. Preparatory wisdom's three kinds arise, respectively, from an innate spiritual disposition, from habituation carried over from previous lives, and from effort exerted in the present life. Fundamental wisdom's "non-discrimination of easy satisfaction" is, tellingly, not always praiseworthy: Vasubandhu applies it both to a bodhisattva who feels genuinely complete after mastering hearing, reflection, and their fruits, and — as a cautionary parallel — to certain non-Buddhist meditators who mistake the peak of worldly meditative attainment for final liberation and stop seeking further, "satisfied" with something short of true freedom. "Non-discrimination free of distorted views" belongs to śrāvakas, who correctly see impermanence, suffering, and the rest without the four classic distortions; "free of conceptual proliferation" belongs distinctively to bodhisattvas, whose realization goes further still, reaching a truth that lies beyond the very reach of ordinary language and worldly wisdom. Subsequently-attained wisdom's five kinds trace a practical sequence: recognizing one's own realization, recalling it afterward, being able to explain it to others, drawing everything into one unified view of suchness, and finally — the most striking of the five — a kind of discernment so accomplished that it can, Vasubandhu says, transform earth itself into gold, precisely because it operates in full accordance with reality's own nature rather than against it.

9. More Proofs That Non-Discriminating Wisdom Must Exist

Chinese Source

「鬼、傍生、人、天，　各隨其所應，

等事心異故，　許義非真實。

於過去事等，　夢像，二影中，

雖所緣非實，　而境相成就。

若義義性成，　無無分別智；

此若無，佛果　證得不應理。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Ghosts, animals, humans, and gods, each according to what suits them,

experience the same thing with different minds — showing that meaning is not truly real.

In past events and the like, and within dreams, images, and the twin reflections [of mirror and echo],

though what is taken as object is not real, its objective aspect is still successfully achieved.

If meaning had a truly established own-nature, there would be no non-discriminating wisdom;

and were this absent, the realization of buddhahood itself would make no sense."

「得自在菩薩，　由勝解力故，

如欲地等成，　得定者亦爾。

成就簡擇者，　有智得定者，

思惟一切法，　如義皆顯現。

無分別智行，　諸義皆不現，

當知無有義，　由此亦無識。」

"A bodhisattva who has attained mastery, through the power of resolve alone,

can transform earth and the like exactly as wished — one who has attained concentration can do just the same.

One who has accomplished discernment, a wise one who has attained concentration,

reflecting on any dharma, finds it appear exactly according to that reflection.

When non-discriminating wisdom is actually operating, no object whatsoever appears;

from this it should be known that there is no object at all — and, by this same token, no perceiving consciousness either."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

This sequence of verses returns to the same style of argument used for Consciousness-Only in Part 3: the same water seen by a hungry ghost as pus and blood, by a fish as its home, and by a human as ordinary water shows that no single fixed "meaning" or object underlies experience in the way naive realism assumes. Vasubandhu then draws the conclusion these verses are building toward: if objects really did have their own fixed, independent nature, there could be no non-discriminating wisdom at all, since such wisdom depends precisely on that fixed nature's absence — and if there were no non-discriminating wisdom, buddhahood itself, which is defined by that very realization, could never be attained. He treats a bodhisattva's ability to transform earth into gold or jewels through the power of concentrated resolve alone as living proof of the same underlying point: matter has no fixed, resistant own-nature the way ordinary experience assumes, or such transformation could never occur regardless of how accomplished the practitioner's concentration. The section closes exactly where Part 3's dream-simile argument closed: once it is seen that no object at all appears when non-discriminating wisdom is genuinely operating, it follows that there was never truly a separate object to begin with — and, since a perceiving consciousness is only ever defined in relation to some object, no separate perceiving consciousness either.

10. Prajñāpāramitā Is Non-Discriminating Wisdom; Five Things a Bodhisattva Leaves Behind

Chinese Source

般若波羅蜜多與無分別智，無有差別，如說：菩薩安住般若波羅蜜多非處相應，能於所餘波羅蜜多修習圓滿。云何名為非處相應修習圓滿？謂由遠離五種處故：一、遠離外道我執處故，二、遠離未見真如菩薩分別處故，三、遠離生死涅槃二邊處故，四、遠離唯斷煩惱障生喜足處故，五、遠離不顧有情利益安樂住無餘依涅槃界處故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The perfection of wisdom and non-discriminating wisdom are without any difference, as it is said: a bodhisattva, abiding in the perfection of wisdom without being fixed to any particular ground, is thereby able to bring the remaining perfections to full, complete cultivation. What is meant by "cultivation to completion without being fixed to any particular ground"? It means being free of five particular grounds: first, free of the ground of self-grasping held by non-Buddhist teachers; second, free of the ground of discrimination held by a bodhisattva who has not yet seen suchness directly; third, free of the twin extremes of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa as fixed grounds; fourth, free of the ground of easy satisfaction that comes from merely cutting off the afflictive obstruction alone; fifth, free of the ground of dwelling in the nirvāṇa without remainder, heedless of sentient beings' own benefit and happiness.

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu treats each of the five as a specific trap a bodhisattva's non-discriminating wisdom must avoid where a less complete practice might fall in. The first is the naive error of a non-Buddhist practitioner who, even while meditating on wisdom itself, still clings to a self doing the meditating. The second is a subtler trap for bodhisattvas who have not yet directly seen suchness: even correctly discriminating "this is the perfection of wisdom" is itself a form of discrimination that must eventually be released. The third rules out settling into either saṃsāra, as an ordinary being does, or nirvāṇa alone, as a śrāvaka does — a bodhisattva's non-discriminating wisdom holds neither as a final resting ground. The fourth rules out the same "easy satisfaction" flagged in section 8 above, where stopping at the removal of the afflictive obstruction alone, without also addressing the cognitive obstruction, is mistaken for completion. And the fifth rules out a śrāvaka's own final destination — entering nirvāṇa without remainder and thereby abandoning further concern for other beings — which a bodhisattva's wisdom, precisely because it does not fix itself anywhere, never does either.

11. Five Differences Between a Śrāvaka's Wisdom and a Bodhisattva's

Chinese Source

聲聞等智與菩薩智有何差別？由五種相應知差別：一、由無分別差別，謂於蘊等法無分別故。二、由非少分差別，謂於通達真如，入一切種所知境界，普為度脫一切有情，非少分故。三、由無住差別，謂無住涅槃為所住故。四、由畢竟差別，謂無餘依涅槃界中無斷盡故。五、由無上差別，謂於此上無有餘乘勝過此故。

Ru-Yi Translation

What difference is there between a śrāvaka's wisdom and a bodhisattva's? Five marks distinguish them. First, the difference of non-discrimination: with regard to the aggregates and the rest, a bodhisattva's wisdom does not discriminate them at all. Second, the difference of not being merely partial: in penetrating suchness, entering every kind of knowable domain, and seeking to liberate all sentient beings without exception, a bodhisattva's wisdom is not merely partial. Third, the difference of non-abiding: it takes non-abiding nirvāṇa as what it abides in. Fourth, the difference of finality: within the nirvāṇa without remainder, it does not come to an end. Fifth, the difference of being unsurpassed: above it, there is no further vehicle that could surpass it.

此中有頌：

「諸大悲為體，　由五相勝智，

世出世滿中，　說此最高遠。」

Here there is a verse:

"Great compassion as its own substance, this wisdom excels through these five marks;

among the fullness of both the worldly and the transcendent, it is said to be the highest and farthest-reaching of all."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu unpacks "not merely partial" into three separate senses at once: a bodhisattva's wisdom penetrates the selflessness of both persons and dharmas, where a śrāvaka's penetrates only the selflessness of persons; it takes every possible knowable object as its domain, where a śrāvaka's is limited to the four noble truths; and it seeks the liberation of literally every sentient being, where a śrāvaka's path seeks only the practitioner's own release. "Finality" distinguishes what actually happens at the very end of each path: a śrāvaka's nirvāṇa without remainder is a genuine, complete ending, with nothing further to accomplish, whereas a bodhisattva's non-abiding nirvāṇa never comes to such a stop — its qualities continue without exhaustion. And "unsurpassed" simply notes that above the Śrāvaka Vehicle stands the Great Vehicle, while above the Great Vehicle stands nothing further at all. The closing verse's identification of "great compassion" as this wisdom's own substance is worth pausing on: for all its technical precision, the five differences ultimately trace back to one thing — a bodhisattva's wisdom is shaped, at every point, by compassion for every being without exception, which a śrāvaka's narrower aim simply does not require.

12. Why Bodhisattvas with Mastery over Wealth Still Let Some Beings Remain Poor

Part Nine closes with a question that might trouble a thoughtful reader: if a bodhisattva's discipline, concentration, and wisdom are genuinely perfected, giving complete mastery over material wealth, why are there still beings who lack it?

Chinese Source

若諸菩薩成就如是增上尸羅，增上質多，增上般若功德圓滿，於諸財位得大自在，何故現見有諸有情匱乏財位？見彼有情於諸財位有重業障故；見彼有情若施財位障生善法故；見彼有情若乏財位厭離現前故；見彼有情若施財位即為積集不善法因故；見彼有情若施財位即便作餘無量有情損惱因故：是故現見有諸有情匱乏財位。

Ru-Yi Translation

If bodhisattvas, having accomplished the excellent qualities of the higher trainings in morality, mind, and wisdom fully perfected, thereby attain great mastery over wealth and position, why is it still actually seen that some sentient beings lack wealth and position? It is because a bodhisattva sees that such beings carry a heavy karmic obstruction with regard to wealth and position; sees that, were wealth and position given, this would obstruct the arising of their wholesome dharmas; sees that, lacking wealth and position, a spirit of renunciation is already present in them; sees that, were wealth and position given, this would become the cause of accumulating unwholesome dharmas in them; and sees that, were wealth and position given, this would become the cause of harming countless other beings. This is why it is still actually seen that some sentient beings lack wealth and position.

此中有頌：

「見業、障、現前、　積集、損惱故，

現有諸有情，　不感菩薩施。」

Here there is a verse:

"Seeing karma, obstruction, present renunciation, accumulation, and harm to others —

for these reasons it is still seen that some sentient beings do not draw forth a bodhisattva's gift."

Reading note — after Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu offers a striking image for the first reason: a river flowing with clean water that anyone could freely drink, and yet certain hungry ghosts, due to their own karmic obstruction, remain unable to drink it no matter how close they stand — the river itself withholds nothing, but their own condition prevents them from receiving it. The bodhisattva, in this simile, is like the river: mastery over wealth is fully present and freely available, and the limitation lies entirely on the receiving side, in causes and conditions the bodhisattva's wisdom can clearly see but a simple act of giving cannot simply override. The remaining four reasons each describe a bodhisattva choosing, out of clear-sighted compassion rather than any lack of generosity, not to give: because wealth would concretely block a specific being's spiritual progress, because their present poverty is already quietly cultivating a valuable renunciation that riches would destroy, because wealth would become the very occasion for that being to commit further unwholesome acts, or because their sudden wealth would become the specific cause of real harm to countless other beings. In every case, the deciding factor is what a bodhisattva's wisdom sees about actual consequences, not any limit on the bodhisattva's own capacity or willingness to give.

Part Nine Complete

This closes Part Nine of Ru-Yi's translation: non-discriminating wisdom examined through its self-nature, support, causes, object, aspect, fruits, emergence across the ten grounds, and a long sequence of similes distinguishing its preparatory, fundamental, and subsequently-attained forms — followed by proofs of its necessity, its identity with the perfection of wisdom, its five-fold superiority over a śrāvaka's wisdom, and the compassionate reasoning behind a bodhisattva's apparent restraint in giving.
