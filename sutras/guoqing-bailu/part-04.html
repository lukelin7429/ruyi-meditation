The Empty Shrine, Two Steles, Letters of Invitation, and the Pond for Releasing Life

This last part covers items 91 to 104 and the material added to the Song edition. It opens with Emperor Yang's oral edict, given after his envoy found Zhiyi's shrine empty: the emperor reads the vanished body as proof that his precept master attained the path, and has the account of conduct copied in every prefecture. Next come two stele inscriptions in ornate parallel prose with closing eulogies. Liu Guyan's stele for Guoqing Monastery retells Zhiyi's life, the conferral of the bodhisattva precepts in 591, Dingguang's prophecy of the name Guoqing, Zhiyi's death at Shicheng, and the "thousand and one" monks at the fast of 605. Huangfu Pi's stele for Yuquan Monastery describes its founding in Jingzhou. The book then goes back to earlier letters of 593–597: greetings from Xiao Cong of the Later Liang, Cai Zheng, and Tanxian; invitations to lecture from Jingzhou, from Qixia Monastery, and from Jizang; and Liu Guyan's report on reading the Profound Meaning. The final item is Zhiyi's letter to the garrison general of Linhai defending the pond for releasing life against local officials. A chronology of Zhiyi's life and two Song postfaces (1185) on the reprinting close the book.

The Empty Shrine and the Gift of Banners

Chinese Source

勅報百司上表賀口勅第九十一

Ru-Yi Translation

Oral Edict Replying to the Memorial of Congratulation from the Hundred Offices, Number 91

Chinese Source

使人盧政力還奏。開先師龕墳，不見舍利。又上行狀一卷，百司並賀。勅語諸公云：智者是我菩薩戒師，先多靈異，恒語我云：必若得道。得道之後，擁護國土利益蒼生，至於涅槃。坐於石室，容範不變。我以仁壽元年遣張乾威往看，儼然如舊。昨更令盧政力往，親開龕門閉塞無有。聞迹遂不見，靈體既已變化，得道非虛，擁護利益之言必應無爽。今有行狀一卷，諸公等共觀之，諸州考使各寫一通，還所部流布。

Ru-Yi Translation

The envoy Lu Zhengli returned and memorialized: when the shrine-tomb of our late master was opened, the śarīra was not seen. He also presented an account of conduct in one fascicle, and the hundred offices all offered congratulations. The edict said to the lords: "Zhizhe was our bodhisattva-precept master. Before, there were many numinous wonders, and he constantly said to us, 'I will surely attain the path. After attaining the path, I will guard the land and benefit the common people, up to nirvāṇa.' He sat in a stone chamber, his countenance unchanged. In the first year of Renshou [601] we sent Zhang Qianwei to go and look, and he was solemnly as before. Recently we again ordered Lu Zhengli to go; he personally opened the door of the shrine, which had been closed and sealed, and there was nothing [inside]. As we hear of these traces, [the body] is no longer seen; since the numinous body has already transformed, his attainment of the path was not empty, and his words about guarding and benefiting will surely not fail. Now there is an account of conduct in one fascicle; you lords should all look at it together, and the inspection envoys of the various prefectures should each copy one and take it back to circulate in the areas under them."

The Guoqing Stele: Preface on the Sui Emperor

Ru-Yi Translation

The Stele Inscription for Guoqing Monastery Made by Edict, Number 93

Chinese Source

天台國清寺智者禪師碑文

Ru-Yi Translation

Stele Inscription for Meditation Master Zhizhe of Guoqing Monastery on Tiantai

Chinese Source

隋兼祕書監直內史省開府儀同三司臣柳顧言奉勅撰

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by edict by your servant Liu Guyan of the Sui, concurrent Director of the Palace Library, on duty in the Palace Secretariat, Kaifu, Equal in Ceremony with the Three Ministers

Chinese Source

臣聞：在天成象，經諱之法存焉；在地成形，區方之均倣矣。二儀既爾，三才冏然。上聖纘極，明王所以敷教；先覺授道，契會方乃昇仙。是故命駕崆峒紆光善卷，籙圖宣業赤誦弘風，練質九府之間，騰虛六合之內，斯並權宜汲引暫保逍遙，終覆蔽於苦空，卒邅迴於生死，未臻夫不生不滅、無去無來、匪實匪虛、非如非異，常樂我淨凝寂恬愉，不可思議之解脫也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Your servant has heard: in heaven [things] form images, and there the laws of the warp and of the taboos are preserved; on earth [things] take shape, and the even ordering of regions and directions is modeled on them. The two modes being so, the three powers are bright. The highest sages continue the ultimate, and this is how enlightened kings spread their teaching; the first awakened transmit the way, and only those who accord with it ascend as immortals. Therefore [the Yellow Emperor] ordered his carriage to Kongtong, and [Shun] bent his radiance to Shan Juan; the registers and charts proclaimed their work, and Chisong spread his influence; they refined their substance among the nine palaces and soared through the void within the six directions. These were all expedient means of drawing [people] in, preserving for a while a carefree roaming; but in the end they were covered over by suffering and emptiness and finally went round and round in birth and death. They did not reach the inconceivable liberation that is neither arising nor ceasing, without going and without coming, neither real nor void, neither thus nor different, permanent, blissful, self, and pure, congealed in stillness and tranquil joy.

Chinese Source

粵若我大隋皇帝法諱總持，載融佛日瑞發淨宮，利見法王應閻浮主。以封唐入紹業，繼高辛立聖，與能祚隆姬發，自天攸縱，包大德而翼小心；希世膺期，內文明而外柔順。知微知彰鑑窮玄覽，迺武迺文能事斯畢。自永嘉失馭海內分崩，恃險擅強各樹君長，禮樂淪於非所、龜玉毀於殊方，書軌競分殆三十紀，天將悔禍稔惡有辰。皇上道藹汾陽迹光代邸，地隆分狹神功潛著。于時高廟靈謨深思統壹專征仗鉞，帝曰斯哉惟君惟親知臣知子。乃揚威萬里、問罪九伐，一舉而定江左，再駕而潛餘燼，浮天爭貢海外有截。雖咎卨之備五臣、周旦之居十亂，本支盛績疇昔多慚，盤石究勳莫不倣是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, our Emperor of the great Sui, whose Dharma-name is Zongchi, carries the harmony of the Buddha-sun; his auspicious [birth] came forth from a pure palace; [as one whom] "it is beneficial to see," he is a Dharma-king responding as lord of Jambudvīpa. Enfeoffed in Tang, he entered and continued the work; succeeding Gaoxin, he was established as a sage; as one chosen for ability, his blessings are as great as [those of] Ji Fa. Heaven itself allowed [him to be a sage]; he embraces great virtue while remaining careful and reverent. Rare in the world, he answered the appointed time, inwardly cultured and bright and outwardly gentle and compliant. Knowing the subtle and the manifest, his mirror exhausts the profound view; martial and cultured, he has completed all he is able to do. Since the Yongjia [era] lost the reins, [the land] within the seas split apart; relying on strongholds and seizing power, each set up its own ruler; rites and music sank into improper places, and the tortoise and the jade were destroyed in separate regions; writing and axles were divided in rivalry for nearly thirty eras. Heaven was about to repent of the calamity, and the ripening of evil had its time. His Majesty's way was luxuriant [as Yao's] at Fenyang, and his traces shone [as Emperor Wen of the Han's] in the residence of Dai; his rank was high and his share narrow, but his divine accomplishment was secretly evident. At that time the numinous counsel of the High Temple thought deeply of unifying [the realm], and [he received] sole command of the campaign, grasping the axe. The emperor said, "This one! As ruler and as parent, I know my servant and I know my son." Then he displayed his might over ten thousand li and called [the guilty] to account with the nine punitive measures; with one move he settled Jiangzuo, and with a second expedition he quenched the remaining embers; [those beyond] the sky-floating [seas] vied to bring tribute, and beyond the seas order was established. Even Gao[yao] and Xie, who completed the five ministers, and Dan of Zhou, who stood among the ten able ministers, have much to be ashamed of beside the abundant achievements of [this] root and branch [of the ruling house] in former times; all who as the bedrock [of the dynasty] completed great merit could only model themselves on this.

Chinese Source

三能宗鉉九命惟揚，本之以仁慈、施之以聲教，行之以要道、體之以無為，姑射杳然尚想淵賾滄波壯矣。思濟舟航以為能仁，種覺降茲忍土，信相入道淨戒居先，梵網明文深傳薩埵，國師僧寶必兼禪慧。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three steps honor him as the ring of the tripod, and the ninefold appointment is proclaimed. He roots [his rule] in humaneness and kindness, spreads it by fame and teaching, practices it by the essential way, and embodies it in non-action. Gushe is distant, yet he still thinks of its profound mysteries — vast indeed are the blue waves! He thought of boats and ferries for crossing over: he held that the Capable and Humane One, of all-mode awakening, descended to this land of endurance; that for faith to enter the path, pure precepts come first; that the clear text of the Brahmā's Net is deeply transmitted to the sattvas; and that a teacher of the state from the Jewel of the Saṅgha must combine dhyāna and wisdom.

Chinese Source

有會稽天台山大禪師者，生而神光照室，兩目重瞳。禪師法諱智顗，頴川陳族太丘道遠季伯風延，典午衰亂播遷華容。父起祖梁使持節散騎常侍，封益陽縣開國侯。禪師風彩超悟，齠齓希有，年過少學便誦《法華》。父為求婚，方便祈止。儒門史館多所著明，柱下濠上彌所深得，渚宮覆沒便即蔬菲。及啟弱冠，於長沙之果願寺出家，護戒如明珠、安心若止水。歷聽經論，但使一聞，得之於心傳之于口。以湘潭局狹未發大機，拂跡衡陽安步墳汝，往大蘇山請業慧思禪師。

Ru-Yi Translation

There was a great meditation master of Mount Tiantai in Kuaiji. At his birth divine light lit up the room, and both his eyes had double pupils. The meditation master's Dharma-name was Zhiyi. [He was of] the Chen clan of Yingchuan: the way of [Chen Shi of] Taiqiu reached far, and the influence of Ji and Bo extended [to him]. When the Jin declined into disorder, [the family] migrated to Huarong. His father, Qizu, was Commissioner Bearing the Credentials and Cavalier Attendant-in-Ordinary of the Liang, enfeoffed as Founding Marquis of Yiyang county. The meditation master's bearing was outstanding in understanding; as a child with milk teeth he was already rare; just past the age of elementary study, he recited the Lotus. His father sought a marriage for him; he used an expedient to beg that it stop. In Confucian learning and the histories he made much clear; of [Laozi] below the pillar and [Zhuangzi] above the Hao he grasped all the more deeply. When Zhugong fell, he at once took to vegetables and coarse food. Coming to the age of capping, he left home at Guoyuan Monastery in Changsha, guarding the precepts like a bright pearl and settling his mind like still water. He listened to sūtras and treatises in turn; having heard them only once, he grasped them in his mind and passed them on by mouth. Because Xiangtan was cramped and narrow and his great capacity had not yet been brought out, he brushed his traces from Hengyang and walked at ease to the Fen and Ru, and went to Mount Dasu to ask for instruction from Meditation Master Huisi.

禪師見便歎曰：憶昔靈鷲同聽《法華》，令進我普賢道場，為說四安樂行。停二七日誦〈藥王品〉，至諸佛同讚之句，寂然入定明慧便發。起而白師。師曰：非爾弗感，非我莫識。所入定者，法華三昧之前方便，及聞持陀羅尼也。縱令文字之師千群億品，尋汝慧辯所不能窮，於說法人中最為第一。嘗約《仁王》、《纓絡》、龍樹、馬鳴，立三觀四教，述師本宗以為大乘樞鍵。得下御內碩學流通。

Ru-Yi Translation

The meditation master saw him and sighed: "I remember long ago on Vulture Peak we listened to the Lotus together." He had him enter his Samantabhadra place of practice and explained to him the four peaceful practices. After staying fourteen days reciting the "Medicine King" chapter, on reaching the line where the buddhas together praise, he entered concentration in stillness, and bright wisdom arose. He rose and told his teacher. The teacher said, "If not you, none could have felt it; if not I, none could have recognized it. The concentration you entered is the preliminary expedient of the Lotus samādhi, together with the dhāraṇī of hearing and retaining. Even if masters of letters came in thousands of crowds and myriads of kinds, searching your wise eloquence they could not exhaust it; among the expounders of the Dharma you are the foremost." He once, on the basis of the Humane Kings, the Necklace [Sūtra], Nāgārjuna, and Aśvaghoṣa, established the three contemplations and the four teachings, and expounded his teacher's root tradition as the pivot and key of the Great Vehicle. It was received by the eminent scholars below and within the court and circulated.

Chinese Source

智以藏往，徵育王之建正教；神以知來，鑑周武之滅大法。乃高蹈豫土翔集天台，歷遊名山言造廬嶽。秦孝王作鎮淮海，遣信迎屈，對使者曰：雖欲相見，終恐緣差。即累旬大風妖賊競起，水陸俱阻，安坐匡岫。既而龍斿龕難，仍代孝王爰屆邦域，潔誠延請順流背風，數日遄到亦既覯止，便定師資。

Ru-Yi Translation

His wisdom stores the past: he cited King Aśoka's establishing of the true teaching; his spirit knows the future: he foresaw Emperor Wu of the Zhou's destruction of the great Dharma. So he stepped high from the land of Yu and alighted on Tiantai; he traveled through famous mountains and came to Mount Lu. Prince Xiao of Qin, in command at Huaihai, sent a messenger to invite him. He said to the envoy, "Though I wish to meet him, I fear in the end our conditions will miss." Then for several ten-day periods great winds blew and rebels rose everywhere; water and land were both blocked, and he sat at peace on Mount Kuang. Soon after, the dragon banners suppressed the troubles, and [our prince] took Prince Xiao's place and came to that region. With pure sincerity he invited [the master], who, with the current [behind him] and the wind at his back, arrived swiftly in a few days; once they had met, they settled [the relation of] teacher and disciple.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Like Good Intent looking up to Wondrous Light, like Gaozong obtaining Fu Yue — [the master] firmly declined again and again, but there was no one to yield to. On the twenty-third day, xinchou, of the month whose pitch is Huangzhong, in the eleventh year of Kaihuang, the year being xinhai [591], at Dating Monastery in Yangzhou he held a great unobstructed fast and reverently received the bodhisattva precepts, lowering the dignity of a governor to follow the rules [due to] an honored teacher. He took off the dragon robe and put on the garment of forbearance; he set aside the huan jade tablet and passed on the incense of the precepts; he aroused the initial mind in full and paid homage to the buddhas. At that time heaven and earth were in harmonious union, and sun and moon were doubly bright; in the courtyard a gentle wind circled, and the sky was clean with auspicious air; the groves shone with the flowers of the seven [factors of] awakening, and the ponds were clear with the water of the eight purities. His transformation extended within and without, the matter equal to Aśoka's city; the teaching turned the Dharma wheel, its principle matching the cave of the hidden treasure. The civil and military officials were all shaded by the cloud of kindness — joyful, dignified, solemn, harmonious. What the sūtra calls the precepts embracing the rules of conduct, the precepts embracing good dharmas, and the precepts embracing living beings, bringing forth the three vows and the true and correct ten acceptances, "like all forms entering the realm of space" — is this not what it means?

Chinese Source

法事云畢，七珍備捨，出居于城外禪眾之精林，四事供養，睿情猶疑未滿，以為師氏禮極必有嘉名，如伊尹之曰阿衡、呂望之稱尚父。檢地持經智者師目，謹依金口虔表玉裕，便克良辰躬出頂禮，雖有熊之登具茨、漢文之適河上，方之蔑如也。智者以內行外獎諸佛之深訓，實慧方便大士之兼通，帝釋檀那既包信解，仁王攝受遠能博益，逡巡告退言歸舊山，殷勤請留重違高意，猶四皓之餌朮南山、二疎之散金東海，振錫離塵始稱出世，睠言儒者未足為榮。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the Dharma ceremony was over, [the prince] gave up the seven treasures in full, and [the master] went out to live in the pure grove of the dhyāna community outside the city. [The prince] provided the four requisites, but his sagely feelings still worried that this was not enough; he held that the rites toward a teacher, at their utmost, must include a fine name, as Yi Yin was called Aheng and Lü Wang was called Shangfu. Examining the Dichi Sūtra, [he found] the designation of the teacher as Zhizhe ("the Wise One"); respectfully following the golden mouth, he reverently set it forth in jade-like abundance. Then he fixed an auspicious day and went out in person to bow his head to the ground. Even [the Yellow Emperor of] Youxiong climbing Juci and Emperor Wen of the Han going to the Heshang [Gong], compared to this, were as nothing. Zhizhe [held that] practicing within and encouraging without is the deep instruction of the buddhas; that real wisdom and expedients are what great beings master together; that [the prince], like Śakra the donor, already embraced faith and understanding; and that a humane king's acceptance and protection could benefit widely and far. So he hesitated and announced his withdrawal, saying he would return to his old mountain. [The prince] earnestly asked him to stay but was reluctant to go against his lofty intention — like the four white-haired [elders] eating atractylis on the Southern Mountain, like the two Shus scattering gold by the Eastern Sea. Shaking the staff and leaving the dust is only then called transcending the world; looking back at the Confucians, [their retirements] are not enough for glory.

Chinese Source

竊以四明天台剡東玉岫。兩謠雲𡵙鄞南之金庭，峻極于天仰捫白日、盤鎮于地俯鏡滄海，雲霞沆瀣霏拂輕襟，虹霓鴛鸞勝承飛舄，華果競發常迷四時，藥草森羅孰分億品。道猷往而證果，興公賦不能申，寺基本屬始豐，峯名佛隴。元有定光禪師，隱顯變現先居此峯。常謂弟子云：不久當有勝善知識，將領徒眾俱集此山。俄而智者越江渡湖，翻然來萃，忽聞鍾磬聲振山谷，即問光師聲之所以。答云：此是犍槌集僧得住之相。頗憶曾經舉手相引時不？智者即悟年至十五，稽顙禮佛恍焉如夢，見極高山臨大海曲，有僧如今光師，舉手接上云：汝當居此處，汝當終此焉。拜言悲喜，滂沱涕泗。

Ru-Yi Translation

I humbly consider that Siming and Tiantai are the jade peaks east of Shan. Both are sung of — the cloud-ridges and the Golden Court south of Yin — steep, reaching to heaven, they touch the white sun above; coiled and settled upon the earth, they look down on the blue sea as a mirror. Clouds, mists, and night dew drift and brush light lapels; rainbows and phoenixes bear up flying slippers; flowers and fruits vie to burst forth, always confusing the four seasons; medicinal herbs are arrayed in profusion — who can distinguish their myriad kinds? Daoyou went there and realized the fruit; Duke Xing's rhapsody could not express it fully. The monastery site originally belonged to Shifeng, and the peak is named Folong. Originally there was Meditation Master Dingguang, hidden and manifest, transforming and appearing, who had lived on this peak first. He often said to his disciples, "Before long there will be an excellent good friend who will lead a following and gather together on this mountain." Soon Zhizhe crossed the river and the lakes and suddenly arrived. All at once he heard the sound of bells and chimes shaking the hills and valleys, and asked Master [Ding]guang the reason for the sound. He answered, "This is the gavel assembling the monks — a sign that you will be able to stay. Do you remember the time when I raised my hand and drew you up?" Zhizhe then realized: at fifteen, bowing his forehead to the ground before the Buddha, as if in a dream, he had seen an extremely high mountain overlooking a bend of the great sea, and a monk like the present Master [Ding]guang raised his hand and took him up, saying, "You will dwell here; you will end [your life] here." He bowed and spoke with sorrow and joy, his tears streaming.

Chinese Source

仍於佛隴之南尋行寺域，便見五峯圍遶等耆闍之山，兩溪夾瀉若縉雲之㵎。披榛開途葺用茆茨，功德叢林常熏薝蔔，忍辱牆院無勞關楗，猨嘯蚿吟泉籟響雜，飛走聽法馴伏軒墀。西南有永豐江派與淛源分嶺東會于海，潮波往還數百里間，村人常以漁捕為業。陶漸迴向焚罛廢梁，墾田種果翻成富實，深信解者多作沙門，慈化所覃皆此連類。

Ru-Yi Translation

Then one can know that our ancestor's way is great and his name weighty, and that the traces of his transforming emperors and ministers are, from beginning to end, as clear as if seen. Seeing it with my own eyes, I have with utmost courtesy and reverence read it from beginning to end, and say thus. On the day the retreat began, in the twelfth year of Chunxi of the sacred Song, the year-star being at yisi [1185]. Respectfully inscribed by Jieying, abbot of Bailian.

Reading Notes for Part 4

Translator's notes

Differences from the Separate Biography — Translated as written here; compare `zhizhe-biezhuan/p1_body.md`. The Guoqing stele (unit 7) has Zhiyi leave home at "Guoyuan Monastery in Changsha"; the biography and the chronology (unit 57) say Guoyuan Monastery in Xiangzhou (Changsha was its seat). In the stele the prophecy of the name Guoqing is spoken by a monk who appears after Dingguang's death; Part 3 (unit 62) has Zhiyi dream of Dingguang's spirit. The biography counts sixty-three fisheries given up; Zhiyi's letter (unit 54) says fifty-five places (in the biography the sponsor is 計詡 Ji Xu, here 計尚兒 Ji Shang'er, as in Part 2). The stele dates the precepts to the twenty-third day of the eleventh month of 591; the chronology gives only "the eleventh month."

Dates in the chronology (unit 57) — Several are off. 天禧 "Tianxi" is not a Chen reign; Emperor Wen's era was 天嘉 Tianjia (first year 560), and no Western year is added. 貞明 is for 禎明 Zhenming (587). The ages are counted from 538 as "one year old," so Zhide 2 (584) should be forty-seven, not forty-eight. 少帝 is rendered "the Last Ruler of the Chen"; it means the Last Ruler of the Chen of Parts 1–2. Unit 3 gives "the third year" with no reign name; it probably means Daye 3 [607].

The Lotus and the five grades (unit 7) — Huisi's words follow the biography: "the preliminary expedient of the Lotus samādhi." Where the biography has "the first, the revolving dhāraṇī," the stele has 聞持陀羅尼 "the dhāraṇī of hearing and retaining." "Like Good Intent looking up to Wondrous Light" refers to the Lotus, chapter 1: Good Intent (善意) was one of the eight princes who took the bodhisattva Wondrous Light as their teacher (site's Lotus: "Wondrous Light").

The precepts and the name Zhizhe (unit 7) — The three kinds of precepts (攝律儀戒、攝善法戒、攝眾生戒) appear in the Necklace Sūtra (T1485) and other works. "Like all forms entering the realm of space" with "the three vows" and "the ten acceptances" is from the Śrīmālā Sūtra (T353, chapter on the three vows; checked in the vault). The emperor says he took the title Zhizhe from the 地持經 (Bodhisattvabhūmi, T1581); a quick search found no list of epithets containing 智者 in T1581, so the source is not located. In the biography the prince simply gives the name.

Allusions, Guoqing stele — Unit 7: the Yellow Emperor at Kongtong; Shan Juan, to whom Shun offered the throne; Chisong (赤誦, usually 赤松); Chen Shi of Taiqiu and his sons; 典午 = the Sima house of the Jin; Yao at Fenyang; Emperor Wen of the Han called from Dai; Gaoyao and Xie, the Duke of Zhou's "ten able ministers"; Yi Yin "Aheng" and Lü Wang "Shangfu"; the four white-haired elders of Mount Shang; Shu Guang and Shu Shou; Daoyou and Duke Xing (Sun Chuo), as in Part 3. "King Golden Dragon Honored" praising the buddhas is from the Golden Light Sūtra, chapter 4 (as in `jinguangming-jing/p1_body.md`); Ratnākara offering canopies is the Vimalakīrti, chapter 1 (site's Vimalakīrti: Ratnākara). 散華／貫華 "scattered flowers and strung flowers" are prose and verse.

Allusions, Yuquan stele and letters — Unit 22: the dream of Emperor Ming of the Han; Mātaṅga and Kumārajīva; the persecutions of the Zhenjun era (Northern Wei, 446) and Jiande era (Northern Zhou, 574); Revata (離越) as a forest dweller. The monkey that built a stūpa and the goose that carried flowers to Amitābha's land were not traced. "The tall carriage [out of] the burning house" and "the destitute son" (unit 42) follow the site's Lotus. Unit 40: "the deer and the goat" are the Lotus carts; "the hare and the horse" are two of the three animals crossing a river (hare, horse, elephant) in the Mahāvibhāṣā (T1545) and Tiantai works such as the Sijiao yi (T1929). "A single phrase perfumes the spirit" is also quoted in the Zongjing lu (T2016); earlier source not located. "Hearing [the way] in the morning…" is Analects 4.8. Unit 54: the rain of flowers repaying the elder is the Jalavāhana chapter of the Golden Light Sūtra; the Marquis of Sui's pearl; Liu Chong's single coin; Shi Miao leaving his calf; Kuai Shen's crane; Kong Yu's tortoise; the "three wonders" of Lu Gong and the "five trousers" song for Lian Fan. "The teaching of the nine words" (九言之教) was not traced.

Uncertain readings — Unit 7: 經諱 is probably for 經緯 "warp and weft"; it is rendered literally as "the laws of the warp and of the taboos." Also uncertain are 得下御內碩學流通 ("received by the eminent scholars below and within the court"), 寶冥之窟 "the cave of the hidden treasure," 癰癰 (probably for 雝雝 "harmonious"), 虔表玉裕 "set it forth in jade-like abundance," and 兩謠 "both are sung of." Unit 22: 外子 is "the emperor's son outside [the capital]," and the sentence 居朋之友…處少之徒 is uncertain. Unit 36: 擊壞 is read as 擊壤 "beating the ground." Unit 44: 傾䗍 is read as the mole of Zhuangzi that drinks from the river only to fill its belly. Unit 42: 不惻 is read as 不測 "unfathomable," and the sentence on the Han emperor and the Great Wind is uncertain. Unit 52: 同壽 is taken as a name (Tongshou); 童英 "the young hero" may mean Maitreya or Mañjuśrī at the opening of the Lotus.

Jizang's letters (units 48–50) — CBETA punctuates 吉藏啟景。上至…, but unit 50 has 景上未至 and 景上尋歸, so 景上 is read as the name of a messenger, "Jingshang." In unit 52 the closing lines pun on 聲聞 (hearing the voice of autumn / śrāvaka) and 緣覺 (awakened by conditions, such as falling leaves / pratyekabuddha).

Names and titles — 曇暹 Tanxian; 惠嵓 Huiyan; 臻法師 Dharma Master Zhen; 陳子秀 Chen Zixiu; 保恭 Baogong; 明僧紹 Ming Sengshao; 吉藏 Jizang; 智照 Zhizhao; 蕭通國 Xiao Tongguo; 解拔國 Xie Baguo; 孔玄達 Kong Xuanda; 羊公賀 Yang Gonghe; 王宣武 Wang Xuanwu; 曇揵 Tanjian; 皇甫毘 Huangfu Pi; 淨梵 Jingfan; 戒應 Jieying; 曇岑 Tancen. 蔡徵 Cai Zheng may be the "Your servant Zheng" of Part 1. In unit 46 Liu Guyan signs 柳正善具成就; this is read as a precept name, "Liu Zhengshan Jucheng," which is uncertain. 臨海內史 is "Administrator of Linhai," as in Part 2. 明府 is "Your Excellency."

Cross-references — The quotation of Guanding's preface in unit 59 uses the English of Part 1 (unit 2). 五時八教 and 三觀十乘 in unit 60 follow the glossary: the Five Periods and Eight Teachings; "the ten modes [of contemplation]." 《次第禪門》 is the Gradual Gates of Dhyāna, as in the Separate Biography.
