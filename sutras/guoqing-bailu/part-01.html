Prefaces, Community Rules, Rites, and the First Letters of the Chen Court

The Hundred Records of Guoqing (T1934) is a dossier of documents about Zhiyi (538–597) gathered by his disciple Guanding (561–632). Guanding's preface explains that the monk Zhiji began collecting the letters and edicts sent to Zhiyi but died before finishing, and that Guanding completed the work, adding Zhiyi's ritual methods, to make one hundred items named after Guoqing Monastery on Mount Tiantai. A second preface, by the Song monk Youyan, records the first printing in 1098. This part covers the two prefaces and items 1 to 18. Items 1 to 7 are Zhiyi's own writings for his community: ten rules for monks living together, the daily round of reverent bowing based on Nāgārjuna's Vibhāṣā, the universal bowing to Rocana, the outlines of the Avalokiteśvara, Golden Light, and Vaipulya repentance rites, and a talk to the monastery officers about not misusing community property. Items 8 to 18 are edicts and letters from the Chen court, from Emperor Xuan, the Last Ruler, his empress and crown prince, the Prince of Yongyang, and Shen Junli, which invite Zhiyi to the capital, grant him support, and ask him for the precepts. Many of the same events appear in Guanding's Separate Biography.

Ru-Yi Translation

Preface to the Hundred Records of Guoqing

Chinese Source

隋沙門灌頂撰

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by the Śramaṇa Guanding of the Sui

Chinese Source

先師以陳太建七年歲次乙未初隱天台，所止之峰舊名佛隴，詢訪土人云：「遊其山者多見佛像，故相傳因而成稱。」至太建十年歲在戊戌，降陳宣帝勅名修禪寺，吏部尚書毛喜題篆牓送安寺門。到太隋開皇十八年其歲戊午，太尉晉王於山下為先師創寺，因山為稱是曰天台。

Ru-Yi Translation

Our late master first went into seclusion on Tiantai in the seventh year of Taijian of the Chen, the year being yiwei [575]. The peak where he stayed was formerly named Folong ("Buddha Ridge"); when he asked the local people, they said, "Many of those who travel on this mountain see images of the Buddha; therefore it has been handed down and the name has come about." In the tenth year of Taijian, the year being wuxu [578], Emperor Xuan of the Chen sent down an edict naming it Xiuchan ("Cultivating Dhyāna") Monastery, and the Minister of Personnel Mao Xi wrote the name plaque in seal script and sent it to be set up over the monastery gate. In the eighteenth year of Kaihuang of the great Sui, the year being wuwu [598], the Grand Defender, the Prince of Jin, built a monastery for our late master at the foot of the mountain; it was called after the mountain, namely Tiantai.

Chinese Source

王登尊極，以大業元年龍集乙丑，勅江陽名僧云：「昔為智者創寺，權因山稱。今須立名，經論之內有何勝目，可各述所懷，朕自詳擇。」諸僧表兩名，一云禪門、一云五淨居。其表未奏，而僧使智璪啟國清之瑞，勅云「此是我先師之靈瑞。即用即用。」勅取江都宮大牙殿牓，填以雌黃，書以大篆，遣兼內史通事舍人盧政力送安寺門。國清之稱從而為始。先師神光而生、結跏而滅。處證妙法，出作帝師，備是渚宮法論、會稽智果、國清灌頂等三傳所載。又沙門智寂，編集先師遣迎信命，搜訪未周而智寂身故，筆墨之功與氣俱棄。余覽其草本，續更撰次諸經方法等，合得一百條，呼為國清百錄，貽示後昆，知盛德之在茲。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the prince ascended to the most honored [throne], in the first year of Daye, the year-star being at yichou [605], he issued an edict to the famous monks of Jiangyang: "Formerly, when we built a monastery for Zhizhe, we provisionally called it after the mountain. Now a name must be established. Within the sūtras and treatises, what excellent names are there? Each of you may state what you have in mind, and we will choose carefully ourselves." The monks submitted two names in a memorial: one was Chanmen ("Gate of Dhyāna"), the other Wujingju ("Five Pure Abodes"). Before the memorial had been presented, the monk-envoy Zhizao reported the auspicious sign of [the name] Guoqing, and the edict said: "This is the numinous auspicious sign of our late teacher. Use it, use it." By edict the plaque of the Daya Hall of the Jiangdu Palace was taken, filled in with orpiment and written in great seal script, and the concurrent Drafter for Communications of the Palace Secretariat, Lu Zhengli, was sent to deliver it and set it up over the monastery gate. The name Guoqing began from this. Our late master was born with divine light and passed away seated in the cross-legged posture. Dwelling [in seclusion], he realized the wondrous Dharma; coming out, he became the teacher of emperors. All this is recorded in full in the three biographies, by Falun of Zhugong, Zhiguo of Kuaiji, and Guanding of Guoqing. Further, the śramaṇa Zhiji compiled the letters and edicts that had been sent to invite our late master, but before his search was complete Zhiji died, and the labor of his brush and ink was abandoned together with his breath. I read his draft and went on to compile and arrange [it further], [adding] the methods of the various sūtras and so on, obtaining one hundred items in all, and called it the Hundred Records of Guoqing. I leave it to show later generations, that they may know that abundant virtue is here.

Youyan's Preface

Ru-Yi Translation

Preface to the Hundred Records of Guoqing

Chinese Source

丹丘沙門有嚴述

Ru-Yi Translation

Written by the Śramaṇa Youyan of Danqiu

Chinese Source

夫事無鴻纖，但有補於見聞，使人警寤而趨善道者，則不可以不錄而貽諸後也。昔我祖智者禪師，本靈山聖眾之一人也。陳隋朝出現世間，代佛宣祕為人天眼目，六十餘州直指人心，具佛知見加修圓行，則妙果不遠。復其所談教法外餘事委積，章安尊者撮其可錄者凡一百條，以國清為目。天聖年中，伏蒙聖朝編入大藏。既緘以函帙，故世人罕得而見之。禪師自開皇十七年丁巳入滅，至紹聖四年足五百歲，明年春四明陳宗逸，始謀鏤版印行果，冠冕法俗因得以讀之，有以知禪師釋部中豪傑之士也。雖不得而見其面，讀其文亦得以見其心，見心愈於見面也。奉命序述，以冠首云。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, whether a matter is great or small, if only it adds to what is seen and heard and makes people alert and awake and hasten on the good path, it may not be left unrecorded but must be handed on to later times. Formerly our ancestor, Meditation Master Zhizhe, was originally one of the holy assembly on Vulture Peak. He appeared in the world in the Chen and Sui dynasties and proclaimed the secret on the Buddha's behalf, becoming the eye of humans and gods. In more than sixty prefectures he pointed directly at people's minds: [he taught that if one] possesses the Buddha's knowledge and vision and adds the cultivation of the perfect practice, the wondrous fruit is not far off. Further, apart from the teachings he expounded, other matters had piled up; Venerable Zhang'an gathered those that could be recorded, one hundred items in all, and titled them by [the name] Guoqing. In the Tiansheng era [1023–1032], by the humble grace of the sage court, it was entered into the great canon. Since it was then sealed up in cases and wrappers, people of the world rarely got to see it. From the Meditation Master's entry into extinction in the dingsi year, the seventeenth of Kaihuang [597], to the fourth year of Shaosheng [1097], a full five hundred years [had passed]. In the spring of the following year, Chen Zongyi of Siming first planned to carve the blocks and print it, and indeed the leading figures among monastics and laity were thereby able to read it, and so came to know that the Meditation Master was a hero among the Buddhist ranks. Though one cannot see his face, by reading his writings one can see his mind, and seeing the mind is better than seeing the face. Having received the request, I have written this preface to stand at its head.

Fascicle One: Establishing the Regulations

Chinese Source

國清百錄卷第一

Ru-Yi Translation

The Hundred Records of Guoqing, Fascicle One

Chinese Source

隋沙門灌頂纂

Ru-Yi Translation

Compiled by the Śramaṇa Guanding of the Sui

Chinese Source

立制法第一（并序）

Ru-Yi Translation

Establishing the Regulations, Number 1 (with preface)

Chinese Source

夫新衣無孔不可補之以縷，宿植淳善不可加之以罰。吾初在浮度、中處金陵，前入天台，諸來法徒各集，道業尚不須軟語勸進，況立制肅之。後入天台，觀乎晚學如新猿馬，若不控鎖，日甚月增。為成就故，失二治一，蒲鞭示恥，非吾苦之。今訓諸學者，略示十條。後若妨起，應須增損。眾共裁之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, a new robe without holes need not be patched with thread; those who have long planted pure goodness need not be subjected to penalties. When I was first at Fudu and in the middle [years] at Jinling, and before I entered Tiantai, the Dharma-followers who came gathered together, and in the work of the path they did not even need gentle words to encourage them onward — how much less the establishing of regulations to discipline them! After I entered Tiantai, I observed that the later students were like new monkeys and horses; if they are not restrained and chained, it will grow worse by the day and increase by the month. In order to bring them to accomplishment, I lose two to govern one: the rush whip only shows shame, and I do not make them suffer. Now, to instruct all the students, I briefly set out ten articles. If hindrances arise later, they should be added to or reduced; let the community decide them together.

Chinese Source

第一夫根性不同，或獨行得道、或依眾解脫。若依眾者當修三行，一依堂坐禪、二別場懺悔、三知僧事。此三行人，三衣六物道具具足，隨有一行則可容受。若衣物有缺、都無一行，則不同止。

Ru-Yi Translation

First: People's faculties and natures differ; some attain the path practicing alone, some are liberated relying on the community. Those who rely on the community should cultivate three practices: first, sitting in meditation in the hall; second, repentance in a separate place [of practice]; third, attending to the affairs of the saṅgha. People with these three practices, complete in the three robes, the six requisites, and the implements of the path, may be admitted if they have any one of these practices. If their robes and requisites are lacking and they have none of the practices, they may not live with us.

Chinese Source

第二依堂之僧本以四時坐禪、六時禮佛，此為恒務，禪禮十時一不可缺。其別行僧行法竟，三日外即應依眾十時。若禮佛不及一時，罰三禮對眾懺。若全失一時，罰十禮對眾懺。若全失六時，罰一次維那。四時坐禪亦如是，除疾礙。先白知事則不罰。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second: For the monks who rely on the hall, seated meditation in the four periods and bowing to the buddhas in the six periods are their constant duties; of these ten periods of meditation and bowing, not one may be missed. As for monks of separate practice, when their method of practice is finished, after three days they should rely on the community for the ten periods. If one does not arrive in time for one period of bowing to the buddhas, the penalty is three bows and repentance before the community. If one misses one period entirely, the penalty is ten bows and repentance before the community. If one misses all six periods entirely, the penalty is to serve one term as karmadāna. The four periods of seated meditation are the same, except for illness or obstruction; if one informs the officer in charge beforehand, there is no penalty.

Chinese Source

第三六時禮佛。大僧應被入眾衣，衣無鱗隴若縵衣悉不得。三下鐘早集敷坐，執香罏互跪，未唱誦不得誦，未隨意不散語話。叩頭彈指頓曳屣履起伏參差，悉罰十禮對眾懺。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third: For bowing to the buddhas in the six periods, the senior monks should wear the robe for entering the community; robes without the scale-like rows [of patches], or plain unpieced robes, are all not permitted. At the third stroke of the bell, gather early and spread out the sitting cloth; hold the censer and kneel with one knee raised. Before the chant is intoned, do not recite; before "as you please" [is called], do not disperse or talk. Knocking the head, snapping the fingers, shuffling the sandals, rising and bowing out of step — all incur the penalty of ten bows and repentance before the community.

Chinese Source

第四別行之意，以在眾為緩，故精進勤修四種三昧，而假託道場不稱別行之意。檢校得實，罰一次維那。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth: The intent of separate practice is that, since [practice] in the community is slack, one diligently and vigorously cultivates the four kinds of samādhi. If one uses the place of practice as a pretext and does not accord with the intent of separate practice, when this is investigated and found true, the penalty is to serve one term as karmadāna.

Chinese Source

第五其知事之僧，本為安立利益，反作損耗割眾潤己自任恩情。若非理侵一毫，雖是眾用而不開白，檢校得實不同止。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth: Monks who are officers in charge are there originally to establish and benefit [the community]; if instead they cause loss and waste, cutting from the community to enrich themselves and dispensing favors at their own will — if without reason they encroach on even a hair's worth, even if it is for the community's use, without announcing it — when this is investigated and found true, they may not live with us.

Chinese Source

第六其二時食者，若身無病病不頓臥，病己瘥皆須出堂，不得請食。入眾食器聽用鐵瓦，薰油二器甌椀匙筯，悉不得以骨角竹木瓢漆皮蚌，悉不得上堂。又不得摚觸己鉢，吸啜等聲含食語話，自為求索，私將醬菜眾中獨噉。犯者罰三禮對眾懺。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth: At the two meals, if one is not ill, or is ill but not entirely bedridden, or has recovered from illness, one must leave [one's room] for the hall; one may not ask for food [to be brought]. As eating vessels for entering the community, iron and earthenware are allowed — the two vessels smoked with oil, bowls, cups, spoons, and chopsticks; none of bone, horn, bamboo, wood, gourd, lacquer, leather, or shell may be brought into the hall. Further, one may not knock one's own bowl, make sounds of slurping and sucking, talk with food in the mouth, ask for things for oneself, or privately bring sauces and pickles to eat alone in the community. Those who offend are penalized with three bows and repentance before the community.

Chinese Source

第七其大僧小戒，近行遠行寺內寺外，悉不得盜噉魚肉辛酒、非時而食。察得實，不同止。除病危篤瞻病用醫語，出寺外投治則不罰。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh: Senior monks and those with the lesser precepts, whether traveling near or far, inside or outside the monastery, may not secretly eat fish, meat, pungent herbs, or wine, or eat at the improper time. When this is examined and found true, they may not live with us. Excepted are those critically ill, [taking] what the physician tending the illness prescribes; if they go outside the monastery to seek treatment, there is no penalty.

Chinese Source

第八僧名和合，柔忍故和，義讓故合。不得諍計高聲醜言動色，兩競者各罰三十拜對眾懺。不應對者不罰。身手互相加者，不問輕重皆不同止。不動手者不罰。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth: The saṅgha is called harmonious and united: gentle and patient, therefore harmonious; righteous and yielding, therefore united. One may not dispute and calculate, raise the voice, use ugly words, or change color [in anger]. When two contend, each is penalized with thirty bows and repentance before the community. One who does not answer back is not penalized. Those who lay hands on each other, regardless of whether lightly or heavily, may not live with us. One who does not raise a hand is not penalized.

Chinese Source

第九若犯重者依律治。若橫相誣，被誣者不罰，作誣者不同止。若學未入眾時，過眾主不受，學眾未攝故，彼自言比丘故入眾。來犯重誣他者，治罰如前。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth: If one commits a grave offense, he is dealt with according to the vinaya. If one falsely accuses another, the one accused is not penalized, and the one who made the false accusation may not live with us. If a student has not yet entered the community, the head of the community does not accept [responsibility for] his faults, because the community of students has not yet taken him in; [but if] he calls himself a bhikṣu and so enters the community, and comes and commits a grave offense or falsely accuses another, he is dealt with and penalized as above.

Chinese Source

第十依經立方見病處藥，非於方吐於藥有何益乎。若上來九制聽懺者，屢懺無慚愧心不能自新，此是吐藥之人宜令出眾。若能改革，後亦聽還。若犯諸制捍不肯懺，此是非方之人，不從眾網則不同止。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth: Prescriptions are set up according to the sūtras, seeing where the illness lies and [giving] the medicine. If one does not [follow] the prescription and vomits up the medicine, what benefit is there? If, among the nine regulations above, those allowed to repent repent again and again without any sense of shame and cannot renew themselves, these are people who vomit the medicine and should be made to leave the community. If they can reform, they are allowed to return afterward. If one violates the regulations and stubbornly refuses to repent, this is a person who rejects the prescription; not following the net of the community, he may not live with us.

The Method of Reverent Bowing: Offering and Praise

Chinese Source

敬禮法第二（并序）

Ru-Yi Translation

The Method of Reverent Bowing, Number 2 (with preface)

Chinese Source

此法正依龍樹毘婆沙，傍潤諸經意。於一日一夜，存略適時。朝午略敬禮用所為三，晡用敬禮略所為。初夜全用，午時十佛代中夜，後夜普禮。

Ru-Yi Translation

This method relies principally on Nāgārjuna's Vibhāṣā, with the intent of the various sūtras added alongside. Within one day and one night it is kept full or abridged to suit the time. In the morning and at noon, abridge the reverent bowings and use the "for the sake of" [intentions], three [of them]; in the late afternoon, use the reverent bowings and abridge the "for the sake of" [intentions]. In the first watch of the night, use the whole. At noon, the ten buddhas; [these] stand in for the middle of the night. In the last watch of the night, the universal bowing.

Chinese Source

一心敬禮常住三寶，嚴持香華如法供養。願此香華雲，遍滿十方界，一一諸佛土，無量香莊嚴，具足菩薩道，成就如來香。供養已如法行道。行道竟，敬禮常住三寶，歎佛呪願。呪願云：色如閻浮金，面逾淨滿月，身光智慧明，所照無邊際。摧破魔怨眾，善化諸人天，乘彼八正船，能度難度者。聞名得不退，是故稽首禮。歎佛功德，三界天龍、皇國七廟、師僧父母、造寺檀越、一切怨親等，會真如共成佛果。上座當用智力自在說。

Ru-Yi Translation

With a single mind I bow in reverence to the eternally abiding Three Jewels. Holding incense and flowers reverently, I make offering as the Dharma directs. May this cloud of incense and flowers / fill the realms of the ten directions; / in each and every buddha-land / may immeasurable fragrance adorn it, / fulfilling the bodhisattva path / and accomplishing the fragrance of the Tathāgata. Having made offering, circumambulate as the Dharma directs. When the circumambulation is finished, bow in reverence to the eternally abiding Three Jewels, praise the Buddha, and make the incantatory wish. The incantatory wish says: His color is like Jambū gold; / his face surpasses the pure full moon; / the light of his body and wisdom is bright, / and what it illumines has no boundary. / He crushes the hosts of Māra's enemies / and skillfully transforms humans and gods. / Riding that ship of the eightfold right [path], / he can ferry across those hard to ferry. / Hearing his name, one attains non-retrogression; / therefore I bow my head and pay homage. Praising the Buddha's merits, [we wish that] the gods and dragons of the three realms, the seven ancestral temples of the imperial state, teachers, monks, fathers and mothers, the donors who built the monastery, all enemies and kin, and so on, may meet suchness and together accomplish the fruit of buddhahood. The senior seat should speak freely by the power of his own wisdom.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Units 86–101 reused — Item 4, the Avalokiteśvara repentance (請觀世音懺法第四), is the same T1934 text already translated as `qing-guanyin-jing` Part 2. Its English is copied verbatim from `qing-guanyin-jing/p2_en_a.txt` (units 0–15 there = units 86–101 here). Only the heading was changed from "Fourth: …" to "…, Number 4" to match the numbering of the other items. The notes in `qing-guanyin-jing/p2_notes.md` apply here too.

Names and events from the Separate Biography — The English of shared passages follows `zhizhe-biezhuan/p1_body.md`: Emperor Xuan's edict on Shifeng county (unit 150), the Prince of Yongyang's vow text (units 186, 188: "equals [Dao']an and [Hui]yuan in virtue…", "blown about by the winds of karma…", "as Jñānākara served Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata…"), and the names Mao Xi, Xu Ling, Zhao Junqing, Huang Jibao, Chen Jianzong, Liu Xuan, Shen Junli, Lingyao, Guangzhai, Waguan, Folong, the Taiji and Chongzheng Halls. The biography's versions differ in details: the Secretary is 朱雷 Zhu Lei there but 朱宙 Zhu Zhou here (unit 155); the fourth envoy is 法昇 Fasheng there but 道昇 Daosheng here (unit 156); the crown prince's precept ceremony is on 二月五日 there but 今月十五日 (letter dated first month, 13th) here (unit 178).

Sources of the bowing rite (units 21–62) — The ten buddhas of the ten directions, the seven buddhas under their trees, the repentance verse 十方無量佛所知無不盡… 三三合九種，從三煩惱起, and the requesting, rejoicing, and dedication verses are from the Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā (十住毘婆沙論, T1521), checked in the vault. The praise 色如閻浮金… 聞名得不退 is a compilation of half-lines from the same treatise's chapter on easy practice; the exact sequence was not found there. The vows 了了見佛性，猶如妙德 also occur in Mohe Zhiguan and the Xiao Zhiguan.

Universal bowing (units 70–84) — The places are the seven sites and nine assemblies of the Avataṃsaka; the three closing invocations (歸佛得菩提…) also occur in T1980 and T1982. The forms 虛空不動戒/定/慧藏 were found only in T1934 (source not located).

Daily schedule (unit 21) — 朝午略敬禮用所為三，晡用敬禮略所為 is obscure. "所為" is taken as the series of "For the sake of…" bowings (units 46–57). 午時十佛代中夜 is translated literally ("At noon, the ten buddhas; [these] stand in for the middle of the night"). Alternatively: "the ten buddhas [used] at noon replace [the rite of] the middle of the night."

Vaipulya quotations (unit 132) — The quotations are close paraphrases of the Great Vaipulya Dhāraṇī Sūtra (大方等陀羅尼經, T1339): the three shares of the jewel, Mañjuśrī's eloquence for an eon, the four continents' treasures, white leprosy, the twenty-four precepts — all checked in the vault. "Within what is sought for nothing, I therefore seek it" (無所求中吾故求之) is also quoted in the site's Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 2.

Ten minds (unit 143) — The contrary and accordant ten minds parallel Mohe Zhiguan, fascicle 4 (on the site), but the wording here is different and shorter, so the passage is translated fresh, with the site's "overturn and break" for 翻破 and "shame and remorse" for 慚愧.

方等 explained (unit 144) — Zhiyi glosses 方 as the four "methods" and 等 as "equal." The Chinese words are added in parentheses where the wordplay depends on them; 呪 is both the noun "mantra" and the verb "cast a spell on."

Rules for the community (units 9–19) — 維那 is rendered "karmadāna" (the duty-monk); 知事 "officer in charge"; 隨意 "as you please," the call that ends a session; 鱗隴 "scale-like rows [of patches]" of a pieced robe. In unit 18 the clause 過眾主不受 is ambiguous; it may mean "the head of the community does not accept [responsibility for] his faults."

Officials — 臣景歷 at the end of Emperor Xuan's edicts is Cai Jingli (蔡景歷), who drafted them; 臣徵 at the end of the Last Ruler's edicts has not been identified (both rendered "Your servant …"). 神筆 is rendered "In the imperial hand." Titles are rendered literally: 吏部尚書 Minister of Personnel, 太尉 Grand Defender, 左僕射 Chief Administrator of the Left (the biography's "Chief Administrator" for 僕射), 主書 Secretary, 舍人 Drafter, 通事舍人 Drafter for Communications, 刺史 Regional Inspector, 儀同 Equal in Ceremony.

Personal names — The Prince of Yongyang is Chen Bozhi (陳伯智); he signs his dedication 陳靜智 Chen Jingzhi and his letter 陳靜惠 Chen Jinghui, apparently Dharma names. The crown prince who signs "Yuan" (淵) is not identified with certainty: in 585 the Chen crown prince was Chen Yin (陳胤), later replaced by Chen Shen (陳深); the biography has the same signature. "Miaojue" (妙覺, unit 174) is taken as Empress Shen's own Dharma name, not the stage "wondrous awakening"; the bodhisattva name given her is 海慧 "Ocean Wisdom." 武元皇帝, 元明皇太后 (unit 49) are the posthumous titles of Emperor Wen of the Sui's father and mother; that unit, with the "Most Honored Sage Ruler" and "crown prince," shows the bowing rite was used under the Sui.

Doubtful readings — Unit 146 一果一樓 is read as 一縷 "a single thread." Unit 158 迎陵 is taken as a place name (Yingling); it may be an error for 延陵. Unit 163 東田口二 is read "two persons of Dongtian," supported by unit 164 不許讓口; the desk 闞寶縷𭷊案 and the whisk 山羊[髟/口/?]麈尾 contain characters that cannot be read with certainty. Unit 144 𭀪瞎 is rendered "sightlessness." Unit 188 無諸善因緣 is translated literally ("Without these good causes and conditions"); 無 may be an error. Unit 191 義同公 is taken as an error for 儀同 "Equal in Ceremony," the title the biography gives Shen Junli. Unit 192 五誓 "the five vows" is unexplained.

Small and Great Vehicles (unit 143) — 小乘／大乘 are rendered "Lesser Vehicle / Great Vehicle," following `fangdeng-sanmei`.
