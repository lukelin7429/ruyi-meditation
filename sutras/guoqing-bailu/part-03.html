The Last Testament, the Prince's Mourning, and the Founding of Guoqing Monastery

This part covers items 59 to 90, from the end of fascicle three into the start of fascicle four. The first letters are the Prince of Jin's invitations of 597 and his inquiry when Zhiyi fell ill at Shicheng Monastery. Then come Zhiyi's own last writings: a statement of vows to restore three sacred images and stūpas, and his last testament to the prince. In the testament he lists six regrets about his teaching career and reports on the Vimalakīrti commentary. He asks the prince to care for Tiantai, Yuquan, and other monasteries, to reform grain transport, and to let unregistered monks register. The prince's reply, his statement of vows, and his letter of condolence follow, with the Tiantai community's memorials of thanks. After Yang Guang became crown prince in 600, the monk-envoys Guanding and Zhizao reported the wonders seen at Zhiyi's shrine. The prince sent texts and gifts for a fast at the shrine and had the commentary copied in the capital. The part closes with documents from his first year as emperor (605): an audience with the envoy Zhizao, the naming of Guoqing Monastery after Zhiyi's prophecy, and the ordination of forty-nine monks with their Dharma names. Many of these events also appear, sometimes with different dates and names, in Guanding's Separate Biography.

Ru-Yi Translation

The Prince's Letter Sending an Envoy into Tiantai to Pay Respects, Number 61

Chinese Source

總持和南。麥序氣清道體和適，福慧莊嚴與時高勝。弟子至來未久，粗復可行。近頻降兩書，爰垂示功德。玄義刪削，文句入初，其舊維新從麁至妙，會須披接方豁煙雲。春律已謝夏坐方結，敢違經教涉暑出迎，白露宵團秋風葉下，必預舟檝迎覲江陽。然仰勞著述已涉數載，般若多障近願成功，日就月將庶方啟沃，率貢別牒示表虔誠。在敬無文仰揆弘亮，其間珍德續事音語。謹和南。

Ru-Yi Translation

Zongchi respectfully salutes [you]. In the wheat season the air is clear; [I hope] your health in the path is harmonious and well, and your adornment of blessings and wisdom grows loftier with time. Your disciple arrived not long ago and is somewhat able to travel again. Recently you have sent down two letters showing your merit: the profound meaning has been pruned, and the text-by-text [commentary] has entered the beginning; the old is made new, from the coarse to the wondrous. I must open and receive them in person before the mist and clouds will clear. The spring season has passed and the summer sitting is about to be bound; I dare not go against the sūtras' teaching by having you come out in the heat to [meet] me. When the white dew gathers at night and the autumn wind [brings] down the leaves, I will surely prepare boats in advance to welcome you at Jiangyang. But you have labored at the writing for several years now; prajñā meets many obstacles, and I hope that you will soon complete it. Day by day and month by month, I hope to be opened and enriched. I offer a separate notice as a token of my sincere reverence. In reverence there is no ornament; I trust your broad understanding. Meanwhile take care of your virtue; I will continue to send word. Respectfully, with salutation.

The Statement of Vows

Chinese Source

發願疏文第六十四

Ru-Yi Translation

Text of a Statement of Vows, Number 64

Chinese Source

吳縣維衛、迦葉二像，願更聚合修復。鄮縣阿育王塔寺頹毀，願更修治。剡縣十丈彌勒石像金色剝壞，願更莊嚴。右三處功德乞修。沙門某敬白。

Ru-Yi Translation

The two images of Vipaśyin and Kāśyapa in Wu county — I vow that they be gathered together again and restored. The stūpa monastery of King Aśoka in Mao county is in ruins — I vow that it be repaired again. The gold of the ten-zhang stone image of Maitreya in Shan county is peeling and damaged — I vow that it be adorned again. The merit of the three places above I beg [you] to cultivate. The śramaṇa So-and-so respectfully addresses [you].

Chinese Source

稽首和南三世諸佛。伏惟法身無像，隨機顯現；淨土不毀，人眾見燒；滬瀆浮來，靈塔地涌。剡山天樂通夢陸咸三處尊儀，表代三世慈善根力利益斯土。但年深歲曩、聖跡凌遲，諸佛影像若淪，眾生善根無寄。某宿世有幸，忝預法門，德行輕微功業無取，謬為道俗所知。顧影羞躬無以自處。上慚三寶無興顯之能，下愧群生少提拔之力，刻骨刻肌將何所補。幸值明時棟梁佛日，願賴皇風又承眾力，將勸有緣修治三處，先為興顯三世佛法，次為擁護大隋國土，下為法界一切眾生。若塔像莊嚴則紹隆不絕，用報佛恩。

Ru-Yi Translation

I bow my head and respectfully salute the buddhas of the three times. I humbly consider that the Dharma-body has no image, appearing according to capacities; the pure land is not destroyed, [though] the human multitude sees it burning. [At] Hudu [the images] floated in; the numinous stūpa welled up from the earth; on Mount Shan heavenly music [sounded], and [the image] came in a dream to Lu Xian: the honored forms of these three places represent the power of the kind and good roots of the three times, benefiting this land. But as the years have grown long, the sacred traces have declined; if the images of the buddhas sink away, the good roots of living beings will have nothing to lodge in. I, So-and-so, was fortunate from past lives to have a part in the Dharma-gate, [yet] my virtuous conduct is slight and my works worth nothing; undeservedly I am known to monastics and laity. Looking at my shadow and ashamed of myself, I have no place to put myself. Above, I am ashamed before the Three Jewels that I have no ability to make them flourish; below, I am ashamed before the multitude that I have little power to lift them up. Carving my bones and carving my flesh, what could I make good? Fortunately I meet a bright age, a ridgepole and beam of the Buddha-sun; I wish to rely on the imperial influence and also receive the power of the many, to encourage those with conditions to restore the three places: first, to make the Buddhadharma of the three times flourish; next, to protect the land of the great Sui; and below, for all living beings of the dharma-realm. If the stūpa and images are adorned, then [the Dharma] will be continued and flourish without break, to repay the Buddha's kindness.

Chinese Source

若處處光新，國界自然殊特妙好，則報至尊水土之澤。若見聞者發菩提心，則利益一切種出世業。但久域此心，緣障未果。此際赴期西出，氣疾增動，毒器坏瓶固非保惜，先以身命奉給三寶，謹聽威神。若形命停留能生物善不污佛法者，伏聽使乞早除差行願速圓。若命雖未盡，方多魔障損物善根、污亂佛法者，豈須停世，願早灰滅。滅後以諸誓願付囑後人，使經紀修立，乞三寶加建速得圓滿。前於佛隴金光明道場已發此願，今重於石尊再述愚誠。伏願聖凡重垂攝受。謹疏。開皇十七年十一月二十一日。

Ru-Yi Translation

If every place is bright and new, the land of the state will naturally be especially fine, and so repay the bounty of water and earth of the Most Honored One. If those who see and hear arouse the bodhi-mind, it will benefit all kinds of supramundane work. But for long I have held this mind; because of obstructing conditions it has not been fulfilled. At this time, setting out west to keep the appointment, my illness of the breath has grown worse. This poisonous vessel, this unfired jar, is surely not worth keeping; first I offer my body and life to the Three Jewels and respectfully submit to [their] majestic spirit. If my form and life remain and can produce good in beings without defiling the Buddhadharma, I humbly ask that [the illness] be removed early so that my practice and vows may soon be fulfilled. If, though my life is not yet ended, it would bring many obstacles of Māra, harm the good roots of beings, and defile and disorder the Buddhadharma, why should I need to remain in the world? I wish to be reduced to ashes early. After my death, I entrust these vows to those who come after, that they manage and carry out the restoration; I beg the Three Jewels to add their support so that it is quickly completed. Earlier, at the Golden Light place of practice at Folong, I made this vow; now I state my foolish sincerity again before the stone honored one. I humbly wish that sages and ordinary beings will again grant their acceptance. Respectfully stated. Twenty-first day of the eleventh month of the seventeenth year of Kaihuang [597].

The Last Testament

Chinese Source

遺書與晉王第六十五

Ru-Yi Translation

Last Testament to the Prince of Jin, Number 65

Chinese Source

貧道初遇勝緣，發心之始，上期無生法忍、下求六根清淨，三業殷勤、一生望獲。不謂宿罪殃深致諸留難，內無實德，外召虛譽，學徒強集檀越自來。既不能絕域遠避，而復依違順彼自招惱亂。道退為虧，應得不得，憂悔何補。上負三寶、下愧本心，此一恨也。然聖法既差，自審非分，欲以先師禪慧授與學人，故留滯陳都八年弘法，諸來學者，或易悟而早亡、或隨分而自益，無兼他之才，空延教化，略成斷種。自行前缺、利物次虛，再負先師百金之寄，此二恨也。而年既西夕，恒惜妙道，思值明時願逢外護，初蒙四事既勵朽年，師與學徒四十，餘僧三百許，日於江都行道，亦復開懷待來問者，儻逢易悟用答王恩。而不見一人求禪求慧，與物無緣頓至於此，謬當信施化導無功，此三恨也。

Ru-Yi Translation

When I, a poor man of the path, first met superior conditions and first aroused the mind, above I hoped for the acceptance of non-arising and below I sought the purity of the six faculties, [cultivating] the three kinds of action earnestly and hoping to attain it in one life. I did not expect that offenses of past lives, deep in calamity, would bring about all kinds of obstacles. Within I had no real virtue, and without I summoned an empty reputation; students gathered by force and donors came of themselves. Since I could not avoid them by [going to] distant regions, and further wavered and went along with them, I brought vexation and confusion on myself. The path receded and [I] fell short; what I should have attained I did not attain; what use is regret? Above I have failed the Three Jewels, and below I am ashamed before my original mind. This is the first regret. Since the sacred Dharma had slipped away from me, and I examined myself and saw it was not my lot, I wished to give my late teacher's dhyāna and wisdom to students; therefore I stayed in the Chen capital eight years spreading the Dharma. Of those who came to study, some awakened easily but died early, some benefited themselves according to their lot; none had the talent to embrace others; my teaching was prolonged in vain, and [the line] was nearly cut off. My own practice lacking before and benefiting beings empty after, I have twice failed my late teacher's trust of a hundred [pieces of] gold. This is the second regret. As my years were already setting in the west, I always cherished the wondrous path, thinking to meet a bright age and wishing to encounter an outer protector. When I first received the four requisites, they encouraged my decaying years; masters and students [numbering] forty, and some three hundred other monks, practiced the path daily at Jiangdu, and I also opened my heart to wait for those who would come to ask, hoping to meet those who would awaken easily, to repay the prince's kindness. Yet I did not see a single person seek dhyāna or seek wisdom. My lack of connection with beings has come to this; undeservedly I receive the faithful offerings, but my transforming guidance has no merit. This is the third regret.

Chinese Source

又作是念：此處無緣，餘方或有先因。荊潭之願願報地恩，大王弘慈霈然垂許。於湘潭功德粗展微心，雖結緣者眾，孰堪委業。初謂緣者不來，今則往求不得，推想既謬，此四恨也。於荊州法集，聽眾一千餘僧，學禪三百，州司惶慮謂乖國式，豈可聚眾用惱官人，故朝同雲合暮如雨散。設有善萠不獲增長，此乃世調無堪，不能諧和得所，五恨也。既再遊江都，聖心重法令著淨名疏。不揆闇識輒述偏懷，玄義始竟麾蓋入謁。復許東歸，而吳會之僧咸欣聽學，山間虛乏不可聚眾，束心待出訪求法門，暮年衰弱許當開化。今出期既斷，法緣亦絕，此六恨也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again I thought: in this place I have no connection; in other regions there may be former causes. The wish [to go] to Jing and Tan was a wish to repay the kindness of the land [of my birth]; the Great Prince's vast kindness abundantly granted it. At Xiang and Tan, in [the work of] merit, I roughly set forth my small mind; though those who formed connections were many, who could be entrusted with the work? At first I thought that those with conditions had not come; now I go seeking and cannot find them. My guess was mistaken. This is the fourth regret. At the Dharma assembly in Jingzhou, the listeners were more than a thousand monks and those studying dhyāna three hundred; the prefectural officials were alarmed, thinking it went against state regulations — how could [I] gather a crowd and trouble the officials? So in the morning they gathered like clouds and in the evening scattered like rain. Even if there were good sprouts, they could not grow. This is because I am unfit in worldly harmony, unable to harmonize and set things right. This is the fifth regret. When I traveled again to Jiangdu, [Your Highness's] sacred mind, valuing the Dharma, had me write the commentary on the Vimalakīrti. Not measuring my dim understanding, I presumed to set forth my partial views; as soon as the profound meaning was finished, [your] banner and canopy went to court for audience. You also permitted me to return east, and the monks of Wu and Kuaiji were all glad to listen and study; [but] the mountains were empty and poor and could not gather a crowd, [so] binding their minds, they waited for me to come out, to seek the Dharma-gates; in my last years, weak, I had promised to open the teaching. Now that the date of coming out is cut off, the Dharma-connection too is broken. This is the sixth regret.

Chinese Source

在山兩夏專治玄義，進解經文至佛道品，為三十一卷。將身不慎遂動熱渴，一百餘日競疾治改，際此夏末慮有追呼，束裝待期。去月十七日使人至山，止留一宿遽比螢光，早希進路行過剡嶺，次至石城氣疾兼篤，不能復前。此之義疏，口授出本一遍自治，皆未搜簡。經論僻謬尚多，不堪流布。既為王造，寧羞其拙，囑弟子抄寫後本仰簡。前所送玄義及入文者，請付弟子焚之。天挺睿智願一遍開讀，覽其大意餘無可觀。貧道灰壞雖謝，願留心佛法，詢訪勝德，使義門無廢。深窮佛教，治道益明，遍行遍學是菩薩行。如來滅度法付國王，貧道何人慧門憑委，欣然就盡沒有餘榮。但著述延歲，文義不同，悵然自慚。生來所以周章者，皆為佛法、為國土、為眾生。今得法門仰寄，三為具足六根釋矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

On the mountain for two summers I worked exclusively on the profound meaning, and went on to explain the text of the sūtra as far as the "Buddha Path" chapter, [making] thirty-one fascicles. Not being careful of my body, I stirred up fever and thirst; for more than a hundred days I contended with the illness, treating and correcting [it]. At the end of this summer, anticipating a summons, I packed and waited for the time. On the seventeenth of last month the envoy arrived at the mountain, staying only one night; hastily, like a firefly's light, I hoped to set out on the road early. Passing over the Shan ridge, I reached Shicheng next, where the illness of the breath became severe, and I could go no further. This commentary on the meaning I dictated; I made one draft and corrected it myself, but none of it has been searched and sorted. Many mistakes regarding the sūtras and treatises remain, and it is not fit to circulate. Since it was made for the prince, how could I be ashamed of its clumsiness? I have charged my disciples to copy the final text and look up to you to examine it. The profound meaning and the entering into the text sent earlier, please give to my disciples to burn. [Your Highness has] heaven-given wise understanding; I hope you will read [it] through once and grasp the main ideas; the rest is not worth looking at. Though I, a poor man of the path, have passed away to ashes, I hope you will keep the Buddhadharma in mind, seek out superior virtuous ones, and not let the gate of meaning fall into disuse. Deeply exhausting the Buddha's teaching, the way of governing will become all the clearer; to practice everything and study everything is the bodhisattva's practice. When the Tathāgata passed into extinction, the Dharma was entrusted to the kings of states; who am I, a poor man of the path, that the gate of wisdom should be entrusted [to me]? Gladly I come to my end, with no glory left over. But the writing has dragged on for years, and the words and meaning are uneven; I am disappointed and ashamed of myself. The reason I have hurried about all my life is all for the Buddhadharma, for the land, for living beings. Now that I have been able to entrust the Dharma-gate [to you], the three are complete, and the six faculties are set free.

Chinese Source

命盡之後，若有神力，誓當影護王之土境，使願法流衍以答王恩，以副本志。菩薩誓願誠而不欺，香火義深安知仰謝。願觀泡幻，知有為法一切無常，會而不離終不可得。唯當勤戒施惠以拒四山，早求出要，豈須傷法煩勞聖懷。蓮華香罏犀角如意，是王所施。今以仰別，願德香遐遠，長保如意也。南嶽大師滅度之後，未有碑頌。前蒙教許自制，願不忘此旨。南嶽師於潭州立大明寺，彌天道安於荊州立上明寺，前蒙教影護願光飾先德，為作檀越主。貧道在世六十年，未嘗作有為功德。年暮力弱多闕用心，又香火施重，近於荊州仰為造玉泉寺，修治十住寺，並蒙教囑。彼總管蘄郡公達奚儒。僧齎教書至夏口，而蘄公亡，書未及付，慈恩已足，願為玉泉作檀越主。今天台頂寺茅菴稍整，山下一處非常之好，又更仰為立一伽藍，始剪木位基命弟子營立，不見寺成冥目為恨。天台未有公額，願乞一名，移荊州玉泉寺貫十僧住天台寺，乞廢寺田為天台基業。寺圖并石像、發願疏，悉留仰簡。

Ru-Yi Translation

After my life ends, if I have spiritual power, I vow to protect the prince's territory as a shadow, so that the Dharma-stream may spread, to repay the prince's kindness and to fulfill my original intent. A bodhisattva's vows are sincere and do not deceive; the meaning of [our bond of] incense and fire is deep — how could I thank you? I hope you will contemplate bubbles and illusions, and know that all conditioned dharmas are impermanent; meeting without parting can never be attained. Only diligently [keep] precepts, giving, and wisdom to ward off the four mountains, and seek early the essential [way] of escape; why should [my passing], troubling the Dharma, weary your sacred heart? The lotus incense burner and the rhinoceros-horn ruyi were given by the prince; now I give them [back] in farewell, wishing that [your] fragrance of virtue may reach afar and that you may long keep [all] as you wish. After the extinction of the Great Master of Nanyue there has been no stele or eulogy; formerly I received your instruction permitting you to compose it yourself; I hope you will not forget this. Ru-Yi Translation

Edict of the Emperor. The Emperor respectfully inquires after Dharma Master Zhiyue and the others of Guoqing Monastery in Kuozhou. The monk-envoy Zhizao has arrived; we have received your letter and understood your feelings fully. Tiantai, a land of blessings, is truly a superior realm; therefore we respectfully built a saṃghārāma for Zhizhe. Since the Dharma connection is deep and the duty of honoring the teacher weighty, we wish the bequeathed model of the master of the school to be upheld and not fall, and the bodhisattva's pure work to grow ever newer with time. But now, the sage being long distant, students decline; they seek gain and support and do not cut off worldly ties, savor rich flavors and violate the precepts — this only increases offenses and defilement; how can it be called a field of blessings? You masters have left conditioned dharmas to seek the supreme path and abandoned the outflows of the world — [this] is clear in your refined minds; you must encourage and instruct those not yet learned to cultivate pure conduct, so that the companions of the Dharma-gate all return to harmony and the prohibitions and precepts of the buddhas are followed to the end. Further, the fine response of this monastery is a sign known beforehand by the [earlier] awakened one; since the principle comes from unseen appeal, it is named Guoqing Monastery, and there are also gifts to express our rejoicing. In the winter season it is very cold; [we trust] your health in the path is pure and well. We are traveling to inspect customs and have arrived at Jiangdu; gazing at the mountains and rivers, we think of you with deep care; therefore we send the concurrent Drafter for Communications Lu Zhengli to go there. This is not much.

Ru-Yi Translation

Memorial of the śramaṇa Zhiyue of Guoqing Monastery on Mount Tiantai and the whole community. The concurrent Drafter for Communications Lu Zhengli has arrived and respectfully proclaimed the edict of the twenty-ninth day of the tenth month, saying: by heavenly kindness [you] instruct us and grant the monastery an auspicious name; gifts of two thousand lengths of goods, one thousand hu of rice, two hu of frankincense, a thousand-monk Dharma fast, the ordination of forty-nine people to leave home, and the repair of the monastery buildings. We at once gathered the community, burned incense, and proclaimed it. We humbly consider that your sacred rule matches heaven and earth in virtue; nurturing the ten thousand states as children and pacifying the four seas, helping the Buddha's teaching and transformation and delivering living beings, the grace of your radiant greatness is truly unequaled. Though Yue and the others wear Dharma robes, our conduct does not match [them]; even when attending our late master we often fell short of his model; day and night we reproach ourselves and have no place to rest. Humbly receiving the edict, we receive and uphold it on our heads. But, ordinary, mediocre, small, and inferior, we did not recognize the great wisdom of the bodhisattva. Formerly, in the age of the Chen, we personally heard the master say: "When the three states become one, a person of great power will build a monastery [for me]. If the monastery is founded, the land of the state will be pure; it must be Guoqing Monastery." At that time carriages and writing were not yet unified, and we did not understand what he meant. Since then we have held the doubt and not understood. [Now] receiving the edict granting the monastery, the name Guoqing matches the original auspicious sign; the mountain monks and mountain people are glad and joyful. Only now do we know that bodhisattvas of [different] vows awaken one another: some act as [those of] the five grades, some govern the ten thousand affairs, making the Three Honored Ones shine and benefiting the land.

Reading Notes for Part 3

Translator's notes

Differences from the Separate Biography: the wonders after death (unit 33) — The envoys' report differs from the biography's "Fourth" to "Sixth" signs (`zhizhe-biezhuan/p1_body.md`). Translated as written here: the two soldiers are Fang Bonu and Wei Bosheng (衛伯生), the latter of Zhao county (釗縣), Xuzhou, on the second day of the eleventh month of 598; the biography has Fang Bonu and Wei Boyu of Shuyang (沭陽) county, Haizhou, on the twelfth of the tenth month. Here Fang Bonu is of Muyang (沐陽, probably for 沭陽). Qiu Biao's vision is on the eighth of the eleventh month here, the eighteenth of the tenth month in the biography; the text writes 於丘彪, and 於 is taken as a slip, not part of the name. Zhang Zao's finger-snap is on the twenty-sixth of the eleventh month of 599 here, the sixth in the biography. The monk who saw light from the shrine is 皎 Jiao, on the nineteenth of the third month of 601; the biography has 法曉 Faxiao on the nineteenth of the first month.

Other differences — The Vimalakīrti commentary has thirty-one fascicles here (units 15, 17); the biography says twenty-eight. The biography dates Zhiyi's death to the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month of 597; unit 33 agrees, while the statement of vows (unit 13) and the testament as received (unit 17) are dated the twenty-first. Unit 21 opens "On the twenty-ninth day of the first month" but is dated "fifth day of the eleventh month" at the end; both are translated as written.

The testament (unit 15) — Compare the biography's account of the farewell letter "with forty-six characters written by hand" and the gifts of the incense burner and ruyi; unit 17 confirms "seven sheets, forty-six characters in his own hand." "The six faculties are set free" (六根釋矣) may also be read "the six faculties are at rest." "When the Tathāgata passed into extinction, the Dharma was entrusted to the kings of states" echoes the Humane Kings Sūtra (T245: 是故付囑諸國王, checked in the vault). The story of the Qi ruler and the charcoal carrier was not traced. 毒器坏瓶 (unit 13) "poisonous vessel, unfired jar" matches the biography's "toil in a poisonous vessel."

Quotations — "All conditioned things are impermanent; this is the nature of arising and ceasing" (unit 21) follows the site's Nirvāṇa Sūtra wording. The closing verse of unit 35 (不著世間如蓮華…稽首如空無所依) is the "Buddha Land" chapter of the Vimalakīrti; the English is the site's Vimalakīrti page, unchanged. "Do not seek liberation apart from words" (unit 35) echoes the goddess chapter of the Vimalakīrti (無離文字說解脫也). The Lotus passage in unit 33 (Śākyamuni opening Prabhūtaratna's stūpa with the finger of his right hand) is paraphrased; the site's Lotus has "with the finger of his right hand, opened the door of the stūpa." Here the eight groups (八部) see the body, rather than "the whole assembly." "Having a beginning and an end — is that not only the sage?" (unit 19) is Analects 19.12. "No word unanswered, no virtue unrewarded" (unit 35) is Odes, "Yi" (抑). "Coming after heaven, he follows heaven's timing" (unit 17) is the Book of Changes, Wenyan commentary on Qian. "Faith is what can enter and wisdom is what can ferry across" (unit 37) is the Great Treatise (T1509, fascicle 1).

Allusions — Unit 17: Kāśyapa at Wolf's Track (狼跡, i.e., Kukkuṭapāda/Gurupāda) waiting in samādhi for Maitreya; Tanguang (曇光), a meditator of Mount Tiantai who died seated. Unit 35: Shu Guang (疏廣), tutor of the Han heir, who retired and received gold; Master Huan (Huan Rong, tutor of Emperor Ming of the Han); Daoyou (道猷, Bo Daoyou) of Tiantai; Duke Xing is Sun Chuo, author of the Rhapsody on Roaming Mount Tiantai, which mentions arhats with "flying staffs"; Lingyun is Xie Lingyun's "Rhapsody on Dwelling in the Mountains." Unit 37: Xiangwang (象罔) finding the dark pearl is from Zhuangzi; "dream of the pillars" and "the beam fell first" refer to Confucius' foreseeing his death (Liji). Unit 19: 杯渡鏡水 alludes to the monk Beidu, who crossed water in a cup; Mirror Water is Jinghu near Kuaiji. 熙連 is the Hiraṇyavatī river where the Buddha entered nirvāṇa; the clause 勝行逾滿熙連 is uncertain.

Stages claimed for Zhiyi (units 17, 19, 21) — "The five grades of disciples" (五品弟子位) is from the Lotus chapter on the merits of discernment; "the faith-mind of the ten abidings" (十住信心) refers to the ten faiths before the ten abidings in the Necklace Sūtra (Yingluo jing). The biography has Zhiyi say "I am only at the stage of the five grades."

Titles and officials — 右庶子 "Right Mentor of the Heir" (Zhang Heng; Wang Hong in unit 44, who is "the Sima" in unit 17); 典籤 Record Keeper; 員外散騎侍郎 Supernumerary Cavalier Attendant Gentleman; 黃門侍郎 Gentleman of the Yellow Gate; 祕書監 Director of the Palace Library; 大都督 Grand Commander; 參軍 Adjutant; 司功參軍 Adjutant for Merit Evaluation. In units 52 and 67 the Director of the Palace Secretariat, Duke of Ju, signs 臣未上, rendered "your servant (not yet in office)"; the name is not given. 右僕射蘇 is Su Wei (蘇威), named in unit 58.

Emperor Yang's self-designation — As crown prince and emperor he still calls himself 弟子 "your disciple" in his speech to the monks (unit 58); this is kept.

Doubtful readings — Unit 21 天宮即應至金剛際 is rendered "a heavenly palace at once responded, [its foundations] reaching to the vajra boundary" (Sudatta's measuring of the Jetavana, when the heavenly palace appeared). Unit 50 十一年三日 is an error for 十一月 (marked sic). Unit 58 若為尊於師氏勝於智者 is uncertain; it introduces the naming edict of unit 60. Unit 62 戒師有行者聖表 is read "the precept masters and those with practice presented a memorial to the sage [ruler]"; Guanding's preface (Part 1, unit 2) says simply "the monks submitted two names." Unit 67 names the sixth group with 私 "private," which seems odd for the matter of the imperial admonition; possibly a copying error. Unit 69 單權獨行 is uncertain: "only one [monastery], provisional and solitary."

Dates in units 67 and 69 — The edict is dated the twenty-ninth of the eleventh month of 605, but the community's reply cites "the edict of the twenty-ninth day of the tenth month" and is itself dated the twenty-fourth of the eleventh month. Translated as written.

Names of the ordained (unit 67) — The forty-nine Dharma names are given in pinyin with a gloss of each character; the first character of each group (Ling, Dao, Jing, Hui, Zheng, Si) heads the names, e.g. Lingrui, Daoji. 寂 and 迹 are both "Ji."

Cross-reference to Part 1 — Units 59–62 retell the naming of Guoqing told in Guanding's preface (Part 1, unit 2); the English of the edict in unit 60 follows that passage.
