# The Vajra Scalpel — Translation Notes

These notes record the main translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of Zhanran's *Jingangpi* (金剛錍, Taishō T46, no. 1932), so that its terms stay consistent across all five parts and with this site's other Tiantai translations.

## The text

- **Base text:** Taishō T1932, via the CBETA digital edition. Zhanran died in 782; the text is in the public domain. The Taishō edition prints a Song-dynasty preface by Jingyue (淨岳) before the treatise; it is translated at the start of Part 1 and marked as his. Jingyue's outline itself is not part of T1932 and is not translated.
- **Commentary consulted:** the *Jingangpi lun siji* (金剛錍論私記, X56, no. 932), for difficult readings. Where its quoted text differs from the Taishō, the note on that passage says so.
- **Other English translations:** the only complete English translation known to us is in Linda Penkower's doctoral dissertation, and Brook Ziporyn has translated excerpts. We have not used either; this translation is made directly from the Chinese.
- **Coverage check:** the Chinese text on each page was compared character by character with the CBETA source to make sure nothing was skipped.

## Title

*The Vajra Scalpel.* 金錍 is the golden instrument a skilled physician uses, in the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*, to cut the membrane from a blind man's eyes. Where the sūtra itself is quoted, this site's translation of that sūtra renders it "golden probe." The title uses "scalpel" for the cutting image; Zhanran adds "vajra" because the treatise shatters doubt.

## Fascicle numbers

Zhanran cites the *Nirvāṇa Sūtra* by fascicle numbers (31, 6, 9, 32, 27) that follow the Southern edition (T375). This site's translation of the sūtra follows the Northern edition (T374), so the reading notes give the matching part on this site where it has been found.
