The Guest's Answer and the Three Thousand in a Single Thought

The guest now confesses what he had feared: that "the insentient have buddha-nature" meant every blade of grass and every pebble must separately cultivate a cause and reach a fruit. Seeing that this is not what Zhanran means, he gives a single answer to all forty-six questions: the three — mind, buddha, and beings — are without distinction, so that each being's mind, in every instant, interpenetrates with the body, mind, and land of Vairocana. Zhanran approves, then points the guest to practice: the path of contemplation taught by Zhiyi in the Mohe Zhiguan, with its twenty-five preliminary methods and ten modes of contemplation. He explains why other Mahāyāna teachers still treat the buddha-nature of the insentient as an odd view, sorts the question through the four teachings, and distinguishes three levels of the claim — the mind creates, the mind transforms, and the substance inherently includes. The part ends with the three thousand dharmas in a single thought, traced to the ten suchnesses of the Lotus Sūtra, and with Zhanran's reasons for crediting the Lotus above all other sūtras.

The Guest's Former Fear

Chinese Source

客曰。仁所立義灼然異僕於昔所聞。僕初聞之。乃謂一草一木一礫一塵。各一佛性各一因果具足緣了。若其然者僕實不忍。何者草木有生有滅。塵礫隨劫有無。豈唯不能修因得果。亦乃佛性有滅有生。世皆謂此以為無情。故曰無情不應有性。僕乃誤以世所傳習難仁至理失之甚矣過莫大矣。余曰。子何因猶存無情之名。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "What you propose is clearly different from what I heard before. When I first heard it, I thought you meant that each blade of grass, each tree, each pebble, and each mote of dust has its own buddha-nature, its own cause and fruit, complete with the conditioning and revealing causes. If that were so, I truly could not accept it. Why? Grasses and trees arise and perish; dust and pebbles exist or not as the eons turn. Not only could they not cultivate the cause and attain the fruit; buddha-nature itself would perish and arise. Everyone calls these things insentient, and so they say the insentient should not have the nature. I mistakenly used what the world passes along to challenge your perfect principle. My error was great; there is no greater fault." I said: "Why do you still keep the name 'insentient'?"

Chinese Source

客曰。乃僕重述初迷之見。今亦粗知仁所立理。只是一一有情心遍性遍。心具性具猶如虛空。彼彼無礙彼彼各遍。身土因果無所增減。故法華云世間相常住。世間之言凡聖因果依正攝盡。余曰觀子所見。似知大旨。何不試答向之一問。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "I was only restating my first, deluded view. Now I understand, roughly, the principle you propose. It is simply that for each and every sentient being, the mind pervades and the nature pervades; the mind includes everything and the nature includes everything, like space. Each is unobstructed by the others, and each pervades everything. In body and land, cause and fruit, nothing is added and nothing lost. That is why the Lotus says, 'The characteristics of the world abide permanently.' The word 'world' takes in everything: ordinary and sage, cause and fruit, environment and beings." I said: "Judging by what you see, you seem to know the main point. Why not try answering one of my questions?"

One Answer to All the Questions

Chinese Source

客曰。仁向自云若思一問眾滯自消。僕若答者即以一答遍答眾問。何一問之有耶。余曰。請述其旨。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "You yourself said earlier that if one thinks through a single question, all blockages dissolve of themselves. If I answer, then one answer answers all the questions. Why speak of just one?" I said: "Please set out what you mean."

Chinese Source

客曰。僕還攬向諸問意。若消眾滯即名為答。何假曲申一一問耶。何者眾問豈不由僕不受無情有性之說。僕今受之此即是答。余曰。大略雖爾未曉子情。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "I take up again the intent of all your questions. If the blockages are dissolved, that is called an answer; why should I labor over each question one by one? Why so? Were not all the questions asked because I did not accept the teaching that the insentient have the nature? Now I accept it, and that is my answer." I said: "In broad outline that is so, but I do not yet know how you understand it."

Chinese Source

客曰。仁所立義關諸大教難可具陳。僕略論之冀垂聽覽。豈非曉最後問三無差別。即知我心彼彼眾生一一剎那。無不與彼遮那果德身心依正。自他互融互入齊等。我及眾生皆有此性故名佛性。其性遍造遍變遍攝。世人不了大教之體。唯云無情不云有性。是故須云無情有性。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "What you propose concerns all the great teachings and can hardly be set out in full. Let me discuss it briefly; I hope you will listen. Is it not this? Understanding the last question — that the three are without distinction — one knows that my mind and every other being, in every single instant, are none other than the fruit-virtues of Vairocana, his body and mind, his environment and his person; self and other interpenetrate, enter one another, and are equal. Because I and all beings have this nature, it is called buddha-nature. This nature creates everything, transforms into everything, and includes everything. People do not understand the substance of the great teaching; they say only 'insentient' and do not say 'has the nature.' That is why it is necessary to say: the insentient have the nature.

Chinese Source

了性遍已則識佛果具自他之因性。我心具諸佛之果德。果上以佛眼佛智觀之。則唯佛無生。因中若實慧實眼冥符。亦全生是佛無別果佛。故．生外無佛。眾生以我執取之。即無佛唯生。初心能信教仰理亦無生唯佛。亡之則無生無佛。照之則因果昭然。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Having understood that the nature pervades, one recognizes that the fruit of buddhahood includes the cause-nature of self and others, and that my mind includes the fruit-virtues of all buddhas. Seen from the fruit, with the buddha-eye and buddha-wisdom, there are only buddhas and no beings. Within the cause, if real wisdom and the real eye silently accord with this, then beings are wholly buddha and there is no separate buddha of the fruit; so there is no buddha outside beings. When beings grasp it through attachment to self, there are no buddhas, only beings. When a beginner can believe the teaching and look up to principle, there are also no beings, only buddhas. When both are let go, there are neither beings nor buddhas; when both are illuminated, cause and fruit are plain to see.

Chinese Source

應知眾生但理諸佛得事。眾生但事諸佛證理。是則眾生唯有迷中之事理。諸佛具有悟中之事理。迷悟雖殊事理體一。故一佛成道法界無非此佛之依正。一佛既爾諸佛咸然。眾生自於佛依正中。而生殊見苦藥昇沈。一一皆計為己身土。淨穢宛然成壞斯在。仁所問意豈不略爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Know that beings have only principle, while buddhas have attained phenomena; beings have only phenomena, while buddhas have realized principle. Thus beings have only the phenomena and principle that lie within delusion, while buddhas fully have the phenomena and principle that lie within awakening. Though delusion and awakening differ, phenomena and principle are one in substance. So when one buddha attains the Way, there is nothing in the dharma-realm that is not this buddha's environment and person. As it is with one buddha, so it is with all buddhas. Within the buddha's environment and person, beings give rise to their own different views — of suffering and joy, of rising and sinking — and each takes it to be his own body and land. Pure and defiled are plainly there; formation and destruction are right there. Is this not, in brief, the meaning of your questions?"

Chinese Source

余曰。善哉善哉。快領斯旨。實可總知諸問綱格。此即已答百千萬問。何獨四十六耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

I said: "Excellent, excellent! You have grasped this point quickly. You truly know the framework of all the questions. This already answers hundreds of thousands of questions, not merely forty-six."

The Path of Contemplation and the Teacher

Chinese Source

客曰。幾不遇仁此生空喪。必依此見獲勝果耶。余曰。必欲修習教法未周。若不善余一家宗途。未可委究行門始末。安能遍括教行事理惑智因果依正心法。用為凡夫初心觀首。然子所領似虛其情。計子觀道猶為罔象。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "Had I not met you, this life would almost have been wasted. If I rely on this view, will I surely attain the supreme fruit?" I said: "If you wish to practice it, your study of the teaching is not yet complete. Unless you are well versed in the path of our school, you cannot thoroughly investigate the gate of practice from beginning to end. How then could you take in teaching and practice, phenomena and principle, delusion and wisdom, cause and fruit, environment and beings, mind and dharmas, and use them as the starting point of contemplation for an ordinary beginner? What you have grasped still seems empty in your heart. I reckon that your path of contemplation is still a shadow without form."

Chinese Source

客曰。觀道者何。仁師誰耶。法依何耶。余曰。子豈不聞。天台大師靈山親承。大蘇妙悟。是余師也。摩訶止觀所承法也。以二十五法為前方便。十法成乘觀於十境。十境互發觀時進否。此觀道之大略也。諸問且令識十乘初妙境而已。餘乘諸境不暇論之。客曰。善哉。僕當慕之。以為永劫之仗託也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "What is the path of contemplation? Who is your teacher? On what dharma do you rely?" I said: "Have you not heard? The Great Master of Tiantai, who received the teaching in person at Vulture Peak and had his wondrous awakening on Mount Dasu — he is my teacher. The Mohe Zhiguan is the dharma I have received. It takes twenty-five methods as preliminary expedients, and with ten methods forms the vehicle to contemplate the ten objects. As the ten objects arise one after another, one discerns, while contemplating, whether one is advancing or not. This is the path of contemplation in outline. My questions were only meant to let you recognize the first of the ten vehicles, the wondrous object; there is no time to discuss the other vehicles and objects." The guest said: "Excellent! I will aspire to it and rely on it for endless eons."

Why Other Teachers Still Doubt It

Chinese Source

客曰。屢聞講說。大乘諸師猶以無情佛性。為一別見何耶。余曰。此有由也。斯等曾覩小乘無情之名。又見大乘佛性之語。亡其所弘融通之譚。而棄涅槃虛空之喻。不達修性三因離合。不思生佛無差之旨。謬斆傳習無情之言。反難己宗唯心之教。專引涅槃瓦石之說。不測時部出沒之意。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "I have often heard lectures in which Mahāyāna teachers still treat the buddha-nature of the insentient as an odd, separate view. Why is that?" I said: "There is a reason. They have seen the term 'insentient' in the Lesser Vehicle, and they have also seen the word 'buddha-nature' in the Mahāyāna. They forget the talk of interpenetration in the very texts they propagate, and discard the Nirvāṇa's simile of space. They do not grasp how the three causes in cultivation and in nature separate and combine, and they do not reflect on the teaching that beings and buddhas are without difference. They wrongly imitate the received talk of the insentient and, on the contrary, attack their own school's teaching of mind-only. They cite only the Nirvāṇa's statement about tiles and stones, without fathoming why teachings appear and disappear across the periods and divisions.

Chinese Source

如福德子而無壽命。弱喪徒歸○猶迷本族。如受貴位不識祖宗。亦如死人而著瓔珞。用是福為用瓔珞為。法相徒施全迷其本。忽遇斯等應以如上諸意問之所弘之典大小乘耶。尚失小乘已如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

"They are like a child blessed with merit but without a long life; like one who left home young and returns in vain, still not knowing his own clan; like one who receives high rank but does not know his ancestors; or like a corpse adorned with jeweled necklaces. What use is such merit? What use are such necklaces? Their analysis of the characteristics of dharmas is wasted, for they are wholly confused about the root. If you happen to meet such people, question them with the points above: do the texts they propagate belong to the Great Vehicle or the Lesser? As I said before, they fall short even of the Lesser Vehicle."

Judging the Question by the Four Teachings

Chinese Source

客曰。斯失者眾。聞仁所宗四教釋義可得聞耶。余曰。此之四釋關涉五時牢籠八教。十方三世大小乘法咸攝其中。豈可率爾譚其始末。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "Many make this mistake. I hear that your school interprets the teachings by the four teachings; may I hear about it?" I said: "These four interpretations touch on the five periods and encompass the eight teachings. All the dharmas of the Great and Lesser Vehicles in the ten directions and three times are included in them. How could I discuss them from beginning to end in a casual way?"

Chinese Source

客曰。若爾可能以四教。略判佛性無情有無心造心變具不具耶。余曰。略示方隅斯亦可矣。何者自法華前藏通三乘俱未稟性。二乘憚教。菩薩不行。別人初心教權理實。以教權故所稟未周。故此七人可云無情不云有性。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "If so, could you use the four teachings to judge briefly whether the insentient have buddha-nature or not, and whether the mind creates, the mind transforms, and the nature includes everything or not?" I said: "To point out the general direction briefly is possible. Why? Before the Lotus, the three vehicles of the Tripiṭaka and Shared teachings had not yet received the teaching of the nature: those of the two vehicles shrank from the teaching, and the bodhisattvas did not practice it. For beginners in the Distinct teaching, the teaching is provisional though the principle is real; because the teaching is provisional, what they have received is incomplete. So these seven kinds of people may say 'insentient' and not say 'has the nature.'

Chinese Source

圓人始末知理不二。心外無境誰情無情。法華會中一切不隔。草木與地四微何殊。舉足修途皆趣寶渚。彈指合掌咸成佛因。與一許三無乖先志。豈至今日云無情無言心造心變咸出大宗。小乘有言而無其理。

Ru-Yi Translation

"People of the Perfect teaching, from beginning to end, know that principle is not two. Outside the mind there is no object; who, then, is sentient and who insentient? In the Lotus assembly nothing is set apart. Grasses, trees, and the earth — how do they differ in their four subtle elements? Every step taken on the road leads to the treasure land; a snap of the fingers or a joining of palms all become causes of buddhahood. Giving the one vehicle after promising three does not go against the Buddha's original intent. How could anyone, to this day, say that the insentient lack it? The phrases 'the mind creates' and 'the mind transforms' all come from the great school; the Lesser Vehicle has the words but not their principle.

Creating, Transforming, and Inherently Including

Chinese Source

然諸乘中其名雖同義亦少別。有共造依報各造正報。有共造正報各造依報。眾生迷故或謂自然梵天等造。造已或謂情與無情。故造名猶通。應云心變。心變復通。應云體具。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Yet among the vehicles, though the names are the same, the meanings differ somewhat. Some say the environment is created in common and the body individually; some say the body is created in common and the environment individually. Beings, in their delusion, may say things are created by nature itself, by Brahmā, and so on; and once things are created, some call them sentient and some insentient. So the word 'create' is still shared by all. One should say 'the mind transforms.' But 'the mind transforms' is also shared; one should say 'the substance inherently includes.'

Chinese Source

以無始來心體本遍。故佛體遍由生性遍。遍有二種。一寬廣遍。二即狹遍所以造通於四。變義唯二。即具唯圓。及別後位。

Ru-Yi Translation

"From beginningless time the substance of mind has been pervasive by its very nature. So the Buddha's substance pervades because the nature of beings pervades. Pervading is of two kinds: first, pervading by breadth; second, pervading in which the narrow is itself the whole. Therefore 'creating' applies to all four teachings; the sense of 'transforming' applies only to two; 'identity and inherent inclusion' belongs only to the Perfect teaching, and to the later stages of the Distinct.

Chinese Source

故藏通造六。別圓造十。此六及十括大小乘教法罄盡。由觀解異故十與六各分二別。藏見六實。通見無生。別見前後生滅。圓見事理一念具足。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So in the Tripiṭaka and Shared teachings the mind creates six realms; in the Distinct and Perfect teachings it creates ten. These six and ten exhaust the teachings of the Great and Lesser Vehicles. Because contemplation and understanding differ, the ten and the six each divide into two. The Tripiṭaka teaching sees the six as real; the Shared teaching sees them as unarising; the Distinct teaching sees arising and perishing in sequence; the Perfect teaching sees phenomena and principle complete in a single thought.

Chinese Source

論生兩教似等。明具別教不詮。種具等義非此可述。故別佛性滅九方見。圓人即達九界三道。即見圓伊三德體遍。

Ru-Yi Translation

"In speaking of production, the two teachings seem equal; but the Distinct teaching does not express inherent inclusion. The meaning of the seed being inherently included cannot be set out here. So in the Distinct teaching, buddha-nature is seen only when the nine realms have been extinguished, while the person of the Perfect teaching, penetrating the nine realms and the three paths as they are, sees in them the perfect i, the three virtues, pervading in substance."

The Three Thousand in a Single Thought

Chinese Source

客曰。如何能攝依正因果。余曰一家所立不思議境於一念中理具三千。故曰念中具有因果凡聖大小依正自他。故所變處無非三千。而此三千性是中理。不當有無有無自爾。何以故。俱實相故。實相法爾具足諸法。諸法法爾性本無生。故雖三千有而不有。共而不雜。離亦不分。雖一一遍亦無所在。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "How can it include environment and beings, cause and fruit?" I said: "The inconceivable object our school establishes is that in a single thought, principle includes the three thousand. So we say that a thought includes cause and fruit, ordinary and sage, great and small, environment and beings, self and other. Wherever there is transformation, it is none other than the three thousand. And the nature of these three thousand is the principle of the middle: it does not fall into existence or nonexistence, yet existence and nonexistence are just as they are. Why? Because both are the true aspect. The true aspect, by its nature, is complete with all dharmas; all dharmas, by their nature, are originally unarising. So though the three thousand exist, they do not exist; they are together yet not mixed, apart yet not divided; though each pervades everything, it is located nowhere."

Chinese Source

客曰。其理必然。僕深仰之。此為憑教為通依諸部。為專在一經。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "This principle must be so; I deeply revere it. Does it rest on the scriptures? Does it rely on all the divisions in general, or on one sūtra in particular?"

Chinese Source

余曰。斯問甚善。能使其理永永不朽。雖則通依一切大部。指的妙境出自法華。故方便品初。佛歎十方三世諸佛所得微妙難解之法。所謂諸法實相如是相等。當知如是相等即是轉釋諸法實相。以諸法故故有相等。以實相故相等皆是。實相無相相等皆如。

Ru-Yi Translation

I said: "This is an excellent question; it can make this principle last forever. Though it relies in general on all the great divisions, the specific source of the wondrous object is the Lotus. At the beginning of the 'Expedient Means' chapter, the Buddha praises the subtle and hard-to-understand dharma that the buddhas of the ten directions and three times have attained: namely, the true aspect of all dharmas — such characteristics, and so on. Know that 'such characteristics and so on' is itself a further explanation of 'the true aspect of all dharmas.' Because there are 'all dharmas,' there are characteristics and the rest. Because there is 'the true aspect,' the characteristics and the rest are all 'such.' The true aspect is without characteristics, and so the characteristics and the rest are all 'thus.'"

Chinese Source

客曰。云何三千。余曰。實相必諸法。諸法必十如。十如必十界。十界必身土。又依大經及以大論。立三世界故有三千。具如止觀及廣記中。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "How are there three thousand?" I said: "The true aspect is necessarily all dharmas; all dharmas necessarily have the ten suchnesses; the ten suchnesses necessarily belong to the ten realms; the ten realms necessarily have bodies and lands. Further, relying on the great sūtra and the great treatise, three kinds of world are set up, and so there are three thousand. All this is set out in full in the Mohe Zhiguan and in my extended record of it.

Why the Lotus Sūtra Is Credited

Chinese Source

故知。因果凡聖恒具三千。是故歎云。唯佛與佛乃能究盡。十方世界稻麻二乘如恒河沙。不退菩薩並不能知斯義少分。即指前之七種人也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So we know that cause and fruit, ordinary and sage, always include the three thousand. That is why the Buddha praises it: 'Only a buddha together with a buddha can fathom it completely.' Followers of the two vehicles filling the worlds of the ten directions like rice and hemp, and non-retreating bodhisattvas as many as the sands of the Ganges, cannot together know even a small part of this meaning. This points to the seven kinds of people mentioned earlier.

Chinese Source

是故身子三請慇懃。十方三世諸佛開顯。釋迦仰同無復異趣。大車譬此。宿世示此。壽量久本唯證於此。根敗適復獲記由此。菩薩疑除損生增道。始初發心終訖補處。豈有餘途並託於此。

Ru-Yi Translation

"That is why Śāriputra asked earnestly three times; the buddhas of the ten directions and three times open and reveal it; and Śākyamuni follows them, with no different aim. The great carriage is a simile for this; the account of former lives shows this; the long-ago origin in the 'Lifespan' chapter bears witness to this alone. That those whose faculties were ruined have just now recovered and received predictions is due to this. For bodhisattvas whose doubts are removed, who reduce birth and increase the path, from first arousing the mind to the final stage of succession to buddhahood — is there any other road? All rely on this.

Chinese Source

由前四時兼但對帶部非究竟。故推功法華。涅槃兼權意如前說。當知一乘十觀即法華三昧之正體也。普現色身之所依也。正因佛性由之果用。緣了行性由之能顯。性德緣了所開發也。涅槃真伊之所喻也。法華大車之所至也。諸大乘意準例可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Because the divisions of the four earlier periods — the combining, the exclusive, the contrasting, and the incorporating — are not ultimate, the merit is credited to the Lotus. As for the Nirvāṇa also carrying the provisional, that is as I explained before. Know that the ten contemplations of the One Vehicle are the very substance of the Lotus samādhi. They are what the samādhi of manifesting all forms everywhere rests on. Through them, the correct cause of buddha-nature functions as the fruit; through them, the conditioning and revealing causes, as the nature of practice, can be revealed. They are what the conditioning and revealing causes, as virtues of the nature, open up and develop. They are what the Nirvāṇa's true letter i symbolizes, and where the Lotus's great carriage arrives. The intent of all the Mahāyāna teachings can be understood by analogy.

Chinese Source

子得聞之。可謂久種勤而習之無使焦敗。願未來世諸佛會中與子相遇。

Ru-Yi Translation

"That you have been able to hear this means you planted the seed long ago. Practice it diligently, and do not let it be scorched and ruined. May I meet you again in the assemblies of the buddhas in ages to come."

Reading Notes for Part 4

Translator's notes

"Suffering and joy" (苦藥). The Taishō text reads 苦藥 ("suffering and medicine"); the translation follows the reading 苦樂, "suffering and joy," found in the text quoted in the Jingangpi lun siji (X932).

"A shadow without form" (罔象). An allusion to the Zhuangzi, where Xiangwang (象罔), "Formless," finds the lost black pearl. Here Zhanran uses it the other way: the guest's grasp is still vague and without substance.

Vulture Peak and Mount Dasu. According to Tiantai tradition, when Zhiyi first met his teacher Huisi, Huisi said they had heard the Lotus Sūtra together at Vulture Peak in a former life. Zhiyi's awakening came while practicing the Lotus samādhi under Huisi on Mount Dasu (大蘇山).

Twenty-five methods, ten methods, ten objects. These are the framework of the Mohe Zhiguan, translated on this site: twenty-five preliminary expedients; ten modes of contemplation (十乘觀法), the first of which is contemplating the mind as the inconceivable object (觀不思議境); and ten objects of contemplation, from the aggregates and sense bases to the views of bodhisattvas. Here 進否 means "advancing or not" in practice, not "the reach" of buddha-nature as elsewhere.

Seven kinds of people. The three vehicles of the Tripiṭaka teaching, the three vehicles of the Shared teaching, and beginners in the Distinct teaching.

Pervading by breadth and by the narrow (寬廣遍、即狹遍). In the Distinct teaching, the nature pervades by being vast; in the Perfect teaching, even the narrowest thing is itself the whole. "Transforming" applies only to the Distinct and Perfect teachings.

Six and ten realms. The six realms of rebirth (hell beings to gods); the ten add śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas, and buddhas. The three paths (三道) are affliction, karma, and suffering.

"Such" and "thus" (是、如). Zhanran plays on the phrase 如是 ("such") in the Lotus Sūtra's list of ten suchnesses: because of the true aspect, every characteristic is 是, "just so," and 如, "thus as it is."

The great sūtra and the great treatise; the extended record. In Tiantai usage "the great sūtra" usually means the Nirvāṇa Sūtra and "the great treatise" the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom, the source of the three kinds of world (aggregates, beings, and lands). Ten realms, each containing ten, times ten suchnesses, times three worlds, gives three thousand. "My extended record" is Zhanran's own commentary on the Mohe Zhiguan, the Zhiguan fuxing zhuan hongjue (止觀輔行傳弘決).

The four earlier periods. In the Tiantai scheme of five periods, the Avataṃsaka period "combines" the perfect with the distinct, the Āgama period teaches the small "exclusively," the Vaipulya period "contrasts" the small with the great, and the Prajñā period "incorporates" the provisional. Only the Lotus is purely perfect.

Former lives (宿世). The Lotus Sūtra's chapter on the conjured city recounts how the Buddha taught his disciples in former lives under the buddha Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū.
