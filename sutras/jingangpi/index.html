Zhanran · Tang dynasty · 金剛錍

The Vajra Scalpel.

"The insentient have buddha-nature." Zhanran (湛然, 711–782), the Tiantai master who revived the school in the eighth century, murmurs these words in his sleep — and a rough "rustic guest" appears in a dream to challenge him with the Nirvāṇa Sūtra itself, which says that buddha-nature is "not walls, fences, tiles, and stones." The short treatise that follows is Zhanran's answer: a single mote of dust and a single mind share the mind-nature of all beings and buddhas, and nothing whatsoever lies outside the three causes of buddha-nature. Its title comes from the golden scalpel that a skilled physician uses, in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, to cut the film of ignorance from a blind man's eyes. This collection translates the whole treatise across five pages, with reading notes written for practitioners.

How to read this collection

The Jingangpi is one fascicle long, written as a dialogue between host and guest. Its arguments lean heavily on texts already translated on this site: the Kāśyapa chapter of the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, the Lotus Sūtra, Zhiyi's Fahua Xuanyi and Mohe Zhiguan, and the Buddha-Nature Treatise. Reading the Jiaoguan Gangzong first will make the four teachings easier to follow.

The five pages follow the treatise's own turns: the opening argument from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, the simile of waves and water, Zhanran's forty-six questions, the guest's answer and the three thousand in a single thought, and Zhanran's advice on how to teach it. The Song-dynasty preface by Jingyue (淨岳), printed with the Taishō text, is translated at the start of Part 1 and marked as his.

What each page includes

  • A short introduction to each part, and reading notes on allusions, technical terms, and textual variants.

The Treatise's Own Structure

One continuous dialogue with no chapter titles of its own, divided here at its natural turning points.

Part 1 · 虛空喻
A Dream Guest and the Simile of Space
Jingyue's Song-dynasty preface, the dream, the rustic guest's objection from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, the Kāśyapa chapter's simile of space, ten wrong views of space, and why buddha-nature is not one thing.
Translated
Part 2 · 波水喻
Waves and Water
Why the provisional teachings limit the causes, mind-only and inherent inclusion, suchness that follows conditions yet does not change, and dharma-nature as another name for buddha-nature.
Translated
Part 3 · 四十六問
Forty-Six Questions
Forty-six terse questions — on the name, the insentient, mind-only, beings, buddha-lands, suchness, the similes, and the practitioner's own mind — one for each stage of the path.
Translated
Part 4 · 一念三千
The Three Thousand in a Single Thought
The guest's single answer, the path of contemplation in the Mohe Zhiguan, the four teachings, creating, transforming, and inherently including, and why the Lotus Sūtra is credited above all.
Translated
Part 5 · 夢覺
How to Teach It, and Waking
Advice for teaching in the latter age: having, pervading, including; Zhiyi's verse of humility; and the dream ends with nothing to be found.
Translated

Translation Notes

Key terms, kept consistent across all five parts and with this site's other Tiantai translations. See the full translation notes, and also this site's note on translation method.

無情有性 — the insentient have buddha-natureThe treatise's single thesis: walls, tiles, grasses, trees, and dust are not outside buddha-nature.
正因 / 緣因 / 了因 — the correct cause / the conditioning cause / the revealing causeMatched to this site's Fahua Xuanyi, Fahua Wenju, and Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra.
佛性進否 — the reach of buddha-natureHow far each teaching extends the term 'buddha-nature'; Zhanran's recurring charge against his opponents.
隨緣不變 — following conditions yet unchangingSuchness becomes every dharma, yet never changes; the waves-and-water simile of Part 2.
依報 / 正報 — the environment / the beingThe two kinds of karmic result; 依正不二, the nonduality of environment and beings.
造 / 變 / 具 — creating / transforming / inherently includingZhanran's three levels of the claim that the mind is the source of all dharmas (Part 4).
一念三千 — the three thousand in a single thoughtThe inconceivable object of the Mohe Zhiguan, traced here to the ten suchnesses of the Lotus Sūtra.
野客 — the rustic guestThe dream-figure who raises every objection; Zhanran is the host.
