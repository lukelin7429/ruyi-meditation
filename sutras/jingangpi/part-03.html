Forty-Six Questions

The guest now accepts that all dharmas have the nature of the correct cause, but cannot yet accept Zhanran's stronger claims: that all three causes pervade everything, as seed, in practice, and in the fruit, and that a single mote of dust and a single mind are the mind-nature of all beings and buddhas. Instead of arguing further, Zhanran poses forty-six questions. They are extremely terse, often only a few characters each, and most end by showing that either answer leads to a fault. Taken together they move from the name "buddha-nature," through the insentient, mind-only, beings, buddha-lands, the Buddha's attainment, suchness, and the classic similes, and end with the practitioner contemplating his or her own mind. Zhanran explains at the end why there are forty-six: one for each stage of the path in the Tiantai scheme of the six identities. The bracketed words in the English supply what the Chinese leaves unstated.

Zhanran Introduces the Questions

Chinese Source

客曰。仁善分別實壞重疑。信一切法皆正因性。而云正中三因種遍修遍果遍。又云一塵一心即一切生佛之心性。情猶未決。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "You make distinctions well, and you have truly broken through my heavy doubts. I now believe that all dharmas have the nature of the correct cause. But you also say that within the correct cause, all three causes pervade everything as seed, pervade everything in practice, and pervade everything in the fruit. And you say that a single mote of dust and a single mind are the mind-nature of all beings and buddhas. On these points my mind is still not settled."

Chinese Source

余曰。良由自昔不善遍攬因果自他依正。觀於己心心佛眾生。亦由不閱諸教大旨。不曉佛說果德之意。不達佛現互融之由。

Ru-Yi Translation

I said: "That is because you have never been skilled at taking in, all together, cause and fruit, self and other, environment and beings, and contemplating them in your own mind as mind, buddha, and beings. It is also because you have not read through the main purport of the teachings, do not understand why the Buddha speaks of the virtues of the fruit, and have not grasped why the buddhas manifest as mutually interpenetrating.

Chinese Source

余欲開導子之情懷。更以四十六問而問於子。子若能曉余之一問。則眾滯自消。法界融通。釋然大觀洞見法界生佛依正。一念具足一塵不虧。

Ru-Yi Translation

"I wish to open and guide your understanding, so I will put forty-six more questions to you. If you can understand even one of my questions, all your blockages will dissolve of themselves, and the dharma-realm will interpenetrate. In a great and clear vision you will see through the dharma-realm, beings and buddhas, environment and beings: all complete in a single thought, nothing lacking in a single mote of dust.

On the Name "Buddha-Nature" (Questions 1–5)

Chinese Source

問佛性之名從因從果。從因非佛。果不名性。問佛性之名常無常耶。無常非性。常不應變。問佛性之名共耶別耶。別不名性。共不可分。問佛性之名大小教耶。小無性名。大無無情。問佛性之名有權實耶。對體辯異其相何耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

1. I ask: is the name "buddha-nature" taken from the cause or from the fruit? If from the cause, it is not yet buddha; the fruit is not called a nature.

2. I ask: is what is named "buddha-nature" permanent or impermanent? If impermanent, it is not a nature; if permanent, it should not change.

3. I ask: is what is named "buddha-nature" shared or individual? If individual, it is not called a nature; if shared, it cannot be divided up.

4. I ask: does the name "buddha-nature" belong to the great teaching or the small? The small teaching has no name "nature"; the great teaching has no "insentient."

5. I ask: does the name "buddha-nature" have provisional and real senses? Distinguishing their differences against the substance, what are their marks?

On the Insentient (Questions 6–10)

Chinese Source

問無情之名大小教耶。大教大部有權實耶。問無情無者無情為色為非色耶。為二俱耶。問無情色等佛見爾耶。為生見耶。為共見耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

6. I ask: does the name "insentient" belong to the great teaching or the small? And within the great teaching and its great divisions, are there provisional and real senses?

7. I ask: when you say the insentient lack it, are the insentient form, or not form, or both?

8. I ask: does the Buddha see form and the rest as insentient? Is that how beings see them? Or is it a seeing shared by both?

Chinese Source

問無情敗壞故無性者。陰亦敗壞性亦然耶。問無情是色。法界處色為亦無耶。為復有耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

9. I ask: if the insentient lack the nature because they decay, the aggregates also decay; does the nature then do the same?

10. I ask: if the insentient are form, does the form included in the dharma-base also lack it, or does it have it?

On Mind-Only (Questions 11–15)

Chinese Source

問唯心之言子曾聞耶。唯只是心。異不名唯。問唯心之言凡聖心耶。若聖若凡二俱有過。問唯心名心造無心耶。唯造心耶。二俱有過。問唯心唯心亦唯色耶。若不唯色。色非心耶。問唯心所造唯依與正。依正能所同耶異耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

11. I ask: have you ever heard the phrase "mind-only"? "Only" means it is just mind; what differs from mind cannot be called "only."

12. I ask: in the phrase "mind-only," is it the mind of ordinary beings or of sages? Whether you say sages or ordinary beings, both answers have faults.

13. I ask: does "mind-only" mean that the mind creates what has no mind? Or that it creates only mind? Both answers have faults.

14. I ask: if it is mind-only, is it also form-only? If it is not form-only, is form not mind?

15. I ask: what mind-only creates is only the environment and the beings. Are environment and beings, what creates and what is created, the same or different?

On Beings (Questions 16–20)

Chinese Source

問眾生量異性隨異耶。不爾非內爾不名性。問眾生惑心。性遍不遍神我四句。為同異耶。問眾生有性唯應身性亦法性耶。亦報性耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

16. I ask: beings differ in size; does the nature differ along with them? If not, it is not merely inside them; if so, it is not called a nature.

17. I ask: the deluded minds of beings — is the nature's pervading or not pervading the same as, or different from, the four propositions about the spirit-self?

18. I ask: when beings have the nature, is it only the nature of the response body? Is it also the nature of the Dharma-body? Also the nature of the reward body?

Chinese Source

問眾生本迷迷佛悟耶。佛既悟已。悟生迷耶。問眾生一身幾佛性耶。一佛身中幾生性耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

19. I ask: beings are originally deluded; are they deluded about the Buddha's awakening? Once the Buddha has awakened, does that awakening give rise to delusion?

20. I ask: how many buddha-natures are there in the body of one being? And how many natures of beings are there in the body of one buddha?

On Buddha-Lands (Questions 21–25)

Chinese Source

問佛國土身為始本耶。始本同耶。為復異耶。問佛土佛身為一異耶。一無能所。異則同凡。問佛土界分生亦居耶。為各所居佛無土耶。問佛土所攝為遠近耶。何土與生一異共別。問佛佛土體為同異耶。娑婆之處為共別耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

21. I ask: are the land and body of a buddha's realm newly attained or original? Are the newly attained and the original the same, or different?

22. I ask: are a buddha's land and a buddha's body one or different? If one, there is no supporting and supported; if different, it is the same as with ordinary beings.

23. I ask: within the boundaries of a buddha's land, do beings also dwell? If each has its own dwelling, does the Buddha have no land?

24. I ask: is what a buddha-land includes far or near? Which land is one with beings or different from them, shared with them or separate?

25. I ask: are the substances of a buddha and a buddha-land the same or different? Is the place called Sahā shared or separate?

On the Buddha's Attainment (Questions 26–30)

Chinese Source

問佛成道時土亦成耶。成廣狹耶。不成有過。問佛成見性與生見處為同異耶。離二不可。問佛成土成。與彼彼成。彼彼不成為一異耶。問佛成三身。與彼彼果及彼彼生為一異耶。問佛成身土成何眼智。見自他境初後如何。

Ru-Yi Translation

26. I ask: when a buddha attains the Way, is the land also attained? If it is attained, is it wide or narrow? If it is not attained, there is a fault.

27. I ask: is the nature a buddha sees on attaining the Way the same as, or different from, the place that beings see? To separate the two is impossible.

28. I ask: a buddha's attainment and the land's attainment, the attainment of each and every other, and the non-attainment of each and every other: are these one or different?

29. I ask: a buddha's attainment of the three bodies, and each and every other fruit, and each and every being: are these one or different?

30. I ask: when a buddha attains body and land, with what eye and what wisdom does he do so? In seeing the realms of self and other, how is it at the beginning and at the end?

On Suchness (Questions 31–35)

Chinese Source

問真如所造互相攝耶。不相攝耶二俱如何。問真如之體通於修性。修性身土等不等耶。問真如隨緣變為無情。為永無耶。何當有耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

31. I ask: do the things suchness creates include one another, or not? How is it either way?

32. I ask: the substance of suchness runs through both cultivation and nature. Are body and land in cultivation and body and land in nature equal or not?

33. I ask: when suchness, following conditions, changes into the insentient, is it forever absent there? When would it be present?

Chinese Source

問真如隨緣隨已與真為同異耶。為永隨耶。問真如本有為本無耶。與惑共住同異如何。

Ru-Yi Translation

34. I ask: when suchness follows conditions, is what it has become the same as, or different from, the true? Does it follow conditions forever?

35. I ask: is suchness originally present or originally absent? Dwelling together with delusion, how is it the same or different?

On the Similes (Questions 36–40)

Chinese Source

問波水同異。前後得失。真妄同異。法譬如何。問病眼見華華處空處。同異存沒法譬如何。問鏡像明體本始同異。前後存沒法譬如何。問帝網之譬唯譬果耶。亦譬因耶。果無因耶。問如意珠身身有土耶。唯在果耶。通因如何。

Ru-Yi Translation

36. I ask: are waves and water the same or different? Before and after, what is gained and lost? Are the true and the false the same or different? How do the dharma and the simile correspond?

37. I ask: diseased eyes see flowers in the sky. Are the place of the flowers and the place of space the same or different, present or gone? How do the dharma and the simile correspond?

38. I ask: the reflections in a mirror and its bright substance — are they original or newly arisen, the same or different? Before and after, present or gone? How do the dharma and the simile correspond?

39. I ask: does the simile of Indra's net illustrate only the fruit? Does it also illustrate the cause? Is there a fruit without a cause?

40. I ask: the wish-fulfilling jewel — does each and every body have a land? Is this only in the fruit? How does it extend to the cause?

On the Practitioner Contemplating the Mind (Questions 41–46)

Chinese Source

問行者觀心心即境耶。能所得名同異如何。問行者觀心一耶多耶。一多心境同異如何。問行者觀心為唯觀心亦觀身耶。亦觀土耶。問行者觀心在惑業苦。內耶外耶同耶異耶。問行者觀心心內佛性為本淨耶。為始淨耶。問行者觀心心佛眾生因果身土法相融攝一切同耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

41. I ask: when the practitioner contemplates the mind, is the mind itself the object? How are the names "contemplating" and "contemplated" the same or different?

42. I ask: is the mind the practitioner contemplates one or many? How are one and many, mind and object, the same or different?

43. I ask: does the practitioner contemplate only the mind, or also the body? Also the land?

44. I ask: the mind the practitioner contemplates lies within affliction, karma, and suffering. Is it inside or outside, the same or different?

45. I ask: when the practitioner contemplates the mind, is the buddha-nature within the mind originally pure, or pure only from some beginning?

46. I ask: when the practitioner contemplates the mind, do mind, buddha, and beings, cause and fruit, body and land, and the characteristics of dharmas all interpenetrate and include one another, so that all are the same?

Why Forty-Six

Chinese Source

如是設問不可窮盡。為斷子疑且至爾許。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Questions like these could be posed without end. To cut off your doubts, I will stop here for now."

Chinese Source

客曰。何以不多不少。唯四十六。余曰。攻惑。攻疑。攻行。攻理。通教通義。通自通他。一問亦足。為對鈍根故四十六。及對六即分證離為四十一位。兼前及後故四十六。應知一問亦皆能攻餘四十五。餘一一位仍須皆具四十六。問乃至無量亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "Why neither more nor fewer, but just forty-six?" I said: "To attack delusion, to attack doubt, to attack practice, to attack principle; to penetrate the teaching and its meaning; to penetrate it for oneself and for others — for all this, a single question would be enough. It is for those of dull faculties that there are forty-six. They also correspond to the six identities: the identity of partial realization is divided into forty-one stages, and adding the stages before it and the one after it makes forty-six. Know that any one question can also attack the other forty-five, and that each of the other stages must in turn contain all forty-six. And so it is, on and on, without limit."

Reading Notes for Part 3

Translator's notes

Why forty-six. In the Tiantai scheme of the six identities (六即), the identity of partial realization (分證即) covers forty-one stages of the perfect teaching: the ten abodes, ten practices, ten dedications, ten grounds, and the stage of near-perfect awakening. The stages "before" are identity in principle, verbal identity, identity in contemplative practice, and identity in resemblance (four); the one "after" is ultimate identity. Forty-one plus four plus one gives forty-six, so each question corresponds to one stage.

The form included in the dharma-base (法界處色, question 10). In Abhidharma, some kinds of form, such as the unmanifest form of precepts, are not objects of the five senses and are counted in the dharma-base (dharmāyatana), the base of mental objects. Zhanran asks whether such "form" is also insentient.

The four propositions about the spirit-self (神我四句, question 17). Non-Buddhist views that the self is identical with the aggregates, different from them, both, or neither. Zhanran asks whether the guest's buddha-nature "inside beings" is any different from such a self.

Three bodies (questions 18 and 29). The Dharma-body (法身), the reward body (報身), and the response body (應身).

The similes (questions 36–40). Waves and water appear in Part 2. Flowers seen in the sky by diseased eyes are a standard image of illusion; the mirror and its reflections, Indra's net of jewels reflecting one another, and the wish-fulfilling jewel that produces whatever is wished for are all images of mutual inclusion. Zhanran asks whether each applies only to the buddha's fruit, or also to the cause in ordinary beings.

The forty-six questions are not answered. The guest's reply in Part 4 shows how they were meant to work: not as a test with right answers but as a way of making the guest see the whole at once.
