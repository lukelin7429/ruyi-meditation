A Dream Guest and the Simile of Space

Zhanran (湛然, 711–782), the sixth patriarch of the Tiantai school, wrote this short treatise late in life to defend a single proposition: the insentient — walls, tiles, grasses, trees, and dust — have buddha-nature. He frames it as a dream. Musing on buddha-nature one night, he murmurs the proposition in his sleep, and a rough "rustic guest" appears to challenge him with the most authoritative text available: the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra itself, which says in so many words that buddha-nature is "not walls, fences, tiles, and stones." This first part is Zhanran's answer on the sūtra's own ground. Reading the Kāśyapa chapter closely, he argues that the passage the guest relies on speaks provisionally, that the sūtra's own simile for buddha-nature — space — includes everything, and that the guest has confused the correct cause of buddha-nature with the conditioning and revealing causes that appear only in the fruits of practice.

Preface to the Outline of the Vajra Scalpel

Chinese Source

科金剛錍序　宋雲間沙門淨岳撰

Ru-Yi Translation

Preface to the Outline of the Vajra Scalpel, composed by the monk Jingyue of Yunjian in the Song dynasty.

Chinese Source

科分大經章段。起自關內憑小山瑤。前代未聞也。吾祖章安作疏益詳。至荊溪將迦葉品。分正緣了別指方隅。則權實進否曉然而明。可謂善乎派深。良哉析重也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Dividing the great sūtra into chapters and sections began with Master Ping of Guannei and Master Yao of Xiaoshan; it was unheard of in earlier generations. Our patriarch Zhang'an composed a commentary that made the divisions more detailed still. Then Jingxi took up the Kāśyapa chapter, separated the correct, conditioning, and revealing causes, and pointed out where each one belongs, so that the provisional and the real, and the reach of each teaching, became perfectly clear. This may truly be called skillful in tracing the stream to its depths, and excellent in analyzing layer upon layer.

Chinese Source

余復以佛性周遍三千具攝而分。今文分而又分賓主問答。引文釋義略無混亂。雖短脛亦可以厲法流。孺子敢當荷負矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

I in turn have divided this text according to the principle that buddha-nature pervades everything and fully contains the three thousand. I have divided the present text and divided it again, following the questions and answers of host and guest, so that its citations and explanations are, in the main, free of confusion. Though my legs are short, I may still wade across the stream of the Dharma; a mere youth, I dare to shoulder this load.

Chinese Source

或曰猶牧女之添水。將非澆漓於乳味乎。不然乳益乳也。苟能鑽搖醍醐可獲。豈仍乳而已耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

Someone may say: "This is like the herd-girl adding water to the milk. Won't it thin out the milk's flavor?" Not so. This is adding milk to milk. If one can only churn it, ghee may be obtained. How would it remain mere milk?

The Title and Its Note

Chinese Source

金剛錍(圓伊金錍。以抉四眼無明之膜。令一切處悉見遮那佛性之指。偏權疑碎加之以剛。假夢寄客立以賓主。觀者恕之)　唐天台沙門湛然述

Ru-Yi Translation

The Vajra Scalpel. (The golden scalpel of the perfect letter i, used to cut away the membrane of ignorance from the four eyes, so that in every place one sees the finger pointing to Vairocana's buddha-nature. Because it shatters the doubts of the partial and provisional teachings, "vajra" is added to its name. I have borrowed a dream and lodged the argument with a guest, setting up a host and a guest. May readers pardon this.) Composed by the Tiantai monk Zhanran of the Tang dynasty.

A Night of Reflection

Chinese Source

自濫霑釋典。積有歲年。未嘗不以佛性義經懷。恐不了之徒為苦行。大教斯立功在於茲。萬派之通途。眾流之歸趣。諸法之大旨。造行之所期。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since I was first, undeservedly, steeped in the Buddhist scriptures, many years have passed, and never once have I failed to keep the meaning of buddha-nature in my heart. I feared that those who do not understand it would turn their practice into mere hardship. The great teaching stands, and its whole merit lies here: this is the common road of ten thousand streams, the place to which all currents return, the great purport of all dharmas, and the goal toward which every practice aims.

Chinese Source

若是而思之。依而觀之。則凡聖一如色香泯淨。阿鼻依正全處極聖之自心。毘盧身土不逾下凡之一念。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one reflects on it in this way and contemplates in accordance with it, then ordinary beings and sages are a single suchness, and every form and fragrance dissolves into purity. The Avīci hell, both its environment and its beings, lies wholly within the very mind of the supreme sage; the body and land of Vairocana do not go beyond a single thought of the lowliest ordinary person.

Chinese Source

曾於靜夜久而思之思之未已。怳焉如睡。不覺寱云無情有性。仍於睡夢忽見一人云。僕野客也。容儀麁獷進退不恒。逼前平立。謂余曰。向來忽聞無情有性。仁所述耶。余曰然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once, on a quiet night, I reflected on this for a long time. Before my reflection was finished, I grew drowsy, as if falling asleep, and without knowing it I murmured in my sleep: "The insentient have buddha-nature." Then, in a dream, I suddenly saw a man, who said: "I am a rustic guest." His appearance was rough and coarse, and his manner of coming and going was unsteady. He pressed forward, stood squarely before me, and said to me: "Just now I suddenly heard, 'The insentient have buddha-nature.' Was that something you said?" I said: "It was."

The Guest's Objection

Chinese Source

客曰。僕忝尋釋教薄究根源。盛演斯宗豈過雙林最後極唱究竟之談。而云佛性非謂無情。仁何獨言無情有耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "I have had the honor of studying the Buddha's teaching and have looked a little into its roots. Of all the ways this doctrine has been widely expounded, what could surpass the final and supreme proclamation at the twin śāla trees, the ultimate word? And yet it says that buddha-nature does not refer to the insentient. Why do you alone say that the insentient have it?"

Chinese Source

余曰。古人尚云一闡提無。云無情無。未足可怪。然以教分大小其言碩乖。若云無情即不應云有性。若云有性即不合云無情。

Ru-Yi Translation

I said: "The ancients even said that the icchantika lacks it, so it is no wonder that they said the insentient lack it. But because the teachings divide into the great and the small, their statements diverge widely. If one says 'insentient,' one should not say it 'has the nature'; if one says it 'has the nature,' it is not right to call it 'insentient.'"

Chinese Source

客曰。涅槃部大。云何竝列。余曰。以子不閑佛性進否教部權實。故使同於常人疑之。今且為子委引經文。使後代好引此文證佛性非無情者。善得經旨不昧理性。知余所立善符經宗。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "The Nirvāṇa belongs to the great teachings. How can you set the two side by side?" I said: "Because you are not versed in the reach of buddha-nature, nor in the provisional and the real within the divisions of the teaching, you doubt this just as ordinary people do. Now let me cite the sūtra passages for you in detail, so that those in later generations who like to cite this passage as proof that buddha-nature is not in the insentient may grasp the sūtra's intent and not be blind to the nature of principle, and may know that what I propose accords well with the sūtra's central teaching."

The Kāśyapa Chapter: Buddha-Nature Is Like Space

Chinese Source

今立眾生正因體遍。經文亦以虛空譬之。故三十一迦葉品云。眾生佛性猶如虛空。非內非外。若內外者。云何得名一切處有。請觀有之一字。虛空何所不收。故知經文不許唯內專外。故云非內外等及云如空。既云眾生佛性。豈非理性正因。

Ru-Yi Translation

"I now propose that the substance of the correct cause in beings pervades everywhere. The sūtra, too, likens it to space. Thus the Kāśyapa chapter, in fascicle thirty-one, says: 'The buddha-nature of beings is like space: neither internal nor external. If it were internal or external, how could it be said to be present everywhere?' Please look at the single word 'present.' What does space not include? So we know the sūtra does not allow buddha-nature to be only internal or solely external, which is why it says 'neither internal nor external' and 'like space.' And since it says 'the buddha-nature of beings,' is this not the correct cause, the nature of principle?

Chinese Source

次迦葉問云。何名為猶如虛空。佛乃以果地無礙而答迦葉。豈非正因因果不二。由佛果答。迦葉乃以權智斷果果上緣了悉皆是有。難佛空喻法喻不齊。故迦葉云。如來佛性涅槃是有。虛空應當亦是有耶。佛先順問答。次復宗明空。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Next, Kāśyapa asks: 'What does "like space" mean?' The Buddha answers Kāśyapa in terms of the unobstructedness of the stage of fruition. Does this not show that, in the correct cause, cause and fruit are not two? Because the Buddha answered from the fruit of buddhahood, Kāśyapa took the conditioning and revealing causes, which belong to the fruits of wisdom and of severance and are provisional, as all being 'existent,' and objected that the Buddha's simile of space does not match the dharma it illustrates. So Kāśyapa said: 'The Tathāgata, buddha-nature, and nirvāṇa are existent. Should space then also be existent?' The Buddha first answered in line with the question, and then returned to his main point to clarify space.

Three "Nots" and What Is Set Against Space

Chinese Source

先順問云。為非涅槃說為涅槃。非涅槃者。謂有為煩惱。為非如來說為如來。非如來者謂闡提二乘。為非佛性說為佛性。非佛性者。謂牆壁瓦礫。

Ru-Yi Translation

"First, answering in line with the question, he said: 'Nirvāṇa is named for what is not nirvāṇa' — what is not nirvāṇa means conditioned afflictions. 'The Tathāgata is named for what is not the Tathāgata' — what is not the Tathāgata means icchantikas and the two vehicles. 'Buddha-nature is named for what is not buddha-nature' — what is not buddha-nature means walls, fences, tiles, and pebbles.

Chinese Source

今問若瓦石永非。二乘煩惱亦永非耶。故知。經文寄方便教說三對治。暫說三有。以斥三非。故此文後便即結云一切世間。無非虛空對於虛空。佛意以瓦石等三。以為所對。故云對於虛空。是則一切無非如來等三。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Now I ask: if tiles and stones are forever 'not,' are the two vehicles and the afflictions also forever 'not'? So we know that this passage, relying on an expedient teaching, speaks of three remedies: it provisionally speaks of three 'existents' in order to reject three 'nots.' That is why, right after this passage, the sūtra concludes: 'There is nothing in the entire world that is not space, set against space itself.' The Buddha's intent is to take the three — tiles and stones and the rest — as what is set against; hence 'set against space.' This means, then, that everything is none other than the Tathāgata and the other two.

Chinese Source

迦葉復以四大為並。令空成有。故迦葉云。世間亦無非四大對四大。是有虛空無對何不名有。迦葉意以空無對。故有之大也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Kāśyapa then offered the four great elements as a parallel, to make space come out existent. So Kāśyapa said: 'In the world, likewise, there is nothing that is not set against the four great elements, and the four great elements are existent. Space has no counterpart; why is it not called existent?' Kāśyapa's point was that since space has no counterpart, it is the greatest of existents.

Chinese Source

佛於此後捨喻從法。廣明涅槃不同虛空。若涅槃不同。餘二亦異。故知。經以正因結難。一切世間何所不攝。豈隔煩惱及二乘乎。虛空之言何所不該。安棄牆壁瓦石等耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

"After this the Buddha set the simile aside and turned to the dharma itself, explaining at length that nirvāṇa is not the same as space. If nirvāṇa is not the same, the other two differ from it as well. So we know the sūtra closes the debate with the correct cause. What in the entire world does it not include? How could it shut out the afflictions and the two vehicles? What does the word 'space' not cover? How could it discard walls, fences, tiles, and stones?

Chinese Source

佛後復云空與涅槃。雖俱非世攝。涅槃如來有證有見。虛空常故是故不然。豈非正與緣了不同。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Later the Buddha said further that although space and nirvāṇa are alike in not being included in the world, nirvāṇa and the Tathāgata can be realized and seen, while space, because it is permanent, is not so. Does this not mean precisely that the correct cause differs from the conditioning and revealing causes?

Ten Wrong Views of Space

Chinese Source

次佛復宗顯空非有。故恐世人以邪計空為佛性喻。更以一十復次。而遮其非。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Next, the Buddha returned to his main point and showed that space is not an existent. He feared that people would take a wrongly conceived space as the simile for buddha-nature, so he added ten more arguments, each beginning 'Furthermore,' to block these errors.

Chinese Source

初云。世人言虛空者。名為無色無對不可見。佛言此即心所三世所攝。語似心所。故佛破之。世言身內。何殊心所。

Ru-Yi Translation

"First: 'People say that space is what has no form, no counterpart, and cannot be seen.' The Buddha said this would be a mental factor, included in the three times. Because the description resembles a mental factor, the Buddha refuted it. People today say buddha-nature lies within the body; how is that different from a mental factor?

Chinese Source

復次外道言。虛空者即是光明。佛言亦是色法。世言身內。何殊色法。有云住處。世言身內。豈非住處。有云次第。世言身內。必須隨身剎那時運。

Ru-Yi Translation

"'Furthermore, non-Buddhists say that space is light.' The Buddha said that would also be a dharma of form. People today say it lies within the body; how is that different from a dharma of form? Some say space is a dwelling place. People today say it lies within the body; is that not a dwelling place? Some say space is sequence. People today say it lies within the body; then it must move along with the body from moment to moment.

Chinese Source

有云。不離三法。一空二實三者空實。佛言若言空者。有處無故。若言實者空處無故。若言空實二處無故。世言身內猶闕外計空及二俱。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Some say space is inseparable from three things: first, the empty; second, the solid; third, the empty and the solid together. The Buddha said: if you call it the empty, it is absent where there is something; if you call it the solid, it is absent where there is emptiness; if you call it both empty and solid, it is absent in both places. People today say it lies within the body; then it still lacks what is outside, and lacks the empty and the 'both together' as well.

Chinese Source

有云。作法。如去舍等。世言身沒與真相應。即同作法。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Some say space is something made, as when a house is removed. People today say that when the body perishes, buddha-nature merges with the true; that makes it the same as something made.

Chinese Source

有云。無礙處。佛言有分。有具餘處無故。世言身內。餘處則無。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Some say space is a place without obstruction. The Buddha said it would then have parts: where it is present, it is absent elsewhere. People today say it lies within the body; then elsewhere it is absent.

Chinese Source

有云。與有並合。佛言合有三種。一如鳥投樹。二如羊相觸。三如二指已合。世言身內。如二指合。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Some say space is joined together with existents. The Buddha said there are three kinds of joining: first, like a bird alighting on a tree; second, like two rams butting each other; third, like two fingers already pressed together. People today say it lies within the body; that is like two fingers pressed together.

Chinese Source

有云。如器中空。世言身內。何異器中。有云。所指之處。佛言。則有方面。世言身中。豈非方面。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Some say space is like the empty space within a vessel. People today say it lies within the body; how is that different from inside a vessel? Some say space is the place one points to. The Buddha said it would then have direction. People today say it lies in the body; is that not having direction?

Chinese Source

佛總結云。從因緣生皆是無常。故此一十邪計虛空非佛性喻。是無常故。三世攝故。虛空異彼遍一切處。此違迦葉問。復宗符空。以喻正因。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Buddha concluded: whatever arises from causes and conditions is impermanent. So these ten wrong conceptions of space are not similes for buddha-nature, because they are impermanent and included in the three times. Space, unlike them, pervades every place. This runs counter to Kāśyapa's challenge: the Buddha returns to his main point and fits space to the correct cause as its simile.

Why the Objection Misses the Mark

Chinese Source

世人何以棄佛正教。朋於邪空。云何乃以智斷果上緣了佛性。以難正因。如來是智果。涅槃是斷果。故智斷果上。有緣了性。所以迦葉難云。如來佛性涅槃是有。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Why do people today abandon the Buddha's correct teaching and side with a wrong notion of space? How can they use buddha-nature as the conditioning and revealing causes, which belong to the fruits of wisdom and severance, to challenge the correct cause? The Tathāgata is the fruit of wisdom; nirvāṇa is the fruit of severance. So it is in the fruits of wisdom and severance that the nature of the conditioning and revealing causes is found. That is why Kāśyapa objected: 'The Tathāgata, buddha-nature, and nirvāṇa are existent.'

Chinese Source

世人多引涅槃為難。故廣引之以杜餘論。子應不見涅槃之文。空斆世人瓦石之妨。緣了難正殊不相應。此即子不知佛性之進否也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"People today often cite the Nirvāṇa to object, so I have cited it at length to close off further argument. You must not have read this passage of the Nirvāṇa, and are merely repeating the popular objection about tiles and stones. Using the conditioning and revealing causes to challenge the correct cause simply does not fit. This is precisely your not knowing the reach of buddha-nature.

Chinese Source

況復以空譬正緣了猶局。如迦葉所引三皆有者。此乃涅槃帶權門說。故佛順迦葉三皆是有。若頓教實說本有三種。三理元遍。達性成修。修三亦遍。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Moreover, when space is used as a simile for the correct cause, the conditioning and revealing causes are still treated as limited. Kāśyapa's claim that all three are existent is the Nirvāṇa speaking through a gate that still carries the provisional along with it; so the Buddha went along with Kāśyapa that all three are existent. But when the sudden teaching speaks the real, all three are originally present, and the three principles pervade everything from the start. When one penetrates the nature and brings it to completion in practice, the three in practice also pervade everything.

Chinese Source

欲示眾生本有正性。且云正遍猶如虛空。欲赴末代以順迦葉。豈非迦葉知機設疑。故佛覆實述權緣了。此子不知教之權實。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Wishing to show that beings originally possess the correct nature, the Buddha said for the time being that the correct cause pervades like space. Wishing to meet the needs of the latter age, he went along with Kāśyapa. Was it not that Kāśyapa, knowing the capacities of his hearers, raised these doubts on purpose? So the Buddha covered the real and spoke of the conditioning and revealing causes provisionally. This is your not knowing the provisional and the real in the teachings.

Buddha-Nature Is Not of One Kind

Chinese Source

故涅槃中佛性之言。不唯一種。如迦葉品下文云。言佛性者。所謂十力無畏不共大悲三念三十二相八十種好。子何不引此文。令一切眾生亦無。何獨瓦石。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So in the Nirvāṇa, the word 'buddha-nature' is used in more than one sense. As a later passage of the Kāśyapa chapter says: 'What is called buddha-nature is the ten powers, the fearlessnesses, the unshared qualities, great compassion, the three foundations of mindfulness, the thirty-two marks, and the eighty minor marks.' Why do you not cite this passage? It would make all sentient beings lack buddha-nature too. Why single out tiles and stones?

Chinese Source

若云此是果德。眾生有此果性者。果性身土何不霑於瓦石等耶。又若許因有果性者。世何但云十方諸佛同一法身力無畏等。而不云生佛亦同法身力無畏等。使一塵一心無非三身三德之性種也。若言但有果地法身性者。何故經云。十力無畏乃至相好。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If you say these are virtues of the fruit, and beings possess the nature of this fruit, then why does the body and land of that fruit-nature not extend to tiles, stones, and the rest? Again, if you allow that the cause possesses the nature of the fruit, why do people say only that the buddhas of the ten directions share one Dharma-body, one set of powers, fearlessnesses, and so on, and not say that beings and buddhas likewise share the Dharma-body, the powers, the fearlessnesses, and so on, so that a single mote of dust and a single mind are none other than the seed-nature of the three bodies and the three virtues? And if you say beings have only the nature of the Dharma-body of the stage of fruition, why does the sūtra say 'the ten powers, the fearlessnesses,' and so on, down to the marks and minor marks?

Chinese Source

又復經中。闡提等人四句辯性。子云眾生有性。為何眾生有何等性。瓦石為復無四句耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Further, in the sūtra, the nature of icchantikas and others is discussed through four propositions. You say beings have the nature; which beings have which nature? And do tiles and stones lack the four propositions?

Chinese Source

又第六第九及三十二。皆以雜血五味。用對凡夫三乘及佛。何故佛性在人差降不同。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Again, fascicles six, nine, and thirty-two all use milk mixed with blood and the five flavors to correspond to ordinary beings, the three vehicles, and the Buddha. Why, then, does buddha-nature differ by degrees from one person to another?

Chinese Source

又二十七云。若修八正即見佛性。婆沙俱舍悉有八正。乃至諸經咸有道品。為修何八正見何佛性。故子不知佛性進否。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Again, fascicle twenty-seven says: 'If one cultivates the eightfold path, one sees buddha-nature.' The Vibhāṣā and the Abhidharmakośa both teach the eightfold path, and every sūtra teaches the factors of the path. Which eightfold path must be cultivated to see which buddha-nature? So you do not know the reach of buddha-nature."

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

The golden scalpel. The title draws on the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra's image of a skilled physician who uses a golden probe to cut the membrane from a blind man's eyes and then holds up one, two, or three fingers, asking whether he can see. (See this site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, Part 8, where 金錍 is rendered "golden probe.") Zhanran adds "vajra" because the treatise is meant to shatter doubt, not merely to clear the eye.

The perfect letter i. 圓伊 refers to the Siddham letter i, written as three dots, which the Nirvāṇa Sūtra uses for the three virtues (Dharma-body, prajñā, liberation): they are neither in a row nor stacked, neither one nor separate. "The four eyes" are the human, divine, wisdom, and Dharma eyes, all short of the buddha-eye.

"The reach of buddha-nature" (佛性進否). Literally "the advancing or not" of buddha-nature: how far each teaching extends the term. Zhanran's charge throughout is that his opponents read a provisional, limited use of the word as if it were the final one.

Three causes. The correct cause (正因) is the principle itself, present in every being; the conditioning cause (緣因) and the revealing cause (了因) are the good practice and the wisdom that bring it to light. Zhanran's argument is that the sūtra's statements that buddha-nature is "existent" and "not walls and stones" concern the latter two as they appear in the fruit, not the correct cause, which the sūtra likens to all-pervading space.

"Not walls, fences, tiles, and stones." The passage the guest relies on appears in this site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, Part 37; "the buddha-nature of beings is like space, neither internal nor external" appears in Part 33. Zhanran's fascicle numbers (31, 6, 9, 32, 27) do not match the Northern edition used on this site and appear to follow the Southern edition (T375); they are kept as written.

"People today say it lies within the body." In the ten refutations, Zhanran applies each of the Buddha's arguments against wrong views of space to the common view of his own day: that buddha-nature is located only inside the bodies of sentient beings. Whatever is located in this way, he argues, is exactly the kind of limited, impermanent "space" the Buddha rejects.
