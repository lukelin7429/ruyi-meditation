How to Teach It, and Waking from the Dream

Moved to tears and joy, the guest asks how he can repay what he has heard, and offers to pass it on. Zhanran's last section is practical advice for teachers. He quotes the Lotus Sūtra's warnings about teaching the One Vehicle too soon, and allows two approaches in the latter age: answering only with the great teaching, or "forcefully administering the poison," as the bodhisattvas Never Disparaging and Joyful Root did, trusting that even those who slander will benefit in the end. He then gives a three-step sequence for introducing the teaching to newcomers: show that they have the nature, then that it pervades, then that it includes everything. He closes with Zhiyi's own humble verse on the difficulty of classifying the teachings. Then the guest bows and leaves, and Zhanran wakes: questioner, answerer, question, and answer — none of it can be found.

Status: Part 5 of 5 · first edition Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T46, no. 1932 (Zhanran)

The Guest Asks How to Repay

Chinese Source

於是野客悲喜交集曰。投身莫報粉骨寧酬。唯以此義隨方轉說。以報所聞如何。

Ru-Yi Translation

At this the rustic guest, overcome with both sorrow and joy, said: "Even giving up my body could not repay this; even grinding my bones to dust would not be enough. I will only pass this meaning on to others wherever I go, to repay what I have heard. Would that be right?"

Choosing When and to Whom to Teach

Chinese Source

余曰。佛有誠誡自可為規。經云。若但讚佛乘眾生沒在苦。我寧不說法疾入於涅槃。尋思方便先小後大。此乃以偏助圓方可為說。

Ru-Yi Translation

I said: "The Buddha gave earnest warnings, and they can serve as your rule. The sūtra says: 'If I only praised the buddha-vehicle, beings sunk in suffering [could not believe it]; I would rather not teach the Dharma and enter nirvāṇa at once.' Then, reflecting on expedient means, he taught the small first and the great afterward. This means using the partial to support the perfect; only then can it be taught.

Chinese Source

又云。當來世惡人破法墮惡道。志求佛道者廣讚一乘道。此即簡人方可為說。

Ru-Yi Translation

"It also says: 'In ages to come, evil people [who hear it] will destroy the Dharma and fall into evil paths; for those who aspire to the buddha-way, widely praise the path of the One Vehicle.' This means choosing one's hearers; only then can it be taught.

Chinese Source

然末代施化復未知根。亦可如安樂行中但以大答。亦可如不輕喜根而強毒之。故首楞嚴中聞生謗者後終獲益。如人倒地還從地起。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Yet in teaching in the latter age, one cannot know people's capacities. So one may, as in the chapter on peaceful practices, answer only with the great teaching. Or one may, like Never Disparaging and Joyful Root, forcefully administer the poison. That is why, in the Śūraṅgama, those who heard and slandered nonetheless benefited in the end, like a person who falls to the ground and rises again by pushing on the ground.

Chinese Source

應運大悲無惱他說。子應從容觀時進否。將獲彼意順佛本懷。

Ru-Yi Translation

"You should act with great compassion and teach without troubling others. Calmly observe the times and whether your hearers can advance, so that you grasp their minds and accord with the Buddha's original intent.

Three Steps: Having, Pervading, Including

Chinese Source

若有眾生未稟教者。來至汝所先當語云。汝無始來唯有煩惱業苦而已。即此全是理性三因由未發心未曾加行。故性緣了同名正因。故云眾生皆有正性。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If beings who have not yet received the teaching come to you, first tell them: 'Since beginningless time you have had nothing but affliction, karma, and suffering. Yet these are wholly the three causes as principle-nature. Because you have not yet aroused the mind for awakening or applied yourself to practice, the conditioning and revealing causes in your nature are, for now, all called the correct cause.' That is why we say that all beings have the correct nature.

Chinese Source

既信己心有此性已。次示此性非內外遍虛空。同諸佛等法界。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Once they believe that their own mind has this nature, next show them that this nature is neither inside nor outside, that it pervades space, that it is the same as all buddhas, and that it is equal to the dharma-realm.

Chinese Source

既信遍已次示遍具。既同諸佛等於法界。故此遍性具諸佛之身。一身一切身。如諸佛之感土。一土一切土。身土相即身說土說。大小一多亦復如是。有彼性故故名有性。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Once they believe that it pervades, next show them that it pervades and includes. Since it is the same as all buddhas and equal to the dharma-realm, this pervading nature includes the bodies of all buddhas: one body is all bodies. Like the lands the buddhas bring forth, one land is all lands. Body and land are identical; the body preaches and the land preaches. So it is with great and small, one and many. Because they have that nature, they are said to have the nature.

What "Having Buddha-Nature" Does Not Mean

Chinese Source

若世人云。眾生唯有清淨之性。加修萬行為功用體。故至果時方有大用。此乃佛有眾生之性。不名眾生有佛性也。三無差別斯言有徵。寄言說者勿負斯教。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose people say that beings have only a pure nature, and that by adding the cultivation of the myriad practices it gains a working substance, so that only at the fruit does it have its great function. That would mean the Buddha has the nature of beings; it cannot be called beings having buddha-nature. 'The three are without distinction' — these words have their proof. I urge those who teach not to betray this teaching.

Chinese Source

若言眾生有正因性與法身等。不與報化等者。還成眾生與眾生等。何者若除報化猶是眾生。若言等於有報化之法身。其如法身非報化外。以是言之故須悉等。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose someone says that beings' nature as correct cause is equal to the Dharma-body but not to the reward and transformation bodies. That only makes beings equal to beings. Why? Remove the reward and transformation bodies, and what remains is still a being. If one says it is equal to a Dharma-body that has reward and transformation bodies, then, since the Dharma-body is not outside the reward and transformation bodies, it follows that it must be equal to them all.

Chinese Source

今此示有是示種性。示遍是示體量。示具是示體德。既示三已。次令緣於一體三寶發四弘誓。進受菩薩清淨律儀。一一緣向理性三因。修行填誓。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Here, showing that they have it is showing the seed-nature; showing that it pervades is showing the measure of its substance; showing that it includes is showing the virtues of its substance. Having shown these three, next have them take as their object the Three Jewels of one substance and make the four great vows, and then go on to receive the pure precepts of the bodhisattva. Each vow and precept is directed toward the three causes as principle-nature, and they practice to fulfill their vows.

Chinese Source

如向所聞種必相續。世世生處以人天身。佛會再聞而得解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Then, as with what you yourself have heard, the seed will surely continue. In life after life, born in human or heavenly bodies, they will hear it again in the assemblies of the buddhas and attain liberation.

Teaching Those Already on the Path

Chinese Source

若已稟方便教者。若聞若行若伏若斷。隨其所得點示體具。故經云。汝等所行是菩薩道。故法華中五章開權。一一但云是法皆為一佛乘。故眾生聞已皆得種智。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If they have already received the expedient teachings — whether they have only heard, or practiced, or suppressed the afflictions, or severed them — point out inherent inclusion according to what they have attained. That is why the sūtra says, 'What you are practicing is the bodhisattva path.' And in the Lotus, the five passages that open up the provisional each say only, 'These teachings are all for the sake of the One Buddha Vehicle.' Therefore beings, once they have heard, all attain the wisdom of all modes.

Chinese Source

散心講授者。隨宜設化。一種觀心者。從心示之。

Ru-Yi Translation

"When you lecture to those with scattered minds, teach as the occasion suits. For those who practice contemplating the mind, show it to them starting from the mind.

Chinese Source

若憚教生諍競者。應當語云聞已成種不敢輕汝。汝等行道皆當作佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If people shrink from the teaching and start disputes, you should tell them: 'Having heard it, the seed is already planted. I dare not belittle you. All of you who practice the path will become buddhas.'

Zhiyi's Verse of Humility

Chinese Source

故大師判教末云。佛法不思議。唯教相難解。二乘及菩薩。尚所不能測。何況諸凡夫。而欲判此事。譬如生盲人。分別日輪相。欲判虛空界。一切諸色像。而言了達者。畢竟無是事。是故有智者。各生慚愧心。自責無明暗。捨戲論諍競。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So at the end of his classification of the teachings, the Great Master said:

The Buddha's Dharma is inconceivable;
only the features of the teaching are hard to understand.
Even the two vehicles and the bodhisattvas
cannot fathom it,
how much less can ordinary beings
wish to judge this matter?
It is like one blind from birth
trying to discern the sun's disk,
or wishing to judge the realm of space
and all its forms and images,
and claiming to understand them completely:
this can never be.
Therefore those who are wise
each give rise to a sense of shame,
blame themselves for the darkness of ignorance,
and give up idle debate and dispute.

Chinese Source

大師親證判已尚自謙喻後輩。余今准此一家宗途。獎導於子。非師己見。子亦順教如是流行。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Great Master had realized it himself and made his classification, and still he spoke humbly to instruct those who came after. Now I follow the path of our school to encourage and guide you; I do not take my own views as my teacher. You too should follow the teaching and spread it in this way."

Waking from the Dream

Chinese Source

野客於是歡喜頂受。自爾永劫唯奉持之。所在宣弘不違尊命。歛容再拜安庠而出。忽然夢覺。問者答者。所問所答都無所得。

Ru-Yi Translation

The rustic guest then joyfully received this on the crown of his head: "From now on, for endless eons, I will uphold only this. Wherever I am, I will proclaim it and never go against your command." Composing his face, he bowed twice and went out calmly. Suddenly I woke from the dream. The one who asked and the one who answered, what was asked and what was answered — none of it could be found.

Chinese Source

金剛錍(竟)

Ru-Yi Translation

The Vajra Scalpel (end).

Reading Notes for Part 5

Translator's notes

The two Lotus Sūtra quotations. Both come from the verses of chapter 2, "Expedient Means." Zhanran abridges them; the words in brackets supply what he leaves out.

Peaceful practices (安樂行). Chapter 14 of the Lotus Sūtra advises the teacher in the latter age to answer questions only with the Great Vehicle, gently and without dispute.

Never Disparaging and Joyful Root; "the poison." Never Disparaging (常不輕) is the bodhisattva of Lotus Sūtra chapter 20, who bowed to everyone, saying "You will all become buddhas," even when they beat him. Joyful Root (喜根) is the bodhisattva whose bold teaching, in the Sūtra on the Non-Activity of All Dharmas (諸法無行經, T650), caused another monk to slander him and fall into hell, yet planted a seed that led to his eventual awakening. Tiantai calls this "the poison drum" (毒鼓): a teaching that harms on first hearing but still creates a connection with the Dharma. "The Śūraṅgama" is the Śūraṅgama-samādhi Sūtra (T642), not the later Śūraṅgama Sūtra. The Jingangpi lun siji (X932) explains this line with the story of Joyful Root and the monk Superior Intent (勝意), and says the Śūraṅgama is cited as proof: falling to the ground stands for falling into suffering through slander, and rising from the ground for the benefit that comes in the distant future.

"Grasp their minds" (將獲彼意). The text quoted in the Jingangpi lun siji reads 將護彼意, "so as to protect their feelings." Either reading fits the context.

The five passages (五章). The passages in Lotus Sūtra chapter 2 where the buddhas of the past, the future, the present, all buddhas in general, and Śākyamuni himself are said to teach expedients only for the sake of the One Buddha Vehicle.

Zhiyi's verse. These lines come from Zhiyi's Profound Meaning of the Vimalakīrti Sūtra (維摩經玄疏, T1777), at the close of its discussion of the features of the teachings. That text, in turn, refers readers to the Fahua xuanyi (translated on this site) for the full account of the four teachings.

"None of it could be found" (都無所得). The treatise ends where it began, in a dream, and the ending turns the whole debate back into emptiness: no questioner and no answerer can be grasped.
