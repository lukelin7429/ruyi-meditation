Waves and Water: Suchness That Follows Conditions

Having shown from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra that the correct cause of buddha-nature pervades like space, Zhanran now explains why the sūtras seem to say otherwise. Many statements are provisional: they begin from beings' attachment to a self and so speak of buddha-nature as located in them. The real teaching, found throughout the Buddha's lifetime, is that the mind inherently includes everything, so that a single mote of dust and a single mind share the mind-nature of all beings and buddhas. The center of this part is the simile of waves and water: suchness is unchanging and so is all dharmas; it follows conditions and so all dharmas are suchness. When the guest repeats a popular claim that suchness in the insentient is only "dharma-nature" and not "buddha-nature," Zhanran answers that the two names have one substance, citing the Avataṃsaka and the Buddha-Nature Treatise. He ends by saying that those who confine buddha-nature to the bodies of sentient beings fall short even of non-Buddhists and of the Lesser Vehicle.

Why the Provisional Teachings Limit the Causes

Chinese Source

客曰。何故權教不說緣了二因遍耶。余曰。眾生無始計我我所。從所計示未應說遍。涅槃經中帶權說實。故得以空譬正。未譬緣了。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "Why do the provisional teachings not say that the two causes, the conditioning and the revealing, pervade everything?" I said: "Since beginningless time, beings have clung to a self and to what belongs to a self. When the teaching starts from what they cling to, it is not yet the time to say that these causes pervade everything. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra teaches the real while still carrying the provisional along with it. So it could liken the correct cause to space, but it did not yet liken the conditioning and revealing causes to it.

Chinese Source

若教一向權則三因俱局。如別初心聞正亦局。藏性理性一切俱然。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If a teaching is wholly provisional, then all three causes are limited. For example, a beginner in the Distinct teaching, hearing of the correct cause, also takes it as limited. The same is true of the nature of the store, the nature of principle, and all the rest.

Chinese Source

所以博地聞無情無。依迷示迷云能造是。附權立性云所造非。

Ru-Yi Translation

"That is why ordinary people hear that the insentient lack it. Speaking from delusion to the deluded, the teaching says that what creates is buddha-nature; setting up the nature in accord with the provisional, it says that what is created is not.

The Sudden Teaching Runs Through the Buddha's Lifetime

Chinese Source

又復一代已多顯頓。如華嚴中依正不二。普賢普眼三無差別。大集染淨一切融通。淨名不思議毛孔含納。思益網明無非法界。般若諸法混同無二。法華本末實相皆如。涅槃唯防像末謬執。分正緣了別指方隅。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Moreover, the teachings of the Buddha's whole lifetime already reveal the sudden in many places. In the Avataṃsaka, the environment and the beings in it are not two; there is Samantabhadra's universal eye, and the three — mind, buddha, and beings — are without distinction. In the Great Collection, the defiled and the pure all interpenetrate. In the Vimalakīrti, the inconceivable is contained within a single pore. In the Viśeṣacinti, Jālinīprabha says that nothing is not the dharma-realm. In the Prajñāpāramitā, all dharmas merge as one without duality. In the Lotus, the true aspect, from root to branch, is entirely thus. The Nirvāṇa only guards against the mistaken attachments of the Semblance and Final Ages, separating the correct, conditioning, and revealing causes and pointing out where each one belongs.

Chinese Source

若執實迷權尚失於實。執權迷實則權實俱迷。驗子尚昧小乘由心。故暗大教心外無境。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If one clings to the real and is confused about the provisional, one still loses the real. If one clings to the provisional and is confused about the real, one is confused about both. This shows that you are unclear even about the Lesser Vehicle's teaching that things arise from the mind, and so you are in the dark about the great teaching that there is no object outside the mind."

Provisional and Real Within the Nirvāṇa Sūtra

Chinese Source

客曰。涅槃豈唯兼帶說耶。余曰。約部通云一切兼帶。部中品內或實或權。如申迦葉難。別為末代一機而已。則權實並明。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "Is the Nirvāṇa only a teaching that carries the provisional along with it?" I said: "Speaking of the division of the canon as a whole, one may say that all of it carries the provisional along. But within the division, chapter by chapter, some passages are real and some provisional. Setting out Kāśyapa's objection, for example, was only for one particular capacity of the latter age. So both the provisional and the real are made clear.

Chinese Source

若一向權。如恒河中七種眾生。若一向實。如三點二鳥三慈十德等。他皆準知不可具述。

Ru-Yi Translation

"What is wholly provisional is, for example, the seven kinds of beings in the Ganges. What is wholly real is, for example, the three dots, the two birds, the three kinds of compassion, the ten virtues, and so on. Other cases can be judged in the same way; they cannot all be described here.

Chinese Source

如云色常。色言豈不收於一切依正。何故制空令局限耶。此世人不知教之權實。

Ru-Yi Translation

"When the sūtra says 'form is permanent,' does the word 'form' not include every environment and every being? Why restrict space and make it limited? This is people today not knowing the provisional and the real in the teachings.

Chinese Source

如二乘人處處聞大。尚至法華方信己性。悔來至此財非己有。此豈非子不知父性耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

"It is like the people of the two vehicles. They heard the great teaching everywhere, yet only at the Lotus did they believe in their own nature. Until they came to it, they regretted that the wealth was not their own. Is this not the son not knowing his father's nature?

Chinese Source

聞開權已。方云口生化生有分。故涅槃中猶恐未來一分有情不信己身有如來性及謂闡提未來永斷。示令知有及以不斷。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Only after hearing the provisional opened up did they say that they too had a share, as those born from the Buddha's mouth and born by transformation from the Dharma. So in the Nirvāṇa, the Buddha still feared that some beings in the future would not believe that they themselves have the tathāgata-nature, and would think that icchantikas are cut off forever. He showed them that they do have it and are not cut off.

Chinese Source

豈部內諸文全無頓耶。今搜求現未建立圓融。不弊性無。但困理壅故於性中點示體遍。

Ru-Yi Translation

"How could the passages in this division be entirely without the sudden? Now, searching out and establishing perfect interfusion for the present and the future, I do not say that the teaching has worn out or that the nature is absent. I am only distressed that principle is blocked, and so, within the nature, I point out that its substance pervades everything.

Chinese Source

傍遮。偏指清淨真如。尚失小真佛性安在。

Ru-Yi Translation

"In passing, I also rule this out: if one points one-sidedly to a pure suchness, one loses even the lesser truth. Where, then, would buddha-nature be?

Mind-Only and Inherent Inclusion

Chinese Source

他不見之空論無情性之有無。不曉一家立義大旨。故達唯心了體具者焉有異同。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Others, not seeing this, argue in vain over whether the insentient have the nature or not. They do not understand the main point of how our school establishes its teaching. For one who has penetrated mind-only and understood that the substance inherently includes everything, how could there be any difference?

Chinese Source

若不立唯心一切大教全為無用。若不許心具圓頓之理乃成徒施。信唯心具。復疑有無。則疑己心之有無也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If mind-only is not established, all the great teachings are entirely useless. If one does not allow that the mind includes everything, the principle of the perfect and sudden teaching is given in vain. If one believes that mind alone includes everything and yet doubts whether the insentient have the nature, then one doubts whether one's own mind exists.

Chinese Source

故知一塵一心即一切生佛之心性。何獨自心之有無耶。以共造故。以共變故。同化境故。同化事故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So we know that a single mote of dust and a single mind are the mind-nature of all beings and buddhas. Why ask only whether one's own mind has it? For they are created in common, transformed in common, the same field of teaching, and the same work of teaching.

Chinese Source

故世不知教之權實。以子不思佛性之名從何教立。無情之稱局在何文。已如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So the world does not know the provisional and the real in the teachings. This is because you have not considered from which teaching the name 'buddha-nature' is established, and in which texts the term 'insentient' is confined, as I said before.

Why I Said "The Insentient Have Buddha-Nature"

Chinese Source

余患世迷恒思點示。是故寱言無情有性。何謂點示。一者示迷元從性變。二者示性令其改迷。是故且云無情有性。

Ru-Yi Translation

"I grieve at the world's delusion and constantly think of how to point the way. That is why I murmured in my sleep, 'The insentient have buddha-nature.' What do I mean by pointing the way? First, to show that delusion has from the beginning arisen by transformation from the nature. Second, to show the nature so that people may turn from their delusion. That is why, for now, I say: the insentient have buddha-nature.

Chinese Source

若分大小。則隨緣不變之說出自大教。木石無心之語。生于小宗。子欲執小道而抗大逵者其猶螳蜋乎。何殊井蛙乎。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If one distinguishes the great and the small, the teaching that suchness follows conditions yet does not change comes from the great teaching, while the saying that wood and stone have no mind arises in the lesser schools. To hold on to the small path and resist the great highway is like the praying mantis raising its arms against a chariot. How is it different from a frog in a well?

The Simile of Waves and Water

Chinese Source

故子應知。萬法是真如。由不變故。真如是萬法。由隨緣故。子信無情無佛性者。豈非萬法無真如耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So you should know this: the ten thousand dharmas are suchness, because suchness does not change. Suchness is the ten thousand dharmas, because it follows conditions. If you believe the insentient lack buddha-nature, does that not mean the ten thousand dharmas lack suchness?

Chinese Source

故萬法之稱寧隔於纖塵。真如之體何專於彼我。是則無有無波之水。未有不濕之波。在濕詎間於混澄。為波自分於清濁。雖有清有濁。而一性無殊。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So how could the term 'the ten thousand dharmas' leave out even the finest speck of dust? How could the substance of suchness belong only to this person or that? It follows that there is no water without waves, and there has never been a wave that is not wet. As wetness, how could it be divided into murky and clear? It is as waves that it divides itself into clear and turbid. Though there is clear and there is turbid, the one nature does not differ.

Chinese Source

縱造正造依。依理終無異轍。若許隨緣不變。復云無情有無。豈非自語相違耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Even though the mind creates beings and creates their environment, in terms of principle there is in the end no separate track. If you accept that suchness follows conditions yet does not change, and then still ask whether the insentient have it or not, are you not contradicting yourself?

Chinese Source

故知。果地依正融通。並依眾生理本故也。此乃事理相對以說。若唯從理。只可云水本無波。必不得云波中無水。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So we know that, at the stage of fruition, the environment and the beings interpenetrate because both rest on the principle that is fundamental in beings. This is said by setting phenomena against principle. If one speaks only from principle, one may say only that water originally has no waves; one may never say that there is no water in the waves.

Chinese Source

如迷東為西。只可云東處無西。終不得云西處無東。若唯從迷說則波無水名。西失東稱。情性合譬思之可知。無情有無例之可見。

Ru-Yi Translation

"It is like mistaking east for west. One may say only that where east is, there is no west; one may never say that where 'west' is, there is no east. If one speaks only from delusion, then waves lose the name of water, and west loses the name of east. Match the sentient and the nature to these similes and think it over, and you will understand. By this example, whether the insentient have it or not can be seen."

Dharma-Nature and Buddha-Nature

Chinese Source

於是野客恭退吳跪而諮曰。波水之譬其理實然。僕曾聞人引大智度論。云真如在無情中但名法性。在有情內方名佛性。仁何故立佛性之名。

Ru-Yi Translation

At this the rustic guest stepped back respectfully, knelt, and asked: "The simile of waves and water is truly right in principle. But I once heard someone cite the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom as saying that suchness in the insentient is called only dharma-nature, and only within the sentient is it called buddha-nature. Why do you use the name 'buddha-nature'?"

Chinese Source

余曰。親曾委讀細撿論文都無此說。或恐謬引章疏之言世共傳之。汎為通之。此乃迷名而不知義。

Ru-Yi Translation

I said: "I have read that treatise thoroughly myself and examined its text closely, and there is no such statement anywhere in it. Perhaps someone mistakenly cited a line from a commentary, and the world has passed it along. To reconcile it broadly: this is being confused by names without knowing their meaning.

Chinese Source

法名不覺。佛名為覺。眾生雖本有不覺之理。而未曾有覺不覺智。故且分之令覺不覺。豈覺不覺不覺猶不覺耶。反謂所覺離能覺耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

"'Dharma' names what is unawakened; 'Buddha' names awakening. Although beings originally possess the principle that is unawakened, they have never had the wisdom that awakens to the unawakened. So, for now, the two are distinguished, in order that beings may awaken to the unawakened. Once one has awakened to the unawakened, is the unawakened still unawakened? Or would you say, on the contrary, that what is awakened to is separate from what awakens?"

Chinese Source

客曰。若爾至佛方會。凡離何乖。余曰。子為學佛。為學凡耶。理本無殊。凡謂之離。故示眾生令覺不覺。故覺不覺自會一如。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "If so, the two come together only at buddhahood. What is wrong with saying they are separate for ordinary beings?" I said: "Are you learning to be a buddha, or learning to be an ordinary being? In principle there is originally no difference; it is ordinary beings who call them separate. So beings are shown this so that they may awaken to the unawakened, and then awakening and the unawakened come together of themselves as one suchness.

Chinese Source

故知。覺無不覺不名佛性。不覺無覺法性不成。覺無不覺佛性寧立。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So we know: awakening without the unawakened is not called buddha-nature; the unawakened without awakening does not amount to dharma-nature; if awakening had nothing unawakened to awaken to, how could buddha-nature be established?

Chinese Source

是則無佛性之法性。容在小宗。即法性之佛性。方曰大教。故今問子。諸經論中。法界實際實相真性等。為同法性在無情中。為同真如分為兩派。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Thus a dharma-nature without buddha-nature may have a place in the lesser schools; only a buddha-nature that is one with dharma-nature can be called the great teaching. So now I ask you: in the sūtras and treatises, are the dharma-realm, the reality-limit, the true aspect, the true nature, and the rest like your dharma-nature, found in the insentient? Or are they like your suchness, split into two streams?

Chinese Source

若同真如。諸教不見無情法界及實際等。若在無情。但名法性非佛性者。何故華嚴須彌山頂偈讚品云。了知一切法。自性無所有。若能如是解。則見盧舍那。豈非諸法本有舍那之性耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If they are like suchness, then the teachings would nowhere speak of a dharma-realm or a reality-limit of the insentient. And if suchness in the insentient is called only dharma-nature and not buddha-nature, why does the chapter 'Verses of Praise on the Summit of Mount Sumeru' in the Avataṃsaka say: 'Know that all dharmas have no self-nature at all. One who can understand in this way sees Locana'? Does this not mean that all dharmas originally have the nature of Locana?

Chinese Source

又云。法性本空寂。無取亦無見。性空即是佛。不可得思量。又精進慧云。法性本清淨。如空無有相。此亦無所修。能見大牟尼。豈於無性又云無修能見牟尼。又真實慧云。一切法無相。是則真佛體。既真佛體在一切法。請子思之。當免迷教。及迷佛性之進否也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"It also says: 'Dharma-nature is originally empty and still; there is no grasping and no seeing. Emptiness of nature is itself the Buddha; it cannot be conceived.' Again, the bodhisattva Vīryaprajñā says: 'Dharma-nature is originally pure; like space, it has no marks. Here too there is nothing to cultivate, yet one can see the Great Sage.' Would the sūtra say, of something that lacks the nature, that without cultivation one can see the Sage? Again, Satyaprajñā says: 'All dharmas are without marks; this is the body of the true Buddha.' Since the body of the true Buddha is in all dharmas, please think it over. Then you will avoid confusion about the teachings and about the reach of buddha-nature.

Chinese Source

故真如隨緣即佛性隨緣。佛之一字即法佛也。故法佛與真如體一名異。故佛性論第一云。佛性者。即人法二空所顯真如。當知真如即佛性異名。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So suchness following conditions is buddha-nature following conditions. The single word 'buddha' here means the Dharma-buddha, and so the Dharma-buddha and suchness are one in substance and different in name. Thus the first fascicle of the Buddha-Nature Treatise says: 'Buddha-nature is the suchness revealed by the twofold emptiness of person and dharmas.' Know, then, that suchness is another name for buddha-nature.

Chinese Source

華嚴又云。眾生非眾生。二俱無真實。如是諸法性。實義俱非有。言眾生非眾生。豈非情與無情。二俱隨緣並皆不變。故俱非有。所以法界實際一切皆然。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Avataṃsaka also says: 'Beings and non-beings both lack true reality. Such is the nature of all dharmas: in the true sense, neither exists.' When it says 'beings and non-beings,' does it not mean the sentient and the insentient? Both follow conditions and both are unchanging; therefore neither exists as something fixed. The same is true of the dharma-realm, the reality-limit, and all the rest.

Chinese Source

故知法性之名不專無情中之真如也。以由世人共迷法相名異體一故也。然雖體同不無小別。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So we know that the name 'dharma-nature' does not refer only to suchness in the insentient. The confusion comes from people not understanding that, in the characteristics of dharmas, names differ while the substance is one. Yet although the substance is the same, there are slight differences.

Names With and Without "Nature"

Chinese Source

凡有性名者多在凡在理。如云佛性理性真性藏性實性等。無性名者多通凡聖因果事理。如云法界及實相等。如三昧陀羅尼波羅蜜等。則唯在於果。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Names that contain the word 'nature' mostly refer to ordinary beings and to principle: buddha-nature, the nature of principle, the true nature, the nature of the store, the real nature, and so on. Names without 'nature' mostly apply across the ordinary and the sage, cause and fruit, phenomena and principle: the dharma-realm, the true aspect, and so on. Names such as samādhi, dhāraṇī, and pāramitā belong only to the fruit.

Chinese Source

所以因名佛性等者。眾生實未成佛得理證真開藏。以煩惱生死是佛等性。示令修習名佛等性。而諸教之中諸名互立。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The reason the cause is called buddha-nature and so on is that beings have not actually become buddhas, attained principle, realized the true, or opened the store. Because affliction and birth-and-death are themselves the nature of buddha and the rest, this is pointed out to them so that they will cultivate it, and so it is called buddha-nature and the like. Across the teachings, these names are used interchangeably.

Chinese Source

涅槃經中多云佛性者。佛是果人。言一切眾生皆有果人之性。故偏言之。世人迷故而不從果。云眾生有故失體遍。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The Nirvāṇa Sūtra mostly says 'buddha-nature' because a buddha is the person of the fruit; it is saying that all beings have the nature of the person of the fruit, and so it uses this name in particular. People today, being confused, do not take the name from the fruit. They say only that beings have it, and so they lose sight of its pervading substance.

Chinese Source

又云遍者。以由煩惱心性體遍。云佛性遍。故知。不識佛性遍者。良由不知煩惱性遍故。唯心之言豈唯真心。子尚不知煩惱心遍。安能了知生死色遍。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Further, we call it pervasive because the substance of the afflicted mind's nature pervades everything; therefore we say buddha-nature pervades everything. So we know that those who do not recognize that buddha-nature pervades everything simply do not know that the nature of affliction pervades everything. Does the phrase 'mind-only' refer only to the true mind? If you do not yet know that the afflicted mind pervades everything, how could you understand that the form of birth and death pervades everything?

Chinese Source

色何以遍。色即心故。何者。依報共造正報別造。豈信共遍不信別遍耶。能造所造既是唯心。心體不可局方所故。所以十方佛土皆有眾生理性心種。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Why does form pervade everything? Because form is mind. How so? The environment, as a karmic result, is created in common; the body, as a karmic result, is created individually. Would you believe that what is common pervades and not that what is individual pervades? Since both what creates and what is created are mind-only, and the substance of mind cannot be confined to any place, every buddha-land in the ten directions contains the principle-nature and mind-seed of beings.

Chinese Source

以性喻空。具如涅槃一十復次。故知。不曉大小教門名體同異。此是學釋教者之大患也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The nature is likened to space, as fully set out in the ten 'furthermores' of the Nirvāṇa. So we know that failing to understand how names and substance are the same or different across the gates of the great and the small teachings is the great affliction of those who study the Buddha's teaching.

Chinese Source

故身子云。我等同入法性。及亦得解脫等。子初不達余之義旨。故聞之驚駭。為子申已理合釋然。

Ru-Yi Translation

"That is why Śāriputra said, 'We have entered the same dharma-nature,' and 'We too have attained liberation,' and so on. At first you did not grasp what I meant, and so you were alarmed when you heard it. Now that I have explained it for you, it should be clear by reason.

Falling Short of Non-Buddhists and the Lesser Vehicle

Chinese Source

故知。世人局我遮那唯陰質內。而直云諸法是無情者。則有二種不如外道。外道尚云我大色小我遍虛空。又外道猶計眾塵所成。亦不直云無情而已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"So we know that people today who confine our Vairocana to the inside of the bodily aggregates and flatly say that all dharmas are insentient fall short of non-Buddhists in two ways. Non-Buddhists at least say that the self is great and form is small, and that the self pervades space. And non-Buddhists still hold that things are formed from many particles; they do not simply call them insentient and stop there.

Chinese Source

又有二種不如小乘。小乘尚云猶業力造造遍三界。又小乘猶知諸法無常。亦不直云無情而已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"They also fall short of the Lesser Vehicle in two ways. The Lesser Vehicle at least says that things are created by the force of karma, and that this creation pervades the three realms. And the Lesser Vehicle still knows that all dharmas are impermanent; it does not simply call them insentient and stop there.

Chinese Source

又有二種不如共乘。共乘尚知造心幻化幻遍三界。又知諸法體性即真。若次第乘故非所擬。子聞是已。亦合薄知教法權實佛性進否。

Ru-Yi Translation

"They also fall short of the Shared Vehicle in two ways. The Shared Vehicle at least knows that the creating mind is an illusory transformation, and that the illusion pervades the three realms; and it knows that the essential nature of all dharmas is itself the true. As for the Gradual Vehicle, there is no comparing them to it at all. Having heard this, you should now know a little about the provisional and the real in the teachings and the reach of buddha-nature."

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

Following conditions yet unchanging (隨緣不變). This phrase comes from the Awakening of Faith tradition and was central to Huayan thought. Zhanran turns it into an argument for his position: if suchness follows conditions, it becomes every dharma, including walls and stones; if it is unchanging, no dharma is ever without it. The waves-and-water simile states both sides at once.

Environment and beings (依正). 依報 is the environment that results from karma (lands, mountains, tiles, and stones); 正報 is the body and mind of the being who experiences it. "Created in common" and "created individually" refer to these two kinds of karmic result.

The sutras cited. The Great Collection is the Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra; the Viśeṣacinti (思益) is the Viśeṣacintibrahma-paripṛcchā, whose questioner Jālinīprabha (網明) is a bodhisattva. Vīryaprajñā (精進慧) and Satyaprajñā (真實慧) are bodhisattvas who speak verses in the same Avataṃsaka chapter. The Buddha-Nature Treatise is translated on this site.

The three dots, the two birds, the three kinds of compassion, the ten virtues. These are images from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra that Zhanran treats as wholly real teaching: the letter i (three dots) for the three virtues, the pair of birds that always fly together for the permanent and the impermanent, compassion directed toward beings, toward dharmas, and without object, and the ten virtues of the bodhisattva who practices the sūtra. The seven kinds of beings in the Ganges, by contrast, are a graded simile for different capacities and so are counted as provisional.

"Not worn out" (不弊). The Jingangpi lun siji (金剛錍論私記, X932), an early commentary, explains 不弊 as meaning that the essence of the Buddha's teaching is naturally permanent and never takes a different track; the problem is only that principle is blocked for those who are confused.

Śāriputra's words. "We have entered the same dharma-nature" comes from Śāriputra's lament in the Lotus Sūtra, chapter 3, before he receives his prediction.

Shared Vehicle and Gradual Vehicle. In the Tiantai fourfold teaching, the Shared teaching (通教) is shared by the three vehicles; the Distinct teaching (別教) is here called the "Gradual Vehicle" (次第乘) because it proceeds step by step.
