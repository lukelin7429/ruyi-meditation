How the Afflictions Are the Path: Lion's Stride's Questions and the Unmoving Lineage

The Sūtra on the Non-Activity of All Dharmas (T0650), translated by Kumārajīva in the early fifth century, is one of the boldest Mahāyāna statements of nonduality. This first fascicle opens on Vulture Peak with the bodhisattva Lion's Stride asking, in verse, how wrong views, craving, and anger can themselves be the path, and how nirvāṇa can be no different from worldly things. The Buddha at first refuses, saying the teaching is too dangerous for beginners, then answers in a long series of verses: whoever wishes to become a buddha should not destroy greed, for greed's nature is the path. The verses turn sharply against bodhisattvas who quote emptiness while quarreling, judging others, and courting donors. The Buddha then tells a story from a distant age: a strict monk named Possessing Deportment condemned the Dharma-master Pure Deportment for going among villagers, and for that subtle impure thought suffered for countless kalpas — and that monk, the Buddha says, was himself. In the second half Mañjuśrī asks how the four noble truths, the four foundations of mindfulness, the eightfold path, the five faculties, and the seven factors of bodhi are truly to be contemplated, and each is answered in terms of emptiness and non-arising. The fascicle ends with the "Dharma-gate of the unmoving lineage," a long question-and-answer litany in which greed, anger, delusion, the aggregates, the sense objects, the elements, and the Three Jewels are each declared to be the lineage or the unmoving mark.

The Buddha Holds Back; Lion's Stride Asks Again

Chinese Source

爾時世尊讚師子遊步菩薩摩訶薩言：「善哉，善哉！善男子！汝所問者甚為希有，一切世間之所難信。善男子！止止勿問。所以者何？新發意菩薩，於此空見、無相見、無作見、無生見、無所有見、無取相見、佛見、菩提見，所不能及。善男子！如此法者不應在新學菩薩前說。何以故？若聞是法，或斷善業，於佛道中則行邪道；若墮斷滅、若墮計常，不知如來以何方便隨宜所說？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Then the World-Honored One praised the bodhisattva-mahāsattva Lion's Stride, saying: "Excellent, excellent, good man! What you have asked is most rare; all the world finds it hard to believe. Good man, stop, stop — do not ask. Why? Bodhisattvas who have newly resolved [on bodhi] cannot reach this view of emptiness, view of signlessness, view of wishlessness, view of non-arising, view of nothingness, view of not grasping marks, view of the Buddha, view of bodhi. Good man, a Dharma like this should not be spoken in front of bodhisattvas who are new learners. Why? If they hear this Dharma, some may cut off their good karma and walk a wrong path within the Buddha-path; they may fall into annihilation or fall into clinging to permanence, not knowing by what skillful means the Tathāgata speaks as is fitting."

Ru-Yi Translation

"In the age to come there will be bodhisattvas with the view of emptiness, the view of signlessness, the view of wishlessness, the view of non-arising, the view of nothingness, the view of not grasping marks, the view of the Buddha, and the view of bodhi, who discriminate, 'This is emptiness, this is signless and wishless,' who are fond of constant praise and study and diligent in affairs, who delight in and are attached to words, take eloquent argument as excellent, and prize fame and gain. When such people hear the Tathāgata speak this Dharma that is without letters and ultimately pure, they will abandon these views. These bodhisattvas, according to what beings are able to believe and understand, teach the Dharma by the power of skillful means. Though they speak of having few desires and being content, they do not take it as supreme; though they speak of the sūtras and precepts, they do not take them as supreme either; though they speak of the faults of living in a crowd, they also know that all dharmas have the mark of separation; though they always praise dwelling alone, away from the bustle, they do not take it as supreme. Though they praise arousing the bodhi-mind, they know that the nature of mind is itself bodhi; though they praise the Great Vehicle sūtras, they know that all dharmas are of the great mark; though they speak of the bodhisattva path, they do not discriminate arhats, pratyekabuddhas, and buddhas; though they praise giving, they penetrate the mark of the equality of giving; though they praise keeping the precepts, they understand that all dharmas alike are the nature of precepts; though they praise patience, they know that all dharmas have the mark of no arising, no ceasing, and no ending; though they praise vigor, they know that all dharmas have the mark of not setting out and not going; though they praise dhyāna-concentration in all sorts of ways, they know that all dharmas have the mark of constant concentration; though they praise wisdom in all sorts of ways, they understand the real nature of wisdom. Though they speak of the faults of greed, they do not see any dharma that can be craved; though they speak of the faults of anger, they do not see any dharma that can be hated; though they speak of the faults of delusion, they know that all dharmas are without delusion and without obstruction; though they show beings the fearful suffering of falling into the three evil destinies, they do not find the marks of hells, hungry ghosts, or animals. Such bodhisattvas, though they teach the Dharma by the power of skillful means according to what beings are able to believe and understand, themselves believe and understand the Dharma of one mark, namely: emptiness, signlessness, wishlessness, non-arising, nothingness, and not grasping marks.

Ru-Yi Translation

Ru-Yi Translation

Those who call themselves bodhisattvas / Further speak like this: / 'I have kindness and compassion for all; / I will become a buddha and save beings.'

How to Contemplate the Four Noble Truths

Chinese Source

Then the Dharma-prince Mañjuśrī said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One, how, then, should one contemplate the four noble truths?"

The Sūtra on the Non-Activity of All Dharmas, Fascicle One

Reading Notes for Fascicle 1

Translator's notes

Title and 行. 無行 is rendered non-activity in the title, as fixed for this project. Within the text 行 has several senses, kept apart: 行 as practice ("practice," "walk the path"); 諸行 conditioned things, "formations"; 心行 "the activity of mind"; 行處 "domain of activity" (gocara). In right vigor, 以無所行故 is rendered "because there is no activity," which echoes the title; alternatively: "because there is nothing practiced."

Bodhisattva names. All twenty names in the opening list are translated. The last, 妙德, "Wondrous Virtue," is a known Chinese rendering of the name Mañjuśrī; whether this text means Mañjuśrī here is not stated, so the name is translated as it stands.

Teacher and monk names. 高須彌山王如來 the Buddha High Sumeru Mountain King; land 金焰明 Golden Flame Brightness (金焰國 "the land of Golden Flame"); 淨威儀 Pure Deportment; 有威儀 Possessing Deportment; 淨明佛 the Buddha Pure Brightness; 然燈佛 Dīpaṃkara. In Jñānagupta's parallel (T0651) the two monks are called 淨善行 and 善行意, and the buddha 迷留上王 — mentioned for comparison only.

種性 / 種子. 種性 is rendered "lineage" (gotra) throughout. In the opening exchanges of the unmoving-lineage section the Buddha's answer twice ends "是名種子" ("this is called the seed") while Mañjuśrī's question asks about 種性; the translation keeps "seed" where the text has 種子. T0651 uses 種子句 throughout for this term, so 種子 may be a variant rather than an error; not resolved.

不動. "Unmoving." T0651 renders the same term 雞羅句, a transcription apparently of a word meaning "stake" or "pillar" (cf. the image of Indra's banner with deep roots); not verified further.

道法御. One of the ten epithets, in place of the usual 調御丈夫 ("Tamer of Persons"); rendered literally, "Tamer by the Dharma of the Path." T0651 has 調御丈夫 at this point.

除菩提分. "The bodhi-factor of removal." This is the factor usually called 猗 or 輕安 (praśrabdhi, pliancy); T0651 calls it 止息覺分. Rendered literally.

無學. "Beyond training" (aśaikṣa) in both places: the verse "無學無羅漢" ("no one beyond training and no arhat") and the closing "一切法無學" ("all dharmas being beyond training"), where it is followed by 不應學 ("should not be trained in"). The same English is kept so the play on 學 is visible.

無作. "Wishlessness" only where it stands with emptiness and signlessness as the three gates (the eight views, the tree-sounds, the verse 無空無無相，亦無有無作); "no action" in the verse 無作無非作.

忍. "Patience" for 忍辱 and the pāramitā; "patient acceptance" for 無生忍, 無生法忍, 柔順法忍 (as in Parts 2–3), "acceptance" for 無上忍, and the verse 是忍是非忍, which follows 無上忍. In 知瞋忍同相 ("anger and patience share the same mark") it is patience.

緣. In the meditative passages 緣 is "object" (ālambana): 緣性離, 不繫念於緣, 緣相, 無緣. 因緣 is "causes and conditions." 無緣無順 is rendered "without objects and without accordance"; 順 is unclear.

離. "Apart" is used for 離 as a predicate ("its nature is itself apart"); 遠離相 is "the mark of separation."

焰 / 野馬. 焰 "mirage" (in the list of similes); 野馬 "heat shimmer," to keep the two words apart.

外道非見者. Rendered "the non-Buddhists, who do not see." T0651's parallel speaks of "non-Buddhists of evil intent" (惡意諸外道); alternatively: "the non-Buddhists of wrong views."

若得則不有. "If something were obtained, then it would not exist." Alternatively: "if there were attainment, it would not be [true attainment]."

名字非名字. "Name and non-name," on the pattern of 戒非戒一相 two stanzas earlier. Alternatively: "names are not names."

我心多憍慢. Rendered "their minds of 'I' are full of arrogance." The 我 may instead be read as the hypocrite's own voice continuing ("my mind is full of arrogance"), but that makes little sense in context.

佛法性亦然. "The nature of the Buddha-dharma is likewise." Alternatively: "the Buddha, the Dharma, and [their] nature are likewise."

好常讚學勤於事業. Rendered "fond of constant praise and study and diligent in affairs"; the phrasing is compressed and could also be divided "fond of always praising study."

離數無數入平等數. "Apart from number and no-number and enter the number of equality." 數 may also mean mental factors (心數); the sentence is obscure. T0651 has 諸數已離無數過數.

斷行處. "The domain of activity of cutting off." T0651 has 諸境界斷 ("all domains are cut off"); alternatively: "the domain where activity is cut off."

分別其實無定無生. "When discriminated, in reality they are without fixity and without arising." Alternatively: "the discriminated [heat] in reality has no fixity and no arising."

Devadatta's thirty-two marks. The text says Devadatta achieved the thirty-two marks; T0651 says thirty (三十相), which matches the usual account. Kept as written.

Numbers. Compound numbers such as 八十百千萬億那由他 are rendered unit by unit ("eighty hundred-thousand-myriad-koṭi nayutas"). Where this text has ninety-two thousand yakṣas arousing the mind of bodhi, T0651 has thirty-six thousand nāgas; noted, not changed.

重檐. "Heavy burden"; the text has 檐 (eaves) for 擔 (burden). Probably a graphic variant; T0651 has 重擔.

Subhūti. The remark that Subhūti "does not come to bow at the Buddha's feet" is given without explanation in the text; no source story was identified in this range.

天帝之幢. "The banner of the Lord of the Gods" (Śakra); the image is of a pillar or banner deeply rooted and unshakable.
