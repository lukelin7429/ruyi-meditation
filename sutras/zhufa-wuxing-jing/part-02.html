Mañjuśrī's Unmoving Marks and the Dharma-Gate of Sounds

The second fascicle opens with Mañjuśrī offering his own account of "the unmoving mark." In a series of short exchanges with the Buddha, he makes statements that sound outrageous and are meant to: all beings have already attained bodhi, all beings are the site of awakening, all buddhas are accomplished in greed, anger, and delusion, all buddhas hold wrong views, and they attain supreme awakening while abiding in the four inversions, the five hindrances, the five desires, and the three poisons. Each statement is followed by the Buddha's question, "Why is this called the unmoving mark?", and Mañjuśrī's answer, which turns on the claim that the true nature of greed, anger, and delusion cannot be found and is the same as that of awakening. Ten thousand gods praise him; he refuses their praise and calls himself an ordinary person, a non-Buddhist, and one who practices wrongly. The Buddha then teaches the bodhisattva Flower-Play Wisdom the "dharma-gate of the wisdom of entering sounds": a bodhisattva who prefers the sound of virtue to the sound of vice, or who belittles other bodhisattvas, is not learning the Buddha-dharma. Finally, asked to tell of his own past obstacles, Mañjuśrī begins the story of the buddha Lion's Roar Drum-Sound King, which continues in the next part.

"Cutting Off Every Unwholesome Dharma"

Chinese Source

爾時佛告文殊師利法王子：「若有人問汝：『斷一切不善法、成就一切善法，名為如來。』汝云何答？」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Buddha said to the Dharma-prince Mañjuśrī: "If someone asked you about 'cutting off every unwholesome dharma and accomplishing every wholesome dharma is called the Tathāgata,' how would you answer?"

Chinese Source

文殊師利言：「世尊！若有人問我，斷一切不善法、成就一切善法名為如來者，我當如是答：『善男子！汝先當親近善知識修集善道，於法無所合無所散，勿取勿捨，莫緣莫求，勿舉勿下，莫求莫覓勿願，勿分別諸法是上是中是下，然後當知，不可思議行處、無行處、斷行處、佛所行處。』」

Ru-Yi Translation

Mañjuśrī said: "World-Honored One, if someone asked me about 'cutting off every unwholesome dharma and accomplishing every wholesome dharma is called the Tathāgata,' I would answer like this: 'Good man, you should first draw near to a good friend and cultivate and gather the good path. With regard to dharmas, join nothing and scatter nothing; do not grasp, do not let go; do not take as object, do not seek; do not raise up, do not put down; do not seek, do not search, do not wish; do not discriminate dharmas as high, middle, or low. Only then will you know the inconceivable place of practice, the place of no practice, the place where practice is cut off, the place where the Buddha practices.'"

Chinese Source

佛告文殊師利：「汝如是答者，為答何義？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Mañjuśrī Refuses the Gods' Praise

Chinese Source

說是語時，虛空中萬天子，以天青黃赤白蓮華散佛及文殊師利上，皆下禮佛及文殊師利足，而作是言：「世尊！文殊師利名為無礙尸利，文殊師利名為不二尸利、名為無餘尸利、名為無所有尸利、名為如尸利、法性尸利、實際尸利、第一尸利、上尸利、無上尸利。」

Ru-Yi Translation

As these words were spoken, ten thousand gods in the sky scattered heavenly blue, yellow, red, and white lotus flowers over the Buddha and Mañjuśrī; all came down and bowed at the feet of the Buddha and Mañjuśrī, and said: "World-Honored One, Mañjuśrī is called Unobstructed Śrī; Mañjuśrī is called Nondual Śrī, called Śrī Without Remainder, called Śrī Who Has Nothing, called Suchness Śrī, Dharma-Nature Śrī, Limit-of-Reality Śrī, Foremost Śrī, Supreme Śrī, Unsurpassed Śrī."

Chinese Source

文殊師利語諸天子言：「止止。諸天子！汝等勿取相分別，我不見諸法是上是中是下，不如汝說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Mañjuśrī said to the gods: "Stop, stop! Gods, do not grasp marks and discriminate. I do not see dharmas as high, middle, or low; it is not as you say."

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Mañjuśrī said: "As for me, I am Greed Śrī, Anger Śrī, Delusion Śrī; that is why I am named Mañjuśrī. Gods, I do not go beyond greed, anger, and delusion. Ordinary people discriminate dharmas and seek to pass beyond, to go out, to arrive, to reach. For bodhisattvas, there is in dharmas no passing beyond, no going out, no arriving, no reaching."

Reading Notes for Fascicle 2

Translator's notes

The unmoving mark (不動相). Fascicle one has the Buddha teaching on what does not move; here Mañjuśrī offers his own list. Each statement is deliberately shocking, and each is grounded in the same point: when the nature of a thing is sought, it cannot be found, and in that unfindability all things are equal.

"Greed is itself bodhi" (貪欲即是菩提). The translation keeps the statement as direct as the Chinese. Zhiyi cites this sūtra repeatedly in the Mohe Zhiguan for the teaching that "greed is itself the path" (貪欲即是道); the verse he quotes appears in Part 3, where the received text reads "greed is nirvāṇa."

"Because there is no discrimination between tormenting and compassion" (以惱悲無分別故). 惱 may mean causing distress, the opposite of compassion. Alternatively: "because there is no discrimination between distress and compassion."

"All buddhas are wrong views" (一切諸佛皆是邪見). The Chinese says "are," not "have." The parallel translation T0651 may be compared for this and the surrounding passage.

"The place of practice … the place where the Buddha practices" (不可思議行處、無行處、斷行處、佛所行處). The four phrases may be read as one inconceivable place described four ways, or as four places; the English keeps the list.

Śrī (尸利). 文殊師利 transcribes Mañjuśrī, "gentle glory"; the gods play on the second half, śrī, "glory," calling him "Unobstructed Śrī" and so on, and Mañjuśrī answers by calling himself "Greed Śrī." The pun is kept by leaving śrī untranslated.

"Passing beyond, going out, arriving, reaching" (過出至到). Four verbs of progress along a path; they are kept as four separate words.

Flower-Play Wisdom (華戲慧). A bodhisattva named only in this sūtra; the name is translated by meaning.

"Divine son" (天子). In the exchange on sounds, Mañjuśrī addresses Flower-Play Wisdom as "divine son." The closing assembly (Part 3) names him 華戲慧天子, so the bodhisattva Flower-Play Wisdom is himself a god; the two titles refer to the same figure.

The eight worldly dharmas (八法). Gain and loss, defamation and fame, praise and blame, suffering and pleasure.

"White-robed" (白衣). Laypeople, from the white clothing worn by householders in India.

"Three times each day and three times each night." The Chinese 晝夜各三時 means six periods in all.

The ten epithets of a buddha. Translated in full as listed.

Lion's Roar Drum-Sound King (師子吼鼓音王). The story of this buddha and of the monks Root of Joy (喜根) and Superior Intent (勝意) continues in Part 3.
