Zongxiao's Preface, the Chronological Record, the Bodhisattva Precept Rite, and the Release of Life

The Record of the Teaching and Practice of the Venerable of Siming (T1937) was compiled in 1202 by Zongxiao of Shizhi, who gathered "barely a hundred-odd pieces" of Siming Zhili's (960–1028) shorter writings, together with documents about him, into seven fascicles. This first part opens with Zongxiao's preface, which places Zhili in the line from Zhiyi through Yitong of Baoyun and explains the title: in the Tiantai school, practice is established by relying on the teaching, and the teaching is revealed through practice. A year-by-year chronology follows, quoting the stūpa inscription, the True Record, and the Record of Conduct and Deeds: Zhili's childhood vocation, his study under Yitong, the rebuilding of Baoen (later Yanqing) Cloister, the rain-prayer with Zunshi, the questions from Japan, the vow to burn his body, imperial honors, and his death while calling Amitābha's name. The rest of the part gives three of Zhili's working texts: a twelve-section rite for conferring the bodhisattva precepts on monastics and lay people, the founding statement of his ten-thousand-member society for recollecting the Buddha (with a later postscript), two documents entrusting lecture seats to disciples, and the liturgy for releasing captive fish and birds, which confers the refuges, Jewel Victory Buddha's name, and the twelve links on the animals before they are set free.

Ru-Yi Translation

People have received it down to the present day. In addition there were the rules he set for the various repentances, the edicts of two courts, the inscriptions on steles for building monasteries, and so on; his disciple Chan Master Zhang'an collected them into a book called The Hundred Records of Guoqing, which by the special favor of Emperor Renzong was granted entry into the great canon and circulates together with the Buddha's scriptures and pronouncements; by this point almost nothing has been left out. Yet the way of our patriarch, which transformed and covered sixty prefectures, flourished in the Sui and Tang and declined in the Five Dynasties. The Dharma does not remain blocked forever; the perfected person appears to benefit [the world]. Ru-Yi Translation

Luoxi (that is, Dharma Master Xiji of Luoxi Cloister on Mount Tiantai), and fully obtained the way of the Tiantai school. When he wished again to cross the sea, the commander Lord Qian firmly kept him (Qian's personal name was Weizhi; he was the son of the King of Wuyue and at that time administered Mingzhou), and so he transmitted [the teaching] widely at Baoyun in Ming (Baoyun was a monastery made from the residence that Gu Chenghui, transport commissioner of Fuzhou, donated to house the master). His Dharma seat opened greatly, and he gained two outstanding disciples who established the house: one called Master Fazhi (personal name Zhili), and one called Master Ciyun (personal name Zunshi). Fazhi presided over the place of practice at Yanqing and restored this teaching; he was known in his time as the Venerable of Siming. Ciyun established the Dharma seat at Lingshan, setting understanding and practice side by side, and was known in the world as the Repentance Master of Tianzhu. Ciyun was born in the second year of Qiande and died at the beginning of Mingdao, a span of a hundred and twenty years. His fifth-generation descendant, Master (Huiguan), gathered his surviving writings into the Three Collections of the Golden Garden, which circulate in the world.

Ru-Yi Translation

Fazhi was born when the era was changed to Jianlong and died in the sixth year of Tiansheng; from then until now it has already been a hundred and seventy-five years. The notes and sub-commentaries he composed run to more than three hundred thousand words, and students all take them as their authority. There were also chapters on the meaning of the teaching, questions and answers resolving objections, letters exchanged with great Confucians and eminent monks, and so on, but of the broken slips and remnant pieces, those that survive are scattered and lost. Alas! For forty years the master lectured and performed repentances with a single mind, transmitting and upholding [the teaching], his side never touching a bed. How could the writings he made in response to circumstances be counted? Even if they were carved on wood and stone or written on bamboo and silk, it would be only one in ten million. I (Zongxiao), a later student without merit, have unworthily taken part in the halls of the teaching. Looking at the words the master handed down, even single pieces and stray words are all the Dharma of the Śākya family; how could they be left to be overgrown and lost?

Ru-Yi Translation

So I searched for them with great effort and obtained barely a hundred-odd pieces, which I arranged and ordered by kind and divided into seven fascicles, naming it Record of the Teaching and Practice of the Venerable of Siming. Siming is where he was born, attained the path, and turned the Dharma-wheel; the world treasures him as a person, and so all call him by this name. In the doors and windows of the Tiantai school, there is none that does not establish practice by relying on the teaching and reveal the teaching by means of practice; may the two roads of teaching and practice become the single track of cultivation. The master once exhorted the assembly, saying: "Whether I go out or stay in, whether I speak or am silent, I have never failed to take the purport of teaching and contemplation as my clothing and food, as my staff and armrest." Now that the record takes "teaching and practice" as its name, this too is the Venerable's intent. Great indeed! Now that this text is complete, I wish it to circulate like the legs of a tripod together with The Hundred Records of Guoqing and Tianzhu's Golden Garden, to be handed down to later generations so that together we may look toward the way of the patriarchs. As for what is not yet complete, if later worthies continue it according to what they see and hear, that will be complete in goodness and beauty. Respectfully prefaced by the bhikṣu (Zongxiao) on the sixteenth day of the twelfth month of the second year of Jiatai, the year renxu [1202].

Chronological Record of the Venerable

Ru-Yi Translation

Emperor Taizu of the Great Song first ascended the precious throne and changed the seventh year of Xiande of the Zhou to the first year of Jianlong, gengshen [960]. In the first month of this year Taizu received the abdication of Emperor Gong of the Zhou. I respectfully note that according to the stūpa inscription composed by Hu Fang and the True Record by his disciple Zequan, the master lived sixty-nine years and showed extinction in the sixth year of Tiansheng, the year wuchen. If one goes by the Record of Conduct and Deeds written by Lord Zhao Qingxian, it says that he returned to stillness in the fifth year of Tiansheng; this is a mistake. Now, counting back by the sexagenary cycle, the master was born in the year gengshen. Is this not a case of the true ruler answering the [heavenly] mandate while worthies and sages are born in between? The master's personal name was (Zhili), his courtesy name Yueyan; Zhenzong specially granted him the title Great Master Fazhi, and in his time he was called the Venerable of Siming. His secular surname was Jin; he was a distant descendant of Jin Midi of the Former Han (the History of the Western Han says: Jin Midi was the son of the Xiongnu King of Xiuchu. When the barbarian state was destroyed, he was taken captive by Emperor Wu of the Han, yet he was earnest, reverent, loyal, and filial. For seven generations [his family] served within the palace. Originally, because the Xiuchu [king] made a golden man to serve as the lord of sacrifice to Heaven, he was granted the surname Jin, "gold"), and in later generations [the family] were people of Yin. His father's personal name was Jing; his mother was of the Li family. At first, because no heir had been born, they prayed to the Buddha with sincere resolve. One night [the father] happened to dream that an Indian monk gave him a child, saying, "This is Rāhula." When [the child] was born, he was named accordingly.

Fourth year, jimao [979]: he was twenty years old. The Record of Conduct and Deeds says: "At twenty he went to Dharma Master [Yi]tong of Baoyun in his home commandery to receive the Tiantai work of contemplation. Just three days after he began, the head seat said to him: 'You should uphold the Sequential Gates of the Dharma-Realm.' The master said: 'What is the dharma-realm?' The head seat said: 'It is the great totality of the marks of dharmas, perfectly fused and unobstructed.' The master said: 'If it is perfectly fused and unobstructed, how can there be a sequence?' The head seat had no reply. After a month he was lecturing on the Heart Sūtra himself, and everyone who heard was astonished and passed it on in amazement."

Eleventh: Making the Universal Vows

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Eleventh: Making the Universal Vows

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

All of you here, since you have obtained the precepts today, must arouse the mind of delivering beings. Truly, because the people of the world from the past until now have done everything only for their own sake and cannot arouse a single thought of benefiting others, they revolve in the six paths, and their merit and wisdom are meager. Now that you have received and keep the great precepts, from now on you must arouse the bodhi-mind and give rise to the four universal vows. These four universal [vows] are what all buddhas take as their teacher; through them all buddhas accomplished right awakening. Bodhi is translated here as "the path"; taking the path as one's mind is therefore called the path-mind. In arousing this path-mind, object and wisdom differ according to the four teachings; now, according to the four truths without fabrication of the perfect teaching, we arouse the path-mind. This path-mind is equal to, and not different from, the minds of the buddhas of the ten directions and of all sentient beings.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence the Avataṃsaka says: "As the mind, so the Buddha; as the Buddha, so beings; mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without distinction." If you know that this mind is the Buddha's mind and also the mind of sentient beings, then phenomena and principle are not two, beings and buddhas are one suchness, and the three truths are naturally so, subtle, wondrous, tranquil, and beyond [words]. Arousing the mind on this basis is called true and correct. Making vows contrary to this, the vows are not called universal. Hence Tiantai says: "If the arousing of the mind is skewed, the myriad practices are bestowed in vain." That is it. So this mind, in principle, is equally called the buddha-mind. "All you beings, from beginningless time you have not heard of the Three Jewels and have not understood taking refuge; therefore you have revolved in the three existences and have now fallen among the animals, repaying old debts. These three are identical to one; this one is identical to three — neither vertical nor horizontal, neither together nor separate, inconceivable — and this is called the secret treasury. Since all the myriad dharmas without exception return to and go toward them, they are called the three refuges. Though the principle is vast and far, it is your own mind-nature, which returns from not far away. All of you should deeply believe this principle and take refuge in it." Chinese Source

佛子！我今更為汝稱寶勝如來十號功德，為汝虔請慈尊方便救護。彼佛本願：若有眾生於十方界聞我名者，即得上生三十三天。流水長者為十千魚稱茲十號，彼諸魚類即得生天。長者成佛號釋迦文，彼十千天子，威德熾王而為上首。夙緣所追，而於光明會上，聞信相開士及其二子受菩提記。時諸天子重聞十號，頓悟本心，深證無生，便得受茲十號記別。願汝今日聞我稱揚寶勝十號，如彼天子所證，等無有異。南無過去寶勝如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊（三說）。

"Children of the Buddha! That Buddha's original vow is: 'If there are beings in the worlds of the ten directions who hear my name, they will at once be born above in the Thirty-three Heavens.' I pray that you today, hearing me call and praise the ten epithets of Jewel Victory, may realize as those sons of the gods did, without any difference. Homage to Jewel Victory Tathāgata of the past, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One" (say three times).

Ru-Yi Translation

The Twelve Links of Dependent Origination

Chinese Source

諸佛子！我今更為汝說十二因緣甚深妙法，願承三寶力，令汝一一了知。此十二法，體是三德大般涅槃，而汝迷故翻為三道，三世因果輪轉不息。我今為汝先唱十二因緣生相，次唱十二因緣滅相，願汝達此生滅之法當處即是不生不滅，當處究竟、當處清淨、當處自在，一究竟一切究竟、一清淨一切清淨、一自在一切自在，同於諸佛證大涅槃。（大眾同聲唱云）所謂無明緣行，行緣識，識緣名色，名色緣六入，六入緣觸，觸緣受，受緣愛，愛緣取，取緣有，有緣生，生緣老死憂悲苦惱（此是因緣生相。再聽因緣滅相）。所謂無明滅則行滅，行滅則識滅，識滅則名色滅，名色滅則六入滅，六入滅則觸滅，觸滅則受滅，受滅則愛滅，愛滅則取滅，取滅則有滅，有滅則生滅，生滅則老死憂悲苦惱滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Children of the Buddha! I will now further teach you the very deep and wondrous Dharma of the twelve links of dependent origination. May it be, by the power of the Three Jewels, that you understand each one clearly. These twelve dharmas are in essence the three virtues, great parinirvāṇa; but because you are deluded, they are turned into the three paths, and the causes and effects of the three times revolve without ceasing. I will now chant for you first the arising aspect of the twelve links, and next the ceasing aspect of the twelve links. May you penetrate that these dharmas of arising and ceasing are, right where they are, neither arising nor ceasing; right where they are, ultimate; right where they are, pure; right where they are, sovereign; one ultimate, all ultimate; one pure, all pure; one sovereign, all sovereign; and, together with all the buddhas, realize great nirvāṇa." (The great assembly chants in unison:) "Namely: ignorance conditions volitional formations, volitional formations condition consciousness, consciousness conditions name-and-form, name-and-form conditions the six entrances, the six entrances condition contact, contact conditions feeling, feeling conditions craving, craving conditions grasping, grasping conditions becoming, becoming conditions birth, birth conditions old age, death, sorrow, grief, suffering, and affliction" (this is the arising aspect of the links; next hear the ceasing aspect of the links). "Namely: when ignorance ceases, volitional formations cease; when volitional formations cease, consciousness ceases; when consciousness ceases, name-and-form ceases; when name-and-form ceases, the six entrances cease; when the six entrances cease, contact ceases; when contact ceases, feeling ceases; when feeling ceases, craving ceases; when craving ceases, grasping ceases; when grasping ceases, becoming ceases; when becoming ceases, birth ceases; when birth ceases, old age, death, sorrow, grief, suffering, and affliction cease."

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Title and names. 教行錄 is rendered Record of the Teaching and Practice, following Zongxiao's own explanation (practice established by the teaching, the teaching revealed by practice). Zhili's names: 法智 Fazhi (his imperial title), 四明尊者 the Venerable of Siming; his teacher 義通 Yitong of Baoyun (called 通公 / 通師 Lord [Yi]tong / Master [Yi]tong); his fellow student 遵式 Zunshi, called 慈雲 Ciyun and 天竺懺主 the Repentance Master of Tianzhu (Tianzhu stands for him in unit 7). Names printed small in CBETA as taboo-avoidance, such as （知禮）, are kept in parentheses as "(Zhili)".

Dates. Western years are added in square brackets. The chronology's entry for 991 reads 二年丁卯, but the True Record it quotes says 淳化辛卯, and 991 is xinmao; the English keeps the text's dingmao. Units 30 and 50 have a month written with a character that CBETA can give only as a component formula ([萉-巴+(日/(句-口+匕))]); it is marked "[month written with an unencoded character]."

Questions from Japan (unit 27). Jakushō (寂照) carried Genshin's (源信) twenty-seven questions in 1003; Zhili's answers appear in fascicle 4 of this record as 答日本國師二十七問 (T1937, fasc. 4).

Burning the body (unit 41). "For half a verse [a bodhisattva] gave up his body" alludes to the Snow Mountain youth of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra; the source of "for one line he threw himself into fire" was not located. Zongxiao's closing remark (unit 52) counts Zhili in four of the ten categories of the Song Biographies of Eminent Monks (exegesis, creating merit, spiritual marvels, giving up the body); the sentence 生於舌根舍利滅後儼然 is hard to construe and is translated literally.

The precept rite's quotations (unit 65). Located: "Great assembly, truly believe…" and "The precepts are like the bright sun and moon…" and "When beings receive the Buddha's precepts…" in the Brahmā's Net Sūtra (T1484); the lines that follow ("if they do not receive these precepts … blocks of wood") were not located there. "As the buddhas of the past…" is the closing verse of the Four-Part prātimokṣa (T1429, T1430). The Avataṃsaka "Faith is the source of the path, the mother of merit and virtue" is T278/T279 (信為道元功德母; Zhili writes 信是道源); "The precepts are the root of unsurpassed bodhi" is also T278. "Though the campaka flower has withered…" is in the Daśacakra Sūtra (T410). The Satyaka line on the mangy jackal is quoted in Daoxuan's Xingshi chao (T1804); the Moon Lamp verse and "One who has offended is called a bodhisattva" (found only in commentaries such as T1813) have no located sūtra source.

Lotus quotation (unit 65). Zhili rearranges the two sons' words in the "King Śubhavyūha" chapter; the English follows the site's Lotus Sūtra wording for that chapter.

Three clusters of pure precepts (unit 96). 攝律儀戒／攝善法戒／饒益有情戒 follow the round-two Hundred Records of Guoqing (the precepts embracing the rules of conduct / embracing good dharmas); 饒益有情戒 is new: "the precepts benefiting sentient beings."

The ten major precepts (units 102–111). Zhili paraphrases the Brahmā's Net list freely (for example, the fifth forbids dealing in alcohol and adds a reason; the sixth forbids speaking of others' faults); the English follows his wording, not the sūtra's.

Four universal vows and the Avataṃsaka verse (unit 114). The vow formulas follow the round-two Zhiguan dayi, except 法門無量誓願學 "Dharma-gates are immeasurable; I vow to learn them" (Zhili's wording differs from 無盡／知). "As the mind, so the Buddha…" follows the round-two Notes on the Profound Meaning of Guanyin. "If the arousing of the mind is skewed, the myriad practices are bestowed in vain," attributed to "Tiantai," is found in Zhanran's Fuxing (T1912).

Society statement (unit 121). "Those who obtain a human body are like the earth on a fingernail" summarizes the Nirvāṇa Sūtra passage on the site; "It does not even have the name of the lower paths" and "the beings born there are all avaivartika" follow the site's Amitābha Sūtra.

Qingshan Tower postscript (unit 122). The signature 青山樓弆 is read as a name, "Qi of Qingshan Tower"; 弆 may instead be a verb ("stored"). In 得亢疏 the character 亢 is probably an error for 元 ("the original statement"). 甲乙 may mean the first and second examination ranks or, alternatively, hereditary (jiayi) cloisters.

Releasing life (units 128–137). Names follow the round-two Golden Light Sūtra: Jewel Victory Tathāgata, Jalavāhana, Majestic-Virtue Blazing King, Faith-Mark, the Thirty-three Heavens / Trāyastriṃśa Heaven, and the ten epithets and twelve links as worded there. 大穢跡金剛 is Ucchuṣma, a wrathful vajra deity. "The forty kinds of evil" are the ten evil acts each done in four ways (doing, teaching, praising, rejoicing), as the text's own list implies.

Entrustments (units 124, 126). The first recipient signs off as 擇交論師, so the title 交法師 is given as "Dharma Master [Ze]jiao." 欝多羅僧 is the uttarāsaṅga, the upper robe.
