Offerings and Eulogies for Fazhi, and the Collections Exalting the Patriarchs at Luoxi and Baoyun

The ninth part completes fascicle seven and begins the two appended collections. It first gives the memorial writings for Zhili: Zunshi's offering text of 1028 and his two mourning poems with their preface, where the line "In heaven there is no second moon; among men, only one monk" comes from; eulogies by the disciple Jiang, the censor Luo Yan, the chief minister Shi Hao, and Xiyan; three anniversary statements read at Yanqing by later abbots; and Shanyue's record of 1200 on restoring the image that holds Zhili's bones. A colophon of 1226 records the reprinting. The Collection Exalting the Patriarch at Luoxi, compiled by Yuanwu, then gathers documents on Xiji (Jingguang), Yitong's teacher: three decrees of the King of Wuyue, the Song plaque edict of 1008, Qian Yan's stele on the building of the cloister (986), Qian Yi's stele, Rujiao's record of the Aśoka-style stūpas, Chengyu's stūpa inscription, eulogies and poems, and Yuanwu's record of moving the stūpa in 1230. Finally Zongxiao's Collection Exalting the Patriarch at Baoyun begins: his preface of 1203, the table of contents, the memorial and edicts by which Yitong's cloister received the name Baoyun (981–982), the gazetteer entry, Xiji's biography from the Song Biographies of Monks, and the stūpa record of Yitong.

維天聖六年歲次戊辰正月丁酉朔某日。杭州天竺同門法弟慈雲大師（遵式），謹遣學徒，齎送香燭、靈山上茶，并雲厨辦齋食，罄食味之品，恭薦于四明延慶寺法兄法智大師尊靈。伏惟靈天生懿德，來輔法王，殆將絕紀，奚振頹綱。章安既往，荊溪次亡，誕此人師，紹彼烈元。一家大教，鍾此三良，三友謝其博識，八俊甘以退藏。四十餘載，教網大張，開慈悲之室，踞法空之床。寬以居眾，謹以化方，是乎金玉市貴，桃李蹊香。蜂屯蟻慕，入室升堂，濟濟乎其徒，甡甡乎惟良，可以分千燈，可以化四方。若乃運如椽之筆，啟麗藻之房。十不二之奧義，我得而詳；十六妙之淵旨，我得而敭。至於觀音別品、金鼓舊章，或闡其幽、或補其亡。涌我義泉、解我智囊，伸先覺之製，實後學之望。

Ru-Yi Translation

On a certain day of the first month, whose first day is dingyou, of the sixth year of Tiansheng, the year-star being in wuchen [1028], the Dharma-brother of the same gate at Tianzhu in Hangzhou, Great Master Ciyun (Zunshi), respectfully sends his students bearing incense and candles, tea of Lingshan, and a vegetarian meal prepared in the cloud kitchen, with the full range of flavors, reverently offered to the honored spirit of his Dharma-elder-brother, Great Master Fazhi of Yanqing Monastery in Siming. I humbly consider that your spirit was born by Heaven with fine virtue and came to assist the Dharma King. [The teaching] was about to be cut off — who would raise up the fallen bonds? Zhang'an had gone, and Jingxi had perished in turn; then this teacher of humans was born to continue their ardent first principles. The great teaching of our school gathered in these three good ones; the three friends deferred to his broad knowledge, and the eight brilliant ones were content to withdraw and hide. For more than forty years the net of the teaching was spread wide; he opened the room of compassion and occupied the seat of the emptiness of dharmas. Broad in dwelling with the assembly, careful in transforming the region, he was thus [like] gold and jade, valued in the market, [like] peaches and plums, whose paths are fragrant. Like swarming bees and admiring ants, they entered the chamber and ascended the hall; numerous were his followers, many were the good ones; they could divide into a thousand lamps and could transform the four quarters. As for wielding a brush like a rafter and opening the chamber of beautiful phrasing: the profound meaning of the ten nondualities I was able to know in detail; the deep purport of the sixteen wondrous [contemplations] I was able to make known. As for the separate chapter on Avalokiteśvara and the old text of the golden drum, he sometimes elucidated their hidden [meaning] and sometimes supplied what was lost. He made our spring of meaning gush forth and opened our bag of wisdom; he extended the works of those first awakened, and was truly the hope of later students.

Chinese Source

嗚呼何期，忽隨世態。奄爾無常，顧有河之渺瀰；俄折橋梁，悲欲海之浩漫。忽喪艅艎，痛兩楹之愕夢，結雙樹之悲涼。且夫世人將啟手足，纏痛寢床。唯兄告終，與物返常，加趺面西，稱佛洋洋，良久泊然，高騖淨方。如此則天眼遐睠，神力隼翔，必願臨弟斯食，鑒弟斯章。

Ru-Yi Translation

Alas, who would have expected that suddenly he would follow the way of the world, and suddenly [meet] impermanence? Looking at the river's vast expanse, suddenly the bridge is broken; grieving at the boundless ocean of desire, suddenly the great ship is lost. I grieve at the startling dream of the two pillars and gather the sad chill of the twin trees. Now, when people of the world are about to reveal their hands and feet, they are bound with pain on their sickbeds. Only you, elder brother, at the end returned to the constant together with things: sitting cross-legged facing west, you called the Buddha's name grandly, and after a long while, serenely, you galloped high to the Pure Region. That being so, with your heavenly eye looking back from afar and your spiritual power swooping like a falcon, may you surely come to this, my offering of food, and look upon this, my writing.

Chinese Source

嗚呼哀哉！詩有伐木，易稱斷金，唯弟於兄，真謂同心，象塤篪之合韻，亦笙磬之和音。有法共議、有過相箴，如切如磋，博我良深，有始有卒，唯天是沈。偶因化緣，自此分襟，阻越之山，隔吳之潯。蹤跡滯夢，夢亦相尋，日飛盡於東箭，情有斷於南金。嗚呼嗚呼，今則已已，痛何可任。理合便離鷲嶺、遠度山陰，為老怯於春冷，亦路阻於霖霪。劣弟蓋年過耳順，齒逼縱心，行坐几杖、起臥呻吟。與兄暫隔，繼送窮林，只期生於安養，願永作於追尋。嗚呼嗚呼！伏惟尚饗。

Ru-Yi Translation

Alas, how grievous! The Odes has "Felling Trees"; the Changes speaks of "cutting metal." I, the younger brother, toward you, elder brother, may truly be called of one mind, like the harmonious rhyme of ocarina and flute, like the concordant sound of reed organ and chime. When there was Dharma, we discussed it together; when there were faults, we admonished each other; as if cutting and polishing, you broadened me deeply; having a beginning and an end — only Heaven [knows] how deep. By chance, for the conditions of transformation, we parted from that time on, blocked by the mountains of Yue, separated by the shores of Wu. Our traces were stuck, [meeting only in] dreams, and in dreams too we sought each other; the days flew by, spent like the arrows of the east; feeling was cut off from the gold of the south. Alas, alas! Now it is over, over; how can the pain be borne? By rights I should leave Vulture Peak at once and cross far to Shanyin, but being old I fear the spring cold, and the road is also blocked by lasting rains. Your poor younger brother is past sixty and nearing seventy; walking and sitting, I need a stool and staff; rising and lying down, I groan. Parted from you, elder brother, for a while, I will soon follow you to the ultimate forest; I only hope to be born in Peaceful Nourishment, and vow to seek you there forever. Alas, alas! Humbly may you partake of this.

予與四明法智大師為友四十餘年，及終不得一哭於寢門之下，由路有五六百里、春有飛霰累旬、身有六十六歲，故杖屐不利收往也。嗟歎之不足，乃詠歌之句云：天上無雙月，人間秖一僧。覽者無謂予厚於所知、薄於所不知，但見其解行有卓卓出人之異，寄極言以暢其所懷耳。異者何也？一家教部，毘陵師所未記者，悉記之。四三昧，人所難行者，悉行之。雖寒暑相代，脇不至席，六十有九而終。其疾且頓，而行道講訓無所閒然，門徒請宴不從。逮加趺氣盡，後及火化，舍利莫知其幾千數矣。噫！非知之艱，行之惟艱也。詩二章章八句。

Ru-Yi Translation

I was friends with Great Master Fazhi of Siming for more than forty years, yet at his end I could not once weep at the gate of his chamber, because the road is five or six hundred li, this spring there has been flying sleet for successive ten-day periods, and I am sixty-six years old, so staff and clogs are not fit for the journey. Since sighing was not enough, I chanted and sang the line: "In heaven there is no second moon; among men, only one monk." Readers should not say that I am generous to the one I know and stingy to those I do not know; it is only that I saw in his understanding and practice an eminence surpassing others, and expressed it in extreme words to give vent to what I feel. What is the eminence? Of the works of our school, whatever the Master of Piling did not annotate, he annotated all; of the four samādhis, which people find hard to practice, he practiced all. Though cold and heat alternated, his side never touched the mat, until he died at sixty-nine. His illness was sudden, yet in practicing the path and lecturing he never slackened; when his followers asked him to rest, he would not. When he sat cross-legged and his breath ceased, and later at the cremation, the śarīra were who knows how many thousands. Ah! It is not knowing that is hard; it is practicing that is hard. Two poems, of eight lines each.

Chinese Source

四明延慶道場教主法智大師，皇宋四葉天聖六年示滅，殆今紹興甲戌一百二十有九年矣。月溪癡絕道人（希顏）頓首為之讚曰：

Ru-Yi Translation

Great Master Fazhi, Teaching-Lord of the place of practice of Yanqing in Siming, showed extinction in the sixth year of Tiansheng in the fourth reign of the August Song; it is now, in jiaxu of Shaoxing [1154], some one hundred twenty-nine years. The Man of the Path of Utter Foolishness of Yuexi, (Xiyan), bowing his head, praises him:

Chinese Source

行天台所難行，而為二浙師。記毘陵所未記，而為百世法。智者教門由此而光明孔碩，延慶道場因之而聲聞維揚。矯矯乎摩雲之標，堂堂乎不世之器。考之擊之而隨大隨小，鑽之仰之而彌堅彌高。於戲！天台之有師也，譬如泰山之有𣏌梓豫章，巨海之有珠璣大貝，東魯仲尼之門而有顏子閔子，西晉印手之室而有汰師遠師。苟非積世願力慈心，豈能一朝開物成務。稽首四明中興之祖。

Ru-Yi Translation

He practiced what Tiantai found hard to practice and became the teacher of the two Zhe; he annotated what Piling had not annotated and became the model for a hundred generations. Through him Zhizhe's teaching gate became bright and great; because of him the place of practice of Yanqing became renowned and exalted. Lofty, his bearing scrapes the clouds; imposing, a vessel not of every age. Knock on it, strike it, and it answers great or small; bore into it, look up to it, and it grows ever firmer and higher. Ah! Tiantai's having this master is like Mount Tai's having qi, catalpa, and camphor trees, the great sea's having pearls and great cowries, the gate of Zhongni of eastern Lu having Yanzi and Minzi, the chamber of the Seal-hand of the Western Jin having Master [Fa]tai and Master [Hui]yuan. If not for vow-power and compassion accumulated over ages, how could he in one morning open things up and complete affairs? I bow my head to the restoring patriarch of Siming.

陳（天俞）

Ru-Yi Translation

Chen (Tianyu)

Chinese Source

右伏以法體如如本無出沒，世緣冉冉乃有會離。自智者示寂于天台，道若懸絲而引石；洎通師流芳於甬水，聲如出谷以遷喬。寥寥像法之餘、渺渺荊溪之下，南輝台嶺、東紹麗師，雖作者篾以加，於若住世，無異此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Stated above. I humbly consider that the Dharma-body, being thus-like, originally has no appearing or disappearing; worldly conditions, moving on and on, have meeting and parting. Since Zhizhe showed stillness on Tiantai, the path was like a hanging thread drawing up a stone; when Master [Yi]tong let his fragrance flow by the Yong River, the sound was like [birds] leaving the valley to move to tall trees. Lonely is the remnant of the semblance Dharma; distant is [the time] after Jingxi. Shining south from the Tai peaks, continuing in the east from the master of [Gor]yeo: though makers could add nothing to it, if [he] were abiding in the world, it would be no different from this.

Chinese Source

恭惟中興教主法智大師，生由佛孕，幼自天成，戒瑩淵珠，貫南山之律部；辯傾海浪，發衡嶽之心宗。可謂人間一僧，天下大士者也。不然，則五時八教孰為中興，百界千如世皆弱喪。所以真宗皇帝遣使以加異，日本國師命徒而就詢。一世儒宗、同年法席，莫不傳上意而留住世，趨丈室以免焚軀，故得一千眾皆預於陞堂，四十軸率歸於秉筆。遂令三觀專美四明，如赫日之中天、似迅雷之出地，草木瓦礫盡演真詮、鳥獸蟲魚咸通妙教。惜乎出已還沒，化身本然；會必有離，世相如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

I reverently consider that Great Master Fazhi, Teaching-Lord of the restoration, was born through the Buddha's conception and from childhood was formed by Heaven. His precepts were lustrous as a pearl in the deep, threading through Nanshan's Vinaya; his eloquence overturned the ocean waves, bringing out the mind-teaching of Hengyue. He may be called the one monk among humans, the great being under heaven. Otherwise, who would have restored the five periods and eight teachings? The hundred realms and thousand suchnesses — the world would all have lost its home. Therefore Emperor Zhenzong sent an envoy to add special honors, and the master of Japan commanded disciples to come and inquire. The Confucian masters of an age and those of the same years at the Dharma seat all conveyed the emperor's intention and kept him abiding in the world, hastening to his abbot's chamber to spare him from burning his body. Thus a thousand people all took part in ascending his hall, and forty scrolls all came from his wielding of the brush. So the threefold contemplation became the special glory of Siming, like the blazing sun in mid-heaven, like swift thunder coming out of the earth; grass, trees, tiles, and pebbles all proclaim the true teaching; birds, beasts, insects, and fish all penetrate the wondrous teaching. Alas, having come forth, he has gone again; the transformation body is originally so. Meeting must have parting; the marks of the world are like this.

Chinese Source

但某等叨霑法乳幸駕宗乘，況嗣凡筵斯揚講貫。雖大師之靈骨有在，而大師之神容可瞻，柰何化往無方，依棲絕跡。遙想鶴林之逝，永纏梁木之悲。今茲日應跏趺，情存薦設，敢効純陀之供，少追宗廟之祠。伏乞大師以無所來而來、以本非食而食，所冀冥符台嶺、永耀鄞城，使彼諫書還成輔教，及今禪學無謂別傳，貽萬劫之孫謀、資一人之睿算，庶協祖道，無忘孝思。謹疏。

Ru-Yi Translation

We, So-and-so and others, have unworthily been moistened by the Dharma-milk and fortunately ride the vehicle of the school; moreover, we succeed to the ordinary seat and spread the lecturing. Though the Great Master's numinous bones remain and the Great Master's spirit-face can be looked upon, alas, his transformation has gone without place, and his dwelling has left no trace. Thinking far back to the passing in the crane grove, we are forever bound in the grief of the [broken] roof beam. To die and be buried is ancient; to change the burial is not ancient. If changing the burial is not ancient, is it then a matter of following expedience to accomplish affairs? Formerly the former lords said that burial is concealing, and concealing is wishing people not to see. Coffins, outer coffins, clothes, and quilts were made to lift [the body], and the site and grave were divined to settle it; thus there was burial. After the Venerable of Luoxi died, his disciples reverently took his whole body and placed it southeast of Guoqing Monastery, using worldly rites. It has been over two hundred years; with the passing of years, the water lost its old course, silting, submerging, and sinking, so that those passing by sighed. I, (Yuanwu), unworthily filling this vacancy, surveyed its flowing springs, observed its yin and yang, and obtained ground behind the abbot's quarters. On the fourth day of the eleventh month of gengyin of Shaoding [1230], I changed the burial and erected a stone stūpa. Now, covering bones and burying flesh is set up for those who have not penetrated. When the Venerable was alive, he already regarded this body as an illusory transformation. Now that he has gone, the truth of the one nature is everywhere; why should he mind the water, and what use is there in moving? 師諱羲寂，字常照，姓胡氏，溫州永嘉人也。母初懷娠，不喜葷血，生乃以紫帽蒙其首焉。幼啟二親，堅求去俗。旋入開元蘭若投師，授《法華經》，期月而徹，寺之耆老莫不稱嘆希有。既而祝髮受具已，往會稽學南山宗。既通律藏，乃造天台山研覈止觀。其所易解，猶河南一遍照也。先是天台智者教迹，遠則安祿兵殘，近則會昌焚毀，殘編斷簡本折枝摧，傳者何憑以正其學。師於是每思鳩集，因適金華，古藏中得《淨名疏》而已。後時忠懿王以教相咨問德韶國師，師指授尊者，因是奏王，請出金門建講，欽若敬奉，為之造寺，今螺溪定慧院是也。王又遣十人往日本國，取天台教藏迴，賜師以淨光大師之號，追諡九祖名銜，皆師之力焉。由是一家教乘以師為重興之人矣。而韶公適與智者同姓，能毘贊吾宗，又居佛隴之側，疑其後身也。微師，此宗學者幾握半珠為家寶歟。

Ru-Yi Translation

The master's personal name was Xiji, his courtesy name Changzhao; his surname was Hu; he was a man of Yongjia in Wenzhou. When his mother first conceived him, she disliked strong-smelling and bloody foods; when he was born, his head was covered as if with a purple cap. As a child he told his parents and firmly asked to leave the secular world. He soon entered the Kaiyuan hermitage and went to a teacher, who taught him the Lotus Sūtra; in a full month he penetrated it, and the elders of the monastery all praised it as rare. Then, having shaved his head and received the full precepts, he went to Kuaiji to study the Nanshan school. Having mastered the Vinaya-piṭaka, he went to Mount Tiantai to study Calming and Contemplation thoroughly. Its ease of understanding [for him] was like the one universal illumination of Henan. Earlier, the teaching traces of Zhizhe of Tiantai had been, remotely, ravaged by An Lushan's troops and, recently, burned and destroyed in the Huichang [persecution]; with broken volumes and fragmentary slips, roots snapped and branches broken, what could transmitters rely on to correct their learning? The master therefore always thought of collecting them, and going to Jinhua, he obtained only the Commentary on the Vimalakīrti in an old repository. Later, when King Zhongyi consulted National Teacher Deshao about the marks of the teaching, the National Teacher pointed to and referred [him to] the Venerable, and thereupon memorialized the king, who invited him to come out to the golden gate to lecture; [the king] revered and served him and built a monastery for him — the present Dinghui Cloister of Luoxi. The king also sent ten men to Japan to fetch the canon of the Tiantai teaching and bring it back, granted the master the title Great Master Jingguang, and posthumously conferred titles on the nine patriarchs; all this was through the master's efforts. Thus the teaching-vehicle of our school takes the master as the one who revived it. And since Lord [De]shao happened to have the same surname as Zhizhe, could aid our school, and lived beside Folong, it was suspected that he was [Zhizhe's] later body. Had it not been for the master, students of this school would nearly be holding half a pearl as a family treasure.

太平興國五年，從山入州治寺，寺東樓安置。樓近大山，夜夢剎柱陷沒于地，意頗惡之。自徙於西偏僧房，其夜春雨驟甚，山崩樓圮。人咸謂師先見，乃證報得天眼焉。續受黃巖人請，乘舟泛江放生，講金光流水。至海門靈石寺，是智者所居道場，因勸人修葺造像，入緣者繁多。時今上遣高品衛紹欽，入山建壽昌寺，眾官同命受戒。又雍熙初，永安縣敦請於光明寺受戒，忽古殿佛像墮，腹中獲發願文。即唐咸通六年，僧希皎為七鄉人施戒勸造此像，願捨報為男子，童真出家，常布衲傳法利生。觀者皆意寂之前身也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the fifth year of Taiping Xingguo [980], he came from the mountain into the prefectural seat and was lodged in the east tower of the monastery. The tower was close to a great mountain; at night he dreamed that the mast-pillar of the monastery sank into the ground, and he found it quite ominous. He moved himself to the monks' quarters on the west side; that night the spring rain was very heavy, the mountain collapsed, and the tower fell. People all said that the master had foresight, and so attested that he had gained the heavenly eye as a karmic reward. Then, at the invitation of people of Huangyan, he went by boat on the river to release life and lectured on the Golden Light's [chapter of] Jalavāhana. When he arrived at Lingshi Monastery at Haimen, which was a place of practice where Zhizhe had lived, he exhorted people to repair it and make images, and those who joined were many. At that time the present emperor sent the high-ranking [eunuch] Wei Shaoqin into the mountain to build Shouchang Monastery, and the various officials together asked to receive the precepts. Also, at the beginning of Yongxi, Yong'an County earnestly invited him to confer the precepts at Guangming Monastery; suddenly a Buddha image in the old hall fell, and inside it was found a text of vows: it was from the sixth year of Xiantong of the Tang [865], when the monk Xijiao conferred the precepts on the people of seven villages and urged them to make this image, vowing that when he gave up this reward he would be a man, leave home as a pure boy, always wear a cloth robe, transmit the Dharma, and benefit beings. Those who saw it all thought it was [Xi]ji's former body.

至四年十月寢疾，仲冬四日囑誡門人，不許哭泣祭奠等事，言已瞑目而終。壽六十九，法臘五十。傳法弟子百餘人，外國十僧。其義通實高者也，如澄或寶翔皆亞焉。是時台之民官曾預傳戒者，共迎師真相於開元寺，盡誠祭供，皆縞素哀泣，天為之變慘。太守鄭元龜作詩悲悼歸空，方丈樹塔志之。厥後徒屬遷葬他所，開塔顏貌如生，髮長餘寸。平素講《法華經》并《玄義》二十遍。《止觀》、《維摩》、《光明》、《梵網》、《金剛錍》、《法界觀》、《禪源詮》、《永嘉集》各數遍，述《義例》、《不二門》等科節數卷。然自智者六代傳法，荊溪之後二百餘年，寂受遺寄，克深負荷。況於炎月講說，曾無流汗沾洽。其不久聽而勝解佛乘，每一宣揚則捉金應玉、召羽和商，較乎九旬談妙，相去何若。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the tenth month of the fourth year [987] he lay ill; on the fourth day of mid-winter he gave final instructions and warnings to his disciples, not permitting weeping, offerings, and the like; having spoken, he closed his eyes and passed away. His age was sixty-nine, his Dharma years fifty. Disciples to whom he transmitted the Dharma were more than a hundred, [including] ten monks from foreign countries. Among them Yitong was truly the highest; Chengyu and Baoxiang were next to him. At this time the people and officials of Tai who had taken part in receiving the precepts together welcomed the master's true image to Kaiyuan Monastery and with utmost sincerity made offerings, all in white mourning clothes, weeping; Heaven changed to gloom for him. The prefect Zheng Yuangui wrote a poem mourning his return to emptiness; a stūpa was erected at the abbot's quarters to mark it. Later his followers moved the burial elsewhere; when the stūpa was opened his face was as in life, and his hair had grown more than an inch. In ordinary times he lectured on the Lotus Sūtra and the Profound Meaning twenty times; Calming and Contemplation, the Vimalakīrti, the Golden Light, Brahmā's Net, the Vajra Scalpel, the Contemplation of the Dharma-Realm, the Chan Source Collection, and the Yongjia Collection several times each; and composed outlines and sections of the Guiding Principles, the Gates of Nonduality, and others in several fascicles. Now, since Zhizhe's transmission of the Dharma in six generations, more than two hundred years after Jingxi, [Xi]ji received the bequest and was able deeply to bear the burden. Moreover, lecturing in the hot months, he never dripped with sweat. Without listening long, he excellently understood the buddha-vehicle; each time he proclaimed it, he grasped the metal and answered the jade, summoned the yu and harmonized the shang. Compared with [Zhizhe's] ninety days of discussing the wondrous, how far apart is it?

Ru-Yi Translation

He also once stayed at Yuwang Monastery in Siming, and dreamed that he climbed Guoqing; at the upper place there was a jewel-adorned banner and seat, inscribed "Mañjuśrī Terrace." Barriers and railings were set up, and there was no way to enter. Suddenly he saw the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara slowly come out of the hall, push away the barrier with his hand, and, lowering [his head], receive him. In a moment he felt that he had merged with Avalokiteśvara's body without separation, and he awoke startled. From then on his delight in preaching was inexhaustible. Some say that entering the wisdom-vehicle of the Universal Gate, above one unites with the Buddha's awakening, because of realizing the unsurpassed; below one unites with living beings, because the ordinary are of the same essence. When it opens, all numinous [beings] are mixed into one dharma. Who obtains this mind, if not Avalokiteśvara? Great is the master's understanding and practice like this; can it not be a model for the world?

Record of the Stone Stūpa of Dharma Master Lord [Yi]tong of Baoyun, Mingzhou, of the Great Song

Chinese Source

住延慶法孫文慧大師（宗正）撰

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Great Master Wenhui (Zongzheng), Dharma-grandson, residing at Yanqing

Chinese Source

法師諱義通，字惟遠，德業詳諸行狀。本海國高麗君族，尹姓。母孰氏，妊誕頗異，因捨龜山院，師釋宗。及冠染，具傳《華嚴》、《起信》，彼尤仰止。殆壯游中國，晉禾福時也。至始訪雲居契悟，嗣謁螺溪寂師了天台宗，繄道且逢源，具體之聲浹聞四遠，姑曰圓頓之學畢茲轍矣。吾欲以此導諸未聞，必生地始乃括囊。東下道由四明，太師錢公惟治問以心要，洊辟為戒師。繼此道俗蘄嚮，請留依怙。師曰：非始心也。公曰：或尼之，或使之，非弟子之力也。如曰利生，何必雞林乎？緣既汝合，辭不我却。因止焉。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Dharma Master's personal name was Yitong, his courtesy name Weiyuan; his virtuous deeds are detailed in the account of his conduct. He was originally of the royal clan of Goryeo, a sea kingdom, of the surname Yun. His mother, of the Shu family, had a remarkable pregnancy and birth; so he was given to Guishan Cloister and took Buddhism as his teacher. When capped, he dyed [his robe] and transmitted in full the Avataṃsaka and the Awakening of Faith, and there he was especially looked up to. When grown, he traveled to the Middle Kingdom; it was the Tianfu [era] of the Jin. Arriving, he first visited Yunju and accorded in awakening; then he visited Master [Xi]ji of Luoxi and understood the Tiantai school, his path meeting its source; his name for complete substance spread far in all four [directions]. For a while he said: "The learning of the perfect and sudden ends here. I wish to use it to guide those who have not heard it; I must begin with my native land." So he packed his bag. Going east, his road passed through Siming. The Grand Preceptor Lord Qian Weizhi asked him about the essentials of the mind and repeatedly invited him to be his precept master. After this monks and laity, eager and inclined, asked him to stay as their support. The master said: "This was not my original intention." The lord said: "Whether someone stops you or someone sends you, it is not your disciple's power. If it is said to be for benefiting beings, why must it be Gyerim? Since conditions accord with you, do not refuse us in your words." So he stayed there.

Ru-Yi Translation

Then the transport commissioner Gu Chenghui gave his residence as a place for transmitting the path, and requested the plaque name Baoyun, to make its auspiciousness manifest. Thereafter he spread the teaching and contemplation daily for more than two years; Zhili and Zunshi were the highest of his students, and the others who ascended the hall and reached the gate cannot be counted. All his writings have been lost and not transmitted. Alas, the noble person says: that the path of Tiantai suddenly revived is due to the master's power. His secular age was sixty-two. In the first year of Duangong [988], when the year-star was in wuzi, on the eighteenth day of the tenth month, he showed illness, and three days later he passed away lying on his left side. After cremation, his disciples gathered his bones and placed them on the south slope of Mount Yuwang, at the northwest corner of the monastery, according to ritual. Seventy-seven years later, the brickwork had become overgrown, so it was rebuilt by piling [bricks] into a square tomb, to make the place more conspicuous. At that time it was the fifth reign of the August Song, the year being jiachen; the year after the Heavenly King ascended the throne, the era was changed to Zhiping, on the tenth day of late spring [1064]. Recorded by the Dharma-great-grandson (Zongzheng).

右通法師石塔記。紹興歲在庚辰十月二十一日，嗣法住持智謙之所重立也。通公來自三韓，譽振中國。住寶雲凡二紀餘，實第一代。如法智、慈雲乃其高弟。天台之教中墮而興，繄師之力。而一時事跡行業等石刻，今不復存。謙公力搜訪之，始得塔記，乃再刊刻。又闢真堂，塑師坐像，及吳越國王所贊頂相併上石碣。除阿育王山烏石塔所，屋之以石，以表示後世。於是通師之道益以昭著，時謙之來纔半歲餘爾。寶雲自通始建，傳二百載，比年頹圮特甚。謙睹之慨然，支傾飭蠹，浸復其舊，亦可謂不負祖師付囑荷擔之意矣。聖宋天寶間，漕使顧承徽捨其第以處於師，其後推官陳雲者，又捨西嶼之田四百三十畝，歲久不復知。謙亦塑二公像，而表出之，人益重其知本。謙得法於延慶第五代明智師，其淵源亦有所自云。左朝散郎主管台州崇道觀王伯庠謹書。

Ru-Yi Translation

The above is the record of the stone stūpa of Master [Yi]tong. It was re-erected on the twenty-first day of the tenth month of the Shaoxing year gengchen [1160] by Zhiqian, abbot succeeding to the Dharma. Lord [Yi]tong came from the Three Han, and his fame shook the Middle Kingdom. He lived at Baoyun more than two decades, truly the first generation. Fazhi and Ciyun were his eminent disciples. That the Tiantai teaching, having fallen midway, revived was due to the master's power. But the stone carvings of his traces and deeds of that time no longer survive. Lord [Zhi]qian searched hard and first found the stūpa record, and had it carved again. He also opened a portrait hall and sculpted a seated image of the master, and put on a stone tablet the eulogy of his head-mark by the King of Wuyue as well. He removed [them] to the site of the black stone stūpa on Mount Aśoka and housed them with stone, to show later ages. Thereupon Master [Yi]tong's path became still more manifest; at that time [Zhi]qian had come only a little over half a year earlier. Since Baoyun was first built by [Yi]tong, it had been transmitted for two hundred years, and in recent years had decayed especially badly. [Zhi]qian saw it and was moved; he propped up what was leaning and repaired what was rotten, gradually restoring its former [state]; he may be said not to have failed the intention of the patriarch's entrustment and his bearing of the burden. In the Tianbao era of the Sage Song, the transport commissioner Gu Chenghui gave his residence to house the master; afterward the judicial official Chen Yun further gave four hundred thirty mu of fields at Xiyu, which over the years was no longer known. [Zhi]qian also sculpted images of the two lords and made [their gifts] known, and people valued all the more his knowing the root. [Zhi]qian obtained the Dharma from Master Mingzhi, the fifth generation of Yanqing; his source too has its origin. Respectfully written by Wang Boxiang, Left Gentleman for Court Leisure, Superintendent of Chongdao Monastery in Taizhou.

Reading Notes for Part 9

Translator's notes

Zunshi's offering text (unit 2). "Tea of Lingshan" refers to Zunshi's own monastery (Lingshan, Tianzhu), while "Vulture Peak" (鷲嶺) near the end is the Feilai/Lingjiu peak at Tianzhu, used poetically; it is kept as "Vulture Peak". "The three good ones" (三良) and "the three friends … the eight brilliant ones" (三友、八俊) are classical figures not further identified. "The ten nondualities" and "the sixteen wondrous [contemplations]" refer to Pointing to the Essentials and The Wondrous Purport; "the separate chapter on Avalokiteśvara" and "the old text of the golden drum" to Zhili's notes on the Separately Circulated Profound Meaning and the Golden Light. "Felling Trees" is Odes 165; "cutting metal" is Changes, "Appended Statements" (two people of one mind).

Mourning poems (units 3–11). The famous line in unit 6 matches the English already used in Parts 7 and 8. "The Master of Piling" is Zhanran. The headings "The First" and "The Second" follow their poems in the text and are kept in that order. Zunshi gives his age as sixty-six, which fits his birth in 964.

Eulogies (units 12–28). "Mao Ridge" (鄮嶺) is Mount Mao near Mingzhou. "Zhangsheng" (unit 23) is Emperor Zhenzong's posthumous title. 𧮯𧮯 is rendered "rumbling." Shi Hao's companion "Lord [Zhi]lian, Jueyun" and "Master [Hui]xun" follow NOTES (p6–p7). Xiyan's preface (unit 27) counts 129 years from 1028 to 1154 (actually 126); it is signed from Yuexi though the heading says Xuexi. In unit 28, "the Seal-hand" is Dao'an, whose disciples were Zhu Fatai and Huiyuan.

Anniversary statements (units 29–37). "Those letters of remonstrance" (unit 31) probably refers to Renyue's Ten Remonstrances (Part 8, unit 105). "The master of [Gor]yeo" (麗師) is Yitong. The western neighbor's spring sacrifice (unit 37) alludes to Changes, hexagram 63; "none under heaven can take me as master" echoes Confucius' last words (Record of Rites, "Tangong"). "The second general who turns the Dharma wheel" alludes to Śāriputra; the sentence is ambiguous.

Shanyue's restoration record (units 38–41). The bones were placed in the image in 1047 and the image refurbished in 1157 and 1200. 當處 follows NOTES ("this very place").

Luoxi collection: decrees (units 59–64). King Zhongyi is Qian Chu of Wuyue. "Lectures for the heavenly realm" (為天界講) may instead name a place or mean "for the departed"; not resolved.

Plaque edict (units 65–69). 偽命 "the illegitimate [regime]" is the Song term for the former Wuyue kingdom. The signatories (Zhao, Feng, Wang) are left as surnames.

Qian Yan's stele (units 70–76). "The present King of Hannan" is Qian Chu after his submission to the Song. Lord Shao of Yunju is Tiantai Deshao. "The two masters Xiang and Hua" (襄華二師) are translated literally; they appear to be the king's sons. 談女亹亹 is read as 談論. "The rich grass of the hedge" (草藩肥膩) seems to allude to the fine grass of the Himalaya in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra; vairambha is the wind that destroys worlds.

Qian Yi's stele (units 77–81). The text has many copying errors (釋 for "Buddhist," 求嘉 for 永嘉, 三願 for 三顧, 丁亥 rightly 987); the English gives the evident sense. The passage on the dharma-nature and ignorance is from Zhanran's Ten Gates of Nonduality (T1927). "The two lords Song and Guang" are uncertain (Qingsong is listed as Xiji's teacher in Part 8, unit 99). Xu Yuandu is Xu Xun, said to have been reborn.

Aśoka stūpas (unit 84). The Lotus sentences follow the site's Lotus Sūtra (chapter 10).

Chengyu's inscription and the eulogies (units 86–105). The disciple Jiaoru (皎如) is probably the Rujiao (如皎) of unit 83. Li Hang's eulogy refers to Xu Ling (disciple of Zhiyi) and Liang Su (disciple of Zhanran). Unit 99 alludes to the story that spirits held up Daoxuan's feet. In unit 105 "the pitch-pipe ashes" (律飛灰) and 一陳遮 are uncertain.

Baoyun collection (units 108–152). "Exalting the Patriarch" (振祖) is the plaque of the patriarchs' hall (unit 111). The memorial (unit 137) writes Gu Chengwei (微) for Chenghui (徽). The biography of Xiji (unit 148) is from the Song Biographies of Eminent Monks (T2061, not in the local Taishō folder); "like the one universal illumination of Henan" was not traced (source not located). Zongzheng's record (unit 151) gives 晉禾福, read as Tianfu; 孰氏 is given as Shu; 逾二祀 is translated literally as "more than two years," though other sources say two decades (二紀). Unit 152's "Tianbao era of the Sage Song" is not a Song era name (Tianbao was a Wuyue era, 908–923); translated as written.
