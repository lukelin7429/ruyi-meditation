The Release-Pond Stele, Fusing the Mind, and Four Short Essays

The second part finishes fascicle one and opens fascicle two. It begins with Pan Hua's record of a dream in which thousands of fish wept at his leaving office, and Zhili's short postscript explaining it through the nonduality of the nature of fish, the Buddha, and the magistrate. Then comes Liu Yun's ornate stele inscription (1025) for the pond for releasing life at Yanqing Cloister, set up under Emperor Zhenzong's edict; it mixes Confucian arguments against wanton killing with praise of Zhili's vow and practice. Fascicle two contains Zhili's short doctrinal essays. Fusing the Mind (1014) answers a student's questions on the Contemplation Sūtra: the sixteen contemplations belong to the contemplation "in terms of practice," and each is to be cultivated as the threefold contemplation in a single mind, so that visualizing the Pure Land and illuminating the three truths do not obstruct each other — the position Zhili later expanded in The Wondrous Purport. The Essential Purport of Repentance Practice is given in the translation already made for this round. Three shorter pieces follow: a reading of the note under the title of Zhanran's Fuxing, a comment on eight phrases about illumination and speech in the Guiding Principles, and a dialogue placing the Awakening of Faith and Fazang's commentary within the four teachings.

The Record of the Dream of the Fish and Zhili's Postscript

Chinese Source

夢魚記

Ru-Yi Translation

Record of the Dream of the Fish

Chinese Source

（守尚書屯田員外郎知越州諸暨縣事潘華記）

Ru-Yi Translation

(Recorded by Pan Hua, Acting Vice Director of the Military Colonies Bureau of the Department of State Affairs and Magistrate of Zhuji County in Yuezhou)

Chinese Source

（四明法師跋）

Ru-Yi Translation

(Postscript by the Dharma Master of Siming)

Chinese Source

予到任，依《普賢觀經》，不令人捕池沼江湖內魚（城外難禁）。景德四年孟冬，奉詔還闕。十月十日夜忽夢，江湖中魚約計數萬，悉號泣云：長者去矣，吾眾烹矣。哭聲沸天，所不忍聞。然而是耶非耶？真耶妄耶？復焉知後來宰邑者非長者乎？所嗟者人何故而為魚，魚何故通人，而知人之去留，一何靈也。君子鑒之，何忍烹之乎。予悶其有情，故特記之云。

Ru-Yi Translation

When I arrived at my post, following the Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra, I did not allow people to catch fish in the ponds, pools, rivers, and lakes (outside the city it was hard to prohibit). In the first month of winter in the fourth year of Jingde [1007], I was recalled to the capital by edict. On the night of the tenth day of the tenth month I suddenly dreamed that the fish in the rivers and lakes, some tens of thousands of them, were all wailing and weeping, saying: "The elder is leaving; our multitudes will be cooked!" The sound of their crying boiled up to the sky; I could not bear to hear it. Yet was it so or not so? Was it true or false? And how do I know that the one who governs the county after me will not be an elder? What I lament is this: why are humans made into fish, and why do fish communicate with humans, so that they know the coming and going of a person — how numinous they are! Let the noble person take this as a mirror; how can he bear to cook them? Because I pity their sentience, I have specially recorded this.

Chinese Source

四明沙門（知禮）偶覩茲事，能知厥由，非釋典了義，莫可原之。蓋魚性、佛性、宰邑之性本不二焉，在事強分，二無二也。佛既先覺，立法教人，觀乎物性，起同體悲，安其危、示其樂，俾其復本與佛齊致。故流水救魚已得成佛，魚亦當成，不二之驗矣。今所夢者，豈孤然哉。乃由佛廣放生之教，魚蘊得脫之緣，人有增善之分，共而成之，其理必也。夫如是聞見之人，得不力行其教，諦觀乎性，使齊於流水者也。時天聖元年四月十一日跋。

Ru-Yi Translation

I, the śramaṇa (Zhili) of Siming, happening to see this matter, can know its cause; apart from the definitive meaning of the Buddhist scriptures, nothing can trace it to its origin. For the nature of fish, the nature of the Buddha, and the nature of the governor of the county are originally not two; in phenomena they are forcibly divided, [but] the two are without two. The Buddha, having awakened first, established the Dharma to teach people: observing the nature of beings, he gave rise to compassion of the same essence, made them safe in their danger and showed them their happiness, so that they might return to the origin and be equal with the Buddha. Hence Jalavāhana, who rescued the fish, has already become a buddha, and the fish too will become [buddhas]; this is the proof of nonduality. The present dream — how could it be an isolated thing? It arises from the Buddha's widely [spread] teaching of releasing life, the fish's storing up of conditions for gaining release, and the human's share of increasing goodness; these together accomplished it, and its principle is certain. That being so, how can those who hear and see it not put this teaching vigorously into practice and truly contemplate the nature, so as to become equal to Jalavāhana? Postscript written on the eleventh day of the fourth month of the first year of Tiansheng [1023].

The Stele for the Pond for Releasing Life: Antiquity and the Song

Chinese Source

勅延慶院放生池碑銘（并序）

Ru-Yi Translation

Stele Inscription for the Pond for Releasing Life at Yanqing Cloister Established by Imperial Edict (with Preface)

Chinese Source

（樞密直學士中大夫尚書禮部侍郎知頴州軍州兼管內勸農使護軍彭城郡開國侯食邑一千七百戶食實封二百戶賜紫金魚袋劉筠撰）

Ru-Yi Translation

(Composed by Liu Yun, Academician of the Bureau of Military Affairs, Grand Master of the Palace, Vice Minister of Rites, Prefect of Yingzhou with Command of Its Troops and concurrently Commissioner for Encouraging Agriculture within His Jurisdiction, Protector of the Army, Founding Marquis of Pengcheng Commandery with a fief of one thousand seven hundred households and an actual fief of two hundred households, granted the purple [robe] and gold fish pouch)

Chinese Source

粵若庖犧氏之王天下也，始作罔罟，以佃以漁，取之有時、用之有節，蓋所以順殺伐而育人民也。是故四靈為畜，禽魚亡獝淰之悲；萬物由庚，草樹遂抽零之性。及乎大道既隱，淳風不還，人欲無厭，天物斯暴，蚳蝝盡取，潛蟄咸傷。聖人所嗟，君子用憫，歷代而下，申禁非一，所貴乎卒天下以仁，而登夫壽域者也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Of old, when Paoxi ruled the realm, he first made nets and snares for hunting and for fishing; [creatures] were taken in their season and used with measure. This was to accord with [the seasons of] killing and to nurture the people. Therefore, the four numinous creatures being [as] domestic animals, birds and fish had none of the grief of being startled into flight and scattering; the myriad things followed their course, and grasses and trees fulfilled their nature of sprouting and withering. When the great Way was hidden and the pure customs did not return, human desire became insatiable and the things of heaven were ravaged: ant eggs and locust larvae were all taken, and the hibernating creatures were all harmed. The sages lamented it and noble men grieved over it; in the ages since, prohibitions have been proclaimed more than once. What is valued is to lead the realm with humaneness and bring it up to the domain of long life.

Chinese Source

聖宋奄有萬國，真宗嗣致太平，既縱雉以升中，復育縠而報本，然猶儲精垂思，修墜起廢。天禧紀元之初，詔淮南江浙荊湖之地，有放生池者，俾繕完而增新之。惟四明之奧區，乃揚州之舊域，水居者眾，鱗族甚繁。蚩蚩之氓惟利是視，蠢蠢之類曾不聊生。濫彼洿池，陳諸數罟，鯤鮞亦及，鰌鱓難逃，幸增無藝之貪，孰救可欺之失。

Ru-Yi Translation

The sage Song has come to possess the myriad states, and Zhenzong succeeded to bring about great peace. Having released the pheasants and ascended to report the achievement, and further nurtured the fledglings to repay the source, he still stored up his essence and extended his thought, repairing what had fallen and raising what had been abandoned. At the beginning of the Tianxi era an edict ordered that, in the lands of Huainan, Jiang, Zhe, and Jinghu, wherever there were ponds for releasing life, they should be repaired, completed, and enlarged anew. Siming, a remote region, is of the old territory of Yangzhou; those who dwell on the water are many and the scaly tribes very numerous. The ignorant people look only to profit, and the wriggling kinds can hardly live. They overrun those pools and set out fine-meshed nets; even fry and roe are caught, and loaches and eels can hardly escape. They take it as their good fortune to increase a greed without bounds; who would rescue [the creatures] from the loss of those who can be deceived?

The Stele: Zhili and the Pond

Chinese Source

有法智大師（知禮）者，道風孤峻，行業純慤。傳天台止觀之宗，修普賢懺滌之法，申旦不寐，三歲是期。每念是身可惡如賊，志當捨離，樂在寂滅。固以比行廁之極厭，垂將効焚軀之真供。而大善知識懇勸住世，官曹府檄督責保全，實徇眾之攸依，思利他之為廣。矧遘聖神之運，宜恢方便之慈，式警群迷、聿形悲濟。

Ru-Yi Translation

There was Great Master Fazhi (Zhili), whose way and manner were solitary and lofty and whose conduct and deeds were pure and sincere. He transmitted the school of calming-and-contemplation of Tiantai and cultivated Samantabhadra's method of repentance and cleansing, not sleeping until dawn, setting three years as the term. He always thought that this body is as hateful as a thief, resolved to give it up, and delighted in tranquil extinction. Thus he compared it to the utter loathsomeness of a privy, and was about to imitate the true offering of burning the body. But great spiritual friends earnestly urged him to remain in the world, and the official dispatches of the prefectural offices pressed for his preservation; truly following what the multitude relied on, he thought it broader to benefit others. Moreover, meeting with the fortune of a sagely and divine [reign], it was fitting to extend the kindness of skillful means, so as to warn the many deluded and give shape to compassionate rescue.

Chinese Source

顧此淨剎旁有積流，窅若神淵達于巨浸，極願力以爰度，獲俞命之亟下。公私率協，終始罔愆，矗然巨防環茲，注注繇是。普化廛里之俗，博市鱗介之品，脫豫且之網朝有千計，返西江之使春盈萬數。又每歲以佛生之旦，眾大和會，浮泛綵鷁，演暢竺墳，寤流水之勝緣、識銜珠之善報，莫不競持詭類，咸造碕潯，縱之於波。快哉共歎，頳尾者戲荷而逝，呴沫者依莆而游。固以樂甚濠梁、望逾斗水，敝笱以之咸屏，枯肆謂之一空，至有斷罟折竿悔過而易業者矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

He saw that beside this pure monastery there was a body of gathered water, deep as a divine abyss and reaching to the great waters. Exerting the power of his vow, he planned it, and obtained a swift command of assent. Public and private all cooperated, without fault from beginning to end; a great embankment rose up to encircle it, and [the water] poured in through it. He transformed universally the customs of the shops and lanes and bought up widely all kinds of scaled and shelled creatures; those freed from the nets of Yu Qie numbered a thousand in a morning, and those returned as messengers to the West River filled ten thousand in a spring. Further, every year on the morning of the Buddha's birth, the assembly gathers in great harmony; colored boats float on the water and the Indian scriptures are expounded. Awakening to the excellent connection of Jalavāhana and recognizing the good reward of [the creature] carrying a pearl in its mouth, none fails to vie in bringing strange kinds to the shore and releasing them into the waves. "How delightful!" all sigh together; the red-tailed play among the lotuses and go off, those spewing foam swim along the rushes. Thus their joy surpasses [that above] the Hao and Liang, and their hope exceeds a dipper of water; broken fish-traps are for this reason all set aside, the dried-fish shops are said to be emptied, and there are even those who break their nets, snap their poles, repent their faults, and change their trade.

The Stele: The Ancients, Government, and Diet

Chinese Source

嗚呼！淳古之俗前志有云：雜人獸之居，靡相為害；食草木之實，各遂所養。聖哲繼作，播種是教。以前民用篾聞餒者。是故二儀訢合、百嘉茂暢，龜龍在宮沼、胎卵不殪殈，雖火化䆮變，而血祀有經。故曰獺祭魚，然後虞人入澤梁。又曰釣而不網，田則不漁。皆所以昭上之德，而塞下之違也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Alas! Of the customs of pure antiquity, the earlier records say: "Humans and beasts dwelt intermingled without harming one another; eating the fruits of grasses and trees, each fulfilled its nourishment." Sages and wise men arose in succession and taught sowing and planting, to anticipate the people's needs, and none were heard of who starved. Therefore the two modes were in joyful harmony and the hundred blessings flourished; tortoises and dragons were in the palace ponds, and wombs and eggs were not destroyed or addled; and though cooking by fire gradually changed [things], blood sacrifices had their rules. Hence it is said: "When the otter offers its fish, then the forester enters the weirs." It is also said: "He fished with a line but not with a net"; "when hunting, they did not fish." All this was to make clear the virtue of those above and to block the transgressions of those below.

Chinese Source

自古致治之君，皆以好生為本，每嚴戒令務抑末游。其如利人之所誘，蕩而忘返。害既為甚，法不勝姦，繼以天災，遂廢嗇事，家乏兼晨之爨、野無遺秉之利，乃至旱乾水溢、山童澤涸，昆蟲為孽、道殪相望，強暴之徒雚蒲是聚，椎牛屠狗鬻鹽盜醞。豈唯棄失本業，率多抵冐刑戮，得非歛飫腥味。天殘物性，犯道家之明忌；事必好還，背春秋之美談，政之所敗者乎。

Ru-Yi Translation

The rulers of old who brought about good government all took the love of life as fundamental, always made their prohibitions and orders strict, and strove to suppress the inessential occupations. But as people were lured by profit, they were swept away and forgot to return. When the harm had become severe and the law could not overcome wickedness, natural calamities followed, and then farming was abandoned: households lacked fire for two mornings' cooking, and the fields had no profit of leftover sheaves; it came to drought and floods, bare mountains and dried marshes, insects becoming plagues, and the dead along the roads within sight of one another; violent men gathered in the reeds and rushes, clubbing oxen, butchering dogs, selling [illicit] salt, and stealing brews. Not only did they abandon their basic occupations; most of them ran into punishment and execution. Was this not from gorging on rank flavors? To cut short by heaven the nature of things violates the clear prohibitions of the Daoists; that affairs surely come back [on oneself] goes against the fine sayings of the Spring and Autumn — is this not where government fails?

Chinese Source

不有慈悲之士，孰臻覺寤之本？且曰凡有血氣同一觸體，盡諸沙界共一真性。庸詎恣口腹之慾，結輪迴之業？其理不昧，緣心可觀，則《金光明經》論之詳矣。況復吳越之區膏膄兼倍，漠漠粳稻油油麻苧，陸則有苞筍薑彙，水則有海苔菰苜，固足以旅踐嘉珍、豐溢兼豆，亦何必剖豢豹之胎、嚼魚子之鬧，滋味煎其府藏、香芳腐其骨髓。食氣既勝，腊毒增厚，而殃病短折者，亦未必不由茲也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Without people of compassion, who would reach the root of awakening? Moreover they say: all that has blood and breath shares one body of sensation, and throughout all the sand-like worlds [beings] share one true nature. How then can one indulge the desires of mouth and belly and form the karma of the cycle of rebirth? Its principle is not obscure and can be observed through the mind; the Golden Light Sūtra discusses it in detail. Moreover, the region of Wu and Yue is doubly fertile: vast [fields of] rice, glossy hemp and ramie; on land there are bamboo shoots, ginger, and their kind; in the water there are sea laver, wild rice, and alfalfa. This is surely enough to set out fine delicacies and fill the dishes to overflowing. Why must one cut open the womb of a reared leopard or chew the roe of fish? Flavors scorch the inner organs, fragrances rot the bones and marrow. When the vapor of food prevails, the poison of dried meat grows thick; and calamity, illness, and early death are perhaps not unconnected with this.

Chinese Source

夫先聖立法本以馭眾，大小貴賤不相踰越。惟辟玉食順時以視膳，惟郊特牲因禮以貴臭。豈料後世有冐於寵利，罔知紀極，管氏設鏤簋、秊氏旅泰山，乃至養食客之三千、探牛心之一割，騁嗜奔慾，窮奢極侈，豪傑胥傚，風俗益訛。固雖罄川陸之毛、殫漁獵之力，驅以就役莫充其求。是知其源至深、其來有漸，機權不足以為禦，鈇鉞不足以用威，故曰善人為邦百年，然後可以勝殘去殺。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now the former sages established laws in order to govern the multitude, so that great and small, noble and lowly did not overstep one another. Only the ruler had jade food, inspecting the meals according to the season; only at the suburban sacrifice was there a special victim, honoring the odor according to ritual. Who would have expected that later generations would be greedy for favor and profit and know no limits: Guan [Zhong] set out carved food vessels and the Ji family made the lü sacrifice to Mount Tai, even to keeping three thousand retainers and probing for the single cut of an ox's heart, galloping after appetites and racing after desires, utterly extravagant, the powerful imitating one another and customs growing ever more corrupt. Thus, even if one exhausted the produce of rivers and lands and spent all the strength of fishing and hunting, driving [people] to the labor, their demands could not be satisfied. From this we know that its source is very deep and its coming has been gradual; schemes and power are not enough to restrain it, and axe and halberd are not enough to exert authority. Hence it is said: "If good people governed a state for a hundred years, they could then overcome cruelty and do away with killing."

The Stele: The Present Reign and the Author's Apology

Chinese Source

洪惟我朝在宥而治，以聖繼聖垂六十年，率上仁而綏群品、用柔道而懷獷俗，尊老氏之三寶、為大雄之外護。大師所以顧逢盛旦，集此妙果、介其祕祉、仰佑慈宸、表洪施之無疆、實含靈之允賴。謂是於益、宜有鋪昭。

Ru-Yi Translation

Vast indeed: our dynasty governs by letting [the people] be, sage succeeding sage for nearly sixty years, leading with supreme humaneness to pacify all kinds of beings, using gentleness to win over wild customs, honoring the three treasures of Laozi and acting as the outer protector of the Great Hero. This is why the Great Master, meeting with this flourishing dawn, gathered this wondrous fruit, to aid its hidden blessing, looking up to protect the compassionate throne, manifesting a vast giving without bounds — truly what living beings rely on. It is said that this is beneficial, and it is fitting that it be set out and made known.

Chinese Source

予病夫也、曩在禁林、尋墮職業、旋承官乏、待罪中司。自時辱書、猥託敘事、而不知中乾已甚，軋思無堪，避讓有初，阻修罔愬，今遂偃藩之適方存喉息之微。而又廣印大師（智環）疊寓訊函，督茲銘述。強攀逸駕，僅成累句。繹顏公之妙作，嗤鄙自彰。諷周沼之靈篇，揄揚曷既。辭不迨意，取媿群英。其銘曰：

Ru-Yi Translation

I am a sick man. Formerly in the Forbidden Grove, I soon fell from my office, then took up a vacancy in office and awaited punishment in the Central Censorate. Since then I have been honored with letters humbly entrusting me with narrating the matter, but [he] did not know how weak I had become within; my thoughts were constrained and incapable, I declined at first, and, obstructed and far away, I could not explain. Now I have retired to the comfort of a frontier post, just preserving the faintest breath in my throat. And Great Master Guangyin (Zhihuan) repeatedly sent letters urging me to compose this inscription. Forcing myself to grasp at a fleeing carriage, I have barely produced a string of phrases. Unrolling Lord Yan's wondrous work, my rusticity shows of itself; reciting the numinous poem of the Zhou pond, how could my praise be enough? My words do not reach my meaning, and I am ashamed before all the talented. The inscription says:

The Inscription

Chinese Source

天地之大，仁聖是則，巍巍居尊，<br>生生為德。順考古道，祇受民時，<br>兆人允殖，萬物由儀。易有中孚，<br>書稱咸若，恩信所加，飛潛自樂。

Ru-Yi Translation

Great as heaven and earth are, / the humane sage takes them as his rule; / lofty, dwelling in honor, / he makes the giving of life his virtue. / Following and examining the ancient way, / he reverently grants the people their seasons; / the millions of people truly flourish, / the myriad things follow their proper course. / The Changes has "Inner Truth"; / the Documents speaks of "all in accord"; / where kindness and good faith are extended, / the flying and the submerged are happy of themselves.

Chinese Source

末俗浸巧，暴殄滋多，麟鳳去棷，<br>鯨鯢駭波。惟天聰明，聿求元聖，<br>邦家大同，幅員底靖。皇哉有宋，<br>疊雉重明，澤均敦葦，惠及跂行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Later customs grew ever more cunning; / wanton destruction multiplied; / the unicorn and phoenix left the thickets; / whales were startled in the waves. / Heaven alone is perceptive and bright; / it sought out the primal sage; / the state attained great unity, / its whole breadth brought to peace. / August is the Song! / Layered ramparts, doubled brightness; / its bounty reaches even the thick reeds, / its kindness extends to crawling things.

Chinese Source

乃眷南服，鮮食為盛，繕治舊防，<br>昭蘇物命。爰有開士，化茲一方，<br>就其寶剎，疎厥金塘。壁立大堤，<br>練澄百丈，灔灔風光，昭昭景象。

Ru-Yi Translation

Then he looked with favor on the southern domain, / where eating fresh [fish] was prevalent; / he repaired the old embankments / and revived the lives of creatures. / Then there was an awakened being / who transformed this region; / beside his jeweled monastery / he opened up the golden pond. / A great dike stands like a wall; / clear as silk for a hundred zhang; / rippling, the wind and light; / bright and clear, the scene.

Chinese Source

日募檀施，歲舉懺儀，魚鳥聱取，<br>刷蕩瀾漪。憯怛忠利，國教胥洎，<br>𪫟惕隱惻，人端斯至。自古及今，<br>惟善可欽，慚非吉頌，聊代虞箴。

Ru-Yi Translation

Phenomenal Objects or the Three Truths?

Jingxi says: "Since the opening title is so, the separate explanations below the title are, in principle, not easy." And the Gates of Nonduality says: "Take the particular as the general, and you will know it by matching it with the text." So the title and the text of the sūtra reveal each other as general and particular and must not be arbitrarily made different. Hence we know that all sixteen are the perfect threefold contemplation.

"This Mind Makes the Buddha; This Mind Is the Buddha"

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence the sūtra says: "Every buddha is a body of the dharma-realm, entering into the mind and thought of beings. Therefore, when the mind contemplates the Buddha, this very mind is itself the thirty-two marks. This mind makes the buddha; this mind is the buddha." The Commentary explains this as the mutual resonance between response and appeal and as understanding entering and according. Without the first explanation, the contemplation would not be contemplating the Buddha; without the second, beings and the Buddha would differ in essence. The two explanations complete each other; this is the present method of contemplation. The Commentary says: "The Buddha's dharma-realm essence pervades everywhere; in the samādhi of buddha-recollection understanding enters and accords; hence it says 'entering into the mind and thought.'" If not by the samādhi of the threefold calming in a single mind, how could one discuss responding and entering together with the dharma-realm? "This mind makes the buddha" clarifies that it is the transformation of the mind itself: the whole nature becomes cultivation. "This mind is the buddha": outside the mind there is no buddha, outside the buddha there is no mind. This makes the practitioner, whatever single object he contemplates, know that all is transformed by the mind and its whole essence is mind. Since it is wholly mind, how could mind see mind? As a finger does not touch itself and a knife does not wound itself. Hence the Pratyutpanna says: "What I recollect I then see; the mind makes the buddha; the mind itself sees the mind." And further on it says: "The mind does not know the mind; the mind does not see the mind. When the mind has conceptions, it is folly; without conceptions, it is nirvāṇa." Calming and Contemplation sets up the contemplation of the middle on the basis of this; when the contemplation of the middle is established, it is spontaneously three. This passage of the Pratyutpanna differs slightly from the present sūtra in fullness and brevity, but its purport is not different.

Therefore Zhizhe of Tiantai first has the practitioner draw near a good teacher to learn the place of repentance, which is the place reached by the inconceivable contemplation of principle. Next he shows that the methods of repentance are of three kinds. First, repentance by ritual action: what body and speech do follows the rules of the method throughout. Second, repentance by seizing a mark: with the mind concentrated, one visualizes, taking the arising of a mark as the goal. Third, repentance of no-generation: clearly understanding that one's own mind is itself empty and that offense and merit have no owner, one contemplates the true mark of action and sees the original source of offense — the dharma-realm perfectly fused, suchness pure. Although the methods are three, they are practiced at one time; one may rather lack each earlier one, but must not fall short in each later one. No-generation is most essential; seizing a mark still allows latitude. For the source of wondrous contemplation is the master of the Great Vehicle: extinguishing offenses is like turning over the great earth, so that the grasses and trees all wither; revealing virtue is like shining on a clear river, so that the myriad phenomena all appear. When this contemplation of principle guides the phenomenal rites, then a single bow or a single circling dissolves offenses of eons as numerous as dust, and a single lamp or a single [offering of] water brings merit equal to empty space. Hence, while the mouth speaks the repentance of the six faculties, the mind keeps the three methods of repentance. Only when one sets the mind in this way is one fit to advance in practice.

Parts One to Three: The Place of Practice, the Body, and Offering

Ru-Yi Translation

The first, the method of adorning and purifying the place of practice, and the second, the method of purifying the body, can both be seen [there] (and so on). The third, the method of cultivating offering with the three kinds of action: when the practitioner makes offering with the three kinds of action, he must give rise to a thought that is hard to conceive and part from the mind that takes things as real. Whether incense or flowers, their essence is the dharma-realm; the one who offers and what is offered are by nature originally truly empty. Their measure pervades everywhere, issuing forth without end; their nature is eternally abiding, extending through without omission. Not only do they reach everywhere in this world and in other directions; they also enter universally into the future and the past. Though it is called giving material things, through material things one reaches the Dharma; this is the true Dharma-offering, which can nourish the Dharma-body. The five fruits are all eternal and the four virtues are all revealed. Hence one silently visualizes the verse of incense and flowers (and so on).

Parts Four to Six: Inviting, Praising, and Bowing to the Three Jewels

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The fourth, the method of inviting the Three Jewels; the fifth, the method of praising the Three Jewels; the sixth, the method of bowing to the Three Jewels (and so on). First, when bowing to the Buddha, one deeply knows that the Buddha's essence is not apart from one's own mind: the same single source of awakening, perfectly illuminating all dharmas. The buddhas, awakened, give rise to compassion of the same essence; sentient beings, deluded, forcibly undergo all illusions. Compassion and suffering face each other, and so appeal and response are accomplished. One body reaches everywhere before all the buddhas; one bow universally dissolves boundless offenses. Hence one silently visualizes the verse of bowing to the Buddha (and so on). Next, when bowing to the Dharma, one deeply knows that the virtues of the fruit realized by the buddhas, the principle-mind about which sentient beings are deluded, all the gates of practice, and the boundless teachings and paths are apart from defilement and pure, and can serve as the norm for sages and ordinary people; according with this Dharma-gate, one pays reverence with the three kinds of action. Hence one silently visualizes the verse of bowing to the Dharma (and so on). Next, when bowing to the Saṅgha: these are the sages and worthies of the three vehicles. Although they are at the causal stage, they have already reached the true source, the same as the Buddha; their loving-kindness without object is the same as the Buddha's, responding without planning. Now with the three kinds of action I appeal to the holy assembly, that what is perfumed by their four vows may extinguish the roots of my offenses and generate in me the fruit of happiness. Hence one silently visualizes the verse of bowing to the Saṅgha (and so on).

Chinese Source

次運普懺之心，用成曠濟之道。若不然者，豈但不成大道，抑亦難滅眾愆。何者？我從無始已來造罪之際，自身為業本、眾生為惡緣，生生於彼愛憎、處處於他婬殺。況一切男子是我父、一切女人是我母，無不業累相關，悉為煩惱所覆。今運同體慈悲如理懺悔，盡妄染際、徹真性源，仰答四恩、旁資三有，有情之類稱性遍該，故默想（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, one exerts the mind of universal repentance, in order to accomplish the path of vast deliverance. If one does not, not only will one fail to accomplish the great path, but it will also be hard to extinguish one's many faults. Why? Since beginningless time, when I created offenses, my own body was the root of action and sentient beings were the evil conditions; life after life I had love and hate toward them, place after place I committed lust and killing against them. Moreover, all men are my fathers and all women are my mothers; there are none not bound up with me by karmic entanglement, all covered over by afflictions. Now, exerting the loving-kindness and compassion of the same essence, I repent in accord with principle, exhausting the limit of false defilement and penetrating the source of the true nature, repaying above the four kindnesses and assisting on every side the three existences, so that all kinds of sentient beings are pervaded and included in accord with the nature. Hence one silently visualizes (and so on).

Part Seven: Repentance of the Six Faculties and the Four Further Repentances

Chinese Source

第七懺悔六根及四悔法，夫六根之罪願悉消除，四悔之心願皆成就。初修懺悔者，所謂發露眾罪也。何故爾耶？如草木之根，露之則枯、覆之則茂，故善根宜覆，則眾善皆生；罪根宜露，則眾罪皆滅。今對三寶真實知見，照我善惡之際、窮我本末之邊，故原始要終、從微至著，悉皆發露更不覆藏。

Ru-Yi Translation

The seventh, the method of repenting the six faculties and of the four [further] repentances: may the offenses of the six faculties all be dissolved, and may the minds of the four repentances all be accomplished. First, cultivating repentance: this is what is called revealing all offenses. Why so? Like the roots of grasses and trees: exposed, they wither; covered, they flourish. So wholesome roots should be covered, and then all goods arise; the roots of offense should be exposed, and then all offenses are extinguished. Now, before the true knowing and seeing of the Three Jewels, which illuminate the bounds of my good and evil and exhaust the limits of my beginning and end, I trace them to their origin and follow them to their end, from the subtle to the manifest, and reveal them all, concealing nothing further.

Chinese Source

所謂逆順十心通於迷悟兩派，故迷真造惡則有十心，逆涅槃流順生死海。始從無始無明起愛起見，終至作一闡提撥因撥果，所以沈淪生死無解脫期。今遇三寶勝緣，能生一念正信，先人後己、改往修來，故起十心，逆生死流、順涅槃道，始則深信因果不亡，終則圓悟心性本寂，一一翻破上之十心。不明前之十心，則不識造罪之相；若非後之十心，則不知修懺之法。故欲行五悔，先運十心，故默想（云云）。想已當說六根罪過。

Ru-Yi Translation

What are called the ten contrary and compliant minds run through the two streams of delusion and awakening. So, deluded about the true and creating evil, there are ten minds, going contrary to the stream of nirvāṇa and complying with the sea of birth-and-death. They begin from beginningless ignorance giving rise to craving and to views, and end in becoming an icchantika who denies cause and denies effect; therefore one sinks in birth-and-death with no time of liberation. Now, meeting the excellent condition of the Three Jewels, one can give rise to a single thought of correct faith, putting others before oneself, changing the past and cultivating the future; so one gives rise to ten minds, going contrary to the stream of birth-and-death and complying with the path of nirvāṇa. They begin with deeply believing that cause and effect do not perish and end with perfectly awakening that the mind-nature is originally still, overturning and breaking one by one the ten minds above. Without understanding the first ten minds, one does not recognize the marks of creating offenses; without the latter ten minds, one does not know the method of cultivating repentance. Therefore, wishing to practice the five repentances, one first exerts the ten minds. Hence one silently visualizes (and so on). Having visualized, one should speak the offenses of the six faculties.

Ru-Yi Translation

Now this text of the repentance of the six faculties was not composed by a human teacher; it is the sacred word proclaimed in person, spoken by our original teacher Śākyamuni: the dharma-gate of removing obstructions that the great being Samantabhadra shows to practitioners of the samādhi. For he saw clearly the source from which sentient beings' faults arise and the marks of their creating offenses, and further knew that all dharmas are originally still and extinguished, their whole essence spiritually bright, without marks and unconditioned, without defilement and without obstruction, mutually including, mutually containing, mutually bringing forth, mutually generating, all true and all suchness, neither broken nor established. Deluded feeling, dark and agitated, is wild and foolish on meeting any matter, taking bodhi and nirvāṇa to be afflictions and birth-and-death. Therefore the great being shows the method of repentance and opens the gate of liberation, causing them to understand that ignorance is itself clarity and to know that bondage is without bondage, and, on this wondrous principle, to repent these deep faults. Hence when repenting the offenses of the eye faculty, one at once sees the eternal form of the buddhas. Next, when repenting the offenses of the ear faculty, one at once hears the wondrous sound of the buddhas. And so on until, repenting the mind faculty, one at once awakens to the abode of the instant, where the three bodies are one essence and the four virtues are clearly present. To put it essentially, all marks of offense are nothing other than the true mark; the ten evils, the five heinous [crimes], the four grave [offenses], and the eight wrong [paths] are all dharma-gates of the poison of principle, all the original function of defilement in the nature. Taking this as what repents, and taking this very thing as what is contemplated, delusion and wisdom are originally suchness, and principle and phenomena are one boundary; what obstructs and what is obstructed both vanish, what repents and what is repented are both forgotten; all day exerting effort, all day without fabrication. This is called repentance of the mark of no offense; it is also called the greatly adorned repentance, and also called the supreme and foremost repentance.

Taking this contemplation of principle of no-generation as the master of repentance, one then uses the fabricated phenomenal rites as the conditions of repentance. As for the phenomenal rites: casting the five limbs to the ground like Mount Tai collapsing, reproaching one's own mind with words and tears falling together, crushing feeling and breaking will, confessing offenses and begging for pity. When such phenomenal practice is diligent, the contemplation of principle advances all the more. It is like the method of washing: though the cleaning is done by clear water, if lye and soap are not added, grime and grease are hard to remove. Hence the main and the auxiliary practiced together are like eyes and feet relying on each other; not only do they extinguish offenses, they can realize the true. Hence if the repentance of the six faculties is accomplished, the purity of the six faculties can be attained. If perfect contemplation is not taken as the master, it is not called a Great Vehicle method of repentance. Even if it can extinguish offenses, it only escapes the three evil paths; even if it can generate good, it does not leave the three realms. First know this intent, then recite the text, so that by practicing both together one achieves great benefit (and so on).

The Four Further Repentances: Requesting and Rejoicing

Chinese Source

I now, in accord with principle, direct my thought and before each and every buddha request them to teach the wondrous Dharma. In this very instant, in the ten directions there are again buddhas as numerous as the dust motes above announcing their entry into nirvāṇa; before all of them I request them to dwell long in the world. It is crucial in using the mind first to exert this mind, and only then to state the words (and so on).

Good is the cause of happiness; now, by rejoicing and helping it to completion, one gives all sentient beings the fruit of peace and happiness. The Four Further Repentances: Dedicating and Vowing

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

First, turning phenomena toward principle: originally, because of what is inherently possessed in principle there is phenomenal function; all cultivation and realization do not go beyond the nature of principle; sentient beings forcibly cling and reckon it as conditioned; now one turns this mind toward the limit of reality. Since one now seeks birth in [the Land of] Peaceful Nourishment, one must adorn it with pure action; without the strong pull of the power of vows, how can one have right mindfulness at the end of life? Ru-Yi Translation

Now, as I presume to comment on the intent of this passage: above, it used the four phrases of mutual illumination to clarify the inconceivable object and wisdom. It feared that a doubter would say: "Whenever the inconceivable is set up, the path of the mind's activity is cut off and the way of words is severed. Yet on the contrary you set up a fourfold distinction, and every phrase says 'illuminate.' Are not the phrases speech, and illumination thought? How is it called the inconceivable object and wisdom?" Therefore it further set up eight phrases, pairing illumination with speech, to settle what is right and dismiss what is wrong. The intent is this: one who illuminates having forgotten feeling only then illuminates; one who speaks apart from the written words only then speaks; one who illuminates like this ought to speak; one who speaks like this ought to illuminate. So, because feeling is forgotten, illumination is perfectly fused, and one can then speak freely; because one is apart from the written words, speech is free, and one can then illuminate in a perfectly fused way. Therefore the four phrases all display what is gained: all day long one speaks and illuminates, yet the way of words and thought is cut off; this is the sages' speech and the sages' silence. If one illuminates while stuck in feeling and speaks while clinging to words, then whether speaking or illuminating, it neither exhausts nor makes clear. Why? Illuminating because stuck in feeling, the illumination becomes partial; speaking while clinging to words, the speech is surely obstructed. How could they fuse with each other like the four phrases above? Therefore the four phrases about the partial and the foolish all rebuke their faults, so as to uphold the name of the inconceivable. In the text, because the phrasing is interwoven, it seems at first hard to see; if one settles it by this intent, then the way of speech and realization is clear of itself and the name of the inconceivable is again revealed. Still fearing that this general account may not be understood, I further annotate each phrase with this intent.

Ru-Yi Translation

Chapter Fusing and Reconciling the Tiantai Teaching and the Awakening of Faith

Chinese Source

有客忽問余曰：《起信論》於天台四教攝屬何耶？余偶對曰：《起信論》是唐朝藏法師製疏申通，天台不見文句解釋。此乃各是一家製作，難可和會。

Ru-Yi Translation

A guest suddenly asked me: "To which of the four teachings of Tiantai does the Awakening of Faith belong?" I happened to answer: "For the Awakening of Faith, Dharma Master [Fa]zang of the Tang made a commentary expounding it; Tiantai has no textual explanation of it. These are works of separate schools and can hardly be reconciled."

Chinese Source

客乃動容問曰：聞天台一宗最能融會，如云以五時八教判釋東流一代時教，罄無不盡。此語何為？余乃立理對曰：只恐不許天台融會。若信天台攝屬，容有其理。則起信攝屬何難，亦乃自見諸家妍醜。今試為先將教攝論，次為以教會疏。且夫此論宗百洛叉經，而首題大乘，則理合通於衍門三教。故天台《淨名玄義》云：佛性唯識等論，通申大乘三教。唯識尚具三教，起信何不具三？況與佛性大同小異。今且於論初後，撮略教文以對三教。論以一心為宗，乃云：總攝世出世法。此則正在圓門，亦兼餘二。真如門有離言依言空不空義，則三教之理明焉。生滅門明初發心住，能少分見於法身，八相成道（《涅槃》云：十住能少分見法身，見不了了。天台判為圓位。妙樂云：唯《華嚴》、《起信》明初住八相最為顯著），豈非圓位耶？次第翻九相，豈非別位耶？八地得無功用道，豈非通教被接之位耶？略引此文，諸可悉例。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest's expression changed and he asked: "I have heard that the Tiantai school is best able to fuse and reconcile; for instance it is said that with the five periods and the eight teachings it classifies and explains the entire teaching that flowed east, leaving nothing out. What does this mean?" I then set up a principle and answered: "I only fear that [you] will not allow Tiantai to fuse and reconcile. If you trust Tiantai's classification, there is reason in it; then what difficulty is there in classifying the Awakening of Faith? And one also sees for oneself the beauty and ugliness of the various schools. Now let me try first to classify the treatise by the teachings, and next to reconcile the commentary with the teachings. Now, this treatise takes a hundred lakṣa of sūtras as its cardinal doctrine, and its title opens with 'Great Vehicle,' so in principle it should extend to the three teachings of the Great Vehicle gate. Hence Tiantai's Profound Meaning of the Vimalakīrti says: 'Treatises such as the Buddha-Nature and Consciousness-Only expound throughout the three teachings of the Great Vehicle.' If even Consciousness-Only contains three teachings, how could the Awakening of Faith not contain three? Moreover, it is largely the same as the Buddha-Nature, with minor differences. Now, from the beginning and end of the treatise, let me gather a few passages of the teaching to match the three teachings. The treatise takes the one mind as its cardinal doctrine and says: 'It gathers in all worldly and world-transcending dharmas.' This lies properly in the perfect gate and also includes the other two. The gate of suchness has the meanings of apart from words and relying on words, empty and not empty; so the principles of the three teachings are clear. The gate of arising and ceasing clarifies that at the abiding of first arousing the mind one can see the Dharma-body in a small portion and [display] the eight aspects of attaining the path (the Nirvāṇa says: 'At the ten abidings one can see the Dharma-body in a small portion, but does not see it clearly.' Tiantai judges this a perfect stage. Miaole says: 'Only the Avataṃsaka and the Awakening of Faith make the eight aspects at the first abiding most clearly manifest'); is this not a perfect stage? Overturning the nine marks in sequence — is this not a distinct stage? Attaining the effortless path at the eighth ground — is this not the stage of the shared teaching being received [into a higher one]? I cite these passages briefly; all the rest can be inferred by analogy."

Fazang's Commentary: One Track of the Distinct Teaching

Chinese Source

客曰：藏師製疏，分齊如何？對曰：賢首立義，望於天台，乃是別教一途之說，未是通方別教。何者？別有教道證道，彼則唯論教道。別有四門被機，彼乃只論雙。亦別有自他橫竪，彼乃獨論自行竪入。別有多義，彼所不云，未是別教通方，蓋是一途之說。客乃難曰：彼疏隨緣不變為宗，天台亦以隨緣不變證於三因體遍乃是圓意，何言別耶？余曰：藏師雖用圓名，而成別義。何者？彼云：真如隨緣作一切法，而真如體性常不變。却謬引《釋論》云：無情唯有法性而無佛性。此則名雖在圓，而義成別。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "How far does Master [Fa]zang's commentary reach?" I answered: "The doctrine Xianshou sets up, viewed from Tiantai, is the account of one track of the distinct teaching, not the distinct teaching in its full range. Why? The distinct has the path of teaching and the path of realization; he discusses only the path of teaching. The distinct has four gates suited to capacities; he discusses only the double. The distinct also has self and other, horizontal and vertical; he discusses only one's own practice and vertical entry. The distinct has many meanings that he does not mention; it is not the distinct teaching in its full range but the account of one track." The guest objected: "His commentary takes following conditions yet unchanging as its cardinal doctrine; Tiantai also uses following conditions yet unchanging to prove that the essence of the three causes is pervasive, and that is the perfect intent. How can you call it distinct?" I said: "Though Master [Fa]zang uses perfect names, he arrives at a distinct meaning. Why? He says: 'Suchness, following conditions, makes all dharmas, while the essential nature of suchness is always unchanging.' And he wrongly cites the Great Treatise: 'The insentient have only the dharma-nature and not buddha-nature.' So though the name lies in the perfect, the meaning becomes distinct."

Following Conditions and the Fixed: Whose Doctrine?

Chinese Source

客曰：別明凝然為理。今以隨緣真如而為別理，此據何文？余返曰：別理隨緣，其文稍顯。凝然為理，出自何文？客曰：此宗講者皆有此言，而未見文疏所出。余曰：盛將隨緣以凝然二理，簡於性相二宗，此乃出自賢首，天台未見此文。據理，隨緣未為圓極。彼宗尚自判終教，未及於圓，豈天台之圓同彼之終？須知若凝然、若隨緣，但據帶方便義邊，皆屬別教。客曰：天台立別理隨緣，文據如何？對曰：《止觀》立別教發心境云：只觀根塵一念為迷解本，記別如來藏為善不善因。妙樂云：別教從無住本立一切法，乃云無明覆理，能覆所覆俱名無住。又云：真如在迷生一切法。又云：無明為因能生九界，必須法性為緣。文證若此，足可依憑。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "The distinct clarifies that the fixed is principle. Now you take suchness that follows conditions as the principle of the distinct; on what text is this based?" I countered: "That the principle of the distinct follows conditions, the texts show fairly clearly. That the fixed is principle — from what text does it come?" The guest said: "The lecturers of this school all say so, but I have not seen the text or commentary it comes from." I said: "To use the two principles of following conditions and the fixed to sift between the schools of nature and of marks comes from Xianshou; I have not seen this text in Tiantai. In principle, following conditions is not yet the perfect ultimate. His school still judges it the final teaching, not yet reaching the perfect; how could Tiantai's perfect be the same as his final? You must know that whether fixed or following conditions, insofar as one goes by the side of the meaning that is accompanied by expedients, all belong to the distinct teaching." The guest said: "What is the textual basis for Tiantai setting up the principle of the distinct as following conditions?" I answered: "Calming and Contemplation, setting up the object for arousing the mind in the distinct teaching, says: 'Only contemplate the single thought of faculty and object as the root of delusion and understanding.' The Notes [explain it as] the tathāgata-garbha of the distinct being the cause of the good and the not good. Miaole says: 'The distinct teaching establishes all dharmas from the root of non-abiding,' and says: 'Ignorance covers principle; what covers and what is covered are both called non-abiding.' He also says: 'Suchness, in delusion, produces all dharmas.' He also says: 'Ignorance as cause can produce the nine realms, but it must have the dharma-nature as condition.' With texts proving it like this, one can certainly rely on them."

The Stages in the Treatise and the Two Schools

Chinese Source

客曰：疏義在別，此則已明。論具三教，更冀剖析。余曰：儻有微礙，盡望陳述。客曰：真如門屬理，具三教可然。生滅門論於地住修證，須依一轍，別則始終俱別、圓則初後俱圓，如何三教位次並陳？對曰：此論宗經既多明理，是故非一。理既不一，依位修證如何一途？如《華嚴經》明圓初住，乃云初發心時便成正覺等。後明地位，却云八地得無功用道。又《仁王》一偈頌中，三賢十聖住果報在圓，十四般若為別，五忍屬通。此一兩經明位尚爾，況此論宗百洛叉經，豈可止有一翻地位。文中缺於三藏位者，既以大乘標題，只可唯明衍教。

Ru-Yi Translation

The guest said: "That the meaning of the commentary lies in the distinct is now clear. That the treatise contains three teachings — I hope you will analyze it further." I said: "If there is any slight obstacle, please state it all." The guest said: "That the gate of suchness belongs to principle and contains three teachings may be so. But the gate of arising and ceasing discusses the cultivation and realization of the grounds and abidings, which must follow a single track: in the distinct, beginning and end are both distinct; in the perfect, first and last are both perfect. How can the stages of three teachings be set out together?" I answered: "Since the sūtras this treatise takes as its cardinal doctrine are many in clarifying principle, they are therefore not one. Since principle is not one, how could cultivation and realization according to stages follow one track? For instance, the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, clarifying the first abiding of the perfect, says, 'At the time of first arousing the mind one at once accomplishes right awakening,' and so on; later, clarifying the stages of the grounds, it says, 'At the eighth ground one attains the effortless path.' Also, within a single verse of the Humane Kings, 'the three worthies and ten sages abide in the fruit and reward' lies in the perfect, the fourteen prajñās are the distinct, and the five acceptances belong to the shared. If one or two sūtras clarify the stages like this, how much more this treatise, which takes a hundred lakṣa of sūtras as its cardinal doctrine — how could it have only one set of grounds and stages? That the text lacks the stages of the Tripiṭaka [teaching] is because, since it takes 'Great Vehicle' as its title, it can only clarify the teachings of the Great Vehicle."

The guest said: "Now Xianshou, for instance, was learned in both nature and marks and in stage succeeded to the four reliances; why should his explanations of meaning fall wholly below Tiantai?" I said: "Bodhisattvas spreading the teaching each suit the capacities and occasions; there is a class of capacities that should hear the account of a single track, and so he made this exposition. It is not necessarily that the four reliances have [anything] not understood." The guest said: "If each has its own excellence and each suits the capacities and occasions, why must one be classified under the other?" I answered: "Since you know that the two schools each suit the capacities and occasions, why did you need to ask? Since you had me classify, [I answered] as set out above. If one speaks of suiting capacities, there is no need to reconcile them." The guest then said, "Yes, yes," and withdrew.
