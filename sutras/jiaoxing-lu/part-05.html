The Second Answers to Japan, the Answers to Chan Master Tai, the Letters with Chan Master Ning of Tiantong, and the Contents of Fascicle Five

The fifth part finishes fascicle four and gives the table of contents of fascicle five. It begins with a short note from Daoyin's Thatched Hut Record on the Japanese monks who came to study with Zhili's disciple Guangzhi. Then come Zhili's answers to a second set of ten questions from Japan, whose author is not known. The questions set Faxiang (Yogācāra) positions against the Tiantai reading of the Lotus: whether śrāvakas of fixed nature, icchantikas, and grasses and trees can become buddhas, where the "Entrustment" chapter belongs, and how to read the dragon girl's buddhahood. Two sets of questions from the Chan Master Qingtai follow (ten, then three more), on ignorance and the dharma-nature, how the first buddha awakened, and how the insentient teach the Dharma. Zhili answers mostly from inherent inclusion and the threefold contemplation. The last major section is the exchange of letters with Chan Master Zining of Tiantong. Zining objected to a story in Pointing to the Essentials about Bodhidharma's three disciples, which Zhili had taken from Guifeng Zongmi. Afterwords by Jizhong (1070) and Daoyin trace how the passage came to be revised. The part ends with the heading of fascicle five and its list of thirty-three items.

The Thatched Hut Record on the Questions from Japan

Chinese Source

草庵錄紀日本國師問事

Ru-Yi Translation

The Thatched Hut Record on the Matter of the Questions from the Master of Japan

Chinese Source

日本國師嘗遣徒抗海，致問二十於法智。法智答之，皆深於理致也。後廣智嗣法席，復遣其徒紹良等二人，齎金字《法華經》，如贄見之禮，因哀泣致敬，請學於輪下，三載其道大成，還國大洪台學。曾魯公碑其塔，具道之。

Ru-Yi Translation

The master of Japan once sent disciples across the sea to put twenty questions to Fazhi. Fazhi answered them, all reaching deep into the principle. Later, when Guangzhi had succeeded to the Dharma-seat, [the Japanese] again sent disciples, Shōryō and another, two people in all, bearing a Lotus Sūtra written in gold characters, as the gift of a first audience. Thereupon, weeping, they paid their respects and asked to study beneath his wheel. In three years their path was greatly accomplished, and they returned to their country and greatly spread the Tiantai learning. Lord Zeng of Lu wrote the stele for his stūpa, which tells of this in full.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second Answers to Ten Questions from Japan

Chinese Source

（此十問不知彼國何師所設而來，相傳但云：日本國問，四明法師答）。

Ru-Yi Translation

(It is not known which master of that country set these ten questions and sent them; tradition only says: "Questions from Japan, answers by the Dharma Master of Siming.")

First and Second Questions: Fixed-Nature Śrāvakas and Icchantikas

Chinese Source

一問：定性聲聞成佛不成佛疑。《解深密經》云：成就第一趣聲聞，一切諸佛盡力教化，不能令其坐道場得無上菩提，我說名為寂滅聲聞。《唯識論》云：定性聲聞入無餘涅槃者，身智俱滅，猶如虛空，非眾生數，更不發心。且經論若爾，而天台宗若何解釋《深密》、《唯識》之文，顯定性聲聞成佛之道理耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

First question: the doubt whether śrāvakas of fixed nature become buddhas or not. The Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra says: "A śrāvaka who has accomplished the first course — even if all the buddhas exert their full strength to teach and transform him, they cannot cause him to sit at the site of awakening and attain unsurpassed bodhi; I call him a śrāvaka of quiescent extinction." The Treatise on Consciousness-Only says: "When śrāvakas of fixed nature enter nirvāṇa without remainder, body and wisdom both cease, like empty space; they are not counted among sentient beings and never again arouse the mind." Since the sūtra and treatise say this, how does the Tiantai school explain the texts of the Saṃdhinirmocana and the Consciousness-Only so as to show the principle by which śrāvakas of fixed nature become buddhas?

Chinese Source

答：《深密》、《唯識》經論，是顯露施權之教。聲聞趣寂唯尚偏真，依理發心但成灰斷，未知實理究竟真常，欲趣菩提終不可得。及至鷲峯法華會上，開權顯實，正直捨方便，但說無上道。三周授記聲聞作佛。不可更執昔日經論聲聞不得作佛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The sūtra and treatise, the Saṃdhinirmocana and the Consciousness-Only, are teachings that set up the provisional openly. The śrāvaka who tends toward quiescence values only the one-sided real; arousing the mind in reliance on [that] principle, he only ends in ashes and severance. Not yet knowing the real principle, ultimately true and permanent, he can never attain bodhi though he wishes to tend toward it. But when it came to the Lotus assembly on Vulture Peak, [the Buddha] opened the provisional to reveal the real, "straightforwardly setting aside skillful means, teaching only the unsurpassed path," and in the predictions of the three rounds the śrāvakas were to become buddhas. One may no longer hold to the sūtras and treatises of former days [that say] śrāvakas cannot become buddhas.

Chinese Source

二問：闡提成佛不成佛疑。《善戒經》云：若無菩薩性者，雖復發心勤修精進，終不能得阿耨菩提。《瑜伽論》云：無種性誦持瑜伽，以無種性故，雖則發心及修加行，不堪圓滿無上菩提。如是經論足顯闡提無成佛義，今天台宗於彼經論若何融會？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second question: the doubt whether icchantikas become buddhas or not. The Sūtra of Good Precepts says: "If one is without the bodhisattva nature, then even though he arouses the mind and diligently cultivates with vigor, he can never attain anuttara-[samyak-saṃ]bodhi." The Yogācārabhūmi says: "A person without lineage, because he has no lineage, even though he arouses the mind and cultivates the preparatory practices, is not able to complete unsurpassed bodhi." Sūtras and treatises such as these are enough to show that icchantikas have no meaning of becoming buddhas. How does the Tiantai school now reconcile those sūtras and treatises?

Chinese Source

答：所引《善戒》、《瑜伽》發心修證稱闡提無性者，此是藏教，發心同前二乘，未明中實真常之性，非是闡提人也。其闡提者，信心未具、未能發心，謂無緣了二因，所以不云成佛。故《涅槃》云：或有佛性，闡提人有，善根人無（謂闡提有惡境界性）。或有佛性，善根人有，闡提人無（謂善根人有善因緣性故）。或有佛性，二人俱無（謂俱無緣了二性故）。以上無緣了二性，故說闡提無佛性義。又經云：或有佛性二人俱有（謂有正因佛性也）。約此正因，闡提極惡有成佛義。是以《涅槃》終極顯性談常，不簡闡提極惡，咸同正性俱得成佛，況二乘耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Those cited from the Good Precepts and the Yogācāra, who arouse the mind and cultivate realization and are called icchantikas without the nature — this is the Tripiṭaka teaching. Their arousing of the mind is the same as that of the two vehicles before; they have not yet understood the nature of the middle, the real, the true and permanent. They are not icchantika people. As for the icchantika: his mind of faith is not yet complete and he cannot yet arouse the mind; that is, he lacks the two causes, conditioning and revealing, and so it is not said that he becomes a buddha. Hence the Nirvāṇa says: "There is a buddha-nature that the icchantika has and the person of good roots lacks" (that is, the icchantika has the nature of evil objects). "There is a buddha-nature that the person of good roots has and the icchantika lacks" (that is, because the person of good roots has the nature of good causes and conditions). "There is a buddha-nature that both people lack" (that is, because both lack the two natures, conditioning and revealing). Because the above lack the two natures, conditioning and revealing, the meaning that the icchantika has no buddha-nature is taught. The sūtra also says: "There is a buddha-nature that both people have" (that is, they have the buddha-nature of the correct cause). In terms of this correct cause, even the utterly evil icchantika has the meaning of becoming a buddha. Therefore the Nirvāṇa, at the very end, reveals the nature and speaks of permanence, not excluding the utterly evil icchantika: all alike share the correct nature and all can become buddhas. How much more the two vehicles?

Third Question: Whether Grasses and Trees Become Buddhas

Chinese Source

三問：非情草木成佛不成佛疑。《涅槃經》云：若拘陀樹有心，我當授與阿耨菩提記。以其無心故，不與授記。《法相論》云：真如能為一切諸法所依。無心草木雖無所變，但有理性無行性，不可成佛（云云）。今天台宗如何解釋經論，建立非情草木成佛耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third question: the doubt whether insentient grasses and trees become buddhas or not. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "If the kuda tree had a mind, I would confer on it the prediction of anuttara-[samyak-saṃ]bodhi. Because it has no mind, I do not confer the prediction." The Faxiang Treatise says: "True suchness can be that on which all dharmas depend. Mindless grasses and trees, although there is nothing they transform, have only the principle-nature and no practice-nature, and cannot become buddhas" (and so on). How does the Tiantai school now explain these sūtras and treatises so as to establish that insentient grasses and trees become buddhas?

Chinese Source

答：《涅槃經》、《法相論》草木無心但有理性無行性者，此是權教，故簡無情。為起真修，乃須進行方得成佛。緣修乃是無常，即是本無今有義也。欲顯真修，須依理性。理非今古，不簡色心，一成一切成，故說無情成佛也。又依緣修說無情成佛者，相盡情忘，則無情悉成佛矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: [The statement in] the Nirvāṇa Sūtra and the Faxiang Treatise that grasses and trees are without mind and have only the principle-nature and no practice-nature — this is a provisional teaching, and so it excludes the insentient. [It holds that] to give rise to true cultivation one must advance in practice before one can become a buddha. But conditioned cultivation is impermanent; it is the meaning of "originally nonexistent, now existent." To reveal true cultivation one must rely on the principle-nature. Principle is neither present nor past and excludes neither form nor mind; when one is accomplished all are accomplished, and therefore it is taught that the insentient become buddhas. Again, if one speaks of the insentient becoming buddhas on the basis of conditioned cultivation: when marks are exhausted and feeling is forgotten, then the insentient all become buddhas.

Fourth Question: The Place of the Entrustment Chapter

Chinese Source

四問：《法華．囑累品》安前安後疑。今羅什譯，於〈神力品〉後次置〈囑累〉。而法相宗立十不可、八相違，須移經末。若爾，如何通彼一十八難？當依羅什所安耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth question: the doubt whether the Lotus "Entrustment" chapter is placed early or late. In Kumārajīva's present translation, the "Entrustment" is placed next after the chapter "Supernatural Powers [of the Tathāgata]." But the Dharma-Characteristics school sets up ten impossibilities and eight contradictions [and says] it must be moved to the end of the sūtra. If so, how are those eighteen objections to be answered? Should one follow Kumārajīva's placement?

Chinese Source

答：〈囑累〉一品，慈恩、安國並令移於〈勸發品〉後。若在於前，有八相違、十不可。天台之意，須依羅什，次〈神力品〉。荊溪總別破之，亦八不可、十相違。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Ci'en and Anguo both have the "Entrustment" chapter moved to after the chapter "Encouragement of Samantabhadra." If it is placed before, [they say,] there are eight contradictions and ten impossibilities. The intent of Tiantai is that one must follow Kumārajīva and place it next to the chapter "Supernatural Powers." Jingxi refutes them in general and in particular, also with eight impossibilities and ten contradictions.

Chinese Source

且出塔已，後凡述多寶，皆云塔中，不云見佛。若移在後，無出塔處，一不可也。分身散後，凡有所述，唯論佛塔，不涉分身。若移在後，佛無散處，二不可也。〈囑累〉文中佛散土穢已下，經文言不涉淨。若移在後，無復穢處，三不可也。會本居地，因塔升空。佛散出塔，後文在地。若移在後，無還地處，四不可也。〈囑累品〉後經既未盡，但述眾喜，不云而去。若移在後，須加而去，五不可也。〈勸發品〉後無復餘文，經既已終，則云而去。若移在後，須除而去，六不可也。本迹事畢，須有所付，是有囑累。若移在後，法無所歸，七不可也。囑累已後，明乘乘人，事須囑累。若移在後，師弟參雜，八不可也。其十相違，一一敘破，具載妙樂記中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, after [the Buddha] has come out of the stūpa, every later mention of Prabhūtaratna says "in the stūpa" and does not say "seeing the Buddha." If it were moved to the end, there would be no place for coming out of the stūpa; this is the first impossibility. After the emanated bodies have dispersed, whatever is mentioned concerns only the buddha in the stūpa and does not touch on the emanated bodies. If it were moved to the end, there would be no place for the buddhas to disperse; this is the second impossibility. From "the buddhas dispersed, the land was defiled" onward in the text of the "Entrustment," the words of the sūtra do not touch on purity. If it were moved to the end, there would be no place where it is defiled again; this is the third impossibility. The assembly originally dwelt on the ground and rose into the air because of the stūpa. The buddhas dispersed and [Śākyamuni] came out of the stūpa, and in the later text they are on the ground. If it were moved to the end, there would be no place for returning to the ground; this is the fourth impossibility. After the "Entrustment" chapter, the sūtra is not yet finished; it only tells of the assembly's joy and does not say "and they departed." If it were moved to the end, "and they departed" would have to be added; this is the fifth impossibility. After the chapter "Encouragement of Samantabhadra" there is no further text; the sūtra having ended, it says "and they departed." If it were moved to the end, "and they departed" would have to be removed; this is the sixth impossibility. When the matters of origin and trace are completed, there must be something to hand on; hence there is the entrustment. If it were moved to the end, the Dharma would have nowhere to go; this is the seventh impossibility. After the entrustment, [the later chapters] make clear those who ride the vehicle and are carried by the vehicle; these matters require the entrustment [first]. If it were moved to the end, teacher and disciples would be mixed up; this is the eighth impossibility. The ten contradictions are each set out and refuted, fully recorded in Miaole's Notes.

Fifth Question: The One Vehicle Then and Now

Chinese Source

五：今昔一乘同異疑。《法華》曰：十方佛土中，唯有一乘法。蓋昔日為三為二，今經開之唯一耳。若然，何故《勝鬘經》云：聲聞緣覺皆入大乘，大乘者即是佛乘，是故三乘即是一乘。此言一乘，與今《法華》一乘，同耶異耶？同則有妨一代化意；異則異相云何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth: the doubt whether the one vehicle of now and of former times is the same or different. The Lotus says: "In the buddha-lands of the ten directions, there is only the Dharma of the one vehicle." For in former days there were three and there were two, and the present sūtra opens them into only one. If so, why does the Śrīmālā Sūtra say: "Śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas all enter the Great Vehicle. The Great Vehicle is the buddha-vehicle; therefore the three vehicles are the one vehicle"? Is the one vehicle spoken of here the same as the one vehicle of the present Lotus, or different? If the same, it hinders the intent of the teaching of [the Buddha's] whole lifetime; if different, what are the marks of the difference?

Chinese Source

答：經論同異固多，要須以義定之則可。且《勝鬘》一經部居第三方等時教，二乘在座，正當彈呵轉小入大之時。台教有曰：若到方等必到法華者，蓋受彈之後次第證入也。而彼經謂二乘入一乘者，一者此經方等部中，預敘法華當來所證耳。故彼經上文云：必當得阿耨菩提。何以故？聲聞緣覺皆入大乘。請觀當得之言，自見指歸矣。二則若就方等自論，則二乘受呵，轉藏成通。通是摩訶衍門，亦得謂之二乘皆入大乘也。二釋之中，初義為正。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The sameness and difference of sūtras and treatises are certainly many; one must settle them by the meaning, and then it can be done. Now the Śrīmālā as a sūtra belongs to the teaching of the third, the Vaipulya period. The two vehicles are present in the seats, and it is exactly the time of rebuking and scolding, of turning from the small and entering the great. The Tiantai teaching has a saying: "Those who reach the Vaipulya will surely reach the Lotus" — for after receiving the rebuke they realize and enter in sequence. As for that sūtra's saying that the two vehicles enter the one vehicle: first, within the Vaipulya division this sūtra only announces in advance what will be realized in the future in the Lotus. Hence the earlier text of that sūtra says: "They will surely attain anuttara-[samyak-saṃ]bodhi. Why? Śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas all enter the Great Vehicle." Look at the words "will attain," and you will see for yourself where it points. Second, if one speaks of the Vaipulya by itself, the two vehicles receive the rebuke and turn from the Tripiṭaka to the shared [teaching]. The shared is the Mahāyāna gate, and so it can also be said that the two vehicles all enter the Great Vehicle. Of the two explanations, the first meaning is correct.

Sixth and Seventh Questions: Mañjuśrī in the Ocean and the Dragon Girl

Chinese Source

六問：文殊無去有來疑。且此經序分，文殊為列眾之首，復為彌勒引古決答。至下三周之中，並無文殊入海之文，如何〈達多品〉忽於大海娑竭羅龍王宮涌出耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth question: the doubt that Mañjuśrī, without going, comes. In this sūtra's introductory section, Mañjuśrī heads the list of the assembly, and he also cites ancient [events] to resolve [the question] for Maitreya. In the three rounds that follow there is no text of Mañjuśrī entering the ocean; how, then, in the "Devadatta" chapter, does he suddenly rise up from the palace of the dragon king Sāgara in the great ocean?

Chinese Source

答：文殊無入海之文者，準佛成道後四十二年，便居靈鷲說《法華經》。首尾八載時既長遠，或有入海之文，經文傳譯此土從略，是故不云也。此猶約事解釋如此。若約理智伸之，此經如來常在海中宣揚，文殊實智周遍，豈隔鷲峯聽受。理既無在，寧局方隅？感應而處處釋迦，隨緣而身身妙德也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: As for there being no text of Mañjuśrī entering the ocean: according to [the account that] forty-two years after the Buddha attained the path he dwelt on Vulture Peak and taught the Lotus Sūtra, from beginning to end eight years — the time is long, and there may have been a text of entering the ocean which was abridged when the sūtra was translated and transmitted to this land; therefore it is not mentioned. This is still an explanation in terms of phenomena. If one sets it out in terms of principle and wisdom: the Tathāgata is always proclaiming this sūtra within the ocean, and Mañjuśrī's real wisdom pervades everywhere — how would he be cut off from hearing and receiving it on Vulture Peak? Since principle has no location, how could it be confined to a direction or a corner? In response to appeals there are Śākyamunis in place after place; following conditions there are Wondrous Virtues in body after body.

Chinese Source

七問：龍女成佛權實疑。經云：八歲龍女於剎那頃發菩提心，乃至云即往南方無垢世界坐寶蓮華成等正覺。且三論、法相宗難曰：彼娑竭羅龍王既是深位，權現龍身，豈其龍女成佛是實證耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh question: the doubt whether the dragon girl's becoming a buddha is provisional or real. The sūtra says: "The dragon girl, eight years old, in the space of a single instant gave rise to the mind of awakening," down to "went at once to the Spotless World in the south, where, seated upon a jeweled lotus, she accomplished perfect and complete awakening." Now the Three Treatise and Dharma-Characteristics schools object: Since the dragon king Sāgara is at a deep stage and provisionally manifests a dragon's body, how could the dragon girl's becoming a buddha be a real realization?

Chinese Source

答：他宗言權者，乃是高位聖人為軌下凡故示近成。若天台云實者，顯佛乘權巧妙力，開性海不思議真常，一念發心便成正覺。故疏引經云：法性如大海，不說有是非。凡夫賢聖人，平等無高下，唯在心垢淨，取證如反掌。舍利弗舉女人五障以為疑，智積明積劫行行而作難，龍女以圓珠獻佛息眾疑心，何至今日更存猶豫故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: When the other schools say "provisional," they mean that a sage of high stage, to be a model for ordinary people below, displays a recent accomplishment. When Tiantai says "real," it reveals the skillful, wondrous power of the buddha-vehicle, which opens the inconceivable true permanence of the ocean of the nature, so that with one thought of arousing the mind one accomplishes right awakening. Hence the commentary cites a sūtra that says: "The dharma-nature is like the great ocean; it does not speak of right and wrong. Ordinary people, worthies, and sages are equal, without high or low; it lies only in the mind being defiled or pure; attaining realization is like turning over the hand." Śāriputra raised the five obstructions of women as a doubt; Prajñākūṭa pointed to practicing practices for accumulated eons as an objection; the dragon girl, by offering the round pearl to the Buddha, put an end to the doubting minds of the assembly. Why, then, should one still hesitate today?

Eighth Question: The Dragon Girl's Lotus Seat

Chinese Source

八問：龍女華座成佛別圓疑。且龍女成佛若是圓乘，當以虛空為座，何故坐寶蓮華，而是別佛座耶？又三周得記皆是未來成佛，何故龍女現身成佛，而無成佛劫國名號？何耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth question: the doubt whether the dragon girl's becoming a buddha on a flower seat is of the distinct or the perfect [teaching]. 答：龍女是圓頓教中成道，法身頓顯；一成一切成、一身一切身、一土一切土。報智頓明。乃於蓮華藏界，身土不相妨，色心為罣礙。雖坐寶蓮華，體即虛空性，乃不思議圓融解脫大用也。若然者，何別之有哉？其三周得記未來成佛者，皆是鈍根聲聞，未曾修菩薩行，是故再歷塵劫修治。龍女乃是乘急戒緩之人，是故一超直入也。其如不言劫國名號者，當以通別二記伸之，通則不言，別則有之也。

The dragon girl is a person urgent in the vehicle and lax in the precepts, and so she leaps beyond and enters directly. As for her eon, land, and name not being mentioned, one should explain this by the two kinds of prediction, general and particular: in the general one they are not mentioned; in the particular one they are present.

First Question: Ignorance and the Dharma-Nature

Chinese Source

一問：無明與法性，為有前後？為無前後？若云有前後者，何云法性無初，無明亦無有始？又云無明即是佛性耶？若言無前後者，何故佛果位中，斷盡無明方成佛果？既云斷盡，應斷法性耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

First question: Do ignorance and the dharma-nature have a before and after, or have they no before and after? If you say they have a before and after, why is it said that the dharma-nature has no beginning and ignorance also has no beginning? And why is it further said that ignorance is identical to buddha-nature? If you say they have no before and after, why is it that at the stage of the buddha-fruit one becomes a buddha only by severing ignorance completely? Since it is said to be completely severed, should the dharma-nature be severed?

Chinese Source

答：若論本具，平等一性，則非真非妄，而不說有無。明法性亦不論於有始有終，但眾生自無始忽然不覺，迷理而生無明，無明有熏真之用、法性有隨妄之能，真妄和合名為緣起。故《金錍》曰：無有無波之水，未有不濕之波。在濕詎間於混澄，為波自分於清濁。雖則有清有濁，而一體無殊。所謂清濁波者，真妄兩用也；清濁濕性者，一體無殊也。無明法性體一，故起無前後。故《起信論》云：如來藏無前際故，無明之相亦無有始是也。若覺悟時，達妄即真，了無明即是法性。約修門說，義當斷妄。雖曰斷妄，妄體本真，妄何所斷？故曰無明亦無有終。又若究其正迷之時，如夢中人，而不知是夢；忽然夢覺，迷妄自息。是則風息水澄，妄消真顯矣。審而思之，無俟多論也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: If one discusses what is originally contained, the one equal nature, it is neither true nor deluded, and one does not speak of there being ignorance and dharma-nature, nor discuss whether they have a beginning or an end. But sentient beings, from beginningless time, suddenly fail to awaken, are deluded about principle, and give rise to ignorance. Ignorance has the function of perfuming the true; the dharma-nature has the capacity of following the deluded; the true and the deluded combining is called conditioned arising. Hence the Vajra Scalpel says: "There is no water without waves, and there has never been a wave that is not wet. As wetness, how could it be divided into murky and clear? It is as waves that it divides itself into clear and turbid. Though there is clear and there is turbid, the one essence does not differ." The waves called clear and turbid are the two functions, true and deluded; the wet nature of the clear and the turbid is the one essence that does not differ. Because ignorance and the dharma-nature are one in essence, their arising has no before and after. Hence the Awakening of Faith says: "Because the Tathāgatagarbha has no prior limit, the mark of ignorance also has no beginning." That is it. At the time of awakening, one penetrates that the deluded is identical to the true and understands that ignorance is identical to the dharma-nature. Speaking in terms of the gate of cultivation, the meaning is that the deluded is severed. But although it is called severing the deluded, the essence of the deluded is originally true; what of the deluded is there to sever? Hence it is said that ignorance also has no end. Again, if one investigates the very moment of delusion, it is like a person in a dream who does not know it is a dream; suddenly waking from the dream, the deluded ceases of itself. Then the wind ceases and the water is clear; the deluded dissolves and the true is revealed. Examine and reflect on it, and there is no need for much discussion.

Second Question: Why Beings Were Deluded and Awaken at Different Times

Chinese Source

二問：一切眾生本來同一法性，法性本來清淨，何因緣故忽迷？只如初迷之時，為一時迷？為前後迷？若云一時迷者，悟時亦應一時開悟證於佛果，何故現有眾生未悟耶？若前後者，既同一法性，以何因緣而致前後迷耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second question: All sentient beings originally share one dharma-nature, and the dharma-nature is originally pure. Through what causes and conditions were they suddenly deluded? At the time of the very first delusion, were they deluded all at one time, or deluded one before another? If you say they were deluded at one time, then at awakening they should also awaken at one time and realize the buddha-fruit; why are there at present sentient beings who have not awakened? If one before another, since they share one dharma-nature, through what causes and conditions does it come about that they are deluded one before another?

Chinese Source

答：具德圓常正性，真空妙有遇緣而發，法爾如斯，不勞造作。且迷妄緣起者，如人忽睡，靈焰潛生，眼觀剎那狂覺忽起。一切眾生所迷真如，能迷不覺，真妄和合二無二相。然則佛性雖一、迷悟雖同，六道四生遇緣熏習親疎不等，根性利鈍有異，是故覺有前後。誠不可以無明法性一故根性俱同也。是知，一切眾生迷無前後，覺有前後。譬如夜間多人同睡，睡時雖同，不妨前後起也。善解此譬，來問自消矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The correct nature, endowed with virtues, perfect and permanent, true emptiness and wondrous existence, is aroused on meeting conditions; it is naturally so and takes no fabricating effort. As for the conditioned arising of delusion: it is like a person who suddenly falls asleep; a numinous flame secretly arises, the eye sees for an instant, and a mad awareness suddenly starts up. For all sentient beings, the true suchness about which they are deluded and the non-awakening that deludes them combine, true and deluded, two without the mark of two. Thus although buddha-nature is one and delusion and awakening are the same, the six paths and the four kinds of birth meet conditions and are perfumed with differing nearness and distance, and their faculties and natures differ in sharpness and dullness; therefore their awakening has a before and after. Truly one cannot say that because ignorance and the dharma-nature are one, all faculties and natures are the same. So we know that the delusion of all sentient beings has no before and after, while their awakening has a before and after. It is like many people sleeping together at night: although they fall asleep at the same time, this does not prevent them from getting up one before another. If you understand this simile well, your question will dissolve of itself.

Third Question: How the First Buddha Awakened

Chinese Source

三問：一切眾生既迷本性已，何故忽然能開悟耶？若言因善知識教法緣熏得開悟者，只如最初一佛，又從何人得法信解開悟耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third question: Since all sentient beings have already been deluded about their original nature, how is it that they can suddenly awaken? If you say they awaken through being perfumed by the conditions of a good friend's teaching, then from whom did the very first buddha receive the Dharma, believe, understand, and awaken?

Chinese Source

答：眾生得悟緣熏不同，或有無師自悟，承本自然智而證之。良由本具靈明，能自推理而得解悟。故祖師云：空寂體上無師智自然能知。既知自性，而便成佛也。一人成佛之後，展轉說法化迷眾生，眾生稟教修行，是故次第成佛。若爾，何疑最初一佛無從開悟耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The conditions and perfuming by which sentient beings awaken are not the same. Some awaken by themselves without a teacher, relying on the original spontaneous wisdom to realize it. This is truly because they originally contain numinous brightness and can of themselves infer principle and attain understanding and awakening. Hence the patriarch says: "On the essence of empty quiescence, teacherless wisdom naturally is able to know." Once one knows one's own nature, one then becomes a buddha. After one person becomes a buddha, he teaches the Dharma in turn and transforms deluded sentient beings; sentient beings receive the teaching and cultivate it, and therefore become buddhas in sequence. If so, why doubt that the very first buddha had no one from whom to awaken?

Fourth Question: Powers of the Fruit Present in the Cause

Chinese Source

四問：欽聞諸佛果上凡所證法，一切眾生悉具有之。今且就人中以論因中，如何辨於三明八解、五眼六通，乃至毛吞巨海、芥納須彌等用耶？若言至佛果方得者，本有之義又何述焉？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth question: I have respectfully heard that all the dharmas realized by the buddhas at the fruit are fully possessed by all sentient beings. Now, taking human beings to discuss the cause, how are the three clear knowledges and eight liberations, the five eyes and six supernormal powers, down to such functions as a hair swallowing the vast ocean and a mustard seed containing Sumeru, to be discerned? If you say they are attained only on reaching the buddha-fruit, how is the meaning of "originally present" to be explained?

Chinese Source

答：諸佛眾生緣起雖異，覺海同源。諸佛悟理，性相無礙；眾生迷妄，事理懸隔。故《金錍》曰：眾生唯有迷中之事理，諸佛具有悟中之事理。迷悟雖殊，事理體一。一佛成道，法界無非此佛之依正，眾生自於佛依正中，而生苦樂升沈。升沈既作，解脫無期。今則籍教熏修，契圓實理，傚菩薩之所修，修無所作；同諸佛之所證，證諸本具。是則一念頓圓於一切，一切普攝於一塵，三明八解日用不虧，五眼六通介爾具足。故荊溪曰：凡聖一如，色香泯淨，阿鼻依正全處極聖之自心，毘盧身土不愈下凡之一念者是也。毛吞芥納者，《淨名》不思議解脫大用也。且凡夫一念造惡之心尚遍十方三世，豈不思議大用不即含納？若不信凡心理本具足此用，則諸佛果上依正融通悉不成矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Although the conditioned arising of buddhas and of sentient beings differs, the ocean of awakening has the same source. The buddhas awaken to principle, and nature and marks are without obstruction; sentient beings are deluded, and phenomena and principle are far apart. Hence the Vajra Scalpel says: "Beings have only the phenomena and principle that lie within delusion, while buddhas fully have the phenomena and principle that lie within awakening. Though delusion and awakening differ, phenomena and principle are one in substance." When one buddha attains the path, there is nothing in the dharma-realm that is not this buddha's dependent and principal [rewards]; sentient beings, within the buddha's dependent and principal [rewards], give rise of themselves to suffering and joy, rising and sinking. Once rising and sinking are created, there is no time of liberation. Now, relying on the teaching, they are perfumed and cultivate, and accord with the perfect, real principle. Imitating what the bodhisattvas cultivate, they cultivate with nothing made; sharing what the buddhas realize, they realize what is originally contained. Then one thought is suddenly complete with all, and all are universally gathered into one mote of dust; the three clear knowledges and eight liberations are not lacking in daily use, and the five eyes and six supernormal powers are complete in the slightest [thought]. Hence Jingxi says: "Ordinary beings and sages are a single suchness, and every form and fragrance dissolves into purity. The Avīci hell, its dependent and principal [rewards], lies wholly within the very mind of the supreme sage; the body and land of Vairocana do not go beyond a single thought of the lowliest ordinary person." That is it. As for a hair swallowing and a mustard seed containing: this is the great function of inconceivable liberation in the Vimalakīrti. Even the ordinary being's single thought of a mind that creates evil pervades the ten directions and the three times; how could the inconceivable great function not at once contain and receive [them]? If one does not believe that the ordinary mind originally contains this function in principle, then the interfusion of the dependent and principal [rewards] at the buddhas' fruit could not be accomplished at all.

Fifth Question: The True and the Deluded

Chinese Source

五問：真妄二法為同為異？若言同者，妄本是真，古人不應云：錯將世智為佛智，如認魚目為明珠。若言異者，應是離妄有真，古人不應云：棄波求水、捨器求金。若真妄兩立又不雙存，云何剖伸耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth question: Are the two dharmas, true and deluded, the same or different? If you say the same, the deluded is originally the true, and the ancients should not have said: "Mistakenly taking worldly wisdom for buddha-wisdom is like recognizing a fish eye as a bright pearl." If you say different, then there should be the true apart from the deluded, and the ancients should not have said: "Abandoning waves to seek water, discarding vessels to seek gold." If the true and the deluded are both established and yet do not both remain, how is this to be analyzed and explained?

Chinese Source

答：有真有妄者，對迷說覺也。絕真絕妄者，泯相離筌也。確論其旨，真則全妄之真、妄則全真之妄，二無二體也。佛大聖人說真說妄者，所以欲人慕其真而破諸妄，使妄不得而興也。世人於茲不了，強執現前一念妄心均已是佛，正墮古人錯將強認之說矣。若更棄此妄念別覓真如，復同偏教所修，猶如棄波求水、捨器求金焉。茲二者過，猶不及也。要須不即不離、妙在其中，斯可矣。來意問以真妄同異者，今答曰：非同異中假立同異也。非同異者，真妄同源，縛脫不二故。假立同異者，迷悟不同，情智有異也。如此甄之，自然懸合諸文，毋勞委論矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Having true and having deluded is speaking of awakening in contrast to delusion. Cutting off true and cutting off deluded is dissolving marks and leaving the fish-trap. Discussing the purport precisely: the true is the true that is wholly the deluded, and the deluded is the deluded that is wholly the true; they are two with no two essences. The Buddha, the great sage, spoke of the true and of the deluded because he wished people to long for the true and break all delusion, so that the deluded could not arise. Worldly people, not understanding this, forcibly hold that the present single thought of the deluded mind is already the Buddha, and so fall right into what the ancients called "mistakenly taking" and "forcibly recognizing." If, again, they abandon this deluded thought to look for true suchness elsewhere, they are the same as what the one-sided teachings cultivate, like abandoning waves to seek water or discarding vessels to seek gold. These two faults — overshooting is like falling short. One must be neither identical nor separate, the wonder lying in between; then it is right. Your question about whether the true and the deluded are the same or different I now answer: within what is neither the same nor different, sameness and difference are provisionally set up. Neither the same nor different, because true and deluded have the same source, and bondage and liberation are nondual. Sameness and difference provisionally set up, because delusion and awakening are not the same, and feeling and wisdom are different. Sifted in this way, it will naturally agree with all the texts, with no need for detailed discussion.

Ru-Yi Translation

First question: In the first answer I received, it says that they "suddenly fail to awaken, are deluded about principle, and give rise to ignorance." Now as for non-awakening: on what does it depend to arise? What is its essence? Through what causes and conditions does it suddenly arise?

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The source of the profound nature of the [Tathāgata]garbha is neither true nor deluded; this nature, following conditions, becomes true and becomes deluded. Hence the Buddha-Nature Treatise says: "The true alone does not give rise; the deluded alone can hardly be accomplished; only when true and deluded combine is there something done." Therefore the gates of the teaching show that the true has the meaning of following conditions yet unchanging, and the deluded has the capacity of being empty in essence yet accomplishing phenomena. Truly, because the numinous source has no abiding, it arises following the deluded; the forced awareness, once deluded, suddenly arises. Yet as for these words "giving rise to the deluded," one must further understand their intent. If one speaks of beings' nature of original awakening, perfect and bright, this points directly to beings' beginningless ignorance as the dharma-nature, because ignorance and the dharma-nature are one in essence. If one speaks of suddenly failing to awaken and giving rise to ignorance, this is speaking in terms of cultivation, discussing arising relative to the nature: from the essence of original awakening there is non-awakening. Were it not so, one could not show that for those who advance in cultivation this is the path of restoring the origin and returning to the source. For this reason the sūtras and treatises mostly say that the deluded arises from the true. In reality, all sentient beings from beginningless time have only been deluded and not awakened. Hence the Awakening of Faith says: "All sentient beings are not called awakened, because from the beginning they have never been free of thoughts; hence beginningless ignorance is spoken of." Ru-Yi Translation

On the eighteenth day of the first month, the bhikṣu (Zining), abbot of the Jingde Chan Monastery on Mount Tiantong, who transmits the Dharma, having respectfully bathed and perfumed himself, writes this letter to the Great Master Fazhi, Teaching Lord in the hall of Yanqing (to his attendant). I (Zining) have heard: "Even the wise, in a thousand reflections, may have one error; even the foolish, in a thousand reflections, may have one gain." This is a maxim of former sages, not the private opinion of a recluse. I (Zining), never having understood the purport of the accomplished, venture to set out the words of a fool; may I trouble your hearing and set out a little of my rash and headstrong [views]. Recently, on a day of leisure, I respectfully read Pointing to the Essentials: Notes on the Ten Gates of Nonduality. Its meanings rise like a solitary peak, which the feet of Xi Ke could not climb; its teaching is a vast ocean, which the ears of Shi Kuang could not exhaust. Treasure it, treasure it.

Ru-Yi Translation

Among what it cites: three people under Bodhidharma attained the Dharma, with differing depth. The nun Zongchi said: "Cut off afflictions and realize bodhi." The master said: "You have my skin." Daoyu said: "Deluded, it is affliction; awakened, it is bodhi." The master said: "You have my flesh." Huike said: "Originally there are no afflictions; from the start it is bodhi." The master said: "You have my marrow." But this is only hearsay and so lacks real evidence. I do not know from whom these words were obtained. In general, to open things up and point out delusion, one must rely on texts to reveal the understanding; how can one take what is heard on the road and told along the way as correct understanding? The Record of Rites says: "Memorized sayings are not enough to make one a teacher of others." This too is concern lest groundless words be taken as correct teaching. How could one point to a deer and call it a horse, a matter like Zhao Gao's, or make the people tremble, just as Zai Wo did? Now according to the Patriarchs' Hall and the Record of the Transmission of the Lamp, it says only that the Second Patriarch made three bows and stood in his place. I do not know how your school further explains it.

Now there are perhaps teachers who say: "Bodhidharma's path only receives those of inferior faculties and does not reach the superior wise." They also say: "It awakens to the principle of the mind itself but is unclear about dharmas outside the mind." These are all looking at the sky through a tube and measuring the sea with a shell. Let me now try to discern this. Now, when a disease is in the vitals, needle and stone fail it; those stuck in names and marks are sealed in words and phrases. Is it that the teaching cannot make the mind clear, or that medicine cannot cure the illness? Truly because accumulated treatments increase the sickness and many words confuse the nature, the patriarch came from the west and pointed directly to the human mind, to see the nature and become a buddha; this too is one measure of skillful means. Someone may still say: "It only points to the mind itself and has not entered the buddha-wisdom." As the Prajñā says that the nine kinds [of beings] all abide in unconditioned nirvāṇa, and the Avataṃsaka says that one thought universally contemplates immeasurable eons — I do not know whether this account is the same as or different from the patriarch's path. Alas, small wisdom is selfish, despising others and prizing itself; the accomplished, with a great view, find nothing permissible or impermissible. The words of the ancient worthies are trustworthy and verified. Thus Tian Ba disparaged the Three Sovereigns and condemned the Five Emperors, and a thousand people followed and echoed him. For by the sea there are men who chase after foul smells; how could Tian Ba's way surpass the way of the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors?

The Great Master, Teaching Lord, has long accumulated pure practice and spread wide the net of the teaching; there was none like him before, and none can follow him after. The bhikṣu (Zining), abbot of the Jingde Chan Monastery on Mount Tiantong, who transmits the Dharma, bows his head and respectfully addresses you.

Reading Notes for Part 5

Translator's notes

The Japanese monks (units 0–1). The note from the Thatched Hut Record concerns the Japanese disciples (Shōryō and another) who came to Guangzhi (Shangxian) after Zhili's death; Zeng Gongliang's stūpa stele for Guangzhi is cited as the source. This is different from Genshin's twenty-seven questions of 1003 (Part 4).

Second answers to Japan (units 2–23). Zongxiao's note says the author of these ten questions is unknown. The questions set Faxiang positions against the Tiantai reading of the Lotus. The Saṃdhinirmocana passage on the śrāvaka "of quiescent extinction" corresponds to T676 (where the śrāvaka is "one who tends only to quiescence," 一向趣寂). The Nirvāṇa Sūtra line on the kuda tree (拘陀樹) is in T374/T375. The Faxiang Treatise (法相論) was not located under that title. The "ten impossibilities and eight contradictions" (unit 10) of the Faxiang school, used to argue that the "Entrustment" chapter belongs at the end of the sūtra, were not located outside this text.

Lotus wording. "Straightforwardly setting aside skillful means…" and the chapter names ("Entrustment," "Supernatural Powers [of the Tathāgata]") follow the Part 4 practice of using the site's Lotus Sūtra names.

Chan Master Tai's questions (units 24–55). Tai signs himself Qingtai (清泰); the questions are those of a Chan monk reading the Awakening of Faith and the Śūraṅgama: why ignorance arises in the true nature, why beings awaken at different times, how the first buddha awakened, and whether the insentient teach the Dharma. Zhili answers from the tathāgatagarbha, inherent inclusion, and the threefold contemplation, and cites The Vajra Scalpel on the buddha-nature of the insentient. The second round (units 47–55) takes up three of his answers again.

The Tiantong letters (units 56–65). Chan Master Zining (子凝) of Jingde Monastery on Mount Tiantong objected to the story in Pointing to the Essentials (T1928) that Bodhidharma told Zongchi, Daoyu, and Huike, "You have my skin… flesh… marrow," with Zhili's judgment that Huike's words were "not yet perfect." Zining appeals to the Patriarchs' Hall and the Record of the Transmission of the Lamp (T2076), where Huike only bows three times and stands in his place; Zhili answers that the story is in Guifeng Zongmi. Five letters survive (three by Zining, two by Zhili).

Jizhong's afterword (units 66–68, 1070). Jizhong (繼忠, "Dharma Master Zhong") explains the earlier dispute with Master Qing of Fengxian (Yuanqing's Pointing to the Pearl) and Master Yu of Tiantai, and records that the prefect Lin asked Zhili to add harmonizing words to Pointing to the Essentials; he quotes the passage of the old text that was revised. The present T1928 shows the revised text. "Off-Mountain" renders 山外 here.

The Thatched Hut note and Zongxiao's comment (units 69–71). Daoyin says the letters went back and forth some twenty times; Zongxiao says he obtained only five rounds and asks readers to send any others.

Table of contents of fascicle five (units 73–107). The thirty-three entries name the pieces translated in Parts 6–7. Several entries differ in wording from the body titles (for example 賀啟 / 資啟, 謝聖果法師書 / 謝聖果法師作指要序啟); the differences are in the Chinese.

Titles of works. Treatise on Wisdom renders 《智論》 and Buddha's Crown Sūtra renders 《佛頂經》; elsewhere in the project these works are the Great Treatise and the Śūraṅgama. "Guifeng's Later Collection" (圭峰後集) is not italicized in the English.
