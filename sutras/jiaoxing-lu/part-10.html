The Collection Exalting the Patriarch at Baoyun, Concluded: Yitong's Stūpa, Eulogies, and the Later Abbots

The tenth and last part completes Zongxiao's Collection Exalting the Patriarch at Baoyun, the documents on Yitong (Baoyun), the Korean teacher of Zhili and Zunshi. It opens with two accounts of the reburial of Yitong's bones on Wushi Hill at Mount Aśoka in 1127, one by the Chan master Daochang, who carried it out, and one by Daoyin of the Thatched Hut; both describe the "firm seeds" (śarīra) that appeared on the bones. Zongxiao then notes Yitong's lost writings and a robe stūpa kept at another monastery. Next come the eulogies: the King of Wuyue's portrait eulogy and two poems sent to Yitong, which Zongxiao recovered in full in 1202; Shi Hao's four eulogies on Zhiyi, Yitong, Zhili, and Zunshi, written for the hall of the four patriarchs that the abbot Zongying built in 1187; and shorter pieces by Puchong and Keguan. Two anniversary statements follow, then short accounts of how Zhili and Zunshi studied under Yitong, with Zunshi's four miraculous signs at Baoyun. The part ends with records of later abbots, Mingzhi Zhongli and Zongying: the endowment treasury of 1192, the address at the feast for the new Dharma hall, and Zongxiao's note on why the hall was named "Dharma Rain."

Daochang's Record of Moving the Stūpa

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Record of Moving the Stūpa of Dharma Master [Yi]tong of Baoyun

Chinese Source

住臨安府靈隱山月堂（道昌）撰

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Yuetang (Daochang), residing at Mount Lingyin, Lin'an Prefecture

Chinese Source

余住育王時，寶雲威法師相訪，因語通法師乃寶雲啟教之宗主也，有塔葬此山。余詢勤舊，皆云在寺西隅，遂往尋之，見荒榛蓬棘中塔已墮毀矣。是時烏石有山，僉云風水甚佳，貴人富家數來求之。余以常住地，非余私有，若自與之必招因果也。其間人情有大不悅者，而余獨守之如初。是時先師妙湛老人居西塔，因以稟之。先師云：通法師天台宗主也。又此地人皆欲得之，若遷通公骨殖葬於此地，則非獨免求地者源源而來，抑亦通公之骨葬得其所。余遂從先師之言，擇日同威師并妙湛老人，集眾遷葬之。

Ru-Yi Translation

When I was residing at [Mount] Aśoka, Dharma Master Wei of Baoyun visited me, and told me that Dharma Master [Yi]tong was the founding master who opened the teaching at Baoyun, and that he had a stūpa burial on this mountain. I asked the old hands of the community, and all said it was at the west corner of the monastery. So I went to look for it, and saw that amid the overgrown hazel, fleabane, and thorns the stūpa had already fallen into ruin. At that time there was a hill at Wushi which everyone said had very fine wind and water, and noble people and wealthy families came several times to ask for it. I held that it was land of the permanent residence, not my private property, and that if I gave it away on my own I would surely invite [bad] cause and effect. Meanwhile some people were greatly displeased, but I alone guarded it as before. At that time my late teacher, the old man Miaozhan, was living at the West Stūpa, and so I reported it to him. My late teacher said: "Dharma Master [Yi]tong is a master of the Tiantai school. Moreover, everyone here wants to get this land. If you move Lord [Yi]tong's bones and bury them on this land, then not only will you be spared those seeking the land coming in an endless stream, but also Lord [Yi]tong's bones will be buried in a fitting place." So I followed my late teacher's words, chose a day, and together with Master Wei and the old man Miaozhan gathered the assembly and moved and reburied them.

Chinese Source

至取其骨，香水洗沐，於日光中，世所謂堅固子者，或青或黃或紅或白，滋生於骨上。見者無不歡喜作禮贊嘆，如是殊勝世所未有。後育王住持人遷寂，其徒知此地之勝而欲邀其福，就彼葬之。未久而皆為人所移，唯通師之塔巍然而獨存。是知用心之善不善者，報應之効曉然可見也。紹興廿八年戊寅歲八月廿八日，延慶若權上人訪余於冷泉，出通師重建石塔記，欲求余書遷葬之因，故特以此示之云。住靈隱山月堂比丘（道昌）謹題。

Ru-Yi Translation

When we took up his bones and bathed them in scented water, in the sunlight what the world calls "firm seeds," some blue, some yellow, some red, some white, grew forth upon the bones. All who saw it rejoiced, bowed, and praised it; such a wonder had never been in the world. Later, when the abbot of [Mount] Aśoka passed into stillness, his followers, knowing the excellence of this land and wishing to obtain its blessing, buried him there. Before long they were all moved away by others; only the stūpa of Master [Yi]tong stands lofty and alone. From this we know that, as to whether one's use of the mind is good or not good, the effect of retribution can be clearly seen. On the twenty-eighth day of the eighth month of the twenty-eighth year of Shaoxing, wuyin [1158], the Venerable Ruoquan of Yanqing visited me at Lengquan and brought out the record of the rebuilding of Master [Yi]tong's stone stūpa, wishing me to write the cause of the reburial; therefore I have shown it specially in this. Respectfully inscribed by the bhikṣu Yuetang (Daochang), residing at Mount Lingyin.

The Thatched Hut's Record of the Śarīra

Chinese Source

草菴紀通法師舍利事

Ru-Yi Translation

The Thatched Hut's Record of Master [Yi]tong's Śarīra

Chinese Source

四明寶雲通法師，新羅人也，得法於天台螺溪。既入滅，骨塔於阿育王山門徑之左，積有年矣。後因別改寺門，此地蕪沒塔亦隨壞。宣和丁未冬，今蔣山昌禪師主育王，徙其骨塔於烏石山，是時大眾半千同送之，今雪峯睿禪師、寶雲威法師亦預焉。其骨晶熒可愛，考之琅琅有聲。雪峯默念之，果若人骨也，當不止如是而已。少頃杲日既昇，見骨中世所謂堅固子者。二三眾驚且譁，則須臾變，數百千熣燦的𣋵瑟瑟如珠璣。人或求之，至有盈掬得之者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Dharma Master [Yi]tong of Baoyun in Siming was a man of Silla. He obtained the Dharma from Luoxi at Tiantai. After he entered extinction, his bones were placed in a stūpa to the left of the gate path of Mount Aśoka, and many years accumulated. Later, because the monastery gate was rebuilt elsewhere, this land became overgrown and the stūpa too fell into ruin. In the winter of dingwei of Xuanhe [1127], Chan Master Chang, now of Jiangshan, presiding over [Mount] Aśoka, moved his bone stūpa to Wushi Hill; at that time the great assembly, half a thousand, saw it off together, and Chan Master Rui, now of Xuefeng, and Dharma Master Wei of Baoyun also took part. His bones were crystalline, shining, and lovely; struck, they rang with a clear sound. [Rui of] Xuefeng silently thought: "If these are truly human bones, it should not stop at just this." After a little while, when the bright sun had risen, they saw in the bones what the world calls "firm seeds." Two or three of the assembly were startled and cried out, and in a moment they changed into hundreds of thousands, brilliant, sparkling, glittering like pearls and gems. When people asked for them, some even obtained them by the double handful.

Chinese Source

余紹興辛亥閣錫於廣利，禪人多以此語余，余尚疑之。續至烏石，禮法師之塔，菴有老僧曰某者，能道其事。果然又曰：老僧甞求二顆，寘掌中握之，移刻開示之，已滋七八矣。余始信之。此老僧非妄語者。然法師生有奇表，昔吳越國王尤所欽重，甞贊之有曰：白毫異相，滿月奇姿。千里同風，瞻之仰之。又詩曰：平生賴慈眼，南望一咨嗟。當是時台道既微，賴師持之。授法智、慈雲，以起家焉，此所謂台宗之命脈也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In xinhai of Shaoxing [1131] I rested my staff at Guangli, and many Chan people told me of this, but I still doubted it. Later I went to Wushi and paid homage at the Dharma Master's stūpa; in the hermitage there was an old monk named So-and-so who could tell of the matter. Indeed it was so. He also said: "This old monk once asked for two grains, put them in my palm and closed my hand; after a while I opened it to look, and they had already multiplied to seven or eight." Only then did I believe it. This old monk is not one who speaks falsely. Moreover, the Dharma Master in life had an extraordinary appearance, and the former King of the State of Wuyue especially revered him; he once eulogized him, saying: "The white-hair mark, a wondrous sign; / a full-moon, rare countenance; / a thousand li share one wind; / we gaze up at him, we look up to him." In a poem he also said: "All my life I have relied on your compassionate eyes; / looking south, I sigh once." At that time the Tai path had already declined, and it relied on the Master to uphold it. He transmitted the Dharma to Fazhi and Ciyun, so that they raised up the house; this is what is called the lifeline of the Tai school.

Master Yitong's Writings and Surviving Traces

Chinese Source

紀通法師著述遺跡

Ru-Yi Translation

Record of Master [Yi]tong's Writings and Surviving Traces

Chinese Source

天台正傳止荊溪禪師為九世祖，然自荊溪後之，傳者亦復不絕焉。雖定慧雙弘，未可並肩九祖，然截瓊枝析栴檀，則皆行天台之道者也。今寶雲通公實繼荊溪之後，復得法智、慈雲分化於江浙，此道遂再振矣。師解行高深、洪通甚力。準石塔記，師所著述並逸而不傳。然考諸四明章記，則甞秉筆《觀經疏記》、《光明玄贊釋》，若餘之法義則法智悉面承，載之於《記鈔》。其《贊釋》一部尚存，但不廣傳耳。惜哉。師所建院宇已二百二十二年，兵塵之後古跡掃地而盡。甞訊諸耆宿，知師有藏衣塔一所，存景清興法院。遂訪之，果奉安懺殿尊像前。雕布奇巧飾以渾金，內空外方高五尺許。此寶寶雲舊物，不知何緣留墜彼剎？萬一合浦珠還，豈不為山家傳持之標幟乎。

Ru-Yi Translation

The correct transmission of Tiantai stops with Chan Master Jingxi as the ninth-generation patriarch; yet after Jingxi those who transmitted it were also not cut off. Though they spread both concentration and wisdom, they cannot stand shoulder to shoulder with the nine patriarchs; yet, [as] cut jade branches and split sandalwood, they all practiced the path of Tiantai. Now Lord [Yi]tong of Baoyun truly followed after Jingxi, and further had Fazhi and Ciyun divide the work of transformation in Jiang and Zhe, so that this path was roused once more. The Master's understanding and practice were lofty and deep, and he spread [the teaching] with great force. According to the Stone Stūpa Record, the Master's writings were all lost and not transmitted. Yet examining the records of Siming, [we find] he once took up the brush on the Notes on the Commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra and the Explanation of the Eulogy on the Golden Light Profound [Meaning]; as for his other Dharma meanings, Fazhi received them all face to face and set them down in his Notes and Sub-commentaries. One work, the Explanation of the Eulogy, still survives, but is not widely transmitted. What a pity! The cloister buildings the Master built [have stood] two hundred twenty-two years; after the dust of war, the ancient traces were swept to the ground and gone. I once asked the elders and learned that the Master had a stūpa for storing his robe, kept at Xingfa Cloister of Jingqing. So I visited it, and indeed it was enshrined before the honored image in the repentance hall. It is carved and laid out with rare skill, adorned with solid gold, empty inside and square outside, about five feet high. This is a treasured old possession of Baoyun; I do not know by what condition it was left behind in that monastery. If by any chance, [as when] the pearls returned to Hepu, [it came back], would it not be a banner of transmission and upholding for the mountain school?

Ru-Yi Translation

When I, Zongxiao, once read the Thatched Hut's Surviving Matters of the Teaching Garden, I came upon the lines "All my life I have relied on your compassionate eyes; looking south, I sigh once." These were written long ago by King Zhongyi of the State of Wuyue and sent as a gift to Dharma Master [Yi]tong of Baoyun. I regretted that I could not see the whole, and always felt it as a loss. One day, visiting an old acquaintance, I obtained it and could not contain my joy; the jade of Jing and the pearl of Sui could not compare. Moreover, from this one can see that the ancients did not let power and rank stand between them, and their eyes of the path illuminated each other across the boundary of heaven and humans. That there was such a thing must be because they had received the entrustment together with the Buddha at Vulture Peak: one assisted the age with loyalty and valor, one spread the Dharma with the power of vows; though worldly and world-transcending differ, their blessings reach their descendants without end, and their measure is one. I, (Zongxiao), a late-born who has been fortunate to receive their compassionate shade — how could I not transmit the great deeds of the patriarchs? Respectfully I have set the two pieces he sent on fine jade [tablets], to supply the past matters of this mountain. Respectfully recorded by the bhikṣu (Zongxiao) of Shizhi at mid-autumn of the renxu year of Jiatai [1202].

晁說之作師行業記曰：明智中立師，明之鄞人也。元祐間住延慶，一日辭去，雖大守亦不得而留也。且曰：待余六十歲再來。自是居隱學山，眾方從之。會缺僧職，復不能捨。太守躬請出住寶雲，實其祖師通公道場。時寶雲頹圮無一全椽，師復新之。咸曰：師前曰隆其三世祖之居，今又興其四世祖之室，孰謂像法末哉？先是伽藍神腹中得願文一帋，曰：後更百年有肉身菩薩重興此地。師復退去，築菴白雲山凡四年，亦無一日不講。大守又命住延慶，不得辭，時年六十歲，果符前言矣（草菴教苑餘事載，明智住寶雲經于八年）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Chao Yuezhi, writing the Master's Record of Conduct and Deeds, says: "Master Mingzhi Zhongli was a man of Yin in Ming[zhou]. During the Yuanyou era he resided at Yanqing; one day he took his leave, and even the prefect could not keep him. He said: 'Wait until I am sixty, and I will come again.' From then on he lived at Yinxue Mountain, and the assembly then followed him there. When the post of monastic official happened to be vacant, he could not decline it again. The prefect in person invited him to come out and reside at Baoyun, which was truly the place of practice of his patriarch Lord [Yi]tong. At that time Baoyun had collapsed into ruin, without a single whole rafter, and the Master renewed it. All said: 'Earlier the Master made prosperous the residence of his third-generation patriarch, and now he has raised up the chamber of his fourth-generation patriarch — who says this is the end of the semblance Dharma?' Before this, a sheet of vow text had been found in the belly of the guardian deity of the monastery, saying: 'After another hundred years a bodhisattva in the flesh will restore this place.' The Master withdrew again and built a hermitage on Baiyun Mountain, [living there] four years in all, and not a single day did he fail to lecture. The prefect again ordered him to reside at Yanqing, and he could not decline; at that time he was sixty, fulfilling indeed his earlier words." (The Thatched Hut's Remaining Matters of the Teaching Garden records that Mingzhi resided at Baoyun for eight years.)

Stated above. At a time when later ages are lonely and desolate, knowing how hard the master's entrustment is, I reverently consider that Lecturer Lord Ying, newly appointed to Baoyun, having received [the teaching] by mind-transmission and not being burdened by the dust, has passed through all the shops of raining flowers and come out in person from the furnace that smelts gold. With crane-white hair and frosty brows, may you receive the entrustment of the men of the path for the rest of their years; with morning incense and evening fire, may you convey the laymen's sincerity [like that of] the border warden of Hua. Ru-Yi Translation

Record of the Treasury of Perpetual Life for the Benefit of Baoyun Cloister

Chinese Source

祖師自雞林來，首訪螺溪，盡得天台之道。復欲杭海，太守錢公固留之，使者顧公亦舍其室，為師傳道授業之所，故法智、慈雲二大士從是出焉。今寶雲之居，邦人目為通師翁道場是也。其間廢興相襲，不得而詳。住持瑩公坐席未溫，首歛巾盂以估於眾得錢一百萬，內外道俗又得錢百萬，太師魏國史公捐國夫人簪珥以施之，合為利益長生庫，以備歲時土木鐘鼓無窮之須。後五年建大講堂，半取其贏以助工役，實其志也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The patriarch came from Gyerim and first visited Luoxi, where he fully obtained the path of Tiantai. When he wished to sail back across the sea, the prefect Lord Qian firmly kept him, and the commissioner Lord Gu also gave his house as the place where the Master would transmit the path and confer the work. Therefore the two great beings Fazhi and Ciyun came forth from here. The present dwelling of Baoyun is what the people of the region regard as the place of practice of Grand-teacher Master [Yi]tong. Its decline and revival in between followed one another, and cannot be known in detail. The abbot, Lord Ying, before his seat had grown warm, first gathered his towel and bowl and had them valued by the assembly, obtaining one million cash; clergy and laity within and without gave another million; and the Grand Preceptor Lord Shi of the State of Wei donated the hairpins and earrings of the Lady of the State, giving them as alms. Together these made the Treasury of Perpetual Life for [the cloister's] Benefit, to provide for the endless needs of the seasons — building work, bells, and drums. Five years later he built the great lecture hall, taking half its surplus to help the labor; this truly was his intent.

Chinese Source

瑩公性淳直，而御眾以寬。寶雲初歸，有侈心者，輒起重輕之議。至有僧吏恃權以橈之，權虎而寇。傍人為震栗，而瑩自若也。太師魏公實知之，至是則人皆悅服。余聞先佛捐軀以求道，無一芥子許地空無佛身，至空劫積塵之初，所謂草樹巖崖成道利生之所率先成就，此豈智術所能致哉？今寶雲盧焰之餘才一甲子，則氣象復還舊觀，此瑩之心與昔人願轂俱馳而不忘也。後世因循苟且之事，瑩恥而不為，如石之堅、如地之載，其所植立如此，故余不得不書。瑩嗣東堂元慧師，瑩公名宗瑩云。紹熙三年七月旦日橘洲老衲寶曇記。

Ru-Yi Translation

Lord Ying is pure and upright by nature, and governs the assembly with leniency. When Baoyun first came back [to the teaching], those of extravagant mind would at once raise disputes about its weight. There was even a monk official who relied on his power to obstruct him, with power like a tiger, acting like a bandit. Onlookers trembled for him, but Ying remained composed. The Grand Preceptor, the Duke of Wei, truly knew him, and at this point everyone was pleased and submitted. I have heard that the former buddhas gave up their bodies to seek the path, so that there is not a spot the size of a mustard seed that is empty of the Buddha's body; and that at the beginning of the accumulation of dust after the eon of emptiness, the so-called places of grass, trees, cliffs, and crags where the path is attained and beings benefited are the first to be completed. How could this be brought about by cleverness? Now Baoyun, only one sexagenary cycle after the flames of the furnace, has had its appearance restored to its former view; this is because Ying's mind runs together with the vow-hubs of the people of old and does not forget them. The habits of later ages, following along and making do, Ying is ashamed of and does not do; like the firmness of stone, like the bearing of the earth, so is what he has established; therefore I cannot but write it down. Ying is the heir of Master Yuanhui of Dongtang; Lord Ying's name is Zongying. Recorded by the old patch-robed Baotan of Juzhou on the first day of the seventh month of the third year of Shaoxi [1192].

The Name of the Hall of Dharma Rain

Chinese Source

法雨堂題名

Ru-Yi Translation

Inscription of the Name of the Hall of Dharma Rain

Chinese Source

紹熈辛亥歲，住山瑩講師建大法堂，廣袤宏曠，殆甲諸方。是時竹院禪師曇公謁是院，因請立名。師應聲曰：院額寶雲。雲能致雨，雨能潤物。茲院乃台宗中興發源處，佛祖雨法雨沃群生非一日，當以法雨為堂之名。宜矣。宗曉深愛此名因依，而山門未暇揭是榜，恐久而堙沒，特表而出之。經不云乎：慈意妙大雲，澍甘露法雨。又曰：無上法雨，雨汝身田。又曰：能雨無上甘露法雨。法雨之名遍出諸大乘經。今吾祖通公大法師，負高明之識，來自三韓；得淨光法道，殆於此地洪通。四明、天竺二尊者，蟠英蓄秀，能荷傳斯宗。迄今垂二百載，其道未艾，此非雨法雨沃群生乎。昔楊無為甞贊宗門陳尊宿曰：叢林處處蒙霑潤，莫測風雷起老龍。今於寶雲通公亦云也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the xinhai year of Shaoxi [1191], the abbot of the mountain, Lecturer Ying, built the great Dharma hall, broad, long, vast, and spacious, nearly the first in all quarters. At that time Chan Master Lord Tan of Zhuyuan paid a visit to this cloister and was asked to give it a name. The Master answered at once: "The cloister's plaque is Baoyun, 'Precious Cloud.' Clouds can bring rain, and rain can moisten things. This cloister is the source from which the restoration of the Tai school arose; the buddhas and patriarchs have rained the Dharma-rain to soak the multitude of beings not for just one day. 'Dharma Rain' should be the name of the hall." Fitting indeed! I, Zongxiao, deeply love the reasons for this name, but the mountain gate has not had time to hang up this plaque; fearing that in time it would be buried and lost, I specially set it forth. Does not the sūtra say: "His very mind loving-kindness, a wondrous great cloud, raining down the sweet dew of the Dharma's own rain"? It also says: "The peerless rain of the Dharma will fall upon the field of your body." It also says: "rain the unsurpassed Dharma-rain of sweet dew." The name "Dharma-rain" appears throughout the great-vehicle sūtras. Now our patriarch, the great Dharma Master Lord [Yi]tong, bearing lofty and bright understanding, came from the Three Han; he obtained the Dharma-path of Jingguang, and spread it widely, nearly all, at this place. The two Venerables of Siming and Tianzhu, coiling brilliance and storing excellence, were able to carry and transmit this school. To the present it is nearly two hundred years, and its path has not ended; is this not raining the Dharma-rain to soak the multitude of beings? Long ago Yang Wuwei praised the Venerable Chen of the Chan school, saying: "The monasteries everywhere receive his moisture; / none can fathom the wind and thunder when the old dragon rises." Now of Lord [Yi]tong of Baoyun one may say the same.

Reading Notes for Part 10

Translator's notes

The reburial (units 2–4). Daochang (Yuetang, later of Lingyin and Jiangshan) moved Yitong's bones to Wushi ("Black Stone") Hill; Daoyin's account dates this to dingwei of Xuanhe, but the Xuanhe era ended in 1125, and dingwei is 1127 (Jianyan 1). Part 9 (unit 155) rendered 烏石塔所 as "the black stone stūpa"; here 烏石 is treated as a place name, Wushi. "Firm seeds" (堅固子) are śarīra grains. Guangli (廣利) is the name of the monastery on Mount Aśoka. Unit 4 calls Yitong "a man of Silla," while the rest of the collection says Goryeo; translated as written.

The King of Wuyue's eulogy. Daoyin (unit 4) quotes a version that differs from the full text in unit 9 ("a thousand li share one wind" against "a gathering of blessings for humans and gods"); each is translated as it stands. The poem lines "All my life I have relied on your compassionate eyes; looking south, I sigh once" are kept identical in units 4, 14, and 19.

Yitong's writings (unit 6). The Notes on the Commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra and the Explanation of the Eulogy on the Golden Light Profound [Meaning] are Yitong's lost works; Zhili's Notes (記) and Sub-commentaries (鈔) are meant generally. "Cut jade branches and split sandalwood" means that every piece is of the same precious stuff. "Pearls returning to Hepu" is the Han story of pearls that came back once a good official ruled.

Shi Hao's four eulogies (units 20–35). "Paoxi" is Fuxi. "The revolving dhāraṇī" (旋陀羅) is the dhāraṇī Zhiyi is said to have attained at Mount Dasu under Nanyue. "Pulling out a drowning sister-in-law" is Mencius 4A.17 (an expedient that breaks a rule to save life); "fish trap and snare" is Zhuangzi, ch. 26. "Deep silence, a voice of thunder" is Zhuangzi, ch. 11. "The flower opened in five petals" echoes the verse attributed to Bodhidharma. "Two spiritual feet" (二神足) means two chief disciples. Zhangsheng is Emperor Zhenzong, as in Part 9.

Zongying's postscript (unit 36). The Duke of the State of Wei is Shi Hao. 大傅 is read as 太傅 (Grand Tutor). 師翁 is rendered "Grand-teacher" (the teacher's teacher), since the hall was named for Yitong as teacher of Zhili and Zunshi.

Puchong's eulogy (units 39–42). "Betting with gold, one is easily confused" is from Zhuangzi, ch. 19 (以黃金注者殙). Tongxuan Peak is on Mount Tiantai; "the white ox" is the great cart of the Lotus.

Keguan's eulogy (unit 45). "He called all people all his fellow villagers" refers to Yitong's saying, recorded in his biographies, that he regarded everyone as a person of his home village; Yixian's statement (unit 51) says the same.

Anniversary statements (units 48, 51). In unit 48, 匪薄 is read as 菲薄 ("meager"). In unit 51, 敬觀 is taken as a copying error for 教觀 ("the teaching and contemplation"); 應身日本 is translated literally ("manifested a response-body in Japan"), though Yitong came from Korea; 玉凡 is explained by the interlinear note itself as Mount Aśoka (usually written 玉几, Yuji); 尼壇 is unclear and rendered "the altar." Chan Master Great Dharma-eye of Qingliang is Fayan Wenyi.

Zunshi at Baoyun (units 55–59). "The four great sūtras" (四大部經) are not named; in Tiantai usage they are usually the Lotus, Vimalakīrti, Nirvāṇa, and Golden Light. In unit 57, 漢魏經壬寅既廢 is unclear; "Han and Wei" is kept literally and the sentence read as saying the society lapsed after 1002. "Immeasurable Awakening" (無量覺) is Amitābha. The rain rite of unit 59 uses the Sūtra of Invoking Avalokiteśvara, as in Zunshi's other works.

Mingzhi (unit 61). Zhongli's "third-generation patriarch" is Zhili (founder of Yanqing) and his "fourth-generation patriarch" is Yitong. The interlinear note cites the Remaining Matters (餘事); the same work is called Surviving Matters (遺事) in unit 19.

Shi Hao's statement and Baotan's record (units 63–65). Wowa is the river where a heavenly horse was found under Han Wudi. "The border warden of Hua" alludes to Zhuangzi, ch. 12 (the three wishes for Yao). "Not a spot the size of a mustard seed" echoes the Lotus, "Devadatta" chapter, on Śākyamuni giving up his body everywhere. "The flames of the furnace" (盧焰, read 爐焰) refers to the burning of Baoyun in 1130 (Part 9, unit 155), about sixty years before 1192.

The address (units 68–69). It is spoken by performers (伶倫 "musicians") at the feast; "the Jibei of this culture" uses Han Yu's image of the region famous for horses; "Shun's strings" is the zither of Emperor Shun and his "South Wind" song.

Dharma Rain (unit 71). The three quotations follow site and round-two English: the Lotus, "Universal Gate" verse (site Lotus Sūtra); the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, Cunda chapter (site Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra); and the Golden Light Sūtra (round two, `jinguangming-jing/p2_body.md`). Yang Wuwei is the layman Yang Jie; the Venerable Chen is Muzhou Daoming; the source of the couplet was not located.
