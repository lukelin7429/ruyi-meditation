The Forty-two Trial Questions, the Miscellaneous Questions, the Four Kinds of Four Truths, and the Answers to Genshin of Japan

The fourth part closes fascicle three and opens fascicle four. It begins with forty-two trial questions that Zhili set for his students in 1024. No answers are recorded; the questions read as an examination on the Profound Meaning of the Lotus, the four teachings, the Vajra Scalpel, and Calming and Contemplation. Two shorter sets follow, both answered by the disciple Ziren. In the first, seven miscellaneous questions from 1014 deal with inherent evil, the relative and absolute wonders, purity, and how lands arise. The second set explains the four kinds of four truths: the truths of generation-and-extinction, of no generation, the immeasurable truths, and the truths without fabrication, matched to the Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect teachings. Fascicle four opens with its table of contents and then gives the record of 1003: twenty-seven questions sent from Japan by Genshin of Mount Tendai through the monk Jakushō, with Zhili's answers. Most of them ask about passages in Zhiyi's and Zhanran's commentaries on the Lotus and in Calming and Contemplation: the stage of the predicted buddhas, eons and world-systems, copying errors in the Japanese texts, and how inherent evil fits the Awakening of Faith. Zhili often answers by appealing to the intent of a passage rather than its letter. Twice he simply says that he has not checked the source or that the book is lost in China.

Ru-Yi Translation

My disciples study diligently; but how do you comprehend the essentials of the teaching and contemplation of our school? Therefore I have respectfully gathered forty-two items on the meanings of the texts of the various works and, opening the curtain, put these trial questions. Please cite the texts and explain the meaning, answering each one, in the hope of enlightening later generations. The Seat-Master of Yanqing, on the sixteenth day of the fifth month of the jiazi year of Tiansheng [1024].

Questions One to Six: The Five Layers of Profound Meaning

Chinese Source

一問：待麁明妙，獨褒《法華》。合對四時三教皆名為麁，文中何故同於諸經，先待鹿苑而為麁耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

First question: Clarifying the wondrous relative to the coarse, [the Profound Meaning] singles out the Lotus for praise. Combined and contrasted, the four periods and the three teachings are all called coarse. Why does the text, the same as for the other sūtras, first take the Deer Park as the coarse to which it is relative?

Chinese Source

二問：絕待釋妙，絕麁開麁，亡能亡所。究論此意，是何法門方能絕待？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second question: Explaining the wondrous as absolute: it is absolute with respect to the coarse and opens the coarse, and both what [opens] and what [is opened] vanish. Investigating this idea thoroughly, what Dharma-gate is it that alone can be absolute?

Chinese Source

三問：金剛藏說：佛甚微智辭異意同，空有不二不異不盡。意明三諦皆是經體。荊溪何故特云空假即中方為經體？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third question: Vajragarbha says: "The Buddha's very subtle wisdom: the words differ but the meaning is the same; emptiness and existence are not two, not different, not exhausted." The intent clarifies that all three truths are the essence of the sūtra. Why does Jingxi specially say that only emptiness and the provisional identical to the middle are the essence of the sūtra?

Chinese Source

四問：因果宗成方能取體，合先明宗方辨於體，約何義故前體後宗？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth question: Only when the tenet of cause and fruit is accomplished can one take up the essence; one ought first to clarify the tenet and only then discern the essence. On what meaning does the essence come first and the tenet after?

Chinese Source

五問：斷權疑近疑，生實信遠信，為妙經用者。且斷疑生信，自是行者智能，那云是經力用耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth question: "Severing provisional doubts and near doubts, giving rise to real faith and far faith" is the function of the Wondrous Sūtra. But severing doubts and giving rise to faith are in themselves the wisdom and ability of the practitioner; how can they be called the power and function of the sūtra?

Chinese Source

六問：名等四章重重已約四教五時判後論開，何故第五更明教相？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth question: The four chapters — name and so on — have layer upon layer already been judged by the four teachings and the Five Periods, and opening has then been discussed. Why does the fifth further clarify the marks of the teaching?

Ru-Yi Translation

Twelfth question: Both teachings within the realms have three vehicles. Why are the three causes greatly different in the Tripiṭaka teaching? Why are the three causes greatly the same in the shared teaching?

Chinese Source

十三問：三藏教中自有小乘中乘大乘，《法華》何故都以三藏為小乘學者？

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirteenth question: Within the Tripiṭaka teaching there are of themselves a Lesser Vehicle, a middle vehicle, and a great vehicle. Why does the Lotus take the Tripiṭaka in every case as "students of the Lesser Vehicle"?

Chinese Source

十四問：《金錍》引《大經》一十復次遮邪計空非佛性喻。離一十空外，為指何空是佛性喻耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourteenth question: The Vajra Scalpel cites the ten "furthermore" [passages] of the Great Sūtra, which block the wrong reckoning that emptiness is not the simile for buddha-nature. Apart from these ten emptinesses, what emptiness does it point to as the simile for buddha-nature?

Questions Fifteen to Twenty-two: Object, Mind, and the Perfect Principle

Chinese Source

十五問：《義例》點不思議境凡有四意，首於無情境立佛乘義。此為是色具耶？心具耶？若云色具，《止觀》正云，介爾有心，三千具足。若是心具者，何謂無情立佛乘耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifteenth question: The Guiding Principles, in pointing out the inconceivable object, has four senses in all; first, it establishes the meaning of the buddha-vehicle in the object of the insentient. Is this form containing, or mind containing? If you say form contains — Calming and Contemplation explicitly says, "If there is even the slightest mind, the three thousand are complete." If it is mind containing, why does it say that the buddha-vehicle is established in the insentient?

Chinese Source

十六問：智者自於普賢道場，見宿世事、證法華三昧。何故却云南嶽授天台圓頓之理？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixteenth question: Zhizhe himself, at the place of practice of Samantabhadra, saw the events of former lives and realized the Lotus samādhi. Why then does it say that Nanyue conferred on Tiantai the principle of the perfect and sudden?

Chinese Source

十七問：觀於緣生，緣生即空，空假不二名為三觀。荊溪云：作此說者，尚未成通。離此觀外，通教三觀其相如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventeenth question: "Contemplating what arises from conditions, what arises from conditions is identical to emptiness; emptiness and the provisional being nondual is called the threefold contemplation." Jingxi says: "Those who give this account have not yet reached even the shared." Apart from this contemplation, what are the marks of the threefold contemplation of the shared teaching?

Chinese Source

十八問：圓理所具惑業之法，與性惡同耶？異耶？若同，性中惑染體相如何？若異，果理究顯有修惡耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighteenth question: Are the dharmas of delusion and karma contained in the perfect principle the same as inherent evil, or different? If the same, what are the essence and marks of delusion and defilement within the nature? If different, when the principle of the fruit is ultimately revealed, is there cultivated evil?

Chinese Source

十九問：為令眾生開佛知見，約心辨相，歸趣如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Nineteenth question: To cause sentient beings to open the knowing and seeing of the Buddha, [the Textual Commentary] discerns the marks in terms of the mind. What is its import?

Chinese Source

二十問：《妙玄》以五重玄義別對四悉，有何深致？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twentieth question: The Wondrous Profound [Meaning] matches the five layers of profound meaning separately to the four siddhāntas. What deep point is there in this?

Chinese Source

二十一問：百界千如、百如千界互辨名數，妙旨如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-first question: The hundred realms and the thousand suchnesses, the hundred suchnesses and the thousand realms — discerning their names and numbers mutually, what is the wondrous purport?

Chinese Source

二十二問：圓教二諦約教行證明隨情智，其相如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-second question: The two truths of the perfect teaching, in terms of teaching, practice, and realization, clarify following feeling and wisdom. What are their marks?

Questions Twenty-three to Twenty-nine: Hearing, the Eight Teachings, and the Three Wisdoms

Chinese Source

二十三問：《四念處》云：一念心中具十界苦惑，名無作苦集。佛界辨相修性如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-third question: The Four Foundations of Mindfulness says: "The suffering and delusion of the ten realms within a single thought of mind are called suffering and origination without fabrication." In discerning the marks of the buddha-realm, what of cultivation and the nature?

Chinese Source

二十四問：三教我聞並約耳根，圓教教即是聞。辨相和會旨趣如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-fourth question: In the three teachings, "I heard" is in terms of the ear faculty; in the perfect teaching, the teaching is itself hearing. Discerning the marks and reconciling them, what is the import?

Chinese Source

二十五問：《大經》八教具八別耶？若具，何謂知常？不具，如何論別？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-fifth question: Does the Great Sūtra's [account of] the eight teachings contain the eight distinctions? If it contains them, how can it be said to know permanence? If it does not contain them, how can distinctions be discussed?

Chinese Source

二十六問：《大經》四教知常，故得相即。既異方等，必同《法華》。若同，《法華》開廢等耶？若異，四教云何相即？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-sixth question: In the Great Sūtra the four teachings know permanence, and so they are mutually identical. Since it differs from the Vaipulya, it must be the same as the Lotus. If the same, does it have the Lotus's opening, discarding, and so on? If different, how are the four teachings mutually identical?

Chinese Source

二十七問：化儀、化法兩種四教，判釋佛法罄無不盡。云何妙樂謂之《法華》超八教外？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-seventh question: The two kinds of four teachings, of method and of content, classify and explain the Buddha-dharma without remainder. How then does Miaole say that the Lotus is beyond the eight teachings?

Chinese Source

二十八問：昔經今經談圓不別，荊溪何故却云：始自《華嚴》終至《般若》，但次第三諦攝？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-eighth question: The earlier sūtras and the present sūtra do not differ in discussing the perfect. Why does Jingxi say: "From the Avataṃsaka to the Prajñā, all are gathered in only by the sequential three truths"?

Chinese Source

二十九問：依《法華》約修約證明三慧，分齊如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-ninth question: Relying on the Lotus, the three wisdoms are clarified in terms of cultivation and in terms of realization. What is their scope?

Questions Thirty-seven to Forty-two: Beings and Buddhas, Great and Small, Torpor and Agitation

Chinese Source

三十七問：生佛皆具三千，同異如何？宜究其旨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirty-seventh question: Beings and buddhas both contain the three thousand; how are they the same and how different? Their purport should be investigated.

Chinese Source

三十八問：《金錍》云：木石無心之語出自小宗，隨緣不變之言生乎大教。此約何義分大小耶？若云半滿者，通無隨緣不變之言，別有木石無心之稱。若云真中者，別妨同前。若云偏圓者，通別定非小宗，其妨彌甚。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirty-eighth question: The Vajra Scalpel says: "The saying that wood and stone have no mind arises in the lesser schools; the teaching of following conditions yet unchanging comes from the great teaching." On what meaning does this distinguish great and small? If you say half and full, the shared [teaching] lacks the words "following conditions yet unchanging," and the distinct has the designation "wood and stone have no mind." If you say the real and the middle, the difficulty for the distinct is as before. If you say partial and perfect, the shared and the distinct are definitely not lesser schools, and the difficulty is even greater.

Chinese Source

三十九問：名詮三法、教判四章。何故《妙宗》云：五重玄義本是經中所詮觀法？

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirty-ninth question: The name expresses the three dharmas; the teaching judges the four chapters. Why does The Wondrous Purport say: "The five layers of profound meaning are originally the method of contemplation expressed in the sūtra"?

Chinese Source

四十問：寂光既具金寶泥沙之異，那得經云唯佛一人居淨土耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fortieth question: Since tranquil light contains the differences of gold and jewels, mud and sand, how can the sūtra say that only the Buddha alone dwells in the pure land?

Chinese Source

四十一問：圓人初心頓以果地三德而為觀體，所以如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Forty-first question: The perfect person in the initial mind suddenly takes the three virtues of the stage of the fruit as the essence of contemplation; why is this so?

Chinese Source

四十二問：《止觀》云：以觀觀昏，即昏而朗；以止止散，即散而寂。荊溪對釋此二句云：照此昏體，一觀而三名，即昏而朗。寂此散體，三止而一名，即散而寂。且一觀昏耶？三止散耶？三觀朗耶？一止寂耶？不然，何以對釋云即昏而朗、即散而寂耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Forty-second question: Calming and Contemplation says: "Use contemplation to contemplate torpor, so that torpor itself becomes clarity; use calming to calm agitation, so that agitation itself becomes stillness." Jingxi, explaining these two phrases by matching them, says: "Illuminating the essence of this torpor: one contemplation with three names — so torpor itself becomes clarity. Stilling the essence of this agitation: three calmings with one name — so agitation itself becomes stillness." Now, is the one contemplation torpor? Are the three calmings agitation? Are the three contemplations clarity? Is the one calming stillness? If not, why does he explain by matching, "torpor itself becomes clarity" and "agitation itself becomes stillness"?

Seven Miscellaneous Questions: Inherent Evil and the Two Wonders

Chinese Source

教門雜問答七章

Ru-Yi Translation

Seven Chapters of Miscellaneous Questions and Answers on the Teaching

Chinese Source

四明法師問門人（自仁）答

Ru-Yi Translation

Questions by the Dharma Master of Siming, Answers by His Disciple (Ziren)

Chinese Source

大中祥符七年講授次，未知學者淺深之解，因出數問，請各答上。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the seventh year of Dazhong Xiangfu [1014], in the course of lecturing, not knowing how shallow or deep the students' understanding was, I put forth several questions and asked each of them to answer.

Chinese Source

一問：荊溪云：忽都未聞性惡之名，安能信有性德之行？然則由性惡而修性行，說聽久矣。的論旨趣，未審若何？

Ru-Yi Translation

First question: Jingxi says: "If one has never heard even the name 'inherent evil,' how can one believe in the practice of inherent virtue?" So the cultivation of the practice of the nature by way of inherent evil has long been spoken of and heard. What exactly is its import?

Chinese Source

答：荊溪立此二句，意顯圓宗無作之行耳。蓋由圓人創心修觀者，皆以見思王數而為發觀之端故。若前三教人，以教不談性具九界，則見思王數不即性惡。既非性惡，定為能障，故須別修觀智破此惑心，方顯本有常住之體。遂致惑智待對、境觀不忘，行成有作矣。今圓實教中既詮性具九界，則見思王數即是性惡。惑既即性，只以此惑而為能觀。惑既為能觀，其孰為所觀？能所一如、境觀不二，《輔行》所謂非但所觀無明法性體性不二，抑亦能觀觀智即無明是。以是義故，方知初心修觀造境即中，無不真實，功由性惡。融通無作之行，於茲成矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: In setting up these two phrases, Jingxi's intent is to reveal the practice without fabrication of the perfect school. For when the perfect person, at the start of the mind, cultivates contemplation, he always takes the king and factors of views and thought as the starting point for giving rise to contemplation. People of the earlier three teachings, because their teaching does not discuss the nine realms included in the nature, find that the king and factors of views and thought are not identical to inherent evil. Since they are not inherent evil, they are definitely what obstructs; therefore one must separately cultivate the wisdom of contemplation to break this deluded mind, and only then is the originally present, permanent essence revealed. Thus delusion and wisdom stand in relativity and opposition, object and contemplation are not forgotten, and the practice becomes fabricated. Now, since the perfect and real teaching expresses the nine realms included in the nature, the king and factors of views and thought are inherent evil. Since delusion is identical to the nature, this very delusion alone is taken as what contemplates. Since delusion is what contemplates, what is it that is contemplated? What contemplates and what is contemplated are one suchness; object and contemplation are nondual. As the Fuxing says: "Not only are ignorance and the dharma-nature that are contemplated nondual in essence and nature; the contemplating wisdom that contemplates is itself ignorance." On this meaning, one knows that in the initial mind, cultivating contemplation, every object one arrives at is the middle and nothing is not true and real; the merit lies with inherent evil. The interfused practice without fabrication is accomplished here.

Chinese Source

二問：《法華》既顯一實，何故《玄文》釋題而明二妙？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second question: Since the Lotus reveals the one real, why does the Profound Text, explaining the title, clarify two wonders?

Chinese Source

答：《法華》為開權顯實之教，廼一代之所歸、五時之極唱。若不以二妙甄之，則一乘顯實妙義難彰矣。且初相待論判麁妙者，彼此互形曰相，以他望己為待。蓋待前四時七教之麁，方顯《法華》一乘之妙。良以昔日諸經機緣未熟，部味教觀兼但對帶，不若《法華》淳圓絕妙，是故為麁。故經曰：正直捨方便，但說無上道。已今當說此《法華經》最為第一，以故此經淳一無雜，獨得妙名，良由以也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The Lotus is the teaching that opens the provisional and reveals the real; it is what the whole lifetime [of teaching] returns to and the highest song of the Five Periods. If one did not distinguish it by the two wonders, the wondrous meaning of the one vehicle revealing the real would be hard to make plain. First, the relative [wonder], discussing and judging coarse and wondrous: "relative" (相待) means that this and that give shape to each other, which is 相, and viewing oneself from the other is 待. For only relative to the coarseness of the seven teachings of the earlier four periods is the wonder of the one vehicle of the Lotus revealed. Because in the sūtras of former days the capacities and conditions were not yet ripe, and in collection, flavor, teaching, and contemplation they were combined, exclusive, contrasted, and accompanied, they were not like the Lotus, purely perfect, absolute and wondrous; therefore they are coarse. Hence the sūtra says: "Straightforwardly setting aside skillful means, I teach only the unsurpassed path." Of [the sūtras] already taught, now taught, and to be taught, this Lotus Sūtra is foremost. Therefore this sūtra, pure, one, and unmixed, alone obtains the name "wondrous"; there is good reason for it.

Further, one should know that although the shared teaching receives the name "identical to emptiness," viewed from the perfect it has the words without the reality. Why? Because the purport of the shared teaching is not that the whole essence gives rise to function and the whole of principle becomes phenomena. "Identical" means "is." The whole essence is the function, the whole function is the essence, essence and function are nondual — this is what the perfect teaching discusses. Although the distinct teaching expresses the principle of the real of the middle, principle lies outside phenomena; it is not yet the meaning of identity — how much less the shared teaching! From this it is clear that the shared teaching has only the name of identity and not the meaning of identity. One should know that because people of this teaching are lightly deluded to the real, the name "no generation" is established from the principle within the realms.

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the immeasurable four truths of the distinct teaching. What this teaching expresses is the dharma of the distinct bodhisattvas, who through eons practice practices and cultivate Buddha-dharmas as many as the Ganges' sands; hence it is called immeasurable. Yet although what is cultivated is called immeasurable, if one points to its essentials, it does not go beyond the four kinds, medicine and illness. Hence it says: suffering has immeasurable marks, because the retributions of the ten realms are not the same. Origination has immeasurable marks, because the afflictions of dust-and-sand are not the same. The path has immeasurable marks, because it is not known by any śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha. Cessation has immeasurable marks, because the pāramitās are not the same. Since there is the illness of suffering and origination, there is the medicine of path and cessation. That medicine and illness counteract each other in various different ways is because, in this teaching, the principle of the Tathāgatagarbha is covered by the afflictions of dust-and-sand, so one cultivates the Dharma-gates [as many as] dust-and-sand to counteract them. Hence the Fuxing says: "Illuminating and knowing that the one mind gives rise to immeasurable minds; because minds are immeasurable, delusion and understanding are immeasurable: in delusion there are the suffering and origination of the ten realms, in awakening the path and cessation of the ten realms." This is the meaning. Yet in terms of principle, this teaching is the middle way, buddha-nature, beyond the realms. This nature acts as the support for all dharmas; but its essence is not discussed as inherent inclusion, only as the arising of the nature; therefore all dharmas, in their very substance, are not identical. This is what Jingxi says: "The branches come forth without its being said that principle is them; so we know it is the distinct [teaching's] 'is.'"

The Four Truths without Fabrication

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

That these four are named "without fabrication," pointing directly to the essential, does not go beyond the nature taking over cultivation; therefore all practices are without fabrication. Now, to broaden this idea, there are three [points]. First, medicine and illness are mutually identical. As the Wondrous Profound [Meaning] says: "Because one is deluded to principle, bodhi and afflictions are called the truth of origination, and nirvāṇa and birth and death are called the truth of suffering. One should know that the single word "identical" is the gate of all wonders. Affliction and birth and death are the illnesses of sentient beings; now one embodies and guides them by the contemplation of the principle of the one real: illness is identical to medicine, medicine is identical to illness, medicine and illness are one suchness, and there is nothing at all to do; hence it says "without fabrication; outside the one real there is nothing else."

This is so because what the perfect teaching expresses does not go beyond the one real, and outside the one real there is no other dharma at all. As Nāgārjuna says: "Apart from the true mark of all dharmas, everything else falls into the work of Māra." The true mark is without marks and pervades all dharmas; there is no mark of birth and death and no mark of nirvāṇa, and all the marks there are, mark after mark, are true and real; therefore the four truths are all called without fabrication. Hence the Meaning of the Four Teachings says: "Clarifying the four real truths in terms of the one real, these four are ultimately unattainable; this is the one real truth, and it is called the four real truths without fabrication." Is this not its meaning?

Seventeenth question: The sixth [fascicle] of the Wondrous Profound [Meaning], showing the suffering and pleasure of lands, has two explanations: one says they come from sentient beings and are not made by the Buddha; one says they come from the Buddha and do not concern sentient beings (and so on). If they are made by the Buddha, would the evil karma of sentient beings then be empty and without fruit or retribution? Would the Tathāgata turn his back on great compassion and skillful means and make sentient beings undergo the suffering of the three paths? So the practice that Never Disparaging practiced and the assembly that Mañjuśrī transformed are all skillful applications of means, changing long into short and turning heavy into light; they are certainly not the setting up of conditions of suffering and affliction.

Chinese Source

答：若論作善惡因招苦樂報，乃由眾生，非關於佛。若約用苦樂事折攝眾生，乃由佛現，不關眾生。良由宜用苦治方受化者，諸佛即為作之。如先王制刑，豈欲以苦加於百姓？蓋至仁也。諸佛亦爾，為折攝故，而強現之。又苦樂之國眾生雖居，不得自在。諸佛於茲而能轉變，故云由佛。又佛是生家之佛，生是佛家之生，故云眾生心中諸佛念念證真，諸佛心內眾生新新作業。故知論生則一時屬生，舉佛則一時屬佛。以是義故，故經云今此三界皆是我有，其中眾生悉是吾子也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: If one discusses making causes of good and evil and bringing about retributions of suffering and pleasure, that comes from sentient beings and does not concern the Buddha. If it is in terms of using matters of suffering and pleasure to subdue and embrace sentient beings, that is manifested by the Buddha and does not concern sentient beings. It is because those who must be cured with suffering before they receive transformation are those for whom the buddhas make it. As when the former kings instituted punishments — did they wish to inflict suffering on the people? It was supreme humaneness. The buddhas are also so: for the sake of subduing and embracing, they forcibly manifest it. Again, although sentient beings dwell in lands of suffering and pleasure, they are not sovereign there; the buddhas can transform them, hence it says "from the Buddha." Again, the Buddha is the buddha of beings' household, and beings are the beings of the Buddha's household; hence it says that within the minds of sentient beings the buddhas realize the true thought after thought, and within the minds of the buddhas sentient beings make karma anew and anew. So we know that if one discusses beings, at once [everything] belongs to beings; if one raises the Buddha, at once [everything] belongs to the Buddha. On this meaning, therefore, the sūtra says: "Now these three realms are entirely my own; the beings within them are all my own children."

Twenty-third question: The intent of Calming and Contemplation and other [works] says: "The beginningless store-mind contains the dharmas of the ten realms and the ten suchnesses, and so on up to the buddha-fruit, which is also so." The Hongjue says: "The icchantika has severed cultivated good entirely; only inherent good remains. The Tathāgata has severed cultivated evil entirely; only inherent evil remains." Doubters of recent times say: The Awakening of Faith, in the gate of counteracting wrong attachments, says: "Hearing the sūtras say that all worldly defiled dharmas of birth and death exist in dependence on the Tathāgatagarbha, and that all dharmas are not apart from true suchness, and not understanding this, they say that the essence of the Tathāgatagarbha itself has all worldly dharmas of birth and death and so on. How is this counteracted? (This is the question.) Because the Tathāgatagarbha from the beginning has only pure virtues exceeding the sands of the Ganges, which are not apart, not severed, and not different from true suchness. Because defiled dharmas of afflictions exceeding the sands of the Ganges exist only falsely, are by nature originally nonexistent, and from beginningless time have never been associated with the Tathāgatagarbha. If the essence of the Tathāgatagarbha had false dharmas, and yet one caused them to cease forever by realizing and meeting [it], there would be no such case. (This is the answer.)" The treatise says "by nature originally nonexistent"; how does this not contradict at once the inherent inclusion discussed by Tiantai?

Chinese Source

答：百界千如，性善性惡，皆是體具微妙法門清淨功德。即《起信》中過恒沙等諸淨功德，不離不斷，皆真如故。若一切世間生死煩惱妄染之法皆是修惡，雖全性起而違於性，故須永滅。若稱理而修，萬行功德皆是修善，亦全性起而順於性，即同常住。故云：像實故稱理本有，虛空故迷轉成性。遂使證會之時，修惡雖盡，性惡常存也。諸家所明感應之義，為不知性惡法門故，其果後垂惡趣之身，皆須以神通力變現，所以今家斥同外道神變也。故云：證本真源，還任眾生轉識現起。若不談體具十界，亦是別教之義。若得此意，一家教旨如觀掌中，與諸經論更無少異也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The hundred realms and thousand suchnesses, inherent good and inherent evil, are all subtle and wondrous Dharma-gates and pure virtues included in the essence. These are the "pure virtues exceeding the sands of the Ganges, not apart, not severed," in the Awakening of Faith, because they are all true suchness. All the worldly dharmas of birth and death and of affliction, false and defiled, are cultivated evil; although they arise from the whole nature, they go against the nature, and so must be extinguished forever. If one cultivates according with principle, the merits of the myriad practices are all cultivated good; they too arise from the whole nature but accord with the nature, and so are the same as the permanently abiding. Hence it says: "Because the image is real, it accords with principle as originally present; because emptiness is void, delusion turns and becomes the nature." Thus at the time of realizing and meeting [it], although cultivated evil is exhausted, inherent evil always remains. The meaning of appeal and response clarified by the other schools — because they do not know the Dharma-gate of inherent evil, the bodies of the evil destinies they let fall after the fruit must all be transformed and manifested by the power of supernormal powers; therefore our school rejects them as the same as the outsiders' supernatural transformations. Hence it says: "Having realized the true source of the origin, it lets the transforming consciousness of sentient beings manifest and arise." If one does not discuss the ten realms included in the essence, it is also the meaning of the distinct teaching. If one grasps this intent, the purport of our school's teaching is like looking at the palm of one's hand, and it is not in the least different from the sūtras and treatises.

Chinese Source

二十四問：《四教義》第一解有頓漸等教異云：鹿野鶴林之文、七處八會之教，豈非無頓漸之異、不定祕密之殊。疑者云：此中應言豈非有，何言無字耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-fourth question: The first [fascicle] of the Meaning of the Four Teachings, explaining that there are different teachings such as sudden and gradual, says: "The texts of the Deer Park and the Crane Grove, the teaching of the seven places and eight assemblies — are these not without the difference of sudden and gradual, without the distinction of indeterminate and secret?" Doubters say: Here it should say "are these not having"; why does it have the character "without"?

Chinese Source

答。若據此地本，而云非頓漸之異。今以義求之，知二處本皆訛。於彼本非字下須除無字，此地本須於非字上加一豈字，則彼此文義成也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The copy of this land reads "not the difference of sudden and gradual." Now seeking it by meaning, I know that the copies of both places are corrupt. In that copy, the character "without" below the character "not" must be removed; in the copy of this land, the character "are" must be added above the character "not." Then the meaning of the text in both is accomplished.

Chinese Source

二十五問：《四教義》第三云：三藏菩薩行菩薩道，二阿僧祇劫是煖法位，第三僧祇是頂法位（云云）。近代疑者云：《婆沙論》後身菩薩坐道樹下，始修四善根，一座成正覺。三藏教義多依《婆沙》，斯文相違何耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-fifth question: The third [fascicle] of the Meaning of the Four Teachings says: "The bodhisattva of the Tripiṭaka [teaching], practicing the bodhisattva path, for two asaṃkhyeya eons is at the stage of warmth; the third asaṃkhyeya is the stage of the summit" (and so on). Doubters of recent times say: The Vibhāṣā [says that] the bodhisattva in his final body, sitting beneath the bodhi tree, only then cultivates the four roots of good and accomplishes right awakening in one sitting. The meaning of the Tripiṭaka teaching mostly follows the Vibhāṣā; why does this text contradict it?

Chinese Source

答：三藏菩薩二三僧祇雖用煖頂觀法，正為伏惑，行諸事行；若到樹下，行為真故，復用四法為斷惑加行也。如三祇久伏三界思惑，而於樹下仍用三十四心。以此驗之，知非碩異。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Although the bodhisattva of the Tripiṭaka [teaching] uses the contemplative methods of warmth and summit in the second and third asaṃkhyeyas, it is properly to subdue delusion and practice the phenomenal practices. On reaching the tree, because the practice is for the true, he again uses the four dharmas as the preparatory practice for severing delusion. Just as for three asaṃkhyeyas he long subdues the delusions of thought of the three realms, and yet beneath the tree still uses the thirty-four minds. Verifying it by this, we know it is not a great discrepancy.

Chinese Source

二十六問：一家圓宗教部所引經論，并舊師所著章疏，多與本文相違。未知何也？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-sixth question: The sūtras and treatises cited by the teaching and collections of the perfect school of our house, and the commentaries and essays written by the former masters, often disagree with the original texts. I do not know why.

Chinese Source

答：山家凡所立義，徵引諸文多取其義，不專寫文。乍似相違，實不相違，當以意會之則可也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: In all the meanings established by our mountain school, the texts drawn on and cited are mostly taken for their meaning and not copied word for word. They may seem to disagree but in fact do not disagree; one should understand them by the intent.

Chinese Source

二十七問：《五百問論》題下云妙樂大師造。疑者云：此論似多訛謬，且舉一二。如言阿難、羅雲，論中不舉供養佛數，及破他師所釋種性等七地義，似歡喜等十地。若是大師所製，不可不通。

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-seventh question: Below the title of the Treatise of Five Hundred Questions it says, "Composed by the Great Master Miaole." Doubters say: This treatise seems to have many errors; let me raise one or two. For example, in speaking of Ānanda and Rāhula, the treatise does not give the number of buddhas to whom they made offerings; and in refuting other masters' explanation of the seven grounds of lineage and so on, they seem like the ten grounds of joy and so on. If it were composed by the Great Master, these must be accounted for.

答：此論宋地闕本，茲不得而評論矣。

Answer: This treatise is missing in the Song lands, so I cannot assess it here.

Translator's notes

Forty-two trial questions (units 0–43). Only the questions survive. They mostly test the Profound Meaning of the Lotus: the relative and absolute wonders, the five layers of profound meaning (name, essence, tenet, function, teaching), the four kinds of four truths, and the four siddhāntas. Vajragarbha's words (unit 4) are from the Daśabhūmika section of the Avataṃsaka, as quoted in the Profound Meaning. "If there is even the slightest mind, the three thousand are complete" (unit 16) and "Use contemplation to contemplate torpor…" (unit 43) follow the site's Mohe Zhiguan. The Vajra Scalpel line (unit 39) follows the vault draft, with 隨緣不變 given as "following conditions yet unchanging." Zhizhe's "essential of mind" verse (unit 35) is quoted in the Guiding Principles (T1913) and the Chan Oral Instructions (T1919).

The Lotus quotations (unit 50). "It settles once and for all the Dharma of the śrāvakas… the king of all sūtras," "the bodhisattva path," and "this sūtra opens the gate of skillful means, and reveals the mark of true reality" follow the site's Lotus Sūtra, shortened to the clauses quoted. "Straightforwardly setting aside skillful means…" is the translator's own wording. In part 4 unit 105, "The meaning of the true mark of all phenomena I have now taught for you" is the site's wording.

Jingxi in unit 54. "The defiled essence of the instant is entirely pure," "the three thousand, while in principle, are all alike called ignorance," and "the three thousand are unchanged, and ignorance is itself illumination" follow the vault draft of The Ten Gates of Nonduality. "The five destinies are pure and receive no form…" is cited as a sūtra; the wording is close to T384 (Pusa chu taishu tian jiangshen mutai shuo guangpu jing).

The four kinds of four truths (units 63–69). The names follow the glossary (generation-and-extinction / no generation / immeasurable), except that 無作 is "without fabrication". "Suffering is the shifting of the three marks…" is quoted as Calming and Contemplation; the same four phrases appear in the Profound Meaning (fasc. 2) and the site's Jiaoguan Gangzong ("suffering is the shifting of the three marks of arising, changing, and ceasing"); "Ordinary beings have suffering but no truth" is the Nirvāṇa Sūtra via the Profound Meaning. "The aggregates, entrances, and realms are suchness…" is Zhili's text of Calming and Contemplation, fasc. 1 (the site page reads "the aggregates and sense-bases are all thusness itself"; the English follows Ziren's wording). "Dharmas are originally unborn / now there is also no cessation" adapts the site's Vimalakīrti. "Overturning each other in turn," "the mind that is produced and contemplated…," and "Illuminating and knowing that the one mind…" are in the Fuxing; "The branches come forth…" is in the Annotations (T1717). "Apart from the true mark of all dharmas…" (Nāgārjuna) and "Clarifying the four real truths in terms of the one real…" (Meaning of the Four Teachings) were not located in that wording. "In the nature, the whole of cultivation becomes the nature…" is attributed to the Profound [Meaning] Annotations; the wording appears in the Zongjing lu.

Missing "second" (unit 68). Ziren announces three points but numbers only the first and third; the passage from Calming and Contemplation onward is probably the second. The English keeps the text as it is.

Japan (units 85–144). Genshin (942–1017) of the Ryōgon-in (楞嚴院) at Yokawa on Mount Hiei (here "Mount Tendai") sent the questions with Jakushō in 1003. His titles 法橋上士位 and 內供奉十大禪師 (Hokkyō Shōnin rank; palace chaplain, one of the ten meditation masters) are translated literally. Zongxiao's note in unit 85 says the Stele of Conduct and Deeds counts twenty questions. In the questions, "the commentary" is Zhiyi's Textual Commentary on the Lotus (T1718) and "the Notes" is Zhanran's Notes on it (T1719). The Hongjue is the Fuxing chuanhong jue (the Fuxing).

Lotus passages in the Japanese questions. The sahā world "transformed… without great oceans, rivers" and "every other god and human was moved" (unit 100), "rose up of itself from the palace of the dragon king Sāgara in the great ocean," the emanated buddhas who "had all gathered," each with "one great bodhisattva as his attendant," and "Now these three realms are entirely my own…" (unit 124) follow the site's Lotus Sūtra. "Endowed with Ten Million Light-Marks" (unit 121) is the buddha predicted for Yaśodharā. The Never Disparaging line (unit 110) is close to the site's "swiftly accomplished the buddha-path."

Other sources in the Japanese questions. "Since Śāriputra was of superior faculties…" (unit 92) and "One mind makes one…" (unit 129) are in the Textual Commentary (T1718) and Calming and Contemplation. "In the end there is one buddha who, at the very first, had no teaching" and "At the ten grounds one has only severed forty grades" are in the Notes (T1719); the Song reading "forty" is confirmed there. The Abhidharmakośa verse (unit 129) is in T1558. The Awakening of Faith passage (unit 135) is T1666. "The icchantika has severed cultivated good entirely…" (unit 135) is attributed by Genshin to the Hongjue, but the wording is found in the Profound Meaning of Avalokiteśvara (T1726). "Because the image is real…" (unit 136) is the Ten Gates of Nonduality (vault draft), read with Zhili's sense of 稱 "accords with."

Zhili's admissions. In unit 134 he says he has not had time to check the Vibhāṣā; in unit 144 that the Treatise of Five Hundred Questions (round-two `fahua-wubai-wen`) was lost in China. Both are translated plainly.
