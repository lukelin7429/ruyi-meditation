The Three Functions of Dispelling and Subduing, the Distinct Principle Following Conditions, the Verses on the Thing Itself, and the Thirty Questions from the Crimson Curtain

The third part finishes fascicle two and gives most of fascicle three: five pieces in which Zhili argues doctrine with opponents, a fellow student, and his own disciples. The first (1017) explains a short passage in Zhiyi's Commentary on [the Sūtra of] Invoking Avalokiteśvara on the three functions of the spell (in phenomena, in practice, and in principle); Zhili argues that the "poison of principle" is inherent evil, so that the deluded mind itself becomes what dispels the poison. The second piece puts nineteen questions to Zhiyuan of Gushan, whose Notes Elucidating the Meaning had denied this. The third piece, twenty questions to a fellow member of the school, defends Zhili's claim in Pointing to the Essentials that even the distinct teaching's true suchness "follows conditions": what marks off the perfect teaching is inherent inclusion, not following conditions, so Fazang's "final teaching" is not equal to the Tiantai perfect teaching. Then come two rounds of verse exchanged with Ciyun Zunshi on why the Golden Light Profound Meaning says the sūtra's name comes "from the thing itself," and thirty short questions on the four teachings, set by Zhili in 1017 and answered by his disciple Renyue.

The Three Functions of Dispelling and Subduing: Preface and Commentary Text

Chinese Source

釋請觀音疏中消伏三用（并序）

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Three Functions of Dispelling and Subduing in the Commentary on [the Sūtra of] Invoking Avalokiteśvara (with Preface)

Chinese Source

消伏神呪，修行要道，功用難思。吾祖發揮，今人受賜。疏文既簡，讀者多迷。惟冠攝一經，實消伏三用，儻釋之不當，修者何依？今附本宗，略評此義，敢言益物，聊軌自心。時皇宋天禧元年丁巳歲十月既望敘。

Ru-Yi Translation

The spiritual spell of dispelling and subduing is an essential path of cultivation and practice; its efficacy is hard to conceive. Our patriarch brought it to light, and people today receive the gift. Yet the text of the commentary is brief, and readers are often confused. Only [this passage], which crowns and gathers in the whole sūtra, is truly [about] the three functions of dispelling and subduing; if it is explained wrongly, on what will practitioners rely? Now, adhering to our own school, I briefly assess this meaning. How would I dare say that it benefits others? It is only to give a rule to my own mind. Prefaced on the sixteenth day of the tenth month of the first year of Tianxi of the August Song, the year being dingsi [1017].

Chinese Source

疏云：用即為三，一事、二行、三理。事者，虎狼刀劍等也。行者，五住煩惱也。理者，法界無礙無染而染，即理性之毒也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The commentary says: "The functions, then, are three: first, phenomena; second, practice; third, principle. 'Phenomena' are tigers, wolves, knives, swords, and so on. 'Practice' is the afflictions of the five abidings. 'Principle': the dharma-realm is unobstructed, undefiled yet defiled; this is the poison of the principle-nature."

The Three Poisons Named by Their Principal Aspect

Chinese Source

釋曰：用即為三，標三用也。一事等者，列三用也。中道總持被十種行者，修之不同，乃成三種消伏之用。觀音分證之法，遍於一切眾生之心，隨乎功行親疎，致使力用差別。如《起信論》明佛菩薩用，並就眾生事識業識辨之，以良醫妙藥狂子服之乃彰功用也。應知三種毒害，捨旁從正受名不同。如《普門別行疏》分別火難等相，報火至初禪，豈此下諸有全無業惑？蓋苦報為正也。業火至有頂，豈三界無惑報？以業為正也。煩惱火通四教，豈三乘人全無業報？以煩惱為正也。今事毒在欲界，此約果報，故受事名。行毒從色界，盡別教教道，以不即理故，別受行名。理毒唯圓，以談即故也。蓋煩惱中分即不即異，故名行名理不同。若分別相，從正受名，與彼不異。所消毒害既爾，能消三用可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explanation: "The functions, then, are three" marks the three functions. "First, phenomena," and so on, lists the three functions. The middle-way dhāraṇī is applied to ten kinds of practitioners; because their cultivation differs, it becomes three kinds of function of dispelling and subduing. The dharma of Avalokiteśvara's partial realization pervades the minds of all sentient beings; according as their effort and practice are close or distant, the power and function differ. Just as the Awakening of Faith, in explaining the function of buddhas and bodhisattvas, distinguishes it entirely in terms of the phenomenal consciousness and the karmic consciousness of sentient beings: a good physician's wondrous medicine shows its efficacy only when the deranged son takes it. One should know that the three kinds of poison and harm receive different names by setting aside the secondary and following the principal. For example, the Separately Circulated Commentary on the Universal Gate, distinguishing the marks of the calamity of fire and so on, [says that] the fire of retribution reaches up to the first dhyāna. Is it that in all the existences below this there is no karma or delusion at all? It is because painful retribution is principal. The fire of karma reaches up to the Peak of Existence. Is it that in the three realms there are no delusion and retribution? It is because karma is taken as principal. The fire of affliction extends through the four teachings. Is it that people of the three vehicles have no karma and retribution at all? It is because affliction is taken as principal. Now the poison of phenomena lies in the desire realm; this is in terms of the fruit of retribution, so it receives the name "phenomena." The poison of practice extends from the form realm up to the path of teaching of the distinct teaching; because it is not identical to principle, it receives the separate name "practice." The poison of principle belongs only to the perfect, because it speaks of identity. For within affliction, identity and non-identity are distinguished as different; hence the names "practice" and "principle" are not the same. If one distinguishes the marks, the names are received by following the principal, no differently from that [commentary]. Since the poison and harm that are dispelled are like this, the three functions that dispel can be known.

釋中事者，牒事消伏用也。虎狼刀劍等者，所消伏毒害也。蓋果報行人為免現在刀虎等難，多用散心持名誦呪。修因戒善者，亦免未來果報刀等。約此人明能消所消，一往屬事。行者者，牒行消伏用也。五住煩惱者，所消伏毒害也。雖不出能消之相，應以所消顯之。五住煩惱非三觀不消，但此三觀攝兩二乘及三菩薩。修因禪定者，亦伏八地之愛。此等行人雖能伏斷煩惱，而皆不即法性。如別教道，縱知能造之心體是佛性，而謂無明自住。以不聞本性具惡法門故，非即理之惑。別人尚爾，前教可知。故此等人所消毒害既當自住，能消之觀全是別修，是則四諦俱非無作，故能消所消皆名為行也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the explanation, "phenomena" takes up the function of dispelling and subduing in phenomena. "Tigers, wolves, knives, swords, and so on" are the poison and harm that are dispelled and subdued. Practitioners [who seek] the fruit of retribution mostly, in order to escape present calamities of knives, tigers, and so on, hold the name and recite the spell with a scattered mind. Those who cultivate the causes of precepts and good also escape knives and so on in the fruit of retribution of the future. Taking these people to clarify what dispels and what is dispelled, it belongs, in a general sense, to phenomena. "Practice" takes up the function of dispelling and subduing in practice. "The afflictions of the five abidings" are the poison and harm that are dispelled and subdued. Although [the text] does not set out the mark of what dispels, it should be revealed through what is dispelled. The afflictions of the five abidings are not dispelled except by the threefold contemplation; but this threefold contemplation gathers in the two [kinds of] two vehicles and the three [kinds of] bodhisattvas. Those who cultivate the causes of dhyāna and concentration also subdue the craving of the eight grounds. Although these practitioners can subdue and sever afflictions, none of them is identical to the dharma-nature. As in the path of teaching of the distinct [teaching]: even though they know that the essence of the mind that creates is buddha-nature, they say that ignorance abides by itself, because they have not heard the Dharma-gate of the nature's original inclusion of evil; [so for them it is] not a delusion identical to principle. If even the distinct person is like this, the earlier teachings can be known. Hence the poison and harm that such people dispel abides by itself, and the contemplation that dispels is wholly a separate cultivation; thus the four truths are none of them without fabrication, and so both what dispels and what is dispelled are called "practice."

Chinese Source

理者者，牒理消伏用也。法界無礙無染而染，即理性之毒也者所消伏也。雖不出能消之相，應以所消顯之。且明所消者，法界是所迷之理，無礙是受熏之德。所迷本淨故無染，受熏變造故而染，全三德而成三障，故曰即理性之毒。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Principle" takes up the function of dispelling and subduing in principle. "The dharma-realm is unobstructed, undefiled yet defiled; this is the poison of the principle-nature" is what is dispelled and subdued. Although [the text] does not set out the mark of what dispels, it should be revealed through what is dispelled. To clarify first what is dispelled: the dharma-realm is the principle to which one is deluded; "unobstructed" is the virtue of receiving perfuming. Because what one is deluded to is originally pure, it is "undefiled"; because it receives perfuming and transforms and creates, it is "yet defiled." The whole of the three virtues becomes the three obstructions; hence it says, "this is the poison of the principle-nature."

Chinese Source

然即理之談難得其意，須以具不具簡，方見即不即殊。何者？若所迷法界不具三障染故有於三障，縱說一性隨緣，亦乃惑染自住，毒害有作。以反本時三障須破，即義不成，不名即理性之毒。屬前別教，等名為行毒也。若所迷法界本具三障染故現於三障，此則惑染依他，毒害無作。以復本時染毒宛然，方成即義，是故名為即理性之毒，的屬圓教也。故荊溪釋無明依他義云：此同體依，依而復即，故知體具三障。起三障用，用還依體，與體不二，此依方即。並由理具方有事用，斯是圓乘，若不談具乃名別教，是知由性惡故，方論即理之毒也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet the talk of "identical to principle" is hard to grasp in its meaning; only by sifting it with "contains" and "does not contain" does one see the difference between identical and not identical. How so? If the dharma-realm one is deluded to does not contain the defilement of the three obstructions, and therefore the three obstructions come to exist, then even if one speaks of the one nature following conditions, delusion and defilement still abide by themselves, and the poison and harm are fabricated. Because at the time of returning to the origin the three obstructions must be broken, the meaning of identity is not established, and it is not called "the poison of the principle-nature." It belongs to the earlier distinct teaching and so on, all called the poison of practice. If the dharma-realm one is deluded to originally contains the defilement of the three obstructions, and therefore the three obstructions appear, then delusion and defilement depend on another, and the poison and harm are without fabrication. Because at the time of restoring the origin the defiled poison remains fully as it is, only then is the meaning of identity accomplished; therefore it is called "the poison identical to the principle-nature," and it definitely belongs to the perfect teaching. Hence Jingxi, explaining the meaning of ignorance depending on another, says: "This is dependence on the same essence; it depends and yet is identical." So we know that the essence contains the three obstructions. Raising the function of the three obstructions, the function in turn depends on the essence and is not two with the essence; only this dependence is identity. All of this is because only through inclusion in principle is there the function in phenomena; this is the perfect vehicle. If one does not speak of inclusion, it is called the distinct teaching. From this we know that only because of inherent evil can one speak of the poison identical to principle.

能消伏用者，所消之毒既即理性，能消之用豈不即理？斯乃理慧理定為能消能伏也。復應了知理消伏用，體是性惡，方得初心即修中觀。故荊溪云：忽都未聞性惡之名，安能信有性德之行。性德非理耶？行非消伏用耶？欲明理消之用，要知性惡之功。何者？以初心人皆用見思王數為發觀之始。前之三教不談性惡，故此王數不能即性。既不即性，故須別緣真中二理破此王數。既有能緣所緣、能破所破，故毒害消伏俱受行名。若圓頓教，既詮性惡，則見思王數乃即性之毒。毒既即性，故只以此毒為能消伏。既以毒為能消，則當處絕待，誰云能破所破？有何能緣所緣？毒害即中，諸法趣毒，遮照相即，言慮莫窮。故荊溪云：非但所觀無明法性體性不二，能觀觀智即無明是。若非理毒，焉即能觀？故一心三觀圓頓十乘更非別修，皆理消伏也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the function that dispels and subdues: since the poison that is dispelled is identical to the principle-nature, how would the function that dispels not be identical to principle? This is the wisdom of principle and the concentration of principle that dispel and subdue. One should further understand that the essence of the function of dispelling and subduing in principle is inherent evil; only so can one, from the initial mind, cultivate the contemplation of the middle. Hence Jingxi says: "If one has never heard even the name 'inherent evil,' how can one believe in the practice of inherent virtue?" Is inherent virtue not principle? Is practice not the function of dispelling and subduing? If you wish to clarify the function of dispelling in principle, you must know the efficacy of inherent evil. How so? People of the initial mind all use the [mind-]king and [mental] factors of views and thought as the starting point for giving rise to contemplation. The first three teachings do not speak of inherent evil, so these king and factors cannot be identical to the nature. Since they are not identical to the nature, one must separately take the two principles of the real and the middle as objects in order to break these king and factors. Since there is what takes as object and what is taken as object, what breaks and what is broken, the poison and harm and the dispelling and subduing all receive the name "practice." In the perfect-sudden teaching, since inherent evil is expressed, the king and factors of views and thought are a poison identical to the nature. Since the poison is identical to the nature, this very poison alone is taken as what dispels and subdues. Since the poison is taken as what dispels, then right in this place it is absolute; who would speak of what breaks and what is broken? What is there that takes as object or is taken as object? The poison and harm are identical to the middle; all dharmas tend toward the poison; negating and illuminating are mutually identical, and words and thought cannot reach its end. Hence Jingxi says: "Not only are ignorance and the dharma-nature that are contemplated nondual in essence and nature; the contemplating wisdom that contemplates is itself ignorance." If it were not the poison of principle, how could it be identical to what contemplates? Therefore the threefold contemplation in a single mind and the perfect-sudden ten modes are not a separate cultivation at all; they are all dispelling and subduing in principle.

Chinese Source

應知三用，得前前者不得後後，得後後者必具前前。且約誦呪為事辨之。如散心誦者，未修禪慧，則唯得事，不名行理。若三教人等，如優波斯那，聞六字已但觀心脈及四大實際，得阿羅漢。此是但修消伏觀行，既不兼口誦，則獨受行名。或有不捨口誦而修三教觀法，此乃誦全成觀。雖兼事用，而須從勝，皆名行消伏也，但不名理耳。若圓教人，如釋迦因地聞此章句即便數息住首楞嚴，則專理用也。若云過去得聞此句受持讀誦，則兼事用。若觀芭蕉幻化以為助道，則兼行用。雖兼事行，既約圓修，無不即性，故須從勝皆名理消伏也。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that, of the three functions, one who obtains an earlier one does not obtain the later ones, while one who obtains a later one necessarily contains the earlier ones. Let us distinguish them by taking the recitation of the spell as the phenomenon. Those who recite with a scattered mind and have not cultivated dhyāna and wisdom obtain only phenomena; it is not called practice or principle. People of the three teachings and so on — like Upasena, who on hearing the six syllables only contemplated the mind's channels and the actual limit of the four elements and attained arhatship — this is cultivating only the contemplative practice of dispelling and subduing; since it does not include oral recitation, it receives the name "practice" alone. Or there are those who do not abandon oral recitation yet cultivate the contemplative methods of the three teachings; then the recitation wholly becomes contemplation. Although it includes the function of phenomena, one must follow the superior, and all of it is called dispelling and subduing in practice; only it is not called principle. People of the perfect teaching — like Śākyamuni in the causal stage, who on hearing these phrases at once counted the breath and abode in the Śūraṅgama [samādhi] — [use] the function of principle exclusively. If it is said that in the past he got to hear these phrases and received, kept, read, and recited them, it includes the function of phenomena. If he contemplated the plantain tree and illusory transformations as aids to the path, it includes the function of practice. Although it includes phenomena and practice, since it is in terms of perfect cultivation, nothing is not identical to the nature; so one must follow the superior, and all of it is called dispelling and subduing in principle.

Chinese Source

又此三用消伏不定，自有以事消惑、自有以行消報，有修事行而能悟理、有修理用但消業報。文中但出所消三種之毒，不釋能消三種之用，蓋有對消互消之意，不可卒備，此令說者準義示之。況不獨此中諸文皆簡，儻迷山家教觀深旨，此疏敷揚誠為不易。今輒解此文，多有疎遺，庶幾達人許為刊正云耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the dispelling and subduing of these three functions is not fixed: some use phenomena to dispel delusion; some use practice to dispel retribution; some cultivate phenomena and practice and are able to awaken to principle; some cultivate the function of principle but dispel only karma and retribution. The text sets out only the three kinds of poison that are dispelled and does not explain the three kinds of function that dispel; there is the meaning of dispelling by correspondence and dispelling mutually, which cannot be fully set out at once, and it leaves the expounder to show it according to the meaning. Moreover, not only here — all the texts are brief, and if one is confused about the deep purport of the teaching and contemplation of our mountain school, it is truly not easy to expound this commentary. Now I have presumed to explain this text with many omissions; I hope that people of understanding will correct it.

Nineteen Questions on the Notes Elucidating the Meaning: Preface

Chinese Source

對《闡義鈔》辨三用一十九問（并序）

Ru-Yi Translation

Nineteen Questions Disputing the Three Functions, in Response to the Notes Elucidating the Meaning (with Preface)

Chinese Source

孤山法師，吾宗之先覺者也。著《闡義鈔》解《請觀音疏》，於中發明消伏三用，義亦詳矣。而於一家教觀大旨，尚復差忒。予切陋之，於是設問一十九章，徵問是否，俾諸學者於茲法義不為異端所惑云。天禧紀元十月一日，四明（知禮）敘。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Dharma Master of Gushan is one who awakened early in our school. He wrote the Notes Elucidating the Meaning to explain the Commentary on [the Sūtra of] Invoking Avalokiteśvara, and in it brought out the three functions of dispelling and subduing; the meaning, too, is detailed. Yet with respect to the great purport of the teaching and contemplation of our school, he still deviates. I privately consider it shallow, and so I have set out nineteen questions, asking whether it is right or not, so that students, in this dharma-meaning, may not be confused by heterodoxy. Prefaced on the first day of the tenth month of the first year of the Tianxi era, by Siming (Zhili).

Questions One to Five: Whose Poison, Which Function

Chinese Source

一問：約事約行二種毒害，為理性本具隨緣發現耶？為理本無因迷始有耶？因迷始有，非今圓義；本具隨緣，能隨之體非性惡耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

First question: Are the two kinds of poison and harm, in terms of phenomena and in terms of practice, originally contained in the principle-nature and manifested by following conditions? Or is principle originally without them, and do they first exist because of delusion? If they first exist because of delusion, that is not the perfect meaning of our school. If they are originally contained and follow conditions, is the essence that can follow [conditions] not inherent evil?

Chinese Source

二問：據何顯文、約何了義，理性毒害非性惡耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second question: On the basis of what explicit text, and in terms of what definitive meaning, is the poison and harm of the principle-nature not inherent evil?

Chinese Source

三問：理性毒害是無明耶？非無明耶？若謂是者，則與約行全同；若謂非者，又非性惡，五住之外別有體耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third question: Is the poison and harm of the principle-nature ignorance, or is it not ignorance? If you say it is, then it is entirely the same as [the poison] in terms of practice. If you say it is not, and it is also not inherent evil, does it have a separate essence outside the five abidings?

Chinese Source

四問：行毒理毒若無別相，何故疏家特分三用耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth question: If the poison of practice and the poison of principle have no distinct marks, why did the commentator specially distinguish three functions?

Chinese Source

五問：鈔云修一心三觀，破五住惑，即約行消伏毒害。至釋理毒。何故復云：今觀諸法唯心、染體悉淨，即神呪治理性之毒。此之二解，能治所治、約行約理、在修在性，異相如何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth question: The Notes say that cultivating the threefold contemplation in a single mind and breaking the delusions of the five abidings is dispelling and subduing poison and harm in terms of practice. When it comes to explaining the poison of principle, why does it again say: "Now one contemplates that all dharmas are mind-only and the essence of defilement is wholly pure; this is the spiritual spell curing the poison of the principle-nature"? In these two explanations — what cures and what is cured, in terms of practice and in terms of principle, in cultivation and in the nature — how do the marks differ?

Questions Six to Nine: Undefiled yet Defiled

Chinese Source

六問：鈔釋無礙無染而染，云法性之與無明遍造諸法名之為染，染故即有事中之毒及約行之毒也。作此解者，無染之染全屬事行毒害明矣，那釋理性毒害復指此耶？理若不具毒害，性惡法門至果永斷耶？三毒化事作意方有耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth question: The Notes, explaining "unobstructed, undefiled yet defiled," say: "The dharma-nature and ignorance pervasively create all dharmas; this is called defilement. Because of defilement there are the poison within phenomena and the poison in terms of practice." By this explanation, it is clear that the defilement of the undefiled belongs wholly to the poison and harm of phenomena and practice. Why then, in explaining the poison and harm of the principle-nature, do you point to this again? If principle does not contain poison and harm, is the Dharma-gate of inherent evil cut off forever at the fruit? Do the three poisons [used] in transforming [beings] exist only by deliberate intention?

Chinese Source

七問：鈔中特陳理毒非性惡者，為顯思議耶？為顯不思議耶？若謂思議，非今所論；若不思議，一體三用那忽永殊？毒害之義唯屬事行，理性本無耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh question: In the Notes, when you specially state that the poison of principle is not inherent evil, is it to reveal the conceivable or the inconceivable? Questions One to Four: The Tripiṭaka Teaching

Chinese Source

一問：三藏初果苦忍真明，何故復云滅非真諦？四皆稱諦，為諦真耶？為諦俗耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

First question: In the Tripiṭaka [teaching], at the first fruit, with forbearance regarding suffering the real becomes clear; why then is it also said that cessation is not the truth of the real? All four are called truths: is "truth" the real, or is "truth" the conventional?

Chinese Source

答：原夫三藏理唯一真，觀有四諦，理因觀顯，故云苦忍真明。理顯觀忘，故云滅非真諦。何者？蓋由滅無之真、離幻之俗，雖則知苦斷集修因證果，皆生滅之事行，非空寂之理體。究論法性，何所迷悟？故大師云：三藏法性自天而然，集不能染、苦不能惱、道不能通、滅不能淨。然而由於苦忍方明於真如，析色盡處乃見於空。雖能析所析俱非是空，而見空者由於析色故也。是知苦忍真明、滅非真諦，義無相違。又上明必下顯，諦俗則諦真，豈前後異耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Now, in the Tripiṭaka [teaching], principle is only the one real, while contemplation has four truths. Principle is revealed through contemplation; hence it says, "with forbearance regarding suffering the real becomes clear." When principle is revealed, contemplation is forgotten; hence it says, "cessation is not the truth of the real." Why? Because, with the real of extinction and nonexistence and the conventional apart from illusion, although one knows suffering, severs origination, cultivates the cause, and realizes the fruit, these are all phenomenal practices of arising and ceasing, not the essence of principle that is empty and quiescent. If one investigates the dharma-nature, what is there in it to be deluded about or awakened to? Hence the Great Master says: "The dharma-nature of the Tripiṭaka is so of itself, by nature: origination cannot defile it, suffering cannot afflict it, the path cannot penetrate it, cessation cannot purify it." Yet it is through forbearance regarding suffering that true suchness becomes clear; at the place where form is analyzed to its end, one sees emptiness. Although neither what analyzes nor what is analyzed is emptiness, the one who sees emptiness does so by analyzing form. From this we know that "with forbearance regarding suffering the real becomes clear" and "cessation is not the truth of the real" do not contradict each other in meaning. Further, what is clear above is revealed below; if the truth is conventional, the truth is [also] real; how could earlier and later differ?

Ru-Yi Translation

Take the case where the link of craving is the head: one investigates from what greedy craving arises, and knows that this greed arises because of feeling, and so on to: formations arise because of ignorance, and ignorance arises because of all the afflictions of the past. Again, investigating in forward order: this craving can give rise to grasping, grasping gives rise to becoming, and becoming gives rise to the birth and death of the twenty-five existences in the future, and so on. As for contemplating and breaking: one contemplates craving as the nature-foundation of mindfulness of the present defiled five aggregates, and so on to contemplating ignorance as the nature-foundation of mindfulness of the past defiled five aggregates. Again, if one contemplates becoming, it is the nature-foundation of mindfulness of the good and not-good five aggregates, and so on to contemplating future birth and death as the nature-foundation of mindfulness of the indeterminate birth and death of retribution. This is called observing in reverse and forward order and breaking the four inversions. When the inversions cease, ignorance ceases, and so on to old age and death ceasing. (The above all briefly draws on the meaning and text of the Four Foundations of Mindfulness.) Those for whom the link of grasping is the head are people of [the delusion of] views; investigating and breaking in reverse and forward order is like the case of the link of craving. Again, Calming and Contemplation, in clarifying the object of dhyāna, lodges [its account] in cultivation to discern [the dhyānas'] manifestation, and takes the link of becoming as the head. Again, the Annotations, in clarifying the contemplation of causes and conditions in the four teachings, all begin from craving.

十二問：別教不談即理，何故能造之心是佛性耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twelfth question: Since the distinct teaching does not speak of identity with principle, why is the mind that creates buddha-nature?

Chinese Source

答：此教真如在迷能生九界，雖不即理，真如豈離於心？如妙樂云：別教法界不出於心，今謂即不即理。譬如一源之水（佛界真如）分為九派（隨九界緣），九派雖異一源（事不即理），派水豈殊源水（心是佛性），是則能造之心終日不即，終日是性也。若爾，何異圓教不即不離義耶？答：圓教性具九界起於修九，全修是性，故云不離。但由迷修，各自謂實，故云不即。別教真中無九，須斷九歸真，故不談即理。九中有真，而依真生九，故能造是佛性。今人見事理不即之說，作霄壤頓異而解者，能造是性，此如何通？真如隨緣，不可與言無也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As Miaole says: "The dharma-realm of the distinct teaching does not go beyond the mind"; now this means [the question of] identical or not identical to principle. It is like the water of one source (the true suchness of the buddha-realm) dividing into nine branches (following the conditions of the nine realms). Only because one is deluded in cultivation, each thinking itself real, it says "not identical." In the distinct teaching there are no nine in the real middle; one must sever the nine and return to the real, hence it does not speak of identity with principle. [Yet] within the nine there is the real, and relying on the real the nine arise; hence what creates is buddha-nature. People today, seeing the statement that phenomena and principle are not identical, understand it as utterly different as heaven and earth; [but then] "what creates is the nature" — how is this to be accounted for? True suchness follows conditions; one cannot say it is absent.

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifteenth question: Our school distinguishes the perfect and the distinct by identity and separation. Since the Avataṃsaka is judged to contain these two teachings, what passages of that sūtra speak of identity and speak of separation?

The perfect person says that the mind contains and creates; then all dharmas are unarisen, and identity lies within it. The distinct person says that the mind does not contain and yet creates; then all dharmas abide by themselves, and separation lies within it. As for the marks of practice of the various stages — sometimes one and many are mutually identical, sometimes they are set out separately in sequence — Jingxi has recorded these fully in his various texts, and I will not repeat them here.

Questions Sixteen to Twenty: The Lotus, the Nirvāṇa, and the Four Ones

Chinese Source

十六問：既將《華嚴》心造一切以立千如妙境，是則彼經已明開顯，何故云開權局此？

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixteenth question: Since the Avataṃsaka's "the mind creates all" is used to establish the wondrous object of the thousand suchnesses, then that sūtra has already clarified the opening and revealing. Why is it said that the opening of the provisional is confined to this [sūtra]?

Chinese Source

答：正由《法華》能開《華嚴》，故得將心造之文立千如之境。如荊溪明《法華》佛慧，須發四味兼帶之大小，豈不以乳味兼權之教全同醍醐一實之詮。又如大師判《華嚴》為枝條，《法華》為根本，須會枝別以入本圓。今引用者，從會入後說也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Precisely because the Lotus can open up the Avataṃsaka, one can take the passage on the mind creating to establish the object of the thousand suchnesses. As Jingxi explains, the Buddha's wisdom in the Lotus must bring out the great and small that the four flavors combine and carry; is this not [to say that] the teaching of the milk flavor, which combines the provisional, becomes wholly the same as the expression of the one real of the ghee [flavor]? Again, as the Great Master judges the Avataṃsaka to be the branches and the Lotus the root, one must merge the distinctness of the branches to enter the perfection of the root. The present citation speaks from after the merging and entering.

Chinese Source

十七問：既云《法華》是醍醐，何故《大經》云：從《摩訶般若》出《涅槃》耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventeenth question: Since it is said that the Lotus is ghee, why does the Great Sūtra say that the Nirvāṇa comes forth from the Mahāprajñā?

Chinese Source

答：所判二經俱是醍醐，蓋有兩番次第熟脫。若云《法華》是醍醐者，即初番熟脫，八千聲聞無量損生菩薩等是也。若云《般若》出《涅槃》者，謂鈍根之人於法華不入，更用《般若》洮汰，方至《涅槃》，即後番熟脫，五千起去人天被移等是也。雖有兩番，初番為正。故荊溪云：《法華》已開，功非彼得；大陣既破，餘黨不難也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Both sūtras so judged are ghee; for there are two rounds of sequence of ripening and liberation. When it is said that the Lotus is ghee, that is the first round of ripening and liberation: the eight thousand śrāvakas, the immeasurable bodhisattvas diminishing birth, and so on. When it is said that the Nirvāṇa comes forth from the Prajñā, it means that people of dull faculties who did not enter at the Lotus were further washed and sifted by the Prajñā and only then reached the Nirvāṇa; that is the later round of ripening and liberation: the five thousand who rose and left, the gods and humans who were moved away, and so on. Although there are two rounds, the first round is the principal one. Hence Jingxi says: "The Lotus has already opened them; the merit is not that sūtra's. Once the great battle line is broken, the remaining bands are not difficult."

Chinese Source

十八問：經云我聞，何故須用四教解釋？

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighteenth question: The sūtra says "I heard"; why must it be explained by the four teachings?

Chinese Source

答：若釋《法華》前經，蓋明部教兼帶等別、機緣大小之殊。若釋《法華》，意在開偏咸令會實。又復應以藏通等四入頓漸等四，揀前四味我聞不同，顯出《法華》我聞亦異，則所聞法體超過八教。故妙樂云：若消諸經，但用藏等，其文稍通。若釋《法華》，無頓等八，舉止失措。問：《法華》既爾，《涅槃》云何？答：妙樂釋我聞云：《大經》顯圓，今乃義開耳。故知二經不無少異。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: In explaining the sūtras before the Lotus, it clarifies the distinctions of collection and teaching, combining and carrying and so on, and the differences of capacities and conditions, great and small. In explaining the Lotus, the intent lies in opening the partial so that all merge into the real. Further, one should use the four — Tripiṭaka, shared, and so on — and enter the four — sudden, gradual, and so on — to sift out how the "I heard" of the earlier four flavors differ, and reveal that the "I heard" of the Lotus is also different, so that the essence of the dharma heard surpasses the eight teachings. Hence Miaole says: "If one explains the various sūtras, using only the Tripiṭaka and the rest, the text more or less goes through. If one explains the Lotus without the eight, sudden and so on, one is at a loss in every move." Question: Since the Lotus is like this, what of the Nirvāṇa? Answer: Miaole, explaining "I heard," says: "The Great Sūtra reveals the perfect; [here] it is now opened by meaning." So we know the two sūtras are not without slight differences.

Chinese Source

十九問：釋〈方便品〉題云：妙達於方，即是真祕。既開顯已，那得云祕？

Ru-Yi Translation

Nineteenth question: Explaining the title of the "Skillful Means" chapter, it says: "Wondrously penetrating the means is the true secret." Since it has already been opened and revealed, how can it be called secret?

Chinese Source

答：觀乎真祕之言，不同隱祕之義。若為實施權，權名隱實，則方便之名正當隱祕。若開權顯實，實外無權，則方便之名即是真祕。蓋指權方之祕即是實相之真，故云真祕。又十界百界即空即中，離言說相、離心緣相，故云真祕也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Looking at the words "true secret," they are not the same as the meaning of "hidden secret." If, for the sake of the real, one dispenses the provisional, the provisional is called hiding the real; then the name "skillful means" is exactly a hidden secret. If one opens the provisional and reveals the real, there is no provisional outside the real; then the name "skillful means" is itself the true secret. For it points out that the secret of the provisional means is itself the truth of the true mark; hence it says "true secret." Further, the ten realms and hundred realms are identical to emptiness and identical to the middle, free of the marks of speech and free of the marks of the mind's objects; hence it says "true secret."

Chinese Source

二十問：常觀三德，能所俱四。試陳行相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Twentieth question: "Constantly contemplating the three virtues, what contemplates and what is contemplated are both four." Try to set out the marks of this practice.

Chinese Source

答：此妙樂觀心四一之文也。文以三德對理教行三，和合三法成假名，人即觀行如來等。而云能所俱四者，蓋顯境觀不二也。何則？且所觀之四者，謂迷中三道即本有三德，眾生妄我即理性如來。能觀之四者，謂見思王數修空假中。如是三觀亦全三德，還用妄我為觀行如來。是則兩重四一皆即障是德、舉妄全真，能觀所觀只一四一。故知境自照境、心不知心，佛之知見於茲悟入。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: This is Miaole's passage on the four ones in contemplating the mind. The text matches the three virtues to the three — principle, teaching, and practice — and [says that] combining the three dharmas forms the provisional name, and the person is the Tathāgata of contemplative practice, and so on. That it says "what contemplates and what is contemplated are both four" is to reveal that object and contemplation are nondual. How so? The four that are contemplated: the three paths within delusion are the originally present three virtues, and the deluded self of sentient beings is the Tathāgata of the principle-nature. The four that contemplate: the king and factors of views and thought cultivate the empty, provisional, and middle. Such a threefold contemplation is also wholly the three virtues, and in turn uses the deluded self as the Tathāgata of contemplative practice. Thus in both sets of four ones, the obstruction is identical to virtue, and raising the deluded is wholly the true; what contemplates and what is contemplated are only one [set of] four ones. So we know that the object illuminates the object itself and the mind does not know the mind; the knowing and seeing of the Buddha is awakened to and entered here.

Questions Twenty-one to Twenty-five: The Carriage, the Land, and Identity in Principle

Chinese Source

二十一問：釋一大車體及具度等既皆從果德，如何用此為十乘觀法耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-first question: Since the explanation of the essence of the one great carriage, together with its complete equipment and so on, is all from the virtues of the fruit, how can this be used for the ten modes of contemplation?

Chinese Source

答：眾生本心具佛果德，雖迷轉三道，必體包十乘。故《輔行》云：理性車體具度資成，白牛觀照在一念心。是知理乘全駕於佛乘，因德豈慚於果德。所謂不動而運、不行而至。善惡不離於車體，故言更無餘乘；果理要在於行門，方名等賜諸子。妙樂云：人天善惡與法界同，故父果車是子理車，但開其情假名等賜。即其義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The original mind of sentient beings contains the virtues of the buddha-fruit; although, deluded, it turns into the three paths, its essence necessarily includes the ten modes. Hence the Fuxing says: "The principle-nature is the essence of the carriage; the complete equipment is the aiding and accomplishing; the white ox, contemplative illumination, lies in the single thought of mind." From this we know that the vehicle of principle is wholly yoked to the buddha-vehicle; how would the virtues of the cause be ashamed before the virtues of the fruit? This is what is called moving without stirring and arriving without going. Good and evil are not apart from the essence of the carriage; hence it says, "there is no other vehicle at all." The principle of the fruit must lie in the gate of practice; only then is it called equally bestowing on all the children. Miaole says: "The good and evil of humans and gods are the same as the dharma-realm; hence the father's carriage of the fruit is the children's carriage of principle; only their feelings are opened, and it is provisionally called equally bestowing." This is its meaning.

Chinese Source

二十二問：大通應佛只在同居，何故名結緣本土？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-second question: The response buddha Mahābhijñā was only in [the land where sages and ordinary beings] dwell together; why is it called the original land of forming connections?

Chinese Source

答：若唯談權教，可云應佛只在同居。今既已說圓乘，即是法身，而依本土。如《法華》中龍女所讚，指釋迦作遮那之身；劫火所燒，以娑婆為寂光之土。大通身土豈不然乎？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: If one spoke only of the provisional teachings, one could say that the response buddha is only in [the land of] dwelling together. Now, since the perfect vehicle has already been taught, it is the Dharma-body, and it relies on the original land. As in the Lotus, the dragon girl's praise points to Śākyamuni as the body of [Vai]rocana, and [the land] burned by the fire at the eon's end takes the sahā [world] as the land of tranquil light. How could Mahābhijñā's body and land not be so?

二十三問：聲聞之法的就何義開為經王？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-third question: On what meaning exactly are the dharmas of the śrāvakas opened into the king of sūtras?

Chinese Source

答：的就偏真之理，開為實相之理。所謂三千三諦統攝自在，故稱王。《釋籤》云：三乘已證權理，以理開理易明。今據此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Exactly on the principle of the one-sided real, opened into the principle of the true mark. That is, the three thousand and the three truths govern and gather in freely; hence it is called king. The Annotations says: "The three vehicles have already realized the provisional principle; to open principle by principle is easy to clarify." The present [answer] is based on this.

Chinese Source

二十四問：博地在事，那云理即？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-fourth question: Ordinary beings are in phenomena; how can it be called identity in principle?

Chinese Source

答：只緣在事，故云理即，蓋指三障之事即三德之理。故妙樂云：理無所存，遍在於事。亦可云：理即簡於後五事即之義，名字乃至究竟豈非解行因果等事耶？如《妙玄》云：聖人得事，凡夫得理。《記釋》曰：聖人得於因果化他感應之事，眾生但得非因非果迷中之理。故知理即簡非事即，乃貶斥之義耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: It is precisely because they are in phenomena that it is called identity in principle; for it points out that the phenomena of the three obstructions are identical to the principle of the three virtues. Hence Miaole says: "Principle has nowhere [of its own] to reside; it is everywhere in phenomena." One can also say: identity in principle is sifted out from the meaning of identity in phenomena of the later five; are [identity] in name up to the ultimate not phenomena of understanding and practice, cause and fruit, and so on? As the Wondrous Profound [Meaning] says: "The sage obtains phenomena; the ordinary person obtains principle." The Notes explain: "The sage obtains the phenomena of cause and fruit, transforming others, and appeal and response; sentient beings obtain only the principle within delusion that is neither cause nor fruit." So we know that "identity in principle" is sifted out as not being identity in phenomena; it is a meaning of disparagement.

Chinese Source

二十五問：性德之行何故須聞性惡？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-fifth question: Why must the practice of inherent virtue hear of inherent evil?

Chinese Source

答：不聞性惡則無性德之行。何哉？眾生本來未曾離惡，若迷性具，須藉別修，故先空次假，緣理斷九。苟或了惡是性，從性立行，不亦性行須聞性惡耶？且如圓人依第六王數為發觀之始，既知王數即是性惡，用此發觀，豈非性行。是則性惡全是智照無減之義，孱然行起真流無作之義明矣。經云：不斷癡愛，起諸明脫。斯之謂歟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: If one does not hear of inherent evil, there is no practice of inherent virtue. Why? Sentient beings have never from the beginning been apart from evil. If they are deluded to inclusion in the nature, they must depend on separate cultivation: first emptiness, then the provisional, taking principle as object and severing the nine. If one understands that evil is the nature and establishes practice from the nature, is it not [clear that] the practice of the nature must hear of inherent evil? Moreover, the perfect person relies on the king and factors of the sixth [consciousness] as the starting point for giving rise to contemplation. Since he knows that the king and factors are inherent evil, giving rise to contemplation with them — is that not the practice of the nature? Thus it is clear that inherent evil is wholly the meaning of wisdom's illumination without diminution, and the meaning of practice arising distinctly as the true flow without fabrication. The sūtra says: "Without severing delusion and craving, one gives rise to the illuminations and liberations." This is what it means.

Questions Twenty-six to Thirty: No Fabrication, Same Essence, and the Permanence of the World

Chinese Source

二十六問：圓教四諦約何義故得稱無作？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-sixth question: On what meaning are the four truths of the perfect teaching called without fabrication?

Chinese Source

答：但約苦集理具，則知道滅空虛。以理不可除故，功無所作。從本迷故，全菩提涅槃為煩惱生死；於今悟故，全煩惱生死為菩提涅槃。四不相傾，一一絕待，皆由四諦元是一理故也。《大經玄義》云：非苦、非苦因、非苦盡、非苦對，而是一實等。豈一實理而有作為取？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Simply in terms of suffering and origination being included in principle, one knows that path and cessation are empty and void. Because principle cannot be removed, the effort has nothing to do. Because one was deluded from the beginning, the whole of bodhi and nirvāṇa became affliction and birth and death; because one now awakens, the whole of affliction and birth and death becomes bodhi and nirvāṇa. The four do not overturn one another; each one is absolute; all this is because the four truths are originally one principle. The Profound Meaning of the Great Sūtra says: "Not suffering, not the cause of suffering, not the ending of suffering, not the counterpart of suffering, yet it is the one real," and so on. How could the principle of the one real have anything made to be taken up?

Chinese Source

二十七問：畢竟空觀纖塵不立，何故空中具一切法？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-seventh question: In the contemplation of ultimate emptiness not a fine speck stands; why does emptiness contain all dharmas?

Chinese Source

答：三諦俱空，方名畢竟，一切諸法悉在其中。且如真諦空有具二乘界法，俗諦空有具菩薩界法，中道雙遮空二邊具佛界法，中道雙照空佛界具九界法，不前不後、非一非三。三諦頓亡，即纖塵息矣；百界圓足，即諸法宛然。是知三千俱空，未始不具；三千俱假，未始不空。此唯一家所談，他莫能及。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Only when all three truths are empty is it called ultimate; all dharmas lie entirely within it. For example: the truth of the real empties existence and contains the dharmas of the realms of the two vehicles; the conventional truth empties emptiness and contains the dharmas of the bodhisattva realm; the middle way, negating both, empties the two extremes and contains the dharmas of the buddha-realm; the middle way, illuminating both, empties the buddha-realm and contains the dharmas of the nine realms; not before and not after, neither one nor three. When the three truths vanish at once, the fine specks come to rest; when the hundred realms are perfectly complete, all dharmas remain fully as they are. From this we know that the three thousand are all empty, yet never fail to be contained; the three thousand are all provisional, yet never fail to be empty. Only our school speaks of this; the others cannot reach it.

Chinese Source

二十八問：同體如何說九界權相？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-eighth question: In [the doctrine of] the same essence, how can the provisional marks of the nine realms be spoken of?

Chinese Source

答：若據同體言之，則百界一念不可分別。今說九界權相者，蓋佛界、九界不分而分，則非權非實而權而實也。何者？雖九權皆實，而相相宛然，淨之與穢參而不雜。且如地獄一界雖具十界，豈以地獄性相便同畜生等性相耶？故知九權是三千少分，不妨非局而局、無差而差；一實是三千全分，故能非遍而遍、差而不差。但以一實不出九權，少分不離全分，故云同體也。若理若果，莫不咸然。如荊溪云：物理本來性具權實。又云：至果契本權實。豈同他宗謂理無九界、果唯一真，同體權相何由可說？

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: Speaking in terms of the same essence, the hundred realms in a single thought cannot be distinguished. That the provisional marks of the nine realms are now spoken of is because the buddha-realm and the nine realms are divided without being divided; then they are neither provisional nor real, yet provisional and real. How so? Although the nine provisional [realms] are all the real, mark after mark remains fully as it is; the pure and the defiled are intermingled yet not mixed. For example, although the single realm of hell contains the ten realms, how could the nature and marks of hell be the same as the nature and marks of animals and so on? So we know that the nine provisional [realms] are a small portion of the three thousand; this does not prevent them from being limited without being limited, differentiated without differentiation. The one real is the whole portion of the three thousand; hence it can be pervasive without being pervasive, differentiated yet not differentiated. Only because the one real does not go beyond the nine provisional, and the small portion is not apart from the whole portion, is it called the same essence. Whether in principle or at the fruit, it is all so. As Jingxi says: "The principle of things originally includes in the nature the provisional and the real." He also says: "On reaching the fruit, one accords with the original provisional and real." How could it be the same as other schools, which say that principle has no nine realms and the fruit is only the one real — then how could the provisional marks of the same essence be spoken of?

Chinese Source

二十九問：一切依正或云眾生業感、或云諸佛變現。是二何者為定耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Twenty-ninth question: Of all dependent and principal [rewards], some say they are the karmic response of sentient beings, some say they are transformed and manifested by the buddhas. Of these two, which is definitive?

Chinese Source

答：二義相須不可偏判。若其依正千差苦樂萬品者，乃眾生業感也。如云苦樂由生，非佛所作。然此業感復是諸佛妙應，隨眾生心而為變現。蓋折伏攝受令成熟得脫。如云苦樂由佛，不關眾生。雖諸文中隨緣別陳，究論二義不可暫缺。良由眾生心地三千與佛心地三千不殊，故得染淨互通感應無忒。眾生迷故，於佛依正而計差別；諸佛悟故，於眾生依正而得自在。是知果中勝用不異三道流轉。又如聖人變化所造，不出眾生三世變造，故云亦令眾生變心所見。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: The two meanings need each other and cannot be judged one-sidedly. That the dependent and principal [rewards] are of a thousand differences, suffering and pleasure of a myriad kinds, is the karmic response of sentient beings; as it says, "suffering and pleasure come from beings and are not made by the Buddha." Yet this karmic response is also the buddhas' wondrous response, transforming and manifesting according to the minds of beings; for by subduing and embracing they cause them to ripen and be liberated. As it says, "suffering and pleasure come from the Buddha and do not concern beings." Although the various texts set them out separately according to the occasion, if one investigates, neither meaning can be lacking even for a moment. It is because the three thousand of the mind-ground of sentient beings and the three thousand of the mind-ground of the buddhas are not different that defiled and pure can communicate with each other, and appeal and response are without error. Because sentient beings are deluded, they reckon differences in the dependent and principal [rewards] of the buddhas; because the buddhas are awakened, they are sovereign in the dependent and principal [rewards] of sentient beings. From this we know that the superior functions in the fruit are not different from the flowing and turning in the three paths. Further, what the transformations of the sages create does not go beyond the transformations and creations of sentient beings in the three times; hence it says, "It also makes beings see according to their transformed minds."

Chinese Source

三十問：人有生死、物有凋變，何故經云世間相常住？

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirtieth question: People have birth and death, and things wither and change; why does the sūtra say that the marks of the world are constantly abiding?

Chinese Source

答：若知常住之體具足三千，則生死凋變皆常住標幟之相也。從本自爾，非今始然。既知生是性生乃至變是性變，性元不動，相豈非常？但局情未忘，則生死相反；儻唯心所見，則凋變皆如。自非性具之談，安會相常之旨？雖藏疏謂之不變、《肇論》謂之不遷，若以今望他，則皆有言無義。荊溪云：永異諸說。梁肅曰：抗折百家。豈虛言哉？良有以也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: If one knows that the essence that constantly abides is complete with the three thousand, then birth and death, withering and change, are all marks that are emblems of constant abiding. It is so from the origin, not just beginning now. Since one knows that birth is the birth of the nature, and so on to change being the change of the nature, and the nature is originally unmoving, how could the marks not be permanent? Only, while confined feeling is not yet forgotten, the marks of birth and death are contrary; if [one sees that] they are only what the mind sees, withering and change are all suchness. If not for the teaching of inclusion in the nature, how could one understand the purport of the permanence of marks? Although [Fa]zang's commentary calls it unchanging and the Zhao Lun calls it not shifting, if one views them from our [school], they all have the words without the meaning. Jingxi says: "Forever different from all [other] teachings." Liang Su said: "It overcame the hundred schools." Are these empty words? There is good reason for them.

The commentary passage (unit 2).
