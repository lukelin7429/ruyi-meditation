Lord Wen Yang's Letters on Remaining in the World, Fazhi's Reply on the Threefold Contemplation and the Pure Land, and the Letters to Chongju

The sixth part is the body of fascicle five. It gathers the letters exchanged from 1017 on, after Zhili and ten monks vowed to burn themselves at the end of a three-year Lotus repentance. It opens with Qian Yi's account of how Emperor Zhenzong, through the Hanlin Academician Yang Yi, asked Zhili to remain in the world and granted him the title Great Master Fazhi. Yang Yi's notes follow, with Zhili's replies. The longest is Zhili's answer to Yang's objection that seeking Sukhāvatī means clinging to delight and aversion. Zhili explains the threefold contemplation in a single mind, the Pure Land as an expedient within the essence, and self-immolation as "the right absorption of fire-light." Yang Yi then asks three questions: whose object the Pure Land is, whether the Brahmā's Net and the Lotus were spoken by Māra, and who receives Sukhāvatī when the eon burns. When Yang answers in Chan language, Zhili does not take it up. Further notes from Yang Yi and the Commandant-Escort Li Zunxu, two postscripts, Qingzhao's letter asking for Pointing to the Essentials, and Zhili's thanks to Zunshi for his preface come next. The part ends with the first eight of Zhili's ten letters to his disciple Chongju.

Great Master Fazhi's Note Thanking Lord Wen Yang

Chinese Source

（知禮）伏念身老林泉，任拘香火，仰明月而遐照我牖、望白雲而高遷帝鄉，徒効葵傾，因慚匏繫。伏惟祕監果行，育德自誠而明，為諸夏之梓材，作九州之木鐸。潤色鴻業，揮彩筆於玉堂；康濟明時，成皇覺於仙室。斯焉駕道，式副具瞻，素仰膺門，長懸丘擣。而知禮也，灰心寄世，砥行先經，切栢代香，忝嗣天台之迺祖；刻蓮為漏，濫承廬阜之遺風。豈期誤泛虛名，遐臻祕府，致諸命服，贈以善言。捧接宸章，感動肌骨，雖懷在笥，猶恐災身。追思梁氏之知然，方茲何逮；退省裴公之優密，類此猶輕。繼都講於山陰，作主人於蓮社。顧微軀受賜，無足稱焉；諒大教益光，有自來矣。造蘭臺而投刺，莫遂衷腸；棲石室以觀心，永期外護。謹奉狀申謝，伏惟台悉。

Ru-Yi Translation

(Zhili) humbly reflects: my body has grown old among the forests and springs, bound to the duties of incense and fire. Looking up at the bright moon that shines from afar into my window, gazing at the white clouds that have risen high to the imperial capital, I can only imitate the sunflower turning [to the sun], and am ashamed to hang idle like a gourd. I humbly consider that you, Director of the Imperial Library, are resolute in conduct and nourish virtue, becoming clear through sincerity; you are the fine timber of all the Xia and the wooden-clappered bell of the nine provinces. Embellishing the great enterprise, you wield a colored brush in the Jade Hall; aiding a bright age, you complete the sovereign's awakening in the immortals' chamber. In this way you harness the path, and so fulfill what all look up to. I have always looked up to the gate of a Li Ying, and long kept up a prayer like Qiu's. As for me, Zhili, with a mind like ashes I lodge in the world, whetting my conduct and putting the scriptures first; cutting cypress in place of incense, I unworthily succeed the forefather of Tiantai; carving a lotus as a water clock, I presume to inherit the surviving style of Mount Lu. How could I have expected that an empty name, drifting by mistake, would reach far to the Imperial Library, bringing about the conferred robe and the gift of good words? Receiving the imperial document in both hands, I am moved to the flesh and bone; though I keep it in its chest, I still fear it may bring calamity on me. Recalling Liang [Su]'s appreciation of [Zhan]ran, how could that compare with this? Reflecting on Lord Pei's generous closeness, beside this it is still light. You carry on [the part of] the chief lecturer at Shanyin and act as host in the Lotus Society. Considering that my insignificant self has received the gift, it is not worth mentioning; but that the great teaching gains more luster surely has its source. To go to the Orchid Terrace and present my card is something my heart's wish cannot fulfill; dwelling in a stone chamber contemplating the mind, I shall always look to you for outer protection. I respectfully submit this note to express my thanks; I humbly hope Your Excellency will take note of it.

億啟：近者竊審，大師期結社於十僧，約入懺之三載，然赴火光之正受，決取淨土於往生。身見頓空，願力彌壯，登蓮華之絕品，聞法樹之至音。茲事匪虛，先佛所證。然於俗念，切貯願心。誠以大師，洞三觀之指歸、為四眾之欽仰，續台巖之慧命、啟越絕之度門。十善誘人，競敦於白業；一音扶教，廣演於金言。慈蔭普均，道風允穆。惟極樂之界，蓋覺皇之示權，而大患之軀，非智人之所樂，儻存忻厭，即起愛憎，既萌取捨之心，乃至能所之見。諒惟通悟，夙究真常，蓋俯就於初機，冀策發於淨行。伏望因承恩詔，彌廣福田，增延住壽之期，恢闡化緣之盛。仍冀懺期之告畢，略迃法步以相從。庶効純陀，恭羞末後之供；願同慶喜，頓釋離塵之心。幸軫瞀蒙，未趨寂滅。切希垂聽，顒俟報音。謹奉咨問。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yi states: Recently I have learned that the Great Master has set a term for forming a society of ten monks, agreeing to enter the repentance for three years and then to go to the right absorption of fire-light, resolved to obtain birth in the Pure Land. The view of the body suddenly empty and the power of the vow ever stronger, you will ascend the highest grade of the lotus and hear the supreme sound of the Dharma-trees. This matter is not empty; the former buddhas have realized it. Yet in my worldly thinking I earnestly store a wish of my own. Truly, the Great Master penetrates the ultimate purport of the threefold contemplation, is revered by the four assemblies, continues the wisdom-life of the Tiantai cliffs, and opens the gate of deliverance in distant Yue. With the ten good [deeds] you lead people on, so that they vie in devoting themselves to white karma; with the one sound you support the teaching, broadly expounding the golden words. The shade of your compassion is universal and even; the wind of your path is truly harmonious. Yet the realm of Sukhāvatī is the provisional [means] shown by the Sovereign of Awakening, and the body that is the great affliction is not what the wise delight in. If one keeps delight and aversion, then love and hatred arise; once the mind of grasping and rejecting sprouts, it extends to the view of subject and object. Surely you, with penetrating awakening, have long investigated the true and permanent; [this practice] is presumably a condescension to beginners, in the hope of spurring them on to pure practice. I humbly hope that, in receipt of the gracious edict, you will further broaden the field of merit, extend the term of your life in the world, and widely expand the flourishing of your conditions for transforming [beings]. I further hope that when the repentance period is announced complete, you will bend your Dharma-steps a little to come to me. I hope to imitate Cunda in respectfully offering the final meal, and wish, like Ānanda, at once to release the mind that leaves the dust. May you kindly pity [us] in our blindness and ignorance and not yet hasten to quiescent extinction. I earnestly hope you will lend an ear; I wait reverently for your reply. Respectfully submitted with inquiries.

Fazhi's Reply to Lord Wen Yang

Chinese Source

（知禮）謹於講懺之隙，依本宗教觀寫書，奉答祕監大檀越。（知禮）伏奉載垂真翰曲賜重言，俾（知禮）畢此懺期，更留講訓。仰承尊旨，合改先心。但以專誠久趣此門，鄙志莫能易轍，況良時難遇，勝事易差，故欲且副夙心，不果恭遵嚴命。所言（知禮）洞三觀之指歸者，稱之太過也。又云極樂本由示權，修道須忘忻厭者，誠哉是言也。而（知禮）今忻淨土，特厭患身，亦有其由，輒伸於後。若其一心三觀，雖非洞達，敢不依凭。（知禮）四十餘年，尋其筌罤、學其去就，神根既鈍，證入無由，而於戶牖關防、行用時節，多所游歷亦略諳知。今者蒙索報音，故難杜口，粗陳梗概，希賜否臧。

Ru-Yi Translation

In an interval in lecturing and repentance, I (Zhili) respectfully write this letter in accordance with the teaching and contemplation of our own school, in reply to the Director of the Imperial Library, great dānapati. I (Zhili) have humbly received your true writing sent again, kindly granting weighty words, [asking] that I (Zhili), after completing this repentance period, further remain to lecture and instruct. Respectfully receiving your honored purport, I ought to change my former mind. But because with single-minded sincerity I have long been heading to this gate, my humble resolve cannot change its track; moreover, a good time is hard to meet, and an excellent matter easily goes amiss. So I wish for the moment to fulfill my long-held mind, and am unable respectfully to follow your stern command. You say that I (Zhili) "penetrate the ultimate purport of the threefold contemplation": this praises me too highly. You also say that Sukhāvatī is originally a provisional [means] shown, and that cultivating the path requires forgetting delight and aversion: how true these words are! Yet that I (Zhili) now delight in the Pure Land and particularly loathe this afflicted body also has its reasons, which I venture to set out below. As for the threefold contemplation in a single mind, though I have not penetrated it, how would I dare not rely on it? For more than forty years I (Zhili) have searched its traps and snares and studied its ways of proceeding; my spiritual faculties being dull, I have had no way to realize and enter, yet with its doors and windows, barriers and defenses, and the seasons of its use in practice, I have had much experience and roughly know them. Now that you ask for a reply, it is hard to keep my mouth shut; I roughly set out the outline, hoping you will judge whether it is good or not.

三觀者，一念即空即假即中也。恢揚肇自於如來，妙悟近推於智者。全由性發，實匪修成，故於一心宛有三用。所謂空者一切皆空，即三觀悉彰破相之用也。假者一切皆假，即三觀悉明立法之功也。中者一切皆中，即三觀悉是絕待之體也。是則終日破相而諸法皆成，終日立法而纖塵必盡，終日絕待而二諦熾然。故般若談空，八十法門得顯；維摩立法，三界見愛皆忘；法華一乘，世間之相常住。皆由三觀相即，致令諸法無遺。故三即非三、一即非一，非次第而入、非並別而觀，不可以有無求、不可以中邊取，故云不並不別、非縱非橫。蓋三一圓融、修性冥泯，豈識心之所測、何言說之能詮，故強示云不可思議之妙觀也。說即非說，無說而說。

Ru-Yi Translation

The threefold contemplation is a single thought being at once empty, at once provisional, and at once the middle. Its broad proclamation began with the Tathāgata; its wondrous awakening is in recent times credited to Zhizhe. It arises entirely from the nature and is really not accomplished by cultivation; therefore in a single mind there are clearly three functions. What is called empty is that all is empty: that is, the threefold contemplation all manifests the function of refuting marks. Provisional is that all is provisional: that is, the threefold contemplation all clarifies the work of establishing dharmas. Middle is that all is the middle: that is, the threefold contemplation is all the absolute essence. Thus all day refuting marks, yet all dharmas are accomplished; all day establishing dharmas, yet every fine mote is surely exhausted; all day absolute, yet the two truths blaze. Therefore the Prajñā speaks of emptiness, and eighty Dharma-gates are revealed; the Vimalakīrti establishes dharmas, and the views and love of the three realms are all forgotten; in the one vehicle of the Lotus, the marks of the world abide permanently. All this is because the three contemplations are identical to one another, so that no dharma is left out. Therefore three is not three, and one is not one; it is not entered in sequence, nor contemplated side by side or separately; it cannot be sought as existence or nonexistence, nor grasped as middle or extreme. Hence it is said: "Neither side by side nor separate, neither vertical nor horizontal." For three and one are perfectly fused, and cultivation and nature merge and vanish: how could the discriminating mind fathom it, or words express it? Therefore it is forcibly indicated as the wondrous contemplation that is inconceivable. Speaking is not speaking; without speaking, one speaks.

能知此已，對一切境以此觀照了之，立一切行以此觀導達之，辦一切事以此觀成就之，設一切教以此觀敷暢之。修此觀者，能所必忘、取捨斯泯，故真如無念、向則心絕，終日說示，不異無言。捨此則必同眾魔，離此則未超諸外。故龍樹云：除諸法實相，餘皆魔事。迦葉云：未聞大涅槃前皆是邪見。大矣哉，一心三觀之妙宗也。故（知禮）所求安養而生，所欲燃燼而死，憑此三觀遣彼百非也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once one can know this, then facing every object one illuminates it with this contemplation; establishing every practice one guides it through with this contemplation; carrying out every task one accomplishes it with this contemplation; setting up every teaching one spreads it with this contemplation. In one who cultivates this contemplation, subject and object are surely forgotten and grasping and rejecting vanish; therefore suchness is without thought, and in turning toward it the mind is cut off; all day one explains and shows, no different from being without words. Abandoning this, one is surely the same as the host of māras; departing from this, one has not yet gone beyond the outsiders. Therefore Nāgārjuna says: "Apart from the true mark of all dharmas, everything else falls into the work of Māra." Kāśyapa says: "Before hearing the great nirvāṇa, all were wrong views." Great indeed is the wondrous tenet of the threefold contemplation in a single mind! Therefore my (Zhili's) seeking birth in [the land of] Peaceful Nourishment and wishing to die by burning to embers relies on this threefold contemplation to dispel the hundred negations.

Ru-Yi Translation

As for saying that the realm of Sukhāvatī is the provisional [means] shown by the Sovereign of Awakening: since sūtras and treatises take the teaching of the Pure Land as an excellent expedient, we can verify that it is the Tathāgata's skillful provisional use. But the name "provisional" is not restricted, and the real principle is also pervasive; one must discriminate them to know what to take and what to leave. The provisional outside the essence must be refuted; the expedient within the essence must be cultivated. Principle apart from phenomena is coarse; the real identical to the provisional is wondrous. Therefore the Lesser Vehicle has no talk of other buddhas, while the great teaching has talk of oceans of lands, because the mind-nature pervades everywhere and the dharma-realm has nothing outside it. Because the principle is ultimate, it makes phenomena vast; because the flower is great, one knows the pond is deep. Lands are like the jewels of Indra's net, appearing layer upon layer without end, which reveals the principle of tranquil light. Bodies are like the food of the gods, the rewards received differing for each, which shows the nature of Vairocana. How could Sukhāvatī not be included in mind-only? Therefore in the tathāgatagarbha nirvāṇa is complete; following conditions it appears, and is known according to capacity. Since it can manifest hells and heavenly palaces, how could it not create defiled lands and pure countries? Though the original nature contains them, they are brought to light by the mind. Amitābha, with compassion without object, took up the land of Sukhāvatī; Śākyamuni, with eloquence delighting in teaching, showed the gate of rebirth. These are all skillful provisional methods, gathering in the kinds [of beings] sunk in the depths, so that those who fear retreat do not retreat, and those not good at contemplation can contemplate the Pure Land. The provisional marks are many; for now I briefly discuss these two.

Moreover, sentient beings have for vast eons dwelt in this sahā [world], greedy for coarse and base forms and sounds and attached to an inferior dependent and principal [reward]; having no thought of aversion and leaving, they only increase the conditions of bondage, and even if they have some perfuming cultivation, few succeed. Second Question: Were the Sūtras Spoken by Māra?

Ru-Yi Translation

These words come from the two sūtras, the Brahmā's Net and the Lotus; but both were spoken by the king of māras and are not the correct teaching.

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: How far apart are the Buddha and Māra? What lack or excess is there between the deviant and the correct? It is because the original principle contains the natures of māra and buddha, nondual yet one, two yet nondual; following conditions they appear, becoming buddha or becoming māra. Since in the nature they are originally fused, how could they differ in cultivation? Therefore the perfect and real teaching speaks in accord with the nature: the suchness of the māra-realm and the suchness of the buddha-realm are one suchness, not two suchnesses. Therefore one can say that outside Māra there is no buddha and outside the Buddha there is no māra; and also that one māra is all māras and one buddha is all buddhas, both called the dharma-realm, because each is one essence pervading everywhere; and all can be called king, because all dharmas pay court to them. If one gives rise to a mind of superior and inferior toward Māra and Buddha, that itself is wrong thought, and not right contemplation at all.

How much more now, when burning the body, arms, and fingers is the principal and the auxiliary practiced together, and giving up body, life, and wealth is wealth and Dharma both bestowed! My former letter said: "Establishing every practice, one guides it through with this contemplation." There was a reason for it.

Third Question: Who Receives Sukhāvatī When the Eon Burns?

Chinese Source

承師有言：火光正受，是苦行法門。當劫火洞然大千俱壞之時，則何人受於極樂？

Ru-Yi Translation

I have received the master's words: "The right absorption of fire-light is the Dharma-gate of ascetic practice." When the fire of the eon blazes and the great thousand [worlds] are all destroyed, then who will receive Sukhāvatī?

Chinese Source

答曰：然身之際，豈定有人受於極樂？劫燒之時，豈定無人受於極樂？故《淨名經》說：雖善惡之業不失，而作受之者本無。《法華》云：劫火所燒時，我此土安穩，天人常充滿，莊嚴所游樂。以苦行人即本來人故，離有無相；本來人即苦行人故，非生滅法。全修在性、全性成修，此時彼時無增無減，實不可思議也。此之正受，體是無上人法，貫徹三際、綿亘十方，成住壞空不能變其質、水火風災未始離其用。以全金作器、離水無波故，豈唯一理該通，抑亦當念總攝。故十方剎海、九世往來，同趣一心，名妙三昧。而以苦行為主者，原治事惡，助開理修，以為最後用心，庶作增上淨業。既正助合運，乃修性一如，不離此心而至極樂。前書云：辦一切事，以此觀成就之，其在此矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: At the moment of burning the body, how is there definitely a person who receives Sukhāvatī? At the time of the eon's burning, how is there definitely no person who receives Sukhāvatī? Therefore the Vimalakīrti Sūtra teaches that though the karma of good and evil is not lost, the one who makes and the one who receives are originally nonexistent. The Lotus says: "When [the world] is consumed by the fire of the eon, this land of mine remains safe and at peace, always filled with gods and humans, adorned for their roaming and joy." Because the ascetic practitioner is the original person, he is free of the marks of existence and nonexistence; because the original person is the ascetic practitioner, it is not a dharma of arising and ceasing. The whole of cultivation is in the nature, and the whole nature becomes cultivation; this time and that time, without increase or decrease, it is truly inconceivable. This right absorption is in essence the unsurpassed person-and-dharma, penetrating the three times and extending through the ten directions; formation, abiding, destruction, and emptiness cannot change its substance, and the calamities of water, fire, and wind have never been apart from its function. Because it is like making a vessel wholly of gold, or there being no waves apart from water, not only does one principle pervade, but also the present thought gathers everything. Therefore the oceans of lands of the ten directions and the comings and goings of the nine times all converge on the one mind; this is called the wondrous samādhi. Yet taking ascetic practice as the chief [practice] is originally to cure phenomenal evil and to help open the cultivation of principle, making it the final application of the mind, in the hope that it may serve as an augmenting pure karma. Since the principal and the auxiliary are carried out together, cultivation and nature are one suchness, and without leaving this mind one reaches Sukhāvatī. My former letter said: "Carrying out every task, one accomplishes it with this contemplation." That is what is meant.

Ru-Yi Translation

Dharma Master Huizhao's Postscript to the Foregoing Letters

Chinese Source

人之死，有重過泰山、有輕如鴻毛，蓋推義所適。比干之於商、伍員之於吳、紀信之於漢，激於愛國救主，故輕死如鴻毛。管仲之就拘、曾子之避寇、子房之藏匿，抱乎智深志遠，故重死如泰山。先師宣尼甞制其法曰：言必信，行必果，硜硜然小人哉。方吾祖誓遺身之際，上蒙天子知賞、次為州將保持，死則損多、生則益大。萬一不遑恤此，潛焚幽谷，則亦匹夫之為諒耳。智者宗系，其能再興乎。建炎二年孟夏十九日，法孫傳教慧照大師（法隣）跋。

Ru-Yi Translation

In a person's death, there is that which is weightier than Mount Tai and that which is lighter than a goose feather; it depends on where righteousness leads. Bi Gan toward the Shang, Wu Yuan toward the Wu, and Ji Xin toward the Han were stirred by love of their country and the wish to save their lords, and so made light of death as of a goose feather. Guan Zhong submitting to captivity, Zengzi avoiding the invaders, and Zifang going into hiding held to deep wisdom and far-reaching aims, and so made death as weighty as Mount Tai. The former teacher Xuanni once set down the rule: "Those whose words must be trustworthy and whose actions must be carried through — stubborn, they are petty men!" When our patriarch vowed to give up his body, above he was recognized and valued by the Son of Heaven and next was protected by the prefect; to die would mean much loss, to live would bring great benefit. Had he heedlessly disregarded this and secretly burned himself in a hidden valley, it would have been the mere faithfulness of a common man. Could the lineage of Zhizhe then have revived? Postscript by the Dharma-grandson, the Teaching-Transmitting Great Master Huizhao (Falin), on the nineteenth day of the first month of summer in the second year of Jianyan [1128].

The Thatched Hut's Remaining Matters of the Teaching Garden on Matters in the Letters

Chinese Source

法智既結社焚身誓生安養。內翰楊公大年聞其風，累申勸請，仍勤致數問。有問淨土是何人境界者，有問《法華》、《梵網》皆魔王所說者，此楊公以理奪事而問之也。法智乃以即理之事而答之，則曰：即此見聞更無道理。又曰：佛之與魔相去幾何？後楊公啟謝所答，問曰：恭聞了義，尤滌昏蒙。蓋龍像之劇談，續雲雷之雄韻。其終幅則曰：有疑必爭，罔關於人我；敵面相示，其達於本源。至曰：惟少室之宗風，本露山之笑視。以至黃蘗痛杖於臨濟，船子揮揖於夾山者，此楊公以禪奪教也。然法智復啟，殊不領略其說，蓋昔禪教互相為謀，至是則不然，則知大宗師手段當自有體。

Ru-Yi Translation

After Fazhi had formed the society and vowed to burn his body and be born in [the land of] Peaceful Nourishment, the Hanlin [Academician] Lord Yang Danian heard of his manner and repeatedly made earnest requests, also diligently sending several questions. There was the question of whose object the Pure Land is, and the question [whether] the Lotus and the Brahmā's Net were both spoken by the king of māras: this was Lord Yang using principle to overturn phenomena in his questions. Fazhi answered with phenomena identical to principle, saying: "Just this seeing and hearing — there is no further principle," and also: "How far apart are the Buddha and Māra?" Later Lord Yang, in his note of thanks for the answers, said: "Respectfully hearing the definitive meaning, my dimness and benightedness are especially cleansed. It is the extreme talk of the dragon-elephants, continuing the mighty rhyme of cloud and thunder." At the end of the sheet he said: "Where there is doubt one will surely contend; this has nothing to do with self and other. Facing one another and showing, one thereby reaches the original source." He went so far as to say: "Only the school's style of Shaoshi is rooted in the smile and glance of Lu Mountain," down to "Huangbo struck Linji painfully with the staff; Chuanzi swung the oar at Jiashan": this was Lord Yang using Chan to overturn the teaching. Yet Fazhi's reply did not take up his talk at all. For formerly Chan and the teaching each schemed [against the other], but at this point it was not so; from this we know that a great master's method naturally has its proper form.

Ru-Yi Translation

Preface to Siming's Letters to Dharma Master Ju

Chinese Source

元豐間，剡溪上方有子通講者，因之三衢，謁長壽水法師。見壁間懸數族，皆四明尊者付門人炬公之書，辭翰平淡、道義優柔，凜然古人風度。通即錄歸，自茲傳播。然四明中興祖道，稟法之徒靡計其數，唯公所得最深，獨擅出藍之美。方其在輪下居席端，眾請貳講，四明聽而駭之，謂吾後之有賴矣。初出世，領三衢本業寺，四明授與手鑪如意，足見印可之篤。後詣京師，真宗知名，召講《四十二章經》，因賜命服。續迴梓里，住持浮石。茲山先係禪剎，長老元勳因聽師講唱，遂請問從真起妄之義。一言相契，迴禮為師，即捨此院，永為教庠。師一開法，學徒雲委，大展黌堂，揄揚聖化。至今三衢台道之行，由師力也。通得此帖，甚珍惜，復跋其所得於後。無何，歲久魚魯成訛，余故芟夷調理，易冠于前，庶幾覽者先知文之自來也。嘉泰二年中秋後一日（宗曉）志。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the Yuanfeng era, a lecturer named Zitong of Shangfang at Shanxi went to Sanqu and called on Dharma Master Shui of Changshou. He saw several scrolls hanging on the wall, all letters from the Venerable of Siming sent to his disciple Lord Ju; the writing was plain and the path and meaning gentle and generous, with the awe-inspiring manner of the ancients. Tong copied them and brought them back, and from then on they circulated. Now, when Siming revived the path of the patriarchs, the followers who received the Dharma were beyond counting, but only this lord obtained it most deeply, alone possessing the excellence of the blue that surpasses the indigo. When he was below the wheel [of the Dharma seat], sitting at the head of the mat, the assembly asked him to serve as assistant lecturer; Siming heard him and was astonished, saying that his successors would have someone to rely on. When he first went out into the world, he led Benye Monastery in Sanqu, and Siming gave him a hand censer and a ruyi, showing how firm his approval was. Later he went to the capital; Zhenzong knew his name, summoned him to lecture on the Sūtra in Forty-two Sections, and so granted him a conferred robe. Afterward he returned to his home village and became abbot of Fushi. This mountain had formerly been a Chan monastery; the elder Yuanxun, hearing the master lecture, then asked about the meaning of falsity arising from the true. With one word they accorded; [Yuanxun] turned to bow to him as teacher, gave up this cloister, and made it forever a school of the teaching. As soon as the master opened the Dharma, students gathered like clouds; he greatly expanded the school hall and spread the holy transformation. That the Tiantai path flourishes in Sanqu to this day is due to the master's strength. Tong, having obtained these notes, treasured them greatly, and added after them a postscript on what he had gained. As the years passed, errors crept in, "fish" copied for "Lu"; so I cut away and set them in order, and moved [this preface] to the front, so that readers may first know where the texts came from. Recorded by (Zongxiao) on the day after mid-autumn in the second year of Jiatai [1202].

崇矩近夏，五迴收得汝書，知受台州黃巖東禪之請。聞若僧若俗、于再于三，既動眾心，獲興吾教，得無喜乎。前次海藏主，將東山信來，亦言及斯事，未知幾時赴彼開講也。汝既受彼請，當聽吾言。凡立身行道，世之大務雖儒釋殊途，安能有異？必須先務立身，次謀行道。謙為德柄，汝當堅執之，使身從此立、道從此行也。吾見學人切於名利者，皆不能鳩徒演教，當視此等如詐親，懼之如虎狼。此先宜誡之，然後進行勤講，豈俟再言也。此外宜覽儒家文集、博究五經雅言，圖於筆削之間，不墮凡鄙之說。吾素乖此學，常所恨焉。汝既少年，不宜守拙，當效圓闍梨之作也。至囑。若見源叔，為我伸誠。佛事所拘，人情多曠。春喧漸熱，更宜減睡、節食為要。餘無可言。不具座主（押）付崇矩闍梨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Chongju: near summer I have received your letters five times, and learn that you have accepted the invitation of Dongchan in Huangyan, Taizhou. I hear that monks and laypeople [invited you] again and again; since you have moved the minds of the multitude and can make our teaching flourish, how could I not rejoice? Earlier, when Repository Head Hai brought news from Dongshan, he also mentioned this matter; I do not know when you will go there to begin lecturing. Since you have accepted their invitation, you should listen to my words. Establishing oneself and practicing the path are the great tasks of the world; though Confucians and Buddhists go by different roads, how could they differ in this? You must first attend to establishing yourself, and then plan to practice the path. Humility is the handle of virtue; you should hold it firmly, so that your self is established through it and your path is practiced through it. I see that students eager for fame and profit are all unable to gather followers and expound the teaching; you should regard such people as false friends and fear them like tigers and wolves. This you should first take as a warning; then advance in diligent lecturing, which needs no repeating. Besides this, you should read the collected works of the Confucians and thoroughly study the elegant words of the Five Classics, aiming in your writing and revising not to fall into commonplace and vulgar talk. I have always been lacking in this learning and constantly regret it. Since you are young, you should not keep to clumsiness; you should imitate the writings of Ācārya Yuan. This I urge most earnestly. If you see Yuanshu, convey my sincere regards. Bound by Buddhist affairs, I neglect many human courtesies. Spring is warm and gradually growing hot; it is especially important to reduce sleep and moderate food. Nothing else to say. I do not write in full. The Seat-Master (signature) sends to Ācārya Chongju.

Chongju: on the fifth day of the sixth month I first saw the postulant Lingyao, who brought your letter of the second month. I learn that officials invited you in person and famous monks yielded their dwelling, that the black-robed and the white turn their hearts to you, and that your supporting conditions are ample: this is brought about by the power of your vow, and also our path will be practiced. Be still more diligent, so that the work expands; only be without attachment in mind, and you will surely succeed in transmitting the Dharma. You must further firmly establish [the cloister] under the abbacy of the ten directions, to be forever a cloister transmitting the teaching and contemplation of Tiantai. Since there is already the imperial edict requested by Drafter Xia, the prefectural government will carry it out very smoothly according to the edict. When the matter is done, wrap up the documents and put them into stone, to be a permanent model forever; only that is complete. I hear that Wenbing is staying with you; be sure to look after him. He is a [true] student and will surely become a vessel. Besides this, be sure to keep your body well and at ease, and naturally your path will flourish. There is nothing else to urge. I do not write in full.

Ācārya Chongju: I have not had news for a long time; how are you of late? Reading Notes for Part 6

Translator's notes

Qian Yi's account (units 0–2). Qian (Xibai) is Qian Yi 錢易 (courtesy name Xibai); the note is dated bingzi of Jingyou [1036]. "Lord Kou" is the chancellor Kou Zhun; Ma (Zhijie) is Ma Zhijie, Commissioner of Military Affairs. The letter quoted in unit 1 for Zunshi is the same as the "enclosed note" in unit 33, in a shorter form; the English of the shared sentences is kept the same. "Begging from a neighbor" (乞隣, unit 5) alludes to Analects 5.24 (borrowing vinegar from a neighbor to give away).

Allusions in the formal notes. Unit 7: Li Ying's gate (膺門); "a prayer like Qiu's" reads 丘擣 as 丘禱 (Analects 7.35), an inference; "carving a lotus as a water clock" is Huiyuan's lotus clepsydra at Mount Lu; Liang [Su] was Zhanran's lay disciple and Lord Pei is Pei Xiu. Unit 31: the letters on the Han River are Xi Zuochi's letter to Dao'an, and Xiangshan is Bai Juyi's society. Unit 27: Master Sheng is Daosheng, who waited for the full Nirvāṇa Sūtra. Unit 50: Sima Qian's "weightier than Mount Tai" and Analects 13.20 ("stubborn, they are petty men") and 14.17 ("the faithfulness of a common man").

Quotations in Zhili's reply (unit 11). "Apart from the true mark of all dharmas…" follows the English already used in Part 4 (Nāgārjuna, Great Treatise). The Awakening of Faith passage is Zhili's shortened wording of T1666; the same shortened form is in the Anle ji (T1958). The Contemplation Sūtra passage follows the site's Contemplation Sūtra ("whose power of mind is weak… a special skillful means"), adjusted from "you" to "one." "Fish roe, mango blossoms…" is from the Great Treatise (T1509). "Soil on a fingernail" is the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T374). The Zhizhe quotation "The mind settled in concentration at the moment of death…" follows the site's Ten Doubts (T1961), adjusted to Zhili's slightly shorter text. "Greed and desire are themselves the path…" is the Zhufa wuxing jing (T650) as cited in Calming and Contemplation (T1911); "I am Greed-Śrī…" follows the wording in round-two guanyin-yishu, with 愚癡 as "Delusion." "Before hearing the great nirvāṇa, all were wrong views" (Kāśyapa) was not located in T374 in this wording; Zongxiao's Lebang wenlei (T1969A), which reprints this letter, has the same wording.

Self-immolation sources (unit 11). The Brahmā's Net line (不燒身臂指) is T1484, minor precept 16. The Śūraṅgama passage on burning one stick of incense on the body is T945, fasc. 6. The wheel-turning king who gouged his body for a thousand lamps is a jātaka (source not located in the vault). Sattva's "body of boils and ulcers" is the Golden Light jātaka.

"Right absorption of fire-light" (火光正受). Yang Yi's phrase (unit 9), taken up by Zhili; 正受 follows the site's Contemplation Sūtra ("right absorption"). 苦行 is "ascetic practice" throughout.

Yang Yi's three questions (units 15–21). The Nirvāṇa line "The worldly truth has the meaning of name without reality…" matches the wording in the Golden Light Profound Meaning (T1783) and The Wondrous Purport (T1751); not checked in T374 itself. The Lotus verse "this land of mine remains safe and at peace…" follows the site's Lotus Sūtra, with Zhili's shortened first line ("When [the world] is consumed by the fire of the eon"). The Vimalakīrti line adapts the site's verse "there is no self, no maker, no one who receives — yet the karma of good and evil is never lost." Vasumitrā and Jayoṣmāyatana follow round-two xing-shan-e-lun.

"The unsurpassed person-and-dharma" (無上人法, unit 21). Read literally. 上人法 is also a vinaya term ("superhuman dharma"); alternatively: "the unsurpassed superhuman dharma."

The Chan exchange (units 23, 52). Yang Yi's note uses Chan stories (Huangbo and Linji, Chuanzi and Jiashan). The Thatched Hut's retelling (unit 52) quotes it with several variants (昏蒙, 劇談, 必爭, 敵面, 其達, 露山, 臨濟, 揮揖); the English follows each text as written, so "Lu Mountain" in unit 52 stands for 靈山 "Vulture Peak" in unit 23.

Titles and seals. 二柱國 (unit 4) is translated "Second Pillar of State"; the usual title is 上柱國, so this may be a copying error. 滭州 (unit 45) is translated Bizhou; possibly an error for 澤州 Zezhou. In unit 41 CBETA writes the self-name as （智禮）, apparently for 知禮; the English gives (Zhili). The title heading of unit 44 reads 資啟 where the table of contents (Part 5) has 賀啟.

Unclear phrases kept literal. Unit 39 "[beside you] Hao of the society" (社顥) — reference not identified. Unit 46 "whetting and paring" (硎削). Unit 48 "The Tang conferred names of four [characters]… the Sui honored [Zhiyi] with two characters" — the reference is to Sui Yangdi's title 智者 for Zhiyi; the rest is uncertain. Unit 56 "Zhuling's four choices" (朱陵之四擇) — source not located. Unit 37 "being spared and kept back, as you once set out" (免留曾布) — alternatively "Zeng Bu" as a name; uncertain.

The letters to Chongju (units 57–75). Zongxiao's preface (1202) explains that Zitong copied them at Changshou; the recipient's name appears as 崇矩 and 崇炬 (unit 73) and the preface also writes 炬公; all are rendered Chongju / Ju. In unit 67 "the sixth month" renders 六明, probably an error for 六月. The "letter of remonstrance" from Renyue (units 63, 65) and the twenty sheets Zhili wrote against it point to the dispute recorded in his Letter Assessing the Slanders (see Part 7's table of contents). "Ācārya Yuan" (unit 61) is probably Zhiyuan of Gushan (an inference).

Titles of works. Notes on the Lotus Sūtra (法華經記, unit 73) is probably Zhanran's Notes on the Textual Commentary; Fusing the Mind (unit 75) is Zhili's Fusing the Mind: An Explanation of the Contemplation Sūtra (Part 2), and "the three classes" are the three classes of the Immeasurable Life Sūtra.
