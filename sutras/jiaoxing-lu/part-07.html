The Last Letters to Disciples, Ciyun's Letters, the Admonitions and Vows, and the Documents of Yanqing Monastery

The seventh part closes fascicle five and opens fascicle six. It first gives the last two of Zhili's ten letters to Chongju, including his final letter, in which illness and approaching death become an occasion to restate the threefold contemplation in a single mind. Short letters follow to other disciples and friends: Yuanbin, the Hermitage Head of Dalei, Benru (Shenzhao), Wenchang of Miaoguo (to whom Zhili sends two fascicles of his sub-commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra), and his nephew Chi, abbot of Yong'an. Several of these survived as autographs, and the Southern Song postscripts copied with them are included. Two letters from Ciyun Zunshi show the two friends arranging the printing of commentaries and the abbacy of Tianzhu as Zunshi faced death. Fascicle six then turns to the documents of Yanqing Monastery: its table of contents; the Admonitions and Vows of 1012 by which Zhili and Yiwen gave the cloister to the abbacy of the ten directions, with severe curses on anyone who seizes it; the commissioner's notice of 1011; Shi Daiwen's record of Baoen Cloister (1009); Zhili's letter of 1025 on the garden land; three government documents of 1144; and the gazetteer entries and family records on Zeng Gongliang and the estate fields his mother gave to the monastery.

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth Letter

Chinese Source

崇矩論師。不見汝面僅十六年，不得汝書亦十年矣。昨聞西游京都，遽蒙宣召講演，復賜命衣，深為吾門之美事也。近知歸在天竺開講，尤副我心。此際書來，解吾思念。切在勤心慈接學流，仍須以香火為緣、以止觀為業。不行唯說，是吾之憂。若非為法須行利益邊事，此外不宜他適。當善調身，餘不細述。不具。

Ru-Yi Translation

Treatise-Master Chongju: I have not seen your face for some sixteen years, nor received a letter from you for ten. Lately I heard that you had gone west to the capital, been summoned to lecture and expound, and also been granted a conferred robe; this is a fine thing for our gate. Recently I learned that you have returned and are lecturing at Tianzhu, which especially fulfills my wish. Your letter came at this time and relieved my longing. Be sure to be diligent in kindly receiving the stream of students, and still take incense and fire as your condition and calming and contemplation as your work. Talking without practicing is my worry. Unless it is for the Dharma and you must do things that benefit [others], you should not go elsewhere. Take good care of your body; I will not write more details. I do not write in full.

Tenth: Final Letter

Chinese Source

吾遺書付浮石崇矩。吾傳教觀四十餘年，既云樂在其中，不覺老之將至。近以綿痼，百骸俱痛，唯冥妙空，達生死相，相亦非相，去來本常。此乃一心三觀之要，吾在為訓物之柄，吾逝為見佛之基。汝早聞思，夙預傳唱，當須勤勵以益諸後，俾祖燈廣曜佛種不斷。若依此者，真吾徒也。捨此以往，復何言哉。臨行書此，不及委悉，深宜體察。不具。

Ru-Yi Translation

My final letter, entrusted to Chongju of Fushi. I have transmitted teaching and contemplation for more than forty years; as I have said, I find joy in it and did not notice old age coming on. Recently, with a lingering illness, all my bones ache; only by merging in the dark with wondrous emptiness do I penetrate the marks of birth and death: the marks are not marks, and going and coming are originally permanent. This is the essential of the threefold contemplation in a single mind; while I live it is the handle for instructing beings, and when I pass it is the foundation for seeing the Buddha. You have long heard and reflected on it and early took part in transmitting and proclaiming it; you must be diligent to benefit those who come after, so that the patriarchs' lamp shines widely and the seed of buddhahood is not cut off. If you rely on this, you are truly my disciple. Apart from this, what more is there to say? On the point of departure I write this, unable to go into details; you should deeply understand it. I do not write in full.

Letters to Yuanbin and to the Hermitage Head of Dalei

Final Letter to Ācārya Bin

Chinese Source

委曲遺願彬闍梨。吾夙以緣法，獲嗣流通，踰四十年。道德無聞，深自為愧。汝亦早以弘教為志，久處座下；及傳唱利人，頗有道譽。我今老病日篤，必不能再與汝等談道，宜以像末隆法為勤，無以名利居懷。自念垂終之次，不能悉書，聊遣此好自珍育。不具。延慶座主（押）付願彬闍梨領。

Ru-Yi Translation

A detailed final [letter] to Ācārya Yuanbin. Through conditions of the Dharma I have long been able to succeed to and spread [the teaching], for more than forty years. Unknown for path and virtue, I am deeply ashamed. You too early set your aim on spreading the teaching and were long below my seat; in transmitting and proclaiming to benefit people you have quite a reputation for the path. Now, as my age and illness grow more serious daily, I surely cannot again discuss the path with you all. You should take making the Dharma flourish in the semblance and final ages as your diligent work, and not harbor fame and profit in your heart. Thinking that I am near the end, I cannot write everything; I merely send this — treasure and nurture yourself well. I do not write in full. The Seat-Master of Yanqing (signature), to Ācārya Yuanbin, to receive.

First Letter

Chinese Source

（知禮）奉白菴長高人。偶違顏色，俄改星霜，徒增言念之勞，且曠緘題之禮。切聆動止，克遂康寧。將屆安居，更希遵理。《指要》印後，曾已附呈，雖筆削之甚凡，且銓量之有據。願於閑暇，略與披尋。今少敏歸寧，謹憑此問訊。不宣。比丘（知禮）奉白大雷菴長（侍者）。

Ru-Yi Translation

I (Zhili) respectfully address the Hermitage Head, a lofty man. Since by chance I parted from your countenance, the stars and frost have suddenly changed; I only add to the labor of thinking of you, and have neglected the courtesy of writing. I earnestly hear that in your activity and rest you have attained health and peace. As the retreat approaches, I further hope you will follow principle. After Pointing to the Essentials was printed, I already sent it to you; though its writing and revision are very ordinary, its weighing [of meanings] has a basis. I hope that in your leisure you will look through it a little. Now that Shaomin is returning home to visit his parents, I respectfully send this greeting through him. I say no more. The bhikṣu (Zhili) respectfully addresses the Hermitage Head of Dalei (to his attendant).

Second Letter

Chinese Source

（知禮）在懺中，忽承賢力生長老奄歸真寂，驚惻久如不能自已。然則道人去住故是尋常，但以久沐慈憐，難平悲愴。雖夙敦戒定之力，更宜多以誦念資之。智者尚令弟子勤禮懺資吾生處，在菴長洞明豈須更說。鄙僧懺法絕於慶吊，情不可遏，故破制作手簡相問，令人送蠟茶二挺、印香一兩，希為供養為幸。延慶苾芻（知禮）謹白。

Ru-Yi Translation

I (Zhili), in the midst of repentance, suddenly received word that the worthy elder Lisheng has suddenly returned to true quiescence; I have been shocked and grieved for a long time and cannot control myself. Yet for a man of the path, going and staying are ordinary; but since I long bathed in his compassion, it is hard to calm my grief. Though you have long been firm in the power of precepts and concentration, you should further support [him] with much recitation and recollection. Zhizhe still had his disciples diligently perform worship and repentance to support the place where he would be born; for you, Hermitage Head, who understand clearly, why need I say more? In this humble monk's method of repentance, congratulations and condolences are cut off, but my feelings cannot be restrained, so I break the rule and write a note by hand to inquire, and have someone send two cakes of wax tea and an ounce of seal incense; I would be fortunate if you would use them as an offering. Respectfully addressed by the bhikṣu (Zhili) of Yanqing.

Letter to Dharma Master Shenzhao

Chinese Source

（予）三術寡修，致名達於朝彥，尋蒙帝澤令被紫衣。有恥無榮，何勞致賀。汝宜深修內行，藏隱名聞，莫墮流俗之僧如於我也。延慶（押）付東掖如法師。

Ru-Yi Translation

I (myself) have cultivated the three arts little, yet my name reached the eminent men of the court, and soon I received imperial favor to be granted a purple robe. It is shame, not glory; why trouble to congratulate me? You should deeply cultivate inner practice and conceal fame and reputation, and not fall into being a monk of the common current like me. Yanqing (signature), to Dharma Master [Ben]ru of Dongye.

Respectfully written by the Dharma-grandson (Huixun), residing at Yanqing, in the first month of summer, dingyou [1177].

Letter to Dharma Master Miaoguo

Chinese Source

妙果講主（文昌）論師。時光急逝，法蠟又增，徒覺力衰，未能果證。日思改報身預勝游，遂我所期，必將剋也。汝修心勤進，為法周旋，二利兼行、三空不爽，必於此世獲妙果矣。吾雖觀力未充，心有所詣，出處說行，敢離智者閫域。常患《十六觀疏》文約理奧，講者學者多淺多近，法既不稱，種乃不強。故率所懷，成乎私鈔。汝既吾黨同吾用心，故旋寫二卷附去。看尋若有所資，宜為眾講說。或有異，可劄取寄來。此外調理身心，俾成願行。餘復何述。老僧（押）達昌公領。

Ru-Yi Translation

Lecture-Master of Miaoguo, Treatise-Master (Wenchang): time passes swiftly and my Dharma-years increase again; I only feel my strength failing, without yet realizing the fruit. Daily I think of changing this reward-body and taking part in the excellent journey; that I fulfill what I hope for will surely be accomplished. You cultivate the mind with diligent progress and work hard for the Dharma; practicing both benefits together and not deviating from the three emptinesses, you will surely obtain the wondrous fruit in this life. Though my power of contemplation is not yet full, my mind has something it has reached; in going forth or staying, in speaking or practicing, how would I dare leave the threshold of Zhizhe? I have always been troubled that the Commentary on the Sixteen Contemplations is terse in wording and profound in principle, and that lecturers and students are mostly shallow and near; since the Dharma is not matched, the seeds are not strong. So following what I had in mind, I have completed a private sub-commentary. Since you are of my party and share my intent, I have just had two fascicles copied and send them. If on reading you find them of help, you should lecture on them for the assembly. If there is any disagreement, you may note it on slips and send them to me. Besides this, regulate body and mind, so as to accomplish your vows and practice. What more is there to say? The old monk (signature), to be received by Lord Chang.

Chinese Source

右法智尊者所賜永嘉妙果始祖昌公法師真蹟。楷公講主拾襲，珍藏積有年矣。（惠詢）夤緣會遇，得獲瞻敬，遐想慈訓，涕泪久之。夫翰墨之寶固有神護，勒石垂耀咸所願焉。淳熙丁酉仲春法孫延慶寺住持比丘（惠詢）謹書。

Ru-Yi Translation

The above is the genuine handwriting granted by the Venerable Fazhi to Dharma Master Lord Chang, first patriarch of Miaoguo in Yongjia. Lecture-Master Kai gathered and stored it, treasuring it for many years. I (Huixun), through a fortunate connection, was able to view it reverently; imagining his compassionate instruction from afar, I wept for a long time. Now the treasures of brush and ink surely have spirits protecting them, and all wish them carved in stone to shine down [the ages]. Respectfully written by the Dharma-grandson, the bhikṣu (Huixun), abbot of Yanqing Monastery, in mid-spring of the dingyou year of Chunxi [1177].

First Letter

Chinese Source

持姪山主昨來訪，及殊乏相延。經夏至秋，諒身安道長。今知蓋泥屋宇，不易指揮。此際丐僧，又須棲託，希好生見納，兼望勸揚。四眾歲歲括煩，深增悚息。既是法屬，罔避嫌疑也。時中宜善將攝，將來大有好事在。不宣。法眷（押）達永安山主（永安即慈溪福嚴禪院舊類也）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Nephew Chi, Mountain Master: when you came to visit recently, I was very remiss in receiving you. Through summer to autumn, I trust you are well and your path grows. Now I learn that you are plastering the buildings, which is not easy to direct. At this time the begging monk again needs lodging; I hope you will receive him kindly and also encourage [donors]. That the four assemblies are burdened with collections year after year makes me deeply apprehensive. Since we are Dharma kin, I do not avoid [the appearance of] impropriety. Take good care of yourself at all times; in the future there will be great good things. I say no more. Your Dharma-relative (signature), to the Mountain Master of Yong'an (Yong'an is the former Fuyan Chan Cloister of Cixi).

吾姪持公院宰。秋來且喜無恙，修進成規。昨咸若棲託名籃，稍垂見待，仍染拙疾，更費看承。見法屬之情，感歲寒之分。今二僧再去收斂，更冀溫存。或檀越登門，亦希勸發。先人後己是大士之用心，此外善保行藏，勤於收拾，裨住持興盛、教觀流通。餘無所囑也。不宣。城隅法眷比丘（押）達永安山主。

Ru-Yi Translation

My nephew Lord Chi, administrator of the cloister: since autumn came, I am glad you are well and keep your rule of cultivation and progress. Lately Xianruo lodged at your famous monastery and was treated rather kindly; and as he fell ill again, you further took the trouble to care for him. I see [in this] the feeling of Dharma kin and feel the loyalty of [pines in] the cold of the year. Now two monks go again to collect [contributions]; I further hope you will treat them warmly. If donors come to your door, I also hope you will encourage them. Putting others before oneself is the use of mind of a great being. Besides this, take good care in your conduct and be diligent in gathering [support], to help the abbacy flourish and teaching and contemplation spread. There is nothing else to urge. I say no more. Your Dharma-relative, the bhikṣu at the city corner (signature), to the Mountain Master of Yong'an.

Chinese Source

右四明法智大師帖，門人（如楷）請以為跋。子不聞越之流人乎，去國既久，見似人者而喜。不亦去國滋久，思人之深乎。於戲！（愚）去大師之世已久，思大師之音已深，今覩筆蹟，宛然平生，不啻似人者。欣慕不足，感泣以書。乾道乙酉歲除門人（道因）再拜謹跋。

Ru-Yi Translation

The above is a letter of Great Master Fazhi of Siming; the disciple (Rukai) asked me to write a postscript. Have you not heard of the exile from Yue? Having been away from his country long, he rejoiced on seeing someone who resembled [his countrymen]. Is it not that the longer one is away from one's country, the deeper one thinks of its people? Alas! I (the foolish one) am long removed from the age of the Great Master, and my longing for the Great Master's voice is deep; now, seeing his handwriting, it is just as in his lifetime — more than [seeing] someone who resembles him. My admiration being insufficient, I write this weeping with emotion. Respectfully written as a postscript, with two bows, by the disciple (Daoyin) on the last day of the yiyou year of Qiandao [1165].

法智與其姪書，前云二子粗著工夫，後期二利之行。於人念念不忘於道，蓋戒誓之緒餘耳，百世之師也。乾道二年四月八日蘿月曇瑩謹書。

Fazhi's letters to his nephew earlier say that the two young men are making some effort, and later hope for the practice of the two benefits. That toward others he never for a moment forgot the path is the overflow of his admonitions and vows; he is a teacher for a hundred generations. Respectfully written by Tanying of Luoyue on the eighth day of the fourth month of the second year of Qiandao [1166].

Chinese Source

四明尊者，道德淵源固未易窺測，而學者仰止高風，雖片言隻字，得之者如獲珙寶。至有甘棠勿敗之比。此帖流落人間，世不多見。而楷公得之，罔敢失墜。因求跋于二三宗匠，用託不朽。噫日新之銘，非即之湯盤則不知。大思之銘，非即之周量則不著。師資心傳之妙，亦可想見於此矣。異世相遇若旦暮，然是亦聖教中一段奇事，神而明之存乎其人，又豈止神物護持而已。述齋薛澄清卿敬跋。紹熙壬子四月五日。

Ru-Yi Translation

The path and virtue of the Venerable of Siming are deep in their source and certainly not easy to fathom; yet students look up to his lofty manner, and those who obtain even a single word or character feel they have gained a precious jade. There is even the comparison to "do not harm the sweet pear tree." This letter, drifting among people, is rarely seen in the world; Lord Kai obtained it and did not dare to let it be lost. So he sought postscripts from two or three masters of the school, to entrust it to immortality. Ah! The inscription "renew daily" would not be known without the bathing basin of Tang; the inscription of great thought would not be manifest without the measure of Zhou. The wonder of the mind-transmission between teacher and disciple can also be imagined from this. Meeting across ages as if between dawn and dusk — this too is a remarkable matter in the holy teaching; making it spiritual and clear lies in the person, and how could it be only that spirits protect the thing? Respectfully written as a postscript by Xue Cheng, [courtesy name] Qingqing, of Shuzhai, on the fifth day of the fourth month of the renzi year of Shaoxi [1192].

Ru-Yi Translation

First Letter

Chinese Source

（遵式）和南四明宗主。吾兄凝寒，道體必康，仍知修懺開講，說行二門、己他二利，一日並運，未之有也。所蒙教乘，一時給借，深感我交之不棄也。亦言外弘量之成我也。涅槃玄義。既許換後一片，今更募僧添滿，二十七貫四百見附去，二貫四百足作來人名，不可移易，望依今附到者書之。又見兄說除二子句當名，移岳公為本院僧甚好。但岳公於此玄義板，只消著句當雕造，不須云募緣。不同彼疏板，須安募緣，便副得吾兄云：二處講院同弘斯典，進發後人之謂也。所定文字，更計所裁必無確定，從長為當耳。倫公恐十九發去也，唯一本出義才之議，亦計伊之情，今特令梵住取。若思望勸，勵伊歸山。不宣。（遵式）和南。

Ru-Yi Translation

(Zunshi) joins his palms and bows to the Lord of the School of Siming. My elder brother, in this freezing cold your body in the path must be healthy; I also learn that you are cultivating repentance and lecturing — the two gates of teaching and practice, the two benefits of self and others, carried out together in one day; there has never been such a thing. For the vehicle of the teaching you favored me with, lent all at once, I am deeply moved that our friendship has not been cast off; it is also your generosity beyond words that accomplishes me. Reading Notes for Part 7

Translator's notes

Chongju's last letters (units 0–3). Letters nine and ten complete the set begun in Part 6. "Treatise-Master" renders 論師, as distinct from "Lecture-Master" (講主). The final letter's "the marks are not marks, and going and coming are originally permanent" restates the threefold contemplation; source not located as a quotation.

Names in the short letters. Yuanbin (願彬) is called "Ācārya Bin" in the heading. "The worthy elder Lisheng" (賢力生長老, unit 10) takes 賢 as an honorific; alternatively the name is Xianlisheng. "Dharma Master [Ben]ru of Dongye" (unit 12) is Benru (Shenzhao). Wenchang (文昌) of Miaoguo is called "Lord Chang" (unit 15), and the pun on miaoguo "wondrous fruit" is kept in the English. The Commentary on the Sixteen Contemplations is Zhiyi's Commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra (round-two `guanjing-shu`); the "private sub-commentary" is presumably The Wondrous Purport (`miaozong-chao`), an inference. "The three arts" (三術, unit 12) is not explained in the text.

Postscripts (units 13, 16, 24–27, 33). The Part 5 table of contents calls the postscripts of units 13 and 16 "Yuetang's"; both are signed by Huixun, so Yuetang appears to be his sobriquet (an inference). Daoyin (the Thatched Hut) signs as 門人 "disciple" in 1165 and 1166, though Zhili had died in 1028; the word is kept and probably means a follower of the lineage. Allusions: the exile from Yue is from Zhuangzi, "Xu Wugui"; "words are the voice of the mind; writing is the picture of the mind" is Yang Xiong's Fayan; "do not harm the sweet pear tree" is Book of Odes, "Gantang"; "renew daily" is the inscription on Tang's bathing basin (Great Learning). "The inscription of great thought… the measure of Zhou" (大思之銘…周量) was not identified.

Zunshi's letters (units 30, 32). The first letter concerns paying for and naming those responsible for woodblocks of the Profound Meaning of the Nirvāṇa and of a commentary; its sums ("twenty-seven strings and four hundred") and persons (Lord Yue, Lord Lun, Yicai, Fanzhu) are translated literally, and the arrangement is not fully clear. In the second letter, Zhenguan and Huipei and Chen Bozhi follow round-two `guoqing-bailu` (no. 17, the Prince of Yongyang's letter). "Your worthy son (Benru)" is Zhili's disciple; Zunshi wants him as abbot of Tianzhu. The Inscription on the Distant Couch (遐榻銘) and Sending-Off of Grief (送哀) are Zunshi's own short pieces; titles translated literally. "The third fascicle of the new notes on the Contemplation Sūtra" is presumably The Wondrous Purport.

Admonitions (unit 62). The opening sentences overlap with Part 1 (units 19 and 21), and the English follows Part 1 for "the crane grove," "the small cloister on the west side of… Qianfu Monastery," "had living quarters but no shrine hall," and "was received in its old [state]" (unit 60). "The three [to whom service is owed]" (在三) are ruler, father, and teacher (Guoyu). "We do not cling to our own bodies or lives, but only cherish the unsurpassed path" follows the site's Lotus Sūtra (chapter 13). One character, 𫻪, could not be read and is marked "[an unclear character]." The sentence on disciples who are "complete in these virtues" is hard to construe; the English keeps the order of the Chinese.

Vows (unit 64). Scriptural references: the Sun Treasury (日藏分 of the Great Collection); the Lotus on halls built after the Buddha's extinction and on reviling the Buddha being lighter than reviling one who upholds the sūtra (chapter 10); the Nirvāṇa Sūtra on kings protecting the Dharma and the past king born in Akṣobhya's land (the story of King Youde and the monk Juede, T374 fasc. 3). "The Inner Court" is Tuṣita's inner court; "the City of Fragrance" is Gandhavatī of the Sadāprarudita story.

The commissioner's notice (unit 67). Part 1 (unit 21) cites it as "the cloister head, the monk Julang Xiantong" (居朗顯通); here the text reads 前兩次院主僧（居明顯通）, "the cloister heads of the two previous terms," so the English gives two names. 十方住持 is "the abbacy of the ten directions" throughout. The dates are consistent: request in 1010, decree in the tenth month of 1010, second request in 1011, notice dated 1011.

Record of Baoen Cloister (unit 73). The closing sentences follow Part 1 (unit 33). The record gives the third year of Zhidao [997] for Zhili's arrival, while the commissioner's notice gives the second year [996]. Jyotiṣka (樹提伽) is the wealthy man of the Jyotiṣka Sūtra. Unit 74 breaks off at "Disciples who obtained the Dharma"; the CBETA text is incomplete here.

The garden letter (units 77–78). "My lay father, Lord Jing" (俗父經公) is translated literally; Zhili's family name was Jin. The postscript is dated 1102 and signed Daoyin, which is early if this is the Thatched Hut Daoyin of the 1160s postscripts.

Government documents of 1144 (units 80–97). Offices follow Hucker's standard translations where possible: 行在 temporary capital, 都省 the Department of State Affairs, 公據 official certificate, 符 tally, 劄子 directive, 祠部 Bureau of Sacrifices. 釣慈/釣旨 are read as 鈞 "esteemed," and 大常 as 太常 (Court of Imperial Sacrifices). Units 88 and 91 differ slightly (居止/安止; 降指揮/候指揮; the hour of the endorsement), and the English follows each.

Zeng Gongliang (units 100–107). Lord Chu is Zeng Hui, his father; Lord Xuanjing of Lu is Zeng Gongliang (999–1078). The estate notice (unit 105) is dated the third year of Tiansheng [1025] yet speaks of the writer's appointment as chief minister, which came much later; the date is translated as written. "The gauze cage" (紗籠) alludes to the Tang story of a future chancellor's poem kept under gauze. "Appearing in the body of an official" echoes the Lotus, Universal Gate chapter.
