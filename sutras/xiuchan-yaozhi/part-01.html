The Essential Purport of Repentance Practice

The Essential Purport of Repentance Practice (Xiuchan yaozhi) is a short treatise by Zhili (960–1028), preserved in fascicle 2 of the Record of the Teaching and Practice of the Venerable of Siming (Siming zunzhe jiaoxing lu, T1937). According to the chronicle in that collection, in 1021 Emperor Zhenzong sent the palace official Yu Yuanqing to Zhili's monastery to have the Lotus repentance performed, and Zhili wrote this for him when Yu asked what the rite was for. Zhili first places the Lotus samādhi among Zhiyi's four kinds of samādhi and explains why practice is set in fixed periods. He then names the faith that must come first — that all dharmas are inherently possessed by the one mind — and lays out the three kinds of repentance, by ritual action, by seizing a mark, and of no-generation, insisting that the third is the master and the rites its conditions. The body of the text follows the ten parts of Zhiyi's Lotus Samādhi Rite, saying for each what the practitioner should hold in mind: offering, inviting, praising and bowing, the repentance of the six faculties with the ten contrary and compliant minds, the four further repentances (with the vow turned toward Amitābha's Pure Land), circumambulation and recitation, and finally seated meditation, with instructions on posture and on contemplating the present single thought.

Title and Occasion

Chinese Source

修懺要旨（因入內殿頭，俞源清奉宣到院修法華三昧三晝夜。欲知懺法旨趣，故述此以示之）

Ru-Yi Translation

The Essential Purport of Repentance Practice (Because Yu Yuanqing, Attendant of the Inner Palace Halls, came to the monastery by imperial proclamation to cultivate the Lotus samādhi for three days and nights, and wished to know the purport of the repentance method, I set this out to show him.)

The Four Samādhis and the Length of Practice

Chinese Source

夫諸大乘經所詮行法，約身儀判不出四種，攝一切行罄無不盡。一曰常坐，即一行三昧。二曰常行，即般舟三昧，並九十日為一期。三曰半行半坐，即方等三昧，七日為一期。又法華三昧，三七日為一期。四曰非行非坐，即請觀音三昧，四十九日為一期。又大悲三昧，三七日為一期，但諸經中有不專行坐及相半者，一切行法並屬此三昧所攝。然限定日數者，蓋令行者剋時破障域，意修真決，取功成理顯也。若欲長修，如法華安樂行，畢世行之。或宜時促，如觀無量壽經，一日至七日。或如普門品，一時禮拜等。然但在用心，不必定日也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The methods of practice expounded in the various Great Vehicle sūtras, classified by bodily deportment, do not go beyond four kinds, which gather in all practices with nothing left over. The first is called constantly sitting: the one-practice samādhi. The second is called constantly walking: the pratyutpanna samādhi; both take ninety days as one period. The third is called half walking and half sitting: the Vaipulya samādhi, with seven days as one period; and also the Lotus samādhi, with three sets of seven days as one period. The fourth is called neither walking nor sitting: the samādhi of Invoking Avalokiteśvara, with forty-nine days as one period; and also the Great Compassion samādhi, with three sets of seven days as one period. But in the various sūtras there are [practices] that are not exclusively walking or sitting, or half of each; all such methods of practice are included in this samādhi. Now the fixing of a set number of days is to make the practitioner, within a limited time, break through obstructions, confine his intent to cultivating the true, and resolutely seek success and the revealing of principle. If one wishes to cultivate for a long time, as with the peaceful practices of the Lotus, one practices them all one's life. Or where the time should be short, as in the Sūtra on the Contemplation of Amitāyus, from one day to seven days. Or as in the "Universal Gate" chapter, bowing even once, and so on. Yet it lies only in the use of the mind; one need not fix the days.

Perfect Faith and the Inconceivable Object

Chinese Source

今所修法華三昧者，若能精至進功，豈不破障顯理。然須預識標心之處、進行之門，所謂圓常正信也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the Lotus samādhi now being cultivated: if one can be thoroughly diligent and advance in effort, how could one not break obstructions and reveal principle? But one must know in advance where to set the mind and the gate by which to advance in practice: namely, perfect, eternal, correct faith.

Chinese Source

云何生信？信一切法唯心本具、全心發生。生無別理，並由本具；具無別具，皆是緣生故。世間相常，緣起理一，事理不二，色心互融，故法法遍周、念念具足。十方三世不離剎那，諸佛眾生皆名法界，當處皆空、全體即假，二邊叵得、中道不存，三諦圓融、一心具足，不一不異、非縱非橫，不可言言，寧容識識？斯是不思議境、入道要門。

Ru-Yi Translation

How does one give rise to faith? One believes that all dharmas are inherently possessed by the mind alone and arise wholly from the mind. Arising has no separate principle; all comes from inherent possession. Possession has no separate possession; all is conditioned arising. "The marks of the world are constant"; conditioned arising is one in principle; phenomena and principle are not two; form and mind mutually fuse; hence dharma after dharma pervades everywhere, and thought after thought is complete. The ten directions and the three times are not apart from a single instant; buddhas and sentient beings are all called the dharma-realm. Right where it is, all is empty; the whole essence is itself provisional; the two extremes cannot be obtained, and the middle way does not remain. The three truths are perfectly fused, complete in the one mind, neither one nor different, neither vertical nor horizontal. It cannot be spoken in words — how could it allow being known by consciousness? This is the inconceivable object, the essential gate for entering the path.

Chinese Source

依此博運慈悲，無緣無念；託此巧安定慧，無作無為。仗茲遍破執情，何情不破；據此反尋塞著，無著不通。道品因其中適，助治附此合行，圓位可登、寂忍不動，不滯相似，速入分真。

Ru-Yi Translation

In dependence on this, one broadly extends loving-kindness and compassion, without object and without thought; relying on this, one skillfully settles concentration and wisdom, without fabrication and without action. Relying on this, one comprehensively breaks attached feelings — what feeling is not broken? On the basis of this, one turns back to search out blockages and attachments — no attachment fails to become passage. The factors of the path are suitably adjusted within it; auxiliary remedies are attached to it and practiced together; the perfect stages can be ascended; still forbearance does not move; not lingering at resemblance, one swiftly enters [identity in] a portion of the truth.

The Three Kinds of Repentance

Chinese Source

故天台智者先令行人親近良師學懺悔處，即不思議理觀所詣之處也。次示懺悔之法乃有三種：一作法懺，謂身口所作一依法度。二取相懺，謂定心運想相起為期。三無生懺，謂了我心自空、罪福無主，觀業實相、見罪本源，法界圓融真如清淨。法雖三種，行在一時，寧可闕於前前，不得虧於後後。無生最要，取相尚寬。蓋妙觀之宗是大乘之主，滅罪如翻大地草木皆枯，顯德如照澄江森羅盡現。以此理觀導於事儀，則一禮一旋罪消塵劫，一燈一水福等虛空。故口說六根懺時，心存三種懺法，如是標心方堪進行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore Zhizhe of Tiantai first has the practitioner draw near a good teacher to learn the place of repentance, which is the place reached by the inconceivable contemplation of principle. Next he shows that the methods of repentance are of three kinds. First, repentance by ritual action: what body and speech do follows the rules of the method throughout. Second, repentance by seizing a mark: with the mind concentrated, one visualizes, taking the arising of a mark as the goal. Third, repentance of no-generation: clearly understanding that one's own mind is itself empty and that offense and merit have no owner, one contemplates the true mark of action and sees the original source of offense — the dharma-realm perfectly fused, suchness pure. Although the methods are three, they are practiced at one time; one may rather lack each earlier one, but must not fall short in each later one. No-generation is most essential; seizing a mark still allows latitude. For the source of wondrous contemplation is the master of the Great Vehicle: extinguishing offenses is like turning over the great earth, so that the grasses and trees all wither; revealing virtue is like shining on a clear river, so that the myriad phenomena all appear. When this contemplation of principle guides the phenomenal rites, then a single bow or a single circling dissolves offenses of kalpas as numerous as dust, and a single lamp or a single [offering of] water brings merit equal to empty space. Hence, while the mouth speaks the repentance of the six faculties, the mind keeps the three methods of repentance. Only when one sets the mind in this way is one fit to advance in practice.

The Ten Parts of the Rite

Chinese Source

《法華三昧儀》云：行者初入道場，當具足十法：一者嚴淨道場、二者清淨身器、三者三業供養、四者奉請三寶、五者讚歎三寶、六者禮佛、七者懺悔、八者行道旋繞、九者誦法華經、十者思惟一實境界。明此十法之中，有但說施為方法、有教運心作念、有教誦文章口自宣說，備詳彼文，此不具載。今但略述用心旨趣而已。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Lotus Samādhi Rite says: "When the practitioner first enters the place of practice, he should fully carry out ten methods: first, adorning and purifying the place of practice; second, purifying the body-vessel; third, offering with the three kinds of action; fourth, respectfully inviting the Three Jewels; fifth, praising the Three Jewels; sixth, bowing to the buddhas; seventh, repentance; eighth, circumambulation and circling; ninth, reciting the Lotus Sūtra; tenth, reflecting on the realm of the one reality." In its explanation of these ten methods, some only describe the procedures of action, some teach how to direct the mind and form thoughts, and some teach texts to be recited and proclaimed aloud oneself; they are detailed in full in that text and not all recorded here. Now I only briefly set out the purport of using the mind.

Parts One to Three: The Place of Practice, the Body, and Offering

Chinese Source

第一嚴淨道場法、第二淨身方法，此並可見（云云）。第三修三業供養法，行者三業供養之際，須起難思之想，離於謂實之心。若香若華，體是法界；能供所供，性本真空。其量遍周，出生無盡；其性常住，亘徹無遺。豈唯遍至此界他方，抑亦普入未來過去。普獻三寶，等熏眾生，雖曰施財，以財通法，是真法供，能資法身。五果皆常，四德咸顯，故默想香華偈（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first, the method of adorning and purifying the place of practice, and the second, the method of purifying the body, can both be seen [there] (and so on). The third, the method of cultivating offering with the three kinds of action: when the practitioner makes offering with the three kinds of action, he must give rise to a thought that is hard to conceive and part from the mind that takes things as real. Whether incense or flowers, their essence is the dharma-realm; the one who offers and what is offered are by nature originally truly empty. Their measure pervades everywhere, issuing forth without end; their nature is eternally abiding, extending through without omission. Not only do they reach everywhere in this world and in other directions; they also enter universally into the future and the past. They are presented universally to the Three Jewels and perfume sentient beings equally. Though it is called giving material things, through material things one reaches the Dharma; this is the true Dharma-offering, which can nourish the Dharma-body. The five fruits are all eternal and the four virtues are all revealed. Hence one silently visualizes the verse of incense and flowers (and so on).

Parts Four to Six: Inviting, Praising, and Bowing to the Three Jewels

Chinese Source

第四請三寶法、第五讚歎三寶法、第六禮三寶法（云云）。且初禮佛時，深知佛體不離我心，同一覺源、圓照諸法。諸佛悟起同體悲，眾生迷強受諸幻，悲苦相對感應斯成。一身遍至諸佛之前，一拜普消無邊之罪，故默想禮佛偈（云云）。次禮法時，深知諸佛所證果德、眾生所迷理心，一切行門無邊教道，離染清淨、能軌聖凡，稱此法門三業致禮，故默想禮法偈（云云）。次禮僧時，即三乘聖賢也。雖是因位，已到真源同佛。無緣之慈同佛，不謀而應。我今三業致感聖眾，四誓所熏滅我罪根、生我樂果，故默想禮僧偈（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

The fourth, the method of inviting the Three Jewels; the fifth, the method of praising the Three Jewels; the sixth, the method of bowing to the Three Jewels (and so on). First, when bowing to the Buddha, one deeply knows that the Buddha's essence is not apart from one's own mind: the same single source of awakening, perfectly illuminating all dharmas. The buddhas, awakened, give rise to compassion of the same essence; sentient beings, deluded, forcibly undergo all illusions. Compassion and suffering face each other, and so appeal and response are accomplished. One body reaches everywhere before all the buddhas; one bow universally dissolves boundless offenses. Hence one silently visualizes the verse of bowing to the Buddha (and so on). Next, when bowing to the Dharma, one deeply knows that the virtues of the fruit realized by the buddhas, the principle-mind about which sentient beings are deluded, all the gates of practice, and the boundless teachings and paths are apart from defilement and pure, and can serve as the norm for sages and ordinary people; according with this Dharma-gate, one pays reverence with the three kinds of action. Hence one silently visualizes the verse of bowing to the Dharma (and so on). Next, when bowing to the Saṅgha: these are the sages and worthies of the three vehicles. Although they are at the causal stage, they have already reached the true source, the same as the Buddha; their loving-kindness without object is the same as the Buddha's, responding without planning. Now with the three kinds of action I appeal to the holy assembly, that what is perfumed by their four vows may extinguish the roots of my offenses and generate in me the fruit of happiness. Hence one silently visualizes the verse of bowing to the Saṅgha (and so on).

Chinese Source

次運普懺之心，用成曠濟之道。若不然者，豈但不成大道，抑亦難滅眾愆。何者？我從無始已來造罪之際，自身為業本、眾生為惡緣，生生於彼愛憎、處處於他婬殺。況一切男子是我父、一切女人是我母，無不業累相關，悉為煩惱所覆。今運同體慈悲如理懺悔，盡妄染際、徹真性源，仰答四恩、旁資三有，有情之類稱性遍該，故默想（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, one exerts the mind of universal repentance, in order to accomplish the path of vast deliverance. If one does not, not only will one fail to accomplish the great path, but it will also be hard to extinguish one's many faults. Why? Since beginningless time, when I created offenses, my own body was the root of action and sentient beings were the evil conditions; life after life I had love and hate toward them, place after place I committed lust and killing against them. Moreover, all men are my fathers and all women are my mothers; there are none not bound up with me by karmic entanglement, all covered over by afflictions. Now, exerting the loving-kindness and compassion of the same essence, I repent in accord with principle, exhausting the limit of false defilement and penetrating the source of the true nature, repaying above the four kindnesses and assisting on every side the three existences, so that all kinds of sentient beings are pervaded and included in accord with the nature. Hence one silently visualizes (and so on).

Part Seven: Repentance of the Six Faculties and the Four Further Repentances

Chinese Source

第七懺悔六根及四悔法，夫六根之罪願悉消除，四悔之心願皆成就。初修懺悔者，所謂發露眾罪也。何故爾耶？如草木之根，露之則枯、覆之則茂，故善根宜覆，則眾善皆生；罪根宜露，則眾罪皆滅。今對三寶真實知見，照我善惡之際、窮我本末之邊，故原始要終、從微至著，悉皆發露更不覆藏。

Ru-Yi Translation

The seventh, the method of repenting the six faculties and of the four [further] repentances: may the offenses of the six faculties all be dissolved, and may the minds of the four repentances all be accomplished. First, cultivating repentance: this is what is called revealing all offenses. Why so? Like the roots of grasses and trees: exposed, they wither; covered, they flourish. So wholesome roots should be covered, and then all goods arise; the roots of offense should be exposed, and then all offenses are extinguished. Now, before the true knowing and seeing of the Three Jewels, which illuminate the bounds of my good and evil and exhaust the limits of my beginning and end, I trace them to their origin and follow them to their end, from the subtle to the manifest, and reveal them all, concealing nothing further.

Chinese Source

所謂逆順十心通於迷悟兩派，故迷真造惡則有十心，逆涅槃流順生死海。始從無始無明起愛起見，終至作一闡提撥因撥果，所以沈淪生死無解脫期。今遇三寶勝緣，能生一念正信，先人後己、改往修來，故起十心，逆生死流、順涅槃道，始則深信因果不亡，終則圓悟心性本寂，一一翻破上之十心。不明前之十心，則不識造罪之相；若非後之十心，則不知修懺之法。故欲行五悔，先運十心，故默想（云云）。想已當說六根罪過。

Ru-Yi Translation

What are called the ten contrary and compliant minds run through the two streams of delusion and awakening. So, deluded about the true and creating evil, there are ten minds, going contrary to the stream of nirvāṇa and complying with the sea of birth-and-death. They begin from beginningless ignorance giving rise to craving and to views, and end in becoming an icchantika who denies cause and denies effect; therefore one sinks in birth-and-death with no time of liberation. Now, meeting the excellent condition of the Three Jewels, one can give rise to a single thought of correct faith, putting others before oneself, changing the past and cultivating the future; so one gives rise to ten minds, going contrary to the stream of birth-and-death and complying with the path of nirvāṇa. They begin with deeply believing that cause and effect do not perish and end with perfectly awakening that the mind-nature is originally still, overturning and breaking one by one the ten minds above. Without understanding the first ten minds, one does not recognize the marks of creating offenses; without the latter ten minds, one does not know the method of cultivating repentance. Therefore, wishing to practice the five repentances, one first exerts the ten minds. Hence one silently visualizes (and so on). Having visualized, one should speak the offenses of the six faculties.

Chinese Source

然此六根懺文非人師所撰，乃聖語親宣，是釋迦本師說，普賢大士為三昧行者示除障法門。蓋由洞見眾生起過之由、造罪之相，又知諸法本來寂滅全體靈明，無相無為無染無礙、互攝互具互發互生，皆真皆如非破非立。迷情昏動觸事狂愚，以菩提涅槃為煩惱生死，是以大士示懺悔法、開解脫門，令了無明即明、知縛無縛，就茲妙理懺此深愆。故懺眼根罪時，即見諸佛常色。次懺耳根罪時，即聞諸佛妙音。乃至懺悔意根，即悟剎那住處三身一體四德宛然。以要言之，一切罪相無非實相，十惡五逆四重八邪，皆理毒之法門、悉性染之本用。以此為能懺、即此為所觀，惑智本如、理事一際，能障所障皆泯、能懺所懺俱忘，終日加功終日無作，是名無罪相懺悔，亦名大莊嚴懺悔，亦名最上第一懺悔。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now this text of the repentance of the six faculties was not composed by a human teacher; it is the sacred word proclaimed in person, spoken by our original teacher Śākyamuni: the dharma-gate of removing obstructions that the great being Samantabhadra shows to practitioners of the samādhi. For he saw clearly the source from which sentient beings' faults arise and the marks of their creating offenses, and further knew that all dharmas are originally still and extinguished, their whole essence spiritually bright, without marks and unconditioned, without defilement and without obstruction, mutually including, mutually containing, mutually bringing forth, mutually generating, all true and all suchness, neither broken nor established. Deluded feeling, dark and agitated, is wild and foolish on meeting any matter, taking bodhi and nirvāṇa to be afflictions and birth-and-death. Therefore the great being shows the method of repentance and opens the gate of liberation, causing them to understand that ignorance is itself clarity and to know that bondage is without bondage, and, on this wondrous principle, to repent these deep faults. Hence when repenting the offenses of the eye faculty, one at once sees the eternal form of the buddhas. Next, when repenting the offenses of the ear faculty, one at once hears the wondrous sound of the buddhas. And so on until, repenting the mind faculty, one at once awakens to the abode of the instant, where the three bodies are one essence and the four virtues are clearly present. To put it essentially, all marks of offense are nothing other than the true mark; the ten evils, the five heinous [crimes], the four grave [offenses], and the eight wrong [paths] are all dharma-gates of the poison of principle, all the original function of defilement in the nature. Taking this as what repents, and taking this very thing as what is contemplated, delusion and wisdom are originally suchness, and principle and phenomena are one boundary; what obstructs and what is obstructed both vanish, what repents and what is repented are both forgotten; all day exerting effort, all day without fabrication. This is called repentance of the mark of no offense; it is also called the greatly adorned repentance, and also called the supreme and foremost repentance.

Chinese Source

以此無生理觀為懺悔主，方用有作事儀為懺悔緣。其事儀者，即五體投地如泰山頹，剋責己心語淚俱下，挫情折意首罪求哀。如此事行既勤、理觀彌進，如洗滌之法，雖淨在清水，若不加之灰皁，垢膩難除。故正助合行，如目足相假，豈獨滅罪，即能證真。故六根懺悔若成，六根清淨可獲。若不以圓觀為主，則不名大乘懺法。縱能滅罪，止免三塗；縱能生善，不出三界。先知此意，然誦其文，俾在兼行取成大益也（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Taking this contemplation of principle of no-generation as the master of repentance, one then uses the fabricated phenomenal rites as the conditions of repentance. As for the phenomenal rites: casting the five limbs to the ground like Mount Tai collapsing, reproaching one's own mind with words and tears falling together, crushing feeling and breaking will, confessing offenses and begging for pity. When such phenomenal practice is diligent, the contemplation of principle advances all the more. It is like the method of washing: though the cleaning is done by clear water, if lye and soap are not added, grime and grease are hard to remove. Hence the main and the auxiliary practiced together are like eyes and feet relying on each other; not only do they extinguish offenses, they can realize the true. Hence if the repentance of the six faculties is accomplished, the purity of the six faculties can be attained. If perfect contemplation is not taken as the master, it is not called a Great Vehicle method of repentance. Even if it can extinguish offenses, it only escapes the three evil paths; even if it can generate good, it does not leave the three realms. First know this intent, then recite the text, so that by practicing both together one achieves great benefit (and so on).

The Four Further Repentances: Requesting and Rejoicing

Chinese Source

次之四悔，所謂勸請、隨喜、迴向、發願也。所以悉稱悔者，蓋皆能滅罪故也。勸請則滅波旬請佛入滅之罪，隨喜則滅嫉他修善之愆，迴向則滅倒求三界之心，發願則滅修行退志之過。初修勸請者，先知現今剎那十方世界，有不可說佛剎微塵數佛，方坐道樹，未轉法輪。我今稱理運想，於一一佛前請說妙法。即此剎那，十方復有如上塵數諸佛，唱入涅槃，我皆於前請久住世。能此運念，不止滅乎魔愆，復能成於法施，其功甚大。切在用心先運此心，方陳其語（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the four [further] repentances: namely, requesting, rejoicing, dedicating, and vowing. The reason all are called repentance is that all can extinguish offenses. Requesting extinguishes the offense of Pāpīyān asking the Buddha to enter extinction; rejoicing extinguishes the fault of being jealous of others' cultivating good; dedicating extinguishes the mind that seeks the three realms in an inverted way; vowing extinguishes the fault of a will that regresses in practice. First, cultivating the request: one first knows that at this present instant, in the worlds of the ten directions, there are buddhas as numerous as the motes of dust of unspeakably many buddha-lands just sitting beneath the tree of the path, not yet having turned the Dharma-wheel. I now, in accord with principle, direct my thought and before each and every buddha request them to teach the wondrous Dharma. In this very instant, in the ten directions there are again buddhas as numerous as the dust motes above announcing their entry into nirvāṇa; before all of them I request them to dwell long in the world. If one can direct one's thought like this, not only does one extinguish the fault of Māra, one can also accomplish the giving of the Dharma; its merit is very great. It is crucial in using the mind first to exert this mind, and only then to state the words (and so on).

Chinese Source

次修隨喜者，則隨他修善喜他得成。謂六道凡夫、二乘賢聖、一切菩薩、三世如來，有為無漏善根、上求下化功德，皆歡喜讚歎、隨順修行。夫善是樂因，今隨喜助成，則與一切眾生安樂之果。當以此意念念行之（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, cultivating rejoicing is to follow others in cultivating good and rejoice in their accomplishing it. That is: the conditioned and uncontaminated wholesome roots, and the merit of seeking above and transforming below, of ordinary beings of the six destinies, the worthies and sages of the two vehicles, all bodhisattvas, and the tathāgatas of the three times — all of these I rejoice in and praise, and follow in cultivating and practicing. Good is the cause of happiness; now, by rejoicing and helping it to completion, one gives all sentient beings the fruit of peace and happiness. One should practice this with this intent in every thought (and so on).

The Four Further Repentances: Dedicating and Vowing

Chinese Source

次修迴向者，所謂迴事向理、迴自向他、迴因向果。初迴事向理者，元由理具方有事用，一切修證不出理性，眾生強執計是有為，今迴此心向於實際。迴自向他者，昔迷理遍，凡所修善莊嚴自身及己眷屬，今順本性迴向眾生。迴因向果者，一毫善種，三業熏修，不趣二乘，寧滯三有？修既順性，則成緣了二因。因必顯真，同歸究竟三德，是名迴因向果。此三種迴向，一切菩薩共修，是故行人依此迴向（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, cultivating dedication: namely, turning phenomena toward principle, turning self toward others, and turning cause toward fruit. First, turning phenomena toward principle: originally, because of what is inherently possessed in principle there is phenomenal function; all cultivation and realization do not go beyond the nature of principle; sentient beings forcibly cling and reckon it as conditioned; now one turns this mind toward the limit of reality. Turning self toward others: formerly, deluded about the pervasiveness of principle, whatever good one cultivated was to adorn oneself and one's own retinue; now, according with the original nature, one dedicates it to sentient beings. Turning cause toward fruit: a seed of good as small as a hair, perfumed and cultivated by the three kinds of action, does not tend toward the two vehicles — how much less would it linger in the three existences? Since cultivation accords with the nature, it becomes the two causes, conditioning and revealing. The cause will certainly reveal the true and return together to the ultimate three virtues; this is called turning cause toward fruit. These three kinds of dedication are cultivated in common by all bodhisattvas; therefore the practitioner dedicates according to these (and so on).

Chinese Source

次修發願者，要誓志行也。一切菩薩通有四誓，謂未度苦者令度、未解集者令解、未安道者令安、未證滅者令證。此則通標其志，已具前門（舊本無此則下二句，今依悅庵所科之本附見于此），今則別要其心專期淨土。蓋此堪忍之界不常值佛、多諸惡緣，深位尚乃有退。若彼安養之土，常得見佛、唯有勝緣，初心即得不退。又彼佛願力普攝有情，若能願求，定得生彼。況過現積集善惡業緣，每至終身咸來責報，臨終惡念增盛，則眾惡成功牽生惡道；臨終善念增盛，則眾善皆成牽生善道。今既求生安養，必須淨業莊嚴，若無願力強牽，焉克臨終正念？故誠心發願決志要期，既欲往生，宜在專切。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, cultivating vowing: this is binding the resolve and practice with an oath. All bodhisattvas in common have four vows: to bring to deliverance those not yet delivered from suffering, to bring to release those not yet released from accumulation, to bring to peace those not yet at peace in the path, and to bring to realization those not yet realizing cessation. This marks their resolve in general and is already contained in the preceding gate (old editions lack these two phrases beginning "This"; now, following the edition with Yue'an's section divisions, they are appended here). Now one specifically binds the mind to aim solely at the Pure Land. For in this world of endurance one does not always meet a buddha and there are many evil conditions; even those of deep stages may regress. But in that Land of Peaceful Nourishment one always gets to see the buddha and there are only excellent conditions; even the initial mind attains non-regression. Further, the power of that buddha's vows universally gathers in sentient beings; if one can vow and seek it, one will certainly be born there. Moreover, the karmic conditions of good and evil accumulated in the past and present all come to demand their repayment at the end of one's life. If at the end of life evil thoughts grow strong, the many evils succeed and drag one to birth in the evil destinies; if at the end of life good thoughts grow strong, the many goods all succeed and drag one to birth in the good destinies. Since one now seeks birth in [the Land of] Peaceful Nourishment, one must adorn it with pure action; without the strong pull of the power of vows, how can one have right mindfulness at the end of life? Hence with sincere mind one makes vows, resolving one's will on a firm aim. Since one wishes to go and be born there, one should be single and earnest.

Parts Eight and Nine: Circumambulation and Recitation

Chinese Source

第八行道法、第九誦經法（云云）。然欲略知觀法之要者，但想繞佛之身、誦經之聲，皆是法界各遍虛空，一切眾生聞此句偈，十方三寶受我繞旋，而無能旋所旋、永絕能誦所誦，旋則步步無跡、誦乃聲聲絕聞，故曰舉足下足無非道場。又云：其說法者無說無示。如此旋誦功莫與京。

Ru-Yi Translation

The eighth, the method of circumambulation; the ninth, the method of reciting the sūtra (and so on). But for one who wishes to know briefly the essentials of the method of contemplation: just visualize that the body circling the buddha and the voice reciting the sūtra are both the dharma-realm, each pervading empty space; that all sentient beings hear these lines and verses, and the Three Jewels of the ten directions receive my circling; and yet there is no one who circles and nothing circled, and forever cut off are the one who recites and what is recited. In circling, step after step leaves no trace; in reciting, voice after voice is beyond hearing. Hence it says: "Lifting the foot and setting it down, nothing is not the place of awakening." It also says: "Whoever teaches the Dharma neither states it nor demonstrates it." The merit of circling and reciting like this has no equal.

Part Ten: Seated Meditation and the Correct Contemplation of the True Mark

Chinese Source

第十坐禪實相正觀法。上諸觀想雖皆稱理而帶事修，蓋欲行人涉事之時，體事即理心無倚著，功不唐捐，故如前施設也。今之禪法乃是正修，純用理觀。今先明坐相，方示觀門。且坐相者，當於別室身就繩床結跏趺坐，以左脚置右脚上，名為半跏；更以右脚置左脚上，牽來就身令齊兩髀，名為全跏。此坐為最，易發禪那。若不能全踟，半跏亦得。次整衣服，不得太寬，寬則袒露；不得過急，急則氣壅。次當正身令不萎不倚，項背相對，其頭不低不昂令平正自然，勿以力制。合眼令斷外光，合口令斷外風。次令氣息調勻，心離昏散。故《止觀》云：調身則不緩不急，調息則不澁不滑，調心則不浮不沈。三事若調，禪定可發也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The tenth, the method of the correct contemplation of the true mark in seated meditation. Although all the contemplations and visualizations above accord with principle, they are cultivated together with phenomena; for it is intended that when the practitioner engages with phenomena, he embodies phenomena as identical with principle, his mind without leaning or attachment, so that the effort is not wasted; hence they were arranged as above. The present method of dhyāna is the correct cultivation, using purely the contemplation of principle. Now I first make clear the posture of sitting, and then show the gate of contemplation. As for the posture of sitting: in a separate room, take your body to a rope-seat and sit cross-legged. Placing the left foot on the right foot is called the half lotus; further placing the right foot on the left foot, drawing them in close to the body so that they are even with both thighs, is called the full lotus. This sitting is best and easily brings forth dhyāna. If one cannot do the full lotus, the half lotus is also acceptable. Next, arrange the clothing: it must not be too loose, for if loose it exposes the body; it must not be too tight, for if tight the breath is blocked. Next, straighten the body so that it neither slumps nor leans, the neck and back aligned, the head neither lowered nor raised, level, upright, and natural; do not hold it by force. Close the eyes to shut off outer light; close the mouth to shut off outer wind. Next, make the breath even and the mind free of dullness and scattering. Hence Calming and Contemplation says: "Regulating the body, it is neither too lax nor too tense; regulating the breath, it is neither too rough nor too smooth; regulating the mind, it is neither floating nor sinking. If the three things are regulated, dhyāna concentration can arise."

Chinese Source

次示觀門者，所謂捨外就內，簡色取心，不假別求他法為境。唯觀當念，現今剎那最促最微，且近且要，何必棄茲妄念別想真如？當觀一念識心德量無邊、體性常住，十方諸佛一切眾生、過現未來虛空剎土，遍攝無外、咸趣其中，如帝網之一珠、似大海之一浪，浪無別體全水所成，水既無邊浪亦無際。一珠雖小影遍眾珠，眾珠之影皆入一珠，眾珠非多、一珠非少。現前一念亦復如是，性徹三世、體遍十方，該攝不遺、出生無盡，九界實造、佛地權施，不離即今剎那、能窮過未作用。然須知性具一切，是故能攝能生，勿謂本覺孤然隨妄緣而方有。不明性具者，法成有作。觀匪無緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, showing the gate of contemplation: namely, leaving the outer and going to the inner, sifting out form and taking up mind, not relying on seeking some other dharma separately as the object. One contemplates only the present thought, this present instant, most fleeting and most subtle, both near and essential; why abandon this deluded thought and think separately of suchness? One should contemplate that a single thought of the conscious mind is boundless in its virtue and measure and eternally abiding in its essence and nature; all the buddhas of the ten directions and all sentient beings, past, present, and future, empty space and lands, are universally included with nothing outside, and all converge within it, like a single jewel of Indra's net, like a single wave on the great ocean. The wave has no separate essence; it is formed wholly of water; since the water is boundless, the wave too is without limit. Though one jewel is small, its reflection pervades all the jewels, and the reflections of all the jewels enter the one jewel; the many jewels are not many, and the one jewel is not few. The present single thought is also like this: its nature penetrates the three times and its essence pervades the ten directions, including all without omission, issuing forth without end; the nine realms are really created, the buddha-ground is provisionally bestowed; not apart from this very instant, it can exhaust the functioning of past and future. Yet one must know that the nature contains all things, and therefore it can include and can generate; do not say that original awakening stands alone and only comes to have [things] by following deluded conditions. If one does not understand inherent possession by nature, the dharma becomes fabricated, and the contemplation is not without object.

Chinese Source

今觀諸法即一心、一心即諸法，非一心生諸法、非一心含諸法，非前非後、無所無能。雖論諸法，性相本空；雖即一心，聖凡宛爾。即破即立、不有不無，境觀雙忘、待對斯絕，非言能議，非心可思？故強示云不可思議微妙觀也。此觀非滅罪之邊際、能顯理之淵源，是首楞嚴禪、是法華三昧，亦稱王三昧，統攝一切三昧故；亦號總持之主，出生一切總持故，功德甚深稱歎莫及。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now one contemplates that all dharmas are identical with the one mind and the one mind is identical with all dharmas; it is not that the one mind generates all dharmas, nor that the one mind contains all dharmas; neither before nor after, without object and without subject. Though discussing all dharmas, their nature and characteristics are originally empty; though identical with the one mind, sages and ordinary beings are clearly distinct. At once broken and at once established, neither existent nor nonexistent, object and contemplation both forgotten, dependence and opposition thereby cut off — it is not what words can discuss, not what the mind can think. Hence it is provisionally indicated by calling it the inconceivable, subtle and wondrous contemplation. This contemplation is not [merely] the boundary of extinguishing offenses; it is the deep source that can reveal principle. It is the Śūraṅgama dhyāna, it is the Lotus samādhi; it is also called the king samādhi, because it comprehends all samādhis; it is also called the master of dhāraṇīs, because it brings forth all dhāraṇīs. Its merit is very deep; praise cannot reach it.

Closing

Chinese Source

上來所述事儀理觀多有漏略，備急披詳不煩援引，若欲廣知，應尋《摩訶止觀》。當知《止觀》一部即法華三昧之筌蹄，一乘十觀即法華三昧之正體，圓頓大乘究竟於此。

Ru-Yi Translation

The phenomenal rites and contemplation of principle described above have many omissions; they are prepared for urgent use and laid out plainly, without troubling to cite sources. If one wishes to know them broadly, one should seek out the Mohe Zhiguan. One should know that the whole of Calming and Contemplation is the fish-trap and snare of the Lotus samādhi, and the ten contemplations of the one vehicle are the correct essence of the Lotus samādhi; the perfect-and-sudden Great Vehicle culminates here.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Occasion (unit 0). The chronicle in T1937 (under the fifth year of Tianxi, xinyou, 1021, when Zhili was sixty-two) says that Emperor Zhenzong sent a palace official (中貴), Yu Yuanqing, to have the Lotus repentance performed, and that Zhili wrote this text because Yu wished to know its purport. 入內殿頭 is a eunuch office title, rendered "Attendant of the Inner Palace Halls."

The four samādhis (unit 1). "Constantly sitting / constantly walking / half walking and half sitting / neither walking nor sitting" and "the one-practice samādhi" follow the site's Mohe Zhiguan fascicle 2; "Invoking Avalokiteśvara" follows the site's title Sūtra of Invoking Avalokiteśvara.

Fixed periods (unit 1). CBETA punctuates 剋時破障域，意修真決，取功成理顯也. The English follows the reading 剋時破障，域意修真，決取功成理顯 ("break through obstructions within a limited time, confine his intent to cultivating the true, and resolutely seek…"). Alternatively, following CBETA: "break the domain of obstructions within a limited time, intend to cultivate the true and decisive, and seek success and the revealing of principle."

Lotus and Universal Gate (unit 1). The peaceful practices are those of the Lotus's "Four Peaceful Practices" chapter. "Bowing even once" (一時禮拜) is from the "Universal Gate" chapter's praise of one who bows to Avalokiteśvara and makes offerings even a single time. "The marks of the world are constant" in the next piece is the Lotus verse 是法住法位，世間相常住 (site Lotus Sūtra, "The Buddha's Hesitation and the Turning of the Wheel").

Ten modes in brief (unit 1, "In dependence on this…"). The sentences after the inconceivable object run through the remaining nine of the ten modes of contemplation (arousing compassion, settling, breaking, passage and blockage, factors of the path, auxiliary remedies, stages, forbearance, freedom from Dharma-attachment), in the wording of Zhili's own prose; the English does not label them.

Three kinds of repentance. Zhiyi's Shi chan boluomi (T1916) gives them as 作法懺悔／觀相懺悔／觀無生懺悔, tied to precepts, concentration, and wisdom; Zhili writes 取相懺 for the second. The washing simile (water alone will not remove grime without lye and soap) is also from that passage of T1916.

The ten parts (十科). Quoted from Zhiyi's Lotus Samādhi Rite (法華三昧懺儀, T1941), which has 淨身 and 行道旋遶 where Zhili writes 清淨身器 and 行道旋繞. The ten names are kept the same throughout this text: adorning and purifying the place of practice; purifying the body-vessel; offering with the three kinds of action; respectfully inviting the Three Jewels; praising the Three Jewels; bowing to the buddhas; repentance; circumambulation and circling; reciting the Lotus Sūtra; reflecting on the realm of the one reality. The site's Mohe Zhiguan fascicle 2 gives a different list of ten for the same samādhi.

"(And so on)" (云云). Zhili marks with 云云 the places where the practitioner recites a verse or text given in the rite itself; the verses are not reproduced here.

Five fruits and four virtues (offering). The five fruits are presumably the Abhidharma's five kinds of effect (ripening, outflowing, human effort, dominant, and separation); the four virtues are eternity, bliss, self, and purity. Source of the pairing not located.

Ten contrary and compliant minds. These are the ten minds of Mohe Zhiguan fascicle 4: ten that follow birth-and-death (from ignorance down to denying cause and effect as an icchantika) and ten that reverse them (from deep faith in cause and effect onward). "Contrary / compliant" follows the glossary's 逆修／順修.

Repentance of the six faculties. The text is that of the Sūtra of the Contemplation of Samantabhadra (T277), in which Samantabhadra teaches the six-faculty repentance; the names "great repentance, adorned repentance, repentance of the mark of no offense" are from T277 (名大懺悔、名莊嚴懺悔、名無罪相懺悔). "Supreme and foremost repentance" was not located there.

Vimalakīrti quotations (Parts Eight and Nine). "Lifting the foot and setting it down, nothing is not the place of awakening" adapts the Vimalakīrti, "The Bodhisattvas Decline" ("wherever he sets his foot down or lifts it up … all of it comes from the place of awakening"); "Whoever teaches the Dharma neither states it nor demonstrates it" is the site's wording in "The Disciples Decline, Part One."

Posture and regulation. "Full lotus" renders 全跏; the text has 全踟 once, a copying error. The quotation from Calming and Contemplation is close to Mohe Zhiguan fascicle 4 (調身令不寬不急，調息令不澁不滑，調心令不沈不浮); the site renders that passage "neither too lax nor too tense … neither too rough nor too smooth … neither too sunken nor too agitated." Zhili's wording differs (不緩不急, 不浮不沈), so "floating nor sinking" is kept literal.

Doubtful readings near the end. 非言能議，非心可思？ ends with a question mark in CBETA but reads as a statement; translated as a statement. 此觀非滅罪之邊際 is translated with "[merely]"; alternatively, "this contemplation is not at the edge of extinguishing offenses [but at its center]."

Land of Peaceful Nourishment. 安養 follows the site's Amitābha Sūtra; 堪忍之界 is "this world of endurance" (Sahā).
