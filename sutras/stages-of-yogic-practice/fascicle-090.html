Fascicle 90 · 瑜伽師地論卷第九十 · The Kinds of Karma

The Kinds of Karma, Catalogued in Full

Fascicle 90 continues directly from fascicle 89, still within the sense-bases section's (處擇攝) first particular verse, establishing (安立攝). Having closed karmic defilement (業雜染) at the end of fascicle 89, this fascicle opens establishing's eighth sub-topic's second matter: the kinds of karma (業種類), unfolding through its own summary verse — five pairs, ten triads, and, last, a fourfold scheme — covering the full range of ways karma is classified: by weight, by whether it increases, by deliberateness, by certainty of ripening, by ripening's timing; by its wholesome, painful, or temporal nature, by the practitioner's stage, by what cuts it off, and by the crooked, filthy, turbid, pure, or silent conduct that produces it; and finally the fourfold scheme of black, white, mixed, and the uncontaminated karma that exhausts all karma entirely.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress Source: Taishō T30, no. 1579, fascicle 90 Commentary guide: Master Cháng Bǎi's lecture notes

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Fascicle 90 continues fascicle 89's own territory, still within the sense-bases section's (處擇攝) first particular verse, establishing (安立攝) — itself unfolding through eight sub-topics: establishing feelings, their distinctions, the foolish, the not-foolish, teaching, liberation, affliction, and karma. This fascicle opens the second matter under the eighth and final sub-topic, karma: the kinds of karma (業種類), following directly on from karmic defilement (業雜染), closed at the end of fascicle 89.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Chinese Source

五二與十三　四業為最後

Ru-Yi Translation

Five pairs, and ten triads; a fourfold scheme comes last.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The kinds of karma (業種類), the second matter of broadly explaining karma (廣說業), opens with this summary verse: five kinds of twofold karma, ten kinds of threefold karma, and, finally, one kind of fourfold karma — explained in full below, first as a standing list, then one by one.

3. Section: The Full List Standing — Five Pairs, Ten Triads, and the Fourfold Scheme Named

There are two kinds of karma: first, heavy karma; second, light karma. Further, there are two kinds of karma: first, increasing karma; second, non-increasing karma. Further, there are two kinds of karma: first, karma created with deliberate intention; second, karma created without deliberate intention. Further, there are two kinds of karma: first, karma with a determinate ripening; second, karma with an indeterminate ripening. Further, there are two kinds of karma: first, karma whose ripening has already ripened; second, karma whose ripening has not yet ripened. There are three kinds of karma: wholesome karma, unwholesome karma, and indeterminate karma. Further, there are three kinds of karma: karma according with pleasant feeling, karma according with painful feeling, and karma according with feeling that is neither painful nor pleasant. Further, there are three kinds of karma: karma received in the present life, karma received in the next birth, and karma received later still. Further, there are three kinds of karma: the learner's karma, the unlearner's karma, and karma of neither the learner nor the unlearner. Further, there are three kinds of karma: karma cut off by seeing, karma cut off by cultivation, and karma not to be cut off. Further, there are three kinds of karma: the three crooked karmas — that is, bodily crookedness and the rest. Further, there are three kinds of karma: the three filthy karmas — that is, bodily filth and the rest. Further, there are three kinds of karma: the three turbid karmas — that is, bodily turbidity and the rest. Further, there are three kinds of karma: the three pure karmas — that is, bodily purity and the rest. Further, there are three kinds of karma: the three silent karmas — that is, bodily silence and the rest. There are four kinds of karma: first, karma black with black ripening; second, karma white with white ripening; third, karma black-and-white with black-and-white ripening; fourth, karma neither black nor white, with no ripening, able to exhaust every karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Twenty-one kinds of karma in all, standing here as a complete list before each is explained individually. The five pairs turn on: severity, growth, intention, certainty of result, and ripening's timing. The ten triads turn on: moral quality, the feeling it accords with, when its fruit is received, the practitioner's stage, what cuts it off, and five further triads (crooked, filthy, turbid, pure, silent) each spanning body, speech, and mind. The fourfold scheme closes with karma black in cause and effect (demerit), white in cause and effect (the unmoving karma of the form and formless realms' concentrations), a black-and-white mixture (the desire realm's ordinary merit), and, last, the noble ones' uncontaminated karma — neither black nor white, bearing no further ripening at all, and alone able to exhaust every kind of karma that precedes it. For further study, the notes point to fascicles 8-9 of the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection and fascicles 59-60 of its Decisions.

4. Section: Heavy and Light Karma Begins — the Three Conditions for Heaviness Named

One should know that here, by three causes and conditions, karma becomes heavy: first, by intention; second, by application; third, by the field.

Chinese Source

當知此中，由三因緣令業成重。一、由意樂故；二、由加行故；三、由田故。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, by three causes and conditions, karma becomes heavy: first, by intention; second, by application; third, by the field.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Explaining one by one (隨釋), the exposition of the twenty-one kinds begins with the first pair, heavy and light karma, itself distinguished by three factors: the strength of the underlying intention, the intensity of the actual deed, and the significance of its object or "field."

5. Section: Intention's Role in Making Karma Heavy

By intention: that is, karma created through fierce entanglement and the rest — seeing a fellow practitioner act likewise and feeling joy, and, according with that same dharma, engaging in extensive speculation and deliberation about it. Such as this is named making karma heavy through intention.

Chinese Source

由意樂者，謂由猛利纏等所作，於同法者見已歡喜，於彼隨法多隨尋思、多隨伺察。如是名為由意樂故，令業成重。

Ru-Yi Translation

By intention: that is, karma created through fierce entanglement and the rest — seeing a fellow practitioner act likewise and feeling joy, and, according with that same dharma, engaging in extensive speculation and deliberation about it. Such as this is named making karma heavy through intention.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

"Entanglement and the rest" includes wholesome mental factors too — heavy karma is not only unwholesome; a fiercely afflicted or fiercely wholesome motivation, reinforced by rejoicing at seeing another do likewise and by extensive premeditation, is what makes karma, whether good or bad, weigh heavily.
