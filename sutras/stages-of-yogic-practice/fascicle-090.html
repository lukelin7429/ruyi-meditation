Fascicle 90 · 瑜伽師地論卷第九十 · The Kinds of Karma

The Kinds of Karma, Catalogued in Full

Fascicle 90 continues directly from fascicle 89, still within the sense-bases section's (處擇攝) first particular verse, establishing (安立攝). Having closed karmic defilement (業雜染) at the end of fascicle 89, this fascicle opens establishing's eighth sub-topic's second matter: the kinds of karma (業種類), unfolding through its own summary verse — five pairs, ten triads, and, last, a fourfold scheme — covering the full range of ways karma is classified: by weight, by whether it increases, by deliberateness, by certainty of ripening, by ripening's timing; by its wholesome, painful, or temporal nature, by the practitioner's stage, by what cuts it off, and by the crooked, filthy, turbid, pure, or silent conduct that produces it; and finally the fourfold scheme of black, white, mixed, and the uncontaminated karma that exhausts all karma entirely.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Twenty-one kinds of karma in all, standing here as a complete list before each is explained individually. The five pairs turn on: severity, growth, intention, certainty of result, and ripening's timing. The ten triads turn on: moral quality, the feeling it accords with, when its fruit is received, the practitioner's stage, what cuts it off, and five further triads (crooked, filthy, turbid, pure, silent) each spanning body, speech, and mind. The fourfold scheme closes with karma black in cause and effect (demerit), white in cause and effect (the unmoving karma of the form and formless realms' concentrations), a black-and-white mixture (the desire realm's ordinary merit), and, last, the noble ones' uncontaminated karma — neither black nor white, bearing no further ripening at all, and alone able to exhaust every kind of karma that precedes it. For further study, the notes point to fascicles 8-9 of the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection and fascicles 59-60 of its Decisions.

4. Section: Heavy and Light Karma Begins — the Three Conditions for Heaviness Named

One should know that here, by three causes and conditions, karma becomes heavy: first, by intention; second, by application; third, by the field.

Chinese Source

當知此中，由三因緣令業成重。一、由意樂故；二、由加行故；三、由田故。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, by three causes and conditions, karma becomes heavy: first, by intention; second, by application; third, by the field.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Explaining one by one (隨釋), the exposition of the twenty-one kinds begins with the first pair, heavy and light karma, itself distinguished by three factors: the strength of the underlying intention, the intensity of the actual deed, and the significance of its object or "field."

5. Section: Intention's Role in Making Karma Heavy

By intention: that is, karma created through fierce entanglement and the rest — seeing a fellow practitioner act likewise and feeling joy, and, according with that same dharma, engaging in extensive speculation and deliberation about it. Such as this is named making karma heavy through intention.

Chinese Source

由意樂者，謂由猛利纏等所作，於同法者見已歡喜，於彼隨法多隨尋思、多隨伺察。如是名為由意樂故，令業成重。

Ru-Yi Translation

By intention: that is, karma created through fierce entanglement and the rest — seeing a fellow practitioner act likewise and feeling joy, and, according with that same dharma, engaging in extensive speculation and deliberation about it. Such as this is named making karma heavy through intention.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

"Entanglement and the rest" includes wholesome mental factors too — heavy karma is not only unwholesome; a fiercely afflicted or fiercely wholesome motivation, reinforced by rejoicing at seeing another do likewise and by extensive premeditation, is what makes karma, whether good or bad, weigh heavily.

6. Section: Application, the Field, and Light Karma, Closing "Heavy and Light Karma" Entirely

By application: that is, karma performed without interruption, performed earnestly, accumulated over a long time; further, urging others to do likewise within it; and further, praising and extolling it. Such as this is named making karma heavy through application. By the field: that is, toward sentient beings who have been kind to oneself, or who abide in right practice and its fruit, giving rise to wholesome or unwholesome deeds — one should know this karma is named heavy. Contrary to this is named light.

Chinese Source

由加行者，謂於彼業無間所作、殷重所作、長時積集，又於其中勸他令作，又即於彼稱揚讚歎。如是名為由加行故，令業成重。

Ru-Yi Translation

By application: that is, karma performed without interruption, performed earnestly, accumulated over a long time; further, urging others to do likewise within it; and further, praising and extolling it. Such as this is named making karma heavy through application.

Chinese Source

由田故者，謂諸有情，於己有恩，若住正行及正行果。於彼發起善作、惡作，當知此業說名為重。

Ru-Yi Translation

By the field: that is, toward sentient beings who have been kind to oneself, or who abide in right practice and its fruit, giving rise to wholesome or unwholesome deeds — one should know this karma is named heavy.

Chinese Source

與彼相違，說名為輕。

Ru-Yi Translation

Contrary to this is named light.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Decisions on the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection (決擇‧有尋有伺等三地)

The "field" is one's parents or teachers (the field of kindness) and those abiding in the path from stream-entry through arhatship, or the Buddha himself (the field of reverence). The Decisions offer a parallel five-cause scheme for heavy killing specifically: fierce intention (strong greed, aversion, or delusion driving it); cruel means (relish in the act, inciting others, prolonged premeditated hatred, killing many at once, or killing the defenseless); lack of antidote (never keeping even minor precepts, never using wealth for merit, never showing reverence to the fields above, without shame, without repentance, without any concentration or noble path to counter it); wrong attachment (holding ritual killing to be righteous, as some outsiders do); and the object itself (killing a great being, a parent, a revered person, one who has taken refuge in oneself, or any noble one — even attempting to wound a Buddha, though he cannot actually be killed). This fascicle's three factors — intention, application, field — map onto that fivefold scheme: intention corresponds directly; application subsumes means, lack of antidote, and wrong attachment; the field corresponds to the object. With this, heavy and light karma (重業輕業), the first of the five pairs, is closed entirely.

7. Section: Increasing and Non-Increasing Karma Begins — Five Reasons Karma Does Not Increase

If karma is not performed with clear awareness, or is performed in a dream, or is performed with unobscured, indeterminate mind; or is unwholesomely performed, but soon followed by regret, its antidote, and renewed discipline; or, further, belongs to the entirely pure continuum of one who has attained it — such as these are all named non-increasing karma.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

If karma is not performed with clear awareness, or is performed in a dream, or is performed with unobscured, indeterminate mind;

Ru-Yi Translation

or is unwholesomely performed, but soon followed by regret, its antidote, and renewed discipline;

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

or, further, belongs to the entirely pure continuum of one who has attained it —

Chinese Source

如是皆名不增進業。

Ru-Yi Translation

such as these are all named non-increasing karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection (有尋有伺地) on the ten non-increasing karmas

Increasing and non-increasing karma (增進業不增進業), the second pair, opens — non-increasing karma covers: a young child's uncomprehending acts; dream-actions; karma performed with an indeterminate mind, neither wholesome nor unwholesome; unwholesome karma sincerely repented and restrained before its fruit ripens (first weakened by mundane concentration, then its seed destroyed entirely by supramundane wisdom); and, finally, any karma at all belonging to an arhat's already-purified continuum. The parallel ten-item list adds: dream-made karma, karma from ignorance, unintentional karma, karma neither forceful nor habitual, karma made while deranged or overwhelmed by pain, karma from a momentary lapse of mindfulness, karma performed under compulsion, karma that is inherently indeterminate, karma weakened by repentance, and karma weakened by its antidote.

8. Section: The Contrary Five Make Karma Increase, Closing "Increasing and Non-Increasing Karma" Entirely

One should know that contrary to this is named increasing karma.

Chinese Source

當知異此，名增進業。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that contrary to this is named increasing karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection (有尋有伺地) on the six marks of "increased" karma

Increasing karma, by contrast, is performed with clear awareness while awake, by a wholesome or unwholesome mind, never repented or restrained, and by an ordinary being rather than an arhat. The six marks that intensify karma further: through application (fierce, diligent effort, whether afflicted or wholesome); through habituation (repeated practice compounding its force); through its own-nature (harsh speech worse than idle chatter, divisive speech worse than harsh speech, lying worst of all; killing worse than stealing, stealing worse than sexual misconduct; among mental karma, delusion worse than aversion, aversion worse than greed; mental karma heaviest of the three, bodily karma lightest — and, for wholesome karma, meditation surpassing precept-keeping, precept-keeping surpassing giving, cultivation-wisdom surpassing reflection-wisdom, reflection-wisdom surpassing hearing-wisdom); through its object (reverence shown to the three jewels or the virtuous yields especially great merit); through leaving unwholesome karma entirely uncountered throughout one's life; and, conversely, through countering unwholesome karma so thoroughly — first through mundane concentration, then through the uncontaminated noble path — that wholesome karma itself becomes especially forceful. With this, increasing and non-increasing karma (增進業不增進業), the second of the five pairs, is closed entirely.

9. Section: Deliberate and Non-Deliberate Karma Begins — Deliberate Karma Named

Among these, karma created with deliberate intention: that is, karma performed only after prior deliberation, subsequent speculation, and subsequent careful examination — whether confused or unconfused in its object.

Chinese Source

此中故思所造業者，謂先思量已，隨尋思已，隨伺察已，而有所作。彼或錯亂，或不錯亂。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, karma created with deliberate intention: that is, karma performed only after prior deliberation, subsequent speculation, and subsequent careful examination — whether confused or unconfused in its object.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection (有尋有伺地) and the Compendium of Abhidharma (雜集論)

Deliberate and non-deliberate karma (故思造業非故思造業), the third pair, opens. Deliberate karma of a determinate ripening — both performed and further increased through continued deliberation, left unrepented — can obstruct the noble path itself, becoming a genuine karmic hindrance. The Compendium names five reasons deliberate karma is undertaken: instructed by another, urged by another, from simple ignorance of merit and fault, from deeply rooted affliction, or from distorted discrimination through wrong view.

10. Section: Confused and Unconfused Deliberate Karma, and Non-Deliberate Karma, Closing "Deliberate and Non-Deliberate Karma" Entirely

Confusion is this: intending, toward one target, to kill, or to steal, or to divide, or to lie and deceive, and so on — and, having so intended, accomplishing this same intention against a different target. One should know that here, it is by intention that this is named heavy, not by the object that it is named heavy. Being unconfused, one should know, has a mark contrary to this. Whatever differs from this karma is then named non-deliberate.

Chinese Source

其錯亂者，謂於餘處思欲殺害，或欲劫盜，或欲別離，或欲妄語及欺誑等。如是思已，即以此想別處成辦。

Ru-Yi Translation

Confusion is this: intending, toward one target, to kill, or to steal, or to divide, or to lie and deceive, and so on — and, having so intended, accomplishing this same intention against a different target.

Chinese Source

當知此中，由意樂故說名為重，不由事故說名為重。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, it is by intention that this is named heavy, not by the object that it is named heavy.

Chinese Source

不錯亂者，當知其相與此相違。

Ru-Yi Translation

Being unconfused, one should know, has a mark contrary to this.

Chinese Source

若異此業，是即名為非故思造。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whatever differs from this karma is then named non-deliberate.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Decisions on the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection (決擇‧有尋有伺地) on the five factors of a complete unwholesome path of action

Killing a look-alike by mistake for one's intended target is still heavy karma, since heaviness turns on the intention, not on hitting the intended object. Every path of unwholesome action — such as killing — requires five complete factors: the object (a sentient or non-sentient target), perception (an undistorted recognition of it), intention (the wish to act), affliction (the tainted mind, whether the perception was correct or mistaken), and completion through action (carrying the deed through to its result) — lacking any one, the path of action is not fully constituted. With this, deliberate and non-deliberate karma (故思造業非故思造業), the third of the five pairs, is closed entirely.

11. Section: Determinate and Indeterminate Receiving, Closed Entirely

Determinate-receiving karma is deliberately-created heavy karma; indeterminate-receiving karma is deliberately-created light karma.

Chinese Source

定受業者，謂故思所造重業。不定受業者，謂故思所造輕業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Determinate-receiving karma is deliberately-created heavy karma; indeterminate-receiving karma is deliberately-created light karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Determinate and indeterminate receiving (定受業不定受業), the fourth pair: heavy deliberate karma, unrepented and uncountered, definitely bears fruit — even into a future evil-destiny rebirth — while genuine repentance and antidote can transform it into indeterminate, lighter, present-life ripening instead; light deliberate karma, minor or not further pursued through continued deliberation, is indeterminate from the start. With this, determinate and indeterminate receiving is closed entirely.

12. Section: Already-Ripened and Not-Yet-Ripened Karma Begins

Already-ripened karma is karma whose fruit has already been given; not-yet-ripened karma is contrary to this.

Chinese Source

異熟已熟業者，謂已與果業。異熟未熟業者，與此相違。

Ru-Yi Translation

Already-ripened karma is karma whose fruit has already been given; not-yet-ripened karma is contrary to this.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Already-ripened and not-yet-ripened karma (異熟已熟業異熟未熟業), the fifth and final pair, opens — karma whose fruit-body has already manifested (as when karma that yields a human birth has already yielded it) versus karma whose cause has not yet come to fruition.

13. Section: Only Manifest Ripening Obstructs Arhatship

If one is about to attain arhatship, determinate-receiving karma previously created is able to obstruct this, because its ripening fruit is presently manifest — not because it follows the body's continuity as a mere seed. Why is this? Only because that karma has already produced an unequal, deficient body one relies on is it able to obstruct, preventing the attainment of arhatship.

Chinese Source

若欲證得阿羅漢時，先所造作決定受業，由異熟果現在前故，能為障礙，不由隨逐身相續故。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one is about to attain arhatship, determinate-receiving karma previously created is able to obstruct this, because its ripening fruit is presently manifest — not because it follows the body's continuity as a mere seed.

Chinese Source

所以者何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this?

Chinese Source

但由彼業生不平等所依身故，能為障礙，令不能得阿羅漢果。

Ru-Yi Translation

Only because that karma has already produced an unequal, deficient body one relies on is it able to obstruct, preventing the attainment of arhatship.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Only karma already ripened into a deficient body or faculties — obstructing the very practice needed for realization — poses a genuine obstacle; karma still dormant as a mere seed does not.

14. Section: Karma Received Later Does Not Obstruct This Life's Arhatship, Closing "Already-Ripened and Not-Yet-Ripened Karma" and "The Five Pairs" Entirely

If there is no karma to be received in the next birth, but only karma to be received later still, this is unable to obstruct the arhatship one is to attain — yet this does not mean it is not determinate-receiving karma. Why is this? Precisely because such karma is, by designation, determinate-receiving in reliance upon affliction as its companion, and in reliance upon the continued flow of the formations it depends on.

Chinese Source

若無生受，而有後受，於所證得阿羅漢果不能為障，然彼非不是定受業。

Ru-Yi Translation

If there is no karma to be received in the next birth, but only karma to be received later still, this is unable to obstruct the arhatship one is to attain — yet this does not mean it is not determinate-receiving karma.

Chinese Source

何以故？由即依彼煩惱助伴，及即依彼諸行相續，施設此業為定受故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this? Precisely because such karma is, by designation, determinate-receiving in reliance upon affliction as its companion, and in reliance upon the continued flow of the formations it depends on.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the *Yogācārabhūmi Compilation* (瑜伽論記) and the Reading Notes (披尋記)

Karma due only in a life two or more lives away cannot obstruct arhatship attained in this one, since its ripening remains outside this present body — yet it still counts as "determinate," since that classification depends on affliction remaining present as its companion and the formations still flowing onward toward it. An arhat's affliction, however, is permanently exhausted, so any such later-life karma can never actually ripen — hence it poses no obstacle at all. The *Compilation* likens the sheer volume of karma still obstructing an ordinary being or learner to "a sea, a mountain" — karma yielding female birth, birth in the northern continent, the formless heavens, or as one of ambiguous sex among them — obstacles that bite only once their fruit manifests, not while still dormant as seed. With this, already-ripened and not-yet-ripened karma (異熟已熟業異熟未熟業), the fifth pair, is closed entirely — and with it, the five kinds of twofold karma (五種二業) are closed entirely.

15. Section: The Ten Triads Begin — Wholesome, Unwholesome, and Indeterminate Karma, Wholesome Karma First

Further, by two causes and conditions, wholesome karma is established: first, by drawing forth a desired fruit; second, by fully and truly knowing the object relied on, and its fruit.

Chinese Source

復次，由二因緣建立善業。一、取愛果故；二、於所緣境如實遍知，及彼果故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, by two causes and conditions, wholesome karma is established: first, by drawing forth a desired fruit; second, by fully and truly knowing the object relied on, and its fruit.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Reading Notes (披尋記)

The ten kinds of threefold karma (十種三業) open with the first triad: wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate karma. Wholesome karma is twofold: the mundane, contaminated kind that draws forth a desired human or heavenly fruit (as through keeping the five precepts), and the supramundane, uncontaminated kind — skillfully and fully knowing whatever object one attends to (body, feeling, mind, dharmas, sentient beings, the physical world, the six sense-doors) as impermanent, suffering, empty, and without self, cutting off affliction and realizing the noble fruit.

16. Section: Unwholesome and Indeterminate Karma, Closing Triad One Entirely

By two causes and conditions, unwholesome karma is established: first, by drawing forth an undesired fruit; second, by wrongly clinging to the object relied on. Because it cannot be recorded as either of the two aspects, wholesome or unwholesome, indeterminate karma is established.

Chinese Source

由二因緣立不善業。一、取非愛果故；二、於所緣境邪執著故。

Ru-Yi Translation

By two causes and conditions, unwholesome karma is established: first, by drawing forth an undesired fruit; second, by wrongly clinging to the object relied on.

Chinese Source

於善不善二種行相不可記故，立無記業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because it cannot be recorded as either of the two aspects, wholesome or unwholesome, indeterminate karma is established.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Unwholesome karma mirrors wholesome karma's structure: drawing an undesired fruit through affliction-driven action, and clinging wrongly to self-view, eternalism, annihilationism, wrong precepts, or any of the sixty-two views as if the object grasped were real. Indeterminate karma includes several kinds — karmic ripening itself, deportment, craft, and transformation — none classifiable as either wholesome or unwholesome. With this, wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate karma (善不善無記業), the first of the ten triads, is closed entirely.

17. Section: Karma According With Pleasant, Painful, and Neutral Feeling, by Total Report

Karma according with pleasant feeling is every wholesome karma bound to the first, second, and third dhyāna grounds, and to the desire realm. Karma according with painful feeling is karma able to draw forth birth in the evil destinies. Karma according with neither painful nor pleasant feeling is every karma bound to the fourth dhyāna and the grounds above it.

Chinese Source

順樂受業者，謂初、二、三靜慮地繫及欲界繫所有善業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma according with pleasant feeling is every wholesome karma bound to the first, second, and third dhyāna grounds, and to the desire realm.

Chinese Source

順苦受業者，謂能招感惡趣生業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma according with painful feeling is karma able to draw forth birth in the evil destinies.

Chinese Source

順不苦不樂受業者，謂第四靜慮及上地等諸所有業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma according with neither painful nor pleasant feeling is every karma bound to the fourth dhyāna and the grounds above it.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The second triad, karma according with pleasant, painful, and neutral feeling (順樂受等業), opens by total report: the unmoving karma of the first three dhyānas yields joy and pleasure, the desire realm's own merit yields the pleasure of a human or heavenly rebirth; karma yielding hell, hungry-ghost, or animal rebirth yields suffering; and the fourth dhyāna's and the formless realms' unmoving karma yields the equanimous feeling of neither pain nor pleasure.

18. Section: By Individual Report, Closing "Karma According With Feeling" Entirely

Being born among hungry ghosts or animals, if prior karma serves as the cause for receiving pleasant feeling, one should know this karma too may be named karma according with pleasant feeling. Excepting only hell, in every other place, one should know both painful and pleasant feeling may be received mixed together — precisely through the increasing power of that karma, this dependent body dwells amid mixed pain and pleasure without either obstructing the other.

Chinese Source

生於餓鬼及傍生中，先業為因感得樂受，當知此業亦得名為順樂受業。唯除那落迦，於所餘處，當知皆得苦樂雜受，即由彼業增上力故，令此依身苦樂雜住不相妨礙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Being born among hungry ghosts or animals, if prior karma serves as the cause for receiving pleasant feeling, one should know this karma too may be named karma according with pleasant feeling. Excepting only hell, in every other place, one should know both painful and pleasant feeling may be received mixed together — precisely through the increasing power of that karma, this dependent body dwells amid mixed pain and pleasure without either obstructing the other.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Beyond the total report of an entire rebirth, individual report accounts for finer variation within it — a bejeweled elephant or a cherished pet among animals, a fortunate spirit among hungry ghosts, still enjoy some genuine pleasure through their own particular karma; only hell, from karma too heavy to admit any relief, is pure suffering without exception. With this, karma according with pleasant, painful, and neutral feeling (順樂受等業), the second of the ten triads, is closed entirely.

19. Section: Karma Received Now, at Next Birth, and Later Still

Karma received in the present life is karma created through such-and-such intention, or through such-and-such application — whether through the application of the matter, the body, or speech — or toward such-and-such a fertile field, whose ripening matures within this very life; such is named karma received in the present life. If karma created has not yet ripened in the present life, but will ripen in the very next birth, such is named karma received at the next birth. If karma created has not yet ripened either in the present life or the next birth, but ripens only from that point onward, one should know this is named karma received later still.

Chinese Source

順現法受業者，謂由如是相狀意樂所作諸業；若由如是相狀加行，謂事加行、或身加行、或語加行所作諸業；若由如是相狀良田所作諸業；於現法中異熟成熟，如是名為順現法受業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma received in the present life is karma created through such-and-such intention, or through such-and-such application — whether through the application of the matter, the body, or speech — or toward such-and-such a fertile field, whose ripening matures within this very life; such is named karma received in the present life.

Chinese Source

若所作業，於現法中異熟未熟，於次生中當生異熟，如是名為順生受業。

Ru-Yi Translation

If karma created has not yet ripened in the present life, but will ripen in the very next birth, such is named karma received at the next birth.

Chinese Source

若所作業，現法、次生異熟未熟，從此已後異熟方熟，當知是名順後受業。

Ru-Yi Translation

If karma created has not yet ripened either in the present life or the next birth, but ripens only from that point onward, one should know this is named karma received later still.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The third triad, karma received now, at next birth, or later still (順現受等業), sorts karma purely by the timing of its ripening — heavy, forceful, or well-tended karma toward a significant field tends to ripen fastest, within this very life. With this, karma received now, at next birth, and later still is closed entirely.

20. Section: The Learner's, the Unlearner's, and Neither's Karma

The learner's karma is that of the noble disciple who, from time to time, relying on the surpassing training in precepts, mind, and wisdom, cultivates the uncontaminated, together with the wholesome, contaminated karma subsequently attained — this is named the learner's karma. The unlearner's karma is whatever karma, as appropriate, belongs to the continuum of any arhat's body. The remaining karma besides these is named karma of neither the learner nor the unlearner.

Chinese Source

有學業者，謂聖弟子，於時時間依增上戒、依增上心、依增上慧修學無漏，及此後得善有漏業，名有學業。

Ru-Yi Translation

The learner's karma is that of the noble disciple who, from time to time, relying on the surpassing training in precepts, mind, and wisdom, cultivates the uncontaminated, together with the wholesome, contaminated karma subsequently attained — this is named the learner's karma.

Chinese Source

無學業者，謂於一切阿羅漢等身相續中隨應諸業。

Ru-Yi Translation

The unlearner's karma is whatever karma, as appropriate, belongs to the continuum of any arhat's body.

Chinese Source

此餘諸業，是名非學非無學業。

Ru-Yi Translation

The remaining karma besides these is named karma of neither the learner nor the unlearner.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The fourth triad, the learner's, unlearner's, and neither's karma (有學等業), spans the noble path's own stages: a learner (stream-entrant through non-returner) generates both uncontaminated karma through the threefold training and wholesome contaminated karma alongside it, since innate affliction still remains; an unlearner's (arhat's) every karma is uncontaminated, all affliction exhausted; and every ordinary being's wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate karma falls under the third, residual category. With this, the learner's, unlearner's, and neither's karma is closed entirely.

21. Section: Karma Cut Off by Seeing, by Cultivation, and Not to Be Cut Off — the Three Own-Natures Named

Whatever is associated with affliction cut off by seeing, and whatever volitional and other karma this generates — every such karma able to lead toward the evil destinies — all of this is named karma cut off by seeing. Whatever is associated with affliction cut off by cultivation, and whatever volitional and other karma this generates — all such karma is named karma cut off by cultivation. Karma not to be cut off is all the supramundane karma of every learner and unlearner.

Chinese Source

若見所斷煩惱相應，若此所發思等諸業，一切能往諸惡趣業，此等皆名見所斷業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whatever is associated with affliction cut off by seeing, and whatever volitional and other karma this generates — every such karma able to lead toward the evil destinies — all of this is named karma cut off by seeing.

Chinese Source

若修所斷煩惱相應，及此所發思等諸業，如是皆名修所斷業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whatever is associated with affliction cut off by cultivation, and whatever volitional and other karma this generates — all such karma is named karma cut off by cultivation.

Chinese Source

無斷業者，所謂一切有學、無學出世間業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma not to be cut off is all the supramundane karma of every learner and unlearner.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Compendium of Abhidharma (集論) and the Abhidharmakośa Compilation (俱舍論記)

The fifth triad, karma cut off by seeing, by cultivation, and not to be cut off (見所斷等業), opens: karma associated with the ten discriminated afflictions cut off at the path of seeing (self-view, extreme views, wrong view, wrong precept-grasping, view-grasping, craving, aversion, delusion, conceit, doubt) — every such karma leading to the evil destinies — is cut off by seeing; karma associated with the sixteen innate afflictions cut off through the path of cultivation (across the three realms) — ordinary human and heavenly merit — is cut off by cultivation; and the uncontaminated karma of every noble one, learner or unlearner, is simply not the kind of thing subject to cutting off at all, since it carries no affliction to begin with.

22. Section: Broadly Explaining Removal Begins — Three Marks Named

One should know that here, by three kinds of mark, deliberately-created unwholesome karma, even after it has been increased in this very life, can still be removed and cut off. What are the three? First, cutting off in the present life; second, cutting off at rebirth; third, cutting off later still.

Chinese Source

當知此中，由三種相，故思所造諸不善業，即於現法作增長已，還復除斷。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, by three kinds of mark, deliberately-created unwholesome karma, even after it has been increased in this very life, can still be removed and cut off.

Chinese Source

何等為三？

Ru-Yi Translation

What are the three?

Chinese Source

一、現法斷故；二、生斷故；三、後斷故。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, cutting off in the present life; second, cutting off at rebirth; third, cutting off later still.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Broadly explaining removal (廣除斷), the second matter of the fifth triad, opens: even unwholesome karma already increased is not necessarily permanent — three grades of removal, distinguished by how thoroughly it is uprooted, follow.

23. Section: Cutting Off in the Present Life

Cutting off in the present life: that is, as when someone, having deliberately created karma in the present life and increased it, soon after grows weary of it, feeling weariness toward what was done. This applies to the ordinary being not yet free of desire. Abiding thus until death, one is still unable to ensure that, at the next birth, one will not create that karma or receive its ripening, nor able to ensure this for any stage thereafter; even in the present life, one is not able to ensure never creating it at all.

Chinese Source

現法斷者，謂如有一，於現法中故思造業，作增長已尋復厭離，於其所作受厭離故。此是異生未得離欲。

Ru-Yi Translation

Cutting off in the present life: that is, as when someone, having deliberately created karma in the present life and increased it, soon after grows weary of it, feeling weariness toward what was done. This applies to the ordinary being not yet free of desire.

Chinese Source

住此命終，而未能令於次生位不造彼業、不受異熟，亦未能令於其後位無有是事。於現法中，亦未一向能令不造。

Ru-Yi Translation

Abiding thus until death, one is still unable to ensure that, at the next birth, one will not create that karma or receive its ripening, nor able to ensure this for any stage thereafter; even in the present life, one is not able to ensure never creating it at all.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This weariness merely suppresses affliction's manifest form without touching its seed — like a habitual gambler who, chastened by conscience or a friend's rebuke, abstains for a time, only to relapse when temptation returns in force.

24. Section: Cutting Off at Rebirth

Cutting off at rebirth: that is, there is another who, having grown weary, though still an ordinary being, has already attained freedom from desire for the desire realm. Abiding thus until death, in the present life such a one no longer creates such karma at all, and, at the very least, does not receive its ripening at the next birth — how much less would one have occasion to create it after being reborn. Yet one is not yet liberated from creating such karma, or receiving its ripening, in a still-later stage.

Chinese Source

生斷故者，謂復有一，受厭離已，雖是異生，而於欲界已得離欲。

Ru-Yi Translation

Cutting off at rebirth: that is, there is another who, having grown weary, though still an ordinary being, has already attained freedom from desire for the desire realm.

Chinese Source

住此命終，彼於現法更不造作，尚於次生不受異熟，況復生已當有所作。然未解脫後位作業及受異熟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Abiding thus until death, in the present life such a one no longer creates such karma at all, and, at the very least, does not receive its ripening at the next birth — how much less would one have occasion to create it after being reborn. Yet one is not yet liberated from creating such karma, or receiving its ripening, in a still-later stage.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Having already left desire for the desire realm behind, such a person is reborn instead into the higher realms, safely bypassing next-life ripening — yet the desire-realm affliction's seed remains merely suppressed, not eternally severed, so later lives are not yet guaranteed free of it.

25. Section: Cutting Off Later Still Begins — the Learner Not Yet Fully Free of Desire

Cutting off later still: that is, there is another who, though a learner, has not yet attained freedom from desire for the desire realm; having grown weary, such a one has attained the first, or else the second, fruit of the ascetic path.

Chinese Source

後斷故者，謂復有一，雖是有學，而於欲界未得離欲，受厭離已，獲得最初、或復第二沙門果證。

Ru-Yi Translation

Cutting off later still: that is, there is another who, though a learner, has not yet attained freedom from desire for the desire realm; having grown weary, such a one has attained the first, or else the second, fruit of the ascetic path.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The stream-entrant or once-returner, still working through the desire realm's own afflictions to be cut off by cultivation, continues toward the non-returner's fruit, at which point unwholesome desire-realm karma is cut off permanently, this life and every life to come.

26. Section: The Reason Explained — Making a Vow to Receive All Fruit Now

Such a one thinks thus: whatever I possess, born of much coarse burden and much distress, belonging only to this despicable body I should abandon — whatever evil karma this has created, I vow to receive it entirely in the present life; or, whatever karma I have to be received in the present life, whether painful or pleasant, I vow to receive it all together at once — let me not, in a future birth or in some later stage, receive its ripening then.

Chinese Source

彼作是念：凡我所有由多麤重、由多熱惱、唯應棄捨可厭賤身所作惡業，願於現法一切皆受；或我所有現法受業，若苦、若樂，皆願與彼俱時而受；勿復令我當於生位、或於後位受彼異熟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Such a one thinks thus: whatever I possess, born of much coarse burden and much distress, belonging only to this despicable body I should abandon — whatever evil karma this has created, I vow to receive it entirely in the present life; or, whatever karma I have to be received in the present life, whether painful or pleasant, I vow to receive it all together at once — let me not, in a future birth or in some later stage, receive its ripening then.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Rather than postpone karmic reckoning, the stream-entrant or once-returner deliberately wills to exhaust every outstanding karmic debt within this very life, refusing to carry any of it forward.

27. Section: Cultivating the Immeasurables to Fulfill the Vow

Having thus rightly resolved and made this vow, in order to cut it off, one further cultivates the immeasurables, rightly arousing effort subsumed under the factor of calming, for the sake of ultimately exhausting the causes and conditions able to give rise to that karma, and for the sake of advancing toward freedom from the craving for desire.

Chinese Source

如是正心發誓願已，為斷彼故，復修無量，以奢摩他品定所攝正起加行，為令能起彼業因緣究竟盡故，及為進趣離欲愛故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having thus rightly resolved and made this vow, in order to cut it off, one further cultivates the immeasurables, rightly arousing effort subsumed under the factor of calming, for the sake of ultimately exhausting the causes and conditions able to give rise to that karma, and for the sake of advancing toward freedom from the craving for desire.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The four immeasurables — loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity — become the concrete practice through which this vow is realized, uprooting the very causes of unwholesome karma while advancing toward the desire realm's own freedom from craving.

28. Section: The Four Afflictions and Their Four Antidotes, Closing "Proper Antidote" Entirely

One should know that here, whether from an intention of aversion, or an intention of harm, or an envious nature, or deep attachment to something cherished — relying on these as cause, wrong conduct toward sentient beings arises: that is, evil deeds and various evil matters set in motion by body, speech, and mind. If one wishes to counter these four causes able to give rise to evil karma, one cultivates the four immeasurables' surpassing concentrations; then, even toward a young boy or girl, there is nowhere, no occasion, for even a momentary thought of committing evil karma to arise again. Therefore, cultivating such effort is able to exhaust every cause and condition of evil karma.

Chinese Source

當知此中，或瞋意樂、或害意樂、或嫉妒性、或可愛事深生染著，由此為因，於諸有情發起邪行，謂身語意所發惡業種種惡事。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, whether from an intention of aversion, or an intention of harm, or an envious nature, or deep attachment to something cherished — relying on these as cause, wrong conduct toward sentient beings arises: that is, evil deeds and various evil matters set in motion by body, speech, and mind.

Chinese Source

若有為欲對治如是能起四種惡業因緣，修四無量勝三摩地，彼乃至於少男、少女，無處無容暫更發起作惡業思，是故彼修如是加行，能盡所有惡業因緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one wishes to counter these four causes able to give rise to evil karma, one cultivates the four immeasurables' surpassing concentrations; then, even toward a young boy or girl, there is nowhere, no occasion, for even a momentary thought of committing evil karma to arise again. Therefore, cultivating such effort is able to exhaust every cause and condition of evil karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Four causes, four antidotes: loving-kindness counters an intention of aversion; compassion counters an intention to harm; sympathetic joy counters envy, the inability to bear another's good fortune; and equanimity counters deep attachment to what is cherished. With these four immeasurable concentrations mastered, not even the most vulnerable object — a young child — can any longer tempt a thought of harm. With this, proper antidote (由正對治) is closed entirely.

29. Section: Universally Overwhelming Begins — Two Reasons the Immeasurables Prevail

One should know that such rightly-cultivated effort, by two causes and conditions, is able to overwhelm every evil karma already created and already increased — that is, through cultivating the immeasurable concentrations. Why is this? Evil karma created arises only from the intention to harm within a limited scope of sentient beings as object; the immeasurables cultivated arise from the intention to benefit an immeasurable scope of sentient beings as object.

Chinese Source

當知如是正修加行，由二因緣，於其所作及所增長一切惡業皆能摧伏。謂由修習無量定故。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that such rightly-cultivated effort, by two causes and conditions, is able to overwhelm every evil karma already created and already increased — that is, through cultivating the immeasurable concentrations.

Chinese Source

所以者何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this?

Chinese Source

所作惡業，但於有量有情境界欲不饒益意樂所起；所修無量，乃於無量有情境界欲作饒益意樂所起。

Ru-Yi Translation

Evil karma created arises only from the intention to harm within a limited scope of sentient beings as object; the immeasurables cultivated arise from the intention to benefit an immeasurable scope of sentient beings as object.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Ordinary unwholesome intention, even at its most malicious, still targets only a finite number of beings; the immeasurables take literally every sentient being as their object, an intention of benefit vastly outweighing any possible intention of harm.

30. Section: Inferior Versus Superior Realm

Further, the mind able to give rise to unwholesome karma is subsumed under the inferior realm, and is what is to be countered; the mind accompanying the cultivated immeasurables is subsumed under the excellent, sublime realm, and is what does the countering.

Chinese Source

又能發起不善業心，下劣界攝，是所對治；所修無量俱行之心，勝妙界攝，是能對治。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the mind able to give rise to unwholesome karma is subsumed under the inferior realm, and is what is to be countered; the mind accompanying the cultivated immeasurables is subsumed under the excellent, sublime realm, and is what does the countering.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The mind that creates unwholesome karma belongs to the desire realm, the lowest of the three; the mind of the immeasurables belongs to the form and formless realms' own superior quality — a higher mind naturally overwhelming a lower one. A third reason follows.

31. Section: Karma's Dependence on Mind, Closing "Universally Overwhelming" Entirely

Further, mind is what is supreme; every karma created is subsumed under mind — hence it is said that the world, too, is mind's own offspring, since it is bound up with mind and turns in dependence on mind.

Chinese Source

又心是勝，諸所造業皆屬於心，故說世間并是心胤，繫屬心故、依心轉故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, mind is what is supreme; every karma created is subsumed under mind — hence it is said that the world, too, is mind's own offspring, since it is bound up with mind and turns in dependence on mind.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Every karma, body or speech included, is set in motion by volition and thus bound inseparably to mind — so mastering one's own mind is the root from which unwholesome karma is overpowered. With this third reason, universally overwhelming (由遍摧伏) — scope, quality, and mind-dependence together — closes entirely.

32. Section: Cultivating the Immeasurables Toward Freedom from Desire, Closing "Cultivating the Immeasurables" Entirely

Practicing thus, one first makes the right vow as one's support, and afterward skillfully cultivates the immeasurable mind-concentrations; then, at the time of advancing toward freedom from the craving for desire, one is able to attain and abide in the fruit of non-returning.

Chinese Source

如是行者，先發正願為所依止，後善修習無量心定，當於進趣離欲愛時，便能獲得住不還果。

Ru-Yi Translation

Practicing thus, one first makes the right vow as one's support, and afterward skillfully cultivates the immeasurable mind-concentrations; then, at the time of advancing toward freedom from the craving for desire, one is able to attain and abide in the fruit of non-returning.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The stream-entrant or once-returner who vows to exhaust all karma in this life, then cultivates the four immeasurables to sever the causes and conditions of evil karma, in time attains the non-returner's fruit and never returns to the desire realm to receive ripening there. With this, cultivating the immeasurables (由修無量) — exhausting the causes of evil karma, and advancing toward freedom from craving — closes entirely.

33. Section: Displaying the Mark of Later Cutting-Off, Closing the Fifth Triad Entirely

If one merely takes temporary satisfaction in this attainment, not seeking further progress in the present life, such a one still does not create such karma even in the present life — how much less in a future birth or a still-later stage. And such a one is certainly unable to receive the ripening of a future birth or a later stage.

Chinese Source

若但於此暫生喜足，於現法中不求上進，彼現法中尚不造業，況於生位、或於後位。又定不能當受生位、後位異熟。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one merely takes temporary satisfaction in this attainment, not seeking further progress in the present life, such a one still does not create such karma even in the present life — how much less in a future birth or a still-later stage. And such a one is certainly unable to receive the ripening of a future birth or a later stage.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Even a stream-entrant or once-returner who rests content without further striving already possesses the path-concomitant precepts that never regress — such a one creates no more unwholesome karma in this life or any life to come, and is certain never to receive an evil-destiny ripening at a future or later stage. With this, cutting off later still (後斷) closes entirely, and with it broadly explaining removal (廣除斷) and the fifth triad, cut-by-seeing and the rest (見所斷等業), close entirely — the three marks of removal being present-life cutting-off, rebirth cutting-off, and later cutting-off.

34. Section: The Sixth Triad — Crooked Karma

Further, all wholesome and unwholesome karma possessed by those who, outside the true teaching, have fallen into wrong views or walk a wrong path, arises from wrong view; because it arises through the empowering force of wrong view, all such karma is called crooked karma.

Chinese Source

又正法外墮邪見者、行邪道者，所有一切善不善業，邪見所起。邪見增上力所生故，皆名曲業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, all wholesome and unwholesome karma possessed by those who, outside the true teaching, have fallen into wrong views or walk a wrong path, arises from wrong view; because it arises through the empowering force of wrong view, all such karma is called crooked karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Thought itself is decisive: when the underlying view and direction are wrong, whatever karma of body, speech, and mind follows — wholesome or unwholesome — is twisted into crooked karma.

35. Section: The Seventh Triad — Defiled Karma

Karma arising from fierce, sharp craving and aversion is all called defiled karma.

Chinese Source

猛利貪瞋所起諸業，皆名穢業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma arising from fierce, sharp craving and aversion is all called defiled karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Karma of body, speech, and mind born of powerful, sharp craving or aversion is defiled — such affliction obstructs the arising of every kind of virtue.

36. Section: The Eighth Triad — Turbid Karma

Karma arising from delusion — whether fierce and sharp delusion, superior-grade dull faculties with a loss of mindfulness, or the most obtuse and dull — is all turbid karma.

Chinese Source

猛利癡者、上品鈍根忘失念者、極闇鈍者，癡所起業，皆是濁業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma arising from delusion — whether fierce and sharp delusion, superior-grade dull faculties with a loss of mindfulness, or the most obtuse and dull — is all turbid karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Delusion clouds the ability to see things clearly, understand the truth of a matter, or comprehend the emptiness of self and phenomena — such turbidity obstructs penetration into the true character of things. Crooked, defiled, and turbid karma are each, in their own way, an obstruction to the path.

37. Section: The Ninth Triad — Pure Karma

Every excellent conduct able to lead to a fortunate destiny is called pure karma.

Chinese Source

一切能往善趣妙行，皆名淨業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Every excellent conduct able to lead to a fortunate destiny is called pure karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The excellent karma of body, speech, and mind able to bring rebirth among the contaminated fortunate destinies of gods and humans is called pure karma.

38. Section: The Tenth Triad — Silent Karma, Closing the Ten Triads Entirely

Every excellent conduct able to lead to nirvāṇa is called silent karma.

Chinese Source

一切能往涅槃妙行，名默然業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Every excellent conduct able to lead to nirvāṇa is called silent karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Excellent, liberating karma of body, speech, and mind able to lead toward supreme nirvāṇa is called silent karma, also called sage karma — "silent" carrying the sense of stillness and quiescence. With this, the ten triads of threefold karma close entirely: wholesome/unwholesome/indeterminate; the three feelings; the three receiving-times; learner/unlearner/neither; cut-by-seeing/cut-by-cultivation/uncuttable; crooked; defiled; turbid; pure; and silent karma.

39. Section: The Fourfold Scheme Begins — Karma with a Uniformly Dark Ripening

Next, evil karma able to bring about rebirth in a particular hell-realm location is called karma with a uniformly dark ripening.

Chinese Source

復次，能感各別處所那落迦惡業，名黑黑異熟業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, evil karma able to bring about rebirth in a particular hell-realm location is called karma with a uniformly dark ripening.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The first "dark" names the cause, the second names the effect — a dark cause producing a dark result, as with the ten evil deeds. This opens the final fourfold scheme of karma, the last item in the summary verse "five pairs, ten triads, and a final fourfold scheme."

40. Section: Karma with a Uniformly Bright Ripening

Wholesome karma able to bring about rebirth in a particular heavenly-realm location is called karma with a uniformly bright ripening.

Chinese Source

能感各別處所天趣善業，名白白異熟業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Wholesome karma able to bring about rebirth in a particular heavenly-realm location is called karma with a uniformly bright ripening.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Elsewhere in the treatise's Level of the Stage with Both Investigation and Analysis this is glossed simply as unmoving karma; here the exposition adds further detail, including — as the text soon explains — the māra-king's own palace, which likewise belongs to this bright-ripening category.

41. Section: Karma with a Mixed Dark-and-Bright Ripening

Karma able to bring about rebirth in any remaining location is called karma with a mixed dark-and-bright ripening. In such locations, both kinds of karmic fruit — desirable and undesirable — are found present together, and are therefore spoken of together as a single karma.

Chinese Source

能感餘處所有諸業，名黑白黑白異熟業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma able to bring about rebirth in any remaining location is called karma with a mixed dark-and-bright ripening.

Chinese Source

於是處所有二業果現前可得，是故總說以為一業。

Ru-Yi Translation

In such locations, both kinds of karmic fruit — desirable and undesirable — are found present together, and are therefore spoken of together as a single karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The six desire-realm heavens apart from the highest, the four continents, and the destinies of hungry ghosts and animals are all such mixed locations — since affliction has not been cut off there, wholesome and unwholesome karma are created by turns, dark and bright intermixed.

42. Section: Karma Neither Dark Nor Bright, Able to Exhaust All Karma, Closing "Bringing Forth the Own-Nature" Entirely

If it is any transmundane, contaminant-free karma, it is entirely called karma that is neither dark nor bright and without ripening, able to exhaust all karma. Karma already exhausted, and karma yet to be exhausted, are together called able to exhaust all karma — causing what has not yet arisen to never arise, and causing what has already arisen to gain release from bondage. Speaking in terms of desirable causes and their ripening fruits, it is called "not bright."

Chinese Source

若出世間諸無漏業，皆名不黑不白無異熟業，能盡諸業。

Ru-Yi Translation

If it is any transmundane, contaminant-free karma, it is entirely called karma that is neither dark nor bright and without ripening, able to exhaust all karma.

Chinese Source

若已盡業、若當盡業，二種總名能盡諸業，令未生者當不生故，令已生者得離繫故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma already exhausted, and karma yet to be exhausted, are together called able to exhaust all karma — causing what has not yet arisen to never arise, and causing what has already arisen to gain release from bondage.

Chinese Source

由約可愛因果異熟，故說不白。

Ru-Yi Translation

Speaking in terms of desirable causes and their ripening fruits, it is called "not bright."

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Transmundane, undefiled karma is neither an evil cause bringing an undesirable fruit (hence "not dark"), nor a contaminated wholesome cause bringing a desirable fruit (hence "not bright"), nor does it ripen anywhere in the three realms at all (hence "without ripening") — and it alone is able to exhaust every karma binding one to birth-and-death. Since affliction is cut through progressively rather than all at once, both what a given practitioner has already exhausted and what remains to be exhausted count as "able to exhaust all karma" — a designation proper to stream-entrants and above, who no longer create fresh unwholesome karma and who release already-ripened fruit from further entanglement, exactly as the saying goes: "requite old karma as conditions bring it, never create new calamity." A citation from Master Kuiji's commentary confirms: undefiled karma is not a desirable cause and does not bring ripening, hence "not bright." With this, bringing forth the own-nature (出體性) — the four kinds of the fourfold scheme — closes entirely.

43. Section: Explaining What Is Difficult Begins — the Heavenly-Destiny Category

One should know that the heavenly destinies whose particular locations are uniformly bright are those beyond the Heaven of Free Enjoyment of Others' Emanations — the demon-king's palace-realms among the desire-realm heavens, and every excellent karma belonging to the Brahma world above up through the Sphere of Neither Perception Nor Non-Perception — all told as a single category. This is because, in such locations, form seen by the eye and every phenomenon known by the mind are uniformly desirable, of surpassing, ever-increasing continuity, and drawn forth through the mind-gate as what is accomplished by mind.

Chinese Source

當知各別處所天趣一向白者，謂過他化自在天處，有欲界中魔王所都眾魔宮殿，及上梵世乃至非想非非想處所有善業，總說為一。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that the heavenly destinies whose particular locations are uniformly bright are those beyond the Heaven of Free Enjoyment of Others' Emanations — the demon-king's palace-realms among the desire-realm heavens, and every excellent karma belonging to the Brahma world above up through the Sphere of Neither Perception Nor Non-Perception — all told as a single category.

Chinese Source

由彼處所，眼所見色乃至意所知法，一向可愛，相續殊勝增上義故，意門引發意成義故。

Ru-Yi Translation

This is because, in such locations, form seen by the eye and every phenomenon known by the mind are uniformly desirable, of surpassing, ever-increasing continuity, and drawn forth through the mind-gate as what is accomplished by mind.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The demon-king's palace and the Brahma heavens above form a single uniformly-bright category, explained by two reasons in turn: "surpassing, ever-increasing continuity" applies to the highest desire-realm sovereign (the demon-king), while "accomplished by mind" applies to the Brahma heavens through the peak of the formless realm, whose inhabitants — possessing no coarse body but only a mind-generated one, born of concentration — experience every object as an outgrowth of mind itself. A questioner asks how the demon's palace, still within the desire realm, could be free of painful feeling; the commentarial tradition (the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary) answers that any apparent suffering there is a transformation-produced appearance rather than a true karmic fruit, so the palace still counts under uniformly-bright ripening.

44. Section: The Hell-Destiny Category and the Meaning of "Particular Location"

The particular locations of the hells are four: first, the great hells; second, the subsidiary hells; third, the cold hells; fourth, the surrounding hells. In these locations, some purely receive the fruit and ripening of karma tending toward pleasant feeling, others purely receive the fruit and ripening of karma tending toward painful feeling — hence they are called particular locations.

Chinese Source

各別處所那落迦有四。一、大那落迦，二、別那落迦，三、寒那落迦，四、邊那落迦。

Ru-Yi Translation

The particular locations of the hells are four: first, the great hells; second, the subsidiary hells; third, the cold hells; fourth, the surrounding hells.

Chinese Source

於此處所，各別純受順樂受業諸果異熟，各別純受順苦受業諸果異熟，是故說名各別處所。

Ru-Yi Translation

In these locations, some purely receive the fruit and ripening of karma tending toward pleasant feeling, others purely receive the fruit and ripening of karma tending toward painful feeling — hence they are called particular locations.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The great hells, their subsidiary hells nearby, the cold hells, and the one hundred twenty-eight "wandering-increase" surrounding hells beside them together make up the four kinds of particular hell-location — each purely painful, standing opposite the purely pleasant realms named above.

45. Section: A Further Distinction — Contentment Versus Joy

Further, in the demon's palace and the first two meditative heavens, one purely experiences contentment; from the third meditative heaven upward, one purely experiences joy. Speaking of "joy," this refers to what makes the mind pliant and at ease, resembling joy — hence called joy, though it is not the feeling of joy itself; resembling pleasure, it is called pleasure, though it is not the feeling of pleasure itself.

Chinese Source

又於魔宮、初二靜慮純受悅樂，若於第三靜慮已上純受喜樂。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, in the demon's palace and the first two meditative heavens, one purely experiences contentment; from the third meditative heaven upward, one purely experiences joy.

Chinese Source

言喜樂者，令心調柔、令心安適，與喜相似，故名為喜，非是喜受；與樂相似，說名為樂，非是樂受。

Ru-Yi Translation

Speaking of "joy," this refers to what makes the mind pliant and at ease, resembling joy — hence called joy, though it is not the feeling of joy itself; resembling pleasure, it is called pleasure, though it is not the feeling of pleasure itself.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The third meditative heaven has already left the feeling of joy behind, and the fourth has no feeling of pleasure at all, resting instead in purified equanimity — so the treatise carefully clarifies that "joy" and "pleasure" here name only the calm, pliant quality of a mind strong in concentration and thin in affliction, borrowed by resemblance, not the literal feelings themselves.

46. Section: Citing the Sūtra's Words, Closing the Fourfold Scheme, "Broadly Explaining Karma," and "Establishing" Entirely

What is constantly experienced through the gate of the six contact-bases — one should know this refers to precisely those six contact-bases together with their particular locations, by the principle that cause and effect are mutually bound.

Chinese Source

六觸處門恆所領受者，當知即彼名六觸處及各別處所，因果相屬道理義故。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is constantly experienced through the gate of the six contact-bases — one should know this refers to precisely those six contact-bases together with their particular locations, by the principle that cause and effect are mutually bound.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary and the Xianyang Shengjiao Lun

The six contact-bases are the present effect-mark; past karma is their cause-mark — cause brings about effect, effect answers to cause, and this is the principle of cause and effect mutually bound. The Xianyang Shengjiao Lun confirms: the present six contact-bases' experience derives from karma deliberately created by volition in a former life. Even facing the identical object, different beings feel differently, precisely because their karmic fruits differ; before learning the Dharma one simply follows pleasure and pain as they come, but having learned it one may transcend both and gain liberation's ease.

With this citation, the sūtra's own explanation is confirmed and the exposition of the kinds of karma (業種類) is complete, closing "broadly explaining karma" (廣說業) — and with it, since establishing (安立攝) has now covered all eight of its topics in full (establishing feelings, their eight distinctions, the foolish, the not-foolish, teaching, liberation, broadly explaining affliction, and broadly explaining karma), establishing itself (安立攝) closes entirely, exactly as the source text marks: "the exposition of the kinds of karma ends here" (以上將業的種類分別到這裡).

47. Section: Wisdom Begins — the Summary Verse

Next, a summary verse says: Not-knowing, wisdom, and concentration; the surpassing, obstruction, the learner and the rest; attachment, non-self, the noble path; and the two oceans' non-identical portions.

Chinese Source

復次，嗢柁南曰：無智智與定　殊勝障學等　著無我聖道　二海不同分

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, a summary verse says: Not-knowing, wisdom, and concentration; the surpassing, obstruction, the learner and the rest; attachment, non-self, the noble path; and the two oceans' non-identical portions.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens Wisdom (智攝), the second of 處擇攝's four particular verses, naming ten topics to be explained in turn: not-knowing, wisdom, concentration, the surpassing, obstruction, the learner and the rest, attachment, non-self, the noble path, and the two oceans' non-identical portions.

48. Section: Not-Knowing Begins — Marking Its Loss of Virtue

Whatever wrong views, whatever self-views, or whatever ignorance there may be — relying on the three classes of sentient beings spoken of before, these arise rooted in not-knowing. If, among all such classes, one is able to cut off this rootless, meaningless one dharma able to give rise to every defilement, one should know that such a one is able rightly to attest to what has been understood.

Chinese Source

若諸邪見、若諸我見、若即無明，依前所說三有情眾無智為根，故得生起。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whatever wrong views, whatever self-views, or whatever ignorance there may be — relying on the three classes of sentient beings spoken of before, these arise rooted in not-knowing.

Chinese Source

若能斷此無義根本一切眾中，能起一切雜染一法，當知彼能正記所解。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, among all such classes, one is able to cut off this rootless, meaningless one dharma able to give rise to every defilement, one should know that such a one is able rightly to attest to what has been understood.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The three classes of sentient beings named earlier — those who create crooked, defiled, and turbid karma — are all rooted in this single dharma, ignorance itself, the meaningless root giving rise to every defilement. One who cuts it off becomes a genuine knower, able to attest as the Saṃyukta Āgama's own formula puts it: "birth for me is ended, the holy life established, what was to be done is done, no further existence is known."

49. Section: Explaining the Marks of Defilement, Closing "Not-Knowing" Entirely

Here, the first defilement thus arisen diminishes what is real; the second defilement adds to what is unreal; the third defilement, regarding the means of truly and clearly revealing the teaching, is able to produce delusion. Owing to delusion with respect to those two causes, one gives rise either to addition or to diminishment.

Chinese Source

此中第一所起雜染，損減實事；第二雜染，增益虛事；第三雜染，於其如實顯了方便能作愚癡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, the first defilement thus arisen diminishes what is real; the second defilement adds to what is unreal; the third defilement, regarding the means of truly and clearly revealing the teaching, is able to produce delusion.

Chinese Source

於彼二因有愚癡故，或起增益、或起損減。

Ru-Yi Translation

Owing to delusion with respect to those two causes, one gives rise either to addition or to diminishment.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Saṃyukta Āgama's Sense-Base Chapter, sūtras 75-76

Diminishing what is real is the nihilistic wrong view that denies parents, denies spontaneously-born beings, denies true arhats or the utter extinction of contaminants; adding to what is unreal is self-view, extreme-view, view-attachment, and precept-attachment, wrongly affirming a self among the five aggregates; delusion regarding what is truly and clearly revealed is failing to trust the sage teaching, obstructing the path and blocking liberation. These trace to two kinds of ignorance — regarding existence and regarding non-existence — matching two Saṃyukta Āgama sūtras: in the first, a monk asks the Buddha how right knowing and seeing sever self-view through contemplating the six sense-faculties as impermanent; in the second, the Buddha teaches that severing this one dharma, ignorance, alone yields the arhat's self-attestation formula, and answers a further question on how to know and see the ending of ignorance: by cultivating insight into the impermanence of the six faculties, objects, consciousnesses, contacts, and feelings. With this, not-knowing (無智), the first of the ten topics of Wisdom, closes entirely.

50. Section: Wisdom Begins — Two Kinds Named

Next, there are two kinds of genuine wisdom: the first is what is brought forth by proper reflection; the second is what is brought forth by concentration.

Chinese Source

復次，有二種如實智。一者、如理作意所發，二者、三摩地所發。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, there are two kinds of genuine wisdom: the first is what is brought forth by proper reflection; the second is what is brought forth by concentration.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Genuine wisdom is the wisdom by which a sage penetrates the true character of phenomena — impermanence, suffering, emptiness, non-self — knowing that every conditioned formation is impermanent, every contaminated dharma is suffering, and every dharma, conditioned or unconditioned, is without self. Two causes give rise to it: proper reflection born of hearing and thinking, and concentration born of cultivated meditative insight, the latter carrying a stronger power to cut off affliction. Both kinds now follow in turn, beginning with the first.

51. Section: Wisdom Born of Proper Reflection, Generally Stated

One should know that here, through attention accomplished by proper hearing and reflection, by the empowering force of hearing the true teaching, one comes to truly know the danger arising from the five feelings' shifting phases; and, reflecting properly on these very shifting phases, this is called the genuine right wisdom of the not-yet-settled stage. Taking this as one's support, one is able to enter progressively into cultivation.

Chinese Source

當知此中，由正聞思所成作意，聽聞正法增上力故，於五種受分位轉變所起過患如實了知，又即於此分位轉變如理思惟，名不定地如實正智。此為依止，能隨入修。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, through attention accomplished by proper hearing and reflection, by the empowering force of hearing the true teaching, one comes to truly know the danger arising from the five feelings' shifting phases; and, reflecting properly on these very shifting phases, this is called the genuine right wisdom of the not-yet-settled stage. Taking this as one's support, one is able to enter progressively into cultivation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Grounded in hearing and reflecting on the true teaching, a practitioner comes to know the danger inherent in every shift of feeling, so that one no longer indulges pleasant feeling or resents painful feeling, seeing their common mark as suffering — this is genuine right wisdom still at the scattered-mind, not-yet-settled stage, serving as the support from which cultivation proper is entered. The exposition of wisdom born of concentration, and of what further belongs to wisdom born of proper reflection, continues next.

52. Section: The Danger in Feeling's Shifting Phases, Explained

What is called the danger arising from the shifting of phases? It refers to the impermanent nature present in both the painful and the pleasant phase — in the painful phase there is the nature of pain itself; in the pleasant phase there is the nature of what is subject to change and decay.

Chinese Source

云何名為分位轉變所起過患？謂苦樂位諸無常性，苦分位中有自性苦性，樂分位中有變壞法性。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is called the danger arising from the shifting of phases? It refers to the impermanent nature present in both the painful and the pleasant phase — in the painful phase there is the nature of pain itself; in the pleasant phase there is the nature of what is subject to change and decay.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

Whatever the phase of feeling — painful, pleasant, or neutral — danger accompanies it: the painful phase is inherently painful, the pleasant phase hides the danger of eventual change and loss, and the neutral phase carries the subtler danger of ceaseless, unrestful flux.

53. Section: What "Shifting of Phases" Means

What is called the shifting of phases? It refers to the fact that the pleasant phase has a distinct nature from the painful phase, and the painful phase has a distinct nature from the pleasant phase — one should thus know that every phase, turning one into another, is mutually distinct.

Chinese Source

云何名為分位轉變？謂樂分位與苦分位有別異性，若苦分位與樂分位有別異性，如是當知一切分位展轉別異。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is called the shifting of phases? It refers to the fact that the pleasant phase has a distinct nature from the painful phase, and the painful phase has a distinct nature from the pleasant phase — one should thus know that every phase, turning one into another, is mutually distinct.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Only pleasure and pain are named here, since the neutral feeling passes unnoticed by most, while pleasure and pain contrast sharply — but every phase of feeling, without exception, shifts and differs from what came before, impermanent and never fixed.

54. Section: Genuine Wisdom Confirmed, Closing "Wisdom Born of Proper Reflection" Entirely

Seeing this distinctness as it really is, one dwells in the perception of impermanence with respect to these phases; seeing as it really is the danger of this distinctness, and knowing that all feeling is suffering, one dwells in the perception of suffering. Having such a perception, having such a view, one is able to attain purity — hence this too is called genuine wisdom.

Chinese Source

於此別異如實觀見，於此分位住無常想，如實觀見別異過患，知所有受皆是苦已，住於苦想。有如是想、有如是見，能證清淨，是故亦得名如實智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seeing this distinctness as it really is, one dwells in the perception of impermanence with respect to these phases; seeing as it really is the danger of this distinctness, and knowing that all feeling is suffering, one dwells in the perception of suffering. Having such a perception, having such a view, one is able to attain purity — hence this too is called genuine wisdom.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Even a scattered-mind practitioner who is not of especially sharp faculties can, through this right view born of hearing and reflection on impermanence and suffering, be said to possess genuine wisdom, already set apart from an ordinary being's outlook. With this, wisdom born of proper reflection (如理作意所發) closes entirely.

55. Section: Wisdom Born of Concentration, Closing "Bringing Forth the Kinds" Entirely

As for genuine wisdom brought forth in dependence on concentration: it is precisely that, when turning in dependence on that very mode, it turns subsumed under tranquility, pure, undisturbed, and quiescent. One should know that this practice is distinct from what came before.

Chinese Source

依定所發如實智者，謂即依彼行相轉時，輕安所攝清淨無擾寂靜而轉。當知此行與前差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for genuine wisdom brought forth in dependence on concentration: it is precisely that, when turning in dependence on that very mode, it turns subsumed under tranquility, pure, undisturbed, and quiescent. One should know that this practice is distinct from what came before.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Both kinds of wisdom turn on the same marks of impermanence and suffering, but the scattered mind's hearing-and-reflection wisdom lacks tranquility and remains liable to lapses of mindfulness, whereas concentration-produced wisdom is stable, pure, and quiescent throughout. With this, bringing forth the kinds (出種類) — both wisdom born of reflection and wisdom born of concentration — closes entirely.

56. Section: Displaying the Surpassing Capacity, Closing "Wisdom" Entirely

Further, the nature of impermanence is the common mark of every formation; the nature of suffering is the common mark of every contaminated dharma. Taking these two kinds of genuine wisdom as one's support, one should know that one is able truly and rightly to reveal these two marks of all dharmas.

Chinese Source

又無常性是一切行共相，苦性是一切有漏法共相。二如實智為依止故，當知如實能正顯了彼法二相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the nature of impermanence is the common mark of every formation; the nature of suffering is the common mark of every contaminated dharma. Taking these two kinds of genuine wisdom as one's support, one should know that one is able truly and rightly to reveal these two marks of all dharmas.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing Saṃyukta Āgama sūtras 77-80

Uncontaminated conditioned dharmas, such as the noble path, are impermanent yet not suffering, since the path brings quiescent joy — so impermanence alone marks every formation, while suffering marks only what is contaminated. Four companion sūtras illustrate this: the first pair, emphasizing reflection, show a monk (and then all the monks) rightly knowing the six faculties, objects, consciousnesses, contacts, and feelings as impermanent and suffering, thereby growing weary, released, and attaining the arhat's self-attestation; the second pair, emphasizing concentration, show the Buddha teaching the monks to cultivate the nine mental abidings and immeasurable concentrations so that, with the mind stilled, this same genuine wisdom of impermanence and suffering manifests of itself with even greater clarity. With this, Wisdom (智), the second of the ten topics under Wisdom's particular verse, closes entirely.

57. Section: Concentration Begins — the Inner Teaching and Outsiders Distinguished

Next, one who abides in the inner teaching, even without having attained a concentrated mind, is still distinguished from an outsider's concentrated mind, since wisdom is what surpasses — how much more so with a concentrated mind.

Chinese Source

復次，住內法者未得定心，尚與外道定心差別，由智勝故，何況定心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, one who abides in the inner teaching, even without having attained a concentrated mind, is still distinguished from an outsider's concentrated mind, since wisdom is what surpasses — how much more so with a concentrated mind.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens Concentration (定), the third of Wisdom's ten topics — a Buddhist practitioner without concentration already surpasses a concentrated outsider, because of the surpassing wisdom born of proper reflection and, more so still, of concentration.

58. Section: The Outsider's Fault Named

Why is this? Those outsiders, though they attain a concentrated mind, even attaining at the furthest extreme the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception, are still unable truly to know the six contact-bases through their five modes, unable rightly to leave desire, and unable to attain liberation.

Chinese Source

何以故？彼諸外道雖得定心，乃至極遠證得非想非非想定，然猶未能於六觸處，以其五轉如實了知，心正離欲，證得解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this? Those outsiders, though they attain a concentrated mind, even attaining at the furthest extreme the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception, are still unable truly to know the six contact-bases through their five modes, unable rightly to leave desire, and unable to attain liberation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

"Their five modes" refers back to the five shifting phases of feeling explained earlier — even the highest worldly concentration, the peak of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, cannot substitute for truly knowing feeling's danger; without that knowing, craving is not uprooted and liberation is not reached.

59. Section: The Difference Displayed, Closing "Outsiders" Entirely

For this reason, they and this true teaching and discipline are as far apart as earth from sky.

Chinese Source

是故彼與此正法律，猶如地空，相去極遠。

Ru-Yi Translation

For this reason, they and this true teaching and discipline are as far apart as earth from sky.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The true teaching and discipline is as lofty as the sky; the outsiders' path, however elevated it may seem, remains as low as the earth by comparison. With this, the outsider category (外道攝) closes entirely.

60. Section: The Inner Teaching Begins — Marking Its Basis in Resolved Understanding

One who abides in the inner teaching, though not yet having attained concentration, is nonetheless, through faith in and hearing of the resolved understanding of non-self, able to attain a mind of samādhi, able to cut off and to know at the six contact-bases, gaining a mind free of desire and realizing liberation. One should therefore know that, with respect to the true teaching and discipline, the outsiders suffer loss and failure, while this one does not.

Chinese Source

住內法者雖未得定，但由信聞無我勝解，便能證得三摩地心，於六觸處能斷、能知，心得離欲及證解脫。是故當知，於正法律彼有失壞，此無失壞。

Ru-Yi Translation

One who abides in the inner teaching, though not yet having attained concentration, is nonetheless, through faith in and hearing of the resolved understanding of non-self, able to attain a mind of samādhi, able to cut off and to know at the six contact-bases, gaining a mind free of desire and realizing liberation. One should therefore know that, with respect to the true teaching and discipline, the outsiders suffer loss and failure, while this one does not.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Through faith, hearing, and inward reflection on non-self, a practitioner comes to understand the causes and conditions of defilement and purity and the sequence of the path, and thereby, without being turned by inner affliction, attains samādhi, penetrates non-self, and cuts off the craving arising from the six contact-bases' feelings — free of the loss that afflicts one lacking this right view.

61. Section: Its Subtle Conduct Begins — Five Kinds Named

Only when this right resolved understanding turns in continuous succession is there, with respect to the six kinds of objects, in dependence on the six faculties, briefly speaking, five kinds of quiescent, subtle conduct.

Chinese Source

唯正勝解相續轉時，於六境界，依止六根，略有五種寂靜妙行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Only when this right resolved understanding turns in continuous succession is there, with respect to the six kinds of objects, in dependence on the six faculties, briefly speaking, five kinds of quiescent, subtle conduct.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

When resolved understanding of non-self remains continuously present as the six faculties meet the six objects, five subtle modes of quiescent conduct follow — modes unknown to any outsider or ordinary being lacking this understanding.

62. Section: Skillful Regulation

That is, because one sees deeply the danger in those objects, this is called skillful regulation.

Chinese Source

謂深於彼見過患故，名為善調。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, because one sees deeply the danger in those objects, this is called skillful regulation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Knowing deeply how contact gives rise to feeling and feeling to craving, and understanding impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self, such a practitioner skillfully regulates affliction rather than being turned by it — a wise person is not easily afflicted, and even when affliction arises, it quickly passes.

63. Section: Skillful Covering

That is, because one does not drive the faculties toward objects that ought not to be pursued, this is called skillful covering.

Chinese Source

於不應役諸境界中而不役故，名為善覆。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, because one does not drive the faculties toward objects that ought not to be pursued, this is called skillful covering.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Like a tortoise withdrawing its limbs into its shell rather than reaching out toward the world, the practitioner does not drive the eye or any other faculty out toward objects it should not pursue — the scriptures' familiar example being a monk who does not gaze upon an attractive young woman while out on alms round.

64. Section: Skillful Guarding

That is, with respect to objects that ought to be pursued, or with respect to an object suddenly appearing before one, because one dwells well in mindfulness, this is called skillful guarding.

Chinese Source

於所應役諸境界中，或於率爾現前境上，善住念故，名為善守。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, with respect to objects that ought to be pursued, or with respect to an object suddenly appearing before one, because one dwells well in mindfulness, this is called skillful guarding.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Some objects, such as old age, sickness, and death, ought to be looked upon, since seeing them rightly gives rise to weariness with the world — one dwells in right mindfulness before such objects, and equally before any pleasant object that suddenly appears, neither pursuing nor resisting, but staying grounded in mindfulness whether the object is one to be sought out or not. Two further modes, skillful protection and skillful cultivation, remain to complete the five.

65. Section: Skillful Protection

That is, because one is able to cut off every affliction, this is called skillful protection.

Chinese Source

一切煩惱皆能斷故，名為善護。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, because one is able to cut off every affliction, this is called skillful protection.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Having seen the danger deeply, covered the faculties well, and guarded mindfulness well, the practitioner prevents every affliction from arising at all, or cuts off whatever does arise — this is skillful protection.

66. Section: Skillful Cultivation, Closing "Concentration" Entirely

That is, because one has already skillfully cultivated the perfected path, this is called skillful cultivation.

Chinese Source

已善修習圓滿道故，名為善修。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, because one has already skillfully cultivated the perfected path, this is called skillful cultivation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing thirteen companion Saṃyukta Āgama sūtras (81-93)

Skillful cultivation names the arhat's own attainment, the perfected path fully realized. With this fifth and final mode, the five subtle, quiescent modes of conduct (regulation, covering, guarding, protection, cultivation) close entirely, closing displaying its subtle conduct (釋其妙行), the inner-teaching category (內法攝), and Concentration (定), the third of Wisdom's ten topics, entirely. The lecture compresses its thirteen companion sūtras (81-93): the first four teach that past, future, and present eyes and the other five faculties are all impermanent — and, by substitution, suffering, empty, and without self — so a learned disciple neither longs for the past faculty nor delights in a future one nor clings to the present, thereby leaving desire; four more apply the same fourfold contemplation to the six internal sense-bases, four more to the six external objects. The final sūtra recounts a monk who, questioned by the Buddha, rightly sees eye-contact as neither self, nor what belongs to self, nor self dwelling within it — cutting the root of the view of self as a palm tree is cut at the root, so that it can never sprout again — and the same for the remaining five contact-bases.

67. Section: The Surpassing Begins — Its Supreme Settling Displayed

Next, in two places, the Tathāgata attains supreme settling-wisdom, able rightly to reveal and teach what transcends all pleasure and pain — this is not something attained by one who has not attained supreme settling-wisdom.

Chinese Source

復次，於二處所，如來證得勝安立智，能正顯說超諸苦樂，非不證得勝安立智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, in two places, the Tathāgata attains supreme settling-wisdom, able rightly to reveal and teach what transcends all pleasure and pain — this is not something attained by one who has not attained supreme settling-wisdom.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

This opens the Surpassing (殊勝), the fourth of Wisdom's ten topics. The "two places" are glossed as pleasure and pain, or alternatively as birth-and-death versus nirvāṇa, or the ultimate versus the conventional truth — every teaching of the Buddha's reduces to one of these two pairs, and only a Buddha's perfected wisdom of skillful designation, attained after fundamental wisdom, is able to establish teachings correctly with respect to both.

68. Section: Refuting the Wrong Understanding

If, among these, there is one who forms this understanding: "This great ascetic of the Gautama clan is without knowledge, without understanding — with respect to a world that is uniformly pleasurable, in order to make his disciples regard this pleasure as intermixed with manifold suffering and thereby grow deeply fearful, in order to make them transcend a pleasure-and-pain-intermixed attachment to the world, and in order to make them transcend pleasure and pain altogether, he expounds his essential teaching" — one should know that this understanding is a wrong notion, a wrong discrimination, a great wrong view.

Chinese Source

於中若有作如是解：此大沙門喬答摩種無知無解，於諸世間一向安樂，為令弟子謂此安樂間雜眾苦深怖畏故，為超苦樂間雜依附諸世間故，為欲超過諸苦樂故，宣說法要。當知此解是為邪想，是邪分別，是大邪見。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, among these, there is one who forms this understanding: "This great ascetic of the Gautama clan is without knowledge, without understanding — with respect to a world that is uniformly pleasurable, in order to make his disciples regard this pleasure as intermixed with manifold suffering and thereby grow deeply fearful, in order to make them transcend a pleasure-and-pain-intermixed attachment to the world, and in order to make them transcend pleasure and pain altogether, he expounds his essential teaching" — one should know that this understanding is a wrong notion, a wrong discrimination, a great wrong view.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This describes a class of eternalist outsiders, proponents of nirvāṇa-in-the-present-life, who cling to the enjoyment of the five sense-pleasures or to the four meditative absorptions as itself liberation, and who accuse the Buddha of needlessly frightening his disciples away from a supposedly uniform worldly pleasure. Such outsiders, unable to truly transcend the three realms' pleasure and pain, hold a great wrong view.

69. Section: Displaying the Right Teaching, Closing "Supreme Settling" Entirely

Yet the Tathāgata rightly knows that the world is sometimes uniformly pleasurable, sometimes uniformly painful, and sometimes an intermixture of pleasure and pain — but that all of it, without exception, is impermanent. For this reason, in order to enable his disciples to transcend the entire impermanent world, to transcend pleasure and pain, he expounds the essential right teaching.

Chinese Source

然其如來善知世間或一向樂、或一向苦、或雜苦樂，然彼一切皆是無常，是故為令諸弟子眾，超過一切無常世間，超過苦樂，說正法要。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet the Tathāgata rightly knows that the world is sometimes uniformly pleasurable, sometimes uniformly painful, and sometimes an intermixture of pleasure and pain — but that all of it, without exception, is impermanent. For this reason, in order to enable his disciples to transcend the entire impermanent world, to transcend pleasure and pain, he expounds the essential right teaching.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The Buddha knows precisely which realms bring uniformly bright ripening (such as the demon's palace or the first three dhyānas), uniformly dark ripening (the hells), and mixed ripening (the human, heavenly, hungry-ghost, and animal destinies of the desire realm) — yet since even the pleasurable ones are impermanent and their afflictive seeds remain unexhausted, pleasure inevitably gives way to pain, so the Buddha teaches disciples to transcend both rather than cling to either. With this, supreme settling (顯勝安立) closes entirely.

70. Section: Broadly Rightly Knowing Begins — Desirable Objects Named

By three marks, one should rightly know desirable things. That is, desirable things belonging to the future are called what is pursued. If they are desirable things belonging to the past, they are called what is dwelt upon in thought. If they are desirable external objects belonging to the present, they are called what is enjoyed.

Chinese Source

由三種相，應正了知諸可愛事。

Ru-Yi Translation

By three marks, one should rightly know desirable things.

Chinese Source

謂未來世諸可愛事，名所追求。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, desirable things belonging to the future are called what is pursued.

Chinese Source

若過去世諸可愛事，名所尋思。

Ru-Yi Translation

If they are desirable things belonging to the past, they are called what is dwelt upon in thought.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens broadly rightly knowing (廣應正知), the second matter of the Surpassing. Two of four kinds of desirable things are named so far — what is endlessly pursued in the future, and what is endlessly recollected from the past; two more, concerning the present, follow.

71. Section: Present Desirable Things, Closing "Naming the Objects" Entirely

If they are desirable external objects belonging to the present, they are called what is enjoyed. If they are desirable internal feelings belonging to the present, they are called what is indulged.

Chinese Source

若現在世可愛外境，名所受用。

Ru-Yi Translation

If they are desirable external objects belonging to the present, they are called what is enjoyed.

Chinese Source

若現在世可愛內受，名所耽著。

Ru-Yi Translation

If they are desirable internal feelings belonging to the present, they are called what is indulged.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Craving for desirable present external objects such as forms and sounds is called what is enjoyed; craving for one's own present six faculties, cared for and indulged at length, is called what is indulged. With this, naming the objects (舉境事) — pursued, dwelt-upon, enjoyed, and indulged — closes entirely.

72. Section: Explaining the Modes Begins — the Three Times and the First Mode

One should know that here, falling within the three times, there are four modes. First, with respect to the future; second, with respect to the past; third, with respect to the present.

Chinese Source

當知此中，墮於三世，有四行相。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, falling within the three times, there are four modes.

Chinese Source

一、於未來，二、於過去，三、於現在。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, with respect to the future; second, with respect to the past; third, with respect to the present.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Toward the future, one endlessly yearns for what is not yet had; toward the past, one endlessly savors what has already passed; toward the present, one indulges and clings to what is presently enjoyed.

73. Section: The Four Modes Explained, Closing "The Surpassing" Entirely

With respect to these modes, able to accord and enter accordingly, there is the mark of the delightful, the mark of what the mind takes pleasure in, the mark of the desirable form, and the mark of the peaceful form — one should know, according to what is fitting, that these are precisely the four modes: the delightful, the pleasing, the desirable, and the agreeable.

Chinese Source

於此行相能隨悟入是悅意相、意所樂相、可愛色相、平安色相，如其所應，當知即是可欣、可樂、可愛、可意四種行相。

Ru-Yi Translation

With respect to these modes, able to accord and enter accordingly, there is the mark of the delightful, the mark of what the mind takes pleasure in, the mark of the desirable form, and the mark of the peaceful form — one should know, according to what is fitting, that these are precisely the four modes: the delightful, the pleasing, the desirable, and the agreeable.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Compendium of Different Topics (攝異門分)

The delightful mark belongs to the future, pursued and longed for; the pleasing mark belongs to the past, dwelt upon in recollection; the desirable-form mark belongs to present external objects, enjoyed; the peaceful-form mark belongs to present internal feelings — a robust, satisfying vigor of one's own six faculties — indulged. The Compendium of Different Topics confirms the same threefold and fourfold breakdown at greater length. A companion sūtra illustrates the point through hell and heaven: hell-beings meet only undesirable, unmemorable, unwholesome objects at every one of the six sense-doors and so experience only sorrow and pain; heavenly beings meet only desirable, memorable, wholesome objects and so experience only joy and pleasure — yet since both conditions are impermanent, the Buddha teaches disciples neither to delight in pleasure nor to dwell in pain, but to transcend both. With this, the Surpassing (殊勝), the fourth of Wisdom's ten topics, closes entirely.

74. Section: Obstruction Begins — Marking Obstruction and Its Antidote

Next, for one diligently cultivating concentration, briefly speaking, all the desire possessed through two gates, two times, and two grounds is able to obstruct the three kinds of samādhi thereby brought forth. In order to cut off such obstruction, one rightly and diligently cultivates five kinds of antidote.

Chinese Source

復次，勤修定者，略由二門、二時、二地所有諸欲，於所引發三種等持能為障礙。為欲斷除如是障礙，正勤修習五種對治。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, for one diligently cultivating concentration, briefly speaking, all the desire possessed through two gates, two times, and two grounds is able to obstruct the three kinds of samādhi thereby brought forth. In order to cut off such obstruction, one rightly and diligently cultivates five kinds of antidote.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens Obstruction (障), the fifth of Wisdom's ten topics. This obstruction is specifically to concentration, not to the noble path itself — even a dull-faculty arhat, whose afflictive obstruction is already removed, may still be obstructed by this concentration-obstruction and so retreat from the present-life pleasant abiding, though never from the path already attained.

75. Section: The Two Gates of Obstruction

One should know that here, desires previously enjoyed in the past, in a place of seclusion, through the gate of discursive thought, make the mind drift and waver; and further, present desires bound up with household gain and veneration, through the gate of discursive thought, make the mind scattered and disordered.

Chinese Source

當知此中，先所受用過去諸欲，於遠離處，由尋思門令心飄蕩；

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, desires previously enjoyed in the past, in a place of seclusion, through the gate of discursive thought, make the mind drift and waver;

Chinese Source

復有現在居家所有利養恭敬俱行諸欲，由尋思門令心散亂。

Ru-Yi Translation

And further, present desires bound up with household gain and veneration, through the gate of discursive thought, make the mind scattered and disordered.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

This opens explaining what obstructs (辨能障). Even one who has gone forth and dwells in a secluded, quiet forest place may still find the mind drifting back through discursive thought to desires once enjoyed before renunciation; a second gate is present veneration and material support offered by lay devotees, which likewise scatters the mind through discursive thought.

76. Section: The Two Times of Obstruction

Here, the desires bound up with gain and veneration make the mind drift and waver during activity; the household desires previously enjoyed make the mind scattered and disordered during stillness.

Chinese Source

此中利養恭敬俱行所有諸欲，於其行時令心飄蕩；先所受用居家諸欲，於其住時令心散亂。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, the desires bound up with gain and veneration make the mind drift and waver during activity; the household desires previously enjoyed make the mind scattered and disordered during stillness.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Gain and veneration distract the mind while going about daily activity, pondering how to receive, keep, or seek more of it; past household pleasures surface as scattering recollection while sitting still.

77. Section: The Two Grounds of Obstruction, Closing "Discerning What Obstructs" Entirely

These very desires are able to obstruct at the ordinary-being ground, and are likewise able to obstruct at the learner ground.

Chinese Source

即此諸欲，於異生地能為障礙，於有學地亦為障礙。

Ru-Yi Translation

These very desires are able to obstruct at the ordinary-being ground, and are likewise able to obstruct at the learner ground.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

These desires obstruct concentration for the ordinary being and equally for the stream-entrant or once-returner still cultivating concentration. With this, discerning what obstructs (辨能障) — the two gates, the two times, and the two grounds — closes entirely.

78. Section: Displaying What Is Obstructed — Three Samādhis Named

Further, the immeasurables-conjoined samādhi cultivated by ordinary beings can be obstructed; the vast samādhi able to penetrate every object of all-wisdom, cultivated by learners, can also be obstructed; and the ultimate samādhi extremely well cultivated by unlearners can also be obstructed.

Chinese Source

又於異生所修無量俱行等持，能為障礙；亦於有學能善通達一切智事廣大等持，能為障礙；亦於無學極善修習究竟等持，能為障礙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the immeasurables-conjoined samādhi cultivated by ordinary beings can be obstructed; the vast samādhi able to penetrate every object of all-wisdom, cultivated by learners, can also be obstructed; and the ultimate samādhi extremely well cultivated by unlearners can also be obstructed.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens displaying what is obstructed (顯所障). The ultimate samādhi names cessation-attainment, achievable by the liberated-by-wisdom arhat; while discursive thought over feeling and perception has not yet ceased, it cannot be attained.

79. Section: All Three Samādhis Accompanied by Joy

One should know that all such samādhis, however arisen, are accompanied by joy. Here, the first is accompanied by joy amid the intention to benefit and bring ease to sentient beings; the second is accompanied by joy through experiencing the joy of a learner's liberation; the third is accompanied by joy through experiencing the joy of an unlearner's liberation.

Chinese Source

當知如是諸所生起一切等持，皆與喜俱。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that all such samādhis, however arisen, are accompanied by joy.

Chinese Source

此中第一，於諸有情利益安樂意樂門中，與喜俱行；第二，領受有學解脫喜故，與喜俱行；第三，領受無學解脫喜故，與喜俱行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, the first is accompanied by joy amid the intention to benefit and bring ease to sentient beings; the second is accompanied by joy through experiencing the joy of a learner's liberation; the third is accompanied by joy through experiencing the joy of an unlearner's liberation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

As clarified earlier regarding the third dhyāna and above, this "joy" names not the literal feeling of joy but the pliant, at-ease quality of a mind strong in concentration and thin in affliction — the ordinary being's joy arising through the four immeasurables' benefiting intention, the learner's and unlearner's joy arising through their respective measures of liberation.

80. Section: Six Objects, Perfection, and the Ultimate Limit, Closing "Obstruction's Object" Entirely

These, owing to the distinction among objects known by the eye and the rest, further have six kinds. Further, because these samādhis are complete in every mark, they are called perfected. Further, the ultimate limit of these samādhis is that which is able to lead to worldly freedom from desire, or able to lead to transmundane freedom from desire — beyond this there is no further samādhi leading to purity to be found, and hence it is said that this is without deficiency.

Chinese Source

彼由眼等所識色等所緣別故，復有六種。

Ru-Yi Translation

These, owing to the distinction among objects known by the eye and the rest, further have six kinds.

Chinese Source

又此等持具諸相故，名為圓滿。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, because these samādhis are complete in every mark, they are called perfected.

Chinese Source

又此等持究竟邊際，謂能往趣世間離欲，或能往趣出世離欲，過此更無能趣清淨等持可得，是故說此無有闕減。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the ultimate limit of these samādhis is that which is able to lead to worldly freedom from desire, or able to lead to transmundane freedom from desire — beyond this there is no further samādhi leading to purity to be found, and hence it is said that this is without deficiency.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Basic Section's Level of What Is Accomplished by Cultivation

"Attaining samādhi" means attaining the not-yet-arrived concentration; "samādhi perfected" means attaining the four root absorptions; "samādhi self-mastered" means being at ease in entry, abiding, and exit, unobstructed by craving, view, or conceit — only with all these together is cultivation called perfected. The ordinary being's ultimate reach is worldly freedom from desire (the four dhyānas and four formless absorptions); the learner's and unlearner's ultimate reach, resting on such concentration to cultivate insight, is cutting off the three realms' craving and views, realizing nirvāṇa and cessation-attainment — beyond which nothing further remains. With this, displaying what is obstructed (顯所障) — the three samādhis, their joy, their six objects, their perfection, and their ultimate limit — closes entirely, closing the obstruction category (障礙攝) entirely.

81. Section: The Antidote Category Begins — Right Mindfulness and Right Knowing

If one wishes swiftly to attest to the fruit of a renunciant, one is without attachment to body or life, constantly and without interruption applying oneself with reverent effort, blazing with diligence. Amid desires, one knows their own mark and firmly holds right mindfulness; knowing their danger, right knowing free of any expectation is present before one.

Chinese Source

若欲速證沙門果者，於身命等無所顧戀，恆常無間、殷重加行，熾然精進。於諸欲中，了知自相，堅守正念；了知過患，無希望等正知現前。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one wishes swiftly to attest to the fruit of a renunciant, one is without attachment to body or life, constantly and without interruption applying oneself with reverent effort, blazing with diligence. Amid desires, one knows their own mark and firmly holds right mindfulness; knowing their danger, right knowing free of any expectation is present before one.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the antidote category (對治攝), naming its five methods, beginning with right mindfulness and right knowing, which counter the two gates of discursive thought: treating practice as the most precious use of a rare and fleeting life, one holds firm mindfulness amid the desirable, and right knowing of its danger prevents any further grasping after it.

82. Section: Non-Negligence

Taking right mindfulness and right knowing as one's support, one makes the effort to diligently cultivate the four modes of non-negligence — that is, whether walking or sitting during the day, purifying one's mind of every obstructive dharma, and so on at length.

Chinese Source

正念、正知為所依故，方便勤修四無放逸，謂於晝分若行、若坐，於諸障法淨修其心，乃至廣說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Taking right mindfulness and right knowing as one's support, one makes the effort to diligently cultivate the four modes of non-negligence — that is, whether walking or sitting during the day, purifying one's mind of every obstructive dharma, and so on at length.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Śrāvaka Level

Abiding in right knowing through all four postures — walking, standing, sitting, and lying down — is called the four modes of non-negligence, countering the desires that scatter the mind across activity and stillness alike; the Śrāvaka Level further details a wakefulness-yoga to be practiced before sleep, with right mindfulness, right knowing, a perception of light, and lying on the right side.

83. Section: Cultivating Calm and Insight — the Calming Portion

Having thus given rise to courageous diligence, one has no timidity or inferiority with respect to what is to be attested. By nine modes settling the mind, one single-mindedly cultivates the concentration of calming; the body attains tranquility, free of craving-taint and the rest, and hence free of defilement. One is not disturbed by the two secondary afflictions of torpor and sleep.

Chinese Source

如是發起勇猛精進，於其所證無所怯劣。由九種相安住其心，一向修習奢摩他定，身得輕安，無愛味等，故無染汙。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having thus given rise to courageous diligence, one has no timidity or inferiority with respect to what is to be attested. By nine modes settling the mind, one single-mindedly cultivates the concentration of calming; the body attains tranquility, free of craving-taint and the rest, and hence free of defilement.

Chinese Source

不為惛沈及以睡眠二隨煩惱之所擾亂。

Ru-Yi Translation

One is not disturbed by the two secondary afflictions of torpor and sleep.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

Through the nine mental abidings — inward abiding, equal abiding, repeated abiding, close abiding, taming, calming, utter calming, one-pointed focus, and even settling — moving from coarse to subtle, the practitioner first attains the not-yet-arrived concentration with bodily tranquility, free of craving, view, conceit, and doubt, and thus unlike the tainted concentration of outsiders.

84. Section: The Insight Portion, Closing "Distinct Cultivation" Entirely

Single-mindedly taking mindfulness-abiding as one's support, one diligently cultivates insight, firmly holding right mindfulness; because one is free from the secondary affliction of agitation, one is without delusion.

Chinese Source

一向念住為所依止，精勤修習毗鉢舍那，堅守正念，遠離掉舉隨煩惱故，無有愚癡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Single-mindedly taking mindfulness-abiding as one's support, one diligently cultivates insight, firmly holding right mindfulness; because one is free from the secondary affliction of agitation, one is without delusion.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Grounded in the four foundations of mindfulness, diligently cultivating insight into impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self, one remains free of agitation and thus of delusion. This describes calm and insight cultivated distinctly, at the beginner's stage; with this, distinct cultivation (辨別修) closes entirely.

85. Section: The Conjoined Path, Closing "Bringing Forth the Five Methods" Entirely

Having entered the path where calming and insight turn conjoined, one's mind rightly settled, these two aspects proceed together upon a single object.

Chinese Source

已入止觀雙運轉道，其心正定，即此二分一境隨行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having entered the path where calming and insight turn conjoined, one's mind rightly settled, these two aspects proceed together upon a single object.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

When calming and insight, sharing one and the same object, turn together in balance, stillness and insight present as one — this is called proceeding on a single object, explaining the conjoined path distinguished from the separate cultivation above. With this, bringing forth the five methods (出五法) — right mindfulness and right knowing, non-negligence, and calm-and-insight both distinct and conjoined — closes entirely.

86. Section: The Antidote's Power Displayed, Closing "The Antidote Category" Entirely

In order to cut off that obstruction, relying on cultivating these five kinds of antidote, one is able fully to know and permanently to cut off that obstruction. With respect to the three samādhis, across all the distinctions among the six kinds of objects, the joy-accompanied concentrations are thereby perfectly and fully drawn forth.

Chinese Source

為斷彼障，修習如是五種對治為依止故，能於彼障遍知、永斷。於三等持，依六境事所有差別，喜俱行定圓滿能引。

Ru-Yi Translation

In order to cut off that obstruction, relying on cultivating these five kinds of antidote, one is able fully to know and permanently to cut off that obstruction. With respect to the three samādhis, across all the distinctions among the six kinds of objects, the joy-accompanied concentrations are thereby perfectly and fully drawn forth.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Cultivating right mindfulness and right knowing, non-negligence, and calm-and-insight together fully knows and permanently cuts off the obstruction from the two gates, two times, and two grounds, so that the three samādhis of ordinary being, learner, and unlearner, across their six objects, are brought to full completion. With this, the antidote category (對治攝) closes entirely, closing explaining severally (隨別釋) entirely.

87. Section: Bringing Forth the Cause of Understanding, Closing "Obstruction" Entirely

By two causes and conditions, the Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, teach their disciples the dharma able to guide oneself: first, in order to generate understanding regarding every fault of the dark class; second, in order to generate understanding regarding every virtue of the white class.

Chinese Source

由二因緣，諸佛世尊為諸弟子宣說自己能引導法。一、於黑品所有過失令生解故；二、於白品所有功德令生解故。

Ru-Yi Translation

By two causes and conditions, the Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, teach their disciples the dharma able to guide oneself: first, in order to generate understanding regarding every fault of the dark class; second, in order to generate understanding regarding every virtue of the white class.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing a companion Saṃyukta Āgama sūtra

The dark class is the truth of suffering and its origin, the three defilements of affliction, karma, and suffering that keep beings turning in birth-and-death; the white class is the truth of cessation and the path, the excellent fruit of nirvāṇa and the means of cultivating it. The companion sūtra recounts the Buddha, before full awakening, observing in solitary meditation that his own mind mostly ran back toward the five sense-pleasures once enjoyed as a prince, and so guarding his mind with great effort — precisely the right mindfulness, right knowing, non-negligence, and calm-and-insight described above — until he drew near to supreme awakening; he then tells the monks to guard their own minds the same way, and, when contact at the six sense-gates gives rise to feeling and then affliction, to keep vigilant right knowing at the six sense-gates. Puzzled by his terse teaching that "if the eye ceases, the perception of form is left behind," the monks seek out Ānanda, who explains the standard dependent-origination chain in reverse — name-and-form ceasing because consciousness ceases, consciousness ceasing because formations cease, formations ceasing because ignorance ceases — so that ignorance's cessation is what truly ceases the six sense-bases. With this citation, bringing forth the cause of understanding (出解因) closes entirely, and with it, since all three of its own topics (marking obstruction and its antidote, explaining severally, and bringing forth the cause of understanding) are now complete, Obstruction (障), the fifth of Wisdom's ten topics, closes entirely.

88. Section: Learner and the Rest Continues — the Ordinary Being's Charge Named

If there is anyone who has not yet attained mind-liberation, such a one should firmly resolve to cultivate non-negligence.

Chinese Source

若有一切未得意者，彼應決定修不放逸。

Ru-Yi Translation

If there is anyone who has not yet attained mind-liberation, such a one should firmly resolve to cultivate non-negligence.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens 異生 (the ordinary being), the third of three practitioner-types under cultivating non-negligence (unlearner, learner, ordinary being) within Learner and the Rest (學等), the sixth of Wisdom's ten topics — one lacking either kind of mind-liberation should resolve firmly to cultivate diligence.

89. Section: Three Marks Accomplishing What Should Be Done

Further, by three marks, one accomplishes what should be done. First, because it is gathered and completed by the faculties, one's provisions are perfected; second, because one grows accustomed to and accords with lawfully obtained bedding and requisites, the mind attains settledness; third, because of the many causes and conditions of relying on and drawing near good persons, hearing the sound of another's teaching, and proper reflection, one comes, in due course, to attain the two kinds of mind-liberation.

Chinese Source

又由三相，辦所應作。一、由諸根所集成故，資糧圓滿；二、由習近隨順如法諸臥具故，心得安住；三、由依止親近善士、聞他法音、如理作意眾因緣故，乃至獲得二心解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, by three marks, one accomplishes what should be done. First, because it is gathered and completed by the faculties, one's provisions are perfected; second, because one grows accustomed to and accords with lawfully obtained bedding and requisites, the mind attains settledness; third, because of the many causes and conditions of relying on and drawing near good persons, hearing the sound of another's teaching, and proper reflection, one comes, in due course, to attain the two kinds of mind-liberation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Perfected provisions come from the wholesome roots of faith, diligence, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom together with a trained mind-faculty; settledness comes from being content with whatever requisites are lawfully obtained, free of craving; and the two mind-liberations come from relying on good friends, hearing their teaching, and reflecting on it properly, putting the Dharma into practice accordingly.

90. Section: What Should Be Known, Generally Stated

Further, precisely when engaged in this very work of non-negligence, by two marks one should know, with respect to the cessation of the six sense-bases, whether there is presumption or no presumption.

Chinese Source

又即於此應不放逸所作轉時，由二種相，應知於彼六處寂滅有增上慢、無增上慢。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, precisely when engaged in this very work of non-negligence, by two marks one should know, with respect to the cessation of the six sense-bases, whether there is presumption or no presumption.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

When cultivating the contemplation that the six faculties' cessation brings the cessation of the perception of their objects, a practitioner may wrongly presume the not-yet-attained to be attained — a danger requiring careful discernment.

91. Section: Presumption Explained — Its Cause and Its Fault

That is, with respect to what has not ceased, one gives rise to wrong discrimination, falsely grasping it as ceased, owing to the object; and with respect to what has not been attained, one gives rise to wrong discrimination, falsely grasping it as attained. Although one thus gives rise to wrong discrimination, claiming cessation and liberation, one is still unable to cause life to be forever ended and the six sense-bases forever ceased once the body has broken apart, nor is one able to leave every perception of objects.

Chinese Source

謂於未滅起邪分別，妄執為滅，由所緣故；及於未得起邪分別，妄執為得。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, with respect to what has not ceased, one gives rise to wrong discrimination, falsely grasping it as ceased, owing to the object; and with respect to what has not been attained, one gives rise to wrong discrimination, falsely grasping it as attained.

Chinese Source

彼雖如是起邪分別，謂滅解脫，而未能令身壞已後壽命永盡、六處永滅，亦不能離諸境界想。

Ru-Yi Translation

Although one thus gives rise to wrong discrimination, claiming cessation and liberation, one is still unable to cause life to be forever ended and the six sense-bases forever ceased once the body has broken apart, nor is one able to leave every perception of objects.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

Two marks of wrong discrimination are distinguished: clinging to a resemblance of emptiness as the object and mistaking it for genuine nirvāṇa is the first; mistaking a settled meditative calm for the attainment of the noble path itself is the second — in neither case has the practitioner actually escaped future rebirth or left every object-perception behind.

92. Section: Mistaken Feeling Named, Closing "Presumption" Entirely

Further, such a one, owing to a mistaken reception of feeling with respect to the six sense-bases' cessation, whether regarding the object or regarding attestation, has such a matter as this. These two marks, one should know, are said to be called presumption.

Chinese Source

又彼由於六處寂滅若緣、若證邪領受故，有如是事。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, such a one, owing to a mistaken reception of feeling with respect to the six sense-bases' cessation, whether regarding the object or regarding attestation, has such a matter as this.

Chinese Source

此二種相，應知說名有增上慢。

Ru-Yi Translation

These two marks, one should know, are said to be called presumption.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The root cause of both errors is a mistaken reading of one's own feeling — inner calm or a trace of joy misread as the mark of liberation itself. With this, presumption (有增上慢) closes entirely.

93. Section: No Presumption by Contrast, Closing "Learner and the Rest" Entirely

Contrary to this, one should know, is said to be called no presumption.

Chinese Source

與此相違，當知說名無增上慢。

Ru-Yi Translation

Contrary to this, one should know, is said to be called no presumption.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing a companion Saṃyukta Āgama sūtra

When the resemblance of emptiness is genuinely left behind, the mind free of discrimination, affliction truly ceased, one has genuinely attested to nirvāṇa and the path — this, contrary to presumption, is no presumption. With this, what should be known (顯應知) closes entirely, closing the ordinary being (異生) entirely, closing cultivating the mark of non-negligence (修不放逸相) — across unlearner, learner, and ordinary being — entirely, and closing Learner and the Rest (學等), the sixth of Wisdom's ten topics, entirely. The companion sūtra has the Buddha explain that he does not teach non-negligence to arhats already free of the three realms' craving and views, since they no longer act negligently at all, but does teach it to those still in training — learners and ordinary beings — precisely so they may fulfill the same three conditions just explained (complete provisions through the five modes of quiescent conduct and the three uncontaminated faculties; a settled mind through contentment with lawful requisites; the two liberations through good friends, hearing the Dharma, and proper reflection) and thereby, before long, attain the arhat's own self-attestation.

94. Section: Attachment Begins — the Dark Class Named, Generally Stated

Next, one who abides in the inner teaching should know two kinds of danger with respect to two kinds of attachment.

Chinese Source

復次，住內法者，於二種著，應當了知二種過患。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, one who abides in the inner teaching should know two kinds of danger with respect to two kinds of attachment.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens Attachment (著), the seventh of Wisdom's ten topics, beginning with the dark class (黑品) — the afflicted side of two attachments and the danger each brings.

95. Section: The Two Kinds of Attachment Explained

That is, ordinary beings, with respect to the two condition-consciousnesses and the feeling that depends on them, are unable to know their nature of non-self. One not yet free of desire has the first attachment, toward the feeling arisen from the empowering karmic condition of gain and veneration; one already free of desire has the second attachment, toward the feeling arisen from the condition of being free of desires.

Chinese Source

謂諸異生，於二緣識及能依受，不能了知無我性故。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, ordinary beings, with respect to the two condition-consciousnesses and the feeling that depends on them, are unable to know their nature of non-self.

Chinese Source

未離欲者，於利養恭敬增上業緣所起諸受，有第一著；已離欲者，於離諸欲緣所起諸受，有第二著。

Ru-Yi Translation

One not yet free of desire has the first attachment, toward the feeling arisen from the empowering karmic condition of gain and veneration; one already free of desire has the second attachment, toward the feeling arisen from the condition of being free of desires.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

Not truly knowing that feeling arisen from contact is without self or what belongs to self, a practitioner not yet free of desire clings to the pleasant feeling that comes from receiving gain and veneration, while one already free of desire, having attained concentration, clings instead to the pleasant feeling of the concentration itself — two distinct attachments, tied to two distinct stages of practice.

96. Section: The First Danger Explained

This attachment, as cause, gives rise to what is to come — this is called birth. Further, outsiders too, owing to grasping and attachment, generate bonds of entanglement; because entanglement is generated, it is able to generate every evil, unwholesome dharma. One should know this is called the first danger.

Chinese Source

此著為因，當來生起，說名為生。

Ru-Yi Translation

This attachment, as cause, gives rise to what is to come — this is called birth.

Chinese Source

又諸外道，由取著故，生諸繫縛；繫縛生故，能生一切惡不善法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, outsiders too, owing to grasping and attachment, generate bonds of entanglement; because entanglement is generated, it is able to generate every evil, unwholesome dharma.

Chinese Source

當知是名第一過患。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know this is called the first danger.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Clinging to the pleasant feeling from gain and veneration sows the seed of future rebirth, since craving is not thereby cut off; outsiders, likewise clinging, generate the fetters of craving, aversion, precept-attachment, and self-view from which every unwholesome dharma proliferates.

97. Section: The Second Danger Explained, Closing "The Dark Class" Entirely

Further, owing to the empowering force of this attachment, there will be, in the true teaching and discipline, decline, and, in the world to come, the manifold distinctions of birth and the rest, arising as suffering. In the present life, this empowering force, as cause and condition, prevents parinirvāṇa. One should know this is called the second danger.

Chinese Source

又由此著增上力故，當於正法毗奈耶沒，及當來世生等眾苦差別而生。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, owing to the empowering force of this attachment, there will be, in the true teaching and discipline, decline, and, in the world to come, the manifold distinctions of birth and the rest, arising as suffering.

Chinese Source

於現法中，此增上力為因緣故，不般涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the present life, this empowering force, as cause and condition, prevents parinirvāṇa.

Chinese Source

當知是名第二過患。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know this is called the second danger.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

One not yet free of desire who cannot relinquish gain and veneration acts carelessly and creates evil karma, causing the true teaching to decline and future lives of birth, old age, sickness, and death to continue; one already free of desire who clings instead to the pleasure of concentration itself, content with that alone, fails to press on toward the path and so does not attain parinirvāṇa even in this very life. With this, the two dangers close entirely, closing explaining severally (別釋) entirely, and closing the dark class (舉黑品), the first matter of Attachment, entirely — a second matter remains to be read before Attachment itself can close.

98. Section: The White Class by Contrast, Closing "Attachment" Entirely

Contrary to this, one should know, is precisely the distinction belonging to the white class.

Chinese Source

與此相違，應知即是白品差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Contrary to this, one should know, is precisely the distinction belonging to the white class.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing a companion Saṃyukta Āgama sūtra

One who has entered the inner teaching, even while still only at the stage of hearing-produced wisdom and not yet having subdued desire, constantly renews proper reflection and right mindfulness rather than losing oneself in gain and veneration; one already free of desire, having attained concentration, does not rest content with the joy it brings, but presses on to cut off affliction and attain nirvāṇa. Both together are the white class's distinction. The companion sūtra has the Buddha teach the "two dharmas" — the six faculties paired with their six objects — from which consciousness, contact, and feeling arise in turn; not truly knowing feeling's arising, ceasing, savor, danger, and escape sows the seeds of craving, aversion, precept-attachment, and self-view and so the whole mass of suffering, while truly knowing them prevents this and brings the mass of suffering to an end. With this, the white class (白品) closes entirely, closing Attachment (著), the seventh of Wisdom's ten topics, entirely.

99. Section: Non-Self Begins — Four Causes Marked

Chinese Source

復次，由四因緣，於法無我能到究竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, by four causes and conditions, one is able to reach the ultimate with respect to the non-self of dharmas.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens Non-Self (無我), the eighth of Wisdom's ten topics.

100. Section: The Four Causes Listed, Closing "Non-Self" Entirely

That is, all dharmas are without self because, apart from consciousness's own-nature, consciousness's causes and conditions, and consciousness's accompanying companions, nothing else whatsoever can be found; further, because this own-nature of consciousness is impermanent; further, because these causes and conditions are impermanent; further, because these accompanying companions are impermanent.

Chinese Source

謂一切法皆無我者，除識自性、識諸因緣、識諸助伴，其餘所有不可得故；又識自性是無常故；又此因緣是無常故；又此助伴是無常故。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, all dharmas are without self because, apart from consciousness's own-nature, consciousness's causes and conditions, and consciousness's accompanying companions, nothing else whatsoever can be found; further, because this own-nature of consciousness is impermanent; further, because these causes and conditions are impermanent; further, because these accompanying companions are impermanent.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing a companion Saṃyukta Āgama sūtra

A sentient being's life is nothing but consciousness's own-nature, its causes and conditions, and its accompanying mental factors turning together in combination — search as one might, no self can ever be found there, and each of these three is itself impermanent, arising and perishing moment by moment. The companion sūtra has the Buddha teach the same "two dharmas" giving rise to the six consciousnesses, and trace forward through contact, feeling, volition, and conceptualization — all impermanent, conditioned, and mind-dependent — as the basis for realizing non-self. With this, the four causes close entirely, closing Non-Self (無我), the eighth of Wisdom's ten topics, entirely.

101. Section: The Noble Path Begins — Generally Stated

102. Section: The Path-Factors Begin — Supremacy Marked

由五種相，當知八聖支道法最勝無罪。

By five marks, one should know that the dharma of the eight noble path-factors is supreme and faultless.

This opens explaining severally (別釋), first the path-factors (道支) themselves, whose five supreme marks follow in turn.

103. Section: Directly Seen and Unblazing

Because, with respect to affliction in this very life, one is able to well discriminate its presence or absence, this is called directly seen. Because it is able to cause affliction to gain release from bondage, this is called unblazing.

Chinese Source

謂於現法煩惱有無善分別故，名為現見。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because, with respect to affliction in this very life, one is able to well discriminate its presence or absence, this is called directly seen.

Chinese Source

能令煩惱得離繫故，名無熾然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because it is able to cause affliction to gain release from bondage, this is called unblazing.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Ordinary worldly people, lacking right view, cannot even tell whether affliction is present in their own minds; one with right view and right thought can clearly discern this. And where the path's wholesome dharmas of precept, concentration, and wisdom are present, affliction is freed from its bonds and its very seeds cut off, so that no blazing affliction remains.

104. Section: Timely and Guiding

Chinese Source

導涅槃故，名為引導。

Ru-Yi Translation

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Practice is bound to no particular posture and no particular hour — mindfulness and right knowing can be cultivated even while lying down — and the eightfold path, together with the whole of the thirty-seven factors of awakening, leads without exception toward nirvāṇa.

105. Section: Seen Only Here, Closing "The Path-Factors" Entirely

Because it is shared with no outsider path whatsoever, this is called seen only here.

Because it is shared with no outsider path whatsoever, this is called seen only here.

These profound teachings — directly seen, unblazing, timely, guiding, and seen only here — belong to the eightfold path and the thirty-seven factors of awakening alone; no outsider teaching reaches this depth. With this, the five supreme marks close entirely, closing the path-factors (道支) entirely.

106. Section: The Path's Fruit Begins — Named as What Is Inwardly Attested

Being free from believing another, free from a mode of delighting in another's path, free from all-around discursive inquiry, free from what arises merely from hearing, and free from a view settled through mere examination and forbearance — because it is attested only by oneself, it is called what is inwardly attested.

Chinese Source

遠離信他、欣樂行相、周遍尋思、隨聞所起、見審察忍，唯自證故，名內所證。

Ru-Yi Translation

Being free from believing another, free from a mode of delighting in another's path, free from all-around discursive inquiry, free from what arises merely from hearing, and free from a view settled through mere examination and forbearance — because it is attested only by oneself, it is called what is inwardly attested.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Compendium of Different Topics (攝異門分)

This opens the path's fruit (道果), the second matter under explaining severally. Attaining any of the four fruits — or, in Mahāyāna terms, any of the ten grounds up through Buddhahood — is a matter of direct inward experience: no longer trusting an outsider's teaching, no longer delighting in outsider views, free of the discursive inquiry that proceeds through language, free of the hearing-produced wisdom stage, and free even of the forbearance and resolve reached through hearing-and-reflection-based examination, since direct attestation surpasses and leaves all of these behind — a state where every word becomes idle speculation, known only to one who has tasted it. Five further marks of the path's fruit remain to be explained.

107. Section: A Further Five Marks Cited, Closing "The Path's Fruit" and "The Noble Path" Entirely

This dharma of the path's fruit likewise has five marks. One should know these are as already distinguished in the Compendium of Different Topics.

Chinese Source

此道果法亦有五相。

Ru-Yi Translation

This dharma of the path's fruit likewise has five marks.

Chinese Source

當知已如攝異門分分別其相。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know these are as already distinguished in the Compendium of Different Topics.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Compendium of Different Topics and a companion Saṃyukta Āgama sūtra (Venerable Pūrṇa)

The Compendium's five marks: transcending doubt about one's own attainment; transcending doubt about another's like attainment; not dependent on another, since direct attestation surpasses hearing and reflection; not swayed by any outsider doctrine, since one never wavers once genuinely attested; and fearlessness before any challenger, since one has truly realized impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self. The companion sūtra has the Buddha answer Venerable Pūrṇa's question on these very terms — directly seen, unblazing, timely, guiding, seen only here, self-realized — with a single example: seeing form with the eye and rightly knowing whether craving for form has arisen is directly seen; seeing form with right mindfulness and proper reflection, without craving arising, is unblazing, timely, guiding, seen only here, and self-realized all at once, exactly as it is with the remaining five faculties. With this citation, the path's fruit (道果) closes entirely, closing explaining severally (別釋) entirely, and closing the Noble Path (聖道), the ninth of Wisdom's ten topics, entirely.

108. Section: The Two Oceans' Non-Identical Portions Begins — Two Oceans Named

Next, there are two kinds of ocean. First, the water-ocean; second, the ocean of birth-and-death.

Chinese Source

復次，海有二種。一者、水海，二、生死海。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, there are two kinds of ocean. First, the water-ocean; second, the ocean of birth-and-death.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the Two Oceans' Non-Identical Portions (二海不同分), the tenth and final topic of Wisdom's ten. The water-ocean has water itself as its own-nature; the ocean of birth-and-death has the five aggregates' every formation as its own-nature.

109. Section: Three Marks of Difference Named

By three marks, one should know that the water-ocean and the ocean of birth-and-death are of non-identical portion. What are the three? First, because their own-nature is of non-identical portion; second, because their submersion is of non-identical portion; third, because their crossing-over is of non-identical portion.

Chinese Source

由三種相，當知水海與生死海而不同分。

Ru-Yi Translation

By three marks, one should know that the water-ocean and the ocean of birth-and-death are of non-identical portion.

Chinese Source

何等為三？

Ru-Yi Translation

What are the three?

Chinese Source

一者、自性不同分故；二者、淪沒不同分故；三者、超渡不同分故。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, because their own-nature is of non-identical portion; second, because their submersion is of non-identical portion; third, because their crossing-over is of non-identical portion.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens explaining (釋), unpacking each of the three marks named — own-nature, submersion, and crossing-over — in turn.

110. Section: Own-Nature Explained

Here, non-identical own-nature means: the water-ocean, taking one portion of form as its own-nature, is bounded and measured; the ocean of birth-and-death, taking every formation as its own-nature, is boundless and measureless.

Chinese Source

此中自性不同分者，謂水大海，用色一分為自性故，有邊有量；生死大海，用一切行為自性故，無邊無量。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, non-identical own-nature means: the water-ocean, taking one portion of form as its own-nature, is bounded and measured; the ocean of birth-and-death, taking every formation as its own-nature, is boundless and measureless.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The world's water-ocean is bounded by the water-element alone; the ocean of birth-and-death, comprising every formation of sentient existence, is a sea of suffering without any edge at all.

111. Section: Submersion Begins — Named, and Who Submerges

Here, non-identical submersion means: whatever submerges, and by what one submerges, and how one submerges — all are of non-identical portion. That is, into the water-ocean, either the animal destiny or the human destiny submerges; into the ocean of birth-and-death, even the heavenly worlds constantly submerge as well.

Chinese Source

此中淪沒不同分者，謂若所有淪沒、若由此淪沒、若如是淪沒，皆不同分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, non-identical submersion means: whatever submerges, and by what one submerges, and how one submerges — all are of non-identical portion.

Chinese Source

謂水大海，或傍生趣、或有人趣於中淪沒；生死大海，諸天世間亦常淪沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, into the water-ocean, either the animal destiny or the human destiny submerges; into the ocean of birth-and-death, even the heavenly worlds constantly submerge as well.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Desire-realm heavenly beings see water as beryl and never drown in it, and the form and formless realms have no water-ocean to fall into at all — but every being of all three realms, gods included, submerges constantly in the ocean of birth-and-death.

112. Section: By What One Submerges Into the Water-Ocean

Further, into the water-ocean, one submerges only by the body — not by speech, not by mind, not by craving, not by aversion, not by delusion, and not by the manifold suffering dharmas of birth and the rest. Here what is being taught is the three portions of karma, affliction, and their fruit — one should know their marks accordingly, in this order.

Chinese Source

又水大海，唯由身故，於中淪沒；不由語故、不由意故、不由貪故、不由瞋故、不由癡故、不由生等眾苦法故，於中淪沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, into the water-ocean, one submerges only by the body — not by speech, not by mind, not by craving, not by aversion, not by delusion, and not by the manifold suffering dharmas of birth and the rest.

Chinese Source

此中宣說諸業、煩惱、彼果三分，如其次第，應知彼相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here what is being taught is the three portions of karma, affliction, and their fruit — one should know their marks accordingly, in this order.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Body, speech, and mind are subsumed under karma; craving, aversion, and delusion under affliction; birth and the rest of suffering under their karmic fruit — the water-ocean claims only the first of these three, the body, while the ocean of birth-and-death, as the next line shows, claims all three at once.

113. Section: By What One Submerges Into the Ocean of Birth-and-Death

The ocean of birth-and-death, too, one submerges by the body — and likewise by speech, by mind, by craving, by aversion, by delusion, up through by the suffering of birth and the rest as well.

Chinese Source

生死大海，亦由身故，乃至亦由生等苦故，於中淪沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ocean of birth-and-death, too, one submerges by the body — and likewise by speech, by mind, by craving, by aversion, by delusion, up through by the suffering of birth and the rest as well.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Submersion in the ocean of birth-and-death comes by karma of body, speech, and mind, driven by craving, aversion, and delusion, and ripening in the suffering of birth and its train of consequences — the full three-fold defilement at once, unlike the water-ocean's single cause.

114. Section: How One Submerges Into the Ocean of Birth-and-Death — Two Causes

Renunciants, through false discursive thought, through false observation, through wrong discrimination arisen from themselves, give rise to manifold improper discursive thoughts that disturb the mind, and so constantly submerge in the ocean of birth-and-death. Further, the remaining outsiders, bound and entangled by the fetters of affliction, constantly submerge in the ocean of birth-and-death.

Chinese Source

諸出家者，由妄尋思，由妄觀察，由自所起諸邪分別，發起種種不正尋思，令心擾亂，於生死海恆常淪沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Renunciants, through false discursive thought, through false observation, through wrong discrimination arisen from themselves, give rise to manifold improper discursive thoughts that disturb the mind, and so constantly submerge in the ocean of birth-and-death.

Chinese Source

又餘外道，諸煩惱繫所纏繫故，於生死海恆常淪沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the remaining outsiders, bound and entangled by the fetters of affliction, constantly submerge in the ocean of birth-and-death.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Even one who has gone forth may submerge through the eight kinds of improper discursive thought — desire, ill will, harm, and the rest — arising from self-generated wrong discrimination; an outsider lacking right view submerges bound fast by craving and views. This is the first of two causes explaining how one submerges.

115. Section: How One Submerges — Two Further Causes

Householders, constantly and without interruption pressed by manifold suffering, afflictions blazing, unable to grow weary of it, are thus called submerged. The rest, relying on karma and affliction, pass back and forth without end among the places of rebirth, and are thus called submerged.

Chinese Source

諸在家者，恆常無間眾苦逼切，煩惱燒然，而不能厭，故名淪沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Householders, constantly and without interruption pressed by manifold suffering, afflictions blazing, unable to grow weary of it, are thus called submerged.

Chinese Source

其餘依止諸業、煩惱，於諸生處往還無絕，故名淪沒。

Ru-Yi Translation

The rest, relying on karma and affliction, pass back and forth without end among the places of rebirth, and are thus called submerged.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra

"Household life presses like a prison, and every affliction is born from it" — most householders, pressed by unrelenting suffering and burning affliction, remain unable to grow weary of craving's objects, still finding themselves drawn to sink into them; all other beings, relying on karma and affliction, cycle endlessly through the five destinies with no end in sight.

116. Section: Submersion Into the Water-Ocean, by Contrast

As for the water-ocean, though one falls into it, this is only a temporary decline. As with the animal destiny, born there through a portion of the power of karma and affliction, the submersion is temporary, not ultimate.

Chinese Source

其水大海，雖墮其中，暫時衰損。或傍生趣，由業、煩惱一分勢力，而生其中，暫時淪沒，而非究竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the water-ocean, though one falls into it, this is only a temporary decline. As with the animal destiny, born there through a portion of the power of karma and affliction, the submersion is temporary, not ultimate.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

A fish or other water-dwelling creature, born there through a fraction of past karma and affliction's power, submerges only until that fruit is exhausted, and may then be reborn as a human, in hell, or in a heaven — unlike the ocean of birth-and-death's submersion, which stretches back through beginningless time without limit.

117. Section: Summarizing, Closing "Submersion" Entirely

One should know this is called the distinction in submersion.

Chinese Source

當知是名沒不同分。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know this is called the distinction in submersion.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Taking together who submerges into each ocean, by what one submerges, and how one submerges, submersion's distinction (淪沒不同分) — the second of the three marks of the two oceans' difference — closes entirely. A third mark, crossing-over (超渡不同分), follows.

118. Section: Crossing-Over Begins — the Water-Ocean

Here, the distinction in crossing-over means: as for the water-ocean, ordinary beings not even yet free of desire and craving are still able to cross it — how much more so others.

Chinese Source

此中超渡不同分者，謂水大海，未離欲貪諸異生類尚能越渡，何況其餘。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, the distinction in crossing-over means: as for the water-ocean, ordinary beings not even yet free of desire and craving are still able to cross it — how much more so others.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the third mark, crossing-over (超渡不同分). Anyone at all, by boat or by swimming, can cross the ordinary water-ocean — a stark contrast with what follows.

119. Section: The Ocean of Birth-and-Death Established in Three Portions

The ocean of birth-and-death is established in three portions. For one not yet free of desire, owing to the distinctions among the five desirable objects; for one already free of desire, owing to the distinctions among the mind-known desirable dharmas; for the various learners, owing to the distinctions within the six internal sense-bases.

Chinese Source

生死大海三分建立。未離欲者，由五可愛境差別故；已離欲者，由意所識可愛諸法境差別故；諸有學者，由內六處有差別故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ocean of birth-and-death is established in three portions. For one not yet free of desire, owing to the distinctions among the five desirable objects; for one already free of desire, owing to the distinctions among the mind-known desirable dharmas; for the various learners, owing to the distinctions within the six internal sense-bases.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

One not yet free of desire easily submerges amid the five sense-pleasures; one already free of desire, resting in concentration, easily submerges instead in the subtler joy, pleasure, and equanimity of the meditative object; a learner — a stream-entrant through non-returner — still carries innate self-grasping toward the six faculties themselves, and so easily submerges in the ocean of the six internal sense-bases.

120. Section: Discerning Who Has Not Yet Crossed

Those ordinary beings not yet free of desire are unable to cross the ocean of the five desirable objects. Those ordinary beings already free of desire are unable to cross the ocean of the six internal sense-bases, each distinct. Because they have not crossed this, they have also not crossed the prior two oceans of objects.

Chinese Source

其未離欲諸異生類，於五可愛境界大海未能超渡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Those ordinary beings not yet free of desire are unable to cross the ocean of the five desirable objects.

Chinese Source

其已離欲諸異生類，於內各別六處大海未能超渡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Those ordinary beings already free of desire are unable to cross the ocean of the six internal sense-bases, each distinct.

Chinese Source

由彼於此未超渡故，於前二種境界大海亦未超渡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because they have not crossed this, they have also not crossed the prior two oceans of objects.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Level of What Is Accomplished by Reflection

The Level of What Is Accomplished by Reflection names six floods — the eye-flood able to see forms, up through the mind-flood able to know dharmas — flowing ceaselessly like a torrent and breeding affliction; permanently cutting the five lower fetters crosses the first five, and permanently cutting the five upper fetters crosses the sixth. Only the arhat cuts the innate self-grasping at the very root of the six internal bases; an ordinary being, with or without concentration, crosses neither the five-objects ocean nor the internal six-bases ocean.

121. Section: The Learner's Cause for Crossing

The learner, fully knowing the six sense-bases as suffering with respect to all of them, cultivates the right path upon that very object. Resting in such an abiding, the objects known by mind and intention become clearly present, with respect to both the not-yet-free and the already-free stage. Further, through vigorous, investigative attention, recollecting what was seen before, by this cause and condition one swiftly penetrates it with wisdom and is also able to remove it.

Chinese Source

其有學者，普於六處遍知為苦，即於所緣修習正道。彼由安住如是住故，於未離欲、已離欲地二種境界，所有心意所緣境相明了現前。

Ru-Yi Translation

The learner, fully knowing the six sense-bases as suffering with respect to all of them, cultivates the right path upon that very object. Resting in such an abiding, the objects known by mind and intention become clearly present, with respect to both the not-yet-free and the already-free stage.

Chinese Source

又由猛利觀察作意，於先所見等隨憶念，由此因緣，於彼速疾以慧通達，亦能除遣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, through vigorous, investigative attention, recollecting what was seen before, by this cause and condition one swiftly penetrates it with wisdom and is also able to remove it.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Having already cut acquired self-grasping, the learner still cultivates the four foundations of mindfulness against innate self-grasping toward the six sense-bases, and through vigorous investigative attention swiftly detects and removes any subtle craving, aversion, or delusion that arises, approaching the arhat's own attainment. With this, bringing forth the cause (出因緣) closes entirely.

122. Section: The Learner's Crossing Displayed, Closing "The Two Oceans" and Wisdom Entirely

Further, that one swiftly crosses the ocean of the six sense-bases; because one crosses it, one ultimately crosses the prior two oceans of objects as well. And one is also able to cross the affliction that gives rise to abandoning the training, the affliction that gives rise to a mind disturbed by discursive thought, the affliction that gives rise to indulgence in worldly gain and veneration, and the affliction that gives rise to every evil conduct.

Chinese Source

又彼於其六處大海速能超渡，能超渡故，於前二種境界大海畢竟超渡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, that one swiftly crosses the ocean of the six sense-bases; because one crosses it, one ultimately crosses the prior two oceans of objects as well.

Chinese Source

及能超渡能發棄捨所學煩惱、能發尋思亂心煩惱、能發耽著世間利養恭敬煩惱、能發一切惡行煩惱。

Ru-Yi Translation

And one is also able to cross the affliction that gives rise to abandoning the training, the affliction that gives rise to a mind disturbed by discursive thought, the affliction that gives rise to indulgence in worldly gain and veneration, and the affliction that gives rise to every evil conduct.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Level of What Is Accomplished by Reflection and a companion Saṃyukta Āgama sūtra cycle (sūtras 100-105)

Once the arhat's fruit is reached, one never again returns to lay life, is never disturbed by the eight kinds of evil discursive thought, never again craves worldly gain or veneration, and creates no further evil conduct — the five marks of homelessness, no hoarding, no expectation, cutting off desire, and solitary conduct. The companion sūtras have the Buddha distinguish the ordinary person's "great ocean" from the sage's: the eye itself is a person's great ocean, and form its crashing waves — one who can bear form's waves crosses the eye-ocean, escaping the whirlpool's undertow, the water-vermin of discursive thought, the rakṣasa of craving for gain and veneration, and the female-ghost of the ten evil deeds; the same holds for ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind against their own objects. The Buddha's closing verse: "The great ocean's mighty waves, the water-vermin and rakṣasa's dread — hard to cross, yet it is crossed; every seed of it forever gone." With this, the two oceans' non-identical portions (二海不同分) — own-nature, submersion, and crossing-over now all explained — close entirely, closing the tenth and final topic of Wisdom's ten-topic verse, and, with all ten topics (not-knowing, wisdom, concentration, the surpassing, obstruction, the learner and the rest, attachment, non-self, the noble path, and the two oceans) now complete, closing Wisdom (智攝), the second of 處擇攝's four particular verses, ENTIRELY.

123. Section: Sameness and Equality Begins — the Summary Verse

嗢柁南曰：道師不同分　王國二世間　有為遮身行　堅執三空性

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens Sameness and Equality (同等攝), the third of 處擇攝's four particular verses, naming eight topics to be explained in turn: the path's non-identical portion, the teacher's non-identical portion, the king's domain, the two worlds, the conditioned, restraining bodily conduct, firm grasping, and the three emptiness-natures.

124. Section: The Path's Non-Identical Portion Begins — Two Kinds Named

Briefly, there are two kinds of the path's non-identical portion. First, non-identical portion of own-nature; second, non-identical portion of mode.

Chinese Source

略有二種道不同分。一、自性不同分，二、行相不同分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Briefly, there are two kinds of the path's non-identical portion. First, non-identical portion of own-nature; second, non-identical portion of mode.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the path's non-identical portion (道不同分), the first of Sameness and Equality's eight topics — its own-nature as defiled or pure differs, and its contemplative mode differs as well.

125. Section: Non-Identical Own-Nature Explained

Whether it is the conduct tending toward the origin of suffering, or the conduct tending toward the cessation of suffering — this is called non-identical portion of own-nature.

Chinese Source

若趣苦集行，若趣苦滅行，是名自性不同分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whether it is the conduct tending toward the origin of suffering, or the conduct tending toward the cessation of suffering — this is called non-identical portion of own-nature.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The conduct that leads toward accumulating the five aggregates' suffering-fruit is the origin (集); the conduct that, through cultivation and attestation to the noble path, permanently ceases that very fruit is cessation (滅) — these two, opposite in own-nature, are the first of the path's two non-identical portions.
