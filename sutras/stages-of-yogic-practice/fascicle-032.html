Fascicle 32 · 瑜伽師地論卷第三十二 · The Third Yogic Process Concludes

The Five Marks and Near-Attainment Concentration

Fascicle 32 completes the third yogic process of the Ground of the Hearer-Disciples. It picks up cultivating mental engagement exactly where fascicle 31 left it — how a teacher guides a beginner to grasp five marks suited to his own affliction — and carries practice forward through near-attainment concentration toward the higher meditative absorptions.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: translation in progress Source: Taishō T30, no. 1579, fascicle 32 Commentary guide: Master Cháng Bǎi's lecture notes

1. Milestone: The Five Marks a Teacher Guides a Beginner to Grasp

Fascicle 32 opens exactly where fascicle 31 closed, detailing how a teacher instructs a genuine beginner: five specific marks to be grasped through seeing, hearing, or inward reflection, each serving a distinct role in cultivating the four mental engagements.

Chinese Source

謂善通達修瑜伽師，最初於彼依瑜伽行初修業者，如是教誨：善來賢首！汝等今者應依三種取相因緣，或見、或聞、或心比度增上分別，取五種相。一、厭離相，二、欣樂相，三、過患相，四、光明相，五、了別事相。

Ru-Yi Translation

A teacher well versed in yogic practice, at the very outset, instructs a beginner newly taking up yogic conduct thus: "Welcome, excellent one! You should now, by way of three conditions for grasping a mark — seeing, hearing, or empowered discernment through inward reflection — grasp five marks. First, the mark of revulsion. Second, the mark of delight. Third, the mark of danger. Fourth, the mark of brightness. Fifth, the mark of discerning the object."

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi maps the three conditions for grasping a mark onto the practical rhythm of daily practice: seeing and hearing typically happen away from the meditation seat — actually witnessing something loathsome or admirable in the world — while inward reflection through empowered discernment happens seated, deliberately reconstructing what was seen or heard as a vivid mental image. He previews how the five marks distribute across the four mental engagements already defined in fascicle 31: revulsion feeds taming the mind, delight feeds moistening the mind, danger reinforces revulsion's force, brightness supports generating pliancy (since a dull, dark mind cannot settle into genuine stillness), and discerning the object underlies purifying wisdom and insight, since insight requires first clearly identifying what is actually being contemplated. A teacher's first task, in other words, is simply making sure a beginner knows how to find each of these five marks at all — everything that follows depends on getting this foundation right.
