Fascicle 87 · 瑜伽師地論卷第八十七 · The Aggregates Section Continues

Cause, and the Branches of Cutting Off

Fascicle 87 continues the Compendium of Bases' aggregates section (行擇攝), covering the fifth through seventh of its eleven overall topics: cause (因攝), the branches of cutting off (斷支攝), and the two categories (二品攝). Cause opens with the shared and unshared causes giving rise to the five aggregates — delight as the shared cause common to both mind and matter, and contact, the immediately preceding cessation of mind, co-arisen mental factors, and the ten kinds of matter as the unshared causes proper to mind alone. This fascicle corresponds to the Saṃyukta Āgama's aggregates recitation, sūtras 56 onward.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress Source: Taishō T30, no. 1579, fascicle 87 Commentary guide: Master Cháng Bǎi's lecture notes

1. Section: The Aggregates Section Continues — Cause Begins

Within the Compendium of Bases: sūtra matters, the aggregates section, the first, part three.

Chinese Source

攝事分中契經事行擇攝第一之三

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the Compendium of Bases: sūtra matters, the aggregates section, the first, part three.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Fascicle 87 continues directly from fascicle 86, still within the Compendium of Bases' own explanation of the Saṃyukta Āgama. Fascicles 85 through 88 together comprise the aggregates section (行擇攝), and this fascicle covers the third portion of its eleven particular verses — specifically, cause (因攝), the branches of cutting off (斷支攝), and the two categories (二品攝). Swift penetration's own remaining sub-parts, left unfinished at fascicle 86's end, are not further covered by this lecture; the source material moves directly to the aggregates section's fifth overall topic.

Chinese Source

復次，嗢柁南曰：因勝利二智　愚夫分位五　二種見差別　於斯聖教等

Further, the summary verse says: cause, benefit, two kinds of wisdom; the fool's five stages; two kinds of view distinguished; and, regarding this, the noble teaching, and so on.

The aggregates section's fifth overall topic, cause, unfolds across six further parts: cause, benefit, two kinds of wisdom, the fool's five stages, two kinds of view distinguished, and the noble teaching — corresponding respectively to the Saṃyukta Āgama's sūtras 56 through 61.

3. Section: Cause Begins — Two Kinds of Cause Named

Every formation's cause has, in brief, two kinds. First, the shared cause; second, the unshared cause.

Chinese Source

一切行因，略有二種。一、共，二、不共。

Ru-Yi Translation

Every formation's cause has, in brief, two kinds. First, the shared cause; second, the unshared cause.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Cause explains the aggregates section's fifth topic, corresponding to sūtra 56 — the shared cause common to both mind and matter, and the unshared cause proper to mind alone.

4. Section: The Shared Cause — Delight

The shared cause means that delight is the leading cause; because of this delight, in this or that place of birth, one's weariness is obstructed, and one's own being is nourished. In order that one be born in the place one is to be born, although every affliction serves as a cause, it is birth in the place where delight arises that occurs — not birth where a thought of aversion arises. Further, this very delight is spoken of only in regard to form: once the prior cause has already given rise to it, it does not wait upon a further cause before ultimately turning into being.

Chinese Source

共因者，謂喜為先因；由此喜故，於彼彼生處障於厭離，滋潤自體。為欲將生所生之處，雖有一切煩惱為因，而於生處生喜者生，非於彼起厭逆想者。又即此喜唯依色說，宿因生已，不待餘因究竟轉故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The shared cause means that delight is the leading cause; because of this delight, in this or that place of birth, one's weariness is obstructed, and one's own being is nourished. In order that one be born in the place one is to be born, although every affliction serves as a cause, it is birth in the place where delight arises that occurs — not birth where a thought of aversion arises. Further, this very delight is spoken of only in regard to form: once the prior cause has already given rise to it, it does not wait upon a further cause before ultimately turning into being.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Notes (fascicle 1)

Delight for one's own being, seeded as a habitual tendency, obstructs weariness and nourishes rebirth in whichever place delight is felt — many afflictions may drive a being toward rebirth, but it is delight specifically, not aversion, that determines where birth actually occurs; a being bound for a hell of intense heat feels delight at the mere sight of a cool place in the intermediate state, and so is drawn there to be born, even though that place is itself the icy hell. This delight belongs to form specifically: once past karma has already been steeped in delight, entering a mother's womb, the next being's material form continues without needing to wait on any further condition beyond that seed.

5. Section: The Unshared Cause — Four Conditions Named and Explained, Closing "Cause"

The unshared cause means the touch that accords with pain, with pleasure, or with neither pain nor pleasure, in relation to feeling and the other mental dharmas; and the immediately preceding cessation of mind, together with co-arisen mental factors and the ten kinds of matter, in relation to the six kinds of consciousness. Although these arise from a prior cause, moment by moment they each still wait upon a further, distinct condition before they can arise.

Chinese Source

不共因者，謂順苦、樂、非苦樂觸，望於受等所有心法；無間滅意及俱生名、十種色等，望六種識。由彼雖從先因所生，剎那剎那別待餘因，方得生起。

Ru-Yi Translation

The unshared cause means the touch that accords with pain, with pleasure, or with neither pain nor pleasure, in relation to feeling and the other mental dharmas; and the immediately preceding cessation of mind, together with co-arisen mental factors and the ten kinds of matter, in relation to the six kinds of consciousness. Although these arise from a prior cause, moment by moment they each still wait upon a further, distinct condition before they can arise.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Notes

Unlike form's own single seed, ripening on its own into a fixed-length life, mind and its concomitants — feeling, perception, formation, consciousness — always need a further immediate condition moment to moment: contact (the six senses meeting their six objects, producing painful, pleasant, or neutral touch) gives rise to feeling, perception, and formation; the immediately preceding moment's own cessation makes room for the next; co-arisen mental factors accompany each moment of mind; and the ten kinds of matter (the five sense-objects and five faculties, plus the sixth mind-object) serve consciousness as its object. Delight's gathering gives form's gathering, and its cessation gives form's cessation; contact's gathering gives feeling, perception, and formation's gathering, and its cessation gives their cessation; name-and-form's gathering gives consciousness's gathering, and its cessation gives consciousness's cessation — cutting off delight, contact born of ignorance, and clinging to name-and-form together cuts off the aggregates' own suffering. With this, cause, corresponding to sūtra 56, is complete.

6. Section: Benefit Begins — the Arhat's Liberated Mind and Pure Wisdom and Vision, and Four Benefits Named

Further, there are arhats who possess a liberated mind, and who possess pure wisdom and vision. There are four benefits one should know are not shared with any outsider. First, a nature of constant abiding while acting. Second, a nature of signless abiding while dwelling. Third, formations produced by past causes spontaneously returning to cessation. Fourth, future formations, their present cause now cut off, no longer arising.

Chinese Source

復次，有解脫心、有淨智見諸阿羅漢。有四勝利，當知不與諸外道共。一、於行時恆常住性；二、於住時無相住性；三、往昔因所生諸行任運歸滅；四、後有行今因斷故，當不復生。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, there are arhats who possess a liberated mind, and who possess pure wisdom and vision. There are four benefits one should know are not shared with any outsider. First, a nature of constant abiding while acting. Second, a nature of signless abiding while dwelling. Third, formations produced by past causes spontaneously returning to cessation. Fourth, future formations, their present cause now cut off, no longer arising.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Notes (fascicles 9, 93)

Benefit explains cause's second topic, corresponding to sūtra 57. An arhat's liberated mind is free of greed toward any object encountered; pure wisdom and vision comes from having realized the aggregates as without self or what-belongs-to-self. In daily life outside formal meditation, the six constant abidings mean that seeing a form, hearing a sound, smelling, tasting, touching, or cognizing a mental object all leave the mind in supreme equanimity, without joy or sorrow, ever mindful and clearly comprehending — the first benefit. In meditation itself, dwelling in the signless truth of cessation without any mark of self, person, or being is the second. The third and fourth belong to nirvāṇa without residue: the present life's aggregates, fruit of past causes, spontaneously cease when the lifespan ends, and craving — the root cause of further existence — being already cut off in this very life, no future aggregates arise at all. The first two benefits mark an arhat's excellence within nirvāṇa with residue; the last two, within nirvāṇa without residue.

7. Section: Three Successive Stages Toward the Four Benefits

In order to realize such four benefits, there are three successive stages. Relying on the wisdom and vision of one still in training, one attains weariness — regarding the formations, no longer generating joy or pleasure, down to no longer generating indulgence or immersion, and so dwelling. Weariness comes first, and from it comes freedom from desire; freedom from desire comes first, and from it the mind is well liberated.

Chinese Source

為證如是四種勝利，有三漸次。謂學智見為依止故，得厭離者，於諸行中不生喜樂，乃至不生耽湎而住；厭離為先，而得離欲；離欲為先，心善解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

In order to realize such four benefits, there are three successive stages. Relying on the wisdom and vision of one still in training, one attains weariness — regarding the formations, no longer generating joy or pleasure, down to no longer generating indulgence or immersion, and so dwelling. Weariness comes first, and from it comes freedom from desire; freedom from desire comes first, and from it the mind is well liberated.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Weariness first appears at the path of seeing's first fruit; freedom from desire deepens gradually through the path of cultivation; the mind's complete liberation is realized only at the culmination, the path beyond training.

8. Section: The First and Second Benefits Explained

From this point onward, precisely through the mind's being thus well liberated and constantly abiding, there is neither favoring nor opposing. Further, whether while acting or while dwelling, one no longer applies attention to any sign whatsoever, but applies attention and reflects within the signless realm, dwelling without any sign. Every view and doctrine capable of obstructing this has already been permanently cut off — how much less could it now pose any hindrance.

Chinese Source

自斯已後，即由如是心善解脫恆常住故，無順無違。又於行時、或於住時，於一切相無復作意，於無相界作意思惟，無相而住。能障於此一切見趣先已永斷，況當為礙。

Ru-Yi Translation

From this point onward, precisely through the mind's being thus well liberated and constantly abiding, there is neither favoring nor opposing. Further, whether while acting or while dwelling, one no longer applies attention to any sign whatsoever, but applies attention and reflects within the signless realm, dwelling without any sign. Every view and doctrine capable of obstructing this has already been permanently cut off — how much less could it now pose any hindrance.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

An arhat meets favorable and adverse circumstances alike without joy or sorrow, ever settled in supreme equanimity — the first benefit fulfilled. And every one of the sixty-two wrong views an outsider might hold, rooted ultimately in self-view, has already been cut off, so the signless realm is entered without any obstruction at all — the second benefit fulfilled.

9. Section: The Third and Fourth Benefits Explained, Closing "Benefit"

Through these two, whether acting or dwelling, right up until the lifespan is exhausted, one then, through the inner nirvāṇa of one beyond training, attains parinirvāṇa; existence previously produced is now permanently exhausted, and future formations no longer arise again.

Chinese Source

彼由是二若行、若住，乃至壽盡，便以無學內般涅槃而般涅槃，先所生有於今永盡，當來諸行無復更生。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through these two, whether acting or dwelling, right up until the lifespan is exhausted, one then, through the inner nirvāṇa of one beyond training, attains parinirvāṇa; existence previously produced is now permanently exhausted, and future formations no longer arise again.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

When the lifespan ends, the fruit of past karma is entirely exhausted — the third benefit; and, craving and views having been permanently uprooted like a felled tree that can never grow again, no future aggregated body arises — the fourth benefit. With this, benefit, corresponding to sūtra 57, is complete.

10. Section: Not Shared With Outsiders Begins — Self-View as the Root of Every Wrong View, in Three Parts

Further, through three parts, one should know that the view of a perishable self is established as the root of every wrong view and doctrine. First, through operating together with the prior limit; second, through operating together with the later limit; third, through operating together with both the prior and later limits.

Chinese Source

又由三分，當知建立薩迦耶見以為根本一切見趣。一、由前際俱行故；二、由後際俱行故；三、由前後際俱行故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, through three parts, one should know that the view of a perishable self is established as the root of every wrong view and doctrine. First, through operating together with the prior limit; second, through operating together with the later limit; third, through operating together with both the prior and later limits.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Not shared with outsiders explains the two kinds of wisdom that follow, corresponding to sūtra 58, by first surveying every outsider view and its ultimate decline — sixty-two wrong doctrines in all, every one rooted in self-view, whether speculating about a past life, a future life, or the present moment (itself standing between the two, hence "both limits together").

11. Section: The Three Parts Explained Individually

Operating together with the prior limit means, for instance, one who reflects thus: did I exist in a past life, or did I not? Who was I? How did I exist? Operating together with the later limit means, for instance, one who reflects thus: will I exist in a future life, or will I not? Who will I be? How will I exist? Operating together with both the prior and later limits means, for instance, one who reflects thus: who was I? Who will be me? Where has this being now here come from? Once it has perished here, where will it go?

Chinese Source

前際俱行者，謂如有一，作是思惟：我於去世為曾有耶？為曾無耶？曾為是誰？云何曾有？後際俱行者，謂如有一，作是思惟：我於來世為當有耶？為當無耶？當為是誰？云何當有？前後際俱行者，謂如有一，作是思惟：我曾有誰？誰當有我？今此有情來何所從？於此沒已去何所至？

Ru-Yi Translation

Operating together with the prior limit means, for instance, one who reflects thus: did I exist in a past life, or did I not? Who was I? How did I exist? Operating together with the later limit means, for instance, one who reflects thus: will I exist in a future life, or will I not? Who will I be? How will I exist? Operating together with both the prior and later limits means, for instance, one who reflects thus: who was I? Who will be me? Where has this being now here come from? Once it has perished here, where will it go?

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Speculating "I was once a great monk of accomplishment" or consulting a fortune-teller about a past life as a high official are alike still self-view at root, contradicting the Buddha's own teaching of no-self and no-what-belongs-to-self — and not outsiders alone fall into this; ordinary people just as readily fill their speech with an unexamined "I," carried along by the very same innate self-view operating beneath the surface of every such thought, past, future, or present.

12. Section: Sixty-Two Wrong Views Named, Rooted in Self-View

Further, outsiders, taking the view of a perishable self as their root, hold sixty-two evil views and doctrines.

Chinese Source

又諸外道，薩迦耶見以為根本，有六十二諸惡見趣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, outsiders, taking the view of a perishable self as their root, hold sixty-two evil views and doctrines.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

All sixty-two wrong views trace to the same root: clinging to one of the five aggregates as self, and the rest as belonging to self. What follows catalogues them by which of the three limits — past, future, or present — each speculates about.

13. Section: Eighteen Wrong Views Regarding the Past Limit

Namely, four doctrines of eternalism, four doctrines of partial eternalism, two doctrines of no cause, four doctrines of a finite or infinite world, and four doctrines of eel-wriggling equivocation — these eighteen evil views and doctrines are held by those who speak of a self with regard to the past limit.

Chinese Source

謂四常見論、四一分常見論、二無因論、四有邊無邊想論、四不死矯亂論，如是十八諸惡見趣，是計前際說我論者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Namely, four doctrines of eternalism, four doctrines of partial eternalism, two doctrines of no cause, four doctrines of a finite or infinite world, and four doctrines of eel-wriggling equivocation — these eighteen evil views and doctrines are held by those who speak of a self with regard to the past limit.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Śrāvaka Ground (fascicles 6-7) and the Great Commentary

The four doctrines of eternalism arise from recalling past lives through weak, middling, or strong meditative concentration (recollecting twenty, forty, or sixty world-cycles back respectively, and mistaking a merely long continuity for permanence), and, fourth, from clairvoyance misreading a being's continued rebirth as an unchanging self. The four doctrines of partial eternalism arise from noticing that consciousness alone seems to persist unbroken across lives while everything else changes, or that a being recalls only their own death while imagining Brahmā himself as never dying, and so declares one part of existence permanent and the rest impermanent, in various combinations. The two doctrines of no cause arise either from being reborn out of the thoughtless heaven with no memory at all of a prior cause, or from merely noticing, without deeper reflection, that events sometimes appear suddenly without any traceable cause. The four doctrines of a finite or infinite world arise from clairvoyance surveying up and down, or side to side, or both, or from recalling the world's dissolution and finding nothing left at all — yielding in turn finite, infinite, both, or neither. And the four doctrines of eel-wriggling equivocation arise from outsiders who, unable to answer honestly about karma's fruit or the path to liberation, evade every direct question with deliberately confusing language — whether from fear of lying, fear of exposing their own ignorance, fear of being proven wrong, or simple genuine dullness — in the hope of thereby securing rebirth in a heaven they imagine to be deathless.

14. Section: Forty-Four Wrong Views Regarding the Future Limit

Further, there are sixteen doctrines of having perception, eight doctrines of no perception, eight doctrines of neither perception nor non-perception, seven doctrines of annihilation, and five doctrines of nirvāṇa in this very life — these forty-four evil views and doctrines are held by those who speak of a self with regard to the future limit.

Chinese Source

又有十六有想見論、八無想論、八非有想非無想論、七斷見論、五現法涅槃論，此四十四諸惡見趣，是計後際說我論者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, there are sixteen doctrines of having perception, eight doctrines of no perception, eight doctrines of neither perception nor non-perception, seven doctrines of annihilation, and five doctrines of nirvāṇa in this very life — these forty-four evil views and doctrines are held by those who speak of a self with regard to the future limit.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Śrāvaka Ground and the Great Commentary

The sixteen doctrines of having perception combine four views on whether self has form, no form, both, or neither, with four further sets of four on whether self is finite or infinite (by the scope of concentration attained), possesses one kind of perception or many (as in the formless absorptions versus the lower realms), a narrow or boundless perception, and experiences only pleasure, only pain, both, or neither — each combination still holding that the self, whatever its nature, retains perception after death. The eight doctrines of no perception similarly combine views of self as having form, formless, both, or neither, and as finite, infinite, both, or neither, but conclude that the self attains the thoughtless heaven and so has no perception after death. The eight doctrines of neither perception nor non-perception apply the same form/formless and finite/infinite combinations to those who, through or without actually attaining the formless absorption of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, conclude the self after death is neither perceiving nor not perceiving. The seven doctrines of annihilation hold that the self — whether a coarse physical body, or reborn in the desire heavens, the form heavens, or any of the four formless heavens — is utterly annihilated at death without remainder, an error born of mistaking the outer limit of one's own attained meditative vision for the outer limit of existence itself, like glimpsing the sky through a tube or touching one part of an elephant in the dark. And the five doctrines of nirvāṇa in this very life mistake the enjoyment of heavenly sensory pleasure, or dwelling in any of the four form-realm absorptions in turn, for nirvāṇa's own supreme purity, simply because each state, in the moment, feels blissful and untroubled.

15. Section: The Forty-Four Views Reduced to Five, and to Three

Such doctrines regarding the future limit are, in brief, gathered into five — first, the doctrine of having perception; second, the doctrine of no perception; third, the doctrine of neither perception nor non-perception; fourth, the doctrine of annihilation; fifth, the doctrine of nirvāṇa in this very life. Such five kinds are, further, in brief, three — first, the doctrine of eternalism; second, the doctrine of annihilation; third, the doctrine of nirvāṇa in this very life.

Chinese Source

如是計度後際論者，略攝有五。一、有想論，二、無想論，三、非有想非無想論，四、斷見論，五、現法涅槃論。如是五種，復略為三。一、常見論，二、斷見論，三、現法涅槃論。

Ru-Yi Translation

Such doctrines regarding the future limit are, in brief, gathered into five — first, the doctrine of having perception; second, the doctrine of no perception; third, the doctrine of neither perception nor non-perception; fourth, the doctrine of annihilation; fifth, the doctrine of nirvāṇa in this very life. Such five kinds are, further, in brief, three — first, the doctrine of eternalism; second, the doctrine of annihilation; third, the doctrine of nirvāṇa in this very life.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The first three of the five (having perception, no perception, neither) all still assert some form of self continuing after death, and so collapse together into eternalism; annihilation and nirvāṇa-in-this-life stand as their own separate categories. With the eighteen views of the past limit and the forty-four of the future limit together, the full sixty-two evil doctrines are complete.

16. Section: Six Causes That Establish Every Wrong View — the First Two

Further, all such evil views and doctrines are established through six causes and conditions. First, through the root cause; second, through reliance on a teaching; third, through reliance on meditative concentration; fourth, through reliance on the times; fifth, through reliance on other views; sixth, through the place of birth. Through the root cause means that all of them take the view of a perishable self as their cause and condition. Through reliance on a teaching means that they rely on a treasury of teaching that displays such views, wrongly transmitted from teacher to disciple in succession as their means.

Chinese Source

又此一切諸惡見趣，由六因緣而得建立。一、由因緣故；二、由依教故；三、由依靜慮故；四、由依世故；五、由依諸見故；六、由生處故。由因緣者，謂彼一切，薩迦耶見以為因緣。由依教者，謂由依彼能顯見趣不正法藏，師弟傳聞展轉相授為方便故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, all such evil views and doctrines are established through six causes and conditions. First, through the root cause; second, through reliance on a teaching; third, through reliance on meditative concentration; fourth, through reliance on the times; fifth, through reliance on other views; sixth, through the place of birth. Through the root cause means that all of them take the view of a perishable self as their cause and condition. Through reliance on a teaching means that they rely on a treasury of teaching that displays such views, wrongly transmitted from teacher to disciple in succession as their means.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Every one of the sixty-two views, however elaborate, ultimately traces to the same self-view; and every one is kept alive and multiplied across generations by being written down and formally taught from teacher to student, exactly the transmission the noble teaching (explained next) stands against.

17. Section: The Third Cause — Reliance on Meditative Concentration

Through reliance on meditative concentration means that, taking meditative concentration as a basis, one becomes fixed in a conclusion regarding what was formerly heard and formerly believed and understood. Further, this meditative concentration is, further, of two kinds — first, that which operates together with recollection of former abodes; second, that which operates together with an attained heavenly eye. That which operates together with recollection of former abodes means, within the three doctrines of eternalism regarding the past limit, the distinction of lower, middling, and higher purity, and also the four doctrines of a finite or infinite world — through recollecting the appearance of the container worlds' cycles of formation and dissolution as the means.

Chinese Source

由依靜慮者，謂以靜慮為依止故，於先所聞、先所信解而得決定。又此靜慮，復有二種。一、與宿住隨念俱行，二、與所得天眼俱行。宿住隨念俱行者，謂計前際三常論中，由下中上清淨差別，及於四種邊無邊論。由彼憶念諸器世間成壞兩劫出現方便。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through reliance on meditative concentration means that, taking meditative concentration as a basis, one becomes fixed in a conclusion regarding what was formerly heard and formerly believed and understood. Further, this meditative concentration is, further, of two kinds — first, that which operates together with recollection of former abodes; second, that which operates together with an attained heavenly eye. That which operates together with recollection of former abodes means, within the three doctrines of eternalism regarding the past limit, the distinction of lower, middling, and higher purity, and also the four doctrines of a finite or infinite world — through recollecting the appearance of the container worlds' cycles of formation and dissolution as the means.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Notes (fascicle 6)

Meditative concentration itself does not mislead — what misleads is a meditator's own unskilled reading of what concentration reveals, not yet grasping dependent origination, merely recollecting the past without genuinely understanding it, and so building a settled wrong view on top of real but misread experience. Recollecting the world's own dissolution, seeing nothing left in any direction, breeds the doctrine of no boundary at all; seeing a boundary in one direction and none in another breeds "both bounded and unbounded" or "neither."

18. Section: Recollection of the World's Formation and Dissolution — Three Wrong Ideas, and a Fourth

If, at the time, one recollects the stage of the world's formation, three false notions then arise. If one recollects only upward and downward, downward as far as the uninterrupted hell, upward as far as above the fourth meditative absorption, and recollects such a measure as the outer limit, one then abides in the notion that the world is finite. With this, the first kind of wrongly settled conclusion relying on meditative concentration — recollection of former abodes — is complete.

19. Section: Three Further Wrong Doctrines Said to Rely on Meditative Concentration

Further, relying on meditative concentration, one should know that a doctrine of partial eternalism, a doctrine of no cause, or a doctrine of eel-wriggling equivocation may in turn be spoken.

Chinese Source

復有依止諸靜慮故，當知或說一分常論，或說無因論，或說不死矯亂論。

Further, relying on meditative concentration, one should know that a doctrine of partial eternalism, a doctrine of no cause, or a doctrine of eel-wriggling equivocation may in turn be spoken.

20. Section: Two Kinds of Pure Heaven — Impure and Genuinely Pure

One should know that there are, in this regard, two pure heavens — first, impurely pure; second, genuinely pure. If one is able to enter only conventional concentration, one should know that heaven is impurely pure, since one has not penetrated the truths, and one's mind has not attained good liberation. If one is able to realize concentration according to the inner teaching, one should know that heaven is named genuinely pure, since one has already penetrated the truths, and one's mind has already attained good liberation.

Chinese Source

應知此中，有二淨天。一、不善清淨，二、善清淨。若唯能入世俗定者，當知是天不善清淨，於諸諦中不了達故，其心未得善解脫故。若能證入內法定者，當知是天名善清淨，於諸諦中已了達故，其心已得善解脫故。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that there are, in this regard, two pure heavens — first, impurely pure; second, genuinely pure. If one is able to enter only conventional concentration, one should know that heaven is impurely pure, since one has not penetrated the truths, and one's mind has not attained good liberation. If one is able to realize concentration according to the inner teaching, one should know that heaven is named genuinely pure, since one has already penetrated the truths, and one's mind has already attained good liberation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

An outsider may enter the four form-realm absorptions just as a Buddhist practitioner can, but without understanding no-self and the four truths, this is merely conventional concentration, suppressing affliction the way a stone presses down grass without uprooting it — impurely pure. Only the concentration of a non-returner, who has already cut off the desire realm's afflictions through penetrating the four truths and dwells in the five pure-abode heavens, is genuinely pure.

21. Section: Two Kinds of Freedom From Confusion, and Their Correspondence

One should know that freedom from confusion likewise has two kinds — first, signless and non-discriminated; second, possessing signs and discriminated. Here, the first is the genuinely pure heaven; the second is the impurely pure heaven.

Chinese Source

當知無亂，亦有二種。一、無相無分別，二、有相有分別。此中第一是善清淨天，第二是不善清淨天。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that freedom from confusion likewise has two kinds — first, signless and non-discriminated; second, possessing signs and discriminated. Here, the first is the genuinely pure heaven; the second is the impurely pure heaven.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary Notes

"Undying" names the great longevity of these heavens; "freedom from confusion" names being free of the disturbance of affliction. Only a non-returner reborn in the pure-abode heavens, having attained non-discriminating wisdom and able to enter the signless concentration, genuinely deserves the name; an outsider, affliction merely suppressed rather than uprooted, still carries signs and discrimination, and cannot enter the signless concentration at all.

22. Section: The Two Names Explained — Genuine Freedom From Confusion, and Confused Evasion

The former, pure heaven, turns without confusion regarding its own undying nature, and is therefore named undying and free of confusion. The latter, impure heaven, if questioned regarding undying freedom from confusion, then resorts to other matters, confusing the issue to evade the question.

Chinese Source

前清淨天，於自不死無亂而轉，是故說名不死無亂。後不清淨，若有依於不死無亂有所詰問，便託餘事矯亂避之。

Ru-Yi Translation

The former, pure heaven, turns without confusion regarding its own undying nature, and is therefore named undying and free of confusion. The latter, impure heaven, if questioned regarding undying freedom from confusion, then resorts to other matters, confusing the issue to evade the question.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

A genuine non-returner, having truly realized the four truths in uncontaminated absorption, understands the path of higher rebirth and the path of final liberation clearly and can speak of it directly. An outsider who merely claims the name "undying, free of confusion" while affliction remains uncut, and who fears being caught out, evades any direct question about the truths or the fruits of karma with deliberately confusing, evasive talk instead.

23. Section: The First Kind of Confused Evasion — Fear of Lying and Fear of Ridicule

Through being unskilled in the signless mind-concentration regarding the truths, one first entertains this thought, reflecting thus: "We ourselves claim to be undying and free of confusion, yet there may be others also undying and free of confusion, already skilled in the signless mind-concentration regarding the noble truths, whose accomplished virtue, compared to mine, is superior. If such a one should question me on this, and I were to answer, I might answer inconsistently, or deny what is real, or assent to what is not — and, seeing such faults in my own answer, I would think: I ought not answer any question put to me at all." Further, one sees a further fault in this: that another, discerning this, would thereby know one's own ignorance, and so, on this account, would mock one's claim to be undying and free of confusion.

Chinese Source

以於諸諦無相心定不善巧故，先興心慮，作是思惟：我等既稱不死無亂，復有所餘不死無亂，於諸聖諦無相心定已得善巧，彼所成德望我為勝。彼若於中詰問於我，我若記別，或為異記、或撥實有、或許非有，彼於記別見如是等諸過失已，作是思惟：我於一切所詰問中皆不應記。又於是中見有餘過，謂他由此鑒我無知，因則輕笑不死無亂。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through being unskilled in the signless mind-concentration regarding the truths, one first entertains this thought, reflecting thus: "We ourselves claim to be undying and free of confusion, yet there may be others also undying and free of confusion, already skilled in the signless mind-concentration regarding the noble truths, whose accomplished virtue, compared to mine, is superior. If such a one should question me on this, and I were to answer, I might answer inconsistently, or deny what is real, or assent to what is not — and, seeing such faults in my own answer, I would think: I ought not answer any question put to me at all." Further, one sees a further fault in this: that another, discerning this, would thereby know one's own ignorance, and so, on this account, would mock one's claim to be undying and free of confusion.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Secretly aware that only the Buddhist noble ones are genuinely undying and free of confusion, this outsider, dreading exposure as a fraud before being questioned at all, resolves in advance simply to refuse every answer, declaring the matter a sacred secret "not to be spoken, only realized for oneself" — the first of the four kinds of confused evasion.

24. Section: The Second Kind — Arousing Overweening Pride

One given to flattery and deceit thinks thus: "Regarding this, I should answer as follows: it is not that our pure heaven permits every hidden matter to be declared — namely, what one has personally realized, and the path of purity." Having thought this, one deliberately sets forth deceptive words to confuse the matter. Abiding in such wrongly settled thinking, pervading one's own mind, one, regarding that supreme pure heaven, thereby claims: I am undying and free of confusion.

Chinese Source

有行諂者作是思惟：我於此中應如是記：非我淨天一切隱密皆許記別，謂自所證及清淨道。如是思已，故設詭言而相矯亂。彼既如是住邪思惟，遍布其心，於彼最上清淨天所，故稱我是不死無亂。

Ru-Yi Translation

One given to flattery and deceit thinks thus: "Regarding this, I should answer as follows: it is not that our pure heaven permits every hidden matter to be declared — namely, what one has personally realized, and the path of purity." Having thought this, one deliberately sets forth deceptive words to confuse the matter. Abiding in such wrongly settled thinking, pervading one's own mind, one, regarding that supreme pure heaven, thereby claims: I am undying and free of confusion.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Rather than admit ignorance, this second type hides behind a claimed vow of secrecy and mistakes the mundane fourth absorption for the uncontaminated pure-abode heaven, declaring the claim outright despite having no real realization behind it.

25. Section: The Third Kind — Awareness Not Yet Settled

Harboring dread, one gives no answer at all, lest one's own inferior dullness become known to others. Through this cause and condition, one is unable to attain liberation, and, taking this very evasion as one's dwelling, settles oneself within it.

Chinese Source

由懷恐怖，而無記別，勿我劣昧為他所知。由是因緣，不能解脫，以此為室，而自安處。

Ru-Yi Translation

Harboring dread, one gives no answer at all, lest one's own inferior dullness become known to others. Through this cause and condition, one is unable to attain liberation, and, taking this very evasion as one's dwelling, settles oneself within it.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Having some half-formed but genuinely uncertain grasp of higher rebirth's causes and final liberation's truths, yet dreading lying, wrong view, and ridicule alike, this third type simply refuses to commit to any answer at all, making evasion itself a permanent home rather than resolving the uncertainty.

26. Section: The Fourth Kind — Feeble and Dull, and the Four Kinds Concluded

Further, there is one foolish and stubborn, devoted solely to the practice of calm, unable to employ flattering and deceptive means to confuse the matter, who simply thinks: whatever anyone comes to ask, I shall question them in return; whatever they then answer, I shall wholly and without diminishment affirm and accord with it. Through such differing calculations, four kinds are established.

Chinese Source

Further, there is one foolish and stubborn, devoted solely to the practice of calm, unable to employ flattering and deceptive means to confuse the matter, who simply thinks: whatever anyone comes to ask, I shall question them in return; whatever they then answer, I shall wholly and without diminishment affirm and accord with it. Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Too plain-spoken and mentally unsupple for clever evasion, this fourth type simply turns every question back on the questioner and echoes whatever answer comes back, avoiding ridicule by never actually committing to a position of their own. With this, the four kinds of eel-wriggling equivocation, and the third cause — reliance on meditative concentration — are complete.

27. Section: The Fourth Cause — Reliance on the Times, Regarding the Past

Through reliance on the times means that, relying on the past and the present to give rise to discrimination, this is named speculation regarding the prior limit.

由依世者，謂依過去及現在世起分別故，名計前際。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through reliance on the times means that, relying on the past and the present to give rise to discrimination, this is named speculation regarding the prior limit.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Wrongly discriminating on the basis of a past or present life is exactly what generates the eighteen views regarding the prior limit, explained earlier.

28. Section: Reliance on the Times, Regarding the Future

Relying on the future to give rise to discrimination, this is named speculation regarding the later limit.

Chinese Source

依未來世起分別故，名計後際。

Ru-Yi Translation

Relying on the future to give rise to discrimination, this is named speculation regarding the later limit.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This wrong discrimination regarding the future gives rise to the forty-four views already catalogued above. With this, the fourth cause, reliance on the times, is complete.

29. Section: The Fifth Cause — Reliance on Other Views, Three Views Named

Through reliance on other views means relying on three views, as should be known as before.

Chinese Source

由依諸見者，謂依三見，如前應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through reliance on other views means relying on three views, as should be known as before.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Śrāvaka Ground (fascicle 6)

The three views are: taking the life-force as identical with the body; taking the life-force as different from the body; and taking self as pervading both, neither dual nor deficient — combining both a material and an immaterial aspect into a single self, distinct from either view taken alone.

30. Section: The Three Views Mapped to Having Form and Being Formless

Through the first view, one speculates, within this present life, that self has form; later, this becomes either self having form and having perception, or having no perception, or neither having nor not having perception. Through the second view, one speculates, within this present life, that self is without form; regarding what follows, it should be known as before. Through the third view, there are two doctrines of self — first, saying self has form and is without form; second, saying self neither has form nor is without form. The rest is as said before.

Chinese Source

由依初見，於現法中計我有色。後或有色有想，或無有想，或非有想非無想。依第二見，於現法中計我無色。於後所計，如前應知。依第三見，我論有二。一者、說我有色、無色，二者、說我非有色非無色。餘如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through the first view, one speculates, within this present life, that self has form; later, this becomes either self having form and having perception, or having no perception, or neither having nor not having perception. Through the second view, one speculates, within this present life, that self is without form; regarding what follows, it should be known as before. Through the third view, there are two doctrines of self — first, saying self has form and is without form; second, saying self neither has form nor is without form. The rest is as said before.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The first view (life-force as body) yields self as having form; the second (life-force as different from body) yields self as formless; the third (self pervading both) yields the remaining two combinations — self as both having and lacking form, or as neither. Each of these four, combined with having perception, having none, or having neither after death, generates the sixteen and eight and eight views already catalogued.

31. Section: Narrow and Boundless, Yielding Finite and Infinite Self

Further, precisely regarding the doctrine that self has form, some speak of it as narrow, others as boundless; regarding self as formless, one should know it is likewise so. These two doctrines of self, relying on the third view, are established as two further doctrines — first, that self is narrow; second, that self is boundless. Through these four distinct doctrines of self, self is said to be finite, self is said to be infinite, self is said to be both finite and infinite, self is said to be neither finite nor infinite — following their proper order, as should be known as before.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Through these four distinct doctrines of self, self is said to be finite, self is said to be infinite, self is said to be both finite and infinite, self is said to be neither finite nor infinite — following their proper order, as should be known as before.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Notes

Self as having form, narrowly conceived, yields "self is finite"; self as formless, boundlessly conceived, yields "self is infinite"; self as both having and lacking form, narrow in one respect and boundless in the other, yields "both finite and infinite"; self as neither having nor lacking form, likewise mixed, yields "neither." Together with having-perception, no-perception, and neither, these generate the sixteen doctrines already catalogued in full.

32. Section: Self Claimed as Pure and Liberated, Concluding Reliance on Other Views

Further, relying on such views and on the doctrines of self, one further declares self to be pure and liberated, having mastery over both desire and meditative absorption — dwelling much in transformation according to one's wish, and abiding in absorption according to one's wish, playfully enjoying the expedient bliss of the dharma through this pure view. Thus is it named: through reliance on other views, one should know this is how such doctrines are established.

Chinese Source

又即依止如是諸見及依我論，復宣說我清淨解脫，於欲、靜慮皆得自在，隨其所欲，多住變化；如其所欲，安住靜慮。以清淨見，遊戲受用方便法樂。如是名為依諸見故，應知安立。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, relying on such views and on the doctrines of self, one further declares self to be pure and liberated, having mastery over both desire and meditative absorption — dwelling much in transformation according to one's wish, and abiding in absorption according to one's wish, playfully enjoying the expedient bliss of the dharma through this pure view. Thus is it named: through reliance on other views, one should know this is how such doctrines are established.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Śrāvaka Ground (fascicle 7)

Mastery over desire is an outsider's claim to freely conjure and enjoy any of the heavens' fine sensory pleasures at will; mastery over absorption is the claim to freely accomplish any of the four form-realm absorptions. Taking such mastery itself for nirvāṇa generates the five doctrines of nirvāṇa in this very life, already catalogued. With this, the fifth cause, reliance on other views, is complete.

33. Section: The Sixth Cause — The Place of Birth, Regarding Perception

Through the place of birth means self as having one perception, and so on, extensively explained. Having one perception means abiding in the formless boundless-space or boundless-consciousness base. Having various perceptions means abiding in a lower stage. Precisely as has been said, following their proper order, one should know this is spoken of as self having a narrow perception, self having a boundless perception.

Chinese Source

由生處者，謂我有一想，乃至廣說。有一想者，謂在無色空無邊處、識無邊處。有種種想者，謂在下地。即如所說，隨其次第，應知說我有狹小想、有無量想。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through the place of birth means self as having one perception, and so on, extensively explained. Having one perception means abiding in the formless boundless-space or boundless-consciousness base. Having various perceptions means abiding in a lower stage. Precisely as has been said, following their proper order, one should know this is spoken of as self having a narrow perception, self having a boundless perception.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Birth in the formless boundless-space or boundless-consciousness base yields the notion of a single, undivided perception; birth anywhere lower — the form realm and desire realm — yields the notion of many varied perceptions. The same two pairs, read by scope rather than number, yield narrow perception (for lower rebirths) and boundless perception (for the two formless bases) — together generating the sixteen doctrines of having perception already catalogued.

34. Section: The Place of Birth, Regarding Feeling, Closing the Six Causes and "Not Shared With Outsiders"

Being exclusively in pleasure means abiding in the lower three meditative absorptions. Being exclusively in pain means abiding in hell. Having both pleasure and pain means abiding among hungry ghosts, animals, humans, or the desire realm's heavens. Being neither in pain nor pleasure means abiding at the fourth meditative absorption and above, up through the base of neither perception nor non-perception.

Chinese Source

一向有樂者，謂在下三靜慮。一向有苦者，謂在捺落迦。有樂有苦者，謂在鬼、傍生、人、欲界天。有不苦不樂者，謂在第四靜慮已上，乃至非想非非想處。

Ru-Yi Translation

Being exclusively in pleasure means abiding in the lower three meditative absorptions. Being exclusively in pain means abiding in hell. Having both pleasure and pain means abiding among hungry ghosts, animals, humans, or the desire realm's heavens. Being neither in pain nor pleasure means abiding at the fourth meditative absorption and above, up through the base of neither perception nor non-perception.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Rebirth's own realm determines which feeling is generalized into a doctrine of self: the lower three absorptions' constant pleasure, hell's constant pain, the mixed pleasure and pain of the lower six realms, or the fourth absorption and above's neither-pain-nor-pleasure — together yielding the last four of the sixteen doctrines of having perception. With this, the sixth cause, the place of birth, and all six causes and conditions establishing the sixty-two evil views, are complete — and with them, not shared with outsiders, and the two kinds of wisdom, corresponding to sūtra 58, are entirely closed.

35. Section: The Fool's Five Stages Begins — Three Kinds of Decline Named

Further, one should know that, in all such places among outsiders, there are, in total, three kinds of decline. First, the decline of views and desired pleasures mutually contradicting one another. Second, the decline of unanswerable questions posed on the doctrine of self's own ignorance. Third, the decline regarding the realization of the dharma and what accords with the dharma.

Chinese Source

又於如是諸外道處，當知總有三種衰損。一者、見及欲樂展轉相違論衰損，二者、依我無智論問記衰損，三者、依法隨法行證得衰損。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, one should know that, in all such places among outsiders, there are, in total, three kinds of decline. First, the decline of views and desired pleasures mutually contradicting one another. Second, the decline of unanswerable questions posed on the doctrine of self's own ignorance. Third, the decline regarding the realization of the dharma and what accords with the dharma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The fool's five stages explains cause's fourth topic, corresponding to sūtra 59 — surveying the outsider's own three kinds of decline: wrong views compounded by the wish to score points off others or dodge hard questions; ignorance of no-self producing endless unanswerable speculation; and mistaking mere concentration for the actual realization of the path.

36. Section: The First Decline Explained

Here, the three — whoever holds the doctrine of having perception, or of no perception, or of neither perception nor non-perception, and also the doctrine of annihilation — either relying on rebuking another as victory, or relying on evading a hard question as victory, give rise to their speculation; one should know this is named the first decline. Because these doctrines, speculating on the later limit, wrongly conceive of self as existing or not existing with regard to the future.

Chinese Source

此中三種，若計有想、若計無想、若計非有想非無想論者，及斷見論者，或依責他為勝利論，或依免難為勝利論，而起計度，當知是名第一衰損。由彼諸論計度後際，依未來世妄計於我為有無故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, the three — whoever holds the doctrine of having perception, or of no perception, or of neither perception nor non-perception, and also the doctrine of annihilation — either relying on rebuking another as victory, or relying on evading a hard question as victory, give rise to their speculation; one should know this is named the first decline. Because these doctrines, speculating on the later limit, wrongly conceive of self as existing or not existing with regard to the future.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The having-perception, no-perception, and neither doctrines assert self exists after death; the annihilation doctrine denies it. Whichever side is taken, the underlying motive is often simply the wish to win an argument or dodge one — a decline in itself, regardless of which conclusion is reached.

37. Section: The Second Decline Begins — Ignorance of the Two Truths

The decline of unanswerable questions posed on the doctrine of self's own ignorance means that, regarding whatever is defilement, whatever is the place of defilement, and whatever is able to defile, one does not know as it truly is the principle of both truths, conventional and ultimate, in every such case; through this ignorance, having a direction toward which one is drawn as the leading factor, this comes to have its own distinctions.

Chinese Source

依我無智論問記衰損者，謂於若諸雜染、若雜染處、若能雜染，如是一切世俗、勝義二諦道理不如實知，由此無智有所趣向以為先故，得有差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

The decline of unanswerable questions posed on the doctrine of self's own ignorance means that, regarding whatever is defilement, whatever is the place of defilement, and whatever is able to defile, one does not know as it truly is the principle of both truths, conventional and ultimate, in every such case; through this ignorance, having a direction toward which one is drawn as the leading factor, this comes to have its own distinctions.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Merely attaining mundane concentration without penetrating the four truths, an outsider remains, in both the place of rebirth and the very mind that observes it, still defiled — neither conventional nor ultimate truth genuinely understood — and this root ignorance is what drives every further speculative wrong view that follows.

38. Section: Three Tetralemmas Named, and Why They Cannot Be Answered

From this ignorance, toward what is one drawn? Namely, three fourfold turnings. First, permanent, impermanent, and so on; second, finite, infinite, and so on; third, self-made, made by another, and so on. Why is this so? Because, drawn by ignorance, one is first drawn toward such distinctions, and only afterward comes the questioning and answering. Further, within the noble dharma and discipline, there are matters the wise do not answer, since neither of the two principles, conventional and ultimate, permits an answer to be given.

Chinese Source

從此無智何所趣向？謂三四轉。一、常無常等，二、有邊無邊等，三、自作他作等。所以者何？彼由無智，要先趣向如是差別，後方問記。又於聖法毗奈耶中，所有智者不可記事，於二道理不容記故，謂世俗、勝義二諦道理。

Ru-Yi Translation

From this ignorance, toward what is one drawn? Namely, three fourfold turnings. First, permanent, impermanent, and so on; second, finite, infinite, and so on; third, self-made, made by another, and so on. Why is this so? Because, drawn by ignorance, one is first drawn toward such distinctions, and only afterward comes the questioning and answering. Further, within the noble dharma and discipline, there are matters the wise do not answer, since neither of the two principles, conventional and ultimate, permits an answer to be given.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Compendium for Elucidation

The three tetralemmas: is the world (and self) permanent, impermanent, both, or neither; finite, infinite, both, or neither; self-made, made by another, made by both, or made by no cause at all. Whether the Tathāgata exists after death, for instance, is unanswerable in either register — not conventionally, since a noble one entering nirvāṇa without residue leaves no future aggregates to locate anywhere at all; and not ultimately, since nirvāṇa itself lies beyond words and mental grasping entirely, exactly as the Compendium for Elucidation's own verse affirms of suchness's uncontaminated nature.

39. Section: The Four Turnings Explained Individually

Here, the four doctrines of unqualified eternalism speculating on the prior limit, and the doctrine of no cause speculating on the prior limit — these two distinct kinds both first speculate on self, and only afterward, taking self as object, generate their various doctrines of unqualified permanence and the rest. Further, precisely the four doctrines of partial eternalism speculating on the prior limit have their own distinction — some take one part as permanent and one part as impermanent; some take one part as neither permanent nor impermanent. The doctrines of finite and infinite, and the rest, are as the finite and infinite doctrines already explained before — their mark should be known accordingly. If one wishes to hold that everything is made by a prior cause, this is named the doctrine of self-made. If one wishes to hold that everything is made by the transformation of Īśvara or another such cause, this is named the doctrine of made-by-another. If one wishes to hold that a small part is made by the transformation of Īśvara or the like, and a part is not so, this is named the doctrine of made-by-both. If it is the doctrine of no cause, this is named the doctrine of made-by-neither.

Chinese Source

此中四種一向常論計前際者，及計前際無因論者，二種差別，皆先計我，後方緣我一向常等諸論差別。又即四種一分常論計前際者，彼有差別，謂有一分緣常無常論，或有一分緣非常非無常論。邊無邊等諸論，如前邊無邊等，應知其相。若欲一切皆宿因作，名自作論。若欲一切皆自在等變化因作，名他作論。若欲少分自在天等變化因作，一分不爾，名俱作論。若無因作論，名俱非作論。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one wishes to hold that everything is made by a prior cause, this is named the doctrine of self-made. If one wishes to hold that everything is made by the transformation of Īśvara or another such cause, this is named the doctrine of made-by-another. If one wishes to hold that a small part is made by the transformation of Īśvara or the like, and a part is not so, this is named the doctrine of made-by-both. If it is the doctrine of no cause, this is named the doctrine of made-by-neither.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Self-made doctrine resembles the common fatalism of "everything is fate" — believing present effort accomplishes nothing since all was fixed by past cause; made-by-another attributes everything to a creator god's will; the remaining two mix or reject both. All four are simply the tetralemma of causation applied to self and world, generated the same way the permanence and finitude tetralemmas were. With this, the second decline, unanswerable questions born of ignorance regarding the two truths, is complete — arising from wrongly discriminating the past and present within meditative recollection.

40. Section: The Third Decline Begins — Mistaking Concentration for Realization

The decline regarding the realization of the dharma and what accords with the dharma means there are ascetics or brahmins who do not regard rebuking another as victory, nor regard evading a hard question as victory, nor rely on the doctrines born of ignorance of self, nor seek gain, honor, and such things, delighting only in expounding their views — who, within a wrongly taught dharma and discipline, seek to go forth solely from a wish for release and liberation. One should know these are of a nature with lighter defilement, but foolish and stubborn by nature, devoted solely to the practice of calm.

Chinese Source

依法隨法行證得衰損者，謂有沙門、若婆羅門，不觀責他為勝利論，不觀免難為勝利論，亦不依我無智諸論，為求利養恭敬等事，樂欲開闡，於惡說法毗奈耶中而求出家。唯除樂求出離解脫，當知彼是薄塵種類，為性愚戇，專修止行。

Ru-Yi Translation

The decline regarding the realization of the dharma and what accords with the dharma means there are ascetics or brahmins who do not regard rebuking another as victory, nor regard evading a hard question as victory, nor rely on the doctrines born of ignorance of self, nor seek gain, honor, and such things, delighting only in expounding their views — who, within a wrongly taught dharma and discipline, seek to go forth solely from a wish for release and liberation. One should know these are of a nature with lighter defilement, but foolish and stubborn by nature, devoted solely to the practice of calm.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Unlike the first two declines, this third kind of outsider practitioner is sincere — genuinely seeking liberation, free of the first decline's contentiousness and the second's speculative tangles — but, cultivating only calm without insight, lacking the wisdom to actually observe the four truths, remains vulnerable to a different mistake entirely: taking concentration itself for realization.

41. Section: Mistaking Each Absorption in Turn for Nirvāṇa

Through receiving instruction in order to attain the first meditative absorption, one is able, regarding the views operating together with the later limit, and the views operating together with the prior limit, to transcend them by not assenting to them; and, within this very life, one is further able to transcend the fetters of the desire realm, realizing the joy of seclusion. From this point on, hearing and knowing nothing further, one then generates the notion of nirvāṇa regarding this very state. Just as, through that, one realizes the joy of seclusion, so too, through other distinct conditions, one may realize the second and third absorptions' pleasure free of craving, and the fourth absorption's feeling free of pain and pleasure; from this upward, even up to the base of neither perception nor non-perception, one should know it is likewise so — since one has transcended the varied perceptions and the distinctions of painful and pleasant feeling operating together with them.

Chinese Source

彼由為得初靜慮定教授教誡，能於後際俱行見趣，及於前際俱行見趣，不然許故，而得超過；於現法中，又能超過欲界諸結，證遠離喜。自斯已上，無聞無知，即於此中生涅槃想。如由彼故，證遠離喜；如是或有由別因緣，證得第二、第三靜慮無愛味樂，第四靜慮無苦樂受，從此已上，乃至非想非非想處，當知亦爾，於種種想俱行苦樂受等差別已超過故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Through receiving instruction in order to attain the first meditative absorption, one is able, regarding the views operating together with the later limit, and the views operating together with the prior limit, to transcend them by not assenting to them; and, within this very life, one is further able to transcend the fetters of the desire realm, realizing the joy of seclusion. From this point on, hearing and knowing nothing further, one then generates the notion of nirvāṇa regarding this very state. Just as, through that, one realizes the joy of seclusion, so too, through other distinct conditions, one may realize the second and third absorptions' pleasure free of craving, and the fourth absorption's feeling free of pain and pleasure; from this upward, even up to the base of neither perception nor non-perception, one should know it is likewise so — since one has transcended the varied perceptions and the distinctions of painful and pleasant feeling operating together with them.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Having genuinely transcended the sixty-two views' speculation about past and future through sheer concentration, and freed the mind of the desire realm's fetters, this sincere practitioner never learns what lies beyond that particular absorption and so mistakes its own joy for nirvāṇa itself — repeating this same mistake at each successive, subtler absorption in turn, all the way up to the peak of the formless realm.

42. Section: The Third Decline Named, and the Buddha's Own Fivefold Understanding, Closing "The Fool's Five Stages"

Thus, unable to transcend the grasping inherent in the destinies still pursued, delighting in a dharma still liable to regress, not yet in parinirvāṇa, one gives rise to conceit regarding nirvāṇa — one should know this is named the third decline. Regarding this matter, the Tathāgata, having himself, of his own accord, realized awakening to the quiescent, sublime path, thoroughly knows as it truly is, through five marks, all three declines of every kind of formation just described — namely, their own nature; the views themselves; how they arise through ignorance; how their object turns; and their object's own coarse, wretched danger, together with the higher escape from it. Thoroughly knowing such matters as they truly are, one is, within escape itself, constantly and truly escaping.

Chinese Source

如是彼於趣諸取行不能超越，樂退還法，未般涅槃，起涅槃慢，當知是名第三衰損。此中如來自然證覺寂靜妙跡，於如所說一切行相三種衰損，由五種相如實了知。謂若彼自性，若彼諸見，若由無智彼得生起，若所緣轉，若彼所緣麤弊過患及上出離。於如是事如實了知，即出離中常自出離。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus, unable to transcend the grasping inherent in the destinies still pursued, delighting in a dharma still liable to regress, not yet in parinirvāṇa, one gives rise to conceit regarding nirvāṇa — one should know this is named the third decline. Regarding this matter, the Tathāgata, having himself, of his own accord, realized awakening to the quiescent, sublime path, thoroughly knows as it truly is, through five marks, all three declines of every kind of formation just described — namely, their own nature; the views themselves; how they arise through ignorance; how their object turns; and their object's own coarse, wretched danger, together with the higher escape from it. Thoroughly knowing such matters as they truly are, one is, within escape itself, constantly and truly escaping.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Notes

Mistaking any absorption for nirvāṇa is still bondage — one remains subject to that realm's own rebirth, still grasping, still liable to fall back, and the pride of thinking oneself already liberated is itself a further obstacle; a worldly absorption, however deep, is still only suppression, like a stone pressing down grass, never the actual uprooting only cutting off the three realms' own craving and views accomplishes. The Buddha's five marks of understanding map exactly onto the three declines: the first two marks (an outsider view's own nature, and its full content) address the first decline; the third mark (its arising through ignorance) addresses the second; and the last two marks (how a meditator's object shifts under mere calm without insight, and the danger of every object up to and including the very highest absorption, together with the true escape beyond it) address the third. Knowing all this, a practitioner who also cultivates insight, not calm alone, is never trapped by any of the three declines, and escapes the three realms for good. With this, the fool's five stages, corresponding to sūtra 59, is complete.

43. Section: Background — Sūtra 57 Retold in Full, the Arhat's Six Constant Abidings and Freedom From Every Wrong View

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi notes that the entire preceding excursus — benefit, the two kinds of wisdom, and the fool's five stages together — unfolds as extended commentary drawn out from the Saṃyukta Āgama's fifty-seventh sūtra itself, which the treatise's own verse further subdivides for its own pedagogical purposes. In brief, the underlying sūtra teaches: an arhat with liberated mind and pure wisdom and vision holds four benefits unshared with any outsider. First, dwelling in the six constant abidings — seeing form (and, alike, hearing sound, smelling, tasting, touching, cognizing a mental object) without delight, without praise, without grasping, without attachment, without seizing upon any mark or secondary feature at all, whether acting or dwelling. Second, this signless concentration is present at every time, acting or dwelling alike. Third, at life's end, the aggregates produced by past causes spontaneously cease. Fourth, the practitioner knows for themselves: the pure life is established, the task done, no further existence remains.

Relying first on a trainee's own right view, one grows weary of the aggregates and, through this very weariness, gains freedom from desire; freedom from desire in turn brings the mind's complete liberation — a liberation that carries with it the signless abiding free of every one of the sixty-two wrong views regarding the past or the future, and free even of the mistaken notion of nirvāṇa arising from concentration alone regarding the present. Taking nothing at all from the world of sentient beings or the container world, seeking nothing further, the arhat rests, unmoved, in the simple, direct knowledge: I have attained nirvāṇa.
