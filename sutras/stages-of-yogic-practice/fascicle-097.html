Fascicle 97 · 瑜伽師地論卷第九十七 · The Embrace of the Factors of Enlightenment Begins

The Embrace of the Factors of Enlightenment Begins

Fascicle 97 opens the embrace of the factors of enlightenment (菩提分法擇攝), the fourth and final of sūtra matters' four great divisions, following directly upon the dependent-origination, food, truths, and elements section closed entirely in fascicle 96. Its own general summary verse names nine great topics across eleven and a half particular verses: the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right efforts, the four bases of supernormal power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, the eightfold path, mindfulness of breathing, the trainee and the adept beyond training, and the four confirmed purities. This fascicle opens the four foundations of mindfulness's own first particular verse, distinguishing the true renunciant, the true path, and the true finality that only the inner dharma possesses — the four causes and conditions by which the noble eightfold path and its ultimate fruit exist within the Buddha's teaching alone, and nowhere among the outside paths.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress Source: Taishō T30, no. 1579, fascicle 97 Commentary guide: Master Cháng Bǎi's lecture notes

1. Section: The Embrace of the Factors of Enlightenment Begins — Concluding What Came Before, Introducing What Follows

Thus has been explained the embrace of dependent origination, food, the truths, and the elements. The embrace of the factors of enlightenment, I shall now explain.

Chinese Source

如是已說緣起、食、諦、界擇攝。菩提分法擇攝，我今當說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus has been explained the embrace of dependent origination, food, the truths, and the elements. The embrace of the factors of enlightenment, I shall now explain.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the embrace of the factors of enlightenment (菩提分法擇攝), the fourth and final of sūtra matters' four great divisions (己四), decisively explaining the Saṃyukta Āgama's teaching on the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment, beginning with concluding what came before, introducing what follows (結前生後) — the fascicles' own account of 攝事分 (spanning fascicles 85 through 100) divides sūtra matters into four embraces by subject: the aggregates' embrace (行擇攝, fascicles 85–88, 178 sūtras), the sense-bases' embrace (處擇攝, fascicles 89–92, 285 sūtras), the dependent-origination, food, truths, and elements' embrace (緣起食諦界擇攝, fascicles 93–96, 296 sūtras, now closed entirely), and the factors of enlightenment's embrace (菩提分法擇攝, fascicles 97–98, 405 sūtras) — the largest of the four, now opening. This fascicle is the first of its two, hence its subtitle "the fourth, part one" (第四之一).

Ru-Yi Translation

A general summary verse says:

Ru-Yi Translation

The foundations of mindfulness and right effort; supernormal bases, and the faculties and powers; the factors of enlightenment, the path-factors, mindfulness of breathing; training and realization, confirmed purity, coming last.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens marking and explaining all of it (標釋一切), the second of two matters, beginning with the general summary verse (總嗢柁南) — nine great topics unfold across eleven and a half particular verses: the four foundations of mindfulness (四念住), given four particular verses of its own (the first three within this fascicle, concerned chiefly with the individual practitioner, the dharma, and the two kinds of defilement); the four right efforts (四正斷) and four bases of supernormal power (四神足), sharing a single half-verse between them; and the five faculties (五根), five powers (五力), seven factors of enlightenment (七覺支), eightfold path (八聖道支), mindfulness of breathing (持息念), the trainee and adept beyond training (學無學), and the four confirmed purities (四證淨), each given one full verse — all of the latter falling within fascicle 98.

4. Section: The Long-Form Explanation Begins — The Renunciant Begins — Generally Marking the Distinction

Relying on the dominant power of cultivating the four foundations of mindfulness, by four causes and conditions, one should know that the inner dharma has the path of the renunciant, and has finality; the outside dharmas definitely have neither the path of the renunciant, nor finality. One should know that, in every other doctrine, both the path of the renunciant and finality are entirely empty.

Chinese Source

依四念住修習增上，由四因緣，應知內法有沙門道，及有究竟；外法決定無沙門道，亦無究竟。當知他論，諸沙門道及以究竟，一切皆空。

Ru-Yi Translation

Relying on the dominant power of cultivating the four foundations of mindfulness, by four causes and conditions, one should know that the inner dharma has the path of the renunciant, and has finality; the outside dharmas definitely have neither the path of the renunciant, nor finality. One should know that, in every other doctrine, both the path of the renunciant and finality are entirely empty.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the long-form explanation (長行釋), an eightfold structure, beginning with the renunciant (沙門), the first, explaining the true renunciant sustained by right practice, beginning with generally marking the distinction (總標簡) — sustained cultivation of the four foundations of mindfulness is what distinguishes the inner dharma's genuine path of renunciation and its genuine finality from outside paths, which possess neither, however elaborate their teachings may appear.

5. Section: Explaining Each Mark Individually Begins — Briefly Explaining All of It: The Four Causes and Conditions

What are called the four causes and conditions? First, relying on four places, one attains the four confirmed wisdoms; second, one is liberated from four kinds of external secondary affliction; third, the inner dharma's disciples and the outside paths' disciples belong to different classes; fourth, the inner dharma's great teacher and the outside paths' teachers belong to different classes.

Chinese Source

云何名為四種因緣？一者、依止四處，得四證智故；二者、解脫四種外隨煩惱故；三者、內法弟子與外道弟子不同品類故；四者、內法大師與外道師不同品類故。

Ru-Yi Translation

What are called the four causes and conditions? First, relying on four places, one attains the four confirmed wisdoms; second, one is liberated from four kinds of external secondary affliction; third, the inner dharma's disciples and the outside paths' disciples belong to different classes; fourth, the inner dharma's great teacher and the outside paths' teachers belong to different classes.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens explaining each mark individually (別釋相), the second of the renunciant's matters, beginning with briefly explaining all of it (略釋一切), a fourfold structure, beginning with the four causes and conditions (四種因緣) — first, the four confirmed wisdoms arise relying on four places: the place of respite where the three fetters are permanently severed, the place of power where one never falls back, the place fixed toward the enlightenment-destined class, and the place of at most seven more rebirths; second, freedom from four kinds of external affliction — in this life, in future lives, from mutual contradiction, and from being unable to teach or awaken others to the noble truths; third, unlike outside-path disciples, who fall through hearing into mere attachment, through thinking into defiled clinging, and through cultivation into further clinging, settling into either the extreme of eternalism or the extreme of nihilism, hoarded and guarded over a long night of habituation, the inner dharma's disciples walk the middle way, free of both extremes; fourth, outside-path teachers, though claiming to teach the severance and full knowledge of every clinging, are unable to actually establish the true severing and full knowledge of clinging — only the inner dharma possesses both the path of the renunciant and its finality. Each of these four causes will be unfolded in fuller detail in what follows.

7. Section: Marking

謂諸沙門略有四種。

Ru-Yi Translation

Renunciants are, briefly, of four kinds.

8. Section: Listing

First, the renunciant of the supreme path; second, the renunciant of the expounded path; third, the renunciant who lives by the path; fourth, the renunciant who defiles the path.

Chinese Source

一者、勝道沙門，二者、論道沙門，三者、命道沙門，四者、汙道沙門。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the renunciant of the supreme path; second, the renunciant of the expounded path; third, the renunciant who lives by the path; fourth, the renunciant who defiles the path.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens listing (列), the third — the renunciant of the supreme path has realized the ultimate truth itself; the renunciant of the expounded path teaches and expounds the noble path without necessarily having fully realized it; the renunciant who lives by the path has devoted their entire life to the eightfold path's cultivation, whether or not they teach it to others; and the renunciant who defiles the path, though present within the Buddhist community, cultivates precepts, concentration, and wisdom in a corrupted, mistaken way.

9. Section: Pointing to Fuller Explanation, Closing "The Inner Dharma's Renunciant" Entirely

These four renunciants, whether briefly or extensively explained, are as already distinguished in their marks in the Śrāvaka Ground.

Chinese Source

是四沙門若略若廣，如聲聞地已辯其相。

Ru-Yi Translation

These four renunciants, whether briefly or extensively explained, are as already distinguished in their marks in the Śrāvaka Ground.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記 and the Śrāvaka Ground

With this, pointing to fuller explanation (指) closes entirely, closing the inner dharma's renunciant (內法沙門4: 徵/標/列/指) entirely. Per the 披尋記, citing the Śrāvaka Ground: the well-gone ones (buddhas and arhats) are the renunciants of the supreme path; those who teach the true dharma are the renunciants of the expounded path; those who cultivate wholesome conduct are the renunciants who live by the path; and those who practice wrongly are the renunciants who defile the path — with fuller detail given elsewhere: trainees and adepts beyond training, having destroyed every affliction severed by seeing and cultivation with the uncontaminated path, are renunciants of the supreme path; in the absence of a buddha or bodhisattvas diligently cultivating for enlightenment's sake, śrāvaka elders who preserve the three canonical baskets, sustaining the conventional dharma and discipline and keeping the wheel of the true dharma turning without interruption, are renunciants of the expounded path; ordinary beings whose nature has grown well-tamed, who diligently cultivate right conduct for their own benefit, possessing shame and remorse, delighting in genuine study, and capable of attaining what is not yet attained, touching what is not yet touched, and realizing what is not yet realized — such beings, possessing the warmth of the preparatory stage capable of nurturing the life-root of noble wisdom, are called renunciants who live by the path, since their path is alive, not dead and unmoving; and those who break the precepts, commit much evil, and falsely claim to practice the holy life without actually doing so — having destroyed the very root of the path from the start, powerless and incapable, not a vessel for the path's arising, unable to attain it even when a genuine teacher and a genuine realizer of the path stand right before them — are renunciants who defile the path. The World-Honored One is said to have declared, of these same four kinds, that they alone exist, and that outside the śramaṇas' and brāhmaṇas' own teachings, no such renunciant can be found at all.

10. Section: The Inner Dharma's Path Begins — Questioning

The inner dharma's path — what is called the path?

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The inner dharma's path — what is called the path?

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the inner dharma's path (內法道), the third of briefly explaining all of it's four matters, explaining the content of the noble path within the Buddhadharma, beginning with questioning (徵).

11. Section: Explaining Begins — Bringing Forth Its Essential Nature

It means the eight-limbed noble path.

Chinese Source

謂八支聖道。

Ru-Yi Translation

It means the eight-limbed noble path.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens explaining (釋), the second, beginning with bringing forth its essential nature (出體性) — right view, right thought, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right mindfulness, right effort, and right concentration, the eightfold path briefly embraced within the three trainings of precepts, concentration, and wisdom, is called the inner dharma's path.

12. Section: Clarifying Its Establishment Begins — Existing Only Within the Inner Dharma

Wherever the eight-limbed noble path is established, that same place establishes the renunciant who defiles the path as the last of the four kinds of renunciant.

Chinese Source

若處施設八支聖道，是處施設汙道為後四種沙門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Wherever the eight-limbed noble path is established, that same place establishes the renunciant who defiles the path as the last of the four kinds of renunciant.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens clarifying its establishment (明施設), the second, beginning with existing only within the inner dharma (唯內法有) — even the renunciant who defiles the path is defined only relative to the eightfold path's own prior establishment; without the path first being taught, there is no path to defile in the first place.

13. Section: Distinguishing Its Absence Within Outside Doctrines, Closing "Clarifying Its Establishment," "Explaining," and "The Inner Dharma's Path" Entirely

If there is a path, and one practices wrongly toward it, one is not a vessel for the arising of the path — by this cause and condition, it becomes possible to speak of a defiled path. Therefore, outside doctrines do not even have a defiled path, much less anything further.

Chinese Source

若有其道，自行邪行，非生道器；由是因緣，容有汙道。是故外法尚無汙道，況得有餘。

Ru-Yi Translation

If there is a path, and one practices wrongly toward it, one is not a vessel for the arising of the path — by this cause and condition, it becomes possible to speak of a defiled path. Therefore, outside doctrines do not even have a defiled path, much less anything further.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, distinguishing its absence within outside doctrines (簡外法無) closes entirely, closing clarifying its establishment (明施設2: 唯內法有/簡外法無) entirely, closing explaining (釋2: 出體性/明施設) entirely, closing the inner dharma's path (內法道2: 徵/釋) entirely — since outside paths never establish the genuine eightfold path in the first place, they lack even the possibility of a "defiled" version of it, let alone the supreme, expounded, or living kinds of renunciant that depend on the path's own prior existence.

14. Section: The Inner Dharma's Finality, Closing "Briefly Explaining All of It" Entirely

The inner dharma's finality — what is called finality? It means severing every clinging. Once every clinging is severed, in the future, there is never again any further continuation.

Chinese Source

內法究竟者，云何究竟？謂斷諸取。諸取斷已，當來畢竟無復相續。

Ru-Yi Translation

The inner dharma's finality — what is called finality? It means severing every clinging. Once every clinging is severed, in the future, there is never again any further continuation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, the inner dharma's finality (內法究竟) closes entirely, closing briefly explaining all of it (略釋一切4: 四種因緣/內法沙門/內法道/內法究竟) entirely — severing every one of the four kinds of clinging (to sense-desire, views, rites and vows, and the doctrine of self) and thereby ensuring no further rebirth ever arises again is precisely what "finality" means, the arhat's own attainment, found nowhere among the outside paths.
