Fascicle 48 · 瑜伽師地論卷第四十八 · The Abode of Heightened Discipline, the Second Ground

Ten Minds Made Pure, and the Ground of Stainlessness

Continuing directly from fascicle 47's complete account of the abode of utmost joy, this fascicle opens with the second of the bodhisattva's thirteen abodes — heightened discipline, matching the Ten Grounds Sutra's own ground of stainlessness — examined through nine aspects: purified aspiration (the ten minds carried forward from utmost joy, now fully ripened), innate precept-fulfillment, freedom from every violation, thorough understanding of karma and its fruit, personally practicing and encouraging pure conduct, compassion for suffering beings, purified wholesome roots (likened to gold refined further in alum), rebirth as a gold-wheel king ruling four continents, and majestic power tenfold greater than the first ground's own.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress Source: Taishō T30, no. 1579, fascicle 48 Commentary guide: Master Cháng Bǎi's lecture notes

1. Milestone: Fascicle 48 Opens — The Ten Minds Carried Forward From Utmost Joy, Now Fully Ripened

Fascicle 48 opens still within the second yoga foundation of sustained practice, continuing the chapter on the bodhisattva's thirteen abodes exactly where fascicle 47 left off — fascicle 47 having covered the lineage-abode, the abode of resolute practice, and the complete eightfold account of the abode of utmost joy; this fascicle takes up the remaining abodes from the abode of heightened discipline (the second ground) through the abode of ultimate accomplishment (the tenth ground), together with the overall qualities, eon-spans, cultivation and severance, purity, and mutual inclusion shared across all the abodes, likened also to the hearers' own abodes — and, further ahead, the third yoga foundation of ultimate practice's own chapter on modes of rebirth and chapter on being received into buddhahood. The treatise opens by posing its now-familiar question: when a bodhisattva at the abode of heightened discipline turns, what conduct, what state, what mark should be understood? The answer begins by explaining that this abode is reached through the very same ten minds of aspiration already perfected — though not yet fully so — back at the abode of utmost joy: bodhisattvas, previously at utmost joy, having already attained purified aspiration through ten kinds of mind, now bring that same purified aspiration to its fullest, most excellent grade, and, listing all ten in turn, the treatise names: a mind free of all deception toward every teacher and elder, a genuine field of merit; a mind of patient forbearance and gentleness, easily dwelling together with fellow bodhisattvas in the same dharma; a mind sovereign over every affliction, secondary affliction, and demonic undertaking; a mind that sees deeply into the faults of all conditioned activity; a mind that sees deeply into great nirvana's own excellent benefit; a mind constantly, diligently cultivating the wondrously excellent factors of awakening; a mind, according with that very cultivation, delighting in solitude; a mind entirely without any longing for the world's own tainted status, gain, or reverence; a mind departed from the lesser vehicles, oriented toward realizing the great vehicle; and a mind wishing to accomplish every kind of benefit for every sentient being.

Chinese Source

本地分中菩薩地第二持隨法瑜伽處住品第四之二

辰三、增上戒住　巳一、問　問：增上戒住菩薩轉時，當知何行、何狀、何相？

巳二、答　午一、略說　未一、別辨相　申一、意樂清淨　酉一、出意樂　戌一、標　答：若諸菩薩，先於極歡喜住，由十種心意樂，已得意樂清淨。

戌二、徵　何等為十？

戌三、列　一者、於一切師長尊重福田，不行虛誑意樂；二者、於同法菩薩忍辱柔和，易可共住意樂；三者、勝伏一切煩惱及隨煩惱眾魔事業，心自在轉意樂；四者、於一切行深見過失意樂；五者、於大涅槃深見勝利意樂；六者、於諸妙善菩提分法，常勤修習意樂；七者、即於彼修為隨順故，樂處遠離意樂；八者、於諸世間有染尊位利養恭敬，無所顧戀意樂；九者、遠離下乘，趣證大乘意樂；十者、欲作一切有情一切義利意樂。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the Basic Section, the Bodhisattva Stage, the second yoga foundation of sustained practice, the chapter on the abodes, part two.

Third abode, heightened discipline; first, the question. Question: when a bodhisattva at the abode of heightened discipline turns, what conduct, what state, what mark should be understood?

Second, the answer; first, the brief account; first, distinguishing each mark; first, purified aspiration; first, presenting the aspiration; first, naming it. Answer: if bodhisattvas, previously at the abode of utmost joy, through ten kinds of mind-aspiration, had already attained purified aspiration —

Second, posing the question. What are the ten?

Third, listing them. First, a mind free of all deception toward every teacher and elder, a genuine field of merit. Second, a mind of patient forbearance and gentleness, easily dwelling together with fellow bodhisattvas of the same dharma. Third, a mind sovereign over every affliction, secondary affliction, and the undertakings of every demonic force. Fourth, a mind that sees deeply into the faults of all conditioned activity. Fifth, a mind that sees deeply into great nirvana's own excellent benefit. Sixth, a mind constantly, diligently cultivating the wondrously excellent factors of awakening. Seventh, according with that very cultivation, a mind delighting in dwelling in solitude. Eighth, a mind entirely without longing toward the world's own tainted status, gain, and reverence. Ninth, a mind departed from the lesser vehicles, oriented toward realizing the great vehicle. Tenth, a mind wishing to accomplish every kind of benefit for every sentient being.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi opens by orienting readers to this fascicle's own scope, spelled out in the treatise's own introductory line: fascicle 47 covered the lineage-abode, the abode of resolute practice, and utmost joy in full; this fascicle will carry the remaining abodes from heightened discipline through ultimate accomplishment, plus an overall summary treatment of qualities, eon-spans, cultivation, purity, and mutual inclusion across all thirteen abodes — and, further ahead still, the third yoga foundation's own chapter on the modes of rebirth (calamity-averting birth, birth according to kind, birth of great power, birth of heightened rebirth, and final birth) together with the chapter on being received into buddhahood.

He explains why this second abode opens by simply naming the same ten minds already introduced, in outline, back at utmost joy: those ten minds were already present at the first ground, but only in a still-maturing form; here, having been cultivated to their most excellent, fullest grade, they themselves constitute entry into the second abode — continuity rather than replacement being the governing principle across the entire ten-ground path. He supplies, for reference, the alternate names each mind bears in Vasubandhu's Commentary on the Ten Grounds Sutra: straightforward mind, gentle mind, tamed mind, wholesome mind, quiescent mind, genuine mind, unmixed mind, undesiring mind, superior mind, and great mind — noting the treatise itself will cross-reference this exact list shortly. Having named all ten minds, he notes, the treatise turns next to explaining how, together, they constitute this abode's own purified aspiration.

2. Milestone: These Ten Undistorted Minds Together Are Called Purified Aspiration

The treatise now closes the first aspect: these ten kinds of undistorted mind, continuously turning and depending on the mind, are for this reason called purified aspiration. The commentary cross-references the Ten Grounds Sutra directly, quoting its own explanation: if a bodhisattva, having already fully accomplished the first ground, wishes to attain the second ground, ten kinds of straightforward mind should arise — straightforward mind, gentle mind, tamed mind, wholesome mind, quiescent mind, genuine mind, unmixed mind, undesiring mind, superior mind, and great mind; a bodhisattva giving rise to these ten minds is thereby able to enter the second bodhisattva ground, the ground free of stain — and these ten undistorted forms of aspiration in this treatise should be understood to correspond, in the very same order, to that sutra's own ten.

酉二、明清淨　如是十種無倒意樂依心而轉，是故說為意樂清淨。

《披》如是十種無倒意樂等者：十地經說：若菩薩已具足初地，欲得第二地者，當生十種直心。何等為十？一、直心，二、柔軟心，三、調柔心，四、善心，五、寂滅心，六、真心，七、不雜心，八、不希望心，九、勝心，十、大心。菩薩生是十心，得入第二菩薩離垢地。此中十種無倒意樂，如次應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying purity. These ten kinds of undistorted mind, continuously turning and depending on the mind, are for this reason called purified aspiration.

Commentary: Regarding "these ten kinds of undistorted mind" and the like — the Ten Grounds Sutra says: if a bodhisattva, having already fully accomplished the first ground, wishes to attain the second ground, ten kinds of straightforward mind should arise. What are the ten? First, straightforward mind. Second, gentle mind. Third, tamed mind. Fourth, wholesome mind. Fifth, quiescent mind. Sixth, genuine mind. Seventh, unmixed mind. Eighth, undesiring mind. Ninth, superior mind. Tenth, great mind. A bodhisattva giving rise to these ten minds is able to enter the second bodhisattva ground, the ground free of stain. The ten kinds of undistorted aspiration here should be understood to correspond in this very order.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi walks through the direct correspondence between this treatise's ten aspirations and the Ten Grounds Sutra's own ten minds: freedom from deception toward teachers matches straightforward mind (a genuinely upright mind, free of twisted discrimination, being itself the very ground of practice); patient, gentle ease dwelling with fellow bodhisattvas matches gentle mind; sovereignty over affliction and demonic undertaking matches tamed mind (having already subdued the root afflictions of greed, hatred, delusion, conceit, doubt, and wrong view, together with the secondary afflictions, such a mind is no longer coarse and unruly); seeing conditioned activity's own faults matches wholesome mind; seeing nirvana's own benefit matches quiescent mind; diligent cultivation of the factors of awakening matches genuine mind (a mind genuinely, sincerely committed to practice); delight in solitude matches unmixed mind; freedom from worldly longing matches undesiring mind; orientation toward the great vehicle matches superior mind; and the wish to benefit every sentient being matches great mind.

He notes the sutra's own explicit naming of the resulting ground — "the ground free of stain" — as the treatise's own first direct confirmation that "heightened discipline" and "the ground of stainlessness" name one and the same reality, a point the treatise itself will make explicit again at this abode's very close. With purified aspiration now fully explained as the first of nine aspects distinguishing this abode, he notes, the treatise turns next to the second aspect: the bodhisattva's own innate fulfillment of precepts.

3. Milestone: Innate Precept-Fulfillment, and Freedom From Even the Slightest Violation

The treatise now explains the second and third of nine aspects. The second is innate precept-fulfillment: precisely because these same ten kinds of aspiration have now reached their most excellent grade, utterly complete, such bodhisattvas are thereby able to enter and be certified into the second abode of heightened discipline, and within this abode, the precepts belonging to their own inherent nature are now entirely fulfilled. The third is freedom from every violation: even the very slightest evil conduct subsumed within a wrongful course of karmic action, violating the precepts, no longer manifests at all — let alone conduct of a middling or superior grade — and, regarding the ten kinds of perfected course of karmic action, these now manifest spontaneously, of their own accord. The commentary specifies that this passage concerns specifically the purity of restraint-precepts: departing from killing all the way through departing from wrong view constitutes the ten perfected courses of karmic action, and manifesting purely in these courses of action without needing deliberate discrimination beforehand is exactly what "manifesting spontaneously" means.

Chinese Source

申二、性戒具足　即由如是十種意樂成上品故，極圓滿故，是諸菩薩入證第二增上戒住。於此住中，性戒具足。

申三、離一切犯　極少邪惡業道所攝諸惡犯戒尚不現行，況中上品。又於十種圓滿業道，自性顯現。

《披》性戒具足至自性顯現者：此說律儀戒淨應知。遠離殺生乃至遠離邪見，是名十種圓滿業道。於此業道清淨現行，不由思擇，是名自性顯現。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, innate precept-fulfillment. Precisely because these ten kinds of aspiration have become of the most excellent grade, utterly complete, such bodhisattvas enter and are certified into the second abode of heightened discipline. Within this abode, precepts of their own inherent nature are fulfilled.

Third, freedom from every violation. Even the very slightest evil conduct subsumed within a wrongful course of karmic action, violating the precepts, does not manifest at all — let alone conduct of a middling or superior grade. Furthermore, regarding the ten kinds of perfected course of karmic action, these manifest of their own inherent nature.

Commentary: Regarding "from innate precept-fulfillment to manifesting of their own inherent nature" — this concerns the purity of restraint-precepts, one should understand. Departing from killing all the way through departing from wrong view are called the ten kinds of perfected course of karmic action. Manifesting purely within these courses of karmic action, without recourse to deliberate discrimination, is called manifesting of their own inherent nature.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi explains "innate" precisely: a first-ground bodhisattva already keeps the precepts, but still through some measure of deliberate effort and vigilance; a second-ground bodhisattva's own precept-keeping has become so thoroughly internalized that body, speech, and mind simply never incline toward violation at all, the way a habit fully mastered no longer requires conscious attention — hence "the precepts belonging to their own inherent nature" rather than precepts merely maintained through discipline imposed from outside.

He notes the vivid comparative structure the treatise uses — not even the very slightest violation, "let alone" anything middling or severe — as deliberately setting a floor far below which ordinary moral effort operates: an ordinary practitioner might struggle daily against major transgressions, whereas a second-ground bodhisattva's own baseline is already free of even trivial lapses. He identifies the ten courses of karmic action by their classical content — refraining from killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, idle speech, covetousness, ill will, and wrong view — noting that "manifesting of their own inherent nature" is the technical mark distinguishing this ground's effortless purity from an ordinary being's own effortful, case-by-case moral deliberation. Having explained innate precept-fulfillment and freedom from violation, he notes, the treatise turns next to the fourth aspect: thoroughly understanding karma, its cause, and its fruit.

4. Milestone: Thorough Understanding of Cause and Fruit, Ripening-Fruit and Outflow-Fruit

The treatise now explains the fourth aspect in two parts. First, regarding cause and fruit: such bodhisattvas, precepts of their own inherent nature now fulfilled, are able, with wondrous wisdom, to genuinely know exactly as it is whatever pertains to tainted or untainted conduct, to unfortunate or fortunate rebirth, and to karmic action currently manifesting across every vehicle — hearer, solitary-realizer, and bodhisattva alike — together with these vehicles' own causes, fruits, and the way their cultivation and realization is established in teaching. Second, regarding the course of karmic action specifically: such bodhisattvas genuinely know exactly as it is the ripening-fruit and the outflow-fruit belonging to these very actions — ripening-fruit determining which realm of rebirth (human, divine, hell, hungry ghost, or animal) a given karma leads to, itself karmically neutral regardless of whether the originating action was wholesome or unwholesome; outflow-fruit instead resembling its own cause in kind, a wholesome cause yielding a pleasant result and an unwholesome cause yielding a painful one, cause and fruit of a piece and continuing in series.

Chinese Source

申四、了知因果業道　酉一、於因果　菩薩如是性戒具足，能以妙慧，於染不染、惡趣善趣，及諸乘中諸業現行、若因若果、修證安立，如實了知。

酉二、於業道　於異熟果及等流果如是諸業，如實了知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, thoroughly understanding karma and its course; first, regarding cause and fruit. Bodhisattvas, precepts of their own inherent nature thus fulfilled, are able, with wondrous wisdom, to genuinely know exactly as it is whatever pertains to tainted or untainted conduct, unfortunate or fortunate rebirth, and karmic action currently manifesting across every vehicle, together with cause, fruit, and how cultivation and realization are established.

Second, regarding the course of karmic action. Regarding the ripening-fruit and the outflow-fruit belonging to these very actions, one genuinely knows exactly as it is.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi distinguishes ripening-fruit from outflow-fruit with care: ripening-fruit (the "true ripening," lodged in the store-consciousness itself) determines only the broad category of rebirth a being receives — human, divine, hellish, hungry-ghost, or animal — and this determination is itself karmically neutral, neither wholesome nor unwholesome, however wholesome or unwholesome the originating action was; outflow-fruit, by contrast, resembles its own originating cause directly and continues on in series — killing yielding a short, sickness-prone life as its outflow-fruit, generosity yielding wealth and nobility as its own, cause and fruit sharing a single continuous flavor.

He treats this pairing of aspects — cause-and-fruit across every vehicle, then the specific ripening-versus-outflow distinction within karmic action itself — as together constituting a second-ground bodhisattva's own thoroughgoing mastery of karmic causality, extending even to knowing precisely what rebirth and what subsequent circumstance a given present action will eventually produce. Having explained this thorough understanding, he notes, the treatise turns next to the fifth aspect: personally practicing pure karma and encouraging others to do the same.

5. Milestone: Personally Practicing Pure Karma, and Delighting in Encouraging Others to Do the Same

The treatise now explains the fifth aspect in two parts, corresponding to the purity of the precepts that gather wholesome dharma. First, personally practicing: such bodhisattvas are personally able to presently sever every unwholesome course of karmic action, and personally able to presently receive and undertake every wholesome one. Second, delighting in encouraging others: precisely within this very same practice, such bodhisattvas delight in guiding others toward it, and are able to guide them correctly — the commentary explaining that cause here means tainted or untainted, fruit means unfortunate or fortunate rebirth, cultivation and realization concerns the three vehicles, and the way this is established in teaching concerns specifically the distinctions among the four noble truths, all of this genuinely known; and, knowing as well precisely which karmic actions yield which ripening-fruit and outflow-fruit, such bodhisattvas are personally able to depart from unwholesome courses of karmic action and abide in wholesome ones, and are likewise able to lead others to correctly depart from the one and correctly abide in the other — and it is for this very reason that they delight in guiding others, and guide them correctly.

Chinese Source

申五、受行淨業　酉一、自能受　自能現斷諸不善業，自能現受一切善業。

酉二、樂勸他　即於其中樂勸導他，能正勸導。

《披》能以妙慧至能正勸導者：此說攝善戒淨應知。言若因者，謂染不染。言若果者，謂惡善趣。言修證者，謂於諸乘。言安立者，謂即於彼四諦差別。於此一切皆如實知。又於能與異熟、等流果業，亦如實知。自能遠離不善業道，安住諸善業道；亦令他人能正遠離，能正安住；是故於中樂勸導他，能正勸導。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, receiving and practicing pure karma; first, personally able to receive it. Personally able to presently sever every unwholesome karma, personally able to presently receive every wholesome karma.

Second, delighting in encouraging others. Precisely within this same practice, delighting in guiding others toward it, able to guide them correctly.

Commentary: Regarding "with wondrous wisdom" through "able to guide correctly" — this concerns the purity of the precepts gathering wholesome dharma, one should understand. "Cause" means tainted or untainted. "Fruit" means unfortunate or fortunate rebirth. "Cultivation and realization" means regarding the vehicles. "Established in teaching" means the very distinctions of the four noble truths. All of this is genuinely known. Furthermore, regarding karma yielding ripening-fruit and outflow-fruit, this too is genuinely known. Personally able to depart from unwholesome courses of karmic action, abiding in wholesome courses; likewise causing others to correctly depart, correctly abide — this is why one delights in guiding others, able to guide them correctly.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi treats this fifth aspect as the natural completion of the fourth: understanding cause, fruit, and karmic action thoroughly (aspect four) is not merely theoretical for a second-ground bodhisattva but immediately actionable — personally severing what should be severed, personally cultivating what should be cultivated, and, crucially, delighting in leading others to do exactly the same. He draws a deliberate contrast with ordinary beings: an ordinary person may also wish to encourage others toward good conduct, but without thoroughly penetrating cause and fruit, such encouragement is not reliably correct, sometimes even well-intentioned but misguided; a second-ground bodhisattva's own encouragement, grounded in genuine, thorough knowledge, is correct without exception.

He notes the commentary's identification of this passage with the second of the three bodhisattva precepts — the precepts gathering wholesome dharma (following restraint-precepts, already covered under aspects two and three) — as confirming the treatise's own systematic structure: each aspect of this abode corresponds to one of the three precept-categories in turn. Having now explained personally practicing and encouraging others, he notes, the treatise turns next to the sixth aspect: a bodhisattva's own compassionate regard for the suffering, difficulty-laden condition of all sentient beings.

6. Milestone: Great Compassion for Beings Steeped in Ultimate Suffering, Whatever Their Worldly Fortune

The treatise now explains the sixth aspect: compassionate regard for suffering beings. Toward the realm of sentient beings, defiled by the fault of manifesting every kind of unequal karmic action, whether flourishing or declining, all such beings, without distinction, fall entirely into the suffering of ultimate truth, abide amid genuine hardship, and are pressed upon by every kind of difficulty — a condition profoundly deserving of compassion. Toward such beings, such bodhisattvas arouse a vast, far-reaching compassion, observing this truly, exactly as it is. The commentary specifies this concerns the purity of the precepts benefiting sentient beings, and identifies "the suffering of ultimate truth" with the suffering inherent in the five aggregates of grasping themselves, cross-referencing the immeasurable distinctions among life's own hardships already detailed in the chapter with both examination and analysis.

Chinese Source

申六、照愍眾苦　於其種種不平等業現行過失之所染汙諸有情界，若興若衰，等無差別，一切皆墮第一義苦，并住艱辛，種種艱辛之所逼切，甚可哀愍。於彼獲得廣大哀愍，如實觀照。

《披》於其種種不平等業至如實觀照者：此說饒益有情戒淨應知。五取蘊苦，是名第一義苦。生艱辛苦無量差別，如有尋有伺地說。（陵本九卷十一頁677）依彼當知，種種艱辛之所逼切。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, compassionate regard for suffering beings. Toward the realm of sentient beings, defiled by the fault of manifesting every kind of unequal karmic action, whether flourishing or declining, equally without distinction, all fall entirely into the suffering of ultimate truth, abiding amid hardship, pressed upon by every kind of difficulty — profoundly deserving of compassion. Toward them, one attains vast compassion, observing this truly, exactly as it is.

Commentary: Regarding "toward the realm of sentient beings" through "observing this truly, exactly as it is" — this concerns the purity of the precepts benefiting sentient beings, one should understand. The suffering of the five aggregates of grasping is called the suffering of ultimate truth. The immeasurable distinctions of hardship in being born are as explained in the stage with both examination and analysis (fascicle 9, page 677 of the Nanjing edition). One should understand "pressed upon by every kind of difficulty" accordingly.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi explains "unequal karmic action" precisely: because ordinary beings still harbor grasping at self and dharmas, even wholesome karma remains, in a subtle sense, unequal — genuinely equal, unconditioned action belongs only to the noble path itself. He unpacks "the suffering of ultimate truth" as the deepest layer of the classical three or eight kinds of suffering: however fortunate a being's worldly circumstance, birth in the higher heavens through deep meditative attainment included, so long as any of the five aggregates of grasping remains, the very fact of being caught in the three realms' own restless flow constitutes suffering in the ultimate sense — the pervasive suffering of formations, distinct from the more obvious suffering of pain or the suffering of change.

He cites the passage cross-referenced from the stage with both examination and analysis, describing beings' own tears, blood, and mother's milk consumed across countless past lives as exceeding the four great oceans combined, former kin now utterly forgotten and even become enemies — a second-ground bodhisattva's own compassion arising not from sentiment but from clearly, truly observing this condition exactly as it is, whether a given being's present circumstance looks fortunate or unfortunate by worldly standards. Having explained this compassionate regard, he notes, the treatise turns next to the seventh aspect: purified wholesome roots, and the simile distinguishing this ground's own purification from the first ground's.

7. Milestone: Purified Wholesome Roots — Gold Refined Further Still in Alum, Explaining the Name "Ground Free of Stain"

The treatise now explains the seventh aspect in two parts. First, pointing back to what was already said: bodhisattvas abiding in this abode of heightened discipline likewise behold the buddhas broadly, and their wholesome roots are purified, exactly as already explained at the abode of utmost joy — the same three pure conditions of offering to the three jewels, maturing sentient beings, and dedicating merit toward awakening continuing to operate here as well. Second, a simile marking the difference: just as, in the world, a skillful craftsman takes gold already refined and places it in alum, this gold, through repeated smelting, becoming ever more bright and pure still, so too should one understand a bodhisattva's own purified wholesome roots at this abode — the gold-in-fire simile from utmost joy now extended one stage further, gold already purified once being purified again through an additional agent, alum, precisely capturing this ground's own distinctive character: not first purification, but purification of what was already pure, made purer still. This distinguishing simile is exactly why the Ten Grounds Sutra itself names this ground "free of stain."

Chinese Source

申七、善根清淨　酉一、指同前　是諸菩薩安住如是增上戒住，廣見諸佛、善根清淨，如前應知。

酉二、喻差別　此差別者，謂如世間善巧工匠，以所鍊金置迦肆娑，置於火中數數燒鍊，轉更明淨。如是菩薩善根清淨，當知亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, purified wholesome roots; first, pointing to what was already said. Bodhisattvas abiding in this abode of heightened discipline likewise broadly behold the buddhas, wholesome roots purified, exactly as already explained.

Second, a simile marking the difference. The difference is this: just as, in the world, a skillful craftsman takes gold already refined and places it in alum, and, placed in fire, smelted again and again, it becomes ever more bright and pure — so too should a bodhisattva's own purified wholesome roots be understood, just so.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi walks through the precise progression across the two similes: at utmost joy, raw ore containing gold was placed directly in fire, external dross burning away to reveal the gold within for the very first time; here, at heightened discipline, the treatise specifies that the gold has already been refined once, and is now placed additionally in alum before further smelting — alum serving, in traditional metallurgy, to draw out even subtler impurities that plain fire alone cannot remove — the point being that this ground's own purification is a second, more exacting refinement of something already fundamentally pure, not a first purification of something still raw.

He connects this directly to the ground's own name, quoting the Ten Grounds Sutra's own gloss (already cited two milestones earlier): "the finished, genuine gold, once placed in alum-stone, has every last stain entirely removed" — precisely why this ground is called, in Sanskrit tradition, vimalā, "stainless," and in this treatise's own thirteen-abode framework, the abode of heightened discipline. He adds Cháng Bǎi's own further observation that at this ground the perfection of giving through kind speech (among the four means of attraction) becomes especially prominent, and that the perfection of moral discipline itself, among the ten perfections, becomes the ground's own especially heightened perfection — the other perfections and means of attraction continuing to be cultivated as capacity allows. Having explained purified wholesome roots and their governing simile, he notes, the treatise turns next to the eighth aspect: where such a bodhisattva typically takes rebirth.

8. Milestone: Rebirth as a Gold-Wheel King, and Majestic Power Tenfold Greater Than the First Ground's Own

The treatise now explains the eighth and ninth aspects. The eighth, rebirth: within this abode, purified aspiration having reached its own complete fulfillment, such a bodhisattva, in whatever place of rebirth, most often becomes a gold-wheel king, ruling all four great continents — a rank two full tiers above utmost joy's own iron-wheel kingship ruling a single continent — with sovereign power able to cause countless sentient beings to cease their own precept-violating, unwholesome courses of karmic action, and to encourage them to undertake wholesome ones instead. The ninth, majestic power: should such a bodhisattva likewise renounce the throne for monastic diligence, the resulting majestic power should be understood to exceed the first ground's own tenfold — the very same twelvefold catalogue of attainments already given for utmost joy (samādhis, buddhas beheld, worlds shaken, worlds visited, worlds illuminated, beings matured, eons of life sustained, eons past and future penetrated, dharma-gateways discerned, bodies manifested, and retinues displayed) now multiplied from a hundred to a full thousand of each.

Eighth, rebirth. Within this abode, purified aspiration having reached complete fulfillment, one attains entry. In whatever place of rebirth, one most often becomes a wheel-turning king, ruling four great continents. Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi supplies the full text the treatise itself abbreviates here, cross-referencing back to the first ground's own twelvefold catalogue: in a single instant, such a renunciate bodhisattva realizes a thousand samādhis; with a pure divine eye beholds a thousand tathāgatas across every kind of buddha-land, understanding fully both their transformation-sustaining and bodhisattva-sustaining power; with spiritual power shakes a thousand worlds, the body likewise able to go there; emits great light illuminating a thousand worlds, causing all to see; transforms into a thousand kinds, maturing a thousand kinds of beings; if wishing to prolong life, remains for a thousand eons; penetrates, with wisdom and vision, matters spanning a hundred thousand eons past and future; discerns correctly a thousand dharma-gateways among the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields; manifests a thousand bodies; and displays, in each and every one of those thousand bodies, a retinue of a thousand bodhisattvas surrounding it — every single figure from the first ground's own "hundred" now multiplied to "a thousand," a tenfold increase applied uniformly across all twelve attainments.

He notes the parallel structure with the first ground once more: there too, kingship (iron-wheel, one continent) preceded renunciation and the resulting spiritual attainments; here, kingship has itself grown grander (gold-wheel, four continents) and the resulting attainments have grown grander in exact proportion, a consistent tenfold scaling the treatise will likely continue to apply at each successive ground. Having now explained all nine aspects distinguishing the abode of heightened discipline, he notes, the treatise turns next to a concluding summary naming all nine together, followed by a final cross-reference confirming this abode's identity with the Ten Grounds Sutra's own ground of stainlessness.

9. Milestone: The Summary Names All Nine Aspects, and the Treatise Confirms — Heightened Discipline Is the Ground Free of Stain

The treatise now closes the entire abode of heightened discipline with a concluding summary and a final cross-reference. In brief, this is called the bodhisattva's abode of heightened discipline: because of purified aspiration; because innate precepts are fulfilled; because every kind of precept-violating stain is departed from; because every course of karmic action and every cause and fruit are genuinely understood; because pure karma is personally practiced and others are delighted in being encouraged to undertake it as well; because vast compassion is attained, truly observing the many sufferings and hardships arising from karmic action across the realm of sentient beings, exactly as it is; because of purified wholesome roots; because of rebirth; and because of majestic power. Should one wish to hear this expounded at greater length, the treatise directs the reader to the Ten Grounds Sutra's own chapter on the ground free of stain. And, in a final clarifying correspondence, the treatise states plainly: because every stain of precept-violation is departed from entirely, it is named the ground free of stain; and it is this very same freedom from every stain of precept-violation that constitutes what this treatise calls the abode of heightened discipline — one should understand that ground free of stain and this abode of heightened discipline are exactly the same reality under two names.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Master Cháng Bǎi reviews all nine aspects as a final capstone: purified aspiration (the ten minds carried forward, now fully ripened); innate precept-fulfillment; freedom from even the slightest violation (the restraint-precepts made pure); thorough understanding of cause, fruit, and karmic action (the precepts gathering wholesome dharma made pure); personally practicing and delighting in encouraging others; vast compassion for suffering beings (the precepts benefiting sentient beings made pure); purified wholesome roots (gold refined once more in alum); rebirth as a gold-wheel king; and majestic power tenfold greater than the first ground's own — the same nine-part structure, he notes, mapping cleanly onto utmost joy's own eight-part structure with one clear addition: this ground splits utmost joy's single treatment of precepts implicitly present throughout into the three explicit precept-categories (restraint, gathering wholesome dharma, benefiting sentient beings), each becoming its own dedicated aspect.

He treats the closing correspondence — ground free of stain and abode of heightened discipline being simply two names, one from the Ten Grounds Sutra's own Sanskrit-derived tradition and one from this treatise's own thirteen-abode framework, for a single underlying reality — as the treatise's own deliberate, recurring practice of reconciling its own terminology with the wider Mahāyāna tradition at the close of each ground, precisely as it already did at utmost joy's own close. With this correspondence confirmed, the entire abode of heightened discipline — the second ground, examined across all nine aspects — reaches its complete conclusion. The treatise turns next to the third of the eleven-part granular analysis's own third abode: heightened mind, the third ground, opening with its own fourfold question about the mind's own thorough purification.
