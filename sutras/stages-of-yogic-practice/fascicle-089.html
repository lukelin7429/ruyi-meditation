Fascicle 89 · 瑜伽師地論卷第八十九 · The Sense-Bases Section Begins

Establishing the Differences Among Feelings

With the aggregates section (行擇攝) closed at the end of fascicle 88, the Compendium of Bases' sūtra matters (契經事) open their second analysis: the sense-bases section (處擇攝), spanning fascicles 89 through 92 and covering the Saṃyukta Āgama's sense-bases recitation. Its own overall summary verse names four sub-analyses — establishing (安立), wisdom (智), sameness and equality (同等), and, last, freedom from desire and the rest (離欲等) — the first of which, establishing (安立攝), opens this fascicle with its own eight-part verse: establishing, distinctions, the foolish and the wise, teaching, liberation, affliction, and karma. Fascicle 89 begins with the first two of these — feeling's fivefold establishment (own-nature, basis, object, companion, and following-along) and its eightfold distinction (grounded in the coming-together of faculty, object, and consciousness; in feeling's cause and condition; in that coming-together's fruit; in discriminated feeling's verbal expression; and in the twin defilements of karma and affliction).

1. Section: The Sense-Bases Section Begins

Within the Compendium of Bases: sūtra matters, the sense-bases section, the second, part one.

Chinese Source

攝事分中契經事處擇攝第二之一

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the Compendium of Bases: sūtra matters, the sense-bases section, the second, part one.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The Compendium of Bases (攝事分) explains the Saṃyukta Āgama through four analyses: the aggregates section (行擇攝), the sense-bases section (處擇攝), the section on dependent arising, food, the truths, and the elements, and the section on the factors of enlightenment. The aggregates section, spanning fascicles 85 through 88, is now complete. This fascicle opens the second analysis, the sense-bases section (處擇攝), concerning the twelve sense-bases — the six faculties and six objects. The Decisions on the Grounds Associated with the Five Consciousness-Bodies and the Mind-Ground give six meanings of "sense-base": the gate through which mind and mental factors grow; the condition (as immediately-antecedent, as object, and as increasing) for the six consciousnesses; the auxiliary cause of their arising; the coming-together that produces contact; the basis on which mind and mental factors depend; and the dwelling-place mind and mental factors inhabit as they make use of their objects. Though the aggregates section has already covered the same territory of birth-and-death from the standpoint of the five aggregates, the sense-bases open a complementary gate onto the identical truth.

2. Section: Concluding the Aggregates Section, Beginning the Sense-Bases Section

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Though the five aggregates, twelve sense-bases, and eighteen elements are all "formations," the aggregates section specifically addressed the five aggregates' flow and cessation; the sense-bases section now takes up the same investigation from the sense-bases' own angle. The sense-bases section spans fascicles 89 through 92, comprising two overall summary verses and eight particular verses.

5. Section: Establishing Feelings Begins — Five Aspects Named

By five kinds of mark, one should know the distinctions of feeling are established: first, by own-nature; second, by basis; third, by object; fourth, by companion; fifth, by following-along.

Chinese Source

由五種相，當知安立諸受差別。一、自性故；二、所依故；三、所緣故；四、助伴故；五、隨轉故。

Ru-Yi Translation

By five kinds of mark, one should know the distinctions of feeling are established: first, by own-nature; second, by basis; third, by object; fourth, by companion; fifth, by following-along.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Establishing feelings (安立), the first of establishing's eight sub-topics, opens with these five angles: feeling's own-nature, what it depends on, what it takes as object, the mental factors that accompany it, and the mind it arises together with.

6. Section: Feeling's Own-Nature — Three Kinds

By own-nature: that is, there are three feelings — first, suffering; second, pleasure; third, neither suffering nor pleasure.

Chinese Source

自性故者，謂有三受。一、苦，二、樂，三、不苦不樂。

Ru-Yi Translation

By own-nature: that is, there are three feelings — first, suffering; second, pleasure; third, neither suffering nor pleasure.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Painful feeling arises toward a disagreeable object, pleasant feeling toward an agreeable one, and the neutral feeling toward neither — including the still deeper equanimous feeling found in the fourth dhyāna and above. Elsewhere feeling is further divided into five — pain, pleasure, grief, joy, and equanimity — folding the sixth consciousness's grief and joy into the scheme, but the three named here span all six consciousnesses.

7. Section: Feeling's Basis — the Six Faculties

By basis: that is, there are six — the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind.

Chinese Source

所依故者，謂有六種，即眼、耳、鼻、舌、身與意。

Ru-Yi Translation

By basis: that is, there are six — the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Feeling arises only in dependence on one of the six internal sense-faculties encountering its object — mind and mental factors following once that encounter occurs.

8. Section: Feeling's Object — the Six External Sense-Bases

By object: that is, the six objects — form and the rest.

Chinese Source

所緣故者，謂色等六所緣境界。

Ru-Yi Translation

By object: that is, the six objects — form and the rest.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Form, sound, smell, taste, touch, and mental objects are the external six sense-bases that feeling takes as its object — whether the eye seeing form, the ear hearing sound, or the mind reflecting on a wholesome or unwholesome dharma, feeling follows in each case.

9. Section: Feeling's Companion — Perception, Volition, and the Rest

By companion: that is, perception and volition, or the remaining wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate mental dharmas that accompany it.

Chinese Source

助伴故者，謂想思，或餘善、不善、無記心法與此相應。

Ru-Yi Translation

By companion: that is, perception and volition, or the remaining wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate mental dharmas that accompany it.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Whenever a moment of mind encounters an object, attention, contact, perception, and volition arise together with feeling, along with whichever further wholesome factors (such as non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion), unwholesome factors, or indeterminate factors happen to accompany it — feeling never operates alone, but as part of a whole, intricate mental event.

10. Section: Feeling's Following-Along — Its Shared Basis With the Corresponding Mind, Closing "Feeling's Fivefold Establishment"

By following-along: that is, this corresponding mind — relying on that mind, the three feelings follow along; that mind is the basis on which the feelings arise together and cease together.

Chinese Source

隨轉故者，謂此相應心，由依彼故，三受隨轉，彼為諸受同生同滅所依止處。

Ru-Yi Translation

By following-along: that is, this corresponding mind — relying on that mind, the three feelings follow along; that mind is the basis on which the feelings arise together and cease together.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The mind-king that feeling accompanies is itself the ground on which any given feeling arises and ceases together with it — the fifth and final aspect. With this, feeling's fivefold establishment — own-nature, basis, object, companion, and following-along — gives a complete account of how feelings arise, and is closed entirely.

11. Section: Feeling's Distinctions Begin — Eight Kinds Named

Further, just as these five aspects establish feelings, one should know there are, further, eight kinds of distinction: first, the distinction of the internal sense-base; second, the distinction of the external sense-base; third, the distinction of the six consciousness-bodies; fourth, the distinction of the six contact-bodies; fifth, the distinction of the six feeling-bodies; sixth, the distinction of the six perception-bodies; seventh, the distinction of the six volition-bodies; eighth, the distinction of the six craving-bodies.

Chinese Source

復次，如是五相安立諸受，當知復有八種差別。一、內處差別，二、外處差別，三、六識身差別，四、六觸身差別，五、六受身差別，六、六想身差別，七、六思身差別，八、六愛身差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, just as these five aspects establish feelings, one should know there are, further, eight kinds of distinction: first, the distinction of the internal sense-base; second, the distinction of the external sense-base; third, the distinction of the six consciousness-bodies; fourth, the distinction of the six contact-bodies; fifth, the distinction of the six feeling-bodies; sixth, the distinction of the six perception-bodies; seventh, the distinction of the six volition-bodies; eighth, the distinction of the six craving-bodies.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Distinctions (差別), the second of establishing's eight sub-topics, opens. Where the fivefold scheme just given established feeling's basic anatomy, this eightfold scheme traces the fuller sequence body and mind actually follow when a feeling arises: the internal faculty and external object first coming together, generating one of the six consciousnesses, whose contact with its object produces feeling itself, followed by perception (which names and articulates the feeling), volition (the unwise reflection that acts on it), and craving (the affliction that follows).

12. Section: The Eight Distinctions Grounded — Five Ways They Are Established, Closing "Feeling's Eightfold Distinction" Entirely

One should know that here, by the meaning of the threefold coming-together, the first three distinctions are established. By the meaning of feeling's cause and condition, the fourth distinction is established. By the meaning of the threefold coming-together's fruit of contact, the fifth distinction is established. By the meaning of discriminated feeling's following verbal expression, the sixth distinction is established. By the twin meanings of the defilements of karma and affliction, one should know the seventh and eighth distinctions are established. Why is this? Because of that feeling, whether in union or in separation, volition arises and acts; just as volition's action arises in this way and that way, so too craving and aspiration arise in this way and that way.

Chinese Source

當知此中，由三和合義，立前三差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, by the meaning of the threefold coming-together, the first three distinctions are established.

Chinese Source

由受因緣義，立第四差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

By the meaning of feeling's cause and condition, the fourth distinction is established.

Chinese Source

由三和合觸果義，立第五差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

By the meaning of the threefold coming-together's fruit of contact, the fifth distinction is established.

Chinese Source

由分別受隨言說義，立第六差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

By the meaning of discriminated feeling's following verbal expression, the sixth distinction is established.

Chinese Source

所以者何？受諸受時，作如是想：我今領受此苦、此樂、此非苦樂。亦復為他隨起言說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this? Because, whenever one experiences any feeling, one forms the thought: I now feel this suffering, this pleasure, this neither-suffering-nor-pleasure — and one likewise gives rise to speech about it for the sake of others.

Chinese Source

由業、煩惱二雜染義，當知建立第七、第八兩種差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

By the twin meanings of the defilements of karma and affliction, one should know the seventh and eighth distinctions are established.

Chinese Source

所以者何？由於彼受若合、若離，起思造作；如如發起思所造作，如是如是生愛求願。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this? Because of that feeling, whether in union or in separation, volition arises and acts; just as volition's action arises in this way and that way, so too craving and aspiration arise in this way and that way.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Faculty, object, and consciousness coming together produces contact, from which the fourth distinction (contact) is established as feeling's direct cause and condition; that very contact's fruit is feeling itself, the fifth distinction. Once feeling occurs, the perception accompanying the sixth consciousness names and speaks of it — "I feel this pain," "I feel this pleasure" — the sixth distinction; and once perceived, unwise volition (an act of karma) forms the intention to unite with or separate from the object, which is itself karmic defilement, the seventh distinction, while the craving and aspiration that follow are afflictive defilement, the eighth. With this, feeling's eightfold distinction (差別) — internal sense-base, external sense-base, the six consciousness-bodies, the six contact-bodies, the six feeling-bodies, the six perception-bodies, the six volition-bodies, and the six craving-bodies, together with the five ways they are grounded — is closed entirely.

13. Section: The Foolish Begins — Two Kinds of "All"

Further, one should know there are, in brief, two kinds of "all": first, partial-all; second, all-all. As when it is said that all is impermanent — one should know this relies on partial-all, since it refers only to the formations, not to the unconditioned. Speaking of "all dharmas are without self" — one should know this relies on all-all.

Chinese Source

復次，當知略有二種一切。一、少分一切，二、一切一切。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, one should know there are, in brief, two kinds of "all": first, partial-all; second, all-all.

Chinese Source

如說一切皆無常者，當知此依少分一切；唯一切行，非無為故。

Ru-Yi Translation

As when it is said that all is impermanent — one should know this relies on partial-all, since it refers only to the formations, not to the unconditioned.

Chinese Source

言一切法皆無我者，當知此依一切一切。

Ru-Yi Translation

Speaking of "all dharmas are without self" — one should know this relies on all-all.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The foolish (愚), the third of establishing's eight sub-topics, opens — the treatise's own account of Saṃyukta Āgama sūtras 11 through 39 on the six sense-bases. Impermanence applies only to conditioned formations, since the unconditioned is neither permanent nor impermanent — hence "all is impermanent" is only a partial "all." Non-self, by contrast, holds across both conditioned and unconditioned dharmas alike — hence "all dharmas are without self" is the full, all-encompassing "all."

14. Section: The Marks of Foolishness — Own-Nature

Further, by three marks, one should know this is foolishness: first, by own-nature; second, by cause and condition; third, by fruit. By own-nature: that is, through entanglement, this is forgetting in the present life; through the latent tendency, this is the dharma of forgetting in the future.

Chinese Source

又由三相，應知是愚。一、由自性故；二、由因緣故；三、由果故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, by three marks, one should know this is foolishness: first, by own-nature; second, by cause and condition; third, by fruit.

Chinese Source

愚自性故者，謂由纏故，即是忘失於現在世；由隨眠故，即是當來忘失之法。

Ru-Yi Translation

By own-nature: that is, through entanglement, this is forgetting in the present life; through the latent tendency, this is the dharma of forgetting in the future.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Foolishness has two faces: as an active entanglement, ignorance darkens the mind moment to moment in the present, causing mindfulness of the noble teaching to be forgotten; as a dormant seed, it guarantees that same forgetting will recur in the future even when not currently manifest.

15. Section: The Cause and Condition of Foolishness — Not Perceiving Feeling's Marks or Life's Disturbances

By cause and condition: that is, within the five-aspect establishment of feeling, being unable to perceive impermanence and the rest; and, throughout one's own being's initial, middle, and final stages, being unable to understand all the disturbances there.

Chinese Source

愚因緣故者，謂於五相受安立中，不能覺了是無常等；及遍自體初中後位，所有惱亂皆不了故。

Ru-Yi Translation

By cause and condition: that is, within the five-aspect establishment of feeling, being unable to perceive impermanence and the rest; and, throughout one's own being's initial, middle, and final stages, being unable to understand all the disturbances there.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Śrāvaka Stage (聲聞地) on the disturbances of birth, sickness, old age, and death

Birth's disturbance carries five afflictions: attended by every other suffering, by every coarse burden, as the basis of every suffering and every affliction, and as unable to be separated from at will. Sickness's disturbance carries five: the body's own decline, grief's increase and prolonged presence, inability to enjoy what is agreeable, being forced to endure what is disagreeable, and the swift severing of the life-faculty. Old age's disturbance carries five: the decline of complexion, of strength, of the faculties, of the capacity to enjoy objects, and of the lifespan itself. Death's disturbance carries five: separation from cherished wealth, from cherished friends, from cherished family, from one's own cherished body, and the extreme grief endured at the moment of death. Failing to be alert to these disturbances throughout birth, sickness, old age, and death, and so failing to cultivate the observation of non-self, is precisely what breeds the cause and condition of foolishness.

16. Section: The Disturbances Matched to Birth, Sickness, and Old Age and Death

One should know this is precisely not perceiving the nature of birth, sickness, aging, and the dharma of death. The initial disturbance is by birth; the middle disturbance is by sickness; the final disturbance is by the two dharmas of aging and death.

Chinese Source

當知即是於生老病及死法性不能覺了。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know this is precisely not perceiving the nature of birth, sickness, aging, and the dharma of death.

Chinese Source

初惱亂者，謂由生故。中惱亂者，謂由病故。後惱亂者，謂由老、死二種法故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The initial disturbance is by birth; the middle disturbance is by sickness; the final disturbance is by the two dharmas of aging and death.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The three stages of life's disturbance are matched directly to their causes: birth opens the initial stage, sickness fills the middle, and aging together with death close the final stage.

17. Section: The Fruit of Foolishness, Closing "The Foolish" Entirely

By fruit: that is, the suffering of grief and the rest, and the defilement of craving and the rest.

Chinese Source

愚果故者，謂愁等苦、愛等雜染。

Ru-Yi Translation

By fruit: that is, the suffering of grief and the rest, and the defilement of craving and the rest.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Not understanding the disturbances throughout life yields the suffering of grief and lament; not understanding feeling's impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self yields the defilement of craving and karma — foolishness's own fruit, the affliction-karma-suffering cycle it perpetuates. With this, the foolish (愚), 安立攝's third sub-topic, is closed entirely.

Further, by three kinds of mark, one should know non-foolishness: first, by own-nature; second, by obstacle; third, by hindrance.

Chinese Source

復次，由三種相，當知不愚。一、自性故；二、由礙故；三、由障故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, by three kinds of mark, one should know non-foolishness: first, by own-nature; second, by obstacle; third, by hindrance.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The not-foolish (不愚), the fourth of establishing's eight sub-topics, opens — the one endowed with the light of the noble path's wisdom, marked by these three angles.

19. Section: Non-Foolishness's Own-Nature — Perceiving Feeling's Own and Shared Marks

Non-foolishness's own-nature: that is, within the five-aspect establishment of feeling, skillfully perceiving its own mark and shared mark, and, through this, being able to cut off every affliction, awaken to the noble truths, and realize nirvāṇa.

Chinese Source

不愚自性者，謂於五相受安立中，善能覺了自相、共相，由此能斷一切煩惱、能覺聖諦、能證涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

Non-foolishness's own-nature: that is, within the five-aspect establishment of feeling, skillfully perceiving its own mark and shared mark, and, through this, being able to cut off every affliction, awaken to the noble truths, and realize nirvāṇa.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Feeling's own mark is simply the three feelings themselves; its shared mark is impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self — perceiving both fully is what cuts off affliction and opens the way to nirvāṇa.

20. Section: Freedom From the Obstacle of the Four Māras

Freedom from the obstacle: that is, by the four kinds of māra. That is, by the aggregate-māra, in the sense of following one everywhere without exception; by that heavenly māra, in the sense of repeatedly, from time to time, obstructing; by the death-māra and the affliction-māra, in the sense of serving as the vessel for all the suffering born of death and rebirth.

Chinese Source

不愚礙者，由四種魔。

Ru-Yi Translation

Freedom from the obstacle: that is, by the four kinds of māra.

Chinese Source

謂由蘊魔，遍一切處隨逐義故；由彼天魔，於時時間能數任持障礙義故；死、煩惱魔，能與死生所生眾苦作器義故。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, by the aggregate-māra, in the sense of following one everywhere without exception; by that heavenly māra, in the sense of repeatedly, from time to time, obstructing; by the death-māra and the affliction-māra, in the sense of serving as the vessel for all the suffering born of death and rebirth.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Śrāvaka Stage (聲聞地)

The Śrāvaka Stage defines the four: the aggregate-māra is the five grasping-aggregates themselves, present wherever there is saṃsāra; the affliction-māra is every affliction throughout the three realms; the death-māra is the actual severing of the life-faculty for any given being; and the heavenly māra is a powerful deity of the highest desire-realm heaven who, whenever a practitioner strives to surpass the other three māras, obstructs them with various disturbances. Overcoming this obstacle is the second mark of the non-foolish.

21. Section: Freedom From Hindrance, Closing "The Not-Foolish" Entirely

Freedom from hindrance: that is, affliction taking a not-presently-seen object as condition, and entanglement — or its latent tendency — taking an object that is not not-presently-seen as condition.

Chinese Source

不愚障者，謂緣不現見境煩惱，及緣非不現見境纏，或彼隨眠。

Ru-Yi Translation

Freedom from hindrance: that is, affliction taking a not-presently-seen object as condition, and entanglement — or its latent tendency — taking an object that is not not-presently-seen as condition.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, on the extensive Saṃyukta Āgama sūtras on the sense-bases (sūtras 1-39)

A desire-realm being clinging to the "not presently seen" form and formless realms' concentrated states still generates affliction through craving, view, conceit, and doubt; a desire-realm being encountering desire-realm objects directly generates affliction through "not-not-presently-seen" entanglement, whether manifest or as a latent seed carried in the ālaya-consciousness. This third mark, freedom from both, completes non-foolishness. Sūtras 1 through 39 elaborate the same teaching from many angles: observing the eye (and the other five faculties) as impermanent yields weariness, then freedom from delight, then freedom from craving, then liberation of mind; the same holds for observing them as suffering, empty, and without self. Later sūtras extend the same fivefold structure — faculty, object, consciousness, contact, and the feeling contact gives rise to — through some twenty-five further epithets: painful, empty, not-self, illusory in its karmic display, a destructible dharma, a dharma of birth, of aging, of sickness, of death, of sorrow, of affliction, of arising, of ceasing; a dharma to be known, to be cognized, to be cut off, to be awakened to, to be realized; a māra, māra's domain, māra's instrument; burning, blazing, and scorching — since craving, aversion, and delusion set the aggregates ablaze at every stage of birth, aging, sickness, and death. With this, the not-foolish (不愚), 安立攝's fourth sub-topic, is closed entirely.

22. Section: Teaching Begins — Three Ways of Proper Instruction

Further, the buddhas, the world-honored ones, and the buddhas' noble disciples, by three kinds of mark, are able to properly instruct their various disciples. What are the three? Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the buddhas, the world-honored ones, and the buddhas' noble disciples, by three kinds of mark, are able to properly instruct their various disciples. What are the three? First, guiding instruction; second, according with what is appropriate, placing instruction upon the object condition; third, causing those transformed to attain their own benefit through instruction.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Śrāvaka Stage (聲聞地) and its background sūtra

The three supernatural transformations, per the Śrāvaka Stage: the transformation of miraculous manifestation (神境神變), corresponding to guiding instruction, wins reverence and attentiveness through displays of supernormal power; the transformation of mind-reading (記說神變), corresponding to appropriately-placed instruction, discerns a disciple's mental disposition to prescribe the fitting object of meditation; and the transformation of admonishment (教誡神變), corresponding to self-benefit instruction, teaches the true dharma directly according to a disciple's faculties, conduct, and stage of realization — the only one of the three not shared with outsiders, and so the one the Buddha most emphasizes. The background sūtra recounts the Buddha demonstrating all three before a thousand monks: flying through the air in the four postures and manifesting fire and water from his body in various colors (miraculous manifestation); reading each monk's mental state to correct their thinking (mind-reading); and finally teaching directly on how the six faculties, objects, consciousnesses, contact, and the feeling they give rise to are all "burning" with the fires of craving, aversion, and delusion at every stage of life — upon which all thousand monks are freed from every affliction. That is, giving rise to the antidote of ultimate cutting-off, every kind of affliction's coarse burden being eternally extinguished, one attests to the transformation of the basis, causing all afflictions to become, decisively and ultimately, a dharma that no longer arises — this is named liberation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The meaning of liberation (成解脫義), the fourth and final matter of liberation (解脫), opens: extinguishing every affliction's coarse residue and seed alike, attaining the transformation of the basis, and guaranteeing affliction can never arise again — this is what "liberation" itself means, yielding the exhaustion-wisdom and non-arising-wisdom of full attainment.

45. Section: Differences in Cutting Off — Already Free of Desire for the Sphere of Nothingness, and Not Yet

If a noble disciple has already attained freedom from desire for the sphere of nothingness, with only the formations of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception remaining, and is further able to abide in the concentration surpassing the peak of existence, at that time, without interruption, one is able, in turn, to attest to the eternal exhaustion of the contaminants. As for the remaining stages, though one is able to gradually cut off this and that contaminant, one is not able, without interruption, to attest in turn to the eternal exhaustion of the contaminants — so it is up to and including not yet having attained freedom from desire for the sphere of nothingness.

Chinese Source

若聖弟子，無所有處已得離欲，唯餘非想非非想處所有諸行，復能安住勝有頂定，爾時無間能隨證得諸漏永盡。

Ru-Yi Translation

If a noble disciple has already attained freedom from desire for the sphere of nothingness, with only the formations of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception remaining, and is further able to abide in the concentration surpassing the peak of existence, at that time, without interruption, one is able, in turn, to attest to the eternal exhaustion of the contaminants.

Chinese Source

若所餘位，雖能漸斷彼彼諸漏，然非無間能隨證得諸漏永盡，如是乃至無所有處未得離欲。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the remaining stages, though one is able to gradually cut off this and that contaminant, one is not able, without interruption, to attest in turn to the eternal exhaustion of the contaminants — so it is up to and including not yet having attained freedom from desire for the sphere of nothingness.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

A non-returner who has already left desire for the sphere of nothingness behind, with only the subtlest affliction of the peak of existence (neither-perception-nor-non-perception) remaining, can — resting on the vajra-like concentration that surpasses even that peak, and cultivating any one of impermanence, suffering, emptiness, or non-self — sever that last, subtlest innate self-grasping without interruption, exhausting the contaminants at once. Anyone at an earlier stage, by contrast, cuts off afflictions only gradually, level by level, without that same uninterrupted culmination, until they too reach freedom from desire for the sphere of nothingness.

46. Section: Broadly Explaining Affliction Begins — the Desire-Contaminant and Existence-Contaminant

Further, every affliction bound to the desire realm, excepting only ignorance, is named the desire-contaminant. Every affliction bound to the two realms of form and formlessness, excepting only ignorance, is named the existence-contaminant.

Chinese Source

復次，諸欲界繫一切煩惱，唯除無明，說名欲漏。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, every affliction bound to the desire realm, excepting only ignorance, is named the desire-contaminant.

Chinese Source

諸色無色二界所繫一切煩惱，唯除無明，說名有漏。

Ru-Yi Translation

Every affliction bound to the two realms of form and formlessness, excepting only ignorance, is named the existence-contaminant.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, on the closing background sūtra of liberation (解脫)

Before this, the venerable Rāhula asks the Buddha how to avoid clinging to the inner consciousness-body and outer objects as self and self's own; the Buddha answers by walking him through contemplating the eye's (and each remaining faculty's) exhaustive nature across past/future/present, internal/external, coarse/fine, superior/inferior, far/near, and its true nature as without self, other-than-self, or mutual containment — the same method later repeated for the six objects, six consciousnesses, and the rest, and for the outer as well as inner sense-bases (sūtras 41-56). Notably, when Rāhula twice asks for a meditation topic to bring him to arhatship, the Buddha instead sends him to teach others first — on the five grasping-aggregates, then the six sense-bases, then dependent origination — recognizing his own wisdom is not yet ripe; only after Rāhula reflects alone on how all these teachings converge toward nirvāṇa does the Buddha judge him ready, teaching him "all is impermanent" (partial-all covering the six sense-bases and their derivatives), whereupon Rāhula attains arhatship in solitary retreat. With this extended teaching, liberation (解脫), the sixth sub-topic of establishing (安立攝), is closed entirely. Broadly explaining affliction (廣說煩惱), the seventh sub-topic — unfolding across sixteen further divisions — now opens with the three contaminants (三漏): the desire-contaminant and existence-contaminant, defined as every desire-realm or form/formless-realm affliction apart from ignorance itself.

47. Section: The Ignorance-Contaminant, Closing "The Three Contaminants" Entirely

If sentient beings, whether not yet free of desire or already free of desire, apart from the various outsiders' wrong and skewed views — the evil views born of discriminating delusion that cover their minds — relying on such evil views, partly seek and partly abandon desire regarding those objects, up to the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception: the ignorance possessed throughout the three realms in this regard is together subsumed as one, and established as the ignorance-contaminant.

Chinese Source

若諸有情，或未離欲、或已離欲，除諸外道所有邪僻，分別愚癡所生惡見蔽覆其心；依此惡見，於彼諸欲一分尋求、一分離欲，乃至非想非非想處。於彼三界所有無智，總攝為一，立無明漏。

Ru-Yi Translation

If sentient beings, whether not yet free of desire or already free of desire, apart from the various outsiders' wrong and skewed views — the evil views born of discriminating delusion that cover their minds — relying on such evil views, partly seek and partly abandon desire regarding those objects, up to the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception: the ignorance possessed throughout the three realms in this regard is together subsumed as one, and established as the ignorance-contaminant.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Decisions on the Stage of What Is Heard (決擇‧聞所成慧地)

Where the desire-contaminant and existence-contaminant are craving-driven, the ignorance-contaminant is view-driven: outsiders who take present desire-realm pleasure itself as nirvāṇa and so pursue it; outsiders who take one of the four dhyānas as nirvāṇa and so pursue freedom from desire toward it; and, at the far end, those who take the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception itself as liberation. The Decisions give five such view-gates generating this ignorance: the existence-theorist, the non-existence-theorist, the neither-existence-nor-non-existence-theorist, the annihilationist, and the present-life-nirvāṇa theorist. With this, the three contaminants (三漏) — desire, existence, and ignorance — the first of 廣說煩惱's sixteen divisions, is closed entirely.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The nine fetters (九結), the second of 廣說煩惱's sixteen divisions, opens — as the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection names them: the craving-fetter, aversion-fetter, conceit-fetter, ignorance-fetter, view-fetter, grasping-fetter, doubt-fetter, envy-fetter, and stinginess-fetter — here grounded in nine concrete life-situations, three tied to household life (craving for loved ones and things, aversion toward hated ones and things, conceit in comparing oneself to others) and the rest to renunciant life or its opposite (three kinds of outsider misdirection, doubt toward the true teaching, envy born of a renunciant's poverty of wisdom, stinginess born of a householder's poverty of wealth).

Among these, through envy destroying the mind, one gives rise to stinginess with the dharma within the true teaching, and by this, poverty of wisdom follows in the future. The rest, according to what is appropriate, should be understood as matched accordingly.

Chinese Source

由此九事，如其所應，當知配屬愛等九結。

Ru-Yi Translation

By these nine matters, according to what is appropriate, one should know they are matched to craving and the other nine fetters.

Chinese Source

此中由嫉變壞心故，於正法內發起法慳，由此當來智慧貧乏。

Among these, through envy destroying the mind, one gives rise to stinginess with the dharma within the true teaching, and by this, poverty of wisdom follows in the future.

The rest, according to what is appropriate, should be understood as matched accordingly.

With this, the nine fetters (九結) is closed entirely.

50. Section: The Three Bonds — Craving, Aversion, and Delusion, Closed Entirely

Ru-Yi Translation

The three bonds (三縛), the third of 廣說煩惱's sixteen divisions, map directly onto the three feelings themselves — craving binds one to what is pleasant, aversion to what is painful, and delusion to what is neutral — each an affliction arising constantly in ordinary experience. With this, the three bonds is closed entirely.

51. Section: The Seven Latent Tendencies Begin — Why They Are Named "Latent"

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The seven latent tendencies (七隨眠), the fourth of 廣說煩惱's sixteen divisions, opens. "Coarse burden" is affliction still in seed form — not yet manifest, but rendering body and mind rigid and unworkable, dormant yet always capable of generating affliction's active entanglement when conditions ripen.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection (有尋有伺地)

The seven: desire-craving-latency, aversion-latency, existence-craving-latency, conceit-latency, ignorance-latency, view-latency, and doubt-latency. One not free of desire meets agreeable objects with craving and disagreeable ones with aversion; one already free of desire in the form or formless realms still harbors subtle craving for concentration itself; and conceit, ignorance, view, and doubt persist in both — conceit growing only stronger with deeper concentration until arhatship, ignorance of the three realms persisting even at their very peak, wrong view persisting wherever affliction remains uncut, and doubt toward the three jewels and four truths persisting wherever the noble path is unattained. With this, the seven latent tendencies (七隨眠), the fourth of 廣說煩惱's sixteen divisions, is closed entirely.

Self-conceit is the root of all seven, since every other comparison presupposes a self to compare; overweening conceit mistakes a mere resemblance to realization for realization itself; inferior conceit still exalts the mind even while nominally conceding inferiority — as when fully recognizing another's superiority yet refusing to show respect; and wrong conceit takes pride even in open wrongdoing, as a criminal boasting of repeated offenses. Every form of conceit breeds suffering and obstructs wholesome growth by fostering complacency and rivalry, keeping beings turning endlessly in saṃsāra. With this, the seven kinds of conceit (七慢), the twelfth of 隨煩惱's forty-eight divisions, is closed entirely.

65. Section: The Five Hindrances, Closing Item Thirty-Five of Forty-Eight Entirely

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Ground of Concentration (三摩呬多地)

The five hindrances (五蓋), item 35 of 隨煩惱's 48, briefly recapitulated: sensual desire, pursuing the five sense-pleasures whether presently encountered or recollected; ill will, arising from being called out by a fellow practitioner, recalling past injury, or plotting retaliation; torpor-and-sleepiness, bred by impure precepts, unguarded senses, poor eating habits, insufficient diligence, lack of clear awareness, infrequent practice, or affliction's own momentum — sleepiness being torpor's nearest companion, hence the two are named as one hindrance; restlessness-and-remorse, born of dwelling on family, homeland, mortality, or past amusements (restlessness) and regret over what was or wasn't done (remorse) — again paired as one hindrance for sharing the same objects; and doubt, an unsettled uncertainty toward the three jewels, the three trainings, nirvāṇa, one's own capacity, or the four truths. Every being not free of desire has the desire-flood (outsiders included, though the treatise here highlights the other three as their specific territory); outsiders in particular are swept along by the view-flood and ignorance-flood, the deepest and hardest to cross of all. With this, the four floods (四暴流), the seventh of 廣說煩惱's sixteen divisions, is closed entirely.

The yoke (軛), the eighth of 廣說煩惱's sixteen divisions, names the person whose afflictions are all present in balanced measure — none especially dominant, none especially weak — yet who is nonetheless bound by the full sum of them, as an ox or horse yoked at the neck cannot move freely. 77. Section: What Is Grasped, and What Grasps

Question: What is grasped? Answer: desire, view, precept-and-vow, and self-referential statement are what is grasped. Question: What grasps? Answer: the four kinds of craving-desire are what grasps.

Chinese Source

問：何所取？答：欲、見、戒禁、我語是所取。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What is grasped? Answer: desire, view, precept-and-vow, and self-referential statement are what is grasped.

Chinese Source

問：何能取？答：四種欲貪是能取。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: What grasps? Answer: the four kinds of craving-desire are what grasps.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

What is grasped: sense-desire's objects (form, sound, and the rest); views (self-view, extreme views, and so on); the precepts and ascetic vows wrongly adopted on the basis of wrong view; and the self and self's-own that ordinary people cling to in speech. What grasps, in each case, is simply craving-desire directed toward that object — four graspings, one craving each.

Ru-Yi Translation

Desire-grasping serves the householder's ordinary pursuit and enjoyment of sense-pleasure; view-grasping serves the outsider debater's craving for reputation, whether attacking a rival's position or defending their own; precept-and-vow-grasping serves the pursuit of mundane concentration through wrongly adopted ascetic conduct, all the way up to the formless realm's highest absorption; and self-statement-grasping serves the need to speak of a doer of karma, a receiver of its fruit, or a person who flows on or ceases in birth-and-death — the same false "person" the treatise has refuted at length across earlier sections of this fascicle. With this, the marks of grasping (取行相) is closed entirely.

79. Section: The Establishment of Grasping — Its Basis in the Two Classes

Chinese Source

Such as these four graspings depend on two classes: that is, the household class who enjoy sensual desire, and the renunciant class within the wrongly-taught dharma and discipline.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The establishment of grasping (取建立) opens, restating the two-class basis already named: householders enjoying sense-pleasure account primarily for desire-grasping, while outsider renunciants within a wrongly-taught path account for the other three.

80. Section: Distinguishing This Teaching — Why the Buddha's Own Disciples Are Not Classed Under the Four Graspings

Since the World-Honored Buddha himself constantly proclaims: I am the great teacher of the true treatise on fully knowing and eternally cutting off every grasping — therefore, for one who has vowed to practice within this dharma, even though affliction still remains in the body at the breaking-up of life, one does not, on that account, classify such a person under these graspings.

Chinese Source

由佛世尊每自稱言：我為諸取遍知、永斷正論大師。故於此法誓修行者，雖帶煩惱身壞命終，而不於彼建立諸取。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the World-Honored Buddha himself constantly proclaims: I am the great teacher of the true treatise on fully knowing and eternally cutting off every grasping — therefore, for one who has vowed to practice within this dharma, even though affliction still remains in the body at the breaking-up of life, one does not, on that account, classify such a person under these graspings.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Because the Buddha's own teaching is itself the treatise on fully knowing and cutting off every grasping, a disciple sincerely practicing within it — even one who dies with some affliction yet unresolved — is on an entirely different footing from the four graspings described here, which characterize the householder's and the outsider's paths, not the inner teaching's own.

81. Section: Why the Buddha's Disciples Are Excepted, Explained

Why is this? They have gone forth having no regard or attachment left toward the desires, and have no clinging toward view, precept-and-vow, or self-referential statement.

Chinese Source

所以者何？

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this?

Chinese Source

彼於諸欲無所顧戀而出家故，於見、戒禁及以我語無執受故。

Ru-Yi Translation

They have gone forth having no regard or attachment left toward the desires, and have no clinging toward view, precept-and-vow, or self-referential statement.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

A genuine renunciant within the Buddha's teaching has left desire behind to go forth at all; holds right view rather than wrong view; follows precepts joined with wholesome dharma, never an outsider's arbitrary ascetic rule; and, knowing non-self even before full attainment, never truly asserts a self in the way the four graspings require.

82. Section: The Basis Explained Further — Two Kinds of Outsider, and Self-Statement Common to Both Classes

Those of wrongly-taught dharma have two distinctions: first, those who repeatedly generate hostile contention over view and craving; second, those able to enter mundane concentration.

Ru-Yi Translation

Relying on those who repeatedly generate hostile contention over view and craving, view-grasping is established; relying on those able to enter mundane concentration, precept-and-vow-grasping is established.

Debate-prone outsiders, attached to their own school's view, generate view-grasping; outsiders who attain mundane concentration through arbitrary ascetic rules generate precept-and-vow-grasping; and self-statement-grasping, unlike the other three, belongs to both householders and outsider renunciants alike, since neither understands non-self.

83. Section: The Name "View" Explained — the Sixty-Two Wrong Views Recapped

Among these, view is the sixty-two, as before, one should know.

此中見者，謂六十二，如前應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, view is the sixty-two, as before, one should know.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing fascicle 87

Rooted in self-view, the sixty-two divide into two halves by time-frame: eighteen views computing a self in the prior life (four eternalist theories, four partial-eternalist theories, two no-cause theories, four finite-or-infinite theories, four theories of confused evasion), and forty-four computing a self in the future life (sixteen theories of a percipient self, eight of a non-percipient self, eight of a neither-percipient-nor-non-percipient self, seven annihilationist theories, five present-life-nirvāṇa theories).

84. Section: "Precept-and-Vow" and "Self-Referential Statement" Explained, Closing the Two-Class Basis Entirely

Ru-Yi Translation

What is upheld in body and speech, following a wrongly discriminated view, is named "precept"; the dress, food, and deportment adopted in accordance with this is named "vow."

Ru-Yi Translation

Speaking of self through affirming its truth and asserting its abiding is named self-referential statement.

Precept is the restraint of body and speech that a wrong view prescribes; vow is the further dress, diet, and outward conduct built on top of it — the bodily analogue of a wrong-view's rules. Self-referential statement itself works in two steps: first grasping that a real, substantial "I" exists at all, then further grasping that this substance can be named and spoken of — the two together constitute clinging to a self in speech. With this, the two-class basis (約二品辨) is closed entirely.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

A second, complementary lens on the same four graspings: desire-grasping traces to craving for the objects and matters of sensual desire; view-grasping traces to craving for gain and honor bound up with clever-sounding theory; precept-and-vow-grasping traces to craving for concentration itself; and self-statement-grasping traces to craving for existence (the eternalist's view) or for non-existence (the annihilationist's). 86. Section: The Four Bonds, Established Only Among Outsiders, Closed Entirely

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing fascicle 87

The four bonds (四繫), the tenth of 廣說煩惱's sixteen divisions: the craving-body-bond, aversion-body-bond, precept-and-vow-grasping-body-bond, and this-is-truly-so-grasping-body-bond (also called the self-view-body-bond) — so named because each binds the mind to form, name, destination, or the object one dwells on, guaranteeing a body in the next existence. Craving binds through attachment to desire's objects; aversion, specific to the desire realm, prevents escape from it; precept-and-vow-grasping spans all three realms, found among concentration-seeking outsiders and desire-realm beings alike; and self-view-grasping binds through the affliction and karma that self-view itself continually breeds. With this, the four bonds is closed entirely.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, on the closing background sūtras (58-74) and their sixteen-part scheme

Possession (所有), the sixteenth and final division: unlike the arrow's unchecked momentum, affliction mixed with some remaining shame and conscience is continuous rather than instantaneous, and so remains restrainable — the craving-bond corresponds to the possession of craving, the aversion-bond to the possession of aversion, and the remaining two bonds to the possession of delusion. Sūtra 58 asks the Buddha how to observe past, present, and future so as to swiftly exhaust the contaminants; sūtras 59 through 74 repeat the identical structure, each substituting a different affliction-category in place of "exhausting the contaminants" — successively: all fetters (three), all bonds (three), all proclivities (ten), all secondary afflictions (the full fifty-three, combining the forty-eight just detailed with five more), all nine fetters, all four floods, all four yokes, all four graspings, the contact conjoined with ignorance, all five hindrances, all eight entanglements, all three defilements, all three cravings, the three of burning/view/possession, transforming wrong view into right view, and transforming ignorance into wisdom — sixteen sūtras in all, each illuminated by this treatise's corresponding division. With this, broadly explaining affliction (廣說煩惱) — the seventh of establishing's (安立攝) eight sub-topics, unfolding across all sixteen of its divisions (three contaminants, nine fetters, three bonds, seven latent tendencies, forty-eight secondary afflictions, entanglement, four floods, the yoke, four graspings, four bonds, the five hindrances' establishment, stumps, three defilements, scorching harm, the arrow, and possession) — is closed entirely.

94. Section: Broadly Explaining Karma Begins — Evil Conduct in Body, Speech, and Mind

Further, every unwholesome bodily karma is named evil conduct. As is said of bodily karma, one should know verbal karma and mental karma are likewise.

Chinese Source

復次，一切不善身業名為惡行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, every unwholesome bodily karma is named evil conduct.

Chinese Source

如說身業，語業、意業，當知亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

As is said of bodily karma, one should know verbal karma and mental karma are likewise.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Broadly explaining karma (廣說業) — the eighth and final sub-topic of establishing (安立攝) — opens, unfolding through karmic defilement (業雜染) first. Karma's own nature is intention (volition-as-thought) preceding action; unwholesome bodily karma is killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct; unwholesome verbal karma is lying, divisive speech, harsh speech, and idle chatter; unwholesome mental karma is greed, aversion, and wrong view — all together named evil conduct.

95. Section: Why Named "Evil Conduct," and What This Displays, Closing "Explaining the Substance and Meaning" Entirely

Because this evil karma manifests repeatedly, one is thereby either already following toward the evil destinies, or will in the future follow toward them, or is presently following toward them — therefore it is said to be named evil conduct. By this, the meaning of karmic defilement is displayed; the meaning of afflictive defilement has already been made clear before.

Chinese Source

由此惡業數現行故，於諸惡趣或已隨得、或當隨得、或現隨得，是故說彼名為惡行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because this evil karma manifests repeatedly, one is thereby either already following toward the evil destinies, or will in the future follow toward them, or is presently following toward them — therefore it is said to be named evil conduct.

Chinese Source

由此示現業雜染義。煩惱雜染前已顯了。

Ru-Yi Translation

By this, the meaning of karmic defilement is displayed; the meaning of afflictive defilement has already been made clear before.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Reading Notes (披尋記) on "already/future/presently following"

Evil conduct is so named because its repeated practice guarantees a connection to the evil destinies — whether that connection is presently only a latent seed (the coarse burden already planted), a future fruit yet to ripen, or a fruit already being experienced now. Sincere repentance can keep such karma from increasing further, so it need not inevitably lead to the evil destinies. With this, explaining the substance and meaning (釋體義) of evil conduct is closed entirely.

96. Section: Distinguishing the Establishment Begins — Two Theories of Karmic Defilement

Further, there are two theories establishing karmic defilement: first, the wrong theory; second, the correct theory.

Chinese Source

復次，有二安立業雜染論。一者、邪論，二者、正論。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, there are two theories establishing karmic defilement: first, the wrong theory; second, the correct theory.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Distinguishing the establishment (辨安立) opens: the outsiders' wrong theory of karma will be contrasted with the correct theory the Buddha teaches.

97. Section: The Wrong Theory Stated and Refuted, Closed Entirely

Speaking of the wrong theory: it is such a claim as this — if there is deliberate intention, then whatever unwholesome karma is thereby created will, in every case, definitely result in rebirth in an evil destiny. This theory then slanders the possibility that cultivating the holy life can attain nirvāṇa. Why is this? It is not easy to find any class of sentient being who, in the present life, is entirely without deliberately created unwholesome karma — how much more so across other lives. If such karma definitely draws forth an evil destiny, then there could be no liberation to be attained at all. Therefore, one should know, this is the wrong theory.

Chinese Source

言邪論者，謂如是說：若有故思，凡所造作諸不善業，一切決定當受惡趣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Speaking of the wrong theory: it is such a claim as this — if there is deliberate intention, then whatever unwholesome karma is thereby created will, in every case, definitely result in rebirth in an evil destiny.

Chinese Source

此論便謗修行梵行能證涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

This theory then slanders the possibility that cultivating the holy life can attain nirvāṇa.

Chinese Source

何以故？

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this?

Chinese Source

諸有情類不易可得，於現法中無有故思造不善業，況在餘生。若彼決定感惡趣者，便應無有解脫可得。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is not easy to find any class of sentient being who, in the present life, is entirely without deliberately created unwholesome karma — how much more so across other lives. If such karma definitely draws forth an evil destiny, then there could be no liberation to be attained at all.

Chinese Source

是故當知此為邪論。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore, one should know, this is the wrong theory.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Since every ordinary being's mind, still bound by innate self- and dharma-grasping, is rarely if ever free of some deliberate unwholesome intention, a theory that treats such karma as inevitably fatal to liberation would leave no one capable of ever attaining it — refuting itself. With this, the wrong theory (邪論) is closed entirely.

98. Section: The Correct Theory Begins — Karma Both Performed and Increased

If it is said thus: whoever deliberately creates unwholesome karma, and this karma is both performed and increased, will definitely, in the future, undergo the ripening of an undesirable, evil destiny —

Chinese Source

若如是說：諸有故思造不善業，此業亦作亦增長者，定於當來受不可愛惡趣異熟。

Ru-Yi Translation

If it is said thus: whoever deliberately creates unwholesome karma, and this karma is both performed and increased, will definitely, in the future, undergo the ripening of an undesirable, evil destiny —

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Decisions on the Grounds With Both Thought and Reflection (決擇‧有尋有伺等三地)

The correct theory (正論) opens, distinguishing karma merely performed from karma performed and repeatedly reinforced through continued deliberation — only the latter, "increased" karma, is said to definitely bear an evil-destiny fruit. Three conditions together carry one to the evil destinies: cultivating desire-realm affliction intensely, earnestly, and without interruption while seeing no fault in it; using that affliction as the basis for evil conduct in body, speech, and mind, both performed and increased; and thereby cutting off one's own wholesome qualities while adopting unwholesome ones.

99. Section: Karma Performed but Not Increased — Its Ripening Explained

If there is karma performed but not increased, then, relying on this or that feeling-conditioned-by-dharma as its basis, whatever is created may be either pleasant or painful; at the very time it is created, in the present life, this karma is definitely conditioned either toward pleasant feeling or toward painful feeling.

Chinese Source

若有雖作不增長者，彼彼法受為依止故，諸所造作或樂、或苦，當於造時，於現法中，此業決定或順樂受、或順苦受。

Ru-Yi Translation

If there is karma performed but not increased, then, relying on this or that feeling-conditioned-by-dharma as its basis, whatever is created may be either pleasant or painful; at the very time it is created, in the present life, this karma is definitely conditioned either toward pleasant feeling or toward painful feeling.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Reading Notes (披尋記) and the Compendium of Abhidharma (集論)

What determines a future pleasant or painful ripening is the wholesome or unwholesome nature of the karma itself — not the feeling that happened to accompany its performance. Four combinations illustrate this: joy now in creating the ten unwholesome acts, but suffering later (an evil-destiny ripening); suffering now in forcing oneself through the ten wholesome acts, but joy later; joy now and joy later, when wholesome practice is itself joyful; and suffering now and suffering later, when unwholesome acts are performed with a troubled conscience. The Compendium of Abhidharma details further: joy-accompanied karma may itself be wholesome (yielding joy), unwholesome (yielding suffering despite present ease — as with the wealthy who still commit the ten evils), or neutral (yielding a neutral ripening, as with a skilled but morally indifferent craft); suffering-accompanied karma follows the identical three-way pattern — wholesome practice pursued with great difficulty by the strongly afflicted still yields a good rebirth; wrong ascetic practice pursued with real hardship still yields suffering; neutral karma performed painfully still yields a neutral result.

100. Section: Repentance Distinguished — Its Absence and Its Presence

Whoever, having created such karma, has no repentance and does not cultivate its antidote — for such a person, whether this karma is further increased or not increased, though it is definitely conditioned toward ripening in the present life, it is turned instead to ripen as conditioned toward the evil destinies, and in the present life is able to obstruct liberation. Whoever, having created such karma, generates repentance and cultivates its antidote — for such a person, whether this karma is not increased or is further increased, though it is conditioned toward ripening in the evil destinies, it is turned instead to ripen as conditioned in the present life, and does not obstruct liberation.

Chinese Source

諸有造作如是業已，若無追悔、不修對治補特伽羅，彼於此業若更增長、若不增長，此業雖定順現法受，亦轉令成順惡趣受，於現法中能障解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whoever, having created such karma, has no repentance and does not cultivate its antidote — for such a person, whether this karma is further increased or not increased, though it is definitely conditioned toward ripening in the present life, it is turned instead to ripen as conditioned toward the evil destinies, and in the present life is able to obstruct liberation.

Chinese Source

諸有造作如是業已，若生追悔、修習對治補特伽羅，彼於此業若不增長、若更增長，此業雖是順惡趣受，亦轉令成順現法受，不障解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whoever, having created such karma, generates repentance and cultivates its antidote — for such a person, whether this karma is not increased or is further increased, though it is conditioned toward ripening in the evil destinies, it is turned instead to ripen as conditioned in the present life, and does not obstruct liberation.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Reading Notes (披尋記)

Karma is not fixed but transformable: unrepented, an otherwise light present-life ripening can worsen into a heavy future evil-destiny fruit that blocks liberation now; genuinely repented and countered through practice, an otherwise heavy evil-destiny-bound karma can lighten into a mild present-life ripening — "a heavy offense lightly repaid" — that does not block liberation at all.

101. Section: The Correct Theory Confirmed, Closing "Distinguishing the Establishment" Entirely

Therefore this theory is not named a slander against the possibility that cultivating the holy life can attain nirvāṇa; one should know this theory is the correct theory.

Chinese Source

是故此論，不名誹謗修習梵行能證涅槃。當知此論是為正論。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore this theory is not named a slander against the possibility that cultivating the holy life can attain nirvāṇa; one should know this theory is the correct theory.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Because karma's ripening can be transformed through repentance and antidote, liberation always remains genuinely possible — unlike the wrong theory's rigid determinism. With this, distinguishing the establishment (辨安立) — the wrong theory and the correct theory of karmic defilement — is closed entirely.

102. Section: Common to Every Kind of Karma Begins — the Antidote's Presence or Absence

Further, if one lacks the ten kinds of antidote, one is defiled by karmic defilement; if one meets with these ten, one attains purity.

Chinese Source

復次，若有闕於十種對治，為業雜染之所染汙；若有會遇如是十種，便得清淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if one lacks the ten kinds of antidote, one is defiled by karmic defilement; if one meets with these ten, one attains purity.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Common to every kind of karma (通諸業攝) opens with its first matter, the antidote (明對治): possessing or lacking ten specific antidotes determines whether one remains defiled by karmic defilement or attains purity from it.

103. Section: The Ten Kinds of Antidote Named

First, by such an antidote, though karma is performed, it is not increased, and so, with respect to the future, comes to an indefinite ripening. Second, by such an antidote, though not yet eternally cut off, it is no longer received. Third, by such an antidote, it is eternally cut off, freed from its binding. Fourth, guarding the gates of the faculties, one skillfully cultivates the body, wishing to cultivate the surpassing training in precepts. Fifth, having cultivated the surpassing training in precepts, one wishes to cultivate the surpassing training in mind. Sixth, having cultivated the surpassing training in mind, one wishes to cultivate the surpassing training in wisdom. Seventh, having cultivated the surpassing training in wisdom, one cuts off every contaminant. Eighth, cultivating with keen resolve. Ninth, cultivating over a long time. Tenth, cultivating the antidote through boundless approaches.

Chinese Source

一者、若由如是對治，雖有作業，而無增長，彼望當來成不定受。二者、若由如是對治，雖未永斷而更不受。三者、若由如是對治，永斷離繫。四者、守護諸根門故，善修其身，為欲修習增上戒學。五者、修習增上戒已，為欲修習增上心學。六者、修習增上心已，為欲修習增上慧學。七者、修習增上慧已，為斷諸漏。八者、猛利意樂修習。九者、長時修習。十者、無量門對治修習。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, by such an antidote, though karma is performed, it is not increased, and so, with respect to the future, comes to an indefinite ripening. Second, by such an antidote, though not yet eternally cut off, it is no longer received. Third, by such an antidote, it is eternally cut off, freed from its binding. Fourth, guarding the gates of the faculties, one skillfully cultivates the body, wishing to cultivate the surpassing training in precepts. Fifth, having cultivated the surpassing training in precepts, one wishes to cultivate the surpassing training in mind. Sixth, having cultivated the surpassing training in mind, one wishes to cultivate the surpassing training in wisdom. Seventh, having cultivated the surpassing training in wisdom, one cuts off every contaminant. Eighth, cultivating with keen resolve. Ninth, cultivating over a long time. Tenth, cultivating the antidote through boundless approaches.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The ten antidotes fall into three natural groups: the first three describe the antidote's own escalating power over karma itself — first preventing its increase, then preventing its further ripening at all, then eternally severing it; the next four trace the threefold training's own sequence — precepts, concentration, and wisdom, culminating in the exhaustion of every contaminant; and the last three describe the manner of cultivation itself — with keen resolve, over a long time, and through boundless approaches.

104. Section: Defilement and Purity Concluded, Closing "The Antidote" Entirely

If one does not meet with these ten kinds of antidote to karma, one is defiled by karmic defilement; contrary to this, one should know, is purity.

Chinese Source

若有不會如是十種業對治者，為業雜染之所染汙；與此相違，當知清淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one does not meet with these ten kinds of antidote to karma, one is defiled by karmic defilement; contrary to this, one should know, is purity.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Even wholesome karma performed without the uncontaminated wisdom of the Buddhadharma remains bound up with self-grasping and so still carries one to a merely human or heavenly rebirth — hence the need for the threefold training pursued with keen resolve, over long duration, and through boundless approaches, to reach true purification. With this, the antidote (明對治) is closed entirely.

Further, in the present life, one who dwells without properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma causes the unwholesome karma previously created to increase further, bringing defilement in the future life. One who dwells properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma is not defiled. What is it to dwell in the present life without properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma? What is it to dwell properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma?

Chinese Source

復次，於現法中，不善防護身語意業而住者，彼先造作惡不善業亦令增長，於當來世令其雜染。若善防護身語意業而住者，彼不雜染。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, in the present life, one who dwells without properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma causes the unwholesome karma previously created to increase further, bringing defilement in the future life. One who dwells properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma is not defiled.

Chinese Source

云何於現法中，不善防護身語意業而住？云何善防護身語意業而住？

Ru-Yi Translation

What is it to dwell in the present life without properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma? What is it to dwell properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma?

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Distinguishing proper dwelling (辨安住), the second matter under common to every kind of karma, opens with these two contrasting questions.

106. Section: Not Properly Guarded Dwelling Begins — Its Kind Explained

Dwelling without properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma: that is, as when someone is able to vow to be free of the entanglement that gives rise to unwholesome bodily, verbal, and mental karma, yet still gives rise to the ignorance conjoined with improper attention that is able to give rise to it, and still receives, learns, and follows wholesome bodily, verbal, and mental karma. By this cause and condition, in the present life, there is no difference in the affliction, the wrong desire and seeking, and the many sufferings thereby created.

Chinese Source

不善防護身語意業而住者，謂如有一，於諸不善身語意業纏所發起，能誓遠離；然於能起不正作意相應無明，猶故發起。又於諸善身語意業受學隨轉。

Ru-Yi Translation

Dwelling without properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma: that is, as when someone is able to vow to be free of the entanglement that gives rise to unwholesome bodily, verbal, and mental karma, yet still gives rise to the ignorance conjoined with improper attention that is able to give rise to it, and still receives, learns, and follows wholesome bodily, verbal, and mental karma.

Chinese Source

由此因緣，於現法中，於諸煩惱、邪欲尋求、所作眾苦無有差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

By this cause and condition, in the present life, there is no difference in the affliction, the wrong desire and seeking, and the many sufferings thereby created.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Reading Notes (披尋記)

Vowing outward restraint from unwholesome conduct is not enough if the root ignorance behind wrong intention remains unaddressed — even the merit-generating and unmoving karma (福行/不動行) built through faithfully following the Buddha's or a teacher's instructions still breeds future rebirth-linked defilement, since the underlying self-grasping is never actually cut.

107. Section: The Defilement This Breeds, Closing "Not Properly Guarded Dwelling" Entirely

Such a person, merely content with this vowed restraint, generates satisfaction with it, and in the present life does not arouse the noble path, nor realize nirvāṇa. Though dwelling with such guarding, in the present life temporarily not creating unwholesome karma, one is still bound by affliction's latent tendency and entanglement. Once life ends, future existence continues on; according to whatever body is received, defilement broadly arises again, conditioned by the prior karma.

Chinese Source

彼唯即於此誓受遠離，便生喜足，於現法中不起聖道，不證涅槃。彼雖如是防護而住，於現法中暫時不作惡不善業，然為煩惱隨眠纏縛。既終歿已，後有續生；隨所受身，依先業緣廣起雜染。

Ru-Yi Translation

Such a person, merely content with this vowed restraint, generates satisfaction with it, and in the present life does not arouse the noble path, nor realize nirvāṇa. Though dwelling with such guarding, in the present life temporarily not creating unwholesome karma, one is still bound by affliction's latent tendency and entanglement. Once life ends, future existence continues on; according to whatever body is received, defilement broadly arises again, conditioned by the prior karma.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Reading Notes (披尋記)

The many sufferings of birth and the rest follow karma, and karma itself follows affliction and wrong desire as its cause and condition — displaying, in sequence, the three defilements of affliction, karma, and rebirth. Mere satisfaction with outward precept-keeping, without pursuing the noble path itself, leaves the deeper root untouched. With this, not properly guarded dwelling (不善防護) is closed entirely.

108. Section: Properly Guarded Dwelling Begins — Its Distinguishing Feature

One who dwells properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma has this distinction: relying on that same vowed restraint, one creates no new karma; therefore, though old karma ripens, the moment it is touched, one is at once able to transform and expel it.

Chinese Source

若善防護身語意業而住者，有此差別。謂此依彼誓受遠離，不造新業，故業雖熟，暫觸異熟尋能變吐。

Ru-Yi Translation

One who dwells properly guarding bodily, verbal, and mental karma has this distinction: relying on that same vowed restraint, one creates no new karma; therefore, though old karma ripens, the moment it is touched, one is at once able to transform and expel it.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Properly guarded dwelling (善防護) opens: because no new karma is created, even the ripening of old karma is met and instantly transmuted rather than allowed to take root and spread.

109. Section: Accomplishing Proper Guarding — Not Content With Restraint Alone

Such a person does not generate satisfaction with this vowed restraint alone, and in the present life is able to arouse the noble path, and is also able to realize its fruit, nirvāṇa.

Chinese Source

彼唯於此誓受遠離不生喜足，於現法中能起聖道，亦能證得彼果涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

Such a person does not generate satisfaction with this vowed restraint alone, and in the present life is able to arouse the noble path, and is also able to realize its fruit, nirvāṇa.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Accomplishing proper guarded dwelling (成善護住) opens — unlike the merely restrained practitioner, this one presses further into cultivating concentration and wisdom, capable of realizing the noble path itself.

110. Section: The Two Kinds of Nirvāṇa Explained — With and Without Remainder

At that time, for as long as the sentient body continues, one constantly receives the various feelings brought about by prior karma. In the present life, this sentient body, up until the stage where the lifespan is not yet exhausted, continues constantly. Once the lifespan is exhausted, the sentient body is relinquished, and the life-faculty is no longer accomplished thereafter. By this cause and condition, consciousness and every feeling cease together, no longer continuing afterward — like a shadow-feeling together with the tree that is its consciousness, both entirely extinguished, so that nothing whatsoever can be designated.

Chinese Source

彼於爾時，乃至有識身相續住，恆受先業所感諸受。於現法中，彼有識身，乃至壽量未滅盡位，常相續住。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, for as long as the sentient body continues, one constantly receives the various feelings brought about by prior karma. In the present life, this sentient body, up until the stage where the lifespan is not yet exhausted, continues constantly.

Chinese Source

壽量若盡，捨有識身，於後命根更不成就。由是因緣，識與一切諸受俱滅，後不相續，彼如影受與其識樹皆滅盡故，遍於一切不可施設。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once the lifespan is exhausted, the sentient body is relinquished, and the life-faculty is no longer accomplished thereafter. By this cause and condition, consciousness and every feeling cease together, no longer continuing afterward — like a shadow-feeling together with the tree that is its consciousness, both entirely extinguished, so that nothing whatsoever can be designated.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Nirvāṇa with remainder is this life's continued body still receiving past karma's fruit, though no longer generating new affliction; nirvāṇa without remainder is what follows once the lifespan itself is exhausted — consciousness likened to a tree and feeling to its shadow, both vanishing together, so that no destination whatsoever, in any of the three realms and five destinies, can any longer be designated for the one who has attained it.

111. Section: Two Conditions Preventing Future Defilement, Closing "Freedom From Defilement" Entirely

At that time, by two causes and conditions, the karma previously created is unable to become a defilement in the future life: first, because affliction, which serves as its companion in bringing about defilement, is cut off without remainder; second, because, relying on this, the continued ripening of the formations that constitutes defilement is extinguished without remainder.

Chinese Source

彼於爾時，由二因緣，先所作業於當來世不能為染。一、由煩惱為其助伴令雜染者無餘斷故；二、由依此諸行相續成熟雜染無餘滅故。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, by two causes and conditions, the karma previously created is unable to become a defilement in the future life: first, because affliction, which serves as its companion in bringing about defilement, is cut off without remainder; second, because, relying on this, the continued ripening of the formations that constitutes defilement is extinguished without remainder.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The first condition is exhaustion-wisdom, eternally severing affliction's companionship with karma; the second is non-arising-wisdom, guaranteeing that the formations' continued ripening — the very engine of rebirth — can never resume. With this, freedom from defilement (由無雜染) is closed entirely.

112. Section: Attesting to Constant Abiding — the Six Kinds Explained

By these, one is fully able to attest to the six kinds of constant abiding.

Chinese Source

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Attesting to constant abiding (由證恆住) opens: an arhat's six constant abidings mean that whatever the six senses encounter, the mind rests always in supreme equanimity, no longer stirred to craving or aversion — grounded in these four qualities of mind: free of grudge, free of hatred, free of defilement, and undistortedly, fully liberated.

113. Section: The Difference From Prior Dwelling Concluded, Closing "Distinguishing Proper Dwelling" Entirely

若有於彼多所住者，於現法中，雖有種種諸惡不善業緣間雜，由此遠離，一向成善。由是因緣，當知此與先防護住有其差別。

Whoever abides largely in this way, in the present life, though various evil, unwholesome karmic conditions may still intermingle in appearance, because of this very freedom, their conduct is wholly and consistently good. Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

An arhat who has attained this undistorted, well-liberated mind can appear to encounter the residue of past unwholesome conditions, yet suffers only in body, never in mind — the decisive difference from the merely-restrained dwelling described earlier, which leaves the root of future defilement untouched. With this, distinguishing proper dwelling (辨安住), the second matter of common to every kind of karma, is closed entirely.

114. Section: Distinguishing Designations Begins — Positing Theories of Karmic Ripening

Distinguishing designations (辨施設), the third matter of common to every kind of karma, opens with theories of how karmic ripening is received — five marks distinguishing a defiled account from an undefiled one.

115. Section: The Five Defiling Marks Begin — Positing Wrong Cause and No Cause

What is named the five kinds of mark that constitute defilement?

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, positing the theory of wrong cause, and positing the theory of no cause, and positing wrong cause and no cause as having three faults.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The first defiling mark posits life's origin in a wrong cause, such as creation by Brahmā; the second posits no cause at all for sentient existence — both wrong views, and, together, said to carry three faults still to be explained.

116. Section: Wrong Cause and No Cause Explained

Among these, positing the theory of wrong cause: that is, as when someone gives rise to such a view, and establishes such a theory — if there is a person, whatever is received is entirely brought about by a prior cause; or, likewise, is said to be brought about by such causes as a sovereign creator or a transforming power. Positing the theory of no cause: that is, as when someone gives rise to such a view, and establishes such a theory — if there is a person, whatever is received, one should know, is entirely without cause or condition.

Chinese Source

此中施設惡因論者，謂如有一，起如是見，立如是論：若有士夫補特伽羅，諸所領受，一切皆是宿因所作。如是或謂自在、變化等因所作。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among these, positing the theory of wrong cause: that is, as when someone gives rise to such a view, and establishes such a theory — if there is a person, whatever is received is entirely brought about by a prior cause; or, likewise, is said to be brought about by such causes as a sovereign creator or a transforming power.

Chinese Source

施設無因論者，謂如有一，起如是見，立如是論：若有士夫補特伽羅，諸所領受，當知一切無因無緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Positing the theory of no cause: that is, as when someone gives rise to such a view, and establishes such a theory — if there is a person, whatever is received, one should know, is entirely without cause or condition.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The wrong-cause theory attributes all present experience either to karma from a prior life alone, or to a sovereign creator such as Īśvara or Brahmā; the no-cause theory denies any cause or condition at all. If everything is fixed by the past or by a controlling deity, present effort becomes meaningless, contradicting the plain fact that dharmas arise through causes and conditions.

117. Section: The Three Faults Asked About

Why is it that positing wrong cause and no cause has three faults?

Chinese Source

云何施設惡因、無因有三種過？

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is it that positing wrong cause and no cause has three faults?

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The question is posed before the three faults — failing to account for unwholesome feeling, slandering diligent effort, and slandering right wisdom — are explained one by one.

118. Section: The First Fault Begins — Neither Past Karma Alone Nor Present Karma Alone Explains Unwholesome Feeling

That is, in the present life, unwholesome feelings accompanied by unwholesome karma — whether attributed to past-life karma as cause, or to present-life karma as cause — in either case involve a fault. If it is said that this feeling has past-life unwholesome karma as its cause, then there would be a case where, relying on past unwholesome karma accompanied by pleasant feeling, a present pleasant unwholesome feeling arises — using past unwholesome karma as the cause for this arising does not accord with reason. Why is this? It is not the case that past unwholesome karma, in the present life, brings about a pleasant ripening — this alone accords with correct reason.

Chinese Source

謂現法中，不善俱行不善諸受，宿世業為因亦有過失，現法業為因亦有過失。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is, in the present life, unwholesome feelings accompanied by unwholesome karma — whether attributed to past-life karma as cause, or to present-life karma as cause — in either case involve a fault.

Chinese Source

若言此受宿世惡業以為因者，是則有一，依於不善諸樂法受，而有其樂不善受生，此用宿世諸不善業以為因生，不應道理。

Ru-Yi Translation

If it is said that this feeling has past-life unwholesome karma as its cause, then there would be a case where, relying on past unwholesome karma accompanied by pleasant feeling, a present pleasant unwholesome feeling arises — using past unwholesome karma as the cause for this arising does not accord with reason.

Chinese Source

何以故？非彼宿世諸不善業於現法中感樂異熟，應正道理。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this? It is not the case that past unwholesome karma, in the present life, brings about a pleasant ripening — this alone accords with correct reason.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Reading Notes (披尋記)

Karma's ripening follows its own wholesome or unwholesome nature, not the feeling that happened to accompany its creation; unwholesome karma, whatever feeling accompanied its performance, should properly ripen into suffering — so attributing a present pleasant feeling to past unwholesome karma, purely to excuse present enjoyment of wrongdoing as predetermined, does not hold up.

119. Section: Present Karma Alone Also Fails, Closing the First Fault Entirely

If it is said that this feeling uses present-life unwholesome karma as its cause, this then abandons one's own established theories of wrong cause and no cause — that whatever is received is entirely brought about by a prior cause, and so on at length. This is named the first fault.

Chinese Source

若言此受用現法中惡業為因，是則退失自意所立諸惡因論及無因論，謂諸所受皆宿因作，乃至廣說。

Ru-Yi Translation

If it is said that this feeling uses present-life unwholesome karma as its cause, this then abandons one's own established theories of wrong cause and no cause — that whatever is received is entirely brought about by a prior cause, and so on at length.

Chinese Source

是名初過。

Ru-Yi Translation

This is named the first fault.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

To fall back on present karma as the cause of present feeling simply abandons the wrong-cause and no-cause theories the outsider set out to defend in the first place — a self-refutation. With this, failing to account for unwholesome feeling (諸不善受因不成過), the first fault, is closed entirely.

120. Section: The Second Fault — Slandering Diligent Effort, Closed Entirely

Further, if it is said that every unwholesome dharma uses past-life unwholesome karma as its cause, then it would definitely follow that every wholesome dharma likewise uses past-life wholesome dharma as its cause — and so every antidote to the unwholesome, and the diligent effort born together with wholesome practice, would become entirely useless. This is named the second fault.

Chinese Source

又若說言：諸不善法皆用宿世惡業為因。是則決定所有善法，亦用宿世善法為因。如是所有不善對治，諸善加行俱生精進，皆成無用。如是名為第二過失。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if it is said that every unwholesome dharma uses past-life unwholesome karma as its cause, then it would definitely follow that every wholesome dharma likewise uses past-life wholesome dharma as its cause — and so every antidote to the unwholesome, and the diligent effort born together with wholesome practice, would become entirely useless. This is named the second fault.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

If wrong-cause theory is applied consistently, wholesome outcomes too must trace only to the past, leaving no room for present diligence to matter at all — slandering the very effort that makes the path possible. With this, slandering diligent effort (謗精進過), the second fault, is closed entirely.

121. Section: The Third Fault Begins — Slandering Right Wisdom, Two Challenges

Further, if there is presently no efficacy in human effort, then there should also be no careful, correct examination, relying on the wholesome or unwholesome, of what should and should not be done. Further, the wisdom of true reality would become useless — that is, the wisdom by which one knows, "this self should be transformed, this self should be accomplished." Because that transformation does not exist, this wisdom likewise does not exist; therefore the principle of true wisdom cannot be established.

Chinese Source

又若現在無有士用，是則應無依善不善審正觀察是所應作、所不應作。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if there is presently no efficacy in human effort, then there should also be no careful, correct examination, relying on the wholesome or unwholesome, of what should and should not be done.

Chinese Source

又如實智應成無用。謂了知已，此我應轉、此我應成。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the wisdom of true reality would become useless — that is, the wisdom by which one knows, "this self should be transformed, this self should be accomplished."

Chinese Source

彼非有故，此亦非有，故如實智理不成就。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because that transformation does not exist, this wisdom likewise does not exist; therefore the principle of true wisdom cannot be established.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Reading Notes (披尋記)

If nothing done now has any power to change outcomes, careful discernment of right and wrong action becomes pointless, and even the wisdom that recognizes "affliction should be transformed away, purity should be accomplished" is useless, since under this view such transformation could never actually happen.

122. Section: The Third Fault's Consequences, and the Three Faults Summarized, Closing "The Five Defiling Marks" Entirely

Ru-Yi Translation

Chinese Source

如是名為第三過失。

Ru-Yi Translation

This is named the third fault.

Chinese Source

若略說此，有三種過。謂現在世諸不善受因不成過、謗精進過、謗正智過。

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Once wisdom collapses, the whole path collapses in sequence — no mindfulness, no concentration, only confused wandering thought, ending in simple admiration for the fool's pursuit of pleasure and abandonment of the ascetic path altogether. Wrong-cause and no-cause theories together carry these three faults. With this, the five kinds of mark that constitute a defiled theory of karmic ripening (由五種相成其雜染) is closed entirely.
