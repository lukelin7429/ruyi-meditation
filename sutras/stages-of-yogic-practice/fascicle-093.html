Fascicle 93 · 瑜伽師地論卷第九十三 · Dependent Origination, Food, Truths, and the Elements Begins

Dependent Origination and the Rest Begins

Fascicle 93 opens with the treatise's own confirmation that the sense-bases section (處擇攝, fascicles 89–92) has been fully explained, and turns to the third of sūtra matters' four major divisions: dependent origination, food, the truths, and the elements (緣起食諦界擇攝), spanning fascicles 93–96 and decisively explaining four topics from the Saṃyukta Āgama through seven gates. This fascicle opens the first two gates — establishing (立等) and the two truths (二諦等) — beginning with establishing's own ten-topic summary verse, and closing its first topic, establishing (立), with the classical twelve-links exposition across the three time periods: arising from the prior into the middle period, arising from the middle into the latter period, and, within the middle period, either continuing to flow onward or turning toward purity.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress Source: Taishō T30, no. 1579, fascicle 93 Commentary guide: Master Cháng Bǎi's lecture notes

1. Section: The Sense-Bases Section Closes; Dependent Origination and the Rest Begins

Thus the sense-bases section has already been explained. Dependent origination, food, the truths, and the elements — I shall now speak of these.

Chinese Source

如是已說處擇攝。緣起、食、諦、界擇攝，我今當說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus the sense-bases section has already been explained. Dependent origination, food, the truths, and the elements — I shall now speak of these.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Sūtra matters' decisive analysis (擇攝) of the Saṃyukta Āgama has four divisions: the aggregates section (行擇攝, fascicles 85–88), the sense-bases section (處擇攝, fascicles 89–92) — both now complete — the section on dependent origination, food, the truths, and the elements (緣起食諦界擇攝, fascicles 93–96), and the factors-of-awakening section (菩提分法擇攝, fascicles 97–98). This third section works through seven gates: establishing and the rest, the two truths and the rest, and contact as condition and the rest, and cessation and the rest (these four decisively explain dependent origination; the first two are in this fascicle, the latter two in fascicle 94); the embrace of food (fascicle 94, the four foods); the embrace of the truths (fascicle 95, the four noble truths); and the embrace of the elements (fascicle 96, the eighteen elements).

2. Section: Marking and Explaining All Seven Gates — the Overall Summary Verse

An overall summary verse says: establishing and the rest; the two truths and the rest; contact as condition and the rest; cessation and the rest; food and the rest — and, lastly, suchness and the rest.

Chinese Source

總嗢柁南曰：

Ru-Yi Translation

An overall summary verse says:

Chinese Source

立等二諦等　以觸為緣等　有滅等食等　最後如理等

Ru-Yi Translation

Establishing and the rest; the two truths and the rest; contact as condition and the rest; cessation and the rest; food and the rest — and, lastly, suchness and the rest.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The verse's seven "and the rest" phrases mark the seven gates; the final "suchness and the rest" stands for the elements (界), covered in fascicle 96.

3. Section: Dependent Origination's First Particular Verse — Establishing and the Rest, Marked and Listed

A particular summary verse says: establishing, suffering, accumulation, the truths and their contemplation; embracing the noble teaching, subtle wisdom; the limit of deliberation, and observation; overweening pride, and, last, the very profound.

Chinese Source

別嗢柁南曰：

Ru-Yi Translation

A particular summary verse says:

Chinese Source

立苦聚諦觀　攝聖教微智　思量際觀察　上慢後甚深

Ru-Yi Translation

Establishing, suffering, accumulation, the truths and their contemplation; embracing the noble teaching, subtle wisdom; the limit of deliberation, and observation; overweening pride, and, last, the very profound.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens dependent origination's embrace (緣起攝), whose first matter, explaining its distinctions (釋差別), begins with its own first particular verse, establishing and the rest (立等), listing ten topics to be explained in the long-form section that follows: establishing, suffering, accumulation, the truths' contemplation, embracing the noble teaching, subtle wisdom, the limit of deliberation, observation, overweening pride, and the very profound.

4. Section: The Long-Form Explanation Begins — Establishing's Three Marks, Briefly Marked and Listed

Briefly, by three marks, one should know the distinctions in establishing dependent origination: first, arising from the prior period into the middle period; second, arising from the middle period into the latter period; third, within the middle period, once arisen, either continuing to flow onward, or turning toward purity.

Chinese Source

略由三相，應知建立緣起差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Briefly, by three marks, one should know the distinctions in establishing dependent origination.

Chinese Source

一、從前際中際得生；二、從中際後際得生；三、於中際生已隨轉及趣清淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, arising from the prior period into the middle period; second, arising from the middle period into the latter period; third, within the middle period, once arisen, either continuing to flow onward, or turning toward purity.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記

This opens the long-form explanation (長行釋), whose ten sections match the summary verse's ten topics, beginning with the first, establishing (立), itself opening with briefly marking and listing (略標列) three marks. The 披尋記 notes this three-mark analysis closely follows the Basic Section's own treatment in fascicle 9. This treatise's own causal scheme (unlike the śrāvaka three-lives/two-round-of-cause-and-effect model) uses a two-lives/one-round scheme: ignorance and formations as the drawing cause; the seeds of consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense-bases, contact, and feeling as what is drawn; craving, grasping, and existence as the generating cause; and birth, aging, and death as what is generated.

5. Section: Broadly Explaining the Marks Begins — Questioning the First Mark and Its Continuing Flow

Here, what is it to arise from the prior period into the middle period, and, within the middle period, once arisen, to continue flowing onward?

Chinese Source

此中云何從其前際中際得生，及於中際生已隨轉？

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, what is it to arise from the prior period into the middle period, and, within the middle period, once arisen, to continue flowing onward?

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens broadly explaining the marks (廣釋相), beginning with arising from the prior into the middle period and continuing to flow onward (從前際中際生及生隨轉), itself opening with questioning (徵).

6. Section: Distinguishing the Arising-and-Turning Mark Begins — the Prior Period's Embrace: Ignorance Conditions Formations

Namely, as with a certain one who, in a former life, lacking wisdom, conditioned by ignorance, creates and augments unwholesome, wholesome, or unmoving actions of body, speech, and mind.

Chinese Source

謂如有一，宿非聰慧，無明為緣，造作增長罪、福、不動身語意業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Namely, as with a certain one who, in a former life, lacking wisdom, conditioned by ignorance, creates and augments unwholesome, wholesome, or unmoving actions of body, speech, and mind.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the reply (釋), beginning with distinguishing the arising-and-turning mark (辨生轉相), distinguishing (辨) arising from the prior period (從前際生), and, within it, the prior period's embrace (前際攝) — ignorance conditions formations (無明緣行), the nineteen kinds of ignorance driving unwholesome, wholesome, or unmoving karma of body, speech, and mind.

7. Section: Formations Condition Consciousness, Closing "The Prior Period's Embrace" Entirely

By this as condition, the karma-following consciousness continues without interruption right up to the end of life, becoming the cause for the next life's continued arising of consciousness.

Chinese Source

由此為緣，隨業行識乃至命終隨轉不絕，能為後世續生識因。

Ru-Yi Translation

By this as condition, the karma-following consciousness continues without interruption right up to the end of life, becoming the cause for the next life's continued arising of consciousness.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, formations condition consciousness (行緣識) closes entirely, closing the prior period's embrace (前際攝2: 無明緣行/行緣識) entirely.

8. Section: The Middle Period's Embrace Begins — Its Stage of Arising

Thus, turning onward, there comes to be craving both inward and outward... When consciousness is about to generate its fruit, this craving arises before it as an accompanying condition. Once life has ended, by the prior period's cause, one's own embodiment arises in the present world.

Chinese Source

如是展轉有內外愛，

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus, turning onward, there comes to be craving both inward and outward —

Chinese Source

識生果時，能為助伴現前而起。既命終已，由前際因，於現在世自體得生。

Ru-Yi Translation

When consciousness is about to generate its fruit, this craving arises before it as an accompanying condition. Once life has ended, by the prior period's cause, one's own embodiment arises in the present world.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記

This opens the middle period's embrace (中際攝), beginning with its stage of arising (生起位). Inward craving is craving for one's own embodiment, born of the delusion that fails to see non-self; outward craving is craving for the objective world, born of the delusion that fails to see conditions as illusion-like. Both, present at the moment of death, moisten the seeds of consciousness, name-and-form, the six bases, contact, and feeling laid down in the prior period, turning them into existence and giving rise to the present embodiment.

9. Section: Its Stage of Growth, Closing "The Middle Period's Embrace" and "Arising from the Prior Period" Entirely

Once born, gradually within the mother's womb, by consciousness as condition, the continuing resultant consciousness turns on without interruption, sustaining the kalala and the rest. Name-and-form's stages grow ever more distinguished, from the womb onward, right up to old age.

Chinese Source

生已漸次於母腹中，因識為緣，續生果識隨轉不絕，任持所有羯羅藍等。名色分位後後殊勝，始從胎藏，乃至衰老。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once born, gradually within the mother's womb, by consciousness as condition, the continuing resultant consciousness turns on without interruption, sustaining the kalala and the rest. Name-and-form's stages grow ever more distinguished, from the womb onward, right up to old age.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

The embryo passes through the eight classical stages — kalala, arbuda, peśī, ghana, praśākhā, then hair-and-nails, the sense-faculties, and their full form — each seven days marking a further transformation, through birth, growth, and on to old age. With this, its stage of growth (增長位) closes entirely, closing the middle period's embrace (中際攝2: 生起位/增長位) entirely, which closes arising from the prior period (從前際生2: 前際攝/中際攝) entirely.

10. Section: Continuing to Flow Onward Once Arisen Begins — Bringing Forth the Two Mutual Supports, Name-and-Form Conditions Consciousness

Further, this very consciousness, when about to continue arising, can bring about the karma that generates and its resultant fruit. The resultant, arisen consciousness in turn relies on name-and-form to continue turning — namely, turning by relying on the eye and the rest, its six supports... By this it is said: name-and-form conditions consciousness.

Chinese Source

又即此識當續生時，能感生業與異熟果。異熟生識復依名色相續而轉，謂依眼等六依轉故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, this very consciousness, when about to continue arising, can bring about the karma that generates and its resultant fruit. The resultant, arisen consciousness in turn relies on name-and-form to continue turning — namely, turning by relying on the eye and the rest, its six supports.

Chinese Source

由是說言：名色緣識。

Ru-Yi Translation

By this it is said: name-and-form conditions consciousness.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記

This opens continuing to flow onward once arisen (生已隨轉), beginning with bringing forth the two mutual supports (出二依止) — consciousness relying on name-and-form, name-and-form relying on consciousness. The consciousness that enters the womb in its first moment is called true resultant, the ālaya-consciousness; the six operating consciousnesses it subsequently gives rise to, drawn out by that same karmic power, are called resultant-born consciousness, and these rely on name-and-form (the six bases) to keep turning.

11. Section: Explaining the Mark of Name-and-Form Conditioning Consciousness

The co-arisen five faculties are called form; the uninterrupted-cessation and the rest are called name — each, as fitting, able to serve as consciousness's support. Consciousness relies on these, and thus, right up to the end of life, turns again and again.

Chinese Source

俱生五根說名為色，無間滅等說名為名，隨其所應，能與六識作所依止。識依彼故，乃至命終數數隨轉。

Ru-Yi Translation

The co-arisen five faculties are called form; the uninterrupted-cessation and the rest are called name — each, as fitting, able to serve as consciousness's support. Consciousness relies on these, and thus, right up to the end of life, turns again and again.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記

"Uninterrupted-cessation and the rest" names two aspects of the mental faculty: the uninterrupted-cessation mental faculty (each prior moment of consciousness ceasing as the condition for the next), common to all three vehicles, and the afflicted mental faculty, the seventh consciousness, found only in the Mahāyāna account — hence the "and the rest."

12. Section: Consciousness Conditions Name-and-Form, Closing "Bringing Forth the Two Mutual Supports" — Distinguishing Each Individually — Entirely

Further, the five form faculties — the forms produced by the great elements the faculties rely on, and the forms produced by the great elements the faculties occupy, together with every other name — are all held and sustained by that consciousness, so that the faculties fall within a continuum, turning on without interruption... By this it is said: consciousness conditions name-and-form, name-and-form conditions consciousness.

Chinese Source

又五色根，根依大種、根處大種所生諸色及諸餘名，由彼執持，所有根等墮在相續，流轉不絕。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the five form faculties — the forms produced by the great elements the faculties rely on, and the forms produced by the great elements the faculties occupy, together with every other name — are all held and sustained by that consciousness, so that the faculties fall within a continuum, turning on without interruption.

Chinese Source

由是故言：識緣名色，名色緣識。

Ru-Yi Translation

By this it is said: consciousness conditions name-and-form, name-and-form conditions consciousness.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Mahāvibhāṣā

With this, consciousness conditions name-and-form (識緣名色) closes entirely, closing distinguishing each individually (別辨2: 名色緣識/識緣名色) entirely. The Mahāvibhāṣā distinguishes the two formulas six ways — displaying consciousness's function versus name-and-form's function; each as the other's mutual support, like two bundled reeds leaning on each other, or an elephant, horse, or boat together with its driver; the moment of rebirth versus the sustained life that follows; consciousness generating name-and-form versus consciousness abiding by relying on it; what is generated versus what generates; and prior-to-posterior sequence versus simultaneous co-arising.

13. Section: The Simile of Bundled Reeds, Closing "Continuing to Flow Onward Once Arisen" and "Distinguishing the Arising-and-Turning Mark" Entirely

In the present life, they are just like bundled reeds leaning on each other, turning on together right up to the sustaining of the lifespan.

Chinese Source

於現在世，猶如束蘆相依而轉，乃至壽住。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the present life, they are just like bundled reeds leaning on each other, turning on together right up to the sustaining of the lifespan.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, the simile of bundled reeds (喻如束蘆) closes entirely, closing continuing to flow onward once arisen (生已隨轉2: 出二依止/喻如束蘆) entirely.

Thus is it called arising from the prior period into the middle period through the formations of dependent origination, and, within the middle period, once arisen, continuing to flow onward.

如是名為從其前際，中際緣起諸行得生，於其中際生已隨轉。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus is it called arising from the prior period into the middle period through the formations of dependent origination, and, within the middle period, once arisen, continuing to flow onward.

With this, the concluding statement (結) closes entirely, closing distinguishing the arising-and-turning mark (辨生轉相2: 辨/結) entirely.

15. Section: Distinguishing Differences Begins — by the Four Kinds of Birth: Referring to Womb-Birth

One should know that here, the sequence of turning just described is stated in terms of womb-birth.

Chinese Source

當知此中，依胎生者說轉次第。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, the sequence of turning just described is stated in terms of womb-birth.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens distinguishing differences (料簡差別), beginning with distinguishing by the four kinds of birth (約四生辨), the first being womb-birth, the model followed above.

16. Section: Distinguishing Egg-Birth and Moisture-Birth

Egg-birth and moisture-birth have no further distinction apart from what occurs within the mother's womb.

Chinese Source

卵生、濕生，除在母腹有餘差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Egg-birth and moisture-birth have no further distinction apart from what occurs within the mother's womb.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Buddhist classification of birth-types sorts by the environment a being is born into rather than by biological reproduction — insects born from eggs, such as moths or mosquitoes, still count as moisture-born in this scheme.

17. Section: Distinguishing Transformation-Birth Individually Begins — the Desire and Form Realms

Beings possessing form who undergo transformation-birth in the desire realm or form realm, at the very moment of birth, have their faculties fully complete — this differs from the rest.

Chinese Source

有色有情，在欲、色界受化生者，於初生時諸根圓滿，與餘差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Beings possessing form who undergo transformation-birth in the desire realm or form realm, at the very moment of birth, have their faculties fully complete — this differs from the rest.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens distinguishing transformation-birth individually (別辨化生), beginning with the desire and form realms (欲色界) — unlike womb-, egg-, or moisture-birth, where faculties may be incomplete, transformation-birth (found among the gods, some humans at the aeon's start, and certain hell-, hungry-ghost-, and animal-realm beings) always brings the six faculties into being all at once, fully formed.

18. Section: The Formless Realm, Closing "Distinguishing Transformation-Birth Individually" and "By the Four Kinds of Birth" Entirely

Among the class of beings in the formless realm, consciousness relies on name and the seed of form, and name and the seed of form turn by relying on consciousness. Because that consciousness contains the seed of form, form — though interrupted — will arise again later. Such is the distinction found here.

Chinese Source

在無色界諸有情類，識依於名及色種子，名及色種依識而轉。由彼識中有色種故，色雖間斷，後當更生。如是名為此中差別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the class of beings in the formless realm, consciousness relies on name and the seed of form, and name and the seed of form turn by relying on consciousness. Because that consciousness contains the seed of form, form — though interrupted — will arise again later. Such is the distinction found here.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

In the formless realm, form does not manifest, but its seed remains held within the ālaya-consciousness; once the formless realm's lifespan ends, that being can still take rebirth in a realm possessing form. With this, the formless realm (無色界) closes entirely, closing distinguishing transformation-birth individually (別辨化生2: 欲色界/無色界) entirely, closing by the four kinds of birth (約四生辨3: 指依胎生/簡卵濕生/別辨化生) entirely.

19. Section: Distinguishing by the Three Kinds of Karma, Closing "Distinguishing Differences" and "Arising from the Prior into the Middle Period, and Continuing to Flow Onward" Entirely

By wholesome karma, one is born into the desire realm's human and heavenly destinies; by unwholesome karma, one is born into the miserable destinies; by unmoving karma, one is born into the form or formless realms.

Chinese Source

由福業故，生於欲界人天兩趣；由罪業故，生惡趣中；由不動業，生色無色。

Ru-Yi Translation

By wholesome karma, one is born into the desire realm's human and heavenly destinies; by unwholesome karma, one is born into the miserable destinies; by unmoving karma, one is born into the form or formless realms.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, distinguishing by the three kinds of karma (約三業辨) closes entirely, closing distinguishing differences (料簡差別2: 約四生辨/約三業辨) entirely, closing the reply (釋2: 辨生轉相/料簡差別) entirely, closing arising from the prior into the middle period, and continuing to flow onward (從前際中際生及生隨轉2: 徵/釋) entirely — the first mark's full explanation.

20. Section: Arising from the Middle into the Latter Period, and Turning Toward Purity Begins — the General Questioning

What is it called to arise from the middle period into the latter period through the formations of dependent origination? And what is it not to arise, so that by this non-arising purity is realized?

Chinese Source

云何名為從其中際，後際緣起諸行得生？云何不生，由不生故證得清淨？

Ru-Yi Translation

What is it called to arise from the middle period into the latter period through the formations of dependent origination? And what is it not to arise, so that by this non-arising purity is realized?

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the second mark, arising from the middle into the latter period, and turning toward purity (從中際後際得生及趣清淨), beginning with the general questioning (總徵).

21. Section: Explaining Each Individually Begins — Arising From the Middle Into the Latter Period: the Middle Period's Embrace, Marking and Listing Its Two Fruits

Namely, such a person, having arisen within the middle period, experiences two fruits obtained from prior karma. First, experiencing the internal resultant fruit; second, experiencing the dominant fruit born of feeling toward objective conditions.

Chinese Source

謂彼如是於中際生補特伽羅，領受先業所得二果。一者、領受內異熟果，二者、領受境界所生受增上果。

Ru-Yi Translation

Namely, such a person, having arisen within the middle period, experiences two fruits obtained from prior karma. First, experiencing the internal resultant fruit; second, experiencing the dominant fruit born of feeling toward objective conditions.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens explaining each individually (別釋), beginning with arising from the middle into the latter period (從中際後際生), and, within it, distinguishing each individually (別辨), and the middle period's embrace (中際攝), marking and listing its two fruits (標列二果) — the internal resultant fruit is one's own five-aggregate embodiment; the dominant fruit is the pleasant, painful, or neutral feeling arising from the surrounding world one experiences.

22. Section: Explaining Its Cause Individually Begins — the Drawing Cause: What Draws — Ignorance Conditions Formations

Because of hearing improper teachings, or because of the power of habituation from former lives, one gives rise to delusion regarding these two fruits. Because of delusion regarding the internal resultant fruit, one fails to truly know the suffering of future rebirth to come. By this power of ignorance regarding both the prior and latter periods, one creates and augments formations just as before.

Chinese Source

彼由聽聞不正法故，或由先世串習力故，於二種果發起愚癡。彼由於內異熟果中有愚癡故，不能如實了知當來後有生苦。由此前際、後際無明增上力故，如前造作增長諸行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because of hearing improper teachings, or because of the power of habituation from former lives, one gives rise to delusion regarding these two fruits. Because of delusion regarding the internal resultant fruit, one fails to truly know the suffering of future rebirth to come. By this power of ignorance regarding both the prior and latter periods, one creates and augments formations just as before.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens explaining its cause individually (別釋其因), beginning with the drawing cause (引因), distinguishing its marks (辨相), and, within it, what draws (能引) — ignorance conditions formations (無明緣行). Not understanding that the resultant embodiment is not self, nor its surrounding conditions self's-possession, one continues creating wholesome, unwholesome, and unmoving karma exactly as before, laying down further seeds for a future fruit.

23. Section: Formations Condition Consciousness Begins — Clarifying Its Arising

By this new karma perfuming and transforming consciousness, one acts in accordance with karma within the present dharma. Thus, ignorance serving as condition, formations arise; formations serving as condition, consciousness is caused to transform.

Chinese Source

由此新業熏變識故，於現法中隨業而行。如是無明以為緣故，諸行得生；行為緣故，令識轉變。

Ru-Yi Translation

By this new karma perfuming and transforming consciousness, one acts in accordance with karma within the present dharma. Thus, ignorance serving as condition, formations arise; formations serving as condition, consciousness is caused to transform.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens formations condition consciousness (行緣識), beginning with clarifying its arising (明緣起) — new karma perfumes the ālaya-consciousness, planting seeds for the destinies of the three realms to come.

24. Section: Bringing Forth Its Essential Nature, Closing "Formations Condition Consciousness" and "What Draws" Entirely

One should know that this consciousness, within the present dharma, is merely of the nature of a cause, embracing what will become future consciousness's fruit — and, in view of every such continuum, is spoken of as the six consciousness-bodies.

Chinese Source

當知此識，於現法中但是因性，攝受當生諸識果故，約就一切相續為名，說六識身。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that this consciousness, within the present dharma, is merely of the nature of a cause, embracing what will become future consciousness's fruit — and, in view of every such continuum, is spoken of as the six consciousness-bodies.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, bringing forth its essential nature (出體性) closes entirely, closing formations condition consciousness (行緣識2: 明緣起/出體性) entirely, closing what draws (能引2: 無明緣行/行緣識) entirely.

25. Section: What Is Drawn

Further, this very consciousness is followed by the seed of future name-and-form; the seed of name-and-form is in turn followed by the seed of the future six sense-bases; the seed of the six sense-bases is in turn followed by the seed of future contacts; this seed of contact is in turn followed by the seed of future feelings.

Chinese Source

又即此識，當來後有名色種子之所隨逐；名色種子，復為當來後有六處種子隨逐；六處種子，復為當來後有諸觸種子隨逐；此觸種子，復為當來後有諸受種子隨逐。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, this very consciousness is followed by the seed of future name-and-form; the seed of name-and-form is in turn followed by the seed of the future six sense-bases; the seed of the six sense-bases is in turn followed by the seed of future contacts; this seed of contact is in turn followed by the seed of future feelings.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens what is drawn (所引) — one karmic act plants, in a single stroke, the seeds of consciousness, name-and-form, the six bases, contact, and feeling all together, each seed's own character (dull or sharp faculties, favorable or unfavorable surroundings) already latent within it.

26. Section: Concluding the Name, Closing "Distinguishing Its Marks" and "The Drawing Cause" Entirely

One should know this is called, within the middle period, the drawing cause for future existence — with consciousness first and feeling last, universally able to draw forth every embodiment.

Chinese Source

當知是名於其中際後有引因。由識為先，受為最後，遍能牽引諸自體故。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know this is called, within the middle period, the drawing cause for future existence — with consciousness first and feeling last, universally able to draw forth every embodiment.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, concluding the name (結名) closes entirely, closing distinguishing its marks (辨相2: 能引/所引) entirely, closing the drawing cause (引因2: 辨相/結名) entirely.

27. Section: The Generating Cause Begins — Feeling Conditions Craving

Thus, having already, by delusion regarding the prior resultant fruit, drawn forth future existence, further, by the second delusion — regarding the dominant fruit born of objective conditions — one gives rise to craving conditioned by feeling toward those conditions.

Chinese Source

如是由先異熟果愚引後有已，復由第二境界所生增上果愚緣境界受，發生貪愛。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus, having already, by delusion regarding the prior resultant fruit, drawn forth future existence, further, by the second delusion — regarding the dominant fruit born of objective conditions — one gives rise to craving conditioned by feeling toward those conditions.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the generating cause (生因), beginning with feeling conditions craving (受緣愛) — pleasant feeling breeds craving, painful feeling breeds aversion, neutral feeling breeds delusion, craving being singled out here as the most severe of the resulting afflictions.

28. Section: Craving Conditions Grasping

By this craving, one seeks either the objects of desire, or continued existence itself. Further, one grasps at the grasping of desire, or grasps at views, precepts and vows, or the doctrine of self.

Chinese Source

由此愛故，或求諸欲、或求諸有。又取欲取，或取見、戒禁、我語取。

Ru-Yi Translation

By this craving, one seeks either the objects of desire, or continued existence itself. Further, one grasps at the grasping of desire, or grasps at views, precepts and vows, or the doctrine of self.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Grasping intensifies craving into active pursuit at any cost — grasping the objects of desire themselves, grasping wrong views as ultimate, grasping ascetic precepts and vows (such as the cow-vow or dog-vow) as the path to heaven or liberation, or grasping the doctrine of a real self, sacrificing others' welfare for one's own.

29. Section: Grasping Conditions Existence, Closing "The Generating Cause" and "Explaining Its Cause Individually" and "The Middle Period's Embrace" Entirely

Having grasped these graspings, craving and grasping together moisten the previously drawn cause, which then turns into what is called existence — this being included within what generates future arising.

Chinese Source

取諸取已，愛、取和合潤先引因，轉名為有，是當生起因所攝故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having grasped these graspings, craving and grasping together moisten the previously drawn cause, which then turns into what is called existence — this being included within what generates future arising.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, grasping conditions existence (取緣有) closes entirely, closing the generating cause (生因3: 受緣愛/愛緣取/取緣有) entirely, closing explaining its cause individually (別釋其因2: 引因/生因) entirely, closing the middle period's embrace (中際攝2: 標列二果/別釋其因) entirely.

30. Section: The Latter Period's Embrace Begins — Distinguishing by Way of Its Own Embodiment: Arising as the Drawing Cause Draws

This existence, without interruption, once life has ended, gives rise — just as the drawing cause drew forth — to the formations with consciousness first and feeling last, whether arising gradually or arising all at once.

Chinese Source

此有無間，既命終已，如其引因所引諸行，識為最初，受為最後，或漸次生、或復頓生。

Ru-Yi Translation

This existence, without interruption, once life has ended, gives rise — just as the drawing cause drew forth — to the formations with consciousness first and feeling last, whether arising gradually or arising all at once.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the latter period's embrace (後際攝), beginning with distinguishing by way of its own embodiment (約自體辨), and, within it, arising as the drawing cause draws (如引因生) — gradual arising for womb-, egg-, and moisture-birth; all-at-once for transformation-birth.

31. Section: Arising Relying on the Generating Cause, Closing "Distinguishing by Way of Its Own Embodiment" Entirely

Thus one should know that, within the present dharma, first, by feeling produced from ignorant contact as condition, craving arises; craving as condition, next grasping arises; grasping as condition, this turns into existence; existence as condition, birth is then obtained; birth as condition, aging, sickness, death, and the rest of suffering's distinctions appear in due sequence.

Chinese Source

如是應知於現法中，初用無明觸所生受為緣生愛；愛為緣故，次生於取；取為緣故，轉成其有；有為緣故，當生得生；生為緣故，老病死等眾苦差別次第現前。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus one should know that, within the present dharma, first, by feeling produced from ignorant contact as condition, craving arises; craving as condition, next grasping arises; grasping as condition, this turns into existence; existence as condition, birth is then obtained; birth as condition, aging, sickness, death, and the rest of suffering's distinctions appear in due sequence.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, arising relying on the generating cause (依生因生) closes entirely, closing distinguishing by way of its own embodiment (約自體辨2: 如引因生/依生因生) entirely.

32. Section: Distinguishing by Way of the Place of Rebirth, Closing "The Latter Period's Embrace" and "Distinguishing Each Individually" Entirely

One should know that here, sometimes the place where one is to be born manifests directly; sometimes only its seed continues to follow along.

Chinese Source

當知此中，或有處所生處現前，或有處所種子隨逐。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, sometimes the place where one is to be born manifests directly; sometimes only its seed continues to follow along.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This distinguishes the immediately-following rebirth from the deferred rebirth whose seed merely continues to follow, dormant, within the ālaya-consciousness. With this, distinguishing by way of the place of rebirth (約依處辨) closes entirely, closing the latter period's embrace (後際攝2: 約自體辨/約依處辨) entirely, closing distinguishing each individually (別辨2: 中際攝/後際攝) entirely.

33. Section: The General Conclusion, Closing "Arising From the Middle Into the Latter Period" Entirely

Thus, within the middle period, ignorance conditioning formations, feeling conditioning craving, and the rest, are able to generate the latter period's formations of dependent origination.

Chinese Source

如是中際無明緣行、受緣愛等，能生後際緣起諸行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus, within the middle period, ignorance conditioning formations, feeling conditioning craving, and the rest, are able to generate the latter period's formations of dependent origination.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

With this, the general conclusion (總結) closes entirely, closing arising from the middle into the latter period (從中際後際生2: 別辨/總結) entirely — the classical twelve links now laid out in full: ignorance and formations as the drawing factors; consciousness, name-and-form, the six bases, contact, and feeling as what is drawn; craving, grasping, and existence as the generating factors; birth and aging-and-death as what is generated.

34. Section: Turning Toward Purity Begins — Attaining the Pure Wisdom-Vision

If, within the present dharma, one hears the Dharma from another, or has already, in a former life, accumulated the provisions — by this as cause, one is able to rightly contemplate the two fruit-natured formations. Whether toward their cause, their cessation, or the practice leading to cessation, applying proper attention and contemplating them, right view arises; and, toward the truths, one gradually attains the pure wisdom-vision of the learner and, ultimately, of the one beyond learning.

Chinese Source

若現法中從他聞法，或於先世已集資糧，由彼為因，能於二種果性諸行如理思惟。若於彼因、若於彼滅、若趣滅行如理作意，思惟彼故，發生正見，又於諸諦漸次獲得有學、無學清淨智見。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, within the present dharma, one hears the Dharma from another, or has already, in a former life, accumulated the provisions — by this as cause, one is able to rightly contemplate the two fruit-natured formations. Whether toward their cause, their cessation, or the practice leading to cessation, applying proper attention and contemplating them, right view arises; and, toward the truths, one gradually attains the pure wisdom-vision of the learner and, ultimately, of the one beyond learning.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the third mark, turning toward purity once arisen in the latter period (後際不生趣清淨), beginning with attaining the pure wisdom-vision (得清淨智見) — right understanding of the twelve links as suffering and its origin, and of their cessation as the path, ripens through the stages from stream-entry to arhatship.

35. Section: Realizing Pure Liberation Begins — Liberation Through Wisdom

By the power of such wisdom-vision, one is able to sever ignorance and craving without remainder; by their severance, the failure to truly know their objects, and the feelings produced by ignorant contact, are likewise severed accordingly; by this severance, within the present dharma, through freedom from ignorance, one realizes liberation through wisdom.

Chinese Source

彼由如是智見力故，能無餘斷無明及愛；由彼斷故，即彼所緣不如實知，諸無明觸所生諸受亦復隨斷；由此斷故，於現法中，由離無明，證慧解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

By the power of such wisdom-vision, one is able to sever ignorance and craving without remainder; by their severance, the failure to truly know their objects, and the feelings produced by ignorant contact, are likewise severed accordingly; by this severance, within the present dharma, through freedom from ignorance, one realizes liberation through wisdom.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens realizing pure liberation (證清淨解脫), beginning with liberation through wisdom (慧解脫) — severing both the discriminated and innate seeds of ignorance and craving brings arhatship.

36. Section: Liberation of Mind, Closing "Realizing Pure Liberation" Entirely

Further, within the mind associated with the feelings produced by ignorant contact, all the associated afflictions of craving become, for that mind, likewise unbound; by freedom from craving, one realizes liberation of mind.

Chinese Source

又無明觸所生諸受相應心中，所有相應貪愛煩惱，彼於其心亦得離繫；由離貪故，證心解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, within the mind associated with the feelings produced by ignorant contact, all the associated afflictions of craving become, for that mind, likewise unbound; by freedom from craving, one realizes liberation of mind.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Yogācārabhūmi Commentary

Per the commentary: through ignorance's cessation, liberation through wisdom is attained; through craving's exhaustion, liberation of mind is attained. With this, liberation of mind (心解脫) closes entirely, closing realizing pure liberation (證清淨解脫2: 慧解脫/心解脫) entirely.

37. Section: Accomplishing the Dharma of Non-Arising Begins — the Absence of the Drawing Cause

Further, by that very cessation of ignorance, all the formations — consciousness and the rest, right up to feeling — that would arise for future existence, dependent on ignorance not yet severed, are simply unable to arise; this accomplishes the dharma of non-arising. Hence it is said: ignorance having ceased, formations cease accordingly; in due sequence, right up to the resultant contacts ceasing, the resultant feelings cease accordingly.

Chinese Source

又即由彼無明滅故，諸有無明猶未斷時，依於後際應生行識乃至諸受，皆不得生，成不生法。是故說言：無明滅故，諸行隨滅；次第乃至異熟所生諸觸滅故，異熟所生諸受隨滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, by that very cessation of ignorance, all the formations — consciousness and the rest, right up to feeling — that would arise for future existence, dependent on ignorance not yet severed, are simply unable to arise; this accomplishes the dharma of non-arising. Hence it is said: ignorance having ceased, formations cease accordingly; in due sequence, right up to the resultant contacts ceasing, the resultant feelings cease accordingly.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens accomplishing the dharma of non-arising (成不生法), beginning with the absence of the drawing cause (無牽引因) — the arhat's mind still carries seeds accumulated over countless past aeons, but plants no new ones.

38. Section: The Absence of the Generating Cause, Closing "Accomplishing the Dharma of Non-Arising" Entirely

Further, within the present dharma, ignorance having ceased, ignorant contact ceases; ignorant contact having permanently ceased, the feeling produced from ignorant contact ceases; the feeling produced from ignorant contact having permanently ceased, craving ceases accordingly; craving having ceased, then, as said before, all the formations — grasping and the rest, right up to affliction — as the final boundary, all cease, accomplishing the dharma of non-arising.

Chinese Source

又現法中，無明滅故，無明觸滅；由無明觸永得滅故，從無明觸所生受滅；由無明觸所生諸受永得滅故，愛亦隨滅；由愛滅故，如前說名所有取等，乃至損惱以為後邊諸行皆滅，成不生法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, within the present dharma, ignorance having ceased, ignorant contact ceases; ignorant contact having permanently ceased, the feeling produced from ignorant contact ceases; the feeling produced from ignorant contact having permanently ceased, craving ceases accordingly; craving having ceased, then, as said before, all the formations — grasping and the rest, right up to affliction — as the final boundary, all cease, accomplishing the dharma of non-arising.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, the absence of the generating cause (無生起因) closes entirely, closing accomplishing the dharma of non-arising (成不生法2: 無牽引因/無生起因) entirely.

39. Section: Realizing Nirvāṇa Begins — Nirvāṇa With Residue: Distinguishing It From Continued Flow

Within the present dharma, all these formations no longer flow onward. Because they no longer flow onward, within the present dharma one abides in the realm of nirvāṇa with residue remaining — this is called realizing nirvāṇa in this very life.

Chinese Source

於現法中，如是諸行皆不流轉。不流轉故，於現法中住有餘依般涅槃界，名為證得現法涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the present dharma, all these formations no longer flow onward. Because they no longer flow onward, within the present dharma one abides in the realm of nirvāṇa with residue remaining — this is called realizing nirvāṇa in this very life.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens realizing nirvāṇa (證得涅槃), beginning with nirvāṇa with residue (有餘依涅槃), and, within it, distinguishing it from continued flow (簡流轉) — the afflictive hindrance is extinguished, though the five aggregates still remain as a basis, purely turning through consciousness and name-and-form alone.

40. Section: Called Pure

At that time, though consciousness conditions name-and-form and name-and-form conditions consciousness still remain, not yet extinguished, this is nonetheless called pure and unblemished.

Chinese Source

彼於爾時，識緣名色、名色緣識有餘未滅，而得說名清淨鮮白。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, though consciousness conditions name-and-form and name-and-form conditions consciousness still remain, not yet extinguished, this is nonetheless called pure and unblemished.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Like a fire no longer fed with fuel that has not yet gone out, the arhat's remaining feelings, though present, are free of affliction's binding.

41. Section: Distinguishing Its Abiding Equanimity, Closing "Nirvāṇa With Residue" Entirely

Right up until the body still bearing consciousness has not yet perished, one constantly experiences unbound feelings, free of any binding. That body bearing consciousness continues, sustained by the lifespan drawn forth by prior karma, right up to its exhaustion. Once the lifespan is exhausted, the consciousness that had sustained it relinquishes the body it held, and the life-faculty is relinquished as well. From this point on, the life-faculty is permanently extinguished without remainder, with nothing whatsoever remaining.

Chinese Source

乃至有識身住未滅，彼恆領受離繫諸受，無有繫縛。彼有識身，乃至先業所引壽量，恆相續住。壽量若盡，能執持識捨所執身，命根亦捨。從此已後，所有命根無餘永滅，都無所有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Right up until the body still bearing consciousness has not yet perished, one constantly experiences unbound feelings, free of any binding. That body bearing consciousness continues, sustained by the lifespan drawn forth by prior karma, right up to its exhaustion. Once the lifespan is exhausted, the consciousness that had sustained it relinquishes the body it held, and the life-faculty is relinquished as well. From this point on, the life-faculty is permanently extinguished without remainder, with nothing whatsoever remaining.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, distinguishing its abiding equanimity (辨住捨) closes entirely, closing nirvāṇa with residue (有餘依涅槃3: 簡流轉/名清淨/辨住捨) entirely.

42. Section: Nirvāṇa Without Residue Begins — Showing Its Ultimate Nature: Explaining the Mark of Cessation

Further, all these consciousnesses, together with every feeling, at this stage cease spontaneously. Because their prior cause has ceased, nothing further continues — nor is there any further remainder to cease.

Chinese Source

又彼諸識與一切受，於此位中任運而滅。先因滅故，餘更不續，亦無餘滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, all these consciousnesses, together with every feeling, at this stage cease spontaneously. Because their prior cause has ceased, nothing further continues — nor is there any further remainder to cease.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens nirvāṇa without residue (無餘依涅槃), beginning with showing its ultimate nature (顯究竟), and, within it, explaining the mark of cessation (釋滅相) — one moment ceases, the next does not arise; the cause of future suffering is utterly stilled.

43. Section: Concluding the Name, Closing "Showing Its Ultimate Nature" Entirely

By this principle, it is called the realm of nirvāṇa without residue — the ultimate, ever-still, sublime trace.

Chinese Source

由此道理，名無餘依般涅槃界，究竟寂靜常住妙跡。

Ru-Yi Translation

By this principle, it is called the realm of nirvāṇa without residue — the ultimate, ever-still, sublime trace.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, concluding the name (結得名) closes entirely, closing showing its ultimate nature (顯究竟2: 釋滅相/結得名) entirely.

44. Section: Bringing Forth the Goal, Closing "Nirvāṇa Without Residue," "Realizing Nirvāṇa," "Turning Toward Purity," and "Explaining Each Individually" Entirely

For the sake of this very aim, one constantly follows nirvāṇa, constantly taking nirvāṇa as the ultimate goal, thoroughly cultivating the pure life in the World-Honored One's presence.

Chinese Source

為此義故，常隨涅槃，常以涅槃為其究竟，於世尊所熟修梵行。

Ru-Yi Translation

For the sake of this very aim, one constantly follows nirvāṇa, constantly taking nirvāṇa as the ultimate goal, thoroughly cultivating the pure life in the World-Honored One's presence.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing the Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā

With this, bringing forth the goal (出為趣) closes entirely, closing nirvāṇa without residue (無餘依涅槃2: 顯究竟/出為趣) entirely, closing realizing nirvāṇa (證得涅槃2: 有餘依涅槃/無餘依涅槃) entirely, closing turning toward purity once arisen in the latter period (後際不生趣清淨4: 得清淨智見/證清淨解脫/成不生法/證得涅槃) entirely, closing explaining each individually (別釋2: 從中際後際生/後際不生趣清淨) entirely, closing arising from the middle into the latter period, and turning toward purity (從中際後際得生及趣清淨2: 總徵/別釋) entirely — the second and third marks' full explanation. The Mahāvibhāṣā describes the arhat entering nirvāṇa without residue: "the pure life well established, the noble path well cultivated, at the exhaustion of the lifespan rejoicing, like setting down a vessel of poison."

45. Section: The Concluding Statement on Establishing, Closing "Broadly Explaining the Marks" and "Establishing" — the First of Ten Topics — Entirely

This is called broadly explaining, by three marks, the establishing of dependent origination: namely, flowing onward from the prior period into the middle period; flowing onward from the middle period into the latter period; and, further, within the middle period, flowing onward toward purity.

Chinese Source

是名廣說由三種相，建立緣起。謂從前際中際流轉，從其中際後際流轉，復於中際流轉清淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

This is called broadly explaining, by three marks, the establishing of dependent origination: namely, flowing onward from the prior period into the middle period; flowing onward from the middle period into the latter period; and, further, within the middle period, flowing onward toward purity.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, the concluding statement (結建立) closes entirely, closing broadly explaining the marks (廣釋相2: 從前際中際生及生隨轉/從中際後際得生及趣清淨) entirely, closing establishing (立3: 略標列/廣釋相/結建立), the first of establishing and the rest's ten topics, entirely — the full classical exposition of dependent origination's three marks across the three time periods, from the drawing cause through the generating cause to their cessation in nirvāṇa.
