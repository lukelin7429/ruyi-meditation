Superior-Grade Craving and the Rest

Fascicle 92 opens 處擇攝's second overall verse's third particular verse, superior-grade craving and the rest (上品貪等攝) — its own twelve-item summary verse names: superior craving, exhortation, sorrowful abiding, observation, drawing forth, unworthiness of offerings, clarifying liberation, cultivation, the treatise on non-self, concentration, the view of dharmas, and suffering. This fascicle opens with the three causes of superior-grade craving and their three remedies, works through the marks of mindfulness-and-clear-comprehension in noble ones as against the foolish, and then turns to exhortation (教授) — the reasons the Buddha delegates teaching to his disciples, the four marks of rightly following instruction, and the paired virtues that make both teacher and disciple complete.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: in progress Source: Taishō T30, no. 1579, fascicle 92 Commentary guide: Master Cháng Bǎi's lecture notes

1. Section: The Third Particular Verse Begins — Marking and Listing Its Twelve Topics

Next, a summary verse says: superior craving, exhortation, and sorrowful abiding; observation, drawing forth, and unworthiness of offerings; clarifying liberation, cultivation, and the treatise on non-self; concentration, the view of dharmas, and suffering, coming last of all.

Chinese Source

復次，嗢柁南曰：

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, a summary verse says:

Chinese Source

上貪教授及苦住　觀察引發不應供
明解脫修無我論　定法見苦最為後

Ru-Yi Translation

Superior craving, exhortation, and sorrowful abiding; observation, drawing forth, and unworthiness of offerings; clarifying liberation, cultivation, and the treatise on non-self; concentration, the view of dharmas, and suffering, coming last of all.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the third particular verse (上品貪等攝) of 處擇攝's second overall summary verse, following directly on from fascicle 91's close of mere action and the rest (唯作緣等攝). Its own summary verse marks and lists twelve topics under which the long-form explanation (長行釋) that follows will unfold: superior craving, exhortation, sorrowful abiding, observation, drawing forth, unworthiness of offerings, clarifying liberation, cultivation, the treatise on non-self, concentration, the view of dharmas, and suffering.

2. Section: The Long-Form Explanation Begins — Superior Craving's Causes

By three causes and conditions, a person gives rise to superior-grade craving toward an object condition. What are the three? First, robustness, not frailty; second, comeliness, not homeliness; third, habituation to craving, not its abandonment.

Chinese Source

三因緣故，補特伽羅於所緣境上品貪行。何等為三？一者、康彊，非羸劣；二者、端嚴，非醜陋；三者、習貪，非捨貪。

Ru-Yi Translation

By three causes and conditions, a person gives rise to superior-grade craving toward an object condition. What are the three? First, robustness, not frailty; second, comeliness, not homeliness; third, habituation to craving, not its abandonment.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the long-form explanation (長行釋), whose twelve sections correspond to the summary verse's twelve topics, beginning with the first topic, superior craving (上貪), and within it bringing forth its causes and conditions (出因緣). Per the 披尋記: robustness and frailty describe the person, comeliness and homeliness the object condition, and habituation-versus-abandonment the craving-conduct itself — together the three causes of superior craving. A healthy, vigorous body inclines the senses toward craving; a comely object, whether one's own body or another's, invites it further; and a mind long habituated to indulging craving cannot let go of it once an object appears.

3. Section: The Remedies Begin — Briefly Marking Them Individually

Further, by three kinds of remedial embrace, even a person harboring such superior-grade craving can be made to diligently cultivate the pure life within the well-taught Dharma-Vinaya, subduing the mind and bringing it to stillness — how much more so one whose craving is merely middling or slight. What are the three? First, secretly guarding the sense-doors as the basis, so as to be far removed from every extreme of sensual pleasure; second, moderation in food, and reduced sleep in the early and later watches of the night, as the basis, so as to be far removed from every extreme of self-affliction; third, foremost mindfulness and clear comprehension as the basis, so as to practice the path of renunciation along the middle way.

Chinese Source

復由三種對治攝受，尚令如是懷上品貪補特伽羅，於善說法毗奈耶中勤修梵行，調伏其心，令得寂靜，何況但懷中軟品貪微薄塵者。何等為三？一者、密護根門為所依止，遠離一切欲樂邊故；二者、於食知量、初夜後夜減省睡眠為所依止，遠離一切自苦邊故；三者、最勝正念正知為所依止，行於中道出離行故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, by three kinds of remedial embrace, even a person harboring such superior-grade craving can be made to diligently cultivate the pure life within the well-taught Dharma-Vinaya, subduing the mind and bringing it to stillness — how much more so one whose craving is merely middling or slight. What are the three? First, secretly guarding the sense-doors as the basis, so as to be far removed from every extreme of sensual pleasure; second, moderation in food, and reduced sleep in the early and later watches of the night, as the basis, so as to be far removed from every extreme of self-affliction; third, foremost mindfulness and clear comprehension as the basis, so as to practice the path of renunciation along the middle way.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens clarifying the remedies (明對治), beginning with briefly marking them individually (略標別) — three practices that together hold the middle way between indulgence and self-affliction: guarding the six sense-doors from grasping at signs, eating and sleeping in moderation so practice supports rather than punishes the body, and foremost mindfulness and clear comprehension carried at all times.

4. Section: Explaining the Harder Points Begins — Distinguishing Marks: the Noble Ones' First Kind, Knowing Feeling and Perception While Walking

One should know that here, one well established in mind through the four foundations of mindfulness — or, when engaged in walking and objects appear before one, if one has not grasped at signs or their finer particulars, truly knows feeling's arising, abiding, and ceasing; if one has grasped at signs and their finer particulars, truly knows perception's arising, abiding, and ceasing.

Chinese Source

當知此中，於四念住善住心者，

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that here, one well established in mind through the four foundations of mindfulness —

Chinese Source

或於行時境界現前，若不取相及與隨好，如實了知受生住滅；若取其相及與隨好，如實了知想生住滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

or, when engaged in walking and objects appear before one, if one has not grasped at signs or their finer particulars, truly knows feeling's arising, abiding, and ceasing; if one has grasped at signs and their finer particulars, truly knows perception's arising, abiding, and ceasing.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記

This opens explaining the harder points (隨難釋), beginning with distinguishing marks (別辨相) — first citing the noble ones (舉賢聖) and, within them, the first kind of most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension (第一種類), starting with knowing feeling and perception and the rest while walking (於行時). The 披尋記 clarifies that "well established in mind through the four foundations of mindfulness" spans every practitioner from the attainment of the access-concentration mental application, free of heedlessness, through the once-returner. Walking through town or temple, whenever an object of the six senses appears, before grasping its composite sign one truly knows only feeling arising and ceasing; once its finer particulars are also grasped, one truly knows perception arising and ceasing.

5. Section: While Abiding

Or, while abiding, one truly knows the arising and ceasing of discursive thought caused by that grasping.

Chinese Source

或於住時，如實了知彼因尋思生住與滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Or, while abiding, one truly knows the arising and ceasing of discursive thought caused by that grasping.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記

The 披尋記 lists the eight kinds of discursive thought: of desire, of ill will, of harming, of kinfolk, of one's homeland, of not dying, of contempt toward others, and of family status — each arising from improper attention to a grasped sign or particular. Seated in stillness, when any of these eight arises from having grasped an object's sign, one truly observes its arising, abiding, and ceasing without being carried away by it.

6. Section: Concluding Its Name as Most Excellent, Closing "The First Kind" Entirely

By such marks of mindfulness and clear comprehension, at all times and toward every kind of object condition, one guards one's own mind exactly according to the proper standard — this is called most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension.

Chinese Source

由如是相正念正知，於一切時，於一切種所緣境界，能如正軌守護其心，是名最勝正念正知。

Ru-Yi Translation

By such marks of mindfulness and clear comprehension, at all times and toward every kind of object condition, one guards one's own mind exactly according to the proper standard — this is called most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

Whether walking or abiding, toward every kind of object at every time, guarding this one thought-moment of mind according to what the Buddha's sūtras and discipline teach — this is the most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension. With this, concluding its name as most excellent (結名最勝) closes entirely, closing the first kind (第一種類2: 知受想等/結名最勝) entirely.

7. Section: The Second Kind Begins — Marking That Class

There is further a most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension belonging to one who has already attained cessation-attainment, or already attained signless concentration, or already attained the concentration free of application and sustained thought.

Chinese Source

復有最勝正念正知，謂已獲得滅盡定者，或已獲得無相定者，或已獲得無尋伺者。

Ru-Yi Translation

There is further a most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension belonging to one who has already attained cessation-attainment, or already attained signless concentration, or already attained the concentration free of application and sustained thought.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens the second kind of most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension, belonging to third-fruit and fourth-fruit noble ones — those who have attained cessation-attainment or signless concentration, or attained concentrations from the second dhyāna upward, free of application and sustained thought.

8. Section: Showing It Has No Superior

One should know that, resting upon the noble abodes and the divine abodes, apart from this most excellent mindfulness-abiding-in-knowledge, there is nothing further that can surpass it.

Chinese Source

當知依止聖住天住，除此最勝正念知住，更無有餘能過上者。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that, resting upon the noble abodes and the divine abodes, apart from this most excellent mindfulness-abiding-in-knowledge, there is nothing further that can surpass it.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記

Resting in cessation-attainment or signless concentration is called the noble abode; resting in the dhyānas and formless attainments is called the divine abode, per the Śrāvaka Stage. Among these, apart from the first dhyāna (which still has application and sustained thought), every concentration from the second dhyāna upward is said to have attained freedom from application and sustained thought.

9. Section: Distinguishing Its Marks Begins — Citing Reliance on Cessation-Attainment to Truly Know Feeling

Whether abiding just after rising from cessation-attainment, or abiding as a skillful means just before entering it, one truly knows feeling's arising, abiding, and ceasing — this is called most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension.

Chinese Source

或從滅定起已而住，或將入定方便而住，如實了知受生住滅，是名最勝正念正知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whether abiding just after rising from cessation-attainment, or abiding as a skillful means just before entering it, one truly knows feeling's arising, abiding, and ceasing — this is called most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

This opens distinguishing its marks (辨彼相), beginning with citing reliance on cessation-attainment (舉依滅定) — a third-fruit or higher noble one, whether just risen from or just about to enter cessation-attainment, truly observes only the dharma of feeling arising and ceasing, with no self found in it whatsoever.

10. Section: Citing by Example Reliance on Signless Concentration, Closing "Distinguishing Its Marks" Entirely

Just as one truly knows feeling by reliance on cessation-attainment, so too one truly knows perception by reliance on signless concentration, and truly knows whatever application and sustained thought there are by reliance on the concentration free of application and sustained thought — one should know these are likewise so.

Chinese Source

如依滅定如實知受，依無相定如實知想、無尋伺定如實了知所有尋伺，當知亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Just as one truly knows feeling by reliance on cessation-attainment, so too one truly knows perception by reliance on signless concentration, and truly knows whatever application and sustained thought there are by reliance on the concentration free of application and sustained thought — one should know these are likewise so.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, citing by example reliance on signless concentration and the rest (例依無相定如實知想等) closes entirely, closing distinguishing its marks (辨彼相2: 舉依滅定/例依無相定) entirely.

11. Section: Clarifying That It Is Most Excellent, Closing "The Second Kind" and "Citing the Noble Ones" Entirely

Because this most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension takes up only dharmas, one does not, toward such feeling, perception, application, or sustained thought, give rise to any false discrimination of self or what belongs to self.

Chinese Source

由此最勝正念正知唯取法故，不於如是受、想、尋伺，起我、我所虛妄分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because this most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension takes up only dharmas, one does not, toward such feeling, perception, application, or sustained thought, give rise to any false discrimination of self or what belongs to self.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi

With this, clarifying that it is most excellent (明最勝) closes entirely, closing the second kind (第二種類4: 標彼類/顯無上/辨彼相/明最勝) entirely, which closes citing the noble ones (舉賢聖2: 第一種類/第二種類) entirely.

12. Section: Distinguishing the Foolish

If, when the various distinctions of feeling, perception, application, and sustained thought arise in the foolish, they are unable to give rise, toward feeling and the other dharmas, to the perception of mere dharma, but instead only think: "I am able to feel" — and so on, at length. ... By this cause and condition, such people lack even ordinary mindfulness and clear comprehension, how much more the most excellent kind.

Chinese Source

若諸愚夫受、想、尋伺差別生時，於受等法不能發起唯有法想，但作是念：我能領受，乃至廣說。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, when the various distinctions of feeling, perception, application, and sustained thought arise in the foolish, they are unable to give rise, toward feeling and the other dharmas, to the perception of mere dharma, but instead only think: "I am able to feel" — and so on, at length.

Chinese Source

由是因緣，彼尚無有正念正知，何況最勝。

Ru-Yi Translation

By this cause and condition, such people lack even ordinary mindfulness and clear comprehension, how much more the most excellent kind.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記

This opens distinguishing the foolish (簡愚夫), the second of two matters under distinguishing marks (別辨相), contrasting the noble ones just described. The 披尋記 glosses "and so on, at length" as: "I am able to discern, I am able to observe" — every experience gets bound to a self, whether exalted or debased. Unable even to perceive that these are merely arising dharmas, the unlearned ordinary being cannot notice affliction arising at all, let alone attain the most excellent mindfulness and clear comprehension that sees no self, no self's-possession, anywhere in feeling, perception, application, or sustained thought.

13. Section: Briefly Gathering the Kinds, Closing "Distinguishing Marks" Entirely

Here, the mindfulness and clear comprehension spoken of later refers to the non-returner or the arhat; one should know that the mindfulness and clear comprehension spoken of earlier spans from the attainment of the access mental application, free of heedlessness, through every ordinary being's stage, up to the once-returner.

Chinese Source

此中後說正念正知，或不還果、或阿羅漢；當知前說正念正知，從得作意無有放逸諸異生位至一來果。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, the mindfulness and clear comprehension spoken of later refers to the non-returner or the arhat; one should know that the mindfulness and clear comprehension spoken of earlier spans from the attainment of the access mental application, free of heedlessness, through every ordinary being's stage, up to the once-returner.

Reading note — after Master Cháng Bǎi, citing 披尋記 and a companion sūtra

With this, briefly gathering the kinds (略攝類) closes entirely, closing distinguishing marks (別辨相2: 舉賢聖/簡愚夫) entirely, which closes explaining the harder points (隨難釋2: 別辨相/略攝類) entirely, which closes clarifying the remedies (明對治2: 略標別/隨難釋) entirely, which closes superior craving (上貪), the first of the third particular verse's twelve topics, entirely.

A companion sūtra recounts the Buddha teaching at Śrāvastī: whatever does not belong to oneself should be relinquished, just as one feels no distress over leaves and twigs in the monastery grove being carried off by others, since they were never "mine." The six sense-bases likewise are neither self nor self's-possession; relinquishing attachment to them brings lasting ease. Another sūtra praises the venerable Nanda, who was famously robust, comely, and given to strong craving — exactly the three causes named in the root text — yet who overcame them through the three remedies: closing the sense-doors, moderate eating with diligent practice in the early and late watches, and accomplished mindfulness and clear comprehension while walking and abiding, guarding all directions so that worldly craving, sorrow, and unwholesome states found no outlet. The Buddha held Nanda up to the assembly as the model disciple to emulate in exactly these three practices.
