The Gate of Stopping and Verifying Wholesome Capacity

This page presents Ru-Yi's own English translation of fascicle 3 of Zhiyi's Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen (釋禪波羅蜜次第法門). Having completed the external expedient means in fascicle 2, the treatise turns to the internal expedient means — five further layers of practice, beginning with the gate of stopping (止, śamatha), the foundation the treatise says every other dhyāna depends on, followed by how to verify what stopping itself brings to the surface: wholesome roots, and how to tell a genuine one from a false or demonic one.

The Internal Expedient Means 分別禪波羅蜜前方便第六之二

Fascicle 2 completed the twenty-five external expedient means. Topic 6 now turns to its second half: the internal expedient means, five further layers of practice for the moment concentration actually begins to take hold. Rather than external circumstances, these concern the direct handling of the mind itself — starting with what the treatise calls the foundation every other gate of practice depends on: stopping.

Chinese Source

第二、明修禪波羅蜜內方便，開為五重：一、先明止門；二、明驗善惡根性；三、明安心法；四、明治病患；五、明覺魔事。此五通稱內方便者，並據初發定時，靜細心中善巧運用，取捨不失其宜，因此必證深禪定，故名方便。今於內方便中，以止為初門者，一切禪定功德皆因制心息亂而發，故經云：「制之一處，無事不辦。」止為初門，則意在此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the internal expedient means for cultivating dhyāna-pāramitā: this opens into five layers — first, clarifying the gate of stopping; second, clarifying how to verify wholesome and unwholesome capacity; third, clarifying the methods for settling the mind; fourth, clarifying the treatment of illness; fifth, clarifying the recognition of demonic affairs. All five of these are called, in general, the internal expedient means, because they concern the skillful application of a still, refined mind at the very moment concentration first begins to arise — taking up and setting aside without ever losing what is proper to the moment — and it is through this that deep dhyāna-concentration is certain to be realized; hence they are called expedient means.

Here, within the internal expedient means, stopping is taken as the first gate, because every merit of dhyāna-concentration is brought forth only through restraining the mind and stilling its confusion — hence the sūtra says: "Restrain it to a single place, and there is nothing that cannot be accomplished." This is exactly the intent behind taking stopping as the first gate.

問曰：上來明外方便行五法中，已辨一心，何故重說？答曰：不然。上但通論一心，未是具足分別微細止門之法，此中為令行者善知安心之本，廣明修止淺深麁細入定之相，重說無咎。問曰：經中說二為甘露門：一者、不淨觀門；二者、阿那波那門。不說止為初門，今云何言止為初門？答曰：不然。於諸禪中，止為通門，通攝於別，別不攝通，故先教止。若止後入餘禪，則有通益；若依餘門，則有乖違之過。治煩惱亦爾。

Question: Above, in explaining the external expedient means, within the practice of the five dharmas, one-pointedness of mind has already been distinguished — why explain it again here? Answer: This does not follow. What was given above only discussed one-pointedness of mind in a general way; it was not yet a complete, detailed distinguishing of the fine and subtle method of the gate of stopping. Here, in order to let the practitioner properly know the very root of settling the mind, the marks of cultivating stopping — shallow and deep, coarse and fine, entering concentration — are explained fully and at length; there is no fault in explaining it once more.

Question: The sūtras speak of two gates to the deathless — the gate of the contemplation of impurity, and the gate of mindfulness of the breath — they do not speak of stopping as the first gate. How, then, can it now be said that stopping is the first gate? Answer: This does not follow. Among the various dhyānas, stopping is the general gate, and the general encompasses the specific, while the specific does not encompass the general — hence stopping is taught first. If, after stopping, one then enters any other dhyāna, there is a general benefit gained; but if one relies on some other gate alone, there is the fault of a mismatch. The same holds for treating affliction.

復次，今明師有二種：第一師者，已得道眼，觀機授法，必扶本習，善識對治。不如舍利弗為二弟子說法，不知機故，金師之子，教不淨觀，浣衣之子，教令數息，違本所習，法則不起，遂生邪見。佛為轉觀，即悟道迹。第二師者，無他心智，不得道眼，不識機根，其有來學坐者，唯當先教止門，心在定故，即發善惡根性。若因靜心發諸禪定，師即應教扶本而修。若都不發法門，或貪、瞋、癡等諸結使發，隨其多者，即教對治破之，遮道法滅，禪定則發。今止門為先者，即是第二師授法之正意。若異此說，則善惡根緣難可分別，妄授他法，必有差機之過。

Furthermore, teachers here are clarified as being of two kinds. The first kind of teacher has already attained the eye of the path, observes each student's capacity, and bestows the teaching accordingly — necessarily matching it to that student's own prior habituation, skillfully recognizing the proper antidote. Not to do so is like Śāriputra teaching his two disciples: not knowing their capacity, he taught the contemplation of impurity to the son of a goldsmith, and taught mindfulness of the breath to the son of a launderer — going against their own prior habituation, so that the practice failed to take hold, and wrong views arose instead. The Buddha, redirecting their contemplations, brought them at once to realize the fruit of the path.

The second kind of teacher lacks the wisdom to know another's mind, has not attained the eye of the path, and cannot recognize a student's capacity. For such a teacher, whoever comes to learn sitting meditation should simply be taught the gate of stopping first — since, as the mind settles into concentration, wholesome and unwholesome capacities are thereby brought forth of themselves. If, through a stilled mind, the various dhyānas are brought forth, the teacher should then teach in accordance with, and building upon, that student's own foundation. If no dharma-gate is brought forth at all, or if craving, anger, delusion, or other fetters are instead brought forth, the teacher should, according to whichever predominates, teach the corresponding antidote to break it — removing what obstructs the path, so that dhyāna-concentration can then be brought forth.

That the gate of stopping is given first here is precisely the proper intent behind how the second kind of teacher bestows the teaching. If this order were otherwise, the causes and conditions of wholesome and unwholesome capacity would be hard to distinguish, and bestowing some other teaching at random would necessarily result in a mismatch with the student's actual capacity.

Reading note: This opening is a candid piece of pedagogical realism: Zhiyi acknowledges that the ideal teacher — one with genuine insight into a student's capacity, like the Buddha correcting Śāriputra's mismatched assignments — is rare, and designs the treatise's own default sequence for the more common case, a teacher without that direct insight. Starting everyone with stopping isn't presented as the single best method in the abstract; it's presented as the safest default precisely because it reveals what a student actually needs next, functioning as a diagnostic as much as a practice in its own right.

Four Parts of the Gate of Stopping 止門

Chinese Source

就止門中，自有四意：一者、分別止門不同；二者、立止大意；三、明修止方法；四、辯證止之相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the gate of stopping, there are four parts in itself: first, distinguishing the different kinds of the gate of stopping; second, establishing the great intent of stopping; third, clarifying the method of cultivating stopping; fourth, distinguishing the marks that verify stopping.

Three Kinds of Stopping, in Practice 分別止門不同 · 約行論止

Chinese Source

第一、分別止門不同，即為二意：一、約行論止；二、約義論止。初、約行明止，乃有多途，今略出三意：一、繫緣止；二、制心止；三、體真止。所以通言止者，止名制止，亦名止息。心起制之，不令流動，故名制。專心定志，息諸亂想，故名止。今言繫緣止者，繫心鼻柱、臍間等處，不令馳蕩，故名繫緣止。制心止者，心若覺觀，即制令不起，故名制心止。體真止者，體諸法空，息諸妄慮，故名體真止。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, distinguishing the different kinds of the gate of stopping: this has two parts — stopping discussed in terms of practice, and stopping discussed in terms of principle.

First, stopping explained in terms of practice has many approaches; here, in brief, three are given: fixed-object stopping; restraining-the-mind stopping; and embodying-truth stopping. The reason "stopping" is used as the general term for all three: "stopping" means restraining, and also means bringing to rest. When the mind arises, restraining it and not letting it flow off — hence it is called restraining. Applying the mind single-mindedly with settled intent, bringing every scattered thought to rest — hence it is called stopping.

Now, as for fixed-object stopping: fixing the mind on the bridge of the nose, the navel, or some other such place, not letting it run off and wander — hence it is called fixed-object stopping. As for restraining-the-mind stopping: whenever coarse or subtle discursive thought arises in the mind, restraining it at once so that it does not arise — hence it is called restraining-the-mind stopping. As for embodying-truth stopping: embodying the emptiness of all dharmas, bringing every deluded deliberation to rest — hence it is called embodying-truth stopping.

Three Kinds of Stopping, in Principle 分別止門不同 · 約義論止

Chinese Source

二、約義論止，亦有多途，今略出三意：一、隨緣止；二、入定止；三、真性止。隨緣止者，隨心起時，悉有三摩提數，故《涅槃經》云：「十大地中定，名為下定。」入定止者，證定之時，定法持心，心息止住，是入定止。真性止者，心性之理，常自不動，故名為止，故《思益經》云：「一切眾生即是滅盡定。」今用此三義，成上三止：約隨緣任性有定故，說繫緣止；約果有定法，說制心止；由具性不動，說體真止。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, stopping explained in terms of principle likewise has many approaches; here, in brief, three are given: stopping that follows conditions; stopping that has entered concentration; and stopping of the true nature.

Stopping that follows conditions: whenever the mind arises, a factor of samādhi is already present within it — hence the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says, "The concentration among the ten mental factors of the great ground is called the lower concentration." Stopping that has entered concentration: at the very moment concentration is realized, the dharma of concentration itself sustains the mind, and the mind and breath come to rest and abide — this is stopping that has entered concentration. Stopping of the true nature: the principle of the very nature of mind is, of itself, forever unmoving — hence it is called stopping; and so the Sūtra of Contemplating the Increase of Goodness says, "All sentient beings are, of themselves, already the concentration of cessation."

These three senses now serve to complete the three kinds of stopping given above: in terms of a concentration already present by following conditions and according with one's own nature, fixed-object stopping is spoken of; in terms of the dharma of concentration present in the fruit, restraining-the-mind stopping is spoken of; and owing to the unmoving nature already fully possessed, embodying-truth stopping is spoken of.

Reading note: The two three-part schemes are not two different lists but the same three kinds of stopping seen from two angles — one describing what a practitioner actually does (fix attention on an object; restrain the mind directly; embody the emptiness of all dharmas), the other describing the principle each practice ultimately rests on. Notice, too, the progression baked into all three kinds: from managing attention externally (an object) to managing it internally (the mind itself) to dissolving the very distinction that made "managing" necessary in the first place — the same coarse-to-fine movement already seen in fascicle 2's body-breath-mind sequence, now applied to the whole of practice.

The Great Intent of Establishing Stopping 立止大意

Chinese Source

第二、明立止大意者，自為四：一、明淺深；二、對治相破；三、隨樂欲；四、隨機宜。一、簡別三種止淺深之相不同者，因麁入細，則有淺深之義：繫緣及制心既是事，故麁淺；體真入細，故為深細。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the great intent of establishing stopping: this itself has four parts — first, clarifying the shallow and the deep; second, mutual antidoting; third, following inclination; fourth, matching capacity.

First, distinguishing that the marks of shallowness and depth differ among the three kinds of stopping: since one proceeds from the coarse into the fine, there is necessarily a sense of shallow and deep. Fixed-object stopping and restraining-the-mind stopping are both matters of practical technique, and so are coarse and shallow; embodying-truth stopping enters into the fine, and so is deep and subtle.

二、明三止對治相破，有二種：一者、以深破淺；二者、迴互相破。以深破淺者，為破緣外之散心故，立繫緣止。制心止者，即破繫緣止。心非色法，豈可繫在鼻膈等處？若欲靜之，但當息諸攀緣，故令制心守一。體真止者，即破前制心止。心無形相，性不可得，云何可制？了心非心，不起妄念，無止之止，止無所止，乃名為止。有止之止，由依妄想，不名為止。此則以深破淺，反本還源，故立三止。二、迴互相破者，隨修止時，若有見生，即互取一止對治破之，細尋可解。

Second, clarifying that the three kinds of stopping mutually break through one another as antidotes: this is of two kinds — first, the deep breaking through the shallow; second, each breaking through the others in turn.

As for the deep breaking through the shallow: fixed-object stopping is established in order to break through the scattered mind that takes external objects. Restraining-the-mind stopping then breaks through fixed-object stopping in turn: the mind is not a material thing — how could it truly be fixed at the nose or the diaphragm or any such place? If one wishes to still it, one need only bring every chasing after objects to rest, restraining the mind instead to hold to the one. Embodying-truth stopping then breaks through the prior restraining-the-mind stopping in turn: the mind has no fixed shape or form, and its very nature cannot be found — how, then, could it be restrained at all? Once it is understood that mind is not fixed as mind, and no deluded thought arises, then a stopping that stops nothing, a stopping with nothing at all left to stop, is what is truly called stopping. A stopping that still has something to stop still depends on deluded thought, and so cannot properly be called stopping. This, then, is the deep breaking through the shallow, turning back to the very root and returning to the source — hence the three kinds of stopping are established in this order.

Second, as for each breaking through the others in turn: whenever, in the course of cultivating stopping, some wrong view arises, whichever of the three kinds of stopping serves as its antidote should be taken up in turn to break through it — this can be understood upon careful examination.

三、隨樂欲者，自有人樂安心境界，自有但樂制心、體真亦爾。若隨所樂以法教之，則歡喜奉行；若乖其情，則心不願樂。四、對機宜者，未必隨樂。如有人樂欲體真，而不入定。若暫繫心守境，即發諸禪。此應隨便宜而授法。

Third, as for following inclination: some people delight in settling the mind upon an object, others delight simply in restraining the mind, and others again in embodying truth. If one is taught according to what one delights in, the teaching is joyfully received and put into practice; but if it goes against one's own inclination, the mind has no wish to delight in it.

Fourth, as for matching capacity: this need not always follow mere inclination. There are cases where a person delights in and wishes for embodying truth, and yet does not thereby enter concentration; whereas if the mind is temporarily fixed upon holding to an object instead, the various dhyānas are brought forth. In such a case, the teaching should be given according to whatever is actually suited to the moment.

Reading note: "Mutual antidoting" gives the three kinds of stopping their internal logic — deep replacing shallow in a fixed progressive order — while "following inclination" and "matching capacity" immediately qualify that order with real pedagogical flexibility: a student's own temperament matters, but so does what actually works for them, and the two do not always coincide. The example given is pointed — someone who longs for the most advanced practice, embodying-truth stopping, may simply fail to enter concentration through it, while a more basic fixed-object practice succeeds — a reminder that spiritual aspiration and present readiness are not the same thing. This closes the second of the gate of stopping's four parts.

The Method of Cultivating Stopping 明修止方法

Chinese Source

第三、明修止方法，亦為三意：一、修繫緣止；二、修制心止；三、修體真止。第一、先明修繫緣止法者，略明有五處：一、繫心頂上；二、繫心髮際；三、繫心鼻柱；四、繫心臍間；五、繫心在地輪。外國金齒三藏說此為五門禪。問曰：身分皆可繫心，云何的說五處？答曰：此五處於用心為便，餘處非安定所。若脇、肋等處皆偏，故不說。如頭圓法天，足方法地，臍是氣海，鼻是風門，髮際是修骨觀之所，故以為門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the method of cultivating stopping: this too has three parts — cultivating fixed-object stopping; cultivating restraining-the-mind stopping; and cultivating embodying-truth stopping.

First, clarifying the method of cultivating fixed-object stopping: in brief, there are five places given — first, fixing the mind at the crown of the head; second, fixing the mind at the hairline; third, fixing the mind at the bridge of the nose; fourth, fixing the mind at the navel; fifth, fixing the mind at the ground-wheel, the base of the body. A foreign Tripiṭaka master named Kanacoṭi called this the five-gate dhyāna. Question: the mind can be fixed on any part of the body — why specify exactly these five places? Answer: these five places are especially convenient for applying the mind; other places are not proper seats of settling. Places such as the ribs and flanks are all uneven, and so are not spoken of. Just as the head, being round, corresponds to heaven, and the feet, being square, correspond to earth; the navel is the sea of vital energy; the nose is the gate of the breath; and the hairline is the place for cultivating the contemplation of bones — hence these are taken as the gates.

令繫心頂上者，為心沈惛多睡故，在上安心；若久久，即令人浮風，乍如風病，或似得通欲飛，有此等過，不可恒用。若繫心髮際，此處髮黑肉白，心則易住。或可發本骨觀，久則過生，眼好上瞻，或可見於黃、赤等色，如華如雲，種種相貌，令情慮顛倒。若繫心鼻柱者，鼻是風門，覺出息、入息，念念不住，易悟無常。亦以扶本安般之習，心靜能發禪定。若繫心臍下，臍是氣海，亦曰中宮，繫心在臍，能除眾病，或時內見三十六物，發特勝等禪。若繫心地輪，此最在下，氣隨心下，則四大調和。亦以扶本修習不淨觀者，多從下起，因此繫心，或能發本。不淨觀門約此五處為緣，令心不散，以辨修繫緣止，意在於此。

As for fixing the mind at the crown of the head: this is done because, when the mind is sunken and dull, prone to drowsiness, settling it above helps counter this; but if done for too long, it can cause a person to become light-headed, resembling wind-illness, or make them feel as though they have gained the power to fly — such faults as these mean it should not be used constantly. As for fixing the mind at the hairline: since the hair here is dark and the flesh pale, the mind settles here easily; it may also bring forth the underlying contemplation of bones — but if done too long, faults arise: the eyes tend to gaze upward, or one may see yellow, red, and other colors, like flowers or clouds, various such appearances, causing the feelings and deliberations to become inverted. As for fixing the mind at the bridge of the nose: the nose is the gate of the breath, and, being aware of the breath going out and coming in, moment by moment never remaining still, impermanence is easily realized; this also builds upon the underlying practice of mindfulness of the breath, and, the mind becoming still, dhyāna-concentration can be brought forth. As for fixing the mind below the navel: the navel is the sea of vital energy, also called the central palace; fixing the mind at the navel is able to remove many illnesses, and one may, at times, inwardly see the thirty-six impure substances of the body, bringing forth the special-excellences dhyāna and the like. As for fixing the mind at the ground-wheel: this is the lowest of all, and, the vital energy following the mind downward, the four elements become harmonized; this also builds upon the underlying cultivation of the contemplation of impurity, which for the most part arises from below — fixing the mind here can bring this forth as well. The gate of the contemplation of impurity takes these same five places as its object, keeping the mind from scattering — the intent behind distinguishing the cultivation of fixed-object stopping lies exactly here.

譬如猨猴得樹，騰躍跳躑，若鎖之於柱，久久自調。心亦如是，若心停住，未入定前復有一止，名凝心止。若得入定，身心泯然，任運自寂，即是入定止。二、明修制心止者，心非形色，亦無處所，豈可繫之在境？但是妄想緣慮，故須制之。心若靜住，則不須制之，但凝其心，息諸亂想，即是修止。問曰：心非上下，有時若寬若急，若沈浮，調適之法，其事云何？答曰：心雖非上下，為治沈浮患故，上下安之，於行無失。若心浮動，可作意下著止之；若心沈沒，可上著止之。復次，若下著安心，利益眾多，略說有二：一、心易得定；二者、眾病不生。

It is like a monkey that, finding a tree, leaps and bounds about — but if chained to a post, it will, in time, settle down of itself. The mind is exactly the same: once the mind comes to rest, but before entering concentration proper, there is a further kind of stopping, called fixed-mind stopping. Once concentration is actually attained, body and mind dissolve away, and stillness arises spontaneously of itself — this is stopping that has entered concentration.

Second, clarifying the cultivation of restraining-the-mind stopping: the mind has no shape or form, nor any location — how could it truly be fixed upon an object at all? It is only deluded thought and deliberation that must be restrained. If the mind is already settled and still, there is no need to restrain it further — one need only fix the mind, bringing every scattered thought to rest, and this is cultivating stopping.

Question: the mind has no up or down — yet at times it becomes too slack or too tense, or sinks or floats; what, then, is the method for adjusting it properly? Answer: though the mind truly has no up or down, in order to treat the troubles of sinking and floating, settling it lower or higher can be applied without fault to the practice. If the mind is floating and agitated, one may direct the intention downward to settle and still it; if the mind is sinking, one may direct it upward to settle and still it. Furthermore, settling the mind lower brings many benefits; in brief, two may be given: first, concentration is more easily attained; second, the many illnesses do not arise.

Reading note: The five points for fixed-object stopping are worth comparing with fascicle 1's earlier note on the kalala stage of conception: the same instinct to ground abstract practice in concrete physiology recurs here, down to specific cautions about side effects (light-headedness from too much attention at the crown, an inverted gaze from too much at the hairline). The monkey-and-post simile is a classical image for the untrained mind that recurs throughout later Buddhist meditation literature.

Embodying-Truth Stopping: Is There Any Karma to Be Found? 明修止方法 · 修體真止

Chinese Source

第三、明修體真止者，以正智慧體一切陰入界、三毒、九十八使及十二因緣等三界因果諸法悉皆空寂，如《大品經》中說：「即色是空，非色滅空，色性自空。空即是色，色即是空。離色無空，離空無色。受、想、行、識等一切諸法亦皆如是。」所以者何？今現見陰入界等諸法自性不有，何能生我、人、眾生、壽命等一切諸顛倒事？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the cultivation of embodying-truth stopping: by means of right wisdom, one embodies the fact that every dharma of the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements, the three poisons, the ninety-eight fetters, the twelve links of dependent origination, and every cause and result within the three realms, is, without exception, empty and still — as the Larger Sūtra says: "Form itself is emptiness; it is not that form ceases and thereby becomes emptiness — form's very nature is, of itself, empty. Emptiness is itself form, and form is itself emptiness. Apart from form there is no emptiness, and apart from emptiness there is no form. Sensation, perception, formations, and consciousness — every dharma whatsoever — are all exactly the same." Why is this? Now, actually observing that the aggregates, sense-bases, elements, and every other such dharma have no self-nature of their own — how could they possibly give rise to a self, a person, a sentient being, a life-span, or any of the other inverted notions?

云何知空？如過去所起一切煩惱業行為因，現在攬父母身分為緣，因緣和合則有果報；有果報故，則有陰入界等一切諸法者，此業為是何法，而能為果報、陰入等作因？若言過去善心即是業者，過去作善之心及心數法皆已滅謝，豈得為現在果報及陰入等法作因？若言心非是業因，心作業，業隨心來者，心轉滅故，業亦應隨心轉滅。若業轉滅，豈能感今世果報及陰入等法？若業轉滅，當知業即不至現在。何以故？業不來故。若業不來而受報者，此報不名報。何以故？無業則報無所酬。

How is emptiness to be known? Suppose it is said: the afflictions and karmic actions arisen in the past serve as cause, and the present taking hold of a body from one's parents serves as condition; when cause and condition come together, a result is thereby produced, and because there is a result, all the dharmas of the aggregates, sense-bases, elements, and the rest come to exist. If this is so — what kind of thing, exactly, is this karma, that it is able to serve as the cause of the result, the aggregates, and the rest?

If it is said that the wholesome mind of the past is itself the karma: the mind, and the mental factors that performed that good deed in the past, have already ceased and passed away — how, then, could they still serve as the cause of the present result, the aggregates, and the rest? If it is said that the mind itself is not the cause of the karma, but that the mind produces the karma, and the karma then follows along with the mind: since the mind ceases and changes, the karma too ought to cease and change along with it. And if the karma has already ceased and changed, how could it possibly bring about the present result, the aggregates, and the rest? If the karma has already ceased and changed, it should be known that this karma simply does not reach into the present at all. Why is this? Because the karma itself does not arrive here. And if a result is received without the karma itself ever arriving, this so-called result cannot properly be called a result at all — why is this? Because, without the karma, there is nothing for the result to answer to.

若言過去心雖滅謝，而次心續生，故業得隨心來者，亦應過去業雖滅謝，次業續生，故得至現在。若爾，即有大失。何以故？或時過去善心滅而惡心續生，今亦應過去善業滅而次惡業續生，此唯見惡業至現在。若爾，應感惡報，何得感善果耶？若言業來而不隨心者，此業應自有報，離心而受。今實不爾。復次，業若有相，即是有為；若是有為，必墮三相。若墮三相；即是生滅；若是生滅，即不至現在。過去既滅，當知本業亦滅，誰感此果？不可以新業始生，能感今果。當知業有相貌，此義不可。若言業無相貌而能感果者，此亦不然。所以者何？無相之法，即是無為。無為無業，何得感果？復次，無相之法即是空義，空無生滅，豈得名業？若說空無相能感果者，三無為法亦應感果。既不得爾，云何而言業是無相而能感果？如是種種因緣，業不可得，當知無有此業。

If it is said that, although the past mind has ceased and passed away, a subsequent mind arises in succession, and so the karma is able to follow along and arrive with this subsequent mind — then it ought equally to be said that, although the past karma has ceased and passed away, a subsequent karma arises in succession, and so it is able to reach into the present. But if this is so, a great fault follows. Why is this? At times the past wholesome mind ceases and an unwholesome mind arises in succession after it — and, by the same logic, the past wholesome karma ought then to cease and an unwholesome karma arise in succession after it, so that only unwholesome karma would ever be seen reaching into the present. If this is so, only unwholesome retribution ought to be received — how, then, could any wholesome fruit ever be obtained? If it is said that the karma arrives without following along with the mind at all, then this karma ought to have its own result entirely apart from the mind, received independently of it — but this is, in fact, not so.

Furthermore: if karma has a mark or characteristic, it is a conditioned dharma; and if it is conditioned, it must fall under the three marks of arising, abiding, and ceasing. And if it falls under these three marks, it is subject to arising and ceasing; and if it is subject to arising and ceasing, it cannot reach into the present at all. Since the past has already ceased, the original karma must likewise have ceased — so who, then, brings about this present result? It cannot be that karma newly arisen now is able to bring about the present result instead. One should know, then, that the notion of karma possessing a fixed mark or characteristic is untenable.

If it is said instead that karma, having no mark or characteristic, is nonetheless able to bring about a result — this too does not hold. Why is this? A dharma without any mark whatsoever is precisely an unconditioned dharma; and an unconditioned dharma has no karma in it at all — how, then, could it bring about a result? Furthermore, a dharma without any mark is precisely what is meant by emptiness itself; emptiness has no arising and ceasing — how, then, could it be called karma at all? And if it is said that emptiness, being without mark, is able to bring about a result, then the three unconditioned dharmas ought equally to be able to bring about a result — and since this cannot be so, how can it still be said that karma, being without mark, is able to bring about a result? Through causes and conditions such as these, karma can, in the end, never be found — one should know, then, that there is, in truth, no such thing as this karma at all.

若業不可得，云何言陰入界等一切皆從內業因生？亦不從外緣生者，若定從緣而有報者，則一切陰陽會時，皆應有果報、陰入界等一切諸法。若爾，則不待業持識來，方乃有生，故知非外緣生。若謂因緣合，故有果報、陰入等法生者，若因緣中各有生，合時應有二生；若各無生，合時何得而有生？若謂離因緣而有生者，此事不然。從因緣故有生尚不可，何況無因緣而有生？若無因緣而有生者，則因果義壞。世間行善之人應得惡報，行惡之人應得善報，亦不應有修道，此即破於世間善惡因果，名大邪見。當知陰入等一切諸法不從內因有，亦不從外緣有，亦不因緣合故有，亦不無因緣有。若非有，即是空。若於無所有空中計有者，當知但是無明顛倒妄計為有。

If karma cannot be found at all, how can it still be said that the aggregates, sense-bases, elements, and the rest all arise from an internal karmic cause? Nor do they arise from an external condition alone: if a result definitely arose merely from external conditions, then whenever any two things of yin and yang came together, a result, and the aggregates and the rest, ought to arise there as well. But if this were so, there would be no need to wait for karma sustaining consciousness to arrive before birth could occur at all — hence it is known that this does not arise from external condition alone either. If it is said that cause and condition come together, and therefore the result, the aggregates, and the rest arise: if each of cause and condition already contains arising within itself, then, when they come together, there ought to be two arisings; but if neither contains arising at all, how could arising possibly occur once they come together? And if it is said that arising occurs apart from both cause and condition, this too does not hold — for if arising through cause and condition is already untenable, how much more untenable is arising with no cause or condition whatsoever? If there could be arising with no cause or condition at all, the very meaning of cause and effect would collapse: those in the world who do good ought then to receive evil retribution, and those who do evil ought to receive good retribution, and there would be no reason for anyone to cultivate the path at all — this would utterly destroy the world's own principle of good and evil, cause and effect, and is called a great wrong view.

One should know, then, that the aggregates, sense-bases, and every other such dharma do not exist from an internal cause alone, nor from an external condition alone, nor from cause and condition coming together, nor without any cause or condition at all. If they are not truly existent in any of these ways, they are empty. And if, within this very emptiness where nothing at all exists, existence is still conceived, one should know that this is nothing but ignorance and inverted thinking, falsely conceiving existence where there is none.

若了知顛倒所計之法一切悉皆虛誑，猶如夢幻，但有名字，名字之法亦不可得，則言語道斷，心行處滅，畢竟空寂，猶如虛空。若行者體知一切諸法如虛空者，無取無捨，無依無倚，無住無著。若心無取捨、依倚、住著，則一切妄想顛倒生死業行悉皆止息，無為無欲，無念無行，無造無作，無示無說，無諍無競，泯然清淨，如大涅槃，是名真止。此則止無所止，無止之止，名體真止。故經偈云：
一切諸法中，因緣空無主，
息心達本源，故號為沙門。

If one comes to understand clearly that everything conceived through such inverted thinking is, in every case, illusory and unreal, just like a dream or a magical apparition — possessing only a name, and even that name-dharma being itself unfindable — then the path of language is cut off, the sphere of mental activity is extinguished, and all is, in the end, empty and still, just like empty space itself.

If a practitioner embodies the knowledge that all dharmas are like empty space, there is nothing to grasp and nothing to reject, nothing to depend on and nothing to lean against, nothing to abide in and nothing to cling to. And if the mind is free of grasping and rejecting, depending and leaning, abiding and clinging, then every deluded thought, every inverted notion, every karmic action of birth-and-death is thereby brought entirely to rest — unconditioned, free of desire, free of thought, free of activity, free of fabrication and construction, free of display and of speech, free of contention and of striving, dissolved away into purity, like great nirvāṇa itself. This is what is called true stopping. This, then, is a stopping that stops nothing, a stopping with nothing left to stop — this is what is called embodying-truth stopping. Hence the sūtra says in verse:
"Within all dharmas whatsoever, causes and conditions are empty, with no master over them;
Stilling the mind, one reaches the very source — hence one is called a śramaṇa."

Reading note: This is the treatise's third sustained tetralemma-style analysis (after fascicle 1's against "contaminated dharma" and fascicle 2's against "offense"), now aimed at karma itself: can it be found in the wholesome mind that produced it, in a mind-independent transmission, in a fixed characteristic, or in the total absence of one? None survive scrutiny. Note carefully what the passage does not conclude — it never says karma and its results are unreal or that ethical cause-and-effect can be dismissed; it explicitly names the opposite conclusion ("those who do good ought to receive evil retribution... this is a great wrong view") as the actual danger of getting this analysis wrong. The point is that karma has no fixed, independently findable essence, not that it doesn't function conventionally. This is "embodying-truth stopping" in full: not a technique applied to the mind from outside, but the mind's own direct realization of emptiness, described here as identical with the language used elsewhere for nirvāṇa itself. This closes the third of the gate of stopping's four parts.

Verifying Stopping: The Five Wheels 明證止 · 五輪禪

Chinese Source

第四、明證止者，有二解不同：有師云：「止無別證，但能為諸禪作前方便。若有所證，即屬餘禪。」此義至下明善根發中，即是其事。二者、有師言：「止非但通發諸禪，亦自有別證之法，即是五輪禪。所以者何？諸餘法門悉別有安心修習之法，然後次第發禪不同。」今明此止，但制心一處，則五輪自發。譬如淨水無波，則萬像悉現，止亦如是。今明因止證五輪。五輪者：一、地輪；二、水輪；三、風輪；四、金沙輪；五、金剛輪。此五法門，悉是借譬立名。通名為輪者，轉也。如世輪若轉，離此至彼。禪中明輪亦爾，如地輪因離下地亂心，轉至上地，故名為輪。乃至金剛輪義亦復如是，轉至無學極果故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying the verification of stopping: there are two differing interpretations here. One master says: "Stopping has no distinct realization of its own — it can only serve as the preliminary expedient means for the other dhyānas. If there is any realization at all, it belongs properly to one of those other dhyānas." This point will be treated further below, exactly where the arising of wholesome roots is clarified. A second master says: "Stopping not only generally brings forth every dhyāna in common, but also has its own distinct method of realization — namely, the five-wheel dhyāna. Why is this? Every other dharma-gate has its own distinct method of settling and cultivating the mind, and only afterward brings forth dhyāna in its own particular sequence." Here, it is clarified that this stopping alone, simply by restraining the mind to a single place, brings forth the five wheels of itself — just as, when clear water is free of ripples, the ten thousand reflections all appear within it of themselves; stopping is exactly the same.

Here, then, the five wheels realized through stopping are clarified. The five wheels are: the earth wheel; the water wheel; the wind wheel; the golden-sand wheel; and the vajra wheel. Each of these five names is borrowed as a comparison to establish its name. They are called, in general, "wheels" because a wheel turns — just as a worldly wheel, once turning, moves from one place to another. The wheels clarified within dhyāna are exactly the same: the earth wheel, for instance, is so named because it turns the scattered mind of the lower stage away and moves it to a higher stage. This holds, in the same way, all the way up to the vajra wheel, which turns one all the way to the ultimate fruit of no-more-learning.

一、地輪者，如地有二義：一者、住持不動；二者、出生萬物。行者因止若證未到地定，忽然湛心，自覺身心相空，泯然入定，定法持心不動，故名住持。因未到地出生初禪種種功德，事同出生萬物。二、水輪者，水有二義：一、潤漬生長；二、體性柔軟。行者於地輪中若證水輪三昧，即是發諸禪種種功德，定水潤心，自覺心中善根增長，即是潤漬義。因得定故，身心濡軟，折伏高心，心隨善法，即是柔軟義，故名水輪。

First, the earth wheel: earth has two properties — first, sustaining and remaining unmoved; second, giving birth to the ten thousand things. If a practitioner, through stopping, realizes the concentration of the "not-yet-arrived" state, the mind suddenly becomes deep and still, and one becomes aware of oneself that the mark of body and mind is empty, dissolving away into concentration; the dharma of concentration itself then sustains the mind unmoved — hence it is called sustaining. And because the "not-yet-arrived" state gives rise to the various merits of the first dhyāna, this matches the property of giving birth to the ten thousand things.

Second, the water wheel: water has two properties — first, moistening and nurturing growth; second, being soft and pliant in its own nature. If the practitioner, within the earth wheel, realizes the samādhi of the water wheel, the various merits of dhyāna are thereby brought forth: the water of concentration moistens the mind, and one becomes aware of oneself that wholesome roots are growing within the mind — this is the meaning of moistening. And because concentration is attained, body and mind become soft and pliant, arrogance is subdued, and the mind comes to follow wholesome dharmas — this is the meaning of softness; hence it is called the water wheel.

三、風輪者，如世間風，有三義：一者、遊空無礙；二者、鼓動萬物；三者、能破壞。行者發風輪三昧亦如是，若因禪定發相似智慧，無礙方便，如風遊空，一切無礙。鼓動者，得方便道，即能擊發種種出世善根，功德生長。破壞者，智慧方便能摧破一切諸見煩惱。若二乘人得此風輪三昧，即是五方便相似無漏解發。若是菩薩，即入鐵輪十信，是名風輪。四、金沙輪者，金則譬真，沙諭無著。行者若發見思真慧，無染無著，得三道果。若是菩薩，即入三賢十地位中，能破一切塵沙煩惱，是名金沙輪。五、金剛輪者，第九無礙道，名金剛輪三昧。譬如金剛，體堅用利，能摧碎諸物。金剛三昧亦復如是，不為妄惑所侵，能斷一切結使，成阿羅漢。若在菩薩心，即是金剛般若，破無明細惑，證一切種智，亦名清淨禪。菩薩依是禪故，得大菩提果。

Third, the wind wheel: worldly wind has three properties — first, traveling through open space without obstruction; second, stirring the ten thousand things; third, being able to destroy. When the practitioner brings forth the samādhi of the wind wheel, it is exactly the same: if, through dhyāna-concentration, an approximate wisdom and unobstructed skillful means are brought forth, this is like wind traveling through open space, unobstructed in every direction. As for stirring: having attained the path of skillful means, one is able to strike and rouse forth every kind of world-transcending wholesome root, and merit grows accordingly. As for destroying: skillful wisdom is able to crush and destroy every wrong view and affliction. If a follower of the two lesser vehicles attains this samādhi of the wind wheel, this is precisely the arising of an approximate, uncontaminated understanding through the five expedient means. If it is a bodhisattva, this is precisely entering the iron wheel of the ten stages of faith. This is called the wind wheel.

Fourth, the golden-sand wheel: gold here serves as a metaphor for truth, and sand serves as a metaphor for non-attachment. If the practitioner brings forth the true wisdom that severs the views and the discursive afflictions, undefiled and unattached, the fruits of the three lower paths are attained. If it is a bodhisattva, this is precisely entering the stages of the three levels of worthies and the ten grounds, able to break through every affliction as countless as sand and dust. This is called the golden-sand wheel.

Fifth, the vajra wheel: the ninth unobstructed path is called the vajra-wheel samādhi. Just as vajra, being hard in its very substance and sharp in its function, is able to crush and shatter every other thing, the vajra samādhi is exactly the same: undisturbed by any deluded confusion, it is able to sever every fetter, and thereby accomplishes arhatship. Within a bodhisattva's mind, this is precisely the vajra-like prajñā that breaks through the subtle delusion of ignorance and realizes the wisdom that knows all things in every mode — also called the pure-and-undefiled dhyāna. In dependence on this dhyāna, the bodhisattva attains the fruit of great bodhi.

復次，如輪若無牛御，終不自轉。五輪禪定亦復如是。雖當地各有諸妙功德，若不體真為導，無著熏修，則於地地有礙，便乖輪用。今行者善修體真，無著故，能從初心轉至極果，輪用乃成。是以《法華經》云：「眾生處處著，引之令得出。」當知行者善修止門，則能具足五輪禪定，證三乘聖果。

Furthermore: just as a wheel, without an ox to drive it, will never turn on its own, the dhyāna-concentration of the five wheels is exactly the same. Although each stage possesses its own wondrous merits, if embodying-truth stopping is not taken as the guide, and cultivation is not carried out free of attachment, then obstruction arises at every single stage, and the wheel's own function fails. Here, because the practitioner skillfully cultivates embodying-truth stopping, free of attachment, they are able to turn from the very first mind all the way to the ultimate fruit, and the wheel's function is thereby accomplished. Hence the Lotus Sūtra says: "Sentient beings are attached everywhere they turn; the Buddha leads them so that they may find their way out." One should know, then, that a practitioner who skillfully cultivates the gate of stopping is thereby able to be complete in the dhyāna-concentration of the five wheels, and realize the noble fruits of all three vehicles.

Reading note: The five wheels trace a single continuous path from the very threshold of concentration (the earth wheel's "not-yet-arrived" state) to arhatship and beyond into the bodhisattva grounds, each wheel's name unpacked through a genuine property of its physical namesake — earth's stability and fertility, water's moistening and softness, wind's unobstructed reach and its power to stir and to destroy. The closing image is the passage's real punchline: five wheels, however wonderful individually, are inert without an ox to pull them — and embodying-truth stopping, the very practice this whole section has been about, is that ox. Depth of insight into emptiness is what actually keeps the practitioner moving from one stage to the next rather than becoming stuck admiring the merits of wherever they currently stand. This closes the fourth and final part of the gate of stopping, and with it, the whole of 止門 — the first of the internal expedient means' five layers.

Verifying Wholesome and Unwholesome Capacity 明驗善惡根性

Chinese Source

第二、明驗善惡根性者，行人既能善修止門，息諸亂想，則其心澄靜；以心靜故，宿世善根自然開發。若無善者，則發諸惡法。故經云：「先以定動，後以智拔。」止為初門，善、惡二事之中，必有其一，行者應當明識其相，取捨之間不乖正道，故須分別。今就善、惡根性中，即為二意：一、明驗善根性；二、明驗惡根性。然論善惡發之前後，各逐其人，未必定前善而後惡也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying how to verify wholesome and unwholesome capacity: once a practitioner has properly cultivated the gate of stopping and brought every scattered thought to rest, the mind becomes clear and still; and because the mind is still, wholesome roots from past lives naturally come to be brought forth of themselves. Where there is nothing wholesome present, unwholesome dharmas are instead brought forth. Hence the sūtra says: "First stir it through concentration, then draw it out through wisdom."

Since stopping serves as the first gate, one or the other of these two things — the wholesome or the unwholesome — is bound to occur, and the practitioner must clearly recognize its mark, so that nothing goes against the correct path in taking up and setting aside; hence this distinction must be made. Here, within the topic of wholesome and unwholesome capacity, there are two parts: clarifying how to verify wholesome capacity, and clarifying how to verify unwholesome capacity. As for the actual order in which the wholesome and the unwholesome arise, however, this follows each individual practitioner — it is not necessarily fixed that the wholesome always arises first and the unwholesome only afterward.

第一、明驗善根性，即為四意：一、列善法章門；二、正明善根發相；三、驗知虛實；四、料揀發禪不定。

First, clarifying how to verify wholesome capacity: this itself has four parts — first, listing the topics of the wholesome dharmas; second, directly clarifying the marks of wholesome roots arising; third, verifying whether they are genuine or false; fourth, distinguishing that the arising of dhyāna through this is not fixed.

Listing the Topics of the Wholesome Dharmas 列善法章門

Here, within external wholesomeness, the wholesome is manifold; in brief, five kinds are given: giving; upholding the precepts; filial devotion to parents and teachers; faith and reverence toward the Triple Gem, together with diligent offering; and reading, reciting, and studying the teachings. These five wholesome roots, in brief, serve to indicate the different marks by which external wholesomeness arises. The reason all five are classed as external wholesomeness is that their original practice is, in every case, cultivated within a scattered mind, not yet able to leave the desire realm behind or bring forth any dhyāna-concentration free of contamination — hence they are called external wholesomeness.

二、明內善者，即是五門禪：一、阿那波那門；二、不淨觀門；三、慈心門；四、因緣門；五、念佛三昧門。此五法門通攝一切諸禪，發諸無漏，故名為內善。

Second, clarifying internal wholesomeness: this is precisely the five-gate dhyāna — the gate of mindfulness of the breath; the gate of the contemplation of impurity; the gate of loving-kindness; the gate of causation; and the gate of the buddha-recollection samādhi. These five dharma-gates, taken together, encompass every dhyāna, and bring forth every uncontaminated attainment — hence they are called internal wholesomeness.

問曰：內善無量，何得但說五門？答曰：五名雖少，而行通諸禪。所以者何？一、阿那波那門者，此通至根本及特勝、通明等諸禪三昧；二、不淨觀門者，此通九想、背捨、超越等諸禪三昧；三、慈心門者，此通四無量等諸禪三昧；四、因緣門者，此通至十二因緣、四諦等慧行諸禪三昧；五、念佛門者，此通至九種禪及百八三昧。

Question: internal wholesomeness is measureless — how can only five gates be spoken of? Answer: though the five names are few, the practice behind them extends through every dhyāna. Why is this? The gate of mindfulness of the breath extends all the way to the root dhyānas, the sixteen special excellences, the thorough-and-bright contemplation, and other such dhyāna-samādhis. The gate of the contemplation of impurity extends to the nine contemplations, the liberations, the transcendence samādhi, and other such dhyāna-samādhis. The gate of loving-kindness extends to the four immeasurable minds and other such dhyāna-samādhis. The gate of causation extends all the way to the twelve links of dependent origination, the Four Noble Truths, and the other wisdom-conduct dhyāna-samādhis. The gate of buddha-recollection extends all the way to the nine kinds of great dhyāna and the one hundred eight samādhis.

復次，初數息門，即是世間凡夫禪；次不淨門，即是出世間禪，諸聲聞人所行；次慈心門，即是凡聖二人為大福德修慈，入四無量心；次因緣門者，即是辟支佛人之所行；次念佛門，功德廣大，即是諸菩薩之所行。此則略明五門次第淺深之相。

Furthermore: the first, the gate of counting the breath, is precisely the worldly dhyāna of the ordinary person; next, the gate of impurity is precisely a world-transcending dhyāna, practiced by śrāvakas; next, the gate of loving-kindness is precisely what both ordinary people and the noble cultivate together, for the sake of great merit, entering the four immeasurable minds; next, the gate of causation is precisely what is practiced by pratyekabuddhas; and next, the gate of buddha-recollection, whose merit is vast and expansive, is precisely what is practiced by bodhisattvas. This, in brief, clarifies the shallow-to-deep sequence and marks of the five gates.

復次，五門禪定對治四分煩惱，四分煩惱出生八萬四千塵勞，當知五門亦能出八萬四千法門。此而言之，但說五門，則攝一切內善具足。數人所明初賢五停心觀發，與此有相開處。

Furthermore: the dhyāna-concentration of the five gates serves as the antidote to the four divisions of affliction, and the four divisions of affliction give rise to the eighty-four thousand forms of worldly defilement — hence it should be known that the five gates are likewise able to lead out from these eighty-four thousand dharma-gates. Speaking in this way, then, though only five gates are named, every internal wholesomeness is thereby encompassed in full. What the Ābhidharma masters clarify as the five contemplations that still the mind, arising at the initial stage of worthies, has points of overlap with what is given here.

Reading note: This section pairs neatly with fascicle 2's twenty-five preliminary conditions: those were conditions for practice to even begin; this is a diagnostic for what stopping actually surfaces once it has begun. "External" wholesomeness (giving, precepts, filial devotion, faith, study) is ordinary ethical cultivation done with a still-scattered mind; "internal" wholesomeness is the five-gate dhyāna that already reaches toward uncontaminated attainment. Notice, too, the deliberate correspondence Zhiyi draws to the Ābhidharma tradition's own "five contemplations that still the mind" (五停心觀) — the treatise is not inventing an idiosyncratic scheme so much as mapping its own five gates onto a category practitioners from other lineages would already recognize.

The Marks of External Wholesome Roots Arising 正明善根發相 · 外善根發相

Chinese Source

第二、次明善根發相，亦還為二：一、明外善根發相；二、明內善根發相。云何外善根發相？外善非一，今依前章門略出五種：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, next clarifying the marks of wholesome roots arising: this too has two parts — clarifying the marks of external wholesome roots arising, and clarifying the marks of internal wholesome roots arising. What, then, are the marks of external wholesome roots arising? External wholesomeness is not of one kind only; here, following the topics already given above, five kinds are set out in brief:

初、明行者若坐中靜定，忽見種種衣服、臥具、飲食、珍寶、田園、池沼、車乘。如是等事，或復因心靜故，自能捨離慳貪，心行惠施，無所悋惜。當知此是過去、今生布施，習、報二種善根發相。

First: if, while the practitioner sits in still concentration, various sorts of clothing, bedding, food and drink, treasures, fields and gardens, ponds, or carriages suddenly appear — or if, because the mind is still, one becomes able of oneself to let go of stinginess and greed, the mind moving toward generosity, giving without any reluctance — one should know that this is the mark of the two kinds of wholesome roots, from habituation and from retribution, arising through giving in the past and in this present life.

二、行者若於止靜定之中，忽見自身相好端嚴，身所著衣清淨如法，洗浴清潔，得好淨物。見如是等事，或復因心靜故，發戒忍心，自然知輕識重，乃至小罪，心生怖畏，忍辱謙卑。當知此是過去、今生戒忍，習、報二種善根發相也。

Second: if the practitioner, within stopping and still concentration, suddenly sees their own body handsome and dignified in form, the robes they wear pure and in accordance with the Dharma, freshly bathed and clean, obtaining fine, pure things — or if, because the mind is still, precept-observance and patience arise within the mind, so that one naturally distinguishes the light offense from the grave, and even at the slightest offense the mind grows fearful, forbearing and humble — one should know that this, too, is the mark of the two kinds of wholesome roots, from habituation and from retribution, arising through precepts and patience in the past and in this present life.

三、行者若於坐中，忽見師僧、父母、宗親、眷屬著淨衣服，歡喜悅豫端嚴。見如是等事，或復以心靜故，自然慈仁、恭敬、孝悌心生。當知此是過去、今生孝順尊長，習、報二種善根發相也。

Third: if the practitioner, while sitting, suddenly sees teachers and monastics, parents, relatives, and family members wearing pure clothing, joyful, content, and dignified — or if, because the mind is still, kindness, reverence, filial devotion, and brotherly respect arise naturally within the mind — one should know that this, too, is the mark of the two kinds of wholesome roots, from habituation and from retribution, arising through filial devotion toward one's elders in the past and in this present life.

四、行者若於坐中，忽見諸塔寺、尊儀形像、經書、供養莊嚴清淨僧眾雲集法會。見如是等事，或復於靜心中發信敬，尊重三寶，心樂供養，精勤勇猛，常無懈倦。當知此是過去、今生信敬三寶，精勤供養，習、報二種善根發相也。

Fourth: if the practitioner, while sitting, suddenly sees stūpas and temples, revered images, sūtra texts, or a pure assembly of monastics gathered for a dharma-offering, adorned and solemn — or if, within the still mind, faith and reverence arise, honoring the Triple Gem, the mind delighting in making offerings, diligent and vigorous, never growing weary — one should know that this, too, is the mark of the two kinds of wholesome roots, from habituation and from retribution, arising through faith and reverence toward the Triple Gem, together with diligent offering, in the past and in this present life.

五、行者若於坐中，因心澄靜，或見解釋三藏、聽受讀誦大乘有德四眾；或時因心靜故，讀誦自然而入，隨所聽聞，即時開悟，或復自然能了解三藏大乘經典，分別無滯。當知悉是過去、今生讀誦聽說，習、報二種善根發相。

Fifth: if the practitioner, while sitting, because the mind is clear and still, sees themselves expounding the Tripiṭaka, or listening to and reciting the Mahāyāna together with the virtuous of the four assemblies — or if, at times, because the mind is still, reading and reciting come naturally and easily, so that whatever is heard is understood at once, or one is naturally able to comprehend the Tripiṭaka and the Mahāyāna sūtras, distinguishing their meaning without any obstruction — one should know that all of this is the mark of the two kinds of wholesome roots, from habituation and from retribution, arising through reading, reciting, and study in the past and in this present life.

行者見如是種種好相，及發諸善心者，此非禪定，多是過去、今生於散心中修諸功德，今以心靜力故，得發其事，見諸相貌，悉屬報因相現，善心開發，皆是習因善發也。如是眾多，說不可盡，此則略示大意。

When the practitioner sees such various favorable marks as these, and wholesome states of mind are brought forth, this is not itself dhyāna-concentration — for the most part it is merit cultivated within a scattered mind in the past and in this present life, now brought forth through the power of the stilled mind: the various appearances seen all belong to the manifestation of the retribution-cause, while the arising of wholesome mind is, in every case, the arising of the habituation-cause. Such cases are many and cannot be exhaustively described; this, in brief, indicates only the general sense of it.

復次，發習、報兩因，行人根性不同，自有行人但發報因相，不發習因善心；自有行人但發習因善心，而不發報因之相；自有行人具發習、報兩因；自有行人二俱不發。如是等事，因緣難解，豈可謬釋？

Furthermore: in the arising of the two causes, habituation and retribution, practitioners' capacities differ. Some practitioners bring forth only the mark of the retribution-cause, without bringing forth the wholesome mind of the habituation-cause; some bring forth only the wholesome mind of the habituation-cause, without bringing forth the mark of the retribution-cause; some bring forth both causes, habituation and retribution, together; and some bring forth neither. Matters such as these, bound up in causes and conditions difficult to fathom, could hardly be explained through careless guesswork.

問曰：散心善根，何得於靜心中現？答曰：於禪定中尚得見過去、今生所起煩惱惡業，何況善根扶理而不得見？

Question: how can wholesome roots cultivated with a scattered mind manifest within a still mind? Answer: within dhyāna-concentration, one can even see the afflictions and unwholesome karmic actions arisen in the past and in this present life — how much more, then, can wholesome roots, which accord with principle, fail to be seen?

問曰：見此等諸相，亦有是魔所作不？答曰：亦有是魔所作。若欲分別，但魔名殺者。若此等相發時，能令行人心識動亂，或復增諸煩惱，逼迫障蔽，眾多妨難，不利定心，悉是魔之所作。其善根發者，行人自覺，見此相已，雖復未證禪定，而身心明白，諸根清淨，身有色力，所為吉利，善念開發，因此已後，自覺心神易可攝錄，身心安隱，無諸過患。當知此為善根發相。

Question: among such marks as these that are seen, is it also possible that some are the work of demons? Answer: yes, some are indeed the work of demons. If one wishes to distinguish them: a demon, simply put, is named "the killer." Whenever such marks as these arise and cause the practitioner's mind and consciousness to become agitated and disordered, or cause afflictions to increase, pressing in and obstructing, bringing many hindrances and obstacles that are of no benefit to the concentrated mind — all of this is the work of a demon. As for the arising of a genuine wholesome root: the practitioner becomes aware of it directly, and, upon seeing such a mark, although dhyāna-concentration is not yet realized, body and mind become clear and bright, the faculties become pure, the body gains vigor and color, what one undertakes turns out well, and wholesome thoughts are brought forth; from this point on, one becomes aware that the mind and spirit are easily gathered in, body and mind are at peace, and free of any fault. One should know that this is the mark of a wholesome root arising.

復次，若此等事，善根發者，報因之相則暫現便謝，習因心善則相續不斷。若是魔作相，則久久不滅，雖謝更來，逼亂行者，善心則暫發還滅，或時變成惡念，當知邪也。復次，邪正之相甚為難測，自非親近明師，非可妄取。

Furthermore: in such matters as these, when it is a genuine wholesome root arising, the mark of the retribution-cause appears only briefly and then passes away, while the wholesome mind of the habituation-cause continues on without interruption. But if it is a mark made by a demon, it fails to disappear for a very long time, and even once it passes, it returns again, pressing in and disturbing the practitioner — the wholesome mind arises only briefly and then vanishes again, and at times even turns into an unwholesome thought; one should know that this is deviant. Furthermore, the marks of the deviant and the correct are extremely difficult to fathom — without drawing close to an enlightened teacher, one should not presume to judge them for oneself.

問曰：此諸善根為當一向前發，亦得證諸禪時於深定中發也？答曰：此事無定，未必一向定前見也。外善既麁，故先明耳。

Question: do these wholesome roots always arise beforehand, or can they also arise within deep concentration, at the very time the various dhyānas are actually realized? Answer: this is not fixed either way — it is not necessarily the case that they are always seen only prior to concentration. It is simply that external wholesomeness is the coarser of the two, and so it has been clarified first.

Reading note: The recurring pair here — the habituation-cause (習因) and the retribution-cause (報因) — distinguishes two things that can look identical from the inside: a vivid mental image of, say, fine robes or a solemn temple (the retribution-cause, a mark surfacing from past karma) versus the actual wholesome disposition that arises alongside it, such as spontaneous generosity or reverence (the habituation-cause, the trace of past cultivation now resuming). The treatise's answer to "how do I know this isn't a demon?" is notably practical rather than doctrinal: genuine wholesome marks are brief and self-limiting while the wholesome mind-state they leave behind persists; demonic marks are persistent and recurring while the wholesome mind-state they produce is fragile and quick to collapse, sometimes into its opposite. This is the treatise's first extended treatment of demonic interference — worth remembering once the fifth internal-expedient-means layer, recognizing demonic affairs (覺魔事), is reached later in the treatise.

Chinese Source

Second, what is meant by the marks of internal wholesome roots arising? Here, internal wholesome roots arising is distinguished with reference to the five-gate dhyāna. Within these five gates, each gate opens into three, giving fifteen kinds of wholesome roots arising in all. First, clarifying that the gate of mindfulness of the breath has three differing marks of wholesome roots arising: the wholesome root of counting the breath; the wholesome root of following the breath; and the wholesome root of contemplating the breath.

一、數息善根者，行人如上善修三止，身心調和，發於欲界及未到地等諸禪，身心湛然空寂，定心安隱，於後或一坐二坐，乃至經旬，或經月經年，將息得所，定心不退，即於定中，心忽覺身心運動，八觸次第而起，此即發根本初禪善根之相。於此定中，喜樂善心安隱不可為諭。如是發初禪已，乃至發四禪定空等。

Within this concentration, joy, happiness, and a wholesome, peaceful mind arise beyond what words can convey. Having thus brought forth the first dhyāna, one goes on to bring forth the second, third, and fourth dhyānas, the formless attainments, and the rest.

Second, the arising of the wholesome root of following the breath: likewise, within the still concentration of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, one suddenly becomes aware of the breath going out and in, long or short, and of every pore of the body open and porous; the mind then clearly sees the thirty-six substances within the body, just as one might open a granary and see grain, millet, hemp-seed, and beans — the mind is greatly astonished and delighted, still and at ease, every bodily formation set to rest, feeling joy and happiness and the rest. This is the mark of the special-excellences dhyāna's wholesome root arising.

三、明觀息善根發者，亦於欲界、未到細靜心中，忽見自身氣息從毛孔出入，遍身無礙，漸漸明利，如羅縠中見皮重數乃至骨肉等亦如是；亦見身內八萬戶蟲麁細長短，言語音聲，定心喜樂，倍於上說，或見自身猶如芭蕉、聚沫、雲影相等。此是通明觀善根發相。

Third, clarifying the arising of the wholesome root of contemplating the breath: likewise, within the fine, still mind of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, one suddenly sees one's own breath going out and in through every pore, unobstructed throughout the body, growing gradually clearer and sharper, as though seeing, through a fine silk gauze, the layered skin, and, in the same way, the flesh and bones beneath; one also sees the eighty thousand households of worms within the body, coarse and fine, long and short, along with their speech and sound, the concentrated mind's joy and happiness doubled beyond what was said above; or one sees one's own body as though it were a plantain trunk, a mass of foam, or the reflection of a cloud. This is the mark of the thorough-and-bright contemplation's wholesome root arising.

Reading note: These three marks recapitulate, in reverse order of subtlety, the three breath-based attainments fascicle 2 already named in passing: the root dhyānas from bare counting of the breath, the sixteen special excellences from following it, and the thorough-and-bright contemplation from directly contemplating it — each gate's arising-mark growing more penetrating than the last, from the eight tactile sensations of ordinary absorption to a vision of the body dissolving into its ultimate constituents, down to the parasitic life the treatise says inhabits it.

Third, clarifying that the contemplation of loving-kindness has three differing marks of wholesome roots arising: loving-kindness that takes sentient beings as its object; loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object; and objectless loving-kindness.

First, the arising of loving-kindness that takes sentient beings as its object: likewise, within the still concentration of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, the mind suddenly arises, holding sentient beings in loving-kindness — first taking the mark of a loved one's happiness as its object, and, through this, concentration is brought forth, peaceful and joyful, until even neutral persons and enemies are all seen gaining happiness, without anger, without resentment, without enmity, without vexation, vast and measureless, filling all ten directions. This is the mark of loving-kindness that takes sentient beings as its object arising. Compassion that takes sentient beings as its object may likewise arise, and joy and equanimity in the same way.

二、明法緣慈發者，亦於欲界、未到靜定心中，忽然自覺一切內外但有陰入法，起唯法起，滅唯法滅，不見眾生及我、我所，但有五陰，於受陰中有樂受。如是知已，即緣此樂受發於慈定，無瞋無恨，無怨無惱，廣大無量，遍滿十方，是為法緣慈。或發法緣悲，乃至喜、捨，亦如慈善根發相。

Second, clarifying the arising of loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object: likewise, within the still concentration of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, one suddenly becomes aware that everything, within and without, is nothing but the dharma of the aggregates and sense-bases — what arises, only dharma arises; what ceases, only dharma ceases — and one no longer sees any sentient being, self, or what belongs to a self, but only the five aggregates, and, within the aggregate of sensation, a pleasant sensation. Having come to know this, one takes this very pleasant sensation as the object from which loving-kindness-concentration is brought forth, without anger, without resentment, without enmity, without vexation, vast and measureless, filling all ten directions — this is loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object. Compassion that takes dharma as its object may likewise arise, and joy and equanimity, following the same pattern as loving-kindness's wholesome root arising.

三、明無緣慈發者，亦於欲界、未到定心中，忽然覺悟一切諸法非有非無，不見二邊，所謂若眾生非眾生，若法非法，皆不可得，則無所緣，以無緣故，顛倒想息，寂然安樂，心與慈定相應，等觀一切，同此安樂，無瞋無恨，無怨無惱，廣大無量，遍滿十方，是為無緣慈善根發相。或發無緣悲定，乃至喜、捨亦如是。

Third, clarifying the arising of objectless loving-kindness: likewise, within the concentration of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, one suddenly awakens to the fact that no dharma whatsoever is either existent or nonexistent, and one no longer sees either of the two extremes — that is, whether "sentient being" or "not a sentient being," whether "dharma" or "not a dharma," none of it can be found, and so there is nothing at all to take as an object. Because there is no object, inverted thinking comes to rest, still and at ease, and the mind accords with loving-kindness-concentration, viewing all things equally, all of it sharing in this same ease, without anger, without resentment, without enmity, without vexation, vast and measureless, filling all ten directions — this is the mark of objectless loving-kindness's wholesome root arising. Objectless compassion-concentration may likewise arise, and joy and equanimity in the same way.

Reading note: The three-object scheme for loving-kindness (sentient beings, dharma, and no object at all) directly parallels the gate of stopping's own three kinds — fixed-object, restraining-the-mind, and embodying-truth — moving the same way from a concrete object of attention, to a doctrinally refined object (the aggregates rather than a "person"), to the collapse of the subject-object structure altogether. Each of the four immeasurable minds (loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity) is said to follow the identical three-stage pattern, though only loving-kindness is worked out in full here.

The Marks of Internal Wholesome Roots Arising: Causation 內善根發相 · 因緣門

Chinese Source

四、明因緣觀中有三種善根發不同者：一、三世十二緣；二、果報十二緣；三、一念十二緣。一、明三世十二因緣善根發者，亦於欲界、未到定心中，忽然覺悟心生，推尋三世，過去無明以來，不見我、人、無明等法，不斷不常，能破六十二種諸邪見網，心得正定，安隱寂然，觀慧分明，通達無礙，身口清淨，正行成就，此是三世十二因緣觀慧善根發相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying that the contemplation of causation has three differing marks of wholesome roots arising: the twelve links across the three periods of time; the twelve links as karmic result; and the twelve links within a single thought-moment.

First, clarifying the arising of the wholesome root of the twelve links across the three periods of time: likewise, within the concentration of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, the awakened mind suddenly arises, tracing back through the three periods of time — from ignorance in the past onward — and one no longer sees any dharma such as a self, a person, or ignorance itself as either cut off or permanent, able thereby to break through the net of sixty-two kinds of wrong views. The mind attains right concentration, at peace and still, contemplative wisdom clear, penetrating without obstruction, body and speech pure, right conduct accomplished. This is the mark of the twelve links across the three periods of time, the wisdom of this contemplation, arising.

二、明果報十二因緣善根發者，亦於欲界、未到定心中，忽覺心識明利，即自思尋：「我初生時，攬父母身分以為己有，名歌羅邏。歌羅邏時，名曰無明，因緣則有行、識，乃至老死，名為十二因緣。若歌羅邏時，但有三事合和，無人無我，三事不實。今無明等十二因緣諸法，竟何所依？若不見無明等諸法定是有者，豈是無邪？」如是念時，破有、無二見，歸心正道，正定相應，慧解開發，離諸邪行，此為果報十二因緣觀智善根發相。如此明十二因緣，出《大集經》中具辨。作此明因緣相，與苦集正同，亦得約此明四諦善根發也。

Second, clarifying the arising of the wholesome root of the twelve links as karmic result: likewise, within the concentration of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, one suddenly becomes aware that mind and consciousness have grown clear and sharp, and reflects directly: "When I was first born, I took hold of a portion of my parents' bodies and made it my own — this is called kalala. At the time of kalala, this is named ignorance; through causes and conditions there is then formation, consciousness, and so on up through old age and death — this is called the twelve links. But at the time of kalala, there was only the coming together of three things, with no person and no self in it at all — the three things themselves are not real. Now, ignorance and the other dharmas of the twelve links — on what, in the end, do they actually depend? And if ignorance and the other dharmas are not seen to definitely exist, how could they be said to definitely not exist either?" Reflecting in this way, one breaks through the two views of existence and nonexistence, the mind returning to the correct path, according with right concentration; wisdom and understanding are brought forth, and every deviant conduct is left behind. This is the mark of the twelve links as karmic result, the understanding of this contemplation, arising. This account of the twelve links is given in full in the Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra. What is clarified here as the mark of causation is exactly the same as suffering and its accumulation — the first two of the Four Noble Truths — and the arising of the Four Noble Truths' own wholesome root may likewise be clarified along these same lines.

三、明一念十二因緣善根發者，亦於欲界、未到靜定心中，忽然自覺剎那之心，無人無我，性本無實。所以者何？一念起時，必藉因緣，言因緣者，即具十二因緣，緣無自性，一念豈有定實？若不得一念之實，即破世性邪執，心與正定相應，智慧開發，猶如涌泉，身口清淨，離諸邪行，是為一念十二緣善根發相。此之十二因緣，亦出《大集經》中具辨，亦得約此十二緣明一心具四諦善根發也。

Third, clarifying the arising of the wholesome root of the twelve links within a single thought-moment: likewise, within the still concentration of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, one suddenly becomes aware that the mind of a single instant has no person and no self in it, its very nature originally without reality. Why is this? Whenever a single thought arises, it necessarily depends on causes and conditions; and what is meant by causes and conditions here is precisely the twelve links in full — and since conditions have no nature of their own, how could a single thought-moment possibly have any fixed reality? Once the reality of a single thought-moment can no longer be found, the deviant grasping at a fixed worldly nature is thereby broken through; the mind accords with right concentration, and wisdom is brought forth as though gushing from a spring, body and speech pure, every deviant conduct left behind. This is the mark of the twelve links within a single thought-moment's wholesome root arising. This account of the twelve links, too, is given in full in the Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra, and the arising of the Four Noble Truths' own wholesome root, complete within a single mind, may likewise be clarified along these same twelve links.

Reading note: The kalala reference deliberately recalls fascicle 1's own three-part account of conception, quoted there from the same Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra — the treatise circling back to reuse a passage already established rather than introducing new source material. All three approaches to the twelve links converge on the identical conclusion reached by fascicle 3's earlier tetralemma against "karma cannot be found": neither a fixed self nor a fixed absence of self can be located, whether the analysis spans three lifetimes, a single conception, or a single thought-moment. The explicit equation with the Four Noble Truths (dependent origination's arising = suffering and its accumulation, the first two truths) is a reminder that this whole apparatus of analysis, however elaborate, is meant to converge on the same handful of truths taught throughout the tradition.

The Marks of Internal Wholesome Roots Arising: Buddha-Recollection 內善根發相 · 念佛門

Chinese Source

五、明念佛中自有三種善根發相不同：一、念應佛；二、念報佛；三、念法佛。明三佛義，出《楞伽經》廣分別其相也。一、明念應佛善根發者，亦於欲界、未到靜定心中，忽然憶念佛之功德，即作是念：「如來往昔阿僧祇劫中，為一切眾生故，備行六波羅蜜，一切功德智慧故，身有相好光明，心有智慧圓照，降伏魔怨，無師自悟，自覺覺他，轉正法輪，普度一切，乃至入涅槃後，舍利經教廣益眾生。如是等功德，無量無邊。」作是念時，即敬愛心生，三昧開發，入定安樂，或於定中見佛身相，善心開發，或聞佛說法，心淨信解。如是等勝善境界非一，是為念應佛善根發相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, clarifying that buddha-recollection has three differing marks of wholesome roots arising: recollecting the response-body buddha; recollecting the reward-body buddha; and recollecting the dharma-body buddha. The meaning of these three buddha-bodies is given in full detail in the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra.

First, clarifying the arising of the wholesome root of recollecting the response-body buddha: likewise, within the still concentration of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, one suddenly recalls the Buddha's own merit, and reflects: "Throughout innumerable asaṃkhyeya kalpas in the past, the Tathāgata, for the sake of all sentient beings, fully practiced the six pāramitās; because of every merit and every wisdom, his body possesses the excellent marks and radiant light, his mind possesses wisdom that illuminates all things completely; he subdued demons and enemies, awakened of himself without a teacher, awakened himself and awakened others, turned the wheel of the true Dharma, and brought universal salvation to all — and even after entering nirvāṇa, his relics and his teachings continue broadly to benefit sentient beings. Such merits as these are measureless and boundless." Reflecting in this way, a mind of reverence and love at once arises, samādhi is brought forth, and one enters concentration in peace and joy; or, within concentration, one sees the Buddha's own bodily form, and a wholesome mind is brought forth; or one hears the Buddha expounding the Dharma, the mind pure with faith and understanding. Such excellent, wholesome states as these are not of one kind only — this is the mark of recollecting the response-body buddha's wholesome root arising.

二、明念報佛善根發者，亦於欲界、未到靜細心中，忽然憶念：「十方諸佛真實圓滿果報之身，湛然常住，色心清淨，微妙寂滅，功德智慧充滿法界，不生不滅，無作無為，豈有王宮之生，亦非雙樹之滅，為化眾生，十方佛土普應生滅。如是等功德無量無邊，不可思議。」作是念時，心定安隱，三昧開發，慧解分明。或作定中見不可思議佛法境界，即便出生無量願行、無量功德、無量智慧三昧法門，是為念報佛善根發相。

Second, clarifying the arising of the wholesome root of recollecting the reward-body buddha: likewise, within the still, fine mind of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, one suddenly recalls: "The true and perfectly complete reward-body of every buddha in the ten directions abides forever, deep and unmoving; form and mind alike pure, subtle and still; merit and wisdom filling the entire dharma-realm; neither arising nor ceasing, neither fabricated nor conditioned. How, then, could there truly be a birth in a royal palace, or truly a passing away between twin trees? It is only for the sake of transforming sentient beings that the buddha-lands of the ten directions respond, each according to its own arising and ceasing. Such merits as these are measureless, boundless, and inconceivable." Reflecting in this way, the mind becomes concentrated and at peace, samādhi is brought forth, understanding clear. Or, within concentration, one beholds inconceivable dharma-states of the Buddha, and thereupon measureless vows and practices, measureless merit, and measureless wisdom-samādhi dharma-gates are brought forth — this is the mark of recollecting the reward-body buddha's wholesome root arising.

三、明念法佛善根發者，亦於欲界、未到靜定心中，忽然憶念：「十方諸佛法身實相，猶如虛空。」即便覺悟一切諸法本自不生，今則無滅，非有非無，非來非去，非增非減，非境非智，非因非果，非常非斷，非縛非脫，非生死非涅槃，湛然清淨，有佛無佛，相性常然，眾生、諸佛同一實相者，即是法身佛也。故《大品經》云：「諸法如實相。」諸法如實即是佛，離是之外，更無別佛。如是念時，三昧現前，實慧開發，即時通達無量法門，寂然不動，一切不思議境界皆現定中成就之相。如《法華經》六根清淨中廣說，是為念法佛善根發相。

Third, clarifying the arising of the wholesome root of recollecting the dharma-body buddha: likewise, within the still concentration of the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state, one suddenly recalls: "The true mark of every buddha's dharma-body in the ten directions is just like empty space." One thereupon awakens to the fact that every dharma is, in itself, originally unarisen, and so now has no cessation either — neither existent nor nonexistent, neither coming nor going, neither increasing nor decreasing, neither object nor knowing subject, neither cause nor effect, neither permanent nor cut off, neither bound nor liberated, neither birth-and-death nor nirvāṇa, deep and pure; whether a buddha appears or not, the mark of this nature remains forever the same — sentient beings and every buddha share this one and the same true mark, and this is precisely the dharma-body buddha. Hence the Larger Sūtra says: "The true mark of every dharma, just as it is." Every dharma, just as it truly is, is itself the Buddha — apart from this, there is no separate buddha to be found. Reflecting in this way, samādhi manifests before one, and true wisdom is brought forth; measureless dharma-gates are penetrated at once, still and unmoving, every inconceivable state manifesting, within concentration, as an accomplished mark. This is given in full in the purification of the six sense-faculties in the Lotus Sūtra — this is the mark of recollecting the dharma-body buddha's wholesome root arising.

Reading note: The three-body scheme (response, reward, dharma) runs through the identical coarse-to-fine progression already seen twice this fascicle — a concrete devotional object (the Buddha's biography and physical marks), a more refined but still positively-described object (a reward-body abiding eternally, filling the dharma-realm), and finally an object with no positive content left to grasp at all (a dharma-body described entirely through negation, "neither... nor," collapsing the very distinction between buddha and non-buddha). That this is placed last among the five internal gates, and that fascicle 2 already called buddha-recollection's merit "vast and expansive... practiced by bodhisattvas," is consistent: the treatise consistently ranks its schemes from the concrete toward the empty, and buddha-recollection turns out to run the entire span in miniature.

First, clarifying how to verify whether they are genuine or false: if, within concentration, the various wholesome roots of dhyāna are brought forth, there is, among them, both the genuine and the false, and one must not carelessly take up or set aside either at random. If, when the various dhyāna-samādhis are brought forth, the mind fails to distinguish them properly, one may see a demonic concentration and take it to be a wholesome root arising, the mind grasping at it with attachment — and, through this deviance, fall ill and become deranged. If one wishes to tell them apart, one should verify them by means of two methods, and thereby know the genuine from the false: mark-verification; and dharma-verification.

Deviant dharmas are manifold; here, with reference to a single tactile sensation, ten pairs of deviant dharmas are given in brief, to clarify the deviant marks: excess or deficiency in the sensation itself; concentration or agitation; emptiness or existence; brightness or darkness; sorrow or joy; pain or pleasure; wholesome or unwholesome; foolishness or cleverness; bondage or liberation; and a mind too rigid or too soft. These ten pairs, clarifying the deviant marks, are each distinguished in terms of excess on the one hand or insufficiency on the other.

一、觸體增減者：如動觸發時，或身動手起脚亦隨，然外人見其兀兀如睡，或如著鬼，身手紛動，或坐時見諸異境，此為增相。減者，動初發時，若上若下，未及遍身，即便漸漸滅壞，因此都失境界，坐時蕭索，無法持身，此為減相。二、定亂：定者，動觸發時，識心及身為定所縛，不得自在，或復因此便入邪定，乃至七日不出。亂者，動觸發時，心意撩亂，攀緣不住。三者、空有：空者，觸發之時，都不見身，謂證空定。有者，觸發之時，覺身堅䩕，猶如木石。四、明闇：明者，觸發之時，見外種種光色，乃至日月星辰青、黃、赤、白種種光明。闇者，觸發之時，身心闇瞑，如入暗室。

First, excess or deficiency in the sensation itself: as when, for instance, the tactile sensation of movement arises — the body moves, the hands rise, and the feet follow along, yet an onlooker sees the practitioner slumped as though asleep, or as though possessed, the body and hands moving about in disorder, or, while sitting, one sees various strange states — this is the mark of excess. As for deficiency: when the sensation of movement first arises, whether upward or downward, before it has spread through the whole body it gradually dissolves and collapses, so that the state is lost entirely, and while sitting one feels desolate and empty, unable to sustain the body at all — this is the mark of deficiency.

Second, concentration or agitation: as for concentration, when the sensation of movement arises, consciousness and the body become bound by the concentration itself, no longer free, and, through this, one may even fall into a deviant concentration, failing to emerge even after seven days. As for agitation: when the sensation of movement arises, the mind and intention become disturbed and disordered, chasing after one thing after another without ever settling.

Third, emptiness or existence: as for emptiness, when the sensation arises, one no longer sees the body at all, taking this to be the realization of an emptiness-concentration. As for existence: when the sensation arises, one feels the body hard and rigid, just like wood or stone.

Fourth, brightness or darkness: as for brightness, when the sensation arises, one sees various external lights and colors, even the sun, moon, and stars, and lights of blue, yellow, red, and white. As for darkness: when the sensation arises, body and mind grow dim and dark, as though entering a darkened room.

五、憂喜：憂者，觸發之時，其心熱惱，憔悴不悅。喜者，觸發之時，心大慶悅，勇動不能自安。六、苦樂：苦者，觸發之時，身心處處痛惱。樂者，觸發之時，甚大快樂，貪著纏綿。七、善惡：善者，觸發之時，念外散善覺觀，破壞三昧。惡者，觸發之時，即無慚無愧等諸惡心生。八、愚智：愚者，觸發之時，心識愚惑，迷惛顛倒。智者，觸發之時，利使知見心生邪覺，破壞三昧。九、縛脫：縛者，觸發之時，五蓋及諸煩惱覆蔽心識。脫者，觸發之時，謂證空、無相定，得道得果，斷結解脫，生增上慢。十、心強軟：強者，觸發之時，其心剛強，出入不得自在，猶如瓦石，難可迴變，不順善道。軟者，觸發之時，心志軟弱，易可敗壞，猶若軟泥，不堪為器。如是等二十種惡觸擾亂坐心，破壞禪定，令心邪僻，是為邪定發相。

Fifth, sorrow or joy: as for sorrow, when the sensation arises, the mind grows feverish and distressed, haggard and unhappy. As for joy: when the sensation arises, the mind is greatly delighted, stirred and unable to settle.

Sixth, pain or pleasure: as for pain, when the sensation arises, body and mind ache and suffer everywhere. As for pleasure: when the sensation arises, one feels an intense delight, craving and clinging to it without end.

Seventh, wholesome or unwholesome: as for the wholesome, when the sensation arises, the mind thinks of external, scattered wholesome discursive thought, breaking the samādhi apart. As for the unwholesome: when the sensation arises, states of mind such as shamelessness and lack of conscience, and other unwholesome minds, arise at once.

Eighth, foolishness or cleverness: as for foolishness, when the sensation arises, the mind and consciousness grow foolish and confused, deluded, dull, and inverted. As for cleverness: when the sensation arises, sharp discursive views arise in the mind as deviant cleverness, breaking the samādhi apart.

Ninth, bondage or liberation: as for bondage, when the sensation arises, the five hindrances and other afflictions cover over and obscure the mind and consciousness. As for liberation: when the sensation arises, one supposes oneself to have realized the concentration of emptiness or signlessness, to have attained the path and its fruit, severed the fetters, and become liberated — giving rise to overweening pride.

Tenth, a mind too rigid or too soft: as for rigidity, when the sensation arises, the mind becomes hard and unyielding, unable to move freely in or out, just like a tile or a stone, difficult to turn or change, and unresponsive to the wholesome path. As for softness: when the sensation arises, the will of the mind becomes weak and yielding, easily broken down, just like soft mud, unfit to be shaped into any vessel.

Such twenty kinds of unwholesome tactile sensations as these disturb the sitting mind, break dhyāna-concentration apart, and cause the mind to become deviant — this is the mark of a deviant concentration arising.

復次，二十邪法隨有所發，若不別邪偽，心生愛著者，因或失心狂逸，或歌或哭，或笑或啼，或時驚狂漫走，或時得病，或時致死，或時自欲投巖赴火，自絞自害，如是障惱非一。復次，二十種邪中，隨有發一邪法，若與九十六種道鬼神法，一鬼神法相應，而不覺識者，即念彼道，行彼法，於所得法中，鬼神隨念便入。因是證鬼神法門，鬼加其勢力，或發諸深邪定，及智慧辯才，知世吉凶，神通奇異，現希有事，感動眾生，廣行邪化，或大作惡，破人善根，或雖作善，而所行偽雜。

Furthermore: whenever any of these twenty deviant dharmas arises, if one fails to distinguish it as deviant and false, and the mind grows attached and clinging to it, this may cause one to lose one's mind and run wild — singing or wailing, laughing or weeping, at times startled and running madly about, at times falling ill, at times even dying, at times wishing to throw oneself from a cliff or into a fire, or to strangle or otherwise injure oneself. Such hindrances and afflictions as these are not of one kind only.

Furthermore: among these twenty deviant dharmas, whenever any single one of them arises, if it happens to correspond with any one of the spirit-practices of the ninety-six paths, and one fails to recognize this, then, in thinking of that path and practicing that dharma, within whatever is thereby attained, the spirit follows the thought and enters in directly. Through this, one comes to realize a spirit's own dharma-gate, the spirit lending it its own power — bringing forth, at times, deep deviant concentrations, and eloquence and apparent wisdom, knowledge of fortune and misfortune in the world, strange and marvelous spiritual powers, manifesting rare and wondrous feats that move sentient beings, spreading deviant transformation widely; at times committing great evil and destroying others' wholesome roots, or, though outwardly doing good, acting in a manner mixed through with falsehood.

世人無智，但見異人，謂是賢聖，深心信伏。然其內心顛倒，專行鬼法，常以鬼法教人，故信行之者，則破正戒，破正見，破威儀，破淨命。或時噉食糞穢，裸形無恥，不敬三尊、父母、師長；或毀壞經書、形像、塔寺，作諸逆罪，斷滅善根，現平等相；或自讚說所行平等，故於非道無障無礙，毀他修善，云非正道；或說無因無果，或說邪因邪果。如是邪說紛然，壞亂正法，其有聞受之者，邪法染心，既內證邪禪、三昧、智斷、功德種種法門，外則辯才無盡，威風化物，故得名聞、眷屬、供養、禮敬、稱歎等利，是以《九十六種道經》云：人為說法，鬼神加力，則一切聞者無不信受，一切見者咸生愛敬。以有如斯等事故，深心執著，不可迴轉，邪行顛倒，種種非一。若如是者，當知是人遠離聖法，身壞命終，墮三惡道中，是事如《大品經》及《摩訶衍論》中廣說。若欲知鬼神之相，當尋《九十六種道經》，細心比類分別，事則可知。

People of the world, lacking wisdom, seeing only someone extraordinary, take them to be a sage or a noble one, and believe in and submit to them with a deeply devoted mind. Yet inwardly, such a person's mind is inverted, devoted single-mindedly to spirit-practices, and constantly teaching others through spirit-practices — so that those who believe and follow them come to break the correct precepts, break right view, break proper conduct, and break the purity of their livelihood. At times they devour filth and excrement, go about naked and shameless, showing no reverence toward the Triple Gem, their parents, or their teachers; or they destroy sūtra texts, images, stūpas, and temples, committing every manner of grave offense, cutting off wholesome roots entirely, all while displaying an outward appearance of equanimity; or they praise themselves, proclaiming that what they do is itself the true equality, so that in following what is not the path they see no hindrance or obstruction whatsoever, while disparaging others who cultivate what is truly wholesome, calling it not the correct path at all; or they teach that there is no cause and no effect, or teach a deviant cause and a deviant effect.

Such deviant teachings as these run rampant, corrupting and disordering the true Dharma; and whoever hears and accepts them has their mind stained by this deviant dharma — inwardly coming to realize a deviant dhyāna, a deviant samādhi, a deviant wisdom and severance, and various such deviant dharma-gates, while outwardly possessing inexhaustible eloquence, an awe-inspiring manner that sways others; and so such a person gains fame, followers, offerings, veneration, praise, and other such benefits. Hence the Sūtra of the Ninety-Six Paths says: when such a person expounds the teaching, spirits lend their power, so that everyone who hears it cannot help but believe and accept it, and everyone who sees it cannot help but come to love and revere it. Because of such matters as these, one's attachment grows deeply fixed and cannot be turned back, and deviant, inverted conduct proliferates in every manner, not of one kind only. If this is how it is, one should know that such a person has become far removed from the noble Dharma, and, when body and life come to an end, falls into the three lower destinies — this matter is explained in full in the Larger Sūtra and in the Mahāyāna treatise. If one wishes to know the marks of spirits, one should search the Sūtra of the Ninety-Six Paths, and, examining it carefully, distinguish like from like — the matter can then be understood.

問曰：邪法相應行惡之者，現在之過，如是命終，當生三惡道中。其有偽心行善之者，現在之失，云何命終復生何處？答曰：此人身、口行善，雖似佛法，而解心邪僻，若不覺知，障發三乘無漏，雖不會真，於顛倒心中，或時亦能興顯三寶，勸物修善。是人命終未必墮於地獄、畜生、餓鬼之中，而隨所與鬼神相應之法，共彼鬼神同生一處，還為彼眷屬。或時得生人天之中，故《九十六種道經》中說，上有六十餘道，邪倒罪障重故，悉須說呪治之；下有二十餘道，邪惑罪障小輕，直覺知而已。

Question: for someone who corresponds to a deviant dharma and does evil, the fault is plain in this present life, and, when life ends in this way, they will be born into the three lower destinies. But for someone whose mind is false while outwardly doing good, what is the fault in this present life, and where, then, are they reborn when life ends? Answer: such a person's body and speech act wholesomely, and although this resembles the Buddhadharma, their understanding and mind are deviant; if they fail to recognize this, it obstructs the arising of the three vehicles' uncontaminated attainment. Although they never truly encounter the truth, within their own inverted mind they may, at times, still be able to promote and display the Triple Gem, and urge others toward wholesome cultivation. When such a person's life ends, they will not necessarily fall into hell, into the animal realm, or among the hungry ghosts, but instead, following whichever spirit-dharma they corresponded to, will be born together with that same spirit in one place, becoming, in turn, one of its own followers. At times they may even be born among humans or gods. Hence the Sūtra of the Ninety-Six Paths says that, of these paths, more than sixty of the higher kind, being weighed down with deviance and grave offense, all require incantations to treat them; while more than twenty of the lower kind, their deviant confusion and offense being slight, require only recognition alone.

復次，是人雖生天人之中，而冥密常係屬魔邪之道，樂近邪師，樂聞邪法，樂行邪道，供養、親近、稱揚、讚歎修邪行者，見有正學三乘之人，不樂親近，或生惱亂，故《法華經》云：「若魔、若魔子、若魔女、若魔民，若為魔所著者。」《大品經》亦云：「若魔天、若魔人。」故知是人雖生人天之中，猶係屬於魔，常起魔業，乃至雖得出家，猶造魔業。故《涅槃經》云：「佛去世後五百歲中，魔道漸興，魔作比丘，壞亂佛法。」亦如《大集經》中廣辯魔業之相，是中應廣分別。

Furthermore: even though such a person is born among gods or humans, they remain secretly and constantly bound to the way of demons and deviance — delighting in drawing near to deviant teachers, delighting in hearing deviant teachings, delighting in practicing deviant paths, making offerings to, drawing close to, praising, and extolling those who cultivate deviant conduct; while, upon meeting those who rightly study the three vehicles, they take no delight in drawing near, and may even grow disturbed and hostile. Hence the Lotus Sūtra says: "Whether a demon, a demon's son, a demon's daughter, a demon's subject, or one possessed by a demon." The Larger Sūtra likewise says: "Whether a demon among the gods, or a demon among humans." Hence it is known that although such a person is born among humans or gods, they remain bound to demons, constantly giving rise to demonic karma — and even should they obtain ordination, they still create demonic karma. Hence the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "In the five hundred years after the Buddha has passed from the world, the way of demons will gradually flourish; demons will become monks, and corrupt and disorder the Buddhadharma." This, too, is discussed at length in the Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra's own broad treatment of the marks of demonic karma, which should be examined at length there.

如一動觸中邪相如是，餘七觸中亦具有此邪相，應當別知。如根本禪邪相如是，餘十四門禪及諸禪中若事若理，皆有邪偽之法，其事云云，非可具說。問曰：發邪觸時，為當具發如上所說二十邪事，為當不具？答曰：或具或不具無在。若觸發時，但有一邪法，不即除之，便墮邪定，何況具足二十邪法。所以者何？譬如二十人共行，若一是賊，則誤十九人。禪中亦爾，有一惡法，破壞諸善，不名正定，況復多耶？略辯一觸相如是，則餘七觸邪相亦然。復次，更有異禪門邪法入定中，亦應識知，所謂餘禪門境界邪法，如一不淨觀禪入此定中，亦有二十邪法來入此定，餘十四門禪亦當如是一一分別，是中應廣說。行者若脫證此法，須善識知，方便照了，不著邪定之法，即自便謝。

Just as the deviant marks within this single tactile sensation of movement are as described, the remaining seven tactile sensations likewise possess these same deviant marks in full, and should be distinguished individually. And just as the deviant marks within the root dhyānas are as described, the remaining fourteen gates of dhyāna, and every dhyāna's phenomenal conduct and underlying principle alike, all have their own deviant and false dharmas — the matter is manifold, and cannot be described in full.

Question: when a deviant tactile sensation arises, must all twenty deviant matters given above arise together in full, or need they not be complete? Answer: whether complete or incomplete makes no difference either way. If, when the sensation arises, even a single deviant dharma is present and is not removed at once, one falls into a deviant concentration regardless — how much more so if all twenty deviant dharmas are present together? Why is this? It is like twenty people traveling together: if even one among them is a thief, all nineteen others are led astray. Within dhyāna it is exactly the same — a single unwholesome dharma is enough to break every wholesome quality apart, and cannot be called right concentration at all, let alone when many such dharmas are present. This, in brief, distinguishes the marks within a single tactile sensation; the deviant marks within the remaining seven sensations follow the same pattern.

Furthermore: there are, besides this, deviant dharmas from other dhyāna-gates that may enter into this same concentration, and these too should be recognized — that is, the deviant dharmas belonging to the states of other dhyāna-gates. For instance, when the contemplation of impurity itself enters into this same concentration, its own twenty deviant dharmas may likewise enter along with it; and the remaining fourteen gates of dhyāna should each be distinguished individually in the same way — this should be explained at length elsewhere. Should the practitioner happen to realize such a dharma, one must skillfully recognize it, illuminating it through skillful means, and, by not clinging to this deviant, concentrated dharma, it will of itself simply pass away.

Reading note: The ten pairs give the treatise's most granular diagnostic scheme yet — every wholesome-looking mark of concentration has a twin that is either too much or too little of the same thing (light on the body versus rigidity of the body, joy versus agitation, liberation versus overweening pride), which is what makes them genuinely hard to catch: each deviant mark isn't the opposite of a good sign but an excess or shortfall of it. The escalation from personal danger (madness, self-harm) to social danger (a false teacher whose spirit-lent charisma draws devoted followers into the same deviance) to cosmic danger (being reborn bound to that same spirit-realm, echoing the *Lotus Sūtra*'s and *Nirvāṇa Sūtra*'s warnings about demons operating from inside the monastic robe) reads as a warning against exactly the kind of uncritical veneration the treatise elsewhere insists a genuine teacher must be able to withstand.

第二、明正相者，若動觸發時，無向二十惡法，具足十種善法。十種善法者：一、觸相如法；二、定相如法；三、空相如法；四、明相如法；五、喜相如法；六、樂相如法；七、善相如法；八、智相如法；九、解脫相如法；十、心調相如法。云何名如法？若與二十不善相法相違，安隱清淨，調和中適，即是如法，名為正相。是事至後第七大段，明證根本初禪覺支中當分別其相。如一動觸正相如是，餘七觸正相皆類之可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the correct marks: if, when the tactile sensation of movement arises, none of the twenty unwholesome dharmas given above is present, and all ten kinds of wholesome dharma are present in full. The ten kinds of wholesome dharma are: the mark of the sensation itself in accordance with the Dharma; the mark of concentration in accordance with the Dharma; the mark of emptiness in accordance with the Dharma; the mark of brightness in accordance with the Dharma; the mark of joy in accordance with the Dharma; the mark of pleasure in accordance with the Dharma; the mark of wholesomeness in accordance with the Dharma; the mark of wisdom in accordance with the Dharma; the mark of liberation in accordance with the Dharma; and the mark of a well-regulated mind in accordance with the Dharma. What is meant by "in accordance with the Dharma"? Whatever runs contrary to the twenty unwholesome marks given above, at peace and pure, harmonized and moderate — this is precisely what is meant by "in accordance with the Dharma," and is called the correct mark. This matter will be distinguished further below, in the seventh great section, within the clarification of realizing the awakening-factors of the root first dhyāna. Just as the correct marks within this single tactile sensation of movement are as described, the correct marks within the remaining seven sensations may all be understood by the same pattern.

問曰：是中一向但逐事說，若隨此相分別，是邪是正，是偽是真，是應捨是應取，豈非顛倒憶想，墮邪僻耶？答曰：正有二種：一、世間正；二、出世間正。若如世間善法相而說，即是世間正相；出世間解脫善法相而說，即是出世間正相。今明根本觸中十種正相，即是辯世間正也，如《摩訶衍論》云：「因世間正見，得出世正見。若破世間正見，即破出世正見。」是故今欲明出世正法，必須先明世間正法。欲因事顯理，借近明遠故，須先分別根本事中初觸正相。

Question: throughout this, one has spoken only in terms of concrete phenomena — if one distinguishes what is deviant and what is correct, what is false and what is genuine, what should be rejected and what should be taken up, simply by following these marks, is this not itself a kind of inverted, deluded thinking, falling into deviance? Answer: "correct" is of two kinds — worldly correctness, and world-transcending correctness. Speaking in terms of the marks of worldly wholesome dharmas, this is worldly correctness; speaking in terms of the marks of world-transcending, liberating wholesome dharmas, this is world-transcending correctness. What has been clarified here, the ten correct marks within the root tactile sensations, is precisely the distinguishing of worldly correctness. As the Mahāyāna treatise says: "Through worldly right view, world-transcending right view is attained. If worldly right view is destroyed, world-transcending right view is destroyed along with it." Hence, wishing now to clarify the world-transcending correct Dharma, one must first clarify the worldly correct Dharma. Wishing to disclose principle through phenomena, borrowing the near at hand to illuminate the far, one must first distinguish the correct mark of the initial tactile sensation within the root, phenomenal dhyāna.

復次，若觸內發時，或見三十六物，或發慈心因緣，正智念佛等諸餘禪定。若有功德安隱如法，資益觸樂，亦是宿世善根發，各有十種正相，並是正定。但欲修根本禪定成就者，悉不得取是相，若謝、不謝，於根本禪無所妨也。然末世行者善根微薄，證觸之時，多不發諸餘事理。禪中境界，今恐有發者不識故，略出此意。餘十四門禪發正相亦當如是，一一自類廣分別之。

Furthermore: when the tactile sensation arises inwardly, one may also see the thirty-six substances, or bring forth loving-kindness, causation, right wisdom, buddha-recollection, and the other dhyāna-concentrations. If there is genuine merit, peace, and accordance with the Dharma, aiding and enriching the joy of the sensation, this too is the arising of a wholesome root from past lives, each of these likewise having its own ten correct marks, and all of them being right concentration. But for one who wishes to cultivate the root dhyānas to their own completion, none of these marks should be seized upon — whether they pass away or do not pass away makes no difference to the root dhyāna's own progress. In this latter age, however, practitioners' wholesome roots are thin and slight, and, at the moment the tactile sensation is realized, most do not bring forth these other phenomenal or principled states within dhyāna at all; fearing that some who do bring them forth might fail to recognize them, this point is given here in brief. The correct marks arising within the remaining fourteen gates of dhyāna should likewise each be distinguished at length, each according to its own kind.

Reading note: The ten right marks are defined purely negatively — simply the absence of the twenty wrong ones — rather than given independent positive content, which the text itself flags as a limitation: this is only "worldly correctness," a coarse phenomenal check, not yet the "world-transcending correctness" of liberating wisdom. The citation from the Mahāyāna treatise (worldly right view as the very foundation world-transcending right view depends on) is the treatise's justification for spending so much space on something as seemingly mundane as bodily sensations during sitting: getting the near, concrete case right is what makes it possible to trust the same diagnostic instinct when it is later turned on far subtler, more abstract objects.

Dharma-Verification 以法驗知

Chinese Source

第二、明以法驗知邪正者，自有邪禪，其相微細難別，與正禪相似，非則相之所能別，應以三法驗知：一、定心研磨；二、用本法修治；三、智慧破析。如《涅槃經》說：「欲知真金，應三種試之，謂燒、打、磨。」行人亦是，難可別識。若欲別之，亦須三種試之，所謂當與共事；共事不知，當與久處；久處不知，以智慧觀察。今借此意以明禪定邪、正之相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying how to verify the deviant from the correct by means of the dharma: there exist deviant dhyānas whose marks are so subtle and fine, so difficult to distinguish, so closely resembling a genuine dhyāna, that marks alone cannot tell them apart; one should verify them instead by three methods: settling the mind deep in concentration and refining it there; treating it by cultivating the very method through which it arose; and analyzing it through wisdom. As the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "To know true gold, one should test it three ways — by burning, striking, and polishing." The practitioner is exactly the same, difficult to recognize by any single means. If one wishes to distinguish it, one likewise needs three tests: one should share a task with it; if sharing a task does not reveal it, one should dwell with it for a long time; if dwelling with it for a long time does not reveal it, one should observe it through wisdom. Here, this same sense is borrowed to clarify the deviant and correct marks of dhyāna-concentration.

如發一動觸，若邪正未了，應當深入定心，於所發境中不取不捨，但平心定住。若是善根定力踰深，善根踰發，若魔所為，不久自壞。二、以本法修治，如發不淨觀禪，還修不淨觀，隨所修時，境界增明，此則非偽。若以本修治，漸漸壞滅，當知即是邪相。三、以智慧觀察者，觀所發法，推檢根源，不見生處，深知空寂，心不住著，邪當自滅，正當自顯。如燒真金，益其光色；若是偽金，即自黑壞。如此簡別，以三法驗之，邪正可知。定譬於磨，修治喻打，智慧觀察，類以火燒。又復久處喻磨，共事如打，火燒即譬智慧觀察。餘禪定例爾驗之，邪正可知。

For instance, when the tactile sensation of movement arises, if the deviant and the correct are not yet clear, one should enter deeply into the concentrated mind, neither taking up nor rejecting whatever state has arisen, simply settling the mind in equanimity. If it is a genuine wholesome root, the power of concentration will grow ever deeper and the wholesome root ever more manifest; if it is the work of a demon, it will, before long, collapse of itself.

Second, treating it by cultivating the very method through which it arose: for instance, if the contemplation of impurity itself arises, one returns to cultivating the contemplation of impurity — if, as one cultivates it, the state grows ever clearer and brighter, this is not false. But if, through cultivating the very method, it gradually collapses and dissolves instead, one should know that this is precisely a deviant mark.

Third, observing it through wisdom: one observes the dharma that has arisen, tracing and examining it back to its root source, and, finding no place where it truly arises, coming to know deeply that it is empty and still, the mind no longer abiding in or clinging to it — the deviant will then vanish of itself, and the correct will then manifest of itself. It is just like burning true gold: this only increases its own light and color; but if it is false gold, it turns black and crumbles of itself. Distinguishing them in this way, verifying them through these three methods, the deviant and the correct can be known. Settling the mind in concentration is comparable to polishing; treating it through the original method is comparable to striking; and observing it through wisdom is comparable to burning with fire. And again: dwelling with it for a long time is comparable to polishing, sharing a task with it is like striking, and burning with fire is precisely comparable to observing through wisdom. Every other dhyāna-concentration should be verified after this same pattern, and the deviant and the correct can then be known.

Reading note: The double analogy — three tests for a genuine friend (share a task, dwell together, observe through hardship) mapped onto three tests for genuine gold (burn, strike, polish), both then mapped onto the three verification methods themselves — is characteristic of the treatise's method of borrowing everyday similes to secure something otherwise hard to pin down in words. What all three methods share is a refusal to judge from the content of the experience alone: settling into equanimity, returning to the original practice, and analytic wisdom all work by seeing what a state does under pressure over time, not what it looks like in the moment.

Discriminating Demonic Activity 簡是魔非魔

Chinese Source

二、簡是魔非魔，即為二意：一、明是魔相；二、明非魔相。今明魔禪有二種不同：一、明禪非是魔，魔入禪中。如行者於正心中發諸禪定，惡魔恐其道高，為作惱亂，入其禪中，若心貪著，或生憂懼，即魔得其便。若能如上用心却之，魔邪既滅，如雲除日顯，定心明淨。二、明一向魔作禪定誑惑行者，若覺知非真，用法治之，魔退之後，則無復毫釐禪法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, discriminating whether something is demonic or not: this itself has two parts — clarifying the marks of what is genuinely demonic; and clarifying the marks of what is not demonic. Here, two differing kinds of "demonic dhyāna" are clarified.

First, clarifying that the dhyāna itself is not demonic, but a demon has entered into it: as when a practitioner, within a correctly settled mind, brings forth the various dhyāna-concentrations, and an evil demon, fearing that the practitioner's path is growing too exalted, seeks to cause disturbance by entering into that very dhyāna — if the mind then grows attached and clinging, or gives rise to worry and fear, the demon thereby gains its opening. But if one is able to use the mind as described above to repel it, the demonic deviance is dispelled, just as clouds part to reveal the sun, and the concentrated mind grows bright and pure once more.

Second, clarifying dhyāna that is, from the very start, entirely the work of a demon, deceiving and confusing the practitioner: if one recognizes that it is not genuine and treats it by the methods given, then, once the demon has withdrawn, not the slightest trace of any dhyāna-dharma remains at all.

次明非魔相者，罪障於禪，似如魔作，理實非魔，難可別識。若用前所說却之，終不得去，若能勤修懺悔，罪既除滅，則禪定自然分明。復次，或入定時，方便不巧，致令境界不如法，若更善作方便，則所證明淨，故知非魔之所作也。

Next, clarifying what is not, in fact, demonic: a karmic offense obstructing the dhyāna may resemble the work of a demon, while, in actual principle, it is not demonic at all — and this is difficult to distinguish. If one uses the methods given above to repel it, it will, in the end, never depart; but if one is able to diligently cultivate repentance, once the offense is removed and extinguished, the dhyāna-concentration will naturally grow clear of itself. Furthermore: at times, when entering concentration, one's skillful means may simply be unskillful, causing the resulting state to fail to accord with the Dharma; if one instead applies skillful means properly, what is then realized will be clear and pure — hence it should be known that this, too, is not the work of any demon.

Reading note: This closing distinction is a diagnostic safety valve: not every obstruction that looks demonic actually is one, and treating a karmic-offense obstruction with anti-demon methods will simply fail, while treating it with repentance succeeds — the very failure of the "wrong" remedy becomes part of how the two are told apart. Together with the "killer" etymology and persistence-test given earlier for wholesome-root marks, this closes the treatise's first sustained treatment of demonic interference, though the topic returns with its own full chapter as the fifth and final internal-expedient-means layer, recognizing demonic affairs (覺魔事). This closes the third of verifying wholesome capacity's four parts.

第四、次料揀發禪不定，略為五意：一、正料揀事、理兩修發禪不定；二、明發禪所由；三、辨發禪多少；四、明發宿善根盡相；五、約有漏、無漏分別。

Fourth, next distinguishing that dhyāna's arising through this is not fixed: in brief, this has five parts — directly distinguishing that dhyāna's arising through both phenomenal and principle cultivation is not fixed; clarifying the reasons dhyāna arises at all; distinguishing how much dhyāna arises; clarifying whether one's wholesome roots from past lives are exhausted; and distinguishing this in terms of the contaminated and the uncontaminated.

Unpredictable Arising Across the Four Cultivations 正料揀事理兩修發禪不定

Chinese Source

第一、先料揀事理兩修，發禪不定者。問曰：上所明三止，若係緣、制心，此二止並是事止，應但發事中禪定，唯體真一止，既是理止，應發理中禪定，今何故三止通皆發事理諸禪？此則因果渾而無別。答曰：不然。今一家所明事理兩修，悉隨行人根緣，是以發法不同，寧可定有分別？如上所明三止，若略說，則應如所問，合為事、理兩修；若具足分別，應開為四修。就四修中，則有二種：一、約止門明四修；二、約觀門明四修。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, distinguishing beforehand that the arising of dhyāna through both phenomenal and principle cultivation is not fixed. Question: as clarified above, of the three kinds of stopping, fixed-object stopping and restraining-the-mind stopping are both phenomenal stopping, and so should bring forth only phenomenal dhyāna-concentration; embodying-truth stopping alone, being principle stopping, should bring forth only dhyāna-concentration of principle. Why, then, do all three kinds of stopping bring forth both phenomenal and principle dhyānas alike? This would seem to blur cause and effect together with no distinction left between them. Answer: this does not follow. What this school clarifies as phenomenal and principle cultivation follows entirely the practitioner's own capacity and conditions, and so the dharmas brought forth differ accordingly — how could there possibly be any fixed distinction? As clarified above, the three kinds of stopping, if spoken of briefly, would indeed combine as the question suggests into phenomenal and principle cultivation alone; but if distinguished in full, they should instead open into four cultivations. Within these four cultivations, there are two approaches: distinguishing the four cultivations with reference to the gate of stopping; and distinguishing the four cultivations with reference to the gate of contemplation.

第一、約止門明四修者：一、事止，所謂係緣、制心等止，即是事修；二、理止，所謂體真止，即是理修；三、事理止，所謂緣俗體真止，即是事理修；四、非事非理止，所謂息二邊分別止，即是非事非理修。第二、約觀門明四修者：一、事觀，所謂安般、不淨觀等，即是事修；二、理觀，所謂空、無相等觀，即是理修；三、事理觀，所謂雙觀二諦，即是事理修；四、非事非理觀，所謂中道正觀，即是非事非理修。

First, distinguishing the four cultivations with reference to the gate of stopping: phenomenal stopping, namely fixed-object and restraining-the-mind stopping, is precisely phenomenal cultivation; principle stopping, namely embodying-truth stopping, is precisely principle cultivation; phenomenal-and-principle stopping, namely stopping that takes the conventional as its object while still embodying truth, is precisely phenomenal-and-principle cultivation; and neither-phenomenal-nor-principle stopping, namely the stopping that sets to rest the distinguishing of both extremes, is precisely neither-phenomenal-nor-principle cultivation.

Second, distinguishing the four cultivations with reference to the gate of contemplation: phenomenal contemplation, namely mindfulness of the breath, the contemplation of impurity, and the like, is precisely phenomenal cultivation; principle contemplation, namely the contemplations of emptiness, signlessness, and the like, is precisely principle cultivation; phenomenal-and-principle contemplation, namely the paired contemplation of the two truths, is precisely phenomenal-and-principle cultivation; and neither-phenomenal-nor-principle contemplation, namely the correct contemplation of the middle way, is precisely neither-phenomenal-nor-principle cultivation.

今為欲成前止門發禪不定義故，但約四止以明修，一一修中各有四種發禪不定，是故四種修中合有十六種發禪不定。行者善識此相，即自了知事理兩修通發一切諸禪三昧，心無疑惑。云何名為四種止中，一一各有四種發禪不定？

Here, in order to establish the point that dhyāna's arising through the gate of stopping is not fixed, only the four kinds of stopping are used to clarify cultivation: within each of these four cultivations there are, in turn, four kinds of unpredictable arising, so that the four cultivations together give sixteen kinds of unpredictable dhyāna-arising in all. Once the practitioner has properly recognized this pattern, one comes to understand of oneself that phenomenal and principle cultivation alike bring forth every dhyāna-samādhi in common, and the mind is left with no doubt about it. What, then, is meant by saying that each of the four kinds of stopping has, in turn, four kinds of unpredictable arising?

今先料揀第一、事修發禪不定，即有四種不同：一、自有行人安心係緣、制心等事止，還發事中禪定，謂根本四禪、四無量心、四無色定及九想、背捨、勝處、一切處等中諸禪三昧。二、自有行人安心事止，而但發理中禪定，謂空、無相、無作、三十七品、四諦、十二因緣等慧行理中諸禪三昧。三、自有行人安心事止，具發事理禪定，謂根本四禪、四無量心、四無色定、九想、背捨、勝處、一切處等事中諸禪三昧，空、無相、無作、三十七品、四諦、十二因緣等慧行理中諸禪三昧，乃至特勝、通明皆屬事理禪定。四、自有行人安心事止，乃發非事非理禪定，謂自性禪、一切禪等及法華三昧、一行三昧、首楞嚴、師子吼等中道所攝諸禪三昧，乃至十力、無畏、不共之法。

Here, first distinguishing the unpredictable arising of dhyāna through phenomenal cultivation: this itself has four differing kinds. First, some practitioners, settling the mind in phenomenal stopping such as fixed-object stopping or restraining-the-mind stopping, bring forth phenomenal dhyāna-concentration in turn — namely the four root dhyānas, the four immeasurable minds, the four formless attainments, and the dhyāna-samādhis among the nine contemplations, the liberations, the stations of mastery, and the totality-bases. Second, some practitioners, settling the mind in phenomenal stopping, bring forth only dhyāna-concentration of principle — namely the wisdom-conduct dhyāna-samādhis of principle among emptiness, signlessness, wishlessness, the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment, the Four Noble Truths, and the twelve links of dependent origination. Third, some practitioners, settling the mind in phenomenal stopping, bring forth both phenomenal and principle dhyāna-concentration together — namely the phenomenal dhyāna-samādhis among the four root dhyānas, the four immeasurable minds, the four formless attainments, the nine contemplations, the liberations, the stations of mastery, and the totality-bases, together with the wisdom-conduct dhyāna-samādhis of principle among emptiness, signlessness, wishlessness, the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment, the Four Noble Truths, and the twelve links of dependent origination — even the special-excellences dhyāna and the thorough-and-bright contemplation both belong to this phenomenal-and-principle dhyāna-concentration. Fourth, some practitioners, settling the mind in phenomenal stopping, bring forth instead neither-phenomenal-nor-principle dhyāna-concentration — namely the samādhi of self-nature, the bodhisattva's other unshared samādhis, the Lotus Samādhi, the samādhi of a single practice, the Śūraṅgama, the lion's roar, and the other middle-way-embracing dhyāna-samādhis, extending even to the ten powers, the fearlessnesses, and the unshared qualities.

問曰：若修事止，但應發事中禪定，今何得發理及非事理等諸禪三昧不共法耶？答曰：發禪有二種：一者、現前方便修得；二者、發宿世善根。若事修還發事禪者，多是修得。若事修而發理、發非事非理等諸禪三昧者，悉是發宿世禪定善根也。如數人辨有二種修義：一、得修；二、行修。得修，名本所未得。行修，名本已曾得。今此類然，故約事修，則發禪有四種不定。

Question: if one cultivates phenomenal stopping, should this not bring forth only phenomenal dhyāna-concentration? How, then, can it bring forth dhyāna-samādhis and unshared dharmas of principle, or of neither phenomenon nor principle? Answer: the arising of dhyāna is of two kinds — attainment through skillful means cultivated in the present; and the arising of wholesome roots from past lives. If phenomenal cultivation brings forth phenomenal dhyāna in turn, this is, for the most part, present attainment through cultivation. But if phenomenal cultivation brings forth dhyāna-samādhis of principle, or of neither phenomenon nor principle, this is, in every case, the arising of dhyāna-concentration's wholesome roots from past lives. As the Ābhidharma masters distinguish, cultivation has two senses: the cultivation of attainment, and the cultivation of exercise. The cultivation of attainment refers to what was not originally attained; the cultivation of exercise refers to what was already attained before. The present case follows this same pattern — hence, with reference to phenomenal cultivation alone, there are four kinds of unpredictable dhyāna-arising.

復次，今此內方便所明，但辨因止發宿世善根，是故雖說事理諸禪三昧發相，皆略而淺近。若論修習成就，因果相稱，從始至終，諸禪三昧事理廣深之相，並屬第七大段，彼中方復具足分別。

Furthermore: what is clarified here, within the internal expedient means, only distinguishes the arising of wholesome roots from past lives through stopping — hence, although the marks of both phenomenal and principle dhyāna-samādhis arising have been described, all of it has been given only in brief and only at a shallow, introductory level. Should one wish to discuss cultivation brought to its own completion, cause matching effect from beginning to end, and the vast, deep marks of every dhyāna-samādhi's phenomenal conduct and underlying principle, this belongs entirely to the seventh great section, where it will at last be distinguished in full.

Reading note: The question this section opens with is a fair one — if fixed-object and restraining-the-mind stopping are "phenomenal" practices, shouldn't they only ever yield phenomenal results? The treatise's answer turns on a distinction worth holding onto: cultivation determines whether dhyāna arises through present effort or through the resurfacing of a wholesome root from some past life, and only the latter can cross from phenomenal practice to a principle-level or "neither" result. This is the same 習因/報因 distinction from the wholesome-root marks earlier in this fascicle, now generalized into a full account of why practice and outcome so often fail to match — a warning against assuming a student's attainment reveals which technique deserves credit.

The Same Pattern Across the Remaining Three Cultivations 正料揀事理兩修發禪不定 · 理修、事理修、非事非理修

Chinese Source

問曰：若事修乃發非事非理等諸禪三昧不共之法，並由先世習因而得者，則一切隨事而修，皆應得首楞嚴等諸禪三昧不共之法。若爾，何故一切十方諸佛殷勤稱歎般若波羅蜜？若能如聞，行者即具足一切佛法。答曰：此難更成今義。所以者何？若行者過去已經值無量諸佛，從諸佛所聞說般若波羅蜜，如聞而行，則今世隨有所修，一切非事非理諸禪三昧不共之法自然開發，若過去不聞般若，不修般若，今世雖聞雖修，而不能發，何況不聞不修而得發耶？復次，今世雖聞般若，雖修般若，而不得發，後世若值諸佛菩薩，聞說般若波羅蜜，如聞而行，即一切大乘諸禪三昧不共之法悉當開發，故知皆是聞般若，如聞而行能發，非無因緣。是以一切十方諸佛殷勤稱歎般若波羅蜜，若有如聞而行，則能具足一切佛法，正成今義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: if phenomenal cultivation can bring forth unshared dharmas of neither-phenomenal-nor-principle dhyāna-samādhi, all of it obtained through habituation-causes from former lives, then everyone who cultivates according to phenomenal practice ought to attain the Śūraṅgama and every other unshared dhyāna-samādhi as well. If this is so, why do all the buddhas of the ten directions so earnestly praise prajñā-pāramitā, saying that if a practitioner acts exactly as they have heard, they are thereby complete in every buddha-dharma? Answer: this challenge only further establishes the very point being made here. Why is this? If a practitioner has, in the past, already encountered measureless buddhas, heard prajñā-pāramitā expounded by them, and acted exactly as heard, then, in this present life, whatever is cultivated will bring forth every unshared dharma of neither-phenomenal-nor-principle dhyāna-samādhi naturally, of itself. But if one did not hear prajñā in the past and did not cultivate prajñā, then, even hearing it and cultivating it in this present life, it cannot be brought forth — how much less could it be brought forth by neither hearing nor cultivating it at all? Furthermore: even if one hears prajñā and cultivates prajñā in this present life without it being brought forth, should one, in a future life, encounter buddhas and bodhisattvas, hear prajñā-pāramitā expounded, and act exactly as heard, then every unshared Mahāyāna dhyāna-samādhi will thereupon be brought forth in full — hence it is known that, in every case, it is hearing prajñā and acting exactly as heard that is able to bring it forth; this is never without its own cause and condition. This is precisely why all the buddhas of the ten directions so earnestly praise prajñā-pāramitā: if one acts exactly as one has heard, one becomes thereby complete in every buddha-dharma — and this precisely establishes the point made here.

復次，行者以般若波羅蜜方便力故，能於事修之中即具非事非理修，勇猛精進，常修習者，則能發一切非事非理諸禪三昧不共之法。問曰：此與前何別？答曰：是中應作四句料揀：一者、因強而緣弱；二者、因弱而緣強；三者、因緣俱強；四者、因緣俱弱。一、因強而緣弱，能發非事非理諸禪三昧不共之法，如前分別；二、因弱而緣強，能發非事非理諸禪三昧不共之法，即是今之所明；三、因緣俱強，能發非事非理諸禪三昧不共之法者，以前合今，即是其事，此人得法最勝；四、因緣俱弱者，則今世或發或不發，設得發禪，微羸淺薄，亦不牢固，多好退失，像末世中極上行人，只得如此。如上三句所明者，萬中或有一無一。

Furthermore: through the power of skillful means of prajñā-pāramitā, a practitioner is able, within phenomenal cultivation itself, to already be complete in neither-phenomenal-nor-principle cultivation — and, cultivating with courage and diligence, constant and unbroken, is thereby able to bring forth every unshared dharma of neither-phenomenal-nor-principle dhyāna-samādhi. Question: how does this differ from what was said before? Answer: here, a fourfold distinction should be made: the cause strong but the condition weak; the cause weak but the condition strong; cause and condition both strong; and cause and condition both weak. First, the cause strong but the condition weak, able to bring forth unshared dharmas of neither-phenomenal-nor-principle dhyāna-samādhi, is exactly as distinguished before. Second, the cause weak but the condition strong, able to bring forth the same, is precisely what has just been clarified here. Third, cause and condition both strong, able to bring forth the same: this combines the prior case with the present one exactly, and such a person attains the dharma in the most excellent way of all. Fourth, cause and condition both weak: in this present life, it may or may not be brought forth at all, and even if dhyāna is brought forth, it is feeble and shallow, not at all firm, and prone to regress — this is all that even the very best practitioners of this latter age generally attain. Of the three cases given above, one may find perhaps one such person in ten thousand, or perhaps none at all.

第二、次料揀理修發禪不定，亦有四種不同：一、自有行人安心體真理止，還發理中禪定，謂空、無相等一切理中諸禪三昧；二、自有行人安心理止，而但發事中禪定，謂根本禪、背捨一切事中諸禪三昧；三、自有行人安心理止，而具發事理禪定，謂根本禪、背捨、空、無相等一切事理諸禪三昧；四、自有行人安心理止，乃發非事非理禪定，謂自性禪等中道所攝一切諸禪三昧不共之法。廣分別諸禪相及料揀修發之義，類如初句中說。

Second, next distinguishing the unpredictable arising of dhyāna through principle cultivation: this likewise has four differing kinds. First, some practitioners, settling the mind in embodying-truth, principle stopping, bring forth dhyāna-concentration of principle in turn — namely every dhyāna-samādhi of principle among emptiness, signlessness, and the like. Second, some practitioners, settling the mind in principle stopping, bring forth only phenomenal dhyāna-concentration — namely every phenomenal dhyāna-samādhi among the root dhyānas, the liberations, and the rest. Third, some practitioners, settling the mind in principle stopping, bring forth both phenomenal and principle dhyāna-concentration together — namely every phenomenal-and-principle dhyāna-samādhi among the root dhyānas, the liberations, emptiness, signlessness, and the rest. Fourth, some practitioners, settling the mind in principle stopping, bring forth instead neither-phenomenal-nor-principle dhyāna-concentration — namely every unshared dharma of middle-way-embracing dhyāna-samādhi among the samādhi of self-nature and the rest. The full distinguishing of these dhyāna-marks, and of the meaning behind their unpredictable cultivation and arising, follows the same pattern already given in the first case above.

第三、次料揀事理修，發禪不定，亦有四種不同：一、自有行人安心緣俗體真事理止，便還發事理禪定，謂根本禪、背捨、空、無相等一切事理諸禪三昧；二、自有行人安心事理止，而但發事中禪定，謂根本禪、背捨等一切事中諸禪三昧；三、自有行人安心事理止，而但發理中禪定，謂空、無相等一切理中諸禪三昧；四、自有行人安心事理止，乃發非事非理禪定，謂自性禪及中道所攝一切諸禪三昧不共之法。廣分別諸禪相及料揀修發之義，類如初諸句中說。第四、次料揀非事非理修，發禪不定，亦有四種不同：一、自有行人安心息二邊分別非事非理止，還發非事非理禪，謂自性禪等及中道所攝一切諸禪三昧不共之法；二、自有行人安心非事非理止，而但發事中禪定，謂根本禪、八背捨等一切事中諸禪三昧；三、自有行人安心非事非理止，而但發理中禪定，謂空、無相等一切理中諸禪三昧；四、自有行人安心非事非理止，而具發事理禪定，謂根本禪、背捨及空、無相等一切事理諸禪三昧。廣分別諸禪相及料揀修發之義，類如初句中說。

Third, next distinguishing the unpredictable arising of dhyāna through phenomenal-and-principle cultivation: this likewise has four differing kinds. First, some practitioners, settling the mind in phenomenal-and-principle stopping that takes the conventional as its object while still embodying truth, bring forth phenomenal-and-principle dhyāna-concentration in turn. Second, some practitioners, settling the mind in phenomenal-and-principle stopping, bring forth only phenomenal dhyāna-concentration. Third, some practitioners, settling the mind in phenomenal-and-principle stopping, bring forth only dhyāna-concentration of principle. Fourth, some practitioners, settling the mind in phenomenal-and-principle stopping, bring forth instead neither-phenomenal-nor-principle dhyāna-concentration, namely the samādhi of self-nature and every other unshared, middle-way-embracing dhyāna-samādhi. The full distinguishing of these dhyāna-marks, and of the meaning behind their unpredictable cultivation and arising, follows the same pattern already given in the earlier cases above.

Fourth, next distinguishing the unpredictable arising of dhyāna through neither-phenomenal-nor-principle cultivation: this likewise has four differing kinds. First, some practitioners, settling the mind in neither-phenomenal-nor-principle stopping that sets to rest the distinguishing of both extremes, bring forth neither-phenomenal-nor-principle dhyāna in turn, namely the samādhi of self-nature and every other unshared, middle-way-embracing dhyāna-samādhi. Second, some practitioners, settling the mind in neither-phenomenal-nor-principle stopping, bring forth only phenomenal dhyāna-concentration, namely every phenomenal dhyāna-samādhi among the root dhyānas, the eight liberations, and the rest. Third, some practitioners, settling the mind in neither-phenomenal-nor-principle stopping, bring forth only dhyāna-concentration of principle, namely every dhyāna-samādhi of principle among emptiness, signlessness, and the rest. Fourth, some practitioners, settling the mind in neither-phenomenal-nor-principle stopping, bring forth both phenomenal and principle dhyāna-concentration together, namely every phenomenal-and-principle dhyāna-samādhi among the root dhyānas, the liberations, emptiness, signlessness, and the rest. The full distinguishing of these dhyāna-marks, and of the meaning behind their unpredictable cultivation and arising, follows the same pattern already given in the first case above.

今但約止門中四修分別，則有十六種發禪不定。若更就觀門中四修分別，亦有十六種發禪不定。止觀合辨，則有三十二種發禪不定。此三十二但就法行人分別，若約信行人，聞說止觀教門發禪悟道不定，亦有三十二種，其事云云，今不具說。此一往通論，略出六十四種。若具約三乘人根性分別，則有一百九十二種發禪之異。若細歷諸禪，及約邪正辨發相分別，則有無量。今此皆是就行人心地分別，非是虛設之言。當知禪定發法不可思議，乃是諸佛菩薩境界，尚非二乘所量，豈是凡夫之所能測？若行者欲自行化他，必須少分識之，寧可謬自師心，則有自損損他之失，己所修治，為無慧利。

Here, distinguished only with reference to the four cultivations within the gate of stopping, there are sixteen kinds of unpredictable dhyāna-arising. If further distinguished with reference to the four cultivations within the gate of contemplation, there are likewise sixteen kinds of unpredictable dhyāna-arising. Taking stopping and contemplation together, there are thirty-two kinds of unpredictable dhyāna-arising in all. These thirty-two are distinguished only with reference to practitioners of the dharma-practice type; if one considers practitioners of the faith-practice type, whose dhyāna-arising and awakening to the path upon hearing the teaching-gate of stopping-and-contemplation expounded are likewise unpredictable, there are again thirty-two kinds — the matter being similar, it is not given here in full. Taken together in this general way, there are, in brief, sixty-four kinds in all. If distinguished fully according to the capacities of practitioners across the three vehicles, there are one hundred ninety-two differing kinds of dhyāna-arising. And if one traces through every dhyāna in fine detail, distinguishing the arising-marks in terms of the deviant and the correct as well, the number becomes measureless.

All of this, however, is distinguished strictly with reference to the practitioner's own state of mind — these are not idle words spoken without purpose. One should know that the dharma of dhyāna's arising is inconceivable, precisely the domain of buddhas and bodhisattvas, not even something the two lesser vehicles can fathom — how much less could an ordinary person hope to measure it? Should a practitioner wish to both practice and teach others, one must recognize at least some small part of this; to presume, in error, to set oneself up as one's own teacher instead would bring the fault of harming both oneself and others — and whatever cultivation one undertakes would then be without the benefit of true wisdom.

Reading note: The escalating count here — 16, then 32 (adding contemplation to stopping), then 64 (adding the faith-practice type), then 192 (adding the three vehicles), then finally "measureless" — is itself the argument: the treatise is demonstrating, by simply continuing to subdivide, that any attempt to fix a one-to-one mapping between technique and result collapses under its own combinatorics. The distinction between "dharma-practice" and "faith-practice" practitioners (法行人 / 信行人) is a standard Abhidharma pairing — roughly, those who progress chiefly through their own contemplative effort versus those who progress chiefly through trust in the teaching as heard — and the humility of the closing lines is pointed: even the treatise's own elaborate sixteen-and-thirty-two-fold analysis is explicitly offered as only a partial map of something it calls, in the end, inconceivable.

Why Dhyāna Arises 明發禪所由

Chinese Source

第二、明發諸禪三昧所由，自有二解不同：一、有師言：「但修上入定，諸禪自發，不勞餘習。」此師一向併用止法教人事等，舊醫純用乳藥。今不同此。所以然者，止者一法，發法亦應但一，既行者因止發禪不同，何得一向由止？此則非唯於理有失，亦是乖佛教門。二、有師言：「宿世經習諸禪，善根為因，今世修止，得定為緣，是故異發不同。」其義可見。譬如陸地雖有藥草、樹木、叢林種類若干，名色各異，若不同霑一味之雨，豈得有異類生長之殊？此亦如是，若依大乘，闇室、瓶、盆、井中七寶之義，此則別論。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the reasons the various dhyāna-samādhis arise at all: there are, here, two differing explanations. One master says: "One need only cultivate entry into concentration as described above, and every dhyāna will arise of itself, with no need to labor over any further practice." This master relies exclusively on the method of stopping to teach students in every matter, much like an old-fashioned physician who relies solely on milk as medicine. The present treatise does not follow this view. Why is this? Stopping is a single method — if the method that brings something forth is likewise supposed to be only a single thing, then, given that practitioners bring forth such different dhyānas through this same stopping, how could this possibly be attributed to stopping alone? Not only would this be mistaken in principle, it would also run contrary to the Buddha's own teaching-gate.

A second master says: "The various dhyānas already experienced and habituated to in past lives serve as the wholesome-root cause; cultivating stopping in this present life serves as the condition through which concentration is attained — this is why what arises differs from person to person." The meaning of this can be readily seen. It is like dry land: although it holds various kinds of medicinal herbs, trees, and thickets, each with its own name and form, if they were not all equally moistened by the same single rain, how could their different kinds possibly grow and flourish as they do? This is exactly the same. As for the sense given in the Mahāyāna teaching of the seven treasures hidden within a dark room, a vase, a basin, or a well, this is a separate discussion, treated elsewhere.

Reading note: The rejected first view is a real position worth taking seriously before dismissing it: a teacher who genuinely believes one method suffices for everyone, "like an old-fashioned physician who relies solely on milk as medicine." The rain-and-vegetation simile that replaces it is the treatise's cleanest statement yet of the whole fascicle's governing logic — one uniform condition (the rain, i.e. stopping) acting on radically different latent causes (the seeds and roots already present, i.e. each practitioner's own past habituation) to produce different results. Every unpredictable-arising list in this section is really just this one image, spelled out combinatorially.

How Much Dhyāna Arises 辨發禪多少

Chinese Source

第三、明發法多少者，上雖辨因止通發一切諸禪三昧，然行人根性不同，發法不無多少之別。有人但發一種禪門，有發二門、三門、四門、五門，或有一人並發十五門及一切諸禪三昧，不可定判。所以而然，此皆由行者過去習因偏圓不等，厚薄之殊，亦以今世精進、懈怠，有慧方便、無慧方便之別，故發法優劣不同，殊途萬品之異，是則略明發法多少之相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying how much dhyāna arises: although it has been distinguished above that stopping, as a cause, brings forth every dhyāna-samādhi in common, practitioners' own capacities differ, and so what actually arises is not without its own difference in quantity. Some people bring forth only a single gate of dhyāna; others bring forth two gates, three, four, or five; and some single individuals bring forth all fifteen gates and every dhyāna-samādhi together — this cannot be judged as fixed in advance. Why is this so? It is entirely because practitioners' past habituation-causes are unequal in their partiality or completeness, and differ in thickness or thinness, and also because of the difference, in this present life, between diligence and laziness, and between skillful means joined with wisdom and skillful means without it — hence what arises differs in superiority and inferiority, taking countless divergent paths and forms. This, in brief, clarifies the marks of how much dhyāna arises.

Reading note: Two variables compound here rather than one: how much was habituated in past lives (thick or thin, partial or complete), and how much effort and wisdom is brought to this present life's cultivation. The treatise's insistence that this "cannot be judged as fixed in advance" is consistent with everything else in this closing section — a teacher watching one student bring forth all fifteen gates while another brings forth only one has no grounds to conclude anything about either student's present diligence from that difference alone.

Whether Past Wholesome Roots Are Exhausted 明發宿善根盡相

Chinese Source

第四、明因止發禪，有盡、不盡。今就易顯者明，故先約一不淨觀中分別，自有行人宿世已經修得不淨、白骨流光。今於止中但發得不淨，未得白骨流光，此名不盡；若具足發者，名之為盡。若過去所習，勢分已盡，雖復修止，則不增進。若更專心諦觀白骨，練於骨人，研修不已即覺，隨心所觀，境界漸漸開發，成八背捨，觀、練、熏、修悉皆具足。此即是今世善巧精勤修習之所成就，非關過去習因善發。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying that dhyāna brought forth through stopping may or may not be fully exhausted. Here, taking what is most easily shown, this is first distinguished with reference to the contemplation of impurity alone: some practitioners have already, in past lives, cultivated and attained the contemplation of impurity together with the flowing light of the white bones. If, now, within stopping, only the contemplation of impurity is brought forth, without yet attaining the flowing light of the white bones, this is called "not exhausted"; if brought forth in full, this is called "exhausted." If what was habituated in the past has already spent its full force, then, even continuing to cultivate stopping, no further progress will be made. But if one instead applies the mind single-mindedly to contemplating the white bones in earnest, refining the skeletal figure, researching and cultivating it without pause, one will come to a realization: according to whatever the mind contemplates, the state gradually opens and develops further, coming to completion as the eight liberations, with observing, refining, perfuming, and mastering all present in full. This, however, is precisely the accomplishment of skillful, diligent cultivation in this present life, and has nothing to do with the arising of any wholesome root habituated in the past.

問曰：修止境界不進，何必由習因已盡，或由罪障障於宿善故，不得增長開發。答曰：實如來問，此別是一途，不妨自有發盡之者，非關罪障，必須方便，依如觀法修習，乃得成就。是中應歷五種根性人料揀：一、退分人；二、護分人；三、住分人；四、進分人；五、達分人。分別云云，今不具記。餘十四禪發盡、不盡相，類爾可知。

Question: when the state within cultivating stopping fails to progress, need this necessarily be because the habituation-cause has already been exhausted? Might it not instead be because some karmic offense is obstructing one's own past wholesome root, preventing it from growing and developing further? Answer: your question is indeed correct — this is a separate matter, a path of its own, and does not rule out that some cases are, in fact, simply exhausted, with no karmic offense involved at all; in such cases, skillful means are necessary, cultivating in reliance on the contemplative method just as described, before completion can be reached. Here, one should distinguish this by passing through five kinds of practitioner capacity: those prone to regression; those who merely maintain their ground; those who abide steadily; those who progress; and those who penetrate through. The distinctions among these are not recorded here in full. The marks of exhaustion or non-exhaustion for the remaining fourteen dhyānas may be understood by this same pattern.

Reading note: The white-bones case gives this abstract "exhausted or not" distinction a concrete, almost diagnostic test: does further effort in the very same practice keep producing new depth, or has it plateaued? A plateau from an exhausted past cause calls for a genuinely new method (turning the same contemplation of impurity toward the eight liberations); a plateau from karmic obstruction calls for repentance instead, as established earlier in this fascicle's discussion of verifying wholesome roots. The five-fold scale of practitioner capacity (prone to regression, merely maintaining, abiding, progressing, penetrating) is a classical scheme for describing how secure an attainment is, not how deep it is — a reminder that "how far has this student gotten" and "how likely are they to keep it" are two different questions.

Contaminated and Uncontaminated 約有漏無漏分別

Chinese Source

第五、約有漏、無漏分別者。問曰：上明十五門諸禪三昧發相，是中自有有漏、無漏。有漏之善，過去經得，可有習因善發，無漏本未經得，豈有過去習因善發耶？答曰：無漏有二種：一者、行行無漏；二者、慧行無漏。行行無漏既是對治事法，不的據緣真，得有過去習因善發慧行無漏；既的約緣真，不可定論有過去習因善發慧行無漏。復有二種：一者、緣理修習，以明慧行；二者、發慧見理緣真，以明慧行。緣理修習，則有習因善發。發慧見理，雖復緣真，則無習因善發。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, distinguishing this in terms of the contaminated and the uncontaminated. Question: as clarified above, the marks of the fifteen gates of dhyāna-samādhi arising include, among them, both the contaminated and the uncontaminated. Contaminated wholesomeness, having already been experienced and attained in the past, can indeed have a habituation-cause behind its arising; but the uncontaminated was, by definition, never experienced before at all — how, then, could it possibly have a habituation-cause from the past behind its arising? Answer: the uncontaminated is of two kinds — uncontaminated conduct-practice, and uncontaminated wisdom-practice. Uncontaminated conduct-practice, being itself a phenomenal dharma serving as an antidote, does not definitely take truth itself as its object — hence it can have a habituation-cause from the past behind its arising. Uncontaminated wisdom-practice, on the other hand, does definitely take truth itself as its object — hence it cannot definitely be said to have a habituation-cause from the past behind its arising. This, too, is of two further kinds: cultivation that takes principle as its object, clarifying wisdom-practice in this sense; and the arising of wisdom that sees principle, taking truth directly as its object, clarifying wisdom-practice in this other sense. Cultivation that takes principle as its object can indeed have a habituation-cause behind its arising. The arising of wisdom that sees principle, although it, too, takes truth as its object, has no habituation-cause behind its arising.

發慧見真復有二種：一者、發相似慧；二者、發真實慧。發相似慧，或有習因善發。發真實慧，則無習因善發。發真實慧復有二種：一者、發苦忍等見諦無漏；二、發無礙解脫等三界思惟無漏。若發苦忍等見諦無漏，一向不論有習因善發。若發思惟無漏，則教門不定，若類薩婆多解意，退法，斯陀含、阿那含、阿羅漢退還初果中，後更證果，則有習因善發。若不退法，三果人既無重發之義，皆不辨有習因善發。若類曇無德解意所明，四果發真無漏，皆無習因善發。

The arising of wisdom that sees truth is, again, of two kinds: the arising of approximate wisdom; and the arising of true, genuine wisdom. The arising of approximate wisdom may, in some cases, have a habituation-cause behind its arising. The arising of true, genuine wisdom has no habituation-cause behind its arising. The arising of true, genuine wisdom is, again, of two kinds: the arising of uncontaminated attainment on the path of vision, such as the forbearance of suffering; and the arising of uncontaminated attainment on the path of cultivation across the three realms, such as unobstructed liberation. If uncontaminated attainment on the path of vision, such as the forbearance of suffering, arises, it is never, under any account, said to have a habituation-cause behind its arising. If uncontaminated attainment on the path of cultivation arises, the teaching on this point is not fixed: if one follows the Sarvāstivādin interpretation, then, for the retrogressible — once-returners, non-returners, and arhats who regress back to the fruit of stream-entry and later realize the fruit again — there is indeed a habituation-cause behind this arising; but for the non-retrogressible, since those who have attained the three higher fruits have, by definition, no sense of realizing them anew, none of these is distinguished as having a habituation-cause behind its arising. If one follows the interpretation given by the Dharmaguptakas, then the true, uncontaminated attainment arising at all four fruits has, in every case, no habituation-cause behind its arising.

問曰：如阿毘曇分別，但初生無漏，無有自種因，今何得一向四果所發真無漏皆無習因善發？答曰：今明諸禪三昧發習因，義意不同，是中的據過去經得之善，中間退失，今因止更發，以明習因善發。四果發真無漏，悉無先世經得、中間失退因，今修止重發，豈得為類？若通論初品心無漏真解，即為二品心無漏作，種類如是，乃至九品。約此明習因善者，則四果所發無漏，皆名習因善發也。今既不約此明習因善發，故云四果所發皆非習因善根發也。

Question: according to the Abhidharma's own distinction, only the very first arising of an uncontaminated state has no cause of its own kind behind it at all — how, then, can it now be said, without qualification, that the true, uncontaminated attainment arising at all four fruits has no habituation-cause behind it? Answer: what is clarified here regarding dhyāna-samādhis' arising through a habituation-cause carries a different sense altogether: what is meant here specifically refers to wholesomeness already experienced and attained in the past, lost through regression in between, and now, through stopping, brought forth once again — this is what is meant by "arising through a habituation-cause." The true, uncontaminated attainment arising at the four fruits, however, involves no cause of ever having been experienced in a former life and then lost through regression in between — how, then, could this possibly be classed together with dhyāna now brought forth again through cultivating stopping? If one speaks generally of the first grade of the true, uncontaminated understanding of mind serving as the basis for the second grade of uncontaminated mind, and so on in the same way up through the ninth grade — with reference to this sense of a habituation-cause, then the uncontaminated attainment arising at all four fruits could indeed be called an arising through a habituation-cause. But since what is clarified here does not take this sense of a habituation-cause as its reference, it is instead said that what arises at the four fruits is, in every case, not an arising from any habituation-cause wholesome root.

復次，行者過去修習事理諸禪三昧，雖未得證成就，而已經修習今世善根時熟，藉修止為緣，悉皆開發，此亦是習因善根發也。類等佛命「善來」，無漏即發，三明八解一時具足。料揀亦有漏亦無漏，乃至非有漏非無漏，類例可知。

Furthermore: a practitioner who, in past lives, cultivated phenomenal and principle dhyāna-samādhis without yet bringing them to completed realization, and who, in this present life, has that wholesome root come to ripeness, brought forth in full through cultivating stopping as its condition — this, too, is an arising of a wholesome root through a habituation-cause. This is comparable to the Buddha's own summons, "Welcome, monk!," through which the uncontaminated is brought forth at once, the three insights and the eight liberations all complete together in that same instant. The distinguishing of what is both contaminated and uncontaminated together, and, further still, what is neither contaminated nor uncontaminated, may be understood by this same pattern.

Reading note: This closing analysis draws the sharpest possible line under everything the fascicle has been building toward: contaminated attainments can always, in principle, be traced to a habituation-cause resurfacing from some past life, but genuinely uncontaminated wisdom — the direct seeing of truth itself, as opposed to an approximation of it — cannot, because by definition nothing in saṃsāra's past could have produced it. The disagreement between the Sarvāstivādin and Dharmaguptaka interpretations on the retrogressible arhat's "renewed" realization is a genuine scholastic dispute the treatise reports rather than resolves — a reminder that even within the Abhidharma tradition this question did not have a single settled answer. This closes the fifth and final part of distinguishing that dhyāna's arising is not fixed, the fourth of verifying wholesome capacity's four parts, and — since the source text itself defers verifying unwholesome capacity (明驗惡根性) to fascicle 4 — the whole of fascicle 3.
