Fascicle 10 · 釋禪波羅蜜次第法門卷第十

The Non-Destructive Path: Detachment, Mastery, Totality

This page presents Ru-Yi's own English translation of fascicle 10 of Zhiyi's Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen (釋禪波羅蜜次第法門). Having completed the destructive path in fascicle 9, the treatise now turns to the non-destructive path (不壞法道): the eight forms of detachment, the eight stations of mastery, the ten totalities, the nine sequential concentrations, the lion's-frolic samādhi, and the samādhi of transcendence — six dharma-gates organized under a four-part framework of contemplation, refinement, permeation, and cultivation. This is the final fascicle of the treatise as it has survived: Zhiyi's own ten-topic outline, announced in fascicle 1, is never completed in the text that has come down to us, and the work closes here with its own frank acknowledgment that its dharma-gates are without limit.

Ru-Yi Translation

Fascicle Ten
Spoken by the Great Master Zhiyi of Tiantai, of the Sui dynasty
Recorded by his disciple Fashen
Revised and edited by his disciple Guanding
Explaining Dhyāna-Pāramitā's Cultivation and Realization, Topic 7, Part 6

From the eight forms of detachment onward, there are six dharma-gates, all belonging to and included within the non-destructive path. A śrāvaka of sharp faculties, complete with these six dharmas, gives rise to true undefilement and thereby becomes a great-strength arhat of the non-destructive path — hence the Mahāyāna scriptures say: "The arhat of the non-destructive path is able to become complete with the samādhi free of contention, the wisdom of aspiration, and the summit dhyāna."

今分此六種法門，即為四意，謂觀、練、熏、修。一者、背捨及勝處、一切處，此三門並屬觀禪，故《摩訶衍》云：「背捨是初行，勝處是中行，一切處是後行。」悉為對治破根本味禪中無明貪著及淨法愛也。二、九次第定，即是鍊禪；三、師子奮迅三昧，即是熏禪；四、超越三昧，即是修禪。今釋第一、觀禪，即為二意：一、先釋三番修觀禪方法；二、明觀禪功能。第一、釋三番觀行方法者：一、先釋背捨；二、次釋勝處；三、釋一切處也。

These six dharma-gates are now divided into four senses, namely: contemplation, refinement, permeation, and cultivation. First, the eight forms of detachment, together with the eight stations of mastery and the ten totalities — these three gates all belong to contemplative dhyāna; hence the Mahāyāna scriptures say: "Detachment is the initial practice; the stations of mastery are the middle practice; the totalities are the final practice." All three, together, serve as the antidote breaking through the ignorant craving and attachment found within the root dhyānas' own flavor, as well as attachment to the pure dharma itself. Second, the nine sequential concentrations are precisely the refining dhyāna. Third, the lion's-frolic samādhi is precisely the permeating dhyāna. Fourth, the samādhi of transcendence is precisely the cultivating dhyāna.

Explaining the first, contemplative dhyāna, now has two senses: first, explaining in turn the method of cultivating the three tiers of contemplative dhyāna; second, clarifying contemplative dhyāna's own function. First, explaining the method of these three tiers of contemplative practice: first, explaining detachment; second, next explaining the stations of mastery; third, explaining the totalities.

Reading note: The observe/refine/permeate/cultivate (觀練熏修) framework organizing this entire fascicle gives the non-destructive path a metallurgical logic that the destructive path never needed: where the nine contemplations, eight recollections, and ten perceptions worked by breaking the body's mark outright, the six dharma-gates here work like successive stages of refining a single piece of metal — first shaping it (the three observational gates), then repeatedly re-tempering it until every stage flows into the next without a seam (the nine sequential concentrations), then working it through completely in both directions (the lion's-frolic samādhi), and finally handling it with total freedom of movement (the samādhi of transcendence). This is also the treatise's own final fascicle as it has survived — a point this page returns to in its closing status note.

The Eight Forms of Detachment: Naming, Stages, and Method 八背捨 · 釋名 · 次位 · 觀法不同

Chinese Source

先釋八背捨。八背捨者：一、內有色相外觀色，是初背捨；二、內無色相外觀色，是二背捨；三、淨背捨身作證，是三背捨；四、虛空處背捨；五、識處背捨；六、不用處背捨；七、非有想非無想背捨；八、滅受想背捨。今釋背捨，即為五意：一、釋名；二、明次位；三、辯觀法不同；四、明修證；五、分別趣道之相。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining detachment. The eight forms of detachment are: first, form within, contemplating form without — this is the first detachment; second, no form within, contemplating form without — this is the second detachment; third, the pure detachment realized in the body — this is the third detachment; fourth, the detachment of the sphere of space; fifth, the detachment of the sphere of consciousness; sixth, the detachment of the sphere of nothingness; seventh, the detachment of neither-perception-nor-non-perception; eighth, the detachment of the cessation of sensation and perception.

Explaining detachment now has five senses: first, explaining the name; second, clarifying the sequence of stages; third, distinguishing the differing contemplative methods across schools; fourth, clarifying cultivation and realization; fifth, distinguishing the mark of how it leads toward the path.

第一、釋名。此八法門所以通名背捨者，背是淨潔五欲，離是著心，故名背捨。言淨潔五欲者，欲界麁弊色、聲、香、味、觸，貪著是法，沈沒三塗，名為不淨五欲。欲界定、未到地、根本四禪、四空，是中雖生味著，皆名淨潔五欲。今以背捨、無漏對治，破除厭離，不著欲界根本禪定喜樂，故言能背是淨潔五欲，捨是著心，名為背捨。復次，多有人言：「背捨即是解脫之異名。」今用《摩訶衍》意往撿，此義不然。所以者何？如《大品經》云：「菩薩依八背捨，入九次第定。」身證阿那含人雖得九次第定，而不得受具足八解之名。故知因中厭離煩惱，名背捨；後具足觀、鍊、熏、修，發真無漏，三界結盡，爾時背捨轉名解脫。如此說者，義則可依。

First, explaining the name: as for why these eight dharma-gates are, in general, named detachment — "turning one's back on" refers to the pure and unblemished five desires, and "relinquishing" refers to the attached mind; hence this is named detachment. As for the pure and unblemished five desires: the desire realm's own coarse and debased forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and tactile sensations — craving and clinging to dharmas of this kind causes one to sink into the three evil destinies, and these are named the impure five desires. The desire-realm concentration, the not-yet-arrived state, the root four dhyānas, and the four formless states — though a subtle savoring attachment may still arise within these — are, all together, named the pure and unblemished five desires. Here, through detachment, one uses the undefiled as an antidote, breaking through and dissolving this attachment, no longer clinging to the joy and happiness of the desire realm's root dhyānas and concentrations — hence it is said: able to turn one's back on the pure and unblemished five desires, and relinquish the attached mind — this is named detachment.

Furthermore, many people say: "Detachment is itself merely another name for liberation." Examining this now with reference to the sense of the Mahāyāna scriptures, this claim does not hold. Why is this? As the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva, based upon the eight forms of detachment, enters the nine sequential concentrations." The non-returner who has realized this in the body, though he has obtained the nine sequential concentrations, does not yet receive the name of being complete with the eight liberations. Hence one knows: within the cause, wearying of and detaching from affliction is named detachment; only afterward, once complete with contemplation, refinement, permeation, and cultivation, does true undefilement arise, the fetters of the three realms entirely exhausted — at that time, detachment is renamed liberation. Explained in this way, the sense may be relied upon.

第二、明次位者，解釋不同。若依曇無德人所明，初二背捨，位在欲界；三淨背捨，位在色界四禪；第四、五、六、七，此四背捨，位在四空；第八滅受想背捨，位過三界。若依薩婆多人所說，初二背捨，位通欲界、初禪、二禪；第三淨背捨，唯在四禪。彼云：「三禪樂多，又離不淨近故，不立背捨。」下五背捨，明位不異於前。復有師言：「三禪無勝處，四禪無背捨。」此則與前有異。今依《摩訶衍》中說，論言：「初背捨，初禪攝；第二背捨，二禪攝。」當知此二背捨位在初、二禪中，為對治破欲界故，皆言以是不淨心觀外色。第三、淨背捨位在三禪中，故論云：「淨背捨者，緣淨故淨，遍身受樂，故名身作證。」三界之法，若除三禪，更無遍身之樂。論文又言：「是四禪中有一背一捨、四勝處。」如比上進退從容，當知位在三禪、四禪，苟而遍屬，即互乖論。今若具以此義破射於前，及融通教意，甚自紜紜。下五背捨配位不異於前，今依後家之釋，以辯位次也。

Second, clarifying the sequence of stages: the explanations here differ across schools. Following what the Dharmaguptaka masters clarify, the first two detachments are placed at the desire realm; the third, the pure detachment, is placed at the form realm's fourth dhyāna; the fourth through seventh — these four detachments — are placed at the four formless states; and the eighth, the detachment of the cessation of sensation and perception, is placed beyond the three realms entirely. Following what the Sarvāstivādin masters explain, the first two detachments are placed across the desire realm, the first dhyāna, and the second dhyāna; the third, the pure detachment, is placed solely at the fourth dhyāna. They say: "Within the third dhyāna, happiness is too abundant, and, moreover, it lies too close to impurity, hence no detachment is established there." The lower five detachments' own stages are explained no differently from before. There is, further, a master who says: "The third dhyāna has no station of mastery; the fourth dhyāna has no detachment" — this, then, differs from what was given before.

Here, following what is explained in the Mahāyāna treatise, the treatise itself says: "The first detachment is included within the first dhyāna; the second detachment is included within the second dhyāna." One should know, then, that these two detachments are placed within the first and second dhyānas, serving as an antidote breaking through the desire realm — in every case it is said that one uses this impure mind to contemplate external form. The third, the pure detachment, is placed at the third dhyāna; hence the treatise says: "The pure detachment takes purity as its object, and is thereby pure, the whole body receiving happiness — hence it is named realization in the body." Among the dharmas of the three realms, apart from the third dhyāna, there is nowhere else where happiness pervades the whole body. The treatise's own text further says: "Within this fourth dhyāna there is one detachment and four stations of mastery" — weighing this passage back and forth with due care, one should know that its own placement spans the third and fourth dhyānas; to assign it wholesale to one alone would run contrary to the treatise's own text. Were one to fully press this sense against what was given before, and attempt to reconcile every school's own teaching, the matter would grow exceedingly tangled. The lower five detachments' own assigned stages are no different from before; here, the later school's own explanation is followed, in order to distinguish the sequence of stages.

第三、釋觀法不同者，若曇無德人明此八解脫觀，並以空觀而為體；若薩婆多人明此背捨不淨觀，並是有觀，厭背為體。今此八背捨具有事、理兩觀，在因則名背捨，果滿則名解脫，亦名俱解脫也。若偏依前二家所說，此則事、理互有不具，豈得受於俱解脫之名？此中觀行方法與前二家不同，淺深之異，在下自當可見。

Third, explaining the differing contemplative methods across schools: following what the Dharmaguptaka masters clarify, these eight liberations' own contemplation takes emptiness-contemplation entirely as its substance; following what the Sarvāstivādin masters clarify, this detachment's own contemplation of impurity is, in every case, a contemplation of concrete existence, taking weariness and turning-away as its substance. Here, these eight forms of detachment are complete with both the contemplation of concrete phenomena and the contemplation of principle together — within the cause, this is named detachment; once the fruit is complete, this is named liberation, also named liberation-in-both-respects. Should one follow, one-sidedly, either of the two schools given above alone, then phenomena and principle would each, mutually, remain incomplete — how, then, could this receive the name of liberation-in-both-respects? The contemplative method given here differs from that of the two schools above; the distinction in relative depth may be seen for itself below.

Reading note: The treatise's own insistence that "detachment" and "liberation" are not synonyms — the same eight-stage list earning a different name depending on whether the fetters are merely weakened or entirely exhausted — is a recurring habit of this text: taking a term commonly used loosely and re-anchoring it to a precise doctrinal milestone. The cross-school comparison of where each detachment is placed (Dharmaguptaka vs. Sarvāstivādin vs. unnamed other masters) is candid about real disagreement in the tradition, and the treatise's own resolution — that the eight forms of detachment combine both the concrete-phenomena contemplation one school favors and the principle-contemplation the other favors — sets up the "liberation-in-both-respects" (俱解脫) framing that closes fascicle 9's own destructive-path arhat by contrast: the destructive path yields an arhat complete with two kinds of nirvāṇa; the non-destructive path yields one complete in both phenomena and principle.

The First Detachment: The Body Reduced to Bone and Light 初背捨 · 內有色相外觀色

Chinese Source

第四、明修證。行者欲修八背捨無漏觀行，必須精持五篇諸戒，極令清淨。復當精勤勇猛，大誓莊嚴，心無退沒，及能成辦大事。所言初背捨者，不壞內外色，不內外滅色相已，是不淨心觀色，是名初背捨。所以者何？眾生有二分行：愛行、見行。愛多者，著樂，多縛在外結使；見多者，多著身見等諸見，為內結使縛。以是故，愛多者，觀外身不淨；見多者，觀內身不淨敗壞。今明背捨觀行，多先從內起，內觀既成，然後以不淨心觀外。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying cultivation and realization. The practitioner wishing to cultivate the eight forms of detachment's own undefiled contemplative practice must diligently uphold the five divisions of the precepts, bringing them to the utmost purity. One should, further, be diligent and vigorous, adorned with a great vow, the mind never retreating or sinking, and able to bring this great matter to completion.

As for the first detachment: without yet destroying either the internal or external form, and without having extinguished the mark of internal or external form, one uses this very impure mind to contemplate form — this is named the first detachment. Why is this? Sentient beings divide into two kinds of conduct: the conduct of craving, and the conduct of view. Those in whom craving predominates are attached to pleasure, bound for the most part by fetters that are external; those in whom view predominates are, for the most part, attached to the view of a bodily self and every other such view, bound by fetters that are internal. For this reason, one in whom craving predominates contemplates the external body's own impurity, while one in whom view predominates contemplates the internal body's own impurity and decay. Here, in clarifying the contemplative practice of detachment, one generally begins first from within — once the internal contemplation is accomplished, one then uses this impure mind to contemplate what is external.

云何觀內？行者端身正心，諦觀足大拇指，想如大豆脹黑，亦如脚繭之相。於靜心中，觀此相成，即復想脹起如梨豆大，如是乃至見一拇指脚如鷄卵大。次觀二指，三、四、五指亦然。次觀脚法，復見腫脹，乃至脚心、脚踵、脚踝、膝、髀臗，悉見腫脹。次觀右脚亦如是。復當靜心諦想大小便道、腰脊、腹背、胸脇，悉見腫脹。復當靜心諦觀左胛、臂肘、腕掌、五指，悉見腫脹，乃至右胛亦復如是。復當靜心諦觀頸項、頭頷，悉見腫脹。舉身項直，如是從頭至足，從足至頭，循身觀察，但見腫脹，心生厭惡。復當觀壞膿爛，血污不淨，大小便道蟲膿流出。腹既坼破，見諸內臟及三十六物臭爛不淨，心生厭惡，自觀己身甚於死狗，觀外所愛男女之身亦復如是，不可愛樂。廣說如九想，但除散、燒二想為異耳。

How does one contemplate what is within? The practitioner, body upright and mind correct, closely contemplates the big toe, imagining it swollen and blackened like a large bean, or like a callus upon the foot. Within the stilled mind, once this mark is accomplished in contemplation, one then imagines it swelling further, the size of a small pear-shaped bean, and so on, until the single big toe is seen as large as a hen's egg. Next, one contemplates the second toe, and the third, fourth, and fifth toes in just the same way. Next, one contemplates the foot itself, seeing it, too, swollen, all the way through the sole, heel, ankle, knee, and thigh, all seen swollen in turn. Next, one contemplates the right foot in just the same way. One should, further, with a stilled mind, closely imagine the passages of urination and defecation, the waist and spine, the belly, back, chest, and flanks, all seen swollen. One should, further, with a stilled mind, closely contemplate the left shoulder blade, arm, elbow, wrist, palm, and five fingers, all seen swollen, and the right shoulder blade in just the same way. One should, further, with a stilled mind, closely contemplate the neck, throat, and jaw, all seen swollen. The whole body rigid, neck upright — in this way, from the head down to the feet, and from the feet back up to the head, one contemplates the body in sequence, seeing only swelling, the mind giving rise to weariness and revulsion.

One should, further, contemplate its decay and festering, blood and filth impure, worms and pus flowing out from the passages of urination and defecation. The belly already split open, one sees the internal organs and the thirty-six substances themselves, stinking, rotten, and impure, the mind giving rise to weariness and revulsion, contemplating one's own body as worse even than a dead dog — and contemplating in just the same way the body of any man or woman one loves in the outside world, no longer anything at all to love or delight in. This is explained at greater length as in the nine contemplations, differing only in that the perceptions of scattering and burning are omitted here.

行者修此觀時，若欲界煩惱未息，當久住此觀中，令厭心純熟。若離貪愛，是時應當進觀白骨，一心靜定，諦觀眉間，想皮肉裂開，見白骨如爪大，的的分明。次當以心向上，裂開皮肉，即見額骨及髮際骨㘈然而開，即見骨相。復觀頂骨，亦見皮肉脫落，髑髏骨出。復當定心，從頭向下，想皮肉皆隨心漸漸剝落至足。皮肉既脫，但見骨人節節相拄，端坐不動。行者爾時即定心，諦觀此骨從因緣生，依因指骨以拄足骨，依因足骨以拄踝骨，依因踝骨以拄膝骨，依因膝骨以拄髀骨，依因髀骨以拄臗骨，依因臗骨以拄腰骨，依因腰骨以拄脊骨，依因脊骨以拄肋骨。復因脊骨上拄項骨，依因項骨以拄頷骨，依因頷骨以拄牙齒，上有髑髏。復因項骨以拄肩骨，依因肩骨以拄臂骨，依因臂骨以拄腕骨，依因腕骨以拄掌骨，依因掌骨以拄指骨。如是展轉相依，有三百六十骨，一一諦觀，知大知小，知強知軟，共相依假，是中無主無我，何者是身見？出入息但是風氣，亦復非身非我，觀受、觀心乃至觀法，悉知虛誑，無主無我。作此觀已，即破我見，憍慢、五欲亦皆除滅。

Should the practitioner, cultivating this contemplation, find the desire realm's own afflictions not yet stilled, one should abide within this contemplation for a long while, until the mind of weariness grows fully mature. Once free of craving and attachment, one should, at this time, advance to contemplating the white bones. One-pointedly still in concentration, one closely contemplates the space between the eyebrows, imagining the skin and flesh splitting open, seeing the white bone the size of a fingernail, utterly distinct and clear. Next, directing the mind upward, the skin and flesh split further open, and one sees the forehead bone and the bone at the hairline opening apart, and the very mark of bone is seen. One further contemplates the crown bone, likewise seeing the skin and flesh fall away, the skull itself exposed.

One should, further, with the mind in concentration, moving from the head downward, imagine the skin and flesh, following the mind, gradually peeling away all the way down to the feet. The skin and flesh having fallen away, one sees only a figure of bone, joint resting upon joint, sitting upright and unmoving. The practitioner, at that moment, with the mind in concentration, closely contemplates that these bones arise from causes and conditions: depending upon the toe bones, the foot bones rest upon them; depending upon the foot bones, the ankle bones rest upon them; depending upon the ankle bones, the knee bones rest upon them; depending upon the knee bones, the thigh bones rest upon them; depending upon the thigh bones, the hip bones rest upon them; depending upon the hip bones, the waist bones rest upon them; depending upon the waist bones, the spinal bones rest upon them; depending upon the spinal bones, the rib bones rest upon them. The spinal bones, further, rest the neck bones above them; depending upon the neck bones, the jaw bones rest upon them; depending upon the jaw bones, the teeth rest upon them, and above them the skull itself. The neck bones, further, rest the shoulder bones upon them; depending upon the shoulder bones, the arm bones rest upon them; depending upon the arm bones, the wrist bones rest upon them; depending upon the wrist bones, the palm bones rest upon them; depending upon the palm bones, the finger bones rest upon them. Resting upon one another in succession in just this way, there are three hundred and sixty bones in all — contemplating each one closely, knowing it as large or small, as hard or soft, resting together upon one another provisionally — within this, there is no master, no self at all: what, then, could be called a view of a bodily self? The breath entering and leaving is merely the movement of air, likewise neither body nor self; contemplating sensation, contemplating mind, and, further still, contemplating every dharma, one knows them all to be empty and illusory, without master, without self. Having performed this contemplation, one thereby breaks through the view of self, and arrogance and the five desires, too, are altogether extinguished.

爾時復當定心，從頭至足，從足至頭，循身諦觀，深鍊白骨，乃經百千許，遍骨人筋骨既盡，骨色如珂如貝，深觀不已，即見骨上白光煜爚。見是相已，即當諦觀眉間，當觀時亦見白光焴焴，悉來趣心。行者不取光相，但定心眉間。若心恬然，任運自住，善根開發，即於眉間見八色光明旋轉而出，遍照十方，皆悉明淨。八色者，謂地、水、火、風、青、黃、赤、白。普照大地，見地色如黃白淨地，見水色如淵中澄清之水，見火色如無煙薪清淨之火，見風色如無塵清風，見青色如金精山，見黃色如薝蔔華，見赤色如春朝霞，見白色如珂雪，隨是色相，悉有光耀。雖復見色分明，而無形質可得，此色超勝，非世所有。是相發時，行者心定安隱，喜樂無量，不可文載也。

At that time, one should, further, with the mind in concentration, from the head down to the feet, and from the feet back up to the head, contemplate the body closely in sequence, deeply refining the figure of bone, continuing on through some hundred or thousand repetitions, until the figure of bone's own tendons and flesh are entirely gone, the bones' own color like agate, like shell — contemplating still more deeply without pause, one then sees a white light shimmering upon the bones themselves. Having seen this mark, one should closely contemplate the space between the eyebrows; contemplating there, one likewise sees a bright white light, all converging toward the mind. The practitioner does not grasp at this mark of light, but simply keeps the mind in concentration at the space between the eyebrows. Should the mind grow calm and still, resting naturally of its own accord, the wholesome root opens forth, and, at the space between the eyebrows, eight colors of light are seen, spinning forth and radiating, illuminating the ten directions entirely, everywhere bright and pure.

The eight colors are: earth, water, fire, wind, blue-green, yellow, red, and white. Illuminating the great earth everywhere, one sees the color of earth like a pure yellow-white ground; one sees the color of water like clear, still water within a deep pool; one sees the color of fire like a smokeless, pure flame of burning wood; one sees the color of wind like a dustless, pure breeze; one sees the color blue-green like a mountain of refined gold; one sees the color yellow like a campaka blossom; one sees the color red like the morning glow of spring's own dawn; one sees the color white like frost or snow upon agate — following each of these colors' own marks, each, in turn, possesses its own radiance. Though one sees these colors distinctly, they possess no material substance whatsoever to be found — this color transcends and surpasses, nothing of its kind found anywhere in the world. When this mark arises, the practitioner's mind is settled and at peace, joy and happiness beyond measure, impossible to record fully in writing.

行者復當從頭至足，深鍊骨人，還復攝心，諦觀於額，住心緣中。復見八色光明旋轉而出。如是次第定心，觀髮際、頂、兩耳孔、眉骨、眼骨、鼻、口、齒、頷骨、頸項骨。從上至下，三百六十諸骨諸節，悉見八色光明旋轉而出。行者攝心轉細，從頭至足，從足至頭，觀此骨人，悉見遍身放光，普照一切，悉皆明淨。若是菩薩大士，咸於光中見諸佛像；若行人善根劣弱，乃至四禪，方得見諸佛像。行者既光明照耀，定心喜樂，倍上所得，是名證初背捨相。所以者何？內骨人未滅故，故名內有色相，見外八種光明及欲界不淨境故，故言以是不淨心觀外色。外色有二種，如欲界不淨，此是不淨外色。八種清淨之色，是出世間色界之色，故名外色。行者見內外不淨色故，背捨欲界，心不喜樂。見八種淨色故，即知根本初禪無明暗蔽，虛誑不實，境界麁劣，即能棄捨，心不染著，故論言：「背是淨潔五欲，離是著心，故名背捨。」

The practitioner should, further, from the head down to the feet, deeply refine the figure of bone once more, again gathering the mind, closely contemplating the forehead, the mind fixed upon its own object. Once more, eight colors of light are seen, spinning forth. In this way, in due sequence, with the mind in concentration, one contemplates the hairline, the crown, the two ear-openings, the brow bone, the eye sockets, the nose, mouth, teeth, jaw bone, and neck bone. From the top downward, all three hundred and sixty bones and joints are seen, each giving forth the eight colors of light, spinning forth in turn. The practitioner, gathering the mind ever more finely, from the head down to the feet, and from the feet back up to the head, contemplates this figure of bone, seeing the whole body radiating light, illuminating everything, entirely bright and pure. Should this be a bodhisattva-mahāsattva, buddha-images themselves are seen within this very light; should the practitioner's own wholesome root be weak and inferior, it is only upon reaching the fourth dhyāna that buddha-images come to be seen at all. The practitioner, the light now radiant, the mind in concentration joyful and happy, redoubled beyond what was obtained before — this is named realizing the mark of the first detachment.

Why is this? Because the internal figure of bone has not yet been extinguished, this is named "form-mark within"; because one sees the eight kinds of external light together with the desire realm's own impure objects, it is said: using this impure mind, one contemplates form without. External form is of two kinds: impurity such as the desire realm's own — this is impure external form; the eight kinds of pure color are the color belonging to the world-transcending form realm — this, too, is named external form. The practitioner, seeing both internal and external impure form, thereby turns away from the desire realm, the mind no longer taking delight in it. Seeing the eight kinds of pure color, one thereby knows that the root first dhyāna is itself obscured by ignorance, empty and unreal, its own objects coarse and inferior, and is thereby able to relinquish it, the mind no longer stained by attachment to it — hence the treatise says: "Turning one's back on the pure and unblemished five desires, relinquishing the attached mind — this is named detachment."

復次，如《摩訶衍》中說初禪一背捨，當知背捨即是無漏初禪。若是初禪，即便具有五支之義，今當分別。如行者從初不淨觀來，乃至鍊骨人光耀，即是觀禪欲界定相；次攝心眉間，泯然定住，即是觀禪未到地相；八種光明旋轉而出，覺此八色昔所未見，心大驚悟，即是觀禪覺支之相。分別八色，其相各異，非世所有，即是觀支；慶心踊躍，即是喜支；恬憺之法怡悅娛心，即是樂支；雖覩此色，無顛倒想，三昧不動，即是一心支。今略事分別此無漏觀禪五支之相，當知與上根本、特勝、通明中五支條然有異。

Furthermore, as explained in the Mahāyāna scriptures, the first dhyāna's own single detachment — one should know that detachment is itself precisely the undefiled first dhyāna. Being the first dhyāna, it is thereby complete with the sense of the five factors as well, which shall now be distinguished. As the practitioner, proceeding from the initial contemplation of impurity all the way through the figure of bone's own radiance, this is precisely the mark of contemplative dhyāna's own desire-realm concentration; next, gathering the mind at the space between the eyebrows, resting still and undisturbed in concentration, this is precisely the mark of contemplative dhyāna's own not-yet-arrived state; the eight kinds of light spinning forth, perceiving these eight colors never before seen, the mind greatly startled into awakening — this is precisely the mark of contemplative dhyāna's own factor of initial thought. Distinguishing the eight colors, each mark differing from the rest, found nowhere else in the world — this is precisely the factor of sustained reflection; the mind leaping with delight — this is precisely the factor of joy; the calm and tranquil dharma bringing pleasure and delight to the mind — this is precisely the factor of happiness; though beholding this very color, no inverted perception arises, samādhi unmoved — this is precisely the factor of one-pointedness of mind. This, in brief, distinguishes the mark of this undefiled contemplative dhyāna's own five factors; one should know that these differ markedly from the five factors already given above, within the root dhyānas, the special excellences, and the thorough-and-bright contemplation.

Reading note: The first detachment reprises fascicle 9's nine contemplations almost exactly (swelling, decay, festering, all the way through skeletonization) before adding something the charnel-ground meditation never reached: once the body is reduced entirely to bone, the practice keeps going, refining the bone itself until it radiates eight colors of light, mapped onto the four elements and four pure hues. This is the treatise's own clearest statement of the difference between the destructive and non-destructive paths in practice, not just in name — the destructive path stops at bones and ash, while this path treats the skeleton as raw material for something further, building an entire second contemplative structure (concentration, near-attainment, and the five dhyāna factors) on top of the same visceral starting point.

The Second and Third Detachments: Internal Purity, and Happiness Through the Whole Body 二背捨 · 三背捨 · 淨背捨身作證

Chinese Source

二、背捨者，壞內色，滅內色相，不壞外色，不滅外色相，以是不淨心觀外色，是第二背捨。所以者何？行者於初背捨中，骨人放光既遍，今欲入二禪內淨故，壞滅內骨人，取盡欲界見思未斷故，猶觀外白骨不淨之相，故云以是不淨心觀外色。今明修證。行者於初背捨後心中，不受覺觀動亂，諦觀內身骨人虛假不實，內外空疎，專取壞散磨滅之相。如是觀時，漸漸見於骨人腐爛碎壞，猶如塵粉，散滅歸空，不見內色，是時但攝心入定，緣外光明及與不淨，一心緣中，不受觀覺，於後內心豁然明淨，三昧正受與大喜俱發，即見八種光明照從內淨，出明十方，倍勝於上。既證內法大喜光明，即知根本二禪虛誑麁劣，厭背不著，故名背捨，亦名無漏第二禪。是中具有四支，推尋可見。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the second detachment: destroying internal form, extinguishing the mark of internal form, while not destroying external form, nor extinguishing the mark of external form — using this impure mind to contemplate external form: this is the second detachment. Why is this? The practitioner, within the first detachment, the figure of bone already radiating light everywhere, now wishing to enter the second dhyāna's own internal purity, thereby destroys and extinguishes the internal figure of bone; yet, since the views and thoughts belonging to the desire realm are not yet entirely severed, one still contemplates the external mark of white bone's own impurity — hence it is said: using this impure mind, one contemplates external form.

Now, clarifying cultivation and realization: the practitioner, in the mind following the first detachment, no longer accepting initial thought and sustained reflection's own agitation and disturbance, closely contemplates that the internal figure of bone is empty and unreal, both internal and external hollow and sparse, taking up exclusively the mark of its own destruction, scattering, and dissolution. Contemplating in this way, one gradually sees the figure of bone rotting away and crumbling apart, like dust and powder, scattering into nothing and returning to emptiness — the internal form no longer seen at all. At this time, one simply gathers the mind and enters concentration, taking the external light together with impurity as one's object, the mind fixed one-pointedly upon it, no longer accepting the contemplation of initial thought. Afterward, the internal mind opens out, bright and pure, right samādhi arising together with great joy — one then sees the eight kinds of light shining forth from this internal purity, illuminating the ten directions, doubly surpassing what was obtained before. Having realized this internal dharma's own great joy and radiance, one thereby knows that the root second dhyāna is itself empty and illusory, coarse and inferior, and turns away from it in weariness, no longer attached to it — hence this is named detachment, also named the undefiled second dhyāna. Within this, the four factors are complete, and may be found through careful inquiry.

三、淨背捨身作證者，如《摩訶衍》中說：「緣淨故淨，遍身受樂，故名身作證。」所以者何？行者欲入是三背捨，於二背捨後，心即不受，觀外不淨，悉皆壞盡散滅，無有遺餘，亦不受大喜勇動，但攝心諦觀八色光耀之相。取是相已，入深三昧，鍊此八色，極令明淨，住心緣中，即泯然入定。定發之時，與樂俱生，見外八色光明清淨皎潔，猶如妙寶光色，各隨其想，昱晃明照，遍滿諸方，外徹清淨，外色照心，心即明淨，樂漸增長，遍滿身中，舉體怡悅。既證此法，背捨根本，心不樂著。是則略說證淨背捨相，亦名無漏三禪。是中具足五支，深思可見，乃至四禪淨色亦復如是，皆淨背捨所攝，但以無遍身樂為異耳。

Third, the pure detachment realized in the body: as explained in the Mahāyāna scriptures, "Taking purity as its object, one is thereby pure; the whole body receiving happiness — hence this is named realization in the body." Why is this? The practitioner, wishing to enter this third detachment, following the second detachment, the mind no longer accepting the contemplation of external impurity, which has, in every case, already been utterly destroyed, scattered, and dissolved, nothing whatsoever remaining, and no longer accepting the great joy's own excited agitation either — simply gathers the mind and closely contemplates the mark of the eight colors' own radiance. Taking up this mark, one enters a deep samādhi, refining these eight colors until they grow utterly bright and pure, the mind resting fixed upon this very object, and thereupon settles, undisturbed, into concentration.

At the moment concentration arises, happiness is born together with it — one sees the eight external colors of light, pure and radiantly clear, like the color of some wondrous jewel's own light, each following its own mental image, shining and brilliantly illuminating, spreading throughout every direction, its own purity penetrating outward; this external color illuminates the mind, and the mind thereby grows bright and pure, happiness gradually increasing, spreading throughout the whole body, the entire body filled with delight. Having realized this dharma, one turns away from the root dhyāna, the mind no longer taking delight in or attaching to it. This, then, briefly explains the mark of realizing the pure detachment, also named the undefiled third dhyāna. Within this, the five factors are complete, and may be found through deep reflection; and, further still, the fourth dhyāna's own pure color is likewise so, equally included within the pure detachment, differing only in that happiness no longer pervades the whole body there.

問曰：若爾，從初背捨來，悉有淨色，何故今方說為淨背捨耶？答曰：是中應用四義分別：一者、不淨不淨；二者、不淨淨；三者、淨不淨；四者、淨淨不淨。不淨者，如欲界三十六物之身性相已是不淨。不淨觀力，更見此身胮脹、膿爛、青瘀、臭處，此則不淨中更見不淨。不淨淨者，如白骨本是不淨之體，諦心觀之，膏膩既盡，如珂如貝，白光焴爍，此則不淨中淨也。

Question: if this is so, from the very first detachment onward, pure color has already been present — why, then, is it only now that this is spoken of as the pure detachment? Answer: within this, four senses should be applied in distinguishing it: first, the impure-impure; second, the impure-pure; third, the pure-impure; fourth, the pure-pure.

As for the impure: such as the desire realm's own thirty-six substances, whose bodily nature and mark are already impure. Through the power of the contemplation of impurity, one further sees this very body swollen, festering, livid, and foul — this is impurity seen further within impurity itself. As for the impure-pure: such as the white bone, itself originally an impure substance; closely contemplating it, once its grease and residue are entirely gone, it becomes like agate, like shell, its white light shimmering — this is purity found within impurity.

淨中不淨者，從初背捨來，雖有淨光，但此光明有三種不淨因緣：一者、出處不淨，謂從骨人出也；二者、所照不淨，謂照外境也；三者、光體未被鍊故不淨，譬如金不被鍊，滓穢未盡，光色不淨。以是因緣，初禪雖有光明，不名緣淨故淨；二禪雖無白骨，光從內淨而出，猶照外不淨，而未被鍊，及大喜故，亦得名為緣淨。今言淨淨者，八色光明本是淨色，今於此地又離三種不淨，故淨言淨淨，亦名緣淨故淨。既淨義具足，所以說為淨背捨也。

As for the pure-impure: from the very first detachment onward, though pure light is already present, this very light bears three kinds of impure cause and condition: first, its place of origin is impure, since it issues from the figure of bone itself; second, that which it illuminates is impure, since it illuminates external objects; third, the light's own substance has not yet been refined, and is thus impure — like gold not yet refined, its dross and impurity not yet exhausted, its own light and color impure. For causes and conditions such as these, though the first dhyāna already possesses light, it is not yet named taking purity as its object and thereby being pure; the second dhyāna, though free of white bone, its light issuing from internal purity itself, still illuminates external impurity, and has not yet been refined — and, on account of the great joy present there as well, this, too, may only be named taking purity as its object in a qualified sense.

As for what is now spoken of as the pure-pure: the eight colors of light are, in their origin, already pure color; here, at this very stage, they are, further, freed of all three kinds of impurity — hence this purity is spoken of as pure-pure, also named taking purity as its object and thereby being pure. The sense of purity now being entirely complete, this is, for this reason, spoken of as the pure detachment.

Reading note: The four-fold impure/pure analysis (impure-impure, impure-pure, pure-impure, pure-pure) is a good example of this treatise's characteristic move of refusing binary categories in favor of a graded matrix — "purity" turns out to have its own internal impurities (where the light comes from, what it illuminates, whether it has been refined) that must themselves be resolved in sequence before the third detachment can rightfully claim the name. The three grounds for the second detachment's light still counting as merely "pure-impure" — tainted origin, tainted object, un-refined substance — read almost like a chemistry of spiritual attainment, each impurity requiring a specific, separate purification before the next stage becomes available.

The Four Formless Detachments and the Cessation of Sensation and Perception 虛空背捨 · 識處 · 不用處 · 非有想非無想 · 滅受想背捨

Chinese Source

四、虛空背捨者，行者於欲界後，已除自身皮肉不淨之色；初背捨後，已滅內身白骨之色；二背捨後，已却外一切不淨之色，唯有八種淨色至第四禪。此八種色皆依心住，譬如幻色，依幻心住，若心捨色，色即謝滅。一心緣空，與空相應，即入無邊虛空處，此明滅色方便異於前也。證虛空處定，義如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the detachment of the sphere of space: the practitioner, after the desire realm, has already removed the impure color of the skin and flesh of his own body; after the first detachment, has already extinguished the internal body's own color of white bone; after the second detachment, has already set aside every impure external color as well, leaving only the eight kinds of pure color, all the way up through the fourth dhyāna. These eight colors, all alike, rest dependent upon the mind — like an illusory color, dependent upon an illusory mind; should the mind relinquish this color, the color thereupon fades and vanishes. The mind fixed one-pointedly upon space, corresponding with space itself, one thereby enters the boundless sphere of space — this clarifies a skillful means for extinguishing color that differs from what came before. Realizing the concentration of the sphere of space follows the same sense as already explained above.

行者欲入虛空背捨，當先入空定。空定即是背捨之初門，背捨色緣無色故。凡夫人入此定，名為無色；佛弟子入此定，深心一向不迴，是名背捨。云何名深心？善修奢摩他故。云何名一向不迴？於此定中，善修毘婆舍那、空、無相、無作、無願故，能捨根本著心，即不退沒，輪轉生死，故名一向不迴。復次，佛弟子當入無色定時，即有八聖種觀。如癰瘡等四種對治觀故，即能厭背無色之法；無常等四種正觀故，即破無色假、實二倒，能發無漏。八聖種觀行方法，並如前離虛空定，修識定時說。但彼欲離虛空故，方修八聖種，今行人即入虛空定時，即修八聖種，雖住定中，而不著虛空定，故名背捨也。五、識處；六、無所有處；七、非有想非無想處背捨，亦當如是一一分別。

The practitioner, wishing to enter the detachment of the sphere of space, should first enter the concentration on emptiness. The concentration on emptiness is precisely the initial gate of detachment, since detachment takes form as its object and moves toward formlessness. The ordinary person entering this concentration, this is named formlessness; the Buddha's disciple entering this concentration, the deep mind turned wholly in one direction, never turning back — this is named detachment. What is meant by "the deep mind"? Skillfully cultivating calming. What is meant by "turned wholly in one direction, never turning back"? Within this concentration, skillfully cultivating insight, emptiness, signlessness, wishlessness, and no-action, one is thereby able to relinquish the root's own attached mind, and thereupon never again retreats or sinks back, turning through the wheel of birth-and-death — hence this is named turned wholly in one direction, never turning back.

Furthermore, when the Buddha's disciple is about to enter the formless concentration, the eight noble lineages' own contemplation is present as well. Through the four kinds of antidote-contemplation, such as seeing it as a boil or a wound, one is thereby able to grow weary of and turn away from the formless dharma; through the four kinds of correct contemplation, such as impermanence, one thereby breaks through the two inversions of the formless — provisional and real — and is able to give rise to the undefiled. The method of the eight noble lineages' own contemplative practice is entirely as already explained above, at the point of departing from the concentration of space and cultivating the concentration of consciousness. It is only that, there, one wishes to depart from the sphere of space, and so cultivates the eight noble lineages for that purpose; here, the practitioner, at the very moment of entering the concentration of space, already cultivates the eight noble lineages — though abiding within the concentration, one is not attached to the concentration of space itself, hence this is named detachment.

Fifth, the detachment of the sphere of consciousness; sixth, the detachment of the sphere of nothingness; seventh, the detachment of neither-perception-nor-non-perception — these, too, should each in turn be distinguished in just this same way.

八、滅受想背捨者，背滅受、想諸心心數法，是名滅受想背捨。所以者何？諸佛弟子厭患散亂心故，入定休息，似涅槃法，安著身中，故名身證。行者修是滅受想背捨，必須滅非想陰界入及諸心數法。云何滅？是非想中雖無麁煩惱，而具足四陰、二入、三界、十種細心數法，所謂：一、受；二、想；三、行；四、觸；五、思；六、欲；七、解；八、念；九、定；十、慧。云何為受？所謂識受。云何為想？所謂識想。云何為行？所謂法行。云何為觸？所謂意觸。云何為思？所謂法思。云何為欲？謂入出定。云何為解？所謂法解。云何為念？謂念於三昧。云何為定？謂心如法住。云何為慧？謂慧根。慧身及無色愛、無明、掉慢心、不相應諸行等苦集法和合因緣，則有非想。

Eighth, the detachment of the cessation of sensation and perception: turning away from and extinguishing sensation, perception, and every mental factor of the mind — this is named the detachment of the cessation of sensation and perception. Why is this? Every disciple of the Buddha, wearying of the scattered and disordered mind, enters concentration to rest, a dharma resembling nirvāṇa itself, settling securely within the body — hence this is named realization in the body.

The practitioner cultivating this detachment of the cessation of sensation and perception must extinguish the aggregates, elements, and sense-bases belonging to neither-perception-nor-non-perception, together with every mental factor. How does one extinguish them? Within this neither-perception-nor-non-perception, though free of coarse affliction, one is nonetheless complete with four aggregates, two sense-bases, the three realms, and ten kinds of subtle mental factors, namely: first, sensation; second, perception; third, formation; fourth, contact; fifth, intention; sixth, desire; seventh, resolve; eighth, mindfulness; ninth, concentration; tenth, wisdom.

What is sensation? Namely, the sensation belonging to consciousness. What is perception? Namely, the perception belonging to consciousness. What is formation? Namely, the formation belonging to dharmas. What is contact? Namely, mental contact. What is intention? Namely, intention toward dharmas. What is desire? Namely, the wish to enter and leave concentration. What is resolve? Namely, resolve regarding dharmas. What is mindfulness? Namely, mindfulness of samādhi itself. What is concentration? Namely, the mind abiding in accordance with the dharma. What is wisdom? Namely, the faculty of wisdom. The body of wisdom, together with craving for the formless realm, ignorance, the agitated and conceited mind, and the dissociated formations and the rest — through the coming together of causes and conditions belonging to the truth of suffering and its accumulation — neither-perception-nor-non-perception thereby comes to be.

前於非想背捨中，雖知是事，不著非想，故名背捨，而未滅諸心數法。今行者欲入滅受想背捨故，必須不受非想，一心緣真，絕陰界入，則非想陰入界滅，一切諸行因緣悉滅，受滅乃至慧滅，愛、無明等諸煩惱滅，一切心數法滅，一切非心數亦滅，是名不與凡夫共，非是世法。若能如是觀者，是名滅受想。以能觀真之受想，滅非想苦集之受想。今行者欲入滅受想之背捨，復須深知能觀真之受想亦非究竟寂靜，即捨能觀之定受慧想。捨此緣真定、慧二心，故云背滅受想諸心數法。譬如以後聲止前聲，前聲既息，即後聲亦如是能除。受想既息，因此心與滅法相應，滅法持心，寂然無所知覺，故云身證想受滅。此定中既無心識，若欲出入，但聽本要期長短也。

In the earlier detachment of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, though this matter was already known, one was not yet attached to neither-perception-nor-non-perception — hence this, too, was named detachment — but the mental factors themselves had not yet been extinguished. Now, the practitioner wishing to enter the detachment of the cessation of sensation and perception must no longer accept neither-perception-nor-non-perception at all, the mind fixed one-pointedly upon reality itself, cutting off the aggregates, elements, and sense-bases entirely — the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements belonging to neither-perception-nor-non-perception are thereby extinguished, every conditioned formation's own causes and conditions entirely extinguished, sensation extinguished, and, further still, wisdom itself extinguished, craving, ignorance, and every other affliction extinguished, every mental factor extinguished, and every non-mental factor, too, extinguished — this is named "not shared in common with the ordinary person," not a worldly dharma at all. Should one be able to contemplate in this way, this is named the cessation of sensation and perception, using the sensation and perception that are able to contemplate reality itself to extinguish the sensation and perception belonging to the suffering and accumulation of neither-perception-nor-non-perception.

Now, the practitioner wishing to enter this very detachment of the cessation of sensation and perception must, further, deeply know that even the sensation and perception able to contemplate reality are not themselves the ultimate stillness, and must thereupon relinquish the very concentration-sensation and wisdom-perception that are able to contemplate. Relinquishing these two minds of concentration and wisdom that take reality as their object — hence it is said: turning away from and extinguishing sensation, perception, and every mental factor. It is like using a later sound to still an earlier sound: the earlier sound having already ceased, the later sound, too, is able to be removed in just the same way. Sensation and perception having already ceased, the mind, on account of this, corresponds with the dharma of cessation itself; the dharma of cessation holds the mind fast, still and silent, without any awareness or perception at all — hence it is said: realization in the body of the cessation of perception and sensation. Within this very concentration, there being no consciousness at all, should one wish to leave it, one simply heeds the original interval of time set beforehand, whether long or short.

Reading note: The eighth detachment's own ten subtle mental factors are a striking piece of phenomenological bookkeeping — even the state traditionally described as "neither perceiving nor not perceiving" is shown to still harbor sensation, perception, formation, contact, intention, desire, resolve, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom, all operating at a level too fine for the ordinary person to detect. The final move — relinquishing even the concentration and wisdom that were doing the contemplating — is the same self-undermining logic that closed the formless attainments in fascicle 8: no observing position is exempt from being observed and then released in turn, all the way down.

How the Eight Forms of Detachment Lead Toward the Path 八背捨 · 趣道

Chinese Source

第五、分別趣道之相。行者修八背捨入道，有三種不同：一者、先用背捨破遮道法，後則具足修習勝處，乃至超越三昧，事、理二觀具足，方發真無漏，證三乘道。二者、若修八背捨時，是人厭離生死，欲速得解脫，是時遍修緣諦真觀等，即於八背捨中發真無漏，證三乘道，亦名具足八解脫也。當知此人未必具下五種法門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, distinguishing the mark of how detachment leads toward the path. The practitioner cultivating the eight forms of detachment enters the path in three differing ways.

First, one first uses detachment to break through the dharmas obstructing the path, and only afterward becomes complete in cultivating the stations of mastery, and, further still, the samādhi of transcendence — the two contemplations of concrete phenomena and of principle both complete, only then does true undefilement arise, and the path of the three vehicles is realized.

Second, should one, while cultivating the eight forms of detachment, already be so wearied by and detached from birth-and-death that one wishes to swiftly obtain liberation, at that time one broadly cultivates the true contemplation taking the truths as object and the rest, and true undefilement thereby arises within the eight forms of detachment themselves, the path of the three vehicles realized, also named being complete with the eight liberations. One should know that this person need not necessarily be complete with the remaining five dharma-gates below.

問曰：若爾，此人未得九次第定，云何已得受八解脫之名？答曰：是義應作四句分別：一者自有九次第定非解脫，自有是解脫非次第定，自有次第定亦是解脫，自有非次第定非解脫，而是八背捨。三者、若人厭離生死心重，但證初背捨時，即深觀四諦真定之理，無漏若發，便於此地入金剛三昧，證三乘道，當知是人亦復未必具上七種背捨。菩薩摩訶薩心如虛空，無所取捨，以方便力，善修背捨，具足成就一切佛法，度脫眾生。當知背捨即是菩薩摩訶衍。

Question: if this is so, this person has not yet obtained the nine sequential concentrations — how, then, does he already receive the name of being complete with the eight liberations? Answer: this sense should be distinguished through four propositions: first, there is the case of one who possesses the nine sequential concentrations but not liberation; second, there is the case of one who possesses liberation but not the sequential concentrations; third, there is the case of one who possesses both the sequential concentrations and liberation together; and fourth, there is the case of one who possesses neither the sequential concentrations nor liberation, and yet still possesses the eight forms of detachment.

Third, should a person's mind be so heavy with weariness toward and detachment from birth-and-death that, upon realizing only the first detachment, he already deeply contemplates the truth of the Four Noble Truths' own real concentration, and, should the undefiled arise, thereupon enters the vajra samādhi at this very stage and realizes the path of the three vehicles — one should know that this person, too, need not necessarily be complete with the seven remaining forms of detachment above.

The bodhisattva-mahāsattva, the mind like empty space, grasping and relinquishing nothing at all, through the power of skillful means, skillfully cultivates detachment, becoming complete with the accomplishment of the whole Buddhadharma, and delivers sentient beings across. One should know, then, that detachment is itself precisely the bodhisattva's own Mahāyāna.

Reading note: The four-proposition breakdown answering "how can someone be called liberated without the nine sequential concentrations?" is a clean piece of logical housekeeping — the treatise is careful to specify that its own eight-stage practice ladder describes a typical, not a mandatory, route, and that swift, partial paths to realization (even from the very first detachment alone) are explicitly acknowledged as valid. This closes the eight forms of detachment exactly as fascicle 9 closed the nine contemplations, eight recollections, and ten perceptions before it: with the same formula naming the practice "the bodhisattva's own Mahāyāna."

The Eight Stations of Mastery: Naming, Stages, and Mastering a Small Object 八勝處 · 釋名 · 階位 · 勝知見一二

Chinese Source

次釋八勝處法門。八勝處者：一、內有色相，外觀色少，若好若醜，是名勝知勝見一勝處也；二、內有色相，外觀色多，若好若醜，是名勝知見二勝處也；三、內無色相，外觀色少，若好若醜，是名勝知見三勝處也；四、內無色相，外觀色多，若好若醜，是名勝知見四勝處也；五、青勝處；六、黃勝處；七、赤勝處；八、白勝處。若依《纓絡經》，用四大為四勝處。今明勝處，即為四意：一者、釋名；二、明階位；三、辯修證之相；四、明趣道。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the dharma-gate of the eight stations of mastery. The eight stations of mastery are: first, form within, contemplating little form without, whether good or ill — this is named the first station of mastery, of superior knowledge and vision; second, form within, contemplating much form without, whether good or ill — this is named the second station of mastery, of superior knowledge and vision; third, no form within, contemplating little form without, whether good or ill — this is named the third station of mastery, of superior knowledge and vision; fourth, no form within, contemplating much form without, whether good or ill — this is named the fourth station of mastery, of superior knowledge and vision; fifth, the station of mastery of blue-green; sixth, the station of mastery of yellow; seventh, the station of mastery of red; eighth, the station of mastery of white. Following the Sūtra of the Original Acts That Adorn a Bodhisattva, the four elements are taken as the four stations of mastery.

Explaining the stations of mastery now has four senses: first, explaining the name; second, clarifying their stages; third, distinguishing the mark of their cultivation and realization; fourth, clarifying how they lead toward the path.

第一、釋名。此八法通明勝處者，則有二義：一者、若淨若不淨五欲，得此觀時，隨意能破，故名勝處；二者、善調觀心，譬如乘馬擊賊，非但破前陣，亦能善制其馬，故名勝處。此則有異背捨。經亦說為八除入。若因勝處斷煩惱盡，則知虛妄陰入皆滅，爾時勝處變名八除入也。第二、明次位者，今但依《摩訶衍》中說，初二勝處，位在初禪；次第三、第四勝處，位在二禪；後四勝處，位在四禪。所以三禪不立勝處者，以樂多心鈍故。前二禪離欲界近，欲界煩惱難破，雖位居二禪，猶觀不淨，破下地結。四禪既是色中之極，故色勝位極於此。四空既無色故，亦以破地煩惱薄故，故不立勝處。

First, explaining the name: as for why these eight dharmas are, in general, called stations of mastery — this has two senses. First, whether the five desires be pure or impure, once one obtains this contemplation, one is thereby able to break through them at will — hence this is named a station of mastery. Second, one skillfully regulates the contemplating mind — like a rider on horseback striking at a bandit, not only breaking through the enemy's own line, but also skillfully mastering the horse itself — hence this, too, is named a station of mastery. This, then, differs from detachment. The sutras also speak of this as the eight bases of transcendence. Should one, through the stations of mastery, sever affliction entirely, one thereby knows that the illusory aggregates and sense-bases are all extinguished — at that time, the stations of mastery are renamed the eight bases of transcendence.

Second, clarifying their stages: here, following only what is explained in the Mahāyāna scriptures, the first two stations of mastery are placed at the first dhyāna; the third and fourth stations of mastery, next, are placed at the second dhyāna; and the last four stations of mastery are placed at the fourth dhyāna. As for why no station of mastery is established at the third dhyāna: because happiness there is too abundant and the mind too dull. The first two dhyānas lie close to the desire realm, whose afflictions are difficult to break through — hence, though placed within the second dhyāna, one still contemplates impurity there, breaking through the lower stage's own fetters. The fourth dhyāna, being the very utmost of the form realm, is thus where form's own mastery reaches its own limit. The four formless states, already lacking form entirely, and their own afflictions already thin, have, for this reason, no station of mastery established within them either.

第三、明修證。所以言「內有色相，外觀色少」者，緣少故名少。觀道未增故，觀少因緣，觀多畏難攝故。譬如鹿遊未調，則不中遠放。云何名觀少？行者自觀見己身不淨，亦觀所愛之人不淨、脹爛、白骨，心甚厭惡，如初背捨中說。「若好若醜」者，觀外諸色，善業果報故名好，惡業報故名醜。復次，行者從師所受觀法，觀外緣種種不淨，是名醜色。行者或時憶念，妄生淨想，觀淨色，是好色。復次，行者自身中繫心一處，觀欲界中色有二種：一者、能生婬欲；二者、能生瞋恚。能生婬欲是淨色，名為好；能生瞋恚者是不淨色，故名醜。

Third, clarifying cultivation and realization: as for why it is said "form within, contemplating little form without" — since the object taken is small, this is named little. Because the contemplative path has not yet increased in strength, one contemplates a small object as the condition; contemplating a large object is feared too difficult to master. It is like a deer not yet tamed, which should not, then, be let loose to roam too far.

What is meant by "contemplating little"? The practitioner contemplates his own body's own impurity, and also contemplates the impurity, swelling, decay, and white bones of the person he loves, the mind deeply wearied and revolted — as already explained within the first detachment. As for "whether good or ill": contemplating external forms in general, that born of wholesome karmic fruit is named good, and that born of unwholesome karmic retribution is named ill. Furthermore, the practitioner, receiving this contemplative method from a teacher, contemplates every kind of external impurity as its object — this is named ill form. Should the practitioner, at times, call something falsely to mind, giving rise to a mistaken perception of purity, and contemplate a pure form — this is good form. Furthermore, the practitioner, fixing the mind upon a single point within his own body, contemplates that form within the desire realm is of two kinds: first, that able to give rise to lustful desire; second, that able to give rise to anger and hatred. That able to give rise to lustful desire is pure form, named good; that able to give rise to anger and hatred is impure form, hence named ill.

「勝知勝見」者，觀心淳熟，於好色中，心不貪愛，於醜色中，心不瞋恚，但觀色四大因緣和合而生，如水沫不堅固，智慧深達假實之相。行者住是不淨門中，婬欲、瞋恚諸結使來能不隨，故名勝處。勝是不淨中淨，顛倒諸煩惱故。復次，好醜者，不淨觀有二種：一者、見自身、他身三十六物臭穢不淨，是名醜；二者、除內外皮肉五臟，但觀白骨如珂如雪，乃至流光照耀，是名為好。行者見不淨時，即知虛假，心不畏沒；見淨色時，知從緣生，心不愛染，是名勝知勝見。復次，行者於少緣中隨意觀色，轉變自在，亦能善制觀心，故名勝處。

As for "superior knowledge, superior vision": the contemplating mind having grown mature, within good form the mind no longer craves or loves it, and within ill form the mind no longer grows angry or hateful toward it, but simply contemplates that form arises from the union of the four elements' own causes and conditions, like foam upon water, without any solidity at all, wisdom penetrating deeply into the mark of the provisional and the real. The practitioner, abiding within this very gate of impurity, is no longer swept along by the fetters of lustful desire and anger and hatred as they arise — hence this is named a station of mastery: mastering the purity found within impurity, and every inverted affliction as well.

Furthermore, "good" and "ill" themselves admit of two kinds of contemplation of impurity: first, seeing one's own body and another's body's own thirty-six substances, stinking and filthy, impure — this is named ill; second, removing the internal and external skin, flesh, and five organs, contemplating only the white bone, like agate, like snow, and, further still, its flowing light radiating forth — this is named good. The practitioner, seeing impurity, thereby knows it to be empty and illusory, the mind no longer fearful and overwhelmed by it; seeing pure form, knows it to arise from conditions, the mind no longer stained by love for it — this is named superior knowledge, superior vision. Furthermore, the practitioner, within a small object, contemplating form at will, transforming it freely, is also thereby able to skillfully master the contemplating mind itself — hence this is named a station of mastery.

Reading note: The stations of mastery share the eight forms of detachment's own imagery but add a distinct new element the treatise flags explicitly: not just breaking attachment, but mastering the contemplating mind itself, "like a rider on horseback striking at a bandit" who must control the horse as well as defeat the enemy. The deer-not-yet-tamed simile for why beginners are restricted to "a small object" is a rare moment of practical pedagogical caution in a text that otherwise moves through extraordinarily intense visualizations without much comment on pacing or risk.

Mastering the Whole World: Second Station of Mastery 二勝處 · 內有色相外觀色多

Chinese Source

二、內有色相，外觀色多，若好若醜，是名勝知勝見者。行者觀心既調，爾時不滅內骨人，更於定中廣觀外色，所謂諦觀一死屍乃至十百千萬，一國土乃至十百千萬國，一閻浮提乃至一四天下等，皆見悉是死屍。若觀一胮脹時，悉見一切胮脹，乃至壞、血污、膿爛、青瘀、剝落亦如是。行者既廣見死屍不淨，心甚厭惡，次當諦觀一死屍脫除皮骨，但見白骨，如是乃至一切死屍悉除皮肉，皆見白骨遍滿世界。此觀如《禪經》廣明，是中應具說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, "form within, contemplating much form without, whether good or ill — this is named superior knowledge, superior vision": the practitioner's own contemplating mind now regulated, at this time one no longer extinguishes the internal figure of bone, but, further, within concentration, broadly contemplates external form — namely, closely contemplating a single corpse, and then ten, a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand; a single land, and then ten, a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand lands; a single Jambudvīpa, and then a single four-continent world-system — all seen, entirely, as corpses. Contemplating a single body swollen, one sees every body swollen at once; and so, too, with decay, blood-smearing, festering, livid discoloration, and skeletonization, each in turn.

The practitioner, having broadly seen the corpses' own impurity, the mind deeply wearied and revolted, next closely contemplates a single corpse stripped of its skin and bone, seeing only white bone — and so, too, all the way through every corpse, each stripped of skin and flesh, until white bone is seen filling the whole world. This contemplation is explained at length in the Dhyāna Sūtra, and should be given there in full.

行者外骨觀既成，復當定心諦觀內身白骨，鍊使明淨，如珂如貝。當自觀骨時，見外一切骨人悉皆起立，行行相對羅列，舉手而來。行者於三昧中即知此諸骨人皆是隨想而來，無有定實，心不驚怖。復當心默念，訶此骨人：「咄！汝諸骨人，從何而來！」如是訶時，悉見骨人悉還躄地，如是或至多反。行者深觀內骨，即見光明普照十方，一切骨人為光所照，悉亦明淨。此觀成時，於一切怨、親、中人及諸好醜，其心平等，無有愛恚，是名若好若醜勝知勝見。好醜勝知見義如前說。

The practitioner, the contemplation of external bone now accomplished, should, further, with the mind in concentration, closely contemplate the internal body's own white bone, refining it to bright purity, like agate, like shell. Contemplating his own bones in this way, he sees every external figure of bone rise up together, arrayed in rows facing one another, raising their hands and approaching. The practitioner, within samādhi, thereby knows that all these figures of bone arise entirely in accordance with his own thought, possessing no fixed reality at all, the mind unafraid and untroubled. He should, further, silently reflect, reproaching these figures of bone: "Ha! You figures of bone — from where have you come?" Reproaching them in this way, he sees every figure of bone collapse back to the ground at once — this may happen many times over.

The practitioner, deeply contemplating the internal bone, thereupon sees light illuminating the ten directions everywhere, every figure of bone illuminated by this light, all alike bright and pure. Once this contemplation is accomplished, toward every enemy, every loved one, and every neutral person, and every mark of good and ill alike, the mind is equal throughout, free of both love and hatred — this is named "whether good or ill, superior knowledge, superior vision." The sense of superior knowledge and vision regarding good and ill is as already explained above.

復次，行者住此觀中，能見一骨人，遍四天下皆是骨人，是名為多。還復攝念，觀一骨人，故名勝知勝見。隨意五欲男女、淨潔相中能勝故，故名勝處。又能善調觀心，雖知能觀之心性無所有，而於緣中自在迴轉，觀諸境界無有障閡，故名勝處。有義如《摩訶衍》中廣說。復次，有師言：「若但觀一切人，見不淨白骨，是名少；若作大不淨觀，是名多。」大不淨觀者，為破一切處貪愛故。何謂一切觀？觀象、馬、牛、羊六畜飛禽走獸之屬，悉見為死屍胮脹。復次，觀飲食皆如蟲如糞，衣服絹布猶如爛皮爛肉之段，臭處可惡。錢財金寶如毒蛇蚖，斯須死變，臭爛不淨，穀米如臭死蟲，宅舍、田園、國土、城邑、大地、山川、林藪，皆悉爛壞，臭處不淨，流溢滂沱，乃至見白骨狼藉，一切世間不淨，如此甚可厭患。行者於三昧中，隨觀即見，迴轉自在，能破一切世間好醜、愛憎、貪憂煩惱，故名「內有色相，外觀色多，若好若醜，勝知勝見」。

Furthermore, the practitioner, abiding within this contemplation, is able to see a single figure of bone, and then see the whole four-continent world filled entirely with figures of bone — this is named "much." Gathering the mind once more, contemplating a single figure of bone again — hence this, too, is named superior knowledge, superior vision: able to master, at will, the five desires, whether male or female, whether pure or unblemished in mark — hence this is named a station of mastery. And, further, one is thereby able to skillfully master the contemplating mind itself — though knowing that the very nature of the mind able to contemplate possesses nothing at all, one nonetheless turns freely within its own object, contemplating every realm without any obstruction whatsoever — hence this, too, is named a station of mastery. This sense is explained at greater length in the Mahāyāna scriptures.

Furthermore, there is a master who says: "Should one merely contemplate every person, seeing the impure white bone alone, this is named little; should one perform the great contemplation of impurity, this is named much." The great contemplation of impurity is for the sake of breaking through craving and attachment toward every possible object. What is meant by contemplating everything? Contemplating elephants, horses, oxen, sheep, and every kind of the six domestic animals, and every bird and beast, all seen as corpses, swollen. Furthermore, contemplating food and drink as though it were worms or excrement, and clothing, silk, and cloth as though they were strips of rotten skin and rotten flesh, foul and abhorrent. Money, wealth, gold, and jewels seen as poisonous snakes and vipers, transforming in an instant into death, stinking and rotten, impure; grain and rice seen as stinking, dead worms; houses, fields and gardens, one's own land, cities and towns, the great earth, mountains, rivers, and forests, all, alike, rotting and decaying, foul and impure, overflowing and pouring forth — and, further still, seeing white bones scattered everywhere, the whole world impure — a matter, in this way, utterly to be wearied of and abhorred. The practitioner, within samādhi, sees whatever he contemplates at will, turning freely, able thereby to break through every worldly good and ill, love and hatred, craving and sorrowful affliction — hence this is named "form within, contemplating much form without, whether good or ill, superior knowledge, superior vision."

問：世間資生既不淨，悉是皮肉筋骨之法，云何悉見不淨爛壞？答曰：此為得解之道，心力轉變，非實觀也。所以者何？一切非實是淨，淨倒力故，遂悉見淨而生貪愛。一切雖非悉不淨，今不淨觀智慧力皆見不淨，破諸煩惱，復有何過？譬如劫燒火起，一切大地有情、無情，若干種類，皆成火焰，以火力故。今以不淨心觀一切世間，悉見不淨，亦如神通之人轉瓦石為金玉，當知諸法有何定性？彼師如是明。第二勝處，深思此意，義理觀行，悉可依用也。

Question: since the world's own resources for living are, in every case, dharmas of skin, flesh, tendon, and bone, and thus already impure, how can they all be seen as impure and decaying? Answer: this is a path toward attaining understanding, the mind's own power transforming appearances — it is not a contemplation of what is literally real. Why is this? Nothing is, in reality, entirely pure; through the power of the inverted view of purity, one comes to see everything as pure and thereby gives rise to craving and attachment toward it. Though nothing is, in reality, entirely impure either, here, through the power of the contemplation of impurity's own wisdom, everything is seen as impure, breaking through every affliction — what fault, then, could there be in this? It is like the fire that arises at the eon's own burning: every being and every non-sentient thing upon the whole earth, of whatever kind, all alike become flame, through the very power of that fire. Here, too, using the impure mind to contemplate the whole world, one sees it, in every case, as impure — just as a person of supernormal power is able to transform tile and stone into gold and jewels. One should know, then: what fixed nature could any dharma possibly possess? This master explains it in just this way, and, deeply reflecting on this sense with respect to the second station of mastery, its own principle and contemplative practice may, in every case, be relied upon and put to use.

Reading note: The escalation from a single corpse to an entire world filled with corpses, and then the alternate master's even more radical extension — every possession, every food, every animal, every city seen as equally impure — pushes the nine contemplations' original single-body meditation to a civilizational scale. The closing question-and-answer is philosophically the most interesting moment in the whole section: the text openly concedes this is "not a contemplation of what is literally real," a deliberate distortion used as a corrective against an opposite, equally unreal distortion (seeing the world as desirable) — the same logic as fighting fire with fire, or the supernormal power that turns tile into gold. Neither the ordinary view nor the corrective view is being presented as ultimately true; both are provisional, matched to what the mind needs at a given stage.

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying the third and fourth stations of mastery: the contemplative method here differs not at all from what came before, differing only in taking "no form within" as its own mark. The method for extinguishing internal form is already explained at the opening gate of the second detachment above. Here, the practitioner, wishing to break through the desire realm's own afflictions still further, once more cultivates these two stations of mastery within the second dhyāna, serving as an antidote to eliminate the lower stage's own fetters entirely, leaving nothing whatsoever behind, and, further, repeatedly transforming the contemplative path itself, so that it grows ever sharper, more mature, and more illuminating, firm and never lost, its own effort and power growing ever more excellent.

次釋青、黃、赤、白四勝處者，行者不受三禪身證之樂，入第四禪時，念慧清淨，四色轉更光顯，如妙寶光明，勝於前色，故名勝處。復次，行者於四禪中用不動智慧鍊此四色，少能多，多能少，轉變自在，欲見即見，欲滅即滅，故名勝處。復次，行者於三昧中見是勝色，結漏未斷，或時法愛心生，為斷法愛，諦觀此色，知從心起，譬如幻師觀所幻色，知從心生，則不生著，是時背捨變名勝處。

Next, explaining the four stations of mastery of blue-green, yellow, red, and white: the practitioner, no longer accepting the third dhyāna's own happiness realized in the body, upon entering the fourth dhyāna, mindfulness and wisdom now pure, these four colors turn ever more radiant and clear, like the light of some wondrous jewel, surpassing the colors that came before — hence this is named a station of mastery. Furthermore, the practitioner, within the fourth dhyāna, using unshakeable wisdom, refines these four colors, able to make the small great and the great small, transforming them freely at will, seeing them the moment he wishes to see them, and dissolving them the moment he wishes to dissolve them — hence this, too, is named a station of mastery. Furthermore, the practitioner, seeing this very superior color within samādhi, the fetters and contaminants not yet severed, may, at times, give rise to a mind of attachment to the dharma itself; in order to sever this attachment to the dharma, one closely contemplates this very color, knowing it to arise from the mind — like a magician contemplating his own conjured form, knowing it to arise from the mind, and thereby giving rise to no attachment toward it. At this time, detachment is renamed a station of mastery.

第四、明趣道之相，亦為三意：一者、先用勝處調心，然後具足修習超越等法，發真無漏，證三乘道；二者、此八勝處具足成就，深入四諦真觀，第四禪中發真無漏，具足三十四心，斷三界結，證三乘道；三者、自有行人得初勝處，入初禪時，厭畏心重，即作念言：「我今何用事中諸禪？但須疾取涅槃。」作此念已，即於此地深觀四諦、十二因緣、中道、實相，若發無漏，即證三乘聖果也。下七勝處亦當一一如是分別。菩薩摩訶薩雖知諸法畢竟空寂，憐愍一切眾生故，深修勝處。於勝處中發大神通，摧伏天魔，破諸外道，度脫眾生，當知勝處即是菩薩摩訶衍。

Fourth, clarifying the mark of how the stations of mastery lead toward the path: this, too, has three senses.

First, one first uses the stations of mastery to regulate the mind, and only afterward becomes complete in cultivating the samādhi of transcendence and the rest, true undefilement thereby arising, and the path of the three vehicles realized. Second, these eight stations of mastery, complete in their own accomplishment, penetrate deeply into the true contemplation of the Four Noble Truths, true undefilement arising within the fourth dhyāna itself, complete with the thirty-four moments of mind, severing the fetters of the three realms, and realizing the path of the three vehicles. Third, there is the case of a practitioner who, obtaining the first station of mastery upon entering the first dhyāna, the mind heavy with weariness and dread, thereupon reflects: "What use, now, do I have for these many dhyānas amid such concrete matters? I need only swiftly seize hold of nirvāṇa itself." Having reflected in this way, he thereupon, at this very stage, deeply contemplates the Four Noble Truths, the twelve links of dependent origination, the middle way, and true reality — should the undefiled arise, he thereby realizes the noble fruit of the three vehicles directly. The remaining seven stations of mastery should each, in turn, be distinguished in this same way.

The bodhisattva-mahāsattva, though knowing that every dharma is, in the end, empty and still, out of compassion for every sentient being, deeply cultivates the stations of mastery. Within the stations of mastery, great supernormal powers arise, subduing the heavenly māras, breaking through every non-Buddhist teaching, and delivering sentient beings across. One should know, then, that the stations of mastery are themselves precisely the bodhisattva's own Mahāyāna.

Reading note: The four colored stations of mastery mark a genuine shift in the whole sequence's own object: after eight stages entirely fixated on decaying bodies, the fourth dhyāna's practitioner is suddenly refining pure, jewel-like color instead, with an explicit warning against the new risk this creates — attachment to the dharma itself (法愛), the beautiful meditative object becoming a subtler trap than the corpse ever was. The magician-and-conjured-form simile used to break that attachment recurs across this entire treatise as the standard antidote whenever a meditative attainment risks becoming an object of craving in its own right. The three routes to the path closing this section again refuse to prescribe one single sequence, echoing the same structural generosity already given for the nine contemplations, the ten perceptions, and the eight forms of detachment before it.

The Ten Totalities 十一切處 · 階位 · 修證

Chinese Source

次釋十一切遍處法門。十一切處者：一、青；二、黃；三、赤；四、白；五、地；六、水；七、火；八、風；九、空；十、識。此十通名一切處者，一一色各照十方遍滿，故名一切處，乃至空亦如是。前背捨、勝處雖有八色，所照既狹，未能普遍，是以不得受一切之名。復次，經中有時說為「十一切入」。有人解言：「此猶是一切處之異名。」今則不爾，初名以一色遍照十方，名一切處，後心轉善巧，能於一切遍照色中一一互得相入，無相妨閡，故處立一切入名。今明十一切處，即為二意：一、明階位；二、辯修證。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the dharma-gate of the ten totalities. The ten totalities are: first, blue-green; second, yellow; third, red; fourth, white; fifth, earth; sixth, water; seventh, fire; eighth, wind; ninth, space; tenth, consciousness. These ten are, in general, named totalities because each and every color illuminates and pervades all ten directions completely — hence they are named totalities, and space, too, follows this same pattern. The eight colors given earlier, within detachment and the stations of mastery, though present, illuminate only a narrow scope, not yet able to pervade everywhere — hence they do not receive the name of totality.

Furthermore, the sutras, at times, speak of these as the "ten totality-entries." Some explain this as merely another name for the totalities themselves. Here, this is not so: the earlier name, taking a single color pervading and illuminating the ten directions, is named a totality; the later mind, growing yet more skillful, is able, within every pervasively illuminating color, to mutually enter one into another without any hindrance whatsoever — hence this stage is given the name totality-entry.

Explaining the ten totalities now has two senses: first, clarifying their stages; second, distinguishing their cultivation and realization.

第一、明階位者，十一切處，初八色一切處位在第四禪中，次第九空一切處位在空處，第十識一切處位在識處。所以前三禪中不立一切處者，行者初學彼三地中有覺觀喜樂動故，不能令色遍滿停住。上無所有處定，無物可廣，亦不得快樂，佛亦不說無所有處無量無邊，故不立一切處；非有想非無想處心鈍，難取想廣大，故不立一切處。

First, clarifying their stages: among the ten totalities, the first eight color-totalities are placed within the fourth dhyāna; next, the ninth, the totality of space, is placed at the sphere of space; and the tenth, the totality of consciousness, is placed at the sphere of consciousness. As for why no totality is established within the first three dhyānas: the practitioner, newly learning within these three stages, still has initial thought, sustained reflection, joy, and happiness moving about, and is thus unable to make color pervade completely and rest unmoving there. As for the sphere of nothingness above, there being nothing there to extend, nor any happiness to be obtained, and the Buddha, moreover, never speaking of the sphere of nothingness as boundless or infinite, no totality is established there either; and the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, the mind too dull, finding it difficult to grasp any broad, extended perception, no totality is established there either.

第二、次明修證。行者住第四禪中，以成就自在勝色，爾時應用念清淨心，捨七種色，直念青色，取少青光焰相，如草葉大，一心緣中，即與少青相應。觀心運此少青遍照十方，即見光明隨心普照，一切世間皆見青相遍滿，停住不動，如青世界，是名青一切處。餘七一切處修觀之相亦當如是一一分別。自有師言：「修一切處，緣取草葉等相，因外色起相，遍滿普照。」如此說者，非唯乖失觀門之法，亦與《摩訶衍》所說都不相關。

Second, next clarifying cultivation and realization: the practitioner, abiding within the fourth dhyāna, having already accomplished mastery's own superior colors, should, at this time, use the pure mind of mindfulness, setting aside the other seven colors, and hold in mind blue-green alone, taking up a small mark of blue-green light, the size of a blade of grass, the mind fixed one-pointedly upon this object, thereupon corresponding with this small blue-green mark. The contemplating mind then extends this small blue-green mark, illuminating the ten directions completely — one thereupon sees this light, following the mind, illuminating everywhere, the whole world seen entirely filled with the mark of blue-green, resting there unmoving, like a world made entirely of blue-green — this is named the totality of blue-green. The remaining seven totalities' own contemplative mark should each, in turn, be distinguished in this same way.

There is a certain master who says: "Cultivating the totalities means taking up the mark of a blade of grass and the like as one's object, the mark arising by way of external color, and pervading and illuminating everywhere." Explained in this way, this not only strays from and loses the very method of this contemplative gate, but also has nothing whatsoever to do with what is explained in the Mahāyāna scriptures.

行者既已成就，以一切處欲入虛空一切處，當入虛空背捨，但背捨緣狹，未名一切處，今更廣緣十方虛空，故名虛空一切處。欲入識一切處者，當亦先入識處背捨，於識定中廣觀此識，遍滿十方，皆見是識，故名識一切處。行者若欲修一切入，既得一切處成，當以一切處為本，然後用善巧觀心，於青一切中，令黃、赤、白等皆入其中，不壞青之本相，而能於青色之中具見餘色。是則略說一切處、一切入竟。

The practitioner, already having accomplished these totalities, wishing to enter the totality of space by way of the totalities themselves, should enter the detachment of the sphere of space; it is only that detachment's own object is narrow, and does not yet receive the name of totality — here, one further broadens the object to the space of the ten directions, hence this is named the totality of space. Wishing to enter the totality of consciousness, one should likewise first enter the detachment of the sphere of consciousness, and, within the concentration on consciousness, broadly contemplate this very consciousness, pervading and filling the ten directions, seeing everything, entirely, as consciousness — hence this is named the totality of consciousness.

Should the practitioner wish to cultivate the totality-entries, having already accomplished the totalities themselves, one should take the totalities as the basis, and then, using a skillful contemplating mind, within the totality of blue-green, cause yellow, red, white, and the rest to each enter within it as well, without destroying blue-green's own original mark, and yet able, within the color blue-green, to see every other color present in full. This, then, briefly explains the totalities and the totality-entries, and brings them to a close.

問曰：何故不於一切處中分別趣道之相？答曰：一切聲聞經中多說一切處是有漏緣，但是修通法，既於發無漏義劣，故不分別。若依摩訶衍義欲分別者，類如前背捨、勝處中說。今菩薩為欲令神通普徧成就普現色，具足一切法界中事，故修是一切處，故《大品經》亦說名一切處波羅蜜。

Question: why is the mark of how the totalities lead toward the path not separately distinguished here? Answer: throughout the śrāvaka scriptures, the totalities are, for the most part, explained as a contaminated object, serving merely as a method for cultivating supernormal powers — being, then, of lesser significance for giving rise to the undefiled, this is not separately distinguished here. Should one wish to distinguish this according to the sense of the Mahāyāna scriptures, it follows the same pattern already given above, within detachment and the stations of mastery. Here, the bodhisattva, wishing to cause supernormal powers to pervade everywhere and to accomplish the manifestation of every form completely, becoming complete with every matter throughout the whole dharma-realm, thereby cultivates these very totalities — hence the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Sūtra, too, speaks of this as the totality-pāramitā.

Reading note: The totalities complete the arc from the eight forms of detachment's own narrow object through the stations of mastery's own controlled expansion to this final stage's true saturation — a single blade-of-grass-sized fragment of color, deliberately expanded until it fills the visualized world entirely, unmoving. The distinction between a "totality" (one color filling everything) and a "totality-entry" (multiple colors interpenetrating within a single totality without destroying each other's own mark) is a subtle but important refinement, and the treatise is careful to flag the wrong way to teach it — starting from an external object like an actual blade of grass, rather than from the mind's own cultivated image — as a real error some other master had made, not just a hypothetical one. The totalities are also the one dharma-gate in this whole three-part observational sequence (detachment, mastery, totality) explicitly not given its own "approach to the path" treatment, on the grounds that śrāvaka tradition treats it as useful chiefly for supernormal powers rather than liberation directly — though the Mahāyāna reading, as usual in this treatise, reclaims it anyway.

The Function of Contemplative Dhyāna: The Six Supernormal Powers 觀禪功用 · 六神通

Chinese Source

第二、明觀禪功用之法者，佛弟子既得此三番觀行，若欲為化眾生，現希有事，令心清淨，應當廣修一切神通道力，所謂：六通、十四變化、四辯、無諍三昧、願智、頂禪、自在定、鍊禪、十八變化等諸大功德，皆應住此背捨、勝處、一切處中學。既學得已，令多眾生覩見歡喜，信伏得度，故修神變。次釋六神通。六通者：一、天眼通；二、天耳通；三、他心通；四、宿命通；五、如意通；六、漏盡通。皆言神通者，神名天心，通是智慧性，以天然之智慧，徹照一切色心等法無閡，故名神通。今約此諸禪後，釋六通，即為三意：一、明得通因緣不同；二、正明修通方法；三、明變化功用。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying contemplative dhyāna's own function. Once the Buddha's disciple has already obtained these three tiers of contemplative practice, should he wish to transform sentient beings, manifesting rare and extraordinary things, purifying the mind, he should broadly cultivate the whole power of the supernormal path — namely, the six supernormal powers, the fourteen transformations, the four kinds of eloquence, the samādhi free of contention, the wisdom of aspiration, the summit dhyāna, mastery over concentration, the refining dhyāna, and the eighteen transformations, and every other great merit of this kind — all of which should be learned while abiding within detachment, the stations of mastery, and the totalities. Once this learning is accomplished, it causes the great multitude of sentient beings to behold it with joy, to believe and submit, and to be delivered thereby — hence one cultivates supernormal transformation.

Next, explaining the six supernormal powers. The six powers are: first, the heavenly eye; second, the heavenly ear; third, knowledge of others' minds; fourth, knowledge of past lives; fifth, mastery at will; sixth, the power of exhausting the contaminants. As for why these are, in general, called supernormal powers: "supernormal" names the heavenly mind, and "power" is the very nature of wisdom — through this heaven-born wisdom, one sees through, without any obstruction whatsoever, every dharma of form and mind alike — hence this is named supernormal power.

Here, following upon these dhyānas, the six powers are now explained, in three senses: first, clarifying the differing causes and conditions by which the powers are obtained; second, directly clarifying the method of cultivating the powers; third, clarifying the function of transformation.

第一、明得通因緣不同者，自有三種：一者、報得。如諸天大福德，淨土中人受生，即得報得五通也。二者、發得。若人但因懺悔，或深修上所說禪定，雖不作取通方便，而神通自發，故經云：「深修禪定，得五神通。」三、修得者，行人雖證上所說諸深禪定，而未斷障通無知，則神通終不發。若於禪定中更作取通方便，斷障通無知，神通即發。今正約此明義。

First, clarifying the differing causes and conditions by which the powers are obtained: there are, of themselves, three kinds. First, obtained through retribution — as when the various heavenly beings of great merit, or a person reborn within a pure land, thereby obtains the five powers as a matter of retribution itself. Second, obtained spontaneously — should a person, merely through repentance, or through deeply cultivating the dhyāna concentrations already explained above, though making no deliberate effort toward obtaining the powers, nonetheless find the supernormal powers arising of themselves; hence the sutra says: "Deeply cultivating dhyāna concentration, one obtains the five supernormal powers." Third, obtained through cultivation: should a practitioner, though having already realized the various deep dhyāna concentrations already explained above, not yet have severed the very ignorance that obstructs the powers, the supernormal powers, in the end, never arise. Should one, within dhyāna concentration, further make deliberate effort toward obtaining the powers, severing the ignorance that obstructs them, the supernormal powers thereupon arise. Here, this very sense is directly clarified.

第二、次明修通法者。經論所說，乃各不同，今但取《摩訶衍》中意，以略明修通方法。一、修天眼通者，行人深心憐愍一切，發願欲見六道眾生死此生彼之相，爾時當住色界背捨、勝處、一切處及四如意足中，正念修習，具足四緣，即生天眼通。何等為四？一、光明常照，晝夜無異；二、諦觀世間隔障悉如虛空，無有覆蔽之相；三、專心先取一易可見境，以心緣之，常勤精進，善巧修習，欲見前境；四、於禪定中發四大造清淨眼根成就。是名具足四緣和合，因此生清淨識，即見十方六道眾生死此生彼苦樂之相，若明暗、近遠、障內障外、麁細之色，徹見無閡，了了分明，是名天眼通。

Second, next clarifying the method of cultivating the powers: the sutras and treatises explain this each in their own differing way; here, taking only the sense given in the Mahāyāna scriptures, the method of cultivating the powers is briefly clarified.

First, cultivating the heavenly eye: the practitioner, the mind deeply compassionate toward all, arouses the vow to see the mark of sentient beings across the six realms dying here and being born there. At that time, one should abide within the form realm's own detachment, stations of mastery, and totalities, together with the four bases of supernormal power, cultivating right mindfulness, complete with four conditions, and the heavenly eye thereby arises. What are these four? First, light constantly illuminating, day and night alike; second, closely contemplating that every obstruction throughout the world is itself like empty space, with no mark of concealment whatsoever; third, single-mindedly first taking up one easily visible object, the mind fixed upon it, constantly diligent and vigorous, skillfully cultivating this, wishing to see what lies ahead; fourth, within dhyāna concentration, giving rise to the four elements' own construction of a pure eye-faculty, brought to accomplishment. This is named being complete with the union of these four conditions, through which pure consciousness thereby arises, and one thereupon sees, throughout the ten directions, the six realms of sentient beings dying here and being born there, their marks of suffering and happiness — whether bright or dark, near or far, obstructed within or without, coarse or fine in form — seeing through them all without any obstruction whatsoever, utterly clear and distinct: this is named the heavenly eye.

二、次明修天耳通。行者既見色已，若欲聞其聲，當於禪定中諦取障外可聞細聲，一心聽之，願欲得聞。若心明利，發得四大造清淨色耳根，即聞障外、障內一切六道音聲，苦樂憂喜，言辭不同，是名天耳通。三、明修他心通。行者既聞聲已，若欲知眾生心所念事，當即於禪定中觀前人喜相、瞋相、怖畏等相，悉知依心而住，借此等相，諦觀其心所緣念法，一心願欲知之。若心明利，因此發通，隨所見眾生，即知心所念事，是名他心通。

Second, next clarifying cultivation of the heavenly ear: the practitioner, having already seen form, wishing further to hear its sound, should, within dhyāna concentration, closely take up some faint sound audible beyond an obstruction, listening to it one-pointedly, wishing and vowing to hear it. Should the mind grow bright and sharp, the four elements' own construction of a pure form-based ear-faculty is thereby brought forth, and one thereupon hears, whether beyond an obstruction or within it, every sound throughout the six realms — of suffering, happiness, sorrow, and joy, their words and expressions each differing from the rest — this is named the heavenly ear.

Third, clarifying cultivation of knowledge of others' minds: the practitioner, having already heard sound, wishing further to know the very thoughts within sentient beings' own minds, should, within dhyāna concentration, contemplate the person before him — his mark of joy, his mark of anger, his mark of fear, and the rest — knowing that these, in every case, rest dependent upon the mind itself; borrowing these very marks, one closely contemplates the very dharma his mind takes as its object, one-pointedly wishing and vowing to know it. Should the mind grow bright and sharp, the power thereby arises through this very method, and, following whatever sentient being one sees, one thereupon knows the very matter held within his mind — this is named knowledge of others' minds.

四、次明修宿命通。行者既知他心已，若欲自知己宿命及他宿命百千萬世所作事業，即當於禪定中自憶己所，於日月歲數中經作之事，乃至歌羅邏時所作之事。如是憶念，一心願欲知之。若心明利，便發神通，即自知過去一世，乃至百千萬世劫數中宿命所作事業之相，了了分明，乃至知他宿命亦如是，是名宿命通。

Fourth, next clarifying cultivation of knowledge of past lives: the practitioner, having already known others' minds, wishing further to know, of himself, his own past lives, and others' past lives, and the deeds performed across a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand lifetimes, should, within dhyāna concentration, recall to himself the deeds performed across the days, months, and years of his own life, all the way back to the very deeds performed at the kalala-stage itself. Recollecting in this way, one-pointedly wishes and vows to know it. Should the mind grow bright and sharp, the supernormal power thereupon arises, and one thereby knows, of oneself, the mark of the deeds performed across one past life, and, further still, across a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand lifetimes and kalpas, utterly clear and distinct — and, further still, knowing others' past lives is likewise so — this is named knowledge of past lives.

五、次明修身如意通。行者既知宿命，若欲得身通變化，當於三昧中繫心身內虛空，滅麁重色相，常取輕空之相，發大欲精進心，智慧籌量心力能舉身。未籌已，自知心力已大，能舉其身。譬如學跳之人，常自輕舉其身。若觀心成就，即發身如意通，有三種：一者、能到；二者、轉變；三者、自在。所言能到者，即有四種：一者、身能飛行，如鳥無閡；二、移遠令近，不往而到；三、此沒彼出；四、一念能到。二、次明轉變者，大能作小，小能作大，一能作多，多能作一，種種諸物皆能轉。三、聖如意者，外六塵中，不受用不淨物，能觀令淨，可受淨物能令不淨，是自在法。如意神通從修勝處、一切處、四如意足中生，是名證身如意通。行者得是身如意通故，即能隨意變現，若欲自得解脫及度眾生，必須斷除心病，是時應修無漏通。修無漏通，下明諦觀中當廣分別。

Fifth, next clarifying cultivation of the power of mastery over the body at will: the practitioner, having already known his past lives, wishing further to obtain the power over the body's own transformation, should, within samādhi, fix the mind upon the emptiness within the body, extinguishing the coarse and heavy mark of form, constantly taking up the mark of lightness and emptiness, arousing a mind of great aspiration and vigorous effort, wisdom weighing and measuring whether the mind's own power is able to lift the body. Before this measuring is complete, one already knows of oneself that the mind's own power has already grown great, able to lift the body — like one learning to leap, constantly lightening his own body of his own accord. Should this contemplating mind be brought to accomplishment, the power of mastery over the body at will thereupon arises, of three kinds: first, the ability to reach anywhere; second, transformation; third, mastery.

As for the ability to reach anywhere, this, too, is of four kinds: first, the body is able to fly through the air, like a bird, without obstruction; second, moving what is distant to make it near, arriving without having traveled at all; third, vanishing here and appearing there; fourth, arriving in a single thought-moment. Second, next clarifying transformation: the great made small, the small made great, one made many, many made one — every kind of thing is able to be transformed. Third, noble mastery at will: among the external six sense-objects, an object not fit to be used, being impure, one is able, through contemplation, to make it pure; an object fit to be used, being pure, one is able to make it impure — this is the dharma of mastery. This mastery-at-will supernormal power arises from cultivating the stations of mastery, the totalities, and the four bases of supernormal power — this is named realizing the power of mastery over the body at will.

The practitioner, having obtained this power of mastery over the body at will, is thereby able to manifest transformation at will; should he wish to obtain liberation for himself and deliver sentient beings across as well, he must sever the very illness of the mind — at this time, he should cultivate the undefiled power. Cultivating the undefiled power will be distinguished at greater length below, within the clarification of close contemplation.

Reading note: The three causes of the supernormal powers — inherited from past merit, arising spontaneously through deep concentration alone, or deliberately cultivated by directly attacking the specific ignorance that blocks each power — matter because they set different expectations: a practitioner who has already realized deep dhyāna but never obtains any power has, per this passage, simply never gotten around to removing the specific obstruction, not failed at meditation itself. The four bodily masteries (flight, teleportation, bilocation, and instantaneous arrival) and the "noble mastery" of transmuting an object's own purity or impurity at will both trace back to the same source material already built up across this whole fascicle: detachment, the stations of mastery, and the totalities are the raw practice: these powers are simply what that same practice starts to do once pointed outward.

The Fourteen Transformations 十四變化

Chinese Source

問曰：修下六次第，一向如前所說耶？答曰：此約一途論次第，若行人隨所樂通，前學即得，未必皆如前辯。第三、明變化者，十四變化能生神通，亦因神通能有變化。云何名十四變化？一者、欲界、初禪成就二變化：一、初禪初禪化；二、初禪欲界化。二者、二禪成就三變化：一、二禪二禪化；二、二禪初禪化；三、二禪欲界化。三者、三禪成就四變化：一、三禪三禪化；二、三禪二禪化；三、三禪初禪化；四、三禪欲界化。四者、四禪成就五變化：一、四禪四禪化；二、四禪三禪化；三、四禪二禪化；四、四禪初禪化；五、四禪欲界化。是為十四變化。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: is the sequence for cultivating these six powers below always exactly as already explained above? Answer: this is explained here with reference to a single general path of sequence; should the practitioner wish to cultivate whichever power he prefers, he may learn it first and obtain it directly — it need not always follow the order distinguished above.

Third, clarifying transformation: the fourteen transformations are able to give rise to supernormal power, and, likewise, through supernormal power one is able to bring about transformation. What is meant by the fourteen transformations?

First, the desire realm and the first dhyāna together accomplish two transformations: first, the first dhyāna transforming into the first dhyāna; second, the first dhyāna transforming into the desire realm. Second, the second dhyāna accomplishes three transformations: first, the second dhyāna transforming into the second dhyāna; second, the second dhyāna transforming into the first dhyāna; third, the second dhyāna transforming into the desire realm. Third, the third dhyāna accomplishes four transformations: first, the third dhyāna transforming into the third dhyāna; second, the third dhyāna transforming into the second dhyāna; third, the third dhyāna transforming into the first dhyāna; fourth, the third dhyāna transforming into the desire realm. Fourth, the fourth dhyāna accomplishes five transformations: first, the fourth dhyāna transforming into the fourth dhyāna; second, the fourth dhyāna transforming into the third dhyāna; third, the fourth dhyāna transforming into the second dhyāna; fourth, the fourth dhyāna transforming into the first dhyāna; fifth, the fourth dhyāna transforming into the desire realm. These are the fourteen transformations.

若人成就此變化，即具十八變化一切神通力，觀行功德無量無邊，是事微細，豈可以文字具載？今但略出名目，欲令學者知一切神通變化皆從觀禪中出。此諸神通若在菩薩心中，名神通波羅蜜。

Should a person accomplish these transformations, he is thereby complete with the whole supernormal power of the eighteen transformations, the merit of this contemplative practice measureless and boundless — the matter itself being subtle and fine, how could it be recorded in full through mere written words? Here, only the names themselves are briefly given, so that the student may know that every supernormal transformation, without exception, issues from contemplative dhyāna itself. Should these supernormal powers reside within the bodhisattva's own mind, they are named the pāramitā of supernormal power.

Reading note: The fourteen transformations follow a clean arithmetic pattern — each dhyāna stage can transform into itself and into every stage below it, but never upward, so the first dhyāna yields 2 transformations, the second 3, the third 4, and the fourth 5, totaling 14. The treatise's own closing admission that this "cannot be recorded in full through mere written words" is a rare, direct acknowledgment of the text's own limits — the fourteen transformations are named here only as a map of what exists, not a manual for performing it, consistent with how this entire fascicle keeps returning to the theme that some dharma-gates can be catalogued but not fully written out.

The Nine Sequential Concentrations 九次第定 · 釋名 · 次位 · 修證

Chinese Source

次釋九次第定。九次第定者，離諸欲，離諸惡不善法，有覺有觀，離生喜樂，入初禪，如是次第入二禪、三禪、四禪、空處定、識處定、不用處定、非有想非無想處定、滅受想定，是名九次第定。釋九次第定，即為三意：一者、釋名；二、明次位；三、明修證。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the nine sequential concentrations. The nine sequential concentrations are: departing from every desire, departing from every unwholesome and evil dharma, possessing initial thought and sustained reflection, departing giving rise to joy and happiness, entering the first dhyāna; and, in this same sequence, entering the second dhyāna, the third dhyāna, the fourth dhyāna, the concentration of the sphere of space, the concentration of the sphere of consciousness, the concentration of the sphere of nothingness, the concentration of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, and the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception — this is named the nine sequential concentrations.

Explaining the nine sequential concentrations now has three senses: first, explaining the name; second, clarifying the sequence of stages; third, clarifying cultivation and realization.

第一、釋名。今此九法皆轉名次第定者，上來諸法門既觀行未熟，入禪時心有間故，不名次第定也。行者定觀之法先已成就，今於此中修鍊既熟，能從一禪心起，次入一禪，心心無間，不令異念得入，若善若垢，如是乃至滅受想定，是名九次第定，亦名鍊禪。所以者何？諸佛弟子心樂無漏，先得諸味禪，今欲除其滓穢，以無漏禪鍊之，皆令清淨，如鍊金之法。

First, explaining the name: as for why these nine dharmas are, in every case, renamed the sequential concentrations — in the dharma-gates already given above, the contemplative practice not yet mature, the mind still had gaps at the moment of entering each dhyāna, and hence these were not named the sequential concentrations. The practitioner, the dharma of concentration and contemplation already accomplished beforehand, here, in this very stage, cultivating and refining this until it grows mature, is able, from the mind of one dhyāna arising, to enter, next, the very next dhyāna, mind following mind without any gap whatsoever, allowing no differing thought whatsoever to intrude, whether wholesome or defiled, and so on, all the way through the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception — this is named the nine sequential concentrations, also named the refining dhyāna. Why is this? Every disciple of the Buddha, the mind delighting in the undefiled, having first obtained the various flavor-dhyānas, now wishes to remove their own dross and impurity, refining them with the undefiled dhyāna, bringing them all to purity — like the method of refining gold.

問曰：說九次第定中鍊法，與阿毘曇人明熏禪之法，有何等異？答曰：有同有異。彼以無漏鍊有漏，今亦以無漏鍊有漏，故同。彼則但明鍊四禪，為防退轉，轉鈍為利，現法樂及生五淨居故，唯鍊四禪。無色界則無鍊法。今明從初禪乃至非想，悉皆鍊之，令一切諸禪清淨調柔，增益功德，故為異也。尋下修證，自當可見。

Question: the refining method spoken of within the nine sequential concentrations — how does this differ from what the Abhidharma masters clarify as the method of the permeating dhyāna? Answer: there is both similarity and difference. They use the undefiled to refine the contaminated; here, too, the undefiled is used to refine the contaminated — hence the similarity. They, however, explain only the refining of the fourth dhyāna, in order to guard against regression, turning dullness into sharpness, for the sake of manifest happiness in this very life and rebirth among the five pure-abode heavens — hence they refine the fourth dhyāna alone, and the formless realm has, for them, no refining method at all. Here, it is clarified that, from the first dhyāna all the way through neither-perception-nor-non-perception, every one, without exception, is refined, causing every dhyāna to grow pure and pliant, its merit increasing — hence the difference. Inquiring into the cultivation and realization given below, this may be seen for itself.

第二、明階位者，此位雖一往約四禪、四空及滅盡定，然實位通諸禪。所以者何？如上所說，特勝、通明、背捨、勝處，悉有四禪、四空，未必但是根本。今修鍊之法，悉應普入諸禪，令心無間，不可的約根本世間禪以為次位也。故《大品》云：「菩薩依八背捨，逆順出入九次第定。」若依《成論》、《毘曇》義，但用無漏心入八禪，緣真入滅，以為九次第定。今用《大品》、《摩訶衍論》所明九次第定，意往望彼，則大有鬪闕。習者尋上來所說，言意叵類，同異之相泠然可見。

Second, clarifying their stages: though this stage, in a general sense, refers to the four dhyānas, the four formless states, and the concentration of cessation, its own stage, in reality, extends throughout every dhyāna. Why is this? As already explained above, the special excellences, the thorough-and-bright contemplation, detachment, and the stations of mastery are all, alike, complete with the four dhyānas and four formless states, and need not be confined to the root dhyānas alone. Here, the method of refining cultivation should, in every case, universally enter every dhyāna, causing the mind to be without any gap — it cannot be fixed narrowly with reference to the root mundane dhyānas alone as its own stage. Hence the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva, based upon the eight forms of detachment, enters and leaves the nine sequential concentrations, both forward and backward."

Should one follow the sense of the Satyasiddhi-śāstra and the Abhidharma, one uses only the undefiled mind to enter the eight dhyānas, taking reality as the object and entering cessation — this alone is taken as the nine sequential concentrations. Here, using what is clarified as the nine sequential concentrations within the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Sūtra and the Mahāyāna treatises, and weighing this sense against that other reading, there proves to be a great deal of conflict and deficiency between them. Should the student inquire into what has already been given above, the sense and intent prove difficult to reconcile — the mark of their similarity and difference may be seen plainly for itself.

第三、明修證者，行者既具足諸禪，今欲入九次第定者，先當從淺至深，修鍊諸禪定觀之法，極令調柔利熟，然後總合定、觀二種法門，一心齊入，善斷法愛，自識其心，從初調心入一禪，不令異念間雜，如是乃至滅受想定。所以者何？行者於根本禪中，定多而智少，則心不調柔，故入禪有間；背捨禪等，觀多而定少，故心不調柔，入禪有間。譬如車有二輪，若一強一弱，則載不安穩，亦如刀刃，強軟不調，則無利用。此亦如是。

Third, clarifying cultivation and realization: the practitioner, already complete with every dhyāna, now wishing to enter the nine sequential concentrations, should first, proceeding from the shallow to the deep, cultivate and refine the dharma of concentration and contemplation belonging to every dhyāna, bringing it to the utmost pliancy, sharpness, and maturity, and only afterward unite the two dharma-gates of concentration and contemplation together, entering them one-pointedly and simultaneously, skillfully severing attachment to the dharma itself, knowing one's own mind directly — proceeding from the initial regulation of the mind into the first dhyāna, allowing no differing thought whatsoever to intrude between them, and so on, all the way through the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception.

Why is this? The practitioner, within the root dhyānas, has concentration in abundance but wisdom in short supply, and the mind is thus not yet pliant — hence entering each dhyāna, there is a gap. Within detachment and the other dhyānas of this kind, contemplation is abundant but concentration is in short supply, and the mind is likewise not yet pliant — hence entering each dhyāna, there is again a gap. It is like a cart with two wheels: should one be strong and the other weak, the load rides unsteadily; it is likewise like a blade's own edge: should its hardness and softness be unbalanced, it proves of no use at all. This, too, is just the same.

今修此定，既定觀均等，定深智利。定深故，在緣則不散；智慧利故，則進入捷疾無閡。是故從一禪起入一禪時利疾，心心相次，無諸雜間，隨念即入，亦名無間三昧。行者若用此心遍入諸禪，非但次第調柔，心無雜間，亦復增益諸禪功德轉深微妙，如鍊金，光色更增，價直亦倍，故說此定名曰鍊禪。問曰：是中亦有欲界、未到、中間，何故但說九定？答曰：雖有此法，既不牢固，又聖人所得大功德不在邊地，是故不說。復次，上來入禪心鈍，故於方便中間經停則久，是故分別有未到、中間之相。今此九定，定慧心利，欲入正地，隨念即入，既不久住方便中間，是故不說。若分別趣道之相，具如前背捨、勝處中說，故不別明。

Here, cultivating this very concentration, concentration and contemplation now equally balanced, concentration grows deep and wisdom sharp. Because concentration is deep, one remains, within one's object, undistracted; because wisdom is sharp, one advances and enters swiftly, without any obstruction. For this reason, rising from one dhyāna and entering the next, one does so swiftly and sharply, mind following mind in immediate succession, free of any intervening gap whatsoever, entering the very moment it is called to mind — this is also named the samādhi without any interval. Should the practitioner use this very mind to enter every dhyāna throughout, not only does this bring pliancy to their own sequence, the mind free of any intervening gap, but it also increases every dhyāna's own merit, growing ever deeper and more subtle — like the refining of gold, whose own light and color grow ever brighter, its own worth doubling as well — hence this concentration is named the refining dhyāna.

Question: within this, there is also the desire realm, the not-yet-arrived state, and the intermediate dhyāna — why, then, is only the nine concentrations spoken of? Answer: though these dharmas are indeed present, they are not yet firm and stable, and, moreover, the great merit obtained by the noble ones does not reside in these peripheral stages — hence they are not separately spoken of here. Furthermore, in the dharma-gates given above, the mind entering dhyāna being dull, one tends to dwell within these expedient, intermediate stages for a long while — hence the not-yet-arrived state and the intermediate dhyāna's own mark were distinguished separately there. Here, within these nine concentrations, the mind of concentration and wisdom being sharp, wishing to enter the true stage itself, one enters the very moment it is called to mind, no longer dwelling long within these expedient, intermediate stages — hence they are not separately spoken of here. Should one wish to distinguish the mark of how this leads toward the path, this is entirely as already explained above, within detachment and the stations of mastery — hence it is not separately clarified again here.

Reading note: The two-wheeled-cart and unbalanced-blade similes give a memorable shorthand for why neither the root dhyānas (heavy on concentration, light on wisdom) nor detachment and the observational gates (heavy on wisdom, light on concentration) can, alone, move without gaps between stages — only once the two are cultivated to equal strength does the "samādhi without interval" become possible. The treatise's own comparison with the Abhidharma tradition's rival account of "refining dhyāna" is unusually direct about the disagreement: not just noting a difference, but naming it a "conflict and deficiency" between readings, and conceding that reconciling them is "difficult" — a rare moment of the text acknowledging genuine unresolved tension in its sources rather than smoothing it over.

The Three Samādhis 三三昧 · 釋名 · 辯相 · 出生三昧

Chinese Source

次釋三三昧。三三昧者：一、有覺有觀三昧；二、無覺有觀三昧；三、無覺無觀三昧。所以次九定後明三三昧者，此二種禪，名雖有異，而法相孱同。所以者何？九定既通鍊諸禪，而自無別體。三三昧亦如是，此義在下自當可見。釋三三昧，即為三意：一、釋名；二、辯相；三、名出生三昧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the three samādhis. The three samādhis are: first, the samādhi possessing initial thought and sustained reflection; second, the samādhi without initial thought but possessing sustained reflection; third, the samādhi without either initial thought or sustained reflection. As for why the three samādhis are explained here, following the nine sequential concentrations: though these two kinds of dhyāna differ in name, their own dharma-marks are closely alike. Why is this? The nine concentrations, already universally refining every dhyāna, possess no separate substance of their own; the three samādhis, too, are just the same — this sense may be seen for itself below.

Explaining the three samādhis now has three senses: first, explaining the name; second, distinguishing their mark; third, naming the samādhis they give rise to.

第一、釋名。覺觀等三法，名同次位，已如前根本禪中說，三昧今當分別。一切禪定攝心，皆名為三摩提，秦言正心行處。是心從無始已來，常曲不端，得是正心行處，心則端直，故名三昧。譬如蛇行常曲，入筒則直。此亦如是。問曰：若言禪定攝心名三昧者，根本禪定與此有何異？答曰：有異。彼則但明根本攝心，今遍約一切諸禪中明攝心當如此，則定深而廣，豈得不異？復次，根本禪但是緣事攝心，未斷邪倒，不名端直。今明三昧，並據緣理攝心，能斷邪倒之曲，故以心端直處為三昧也。

First, explaining the name: the three dharmas of initial thought, sustained reflection, and the rest, their name matching their own sequence of stages, have already been explained above, within the root dhyānas; samādhi shall now be distinguished. Every dhyāna concentration that gathers in the mind is, in general, named samādhi — in the language of the Qin, "the place where the mind moves correctly." This mind, ever since beginningless time, has constantly been crooked and never upright; obtaining this very place where the mind moves correctly, the mind thereby becomes upright and straight — hence this is named samādhi. It is like a snake, whose movement is always crooked, entering a bamboo tube and thereby made straight — this, too, is just the same.

Question: should one say that dhyāna concentration gathering in the mind is named samādhi, how, then, does this differ from the root dhyāna concentrations themselves? Answer: there is a difference. Those explain only the gathering of the mind within the root dhyānas; here, it is clarified, universally, with reference to gathering the mind within every dhyāna — this being so, the concentration is both deep and broad — how could it not differ? Furthermore, the root dhyānas merely gather the mind by taking concrete phenomena as their object, not yet severing the crookedness of wrong views, and thus are not named upright and straight. Here, clarifying samādhi, this is, in every case, based upon gathering the mind by taking principle itself as the object, able to sever the very crookedness of wrong views — hence the place where the mind is upright and straight is named samādhi.

第二、次辯相者，此三三昧義同九定，既無別體，但約諸禪以辯相也。一、明有覺三昧，如上所說根本初禪乃至特勝、通明、背捨、勝處等初禪，各有覺有觀相應心數及諸功德。行者入此等諸初禪時，住正心行處，皆名有覺有觀三昧。二、如上所說諸禪中間乃至特勝、通明、背捨、勝處，各有中間與觀相應心數法及諸功德。行者以正心行處入此等諸禪中間，皆名無覺有觀三昧。三、如上所說根本二禪乃至有頂及特勝、通明、背捨、勝處等，各有二禪，從二禪已去，乃至有頂及滅受想定，有無覺無觀相應法并諸功德。行者正心行處，入此等諸禪功德，皆名無覺無觀三昧。

Second, next distinguishing their mark: the sense of these three samādhis is identical to that of the nine concentrations, possessing no separate substance of their own, and are distinguished in their mark solely with reference to the various dhyānas themselves.

First, clarifying the samādhi possessing initial thought: as already explained above, the root first dhyāna, and, further still, the first dhyāna belonging to the special excellences, the thorough-and-bright contemplation, detachment, and the stations of mastery, each, alike, possess initial thought and sustained reflection, their own corresponding mental factors, and every merit belonging to them. The practitioner, entering these various first dhyānas, abiding in the place where the mind moves correctly — this is, in every case, named the samādhi possessing initial thought and sustained reflection.

Second, as already explained above, the various dhyānas' own intermediate stage, and, further still, the intermediate stage belonging to the special excellences, the thorough-and-bright contemplation, detachment, and the stations of mastery, each, alike, possess an intermediate stage together with sustained reflection's own corresponding mental factors and every merit belonging to them. The practitioner, through the place where the mind moves correctly, entering these various intermediate stages — this is, in every case, named the samādhi without initial thought but possessing sustained reflection.

Third, as already explained above, the root second dhyāna, and, further still, up through the peak of existence, together with the second dhyāna belonging to the special excellences, the thorough-and-bright contemplation, detachment, and the stations of mastery — from the second dhyāna onward, all the way through the peak of existence and the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception, there is present the dharma corresponding to neither initial thought nor sustained reflection, together with every merit belonging to it. The practitioner, through the place where the mind moves correctly, entering these various dhyānas' own merit — this is, in every case, named the samādhi without either initial thought or sustained reflection.

當知此三三昧更無別體，但是總諸禪以為三分。大聖欲令眾生雖聞廣說諸禪，而不失根本故，總以三法收攝諸禪，罄無不盡。譬如數法，若至百萬，總為一億，此亦如是。第三、明出生三昧之相者，則有二種：一者、出生二乘三昧。所以者何？如上所說，諸有覺有觀初禪等，悉發念處三昧，乃至八聖道、空、無相、無作、十六行、十二因緣、暖、頂、忍、世第一等三昧、電光三昧、金剛三昧乃至佛智無諍等三昧。此諸法門，《涅槃經》中悉說名三昧也。若於諸初禪中發此等三昧，即證二乘若道若果，故名有覺有觀三昧。乃至無覺有觀、無覺無觀，亦當如是一一分別。

One should know, then, that these three samādhis possess no separate substance of their own whatsoever, but are simply every dhyāna gathered together and divided into three portions. The great sage, wishing to ensure that sentient beings, though hearing every dhyāna explained at length, would not lose sight of the root, therefore gathered in every dhyāna, in general, through these three dharmas, leaving nothing whatsoever uncovered — like the method of counting: reaching a million, one gathers this, in general, into a single hundred-million; this, too, is just the same.

Third, clarifying the mark of the samādhis these three give rise to: this, too, is of two kinds. First, giving rise to the two vehicles' own samādhis. Why is this? As already explained above, the various first dhyānas possessing initial thought and sustained reflection all give rise to the samādhi of the foundations of mindfulness, and, further still, the eight-fold noble path, emptiness, signlessness, no-action, the sixteen practices, the twelve links of dependent origination, the samādhis of heat, summit, forbearance, and the supreme mundane dharma, the lightning-flash samādhi, the vajra samādhi, and, further still, the buddha-wisdom's own samādhi free of contention and the rest. These very dharma-gates are, throughout the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, in every case, spoken of and named samādhis. Should one, within these various first dhyānas, give rise to samādhis such as these, one thereby realizes the path or the fruit belonging to the two vehicles — hence this is named the samādhi possessing initial thought and sustained reflection. And, further still, the samādhi without initial thought but possessing sustained reflection, and the samādhi without either, should each, in turn, be distinguished in this same way.

二者、如上所說諸有覺有觀禪各發大乘諸三昧者，如觀佛三昧、二十五三昧、般舟三昧、首楞嚴等諸菩薩三昧，百則有八，諸佛三昧不動等，百則有二十及八萬四千諸三昧等，皆因有覺有觀三昧發。乃至無覺有觀、無覺無觀，亦當如是一一分別。菩薩摩訶薩得此諸三昧故，即入菩薩位，亦能現身，如佛度一切眾生。三三昧義，如《摩訶衍》中廣分別。

Second, as already explained above, the various dhyānas possessing initial thought and sustained reflection each also give rise to the Mahāyāna's own samādhis — such as the samādhi of buddha-contemplation, the twenty-five samādhis, the pratyutpanna samādhi, the Śūraṅgama samādhi, and every other bodhisattva samādhi, a full hundred and eight in number; and the buddhas' own samādhis, the unmoving and the rest, a full hundred and twenty, together with the eighty-four thousand samādhis and the rest — all, alike, arising through the samādhi possessing initial thought and sustained reflection. And, further still, the samādhi without initial thought but possessing sustained reflection, and the samādhi without either, should each, in turn, be distinguished in this same way.

The bodhisattva-mahāsattva, having obtained these samādhis, thereupon enters the bodhisattva's own stage, and is also able to manifest the body, delivering every sentient being across, just as the Buddha himself does. The sense of the three samādhis is explained at greater length in the Mahāyāna scriptures.

Reading note: The etymology offered for samādhi — "the place where the mind moves correctly," a naturally crooked mind straightened the way a snake is straightened by entering a bamboo tube — is one of this treatise's more vivid images for what concentration actually does to an untrained mind. The chapter's real payoff, though, is structural: the three samādhis are explicitly declared to have "no separate substance of their own," merely the entire preceding catalogue of dhyānas sorted into three bins by whether initial thought and sustained reflection are present, absent-but-partial, or absent entirely — the "reaching a million, gathered into a single hundred-million" simile makes the point that this is a classification scheme, not a ninth or tenth new practice. The scale of what these three samādhis are said to give rise to — over a hundred named Mahāyāna samādhis, eighty-four thousand more besides — closes the "observational gates" material (detachment, mastery, totality, sequential concentration) on the largest possible register before the fascicle's final two, most virtuosic samādhis.

The Lion's-Frolic Samādhi 師子奮迅三昧

Chinese Source

次釋師子奮迅三昧。今明師子奮迅三昧者，如《般若經》中說，行者依九次第定入師子奮迅三昧。云何名師子三昧？離欲，離不善法，有覺有觀，離生喜樂，入初禪，如是次第入二禪、三禪、四禪、空處、識處、不用處、非有想非無想處，入滅受想定，從滅受想定起，還入非有想非無想定，從非有想非無想處起，還入不用處，如是次第，還入識處，入空處，入四禪，入三禪，入二禪，入初禪，是名師子奮迅三昧也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the lion's-frolic samādhi. Here, clarifying the lion's-frolic samādhi: as explained in the Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra, the practitioner, based upon the nine sequential concentrations, enters the lion's-frolic samādhi. What is meant by the lion's samādhi? Departing from desire, departing from unwholesome dharmas, possessing initial thought and sustained reflection, departing giving rise to joy and happiness, entering the first dhyāna; and, in this same sequence, entering the second dhyāna, the third dhyāna, the fourth dhyāna, the sphere of space, the sphere of consciousness, the sphere of nothingness, and the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, entering the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception; then, rising from the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception, entering back into the concentration of neither-perception-nor-non-perception; rising from the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, entering back into the sphere of nothingness; and so, in this same sequence, entering back into the sphere of consciousness, entering the sphere of space, entering the fourth dhyāna, entering the third dhyāna, entering the second dhyāna, entering the first dhyāna — this is named the lion's-frolic samādhi.

譬如師子奮迅之時，非但能前進奮迅而去，亦能却行奮迅而歸，一切諸獸所不能爾。行者入此法門亦復如是，非但能心心次第從於初禪，直至滅受，亦能從滅受想定却入非想，入至初禪。此則義同師子奮迅，上來諸禪所不能爾，故說此定為師子奮迅三昧。行者住此法門，即能覆却，遍入一切諸禪，熏諸觀定，悉令通利，轉變自在，出生諸深三昧種種功德，神智轉勝，亦名熏禪。譬如牛皮熏熟，隨意作諸世物。此亦如是。分別次位，此同九定，但有却出無間之異。是中用心巧細修習之相，略知大意，不廣分別也。

It is like the lion, at the moment of its own frolicking leap, not merely able to bound forward in its leap, but also able to leap backward in its return — something no other beast whatsoever is able to do. The practitioner, entering this dharma-gate, is likewise so: not merely able, mind following mind in sequence, to proceed from the first dhyāna directly through to the cessation of sensation, but also able, from the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception, to turn back and enter neither-perception-nor-non-perception, all the way back to the first dhyāna. This, then, is identical in sense to the lion's own frolicking leap, something none of the dhyānas given above were able to do — hence this concentration is named the lion's-frolic samādhi.

The practitioner, abiding within this dharma-gate, is thereby able to turn back and forth, entering every dhyāna throughout completely, permeating every contemplative concentration, causing them all to flow freely, transforming at will, giving rise to every kind of merit belonging to the deepest samādhis, spiritual wisdom growing ever more excellent — also named the permeating dhyāna. It is like an ox-hide, once thoroughly permeated and softened, able to be fashioned into any worldly object at will — this, too, is just the same. Distinguishing its own stage, this is identical to the nine concentrations, differing only in its own turning back and forth without any interval. The skillful and fine application of the mind's own cultivation here is, in brief, known in its general sense, and not distinguished at greater length.

Reading note: The lion's-frolic samādhi's defining move — not just ascending through all nine sequential concentrations, but reversing direction at the very peak and descending back through them in the same unbroken sequence — earns its name from a lion's own unique capacity, unlike every other beast, to leap backward as fluidly as forward. This is the treatise's clearest image yet for what "permeating" (熏) means as distinct from "refining" (鍊): refining makes each individual stage purer in isolation, like tempering metal, while permeating works the entire sequence bidirectionally until it is uniformly pliant throughout, like an ox-hide worked until it can be shaped into anything at all.

The Samādhi of Transcendence 超越三昧

Chinese Source

次釋超越三昧。今明超越三昧者，如《般若經》中說：「行者因師子奮迅三昧，逆順出入超越三昧。云何超越三昧？離諸欲惡、不善法，有覺有觀，離生喜樂，入初禪；從初禪起，超入非有想；非有想起，入非無想處；非無想處起，入滅受想定；滅受想定起，還入初禪；從初禪起，入滅受想定；滅受想定起，入二禪；二禪起，入滅受想定；滅受想定起，入三禪；三禪起，入滅受想定；滅受想定起，入四禪；四禪起，入滅受想定；滅受想定起，入虛空處；虛空處起，入滅受想定；滅受想定起，入識處；識處起，入滅受想定；滅受想定起，入無所有處；無所有處起，入滅受想定；滅受想定起，入非有想；非有想起，入非無想處；非無想處起，入滅受想定。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining the samādhi of transcendence. Here, clarifying the samādhi of transcendence: as explained in the Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra: "The practitioner, through the lion's-frolic samādhi, enters and leaves the samādhi of transcendence, both forward and backward. What is meant by the samādhi of transcendence?

Departing from every desire, evil, and unwholesome dharma, possessing initial thought and sustained reflection, departing giving rise to joy and happiness, entering the first dhyāna; rising from the first dhyāna, transcending directly into neither-perception; rising from neither-perception, entering non-not-perception; rising from non-not-perception, entering the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception; rising from the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception, entering back into the first dhyāna; rising from the first dhyāna, entering the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception; rising from that concentration, entering the second dhyāna; rising from the second dhyāna, entering that concentration; rising from that concentration, entering the third dhyāna; rising from the third dhyāna, entering that concentration; rising from that concentration, entering the fourth dhyāna; rising from the fourth dhyāna, entering that concentration; rising from that concentration, entering the sphere of space; rising from the sphere of space, entering that concentration; rising from that concentration, entering the sphere of consciousness; rising from the sphere of consciousness, entering that concentration; rising from that concentration, entering the sphere of nothingness; rising from the sphere of nothingness, entering that concentration; rising from that concentration, entering neither-perception; rising from neither-perception, entering non-not-perception; rising from non-not-perception, entering that concentration once more.

從滅受想定起，入散心中；散心中起，入滅受想定；滅受想定起，還入散心中；散心中起，入非有想；非有想起，入非無想處；非無想處起，住散心中；散心中起，入第四禪中；第四禪中起，住散心中；散心中起，入第三禪中；第三禪中起，住散心中；散心中起，入第二禪；第二禪起，住散心中；散心中起，入初禪；初禪起，住散心中。是超越三昧。」今明超越之相，自有超入、超出相，如前二番經文說。超入、出中，各有四種：一者、順入超；二者、逆入超；三者、順逆入超；四者、逆順入超。超出亦如是。

Rising from the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception, entering the scattered mind; rising from the scattered mind, entering that concentration; rising from that concentration, entering back into the scattered mind; rising from the scattered mind, entering neither-perception; rising from neither-perception, entering non-not-perception; rising from non-not-perception, abiding in the scattered mind; rising from the scattered mind, entering the fourth dhyāna; rising from the fourth dhyāna, abiding in the scattered mind; rising from the scattered mind, entering the third dhyāna; rising from the third dhyāna, abiding in the scattered mind; rising from the scattered mind, entering the second dhyāna; rising from the second dhyāna, abiding in the scattered mind; rising from the scattered mind, entering the first dhyāna; rising from the first dhyāna, abiding in the scattered mind. This is the samādhi of transcendence."

Here, clarifying the mark of transcendence itself: there is, of itself, the mark of transcending inward and the mark of transcending outward, as explained in the two passages of scripture given above. Within transcending inward and transcending outward, each, alike, has four kinds: first, forward transcendence, entering; second, backward transcendence, entering; third, forward-then-backward transcendence, entering; fourth, backward-then-forward transcendence, entering. Transcending outward follows this same pattern as well.

復次，此超越三昧中，復有傍超。傍超亦有四種，類如前說。譬如師子有四種趠：一者、前擲四十里，即譬順超之相；二者、却擲四十里，即譬逆超之相；三者、右傍擲四十里，即譬傍超入根本禪定之相；四者、左傍擲四十里，即譬傍超入觀禪之相。復有二種超越：一者、具足超；二者、不具足超。具足超，即是菩薩超越，如上所說。不具足超，即是聲聞超越三昧，不能自在，遠超入故。故《摩訶衍》云：「譬如黃師子、白師子，二俱能趠。若黃師子趠，則不遠；若白師子，則能遠擲。聲聞之人入超越三昧，但能從初禪超入三禪，尚不能超二，何能超三？此則如黃師子之超。菩薩不爾，從於初禪逈能超入滅受想定，隨意自在，此則如白師子之超。」

Furthermore, within this very samādhi of transcendence, there is, further, sideways transcendence as well. Sideways transcendence, too, has four kinds, following the same pattern as already given above. It is like the lion, which has four kinds of leap: first, leaping forward forty li — this illustrates the mark of forward transcendence; second, leaping backward forty li — this illustrates the mark of backward transcendence; third, leaping sideways to the right forty li — this illustrates the mark of sideways transcendence into the root dhyāna concentrations; fourth, leaping sideways to the left forty li — this illustrates the mark of sideways transcendence into contemplative dhyāna.

There are, further, two kinds of transcendence: first, complete transcendence; second, incomplete transcendence. Complete transcendence is precisely the bodhisattva's own transcendence, as already explained above. Incomplete transcendence is precisely the śrāvaka's own samādhi of transcendence, unable to move freely, since it can transcend across only a short distance. Hence the Mahāyāna scriptures say: "It is like the yellow lion and the white lion, both, alike, able to leap. Should the yellow lion leap, it does not go far; should the white lion leap, it is able to leap a great distance. The śrāvaka, entering the samādhi of transcendence, is able only to transcend from the first dhyāna directly into the third — unable even to transcend across two stages, how much less three? This, then, is like the yellow lion's own leap. The bodhisattva is not so: from the first dhyāna, he is able to transcend directly all the way into the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception, moving freely at will — this, then, is like the white lion's own leap."

Reading note: The samādhi of transcendence takes the lion's-frolic samādhi's back-and-forth motion and adds true discontinuity — leaping directly from the first dhyāna to neither-perception-nor-non-perception, skipping every intermediate stage, and then, in the sequence's second half, alternating unpredictably between deep concentration and "the scattered mind" (the ordinary waking state) itself, treated here as just one more station in the sequence rather than a lapse from it. The yellow-lion/white-lion simile closing the section is the treatise's sharpest image yet for the difference between śrāvaka and bodhisattva attainment of the identical samādhi: not a different practice, but a different range — the śrāvaka's leap reaches only the third dhyāna, while the bodhisattva's reaches all the way to the cessation of sensation and perception in a single bound.

Should a practitioner of the three vehicles enter this samādhi, becoming complete in the cultivation of every dharma-gate, at that time the dharmas of contemplation and concentration alike grow ever deeper, brighter, and sharper, further giving rise to a hundred, a thousand samādhis, their merit deep and abundant, their supernormal power fierce and sharp — hence this is named contemplative dhyāna.

Furthermore, within all these dhyānas there are, present throughout, the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment, the three liberation-gates, the Four Noble Truths' own sixteen practices of contemplation, the eleven kinds of wisdom, and the three undefiled faculties — wisdom and practice of this kind shall be distinguished further below.

Should this be a bodhisattva, entering this samādhi within the correctly contemplating mind, obtaining the equal mark of every dharma, he thereby obtains the twenty-five samādhis, able to break through the twenty-five states of existence, abiding in the king of samādhis, every samādhi entirely entering within it — at this time, this is also named the pāramitā of dhyāna, fulfilled and complete.

This, then, briefly concludes the practice-dharma-gates of dhyāna common to the three vehicles. Within this, the dharma-gates are without limit — how, then, could one who wishes to explain further the essential practices for entering the path ever hope to set them all forth in full?

Reading note: This closing paragraph functions as a summary index of nearly every advanced attainment named across fascicles 8 through 10 — the summit dhyāna, the buddha-wisdom samādhi, the samādhi free of contention, the four kinds of eloquence, the supernormal powers and transformations — gathered together as the reward of the non-destructive path's full completion, contrasted one final time with the destructive path's own two kinds of nirvāṇa from fascicle 9. The treatise's very last line is worth sitting with: rather than closing with a triumphant flourish, it closes with a question — "how could one who wishes to explain further the essential practices for entering the path ever hope to set them all forth in full?" — an author's own acknowledgment, in the text's final surviving words, that the subject has outrun the book.

Status of this fascicle — and of the treatise as a whole: Fascicle 10 is now complete, and with it, this translation project reaches the end of the Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen as it survives today. This fascicle covers the non-destructive path (不壞法道) in full: the eight forms of detachment (八背捨), the eight stations of mastery (八勝處), and the ten totalities (十一切處) — the three "contemplative dhyāna" gates — followed by their function (the six supernormal powers and fourteen transformations), then the nine sequential concentrations (九次第定, "refining dhyāna"), the three samādhis (三三昧), the lion's-frolic samādhi (師子奮迅三昧, "permeating dhyāna"), and the samādhi of transcendence (超越三昧, "cultivating dhyāna"). This closes dhyāna that is purely world-transcending (出世間禪), topic 7's third major division, in full.

Zhiyi's own opening outline, given in fascicle 1, announced ten topics for this treatise. The text as it has come down to us completes topic 7's first three divisions (mundane dhyāna, dhyāna both mundane and world-transcending, and world-transcending dhyāna) but never reaches topic 7's fourth division (dhyāna that is neither mundane nor world-transcending) or topics 8 through 10 (the teachings dhyāna gives rise to, dhyāna's own fruits of retribution, and dhyāna's ultimate destination). Fascicle 10's own closing line — "the dharma-gates are without limit; how could one hope to explain them all in full?" — reads, in context, as the treatise's own frank acknowledgment of its incompleteness, consistent with the long-standing scholarly understanding that Zhiyi never finished this particular work. Nothing in the received Taishō text (T46, no. 1916) continues past this point.

This translation project accordingly closes here as well, ten fascicles in — the first complete English rendering, so far as this project is aware, of the whole of Zhiyi's Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen as it has survived, from its opening framing of the ten topics in fascicle 1 through the non-destructive path's own closing samādhis here in fascicle 10.
