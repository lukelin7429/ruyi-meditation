The Four Immeasurable Minds and the Formless Concentrations

This page presents Ru-Yi's own English translation of fascicle 6 of Zhiyi's Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen (釋禪波羅蜜次第法門). Having covered the four dhyānas in fascicle 5, the treatise completes mundane dhyāna's remaining two sections: the four immeasurable minds — loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity — and the four formless concentrations, from the sphere of boundless space through neither perception nor non-perception.

Explaining Dhyāna-Pāramitā's Cultivation and Realization, Topic 7, Part 2

Explaining the four immeasurable minds: this opens into five parts — first, clarifying their sequence; second, explaining their name; third, clarifying where they are cultivated; fourth, clarifying their cultivation and realization; and fifth, clarifying their merit.

Why the Immeasurable Minds Follow the Four Dhyānas 四無量心 · 明次第

Chinese Source

第一、釋四無量心所以次四禪後者，明行人有二種：一者、世間行人；二者、出世間行人。就凡夫行人中，則有三：一者、樂高勝自在，求作梵天王，是故雖得四禪，而更進修無量心。何以故？然四禪但是色界，自行具足，而無益他之德淺薄。若生彼天，不得王領。若修四無量心，緣於十方眾生而入三昧，慈悲普攝，利他心大，是故功德轉多。若生彼天，必作梵天王，王領自在，是故能得四禪，猶更修習四無量心。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining why the four immeasurable minds come after the four dhyānas: this is clarified with reference to two kinds of practitioner — worldly practitioners; and world-transcending practitioners.

Among ordinary, worldly practitioners, there are, in turn, three kinds. First, those who delight in a lofty, sovereign freedom, seeking to become the Great Brahmā King — such a person, although already having attained the four dhyānas, cultivates the immeasurable minds still further. Why is this? Because the four dhyānas alone belong only to the form realm, complete in their own self-cultivation, but shallow and slight in any merit that benefits others. Were one to be born into that heaven on the strength of the four dhyānas alone, one would not thereby rule as its king. But if one cultivates the four immeasurable minds, entering samādhi with the ten directions' own sentient beings as its object, loving-kindness and compassion embracing all universally, the mind of benefiting others thereby made great — the merit is correspondingly far greater. Reborn into that heaven on this strength, one is certain to become the Great Brahmā King, ruling there in sovereign freedom — hence, even having already attained the four dhyānas, one cultivates the four immeasurable minds still further.

二者、外道行人雖得四禪，而見有心識之患，欲求涅槃無想寂滅，不知破色，直用邪智滅心，入無想定。三者、或有凡夫外道行人悉厭患色，猶如牢獄，一心破色，修四空定，是為凡夫行人同得此定，志樂不同，各隨所習，愛樂不同。若佛弟子，有二種人，所謂小、大兩乘。是二種人得四禪時，進修無量心者，小乘之人為自調心，增長福德，易得涅槃故；大乘之人欲度眾生，必以大悲為本，故次四禪，明修四無量心。

Second, non-Buddhist practitioners: although they, too, attain the four dhyānas, they come to see a defect in the very presence of mind and consciousness, and, wishing to seek nirvāṇa as a thoughtless cessation, not knowing how to break through form, simply use a deviant wisdom to extinguish the mind instead, entering the concentration of no-thought.

Third, there are ordinary, non-Buddhist practitioners who grow entirely weary of form, as though it were a prison, and, single-mindedly breaking through form, cultivate the four formless concentrations. These, then, are ordinary practitioners who attain these same concentrations in common, yet whose aspirations and delights differ, each following their own habituation, their own objects of attachment differing from one another.

As for the Buddha's own disciples, there are two kinds of person, namely the two vehicles, lesser and greater. These two kinds of person, upon attaining the four dhyānas, go on to cultivate the immeasurable minds further as well: the practitioner of the lesser vehicle does so in order to regulate their own mind and increase merit, so that nirvāṇa may be more easily attained; the practitioner of the greater vehicle, wishing to liberate sentient beings, must necessarily take great compassion as the very root of this — hence, following the four dhyānas, the cultivation of the four immeasurable minds is now clarified.

問曰：如《摩訶衍》中假設問云：「是四禪中有四無量及十一切入等諸定，今何故別說？答曰：雖四禪中皆有是法，若不別說，人則不知其功德。譬如囊中有寶，若不示人，即無人知者。若欲示大福德，為說四無量心；患厭色如牢獄，為說四無色定；於緣中不得自在觀所緣，為說八勝處；若有遮道，不得通達，為說八背捨；心不調柔，不能從禪起，次第入禪，為說九次第定；不能得一切緣遍照隨意，為說十一切處。」

Question: as the Mahāyāna treatise poses this very question: "Within the four dhyānas themselves, the four immeasurable minds, the ten totality-bases, and other such concentrations are already present — why, then, are they spoken of separately?" Answer: although these dharmas are indeed all already present within the four dhyānas, were they not spoken of separately, people would have no way of knowing their own particular merit. It is like a treasure kept within a sack: unless it is shown to someone, no one will ever know it is there. Wishing to display great merit, the four immeasurable minds are spoken of; being troubled by and weary of form as though it were a prison, the four formless concentrations are spoken of; not yet being free and at ease in contemplating one's own object within concentration, the eight stations of mastery are spoken of; where the path is obstructed and cannot be penetrated through, the eight liberations are spoken of; where the mind is not yet pliant and soft, unable to arise from one dhyāna and enter the next in due sequence, the nine sequential concentrations are spoken of; and where one is not yet able to illuminate every object at will, the ten totality-bases are spoken of."

問曰：若以論說，今得四禪者，亦應悉得四無量等諸禪定否？答曰：此依義而說，若無漏四禪中說有四無量心，則於義無過。何以故？無漏禪中具諸觀行法門故。若有漏根本禪，說者當知乳中說酪耳。

Question: if the treatise says this, then should one who has now attained the four dhyānas also be understood to have thereby already attained the four immeasurable minds and the other such concentrations as well? Answer: this is spoken of with reference to a particular sense. If it is the uncontaminated four dhyānas that are said to already contain the four immeasurable minds, this is indeed without fault, since the uncontaminated dhyānas are already complete with every contemplative dharma-gate within them. But if it is the contaminated root dhyānas that are meant, then one should know that this is like speaking of butter as already present within milk.

Reading note: The three kinds of ordinary practitioner sketched here — the status-seeker aiming for kingship over Brahmā's heaven, the non-Buddhist practitioner who mistakes extinguishing the mind for nirvāṇa, and the practitioner who treats form itself as a prison to escape — quietly set up two false paths (the coming aside on 無想定, deviant no-thought concentration, and the four formless concentrations both trace back to these same motives) against which the properly Buddhist cultivation of the immeasurable minds, and later the formless concentrations, will be contrasted. The milk-and-butter simile is a precise piece of reasoning: something can be truly "already present" in a potential sense (butter in milk) without yet being present in an actualized sense — exactly the distinction between the uncontaminated four dhyānas (where the immeasurable minds are already implicit) and the ordinary, contaminated four dhyānas (where they still must be separately cultivated).

Naming the Four Immeasurable Minds 四無量心 · 釋名

Chinese Source

第二、釋四無量名者：一、慈無量心者，慈名愛念眾生，常求樂事以饒益之；二、悲無量心者，悲名愍念眾生，受五道中種種身苦；三、喜無量心者，喜名欲令眾生從樂得歡喜；四、捨無量心者，捨三種心，但念眾生，不憎不愛。緣此四法故，說於四心，遍十方平等無隔，名無量心。修慈心，為除眾生中瞋覺故；修悲心，為除眾生中惱覺故；修喜心，為除眾生中不悅樂故；修捨心，為除眾生中憎愛故。此四定次第階級之相，在下當釋。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the name of the four immeasurable minds: first, the immeasurable mind of loving-kindness — loving-kindness means holding sentient beings in loving regard, constantly seeking what brings them happiness, in order to benefit them. Second, the immeasurable mind of compassion — compassion means holding sentient beings in pitying regard, aware of the various bodily sufferings they undergo across the five realms. Third, the immeasurable mind of sympathetic joy — sympathetic joy means wishing sentient beings to attain delight through their own happiness. Fourth, the immeasurable mind of equanimity — equanimity means relinquishing these three preceding states of mind, simply holding sentient beings in mind without either aversion or attachment.

Taking these four dharmas as their object, four states of mind are spoken of, pervading the ten directions equally, without any division between them — hence they are named the immeasurable minds. Cultivating loving-kindness serves to remove the disturbance of anger among sentient beings; cultivating compassion serves to remove the disturbance of vexation among sentient beings; cultivating sympathetic joy serves to remove displeasure among sentient beings; and cultivating equanimity serves to remove aversion and attachment among sentient beings. The mark of these four concentrations' own graduated sequence will be explained below.

Where the Immeasurable Minds Are Cultivated 四無量心 · 明處所

Chinese Source

第三、明修處所，自有二種：一、為通明處；二者、別明處。第一、通明處者，四禪、中間定悉得修四無量心，如《釋論》中說：「是慈在色界、根本禪，亦在禪中間。」無色界無色，於緣眾生為不便，欲界、未到地定淺，不任修諸功德。問曰：欲界、未到地，利根之人能用此定發見思真解，何故不得修四無量心？答曰：緣理之慧利，故得發。若神通無量等，是事法，必假深定，而欲界、未到非全不得修無量心，但發得，即屬初禪，是故不說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying where they are cultivated: this itself has two kinds — the general locus; and the particular locus.

First, as for the general locus: the four dhyānas and the intermediate dhyānas are all places where the four immeasurable minds may be cultivated, as the Exegetical Treatise says: "Loving-kindness is present within the form realm's root dhyānas, and within the intermediate dhyānas as well." The formless realm has no form, and so is unsuited to taking sentient beings as an object; and desire-realm concentration and the "not-yet-arrived" state are too shallow to sustain the cultivation of such merit.

Question: practitioners of sharp faculty within the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state are able to use this very concentration to bring forth the true understanding of the path of vision and cultivation — why, then, can the four immeasurable minds not be cultivated there? Answer: it is the sharpness of wisdom directed at principle that allows this to be brought forth there. But supernormal powers, the immeasurable minds, and the like are matters of actual practice, and necessarily depend upon deep concentration; it is not that the desire realm and the "not-yet-arrived" state are entirely incapable of cultivating the immeasurable minds at all, only that, whenever they are brought forth there, they properly belong to the first dhyāna already — hence this is not spoken of separately.

如初禪五支、覺觀二支分別欲界，則生悲易，喜支生喜易，樂支生慈易，一心支生捨易，故說為修證之處。問曰：第四禪及中間無喜樂，云何以喜樂與眾生？答曰：內雖無有喜樂，緣取外喜樂人相而平等與樂。譬如離欲行人，自不須五塵，亦不與塵欲交染，而為大福德故，亦以五欲勝妙樂具給施前人，而於自心無所染污。於四禪中與他喜樂亦復如是。未到、中間類即可解。

As with the first dhyāna's five factors: the two factors of initial thought and sustained reflection, distinguishing the desire realm, make compassion easy to bring forth; the factor of joy makes sympathetic joy easy to bring forth; the factor of happiness makes loving-kindness easy to bring forth; and the factor of one-pointedness of mind makes equanimity easy to bring forth — hence these are spoken of as the proper places for their cultivation and realization.

Question: the fourth dhyāna and the intermediate dhyāna have no joy or happiness at all — how, then, can joy and happiness be given to sentient beings there? Answer: although there is no joy or happiness present within, one takes up the mark of another's outward joy and happiness as one's object, and bestows this happiness equally. It is like a practitioner free of desire, who, having no need of the five sense-objects for themselves, and no longer mingling with or stained by desire for these objects, may nonetheless, for the sake of great merit, still bestow the finest and most excellent pleasures of the five desires upon another person, all without the practitioner's own mind being stained by it in the least. Bestowing joy and happiness upon others within the fourth dhyāna is exactly the same. The "not-yet-arrived" state and the intermediate dhyāna may be understood by the same pattern.

第二、別明修處者，如初禪以覺觀為主，深識欲界眾生苦惱之相，此處修悲則易。二禪內有大喜，此處修喜無量則易。三禪內有遍身之樂，此處修慈則易。四禪妙捨莊嚴，此處修捨為易。此則隨地各有其便。問曰：若爾，佛何故說住四禪修四無量易得耶？答曰：第四禪名念清淨，得不動定，於此中修一切佛法功德易成，故作是說耳。問曰：上說初禪行悲，此則壞於次第。如慈在前，應初禪而修慈，二禪修悲，三禪修喜，四禪修捨，何故不爾？答曰：此逐義便，不隨次第。譬如佛十弟子各有第一，若問何人智慧第一，應答身子是。若以夏臘大而答第一者，則於義大僻。

Second, clarifying the particular locus: within the first dhyāna, initial thought and sustained reflection are its ruling factors, and deeply recognize the desire realm's own sentient beings and the mark of their suffering — hence compassion is easy to cultivate here. The second dhyāna has great joy present within it — hence sympathetic joy is easy to cultivate here. The third dhyāna has happiness pervading the whole body present within it — hence loving-kindness is easy to cultivate here. The fourth dhyāna is adorned with subtle equanimity — hence equanimity is easy to cultivate here. This, then, is a matter of each stage having its own particular convenience.

Question: if this is so, why does the Buddha say that abiding in the fourth dhyāna makes the four immeasurable minds easy to attain? Answer: the fourth dhyāna is named purity of mindfulness, attaining the unmoving concentration — within this stage, every merit of the Buddhadharma is easily accomplished, and so it is spoken of in this way.

Question: it was said above that compassion is practiced within the first dhyāna — does this not disrupt the proper sequence? Since loving-kindness comes first, should not loving-kindness be cultivated in the first dhyāna, compassion in the second, sympathetic joy in the third, and equanimity in the fourth — why is it not so? Answer: this follows whatever is most fitting to the sense at hand, not a fixed sequence. It is like the Buddha's ten chief disciples, each foremost in his own respect: if asked who is foremost in wisdom, the answer should be Śāriputra; but were one to answer according to seniority in ordination instead, this would be a serious error of sense.

Reading note: The pairing between each dhyāna's own dominant factor and the immeasurable mind it makes "easy" is a genuinely elegant piece of structural matching: initial thought and sustained reflection's raw awareness of others' suffering suits compassion; the second dhyāna's excited joy suits sympathetic joy; the third dhyāna's pervasive happiness suits loving-kindness; the fourth dhyāna's settled equanimity suits equanimity. That this "easy" sequence (compassion, joy, loving-kindness, equanimity) doesn't match the standard naming order (loving-kindness, compassion, joy, equanimity) is explicitly flagged and defended with the Śāriputra example — a reminder, consistent with this treatise's handling of other apparent contradictions, that different orderings can each be correct relative to a different organizing question.

Cultivating and Realizing Loving-Kindness 四無量心 · 修慈證慈

Chinese Source

第四、正明修證。約四無量心，即自有四：一、修慈證慈；二、修悲證悲；三、修喜證喜：四、修捨證捨。第一、明修慈證慈者，即開為二：第一、正明修習方法，此如佛處處經中說：「有比丘以慈相應心，無恚無恨，無怨無惱，廣大無量，善修習。」云何名以慈相應心？如《釋論》說：「若念十方眾生令得樂時，心數法中生法，名為慈。」善是相應，欲入禪定，當先作誓願：「一切眾生悉受快樂，我於定中悉得見受、想、行、識。」是名心數法。諸身業、口業及心不相應諸行是法和合，皆名為慈。是法皆以慈為主，故慈得名。譬如一切心數法皆是後世因緣，而但思得名，於作業中，思最有力故，是名慈相應相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, directly clarifying cultivation and realization. With reference to the four immeasurable minds, this itself has four parts: cultivating and realizing loving-kindness; cultivating and realizing compassion; cultivating and realizing sympathetic joy; and cultivating and realizing equanimity.

First, clarifying the cultivation and realization of loving-kindness: this opens into two parts. First, directly clarifying the method of cultivation: this is exactly as the Buddha says throughout the sūtras, everywhere: "There is a monk who, with a mind corresponding to loving-kindness, free of anger, free of resentment, free of enmity, free of vexation, vast and immeasurable, cultivates it well." What is meant by "a mind corresponding to loving-kindness"? As the Exegetical Treatise says: "Whenever one holds the ten directions' sentient beings in mind, wishing them to attain happiness, the dharma that arises within the mental factors is what is named loving-kindness." Being properly corresponding, one wishing to enter dhyāna-concentration should first make this vow: "May all sentient beings receive complete happiness — may I, within concentration, come to see this in every one of the aggregates of sensation, perception, formation, and consciousness." This is what is named the mental factor. Every bodily action, verbal action, and every activity not directly corresponding with the mind, this dharma taken together as a whole, is named loving-kindness. Since this dharma, in every case, takes loving-kindness as its ruling factor, it receives the name "loving-kindness" accordingly. It is like every mental factor being, in its own right, a cause and condition for a future life, and yet receiving its name simply from intention alone, since, among the various karmic actions, intention is the most powerful of all — this is the mark of a mind corresponding to loving-kindness.

復次，行者初修時，用念清淨心，取外所愛親人受樂之相，若父母兄弟隨取一最愛者，一心緣之。若有異念，攝之令還，令於心想的的分明，見於親人受樂之相。其心愛念，乃至中人、怨人，餘五道亦如是。復次，行者如是修時，若見種種善惡境界及發諸禪中事，悉不得取，但一心觀於親人得樂之相，心心相續。是則略說修慈方法。

Furthermore: the practitioner, at the outset of cultivation, uses a mind of pure mindfulness to take up the mark of an outwardly loved one's own happiness — if it is a parent or a sibling, taking up whichever one is most beloved, fixing the mind single-mindedly upon them. Should some other thought arise, one gathers the mind back in, making the mental image entirely clear and distinct, seeing the mark of this loved one's own happiness. This loving regard of mind is then extended, in turn, to neutral persons and even to enemies, and to the remaining five realms as well, following the same pattern. Furthermore: the practitioner, cultivating in this way, should, even upon seeing various wholesome or unwholesome states, or matters arising from within the various dhyānas, take up none of them at all — simply contemplating, single-mindedly, the mark of the loved one's own happiness, mind following mind in unbroken continuity. This, in brief, describes the method for cultivating loving-kindness.

第二、明慈定發相。行者禪定智慧、福德善根力清淨故，如是一心慈念眾生時，三昧即發。三昧力故，即於定心中見所愛親人受於快樂之相，身心悅豫，顏色和適，了了分明。如是見親人得樂已，次見中人乃至怨人亦復如是。於定心中見一人，次見於十人、千人、萬億、一聚落、一國土、一閻浮提、一四天下，乃至十方世界一切眾生，悉皆受樂。行者於定中見外人受樂，而內定轉深，與外相應，湛然無動，是名慈相應心。即是相應受、想、行、識、陰、入、界等法，如前說。問曰：慈相應定見眾生時，為當如上說，從一至十漸漸而見，為當一時併見？答曰：行者根有漸、頓、不定。

Second, clarifying the mark of the loving-kindness concentration's own arising. The practitioner, through the pure power of dhyāna-concentration, wisdom, merit, and wholesome roots, holding sentient beings single-mindedly in loving-kindness in this way, samādhi is thereby brought forth. Through the power of this samādhi, one sees, within the concentrated mind, the mark of the loved one's own happiness, body and mind delighted, countenance at ease, entirely clear and distinct. Having seen the loved one attain happiness in this way, one next sees the same for a neutral person, and then even for an enemy as well. Within the concentrated mind, one sees a single person; then one sees ten people, a thousand, ten thousand, a hundred million; a single village; a single country; a single Jambudvīpa; a single set of the four continents; and, further still, every sentient being throughout the worlds of the ten directions, all alike receiving happiness.

The practitioner, seeing others receiving happiness within concentration, while the inward concentration itself grows ever deeper, corresponding with what is seen without, deep and unmoving — this is named the mind corresponding with loving-kindness. This is precisely the corresponding dharma of sensation, perception, formation, and consciousness, the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements, exactly as already described above. Question: within the concentration corresponding with loving-kindness, when sentient beings are seen, is it exactly as described above, seen gradually, one at a time up through ten, or are they all seen together, all at once? Answer: practitioners' own faculties differ — gradual, sudden, or unfixed.

一種慈相應心者，慈名心數法，能除心中憒濁，所謂瞋、恨、慳、貪煩惱。譬如淨水明珠置濁水中，水即澄清。無恚無恨，無怨無惱者，於眾生中，若有因緣，若無因緣，初生名為瞋；瞋增長籌量，持著心中而未決了，是名為恨，亦名為怨；若心已定，無所畏忌，欲損於他，是名為惱。以慈心力除、捨、離此三事，是名無瞋無恨，無怨無惱。此無瞋無恨，無怨無惱，以是讚歎慈心功德。

As for the single mind corresponding with loving-kindness: loving-kindness, being a mental factor, is able to remove the mind's own turbidity — namely, the afflictions of anger, resentment, stinginess, and craving. It is like a clear-water pearl placed into turbid water: the water is thereby made clear at once. As for being free of anger, free of resentment, free of enmity, and free of vexation: toward sentient beings, whether for some cause or condition, or for none at all, the initial arising of ill will is named anger; anger, growing and deliberating further, held fixed within the mind without yet being resolved, is named resentment, also called enmity; and, once the mind has become fixed on it, with no fear or restraint left, wishing to harm the other, this is named vexation. Through the power of loving-kindness, removing, relinquishing, and leaving behind these three matters is what is named being free of anger, free of resentment, free of enmity, and free of vexation. These qualities are given here in praise of loving-kindness's own merit.

廣大無量者，一心分別，有二種名：如慈相緣見一方為廣，四方為大，緣四維及上下為無量。復次，破瞋恨心名為廣，破怨心名為大，破惱心名為無量。慈緣親人為廣，慈緣中人為大，慈緣怨人得福多，故名無量。復次，為狹緣故，名為廣。為小緣故，名為大。為有量緣，故名無量。善修者，是慈心牢固。初得慈，不名為善修；非但愛念眾生中，非但好眾生中，非但益一眾生中，非但一方眾生中，名為善修。行者於上親、中親、下親、上中人、中中人、下中人、下怨、中怨、上怨，是九種人中，愛憎正等無異，乃至愛念五道眾生中，以一慈心視之如父如母，如兄弟、子姪、知識，常求好事，欲令利益安樂，如是之心遍滿十方，是名善修。復次，若但與眾生欲界樂，不名善修；但與初禪樂，不名善修；但與二禪樂，不名善修。若能具足與欲界樂，乃至三、四禪樂，是名善修。

As for "vast and immeasurable": distinguished single-mindedly, this carries two kinds of name. In terms of loving-kindness's own object: taking a single direction as its object is "vast"; taking all four directions is "great"; and taking the four intermediate directions together with above and below is "immeasurable." Furthermore: breaking through anger and resentment is named "vast"; breaking through enmity is named "great"; and breaking through vexation is named "immeasurable." Loving-kindness taking a loved one as its object is "vast"; taking a neutral person as its object is "great"; and taking an enemy as its object, gaining the greater merit thereby, is named "immeasurable." Furthermore: because the object is narrow, it is named "vast"; because the object is small, it is named "great"; and because the object has become truly boundless, it is named "immeasurable."

As for "cultivated well": this means loving-kindness of mind made secure and firm. Merely first attaining loving-kindness is not yet named cultivating it well; loving-kindness confined only to loving certain sentient beings, only to those one already likes, only to benefiting a single sentient being, or only to sentient beings in a single direction, cannot be named cultivating it well either. The practitioner, among these nine kinds of person — the highest, middle, and lowest grade of loved one; the highest, middle, and lowest grade of neutral person; and the lowest, middle, and highest grade of enemy — holds love and aversion exactly equal, with no distinction at all between them, extending this loving regard even to sentient beings across all five realms, viewing each with this same single mind of loving-kindness as though they were one's own father, one's own mother, one's own sibling, child, nephew or niece, or close friend, constantly seeking what is good for them, wishing to bring them benefit and peace — such a mind as this, pervading all ten directions completely, is what is named cultivating it well. Furthermore: bestowing upon sentient beings only the happiness of the desire realm cannot be named cultivating it well; bestowing only the happiness of the first dhyāna cannot be named cultivating it well; bestowing only the happiness of the second dhyāna cannot be named cultivating it well either. Only if one is able to bestow, in full completeness, the happiness of the desire realm all the way through the happiness of the third and fourth dhyānas, is this named cultivating it well.

如是慈心，名眾生緣。或在凡夫人行處，或有學人未漏盡者亦行此悲為調心，得大福德，入無漏故。法緣者，諸漏盡阿羅漢、辟支佛、諸佛，是諸聖人破吾我相，滅一異相故，但觀從因緣相續生。以慈念眾生時，從和合因緣相續，但空五陰即是眾生，念是五陰。此慈念眾生不知是法空，眾生常一心欲得樂，聖人愍之，令隨意得樂，為世俗法故，名為法緣。無緣者，是慈但諸佛有。何以故？諸佛不住有為、無為性中，不依上、下、過去、未來、現在，知諸因緣為不實，顛倒虛誑，故心無所緣。佛以眾生不知是諸法實相，往來五道，心著諸法，而分別取捨，以是諸法實相智慧令眾生得之，是為無緣。譬如給濟貧人，或與財物、金銀、寶物，或與如意神珠。眾生緣、法緣、無緣亦復如是。此義如《摩訶衍》中廣說。

Loving-kindness of this kind is named loving-kindness that takes sentient beings as its object. This may be practiced by an ordinary person, or by a trainee who has not yet exhausted the contaminants, cultivating this compassion in order to regulate the mind, gaining great merit, and thereby entering the uncontaminated.

As for loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object: arhats who have exhausted every contaminant, pratyekabuddhas, and buddhas — these noble ones, having broken through the mark of self and other, and extinguished the mark of sameness and difference, contemplate only the continuous arising of things through causes and conditions. When holding sentient beings in loving-kindness, they see only the continuity of causes and conditions combining together — the empty five aggregates are themselves precisely what "sentient being" refers to, and it is these five aggregates that are held in mind. Sentient beings, not knowing that this dharma is empty, constantly wish single-mindedly to attain happiness; the noble ones, taking pity on them, allow them to attain happiness according to their own wish, for the sake of this same conventional dharma — hence this is named loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object.

As for objectless loving-kindness: this loving-kindness belongs only to the buddhas. Why is this? The buddhas do not dwell within the nature of either the conditioned or the unconditioned, do not rely upon high or low, past, future, or present, and know that all causes and conditions are unreal, inverted and illusory — hence the mind has nothing at all that it takes as an object. The Buddha, because sentient beings do not know the true mark of all dharmas, and so come and go through the five realms, the mind clinging to dharmas, discriminating, taking up and rejecting — through this very wisdom of the true mark of all dharmas, causes sentient beings to attain it as well: this is objectless loving-kindness. It is like giving relief to the poor: one might give them wealth and goods, gold, silver, and jewels; or one might give them a wish-fulfilling spirit-jewel instead. Loving-kindness that takes sentient beings as its object, loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object, and objectless loving-kindness are exactly the same in this respect. This sense is given at length within the Mahāyāna treatise.

復次，眾生緣慈，但見受果報樂相；法緣慈，則見受諸法門及涅槃樂相；無緣慈，則見一切同是佛性常樂平等相。復次，眾生緣慈，則在根本禪中；法緣慈，多在特勝、通明、背捨諸無漏禪中；無緣慈，多是首楞嚴、法華三昧及九種禪中。

Furthermore: loving-kindness that takes sentient beings as its object sees only the mark of happiness received as a karmic result; loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object sees the mark of happiness received through the various dharma-gates and nirvāṇa itself; and objectless loving-kindness sees the mark that all things alike share the same buddha-nature, permanent, blissful, and equal. Furthermore: loving-kindness that takes sentient beings as its object belongs to the root dhyānas; loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object belongs, for the most part, to the special-excellences, the thorough-and-bright contemplation, the liberations, and the other uncontaminated dhyānas; and objectless loving-kindness belongs, for the most part, to the Śūraṅgama, the Lotus samādhi, and the nine kinds of dhyāna.

Reading note: The three-tier structure here (sentient-being-object, dharma-object, objectless) directly parallels fascicle 3's own three-tier scheme for loving-kindness within the internal expedient means' wholesome-root marks — confirmation that this is a stable structure the treatise applies consistently wherever loving-kindness is analyzed in depth, not a one-off scheme. The wish-fulfilling-jewel-versus-ordinary-wealth simile makes a point worth carrying forward: all three levels are genuinely loving-kindness, differing only in the depth of what they're actually able to give — conventional happiness, versus the dharma-gates themselves, versus the very wisdom that dissolves the distinction between giver and given. The closing fire-by-friction simile addresses a real objection: how can contemplating all beings as "receiving happiness" be anything but self-deception given how much of the world plainly suffers? The treatise's answer is that this is a deliberately cultivated skillful orientation, not a factual claim about anyone's present circumstances — and it holds only because suffering and happiness are themselves impermanent for every being across the five realms.

Chinese Source

第二、釋修證悲，即為二：一者、正明修悲方法，如佛說：「若有比丘以悲相應心，無瞋無恨，無怨無惱，廣大無量，善修。」悲相應心者，行者於慈定中常念欲與眾生樂，從慈定起，猶見眾生受種種身苦、心苦，心生憐愍，即作是念：「眾生可念，莫令受是種種身苦、心苦。」復更念言：「我今無目，五道之中親、中、怨人並受種種身心諸苦，而我不知不見，長夜懈怠，不生救拔之心。」作是念已，即發願言：「若有眾生受種種苦，我於定中悉願得見，勤加救護。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the cultivation and realization of compassion: this itself has two parts.

First, directly clarifying the method of cultivating compassion, as the Buddha says: "There is a monk who, with a mind corresponding to compassion, free of anger, free of resentment, free of enmity, free of vexation, vast and immeasurable, cultivates it well." As for the mind corresponding with compassion: the practitioner, within the concentration of loving-kindness, constantly holds the wish to give sentient beings happiness; arising from this concentration of loving-kindness, one nonetheless still sees sentient beings undergoing various kinds of bodily and mental suffering, and a mind of pity arises, reflecting: "Sentient beings are worthy of loving regard — may they not have to undergo these various kinds of bodily and mental suffering." One reflects further still: "I have, until now, been without eyes — throughout the five realms, loved ones, neutral persons, and enemies alike all undergo these various kinds of bodily and mental suffering, and yet I, neither knowing nor seeing it, have spent the long night in idleness, never giving rise to a mind that would rescue them." Having reflected in this way, one then makes this vow: "Should there be sentient beings undergoing various kinds of suffering, may I, within concentration, come to see this in every case, and diligently apply myself to rescuing and protecting them."

作是願已，即入禪定，用定念淨心先取一所愛親人受苦之相，繫心緣之。若有異念，攝之令還，令於心想的的分明，其心憐愍，悲念無極，如是乃至中、怨、憎一方，乃至十方，一道乃至五道亦如是，是則略說修悲方法。二、明悲定發相。行者福德智慧善根清淨，作是觀時，三昧便發，即於定中見於親人受苦之相，了了分明，其心悲愍，欲加救護。既見親人受苦，生憐愍心已，次見中人、怨人如是，乃至十方、五道眾生受苦之相。行者於定心中見外人受苦，而內心憐愍，從悲定起，心轉深固，定心與外相應，湛然無動，是名悲相應心。無瞋無恨，無怨無惱，廣大無量，皆如上說。

Having made this vow, one then enters dhyāna-concentration, using the pure mind of concentration and mindfulness to first take up the mark of a single loved one's own suffering, fixing the mind upon it as its object. Should some other thought arise, one gathers the mind back in, making the mental image entirely clear and distinct, the mind full of pity, compassionate regard without limit — extending, in this way, to a neutral person, an enemy, one to be hated, then to a single direction, and, further still, to all ten directions; to a single realm, and, further still, to all five realms, following the same pattern. This, in brief, describes the method for cultivating compassion.

Second, clarifying the mark of the compassion concentration's own arising. The practitioner, through the purity of merit, wisdom, and wholesome roots, contemplating in this way, samādhi is thereupon brought forth; one sees, within concentration, the mark of the loved one's own suffering, entirely clear and distinct, the mind full of compassionate pity, wishing to rescue and protect them. Having seen the loved one undergoing suffering, and a mind of pity having arisen, one next sees the same for a neutral person and an enemy, and, further still, for sentient beings throughout the ten directions and all five realms undergoing suffering. The practitioner, seeing others undergoing suffering within the concentrated mind, while the inward mind of pity, arising from the concentration of compassion, grows ever deeper and more secure, the concentrated mind corresponding with what is seen without, deep and unmoving — this is named the mind corresponding with compassion. Free of anger, free of resentment, free of enmity, free of vexation, vast and immeasurable — all exactly as already described above.

善修者，於悲定中非但見親人受苦，深憐愍，乃至中、怨九種、十方、五道諸受苦者，憐愍救護，其心平等，故名善修。復次，若見是受苦之人生愍念，受樂者、受不苦不樂者而不憐念，不名善修；若見三種之人悉皆是苦，憐愍不二，是名善修。復次，見五道眾生受苦差別，名不善修；若見受苦不異，憐愍平等，名曰善修。亦可得言：若見五道眾生受苦一種，名不善修；若能分別五道眾生受苦差別不同，名曰善修。如是略說善修之相。

As for "cultivated well": within the concentration of compassion, not merely seeing a loved one undergo suffering and feeling deep pity for them, but extending this, all the way through the nine kinds of person, neutral and enemy alike, and to every being undergoing suffering throughout the ten directions and the five realms, feeling pity and offering rescue and protection with a mind held perfectly equal throughout — this is what is named cultivating it well. Furthermore: if, seeing one undergoing suffering, pity arises, but toward one experiencing happiness, or one experiencing neither suffering nor happiness, no such pitying regard arises at all, this cannot be named cultivating it well; only if, seeing all three kinds of person, one recognizes that each, in their own way, is equally undergoing suffering, and holds pity for them without any distinction, is this named cultivating it well. Furthermore: seeing sentient beings across the five realms as undergoing differing kinds of suffering is not named cultivating it well; seeing that the suffering they undergo is, at root, not different at all, and holding pity for them equally, is what is named cultivating it well. It may also, however, be said the other way: seeing sentient beings across the five realms as undergoing a single, uniform kind of suffering is not named cultivating it well; being able to properly distinguish the differing kinds of suffering sentient beings across the five realms actually undergo is what is named cultivating it well. This, in brief, describes the mark of cultivating it well.

問曰：五道眾生果報不同，苦樂有異，如三塗眾生多苦報，人道眾生半受苦樂，天道眾生多受樂果。云何行慈因緣皆見一切受樂，行悲因緣皆見一切受苦，豈非顛倒耶？答曰：不然，是為得解之道。行者欲學是慈無量心時，先當作願：「願諸眾生受種種樂。」取受樂人相，攝心入定，即見眾生皆悉受樂。譬如鑽火，先以軟草、乾牛糞等，火勢轉大，能燒大濕。大慈心初發亦如是。初生之火，唯及親人，慈心轉廣，怨親同等，皆見受樂，無復苦相。復次，一切眾生五道輪轉，苦樂不定，即雖暫樂，後必大苦，今雖大苦，後當得樂。雖即未然，必有其事。是故行者用得解之心，緣於一切皆樂，不墮顛倒。悲、喜、捨心亦復如是。

Question: sentient beings across the five realms have differing karmic results, their suffering and happiness differing accordingly — beings in the three lower realms undergo, for the most part, suffering as their karmic result; beings in the human realm receive suffering and happiness in roughly equal measure; and beings in the heavenly realms receive, for the most part, happiness as their karmic result. How, then, can the causes and conditions of practicing loving-kindness lead one to see all beings alike receiving happiness, while the causes and conditions of practicing compassion lead one to see all beings alike undergoing suffering — is this not itself a form of inverted, deluded thinking? Answer: this does not follow; this is, rather, precisely the path to genuine understanding.

The practitioner, wishing to study this immeasurable mind of loving-kindness, should first make this vow: "May all sentient beings receive every kind of happiness." Taking up the mark of a person receiving happiness, gathering the mind and entering concentration, one thereby comes to see sentient beings all receiving happiness. It is like starting a fire by friction: using soft grass and dried cattle-dung at first, the fire's own strength gradually grows greater, until it is able to burn even what is thoroughly damp. The great mind of loving-kindness, at its first arising, is exactly the same: the fire, at its very first kindling, reaches only a loved one, but, as the mind of loving-kindness grows ever wider, enemy and loved one alike are seen equally, all receiving happiness, with no mark of suffering left at all.

Furthermore: every sentient being, turning through the five realms, has suffering and happiness that are themselves never fixed — though there be happiness for the moment, great suffering is bound to follow later; and though there be great suffering now, happiness is bound to be attained later as well. Though this has not yet actually come to pass, it is nonetheless bound to occur. Hence the practitioner, using this mind of genuine understanding, takes every being as receiving happiness alike, without falling into any inverted, deluded thinking at all. The minds of compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity are exactly the same in this respect.

Reading note: The nine-fold grid of persons (three grades each of loved one, neutral person, and enemy) recurs from loving-kindness into compassion unchanged — the same diagnostic structure, different content held in mind. The two competing definitions of "cultivated well" offered back-to-back at the end (seeing suffering as fundamentally undifferentiated versus being able to distinguish its different kinds precisely) are presented as equally valid without forcing a choice between them, consistent with how this treatise generally treats apparently competing formulations as complementary rather than as rivals to be resolved. The fire-by-friction and impermanence arguments answering the "isn't this delusion" objection apply symmetrically to compassion's own seeming inversion (seeing beings in bliss as also, truly, in need of rescue) — the same logic run in the opposite direction.

第三、釋修喜證喜，即為二：一者、正明修喜方法，如佛說：「若比丘以喜相應心，無瞋無恨，無怨無惱，廣大無量，善修。」喜相應心者，行者入悲定已，其心愍傷一切眾生長夜為諸苦惱之所逼迫，我當云何而拔濟之，令是等眾生從苦得樂，從樂生歡喜？爾時深觀眾生雖受苦惱，此苦虛妄，本無今有，易可除滅。所以者何？如人有病苦，若遇良藥，即便差愈，更以衣食供給，快樂無量。復次，如人火熱，身受苦惱，若得清冷之水，火苦即滅，歡樂便生。如人現受貧困，以是因緣，慳貪造惡，若給施珍寶，教修布施行善，則現在離於貧弊，身心慶快，未來之世長受安樂。復次，又如世人愚癡顛倒，縈纏煩惱，受種種苦，若聞無漏清淨妙法，如說修行，煩惱病除，即便獲得禪定、智慧及涅槃樂。如是種種因緣，苦無定性，易可除滅，令得歡樂。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, explaining the cultivation and realization of sympathetic joy: this itself has two parts.

First, directly clarifying the method of cultivating sympathetic joy, as the Buddha says: "There is a monk who, with a mind corresponding to sympathetic joy, free of anger, free of resentment, free of enmity, free of vexation, vast and immeasurable, cultivates it well." As for the mind corresponding with sympathetic joy: the practitioner, having entered the concentration of compassion, the mind grieves over how every sentient being has, throughout the long night, been oppressed by every kind of suffering and affliction — how, then, should I go about rescuing and delivering them, so that these very sentient beings may pass from suffering into happiness, and, from happiness, give rise to delight? At this point, one deeply contemplates that, although sentient beings undergo suffering and affliction, this suffering is itself illusory, originally absent and only now present, and so easily removed and extinguished.

Why is this? It is like a person suffering from illness: should they encounter the right medicine, they are cured at once, and, further supplied with clothing and food, immeasurable happiness follows. Furthermore: it is like a person burning with fever, the body suffering greatly — should they obtain cool, refreshing water, the suffering of the fever is extinguished at once, and joy and happiness arise instead. It is like a person presently suffering poverty and hardship, and, through this very cause and condition, committing wrongdoing out of stinginess and greed — should they be given precious treasure, and taught to practice generosity and cultivate goodness, they would, in the present, be freed from poverty's own hardship, body and mind rejoicing, and, in the world to come, long enjoy peace and happiness. Furthermore: it is like people in the world, deluded and inverted, bound up in affliction, undergoing every kind of suffering — should they hear the uncontaminated, pure, wondrous Dharma, and practice accordingly as it is taught, the illness of affliction is removed, and dhyāna-concentration, wisdom, and the happiness of nirvāṇa are thereby obtained at once. Through such various causes and conditions as these, suffering has no fixed nature of its own, and so is easily removed and extinguished, allowing happiness and delight to be attained.

行者作是觀已，即發願言：「願諸眾生一切諸苦悉皆除滅，受樂歡喜，我於定中悉皆得見。」作是願已，即入禪定，用念清淨心取於親人，從苦得脫，受樂歡喜相，一心觀之，令於念心的的分明，見於親人受歡樂相，其心悅豫，欣慶無量。次緣中人、怨人，乃至十方、五道眾生受喜之相，心生慶悅。是則略明修喜方法。二者、明喜定發相。行者如是修已，念慧福德善根力故，作是緣時，即發三昧力故，即於定中任運見於所愛親人離苦得樂歡喜之相，了了分明，於三昧中，其心悅豫不可說，乃至十方、五道眾生受於歡喜亦復如是。行者於三昧中見於外人受喜之相，而於內心無有動轉，定漸增深，是名喜相應心。無瞋無恨，無怨無惱，廣大無量，善修之義，如慈心中說。

The practitioner, having made this contemplation, then makes this vow: "May every suffering of every sentient being be entirely removed and extinguished, that they may receive happiness and delight — may I come to see this, in every case, within concentration." Having made this vow, one then enters dhyāna-concentration, using a mind of pure mindfulness to take up a loved one, freed from suffering and receiving the mark of happiness and delight, contemplating this single-mindedly, making the mental image entirely clear and distinct, seeing the mark of the loved one receiving joy and happiness, the mind delighted, rejoicing beyond measure. One then extends this to a neutral person, an enemy, and, further still, to sentient beings throughout the ten directions and the five realms receiving this same joy, the mind giving rise to delight. This, in brief, describes the method for cultivating sympathetic joy.

Second, clarifying the mark of the sympathetic-joy concentration's own arising. The practitioner, having cultivated in this way, through the power of mindfulness, wisdom, merit, and wholesome roots, taking up this object, samādhi is thereupon brought forth through its own power; one sees, within concentration, spontaneously and without effort, the mark of the loved one, freed from suffering, receiving happiness and delight, entirely clear and distinct — within this samādhi, the mind's own delight beyond all description; and, further still, sentient beings throughout the ten directions and the five realms receiving this same delight, exactly the same. The practitioner, seeing others receiving delight within samādhi, while the inward mind remains unmoved, concentration gradually growing ever deeper — this is named the mind corresponding with sympathetic joy. Free of anger, free of resentment, free of enmity, free of vexation, vast and immeasurable, and the sense of "cultivated well" — all exactly as already described within loving-kindness.

問曰：慈心令眾生樂，喜心令眾生喜。樂之與喜，有何等異？答曰：如《摩訶衍》中說，身樂名樂，心樂名喜。五識相應名樂，意識相應名喜。五塵中生樂名樂，法塵中生樂名喜。復次，欲界中五識相應名樂，初禪中三識相應名樂，三禪中一切樂是名樂，欲界及初禪意識相應名樂。二禪中一切樂是名喜，麁樂名樂，細樂名喜。因時名樂，果時名喜。初得樂時名樂，歡心內發，樂相外現，歡喜踊躍是名喜。樂根相應名為樂，喜根相應故名喜。如是等種種分別喜、樂之相異。

Question: loving-kindness gives sentient beings happiness, and sympathetic joy gives sentient beings delight — what, then, is the actual difference between happiness and delight? Answer: as the Mahāyāna treatise says, bodily happiness is named "happiness," and mental happiness is named "delight." Corresponding with the five sense-consciousnesses is named "happiness"; corresponding with mind-consciousness is named "delight." Happiness arising from the five sense-objects is named "happiness"; happiness arising from the dharma-object is named "delight." Furthermore: within the desire realm, correspondence with the five consciousnesses is named "happiness"; within the first dhyāna, correspondence with the three consciousnesses is named "happiness"; within the third dhyāna, every kind of happiness whatsoever is named "happiness"; and, within both the desire realm and the first dhyāna, correspondence with mind-consciousness specifically is named "happiness" as well. Within the second dhyāna, every kind of happiness whatsoever is named "delight." Coarse happiness is named "happiness"; fine happiness is named "delight." At the time of its cause, it is named "happiness"; at the time of its result, it is named "delight." At the very moment happiness is first attained, this is named "happiness"; a delighted mind arising within, its mark of happiness manifesting without, delight leaping up — this is named "delight." Correspondence with the happiness-faculty is named "happiness"; correspondence with the joy-faculty is named "delight." Such various distinctions as these mark the difference between the marks of delight and happiness.

問曰：若爾者，何以不慈、喜次第？答曰：行慈心時，愛念眾生猶如赤子，心願與樂，出慈三昧，猶見眾生受種種苦，深心愛念，欲拔其苦，令得安樂。當如初樂後喜，中隔於悲，故不次慈記喜也。譬如人母，雖常念子，令得安樂，而未名喜，後見染病，其心愁毒。病既得差，家業付之，大歡喜故，次悲說喜也。問曰：何故約禪明喜、樂，喜即為麁，約無量心明喜則為細？答曰：禪則以定為貴，樂心恬靜，與定相扶，故為勝。無量則心緣眾生，因緣眾生歡喜為勝，故細。復次，行者初定既淺，但以樂緣眾生。何以故？若取喜相，心散難攝，後緣三昧漸深，雖歡喜踊躍，心不散亂，故為細。

Question: if this is so, why are loving-kindness and sympathetic joy not spoken of in direct sequence with one another? Answer: when practicing loving-kindness, one holds sentient beings in loving regard exactly as one would one's own newborn child, the mind wishing to give them happiness; and, emerging from the concentration of loving-kindness, one nonetheless still sees sentient beings undergoing every kind of suffering, a deep and loving regard wishing to draw out this suffering and bring them to peace and happiness instead. This should follow the pattern of happiness first, delight afterward, with compassion set between them — hence sympathetic joy is not spoken of in direct sequence right after loving-kindness. It is like a mother: though she constantly holds her child in mind, wishing them peace and happiness, this alone is not yet named delight; but later, seeing the child fall ill, her own mind is consumed with anguish and grief — and, once the illness is cured, and the family's own fortune is entrusted to the child, great delight follows. Hence sympathetic joy is spoken of only after compassion.

Question: why is it that, discussed in terms of dhyāna, delight is the coarser of the two, while, discussed in terms of the immeasurable minds, delight is instead the finer? Answer: within dhyāna, concentration itself is what is most prized — a tranquil, still mind of happiness supports concentration, and so is considered superior there. Within the immeasurable minds, however, the mind takes sentient beings as its object, and delight arising through the cause and condition of sentient beings is considered superior there instead — hence it is the finer of the two in this context. Furthermore: the practitioner's concentration, still shallow at the outset, can only take sentient beings as its object through happiness. Why is this? Because, were one to take up the mark of delight too soon, the mind would scatter and become difficult to gather in; only later, as samādhi as its object gradually deepens, can delight leap up without the mind becoming scattered or disordered — hence it is considered the finer of the two here.

Reading note: The happiness/delight distinction, worked out here through no fewer than six overlapping criteria (body versus mind, sense-consciousness versus mind-consciousness, coarse versus fine, cause versus result, and so on), is the same pair (樂/喜) already distinguished for the first dhyāna's own factors in fascicle 5 — the hungry-person simile there (joy at first securing food, happiness in actually savoring it) maps directly onto this section's cause/result distinction. The reversal of which one counts as "coarse" depending on context (delight is coarse relative to dhyāna's own priorities, but fine relative to the immeasurable minds' priorities) is a genuinely careful point: "coarse" and "fine" are not fixed properties of a mental state but relative to what a given practice is actually trying to optimize for.

Cultivating and Realizing Equanimity 四無量心 · 修捨證捨

Chinese Source

第四、釋修捨證捨，亦為二：一者、正明修捨方法，如佛說：「若比丘以捨相應心，無瞋無恨，無怨無惱，廣大無量，善修。」捨相應心者，行者從喜定出，心自思念：「若慈與眾生樂，悲欲拔苦，喜令歡喜，而計我能利益，不忘二事，即非勝行。譬如慈父益子，不求恩德，乃曰真親。復次，眾生得樂有多因緣，不獨由我，若言我能與樂，則為過分。復次，慈心與樂，但是得解，然諸眾生實不得樂，若以為實，即是顛倒。復次，是諸眾生受苦樂，憂喜心生。憂喜心生即是結使，難得解脫。我今欲與清淨善法，不應住此三心。復次，我雖慈悲愛念，於彼無益，今當捨此三心，行諸善法，實利眾生。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, explaining the cultivation and realization of equanimity: this too has two parts.

First, directly clarifying the method of cultivating equanimity, as the Buddha says: "There is a monk who, with a mind corresponding to equanimity, free of anger, free of resentment, free of enmity, free of vexation, vast and immeasurable, cultivates it well." As for the mind corresponding with equanimity: the practitioner, emerging from the concentration of sympathetic joy, reflects within: "If loving-kindness gives sentient beings happiness, compassion wishes to draw out their suffering, and sympathetic joy brings them delight, and yet I still reckon myself as able to benefit them, unable to forget these two matters, this is not yet the superior practice. It is like a loving father who benefits his own child without seeking any gratitude or reward in return — only this is called true parental affection. Furthermore: sentient beings attain happiness through many causes and conditions, not through myself alone; to say that I myself am able to give them happiness would be to overstate my own part in it. Furthermore: loving-kindness's giving of happiness is itself only a matter of skillful understanding — sentient beings do not, in truth, actually attain happiness in any fixed sense at all, and to take this as literally real would itself be a form of inverted thinking. Furthermore: these very sentient beings, undergoing suffering and happiness, give rise to worry and delight in turn — and this arising of worry and delight is itself nothing but a fetter, difficult to be liberated from. I now wish to give them the pure, wholesome dharma instead, and should not remain dwelling in these three states of mind. Furthermore: although I hold loving-kindness and compassion toward them, this brings them no true benefit in itself — I should now relinquish these three states of mind, and instead practice the various wholesome dharmas that truly benefit sentient beings."

如是念已，即捨三心，一心發願：「願一切眾生皆得妙捨莊嚴，令我悉見。」作是念已，即入禪定，用念清淨心取於親人受不苦不樂之相，一心緣之。若有異念，攝之令還，令於心想的的分明，見於前人受不苦不樂，如是次第緣中人、怨人、十方、五道一切眾生皆是不苦不樂，其心平等。是則略明修捨方法。二者、明捨定發相。行者如是修已，正念福德善根力故，作是緣時，三昧便發，即於定中不加功力，任運見於所愛親人受於不苦不樂之相，了了分明。於禪定中雖見眾生，心無憎愛，乃至十方、五道眾生亦復如是。行者爾時於此定中見諸眾生皆是捨相，三昧開發，無有動轉，深妙堅固，其心安隱，平等不二，是名捨相應心。無瞋無恨，無怨無惱，廣大無量，善修之義，並如上說。

Having reflected in this way, one relinquishes these three states of mind, and, with a single mind, makes this vow: "May all sentient beings attain this same subtle equanimity, adorned in its fullness — may I come to see this in every case." Having made this vow, one then enters dhyāna-concentration, using a mind of pure mindfulness to take up the mark of a loved one experiencing neither suffering nor happiness, fixing the mind single-mindedly upon this. Should some other thought arise, one gathers the mind back in, making the mental image entirely clear and distinct, seeing this person before one experiencing neither suffering nor happiness — extending this, in the same sequence, to a neutral person, an enemy, and, further still, to every sentient being throughout the ten directions and the five realms, all alike experiencing neither suffering nor happiness, the mind held perfectly equal throughout. This, in brief, describes the method for cultivating equanimity.

Second, clarifying the mark of the equanimity concentration's own arising. The practitioner, having cultivated in this way, through the power of right mindfulness, merit, and wholesome roots, taking up this object, samādhi is thereupon brought forth; within concentration, without any further application of effort, one sees, spontaneously and of its own accord, the mark of the loved one experiencing neither suffering nor happiness, entirely clear and distinct. Within this dhyāna-concentration, although sentient beings are seen, the mind holds neither aversion nor attachment toward them, and the same holds, further still, for sentient beings throughout the ten directions and the five realms. The practitioner, at this point, sees every sentient being alike bearing this same mark of equanimity within this concentration, samādhi opening and unfolding, utterly unmoved, deep, subtle, and secure, the mind at peace, equal throughout without any duality at all — this is named the mind corresponding with equanimity. Free of anger, free of resentment, free of enmity, free of vexation, vast and immeasurable, and the sense of "cultivated well" — all exactly as already described above.

問曰：前三種心中，應有福德，是捨心於眾生不苦不樂，有何等益？答曰：行者作是念：「一切眾生離苦得樂，失時即是苦，皆是惱累；得不苦不樂，則心安隱，始終無患。」以捨饒益故，得福亦大。復次，行者慈喜心時，或時愛著心生；行悲心時，或時憂悲心生。貪憂故，則功德尠薄。入是捨心，除此貪過，無諸煩惱，當知行捨福德甚大。復次，行者於捨心中能作種種益眾生事，是故福德增多。略說捨無量心竟。

Question: within the previous three states of mind, merit is bound to be present, but this equanimity, giving sentient beings neither suffering nor happiness, what benefit does it actually bring? Answer: the practitioner reflects: "When sentient beings pass from suffering into happiness, and then lose this happiness once more, this loss is itself a form of suffering — a trouble and a burden in every case. But attaining a state of neither suffering nor happiness, the mind remains at peace, free of affliction from beginning to end." Since equanimity thus truly benefits them, the merit gained through it is correspondingly great as well.

Furthermore: the practitioner, while practicing loving-kindness and sympathetic joy, may, at times, give rise to a mind of attachment; and, while practicing compassion, may, at times, give rise to a mind of grief and sorrow. Because of this craving and sorrow, the resulting merit is correspondingly thin and slight. Entering this state of equanimity, one removes this very fault of craving, free of every such affliction — one should know that the merit gained through practicing equanimity is exceedingly great. Furthermore: the practitioner, within this state of equanimity, is able to perform every kind of beneficial action toward sentient beings — hence the merit gained is increased all the more. This concludes, in brief, the immeasurable mind of equanimity.

問曰：悲喜捨中，何故不說法緣、無緣？答曰：義類前慈心可見，不煩重說。問曰：是四無量心，樂為二分，悲、喜、捨何故不作二分？答曰：樂是一切眾生所愛重，故作二分；苦不愛不欲，故不作二分。問曰：四無量發願入定見眾生，為實見，為心想見？答曰：見有二種：一、得天眼無量心，此實見。二者、但用得解憶想，緣眾生而入三昧。既證三昧，三昧力故，入則得見，出則不見。此為三昧得解之力，非實見。問曰：證四無量心，何故不分別支及體用、淺深、進退等相？答曰：證無量心時，亦非全無其義，但既無的文，故不須分別。

Question: within compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity, why is loving-kindness's own distinction between taking dharma as its object and being objectless not spoken of again? Answer: the sense follows the same pattern already seen within loving-kindness, and may be readily understood — there is no need to repeat it here.

Question: among these four immeasurable minds, happiness is divided into two kinds, happiness and delight — why, then, are compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity not likewise each divided into two? Answer: happiness is what every sentient being cherishes and loves, and so it is divided into two; suffering is neither loved nor desired, and so it is not divided into two in the same way.

Question: when the four immeasurable minds make their vow and enter concentration to see sentient beings, is this a truly real seeing, or merely a seeing through mental imagination? Answer: seeing is of two kinds: one who has attained the immeasurable mind together with the heavenly eye sees truly and really; but one who merely uses understanding and mental recollection, taking sentient beings as an object in order to enter samādhi, upon actually realizing this samādhi, through the very power of the samādhi, sees them while within it, but no longer sees them once having emerged from it. This is the power of samādhi's own understanding, not a truly real seeing.

Question: in realizing the four immeasurable minds, why are their factors, substance and function, depth, and progress and regression not distinguished, the way they were for the four dhyānas? Answer: at the moment the immeasurable minds are realized, it is not that this sense is entirely absent either — it is simply that, there being no definite text addressing it, it is not necessary to distinguish it here.

Reading note: Equanimity's cultivation method is structurally distinct from the other three: rather than intensifying a positive wish toward beings, it is reached by critiquing the very presumption behind loving-kindness, compassion, and sympathetic joy — the quiet self-congratulation of "I am the one benefiting them" — and relinquishing it. The loving-father simile makes the point precisely: the parent who helps without seeking credit is "true" parental affection, implying the first three immeasurable minds, however genuine, still carry a residue of self-reference that equanimity alone removes. The closing admission that equanimity brings real merit specifically because it is not attached even to the good it produces is a small but genuine paradox this treatise states plainly rather than glossing over.

The Merit of the Four Immeasurable Minds 四無量心 · 明功德

Chinese Source

第五、釋四無量功德，即為二：一者、現世；二者、未來世。一、現世功德者，如佛《阿含》中說：「若入慈心三昧者，現世得五種功德：一、入火不燒；二、中毒不死；三、兵刃不傷；四、終不橫死；五、善神擁護。」以利益無量眾生故，得無量福德。二、未來功德者，善修四無量心，若生色界，多作梵王，以無量心廣攝眾生故；若於初禪得，即作初禪王，乃至四禪亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, explaining the merit of the four immeasurable minds: this itself has two parts — merit in this present life; and merit in the life to come.

First, as for merit in this present life: as the Buddha says in the Āgamas: "One who enters the samādhi of loving-kindness attains five kinds of merit in this present life: entering fire, one is not burned; encountering poison, one does not die; struck by weapons, one is not wounded; one never dies an untimely death; and one is protected and guarded by wholesome spirits." Because of benefiting immeasurable sentient beings, one attains immeasurable merit in turn.

Second, as for merit in the life to come: one who cultivates the four immeasurable minds well, should they be born into the form realm, for the most part becomes a Brahmā king, since the immeasurable minds embrace sentient beings so broadly. Should this attainment be reached within the first dhyāna, one becomes king of the first dhyāna's heaven, and the same holds, further still, all the way through the fourth dhyāna as well.

問曰：三藏中但說初禪號娑婆世界主、梵天王，今何故說乃至四禪悉有梵王？答曰：《纓絡經》中明四禪，禪禪並有梵王。問曰：若爾，佛何以故說慈報生梵天上？答曰：以梵天眾生所尊，皆聞皆識故。佛在天竺國常多婆羅門，婆羅門法所有福德盡願生梵天，若聞行慈生梵天，聞多信教，修行慈法，以是故說行慈生梵天上。復次，斷婬欲天皆名為梵，說梵則攝四禪、四無色定，如五戒中律儀，但說一種不妄語則攝三事。復次，若於四禪中修四無量心，隨是禪中，悉得受生。既隨禪生無量心，福德大故，果報亦應有異，豈得生於彼天而無君民之別？復次，如佛於《仁王經》說十八梵，亦應有王民之異。又云四禪中有大靜王，而佛於三藏中但說初禪有大梵王者，以初禪內有覺觀心。雖則有語言法主領下地眾生為便，上地無此，故不別出。

Question: the Tripiṭaka speaks only of the first dhyāna's own heaven as bearing the title Lord of the Sahā World, the Great Brahmā King — why, then, is it now said that Brahmā kings are present all the way through the fourth dhyāna as well? Answer: the Yingluo Sūtra, in clarifying the four dhyānas, states that a Brahmā king is present within each and every one of the four dhyānas' own heavens.

Question: if this is so, why does the Buddha say that the karmic reward of loving-kindness is birth in the Brahmā heaven specifically? Answer: because the Brahmā heaven's own beings are widely honored, universally heard of, and universally known. The Buddha, while in the land of India, was constantly surrounded by many brāhmaṇas, and the brāhmaṇa teaching holds that every merit whatsoever should ultimately aim at rebirth in the Brahmā heaven; hence, hearing that practicing loving-kindness leads to birth in the Brahmā heaven, many among them, upon hearing this, came to believe and follow the teaching, cultivating and practicing the dharma of loving-kindness — hence it is said, for this very reason, that practicing loving-kindness leads to birth in the Brahmā heaven.

Furthermore: every heaven that has severed lustful desire is named, in general, "Brahmā" — and so speaking of "Brahmā" already encompasses the four dhyānas and the four formless concentrations together, just as, among the five precepts' own moral codes, speaking of the single precept against false speech alone is already understood to encompass the other three related precepts as well. Furthermore: if the four immeasurable minds are cultivated within any of the four dhyānas, one is bound to be reborn according to that very dhyāna. Since one is thus reborn according to whichever dhyāna the immeasurable minds were cultivated within, and the merit involved is itself great, the karmic result, too, should correspondingly differ — how, then, could one possibly be reborn into that heaven with no distinction at all between ruler and ruled? Furthermore, as the Buddha says in the Humane Kings Sūtra, there are eighteen Brahmā heavens, and these, too, should have their own distinction between ruler and ruled. It is also said that there is a Great Tranquil King present within the fourth dhyāna as well; and, as for why the Buddha, within the Tripiṭaka, speaks only of the first dhyāna as having a Great Brahmā King, this is because the first dhyāna alone still has initial thought and sustained reflection present within it — and so, although it still involves language, it is convenient for it to serve as the ruling lord over the realms below; the higher stages have no such need for this, and so are not separately named.

問曰：若爾，佛何故說四無量功德，慈心好修善修，福德極遍淨天；悲心好修善修，福德極虛空處；喜心好修善修，福德極識處；捨心好修善修，福德極無所有處？云何言慈果報應生梵天上？答曰：佛法不可思議，隨眾生應度者如是說。復次，從慈定起，入三禪易；從悲定起，向虛空處易；從喜定起，入識處易；捨定起，入無所有處易。復次，慈心願令眾生得樂，此果報自應受樂。三界中，遍淨天最為樂故，言福德極遍淨。悲心觀眾生老病殘害，行者憐愍心生，云何令得離苦？若除內苦，外苦復來；若為除外苦，心苦復來。行者思惟：「有身則有苦，唯有無身，乃得無苦。」虛空能破色，是故福德極虛空處。喜心欲與眾生樂，心識樂者，心得離身，如鳥出籠。虛空處，心雖得出身，猶繫心虛空處無得礙。於一切法中皆有心識，識得自在無邊故，以喜福極在識處。捨心者，捨眾生中苦樂，故得真法，所謂無所有處，以是故，捨心福極無所有處。

Question: if this is so, why does the Buddha then speak of the four immeasurable minds' merit in this other way — that loving-kindness, cultivated well, has its merit reaching its utmost in the Heaven of Universal Purity; compassion, cultivated well, has its merit reaching its utmost in the sphere of boundless space; sympathetic joy, cultivated well, has its merit reaching its utmost in the sphere of boundless consciousness; and equanimity, cultivated well, has its merit reaching its utmost in the sphere of nothingness — how, then, can it also be said that loving-kindness's own karmic result leads to birth in the Brahmā heaven? Answer: the Buddhadharma is inconceivable, spoken of in whatever way suits whichever sentient beings are to be delivered by it at the time. Furthermore: arising from the concentration of loving-kindness, one enters the third dhyāna easily; arising from the concentration of compassion, one turns toward the sphere of boundless space easily; arising from the concentration of sympathetic joy, one enters the sphere of boundless consciousness easily; and arising from the concentration of equanimity, one enters the sphere of nothingness easily.

Furthermore: the mind of loving-kindness vows that sentient beings attain happiness, and so its own karmic result naturally receives happiness in turn — and, among the three realms, the Heaven of Universal Purity is the very happiest of all, hence its merit is said to reach its utmost there. The mind of compassion contemplates sentient beings growing old, falling ill, and being harmed, a mind of pity arising in the practitioner — how, then, might they be freed from suffering? If the inward suffering is removed, outward suffering returns; and if the outward suffering is removed instead, inward suffering returns in its place. The practitioner reflects: "Wherever there is a body, there is suffering; only where there is no body at all can there be freedom from suffering." Since empty space is able to break through form entirely, its merit is said to reach its utmost in the sphere of boundless space. The mind of sympathetic joy wishes to give sentient beings happiness — and one whose mind and consciousness attain happiness gains a mind freed from the body, like a bird escaping its cage; in the sphere of boundless space, although the mind has already escaped the body, it remains bound to the sphere of space itself, still unable to move freely — whereas, within every dharma whatsoever, mind and consciousness are already present, and consciousness itself is boundless and free; hence sympathetic joy's merit reaches its utmost in the sphere of boundless consciousness. The mind of equanimity relinquishes both suffering and happiness among sentient beings, and so attains the true dharma of what is called the sphere of nothingness; hence equanimity's own merit reaches its utmost in the sphere of nothingness.

若作如是明四無量心功德，但諸聖人智慧方便自在故如是，非諸凡夫。何以故？凡夫之人住初禪乃至四禪，修四無量，隨禪受報，不能方便巧入無色修四無量。復次，佛知未來世諸弟子鈍根故，分別著諸法，錯說四無量相，是四無量心，聖人所知，眾生緣故，但是有漏，但緣欲界故，無色界中無。所以者何？無色界不緣欲界故。為斷如是人妄見故，說四無量心。無色界中亦以四無量心普緣十方眾生故，不重言不緣無色界。如是說者，多存法緣、無緣。復次，行者若於眾生緣中具足入法緣、無緣，是時眾生緣四無量心是摩訶衍。復次，菩薩發菩提心，行菩薩道，此眾生緣四無量心，雖是凡夫所行，亦應知應證。證已，以不可得空無所著，善巧方便，即能於此定中具足一切善法，度諸眾生，即是行菩薩道。復次，四無量心中觀行功德眾多，更欲論餘事，不具明也。

If the merit of the four immeasurable minds is explained in this way, this holds only because the noble ones' own wisdom and skillful means are themselves free and unobstructed — it does not hold for ordinary people. Why is this? Ordinary people, abiding within the first dhyāna all the way through the fourth, cultivate the four immeasurable minds and receive their karmic result according to whichever dhyāna they cultivated them in, unable to skillfully enter the formless realm and cultivate the four immeasurable minds there as well.

Furthermore: the Buddha, knowing that future disciples would be of dull faculty, clinging to distinctions among dharmas, and so might mistakenly describe the mark of the four immeasurable minds — these four immeasurable minds, as known by the noble ones, take sentient beings as their object, and so are contaminated; and, since they take only the desire realm as their object, they are absent within the formless realm. Why is this? Because the formless realm does not take the desire realm as its object at all. In order to sever such a mistaken view as this, the four immeasurable minds are spoken of as they are here; within the formless realm, too, the four immeasurable minds broadly take the ten directions' sentient beings as their object, and so it is not said again separately that they fail to take the formless realm as their object. Those who speak in this way, for the most part, are describing loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object, and objectless loving-kindness.

Furthermore: should the practitioner, within loving-kindness that takes sentient beings as its object, become fully complete, entering also loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object and objectless loving-kindness, then, at this point, the immeasurable mind that takes sentient beings as its object is itself the Mahāyāna. Furthermore: the bodhisattva, having given rise to the bodhi-mind and practicing the bodhisattva path, should know and realize this very immeasurable mind that takes sentient beings as its object as well, even though it is, in itself, a practice of the ordinary person. Having realized it, through the unfindable emptiness of non-attachment, and skillful means, one is thereby able to be complete in every wholesome dharma within this very concentration, liberating all sentient beings — this is precisely the practice of the bodhisattva path. Furthermore: the contemplative merit within the four immeasurable minds is itself manifold; wishing to discuss the remaining matters further would exceed what can be given here in full.

Reading note: The apparent contradiction resolved here — loving-kindness leading to the Brahmā heaven in one teaching, but to the Heaven of Universal Purity's own third-dhyāna bliss in another — gets the treatise's characteristic non-resolution: both are true, spoken to different audiences ("the brāhmaṇas of India already revered the Brahmā heaven, so that framing met them where they were") rather than one superseding the other. The closing move is the same one that closed fascicle 5: this entire apparatus of loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity is, strictly speaking, a contaminated, ordinary-person's practice tied to karmic rebirth — and yet a bodhisattva must still know and realize it, because it is precisely the ground on which loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object and objectless loving-kindness are eventually built. This closes the four immeasurable minds — mundane dhyāna's second of three sections — in full.

A Deviant Path: The Concentration of No-Thought 無想定

Chinese Source

次四無量心後，應釋無想定。何以故？有諸外道深厭有為心識生滅，欲求涅槃寂靜常樂，既無智慧，不知真實，得四禪時，不見細色之過，但覺心識生滅虛誑，則厭患其心。既不知破色斷色繫縛，直以邪智滅却其心，邪法相應，心無憶想，謂證涅槃。既未斷色繫縛，若捨命時，即生無想天中，猶是色界生死，不得解脫，亦名客天。猶如阿那含人修五品熏禪，為色界思惟惑未盡，寄生色界，亦名客天。此無想定既是邪法，非佛弟子所修，今欲具足明三界定，所以略明，示知邪正相耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, following the four immeasurable minds, the concentration of no-thought should be explained. Why is this? There are non-Buddhist practitioners who grow deeply weary of the arising and ceasing of conditioned mind and consciousness, wishing to seek nirvāṇa as a still, permanent happiness; yet, lacking true wisdom, and not knowing the truth of the matter, upon attaining the fourth dhyāna, they fail to perceive the fault present within its own subtle form, and are aware only of mind and consciousness's own arising and ceasing as illusory and false, growing weary of and troubled by this very mind. Not knowing how to break through form and sever the bondage of form, they simply use a deviant wisdom to extinguish the mind directly instead — corresponding with this deviant dharma, the mind free of any thought at all, they take this to be the realization of nirvāṇa.

Since the bondage of form has not, in fact, been severed at all, when their life ends, they are reborn into the heaven of no-thought instead — still nothing more than birth-and-death within the form realm, never actually attaining liberation — also called a "guest heaven." This is comparable to the anāgāmin who cultivates the five-grade dhyāna of habituation: since the form realm's own discursive delusion has not yet been entirely exhausted, they, too, take temporary lodging within the form realm, likewise called a "guest heaven." Since this concentration of no-thought is itself a deviant dharma, and not something a disciple of the Buddha would ever cultivate, it is given here only in brief, for the sake of completeness in clarifying the concentrations of the three realms, simply to show and make known the marks of the deviant and the correct.

Reading note: This deviant path completes the diagnostic sketch this fascicle opened with — the non-Buddhist practitioner who "sees a defect in the very presence of mind and consciousness" and tries to extinguish the mind directly rather than understanding it. The distinction being drawn is precise: mistaking the mere absence of thought for liberation, without ever actually breaking through form's own bondage, produces only another form of rebirth (a "guest heaven," temporary lodging rather than a true home) — a warning that a still, thoughtless mind is not automatically the same thing as realization, however peaceful it may feel from within.

The Four Formless Concentrations 四無色定

Chinese Source

第三、釋四無色定。四無色定者：一、空處；二、識處；三、無所有處；四、非有想非無想處。今釋此四定為二意：一、總釋；二、別釋。一、總釋者，前四禪、四無量定悉依色法故有，今此四定悉依無色法，從境得名，故云無色定。是故經云：「四空滅色道，心心互相依，亦名四空定。」無形無質即是義同虛空，故名四空定，亦名四空定處。此四種定心，亦名定處。此四種定心，以所觀之境為處，如念處、勝處、一切處，悉從所觀處得名。四空定次第之相，在下當明。而不名禪者，前已受名，不應重立，今應更立勝名。復次，此四無色自體支林有闕，不得名禪。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, explaining the four formless concentrations. The four formless concentrations are: the sphere of boundless space; the sphere of boundless consciousness; the sphere of nothingness; and the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception. Here, these four concentrations are explained in two parts: a general explanation; and a particular explanation.

First, the general explanation: the previous four dhyānas and the four immeasurable-mind concentrations are all present in dependence on form-dharma; these present four concentrations, however, are all present in dependence on formless dharma, taking their very name from their own object — hence they are called the formless concentrations. Hence the sūtra says: "The four emptinesses extinguish the path of form, mind after mind mutually depending on one another" — also called the four concentrations of emptiness. Having no shape and no substance, their meaning is the same as empty space itself — hence they are named the four concentrations of emptiness, also called the four spheres of the concentration of emptiness.

These four kinds of concentrated mind are also named "spheres," since these four kinds of concentrated mind take the very object contemplated as their own sphere — just as the foundations of mindfulness, the stations of mastery, and the totality-bases all likewise take their name from the object contemplated. The mark of the four concentrations of emptiness' own sequence will be clarified below. As for why they are not named "dhyāna": having already received their own name earlier, it would not be proper to establish this same name again, and so a more excellent name is established here instead. Furthermore: these four formless attainments are themselves deficient in the substance of a grove-of-factors, and so cannot be named "dhyāna."

問曰：《纓絡經》說：「五支為因，默然為定體。」此復云何？答曰：此但約義方便立支，非如四禪具足成就支林之法，故諸經論中並不說有支也。

Question: the Yingluo Sūtra says: "The five factors serve as the cause, and silence serves as the substance of concentration" — how, then, does this apply here? Answer: this is simply establishing factors as a matter of convenient sense, not the same as the four dhyānas' own complete accomplishment of the dharma of a grove-of-factors — hence the various sūtras and treatises, in general, do not speak of factors being present here at all.

The Sphere of Boundless Space: Name and Cultivation 空處 · 釋名、修行

Chinese Source

第二、別釋空處，即開為三：一、釋名；二、修行；三、證相。第一、釋名，所以名空處定者，此定最初離三種色，心緣虛空，既與無色相應，故名虛空定。今此空處及上三無色定並是無覺、無觀、聖默然及捨俱所攝，故《摩訶衍》云：「得虛空處定，不苦不樂，其心轉增。」問曰：若虛空無色名空定者，上來諸禪亦見空想，何故不名虛空定耶？答曰：不爾。彼六地中，但是入定心細，不見麁色之相，意謂為空，而實未能觀色，破散色法，斷色繫縛。所以定中或時見色，或不見色，非如空定一向永絕色相。是故六地定中雖有空相，不名虛空無色定也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the sphere of boundless space in particular: this opens into three parts — explaining the name; cultivation; and the mark of realization.

First, explaining the name: as for why it is named the concentration of the sphere of space, this concentration is the very first to leave the three kinds of form behind, the mind taking empty space as its object, corresponding, at this point, with formlessness itself — hence it is named the concentration of empty space. This sphere of space, together with the three formless attainments above it, all belong to the samādhi without initial thought or sustained reflection, noble silence, joined with equanimity — hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "Having attained the concentration of the sphere of space, free of both suffering and happiness, the mind grows ever further."

Question: if it is empty space's own formlessness that gives the concentration of emptiness its name, then the dhyānas already discussed above also involve a perception of emptiness — why, then, are they not named the concentration of empty space as well? Answer: this does not follow. Within those six stages, it is simply that, upon entering concentration, the mind grows fine, no longer perceiving the mark of coarse form, and so is taken, in one's own mind, to be "empty" — but, in truth, one has not yet actually been able to contemplate form directly, break it apart, and sever the bondage of form. Hence, within those concentrations, form is at times seen and at times not seen — unlike the concentration of emptiness, which leaves the mark of form behind entirely and permanently. Hence, although those six stages of concentration do involve a mark resembling emptiness, they are not named the concentration of empty, formless space.

第二、明修空方法。就中有二：一、明所修之境；二、明能修之心。一、明所修之境中有二種：一者、障境；二者、相成境。一、障者，行者欲入空處，要須滅三種色：一、可見有對色；二、不可見有對色；三、不可見無對色。故經中說：「過一切色相，滅有對相，不念種種相，入無邊虛空處。」《摩訶衍》云：「過一切色相，即破可見有對色；滅有對相，即是破不可見有對色；不念種種相，是滅不可見無對色。」一切色法不過十一，謂五塵、五根及一入少分，即是色法塵。如《阿毘曇》說：「一則見十，則說有對。」一入少分，是不可見無對，行者欲入虛空處定，必須破此三色，此三種色即是障境。二、成定境者，虛空為智所緣，因此入定，即是成定之境。

Second, clarifying the method for cultivating the sphere of space. Within this, there are two parts: clarifying the object to be cultivated; and clarifying the mind that does the cultivating.

First, clarifying the object to be cultivated: this itself is of two kinds — the object that obstructs; and the object that accomplishes concentration. First, as for the obstructing object: the practitioner wishing to enter the sphere of space must extinguish three kinds of form: visible form with resistance; invisible form with resistance; and invisible form without resistance. Hence the sūtra says: "Passing beyond every mark of form, extinguishing the mark of resistance, no longer taking up any of the various marks, one enters the sphere of boundless space." The Mahāyāna treatise says: "'Passing beyond every mark of form' means breaking through visible form with resistance; 'extinguishing the mark of resistance' means breaking through invisible form with resistance; and 'no longer taking up any of the various marks' means extinguishing invisible form without resistance." Every form-dharma whatsoever numbers no more than eleven: the five sense-objects, the five sense-faculties, and a small portion of the sense-base of dharma, this last being itself a form-dharma-object. As the Abhidharma says: "Where one sense perceives it, all ten of the others do as well — hence it is said to have resistance." The small portion of the dharma-sense-base is invisible and without resistance. The practitioner wishing to enter the concentration of the sphere of space must break through these three kinds of form — these three kinds of form are precisely the obstructing object. Second, as for the object that accomplishes concentration: empty space, taken as the object of wisdom, is the very means by which concentration is entered — this is precisely the object that accomplishes concentration.

第二、明能修之心，即為二：一、訶讚；二、觀析修習。言訶讚者，如行者欲求虛空處定，應深思色法過罪，所謂若有身色，則內有饑渴、疾病、大小便利、滓穢、麁重弊惡、欺誑虛假等一切諸苦，外受寒熱、刀杖、枷鎖、刑罰等一切諸苦。從先世因緣和合，報得此身，即是種種眾苦之本，不可保著。復次，一切色法繫縛於心，不得自在，即是心之牢獄，令心受惱，無可貪樂。是則略說訶色過罪之相。讚者，讚歎虛空無色，則無此過，虛豁安樂，此處寂靜，無眾惱患。今明訶責、讚歎者，即是修習六行之相。類前可知也。

Second, clarifying the mind that does the cultivating: this itself has two parts — reproach and praise; and analytic contemplation.

As for reproach and praise: the practitioner wishing to seek the concentration of the sphere of space should deeply reflect on the faults and offenses of the dharma of form — namely, that, wherever there is a bodily form, there is, within it, hunger, thirst, illness, urination and defecation, filth and impurity, coarse heaviness and repugnance, deception and falsity, every such suffering; and, without it, cold, heat, weapons and rods, fetters and chains, punishment, every such suffering as well. Obtained as a karmic result through the coming together of causes and conditions from past lives, this very body is the root of every manner of suffering, and cannot be relied upon or clung to. Furthermore: every form-dharma whatsoever binds the mind, denying it freedom — it is itself the very prison of the mind, causing the mind to suffer affliction, with nothing in it worth craving or delighting in. This, in brief, describes the mark of reproaching form's own faults and offenses. As for praise: one praises empty, formless space as free of these very faults, vast, open, and at peace — this stage being still and tranquil, free of every affliction and trouble. What is clarified here, reproach and praise, is precisely the mark of cultivating the six practices, understood by the same pattern as before.

二、明觀析修習。行者於四禪中應作是念：「我今此定，依欲界身具足色法，何故不見？」作此念已，即當一心諦觀己身一切毛道及與九孔，身內空種皆悉虛疎，猶如羅縠，內外相通；亦如芭蕉，重重無實。作是觀時，即便得見。既得見已，復更諦心觀察，見身如簁、如甑、如蜘蛛網，漸漸微末，身分皆盡，不見於身及五根等。如內身既盡，外色亦然。所以者何？內身四微、四大一切色法，不異外身四微、四大一切色法故。

Second, clarifying analytic contemplation. The practitioner, within the fourth dhyāna, should reflect: "My present concentration relies upon a body of the desire realm, complete with form-dharma — why, then, do I not see it directly?" Having reflected in this way, one should single-mindedly and closely contemplate every pore and the nine bodily orifices of one's own body, the space-element within the body entirely empty and porous throughout, just like a fine silk gauze, inward and outward freely interpenetrating; and also like a plantain trunk, layer upon layer with nothing solid at its core. Contemplating in this way, one comes to see this directly. Having seen it, one should further and more closely observe, seeing the body as though it were a sieve, a steaming-basket, or a spider's web, growing gradually more and more insubstantial, the bodily parts entirely exhausted, until neither the body nor the five faculties are seen at all. Just as the inward body is thus exhausted, so too is outward form. Why is this? Because the inward body's own four subtle elements and four great elements, every form-dharma present, are no different at all from the outward body's own four subtle elements and four great elements, every form-dharma present there as well.

復次，行者如是觀時，眼見色壞，故名過色。耳聲、鼻香、舌味、身觸覺壞，故名滅有對相。於二種餘色及無教色種種不分別，故名不念種種別異相。一切色法既滅，但一心緣空，念空不捨，即色定便謝，而空定未發，亦有中間禪。爾時慎勿憂悔，勤加精進，一心念空，當度色難。是則略說修習禪定方法。

Furthermore: the practitioner, contemplating in this way, the eye's own seeing of form broken down — this is named "passing beyond form." The ear's sound, the nose's fragrance, the tongue's flavor, and the body's own tactile sensing all broken down as well — this is named "extinguishing the mark of resistance." No longer discriminating among the remaining two kinds of form, nor among the various forms not directly instructed by the senses — this is named "no longer taking up any of the various distinct marks." Once every form-dharma has thus been extinguished, one simply fixes the mind single-mindedly on emptiness, holding this mindfulness of emptiness without relinquishing it — at this point, the concentration of form passes away and is extinguished, while the concentration of emptiness has not yet arisen; there is, in the interval, an intermediate dhyāna present here as well. At this point, one must take great care not to fall into worry or regret, but instead apply oneself diligently, holding the mind single-mindedly on emptiness, until the difficulty of form is finally overcome. This, in brief, describes the method for cultivating this dhyāna-concentration.

Reading note: The body-dissolving contemplation here — pores and orifices, then the body as sieve, steaming-basket, and spider's web, until neither the body nor the five faculties are seen at all — extends the treatise's recurring physiological grounding of abstract states (the kalala stage in fascicle 1, the sixteen tactile sensations in fascicle 5) into its most radical instance yet: form is not denied philosophically but watched dissolving experientially, pore by pore, until nothing is left to hold onto. The three-kinds-of-form scheme (visible-with-resistance, invisible-with-resistance, invisible-without-resistance) gives real content to what "passing beyond form" actually requires — not just the coarse visible world but two further, subtler layers most practitioners would never think to name.

The Sphere of Boundless Space: Its Realization 空處 · 明證虛空定

Chinese Source

第二、明證虛空定，亦為六意：一、證相；二、明有支無支；三、體用；四、淺深；五、進退；六、功德。第一、明證相者，行者既一心念空不捨，則其心泯然，任運自住空緣。此亦似如前說未到地之相。於後豁然，與空相應，其心明淨，不苦不樂，益更增長，於深定中唯見虛空，無諸色相。雖緣無邊虛空，心無分散。既無色縛，心識澄靜，無礙自在，如鳥在籠中，籠破得出，飛騰自在。證虛空定亦復如是。復次，得空處定，出過色界，故名過一切色相。空法持心，種種諸色而不得起，故名滅有對相。既得勝妙空處，決定能捨色法，心不憶戀，故名不念種種相。是故經中多以此義明證虛空處定。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the realization of the concentration of empty space: this too has six parts — the mark of realization; clarifying whether factors are present or absent; substance and function; depth; progress and regression; and merit.

First, clarifying the mark of realization: the practitioner, holding the mind single-mindedly on emptiness without relinquishing it, the mind grows indistinct, abiding of its own accord, spontaneously, within emptiness as its object. This, too, resembles the mark of the "not-yet-arrived" state already described above. Afterward, the mind opens out, corresponding with emptiness, bright and pure, free of both suffering and happiness, this state growing ever further — within this deep concentration, one sees only empty space itself, with no mark of form present at all. Although taking boundless empty space as its object, the mind is free of any scattering. Freed now from the bondage of form, mind and consciousness are clear and still, unobstructed and at ease — like a bird within a cage, the cage broken open, able to fly forth freely. The realization of the concentration of empty space is exactly the same.

Furthermore: having attained the concentration of the sphere of space, one has passed entirely beyond the form realm — hence this is named "passing beyond every mark of form." The dharma of emptiness sustains the mind, and the various forms are no longer able to arise at all — hence this is named "extinguishing the mark of resistance." Having attained this excellent, wondrous sphere of space, one is decisively able to relinquish the dharma of form, the mind no longer dwelling on or longing for it — hence this is named "no longer taking up any of the various marks." Hence the sūtras, for the most part, use this very sense to clarify the realization of the concentration of the sphere of space.

第二、明有支無支者，餘經論中明四無色定，例不立支，唯《纓絡經》云：「四空定，五支為因，第六默然心為定體，方便道同，體用相似故。」若依《纓絡》所說，虛空定即有五支。五支者，如經說：「一、想；二、護；三、正；四、觀；五、一心。」但上來四禪悉有支相貌可見，今此空定既無別證支離之法，此恐是據修空方便，義立為支，故經言：「方便道同，體用相似故。」餘經論悉不立支者，當是為自體無有別證支林成就之相，而於《纓絡》中說有支者，多是據方便及約義故說支。

Second, clarifying whether factors are present or absent: the various other sūtras and treatises, in clarifying the four formless concentrations, generally do not establish any factors for them at all; only the Yingluo Sūtra says: "Within the four concentrations of emptiness, five factors serve as the cause, and the sixth, the mind of noble silence, serves as the substance of concentration, the expedient path being the same, substance and function resembling one another." Following what the Yingluo Sūtra says, then, the concentration of empty space does indeed have five factors. These five factors, as the sūtra states, are: conception; guarding; correctness; contemplation; and one-pointedness of mind. But, whereas the four dhyānas already discussed above all had visibly distinct marks of their own factors, this concentration of emptiness has no such distinctly realized, branching dharma of its own — this is likely, then, spoken with reference to the expedient means for cultivating emptiness, factors established here only as a matter of convenient sense, hence the sūtra's own qualification: "the expedient path being the same, substance and function resembling one another." As for why the other sūtras and treatises generally do not establish factors here at all: this must be because, in its own substance, there is no distinctly realized, grove-of-factors-like mark of accomplishment present; and, as for why the Yingluo Sūtra speaks of factors being present, this, for the most part, is spoken with reference to expedient means and to sense alone.

約方便立支，其義云何？一、想者，修空定時，想身如簁如甑想。二、護者，即是捨支，捨於三種色相；又護者，名護持，遮三種色，不令破於空心。三、正者，不邪為義。今修空定為正，若念色相，是則為邪。四、觀者，觀達正念，破三種色，達於空理。若觀心住虛空，無有分散，名一心支。通明支者，謂支離為義，因此五法支離非一，故名為支。約修方便論支正應如此，佛意難知，既無的文，不可定判。或是證空定時，於空定中義立五支。何以故？經亦說言：「五支為因，第六默然心為定體。」今約修空立支隱顯，明因果體用，如似不便。若約證空定時，義立五支，亦復宛然，似如可見，深推自解，不煩多釋。

As for establishing factors with reference to expedient means, what is the sense of this? First, conception: while cultivating the concentration of emptiness, conceiving of the body as though it were a sieve or a steaming-basket. Second, guarding: this is precisely the factor of relinquishing, relinquishing the three kinds of mark of form; "guarding" also means maintaining and protecting, warding off the three kinds of form, not allowing them to break through the mind of emptiness. Third, correctness: this means being free of deviance — cultivating the concentration of emptiness here is what is correct; were one instead to dwell on the mark of form, this would be deviant. Fourth, contemplation: contemplating and penetrating right mindfulness, breaking through the three kinds of form, penetrating the principle of emptiness. Fifth, if the contemplating mind abides within empty space, free of any scattering, this is named the factor of one-pointedness of mind.

Explaining the general sense of "factor": this means the sense of branching apart — because these five dharmas branch apart from one another, not being a single unity, they are named "factors." Discussed with reference to the expedient means of cultivation, the sense of the factors should properly be exactly as given here; but the Buddha's own intent is difficult to know for certain, and, there being no definite text on the matter, this cannot be judged with full certainty. Alternatively, it may be that, at the very moment the concentration of emptiness is realized, five factors are established there as a matter of sense as well. Why is this? Because the sūtra likewise says: "The five factors serve as the cause, and the sixth, the mind of noble silence, serves as the substance of concentration." Here, establishing factors with reference to cultivating emptiness leaves cause and effect, substance and function, somewhat obscured and inconvenient to explain; but, if factors are instead established with reference to the moment emptiness is actually realized, in accordance with their own sense, this, too, seems entirely coherent and readily seen — examined closely, this may be resolved for oneself, and need not be explained further here.

第三、體用者，前五支為因，第六默然心為果，果後更起五支則為用，默然為體，例如前四禪不異。問曰：向言無證支，那得例上？答曰：還用方便支義支對，隱顯例作，亦當於義無失。第四、淺深者，初得虛空定，即離三種色，心與十方虛空相應，於後定既重發，復覺心識明淨，見空亦廣，定又增深。自覺初淺狹，今則漸廣深，如是乃至九品，類前可知。第五、進退者，得虛空定亦有四種人不同，所謂退分、住分、進分、達分，類如上四禪中說，今不廣明。第六、明功德者，亦有共、不共。共如上說。不共離過者，始於此空定中，方得離三種色過。善心不共者，得離色證空，更得增勝、信敬、慚愧等諸功德。

Third, as for substance and function: the first five factors serve as the cause, and the sixth, the mind of noble silence, serves as the effect; after this effect, the five factors arise once more, serving then as the function, with noble silence itself as the substance — exactly the same pattern as already given for the four dhyānas. Question: it was just said that there are no distinctly realized factors here at all — how, then, can this be treated the same as the dhyānas above? Answer: this simply uses the expedient-means sense of the factors as a corresponding parallel, obscured and revealed by the same pattern in turn — and this, too, should be without fault in its own sense.

Fourth, as for depth: upon first attaining the concentration of empty space, one leaves the three kinds of form behind, the mind corresponding with the ten directions' own empty space; afterward, as this concentration arises anew, one becomes aware once more of mind and consciousness growing bright and pure, the perception of emptiness itself growing ever broader, concentration itself growing ever deeper. Aware, at first, of shallowness and narrowness, and now of gradually growing breadth and depth — proceeding in this way all the way through nine grades, understood by the same pattern as already given.

Fifth, as for progress and regression: attaining the concentration of empty space likewise involves four differing kinds of practitioner — those prone to regression, those who abide, those who progress, and those who penetrate through — following the same pattern as already given within the four dhyānas above; this is not explained again here at length.

Sixth, clarifying merit: this too has both shared and distinct merit. The shared merit is as already described above. As for the distinct merit of freedom from fault: it is only beginning with this very concentration of emptiness that one is finally able to gain freedom from the fault of the three kinds of form. As for the distinct merit of a wholesome mind: having gained freedom from form and realized emptiness, one attains further excellent qualities still — faith, reverence, shame, remorse, and the other such merits.

Reading note: The bird-escaping-its-cage simile marks a real threshold in the treatise's own imagery: every simile through the four dhyānas involved refinement within embodiment (a pool, a bell, a spring within rock); here, for the first time, the image is of leaving the container itself behind. The uncertainty about whether the five "factors" genuinely apply to the formless concentrations at all — worked through at unusual length, with the treatise honestly admitting "the Buddha's own intent is difficult to know for certain" — is a rare moment of explicit interpretive humility, distinguishing what the source texts actually support from what this school's own systematic framework would prefer to find.

The Sphere of Boundless Consciousness 識處

Chinese Source

第二、明識處定者，亦為三：第一、釋名；二、修行方法；三、證相。第一、釋名者，所以名識處者，捨空緣識，以識為處，正從所緣處受名，故名識處。第二、修行方法者，有二種：一者、訶毀空處，讚歎識處；二者、觀破空處，繫緣念識處。云何名訶責空定？行者知空處定與空相應。虛空無邊，心緣虛空，緣多則散，能破於定。復次，虛空是外法，緣外法入定，定從外生，則不安隱過罪多，是名訶虛空定識處。既是內法緣內法入定，則多寂靜安隱，是故讚歎識處。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the concentration of the sphere of consciousness: this too has three parts — explaining the name; the method of cultivation; and the mark of realization.

First, explaining the name: as for why it is named the sphere of consciousness, one relinquishes emptiness and takes consciousness as one's object instead, taking consciousness itself as one's sphere, receiving its name, properly speaking, from the very object taken — hence it is named the sphere of consciousness.

Second, the method of cultivation: this is of two kinds — reproaching and disparaging the sphere of space, praising the sphere of consciousness instead; and contemplating and breaking through the sphere of space, fixing the mind on consciousness as its object. What is meant by reproaching the concentration of emptiness? The practitioner knows that the concentration of the sphere of space corresponds with emptiness — but empty space is boundless, and, the mind taking empty space as its object, this object being so vast, the mind readily scatters, able to break apart concentration itself. Furthermore: empty space is itself an external dharma, and, taking an external dharma as the object through which one enters concentration, this concentration arises from something external, and so is not truly secure, carrying many faults — this is what is meant by reproaching the concentration of the sphere of space, in favor of the sphere of consciousness. Since consciousness, by contrast, is an internal dharma, taking this internal dharma as the object through which one enters concentration, this concentration is, for the most part, still and secure — hence the sphere of consciousness is praised instead.

第二、觀破空處者，觀緣空，受、想、行、識如病如癰，如瘡如刺，無常、苦、空、無我，和合而有，欺誑不實。此即是八聖種觀。前四是對治方法，便是事觀；後無常等四，即是緣諦理觀。就此八種觀中，即有總、別。總者，用此八法總觀空處定，四陰和合，故有此定，可患無實。別觀者，用此八法，前四對治觀四陰事：如病者，對治受陰；如癰者，對治想陰；如瘡者，對治行陰；如刺者，對治識陰。復次，四無常等，即對觀四陰理相：無常觀識陰，苦觀受陰，空觀想陰，無我觀行陰。此事、理二觀，總別觀虛空處事理無可貪樂，即心易生厭，疾能捨離，善用念處中意，尋此別對之義可見。

Second, as for contemplating and breaking through the sphere of space: one contemplates that the very sensation, perception, formation, and consciousness taken as the object of emptiness are themselves like an illness, like a boil, like a wound, like a thorn — impermanent, suffering, empty, and without self, arisen only through the coming together of causes and conditions, deceptive and unreal. This is precisely the contemplation of the eight noble lineages. The first four serve as the antidote method, and are thus the contemplation of phenomenal conduct; the latter four, impermanence and the rest, are the contemplation of the truths and principle taken as their object.

Within these eight kinds of contemplation, there is, in turn, a general sense and a particular sense. The general sense: using these eight dharmas to contemplate the concentration of the sphere of space as a whole, the four aggregates combining together to produce this very concentration, worthy of concern and without any reality of its own. The particular sense: using these eight dharmas, the first four serve as the antidote, contemplating each of the four aggregates' own particular matter — illness treating the aggregate of sensation; boil treating the aggregate of perception; wound treating the aggregate of formation; and thorn treating the aggregate of consciousness. Furthermore, the latter four, impermanence and the rest, correspondingly contemplate each of the four aggregates' own principle: impermanence contemplating the aggregate of consciousness; suffering contemplating the aggregate of sensation; emptiness contemplating the aggregate of perception; and no-self contemplating the aggregate of formation. Through this twofold contemplation of phenomenal conduct and principle, general and particular, contemplating that the sphere of space's own phenomenal conduct and principle hold nothing worth craving or delighting in, the mind readily grows weary of it, quickly able to relinquish and leave it behind — properly applying the sense found within the foundations of mindfulness, this particular correspondence may be traced and understood.

問曰：離四禪時，何故但說三方便？今離四空定，說八聖種耶？答曰：空定既細，若不說聖種往觀，則過難見。問曰：若爾，凡夫無八聖種，云何得離？答曰：善修六行亦得離之，但不如八聖種疾。問曰：若修有漏禪，得用八聖種者，與無漏復有何異？答曰：今此中用八聖種，但是欲疾離下修上地定，不能即深觀自地，發無漏慧故，與無漏有異。

Question: in leaving the four dhyānas behind, only three expedient means were spoken of — why, then, in leaving the four concentrations of emptiness behind, are the eight noble lineages spoken of instead? Answer: the concentrations of emptiness are already fine and subtle, and, were the noble lineages' own contemplation not spoken of here, their fault would be difficult to perceive. Question: if this is so, ordinary people, lacking the eight noble lineages, how could they possibly leave these concentrations behind at all? Answer: properly cultivating the six practices is likewise able to achieve this, only not as quickly as the eight noble lineages. Question: if, even in cultivating the contaminated dhyānas, one is able to use the eight noble lineages, how, then, does this differ from the uncontaminated? Answer: what is used here, the eight noble lineages, is simply for the sake of quickly leaving the lower stage behind and cultivating the stage above it — one is not thereby able to directly and deeply contemplate one's own present stage and bring forth uncontaminated wisdom from it — this is how it differs from the truly uncontaminated.

次明繫心緣識。行者既善知空定過罪，心不喜樂，便捨空處，一心繫緣現在心識，念念不離。未來、過去亦復如是，常念於識，欲得與識相應，加功專至，不計旬月，一心緣識無異念。問曰：過去識已滅，未來未至，現在不住，云何可緣而入定耶？答曰：心識之法，實如所問，雖三世心識不可得，而亦可憶持。如過去瞋心已滅，不可復得，猶可憶知。亦如得他心智，即能知他三世之心，諸法雖空，而不斷故，何況自緣己三世識心而不得作入定因。緣此而推之，亦得有緣識入定之義。是故行者一心緣識，空定即謝，識定未生，中間亦例如前。問曰：若爾，亦說中間禪相耶？答曰：上已解之，其義可見。

Next, clarifying fixing the mind on consciousness as its object. The practitioner, having properly recognized the fault of the concentration of emptiness, the mind no longer delighting in it, thereupon relinquishes the sphere of space, fixing the mind single-mindedly instead on the present mind and consciousness as its object, moment to moment never departing from it. The same holds for the future and the past as well — constantly holding consciousness in mind, wishing to correspond fully with consciousness itself, applying effort with single-minded devotion, not counting the days or months that pass, holding the mind single-mindedly on consciousness with no other thought at all.

Question: past consciousness has already ceased, future consciousness has not yet arrived, and present consciousness does not remain fixed even for a moment — how, then, can one possibly take it as an object and enter concentration through it at all? Answer: the dharma of mind and consciousness is, in truth, exactly as this question describes — although the mind and consciousness of the three periods of time cannot, in themselves, be found, they can nonetheless still be recollected and retained. It is like an angry thought from the past, already ceased and no longer able to be found again, and yet still able to be recollected and known; or like one who has attained the wisdom of knowing others' minds, thereby able to know another's mind across all three periods of time as well — every dharma, though empty, is nonetheless not simply annihilated. How much more, then, could one's own mind and consciousness across the three periods of time, taken as one's own object, fail to serve as the very cause for entering concentration? Reasoning this through, one finds that there is indeed a genuine sense in which taking consciousness as an object allows one to enter concentration.

Hence the practitioner, holding the mind single-mindedly on consciousness as its object, the concentration of emptiness thereupon passes away, while the concentration of consciousness has not yet arisen — an intermediate stage is present here as well, following the same pattern as already given. Question: if this is so, should the mark of this intermediate dhyāna also be explained here? Answer: this has already been explained above, and its sense may be readily seen there.

第三、證相，亦有六義：一、證相；二、明支；三、體用；四、淺深；五、進退；六、功德。一、證定發相者，行者一心緣識，即便泯然，任運自住識緣因，此後豁然，與識相應，心定不動，而於定中不見餘事，唯見現在心識念念不住，定心分明，識慮廣闊，無量無邊，亦於定中憶過去已滅之識無量無邊，及未來應起之識亦無量無邊，悉現定中，與識法相應。識法持心，無分散意。此定安隱，清淨寂靜，心識明利，不可說也。問曰：行者未得三通，云何知三世心？答曰：此是三昧之力，類上四無量心，其義可知。二、明支者，如《纓絡經》說：「四空，五支，方便道同，用相似故。」例如空處，不煩更說。三、體用及淺深、進退、功德等，並類可知，今不別釋。

Third, the mark of realization: this too has six senses — the mark of realization; clarifying the factors; substance and function; depth; progress and regression; and merit.

First, the mark of this concentration's own arising: the practitioner, holding the mind single-mindedly on consciousness as its object, the mind grows indistinct, abiding of its own accord, spontaneously, within consciousness as its cause and object. Afterward, the mind opens out, corresponding with consciousness, the concentrated mind unmoving; and, within this concentration, one sees nothing else at all, only the present mind and consciousness, moment to moment never remaining still, the concentrated mind entirely clear, the scope of consciousness itself vast and expansive, measureless and boundless — and, within this same concentration, one also recollects the measureless, boundless consciousness of the past, already ceased, and the measureless, boundless consciousness of the future, yet to arise, all manifesting within this very concentration, corresponding with the dharma of consciousness itself. The dharma of consciousness sustains the mind, with no thought of scattering at all. This concentration is secure, pure, and still, mind and consciousness bright and sharp — beyond what words can convey.

Question: the practitioner has not yet attained the three supernormal penetrations — how, then, can the mind across the three periods of time be known at all? Answer: this is the very power of samādhi itself, following the same pattern as already given for the four immeasurable minds, and its sense may be understood accordingly.

Second, clarifying the factors: as the Yingluo Sūtra says, "the four concentrations of emptiness' own five factors, the expedient path being the same, their function resembling one another" — following the same pattern as the sphere of space, this need not be explained again here. Third, substance and function, and depth, progress and regression, and merit, all these, too, may be understood by the same pattern, and are not separately explained here.

Reading note: The response to "how can one take past, future, and momentary present consciousness as a stable object of concentration?" is a genuinely careful piece of philosophical reasoning: something can be causally efficacious (able to serve as "the very cause for entering concentration") without being findable as a fixed, persisting thing — the same point fascicle 3's tetralemma against "karma cannot be found" made about karma itself, now applied to consciousness as an object of meditation rather than an object of ethical analysis. The eight noble lineages' contemplation (illness/boil/wound/thorn paired with impermanence/suffering/emptiness/no-self, each pair mapped onto one of the four aggregates) gives the treatise's clearest statement yet of how its "antidote" contemplations (event-level, matched to a specific aggregate) and its "principle" contemplations (truth-level, cutting across all four) are meant to work together rather than as alternatives.

The Sphere of Nothingness 無所有處

Chinese Source

第三、明不用處，亦為三：一、釋名；二、明修行方法；三、明證相。一、釋名者，不用處者，修此定時，不用一切內外境界。外境名空，內境名心，捨此二境因，初修得名，故言不用處。亦名少處，亦名無所有處，亦名無想處，此三名從定體得名也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, explaining the sphere of nothingness: this too has three parts — explaining the name; clarifying the method of cultivation; and clarifying the mark of realization.

First, explaining the name: as for the sphere of nothingness, when cultivating this concentration, one makes use of no internal or external state at all. The external state is called "space"; the internal state is called "mind" — relinquishing the cause of both these states at the very outset of cultivation, this concentration receives its name — hence it is called the sphere of nothingness. It is also named the sphere of the small; also named the sphere of nothing whatsoever; and also named the sphere of no-thought — these three names are all taken from the substance of the concentration itself.

二、明修無所有處定方法，為二：一者、訶讚；二、觀行修習。云何訶責識處？行者深知識處過故。所以者何？識定心與識法相應，若於定中，心緣於識，過去、現在、未來心識悉無量無邊，若心緣無邊，緣多則散壞於定。復次，上緣空入定，名為外定。今緣識入定，名為內定。而依內依外，皆非寂靜。若依內心，以心緣心入定者，此定已依三世心生，非真實。唯有無心識處，心無依倚，乃名安隱。如是知已，讚無所有處。

Second, clarifying the method for cultivating the concentration of the sphere of nothingness: this is of two kinds — reproach and praise; and analytic contemplation.

What is meant by reproaching the sphere of consciousness? The practitioner deeply understands the fault of the sphere of consciousness. Why is this? The mind of this concentration corresponds with the dharma of consciousness, and, within this concentration, the mind takes consciousness as its object — the consciousness of the past, present, and future all being measureless and boundless; and, the mind taking a boundless object, this very vastness of object causes the mind to scatter, breaking apart concentration itself. Furthermore: taking emptiness as the object through which one previously entered concentration is named an "external" concentration; taking consciousness as the object through which one now enters concentration is named an "internal" concentration — but, whether relying on the internal or the external, neither is truly still. If one relies on the inner mind, taking mind as the object through which mind itself enters concentration, this concentration still depends upon the mind of the three periods of time for its arising, and so is not truly real. Only where there is no sphere of mind and consciousness at all present is the mind free of any reliance whatsoever — only this is properly named secure. Knowing this, one praises the sphere of nothingness instead.

二、觀行修習者，觀於緣識，受、想、行、識如病如癰，如瘡如刺，無常、苦、空、無我，和合而有，虛誑不實，義如前釋。如是知已，即捨識處，繫心無所有處。無所有處既無所依，緣心識則內靜息求，不用一切心識之法，知無所有法非空非識，無為法塵無有分別。如是知已，靜息其心，念無所有法，是時識定即謝，少定未發，於其中間證相如前說。問曰：有人言：修無所有，取少識，緣之入定，此事云何？答曰：不然。應捨一切，但念無所有法，故名無所有處。而說言少處者，但意根對無所有法塵，生於少處，非是緣少識入定，名為少處也。

Second, as for analytic contemplation: one contemplates that the very consciousness taken as an object, sensation, perception, formation, and consciousness, is itself like an illness, like a boil, like a wound, like a thorn, impermanent, suffering, empty, and without self, arisen only through the coming together of causes and conditions, deceptive and unreal, the sense of this exactly as already explained above. Knowing this, one relinquishes the sphere of consciousness, and fixes the mind instead on the sphere of nothingness. The sphere of nothingness, having nothing at all to rely upon, and taking mind and consciousness as an object now stilled and sought no further from within, makes use of no dharma of mind and consciousness whatsoever, knowing that the dharma of nothingness is neither empty nor consciousness, an unconditioned dharma-object with no discrimination present in it at all. Knowing this, one stills the mind, holding in mind the dharma of nothingness — at this point, the concentration of consciousness passes away, while the concentration of the small has not yet arisen; in this interval, the mark of realization is exactly as already described above.

Question: some say that, in cultivating the sphere of nothingness, one takes up a small portion of consciousness and takes this as the object through which one enters concentration — what should be made of this? Answer: this does not follow. One should relinquish everything entirely, simply holding in mind the dharma of nothingness — hence it is named the sphere of nothingness. As for why it is also spoken of as the sphere of "the small": this is simply because the mind-faculty, corresponding with the dharma-object of nothingness, gives rise to this sphere of "the small" — it is not that one takes a small portion of consciousness as the object through which one enters concentration that it comes to be named the sphere of "the small."

第三、明證相，亦為六：一者、正明證相；二者、明支；三、明體用；四、淺深；五、進退；六、功德。第一、明無所有定發相者，行者於中間，心不憂悔，專精不懈。一心內淨，空無所依，不見諸法，寂然安隱，心無動搖，此為證無所有定相。入此定時，怡然寂絕，諸想不起，尚不見心相，何況餘法？無所分別，是名無所有處定，亦名無想定。二、明支；三、明體用；四、明淺深；五、進退；六、功德，例如前說。

Third, clarifying the mark of realization: this too has six parts — directly clarifying the mark of realization; clarifying the factors; clarifying substance and function; depth; progress and regression; and merit.

First, clarifying the mark of the concentration of nothingness's own arising: the practitioner, within the intermediate stage, the mind free of worry or regret, applies effort with single-minded devotion, unflagging. The mind, single-mindedly pure within, empty, with nothing at all to rely upon, no dharma seen at all, still and secure, the mind free of any agitation whatsoever — this is the mark of realizing the concentration of nothingness. Entering this concentration, one is tranquil, utterly still, no thought arising at all — not even the mark of mind itself is seen, how much less any other dharma? Free of all discrimination whatsoever — this is named the concentration of the sphere of nothingness, also named the concentration of no-thought in this further, correct sense. Second, clarifying the factors; third, clarifying substance and function; fourth, clarifying depth; fifth, progress and regression; and sixth, merit — all follow the same pattern as already described above.

Reading note: The reproach of the sphere of consciousness — that relying on mind to take mind itself as an object still "depends upon the mind of the three periods of time for its arising, and so is not truly real" — pushes the same regress-avoidance logic used against form all the way to its natural limit: even consciousness, once made an object, is still a dependent, constructed thing rather than a genuine resting place. The etymological aside distinguishing the sphere of "nothingness" from the sphere of "the small" (a mind-faculty correspondence, not a residue of consciousness carried over) matters because it blocks a natural but mistaken reading — that this attainment is simply consciousness made very small rather than consciousness relinquished outright.

Neither Perception Nor Non-Perception: Its Name 非想非非想處 · 釋名

Chinese Source

第四、釋非想非非想定，亦為三：一、釋名；二、修行方法；三、證相。一、釋名者，言非想非非想者，解釋不同。有言：此定名一存一亡觀，所言非想者，非麁想，此則亡於麁想。非非想者，非非細想，此則存於細想。又解云：前觀識處是有想，不用處是無想。今雙除上二想，非想遣識處有想，非非想遣不用處無想故。又解言：若非有想者，此定中不見一切相貌故。言非有想非無想者，行人或作是念：若一向無想者，如木石無知，云何能知無想？故言非無想也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, explaining the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception: this too has three parts — explaining the name; the method of cultivation; and the mark of realization.

First, explaining the name: as for "neither perception nor non-perception," this is explained differently by different sources. Some say: this concentration is named the contemplation of one thing retained and one thing relinquished — as for "not perception," this means not coarse perception, this being what is relinquished, coarse perception; as for "not non-perception," this means not the absence of fine perception, this being what is retained, fine perception.

Another explanation says: the sphere of consciousness discussed earlier has perception present; the sphere of nothingness has no perception at all. Here, both of these two kinds of perception are simultaneously removed — "not perception" dispels the sphere of consciousness's own presence of perception, and "not non-perception" dispels the sphere of nothingness's own absence of perception.

A further explanation says: as for "not having perception," this is because, within this concentration, no mark or appearance whatsoever is seen. As for saying "neither perception nor non-perception": the practitioner might reflect: if it were an absolute absence of perception, this would be like wood or stone, without any awareness at all — how, then, could it possibly know that it is without perception? Hence it is said to be "not without perception" either.

問曰：非有想非無想中實有想，云何言無想耶？答曰：非想有四陰共成，豈得言無？但凡夫人入此定中，陰、界、入細故不覺，謂言無想。佛法中說有四陰共成，但因其本名，故言非有想非無想。亦有解言：約凡夫說，言非有想；約佛法中說，言非無想；合而論之，故言非有想非無想也。

Question: within "neither perception nor non-perception," perception is, in truth, genuinely present — how, then, can it be said to be "without perception"? Answer: this "non-perception" is itself made up of the four aggregates together — how, then, could it possibly be said to be entirely absent? It is simply that an ordinary person, entering this concentration, the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements being so fine, fails to perceive them, and so says there is "no perception" present. Within the Buddhadharma, it is said that the four aggregates together make up this concentration, but, following its own original name, it is still spoken of as "neither perception nor non-perception." There is also an explanation that says: spoken of with reference to the ordinary person, it is said to be "without perception"; spoken of with reference to the Buddhadharma, it is said to be "not without perception" — taken together, it is thus spoken of as "neither perception nor non-perception."

Reading note: The four competing etymologies offered without a forced choice among them is the treatise's characteristic move once again, but the substantive philosophical point underneath all four is sharper here than usual: a state can genuinely contain the four aggregates — and so, in the treatise's own technical sense, contain "perception" — while still feeling like the total absence of perception from within, simply because it has grown too fine for an untrained observer to detect. This is the same warning as the earlier aside on deviant no-thought concentration, now given its precise philosophical grounding: subjective absence of felt content is not the same as objective absence of mental activity.

Neither Perception Nor Non-Perception: Cultivation and Realization 非想非非想處 · 修行方法、證相

Chinese Source

第二、修行方法，亦有二：一者、訶讚；二者、觀行修習。訶責者，深知無想中過罪，是無所有定，如癡如醉，如眠如暗，無明覆蔽，無所覺了，無可愛樂，故《摩訶衍》云：「觀於識處如瘡如箭，觀無想處如癡，皆是心病，非真寂靜處。更有妙定，名曰非想，是處安隱，無諸過罪，我當求之。」二、明觀行修習。行者爾時諦觀無所緣，受、想、行、識如病如癰，如瘡如刺，無常、苦、空、無我，欺誑不實，和合而有，非實有。如是觀已，即便捨離，心觀於非有非無。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the method of cultivation: this too has two parts — reproach and praise; and analytic contemplation.

As for reproach: one deeply understands the fault within the concentration of no-thought — this concentration of nothingness resembles delusion, resembles intoxication, resembles sleep, resembles darkness, ignorance covering it over, nothing at all perceived clearly, nothing in it worth loving or delighting in — hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "Observing the sphere of consciousness as a wound, as an arrow; observing the sphere of nothingness as delusion — both are themselves illnesses of the mind, not a truly still place at all. There is a further, more subtle concentration, named 'neither perception,' where this place is truly secure, free of every such fault — I shall now seek it out."

Second, clarifying analytic contemplation. The practitioner, at this point, closely contemplates that there is nothing at all truly taken as an object — sensation, perception, formation, and consciousness are themselves like an illness, like a boil, like a wound, like a thorn, impermanent, suffering, empty, and without self, deceptive and unreal, arisen only through the coming together of causes and conditions, not truly existent at all. Having contemplated in this way, one relinquishes and leaves this behind, the mind now contemplating what is neither existent nor nonexistent.

何法非有？謂心非有。何以故？過去、現在、未來，求之都不可得，無有形相，亦無處所，當知非有。云何非無？若言是無，何名無為？心是無為，離心是無。若心是無，不名為心，以無覺無緣故；若心非無，更無別無。何以故？無不自無，破有故說無，無有則無無，故言非有非無。如是觀時，不見有無，一心緣中，不念餘事，是名修習非有想非無想定。如是即依非有非無，常念不捨，則不用處定便自謝滅，而非有想非無想定未發，於其中間亦如上說。

What dharma is "not existent"? The mind is said to be "not existent." Why is this? Sought throughout the past, present, and future, it can nowhere be found at all, having no form or mark, and no location either — hence it should be known as "not existent." How, then, is it "not nonexistent"? If it were said to be simply nonexistent, how could it then be named "unconditioned" at all? The mind is itself unconditioned; apart from mind, there is nonexistence. If mind itself were nonexistent, it could not be named "mind" at all, since it would then have no awareness and no object whatsoever; and if mind is not nonexistent, then there is no further, separate "nonexistence" to speak of either. Why is this? Nonexistence is not, in itself, self-subsisting — it is spoken of only in order to break through existence; and, once existence is gone, nonexistence, too, is gone along with it — hence it is said to be "neither existent nor nonexistent."

Contemplating in this way, seeing neither existence nor nonexistence, the mind held single-mindedly on this very object, taking up no other matter at all — this is named cultivating the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception. Continuing in this way, relying on what is neither existent nor nonexistent, holding this in mind constantly without relinquishing it, the concentration of nothingness thereupon passes away and is extinguished of itself, while the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception has not yet arisen; in this interval, the same holds as already described above.

第三、明證相，亦為六意：一者、正明證相；二、明支；三、明體用；四、明淺深；五、進退；六、功德。第一、明證相者，行者既一心專精，加功不已，其心任運，住在緣中，於後忽然真實定發，不見有無相貌，泯然寂絕，心無動搖，恬然清淨，如涅槃相。是定微妙，三界無過，外道證之，謂是中道、實相、涅槃、常、樂、我、淨，愛著是法，更不修習。彼若正觀，如步屈蟲行至樹表，更不復進，到退迴還。如經中說：「凡夫證此定法，如繩繫鳥，繩盡則還。」已其不知四陰和合而有自性。

Third, clarifying the mark of realization: this too has six parts — directly clarifying the mark of realization; clarifying the factors; clarifying substance and function; depth; progress and regression; and merit.

First, clarifying the mark of realization: the practitioner, single-mindedly devoted, applying effort without pause, the mind now abiding spontaneously within this object; afterward, suddenly, a truly genuine concentration is brought forth, seeing no mark of either existence or nonexistence at all, indistinct and utterly still, the mind free of any agitation whatsoever, tranquil and pure, resembling the very mark of nirvāṇa itself. This concentration is subtle and wondrous, unsurpassed within the three realms; non-Buddhist practitioners, realizing it, take it to be the middle way, the true mark, nirvāṇa itself, permanent, blissful, possessing self, and pure — clinging to this very dharma with attachment, and cultivating nothing further. Were they to contemplate it correctly, however, they would see it is like an inchworm crawling to the very tip of a branch, unable to advance any further, and forced to turn back the way it came. As the sūtra says: "When an ordinary person realizes this dharma of concentration, it is like a bird tied by a rope — once the rope has run its length, the bird is pulled back again." This is because they do not know that this concentration is itself made up of the four aggregates combining together, with no fixed nature of its own.

然其雖無麁煩惱，而亦成就十種細煩惱，以不知故，謂是真實，外道入此定中，不見有無，而覺有能知非有非無之心，即計此心謂是真神不滅，故言神至細，不破神能知。若佛弟子，知是四陰和合而有，虛誑不實，是中心想，故知無別神知。復次，前虛空處破色故說空，識處破空故說識，說識為有想，不用處破識故無識，說無識為無想。今此定破無所有，說非無想，故言非有想非無想。此定於世間中沈浮等故，智、定、空、有均平，是定安隱，於世間中最為尊勝，等智所不能破，故數人言：「一常有漏。」

And although such a person is free of coarse afflictions here, they are nonetheless still complete with ten kinds of subtle affliction — and, not knowing this, they take the state to be truly real. Non-Buddhist practitioners, entering this concentration, see neither existence nor nonexistence, and yet become aware of a mind that seems able to know this very neither-existence-nor-nonexistence — and, reckoning this very mind to be the true, imperishable spirit-self, they say that this spirit-self is exceedingly subtle, and cannot be shown to lack the capacity to know. A disciple of the Buddha, by contrast, knows that this, too, is nothing but the four aggregates combining together, deceptive and unreal, simply a state of mind occurring within it — and so knows there is no separate, knowing spirit-self present here at all.

Furthermore: the previous sphere of space breaks through form, and so is called "emptiness"; the sphere of consciousness breaks through emptiness, and so is called "consciousness," and consciousness is spoken of as having perception. The sphere of nothingness breaks through consciousness, and so is without consciousness, and this absence of consciousness is spoken of as "no perception." This present concentration breaks through nothingness itself, and so is spoken of as "not without perception" — hence it is called "neither perception nor non-perception." This concentration, relative to the mundane world, is evenly balanced, neither sinking nor rising — wisdom, concentration, emptiness, and existence all held in perfect equilibrium; this concentration is secure, the very most exalted and excellent within the mundane world, something ordinary, equalizing wisdom is unable to break through — hence the Ābhidharma masters say: "This alone is forever contaminated."

復次，無想有三義：一、無想天定；二、非有想非無想定；三、滅受想定。無方便外道滅心，入無想天定；有方便凡夫外道滅心，入非有想非無想定；佛弟子滅心，入滅受想定。問曰：無所有處亦名無想定，何故不入三種滅心耶？答曰：不善滅無所有中心數法故非妙。復次，若在色界，無想為極；若在無色界，非有想非無想為極。若佛法中，自有滅、受、想，不用處於三處中皆非勝定，故不取也。第二、明支；三、體用；四、淺深；五、進退；六、功德，義類前可知。

Furthermore: "no-thought" carries three distinct senses: the concentration of the heaven of no-thought; the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception; and the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception. The non-Buddhist practitioner without any proper skillful means, extinguishing the mind, enters the concentration of the heaven of no-thought; the ordinary, non-Buddhist practitioner with some skillful means, extinguishing the mind, enters the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception; and the Buddha's own disciple, extinguishing the mind, enters the concentration of the cessation of sensation and perception instead.

Question: the sphere of nothingness is also named the concentration of no-thought — why, then, is it not included among these three kinds of extinguished mind? Answer: it is because it does not properly extinguish the mental factors present even within nothingness itself, and so is not counted among the subtle ones. Furthermore: within the form realm, the heaven of no-thought is the very utmost; within the formless realm, neither perception nor non-perception is the very utmost. Within the Buddhadharma itself, there is properly the cessation of sensation and perception — the sphere of nothingness, among these three, is, in every case, not the superior concentration, and so is not taken as one of the three. Second, clarifying the factors; third, substance and function; fourth, depth; fifth, progress and regression; and sixth, merit — their sense may be understood by the same pattern as already given.

Reading note: The inchworm and tied-bird similes are among this treatise's most memorable images precisely because they describe failure rather than success — a state genuinely more refined than anything before it, mistaken by non-Buddhist practitioners for the finish line itself, when it is in fact the very last rung of a ladder that still, eventually, runs out. The "true, imperishable spirit-self" critique directly names the ātman doctrine central to competing Indian traditions, diagnosed here as a subtle category error: mistaking the bare fact that something is aware of this refined state for evidence that an independent, permanent knower exists behind the awareness. The threefold taxonomy of "no-thought" — deviant, refined-but-still-ordinary, and the properly Buddhist cessation of sensation and perception — gives exact coordinates for a distinction the fascicle has been building toward since its opening aside on deviant no-thought concentration.

Why the Formless Concentrations Belong to Prajñā 問答：般若與四無色

Chinese Source

般若滅一切法而能生一切法，如從初禪來滅憂，乃至非想非非想滅不用處之想，皆是般若中前方便，滅諸法為入空。以其滅諸法故，能生後勝法故，般若能生萬法故，此十二門禪皆般若氣分所攝。問曰：菩薩行菩提道，入實相空，尚不得空，今云何隨此不實顛倒之空分別有四耶？答曰：如《釋論》解四空義中說：「與諸法實相共智慧行，是四無色中，無有顛倒，是摩訶衍中四無色。」問曰：何等是諸法實相智？答曰：諸法自性空是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Prajñā extinguishes every dharma and yet is able to give rise to every dharma as well — just as, proceeding from the first dhyāna onward, sorrow is extinguished, all the way through neither perception nor non-perception extinguishing even the very perception of the sphere of nothingness, all of this is precisely the preliminary expedient means within prajñā itself, extinguishing every dharma in order to enter emptiness. Because these dharmas are thus extinguished, later, superior dharma is able to be brought forth in turn — and, since prajñā itself is able to give rise to the ten thousand dharmas, these twelve gates of dhyāna are all encompassed within prajñā's own essential nature.

Question: the bodhisattva, practicing the path to bodhi, enters the emptiness of the true mark itself, not even grasping at emptiness there — how, then, can it now be said that, following this unreal, inverted emptiness of the formless attainments, four distinct kinds are still distinguished? Answer: as the Exegetical Treatise explains in its own discussion of the meaning of the four emptinesses: "Practiced together with the wisdom of the true mark of all dharmas, these four formless attainments are free of any inversion at all — this is precisely the four formless attainments as understood within the Mahāyāna." Question: what, then, is this wisdom of the true mark of all dharmas? Answer: it is precisely that all dharmas are, of their own nature, empty.

問曰：色法和合，分別因緣故空，此無色中云何空？答曰：色是眼見耳聞，麁事能令空，何況不可見，無有對，不覺苦樂而不空耶？復次，色分別乃至微塵皆散滅歸空，是心心數法在日月時節乃至一念中不可得，是名真實四無色空義。菩薩如是知已，亦能分別種種諸相，以大悲方便為一切眾生故，行而不著。以此功德迴向菩提，具一切佛法，普施眾生，即是行菩薩道也。
釋禪波羅蜜次第法門卷第六

Question: form-dharmas, combining together, are distinguished as empty through their own causes and conditions — but how, then, can these formless attainments be said to be empty as well? Answer: form is what the eye sees and the ear hears — even this coarse matter can be shown to be empty; how much more, then, could what is invisible, without any resistance at all, and unfelt as either suffering or happiness, fail to be empty as well? Furthermore: form, analyzed down even to the finest particle, is entirely scattered and dissolved, returning to emptiness — and this very mind and its mental factors, examined across days, months, seasons, and even down to a single thought-moment, cannot be found at all either. This is what is truly meant by the emptiness of the four formless attainments.

The bodhisattva, having known this, is nonetheless still able to distinguish every kind of particular mark as well, and, through great compassion and skillful means, acts for the sake of all sentient beings without becoming attached to any of it. Dedicating this very merit toward bodhi, complete in the whole of the Buddhadharma, bestowing it universally upon sentient beings — this is precisely the practice of the bodhisattva path.
The Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen, Fascicle Six, ends here.

Reading note: This closing apologetic completes the argument fascicle 5 opened: dhyāna and prajñā are not two separate paths that need reconciling, because prajñā's own defining activity — extinguishing every dharma without becoming attached to the extinguishing — is exactly what every dhyāna already does at its own level, from the first dhyāna leaving behind sorrow to the formless attainments leaving behind perception itself. The "twelve gates of dhyāna" named here (the four dhyānas, four immeasurable minds, and four formless concentrations together) are thus reframed one final time as prajñā's own preliminary expedient means, not a lesser, contaminated detour from it. The extension of emptiness from form (visible, tangible, easy to doubt) to the formless attainments themselves (invisible, resistance-free, and thus even easier to mistake for something truly ultimate) closes the loop the earlier aside on deviant no-thought concentration and the inchworm simile both anticipated: the subtler an attainment feels, the more urgent it is not to mistake its subtlety for finality. This closes fascicle 6 in its entirety — the four immeasurable minds and the four formless concentrations complete, and, with them, the whole of mundane dhyāna (世間禪), the first of topic 7's four major divisions.
