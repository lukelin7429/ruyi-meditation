Why Cultivate Dhyāna at All

This page begins Ru-Yi's own English translation of Zhiyi's Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen (釋禪波羅蜜次第法門), the Tiantai founder's major treatise on dhyāna as a graduated pāramitā. We translate slowly, section by section, with the Chinese source beside the English so readers can study the meaning, not merely read around it.

Status: fascicle 1 complete (all 5 of its topics) Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T46, no. 1916 Covers: why cultivate dhyāna, its name, its three gates, the sequence of its attainments, and the states of mind proper to it

Project Background

Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597 CE) is the founder of the Tiantai school and one of the great systematizers of Chinese Buddhism. Like several of his major works, this treatise was not written but spoken: delivered as oral lectures, taken down by his disciple Fashen (法慎), and later revised into its final form by his other principal disciple, Guanding (灌頂). It runs to ten fascicles and treats dhyāna — meditative absorption — not as a single technique but as a pāramitā, a perfection walked step by step: from the right motivation for practice, through preliminary conditions, method, and cultivation, to realization and its fruits.

No complete English translation of this treatise is known to exist. A 2021 academic article by an Italian scholar translates a portion of fascicle 1 for scholarly analysis, but not the work as a whole. If this project is carried through to completion, it will likely be the first full English translation of this treatise.

Zhiyi also wrote two considerably shorter meditation manuals, the Six Dharma Gates to the Sublime (六妙法門) and the Concise Śamatha-Vipaśyanā (童蒙止觀, also called the Xiao Zhiguan), both already available in a published English translation by Bhikshu Dharmamitra. This treatise is a distinct, much longer work — not a variant of either of those two — and this translation is made independently from the Chinese, without reference to that published translation of Zhiyi's other texts.

Translation Method

  • Made directly from the Chinese source text preserved by CBETA, which is in the public domain — Zhiyi died in 597 CE, well over a millennium ago.
  • We keep Zhiyi's own ten-topic outline (十意), stated at the very opening of fascicle 1, as the structure for the whole treatise — see the translation notes for the running glossary.
  • CBETA's edition splits several fascicles into "upper" and "lower" halves (之上 / 之下) for print-volume reasons; we treat both halves as one continuous fascicle, matching how the content is actually organized.
  • Every completed section can be revised after review by Dharma teachers and readers familiar with the Chinese text.

The Ten Topics 大開十意

Chinese Source

《釋禪波羅蜜次第法門》，大開為十意不同。所言十意者：修禪波羅蜜大意第一，釋禪波羅蜜名第二，明禪波羅蜜門第三，辨禪波羅蜜詮次第四，簡禪波羅蜜法心第五，分別禪波羅蜜前方便第六，釋禪波羅蜜修證第七，顯示禪波羅蜜果報第八，從禪波羅蜜起教第九，結會禪波羅蜜歸趣第十。

Ru-Yi Translation

This treatise, Explaining the Sequential Dharma-Gates of Dhyāna-Pāramitā, opens broadly into ten distinct topics. The ten topics are: first, the great intent of cultivating dhyāna-pāramitā; second, explaining the name of dhyāna-pāramitā; third, clarifying the gates of dhyāna-pāramitā; fourth, distinguishing the sequence in which dhyāna-pāramitā is explained; fifth, discriminating the states of mind proper to dhyāna-pāramitā's method; sixth, distinguishing the preliminary expedient means of dhyāna-pāramitā; seventh, explaining the cultivation and realization of dhyāna-pāramitā; eighth, revealing the fruits and rewards of dhyāna-pāramitā; ninth, the teachings that arise from dhyāna-pāramitā; and tenth, gathering together and returning to where dhyāna-pāramitā ultimately leads.

今約此十義以辨禪波羅蜜者，文則略收諸佛教法之始終，理則遠通如來之祕藏。一切圓妙法界，若教若行，若事若理，始從凡夫，終至極聖，所有因、果、行、位悉在其中。若行人深達禪門意趣，則自然解了一切佛法，不俟餘尋。故《摩訶衍》云：「譬如牽衣一角，則眾處皆動。」

To treat dhyāna-pāramitā by means of these ten topics is, in its written form, to gather in outline the whole beginning and end of every buddha's teaching; in its principle, it reaches far into the hidden treasury of the Tathāgata himself. The entire perfect and wondrous dharma-realm — every teaching and every practice, every phenomenon and every principle, from the ordinary person at the very start to the ultimate sage at the very end — with every cause, fruit, practice, and stage, is contained within it. If a practitioner penetrates deeply into the intent of these gates of dhyāna, they will naturally come to understand the whole of the Buddhadharma, with no need to search further. Hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "It is like tugging at one corner of a garment — every part of it moves."

所以第一先明修禪波羅蜜大意者，菩薩發心，所為正求菩提淨妙之法，必須簡擇真偽，善識祕要。若欲具足一切諸佛法藏，唯禪為最。如得珠玉，眾寶皆獲。是故發意修禪。既欲修習，應知名字，尋名取理，其義不虛。以釋禪名，尋名求理，理則非門不通。次明禪門。

This is why the first topic explains, first of all, the great intent of cultivating dhyāna-pāramitā: when a bodhisattva sets the mind on the pure and wondrous Dharma of bodhi, they must discriminate the true from the false, and skillfully recognize what is essential. If one wishes to be complete in the entire treasury of every buddha's Dharma, dhyāna alone is foremost — like obtaining a single pearl through which every other treasure is gained at once. This is why the mind is first set on cultivating dhyāna. Having resolved to cultivate it, one should know its name; by tracing the name one grasps the principle, and the meaning is not empty. Hence the name "dhyāna" is explained — tracing the name to seek the principle, and the principle cannot be entered except through its gate. Next, then, the gates of dhyāna are clarified.

禪定幽遠，無由頓入，必須從淺至深，故應辯詮次。夫欲涉淺遊深，復當善識禪中境智，是以次簡法心。既明識法心，若欲習行，事須善巧，次分別方便。依法而行，必有所證，次釋修證。若得內心相應，因成則感果，次顯示果報。從因至果，自行既圓，便樹立益物之功，次釋教門。理教既已圓備，法相同歸平等一實之道，次結會指歸。

Since dhyāna-concentration is deep and far-reaching, it cannot be entered all at once; one must proceed from the shallow to the deep, and so the order of its exposition must be distinguished. And in order to wade from the shallows into the depths, one must also skillfully recognize the objects and the wisdom at work within dhyāna — hence the states of mind proper to its method are next discriminated. Once the states of mind proper to the method are understood, if one wishes to put it into practice, skillful means are needed — hence the preliminary expedient means are next distinguished. Practicing in accordance with the Dharma, one is certain to attain realization — hence cultivation and realization are next explained. Once inner accord is attained, the cause, once complete, brings forth its fruit — hence the fruits and rewards are next revealed. Once the practice for oneself, from cause to fruit, is complete, one then establishes the work of benefiting others — hence the gate of teaching is next explained. Once both principle and teaching are complete, all dharma-characteristics return alike to the one real way of equality — hence the topics are last gathered together and their destination pointed out.

以此十義相生，辯釋禪波羅蜜，總攝一切眾行法門。至下尋文，泠然可見。故《大品經》云：「菩薩從初已來，住禪波羅蜜中，具足修一切佛法，乃至坐道場，成一切種智，起轉法輪，是名菩薩次第行、次第學、次第道。」

By these ten topics, arising one from another, dhyāna-pāramitā is analyzed and explained, gathering together every dharma-gate of practice as a whole. As the text is followed further below, this will become clear of itself. Hence the Larger Sūtra says: "From the very beginning, the bodhisattva abides within dhyāna-pāramitā, cultivating the entire Buddhadharma in full, until at last sitting at the seat of enlightenment, accomplishing the wisdom that knows all modes, and turning the wheel of the Dharma. This is called the bodhisattva's sequential practice, sequential study, sequential path."

Reading note: This opening sets the whole treatise's method: ten topics, each one generating the need for the next, so that the order of exposition is itself an argument, not just a table of contents. The chain runs from motivation (why cultivate dhyāna at all) through name, gates, sequence, and mental states, into actual practice — expedient means, cultivation, realization, fruit — and out again into teaching others and, finally, the single "real way of equality" everything was always pointing toward. Ru-Yi's translation follows this same sequence fascicle by fascicle; see the translation notes for how key terms here — dhyāna-pāramitā, the "Mahāyāna treatise," the "Larger Sūtra" — are rendered consistently going forward.

I. The Great Intent of Cultivating Dhyāna-Pāramitā 修禪波羅蜜大意第一

Zhiyi turns to the first of his ten topics. Before saying anything about how to cultivate dhyāna, he insists on asking why — and on ruling out nine ways of answering that question wrongly before giving the tenth, correct one.

Rejecting What Is Wrong 簡非

Chinese Source

今明菩薩修禪波羅蜜，所為有二：一者、簡非；二者、正明所為。第一、簡非者，有十種行人，發心修禪不同，多墮在邪僻，不入禪波羅蜜法門。何等為十？

Ru-Yi Translation

Here the bodhisattva's cultivation of dhyāna-pāramitā is explained under two headings: first, rejecting what is wrong; second, rightly clarifying its true purpose. First, as to rejecting what is wrong: there are ten kinds of practitioners whose resolve to cultivate dhyāna differs from the rest, and who mostly fall into deviance, never entering the dharma-gate of dhyāna-pāramitā. What are these ten?

一、為利養故，發心修禪，多屬發地獄心。二、邪偽心生，為名聞稱歎故，發心修禪，多屬發鬼神心。三、為眷屬故，發心修禪，多屬發畜生心。四、為嫉妬勝他故，發心修禪，多屬發修羅心。五、為畏惡道苦報，息諸不善業故，發心修禪，多屬發人心。六、為善心安樂故，發心修禪，多屬發六欲天心。七、為得勢力自在故，發心修禪，多屬發魔羅心。八、為得利智捷疾故，發心修禪，多屬發外道心。九、為生梵天處故修禪，此屬發色、無色界心。十、為度老病死苦，疾得涅槃故，發心修禪，此屬發二乘心。

1. Setting the mind on cultivating dhyāna for the sake of profit and support — this mostly belongs to a mind bound for the hells.
2. A deceitful mind arising, setting the mind on cultivating dhyāna for the sake of fame and praise — this mostly belongs to a mind bound for the realm of ghosts and spirits.
3. Setting the mind on cultivating dhyāna for the sake of one's followers — this mostly belongs to a mind bound for the animal realm.
4. Setting the mind on cultivating dhyāna out of jealousy and the wish to surpass others — this mostly belongs to a mind bound for the realm of asuras.
5. Setting the mind on cultivating dhyāna out of fear of the suffering retribution of the evil destinies, to put unwholesome karma to rest — this mostly belongs to an ordinary human mind.
6. Setting the mind on cultivating dhyāna for the sake of a wholesome mind's own ease and happiness — this mostly belongs to a mind bound for the six heavens of desire.
7. Setting the mind on cultivating dhyāna for the sake of gaining power and mastery — this mostly belongs to a mind bound for the realm of Māra.
8. Setting the mind on cultivating dhyāna for the sake of gaining sharp and swift intelligence — this mostly belongs to a mind bound for the non-Buddhist paths.
9. Cultivating dhyāna for the sake of being born in the Brahmā heavens — this belongs to a mind bound for the form and formless realms.
10. Setting the mind on cultivating dhyāna in order to cross over the suffering of old age, sickness, and death, and swiftly attain nirvāṇa — this belongs to a mind bound for the two lesser vehicles.

就此十種行人，善惡雖殊，縛脫有異，既並無大悲正觀，發心邪僻，皆墮二邊，不趣中道。若住此心修行禪定，終不得與禪波羅蜜法門相應。

Among these ten kinds of practitioners, though good and evil differ, and bondage and liberation differ, none of them possess great compassion or correct contemplation; the mind they set is deviant, and every one falls to one of the two extremes, never reaching the middle way. If one dwells in such a mind while cultivating dhyāna-concentration, one will never come into accord with the dharma-gate of dhyāna-pāramitā.

Reading note: The ten wrong motivations are graded, not random — they run from outright unwholesome (profit, fame, faction, envy: motivations that Zhiyi says point straight at unfortunate rebirths) through ordinary wholesome ones (fear of suffering, a wish for ease: a merely human or heavenly rebirth) to motivations that look impressive but are still wrong (power, sharp intelligence, rebirth in the Brahmā heavens) and finally to the subtlest failure of all: motivation ten, cultivating dhyāna simply to escape one's own suffering and reach nirvāṇa quickly. That last one is not "evil" in any ordinary sense — it is the motivation of the śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha, the two lesser vehicles — but Zhiyi still classifies it among the nine to reject, because it lacks the one thing that defines the tenth, correct motivation still to come: great compassion for others, not just concern for one's own liberation.

Rightly Clarifying the True Purpose 正明所為 · 發心之相

Chinese Source

第二、正明菩薩行人修禪波羅蜜大意，即為二意：一、先明菩薩發心之相；二、正明菩薩修禪所為。第一、云何名菩薩發心之相？所謂發菩提心。菩提心者，即是菩薩以中道正觀，以諸法實相，憐愍一切，起大悲心，發四弘誓願。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, rightly clarifying the great intent with which a bodhisattva-practitioner cultivates dhyāna-pāramitā: this too has two parts — first, clarifying the mark of a bodhisattva's arising resolve; second, rightly clarifying the true purpose for which a bodhisattva cultivates dhyāna. First, what is called the mark of a bodhisattva's arising resolve? It is what is called the arising of the bodhi-mind. The bodhi-mind is this: through the correct contemplation of the middle way, and through the true mark of all dharmas, the bodhisattva feels compassion for all beings, gives rise to the mind of great compassion, and sets forth the four universal vows.

四弘誓願者：一、未度者令度，亦云眾生無邊誓願度。二、未解者令解，亦云煩惱無數誓願斷。三、未安者令安，亦云法門無盡誓願知。四、未得涅槃令得涅槃，亦云無上佛道誓願成。此之四法，即對四諦。故《纓絡經》云：「未度苦諦令度苦諦，未解集諦令解集諦，未安道諦令安道諦，未證滅諦令證滅諦。」

The four universal vows are these: first, to bring across those not yet brought across — also stated as, "sentient beings are numberless; I vow to bring them across." Second, to bring understanding to those who do not yet understand — also stated as, "afflictions are countless; I vow to sever them." Third, to bring peace to those not yet at peace — also stated as, "dharma-gates are inexhaustible; I vow to know them." Fourth, to bring to nirvāṇa those who have not yet attained it — also stated as, "the supreme buddha-path is unsurpassed; I vow to accomplish it." These four things correspond exactly to the Four Noble Truths. Hence the Yingluo Sūtra says: "To bring across those not yet across the truth of suffering; to bring understanding of the truth of accumulation to those who do not yet understand it; to bring peace through the truth of the path to those not yet at peace; and to bring to realization the truth of cessation for those who have not yet realized it."

而此四法若在二乘心中，但受諦名，以其緣理審實不謬故；若在菩薩心中，即別受弘誓之稱。所以者何？菩薩雖知四法畢竟空寂，而為利益眾生，善巧方便，緣此四法，其心廣大，故名為弘。慈悲憐愍，志求此法，心如金剛，制心不退不沒，必取成滿，故名誓願。

Yet when these four things are held in the mind of the two lesser vehicles, they receive only the name "truth," since their contemplation of principle is exact and unmistaken; but when held in the mind of a bodhisattva, they receive instead the distinct name "universal vow." Why is this? Although the bodhisattva knows these four things to be, in the end, empty and still, yet for the benefit of sentient beings, through skillful means, taking hold of these four things, the mind becomes vast and expansive — hence it is called "universal." Out of compassion and loving-kindness, aspiring to and seeking this Dharma, the mind becomes like vajra, holding itself without retreat and without sinking, resolved without fail to bring it to completion — hence it is called "vow."

行者若能具足發此四願，善知四心，攝一切心，一切心即是一心，亦不得一心而具一切心，是名清淨菩提之心。因此心生，得名菩薩。故《摩訶衍論》偈說：
若初發心時，誓願當作佛，
已過於世間，應受世供養。

If a practitioner can set forth these four vows in full, skillfully knowing these four minds, gathering in every mind — every mind is itself the one mind, and yet the one mind is not grasped at, while it is complete with every mind — this is called the pure mind of bodhi. Because this mind arises, one comes to be called a bodhisattva. Hence the Mahāyāna treatise says in verse:
"At the very moment the mind first arises,
Vowing that one shall become a buddha —
Already one has gone beyond the world,
And is fit to receive the world's own offerings."

Reading note: The same four vows, held by a śrāvaka or a bodhisattva, get different names — Zhiyi is careful to say this is not a difference in the content of the four truths themselves (both understand them "exactly and unmistaken"), but a difference in scope and resolve. "Universal" (弘) names the scope: taken up not for oneself alone but on behalf of all beings, out of a mind made "vast and expansive" by that concern. "Vow" (誓願) names the resolve: a mind "like vajra," unable to retreat or sink until the vow is actually fulfilled. This is also where the bodhisattva's paradoxical relationship to emptiness first appears in this fascicle: the bodhisattva "knows these four things to be, in the end, empty and still" — exactly what the two lesser vehicles also realize — and takes up the vow anyway, for the sake of others. Emptiness does not cancel the vow here; it is what allows the vow to be taken up without clinging.

Why Dhyāna, Specifically 正明菩薩修禪所為

Chinese Source

第二、正明菩薩行人修禪所為者，菩薩摩訶薩既已發菩提心，思惟為欲滿足四弘誓願，必須行菩薩道。所以者何？有願而無行，如欲度人彼岸，不肯備於船筏，當知常在此岸，終不得度。如病者須藥，得而不服，當知病者必定不差。如貧須珍寶，見而不取，當知常弊窮乏。如欲遠行，而不涉路，當知此人不至所在。菩薩發四弘誓，不修四行亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, rightly clarifying the true purpose for which a bodhisattva-practitioner cultivates dhyāna: once the bodhisattva-mahāsattva has given rise to the bodhi-mind, reflecting on the wish to fulfill the four universal vows, they must necessarily practice the bodhisattva path. Why is this? To have the vow but not the practice is like wishing to ferry someone across to the other shore while refusing to prepare a boat or raft — know that such a person will remain forever on this shore, never able to cross. It is like a sick person who needs medicine, but, having obtained it, does not take it — know that such a sick person will certainly not recover. It is like a poor person who needs treasure, but, seeing it, does not take hold of it — know that such a person will remain forever destitute. It is like someone wishing to travel far, who does not set foot on the road — know that such a person will never arrive where they intend. A bodhisattva who sets forth the four universal vows without cultivating the four practices needed to fulfill them is exactly the same.

復作是念：「我今住何法門修菩薩道，能得疾滿如此四願？」即知住深禪定，能滿四願。何以故？如無六通、四辯，以何等法而度眾生。若修六通，非禪不發，故經言：「深修禪定，得五神通。」欲斷煩惱，非禪不智。從禪發慧，能斷結使。無定之慧，如風中燈。欲知法門，當知一切功德智慧並在禪中，如《摩訶衍論》云：「若諸佛成道，起轉法輪，入般涅槃，所有種種功德悉在禪中。」

The bodhisattva then further reflects: "By abiding in what dharma-gate, cultivating the bodhisattva path, can I swiftly fulfill these four vows?" And thereupon comes to know that abiding in deep dhyāna-concentration can fulfill the four vows. Why is this? Without the six supernormal powers and the four unhindered eloquences, by what means could one bring sentient beings across? And the six supernormal powers are never brought forth except through dhyāna — hence the sūtra says: "Deeply cultivating dhyāna-concentration, one attains the five supernormal powers." To sever affliction, there is no wisdom apart from dhyāna: wisdom arises from dhyāna, and it is this that can sever the fetters. Wisdom without concentration is like a lamp in the wind. To know the dharma-gates, one should know that every merit and every wisdom is contained within dhyāna — as the Mahāyāna treatise says: "Whatever merits belong to the buddhas' accomplishing the path, turning the wheel of the Dharma, and entering parinirvāṇa, every one of them is contained within dhyāna."

復次，菩薩入無量義處三昧，一心具足萬行，能知一切無量法門。若欲具足無上佛道，不修禪定，尚不能得色、無色界及三乘道，何況能得無上菩提？當知欲證無上妙覺，必須先入金剛三昧，而諸佛法乃現在前。菩薩如是深心思惟，審知禪定能滿四願，如《摩訶衍》偈說：
禪為利智藏，功德之福田；禪如清淨水，能洗諸欲塵；禪為金剛鎧，能遮煩惱箭。雖未得無為，涅槃分已得。得金剛三昧，摧碎結使山。得六神通力，能度無量人。囂塵蔽天日，大雨能淹之。覺觀風動之，禪定能滅之。

Furthermore, when a bodhisattva enters the samādhi of the place of measureless meanings, the one mind is complete with the ten thousand practices, able to know every one of the measureless dharma-gates. If one wishes to be complete in the unsurpassed buddha-path without cultivating dhyāna-concentration, one could not even attain the form and formless realms or the paths of the three vehicles — how much less the unsurpassed bodhi? One should know that to realize unsurpassed wondrous enlightenment, one must first enter the vajra-samādhi, and only then do all the buddhadharmas actually stand present. Reflecting thus, deep in mind, the bodhisattva comes to know for certain that dhyāna-concentration can fulfill the four vows — as the Mahāyāna treatise says in verse:
"Dhyāna is the treasury of sharp wisdom, the field of merit that bears its fruit;
Dhyāna is like clear, pure water, able to wash away every dust of desire;
Dhyāna is a vajra armor, able to turn back the arrows of affliction.
Though the unconditioned is not yet attained, a portion of nirvāṇa is already gained.
Attaining the vajra-samādhi, one shatters the mountain of the fetters to dust;
Attaining the power of the six supernormal faculties, one can bring countless beings across.
As clamorous dust obscures the sky and sun, yet a great rain can settle it entirely —
So the wind of coarse and subtle discursive thought stirs the mind, yet dhyāna-concentration can still it."

Reading note: Notice the shape of the argument: Zhiyi does not simply assert that dhyāna is important — he traces a dependency chain. The four vows require the bodhisattva path; the bodhisattva path requires the six supernormal powers and four unhindered eloquences to actually help anyone; the six supernormal powers, wisdom sharp enough to sever affliction, and every buddha's own accomplishment of the path all, without exception, depend on dhyāna. The verse's images are worth sitting with individually — dhyāna as treasury, clear water, armor, and, in the final couplet, as what can still "the wind of coarse and subtle discursive thought" (覺觀, vitarka and vicāra) the way a heavy rain settles a dust storm. That closing image previews a distinction — discursive thought that is coarse (覺) versus subtle (伺/觀) — that recurs throughout this treatise's account of meditative absorption.

An Objection: Why Withdraw at All? 十波羅蜜攝於一禪 · 問答

Chinese Source

問曰：菩薩若欲滿足四弘誓願，應當遍行十波羅蜜，何得獨讚禪定？答曰：前四義劣，後五因禪，今則處中而說。所以者何？菩薩修禪，即能具足增上四度，下五亦然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: If a bodhisattva wishes to fulfill the four universal vows, should they not practice all ten pāramitās alike? Why single out dhyāna-concentration alone for praise? Answer: Of the ten pāramitās, the first four, taken alone, are the lesser in meaning, and the latter five depend on dhyāna; here, then, dhyāna is spoken of as occupying the very center between them. Why is this? Because when a bodhisattva cultivates dhyāna, the four preceding perfections are thereby brought to completion in a higher degree, and the same holds for the five that follow.

如菩薩發心為修禪故，一切家業內外皆捨，不惜身命，寂然閑居，無所慳吝，是名大捨。復次，菩薩為修禪故，身心不動，關閉六情，惡無從入，名大持戒。復次，菩薩為修禪故，能忍難忍，謂一切榮辱皆能安忍，設為眾惡來加，恐障三昧，不生瞋惱，名為忍辱。復次，菩薩為修禪故，一心專精進，設身疲苦，終不退息，如鑽火之喻，常坐不臥，攝諸亂意，未甞放逸，設復經年無證，亦不退沒，是為難行之事，即是大精進也。故知修禪因緣，雖不作意別行四度，四度自成。

Thus: because the bodhisattva sets the mind on cultivating dhyāna, every family estate, inward and outward alike, is relinquished; life itself is not spared; one dwells in still and quiet seclusion, with nothing held back in miserliness — this is called great giving. Furthermore, because the bodhisattva cultivates dhyāna, body and mind remain unmoved, the six faculties of feeling are closed off, and evil has no way to enter — this is called great upholding of precepts. Furthermore, because the bodhisattva cultivates dhyāna, they are able to bear what is hard to bear — every honor and disgrace alike can be calmly endured; and should every kind of ill-treatment be inflicted on them, fearing only that it might obstruct their samādhi, no anger or vexation arises — this is called patience. Furthermore, because the bodhisattva cultivates dhyāna, the mind is single and wholly devoted to diligence; even should the body grow weary and pained, they never retreat or rest — as in the simile of drilling for fire — sitting always, never lying down, gathering in every scattered thought, never once indulging in laxity; and even should years pass without realization, still they do not retreat or sink. This is a matter hard to carry out, and is itself great diligence. Hence it can be known: through the causes and conditions of cultivating dhyāna, even without deliberately practicing the preceding four perfections separately, the four perfections come to completion of themselves.

復次，菩薩因修禪定，具足般若波羅蜜者，菩薩修禪，一心正住，心在定故，能知世間生滅法相，智慧勇發，如石中泉，故《摩訶衍》偈說：
般若波羅蜜，實法不顛倒。念想觀已除，言語法皆滅，無量眾罪除，清淨心常一，如是尊妙人，則能見般若。

Furthermore, as for the bodhisattva's becoming complete in the perfection of wisdom through cultivating dhyāna-concentration: when the bodhisattva cultivates dhyāna, the one mind abides correctly, and because the mind is fixed in concentration, it is able to know the marks of arising and ceasing in worldly dharmas, and wisdom bursts forth with vigor, like a spring breaking out from within rock — hence the Mahāyāna treatise says in verse:
"The perfection of wisdom is the true Dharma, free of every inversion.
Once thought, conception, and discursive contemplation are removed,
Every dharma of speech and language is likewise extinguished;
Measureless offenses are removed, and the pure mind remains ever one —
Only a person so honored and wondrous as this can truly see wisdom itself."

復次，因禪具足方便波羅蜜者，一切方便善巧，要須見機，若不入深禪定，云何能得明見根性，起諸方便，引接眾生？復次，因禪具足力波羅蜜者，一切自在變現，諸神通力皆藉禪發，具如前辨。復次，因禪具足願波羅蜜者，如《摩訶衍》中說：「菩薩禪定，如阿修羅琴。」當知即是大願成就之相。復次，因禪具足智波羅蜜者，若一切智、道種智、一切種智，非定不發，其義可見。行者善修禪故，即便成就十波羅蜜，滿足萬行一切法門。是故菩薩欲具一切願行諸波羅蜜，要修禪定，是事如《摩訶衍論》中說。

Furthermore, as for the perfection of skillful means becoming complete through dhyāna: every skillful means depends on discerning the occasion; if one does not enter deep dhyāna-concentration, how could one clearly see a being's own capacity, and give rise to the skillful means needed to draw them in? Furthermore, as for the perfection of power becoming complete through dhyāna: every manner of free and self-mastered manifestation, every supernormal power, is brought forth only by means of dhyāna, exactly as already distinguished above. Furthermore, as for the perfection of vow becoming complete through dhyāna: as the Mahāyāna treatise says, "A bodhisattva's dhyāna-concentration is like the asura's lute." One should know that this is precisely the mark of the great vow's own accomplishment. Furthermore, as for the perfection of wisdom becoming complete through dhyāna: whether it is the wisdom that knows all things generally, the wisdom that knows the seeds of every path, or the wisdom that knows all things in every one of their modes — none of these is brought forth apart from concentration; the meaning of this can be seen for oneself. Because the practitioner skillfully cultivates dhyāna, the ten pāramitās are thereby accomplished at once, and the ten thousand practices — every dharma-gate — are fulfilled. This is why, if a bodhisattva wishes to be complete in every vow and practice of all the pāramitās, dhyāna-concentration must be cultivated — this matter is exactly as stated in the Mahāyāna treatise.

問曰：菩薩之法，正以度眾生為事，何故獨處空山，棄捨眾生，閑居自善？答曰：菩薩身雖捨離，而心不捨。如人有病，將身服藥，暫息事業，病差則修業如故。菩薩亦爾，身雖暫捨眾生，而心常憐愍，於閑靜處服禪定藥，得實智慧，除煩惱病，起六神足，還生六道，廣度眾生。以如是等種種因緣，菩薩摩訶薩發意修禪波羅蜜，心如金剛，天魔、外道及諸二乘無能沮壞。

Question: The bodhisattva's own Dharma takes bringing sentient beings across as its very task — why, then, dwell alone in an empty mountain, abandoning sentient beings, living in quiet seclusion for one's own good alone? Answer: Though the bodhisattva's body withdraws, the mind does not withdraw. It is like someone who is sick: taking the body to receive medicine, they set worldly affairs briefly to rest, and once the illness is cured, they resume their work exactly as before. So too with the bodhisattva: though the body temporarily withdraws from sentient beings, the mind remains ever compassionate; in a quiet, secluded place, taking the medicine of dhyāna-concentration, they attain true wisdom, remove the sickness of affliction, and give rise to the six supernormal faculties — and then return again into the six destinies, bringing sentient beings across far and wide. For all these various causes and conditions, the bodhisattva-mahāsattva sets the mind on cultivating dhyāna-pāramitā, the mind become like vajra — one that no heavenly demon, non-Buddhist path, or follower of the two lesser vehicles can ever obstruct or destroy.

Reading note: This closing stretch does two things. First, it works through all ten pāramitās one at a time to show that dhyāna is not merely one perfection among ten, but the hinge the other nine turn on — giving, precepts, patience, and diligence arise as side effects of sustained meditative discipline, while wisdom, skillful means, power, vow, and a further wisdom all depend on concentration directly. Second, it answers the standing objection to this whole picture: doesn't retreating to meditate abandon the very beings the bodhisattva vowed to save? Zhiyi's answer turns on a body/mind distinction and a medical simile — withdrawal is not abandonment but treatment, temporary and instrumental, undertaken so that the bodhisattva can "return again into the six destinies" with something actually worth bringing. This closes topic 1.

II. Explaining the Name of Dhyāna-Pāramitā 釋禪波羅蜜名第二

Having established why dhyāna should be cultivated at all, Zhiyi turns to the treatise's second topic: what the name "dhyāna-pāramitā" itself actually means. He works through it in three stages — distinguishing a shared sense of the name from an unshared one, translating each half of the compound term, and finally resolving a set of standing objections.

A Shared Name, and an Unshared Name 簡別共不共名

Chinese Source

今釋禪波羅蜜名，略為三意：一、先簡別共不共名；二、翻釋；三、料簡。第一、簡別共不共名，即為二意：一、共名；二、不共名。共名者，如禪一字，凡夫、外道、二乘、菩薩、諸佛所得禪定，通得名禪，故名為共。不共名者，波羅蜜三字，名到彼岸，此但據菩薩、諸佛，故《摩訶衍論》云：「禪在菩薩心中，名波羅蜜。」是名不共。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here the name "dhyāna-pāramitā" is explained under, in brief, three headings: first, distinguishing the shared name from the unshared name; second, its etymological translation; third, resolving points of doubt. First, distinguishing the shared name from the unshared name: this too has two parts — the shared name, and the unshared name. As for the shared name: the single word "dhyāna" applies in common to whatever concentration is attained by ordinary people, non-Buddhist practitioners, the two lesser vehicles, bodhisattvas, and buddhas alike — hence it is called "shared." As for the unshared name: the three characters "波羅蜜" [pāramitā], meaning "gone to the other shore," apply only to bodhisattvas and buddhas. Hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "When dhyāna is held within a bodhisattva's mind, it is called pāramitā." This is why it is called "unshared."

所以者何？凡夫著愛，外道著見，二乘無大悲方便，不能盡修一切禪定，是以不得受到彼岸名，故言波羅蜜即是不共。復次，禪名四禪，凡夫、外道、二乘、菩薩、諸佛，同得此定，故名為共。波羅蜜名度無極，此獨菩薩、諸佛。因禪能通達中道佛性，出生九種大禪，得大涅槃，不與凡夫、二乘共，故波羅蜜者，名為不共。通而為論，即無勞分別。所以者何？禪自有共禪、不共禪，波羅蜜亦爾，有共、不共，故《摩訶衍論》云：「天竺語法，凡所作事竟，皆名波羅蜜。」

Why is this? Ordinary people are attached to craving; non-Buddhist practitioners are attached to views; the two lesser vehicles lack great compassion and skillful means, and so cannot cultivate the whole of every dhyāna-concentration to completion — this is why they cannot receive the name "gone to the other shore." Hence it is said that "pāramitā" is precisely the unshared name.

Furthermore: "dhyāna" names the four dhyānas, which ordinary people, non-Buddhist practitioners, the two lesser vehicles, bodhisattvas, and buddhas attain alike — hence it is called "shared." "Pāramitā" names "crossing to the limitless," which belongs to bodhisattvas and buddhas alone. Because through dhyāna they penetrate the buddha-nature of the middle way, give rise to the nine kinds of great dhyāna, and attain great nirvāṇa — not shared with ordinary people or the two lesser vehicles — "pāramitā" is called the unshared name.

Taken as a whole, however, there is no need to labor over the distinction. Why is this? Dhyāna itself has both a shared and an unshared sense, and pāramitā is likewise both shared and unshared. Hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "In the grammar of the Indian language, whenever any undertaking is brought to completion, it is called pāramitā."

Reading note: This is a small but useful piece of Zhiyi's method: he offers two different ways of drawing the same shared/unshared line — first as "dhyāna" in general versus "pāramitā" specifically, then as the four ordinary dhyānas versus the nine kinds of great dhyāna that only a bodhisattva's realization of the middle way produces — and then relativizes both, since in the end any dhyāna can be "shared" or "unshared" depending on who holds it. The nine kinds of great dhyāna (九種大禪) are named here for the first time; they recur later in the treatise and are not yet reached in translation.

Translating "Dhyāna" 翻釋共名

Chinese Source

第二、翻釋，即為二意：一、翻釋共名；二、翻釋不共名。第一、先翻釋共名。共名者，即是禪也。亦為二意：一、正翻名；二者、解釋。第一、先翻共名者，禪是外國之言，此間翻則不定。今略出三翻：一、《摩訶衍論》中翻禪，秦言思惟修；二、舉例往翻，如檀波羅蜜，此言布施度，禪波羅蜜，此言定度，故知用定以翻禪；三、阿毘曇中用功德叢林以翻禪。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, its etymological translation: this too has two parts — translating the shared name; and translating the unshared name. First, translating the shared name — that is, "dhyāna" itself. This also has two parts: giving the direct translation of the name; and explaining it. First, as to the direct translation of the shared name: "dhyāna" is a foreign word, and its translation here is not fixed. Here we give, in brief, three renderings: first, the Mahāyāna treatise translates "dhyāna," in the language of the Qin dynasty, as "contemplative cultivation" (思惟修); second, by analogy — just as dāna-pāramitā is rendered "the perfection of giving," so dhyāna-pāramitā is rendered "the perfection of concentration," from which it can be known that "concentration" is used to translate "dhyāna"; third, the Abhidharma texts translate "dhyāna" as "the grove of merit and virtue."

第二、釋此三翻，即作二意：一、別；二、通。若釋別，翻思惟修者，此可對因。何以故？思惟是籌量之念，修是專心研習之名，故以對修因。翻禪為定者，此可對果。何以故？定名靜默，行人離散求靜，既得靜住，詶本所習，故以對果。翻禪為功德叢林者，此可通對因果：如功是功夫，所以對因；積功成德，可以對果。如萬行對因，萬德對果，因果合翻，故名功德叢林者，譬顯功德非一。所以然者，如多草共聚，名為叢；眾樹相依，名為林。草叢小故，可以譬於因中之功小；林木大故，可以對果上之德大。此而推之，功德叢林通對因果，於義則便。

Second, explaining these three renderings: this too has two parts — taken separately, and taken together. Taken separately: the rendering "contemplative cultivation" may be matched to the cause. Why is this? "Contemplation" names the thought that deliberates and weighs; "cultivation" names single-minded, focused practice — hence it is matched to the cause of cultivation. The rendering of "dhyāna" as "concentration" may be matched to the fruit. Why is this? "Concentration" names stillness and silence; the practitioner leaves the scattered mind behind to seek stillness, and once stillness is attained and abided in, the original practice is answered — hence it is matched to the fruit. The rendering of "dhyāna" as "the grove of merit and virtue" may be matched to cause and fruit together: "merit" is effort, and so is matched to the cause; effort accumulated becomes virtue, and so may be matched to the fruit — just as the ten thousand practices are matched to the cause and the ten thousand virtues to the fruit. Cause and fruit are translated together, and so it is called "the grove of merit and virtue," an image that shows merit and virtue to be not merely one thing. Why is this so? Just as many blades of grass gathered together are called a "thicket," and many trees relying on one another are called a "forest" — the thicket of grass is small, and so may be likened to the small merit within the cause; the forest of trees is large, and so may be matched to the great virtue upon the fruit. Reasoned through in this way, "the grove of merit and virtue" matches both cause and fruit together, and is convenient in meaning.

第二、通釋禪，三翻並對因果。所以者何？如思惟修，雖言據因，亦得對果。何以故？定中靜慮，即是思惟；乘上益下，故名為修。此可以數人九修中乘上修義為類，故於果中亦得說思惟。因中亦得說定者，如十大地心數，散心尚得言定，何況行者專心歛念，守一不散而不名定？故知因中亦得說定。因中亦得名功德叢林者，因中功義，前已說之。由運功故，即成行因之德。果中德義，說亦如前。所言功者，即是功用。果上有寂靜離過，神通變化益物之用，故名為功。因之與果，悉是眾善功德之所成，故通言功德叢林。復次，諸經論中，翻名立義不同，或言禪名棄惡，或言疾大、疾住、大住，如是處不同，不可偏執。

Second, explaining "dhyāna" as a whole, the three renderings all match both cause and fruit together. Why is this? Although "contemplative cultivation" is said to be based on the cause, it can also be matched to the fruit. Why is this? Stillness and reflection within concentration are themselves contemplation; "riding what is higher to benefit what is lower" is what is called cultivation. This can be classed together with the sense of "riding what is higher" among the Ābhidharmikas' nine kinds of cultivation, and so "contemplation" can also be spoken of within the fruit. That "concentration" can also be spoken of within the cause: just as, among the ten mental factors of the great wholesome ground, even a scattered mind can still be called "concentrated" to some degree — how much more so a practitioner who has gathered the mind single-mindedly, holding to the one without scattering? Hence it can be known that "concentration" too may be spoken of within the cause. That "the grove of merit and virtue" can also be named within the cause: the sense of "merit" within the cause has already been explained above — through the exertion of effort, the virtue of the practice-cause is thereby accomplished. The sense of "virtue" within the fruit is likewise as explained above. What is called "merit" is precisely functioning effect: upon the fruit there is the function of still, error-free tranquility and of supernormal transformation that benefits beings — hence it is called "merit." Both cause and fruit are, in every case, accomplished by a multitude of wholesome merits and virtues — hence, taken together, they are called "the grove of merit and virtue."

Furthermore, the various sūtras and treatises render the name and establish its meaning differently — some say "dhyāna" means "abandoning evil"; some say "swift and great," "swiftly abiding," or "greatly abiding." Since the renderings differ from place to place in this way, one should not cling one-sidedly to any single one.

Reading note: The three renderings of "dhyāna" — contemplative cultivation, concentration, the grove of merit and virtue — map cleanly onto cause and fruit at first (practice versus its result), and then Zhiyi deliberately complicates that mapping, showing each rendering can describe both ends of the path. The forest image is worth pausing on for its own sake: a "thicket" of grass for the small, accumulating efforts of the cause, a "forest" of trees for the large, settled virtue of the fruit — the same growth, at two different scales.

Translating "Pāramitā" 翻釋不共名

Chinese Source

第二、翻釋不共名。不共名者，即是波羅蜜，亦為二意：一者、翻名；二者、解釋。就第一、翻名中略出三翻不同：一者、諸經論中多翻為到彼岸；二、《摩訶衍論》中別翻云事究竟；三、《瑞應經》中翻云度無極。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, translating the unshared name. The unshared name is "pāramitā" itself, and this too has two parts: translating the name; and explaining it. As to translating the name, here in brief are three differing renderings: first, most sūtras and treatises render it as "gone to the other shore"; second, the Mahāyāna treatise gives the distinct rendering "the completion of the task"; third, the Sūtra of Auspicious Manifestations renders it as "crossing to the limitless."

第二、釋此三翻，亦為二意：一、別；二、通。此皆對事理名義。第一、別釋，言到彼岸者，生死為此岸，涅槃為彼岸，煩惱為中流。菩薩以無相妙慧，乘禪定舟航，從生死此岸，度涅槃彼岸。故知約理定以明波羅蜜。言事究竟者，即是菩薩大悲，為眾生遍修一切事行滿足，故《摩訶衍》云：「菩薩因禪能究竟眾事，禪在菩薩心中，名波羅蜜。」此據事行說波羅蜜。言度無極者，通論事理，悉有幽遠之義，合而言之，故云度無極。此約事理行滿說波羅蜜。

Second, explaining these three renderings: this too has two parts — taken separately, and taken together. All of them correspond to the name and meaning of either phenomenal conduct or principle. First, taken separately: "gone to the other shore" — birth-and-death is this shore, nirvāṇa is the other shore, and affliction is the current running between them. The bodhisattva, by means of signless and wondrous wisdom, rides the vessel of dhyāna-concentration, crossing from this shore of birth-and-death to the other shore of nirvāṇa. Hence it can be known that this explains pāramitā in terms of principle and concentration. "The completion of the task" — this is the bodhisattva's great compassion, cultivating every practical conduct on behalf of sentient beings to its full completion. Hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "Through dhyāna, the bodhisattva is able to bring every task to completion; when dhyāna is held within the bodhisattva's mind, it is called pāramitā." This explains pāramitā in terms of phenomenal conduct. "Crossing to the limitless" — taken to cover both phenomenal conduct and principle together, both have the sense of the profound and far-reaching, and taken jointly, it is therefore called "crossing to the limitless." This explains pāramitā in terms of phenomenal conduct and principle both being fulfilled.

第二、通釋三翻，並得同對事理俱隨緣化物，故立異名。所以者何？若言無相之慧能度生死，故為理行者，今言理中，有佛無佛，性相常然，豈論無相之慧能度生死？終是就事作此說也。事究竟亦是從理立名者，若緣理而起事行，當知說事究竟亦是約理名波羅蜜度無極，亦未必一向就事理無極名波羅蜜。所以者何？諸佛隨緣利物，出沒不定，無極或時對事，或時對理，豈有定準？當知三名理事互通，未必偏有所屬，餘例可知。釋波羅蜜義，至下第十、結會歸趣中，自當廣明。

Second, explaining the three renderings taken together: all three can equally be said to correspond to both phenomenal conduct and principle, both following conditions to transform beings — hence different names are established for them. Why is this? If it is said that signless wisdom alone can cross birth-and-death, and this is therefore taken to be a matter of principle — yet within principle itself, whether a buddha appears or not, nature and characteristic remain ever the same; how, then, could one speak of signless wisdom crossing birth-and-death at all? In the end this too is a statement made in terms of phenomenal conduct. As for "the completion of the task" also being a name established from principle: if phenomenal conduct arises in dependence on principle, one should know that speaking of "the completion of the task" is also, in terms of principle, naming pāramitā as "crossing to the limitless" — nor need it be assumed that "crossing to the limitless" names pāramitā exclusively in terms of phenomenal conduct and principle taken together, either. Why is this? The buddhas, following conditions to benefit beings, appear and withdraw without any fixed pattern; "the limitless" sometimes corresponds to phenomenal conduct, sometimes to principle — how could there be any fixed rule? One should know that these three names interpenetrate between principle and phenomenal conduct, and need not be assigned one-sidedly to only one or the other; the remaining cases may be understood by the same logic. The meaning of pāramitā will be explained fully further below, in the tenth topic, where the treatise is gathered together and its destination pointed out.

Reading note: Zhiyi's three renderings of "pāramitā" line up with a recurring pair in this treatise — principle (理) and phenomenal conduct (事) — the same pair invoked back in topic 1's account of emptiness and the vow. "Gone to the other shore" leans toward principle (the destination itself, nirvāṇa); "the completion of the task" leans toward conduct (the bodhisattva's actual work for beings); "crossing to the limitless" is offered as covering both. True to form, Zhiyi then refuses to let the three stay neatly sorted, arguing that even "gone to the other shore" is, on inspection, a statement about conduct rather than a bare metaphysical claim about principle — principle itself, he says, "remains ever the same" whether or not a buddha appears to cross it.

Why Dhyāna Alone Is Called Pāramitā 料簡（一）

Chinese Source

第三、料簡，如《摩訶衍論》中云：「問曰：背捨、勝處、一切處等，何故不名波羅蜜，獨稱禪為波羅蜜？答曰：禪最大如王，言禪波羅蜜者，一切皆攝。是四禪中，有八背捨、八勝處、十一切處、四無量心、五神通、練禪、自在定、十四變化心、無諍三昧、願智、頂禪、首楞嚴等諸三昧，百則有八，諸佛、不動等，百則二十，皆在禪中。若諸佛成道，轉法輪，入涅槃，所有勝妙功德悉在禪中，說禪則攝一切，若說餘定，則有所不攝，故禪名波羅蜜。復次，四禪中，智定等故，說波羅蜜。未到地、中間禪，智多而定少；四無色，定多而智少。如車輪，一強一弱，則不任載。四禪智定等故，說波羅蜜。復次，約禪說波羅蜜，則攝一切諸定。所以者何？禪，秦言思惟修。此諸定悉是思惟修功德故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, resolving points of doubt. As the Mahāyāna treatise says: "Question: Why are the liberations, the stations of mastery, the totality-bases, and the like not called pāramitā, while dhyāna alone is called pāramitā? Answer: Dhyāna is greatest of all, like a king; when we speak of 'dhyāna-pāramitā,' everything is thereby included. Within the four dhyānas are contained the eight liberations, the eight stations of mastery, the ten totality-bases, the four immeasurable minds, the five supernormal powers, the refining of dhyāna, the sovereign concentrations, the fourteen transformation-minds, the concentration free of contention, wish-wisdom, the summit-dhyānas, the Śūraṅgama and other samādhis — a hundred and eight of these — together with the buddhas' own samādhis, the unshakeable, and the rest — a hundred and twenty of these — all are contained within dhyāna. Since whatever sublime and wondrous merit belongs to the buddhas' accomplishing the path, turning the wheel of the Dharma, and entering nirvāṇa is, without exception, contained within dhyāna, to speak of dhyāna is to include everything; but to speak of any other concentration leaves something not included. Hence dhyāna alone receives the name pāramitā.

Furthermore: within the four dhyānas, wisdom and concentration are equal, and so it is called pāramitā. In the 'not-yet-arrived' state and in the intermediate dhyāna, wisdom is greater and concentration lesser; in the four formless absorptions, concentration is greater and wisdom lesser — like a cart-wheel, one part strong and the other weak, unable to bear its load. Since the four dhyānas have wisdom and concentration equal, they are called pāramitā.

Furthermore: to speak of pāramitā in terms of dhyāna is to include every concentration whatsoever. Why is this? 'Dhyāna,' in the language of the Qin dynasty, means 'contemplative cultivation' — and every one of these concentrations is, without exception, accomplished through the merit of contemplative cultivation."

當知諸定悉得受波羅蜜名。如《大品》中說百波羅蜜，亦說背捨、勝處等皆名波羅蜜，但四禪在根本，先受其名，非不通於餘定。問曰：上明禪、定、三昧、波羅蜜等，為同為異？答曰：通而為論，名義互通；別而往解，四法名義各有主對。所以者何？根本四禪但名禪，非定、三昧，亦不名波羅蜜。無色但名定，非禪、三昧，亦不名波羅蜜。未到地禪、中間，雖非正禪定，是方便故，或名禪，或名定，非三昧，亦不名波羅蜜。空、無相等，但名三昧，非禪、定，亦不名波羅蜜。背捨、勝處、六通、四辯等，具有禪、定、三昧等三法，而不名禪定、三昧，亦非波羅蜜。九次第定具有三法，但名為定，不名禪、三昧，亦非波羅蜜。有覺有觀，及師子、超越、無諍等，亦具三法，但名三昧，不名禪、定，亦非波羅蜜。願智頂等，具有三法，但名禪，不名定、三昧，亦非波羅蜜。九種大禪及首楞嚴等，並具四法，亦名禪，亦名定，亦名三昧，即是波羅蜜。若用首楞嚴心入前三法中，一切皆名波羅蜜，故百波羅蜜中一切法門皆名波羅蜜。

One should know from this that every concentration can, in fact, receive the name pāramitā. As the Larger Sūtra speaks of a hundred pāramitās, and also says that the liberations, the stations of mastery, and the rest are all called pāramitā — it is only that the four root dhyānas, being the foundation, receive the name first, not that the name fails to extend to the other concentrations as well.

Question: In the foregoing, "dhyāna," "concentration," "samādhi," and "pāramitā" have all been explained — are they the same, or different? Answer: Taken as a whole, their names and meanings interconnect; taken individually, each of these four terms has its own proper referent. Why is this? The root four dhyānas are called only "dhyāna" — not "concentration" or "samādhi," nor are they called "pāramitā." The formless absorptions are called only "concentration" — not "dhyāna" or "samādhi," nor are they called "pāramitā." The "not-yet-arrived" dhyāna and the intermediate dhyāna, though not concentration proper, being expedient means, may be called either "dhyāna" or "concentration," but not "samādhi," nor are they called "pāramitā." Emptiness, signlessness, and the like are called only "samādhi" — not "dhyāna" or "concentration," nor are they called "pāramitā." The liberations, the stations of mastery, the six supernormal powers, the four unhindered eloquences, and the like possess all three qualities of dhyāna, concentration, and samādhi, and yet are not called "dhyāna," "concentration," or "samādhi," nor are they "pāramitā." The nine sequential concentrations possess all three qualities, but are called only "concentration" — not "dhyāna" or "samādhi," nor are they "pāramitā." Dhyāna accompanied by both coarse and subtle discursive thought, together with the lion's-sport concentration, the transcendence concentration, the concentration free of contention, and the like, likewise possess all three qualities, but are called only "samādhi" — not "dhyāna" or "concentration," nor are they "pāramitā." Wish-wisdom, the summit-dhyānas, and the like possess all three qualities, but are called only "dhyāna" — not "concentration" or "samādhi," nor are they "pāramitā." The nine kinds of great dhyāna, the Śūraṅgama, and the like alone possess all four qualities together — they are called "dhyāna," called "concentration," and called "samādhi" all at once: this is precisely "pāramitā." And if the mind of Śūraṅgama is brought to bear within the three preceding categories, every one of them can then also be called "pāramitā" — this is why, among the hundred pāramitās, every dharma-gate whatsoever can be called pāramitā.

今略對四法分別如前。若諸大聖善巧隨緣利物，則言無定準解釋。故諸經論中出沒立名，其意難見，不可謬執。而經論中多約禪明波羅蜜者，以根本四禪是眾行之本，一切內行功德皆因四禪發，依四禪而住，是以獨禪得受波羅蜜名。

Here, in brief, the four terms have been distinguished as above. Since the great sages skillfully follow conditions to benefit beings, there is no fixed standard governing how they are explained — hence the various sūtras and treatises name things as they appear and recede, and their intent is not easily seen; one should not cling wrongly to any single reading. Yet the reason the sūtras and treatises most often explain pāramitā specifically in terms of dhyāna is that the root four dhyānas are the foundation of every practice: every inward practice and its merit is brought forth through the four dhyānas and abides in dependence on them — this is why dhyāna alone receives the name pāramitā.

Reading note: This is a technical but important piece of classification: Zhiyi (quoting the Mahāyāna treatise) sorts every meditative state named in the canon into which of four overlapping terms — dhyāna, concentration, samādhi, pāramitā — actually apply to it, and shows that only the rarest states (the nine kinds of great dhyāna, the Śūraṅgama samādhi) qualify for all four at once. The practical point beneath the taxonomy: "pāramitā" is not a synonym for "meditative attainment" in general — it marks out states complete enough, in both wisdom and concentration together, to carry the name. The Śūraṅgama samādhi named here is the same samādhi at the center of the Śūraṅgama Sūtra, translated elsewhere on this site — Zhiyi treats it as the paradigm case of a "complete" concentration.

Other Names for Dhyāna-Pāramitā, and a Bodhisattva's Claim to It 料簡（二）

Chinese Source

問曰：禪波羅蜜但有一名，更有餘稱？答曰：如《涅槃》中說：「言佛性者，有五種名，亦名首楞嚴，亦名般若，亦名中道，亦名金剛三昧大涅槃，亦云禪波羅蜜，即是佛性。」故知諸餘經中所說種種勝妙法門，名字無量，皆是禪波羅蜜之異名，故《摩訶衍》偈說：
般若是一法，佛說種種名，
隨諸眾生類，為之立異字。
若人得般若，戲論心皆滅，
譬如日出時，朝露一時失。
以此類之，禪名豈不遍通？若其禪定不具足攝一切諸法，則非究竟，何得名波羅蜜義？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Does dhyāna-pāramitā have only this one name, or does it also go by other names? Answer: As the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "As for what is called buddha-nature, it has five names: it is also called Śūraṅgama, also called prajñā, also called the middle way, also called the vajra-samādhi of great nirvāṇa, and also called dhyāna-pāramitā — and this is precisely buddha-nature." From this it can be known that the various sublime and wondrous dharma-gates spoken of, under measureless different names, throughout the rest of the sūtras are all alternate names for dhyāna-pāramitā. Hence the Mahāyāna treatise says in verse:
"Prajñā is a single Dharma; the Buddha speaks of it under many names,
Following the type of each sentient being, distinct terms are established for it.
If a person attains prajñā, every idly theorizing mind is extinguished at once,
Just as, when the sun rises, the morning dew vanishes all together."
Reasoning by this same kind of logic, how could the name "dhyāna" not extend everywhere? If dhyāna-concentration did not, in its completeness, include every dharma whatsoever, it would not be ultimate — how, then, could it be entitled to the meaning of "pāramitā"?

問曰：諸法實相、首楞嚴及到彼岸等，唯佛一人方稱究竟，菩薩所行禪定，云何名波羅蜜？答曰：因中說果故，隨分說故，頓教所明發心畢竟二不別故，以如是等眾多義故，菩薩所行禪定亦得名波羅蜜。

Question: The true reality of all dharmas, the Śūraṅgama, "gone to the other shore," and the like are said to be ultimate only in the Buddha alone — how, then, can the dhyāna-concentration practiced by a bodhisattva be called pāramitā? Answer: Because the fruit is spoken of within the cause; because it is spoken of according to one's own portion of the path; because, as the sudden teaching makes clear, the arising of the mind at the very start and its ultimate completion are, in the end, not two different things — for these and many similar reasons, the dhyāna-concentration practiced by a bodhisattva can indeed also be called pāramitā.

Reading note: The closing move is a small but important piece of Tiantai doctrine in miniature: an apparent problem (only a buddha's realization is truly "ultimate," so how can a bodhisattva's still-imperfect dhyāna be called pāramitā, a word that means "gone all the way across"?) is resolved by appeal to what later Tiantai writing calls "the identity of beginning and end" — the sudden teaching's claim that the very first arising of the bodhi-mind and its final completion are, in principle, not two different things. This same move — the fruit already present, in some sense, within the cause — will recur through the rest of this treatise. This closes topic 2.

III. Clarifying the Gates of Dhyāna-Pāramitā 明禪波羅蜜門第三

Knowing the name is not yet knowing how to practice. Zhiyi's third topic turns from what dhyāna is called to how a practitioner actually enters it — reducing the countless gates named across the sūtras and treatises to just three, and then showing why three, no more and no fewer, is exactly the right number.

Stating the Gates 標禪門

Chinese Source

行者善尋名故，自知其體。若欲進修，必因門而入。今略明禪門，即為三意：第一、標禪門；第二、解釋；三、料簡。第一、標禪門者，若尋經論所說禪門，乃有無量，原其根本，不過有二，所謂：一、色；二、心。如《摩訶衍》中偈說：
一切諸法中，但有名與色，
若欲如實觀，亦當觀名色。
雖癡心多想，分別於諸法，
更無有一法，出於名色者。

Ru-Yi Translation

The practitioner who has skillfully traced the name thereby comes to know its essence. But to actually advance in cultivation, one must enter through a gate. Here, then, the gates of dhyāna are briefly clarified, under three headings: first, stating the gates; second, explaining them; third, resolving points of doubt.

First, stating the gates: if one traces the gates of dhyāna spoken of throughout the sūtras and treatises, they are limitless in number — but tracing them to their root, they do not exceed two: namely, form, and mind. As the Mahāyāna treatise says in verse:
"Among all dharmas whatsoever, there is only name and form;
If one wishes to observe reality as it truly is, one should likewise observe name and form.
Though a confused mind conceives many notions, discriminating among the various dharmas,
There is not a single dharma that goes beyond name and form."

今就色門中，即開為二，如經中說：「二為甘露門：一者、不淨觀門；二者、阿那波那門。」心門唯有一門，如經中說：「能觀心性，名為上定。」開色別立於心，此則禪門有三，所謂：一、世間禪門；二、出世間禪門；三、出世間上上禪門。故《大集經》云：「有三種攝心：一者、出法攝心；二者、滅法攝心；三者、非出非滅法攝心。」

Now, within the gate of form, this opens further into two, as the sūtra says: "There are two gates to the deathless: first, the gate of the contemplation of impurity; second, the gate of mindfulness of the breath." The gate of mind is a single gate alone, as the sūtra says: "The ability to contemplate the nature of mind is called the supreme concentration." Opening form apart and establishing mind separately, the gates of dhyāna thus number three: namely, the gate of worldly dhyāna; the gate of world-transcending dhyāna; and the gate of dhyāna transcending the world and beyond. Hence the Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra says: "There are three kinds of gathering the mind: first, gathering the mind through the dharma of emergence; second, gathering the mind through the dharma of extinction; third, gathering the mind through the dharma that is neither emergence nor extinction."

Reading note: "Name and form" (名色, nāmarūpa) is a term from dependent origination, but Zhiyi uses it here in a wider sense — as shorthand for everything that isn't purely physical (name, i.e. mind) and everything that is (form) — to argue that all of practice reduces to working with mind and body. From there the three gates fall out almost mechanically: form splits into the breath and the body's own impurity; mind stands alone; three gates, three grades of practitioner.

Explaining Each Gate 解釋 · 別明三門

Chinese Source

第二、解釋。此三門中，即各為二意：一、別；二、通。第一、別明門者，門名能通，如世門通人，有所至處。一、以息為禪門者，若因息攝心，則能通行心至四禪、四空、四無量心、十六特勝、通明等禪，即是世間禪門，亦名出法攝心。此一往據凡夫禪門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining them. Within these three gates, each in turn has two aspects: taken separately, and taken together. First, explaining the gates separately: "gate" means what allows passage through, just as a worldly gate allows a person passage through to wherever they are going.

First, taking the breath as the gate of dhyāna: if the mind is gathered by means of the breath, it can pass through to the four dhyānas, the four formless absorptions, the four immeasurable minds, the sixteen special excellences, the thorough-and-bright concentrations, and the like — this is the gate of worldly dhyāna, also called "gathering the mind through the dharma of emergence." This, broadly speaking, belongs to the dhyāna-gate of ordinary people.

二、以色為禪門者，如因不淨觀等攝心，則能通行心至九想、八念、十想、背捨、勝處、一切處、次第定、師子奮迅、超越三昧等處，即是出世間禪門，亦名滅法攝心。一往據二乘禪門。三、以心為禪門者，若用智慧反觀心性，則能通行心至法華、念佛、般舟、覺意、首楞嚴諸大三昧及自性禪，乃至清淨淨禪等，是出世間上上禪門，亦名非出非滅法攝心。此一往據菩薩禪門。以此義故，約三法為門。

Second, taking form as the gate of dhyāna: if the mind is gathered through the contemplation of impurity and the like, it can pass through to the nine contemplations, the eight recollections, the ten contemplations, the liberations, the stations of mastery, the totality-bases, the sequential concentrations, the lion's-sport samādhi, the transcendence samādhi, and the like — this is the gate of world-transcending dhyāna, also called "gathering the mind through the dharma of extinction." This, broadly speaking, belongs to the dhyāna-gate of the two lesser vehicles.

Third, taking mind as the gate of dhyāna: if wisdom is used to turn contemplation back upon the nature of mind itself, it can pass through to the great samādhis of the Lotus, of buddha-recollection, of pratyutpanna, of awareness, of Śūraṅgama, and the like, together with the samādhi of self-nature, up through the pure-and-undefiled samādhi — this is the gate of dhyāna transcending the world and beyond, also called "gathering the mind through the dharma that is neither emergence nor extinction." This, broadly speaking, belongs to the dhyāna-gate of the bodhisattva. It is for this reason that these three things are taken as the gates.

Reading note: The three gates map onto the three kinds of practitioner named back in topic 1: breath-practice for the ordinary person, contemplation of form for the two lesser vehicles, direct contemplation of mind itself for the bodhisattva. But — as the very next passage insists — this mapping is only "broadly speaking" (一往); Zhiyi immediately goes on to complicate it.

Why Exactly Three Gates 解釋 · 三義

Chinese Source

問曰：諸法無量，何故但取此三為禪門？答曰：今略明有三意，故立三法為門：一、如法相；二、隨便易；三、攝法盡。一、如法相者，如《大集經》說：歌羅邏時，即有三事：一、命；二、暖；三、識。出入息者，名為壽命。不臭不爛，名之為暖，即是業持火大故，地、水等色不臭爛也。此中心、意名之為識，即是剎那覺知心也。三法和合，從生至長，無增無減，愚夫不了，於中妄計我、人、眾生，作諸業行，心生染著，顛倒因緣往來三界。若尋其源本，不出此之三法，故以三法為門，不多不少。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The dharmas are limitless in number — why take only these three as the gates of dhyāna? Answer: Here, in brief, there are three reasons why these three things are established as gates: first, because they match the very mark of what a living being is; second, because they are convenient and easy to follow; third, because they encompass every dharma completely.

First, because they match the very mark of what a living being is: as the Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra says, at the stage of the kalala — the earliest embryonic stage — three things are already present: life, warmth, and consciousness. The breath, going out and coming in, is called "life." Not decaying and not putrefying is called "warmth" — this is because karma sustains the fire element, so that the earth, water, and other elements of form do not decay or putrefy. Mind and intention here are called "consciousness" — that is, the mind that is aware moment by moment. These three things, joined together, carry a being from birth through growth, neither increasing nor decreasing — yet the foolish and unenlightened, failing to understand this, wrongly conceive of a self, a person, and sentient beings within it, carry out every kind of karmic action, give rise to a defiled and attached mind, and, through inverted causes and conditions, come and go through the three realms. If one traces this to its very source, it never goes beyond these three things — hence these three are taken as the gates, neither more nor fewer.

二、隨便易故，立三法為門者，如因息修禪，則有二便：一、疾得禪定；二、易悟無常。以色為門，亦有二便：一、能斷貪欲；二、易了虛假。心為門者，此亦有二便：一、能降一切煩惱；二、易悟空理。三、攝法盡者，此三法是禪門根本故。所以者何？舉要說三，開即無量。如息門中，或數或隨，或時觀息，如此非一，至處亦異。如色門中，或緣外色，或緣內色，或作慈悲，或緣佛相，乃至得解實觀，如此非一，至處亦異。如心門中，或止或觀，或覺或了，或覺了諸心入於非心，覺了非心出無量心，或覺了非心非不心，能知一切心非心，如是緣心不同，至處亦復非一，故說三門攝一切禪門。此事至第七、八釋修證中方乃可見。

Second, because they are convenient and easy to follow, these three things are established as gates: cultivating dhyāna by means of the breath brings two conveniences — first, concentration is quickly attained; second, impermanence is easily realized. Taking form as the gate likewise brings two conveniences — first, craving and desire can be severed; second, what is illusory and unreal is easily understood. Taking mind as the gate likewise brings two conveniences — first, every affliction can be subdued; second, the principle of emptiness is easily realized.

Third, because they encompass every dharma completely: these three things are the root of every gate of dhyāna. Why is this? Taking the essentials, only three are named — but opened out, they are limitless. Within the gate of the breath, there is counting, following, and at times observing the breath directly — these are not one single thing, and where they lead also differs. Within the gate of form, there is taking an external form as the object, taking an internal form as the object, cultivating loving-kindness and compassion, taking the Buddha's marks as the object, and further, attaining understanding through the contemplation of reality — these too are not one single thing, and where they lead also differs. Within the gate of mind, there is śamatha and vipaśyanā, awareness and clear knowing, becoming clearly aware that every mind enters into "not-mind," becoming clearly aware that "not-mind" gives rise to measureless minds, or becoming clearly aware that it is neither "not-mind" nor "not not-mind," able to know that every mind is not a fixed mind — taking the mind as object in these various ways, where they lead is likewise not one single thing. Hence it is said that these three gates encompass every gate of dhyāna whatsoever. This matter will only become fully visible further below, in the seventh and eighth topics, where cultivation and realization are explained.

Reading note: The first justification is the most striking: Zhiyi grounds the three gates not in scripture or tradition but in a claim about what a living being actually is, down to the earliest moment of conception — the kalala stage, where life (breath), warmth (the fire element sustaining the body), and consciousness are already present together. The three gates of practice, in other words, simply work with the same three things that constitute a living being in the first place.

The Gates Interpenetrate 解釋 · 通名三門

Chinese Source

第二、通名三門者，此三法通得作世間、出世間、出世間上上等禪門。所以者何？一、如息法不定，但屬世間禪門。何以得知？如《毘尼》中，佛為聲聞弟子說觀息等十六行法，弟子隨教而修，皆得聖道，故知亦是出世間禪門，即大乘門者，如《大品》說：「阿那波那，即是菩薩摩訶衍。」故《請觀音經》約數息辨六字章句，明三乘得道，此豈可但是世間禪門？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the three gates taken together: these three things can, in fact, each serve as the gate of worldly dhyāna, world-transcending dhyāna, or dhyāna transcending the world and beyond alike. Why is this?

First, the method of the breath is not fixed to belonging only to the gate of worldly dhyāna. How can this be known? As in the Vinaya, where the Buddha teaches his śrāvaka disciples the sixteen practices of observing the breath, and the disciples, cultivating in accordance with this teaching, all attain the noble path — hence it can be known that this is also the gate of world-transcending dhyāna. That it is also the Mahāyāna gate can be seen from the Larger Sūtra, which says: "Mindfulness of the breath is itself the bodhisattva's Mahāyāna." Hence the Sūtra of Invoking Avalokiteśvara explains the six-syllable formula in terms of counting the breath, clarifying how all three vehicles attain the path through it — how, then, could this be merely the gate of worldly dhyāna?

二、色法為門，亦不得但是二乘所行，不通大乘及凡夫、外道。何以故？如《涅槃》中說：「外道但能治色，不能治心，我弟子善治於心。」故知凡夫亦得觀色。大乘觀色，如《大品》中說：「脹想、爛想等，是菩薩摩訶衍。」此豈可但是出世間禪門？三、約心為門，亦不得但據菩薩。何以故？如外道亦觀心起四十八見，凡夫緣心入四空，通聲聞者，如《涅槃》說：「我弟子善治心故，能離三界。」此豈唯是出世間上上禪門？當知三門互通，但三種人用心異故，發禪得道亦各不同。此義至第九、明從禪波羅蜜起教中，當廣分別。

Second, taking form as the gate cannot be said to belong only to the practice of the two lesser vehicles, without extending to the Mahāyāna, or to ordinary people and non-Buddhist practitioners. Why is this? As the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Non-Buddhist practitioners can only treat form, and cannot treat mind; my own disciples treat mind well." Hence it can be known that ordinary people, too, can contemplate form. That the Mahāyāna also contemplates form can be seen from the Larger Sūtra, which says: "The contemplation of swelling, the contemplation of putrefaction, and the like are themselves the bodhisattva's Mahāyāna." How, then, could this be merely the gate of world-transcending dhyāna?

Third, taking mind as the gate cannot be said to belong only to bodhisattvas. Why is this? Non-Buddhist practitioners, too, contemplate mind and give rise to the forty-eight wrong views from it; ordinary people take mind as the object and enter the four formless absorptions; and as for its extending to the śrāvakas, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Because my disciples treat mind well, they are able to leave the three realms behind." How, then, could this be only the gate of dhyāna transcending the world and beyond? One should know that these three gates interpenetrate one another — it is only that, because the three kinds of practitioners use the mind differently, the dhyāna they give rise to, and the path they attain, likewise each differ. This point will be explained fully further below, in the ninth topic, where the teachings arising from dhyāna-pāramitā are clarified.

Reading note: Having just mapped each gate to one kind of practitioner, Zhiyi now takes the mapping apart again — with scriptural counter-examples for every case: śrāvakas reaching the noble path through breath-practice, ordinary people and even non-Buddhist practitioners contemplating form, non-Buddhists and śrāvakas alike working with mind. The lesson is not that the earlier three-way mapping was wrong, but that it was only ever a rough default ("broadly speaking," as the previous section put it) — any gate can, in principle, be walked by any practitioner, and what actually differs between them is how the mind is used once inside the gate, not which gate is entered.

IV. Distinguishing the Sequence in Which Dhyāna-Pāramitā Is Explained 辨禪波羅蜜詮次第四

Having named the gates, Zhiyi now maps the terrain behind them: the full sequence of dhyāna attainments a practitioner moves through, from the very first steps of concentration up to the peculiar dhyānas available to a bodhisattva alone. This topic is dense with named lists — a first orientation to vocabulary this treatise will return to and unpack in far more detail in later fascicles.

The Contaminated Dhyānas: Twelve Gates of Form and Formlessness 正明諸禪次第 · 有漏法

Chinese Source

行者既知禪門之相，菩薩從初發心乃至佛果，修習禪定，從淺至深，次第階級，是義應知。今略取經論教意，撰於次第，故《大品經》云：「菩薩摩訶薩次第行，次第學，次第道。」辨禪定次第，即為二意：一者、正明諸禪次第；二者、簡非次第。

Ru-Yi Translation

The practitioner, having now understood the mark of the gates of dhyāna, should also understand this: that from a bodhisattva's very first arising of the mind through to buddhahood, the cultivation of dhyāna-concentration proceeds from the shallow to the deep, through graduated stages and sequence. Here, in brief, the intent of the sūtras and treatises is drawn together to compile this sequence — hence the Larger Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva-mahāsattva practices in sequence, studies in sequence, and walks the path in sequence." Distinguishing the sequence of dhyāna-concentration has two parts: first, rightly clarifying the sequence of the various dhyānas; second, distinguishing what is not sequential.

一、正釋諸禪次第義者，行人從初持戒清淨，厭患欲界，繫念修習阿那波那，入欲界定，依欲界定，得未到地，如是依未到地，次第獲得初禪乃至四禪，是名內色界定。次為大功德緣外眾生，受樂歡喜，次第獲得四無量心，是名外色界定。此八種禪定雖緣內外境，入定有殊，而皆屬色界攝。行者於第四禪中厭患色如牢獄，滅前內、外二種色，一心緣空，得度色難，獲得四空處定，是名無色界定。此十二門禪皆是有漏法。

First, rightly explaining the meaning of the sequence of the various dhyānas: the practitioner, beginning from pure moral discipline, grows weary of the desire realm, fixes the mind in mindfulness, and cultivates ānāpāna, entering the concentration of the desire realm. In dependence on the concentration of the desire realm, the "not-yet-arrived" state is attained; and in dependence on that, the first dhyāna is attained in sequence, on up through the fourth dhyāna — this is called the inward concentration of the form realm. Next, for the sake of great merit, taking external sentient beings as the object, receiving joy and delight, the four immeasurable minds are attained in sequence — this is called the outward concentration of the form realm. These eight kinds of concentration, though they take inward or outward objects and differ in how concentration is entered, all belong to the form realm.

The practitioner, within the fourth dhyāna, grows weary of form as though it were a prison, extinguishes the two prior kinds of form, inward and outward, and, with a single mind, takes emptiness as the object; having thus crossed over the difficulty of form, the concentration of the four formless bases is attained — this is called the concentration of the formless realm. These twelve gates of dhyāna are all contaminated dharmas.

Reading note: This is the treatise's first full run through the classical Abhidharma map of concentration: the four dhyānas of the form realm (inward, through breath-practice) plus the four immeasurable minds (outward, toward other beings) make eight "form realm" attainments, and the four formless bases beyond them make twelve in total — the "twelve gates of dhyāna." All twelve are still called "contaminated" (有漏, sāsrava) because none of them, on their own, cut through the delusion that produces further rebirth; that requires the uncontaminated dhyānas introduced next.

Both Contaminated and Uncontaminated: Three Further Dhyānas 亦有漏亦無漏禪

Chinese Source

次此應明亦有漏亦無漏禪。行者既得根本禪已，為欲除此禪中見著，次還從欲界修六妙門。所以者何？此六門中，數、隨、止是入定方便，觀、還、淨是慧方便，定愛慧策。愛故說有漏，策故說無漏。此六法多是欲界、未到地、四禪中具足，亦有至上無色地者。次此應明十六特勝，橫則對四念處，竪則從欲界乃至非想，但地地中立觀破析故，能生無漏。次應說通明觀，前十六特勝總觀故麁，今通明別觀故細。此禪亦從欲界至非想，乃至入滅定。此三種禪亦名淨禪，五種禪中猶是根本攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, what is both contaminated and uncontaminated dhyāna should be clarified. Once the practitioner has attained the root dhyānas, in order to remove the views and attachments found within them, one returns again, from the desire realm, to cultivate the six wondrous gates. Why is this? Among these six gates, counting, following, and stilling are the expedient means for entering concentration; contemplating, returning, and purifying are the expedient means of wisdom. Concentration is what is craved, wisdom is what spurs one onward — because it is craved, it is called "contaminated"; because it spurs one on, it is called "uncontaminated." These six practices are, for the most part, complete within the desire realm, the "not-yet-arrived" state, and the four dhyānas, though some extend up to the formless bases as well.

Next, the sixteen special excellences should be clarified: taken across, they correspond to the four foundations of mindfulness; taken down through the stages, they run from the desire realm all the way to the peak of existence — but because contemplation is established and analysis carried out stage by stage, they are able to give rise to the uncontaminated. Next, the thorough-and-bright contemplation should be explained: the sixteen special excellences just given contemplate as a whole, and so are coarse; the thorough-and-bright contemplation contemplates each part separately, and so is fine. This dhyāna, too, runs from the desire realm all the way to the peak of existence, up through entering the concentration of cessation. These three kinds of dhyāna are also called "pure dhyānas," and, among the five kinds of dhyāna, still belong to the category of the root attainments.

Reading note: The six wondrous gates (六妙門) — counting, following, stilling, contemplating, returning, and purifying the breath — get their name "wondrous" from exactly this dual character: the first three build concentration (and so are still "craved," still contaminated), the last three build wisdom (and so "spur one onward" toward the uncontaminated). This is Zhiyi's own subject in a separate, shorter treatise, the Six Dharma Gates to the Sublime, already published in English translation by Bhikshu Dharmamitra — see this fascicle's translation notes for why this project translates independently of that edition.

Practice-Conduct: Observing, Refining, Perfuming, Mastering 無漏禪次第 · 行行次第

Chinese Source

今明無漏禪次第之相，即有二意不同：一者、行行次第；二者、慧行次第。行行次第，所謂觀、鍊、熏、修。初明觀禪次第，有六種禪：初修九想，無漏之前，用此對治，破欲界煩惱故。次八念，為除修九想時怖畏心生故。次十想，壞法人於欲界修此十想，斷三界煩惱故。次八背捨，不壞法人修此觀禪，對治三界根本定中見著故。次明八勝處，為於諸禪定觀緣中得自在故。次明十一切處，為欲廣禪定中色心令普遍故。乃至修六神通，由是觀禪攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, as for the mark of the sequence of uncontaminated dhyāna, there are two distinct aspects: first, the sequence of practice-conduct; second, the sequence of wisdom-conduct. The sequence of practice-conduct is what is called observing, refining, perfuming, and mastering.

First, clarifying the sequence of observing dhyāna: there are six kinds of dhyāna here. First, the nine contemplations are cultivated — prior to the uncontaminated, these serve as the antidote, breaking the afflictions of the desire realm. Next, the eight recollections, to remove the fear that arises while cultivating the nine contemplations. Next, the ten contemplations — practitioners of the "destructible-dharma" type cultivate these ten contemplations within the desire realm, severing the afflictions of the three realms. Next, the eight liberations — practitioners of the "indestructible-dharma" type cultivate this observing dhyāna, as an antidote to the views and attachments found within the root concentrations of the three realms. Next, the eight stations of mastery are clarified, for the sake of gaining mastery over the objects observed within the various concentrations. Next, the ten totality-bases are clarified, in order to make form and mind pervasive throughout concentration. And on up through the cultivation of the six supernormal powers — all of these belong to observing dhyāna.

次明鍊禪者，即九次第定為總，前定觀二種禪，令心調柔，入諸禪時，心心次第無間故，及有覺有觀等三三昧皆是鍊禪攝。次明熏禪。熏禪者，即是師子奮迅三昧，順逆次第入出熏諸禪，令定觀分明純熟，增益功德故。次明修禪。修禪者，即是超越三昧，於諸禪中超越入出，為得無礙自在解脫故。

Next, clarifying refining dhyāna: the nine sequential concentrations serve as its overall name — the two prior kinds of dhyāna, concentration and observation, make the mind pliant and gentle, so that, when entering each dhyāna in turn, one mind-moment follows the next without any interval. The three samādhis — dhyāna accompanied by both coarse and subtle discursive thought, and the rest — also belong to refining dhyāna.

Next, clarifying perfuming dhyāna. Perfuming dhyāna is precisely the lion's-sport samādhi: entering and emerging from each dhyāna in turn, in forward and reverse sequence, perfuming every dhyāna in turn, so that concentration and observation both become clear and fully ripened, and merit is increased.

Next, clarifying mastering dhyāna. Mastering dhyāna is precisely the transcendence samādhi: transcending, entering, and emerging among the various dhyānas, in order to attain unobstructed, self-mastered liberation.

是以《大品經》云：「菩薩摩訶薩住般若波羅蜜，取禪波羅蜜。除諸佛三昧，入餘一切三昧，若聲聞三昧，若辟支佛三昧，若菩薩三昧，皆行皆入。」餘一切三昧者，根本定是。若聲聞三昧者，三十七品、空、無相等三三昧，四諦十六行是。若辟支佛三昧者，十二因緣三昧是。菩薩三昧者，自性禪等，皆名三昧是。菩薩住諸三昧，逆順出入八背捨，依八背捨逆順出入九次第定，依九次第定逆順出入師子奮迅三昧，依師子奮迅三昧逆順出入超越三昧。是菩薩依諸三昧，得諸法相等，齊此始是二乘行，行共禪滿。何以故？大阿羅漢亦得超越三昧故。

Hence the Larger Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva-mahāsattva, abiding in the perfection of wisdom, takes up dhyāna-pāramitā. Setting aside the buddhas' own samādhis, they enter every other samādhi — whether the śrāvaka's samādhi, the pratyekabuddha's samādhi, or the bodhisattva's samādhi — practicing and entering all of them alike." "Every other samādhi" refers to the root concentrations. The "śrāvaka's samādhi" refers to the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment, the three samādhis of emptiness and signlessness, and the sixteen aspects of the four truths. The "pratyekabuddha's samādhi" refers to the samādhi on the twelve links of dependent origination. The "bodhisattva's samādhi" refers to the samādhi of self-nature and the like — all of these are called samādhi.

The bodhisattva, abiding in these various samādhis, enters and emerges from the eight liberations in forward and reverse order; in dependence on the eight liberations, enters and emerges from the nine sequential concentrations in forward and reverse order; in dependence on the nine sequential concentrations, enters and emerges from the lion's-sport samādhi in forward and reverse order; and in dependence on the lion's-sport samādhi, enters and emerges from the transcendence samādhi in forward and reverse order. It is by depending on these various samādhis that the bodhisattva attains the equal characteristics of all dharmas. Only up to this point does the practice of the two lesser vehicles coincide with the bodhisattva's own, dhyāna held fully in common between them. Why is this? Because even the great arhat can also attain the transcendence samādhi.

Reading note: "Observing, refining, perfuming, mastering" (觀鍊熏修) names four successive grades of working with dhyāna once concentration is already established — not four different objects of meditation, but four different things one does with the whole set of attainments. Observing dhyāna is the largest category, gathering nine separate named contemplative practices under one heading. Refining, perfuming, and mastering then each take the entire stock of prior attainments and put it through a further process — smoothing the mind's movement between states (refining), cycling through them repeatedly to deepen mastery (perfuming), and finally leaping freely between them in any order (mastering, the transcendence samādhi). The passage's closing point is worth sitting with: everything up through the transcendence samādhi is still shared with the two lesser vehicles — "even the great arhat can also attain" it. What comes next belongs to the bodhisattva alone.

Wisdom-Conduct, and the Bodhisattva's Own Unshared Dhyānas 無漏禪次第 · 慧行次第

Chinese Source

二、明無漏慧行次第之相。因聞四諦，即修三十七品，次入三解脫門，次用十六行觀分別四諦，次具十智、三無漏根，成就九修，獲九斷。如此略辨聲聞所行無漏慧行，次應說十二因緣觀門，即是辟支迦羅之所行無漏慧行。若菩薩次第成就，二乘學、無學所得智斷，是名從假入空，通觀具足也，故《大品經》云：「菩薩摩訶薩行般若波羅蜜，以方便力故，從乾慧地、入性地、八人地、見地、離欲地、阿羅漢、辟支佛地，皆行皆入而不取證。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the mark of the sequence of uncontaminated wisdom-conduct. Upon hearing the Four Noble Truths, one cultivates the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment; next enters the three gates of liberation; next uses the sixteen aspects of contemplation to distinguish the Four Noble Truths; next becomes complete in the ten kinds of knowledge and the three uncontaminated faculties, accomplishing the nine kinds of cultivation and attaining the nine severances. This is, in brief, the uncontaminated wisdom-conduct practiced by the śrāvaka. Next should be explained the gate of contemplating the twelve links of dependent origination, which is the uncontaminated wisdom-conduct practiced by the pratyekabuddha.

As for the bodhisattva accomplishing, in sequence, the knowledge and severance attained by both the trainees and the adepts of the two lesser vehicles — this is called "entering emptiness by way of the provisional," complete with thorough contemplation. Hence the Larger Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva-mahāsattva, practicing the perfection of wisdom, by the power of skillful means, passes from the ground of dry wisdom, into the ground of conforming nature, the ground of the eighth-stage practitioner, the ground of vision, the ground free of desire, the ground of the arhat, and the ground of the pratyekabuddha — practicing and entering every one of them, yet without taking hold of their realization."

次明菩薩不共禪次第者：一、自性禪；二、一切義禪；三、難禪；四、一切門禪；五、善人禪；六、一切行禪；七、除惱禪；八、此世他世樂禪；九、清淨淨禪。菩薩依是禪故，得大菩提果，具足十力、四無所畏、十八不共等一切佛法。此則略明菩薩從初發心修禪，次第行，次第學，次第道，乃至佛地，名住大涅槃深禪定窟。此義至釋第七、修證，第八、顯示果報中方乃具辨。

Next, clarifying the sequence of dhyāna unshared with the two lesser vehicles, proper to the bodhisattva alone: first, the samādhi of self-nature; second, the samādhi of every meaning; third, the difficult samādhi; fourth, the samādhi of every gate; fifth, the samādhi of the good person; sixth, the samādhi of every practice; seventh, the samādhi that removes affliction; eighth, the samādhi of joy in this life and lives to come; ninth, the pure-and-undefiled samādhi. In dependence on these dhyānas, the bodhisattva attains the fruit of great bodhi, complete with the ten powers, the four kinds of fearlessness, the eighteen unshared qualities, and the whole of the buddhadharma.

This, then, briefly clarifies how the bodhisattva, from the very first arising of the mind, cultivates dhyāna, practicing in sequence, studying in sequence, walking the path in sequence, all the way to the ground of buddhahood — called abiding within the deep cave of dhyāna-concentration that is great nirvāṇa. This point will be treated in full only further below, where the seventh topic explains cultivation and realization, and the eighth reveals the fruits and rewards.

Reading note: The nine unshared dhyānas named here (自性禪 and the rest) are the "nine kinds of great dhyāna" already referred to back in topic 2's discussion of why dhyāna alone is called pāramitā — this is their first full listing. Their names alone give a sense of what sets them apart from everything discussed so far: a "samādhi of every meaning," a "samādhi of every gate," a "samādhi that removes affliction," a samādhi of "joy in this life and lives to come" — attainments defined by comprehensive scope and benefit to others, not merely by depth of absorption. None of the nine is yet explained individually; that is left for later fascicles.

Must a Beginner Follow This Sequence? 問答

Chinese Source

問曰：菩薩大士為通達諸禪淺深，具足一切佛法次第行，次第學，可如上說。今行人初學禪時，為當一向如上依次第修，為當不爾？答曰：今且欲明諸禪淺深相，一往作此次第分別，若論初心學人，隨所欲樂，便宜對治。易入泥洹者，從諸禪方便初門而修，不必定如前一一依次第。此義至內方便安心禪門中當廣分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: That a great bodhisattva, in order to penetrate the shallow and deep among the various dhyānas and become complete in the whole buddhadharma, practices in sequence and studies in sequence — this may indeed be said as above. But when a practitioner is first learning dhyāna, should they always cultivate strictly according to this sequence, or is this not necessarily so? Answer: What has just been said is meant only to clarify the shallow-to-deep pattern among the various dhyānas in a general way, this sequence given only in broad outline. As for a beginning student, they should follow whatever they find most inclined toward and easiest to apply as an antidote. One for whom entering nirvāṇa comes easily may cultivate starting from whichever preliminary gate among the various dhyānas' expedient means suits them, without necessarily having to follow the sequence given above step by step. This point will be treated fully further below, within the inner expedient means, in the gate of dhyāna for settling the mind.

Reading note: A practical relief after all the taxonomy: the elaborate sequence just given is a map of the terrain, not a rigid itinerary every beginner must follow step by step. What a practitioner actually starts with should be matched to their own temperament and to whatever obstacle needs treating first — a theme this treatise will return to when it reaches the preliminary expedient means in later fascicles.

Four Ways Sequence and Non-Sequence Combine 簡非次第義

Chinese Source

第二、簡非次第義。問曰：菩薩修禪為一向次第，修禪亦有非次第？答曰：此得為四：一、明次第；二、明非次第；三、明次第非次第；四、明非次第次第。今明次第，如上說。《大品經》云：「菩薩次第行，次第學，次第道。」非次第者，菩薩修法華、一行等諸三昧，觀平等法界非深非淺，故名非次第。如《無量義經》說：「行大直道，無留難故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, distinguishing what is not sequential. Question: Is the bodhisattva's cultivation of dhyāna always sequential, or is there also a cultivation of dhyāna that is not sequential? Answer: This can be sorted into four: first, clarifying what is sequential; second, clarifying what is not sequential; third, clarifying what is sequential-then-not-sequential; fourth, clarifying what is not-sequential-then-sequential.

What is sequential is as explained above — the Larger Sūtra says: "The bodhisattva practices in sequence, studies in sequence, and walks the path in sequence." What is not sequential: the bodhisattva who cultivates the Lotus samādhi, the samādhi of a single practice, and the like, contemplating the equal dharma-realm as neither deep nor shallow — this is called not sequential. As the Sūtra of Measureless Meanings says: "Walking the great, direct road, with nothing left to hinder it."

次第非次第者，如《大品》中須菩提白佛：「次第心應行般若，應生般若，應修般若不？」佛告須菩提：「常不離薩婆若故，為行般若，為生般若，為修般若。」非次第次第者，如須菩提白佛：「一切諸法皆無自性，云何菩薩得從一地至一地？」佛告須菩提：「以諸法空故，菩薩得從一地至一地。」

What is sequential-then-not-sequential: as in the Larger Sūtra, where Subhūti addresses the Buddha, saying: "Should one practice prajñā with a sequential mind, give rise to prajñā, and cultivate prajñā?" The Buddha tells Subhūti: "Because one is never apart from omniscience, one thereby practices prajñā, gives rise to prajñā, and cultivates prajñā."

What is not-sequential-then-sequential: as when Subhūti addresses the Buddha, saying: "If all dharmas whatsoever are without any nature of their own, how can a bodhisattva pass from one ground to the next?" The Buddha tells Subhūti: "Precisely because all dharmas are empty, the bodhisattva is able to pass from one ground to the next."

問曰：今此四句但據菩薩，亦得通二乘否？答曰：二乘亦得作此說。何以故？知自有聲聞初發心行於行行，從根本初禪而修，乃至超越禪，方得阿羅漢果，是為次第。或有聲聞人聞說善來，一時具足三明、八解脫等，是為非次第。或有聲聞人修次第行行時即用慧行，善觀次第性空，從初心，乃至得阿羅漢，是名次第非次第。四、或有聲聞從初發心即修慧行，發電光三昧，得四果，未具諸禪，為欲滿足有為功德故，次第修五種禪定滿足，即是非次第而次第也。此義至第七、釋修證及第八、顯示果報等十意竟，即自分明。

Question: These four statements have just been given in terms of the bodhisattva — do they also apply to the two lesser vehicles? Answer: The two lesser vehicles can also be spoken of in this same way. Why is this? It is known that there are śrāvakas who, from their very first arising of the mind, practice practice-conduct, cultivating from the root first dhyāna onward, all the way to the transcendence dhyāna, only then attaining the fruit of arhatship — this is what is sequential. There are also śrāvakas who, upon simply hearing the words "welcome, come forth," become complete all at once in the three insights, the eight liberations, and the rest — this is what is not sequential. There are also śrāvakas who, while cultivating practice-conduct in sequence, make use of wisdom-conduct at the same time, skillfully contemplating the emptiness of the nature of that very sequence, from the first mind onward, all the way to attaining arhatship — this is called sequential-then-not-sequential. Fourth, there are also śrāvakas who, from the very first arising of the mind, cultivate wisdom-conduct directly, give rise to the lightning-flash samādhi, and attain the four fruits without yet being complete in the full range of dhyānas — and then, wishing to fulfill conditioned merit completely, cultivate the five kinds of dhyāna-concentration in sequence to completion — this is precisely what is not-sequential-then-sequential. This point will become clear of itself once the seventh topic, explaining cultivation and realization, and the eighth, revealing the fruits and rewards, and the rest of the ten topics are complete.

Reading note: This four-part grid — sequential, non-sequential, sequential-then-non-sequential, non-sequential-then-sequential — is a recognizably Tiantai move: rather than picking a side in "is the path gradual or sudden," Zhiyi sorts canonical proof-texts for both into a single fourfold scheme, and then, in the closing exchange, shows the same fourfold scheme applies just as well to the śrāvaka path as to the bodhisattva's. The overall effect undercuts any simple claim that "gradual" and "sudden" sort neatly by vehicle — both patterns of practice show up on both paths. This closes topic 4.

V. Discriminating the States of Mind Proper to Dhyāna's Method 簡禪波羅蜜法心第五

The fifth and final topic of fascicle 1 turns inward, from the map of attainments to the mind that walks through them. Zhiyi sorts both the object of practice ("dharma") and the practicing mind itself into the same fourfold scheme — contaminated, uncontaminated, both, neither — and then, in its closing stretch, subjects the whole scheme to a searching Madhyamaka-style analysis of what it could possibly mean to call anything "contaminated" in the first place.

Four Kinds of Dharma 辨法

Chinese Source

已略說諸禪詮次竟，諸禪中法心之相復應知之。今就明法心中，即為三意：一、先辨法；二、明心；三、分別簡定法心之別。就第一、先辨法中，法有四種：一、有漏法；二、無漏法；三、亦有漏亦無漏法；四、非有漏非無漏法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now that the sequence of the various dhyānas has been briefly explained, the mark of the dharma and the mind within these dhyānas should also be known. Here, in clarifying dharma and mind, there are three parts: first, distinguishing dharma; second, clarifying mind; third, discriminating and settling the distinction between dharma and mind.

First, distinguishing dharma: dharma is of four kinds — first, contaminated dharma; second, uncontaminated dharma; third, both contaminated and uncontaminated dharma; fourth, neither contaminated nor uncontaminated dharma.

一、有漏法者，謂十善、根本四禪、眾生緣四無量心、四空定是。所以者何？此十二門禪，體非觀慧之法，不能照了斷諸煩惱故。二、無漏法者，九想、八念、十想、背捨、勝處、一切處、次第定、師子奮迅、超越三昧、四諦十六行、十二因緣法緣四無量心、三十七品、三三昧，乃至願智、頂禪、十一智、三無漏根等諸無漏定是。所以者何？此諸禪中悉有對治，觀慧具足，能斷三漏故。三、亦有漏亦無漏法者，六妙門、十六特勝、通明等是。所以者何？此三種禪中雖有觀慧對治，力用劣弱，故名亦有漏亦無漏。四、非有漏非無漏法者，法華三昧、般舟念佛、首楞嚴等百八三昧，自性禪等九種禪，乃至無緣大慈大悲、十波羅蜜、四無礙智、十八空、十力、四無所畏、十八不共法、一切種智等是。所以者何？修是等法，不墮二邊，故名非有漏非無漏法。

First, contaminated dharma: this refers to the ten wholesome deeds, the root four dhyānas, the four immeasurable minds taken with sentient beings as their object, and the four formless concentrations. Why is this? These twelve gates of dhyāna are not, in their own nature, dharmas of contemplative wisdom, and so are unable to illuminate and sever the afflictions.

Second, uncontaminated dharma: this refers to the nine contemplations, the eight recollections, the ten contemplations, the liberations, the stations of mastery, the totality-bases, the sequential concentrations, the lion's-sport and transcendence samādhis, the sixteen aspects of the Four Noble Truths, the samādhi on the twelve links of dependent origination taken together with the four immeasurable minds, the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment, the three samādhis, and on through wish-wisdom, the summit-dhyānas, the eleven kinds of knowledge, the three uncontaminated faculties, and the other uncontaminated concentrations. Why is this? Within all these dhyānas there is, in every case, an antidote at work, contemplative wisdom complete, able to sever the three contaminants.

Third, both contaminated and uncontaminated dharma: this refers to the six wondrous gates, the sixteen special excellences, the thorough-and-bright contemplation, and the like. Why is this? Although these three kinds of dhyāna do contain contemplative wisdom as an antidote, its power and function are weak, and so they are called both contaminated and uncontaminated.

Fourth, neither contaminated nor uncontaminated dharma: this refers to the Lotus samādhi, the buddha-recollection samādhi of pratyutpanna, the hundred and eight samādhis beginning with the Śūraṅgama, the nine dhyānas beginning with the samādhi of self-nature, and on through unconditioned great loving-kindness and great compassion, the ten pāramitās, the four unhindered wisdoms, the eighteen emptinesses, the ten powers, the four kinds of fearlessness, the eighteen unshared qualities, the wisdom that knows all things in every mode, and the like. Why is this? Cultivating these dharmas does not fall into either extreme — hence they are called neither contaminated nor uncontaminated dharma.

Reading note: This fourfold grid resorts everything named across topics 3 and 4 by a new criterion: not which gate it's entered through, or where it falls in sequence, but how completely it cuts through affliction. The twelve gates of dhyāna (topic 4's foundational attainments) turn out to be merely contaminated on their own — concentration without the wisdom to dismantle delusion. The bodhisattva's nine unshared dhyānas, by contrast, land in the most radical category of all: not just "uncontaminated" but "neither contaminated nor uncontaminated," a distinction the next passage goes on to explain.

Why "Uncontaminated" Isn't Enough for the Highest Dharmas 辨法 · 問答

Chinese Source

問曰：何故言法華三昧等法皆名非有漏非無漏？如《法華》中說：「是德藏菩薩於無漏實相心已得通達，其次當作佛，號曰為淨身。」又如四無畏中，第二無畏名無漏無畏。如是等法，諸經論中多悉說為無漏，今何以言皆是非有漏非無漏法？答曰：此欲簡諸佛菩薩有中道不共之法，故須作此分別。如凡夫專依有漏，二乘偏行無漏。今諸佛菩薩所得不共之法，不滯二邊，則無二邊之漏失，是以悉云無漏。何故得免二邊漏失？正以中道之法非二邊所攝，故云非有漏非無漏也。此之二說，語異而意同，故無乖失。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Why is it said that the Lotus samādhi and dharmas like it are all called "neither contaminated nor uncontaminated"? As the Lotus Sūtra itself says: "This Bodhisattva Store of Virtue has already penetrated the mind of the uncontaminated true reality; next he shall become a buddha, named Pure Body." And likewise, among the four kinds of fearlessness, the second is called "the fearlessness of the uncontaminated." Dharmas of this kind are, in most sūtras and treatises, spoken of simply as uncontaminated — why, then, is it now said that they are all "neither contaminated nor uncontaminated"?

Answer: This is meant to set apart the unshared dharma of the middle way possessed by the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and so this distinction must be made. Just as ordinary people rely exclusively on the contaminated, and the two lesser vehicles practice one-sidedly in the uncontaminated — the unshared dharma attained by the buddhas and bodhisattvas does not become fixed in either extreme, and so has no leakage into either extreme, and is therefore, in every case, called "uncontaminated." How is leakage into either extreme avoided? Precisely because the dharma of the middle way is not contained within either extreme — hence it is called "neither contaminated nor uncontaminated." These two ways of putting it differ in wording but agree in meaning, and so there is no contradiction between them.

若任理性而論，則一切皆名非有漏非無漏法，故《大品經》云：「色無縛無脫，乃至一切種智無縛無脫。」理既無縛無脫，稱理之行豈不同名無縛無脫？無縛無脫者，即是非有漏非無漏之異名也。問曰：分別定慧為四句可爾，戒復云何？答曰：從十善、三歸、五戒、八齋戒、沙彌十戒、大比丘二百五十戒、菩薩十重四十八輕戒，亦得作四句分別其義，今不具釋。問曰：上第四、明禪詮次及下第七、辨修證中，皆先明有漏，次亦有漏亦無漏，次無漏，次非有漏非無漏。今分別四句法，何故異於前後，乃以三為二也？答曰：前後皆約修行入證以為詮次。今欲簡別法心之相事，須約言句為便。亦以諸經論中說四句皆爾，故云行時非說時，說時非行時，此義易明。

If one argues purely in terms of principle, then everything whatsoever may be called "neither contaminated nor uncontaminated" — hence the Larger Sūtra says: "Form has no bondage and no liberation, and so on up through the wisdom that knows all things in every mode, which likewise has no bondage and no liberation." Since principle itself has no bondage and no liberation, how could conduct that accords with principle not equally be called "without bondage, without liberation"? "Without bondage, without liberation" is simply another name for "neither contaminated nor uncontaminated."

Question: Sorting concentration and wisdom into this fourfold scheme is understandable enough — but what of precepts? Answer: From the ten wholesome deeds, the three refuges, the five precepts, the eight precepts of the fast-day, the śrāmaṇera's ten precepts, the full monk's two hundred and fifty precepts, and the bodhisattva's ten major and forty-eight minor precepts — these too can be sorted into this same fourfold scheme, though the full explanation will not be given here.

Question: Above, in the fourth topic, and further below, in the seventh topic distinguishing cultivation and realization, both first clarify the contaminated, next both contaminated and uncontaminated, next the uncontaminated, and next neither contaminated nor uncontaminated. Why, in sorting this fourfold scheme here, does the order differ, placing what was third now second? Answer: The order given before and after is arranged in terms of the sequence of cultivation entering realization. Here, in wishing to distinguish the mark of dharma and mind as a matter of exposition, the order is arranged for convenience of statement instead. This is likewise how the various sūtras and treatises state this fourfold scheme in general — hence it is said, "the time of practice is not the time of exposition, and the time of exposition is not the time of practice" — this point is easily understood.

Reading note: The middle way is defined here by what it escapes rather than what it positively is: not "uncontaminated" in the ordinary sense (which still implies an opposition to "contaminated," and so still leans on one side of a pair), but a dharma that cannot leak into either side of any such pair to begin with. Pushed to its limit, this same reasoning — via the Larger Sūtra's "no bondage, no liberation" — threatens to erase the fourfold scheme altogether, since on that logic everything is ultimately "neither contaminated nor uncontaminated." Zhiyi doesn't shy from this; he simply notes that ordering matters for exposition even where it doesn't for attainment, a point picked up again in the tetralemma discussion below.

Four Kinds of Mind, across Four Moments 明心

Chinese Source

第二、明心，有四種心：一、有漏心；二、無漏心；三、亦有漏亦無漏心；四、非有漏非無漏心。一、有漏心者，即是凡夫、外道心，具三漏故，名有漏心。所以者何？凡夫、外道修禪定時，約四時中分別，不得離結漏故。何等為四時中分別？一者、初發心欲修禪時，不能厭患世間，為求禪定中樂及果報故；二者、當修禪時，不能返照觀察，生見著心；三者、證諸禪時，即計為實，不知虛誑，於地地中見著心生；四者、從禪定起，若對眾境，還生結業，以是因緣，名為漏心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying mind: mind is of four kinds — first, contaminated mind; second, uncontaminated mind; third, both contaminated and uncontaminated mind; fourth, neither contaminated nor uncontaminated mind.

First, contaminated mind: this is precisely the mind of the ordinary person and the non-Buddhist practitioner, complete with the three contaminants — hence it is called contaminated mind. Why is this? When ordinary people and non-Buddhist practitioners cultivate dhyāna-concentration, this can be distinguished across four moments, in none of which are they able to leave the fetters and contaminants behind. What are these four moments? First, at the very moment the mind first arises, wishing to cultivate dhyāna, they are unable to grow weary of the world, seeking instead the pleasure and the fruit found within dhyāna-concentration. Second, at the moment of actually cultivating it, they are unable to turn contemplation back upon themselves, and a mind of views and attachment arises. Third, at the moment of realizing the various dhyānas, they take them at once to be real, not knowing them to be illusory and deceptive, so that a mind of views and attachment arises at every single stage. Fourth, upon emerging from dhyāna-concentration, when facing the various objects of the world, the fetters and karma arise once again. For these reasons, this is called a contaminated mind.

第二、明無漏心，亦約四時中分別：一、約發心者，二乘之人初發心欲修禪時，厭患世間，不樂禪樂及求果報，但為調心，則漏心自然微薄不起，因此能發無漏；二、修行者隨所修禪，悉知虛假，能伏見著，不生結業；三、得證者入諸禪定之時，若於定中發真空慧，斷諸煩惱，則三漏永盡；四、從禪定起，隨所對境，不生見著，造諸結業，以是因緣，名無漏心。前二心雖是有漏，而為無漏作因，因中說果，亦名無漏。

Second, clarifying uncontaminated mind: this too is distinguished across four moments. First, as to the arising of the mind: when a follower of the two lesser vehicles first sets the mind on cultivating dhyāna, they grow weary of the world, taking no delight in the pleasure of dhyāna or in seeking its fruit, but cultivate only to tame the mind — the contaminated mind then naturally grows faint of itself and does not arise, and through this the uncontaminated can be brought forth. Second, as to practicing: whatever dhyāna is cultivated, it is entirely known to be illusory and unreal, so that views and attachment can be subdued, and fetters and karma do not arise. Third, as to attaining realization: at the moment of entering the various concentrations, if true wisdom of emptiness is brought forth within concentration, severing every affliction, then the three contaminants are forever exhausted. Fourth, upon emerging from dhyāna-concentration, whatever objects are faced, views and attachment do not arise, nor is any fettering karma produced. For these reasons, this is called uncontaminated mind. Though the first two moments are still, strictly speaking, contaminated, they serve as the cause of the uncontaminated — and since the fruit is spoken of within the cause, they too are called uncontaminated.

第三、明亦有漏亦無漏心，亦約四時中分別：一、約發心者，此行人初發心欲修禪時，恛惶不定，或時厭離生死，不樂禪樂；或生見著，悕望定樂，愛樂果報。以生厭故，結業微羸；悕望定樂故，增長煩惱。二、約修行者，如不斷善根人欲修禪時，是人雖成就信等五法，不得名根。以其不能定伏結使，故名亦有漏。生於信等善法，故名亦無漏。三、約得證者，七種學人入諸禪時，雖發真智，結漏未盡，故名亦有漏亦無漏，乃至退法羅漢亦有此義。所以者何？未得無生智，故名亦有漏；得盡智，故名亦無漏。四、諸學人等從禪定起，隨對眾境，隨所斷惑，未盡之處或猶生著，故名亦有漏；斷惑盡處雖對眾境，結業不起，名亦無漏。

Third, clarifying mind that is both contaminated and uncontaminated: this too is distinguished across four moments. First, as to the arising of the mind: when this kind of practitioner first sets the mind on cultivating dhyāna, they waver, unsettled — at times growing weary of birth-and-death, taking no delight in the pleasure of dhyāna; at other times giving rise to views and attachment, longing for the bliss of concentration and delighting in its fruit. Because weariness arises, fettering karma is somewhat weakened; but because longing for the bliss of concentration arises, affliction grows. Second, as to practicing: take, for instance, a person who has not severed their wholesome roots and wishes to cultivate dhyāna — though such a person has accomplished the five factors beginning with faith, these cannot yet be called "faculties," since they are unable to fix and subdue the fetters — hence this is called "also contaminated." Yet because faith and the other wholesome factors have arisen, it is also called "also uncontaminated." Third, as to attaining realization: when the seven kinds of trainee enter the various dhyānas, though true wisdom is brought forth, the fetters and contaminants are not yet exhausted — hence this is called both contaminated and uncontaminated; the same holds even for the arhat liable to regression. Why is this? Since the wisdom of no-arising is not yet attained, it is called "also contaminated"; since the wisdom of exhaustion is attained, it is called "also uncontaminated." Fourth, when these various trainees emerge from dhyāna-concentration and face the various objects of the world, wherever the delusion they are severing is not yet exhausted, attachment may still arise there — hence this is called "also contaminated"; but wherever the delusion is already exhausted, though objects are faced, fettering karma does not arise — hence this is called "also uncontaminated."

第四、釋非有漏非無漏心，亦約四時中明：一、約發心者，菩薩大士初發意欲修禪時，不為生死，不為涅槃，則心不墮二邊；二、約修行者，菩薩修禪波羅蜜時，為福德故，不住無為，為智慧故，不住有為；三、約得證者，菩薩入諸禪時，若於禪中發無生忍慧，爾時心與法性相應，不著生死，不染涅槃；四、菩薩從禪定起，隨對眾境，心常不依有無二邊，以是因緣，菩薩之心名非有漏非無漏心。

Fourth, explaining mind that is neither contaminated nor uncontaminated: this too is clarified across four moments. First, as to the arising of the mind: when a great bodhisattva first sets the intention to cultivate dhyāna, it is neither for the sake of birth-and-death nor for the sake of nirvāṇa — so the mind does not fall into either extreme. Second, as to practicing: when the bodhisattva cultivates dhyāna-pāramitā, for the sake of merit they do not abide in the unconditioned, and for the sake of wisdom they do not abide in the conditioned. Third, as to attaining realization: when the bodhisattva enters the various dhyānas, if the wisdom of the forbearance of no-arising is brought forth within dhyāna, then the mind, at that moment, is in accord with the dharma-nature — neither attached to birth-and-death nor defiled by nirvāṇa. Fourth, when the bodhisattva emerges from dhyāna-concentration and faces the various objects of the world, the mind never relies on either the extreme of existence or the extreme of nonexistence. For these reasons, the bodhisattva's mind is called neither contaminated nor uncontaminated mind.

Reading note: The same fourfold grid now applies to the practicing subject rather than the object practiced, and Zhiyi runs each of the four kinds of mind through the identical four-moment template — arising, practicing, realizing, and emerging from concentration — which makes the four minds easy to compare side by side. Watch what changes across the row for "arising": ordinary craving for pleasure and result (contaminated), weariness of the world alone (uncontaminated), an unstable wavering between the two (both), and a motivation that simply never frames the question in terms of saṃsāra versus nirvāṇa at all (neither) — the bodhisattva's mind, from the very first moment, is not on the same axis the other three are measured against.

第三、料簡法心。問曰：諸佛說一切法皆空，絕諸言句。如《摩訶衍論》偈說：「般若波羅蜜，譬如大火焰，四邊不可取，邪見火燒故。」今云何作四句分別，將非墮戲論乎？答曰：佛法中不可得空，於諸法無所礙，因是不可得空故，說一切佛法十二部經。今說有四句無咎。譬如虛空，雖無所有，而一切物依以長成。如《摩訶衍論》偈說：
若信諸法空，是則順於理；
若不信法空，一切皆違失。
若以無是空，無所應造作。
未作已有業，不作有作者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, resolving points about dharma and mind. Question: The buddhas teach that all dharmas are empty, cutting off every word and phrase — as the Mahāyāna treatise says in verse: "The perfection of wisdom is like a great blazing flame; it cannot be grasped from any of its four sides, since wrong views are burned away by it." Why, then, is a fourfold scheme now being set forth — is this not falling into idle theorizing?

Answer: Within the Buddhadharma, the emptiness that cannot be grasped is precisely what places no obstruction on any dharma whatsoever — it is because of this ungraspable emptiness that the whole Buddhadharma, the twelve divisions of scripture, can be spoken at all. There is no fault, then, in now speaking of a fourfold scheme. It is like empty space itself: though it possesses nothing at all, every single thing depends on it in order to grow and take form. As the Mahāyāna treatise says in verse:
"If one has faith that all dharmas are empty, this accords with principle;
If one lacks faith in the emptiness of dharmas, everything goes astray.
If, taking this 'nothing' to be emptiness, one concludes there is nothing to be done at all —
Then karma already exists before any action, and there is one who acts even without acting.
Such being the marks of all dharmas, who is there that could ever fully fathom them?
Only one who has attained a straightforward mind, whose every word rests on nothing at all,
Free of the views of existence and nonexistence, whose mind is naturally extinguished from within."

今為開發行人方便知見，分別種種法門，故無句義中辨於句義，於理無失，故《大品經》云：「無句義是菩薩句義。」若汝欲離四句求解脫者，即還被無句縛。所以然者，如說有四句、無四句、亦有四句亦無四句、非有四句非無四句，汝尚不免無四句縛，豈得免亦有亦無等四句縛？當知了句非句，於句義無礙而得解脫，非是離句求於無句而得解脫，如天女呵身子云：「無離文字說解脫也。文字性離即解脫相。」

Here, in order to open up skillful understanding and insight for practitioners, the various dharma-gates are distinguished — hence, within what is without any conceptual content, conceptual content is nonetheless discerned, and this involves no fault in principle. Hence the Larger Sūtra says: "What is without conceptual content is itself the bodhisattva's own conceptual content." If you wish to seek liberation by leaving the fourfold scheme behind, you will only be bound instead by "no fourfold scheme." Why is this? For if one speaks of "having the fourfold scheme," "lacking the fourfold scheme," "both having and lacking it," or "neither having nor lacking it" — if you cannot even escape being bound by "lacking the fourfold scheme," how could you escape being bound by "both having and lacking" or the rest? One should know that liberation is attained by understanding that a concept is not fixed as a concept, remaining unobstructed within conceptual content itself — not by leaving concepts behind to seek some concept-free state and thereby attaining liberation. As the heavenly maiden rebuked Śāriputra, saying: "There is no such thing as speaking of liberation apart from the written word. The very nature of the written word being itself free of fixed self-nature is precisely the mark of liberation."

復次，今明法之與心合為八句，迴轉分別則有三十六句。若細歷法而明，即出無量句。若能於一句法通達一切句，則此辨若虛空，無有邊際。問曰：若爾，何以不約法心各作五句？答曰：諸佛出世，對緣化物，教門多約四句，如《摩訶衍論》中說，有四種悉檀：一、世界悉檀；二、為人悉檀；三、對治悉檀；四、第一義悉檀。初、有漏法心即是世界悉檀攝；二、無漏法心即是對治悉檀攝；三、亦有漏亦無漏法心即是為人悉檀攝；四、非有漏非無漏法心即是第一義悉檀攝。是中相攝之意，細尋可見。復次，《摩訶衍論》又於第一義悉檀中分別四門，如論偈說：
一切實一切不實，一切亦實亦不實，
一切非實非非實，如是皆名諸法實。
如是等，但有四句，更無第五句。今約四句明法心可以類此。餘經論中設有五句明義別有因緣，今取一途義便，故不約五句分別。

Furthermore: dharma and mind, taken together here, make eight statements; turned and distinguished further, they make thirty-six. If one traces through every dharma in fine detail, measureless statements emerge. And if one can, through a single statement about a single dharma, penetrate every statement whatsoever, then this analysis is as boundless as empty space itself.

Question: If this is so, why not sort dharma and mind each into five statements instead? Answer: When the buddhas appear in the world, teaching according to the occasion to transform beings, the gate of teaching is, for the most part, arranged around a fourfold scheme — as the Mahāyāna treatise explains, there are four kinds of siddhānta: first, the worldly siddhānta; second, the siddhānta for the individual; third, the siddhānta of the antidote; fourth, the siddhānta of ultimate meaning. Contaminated dharma-mind belongs to the worldly siddhānta; uncontaminated dharma-mind belongs to the siddhānta of the antidote; both contaminated and uncontaminated dharma-mind belongs to the siddhānta for the individual; neither contaminated nor uncontaminated dharma-mind belongs to the siddhānta of ultimate meaning. The sense in which each corresponds to the other can be seen by tracing it through in detail.

Furthermore, the Mahāyāna treatise, within the siddhānta of ultimate meaning itself, further distinguishes four approaches, as the treatise says in verse:
"Everything is real, everything is unreal,
Everything is both real and unreal,
Everything is neither real nor unreal —
All of these alike are called the true reality of all dharmas."
Statements of this kind number only four, with never a fifth. The present fourfold clarification of dharma and mind may be understood along the same lines. Where other sūtras and treatises do set forth a fivefold scheme for their own purposes, that has its own separate rationale; here, for the sake of a single, convenient approach, a fivefold division is not adopted.

Reading note: This is the treatise's first sustained excursion into explicit Madhyamaka-style argument, and it earns its place here by answering a real objection: if everything is empty and beyond words, doesn't a treatise this elaborate — ten topics, endless named lists, a fourfold scheme applied to everything — betray the very teaching it claims to serve? Zhiyi's answer is that emptiness is not a reason to stop speaking, but the very thing that makes speech possible without fixing anything in place, "like empty space itself... [which] everything depends on... in order to grow and take form." The four kinds of siddhānta (四悉檀) named here — worldly, for-the-individual, antidotal, and ultimate — are Nāgārjuna's own framework for why the Buddha teaches the same truth differently depending on the audience and occasion; matching each of the four dharma-mind categories to one siddhānta each is Zhiyi's own addition.

Is Dharma Contaminated, or Mind, or Neither Alone? 料簡法心 · 四句推檢

Chinese Source

問曰：此四種法心，法之與心，有何等異？如有漏法有漏心，此法心為當各是有漏，為當各非？故說漏若二各有者，法心合時應有二漏法起；若各無，和合亦應無。答曰：今不得言二各是漏，亦不得言二法中各都無漏。何以故？若心即是漏，如阿羅漢漏盡時，心應盡，法亦如是。所以者何？若法定是漏者，聖人入根本四禪亦應生漏。此四禪法未與心合，亦應自是漏。而聖人入四禪法不生於漏，四禪法未與心相應時，亦自無有漏法生，云何言法即是有漏？今言此漏不獨在法，亦不獨在心，法心合時便有漏生。以有有漏故，二處受名。譬如仙藥，人若服之，即令得仙。而藥之與人，本各非仙，藥人和合，則便有仙。故藥受仙藥之名，人受仙人之稱。若藥不因人，不名仙藥；人不因藥，不名仙人。漏法、漏心亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Among these four kinds of dharma-mind, what is the actual difference between dharma and mind? Take contaminated dharma and contaminated mind: are these each, individually, contaminated, or are they not? For if it is said that the contaminant belongs to each of the two separately, then when dharma and mind come together, two separate contaminated dharmas ought to arise; and if it belongs to neither separately, their union ought likewise to produce nothing.

Answer: One cannot say that both, taken separately, are contaminated, nor can one say that neither, taken separately, is contaminated at all. Why is this? If mind itself simply were the contaminant, then, just as at the moment an arhat's contaminants are exhausted the mind itself ought to be exhausted, so too should dharma be exhausted along with it. And why is this? If dharma were fixed as being, in itself, the contaminant, then a noble one entering the root four dhyānas ought also to give rise to the contaminant — since these four dhyāna-dharmas, prior to ever joining with mind, ought already to be contaminated in themselves. Yet a noble one entering the four dhyānas does not give rise to the contaminant at all; before the dharma of the four dhyānas has even come into accord with mind, no contaminated dharma arises there either — how, then, could dharma itself simply be the contaminant?

Here it is said that this contaminant belongs neither to dharma alone nor to mind alone — it is only when dharma and mind come together that the contaminant arises. And because there is a contaminant, both are given their respective names. It is like an elixir of immortality: when a person takes it, it grants them immortality — yet the elixir, and the person, are each, in themselves, neither one immortal. Only when elixir and person come together is there immortality at all. Hence the elixir receives the name "elixir of immortality," and the person receives the title "immortal." If the elixir did not depend on the person, it would not be called the elixir of immortality; if the person did not depend on the elixir, they would not be called an immortal. The contaminated dharma and the contaminated mind are exactly the same.

餘三種法心義類爾可知，故阿難說示比丘為舍利弗說偈：
諸法從緣生，是法說因緣，
是法緣及盡，我師如是說。
復次，若謂有漏之法，自有有漏法；若有漏之法由有漏心故，有有漏法；若有漏法由法由心故，有有漏法；若有漏之法不由法不由心故，有有漏法。如此之計，皆墮邪見。所以者何？若謂由有漏法故，有有漏法者，即是自性有漏法。若是自性有漏法，則應有無窮之漏法，以自性復有自性故。今實不爾。若謂有漏法不能自有，由有漏心故有者，即是他性有漏法。所以者何？若有漏法待有漏心為自性者，今有漏心待有漏法，豈非他性？若由他性而有有漏法者，他性若是有有漏法，則有漏法還是有漏法，更無心法之別；他性若非有漏法，非有漏法何能有有漏法？故知有漏法不由有漏心故有。若謂有漏法由有漏法、有漏心故有者，即是共有。若是共有，則從自、他性中而有有漏法。若爾，則一時應有二有漏法。今實不然，故知非自、他、共故有有漏法。若謂離有漏法，離有漏心，有有漏法者，即是無因緣而有有漏法。從因緣有有漏法尚不可，何況無因緣而有有漏法？破因成假，廣說如《止觀》。有漏心亦如是，餘三種法心亦如是。

The remaining three kinds of dharma-mind may be understood along the same lines. Hence Ānanda, addressing the monks, spoke this verse for Śāriputra:
"All dharmas arise from conditions;
This dharma is spoken of as cause and condition;
This dharma has both its conditions and their extinction —
Thus has my Teacher taught."

Furthermore: suppose one holds that contaminated dharma has, of itself, contaminated dharma; or that contaminated dharma exists because of contaminated mind; or that contaminated dharma exists because of both dharma and mind together; or that contaminated dharma exists apart from both dharma and mind. Every such supposition falls into wrong view. Why is this?

If it is held that contaminated dharma exists because of contaminated dharma itself, this is "contaminated dharma from self-nature." But if it were contaminated dharma from self-nature, then there would have to be a contaminated dharma without end, since a self-nature would itself have to have a further self-nature behind it — and this is, in fact, not so.

If it is held that contaminated dharma cannot exist of itself, but exists because of contaminated mind, this is "contaminated dharma from another's nature." But why is this? If contaminated dharma depends on contaminated mind as its own nature, then contaminated mind, in turn, depends on contaminated dharma — how, then, is this not equally "another's nature"? And if contaminated dharma exists because of another's nature: if that other nature is itself already contaminated dharma, then contaminated dharma is still simply contaminated dharma, with no distinction from mind-dharma left at all; and if that other nature is not contaminated dharma, how could what is not contaminated dharma give rise to contaminated dharma? Hence it is known that contaminated dharma does not exist because of contaminated mind.

If it is held that contaminated dharma exists because of contaminated dharma and contaminated mind together, this is "joint existence." But if it is joint existence, then contaminated dharma would have to exist out of both self-nature and another's nature at once — and if so, there ought to be two contaminated dharmas arising at the same time. This, too, is in fact not so. Hence it is known that contaminated dharma does not exist through self-nature, another's nature, or their joint action.

If it is held that contaminated dharma exists apart from both contaminated dharma and contaminated mind, this is contaminated dharma existing without any cause or condition at all. If contaminated dharma existing through causes and conditions is already untenable, how much more untenable is contaminated dharma existing without any cause or condition whatsoever? Refuting the provisional reality established by cause is explained fully in the Great Calming and Contemplation. Contaminated mind is exactly the same, and the remaining three kinds of dharma-mind are likewise the same.

復次，若有漏法定是有漏法者，是有漏法即是生滅相續法，為生故生，為滅故生，為生滅故生，為離生離滅得生？若是生生，即是自生；若由滅故生，即是他生；若由生滅故生，即共生。若離生滅而說生者，即是無因緣生。從因緣生尚不可，何況無因緣生？當知有漏生畢竟不可得。若無生則無滅，若無生滅即無相續，若無生滅相續，則無有漏法。破相續假，廣說如《止觀》。有漏心亦如是，餘三種法心亦如是。復次，若有漏法是生者，為生生故生，為不生生故生，為生不生故生，為非生非不生故生？若生生，則是自性生；若不生生，即是他性生；若生不生故生，即是共生；若非生非不生故生，即是無因緣生。從因緣生尚不可，何況無因緣生？是則於相待假中求有漏法生畢竟不可得。若無生，則無有漏。破相待假，廣說如《止觀》。有漏心亦如是，餘三種法心亦如是。當知有漏之法於因成、相續、相待中各各四句求畢竟不可得。若不可得，云何分別有有漏法？若無有有漏之法而說有漏法者，當知但有名字，是中不應定有所依，生諸戲論，破智慧眼。次明有漏心亦如是，若有漏法心如是，餘三句法心亦如是，但以世間名字故，說名字之法不在內、外、兩中間，亦不常，自有無名之名，故曰假名。

Furthermore: if contaminated dharma were fixed as, definitely, contaminated dharma, then this contaminated dharma would be a dharma of arising, ceasing, and continuing on — does it arise because of arising, because of ceasing, because of both arising and ceasing, or by leaving both arising and ceasing behind? If it arises by way of arising, this is self-arising; if it arises because of ceasing, this is other-arising; if it arises because of both arising and ceasing, this is joint arising; and if arising is spoken of apart from both arising and ceasing, this is arising without any cause or condition. If arising through causes and conditions is already untenable, how much more untenable is arising without any cause or condition? One should know, then, that the arising of the contaminated can, in the end, never be found. If there is no arising, there is no ceasing; if there is no arising and ceasing, there is no continuity; and if there is no continuity of arising and ceasing, then there is no contaminated dharma at all. Refuting the provisional reality established by continuity is explained fully in the Great Calming and Contemplation. Contaminated mind is exactly the same, and the remaining three kinds of dharma-mind are likewise the same.

Furthermore: if contaminated dharma is something that arises, does it arise because of arising, because of not-arising, because of both arising and not-arising, or because of neither arising nor not-arising? If it arises through arising, this is arising from self-nature; if it arises through not-arising, this is arising from another's nature; if it arises because of both arising and not-arising, this is joint arising; and if it arises because of neither arising nor not-arising, this is arising without any cause or condition. If arising through causes and conditions is already untenable, how much more untenable is arising without any cause or condition? Hence, seeking the arising of contaminated dharma within the provisional reality established by mutual dependence, it can, in the end, never be found. If there is no arising, there is no contaminated dharma. Refuting the provisional reality established by mutual dependence is explained fully in the Great Calming and Contemplation. Contaminated mind is exactly the same, and the remaining three kinds of dharma-mind are likewise the same.

One should know, then, that contaminated dharma, sought through this same fourfold scheme within each of the three provisional realities — establishment by cause, continuity, and mutual dependence alike — can, in the end, never be found. If it can never be found, how can a contaminated dharma be distinguished as existing at all? And if there is, in truth, no contaminated dharma, yet contaminated dharma is still spoken of — one should know that this is merely a matter of names, and that no fixed basis should be assumed to underlie it, lest idle theorizing be produced and the eye of wisdom destroyed. Contaminated mind, next, should be understood in exactly the same way. If contaminated dharma-mind is thus, the remaining three kinds of dharma-mind are likewise thus — it is only in terms of worldly names that this dharma of naming is spoken of; it is not located inside, outside, or in between the two, nor is it permanent; it possesses, of itself, a name that is really no name at all — hence it is called a "provisional name."

問曰：若爾，云何分別法心之異？答曰：但以世間名字故，分別法心之別，是中無有定實。問曰：云何於名字中分別法心之別？答曰：若知法心無所有，但有名字，則還如上分別法心之相無咎。故《大品經》云：「須菩提！不壞假名而說諸法實相。」復次，如心數為法，心王為心。受、想、行三陰及色陰為法，識陰為心。心相應法、心不相應法及色法、無為法為法，心法為心。所緣為法，能緣為心。能生為法，所生為心。所觀之境為法，能觀之智為心。法成於心，心依於法。如是等，於名字中種種分別法、心之別。雖作此分別，皆如幻化，無所取著，同歸一相。此義至下第十、結會歸趣中當廣釋。

Question: If this is so, how can the difference between dharma and mind be distinguished at all? Answer: It is only in terms of worldly names that the difference between dharma and mind is distinguished — there is nothing fixed or truly real underlying it. Question: How, then, is the difference between dharma and mind distinguished within mere names? Answer: If one knows that dharma and mind possess nothing at all, and are only names, then distinguishing the mark of dharma and mind as above involves no fault. Hence the Larger Sūtra says: "Subhūti! Without destroying the provisional name, the true reality of all dharmas is spoken of."

Furthermore: the mental factors are dharma, the ruling mind is mind. The three aggregates of sensation, perception, and formations, together with the aggregate of form, are dharma; the aggregate of consciousness is mind. Mind-associated dharmas, mind-dissociated dharmas, form-dharmas, and unconditioned dharmas are dharma; mind-dharma itself is mind. What is taken as an object is dharma; what takes an object is mind. What is generated is dharma; what generates is mind. The object contemplated is dharma; the wisdom that contemplates is mind. Dharma is accomplished through mind, and mind depends upon dharma. In ways such as these, the difference between dharma and mind is variously distinguished within mere names. Though these distinctions are made, they are, every one, like a magical illusion — nothing to be grasped at or clung to, all alike returning to a single mark. This point will be explained fully further below, in the tenth topic, where the treatise is gathered together and its destination pointed out.

Reading note: This closing stretch runs the "contaminated" through Nāgārjuna's classic fourfold refutation of causation three separate times — first asking whether contaminated dharma is produced by itself, by mind, by both, or by neither (rejecting all four); then whether it arises through arising, ceasing, both, or neither (the analysis of "establishment by cause," 因成假); then whether contaminated dharma continues in unbroken succession (相續假); then whether it arises through mutual dependence between arising and not-arising (相待假). Each of the "three provisional realities" (因成假, 相續假, 相待假) is a distinct Tiantai lens for how something can seem to exist conventionally while having no fixed essence under any of the four alternatives — full treatment is deferred here to the Great Calming and Contemplation (《止觀》, i.e. the Mo-ho Chih-kuan), a separate, much longer work of Zhiyi's not part of this site's current translation projects.

The elixir-and-immortal analogy is worth remembering on its own: it is Zhiyi's answer to a puzzle that recurs constantly in Buddhist philosophy — how two things that are each, on their own, "neither this quality nor that," can combine to produce something with a real, functioning quality neither one possessed alone. The upshot for practice: dharma and mind, contamination and purity, are none of them free-standing, self-sufficient properties waiting to be found — they arise, and are named, only in relation. This closes topic 5, and with it, the whole of fascicle 1.
