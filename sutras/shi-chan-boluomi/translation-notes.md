# Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen* (釋禪波羅蜜次第法門, "Explaining the Sequential Dharma-Gates of Dhyāna-Pāramitā," Taishō T46, no. 1916). They exist so that terminology stays consistent across all ten fascicles, the way the running glossary already works for [`sutras/cheng-weishi-lun/translation-notes.md`](../cheng-weishi-lun/translation-notes.md) and the two Śūraṅgama projects.

## The text and its author

Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597 CE), posthumously honored as the Great Master of Tiantai (天台智者大師), is the founder of the Tiantai school and one of the most important systematizers in Chinese Buddhist history. This treatise is one of his three major works on meditation practice, delivered orally as lectures, recorded by his disciple Fashen (法慎), and later revised by his other major disciple Guanding (灌頂) — the same Guanding responsible for recording Zhiyi's other great meditation treatises. It runs to 10 fascicles and lays out the practice of dhyāna (禪, meditative absorption) as a *pāramitā* — a perfection to be walked through in careful, graduated stages, from the preliminary conditions for practice through to the deepest attainments and their fruits.

## Source and copyright

- Base text: Taishō T1916, via the CBETA digital edition. Zhiyi died in 597 CE — the text itself has been in the public domain for well over a millennium, under any jurisdiction's copyright term. CBETA's own transcription is distributed under a non-commercial-use license (full header preserved), consistent with how this non-profit site already uses CBETA-sourced text across every other translation project here.
- No complete English translation of this treatise is known to exist. A 2021 academic article by an Italian scholar translates a portion of fascicle 1 for scholarly analysis, not as a complete rendering of the work. If this project is completed, it will likely be the first full English translation.
- Zhiyi wrote two other, considerably shorter meditation manuals — the *Six Dharma Gates to the Sublime* (六妙法門) and the *Concise Śamatha-Vipaśyanā* / *童蒙止觀* (also known as the *Xiao Zhiguan* 小止觀) — both already available in published English translation by Bhikshu Dharmamitra (Kalavinka Press). This treatise is a distinct, much longer work, not a variant of either of those two. Because Dharmamitra's translations of Zhiyi's related shorter texts already exist and are well known, we take particular care here to translate independently from the Chinese source and not to consult, echo, or fall into the phrasing of those published translations — this project's English wording, sentence structure, and section framing must be its own throughout.

## Structure

- CBETA's edition of this treatise splits several of the ten fascicles into "upper" and "lower" halves (之上 / 之下) for print-volume reasons; both halves belong to the same fascicle and are treated as one continuous unit on this site, matching how the fascicle is actually organized by content.
- Zhiyi structures the entire treatise around ten framing topics (十意), stated at the very opening of fascicle 1: (1) the great intent of cultivating dhyāna-pāramitā, (2) explaining its name, (3) its gates, (4) the sequence of its exposition, (5) the states of mind proper to its method, (6) its preliminary expedient means — including the famous twenty-five preliminary conditions (二十五方便), not yet reached in translation — (7) cultivation and realization, (8) fruits and rewards, (9) the teachings arising from it, and (10) where it all ultimately leads. We keep this outline as the page structure, the way Kuiji's 科判 organizes the *Cheng Weishi Lun* pages.

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 禪波羅蜜 | dhyāna-pāramitā | The perfection of meditative absorption; kept transliterated rather than translated as "the perfection of meditation," since "dhyāna" is already standard in English-language Buddhist writing and the text itself treats the term technically (see fascicle 1's own explanation of the name, topic 2). |
| 十意 | the ten topics | Zhiyi's own framing outline for the entire treatise, given in full at the opening of fascicle 1. |
| 發菩提心 / 菩提心 | to give rise to the bodhi-mind / the bodhi-mind | bodhicitta. Kept as "the bodhi-mind" rather than the fuller Sanskrit transliteration, matching plain-English usage elsewhere on this site. |
| 四弘誓願 | the four universal vows | The bodhisattva's four vows (to bring across all beings, sever all afflictions, master all dharma-gates, accomplish the unsurpassed buddha-path), mapped in this fascicle onto the Four Noble Truths. |
| 四諦 | the Four Noble Truths | Suffering (苦), accumulation (集), cessation (滅), and the path (道). |
| 十波羅蜜 | the ten pāramitās | The ten perfections (giving, precepts, patience, diligence, dhyāna, wisdom, skillful means, vow, power, wisdom [as a distinct tenth]) — this fascicle's argument is that cultivating dhyāna alone brings the other nine to completion. |
| 六通 | the six supernormal powers | ṣaḍabhijñā. |
| 四辯 | the four unhindered eloquences | pratisaṃvid — unhindered eloquence in dharma, meaning, language, and eloquence itself. |
| 二乘 | the two [lesser] vehicles | Śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, contrasted throughout with the bodhisattva's own motivation. |
| 金剛三昧 | the vajra-samādhi | The adamantine concentration described as the threshold to unsurpassed enlightenment. |
| 無量義處三昧 | the samādhi of the place of measureless meanings | 無量義處三昧, the samādhi the Buddha is described entering in the *Lotus Sūtra*'s opening chapter; cited here as an example of dhyāna's completeness. |
| 摩訶衍(論) | the Mahāyāna [treatise] | Zhiyi's own shorthand for the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra* (大智度論), attributed to Nāgārjuna — already a translation project on this site at [`sutras/mahaprajnaparamita-sastra/`](../mahaprajnaparamita-sastra/). Render literally as "the Mahāyāna [treatise]" in translated prose per this fascicle's own citation style, since Zhiyi never spells out the full title inline. |
| 大品經 | the Larger [Perfection of Wisdom] Sūtra | The Pañcaviṃśatisāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra, Kumārajīva's translation — the sūtra the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra* itself comments on. |
| 纓絡經 | the Yingluo Sūtra | 菩薩瓔珞本業經, cited for its own mapping of the four vows onto the Four Noble Truths. |
| 六情 | the six faculties of feeling | The six sense-faculties, named here (topic 1) for their role in the perfection of moral discipline — "closing off" the six faculties of feeling to keep evil from entering. |
| 天魔、外道 | heavenly demons, non-Buddhist paths | A fixed pair recurring wherever the text names external obstacles to genuine practice, alongside "the two [lesser] vehicles" as an internal one. |

Keep this table growing as new recurring terms appear in later fascicles — add a row rather than improvising a fresh translation of a term that already has an entry.

## Register

- Plain English, close to the Chinese, not a loose paraphrase — matching the method already used for the *Cheng Weishi Lun* and the two Śūraṅgama projects on this site.
- Zhiyi's prose in this fascicle moves through tightly reasoned enumerations (the ten wrong motivations, the ten pāramitās argument) — keep the enumerated structure explicit in translation (numbered or clearly parallel clauses) rather than smoothing it into continuous narrative prose.
- Mark every fascicle's status honestly. Fascicle 1 alone runs to roughly 14,000 Chinese characters across its upper and lower halves and all ten of Zhiyi's ten topics are only introduced, not exhausted, within it — a fascicle page may cover only one topic at a time. Label each page clearly with exactly how much of the fascicle it covers, rather than implying more is finished than actually is.

## Project status

Fascicle 1 is in progress: the treatise's front matter (title, ten-topic outline) and the whole of topic 1 (the great intent of cultivating dhyāna-pāramitā) are translated. Topics 2 through 10 of fascicle 1, and fascicles 2 through 10 of the treatise, remain untranslated.
