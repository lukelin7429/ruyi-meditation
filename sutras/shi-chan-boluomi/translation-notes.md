# Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen* (釋禪波羅蜜次第法門, "Explaining the Sequential Dharma-Gates of Dhyāna-Pāramitā," Taishō T46, no. 1916). They exist so that terminology stays consistent across all ten fascicles, the way the running glossary already works for [`sutras/cheng-weishi-lun/translation-notes.md`](../cheng-weishi-lun/translation-notes.md) and the two Śūraṅgama projects.

## The text and its author

Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597 CE), posthumously honored as the Great Master of Tiantai (天台智者大師), is the founder of the Tiantai school and one of the most important systematizers in Chinese Buddhist history. This treatise is one of his three major works on meditation practice, delivered orally as lectures, recorded by his disciple Fashen (法慎), and later revised by his other major disciple Guanding (灌頂) — the same Guanding responsible for recording Zhiyi's other great meditation treatises. It runs to 10 fascicles and lays out the practice of dhyāna (禪, meditative absorption) as a *pāramitā* — a perfection to be walked through in careful, graduated stages, from the preliminary conditions for practice through to the deepest attainments and their fruits.

## Source and copyright

- Base text: Taishō T1916, via the CBETA digital edition. Zhiyi died in 597 CE — the text itself has been in the public domain for well over a millennium, under any jurisdiction's copyright term. CBETA's own transcription is distributed under a non-commercial-use license (full header preserved), consistent with how this non-profit site already uses CBETA-sourced text across every other translation project here.
- No complete English translation of this treatise is known to exist. A 2021 academic article by an Italian scholar translates a portion of fascicle 1 for scholarly analysis, not as a complete rendering of the work. If this project is completed, it will likely be the first full English translation.
- Zhiyi wrote two other, considerably shorter meditation manuals — the *Six Dharma Gates to the Sublime* (六妙法門) and the *Concise Śamatha-Vipaśyanā* / *童蒙止觀* (also known as the *Xiao Zhiguan* 小止觀) — both already available in published English translation by Bhikshu Dharmamitra (Kalavinka Press). This treatise is a distinct, much longer work, not a variant of either of those two. Because Dharmamitra's translations of Zhiyi's related shorter texts already exist and are well known, we take particular care here to translate independently from the Chinese source and not to consult, echo, or fall into the phrasing of those published translations — this project's English wording, sentence structure, and section framing must be its own throughout.

## Structure

- CBETA's edition of this treatise splits several of the ten fascicles into "upper" and "lower" halves (之上 / 之下) for print-volume reasons; both halves belong to the same fascicle and are treated as one continuous unit on this site, matching how the fascicle is actually organized by content.
- Zhiyi structures the entire treatise around ten framing topics (十意), stated at the very opening of fascicle 1: (1) the great intent of cultivating dhyāna-pāramitā, (2) explaining its name, (3) its gates, (4) the sequence of its exposition, (5) the states of mind proper to its method, (6) its preliminary expedient means — including the famous twenty-five preliminary conditions (二十五方便) — (7) cultivation and realization, (8) fruits and rewards, (9) the teachings arising from it, and (10) where it all ultimately leads. We keep this outline as the page structure, the way Kuiji's 科判 organizes the *Cheng Weishi Lun* pages.
- Confirmed from the source text: fascicle 1 (both its upper and lower halves) contains only topics 1 through 5 in full. Topics 6 through 10 — including the twenty-five preliminary conditions — belong to fascicle 2 and beyond; do not assume the "十意" outline maps one-to-one onto fascicle boundaries when planning future fascicle pages.

## Register

- Plain English, close to the Chinese, not a loose paraphrase — matching the method already used for the *Cheng Weishi Lun* and the two Śūraṅgama projects on this site.
- Zhiyi's prose in this fascicle moves through tightly reasoned enumerations (the ten wrong motivations, the ten pāramitās argument) — keep the enumerated structure explicit in translation (numbered or clearly parallel clauses) rather than smoothing it into continuous narrative prose.
- Mark every fascicle's status honestly. Fascicle 1 runs to roughly 14,000 Chinese characters across its upper and lower halves and contains topics 1 through 5 of Zhiyi's ten-topic outline in full (topics 6–10 belong to later fascicles) — a fascicle page may still cover only part of a fascicle at a time for texts further ahead. Label each page clearly with exactly how much of the fascicle it covers, rather than implying more is finished than actually is.
