Zhiyi · Tiantai · 釋禪波羅蜜次第法門

Dhyāna, Walked Step by Step to Its Own Perfection.

The Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen is the Tiantai founder Zhiyi's major treatise on meditation as a pāramitā — not a single technique but a graduated path, walked in careful sequence from the right motivation for practice through preliminary conditions, method, cultivation, realization, and fruit. No complete English translation of this ten-fascicle work is known to exist. This page is the entrance for reading Ru-Yi's own translation, fascicle by fascicle, as it is made.

How to read this collection

Zhiyi delivered this treatise as oral lectures, recorded by his disciple Fashen and revised by his disciple Guanding — the same pairing behind his other great meditation works. It opens by naming ten framing topics for the whole treatise, from the right motivation for practice through to where the path ultimately leads, and then works through them one at a time, at real length.

This is a new, ongoing translation. Fascicles 1 and 2 are now complete — the treatise's first six topics, including the famous twenty-five preliminary conditions in full: the five conditions, the five desires to reject, the five hindrances to abandon, the five things to harmonize (the treatise's most practical instruction so far), and the five dharmas to practice.

Read by Fascicle

Fascicle 1 is complete and fascicle 2 has begun. Use this as the table of contents for the treatise as it grows — fascicles 1 and 2 link to Ru-Yi's own translation, and the rest link out to the source text on CBETA for now.

Fascicle 1 · Complete, all 5 topics

Why, What It's Called, Its Gates, the Sequence, and the Mind That Practices

The treatise's ten-topic outline, the great intent behind cultivating dhyāna-pāramitā, its name, its three gates of practice, the whole sequence of attainments from the twelve contaminated dhyānas through the bodhisattva's nine unshared samādhis, and a Madhyamaka-style analysis of the mind that practices them.

Fascicle 2 · Complete, all 25 conditions

The Twenty-Five Preliminary Conditions, in Full

Topic 6 complete: pure moral discipline (precepts, upholding/violation, repentance), the five desires to reject, the five hindrances to abandon, the five things to harmonize — food, sleep, and a full posture-breath-mind meditation manual — and the five dharmas to practice: aspiration, diligence, mindfulness, skillful wisdom, one-pointedness.

Translation Notes

Recurring terms, kept consistent as the fascicles grow. See the full translation-notes.md in the site repository for the complete, growing list.

禪波羅蜜 — dhyāna-pāramitāThe perfection of meditative absorption — this treatise's central subject, treated as a graduated path rather than a single technique.
十意 — the ten topicsZhiyi's own framing outline for the whole treatise, stated at the very opening of fascicle 1.
發菩提心 — the bodhi-mindBodhicitta: the compassionate resolve, grounded in correct contemplation of the middle way, that distinguishes a bodhisattva's motivation for practice from every other kind.
四弘誓願 — the four universal vowsTo bring across all beings, sever all afflictions, master all dharma-gates, and accomplish the unsurpassed buddha-path — mapped in fascicle 1 onto the Four Noble Truths.
十波羅蜜 — the ten pāramitāsThe ten perfections. Fascicle 1's argument: cultivating dhyāna alone brings the other nine to completion.
二十五方便 — the twenty-five preliminary conditionsThe treatise's well-known preparatory framework for practice, covered in later fascicles — not yet reached in translation.
