Settling the Mind, Illness, and Demonic Affairs

This page presents Ru-Yi's own English translation of fascicle 4 of Zhiyi's Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen (釋禪波羅蜜次第法門). Fascicle 3 closed with the "wholesome" half of the second internal-expedient-means layer; fascicle 4 opens with its unwholesome counterpart, then completes the internal expedient means' remaining three layers — how to settle the mind on whatever gate has actually opened, how to treat illness that arises during practice, and how to recognize demonic interference — bringing the whole of topic 6 to its close.

Verifying Unwholesome Capacity 明驗惡根性

Chinese Source

第二、明驗惡根性中，即有四意：一、先明煩惱數量；二、次明惡根性發；三、立對治法；四、結成悉檀，廣攝佛法。今釋第一、煩惱數量。煩惱者，《涅槃經》云：「煩惱即是惡法。」若具論惡法，名數眾多，今略約五種不善惡法開合，以辨數量。五種不善法者：一、覺觀不善法；二、貪欲不善法；三、瞋恚不善法；四、愚癡不善法；五、惡業不善法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying how to verify unwholesome capacity: this itself has four parts — first, clarifying beforehand the number and categories of affliction; second, clarifying next the arising of unwholesome roots; third, establishing the antidote methods; fourth, concluding by relating this to the siddhāntas, broadly encompassing the whole of the Buddhadharma.

Here, the first is now explained: the number and categories of affliction. As for affliction, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Affliction is itself unwholesome dharma." Were unwholesome dharma discussed in full, its names and categories would be manifold; here, in brief, the number and categories are distinguished with reference to five kinds of unwholesome dharma, opened out or brought together. The five unwholesome dharmas are: the unwholesome dharma of discursive thought; the unwholesome dharma of craving; the unwholesome dharma of anger; the unwholesome dharma of delusion; and the unwholesome dharma of unwholesome karma.

若開，乃有八萬四千，論其根本，不過但有三毒等分。若合，五不善法為四分煩惱者，三毒即守本，還為三分，並屬習因覺觀、惡業障道，此二不善，合為一分。所以者何？覺觀，即是帶三分煩惱而生，亦得說為習因等分。惡業障道，屬報因等分。習、報合論，但說一等分。故五種不善法，若一往合說，即但有四分，開論，即為八萬四千者，如《摩訶衍論》中所明：「貪欲煩惱具足二萬一千，瞋恚煩惱具足二萬一千，愚癡煩惱具足二萬一千，等分煩惱具足二萬一千，四分煩惱合出八萬四千塵勞。佛為對此說八萬四千法門為治。」

If opened out, these come to eighty-four thousand; discussing their root, however, they amount to no more than the three poisons together with the equal-portion affliction. If brought together, the five unwholesome dharmas form the four divisions of affliction: the three poisons remain as they are, forming three divisions, while discursive thought (belonging to the habituation-cause) and unwholesome karma obstructing the path (belonging to the retribution-cause) — these two unwholesome dharmas — combine to form a single division. Why is this? Discursive thought is itself brought forth carrying all three divisions of affliction within it, and so may also be spoken of as the habituation-cause's equal-portion affliction; unwholesome karma obstructing the path belongs to the retribution-cause's equal-portion affliction. Discussed together, habituation and retribution are simply spoken of as a single equal-portion.

Hence the five unwholesome dharmas, spoken of together in summary, come to just four divisions; discussed as opened out, they come to eighty-four thousand — as clarified in the Mahāyāna treatise: "The affliction of craving is complete in twenty-one thousand; the affliction of anger is complete in twenty-one thousand; the affliction of delusion is complete in twenty-one thousand; the equal-portion affliction is complete in twenty-one thousand; the four divisions of affliction together give rise to eighty-four thousand forms of worldly defilement. The Buddha, in response to this, taught the eighty-four thousand dharma-gates as their antidote."

今處中而明，是故但約五種不善惡法以辨惡根性發相。所以者何？上明善根性發，既約五種分別，今明惡根性發，豈不但據五不善法而辨？此則藥病相對，法相孱齊，行者欲修禪定，必須善分別之。

Here, a middle course is taken in the exposition, and so the arising of unwholesome capacity is distinguished only with reference to these five kinds of unwholesome dharma. Why is this? Since the arising of wholesome capacity above was already distinguished with reference to five kinds, how could the arising of unwholesome capacity now fail to be distinguished on the very same basis of five unwholesome dharmas? This is precisely medicine matched to illness, the two patterns held evenly together — and the practitioner who wishes to cultivate dhyāna-concentration must be able to distinguish this well.

Reading note: The four-divisions-of-affliction scheme (craving, anger, delusion, and an "equal-portion" affliction, each at 21,000, totaling the traditional 84,000) already appeared in fascicle 3's note on the five-gate dhyāna; here the treatise supplies the reasoning behind it directly — the "equal-portion" division is itself a merger of discursive thought (a habituation-cause phenomenon) with unwholesome karma obstructing the path (a retribution-cause phenomenon), the same habituation/retribution pairing used throughout fascicle 3's account of wholesome roots, now doing the identical work for their unwholesome counterparts. The five-fold structure chosen here is explicitly a mirror of the wholesome-root scheme's own five gates — "medicine matched to illness" — so that every unwholesome mark that follows has a named antidote already waiting for it.

Discursive Thought 明惡根性發 · 覺觀

Chinese Source

第二、次明惡根性發者，自有行人修禪定時，煩惱罪垢深重，雖復止心靜住，如上所說，內外善法都不發一事，唯覺煩惱起發，是故次明惡根性發。今就惡法發中，還約五種不善而辨，一不善法中，各自為三，三五則為十五不善法。若論行人發不善時，乃無的次第。今約教門依次而辨，具如前列。一、明覺觀發相，即為三種：一者、明利心中覺觀；二者、半明半昏心中覺觀；三者、一向昏迷心中覺觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, next clarifying the arising of unwholesome roots: there are practitioners who, at the time of cultivating dhyāna-concentration, carry afflictions and karmic defilements so deep and heavy that, although the mind is brought to stillness exactly as described above, no single wholesome dharma — external or internal — is brought forth at all; only affliction itself is felt stirring and arising. Hence, next, the arising of unwholesome roots is clarified. Here, within the arising of unwholesome dharma, the same five kinds of unwholesome dharma are again used as the basis for distinguishing it, each of the five itself opening into three, so that five times three gives fifteen unwholesome dharmas in all. As for the actual order in which a practitioner brings these forth, there is no fixed sequence to it; here, they are distinguished in the order given by the teaching itself, exactly as listed above.

First, clarifying that discursive thought has three differing marks of arising: discursive thought within a sharp, lucid mind; discursive thought within a mind half-lucid and half-dull; and discursive thought within a mind entirely sunk in dullness.

一、明利心中覺觀發者，若行人過去既不深種善根，於修定時都不發種種善法，但覺觀攀緣，念念不住，三毒之中亦無的緣，或時緣貪，或時緣瞋，或時緣癡，而所緣之事，分明了了，如是雖經年累月，而不發諸禪定，此為明利心中覺觀發相。二、半明半昏心中覺觀者，若人於攝念之時，雖覺覺觀煩惱念念不住，但隨所緣時，或明或昏。明則覺觀攀緣，思想不住；昏則無記瞪矒，無所覺了，名半明半昏覺觀發相。三、一向沈昏心中覺觀者，若行者於修定之時，雖心昏闇，似如睡眠，而於昏昏之中切切攀緣，覺觀不住，是名沈昏心中覺觀煩惱發相。

First, the arising of discursive thought within a sharp, lucid mind: if a practitioner has not, in the past, deeply planted any wholesome root, then, at the time of cultivating concentration, no wholesome dharma of any kind is brought forth at all — only discursive thought chasing after one object after another, never remaining still for a single moment. Even within the three poisons, there is no fixed object either: at times it takes craving as its object, at times anger, at times delusion — yet whatever object it takes is entirely clear and distinct. Even after years and months pass in this way, no dhyāna-concentration is ever brought forth. This is the mark of discursive thought arising within a sharp, lucid mind.

Second, discursive thought within a mind half-lucid and half-dull: if a person, at the time of gathering the mind in, still feels discursive affliction never remaining still moment to moment, but, depending on when an object is taken, is at times lucid and at times dull — when lucid, discursive thought chases after its object, thinking never settling; when dull, the mind is blank and glazed over, aware of nothing at all — this is called the mark of discursive thought arising within a mind half-lucid and half-dull.

Third, discursive thought within a mind entirely sunk in dullness: if the practitioner, at the time of cultivating concentration, has a mind dark and dull, resembling sleep, and yet, within this very dullness, chases urgently after objects, discursive thought never remaining still — this is called the mark of discursive affliction arising within a mind entirely sunk in dullness.

Reading note: These three marks form a spectrum by clarity rather than by object — the same restless discursive thought (覺觀) that fascicle 3 named as what restraining-the-mind stopping is meant to restrain, here graded from sharp-but-scattered, through intermittently dull, to thoroughly sunk. Notably, even the "sharpest" version of this pattern is still classed as an unwholesome-root problem, not a wholesome one — clarity of mind alone, without any object of genuine wholesome content, is not itself progress.

Craving 明惡根性發 · 貪欲

Chinese Source

二、明貪欲中即有三種發相：一、外貪欲；二、內外貪欲；三、遍一切處貪欲。一、外貪欲煩惱發者，若行人當修定時，貪欲心生，若是男子，即緣於女，若是女人，即緣於男子，取其色貌、姿容、威儀、言語，即結使心生，念念不住，即此是外貪婬結使發相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying that craving has three differing marks of arising: external craving; craving both external and internal; and craving pervading every place.

First, the arising of the affliction of external craving: if, at the time the practitioner is cultivating concentration, a craving mind arises — if male, taking a woman as its object; if female, taking a man as its object — seizing upon their form, appearance, bearing, and speech, so that a fetter-mind arises, never remaining still moment to moment — this is precisely the mark of the fetter of external lustful craving arising.

二、內外貪欲煩惱發者，若行人於修定之時，欲心發動，或緣外男女身相、色貌、姿態、儀容，起於貪著，或復自緣己身形貌，摩頭拭頸，念念染著，起諸貪愛，是以障諸禪定，此即內外貪欲煩惱發相。三、遍一切處貪欲煩惱起者，此人愛著內外如前，而復於一切五塵境界資生物等皆起貪愛，或貪田園、屋宅、衣服、飲食，於一切處貪欲發相。

Second, the arising of the affliction of craving both external and internal: if, at the time the practitioner is cultivating concentration, desire stirs and arises, whether taking as its object the bodily form, appearance, bearing, and manner of a man or woman externally, giving rise to attachment, or taking as its object one's own bodily form instead, stroking one's own head or touching one's own neck, growing attached and stained moment to moment, giving rise to every kind of craving and desire, thereby obstructing dhyāna-concentration — this is precisely the mark of the affliction of craving both external and internal arising.

Third, the arising of the affliction of craving pervading every place: this is a person who is attached both externally and internally exactly as described above, but who further gives rise to craving toward every object among the five sense-objects and every article of daily necessity as well — craving fields and gardens, houses, clothing, food and drink. This is the mark of craving pervading every place arising.

Reading note: The progression here — from a specific person as object, to one's own body added as a second object, to every object of daily life without exception — is the same expanding-scope pattern already seen twice in fascicle 3 (the five wheels, the three grades of the contemplation of impurity), now run in the unwholesome direction: craving does not stay contained to its original object but tends to generalize outward until it colors everything.

Anger 明惡根性發 · 瞋恚

Chinese Source

三、明瞋恚發相，即有三種：一、非理瞋；二、順理瞋；三、諍論瞋。一、違理瞋發者，若行人於修定時，瞋覺欻然而起，無問是理非理，他犯、不犯，無事而瞋，是為違理邪瞋發相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying that anger has three differing marks of arising: anger contrary to reason; anger in accord with reason; and anger through disputation.

First, the arising of anger contrary to reason: if, at the time the practitioner is cultivating concentration, an angry thought suddenly arises regardless of whether there is any reason for it or not, whether another has actually done wrong or not, growing angry over nothing at all — this is the mark of deviant anger contrary to reason arising.

二、順理正瞋發者，若於修定之時，外人實來惱觸，以此為緣而生瞋覺，相續不息，亦如持戒之人見非法者而生瞋恚，故《摩訶衍》中說：「清淨佛土中，雖無邪三毒，而有正三毒。」今言順理正瞋者，即其人也。三、諍論瞋者，行人於修禪時，著己所解之法為是，謂他所行所說悉以為非。既外人所說不順己情，即惱覺心生。世自有人，雖財帛相侵，猶能安忍少諍，義理即大瞋恨，風馬不交，是名諍論瞋發相。

Second, the arising of correct anger in accord with reason: if, at the time of cultivating concentration, another person truly comes and causes disturbance, and, taking this as its condition, an angry thought arises and continues without ceasing — just as one who upholds the precepts feels anger upon seeing another act against the Dharma — hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "Within a pure buddha-land, although there is no deviant form of the three poisons, there is still a correct form of the three poisons." What is meant here by correct anger in accord with reason is precisely this kind of person.

Third, anger through disputation: if the practitioner, at the time of cultivating dhyāna, clings to the dharma of their own understanding as correct, taking whatever another practices or teaches to be entirely mistaken — then, since what the other person says fails to accord with one's own feelings, a mind of irritation arises. There are, in the world, people who, even when their own money or goods are encroached upon, are still able to remain calm and forbearing with little dispute, and yet, over a mere matter of doctrine, grow greatly enraged, refusing to have anything further to do with the other — this is called the mark of anger through disputation arising.

Reading note: "Correct anger" is a striking admission — the treatise concedes that even a pure buddha-land retains some form of the three poisons, so long as it arises for the right reason, and that a precept-keeper's anger at witnessed wrongdoing is this same "correct" variety rather than a fault to be immediately suppressed. Anger through disputation is aimed squarely at the treatise's own likely readership: practitioners calm about material loss but instantly enraged over a doctrinal disagreement, a pointed observation about where scholars' and meditators' actual attachments tend to hide.

Delusion 明惡根性發 · 愚癡

Chinese Source

四、明愚癡發相，自有三種：一、計斷常；二、計有無；三、計世性。此三並是著眾邪見，不出生死，是故通名愚癡。一、計斷常癡者，行者於修定中，忽爾發邪思惟，利心分別：「過去我及諸法為滅，而有現在我及諸法邪？為不滅而有邪？」因是思惟，見心即發，推尋三世，若謂滅，即墮斷中；若謂不滅，即墮常中。如是癡覺，念念不住，因此利智捷疾，辯才無滯，諍競戲論，作諸惡行，能障正定出世之法，是為計斷常癡發之相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying that delusion has three differing marks of arising: holding to annihilationism or eternalism; holding to existence or nonexistence; and holding to a fixed worldly nature. All three of these are attachment to various wrong views, none of them leading out of birth-and-death — hence they are called, in general, delusion.

First, the delusion of holding to annihilationism or eternalism: the practitioner, within cultivating concentration, suddenly gives rise to a deviant train of thought, discriminating with a sharp mind: "Have the self and every other dharma of the past ceased, so that the self and every dharma of the present now exist? Or have they not ceased, and so continue to exist?" Reflecting in this way, the mind of view at once arises, tracing back through the three periods of time — if it is held that they have ceased, one falls into annihilationism; if it is held that they have not ceased, one falls into eternalism. Such a deluded discursive mind, never remaining still moment to moment, brings with it a sharp and quick intelligence, an eloquence with nothing to obstruct it, given over to disputation and idle discourse, and every manner of unwholesome conduct — able to obstruct right concentration and the world-transcending Dharma. This is the mark of the delusion of holding to annihilationism or eternalism arising.

二、計有無癡發者，亦於修定之時，忽爾分別，思惟覺觀，謂今我及陰等諸法為定有耶？為定無耶？乃至非有非無耶？如是推尋，見心即發，隨見生執，以為定實邪覺，念念不住，因此利智捷疾，戲論諍競，起諸邪行，障礙於正定，不得開發，是為計有無癡發之相。

Second, the arising of the delusion of holding to existence or nonexistence: likewise, at the time of cultivating concentration, one suddenly discriminates, reflecting with discursive thought on whether the self and the aggregates and other such dharmas definitely exist, or definitely do not exist, or, further still, neither exist nor do not exist. Tracing this back in this way, the mind of view at once arises, and, following whatever view arises, one clings to it as definitely real — a deviant discursive mind never remaining still moment to moment, bringing with it a sharp and quick intelligence, idle discourse and disputation, giving rise to every kind of deviant conduct, obstructing right concentration so that it cannot be brought forth. This is the mark of the delusion of holding to existence or nonexistence arising.

三、計世性癡發者，亦於修定之時，忽作是念：「由有微塵，所以即有實法；有實法故，便有四大；有四大故，而有假名眾生及諸世界。」如是思惟，見心即發，念念不住，因此利智辯才，能問能說，高心自舉，是非諍競，專行邪行，離真實道，乃至思惟分別剎那之心亦復如是。以是因緣，不得發諸禪定，設發禪定，墮邪定聚，是為計世性癡發之相。

Third, the arising of the delusion of holding to a fixed worldly nature: likewise, at the time of cultivating concentration, one suddenly has this thought: "Because there are subtle particles, there is therefore a real dharma; because there is a real dharma, there are therefore the four elements; because there are the four elements, there are therefore sentient beings and the various worlds, existing only as provisional names." Reflecting in this way, the mind of view at once arises, never remaining still moment to moment, bringing with it a sharp intelligence and eloquence, able to question and able to expound, exalting oneself with a haughty mind, given over to disputes over right and wrong, devoted single-mindedly to deviant conduct, straying far from the true path — and even the thought that discriminates a single instant of mind is exactly the same. Because of this, dhyāna-concentration cannot be brought forth at all; and even should some concentration be brought forth, it falls among the deviant concentrations. This is the mark of the delusion of holding to a fixed worldly nature arising.

Reading note: All three marks of delusion share the same telltale symptom: a sharp intelligence and fluent eloquence, not dullness — delusion here is explicitly an intellectual's affliction, wrong views seized on and defended with real cleverness rather than simple confusion. Holding to existence or nonexistence directly inverts the tetralemma-style analyses fascicle 3 used against "contaminated dharma" and "karma cannot be found" — there, the same four alternatives (existence, nonexistence, both, neither) were tested and found empty; here, seizing on any one of those same four alternatives as definitely true is itself classed as an unwholesome root.

Unwholesome Karma Obstructing the Path 明惡根性發 · 惡業障道

Chinese Source

五、明惡業障道發相，亦有三種：一、沈昏闇蔽障；二、惡念思惟障；三、境界逼迫障。一、沈昏闇蔽障者，行者於修定欲用心之時，即便沈昏闇睡，無記瞪矒，無所別知，障諸禪定，不得開發，是為沈昏闇蔽障發之相。二、惡念思惟障者，若行者欲修定時，雖不沈昏闇睡，而惡念心生，或念欲作十惡、四重、五逆、毀禁、還俗等事，無時暫停，因是障諸禪定，不得開發，是為惡念思惟障發之相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, clarifying that unwholesome karma obstructing the path has three differing marks of arising: the obstruction of sunken, dark dullness; the obstruction of unwholesome, deliberate thought; and the obstruction of oppressive states.

First, the obstruction of sunken, dark dullness: if the practitioner, at the very moment of wishing to apply the mind in cultivating concentration, immediately sinks into dark, dull drowsiness, blank and glazed over, aware of nothing distinctly, obstructing dhyāna-concentration so that it cannot be brought forth — this is the mark of the obstruction of sunken, dark dullness arising.

Second, the obstruction of unwholesome, deliberate thought: if the practitioner, wishing to cultivate concentration, though not sunk into dark, dull drowsiness, has an unwholesome thought arise instead — thinking, perhaps, of committing the ten unwholesome actions, the four grave offenses, the five heinous crimes, violating the precepts, returning to lay life, and other such matters, never pausing for a moment — thereby obstructing dhyāna-concentration so that it cannot be brought forth — this is the mark of the obstruction of unwholesome, deliberate thought arising.

三、境界逼迫障者，若行人於修定之時，雖無上事，而身或時卒痛，覺有逼迫之事，見諸外境，或見無頭手足、無眼目等，或見衣裳破壞，或復陷入於地，或復火來燒身，或見高崖而復墮落，二山隔障，羅剎虎狼，或復夢見有諸惡相，如是事皆是障道罪起，逼迫行人，或令驚怖，或時苦惱，如此種種，非可備說，是名境界逼迫障發之相。

Third, the obstruction of oppressive states: if the practitioner, at the time of cultivating concentration, though none of the matters above is present, suddenly feels pain somewhere in the body, sensing something oppressive taking place, and sees various external states — perhaps seeing bodies without head, hands, or feet, without eyes; or seeing one's own clothing torn and ruined; or sinking into the earth; or fire coming to burn the body; or standing on a high cliff and then falling from it; or two mountains closing in to block the way; rākṣasas, tigers, and wolves; or, in dreams, seeing various unwholesome signs — all such matters as these arise from the offense that obstructs the path, oppressing the practitioner, at times causing terror, at times causing distress; such things are manifold and cannot be described in full. This is the mark of the obstruction of oppressive states arising.

今約此五不善法，即合為三障。前三毒，即為習因煩惱障。等分之中覺觀亂法，即是麁四陰，故名為報障。三種障道，即為業障。何以知之？由過去造惡，未來應受惡報，即以業持此惡。若行者於未受報中間而修善者，善與惡乖，業即扶惡而起，來障於善，故知即是業障。如是三障，障一切行人禪定、智慧不得開發，故名為障。

Here, with reference to these five unwholesome dharmas, they are brought together as three obstructions. The first three — the three poisons — form the obstruction of affliction, belonging to the habituation-cause. Within the equal-portion affliction, the disordered dharma of discursive thought is itself the coarse four aggregates, and so is called the obstruction of retribution. The three kinds of the path's obstruction described above form the obstruction of karma. How is this known? Because of unwholesome deeds committed in the past, unwholesome retribution is bound to be received in the future, and it is karma itself that sustains this unwholesomeness. If the practitioner, in the interval before that retribution is received, cultivates what is wholesome, the wholesome and the unwholesome come into conflict, and the karma rises up in support of the unwholesome, coming to obstruct the wholesome — hence it is known that this is precisely the obstruction of karma. These three obstructions obstruct every practitioner's dhyāna-concentration and wisdom from being brought forth at all — hence they are called obstructions.

Reading note: This closing regrouping — five unwholesome dharmas collapsing into the three classical obstructions of the Buddhist tradition (affliction, retribution, and karma) — gives the fifteen marks just described their place within a much older framework than this treatise's own five-gate scheme. The three obstructions also explain something the marks themselves only show from the inside: why a practitioner sincerely trying to do good can feel actively opposed by their own unwholesome karma rising up "in support of the unwholesome" — not a failure of effort, but a named mechanism the treatise says the antidotes that follow are meant to address directly. This closes the second of verifying unwholesome capacity's four parts.

Matched Antidote Treatment 對治治

Chinese Source

一、明治覺觀多病者，如經中說：「覺觀多者，教令數息。」今覺觀之病既有三種，息為對治，亦為三意：一、明利心覺觀者，行者坐中明利之心攀緣，念念不住，此應教令數息。何以故？數息之法，繫心在息，息是治亂之良藥也。若能從一至十，中間不忘，必得入定，能破亂想。數息之法，於沈審心中記數，沈審之心能治明利，是以數息能除明利心中覺觀病也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying the treatment for one whose ailment is much discursive thought: as the sūtra says, "One with much discursive thought should be taught to count the breath." Since the ailment of discursive thought is itself of three kinds, the breath serves as its antidote in three corresponding senses as well.

First, treating discursive thought within a sharp, lucid mind: the practitioner's sharp mind, while sitting, chases after its object, never remaining still moment to moment — such a person should be taught to count the breath. Why is this? In the method of counting the breath, the mind is fixed upon the breath, and the breath is the true medicine for treating disorder. If one is able to count from one to ten without forgetting partway through, concentration is bound to be attained, and scattered thought is thereby broken through. The method of counting the breath keeps count within a settled, deliberate mind, and a settled, deliberate mind is able to treat sharp restlessness — hence counting the breath is able to remove the ailment of discursive thought within a sharp, lucid mind.

二、明治半明半昏覺觀者，病相如前說，今對治之法，應教令隨息。隨息出入，則心常依息。以依息故，息麁心即麁，息細心亦細，細息出入，繼心緣之，能破覺觀。心靜明鑒，知息出入，長短去就，照用分明，能破昏沈，是故說隨為治。若但數息者，即有扶昏之過。若但觀息，亦有浮亂之失，不名善對治也。三、明治昏沈心中覺觀者，覺觀起相如前說，對治之法，應教令觀息，息入時，諦觀此息從何處來，中間何所經遊，入至何處住。口出息亦如是。此法後當廣說。如是求其根源，出無分散，入無積聚，不見定想，明心觀照，心眼即開，破於沈昏。靜心依息，能破散亂，故以觀息對治沈昏覺觀之病。

Second, treating discursive thought within a mind half-lucid and half-dull: the mark of this ailment is as described above; here, the antidote method should teach following the breath instead. As the breath is followed, going out and coming in, the mind constantly relies upon the breath. Because the mind relies upon the breath, when the breath is coarse the mind, too, is coarse, and when the breath is fine the mind, too, becomes fine — as the fine breath goes out and comes in, the mind, sustained by following it, is thereby able to break through discursive thought. The mind, stilled and clearly discerning, knows the breath's coming and going, its length and direction, illuminating it distinctly — able thereby to break through dullness and sinking as well. Hence following the breath is said to be its antidote. Were one to use counting the breath alone, there would be the fault of encouraging dullness; were one to use observing the breath alone, there would be the fault of encouraging restlessness — neither, by itself, could be called a properly matched antidote.

Third, treating discursive thought within a mind sunk in dullness: the mark of discursive thought arising here is as described above; the antidote method should teach observing the breath instead. As the breath enters, one closely observes exactly where this breath comes from, what course it travels through in between, and where it finally comes to rest upon entering; the same applies to the breath as it goes out through the mouth — this method will be explained more fully later. Tracing the breath back to its very root source in this way, one finds that, going out, it does not scatter, and, coming in, it does not accumulate; no fixed notion of it can be found at all. The mind grows clear, contemplating and illuminating; the mind's own eye thereby opens, breaking through sunken dullness. A mind stilled through relying on the breath is able to break through scattered disorder — hence observing the breath serves as the antidote to the ailment of discursive thought within sunken dullness.

二、明治貪欲多病，如經中說：「貪欲多者，教不淨觀。」欲病既有三種，今對治亦為三意：一、明治外貪欲多者，病發從著外境男女容色、姿態、語言、威儀、細滑等相，是故婬火熾然不息。對治之法，應教作九想觀，若至塚間取死屍相，亦當諦觀可愛之境，躃著地上，觀見死屍胮脹、爛壞、膿血流出、大小便利、諸蟲唼食。今我著者亦復如是，何處可愛？作是觀已，婬心自息，是故九想能治愛著外境貪婬重病。

Second, clarifying the treatment for one whose ailment is much craving: as the sūtra says, "One with much craving should be taught the contemplation of impurity." Since the ailment of desire is itself of three kinds, its antidote likewise has three corresponding senses.

First, treating one whose ailment is much external craving: this ailment arises from attachment to an external object's form, appearance, manner, speech, bearing, smooth skin, and other such marks, so that the fire of lust burns on without ceasing. The antidote method should teach the practice of the nine contemplations: going, if possible, to a charnel ground to take up the mark of an actual corpse, closely observing this very object once found so lovely, now fallen flat upon the ground, observing the corpse swollen, decaying, pus and blood flowing out, excrement and urine, and the various worms devouring it — reflecting that what I myself am so attached to is exactly the same as this: where, in it, could there possibly be anything to love? Once this contemplation is made, the mind of lust comes to rest of itself — hence the nine contemplations are able to treat the grave ailment of craving attached to an external object.

二、明治內外貪欲煩惱，煩惱病發如前說，若欲治之，當教作初背捨等觀，諦觀內身不淨，破壞可惡，即破緣內貪愛，復當如前，觀外不淨可惡，即離外境貪愛，即是初背捨，以是不淨心觀內外色，能破內外愛著貪婬之病。三、明治一切處皆起貪愛者，貪病發相，如前說。治法，應教緣一切處大不淨觀，觀一切境男女、自身、他身、田園、屋宅、衣服、飲食、一切世間所有，皆見不淨，無有一處可生貪心，爾時一切處中生厭離心，則一切貪欲無復起處，是名對治一切處貪欲病。

Second, treating the affliction of craving both external and internal: the arising of this affliction is as described above. To treat it, one should be taught to practice the first liberation and its related contemplations, closely observing the inner body as impure, decaying, and repugnant, thereby breaking through craving that takes the inward body as its object; and, as before, observing the outward body as impure and repugnant as well, thereby leaving behind craving that takes an external object — this is precisely the first liberation. Through this mind that contemplates form, both inward and outward, as impure, one is able to break through the ailment of craving attached both externally and internally.

Third, treating one in whom craving arises toward every place without exception: the mark of this ailment's arising is as described above. The treatment method should teach the great contemplation of impurity taking every place as its object, observing every state — men and women, one's own body, others' bodies, fields and gardens, houses, clothing, food and drink, everything whatsoever in the world — all seen as impure, with not a single place left where a craving mind could possibly arise; at that point, a mind of weariness and detachment arises with respect to every place, and craving no longer has anywhere left to arise from at all. This is called the antidote to the ailment of craving toward every place.

三、明治瞋恚多病，如經中說：「瞋恚多者，教慈心觀。」治瞋病既有三種，今對治亦復有三：一、明治邪瞋者，日夜心中思惟非理，欲以惡事惱他瞋發之相，具如上說。治之，應令修眾生緣慈，取一親人得樂之相，緣之入定。如是見親人得樂中，怨人等皆令得樂，取他樂相，能生愛念，即破於眾生中瞋惱怨害之心。

Third, clarifying the treatment for one whose ailment is much anger: as the sūtra says, "One with much anger should be taught the contemplation of loving-kindness." Since the ailment of anger is itself of three kinds, its antidote likewise has three corresponding forms.

First, treating deviant anger: day and night, the mind dwells on what is contrary to reason, wishing to trouble another through some unwholesome act — the mark of this ailment's arising is exactly as described above. To treat it, one should be led to cultivate loving-kindness that takes sentient beings as its object, taking up the mark of a single loved one's happiness and taking this as the object through which concentration is entered. Seeing this loved one gaining happiness in this way, one then extends the same to neutral persons and even to enemies, letting them all gain happiness as well — taking up the mark of another's happiness in this way is able to give rise to a loving regard, thereby breaking through the mind of anger, vexation, and enmity harbored toward sentient beings.

二、明治正瞋者，若於餘事之中都無瞋心，但見人作惡，或復犯戒而起瞋心病發之相，具如前說。治之，應教修法緣慈，觀五陰虛假，不見眾生，豈有持犯是非之事？但緣諸受中法樂，以與於他，慈心愛念，不應加惱。是非既泯，瞋心自息，是為行法緣慈能治順理瞋病。三、明治一切法中諍論故瞋者，病發如前說。對治方法，應教修無緣慈。何以故？此人隨所得法，既自以為是，謂他即非，同我者喜，違我者即瞋，或於四句及絕四句中生執，或復執於中道。如是皆有所依，故有諍訟執計因緣，便生瞋覺。對治方法，令修無緣之慈，行此慈時，言語道斷，心行處滅，於一切法不憶不念。若無憶念，因何諍訟而生瞋心？大慈平等，同與本淨之樂，離惱他相，故名慈能與樂。亦得言菩薩為諸眾生說如是法，名為大慈。當知修無緣慈對治一切法中諍論瞋恚。

Second, treating correct anger: this is a person with no angry mind at all in every other matter, yet who, simply upon seeing another do wrong, or violate the precepts, has an angry mind arise — the mark of this ailment's arising is exactly as described above. To treat it, one should be taught to cultivate loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object, contemplating the five aggregates as empty and unreal, so that no sentient being is seen at all — how, then, could there be any matter of upholding or violating, right or wrong, to speak of? One simply takes the dharma-joy present within every sensation and offers it to others, a mind of loving-kindness and affection that ought never to add further vexation. Once right and wrong have dissolved away in this manner, the angry mind comes to rest of itself — hence practicing loving-kindness that takes dharma as its object is able to treat anger in accord with reason.

Third, treating anger through disputation over every dharma: the mark of this ailment's arising is exactly as described above. The antidote method should teach the cultivation of objectless loving-kindness. Why is this? Such a person, whatever teaching they have grasped, takes it to be correct of themselves and takes another's as therefore mistaken — delighting in whoever agrees, growing angry at whoever differs — clinging, perhaps, to one of the four propositions or to their total negation, or even clinging to the middle way itself. In every such case there is something relied upon, and so there is contention and fixed conceptual grasping as its cause and condition, giving rise to an angry thought. The antidote method has one cultivate objectless loving-kindness instead: in practicing this loving-kindness, the path of language is cut off, the sphere of mental activity is extinguished, and with respect to every dharma there is nothing recalled, nothing dwelt upon. If there is nothing recalled or dwelt upon, on what basis could contention give rise to an angry mind at all? Great loving-kindness, equal toward all, bestows this same, originally pure happiness on every being alike, free of any mark of troubling another — hence loving-kindness is said to be that which bestows happiness. It may also be said that when a bodhisattva teaches this same dharma to sentient beings, this, too, is called great loving-kindness. One should know that cultivating objectless loving-kindness serves as the antidote to anger through disputation over every dharma.

四、明治愚癡多病，如經中說：「愚癡多者，教觀因緣。」問曰：因緣之法，其義甚深，云何愚癡之人教觀因緣？答曰：言愚癡者，非謂如牛、羊等，但是人聰明利根，分別籌量，不得正慧，邪心取理，名為愚癡。愚癡之病既有三種，對治亦應立三：一、明治斷常癡病者，邪思執著，或起常見，或起斷見，便破因果，病相如前說。對治方法，應教觀三世十二因緣，過去有二，現在有八，未來有二，是為十二因緣。三世相因，不常不斷，如經偈說：
我真佛法中，雖空亦不斷，
相續亦不常，善惡亦不失。
若行者能善觀十二因緣，不執斷常，則邪見心息，亦得以此對治，破相續假惑。

Fourth, clarifying the treatment for one whose ailment is much delusion: as the sūtra says, "One with much delusion should be taught to contemplate causation." Question: the dharma of causation is exceedingly profound in its meaning — how, then, can a deluded person be taught to contemplate causation at all? Answer: what is meant here by "delusion" does not refer to something like an ox or a sheep; rather, it refers to a person of genuine intelligence and sharp faculties who nonetheless, in discriminating and reasoning things through, fails to attain right wisdom, seizing upon principle instead with a deviant mind — this is what is called delusion. Since the ailment of delusion is itself of three kinds, its antidote should likewise be established in three forms.

First, treating the delusion of holding to annihilationism or eternalism: deviant thought clings fixedly, giving rise to either the view of eternalism or the view of annihilationism, thereby breaking apart cause and effect — the mark of this ailment is exactly as described above. The antidote method should teach the contemplation of the twelve links of dependent origination across the three periods of time: two links belong to the past, eight to the present, and two to the future — these together are the twelve links. Causing one another across the three periods of time, they are neither permanent nor cut off, exactly as the sūtra verse says:
"Within my own true Buddhadharma, though empty, it is nonetheless not cut off;
Continuing on, it is nonetheless not permanent; the wholesome and unwholesome are nonetheless never lost."
If the practitioner is able to properly contemplate the twelve links of dependent origination, clinging neither to annihilationism nor to eternalism, the mind of wrong view comes to rest — and this may likewise serve as the antidote breaking through the delusion of provisional reality established by continuity.

二、明治計有無癡病發者，邪念思惟有我無我、有陰無陰等，如前說。立對治者，應教觀果報十二因緣。果報十二因緣者，觀現在歌羅邏時，名曰無明，乃至生老死等。現在即有五陰、十二入、十八界成就，皆從因緣生。此歌羅邏時，即有三事：一、命；二、暖；三、識，故名無明。此既從緣而生，無有自性，不可言有，不可言無，乃至老死亦復如是。若知非空非有，即破空、有二見。當知果報十二因緣觀，即治有無見病，今亦得以此對治，破執因成假惑。三、明治世性愚癡發者，若見細微之性能生萬法，如是邪念，名計世性，廣說如前。對治之法，還作一念十二緣觀。何以故？行者深觀一念之中具足十二，一非十二，十二非一，而今約一說十二，約十二說一。當知一無定性，無定一故，則世性不可得，故以一念十二緣觀破執世性邪癡。此一念十二緣觀多破執一異見，今亦即得以此破相待假惑。

Second, treating the delusion of holding to existence or nonexistence arising: deviant thought reflects on whether a self exists or does not exist, whether the aggregates exist or do not exist, and so on, exactly as described above. To establish its antidote, one should be taught the contemplation of the twelve links as karmic result. The twelve links as karmic result are contemplated thus: observing that, at the present moment of kalala, this is named ignorance, on through birth, old age, and death; in the present, the five aggregates, the twelve sense-bases, and the eighteen elements are already complete, all arisen from causes and conditions. At the very moment of kalala, there are already three things present: life-force, warmth, and consciousness — hence it is named ignorance. Since this arises entirely from conditions, it has no nature of its own; it cannot be said to exist, and it cannot be said not to exist — and the same holds all the way through to old age and death as well. Knowing that it is neither empty nor existent breaks through the two views of emptiness and existence. One should know that the contemplation of the twelve links as karmic result serves precisely to treat the ailment of the view of existence or nonexistence — and this may likewise serve as the antidote breaking through the delusion of provisional reality established by cause.

Third, treating the delusion of holding to a fixed worldly nature arising: seeing some subtle, fundamental nature as able to give rise to the ten thousand dharmas — such a deviant notion is called holding to a fixed worldly nature, described at length above. Its antidote method returns instead to the contemplation of the twelve links within a single thought-moment. Why is this? The practitioner deeply contemplates that, within a single thought-moment, all twelve links are already present in full: the one is not the twelve, and the twelve are not the one, and yet, here, the twelve are spoken of in terms of the one, and the one is spoken of in terms of the twelve. One should know that the one has no fixed nature of its own — and, since there is no fixed "one," no fixed worldly nature can be found either. Hence the contemplation of the twelve links within a single thought-moment breaks through the deviant delusion of holding to a fixed worldly nature. This same contemplation of the twelve links within a single thought-moment is, moreover, especially effective at breaking through the view that clings to sameness or difference — and this, too, may serve as the antidote breaking through the delusion of provisional reality established by mutual dependence.

五、明治惡業障道多病，如經中說：「障道者，教令念佛。」今障道既有三種，對治則亦立三：一、明治沈昏闇塞障發者，惡業病相，如前說。對治，應教觀應佛三十二相中隨取一相，或先取佛眉間毫相，閉目而觀。若心闇鈍，懸作不成，當對一好端嚴形像，一心取相，緣之入定。若不明了，即開眼更觀，復更閉目。如是取一相明了，次第遍觀眾相，使心眼開明，即破昏睡沈闇之心，念佛功德則除罪障。問曰：若取其相分明，能破沈昏者，何不作九想白骨等觀？答曰：九想白骨但是生死不淨之身，除罪義劣，故非對治。

Fifth, clarifying the treatment for one whose ailment is much unwholesome karma obstructing the path: as the sūtra says, "One whose path is obstructed should be taught to recollect the Buddha." Since the path's obstruction is itself of three kinds, its antidote should likewise be established in three forms.

First, treating the obstruction of sunken, dark dullness arising: the mark of this unwholesome-karma ailment is as described above. Its antidote should teach taking up, from among the thirty-two marks of the response-body buddha, any single mark as its object — perhaps first taking the mark of the tuft of hair between the Buddha's brows, closing the eyes and contemplating it. If the mind is too dull and dark to sustain this without any physical support, one should instead face a fine, dignified image, single-mindedly taking up its mark as the object through which concentration is entered. If it remains unclear, one should open the eyes and look again, then close them once more — taking up a single mark in this way until it becomes clear, then, in sequence, contemplating the rest of the marks one after another, so that the mind's own eye opens into clarity, thereby breaking through the sunken, drowsy, dark mind; the merit of buddha-recollection then removes the offense obstructing the path. Question: if simply taking up a mark clearly is able to break through sunken dullness, why not instead practice the nine contemplations, the white bones, and the like? Answer: the nine contemplations and the white bones concern only this body of birth-and-death and its impurity — their power to remove offense is comparatively weak, and so they do not serve as the antidote here.

二、明治惡念思惟障者，障發如前說。對治，應教念佛功德。云何為念？正念之心緣佛十力、四無所畏、十八不共、一切種智圓照法界，常寂不動，普現色身，利益一切，功德無量，不可思議。如是念時，即是對治。何以故？此念佛功德從緣勝善法中生心數，惡念思惟從緣惡法中生心數。善能破惡，故應念報佛。譬如醜陋少智之人，在端正大智人中即自鄙恥。惡亦如是，在善心中則恥愧自息，緣佛功德，念念之中滅一切障。三、明治境界逼迫障者，罪業發相，如上所說。對治方法，應教念法佛。法佛者，即是法性平等，不生不滅，無有形色，空寂無為。無為之中既無境界，何者是逼迫之相？知境界空故，即是對治。若念三十二相，即非對治。何以故？是人未緣相時，已為境界惱亂，而更取相者，多因此著魔，狂亂其心。今觀空，破除諸境界，存心念佛，功德無量，即滅重罪。此為對治，於義可見。略說對治治竟。

Second, treating the obstruction of unwholesome, deliberate thought: the arising of this obstruction is as described above. Its antidote should teach recollecting the Buddha's own merit. What is meant by such recollection? A mind of right mindfulness taking as its object the Buddha's ten powers, four fearlessnesses, eighteen unshared qualities, and the wisdom that knows all things in every mode, illuminating the entire dharma-realm completely, forever still and unmoving, manifesting bodily forms everywhere, benefiting all beings, its merit measureless and inconceivable. Recollecting in this way is itself the antidote. Why is this? This merit of buddha-recollection gives rise to a mental factor arisen from taking the finest wholesome dharma as its object, whereas unwholesome, deliberate thought gives rise to a mental factor arisen from taking an unwholesome dharma as its object; the wholesome is able to overcome the unwholesome — hence one should recollect the reward-body buddha. It is like an ugly, unintelligent person who, standing among the handsome and greatly wise, feels ashamed of themselves of their own accord; the unwholesome is exactly the same — standing within a wholesome mind, shame and remorse come to rest of themselves. Taking the Buddha's own merit as one's object, every obstruction is extinguished moment by moment.

Third, treating the obstruction of oppressive states: the mark of this karmic-offense arising is as described above. Its antidote method should teach recollecting the dharma-body buddha. The dharma-body is precisely the equality of the nature of dharmas itself, neither arising nor ceasing, without any form or shape, empty, still, and unconditioned. Within the unconditioned, there is no state present at all — what, then, could possibly be an oppressive mark? Knowing that every state is empty is itself the antidote. Were one instead to recollect the thirty-two marks, this would not serve as the antidote here. Why is this? Such a person, even before taking up any mark at all, has already been troubled and disturbed by these very states — and to take up a mark under these conditions risks, more often than not, becoming possessed by a demon through it, throwing the mind into further derangement. Here, instead, one contemplates emptiness, breaking through every state entirely, while still holding the mind fixed on buddha-recollection, whose merit is measureless — and grave offense is thereby extinguished. This is the antidote; its meaning can be readily seen. This concludes, in brief, the matched antidote treatment.

Reading note: The fifteen matched antidotes map exactly onto the fifteen unwholesome marks and, in most cases, directly onto the fifteen wholesome-root gates already named in fascicle 3 — the treatise's basic move throughout this whole layer being to treat the practice that produces a wholesome mark as also the cure for its corresponding unwholesome one. Two details stand out: the aside on why the nine contemplations and white bones are the wrong antidote for karmic obstruction specifically (their power to remove offense is "comparatively weak"), and the warning against recollecting the thirty-two marks when oppressive states are already present, on the grounds that taking up any mark under active demonic disturbance risks making things worse rather than better — a rare case of the treatise naming a technique that is excellent in general but actively contraindicated for one particular ailment.

Transposed and Non-Transposed Treatment 轉治 · 不轉治

Chinese Source

第二、明轉治者，出《摩訶衍論》解十力，明定力垢淨，智力中說。彼論云：「貪欲之人教修慈心，多瞋之人作不淨觀，愚癡多者教思惟邊無邊，掉散心中教令用智慧分別，沒心人中教令攝心。若如是者，名為轉治；若不爾者，名不轉治。」此為反上所說。今明轉治有二種：一者、病轉法亦轉；二者、病不轉而法轉。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying transposed treatment: this is given in the Mahāyāna treatise, in its explanation of the ten powers, within the discussion of the power of concentration's defilement and purity, and the power of wisdom. That treatise says: "One with much craving is taught to cultivate loving-kindness; one with much anger is taught the contemplation of impurity; one with much delusion is taught to contemplate the bounded and the unbounded; a scattered, agitated mind is taught to apply the discrimination of wisdom; and a sunken mind is taught to gather the mind in. If it is done in this way, it is called transposed treatment; if not, it is called non-transposed treatment." This runs contrary to what was said above. Here, transposed treatment is clarified as being of two kinds: the ailment itself is transposed and the dharma transposes along with it; or the ailment itself is not transposed while the dharma alone transposes.

今約前一觀中明轉治義，前對治治中，貪心多者，教觀不淨。觀心既成，見於不淨，厭患前境，便生瞋心。如佛在世，有諸比丘學不淨觀，成即顧人自害。如是之類，應教轉觀修慈，以治於瞋，名為轉治，此即藥病俱轉。如此說者，細熟尋檢，猶未稱教意。

Here, transposed treatment is first clarified with reference to a single contemplation as an example. Within the matched antidote treatment above, one with much craving was taught to contemplate impurity. Once this contemplation is accomplished, and impurity is genuinely seen, weariness and disgust arise toward the very object once desired, and an angry mind then arises instead — just as, while the Buddha was still in the world, there were monks who studied the contemplation of impurity and, upon its completion, turned around and harmed themselves. In such cases as these, one should be taught to shift the contemplation, cultivating loving-kindness instead, in order to treat this anger — this is called transposed treatment, the medicine transposing right along with the ailment itself. Stated in this way, however, upon careful and thorough examination, this still does not quite fit the teaching's true intent.

二者、病不轉而藥轉者，貪病不轉前不淨觀，修慈觀治。問曰：貪心之法，取人好相，慈亦取人好相，云何為對治？答曰：菩薩戒有明文：「一切男子皆是我父，一切女人皆是我母，而菩薩不起慈悲，行婬無度，不避六親，犯波羅夷罪。」若觀前境男女，皆如父、如母、如子，則自敬愛，心生慈念，能破貪欲，譬如父母，終不於子所生非法心。復次，慈名與他之樂，貪欲不善，增他煩惱，此非與樂之道。如是思惟，繫心修慈，慈定若發，即治貪欲。何以故？無量心是色界法，不應得有貪欲心生，此則病雖不轉，轉觀治之。今以不淨一門類之，餘十四門禪悉有二種轉治之義可知。復次，轉觀有二種：一者、轉心不轉境；二者、心境俱轉。善自推尋，其義可見。

Second, as for the ailment not transposing while the medicine alone transposes: the ailment of craving does not transpose, and yet, rather than continuing with the contemplation of impurity, loving-kindness is cultivated to treat it instead. Question: the dharma of a craving mind takes up another's attractive appearance as its object, and loving-kindness likewise takes up another's attractive appearance as its object — how, then, could this serve as an antidote at all? Answer: the bodhisattva precepts state explicitly: "Every man is my own father, and every woman is my own mother — and should a bodhisattva fail to give rise to loving-kindness and compassion, engaging in lust without restraint, showing no regard for these six kinds of kin, this constitutes a pārājika offense." If one contemplates the man or woman before them as though each were truly one's own father, mother, or child, then a reverent affection arises of itself, the mind giving rise to a thought of loving-kindness able to break through craving — just as a parent, after all, never gives rise to any improper thought toward their own child. Furthermore, loving-kindness is precisely that which bestows happiness upon another, whereas craving, being unwholesome, only increases another's affliction — this is no path toward truly bestowing happiness at all. Reflecting in this way, fixing the mind on cultivating loving-kindness, once the concentration of loving-kindness is brought forth, craving is thereby treated. Why is this? The immeasurable minds are dharmas of the form realm, within which no craving mind ought to be able to arise at all — this, then, is a case where, although the ailment itself does not transpose, the contemplation is transposed instead to treat it.

Here, using the contemplation of impurity as a single example, the same twofold sense of transposed treatment should be understood to hold for the remaining fourteen gates of dhyāna as well. Furthermore, transposed contemplation is itself of two kinds: the mind is transposed while the object is not; or both mind and object are transposed together. This may be readily seen through careful reflection of one's own.

第三、明不轉治者，亦為二意：一者、病不轉，觀亦不轉；二者、病轉觀不轉。一、病不轉，觀亦不轉者，如貪心人作不淨觀，貪心不息，更增想作觀，不須轉觀，當更作膿血爛壞相等，作一人不息，復作多人，如是乃至一城、一聚落皆作不淨，如《禪經》中廣說。或進入白骨流光等治，貪心方息，故名不轉治。雖有此義，理而推之，猶恐未是教之正意。

Third, clarifying non-transposed treatment: this, too, has two senses — the ailment itself does not transpose and the contemplation does not transpose either; or the ailment itself transposes while the contemplation does not.

First, the ailment does not transpose and the contemplation does not transpose either: as when a person of much craving practices the contemplation of impurity, and the craving mind fails to come to rest — rather than shifting to a different contemplation, one instead intensifies the very same contemplation further: producing marks of pus, blood, and decay in still greater detail; if picturing a single corpse fails to bring it to rest, picturing many corpses instead; and so on, all the way to picturing an entire city or an entire village as equally impure, exactly as explained at length in the Chan Sūtra. Or one may progress further into the white bones and their flowing light as the treatment, until the craving mind finally comes to rest — this is called non-transposed treatment. Although this sense has its own validity, upon careful reflection, this, too, may not quite be the teaching's true intent.

二、病轉觀不轉治者，行者為有貪欲病，作不淨觀治，貪欲轉而生瞋恚，爾時不轉不淨觀，即於不淨中增想，作不淨觀及白骨流光入定，瞋心自除，亦得即為二意：一、境不轉而心轉；二、境不轉，心亦不轉。餘十四對治不轉治義，類亦如是。

Second, the ailment transposes while the contemplation does not: the practitioner, having the ailment of craving, practices the contemplation of impurity to treat it, and the craving then transposes into anger instead. At this point, rather than shifting away from the contemplation of impurity, one instead intensifies this very same contemplation further, practicing the contemplation of impurity together with the white bones and their flowing light to enter concentration, and the angry mind is thereby removed of itself. This, too, may be understood as two further senses: the object does not transpose while the mind does; or neither the object nor the mind transposes at all. The same pattern of non-transposed treatment should be understood to hold for the remaining fourteen antidotes as well.

Reading note: The distinction between transposed and non-transposed treatment turns on a genuine clinical judgment call the treatise refuses to resolve with a single rule: when a practitioner's ailment shifts mid-treatment (craving curdling into anger, say), should the antidote shift to match the new symptom, or should the original practice simply be intensified until it works through the new symptom as well? Both are presented as legitimate, each illustrated with the same starting case (craving treated by the contemplation of impurity) so the two can be compared directly — a reminder that the fifteen matched antidotes just given are a starting point for a teacher's judgment, not a rigid lookup table.

Combined Treatment 兼治 · 兼轉兼不轉治

Chinese Source

第四、明兼治者，亦出《摩訶衍論》解八念念捨文中。彼論云：「菩薩法施者，法施因緣，或復說法，或現神通，或復放光，如是等利益度脫眾生，名為法施。復次，行法施者，應當善識眾生煩惱多少，或但有一煩惱病，或兩兩雜，或三三雜。若一煩惱，說一法治；兩兩雜者，說二法治；三三雜者，說三法治。」此即是兼治相。一病說一法治，如前說。兩病二法治者，如有貪欲病，復有瞋恚，當用不淨、慈心觀共治。何以故？若但用一法，雖偏治一邊，復增一邊，則為過失。今二法相兼，病則皆差。或不淨兼慈，或慈兼不淨，今應隨病起，以義斟酌。兼三兼四，乃至五等，悉有其義，今不具說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying combined treatment: this, too, is given in the Mahāyāna treatise, within its explanation of the passage on the eight recollections' recollection of relinquishment. That treatise says: "As for a bodhisattva's giving of the Dharma: through the causes and conditions of giving the Dharma, one may expound the teaching, or manifest supernormal powers, or emit light — benefiting and liberating sentient beings through such means as these is called giving the Dharma. Furthermore, one who practices giving the Dharma should skillfully recognize how many afflictions a sentient being carries — whether only a single afflictive ailment, or two mixed together, or three mixed together. If there is a single affliction, a single dharma is taught as its treatment; if two are mixed together, two dharmas are taught as their treatment; if three are mixed together, three dharmas are taught as their treatment." This is precisely the mark of combined treatment.

A single ailment treated by a single dharma is as described above. As for two ailments treated by two dharmas together: if a person has both the ailment of craving and the ailment of anger, one should use the contemplation of impurity together with the contemplation of loving-kindness to treat both at once. Why is this? If only a single method were used, although it might treat one side, it would only aggravate the other — this would itself be an error. Here, with the two methods combined together, both ailments are cured at once — whether the contemplation of impurity combined with loving-kindness, or loving-kindness combined with the contemplation of impurity, one should weigh and judge according to whichever ailment has actually arisen. Combining three, combining four, and even combining five methods together, all have their own applicable sense — this is not given here in full.

第五、明兼不轉治者，此義亦如轉治、不轉治意，但於兼中對病發多少，還約上轉、不轉意細推可見。

Fifth, clarifying combined transposed-and-non-transposed treatment: this sense, too, follows the same pattern already given for transposed and non-transposed treatment — only here, among however many ailments have arisen in combination, one applies the same distinction between transposed and non-transposed given above, and this may be seen through careful reflection.

Reading note: Combined treatment addresses a case the first three antidote types quietly assume away: real practitioners rarely present with a single, isolated ailment. Treating craving and anger together by combining their two matched antidotes, rather than picking one and letting the other fester, is a practical acknowledgment that the fifteen-fold taxonomy is analytic rather than how affliction actually shows up in a given sitting.

Treatment Through Neither Matching, Transposing, Nor Combining 非對非轉非兼治

Chinese Source

第六、非對非轉非兼治者，即是第一義悉檀波若正觀。此觀通能治十五種病，亦通能發十五種門禪，所以言非對非轉非兼治。正觀法性即法，不可以法對法，故云非對正觀，無偏不增，餘病不須轉也。力能遍破眾病，故不須兼。雖不得能破、所破，而治諸不善，悉皆除滅，故名為治。是以《摩訶衍》云：「有三昧但能除貪，不能除瞋，不能除癡，有三昧能除三毒。」即是今所明正觀第一義悉檀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, clarifying treatment that is neither matched, transposed, nor combined: this is precisely the right contemplation of prajñā, the siddhānta of ultimate meaning. This contemplation is able, in common, to treat all fifteen ailments, and is able, in common, to bring forth all fifteen gates of dhyāna as well — hence it is said to be neither matched, transposed, nor combined. Right contemplation is itself the very nature of dharmas, itself dharma — one dharma cannot be matched against another dharma, hence it is said to be unmatched. Right contemplation, being without any partial bias, needs no further increase, and no other ailment needs to be transposed against it either; its power is able to break through every ailment universally, and so it has no need to be combined with anything else. Although nothing can be found that breaks through, and nothing that is broken through, every unwholesome dharma is nonetheless treated and utterly removed — hence it is called treatment. Hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "There is a samādhi able only to remove craving, unable to remove anger or delusion; and there is a samādhi able to remove all three poisons together" — this latter is precisely the right contemplation, the siddhānta of ultimate meaning, clarified here.

所以波若一觀能治五病者：一、正觀能治貪欲，如《思益經》云：「貪欲之人以淨觀得脫，不以不淨，世尊自知。」二、正觀能治瞋者，如《般若》說：「我昔為歌利王割截身體，爾時無我相、人相、眾生相，則瞋恚不生。」故知實相能治於瞋。三、正觀能破治癡者，智慧破於無明，其義可見，故《涅槃經》云：「明時無闇，闇時無明。有智慧時則無煩惱，有煩惱時則無智慧。」四、正觀能治覺觀者，正觀心中，語言道斷，心行處滅，則覺觀從何而生？故《維摩詰經》言：「云何息攀緣？謂心無所得。」五、正觀能治罪障者，如前引《普賢觀》云：「端坐念實相，是名第一懺。眾罪如霜露，慧日能消除。」

As for why the single contemplation of prajñā is able to treat all five ailments: first, right contemplation is able to treat craving — as the Sūtra of Resolving Doubts says: "Those with much craving are liberated through the pure contemplation, not through the impure — the World-Honored One knows this of himself." Second, right contemplation is able to treat anger — as the Prajñāpāramitā says: "When, in the past, I had my body cut apart limb by limb by King Kalinga, at that very moment there was no mark of a self, no mark of a person, no mark of a sentient being — and so no anger arose." Hence it is known that the true mark itself is able to treat anger. Third, right contemplation is able to break through and treat delusion — wisdom breaks through ignorance; the sense of this can be readily seen. Hence the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "When there is brightness, there is no darkness; when there is darkness, there is no brightness. When there is wisdom, there is no affliction; when there is affliction, there is no wisdom." Fourth, right contemplation is able to treat discursive thought — within right contemplation's own mind, the path of language is cut off and the sphere of mental activity is extinguished; from what, then, could discursive thought possibly arise? Hence the Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "How does one bring the chasing after objects to rest? By the mind's having nothing at all that it can find." Fifth, right contemplation is able to treat the obstruction of offense — as already cited above from the Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra: "Sitting upright and being mindful of the true mark — this is called the foremost repentance. Every offense, like frost or dew, the sun of wisdom is able to dissolve away entirely."

復次，如世餘藥，各隨對治，能治一病，不能遍治一切病也。阿竭陀藥即能遍治一切眾病，是名非對非轉非兼治，亦能具足一切禪門。如《大品經》說：「欲學一切善法，當學般若。」所以者何？譬如王來，必有營從。若般若慧發，則一心具足萬行。此則可以如意寶珠為喻。

Furthermore: it is like every other worldly medicine, each matched to its own particular ailment, able to treat only that one ailment and no other — unable to treat every ailment universally. The agada medicine, however, is able to treat every ailment universally — this is precisely what is meant by treatment that is neither matched, transposed, nor combined, and it is likewise able to be complete in every gate of dhyāna at once. As the Larger Sūtra says: "Wishing to learn every wholesome dharma, one should learn prajñā." Why is this? It is like a king's arrival, which is necessarily accompanied by his whole retinue — once the wisdom of prajñā is brought forth, the whole of the myriad practices is thereby complete within a single mind. This may be illustrated by the simile of the wish-fulfilling jewel.

Reading note: This sixth and final antidote type is the same move the treatise has made twice before in this fascicle alone — embodying-truth stopping breaking through the other two kinds of stopping, and the vajra wheel completing the other four wheels — a single approach through direct wisdom that supersedes, rather than joins, every graduated technique before it. The agada-medicine simile (a legendary cure-all in classical Indian medicine) makes the point precisely: the fifteen matched antidotes are each a specific medicine for a specific ailment, while prajñā's right contemplation is the one medicine that needs no diagnosis at all, because it treats the underlying condition — the belief in a fixed self and fixed objects — that every one of the fifteen ailments merely expresses in a different guise.

Concluding in Terms of the Four Siddhāntas 結成悉檀

Chinese Source

第四、次明結成悉檀，廣攝佛法者，今約此驗惡根性中辨對治，即以對《摩訶衍論》所明四種悉檀義。所以者何？如十五種不善境界發相，此正是世界悉檀，乃至前明善根發相亦屬世界悉檀，以其皆是因緣生陰入界攝故。次明十五種對治禪門，即是對治悉檀，是中正辨藥病相對故。次明轉治兼轉不轉治，即是為人悉檀。此正逐人根緣不定，方便利益，故名為人。次明非對非轉，即是第一義悉檀，其義可見。故《摩訶衍論》云：「此四悉檀，即攝十二部經八萬四千法藏一切佛法。」理而推之，當知禪門之義，則為廣博，靡所不收。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, next clarifying the conclusion in terms of the siddhāntas, broadly encompassing the Buddhadharma: here, with reference to the antidotes distinguished within this verification of unwholesome capacity, they are now matched against the sense of the four siddhāntas clarified in the Mahāyāna treatise. Why is this? The fifteen kinds of unwholesome states and their marks of arising are precisely the worldly siddhānta — and, further back, the marks of wholesome roots arising already clarified likewise belong to the worldly siddhānta, since both are, in every case, encompassed within the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements arisen through causes and conditions.

Next, the fifteen kinds of antidote-gates of dhyāna just clarified are precisely the siddhānta of the antidote, since it is exactly there that medicine is properly matched against illness. Next, the transposed treatment and the combined transposed-and-non-transposed treatment just clarified are precisely the siddhānta for the individual — this precisely follows each person's own capacity and conditions, unfixed from person to person, benefiting through skillful means according to the individual, hence it is called "for the individual." Next, the treatment that is neither matched nor transposed, just clarified, is precisely the siddhānta of ultimate meaning; its sense can be readily seen.

Hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "These four siddhāntas encompass the twelve divisions of the scriptures, the eighty-four thousand stores of the Dharma, and the whole of the Buddhadharma." Reasoning this through, one should know that the meaning of the dharma-gates of dhyāna is thus vast and far-reaching, leaving nothing whatsoever outside what it gathers in.

Reading note: This closing move retroactively reorganizes everything covered since fascicle 3's opening of the second internal-expedient-means layer under Nāgārjuna's four siddhāntas, already introduced back in fascicle 1 topic 5: the wholesome and unwholesome marks themselves as the worldly siddhānta (things simply as they conventionally appear), the fifteen matched antidotes as the siddhānta of the antidote (the specific-remedy level), transposed and combined treatment as the siddhānta for the individual (calibrated to the person, not the abstract diagnosis), and the sixth antidote type — prajñā's right contemplation — as the siddhānta of ultimate meaning. That all four fit so exactly is the treatise's way of showing that this whole elaborate apparatus of fifteen wholesome marks, fifteen unwholesome marks, and six kinds of antidote was never a free-standing system of its own, but a specific application of the same four-siddhānta framework running through the entire treatise. This closes the fourth and final part of verifying unwholesome capacity, the whole of verifying wholesome and unwholesome capacity (明驗善惡根性), and the second of the internal expedient means' five layers.

Settling the Mind 明安心法

Chinese Source

第三、次明安心禪門者，略為五意：一、明隨便宜；二、明隨對治成就；三、明隨樂欲；四、明隨次第；五、明隨第一義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, next clarifying the dhyāna-gates for settling the mind: in brief, this has five parts — clarifying following what is expedient; clarifying following what the antidote has already accomplished; clarifying following inclination; clarifying following sequence; and clarifying following ultimate meaning.

今釋第一、隨便宜者，如驗善根性中發十五種禪門，隨其發法，當知過去已經修習，可還修令成就，隨所發法，安心修之，如發覺觸後，欲修安心，當教數息。所以者何？根本初禪多從數息中發，當知是人過去已曾數息修禪。今若從息道而入，與本相扶，禪則易發，加功不止，則能具足四禪空定，因此即發三乘聖道事等。金師之子，教令數息，是為隨本，善根發後，說安心法。餘十四善根發，隨便宜立安心亦如是。

Here, the first is now explained: following what is expedient. Among the fifteen dhyāna-gates whose arising was clarified within the arising of wholesome roots, whatever dharma has actually arisen should be understood to have already been cultivated in the past — it can be cultivated again and brought to completion; one should settle the mind by cultivating whatever dharma has actually arisen. For instance, if, after the tactile sensation of movement has arisen and one wishes to settle the mind, one should be taught to count the breath. Why is this? The root first dhyāna, for the most part, arises through counting the breath — hence it should be understood that such a person has already cultivated dhyāna through counting the breath in some past life. If, now, one enters again through the path of the breath, this supports what was already there, and dhyāna is thereby brought forth easily; continuing to apply effort without pause, one is able to become complete in the four dhyānas and the formless concentrations, and, through this, the noble path of the three vehicles and its fruits are brought forth as well.

The goldsmith's son was taught to count the breath — this is precisely following what is already one's own foundation: once the wholesome root has arisen, the method for settling the mind is then given accordingly. The same holds for settling the mind following what is expedient among the remaining fourteen wholesome roots arising as well.

二、明隨對治成就立安心法者，如行人本有貪欲不善障法，為治此病，作不淨觀，觀成病滅。爾時雖無欲病，而未證深法，當更加心修習不淨。作種種不淨成已，次當却除皮肉，修白骨流光，入八背捨，斷三界結，成三乘道，此則不失其功。若更安心餘法，方復造功，則於事難成。餘十四隨對治成就，辨安心法，類之可知。

Second, clarifying settling the mind by following what the antidote has already accomplished: as when a practitioner originally has the unwholesome, obstructing dharma of craving, and, in order to treat this ailment, practices the contemplation of impurity, this contemplation being accomplished and the ailment extinguished. At that point, although the ailment of desire is now absent, deep attainment has not yet been realized — one should apply the mind further still, continuing to cultivate the contemplation of impurity. Having accomplished the various forms of impurity, one should next strip away skin and flesh, cultivating the white bones and their flowing light, entering the eight liberations, severing the fetters of the three realms, and accomplishing the path of the three vehicles — this way, the effort already made is not lost. Were one instead to settle the mind on some other method at this point, effort would have to be built up all over again, and the matter would be difficult to accomplish. The same pattern, settling the mind by following what the antidote has already accomplished, should be understood to hold for the remaining fourteen antidotes as well.

三、隨樂欲者，若能對治，斷欲界煩惱不善之患，則十五種禪通無遮障。爾時當隨行者心所欲樂諸禪三昧，各安心其門而修習之，即皆開發，始終成就。此可以數人同治修為類。四、約次第立安心法者，遮障既除，自有行人欲從淺至深，具足修一切禪定，應從阿那般那中而教數息；證根本四禪空定已，次教隨息；證十六特勝已，次應觀息；具足通明之禪，次教不淨觀，入九想、背捨等禪，乃至次應觀心性，入九種大禪。禪定次第並如上。第五、明禪次第中，分別修證方法在下自當具說，今明約驗善惡根性，後用安心法。既有如此之便利，故次後而說。

Third, following inclination: if one is able, through the antidotes, to sever the afflictive, unwholesome troubles of the desire realm, then all fifteen kinds of dhyāna are open in common, with no obstruction remaining. At that point, one should settle the mind according to whichever dhyāna-samādhi the practitioner's own mind actually delights in and inclines toward, cultivating it through its own gate — and it will be brought forth in every case, accomplished from beginning to end. This may be understood on the model of the Ābhidharma masters' shared treatment through cultivation.

Fourth, establishing the method for settling the mind with reference to sequence: once the obstruction is removed, there are practitioners who wish to proceed from the shallow to the deep, cultivating every dhyāna-concentration in full — such a person should be taught to begin with counting the breath, within mindfulness of the breath; once the four root dhyānas and the formless concentrations are realized, one is next taught to follow the breath; once the sixteen special excellences are realized, one should next observe the breath; once the thorough-and-bright contemplation is complete, one is next taught the contemplation of impurity, entering the nine contemplations, the liberations, and the other such dhyānas; and, further still, one should next contemplate the nature of mind, entering the nine kinds of great dhyāna. The sequence of dhyāna-concentration follows entirely as given above.

The fifth topic's own sequence of dhyāna — its methods of cultivation and realization distinguished in detail — belongs properly below, where it will be given in full; here, it is only clarified with reference to how, once wholesome and unwholesome capacity has been verified, the method for settling the mind is used afterward. Since there is this kind of expedience already available, it is therefore explained here, immediately following.

五、明隨第一義者，泥洹真法寶，眾生種種門。入此十五種善根發後，及五對治除障已後，隨於一法門易悟之處，即以此為安心者，行人多因是門入聖道也。

Fifth, clarifying following ultimate meaning: nirvāṇa, the true jewel of the Dharma, has as many gates as there are sentient beings. After these fifteen kinds of wholesome roots have arisen, and after the obstructions have been removed through the five antidotes, one should settle the mind on whichever single dharma-gate proves easiest to awaken through — practitioners, for the most part, enter the noble path precisely through this gate.

Reading note: The five ways of settling the mind form a rough sequence of their own: the first two are diagnostic, following whatever the practitioner's own history (past habituation, or the antidote that just worked) has already revealed; the third and fourth are prescriptive, letting the practitioner follow either taste or a fixed curriculum once the obstructions are actually cleared; and the fifth cuts across all of them, settling on whatever single gate — among fifteen wholesome marks and five antidotes now available — happens to be "easiest to awaken through" for this particular person. Read together with fascicle 3's opening pedagogical note (the ideal teacher reads a student's capacity directly; the realistic default is to start everyone with stopping and let the results reveal what comes next), this section is that same diagnostic logic completed: verification tells you what a student has, settling the mind tells you what to do with it.

Treating Illness 明治病患

Chinese Source

第四、次明治病方法，行者既安心修道，或本四大有病，因今用心，心息鼓擊，發動成病，或時不能善調適身、息、心三事，內外有所違犯，故有病發。夫坐禪之法，若能善用心者，則四百四病自然差矣。若用心失所，則動四百四病。是故若自行化他，應當善識病源，善知坐中內心治病方法。若不知治病方法，一旦動病，非唯行道有障，則大命有慮。今明治病法中，即為二意：一、明病發相；二、明治病方法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, next clarifying the methods for treating illness: once the practitioner has settled the mind and taken up cultivation of the path, it may happen that the four elements, already carrying some underlying illness, are struck and stirred by the very application of mind and breath now underway, and illness is thereby brought on; or, at times, the practitioner simply fails to properly regulate the three matters of body, breath, and mind, some fault arising internally or externally, and illness is brought on in this way as well. As for the method of sitting meditation: if one is able to apply the mind skillfully, the four hundred and four illnesses will naturally be cured of themselves; but if the mind is applied improperly, these same four hundred and four illnesses will instead be stirred into motion. Hence, whether practicing for oneself or teaching others, one must properly recognize the source of illness, and properly know the methods for treating illness with the inner mind while sitting. If these methods are unknown, then, once illness is actually stirred up, not only will one's practice of the path be obstructed, but one's very life may be placed in jeopardy. Here, within clarifying illness, there are two parts: clarifying the marks of illness arising; and clarifying the methods of treating illness.

The Marks of Illness Arising 明病發相

Chinese Source

病發雖復多途，略出不過三種：一者、四大增動病相；二者、從五臟生病；三者、五根中病。略明四大病者，地大增故，腫結沈重，身體枯瘠，如是等百一患生。水大增故，痰癊脹滿，飲食不消，腹痛下利等百一患生。火大增故，煎寒壯熱，支節皆痛，口爽、大小行不通利等百一患生。風大增故，虛懸戰掉，疼痛轉筋，嘔吐嗽氣急，如是等百一患生。故經云：「一大不調，百一病惱；四大不調，四百四病一時俱動。」四大病發，各有相貌，當於坐時及夢中察之，其相眾多，不可具記。

Ru-Yi Translation

Although illness arises through many different paths, in brief, no more than three kinds are given: the marks of illness arising through the four elements' excess and disturbance; illness arising from the five internal organs; and illness within the five faculties.

Clarifying briefly the illnesses of the four elements: when the earth element is in excess, swellings, hardness, and heaviness arise, the body withering and thin — a hundred and one such afflictions arise from this. When the water element is in excess, phlegm and distension arise, food and drink failing to digest, abdominal pain and diarrhea — a hundred and one such afflictions arise from this. When the fire element is in excess, alternating chills and high fever arise, every joint aching, the mouth parched, urination and defecation obstructed — a hundred and one such afflictions arise from this. When the wind element is in excess, a hollow, unsteady trembling arises, aching pain and cramping, vomiting, coughing, and shortness of breath — a hundred and one such afflictions arise from this. Hence the sūtra says: "When a single element is unregulated, a hundred and one illnesses trouble the body; when all four elements are unregulated, four hundred and four illnesses stir all at once." The illnesses of the four elements each have their own distinct appearance, to be observed both while sitting and in dreams; their marks are manifold and cannot be recorded in full.

二、次明五臟生患之相：從心生患者，多身體寒熱、口燥等，心主口故；從肺生患者，多身體脹滿、四支煩疼、悶、鼻塞等，肺主鼻故；從肝生患者，多喜愁憂不樂，悲思瞋恚，頭痛、眼痛疼闇等，肝主眼故；從脾生患者，身體面上遊風，通身㿇㿇，痒悶疼痛，飲食失味，脾主舌故；從腎生患者，或咽喉噎塞，腹脹耳滿，腎主耳故。五臟生患眾多，各有其相，於坐時及夢中察之可知。其相眾多，不可具記。

Second, next clarifying the marks of illness arising from the five internal organs: illness arising from the heart mostly shows as alternating chills and fever throughout the body, a parched mouth, and the like, since the heart governs the mouth. Illness arising from the lungs mostly shows as distension throughout the body, aching in all four limbs, a feeling of oppression, a stuffed nose, and the like, since the lungs govern the nose. Illness arising from the liver mostly shows as excessive worry, sorrow, and unhappiness, grief and anger, headache, and eye pain or dimness, since the liver governs the eyes. Illness arising from the spleen shows as a wandering, wind-like sensation over the face and body, an all-over numbness, itching, oppression, and pain, and a loss of taste in food and drink, since the spleen governs the tongue. Illness arising from the kidneys shows as, perhaps, a choked, obstructed throat, abdominal distension, and a stuffed feeling in the ears, since the kidneys govern the ears. Illness arising from the five organs is manifold, each with its own distinct mark, to be observed both while sitting and in dreams; their marks are manifold and cannot be recorded in full.

三、次略明五根中患相。身患者，身體卒痛，百節酸疼、瘡痒等。舌患者，瘡強急，飲食失味等。鼻患者，鼻塞甕及流濃涕等。耳患者，耳滿疼聾及或時嘈嘈然作聲等。眼患者，眼懸視𥆨𥆨及瞖闇疼痛等，如是四大、五臟、五根病患，因起非一，病患眾多，不可具說。問曰：五根之患，無異五臟內外相因，今何以別說？答曰：為坐中別有治法故，須別說其相。

Third, next briefly clarifying the marks of illness within the five faculties. Illness of the body shows as sudden pain throughout the body, aching in every joint, sores and itching, and the like. Illness of the tongue shows as sores, stiffness, a loss of taste in food and drink, and the like. Illness of the nose shows as a blocked, stuffy nose and thick, flowing mucus, and the like. Illness of the ears shows as a stuffed, aching, or deafened feeling, and, at times, a buzzing sound, and the like. Illness of the eyes shows as a strained, unsteady gaze, and cloudiness, dimness, or pain, and the like. Such illnesses of the four elements, the five organs, and the five faculties arise from more than one cause, and their afflictions are manifold, not to be described in full. Question: are the afflictions of the five faculties not simply the inward-outward counterpart of the five organs' own afflictions — why, then, are they explained separately here? Answer: because there is a distinct treatment method specific to sitting practice for each, their marks must be explained separately.

行者若欲修禪，脫有患生，應當善自知因，起三種病，通因內外發動。若外傷寒冷熱風，飲食不慎，而病從三處發者，當知因外發。若用心不調，觀行違僻，或內心法起，不知將息，而致此三處病發，此因內發。復次，行者應知得病有三種不同：一者、四大增損故病，如前說；二者、鬼神所作及因魔事觸惱，故得病；三者、業報所得病。如此等病，初得即治，甚易得差；若經久，則病成身羸，治之則為難愈。

If the practitioner, wishing to cultivate dhyāna, happens to have illness arise, one should properly recognize its cause oneself. The arising of the three kinds of illness comes about through both external and internal triggers in common. If external injury from cold, chill, heat, or wind, or carelessness with food and drink, causes illness to arise from these three sources, one should know this arises from an external cause. If the mind is applied unevenly, contemplative practice goes astray, or some internal mental dharma arises without one knowing how to properly rest and regulate it, so that illness arises from these same three sources, this arises from an internal cause.

Furthermore, the practitioner should know that falling ill comes about in three differing ways: illness from the excess or deficiency of the four elements, as described above; illness obtained through the actions of spirits or through contact and disturbance from demonic affairs; and illness obtained through karmic retribution. Illnesses such as these, if treated as soon as they first arise, are cured quite easily; but if allowed to persist for a long time, the illness becomes established and the body grows weak, and treatment then becomes difficult to bring to a cure.

Reading note: The four-elements/five-organs/five-faculties triad gives three overlapping diagnostic lenses on the same body rather than three separate ailments, and the Q&A is explicit about why: the five faculties get their own listing not because they're physiologically distinct from the five organs but because sitting practice has a distinct treatment for each, a reminder that this whole section is organized around what a meditator can actually do about an illness, not around anatomy for its own sake. The three-cause scheme (elemental imbalance, demonic disturbance, karmic retribution) at the end quietly cross-references the previous section's third obstruction (karma) and anticipates the coming chapter on demonic affairs — illness, obstruction, and demons are being treated as three points on a continuum rather than unrelated topics.

Methods of Treating Illness 正明治病方法

Chinese Source

二、正明治病方法者，既深知病源起發，當作方法治之。治病之法，乃有多途，舉要言之，不過五種：一者、氣息治病，所謂六種息及十二種息。何等為六種氣？一、吹；二、呼；三、嘻；四、呵；五、噓；六、呬。此六種息皆於脣口之中方便轉側而作，若於坐時，寒時應吹，熱時應呼。若以治病，吹以去寒，呼以去熱，嘻以去痛，及以治風，呵以去煩，又以下氣。噓以散痰，又以消滿，呬以補勞。若治五臟，呼、吹二氣可以治心，噓以治肝，呵以治肺，嘻以治脾，呬以治腎。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, directly clarifying the methods of treating illness: once the source of the illness's arising is deeply understood, one should apply the proper method to treat it. The methods for treating illness are themselves manifold; stated in their essentials, they come to no more than five: first, treating illness through the breath, namely the six kinds of therapeutic exhalation and the twelve kinds of therapeutic breath.

What are the six kinds of therapeutic exhalation? Chuī; hū; xī; hē; xū; and xì. These six kinds of exhalation are all made through skillfully shaping the lips and mouth in different ways: while sitting, one should use chuī when cold and hū when hot. As treatments: chuī expels cold; hū expels heat; xī expels pain, and also treats wind; hē expels vexation, and also brings the breath down; xū disperses phlegm, and also relieves distension; and xì replenishes fatigue. As treatments for the five organs: hū and chuī together may treat the heart; xū treats the liver; hē treats the lungs; xī treats the spleen; and xì treats the kidneys.

復次，有十二種息能治眾患：一、謂上息；二、下息；三、滿息；四、燋息；五、增長息；六、滅壞息；七、暖息；八、冷息；九、衝息；十、持息；十一、和息；十二、補息。此十二息皆心中作想而用。今略明十二息對治患之相。上息治沈重，下息治虛懸，滿息治枯瘠，燋息治腫滿，增長息治損，滅壞息治增，暖息治冷，冷息治熱，衝息治壅結不通，持息治戰動，和息通治四大不和，補息資補四大。善用此息，可以遍治眾患，用之失所，各生眾患，推之可知。諸師用息治病，方法眾多，不備說，今略示一兩條，令知大意。

Furthermore: there are twelve kinds of therapeutic breath able to treat every affliction: the rising breath; the sinking breath; the filling breath; the scorching breath; the augmenting breath; the dissolving breath; the warming breath; the cooling breath; the surging breath; the sustaining breath; the harmonizing breath; and the replenishing breath. All twelve of these breaths are applied through visualization within the mind. Here, the mark of each of the twelve breaths' treatment is briefly clarified: the rising breath treats heaviness and sinking; the sinking breath treats a hollow, unsteady feeling; the filling breath treats withering and thinness; the scorching breath treats swelling and distension; the augmenting breath treats depletion; the dissolving breath treats excess; the warming breath treats cold; the cooling breath treats heat; the surging breath treats blockage and obstruction; the sustaining breath treats trembling and shaking; the harmonizing breath treats disharmony among the four elements generally; and the replenishing breath restores and nourishes the four elements. Used skillfully, these breaths are able to treat every affliction universally; used improperly, each in turn gives rise to its own further afflictions — this may be readily understood through reflection. The various masters' methods for treating illness through the breath are themselves manifold and are not given here in full; only one or two points are shown here in brief, so that the general sense may be known.

二、明假想治病者，具如《雜阿含》、《治禪病祕法》七十二法中廣說。但今人神根既鈍，作此觀想，多不成就。或不得其意，非唯治病不差，更增眾患。故諸師善得意者，若有祕要，假想用之，無往不愈，但不可具以文載。三、呪術治病者，萬法悉有對治，以相厭禳，善知其法術，用之無不即愈。呪法出諸修多羅及禪經中，術法諸師祕之，多不妄傳。

Second, clarifying treating illness through visualization: this is explained at length in the Saṃyukta Āgama and in the Secret Methods for Treating the Illnesses of Dhyāna, among its seventy-two methods. But since people's spiritual faculties in the present age are already dull, this contemplative visualization is, for the most part, not successfully accomplished; or, failing to grasp its proper sense, not only does it fail to cure the illness, it may increase one's afflictions still further. Hence, among masters who have genuinely grasped its sense, where there is some secret essential, visualization is used and there is nowhere it fails to cure — but this cannot be conveyed in full through writing.

Third, treating illness through mantra: every dharma whatsoever has its own antidote, warded off and dispelled through its corresponding counter-force; one who properly knows this method and its technique, using it, finds nowhere it fails to cure at once. Mantra methods are given in the various sūtras and in the Chan Sūtra; the technique itself is kept secret by the various masters, and, for the most part, is not transmitted carelessly.

四、用心主境治病者，有師言：「心是一期果報之主，譬如王有所至處，群賊迸散。心王亦爾，隨有病生之處，住心其中，經久不散，病即除滅。」又師云：「用心住憂陀那。此云丹田，去臍下二寸半，多治眾患。」又師云：「安心足下，多有所治。」其要眾多，今不具說。五、觀析治病者，用正智慧檢受病既不可得，四大之患即自消滅。若是鬼神及因魔羅得病，當用強心加呪，及以觀照等法助治之。若是業病，必須助以修福、懺悔、轉讀，患即自滅。

Fourth, treating illness by fixing the mind as ruler over its object: one master says: "The mind is the ruler of this present life's own retribution — just as, wherever a king arrives, the surrounding bandits scatter and flee, the mind as ruler is exactly the same: wherever illness has arisen, if the mind is made to dwell there and not disperse for a long while, the illness is thereby removed and extinguished." Another master says: "Fix the mind, dwelling on udāna" — this refers to the cinnabar field, two and a half inches below the navel — "which treats a great many afflictions." Yet another master says: "Settling the mind at the soles of the feet also treats a great many afflictions." The essentials here are themselves manifold, and are not given here in full.

Fifth, treating illness through analytic contemplation: using right wisdom to examine the illness one is undergoing, and finding that it cannot be found at all, the affliction of the four elements is thereby extinguished of itself. If the illness is caused by spirits or by contact with a demon's affairs, one should use a fortified mind together with mantra, and such methods as contemplative illumination, to assist in its treatment. If it is a karmic illness, one must assist its treatment with cultivating merit, repentance, and reciting the sūtras, and the affliction will be extinguished of itself.

此五種治病之法，若行人善得一意，則可自行兼他，況復具足通達。若都不知其一，則患生無治，非唯廢修正業，亦恐性命有慮，豈可自行教人？是故欲修禪之者，必須善解內心治病之法。內心治病方法眾多，豈可具傳於文？若欲習知，當更尋訪上來所出是旨，是示其大意。若但依此文，文既闕略，恐未可定怙，智者善得其意，方便迴轉，無善知識之處，亦足權以救急。

As for these five methods of treating illness: if the practitioner properly grasps even a single one of them, this alone is enough to practice for oneself and assist others as well, let alone were one to master all five completely. But if none of the five is known at all, then, once illness has arisen, there is no way to treat it — not only would this cause one's proper cultivation to be abandoned, it might also place one's very life at risk. How, then, could one practice or teach others without this knowledge? Hence anyone wishing to cultivate dhyāna must properly understand these inner methods of treating illness with the mind. The methods for treating illness with the inner mind are themselves manifold — how could they possibly be conveyed in full through writing? Should one wish to study them properly, one should further seek out the sources cited above; what is given here only indicates their general sense. If one relies on this text alone, the text itself being incomplete, it may not be entirely reliable as a guide — the wise, having properly grasped its sense, will apply it with skillful adaptability; and, in a place with no wise teacher at hand, even this alone is enough to serve as an emergency remedy.

Reading note: The five methods move from the most externally verifiable (breath, with an audible or visible technique) to the most purely internal (analytic contemplation, which needs no physical component at all), with visualization and mantra explicitly flagged as effective in principle but risky in practice for "people's spiritual faculties in the present age" — a candid admission that a technique's theoretical power doesn't guarantee it suits every practitioner or era. The treatise's own closing caveat is worth taking seriously on its own terms: it says outright that its account is incomplete and that a skilled teacher's live judgment matters more than the text — unusually explicit humility about the limits of a written medical-contemplative manual.

Ten Conditions for Effective Treatment 用心治病十法

Chinese Source

問曰：用心坐中治病，必有効不？答曰：若具十法，無有不益。十法者：一、信；二、用；三、勤；四、恒住緣中；五、別病因起；六者、方便；七、久行；八、知取捨；九、善將護；十、識遮障。何謂為信？謂信此法必能治病。何謂為用？謂隨時常用。何謂為勤？用之專精不息，取得汗為度。何謂為恒住緣中？謂細心念念，依法而不散亂。何謂別病因起？別病因起如上說。何謂為方便？謂吐納運心緣想，善巧成就，不失其宜。何謂為久行？謂若用之未即有益，不計日月，常習不廢。何謂知取捨？謂知益則勤，用損則捨之，漸轉心取治。何謂知將護？謂善識異緣犯觸。何謂遮障？謂得益不向外說，未損不疑謗。若依此十法所治，必定有効。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: is applying the mind while sitting to treat illness certain to be effective? Answer: if the ten conditions are all present, there is no case where it fails to benefit. The ten conditions are: faith; application; diligence; constantly dwelling within the object; distinguishing the illness's cause; skillful means; sustained practice over time; knowing what to take up and what to set aside; skillfully guarding and protecting oneself; and recognizing obstruction.

What is meant by faith? Believing that this method is truly able to treat the illness. What is meant by application? Applying it constantly, at every appropriate time. What is meant by diligence? Applying it with single-minded devotion, without pause, taking the appearance of sweat as the measure of its effect. What is meant by constantly dwelling within the object? A fine, attentive mind, moment to moment, relying on the method without becoming scattered. What is meant by distinguishing the illness's cause? Distinguishing its cause exactly as described above. What is meant by skillful means? Skillfully and successfully coordinating the breath, the movement of the mind, and visualization together, without losing what is proper to the moment. What is meant by sustained practice over time? Even if the method brings no immediate benefit, continuing to practice it without ever abandoning it, regardless of how many days or months pass. What is meant by knowing what to take up and what to set aside? Knowing that where it benefits, one should apply it diligently, and where it causes harm, one should set it aside, gradually redirecting the mind toward what actually treats the condition. What is meant by skillfully guarding and protecting oneself? Properly recognizing and avoiding whatever different conditions might cause a relapse. What is meant by recognizing obstruction? Not speaking of one's benefit to others, and not doubting or denouncing the method before any harm has actually occurred. If one relies on these ten conditions in treatment, there is certain to be an effect.

Reading note: The ten conditions read less like a meditation instruction and more like a general theory of what makes any sustained self-treatment actually work — faith and diligence at the start, accurate diagnosis and skillful coordination in the middle, and, at the end, two conditions about patience with slow results and quiet discretion about outcomes either way. The closing pair — not boasting when it works, not doubting or denouncing it before any harm has occurred — targets a specific failure mode: undermining a treatment through impatience or through talking about it prematurely, before it has had the chance to actually work. This closes the fourth of the internal expedient means' five layers.

Recognizing Demonic Affairs 明覺魔事

Chinese Source

次第五、明魔事者，魔羅，秦言殺者。奪行人功德之財，殺智慧命，故名魔羅。云何名魔事？如佛以功德智慧度脫眾生，入涅槃為事。魔亦如是，常以破壞眾生善根，令流轉生死為事。若能安心道門，道高則魔盛，故須善識魔事。今釋即為三：一、分別魔法不同；二、明魔事發相；三、明壞魔之法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the fifth topic, clarifying demonic affairs: Māra, in the Qin-dynasty translation, means "the killer." It seizes the wealth of the practitioner's merit and kills the very life of wisdom — hence it is named Māra. What is meant by "demonic affairs"? Just as the Buddha takes liberating sentient beings through merit and wisdom, entering nirvāṇa, as his own work, Māra is exactly the same: it constantly takes destroying sentient beings' wholesome roots and keeping them turning through birth-and-death as its own work. If one is able to settle the mind on the gate of the path, then, as the path grows exalted, Māra grows correspondingly active — hence one must properly recognize demonic affairs. Here, this is explained in three parts: distinguishing the different kinds of demonic dharma; clarifying the marks of demonic affairs arising; and clarifying the methods for defeating demons.

The Four Kinds of Demon 分別魔法不同

Chinese Source

第一、分別魔法不同，魔有四種：一者、煩惱魔；二者、陰入界魔；三者、死魔；四者、欲界天子魔。一、煩惱魔者，即是三毒、九十八使、取、有、流、扼、縛、蓋、纏、惱、結等，皆能破壞修道之事，如《摩訶衍論》偈說：
欲是汝初軍，憂愁為第二，
饑渴為第三，觸愛為第四，
睡眠第五軍，怖畏為第六，
疑悔為第七，瞋恚為第八，
利養虛稱九，自高蔑人十。
如是等軍眾，厭沒出家人。
我以禪智力，破汝此諸軍，
得成佛道已，度脫一切人。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, distinguishing the different kinds of demonic dharma: there are four kinds of demon — the demon of affliction; the demon of the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements; the demon of death; and the demon of the desire realm's Heavenly Son.

First, the demon of affliction: this is precisely the three poisons, the ninety-eight fetters, grasping, existence, the outflows, the yokes, the bonds, the hindrances, the entanglements, the vexations, the fetters, and the rest — all able to destroy the work of cultivating the path. As the Mahāyāna treatise says in verse:
"Desire is your first army, sorrow the second;
Hunger and thirst the third, contact and craving the fourth;
Sleep the fifth army, fear the sixth;
Doubt and regret the seventh, anger the eighth;
Profit and empty fame the ninth, self-exaltation and contempt for others the tenth.
Such armies as these overwhelm and submerge the one gone forth.
Through the power of dhyāna and wisdom, I shall break through these very armies of yours,
And, having accomplished the buddha-path, shall liberate every person."

二、陰界入魔，為五陰、十二入、十八界，一切名色繫縛眾生，陰覆行者清淨善根，功德智慧不得增長，故名為魔。所謂欲界陰入，乃至色、無色界陰入亦如是。行者若心不了受著，悉名為魔。若能不受不著，觀如虛空，不為覆障，即破魔業。三、死魔者，一切生死業報，輪轉不息，皆名為魔。復次，若行人欲發心修道，便得病命終，或為他害，不得修道，即為廢。今修習聖道，比至後世，因緣轉異，忘失本心，皆名魔事。復次，行者當修道時，慮死不活，便愛著其身而不修道，亦是死魔所攝。四、天子魔者，即是波旬。此魔是佛法怨讐，常恐行人出離其界，故令諸鬼神眷屬作種種惱亂，破壞行者善根，是為他化自在天子魔。

Second, the demon of the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements: the five aggregates, the twelve sense-bases, and the eighteen elements — every name and form binding sentient beings — obscure the practitioner's own pure wholesome roots, so that merit and wisdom fail to grow; hence they are named demons. This holds for the aggregates and sense-bases of the desire realm, and likewise for those of the form and formless realms as well. If the practitioner's mind fails to understand this and remains attached, all of it is named demonic; but if one is able to remain free of both reception and attachment, contemplating it as though empty space, it no longer serves as any obscuring obstruction, and the demon's own work is thereby broken through.

Third, the demon of death: every karmic result of birth-and-death, turning on without cease, is named demonic. Furthermore: if a practitioner, wishing to give rise to the resolve to cultivate the path, instead falls ill and dies, or is harmed by another, and so fails to cultivate the path — this, too, is an abandonment of practice. Even should one continue cultivating the noble path into a subsequent life, causes and conditions having shifted and changed, one's original resolve forgotten — all of this, too, is named demonic affairs. Furthermore: if the practitioner, at the time of cultivating the path, worries over death and clings to life, and so becomes attached to their own body instead of cultivating the path, this too is subsumed under the demon of death.

Fourth, the demon of the Heavenly Son: this is precisely Pāpīyas. This demon is the sworn enemy of the Buddhadharma, constantly fearing that the practitioner might escape from its own domain — hence it sends its various spirit-retinues to cause every kind of disturbance, destroying the practitioner's wholesome roots. This is the demon of the Heavenly Son of the Heaven of Free Enjoyment of Others' Emanations.

Reading note: The four classical demons scale outward from the most intimate to the most cosmic: affliction (one's own mental defilements), the aggregates (the very constituents of body and mind), death (the basic condition of embodied existence), and finally an actual external adversary, Pāpīyas — the same "killer" etymology already introduced in fascicle 3's note on distinguishing genuine wholesome marks from demonic ones. The Mahāyāna treatise's ten-army verse (desire, sorrow, hunger, craving, sleep, fear, doubt, anger, vanity, contempt) doubles as a fairly complete inventory of what actually derails a meditation practice, framed as literal combatants a practitioner must defeat rather than as abstract categories.

The Marks of the Four Demons Arising 明魔事發相

Chinese Source

第二、明四魔發相者：若煩惱魔，如前不善根性中三毒等分煩惱中廣說。若陰入界魔發相，如前不善及善根性中發種種色心境界說；若死魔發相，如前病患法中廣說。所以者何？病為死因。若鬼神魔者，今當分別說。鬼神魔有三種：一者、精媚；二者、垖惕鬼；三者、魔羅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the marks of the four demons arising: as for the demon of affliction, this is explained at length above, within the three poisons and the equal-portion affliction, in the discussion of unwholesome capacity. As for the marks of the demon of the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements arising, this is explained above, within the various states of form and mind arising, in the discussion of both unwholesome and wholesome capacity. As for the marks of the demon of death arising, this is explained at length above, within the dharma of illness — why is this? Because illness is the very cause of death.

As for the demon of spirits, this shall now be distinguished. Spirit-demons are of three kinds: spirit-glamours; mischievous spirits; and Māra proper.

精媚者，十二時獸變化，作種種形色，或作少男、少女、老宿之形，及可畏身相等非一，以惱行人。各當其時而來，善須別識。若多卯時來者，必是狐、兔、狢等，說其名字，精媚即散。餘十一時形相，類此可知。二、垖惕鬼者，亦作種種惱亂行人，或如蟲緣人頭面鑽刺熠熠，或擊攊人兩掖下，或乍抱持於人，或復言說音聲喧鬧，及作諸獸之形，異相非一，來惱行人者，應即覺知，一心閉眼，陰而罵之，作是言：「我今識汝。汝是此閻浮提中食火嗅香偷臘吉支，邪見、喜破戒種。我今持戒，終不畏汝。」若出家人，應誦戒序，若在家人，應誦三歸、五戒、菩薩十重四十八輕戒等，鬼便却行，匍匐而去。如是作種種留難相貌，及除却之法，並如禪經中廣說。

As for spirit-glamours: these are the twelve double-hour animals in transformed shape, taking on various forms and colors — perhaps the form of a young man, a young woman, or an elder, and other such fearsome bodily forms not of one kind only — in order to trouble the practitioner. Each comes at its own proper hour, and one must properly learn to distinguish them. If one comes for the most part during the hour of the rabbit, it must be a fox-spirit, a hare-spirit, a badger-spirit, or the like — speaking its name aloud causes the spirit-glamour to disperse at once. The forms belonging to the remaining eleven double-hours may be understood by this same pattern.

Second, as for mischievous spirits: these, too, cause every kind of disturbance to the practitioner — crawling like insects over a person's head and face, pricking and tingling; striking or tickling under both armpits; suddenly embracing a person; or making loud, clamorous sounds and speech, and taking on the forms of various beasts, in strange forms not of one kind only. Should such a spirit come to trouble the practitioner, one should recognize it at once, closing the eyes with a single-pointed mind, and silently rebuke it, saying: "I now recognize you. You are, within this Jambudvīpa, a fire-eating, fragrance-sniffing piśāca-thief-spirit, a seed of wrong view delighting in breaking the precepts. I now uphold the precepts, and shall never fear you." If one is ordained, one should recite the preamble to the precepts; if a layperson, one should recite the three refuges, the five precepts, or the bodhisattva's ten grave and forty-eight lesser precepts — and the spirit will then retreat, crawling away. Such various forms of hindrance as these, and the methods for dispelling them, are explained at length in the Chan Sūtra.

三、魔羅惱亂者，是魔多作三種相來破行人：一、作違情事，即是作可畏五塵；二、作順情事，即是作可愛五塵令人心著；三、作非違非順事，即是作平品五塵動亂行者。是故魔名殺者，復名華箭，亦並名五箭，射五情故。一情中有三種境，對情而惱行者，五情合有十五種境。

Third, as for the disturbance caused by Māra proper: this demon, for the most part, creates three kinds of appearance in order to destroy the practitioner. First, creating matters contrary to one's feelings — that is, creating fearsome objects among the five sense-objects. Second, creating matters in accord with one's feelings — that is, creating lovely objects among the five sense-objects, causing the mind to become attached. Third, creating matters neither contrary nor in accord — that is, creating neutral objects among the five sense-objects that nonetheless disturb the practitioner. Hence Māra is named "the killer," and is also called "the flower-arrow," and, further still, "the five arrows," since it shoots the five faculties of feeling. Within each single faculty there are three kinds of object, each troubling the practitioner in relation to that faculty — the five faculties together thus having fifteen kinds of object in all.

色中三者：一、順情色，或作父母、兄弟、諸佛形像、端正男女可愛之境，令人心著；二、色中違者，或作虎、狼、師子、羅剎之形，種種可畏像來怖行者；三、色中非違非順者，但作平品之形色，亦不令人生愛，亦不令人生怖，皆能動亂人心，令失禪定，故名為魔。餘諸情中，亦當如是分別，但約塵相有異。行者若不別諸邪偽，則為所壞，狂亂作罪，裸形無恥，起種種過，破他善事，毀損三寶，非可具說，或時得病致死，必須慎之，善加覺識。

Among the three within form: first, form in accord with one's feelings — perhaps taking the form of one's parents, siblings, or the images of various buddhas, or a lovely object of a handsome man or woman, causing the mind to become attached. Second, form contrary to one's feelings — perhaps taking the form of a tiger, a wolf, a lion, or a rākṣasa, various fearsome images coming to frighten the practitioner. Third, form neither contrary nor in accord — simply taking on neutral forms and colors, causing neither love nor fear to arise, yet still able to disturb the mind, causing the loss of dhyāna-concentration; hence it, too, is named demonic. The same distinctions should be understood to hold for every other faculty as well, differing only in terms of the particular sense-object involved. Should the practitioner fail to distinguish these various deviant illusions, one will be undone by them — thrown into deranged conduct and wrongdoing, going about naked and shameless, giving rise to every kind of fault, destroying others' wholesome undertakings, and damaging the Triple Gem — matters not possible to describe in full; at times, one may even fall ill and die from it. One must therefore be exceedingly cautious, applying careful recognition.

問曰：何故不約法塵對意根中論三種魔事？答曰：從多為論，一切魔事多從五情中入，故但說五情，細而論檢，意根中亦不無三種惱亂之事，類而可知。復次，諸大乘經中辨種種六塵中幻偽，對意根魔事起相，是中廣說，故《大品經》云：「如是等魔事魔罪不說不教，當知即是菩薩惡知識。」

Question: why is the discussion not framed in terms of the dharma-object paired with the mind-faculty, as a third kind of demonic affair? Answer: speaking in terms of what is most common, demonic affairs, for the most part, enter through the five faculties of feeling — hence only these five faculties are discussed here. Examined closely, however, the mind-faculty, too, is certainly not without its own three kinds of disturbance; this may be understood by the same pattern. Furthermore, the various Mahāyāna sūtras discuss, at length, the illusory falseness within the various six sense-objects, and the marks of demonic affairs arising in relation to the mind-faculty specifically — hence the Larger Sūtra says: "Such demonic affairs and demonic offenses as these, if not spoken of and not taught, should be known as the mark of an evil companion to the bodhisattva."

Reading note: The three-kind scheme applied to each of the five faculties (pleasant, frightening, neutral — fifteen states in all) is structurally identical to the fifteen wholesome marks and fifteen unwholesome marks already given earlier in this fascicle, but reorganized around a different axis: not what kind of affliction is present, but what emotional pull an object exerts, regardless of content. The insistence that even a neutral, unremarkable state "disturbs the mind" and therefore counts as demonic is the section's least intuitive but most practically important claim — demonic interference isn't defined by its content being frightening or alluring, but simply by whatever succeeds in breaking concentration.

Methods for Defeating Demons 明壞魔之法

Chinese Source

三、明破魔法者，當用三法除却魔罪：一者、了知所見聞覺知皆無所有，不受不著，亦不憂慼，亦不分別，彼即不現。二者、但反觀能見聞覺知之心，不見生處，何所惱亂？如是觀時，不受不分別，便自謝滅。三者、若作此觀，不即去者，但當正念，勿生懼想，不著軀命，正心不動，知魔界如即是佛界如。魔界如、佛界如，一如無二如，於魔界無所捨，於佛界無所取，即佛法現前，魔自退散。既不見去來，亦不憂喜，爾時豈為魔所惱？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the methods for defeating demons: one should employ three methods to remove demonic offense.

First, understanding clearly that everything seen, heard, felt, and known has, in truth, nothing to it at all — neither receiving it nor becoming attached to it, neither troubled by it nor discriminating it — and it will not manifest at all.

Second, simply turning the contemplation back upon the very mind that sees, hears, feels, and knows, and finding no place where it truly arises — from what, then, could any disturbance possibly come? Contemplating in this way, neither receiving nor discriminating it, it will then pass away and vanish of itself.

Third, if, having made this contemplation, it still fails to depart at once, one should simply hold to right mindfulness, giving rise to no fear, unattached even to one's own body and life, the mind kept correct and unmoving, knowing that the suchness of the demon's realm is precisely the very same suchness as the buddha's realm. The suchness of the demon's realm and the suchness of the buddha's realm are one and the same suchness, with no second suchness apart from it — within the demon's realm there is nothing to reject, and within the buddha's realm there is nothing to grasp; the Buddhadharma then manifests directly before one, and the demon retreats and scatters of itself. Once one no longer sees any coming or going, and is neither troubled nor delighted by it, how, then, could one possibly still be troubled by the demon at all?

復次，亦未曾見有人坐中見魔作虎來剩食此人，骨肉狼藉，正是怖人，令心驚畏耳，都無實事，當知虛誑。如是知已，心不驚怖。復作是念：「設令是實，我今身命為道故死，何足可懼？今我此身，隨汝分別，心如金剛，不可迴轉。」如是或一月、二月，乃至經年不去，亦當端心，正念堅固，莫懷憂懼，當誦大乘方等諸治魔呪，默念誦之，存心三寶。若出禪定，亦當誦呪自防，懺悔慚悚，及誦波羅提木叉戒，邪不干正，久久自滅。事理除魔，其法眾多，非可備說，行者善須識之，方便除滅。故初心行人欲學坐時，必須親近善知識者，為有如此等難。是魔入人心時，能令行人證諸禪定、三昧、智慧、神通、陀羅尼，何況不能作此小小境界？若欲知之，諸大乘經及《九十六種道經》中，亦少分分別。今略說此，為令行者深知此意，則不妄受諸境。

Furthermore: no one has ever actually been seen, while sitting, to have a demon truly appear as a tiger and devour them, flesh and bone scattered about — this is simply meant to frighten a person, causing the mind to startle and fear; there is, in truth, no real matter to it at all, and one should know it to be empty and false. Knowing this, the mind is no longer startled or afraid. One should further reflect: "Even should this be real, I have already given this body and life for the sake of the path — why, then, should it be feared at all? Let this body of mine now be however you would have it — my mind is like vajra, and cannot be turned back." Even should it persist in this way for a month, two months, or even a full year without departing, one should still keep the mind upright, right mindfulness held firm, harboring no worry or fear, reciting the various Mahāyāna Vaipulya mantras for treating demons, silently reciting them, keeping the mind fixed on the Triple Gem. Upon emerging from concentration, one should likewise recite mantras to protect oneself, feeling shame and remorse in repentance, and recite the prātimokṣa precepts as well — deviance cannot interfere with the correct, and, over time, it will vanish of itself.

The methods for removing demons, in terms of both phenomenal practice and underlying principle, are themselves manifold and cannot be given here in full; the practitioner must properly recognize them, and remove them through skillful means. Hence a beginning practitioner wishing to learn sitting meditation must draw close to a wise teacher, precisely because of difficulties such as these. When this demon enters into a person's mind, it is able to make the practitioner believe they have realized the various dhyāna-concentrations, samādhis, wisdom, supernormal powers, and dhāraṇīs — how much more, then, could it fail to produce these lesser, trivial states as well? Should one wish to know more of this, the various Mahāyāna sūtras and the Sūtra of the Ninety-Six Paths distinguish it in some small part as well. This is given here only in brief, so that the practitioner may deeply understand this point, and thereby not carelessly accept any such state at face value.

取要言之，若欲遣邪歸正，當觀諸法實相。是故《摩訶衍論》云：「除諸法實相，其餘一切皆是魔事。」故偈言：
若分別憶想，是即魔羅網。
不動不分別，是即為法印。
常念常空理，是人非行道，
不生不滅中，而作分別想。

To state the essential point: if one wishes to dispel the deviant and return to the correct, one should contemplate the true mark of all dharmas. Hence the Mahāyāna treatise says: "Apart from the true mark of all dharmas, everything else whatsoever is demonic affairs." Hence the verse says:
"If one discriminates and gives rise to deluded thought, this is precisely Māra's own net;
Unmoving and free of discrimination, this is precisely the Dharma-seal.
One who is forever mindful of the principle of emptiness, and yet
Still gives rise to discriminating thought within what neither arises nor ceases — this person is not truly walking the path."

復次，略明破魔義不同，如《摩訶衍》中說：「得菩薩道故，破煩惱魔；得法性身故，破陰界入魔；得菩薩道，得法性身故，破死魔；得不動三昧，一切法中自在無住故，破欲界他化自在天子魔。」若《大集經》，明得四念處即破四魔。此二說，名異意同。若《纓絡經》，明等覺如來三魔已過，唯有一品死魔在。若《法華》說，二乘之人但破三魔，餘有欲界天子魔所未能破。此則經論互說不同，悉有深意。若通明四魔，並至菩提方盡。

Furthermore: the sense in which demons are defeated differs somewhat depending on the source. As the Mahāyāna treatise says: "Through attaining the bodhisattva path, the demon of affliction is defeated; through attaining the dharma-nature body, the demon of the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements is defeated; through attaining both the bodhisattva path and the dharma-nature body, the demon of death is defeated; through attaining the unmoving samādhi, free and unattached within every dharma, the demon of the desire realm's Heavenly Son is defeated." The Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra clarifies that attaining the four foundations of mindfulness defeats all four demons at once — these two accounts differ in name but agree in sense. The Yingluo Sūtra clarifies that the Tathāgata of Sublime Enlightenment has already passed beyond three of the demons, with only a single remaining grade of the demon of death still present. The Lotus Sūtra says that followers of the two lesser vehicles defeat only three of the demons, the demon of the desire realm's Heavenly Son alone remaining undefeated by them. These sūtras and treatises thus differ from one another in what they say, each carrying its own profound intent. Speaking generally of all four demons together, they are, in every case, only fully exhausted upon reaching bodhi itself.

所以者何？如煩惱魔，無明細惑，佛菩提智之所能斷。陰界入魔，如告憍陳如：「色是無常，因滅是色，獲得常色。受、想、行、識亦復如是。」死魔，如前取《纓絡經》所說。欲界天子魔，坐道場時，方來與菩薩興大鬪戰。故知四魔皆至菩提究竟永盡。菩薩摩訶薩心廣大故，安住不動，修深禪定，從初發心乃至佛果，降伏四魔而作佛事，廣化眾生，心不退沒。《涅槃經》中說有八魔，《華嚴經》中說有十魔，善得其意，四魔攝盡，更無別法。諸經辨魔事眾多，略說不具足。

Why is this? The demon of affliction — the subtle delusion of ignorance — can only be severed by the Buddha's own bodhi-wisdom. The demon of the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements is as the Buddha told Kauṇḍinya: "Form is impermanent; through the cessation of this form, a permanent form is attained. Sensation, perception, formations, and consciousness are exactly the same." The demon of death is as already cited above from the Yingluo Sūtra. The demon of the desire realm's Heavenly Son comes to wage great battle against the bodhisattva only at the very moment of sitting at the seat of enlightenment itself. Hence it is known that all four demons are only ever brought to their final, eternal end upon reaching bodhi.

Because the mahāsattva bodhisattva's mind is vast and expansive, abiding unmoved, cultivating profound dhyāna-concentration from the very first arising of the resolve all the way through to the fruit of buddhahood, subduing the four demons while carrying out the Buddha's own work, broadly transforming sentient beings, the mind never regressing or sinking. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra speaks of eight demons, and the Avataṃsaka Sūtra speaks of ten demons; properly grasping their sense, all are fully encompassed within these same four demons, with no separate dharma apart from them. The various sūtras' discussions of demonic affairs are themselves manifold; this is only given here in brief, not in full.

Reading note: The three methods for defeating demons trace the exact same coarse-to-fine escalation this treatise has used repeatedly since fascicle 3: first a direct refusal to engage (neither receiving nor discriminating what appears), then turning analysis back on the perceiving mind itself, and, only if the first two fail, the "suchness of the demon's realm is precisely the suchness of the buddha's realm" — the identical move embodying-truth stopping and the vajra wheel already made, dissolving the demon/buddha distinction rather than defeating one side of it. The closing survey — different sūtras crediting different attainments with defeating different demons, none of them actually contradicting each other once "each carrying its own profound intent" is taken seriously — is a fitting close to the entire internal expedient means: even total victory over the four demons is deferred all the way to buddhahood itself, so a practitioner still deep in this fascicle's earlier layers should expect ongoing struggle, not a clean finish line.
