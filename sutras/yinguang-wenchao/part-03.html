Chapter 4 of 10 · 印光法師文鈔菁華錄

Discussing the Magnitude of Life and Death

Twenty-seven passages in three groups: warning that human life is impermanent, teaching exclusive reliance on the Buddha's own power rather than self-power alone, and — in its longest and most practical stretch — instructing exactly what to do at another person's deathbed. This chapter closes with Yinguang's own "Three Great Essentials at the End of Life," among the most widely circulated single pieces of Pure Land deathbed guidance in the modern Chinese tradition.

Status: Chapter 4 of 10 translated Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: 印光法師文鈔菁華錄, ed. Li Jingtong, 1952 Author: Yinguang (1862–1940), 13th patriarch of Pure Land

Group A
甲、警人命無常 · Warning That Human Life Is Impermanent
Entry 1
Chinese Source

光陰迅速，時序更遷。剎那剎那，一念不住。此殆造物出廣長舌，普為爾我一切衆生，說人命無常，榮華不久，急尋歸路，免受沈淪之無上妙法耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Time passes swiftly; the seasons turn ever onward. Moment by moment, not a single thought ever stays still. This is, in truth, nothing other than the Creator himself putting forth a broad, long tongue, universally proclaiming to you, to me, to every sentient being alike, that human life is impermanent, that glory and prosperity never last long — urging us to swiftly seek the path home, the unsurpassed, wondrous dharma by which we may avoid being swept under and lost.

示某比丘尼 · Instruction to a Bhikṣuṇī 正編

生死大事，須當預辦。若待臨行方修，恐被業力所奪。

Ru-Yi Translation

The great matter of birth-and-death must be prepared for in advance. Should one wait until the very moment of departure to begin cultivating, one risks being overwhelmed by the force of one's own karma.

復周羣錚書 · Reply to Zhou Qunzheng 正編

Entry 3
Chinese Source

古語云：聰明不能敵業，富貴豈免輪迴。生死到來，一無所靠。唯阿彌陀佛，能為恃怙。惜世人知者甚少，知而真信實念者更少也。

Ru-Yi Translation

An old saying goes: "Cleverness cannot withstand karma; wealth and rank can hardly escape transmigration." When birth-and-death finally arrives, there is nothing at all left to rely on — only Amitābha Buddha can serve as one's true refuge and support. It is a pity that so few people in the world know this, and fewer still, even knowing it, truly believe it and genuinely practice it.

復包右武書二 · Reply to Bao Youwu, Letter Two 正編

Entry 4
Chinese Source

求生西方者，不可怕死。若今日即死，即今日生西方。所謂朝聞道，夕死可矣。豈可今日要死，且不願死。既貪戀塵境，不能放下，便因貪成障。淨土之境不現，而隨業受生於善惡道中之境便現。境現，則隨業受生於善惡道中矣。往生西方，便成畫餅。故修西方人，今日死也好，再活一百二十歲死也好。一切任彼前業，不去妄生計校。倘信願真切，報終命盡，便即神超淨域，業謝塵勞。蓮開九品之花，佛授一生之記矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

One who seeks rebirth in the West must not fear death. Should one die today, then today one is reborn in the West — this is precisely what is meant by "hearing the path in the morning, one may die content that very evening." How, then, could one, facing death today, still refuse to accept it? Clinging to and craving after the objects of this defiled world, unable to let them go, one thereby turns that very craving into an obstruction. The scene of the Pure Land then fails to appear, and instead the scene of rebirth into some good or evil path, following one's own karma, appears in its place. Once that scene appears, one is reborn, following one's karma, into that very good or evil path — and rebirth in the West becomes nothing more than a painted cake, impossible to eat. And so, for one who cultivates for the West, dying today is fine, and living on for another hundred and twenty years before dying is equally fine — one simply lets one's own prior karma run its course, without indulging in reckless calculation about it. Should one's faith and vow be genuine and earnest, then, when one's karmic reward comes to its own end, one's spirit at once transcends into the Pure Land, one's karma dissolved and worldly toil laid to rest — the lotus opens into one of its nine grades of blossom, and the Buddha bestows his own prophecy of buddhahood.

復高邵麟書一 · Reply to Gao Shaolin, Letter One 正編

Entry 5
Chinese Source

大丈夫生於世間，事事無不豫為之計。唯有生死一事，反多置之不問。直待報終命盡，則隨業受報。不知此一念心識，又向何道中受生去也。人天是客居，三途是家鄉。三途一報百千劫，復生人天了無期。由是言之，則了生死之法，固不可不汲汲講求也。

Ru-Yi Translation

A truly great person, born into this world, plans in advance for every single matter without exception — and yet it is precisely this one matter, birth-and-death, that is, more often than not, left entirely unaddressed. One simply waits until one's own karmic reward comes to its end, and then receives whatever retribution follows from it, with no idea at all into which path this very single thought of consciousness will then be reborn. Rebirth among humans and gods is only a temporary lodging; the three lower paths are one's own true home. A single retribution in the three lower paths lasts a hundred thousand kalpas, and there is no telling when one might be reborn among humans or gods again. Considered from this, the method of being done with birth-and-death is certainly something that must be urgently sought out and thoroughly investigated.

淨土問辨序 · Preface to Questions and Debates on the Pure Land 續編

Entry 6
Chinese Source

佛法無一人不堪修，亦無一人不能修。但能念念知不修淨業生西方，則長劫輪迴，莫之能出。以茲自愍愍他，自傷傷他，大聲疾呼，俾近而家人，遠而世人，同修此道。其利益較之唯求自了者，何止天地懸隔也。

Ru-Yi Translation

There is no single person unfit to cultivate the Buddha-dharma, and no single person unable to cultivate it. One need only be mindful, thought after thought, that without cultivating pure karma and seeking rebirth in the West, one has no way at all to escape endless kalpas of transmigration. It is on account of this that, grieving for myself and grieving for others, wounded for myself and wounded for others, I raise my voice urgently, calling upon those near me, my own family, and those far from me, the people of the whole world, to cultivate this very path together. The benefit of this, compared with seeking only one's own private liberation, is nothing less than the difference between heaven and earth.

復駱季和書 · Reply to Luo Jihe 三編

Group B
乙、教專仗佛力 · Teaching Exclusive Reliance on Buddha-Power
Entry 1
Chinese Source

佛法，法門無量。無論大、小、權、實，一切法門，均須以戒、定、慧，斷貪、瞋、癡，令其淨盡無餘，方可了生脫死。此則難如登天，非吾輩具縛凡夫所能希冀。若以真信、切願、念佛，求生西方，又無論功夫淺深，功德大小，皆可仗佛慈力，往生西方。此如坐火輪船過海，但肯上船，即可到於彼岸，乃屬船力，非自己本事。信願念佛，求生西方，亦然。完全是佛力，不是自己道力。然一生西方，則生死已了，煩惱不生，已與在此地久用功夫，斷煩惱淨盡了生死者相同。故念佛決定要求生西方，切不可求來生人天福報。彼離信願以教人念佛求開悟之開示，切不可依。念佛之要，在於都攝六根。當念佛時，攝耳諦聽，即是攝六根之下手處。能志心諦聽，與不聽而散念，其功德大相懸殊。此法無論上中下根人皆可用，皆可得益。有利無弊，宜令一切人皆依此修。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha-dharma's own Dharma-gates are immeasurable in number. Regardless of whether great or small, provisional or real, every single Dharma-gate requires precept, concentration, and wisdom to sever greed, hatred, and delusion, purifying them entirely without remainder, before one can be done with birth-and-death — and this is as difficult as climbing to heaven, something we ourselves, ordinary beings fully bound by affliction, could never hope to attain. But should one, with genuine faith and an earnest vow, recollect the Buddha and seek rebirth in the West, then, regardless of how deep or shallow one's effort, how great or small one's merit, one may still, relying on the Buddha's own merciful power, be reborn in the West. This is like riding a steamship across the sea: one need only be willing to board the ship, and one reaches the farther shore — this is entirely the ship's own power, not one's own personal capability. Faith, vow, and buddha-recollection, seeking rebirth in the West, are exactly the same: entirely the Buddha's own power, not one's own power of the path. And yet, once one is reborn in the West, birth-and-death is already ended, affliction no longer arises — already the same as one who has long applied effort here in this world and severed affliction entirely, thereby being done with birth-and-death.

For this reason, buddha-recollection must be determined toward seeking rebirth in the West — one must never seek instead the blessed reward of a human or heavenly rebirth in a future life. As for any instruction that teaches people to recollect the Buddha merely in hope of awakening, apart from faith and vow, this should never be relied upon at all. The essential point of buddha-recollection lies in fully gathering in the six sense-faculties. While recollecting the Buddha, gathering in the ear to listen attentively is itself the starting point for gathering in all six sense-faculties. Listening attentively with a sincere heart, compared with reciting without listening at all, in a scattered manner — the merit of the two differs vastly. This method, regardless of upper, middle, or lower capacity, can be used by anyone, and everyone can obtain its benefit from it. It has benefit and no drawback at all — every person should be led to cultivate according to this very method.

與張靜江書 · Letter to Zhang Jingjiang 續編

Entry 2
Chinese Source

吾人在生死輪迴中，久經長劫，所造惡業，無量無邊。若仗自己修持之力，欲得滅盡煩惱惑業，以了生脫死，其難逾於登天。若能信佛所說之淨土法門，以真信切願，念阿彌陀佛名號，求生西方，無論業力大，業力小，皆可仗佛慈力，往生西方。譬如一顆沙子，入水即沈。縱有數千萬斤石，裝於大火輪船中，即可不沈，而運於他處，以隨意使用也。石喻眾生之業力深重，大火輪喻彌陀之慈力廣大。若不念佛，仗自己修持之力，欲了生死，須到業盡情空地位方可。否則縱令煩惱惑業，斷得只有一絲毫，亦不能了。喻如極小之沙子，亦必沈於水中，決不能自己出於水外。閣下但生信心，念佛求生西方，不可再起別種念頭。果能如是，壽未盡則速得痊癒，以專一志誠念佛功德，便能滅除宿世惡業，猶如杲日既出，霜雪即化。壽已盡，則即能往生，以心無異念，即得與佛感應道交，故蒙佛慈接引往生也。閣下若信此話得及，則生也得大利益，死也得大利益。

Ru-Yi Translation

We ourselves, revolving through birth-and-death across long kalpas, have created evil karma that is immeasurable and boundless. Should one rely on one's own power of cultivation alone, wishing to extinguish every affliction and karmic delusion entirely, in order to be done with birth-and-death, this is more difficult than climbing to heaven. But should one be able to believe in the Pure Land Dharma-gate the Buddha himself spoke of, with genuine faith and an earnest vow reciting Amitābha Buddha's own name, seeking rebirth in the West, then, regardless of whether one's own karmic force is great or small, one may still, relying on the Buddha's own merciful power, be reborn in the West. It is like a single grain of sand: dropped into water, it sinks at once. And yet, even several thousand tons of stone, loaded onto a great steamship, need not sink at all, but may instead be carried elsewhere and put to whatever use one wishes. The stone represents the deep, heavy weight of a sentient being's own karmic force; the great steamship represents the vast breadth of Amitābha's own merciful power. Should one not recollect the Buddha, but instead rely on one's own power of cultivation alone, wishing to be done with birth-and-death, one must reach the very state of karma exhausted and craving emptied before this is possible at all. Otherwise, even were affliction and karmic delusion severed down to the merest thread, one still could not be done with it — like even the smallest grain of sand, which must still sink beneath the water, utterly unable to raise itself back above the surface on its own.

You, sir, need only give rise to faith, recollecting the Buddha and seeking rebirth in the West, without allowing any other kind of thought to arise. Should you truly be able to do this, then, if your allotted lifespan has not yet run its course, you will swiftly recover — for the merit of single-minded, sincere buddha-recollection is able to dissolve away the evil karma of past lives, just as frost and snow melt away the instant the bright sun rises. And if your allotted lifespan has already come to its end, you will at once attain rebirth — for, your mind free of any other thought, you will thereby achieve response and resonance with the Buddha along the path, and so receive the Buddha's own merciful guidance to rebirth. Should you, sir, be able to believe these words fully, then you will obtain great benefit whether you live or whether you die.

復裘佩卿書二 · Reply to Qiu Peiqing, Letter Two 正編

Entry 3
Chinese Source

楞嚴一經，不知淨土者讀之，則為破淨土之元勳。知淨土者讀之，則為宏淨土之善導。何以言之。以自力悟道之難，淨土往生之易，十法界因果，一一分明。若不仗佛力，雖陰破一二，尚或著魔發狂，為地獄種子。而且二十四圓通之工夫，今人誰能修習。唯如子憶母之念佛，凡有心者，皆堪奉行。但得淨念相繼，自可親證三摩。知好歹者讀之，其肯唯主自力不仗佛力乎。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the Śūraṅgama Sūtra: read by one who does not understand the Pure Land, it becomes the very foremost weapon for demolishing the Pure Land; read by one who does understand the Pure Land, it becomes an excellent guide for propagating the Pure Land. What does this mean? Because the sūtra makes utterly clear, point by point, both the difficulty of awakening to the path through self-power alone and the ease of rebirth through the Pure Land, together with the causes and effects across all ten dharma-realms. Should one not rely on Buddha-power, even breaking through one or two of the aggregates in the dark may still lead to demonic possession and madness, becoming a very seed for hell. And as for the effort required by the twenty-four accounts of perfect penetration described there — who among people today could ever actually cultivate them? Only buddha-recollection, like a child recollecting its mother, is something every single person with a mind at all is capable of undertaking. One need only achieve pure thought following upon pure thought without interruption, and one may thereby personally realize samādhi for oneself. Should one who truly knows good from bad read this sūtra, would he still insist on relying on self-power alone, refusing to rely on Buddha-power at all?

復永嘉某居士書七 · Reply to a Layman of Yongjia, Letter Seven 正編

Entry 4
Chinese Source

念佛人有病，當一心待死。若世壽未盡，則能速癒，以全身放下念佛，最能消業，業消則病癒矣。若不放下，欲求好，倘不能好，則決定無由往生。以不願生故。此等道理不明白，尚能得仗佛慈力乎。

Ru-Yi Translation

A person who recollects the Buddha, should illness come upon him, ought to set his whole mind on awaiting death. Should his allotted worldly lifespan not yet be exhausted, he will thereby recover all the swifter, for setting down his whole body and mind and recollecting the Buddha is the most effective means of dissolving karma, and once karma dissolves, illness heals. But should he fail to let go, seeking only to recover instead, and should he then fail to recover after all, he will certainly have no way to attain rebirth — for he never truly wished to be reborn in the first place. Failing to understand a principle as basic as this, how could one still expect to rely on the Buddha's own merciful power at all?

復周孟由昆弟書 · Reply to the Brothers Zhou Mengyou 正編

Entry 5
Chinese Source

淨土法門，乃極難極易之法門。說其難，則大徹大悟，深入經藏者，尚不信。說其易，則愚夫愚婦，至誠懇切念，即能臨終現諸瑞相，往生西方。彼大徹大悟，深通經論者，猶不能望其肩背。良以一則棄佛力以專主自力。一則專仗佛力，而由佛力以引發自力。以佛力、法力、自心本具之力，三法契合，故得超凡入聖，了生脫死也。此法最要在信願。有信願，則決定肯認真修持。肯修持則即可得往生之益。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Pure Land Dharma-gate is a Dharma-gate that is, at once, the most difficult and the most easy of all. Speak of its difficulty, and even those of great, thorough awakening, who have gone deep into the scriptural canon, still fail to believe it. Speak of its ease, and even the simplest, most foolish man or woman, reciting with utmost sincerity and earnestness, is able, at the very moment of death, to manifest every auspicious sign and attain rebirth in the West — a rebirth those of great, thorough awakening, who deeply understand every sūtra and treatise, still cannot match even from behind. This is truly because the one relies exclusively on self-power alone, setting aside Buddha-power; while the other relies exclusively on Buddha-power, and, through Buddha-power, draws forth self-power in turn. Because Buddha-power, Dharma-power, and the power inherently possessed by one's own mind, these three dharmas, converge together, one is thereby able to transcend the ordinary and enter sagehood, being done with birth-and-death. The single most essential point of this method lies in faith and vow. Where there is faith and vow, one is certain to be willing to genuinely cultivate practice; and being willing to cultivate practice, one thereby obtains the benefit of rebirth.

復習懷辛書 · Reply to Xi Huaixin 續編

Chinese Source

佛祖出世，悉皆法隨機立。末世鈍根，當擇其契理而又契機者，專精致力。庶可仗佛慈力，橫超三界。於此一生，即了百千萬劫不易了之生死大事。

Ru-Yi Translation

Whenever a buddha or patriarch appears in the world, every dharma he establishes is always set up according to the capacity of those he teaches. Beings of dull faculty in this final age should choose the dharma that accords with both principle and capacity at once, and devote their focused, undivided effort to it — so that they may thereby rely on the Buddha's own merciful power, transcending the three realms directly, crosswise. Within this very single life, one may then be done with the great matter of birth-and-death — a matter that would otherwise remain unresolved across a hundred thousand upon ten thousand kalpas.

Group C
丙、示臨終切要 · Instructing the Essentials at the Moment of Death
Entry 1
Chinese Source

臨終一關，最為要緊。世有愚人，於父母眷屬臨終時，輒為悲痛哭泣，洗身換衣。只圖世人好看，不計貽害亡人。不念佛者，且置勿論。即志切往生，臨終遇此眷屬，多皆破壞正念，仍留此界。臨終助念，譬如怯夫上山，自力不足，幸有前牽後推左右扶掖之力，便可登峰造極。臨終正念昭彰，被魔眷愛情搬動等破壞者，譬如勇士上山，自力充足，而親友知識，各以己物，令其擔負。擔負過多，力竭身疲，望崖而退。此之得失，雖由他起，實屬自己往昔劫中，成全破壞人之善惡業力所致，凡修淨業者，當成全人之正念，及預為眷屬，示其利害。俾各知所重在神識得所，不在世情場面好看，庶可無虞矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

The single gate at the moment of death is the most crucial of all. There are foolish people in this world who, at the very moment their parents or family members are dying, immediately break into grieving cries, washing the body and changing its clothes — concerned only with how this looks to onlookers, giving no thought at all to the harm this does the dying person. As for those who do not recollect the Buddha at all, this need not even be discussed. But even for one whose intent to attain rebirth is earnest, should his own family behave this way at the moment of his death, in most cases his own correct mindfulness is thereby destroyed, and he remains trapped in this world after all. Deathbed assistance in recollection is like a timid man climbing a mountain, whose own strength alone is insufficient — but who, fortunate enough to have others pulling from ahead, pushing from behind, and supporting him on either side, is thereby able to reach the very summit. A dying person's own correct mindfulness, clear and manifest, yet destroyed by demonic relatives moved by attachment and affection, is like a valiant warrior climbing a mountain whose own strength is entirely sufficient — and yet his own friends and acquaintances, each handing him some further item of their own, cause him to carry too heavy a load, until his strength is exhausted and his body worn out, and he turns back within sight of the very summit. Whether this outcome is gain or loss, though set in motion by others, is in truth brought about by one's own good or evil karmic force, accumulated across past kalpas, in either supporting or undermining others in turn. Whoever cultivates pure karma should therefore support the correct mindfulness of others, and prepare their own family members in advance, showing them clearly what helps and what harms — so that everyone understands that what truly matters is where the dying person's own consciousness comes to rest, not how the outward scene appears to worldly eyes — and only then can one be free of concern in this matter.

陳了常往生發隱 · Revealing the Hidden Circumstances of Chen Liaochang's Rebirth 正編

Entry 2
Chinese Source

凡人有病，可以藥治者，亦不必決不用藥。不可以藥治者，雖仙丹亦無用處，況世間藥乎。無論能治不能治之病，皆宜服阿伽陀藥。此藥絕不誤人。服則或身或心，必即見效。然人生世間，無論久暫，終有一死，其死不足惜。其死而所歸之處，可不預為安頓乎。有力量者，自己預為安頓妥帖，則臨終固不須他人為之輔助。然能輔助，則更為得力。無力量者，當令家屬代為念佛，則必能提起正念，不致恩愛牽纏，仍被愛情所縛，住此莫出也。

Ru-Yi Translation

For any illness that can be treated with medicine, one need not resolutely refuse medicine altogether. For an illness that cannot be treated with medicine, even an immortal's own elixir would be of no use, how much less any ordinary worldly medicine? Regardless of whether an illness can or cannot be cured, one should, in every case, take the medicine of agada — this medicine never fails anyone; taking it, whether in body or in mind, its effect is certain to appear at once. Yet a person, born into this world, whether living long or briefly, must in the end come to death; death itself is not worth grieving over. But is the place to which one goes after death not something one ought to prepare for in advance? One with sufficient capacity, having already prepared this for himself thoroughly, need not rely on others' assistance at the moment of death at all — though should such assistance still be available, it will only prove all the more effective. One without such capacity should have his own family recollect the Buddha on his behalf, so that his own correct mindfulness may certainly be raised up, rather than being entangled in ties of affection, bound still by love, trapped here with no way out.

復黃涵之書一 · Reply to Huang Hanzhi, Letter One 正編

First: Skillfully Guiding and Comforting, So That Correct Faith Arises

Chinese Source

切勸病人，放下一切，一心念佛。如有應交代事，速令交代。交代後，便置之度外。即作我今將隨佛往生佛國，世間所有富樂眷屬、種種塵境，皆為障礙，致受禍害，以故不應生一念繫戀之心。須知自己一念真性，本無有死。所言死者，乃捨此身而又受別種之身耳。若不念佛，則隨善惡業力，復受生於善惡道中。若當臨命終時，一心念南無阿彌陀佛，以此志誠念佛之心，必定感佛大發慈悲，親垂接引，令得往生。且莫疑我係業力凡夫，何能以少時念佛，便可出離生死，往生西方。當知佛大慈悲，即十惡五逆之極重罪人，臨終地獄之相已現，若有善知識教以念佛，或念十聲，或止一聲，亦得蒙佛接引，往生西方。此種人念此幾句，尚得往生。又何得以業力重，念佛數少，而生疑乎。須知吾人本具真性，與佛無二。但以惑業深重，不得受用。今既歸命於佛，如子就父，乃是還我本有家鄉，豈是分外之事。又佛昔發願，若有衆生，聞我名號，志心信樂，乃至十念，若不生者，不取正覺。以故一切衆生，臨終發志誠心，念佛求生西方者，無一不垂慈接引也。千萬不可懷疑。懷疑即是自誤。其禍非小。况離此苦世界，生彼樂世界，是至極快意之事，當生歡喜心。千萬不可怕死，怕死則仍不能不死，反致了無生西之分矣。以自心與佛相違反故。佛雖具大慈悲，亦無奈不依佛教之衆生何。阿彌陀佛萬德洪名，如大冶洪鑪。吾人多生罪業，如空中片雪。業力凡夫，由念佛故，業便消滅。如片雪近於洪鑪，即便了不可得。又況業力既消，所有善根，自然增長殊勝。又何可疑其不得生，與佛不來接引乎。如此委曲宛轉開導安慰，病人自可生正信心。此係為病人所開導者。至於自己所應盡孝致誠者，亦唯在此。

Ru-Yi Translation

Earnestly urge the dying person to set everything down, and single-mindedly recollect the Buddha. Should there be any matter still requiring his attention, have him settle it swiftly; and once settled, let it be put entirely out of mind. He should hold the thought: "I am now about to follow the Buddha to rebirth in his own buddha-land; every worldly wealth, joy, family member, and every other defiled object is nothing but an obstruction, bringing about only misfortune — and so I should not give rise to even a single thought of attachment toward any of it." He must understand that his own single true nature, in itself, has never known death at all. What is called "death" is nothing more than setting down this present body to receive some other body in its place. Should he not recollect the Buddha, he will simply be reborn, according to his own good or evil karmic force, into some good or evil path once more. But should he, at the very moment of death, single-mindedly recite "Homage to Amitābha Buddha," this sincere, earnest mind of buddha-recollection will certainly move the Buddha to arouse his own great compassion, personally descending to guide him, so that rebirth is attained.

And let him not doubt: "I am but an ordinary being weighed down by karmic force — how could reciting the Buddha's name for so brief a time possibly free me from birth-and-death, securing rebirth in the West?" He should know that the Buddha's great compassion is such that even a person guilty of the ten evils and the five heinous crimes, the gravest offenses of all, with the very marks of hell already appearing before him at the moment of death, may still, should a good spiritual friend teach him to recollect the Buddha — whether ten recitations, or even a single recitation alone — be received and guided by the Buddha to rebirth in the West all the same. If even such a person as this, reciting only these few phrases, can still attain rebirth, how, then, could one doubt this on the grounds that one's own karmic force is heavy and one's own recitations too few?

One must understand that we ourselves are, at root, already endowed with the very same true nature as the Buddha — it is only because our own delusion and karma run so deep that we fail to make use of it. Now, taking refuge in the Buddha, like a child returning to its father, is nothing but a return to one's own original home — how could this ever be regarded as something extraneous or unearned? And moreover, the Buddha himself once made the vow: "Should any sentient being, hearing my name, hold it in mind with sincere faith and joy, even to as little as ten recollections, and still fail to be reborn, then may I myself not attain true, complete awakening." This is why every sentient being who, at the moment of death, arouses a sincere, earnest heart, recollecting the Buddha and seeking rebirth in the West, is, without a single exception, received and guided by his own merciful compassion. One must never, under any circumstance, harbor doubt about this — to harbor doubt is itself to do oneself harm, and the resulting misfortune is no small matter.

Moreover, to leave behind this world of suffering and be born into that world of joy is a matter of the utmost delight — one should give rise to a joyful heart over it. One must never, under any circumstance, fear death — for fearing death still does not prevent death from coming, and only results, on the contrary, in losing one's own share in rebirth in the West altogether, since one's own mind is thereby turned directly against the Buddha himself. However great the Buddha's own compassion, even he can do nothing for a sentient being who refuses to follow his teaching. Amitābha Buddha's own great name of ten thousand virtues is like a vast, blazing furnace; our own karmic offense, accumulated across many lifetimes, is like a single flake of snow adrift in the sky. An ordinary being weighed down by karmic force, through buddha-recollection alone, has his own karma dissolved away entirely — just as a single flake of snow, brought near a blazing furnace, vanishes at once beyond any trace. And once karmic force has thus dissolved away, every root of goodness one possesses naturally increases and flourishes all the more — how, then, could one still doubt that rebirth might fail, or that the Buddha might not come to guide one after all?

Comforting and guiding the dying person patiently and thoroughly in this way, he will naturally give rise to correct faith of his own. This is the manner of guidance to be given to the dying person himself. As for what one should oneself carry out, in fulfilling filial devotion with utmost sincerity, this too lies entirely in doing exactly this.

Second: Everyone Taking Turns Reciting to Assist Pure Recollection

Chinese Source

前已開導病人，令生正信。然彼病人，心力孱弱。勿道平素絕不念佛之人，不易相繼長念。即向來以念佛為事者，至此亦全仗他人相助，方能得力。以故家中眷屬，同應發孝順慈悲之心，為其助念佛號。若病尚未至將終，當分班念。應分三班，每班限定幾人。頭班出聲念，二三班默持。念一點鐘，二班接念，頭班三班默持。若有小事，當於默持時辦。值班時，斷斷不可走去。二班念畢，三班接念。終而復始。念一點鐘，歇兩點鐘。縱經晝夜，亦不甚辛苦。須知肯助人淨念往生，亦得人助念之報。且莫說是為父母盡孝應如是。即為平人，亦培自己福田，長自己善根，實為自利之道，不徒為人而已。成就一人往生淨土，即是成就一衆生作佛。此等功德，何可思議。三班相續，佛聲不斷。病人力能念，則隨之小聲念。不能念，則攝耳諦聽心無二念，自可與佛相應矣。念佛聲不可太高，高則傷氣，難以持久。亦不可太低，以致病人聽不明白。不可太快，亦不可太慢。太快，則病人不能隨，即聽亦難明了。太慢，則氣接不上，亦難得益。須不高不低，不緩不急，字字分明，句句清楚。令病者字字句句，入耳經心，斯易得力。念佛法器，唯用引磬。其他一切，概不宜用。引磬聲清，聽之令人心地清淨。木魚聲濁，故不宜用於臨終助念。又宜念四字佛號。初起時，念幾句六字。以後專念阿彌陀佛四字，不念南無。以字少易念，病人或隨之念，或攝心聽，皆省心力。家中眷屬如此念，外請善友亦如此念，人多人少均如此念。不可一起念，歇歇又念，致令病人，佛念間斷。若值飯時，當換班喫，勿斷佛聲。若病人將欲斷氣，宜三班同念，直至氣斷以後，又復分班念三點鐘，然後歇氣，以便料理安置等事。當念佛時，不得令親友來病人前問訊諭慰。既感情來看，當隨念佛若干時，是為真實情愛，有益於病人。若用世間俗情，直是推人下海。其情雖可感，其事甚可痛。全在主事者明道理，預令人說之。免致有礙面情，及貽害病人，由分心而不得往生耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Guidance has already been given to the dying person, so that correct faith arises within him. Yet the dying person's own strength of mind is frail. Never mind someone who has never ordinarily recollected the Buddha at all, who will find it difficult to sustain continuous recitation — even one who has long made buddha-recollection his own regular practice will, at this point, rely entirely on the assistance of others to find any real strength in it. This is precisely why one's own family, together, should arouse a heart of filial devotion and compassion, assisting him in reciting the Buddha's name.

Should the illness not yet have reached its very final stage, one should divide into shifts to recite. One should divide into three groups, each group limited to a set number of people. The first group recites aloud; the second and third groups hold the recitation silently. After reciting for one hour, the second group takes over the recitation aloud, while the first and third groups hold it silently. Should some small matter need attending to, it should be handled during one's own silent-holding turn. While on one's own active shift, one must never, under any circumstance, simply walk away. Once the second group finishes, the third group takes over in turn, and so it continues, cycling back to the beginning. Recite for one hour, rest for two — even carried on through an entire day and night, this need not become excessively tiring.

One must understand that being willing to help another person's pure recollection toward rebirth also earns, in return, the very same assistance from others when one's own time comes. And this is not merely something one should do for one's own parents out of filial devotion — even done for an ordinary stranger, it likewise cultivates one's own field of blessing and increases one's own roots of goodness; it is, in truth, a path of benefiting oneself as much as others, not merely a matter of helping someone else. To help a single person attain rebirth in the Pure Land is itself to help a single sentient being become a buddha — merit of this kind is truly inconceivable.

With the three groups continuing in succession, the sound of the Buddha's name never breaks off. Should the dying person still have strength to recite, let him follow along in a soft voice; should he no longer be able to recite, let him instead gather in his ear and listen attentively, his mind free of any other thought, and he will thereby naturally come into accord with the Buddha. The voice reciting the Buddha's name should not be too loud — too loud, and it strains the breath, making it hard to sustain; nor should it be too soft, lest the dying person fail to hear it clearly. It should not be too fast, nor too slow. Too fast, and the dying person cannot keep pace, finding it difficult to follow even by ear; too slow, and the breath cannot be sustained between phrases, likewise making it difficult to benefit from. It should be neither too loud nor too soft, neither too slow nor too fast, every single character distinct, every single phrase clear — so that each character, each phrase, enters the dying person's ear and settles into his own mind, and it thereby becomes easy for him to draw strength from it.

As for the ritual instrument to use while reciting: only the hand-bell should be used; every other instrument should, without exception, be avoided. The hand-bell's tone is clear, and hearing it brings a person's own mind to a state of purity; the wooden fish's tone is muddy, and so should not be used for deathbed assistance in recollection. One should also recite the four-character form of the Buddha's name. At the very start, one may recite a few phrases of the six-character form; thereafter, one should recite exclusively the four characters "Amitābha Buddha," without the "Namo" — since fewer characters are easier to recite, whether the dying person follows along himself or simply gathers his mind to listen, either way it conserves his own strength of mind.

Whether it is one's own family reciting in this manner, or good friends invited from outside doing the same, whether there are many people present or few, all should follow this same method. One must not recite all together for a while, then stop, then recite again, causing the dying person's own recollection of the Buddha to be interrupted. Should mealtime arrive, one should simply change shifts to eat, without ever letting the sound of the Buddha's name break off. Should the dying person be on the very point of drawing his last breath, all three groups should recite together at once, continuing straight through even after the breath has stopped, and then, resuming shifts, continue reciting for a further three hours before finally resting — so as to allow time for handling the arrangements that follow.

While reciting the Buddha's name, one must not allow friends or relatives to come before the dying person to offer greetings or words of comfort. Should someone, moved by genuine feeling, come to visit, he should be asked to join in reciting the Buddha's name for some period of time instead — this is true, genuine affection, and of real benefit to the dying person. But should one instead indulge in ordinary worldly sentiment, this is nothing less than pushing the dying person down into the sea. However moving such feeling may be, the matter itself is deeply painful to witness. The whole matter rests entirely on whoever is in charge understanding this principle clearly, and instructing everyone present of it in advance — so as to avoid any awkwardness over social propriety, and to avoid harming the dying person through the very distraction that would otherwise prevent his rebirth.

Third: Strictly Guarding Against Moving the Body or Weeping Aloud

Chinese Source

病人將終之時，正是凡聖人鬼分判之際。一髮千鈞，要緊之極。只可以佛號開導彼之神識。斷斷不可洗澡換衣，或移寢處。任彼如何坐臥，只可順彼之勢。不可稍有移動，亦不可對之生悲感相，或至哭泣。以此時身不自主，一動則手足身體，均受拗折扭挒之痛。痛則瞋心生，而佛念息。隨瞋心去，多墮毒類，可怖之至。若見悲痛哭泣，則情愛心生，佛念便息矣。隨情愛心去，以致生生世世，不得解脫。此時，所最得益者，莫過於一心念佛。所最貽害者，莫過於妄動哭泣。若或妄動哭泣，致生瞋恨及情愛心，則欲生西方，萬無有一矣。又人之將死，熱氣自下至上者，為超昇相。自上至下者，為墮落相。故有頂聖、眼天生，人心、餓鬼腹，畜生膝蓋離，地獄腳板出之說。然果大家至誠助念，自可直下往生西方。切不可屢屢探之，以致神識未離，因此或有刺激，心生煩痛，致不得往生。此之罪過，實為無量無邊。願諸親友，各各懇切念佛，不須探彼熱氣後冷於何處也。為人子者，於此留心，乃為真孝。若依世間種種俗情，即是不惜推親以下苦海。為邀一般無知無識者，羣相稱贊其能盡孝也。此孝與羅剎女之愛正同。經云：羅剎女食人。曰我愛汝，故食汝。彼無知之人之行孝也，令親失樂而得苦，豈不與羅剎女之愛人相同乎。吾作此語，非不近人情，欲人各於實際上講求，必期亡者往生，存者得福，以遂孝子賢孫親愛之一片血誠，不覺其言之有似激烈也。真愛親者，必能諒之。

Ru-Yi Translation

The moment a dying person is about to breathe his last is precisely the very juncture at which the ordinary being and the sage, the human and the ghost, are being decided between. Everything hangs on the slenderest thread — the stakes could not be higher. One should do nothing but use the Buddha's name to guide his own consciousness onward. One must never, under any circumstance, bathe him, change his clothes, or move him to a different resting place. Whatever position he happens to be sitting or lying in, one should simply accommodate it exactly as it is. There must not be the slightest movement of the body at all, nor should one display any sign of grief before him, let alone break into open weeping. This is because, at this moment, the body is no longer under his own control — any movement causes the limbs and body to suffer the pain of being wrenched and twisted. Pain gives rise to anger, and anger extinguishes the recollection of the Buddha; and following anger as one departs, one is, in most cases, reborn among venomous, malevolent beings — a truly terrifying prospect. Should he witness grief and open weeping, a mind of attachment and affection arises, and this too extinguishes the recollection of the Buddha; and following attachment and affection as one departs, one remains bound, life after life, unable ever to attain liberation.

At this moment, nothing benefits the dying person more than single-minded buddha-recollection; nothing harms him more than careless movement or open weeping. Should careless movement or open weeping occur, giving rise to anger, resentment, or attachment, then the hope of rebirth in the West becomes, without a single exception, entirely lost.

Moreover, when a person is dying, warmth leaving the body from the lower parts upward toward the head is a sign of ascent; warmth leaving from the head downward is a sign of descent. Hence the old saying: "the crown for a sage, the eyes for the heavenly realms, the heart for the human realm, the belly for hungry ghosts, the knees for animals, the soles of the feet for hell." Yet should everyone present recollect the Buddha with utmost sincerity, one may still attain rebirth in the West directly, regardless. One must never, under any circumstance, repeatedly check the body for warmth, since this may disturb his consciousness before it has fully departed, causing agitation and pain that prevent rebirth after all — an offense of truly immeasurable, boundless gravity. I ask every relative and friend present to earnestly recollect the Buddha together, without ever needing to check where the warmth has cooled first.

For a child of the deceased, taking care over this very matter is what constitutes true filial devotion. Should one instead follow every worldly custom and sentiment, this is nothing less than callously pushing one's own parent down into the sea of suffering, merely in order to win the praise of ignorant onlookers who declare, in a chorus, how filial one has been. This kind of "filial devotion" is exactly the same as the love of the rākṣasī demoness — a sūtra says: "The rākṣasī devours people, saying, 'I love you, and so I devour you.'" The filial devotion of such an ignorant person, causing his own parent to lose joy and receive suffering instead, is this not exactly the same as the rākṣasī's own love for the one she devours?

I do not make these statements because I lack any understanding of ordinary human feeling — I wish only for everyone to seek what is genuinely effective in actual practice, so that the departed truly attains rebirth and the living truly obtain blessing, thereby fulfilling the sincere, wholehearted devotion of a filial son or worthy grandson. I have not failed to notice that my own words here may seem rather forceful — but anyone who truly loves their own parent will certainly understand.

Translator's note

The "Three Great Essentials at the End of Life" (臨終三大要) is likely the single most widely circulated practical text Yinguang ever wrote — reprinted independently as a standalone pamphlet across Chinese Buddhist communities throughout the twentieth century and still distributed at hospice and deathbed-care trainings today. Its warmth-location folk saying ("the crown for a sage...") is a traditional Chinese Buddhist mnemonic correlating the last place bodily warmth leaves with the destination of rebirth; Yinguang cites it while explicitly warning against the very practice — repeatedly checking the body for warmth — that a literal-minded reading of the saying might otherwise encourage. The rākṣasī citation is a stock image from Buddhist literature for love that harms in the name of loving.

Chapter 4 Complete

This closes the fourth of ten chapters in the Wenchao Jinghualu: the urgency of impermanence, exclusive reliance on the Buddha's own power, and the full practical instruction for deathbed care that has shaped Chinese Pure Land hospice practice for a century. This completes the three chapters — Chapter 1 (讚淨土超勝), Chapter 2 (誡信願真切), and Chapter 4 (論生死事大) — selected for this collection so far.
