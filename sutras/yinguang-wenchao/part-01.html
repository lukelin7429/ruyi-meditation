Praising the Pure Land's Superiority

Eighteen passages, drawn from letters, prefaces, and treatises written across Master Yinguang's long teaching career, all making the same claim from different angles: that the Pure Land is not one practice among many but the destination every other school of Buddhism ultimately returns to. The chapter closes with a long historical account tracing Pure Land practice from Huiyuan's founding of the Lotus Society on Mount Lu in 402 CE down to Yinguang's own immediate predecessors.

大矣哉，淨土法門之為教也！是心作佛，是心是佛；直指人心者，猶當遜其奇特。即念念佛，即念成佛；歷劫修證者，益宜挹其高風。普被上中下根，統攝律教禪宗；如時雨之潤物，若大海之納川。偏圓頓漸一切法，無不從此法界流；大小權實一切行，無不還歸此法界。不斷惑業，得預補處；即此一生，圓滿菩提。九界衆生離是門，上不能圓成佛道；十方諸佛捨此法，下不能普利羣萌。是以華嚴海衆，盡遵十大願王；法華一稱，悉證諸法實相。最勝方便之行，馬鳴示于起信；易行疾至之道，龍樹闡於婆沙。釋迦後身之智者，說十疑論而專志西方；彌陀示現之永明，著四料簡而終身念佛。匯三乘五性，總證真常；導上聖下凡，同登彼岸。故得九界咸歸，十方共讚；千經倂闡，萬論均宣。誠可謂一代時教之極談，一乘無上之大教也。不植德本，歷劫難逢；既獲見聞，當勤修習！

Ru-Yi Translation

How great indeed is the teaching of the Pure Land Dharma-gate! "This very mind creates the Buddha, this very mind is the Buddha" — even those who point directly at the human mind must yield to its singular distinction. In thought after thought one recollects the Buddha, and in that very thought one becomes a buddha — even those who cultivate and realize across many kalpas should still draw on its lofty example. It universally covers the upper, middle, and lower faculties alike, and gathers in Vinaya, doctrinal, and Chan schools together — like seasonable rain moistening every living thing, like the great ocean receiving every river. Every dharma, one-sided or perfect, sudden or gradual, flows from nowhere but this Dharma-realm; every practice, great or small, provisional or real, returns to nowhere but this Dharma-realm. Without severing affliction or karma, one may still be admitted to the rank of one bound for buddhahood after a single further birth; in this very life, one may perfect bodhi. Were the beings of the nine dharma-realms to depart from this gate, above they could never perfect the buddha-path; were the buddhas of the ten directions to set aside this dharma, below they could never broadly benefit the multitude. This is why the ocean-vast assembly of the Avataṃsaka all follow the King of the Ten Great Vows, and why a single recitation at the Lotus assembly is already a realization of the true mark of every dharma. As the most excellent of skillful means, Aśvaghoṣa demonstrated it in his Awakening of Faith; as the swift and easy path, Nāgārjuna expounded it in the Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā. Zhiyi, said to be Śākyamuni come again, composed the Treatise on Ten Doubts with his whole intent set on the West; Yongming, a manifestation of Amitābha himself, wrote the Four Alternatives and recited the Buddha's name his whole life through. It gathers the three vehicles and the five natures, together realizing the true and constant; it guides the sages above and the ordinary below, together crossing to the farther shore. And so all nine dharma-realms return to it, and the ten directions join in praising it; a thousand sūtras expound it together, ten thousand treatises proclaim it as one. Truly, it may be called the ultimate word of an entire teaching career, the unsurpassed great teaching of the One Vehicle. Without having planted the roots of virtue, one could pass through kalpa after kalpa and never meet it; having now come to see and hear it, one should diligently cultivate it!

竊聞淨土者，乃究竟暢佛本懷之法也。高超一切禪教律，統攝一切禪教律。略言之，一言、一句、一偈、一書，可以包括無餘；廣說之，雖三藏十二部之玄言，五宗諸祖師之妙義，亦詮不盡。縱饒盡大地衆生，同成正覺，出廣長舌，以神通力、智慧力，塵說、剎說、熾然說、無間說，又豈能盡？良以淨土本不思議故也。試觀華嚴大經，王於三藏；末後一著，歸重願王。法華奧典，妙冠羣經；聞即往生，位齊等覺。則千經萬論，處處指歸者，有由來也。文殊發願，普賢勸進。如來授記於大集，謂末法中，非此莫度；龍樹簡示於婆沙，謂易行道，速出生死。則往聖前賢，人人趣向者，豈徒然哉？誠所謂一代時教，皆念佛法門之註腳也。不但此也，舉凡六根所對一切境界，所謂山河大地，明暗色空，見聞覺知，聲香味等，何一非闡揚淨土之文字耶？寒暑代謝，老病相摧，水旱兵疫，魔侶邪見，何一非提醒當人速求往生之警策耶？廣說其可盡乎？言一言統攝者，所謂淨也。淨極則光通，非至妙覺，此一言豈易承當？於六即佛頌研之可知也。一句者，信願行也。非信不足以啟願，非願不足以導行，非持名妙行，不足滿所願而證所信。淨土一切經論，皆發明此旨也。一偈者，讚佛偈也。舉正報以攝依果，言化主以包徒衆；雖只八句，淨土三經之大綱盡舉也。一書者，淨土十要也，字字皆末法之津梁，言言為蓮宗之寶鑑。痛哭流涕，剖心瀝血，稱性發揮，隨機指示；雖拯溺救焚，不能喻其痛切也。捨此則正信無由生，邪見無由殄也。

Ru-Yi Translation

I have heard it said that the Pure Land is the teaching that most fully and ultimately expresses the Buddha's own original intent — surpassing every school of Chan, doctrine, and Vinaya, while at the same time gathering every school of Chan, doctrine, and Vinaya within it. Stated briefly, a single word, a single phrase, a single verse, or a single book can already contain it entirely without remainder; stated at length, even the profound words of the Tripiṭaka's twelve divisions and the wondrous teachings of every patriarch of the five schools could not exhaust it. Even if every being on the great earth attained true awakening at once, and each, putting forth a broad and long tongue, spoke of it with the full power of spiritual attainment and wisdom — speaking of it in every mote of dust, every land, blazing forth without interruption — how could even this exhaust it? This is truly because the Pure Land is, at root, inconceivable. Consider the great Avataṃsaka Sūtra, king among the Tripiṭaka: its very final word returns weightily to the King of Vows. Consider the profound canon of the Lotus Sūtra, wondrous crown among all sūtras: a single hearing already secures rebirth, at a rank equal to that of equal enlightenment. That a thousand sūtras and ten thousand treatises, everywhere, point back to this — this has its reasons. Mañjuśrī made the vow; Samantabhadra urged it onward. The Tathāgata, in the Mahāsaṃnipāta Sūtra, gave the prediction that in the degenerate age nothing but this could liberate beings; Nāgārjuna, in the Daśabhūmika-vibhāṣā, distinguished it as the easy path, swiftly leading out of birth-and-death. That every sage and worthy of ages past turned toward it, one and all — was this truly for nothing? Truly, one may say that an entire teaching career is nothing but a footnote to the Dharma-gate of buddha-recollection.

Nor is this all. Every single object encountered by the six sense-faculties — mountains, rivers, the great earth; light and dark, form and emptiness; seeing, hearing, sensing, knowing; sound, fragrance, and flavor — which of these is not itself a text proclaiming the Pure Land? The turning of the seasons from cold to heat; old age and illness wearing one down; flood and drought, war and plague; demonic companions and wrong views — which of these is not itself a warning, urging a person to seek rebirth swiftly? How could one speak of it fully, however much one said? As for "a single word that contains it entirely": this word is "pure." When purity reaches its utmost, its light penetrates everywhere — short of wondrous enlightenment itself, how easily could one truly take up this single word? Study the verses on the six identities of buddhahood, and this will become clear. As for "a single phrase": this is faith, vow, and practice. Without faith, one cannot open up vow; without vow, one cannot guide practice; without the wondrous practice of holding the name, one cannot fulfill the vow one has made and verify the faith one holds. Every sūtra and treatise on the Pure Land expounds nothing but this single purport. As for "a single verse": this is the verse of praising the Buddha. Raising up the direct reward, it gathers in the dependent fruit as well; speaking of the teaching-lord, it takes in his disciples as well — though only eight lines long, it already raises up the whole outline of the three Pure Land sūtras. As for "a single book": this is the Ten Essentials of the Pure Land — every single character a bridge across the degenerate age, every single word a precious mirror for the Lotus tradition. Weeping aloud, pouring out heart's blood, expounding fully in accord with one's own nature, guiding according to capacity — even rescuing the drowning or the burning could not compare to its urgency. Setting this aside, correct faith has nowhere to arise from, and wrong views have nothing to dissolve them.

與悟開師書 · Letter to Master Wukai 正編

Translator's note

"The six identities of buddhahood" (六即佛) is the standard Tiantai scheme — identity in principle, name, contemplative practice, resemblance, a portion of the truth, and the ultimate — already established across this site's Tiantai translations, including the Jiaoguan Gangzong. Yinguang's own "single book," the Ten Essentials of the Pure Land (淨土十要), is Ouyi Zhixu's own anthology of ten foundational Pure Land texts — Ouyi's Amituo Yaojie, translated in full elsewhere on this site, is itself one of the ten.

衆生一念心性，與佛無二。雖在迷不覺，起惑造業，備作衆罪，其本具佛性，原無損失。譬如摩尼寶珠，墮于圊廁，直與糞穢，了無有異。愚人不知是寶，便與糞穢一目視之。智者知是無價妙寶，不以汙穢為嫌，必於廁中取出，用種種法，洗滌令潔，然後懸之高幢，即得放大光明，隨人所求，普雨衆寶。愚人由是，始知寶貴。大覺世尊，視諸衆生，亦復如是。縱昏迷倒惑，備作五逆十惡，永墮三途惡道之人，佛無一念棄捨之心；必伺其機緣，冥顯加被，與之說法，俾了幻妄之惑業，悟真常之佛性，以至於圓證無上菩提而後已。於罪大惡極之人尚如是，其罪業小者，其戒善具修、禪定力深者，亦無一不如是也。以凡在三界之中，雖有執身攝心、伏諸煩惑之人，而情種尚在，福報一盡，降生下界，遇境逢緣，猶復起惑造業；由業感苦，輪迴六道，了無已時。故法華經云：三界無安，猶如火宅；衆苦充滿，甚可怖畏！若非業盡情空，斷惑證真，則無出此三界之望。此則唯有淨土法門，但具真信切願，持佛名號，即可仗佛慈力，往生西方。既得往生，則入佛境界，同佛受用；凡情聖見，二皆不生。乃千穩萬當，萬不漏一之特別法門也。時當末法，捨此無術矣！

Ru-Yi Translation

The mind-nature of a single thought in every sentient being is no different from that of a buddha. Though lost in delusion and unawakened, giving rise to affliction and creating karma, committing every kind of offense, the buddha-nature originally possessed is never in the least diminished by it. It is like a wish-fulfilling jewel that has fallen into a latrine — mixed in with the filth, it appears no different from the filth itself. A foolish person, not knowing it is a jewel, regards it with the very same eye as the filth around it. A wise person, knowing it to be a priceless, wondrous treasure, does not shrink from the defilement, but retrieves it from the latrine, cleanses it with every means until it is spotless, and then hangs it high on a banner — whereupon it emits great light, and, according to whatever anyone seeks, rains down every kind of treasure. Only then does the foolish person come to know its worth. The Great Awakened World-Honored One regards every sentient being in exactly this way. Even one utterly confused and inverted, guilty of the five heinous crimes and the ten evils, forever fallen into the three lower paths — toward such a person, the Buddha never holds, for even a single thought, any mind of abandonment. He waits only for the right occasion and condition, blessing that being seen and unseen, teaching the Dharma to him, so that he may understand the illusory affliction and karma he carries and awaken to the true, constant buddha-nature — all the way until he has fully and perfectly realized unsurpassed bodhi. If this holds true even for a person of the gravest, most extreme evil, how much more so for one whose offenses are lighter, or one who has fully cultivated precepts and goodness and gone deep in the power of meditative concentration! For within the three realms, even those who discipline the body and gather in the mind, subduing every affliction, still retain the seed of craving — and once their store of blessings is exhausted, they descend again to a lower realm, and, meeting conditions and circumstances, once more give rise to affliction and create karma; and from karma, suffering is brought about, and they revolve through the six destinies without ever coming to an end. Hence the Lotus Sūtra says: "The three realms are without peace, like a burning house; every kind of suffering fills them, deeply to be feared!" Short of karma exhausted and craving emptied, affliction severed and the true realized, there is no hope of escaping these three realms. And so there is nothing but the Pure Land Dharma-gate: merely possess genuine faith and an earnest vow, hold fast to the Buddha's name, and one may thereby rely on the Buddha's own merciful power to be reborn in the West. Once reborn there, one enters the Buddha's own realm, sharing in the very same enjoyment as the Buddha — neither the views of the ordinary nor the views of the sage any longer arise. This is truly the special Dharma-gate, sound and secure in every one of its thousand and ten thousand particulars, in which not a single case ever fails. In this present age of the Dharma's decline, there is no other method but this!

傅大士傳錄序 · Preface to the Recorded Biography of Layman Fu 正編

念佛法門，其來尚矣。以吾人一念心性，猶如虛空，常恆不變；雖常不變，而復念念隨緣。不隨佛界之緣，便隨九界之緣；不隨三乘之緣，便隨六道之緣；不隨人天之緣，便隨三途之緣。由其緣之染淨不同，致其報之苦樂迥異；雖于本體，了無改變，而其相用，固已天淵懸殊矣。譬如虛空，日照則明，雲屯則暗。雖虛空之本體，不因雲日而為增減；而其顯現障蔽之相，固不可以同年而語也。如來以是義故，普令衆生緣念于佛。故曰：若衆生心，憶佛念佛，現前當來，必定見佛，去佛不遠。又曰：諸佛如來，是法界身，入一切衆生心想中。是故汝等心想佛時，此心即是三十二相，八十隨形好。是心作佛，是心是佛。諸佛正徧知海，從心想生。夫隨佛界之緣，則是心作佛，是心是佛矣；若隨衆生各界之緣，則是心作衆生，是心是衆生矣。了此而不念佛者，未之有也。念佛一法，乃以如來萬德洪名為緣。即此萬德洪名，乃如來果地所證之無上覺道。由其以果地覺，為因地心；故得因該果海，果徹因源。如染香人，身有香氣；如蜾蠃之祝螟蛉，久則化之。即生作佛，轉凡成聖；其功能力用，超過一代時教一切法門之上。以一切法門，皆仗自力，斷惑證真，方得了生脫死。念佛法門，自力、佛力，二皆具足，故得已斷惑業者，速證法身；具足惑業者，帶業往生。其法極其平常，雖愚夫愚婦，亦能得其利益；而復極其玄妙，縱等覺菩薩，不能出其範圍。故無一人不堪修，亦無一人不能修；下手易而成功高，用力少而得效速。實為如來一代時教中之特別法門，固不可以通途教理而為論判也。末法衆生，福薄慧淺，障厚業深；不修此法，欲仗自力斷惑證真，以了生死，則萬難萬難！

Ru-Yi Translation

The Dharma-gate of buddha-recollection has a lineage stretching far back indeed. The nature of our own single thought of mind is like empty space, constant and unchanging; yet, though constant and unchanging, it also follows conditions, thought after thought. If it does not follow the conditions of the buddha-realm, it follows the conditions of the nine other realms; if it does not follow the conditions of the three vehicles, it follows the conditions of the six destinies; if it does not follow the conditions of humans and gods, it follows the conditions of the three lower paths. Because the conditions followed differ in their defilement or purity, the reward brought about differs vastly in its suffering or joy — though, in its essential substance, nothing at all has changed, in its manifested marks and functions, the difference is as wide as heaven from earth. It is like empty space itself: illuminated by the sun, it grows bright; massed with cloud, it grows dark. Though the essential substance of empty space is neither increased nor diminished by cloud or sun, the manifested marks of brightness or obstruction cannot be spoken of as though they were one and the same. It is for this very reason that the Tathāgata leads all beings universally to take the Buddha as their object of recollection. Hence it is said: "If the mind of a sentient being recollects the Buddha and holds the Buddha in mind, then, now or in time to come, that being will certainly behold the Buddha, and is not far from the Buddha." And again: "Every Tathāgata is a body of the Dharma-realm, entering into the mind and thought of every sentient being. Therefore, when your own mind thinks of the Buddha, this very mind is already the thirty-two marks and the eighty subsidiary signs of physical excellence. This very mind creates the Buddha; this very mind is the Buddha. The ocean of perfect, universal knowledge of every buddha arises from the mind's own thought." Now, when one follows the conditions of the buddha-realm, then this very mind creates the Buddha, this very mind is the Buddha; but when one follows the conditions of each of the various realms of sentient beings, then this very mind creates a sentient being, this very mind is a sentient being. To understand this and yet not recollect the Buddha — there has never been such a case.

The single method of buddha-recollection takes as its object of recollection the Tathāgata's own great name of ten thousand virtues. This very great name of ten thousand virtues is precisely the unsurpassed path of awakening the Tathāgata realized upon the ground of the fruit. Because it takes the awakening of the fruit-ground as the very mind of the causal-ground, the cause already encompasses the ocean of the fruit, and the fruit already penetrates through to the source of the cause. It is like a person steeped in incense, whose very body carries its fragrance; like the sphex wasp's chant to the silkworm-moth larva, which in time transforms it into its own kind. In this very life one becomes a buddha, turning the ordinary into the sacred — its power, capacity, and function surpass every other Dharma-gate across an entire teaching career. For every other Dharma-gate relies entirely on one's own power, severing affliction and realizing the true, before one can escape birth and death. The Dharma-gate of buddha-recollection alone is fully endowed with both self-power and Buddha-power together — hence one who has already severed affliction and karma swiftly realizes the Dharma-body, while one still fully possessed of affliction and karma is reborn there carrying that karma along. This method is utterly ordinary — even the most foolish man or woman can obtain its benefit — and yet it is, at the very same time, utterly profound and subtle — even a bodhisattva of equal enlightenment cannot step outside its scope. Hence there is no one unfit to cultivate it, and no one unable to cultivate it; it is easy to take up as a starting point, yet its accomplishment is lofty; it requires little effort, yet its effect comes swiftly. It is truly the special Dharma-gate within the Tathāgata's entire teaching career, one that certainly cannot be judged by the ordinary standards of doctrine. Beings of the degenerate age, with thin blessings and shallow wisdom, thick obstruction and deep karma — should they fail to cultivate this dharma, and instead wish to rely on their own power alone to sever affliction and realize the true, in order to be done with birth and death, this would be exceedingly, exceedingly difficult!

棲真常住長年念佛序 · Preface for the Perpetual Buddha-Recollection Endowment at Qizhen Monastery 正編

Translator's note

The sphex wasp's chant to the silkworm-moth larva (蜾蠃之祝螟蛉) is a classical Chinese image, drawn from the Book of Odes and long used in Buddhist writing: an old folk belief held that the solitary wasp captured a caterpillar, chanted "become like me, become like me" over it, and thereby transformed it into its own offspring. Yinguang and other writers in this chapter use it as an analogy for how sustained recollection of the Buddha's name gradually transforms the practitioner into the Buddha's own likeness.

大覺世尊，愍諸衆生，迷背自心，輪迴六道，久經長劫，莫之能出；由是興無緣慈，運同體悲，示生世間，成等正覺；隨順機宜，廣說諸法。括舉大綱，凡有五宗。五宗維何？曰律、曰教、曰禪、曰密、曰淨。律者佛身，教者佛語，禪者佛心。佛之所以為佛，唯此三法；佛之所以度生，亦唯此三法。衆生果能依佛之律教禪以修持，則即衆生之三業，轉而為諸佛之三業；三業既轉，則煩惱即菩提，生死即涅槃矣。又恐宿業障重，或不易轉，則用陀羅尼三密加持之力，以熏陶之；若蜾蠃之祝螟蛉，曰似我似我，七日而變成蜾蠃矣。又恐根器或劣，未得解脫，而再一受生，難免迷失，於是特開信願念佛，求生淨土一門，俾若聖若凡，同於現生，往生西方；聖則速證無上菩提，凡則永出生死繫縛。以其仗佛慈力，故其功德利益，不可思議也。須知律為教禪密淨之基址，若不嚴持禁戒，則教禪密淨之真益莫得；如修萬丈高樓，地基不固，則未成即壞。淨為律教禪密之歸宿，如百川萬流，悉歸大海；以淨土法門，乃十方三世諸佛上成佛道，下化衆生，成始成終之法門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Great Awakened World-Honored One, in compassion for every sentient being lost in delusion, turning away from their own mind, revolving through the six destinies across kalpa after long kalpa, unable to escape — thereupon aroused a compassion that needs no cause and a kindness born of shared essence, manifesting his birth in the world and attaining perfect, complete awakening; and, according with capacity and occasion, broadly expounded every dharma. Gathering up the great outline, there are, in all, five schools. What are the five schools? Vinaya, doctrine, Chan, esoteric practice, and Pure Land. Vinaya is the Buddha's body; doctrine is the Buddha's word; Chan is the Buddha's mind. That by which the Buddha is the Buddha is nothing but these three dharmas; that by which the Buddha liberates beings is likewise nothing but these three dharmas. Should any good son or good daughter wish to swiftly attain buddhahood, none surpasses buddha-recollection — through it, one can swiftly realize unsurpassed bodhi." Having spoken, the two great sages then recited verses to one another in turn. Hearing this, the Master leapt for joy, and the net of his every doubt was entirely dissolved.

This is the very account of Master Fazhao personally arriving at the sacred Bamboo Grove Monastery and receiving the instruction of these two great sages. Mount Wutai is the very site where Mañjuśrī manifests himself for the sake of converting beings; Mañjuśrī is himself the teacher of seven buddhas. He himself said: "In ages past, it was through contemplating the Buddha, through recollecting the Buddha, that I attained the wisdom of all modes." From this, every dharma — the perfection of wisdom, the most profound meditative concentration, and even every buddha himself — arises from nothing but buddha-recollection. If even the buddhas of the past arose through buddha-recollection, how much more so, then, should beings of the degenerate age — heavy in karma and light in blessing, deep in obstruction and shallow in wisdom — refrain from looking down upon buddha-recollection and refusing to cultivate it, wishing instead to leap directly into the Tathāgata's own ground in a single bound, without realizing that even following in the footsteps of the Fifth Patriarch Jie and Caotang Qing is itself something they cannot attain?
