The Opening and the General Explanation of the Name

The Profound Meaning of Guanyin is a short work in two fascicles, attributed to Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597) and recorded by his disciple Guanding (灌頂, 561–632), that explains the Lotus Sūtra's chapter on Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate in the same five sections Zhiyi used for the whole sūtra: the name, the essence, the cardinal doctrine, the function, and the characteristics of the teachings. (Modern scholars have questioned how much of it is Zhiyi's own; the traditional attribution is kept here.) This first part covers the opening and the first half of the general explanation of the name. The two words of the chapter title — "Avalokiteśvara" (a person) and "Universal Gate" (a dharma) — are unfolded through ten pairs: person and dharma, loving-kindness and compassion, merit and wisdom, the true and the responsive, the medicine-tree king and the wish-fulfilling jewel king, hidden and manifest benefit, the provisional and the real, origin and trace, the conditioning and the revealing causes, and wisdom and severance. The text first sets out the order of the ten, then shows how far each teaching period reaches through them, and then explains each pair through the four teachings, ending each time with how the two questions and answers of the sūtra chapter correspond to the pair.

Title and Opening

Chinese Source

觀音玄義卷上

Ru-Yi Translation

The Profound Meaning of Guanyin, Fascicle One

Chinese Source

隋天台智者大師說　門人灌頂記

Ru-Yi Translation

Spoken by the Great Master Zhizhe of Tiantai, of the Sui dynasty; recorded by his disciple Guanding.

Chinese Source

夫法界圓融，像無所像；真如清淨，化無所化。雖像無所像，無所而不像；化無所化，無所而不化。故無在、無不在，化應九道之身，處有不永寂，入不二之旨，

Ru-Yi Translation

The dharma-realm is perfectly merged: it images, yet there is nothing imaged. True suchness is pure: it transforms, yet there is nothing transformed. Though it images with nothing imaged, there is nothing it does not image; though it transforms with nothing transformed, there is nothing it does not transform. So it is nowhere and nowhere absent; it transforms in response into bodies in the nine destinies; it dwells in existence without being forever still; it enters the purport of nonduality.

Chinese Source

是以三業致請蒙脫苦涯，四弘為誓使霑上樂，故娑婆世界受無畏之名，寶藏佛所稟觀音之目，已成種覺，號正法明，次當補處，稱為普光功德。其本迹若此，寧可測知？方便隨緣，趣舉一名耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

That is why, when beings call on it with the three kinds of action, they are freed from the shore of suffering; and with the four universal vows as its pledge, it lets them be steeped in the highest bliss. So in the Sahā world it receives the name "Bestower of Fearlessness"; in the presence of the Buddha Jewel Treasury it received the name "Avalokiteśvara"; it has already attained the awakening of all modes, and is named "Bright True Dharma"; next it will fill the [Buddha's] place, and will be called "Universal Light Merit." Its origin and trace being like this, how could they be fathomed? As a skillful means according with conditions, one name is simply taken up.

Chinese Source

今言觀世音者，西土正音名阿耶婆婁吉低輸，此言觀世音。能、所圓融，有、無兼暢，照窮正性，察其本末，故稱觀也。世音者，是所觀之境也。萬像流動，隔別不同，類音殊唱，俱蒙離苦，菩薩弘慈，一時普救，皆令解脫，故曰觀世音。此即境智雙舉，能所合標。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "Avalokiteśvara": in the correct pronunciation of the Western Land the name is Ayebaloujidishu, which here means "contemplating the sounds of the world." Subject and object perfectly merged, existence and nonexistence both penetrated, illuminating the true nature to the end and examining its root and branch — hence it is called "contemplating." "The sounds of the world" is the object contemplated. The myriad forms flow and move, separate and unlike; each kind of sound calls out differently, yet all receive freedom from suffering; the bodhisattva's vast loving-kindness saves them all at once and brings all to liberation — hence "contemplating the sounds of the world." This raises object and wisdom together, and names subject and object jointly.

Chinese Source

經者由義，文理表發，織成行者之心，故曰經。普門者，普是遍義，門曰能通，用一實相開十普門，無所障閡故稱普門。品者類也，義類相從，故名為品也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Sūtra" means "proceeding from": its words and principles display and bring forth, weaving the practitioner's mind; hence it is called a sūtra. As for "Universal Gate": "universal" means pervasive, and "gate" means what gives passage. Using the one true mark to open ten universal gates, without obstruction — hence "Universal Gate." "Chapter" means a class: meanings of a kind follow one another; hence it is called a chapter.

The Five Sections and the Ten Meanings

Chinese Source

大部既有五章明義，今品例為此釋。五意者：一釋名、二出體、三明宗、四辯用、五教相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since the great work explains the meaning in five sections, this chapter is explained by analogy. The five are: first, Explaining the Name; second, Bringing Out the Essence; third, Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine; fourth, Discussing the Function; fifth, the Characteristics of the Teachings.

Chinese Source

釋名為二：一通釋、二別釋。通者，人法合明。別者，人法各辯。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Name has two parts: first, the general explanation; second, the specific explanation. "General" means explaining person and dharma together; "specific" means discussing person and dharma each separately.

Chinese Source

何故爾？緣有利鈍，說有廣略。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why so? Because conditions are sharp or dull, the explanation is extensive or brief.

Chinese Source

今就通釋，為四：一列名、二次第、三解釋、四料簡。一列名者，十義以為通釋。所以者何？至理清淨，無名、無相，非法、非人，過諸數量，非一、二、三，但妙理虛通，無名相中假名相說，故立無名之名，假稱人法，雖非數量，亦論數量。

Ru-Yi Translation

The general explanation has four parts: first, listing the names; second, the sequence; third, the explanation; fourth, resolving doubts. First, listing the names: ten meanings serve as the general explanation. Why? The ultimate principle is pure, without name or mark, neither dharma nor person, beyond all number and measure, neither one, two, nor three. Yet the wondrous principle is open and passable, and within what has no name or mark, names and marks are provisionally used to speak of it. So a name is set up for the nameless, and "person" and "dharma" are provisionally spoken of; though it is not number or measure, number and measure are discussed.

Chinese Source

故《大論》云「般若是一法，佛說種種名，隨諸眾生類，為之立異字。」今處中說，略用十義以釋通意也。十義者，一人法、二慈悲、三福慧、四真應、五藥珠、六冥顯、七權實、八本迹、九緣了、十智斷。

Ru-Yi Translation

So the Great Treatise says: "Prajñā is a single dharma; the Buddha speaks of it by many names; according to the kinds of beings, he sets up different words for it." Here, speaking from the middle, ten meanings are used briefly to explain the general sense. The ten meanings are: first, person and dharma; second, loving-kindness and compassion; third, merit and wisdom; fourth, the true and the responsive; fifth, the medicine [tree] and the jewel; sixth, hidden and manifest; seventh, the provisional and the real; eighth, origin and trace; ninth, the conditioning and the revealing; tenth, wisdom and severance.

The Sequence of the Ten Meanings: In Terms of Contemplation

Chinese Source

第二次第者，此有兩意：一約觀明次第、二約教明次第。約觀則總，初、中、後心因圓果滿；約教則該，括漸、頓，小、大諸經。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the sequence. This has two aspects: first, explaining the sequence in terms of contemplation; second, explaining the sequence in terms of the teachings. In terms of contemplation, it is comprehensive: the beginning, middle, and final mind, the cause complete and the fruit full. In terms of the teachings, it is inclusive: it embraces the gradual and the sudden, the lesser and the great sūtras.

Chinese Source

約觀以人法為初者，欲明觀行，必有其人，人必秉法。譬如人受一期果報，攬陰成人，雖具無量德行，必先標名字，故以人法居初，意亦例此。人法居九義之初可爾，何意乘以人法為次耶？此須據經，經云「以是因緣名觀世音」，即前辯人，後云「方便之力，普門示現」，即却論於法，人能秉法，故言人法也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In terms of contemplation, person and dharma come first because, to explain contemplative practice, there must be a person, and a person must hold a dharma. It is like a person receiving the retribution of one lifetime, taking up the aggregates to become a person: though possessing countless virtues and practices, he or she must first be marked by a name. So person and dharma are placed first, and the meaning is by analogy with this. That person and dharma come before the other nine meanings is acceptable; but why is it person first and dharma next? Here one must rely on the sūtra. The sūtra says, "For these causes and conditions he is named Avalokiteśvara" — this first discusses the person; later it says, "the power of skillful means, the manifestation of the Universal Gate" — this then discusses the dharma. A person can hold a dharma; hence "person and dharma."

Chinese Source

二次慈悲者，良由觀音之人觀於實相普門之法，達於非人、非法實相之理，一切眾生亦復如是，故《華嚴》云「心、佛及眾生，是三無差別。」此理圓足，無有缺減，云何眾生理具情迷，顛倒苦惱？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second comes loving-kindness and compassion. It is truly because the person Avalokiteśvara contemplates the dharma of the true mark, the Universal Gate, and penetrates the principle of the true mark that is neither person nor dharma — and all beings are likewise. So the Avataṃsaka says: "Mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without distinction." This principle is perfect and complete, lacking nothing. How is it that beings, though containing it in principle, are deluded in feeling, inverted, suffering, and distressed?

Chinese Source

既觀是已，即起慈悲，誓拔苦與樂，是故明慈悲也。復次，若就言說為便，初慈後悲，亦是就菩薩本懷，欲大慈與樂，既不得樂，次大悲拔苦，故初慈後悲。若從用次第者，初以大悲拔苦，後方以大慈與樂，又就行者先脫苦、後蒙樂，故先悲後慈。今從前義次第也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having contemplated this, one gives rise to loving-kindness and compassion, vowing to remove suffering and give happiness; that is why loving-kindness and compassion are explained. Further, for convenience of speech, loving-kindness comes first and compassion after; and it is also in keeping with the bodhisattva's original wish: wishing with great loving-kindness to give happiness, and finding that beings cannot obtain happiness, he next with great compassion removes suffering; so loving-kindness first and compassion after. If one follows the sequence of function, one first removes suffering with great compassion and only then gives happiness with great loving-kindness; and from the practitioner's side, one is first freed from suffering and then receives happiness; so compassion first and loving-kindness after. Here the sequence follows the former meaning.

Chinese Source

三福慧者，初則人法相成，此據其信；次則慈悲與拔者，此明其願。欲滿此願必須修行，修行不出福、慧，慧即般若、福即五度，互相資導，以行順願，事理圓足。若智慧增明，則大悲誓滿，拔苦義成；若福德深厚，則大慈誓滿，與樂義成。故福慧居三也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, merit and wisdom. First, person and dharma accomplish each other — this concerns faith. Next, loving-kindness and compassion give and remove — this explains the vow. To fulfill this vow, one must cultivate practice, and practice does not go beyond merit and wisdom. Wisdom is prajñā; merit is the [other] five perfections. They support and guide each other; practice accords with the vow, and event and principle are perfect and complete. If wisdom grows bright, the vow of great compassion is fulfilled, and the meaning of removing suffering is accomplished; if merit is deep and thick, the vow of great loving-kindness is fulfilled, and the meaning of giving happiness is accomplished. So merit and wisdom are placed third.

Chinese Source

復次，言說為便，先福後慧；若化他本意，先欲實慧利益，如其不堪，方示福德。又，資故先福、導故先慧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, for convenience of speech, merit comes first and wisdom after. But in the original intent of transforming others, one first wishes to benefit them with real wisdom, and only if they cannot bear it does one show merit. Again, as support, merit comes first; as guide, wisdom comes first.

Chinese Source

四真應者，若智慧轉明則契於法性，法性即實相，名為法身。法身既顯，能從真起應，真顯應起，只由福慧開發，故次第四也。又，若就方便化物，先用應、後用真。今從前義為次第也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the true and the responsive. When wisdom grows ever brighter, it accords with the Dharma-nature; the Dharma-nature is the true mark, and is called the Dharma-body. Once the Dharma-body is revealed, one can give rise to response from the true. That the true is revealed and the response arises is only because merit and wisdom have opened out; so this comes fourth. Again, in terms of transforming beings by skillful means, the responsive is used first and the true after. Here the sequence follows the former meaning.

Chinese Source

五明藥珠二身者，先明真應，直語證得，未涉利人，今明兩身俱能益物，真身破取相諭如藥，應身對萬機類於珠。就兩字明次第者，與慈悲相似也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, explaining the two bodies of the medicine [tree] and the jewel. The true and the responsive explained before speak only of what is realized and do not yet concern benefiting people. Here it explains that both bodies can benefit beings: the true body breaks grasping at marks and is likened to medicine; the responsive body meets the myriad capacities and resembles a jewel. As for the sequence of the two words, it is similar to that of loving-kindness and compassion.

Chinese Source

六明冥顯者，前明二身道理即能顯益，今辯被緣得冥益、或得顯益，故次二身後明也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, explaining hidden and manifest. Before, it explained that the principle of the two bodies can benefit; now it discusses that those who are reached may obtain hidden benefit or manifest benefit. So this is explained after the two bodies.

Chinese Source

七明權實者，前緣得益，何意不同？良由權巧無方，赴機允當，不失其宜，二智之力，故以此為次也。先權後實者，此就淺深為次也。若依文者，先以實益，次以權度，此隨物為次。若就佛本意，先只為一大事因緣，先顯實益，眾生未堪，後用權度。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, explaining the provisional and the real. The beings reached before obtain benefit; why is it different? It is truly because provisional skill has no fixed method, meeting capacities fittingly and never missing what is appropriate — the power of the two wisdoms. So this comes next. Placing the provisional first and the real after follows shallow and deep. Following the text, one first benefits with the real and then saves with the provisional — this follows beings. In terms of the Buddha's original intent, he [appeared] only for the one great cause and condition: first he reveals the real benefit, and when beings cannot yet bear it, he then saves them with the provisional.

Chinese Source

八明本迹者，雖復益物，權實之巧，而巧有優降，必是上、中、下智本迹之殊，權實略而且橫，今欲細判高下以明次位，若其本高，所作權實之迹則妙，是故次總略之後，辯其細妙之能也。非本無以垂迹，故先明本；非迹無以顯本，應先迹也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, explaining origin and trace. Although beings are benefited by the skill of the provisional and the real, the skill has higher and lower degrees; this must be the difference in origin and trace of upper, middle, and lower wisdom. The provisional and the real are brief and horizontal; now, wishing to judge high and low in detail and explain the order of stages: if the origin is high, the traces of the provisional and the real that it makes are wondrous. So after the general and brief, it discusses the capacity of the fine and wondrous. Without the origin there is nothing from which to let fall traces, so the origin is explained first; without the trace there is nothing by which to reveal the origin, so the trace should come first.

Chinese Source

九明了因緣因者，上來行人發心修行，從因剋果，化他利物，深淺不同，從人法至真應是自行次第，藥珠至本迹是化他次第，此乃順論，未是却討根本。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, explaining the revealing cause and the conditioning cause. Above, the practitioner gives rise to the resolve and cultivates, going from cause to attain the fruit, transforming others and benefiting beings, with differences of depth. From person and dharma through the true and the responsive is the sequence of one's own practice; from the medicine [tree] and the jewel through origin and trace is the sequence of transforming others. This is discussion in the forward direction; it is not yet tracing back to the root.

Chinese Source

今原其性德種子，若觀智之人悲心誓願，智慧莊嚴顯出真身，皆是了因為種子；若是普門之法，慈心誓願，福德莊嚴，顯出應身者，皆是緣因為種子，故次第九也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now it traces their seeds in inherent virtue. If the person of contemplative wisdom, with a compassionate mind and vows, adorned by wisdom, reveals the true body, all of this has the revealing cause as its seed. If the dharma of the Universal Gate, with a loving mind and vows, adorned by merit, reveals the responsive body, all of this has the conditioning cause as its seed. So this comes ninth.

Chinese Source

十明智斷者，前明緣了是却討因源，今明智斷是順論究竟。始則起自了因，終則菩提大智；始則起自緣因，終則涅槃斷德。若入涅槃，眾行休息，故居第十也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, explaining wisdom and severance. The conditioning and revealing explained before trace back to the source of the cause; wisdom and severance explained now discuss the ultimate in the forward direction. It begins from the revealing cause and ends in the great wisdom of bodhi; it begins from the conditioning cause and ends in the virtue of severance, nirvāṇa. When one enters nirvāṇa, all practices come to rest; so this is placed tenth.

The Sequence in Terms of the Teachings

Chinese Source

二約諸教明次第者，又為通別，通義可解，別今當說。如華嚴頓教，教名大方廣佛華嚴。依題初明人法，此人秉法必具慈悲。菩薩修因，居然福慧，既入地位，必證真應；既能利物，則辯藥珠，物得其益，有冥有顯，而未得別論權實本迹。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the sequence in terms of the various teachings. This again is general and specific; the general meaning can be understood, and the specific I will now explain. Take the sudden teaching of the Avataṃsaka, whose teaching is named The Great, Vast Buddha-Flower-Garland. Following its title, it first explains person and dharma; this person, holding the dharma, must have loving-kindness and compassion. The bodhisattva cultivating the cause naturally has merit and wisdom; having entered the grounds, he must realize the true and the responsive; being able to benefit beings, it then discusses the medicine [tree] and the jewel; beings obtain its benefit, hidden and manifest. But it does not yet discuss specifically the provisional and the real, or origin and trace.

Chinese Source

緣了智斷者，通義則有，別意則無。何故爾？佛一期化物明於頓漸，頓教雖說，漸教未彰，故不明四意也。所以不明者，彼經明小隔於大，如聾如瘂，覆於此權，未顯其實，故云「久默斯要，不務速說」，故言無權實也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the conditioning and the revealing, wisdom and severance: in the general sense they are there; in the specific sense they are not. Why so? The Buddha's transformation of beings over his lifetime explains the sudden and the gradual; though the sudden teaching had been spoken, the gradual teaching was not yet displayed, so the four meanings are not explained. The reason they are not explained is that that sūtra shows the lesser to be cut off from the great, as if deaf and dumb; it covers the provisional and does not yet reveal the real. Hence "long silent about this essential, not hastening to speak it." So it is said there is no provisional and real.

Chinese Source

言無本迹者，彼經未發王宮生身之迹、寂滅道場法身之迹、未彈指謦欬發久遠所得生法二身之本，故言無本迹。言無緣了智斷者，不明小乘根性及有心之者，本自有常住之因，當剋智斷菩提本果，故言無也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for there being no origin and trace: that sūtra had not yet disclosed the trace of the birth-body in the royal palace, or the trace of the Dharma-body at the site of the Way of quiescence, and had not yet, with a snap of the fingers and a clearing of the throat, disclosed the origin of the two bodies, birth-body and Dharma-body, attained in the distant past; so it is said there is no origin and trace. As for there being no conditioning and revealing, wisdom and severance: it does not explain that those of lesser-vehicle faculties, and all who have mind, originally have the cause of permanence and will attain wisdom and severance, the original fruit of bodhi; so it is said there are none.

Chinese Source

次約三藏教，但明人法、慈悲、福慧三義，無真應等七種。何故爾？二乘教中但明灰身滅智，那得從真起應？既無真應，將何益物？

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the Tripiṭaka teaching explains only three meanings — person and dharma, loving-kindness and compassion, merit and wisdom — and lacks the seven, the true and the responsive and the rest. Why so? The teaching of the two vehicles explains only reducing the body to ashes and extinguishing wisdom; how could response arise from the true? Since there is no true and responsive, with what would it benefit beings?

Chinese Source

私難：通論備十，別語但三，此三若約真諦，則隨通義乃具十意，何止但三？若言是別，別應約中道。既得有中道，人法三種，何意無七？

Ru-Yi Translation

A personal objection: in general all ten are present; specifically, only three. If these three are in terms of the real truth, then by the general meaning all ten are present — why stop at three? If one says they are specific, the specific should be in terms of the middle way. Since there could be the middle way, and the three — person and dharma and the rest — why are the seven lacking?

Chinese Source

私答：通論十意，此約三乘，別語三科，的據菩薩三藏。菩薩得有慈、悲、福、慧、伏惑之義，既伏而不斷，故無真應七法。師云：「齊教止三。」

Ru-Yi Translation

A personal answer: the ten meanings in general are in terms of the three vehicles; the three categories specifically are based precisely on the bodhisattva of the Tripiṭaka. The bodhisattva can have loving-kindness, compassion, merit, wisdom, and the meaning of subduing delusion; since he subdues it without cutting it off, he lacks the seven dharmas, the true and the responsive and the rest. The master said: "Limited to the teaching, it stops at three."

Chinese Source

若約方等教對小明大，得有中道、大乘、人、法、至冥、顯兩益等六意，然猶帶方便調熟眾生，故不得說權實等四意。若明般若教，雖未會小乘之人，已會小法，皆是摩訶衍，但明人、法等六意，亦帶方便，未明權實等也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In terms of the Vaipulya teaching, which explains the great in contrast to the lesser, there can be the middle way, the great vehicle, and six meanings, from person and dharma through the two benefits, hidden and manifest. But it still carries skillful means to ripen beings, so it cannot speak of the four meanings, the provisional and the real and the rest. As for the Prajñā teaching, although it has not yet merged the people of the lesser vehicle, it has already merged the lesser dharmas, all of which are the Mahāyāna; but it explains only six meanings, person and dharma and the rest, and also carries skillful means, not yet explaining the provisional and the real and the rest.

Chinese Source

The second question and answer — abiding in the Śūraṅgama [samādhi], manifesting form-bodies everywhere; without rising from the concentration of cessation, displaying these deportments; settled in dhyāna, praising the Dharma kings in a thousand verses — so we know that the manifestation of the Universal Gate takes its name from merit. Truly because of the causes and conditions of merit and wisdom, it is called "Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate."

4. The True and the Responsive

Chinese Source

四釋真應者，真名不偽、不動，應名稱適根緣、集藏名身，若契實相不偽、不動之理，即能稱機而應，譬如攬鏡像對即形。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, explaining the true and the responsive. "True" means not false and unmoving; "responsive" means fitting faculties and conditions; "body" means accumulation and storehouse. If one accords with the principle of the true mark, not false and unmoving, one can respond in accord with capacities — like taking up a mirror: when an image faces it, the form appears.

此之真應不得相離，苦外道作意修通，雖能變化，譬如瓦石光影不現，豈可以此為應？尚未破四住顯偏真理，那忽有中道真應？

Ru-Yi Translation

This true and responsive cannot be separated. As for non-Buddhists who cultivate powers by deliberate effort: though they can transform things, it is like tiles and stones, in which no light or reflection appears — how could this be called response? They have not even broken the four abodes to reveal the one-sided real principle; how could they suddenly have the true and responsive of the middle way?

Chinese Source

若二乘變化修通所得，此亦非應，譬如圖畫作意乃成，了不相似。大乘不爾，得實相真，譬得明鏡，不須作意，法界色像即對即應，如鏡寫像與真不殊，是時乃名真寂身應。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the transformations of the two vehicles, obtained by cultivating powers, these too are not response; they are like a painting, made only by deliberate effort and not at all like [the original]. The great vehicle is not so. It obtains the true of the true mark, like obtaining a bright mirror: without deliberate effort, the forms and images of the dharma-realm, as soon as they face it, are responded to, like a mirror reproducing an image no different from the real. Only then is it called the true, still body responding.

Chinese Source

菩薩從初發心歷於六即，今經前問答明於真寂而不動法界大益，觀音從真身得名；後問答明隨機廣利，出沒多端，普門是從應身得名。良以真應因緣故，名觀世音普門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The bodhisattva, from the initial resolve, passes through the six identities. In this sūtra, the first question and answer explains the true and still, bringing great benefit to the dharma-realm without moving: Avalokiteśvara takes the name from the true body. The second question and answer explains benefiting widely in accord with capacities, appearing and disappearing in many ways: the Universal Gate takes the name from the responsive body. Truly because of the causes and conditions of the true and the responsive, it is called "Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate."

[As for this] body, this too is judged in stages by the six identities.

Ru-Yi Translation

In the first question and answer, he saves everywhere from hidden dangers and suffering; this takes its name from the medicine-king body. In the second question and answer, he fits what is sought, rains the rain of the true mark, and [beings] obtain the bliss of nirvāṇa; this takes its name from the wish-fulfilling jewel king body. So we know that because of the causes and conditions of the two bodies it is called "Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate."

6. Hidden and Manifest Benefit

Chinese Source

六釋冥顯兩益者，冥是冥密、顯是彰露，大聖恒以二益利安一切，而眾生及以下地日用不知。譬如日月照世，盲雖不見，實荷深恩，故《藥草喻》云「而諸草木不覺不知」。只同是一地，下品不知上品，冥顯兩益，如文殊不知妙音神力所作，以不知故，名為冥益，此亦約六即判位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, explaining the two benefits, hidden and manifest. "Hidden" means concealed and secret; "manifest" means displayed and exposed. The great sage always benefits and comforts all with the two benefits, but beings and those of lower grounds use it daily without knowing. It is like the sun and moon illuminating the world: although the blind do not see them, they truly receive their deep kindness. So the [parable of the] Medicinal Herbs says: "And the grasses and trees are unaware and do not know." Even on the same ground, the lower grade does not know the upper grade's two benefits, hidden and manifest — as Mañjuśrī did not know what Wondrous Sound's spiritual power had done. Because it is not known, it is called hidden benefit. This too is judged in stages by the six identities.

Chinese Source

若就前問答，不見形聲，密荷深祐，名為冥益，聖人之益雖不可知，聖欲使知，蜫蟲能知；如後問答，親覩色身，得聞說法，視聽彰灼，法利顯然。故知觀音從冥益得名，普門從顯益得名，以冥、顯因緣故，名觀世音普門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the first question and answer, one does not see form or hear voice but secretly receives deep protection; this is called hidden benefit. Although the benefit of the sage cannot be known, if the sage wishes to make it known, even insects can know it. In the second question and answer, one personally sees the form-body and hears the Dharma taught; sight and hearing are vivid, and the benefit of the Dharma is evident. So we know that "Avalokiteśvara" takes its name from hidden benefit, and "Universal Gate" from manifest benefit; because of the causes and conditions of hidden and manifest, it is called "Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate."

7. The Provisional and the Real

Chinese Source

七釋權實者，權是暫用，實非暫用。略言權實則有三種：一自行論權實，自觀中道為實，二觀為權；二就化他論權實，他根性不同，或說權為實、說實為權，不可定判，但約他意以明權實也；

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, explaining the provisional and the real. The provisional is temporary use; the real is not temporary use. Briefly, the provisional and the real are of three kinds. Second, the provisional and the real discussed in terms of transforming others: since others' faculties differ, sometimes the provisional is taught as the real and the real as the provisional; it cannot be fixedly judged, and the provisional and the real are explained only in terms of others' intentions.

Chinese Source

三自行化他合明權實者，若自觀三諦，有權、有實，皆名為實，化他隨緣亦有權、有實，皆名為權。用此三義歷四教。復就自行權實明六即判位，尋此品意是明自行化他論權實，前問答從自行化他之實智益物，後問答從自行化他之權以益物，故知權實因緣故，名觀世音普門也。

Third, the provisional and the real explained in terms of one's own practice and transforming others combined: one's own contemplation of the three truths, which has the provisional and the real, is all called the real; transforming others according to conditions, which also has the provisional and the real, is all called the provisional. These three meanings are applied through the four teachings. Further, the six identities are explained in terms of the provisional and the real of one's own practice to judge the stages. Looking into the intent of this chapter, it explains the provisional and the real in terms of one's own practice and transforming others: the first question and answer benefits beings from the real wisdom of one's own practice and transforming others; the second benefits beings from the provisional of one's own practice and transforming others. So we know that because of the causes and conditions of the provisional and the real, it is called "Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate."

8. Origin and Trace

Chinese Source

八釋本迹者，本名實得，迹名應現。若通途作本迹者，世智凡夫本意難測，乃至別教本迹。若圓教無始發心，初破無明所得法身者，名之為本；垂形百億，高下不定，稱之為迹。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, explaining origin and trace. "Origin" means what is really attained; "trace" means what is manifested in response. If origin and trace are treated in the general way, the original intent of worldly-wise ordinary people is hard to fathom, and so on through the origin and trace of the distinct teaching. In the perfect teaching, the Dharma-body attained by first breaking ignorance, after giving rise to the resolve from beginningless time, is called the origin; letting down forms by the hundreds of millions, high and low without fixity, is called the trace.

Chinese Source

若一往判真應，多用上地為真、為本，下地為應、為迹。地地傳作此判，真本唯據於高，應迹唯指於下。此義不可，今細明本迹則與真應異。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the true and the responsive are judged at a first look, the higher grounds are mostly taken as true and as origin, and the lower grounds as responsive and as trace; ground after ground is judged in this way in turn, the true and the origin based only on the high, the responsive and the trace pointing only to the low. This meaning is not acceptable. Now, explaining origin and trace in detail, they differ from the true and the responsive.

Chinese Source

本是實得，始坐道場及初住所得法身即是其本；迹為上地之佛及作上地菩薩悉名為迹。不可以上地高故稱之為本，始得初住目之為迹。何以故？實不得上地，上地非本；實得下地，下地非迹。故〈壽量〉云「隨自意、隨他意，是本迹意也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The origin is what is really attained: the Dharma-body attained on first sitting at the site of the Way, or at the first abiding, is the origin. The trace: acting as a buddha of a higher ground, or as a bodhisattva of a higher ground, is all called trace. One cannot call the higher ground the origin because it is high, and regard the first abiding, just attained, as the trace. Why? What is not really attained — the higher ground — is not the origin; what is really attained — the lower ground — is not the trace. So the [chapter on the] Lifespan says: "According to his own intent, according to others' intent" — this is the meaning of origin and trace.

Chinese Source

就本迹明六即，就前問答不可說示，但冥祐前人，從本地得名；後問答殊形異狀，應現度脫，從迹地得名。故知本迹因緣故，名觀世音普門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The six identities are explained in terms of origin and trace. In the first question and answer, he cannot be pointed out, but secretly protects the person before him; this takes its name from the ground of the origin. In the second question and answer, with different forms and various appearances, he manifests in response to deliver; this takes its name from the ground of the trace. So we know that because of the causes and conditions of origin and trace, it is called "Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate."

9. The Revealing Cause and the Conditioning Cause

Chinese Source

九釋了因緣因者，了是顯發，緣是資助，資助於了，顯發法身。了者，即是般若觀智，亦名慧行正道，智慧莊嚴。緣者，即是解脫，行行助道，福德莊嚴。《大論》云「一人能耘，一人能種。」種喻於緣，耘喻於了，

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, explaining the revealing cause and the conditioning cause. "Revealing" means bringing forth; "conditioning" means supporting: it supports the revealing, which brings forth the Dharma-body. The revealing is the contemplative wisdom of prajñā, also called the correct path of the practice of wisdom, the adornment of wisdom. The conditioning is liberation, the auxiliary path of the practice of practices, the adornment of merit. The Great Treatise says: "One person can weed, one person can sow." Sowing is likened to the conditioning, weeding to the revealing.

Chinese Source

通論教教皆具緣了義，今正明圓教二種莊嚴之因，佛具二種莊嚴之果，原此因果根本即是性德緣了也，此之性德本自有之，非適今也。《大經》云「一切諸法本性自空。」亦用菩薩修習空故，見諸法空，即了因種子本自有之。

Ru-Yi Translation

In general, every teaching has the meaning of the conditioning and the revealing. Here it explains precisely the cause of the two adornments in the perfect teaching; the Buddha has the fruit of the two adornments. Tracing the root of this cause and fruit, it is the conditioning and revealing of inherent virtue. This inherent virtue is originally present, not just now. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The original nature of all dharmas is empty of itself"; and also, because the bodhisattva cultivates emptiness, he sees that dharmas are empty. That is, the seed of the revealing cause is originally present.

Chinese Source

又云「一切眾生皆有初地味禪。」《思益》云「一切眾生即滅盡定。」此即緣因種子本自有之。以此二種方便修習，漸漸增長，起於毫末，得成修得合抱大樹，摩訶般若首楞嚴定，此一科不論六即，但就根本性德義爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

It also says: "All beings have the savored dhyāna of the first ground." The Viśeṣacinti says: "All beings are themselves the concentration of complete cessation." That is, the seed of the conditioning cause is originally present. Cultivating these two by skillful means, they gradually grow, starting from the tip of a hair, until they become, by cultivation, a great tree a full embrace around — the great prajñā and the Śūraṅgama concentration. This one category does not discuss the six identities; it concerns only the root meaning of inherent virtue.

Chinese Source

前問答從了種受名，後問答從緣種受名，故知了因、緣因，故名觀世音普門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first question and answer takes its name from the seed of the revealing; the second takes its name from the seed of the conditioning. So we know that because of the revealing cause and the conditioning cause, it is called "Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate."

10. Wisdom and Severance

Chinese Source

十釋智斷者，通途意智即有為功德滿，亦名圓淨涅槃。言有為功德者，即是因時智慧，有照用修成之義，故稱有為。因雖無常，而果是常，將因來名果，故言有為功德滿也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, explaining wisdom and severance. In the general sense, wisdom is the fulfillment of conditioned merit, also called the nirvāṇa of perfect purity. "Conditioned merit" is the wisdom at the time of the cause, which has the meaning of illuminating function accomplished by cultivation; so it is called conditioned. Although the cause is impermanent, the fruit is permanent; the cause is used to name the fruit; hence "the fulfillment of conditioned merit."

Chinese Source

斷即無為功德滿，亦名解脫，亦名方便淨涅槃。言無為者，若小乘但取煩惱滅、無為斷，但離虛妄，名為解脫，其實未得一切解脫，此乃無體之斷德也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Severance is the fulfillment of unconditioned merit, also called liberation, and also called the nirvāṇa of expedient purity. As for "unconditioned": the lesser vehicle takes only the cessation of afflictions, unconditioned severance, merely leaving the false, and calls it liberation; in reality it has not attained complete liberation. This is a virtue of severance without substance.

Chinese Source

大乘是有體之斷，不取滅無為斷，但取隨所調伏眾生之處惡不能染，縱任自在，無有累縛，名為斷德，指此名無為功德。故《淨名》云「不斷癡愛起諸明脫。」又云「於諸見不動而修三十七品，愛見為侍，亦名如來種，乃至五無間皆生解脫。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The great vehicle's is a severance with substance. It does not take extinction as unconditioned severance, but takes it that wherever one tames beings, evil cannot stain one; free and at ease, without encumbrance or bondage — this is called the virtue of severance, and this is what is called unconditioned merit. So the Vimalakīrti says: "Without cutting off delusion and craving, giving rise to the clear knowledges and liberations." It also says: "Unmoved by views, cultivating the thirty-seven factors"; "craving and views are attendants"; "they are also called the seed of the Tathāgata"; and so on to "the five uninterrupted [deeds] all give rise to liberation."

Chinese Source

無所染礙名為一切解脫，即是斷德，無為也，寂而常照即智德也。小乘灰身滅智，既其無身，將何入生死而論調伏無礙、無染？滅智何所照寂？如此智斷圓極，故法身顯著，即是三種佛性義圓也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Being without stain or obstruction is called complete liberation; this is the virtue of severance, the unconditioned. Still and always illuminating is the virtue of wisdom. The lesser vehicle reduces the body to ashes and extinguishes wisdom; having no body, with what would it enter birth and death and speak of taming unobstructed and unstained? With wisdom extinguished, what would illuminate stillness? When wisdom and severance are perfect and ultimate in this way, the Dharma-body is revealed; this is the perfection of the meaning of the three kinds of buddha-nature.

Chinese Source

法身滿足即是非因非果正因滿，故云「隱名如來藏，顯名法身。」雖非是因而名為正因，雖非是果而名為法身。《大經》云「非因非果名佛性」者，即是此正因佛性也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Dharma-body fulfilled is the correct cause, neither cause nor fruit, fulfilled. Hence "concealed, it is called the tathāgatagarbha; revealed, it is called the Dharma-body." Though it is not a cause, it is called the correct cause; though it is not a fruit, it is called the Dharma-body. When the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says, "What is neither cause nor fruit is called buddha-nature," this is the buddha-nature of the correct cause.

Chinese Source

又云「是因非果名為佛性」者，此據性德緣了，皆名為因也。又云「是果非因名佛性」者，此據修德緣了皆滿，了轉名般若，緣轉名解脫，亦名菩提果，亦名大涅槃果果，皆稱為果也。

Ru-Yi Translation

When it also says, "What is a cause and not a fruit is called buddha-nature," this is based on the conditioning and revealing of inherent virtue, which are both called causes. When it also says, "What is a fruit and not a cause is called buddha-nature," this is based on the conditioning and revealing of cultivated virtue both fulfilled: the revealing turns and is called prajñā; the conditioning turns and is called liberation; these are also called the fruit of bodhi, and also the fruit of the fruit, great nirvāṇa — all are called fruit.

Chinese Source

佛性通於因果，不縱不橫性德時三因，不縱不橫果滿時名三德，故《普賢觀》云「大乘因者，諸法實相；大乘果者，亦諸法實相。」智德既滿，湛然常照，隨機即應，一時解脫；斷德處處調伏，皆令得度。

Ru-Yi Translation

Buddha-nature extends through cause and fruit: neither vertical nor horizontal, in the time of inherent virtue, it is the three causes; neither vertical nor horizontal, when the fruit is fulfilled, it is called the three virtues. So the Contemplation of Samantabhadra says: "The cause of the great vehicle is the true mark of all dharmas; the fruit of the great vehicle is also the true mark of all dharmas." When the virtue of wisdom is fulfilled, it is clear and always illuminating, responding immediately to capacities, liberating all at once; the virtue of severance tames everywhere and brings all to deliverance.

Chinese Source

前問答從智德分滿受名，後問答從斷德分滿受名，故知以智斷因緣名觀世音普門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first question and answer takes its name from the partial fulfillment of the virtue of wisdom; the second from the partial fulfillment of the virtue of severance. So we know that because of the causes and conditions of wisdom and severance it is called "Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate."

Chinese Source

問：此十義名字出餘經，那得用釋此品？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: the names of these ten meanings come from other sūtras. How can they be used to explain this chapter?

Chinese Source

答：大乘義通，眾經共用。若不許此者，佛性出《涅槃》、五住二死出《勝鬘》，諸師那得浪用通眾經耶？此品在文雖無十名，總將二問答帖十義意，宛然可解。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: the meanings of the great vehicle are shared, used in common by many sūtras. If this were not allowed — buddha-nature comes from the Nirvāṇa, and the five abodes and two kinds of death come from the Śrīmālā — how could the teachers freely use them for all sūtras? Although the text of this chapter does not have the ten names, if one matches the two questions and answers as a whole to the meaning of the ten, it can be clearly understood.

Chinese Source

今已如前，今更別點句句來證十義者，如文云：「以何因緣名觀世音」，又云「以是因緣名觀世音」，即是據人名也。後文云「普門示現」，即是明法也。「有如是等大威神力多所饒益」，即慈也。「愍諸四眾」，即悲也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This has been done above; now I will further point out, phrase by phrase, passages that prove the ten meanings. The text says, "For what causes and conditions is he named Avalokiteśvara?" and also "For these causes and conditions he is named Avalokiteśvara" — this is based on the name of the person. A later passage says "the manifestation of the Universal Gate" — this explains the dharma. "He has such great majestic and spiritual powers and benefits much" — this is loving-kindness. "Out of pity for the fourfold assembly" — this is compassion.

Chinese Source

欲知智在說，「十九說法」即智慧也。「一時禮拜，得無量無邊福德之利」，即福德也。「自在之業」，即法身也。何故爾？法身於一切得自在，智慧契此，故名為業。〈壽量〉云「慧光照無量，久修業所得。」

Ru-Yi Translation

If you wish to know where wisdom is, it is in teaching: "nineteen [ways of] teaching the Dharma" is wisdom. "Paying homage even once, one obtains the benefit of measureless and boundless merit" — this is merit. "Sovereign deeds" is the Dharma-body. Why? The Dharma-body attains sovereignty over everything, and wisdom accords with it; so it is called "deeds." The [chapter on the] Lifespan says: "The light of wisdom shines immeasurably, obtained through deeds cultivated long."

Chinese Source

威神之力巍巍如是，如是滿足之名即是真身也；普門示現神通力，即應身也。遊諸國土度脫眾生，即藥樹王身也；於怖畏急難之中能施無畏，即如意珠王身也。福不唐捐，即冥益也；三十三身，即顯益也。現佛身即實智也，現餘身即權智也。觀音身即本，餘身即迹也。又，大威神力是本，方便力是迹。

Ru-Yi Translation

"His majestic and spiritual power is lofty like this" — the name of such fullness is the true body; "the spiritual power of the manifestation of the Universal Gate" is the responsive body. "Traveling through lands, he delivers beings" is the medicine-tree king body; "in the midst of fear and urgent danger he can bestow fearlessness" is the wish-fulfilling jewel king body. "The merit is not spent in vain" is hidden benefit; the thirty-three bodies are manifest benefit. Manifesting a buddha's body is real wisdom; manifesting the other bodies is provisional wisdom. The body of Avalokiteśvara is the origin; the other bodies are the trace. Again, "great majestic and spiritual power" is the origin; "the power of skillful means" is the trace.

Chinese Source

聞是觀世音菩薩名者，若有聞是品者，即證了因；功德不少，即緣因。不肯受，常捨行故。及即時觀其音聲，觀即智；皆得解脫，種種調伏眾生八萬四千發心等是利益，即斷也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Hearing the name of this bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara," and "if there are those who hear this chapter" — these prove the revealing cause; "their merit is not small" — the conditioning cause. "He was unwilling to accept" — because of the practice of constant giving up. And "at once contemplating their voices": contemplating is wisdom; "all are liberated," and the various ways of taming beings, the eighty-four thousand giving rise to the resolve, and so on, are benefit — that is severance.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Names of the bodhisattva. "Bestower of Fearlessness" (施無畏者) is from the Universal Gate chapter itself. The past name "Bright True Dharma" (正法明如來) and the future name "Universal Light Merit" (普光功德山王如來) follow traditional sources, the latter from the Sūtra of the Prophecy of Avalokiteśvara (觀世音菩薩授記經); "the Buddha Jewel Treasury" (寶藏佛) is the buddha under whom the bodhisattva made his vows in the Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka. These identifications are given as the text gives them.

The Sanskrit name. 阿耶婆婁吉低輸 is a transcription of Āryāvalokiteśvara; it is romanized here only as the Chinese gives it.

"Sūtra means 'proceeding from'" (經者由義). A traditional gloss on 經.

"Bringing Out the Essence" (出體). Here the second section is called 出體; later in the text (Part 4) it is 釋體. The site's Fahua Xuanyi uses "Clarifying the Essence" for 辨體／顯體.

"Prajñā is a single dharma …" A verse from the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra.

The two rounds of question and answer. The Universal Gate chapter has two questions by Akṣayamati: why the bodhisattva is named Avalokiteśvara (answered by the rescues from the seven perils and so on), and how he travels in the Sahā world and teaches (answered by the thirty-three bodies). The text consistently matches the first to "Avalokiteśvara" and the second to "Universal Gate."

"Long silent about this essential …" From the Lotus Sūtra, chapter on skillful means. "I have never spoken of such things to anyone" and "now I will speak to you of the most real matter" are also from the Lotus.

"With a snap of the fingers and a clearing of the throat." From the Lotus Sūtra's chapter on the spiritual powers of the Tathāgata.

"A personal objection … A personal answer" (私難／私答). Guanding's own interpolated question and answer; "the master" is Zhiyi.

The Five Flavors and the six or eight meanings. The final answer correlates each teaching period with the flavor it produces for sharp and dull hearers.

The four gates of the Tripiṭaka teaching. The gate of existence, of emptiness, of both, and of neither; the text works through the first two and says the others follow by analogy. "The three kinds of provisional reality" (三假) are the provisional by causes, by continuity, and by mutual dependence (as on this site's Shi Chan Boluomi).

"The nature of form is like the nature of self …" Quoted as from the Large Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra; wording as given.

"Not identical to the six dharmas, and not apart from the six dharmas." From the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra; "the six dharmas" are the five aggregates and the self (or person). The text's chain "not the aggregates, so not dharma; not the self, so not person …" is kept exactly.

The four universal vows. Rendered in their usual form; the text gives slightly different wordings in the Tripiṭaka passage (無量 for 無邊), and the English follows each.

The Four Noble Truths with fabrication (有作四諦). Corresponding to what this site's Mohe Zhiguan calls the Four Noble Truths of generation-and-extinction; here the Chinese has 有作, so it is rendered separately.

"If there were a dharma surpassing nirvāṇa …" From the Large Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra.

Loving-kindness and compassion without object (無緣慈悲). The highest of three kinds (with beings as object, with dharmas as object, without object).

"Concentration attaches and wisdom urges …" Quoted as from the Great Treatise; the passage combines several images (mirror, lamp, eyes and feet, the cool pond). Boundaries of the quotation are approximate.

Scriptures cited. Viśeṣacinti (思益經); Invitation to Avalokiteśvara (請觀音經); Āmrapālī Sūtra (柰女經); Contemplation of Samantabhadra (普賢觀經). "Wondrous Sound" is the bodhisattva Gadgadasvara of the Lotus Sūtra, whose powers Mañjuśrī asks about.

"Body means accumulation and storehouse" (集藏名身). A traditional gloss on 身 (kāya).

"Weeding" and "sowing." The Great Treatise image of two people working one field.

"The savored dhyāna of the first ground" (初地味禪). The dhyāna of the first [dhyāna] stage as savored with attachment; the point is that a seed of concentration is present in all beings.

The three statements of buddha-nature. "Neither cause nor fruit," "a cause and not a fruit," and "a fruit and not a cause" are from the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra; the text matches them to the correct cause and to the conditioning and revealing causes in inherent and cultivated virtue.

"Concealed, it is called the tathāgatagarbha; revealed, it is called the Dharma-body." A widely cited formula; the exact source is not identified here.

The Vimalakīrti citations. Several phrases from the chapter on the disciples and the chapter on the Buddha-path are strung together; they are given as quoted.

"Nineteen [ways of] teaching the Dharma" (十九說法). The nineteen kinds of beings in whose forms the bodhisattva teaches, which with the fourteen kinds of fearlessness and other counts make up the "thirty-three bodies."

"He was unwilling to accept" (不肯受). In the sūtra, Avalokiteśvara at first declines Akṣayamati's offering of a necklace.
