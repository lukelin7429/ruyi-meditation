Resolving Doubts, Inherent Evil, and the Name "Avalokiteśvara"

This part completes the general explanation of the name and begins the specific one, closing the first fascicle. The section "resolving doubts" takes up each of the ten pairs in turn and answers objections to it. The best-known passage in the whole work comes under the conditioning and revealing causes: do these "inherent virtues" include inherent evil as well as inherent good? The answer is yes. An icchantika has cut off cultivated good but still has inherent good; a buddha has cut off cultivated evil but still has inherent evil. Because the buddha penetrates evil, he is free in it and is never stained by it, and so can use the dharma-gates of evil to save beings without deliberate effort — unlike those who say a buddha must deliberately "make" evil by spiritual power. The specific explanation then begins with "Avalokiteśvara": first refuting ways of thinking about object and wisdom that lie outside the principle, then those within it that are still conceivable, and finally setting out the inconceivable object and wisdom, before explaining "world" as the three kinds of world and the ten dharma-realms, and asking why it is the world's sounds that are contemplated.

Resolving Doubts: Person and Dharma

Chinese Source

第四料簡者，問：人對觀音、法對普門者，方等有普門法王子標於人名，此義云何同？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, resolving doubts. Question: the person is matched to "Avalokiteśvara" and the dharma to "Universal Gate"; but in the Vaipulya [sūtras] there is a Dharma-prince named Universal Gate, which marks a person's name. How is this meaning the same?

Chinese Source

答：此應作四句分別：人非法、法非人、人即法、法即人。若約華嚴次第意，地前生死行人未是實相之法，此法亦非彼人；若作不次第意者，人即實相、實相即人，人、法不二也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: this should be distinguished in four propositions: the person is not the dharma; the dharma is not the person; the person is the dharma; the dharma is the person. In terms of the sequential meaning of the Avataṃsaka, the practitioner in birth and death before the grounds is not yet the dharma of the real mark, and this dharma is not that person. In terms of the non-sequential meaning, the person is the real mark and the real mark is the person; person and dharma are not two.

Chinese Source

若三藏有門，明無假人、但實法，此法非人；若空門攬實法成假人，人、法兩異；若其不離人論法、不離法論人，此乃是二諦意，非中道之人法也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the gate of existence of the Tripiṭaka [teaching], it explains that there is no provisional person, only real dharmas; this dharma is not the person. In the gate of emptiness, taking up real dharmas constitutes a provisional person; person and dharma are two different things. Discussing the dharma without leaving the person, and the person without leaving the dharma, is the meaning of the two truths, not the person and dharma of the middle way.

Chinese Source

若方等對小明大論人法者，明小同三藏、明大同華嚴，般若涅槃等例爾。今方等中明普門者，即大乘意。今明普門是法，何得有法無人？彼明普門是人，何得但人無法？此則人法互舉，彼經標人，此處標法爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the Vaipulya discusses person and dharma by explaining the great in contrast to the lesser, its explanation of the lesser is the same as the Tripiṭaka, and its explanation of the great the same as the Avataṃsaka; the Prajñā, the Nirvāṇa, and the rest are likewise. The Universal Gate explained in the Vaipulya is the meaning of the great vehicle. Here the Universal Gate is explained as a dharma — how could there be a dharma without a person? There the Universal Gate is explained as a person — how could there be only a person and no dharma? This is person and dharma raised alternately: that sūtra marks the person, and here the dharma is marked.

Chinese Source

例如小乘明身子智慧第一，餘弟子各就餘法門論第一，本以智慧斷惑，發真無漏，餘人無慧，那得入道？既得道果，果知有慧，但各舉其初門，別稱第一。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is like the lesser vehicle explaining Śāriputra as foremost in wisdom, while the other disciples are each called foremost in some other dharma-gate. Originally it is by wisdom that delusion is cut off and the true uncontaminated is brought forth; if the others had no wisdom, how could they enter the path? Since they attained the fruit of the path, we know they had wisdom; but each is named for the gate by which they first entered, and specially called foremost.

Chinese Source

譬如刀刃斷物，必藉於背方有利用，諸數如刀背，慧數如刀刃。今普門義亦爾，但以因緣之法當普門之名，何得無了因之人耶？若併從觀音標名者，此則通漫。欲使世諦不亂，互舉別名，如身具六根，但稱為淨眼淨意，豈得無餘根耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

It is like a blade cutting a thing: it must rely on the back of the knife before it has a sharp use. The [other] mental factors are like the back of the knife, and the mental factor of wisdom is like the blade. The meaning of the Universal Gate is likewise: only the dharma of causes and conditions is matched to the name "Universal Gate" — how could there be no person of the revealing cause? If everything were named from "Avalokiteśvara," it would be general and vague. To keep the conventional truth in order, specific names are raised alternately — as when a body has the six faculties, yet one speaks only of "pure eye" or "pure mind"; how could it lack the other faculties?

Resolving Doubts: Loving-Kindness and Compassion

Chinese Source

料簡慈悲者，問：若大悲拔苦，苦除即是得樂；大慈與樂，樂至即是拔苦。何意兩分？

Ru-Yi Translation

Resolving doubts about loving-kindness and compassion. Question: if great compassion removes suffering, the removal of suffering is itself attaining happiness; if great loving-kindness gives happiness, the arrival of happiness is itself removing suffering. Why divide them in two?

Chinese Source

答：通論如此，別則不然。譬如拔罪於獄，未施五塵，身雖免痛，根情未娛，此但拔苦，未名與樂。又如施五塵於獄，耳、眼雖悅，不名拔苦。為從別義，各顯一邊，故別說爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: in general it is so, but specifically it is not. It is like freeing a criminal from prison without giving him the five objects of the senses: though his body escapes pain, his faculties are not yet delighted. This is only removing suffering, not yet called giving happiness. Again, it is like giving the five objects of the senses to someone in prison: though his ears and eyes are pleased, it is not called removing suffering. Following the specific meaning, each shows one side; so they are explained separately.

Chinese Source

問：此中何意不論喜捨？答：四無量心名雖有四，但是三義。《大經》云「憂畢叉」，畢叉名捨，捨者，兩捨也，即是非慈、非悲，不二之意，不二而二即是慈悲。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: why are sympathetic joy and equanimity not discussed here? Answer: although the four immeasurable minds have four names, they are only three meanings. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says "upekṣā"; upekṣā means equanimity; equanimity is relinquishing both, that is, neither loving-kindness nor compassion — the meaning of nonduality; and not two, yet two, is loving-kindness and compassion.

Chinese Source

喜者，從樂生喜，初欲與樂，眾生苦重，不能得樂，則無所可喜；若拔苦竟，即能得樂，還遂本懷，故樂後加喜。苦後無此，故不開喜。如阿輸加王七日應死，雖有五欲之樂，憂苦切心；又如一身少許痛惱能奪一身之樂，故知苦重不得樂也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for sympathetic joy: joy arises from happiness. At first one wishes to give happiness, but beings' suffering is heavy and they cannot attain happiness, so there is nothing to rejoice in. When the removal of suffering is complete, they can attain happiness, and one's original wish is fulfilled; so joy is added after happiness. There is no such thing after suffering; so joy is not opened out [there]. It is like [the brother of] King Aśoka, who was to die in seven days: though he had the pleasures of the five desires, sorrow and suffering cut into his mind. Again, a little pain in one body can rob the whole body of its pleasure. So we know that when suffering is heavy, one cannot attain happiness.

Chinese Source

問：禪支明喜在前，樂支在後，復云何？答：禪支就從麁入細，此中慶彼得樂，故喜心在後也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: among the factors of dhyāna, joy is explained first and happiness after. What about that? Answer: the factors of dhyāna go from coarse to fine; here one rejoices that the other has attained happiness, so the mind of joy comes after.

Chinese Source

復次，外道修四無量，自證禪定，作想虛運，彼無實益，不能令他拔苦得樂，雖自獲定，虛妄世法，報盡還墮，不免於苦，自、他俱無利益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, non-Buddhists cultivate the four immeasurables and realize dhyāna and concentration for themselves, but they operate emptily with deliberate notions and bring no real benefit to others; they cannot make others free of suffering and attain happiness. Though they themselves attain concentration, it is a false worldly dharma; when the reward is exhausted they fall again and do not escape suffering. Neither self nor others benefit.

Chinese Source

若二乘修四無量，但自拔苦，於他無益，自拔分段未免變易，灰身滅智非究竟樂。今菩薩不爾，非凡夫行、非賢聖行。非凡夫者，不同自受禪樂；非賢聖者，不同自拔於苦。不同自受樂故，即與他樂；不同自拔苦故，即拔他苦。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the two vehicles cultivate the four immeasurables, they only remove their own suffering and do not benefit others; they free themselves from segmented [birth and death] but do not escape [the birth and death of] change; reducing the body to ashes and extinguishing wisdom is not ultimate happiness. The bodhisattva here is not so: his is neither the practice of ordinary people nor the practice of the worthies and sages. Not that of ordinary people: unlike them, he does not himself enjoy the pleasure of dhyāna. Not that of the worthies and sages: unlike them, he does not merely free himself from suffering. Because he does not himself enjoy pleasure, he gives others happiness; because he does not merely free himself from suffering, he removes others' suffering.

Chinese Source

亦是即拔苦是即與樂、即與樂亦即是拔苦，但分別說之，誓願相對，前明拔苦，後明與樂爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

And removing suffering is itself giving happiness, and giving happiness is itself removing suffering; they are only explained separately, matched to the vows: first explaining removing suffering, then giving happiness.

Resolving Doubts: Merit and Wisdom

Chinese Source

料簡福慧者，問：觀音對智稱之而拔苦，普門對福見之而得樂，何也？

Ru-Yi Translation

Resolving doubts about merit and wisdom. Question: "Avalokiteśvara" is matched to wisdom — calling on him removes suffering; "Universal Gate" is matched to merit — seeing him one attains happiness. Why?

Chinese Source

答：智是光明，正治闇惑，惑是生死苦惱，若治闇惑之苦，豈不用智解之光？故稱智慧人名即拔苦也。法是法門，門名能通，通至涅槃安樂之處，初習此法是得樂因，後證此法是得樂果，故對此普門明其與樂也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: wisdom is light; it precisely treats dark delusion, and delusion is the suffering and distress of birth and death. To treat the suffering of dark delusion, how could one not use the light of wise understanding? So calling the name of the person of wisdom removes suffering. The dharma is a dharma-gate; "gate" means what gives passage, passage to the place of nirvāṇa's peace and happiness. First practicing this dharma is the cause of attaining happiness; later realizing this dharma is the fruit of attaining happiness. So, matched to the Universal Gate, it explains giving happiness.

Chinese Source

問：福慧相須，本不相離，若定而無慧者，此定名癡定。譬如盲兒騎瞎馬，必墮坑落塹而無疑也。若慧而無定者，此慧名狂慧，譬如風中然燈搖颺，搖颺照物不了。故知福慧相資，二輪平等，堪能運載也。若爾，何意以智慧拔苦、福德與樂耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: merit and wisdom need each other and are originally inseparable. Concentration without wisdom is called deluded concentration — like a blind child riding a blind horse, certain to fall into a pit or ditch. Wisdom without concentration is called wild wisdom — like a lamp lit in the wind, flickering, which flickers and does not light things clearly. So we know that merit and wisdom support each other; with the two wheels equal, they can carry. If so, why does wisdom remove suffering and merit give happiness?

Chinese Source

自有福德是智慧、智慧是福德，自有福德非智慧、智慧非福德，大小乘皆備四句，如六度菩薩修般若，分閻浮提為七分，此是世智，不能斷惑，此猶屬福德攝，即名此福是智故，此智是福，不斷惑故。

Ru-Yi Translation

There are cases where merit is wisdom and wisdom is merit; there are cases where merit is not wisdom and wisdom is not merit. The great and lesser vehicles both have the four propositions. For example, a bodhisattva of the six perfections cultivating prajñā divides Jambudvīpa into seven parts; this is worldly wisdom and cannot cut off delusion; it still belongs to merit. So this merit is called wisdom, and this wisdom is merit, because it does not cut off delusion.

Chinese Source

若聲聞人智慧能斷，若名智慧非福德，如餓羅漢也。若福德非世智、亦非出世智者，如白象也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The wisdom of a hearer can cut [delusion]; this is called wisdom that is not merit, as with the hungry arhat. Merit that is neither worldly wisdom nor world-transcending wisdom is like the white elephant.

Chinese Source

若大乘四句者，別教地前三十心，行行名福德，慧行名智慧，此慧不能破無明，此慧還屬福德攝，不破無明故，此福是智慧方便治取相故。若地前皆名福德，地上皆名智慧，此智慧非福德，福德非智慧。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the four propositions of the great vehicle: in the thirty minds before the grounds in the distinct teaching, the practice of practices is called merit, and the practice of wisdom is called wisdom. This wisdom cannot break ignorance; this wisdom still belongs to merit, because it does not break ignorance; and this merit is wisdom, because it is a skillful means of wisdom that treats grasping at marks. If everything before the grounds is called merit and everything on the grounds is called wisdom, then this wisdom is not merit and this merit is not wisdom.

Chinese Source

方等般若帶小明大，若帶小，福慧如前四句；明大，福慧如向四句。今此普門名福慧者，福即是慧、慧即是福，福、慧不二，故《大論》云「如是尊妙人則能見般若。」此慧那得無定？得首楞嚴定。何曾無慧？論云「健相三昧，能破彊敵。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Vaipulya and the Prajñā explain the great while carrying the lesser: insofar as they carry the lesser, merit and wisdom are as in the earlier four propositions; insofar as they explain the great, merit and wisdom are as in the four just given. Here, the merit and wisdom called the Universal Gate: merit is wisdom and wisdom is merit; merit and wisdom are not two. So the Great Treatise says: "Such an honored and wondrous person can see prajñā." How could this wisdom lack concentration? He attains the Śūraṅgama concentration. When has it ever lacked wisdom? The Treatise says: "The samādhi of the heroic march can break strong enemies."

Chinese Source

《大經》云「佛性者，有五種名：亦名般若、亦名師子吼、亦名首楞嚴、亦名金剛。」佛性等即是定慧具足之名也。非禪不慧、非慧不禪，禪慧不二，不二而二，分門別說作定慧二解，故《釋論》解般若明十八空、解禪定明百八三昧，此是二說，二即不二。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Buddha-nature has five kinds of names: it is also called prajñā, also called the lion's roar, also called Śūraṅgama, also called vajra." Buddha-nature and the rest are names in which concentration and wisdom are complete. Without dhyāna there is no wisdom; without wisdom there is no dhyāna; dhyāna and wisdom are not two. Not two, yet two: dividing the gates and explaining separately, two explanations are made, concentration and wisdom. So the Commentary on the Treatise explains prajñā by the eighteen emptinesses and explains dhyāna-concentration by the one hundred eight samādhis. These are two explanations; the two are not two.

Resolving Doubts: The True and the Responsive

Chinese Source

料簡真應者，亦有四句之殊，非真非應、應而非真、真而非應、亦真亦應。若非真非應，此就理可解，又就凡夫不見理故，非真無用故非應，此亦可解。

Ru-Yi Translation

Resolving doubts about the true and the responsive. These too differ in four propositions: neither true nor responsive; responsive but not true; true but not responsive; both true and responsive. "Neither true nor responsive" can be understood in terms of principle; it can also be understood in terms of ordinary people, who, not seeing the principle, are not true, and having no function, are not responsive.

Chinese Source

應而非真者，外道亦得五通，同他施化，通論亦得，是應而不得名真。真而非應者，二乘人入真斷結，灰身滅智不能起應，此亦是通論其真爾。亦真亦應者，此則別顯中道為真，即真而論用為應，真應不二，不二而二者，故言真應爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Responsive but not true": non-Buddhists also attain the five powers and transform others in the same way; in general this is acceptable — it is responsive but cannot be called true. "True but not responsive": people of the two vehicles enter the true and cut off the fetters, but reducing the body to ashes and extinguishing wisdom, they cannot give rise to response; this too is the true spoken of in general. "Both true and responsive": this shows specifically the middle way as the true, and the function discussed right in the true as the responsive; the true and the responsive are not two — not two, yet two; hence "the true and the responsive."

Chinese Source

今依文互舉，一往言其真應，前番問答明真身常益，後番問答明應身間益，常間不得相離，二鳥俱游，二往為論，真身亦恒、亦不恒，應身亦間、亦不間。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, following the text, they are raised alternately. At first look, as to the true and the responsive: the first round of question and answer explains the constant benefit of the true body, and the second round the intermittent benefit of the responsive body; the constant and the intermittent cannot be separated, like two birds flying together. Discussed at second look, the true body is both constant and not constant, and the responsive body is both intermittent and not intermittent.

Chinese Source

若小乘明義，例如善吉石窟觀空見佛法身，蓮華尼則不見，此豈非小乘中真身恒益、不恒益義？丈六之應亦有見、不見，此豈非應身有間、有不間義？

Ru-Yi Translation

In the lesser vehicle's explanation: for example, Subhūti, contemplating emptiness in his stone cave, saw the Buddha's Dharma-body, while the nun Utpalavarṇā did not. Is this not the meaning, within the lesser vehicle, of the true body's benefit being constant and not constant? The sixteen-foot responsive [body] too was both seen and not seen. Is this not the meaning of the responsive body being both intermittent and not intermittent?

Chinese Source

大乘法身亦爾，於理為恒益、於情為不恒益；應身亦爾，此緣滅、彼緣興，無有斷絕是不間義，同質異見是其間義。而今分別，一往前問答屬恒益，後問答屬不恒益也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Dharma-body of the great vehicle is likewise: in principle its benefit is constant; to feeling, its benefit is not constant. The responsive body is likewise: when conditions here cease, conditions there arise, without interruption — this is the meaning of not intermittent; the same substance seen differently — this is the meaning of intermittent. In the present distinction, at first look, the first question and answer belongs to constant benefit and the second to benefit that is not constant.

Resolving Doubts: The Medicine Tree and the Jewel; Hidden and Manifest; the Provisional and the Real

Chinese Source

料簡藥珠二身者，藥有差病拔苦之功，亦有全身增命致寶之用，故經云「若全身命，便為已得玩好之具也。」如意珠王非但雨寶，亦能除病，大施太子入海得珠，還治父母眼，《大品》云「若人眼痛，珠著身上，病即除愈。」故知通具二義。若別據一邊，約除患以譬藥，證樂以況珠爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Resolving doubts about the two bodies, medicine [tree] and jewel. Medicine has the power to cure illness and remove suffering, and also the use of preserving the body, lengthening life, and bringing treasure; so a sūtra says: "If you preserve your body and life, you have already obtained the things of pleasure." The wish-fulfilling jewel king not only rains treasure but can also remove illness: Prince Great Giving entered the sea, obtained the jewel, and returned to cure his parents' eyes; the Large [Prajñāpāramitā] says: "If a person's eyes hurt, when the jewel is placed on the body, the illness is cured." So we know that in general each has both meanings. Specifically, based on one side, removing affliction is likened to medicine, and realizing happiness is compared to the jewel.

Chinese Source

料簡冥顯兩益，凡有三十六句。料簡權實二智者，前問答，實智照真而眾生得脫，權智照假而眾生得度。度為度權，亦度於實；脫為脫真，亦脫於假。

Ru-Yi Translation

Resolving doubts about the two benefits, hidden and manifest: there are thirty-six propositions in all. Resolving doubts about the two wisdoms, provisional and real: in the first question and answer, real wisdom illuminates the true and beings are freed; provisional wisdom illuminates the provisional and beings are delivered. Delivering is delivering into the provisional, and also delivering into the real; freeing is freeing into the true, and also freeing into the provisional.

Chinese Source

答此亦具四句，或因真智解脫於權，七難消除，二求願滿是也；或因真智解脫於實，三毒皆離是也；或因權智得度於實，三十三身得度是也；或因權智得度於權，於怖畏急難之中得無畏是也。或二俱度脫、或二俱不度不脫，今依文判，互出一邊，前文脫權、後文度實。

Ru-Yi Translation

The answer here also has four propositions. Some, through true wisdom, are liberated into the provisional — the seven calamities are dispelled and the two wishes fulfilled. Some, through true wisdom, are liberated into the real — they are free of all three poisons. Some, through provisional wisdom, are delivered into the real — those delivered by the thirty-three bodies. Some, through provisional wisdom, are delivered into the provisional — those who obtain fearlessness in the midst of fear and urgent danger. Some are both delivered and freed in both ways; some are neither delivered nor freed. Here, judging by the text, each brings out one side: the earlier passage frees into the provisional, the later delivers into the real.

Resolving Doubts: Origin and Trace

Chinese Source

料簡本迹者，通論本迹俱能拔苦與樂，故〈壽量〉云「聞佛壽無量，得清淨無漏無量之果報。」即是從本得樂。《請觀音》云「或遊戲地獄，大悲代受苦。」此是從迹拔苦。

Ru-Yi Translation

Resolving doubts about origin and trace. In general, origin and trace can both remove suffering and give happiness. So the [chapter on the] Lifespan says: "Hearing that the Buddha's lifespan is immeasurable, they obtain the pure, uncontaminated, immeasurable reward" — this is attaining happiness from the origin. The Invitation to Avalokiteśvara says: "Or he sports in the hells, and with great compassion suffers in their place" — this is removing suffering from the trace.

Chinese Source

眾生不達本源，後流轉苦惱，若識本理即於苦而得解脫也。眾生若不見迹中施化，不能三業種福，則無功德之因，焉致樂果？非本無以垂迹，非迹無以顯本，前問答是明迹本，後問答是明本迹。

Ru-Yi Translation

Beings who do not penetrate the original source thereafter flow on in suffering and distress; if they recognize the original principle, they are liberated right in suffering. If beings did not see the transformation bestowed in the trace, they could not plant merit with the three kinds of action, and so would have no cause of merit — how could they arrive at the fruit of happiness? Without the origin there is nothing from which to let fall the trace; without the trace there is nothing by which to reveal the origin. The first question and answer explains trace and origin; the second explains origin and trace.

Chinese Source

問：本迹與真應云何異？答：真應就一世橫辯，如諸經所明。本迹就三世豎論，如〈壽量〉所說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: how do origin and trace differ from the true and the responsive? Answer: the true and the responsive are discussed horizontally in terms of one lifetime, as explained in the various sūtras. Origin and trace are discussed vertically in terms of the three times, as taught in the [chapter on the] Lifespan.

Resolving Doubts: The Conditioning and the Revealing — Inherent Good and Inherent Evil

Chinese Source

料簡緣了者，問：緣了既有性德善，亦有性德惡否？答：具。

Ru-Yi Translation

Resolving doubts about the conditioning and the revealing. Question: since the conditioning and the revealing have the good of inherent virtue, do they also have the evil of inherent virtue? Answer: they contain it.

Chinese Source

問：闡提與佛斷，何等善惡？答：闡提斷修善盡，但性善在；佛斷修惡盡，但性惡在。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: what good and evil have the icchantika and the Buddha cut off? Answer: the icchantika has cut off cultivated good completely, but inherent good remains; the Buddha has cut off cultivated evil completely, but inherent evil remains.

Chinese Source

問：性德善惡何不可斷？答：性之善惡但是善惡之法門，性不可改，歷三世無誰能毀，復不可斷壞。譬如魔雖燒經，何能令性善法門盡？縱令佛燒惡譜，亦不能令惡法門盡。如秦焚典坑儒，豈能令善惡斷盡耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: why can the good and evil of inherent virtue not be cut off? Answer: the good and evil of the nature are simply the dharma-gates of good and evil. The nature cannot be changed; through the three times no one can destroy it, nor can it be cut off or broken. It is like Māra burning the scriptures: how could he make the dharma-gates of inherent good come to an end? Even if the Buddha burned the records of evil, he could not make the dharma-gates of evil come to an end. As when Qin burned the books and buried the scholars — could that make good and evil come completely to an end?

Chinese Source

問：闡提不斷性善，還能令修善起；佛不斷性惡，還令修惡起耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: since the icchantika does not cut off inherent good, it can again cause cultivated good to arise. Since the Buddha does not cut off inherent evil, does it again cause cultivated evil to arise?

Chinese Source

答：闡提既不達性善，以不達故還為善所染，修善得起，廣治諸惡。佛雖不斷性惡，而能達於惡，以達惡故於惡自在，故不為惡所染，修惡不得起，故佛永無復惡，以自在故，廣用諸惡法門化度眾生，終日用之、終日不染，不染故不起，那得以闡提為例耶？若闡提能達此善惡，則不復名為一闡提也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: since the icchantika does not penetrate inherent good, because of not penetrating it he is again stained by good; cultivated good can arise, and it treats all evils broadly. Although the Buddha does not cut off inherent evil, he can penetrate evil; because he penetrates evil, he is free in evil, and so is not stained by evil; cultivated evil cannot arise. So the Buddha never again has evil. Because he is free, he broadly uses the dharma-gates of evil to transform and deliver beings; he uses them all day and all day is not stained; because he is not stained, [evil] does not arise. How could the icchantika be taken as a parallel? If the icchantika could penetrate this good and evil, he would no longer be called an icchantika.

Chinese Source

若依他人明闡提斷善盡，為阿梨耶識所熏更能起善，梨耶即是無記無明，善惡依持為一切種子，闡提不斷無明無記故還生善。佛斷無記無明盡，無所可熏，故惡不復還生；若欲以惡化物，但作神通變現度眾生爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Others explain that the icchantika has cut off good completely, but, perfumed by the ālaya consciousness, can again give rise to good. The ālaya is morally indeterminate ignorance, on which good and evil depend, the seed of everything; because the icchantika does not cut off indeterminate ignorance, good arises again. The Buddha has cut off indeterminate ignorance completely, and there is nothing to be perfumed, so evil does not arise again; if he wishes to transform beings by means of evil, he merely makes transformations by spiritual power to deliver beings.

Chinese Source

問：若佛地斷惡盡，作神通以惡化物者，此作意方能起惡。如人畫諸色像，非是任運，如明鏡不動，色像自形，可是不可思議理能應惡，若作意者與外道何異？

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: if at the stage of buddhahood evil is cut off completely, and [the Buddha] makes spiritual powers to transform beings by means of evil, then it is only by deliberate effort that he can give rise to evil. It is like a person painting forms and images — not spontaneous. [Whereas] like a bright mirror that does not move while forms and images appear of themselves — that would be the inconceivable principle able to respond with evil. If it is by deliberate effort, how is it different from non-Buddhists?

Chinese Source

今明闡提不斷性德之善，遇緣善發；佛亦不斷性惡，機緣所激，慈力所熏，入阿鼻同一切惡事化眾生。以有性惡故名不斷，無復修惡名不常，若修性俱盡則是斷，不得為不斷不常。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here it is explained that the icchantika does not cut off the good of inherent virtue, and when he meets conditions, good arises. The Buddha likewise does not cut off inherent evil; stirred by capacities and conditions and perfumed by the power of loving-kindness, he enters Avīci and joins in all evil affairs to transform beings. Because there is inherent evil, it is called not cut off; because there is no more cultivated evil, it is called not permanent. If both cultivated and inherent were exhausted, that would be annihilation, and it could not be "neither annihilated nor permanent."

Chinese Source

闡提亦爾，性善不斷還生善根。如來性惡不斷，還能起惡，雖起於惡，而是解心無染，通達惡際即是實際，能以五逆相而得解脫，亦不縛不脫，行於非道，通達佛道；闡提染而不達，與此為異也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The icchantika is likewise: inherent good is not cut off, and good roots arise again. The Tathāgata's inherent evil is not cut off, and he can again give rise to evil; though he gives rise to evil, it is with an understanding mind, without stain. Penetrating the limit of evil is the limit of reality; he can attain liberation by way of the marks of the five heinous deeds, neither bound nor freed; practicing what is not the path, he penetrates the Buddha path. The icchantika is stained and does not penetrate; that is the difference.

Resolving Doubts: Wisdom and Severance

Chinese Source

料簡智斷者，此是一法異名，不得相離，如人一體，何故從智拔苦、從斷與樂？然而慧解之心稱智，無縛礙身稱斷。

Ru-Yi Translation

Resolving doubts about wisdom and severance. These are different names of one dharma and cannot be separated, like the one body of a person. Why then does wisdom remove suffering and severance give happiness? Rather, the mind of wise understanding is called wisdom, and the body without bondage or obstruction is called severance.

Chinese Source

譬如人被縛，運力屬智，肅然附外屬斷；運力屬心，故名智慧莊嚴；附斷體散屬色身，名福德莊嚴。今經文言說不得一時，故互舉智斷。若深得此十義意者，解一千從，廣釋觀世音普門義則不可盡也。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is like a person who is bound: exerting strength belongs to wisdom; [the bonds] falling away outside belongs to severance. Exerting strength belongs to the mind, so it is called the adornment of wisdom; [the bonds] falling away and the body being loosened belongs to the form-body, and is called the adornment of merit. In the sūtra's words they cannot be spoken at the same time, so wisdom and severance are raised alternately. If one deeply grasps the meaning of these ten, understand one and a thousand follow; explaining the meaning of "Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate" at length could never be exhausted.

The Specific Explanation: Object and Wisdom Outside the Principle

Chinese Source

第二別釋名者，為二：先明觀世音、次明普門。以何因緣名觀世音？通釋如前；別者則以境智因緣故名觀世音。云何境智？境智有二：一思議境智、二不思議境智。思議境智又二：一約理外、二約理內。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the specific explanation of the name, in two parts: first, explaining "Avalokiteśvara"; next, explaining "Universal Gate." For what causes and conditions is he named Avalokiteśvara? The general explanation is as above. Specifically, he is named Avalokiteśvara because of the causes and conditions of object and wisdom. What are object and wisdom? Object and wisdom are of two kinds: first, conceivable object and wisdom; second, inconceivable object and wisdom. Conceivable object and wisdom are again of two kinds: first, outside the principle; second, within the principle.

Chinese Source

理外為四：一天然境智，只問此境為當，由境故境、由智故境；此智為當，由智故智、由境故智。若由境故境，此境是境，境即自生境；若智由智故智，亦是自生智，自生名性自爾，非佛、天、人所作，照與不照恒是境智，故名天然境智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Outside the principle there are four. First, naturally existing object and wisdom. One simply asks: is this object an object because of the object, or an object because of wisdom? Is this wisdom wisdom because of wisdom, or wisdom because of the object? If the object is an object because of the object, this object is an object, and the object is self-produced. If wisdom is wisdom because of wisdom, it is likewise self-produced wisdom. Self-produced means its nature is so of itself, not made by buddhas, gods, or humans; illuminating or not illuminating, they are always object and wisdom. So it is called naturally existing object and wisdom.

Chinese Source

二明相待者，若境不自境，因智故境；智不自智，因境故智，此即他生義。何故爾？境自生境，既稱為自，以境望智，智即是他。今境從智生，豈非他境？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining relative [object and wisdom]. If the object is not an object of itself but an object because of wisdom, and wisdom is not wisdom of itself but wisdom because of the object, this is the meaning of produced from other. Why? The object producing itself as object is called "self"; looking from the object toward wisdom, wisdom is "other." Now if the object is produced from wisdom, is it not an object [produced] from other?

Chinese Source

智亦如是，故名相待。次明因緣境智者，若境不由智故境，亦不由境故境，智境因緣故境；智亦如是，此即境智因緣共生義。共生有二過，墮自他性中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Wisdom is likewise; so it is called relative. Next, explaining object and wisdom by causes and conditions: if the object is an object neither because of wisdom nor because of the object, but because of the causes and conditions of wisdom and object; and so too with wisdom — this is the meaning of object and wisdom jointly produced by causes and conditions. Joint production has two faults: it falls into both self-nature and other-nature.

Chinese Source

次絕待明境智者，非境非智而說境智，此即離境離智，無因緣而辯境智者，此是無因緣絕待，從因緣尚不可得，何況無因緣？

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining object and wisdom as absolute: speaking of object and wisdom as neither object nor wisdom — this is apart from object and apart from wisdom; discussing object and wisdom without causes and conditions — this is the absolute without causes and conditions. If they cannot be found even from causes and conditions, how much less without causes and conditions?

Chinese Source

一往謂絕理而窮之，不成絕待，並是理外行心妄想推計，故《中論》云「諸法不自生，亦不從他生，不共不無因，是故說無生。」那得如前四種計執是實，餘妄語。

Ru-Yi Translation

At first look, one calls it cutting off principle; but when examined to the end, it does not amount to the absolute. All are deluded reckonings of a mind moving outside the principle. So the Middle Treatise says: "Dharmas are not produced from themselves, nor from another, nor from both, nor without cause; therefore we say they are unproduced." How could one cling to the four reckonings above as "this is real, the rest is false"?

Chinese Source

性實之執，見愛生著，九十八使，苦集浩然，流轉不息，云何執此而生苦集？隨執一種境智謂以為是，隨順讚歎，心則愛著而生歡喜，即是貪使；若人違逆責毀，心則忿怒而生瞋恚，即是瞋使。

Ru-Yi Translation

From clinging to a real nature, views and craving produce attachment; the ninety-eight latent tendencies, suffering and origination vast, flowing on without rest. How does clinging to this produce suffering and origination? Clinging to any one kind of object and wisdom as right, when someone agrees and praises it, the mind clings with craving and delights — that is the latent tendency of greed. When someone opposes and criticizes it, the mind becomes angry and resentful — that is the latent tendency of anger.

Chinese Source

貪、恚既起，豈非癡使？我解此境智，他所不解，以其所執矜傲於人，豈非慢使？既執此為是，今雖無疑，後當大疑，豈非疑使？我知解此法，法中計我，豈非身見？六十二見隨墮一邊，豈非邊見？如此妄執不當道理，豈非邪見？執此是實，計為涅槃，豈非見取果盜？謂此為道，依之進行，豈非戒取因盜？

Ru-Yi Translation

Once greed and anger have arisen, is that not the latent tendency of delusion? "I understand this object and wisdom, which others do not understand" — being proud toward others because of what one clings to, is that not the latent tendency of conceit? Since one clings to this as right, though now without doubt, later there will be great doubt — is that not the latent tendency of doubt? "I know and understand this dharma" — reckoning a self within the dharma, is that not the view of a body? Falling to one side among the sixty-two views, is that not the view of extremes? Such deluded clinging, not in accord with the principle, is that not wrong view? Clinging to this as real and reckoning it as nirvāṇa, is that not attachment to views, stealing the fruit? Calling this the path and advancing on its basis, is that not attachment to precepts, stealing the cause?

Chinese Source

十使宛然，皆從所執境智上起，將此歷三界四諦則有八十八使，就思惟歷三界則有九十八使，此則集諦結業，顛倒浩然，方招苦果，生死不絕。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten latent tendencies are all present, all arising from the object and wisdom one clings to. Taken through the Four Noble Truths of the three realms, there are eighty-eight latent tendencies; with [the delusions of] thought through the three realms, there are ninety-eight. This is the truth of origination, fetters and karma, inversions vast, which then bring the fruit of suffering, birth and death without end.

Chinese Source

於其境智不識苦集，何處有道滅？既不識四諦，則破世間、出世間因果，無世、出世法，故無法寶；不識出世果，無佛寶；不識出世因，無僧寶，賢聖之義，一切俱失。

Ru-Yi Translation

If, in that object and wisdom, one does not recognize suffering and origination, where would there be the path and cessation? Since one does not recognize the Four Noble Truths, one destroys worldly and world-transcending cause and effect; there are no worldly or world-transcending dharmas, so there is no Dharma-jewel; not recognizing the world-transcending fruit, there is no Buddha-jewel; not recognizing the world-transcending cause, there is no Saṃgha-jewel; the meaning of the worthies and sages is entirely lost.

Chinese Source

若作如此執自生境智者，只是結構生死，增長結業，過患甚多。若非理外境智，更將何等為理外耶？故《大論》云「凡夫三種語，見、慢、名字；聖人，但一種語，名字。」

Ru-Yi Translation

One who clings in this way to self-produced object and wisdom only constructs birth and death and increases fetters and karma; the faults are very many. If this is not object and wisdom outside the principle, what else would be outside the principle? So the Great Treatise says: "Ordinary people have three kinds of speech: views, conceit, and names; noble ones have only one kind of speech: names."

Chinese Source

今凡夫見慢取著，謬用佛語，介爾取著，乖理成諍。雖傍經論，引證文字，如蟲蝕木，偶得成字；尋其內心，實不能解是字非字，口言境智，不解境智，以不解故，如服甘露，則以境智起見，傷命早夭，故為龍樹所破，今不取此為境智以釋觀世音。自生境智既爾，餘三句亦然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now ordinary people, grasping with views and conceit, misuse the Buddha's words; with the slightest grasping, they go against principle and make disputes. Though they lean on the sūtras and treatises and cite texts, it is like insects eating wood and happening to form characters; when one examines their inner minds, they truly cannot understand that the characters are not characters. With the mouth they speak of object and wisdom, but they do not understand object and wisdom. Because they do not understand, it is like taking ambrosia [wrongly]: they give rise to views with object and wisdom, harm their lives, and die early. So they are refuted by Nāgārjuna, and here this is not taken as the object and wisdom by which to explain "Avalokiteśvara." As with self-produced object and wisdom, so with the other three propositions.

Object and Wisdom Within the Principle, and the Inconceivable

Chinese Source

二明思議理內境智者，亦作上四門，名字雖同，觀智淳熟，不生執見，畢故不造新，成方便道，發生煖頂乃至十六心眼智明覺，豁然得悟，破諸見惑，與理相應。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining conceivable object and wisdom within the principle. One also makes the four gates above; although the names are the same, contemplative wisdom is pure and ripe, does not give rise to clinging views, finishes off the old and does not create the new, and accomplishes the path of skillful means. Warmth and summit arise, up to the eye, knowledge, clarity, and awakening of the sixteen minds; suddenly one is awakened, breaks the delusions of views, and accords with the principle.

Chinese Source

譬如盲人金錍抉膜，灼然不謬，此之真觀名之為智，所照之理名之為境，以發無漏故稱理內境智；雖見此理，終是作意入真，故名思議境智也。今明觀世音，亦不從此境智因緣得名也。

Ru-Yi Translation

It is like a blind person whose membrane is cut away with the golden probe: clearly and without error. This true contemplation is called wisdom, and the principle illuminated is called the object; because the uncontaminated arises, it is called object and wisdom within the principle. But although one sees this principle, in the end one enters the true by deliberate effort; so it is called conceivable object and wisdom. In explaining "Avalokiteśvara" here, the name is also not taken from the causes and conditions of this object and wisdom.

Chinese Source

次明不思議境智者，若自他共無因等四句俱非境智者，今諸經論所明，或從自生、他生、共生、無因等，若不爾者，云何辯境智耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining inconceivable object and wisdom. [Question:] If the four propositions — self, other, both, and without cause — are all not object and wisdom, [yet] the sūtras and treatises explain [things] as produced from self, from other, from both, or without cause, and so on — if it were not so, how would they discuss object and wisdom?

Chinese Source

答：經中所明，皆是四悉檀赴緣，假名字說，無四性執。若人樂聞自生境智，即說境是自境、智是自智，以赴其欣欲之心；或時宜聞自境、自智，聞必生善；或時對治，說自生境智，說必破惑；有時說此，令即悟道。

Ru-Yi Translation

Answer: what the sūtras explain is all the four siddhāntas meeting conditions, spoken with provisional names, without clinging to the four natures. If a person delights in hearing of self-produced object and wisdom, it is said that the object is a self-object and wisdom is self-wisdom, to meet his mind of delight and desire. Sometimes it is fitting for him to hear of self-object and self-wisdom, and hearing it, good will surely arise. Sometimes, as a remedy, self-produced object and wisdom are taught, and the teaching will surely break delusion. Sometimes this is taught so that he awakens to the path at once.

Chinese Source

若無四悉檀益，諸佛如來不空說法，雖作四說，無四種執，無執故無見愛，眾生聞者如快馬見鞭影，即破惑入道，故名為智，此智所照名之為境。如是通達則識苦集道滅，三寶四諦宛然具足。

Ru-Yi Translation

Without the benefit of the four siddhāntas, the buddhas, the tathāgatas, would not teach the Dharma in vain. Though making four explanations, there is no clinging to four; because there is no clinging, there are no views or craving. Beings who hear it are like a fine horse seeing the shadow of the whip: they break delusion and enter the path at once. So it is called wisdom, and what this wisdom illuminates is called the object. Penetrating in this way, one recognizes suffering, origination, the path, and cessation; the Three Jewels and the Four Noble Truths are fully present.

Chinese Source

若以智照境，入空取證，成真諦理內思議境智，如前說。若不以果為證，知此境智但有名字，名為境智。是字不在內、外、中間，是字不住亦不不住，是字無所有故。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one illuminates the object with wisdom, enters emptiness, and takes realization, it becomes conceivable object and wisdom within the principle of the real truth, as explained above. If one does not take the fruit as realization, one knows that this object and wisdom are only names, called object and wisdom. These words are not inside, outside, or in between; these words neither abide nor do not abide, because these words are without anything.

Chinese Source

雖作四句明境智，實不分別四句境智；雖作四句聞境智，實不得四句境智；雖體達四句境智，實不作四句思量境智。言語道斷，心行處滅，不可四句思惟圖度，故名不思議境智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Though making four propositions to explain object and wisdom, one does not really discriminate object and wisdom by the four propositions; though making four propositions in hearing of object and wisdom, one does not really find object and wisdom in the four propositions; though embodying and penetrating object and wisdom of the four propositions, one does not really think about object and wisdom with the four propositions. The path of language is cut off and the place of the mind's movement is extinguished; it cannot be thought about or measured with the four propositions. So it is called inconceivable object and wisdom.

Chinese Source

《金光明》云「不思議智照不思議智境。」此具如《大本玄義》境智妙中廣說。龍樹先破一異時方，然後釋如是我聞等義，今類此先破理外境智，後明不思議四悉檀，悉檀義如《大本玄義》。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Golden Light says: "Inconceivable wisdom illuminates the inconceivable object of wisdom." This is explained in full in the wondrous of the object and of wisdom in the Profound Meaning of the great text. Nāgārjuna first refuted one and different, time and place, and then explained the meaning of "Thus have I heard" and the rest; here, by analogy, it first refutes object and wisdom outside the principle and then explains the inconceivable four siddhāntas. The meaning of the siddhāntas is as in the Profound Meaning of the great text.

Chinese Source

夫依名字為便，應先明觀智，次辯世境之音；若解義為便，前明世境，次辯觀智。如先有境可得論觀，若未有境，何所可觀？譬如境鼓後方映擊，今從義便，先明世音，後論觀智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

For convenience of wording, one should first explain the contemplating wisdom and then discuss the sounds of the world-object. For convenience of understanding the meaning, one first explains the world-object and then discusses the contemplating wisdom. If there is first an object, contemplation can be discussed; if there is no object yet, what is there to contemplate? It is like an object-drum, which only then is struck. Here, following convenience of meaning, it first explains "the sounds of the world" and then discusses the contemplating wisdom.

"The Sounds of the World"

Chinese Source

世者為三：一五陰世間、二眾生世間、三國土世間。既有實法，即有假人，假實正成即有依報，故名三種世間也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"World" is of three kinds: first, the world of the five aggregates; second, the world of sentient beings; third, the world of lands. Since there are real dharmas, there is a provisional person; once the provisional and the real are formed, there is the dependent reward. So they are called the three kinds of world.

Chinese Source

世是隔別，即十法界之世，亦是十種五陰、十種假名、十種依報，隔別不同，故名為世也。間是間差，三十種世間差別，不相謬亂，故名為間。各各有因、各各有果，故名為法。各各有界畔分齊，故名為界。

Ru-Yi Translation

"World" (世) means separate and distinct: the worlds of the ten dharma-realms, which are also ten kinds of five aggregates, ten kinds of provisional names, and ten kinds of dependent reward, separate and distinct and unlike; hence they are called "world." "-Between" (間) means intervals and differences: the thirty kinds of world are different and not confused with one another; hence "between." Each has its cause and each its fruit; hence "dharma." Each has its boundaries and divisions; hence "realm."

Chinese Source

今就一法界復有十法，所謂如是相性究竟等，十界即有百法，十界相互則有千法，如是等法皆是因緣生法，六道是惑因緣法、四聖是解因緣法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, each dharma-realm again has ten dharmas, namely the suchness of mark, nature, and so on through ultimate [consistency]; the ten realms then have a hundred dharmas; the ten realms mutually [containing one another] have a thousand dharmas. All such dharmas are dharmas produced by causes and conditions: the six paths are dharmas of the causes and conditions of delusion, and the four noble ones are dharmas of the causes and conditions of understanding.

Chinese Source

《大經》云「無漏亦有因緣，因滅無明即是三菩提燈。」是諸因緣法即是三諦，「因緣所生法，我說即是空，亦名為假名，亦名中道義」，故明十種法界、三十種世間即是所觀之境也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "The uncontaminated also has causes and conditions; because ignorance ceases, it is the lamp of [samyak]saṃbodhi." These dharmas of causes and conditions are the three truths: "Dharmas produced by causes and conditions I declare to be empty; they are also provisional names; this is also the meaning of the middle way." So it is explained that the ten kinds of dharma-realms and thirty kinds of world are the object contemplated.

Chinese Source

此境復為二：所謂自、他。他者，謂眾生、佛；自者，即心而具。如《華嚴》云「心如工畫師，造種種五陰，一切世間中，莫不由心造。」

Ru-Yi Translation

This object is again of two kinds, namely self and other. "Other" means beings and buddhas; "self" is the mind, which contains [them]. As the Avataṃsaka says: "The mind is like a skilled painter, creating the various five aggregates; in all the world, there is nothing not created by the mind."

Chinese Source

問：自、他那得各具十法界？答：觀身實相，觀佛亦然，《華嚴》云「心然佛亦然，心、佛及眾生，是三無差別」，豈不各各具三諦境耶？音者，即十法界口業之機也。界既不同，音亦有異。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: how can self and other each contain the ten dharma-realms? Answer: "As one contemplates the real mark of the body, so one contemplates the Buddha." The Avataṃsaka says: "As the mind is, so is the Buddha; mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without distinction." Do they not each contain the object of the three truths? "Sounds" are the capacities of the verbal action of the ten dharma-realms. Since the realms differ, the sounds also differ.

Why "Sounds"?

Chinese Source

問眾生各有三業，何意但觀音然？通論皆得。常念恭敬，得離三毒，即是觀世音；禮拜供養，所求願滿，即是觀世身。而今但言觀世音者，舊釋此義為六：

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: beings each have three kinds of action; why does he contemplate only sound? In general, all are possible. "Constantly being mindful and reverent, one is freed from the three poisons" — that is contemplating the world's [mind]; "paying homage and making offerings, one's wishes are fulfilled" — that is contemplating the world's bodies. But here it says only "contemplating the sounds of the world." The old explanations give six meanings for this.

Chinese Source

一趣立者，諸名不可累出，舉一趣以標名，「若稱為觀世身者」已，復還問此言「何意不名觀世音」，此則非問。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, establishing [the name] at random: the names cannot all be listed, so one is taken up at random to mark the name. "If it were called 'contemplating the world's bodies,'" one would then ask in turn, "Why is it not named 'contemplating the sounds of the world'?" — this is no question at all.

Chinese Source

二隨俗者，釋迦所說以音聲為佛事，故言觀世音，若遊諸國土隨彼所宜。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, following convention: in Śākyamuni's teaching, sound is used to do the Buddha's work; hence "contemplating the sounds of the world." When he travels to other lands, it is according to what is fitting there.

Chinese Source

三互舉者，能觀所觀，所觀即眾生色心也，今從能觀故但言觀。能聞所聞，能聞是聖人耳識，所聞是眾生音聲，今取所聞之音聲。舉所聞得能聞，舉能觀得所觀，從此為名故言觀世音。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, raising alternately: there is the contemplating and the contemplated; the contemplated is the form and mind of beings; here, following the contemplating, it says only "contemplating." There is the hearing and the heard; the hearing is the noble one's ear-consciousness, and the heard is the voices of beings; here the heard, the voices, is taken. Raising the heard, one gets the hearing; raising the contemplating, one gets the contemplated. Naming from these, it says "contemplating the sounds of the world."

Chinese Source

舊問：能所既爾，何不取所觀之色心、能聞之耳識以標名稱，為聞色心菩薩耶？舊答云：菩薩一觀於色心，此是應廣；眾生之一音，此是機狹。若從難者，則機有兩字，應但一字，便是應狹機廣，故不如所難。

Ru-Yi Translation

The old question: since subject and object are so, why not take the contemplated form and mind and the hearing ear-consciousness to mark the name, as "the bodhisattva who hears form and mind"? The old answer: the bodhisattva's single contemplation of form and mind — this is the response being broad; the single sound of beings — this is the capacity being narrow. If it followed the objection, the capacity would have two words and the response only one; the response would be narrow and the capacity broad. So it is not as the objection has it.

Chinese Source

今更作難，此語應從義理，那得逐字？菩薩以能觀色心，何意不能觀音聲？眾生何意但以聲感色，心不能感耶？若其俱感、俱應，此逐字為觀，則感應齊等，若為判其廣狹？

Ru-Yi Translation

Now I raise a further objection: these words should follow the meaning; how can one chase the characters? If the bodhisattva can contemplate form and mind, why can he not contemplate sounds? Why can beings appeal only with sound and not with form and mind? If all appeal and all respond, and one contemplates by chasing the characters, then appeal and response are equal; how could one judge one broad and the other narrow?

Chinese Source

今不作此明，互舉凡聖感應皆通三業，而聖人與意、凡夫與聲，故言觀世音爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here it is not explained in that way. Raising them alternately, the appeal of the ordinary and the response of the noble both extend to the three kinds of action; but the noble one is given the mind and the ordinary person the voice; hence "contemplating the sounds of the world."

Chinese Source

四義攝者，如發聲必先假意，氣觸脣口，其音能出；口業若成，則攝得身意。若觀於口業，亦攝得身意，觀餘不爾，故言義攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, inclusion by meaning: to utter a sound, one must first rely on the mind; breath touches the lips and mouth, and then the sound can come out. Once verbal action is accomplished, it includes the body and mind. If one contemplates verbal action, one also includes body and mind; contemplating the others is not so. Hence "inclusion by meaning."

Chinese Source

五隱顯者，身雖禮拜、意雖存想，未知歸趣何等？故名隱。若口音宣暢，事義則彰，故名顯。舉顯沒隱，故言觀世音。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, hidden and manifest: although the body pays homage and the mind holds a thought, one does not yet know to whom it is directed; so it is called hidden. If the voice of the mouth proclaims it clearly, the matter and its meaning are displayed; so it is called manifest. Raising the manifest and omitting the hidden, it says "contemplating the sounds of the world."

Chinese Source

六難易者，臨危在厄，意則十念難成，身則拜跪遲鈍，口唱為急故成機，從易受名也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, difficult and easy: facing danger and in distress, it is hard for the mind to complete ten thoughts, and for the body, bowing and kneeling are slow; calling out with the mouth is quickest, so it forms the capacity. The name is taken from the easy.

Chinese Source

又，第六為有緣，觀音昔為凡夫，居茲忍界，見苦發誓；今生西方，多還此土，既有誓緣，急須稱名。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, the sixth is having a connection: Avalokiteśvara was formerly an ordinary person dwelling in this Sahā world, who saw suffering and made vows; now born in the West, he often returns to this land. Since there is a connection through his vows, one urgently needs to call his name.

Chinese Source

今明若如前六義，皆遍有所舉。若依《釋論》其義即圓。何以故？出入息是身行、覺觀是口行、受為心行，心覺觀故尚具三業，何況發音成聲而不備三業耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

I now explain: if it is as in the six meanings above, each raises something one-sidedly. If one relies on the Commentary on the Treatise, the meaning is perfect. Why? The breath going out and coming in is bodily formation; initial thought and sustained reflection are verbal formation; sensation is mental formation. If the mind's initial thought and sustained reflection already contain the three kinds of action, how much more does uttering a voice and forming a sound contain all three?

Chinese Source

但舉一觀即備三應、但舉一音即備三機，而凡情謂聲彊智利，逐物標名；圓義往推，悉皆具足。

Ru-Yi Translation

Raising just one contemplation contains the three responses; raising just one sound contains the three capacities. Ordinary feeling regards sound as strong and wisdom as sharp, and marks the name according to things; but pushed forward by the perfect meaning, all are fully present.

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

"A Dharma-prince named Universal Gate" (普門法王子). A bodhisattva of that name appears in Vaipulya sūtras; the objection is that there "Universal Gate" names a person, not a dharma.

The four propositions on person and dharma. The text distinguishes the sequential reading (person and dharma differ until the real mark is realized) from the non-sequential (they are one from the start).

Śāriputra "foremost in wisdom." Each of the Buddha's great disciples is called "foremost" in one quality; the point is that all had wisdom, but each is named for his own gate of entry. "The [other] mental factors are like the back of the knife": wisdom (慧數) is one of the mental factors (數, older term for 心所).

"Upekṣā" (憂畢叉). The text glosses the Sanskrit as "relinquishing both," that is, neither loving-kindness nor compassion.

[The brother of] King Aśoka. In the Aśoka legend, the king's brother, sentenced to die in seven days and given every pleasure meanwhile, cannot enjoy it for fear. The Chinese says only "King Aśoka"; the bracketed words follow the story.

"The hungry arhat" and "the white elephant." Stock examples: an arhat with wisdom but no merit, who goes hungry on almsround; and a richly adorned elephant with merit but no wisdom.

"Divides Jambudvīpa into seven parts." A worldly skill attributed to the bodhisattva Mahāgovinda (or to a minister) who divided the land fairly; given as an example of worldly wisdom.

"The samādhi of the heroic march" (健相三昧). A rendering of Śūraṅgama; the quotation is from the Great Treatise as given.

"Buddha-nature has five kinds of names." The Nirvāṇa Sūtra lists five; the quotation here gives four (prajñā, lion's roar, Śūraṅgama, vajra); the fifth, the one-vehicle (一乘), is omitted in the Chinese and is not supplied.

The Commentary on the Treatise (釋論). Another name for the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra.

Subhūti and Utpalavarṇā. A well-known story: when the Buddha descended from the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, the nun Utpalavarṇā rushed to be first to greet him, but Subhūti, sitting in his cave contemplating emptiness, was said to be the first to "see" the Buddha's Dharma-body.

"Two birds flying together" (二鳥俱游). An image from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra of two inseparable things.

Prince Great Giving (大施太子). A jātaka hero who went to sea to obtain the wish-fulfilling jewel.

The seven calamities and the two wishes. In the Universal Gate chapter: fire, water, wind (rākṣasas), blades, demons, fetters, and bandits; and the wishes for a son or a daughter.

Inherent evil (性惡). This passage is the classical source of the Tiantai doctrine. The rendering of 性善／性惡／修善／修惡 matches Pointing Out the Essentials. The text contrasts its view with "others" who explain the icchantika's recovery of good through perfuming by the ālaya consciousness (a Yogācāra view, here identified only as "others").

"Māra burning the scriptures … Qin burned the books." The first Qin emperor's burning of books and burying of scholars (213–212 BCE).

"Penetrating the limit of evil is the limit of reality" (通達惡際即是實際). Compare the Vimalakīrti Sūtra: "practicing what is not the path, he penetrates the Buddha path"; and the claim that one can be liberated by way of the five heinous deeds.

The ten latent tendencies and the ninety-eight. The five "dull" tendencies (greed, anger, delusion, conceit, doubt) and the five "sharp" views (view of a body, view of extremes, wrong view, attachment to views, attachment to precepts) are shown arising from clinging to a theory of object and wisdom. Distributed over the Four Noble Truths of the three realms they make eighty-eight; adding the ten delusions of cultivation (thought) gives ninety-eight. "Stealing the fruit" and "stealing the cause" (果盜／因盜) are traditional glosses on attachment to views and to precepts.

"Insects eating wood and happening to form characters." A stock image from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra for accidental correctness.

"Like taking ambrosia [wrongly]." The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says ambrosia can kill those who take it wrongly; the bracketed "wrongly" supplies the point of the image.

"The sixteen minds" (十六心). The sixteen moments of the path of seeing (patience and knowledge for each of the Four Noble Truths in the desire realm and in the upper realms). "Eye, knowledge, clarity, and awakening" (眼智明覺) are the four terms of the first sermon.

The four siddhāntas. The four kinds of teaching method (the worldly, the individual, the therapeutic, the ultimate-meaning); the four sentences "if a person delights … sometimes it is fitting … sometimes as a remedy … sometimes so that he awakens at once" correspond to them in order.

"The Profound Meaning of the great text" (大本玄義). Zhiyi's Fahua Xuanyi; "the wondrous of the object and of wisdom" are its first two wondrous aspects.

"Nāgārjuna first refuted one and different, time and place." The Great Treatise, before commenting on "Thus have I heard," refutes various views.

"Object-drum" (境鼓). The wording is unusual; the sense of the simile is that a drum must exist before it can be struck. Alternatively 境 may be an error for another character.

"World" (世間). The text glosses the two characters separately: 世 as "separate and distinct," 間 as "intervals and differences." The English "world" and "-between" mark the two parts.

"Dharmas produced by causes and conditions …" The verse of Nāgārjuna's Middle Treatise (24:18) as read in Tiantai as the three truths.

"Constantly being mindful … paying homage …" Paraphrases of the Universal Gate chapter, where mindfulness of the bodhisattva frees one from the three poisons and homage brings the birth of a son or daughter. The text calls these "contemplating the world's [mind]" and "contemplating the world's bodies"; the first is supplied in brackets.

"Old explanations" (舊釋). Earlier commentators on the chapter, not named.

Bodily, verbal, and mental formations. In the Abhidharma: breath is bodily formation; initial thought and sustained reflection are verbal formation; sensation and perception are mental formation. The Commentary on the Treatise is cited for this.
