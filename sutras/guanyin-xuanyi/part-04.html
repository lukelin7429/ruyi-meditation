The Ten Universals, and the Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching

Part 4 completes the explanation of "the Universal Gate" and then runs quickly through the remaining four of the Five Sections. It opens by finishing the list of the ten universals and explaining their order: loving-kindness and compassion give rise to the bodhi-mind, vows make it firm, practice fills out the vows, and so on through bringing beings to maturity. The first universal, loving-kindness and compassion, receives the longest treatment. Here the text sets out the ten suchnesses of the hell realm and of the buddha realm and argues that even hell contains the buddha-nature; the bodhisattva "illuminates the evil of inherent virtue and the good of inherent virtue," and his compassion arises from that. This is one of the passages on which the later Tiantai doctrine of inherent evil was built. Great vows are explained through the four truths, and the remaining universals more briefly, often by reference to the great text. A note by the recorder then finds all ten universals in the words of the Lotus chapter itself. The essence is numinous wisdom joined with the Dharma-body; the cardinal doctrine is appeal and response, discussed in a series of questions, including another teacher's analysis and the recorder's objections to it; the function is loving-kindness and compassion benefiting beings; and the characteristics of the teaching place the chapter as the propagation section of the Lotus, the perfect teaching, the flavor of ghee. The fascicle closes with a short exchange on skillful means.

The Order of the Ten Universals

Chinese Source

諸佛普、十成就眾生普。上過途普門已約法竟，此十普門皆約修行福德莊嚴，前五章是自行、次三章是化他、後二章結前兩意。自行中，前四是修因、後一是明果。修因又二：初二是願、後二是行。

Ru-Yi Translation

[(9) universal in making offerings] to the buddhas; (10) universal in bringing beings to maturity. The general account of the Universal Gate above has finished treating it in terms of dharma; these ten universal gates are all in terms of practice and the adornment of merit. The first five sections are one's own practice; the next three are transforming others; the last two conclude the two preceding. Within one's own practice, the first four are cultivating the cause, and the last one clarifies the fruit. Cultivating the cause again has two: the first two are aspirations, and the latter two are practice.

Chinese Source

總生起者，菩薩見一切苦惱眾生起大慈悲，此心雖不即是菩提心，能發生菩提心，譬如地、水雖非種子，能令芽生，今因大悲起菩提心亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

The overall sequence: the bodhisattva sees all suffering and afflicted beings and gives rise to great loving-kindness and compassion. Although this mind is not itself the bodhi-mind, it can give rise to the bodhi-mind — just as earth and water, though they are not seeds, can make sprouts grow. Arousing the bodhi-mind on the basis of great compassion is also like this.

Chinese Source

次誓願者，若但慈悲，喜多退墮，「魚子菴羅華，菩薩初發心，三事因時多，及其成就少。」以不定故，須起誓願，要期制持此心，即菩提堅固。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, vows and aspirations: if there is only loving-kindness and compassion, it is often apt to retreat and fall. "Fish roe, mango blossoms, and the bodhisattva's first arousing of the mind: these three things are many at the causal stage and few when they come to completion." Because it is not settled, one must arouse vows and aspirations, binding and holding this mind with a pledge; then bodhi is firm.

Chinese Source

次明修行者，若但發願，於他未益，如無財物、勢力、權謀不能拔難，菩薩亦爾，須福德財、神通力、智慧謀乃可化道。《大經》云「先以定動，後以慧拔。」修行填願，意在此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying practice: if one only makes aspirations, it is of no benefit to others. Just as without wealth, power, and strategy one cannot rescue others from calamity, so it is with the bodhisattva: he needs the wealth of merit, the power of spiritual powers, and the strategy of wisdom; only then can he transform and guide. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "First shake with concentration, then pull out with wisdom." Practice filling out the aspirations — the meaning lies here.

Chinese Source

次斷惑者，成論人無礙道伏、解脫道斷。若然者，修行是伏道為因，斷惑是解脫道為果。若毘曇明無礙道，一念即斷，那得容與七覺而有伏惑之義？以方便道伏、無礙道斷、解脫道證，引《釋論》云「無礙道中行名菩薩，解脫道中行名佛。」此約究竟為語，佛證三菩提，名解脫道也。若然者，修行是方便道、斷惑是無礙道、入法門是解脫道，取此自行次第也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, severing delusion: the Satyasiddhi teachers [hold that] the path without obstruction subdues and the path of liberation severs. If so, practice is the path of subduing, the cause, and severing delusion is the path of liberation, the fruit. [But] the Abhidharma clarifies that the path without obstruction severs in a single moment; how could it allow for the seven factors of awakening and have the meaning of subduing delusion? The path of skillful means subdues, the path without obstruction severs, and the path of liberation realizes. Citing the Commentary on the Treatise: "Practicing in the path without obstruction is called a bodhisattva; practicing in the path of liberation is called a buddha." This is said in terms of the ultimate: the Buddha's realizing saṃbodhi is called the path of liberation. If so, practice is the path of skillful means, severing delusion is the path without obstruction, and entering the dharma-gates is the path of liberation. This is taken as the sequence of one's own practice.

Chinese Source

次神通者，若欲化他，示三密神通是示色身方便，示意同情說法是示口隨其類音，此是化他次第也。供養諸佛結自行，非但華香四事是供養，隨順修行是法供養，於供養中最。《大經》云「汝隨我語即供養佛。」稟教而行是結自行也，成就眾生是結化他。菩薩四威儀中尚不忘眾生，何況入諸法門、淨佛國土皆為饒益一切眾生，故一句結化他也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, spiritual powers: if one wishes to transform others, showing the spiritual powers of the three secrets is showing the skillful means of the form-body; showing the mind in sympathy and teaching the Dharma is showing the mouth according with their kinds of voice. This is the sequence of transforming others. Making offerings to the buddhas concludes one's own practice: not only are flowers, incense, and the four requisites offerings; practicing in accordance [with the teaching] is the Dharma-offering, the highest among offerings. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "If you follow my words, that is making offerings to the Buddha." Receiving the teaching and practicing it concludes one's own practice. Bringing beings to maturity concludes transforming others: even in the four postures the bodhisattva does not forget beings; how much more are his entering the dharma-gates and purifying buddha-lands all for the benefit of all beings! So this one phrase concludes transforming others.

1. Universal in Loving-Kindness and Compassion: Three Kinds of Loving-Kindness

Chinese Source

次解釋者，始從人、天乃至上地皆有慈悲，此語乃通，不出眾生法緣無緣。若緣眾生，眾生差別，假名不同，因果苦樂有異，尚不得入於法緣之慈，何得稱普耶？若法緣，無人、無我、無眾生，從假以入空，尚不得諸假名，何況是普？若無緣慈者，不緣二十五有假名、不緣二乘涅槃之法，不緣此二邊，雖無所緣而能雙照空假，約此起慈，名無緣慈。心通三諦，稱之為普也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, the explanation. From humans and gods up to the higher grounds, all have loving-kindness and compassion; the word is shared, but it does not go beyond [loving-kindness] with beings as object, with dharmas as object, and without object. If it takes beings as object: beings differ, their provisional names are not the same, and their causes and effects, suffering and happiness, are different; it cannot even enter the loving-kindness with dharmas as object, so how could it be called universal? If with dharmas as object: there is no person, no self, no being; going from the provisional into the empty, it cannot even reach the various provisional names, let alone be universal. As for loving-kindness without object: it does not take as object the provisional names of the twenty-five existences, and does not take as object the dharma of nirvāṇa of the two vehicles; it does not take these two extremes as object. Although there is nothing it takes as object, it can illuminate both the empty and the provisional; loving-kindness arising on this basis is called loving-kindness without object. The mind passes through the three truths; this is called universal.

Loving-Kindness with Beings as Object: The Ten Suchnesses of Hell

Chinese Source

別釋者，若修眾生緣慈者，觀一法界眾生假名，可不名普。今觀十法界眾生假名，一一界各有十種性相本末究竟等，十法界交互即有百法界千種性相，冥伏在心，雖不現前，宛然具足。譬如人面備休否相，庸人不知，相師善識。今眾生性相一心具足亦復如是，

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining distinctly: if one cultivates loving-kindness with beings as object and contemplates the provisional names of the beings of one dharma-realm, it could not be called universal. Now one contemplates the provisional names of the beings of the ten dharma-realms: each realm has its ten kinds — nature, characteristic, … root-and-branch ultimately equal; the ten dharma-realms interpenetrating, there are a hundred dharma-realms and a thousand kinds of nature-and-characteristics, latent in the mind; though they do not appear in the present, they are distinctly complete. It is like a person's face, which carries the signs of fortune and misfortune: ordinary people do not know them, but a physiognomist recognizes them well. The nature-and-characteristics of beings being complete in one mind is also like this.

Chinese Source

凡人多顛倒，少不顛倒，理具情迷，聖人知覺即識，如彼相師，知此千種性相皆是因緣生法。若是惡因緣生法即有苦性相乃至苦本末，既未解脫，觀此苦而起大悲；若觀善因緣生法，即有樂性相乃至樂本末，觀此而起大慈。具解如《大本》。今約初、後兩界，中間可解。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ordinary people are mostly inverted and seldom not inverted: the principle is complete, but their feelings are deluded. The sage knows and is aware, and recognizes them, like that physiognomist; he knows that these thousand kinds of nature-and-characteristics are all dharmas produced by causes and conditions. If they are dharmas produced by evil causes and conditions, there are the nature-and-characteristics of suffering up to the root-and-branch of suffering; since [beings] are not yet liberated, contemplating this suffering he gives rise to great compassion. If he contemplates dharmas produced by good causes and conditions, there are the nature-and-characteristics of happiness up to the root-and-branch of happiness; contemplating this, he gives rise to great loving-kindness. The full explanation is as in the great text. Here we take the first and last realms; those in between can be understood.

Chinese Source

地獄界如是性者，性名不改。如竹中有火性，若其無者，不應從竹求火、從地求水、從扇求風，心有地獄界性亦復如是。地獄相者，攬而可別，名之為相。善觀心者即識地獄之相，如善相師別相無謬，故名相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Such nature" in the hell realm: nature means unchanging. Just as there is a fire-nature in bamboo — if there were not, one should not seek fire from bamboo, water from earth, or wind from a fan — the mind's having the nature of the hell realm is also like this. The characteristic of hell: what can be taken up and distinguished is called the characteristic. One who contemplates the mind well recognizes the characteristic of hell, like a skilled physiognomist distinguishing signs without error; so it is called the characteristic.

Chinese Source

體者，以心為體，心覺苦樂故以當體。譬如釵、鐺、環、釧之殊，終以銀為體質；六道之色乃異只是約心，故心為體也。乃至運御名力，緣山入火皆是其力也。作者，為動曰作，已能有力即有所作，或作善、作惡也。因者，業是因也。緣者，假藉為緣也。如愛潤業，因緣合也。果者，習果也。如地獄人前世多淫，生地獄中還約多淫，見可愛境即往親附，名習果也。報者，報果也。昔有淫罪，今墮地獄受燒炙之苦，名報果也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Essence: mind is taken as the essence. Because mind is aware of suffering and happiness, it serves as the essence. It is like the differences between hairpins, earrings, rings, and bracelets: in the end, silver is their substance. The forms of the six paths may differ, but they are only in terms of mind; so mind is the essence. And so on up to: wielding is called power — going up mountains and entering fire are all its power. Activity: moving is called activity; having power, there is something done, whether doing good or doing evil. Cause: karma is the cause. Condition: what is relied on is the condition, as craving moistens karma — cause and condition joined. Effect: the habitual effect. A person in hell who in a former life was given much to lust is, born in hell, still marked by much lust; seeing a lovely object, he goes to cling to it. This is called the habitual effect. Retribution: the retributive effect. Having had the offense of lust in the past, he now falls into hell and undergoes the suffering of burning and roasting; this is called the retributive effect.

Chinese Source

本者，性德法也。末者，修得法也。究竟等者，攬修得即等有性德，攬性德即具有修得，初後相在，故言等也。地獄界十相性既如此，餘九亦然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root: the dharma of inherent virtue. Branch: the dharma attained through cultivation. Ultimately equal: taking up what is attained through cultivation, it equally has inherent virtue; taking up inherent virtue, it is complete with what is attained through cultivation; beginning and end are present in each other; so it is called equal. The ten characteristics and natures of the hell realm being thus, the other nine are likewise.

Buddha-Nature in the Hell Realm

Chinese Source

問：當界有十性相可然，云何交互相有？餘界交互已難可信，云何地獄有佛性相本末耶？答：《大經》云「夫有心者，皆當得三菩提。」如仙豫殺婆羅門即有三念，又婆藪地獄人好高剛柔等義，雖在地獄，佛性之理究竟不失，故知地獄界即有佛性。佛相者即是性德之相也。《淨名經》云「一切眾生即菩提相」，聖人鑑之，冷然可別也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: That each realm has its own ten natures and characteristics may be so; but how can they mutually possess one another? That the other realms mutually possess one another is already hard to believe; how can hell have the nature, characteristic, root, and branch of buddhahood? Answer: The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "All who have mind will attain saṃbodhi." For example, when Xianyu killed the brahmins, they then had three thoughts; and there is the hell-dweller Vasu, with the meanings of "fond of the high," "hard and soft," and so on. Although in hell, the principle of buddha-nature is ultimately not lost; so one knows that the hell realm has the buddha-nature. The characteristic of buddhahood is the characteristic of inherent virtue. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "All beings are the characteristic of bodhi." The sage mirrors it, and it can be clearly distinguished.

Chinese Source

體者，即是地獄界心實相理也。力者，法性十力變通大用也。作者，從無住本立一切法，如師子筋、師子乳也。因者，正因也。緣者，性德緣了也。果即般若菩提大果也。報即大涅槃果果也。本即性德，末即修得。等者，修得相貌在性德中，性德中亦具修得相貌，故言究竟等也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Essence: it is the principle of the real mark of the mind of the hell realm. Power: the great function of transformation and penetration, the ten powers of the dharma-nature. Activity: from the root of non-abiding all dharmas are established — like a lion's sinew, like a lion's milk. Cause: the correct cause. Condition: the conditioning and revealing [causes] of inherent virtue. Effect: the great effect of prajñā and bodhi. Retribution: the effect-of-effect, great nirvāṇa. Root is inherent virtue; branch is what is attained through cultivation. Equal: the appearance of what is attained through cultivation is within inherent virtue, and inherent virtue also is complete with the appearance of what is attained through cultivation; so it is called ultimately equal.

Chinese Source

《大經》云「雪山之中有妙藥王，亦有毒草。」地獄一界尚具佛果性相十法，何況餘界耶？地獄互有九界，餘界互有亦如是。菩薩深觀十法界眾生，千種性相具在一心，遠討根源，照其性德之惡、性德之善，尚自冷然，何況不照修得善惡耶？如見雪山藥王毒草，以觀性德惡毒，惻愴憐愍，起大悲心，欲拔其苦；以觀性德善樂，愛念歡喜，起大慈心，欲與其樂。此十法界收一切眾生罄無不盡，緣此眾生假名修慈，豈非眾生慈普耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "In the Snowy Mountains there is a wondrous king of medicines, and there are also poisonous herbs." If even the one realm of hell is complete with the ten dharmas of nature and characteristic of the buddha-fruit, how much more the other realms! Hell mutually possesses the nine realms, and the other realms mutually possess [the rest] likewise. The bodhisattva deeply contemplates the beings of the ten dharma-realms, the thousand kinds of nature-and-characteristics all present in one mind. Searching far to the root source, he illuminates the evil of their inherent virtue and the good of their inherent virtue, which even so are clear; how much less would he fail to illuminate the good and evil attained through cultivation! As when one sees the king of medicines and the poisonous herbs of the Snowy Mountains: contemplating the evil and poison of inherent virtue, he grieves and pities them and gives rise to the mind of great compassion, wishing to remove their suffering; contemplating the good and happiness of inherent virtue, he cherishes them and rejoices, and gives rise to the mind of great loving-kindness, wishing to give them happiness. These ten dharma-realms take in all beings without exception; taking these beings and their provisional names as object, he cultivates loving-kindness — how is this not loving-kindness with beings as object being universal?

Chinese Source

問：地獄界重苦未拔，云何言與樂耶？答：眾生入地獄時多起三念，菩薩承機即與樂因，故言與樂也。又，菩薩能大悲代受苦，令其休息，餘界苦輕，與樂義可解。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The heavy suffering of the hell realm has not been removed; how can one speak of giving happiness? Answer: When beings enter hell, they often give rise to three thoughts; the bodhisattva, taking up this capacity, gives them the cause of happiness; so it is said he gives happiness. Also, the bodhisattva, with great compassion, can undergo the suffering in their place and let them rest. In the other realms the suffering is lighter, and the meaning of giving happiness can be understood.

Loving-Kindness with Dharmas as Object, and without Object

Chinese Source

二法緣慈者，觀十法界性相、一切善惡悉皆虛空。十法界假名，假名皆空，十法界色、受、想、行、識，行識皆空，十法界處所處所皆空，無我、無我所皆不可得，如幻、如化，無有真實，常寂滅相，終歸於空，眾生云何彊計為實？良以眾生不覺不知，為苦為惱，不得無為寂滅之樂，拔其此苦而起大悲，欲與其此樂故起大慈。《淨名》云「能為眾生說如此法即真實慈也。」若緣一法界法起慈者，可不名普；今緣十法界法，豈非普耶？是名法緣慈普也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, loving-kindness with dharmas as object: one contemplates the nature-and-characteristics of the ten dharma-realms and all good and evil as entirely empty space. The provisional names of the ten dharma-realms — the provisional names are all empty; the form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness of the ten dharma-realms — formations and consciousness are all empty; the places of the ten dharma-realms — the places are all empty; no self and nothing belonging to self is obtainable at all; like illusions, like magical creations, without anything true or real; always of the mark of quiescent extinction, ultimately returning to emptiness. How can beings insist on reckoning them real? Precisely because beings do not awaken and do not know, they suffer and are afflicted and do not attain the happiness of unconditioned quiescent extinction. To remove this suffering of theirs he gives rise to great compassion; wishing to give them this happiness, he gives rise to great loving-kindness. The Vimalakīrti says: "To be able to teach such a dharma to beings — that is true, real loving-kindness." If one gives rise to loving-kindness taking the dharmas of one dharma-realm as object, it could not be called universal; now, taking the dharmas of ten dharma-realms as object, how is it not universal? This is called loving-kindness with dharmas as object being universal.

Chinese Source

三無緣慈者，若緣十法界性相等差別假名，此假則非假，十法界如幻如化，空則非空，非假故不緣十法界性相、非空故不緣十法界之真。既遮此二邊，無住無著，名為中道，亦無中可緣，畢竟清淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, loving-kindness without object: if one takes as object the differentiated provisional names of the nature-and-characteristics of the ten dharma-realms, this provisional is then not provisional; the ten dharma-realms are like illusions, like magical creations, and the empty is then not empty. Because it is not provisional, one does not take the nature-and-characteristics of the ten dharma-realms as object; because it is not empty, one does not take the true of the ten dharma-realms as object. Having negated these two extremes, without abiding and without attachment, it is called the middle way; and there is also no middle to be taken as object. It is ultimately pure.

Chinese Source

如是觀時，雖不緣於空假，任運雙照二邊，起無緣慈悲，拔二死之苦，與中道之樂，如磁石吸鐵，無有教者自然相應。無緣慈悲吸三諦機更無差忒，不須作念，故言無緣慈悲也。行者始於凡地，修此慈悲即得入於五品弟子，觀行無緣慈悲進入十信位，相似無緣慈悲入於十住，方是分證無緣慈悲；乃至等覺隣極慈悲熏眾生，不動如明鏡，無念如磁石，任運吸鐵，故名無緣慈悲。三諦具足名之為普，通至中道故稱為門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

When contemplating thus, though one does not take the empty or the provisional as object, one spontaneously illuminates both extremes and gives rise to loving-kindness and compassion without object, removing the suffering of the two kinds of death and giving the happiness of the middle way — like a lodestone drawing iron, which accords naturally without anyone teaching it. Loving-kindness and compassion without object draws the capacities of the three truths without any error, with no need to form a thought; so it is called loving-kindness and compassion without object. The practitioner, beginning at the ordinary level, cultivates this loving-kindness and compassion and so enters the five grades of disciples — loving-kindness and compassion without object in contemplative practice; advancing into the stage of the ten faiths — loving-kindness and compassion without object in resemblance; entering the ten abodes — only then is it loving-kindness and compassion without object in partial realization; up to virtual awakening, adjacent to the summit, loving-kindness and compassion perfume beings, unmoving like a bright mirror, without thought like a lodestone spontaneously drawing iron; so it is called loving-kindness and compassion without object. Being complete with the three truths is called universal; leading through to the middle way, it is called a gate.

2. Universal in Great Vows

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, universal in great vows. "Great" (弘) means broad; "vow" (誓) means restraint; "aspiration" (願) means seeking with a pledge. So, restraining and governing one's mind and broadly seeking the supreme Dharma is called "great vows." Great vows originally accomplish loving-kindness and compassion; since loving-kindness and compassion take suffering and happiness as object, great vows also are in terms of the four truths. Seeing the oppression of the truth of suffering, its torment, bitterness, and sourness, and taking this as object, one makes a vow; so it is said, "Those not yet delivered, may I deliver." Seeing the inversion and transmigration of the truth of origin — confusion, delusion, and bondage, birth and death vast and without shore, deeply pitiable — on this basis one makes a vow; so it is said, "Those not yet freed, may I free." Beings do not recognize the pure path; those who practice this path can leave birth and death and reach the land of peace and happiness; wishing to show beings and establish them in this path, it is said, "Those not yet at peace, may I bring to peace." The place where afflictions are extinguished is called nirvāṇa; when the bonds of seed and fruit are cut, the two nirvāṇas are obtained; on this basis one makes a vow; so it is said, "Those who have not yet attained nirvāṇa, may I lead to attain nirvāṇa." The cause of birth and death is hard to recognize, and the fruit, suffering, is easy to know; so the fruit comes first and the cause after. The principle of nirvāṇa is wondrous and requires good skillful means; so the cause comes first and the fruit after.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Not understanding how to churn and shake, even buttermilk is hard to get — how much more curdled butter or ghee?" These four meanings, taken at a first pass, are just this: the deluded mind gives rise to karma, and karma brings about the fruit. Wishing to recognize the source of the fruit, one knows that the cause of the fruit is origin. Restraining the mind and stopping karma, the wheel of birth and death is destroyed and afflictions are tamed; this is called the path. Cultivating practice without slackening, the forbearance of suffering brightly arises, seed and fruit are both severed, and one realizes exhaustion and non-generation; this is called cessation. Although there are four distinctions, in the end they are a single thought, not different dharmas. As the four truths are, so are the great vows.

Next, clarifying universal and not universal. Ordinary people, disliking the lower and climbing toward the higher, make vows on this basis; this is not called universal. The two vehicles see the three realms as a burning house and, fearing it, cultivate the path; this is seeing the four truths of the segmented, and is also not called universal. The distinct teaching deals first with the segmented and then with change; this too is not universal. The bodhisattva of the perfect teaching, within one mind, illuminates all suffering, origin, cessation, and path, and knows pervasively that the views and craving of ordinary people are origin with fabrication, and that the two vehicles' attachment to emptiness is origin without fabrication. So the Vimalakīrti says: "The Dharma is called stainless; if one is stained by dharmas, that is called being stained by dharmas, not seeking the Dharma." It also says: "When bonds and habits are not yet exhausted, the flowers cling to the body" — that is, the delusion of change has not been removed at all. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "You monks have not yet, in this Great Vehicle, removed the fetters for the sake of the true Dharma" — that is origin without fabrication. Up to: the arising of dharma-love that accords with the path is also origin without fabrication. This is called knowing origin pervasively.

Knowing suffering pervasively: because there is origin, it can bring the retribution of suffering. Conditioned origin brings the suffering of the segmented; origin without fabrication brings the suffering of change. This is knowing the truth of suffering. Knowing pervasively path and cessation, which counteract suffering and origin: from the five precepts and the ten good deeds, the immovable and the non-departing, [through] the four truths and twelve causes and conditions of the two vehicles leading to nirvāṇa with and without remainder — the shared teaching is likewise. The distinct teaching is separate and sequential; it leads to the eternally abiding, but it cannot have immeasurable paths within one path, so it is not called the universal path. The middle way of the perfect teaching is the real mark. The Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra says: "The cause of the Great Vehicle is the real mark of all dharmas." Cultivating such a path is called the perfect cause and is called the universal path; so the nirvāṇa attained is the ultimate, eternally abiding, with all afflictions forever without remainder — like the fire at the end of an eon, with no ash remaining; so it is called universal cessation. Since the four truths contemplated are complete, and vows are made taking the truths as object, how could they not be pervasive? So it is called universal in great vows.

Ru-Yi Translation

I have privately used the contemplation of the good and evil of inherent virtue and of what is attained through cultivation in the ten dharma-realms, and the arousing of great vows [from it], to discuss universal and not universal. This is in itself a section of great meaning, and its wording differs from [that of] the four truths; therefore I have avoided using it [here], though it too should be good.

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying universal in practice: first clarifying practice in sequence, then clarifying practice not in sequence. This is set out in full in the great text, in [the section on] the wondrous of practice.

Chinese Source

Fourth, clarifying universal in severing delusion. If one enters the empty from the provisional, one severs only the delusions of the four abodes; "the flowers still cling to the body," and one has "not yet, for the sake of the true Dharma, removed the fetters"; one is "only free from the false" and not liberated from everything. If empty and provisional are nondual and one correctly contemplates the middle way, then when the root has fallen, the branches go of themselves — as when the great earth is overturned, the grasses and trees are all crushed; so it is called universal in severing delusion.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, universal in entering dharma-gates. If the two vehicles enter one dharma-gate, they cannot enter a second, let alone many. If one cultivates the practice of separate sequence, with grades of shallow and deep — "I know only this one dharma-gate; the others I cannot know" — this too is not universal. If one enters the king samādhi, all enter within it. It is like a king coming: he must have an entourage, and the entourage in turn has its entourage. The king samādhi is also like this: entering this samādhi, all samādhis enter within it — namely, the samādhis of the three truths; and the samādhis of the three truths in turn have immeasurable dharma-gates as their retinue, which likewise all enter into the king samādhi. So it is called universal in entering dharma-gates.

Chinese Source

六神通普者，若大羅漢天眼見大千、支佛見百佛土、菩薩見恒沙佛土，皆是限量之通，故不名普。何以故？緣境既狹，發通亦小。今圓教菩薩緣十法界境發通，遍見十法界而無限極，三乘尚不知其名，何況見其境界？眼見既爾，餘例可知，神通妙中當廣說。

Why? Since the object taken is narrow, the power that arises is also small. Now the bodhisattva of the perfect teaching takes the object of the ten dharma-realms and gives rise to powers, seeing the ten dharma-realms pervasively and without limit. The three vehicles do not even know its name, let alone see its sphere. As with the eye's seeing, the rest can be known by analogy; it will be explained at length under the wondrous of spiritual powers.

The skillful means practiced by the two vehicles and lesser bodhisattvas enter one dharma-gate; if they wish to transform others, they produce function to transform others only to the extent of what they themselves have attained; before the path and after the path, neither is universal. The bodhisattva of the perfect teaching takes the two truths as skillful means and gathers in all skillful means; having entered the middle way, he illuminates both of the two truths, and the spiritual transformations of the two truths pervade the ten dharma-realms, while nothing of the Dharma-body is diminished. Both before the path and after the path are called universal.

Now the person of the perfect teaching expounds the Dharma with one sound, and each understands according to kind; with one wondrous sound he fills the realms of the ten directions, like the asura's lute, which gives out sounds according to people's wishes. So it is called [universal in] teaching the Dharma. It is explained at length under the wondrous of teaching the Dharma.

Non-Buddhists are like fireflies; the two vehicles like lamps and candles; the shared teaching like the stars; the distinct teaching like the moon: their bringing to maturity is limited. Now the sage of the perfect teaching, with loving-kindness and wisdom, enriches and moistens, with benefit both hidden and manifest and without limit. Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the cardinal doctrine: appeal and response are taken as the cardinal doctrine. The capacities of the ten realms knock on the knowing of tranquil illumination, bringing about the benefits of appeal and response in the earlier and later [parts]. Though the passages on benefit are broad, just take appeal and response and gather them in, as when one pulls the main rope and the meshes move. So appeal and response are used as the cardinal doctrine. Other sūtras sometimes use cause and effect as the cardinal doctrine; this chapter does not, because the term "cause and effect" is shared: from ordinary people up to the higher grounds, each has cause and effect, and both that which appeals and that which is appealed to have cause and effect; but the intent of the sūtra text seems not to reach this. On the side of capacity, though there is cause and effect, it is named only by appeal; the sage, though without cause and effect, is named only by response. This suits the text and its meaning. The meaning of appeal and response has six [parts]: first, listing the names; second, explaining the characteristics; third, explaining sameness and difference; fourth, clarifying their correspondence; fifth, clarifying universal and not universal; sixth, discussing the contemplation of mind. All are in the great text.

Reading Notes for Part 4

Translator's notes

Where Part 4 begins. The source range begins in the middle of item 9 of the list (「諸佛普」); the bracketed words in the first paragraph supply the rest of the item, whose Chinese is at the end of Part 3.

"The general account above" (上過途普門). 過途 makes little sense; it is probably an error for 通途 ("general"), the word used at the start of the section (「一通途明門」). The English follows 通途.

The ten suchnesses. 性 nature, 相 characteristic, 體 essence, 力 power, 作 activity, 因 cause, 緣 condition, 果 effect, 報 retribution, 本末究竟等 root-and-branch ultimately equal. 作 is "activity" so that 用 can remain "function," as in the Five Sections. In the habitual and retributive "effects" (習果, 報果) 果 is "effect"; elsewhere, in the sense of the fruit of practice (修因 / 果, 子果), it is "fruit."

Inherent virtue (性德) and what is attained through cultivation (修得). These are "root" and "branch" among the ten suchnesses. "The evil of inherent virtue" (性德之惡) and "the good of inherent virtue" (性德之善) are what later Tiantai calls inherent evil and inherent good (性惡, 性善); 性善 itself appears in Part 4 in the question on capacity ("inherent good lies hidden and latent"). Zhili's Guanyin xuanyi ji builds much of its doctrine of inherent evil on these passages; that reading is not imported into the English.

"Fish roe, mango blossoms…" Verified in the Great Treatise (T1509, fasc. 4): 「菩薩發大心，魚子、菴樹華，三事因時多，成果時甚少」; the wording here differs. 「喜多退墮」: 喜 ("apt to") may be an error for 易 ("easily").

"First shake with concentration, then pull out with wisdom." Verified in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374): 「先以定動，後以智拔」 (智 there, 慧 here).

The three paths. The path of skillful means (方便道), the path without obstruction (無礙道), and the path of liberation (解脫道). The quotation "Practicing in the path without obstruction is called a bodhisattva…" is verified in the Great Treatise (T1509: 「菩薩摩訶薩無礙道中行，是名菩薩摩訶薩；解脫道中無一切闇蔽，是為佛」). 「那得容與七覺而有伏惑之義」 is obscure; the English "allow for the seven factors of awakening" is tentative. 三菩提 is taken as saṃbodhi (short for anuttarā samyaksaṃbodhi), not "three kinds of bodhi."

The three secrets in transforming others. The sentence seems to match items 6–8 to body (spiritual powers), mind (skillful means), and speech (teaching the Dharma). 「示意同情說法」 is ambiguous; alternatively: "showing [the secret of] mind, in sympathy with their feelings, and teaching the Dharma."

"If you follow my words, that is making offerings to the Buddha." Verified in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374: 「汝隨我語，則供養佛」).

Xianyu and Vasu. King Xianyu (仙預 in T0374, verified) killed brahmins who slandered the Mahāyāna; they fell into hell and there, according to the Tiantai reading, gave rise to three thoughts that led to their rebirth in his land. The hell-dweller Vasu (婆藪) is the seer who taught that sacrificing sheep leads to heaven (T0374 mentions him, verified). 「好高剛柔等義」 is obscure and is translated word for word; source not identified.

"Clearly" (冷然). 冷 ("cold") is probably an error for 泠 or 了; the sense must be "clearly." The English follows that sense in both places.

"All who have mind will attain saṃbodhi." Verified in substance in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374: 「凡有心者，定當得成阿耨多羅三藐三菩提」). "All beings are the characteristic of bodhi" is verified in the Vimalakīrti (T0475).

Lion's sinew, lion's milk. In the Nirvāṇa Sūtra a lute strung with lion sinew silences all other strings, and lion's milk dissolves all other milk; both are images for the power of the bodhi-mind. Not checked line by line.

"In the Snowy Mountains there is a wondrous king of medicines, and there are also poisonous herbs." The Nirvāṇa Sūtra speaks of the Snowy Mountains having many medicines and also poisonous herbs (T0374: 「多生諸藥，亦有毒草」); the exact wording quoted here was not found (paraphrase).

"Formations and consciousness are all empty" (行識皆空). The text lists all five aggregates but then repeats only the last two, in the pattern 「假名，假名皆空」, 「處所，處所皆空」. Translated as written. 虛空 is kept as "empty space," as elsewhere in the text.

"True, real loving-kindness." Verified in the Vimalakīrti (T0475: 「我當為眾生說如斯法，是即真實慈也」).

Stages of loving-kindness without object. The English follows the Tiantai correspondence: five grades of disciples = identity in contemplative practice; ten faiths = identity in resemblance; ten abodes = partial realization. CBETA's commas run the clauses together differently (「入於五品弟子，觀行無緣慈悲進入十信位」), and the Chinese could also be read "with loving-kindness and compassion without object in contemplative practice, he advances into the ten faiths," and so on. "The two kinds of death" are segmented birth-and-death and the birth-and-death of change.

The four universal vows. "Those not yet delivered, may I deliver…" are the vows of the Lotus and other sūtras, matched to the four truths. "The two nirvāṇas" are nirvāṇa with and without remainder; "the bonds of seed and fruit" are the bonds of cause (afflictions) and effect (the body).

"Not understanding how to churn and shake…" Verified in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374: 「不知攢搖，漿猶難得，況復生酥」; 攢 there, 鑽 here). "Exhaustion and non-generation" (盡無生) are the knowledge of exhaustion and the knowledge of non-generation of the arhat.

Origin with and without fabrication (有作之集 / 無作之集). 無作 is rendered "without fabrication," as in "the four truths without fabrication." Here the point is that the two vehicles' attachment to emptiness is itself the origin of suffering as seen at the level of the four truths without fabrication.

Vimalakīrti and Nirvāṇa quotations on origin. "The Dharma is called stainless…" (T0475, verified, slightly abridged); "When bonds and habits are not yet exhausted, the flowers cling to the body" (T0475: 「結習未盡，華著身耳」, verified). The Nirvāṇa Sūtra passage reads 「雖除鬚髮，未為正法除諸結使」 (T0374, verified); the text's 「於此大乘」 is added. 「未為正法」 is taken as "not yet, for the sake of the true Dharma"; alternatively: "have not yet [practiced] the true Dharma."

"The immovable and the non-departing" (不動、不出). Probably the immovable karma of the form and formless realms and the worldly concentrations that do not lead out of the three realms. Tentative.

"The cause of the Great Vehicle is the real mark of all dharmas." Verified in the Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra (T0277: 「大乘因者，諸法實相是」).

The recorder's notes (私). Passages beginning 私 ("privately") are taken as remarks by the recorder, Guanding. 「故逭用之」: 逭 means "to evade"; the English "avoided using it" is literal and tentative; the character may be corrupt.

"Only free from the false." From the Lotus, "Parable" chapter: one is said to be liberated only by being free from the false, but has not really attained complete liberation. Not checked in T0262.

The king samādhi. In the Fahua Xuanyi, the twenty-five samādhis that break the twenty-five existences are called king samādhis. "I know only this one dharma-gate" echoes the good friends of the Avataṃsaka "Entering the Dharma-Realm" chapter.

Skillful means before and after the path. 「今正明道，後方便也」 follows CBETA's comma; alternatively: "Here we directly clarify the skillful means after the path."

Kauṣṭhila. Verified in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374: 「摩訶拘絺羅四無礙第一」); "not among bodhisattvas" is not in the passage found (paraphrase). The asura's lute that plays by itself according to the hearer's wish appears in Tiantai works (e.g., T1718); its sūtra source was not identified.

The Avataṃsaka quotations on offerings and on arousing the mind. Not found verbatim in T0278 or T0279 (source not identified). "With one meal giving to all" is verified in the Vimalakīrti (T0475: 「以一食施一切」).

"Making offerings to the wisdom that knows all things" (供養一切智). A wordplay: "offerings to all [buddhas]" is read as "offerings to all-wisdom." The fixed rendering of 一切智 is kept.

The three epithets (三號). Buddha (佛, "awakened"), Tathāgata (如, "suchness"), and Samyaksaṃbuddha (正遍知, "Correctly and Universally Knowing").

Words of the chapter. Verified in the Lotus (T0262): 「愍諸四眾…受其瓔珞，分作二分，一分奉釋迦牟尼佛，一分奉多寶佛塔」; 「以種種形，遊諸國土，度脫眾生」; 「方便之力，其事云何」; 「而為說法」; 「多所饒益」. 「偏悉之言」: 偏 ("partial") is probably an error for 遍 ("pervasive"); the English reads "words of totality."

Numinous wisdom (靈智). 「今知權實，相與理不二」 is ambiguous. The English takes 知 as the knowing of the provisional and the real and 相與 as "together with." Alternatively: "Now [this] knowing — the provisional and the real — and principle are not two with each other," or, with 知 as 智, "the wisdom of the provisional and the real."

Appeal and response (感應). 機 is "capacity," 感 "appeal," 應 "response." 能感／所感 are "the appealer / what is appealed to," and 能應／所應 "the responder / what is responded to." 感應道交 follows the glossary ("the mutual resonance between response and appeal"). In 「惡則感苦」 感 has its ordinary sense and is rendered "elicits."

Three glosses of "capacity." The Fahua Xuanyi gives three meanings of 機: "the faint [good] about to be generated" (微), "connection" (關), and "fitting" (宜). The questions here test each one.

"Answer: [And so on.] / Following another [teacher's account]" (答：云云。以他). CBETA ends the line with 以他 and starts a new line with 問. The English takes 以他 as introducing another teacher's account (the following question and answer), which the recorder then objects to (「私難此語」). Alternatively 以他 may be a stray fragment.

"The sage submerges the appealer." The recorder's objection says 聖沒能感、凡沒能應, while the passage it criticizes says 聖沒所感、凡沒所應. The English follows the text in both places; the objection may misquote, or the text may be corrupt.

Generation from self, other, both, and no cause. The objection applies the Middle Treatise tetralemma on arising (自生, 他生, 共生, 無因生).

The two wisdoms (二智). The provisional and real wisdoms. The text lacks 問 before 「二智不當用耶」; "[Question:]" is supplied.

"The two birds roam together." From the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's image of a pair of birds (kalaviṅka or mandarin ducks) for permanence and impermanence. Not checked line by line.

Was Avalokiteśvara already a buddha? Verified in the Avalokiteśvara Prediction Sūtra (T0371): the land of 金光師子遊戲如來, King 威德, the two sons 寶意 and 寶上 (the text here has 寶尚), the future names 普光功德山王 and 善住功德寶王. Verified in the Tathāgatagarbha Sūtra (T0666): 「彼四菩薩未成佛者，文殊師利、觀世音、大勢至、金剛慧」. The Avalokiteśvara Samādhi Sūtra is not in the Taishō; source not identified.

The separate transmission. Dharmakṣema (曇摩羅讖, 385–433) translated at the court of Juqu Mengxun (沮渠蒙遜) of the Northern Liang in Hexi. "Yipolei" (伊波勒) is given as his other name; the Sanskrit is uncertain. The count "one thousand five hundred thirty words" is the text's.

The propagation section and ghee. In Tiantai division the Lotus has an introduction, main teaching, and propagation section; the Avalokiteśvara chapter falls in the last. The flavor of ghee is the fifth of the Five Flavors, the Lotus–Nirvāṇa period.

"By the power of skillful means, in various different ways; the teaching too should differ." CBETA puts the whole sentence in quotation marks, but only 「方便之力」 is in the chapter (T0262: 「方便之力，其事云何」); the rest is the questioner's inference. "The entrustment" is the Lotus "Entrustment" chapter; 「於如來餘深法中，示教利喜」 is verified in T0262.

"Had not yet disclosed the origin and revealed the trace" (未發本顯迹). The usual Tiantai phrase is 開迹顯本 ("opening the trace to reveal the origin"); the wording here is translated as written.
