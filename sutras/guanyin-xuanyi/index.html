The Profound Meaning of Guanyin.

This short work in two fascicles, attributed to Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597) and recorded by Guanding (灌頂, 561–632), explains the Lotus Sūtra's chapter on Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate by the same five sections Zhiyi used for the whole sūtra: name, essence, cardinal doctrine, function, and the characteristics of the teachings. The chapter's title is unfolded through ten pairs, from person and dharma to the true and the responsive, and in the course of it the text makes its most famous claim: that the buddha does not cut off evil inherent in the nature, and so can respond freely within every realm.

The Text's Structure

Two fascicles, divided here into four parts.

The Opening and the General Explanation of the Name

Part 2
Resolving Doubts, Inherent Evil, and the Name "Avalokiteśvara"
This part completes the general explanation of the name and begins the specific one, closing the first fascicle.
Translated
Part 3
Contemplating the Sounds of the World, and the Universal Gate in General
Fascicle Two opens by finishing the explanation of the name "Avalokiteśvara" (觀世音), taken word by word as "contemplating" (觀) "the sounds of the world" (世音).
Translated
Part 4
The Ten Universals, and the Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and Teaching
Part 4 completes the explanation of "the Universal Gate" and then runs quickly through the remaining four of the Five Sections.
