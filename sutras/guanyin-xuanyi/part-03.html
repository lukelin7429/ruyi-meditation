Contemplating the Sounds of the World, and the Universal Gate in General

Fascicle Two opens by finishing the explanation of the name "Avalokiteśvara" (觀世音), taken word by word as "contemplating" (觀) "the sounds of the world" (世音). Part 2 dealt with "the sounds of the world" as the object; here the text turns to "contemplating." After naming six kinds of object, it explains contemplation through the three truths in two ways: the three contemplations taken in sequence and the three contemplations in a single mind. The sequential contemplations are matched with the three wisdoms of the Large Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra, the four contemplations of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, and the five eyes, and then with the four teachings. For each teaching — Tripiṭaka, shared, distinct, and perfect — the text describes the bodhisattva's practice and stages and closes with "the meaning of Avalokiteśvara" in that teaching. A series of questions then defends the perfect contemplation (does contemplating "neither empty nor provisional" destroy cause and effect?), using the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's simile of the waxing and waning moon. The section ends with contemplation of the mind itself. The text then begins the second half of the name, "the Universal Gate" (普門): a general account of gates (worldly gates, the sixteen gates of the four teachings, provisional and real, universal and not universal, and the gate of nonduality in the Vimalakīrti), and the opening list of the ten universals, which Part 4 explains one by one. Part 3 ends in the middle of that list, where the source range ends.

Contemplating: Gathering Up the Object

Chinese Source

第二明觀者，又為二：一結束世音之境、二明能觀之智。結境即為六：一結十法界是因緣境、二四諦境、三三諦境、四二諦境、五一實諦境、六無諦境，此具出《大本玄義》。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying "contemplating." This again has two parts: first, gathering up the object, "the sounds of the world"; second, clarifying the wisdom that contemplates. Gathering up the object has six [aspects]: first, gathering the ten dharma-realms as the object of causes and conditions; second, the object of the four truths; third, the object of the three truths; fourth, the object of the two truths; fifth, the object of the one real truth; sixth, the object of no truth. These are set out in full in the Profound Meaning of the great text.

The Contemplating Wisdom

Chinese Source

二明觀智者，傍境明智作五番明觀智，就因緣則四番因緣論觀，四諦亦有四番論觀、三諦有兩番論觀、二諦有七番論觀、一實諦則一番論觀、無諦則無觀，如此等義具在《大本》。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the contemplating wisdom. Alongside the objects, wisdom is clarified; the contemplating wisdom is clarified in five sets. On causes and conditions, contemplation is discussed in four sets; on the four truths, contemplation is also discussed in four sets; on the three truths, in two sets; on the two truths, in seven sets; on the one real truth, in one set; and on no truth there is no contemplation. Such meanings are all in the great text.

Chinese Source

今約三諦明觀，若通論十法界皆是因緣所生法，此因緣即空、即假、即中，即空是真諦、即假是俗諦、即中是中道第一義諦。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here contemplation is clarified in terms of the three truths. Speaking generally, the ten dharma-realms are all dharmas produced by causes and conditions, and these causes and conditions are identical to the empty, identical to the provisional, identical to the middle. Identical to the empty is the real truth; identical to the provisional is the conventional truth; identical to the middle is the truth of the middle way of the first meaning.

Chinese Source

若別論六道界是因緣生法，二乘界是空、菩薩界是假、佛界是中。論境即有二意，今對境明觀亦為二意：一次第三觀、二一心三觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Speaking distinctly, the realms of the six paths are dharmas produced by causes and conditions; the realms of the two vehicles are the empty; the bodhisattva realm is the provisional; the buddha realm is the middle. Since discussing the object has two senses, clarifying contemplation in relation to the object also has two senses: first, the three contemplations in sequence; second, the three contemplations in a single mind.

The Three Contemplations in Sequence and the Three Wisdoms

Chinese Source

次第者，如《瓔珞》云「從假入空名二諦觀，從空入假名平等觀，二觀為方便，得入中道第一義諦觀。」

Ru-Yi Translation

As for "in sequence": as the Yingluo Sūtra says, "Entering the empty from the provisional is called the contemplation of the two truths; entering the provisional from the empty is called the contemplation of equality. The two contemplations are the skillful means by which one enters the contemplation of the truth of the middle way of the first meaning."

Chinese Source

此之三觀即是《大品》所明三智：一一切智，知一切內法內名，一切能知能解，一切外法外名能知能解，但不能用以一切道起一切種，故名一切智；

Ru-Yi Translation

These three contemplations are the three wisdoms clarified in the Large Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra. First, the wisdom that knows all things: all inner dharmas and inner names it can know and can understand; all outer dharmas and outer names it can know and can understand; but it cannot use all paths to give rise to all modes. Therefore it is called the wisdom that knows all things.

Chinese Source

二道種智，能知一切道種差別，則分別假名無謬，故名道種智；三一切種智，能於一種智知一切道、知一切種，一相、寂滅相，種種行類能知能解，名一切種智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the wisdom of the paths: it can know the distinctions of all paths and modes, and so it distinguishes provisional names without error. Therefore it is called the wisdom of the paths. Third, the wisdom knowing all modes: within a single wisdom of modes it can know all paths and know all modes; the single mark, the mark of quiescent extinction, and the various kinds of practice it can know and can understand. This is called the wisdom knowing all modes.

Chinese Source

通而為論，觀智是其異名，別而往目，因時名觀、果時名智，此三觀智即是《大經》四種十二因緣觀，下、中、上、上上。

Ru-Yi Translation

Speaking generally, contemplation and wisdom are different names for it; naming them distinctly, at the time of the cause it is called contemplation, and at the time of the fruit it is called wisdom. These three contemplating wisdoms are the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's four kinds of contemplation of the twelve causes and conditions: lower, middle, upper, and highest.

Chinese Source

《涅槃》通取析法明於四觀，《大品》、《瓔珞》直就摩訶衍但明三觀三智，今若開二經合《涅槃》者，應開衍法從假入空，觀生滅一切智也；若合《涅槃》就二經，合下、中二觀同是一切智也；若將三經若開、若合對五眼者，天眼、肉眼照麁細事皆是世智，悉為諸觀境本。若三觀三智，從此即入體法一切智；若四觀四智，此即入析法一切智，故肉眼、天眼為本。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Nirvāṇa takes in generally [even] the analysis of dharmas, and clarifies four contemplations; the Large Prajñāpāramitā and the Yingluo deal directly with the Mahāyāna only, and clarify just three contemplations and three wisdoms. Now, if the two sūtras are opened up to match the Nirvāṇa, then the Mahāyāna dharma of entering the empty from the provisional should be opened [into two, adding] the wisdom that knows all things which contemplates generation and extinction. If the Nirvāṇa is combined to match the two sūtras, then the lower and middle contemplations are combined, both being the wisdom that knows all things. If the three sūtras, whether opened or combined, are matched with the five eyes: the heavenly eye and the physical eye, illuminating coarse and fine phenomena, are both worldly wisdom, and both are the base of the objects of the various contemplations. In the case of three contemplations and three wisdoms, from these one enters the wisdom that knows all things by embodying dharmas; in the case of four contemplations and four wisdoms, from these one enters the wisdom that knows all things by analyzing dharmas. Therefore the physical eye and the heavenly eye are the base.

Chinese Source

若入一切智對慧眼、道種智對法眼、一切種智對佛眼。中論偈「因緣所生法」一句為觀智之本，三句對三智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Entering the wisdom that knows all things corresponds to the wisdom-eye; the wisdom of the paths corresponds to the dharma-eye; the wisdom knowing all modes corresponds to the buddha-eye. In the verse of the Middle Treatise, the one line "dharmas produced by causes and conditions" is the base of contemplating wisdom, and the [other] three lines correspond to the three wisdoms.

Chinese Source

若將三觀智對四教，即須開之如前；若將涅槃四觀對四教，下智是生滅一切智對三藏教也、中智是體法一切智對通教也、上智即道種智對別教、上上智即一切種智對圓教。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the three contemplating wisdoms are matched with the four teachings, they must be opened up as above. If the Nirvāṇa's four contemplations are matched with the four teachings: the lower wisdom is the wisdom that knows all things [through] generation and extinction, corresponding to the Tripiṭaka teaching; the middle wisdom is the wisdom that knows all things by embodying dharmas, corresponding to the shared teaching; the upper wisdom is the wisdom of the paths, corresponding to the distinct teaching; the highest wisdom is the wisdom knowing all modes, corresponding to the perfect teaching.

Chinese Source

所以應明三觀，那忽對四教者何？若無教即無觀，稟教修觀得成於智，所以明教也。教必有主，有主即佛也，或可一佛說四教、或可示四相明四佛。四教既有四主，即應有四補處，即是四種菩薩輔佛弘此四教也。

Ru-Yi Translation

We should be clarifying the three contemplations; why do we suddenly match them with the four teachings? Without teaching there is no contemplation; receiving the teaching and cultivating contemplation, one accomplishes wisdom. That is why the teaching is clarified. A teaching must have a master, and the master is the Buddha. Perhaps one buddha teaches the four teachings; or perhaps four forms are shown, clarifying four buddhas. Since the four teachings have four masters, there should be four successors-in-waiting, that is, four kinds of bodhisattvas who assist the Buddha in spreading these four teachings.

Can What Is Inexpressible Be Taught in Four Ways?

Chinese Source

若言諸法寂滅相不可以言宣，《大經》云「生生不可說，乃至不生不生亦不可說。」一教尚不可說，云何有四？答：理論實爾皆不可說，赴緣利物有因緣故亦可得說，非但生生可說，乃至不生不生亦可說，以佛教門出生死苦。

Ru-Yi Translation

Suppose someone says: "The mark of quiescent extinction of all dharmas cannot be proclaimed in words. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says, 'Generation that generates cannot be explained, … up to: non-generation that does not generate also cannot be explained.' If even one teaching cannot be explained, how can there be four?" Answer: In terms of principle it is truly so: none of them can be explained. But in going to meet conditions to benefit others, because there are causes and conditions, it can also be explained. Not only can generation that generates be explained; even non-generation that does not generate can be explained, using the gates of the Buddha's teaching to get out of the suffering of birth and death.

The Tripiṭaka Teaching

Chinese Source

三藏教者，如《釋論》引迦旃延子明菩薩義，釋迦初為陶師，值昔釋迦佛發願，從是已來始發菩薩心，即是行人所求菩提，即名為法。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Tripiṭaka teaching: as the Commentary on the Treatise cites Kātyāyanīputra's explanation of the meaning of "bodhisattva": Śākyamuni was first a potter; meeting the former Buddha Śākyamuni, he made a vow, and from then on he first aroused the bodhisattva mind. That is the bodhi sought by the practitioner, and it is called the dharma.

Chinese Source

深厭苦集，欣求滅道，即起慈悲心誓度一切，行六度行，行願相扶，拔苦與樂。所以者何？慳名為集，墮餓鬼名苦，行檀名道，慳息名滅。菩薩自伏慳貪，悲心熏物，眾生稱名即能脫苦；自行檀施，慈心熏物，物應可度，即能示現，令得安樂。當知為滿弘誓而修檀行也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Deeply wearied of suffering and origin and joyfully seeking cessation and the path, he gives rise to a mind of loving-kindness and compassion and vows to deliver all. He practices the practices of the six perfections; practice and aspiration support each other, removing suffering and giving happiness. Why is this? Stinginess is called origin; falling among the hungry ghosts is called suffering; practicing dāna is called the path; the ceasing of stinginess is called cessation. The bodhisattva himself subdues stinginess and greed, and with a compassionate mind perfumes others; when beings call his name, they can be freed from suffering. He himself practices the giving of dāna, and with a loving mind perfumes others; when others are fit to be delivered, he can manifest and enable them to attain peace and happiness. One should know that it is to fulfill the great vows that he cultivates the practice of dāna.

Chinese Source

乃至愚癡名集、生天名苦、修慧名道、癡伏名滅，修慧度時自破苦、集，為成悲心，以熏眾生，眾生稱名即得解脫；自證道、滅，以成慈心，以熏眾生，眾生有感，應機得度。故知行填於願。

Ru-Yi Translation

And so on up to: foolishness is called origin; being born in heaven is called suffering; cultivating wisdom is called the path; the subduing of foolishness is called cessation. When he cultivates the perfection of wisdom, he himself breaks suffering and origin; to accomplish the compassionate mind, he perfumes beings with it, and when beings call his name they attain liberation. He himself realizes path and cessation; to accomplish the loving mind, he perfumes beings with it, and when beings have an appeal, he responds to the capacity and they are delivered. So one knows that practice fills out the aspiration.

Chinese Source

行此六度各論時節，尸毘代鴿是檀滿、須摩提不妄語是尸滿、歌利王割截不動是忍滿、大施抒海是精進滿、尚闍梨坐禪是定滿、劬儐大臣分地是般若滿，

Ru-Yi Translation

In practicing these six perfections, each is discussed with its occasion: King Śibi taking the dove's place is the fulfillment of dāna; Sumati not lying is the fulfillment of śīla; not being moved when King Kali cut him apart is the fulfillment of patience; Mahādāna bailing out the ocean is the fulfillment of vigor; the seer Shangsheli sitting in meditation is the fulfillment of concentration; the great minister Govinda dividing the land is the fulfillment of prajñā.

Chinese Source

如此修行至初僧祇劫，不知作佛不作佛；第二僧祇，心知作佛，口不言作佛；第三僧祇，心知口言；過三僧祇已，又百劫種相，百福凡用三千二百福，修成三十二大人相現時方稱菩薩摩訶薩，但伏惑不斷，如無脂肥羊，取世智為般若即此意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Practicing thus, up to the first asaṃkhyeya eon he does not know whether he will become a buddha or not; in the second asaṃkhyeya, his mind knows he will become a buddha, but his mouth does not say he will become a buddha; in the third asaṃkhyeya, his mind knows and his mouth says it. When the three asaṃkhyeyas have passed, he further plants the marks for a hundred eons — a hundred merits [for each mark], using three thousand two hundred merits in all — and when the thirty-two marks of a great person have been cultivated to completion and appear, only then is he called a bodhisattva-mahāsattva. But he only subdues delusion and does not sever it, like a sheep that is plump without fat. "Taking worldly wisdom as prajñā" has just this meaning.

Chinese Source

用此菩薩行對聲聞行位者，初僧祇可對總別念處；二僧祇可對煖法；三僧祇可對頂法；百劫種相可對忍法；坐道場時可對世第一；三十四心斷結成佛，即對十六心發真，乃至九解脫無學也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Matching this bodhisattva practice with the stages of practice of the śrāvakas: the first asaṃkhyeya can be matched with the general and particular foundations of mindfulness; the second asaṃkhyeya with the dharma of warmth; the third asaṃkhyeya with the dharma of summit; the hundred eons of planting the marks with the dharma of forbearance; the time of sitting at the site of awakening with the supreme worldly [dharma]; severing the bonds with thirty-four moments of mind and becoming a buddha corresponds to the sixteen moments of mind in which the true arises, up through the nine liberations and the one beyond learning.

Chinese Source

爾時，坐道場上三十四心斷惑，正習俱盡，名為三藏佛。所以釋迦精進，弟子純熟，以精進故，九劫前超八相成佛，此即是三藏教主所說教門。此中補處位在百劫種相，伏惑住最後身，六度行成，誓願將滿，慈悲熏於眾生，拔苦與樂。若就此辯者，但是因緣生法，世智明觀，即是三藏教觀世音義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, on the seat at the site of awakening, with thirty-four moments of mind he severs delusion, and both the main [delusion] and the habit-energy are exhausted. This is called the Tripiṭaka buddha. Thus, because Śākyamuni was vigorous while his disciples were fully ripened, through vigor he leapt ahead by nine eons and accomplished buddhahood with the eight phases. This is the gate of teaching taught by the master of the Tripiṭaka teaching. The position of the successor-in-waiting in this [teaching] lies in the hundred eons of planting the marks: subduing delusion, he dwells in his last body; his practice of the six perfections is accomplished and his vows and aspirations are about to be fulfilled; with loving-kindness and compassion he perfumes beings, removing suffering and giving happiness. Discussed on this basis, it is only dharmas produced by causes and conditions, with contemplation clarified by worldly wisdom. This is the meaning of "Avalokiteśvara" in the Tripiṭaka teaching.

A Question on the Hundred Eons

Chinese Source

問：依三藏說，釋迦、彌勒同時發心，一超九劫，何意二佛俱成賢劫中佛耶？答：釋迦值弗沙，促百劫；彌勒值諸佛，何必不促為九十一劫耶？若爾，則無百劫義。答：任此法門則有百劫，以精進力傳超。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: According to the Tripiṭaka teaching, Śākyamuni and Maitreya aroused the mind at the same time, and one leapt ahead by nine eons. Why then are both buddhas buddhas of the present Auspicious Eon? Answer: Śākyamuni, meeting Puṣya, shortened the hundred eons; Maitreya met many buddhas — why must he not have shortened them to ninety-one eons? [Question:] If so, then there is no meaning of a hundred eons. Answer: According to this dharma-gate, there are a hundred eons; by the power of vigor one leaps ahead in turn.

The Shared Teaching

Chinese Source

通教者，如《大品》明三乘之人同以第一義諦無言說道，斷煩惱、入涅槃，共緣一理，用觀斷惑通也，亦名共般若教。

Ru-Yi Translation

The shared teaching: as the Large Prajñāpāramitā clarifies, people of the three vehicles alike, by the wordless path of the truth of the first meaning, sever afflictions and enter nirvāṇa. They take one principle as object in common, and the use of contemplation to sever delusion is shared. It is also called the teaching of shared prajñā.

Chinese Source

此事與三藏異，《釋論》破云「豈以不淨心修菩薩行？如毒器盛食，食則殺人。」檀有上、中、下，謂捨財、身、命也。勇士、烈女亦能捨身，何得中捨名檀滿？中檀但名施，非波羅蜜；不見能所、財物三事皆空、非慳非施，此是真檀波羅蜜；乃至非愚、非智，無著空慧，名真般若，不取世智。

Ru-Yi Translation

This matter differs from the Tripiṭaka. The Commentary on the Treatise refutes [the Tripiṭaka view], saying: "How could one cultivate bodhisattva practice with an impure mind? It is like food served in a poisoned vessel: whoever eats it is killed." Dāna has upper, middle, and lower: giving up wealth, body, and life. Brave men and chaste women can also give up their bodies; how can the middle giving-up be called the fulfillment of dāna? Middle dāna is only called giving, not pāramitā. Not seeing giver and receiver, the three things together with the goods being all empty, neither stingy nor giving — this is true dāna-pāramitā; and so on up to: neither foolish nor wise, unattached empty wisdom, is called true prajñā; worldly wisdom is not taken.

Chinese Source

論云「若不信空，一切皆違失。」當知：汝所修皆不與理相應。若信諸法空，一切有所作。良以空故，能成一切諸法，故知若得空慧，能具一切法也。又復菩薩無量劫修行，何但三阿僧祇？如是等種種破三藏失，以顯摩訶衍中通教意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Treatise says: "If one does not believe in emptiness, everything goes wrong." You should know that none of what you cultivate accords with principle. If one believes that all dharmas are empty, everything can be carried out. Precisely because of emptiness, all dharmas can be accomplished; so one knows that if one attains the wisdom of emptiness, one can be complete with all dharmas. Moreover, the bodhisattva practices for immeasurable eons — why only three asaṃkhyeyas? With various [arguments] such as these the faults of the Tripiṭaka are refuted, to reveal the meaning of the shared teaching within the Mahāyāna.

Chinese Source

《大品》云「菩薩發心與薩婆若相應」，此即觀真斷結與理相應也。發心已來即觀真斷結，便稱菩薩，即是假人也。又，觀真即是法也，常與慈悲俱起，自斷苦集、修道滅，亦以慈悲誓願斷一切眾生苦集與其道滅，體達諸法如幻、如化，不生、不滅，三事俱亡以行檀，乃至一切法無所著名般若。以此諸行填願，即能破四住惑，見第一義，則有三乘共十地，所謂乾慧乃至佛地。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Large Prajñāpāramitā says: "When a bodhisattva arouses the mind, it accords with sarvajñatā." This is contemplating the true and severing the bonds in accordance with principle. From the arousing of the mind onward he contemplates the true and severs the bonds, and is then called a bodhisattva; that is the provisional person. Moreover, contemplating the true is the dharma. It always arises together with loving-kindness and compassion: he himself severs suffering and origin and cultivates path and cessation, and with loving-kindness, compassion, vows, and aspirations he also severs the suffering and origin of all beings and gives them path and cessation. Thoroughly realizing that all dharmas are like illusions, like magical creations, neither generated nor extinguished, with the three things all vanished, he practices dāna; and so on up to: being attached to no dharma at all is called prajñā. When these practices fill out the aspirations, he can break the delusions of the four abodes and see the first meaning. Then there are the ten grounds shared by the three vehicles, namely the dry-wisdom [ground] up to the buddha ground.

If one contemplates the mind as neither empty nor existent, then all that is generated from mind — dharmas too — are neither empty nor existent; thus all dharmas are within a single mind. One who can contemplate the mind thus is said to have the highest contemplation and attains the bodhi of all buddhas. The Vimalakīrti says: "As I contemplate the real mark of the body, so I contemplate the Buddha." Since contemplating the mark of the body is equal to the Buddha, contemplating the mark of the mind is also equal to the Buddha. The Avataṃsaka says: "Mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without difference." One should know that contemplating this source of mind is equal to the Tathāgata. Contemplating the mind with any other contemplation is all skillful means and is called false contemplation.

Chinese Source

若作如此圓觀，名為真實正觀，即開佛知見，坐如來座，如此慈悲即是入如來室、安忍此法即是著如來衣、修此觀慧即是如來莊嚴，其人行住坐臥皆應起塔，生如來想，如此觀心名觀佛心也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Making such a perfect contemplation is called true and real correct contemplation. It is opening the buddha's knowing and seeing and sitting on the Tathāgata's seat. Such loving-kindness and compassion is entering the Tathāgata's room; patiently accepting this dharma is putting on the Tathāgata's robe; cultivating this contemplative wisdom is the Tathāgata's adornment. Wherever such a person walks, stands, sits, or lies down, a stūpa should be raised and he should be regarded as the Tathāgata. Contemplating the mind thus is called contemplating the buddha-mind.

Chinese Source

第二明普門，即為二：一通途明門、二歷十義解釋。通六意者，一略列門名、二示門相、三明權實、四明普不普、五約四隨、六明觀心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying "the Universal Gate." This has two parts: first, clarifying the gate in general terms; second, explaining it through ten meanings. The general account has six points: first, briefly listing the names of the gates; second, showing the characteristics of the gates; third, clarifying the provisional and the real; fourth, clarifying universal and not universal; fifth, in terms of the four accommodations; sixth, clarifying the contemplation of mind.

Chinese Source

列門名者，通從世間，如人門戶通至貴賤居室，凡鄙以十惡、五逆為門，通至三途；清昇以五戒、十善、四禪、四定等為門，通至人、天；外道以斷、常為門，通至惑苦；愛以四倒為門，見以四句為門。善惡雖殊，束而為言，俱是有漏世間之門，通至生死爾。

Listing the names of the gates. Generally, from the worldly, it is like a person's doorway, which leads into the dwellings of the noble or the humble. The base and vulgar take the ten evils and the five heinous crimes as their gate, leading to the three bad paths. The pure and ascending take the five precepts, the ten good deeds, the four dhyānas, the four [formless] concentrations, and so on as their gate, leading to humans and gods. Non-Buddhists take annihilation and permanence as their gate, leading to delusion and suffering. Craving takes the four inversions as its gate; views take the four propositions as their gate. Though good and evil differ, gathered together and spoken of, all are gates of the contaminated world, leading only to birth and death.

Chinese Source

若就佛法論門亦復眾多，三藏四門通有餘、無餘涅槃；通教四門近通化城，遠通常住；別教四門漸通常住；圓教四門頓通常住，此則四四十六教門，又有十六觀門，合三十二門，能通之義分別其相，在《大本玄》中。

Ru-Yi Translation

If gates are discussed in terms of the buddha-dharma, they too are many. The four gates of the Tripiṭaka lead to nirvāṇa with and without remainder. The four gates of the shared teaching lead near to the phantom city and far to the eternally abiding. The four gates of the distinct teaching lead gradually to the eternally abiding. The four gates of the perfect teaching lead suddenly to the eternally abiding. These are the four times four, sixteen gates of teaching. There are also sixteen gates of contemplation, making thirty-two gates in all. The meaning of their giving passage and the distinctions of their characteristics are in the Profound Meaning of the great text.

The Characteristics of the Gates

Chinese Source

二示門相者，三藏四門，所謂：阿毘曇是有門、成實是空門、昆勒亦空亦有門、車匿非空非有門，一一廣明行法判賢聖位，由門通理。通教四門者，謂如幻之有、如幻之空、亦空亦有、非空非有，一一作行相判賢聖位，由門通理。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, showing the characteristics of the gates. The four gates of the Tripiṭaka are: the Abhidharma is the gate of existence; the Satyasiddhi is the gate of emptiness; the Kunle is the gate of both empty and existent; Chandaka's is the gate of neither empty nor existent. Each one sets out the method of practice at length and determines the stages of worthies and sages; through the gate one reaches principle. The four gates of the shared teaching are: existence that is like an illusion, emptiness that is like an illusion, both empty and existent, and neither empty nor existent. Each one sets out the characteristics of practice and determines the stages of worthies and sages; through the gate one reaches principle.

Chinese Source

別教四門，觀佛性如闇室瓶盆即有門；觀佛性如空迦毘羅城空即無門；觀佛性如石中金，福人得寶、罪人見石，是亦有亦無門；觀佛性離二邊，即中道，非有非無門。一一作行相判位，由門通理。

Ru-Yi Translation

The four gates of the distinct teaching: contemplating the buddha-nature as like a jar or basin in a dark room is the gate of existence; contemplating the buddha-nature as empty, as the city of Kapilavastu is empty, is the gate of nonexistence; contemplating the buddha-nature as like gold in stone — the fortunate person obtains the treasure, the guilty person sees stone — is the gate of both existence and nonexistence; contemplating the buddha-nature as apart from the two extremes, that is, the middle way, is the gate of neither existence nor nonexistence. Each one sets out the characteristics of practice and determines the stages; through the gate one reaches principle.

Chinese Source

圓教四門名不異別，但一門即三門、三門即一門，不一、不四，無歷別之殊，圓融不四之四，一一判不思議行位之相，由門通理，此義皆在《大本》。

Ru-Yi Translation

The names of the four gates of the perfect teaching do not differ from the distinct, but one gate is identical to three gates and three gates are identical to one gate; not one, not four, without the difference of separate sequence; perfectly fused, the four that are not four. Each one determines the characteristics of the inconceivable stages of practice; through the gate one reaches principle. These meanings are all in the great text.

Provisional and Real; Universal and Not Universal

Chinese Source

次論諸門權實，三藏通教教觀，十六門能通、所通皆是權；別教教觀，能通是權、所通是實；圓教教觀，八門能通、所通皆是實，具論在彼《玄義》。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, discussing the provisional and the real of the gates. In the teaching and contemplation of the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings, of the sixteen gates both what gives passage and what is passed through are provisional. In the teaching and contemplation of the distinct teaching, what gives passage is provisional and what is passed through is real. In the teaching and contemplation of the perfect teaching, of the eight gates both what gives passage and what is passed through are real. This is discussed in full in that Profound Meaning.

Chinese Source

次明普不普者，若凡夫外道見、愛等門，尚不能通出三界，何況普耶？三藏通教雖通化城，亦復非普；別教漸通，亦非普義；唯圓教教觀實相法門能遍十法界，千性相三諦一時圓通，圓通中道，雙照二諦，獨稱為普門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, clarifying universal and not universal. The gates of views, craving, and so on of ordinary people and non-Buddhists cannot even lead out of the three realms, let alone be universal. The Tripiṭaka and shared teachings, though they lead to the phantom city, are also not universal. The distinct teaching leads gradually, and this too is not the meaning of universal. Only the dharma-gate of the real mark in the teaching and contemplation of the perfect teaching can pervade the ten dharma-realms — the thousand natures and characteristics and the three truths perfectly penetrated all at once; perfectly penetrating the middle way and illuminating both of the two truths — and it alone is called the Universal Gate.

Entering the Gate of Nonduality

Chinese Source

復次，如《淨名》中說入不二門者，生死、涅槃為二，不依生死、不依涅槃，名為不二，亦復非一。何以故？既除於二，若復在一，一對不一，還復成二，豈名不二耶？今不在二，故言不一、不二，亦名不有、不無，不有是破假、不無是破空，不有是破二、不無是破一，若爾者，應存中道，中道亦空。《大經》云「明與無明，其性不二。」不二之性即是中道，中道既空於二邊，此空亦空，故名空空，空名不可得空，是為入不二法門，即是圓教就空門辯普門之意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, as the Vimalakīrti explains entering the gate of nonduality: birth and death and nirvāṇa are two; relying neither on birth and death nor on nirvāṇa is called nonduality, and yet it is also not one. Why? Having removed the two, if one then remains in one, one stands opposed to not-one, and it becomes two again; how could that be called nonduality? Now, not remaining in two, it is said to be neither one nor two; it is also called neither existent nor nonexistent. "Not existent" breaks the provisional; "not nonexistent" breaks the empty; "not existent" breaks the two; "not nonexistent" breaks the one. If so, one should keep the middle way; but the middle way too is empty. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: "Brightness and ignorance — their nature is not two." The nature of nonduality is the middle way. Since the middle way empties the two extremes, this emptiness too is empty; so it is called the emptiness of emptiness. Emptiness is called the emptiness of unobtainability. This is entering the dharma-gate of nonduality; it is the meaning of the perfect teaching discussing the Universal Gate by way of the gate of emptiness.

Chinese Source

三十一菩薩各說不二門、文殊說無說為不二門、淨名杜口為不二門，細尋彼文皆有四門義。肇師注云「諸菩薩歷言法相即有門，文殊言於無言此即空門。」《思益》云「一切法正，一切法邪」，亦是普門意。遊心法界如虛空，是亦空、亦有門。淨名默然即非空、非有門。《大品》四十二字門，先阿後茶，中有四十字，皆具諸字功德，此亦是不二普門。上〈方便品〉云「其智慧門難解難入。」譬喻云「唯有一門而復狹小。」眾經明實理門者，悉普門意也。四隨、觀心等悉在《大本》。

Ru-Yi Translation

The thirty-one bodhisattvas each spoke of the gate of nonduality; Mañjuśrī spoke of not speaking as the gate of nonduality; Vimalakīrti shut his mouth as the gate of nonduality. Examining that text closely, all have the meaning of the four gates. Master Zhao's commentary says: "The bodhisattvas' speaking through the dharma-marks one by one is the gate of existence; Mañjuśrī's speaking about the wordless is the gate of emptiness." The Viśeṣacinti says: "All dharmas are correct; all dharmas are false"; this too is the meaning of the Universal Gate. Letting the mind roam the dharma-realm like empty space is the gate of both empty and existent. Vimalakīrti's silence is the gate of neither empty nor existent. The forty-two letter-gates of the Large Prajñāpāramitā, first a and last ḍha, with forty letters between, each having the merits of all the letters — this too is the nondual Universal Gate. The "Skillful Means" chapter above says: "The gate of their wisdom is hard to understand and hard to enter." The "Parable" [chapter] says: "There is only one gate, and moreover it is narrow." Wherever the sūtras clarify the gate of the real principle, it is entirely the meaning of the Universal Gate. The four accommodations, the contemplation of mind, and so on are all in the great text.

Reading Notes for Part 3

Translator's notes

Where Part 3 begins and ends. Line 607 is the heading of Fascicle Two; line 606 (end title of Fascicle One) belongs to Part 2. The source range ends in mid-list (「九供養」); the rest of item 9 (「諸佛普」) opens Part 4. The English of the last paragraph is cut at the same point and marked.

Contemplating (觀), illuminating (照), seeing (見). 觀 is always "contemplate/contemplation," 照 "illuminate," 見 "see." 智 is "wisdom" (as in the fixed names of the three wisdoms); 慧 is also "wisdom," as in Parts 1–2 (修慧, 實相慧, 定慧). 實 is "real," 真 "true," except in the fixed term "the real truth" (真諦). 無為 is "unconditioned," as in Part 1; 無作 is "without fabrication," as in "the Four Noble Truths without fabrication" (Part 1).

Vow (誓) and aspiration (願). Part 4 glosses the two words separately (誓 "restraint," 願 "seeking with a pledge"), so they are kept apart throughout: 誓 "vow," 願 "aspiration," 誓願 "vows and aspirations," and 弘誓 "great vows" as fixed.

Six objects and five sets of contemplation. The six objects (causes and conditions, four truths, three truths, two truths, one real truth, no truth) and the numbers of "sets" (番) of contemplation follow the treatment of the wondrous of the object and of wisdom in the Fahua Xuanyi. The text calls that work 《大本玄義》, 《大本玄》, or simply 《大本》; these are rendered Profound Meaning of the great text and great text. The usual reading (e.g., in Zhili's Guanyin xuanyi ji) is that it means the "larger" exposition of the Lotus, i.e., the Fahua Xuanyi.

The Yingluo Sūtra (《瓔珞》). The Pusa yingluo benye jing (T1485). Verified: T1485 has 「三觀者，從假名入空二諦觀，從空入假名平等觀。是二觀方便道」; the wording quoted here differs slightly (「從假入空」, 「二觀為方便，得入中道第一義諦觀」).

Three wisdoms and the Large Prajñāpāramitā. The definitions of the three wisdoms paraphrase the Large Prajñāpāramitā as read in the Great Treatise; the exact wording was not found in T0223 (source not identified verbatim). 「能於一種智」 is rendered "within a single wisdom of modes"; alternatively: "with a single kind of wisdom."

Four contemplations of the twelve causes and conditions. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374, fasc. 27) distinguishes contemplation of the twelve links by lower, middle, upper, and highest wisdom. "The two sūtras" are the Large Prajñāpāramitā and the Yingluo; "the three sūtras" add the Nirvāṇa.

The Middle Treatise verse. The famous four lines (「因緣所生法，我說即是空，亦為是假名，亦是中道義」) are quoted in the Tiantai form; Kumārajīva's text (T1564) reads 「眾因緣生法，我說即是無」. Only the first line is quoted here.

"Successors-in-waiting" (補處). A bodhisattva one life from buddhahood, who will succeed the present buddha.

The Commentary on the Treatise (《釋論》). Another name for the Great Treatise (Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra, T1509); kept distinct in English only because the Chinese title differs. The account of Kātyāyanīputra's Sarvāstivāda view of the bodhisattva (three asaṃkhyeya eons, a hundred eons planting the marks, the six perfections exemplified by Śibi and others) and its refutation are in fascicle 4 of the treatise. The examples of the perfections are verified there (Śibi, Mahādāna bailing the ocean 大施菩薩…誓抒大海, the seer 尚闍梨 with birds nesting in his hair, the minister 劬嬪 dividing Jambudvīpa into seven). For śīla the treatise's example is King 須陀須摩 (Sutasoma), who kept his word; the text here writes 須摩提, which in the treatise is the name of a different bodhisattva (Sumati, who bought flowers for Dīpaṃkara). The English follows the text.

"That is the bodhi sought by the practitioner, and it is called the dharma" (即是行人所求菩提，即名為法). CBETA's punctuation is followed. Alternatively: "That is the practitioner; the bodhi he seeks is called the dharma" — the text later contrasts the person (人) and the dharma (法) in the word "bodhisattva."

"Being born in heaven is called suffering" (生天名苦). Taken literally: in the scheme for the perfection of wisdom, rebirth in heaven still counts as the truth of suffering.

"Like a sheep that is plump without fat" (如無脂肥羊). The image is of something that looks substantial but lacks the essential: the Tripiṭaka bodhisattva appears accomplished but has not severed delusion. Source not identified.

Stages matched to śrāvaka stages. Warmth, summit, forbearance, and the supreme worldly dharma are the four wholesome roots; "sixteen moments in which the true arises" is the path of seeing; "nine liberations" are the nine paths of liberation of the path of cultivation; "the one beyond learning" (無學) is the arhat.

"Leapt ahead by nine eons." The Great Treatise (fasc. 4) tells how Śākyamuni, praising the buddha Puṣya (弗沙) for seven days and nights, passed over nine eons. "The present Auspicious Eon" renders 賢劫. 「以精進力傳超」: 傳 is odd; it may be an error for 得 ("is able to leap ahead"). The English "leaps ahead in turn" is tentative.

"Food served in a poisoned vessel" and "if one does not believe in emptiness." Both are given as quotations from the treatise, but neither phrase was found verbatim in T1509 (source not identified). The idea of the second is close to the Middle Treatise 24.14 (「以有空義故，一切法得成」, verified in T1564).

"When a bodhisattva arouses the mind, it accords with sarvajñatā." Not found verbatim in T0223; the Large Prajñāpāramitā often speaks of practice "accorded with" sarvajñatā (薩婆若). Source not identified verbatim.

The shared ten grounds. Dry-wisdom (乾慧), nature (性), eighth-person (八人), seeing (見), thinning (薄), free of desire (離欲), what has been done (已辦), pratyekabuddha, bodhisattva, buddha. 「道觀雙流斷正侵，習，佛地盡故」: CBETA's commas look misplaced; the English reads 「斷正、侵習；佛地[正習]盡故」 ("severing the main and encroaching on the habit-energy; the buddha ground, because [both] are exhausted"). The Chinese is left as CBETA has it.

"This person's afflictions are exhausted…" Given as a quotation from the treatise; not found verbatim in T1509 (source not identified).

"This teaching" (比教). The text reads 比 ("compare"), almost certainly an error for 此 ("this"); the English reads 此.

The Large Prajñāpāramitā on the distinct teaching. The first clause (bodhisattvas who "play freely in spiritual powers and purify buddha-lands") is in T0223; the rest of what CBETA puts inside the quotation marks ("first driving back the four abodes…") reads like Zhiyi's own gloss.

Iron wheel, copper wheel. In the Tiantai stage scheme the ten faiths are the iron-wheel stage and the ten abodes the copper-wheel stage, after the wheels of the wheel-turning kings. "Resemblance-understanding" (似解) is the stage of identity in resemblance.

"Opening, showing, awakening, entering" (開示悟入). From the Lotus, "Skillful Means" chapter: the buddhas appear to open, show, awaken beings to, and lead them into the buddha's knowing and seeing.

Lotus quotations. "Straightforwardly setting aside skillful means…" and "Now I shall tell you the most real matter" (今為汝等說最實事) are verified in T0262. "Although various paths are taught…" is close to T0262 「雖示種種道，其實為佛乘」. "Only this one matter is real…" and "among the Tathāgata's other profound dharmas…" are verified (T0262).

Other verified quotations. Large Prajñāpāramitā, gate of the letter a (阿字門, T0223). Nirvāṇa Sūtra: 「發心畢竟二不別，如是二心先心難」 (T0374; text has 前心); "One who studies the Great Vehicle, although he has the physical eye…" (T0374); "brightness and ignorance… their nature is not two" (T0374, 「明與無明…其性無二」). Great Treatise: "The ten wisdoms enter the wisdom accordant with reality…" (T1509, 「是十智入如實智中，失本名字」). Vimalakīrti: "As I contemplate the real mark of the body, so I contemplate the Buddha" (T0475). Avataṃsaka: "Mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without difference" (T0278). The "skilled painter" verse is in T0278 in a different order (「心如工畫師，畫種種五陰，一切世界中，無法而不造」). Viśeṣacinti: "All dharmas are correct; all dharmas are false" (T0586). Lotus "Parable" chapter: "only one gate, and moreover it is narrow" (T0262 「唯有一門，而復狹小」); "Skillful Means" chapter: "the gate of their wisdom is hard to understand and hard to enter" (T0262).

Medicine-jewel; the flawless treasure-store; Śūraṃgama. The passage maps the two bodies and the three virtues: the true body pervading the dharma-realm, vajra prajñā removing suffering at the root (liberation), and the Śūraṃgama samādhi, "the mark of strength" (健相 is the usual Chinese gloss of śūraṃgama), giving the happiness of "the three-dot nirvāṇa" (the three dots of the letter i, standing for the three virtues).

The waxing and waning moon. Verified in the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374): 「而此月性，實無虧盈」. "Neither lacking nor full" renders 不偏圓; "not without fullness and decline" renders 不無盈昃. 體 is kept as "essence" even for the moon. "The thirty minds" are the ten abodes, ten practices, and ten dedications; the forty-two worthies and sages of the perfect teaching are those stages plus the ten grounds, virtual awakening, and wondrous awakening. The Benevolent Kings (T0245) speaks of fourteen forbearances and of "fourteen prajñāpāramitās" (verified); the Heavenly King is presumably the Pravara-devarāja-paripṛcchā, not checked.

"One teaching with four expressions" (一教四詮). Alternatively: "one teaching expressing [what] the four [teachings express]." The quotation from "opening the chapter above" does not match the earlier wording exactly; it is a paraphrase of 「今對境明觀亦為二意：一次第三觀、二一心三觀」.

"The distinct is used to reveal the perfect" (用別顯圓). Alternatively: "differentiation is used to reveal the perfect"; 別 here may mean the differentiated four teachings rather than the distinct teaching alone.

The four phases (四運). Not yet thinking, about to think, thinking, and having thought, the moments in which the mind is examined in Tiantai practice. 「若念未念」 is rendered "whether in thought or before thought."

Room, robe, and seat of the Tathāgata. From the Lotus, "Teacher of the Dharma" chapter. Raising a stūpa wherever the practitioner is also echoes that chapter.

The four accommodations (四隨). Accommodating to beings' wishes, to what suits them, to what counteracts their faults, and to the first meaning — the four siddhāntas seen as ways of following beings. This point and the contemplation of mind are not expanded here.

The four gates of the Tripiṭaka. The Kunle (昆勒, also written 蜫勒 or 毘勒) is a lost treatise associated with Mahākātyāyana; the Sanskrit name is uncertain. Chandaka (車匿) is the Buddha's former charioteer, who was taught through the gate of "neither empty nor existent."

The gates of the distinct teaching. The images (a jar in a dark room, the empty city of Kapilavastu, gold in stone) come from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's discussion of buddha-nature. 有 / 無 are rendered "existence / nonexistence," and 空 "emptiness," to keep them apart.

"Master Zhao's commentary." Sengzhao's commentary in the Zhu Weimojie jing (T1775). Verified in substance: 「上諸菩薩措言於法相，文殊有言於無言，淨名無言於無言」; the wording here is a paraphrase.

The forty-two letter-gates. The Large Prajñāpāramitā's syllabary (T0223, verified 「四十二字」), from a to ḍha (茶).
