The Three Bodies and the Introduction to the Lifespan Chapter

This part covers the second half of the second fascicle of Zhili's Notes, following Zhiyi's Textual Commentary from the end of the narrating introduction to the end of the introductory part of the Golden Light Sūtra. Zhili first finishes the verses that narrate the "Emptiness" chapter: bathing, listening, and utmost mind are a method that uses the three kinds of action to indicate the three virtues. The longest section explains the title "Lifespan." Zhili works through the Commentary's account of the body, life, and measure of the Dharma-, reward-, and response-buddhas, adds the self-enjoyment and other-enjoyment bodies and the two consciousnesses of the Awakening of Faith to show how the response pervades the three lands, and insists that the three bodies, as "cultivation two, nature one," are neither vertical nor horizontal. He then follows the Commentary's second explanation (profound meaning, proof, and return to the source) and the remaining five particular introductions: Faith-Mark's doubt about the eighty-year life, with the restraint and action of not killing and giving food across the five vehicles; the ten auspicious marks read as the ten grounds; the stopping of the doubt by the three "should nots"; and the gathering of the assembly, which he uses to classify the sūtra as vaipulya and to discuss the four alternatives of vehicle and precepts.

The Title "Lifespan": The Dharma-Buddha

Chinese Source

三、明疑念序，二：初、釋〈壽量品〉題，二：初、正釋，二：初、約三佛難思以釋，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the introduction of harboring doubt, in two: first, explaining the title of the "Lifespan" chapter, in two: first, explaining it directly, in two: first, explaining in terms of the inconceivability of the three buddhas, in two:

Chinese Source

初、佛本無三。不唯法佛無身壽量，報應亦乃不可說三，以一切法離於名字言說相故。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the Buddha at root has no three. Not only does the Dharma-buddha have no body, life, or measure; the reward[-buddha] and the response[-buddha] too cannot be spoken of as three, because all dharmas are apart from the marks of names, words, and speech.

Chinese Source

二、「隨順」下，隨世俱立。眾生若有得益因緣，即須隨世立諸名相。非唯應佛立身壽量，法報亦乃可論此三。須知無三，不減一法；雖立三三，不增一法。良以有無皆法界故，故真無俗有、真有俗無皆是悉檀，令於三身得四益也。此文分二：初、列三身各三。二、釋三佛三義，二：初、約義分別；二、據理融即。分別且從修二性一，令義易明。法是本覺、報是始覺，始本一合方有應用。一往似縱，說者應以次融即文非縱非橫，妙會此義，令其聞者識圓三身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "in accord with the world" on, establishing them all in accord with the world. If beings have the causes and conditions for gaining benefit, one must, in accord with the world, establish names and marks. Not only for the response-buddha are body, life, and measure established; for the Dharma[-buddha] and the reward[-buddha] too these three can be discussed. One must know that though there are no three, not a single dharma is diminished; though the three times three are established, not a single dharma is added. Truly because existence and nonexistence are both the dharma-realm, "nonexistent in the real, existent in the conventional" and "existent in the real, nonexistent in the conventional" are all siddhāntas, making [beings] obtain the four benefits with respect to the three bodies. This text is divided into two: first, listing three of each for the three bodies; second, explaining the three meanings of the three buddhas, in two: first, distinguishing in terms of meaning; second, interfusing in identity according to principle. The distinguishing for the time being follows "cultivation two, nature one," to make the meaning easy to clarify. The Dharma[-body] is original awakening, the reward is initial awakening; only when initial and original unite as one is there the responsive function. In a first pass this seems vertical; the expounder should use the following text on interfusion and identity, neither vertical nor horizontal, to grasp this meaning wondrously, so that hearers recognize the perfect three bodies.

文分三：初、「法身」下，法佛，二：初、別釋，三：初、身。始覺報智依本覺成，故以法性而為師軌。究論始本唯是一覺，故云還以法性為身。是故馬鳴歸命三寶，即以佛身及體相等而為法寶。此身等者，非分段變易色質心智三科所攝、乃是常住五陰等法聚以為身。此法無相不可說示、為眾生故彊名法身。

Ru-Yi Translation

The text is divided into three: first, from "The Dharma-body" on, the Dharma-buddha, in two: first, explaining separately, in three: first, body. The reward-wisdom of initial awakening is accomplished in reliance on original awakening; hence it takes the dharma-nature as teacher and norm. Discussed thoroughly, initial and original are only one awakening; hence it says "in turn takes the dharma-nature as body." Therefore Aśvaghoṣa, taking refuge in the Three Jewels, takes the Buddha's body together with its essence, marks, and so on as the Dharma Jewel. "This body" and so on: it is not included in the three categories of form-substance and mind-wisdom of the segmented [birth-and-death] and [the birth-and-death of] change; rather, a heap of dharmas such as the eternally abiding five aggregates is taken as the body. This dharma is markless and cannot be spoken or shown; for beings' sake it is forcibly named the Dharma-body.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "The life of the dharma-nature" on, clarifying life. Only when form and mind join is there the life of continuity obtained as retribution. Since the body is not the aggregates, how could it have a life-faculty? For beings' sake, not shifting is forcibly designated life.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "This life" on, clarifying measure. Measure depends on body and life, so it is likewise forcibly designated.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "This is the body that is no body" on, explaining body, life, and measure in general. As for the "no," "no," and "no" (非、無、不) said above: according to the next passage, the meaning should be the double negation. Because this Dharma-essence is apart from the two extremes and surpasses the reward and the response, the three names body, life, and measure are forcibly established on this principle, to make beings forget their feelings.

The Reward-Buddha

Chinese Source

二、「報身」下，明報佛，二：初、稱法有報，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "The reward-body" on, clarifying the reward-buddha, in two: first, that there is a reward in accord with the Dharma, in two:

Chinese Source

初、引經。此從順修稱理事說，修行所感，釋報義也。《法華》文證智德之報，以彼經云「慧光照無量」故。《涅槃》文證斷德之報，以云「大般涅槃」故。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, citing the sūtras. This speaks from the matter of compliant cultivation according with principle: "brought about by practice" explains the meaning of reward. The text of the Lotus proves the reward of the virtue of wisdom, because that sūtra says, "its light of insight shines without limit." The text of the Nirvāṇa proves the reward of the virtue of severance, because it says "great parinirvāṇa."

Chinese Source

二、「如如」下，釋義。修行所感二種之報，乃是始覺與本覺合，即以始本而為境智，此二不異故名曰如。各二如者，智如如境、境如如智故也。復以菩提名如、如智為能應冥，法性名於如、如之境為所應冥。以函蓋合，先喻應義。函蓋雖合，猶存際畔，復以水乳喻其相冥，此乃泯然而成一相。始覺本覺義二體一，正同於此。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Suchness-wisdom" on, explaining the meaning. The two kinds of reward brought about by practice are initial awakening uniting with original awakening: initial and original are taken as object and wisdom, and because these two are not different it is called "suchness." As for each having the doubled "such" (如如): wisdom is such as the object is such, and the object is such as wisdom is such. Further, bodhi is named "such," and the "such" wisdom is what accords and merges; the dharma-nature is named "such," and the "such" object is what is accorded and merged with. By the uniting of box and lid it first illustrates the meaning of according. Though box and lid unite, they still keep their edges, so it further illustrates their merging by water and milk; this dissolves without trace and forms a single mark. Initial awakening and original awakening, two in meaning and one in essence, are exactly like this.

Chinese Source

二、「法身」下，明就報立三，即身、壽、量也。三中一一言法身者，報智所冥，離法無報，故初身。言非身者，非應佛有分齊身。非不身者，非報佛無分齊身。又非身則非有、非不身則非空，中道法身乃本覺體。始覺冥此，能冥亦忘，為成觀故，彊名報智。二壽、三量，稱本雙非，為物彊指，義皆同身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "The Dharma-body is neither body nor not body" on, clarifying the establishing of three in terms of the reward, namely body, life, and measure. In each of the three it says "Dharma-body" because it is what the reward-wisdom merges with: apart from the Dharma there is no reward. Hence first, body. Saying "neither body" [means] it is not the response-buddha's body with boundaries. "Nor not body" [means] it is not the reward-buddha's body without boundaries. Further, "neither body" is not existence, and "nor not body" is not emptiness: the Dharma-body of the middle way is the essence of original awakening. Initial awakening merges with this, and what merges is also forgotten; in order to accomplish the contemplation, it is forcibly named the reward-wisdom. Second, life, and third, measure, according with the original as the double negation and forcibly designated for beings, are all the same in meaning as body.

Chinese Source

初義者，上所說報但論冥法，即自受用也。金明垂應，以他受用常住之應，對於生身無常之應，示二迹用。是故雙明身非身等，身即生身，有分齊相，故名為身；非身是報，無分齊相，故曰非身。《小般若》云「佛說非身，是名大身。」大身者，乃他受用身也。無分齊身，其壽則常，故無量也；有分齊身，壽則無常，故有量也。此二應用，乃依真中二理而住，機依事業二識而見。住理，廣如〈序品〉疏文辨之。二識，委在《起信論》明。論意要在事識見則取色分齊，故名應佛；業識見則離分齊相，故是報身。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the first meaning: the reward spoken of above discusses only merging with the Dharma[-body]; this is the self-enjoyment [body]. Now, clarifying the sending down of responses, the permanently abiding response of the other-enjoyment [body] is set against the impermanent response of the birth-body, to show the function of the two traces. Therefore it clarifies both "body" and "not body" and so on. "Body" is the birth-body, which has marks of boundaries, so it is called "body." "Not body" is the reward, without marks of boundaries, so it is called "not body." The Smaller Prajñā says, "The Buddha speaks of no body; this is called a great body." The great body is the other-enjoyment body. The body without boundaries has a permanent life, hence it is immeasurable; the body with boundaries has an impermanent life, hence it has measure. These two responsive functions dwell relying on the two principles, the real and the middle, and capacities see them relying on the two consciousnesses, of phenomena and of karma. Dwelling in principle is discussed at length in the Commentary's text on the "Introduction" chapter; the two consciousnesses are made clear in detail in the Awakening of Faith. The essential intent of that treatise is that what is seen by the consciousness of phenomena takes form with boundaries, hence it is called the response-buddha; what is seen by the karmic consciousness is apart from the marks of boundaries, hence it is the reward-body.

Chinese Source

行者應知常身無量，通應三土；無常有量，但應同居。所以者何？蓋實報機分證，論見他受用身方便土人唯稟別圓，所見佛相雖小優降，然匪生身悉是報佛。若同居土，具四教機，稟別圓者能覩報佛。故《法華》明常在靈山，《華嚴》說法盡未來際，及諸大乘即於應相見是法性尊特之身，故知常身遍應三土。若無常身，唯應同居，逗藏通機及凡夫眾也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Practitioners should know that the permanent body, immeasurable, responds throughout the three lands; the impermanent, having measure, responds only in the land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together. Why? Because capacities in the land of true reward, realizing in a portion, may be discussed as seeing the other-enjoyment body; people of the land of expedients receive only the distinct and perfect [teachings], and though the marks of the Buddha they see are somewhat superior or inferior, they are not the birth-body but all the reward-buddha. In the land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together, which contains capacities for all four teachings, those who receive the distinct and perfect can behold the reward-buddha. Hence the Lotus makes clear [that the Buddha is] ever on the Numinous Mountain, the Avataṃsaka teaches the Dharma to the end of the future, and in the various Mahāyāna [sūtras], right in the responsive marks, one sees the specially honored body of the dharma-nature; hence we know that the permanent body responds throughout the three lands. The four alternatives of vehicle and precepts: first, urgent in both vehicle and precepts; second, lax in both vehicle and precepts; third, urgent in the vehicle and lax in the precepts; fourth, lax in the vehicle and urgent in the precepts. First one must understand the essence and features of the vehicle and the precepts. In discussing the precepts as ten kinds, it takes only "not deficient," "not broken," "not pierced," and "not mixed"; these four precepts, though divided into concentrated and scattered, are all causes of [birth as] humans and gods: these are the present precepts. It does not take "according with the path," "without attachment," "praised by the wise," "sovereign," "according with concentration," and "complete," because these six, though called precepts, are in essence the three contemplations and belong of themselves to the vehicle. In discussing the vehicle as five vehicles, it does not take [those of] humans and gods, because these two, though called vehicles, neither move nor emerge; in essence they are contaminated good, included in the phenomenal precepts.
