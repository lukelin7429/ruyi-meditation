Notes on the Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra.

Siming Zhili (四明知禮, 960–1028) wrote these Notes in his last years as a running subcommentary on Zhiyi's Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra. A preface dated 1027 says the notes taken at his teacher Baoyun Yitong's lectures had been garbled, so he set down his own. He explains each phrase by the four teachings and by contemplation of mind, defends the contemplations attached to phenomena against critics, and returns often to repentance, the three bodies, and the twofold emptiness of beings and dharmas. A colophon says Zhili died after seventeen chapters; a disciple compiled the last chapter, Praising the Buddha, from his lectures.

How to read this collection

Every lemma quoted from the Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra uses this site's translation, and every line of the sūtra uses this site's Golden Light Sūtra; read the three together.

Where Zhili reads a phrase differently from our English of the Commentary or the sūtra, the quotation keeps our wording and the note explains his reading.

What each page includes

  • The Chinese source text beside our English translation, paragraph by paragraph, checked character by character against the CBETA edition so that nothing is skipped.
  • A short introduction to each part, and reading notes on allusions, technical terms, and textual variants.
  • Terminology matched to this site's existing Tiantai translations.
