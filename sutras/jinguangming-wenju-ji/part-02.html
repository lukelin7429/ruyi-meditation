The Narrating Introduction and the Five Precepts

This part covers the second half of the first fascicle of Zhili's Notes, which follows Zhiyi's Textual Commentary through the particular introduction of the Golden Light Sūtra. Zhili first explains the seven particulars of the introduction and how each verse line of the "Introduction" chapter narrates a later chapter, and he defends the Commentary's view that the Buddha himself composed the narration. He then works phrase by phrase through "At this time the Tathāgata was roaming in the immeasurable, very deep dharma-nature": the time of discerning capacities, the meanings of "Tathāgata," roaming without entering, and the dharma-nature as both the norm of the buddhas and the inherent inclusion of defilement and evil in all ten realms. The longest discussion concerns "king of sūtras." Zhili sets out the Commentary's nine kinds of sūtra (three conventional, three real, three middle), rejects Paramārtha's one-virtue-per-sūtra scheme and an unnamed contemporary's reading, and shows why only the perfect middle, where words and principle are combined, is king. The part closes with the four buddhas as the four gates and four truths in contemplation of mind, the three obstructions as the fruit of breaking the five precepts, and the five precepts read as the dharma of humans and gods, of the two vehicles, and of the Mahāyāna.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Their arising in sequence" on, clarifying the order of the seven. First, entering concentration: the Great Awakened One, suddenly perfect, illuminates and is ever tranquil. His present entering of concentration is meant to show a model and standard, making people delight in concentration. Next, narrating: having entered wondrous concentration, he sees that the Dharma is honored and precious, and within this very concentration he narrates the king of sūtras. As for saying "coming out, he narrates": in fact [the sūtra] does not show the mark of coming out of concentration; it is because tranquility does not hinder illumination that it says "coming out." The Buddha teaches this sūtra without coming out of concentration; the intent is originally to manifest that within tranquility there is illumination. Next, harboring doubt: since he has narrated that the Dharma is what the Buddha realized, Faith-Mark then doubts: how is it that the lifespan of the person who realizes does not accord with the Dharma? The order of the auspicious response and the rest is plain in the text and can be known.

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing the narration directly. "Narrate the harboring of doubt": reflecting on the deep meaning, he must surely doubt that the Dharma realized is permanent while the lifespan of the one who realizes it is short. "Narrate the cutting off of doubt": the Buddha protects the origin, making him cut off doubt and give rise to faith. "Narrate the 'Repentance' chapter": because it is chiefly for destroying evil and secondarily for producing good. "Narrate the 'Praise' chapter": because it is chiefly for producing good and secondarily for destroying evil. "Narrate the 'Emptiness' chapter": this chapter guides and completes the producing of good and the extinguishing of evil; the present text narrates one side only, because it extinguishes the three obstructions. The great Brahmā and Śakra are the lords of Saṃjñeya; the kiṃnaras and the rest are the divisions and retinue led by Saṃjñeya; the sūtra raises the lords to narrate his chapter. "Narrate 'The Right Treatise' and 'Good Gathering'": "The Right Treatise" governs the country, and "Good Gathering" listens to the sūtra; both flow out of the secret treasury, so "secret" narrates the two chapters. "Narrate the 'Predictions' chapter": there, through hearing the sūtra, their minds were pure as empty space, and so they obtained the prediction; hence the present [text] says "pure in body and intention, without any defilement." "Narrate the 'Giving Up the Body' chapter": [the tigress] who fed on the prince's body attained liberation, which shows that it was not wrong food by which she kept herself alive; hence here the one phrase "right livelihood" narrates it. "Narrate the 'Praising the Buddha' chapter": though bodhisattvas praise the Buddha in verse in three rounds, these bodhisattvas are mostly ancient buddhas, and even if they are actual practitioners, they are future buddhas; hence the present "praised by the buddhas" [means] the practitioner [is praised by those who] will be buddhas, and so it narrates that chapter. "Approximated" (彷彿) is the appearance of not being clear. 薳: (pronunciation note); it means "far." It is as if to say one may not overstep and make the portion clear by oneself.

The rejection, "it is a treatise and not the sūtra," [is made] because what the Buddha teaches is called sūtra, and what the disciples of the three vehicles composed after his extinction is all called treatise. The sūtra says, "I shall now teach the dharmas of repentance and the rest"; how could the dharma of repentance be taught by Ānanda? Is this not contradicting the text? The golden drum in the dream is the Buddha's Dharma-body; struck by wisdom, it therefore teaches the dharma of repentance according to capacities. One must know that the golden drum is the Buddha's true self; hence it says "I teach." The second said it was Faith-Mark. The rejection says: if Faith-Mark "could already narrate the profound," from beginning to end, he should not have doubted. As for "narrate the profound" (玄敘): 玄 is the same as 懸 ("in advance").

The marks of dharmas of the three truths are the threefold Dharma-body: the Dharma-body is perfect. Understanding in accord with this is the threefold prajñā: the reward-body is perfect. Teaching in accord with this is the threefold liberation: the response-body is perfect. Viewing the former from the present, there is no lack of distinction.

Third, from "'Roaming'" on, explaining the character "roaming." This is the Tathāgata roaming into the dharma-nature with ultimate wisdom. Now, the dharma-nature that is realized and the fruit-wisdom that realizes are set up in meaning as realizer and realized, but in essence they do not reach each other. For initial awakening, when ultimate, is the same as original awakening; only the wisdom of suchness exists alone. Therefore, if this is so, who can enter? And what is entered? Dwelling and leaving are all likewise. The citing of the smaller Prajñā[pāramitā] has the same intent. Now saying "roaming" is to lead beings to learn the Buddha's entering of principle; hence he shows the mark of entering. To make beings dwell in accord with principle, he shows the mark of dwelling. Sweet dew is the medicine of deathlessness, a simile for permanent principle. Proclaiming this flavor of sweet dew for beings, one should also speak of "leaving." Leaving without leaving is leaving to narrate the king of sūtras. In the Lotus's "Welling Up from the Earth," the great bodhisattvas, in the deep dharma-nature, in hundreds of thousands of samādhis, enter without entering, and this is called "skillfully entering"; leaving and dwelling are likewise. If even bodhisattvas are so, the Buddha of the fruit can be known.

初、消無量甚深。將欲明示佛所遊入，先須簡顯其體高廣，乃以法界顯其廣。中道之法非界為界，此界無外，故言無量。又以三諦顯其高。然真俗二諦雖俱究竟，而乃通於二乘及徧菩薩少分而證，若其中諦非圓，實智莫能證入。今明三諦體非優劣，乃是三德祕密之藏，即法性底，故云徹到。方稱經文甚深之歎。深即高義。須知法界與圓三諦無二無別，今取二名顯無量甚深，令易見耳。顯已次簡。若約二乘，以圓簡偏；若約菩薩，以深簡淺，降佛已還皆下地故。圓聖尚簡，況三教耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

First, interpreting "immeasurable, very deep." About to clarify and show what the Buddha roams into, one must first sift and show the height and breadth of its essence. So it shows its breadth by the dharma-realm: the dharma of the middle way takes non-realm as realm; this realm has nothing outside it; hence "immeasurable." Further, it shows its height by the three truths. Although the two truths, the real and the conventional, are both ultimate, they are shared with the two vehicles and with bodhisattvas of the partial [teachings], who realize a small part of them; as for the truth of the middle, without perfect, real wisdom none can realize and enter it. Now it clarifies that the three truths in essence are not superior or inferior; they are the secret treasury of the three virtues, the very bottom of the dharma-nature; hence "penetrates through." Only then does it accord with the sūtra's praise "very deep." "Deep" means high. One must know that the dharma-realm and the perfect three truths are not two and not different; now the two names are taken to show "immeasurable, very deep," only to make it easy to see. Having shown it, next it sifts. In terms of the two vehicles, the perfect sifts out the partial; in terms of bodhisattvas, the deep sifts out the shallow, since all below the Buddha are lower grounds. If even the sages of the perfect are sifted out, how much more the three [other] teachings?

二、「法」下，消法性二字。上明高廣是體之德，今明法性是德之體。釋二字義，顯高廣體。所言法者，軌則為義，諸佛軌之、萬德成就。故《涅槃》云「諸佛所師，所謂法也。」然此法性，三乘六道誰不軌則而成立耶？以迷性具，無事用故，雖軌而違，故成三障。其猶七眾，誰不師佛？而有違順，故分縛脫。今就極順，用顯所師，故云諸佛所軌名之為法。所言性者，不變為義，謂四德之體無遷易故。須知此四遍一切法，下至地獄依正因果，一一無非常樂我淨，世間相常，斯之謂也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "'Dharma-nature'" on, interpreting the two characters "dharma-nature." Above it clarified height and breadth as the virtue of the essence; now it clarifies the dharma-nature as the essence of the virtue. Explaining the meaning of the two characters shows the essence of the height and breadth. As for "dharma," its meaning is norm and rule: the buddhas take it as their norm, and myriad virtues are accomplished. Hence the Nirvāṇa says, "That which every buddha takes as teacher is the Dharma itself." Yet as for this dharma-nature, among the three vehicles and the six paths, who does not take it as norm and thereby become established? Because they are deluded about the inherent inclusion and have no phenomenal function, though they take it as norm they go against it, and so form the three obstructions. It is like the seven groups [of disciples]: who does not take the Buddha as teacher? Yet there is going against and according, so they are divided into bound and liberated. Now, taking the utmost accordance, it uses [it] to show what is taken as teacher; hence "What the buddhas take as their norm is called 'dharma.'" As for "nature," its meaning is unchanging, because the essence of the four virtues has no shifting or change. One must know that these four pervade all dharmas: down to the dependent and the principal, the cause and fruit of the hells, each and every one is nothing but permanence, bliss, self, and purity. "The marks of the world are permanent" — this is what it means.

If one says only that the middle is the sūtra's essence, then there is an essence apart from cardinal doctrine and function, and the essence is narrow and not all-pervading; hence the three truths must all be the sūtra's essence. If one does not, within each of the three, sift out emptiness and the provisional and take only the middle as the essence, one cannot show that the essence is not wisdom or severance. Discerning the essence in this way, it is not identical to cardinal doctrine and function, yet not apart from them; thought and deliberation dissolve clean, and the wondrous essence is natural. Because emptiness pervades the three truths, this cardinal doctrine is not narrow; because the provisional pervades the three truths, this function is all the broader. Second, from "Gathering" on, corresponding to the three kinds of action in terms of phenomena. Not killing, stealing, or lust is bodily action; no false speech is verbal action; not drinking wine is mental action. Since upholding accords with the nature, it establishes the cause of the precepts and accomplishes the Buddha's three kinds of action. The Buddha's body, speech, and mind follow wisdom and are without fault; this is called the three without fault. Because there is no fault, there is no need of guarding; this is called the three needing no guarding. Bodily action manifesting transformations is called the wheel of supernormal power; verbal action teaching the Dharma is called the wheel of correct teaching; mental action discerning capacities is called the wheel of reading minds. All three crush and grind beings' delusion and karma, and the lower grounds cannot fathom them; hence they are called the three secrets.

Translator's notes

Lemmas. Zhili cites the Commentary by its first one or two characters (「從是」下, 「別義」下 …). Every lemma reuses the English of `jinguangming-wenju/p1_body.md` (the passage on the particular introduction through the five precepts); sūtra phrases reuse `jinguangming-jing/p1_body.md` and p3_body.md (Saṃjñeya's words "abide in the suchness-nature of all dharmas," "an inconceivable sphere of wisdom").

"Narrate the profound" (玄敘, unit 11). The Commentary's English reads 玄 as "profound"; Zhili glosses 玄 as 懸 "in advance." The Commentary's wording is kept inside the quotation, and Zhili's gloss is given after it (see p2_decisions).

"Most trustworthy" (彌為可信, unit 46). Zhili quotes the Profound Meaning as 彌為可信; T1783 and our Profound Meaning English read 彌為可用 "most usable." Zhili's wording is translated.

"教尚稱王，名等可見" (unit 60). Only "The text calls itself the king of sūtras, and its teaching gathers in all scriptures" is found in the Profound Meaning (jinguangming-xuanyi); the second sentence, inside the same CBETA quotation marks, was not located there and may be Zhili's paraphrase.

Sources located. "Someone" (有人, unit 54). The unnamed interpreter who builds nine kinds of sūtra on the three characters Golden Light is not identified; he may be a contemporary of Zhili (perhaps of the Shanwai side), but this is conjecture.
