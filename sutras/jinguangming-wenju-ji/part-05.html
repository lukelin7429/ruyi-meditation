The Main Part Begins: The Four Buddhas' Verses and the Meaning of Repentance

This is the first half of the third fascicle of Siming Zhili's Notes on the Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra (T1786). Here the main part of the sūtra begins. Zhili first weighs the three older views of where the main teaching starts and what it teaches, sides with Zhiyi's choice, and uses the longer "new sūtra" to describe three cycles in which the cardinal doctrine of permanence is taught to hearers of superior, middling, and inferior faculties. He then comments on Zhiyi's three readings of the four buddhas' verses on the Buddha's lifespan, by the four truths, the four foundations of mindfulness, and the four virtues, stressing that the lifespan of the response-body is wholly the Dharma-body and that the three bodies are one essence. The second half of the part turns to the "Repentance" chapter. Zhili follows the Commentary's ten explanations of the name, its "place" of repentance (the true mark, identical to the mark of offense), its correct and auxiliary methods, and its stages of repentance, which extend from those guilty of the five rebellious acts up to equal awakening. He closes with the three kinds of repentance, by ritual action, by seizing a mark, and of no-generation, adding Vinaya procedures, sūtra passages on the rites, and the obstructions each kind removes.

Thus the treatise asks: "What is correct and universal knowledge?" It answers: "Knowing suffering according to the mark of suffering," and so on. The old [masters] read this text. Although they understood the character 如 as "not different," they were in the dark that the Tripiṭaka [teaching] takes knowing phenomena in accord with principle as "not different," while the Mahāyāna takes knowing phenomena as identical to principle as "not different." As a result they understood the meaning as stopping only at phenomena and did not reveal principle at all. Further, in this text explaining "correct and universal knowledge," the correct is knowing the real and the universal is knowing the conventional.

In the truth of extinction, the Lotus is cited to illustrate emptiness with emptiness; this emptiness is ultimate, hence it says "finally returning." The five obscurations are smoke, clouds, dust, fog, and the asura's hand. The three lights are the sun, the moon, and the stars. This wisdom is like the sun, melting the frost and dew of offenses. Taking the unconditioned as object, ultimately empty, without the marks of middle and extremes: no principle surpasses this, hence it is called supreme. From "though it does not properly perform phenomenal marks" on: these three kinds of repentance are cultivated at the same time, no-generation being the correct and the other two aiding conditions; hence it says "includes the two." This is the correct and the auxiliary practiced as one combination; like oil increasing the light, principle is realized all the more swiftly.
