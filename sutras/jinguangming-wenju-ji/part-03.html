The Five Precepts in Phenomena and Principle, and the Obstructions of Affliction and Karma

This is the first half of the second fascicle of Siming Zhili's Notes on the Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra (T1786). It continues his comments on the passage of Zhiyi's Textual Commentary that reads the "Emptiness" verses of the sūtra as destroying three obstructions, and that traces those obstructions to breaking the five precepts. Most of the part follows Zhiyi's long treatment of each precept in turn: not killing, not stealing, the precept against lust, the precept against false speech, and the precept against drinking wine. For each, Zhili lays out the same plan. Phenomenal upholding by humans and gods is followed by upholding in principle by the two vehicles and the distinct teaching, each of which, seen from the perfect, still counts as a violation. Last comes the perfect person's upholding and "violation that has grasped the intent." Zhili's repeated point is that the perfect contemplation depends on inherent inclusion (性具) and inherent evil (性惡). Without them, the Dharma-gates of killing, stealing, lust, false speech, and wine would be only compassionate expedients that any bodhisattva could use. He also supplies glosses on Indian lore and on the Chinese drinkers, astronomers, and diviners whom Zhiyi mentions. The part ends with the contemplation of mind (the five precepts as five buddhas), the distinction between affliction and karma, and the evil stars as signs of the obstruction of karma.

Embodying dharmas as identical to emptiness belongs to the partial contemplation; viewed from the perfect, it is killing. One must embody all dharmas as nothing but the middle way, the marks of the world all permanent; this is the precept of not killing in principle discussed here. Wishing to reveal this idea, it distinguishes [the precept] from humans and gods up to the perfect ultimate; hence it says that in cause and fruit it "is not of one kind."

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the two vehicles, whose meaning includes the Tripiṭaka and the shared [teachings]. The Nirvāṇa Sūtra, because the non-Buddhists appeared first, likens them to the old physician; the Tathāgata, appearing later, is likened to the guest physician; [hence] the precepts instituted by the Buddha are called guest precepts. Beyond the phenomenal precepts one further cultivates the precepts in accord with the path and without attachment. These precepts are the highest stage; in their own teaching [they end in] reduction to ashes and annihilation, and no retribution is spoken of. Now, according to the Great Vehicle, they are born in the land of expedients and receive the life of change. The texts do not state the limit of life in this land; it is only that the cause shifts and the fruit changes. That [the Commentary] now says "seven hundred asaṃkhyeyas" must mean that only after that many eons does one enter the [land of] true reward. If in this land one destroys the delusions of dust-and-sand, one also obtains the Dharma-eye; here it discusses those of the two vehicles when first born [there], who obtain only the wisdom-eye.

As for "and so on" [in] the wisdom [of permanence and impermanence], it is the wisdom of the middle. These are the precepts in accord with samādhi and complete; partially attaining these two corresponds to the first ground. Being born in the land of true reward is called the Lotus Treasury Ocean. The Perfect Upholding of Not Killing

Second, from "and also upholds the precept of not killing in principle" on, clarifying at length upholding in principle, in three:

Chinese Source

初、約體明持。此理不殺，若其不解性具九界，但云體達諸法即理、全波是水，猶濫通別未顯圓修。故荊谿云：「若不談具，乃屬別教。」故須體達假合之身、三惑癡愛、三科實法，皆性本具，性無差別，故名一相。性非暗縛，稱為明脫。既即性具，何可毀傷？癡愛是子，假實是果，全體即性，性豈生滅？如斯妙觀，即障是德不待轉除，方是持於理不殺戒。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying upholding in terms of embodying. As for this not killing in principle, if one does not understand that the nature inherently includes the nine realms, and only says that one embodies and penetrates all dharmas as identical to principle, the whole wave being water, this still blurs into the shared and the distinct and does not yet reveal perfect cultivation. Hence Jingxi says: "If inherent inclusion is not discussed, it belongs to the distinct teaching." Therefore one must embody and penetrate that the body of provisional combination, delusion and craving (the three delusions), and the real dharmas of the three categories are all originally included in the nature; the nature is without difference, hence it is called "one mark." The nature is not dark bondage; it is called "clarity and liberation." Since they are identical to what the nature includes, how could they be damaged? Delusion and craving are the seed; the provisional and the real are the fruit; the whole essence is the nature — how could the nature arise or cease? Only such a wondrous contemplation, in which the obstruction itself is virtue, without waiting to be turned or removed, is upholding the precept of not killing in principle.

There is no retribution in a later birth, only the present retribution; hence it is called "permanent life, still and deep," without diminishing. "The five eyes complete" — the faculties likewise — is being free of incompleteness. The Perfect Violation: King Xianyu

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Further, why would the person of the perfect teaching merely uphold it?" on, clarifying violation that has grasped the intent, in two:

Chinese Source

初、總示二犯。此事理犯，若其不解性具違用及殺法門，但以慈愍能現逆相而解釋，此豈前三教菩薩之行不能作耶？故《普門疏》明嗔法門為成就故，常念觀音。是知須得性殺之意，慈方無緣，故云唯殺唯慈，名得意犯。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing the two violations as a whole. As for these violations in phenomena and in principle, if one does not understand that the nature includes contrary function and the Dharma-gate of killing, and only explains them as manifesting contrary marks out of kindness and pity, is this not something the bodhisattvas of the former three teachings could also do? Hence the Commentary on the Universal Gate clarifies the Dharma-gate of anger, [saying that] in order to fulfill it one constantly holds Avalokiteśvara in mind. Thus we know one must grasp the meaning of inherent killing; only then is kindness unconditioned. Hence it says "only killing, only kindness," which is called violation that has grasped the intent.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Like King Xianyu" on, showing the two marks separately, in two:

Chinese Source

初、引人明事殺。《大經．聖行品》佛說本生，曾為國王，名曰仙豫，愛念大乘。時世無佛，十二年事婆羅門為師，後遂勸彼發菩提心。而婆羅門不信謗法，王乃殺之，而王不墮獄，以無殺罪故。至〈梵行品〉，佛說慈心之果住一子地。迦葉難言：「若菩薩住一子地，云何佛昔為王斷婆羅門命耶？」佛言：「我以愛念故斷，非惡心也。諸婆羅門命終生阿鼻獄，即有三念：一自知從人道來、二知是地獄、三自知謗法為王所殺。念是事已即信大乘，尋時命終，生甘露鼓王世界，於彼壽命十劫。我於往昔乃與是十劫壽命，云何名殺？」然須明於得殺法門，令其愛念成無緣慈，方合疏文唯殺唯慈也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, citing a person to clarify phenomenal killing. In the "Noble Practice" chapter of the Great Sūtra, the Buddha tells of a former life: he was once a king named Xianyu, who cherished the Great Vehicle. At that time there was no buddha in the world. For twelve years he served brahmins as his teachers, and afterward urged them to arouse the resolve for bodhi. But the brahmins did not believe and slandered the Dharma, so the king killed them; yet the king did not fall into hell, because he had no offense of killing. In the "Pure Practice" chapter, the Buddha teaches that the fruit of the mind of kindness is dwelling in the ground of [regarding all as] one's only child. Kāśyapa objected: "If bodhisattvas dwell in the ground of the only child, how is it that the Buddha formerly, as a king, cut off the lives of brahmins?" The Buddha said: "I cut them off out of loving care, not with an evil mind. When those brahmins died they were born in the Avīci hell and at once had three thoughts: first, they knew of themselves that they had come from the human path; second, they knew that this was hell; third, they knew of themselves that they had slandered the Dharma and been killed by the king. Having reflected on this, they at once believed in the Great Vehicle, soon died, and were born in the world of Amṛtadundubhi ('Drum of Sweet Dew'), where their lifespan was ten eons. In the past I in fact gave them this lifespan of ten eons; how can it be called killing?" Yet one must clarify the attainment of the Dharma-gate of killing, so that loving care becomes unconditioned kindness; only then does it fit the Commentary's "only killing, only kindness."

Killing in Principle: The Tree Spirit and the Fire at the End of the Eon

Chinese Source

二、「又作」下，據經明理殺，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Further, as for killing as a Dharma-gate" on, clarifying killing in principle according to the sūtra, in two:

Chinese Source

初、正釋。前就不斷，名持不殺。今明有斷，故云犯殺。圓教自論斷與不斷二義同時。既明六即，六故有斷，即故不斷。亦可秖就即之一字明於二義：障體即德，無障可論，斯為斷義。障既即德，障何甞斷？斯不斷義。故煩惱即菩提、生死即涅槃，斷與不斷妙在其中。諸大乘經說圓頓觀，有此兩門。今就有斷名為理殺，故云析蕩累著是業及諸煩惱以為所斷。樹神諭於修觀之人，劫火諭於能斷之智。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining it proper. Above, in terms of not cutting off, it was called upholding [the precept of] not killing; now, clarifying that there is cutting off, it is called violating [by] killing. The perfect teaching itself discusses the two senses of cutting off and not cutting off as simultaneous. Since it clarifies the six identities: because they are six, there is cutting off; because they are identities, there is no cutting off. One may also, on the single word "identity" alone, clarify both senses: the essence of the obstruction is itself virtue, and there is no obstruction to speak of — this is the sense of cutting off. Since the obstruction is itself virtue, how has the obstruction ever been cut off? This is the sense of not cutting off. Hence afflictions are identical to bodhi and birth-and-death is identical to nirvāṇa; cutting off and not cutting off are wondrously within this. When the Mahāyāna sūtras teach the perfect and sudden contemplation, they have these two gates. Now, in terms of there being cutting off, it is called killing in principle; hence it says "analyzing and sweeping away encumbrances and attachments," taking karma and all afflictions as what is cut off. The tree spirit illustrates the person who cultivates contemplation; the fire at the end of the eon illustrates the wisdom that can cut off.

Chinese Source

故《大論》三十云「譬如空澤大樹，眾鳥集宿。一鴿後至，住一枝上，其枝即折。澤神問其故。樹神答曰：『此鳥從我怨家樹來。食彼尼俱類子，或當放糞子墮地者。惡樹復生，為害必大，是故寧捨一枝，所全者大。』菩薩亦爾，於魔外惑業無如是畏，而畏二乘。二乘於菩薩邊亦如彼鳥，壞彼大乘心，永滅佛乘心。」今文但以怨鳥通累著等也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence the Great Treatise, fascicle thirty, says: "It is like a great tree in an empty marsh, where flocks of birds gather to roost. A pigeon arrived last and perched on a branch, and the branch at once broke. The marsh spirit asked the reason. The tree spirit answered: 'This bird comes from the tree of my enemy. It has eaten the seeds of the nyagrodha there and may drop dung, so that the seeds fall to the ground. If the evil tree grows again, the harm will surely be great. Therefore I would rather give up one branch; what is preserved is great.' The bodhisattva is likewise: toward Māra, non-Buddhists, delusion, and karma he has no such fear, but he fears the two vehicles. The two vehicles, to the bodhisattva, are like that bird: they destroy his Great Vehicle mind and forever extinguish the mind of the buddha-vehicle." The present text merely uses the enemy bird to stand generally for encumbrances, attachments, and the rest.

劫火等者，亦《大論》文。論第二云「二乘雖破三毒，氣分不盡。如草木薪火燒，煙出炭灰不盡，火力薄故。佛三毒永盡無餘，譬如劫火燒須彌山，一切地都盡，無煙無灰。」今謂佛智，即圓智也。斷陰入界生死，即涅槃也。約即論斷，名得意犯。理觀斷破，名不持戒，此為誠證。下諸理犯，其義皆成。一切塵勞是如來種，此是性種，亦敵對種。惑非性染，安名佛種？以知即性修染本虛，本虛名滅，即理殺也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Like the fire at the end of the eon" and so on is also from the Great Treatise. The Treatise, fascicle two, says: "Although the two vehicles have destroyed the three poisons, their residual traces are not exhausted, like grass and wood burned by fire: smoke comes out and the charcoal and ash are not exhausted, because the fire's power is weak. The Buddha's three poisons are forever exhausted without remainder, like the fire at the end of the eon burning Mount Sumeru: all the earth is entirely consumed, with neither smoke nor ash." Here it refers to the Buddha's wisdom, that is, the perfect wisdom. "Cutting off the aggregates, realms, and entrances": birth-and-death is identical to nirvāṇa. Discussing cutting off in terms of identity is called violation that has grasped the intent. That the contemplation of principle cuts off and destroys is called "not upholding the precept": this [Aṅgulimāla passage] is sincere proof. For the violations in principle below, the meaning is likewise established. "All the dusts and toils are the seed of the Tathāgata": this is the seed of the nature, and also the seed by opposition. If delusion were not inherent staining, how could it be called the Buddha's seed? Knowing that, being identical to the nature, cultivated staining is originally empty — and originally empty is called extinction — this is killing in principle.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "accomplishing the vajra-like, subtle and wondrous Dharma-body" on, obtaining retribution. Since the two violations in phenomena and in principle accord with the Dharma-gate of killing in the inherent virtue, the dharma of killing is accomplished and one obtains the Dharma-body, letting responses descend into the nine realms, showing long or short lives and manifesting lacking or complete faculties. This sovereign function is partially obtained by the true cause and completed in the ultimate fruit.

Chinese Source

二、「前」下，結勝。先且斥劣。人天近事、藏通淺理、別教次第，是故隘塞，此等皆非修性不二通達之途。唯圓實戒，一攝一切宏廣無邊，即事而中深遠莫測。凡小之徑必會逆順之異，能同十戒之中名畢竟戒。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "The earlier practices of the precepts" on, concluding its superiority. First it rejects the inferior. Unit 2, "extended through six": 上雖通六 probably refers to the six sets matched with the five precepts in the preceding passage (the Tiwei Sūtra's planets, peaks, and organs; the five constants; the Five Classics; the ten wholesome deeds; the five aggregates; the four virtues). It is translated literally.

Unit 15: 無生後報但現報 is read as "there is no retribution in a later birth, only the present retribution." Alternatively: "in non-arising, the later retribution is just the present retribution."

Unit 54: 於通不塞 is read as "it penetrates without blockage." Alternatively: "it is not blocked at [the level of] the shared teaching."

Unit 75: The lemma 「非此二空」 is not in the extant Textual Commentary, which reads 亦不以無觀得是解脫. Zhili may have had a different text. 徧空 is read as 偏空 "partial emptiness."

Unit 99: In 其德失意但中, 德 is unclear; the sentence is translated without it ("for those who lose the intent it is the mere middle"). The Commentary on the Sixteen Contemplations is Zhiyi's Commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra (觀經疏).

Unit 106: Mallikā is said to have broken "five" of the eight precepts, but six items are listed (eating after noon, perfume and flowers, music and dance, high beds, wine, false speech). The translation keeps both.

Unit 125: 難傳 is read as 難轉 "hard to turn."

Unit 133: The twenty-eight lunar mansions are given English names (Horn = 角, Neck = 亢, Root = 氐 …). The text's 「北方玄武七星」 sits after its list instead of before it, as in the other three directions. The Offices of Zhou list is the Zhouli allotted-field (分野) scheme, cited through commentaries; source not checked.

Unit 137: Guan Lu (管輅) was from Pingyuan; Zhili's "a man of Huai" (槐人) may be a copying error. "Wu's Spring and Autumn" (吳氏春秋) is probably the Lüshi chunqiu (呂氏春秋), which tells the story of Duke Jing of Song and Zi Wei; source not checked.
