Saṃjñeya, the Right Treatise, Good Gathering, and Ghosts and Spirits

This part covers the second half of the fifth fascicle of Zhili's Notes, following Zhiyi's Textual Commentary through four chapters of the Golden Light Sūtra. It opens in the middle of "The Spirit Saṃjñeya," whose name the Commentary renders "secret." Zhili works through the spirit's fifteen phrases as three sets of five: the three contemplations, the three truths and liberations, and the three bodies. Along the way he defines the "sphere of wisdom" with the Awakening of Faith's original and initial awakening, reads "neither awakening nor non-awakening" as the Dharma-body, and matches the phrases to the five buddha-natures of two earlier schools. In "The Right Treatise" he supplies the Chinese classical background the Commentary assumes: the Classic of Filial Piety, the "Record of Music," the hundred grains and the altars of soil and millet, and the old man beating the clod in Yao's time. He reads the king's "holding happiness" (執樂) as holding rites and music. He then shows how the six meanings of "son of the gods" and "king" become a contemplation relying on phenomena, rooted in the Golden Light as a dharma rather than a simile. "Good Gathering" is read through dāna and wisdom and the four siddhāntas, with glosses on the sūtra's rarer words. "Ghosts and Spirits" turns on the distinct and the perfect offering: listening to the sūtra is itself the offering to the three bodies of all buddhas. The part ends with names of spirits and the four colored lotuses.

Saṃjñeya: The Middle Contemplation

Chinese Source

◎三、「如法」下，後句明中觀。以二觀等者，出安住行相也。圓論三觀若非一心，觀體則縱；若其不以二為方便、中為真實，觀體則橫。今論三觀不縱不橫，如此修之，即能安住十界之法，如於本性即修成性，功由一心三觀之力也。故荊谿云：「以正觀安故，世諦方成不思議也。」此則雙遮中道也。雙照二諦，故言含受。遮乃遮情，照則照性，邊情既泯，二諦皆中。中既不偏，是故空假各含一切。經云「於一切法含受一切法，不作雙照中道解之。」經意莫顯也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "In accord with the Dharma" on, the last phrases making clear the middle contemplation. "Taking the two contemplations as expedient means" and so on: this brings out the marks of the practice of abiding at peace. In discussing the perfect three contemplations, if they were not in a single mind, the essence of contemplation would be longitudinal; if they did not take the two as expedient means and the middle as the real, the essence of contemplation would be horizontal. The three contemplations discussed now are neither longitudinal nor horizontal; cultivating them thus, one can abide at peace in the dharmas of the ten realms as in their original nature, and cultivation accomplishes the nature; the merit comes from the power of the threefold contemplation in a single mind. Hence Jingxi says, "Because it is settled by correct contemplation, the worldly truth only then becomes inconceivable." This is the middle way negating both. Illuminating both the two truths, hence it says "contain and receive." Negating is negating feelings; illuminating is illuminating the nature. Once the feelings of the extremes are gone, both truths are the middle. Since the middle is not partial, the empty and the provisional each contain all. The sūtra says "within all dharmas I contain and receive all dharmas"; if one does not explain it as the middle way illuminating both, the intent of the sūtra is not revealed.

The Three Truths and the Three Liberations

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, matching the next five phrases separately to the three liberations. This makes clear that Saṃjñeya himself reveals his original ground. That he has already realized liberation and it also includes the three truths: obtaining liberation comes from seeing the truths. In three: first, citing the text and showing the meaning. Second, from "See directly" on, explaining the phrases according to the meaning, in three:

Chinese Source

初、一句示圓淨。實智照不思議真諦者，真諦體是性德般若，全性起修名為實智。諦非般若，寧發實智？此諦離縛乃成分滿圓淨解脫，當知理果終始一如。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, one phrase showing the perfect-pure. "This real wisdom illuminates the inconceivable object of the truth of the real": the essence of the truth of the real is the prajñā of inherent virtue; cultivation arising from the whole nature is called real wisdom. If the truth were not prajñā, how could it bring forth real wisdom? When this truth is freed from bonds, it becomes the partial and full perfect-pure liberation. You should know that principle and fruit are one suchness from beginning to end.

Chinese Source

二、三句示方便淨。隨機屈曲、因果廢興，皆是權智照不思議俗諦。俗諦體是性德解脫，全性起修名為權智，離縛乃成分滿方便淨解脫。理果不二，準前可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, three phrases showing the expedient-pure. Bending and turning according to capacity, cause and fruit set aside and arising: all this is provisional wisdom illuminating the inconceivable conventional truth. The essence of the conventional truth is the liberation of inherent virtue; cultivation arising from the whole nature is called provisional wisdom; freed from bonds, it becomes the partial and full expedient-pure liberation. That principle and fruit are not two can be known from the preceding.

Chinese Source

三、後一句示性淨。前云智光乃至智聚，四名但召能照之智。唯此智境雖亦標智，體是諦理義當所照，故疏引經「法如如智，此智與法如如冥故」，意彰境智如如不二。然而此說人皆知之，所解終成二物相合，蓋以不曉境智之體故也。欲知境體，須簡頑境及偏小妄心假立真如，此境安能與智不二？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the last phrase showing the nature-pure. Earlier [the sūtra] says "light of wisdom" and so on up to "heap of wisdom"; these four names only call up the wisdom that illuminates. Only this "sphere of wisdom" (智境), though it too marks "wisdom," has as its essence the principle of the truths and in meaning corresponds to what is illuminated. Hence the Commentary cites the sūtra, "the suchness-wisdom of dharmas; this wisdom merges with the suchness of dharmas," meaning to show that object and wisdom are suchness and not two. Yet everyone knows this saying, but what they understand ends up as two things joined together, because they do not understand the essence of object and wisdom. If you wish to know the essence of the object, you must sift out the inert object and the suchness provisionally set up by the deluded mind of the partial and the small: how could such an object be not two with wisdom?

Chinese Source

今依馬鳴，立境體者所謂本覺，其智體者所謂始覺。故《起信論》云「所言覺義者，謂心體離念，離念相者等虛空界無所不遍，法界一相即是如來常住法身，依此法身說名本覺（文畢）。」此覺是性，全性起修名為始覺。論云始覺者，即同本覺。既云離念，豈有思議？既等虛空無所不遍，豈有一時一塵一心而非本覺乃始覺耶？是故得云三世十方生佛依正為所觀境，三世一方生佛依正為能觀智。境智名別，其體不殊，是故能所二而不二。境照於境、境照於智、智照於智、智照於境，此四句說，說即無說、無說而說。此四句照，照而無照、無照而照。不可思議智境，斯之謂歟！此之智境須論六即，今是分真、究竟二位。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, relying on Aśvaghoṣa, what establishes the essence of the object is so-called original awakening; the essence of wisdom is so-called initial awakening. Hence the Awakening of Faith says, "What is called the meaning of awakening is that the essence of mind is free of thought. The mark of being free of thought is equal to the realm of empty space, pervading everywhere. The single mark of the dharma-realm is the eternally abiding Dharma-body of the Tathāgata; on the basis of this Dharma-body it is called original awakening" (end of quotation). This awakening is the nature; cultivation arising from the whole nature is called initial awakening. The treatise says that initial awakening is the same as original awakening. Since it says "free of thought," how could there be conceiving and discussing? Since it is "equal to empty space, pervading everywhere," how could there be a single time, a single mote, a single mind that is not original awakening and thus initial awakening? Therefore one can say: beings and buddhas, dependent and principal, of the three times and the ten directions are the object contemplated; beings and buddhas, dependent and principal, of the three times and one direction are the wisdom that contemplates. Object and wisdom differ in name, but their essence is not different; therefore what contemplates and what is contemplated are two yet not two. The object illuminates the object, the object illuminates wisdom, wisdom illuminates wisdom, wisdom illuminates the object: these four propositions, as spoken, are speaking that is no-speaking, no-speaking yet speaking; as illuminations, they are illuminating without illuminating, not illuminating yet illuminating. "Inconceivable sphere of wisdom": is this not what it means? This wisdom and object must be discussed in terms of the six identities; here it is the two stages of a portion of the truth and the ultimate.

The Three Bodies

Chinese Source

三、「若三」下，結脫名密，二：初、簡非。二、「以不」下，示密，俱例前。三、約後五句別對三身，三：初：牒文示義。二、「正解」下，依義釋句，三：

Chinese Source

初、二句，示報。正解能顯體等者，此乃似解能顯真體，真體乃是性德般若，既得顯發，即能觀達根本無明，故名正觀。而言報身者，在心名觀，就身名報，四十二位皆得論之。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, two phrases showing the reward[-body]. "Right understanding can reveal the essence" and so on: this is resembling understanding that can reveal the true essence. The true essence is the prajñā of inherent virtue; once it is revealed and brought forth, one can contemplate and penetrate fundamental ignorance; hence it is called right contemplation. As for calling it the reward-body: in the mind it is called contemplation; in terms of the body it is called reward. It can be discussed for all forty-two stages.

Chinese Source

二、「得正」下，二句，示應。分別約法知藥病也，解緣約機知生熟也。非此解別則無應身，故應身對機未熟而出乃名待時，已發後出乃名過時。啐啄同時，是應機相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Obtained right discrimination" on, two phrases showing the response[-body]. Discriminating is in terms of dharmas: knowing medicine and illness. Understanding conditions is in terms of capacities: knowing the raw and the ripe. Without this understanding and discrimination there would be no response-body. Hence, for the response-body to come forth while capacities are not yet ripe would be called "waiting for the time"; to come forth after they have already arisen would be called "letting the time pass." The chick pecking from within and the hen pecking from without at the same moment: this is the mark of responding to capacity.

Chinese Source

三、一句，示法無覺等者，此義須對報應簡之。秖一大覺而有寂照及非寂照三種之能，故名三身。覺即照覺，能照理故，即是報身。無覺即寂，覺能現形故，即是應身。今云無覺無不覺者，即非照非寂，覺能雙亡雙用，名究竟覺，即是法身。是故三身名為三佛。三種之了亦復如是，就身名覺、就心名了，三義宛然。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, one phrase showing the Dharma[-body]. "Neither awakening nor non-awakening" and so on: this meaning must be sifted against the reward and the response. Just one great awakening has three kinds of ability, the tranquil, the illuminating, and neither tranquil nor illuminating; hence they are called the three bodies. Awakening is illuminating awakening: because it can illuminate principle, it is the reward-body. Non-awakening is tranquil: because the awakening can manifest forms, it is the response-body. Now "neither awakening nor non-awakening" is neither illuminating nor tranquil: the awakening that can both negate both and use both is called ultimate awakening, and this is the Dharma-body. Therefore the three bodies are called the three buddhas. The three kinds of understanding (了) are likewise: in terms of the body it is called awakening; in terms of the mind it is called understanding. The three meanings are distinct.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "If these three bodies" on, concluding that the body is named secret, in two: first, sifting out what is not [secret]; second, from "neither one nor different" on, showing the secret; it can be seen.

Matching the Title and Mutual Pervasion

Chinese Source

二、「約正」下，約合對題，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Clarifying the Dharma-body in terms of right" on, matching them combined to the title, in two:

Chinese Source

初、合三德對題。上文約正明於三身、觀明三觀、脫明三脫，三三皆可對金光明，此明離也。今合三身為一法身，但對金字，合於三觀為一般若，但對光字，合於三脫為一解脫，但對明字。諸經諸論以三德等作修二性一說者，圓人解之，是合三義。此意至妙，學者應知。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, combining the three virtues and matching them to the title. The text above clarified the three bodies in terms of right, the three contemplations in terms of contemplation, and the three liberations in terms of liberation; all three sets of three could be matched to gold, light, and brightness; this makes clear their separation. Now the three bodies are combined into one Dharma-body, matched only to the character "gold"; the three contemplations are combined into one prajñā, matched only to the character "light"; the three liberations are combined into one liberation, matched only to the character "brightness." Where the sūtras and treatises speak of the three virtues and the like as "cultivation two, nature one," the person of the perfect understands it as the combining of the three meanings. This intent is utterly wondrous; students should know it.

Chinese Source

二、「三德」下，明五章皆密。三德是佛所證密藏，以被機故，說金光明微密之教。散脂本因稟此密教而生密解，即聞密名也。住理乃是顯於密體也，行行乃是修於密宗也，利他乃是起於密用也。不言教者，同佛故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "The three virtues" on, making clear that the five sections are all secret. The three virtues are the secret treasury realized by the Buddha; in order to reach capacities, he teaches the subtle and secret teaching of the Golden Light. Saṃjñeya originally received this secret teaching and gave rise to secret understanding; this is hearing the secret name. Dwelling in principle is revealing the secret essence; practicing the practice is cultivating the secret cardinal doctrine; benefiting others is giving rise to the secret function. The teaching is not mentioned, because [here] it is the same as the Buddha's.

Chinese Source

二、「復次」下，明互通。雖作三身、三脫、三觀三節解之，而十五句一一皆是金光明海，體量高廣，故使名義展轉相釋。如《涅槃》中百句解脫，以一一句皆是三德微密藏，故大師釋句句具百成於萬句。今舉正解具十四句、能具成十五句，合云由正解故正觀，由正解故得正分別，由正解故正解於緣，由正解故正能覺了，由正解故不思議智光，乃至由正解故於一切法含受一切法。以正解一句為首既然，餘句為首例亦如是，乃成一百七十五句，以體量一貫故名義相成。疏於三五皆舉頭句，例於四句皆十五句，注云云令此銷釋也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Further" on, making clear their mutual pervasion. Although they are explained in three sections, as the three bodies, the three liberations, and the three contemplations, each of the fifteen phrases is the ocean of the Golden Light, its essence and measure high and broad; hence names and meanings explain one another in turn. It is like the hundred phrases on liberation in the Nirvāṇa: since each phrase is the subtle and secret treasury of the three virtues, the Great Master, explaining them, made each phrase include a hundred, forming ten thousand phrases. Now take "right understanding" as including the [other] fourteen phrases; as what includes them it forms fifteen phrases. Combined, it should say: because of right understanding, right contemplation; because of right understanding, obtaining right discrimination; because of right understanding, rightly understanding conditions; because of right understanding, rightly able to awaken; because of right understanding, inconceivable light of wisdom; and so on up to: because of right understanding, within all dharmas containing and receiving all dharmas. As it is with the one phrase "right understanding" at the head, so it is with each of the other phrases at the head; thus it forms one hundred seventy-five phrases. Because essence and measure are one throughout, names and meanings complete one another. For [each of] the three [sets of] five the Commentary raises only the head phrase; by analogy, the [other] four phrases each have fifteen phrases. The note "(and so on)" has us explain it thus.

The Five Buddha-Natures

Chinese Source

二、「又作」下，用五性釋所用。五種皆名佛者，是果德故。皆稱性者，不改義故。若將此五對今三節五句義者，能顯散脂本證圓常、迹用周遍，護經德妙、利人益深，此乃今家對釋意也。文二：初、大師對初五句，二：初、示五性異同，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Further, explaining by the five kinds of buddha-nature" on, explaining what is used by the five natures. All five are called "buddha" because they are the virtue of the fruit. All are called "nature" because of the meaning of not changing. If these five are matched to the five-phrase meaning of the present three sections, it can show that Saṃjñeya's original realization is perfect and permanent, his trace-function pervasive, his virtue in protecting the sūtra wondrous, and his benefit to people deep; this is our school's intent in matching and explaining them. The text has two [parts]: first, the Great Master matches the first five phrases, in two: first, showing the sameness and difference of the five natures, in two:

Ru-Yi Translation

First, making clear that the three kinds do not differ. "Correct" means central and correct; "conditioning" means aiding condition; "revealing" means awakened understanding. All masters establish the names and meanings of these three alike; hence it says "do not differ."

Second, from "One school" on, making clear how two natures appear and disappear, in two: first, the two schools establish them differently, in two:

Chinese Source

初、明異相。果性者，緣了所剋智斷果也。境果者，緣了所轉境界，即陰等十種境也。果及果果者，果則別在智德；果果者則在斷德，智德之上又加斷德，故重言果。此之二二皆稱性者，悉以常住不改為義。境界不改者，修惡即性惡故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, making clear the marks of difference. "Fruit-nature" is the fruit of wisdom and severance won by the conditioning and revealing [causes]. "Object-nature" is the object-realms turned by the conditioning and revealing [causes], that is, the ten objects, the aggregates and the rest. "Fruit" and "fruit-of-fruit": the fruit lies particularly in the virtue of wisdom; the fruit-of-fruit lies in the virtue of severance. On top of the virtue of wisdom the virtue of severance is added; hence "fruit" is said twice. These two pairs are all called "nature" because all take permanence and non-change as their meaning. That the object-realm does not change is because cultivated evil is itself inherent evil.

"The fruit-of-fruit nature is submerged and absorbed into the fruit-nature": although wisdom and severance differ, both are called fruit.

Second, from "Now the five 'knows'" on, matching the five phrases to the natures, in two:

Chinese Source

初、對初家五性。安樂性即正因佛性者，安樂乃是涅槃之義，具足三法；今就合說，但名正因。世出世等者，六道是世間因果，三乘是出世因果。言因佛性者，境界之性但齊九界十如，是法望佛是因，以其佛界十如，是法望九稱果，皆屬能觀，不名境界。故《起信論》但以九相而為境界。是故業相名細中細，為佛境界。又云「依轉識故說名境界，而此證者無有境界可說。」故知境界是九界法。若爾，何名佛性？良以果人不獨成就佛界十法，亦能成就九界十法，是故千法。眾生雖具，體用不彰；唯佛究竟圓融自在，所以因法是果人性。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, matching them to the five natures of the first school. "The nature of peace and happiness is the correct-cause buddha-nature": peace and happiness is the meaning of nirvāṇa and is complete with the three dharmas; here, speaking in combination, it is called only the correct cause. "The worldly and the world-transcending" and so on: the six paths are worldly cause and fruit; the three vehicles are world-transcending cause and fruit. As for saying "cause buddha-nature": the nature of the object-realm extends only to the ten suchnesses of the nine realms; these dharmas, relative to the Buddha, are causes. As for the ten suchnesses of the buddha-realm, these dharmas, relative to the nine, are called fruit; all belong to what contemplates and are not called object-realm. Hence the Awakening of Faith takes only the nine marks as the object-realm. Therefore the mark of karma is called the subtle within the subtle and is the object-realm of the Buddha.
