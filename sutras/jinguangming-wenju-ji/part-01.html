Zhili's Preface, the Division of the Sūtra, and the Ordering Introduction

This is the first half of the first fascicle of Siming Zhili's Notes on the Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra (T1786), a running commentary on Zhiyi's Textual Commentary (T1785). A short preface, dated 1027, says that the lecture notes taken down from his late teacher Baoyun Yitong were garbled by other hands. Zhili therefore wrote his own record and borrowed secular and historical glosses from Gushan Zhiyuan's Searching Out the Hidden. He then explains the title "Textual Commentary" and follows Zhiyi's division of the sūtra into introduction, main teaching, and propagation. He defends the claim that the three parts interpenetrate against the masters north and south of the Yangzi and against Paramārtha. Most of the part comments on the ordering introduction, phrase by phrase: "Thus," "I heard," "at one time," "the Buddha," "dwelling," Rājagṛha, and Gṛdhrakūṭa. Each phrase is explained by the old masters, by the four siddhāntas, by the four teachings, and by contemplation of mind. The longest passage (unit 104) is a polemic against an unnamed contemporary. That person held that the contemplations of mind attached to phenomena in the commentaries cannot be practiced apart from Calming and Contemplation, and that a teacher's private record cannot guide practice. Zhili answers from Zhanran's Examples of Meaning and Annotations.

Ru-Yi Translation

When my late teacher formerly dwelt at Baoyun, he lectured on this scripture. His followers gathered in great numbers and vied to record what they heard, privately shaping it into scrolls; moreover, since the brush was entrusted to other hands, it instead fettered the far-reaching purport. As for the citing of sūtras and treatises as proof, the wording is often confused and mistaken, and whenever I come to the text I cannot but sigh. Recently, in the course of lecturing, I recalled the great meaning I had received and composed this record, while also adopting secular books and historical facts from Gushan's Searching Out the Hidden to serve as aids, in the hope that readers will not concern themselves with the matters of phenomena but will reflect on the benefit of principle and contemplation. Recorded on the third day of the twelfth month of the fifth year of Tiansheng.

The Title and the One Who Taught It

Chinese Source

釋題，二：初、正標題目。金等四字即所釋也，文句二字是能釋也。所釋經題，委在玄義。能釋文句者，文即經文，句謂章句，亦句逗也。即以章句節其經文，令其詮旨各有分齊。故荊谿云：「以由釋題，大義委悉，故至經文但粗分章段。題云文句，良由於此。」然立此二字，蓋謙辭耳。若觀釋經，大義非少。又解諸經皆稱文句，乃是通稱，故以經題簡之令別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the title, in two: first, marking the title proper. The four characters "Golden" and the rest are what is explained; the two characters "Textual Commentary" (文句, literally "text and phrases") are what does the explaining. The sūtra title that is explained is [treated] in detail in the Profound Meaning. As for "Textual Commentary," which does the explaining: 文 is the text of the sūtra; 句 means chapters and sentences (章句), and also phrase-breaks (句逗). That is, by chapters and sentences it divides the sūtra's text, so that what it expresses has its own limits. Hence Jingxi says: "Because the great meaning is set out in detail in explaining the title, when it comes to the sūtra's text it only roughly divides it into sections. That the title says 'Textual Commentary' is really due to this." Yet setting up these two characters is probably a word of modesty; if one looks at its explanation of the sūtra, the great meaning is not slight. Further, explanations of all sūtras are called "textual commentary"; this is a common name, so the sūtra title is used to sift it and make it distinct.

He again rejects [the masters] south of the River for putting "Predictions" and "Curing Illness" entirely in the propagation; hence it says "the later cause and fruit of teacher and disciples" and so on. It ought to say "the teacher's cause and the disciples' fruit"; the present wording seems inverted.

Our Master's Refutation: The Three Parts Interpenetrate

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Our master says" on, showing our master's intent, in two: first, refuting the masters in terms of principle, in two:

Chinese Source

初、總斥人情。正斥真諦，義兼江北。以此二師判師弟因果並在正宗，意謂序分全未辨說修證之道。至流通分，道味已歇微末而已，故總斥云是義不然。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, rejecting human sentiment as a whole. It rejects Paramārtha directly, and the meaning also covers [the masters] north of the River. For these two masters classify the cause and fruit of teacher and disciples as all within the main part, thinking that the introductory part does not yet discuss at all the path of cultivation and realization, and that by the propagation part the flavor of the path has already petered out into trifles. Hence it rejects them as a whole: "This is not so."

Second, from "The three sections" on, showing separately the sūtra's intent, in two: first, setting out the idea, in two:

Chinese Source

初、明三分互通。杜，塞也。三分共成一經感應，豈得義理互不相兼？如初序分敘述發起，若不闕於下之二分，何名正通之序？正是序中所發所述，復是流通所宣流布，不爾何名一經正說？流通一段，若捨序、正，為通何法？是故三分各具三義。上中下語，即七善中時節善也。《妙經》云「初中後善」，若其杜絕，何名善耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying that the three parts interpenetrate. "Blocked off" (杜) means stopped up. The three parts together make up the resonance and response of one sūtra; how could their meaning and principle fail to include one another? Take the narrating and initiating of the introductory part at the start: if it did not lack the two parts below, how could it be called the introduction of the main and the propagation? What is initiated and narrated within the introduction is precisely the main [teaching], and again what the propagation proclaims and spreads; otherwise, how could it be called the main teaching of one sūtra? As for the propagation section, if it abandoned the introduction and the main, what dharma would it propagate? Therefore each of the three parts contains three senses. The words "at the beginning, in the middle, and at the end" are the goodness of timing among the seven goods. The Wondrous [Dharma] Sūtra says "good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end"; if they were blocked off, how could they be called good?

二、「又眾」下，眾機遍益。經所被機，益有遲速，不必皆在正宗悟入，故云根性不定，何容序分全無法味？通中法味歇滅微末耶？既其不然，於流通中說流水因及信相果，復何所妨？彌、益也。督、率也。勵、勸也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Moreover, the faculties and natures" on, the capacities of the assembly benefit everywhere. The capacities the sūtra reaches benefit slowly or quickly; they do not all necessarily awaken and enter in the main part. Hence it says "the faculties and natures are not fixed." How could the introductory part be allowed to have no Dharma-flavor at all, or the Dharma-flavor in the propagation to have ceased and petered out into trifles? Since this is not so, what harm is there in teaching Jalavāhana's cause and Faith-Mark's fruit in the propagation? "All the more" (彌) means to increase. "Urges" (督) means to lead. "On" (勵) means to exhort.

Chinese Source

二、「又法」下，引經。師，即釋迦也。〈達多品〉中明佛往劫以國王身事阿私仙求大乘法，故曰師因。〈持品〉授記波闍、耶輸等成佛劫國，即弟子果。古諸法師判《法華經》，自〈法師品〉後皆屬流通，而人共許。此與江南判今流通有師弟因果，其意齊也，故云與義無妨。然不可定執，故云與奪由人。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Further, the Lotus makes clear" on, citing a sūtra. "The teacher" is Śākyamuni. The "Devadatta" chapter makes clear that in past eons the Buddha, in the body of a king, served the seer Asita, seeking the Dharma of the Great Vehicle; hence "the teacher's cause." The "Upholding" chapter gives predictions to [Mahā]prajāpatī, Yaśodharā, and others of their buddhahood, eon, and land; this is the disciples' fruit. The ancient Dharma masters, in classifying the Lotus Sūtra, assigned everything from the "Dharma Teacher" chapter on to the propagation, and people all accepted it. This is equal in intent to [the masters] south of the River classifying the present propagation as containing the cause and fruit of teacher and disciples; hence it says "the meaning is not harmed." Yet one may not cling to it fixedly; hence it says "Granting or withholding depends on the person."

It wishes the verbal teaching to flow on into the future ages of the true, semblance, and final [Dharma], always letting the multitude of beings grasp the meaning and principle; hence "not blocked in future ages."

Ru-Yi Translation

First, citing other texts as precedents. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra takes the cause and fruit of the buddha-land as its main [teaching]; since it has no introductory chapter, the masters took half of the "Buddha-Land" chapter as the introductory part. As for the Larger Prajñāpāramitā, its "Introduction" chapter says: "The Buddha, knowing that the assembly had gathered, told Śāriputra: 'Bodhisattvas who wish to know all dharmas with the wisdom knowing all modes should practice the prajñāpāramitā,'" and so on. Such teaching is already the main part, yet the text lies in the introductory part. As for the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, permanent life is its main [teaching]; since it has no introductory chapter, the gathering of the assembly in the "Longevity" chapter is taken as the introduction; this is in terms of the northern version. In the southern version, edited and settled by Duke Xie, the text on the gathering of the assembly in "Longevity" was taken and titled the "Introduction" chapter. In these three sūtras the introduction enters the main or the main enters the introduction, and the masters who divided and explained them did not find it doubtful; how can one doubt only our present classification of the introductory part as entering the "Lifespan" chapter?

For if the title of the "Lifespan" chapter had been placed after the gods and dragons gather in Faith-Mark's chamber, the text of the four buddhas resolving the doubt would arise abruptly, alone; hence it says "stands steeply apart." 嶄 (pronunciation note). 嶄 is the appearance of a craggy mountain. If the text arose abruptly alone, it would be like the dangerous sheerness of a mountain crag, not joined to what comes before. For this reason the title "Lifespan" was placed within the introductory section.

The Three Senses of "Introduction" and the Meaning of "Chapter"

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "The introduction has three senses" on, explaining the three parts of the sūtra proper, in three: first, explaining the introductory part, in two: first, explaining the chapter title, in two: first, explaining "introduction," in two:

Chinese Source

初、標三義。經之序分，義合有三，諸部之中或秖一二，蓋闕略也。今經與《法華》等諸大部中皆具此三。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, marking the three senses. The introductory part of a sūtra ought by rights to have three senses; among the various texts some have only one or two, which is due to omission and abbreviation. This sūtra and the great texts such as the Lotus all have these three.

Chinese Source

二、「次緒」下，釋三義。緒，謂繭之緒也。凡繭之抽絲，先抽其緒，緒盡方見其絲。今以五事在初，如絲之緒也。冠，去聲呼。敘方將述當益，蓋言敘述當時有正宗、流通之益，故此序述下一十七品也。發，謂開發物機之信也。起，謂興起聖應之教也。此序即以現瑞駭動物情，令信心顒顒，教必深益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "'Ordering'" on, explaining the three senses. 緒 ("ordering") refers to the end-thread of a cocoon. Whenever silk is drawn from a cocoon, one first draws out its end-thread; only when the end-thread is used up does one see the silk. Now, because the five items stand at the beginning, they are like the end-thread of the silk. 冠 ("cap") is read in the departing tone. "It narrates what is about to come and recounts the benefit to come" means that it narrates and recounts that at that time there will be the benefits of the main part and the propagation; hence this introduction narrates the seventeen chapters below. "Brings forth" (發) refers to opening and bringing forth the faith of beings' capacities; "initiates" (起) refers to raising up the sage's responsive teaching. This introduction, by manifesting auspicious signs, startles and moves beings' feelings, making their minds of faith expectant, so that the teaching will certainly benefit [them] deeply.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "'Chapter' (品)" on, explaining "chapter," in two: first, giving the Sanskrit and translating the name. Second, from "品 means 'class'" on, explaining the meaning according to the name, in two:

Chinese Source

初、正釋。此中文句俱在經也。以能詮教皆用四法，謂聲、名、句、文。此有二種：佛世滅後，若約佛世八音四辯梵音聲相，此一是實；名、句、文身但是聲上屈曲建立，此三是假。聲屬色法，名句文身屬第三聚不相應行，《毘曇》十四、《成論》十七、《瑜伽》二十四種，此則大小二宗所立有異。名句文者，《唯識》云「名詮自性、句詮差別，文即是字，為二所依。」若約滅後諸聖結集，彼土貝葉此方黃卷，其中所載名句文者，皆依形顯，色法建立也。今略舉此二，具足應四。氣類相從者，如以四法同明發起等義，故節為〈序品〉，乃至同明讚佛之義，故節為〈讚品〉。今雖釋序，而品義貫下。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining it proper. Here the "phrases and sentences" are both in the sūtra. The teaching that expresses always uses four dharmas: sounds, names, phrases, and syllables. These are of two kinds: in the Buddha's lifetime and after his extinction. In terms of the Buddha's lifetime — the features of the Brahmā-voice with its eight qualities and four kinds of eloquence — sound alone is real; the bodies of names, phrases, and syllables are merely established as inflections upon the sound, and these three are provisional. Sound belongs to form-dharmas; the bodies of names, phrases, and syllables belong to the third group, the formations not associated [with mind], [which number] fourteen in the Abhidharma, seventeen in the Satyasiddhi, and twenty-four kinds in the Yogācārabhūmi; here what the Lesser and Great schools establish differs. As for names, phrases, and syllables, the [Treatise on] Consciousness-Only says: "Names express self-nature; phrases express distinctions; syllables are letters, the basis of the two." Ru-Yi Translation

Second, contemplation of mind. With the perfect threefold contemplation one contemplates the aggregates and so on. The mind of cultivated evil is itself inherent evil, called the dharma-realm of evil; there is no dharma it does not gather in. Its essence is the three virtues, also called the three truths. Illuminating in accord with the truths, contemplation and object are not different; hence it is called "thus." "The object is itself correct contemplation": the object is original awakening; arising, it is initial awakening; though divided into new and old, the essence of awakening is not different; hence it can be called "the object is itself correct contemplation," and only then is this meaning accomplished. Were it not so, how could the four phrases "object illuminating object" and so on be established? As for "A sūtra says" and so on: contemplating an inert object, partial and lesser, forgetting the mind, or provisionally setting up suchness and so on — all are called wrong contemplation. Although this correct contemplation is not [set out] in terms of practice, if the practitioner wishes to gather phenomena into principle and cultivate as soon as he hears, he must, in the mind contemplated, sift the ten objects — the object of the aggregates is always present, the other nine await arising — and, in the contemplating contemplation, he must know the ten modes: those of superior faculties cultivate one, those of middling faculties up to seven, those of inferior faculties all seven. If one does not yet understand the Great Calming and Contemplation oneself, one should consult teachers and friends item by item and cultivate it only after clearly knowing the medicine and the illness; do not say that practicing a single phrase is enough. The explanations by contemplation that follow should be understood by this standard.

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying the four [senses of] "I." "I as I" and the rest are established after the model of the four phrases "birth birth" and so on in the Great Sūtra. Now, "I" is a provisional name, existing by taking hold of the aggregates. Since the dharmas of the aggregates have the four, "birth birth" and the rest, how could the "I," following the real dharmas, not be so too? For example, in explaining "birth birth" it says, "the great birth gives birth to the small birth": this is arising-and-ceasing giving birth to arising-and-ceasing. Taking hold of arising-and-ceasing dharmas forms an arising-and-ceasing self; hence, after the model of "birth birth," it forms "I as I." Although this is a contemplation of emptiness, it is not embodying emptiness; therefore the Tripiṭaka corresponds to the first phrase. Further, one should know that the arising-and-ceasing of the provisional and the real is originally so for beings, though they do not know it; now, receiving this teaching, they contemplate according to what is originally so. How is it only this teaching? The three below are all like this. In the shared teaching, real dharmas, as they arise, do not arise; hence the self in the aggregates is itself no-self; hence it belongs to the second phrase. The person of the distinct [teaching] knows that the aggregates do not arise yet arise; therefore he contemplates no-self as self. For this teaching knows the true self from the start; because views and pride are strong, it first contemplates no-self, next refutes no-self and establishes a self, and finally enters the true self; therefore this teaching corresponds to the third phrase. The person of the perfect [teaching] directly penetrates that the provisional and real before him are not-arising not-arising, and takes hold of the eternally abiding aggregates to form the true self. Since the self is identical to the middle, both truths converge on it; hence it says "the true I in which I and no-I are not two"; therefore this teaching corresponds to the fourth phrase. A teaching by its nature reaches capacities; hence the meanings of the four "I"s are matched to four kinds of faculty and nature, so that later expounders may distinguish them as above; hence the note "(and so on)."

Second, clarifying the four [senses of] "heard." The "I" is the master of hearing, and hearing is the function of the "I"; the master is a provisional person, the function a real dharma. If one understands the meaning of birth, the meaning of hearing is revealed of itself. But birth is a general discussion of dependent arising, while hearing comes specifically from teaching and listening; they are not without slight difference. From the causes and conditions of hearing there is further hearing; hence "hearing hearing." Since it arises from conditions, it ultimately returns to decay and extinction; this is the hearing of arising-and-ceasing. Hearing lacks the four natures; right where it is there is no hearing; hence "hearing not-hearing"; this is the hearing of non-arising. Although in the real there is no hearing, within the conventional there is hearing; hence "not-hearing hearing"; this is the hearing of the immeasurable. The two truths are identical to the middle; hence "not-hearing." The middle too cannot be grasped; hence again "not-hearing." In forty-nine years not a single word was spoken; how could there be a middle or extremes to be heard? This is the hearing of the unconditioned. The intent of the note is the same as before.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Lemmas: Every phrase Zhili quotes from Zhiyi reuses the English of `jinguangming-wenju` Part 1 ("How the Sūtra Is Divided" through "'Dwelling,' Rājagṛha, and Gṛdhrakūṭa"). The formula 二、「……」下 is rendered "Second, from '…' on," with the quoted words taken from the opening of the matching Textual Commentary sentence in English. 「……」者 is a direct quotation followed by a colon. The divisions use "in two: first …," as in the Gleanings.

Baoyun and Gushan (unit 2): "My late teacher" is Baoyun Yitong; "dwelt at Baoyun" refers to Baoyun Monastery in Siming, from which he takes his name. Gushan's Searching Out the Hidden (索隱) is Zhiyuan's commentary on the same Textual Commentary (金光明經文句索隱記). Zhili says he borrowed only its secular and historical glosses. The date is the twelfth month of Tiansheng 5, which falls at the turn of 1027–1028.

Paramārtha's version (units 5, 11): Paramārtha's Golden Light Emperor-King Sūtra (seven fascicles, twenty-two chapters) is lost. Its four added chapters survive in the combined version (合部金光明經, T664). Zhili's claim that no translation has an "Entrustment" chapter conflicts with the Taishō edition of Dharmakṣema's text (T663), which has a nineteenth chapter, 囑累品 (see `jinguangming-jing` notes). The translation follows Zhili.

Duke Xie (unit 26): Xie Lingyun, who with Huiyan and Huiguan revised the northern Nirvāṇa Sūtra into the southern version (T375). In that version the assembly's gathering became the "Introduction" chapter. "Longevity" (壽命品) renders the Nirvāṇa chapter title so as to keep it apart from the Golden Light's "Lifespan" (壽量品).

Lotus references (units 15, 17, 77, 101): "Good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end," the dragon girl's verse, "the marks of the world are constantly abiding," and "in every one of these places, a stūpa should be raised" follow the site's Lotus Sūtra (`ref/lotus-sutra.txt`), shortened as Zhili shortens them.

Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra (unit 96): The quotation is abbreviated from T277. The wording follows `puxian-guan-jing/p1_body.md`, without "Pervading All Places," which Zhili omits.

Great Treatise quotations (units 41, 67, 91, 99): These are from the Mahāprajñāpāramitā-upadeśa (T1509), fascicles 1–3: why sūtras begin with "Thus," Ānanda's question before the parinirvāṇa, the four conditions of ear-consciousness, the ten epithets, and the four dwellings. In this draft they were translated from Zhili's wording, not checked against CBETA.

Other sources: The "a-u" debate (unit 50) is from Āryadeva's Hundred Treatise (百論, T1569), fascicle 1. The Guangzhu Sūtra (廣主經) is a non-Buddhist text named there; the source was not located. Ānanda's four wishes (unit 110) are from the Sūtra of Repaying Kindness (大方便佛報恩經, T156). The Miaole quotations in units 99 and 104 are from Zhanran's Notes on the Textual Commentary on the Lotus (法華文句記, T1719). The Examples of Meaning passage is from 止觀義例 (T1913). The Annotations is 法華玄義釋籤 (T1717). The verse "Following and learning from a good teacher" was not located in this draft. "If one can resolve [doubts] oneself …" is attributed to the Great Master (Zhiyi); compare the five conditions in Mohe Zhiguan 4.

The five items, six items (units 38, 43): The six items of the Dilun masters ("Thus" as the Dharma heard, "I" as the upholder, "heard," "the Buddha," the place, the assembly) are the usual analysis of the opening formula. Zhili notes that this sūtra has only four of the five, since it lists no assembly that heard together.

Possible copying errors: Unit 29 「正釋經三分，也：」 is read as 三：(in three). Unit 63 「下根具七」 would usually read 具十 (all ten modes); it is translated literally as "all seven." Unit 72 「別教五談塵沙佛法」: 五 is unclear, perhaps for 廣 ("at length"); it is rendered "discusses in full." Unit 104 「記事附法」 is probably 託事附法; it is translated "recorded from phenomena" and keeps the parallel.

Unit 86 punctuation: 「不以此體應於眾生。眾生無由智合於諦」 is read as one conditional sentence: "if he did not respond … beings would have no means …".

This assumes the shared-teaching doctrine that bodhisattvas keep residual habits by vow (誓扶習生) and cut them off at buddhahood. Alternatively: "the Buddha originally, through his vow, has no residual habits."

Four truths (units 70, 77, 79): "Hearing of arising-and-ceasing / non-arising / the immeasurable / the unconditioned" stand for the four kinds of four truths (生滅、無生、無量、無作四諦), matched to the four teachings.
