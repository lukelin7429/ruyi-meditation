The Four Heavenly Kings, Sarasvatī, Śrī, Dṛḍhā, and Saṃjñeya's Secret

This is the first half of the fifth fascicle of Siming Zhili's Notes on the Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra (T1786). Here the propagation part begins, and Zhili follows Zhiyi's Commentary through the chapters on the protecting deities. On "The Four Heavenly Kings" he explains the place and names of the kings and their retinues, then reads the whole chapter as a contemplation "relying on phenomena": the four heavens are the four unconditioned truths, the kings are wisdom, and the ghosts are views and habituated thought. Through the six rounds of address and confirmation he adds many glosses from Chinese classics on government, calamities, omens, and court ritual, and he argues that the verses on space, sun, and moon show each of the three bodies containing all three, with capacity and response both being the dharma-realm. On Sarasvatī he matches four kinds of eloquence to the four teachings; on Śrī he explains why the goddess of merit may be honored although the Nirvāṇa Sūtra tells the wise to refuse her; on Dṛḍhā he reads the earth as the perfection of wisdom. The part ends partway through the "Spirit Saṃjñeya" chapter, where the name "secret" is explained through wisdom, object, and the right, and the first contemplations are set out.

The "Four Heavenly Kings" Chapter: The Place and the Persons

Chinese Source

大章流通，二：初、釋〈四天王品〉題，二：初、因緣釋，二：初、約處釋人，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

The major section, the propagation part, in two: first, explaining the title of the chapter "The Four Heavenly Kings," in two: first, explanation by causes and conditions, in two: first, explaining the persons in terms of the place, in two:

Chinese Source

初、明處。二十八天皆上升之趣，此當其首。天分三界，於下界中此復在初。居半須彌者，處之所依。欲界六天，二天依山，忉利居頂，四王在半。梵語須彌，此云妙高，出水八萬四千由旬，四寶所成。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying the place. The twenty-eight heavens are all destinies of ascent, and this is the first of them. The heavens are divided into the three realms, and within the lower realm this again is the first. "They dwell halfway up Sumeru": this is the place on which they rely. Of the six heavens of the desire realm, two heavens rely on the mountain: the Trāyastriṃśa [Heaven] dwells on its summit, and the four kings halfway up. The Sanskrit "Sumeru" is rendered here "wondrously high"; it stands eighty-four thousand yojanas above the water and is made of four jewels.

Chinese Source

二、「東黃」下，出人。持國者，護持國土故。乾闥婆，此云尋香，行天帝俗樂神也。富單那，此云臭，餓鬼中勝者，或云主熱病鬼也。增長者，令自他善根增長故。鳩槃茶，此云甕形，頗似冬瓜，是厭魅鬼也。薜荔多，此云祖父鬼，餓鬼中最劣者。雜語者，能作種種語故。毘舍闍，此云噉精氣鬼，噉人及五穀精氣故，亦云顛狂鬼也。毘沙門，此云多聞，福德之名聞四方故。夜叉，此云輕健，飛空健疾故。羅剎，此云可畏，亦名暴惡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "On the east is the terrace of yellow gold" on, setting out the persons. "Upholding the Country": because he protects and upholds the land. Gandharva is rendered here "seeking fragrance"; they are the spirits of secular music who attend the heavenly emperor. Pūtana is rendered here "stinking"; it is the superior among hungry ghosts, or is said to be the ghost that governs fevers. "Increase and Growth": because he makes the wholesome roots of self and others increase and grow. Kumbhāṇḍa is rendered here "jar-shaped," rather like a winter melon; it is a ghost that bewitches [people]. Preta is rendered here "ancestor ghost"; it is the lowest among hungry ghosts. "Mixed Speech": because he can make all kinds of speech. Piśāca is rendered here "ghost that devours vital essence," because it devours the vital essence of humans and of the five grains; it is also called the ghost of madness. Vaiśravaṇa is rendered here "Much Hearing," because the fame of his blessings and virtue is heard in the four directions. Yakṣa is rendered here "light and swift," because they fly through the air swiftly. Rākṣasa is rendered here "fearsome," also called "violent and evil."

Explanation by Contemplative Practice

Chinese Source

二、觀行釋。三種觀法，此當託事，以其境智一一皆借事義而立也。須知託事多兼法相，如王舍城而觀五陰、耆闍崛山而表三德，此之五三何殊四諦？然此正是託感應事明境觀也。分二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explanation by contemplative practice. Of the three kinds of contemplative method, this corresponds to relying on phenomena, because its object and wisdom are each established by borrowing the meaning of phenomena. One must know that relying on phenomena often also includes the marks of dharmas: as with contemplating the five aggregates through the city of Rājagṛha, and representing the three virtues by Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa — how do these fives and threes differ from the four truths? Yet this is precisely relying on the phenomena of resonance and response to make clear object and contemplation. It divides into two:

Chinese Source

初、示觀相。四諦四智乃至諦下各論見思，何教不說？今釋教義既專約圓，故所明觀不關三教。四天須表無作諦理，四王乃表無緣妙智，照事即理名護諦境，了縛為脫名護心數，為他說此名為護世。通別見思同體為障，名為鬼神。法花指此等者，不無少異，彼以鬼神但譬五利，乃將蟲鳥而譬五鈍。今則利鈍俱類鬼神，但取分同也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing the marks of the contemplation. The four truths, the four wisdoms, and even the discussion of views and habituated thought under each truth — what teaching does not teach them? Since the present explanation of the teaching's meaning is solely in terms of the perfect, the contemplation it makes clear does not concern the [other] three teachings. The four heavens must represent the principle of the unconditioned truths; the four kings represent wondrous wisdom without condition. Illuminating phenomena as identical to principle is called protecting the object of the truths; understanding bondage as liberation is called protecting the mental factors; teaching this for others is called protecting the world. Views and habituated thought, common and particular, of the same essence, form the obstruction and are called ghosts and spirits. "The Lotus points to these" and so on: this is not without a slight difference. There, ghosts and spirits are a simile only for the five keen [agents], while insects and birds are used as a simile for the five dull. Here, keen and dull are both classed as ghosts and spirits; it takes only a partial similarity.

Chinese Source

二、「若不」下，明利益。而且先明不觀有損。侵害心王，妙智不發也；毀損境界，諦理不彰也。心王至迸散，示迷三德而為三障。雖論三障，迷由煩惱，故云俱為鬼神所惱。「能觀」下，正示觀之有益。無明即明，為能觀智。此觀觀苦，生死即涅槃也；此觀觀集，煩惱即菩提也。控御等者，不動諸見而修道品、不斷癡愛起諸明脫，如斯控御豈獨不被侵害，而能顯理備德，顯理故諦境國安、備德故心數民寧，以轉八萬塵勞成八萬定慧也。自行了達既其若此，令他修證豈不然乎？託護世天王之事，修觀獲益其相略爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "If one does not illuminate" on, clarifying the benefit. Yet first it makes clear the harm of not contemplating. "Invade and harm the mind-king": wondrous wisdom does not arise. "Damage its realm": the principle of the truths is not manifest. "The mind-king" through "scatter" shows delusion about the three virtues becoming the three obstructions. Though it speaks of three obstructions, the delusion comes from afflictions; hence it says "both troubled by the ghosts and spirits." From "If one can contemplate" on shows properly the benefit of contemplation. Ignorance identical to illumination is the contemplating wisdom. When this contemplation contemplates suffering, birth-and-death is nirvāṇa; when this contemplation contemplates accumulation, afflictions are bodhi. "Bring … under control" (控御) and so on: without moving the various views one cultivates the factors of the path; without cutting off delusion and craving one gives rise to the illuminations and liberations. Controlling like this, not only is one not invaded and harmed, but one can reveal principle and be complete in virtue. Because principle is revealed, "the country" of the object of the truths is at peace; because virtue is complete, "the people" of the mental factors are tranquil; one turns the eighty thousand toils of dust into eighty thousand concentrations and wisdoms. Since one's own practice of understanding is like this, how could leading others to cultivate and realize not be so? Relying on the phenomenon of the world-protecting heavenly kings, the marks of the benefit gained by cultivating contemplation are roughly thus.

The First Round: Praising the Sūtra's Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, and Function

Second, explaining the sūtra text proper, in seven: the first section is divided into five; the first again into six: first, stating the ability to protect the country, in two: first, dividing the text. Second, from "The narration of reverence" on, explaining the meaning, in two: first, the four kings address the Buddha, in two:

初、經家敘。先標北方天王者，西土以北方為上也。偏袒者，西方之禮，弟子詣師必須偏袒，表有執役。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the compilers' narration. The heavenly king of the north is named first because in the Western Land the north is regarded as superior. Baring [the shoulder] is a rite of the West: when a disciple goes to his teacher he must bare [his shoulder], showing that he is ready to serve.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the address proper, in two: first, dividing the text and establishing the meaning. Second, from "The principle of the dharma-nature" on, explaining the text by means of the meaning, in two: first, praising the sūtra, in three:

Chinese Source

初、歎體。佛所護理無量甚深，橫攝法周、竪收法盡。理若不爾，豈名經王？問：文詮此理，故言經王。是則經文但是能詮，王惟在理。釋題那云文號經王、教攝眾典？指文、指理二處不同，如何和會？答：若前三教文理不合，此則為妨。今乃從圓，法皆不二，文外無理、理外無文，釋題舉文若其孤立，豈得稱王？今疏指理不攝文者，翻屬前教信文理合是經是王，其義祕妙不可情求。今經王歎體，與〈序品〉不別。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, praising the essence. The principle held in mind and protected by the buddhas is immeasurable and very deep: horizontally it gathers in dharmas in their entirety; vertically it takes in dharmas exhaustively. If principle were not so, how could it be called the king of sūtras? Question: "The text expresses this principle; hence 'king of sūtras.'" Then the sūtra text is only what expresses, and "king" lies only in principle. Why then does the explanation of the title say that the text is called the king of sūtras and that the teaching gathers in all scriptures? Pointing to the text and pointing to principle — the two places differ; how are they reconciled? Answer: If this were the earlier three teachings, in which text and principle do not combine, this would be a problem. Now, following the perfect, dharmas are all nondual: there is no principle outside the text and no text outside principle. If the explanation of the title, in raising the text, left it standing alone, how could it be called king? To take the present Commentary as pointing to principle without including the text would, on the contrary, belong to the earlier teachings. Believe that text and principle combine, that it is both sūtra and king; this meaning is secret and wondrous and cannot be sought by feeling. The present praise of the essence as "king of sūtras" is no different from the "Introduction" chapter.

Chinese Source

二、「約體」下，歎經宗。約體修行者，體是本覺，起成始覺，方得名為約體修行。體具佛界因果二嚴，全體成修、二嚴無作，名非莊嚴而為莊嚴。今以極果二嚴為宗。「世天」下，經云「諸天」，包此三也。然應有四，恐文脫誤。或可天王本是生天，故疏不出。此四天名義出《大經》。〈德王品〉云「一者世間天，如諸國王；二者生天，從四天王乃至非非想；三者淨天，謂四果支佛；四者義天，謂十住菩薩，以見一切法是空義故。」《大論》明三種天：一假名天，即世天也；二生天；三清淨天，則兼三乘也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Cultivating practice on the basis of the essence" on, praising the sūtra's cardinal doctrine. "Cultivating practice on the basis of the essence": the essence is original awakening; arising, it accomplishes initial awakening; only then can it be called cultivating practice on the basis of the essence. The essence includes the two adornments of cause and fruit of the buddha-realm; the whole essence becomes cultivation, and the two adornments are unconditioned; this is called "not adornment, and therefore adornment." Here the two adornments of the ultimate fruit are taken as the cardinal doctrine. From "The worldly gods" on: the sūtra says "the gods," which includes these three. Yet there should be four; I fear the text has an omission. Or perhaps the heavenly kings are themselves born gods, so the Commentary does not list them. The names and meanings of these four gods come from the Great Sūtra. The "Virtuous King" chapter says: "First, the worldly god, like the kings of the realms; second, the born god, from the Four Heavenly Kings up through neither perception nor non-perception; third, the pure god, namely the four fruits and the pratyekabuddhas; fourth, the meaning god, namely the bodhisattvas of the ten abidings, because they see that all dharmas bear the meaning of emptiness." The Great Treatise clarifies three kinds of gods: first, gods by provisional name, which are the worldly gods; second, born gods; third, pure gods, which include the three vehicles.

Chinese Source

三、「又下」下，歎經用。上至菩薩者，經無菩薩之言。既云能滅一切眾生苦惱，則通指九界方名一切。經文有四：

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "Further, from the hells below" on, praising the sūtra's function. "Up to the bodhisattvas above": the sūtra has no word "bodhisattvas." Since it says it "can extinguish the … sufferings and distresses of all beings," it points to all nine realms in common, which alone can be called "all." The sūtra text has four [parts]:

Chinese Source

初、天趣。能與眾生快樂者，天趣眾生也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the heavenly destiny. "Can give happiness to beings": these are beings of the heavenly destiny.

Chinese Source

次、「是經」下，三惡趣。諸河，三趣沈沒，猶如大河。焦乾枯竭者，滅三惡苦也。有經於「枯竭」上加「能令」二字者，妄也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, from "this sūtra" on, the three evil destinies. "The rivers": the three destinies sink and drown, like great rivers. "Scorch, dry up, and run out": extinguishing the sufferings of the three evil [destinies]. Some sūtra [copies] add the two characters 能令 ("can make") before 枯竭 ("run out"); this is wrong.

Chinese Source

三、「能除怖畏」去，是人趣。初、言一切怖畏是總；次別明破三障惡。三災，是報障。怨賊，即刀兵也，并饑饉、疫病，名三災。惡星，是業障。業來責報，故惡星現也。憂惱，是煩惱障。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "can remove all fears" on, this is the human destiny. First, "all fears" is general; next it particularly clarifies breaking the evils of the three obstructions. The three calamities are the obstruction of retribution. "Hostile invaders" are weapons; together with famine and epidemics they are called the three calamities. "Evil stars" are the obstruction of karma: when karma comes to demand its retribution, evil stars appear. "Worry and distress" are the obstruction of afflictions.

Chinese Source

四、「舉要」下，總指九界眾生也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, from "To speak of the essentials" on, pointing generally to the beings of the nine realms.

Protecting the Country Inwardly by the Dharma

Chinese Source

二、「從世」下，述能護，二：初、分文。二、「護國」下，釋義，二：初、內以法護國，四：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "From 'World-Honored One! When this … the Golden Light'" on, stating their ability to protect, in two: first, dividing the text. Second, from "Protecting the country by the Dharma is again four" on, explaining the meaning, in two: first, protecting the country inwardly by the Dharma, in four:

Chinese Source

初、護國之由。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the reason for protecting the country.

Chinese Source

二、以法護國。即是述其護國所以。無法安能護持國土？而其所以有內有外、外即世法、內即心法。若但行說世間之法，則令此經非方等教。天王全是凡夫心口，須知文文皆有世間出世間意，但以此句顯內義便，故云正與觀心相應。如下〈散脂鬼神品〉中諸文，且順世法詮辨。及至自述得名之由，則全廢事解，故云「現見不思議智境、不思議智光」，乃至云「我能安住一切法如性，於一切法含受一切法」。境智若是，故名為密，方能外現鬼神大將。豈非有理密故乃有事密？今文經雖不顯，大師得意，乃以行說諦智之法，而為護世安民所以。心數不行等者，心心數法全體即是方便般若，心王邪故數行邪境；今王既正導令不行、不行而行、行深般若到於彼岸。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, protecting the country by the Dharma. This is stating the reason for their protecting the country: without the Dharma, how could they protect and uphold the land? And the reason has an inner and an outer: the outer is worldly dharmas; the inner is the dharma of mind. If one practiced and taught only worldly dharmas, this sūtra would not be a vaipulya teaching. The heavenly kings [speak] wholly with the mind and mouth of ordinary beings; one must know that every passage has both a worldly and a world-transcending sense. Only because this phrase readily reveals the inner meaning does [the Commentary] say "corresponds exactly with contemplation of mind." For example, the passages in the "Spirit Saṃjñeya" chapter below are for the time being explained in accord with worldly dharmas; but when he comes to state the reason for his name, he wholly abandons the phenomenal explanation; hence he says, "I see directly … an inconceivable sphere of wisdom," "inconceivable light of wisdom," and even, "I can abide in the suchness-nature of all dharmas; within all dharmas I contain and receive all dharmas." Since object and wisdom are thus, he is called "secret" and can outwardly manifest as a great spirit general. Is it not that because there is a secret of principle there is a secret of phenomena? In the present text, though the sūtra does not make it plain, the Great Master grasped the intent and took practicing and teaching the dharma of the truths and wisdom as the reason for protecting the world and giving peace to the people. "So that the mental factors do not act" and so on: the whole essence of mind and mental factors is expedient prajñā. When the mind-king is perverse, the factors act on perverse objects; now that the king is correct, he guides them so that they do not act — not acting, yet acting — practicing the deep prajñā and arriving at the other shore.

Chinese Source

三、天黨護國。意同向說。黨謂徒黨，即八部也。黨，輩也。帥黨者，帥音率，導引也。「如轉輪王」下，是事釋。以輪王降四方，喻天王遮惡鬼。七寶，謂輪、象、馬、女、珠、臣、兵也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, protecting the country with the heavenly host. The intent is the same as stated before. 黨 ("host") means a band of followers, namely the eight classes; 黨 means a company. "Leading the host" (帥黨): 帥 (pronunciation note) means to guide. From "just as a wheel-turning king" on is the phenomenal explanation: the wheel-turning king subduing the four directions is a simile for the heavenly kings holding back the evil ghosts. The seven jewels are the wheel, the elephant, the horse, the maiden, the jewel, the minister, and the general.

Chinese Source

四、天眼護國。以報得天眼者，非修得也。受天身則眼必徹障。幽，暗也。燭，照也。萌，種子初剖也。杜，塞也。謂以天眼徹照，防將萌之禍根、塞漸起之惡源也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, protecting the country with the heavenly eye. "The heavenly eye obtained as reward" is not obtained by cultivation: receiving a heavenly body, the eye necessarily sees through obstructions. 幽 ("dark") means darkness. 燭 ("lit") means to illuminate. 萌 ("sprout") is a seed first splitting open. 杜 ("stopping") means to block. That is, seeing through with the heavenly eye, they guard against the root of misfortune about to sprout and block the source of evil that is gradually arising.
