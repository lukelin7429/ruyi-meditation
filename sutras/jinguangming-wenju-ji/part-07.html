Golden Dragon Honored's Praise Concluded and the Object of Emptiness

This is the first half of the fourth fascicle of Siming Zhili's Notes on the Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra (T1786). It finishes the "Praise" chapter and covers most of the "Emptiness" chapter. Zhili first matches each line of King Golden Dragon Honored's verses to a numbered item among the thirty-two major marks and eighty minor features, and argues that the "specially honored body" does not need a larger size or more marks: the ordinary sixteen-foot body, seen through the principle of the middle, is itself specially honored. He then comments on the praise beyond words, the dedication, and the king's five vows. On the "Emptiness" chapter he explains Zhiyi's four kinds of emptiness and the six phrases that set the perfect teaching's emptiness against the "double negation" of heretics, the two vehicles, and the distinct teaching. He discusses why the chapter speaks of emptiness only briefly, how "sharp" and "dull" faculties are judged by hearing and retaining, and the immeasurable meaning of emptiness. The rest of the part follows the verses on the body as an empty village, the six, twelve, and eighteen sense-fields, the four elements as four snakes in a basket, and mind, intention, and consciousness, where Zhili insists that the perfect contemplation uses the three consciousnesses even when only the sixth is taken as its object.

Ru-Yi Translation

First, praising seven oceans of major marks. The thirty-two are the major [marks] and the eighty minor features are the minor [marks]; each mark and feature is the dharma-realm, boundless and bottomless, hence called an ocean. That is, the first phrase praises the fourteenth, the golden light, subtle; hence it says "the highest color." Next, one phrase praises the fifteenth, the body's light; third, one line praises the twenty-eighth, the Brahmā voice, deep and far-reaching; fourth, one line praises the seventy-ninth among the minor marks, the hair colored like a blue pearl; fifth, one line praises the twenty-second, the full forty teeth; sixth, one line praises the twenty-ninth, the eyes; seventh, one line praises the twenty-seventh, the tongue large and thin, covering the face up to the hairline; eighth, one line praises the thirty-first, the mark of the white tuft between the brows. Of these eight sections, one is a minor mark and seven are major marks.

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, from "he obtains the true taste" on, again praising four oceans of major marks. First, two phrases praise the twenty-sixth, the saliva in the throat, obtaining the highest of tastes. Second, four phrases praise the thirteenth, the hairs pointing upward, dark blue, soft, and curling to the right. Third, from "his long arms" on, four phrases praise the ninth, the mark of the hands stroking the knees when standing. Fourth, from "his halo of a fathom" on, four phrases praise the fifteenth, the body's light, one zhang on each side. "A fathom" here is said in terms of the Buddha['s own measure]: since his hands stroke his knees, it is precisely one zhang on each side. Since this is the ordinary light (常光), we can verify that all the marks are ordinary marks. As for saying that it praises the specially honored [body]: hence we know that the body need not be large nor the marks many; simply when the karmic consciousness sees [the body] relying on the principle of the middle, it is called specially honored.

學者應知，此丈六身若其量度即不得際；若不度之，所見如故。如淨名室但是一丈，而能容受百千人天，又能容於三萬二千師子之座皆高八萬四千由旬，其一丈量初無所改，良以三脫不思議力使之然也。今即常身歎尊特相，義豈不然？微妙寂滅，斯言得矣。經文除此四大相外，或歎放光拔苦與樂行業之因、功德之果，悉如經文，但須皆作十番益解。其有二小相，以文顯故疏不指之，即面貌如月，乃第四十一面淨如滿月也；身無垢穢，即十一身淨潔也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Students should know: if this sixteen-foot body is measured, its limit cannot be reached; if it is not measured, what is seen is as before. Just as Vimalakīrti's room was only one zhang, yet could hold hundreds of thousands of humans and gods, and could also hold thirty-two thousand lion thrones, each eighty-four thousand yojanas high, while its one-zhang measure was not changed at all — truly the inconceivable power of the three liberations makes it so. Now, praising the marks of the specially honored [body] right in the ordinary body — how could the meaning not be so? "Subtle, in quiescent extinction": these words hit the mark. Apart from these four major marks, the sūtra text sometimes praises the releasing of light that removes suffering and gives happiness, the causes of practice and karma, and the fruits of merit, all as in the sūtra text; but all must be explained as the ten sets of benefit. There are two minor marks which, because the text makes them plain, the Commentary does not point out: "the countenance like the moon" is the forty-first, the face pure like the full moon; "the body without any defilement" is the eleventh, the body pure and clean.

Sixth, from "his hands and feet" on, two phrases again praise one ocean of major marks, namely the fourth, the hands and feet soft, surpassing the other parts of the body. The thirty-two marks and the eighty minor features are fully [set out] as in the Sequential Gates of the Dharma-Realm. Dragon Honored's wise skill, though brief, is all-round.

Praise Extended by Kind, Praise beyond Words, and the Dedication

Chinese Source

三、「從去」下，遍類讚。三世塵數而言一者，過去不滅、未來不生、現在不住，法身平等報應無差，一不離多、多即是一，生佛尚即，佛佛豈殊？是故讚一類於一切。此文復是讚尊特身。何者？如前疏云「若見四佛同尊特身，一身一智慧即是常身，弟子眾一故。若四佛不同，即是應化，弟子眾多故。」知秖就一身一智及常住義是尊特相，不必須論現起大身也。《文殊問般若》者，「問」字誤也，應作「說」字。今家依二經明常坐三昧：一《文殊說般若》、二《文殊問菩提》。今所引文，是《說般若》文也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "The buddhas of past and future" on, praise extended by kind. As for saying "one" of the dust-mote numbers of the three times: the past does not cease, the future does not arise, the present does not abide; the Dharma-body is equal, and the reward and response [bodies] are without difference; one does not depart from many, and many are identical to one. Beings and buddhas are still identical — how could buddha and buddha differ? Therefore praising one extends by kind to all. This text is again praise of the specially honored body. Why? As the Commentary said earlier: "If one sees the four buddhas as the same specially honored body, 'one body, one wisdom,' this is the permanent body, because the assembly of disciples is one. If the four buddhas are not the same, this is response and transformation, because the assemblies of disciples are many." We know that it is solely in terms of "one body, one wisdom" and the meaning of permanent abiding that it is the mark of the specially honored [body]; there is no need to discuss the manifesting of a large body. "The Prajñā [Sūtra] of Mañjuśrī's Questions": the character 問 ("questions") is a mistake; it should be the character 說 ("spoken"). Our school, relying on two sūtras, makes clear the samādhi of constant sitting: first, the Prajñā Spoken by Mañjuśrī; second, Mañjuśrī's Questions on Bodhi. The text cited here is a passage of the Spoken Prajñā.

Ru-Yi Translation

The superiority and inferiority of the three bodies does not depart from distinction, [yet] there is the meaning of fusion and identity; students should know this. From "[Fifth,] from 'I now'" on, the dedication. First, dedicating phenomena to principle, which is the reality-limit; second, dedicating self to others, which is the limit of beings; third, dedicating cause to fruit, which is the limit of bodhi. Here the reality-limit is lacking; bodhi includes it, since "the unsurpassed path" is unsurpassed by its original nature.

The "Emptiness" Chapter: Four Kinds of Emptiness

Chinese Source

三、「從信」下，結會二世事釋〈空品〉，二：初、釋題，二：初、正釋題，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, from "Faith-Mark, you should know" on, concluding and connecting the events of the two ages. Explaining the "Emptiness" chapter, in two: first, explaining the title, in two: first, explaining the title proper, in two:

Chinese Source

初、約四教詮空示。所言空者，破相為義，故一代教四空不同，若不辨之迷名昧理。此自分二：初、約部列四，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing [emptiness] as expressed by the four teachings. What is called emptiness has refuting marks as its meaning; hence in the teachings of a lifetime the four emptinesses are not the same. If they are not distinguished, one is confused about the names and in the dark about principle. This in turn divides into two: first, listing the four in terms of the group [of sūtras], in two:

Chinese Source

初、正判四空。滅色入空者，三藏教也。且寄色言諸法皆滅，謂析破見愛，陰乃不生。既詮實有，滅方入空也。即色是空者，通教也。體乎因果非四性生，既詮幻有，故即色是空也。滅邊入空者，別教也。中道為空，不唯空有亦乃空空，然不知中具於空有，是故次第滅二邊已，方入中空也。即邊是空者，圓教也。中道具德，何邊不中？唯假唯空三皆絕待，頓破諸相，名即邊空。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, classifying the four emptinesses proper. "Entering emptiness by extinguishing form" is the Tripiṭaka teaching. Using form as an example, it says that all dharmas are extinguished: that is, when views and craving are analyzed and broken, the aggregates then do not arise. Since it expresses real existence, only by extinction does one enter emptiness. "Form itself being empty" is the shared teaching. It embodies cause and fruit as not born in any of the four ways; since it expresses illusory existence, form itself is empty. "Entering emptiness by extinguishing the extremes" is the distinct teaching. The middle way is taken as emptiness; it empties not only existence but also emptiness. Yet it does not know that the middle includes emptiness and existence; therefore only after extinguishing the two extremes in sequence does it enter the emptiness of the middle. "The extremes themselves being empty" is the perfect teaching. The middle way includes [all] virtues — what extreme is not the middle? Solely provisional, solely empty: all three are absolute, suddenly refuting all marks; this is called "the extremes themselves being empty."

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Since the repentance of this sūtra" on, in terms of the group the four are required; classifying the teaching as common [to the vehicles]. The three vehicles repent together: before, it gathers in the Tripiṭaka; after, it extends to the distinct and the perfect. The emptiness that guides and accomplishes, if discussed together, is of four kinds; hence the wisdom of the four emptinesses cannot but be made clear.

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing it by principle. Using the four to gather in capacities is the common way; that the chapter lies in the perfect emptiness is its precise intent. Therefore the emptiness of the middle is shown as identical to the extremes.

Chinese Source

二、「何故」下，引文示。諸部《般若》廣示衍中三教空慧，復以三藏為助道觀，斯為聞持利根之者廣談空相。此空慧機，義持雖利、聞持根鈍，故不廣談四種空相，唯說即邊一中空慧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Why so?" on, showing it by citing the text. The various Prajñā [sūtras] broadly show the wisdom of emptiness of the three teachings of the Mahāyāna, and further take the Tripiṭaka as contemplation aiding the path; this is discussing the marks of emptiness broadly for those sharp in faculty in hearing and retaining. These capacities for the wisdom of emptiness, though sharp in retaining the meaning, are dull in faculty in hearing and retaining; hence [this sūtra] does not broadly discuss the marks of the four kinds of emptiness, but teaches only the wisdom of the one emptiness of the middle identical to the extremes.

Chinese Source

二、「又空」下，略示圓相。中受空名，意彰蕩相。凡夫執有，塊然質礙：二乘證無，灰滅歸寂；中觀絕念，空此兩邊令畢竟淨。約次不次分於別圓，今不次也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Further, emptiness empties existence" on, briefly showing the marks of the perfect. The middle receives the name "emptiness" to make plain its intent of sweeping away marks. Ordinary beings cling to existence, lump-like and obstructive in substance; the two vehicles realize nonexistence, extinguished to ashes and returning to stillness. The contemplation of the middle cuts off thought, emptying these two extremes and making them ultimately pure. The distinct and the perfect are divided as sequential and non-sequential; this one is non-sequential.

The Six Phrases: Emptiness Refuting the Double Negations

Chinese Source

二、「直作」下，約六句對中簡，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "If one simply says this" on, sifting the middle by means of the six phrases, in two:

Chinese Source

初、明用句意。若直說空是中道義能空二邊，其如邪小及以別教皆說雙非空於有無，故迷名者謂與今同，何能超悟邊即中空？又復恐謂若是圓實合談中道，那但名空？空唯離有，豈此中道雙離二邊？為防此計，故作相破相修相，即三雙六句而對辨之。若諸法相名相濫者，無此六句莫辨異同。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying the intent of using the phrases. If one simply says that emptiness is the meaning of the middle way, able to empty the two extremes, then since the heretics, the Small [Vehicle], and the distinct teaching all speak of the double negation emptying existence and nonexistence, those confused by names would say these are the same as ours; how could they rise to awaken to the emptiness of the middle identical to the extremes? It was further feared that they would say: if this is the perfect and the real, it ought to discuss the middle way; why only name it emptiness? Emptiness departs only from existence; how could it be this middle way that departs from both extremes? To guard against this reckoning, [the Commentary] makes mutual refuting, mutual cultivating, and mutual identity, that is, three pairs and six phrases, to distinguish them by contrast. When the names and marks of dharmas overlap, without these six phrases one cannot distinguish sameness and difference.

Chinese Source

二、「空破」下，正用句簡，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "emptiness refutes neither-existent-nor-nonexistent" on, using the phrases proper to sift, in two:

Chinese Source

初、列六句。雙非是中沒於中名存雙非者，以中道名邪小稀立，若雙非名處處皆有，故特用之，對今圓空辨於同異。又外道二乘別教空句及雙非句，體是思議可破壞法；圓教雙非及空若立，諸義皆滅，是以圓二互破諸二。又復諸二互修，圓二則二酥中外道菩薩入圓者是，二乘初心亦有修義。此之兩雙，諸二對圓優劣有異。第三一雙屬圓當教，名別體同是故相即，不言修破。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, listing the six phrases. As for the double negation being the middle, with the name "middle" dropped and "double negation" kept: the name "middle way" is rarely established by the heretics and the Small [Vehicle], whereas the name "double negation" is found everywhere; hence it is specially used to distinguish sameness and difference against the present perfect emptiness. Further, the emptiness-phrases and double-negation phrases of the heretics, the two vehicles, and the distinct teaching are in essence conceivable and destructible dharmas; once the double negation and the emptiness of the perfect teaching are established, all those meanings are extinguished; hence the two of the perfect mutually refute the [other] twos. Further, the [other] twos mutually cultivate the two of the perfect: the heretics and bodhisattvas in the two butters who enter the perfect are such, and the two vehicles at the initial mind also have the meaning of cultivating. In these two pairs, the twos and the perfect differ in superiority and inferiority. The third pair belongs to the perfect's own teaching: the names differ but the essence is the same, hence they are mutually identical, and refuting and cultivating are not spoken of.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "Emptiness refuting neither-existent-nor-nonexistent" on, explaining the marks of the phrases, in three: first, explaining the phrases of mutual refuting, in two: first, emptiness refuting the middle, in two:

初、略示。以理定詮，不遭名惑，豈聞雙非便是實理？蓋能了知凡邪雙非體是見惑，二乘雙非但證偏真，別教雙非既存教道，未與三融。圓空若立，前諸雙非須皆銷殞，故云破也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, a brief showing. Fixing the expression by principle, one is not deceived by names; how, on hearing "double negation," would one take it as the real principle? One can understand that the double negation of ordinary and heretical people is in essence the delusion of views; the double negation of the two vehicles only realizes the one-sided real; the double negation of the distinct teaching still keeps the teaching-path and has not yet fused with the three.
