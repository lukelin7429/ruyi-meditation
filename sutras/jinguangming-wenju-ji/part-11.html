Predictions, Curing Illness, Jalavāhana, and Giving Up the Body

This part covers the first half of the sixth and last fascicle of Zhili's Notes, following Zhiyi's Textual Commentary through the chapters "Predictions," "Curing Illness," "Jalavāhana," and "Giving Up the Body," and into the title of "Praising the Buddha." On "Predictions" Zhili sets out the four kinds of prediction of the Śūraṅgama Samādhi Sūtra, corrects the Commentary's count of the four alternatives for a world's changing name and land, and reads "cultivated in accord with the marks" as the ten modes of contemplation. "Curing Illness" is largely a gloss on Chinese calendar and medical lore: the four and three seasons, the summer retreat and the Kārttika month, the five phases and the five organs, the six bowels. Zhili then argues that the Commentary's contemplation of mind, though it uses the four methods for stilling the mind, belongs to all four teachings, and shows how kindness, impurity, causes and conditions, and counting breaths each cure three levels of illness. On "Jalavāhana" he reads the ten epithets as three sets of three virtues and the twelve links as the three paths, and defends the omission of the three refuges in Dharmakṣema's version. On "Giving Up the Body" he defends giving up the body against critics who cite the Great Treatise, and adds notes on names, stūpa tiers, and rare words. The last three units, on the title of "Praising the Buddha," belong to the portion that, according to the colophon at the end of Part 12, Zhili left unwritten and a disciple completed.

Ru-Yi Translation

First, making clear that the present case is two of the kinds. "Four kinds of prediction": in the Śūraṅgama Samādhi Sūtra the Buddha tells Dṛḍhamati: "Predictions are of four kinds. First, the prediction before arousing the mind. Someone revolving in the six paths is born among humans and delights in the Buddha-dharma; after hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of eons he will arouse the mind; for hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of asaṃkhyeya eons he will practice the bodhisattva path, up to making offerings to buddhas and transforming beings; after so many eons in all he will attain bodhi. Second, giving the prediction just on arousing the mind: this person has planted wholesome roots over long eons, delights in the Great Dharma, and has a mind of kindness and compassion; on arousing the mind he at once dwells in the ground of non-retrogression; hence he is given the prediction on arousing the mind. Third, the secret prediction: there is a bodhisattva who has not yet obtained a prediction and practices the six perfections, his merit complete; the gods, dragons, and the eight classes all think: 'When will this bodhisattva attain bodhi? What will be his eon, his land, and the number of his disciples?' The Buddha cuts off this doubt and gives him a prediction. The whole assembly knows; only this bodhisattva does not know. Fourth, the prediction of acceptance of non-arising: in the great assembly he is given the prediction openly." "This is two of the kinds": that is, the prediction given just on arousing the mind and the prediction of non-arising.

Third, from "Hearing the prediction of their future fruit" on, making clear the people awakened in accord with the sūtra. There is no attaining buddhahood without being complete with the three bodies. The fruit of the three bodies comes from hearing the Golden Light today as the wondrous cause: from the brightness of the dharma-nature an earnest mind arises, which is the cause of the response-body; from the light of the dharma-nature a mind without stain arises, which is the cause of the reward-body; from the gold of the dharma-nature a mind [like] empty space arises, which is the cause of the Dharma-body. This Golden Light and those three virtues are not two and not different. The three virtues are the root, adorned by immeasurable merit. Whether in resemblance or in truth, these three virtues are revealed; therefore the Tathāgata gives the prediction of the fruit. Such are the marks of the benefit of listening to the sūtra.

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, from "Cultivated in accord with the marks" on, pointing to the causes and conditions of present and past. The sūtra's "All have causes and conditions": obtaining a prediction on one hearing — how could there be no causes and conditions? This is the general heading; "they have wondrous wholesome roots" separately heads the distant causes and conditions; "cultivated in accord with the marks" separately heads the present causes and conditions. "Causes and conditions" means resonance and response; or the inner mind and the outer teaching are the cause and condition. "Why?" on brings out the practice of the present causes and conditions. "Cultivated in accord with the true mark": taking the ten modes of contemplation as the practice. The inconceivable object is called the true mark; the object itself is the contemplation, and this contemplation accords with the object; this is called "cultivated in accord with the marks." Without the intent of our school, this phrase cannot be interpreted. Yet in cultivating these ten contemplations there are three capacities: the superior capacity [uses] one contemplation; the middle, two up to seven; the inferior uses all ten. Now the ten thousand sons of the gods, hearing the sūtra in one sitting, perhaps used only one, or were at the second or third; these are faith-practitioners, who cultivate according to the words and enter the stages of resemblance and of the true. "They have wondrous wholesome roots" on brings out the distant causes and conditions. Since the good is called wondrous, it is their hearing the perfect in the past as the seed of the roots; hence it says, "in accord with the true mark they planted wholesome roots."

Chinese Source

二、「由醫」下，釋題。方等經王標除病目，不止除於果報病苦，義合該收惡業煩惱十種之病。故下文云「治諸眾生所有病苦悉令除差。」文意含攝十種行人，故上〈正論品〉疏云「半名世論、半名出世論。」今豈不爾？又荊谿云：「上根即於境種而生於果。」故文云「直聞是言，病即除愈。」為中下根更須後法，是故文云「至長者所，為合眾藥。」故知不獨除果報病。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "because the king of physicians saved [people] from illness" on, explaining the title. The Vaipulya king of sūtras marks the heading "Curing Illness"; it does not stop at curing the illness and suffering of retribution, but in meaning should include all ten kinds of illness of evil karma and afflictions. Hence the text below says, "cured all the sufferings of illness of beings, and all were healed." The intent of the text includes the ten kinds of practitioners; hence the Commentary on "The Right Treatise" above says, "[one] half is the worldly right treatise; [the other] half is called the world-transcending right treatise." Is it not so now as well? Further, Jingxi says, "The superior capacity, right in the seed of the object, produces the fruit." Hence the text says, "merely by hearing these words … the various ailments … were at once cured." For the middle and inferior capacities the later method is further needed; therefore the text says, "they came to the elder's son, [who] gave them … medicines." Hence we know that it does not cure only the illness of retribution.

二、依佛法。一歲三時，以四月為一時也。何故沒秋時耶？「為破」下，凡有二：一、為破常計；二、為開迦提。以秋是收成，物皆結實，易起保著，故不言秋。為開安居者，為後安居人續結，令成為前安居人，開迦提月也。律中有三種安居，謂前、中、後也。四月十六日是前安居，十七以去至五月十五日名中安居，五月十六是後安居。若四月十六日結者，至七月十五日夜分盡名夏竟。至明相出十六日後，至八月十五日以來，名迦提月。《明了論》云「本言迦絺羅，為存略故，但云迦提。此翻功德衣。以前安居人坐夏有功，五利賞德，律中受此衣故。一畜長財、二離衣宿、三背請、四別眾食、五食前後至他家。」《西域記》以迦提翻昴星，以昴星直此月故。於昴星月中得受功德衣故，是知若不沒秋中後安居，不名坐夏，以後安居人至八月十五方解故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, according to the Buddha's Dharma. A year has three seasons, taking four months as one season. Why is the autumn season omitted? "To break" on: there are two [reasons]: first, to break the reckoning of permanence; second, to open the Kārttika [month]. Because autumn is the harvest, when things all bear fruit, holding on and attachment easily arise; hence autumn is not mentioned. "To open the summer retreat": for those of the later retreat, continuing to bind [the retreat] lets it be completed; for those of the earlier retreat, the Kārttika month is opened. In the Vinaya there are three kinds of retreat, namely the earlier, the middle, and the later. The sixteenth of the fourth month is the earlier retreat; from the seventeenth until the fifteenth of the fifth month is called the middle retreat; the sixteenth of the fifth month is the later retreat. If one binds [the retreat] on the sixteenth of the fourth month, when the night of the fifteenth of the seventh month is over, it is called the end of summer. From the appearance of dawn on the sixteenth onward, up to the fifteenth of the eighth month, is called the Kārttika month. Tree spirit, good woman, today you have spoken these words well." Though the sūtra praises only the tree spirit, the intent includes the earlier two; hence it says that the three sets are the praised. "The three sets are buddhas-to-be" and so on resolves a doubt. It was feared people would doubt, saying: since the praise includes praiser and praised, why is it titled only "Praising the Buddha"? Hence it explains that "buddha" extends to present and future; all are praising buddhas.

Reading Notes for Part 11

Translator's notes

Scope. Fascicle 6, Part One of the Notes (T1786, 149c–155a) covers Chapters 14–17 of the sūtra and the title of Chapter 18 ("Praising the Buddha"). Lemmas reuse `jinguangming-wenju/p6_body.md`; sūtra phrases reuse `jinguangming-jing/p3_body.md` (Chapters 14–15) and `p4_body.md` (Chapters 16–17). Where Zhili abridges or rewords the sūtra (units 24, 76), the English follows our sūtra English with ellipses.

Four kinds of prediction (unit 3). Zhili's quotation is a free summary of the Śūraṅgama Samādhi Sūtra (T642), which names the four as 未發心而與授記, 適發心而與授記, 密授記, 得無生法忍現前授記; the interlocutor 堅意 is Dṛḍhamati.

"It should say two" (unit 9). The Commentary says the Pure Banner passage shows "one" of the four alternatives. Zhili finds two in the sūtra passage: the first (name changes, land does not: Pure Banner) and the fourth (neither changes: Silver Light's world "as before, no different"), so he proposes 二 for 一. Our Commentary English "this is one of them" is kept inside the quotation.

Sources located (vault CBETA). The Invitation to Avalokiteśvara (T1043) on counting breaths and abiding in the Śūraṅgama samādhi (unit 70); its verse reads 淨於三毒根 where Zhili quotes 斷除三毒根 (unit 67), and the English follows our translation of T1043 with "severs." The Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra line "The cause of the Great Vehicle is the true mark of all dharmas" reuses `puxian-guan-jing`; the parallel "fruit" sentence was not found in our draft and is translated directly (unit 88). The stūpa tiers (unit 117) also appear in the Fanyi mingyi ji (T2131).

Sources not located. Jingxi's "The superior capacity, right in the seed of the object, produces the fruit" (unit 24); the Great Treatise's "Giving up the body is easy; giving up the mind is hard" (unit 97); the Commentary sentence "Citing the past giving up of the body …" (unit 97), which is not in our text of the Commentary; Feng Junda (unit 51); the Clear Understanding Treatise on kaṭhina (unit 35, probably T1461); the Record of the Western Regions on the Pleiades; the Twelve Links Sūtra and the cremation chapter of the Latter Part of the Nirvāṇa (unit 117). The Chinese classics (Classic of Filial Piety, Shuowen, Guangya, Book of Rites, Zhouyi, Shiming, Baihu tong, Yellow Emperor's Classic of the Pulse, Sancang) are not in the vault; source not checked.

Medical vocabulary. 藏 organ, 府 bowel, 三焦 the triple burner; wind / heat / phlegm (lung) / the balanced illness, and the seasons, follow our sūtra and Commentary English. 陽遁／陰遁 (unit 36) is rendered "the yang course / the yin course."

Three by three (units 39–41). 三三本攝 keeps the Commentary's "the three-by-three is the basic grouping"; Zhili's own analysis speaks of "the three threes" as the basis and "the three ones" as the absorbed months.

The four contemplations (unit 70). Each of the four methods for stilling the mind cures a delusion of ordinary beings, of the two vehicles, and of bodhisattvas. "The meaning of the excellent and the distinct in Calming and Contemplation" (勝別意) is literal; it may refer to the Great Master's distinction between the general and the particular use of the counteracting methods. Alternatively: "the specially excellent intent of Calming and Contemplation."

Ten epithets (unit 88). Zhili groups the epithets into three triads for the Dharma-body, liberation, and prajñā, with "World-Honored One" as the general conclusion. "Unsurpassed Being" and "Tamer of Those to be Tamed" are treated as one head; 丈夫 ("man") inside 調御丈夫 is glossed in parentheses because our sūtra English does not render it.

Punctuation choices. Unit 21: 此乃總標，有妙善根別標遠因緣 (the general heading, then "wondrous wholesome roots" heads the distant conditions). Unit 73: 十二因緣經稱甚深 is read "the twelve links the sūtra calls 'very deep'"; alternatively, "the Twelve Links Sūtra calls it very deep" (the same title is cited in unit 117). Unit 99: 小人即國病者，小蟲一萬枯魚 and 獲成佛記，例病差者….

Copying variants. Unit 31 writes 髦 where the sūtra has 耄 (both "decrepit"). Unit 51 "bitter vegetables" (苦菜) is not in our Commentary text. Unit 101's lemma 「就本」 points to the Commentary's "The original household is five," inside the section that begins "In a past age there was a king."

Names. 鹿野 (unit 116) is given as Mṛgajā; 拘利太子 as Prince Kulika; 胡豆 is rendered literally "Hu Bean," with 菉豆 as mung beans. 慈恩 is Ci'en (Kuiji).

Authorship of units 121–123. The colophon at the end of fascicle 6 (Part 12, unit 76) says Zhili died after finishing seventeen chapters and that a disciple compiled the comments on "Praising the Buddha" from what he had heard. Units 121–123 (the title of Chapter 18) therefore belong to the disciple's portion; the disciple is not named in the text.
