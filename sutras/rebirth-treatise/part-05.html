The Bodhisattvas' Four Adornments

The fourth gate, contemplation, closes with its third and final object: not the land, not its lord, but the bodhisattvas who dwell there — described entirely in terms of how they act in every other world without ever once leaving their own. This closes the third of Tanluan's ten movements, "the substance of contemplative practice," begun back in Part 3.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: Part 5 of 7 complete Source: Taishō T1524, tr. Bodhiruci, Northern Wei Reading notes after Tanluan's 往生論註 (T1819)

Four Ways of Acting Without Leaving

Chinese Source

云何觀菩薩功德莊嚴成就？觀菩薩功德莊嚴成就者，觀彼菩薩有四種正修行功德成就應知。何等為四？

一者於一佛土身不動搖，而遍十方種種應化，如實修行常作佛事。偈言「安樂國清淨，常轉無垢輪，化佛菩薩日，如須彌住持」故，開諸眾生淤泥華故。

二者彼應化身，一切時不前不後，一心一念放大光明，悉能遍至十方世界教化眾生，種種方便修行所作，滅除一切眾生苦故。偈言「無垢莊嚴光，一念及一時，普照諸佛會，利益諸群生」故。

三者彼於一切世界無餘，照諸佛會大眾無餘，廣大無量供養恭敬讚歎諸佛如來。偈言「雨天樂華衣，妙香等供養，讚佛諸功德，無有分別心」故。

四者彼於十方一切世界無三寶處，住持莊嚴佛法僧寶功德大海，遍示令解如實修行。偈言「何等世界無，佛法功德寶，我皆願往生，示佛法如佛」故。

Ru-Yi Translation

How does one contemplate the bodhisattvas' own merit and adornment? The accomplishment of the bodhisattvas' merit and adornment lies in contemplating that those bodhisattvas possess four kinds of true, accomplished practice, which should be known. What are the four?

First, that while their bodies never stir from a single buddha-land, they nonetheless manifest in every kind of responsive transformation throughout the ten directions, truly and constantly carrying out the Buddha's own work. This is what the verse means by "the Land of Peace and Bliss, pure and undefiled, constantly turns the stainless wheel of the Dharma; its transformation-buddhas and bodhisattva-suns sustain it, steadfast as Sumeru" — opening the lotus flower of every sentient being still mired in mud.

Second, that their responsive, transformed bodies, at every time, neither before nor after, in a single mind and a single thought, emit a great light, able to reach every world of the ten directions at once to teach and transform sentient beings — practicing every kind of skillful means in order to extinguish the suffering of every being. This is what the verse means by "its stainless, adorning light, in a single thought and a single moment, universally illuminates every assembly of every buddha, benefiting every kind of being."

Third, that they leave not a single world unreached, leave not a single assembly of any buddha unilluminated, offering, in vast and measureless abundance, reverent worship and praise to every Tathāgata. This is what the verse means by "a rain of heavenly music, flowers, and robes, wondrous fragrances offered equally to all, praising every merit of every buddha, with a mind entirely free of discrimination."

Fourth, that in every world of the ten directions where the Three Jewels are absent, they sustain and adorn the great treasure-sea of merit that is the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha, showing it forth everywhere so that beings may understand and truly practice it. This is what the verse means by "in whatever world there is none of this treasure of the Buddha-dharma's merit, I vow to be reborn there too, and to show forth the Buddha-dharma, exactly as a buddha would."

Reading note — after Tanluan

Tanluan opens this section with a single sentence that frames all four adornments at once: "suchness is the true substance of every dharma; to act in accordance with suchness is itself, in a sense, not to act at all — and this acting-through-not-acting is what is meant by true and real practice. The substance is one and the same suchness throughout, though its meaning here divides into four; it is by this one substance that the four are unified." Whatever these bodhisattvas appear to be doing across the ten directions, in other words, none of it is separate activity layered on top of realization — it simply is what realization looks like from the outside.

On the first adornment — bodies that never stir, yet act everywhere — Tanluan explains that bodhisattvas of the eighth ground and above dwell constantly in samādhi, and it is this very stillness, not any exertion, that lets them reach every direction "the way the sun stands in the sky and yet its reflection appears in every river at once — does the sun itself come to the river? Does it not come?" He glosses the closing phrase, "opening the lotus flower of every sentient being still mired in mud," with a canonical image: "lotus flowers do not grow on high, dry ground; they grow only in low, damp mud." A sentient being caught in affliction is exactly this mud, and a bodhisattva's teaching is exactly what a lotus needs that mud in order to open at all — the affliction is not merely tolerated on the way to enlightenment, but is the very medium enlightenment grows out of.

On the second adornment, Tanluan catches a distinction easy to read past: the first adornment already established that these bodies act everywhere without moving — but "without moving" could still permit acting in one place slightly before another. The second adornment closes that gap specifically: "in a single thought and a single moment," with no sequence of before and after at all. What sounded like the same claim twice is, on his reading, two separate claims — first that no travel is involved, and second that no time is involved either.

On the third adornment, Tanluan reaches outside the Pure Land corpus entirely, quoting a line from Sengzhao's Zhao Lun — the celebrated early-5th-century treatise by Kumārajīva's own disciple: "the Dharma-body has no fixed image, and yet it responds in every differentiated form; its ultimate tone speaks no words, and yet its hidden teaching spreads everywhere; its unseen skillful means makes no deliberate plan, and yet its every movement coincides exactly with what each occasion requires." This, Tanluan says, is exactly the sense in which these bodhisattvas manage to reach every buddha's assembly in every world at once, with nothing that could be called effort involved. And on the fourth — reaching specifically the worlds where no Buddha, Dharma, or Sangha exists at all, not merely the worlds already blessed with them — he adds only a brief but pointed remark: without this fourth vow, "the Dharma-body would have something it fails to reach as Dharma, and the highest good would have something it fails to make good." Universality, on this reading, is not proven by how far a teaching spreads among the already-receptive; it is proven only by whether it reaches where nothing has ever been planted at all.

Part 5 Complete

This closes Part 5, and with it Tanluan's third movement, "the substance of contemplative practice" — the land's seventeen adornments, the Buddha's eight, and now the bodhisattvas' four, twenty-nine adornments in total. Part 6 turns to what the treatise does with all twenty-nine at once: folding them into a single phrase, and setting out what a bodhisattva must leave behind, and take up in its place, to enter the gate of bodhi at all.
