The Five Profound Meanings and the Preface Section

This commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra (T1750) is traditionally attributed to Zhiyi, the founder of the Tiantai school; some modern scholars question this attribution and date the work later, and it is presented here under its traditional ascription without taking a position. Part 1 covers the opening of the commentary. A short preface in parallel prose sets the pure land against this world of suffering and names the sixteen contemplations and the three kinds of pure karma. The commentary then states its thesis in one line — the contemplation of mind is the sūtra's cardinal doctrine and the true mark is its essence — and works through the five profound meanings. Under the name, it explains "Buddha" through the six identities, "contemplation" through the sequential and the single-mind threefold contemplation, and "Immeasurable Life" through three bodies, three lives, and three measures. Under the cardinal doctrine it sets out the four lands, each with pure and defiled forms. It then classes the sūtra as a sudden Mahāyāna teaching, divides the text, and comments on the sūtra's preface: the six phrases of attestation and the story of Ajātaśatru, Bimbisāra, and Vaidehī, ending with her request to be taught "a place of pure karma." This text is the base on which Zhili later wrote his Miaozong chao.

Commentary on the Sūtra on the Contemplation of Amitāyus Buddha, Spoken by the Buddha (with Preface)

天台智者大師說

Ru-Yi Translation

Spoken by the Great Master Zhizhe of Tiantai

Chinese Source

夫樂邦之與苦域、金寶之與泥沙、胎獄之望華池、棘林之比瓊樹，誠由心分垢淨，見兩土之升沈；行開善惡，覩二方之麁妙。喻形端則影直、源濁則流昏，故知欲生極樂國土，必修十六妙觀；願見彌陀世尊，要行三種淨業。然化因事漸、教藉緣興，是以闍王殺逆、韋提哀請，大聖垂慈，乘機演法，曜玉相而流彩、聳珍臺而顯瑞，雖廣示珍域而宗歸安養，使末俗有緣遵斯妙觀。落日懸鼓，用標送想之方；大水結氷，實表瑠璃之地。風吟寶葉，共天樂而同繁；波動金渠，將契經而合響；觀肉髻而瞻侍者，念毫相而覩如來。及其瞑目告終，上珍臺而高踊，文成印壞，坐金蓮而化生。隨三輩而橫截，越五苦而長騖，可謂微行妙觀至道要術者哉。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, the land of bliss and the realm of suffering, gold and jewels and mud and sand, the womb-prison set against the flower-ponds, the thorny forest compared with the jade trees: truly it is because the mind divides into defiled and pure that one sees the rising and sinking of the two lands, and because practice opens into good and evil that one beholds the coarseness and wondrousness of the two regions. It is as when the form is upright the shadow is straight, and when the source is muddy the stream is dark. Hence we know that whoever wishes to be born in the land of Sukhāvatī must cultivate the sixteen wondrous contemplations, and whoever vows to see Amitābha, the World-Honored One, must practice the three kinds of pure karma. Yet transformation proceeds gradually by way of events, and the teaching arises in dependence on conditions. Thus King Ajātaśatru committed the heinous act of killing and Vaidehī made her sorrowful plea; the great sage let fall his compassion and, availing himself of the occasion, expounded the Dharma. He made his jade-like marks shine and pour forth color, raised up the jeweled platform and displayed auspicious signs; though he showed the jeweled realms widely, the cardinal point returns to [the land of] Peaceful Nourishment, so that people of the latter age who have the connection may follow these wondrous contemplations. The setting sun hanging like a drum serves to mark the method of sending forth thought; the great water freezing into ice truly represents the ground of lapis lazuli. The wind sings in the jeweled leaves, as rich as the music of the heavens; the waves stir in the golden channels, their sound in harmony with the scriptures. One contemplates the fleshy crown-protuberance and gazes upon the attendants; one is mindful of the mark of the tuft and beholds the Tathāgata. And when one closes one's eyes at the end of life, one mounts the jeweled platform and leaps on high; when the pattern is formed and the seal is broken, one sits on a golden lotus and is born by transformation. Following the three classes, one cuts across horizontally; passing beyond the five sufferings, one races on forever. These may well be called subtle practices and wondrous contemplations, the essential art of the ultimate way.

The Title and the Five Meanings

Chinese Source

此經心觀為宗、實相為體。所言「佛說觀無量壽佛」者，佛是所觀勝境，舉正報以收依果、述化主以包徒眾。觀雖十六，言佛便周，故云佛說觀無量壽佛。「經」者，訓法、訓常。由聖人金口，故言經也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This sūtra takes the contemplation of mind as its cardinal doctrine and the true mark as its essence. As for the words "The Buddha Speaks of the Contemplation of Amitāyus Buddha": the Buddha is the excellent object contemplated; [the title] raises the principal reward so as to include the dependent fruit, and names the lord of the teaching so as to encompass the assembly of followers. Though the contemplations are sixteen, saying "Buddha" covers them all; hence it says "The Buddha Speaks of the Contemplation of Amitāyus Buddha." "Sūtra" (經) is glossed as "standard" (法) and glossed as "constant" (常). Because it comes from the golden mouth of the sage, it is called a sūtra.

Chinese Source

釋經五義：名、體、宗、用、教相（云云）。第一釋名者，一切眾經皆有通、別二名，通則經之一字，別則有七：或單人、法、譬，或複、或具。今經從能說所說人以立名，即教別同名為經，即教通為行不同。從一乃至無量即行別，同會常樂即行通。理雖無名，將門名理，理隨於門，四四十六，即名理別；門隨於理，即名理通。此約一化以明通別。更約一題，佛說即教，觀即是行，無量壽佛即是理。教行理足，任運有通別意。更就一字說者，釋論云「所行如所說」，說即是教，如即是理，行即是行。佛即法身，觀即般若，無量壽即解脫。當知即一達三、即三達一，一中解無量、無量中解一。於一字尚達無量義，況諸字、況一題、況一經、一切經耶！故經云「若聞首題名字，所得功德不可限量。」若不如上解者，安獲無限功德耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the sūtra by five meanings: name, essence, cardinal doctrine, function, and the characteristics of the teaching (and so on). First, Explaining the Name. All sūtras have two kinds of name, common and particular. The common [name] is the one word "sūtra"; the particular [names] are of seven kinds: [named by] person, dharma, or simile singly, or compound, or complete. The present sūtra establishes its name from the persons who speak and who are spoken of: this is the teaching being particular. Being alike named "sūtra" is the teaching being common. Because practices are not the same, from one up to the immeasurable, this is practice being particular; that they alike converge on the eternal and blissful is practice being common. Although principle has no name, the gates are used to name the principle. When principle follows the gates, four times four makes sixteen: this is called principle being particular. When the gates follow principle, this is called principle being common. This explains common and particular in terms of the entire teaching career. Further, in terms of the single title: "the Buddha speaks" is the teaching, "contemplation" is the practice, and "Amitāyus Buddha" is the principle. Teaching, practice, and principle being complete, the meaning of common and particular is present of itself. Further, speaking in terms of a single word: the Great Treatise says, "What is practiced is as what is spoken." "Spoken" is the teaching, "as" (如) is the principle, "practiced" is the practice. "Buddha" is the Dharma-body, "contemplation" is prajñā, "Amitāyus" is liberation. You should know that being identical to the one one penetrates the three, being identical to the three one penetrates the one; in the one one understands the immeasurable, in the immeasurable one understands the one. If even in a single word one penetrates immeasurable meanings, how much more in all the words, in a single title, in a single sūtra, in all sūtras! Hence the sūtra says, "If one hears the name of its title, the merit obtained cannot be measured." If one does not understand as above, how could one obtain limitless merit?

Chinese Source

初釋佛者，佛是覺義，有六種，即《涅槃經》云「一切眾生即是佛，如貧女舍寶眾物具存、力士額珠圓明頓在」。《如來藏經》舉十喻，弊帛裹黃金、土模內像、闇室瓶盆、井中七寶，本自有之，非適今也。《淨名》云「一切眾生皆如也」。《寶篋》云「佛界眾生界，一界無別界」。此是圓智圓覺諸法，遍一切處無不明了。雖五無間，皆解脫相；雖昏盲倒惑，其理存焉。斯理灼然，世間常住，有佛不能益、無佛不能損，得之不為高、失之不為下，故言眾生即是佛，理佛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining "Buddha." "Buddha" means awakening, and it is of six kinds. That is, the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says, "All beings are buddha, just as in the poor woman's house the treasure and all things are present, and the bead on the strong man's forehead is round, bright, and wholly there." The Tathāgatagarbha Sūtra gives ten similes: gold wrapped in tattered cloth, an image within a clay mold, jars and basins in a dark room, the seven treasures in a well — they have been present from the beginning, not just now. The Vimalakīrti says, "Every being is suchness." The Precious Casket says, "The buddha-realm and the realm of beings: one realm, with no separate realm." This is perfect wisdom perfectly awakened to all dharmas, pervading all places, nowhere not clear. Though [beings commit] the five uninterrupted [offenses], all are the mark of liberation; though dull, blind, inverted, and deluded, the principle is present in them. This principle is evident, eternally abiding in the world: the presence of a buddha cannot add to it, the absence of a buddha cannot diminish it; obtaining it is not higher, losing it is not lower. Hence it is said that beings are buddha: this is the buddha in principle.

Chinese Source

如斯之理，佛若不說，無能知者。《法華》云「一百八十劫，空過無有佛。世尊未出時，十方常闇瞑。」《涅槃》云「於無量世，亦不聞有如來出世、大乘經名。若佛出世，方能闡智慧日、識三寶之光明、開甘露門、知十號之妙味。」因說生解、於寶適悅，故須達聞名身毛皆竪、昏夜大朗巨關自闢。此名字佛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Such a principle, if the Buddha did not speak of it, no one could know. The Lotus says, "A hundred and eighty kalpas passed empty, with no buddha in the world. While the World-Honored One had not yet appeared, the ten directions were always dark." The Nirvāṇa says, "For immeasurable ages one did not even hear that a tathāgata had appeared in the world, or the names of the Mahāyāna sūtras. When a buddha appears in the world, only then can the sun of wisdom be made known, the light of the Three Jewels be recognized, the gate of sweet dew be opened, and the wondrous flavor of the ten epithets be known." Understanding arises from the teaching, and there is delight in the treasure; thus Sudatta, on hearing the name, felt the hair on his body stand on end, the dark night grew bright, and the great gate opened of itself. This is the buddha in name.

Chinese Source

觀行佛者，觀佛相好如鑄金像，心緣妙色與眼作對，開眼閉目、若明若闇常得不離。見佛世尊從大相海流出小相，浩浩瀁瀁如大劫水，周眸遍覽無非佛界。念一佛與十方佛等，念現在佛與三世佛等，一身一智慧，力無畏亦然。念色身、念法門、念實相，常運念無不念時，念念皆覺。是名觀行佛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The buddha in contemplative practice: one contemplates the Buddha's marks and features as if [beholding] an image cast in gold; the mind takes the wondrous form as its object, set face to face with the eye; with eyes open or closed, in brightness or in darkness, one is never apart from it. One sees the World-Honored Buddha, from whose ocean of great marks flow forth the minor marks, vast and surging like the waters of a great eon; wherever the eye turns and looks all around, there is nothing that is not the buddha-realm. One is mindful of one buddha as equal to the buddhas of the ten directions, mindful of the present buddha as equal to the buddhas of the three times: one body, one wisdom, and so too the powers and fearlessnesses. One is mindful of the form-body, mindful of the Dharma-gates, mindful of the true mark; constantly keeping mindfulness at work, there is no time without mindfulness, and in thought after thought there is awakening. This is called the buddha in contemplative practice.

Chinese Source

相似佛者，念佛相好身，得相似相應；念佛法門身，得相似相應；念實相身，得相似相應。相似者，二物相類，如鍮似金、若瓜比瓠，猶火先煖、涉海初平。水性至冷，飲者乃知。渴不掘井，聽說何為？略舉其要，如《法華》中六根清淨，即是其相。名相似佛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The buddha in resemblance: being mindful of the Buddha's body of marks and features, one attains a resembling accord; being mindful of the Buddha's body of Dharma-gates, one attains a resembling accord; being mindful of the body of the true mark, one attains a resembling accord. "Resembling" means that two things are of a kind, as brass resembles gold, or as a melon is compared to a gourd; it is like the warmth that comes before the fire, or the ground first leveling out on the way to the sea. The water's nature is utterly cold, but only the one who drinks it knows. If, being thirsty, you do not dig a well, what is the use of hearing it described? To give its essentials briefly: the purity of the six faculties in the Lotus is its mark. This is called the buddha in resemblance.

分證佛者，初發心住，一發一切發，發一切功德、發一切智慧、發一切境界，不前不後亦不一時，三智一心中得。得如來妙色身，湛然應一切，開祕密藏，以不住法即住其中。以普現色身作眾色像，一音隨類報答諸聲，不動真際群情等悅。應以三輪度者，能八相成道具佛威儀，以佛音聲方便而度脫之，況九法界三輪耶。初住尚爾，況等覺耶。是名分證佛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The buddha in a portion of the truth: at the first abiding, the arousal of the mind, when one arises all arise — all merits arise, all wisdoms arise, all objects arise — not before, not after, and not at one time; the three wisdoms are attained in a single mind. One attains the Tathāgata's wondrous form-body, serene yet responding to all; one opens the secret treasury, and by the dharma of non-abiding abides within it. With the universally manifesting form-body one makes all forms and images; with a single voice one answers all sounds according to their kinds; without moving from the true limit, the multitudes of beings are equally gladdened. For those who should be delivered through the three wheels, one is able, through the eight phases, to attain the path endowed with a buddha's dignified bearing, and delivers them by a buddha's voice and expedients — how much more [is this so] of the three wheels of the nine dharma-realms! If even the first abiding is so, how much more virtual awakening! This is called the buddha in a portion of the truth.

Chinese Source

究竟佛者，道窮妙覺位，極於茶故，唯佛與佛乃能究盡諸法實相，邊際智滿、種覺頓圓。無上士者，名無所斷。無上士者，更無過者。如十五日月圓滿具足，眾星中王，最上最勝威德特尊。是名究竟佛義。佛有無量德，應有無量號，舉一蔽諸，華嚴有十萬號。又經有萬號。三世諸佛通有十號，《淨名》三號以劫壽說，不能令盡，何況諸號耶。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ultimate buddha: the path is exhausted at the stage of wondrous awakening, reaching to the [letter] ḍha; hence only a buddha together with a buddha can fathom the true mark of all dharmas to its very limit. The wisdom of the farthest limit is full, the awakening of [all] modes is suddenly perfect. "Unsurpassed Being" means "nothing [left] to be cut off." "Unsurpassed Being" means "none surpasses him." He is like the moon on the fifteenth day, round, full, and complete, king among the stars, highest, most excellent, his majestic virtue singularly honored. This is called the meaning of the ultimate buddha. The Buddha has immeasurable virtues and so should have immeasurable epithets; one is raised to cover all the rest. The Avataṃsaka has a hundred thousand epithets; another sūtra has ten thousand epithets. The buddhas of the three times all share the ten epithets. The Vimalakīrti [says that even] three epithets, explained for a lifespan of an eon, could not be exhausted — how much less all the epithets!

"Speaks" and "Contemplation"

Chinese Source

說者，悅所懷也，即十二部經八萬法藏，六度四等一切法門。又於一法中作四門分別，於一一門巧作四悉檀利益，聞者歡喜讚用受行，信戒進念而得開發，貪恚愚癡豁爾氷消，革凡成聖入法流水。或三二一益，若都無益則樂默然。若一機扣，聖於一門施四益者，餘三門亦如是。為一緣說一法既爾，諸緣諸法亦如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Speaks" (說) is delighting (悅) in what is held in the heart: that is, the twelve divisions of the scriptures, the eighty thousand Dharma-treasuries, the six perfections, the four immeasurable minds, and all Dharma-gates. Further, within a single dharma one distinguishes four gates, and within each gate one skillfully applies the benefits of the four siddhāntas, so that those who hear rejoice, praise, make use of it, receive it, and practice it; faith, precepts, vigor, and mindfulness are opened up, greed, anger, and delusion melt away like ice, the ordinary is transformed into the sage, and one enters the stream of the Dharma. Or there are three, two, or one benefit; if there is no benefit at all, then he prefers to remain silent. If a single capacity knocks and the sage bestows the four benefits through one gate, the other three gates are likewise. As it is when speaking one dharma for one condition, so it is for all conditions and all dharmas.

Chinese Source

觀者觀也，有次第三觀。一心中三觀，從假入空觀亦名二諦觀，從空入假觀亦名平等觀，二空觀為方便得入中道第一義諦觀，心心寂滅，自然流入薩婆若海。此名出《瓔珞經》。今釋其意，假是虛妄，俗諦也。空是審實，真諦也。今欲去俗歸真，故言從假入空觀。假是入空之詮，先須觀假，知假虛妄而得會真，故言二諦觀。此觀若成，即證一切智也。從空入假觀者，若住於空，與二乘何異？不成佛法、不益眾生。是故觀空不住於空而入於假，知病識藥、應病授藥令得服行，故名從空入假觀。而言平等者，望前稱平等。前破假用空，今破空用假，破用既均，故言平等觀。此觀成時，證道種智。二空為方便者，初觀空生死、次觀空涅槃，此之二空為雙遮之方便。初觀用空、次觀用假，此之二用為雙照之方便。心心歸趣入薩婆若海，雙照二諦也。此觀成時，證一切種智。是為次第三觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Contemplation" (觀) is contemplating. There is the sequential threefold contemplation and the threefold contemplation in a single mind. The contemplation of entering emptiness from the provisional is also called the contemplation of the two truths; the contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness is also called the contemplation of equality; the two contemplations of emptiness serve as expedients for entering the contemplation of the middle way, the truth of the first principle; mind after mind is tranquil and extinct, and one flows naturally into the ocean of sarvajña. These names come from the Jeweled Necklace Sūtra. Now to explain their meaning: the provisional is empty and false — the conventional truth. Emptiness is examined and real — the truth of the real. Now one wishes to leave the conventional and return to the real; hence it is called the contemplation of entering emptiness from the provisional. The provisional is the expression [through which one] enters emptiness: one must first contemplate the provisional, know the provisional to be empty and false, and so meet with the real; hence it is called the contemplation of the two truths. When this contemplation is accomplished, one realizes the wisdom that knows all things. As for the contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness: if one abides in emptiness, how is one different from the two vehicles? One does not accomplish the Buddha-dharma and does not benefit beings. Therefore one contemplates emptiness without abiding in emptiness and enters the provisional, knowing the illness and recognizing the medicine, giving medicine in response to the illness so that [beings] may take it and practice; hence it is called the contemplation of entering the provisional from emptiness. It is called "equality" in comparison with the former. Before, one broke the provisional and used emptiness; now one breaks emptiness and uses the provisional. Breaking and using being balanced, it is called the contemplation of equality. When this contemplation is accomplished, one realizes the wisdom of the paths. As for "the two emptinesses serving as expedients": first one contemplates birth-and-death as empty, next one contemplates nirvāṇa as empty; these two emptinesses are the expedient for negating both. First the contemplation uses emptiness, next the contemplation uses the provisional; these two uses are the expedient for illuminating both. Mind after mind returns and enters the ocean of sarvajña, illuminating both truths. When this contemplation is accomplished, one realizes the wisdom knowing all modes. This is the sequential threefold contemplation.

Chinese Source

一心三觀者，此出《釋論》。論云「三智實在一心中得」，秖一觀而三觀，觀於一諦而三諦，故名一心三觀。類如一心而有生住滅，如此三相在一心中。此觀成時，證一心三智，亦名一切種智。寂滅相種種行類相貌皆知也。寂滅相者，是雙亡之力。種種相貌皆知者，雙照之力也。《中論》云「因緣所生法，即空即假即中」，《釋論》云「三智實在一心中得」，即此意也。此觀微妙，即一而三、即三而一，一觀一切觀、一切觀一觀，非一非一切，如此之觀攝一切觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The threefold contemplation in a single mind comes from the Great Treatise. The treatise says, "The three wisdoms are actually attained in a single mind." Just one contemplation is three contemplations; contemplating one truth is [contemplating] three truths; hence it is called the threefold contemplation in a single mind. It is comparable to a single mind having arising, abiding, and ceasing: these three marks are within one mind. When this contemplation is accomplished, one realizes the three wisdoms in a single mind, also called the wisdom knowing all modes: the mark of tranquil extinction and the various features of practices and kinds are all known. "The mark of tranquil extinction" is the power of both vanishing; "the various features are all known" is the power of illuminating both. The Middle Treatise says, "Dharmas produced by causes and conditions are identical to the empty, identical to the provisional, identical to the middle"; the Great Treatise says, "The three wisdoms are actually attained in a single mind." This is the meaning. This contemplation is subtle and wondrous: identical to the one yet three, identical to the three yet one; one contemplation is all contemplations, all contemplations are one contemplation, neither one nor all. Such a contemplation encompasses all contemplations.

"Immeasurable Life": Three Bodies, Three Lives, Three Measures

Chinese Source

無量壽者，天竺稱阿彌陀佛，本無身無壽亦無於量，隨順世間而論三身，亦隨順世間而論三壽，亦隨順世間而論三量。法身者，師軌法性，還以法性為身。此身非色質亦非心智，非陰界入之所攝持，強指法性為法身耳。法性壽者，非報得命根，亦無連持，強指不遷不變名之為壽。此壽非長量亦非短量，無延無促，強指法界同虛空量。此即非身之身，無壽之壽，不量之量也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Immeasurable Life" (無量壽): in India he is called Amitābha Buddha. Originally there is no body, no life, and no measure either; following the world, one speaks of three bodies; also following the world, one speaks of three lives; also following the world, one speaks of three measures. The Dharma-body takes dharma-nature as its teacher and norm, and in turn takes dharma-nature as its body. This body is neither form-substance nor mind-wisdom, not held within the aggregates, realms, and entrances; dharma-nature is merely designated, for want of a better word, as the Dharma-body. The life of dharma-nature is not a life-faculty obtained as reward, nor does it have continuity; what neither shifts nor changes is merely designated "life." This life is neither a long measure nor a short measure, neither extended nor shortened; the dharma-realm, equal to empty space, is merely designated its measure. This is the body that is no body, the life that is no life, the measure that is no measure.

Ru-Yi Translation

The reward-body is what is brought about by cultivation and practice. The Lotus says, "attained through long cultivation of karma"; the Nirvāṇa says, "because the great parinirvāṇa is attained by cultivating the path." The wisdom of suchness illuminates the object of suchness; the wisdom of bodhi accords with and merges with dharma-nature. "Accords" is like a box and its lid according; "merges" is like water and milk merging. The Dharma-body is neither body nor not body; since the wisdom accords and merges [with it], it too is neither body nor not body; this wisdom is designated the reward-body. The Dharma-life is neither life nor not life; since the wisdom accords and merges, it too is neither life nor not life; what is not life is designated life. The Dharma-measure is neither measure nor without measure; since the wisdom accords and merges, it too is neither measure nor without measure; the immeasurable is designated measure.

Ru-Yi Translation

The response-body: responding in conformity with the myriad things is its body; responding in conformity with continuity is its life; responding in conformity with long and short is its measure. Wisdom merging with the essence can give rise to great function, as mercury mixed with real gold can coat all images. Merit joined with the Dharma-body responds and appears, going everywhere: it can be body or not body, can be eternal life or impermanent life, can be immeasurable or measured. "Measured" has two meanings: first, the measure of the immeasurable; second, the measure of the measured. Seven hundred asaṃkhyeyas, eighty [years], and the like are the measure of the measured. That Amitābha truly has a fixed limit which humans and devas cannot count is the immeasurable of the measured. Response-buddhas all have both measures, following beings and according with conditions, uneven, long or short. However, these three bodies and three lives cannot be [taken as] together or separate, one or different — that would go against the essence of the Dharma; identical to the one yet three, identical to the three yet one — only so does it accord with the profound text. The Explanation of the Name is finished.

Clarifying the Essence

Chinese Source

次辯體者，體是主質。《釋論》云「除諸法實相，餘皆魔事」。大乘經以實相為印，為經正體，無量功德共莊嚴之，種種眾行而歸趣之，言說問答而詮辯之，譬眾星之環北辰、如萬流之宗東海，故以實相為經體也。書家解禮者，訓體也。體有尊卑長幼，君父之體尊、臣子之體賤。當知體禮之釋，是貴極之法也。復次體是底也，窮源極底、理盡淵府、究暢實際，乃名為底。《釋論》云「智度大海，唯佛窮底」，故以底釋體也。復次體是達義，得此體意通達無壅，如風行空中自在無障礙，一切異名別說皆與實相不相違背。《釋論》云「般若是一法，佛說種種名」，故以體達釋經體也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, Clarifying the Essence. The essence (體) is the principal substance. The Great Treatise says, "Apart from the true mark of all dharmas, everything else is the work of Māra." The Mahāyāna sūtras take the true mark as their seal and as the sūtra's proper essence: immeasurable merits together adorn it, all kinds of practices converge on it, and words, questions, and answers express and discuss it — like the stars circling the North Star, like the ten thousand streams paying homage to the Eastern Sea. Therefore the true mark is taken as the essence of the sūtra. Literary scholars explain "rites" (禮) as glossed by "body" (體). Bodies are honored or humble, elder or younger: the body of lord and father is honored, the body of minister and son is lowly. One should know that explaining 體 by 禮 makes it a dharma of utmost nobility. Further, 體 is "bottom" (底): to trace the source to its very bottom, to exhaust the principle to the depths of the abyss, to reach thoroughly the limit of reality — this is called "the bottom." The Great Treatise says, "Of the great ocean of the perfection of wisdom, only the Buddha reaches the bottom"; hence "bottom" is used to explain 體. Further, 體 means "penetrating" (達): one who grasps the meaning of this essence penetrates without blockage, like the wind moving through the sky, free and unobstructed, and all different names and particular explanations do not contradict the true mark. The Great Treatise says, "Prajñā is a single dharma; the Buddha speaks of it by various names"; hence "penetrating" is used to explain the essence of the sūtra.

Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine: The Four Lands

Chinese Source

次明經宗。初簡宗體，次正明宗。有人言：宗即是體、體即是宗。今所不用。何者？宗既是二，體即不二。體若是二，體即非體；宗若不二，宗即非宗。如梁柱是屋之綱維，屋空是梁柱所取，不應以梁柱是屋空、屋空是梁柱。宗體若一，其過如是。宗體異者，則二物孤調，宗非顯體之宗、體非宗家之體。宗非顯體之宗，宗則邪倒無印；體非宗家之體，則體狹不周，離法性外別有諸法。宗體若異，其過如是。今言不異而異，故有宗不一而一，故有體也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, Clarifying the Cardinal Doctrine of the sūtra: first distinguishing the cardinal doctrine from the essence, then directly clarifying the cardinal doctrine. Someone says: the cardinal doctrine is the essence and the essence is the cardinal doctrine. We do not accept this here. Why? The cardinal doctrine is twofold; the essence is nondual. If the essence were twofold, the essence would not be the essence; if the cardinal doctrine were nondual, the cardinal doctrine would not be the cardinal doctrine. Beams and pillars are the framework of a house, and the empty space of the house is what the beams and pillars enclose; one should not take the beams and pillars to be the empty space of the house, nor the empty space of the house to be the beams and pillars. If cardinal doctrine and essence were one, the fault would be like this. If cardinal doctrine and essence were different, the two things would stand alone: the cardinal doctrine would not be a cardinal doctrine that reveals the essence, and the essence would not be the essence that belongs to the cardinal doctrine. If the cardinal doctrine were not one that reveals the essence, it would be deviant and inverted, without the seal; if the essence were not the essence that belongs to the cardinal doctrine, the essence would be narrow and not pervasive, and there would be dharmas apart from dharma-nature. If cardinal doctrine and essence were different, the fault would be like this. Now we say: not different yet different, hence there is a cardinal doctrine; not one yet one, hence there is an essence.

The four kinds of pure land are the land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together, the land of expedients with residue, the land of true reward free of obstruction, and the land of eternal tranquil light; each has pure and defiled [forms]. Only bodhisattvas dwell there; there are no two vehicles. The Humane Kings Sūtra says, "The three worthies and ten sages abide in the fruit and reward"; this is its meaning. The Great Treatise says, "The excellent and wondrous five desires of the bodhisattvas can make Kāśyapa rise and dance." The Avataṃsaka speaks of "immeasurable fragrant cloud-platforms": these are the pure and wondrous five sense-objects of that land. Within it one further discusses sequential and sudden awakening, higher and lower, pure and defiled, and so on. The land of eternal tranquil light: "eternal" is the Dharma-body, "tranquil" is liberation, "light" is prajñā; these three dots are neither vertical nor horizontal, neither together nor separate, and are called the secret treasury. [In it there are] partial attainment and the ultimate, higher and lower, pure and defiled. Therefore that the wondrous contemplation that cultivates the mind can bring about the pure land is taken as the sūtra's cardinal doctrine.

Discussing the Function and Classifying the Teachings

Chinese Source

次辯經用。用者力用也，生善滅惡為經力用。滅惡故言力，生善故言用。滅惡故言功，生善故言德。此皆偏舉，具論必備也。苦是惡果，貪恚癡是惡因。惡因不除，果不得謝。是故此經能令五逆罪滅，往生淨土，即是此經之大力用也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, Discussing the Function of the sūtra. "Function" means power and function: producing good and extinguishing evil is the sūtra's power and function. Because it extinguishes evil it is called power; because it produces good it is called function. Because it extinguishes evil it is called merit (功); because it produces good it is called virtue (德). These each raise one side; a full discussion must include both. Suffering is the fruit of evil; greed, anger, and delusion are the causes of evil. If the causes of evil are not removed, the fruit cannot fall away. Therefore this sūtra can extinguish the offenses of the five heinous transgressions and [let one] go to be born in the pure land: this is the sūtra's great power and function.

Classifying the Teachings: this is included in the Mahāyāna vaipulya teaching. Going out to meet capacities and suiting the transformation, [the versions] differ in being full or brief. The larger version, in two fascicles, was translated by Zhu Fahu during the Yongjia years of the Jin; this version was translated by Kālayaśas at Yangzhou in the Yuanjia period of the Song. Both sūtras were spoken at the City of Rājagṛha. There is also a smaller version named Amitābha, spoken in the country of Śrāvastī. "Amitābha" and "Immeasurable Life" are the speech of that region and of this one. Of the two piṭakas that make the meaning clear, it is included in the bodhisattva-piṭaka. Of gradual and sudden entry into awakening, this is the sudden teaching. It is addressed directly to Vaidehī and her attendant women, all ordinary beings who had not realized even the lesser fruit; hence we know it is sudden and not entered gradually. The title says "spoken by the Buddha" to distinguish it from what is spoken by the four [kinds of] persons: disciples, seers, devas, and transformation-beings.

「如是」者，諸法實相古今不異，名如。如理而說，故為是。決定可信，故云如是。「我聞」者，表異外道，親承有在。我者，自在義。一切法空無我，何故說我？隨俗假名說我，謂見慢名字。若無我，則無聞；若無聞，化道則絕。為此義故，雖知無我，為傳化不絕，假名說我。如人以金錢易銅錢及草木等，賣買法爾，人無笑者。故言我聞。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Thus" (如是): the true mark of all dharmas, not different in past and present, is called "such" (如); speaking in accordance with principle is therefore "so" (是). Because it can be believed with certainty, it says "thus." "I have heard" shows it is different from non-Buddhists: it was received in person, from one who was there. "I" (我) means sovereignty. All dharmas are empty and without self; why speak of "I"? Following the world, one speaks of "I" as a provisional name — namely, the "I" of views, of pride, and of names. If there were no "I," there would be no hearing; if there were no hearing, the way of transformation would be cut off. For this reason, though knowing there is no self, so that transmission and transformation are not cut off, one speaks of "I" as a provisional name. It is like someone exchanging gold coins for copper coins, or for grass and wood and the like: buying and selling are like this, and no one laughs. Hence it says "I have heard."

By the wisdom that knows all things he differs from non-Buddhists; by compassion he differs from the two vehicles; by equality he differs from lesser bodhisattvas; most honored, he is called Buddha. The abiding of the devas refers to the six heavens of desire; its cause is the wholesome mind of giving and precepts. The abiding of Brahmā is from the first dhyāna up to [the heaven of] neither perception nor non-perception; its cause is the four immeasurable minds. The abiding of the sages is [that of] people of the three vehicles; its cause is the three samādhis. The abiding of the Buddha is the Śūraṃgama, the hundred and eight samādhis, the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses, and the eighteen unshared [qualities].

If a worthy minister leaves, the country will surely perish. "Will you not stand by me?": Jīvaka again remonstrated that he must by no means harm his mother. [The king] repented and sought to be delivered from his former fault; he then cast down the sword, stopped, and did not harm his mother, and ordered the palace attendants to imprison her deep in the palace.

Chinese Source

「韋提希被幽閉」下，第二請佛，謂請如來令遣弟子與己相見。文為二：初明請人、次明請法。韋提何故請見目連及以阿難？目連是門師、阿難佛侍者，先恒教誡，故偏求見。居在深宮不敢偏求，內厭惡界願生淨土，欲令二人傳意請佛。悲泣雨淚望佛哀憐，遙向佛禮。前已禮竟，今復重禮，表己殷勤。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "When Vaidehī had been shut away" on is the second [part], asking for the Buddha, that is, asking the Tathāgata to send disciples to meet with her. The text has two [parts]: first, making clear the request for persons; next, making clear the request for the Dharma. Why did Vaidehī ask to see Maudgalyāyana and Ānanda? Maudgalyāyana was the family's teacher and Ānanda the Buddha's attendant; they had constantly instructed her before, hence she asked to see them in particular. Living deep in the palace, she did not dare to ask only [for the Buddha]; inwardly loathing the evil realm and wishing to be born in the pure land, she wanted the two to convey her wish and invite the Buddha. Weeping, her tears falling like rain, hoping for the Buddha's compassion, she bowed toward the Buddha from afar. She had already bowed before, and now bowed again, showing her earnestness.

Chinese Source

「世尊在耆闍」下，如來現宮，不異《勝鬘》。即生此念時，佛於空中現也。文為五：一神通、二色身、三坐座、四眷屬、五雨華。「知韋提希心念」者，是知他心。「從崛山沒王官出」，顯神通也。問：前頻婆請弟子，意在如來。今夫人亦請弟子，意在佛。何故前請遣弟子，今請自往耶？解有二義：一、闍王與調達殺父，如來若躬赴，恐世王起怨嫌心，為護彼故，不得自往。二者佛法寄在國王，頻婆定死、闍世當為國主，如來若往者，王得國主，佛法不行，故不得往。夫人無此諸事，如來自往。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "the World-Honored One, on Vulture Peak" on, the Tathāgata appears in the palace, no different from the Śrīmālā. At the very moment she gave rise to this thought, the Buddha appeared in the sky. The text has five [parts]: first, spiritual powers; second, the form-body; third, the seat; fourth, the retinue; fifth, the rain of flowers. "Knowing what was in Vaidehī's heart" is knowing others' minds. "Vanished from Vulture Peak and appeared within the royal palace" displays spiritual powers. Question: Before, Bimbisāra asked for a disciple, [though] his intention was on the Tathāgata. Now the queen also asks for disciples, her intention on the Buddha. Why, at the earlier request, were disciples sent, while at this request he went himself? The explanation has two meanings. First: King Ajātaśatru and Devadatta [were set on] killing the father; if the Tathāgata had gone in person, he feared that King [Ajātaśatru] would give rise to resentment; to protect him, he could not go himself. Second: the Buddha-dharma is entrusted to the king of the country; Bimbisāra was certain to die, and Ajātaśatru would become ruler of the country; if the Tathāgata had gone, then when the king became ruler of the country, the Buddha-dharma would not prevail; hence he could not go. The queen had none of these concerns, so the Tathāgata went himself.

Chinese Source

傷歎請法中有二意：「韋提見佛」下，正明請其生處。「今向世尊」下，明請往生之因。初明供養，問往生因；次問生處也。「我有何罪？生此惡子？世尊復有何等因緣，與提婆達多而為眷屬？」此經不答，餘經說之。昔於錠光佛時，釋迦為摩納，就珍寶仙人學。學習既成，念欲報恩，自惟貧乏。于時耶若達欲嫁女，時有須摩提求為女婿，聰明有智而形貌醜。摩納遇見論義，須摩提屈在言下。耶若達歡喜，大賜珍寶，以女妻之。摩提生忿發誓：「未來世世常惱。」為此因緣常觸惱也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within grieving, lamenting, and requesting the Dharma there are two meanings: from "When Vaidehī saw the World-Honored One" on, it directly makes clear her request for a place of birth; from "Now, before the World-Honored One" on, it makes clear her request for the cause of rebirth. First it makes clear her offering, asking about the cause of rebirth; next she asks about the place of birth. "What sin did I commit in a former life, that I should bear such a wicked son? And what karmic bond does the World-Honored One himself have, to be kin to Devadatta?" This sūtra does not answer; other sūtras speak of it. Formerly, in the time of the Buddha Dīpaṃkara, Śākyamuni was the youth Māṇava, studying under the seer Precious Jewel. When his studies were complete, he thought of repaying [his teacher's] kindness, but considered himself poor. At that time Yajñadatta wished to marry off his daughter, and there was one Sumati who sought to be the daughter's husband, intelligent and wise but ugly in appearance. Māṇava met him in debate, and Sumati was defeated by his words. Yajñadatta was delighted, gave [Māṇava] great treasures, and gave him his daughter as wife. Sumati, in anger, made a vow: "In future ages, life after life, I will always vex [you]." Because of this cause and condition, he always offends and vexes him.

Chinese Source

「濁惡」者，濁，五濁也，一見、二煩惱、三眾生、四命、五劫。惡者，十惡也，殺、盜、婬、妄語、惡口、兩舌、綺語、貪、瞋、邪見也。「三途」，地獄名泥犁，譯云不可樂。畜生云帝行，從主畜養，為人驅使食噉。餓鬼飢虛怯畏。三千剎土同有此惡，故曰盈滿。多不善聚，惡道因也。無人不起，故名曰多。人常現行殺盜婬等，違理枉物為不善。積集稱聚也。「願我未來，不聞惡聲、不見惡人。今向世尊五體投地」，兩肘、兩膝、頭頂，是為五體也。懺摩，梵言；悔過，漢語。彼此並舉，故云懺悔。將果驗因，知過去有罪，恐償未盡來更受，故須懺悔。「惟願佛日」，啟告所求。佛能破壞眾生癡闇，如日除昏，故言佛日教我觀於清淨業處。序文竟。◎

Ru-Yi Translation

"Defiled and evil": "defiled" (濁) is the five turbidities: first, of views; second, of afflictions; third, of beings; fourth, of life; fifth, of the eon. "Evil" is the ten evils: killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech, harsh speech, double-tongued speech, frivolous speech, greed, anger, and wrong views. The "three paths": hell is called niraya, translated "not delightful." Animals are called "going [under] a master," raised by their owners, driven and eaten by people. Hungry ghosts are starved, empty, and fearful. In three thousand lands alike there are these evils, hence it says "filled to overflowing." "Heaped with so much that is not good" is the cause of the evil paths. There is no one who does not give rise to it, hence it is called "much"; people constantly commit killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, and so on, going against principle and wronging beings — this is "not good"; accumulating is called "heaped." "I vow that in the future I will neither hear evil words nor see evil people. Now, before the World-Honored One, I throw all five limbs of my body to the ground": the two elbows, the two knees, and the crown of the head are the five limbs. Kṣama (懺摩) is the Sanskrit word; "repenting of faults" (悔過) is the Chinese; the two are given together, hence it says "repentance" (懺悔). Verifying the cause from the fruit, she knows that in the past she had offenses; fearing that the repayment is not yet finished and that she will receive more in the future, she must repent. "I ask only that the Buddha, like the sun" sets out what she seeks. The Buddha can destroy the darkness of beings' delusion as the sun removes dimness; hence it says "the Buddha, like the sun, teach me to contemplate a place of pure karma." The text of the preface is finished.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Sūtra lemmas. Every phrase quoted from the Contemplation Sūtra uses the wording of the site's translation (sutras/contemplation-sutra, mainly "Vaidehī's Imprisonment and the Three Blessings"; the transmission lemma is from "The Final Entrustment"). Where the commentary abbreviates a lemma (e.g. 爾時王舍太子阿闍世, 爾時阿難白佛當何名), the site's words are given with ellipses.

Attribution. The ascription to Zhiyi is traditional; the commentary is often treated in modern scholarship as a later work circulating under his name. Zhili's Miaozong chao (T1751) defends and expounds it.

Six identities. 理佛, 名字佛, 觀行佛, 相似佛, 分證佛, 究竟佛 follow the glossary's six identities (identity in principle / in name / in contemplative practice / in resemblance / in a portion of the truth / in the ultimate), with "buddha" in place of "identity."

Common and particular (通／別). The sentence 即教別同名為經即教通為行不同 is punctuated in CBETA as 即教別同名為經，即教通為行不同. The translation reads 即教別；同名為經即教通；為行不同，從一乃至無量即行別. Alternatively, with the CBETA punctuation: "this is teachings being particular yet alike named 'sūtra'; this is the teaching being common while practice is not the same."

Quotations located. Lotus: the two verse lines are combined from chapter 7 (site, Lotus Sūtra: "a hundred and eighty kalpas passed empty, with no buddha in the world" and "while the World-Honored One had not yet appeared, the ten directions were always dark"); "attained through long cultivation of karma" (chapter 16); "only a buddha together with a buddha can fathom the true mark" (chapter 2); the arhat passage (chapter 2) is paraphrased by the commentary. Vimalakīrti: "Every being is suchness" (site, Vimalakīrti, chapter 4). The site's Lotus uses "kalpa"; this translation otherwise uses "eon," following the site's Contemplation Sūtra.

Quotations in the Great Treatise (T1509). "Prajñā is a single dharma; the Buddha speaks of it by various names" is in T1509 verbatim. "Apart from the true mark of all dharmas, everything else is the work of Māra" paraphrases T1509 (除諸法實相，餘殘一切法，盡名為魔). The four kinds of eating and the nun Pure Eyes are in T1509. "What is practiced is as what is spoken" corresponds to T1509 所作如所言. "The three wisdoms are actually attained in a single mind": T1509 has 一心中得一切智 and related wording; the exact phrase was not located. "Outside the three realms there is a pure land," "Kāśyapa rise and dance," "only the Buddha reaches the bottom": source not located in T1509 as worded.

Other sources. The three contemplations and their names are in the Jeweled Necklace Sūtra (T1485): 從假名入空二諦觀，從空入假名平等觀…因是二空觀，得入中道第一義諦觀. "The three worthies and ten sages abide in the fruit and reward" is in the Humane Kings Sūtra (T0245). The Middle Treatise verse (T1564) reads differently (眾因緣生法，我說即是空，亦為是假名，亦是中道義); the commentary gives the Tiantai summary form. The Precious Casket line (佛界眾生界…) was not located in its sūtra.

Pun glosses. 說 is explained by 悅 ("delight"), 經 by 法 and 常, 體 by 禮, 底, and 達, 如是 by 如 and 是. The Chinese characters are kept in parentheses because the explanations depend on them.

Breaking and negating. 破 is rendered "break," 遮 "negate," 照 "illuminate," 亡 "vanish," following the glossary's candidate list.

Four lands. Full names: 凡聖同居土 the land where sages and ordinary beings dwell together; 方便有餘土 the land of expedients with residue; 實報無障礙土 the land of true reward free of obstruction; 常寂光土 the land of eternal tranquil light. 安養 is rendered "[the land of] Peaceful Nourishment."

The measures. The text says "measured" has two meanings (the measure of the immeasurable; the measure of the measured) but then illustrates "the measure of the measured" and "the immeasurable of the measured." The inconsistency is in the Chinese and is kept.

Translators. The commentary credits the two-fascicle Immeasurable Life Sūtra to Zhu Fahu (Dharmarakṣa); the received Taishō text (T0360) is credited to Saṅghavarman. This is reported as the commentary states it.

Jīvaka. 菴羅女子也 is read as "he was the son of the Āmra woman" (Āmrapālī), the usual story of Jīvaka's birth. CBETA punctuates 菴羅，女子也.

Maudgalyāyana's past act. 昔為辟支佛剃頭作袈裟 is read "formerly, for a pratyekabuddha, he shaved the head and made a kāṣāya." Alternatively: "formerly he was a pratyekabuddha, shaved his head, and made a kāṣāya."

Śāriputra's clan. 姓拘栗陀 (Kolita) is elsewhere the personal name of Maudgalyāyana; possibly a slip in the text. Translated as written.

Preface imagery. "Cuts across horizontally" (橫截) and "the five sufferings" echo the Immeasurable Life Sūtra (T0360: 橫截五惡趣, "cutting across the five evil destinies").
