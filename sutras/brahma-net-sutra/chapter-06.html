Chapter 6 · 梵網經 · The Ten Grounds, and Vairocana's Transmission

The Ten Grounds

The thirty minds closed at the end of Chapter 5 with a promise: the Ten Grounds, the fourth and final group naming this stage of the path, would open next. They open here — ten grounds of steadily accumulating wisdom, power, and radiance, each one built from what the ground before it already established, culminating in a full explanation of the ten traditional epithets of a buddha. The chapter closes with Vairocana Buddha entrusting this entire teaching to a thousand emanated buddhas, who scatter across every heaven to teach it in turn — the narrative hinge where the sūtra's tradition marks the end of its first two rolls and the opening of its third, the roll containing the bodhisattva precepts themselves.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: translation in progress Source: Taishō T24, no. 1484 Reading notes: Ru-Yi's own, cross-checked against A. Charles Muller & Kenneth Tanaka's scholarly translation

1. Milestone: The Ground of Equal Nature 體性平等地

Vairocana Buddha answers the question left open at the end of Chapter 5 — what do the Ten Grounds actually mean? — by describing the first ground as a place where a single wisdom already reaches everything at once: every buddha-land, every teaching, the twelve links of dependent origination, form itself, all known without the mind ever leaving the middle way.

Chinese Source

盧舍那佛言：「千佛！汝先問十地者有何義？若佛子！菩提薩埵入平等慧體性地，真實法化，一切行華光滿足，四天果乘用任化，無方理化，神通、十力、十號、十八不共法，住佛淨土。無量大願，辯才無畏，一切論、一切行我皆得入。生出佛家，坐佛性地，一切障礙凡夫因果畢竟不受，大樂歡喜。從一佛土入無量佛土，從一劫入無量劫，不可說法為可說法，及照見一切法、逆順見一切法，常入二諦而在第一義中。以一智知十地次第，一一事示眾生，而常心心中道。以一智知一切佛土殊品及佛所說法，而身心不變。以一智知十二因緣、十惡種性，而常住善道。以一智見有無二相。以一智知入十禪支行三十七道，而現一切色身六道。以一智知十方色色，分分了起入受色報，而心心無縛，光光照一切，是故無生信忍空慧常現在前。從一地二地乃至佛界，其中間一切法門一時而行故。略出平等地功德海藏行願，如海一渧毛頭許事。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Vairocana Buddha said: "Thousand Buddhas — listen. You have asked what these ten grounds mean. Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva who enters the Ground of Equal Nature teaches the true dharma and transforms through it; every practice comes to full flower, freely applying the fruit of the four heavens by no fixed method, dwelling in a buddha's pure land complete with spiritual powers, the ten powers, the ten epithets, and the eighteen unshared qualities. With boundless vows and fearless eloquence, every treatise and every practice is now entered. Born into the Buddha's own family, seated on the ground of buddha-nature, no obstruction and no ordinary cause-and-effect is any longer received — only great joy remains. From one buddha-land they enter countless buddha-lands; from one kalpa they enter countless kalpas; what could not be said becomes sayable, and every dharma is seen clearly, seen both forward and in reverse, always entering the two truths while standing within the ultimate truth. With a single wisdom they know the sequence of all ten grounds, showing each thing to sentient beings while their own mind remains constantly within the middle way. With a single wisdom they know every distinct buddha-land and every teaching a Buddha has spoken, and yet body and mind stay unchanged. With a single wisdom they know the twelve links of dependent origination and the seeds of the ten unwholesome deeds, and yet abide constantly in the wholesome path. With a single wisdom they see that existence and nonexistence are not two separate marks. With a single wisdom they enter the ten branches of meditative absorption and practice the thirty-seven factors of the path, while manifesting bodies throughout the six destinies. With a single wisdom they know every form in every direction, each arising and receiving its own retribution, and yet no thought is ever bound; light meets light and illuminates everything, and so the patient acceptance of the unborn and the wisdom of emptiness stand constantly before them. From the first ground to the second, all the way to the realm of buddhahood, every dharma-gate between is practiced at once. This barely indicates the ocean-treasury of merit, practice, and vow belonging to the Ground of Equal Nature — a single drop from that ocean, no more than the tip of a hair."

Ru-Yi's reading note

Notice what this first ground refuses to save for later: it does not claim a single wisdom for the tenth ground and a partial one for the first. "With a single wisdom they know X, and yet Y remains unchanged" is stated here, at the very opening, as already complete — the remaining nine grounds will turn out to be refinements of what this one wisdom does, not additions to what it can reach. That repeated grammatical shape — full knowing paired with total non-attachment inside the very same clause — is doing with parallel structure exactly what the Diamond Sūtra's "is not X, that is why it is called X" did with negation: holding complete engagement and complete release together, refusing to let either one cancel the other.

2. Milestone: The Ground of Wholesome Wisdom 體性善慧地

The second ground opens with the four immeasurable states of mind — kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity — but spends most of its length on something more exacting: a precise, almost clinical anatomy of suffering itself, breaking a single moment of pain into three distinguishable sensations arising one after another.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！菩提薩埵善慧體性地，清淨明達一切善根，所謂慈悲喜捨慧。一切功德本從初觀入大空慧方便道智中，見諸眾生無非苦諦皆有識心，三惡道刀杖一切苦惱緣中生識，名為苦諦。三苦相者，如身初覺，從刀杖身色陰二緣中生覺，為行苦緣。次意地覺，緣身覺所緣得刀杖及身創腫等法故，覺苦苦緣，重故苦苦。次受行覺二心，緣向身色陰壞創中生苦覺故，名為壞苦緣。是以三覺次第生三心，故為苦苦。……我於是中入教化道三昧，現一切色身於六道中，十種辯才說諸法門……逼迮生覺如斲石火，於身心念念生滅，身散壞轉變化，識入壞緣，緣集散心苦心惱，受念後緣染著心心不捨，是為壞苦。三界一切苦諦，復觀無明集無量心作一切業，相續相連習因集因，名為集諦。正見解脫空空智道心心，名以智道道諦。盡有果報盡有因，清淨一照體性，妙智寂滅一諦。慧品具足名根，一切慧性起空入觀，是初善根。第二觀捨一切貪著，行一切平等空捨，無緣而觀諸法。空際一想，我觀一切十方地土皆吾昔身所用故土，四大海水是吾故水，一切劫火是吾昔身故所用火，一切風輪是吾故所用氣。我今入此地中，法身滿足，捨吾故身，畢竟不受四大分段不淨故身，是為捨品具足。第三次觀於所化一切眾生，與人天樂、十地樂、離十惡畏樂、得妙華三昧樂乃至佛樂。如是觀者慈品具足。菩薩爾時住是地中無癡無貪無瞋，入平等一諦智一切行本，遊佛一切世界，現化無量法身。如一切眾生天華品說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva in the Ground of Wholesome Wisdom brings every wholesome root to pure clarity — that is, loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, equanimity, and wisdom. Every merit here is rooted in a wisdom that, from the very first contemplation, enters the skillful path of the wisdom of great emptiness: seeing that no being is exempt from the truth of suffering, that every being with a mind experiences consciousness arising from the sword-and-staff of the three lower destinies and all their pain — this is called the truth of suffering. As for the three marks of suffering: first, the body's initial sensation, arising from the two conditions of sword-and-staff and the body's own form, is the condition for the suffering of activity itself. Next, a feeling arises at the level of mind, conditioned by that first bodily sensation, taking in the sword-and-staff and the body's own wounds — this is felt as pain doubled upon pain, and because it doubles, it is called the suffering of suffering. Next, sensation and mental formation arise together, conditioned toward the body's own decay and injury, giving rise to a felt sense of loss — this is the condition of the suffering of decay. Three sensations, arising in sequence, give rise to three minds — this is why it is called suffering upon suffering. … And so I, within this very ground, enter the samādhi of the path of teaching and transformation, manifesting bodies of every kind throughout the six destinies, and with ten forms of eloquence expound the gateways of the dharma. … Pressed upon, sensation arises the way a spark leaps from struck stone: in body and mind, moment by moment, arising and ceasing; the body scatters, decays, and transforms, and consciousness enters into the very condition of that decay. Conditions gather and disperse, the mind suffers and is afflicted, and in the wake of what is felt, attachment clings on, unwilling to let go, moment after moment — this is the suffering of decay. The three realms, in their entirety, are nothing but this truth of suffering. Looking further, one sees that ignorance accumulates boundless states of mind, producing every kind of karma, one link joined to the next, habit-cause compounding gathered-cause — this is called the truth of origination. Right view, liberation, the wisdom that knows the emptiness of emptiness, mind after mind — this is called, through wisdom, the truth of the path. Every karmic fruit exhausted, every cause exhausted, one clear illumination of the essential nature itself, the subtle wisdom of cessation as a single truth — wisdom, complete, is called the root. All the nature of wisdom, arising from emptiness, entering contemplation — this is the first wholesome root. The second is the contemplation that relinquishes every form of craving and attachment, practising a relinquishment that is itself equal and empty, observing every dharma without any object to hold onto — a single perception at the very edge of emptiness: 'I see every land in the ten directions as ground I myself once used, in some life now past. The waters of the four great oceans are waters I once used. Every fire that has ever ended an age is a fire I once used in some body now past. Every wheel of wind is breath I once used. Now, entering this ground, my dharma-body complete, I let go of that former body — in the end, I no longer receive the impure, fragmented body made of the four elements.' This is the relinquishment root, complete. The third is the contemplation, turned toward every being still to be transformed, of the joy of gods and humans, the joy of the ten grounds, the joy of freedom from the fear of the ten unwholesome deeds, the joy of the wondrous flower samādhi, up to the very joy of buddhahood itself. Whoever contemplates in this way completes the root of loving-kindness. A bodhisattva abiding in this ground is at that time free of delusion, free of greed, free of anger; entering the single wisdom of the truth of equality, the root of every practice, they wander through every buddha-world, manifesting boundless dharma-bodies. This is explained fully in the Chapter on the Heavenly Flower of All Sentient Beings."

Ru-Yi's reading note

Honestly, this ground reads less like devotional teaching and more like a technical classification, closer to the fine-grained mental-factor analysis of Abhidharma literature than to anything in the forty minds before it — and it's worth naming that shift rather than smoothing it over. But the sequence itself is worth sitting with: wisdom, then relinquishment, then kindness, in that order and not the reverse. Compassion is the third root here, not the first, built on top of a clarity that has already stopped grasping — "I see every land as ground I once used" is renunciation of ownership, stated as a completed fact, before the text ever turns to wishing others well. For a course whose whole title promises "compassion and wisdom as one practice," this ground is a reminder that the sequence is not arbitrary: this text puts the clear seeing first.

3. Milestone: The Ground of Radiant Light 體性光明地

The third ground turns to how a bodhisattva actually teaches — the traditional twelve forms scripture itself can take — and, within that same breath, traces one being's entire path from conception to death as material already seen through.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！菩提薩埵光明體性地，以三昧解了智，知三世一切佛法門，十二法品名味句：重誦、記別、直語、偈、不請說、律戒、譬喻、佛界、昔事、方正、未曾有、談說，是法體性名第一義。別是名味句中，說一切有為法分分受生，初入識胎，四大增長色心名六住，於根中起實覺，未別苦樂名觸識，又覺苦樂識名三受，連連覺著受無窮已，欲、我見、戒取善惡有，識初名生，識終名死。是十品現在苦。因緣果觀是行相中道，我久已離故，無自體性。入光明神通，總持辯才，心心行空，而十方佛土中現劫化轉化百劫千劫，國土中養神通，禮敬佛前諮受法言。復現六道身，一音中說無量法品，而眾生各自分分得聞心所欲之法。苦空無常無我一諦之音，國土不同身心別化。是妙華光明地中略開一毛頭許，如法品解觀法門千三昧品說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva in the Ground of Radiant Light knows, through the discerning wisdom of samādhi, every dharma-teaching of the buddhas across all three times — the words, meanings, and phrases of the twelve divisions of the teaching: repeated verse, prophecy, plain narration, spontaneous verse, teaching given unasked, precept and discipline, parable, accounts of buddha-realms, stories of past lives, right and proper exposition, the never-before-told, and philosophical discourse. This essential nature of the dharma is itself called the ultimate meaning. Within these distinct words and phrases, every conditioned thing is shown arising bit by bit: first entering consciousness in the womb, the four elements increasing to form body and mind — this is called the six abodes; sensation arising truly at the level of the six sense-faculties, not yet distinguishing pleasure from pain, is called contact-consciousness; consciousness once it distinguishes pleasure and pain is called the three feelings; feeling, clung to again and again without end, becomes craving; taking a view of self, grasping at precepts, wholesome and unwholesome existence — the first moment of consciousness is called birth, its last moment is called death. These ten categories are the suffering present before us. To contemplate cause, condition, and fruit is itself the middle way of practice — long ago I already left this behind, for it has no essential nature of its own. Entering the spiritual power of radiant light, with total command of retention and eloquence, mind after mind moving through emptiness, I manifest, throughout the buddha-lands of the ten directions, the unfolding and transformation of a hundred, a thousand kalpas, cultivating spiritual power within these lands, paying homage before each Buddha and receiving the teaching. I again manifest bodies throughout the six destinies, and in a single sound expound boundless categories of dharma, so that each being, according to its own portion, hears exactly the teaching its own mind desires — the single sound of suffering, emptiness, impermanence, and no-self, though lands differ and bodies and minds are each transformed differently. This barely opens, to the width of a single hair, the Ground of Wondrous Flower-Light — as is explained fully in the Chapter on the Thousand Samādhis of Understanding and Contemplating the Dharma."

Ru-Yi's reading note

The twelve traditional forms of scripture listed here are the same taxonomy Chinese commentators have always used to classify how a teaching arrives — as prophecy, as parable, as verse never requested — and their presence this deep into the sūtra is a quiet claim: teaching itself, in every one of its literary forms, is now counted among the powers this ground has mastered, not set apart from realization as a separate, lesser skill. The single sound heard differently by different beings, "each according to its own portion," is the same idea Chapter 1 already introduced with Brahma's net — one teaching, refracted through countless jewels, landing as whatever each listener is actually able to receive.

4. Milestone: The Ground of the Known 體性爾焰地

The fourth ground's name comes from a Sanskrit term, jñeya — "the knowable" — everything an awakened mind could, in principle, know without exception. Its practice is a specific kind of concentration, one so complete that the mind described here never sinks, retreats, falls, or lingers.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！菩提薩埵體性地中，爾真焰俗，不斷不常，即生即住即滅，一世一時一有，種異異現異故。因緣中道非一非二、非善非惡、非凡非佛故。佛界凡界一一，是名為世諦。……故諸佛於入光光華三昧中，現無量佛，以手摩頂一音說法，百千起發而不出定，住定味樂定、著定貪定、一劫千劫中住定。……時諸佛光中摩頂，發起定品出相進相，去向相故。不沒不退、不墮不住，頂三昧法上樂忍，永盡無餘。即入一切佛土修行無量功德品，行行皆光明，入善權方便，化教一切眾生，能使得見佛體性常樂我淨。是人生住是地中行化法門，漸漸深妙華觀智入體性中道，一切法門品滿足，猶如金剛。上日月道品已明斯義。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva in the Ground of the Known accords with what is real and illuminates the conventional at once — neither cut off nor eternal, arising, abiding, and ceasing all at the same instant, each life, each moment, each existence appearing differently through its own distinct causes. Within the middle way of cause and condition, nothing is one or two, good or evil, ordinary being or buddha — and yet buddha-realm and ordinary realm are each still distinguished; this is called the conventional truth. … And so the buddhas, entering the samādhi of light meeting light as a flower, manifest countless buddhas, place a hand upon the practitioner's head, and teach the dharma in a single voice; through a hundred thousand such awakenings, the practitioner never once leaves concentration — abiding in it, savoring it, absorbed in it, for a single kalpa, a thousand kalpas. … Then, within the buddhas' own light, the hand is again placed upon the crown, and the aspects of transcending, advancing, and moving onward arise: the practitioner does not sink, does not retreat, does not fall, and does not linger — the dharma of the crown-samādhi, the patient acceptance of the highest joy, is brought to its complete and final end. They then enter every buddha-land, cultivating boundless categories of merit, every practice itself radiant, entering skillful means, teaching and transforming every being, enabling them to see that the essential nature of buddhahood is permanence, bliss, self, and purity. One who lives and abides in this ground, practising and teaching in this way, grows gradually deeper and more subtle, the flower-like contemplative wisdom entering the middle way of the essential nature itself, every dharma-gate brought to fulfillment — indestructible, like the diamond. This meaning has already been made clear above, in the Chapter on the Course of the Sun and Moon."

Ru-Yi's reading note

"Does not sink, does not retreat, does not fall, does not linger" is close kin to the Diamond Sūtra's "a mind that dwells nowhere at all" from Session 2 of this course — the same freedom, described this time not as an absence of fixation but as a positive momentum that simply has nowhere left to get stuck. And notice the sūtra's own honesty about scale here: even this deep into the ten grounds, the text says plainly that "the ten grounds are practiced at one and the same time" from within any single one of them — the sequence of ground one through ground ten is a way of narrating something that was never actually sequential to begin with.

5. Milestone: The Ground of Wisdom's Light 體性慧照地

The fifth ground names ten specific powers of knowing — a famous fixed list in Buddhist literature, traditionally what is said to distinguish a buddha's own omniscience from more limited forms of realization.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！菩提薩埵慧照體性地。法有十種力生品，起一切功德行。以一慧方便知善惡二業別行處力品、善作惡作業智力品、一切欲求願六道生生果欲力品、六道性分別不同性力品、一切善惡根一一不同根力品、邪定正定不定是名定力品、一切因果乘是因乘是果至果處乘因道是道力品、五眼知一切法見一切受生故天眼力品、百劫事一一知宿世力品、於一切生煩惱滅一切受無明滅解脫力品，是十力品智。知自修因果，亦知一切眾生因果分別，而身心口別用。以淨國土為惡國土、以惡國土為妙樂土，能轉善作惡、轉惡作善，色為非色、非色為色，以男為女、以女為男，以六道為非六道、非六道為六道，乃至地水火風非地水火風。是人爾時以大方便力，從一切眾生而見不可思議、下地所不能知覺舉足下足事。是人大明智，漸漸進分分智，光光無量無量，不可說不可說法門現在前行。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva in the Ground of Wisdom's Light gives rise, through a single skillful wisdom, to ten kinds of power, and from them, every meritorious action: the power of knowing what is fitting and what is not, discerning wholesome action from unwholesome; the power of knowing karma itself, wholesome and unwholesome alike; the power of knowing what every being in the six destinies desires and aspires to; the power of knowing the differing natures of beings across the six destinies; the power of knowing which faculties, wholesome or unwholesome, belong to which being; the power of knowing what is wrongly fixed, rightly fixed, or not yet fixed at all; the power of knowing that every cause leads, by the very same vehicle, to its own fruit, and that the fruit is reached only by riding that same causal path; the power of the five eyes, knowing every dharma and seeing every being's rebirth through the divine eye; the power of knowing, one by one, the events of a hundred kalpas past; and the power of knowing the ending of every affliction produced by rebirth, and the ending of the ignorance that produces karma — the power of liberation itself. Knowing these ten powers, a bodhisattva knows the causes and fruits of their own cultivation, and knows, too, the causes and fruits distinguishing every other being. And yet body, speech, and mind each still act with total freedom: taking a pure land as though it were defiled, and a defiled land as though it were a realm of wonder; turning the wholesome into the unwholesome and the unwholesome into the wholesome; taking form as no-form and no-form as form; taking male as female and female as male; taking the six destinies as not the six destinies, and what is not the six destinies as the six destinies — down to earth, water, fire, and wind taken as not earth, water, fire, and wind at all. At this time, through the power of great skillful means, this being moves among all sentient beings, seeing what cannot be thought, what those in lower grounds cannot know — even the awareness bound up in the simple lifting and setting-down of a single foot. This great and radiant wisdom advances by degrees, one discernment at a time, light meeting light without limit, without limit — inexplicable, ineffable dharma-gates standing directly ahead."

Ru-Yi's reading note

These ten powers (the daśabala) are one of Buddhist literature's oldest fixed lists, usually reserved for describing what makes a buddha's knowledge complete rather than partial. What's worth pausing on is the final image: form as no-form, male as female, land as its opposite — not offered as illusion for its own sake, but as evidence that categories this course has been quietly unsettling since Session 1 (a fixed self, a fixed bodhisattva, a fixed universe) are, at this level of the path, no longer fixed in practice either, not only in theory. The closing detail — awareness present in "the simple lifting and setting-down of a single foot" — is worth setting beside Session 4's Chapter of Pure Conduct: total realization, here, still shows up at the scale of a single footstep.

6. Milestone: The Ground of Flower-Light 體性華光地

The sixth ground lists ten kinds of supernatural cognition — each one a form of unlimited access to some part of another being's experience: their form, their voice, their mind, their past, their future liberation.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！菩提薩埵體性華光地，能於一切世界中，十神通明智品，以示一切眾生種種變化。以天眼明智知三世國土中微塵等一切色，分分成六道眾生身，一一身微塵細色成大色分分知。以天耳智知十方三世六道眾生苦樂音聲、非非音、非非聲、一切法聲。以天身智知一切色、色非色、非男非女形。於一念中遍十方三世國土劫量大小國土中微塵身，以天他心智知三世眾生心中所行，十方六道中一切眾生心心所念苦樂善惡等事。以天人智知十方三世國土中一切眾生宿世苦樂受命，一一知命續百劫。以天解脫智知十方三世眾生解脫，斷除一切煩惱若多若少，從一地乃至十地滅滅皆盡。以天定心智知十方三世國土中眾生心，定、不定、非定非不定、起定方法，有所攝受三昧百三昧。以天覺智知一切眾生已成佛未成佛，乃至一切六道人心心，亦知十方佛心中所說法。以天念智知百劫千劫大小劫中，一切眾生受命命久近。以天願智知一切眾生賢聖十地，三十心中一一行願，若求苦樂、若法非法，一切求十願百千大願品具足。是人住地中十神通明中，現無量身心口分別用。說地功德，百千萬劫不可窮盡。而爾所釋迦略開神通明品，如觀十二因緣品中說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva in the Ground of Flower-Light can, throughout every world, call on ten kinds of supernatural cognition to show every being every kind of transformation: with the divine eye, knowing every mote of form in every land across the three times, each one combining, particle by particle, into the bodies of beings in the six destinies; with the divine ear, knowing every sound of suffering and joy from beings in the six destinies in all directions and times — sound that is not quite sound, voice that is not quite voice, every dharma-voice there is; with the divine body, knowing every form, form that is not form, shape that is neither male nor female, pervading, in a single thought, every land of every size across the ten directions and three times; with the divine mind that knows others' minds, knowing what every being in the six directions is thinking, in every land, across the three times, their pleasure and pain, their good and evil; with the divine knowledge of destiny, knowing the pleasure and pain and lifespan of every being's past lives, one by one, spanning a hundred kalpas; with the divine knowledge of liberation, knowing the liberation of beings in the ten directions and three times, the greater or lesser ending of every affliction, from the first ground all the way to the tenth, each ending traced to its full completion; with the divine knowledge of concentration, knowing whether the minds of beings in every land are settled, unsettled, or neither, and by what method concentration arises, and every samādhi it can hold; with the divine knowledge of awakening, knowing which beings have become buddhas and which have not, down to the minds of beings in all six destinies, and knowing, too, what is spoken in the minds of buddhas in every direction; with the divine knowledge of recollection, knowing, across a hundred kalpas or a thousand, how long or short each being's life has been; and with the divine knowledge of vows, knowing the vows and practices of every noble being across the ten grounds, and the thirty minds before them, whatever they seek, wholesome or not — every vow, small and great, brought to fulfillment. One abiding in this ground, within these ten kinds of supernatural cognition, manifests boundless bodies and distinct uses of mind and speech, expounding the merit of this ground for a hundred thousand million kalpas without exhausting it. And this is Śākyamuni's own brief opening of the chapter on supernatural cognition, as explained in the Chapter on Contemplating the Twelve Links of Dependent Origination."

Ru-Yi's reading note

Set this list beside Session 4's Chapter of Pure Conduct, and the contrast is instructive. That chapter turned every ordinary sense-object a bodhisattva happens to encounter — a bridge, a bowl of rice, a locked door — into a wish for all beings. This ground describes something more totalizing: not encountering beings one at a time as the day happens to bring them, but holding unlimited, simultaneous access to every being's form, voice, mind, and history at once. The scale is entirely different. The direction of attention — outward, toward what other beings are actually experiencing, rather than inward toward one's own state — is exactly the same.

7. Milestone: The Ground of Fulfillment 體性滿足地

The seventh ground names the eighteen distinctive qualities traditionally said to belong to a sage alone, then explains them as the completion of six specific practices built directly on everything the prior six grounds already established.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！菩提薩埵滿足體性地，入是法中十八聖人智品，下地所不共。所謂身無漏過、口無語罪、念無失念。離八法，一切法中捨，常在三昧。是入地六品具足，復從是智生六足智。三界結習畢竟不受，故欲具足。一切功德一切法門所求滿，故進心足。一切法事一切劫事一切眾生事，以一心中一時知，故念心足。是二諦相，六道眾生一切法，故智慧足。知十發趣人中至一切佛，無結無習，故解脫足。是一切眾生知他人自我弟子無漏，無諸煩習故，以智知他身，解脫足。是人入六滿足明智中便起智，身隨六道眾生心行口辯說無量法門品，示一切眾生故。隨一切眾生心行，常入三昧，而十方大地動、虛空化華，故能令眾生心行。以大明具足，見過去一切劫中佛出世，亦是示一切眾生心。以無著智見現十方一切國土中一切佛，一切眾生心心所行。以神通智，見未來中一切劫一切佛出世，一切眾生從是佛受道聽法故。住是十八聖人中心心三昧，觀三界微塵等色是我故身，一切眾生是我父母，而今入是地中，一切功德、一切神光、一切佛所行法，乃至八地九地中一切法門品，我皆已入，故於一切佛國土中示現作佛、成道、轉法輪、示入滅度，轉化他方過去來今一切國土中。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva in the Ground of Fulfillment enters, within this dharma, the eighteen distinctive qualities of a sage, shared with no lower ground: the body free of all fault, speech free of all wrongdoing, thought free of all lapse of mindfulness; free of the eight kinds of error, always within samādhi with regard to every dharma. Entering this ground, six qualities are brought to completion, and from this wisdom, six further capacities arise in turn: the three realms' habitual bonds are, in the end, no longer received — this is the completion of desire; every merit and every dharma-gate sought is now fulfilled — this is the completion of diligence; every matter across every kalpa and every being is known in a single moment, with a single mind — this is the completion of mindfulness; the two truths and every dharma across the six destinies are known — this is the completion of wisdom; knowing that those who first set out toward buddhahood, all the way up to the buddhas themselves, are free of every bond and every habitual trace — this is the completion of liberation; and knowing that every being is free of defilement exactly as one's own students are, and knowing others' liberation through this same wisdom — this is liberation completed once more. Having entered these six completions and their wisdom, one then, according to the mind of every being in the six destinies, speaks with eloquence across boundless dharma-gates, showing every being what accords with their own mind, entering samādhi at will, moving the whole earth and empty space alike, and transforming flowers out of the sky — able to move the very minds of beings. With this great and complete clarity, one sees every buddha who has ever appeared in every kalpa now past, showing this to every being's mind as well; with wisdom free of any clinging, one sees every buddha now present in every land of the ten directions and the minds of every being there; and with the wisdom of spiritual power, one sees every buddha yet to appear, in every kalpa yet to come, and every being who will receive the path and hear the dharma from that buddha. Abiding within these eighteen qualities of a sage, mind after mind in samādhi, one contemplates the motes of dust filling the three thousand worlds as one's own past bodies, and every being as one's own father and mother — and now, entering this ground, every merit, every spiritual radiance, every dharma a buddha practises, all the way through the eighth and ninth grounds, is already entered; and so, throughout every buddha-land, one manifests the appearance of becoming a buddha, attaining the path, turning the wheel of dharma, and entering final nirvāṇa, transforming beings across every land, past, present, and future, everywhere at once."

Ru-Yi's reading note

"Fulfillment" here is not described as a plateau reached and then simply occupied — it is explicitly the completion of six named practices (renunciation, diligence, mindfulness, wisdom, liberation, and liberating others in turn), each one demonstrably built on the ground before it. The sixth completion is worth lingering on: liberation "completed once more," through knowing that other beings are free of defilement exactly as one's own students are. Even at this altitude, the text still needs a second beat of liberation aimed outward, at others, after the first beat aimed at oneself is already finished — wisdom and compassion, once again, treated as two motions rather than one static state.

8. Milestone: The Ground of the Buddha's Roar 體性佛吼地

The eighth ground opens with a list of ten kinds of emptiness — far more differentiated than the single word "emptiness" this course has used since Session 1 — and closes with a wisdom the text says only this ground, and no lower one, can actually attain.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！菩提薩埵佛吼體性地，入法王位三昧，其智如佛，佛吼三昧故。十品大明定門常現在前，華光音入心三昧。其空慧者，謂內空慧門、外空慧門，有為空慧門、無為空慧門，性空慧門，無始空慧門，第一義空慧門，空空慧門，空空復空慧門，空空復空空慧門。如是十空門，下地所不知。虛空平等地，不可說不可說。神通道智，以一念智知一切法分分別異，而入無量佛國土中，一一佛前諮受法，轉法度與一切眾生。而以法藥施一切眾生，為大法師、為大導師，破壞四魔，法身化化入佛界，是諸佛數。是諸九地、十地數中。長養法身，百千陀羅尼門、百千三昧門、百千金剛門、百千神通門、百千解脫門，如是百千虛空平等門中而大自在，一念一時行。劫說非劫、非劫說劫，非道說道、道說非道，非六道眾生說六道眾生、六道眾生說非六道眾生，非佛說佛、佛說非佛。而入出諸佛體性三昧中，反照順照逆照，前照後照、因照果照、空照有照、第一中道義諦照。是智唯八地所證，下地所不及。不動不到、不出不入、不生不滅。是地法門品，無量無量，不可說不可說，今以略開地中百千分一毛頭許事。羅漢品中已明。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva in the Ground of the Buddha's Roar enters the samādhi of the very throne of the dharma-king, wisdom equal to a buddha's own — the samādhi of the buddha's roar itself. Ten categories of great and radiant concentration stand constantly before this being, the samādhi of flower-light and sound entering the mind. As for the wisdom of emptiness held here: the wisdom of inner emptiness, of outer emptiness, of the emptiness of the conditioned, of the emptiness of the unconditioned, of the emptiness of essential nature, of beginningless emptiness, of the emptiness of ultimate meaning, of the emptiness of emptiness, of the emptiness that is again emptied, and of the emptiness that empties even that emptying — these ten gateways of emptiness are unknown to any lower ground. The ground of empty, equal space itself cannot be told, cannot be told. Through spiritual power and the wisdom of the path, in a single thought this being knows every dharma in each of its distinctions, entering countless buddha-lands, receiving the teaching before every buddha there, turning the dharma-wheel and handing it on to every being, offering the medicine of the dharma to every being as its great teacher, its great guide, overturning the four demonic hosts, its dharma-body transforming again and again until it enters the very realm of buddhahood — one now counted among the buddhas themselves, among those of the ninth and tenth grounds. Nurturing the dharma-body, a hundred thousand gateways of retention, of samādhi, of the diamond, of spiritual power, of liberation — through these hundred thousand gateways of empty, equal space, moving with total freedom, one thought accomplishing all at once: speaking of a kalpa as no kalpa, of no kalpa as a kalpa; speaking of the path as no path, of no path as the path; speaking of the beings of the six destinies as not the beings of the six destinies, and of what is not those beings as those beings; speaking of a buddha as no buddha, and of no buddha as a buddha. Entering and leaving the very samādhi of every buddha's essential nature, illuminating in reverse and in accord, before and after, cause and fruit, emptiness and existence, and the meaning of the ultimate middle way itself — this wisdom only the eighth ground can attain; no lower ground can reach it. Unmoving and unarriving, neither leaving nor entering, neither arising nor ceasing — the dharma-gates of this ground are boundless, boundless, inexplicable, inexplicable. What is opened here is no more than a hundred-thousandth part, the width of a single hair, of this ground — this has already been made clear in the Chapter on the Arhat."

Ru-Yi's reading note

This is the moment the course's plain-English word "emptiness" turns out to have been standing in for a far more differentiated technical vocabulary all along. The formal analysis of emptiness into distinct kinds — inner, outer, conditioned, unconditioned, and so on down to "the emptiness that empties even the emptying of emptiness" — is the same territory Nāgārjuna's school catalogued at far greater length; ten gateways here is already a compression. And "speaking of a kalpa as no kalpa, of a buddha as no buddha" is the Diamond Sūtra's own "is not X, that is why it is called X" formula, run without a single softening word, on the largest and smallest units of time and the very figure of the Buddha himself.

9. Milestone: The Ground of Flower Adornment 體性華嚴地

The ninth ground pushes the negation-and-restoration structure familiar from earlier sessions to its most extreme case yet — not merit, not a name, but the boundary between the realm of demons and the realm of a buddha itself.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！菩提薩埵佛花嚴體性地，以佛威儀，如來三昧自在王王定出入無時，於十方三千世界中百億日月百億四天下，一時成佛、轉法輪乃至滅度。一切佛事以一心中一時示現一切眾生，一切色身八十種好三十二相自在，樂虛空同。無量大悲，光明相好莊嚴，非天非人非六道。一切法外而常行六道，現無量身、無量口、無量意，說無量法門，而能轉魔界入佛界、佛界入魔界，復轉一切見入佛見、佛見入一切見，佛性入眾生性、眾生性入佛性。其地光光光照、慧慧照、明焰明焰，無畏、無量、十力、十八不共法、解脫涅槃無為一道清淨。而以一切眾生作父母兄弟，為其說法盡一切劫得道果。又現一切國土，為一切眾生相視如父如母，天魔外道相視如父母。住是地中，從生死際起至金剛際，以一念心中現如是事，而能轉入無量眾生界。如是無量，略說如海一渧。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva in the Ground of Flower Adornment holds the very dignity of a buddha — the sovereign king of the Tathāgata's own samādhi, entering and leaving it at no fixed time, and within the three thousand worlds of the ten directions, among a hundred million suns and moons and a hundred million of the four continents, becomes a buddha, turns the dharma-wheel, and enters final nirvāṇa, all at once. Every deed of a buddha is shown, in a single mind, to every being at the same time — every body, its eighty subtle marks and thirty-two signs, moving with total freedom, equal to the whole of empty space. With boundless great compassion, radiant with every mark of adornment, this being is neither a god nor a human nor any being of the six destinies at all, and yet, standing outside every dharma, constantly moves through the six destinies, manifesting boundless bodies, boundless speech, boundless minds, teaching boundless dharma-gates — able to turn the realm of demons into the realm of the buddha, and the realm of the buddha into the realm of demons; every view turned into the buddha's own seeing, and the buddha's own seeing turned into every view; buddha-nature entering the nature of sentient beings, and the nature of sentient beings entering buddha-nature. This ground shines with light upon light, wisdom upon wisdom, its radiance blazing upon radiance — fearless, boundless, holding the ten powers, the eighteen unshared qualities, liberation, and nirvāṇa as a single unconditioned path, wholly pure. Taking every being as a father, a mother, a brother, this being teaches the dharma across every kalpa until each one attains the fruit of the path; and appearing, too, in every land, regards every being — even demons and those of other paths — as a father or mother would. Abiding in this ground, from the very edge of birth-and-death to the very edge of the diamond itself, this being manifests every such thing within a single thought, and can turn and enter boundless realms of beings in just this way. All of this, immeasurable as it is, has only been briefly said here — like a single drop drawn from the ocean."

Ru-Yi's reading note

Every other negation in this course — merit is not merit, a bodhisattva is not a fixed thing called a bodhisattva, a universe is not truly one or many — has stopped short of touching the boundary between good and its opposite. This ground does not stop short: the realm of demons turned into the realm of a buddha, and the realm of a buddha turned into the realm of demons, stated as plainly as everything else in the sūtra's repeated formula. Read uncharitably, that sounds like it erases the difference between the two. Read the way the rest of this course has trained you to read it, it is the same claim made everywhere else — that even "demon" and "buddha" are labels without a fixed, separate essence behind them — pushed, deliberately, to the one case most readers would want to treat as an exception.

10. Milestone: The Ground of Entering the Buddha's Realm 體性入佛界地

The tenth and final ground closes the sequence by explaining, one at a time, the ten traditional epithets of a buddha — words recited daily in East Asian Buddhist liturgy, here given an etymology grounded in what the nine grounds before this one have already built.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！菩提薩埵入佛界體性地，其大慧空，空復空空復空，如虛空性。平等智有如來性，十功德品具足，空同一相體性無為，神虛體一法同法性，故名如來。應順四諦二諦，盡生死輪際，法養法身無二，是名應供。遍覆一切世界中一切事，正智聖解脫智，知一切法有無一切眾生根故，是正遍知。明明修行佛果時足故，是明行足。善逝三世佛法，法同先佛去佛，去時善善、來時善善，是名善逝。是人行是上德，入世間中教化眾生，使眾生解脫一切結縛，故名世間解脫。是人一切法上入佛威神，儀形如佛大士行處，為世間解脫，名無上士。調順一切眾生，名為丈夫。於天人中教化一切眾生，諮受法言故，是天人師。妙本無二、佛性玄覺，常常大滿，一切眾生禮拜故、尊敬故，是佛世尊。一切人諮受奉教故，是佛地。是地中一切聖人之所入處故，名佛界地。爾時坐寶蓮花上，一切與授記歡喜。法身手摩其頂，同見同學菩薩異口同音讚歎無二。又有百千億世界中一切佛、一切菩薩一時雲集，請轉不可說法輪、虛空藏化導法門。是地有不可說奇妙法門品，奇妙三明、三昧門、陀羅尼門，非下地凡夫心識所知，唯佛佛無量身口心意可盡其原。如光音天品中說十無畏與佛道同。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha: a bodhisattva-mahāsattva entering the Ground of Entering the Buddha's Realm holds a great wisdom that is empty — empty, and emptied again of that very emptiness, emptied once more, like empty space itself. With the wisdom of equality and the nature of the Tathāgata, ten kinds of merit stand complete. Since emptiness bears a single mark, the essential nature is unconditioned; spirit and emptiness, one substance; dharma and dharma-nature, the very same — and so this being is called Tathāgata, the Thus-Come. According with the four truths and the two truths, exhausting the very wheel of birth-and-death, nourished by the dharma, the dharma-body itself not two — and so is called Worthy of Offerings. Covering every matter in every realm at once, with right wisdom and the sacred wisdom of liberation, knowing what exists and does not exist in every dharma, and the very faculties of every being — and so is called Perfectly and Completely Awakened. Luminous wisdom and luminous practice, at the stage of buddhahood, brought to their fullest — and so is called Perfect in Wisdom and Conduct. Well-gone through the dharma of the buddhas of the three times, this dharma the very same as every buddha before — leaving well, and well, and arriving well, and well — and so is called Well-Gone. Carrying out this highest virtue, entering the world to teach and transform beings, freeing them from every bond — and so is called Knower of the World. Entering, above every dharma, the very dignity of a buddha, its bearing exactly a buddha's own, the very place a great being walks, freeing the world — and so is called Unsurpassed One. Taming every being — and so is called the Tamer. Teaching every being among gods and humans, receiving from this being the very words of dharma — and so is called Teacher of Gods and Humans. The wondrous root, without a second, buddha-nature itself, the mysterious awakening, always, always, wholly full — every being bows before it, every being honors it — and so is called Buddha, the World-Honored One. Every person in the world receives and upholds its teaching — and so it is called the ground of buddhahood; it is the ground every sage enters — and so it is called the ground of the buddha-realm. At that time, seated on a jeweled lotus, every being received the prediction of buddhahood and rejoiced; the dharma-body reached out to touch each crown, and bodhisattvas who shared this same vision and study, with a hundred different voices, praised it as one — without a second. And in a hundred thousand million worlds, every buddha and every bodhisattva gathered like clouds at once, asking that the inexplicable wheel of dharma be turned, the dharma-gate of the treasury of empty space itself opened for their guidance. This ground holds dharma-gates beyond telling, beyond telling — wondrous threefold clarity, samādhi, and dhāraṇī, none of it known to the minds of ordinary beings in the lower grounds; only a buddha's own boundless body, speech, and mind can reach its very source. As is explained in the Chapter on the Heaven of Light and Sound, the ten fearlessnesses are one and the same as the buddha's own path."

Ru-Yi's reading note

These ten epithets — Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings (arhat), Perfectly and Completely Awakened, and the rest — are recited at the opening of nearly every East Asian Buddhist service, usually as pure honorifics. What this ground actually does is give each one an origin: Tathāgata because dharma and dharma-nature are the same; Worthy of Offerings because the dharma-body is undivided; Well-Gone because arrival and departure are each, in their own way, already complete. Read this way, the titles stop being decoration and become a compressed description of exactly what the preceding nine grounds' practice adds up to — the same shift from honorific to description that this course has been making, since Session 1, with words like "bodhisattva" and "emptiness" themselves.

11. Milestone: Vairocana's Transmission, and What Comes Next

With the tenth ground complete, the sūtra shifts from teaching to transmission: Vairocana Buddha hands the entire Mind Ground chapter to a thousand emanated buddhas, who scatter to teach it stage by stage in every heaven — the narrative hinge marking, in the sūtra's own traditional division, the close of its first two rolls and the opening of its third.

Chinese Source

爾時，盧舍那佛為此大眾，略開百千恆河沙、不可說法門中心地，如毛頭許。「是過去一切佛已說，未來佛當說，現在佛今說，三世菩薩已學、當學、今學。我已百劫修行是心地，號吾為盧舍那。汝諸佛，轉我所說，與一切眾生開心地道。」時蓮花臺藏世界赫赫天光師子座上，盧舍那佛放光光，告千華上佛：「持我心地法門品而去，復轉為千百億釋迦，及一切眾生，次第說我上心地法門品，汝等受持讀誦，一心而行。」爾時千華上佛、千百億釋迦，從蓮花臺藏世界赫赫師子座起，各各辭退，舉身放不可思議光光，皆化無量佛，一時以無量青黃赤白華，供養盧舍那佛，受持上說心地法門品竟，各各從此蓮花臺藏世界而沒。沒已，入體性虛空華光三昧，還本源世界閻浮提菩提樹下，從體性虛空華光三昧出。出已，方坐金剛千光王座及妙光堂，說十世界海。復從座起，至帝釋宮說十住。復從座起，至燄天中說十行，復從座起，至第四天中說十迴向。復從座起，至化樂天說十禪定。復從座起，至他化天說十地。復至一禪中說十金剛。復至二禪中說十忍。復至三禪中說十願。復至四禪中摩醯首羅天王宮，說我本源蓮花臺藏世界盧舍那佛所說心地法門品。其餘千百億釋迦，亦復如是無二無別，如賢劫品中說。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Vairocana Buddha, for the sake of this whole assembly, briefly opened up the Mind Ground — set within dharma-gates beyond telling, as numerous as the grains of sand in a hundred thousand Ganges rivers — down to the width of a single hair. "This is what every buddha of the past has already taught, what every buddha of the future will teach, and what every buddha of the present now teaches — what bodhisattvas across the three times have already studied, will study, and are studying now. I myself have cultivated this Mind Ground for a hundred kalpas, and so I am named Vairocana. Buddhas, all of you: carry forward what I have taught, and open this path of the Mind Ground for every being." Then, seated upon the blazing lion-throne of heavenly light in the Lotus Treasury World, Vairocana Buddha released light upon light, and spoke to the thousand buddhas seated on the lotus petals: "Take up this chapter on the dharma-gate of the Mind Ground, and go. Become, in turn, a hundred million Śākyamunis, and to every being, in sequence, teach this same chapter on the Mind Ground that I have just taught. Uphold it, recite it, and practise it with a single mind."

With this, the thousand buddhas on the lotus petals, and the hundred million Śākyamunis, rose from the blazing lion-thrones of the Lotus Treasury World and each took their leave, their whole bodies releasing inconceivable light, each ray itself becoming countless further buddhas. At once, with countless blue, yellow, red, and white lotus flowers, they made offerings to Vairocana Buddha, and having received and upheld this chapter on the Mind Ground just taught, each departed from the Lotus Treasury World.

Having departed, each entered the samādhi of the essential nature's own empty flower-light, and returned to their own original world, beneath the bodhi tree in Jambudvīpa. Emerging from that samādhi, each sat upon a throne lit by a thousand adamantine rays, in a hall of wondrous radiance, and taught the ten world-oceans. Rising again, each went to Indra's palace and taught the ten abodes; rising again, to the Heaven of Flame, and taught the ten practices; rising again, to the fourth heaven, and taught the ten dedications; rising again, to the Heaven of Transformed Bliss, and taught the ten concentrations; rising again, to the Heaven of Mastery Over Others' Transformations, and taught the ten grounds. Further, in the first meditation heaven, they taught the ten vajra minds; in the second meditation heaven, the ten forms of patience; in the third meditation heaven, the ten vows; and in the fourth meditation heaven, in the palace of King Maheśvara himself, they taught this very chapter on the Mind Ground first spoken by Vairocana Buddha in our own original world, the Lotus Treasury. And every one of the hundred million other Śākyamunis did exactly the same, without the slightest difference — as is told fully in the Chapter of the Fortunate Aeon.

Ru-Yi's reading note

This is where the sūtra's own traditional structure actually turns a page. Everything translated across these six chapters — the Mind Ground's meaning, the four heavens' worth of narrative, and all forty stages from the Ten Minds of Development through this final ground — belongs to what the tradition calls the sūtra's first two rolls. What follows, not yet translated on this site, is the third roll: the Grave Precepts, the ten major and forty-eight minor precepts this entire forty-stage path has been building toward, the specific rules of conduct most people mean when they say "the bodhisattva precepts." That translation is still ahead. It is worth sitting, before moving on, with what this chapter has just spent ten grounds establishing: everything the precepts will ask of a bodhisattva is asked of someone who has already, at least in principle, walked this entire path of wisdom first. The rules are not the starting point. They come last, addressed to a mind this whole sūtra has spent five prior chapters trying to prepare.
