Chapter 7 · 梵網經 · The Lower Fascicle Opens

The Precept Preface, and the First Five Grave Precepts

Chapter 6 closed with Vairocana Buddha entrusting the Mind Ground teaching to a thousand emanated buddhas, who carried it stage by stage through every heaven — the close of the sūtra's entire upper fascicle. The lower fascicle opens here: first with the precept preface still recited before any formal bestowal of these precepts today, then with Śākyamuni's own retelling of the same transmission in his own voice, culminating in the sūtra's most famous verse. What follows is the opening of the ten grave precepts themselves — the specific rules of conduct most people mean when they say "the bodhisattva precepts" — beginning with the first five: killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech, and selling intoxicants.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: translation in progress Source: Taishō T24, no. 1484 Reading notes: Ru-Yi's own, cross-checked against A. Charles Muller & Kenneth Tanaka's scholarly translation

1. Milestone: The Precept Preface 梵網經菩薩戒序

Before the sūtra's narrative resumes, the text opens with a short liturgical preface — still recited today, essentially unchanged, before any formal bestowal of the bodhisattva precepts. It begins not with instruction but with an invitation to confess.

Chinese Source

諸佛子等。合掌至心聽。我今欲說諸佛大戒序。眾集默然聽。自知有罪當懺悔。懺悔即安樂。不懺悔罪益深。無罪者默然。默然故當知眾清淨。諸大德優婆塞優婆夷等諦聽。佛滅度後於像法中。應當尊敬波羅提木叉。波羅提木叉者即是此戒。持此戒時如暗遇明如貧得寶如病得差如囚繫出獄如遠行者得歸。當知此則是眾等大師。若佛住世無異此也。怖心難生善心難發。故經云。勿輕小罪以為無殃。水滴雖微漸盈大器。剎那造罪殃墮無間。一失人身萬劫不復。壯色不停猶如奔馬。人命無常過於山水。今日雖存明亦難保。眾等各各一心勤修精進。慎勿懈怠懶惰睡眠縱意。夜即攝心存念三寶。莫以空過徒設疲勞後代深悔。眾等各各一心謹依此戒。如法修行應當學。

Ru-Yi Translation

All you disciples of the Buddha — join your palms, and listen with your whole heart. I wish now to speak the preface to the great precepts of all buddhas. Let the assembly gather in silence and listen. Whoever knows their own fault should now confess it; confession itself brings peace, while an unconfessed fault only grows deeper. Whoever is without fault should remain silent — and by that silence, know the assembly to be pure. Elders, laymen, and laywomen — listen closely. After the Buddha's final passing, in the age of the semblance dharma, you should honor the prātimokṣa above all else. The prātimokṣa is nothing other than this very precept. To hold this precept is like meeting light after darkness, like a poor man finding treasure, like the sick recovering, like a prisoner released from bondage, like a traveler far from home finally returning. Know that this precept is your own great teacher, no different than if the Buddha himself still dwelled in the world. A fearful mind is hard to rouse, and a wholesome mind is hard to bring forth. And so the sūtra says: do not treat a small fault lightly, thinking it brings no harm — a single drop of water, however small, will in time fill a great vessel. A single instant's wrongdoing can bring one down into the hell without interval; and once a human body is lost, ten thousand kalpas will not bring it back. The bloom of youth does not linger — it runs like a startled horse. A human life is more uncertain than a mountain stream; though you are alive today, tomorrow is hard to promise. Each of you, with a single mind, should practise diligently — take care not to grow lax, lazy, drowsy, or indulgent. At night, gather in your mind and hold the Three Jewels always in thought. Do not let this life pass by in vain, only to labor uselessly and regret it bitterly in a life to come. Each of you, with a single mind, should carefully rely upon this precept, and practise according to the dharma — this you should learn.

Ru-Yi's reading note

Notice what this opens with: not accusation, but an invitation to confess, followed immediately by the reassurance that confession itself "brings peace." An assembly listens in silence; anyone with a fault names it; and silence itself, at the end, becomes the evidence of purity — no one is asked to prove their innocence, only to not hide a fault they already know they carry. The urgency about time that follows — youth running "like a startled horse," a life "more uncertain than a mountain stream" — is not rhetorical decoration. It is the sūtra's own reason for insisting, later in this chapter, that these precepts be recited twice a month rather than received once and set on a shelf.

2. Milestone: The Narrative Resumes, and the Sūtra's Most Famous Verse

The lower fascicle's narrative opens by repeating, almost verbatim, the scene that closed Chapter 6 — Vairocana's transmission and the thousand buddhas' journey through every heaven. Rather than translate that repetition twice, this milestone picks up where the sūtra adds something genuinely new: Śākyamuni Buddha's own retelling of that same transmission, in his own voice and his own biography, culminating in the verse chanted at ordinations across East Asia to this day.

Chinese Source

爾時釋迦牟尼佛，從初現蓮花臺藏世界，東方來入天王宮中，說魔受化經已，下生南閻浮提迦夷羅國。母名摩耶，父字白淨，吾名悉達。七歲出家，三十成道，號吾為釋迦牟尼佛。於寂滅道場，坐金剛花光王座，乃至摩醯首羅天王宮，其中次第十住處所說。時佛觀諸大梵天王網羅幢，因為說：無量世界猶如網孔，一一世界各各不同，別異無量，佛教門亦復如是。吾今來此世界八千返，為此娑婆世界坐金剛花光王座，乃至摩醯首羅天王宮，為是中一切大眾略開心地法門品竟。復從天王宮下至閻浮提菩提樹下，為此地上一切眾生凡夫痴闇之人，說我本盧舍那佛心地中，初發心中常所誦一戒光明。金剛寶戒是一切佛本源，一切菩薩本源，佛性種子。一切眾生皆有佛性，一切意識色心是情是心，皆入佛性戒中。當當常有因故，有當當常住法身。如是十波羅提木叉，出於世界，是法戒是三世一切眾生頂戴受持。吾今當為此大眾重說十無盡藏戒品，是一切眾生戒本源自性清淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Śākyamuni Buddha said: "From my first manifestation in the Lotus Treasury World, I came from the east into the palace of the heavenly king, and having finished teaching the Sūtra on Converting Demons, I descended to be born in the country of Kapilavastu in the southern continent of Jambudvīpa. My mother was named Māyā, my father Śuddhodana, and I myself was named Siddhārtha. At seven I left home, and at thirty I attained the path, taking the name Śākyamuni Buddha. Seated at the site of stillness and extinction upon the throne of the adamantine flower-light king, I taught, in sequence, at all ten dwelling-places up through the palace of King Maheśvara. At that time the Buddha, contemplating the bannered net of the great Brahma kings, spoke of this: countless worlds are just like the holes of that net — each single world different from every other, distinct without limit, and the Buddha's own teaching gateways are exactly the same. I have now come to this world eight thousand times. For the sake of this sahā world, I sat upon the throne of the adamantine flower-light king, all the way through the palace of King Maheśvara, and for the great assembly there, briefly opened the chapter on the dharma-gate of the Mind Ground. Now, descending from that heavenly king's palace, down to beneath the bodhi tree in Jambudvīpa, for the sake of every being on this ground — every ordinary being, every being still in the darkness of delusion — I speak the one radiant precept I have always recited from my first arousal of mind, within Vairocana Buddha's own Mind Ground: this luminous, vajra-like, precious precept is the very source of every Buddha and the very source of every bodhisattva — the seed of buddha-nature itself. Every being without exception has buddha-nature; every form of consciousness, whether sentient or merely mental, enters into this same precept of buddha-nature. And because there is always, constantly, a cause here, there is always, constantly, an abiding dharma-body. These ten prātimokṣa have thus come forth into the world. This dharma-precept is upheld and carried on the crown of every being across the three times. I shall now, for the sake of this great assembly, speak again the chapter of the ten inexhaustible treasury precepts — the very source of every being's precept, pure in its own nature."

Chinese Source · Verse

我今盧舍那　方坐蓮花臺

周匝千花上　復現千釋迦

一花百億國　一國一釋迦

各坐菩提樹　一時成佛道

如是千百億　盧舍那本身

千百億釋迦　各接微塵眾

俱來至我所　聽我誦佛戒

甘露門則開　是時千百億

還至本道場　各坐菩提樹

誦我本師戒　十重四十八

戒如明日月　亦如瓔珞珠

微塵菩薩眾　由是成正覺

是盧舍那誦　我亦如是誦

汝新學菩薩　頂戴受持戒

受持是戒已　轉授諸眾生

諦聽我正誦　佛法中戒藏

波羅提木叉　大眾心諦信

汝是當成佛　我是已成佛

常作如是信　戒品已具足

一切有心者　皆應攝佛戒

位同大覺已　真是諸佛子

大眾皆恭敬　至心聽我誦

Ru-Yi Translation · Verse

I, now Vairocana,

sit upon the lotus platform;
circling on a thousand flowers above,
a thousand Śākyamunis appear once more.
Each flower, a hundred million lands;
each land, one Śākyamuni;
each seated beneath his own bodhi tree,
all at once attaining the path.

Such are these hundred million,
Vairocana's own original body;
these hundred million Śākyamunis,
each gathering beings numerous as motes of dust,
all come together to where I am,
to hear me recite the buddhas' precepts.
The gate of ambrosia then opens wide.

At this time, the hundred million
each return to their own place of practice,
each sit beneath their own bodhi tree,
and recite the precepts of our root teacher:
ten grave, and forty-eight minor.
These precepts are like the bright sun and moon,
and like a string of pearls;
bodhisattvas numerous as motes of dust
attain true awakening through them.

As Vairocana recites,
so I too now recite.
You, bodhisattvas newly training,
receive and uphold this precept upon your crown.
Having received and upheld this precept,
pass it on in turn to every being.
Listen closely as I recite it truly —
the treasury of precepts within the buddha-dharma.

Prātimokṣa —
let the whole assembly believe this with a true mind:
you are a buddha yet to be,
I am a buddha already become.
Constantly holding this very faith,
the precepts stand already complete.
Every being who has a mind at all
should hold fast to this precept of the buddhas.

When a being receives the buddhas' precept, they enter directly into the rank of every buddha — a rank the same as great awakening itself, truly a child of the buddhas. Let the whole assembly show reverence, and listen with a whole heart as I recite.

Ru-Yi's reading note

Look closely at "this luminous, vajra-like, precious precept is the very source of every Buddha and the very source of every bodhisattva — the seed of buddha-nature itself." This is nearly word for word the line Chapter 1 spent an entire milestone unpacking as the whole chapter's thesis — "光明金剛寶戒是一切佛本源." The sūtra is telling you directly, six chapters later, that the precepts you are about to receive are not a new teaching stacked on top of everything already translated on this site. They are the very same "Mind Ground" Chapter 1 opened with, now finally spoken in the specific, enumerated form of actual rules. And the verse's own turning point — "you are a buddha yet to be, I am a buddha already become" — compresses Chapter 1's entire treatment of the Six Identities With Buddha into a single line, addressed directly to whoever is reading it.

3. Milestone: The Ten Prātimokṣa Are Opened

Before naming the precepts one by one, the sūtra frames who may receive them — a list notable for what it does not restrict.

Chinese Source

爾時釋迦牟尼佛，初坐菩提樹下，成無上覺，初結菩薩波羅提木叉。孝順父母師僧三寶，孝順至道之法，孝名為戒，亦名制止。佛即口放無量光明，是時百萬億大眾諸菩薩，十八梵天六欲天子，十六大國王，合掌至心聽佛誦一切佛大乘戒。佛告諸菩薩言：「我今半月半月，自誦諸佛法戒，汝等一切發心菩薩亦誦，乃至十發趣十長養十金剛十地，諸菩薩亦誦。是故戒光從口出，有緣非無因故。光光非青黃赤白黑，非色非心，非有非無，非因果法，是諸佛之本源，菩薩之根本，是大眾諸佛子之根本。是故大眾諸佛子應受持，應讀誦，善學。佛子諦聽：若受佛戒者，國王王子百官宰相，比丘比丘尼，十八梵天六欲天子，庶民黃門淫男淫女奴婢，八部鬼神金剛神畜生乃至變化人，但解法師語，盡受得戒，皆名第一清淨者。」佛告諸佛子言：「有十重波羅提木叉，若受菩薩戒不誦此戒者，非菩薩非佛種子。我亦如是誦，一切菩薩已學，一切菩薩當學，一切菩薩今學。已略說菩薩波羅提木叉相貌，是事應當學，敬心奉持。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Śākyamuni Buddha, having just sat beneath the bodhi tree and attained unsurpassed awakening, first established the bodhisattva prātimokṣa: filial devotion to one's parents, teachers, monastics, and the Three Jewels. Filial devotion is itself the dharma of the highest path — this filial devotion is what is called precept, and is also called restraint. The Buddha then released boundless light from his own mouth. At that time a great assembly of a million million bodhisattvas, the eighteen Brahma heavens, the six heavens of desire, and sixteen great kings, joined their palms and listened with their whole hearts as the Buddha recited the great vehicle precepts of all buddhas. The Buddha told the bodhisattvas: "I myself now recite the dharma-precepts of all buddhas twice each month. You — every bodhisattva who has set out on the path — should recite them too, all the way through the bodhisattvas of the Ten Minds of Development, the Ten Minds of Long Nurturing, the Ten Vajra Minds, and the Ten Grounds. And so this radiance of the precept comes forth from my very mouth — for it arises from real cause, not from nothing. This light is not blue, yellow, red, white, or black; it is neither form nor mind; neither existent nor nonexistent; not a dharma of cause and effect at all. It is the very source of every buddha, the very root of every bodhisattva, the very root of every one of you, disciples of the Buddha in this great assembly. And so this whole assembly, every disciple of the Buddha, should uphold it, recite it, and study it well. Disciples of the Buddha, listen closely: whoever receives this precept — a king, a prince, a minister of state, a monk, a nun, one of the eighteen Brahma heavens or six heavens of desire, a commoner, one born without full faculties, a man or woman given to lust, a manservant or maidservant, one of the eight kinds of spirit, a vajra spirit, an animal, or even a being manifested by transformation — so long as they understand the words of their teacher, every one of them fully receives the precept, and every one is called foremost in purity." The Buddha told the disciples of the Buddha: "There are ten grave prātimokṣa. Whoever receives the bodhisattva precepts and does not recite these precepts is no bodhisattva, and no seed of buddhahood at all. I too recite them in just this way. Every bodhisattva has already studied them; every bodhisattva will study them; every bodhisattva is studying them now. I have now briefly described the features of the bodhisattva prātimokṣa — this you should study, and uphold with a reverent mind."

Ru-Yi's reading note

Read the list of who may receive these precepts slowly: kings and commoners, monastics and laypeople, someone the text names as "born without full faculties," even animals and spirits, "so long as they understand the words spoken to them." Nothing here restricts the teaching by rank, ordination status, gender, or species — the sūtra's own only stated qualification is comprehension. And note the phrase "no seed of buddhahood at all" for someone who takes the precepts without reciting them: receiving these rules is framed here as inseparable from actually returning to them, twice a month, for as long as one holds them — not a one-time transaction.

4. Milestone: The First Grave Precept — Killing 快意殺生戒

The precepts open with what the tradition treats as the gravest possible harm: the taking of a life.

Chinese Source

佛言：「佛子！若自殺，教人殺，方便讚歎殺，見作隨喜，乃至咒殺。殺因、殺緣、殺法、殺業，乃至一切有命者不得故殺。是菩薩應起常住慈悲心、孝順心，方便救護一切眾生，而自恣心快意殺生者，是菩薩波羅夷罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said: "Disciples of the Buddha — if you kill with your own hand, direct another to kill, kill by some indirect means, praise killing, take pleasure in seeing a killing take place, or kill through incantation; if you engage in the cause of killing, the condition of killing, the method of killing, or the very act of killing — this extends to every living being without exception: none may be killed with intent. A bodhisattva should instead give rise to an enduring mind of compassion and filial devotion, using every skillful means to save and protect every being. But if, on the contrary, a bodhisattva indulges their own mind and takes pleasure in killing a living being, this is a bodhisattva's pārājika offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

The precept names six distinct ways of being implicated in a single killing — doing it yourself, directing it, arranging it by indirect means, praising it, delighting in watching it, or accomplishing it through incantation — treating incitement and approval as gravely as the act itself. Pārājika (translated here by its Sanskrit term, matching the sūtra's own usage) names the most severe category of offense the tradition recognizes. Set against everything already translated in this course and series: this is the first fully concrete answer, seven chapters and four course sessions in, to the question every prior text has circled — what, in the end, is all this teaching about "no fixed self" actually for.

5. Milestone: The Second Grave Precept — Stealing 劫盜人物戒

Chinese Source

「若佛子！自盜，教人盜，方便盜。盜因、盜緣、盜法、盜業，咒盜，乃至鬼神有主，劫賊物，一切財物一針一草不得故盜。而菩薩應生佛性孝順慈悲心，常助一切人生福生樂，而反更盜人財物者，是菩薩波羅夷罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — if you steal with your own hand, direct another to steal, or steal by some indirect means; if you engage in the cause of stealing, the condition of stealing, the method of stealing, the very act of stealing, or steal through incantation — this extends even to the property of spirits, to what belongs to another, or to property already stolen by a thief: not a single needle, not a single blade of grass may be taken with intent to steal. A bodhisattva should instead give rise to the buddha-nature mind of filial devotion and compassion, constantly helping every person toward well-being and joy. But if, on the contrary, a bodhisattva steals another's wealth or possessions, this is a bodhisattva's pārājika offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

"Not a single needle, not a single blade of grass" is deliberately, almost startlingly small — the precept is not calibrated to the value of what is taken, only to the fact of taking what was not given. And notice what it does not exempt: the property of a thief. The sūtra does not treat stolen goods as fair game a second time; whatever already belongs to someone else, by whatever means, is still off-limits.

6. Milestone: The Third Grave Precept — Sexual Misconduct 無慈行欲戒

Chinese Source

「若佛子！自淫，教人淫，乃至一切女人不得故淫。淫因、淫緣、淫法、淫業，乃至畜生女、諸天鬼神女，及非道行淫。而菩薩應生孝順心，救度一切眾生，淨法與人，而反更起一切人淫，不擇畜生乃至母女姊妹六親行淫，無慈悲心者，是菩薩波羅夷罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — if you engage in lustful conduct with your own body, or direct another to engage in lustful conduct — this extends to every woman without exception: none may be violated with intent. If you engage in the cause of lustful conduct, the condition of lustful conduct, the method of lustful conduct, or the very act of lustful conduct — this extends even to a female animal, or a female spirit or celestial being, or to conduct through means entirely improper. A bodhisattva should instead give rise to a mind of filial devotion, saving and freeing every being, and offering the pure dharma to others. But if, on the contrary, a bodhisattva indulges in lustful conduct with anyone at all — making no distinction even of an animal, or a mother, daughter, sister, or other close relative — with no mind of compassion whatsoever, this is a bodhisattva's pārājika offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

This precept names no exception for a close relative — the text states the case explicitly rather than leaving it assumed. What the bodhisattva is asked to offer instead is described in specific terms: "the pure dharma," a source of freedom for another person, not harm. The structural shape is the same one running through every precept in this chapter — a named wrong, paired with the specific wholesome mind a bodhisattva is expected to bring in its place — but this is the precept where that pairing is drawn most starkly, care set directly against its most complete absence.

7. Milestone: The Fourth Grave Precept — False Speech 故心妄語戒

Chinese Source

「若佛子！自妄語，教人妄語，方便妄語。妄語因、妄語緣、妄語法、妄語業，乃至不見言見，見言不見，身心妄語。而菩薩常生正語正見，亦生一切眾生正語正見，而反更起一切眾生邪語邪見邪業者，是菩薩波羅夷罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — if you speak falsely with your own mouth, direct another to speak falsely, or speak falsely by some indirect means; if you engage in the cause of false speech, the condition of false speech, the method of false speech, or the very act of false speech — this extends even to saying you have seen what you have not seen, or saying you have not seen what you have seen, whether the falsehood is spoken through the body or held only in the mind. A bodhisattva should instead constantly give rise to right speech and right view, and lead every being to right speech and right view as well. But if, on the contrary, a bodhisattva leads any being into wrong speech, wrong view, or wrong action, this is a bodhisattva's pārājika offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

"Whether the falsehood is spoken through the body or held only in the mind" is worth pausing on — the precept recognizes a lie told through gesture without a single word, and even a private, wordless act of sustaining someone else's wrong impression. Set beside the Diamond Sūtra's own repeated formula from earlier sessions — is not X, that is why it is called X — this precept is precise about the same gap between what is said and what is true, though the register here is entirely different: not a metaphysical claim about language, but an ordinary, literal insistence on saying what actually happened.

8. Milestone: The Fifth Grave Precept — Selling Intoxicants 酤酒生罪戒

Chinese Source

「若佛子！自酤酒，教人酤酒。酤酒因、酤酒緣、酤酒法、酤酒業，一切酒不得酤，是酒起罪因緣。而菩薩應生一切眾生明達之慧，而反更生一切眾生顛倒之心者，是菩薩波羅夷罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — if you sell intoxicating drink with your own hand, or direct another to sell it; if you engage in the cause of selling drink, the condition of selling drink, the method of selling drink, or the very act of selling drink — no intoxicating drink of any kind may be sold, for drink itself is the very cause and condition of wrongdoing. A bodhisattva should instead give rise to clear and penetrating wisdom in every being. But if, on the contrary, a bodhisattva gives rise to a confused and inverted mind in any being, this is a bodhisattva's pārājika offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

This is usually the precept that surprises a Western reader most, ranked as gravely as killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, and lying. The sūtra's own reasoning is worth taking at face value rather than reading past: the precept does not forbid drinking so much as it forbids selling — profiting from an act that clouds someone else's clarity of mind, named explicitly as "the very cause and condition of wrongdoing." Read this way, the precept is less about personal restraint than about not becoming the supply line for other people's loss of judgment — a harm one step removed from the act itself, which is exactly why the sūtra insists on naming it as gravely as the others.

Chapter 8 continues directly from here with the remaining five grave precepts — speaking of others' faults, self-praise and disparaging others, withholding generosity, harboring anger, and slandering the Three Jewels — closing the full set of ten before the sūtra turns to the forty-eight minor precepts that follow.
