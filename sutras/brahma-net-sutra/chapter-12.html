Chapter 12 · 梵網經 · Minor Precepts 31–39

Nine More Minor Precepts

This chapter's group runs to nine precepts rather than ten, following the sūtra's own internal division. It opens with ransoming captive Buddhists and sacred images, moves through right livelihood and monastic conduct, and includes two of the sūtra's most enduring passages: the vow to uphold the precepts "like vajra," and the Ten Great Vows still chanted in East Asian Buddhist liturgy today.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: translation in progress Source: Taishō T24, no. 1484 Reading notes: Ru-Yi's own, cross-checked against A. Charles Muller & Kenneth Tanaka's scholarly translation

1. Milestone: Minor Precept 31 — Ransoming Captive Buddhists and Sacred Images

Chinese Source

佛言：「佛子！佛滅度後於惡世中，若見外道一切惡人劫賊，賣佛菩薩父母形像，販賣經律，販賣比丘比丘尼，亦賣發心菩薩道人，或為官使，與一切人作奴婢者，而菩薩見是事已，應生慈心，方便救護，處處教化，取物贖佛菩薩形像，及比丘比丘尼、發心菩薩、一切經律。若不贖者，犯輕垢罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said: "Disciples of the Buddha — after the Buddha's final passing, in an evil age, if you see outsiders, evildoers, or thieves selling images of the Buddha, a bodhisattva, or one's own parents, trading in the scriptures and discipline, selling monks or nuns, or even a bodhisattva who has set out on the path — whether to serve as a government slave or as anyone's servant — a bodhisattva who witnesses this should give rise to a compassionate mind, using whatever skillful means are available, teaching wherever they go, and using their own means to ransom back the images of the Buddha or a bodhisattva, and to ransom monks, nuns, bodhisattvas who have set out on the path, and the scriptures and discipline themselves. If you fail to ransom them, this is a light offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

This precept reflects a real historical concern rather than a hypothetical one — periods of persecution, war, and banditry across Chinese history genuinely put Buddhist institutions, texts, and people at risk of exactly this kind of loss. The precept turns ransoming from an optional act of charity into an active duty for anyone who happens to witness the sale.

2. Milestone: Minor Precept 32 — Weapons, Exploitation, and Harmful Trade

Chinese Source

「若佛子！不得畜刀仗、弓箭，販賣輕秤、小斗，因官形勢，取人財物，害心繫縛，破壞成功，長養貓狸豬狗。若故作者，犯輕垢罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — you should not keep knives or weapons, bows or arrows; you should not trade using light scales or small measures to cheat others; you should not rely on official power or position to take another's property; you should not bind or confine others with harmful intent, or sabotage another's accomplishments; nor should you raise cats, weasels, pigs, or dogs. If you intentionally do this, this is a light offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

The list groups together several distinct forms of harm — weapons, cheating in trade, abuse of official power, and keeping predatory animals — under one precept. The commentary tradition reads the last item through the same lens as the meat precept from Chapter 9: cats and similar animals kept as pets routinely hunt and kill, implicating their keeper in that harm even without direct intent.

3. Milestone: Minor Precept 33 — Idle and Harmful Diversions

Chinese Source

「若佛子！以惡心故，觀一切男女等鬥，軍陣兵將、劫賊等鬥。亦不得聽吹貝鼓角、琴瑟箏笛、箜篌歌叫伎樂之聲。不得摴蒱圍碁、波羅賽戲、彈碁、六博、拍毬、擲石、投壺，八道行城，爪鏡、蓍草、楊枝、缽盂、髑髏，而作卜筮。不得作盜賊使命，一一不得作。若故作者，犯輕垢罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — you should not, out of an unwholesome mind, watch quarrels between men and women, or battles between armies, soldiers, or thieves. Nor should you listen to the blowing of conches, drums, and horns, or to lutes, zithers, flutes, harps, and the sound of song and performance. You should not gamble at dice, chess, board games, finger-flicking games, backgammon, ball games, stone-throwing, pitch-pot, or the game of the eight roads to the walled city; nor practise divination with a mirror and fingernail, with milfoil stalks, with willow twigs, with a bowl, or with a skull. You should not serve as a messenger for thieves. None of these should be done. If you intentionally do any of them, this is a light offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

The specific games and divination methods named here — several of them now essentially unidentifiable even to specialists — are a small historical window in their own right, a snapshot of ordinary entertainment and folk fortune-telling in the culture this sūtra was compiled and translated within. The underlying concern is more legible than the individual games: attention spent on distraction and speculation about fortune, rather than on practice.

4. Milestone: Minor Precept 34 — Upholding the Precepts Like Vajra

Chinese Source

「若佛子！護持禁戒，行住坐臥，日夜六時，讀誦是戒，猶如金剛，如帶持浮囊，欲度大海，如草繫比丘。常生大乘善信，自知我是未成之佛，諸佛是已成之佛。發菩提心，念念不去心。若起一念二乘外道心者，犯輕垢罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — guard and uphold the precepts, walking, standing, sitting, and lying down, reciting them at all six periods of day and night, holding them as firmly as a diamond, as one who carries an inflated raft to cross a great ocean, as a monk bound by a single blade of grass who will not break free of it. Constantly give rise to genuine faith in the great vehicle, knowing that you yourself are a buddha not yet realized, while the buddhas are buddhas already realized. Give rise to the mind of awakening, and let it never leave your thoughts, moment after moment. If you give rise to even a single thought inclined toward the lesser vehicles or outside paths, this is a light offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

"You yourself are a buddha not yet realized, the buddhas are buddhas already realized" is the same line from Chapter 7's ordination verse, returning here as a lived instruction rather than a recited formula. And "holding the precepts as firmly as a diamond" is the same image Chapter 6's Ground of the Known already used for a mind that does not sink, retreat, fall, or linger — the vajra image threading all the way from the upper fascicle's philosophy into the lower fascicle's daily conduct.

5. Milestone: Minor Precept 35 — Vowing to Seek Good Teachers

Chinese Source

「若佛子！常應發一切願，孝順父母師僧三寶，願得好師、同學、善友知識，常教我大乘經律，十發趣、十長養、十金剛、十地，使我開解，如法修行，堅持佛戒，寧捨身命，念念不去心。若一切菩薩不發是願者，犯輕垢罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — you should constantly make every vow: to be filial and devoted to your parents, teachers, monastics, and the Three Jewels; to obtain a good teacher, fellow students, and good spiritual friends who will constantly teach you the great vehicle's scriptures and discipline — the Ten Minds of Development, the Ten Minds of Long Nurturing, the Ten Vajra Minds, and the Ten Grounds — so that you come to understand them, practise according to the dharma, and firmly hold the Buddha's precepts, willing to give up your very body and life rather than let this vow leave your thoughts, moment after moment. If any bodhisattva fails to make this vow, this is a light offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

This is the third time this specific curriculum — the forty mind-gates translated across Chapters 2 through 6 of this site — has been named directly within the precepts themselves, after precepts 15 and 24. The repetition is not accidental: the sūtra keeps returning to the same insistence that the precepts and the philosophical path are one continuous curriculum, not two separate tracks.

6. Milestone: Minor Precept 36 — The Ten Great Vows

This single precept — despite its length — is counted as one among the forty-eight, and is among the most widely chanted passages of the entire sūtra, recited in ordination ceremonies and daily liturgy across East Asian Buddhism to this day.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！發十大願已，持佛禁戒，作是願言：『寧以此身投熾然猛火大坑刀山，終不毀犯三世諸佛經律，與一切女人作不淨行。』

復作是願：『寧以熱鐵羅網千重周匝纏身，終不以破戒之身，受於信心檀越一切衣服。』

復作是願：『寧以此口吞熱鐵丸，及大流猛火，經百千劫，終不以破戒之口，食信心檀越百味飲食。』

復作是願：『寧以此身臥大猛火羅網、熱鐵地上，終不以破戒之身，受信心檀越百種床座。』

復作是願：『寧以此身受三百鉾刺，經一劫二劫，終不以破戒之身，受信心檀越百味醫藥。』

復作是願：『寧以此身投熱鐵鑊，經百千劫，終不以破戒之身，受信心檀越千種房舍屋宅園林田地。』

復作是願：『寧以鐵鎚打碎此身，從頭至足，令如微塵，終不以破戒之身，受信心檀越恭敬禮拜。』

復作是願：『寧以百千熱鐵刀鉾，挑其兩目，終不以破戒之心，視他好色。』

復作是願：『寧以百千鐵錐，遍剌刺耳根，經一劫二劫，終不以破戒之心，聽好音聲。』

復作是願：『寧以百千刃刀，割去其鼻，終不以破戒之心，貪嗅諸香。』

復作是願：『寧以百千刃刀，割斷其舌，終不以破戒之心，食人百味淨食。』

復作是願：『寧以利斧，斬斫其身，終不以破戒之心，貪著好觸。』

復作是願：『願一切眾生，悉得成佛。』而菩薩若不發是願者，犯輕垢罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — having made the ten great vows, and upholding the Buddha's precepts, one should vow: 'I would rather throw this body into a blazing fire, a great pit, or a mountain of knives, than ever violate the scriptures and discipline of the buddhas of the three times by engaging in impure conduct with any woman.'

'Again I vow: I would rather have this body bound a thousand times over in a net of burning iron, than, with a body that has broken the precepts, accept even a single garment from a devoted patron.'

'Again I vow: I would rather swallow a ball of burning iron, and a great flood of raging fire, for a hundred thousand kalpas, than, with a mouth that has broken the precepts, eat the hundred flavors of food offered by a devoted patron.'

'Again I vow: I would rather lie down upon a net of blazing fire, on ground of burning iron, than, with a body that has broken the precepts, accept the hundred kinds of bedding offered by a devoted patron.'

'Again I vow: I would rather have this body pierced by three hundred spears, for one kalpa or two, than, with a body that has broken the precepts, accept the hundred kinds of medicine offered by a devoted patron.'

'Again I vow: I would rather throw this body into a cauldron of molten iron, for a hundred thousand kalpas, than, with a body that has broken the precepts, accept a thousand kinds of dwelling, garden, or field offered by a devoted patron.'

'Again I vow: I would rather have this body shattered by an iron hammer, from head to foot, ground down to dust, than, with a body that has broken the precepts, accept the reverence and prostrations of a devoted patron.'

'Again I vow: I would rather have my own two eyes gouged out by a hundred thousand burning iron blades, than, with a mind that has broken the precepts, gaze upon another's beauty.'

'Again I vow: I would rather have my own ears pierced through, again and again, by a hundred thousand iron awls, for one kalpa or two, than, with a mind that has broken the precepts, listen to pleasing sound.'

'Again I vow: I would rather have my own nose cut off by a hundred thousand blades, than, with a mind that has broken the precepts, crave the scent of any fragrance.'

'Again I vow: I would rather have my own tongue cut out by a hundred thousand blades, than, with a mind that has broken the precepts, eat the hundred flavors of another's pure food.'

'Again I vow: I would rather have this body hacked apart by a sharp axe, than, with a mind that has broken the precepts, crave any pleasing touch.'

'Again I vow: may every being, without exception, attain buddhahood.' And if a bodhisattva fails to make these vows, this is a light offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

Read past the violence of the imagery to what is actually being weighed against what: on one side, extreme physical suffering; on the other, accepting food, clothing, shelter, or reverence from "a devoted patron" while having broken one's own precepts. The vow is not really about self-harm — it is a statement of priority, insisting that betraying someone who has given in good faith is worse than any suffering the vower could name. The final line breaks the pattern entirely: after eleven vows built on personal sacrifice, the twelfth asks for nothing for oneself at all — only that every being attain buddhahood.

7. Milestone: Minor Precept 37 — Ascetic Discipline and Avoiding Danger

Chinese Source

「若佛子！常應二時頭陀，冬夏坐禪，結夏安居，常用楊枝、澡豆、三衣、瓶、缽、坐具、錫杖、香爐、漉水囊、手巾、刀子、火燧、鑷子、繩床、經律、佛像、菩薩形像。而菩薩行頭陀時，及遊方時，行來百里千里，此十八種物，常隨其身。頭陀者，從正月十五日至三月十五日，八月十五日至十月十五日，是二時中，此十八種物常隨其身，如鳥二翼。若布薩日，新學菩薩，半月半月布薩，誦十重四十八輕戒時，於諸佛菩薩形像前，一人布薩即一人誦，若二人三人，乃至百千人，亦一人誦，誦者高座，聽者下坐，各各披九條、七條、五條袈裟。結夏安居，一一如法。若頭陀時，莫入難處：若國難、惡王，土地高下，草木深邃，師子虎狼，水火風難，及以劫賊、道路毒蛇，一切難處悉不得入。若頭陀行道，乃至夏坐安居，是諸難處悉亦不得入。若故入者，犯輕垢罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — you should always practise ascetic discipline during the two designated periods, and hold meditation retreats in winter and summer, entering the summer retreat with these eighteen things: willow twigs, cleansing powder, the three robes, a water bottle, a bowl, a sitting mat, a walking staff, an incense censer, a water strainer, a hand towel, a knife, a fire striker, tweezers, a folding seat, the sūtras and discipline, an image of the Buddha, and an image of a bodhisattva. Whether practising austerities or travelling from place to place, a hundred or a thousand miles, a bodhisattva should always carry these eighteen things. Ascetic discipline is practised from the fifteenth day of the first month to the fifteenth day of the third month, and from the fifteenth day of the eighth month to the fifteenth day of the tenth month; during these two periods, these eighteen things should stay with the body always, like a bird's own two wings. On the day of the fortnightly recitation, newly training bodhisattvas gather twice a month to recite the ten grave and forty-eight minor precepts before images of the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Whether one person, two, three, or even a thousand are present, only one person recites at a time — the reciter sits in the high seat, the listeners sit below, each wearing a robe of nine, seven, or five panels. The summer retreat should be observed exactly according to the dharma. When practising asceticism, one should not enter dangerous places: not a country in turmoil or ruled by a cruel king, not treacherous terrain or dense wilderness, not places where lions, tigers, or wolves are found, not places threatened by flood, fire, or storm, nor roads haunted by thieves or venomous snakes — none of these dangerous places should ever be entered. Whether practising austerities or observing the summer retreat, these dangerous places should never be entered. If you intentionally enter such a place, this is a light offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

The two ascetic periods named here map onto the actual agricultural and seasonal calendar of the region this text comes from, and the eighteen carried items are almost entirely practical — washing, eating, shelter, and study, rather than anything ornamental. Even a text this willing to describe extreme vows of self-sacrifice, as the precept just before this one shows, draws a firm line here: seeking out genuine physical danger unnecessarily is not treated as devotion, but as its own kind of fault.

8. Milestone: Minor Precept 38 — Seating by Ordination Order

Chinese Source

「若佛子！應如法次第坐，先受戒者在前坐，後受戒者在後坐，不問老少，比丘、比丘尼、貴人、國王、王子，乃至黃門、奴婢，皆應先受戒者在前坐，後受戒者次第而坐。莫如外道癡人，若老若少，無前無後，坐無次第，兵奴之法。我佛法中，先者先坐，後者後坐。而菩薩不次第坐者，犯輕垢罪。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — you should sit in proper order according to the dharma: whoever received the precepts first sits toward the front, whoever received them later sits toward the back — regardless of age, whether monk, nun, person of standing, king, prince, or servant of any station, whoever received the precepts first should sit toward the front, and whoever received them later should sit accordingly after. Do not follow the confused custom of outside paths, where seating has no order at all, older or younger, first or last — this is the disordered way of soldiers and servants. In our own Buddha's teaching, whoever comes first sits first, and whoever comes later sits after. If a bodhisattva does not sit in this proper order, this is a light offense."

Ru-Yi's reading note

Like precept 28's insistence on inviting monastics by turn rather than by fame, this precept replaces one hierarchy — secular rank, explicitly naming kings and servants in the same breath — with a different one entirely: the simple date on which the precepts were received. Whatever standing a person holds outside the assembly, it carries no weight in deciding where they sit within it.

9. Milestone: Minor Precept 39 — Building Places of Practice, and Teaching Through Every Circumstance

This group of nine closes with a precept that treats the sūtra's own recitation as an appropriate response to nearly every crisis a life can hold.

Chinese Source

「若佛子！常應教化一切眾生，建立僧房、山林、園田，立作佛塔，冬夏安居坐禪處所，一切行道處，皆應立之。而菩薩應為一切眾生講說大乘經律。若疾病、國難、賊難，父母兄弟、和上阿闍梨亡滅之日，及三七日乃至七七日，亦應讀誦講說大乘經律。齋會求福，行來治生，大火所燒，大水所漂，黑風所吹船舫，江河大海羅剎之難，亦應讀誦講說此經律。乃至一切罪報、三報、七逆、八難，杻械枷鎖繫縛其身，多婬、多嗔、多愚痴、多疾病，皆應讀誦講說此經律。而新學菩薩若不爾者，犯輕垢罪。如是九戒，應當學敬心奉持，梵壇品當說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Disciples of the Buddha — you should constantly teach and transform every being, establishing monasteries, forest retreats, gardens and fields, raising stūpas, and building places for winter and summer meditation retreat — every place needed for practice should be established. A bodhisattva should expound the great vehicle's scriptures and discipline for the sake of every being. In times of sickness, national calamity, or the threat of bandits, on the day a parent, sibling, preceptor, or teacher passes away, and through the following weeks up to forty-nine days afterward, you should recite and expound the great vehicle's scriptures and discipline. At times of seeking merit through religious gatherings, in the course of travel or livelihood, when threatened by great fire, great flood, storms at sea, or the dangers of rivers, seas, or malevolent spirits, you should also recite and expound this sūtra and its discipline. And in the face of every kind of karmic retribution — the three kinds of retribution, the seven heinous acts, the eight difficult circumstances, chains, fetters, and bondage of the body, excessive lust, excessive anger, excessive delusion, or persistent sickness — you should recite and expound this sūtra and its discipline as well. If a newly training bodhisattva fails to do this, this is a light offense. These nine precepts should be studied and upheld with a reverent mind — they are explained in the Chapter on the Platform of Brahmā."

Ru-Yi's reading note

The range of circumstances named here — illness, war, death, natural disaster, karmic hardship — is close to exhaustive by design. The precept is effectively arguing that there is no crisis severe enough to set this text aside; if anything, difficulty is named here as precisely the occasion this teaching is for. This closes the fourth group of minor precepts. Precepts 40 through 48, and the sūtra's own closing exhortation, come next in the final chapter.
