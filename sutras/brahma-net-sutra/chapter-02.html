Chapter 2 · 梵網經 · The Assembly, and Vairocana's Original Vow

A Light With No Explained Cause

With the five-part preface now complete, the sutra's own narrative finally opens: an unexplained light, a doubt shared by an entire assembly, an ascent to a world built from a single lotus with a thousand petals, and a vow, cultivated over a hundred asaṃkhyeya kalpas, that multiplies one Buddha into a thousand and each of those into a hundred koṭis more — closing with the forty mind-gates named for the first time, still waiting to be explained.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: translation in progress Source: Taishō T24, no. 1484 Reading notes: Ru-Yi's own, cross-checked against the BDK Tripiṭaka translation

1. Milestone: A Light With No Explained Cause

The sutra opens not with a teaching but with a mystery: Śākyamuni Buddha, already expounding a chapter he says was first spoken by another Buddha entirely, suddenly radiates a light that reaches across worlds — and not one being present, however exalted, can explain why.

Chinese Source

爾時釋迦牟尼佛，在第四禪地中摩醯首羅天王宮，與無量大梵天王、不可說不可說菩薩眾說蓮花臺藏世界盧舍那佛所說心地法門品。是時釋迦身放慧光，所照從此天王宮乃至蓮花臺藏世界。其中一切世界一切眾生，各各相視歡喜快樂，而未能知此光光何因何緣，皆生疑念；無量天人亦生疑念。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Śākyamuni Buddha, in the palace of the Heavenly King Maheśvara within the Fourth Dhyāna Heaven, was expounding — to countless great Brahma kings and an inexpressible, inexpressible number of bodhisattvas — the chapter on the Mind Ground Dharma-Door originally spoken by Vairocana Buddha in the Lotus Treasury World. At that moment Śākyamuni's own body emitted a light of wisdom, illuminating everything from this heavenly palace all the way to the Lotus Treasury World itself. Within it, every being in every world looked upon one another with joy and delight, yet none could tell what cause or condition had given rise to this light, and all gave rise to doubt in their minds; countless gods and humans likewise gave rise to doubt.

Ru-Yi's reading note

The sutra's very first line already unsettles the ordinary sense of who is teaching whom: Śākyamuni, sitting in the palace of Maheśvara — traditionally the ruling god of the entire triple world, at the very summit of the form realm's fourth dhyāna heaven — is not delivering his own original teaching at all, but transmitting a chapter he attributes to Vairocana Buddha, whom no one in the assembly has yet actually seen. The radiant light that follows is deliberately left unexplained in the moment it appears: everyone present feels its joy immediately, and no one — not even the "countless great Brahma kings" nominally ruling their own heavens — can say where it comes from. This shared, honest not-knowing, held by beings of vastly different rank at once, is what actually sets the entire narrative in motion; the light itself will not be explained until the assembly is carried to its source.

2. Milestone: Flower Light King's Question, and the Ascent to the Lotus Treasury World

A bodhisattva breaks the silence — not with an answer, but by matching the mystery with a second light of his own, drawing the whole assembly together into a single question, which Śākyamuni answers by lifting everyone bodily to the light's true source.

Chinese Source

爾時眾中玄通華光王菩薩從大莊嚴花光明三昧起，以佛神力放金剛白雲色光，光照一切世界。是中一切菩薩皆來集會與共，同心異口問：「此光光為何等相？」是時釋迦即擎接此世界大眾，還至蓮花臺藏世界百萬億紫金剛光明宮中，見盧舍那佛坐百萬蓮花赫赫光明座上。時釋迦佛及諸大眾，一時禮敬盧舍那佛足下已。釋迦佛言：「此世界中地及虛空一切眾生，為何因何緣得成菩薩十地道？當成佛果為何等相？」如如佛性本原品中廣問一切菩薩種子。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, within the assembly, the bodhisattva Profound Penetration, King of Flower Light, rose from the samādhi of Great Adornment and Flower Radiance, and, by the Buddha's spiritual power, emitted a light the color of vajra-white clouds, illuminating every world. All the bodhisattvas within it then gathered together as one and, with a single mind but many voices, asked: "What mark does this light actually bear?" At that moment Śākyamuni at once lifted up the whole assembly of this world and returned with them to the Lotus Treasury World, into the palace of a million koṭis of vajra-purple light, where they beheld Vairocana Buddha seated upon a blazing, radiant throne of a million lotus flowers. Śākyamuni Buddha and the whole assembly together bowed in reverence at Vairocana Buddha's feet. Śākyamuni Buddha then asked: "By what cause and what condition do the beings of this world, in its lands and in its empty space, come to accomplish the bodhisattva path of the ten grounds? And what mark does the fruit of Buddhahood itself bear, once accomplished?" — a question already asked at length, seeking every bodhisattva's own seed, in the chapter on the True Suchness, the Original Source of Buddha-Nature.

Ru-Yi's reading note

Notice what actually resolves the assembly's doubt: not an explanation offered in place, but movement — Śākyamuni simply lifts the entire gathering and carries it to where the light originates. No one is told the answer from a distance; they are brought bodily into the presence of its source. The bodhisattva who initiates this, Flower Light King, answers one unexplained light with a second light of his own making, as though the only honest response to a mystery already this vast is to add to it rather than to claim a premature explanation — and it is precisely this gesture that draws the whole assembly into a single shared question, asked with one mind though voiced by many mouths.

Once arrived, it is Śākyamuni himself — not Flower Light King — who puts the assembly's real question to Vairocana directly: by what cause do beings actually reach the bodhisattva path's ten grounds, and what does Buddhahood's own fruit look like once reached. This is the question the entire remainder of the chapter exists to answer. That Śākyamuni asks it on behalf of an assembly he himself is nominally teaching is the sutra's own quiet way of establishing where its authority actually rests: even the Buddha presently teaching human beings in this world defers, without any awkwardness at all, to the Buddha who first cultivated the very path in question.

3. Milestone: Vairocana's Own Vow — A Hundred Asaṃkhyeya Kalpas, One Buddha Multiplied Beyond Counting

Vairocana's answer is not a doctrine but an autobiography: a hundred asaṃkhyeya kalpas of cultivating this same Mind Ground, and a cosmology that follows directly from that cultivation — one lotus, a thousand petal-worlds, and within each of those, a hundred koṭis more.

Chinese Source

爾時盧舍那佛即大歡喜，現虛空光體性本原成佛常住法身三昧，示諸大眾：「是諸佛子！諦聽，善思修行。我已百阿僧祇劫修行心地，以之為因，初捨凡夫成等正覺，號為盧舍那，住蓮花臺藏世界海。其臺周遍有千葉，一葉一世界為千世界，我化為千釋迦據千世界。後就一葉世界，復有百億須彌山、百億日月、百億四天下、百億南閻浮提、百億菩薩釋迦坐百億菩提樹下，各說汝所問菩提薩埵心地。其餘九百九十九釋迦，各各現千百億釋迦亦復如是。千花上佛是吾化身，千百億釋迦是千釋迦化身。吾已為本原，名為盧舍那佛。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Vairocana Buddha rejoiced greatly, and manifested the samādhi of the empty, luminous, original source of his own true nature — the ever-abiding Dharma Body of accomplished Buddhahood — and showed the whole assembly: "You disciples of the Buddha, listen carefully, and reflect well as you practice. I have already cultivated the Mind Ground for a hundred asaṃkhyeya kalpas; taking this as my cause, I first left the state of an ordinary being behind and accomplished complete, perfect enlightenment, and was named Vairocana, dwelling in the ocean of the Lotus Treasury World. This lotus throne has a thousand petals spread all around it; each single petal is itself a world, a thousand worlds in all, and I transform myself into a thousand Śākyamunis, one occupying each of these thousand worlds. Then, within each single one of these petal-worlds, there are further a hundred koṭis of Mount Sumerus, a hundred koṭis of suns and moons, a hundred koṭis of the four continents, a hundred koṭis of Jambudvīpas, and a hundred koṭis of bodhisattva-Śākyamunis, each seated beneath a hundred koṭis of bodhi trees, each one expounding exactly what you have just asked — the bodhisattva's own Mind Ground. Each of the remaining nine hundred and ninety-nine Śākyamunis manifests, in just the same way, a hundred koṭis of further Śākyamunis of his own. The Buddha seated upon each of the thousand flowers is my own emanation body; the hundred koṭis of Śākyamunis are, in turn, emanation bodies of each of these thousand Śākyamunis. I myself am their original source, and am named Vairocana Buddha."

Ru-Yi's reading note

The arithmetic here is easy to read past too quickly: one Buddha, one lotus of a thousand petals, one world per petal, and then a hundred koṭis — traditionally a hundred million — of further worlds, suns, moons, and buddha-figures nested inside each single petal-world. The Śākyamuni Buddha who has been teaching this entire assembly from the very first line, in other words, is only one out of a thousand, itself only one instance out of a further hundred koṭis, all understood as emanations of the single Vairocana now speaking. This is the same three-bodies teaching already worked out in Chapter 1's preface, now shown rather than merely explained: Vairocana is the underlying Dharma Body, and every Śākyamuni in every one of these worlds is a Response Body doing exactly the same work, teaching exactly the same Mind Ground, to exactly this kind of assembly, everywhere at once.

What Vairocana actually credits for all of this is not a single dramatic moment of realization, but a hundred asaṃkhyeya kalpas — an asaṃkhyeya being the largest named unit in the classical Indian numbering system, itself already meaning something like "beyond counting" — of sustained cultivation of this same Mind Ground the sutra has not yet even defined for its audience. The scale is the point: whatever the forty stages to come actually demand, the text has already shown, before naming a single one of them, that this is what real, complete cultivation of the path looks like once it is finished — not a shortcut, but a vow patiently kept across a genuinely inconceivable span of time.

4. Milestone: The Forty Mind-Gates Named — One Path in Four Stages of Ten

Vairocana finally answers the question Śākyamuni carried across worlds on the assembly's behalf — not yet with explanation, but with a bare list: forty names, in four groups of ten, mapping the entire distance from a beginner's first resolve to the very ground of Buddhahood itself.

Chinese Source

爾時蓮花臺藏座上盧舍那佛，廣答告千釋迦千百億釋迦所問心地法品：「諸佛當知，堅信忍中十發趣心向果：一捨心、二戒心、三忍心、四進心、五定心、六慧心、七願心、八護心、九喜心、十頂心……是四十法門品，我先為菩薩時修入佛果之根原。如是一切眾生，入發趣、長養、金剛、十地，證當成果，無為無相大滿常住，十力、十八不共行，法身智身滿足。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Vairocana Buddha, upon the Lotus Treasury throne, gave a full answer to the thousand Śākyamunis and their hundred koṭis of further Śākyamunis, on the very Mind Ground teaching they had asked about: "All you Buddhas should know: within the patience of firm faith, there are ten minds of development advancing toward their fruit — first, the mind of giving up; second, the mind of discipline; third, the mind of patience ... [through all forty names, in four groups of ten] ... These forty dharma-gates are the very root and source through which I myself, when I was formerly a bodhisattva, cultivated my way into the fruit of Buddhahood. In just the same way, once any being enters development, long nurturing, the vajra minds, and the ten grounds, and realizes their fruit, they attain the unconditioned, markless, vast fulfillment of the ever-abiding — the ten powers, the eighteen exclusive attributes, and the complete fulfillment of both the Dharma Body and the body of wisdom."

Ten Minds of Development (十發趣心)
  1. Giving up
  2. Discipline
  3. Patience
  4. Diligence
  5. Concentration
  6. Wisdom
  7. Vowing
  8. Guarding
  9. Joy
  10. The summit mind
Ten Minds of Long Nurturing (十長養心)
  1. Loving-kindness
  2. Compassion
  3. Joy
  4. Giving up
  5. Giving
  6. Kind speech
  7. Benefiting
  8. Working alongside
  9. Concentration
  10. Wisdom
Ten Vajra Minds (十金剛心)
  1. Faith
  2. Mindfulness
  3. Dedication
  4. Penetration
  5. Directness
  6. Non-retreat
  7. The Great Vehicle
  8. No-mark
  9. Wisdom
  10. Indestructibility
Ten Grounds (十地)
  1. Equality
  2. Excellent wisdom
  3. Light
  4. Blazing wisdom
  5. Illuminating wisdom
  6. Flower-light
  7. Fulfillment
  8. The Buddha's lion's roar
  9. Flower adornment
  10. Entry into the Buddha's own realm
Ru-Yi's reading note

This is the same forty-stage structure long familiar from the Huayan tradition under other names — the ten dwellings, ten practices, ten transferences, and ten grounds — here given its own distinct vocabulary of "minds" rather than "stages," which keeps the emphasis exactly where Vairocana places it himself: this is a description of an inner disposition cultivated in sequence, not a series of external ranks conferred from outside. Each group of ten is explicitly said to advance "toward its fruit" and then to open directly into the next group's own "patience" — firm faith, firm dharma, firm cultivation, and finally firm sagehood — so that the forty names are not forty independent virtues to acquire in any order, but forty successive expressions of a single, continuously deepening resolve.

Only a bare list has been given so far — forty names with no explanation of any one of them — yet Vairocana is already explicit about what completing all forty actually yields: not a mere technical qualification, but the ten powers and eighteen exclusive attributes traditionally reserved for a fully accomplished Buddha, and the complete fulfillment of both the Dharma Body and the body of wisdom. The distance between a beginner's first "mind of giving up" and the tenth ground's "entry into the Buddha's own realm" is, on this reading, not a gap to somehow bridge later — it is the very same hundred-asaṃkhyeya-kalpa path Vairocana has just finished describing as his own.

5. Milestone: The Request for Detailed Teaching

A list of forty bare names cannot yet be practiced. Flower Light King, in the very same assembly, asks the question every reader arrives at with him — what does each one of these forty actually mean — setting up the detailed exposition that will occupy the whole of the sutra's upper fascicle.

Chinese Source

爾時蓮花臺藏世界盧舍那佛赫赫大光明座上，千花上佛千百億佛一切世界佛。是座中有一菩薩，名華光王大智明菩薩，從坐而立，白盧舍那佛言：「世尊佛！上略開十發趣、十長養、十金剛、十地名相，其一一義中未可解了。唯願說之，唯願說之，妙極金剛寶藏一切智門。」如來百觀品中已明問。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, upon Vairocana Buddha's own blazing, greatly luminous throne in the Lotus Treasury World — with the Buddha seated on each of the thousand flowers, the hundred koṭis of further Buddhas, and the Buddhas of every world all present — there was, within that same assembly, a bodhisattva named Flower Light King, Great Wisdom and Light, who rose from his seat and said to Vairocana Buddha: "World-Honored Buddha! You have, above, briefly opened up the names and marks of the ten minds of development, the ten minds of long nurturing, the ten vajra minds, and the ten grounds, yet the actual meaning within each single one of them has not yet been made plain to us. We beg you to explain it, we beg you to explain it — this most wondrous gate of all wisdom, this vajra treasury of every precious thing." — a question already asked at length, in the chapter on the Hundred Contemplations of the Tathāgata.

Ru-Yi's reading note

Flower Light King's doubled plea — "we beg you to explain it, we beg you to explain it" — is the sutra's own way of marking that a bare list, however cosmically framed, is not yet a teaching anyone can actually practice. Naming a stage is not the same as understanding what distinguishes it from the one before or after it, and the text is careful to have its own audience say so plainly before Vairocana obliges. This closes the narrative frame proper: a light with no explained cause, an ascent to its source, a vow spanning a hundred asaṃkhyeya kalpas, and forty names now waiting for their meaning. Vairocana's actual exposition of the first ten of these — the ten minds of development, one by one — opens in the chapter that follows.
