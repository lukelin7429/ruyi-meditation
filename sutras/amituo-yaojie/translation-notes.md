# Amituo Yaojie — Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of Ouyi Zhixu's *Amituo Yaojie* (阿彌陀經要解, Taishō T37, no. 1762), his 1647 commentary on the Amitābha Sūtra. They exist so that this commentary's terminology stays consistent across all ten parts, and so later translation projects on this site can inherit these renderings rather than re-coin them.

## The text and its author

Ouyi Zhixu (蕅益智旭, 1599–1655) was a late-Ming dynasty monk, one of the four eminent masters of that era, whose own writing draws on Tiantai, Chan, Pure Land, and Vinaya alike — the same author behind this site's *[Jiaoguan Gangzong](../jiaoguan-gangzong/)*. The *Amituo Yaojie* is his commentary on the Amitābha Sūtra, written in nine days in the autumn of 1647 (the twenty-seventh of the ninth month to the fifth of the tenth month of the dinghai year) at forty-nine years old, four years after founder Chuandeng's own *Yuanzhong Chao* and roughly half a century after Zhuhong's *Shuchao* — the two major commentaries Ouyi's own preface explicitly measures itself against, aiming for brevity and directness rather than competing in scope. It went on to become the single most widely read of the sūtra's classical commentaries, and its framework of faith, vow, and practice (信願行) is the lens through which Chinese Pure Land teaching has read the sūtra ever since.

Before this project, the *Yaojie* was represented on this site only secondhand, paraphrased into reading notes on the [Amitābha Sūtra](../amitabha-sutra.html) page itself. This project is the first complete, independent translation of the commentary as its own text.

## Source and copyright

- Base text: Taishō T37, no. 1762, via the CBETA digital edition (CBETA 2025.R3). Ouyi died in 1655 — the text has been in the public domain for well over three centuries, under any jurisdiction's copyright term. CBETA's own transcription is distributed under a non-commercial-use license, consistent with how this non-profit site already uses CBETA-sourced text across every other translation project here.
- No prior complete English translation is known to exist. This is an original translation made directly from the Chinese.

## Structure and pagination

The base text has no fascicle divisions of its own — a continuous commentary running from Ouyi's preface through the sūtra's own closing line, followed by three postscripts from its print history (Ouyi's own, a disciple's, and a donor's dedication of merit). This collection splits it into ten pages at the commentary's own major structural joints — the Five Profound Meanings (五重玄義), then the sūtra's own three-part division of Introduction (序分), Main Teaching (正宗分), and Transmission (流通分):

1. **Part 1**: Ouyi's preface; the first two Profound Meanings — explaining the name (釋名), distinguishing the essence (辨體).
2. **Part 2**: the third Profound Meaning in full — elucidating the essential teaching (明宗): the six kinds of faith, taking and forsaking, holding the name as the foremost skillful means.
3. **Part 3**: the fourth and fifth Profound Meanings — power and function (明力用), doctrinal classification (教相) — and the Introduction's general preface (通序): the assembly of śrāvakas, bodhisattvas, and gods.
4. **Part 4**: the Introduction's specific preface (別序): why the Buddha speaks unprompted, the four Pure Lands and three bodies in full detail, the "why west" dialogue.
5. **Part 5**: the Main Teaching opens (正宗分): why the land is named Utmost Bliss, the ground and trees, the ponds and lotus flowers.
6. **Part 6**: heavenly music and the rain of flowers; the birds, with the full thirty-seven factors of enlightenment enumerated.
7. **Part 7**: why the birds are not karmically born; the wind in the jeweled trees, including Ouyi's direct argument against a purely inward "mind-only" reading; the close of the land's description.
8. **Part 8**: Amitābha himself — light and life explained through the tranquility/illumination pair, ten kalpas since his attainment, the retinue that never falls back, the exhortation to vow.
9. **Part 9**: the sūtra's central passage — holding the name, one to seven days, undistracted; phenomenal and noumenal holding and one-pointedness; ten questions and answers; the summary exhortation closing the Main Teaching.
10. **Part 10**: the Transmission (流通分) in full — six directions of buddhas, the sūtra's two titles, the hardest-to-believe passage, the assembly's joy — and, past the sūtra's own text, Ouyi's postscript, the printer's postscript, and the donor's dedication of merit.

Every page's `source`/`translation` columns were checked against the CBETA base text for full coverage of Ouyi's commentary; his embedded quotations of the sūtra itself are additionally set apart in dedicated `scripture-quote` boxes, matched wherever possible to the wording already established on the [Amitābha Sūtra](../amitabha-sutra.html) page, so the same sūtra passage reads identically wherever it appears on this site.

## A design note: separating scripture from commentary

Unlike this site's other commentary-and-base-text pairings (*Fahua Wenju*, the *Surangama Sutra Jiangyi*), where the source column mixes quoted scripture and commentary together as one continuous block of classical Chinese, the *Yaojie* alternates cleanly between short quoted bursts of the Amitābha Sūtra and Ouyi's own, much longer exegesis of each burst. Since Luke's brief for this project specifically asked that quoted scripture be visually distinguished from the commentary being translated in full, each page uses a dedicated jade-bordered `scripture-quote` box for every sūtra quotation, styled apart from the terra/gold `source`/`translation` columns that carry Ouyi's own commentary — the actual subject of this translation. Where a `source` column also happens to include a further embedded quotation (for instance, an inner citation from the *Miaozong Chao* or the *Divination Sūtra*), that citation is translated inline as part of Ouyi's own prose rather than boxed separately, since it is Ouyi's own act of quotation, not the base sūtra's text.

## Terminology coined for this project

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 凡聖同居土 | the land of ordinary beings and sages dwelling together | The first and most accessible of the four Pure Lands; no prior site-wide rendering was found. |
| 常寂光土 | the land of eternal, tranquil light | The fourth and ultimate Pure Land, built from the 寂/照 (tranquility/illumination) pair below. |
| 寂 / 照 | tranquility / illumination | Ouyi's own governing pair for the true mark of reality (Part 1): the essence of reality is "tranquil yet ever illuminating, illuminating yet ever tranquil" — later reused to explain Amitābha's own two names, light and life (Part 8). |
| 事持 / 理持 | phenomenal / noumenal holding of the name | Paired with the sūtra's own established 事一心/理一心; 持 (holding, i.e. how one relates to the practice of reciting the name) is a distinct axis from 一心 (one-pointedness, i.e. what breaks through as a result) — a practitioner can practice noumenal holding without yet reaching noumenal one-pointedness. |
| 受用身 | the body of enjoyment | One of two sub-types Ouyi gives for the reward-body (報身) in Part 1's fourfold nested tranquility/illumination scheme — turned toward oneself, as distinct from the response-and-transformation body (應化身), turned toward others; closer to the Sanskrit *sambhogakāya* than the general site-wide "reward-body" gloss. |
| 通序 / 別序 | the general preface / the specific preface | The Introduction's own two-part division — the standing scene (general) versus the specific occasion that opens the actual teaching (specific). |
| 事持者 vs. 理持者 as applied within 信自／信他／信因／信果／信事／信理 | faith in oneself / in others / in cause / in effect / in phenomena / in principle | Ouyi's own six-part faith scheme (Part 2), his single most influential contribution to Pure Land exegesis; kept as six distinct, parallel English phrases to preserve the catechism-like structure of the original. |

## A note on corrections to the sūtra's own reading guide

Before this project, the [Amitābha Sūtra](../amitabha-sutra.html) page's reading notes paraphrased the *Yaojie* secondhand. Comparing that paraphrase against Ouyi's actual text surfaced several points needing correction, applied as part of this project:

- **Section 6** (why he is called Amitābha) previously framed light and life as "wisdom reaching outward" and "compassion sustained through time." Ouyi's own language is specifically the tranquility/illumination (寂/照) pair from the true-mark discussion in Part 1 of this collection — corrected accordingly.
- **Section 13** (the assembly rejoices) previously attributed to Ouyi a specific observation about asuras in the closing assembly. No such comment appears anywhere in the *Yaojie*'s own closing passage; the note has been corrected.
- **Section 3** (the jeweled ponds) previously omitted the fifth of the eight virtues of water, moistness (潤澤) — added.
- **Section 2** (why the land is named Utmost Bliss) previously attached Ouyi's argument against a purely inward "mind-only" reading to the "ten hundred thousand million buddha-lands" passage. That argument in fact occurs later, in the wind-and-trees passage (Part 7 of this collection) — the note now points to the correct location.

Keep this table growing, and this corrections note updated, as later projects on this site draw on the *Yaojie*'s own vocabulary or surface further points needing correction against the sūtra's reading guide.
