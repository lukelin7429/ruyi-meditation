Infinite Light, Infinite Life

The sūtra turns from the land to its teaching-lord. Ouyi explains Amitābha's two names through the tranquility/illumination pair first set up on Part 1 — light is mind tranquil yet ever illuminating, life is mind illuminating yet ever tranquil — works through the puzzle of "ten kalpas" against Yogācāra's own account of time, then closes with the retinue that never falls back and the exhortation to vow rebirth among them.

Why Is He Called Amitābha?

正宗分・陳正報主伴妙・教主妙・總徵
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》 — see the full sūtra translation

舍利弗！於汝意云何？彼佛何故號阿彌陀？

Śāriputra, what do you think? Why is that buddha called Amitābha?

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

二、陳正報主伴妙，二：初、明教主妙；二、明眷屬妙。初中二：初、總徵；二、別釋。今初。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, setting forth the wondrous direct reward, the host and retinue, in two parts: first, clarifying the wondrous teaching-lord; second, clarifying the wondrous retinue. The first has two: first, a general inquiry; second, a detailed explanation. Here is the first.

Light: Illumination That Is Ever Tranquil

正宗分・陳正報主伴妙・教主妙・別釋（一）約光明釋
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

舍利弗！彼佛光明無量，照十方國無所障礙，是故號為阿彌陀。

Śāriputra, that buddha's light is immeasurable, shining on the lands of the ten directions without obstruction — this is why he is called Amitābha.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

二、別釋，二：初、約光明釋；二、約壽命釋。今初。心性寂而常照故為光明，今徹證心性無量之體，故光明無量也。十方三世諸佛皆悉徹證心性全體，皆悉照十方國無所障礙，皆可名無量光，而由因中願力不同，各隨因緣以立別名。今佛昔為法藏比丘，所發四十八願曾有光明恒照十方之願，故今果成如先所願也。法身光明竪窮橫遍故無量，報身光明稱真法性故無量，此則佛佛道同。若夫應身光明，則有或照一由旬者，或照十旬百旬千旬者，或照一世界者，或照十百千世界者，惟彌陀普照十方無量世界，故別名無量光。然三身不一不異，為令眾生得四益故強作此分別耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the detailed explanation, in two parts: first, explaining in terms of light; second, explaining in terms of life. Here is the first. The nature of mind, tranquil and yet ever illuminating, is precisely what constitutes light; now, having thoroughly realized the immeasurable substance of the nature of mind itself, his light is therefore immeasurable. Every buddha of the ten directions and three time-periods has, without exception, thoroughly realized the whole substance of the nature of mind, and every one of them, without exception, shines upon the lands of the ten directions without obstruction — every one of them could rightly be called "infinite light." Yet because the power of their vows made while still causal beings differs from one buddha to the next, each takes up a distinct name according to his own causes and conditions. This present buddha, in a former life as the monk Dharmākara, made forty-eight vows, among which was once a vow that his light should constantly shine upon the ten directions — hence his fruit, now accomplished, is exactly as he had earlier vowed.

The Dharma-body's light is immeasurable because it is exhausted vertically and pervades horizontally without limit; the reward-body's light is immeasurable because it accords fully with the true nature of dharmas — in this, every buddha's path is the same as every other's. As for the light of the response-body, however, there is variation: some illuminate only a single yojana, some ten, a hundred, or a thousand yojanas; some illuminate a single world, some ten, a hundred, or a thousand worlds. Only Amitābha universally illuminates the immeasurable worlds of the ten directions — hence he alone is specially named "Infinite Light." Yet the three bodies are neither one nor different; this distinction is only forcibly drawn in order to enable beings to obtain the four benefits.

Translator's note

This is the passage the reading note to Section 6 of the sūtra's own page currently misreports — that page's note frames the light/life pair as "wisdom reaching outward" and "compassion sustained through time," but Ouyi's own language here is the specific tranquility/illumination (寂/照) pair from the true-mark discussion on Part 1: light is mind tranquil yet ever illuminating; life, in the next passage, is mind illuminating yet ever tranquil — the identical formula from Part 1, simply run in reverse for the second term. "The four benefits" (四益) are the four siddhāntas, invoked here as the reason for drawing a distinction — light vs. life, this body vs. that — within a reality Ouyi has already insisted is, at bottom, undivided.

Life: Tranquility That Is Ever Illuminating

正宗分・陳正報主伴妙・教主妙・別釋（二）約壽命釋
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

又，舍利弗！彼佛壽命及其人民無量無邊阿僧祇劫，故名阿彌陀。

And, Śāriputra, the lifespan of that buddha and of his people extends for immeasurable, boundless asaṃkhyeya kalpas — this too is why he is named Amitābha.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

二、約壽命釋，又二：初、正明主伴壽皆無量；二、兼示成道已經十劫。今初。猶云彼佛及其人民壽命皆無量無邊阿僧祇劫，蓋西域每用倒文故也。心性照而常寂故為壽命，今徹證心性無量之體，故壽命無量也。法身壽命無始無終故無量，報身壽命有始無終故無量。此亦佛佛道同，皆可名無量壽。應身則隨願隨機延促不等。今以法藏比丘四十八願曾有佛壽無量及人民壽命亦無量之願故，今果成亦如先願，別名無量壽也。此則實有限量，特以人天莫數，名無量耳。然三身既不一異，則應身亦可即是無量之無量矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining in terms of life, again in two parts: first, directly clarifying that both host's and retinue's lifespans are immeasurable; second, showing in passing that ten kalpas have already passed since his attainment of buddhahood. Here is the first. This is as if to say that the lifespans of both that buddha and his people are immeasurable, boundless asaṃkhyeya kalpas — for the languages of the Western Regions often invert their word order in just this way. The nature of mind, illuminating and yet ever tranquil, is precisely what constitutes life; now, having thoroughly realized the immeasurable substance of the nature of mind itself, his life is therefore immeasurable. The Dharma-body's life is immeasurable because it has neither beginning nor end; the reward-body's life is immeasurable because, though it has a beginning, it has no end. In this, too, every buddha's path is the same as every other's, and every one could rightly be called "infinite life." The response-body, however, varies in length according to vow and according to capacity. This present buddha, because as the monk Dharmākara he made forty-eight vows, among which was once a vow that both his own lifespan as buddha and the lifespan of his people should likewise be immeasurable, his fruit, now accomplished, is exactly as he had earlier vowed — hence he alone is specially named "Infinite Life." This span, in fact, does possess a real limit; it is only because no reckoning by gods or humans could number it that it is called "immeasurable." Yet since the three bodies are already neither one nor different, then the response-body, too, may be said to be precisely the infinity of the infinite itself.

Question: "That the buddha's own lifespan is immeasurable, this may be granted — but how could the lifespan of his people be immeasurable as well?"

Answer: "It is because the power of the Buddha's own original vow is inconceivable, and because the merit and virtue of faith, vow, and holding the name are inconceivable. Faith, vow, and holding the name are the direct cause and condition; the power of the Buddha's own original vow is the contributing condition."

Ten Kalpas, and Whether the Past Is Real

正宗分・陳正報主伴妙・教主妙・別釋（二）約壽命釋（續）
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

舍利弗！阿彌陀佛成佛已來於今十劫。

Śāriputra, it has now been ten kalpas since Amitābha Buddha attained buddhahood.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

二、兼示成道已經十劫。前文既明彌陀現在說法，此文又明未來壽命無量，故今更明過去成佛已經十劫也。然法身本無成與不成，不應論劫；報身因圓果滿名成，應身為物示生名成，皆可論劫。又法身因修德顯亦可論成論劫，報身別無新得，應身如月印川亦無成與不成，亦復不應論劫。然諸佛成道各有本迹，本地並不可測，且約西方極樂示成之迹則成佛十劫，即是三身一成一切成，亦是非成非不成而論成也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, showing in passing that ten kalpas have already passed since his attainment of buddhahood. The passage above has already made clear that Amitābha is presently teaching the Dharma; this passage now further makes clear that his future lifespan is immeasurable; and so this passage now further clarifies that, in the past, his attainment of buddhahood already occurred ten kalpas ago. Now, the Dharma-body, in itself, has neither an attaining nor a not-attaining, and so should not properly be discussed in terms of kalpas; the reward-body, whose cause is fulfilled and whose fruit is complete, is called "attained," and the response-body, manifesting a birth for the sake of beings, is likewise called "attained" — both of these may properly be discussed in terms of kalpas. Again: the Dharma-body, in that its cause, cultivated virtue, is thereby manifested, may also be discussed in terms of attainment and kalpas; the reward-body has, apart from this, no separately new attainment; and the response-body, like the moon reflected in a river, likewise has neither an attaining nor a not-attaining, and so should likewise not be discussed in terms of kalpas. Yet every buddha's attainment of the path has both its original ground and its manifested trace, and the original ground itself can never be fathomed; here, speaking only in terms of the trace manifested as attainment in the Western land of Utmost Bliss, his buddhahood was attained ten kalpas ago — and this is precisely a case where, the three bodies being one, all three are attained at once; it is likewise a case of discussing "attainment" in terms of neither attainment nor non-attainment.

Question: "The Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi Śāstra says that neither the past nor the future truly, substantially exists. How, then, can it be said here that his attainment lies ten kalpas in the past, and his lifespan immeasurable kalpas into the future?"

Answer: "The past and the future are neither presently existing nor eternally constant — and for this very reason they cannot be clung to as substantially real, but equally, how could they be clung to as definitely nonexistent either? Consider even the present, a single instant: even the Vijñaptimātratā treatise, granting it a real substance and function, would surely not deny that it perishes without interval, incapable of remaining still for even a moment — so how could it ever be clung to as fixed and truly constant either? One must understand: it is only relative to the present now that one speaks of "attainment ten kalpas ago" — but the past has already perished; how could there be some separate pile of ten kalpas stacked up somewhere, not yet dissolved away? And again: it is only relative to the present now that one speaks of "lifespan immeasurable kalpas into the future" — but the future has not yet arrived; how could there be some separate store of immeasurable kalpas held in reserve somewhere, gradually arriving one after another? One must understand that time and kalpas have no fixed nature of their own — hence the three time-periods, in their very substance, are entirely empty; and yet having no fixed nature is never the same as sheer annihilation — hence the distinctions among time and kalpas remain vividly, unmistakably real. And though these distinctions remain vividly, unmistakably real, they are, every one of them, nothing but a shadow manifested within the single instant present right now. Hence it is said: the ten time-periods, past and present, beginning and end, never depart from the single thought present in this very moment. Ponder this well!"

Translator's note

This closing dialogue applies, to the specific puzzle of "ten kalpas ago," the same current-single-thought-of-mind framework the commentary opened with on Part 1. "The moon reflected in a river" (月印川) is a standard image for the response-body: one moon, many reflections, each arising and vanishing with the water that holds it, without the moon itself ever truly appearing or disappearing.

The Retinue: Śrāvakas and Bodhisattvas Beyond Counting

正宗分・陳正報主伴妙・眷屬妙（一）聲聞菩薩妙
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

又，舍利弗！彼佛有無量無邊聲聞弟子，皆阿羅漢，非是算數之所能知；諸菩薩眾亦復如是。舍利弗！彼佛國土成就如是功德莊嚴。

And, Śāriputra, that buddha has immeasurable, boundless śrāvaka disciples, every one of them an arhat, a number beyond what any reckoning could know; and his host of bodhisattvas is just the same. Śāriputra, that buddha-land is accomplished with just such adornments of merit.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

二、明眷屬妙，又二：初、聲聞菩薩妙；二、生者不退妙。今初。聲聞菩薩不可算數，並是彌陀因中大願大行所致，故云成就如是功德莊嚴。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the wondrous retinue, again in two parts: first, the wondrousness of the śrāvakas and bodhisattvas; second, the wondrousness of those born there never falling back. Here is the first. The śrāvakas and bodhisattvas beyond all reckoning are, every one of them, brought about by Amitābha's own great vow and great practice while still a causal being — hence it says "accomplished with just such adornments of merit."

Question: "The Rebirth Treatise says that the seed of the two vehicles is not reborn there. Why, then, does that buddha still have śrāvaka disciples?"

Answer: "A śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha of fixed nature, who neither dedicates his merit toward bodhi nor makes the great vow, cannot be reborn there; but one who has previously practiced the lesser vehicle, and who, at the end of life, dedicates his merit and makes the vow, is then able to be reborn there. Although, once reborn, his earlier habituation to the lesser vehicle has already ripened first, the Buddha, according with his capacity, teaches him the lesser Dharma; he then quickly realizes its fruit, and thereupon at once turns toward the great vehicle, never remaining fixed in the lesser vehicle to the end. This is therefore unlike the śrāvakas of the Tripiṭaka teaching in this world, who must wait directly for the Lotus assembly's opening-and-merging before they can enter the perfect teaching — these must instead be śrāvakas of the shared teaching, or perhaps of the distinct teaching's seventh abiding, who have already severed views and habituated thought beforehand, and are hence called arhats. For the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings do not hear of another buddha's name at all; and now, having already heard Amitābha's name and, with faith and vow, attained rebirth, they must, in every case, belong to what is gathered in by the distinct and perfect teachings alone."

Translator's note

The Rebirth Treatise (往生論), in full the Wuliangshou Jing Youpotishe Yuansheng Ji, is Vasubandhu's Sukhāvatīvyūhopadeśa, a foundational verse treatise on Pure Land rebirth translated by Bodhiruci in the sixth century — a different work from the Vasubandhu commentaries already translated elsewhere on this site.

Non-Retrogression, Guaranteed by the Land Itself

正宗分・陳正報主伴妙・眷屬妙（二）生者不退妙
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

又，舍利弗！極樂國土眾生生者，皆是阿鞞跋致，其中多有一生補處，其數甚多，非是算數所能知之，但可以無量無邊阿僧祇說。

And, Śāriputra, all the beings born in the land of Utmost Bliss are avaivartika, non-retrogressing. Among them a great many are bound for buddhahood after just one more birth; their number is so great that no reckoning could know it — it can only be spoken of as immeasurable, boundless asaṃkhyeyas.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

二、生者不退妙。阿鞞跋致此云不退，而有三義：一、位不退，證入聖流不墮凡地故；二、行不退，恒欲度生不墮二乘地故；三、念不退，心心流入薩婆若故。若約此土，則藏初果、通見地、別七信、圓初信名位不退，通菩薩、別十行、圓十信名行不退，別初地、圓初住名念不退。今淨土中則雖五逆、十惡，十念成就帶業往生居下下品者，例皆得三不退，以淨土無退緣故。又一生淨土則皆壽命無量，任運進修直至一生補處，觀彼十方世界何處無佛何處機熟，便往示現成佛。故一生補處甚多，非是算數所能知也。初、廣陳彼土依正妙果以啟信竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the wondrousness of those born there never falling back. Avaivartika translates as "non-retrogressing," and carries three senses: first, non-retrogression in stage — having entered the stream of sagehood, one no longer falls back into the state of an ordinary being; second, non-retrogression in practice — constantly wishing to liberate beings, one no longer falls back into the state of the two lesser vehicles; third, non-retrogression in thought — thought after thought flows into the all-knowing wisdom. Reckoned in terms of this world, non-retrogression in stage corresponds to the Tripiṭaka teaching's first fruit, the shared teaching's ground of vision, the distinct teaching's seventh degree of faith, and the perfect teaching's first degree of faith; non-retrogression in practice corresponds to the shared teaching's bodhisattva stage, the distinct teaching's ten practices, and the perfect teaching's ten degrees of faith; non-retrogression in thought corresponds to the distinct teaching's first ground and the perfect teaching's first abiding. But within the Pure Land, even one who has committed the five heinous crimes or the ten evils, and who accomplishes the ten recollections and, carrying his karma along, is reborn in the very lowest of the lowest grades, attains all three kinds of non-retrogression alike, without exception — for the Pure Land has no condition that could cause backsliding at all. Moreover, once born in the Pure Land, every being's lifespan is immeasurable, advancing spontaneously in cultivation straight up to the rank of one bound for buddhahood after a single further birth; observing the ten directions' worlds, wherever there is no buddha, wherever capacities have ripened, they go there and manifest the attainment of buddhahood. Hence those bound for buddhahood after one more birth are exceedingly many, beyond what any reckoning could know. This closes the first section: broadly setting forth the wondrous fruits of that land's dependent and direct rewards, in order to give rise to faith.

Exhorting the Vow

正宗分・正勸眾生發願持名以成因（一）勸發願
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

舍利弗！眾生聞者，應當發願願生彼國。所以者何？得與如是諸上善人俱會一處。

Śāriputra, any being who hears this should vow — should aspire to be born in that land. And why? Because there, one gets to be gathered together in one place with such supremely good people as these.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

二、正勸眾生發願持名以成因，三：初、勸發願；二、勸立行；三、總結勸。今初。前既舉彼土依正妙果以勸深信，今更勸聞而信者隨應發願以求往生，信願二種即是往生淨土之要領也。何者？若不聞有極樂依正之名，或復為說而不肯信，則不得名為聞。今既得聞，便屬別圓二教初機，故教之以發菩提願。蓋彼國雖通四土，然既在此娑婆三界之外，又非二乘所證偏空涅槃境界，故但肯發願求生，即屬別圓二教弘誓所攝，以既不求人天福報又不求聲聞緣覺，非是菩提大願而何！設使或求人天或求趨寂，則非生彼國之願矣。故此願生彼國之願從深信生，合此信願的為淨土指南，而下文執持名號乃為正行。

若信願堅固，縱使臨終十念一念亦決得生。若無信願，縱使將此名號作个語頭，持至風吹不入雨打不濕如銀牆鐵壁一般，亦萬無一得生淨土之理，修淨業者不可不知也。所以者何下，徵釋應當發願往生之故。得與如是諸上善人俱會一處，指前文所說無數阿羅漢、無數諸菩薩乃至無數一生補處人也。苟無大信大願，何得與此諸上善人會一處哉？大本阿彌陀經亦以發菩提願為要，正與此同。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, directly exhorting sentient beings to make the vow and hold the name, in order to accomplish the cause, in three parts: first, exhorting them to make the vow; second, exhorting them to establish the practice; third, a summary exhortation. Here is the first. Having already set forth that land's wondrous fruits of dependent and direct reward in order to exhort deep faith, the text now further exhorts whoever has heard and believed to make the vow accordingly, seeking rebirth — faith and vow together are precisely the essential guide to rebirth in the Pure Land. Why is this? If one has not heard the names of Utmost Bliss's dependent and direct rewards, or has heard them explained yet refuses to believe, then this cannot properly be called "hearing" at all. But having now truly heard, one already belongs to the initial capacity of the distinct and perfect teachings, and is therefore instructed to arouse the bodhi-vow. For although that land is, in general, common to all four kinds of Pure Land, it nonetheless lies beyond this sahā world's own three realms, and is moreover not the state of one-sided empty nirvāṇa realized by the two lesser vehicles — hence merely being willing to vow and seek rebirth there already belongs to what is gathered in by the vast vows of the distinct and perfect teachings, since one thereby seeks neither the blessings of humans and gods nor the fruits of the śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha — what else could this be but the great vow of bodhi itself! Were one instead to seek the blessings of humans and gods, or to seek only the tranquility of the lesser vehicle, this would not be the vow to be reborn in that land at all. Hence this vow to be reborn in that land arises from deep faith — faith and vow together are precisely the compass of the Pure Land — and the text that follows, holding fast to the name, is the proper practice itself.

If faith and vow are firm and solid, then even ten recollections, or even a single recollection, at the very moment of death, will still certainly secure rebirth. But without faith and vow, even if one took up this very name as a meditative topic and held it fast until it were like a silver wall or an iron rampart, impervious to wind and unwetted by rain, there would still not be so much as one chance in ten thousand of thereby attaining rebirth in the Pure Land — this is something a practitioner of pure karma must not fail to know. The clause "and why" that follows resolves the reason one should vow to seek rebirth. "To be gathered together in one place with such supremely good people as these" points back to the innumerable arhats, innumerable bodhisattvas, and innumerable beings bound for buddhahood after one more birth spoken of in the passage above. Without great faith and a great vow, how could one possibly be gathered together in one place with such supremely good people as these? The larger version of the Amitābha Sūtra likewise takes the arousing of the bodhi-vow as essential — in precise agreement with this.

Translator's note

"The larger version of the Amitābha Sūtra" (大本阿彌陀經) refers to the Sukhāvatīvyūha proper — the longer Pure Land sūtra already translated on this site as the Larger Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra — as distinct from this shorter sūtra Ouyi is commenting on. Ouyi's warning that holding the name "like a silver wall or an iron rampart" without faith and vow still yields "not one chance in ten thousand" of rebirth is one of the sharpest statements in the whole commentary of his central claim: mechanical repetition of the name, however disciplined, is not itself the cause of rebirth — faith and vow are.

Part 8 Complete

This closes the direct reward — Amitābha's own name explained through light and life, his ten kalpas as a buddha, his innumerable retinue, and the exhortation to vow rebirth among them — and opens the exhortation to practice. Part 9 covers the sūtra's single most consequential passage: holding the name for one to seven days with an undistracted mind, the difference between phenomenal and noumenal one-pointedness, and the long chain of questions and answers — on death-bed visions, on the number seven, on whether scattered recitation still helps — that closes out the Main Teaching.
