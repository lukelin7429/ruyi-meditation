Six Directions of Buddhas, and Ouyi's Own Postscript

The Transmission closes the whole commentary: buddhas of six directions, each putting forth a tongue broad enough to cover a universe, testifying to a teaching Śākyamuni himself calls the hardest in the world to believe. Then, past the sūtra's own final line, three postscripts survive from the print history of the text itself — Ouyi's own account of writing this commentary in nine days at forty-nine, a disciple's tribute to his teacher, and a bereaved son's dedication of merit to his family.

The Transmission Begins

流通分
Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

三、流通分，信願行三，一經之要旨備矣。此無上法門甚深難信，故既無問自說以為發起，復引六方諸佛廣長舌相同為證明，以廣流通。若非流通之力，何能使妙法筌蹄不壅於來世，圓頓心要永傳於未聞也哉。文分為五：初、引六方諸佛稱讚勸信流通；二、重徵名解釋勸信流通；三、約已今當生勸願流通；四、引諸佛稱讚難事敦信流通；五、大眾歡喜信受流通。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the Transmission. Faith, vow, and practice, these three, now stand complete as the essential purport of the entire sūtra. This unsurpassed Dharma-gate is so profound that it is difficult to believe; hence, having already spoken it entirely unprompted, without being asked, in order to open its own occasion, the Buddha now further calls upon the buddhas of the six directions, putting forth their broad, long tongues together, to bear witness to it, in order to broadly transmit it. Were it not for the power of the Transmission, how could this wondrous Dharma's own device avoid being obstructed from reaching ages yet to come, and the perfect and sudden essential teaching of the mind be forever transmitted to those who have not yet heard it? The text divides into five parts: first, citing the buddhas of the six directions praising it, exhorting faith, and transmitting it; second, re-citing the name and explaining it, exhorting faith and transmitting it; third, speaking in terms of past, present, and future rebirth, exhorting the vow and transmitting it; fourth, citing the buddhas' own praise of how difficult a matter this is, urging faith and transmitting it; fifth, the assembly rejoicing, believing, and accepting it, transmitting it.

The East, and Why the Tongue Covers a Universe

流通分・引六方諸佛稱讚（一）東方
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》 — see the full sūtra translation

舍利弗！如我今者讚歎阿彌陀佛不可思議功德之利。東方亦有阿閦鞞佛、須彌相佛、大須彌佛、須彌光佛、妙音佛，如是等恒河沙數諸佛，各於其國，出廣長舌相，遍覆三千大千世界，說誠實言：汝等眾生當信是稱讚不可思議功德，一切諸佛所護念經。

Śāriputra, just as I now praise the inconceivable benefit of Amitābha Buddha's merit, so too in the east there are the buddhas Akṣobhya, Sumeru-Appearance, Great Sumeru, Sumeru-Light, and Wondrous-Sound — buddhas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges — each of whom, in his own land, puts forth the sign of the broad and long tongue, covering an entire billion-world universe, and speaks these true words: "You beings should believe in this sūtra, praised for its inconceivable merit, that all buddhas keep in mind and protect."

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

初中六：初、東方(至)六、上方。唐譯十方，當知文有詳略、義無增減也。今初。承上正宗分中廣讚阿彌陀佛依正莊嚴，勸人信願持名徑登不退，此於出世大功德利之中尤為不可思議，故不惟我釋迦如來讚歎，舉凡六方諸佛無不異口同音讚歎也。言不可思議者，略有五意：一、橫超三界不俟斷惑故；二、即於西方橫具四土非由漸證故；三、但持名號不假禪觀諸方便故；四、一七為期不藉多劫多生多年月故；五、持一佛名即為諸佛護念不異持一切佛名故。阿閦鞞此云無動，餘皆可知。佛有無量德，應有無量名，隨機立名，或取因或取果、或性或相、或行願等，雖偏舉一隅，仍各具四悉，隨一一名顯所詮德，劫壽說之亦不可盡，但可意解而已。東方虛空不可盡故，世界亦不可盡。世界不可盡故，現住世間諸佛亦不可盡，略舉五名以等恒河沙數諸佛。如此諸佛各出廣長舌相說誠實言勸信此經，而眾生猶不生信，頑冥極矣。言廣長舌相者，尋常之人三世不妄語則舌能至鼻；藏頭果佛三大阿僧祇劫久不妄語，故舌薄而廣長出可覆面。

今證大乘淨土妙門，所以遍覆三千大千世界，用表言必誠實決非虛謬也。此經正名「稱讚不可思議功德一切諸佛所護念經」，羅什法師順此方好略之機故，但譯云「佛說阿彌陀經」，玄奘法師取捨兩楹，故又譯云「稱讚淨土佛攝受經」，當知亦是文有詳略、義無增減。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first has six parts: first, the east, up to sixth, above. Xuanzang's translation gives ten directions — one should understand that the text differs in fullness of detail, though its meaning gains and loses nothing. Here is the first. Carrying forward from the Main Teaching above, which broadly praised Amitābha Buddha's dependent and direct adornments, exhorting people to hold the name with faith and vow and thereby ascend directly to non-retrogression — this, among the great benefits of merit that transcend the world, is especially inconceivable; hence it is not only I, the Tathāgata Śākyamuni, who praise it, but every buddha of the six directions, without exception, praising it with one and the same voice. As for "inconceivable," there are, in brief, five senses: first, transcending the three realms directly, without waiting to sever affliction; second, at this very West already possessing all four kinds of land side by side, not attained through gradual realization; third, merely holding the name, requiring no reliance on meditative contemplation or other skillful means; fourth, a period of only one to seven days, requiring no reliance on many kalpas, many lifetimes, or many years and months; fifth, holding a single buddha's name is itself already being kept in mind and protected by every buddha, no different from holding every buddha's name at once. Akṣobhya translates as "Unmoving"; the rest may be understood similarly. A buddha possesses immeasurable virtues, and so should possess immeasurable names — names established according to capacity, sometimes taken from cause, sometimes from fruit, sometimes from nature, sometimes from characteristic, sometimes from practice or vow, and so on; though each name singles out only one particular facet, each nonetheless carries all four siddhāntas within it. Each and every name reveals the virtue it designates; to speak of them for a whole kalpa's lifespan could still not exhaust them — one can only grasp their meaning and let the rest be understood. Since the east's empty space cannot be exhausted, its worlds too cannot be exhausted; since its worlds cannot be exhausted, the buddhas presently dwelling in the world there, too, cannot be exhausted — five names are given only in brief, to stand for buddhas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges. Buddhas such as these, each putting forth the sign of the broad and long tongue, speaking true words to exhort belief in this sūtra — and yet sentient beings still fail to give rise to faith — how utterly obstinate and benighted this is! As for the sign of the broad and long tongue: an ordinary person who has not spoken falsely across three lifetimes has a tongue able to reach to the tip of the nose; a buddha, the fruit of the deepest root, having gone without false speech for three great asaṃkhyeya kalpas, has a tongue thin and broad, long enough, once put forth, to cover the entire face.

Now, in bearing witness to this wondrous gate of the Mahāyāna Pure Land, the tongue covers an entire billion-world universe, precisely in order to represent that these words are certainly true and sincere, by no means false or mistaken. This sūtra's own proper title is "The Sūtra of Praising the Inconceivable Merit that All Buddhas Keep in Mind and Protect." Master Kumārajīva, according with this land's own preference for brevity, translated it simply as "The Buddha's Discourse on Amitābha"; Master Xuanzang, taking the middle course between the two, translated it instead as "The Sūtra of Praising the Pure Land, Received by the Buddha." One should understand that here, too, the text differs in fullness of detail, though its meaning gains and loses nothing.

South, West, North, Below, and Above

流通分・引六方諸佛稱讚（二至六）
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

舍利弗！南方世界有日月燈佛、名聞光佛、大焰肩佛、須彌燈佛、無量精進佛……西方世界有無量壽佛、無量相佛、無量幢佛、大光佛、大明佛、寶相佛、淨光佛……北方世界有焰肩佛、最勝音佛、難沮佛、日生佛、網明佛……下方世界有師子佛、名聞佛、名光佛、達磨佛、法幢佛、持法佛……上方世界有梵音佛、宿王佛、香上佛、香光佛、大焰肩佛、雜色寶華嚴身佛、娑羅樹王佛、寶華德佛、見一切義佛、如須彌山佛，如是等恒河沙數諸佛，各於其國，出廣長舌相，遍覆三千大千世界，說誠實言：汝等眾生當信是稱讚不可思議功德，一切諸佛所護念經。

See the full text of all six directions in the sūtra's own Section 9 — the south names Sun-and-Moon-Lamp and four others; the west, Infinite Life and six others; the north, Blazing-Shoulders and four others; below, Lion and five others; above, Brahma-Sound and nine others — each direction closing with the identical refrain of the broad, long tongue and the call to believe.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary (west: is this Amitābha?)

問：「無量壽佛即是極樂世界阿彌陀佛否？」答：「即是亦可，不是亦可。即是亦可者，佛無我相，為度眾生不妨自讚，如釋迦亦自讚故。不是亦可者，十方同名諸佛無量，非止一佛名為無量壽故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: "Is this buddha "Infinite Life," named among the west's own buddhas, the very same Amitābha Buddha of the World of Utmost Bliss?" Answer: "It may be granted that he is the same, and it may equally be granted that he is not. That he may be granted as the same is because a buddha has no mark of self, and so, for the sake of liberating beings, there is no harm in praising himself — just as Śākyamuni, too, praises himself. That he may be granted as not the same is because, throughout the ten directions, buddhas sharing an identical name are immeasurable — it is not only a single buddha who is named "Infinite Life.""

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary (below and above)

下方者，此界水輪、金輪、風輪之下，復有下界非非想天等，乃至重重無盡不可說不可說也。達磨此云法，餘可知。上方者，此界非非想天之上，復有上界風輪、銀輪及三界等，重重無盡不可說不可說也。

As for "below": beneath this world's own wheel of water, wheel of gold, and wheel of wind, there is, further below, yet another world with its own peak of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, and so on, layer upon layer without end, beyond all telling, beyond all telling. "Dharma" [達磨] means precisely "Dharma"; the rest may be understood similarly. As for "above": above this world's own peak of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, there is, further above, yet another world with its own wheel of wind, wheel of silver, and the three realms again, and so on, layer upon layer without end, beyond all telling, beyond all telling.

Question: "Since each of the six directions has its own buddhas, each must also have its own pure land — why praise the west alone and specially?"

Answer: "It is so that beginners may easily fix their minds on a single point; and moreover because Amitābha's own original vow is supreme; and moreover because beings of this land have a special affinity with him; and moreover because being mindful of the single buddha Amitābha is already being mindful of immeasurable buddhas, and being born in the single pure land of the west is already being born throughout immeasurable pure lands at once. If a person of shallow attainment understands that the west surpasses every other direction, he can then resolve his intent and seek it exclusively; if a person of deep attainment understands that the west already gathers in the ten directions, already contains all four kinds of land within it, he likewise need not abandon the west in order to seek the Flower-Treasury World elsewhere. For to claim that the west is provisional and the Flower-Treasury World is real, that the west is small and the Flower-Treasury World is great, falls entirely into sentient beings' own universally-clung-to, discriminating feeling — a failure to understand that provisional and real share one and the same essence, and that great and small have no fixed nature of their own."

Translator's note

Compare this closing Q&A with the corresponding reading note to Section 9 of the sūtra's own page, which discusses the same textual variant between Kumārajīva's six directions and Xuanzang's ten. "The Flower-Treasury World" (華藏) is the Avataṃsaka's own cosmic setting, presided over by Vairocana — Ouyi's closing point is that treating it as somehow more "real" or "large" than Sukhāvatī repeats the exact error his commentary has argued against since Part 4: mistaking a difference of skillful emphasis for a difference in ultimate status.

Why "Kept in Mind by All Buddhas," and Whether Mere Hearing Is Enough

流通分・重牒名解釋勸信流通
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

舍利弗！於汝意云何？何故名為一切諸佛所護念經耶？舍利弗！若有善男子、善女人聞是經受持者及聞諸佛名者，是諸善男子、善女人皆為一切諸佛之所護念，皆得不退轉於阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。是故，舍利弗！汝等皆當信受我語及諸佛所說。

Śāriputra, what do you think? Why is this called the sūtra that all buddhas keep in mind and protect? Śāriputra, if there is a good son or good daughter who hears this sūtra and upholds it, or who hears the names of these buddhas, then that good son or good daughter will be kept in mind and protected by all buddhas, and will attain non-retrogression from unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening. Therefore, Śāriputra, you should all believe and accept my words, and the words spoken by all these buddhas.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

二、重牒名解釋勸信流通。阿耨多羅此云無上，三藐三菩提此云正等正覺，即大乘果覺也。此大乘經獨詮簡易直捷圓頓心要，又諸佛名字竝詮無上圓滿究竟萬德，故聞經受持及聞諸佛名者，皆為諸佛所護念，皆得不退大菩提也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, re-citing the name and explaining it, exhorting faith and transmitting it. Anuttara translates as "unsurpassed"; samyaksaṃbodhi translates as "perfect and complete awakening" — this is precisely the fruit-realization of the Mahāyāna. This Mahāyāna sūtra alone expounds the simple, easy, direct, perfect and sudden essential teaching of the mind; and moreover, the buddhas' names, every one of them, expound the unsurpassed, perfect, complete, ultimate ten thousand virtues — hence whoever hears this sūtra and upholds it, and whoever hears the names of these buddhas, is, without exception, kept in mind and protected by all buddhas, and, without exception, attains non-retrogression from great bodhi.

Question: "That hearing the sūtra, upholding it, being kept in mind and protected, and attaining non-retrogression should go together — this may be granted. But merely hearing a buddha's name — could this too secure being kept in mind and protected, and non-retrogression?"

Answer: "This point has both a restricted and a general sense. As for the restricted sense: the Divination Sūtra says: 'If, with a chaotic, defiled mind, one merely recites my name, this cannot properly be called "hearing," since it is unable to give rise to definite belief and understanding, and one obtains only a worldly, wholesome reward, not the vast, profound, subtle benefit. Only upon reaching the samādhi of a single practice does one accomplish a vast, subtle mind of practice, called attaining a resemblance to the patient acceptance of the unborn — only then is it truly "hearing" the names of the buddhas of the ten directions.' The present case should likewise be understood this way — one must therefore hear this sūtra and thereupon uphold the method of holding the name it sets forth, holding it all the way to one mind, undistracted, before one truly "hears" the buddhas' names, and before one is truly kept in mind and protected by all buddhas.

"As for the general sense: the compassion of the buddhas is inconceivable, and the merit and virtue of their names are likewise inconceivable. Hence, the very moment one hears a buddha's name — regardless of whether one's mind is engaged or not, whether one believes or even reviles it — this already becomes a conditioning seed. It is like entering a forest of sandalwood: whether one bows to the trees or strikes them, one is dyed with their fragrance all the same. How much more so, then, with a buddha liberating beings, making no distinction between enemy and friend, constantly untiring — if one merely hears a buddha's name, that buddha will certainly keep one in mind and protect one. What further doubt could there be? Yet according to the three Prajñā treatises on the Vajracchedikā, all of them say that only a bodhisattva of ripened capacity is kept in mind and protected by the buddhas, a stage that must lie at the distinct teaching's grounds or the perfect teaching's abidings — this, however, is reckoned only in terms of one's own self-power, and so one must have already entered the same generative nature before being kept in mind and protected at all. Here, by contrast, one relies on other-power, and so even at the level of resemblance, one already receives this protection, and is able to fully attain all three kinds of non-retrogression. Down to the mere hearing of a buddha's name, this too, through the shared essence of the nature of dharmas, has a power to assist and rouse one, and likewise serves as a distant cause of ultimate non-retrogression. The merit of hearing a name is as great as this — spoken jointly by Śākyamuni and the buddhas of the six directions alike — how could this not be believed?"

Translator's note

"The Divination Sūtra" (占察經) is the Sūtra on the Divination of Good and Evil Karma, already translated in full elsewhere on this site as the Divination of Good and Evil Karma. "Self-power" and "other-power" (自力/他力) name the central distinction Pure Land teaching draws between paths that depend on the practitioner's own cultivated realization and paths that depend on a buddha's vow-power reaching a practitioner regardless of their present attainment — the same distinction underlying "non-retrogression in stage," secured merely by rebirth, first introduced back on Part 3.

Already Vowed, Now Vowing, Will Vow

流通分・約已今當生勸願流通
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

舍利弗！若有人已發願、今發願、當發願，欲生阿彌陀佛國者，是諸人等皆得不退轉於阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，於彼國土若已生、若今生、若當生。是故，舍利弗！諸善男子、善女人，若有信者，應當發願生彼國土。

Śāriputra, if there are people who have already made the vow, are now making the vow, or will make the vow to be born in Amitābha Buddha's land, all these people attain non-retrogression from unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and with regard to that land, they have already been born, are being born now, or will be born there. Therefore, Śāriputra, any good son or good daughter who has faith should make the vow to be born in that land.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

三、約已今當生勸願流通。已發願者即已生，今發願者即今生，當發願者即當生，正顯依信所發之願，願無虛發必剋果也。非信則不能發願，雖信而不發願亦不能生，故殷勤再勸發願。願者信之券，而行之樞尤為要務，舉願則信行在其中矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, speaking in terms of past, present, and future rebirth, exhorting the vow and transmitting it. Whoever has already vowed has already been born; whoever is now vowing is being born now; whoever will vow will be born then — this directly reveals that the vow, made in dependence on faith, is a vow never made in vain, but certain to bear its fruit. Without faith one cannot make the vow, and even having faith, if one does not make the vow, one still cannot be reborn — hence the earnest, repeated exhortation to make the vow. Vow is the pledge of faith, and, as the pivot of practice, is a matter of especial importance; raise up the vow, and faith and practice are already contained within it.

Question: "One who is now making the vow should only attain future rebirth — how can it say "being born now"?"

Answer: "This too carries two senses. First, reckoned in the general sense of an entire period as "now": if, in this present life, one makes the vow and holds the name, then, at the end of life, one is certain to attain rebirth in the Pure Land — this is what is meant by "vowing now is being born now." Second, reckoned in the strict sense of a single instant as "now": one thought in accord is one thought's birth; thought after thought in accord is birth after birth — the wondrous cause and the wondrous fruit never depart from the single mind, like the two ends of a scale, rising and falling together in the very same instant. Why, then, wait for the sahā world's karmic reward to be exhausted before being nurtured in the jeweled pond? This very moment, with faith, vow, and holding the name, one is already no longer a person within the sahā world's own boundary. This is the utmost in perfection, the utmost in suddenness — difficult to discuss, difficult to conceive; only one of great wisdom can truly and fully believe it."

The Hardest Teaching in the World

流通分・引諸佛稱讚難事敦信流通
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

舍利弗！如我今者稱讚諸佛不可思議功德，彼諸佛等亦稱讚我不可思議功德，而作是言：釋迦牟尼佛能為甚難希有之事，能於娑婆國土五濁惡世劫濁、見濁、煩惱濁、眾生濁、命濁中得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，為諸眾生說是一切世間難信之法。舍利弗！當知我於五濁惡世行此難事，得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，為一切世間說此難信之法是為甚難！

Śāriputra, just as I now praise the inconceivable merit of all the buddhas, those buddhas likewise praise my own inconceivable merit, speaking these words: "Śākyamuni Buddha has been able to do an extremely difficult and rare thing — able, in the sahā world, in the evil age of the five turbidities — the turbidity of the kalpa, the turbidity of views, the turbidity of afflictions, the turbidity of beings, and the turbidity of lifespan — to attain unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and, for the sake of all beings, to speak this Dharma that the whole world finds hard to believe." Śāriputra, you should know that I, in this evil age of the five turbidities, have done this difficult thing — attaining unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, and speaking, for the sake of the whole world, this Dharma that is hard to believe — this indeed is extremely difficult!

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

四、引諸佛稱讚難事敦信流通。又二：初。引諸佛同讚；二、自結歎難信。今初。諸佛功德智慧雖皆平等，而施化則有難易。於淨土成菩提易，於濁世成菩提難；為淨土眾生說法易，為濁世眾生說法難；為濁世眾生說漸法猶易，說頓法尤難；為濁世眾生說餘頓法猶易，說此淨土橫超頓法尤難；為濁世眾生說淨土橫超頓修頓證妙觀已自不易，說此無藉劬勞修證，但持名號徑登不退，奇特勝妙超出思議第一方便，更為難中之難。以是一切九法界世間所難信故，所以名為甚難希有之事。而十方一切諸佛，無一不稱揚讚歎推我釋迦偏為勇猛也。劫濁者，濁法聚會之時。見濁者，五利使邪見增盛，所謂身見、邊見、見取、戒取及諸邪見，昏昧汩沒故名為濁。煩惱濁者，五鈍使煩惑增盛，所謂貪、瞋、癡、慢、疑，煩動惱亂故名為濁。眾生濁者，見煩惱所感麁弊五陰和合假名眾生，色心竝皆陋劣故名濁。命濁者因果竝劣壽命短促，乃至不滿百歲故名濁。

二、自結歎難信。信願持名萬修萬去，永離五濁圓淨四土，如此妙法真實難信，故不惟諸佛稱讚，即自亦歎其甚難。設非大悲弘誓塵劫熏心，何能行此難事也哉？此而不信，辜負慈尊甚矣。四、敦信流通竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, citing the buddhas' own praise of how difficult a matter this is, urging faith and transmitting it. Again in two parts: first, citing the buddhas' own joint praise; second, concluding by lamenting how difficult it is to believe. Here is the first. Although the merit and wisdom of every buddha alike are equal, the ease or difficulty of their converting activity nonetheless varies. Attaining bodhi in a pure land is easy; attaining bodhi in a turbid world is difficult. Teaching the Dharma to beings of a pure land is easy; teaching beings of a turbid world is difficult. Teaching a gradual dharma to beings of a turbid world is still relatively easy; teaching a sudden dharma is especially difficult. Teaching some other sudden dharma to beings of a turbid world is still relatively easy; teaching this Pure Land's directly-transcending sudden dharma is especially difficult. Teaching beings of a turbid world the Pure Land's directly-transcending, suddenly-cultivated, suddenly-realized wondrous contemplation is, in itself, already no easy matter — but teaching this dharma that requires no toilsome cultivation and realization at all, merely holding the name and directly ascending to non-retrogression, extraordinary and supremely wondrous, surpassing all conceivable thought, the foremost of all skillful means — this is harder still, the most difficult of the difficult. It is for this reason that it is hard to believe throughout every world of the nine dharma-realms, and is therefore called an extremely difficult and rare thing. And every buddha of the ten directions, without a single exception, praises and extols this, crediting me, Śākyamuni, with a valor uniquely my own.

The turbidity of the kalpa is the time when turbid dharmas gather and converge. The turbidity of views is the flourishing of the five sharp afflictive views — namely, the view of self, extreme views, the clinging to views, the clinging to precepts and rituals, and every other wrong view — dark, confused, and drowning, hence called turbid. The turbidity of afflictions is the flourishing of the five dull afflictive delusions — namely, greed, hatred, delusion, arrogance, and doubt — agitating and afflicting the mind, hence called turbid. The turbidity of beings is the coarse, defiled coming-together of the five aggregates brought about by views and affliction, provisionally called "a sentient being," both form and mind being base and inferior, hence called turbid. The turbidity of lifespan is where both cause and effect are inferior, and lifespan itself is short and pressing, not even reaching a full hundred years, hence called turbid.

Second, concluding by lamenting how difficult it is to believe. With faith, vow, and holding the name, ten thousand out of ten thousand who practice it depart there — forever free of the five turbidities, perfectly purifying all four kinds of land. A wondrous dharma such as this is truly difficult to believe; hence it is not only the buddhas who praise it, but he himself, too, laments how extremely difficult it is. Were it not for a great compassionate vow that has steeped his mind across kalpas as numerous as motes of dust, how could he ever accomplish so difficult a matter? To fail to believe this would be to gravely betray the compassionate lord. This closes the fourth part: urging faith and transmitting it.

The Assembly Rejoices

流通分・大眾歡喜信受流通
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

佛說此經已，舍利弗及諸比丘，一切世間天、人、阿修羅等，聞佛所說，歡喜信受，作禮而去。

When the Buddha had finished speaking this sūtra, Śāriputra, together with the monks, and all the devas, humans, and asuras of the world, having heard what the Buddha had spoken, rejoiced, believed, and accepted it; and, making obeisance, they departed.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

五、大眾歡喜信受流通。法門不可思議難信難知，故無一人能發問者；佛智鑒機知機已熟，故無問自說令得四益。既得聞已，如時雨化，故歡喜信受也。身心怡悅名為歡喜，毫無疑惑名之為信，領持不忘名之為受，感大恩德投身歸命名為作禮，依教修一往不退名為而去。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the assembly rejoicing, believing, and accepting it, transmitting it. This Dharma-gate is inconceivable, hard to believe, and hard to know — hence no single person was able to raise a question about it. The Buddha's own wisdom, discerning capacity, knew that capacity had already ripened, and so he spoke it entirely unprompted, without being asked, causing beings to obtain the four benefits. Having now heard it, they were transformed like plants by seasonable rain — hence they rejoiced, believed, and accepted it. Body and mind at ease and delighted is called "rejoicing"; utterly without doubt or confusion is called "believing"; receiving and holding it without forgetting is called "accepting"; moved by the great debt of kindness, casting oneself down and entrusting one's very life, is called "making obeisance"; and cultivating in accordance with the teaching, going forth without ever turning back, is called "departing."

Translator's note

This closes the sūtra's own text and Ouyi's line-by-line commentary on it. No comment on the asuras present in this closing assembly appears anywhere in the commentary — for the corresponding reading note on the sūtra's own page, which currently attributes to Ouyi a specific observation about them, see the note to Section 13.

Ouyi's Postscript

跋語
Chinese Source

佛說阿彌陀經要解跋語

經云：「末法之中億億人修行，罕有一得道者，惟依念佛得度。」嗚呼！今正是其時矣！捨此不可思議法門，其何能淑？旭於初出家時，亦以宗乘自負藐視教典，妄謂持名法門不過曲為中下，後因大病發意西歸，嗣復研求《妙宗》《圓中》二鈔及雲棲疏鈔等書，始知念佛三昧實稱無上寶王，方肯死心執持名號，萬牛莫能挽也。適吾友去病大士久事淨業，欲令此經大旨辭不繁而即炳著，命余述此要解；余正欲普與法界有情同生極樂，理不可却。舉筆於丁亥年九月二十有七，脫藁於十月初五，凡九日而告成。所願一句一字咸作資糧，一見一聞同階不退，或信或疑皆植道種，或讚或謗等歸解脫。仰惟諸佛菩薩攝受證明，同學友人隨喜加被。西有道人蕅益智旭閣筆故跋，時年四十有九。

Ru-Yi Translation

Postscript to the Essential Explanation of the Buddha's Discourse on Amitābha.

A sūtra says: "In the age of the Dharma's decline, among a hundred million upon a hundred million who practice, rarely is even a single one who attains the path — only by relying on buddha-recollection can one be liberated." Alas! Now is precisely that very time! Setting aside this inconceivable Dharma-gate, by what other means could one possibly be made whole? When I, Zhixu, first left the household life, I too, proud of my own grasp of the doctrinal vehicles, looked down upon the teaching-canon, foolishly supposing that the Dharma-gate of holding the name was fit only for the middling and the lesser. Later, on account of a grave illness, I set my mind on returning to the West; and thereafter I studied and sought out the two commentaries, the Miaozong Chao and the Yuanzhong Chao, together with Yunqi's Shuchao and other such works, and only then came to know that the samādhi of buddha-recollection is truly worthy of the title "unsurpassed jewel king." Only then was I willing to set my mind to rest and hold fast to the name — a resolve ten thousand oxen could not have dragged away.

It happened that my friend the great being Qubing, who had long devoted himself to pure karma, wished this sūtra's own essential meaning to be made brilliantly clear without excessive elaboration, and asked me to set forth this Essential Explanation. I myself had long wished to be reborn together in Utmost Bliss with every sentient being throughout the whole Dharma-realm, and so, by reason itself, could not decline. I took up the brush on the twenty-seventh day of the ninth month of the year dinghai [1647], and completed the draft on the fifth day of the tenth month — nine days in all before it was finished. My wish is that every single phrase and word may serve as provisions for the journey; that every seeing, every hearing, may equally ascend to non-retrogression; that whether one believes or doubts, a seed of the path is planted either way; that whether one praises or reviles it, both alike return to liberation. I look up in reverence to every buddha and bodhisattva to receive and bear witness to this, and to my fellow students and friends to rejoice in it and lend their support. Written and set down by the Way-farer of Xiyou, Zhixu of Ouyi, who lays down his brush with this postscript, at the age of forty-nine.

Translator's note

This is the first-hand record behind the memory of this project noted at the outset: nine days, from the twenty-seventh of the ninth month to the fifth of the tenth month, in the dinghai year of the Chinese sexagenary cycle — 1647 by the Western calendar, the third year of the Shunzhi era of the newly established Qing dynasty, four years before Ouyi's own death in 1655. "Qubing" is the courtesy name of Ouyi's lay disciple and patron; nothing further is recorded of him in this commentary itself.

The Printer's Postscript

刻彌陀要解後序

Postscript to the Printing of the Amituo Yaojie

A sūtra says: "The three realms are mind-only; the ten thousand dharmas are consciousness-only." The ancients said: "Recollect the Amitābha of one's own nature, and be born in the Pure Land of one's own mind." Considered together, this makes plain the meaning of "this very mind creates the Buddha, this very mind is the Buddha," and "there is no buddha outside the mind, no mind outside the Buddha." Later readers, failing to grasp this, set aside the Western land of Utmost Bliss to speak instead of a separate "Pure Land of mind-only," and set aside the compassionate lord of ten thousand virtues to speak instead of a separate "Amitābha of one's own nature" — is this not very close to positing a buddha outside the mind, a mind outside the Buddha?

My own teacher Ouyi has deeply realized the substance of mind-nature as having nothing outside it, and so threads doctrine and teaching, nature and characteristic, together as one. Having already composed such works as the Fanwang Hezhu, the Foding Xuanwen, the Weishi Xinyao, and the Dasheng Zhiguan Shiyao, clarifying the wondrous principle of the provisional and the real, he has now further composed this Amituo Yaojie for the sake of one truly aware — and the moment one opens and reads it, one at once senses that anyone who holds the name with faith and vow can grasp its very warrant in his own hand. It may truly be called direct, forthright, its language spare yet its meaning complete.

For among the great beings who propagate the Dharma in this final age, each tends to specialize in his own particular field, and few are able to master both nature-teaching and characteristic-teaching together; only Master Ouyi, beginning first from the very source of mind and penetrating it thoroughly, is able to move with total ease through nature and characteristic, doctrine and teaching alike, understanding fully that mind, Buddha, and sentient beings are, the three of them, without any difference at all; that the hundred realms and thousand suchnesses are both inherently possessed by mind and created by mind; and that every feature of the Pure Land's dependent and direct reward is nothing but a projected aspect transformed from one's own mind — hence he holds the intricate structure of the four lands, crosswise and lengthwise, as clearly as pointing to his own palm. And yet this is but a single drop from the ocean of the Dharma! Should one wish to plumb the full depths of Master Ouyi's own learning, one ought to read widely through his major works — the Hezhu, the Xuanwen, the Xinyao, and the rest — and further seek to understand him even before he ever took up the brush at all; only then will one avoid mistaking the pointing finger for the moon itself.

— Zhengzhi, a disciple in pure karma of ancient Wu

Translator's note

Zhengzhi (正知) identifies himself only as a lay disciple of Ouyi's own; nothing further is recorded of him beyond this postscript. The works he names — the Fanwang Hezhu (梵網合註), Foding Xuanwen (佛頂玄文), Weishi Xinyao (唯識心要), and Dasheng Zhiguan Shiyao (大乘止觀釋要) — are among Ouyi's major independent commentaries, distinct from the Fanwang Xuanyi cited on Part 2.

A Dedication of Merit

迴向文

The disciple Shen Chunshe vows, by the merit of printing and distributing this text, to dedicate it upward for his late father, the venerable gentleman styled Qiasuo, personal name Zixun; his late mother, the pious lady of the Pan clan; his late wife, the pious lady of the Chen clan; his deceased concubine, of the Peng clan; and his deceased daughter, Qiguan — that they may together be born in the Pure Land, forever without retrogression; and further prays for the protection of his grandsons Fuyin and Nianwen, and his great-grandsons Shitang, Zhaojia, Duanci, and the rest — that they may take refuge in the Three Jewels, and cultivate blessings and wisdom across the generations. I humbly beseech our Buddha's compassion to keep them in mind and protect them.

Translator's note

This closing dedication, attached to a later printing of the text rather than to Ouyi's own original manuscript, is the kind of colophon common on Chinese Buddhist woodblock editions — a donor's own act of merit-transfer for deceased family members, preserved here alongside the commentary itself as part of the transmitted text.
