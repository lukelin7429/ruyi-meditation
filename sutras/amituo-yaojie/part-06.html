Heavenly Music, and Birds That Preach the Path

The description of Sukhāvatī continues: ceaseless music, a ground of gold, and beings who breakfast across a hundred thousand million buddha-lands before returning home in time for lunch. Then the sūtra turns from scenery to sound — birds whose song is itself a Dharma teaching, prompting Ouyi to gloss all thirty-seven factors of enlightenment in full, one by one, the fullest single doctrinal digression in the whole commentary.

Heavenly Music, and the Morning Offering-Round

正宗分・陳依報國土妙・別釋莊嚴（三）天樂華雨莊嚴
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》 — see the full sūtra translation

又，舍利弗！彼佛國土，常作天樂，黃金為地，晝夜六時，雨天曼陀羅華。其土眾生，常以清旦，各以衣裓盛眾妙華，供養他方十萬億佛；即以食時，還到本國，飯食經行。舍利弗！極樂國土成就如是功德莊嚴。

And, Śāriputra, in that buddha-land heavenly music is always playing, the ground is made of gold, and in each of the six periods of the day and night a rain of heavenly māndārava flowers falls. Each morning, at first light, the beings of that land fill the folds of their robes with these wondrous flowers and make offerings to the hundred thousand million buddhas of other worlds; then, in time for the midday meal, they return to their own land, take their meal, and walk in mindful exercise. Śāriputra, the land of Utmost Bliss is accomplished with just such adornments of merit.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

三、明天樂華雨莊嚴。諸天所作微妙樂音恒無間斷，故云常作天樂。七寶所嚴清淨地界體是黃金，故云黃金為地。初日分、後日分名晝三時，初夜分、中夜分、後夜分名夜三時，故云晝夜六時。然彼土佛及菩薩各有身光，眾生生者亦有身光，常明不闇，不假日月光明，安分晝夜？或以華開鳥鳴為晝，華合鳥棲為夜。或雖實無晝夜，且順此方常習假說晝夜耳。曼陀羅者，天華之名，此云適意亦云白華。從空而下故名為雨。眾生者，上自等覺下至凡夫。衣裓者，盛華之器。眾妙華者，即所雨天華，明其非止曼陀羅一種而已，應如《法華經》所明四華，以表住、行、向、地四因位也。供養他方佛者，即表真因會趨極果、果德無所不遍也。僅言十萬億者，且據極樂去此娑婆十萬億土，意顯得生極樂國已，欲還供釋迦彌勒皆不難耳。若據彌陀神力所加，則何遠不到哉？即以食時還到本國者，明其神足自在不假逾時也。飯食經行者，既云食時故云飯食，念食則食自至，不俟造作安排，食畢則鉢自去，不勞洗拭收舉，故但經行寶地任運進修而已。凡聖同居淨土其樂已自如此，況即於彼土又可橫超方便、實報、寂光，其樂又當何如！

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying the adornment of heavenly music and the rain of flowers. The subtle, wondrous music made by the various devas never ceases, hence it says "heavenly music is always playing." The pure ground, adorned with the seven treasures, has gold as its very substance, hence it says "the ground is made of gold." The first and latter portions of the day are called the day's three watches; the first, middle, and latter portions of the night are called the night's three watches — hence it says "the six periods of the day and night." Yet the buddha and bodhisattvas of that land each possess a bodily radiance of their own, and the beings born there likewise possess a bodily radiance, constantly bright and never dark, needing no light from sun or moon — how, then, could day and night even be distinguished? Perhaps it is that the flowers opening and the birds singing are taken as day, and the flowers closing and the birds roosting are taken as night; or perhaps, although there is truly no day and night at all, this is only spoken of provisionally as "day and night," following this world's own habitual usage. "Māndārava" is the name of a heavenly flower, meaning "pleasing to the mind," also called "white flower." Falling down from the sky, it is called a "rain." "The beings" here means everyone from those at the stage of equal enlightenment down to ordinary beings. "The folds of their robes" are vessels for holding flowers. "These wondrous flowers" are precisely the heavenly flowers that rain down, making clear that it is not the māndārava alone — one should understand this as the four kinds of flowers set out in the Lotus Sūtra, representing the four causal stages of abiding, practice, dedication, and ground. "Making offerings to the buddhas of other worlds" represents the true cause converging toward the ultimate fruit, the fruit's own virtue pervading everywhere without exception. That it names only "a hundred thousand million" is because this is reckoned from Utmost Bliss's own distance of a hundred thousand million lands from this sahā world — the meaning being that, once one has attained birth in the land of Utmost Bliss, returning to make offerings to Śākyamuni and Maitreya presents no difficulty whatsoever. And if one further relies on the empowering of Amitābha's own spiritual power, then how could any distance be too far to reach? "Then, in time for the midday meal, they return to their own land" makes clear that their spiritual feet move freely at will, requiring no passage of time at all. As for "take their meal, and walk in mindful exercise": since it has already said "in time for the meal," it therefore says "take their meal" — the moment food is thought of, the food itself arrives, requiring no preparation or arrangement; once the meal is finished, the bowl of itself departs, requiring no washing, wiping, or putting away — hence they simply walk about the jeweled ground, advancing spontaneously in their own cultivation. If the joy of the pure land of common dwelling is already like this, how much more so, then, when one may, from within that very land, also transcend directly into the expedient land, the realm of real reward, and tranquil light itself — what must that joy be like!

Translator's note

Compare the shorter treatment of this passage in the reading note to Section 4 of the sūtra's own page. "The four kinds of flowers set out in the Lotus Sūtra" refers to that sūtra's opening scene, where four flower-showers mark four distinct grades of realization — a cross-reference Ouyi expects a Tiantai-trained reader to recognize without further gloss.

Fourth: The Adornment of the Dharma Sound — the Birds

正宗分・陳依報國土妙・別釋莊嚴（四）法音莊嚴・化鳥音
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》

復次，舍利弗！彼國常有種種奇妙雜色之鳥，白鶴、孔雀、鸚鵡、舍利、迦陵頻伽、共命之鳥，是諸眾鳥晝夜六時，出和雅音，其音演暢五根、五力、七菩提分、八聖道分，如是等法。其土眾生聞是音已，皆悉念佛、念法、念僧。

Furthermore, Śāriputra, that land always has flocks of rare and wondrous birds of every color — white cranes, peacocks, parrots, śārikas, kalaviṅkas, and birds of shared life. Throughout the six periods of day and night these birds send out a gentle, elegant call, and their call proclaims the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of awakening, the eightfold noble path, and teachings such as these. Hearing this call, the beings of that land all become mindful of the Buddha, mindful of the Dharma, mindful of the Saṅgha.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

四、明法音莊嚴。又二：初、別明二種法音；二、結歎功德成就。初中二：初、化鳥音；二、風樹音。初又二：初、正明法利；二、徵釋伏疑，今初。種種奇妙雜色之鳥，言其多且美也。且寄此間所愛賞者，以言其似。舍利即鶖鷺，及白鶴、孔雀、鸚鵡四種，皆此地所有，人所共珍。迦陵頻伽此云妙音，未出㲉時已有音聲超眾鳥故，此地所無。共命鳥，一身兩頭識別報同，亦此地所無。西土雪山等處有此二種，然皆不可比於極樂奇妙眾鳥，特言其似而已。言晝夜六時出和雅音，則知淨土亦不以鳥棲為夜，當知實無晝夜假說晝夜，良以蓮華化生之身本無昏睡不假夜臥故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, clarifying the adornment of the Dharma sound, in two parts: first, distinguishing the two kinds of Dharma sound; second, closing by praising the accomplishment of merit. The first has two: first, the transformed birds' sound; second, the wind-and-trees' sound. The first again has two: first, directly clarifying the Dharma's benefit; second, raising and resolving a lurking doubt. Here is the first. "Rare and wondrous birds of every color" speaks of their great number and beauty. And this borrows, from among the birds prized in our own world, those most fitting to describe their likeness. The śārika is simply the shrike; together with the white crane, peacock, and parrot, these four kinds all exist in this land, prized in common by all people. The kalaviṅka translates as "wondrous sound" — before it has even broken from its shell, its voice already surpasses every other bird — and this land has none. The bird of shared life has one body with two heads, its consciousness distinct though its karmic reward shared — this land, too, has none. The snowy mountains of the western regions do have these last two kinds, and yet even these cannot compare to the rare and wondrous birds of Utmost Bliss — this is specially spoken of only as a likeness. Saying that they send out their gentle, elegant call throughout the six periods of day and night shows that the Pure Land, too, does not take the birds' roosting as marking night — one should understand that there is truly no day and night there, spoken of only provisionally as "day and night," precisely because a body born by transformation from a lotus is, by its very nature, never drowsy and requires no lying down to sleep at night.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary (continued: the thirty-seven factors)

五根五力等者，三十七道品也。不言四念處、四正勤、四如意足者，文省略耳。三十七品通於四教。四念處者：一、身念處；二、受念處；三、心念處；四、法念處。四正勤者：一、已生惡法令斷；二、未生惡法不令生；三、未生善法令生；四、已生善法令增長。四如意足者：一、欲如意足；二、進如意足；三、心如意足；四、思惟如意足。五根者，信、進、念、定、慧也。信正道及助道法名信根；行正道及諸助道善法勤求不息名精進根；念正道及諸助道善法更無他念名念根；攝心在正道及諸助道善法中相應不散是名定根；為正道及諸助道善法觀於苦等四諦名慧根。五力者：信根增長，能破疑惑破諸邪信及破煩惱，名信力；精進根增長，破種種身心懈怠，成辦出世大事，名精進力；念根增長，破諸邪念，成就一切出世正念功德，是為念力；定根增長，能破亂想，發諸事理禪定，是為定力；慧根增長，能遮通別諸惑，發真無漏，是為慧力。七菩提分者亦名七覺分：一、智慧觀諸法時，善能簡別真偽，不謬取諸虛偽法，名擇法覺分；二、精進修諸道法時，善能覺了，不謬行於無益苦行，常勤心在真法中行，名精進覺分；三、若心得法喜，善能覺了此喜不依顛倒之法而生，歡喜住真法喜，名喜覺分；四、若斷除諸見煩惱之時，善能覺了除諸虛偽不損真正善根，名除覺分；五、若捨所見念著境時，善能覺了所捨之境虛偽不實永不追憶，名捨覺分；六、若發諸禪定之時，善能覺了諸禪虛假不生愛見妄想，名定覺分；七、若修出世道時，善能覺了常使定慧均平，或心沈沒當念用擇法、精進、喜三覺分以察起之，或心浮動當念用除、捨、定三覺分以攝持之，調和適中，名念覺分。八聖道分者亦名八正道分：一、修無漏行觀見四諦分明，名正見；二、以無漏心相應思惟動發覺知籌量，為令增長入大涅槃，名正思惟；三、以無漏慧除四邪命，攝諸口業住一切正語中，名正語；四、以無漏慧除身一切邪業，住清淨正身業中，名正業；五、以無漏慧通除三業中五種邪命，住清淨正命中，是為正命（五邪命者：一、為利養故詐現異相奇特；二、為利養故自說功德；三、為利養故占相吉凶為人說法；四、為利養故高聲現威令人敬畏；五、為利養故說所得供養以動人心）；六、以無漏慧相應勤精進修涅槃道，名正精進；七、以無漏慧相應念正道及助道法，名正念；八、以無漏慧相應入定，名正定。

As for "the five faculties, the five powers," and so on: these are the thirty-seven factors of the path. That the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right efforts, and the four bases of spiritual power are not named is merely a brevity of the text. The thirty-seven factors run through all Four Teachings alike. The four foundations of mindfulness are: first, mindfulness of the body; second, mindfulness of feeling; third, mindfulness of mind; fourth, mindfulness of dharmas. The four right efforts are: first, causing evil already arisen to be cut off; second, preventing evil not yet arisen from arising; third, causing good not yet arisen to arise; fourth, causing good already arisen to increase. The four bases of spiritual power are: first, the basis of aspiration; second, the basis of effort; third, the basis of mind; fourth, the basis of investigation. The five faculties are faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom: believing in the correct path and the dharmas that assist it is called the faculty of faith; practicing the correct path and every wholesome dharma that assists it, diligently seeking without rest, is called the faculty of effort; being mindful of the correct path and every wholesome dharma that assists it, with no other thought intervening, is called the faculty of mindfulness; gathering the mind within the correct path and its assisting dharmas, in accord with them and undistracted, is called the faculty of concentration; contemplating the Four Noble Truths of suffering and the rest, for the sake of the correct path and its assisting dharmas, is called the faculty of wisdom. The five powers: as the faculty of faith increases, it is able to break through doubt and confusion, break through every deviant belief, and break through affliction — this is called the power of faith; as the faculty of effort increases, it breaks through every kind of bodily and mental indolence, accomplishing the great matter of transcending the world — this is called the power of effort; as the faculty of mindfulness increases, it breaks through every deviant thought, accomplishing every merit of correct, world-transcending mindfulness — this is the power of mindfulness; as the faculty of concentration increases, it is able to break through scattered thinking, giving rise to meditative concentration in both phenomena and principle — this is the power of concentration; as the faculty of wisdom increases, it is able to obstruct every delusion, both shared and specific, giving rise to genuine, uncontaminated realization — this is the power of wisdom. The seven factors of awakening, also called the seven limbs of enlightenment: first, when wisdom contemplates every dharma, being well able to distinguish the true from the false, not mistakenly grasping at any false dharma — this is called the limb of discerning dharmas; second, when diligently cultivating every practice of the path, being well able to discern clearly, not mistakenly engaging in fruitless austerities, constantly and diligently keeping the mind engaged in the true dharma — this is called the limb of effort; third, when the mind attains joy in the Dharma, being well able to discern that this joy does not arise from any inverted dharma, joyfully abiding in the true joy of the Dharma — this is called the limb of joy; fourth, when cutting off the afflictions of every view, being well able to discern that removing every falsehood does not harm one's own genuine roots of goodness — this is called the limb of removal; fifth, when relinquishing whatever view or object of attachment one has held to, being well able to discern that the object relinquished is false and unreal, never to be recalled again — this is called the limb of relinquishment; sixth, when giving rise to meditative concentration of any kind, being well able to discern that every such concentration is illusory and false, giving rise to no craving, view, or false thought about it — this is called the limb of concentration; seventh, when cultivating the world-transcending path, being well able to discern how to keep concentration and wisdom constantly in balance — when the mind sinks, being mindful to use the three limbs of discerning dharmas, effort, and joy to rouse it; when the mind grows agitated, being mindful to use the three limbs of removal, relinquishment, and concentration to gather it in — harmonizing and adjusting to the proper measure — this is called the limb of mindfulness. The eightfold noble path, also called the eight right paths: first, cultivating uncontaminated practice, seeing the Four Noble Truths clearly — this is called right view; second, with an uncontaminated mind, thinking, stirring, discerning, and deliberating in accord with it, so as to increase and enter great nirvāṇa — this is called right intention; third, with uncontaminated wisdom, eliminating the four wrong livelihoods of speech, gathering every verbal karma into entirely right speech — this is called right speech; fourth, with uncontaminated wisdom, eliminating every wrong bodily karma, abiding in pure and correct bodily karma — this is called right action; fifth, with uncontaminated wisdom, thoroughly eliminating the five wrong livelihoods among the three karmas, abiding in pure right livelihood — this is right livelihood. (The five wrong livelihoods are: first, for the sake of gain, falsely displaying strange and extraordinary appearances; second, for the sake of gain, praising one's own merit and virtue; third, for the sake of gain, divining fortune and misfortune and preaching the Dharma to people accordingly; fourth, for the sake of gain, raising one's voice and displaying authority to inspire awe in others; fifth, for the sake of gain, speaking of the offerings one has received in order to stir people's hearts.) Sixth, with uncontaminated wisdom, diligently and vigorously cultivating the path to nirvāṇa — this is called right effort; seventh, with uncontaminated wisdom, being mindful of the correct path and the dharmas that assist it — this is called right mindfulness; eighth, with uncontaminated wisdom, entering into concentration — this is called right concentration.

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary (which teaching uses which factors)

此等道品若依生滅四諦而修，即藏教道品；若依無生四諦而修，即通教道品；若依無量四諦而修，即別教道品；若依無作四諦而修，即圓教道品。藏道品名為半字法門，淨土濁輕似不必用，為小種先熟者或暫用之。通教道品名大乘初門，三乘共稟，同居淨土多應說之。別教道品名為獨菩薩法，同居、方便二種淨土亦多說之。圓教道品名為無上佛法，有利根者於四淨土應皆得聞也。結云如是等法者，等前念處、正勤、如意足等，餘一切四攝、六度、十力、無畏無量法門也。雖諸佛法三十七品收無不盡，而為機緣不等，或作種種開合名義不同。以要言之，隨所欲聞無不演暢，故能令聞者皆生念佛念法念僧之心，所念三寶亦有別相一體乃至四教義意之不同，如餘經論廣明。

If these factors of the path are cultivated in dependence on the Four Noble Truths of generation-and-extinction, they are the Tripiṭaka teaching's own factors of the path; if cultivated in dependence on the Four Noble Truths of no generation, they are the shared teaching's own; if cultivated in dependence on the Four Noble Truths of the immeasurable, they are the distinct teaching's own; if cultivated in dependence on the Four Noble Truths of the unconditioned, they are the perfect teaching's own. The Tripiṭaka teaching's factors of the path are called the half-word Dharma-gate — since the Pure Land's turbidity is light, they would seem unnecessary there, though for one whose small-vehicle seed has already ripened first, they may be used there temporarily. The shared teaching's factors of the path are called the initial gateway of the Mahāyāna, received in common by all three vehicles; the land of common dwelling should mostly teach these. The distinct teaching's factors of the path are called the dharma unique to bodhisattvas; the lands of common dwelling and expedient means, too, mostly teach these. The perfect teaching's factors of the path are called the unsurpassed Buddha-dharma; one of sharp faculties should be able to hear these across all four kinds of Pure Land. The closing phrase "teachings such as these" refers back to the foundations of mindfulness, right efforts, bases of spiritual power, and the rest, together with every other immeasurable Dharma-gate — the four means of attraction, the six pāramitās, the ten powers, the fearlessnesses, and so on. Although the thirty-seven factors gather in the whole of the Buddha-dharma without remainder, since capacities and conditions differ from one being to the next, various openings and closings, names and meanings, are given accordingly. To state the essential point: whatever a listener wishes to hear, it is expounded without exception, and so is able to cause every listener to give rise to a mind mindful of the Buddha, mindful of the Dharma, mindful of the Saṅgha — though the Three Jewels thus recollected also carry distinctions of specific mark and single essence, and even differences of meaning across the Four Teachings, as is set out at length in other sūtras and treatises.

Translator's note

This full run through the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment — the standard core curriculum shared across every stage of Buddhist practice, from the earliest canonical sources through every later Mahāyāna school — is the single longest doctrinal digression in the whole commentary, and Ouyi treats it as worth the length precisely because the sūtra names only the last two categories (the five faculties, five powers, seven factors of awakening, and eightfold path) while silently assuming the first two (the four foundations of mindfulness, four right efforts, four bases of spiritual power) that a trained reader would supply from memory. The "four means of attraction" (四攝) and "ten powers" (十力) named in the closing lines are standard Mahāyāna bodhisattva-path categories, listed here only as further examples of "teachings such as these" rather than explained in full.

Part 6 Complete

This closes the third adornment-category and the first half of the fourth: heavenly music and the ceaseless rain of flowers, and the birds whose song directly proclaims the thirty-seven factors of the path. Part 7 closes out the description of Sukhāvatī entirely — the Buddha's own clarification that these birds are not born from karmic retribution, the wind moving through the jeweled trees with a sound like a hundred thousand instruments at once (where Ouyi makes his direct argument against a purely inward reading of the Pure Land), and the closing statement of accomplished merit.
