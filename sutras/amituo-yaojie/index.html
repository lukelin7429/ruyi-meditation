Ouyi Zhixu · 1647 · 阿彌陀經要解

The Amituo Yaojie.

Written in nine days in the autumn of 1647, at forty-nine years old, Ouyi Zhixu's Essential Explanation is the most widely read of the Amitābha Sūtra's classical commentaries — shorter and more direct than the two great works it deliberately stands beside, Zhuhong's Shuchao and Chuandeng's Yuanzhong Chao. Its central legacy is the framework of faith, vow, and practice (信願行) that later Pure Land teaching treats as its cardinal structure. This collection translates the whole commentary across ten pages, with the sūtra's own quoted lines set apart in their own boxes so a reader can always tell scripture from exegesis at a glance.

How to read this collection

This is not a sūtra but a commentary on one — Ouyi quotes the Amitābha Sūtra's own text in short bursts, then writes at length on each passage. To keep that distinction visible, every quoted sūtra line appears here in its own jade-bordered box, matched against the wording already established on this site's Amitābha Sūtra page; everything else — the bulk of every page — is Ouyi's own commentary, translated in full for the first time on this site as an independent text rather than paraphrased into reading notes.

The ten pages follow the commentary's own five-part structure: the Five Profound Meanings that open every Tiantai commentary (Parts 1–3), the Introduction (Part 3–4), the Main Teaching (Parts 4–9), and the Transmission with Ouyi's own postscript (Part 10). Reading notes on each page are drawn from Ouyi's own text directly, not paraphrased secondhand — this is, in effect, the primary source the sūtra's own reading guide had previously only summarized.

What each page includes

  • The Chinese source text of Ouyi's commentary, beside a complete English translation.
  • The Amitābha Sūtra's own quoted lines, set apart in distinct boxes, matched to this site's existing sūtra translation.
  • Every embedded question-and-answer dialogue, translated in full — Ouyi's commentary is unusually dialogic, anticipating and answering objections as it goes.
  • Translator's notes cross-referencing this site's other Tiantai and Pure Land translations, and flagging where the sūtra's own reading guide needs correction.

The Commentary's Own Structure

Ouyi follows the standard Tiantai five-part framework for opening a sūtra commentary — name, essence, essential teaching, power and function, doctrinal classification — before turning to the sūtra's own three-part structure of Introduction, Main Teaching, and Transmission, the same division his own commentary insists no reader should skip past.

Parts 1–3 · 五重玄義
The Five Profound Meanings
Ouyi's own preface, the sūtra's name explained syllable by syllable, the true mark of reality as the current single thought of mind, the six kinds of faith, taking and forsaking as the whole Dharma-realm, and the four kinds of non-retrogression.

Parts 3–4 · 序分
The Introduction
The assembly of śrāvakas, bodhisattvas, and gods gathered to hear an unasked teaching, and the specific preface — why the Buddha speaks unprompted, the four kinds of Pure Land, and the three bodies of a buddha.
Parts 3–4 · complete
Parts 4–9 · 正宗分
The Main Teaching
The full description of Sukhāvatī's adornments, correlated across all four Pure Lands; Amitābha's own light and life; the retinue that never falls back; and the sūtra's central passage — holding the name with one mind, undistracted — defended across ten questions and answers.
Parts 4–9 · complete
Part 10 · 流通分
The Transmission, and Ouyi's Own Postscript
Buddhas of six directions bearing witness with their broad, long tongues; the sūtra's two titles; Śākyamuni's own lament that this is the hardest teaching in the world to believe; the assembly's closing joy; and, past the sūtra's own final line, Ouyi's first-hand account of writing this commentary in nine days, together with a disciple's tribute and a donor's dedication of merit preserved from the text's print history.
