Why the Buddha Speaks Unasked

The specific preface is where the sūtra actually begins to speak — Śākyamuni turns to Śāriputra and simply announces a world no one has asked about. Ouyi reads even the bare geography of "west, past a hundred thousand million buddha-lands" as doctrinally loaded, answers the standing objection to why the Pure Land is specifically west, and gives his fullest exposition anywhere in the commentary of the four kinds of Pure Land and the three bodies of a buddha, correlated in careful detail.

The Specific Preface: An Unasked Teaching

序分・別序（發起序）
Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

二、別序，發起序也。淨土妙門甚深不可思議，無人能問，故佛自唱彼土依正名字而為發起。又佛智鑒機無有錯謬，見此大眾應聞淨土妙門而獲四益，故不俟人問便自發起，如《梵網》下卷自唱位號云我今盧舍那等。智者大師亦判作發起序，例可知也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the specific preface — the preface that opens up the occasion for the teaching. The wondrous gate of the Pure Land is so profound and inconceivable that no one was able to ask about it; hence the Buddha himself proclaimed the names of that land's dependent and direct rewards, thereby opening up the occasion himself. Moreover, the Buddha's wisdom, discerning capacity, is never mistaken: seeing that this assembly was fit to hear the wondrous gate of the Pure Land and thereby obtain the four benefits, he did not wait to be asked, but opened up the occasion of his own accord — just as, in the closing fascicle of the Brahmā Net Sūtra, he proclaims his own rank and title of his own accord, saying "I am now Vairocana," and so on. Great Master Zhiyi likewise classifies that passage as a preface opening its own occasion — the parallel may be understood from this example.

West, Past a Hundred Thousand Million Buddha-Lands

序分・別序（發起序）
Sūtra Text Quoted 《阿彌陀經》 — see the full sūtra translation

爾時，佛告長老舍利弗：從是西方過十萬億佛土，有世界名曰極樂，其土有佛，號阿彌陀，今現在說法。

Then the Buddha said to the elder Śāriputra: "West from here, past a hundred thousand million buddha-lands, there is a world named Utmost Bliss. In that land there is a buddha called Amitābha, who is teaching the Dharma there right now."

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

淨土法門三根普攝，絕待圓融不可思議，甚深難信，故特呼大智慧者而與之言，以非第一智慧不能直下無疑故也。從是者，從此娑婆世界也。西方者，橫亙直西，標所示現之處也。過者，超越也。十萬億者，千萬曰億，今又積億至十萬也。佛土者，三千大千世界通為一佛所化，名一佛土。且以此土言之，一須彌山東西南北各一洲，同一日月所照，同一小鐵圍山所遶，名一四天下；千四天下名小千世界；千小千名中千世界，則有百萬須彌四洲日月及小鐵圍；千中千界名為大千世界，則有十萬須彌等，故名三千大千世界。過如此等佛土，共十萬億之西方，是極樂世界也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Pure Land teaching gathers in all three levels of capacity universally, absolute, perfectly fused, and inconceivable — so profound, so difficult to believe, that the Buddha addresses it specially to the one of great wisdom, since only foremost wisdom can, straightaway, accept it without doubt. "From this" means from this very sahā world. "West" means straight and level, due west, marking the direction in which the place he is about to reveal lies. "Past" means going beyond. As for "a hundred thousand million": ten million is called a koṭi, and here kotis are further accumulated up to a hundred thousand of them. As for "buddha-land": a three-thousand great-thousand-fold world-system, taken together as a whole, converted by a single buddha, is called one buddha-land. Speaking of this present world in particular: a single Mount Sumeru, with one continent to its east, west, south, and north, illuminated by one and the same sun and moon, encircled by one and the same ring of lesser iron mountains, is called a single set of four continents; a thousand such sets of four continents is called a small thousand-world; a thousand small-thousands is called a middle thousand-world, containing a million Sumerus, sets of four continents, suns and moons, and rings of lesser iron mountains; a thousand middle-thousand-worlds is called a great thousand-world, containing a hundred thousand Sumerus and so forth — hence it is called a "three-thousand great-thousand-fold world." Passing beyond buddha-lands such as these, together numbering a hundred thousand million, due west, is the World of Utmost Bliss.

Question: "Why must Utmost Bliss lie fixed in the west?"

Answer: "This is not a good question. Suppose Utmost Bliss lay in the east instead — you would then need to ask, 'Why is it in the east?' Is this not idle argument? Moreover, seen from this sahā world, Utmost Bliss lies to the west; but seen from a hundred and ten thousand million buddha-lands further on, Utmost Bliss would then lie to the east. How, then, is there any cause for doubt at all?"

Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary (continued)

有世界名曰極樂者，序依報國土之名也。過去、現在、未來遷流名世，東西、南北、四維、上下方隅為界，既示依報則必竪約三世以辨時劫，橫約十方以定疆隅，故稱世界。言極樂者，梵語須摩提，此翻極樂，亦翻安養亦翻安樂亦翻清泰，乃永離眾苦第一安隱之謂，具如下文廣釋。然佛土有四，各分淨穢，所謂凡聖同居土，五濁重者則穢，五濁輕者則淨。方便有餘土，析空拙度證入者穢，體空巧度證入者淨。實報無障礙土，次第三觀證入者穢，一心三觀證入者淨。常寂光土，分證者穢，究竟滿證者淨。今正指同居淨土，亦即橫具上三淨土也。

"There is a world named Utmost Bliss": this proclaims the name of that dependent-reward land. The flowing succession of past, present, and future is called "world" [世]; the directions of east, west, south, north, the four intermediate points, and above and below are called "boundary" [界]. Having now revealed a dependent reward, one must necessarily reckon it vertically across the three time-periods to distinguish its kalpa, and horizontally across the ten directions to fix its territory and its corners — hence it is called a "world" [世界]. As for "Utmost Bliss": in Sanskrit, Sukhāvatī, translated here as "Utmost Bliss," also translated as "Peaceful Nurturing," "Peace and Bliss," or "Pure Tranquility" — meaning that which is forever free of every suffering, foremost among all places of peace and security, as will be fully explained below. Now, a buddha-land admits of four kinds, each in turn divided into pure and defiled: the land of ordinary beings and sages dwelling together — where the five turbidities weigh heavily, it is defiled; where the five turbidities are light, it is pure. The expedient land with residue remaining — realized by one who enters through the clumsy method of analysis into emptiness, it is defiled; realized by one who enters through the skillful method of embodying emptiness directly, it is pure. The realm of real reward, unobstructed — realized by one who enters through the sequential three contemplations, it is defiled; realized by one who enters through the single mind's three contemplations at once, it is pure. The land of eternal, tranquil light — where realization is only partial, it is defiled; where realization is ultimate and complete, it is pure. What is meant here is precisely the pure land of common dwelling — which, at the same time, already contains the other three pure lands within it, laid out side by side.

Translator's note

Ouyi's argument that Sukhāvatī "already contains the other three pure lands within it, laid out side by side" is the same claim behind the reading note to Section 2 of the sūtra's own page — that Ouyi reads "west, past a hundred thousand million buddha-lands" as a specific, literal claim rather than a metaphor, and argues directly against collapsing it into a purely inward "Pure Land is only the pure mind" reading. That argument itself, however, does not appear in Ouyi's commentary at this point in the text; see the note to the next section below, on the wind-and-trees passage, for where it actually occurs.

Amitābha, and the Three Bodies of a Buddha

序分・別序（發起序）
Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

其土有佛號阿彌陀者，序正報教主之名也。佛是通名，阿彌陀是別名，梵語阿彌陀此云無量壽、無量光等，亦如下廣釋。佛有三身，名論單複：法身單者指所證理性；報身單者指能證功德智慧；化身單者指所現相好色像。法身複者：一、自性清淨法身；二、離垢妙極法身。報身複者：一、自受用報身；二、他受用報身。化身複者：一、示生化身；二、應現化身。又一、佛界化身；二、隨類化身。雖辨單複三身，實則非一非三而三而一，不縱橫不並別，離過絕非不可思議。今云阿彌陀佛，正指同居土中示生化身，仍復即報即法，故令聞名必得不退也。今現在說法者，簡非過去已滅，亦非未來未成，正爾說法度生，故應發願往生，親覲說法速成正覺也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"In that land there is a buddha called Amitābha": this proclaims the name of the direct-reward teaching-lord. "Buddha" is a general title; "Amitābha" is his specific name. In Sanskrit, Amitābha means "Infinite Life," "Infinite Light," and so forth, as will be fully explained below. A buddha possesses three bodies, discussed under both simple and compound names: the Dharma-body, taken simply, points to the principle-nature that is realized; the reward-body, taken simply, points to the merit and wisdom that does the realizing; the transformation-body, taken simply, points to the manifested marks and physical form. The Dharma-body, taken as compound, has two: first, the Dharma-body intrinsically pure by its own nature; second, the wondrous, utterly perfect Dharma-body free of every defilement. The reward-body, taken as compound, has two: first, the reward-body enjoyed by oneself; second, the reward-body enjoyed for the sake of others. The transformation-body, taken as compound, has two: first, the transformation-body that manifests a birth; second, the transformation-body that responsively appears. And again: first, the transformation-body within the buddha-realm; second, the transformation-body that accords with each kind of being. Although the three bodies are thus distinguished as simple and compound, in truth they are neither one nor three, and yet three and yet one — neither stacked vertically nor set side by side, apart from every excess and every negation, inconceivable. Now, when it says "Amitābha Buddha," this refers precisely to the transformation-body manifesting a birth within the land of common dwelling — and yet this is, at the very same time, identical with the reward-body, identical with the Dharma-body. This is why hearing the name is certain to bring about non-retrogression. "Who is teaching the Dharma there right now" distinguishes him from a buddha of the past who has already passed into extinction, and likewise from a buddha of the future not yet come to be — he is, at this very moment, teaching the Dharma and liberating beings. This is why one should make the vow to be born there, to behold him in person, hear him teach, and swiftly perfect true and complete awakening.

Translator's note

This is the fullest working-out anywhere in the commentary of the four-body scheme first sketched on Part 1. Ouyi's closing point is the sharpest: Amitābha as presently encountered is specifically a transformation-body — the very kind of manifestation the Dharma-body/reward-body/response-body scheme treats as "turned toward others" — and yet, because the three bodies are ultimately not separate substances, this transformation-body is fully, not partially, identical with the reward-body and Dharma-body behind it. This is the doctrinal basis for his repeated claim across this commentary that hearing or holding Amitābha's name reaches all the way to non-retrogression: the name does not merely point at a lesser, derivative body, but at the whole of what a buddha is.

Seven Ways One Passage Prefaces the Whole Sūtra

序分・別序（發起序）
Chinese Source — Ouyi's Commentary

復次，有世界有佛現在，即勸信序也；世界名極樂，即勸願序也；佛號阿彌陀，即勸持名妙行序也。復次，阿彌陀，序佛也；說法，序法也；說法必有聽眾，序僧也。佛法僧同一實相，序體也；從此起信願行，序宗也；信願行成必得往生見佛聞法，序用也；唯一佛界為所緣境不雜餘事，序教相也。文略意周矣。初序分竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore: that there is a world, and that a buddha is presently there, is precisely what prefaces the exhortation to faith; that the world is named Utmost Bliss is precisely what prefaces the exhortation to vow; that the buddha is named Amitābha is precisely what prefaces the exhortation to the wondrous practice of holding the name. Furthermore: "Amitābha" prefaces the Buddha [as one of the Three Jewels]; "teaching the Dharma" prefaces the Dharma; and teaching the Dharma necessarily implies an audience listening, which prefaces the Saṅgha. The Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha share one and the same true mark, which prefaces the essence; from this, faith, vow, and practice arise, which prefaces the essential teaching; faith, vow, and practice, once accomplished, must lead to rebirth, beholding the Buddha, and hearing the Dharma, which prefaces the power and function; and the single buddha-realm alone, taken as the object of one's mind, without any admixture of other concerns, prefaces the doctrinal classification. The text is brief, yet its meaning is complete. This closes the Introduction.

Translator's note

Ouyi closes the entire Introduction by folding this one short passage back through all Five Profound Meanings from Parts 1 through 3 — a favorite Tiantai exegetical move, showing that a sūtra's opening lines already contain, in miniature, everything its commentary will later unpack at length. With this, the Introduction (序分) is complete; Part 5 opens the Main Teaching itself (正宗分), where the sūtra's own extended description of Sukhāvatī's adornments begins.
