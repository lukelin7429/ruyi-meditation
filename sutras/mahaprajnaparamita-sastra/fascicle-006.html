The Ten Similes

Fascicle 6 begins the explanation of ten similes for emptiness: illusion, mirage, moon in water, empty space, echo, gandharva city, dream, shadow, mirror image, and magical transformation.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: translation in progress Source base: Taishō T25, no. 1509 Ten similes for emptiness

Opening the Ten Similes

Chinese Source

大智度初品中十喻釋論第十一（第六卷）

Ru-Yi Translation

Explanation of the Ten Similes in the First Chapter of the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra, Part Eleven, Fascicle Six

﹝經﹞

解了諸法如幻、如焰、如水中月、如虛空、如響、如犍闥婆城、如夢、如影、如鏡中像、如化。

Sutra:

They understood all dharmas as like an illusion, like a mirage, like the moon in water, like empty space, like an echo, like a gandharva city, like a dream, like a shadow, like an image in a mirror, and like a magical transformation.

﹝論﹞

是十喻，為解空法故。

Treatise:

These ten similes are given in order to explain the Dharma of emptiness.

Like an Illusion

問曰：若一切諸法空如幻，何以故諸法有可見、可聞、可嗅、可甞、可觸、可識者？

若實無所有，不應有可見乃至可識。復次，若無而妄見者，何以不見聲、聞色？若皆一等空無所有，何以有可見、不可見者？以諸法空故，如一指第一甲無，第二甲亦無，何以不見第二甲，獨見第一甲？以是故知：第一甲實有故可見，第二甲實無故不可見。

Question: If all dharmas are empty like an illusion, why are there dharmas that can be seen, heard, smelled, tasted, touched, and known?

If they truly do not exist at all, there should be nothing that can be seen, and so on, up to nothing that can be known. Furthermore, if what does not exist is falsely seen, why does one not see sounds and hear forms? If all are equally empty and without anything existent, why are there things that can be seen and things that cannot be seen? Since all dharmas are empty, then just as the first fingernail of one finger does not exist, the second fingernail also does not exist. Why, then, is the second fingernail not seen, while only the first fingernail is seen? For this reason one knows: because the first fingernail truly exists, it can be seen; because the second fingernail truly does not exist, it cannot be seen.

答曰：諸法相雖空，亦有分別可見、不可見。

譬如幻化象、馬及種種諸物，雖知無實，然色可見，聲可聞，與六情相對，不相錯亂。

諸法亦如是，雖空而可見、可聞，不相錯亂。

Answer: Although the marks of all dharmas are empty, there are still distinctions between what can be seen and what cannot be seen.

It is like an illusionist conjuring elephants, horses, and many other things. Although one knows they are not real, their forms can be seen and their sounds can be heard. They stand in relation to the six faculties without being confused with one another.

All dharmas are also like this. Although they are empty, they can still be seen and heard, without confusion.

如《德女經》說：「德女白佛言：『世尊！如無明內有不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『外有不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『內外有不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『世尊！是無明從先世來不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『從此世至後世不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『是無明有生者、滅者不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『有一法定實性，是名無明不？』

「佛言：『不！』

As the Sutra of the Virtuous Woman says, "The Virtuous Woman said to the Buddha, 'Blessed One, does ignorance exist internally?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Does it exist externally?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Does it exist both internally and externally?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Blessed One, does this ignorance come from a former life?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Does it go from this life to a later life?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Does this ignorance have one who produces it or one who destroys it?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Is there one dharma with a fixed real nature that is called ignorance?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

「爾時德女復白佛言：『若無明無內，無外，亦無內外，不從先世至今世、今世至後世，亦無真實性者，云何從無明緣行乃至眾苦集？世尊！譬如有樹，若無根者，云何得生莖、節、枝、葉、華、果？』

「佛言：『諸法相雖空，凡夫無聞無智故，而於中生種種煩惱，煩惱因緣作身、口、意業，業因緣作後身，身因緣受苦受樂。是中無有實作煩惱，亦無身、口、意業，亦無有受苦樂者。譬如幻師，幻作種種事。於汝意云何？是幻所作內有不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『外有不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『內外有不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『從先世至今世，今世至後世不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『幻所作有生者滅者不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『實有一法是幻所作不？』

「答言：『不！』

"At that time the Virtuous Woman again said to the Buddha, 'If ignorance is not internal, not external, and not both internal and external; if it does not come from a former life to this life or go from this life to a later life; and if it has no real nature, then how can formations arise conditioned by ignorance, and so on up to the gathering of all suffering? Blessed One, suppose there is a tree. If it has no roots, how can it produce trunk, joints, branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit?'

"The Buddha said, 'Although the marks of all dharmas are empty, ordinary beings are without learning and without wisdom. Therefore, within them they produce many kinds of afflictions. Conditioned by afflictions, they create bodily, verbal, and mental karma. Conditioned by karma, they create a later body. Conditioned by the body, they experience suffering and pleasure. Within this, there is no real one who creates afflictions, no bodily, verbal, or mental karma, and no one who experiences suffering and pleasure. It is like an illusionist creating many illusory things. What do you think? Do the things made by illusion exist internally?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Do they exist externally?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Do they exist both internally and externally?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Do they come from a former life to this life, or go from this life to a later life?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Do the things made by illusion have one who produces them or one who destroys them?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Is there truly one dharma that is made by illusion?'

"She answered, 'No.'

「佛言：『汝頗見頗聞幻所作妓樂不？』

「答言：『我亦聞亦見。』

「佛問德女：『若幻空，欺誑無實，云何從幻能作伎樂？』

「德女白佛：『世尊！是幻相爾，雖無根本而可聞見。』

「佛言：『無明亦如是，雖不內有，不外有，不內外有；不先世至今世，今世至後世，亦無實性，無有生者、滅者，而無明因緣諸行生，乃至眾苦陰集。如幻息，幻所作亦息；無明亦爾，無明盡，行亦盡，乃至眾苦集皆盡。』」

復次，是幻譬喻，示眾生一切有為法空不堅固；如說：「一切諸行如幻，欺誑小兒，屬因緣，不自在，不久住。」

是故說「諸菩薩知諸法如幻」。

"The Buddha said, 'Have you ever seen or heard music and performances made by illusion?'

"She answered, 'I have both heard and seen them.'

"The Buddha asked the Virtuous Woman, 'If illusion is empty, deceptive, and unreal, how can music and performances be made from illusion?'

"The Virtuous Woman said to the Buddha, 'Blessed One, such is the mark of illusion. Although it has no root, it can still be heard and seen.'

"The Buddha said, 'Ignorance is also like this. Although it does not exist internally, does not exist externally, and does not exist both internally and externally; although it does not come from a former life to this life or go from this life to a later life; although it has no real nature and has no one who produces it or destroys it, still, conditioned by ignorance, formations arise, and so on up to the gathering of all aggregates of suffering. When illusion ceases, what was made by illusion also ceases. Ignorance is the same. When ignorance is exhausted, formations are also exhausted, and so on until the whole mass of suffering is also exhausted.'"

Furthermore, the simile of illusion shows beings that all conditioned dharmas are empty and not solid. As it is said, "All formations are like illusions. They deceive children. They depend on causes and conditions, are not autonomous, and do not abide for long."

For this reason, it says, "Bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like illusions."

Like a Mirage

「如炎」者，炎以日光風動塵故，曠野中見如野馬，無智人初見謂之為水。

男相、女相亦如是，結使煩惱日光熱諸行塵，邪憶念風，生死曠野中轉，無智慧者謂為一相，為男、為女，是名如炎。

"Like a mirage" means this: because sunlight, wind, and moving dust come together, in a wilderness one sees something like a shimmering haze. When people without wisdom first see it, they think it is water.

The marks of male and female are also like this. The sunlight of fetters and afflictions heats the dust of formations; the wind of wrong recollection moves through the wilderness of birth and death. Those without wisdom take it to be one fixed mark, calling it male or female. This is called being like a mirage.

復次，若遠見炎，想為水，近則無水想。無智人亦如是，若遠聖法，不知無我，不知諸法空，於陰、界、入性空法中，生人相、男相、女相；近聖法，則知諸法實相，是時虛誑種種妄想盡除。

以是故說：「諸菩薩知諸法如炎。」

Furthermore, when one sees a mirage from far away, one imagines it to be water. When one comes near, there is no perception of water. People without wisdom are also like this. When they are far from the noble Dharma, they do not know non-self and do not know that all dharmas are empty. Within the empty nature of the aggregates, elements, and sense fields, they produce the mark of a person, the mark of male, and the mark of female. When they draw near to the noble Dharma, they know the true mark of all dharmas, and at that time all kinds of false and deceptive imaginings are removed.

For this reason, it says, "Bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like a mirage."

Like the Moon in Water

「如水中月」者，月實在虛空中，影現於水；實法相月在如、法性、實際虛空中，而凡天人心水中有我、我所相現。以是故名「如水中月」。

"Like the moon in water" means this: the moon truly exists in empty space, while its reflection appears in water. The moon of the true mark of dharmas abides in the empty space of suchness, Dharma-nature, and the true limit, while in the mind-water of ordinary devas and humans the marks of self and what belongs to self appear. For this reason it is called "like the moon in water."

復次，如小兒見水中月，歡喜欲取，大人見之則笑。無智人亦如是，身見故見有吾我；無實智故，見種種法；見已歡喜，欲取諸相：男相、女相等；諸得道聖人笑之。如偈說：

如水中月炎中水，夢中得財死求生；有人於此實欲得，是人癡惑聖所笑！

Furthermore, it is like a child who sees the moon in water, becomes delighted, and wants to grasp it. An adult who sees this laughs. People without wisdom are also like this. Because of the view of the body, they see a self. Because they lack true wisdom, they see many kinds of dharmas. Having seen them, they become delighted and want to grasp all their marks: the mark of male, the mark of female, and so on. Noble ones who have attained the path laugh at them. As a verse says:

The moon in water, water in a mirage,
wealth gained in a dream, life sought in death:
if someone truly wants to obtain these,
that person's delusion is laughed at by the noble ones.

復次，譬如靜水中見月影，攪水則不見。無明心靜水中，見吾我、憍慢諸結使影；實智慧杖攪心水，則不見吾我等諸結使影。

以是故說：「諸菩薩知諸法如水中月。」

Furthermore, it is like seeing the moon's reflection in still water: when the water is stirred, the reflection is no longer seen. In the still water of the mind of ignorance, one sees the shadows of self, pride, and the fetters. When the staff of true wisdom stirs the water of the mind, the shadows of self and the other fetters are no longer seen.

For this reason, it says, "Bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like the moon in water."

Like Empty Space

「如虛空」者，但有名而無實法；虛空非可見法，遠視故，眼光轉見縹色。

諸法亦如是，空無所有，人遠無漏實智慧故，棄實相，見彼我、男女、屋舍、城郭等種種雜物心著。

如小兒仰視青天，謂有實色；有人飛上極遠而無所見，以遠視故，謂為青色。諸法亦如是，以是故，說如虛空。

"Like empty space" means this: it has only a name and no real dharma. Empty space is not a visible dharma, but because one looks from far away, the eye's light turns and sees a pale blue color.

All dharmas are also like this. They are empty and have nothing at all, but because people are far from undefiled true wisdom, they abandon the true mark and see self and other, male and female, houses, cities, and many other mixed things, and their minds cling to them.

It is like a child looking up at the blue sky and thinking there is a real color there. If someone flies up extremely far, there is nothing to be seen. Because it is viewed from far away, it is taken to be blue. All dharmas are also like this. For this reason, they are said to be like empty space.

復次，如虛空性常清淨，人謂陰曀為不淨。諸法亦如是，性常清淨，婬欲、瞋恚等曀故，人謂為不淨。如偈說：

如夏月天雷電雨，陰雲覆曀不清淨；凡夫無智亦如是，種種煩惱常覆心。

如冬天日時一出，常為昏氣雲蔭曀；雖得初果第二道，猶為欲染之所蔽。

若如春天日欲出，時為陰雲所覆曀；雖離欲染第三果，餘殘癡慢猶覆心。

若如秋日無雲曀，亦如大海水清淨；所作已辦無漏心，羅漢如是得清淨。

Furthermore, the nature of empty space is always pure, yet people say it is impure when it is covered by clouds and haze. All dharmas are also like this: their nature is always pure, but because they are obscured by lust, anger, and the like, people say they are impure. As a verse says:

In the summer sky, thunder, lightning, and rain:
clouds cover it, so it seems impure.
Ordinary beings without wisdom are also like this:
many afflictions always cover the mind.

When the winter sun first appears,
it is still shaded by dim vapors and clouds.
Even one who has attained the first fruit or the second path
is still covered by the stain of desire.

When the spring sun is about to rise,
it may still be covered by clouds.
Even one who has left the stain of desire and reached the third fruit
still has remaining delusion and conceit covering the mind.

When the autumn sun has no clouds or haze,
it is also like the great ocean with water clear and pure.
With what had to be done already done, the undefiled mind
of an arhat becomes pure in this way.

復次，虛空無初、無中、無後，諸法亦如是。

復次，如摩訶衍中，佛語須菩提：「虛空無前世，亦無中世，亦無後世，諸法亦如是。」彼經，此中應廣說。

是故說諸法如虛空。

Furthermore, empty space has no beginning, no middle, and no end. All dharmas are also like this.

Furthermore, as in the Mahayana, the Buddha said to Subhuti, "Empty space has no past life, no middle life, and no future life. All dharmas are also like this." That sutra should be explained extensively here.

For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like empty space.

問曰：虛空實有法。何以故？若虛空無實法者，若舉若下，若來若往，若屈若申，若出若入等，有所作應無有，以無動處故。

答曰：若虛空法實有，虛空應有住處。何以故？無住處則無法。若虛空在孔穴中住，是為虛空在虛空中住，以是故不應孔中住；若在實中住，是實非空，則不得住，無所受故。

復次，汝言：「住處是虛空。」如石壁實中，無有住處；若無住處則無虛空。以虛空無住處，故無虛空。

Question: Empty space is a real dharma. Why? If empty space were not a real dharma, then actions such as lifting and lowering, coming and going, bending and stretching, entering and exiting should not exist, because there would be no place for movement.

Answer: If the dharma of empty space truly existed, empty space should have an abiding place. Why? Without an abiding place, there is no dharma. If empty space abides in holes and openings, then empty space would be abiding in empty space; for this reason, it should not abide in holes. If it abides in something solid, that solid thing is not empty, and so it cannot abide there, because there is no room to receive it.

Furthermore, you say, "The abiding place is empty space." But in a solid stone wall there is no abiding place. If there is no abiding place, then there is no empty space. Because empty space has no abiding place, there is no empty space.

復次，無相故無虛空；諸法各各有相，相有故知有法。如地堅相，水濕相，火熱相，風動相，識識相，慧解相，世間生滅相，涅槃永滅相；是虛空無相故無。

問曰：虛空有相，汝不知故言無；無色處是虛空相。

答曰：不爾！無色是名破色，更無異法，如燈滅，更無法。以是故，無有虛空相。

復次，是虛空法無。何以故？汝因色故，以無色處是虛空相。若爾者，色未生時，則無虛空相。

復次，汝謂：「色是無常法，虛空是有常法。」色未有時，應先有虛空法，以有常故。若色未有，則無無色處；若無無色處，則無虛空相；若無相則無法，以是故虛空但有名而無實。

如虛空，諸法亦如是，但有假名而無實。

以是故，諸菩薩知諸法如虛空。

Furthermore, because empty space has no mark, there is no empty space. Each dharma has its own mark, and because there is a mark, one knows that there is a dharma. For example, earth has the mark of hardness, water has the mark of wetness, fire has the mark of heat, wind has the mark of movement, consciousness has the mark of knowing, wisdom has the mark of understanding, the world has the mark of arising and ceasing, and nirvana has the mark of permanent cessation. Because empty space has no mark, it does not exist.

Question: Empty space has a mark, but because you do not know it, you say it has none. The place without form is the mark of empty space.

Answer: That is not so. "Without form" is only the breaking apart of form; there is no separate dharma. It is like a lamp going out: there is no further dharma. For this reason, there is no mark of empty space.

Furthermore, this dharma of empty space does not exist. Why? You depend on form and take the place without form to be the mark of empty space. If that is so, then before form has arisen, there would be no mark of empty space.

Furthermore, you say, "Form is an impermanent dharma, while empty space is an existent permanent dharma." Before form existed, the dharma of empty space should already have existed, because it is permanent. But if form has not yet existed, then there is no place without form. If there is no place without form, then there is no mark of empty space. If there is no mark, then there is no dharma. For this reason, empty space has only a name and no reality.

Just as with empty space, all dharmas are also like this: they have only provisional names and no reality.

For this reason, bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like empty space.

Like an Echo

「如響」者，若深山狹谷中，若深絕澗中，若空大舍中；若語聲，若打聲，從聲有聲，名為響。無智人謂為有人語聲，智者心念是聲無人作，但以聲觸，故更有聲名為響；響事空，能誑耳根。

"Like an echo" means this: in deep mountains and narrow valleys, in deep and sheer ravines, or in a large empty hall, whether there is the sound of speech or the sound of striking, from one sound another sound arises. This is called an echo. People without wisdom think it is the voice of someone speaking. Wise people think, "This sound is not made by a person. It is only because sound has struck something that another sound arises and is called an echo." The event of an echo is empty, yet it can deceive the ear faculty.

如人欲語時，口中風名憂陀那，還入至臍，觸臍響出，響出時觸七處退，是名語言。如偈說：

風名憂檀那，觸臍而上去；是風七處觸，項及齗齒脣，舌咽及以胸，是中語言生。

愚人不解此，惑著起瞋癡；中人有智慧，不瞋亦不著，亦復不愚癡，但隨諸法相。

曲直及屈申，去來現語言，都無有作者。是事是幻耶？為機關木人？為是夢中事？我為熱氣悶，有是為無是？是事誰能知？是骨人筋纏，能作是語聲，如融金投水。

以是故，言諸菩薩知諸法如響。

When a person is about to speak, the wind in the mouth is called udāna. It returns inward to the navel, touches the navel, and sound comes out. When the sound comes out, it touches seven places and withdraws. This is called speech. As a verse says:

The wind is called udāna;
it touches the navel and rises upward.
This wind touches seven places:
the neck, gums, teeth, lips,
the tongue, throat, and chest.
From these, speech is born.

Foolish people do not understand this.
Confused and attached, they give rise to anger and delusion.
A person of moderate wisdom
does not become angry and does not cling,
and also does not become foolish,
but simply follows the marks of dharmas.

Bent or straight, contracted or extended,
coming and going, speech appears;
yet there is no maker at all.
Is this an illusion?
Is it a wooden puppet mechanism?
Is it something in a dream?
Am I oppressed by heat,
so that what exists seems not to exist?
Who can know this matter?
This person of bones wrapped in sinews
can make these sounds of speech,
like molten gold poured into water.

For this reason, it says that bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like an echo.

Like a Gandharva City

「如犍闥婆城」者，日初出時，見城門、樓櫓、宮殿、行人出入，日轉高轉滅；此城但可眼見而無有實，是名犍闥婆城。

有人初不見犍闥婆城，晨朝東向見之，意謂實樂；疾行趣之，轉近轉失，日高轉滅；飢渴悶極，見熱氣如野馬，謂之為水，疾走趣之，轉近轉滅；疲極困厄，至窮山狹谷中，大喚啼哭，聞有響應，謂有居民，求之疲極而無所見；思惟自悟，渴願心息。

"Like a gandharva city" means this: when the sun first rises, one sees city gates, watchtowers, palaces, and people coming and going. As the sun rises higher, it disappears. This city can only be seen by the eye, but it has no reality. This is called a gandharva city.

There may be someone who has never before seen a gandharva city. In the early morning, facing east, he sees it and thinks it is real and delightful. He quickly walks toward it, but the closer he gets, the more it disappears; as the sun rises higher, it vanishes. Extremely hungry, thirsty, and distressed, he sees hot vapors like a mirage and thinks they are water. He runs quickly toward them, but the closer he gets, the more they vanish. Exhausted and in distress, he reaches a poor mountain and narrow valley. He cries out loudly and weeps; hearing an echo reply, he thinks there are residents there. He searches until he is exhausted, but sees nothing. Reflecting on this, he awakens for himself, and his thirsty wish comes to rest.

無智人亦如是，空陰、界、入中見吾我及諸法，婬瞋心著，四方狂走，求樂自滿，顛倒欺誑，窮極懊惱；若以智慧知無我、無實法者，是時顛倒願息。

復次，犍闥婆城非城，人心想為城；凡夫亦如是，非身想為身，非心想為心。

People without wisdom are also like this. Within the empty aggregates, elements, and sense fields, they see self and dharmas. Their minds cling through lust and anger, and they run wildly in the four directions, seeking pleasure to satisfy themselves. Deceived by inverted views, they become utterly distressed. If, through wisdom, they know that there is no self and no real dharma, then at that time their inverted wishes come to rest.

Furthermore, a gandharva city is not a city, but the mind imagines it to be a city. Ordinary beings are also like this: what is not a body, they imagine to be a body; what is not mind, they imagine to be mind.

問曰：一事可知，何以多喻？

答曰：我先已答，是摩訶衍如大海水，一切法盡攝；摩訶衍多因緣故，多譬喻無咎。

復次，是菩薩甚深利智故，種種法門，種種因緣，種種喻，壞諸法；為人解故，應多引喻。

復次，一切聲聞法中，無犍闥婆城喻，有種種餘無常喻：「色如聚沫，受如泡，想如野馬，行如芭蕉，識如幻」；及《幻網經》中空譬喻。以是犍闥婆城喻異故，此中說。

Question: If one matter can be understood, why give many similes?

Answer: I have already answered this earlier. The Mahayana is like the water of the great ocean: it includes all dharmas completely. Because the Mahayana has many causes and conditions, there is no fault in giving many similes.

Furthermore, because these bodhisattvas have profound and sharp wisdom, they use many Dharma gateways, many causes and conditions, and many similes to break down dharmas. In order to help people understand, they should draw on many similes.

Furthermore, in all the Dharma of the śrāvakas, there is no simile of a gandharva city. There are many other similes for impermanence: "Form is like a mass of foam, feeling is like a bubble, perception is like a mirage, formations are like a plantain tree, and consciousness is like an illusion," as well as the emptiness similes in the Sutra of the Net of Illusions. Because the simile of a gandharva city is different, it is explained here.

問曰：聲聞法中以城喻身，此中何以說犍闥婆城喻？

答曰：聲聞法中城喻眾緣實有，但城是假名；犍闥婆城眾緣亦無，如旋火輪，但惑人目。聲聞法中為破吾我故，以城為喻；此中菩薩利根深入諸法空中故，以犍闥婆城為喻。

以是故，說如犍闥婆城。

Question: In the Dharma of the śrāvakas, a city is used as a simile for the body. Why does this text use the simile of a gandharva city?

Answer: In the Dharma of the śrāvakas, the city simile means that the many conditions truly exist, while the city is only a provisional name. In a gandharva city, even the many conditions do not exist. It is like a spinning wheel of fire: it only deceives the eye. In the Dharma of the śrāvakas, the city is used as a simile in order to break the notion of self. Here, because bodhisattvas have sharp faculties and enter deeply into the emptiness of all dharmas, a gandharva city is used as the simile.

For this reason, it is said to be like a gandharva city.

Like a Dream

「如夢」者，如夢中無實事，謂之有實，覺已知無，而還自笑。

人亦如是，諸結使眠中，實無而著，得道覺時，乃知無實，亦復自笑；以是故言，如夢。

"Like a dream" means this: in a dream there is no real event, yet one takes it to be real. After waking, one knows it was not real and laughs at oneself.

People are also like this. While asleep in the fetters and latent tendencies, they cling to what is not real. When they awaken by attaining the path, they then know it is not real and also laugh at themselves. For this reason, it is called like a dream.

復次，夢者眠力故，無法而見；人亦如是，無明眠力故，種種無而見有，所謂我、我所，男女等。

復次，如夢中無喜事而喜，無瞋事而瞋，無怖事而怖；三界眾生亦如是，無明眠故不應瞋而瞋，不應喜而喜，不應怖而怖。

Furthermore, in a dream, because of the power of sleep, one sees what has no dharma. People are also like this: because of the power of the sleep of ignorance, they see many things as existent although they do not exist, such as self, what belongs to self, male and female, and so on.

Furthermore, in a dream, though there is nothing to be joyful about, one is joyful; though there is nothing to be angry about, one is angry; though there is nothing to fear, one is afraid. Beings in the three realms are also like this. Because they sleep in ignorance, they are angry when they should not be angry, joyful when they should not be joyful, and afraid when they should not be afraid.

復次，夢有五種：若身中不調，若熱氣多，則多夢見火，見黃、見赤；若冷氣多，則多見水、見白；若風氣多，則多見飛、見黑；又復所聞見事多思惟念故，則夢見；或天與夢，欲令知未來事故。

是五種夢皆無實事而妄見。人亦如是，五道中眾生，身見力因緣故，見四種我：色陰是我，色是我所，我中色，色中我。如色，受、想、行、識亦如是；四五二十。得道實智慧覺已，知無實。

Furthermore, there are five kinds of dreams. If the body is out of balance and there is much heat, one often dreams of fire, yellow, or red. If there is much cold, one often sees water or white. If there is much wind, one often sees flying or black. Again, because one has heard and seen certain things and thought about them often, one sees them in dreams. Or devas may give a dream because they want one to know future events.

These five kinds of dreams all have no real events, yet one falsely sees them. People are also like this. Beings in the five destinies, because of the power and conditions of the view of the body, see four kinds of self: the form aggregate is self; form belongs to self; form is within self; and self is within form. As with form, so too with feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness. Four times five makes twenty. After awakening by attaining the true wisdom of the path, one knows that these are not real.

問曰：不應言夢無實，何以故？識心得因緣便生；夢中識有種種緣，若無此緣，云何生識？

答曰：無也！不應見而見，夢中見人頭有角，或夢見身飛虛空，人實無角，身亦不飛，是故無實。

問曰：實有人頭，餘處亦實有角，以心惑故，見人頭有角。實有虛空，亦實有飛者，以心惑故，自見身飛，非無實也。

答曰：雖實有人頭，雖實有角，但人頭生角者，是妄見。

Question: One should not say that dreams are unreal. Why? When the mind-consciousness obtains causes and conditions, it arises. In a dream, consciousness has many kinds of conditions. If there were no such conditions, how could consciousness arise?

Answer: There are none. One sees what should not be seen: in a dream one sees horns on a person's head, or dreams that one's body flies through empty space. In reality, people do not have horns, and the body does not fly. For this reason, dreams are unreal.

Question: There really are human heads, and elsewhere there really are horns. Because the mind is confused, one sees horns on a human head. Empty space really exists, and there are also really beings who fly. Because the mind is confused, one sees one's own body flying. This is not unreal.

Answer: Although there really are human heads and although there really are horns, a human head growing horns is a false seeing.

問曰：世界廣大，先世因緣種種不同；或有餘國，人頭生角，或一手一足，有一尺人，有九頭人，人有角何所怪？

答曰：若餘國人有角可爾，但夢見此國所識人有角，則不可得。

復次，若人夢見虛空邊、方邊、時邊，是事云何有實？何處無虛空、無方、無時？以是故夢中無而見有。汝先言：「無緣云何生識？」雖無五塵緣，自思惟念力轉故，法緣生。若人言有二頭，因語生想，夢中無而見有，亦復如是。

諸法亦爾，諸法雖無而可見、可聞、可知。如偈說：「如夢、如幻、如犍闥婆；一切諸法，亦復如是。」

以是故，說諸菩薩知諸法如夢。

Question: The world is vast, and the causes and conditions from former lives differ in many ways. In other lands, there may be people with horns growing from their heads, or people with one hand and one foot, or people one foot tall, or people with nine heads. What is strange about a person having horns?

Answer: If people in another land have horns, that may be so. But in a dream, to see someone one knows in this land having horns is impossible.

Furthermore, if someone dreams of the boundary of empty space, the boundary of direction, or the boundary of time, how could such a thing be real? Where is there no empty space, no direction, or no time? For this reason, in a dream one sees as existent what does not exist. You said earlier, "Without conditions, how can consciousness arise?" Although there are no conditions from the five sense objects, because the power of one's own thought and recollection turns, consciousness arises with dharmas as its condition. If someone says there is a person with two heads, then because of those words an image arises in the mind. In a dream, seeing as existent what does not exist is also like this.

All dharmas are also like this. Although dharmas do not exist, they can still be seen, heard, and known. As a verse says, "Like a dream, like an illusion, like a gandharva city: all dharmas are also like this."

For this reason, it says that bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like a dream.

Like a Shadow

「如影」者，影但可見而不可捉；諸法亦如是，眼情等見聞覺知，實不可得。如偈說：

是實知慧，四邊叵捉；如大火聚，亦不可觸；法不可受，亦不應受。

"Like a shadow" means this: a shadow can only be seen, but cannot be grasped. All dharmas are also like this. Through the eye faculty and the others they may be seen, heard, felt, and known, but in reality they cannot be obtained. As a verse says:

This true knowing wisdom
cannot be grasped from any of the four sides.
Like a great mass of fire,
it also cannot be touched.
Dharmas cannot be received,
and should not be received.

復次，如影映光則現，不映則無；諸結煩惱遮正見光，則有我相、法相影。

復次，如影，人去則去，人動則動，人住則住。善惡業影亦如是，後世去時亦去，今世住時亦住，報不斷故，罪福熟時則出。如偈說：

空中亦逐去，山石中亦逐，

地底亦隨去，海水中亦入！

處處常隨逐，業影不相離。

以是故，說諸法如影。

Furthermore, a shadow appears when it reflects light, and when there is no reflection, it does not exist. When the fetters and afflictions block the light of right view, then there are the shadows of the mark of self and the mark of dharmas.

Furthermore, a shadow goes when the person goes, moves when the person moves, and stays when the person stays. The shadow of good and bad karma is also like this. When one goes to a later life, it also goes; when one stays in this life, it also stays. Because retribution does not cease, when offenses and merits mature, they appear. As a verse says:

It follows even through the air;
it follows even into mountains and rocks.

It follows even beneath the earth;
it enters even into the waters of the sea.

Everywhere it constantly follows;
the shadow of karma never separates.

For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like a shadow.

復次，如影空無，求實不可得；一切法亦如是，空無有實。

問曰：影空無有實，是事不然！何以故？《阿毘曇》說：「云何名色入？青、黃、赤、白、黑、縹、紫、光、明、影等，及身業三種作色，是名可見色入」；汝云何言無？

復次，實有影有因緣故；因為樹，緣為明，是二事合有影生，云何言無？若無影，餘法因緣有者，亦皆應無！

復次，是影色，可見長短、大小、麁細、曲直、形動影亦動，是事皆可見，以是故應有。

Furthermore, just as a shadow is empty and nonexistent, and when one seeks its reality it cannot be obtained, all dharmas are also like this: empty and without reality.

Question: It is not right to say that a shadow is empty, nonexistent, and unreal. Why? The Abhidharma says, "What is called the sense field of form? Blue, yellow, red, white, black, pale blue, purple, light, brightness, shadow, and so on, as well as the three kinds of constructed form belonging to bodily karma: these are called the visible sense field of form." How can you say there is no shadow?

Furthermore, a shadow really exists because it has causes and conditions. The cause is a tree, and the condition is light. When these two things come together, a shadow is produced. How can you say it does not exist? If there were no shadow, then other dharmas that exist through causes and conditions should also all not exist.

Furthermore, this shadow-form can be seen as long or short, large or small, coarse or fine, curved or straight. When the form moves, the shadow also moves. All these things can be seen. For this reason, it should exist.

答曰：影實空無，汝言阿毘曇中說者，是釋阿毘曇義人所作；說一種法門，人不體其意，執以為實。如《鞞婆沙》中說：微塵至細，不可破，不可燒，是則常有！復有三世中法，未來中出至現在，從現在入過去，無所失，是則為常！又言：諸有為法，新新生滅不住。若爾者，是則為斷滅相！何以故？先有今無故。

如是等種種異說，違背佛語，不可以此為證。

影今異於色法，色法生必有香、味、觸等，影則不爾，是為非有。

如瓶二根知：眼根、身根；影若有，亦應二根知，而無是事。以是故，影非有實物，但是誑眼法，如捉火㷮，疾轉成輪，非實。

影非有物，若影是有物，應可破可滅；若形不滅，影終不壞，以是故空。

復次，影屬形，不自在故空，雖空而心生眼見。

以是故說，諸法如影。

Answer: A shadow is truly empty and nonexistent. What you say is found in the Abhidharma was made by people who explained the meaning of the Abhidharma. They taught one kind of Dharma gateway, but people did not grasp its meaning and clung to it as real. As the Vibhāṣā says, "Atoms are extremely fine, cannot be broken, and cannot be burned." This would mean they are permanent. It also says, "Dharmas in the three times emerge from the future into the present, and from the present enter the past, without being lost." This would mean they are permanent. Again, it says, "All conditioned dharmas arise and cease newly, newly, and do not abide." If so, this would be a mark of annihilation. Why? Because what existed before does not exist now.

Many different statements such as these contradict the Buddha's words and cannot be used as proof.

A shadow is different from form dharmas. When a form dharma arises, it necessarily has smell, taste, touch, and so on. A shadow is not like this. Therefore it does not exist.

For example, a pot is known by two faculties: the eye faculty and the body faculty. If a shadow existed, it should also be known by two faculties, but this is not the case. For this reason, a shadow is not a real thing. It is only a dharma that deceives the eye, like a person holding a firebrand and swiftly turning it into a wheel. It is not real.

A shadow is not an existing thing. If a shadow were a thing, it should be breakable and destructible. But if the form is not destroyed, the shadow is never destroyed. For this reason, it is empty.

Furthermore, because a shadow depends on form and is not autonomous, it is empty. Although it is empty, the mind gives rise to it and the eye sees it.

For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like a shadow.

Like an Image in a Mirror

「如鏡中像」者，如鏡中像非鏡作，非面作，非執鏡者作，亦非自然作，亦非無因緣。

何以非鏡作？若面未到，鏡則無像，以是故非鏡作。何以非面作？無鏡則無像。何以非執鏡者作？無鏡無面則無像。何以非自然作？若未有鏡，未有面，則無像，像待鏡、待面然後有。以是故非自然作。何以非無因緣？若無因緣應常有；若常有，若除鏡、除面，亦應自出，以是故非無因緣。

"Like an image in a mirror" means this: an image in a mirror is not made by the mirror, not made by the face, not made by the person holding the mirror, not naturally produced, and not without causes and conditions.

Why is it not made by the mirror? If the face has not come before it, there is no image in the mirror. For this reason, it is not made by the mirror. Why is it not made by the face? Without a mirror, there is no image. Why is it not made by the person holding the mirror? Without a mirror and without a face, there is no image. Why is it not naturally produced? If there is no mirror and no face, there is no image. The image depends on the mirror and depends on the face; only then does it exist. For this reason, it is not naturally produced. Why is it not without causes and conditions? If it were without causes and conditions, it should always exist. If it always existed, then even if the mirror and the face were removed, it should still appear by itself. For this reason, it is not without causes and conditions.

諸法亦如是，非自作，非彼作，非共作，非無因緣。

云何非自作？我不可得故，一切因生法不自在故，諸法屬因緣故，是以非自作。

亦非他作者，自無故他亦無，若他作則失罪福力，他作有二種：若善、若不善；若善應與一切樂，若不善應與一切苦。若苦樂雜，以何因緣故與樂？以何因緣故與苦？

若共有二過故，自過、他過。

若無因緣生苦樂，人應常樂，離一切苦；若無因緣，人不應作樂因，除苦因；一切諸法必有因緣，愚癡故不知。

All dharmas are also like this. They are not made by themselves, not made by another, not made by both together, and not without causes and conditions.

How are they not made by themselves? Because a self cannot be obtained; because all dharmas born from causes are not autonomous; and because dharmas belong to causes and conditions. For these reasons, they are not made by themselves.

Nor are they made by another. Because there is no self, there is also no other. If they were made by another, then the power of offenses and merits would be lost. What is made by another would be of two kinds: good or not good. If it were good, it should give everyone only happiness. If it were not good, it should give everyone only suffering. If suffering and happiness were mixed, then by what causes and conditions would it give happiness? By what causes and conditions would it give suffering?

If they were made by both together, there would be two faults: the fault of self-production and the fault of production by another.

If suffering and happiness arose without causes and conditions, people should always be happy and apart from all suffering. If there were no causes and conditions, people should not have to create causes for happiness or remove causes of suffering. All dharmas necessarily have causes and conditions, but because of delusion people do not know this.

譬如，人從木求火，從地求水，從扇求風；如是等種種各有因緣。是苦樂，和合因緣，是苦樂和合因緣生，先世業因今世若好行、若邪行緣，從是得苦樂；是苦樂種種因緣，以實求之，無人作，無人受，空五眾作，空五眾受。

無智人得樂，婬心愛著，得苦生瞋恚；是樂滅時，更求欲得。如小兒見鏡中像，心樂愛著，愛著失已，破鏡求索，智人笑之；失樂更求，亦復如是，亦為得道聖人所笑。

以是故，說諸法如鏡中像。

For example, people seek fire from wood, water from the ground, and wind from a fan. In these and many other cases, each has its causes and conditions. Suffering and happiness are also causes and conditions gathered together. Suffering and happiness are born from gathered causes and conditions: past-life karma is the cause, and in the present life good conduct or wrong conduct is the condition. From these one obtains suffering and happiness. Suffering and happiness have many kinds of causes and conditions. If one seeks them according to reality, there is no person who creates them and no person who receives them. The empty five aggregates create them; the empty five aggregates receive them.

When people without wisdom obtain happiness, their minds become attached through desire. When they obtain suffering, they give rise to anger. When that happiness ceases, they seek to obtain it again. This is like a small child who sees an image in a mirror, takes delight in it, and becomes attached to it. When the object of attachment is lost, the child breaks the mirror and searches for it. A wise person laughs at this. Seeking again after lost happiness is just the same, and is also laughed at by noble ones who have attained the path.

For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like an image in a mirror.

復次，如鏡中像實空，不生不滅，誑惑凡人眼；一切諸法亦復如是空無實，不生不滅，誑惑凡夫人眼。

問曰：鏡中像從因緣生，有面、有鏡、有持鏡人、有明，是事和合故像生；因是像生憂喜，亦作因，亦作果，云何言實空不生不滅？

Furthermore, an image in a mirror is truly empty, neither born nor destroyed, and it deceives and confuses the eyes of ordinary people. All dharmas are also like this: empty and without reality, neither born nor destroyed, deceiving and confusing the eyes of ordinary people.

Question: An image in a mirror is born from causes and conditions. There is a face, there is a mirror, there is a person holding the mirror, and there is light. Because these things come together, the image is born. On account of this image, sadness and joy arise. It also acts as a cause and also acts as an effect. How can you say it is truly empty, unborn, and undestroyed?

答曰：從因緣生，不自在故空；若法實有，是亦不應從因緣生。何以故？若因緣中先有，因緣則無所用；若因緣中先無，因緣亦無所用。

譬如，乳中若先有酪，是乳非酪，因酪先有故，若先無酪，如水中無酪，是乳亦非因；若無因而有酪者，水中何以不生酪？若乳是酪因緣，乳亦不自在，乳亦從因緣生；乳從牛有，牛從水草生，如是無邊，皆有因緣。

Answer: Because it is born from causes and conditions, it is not autonomous; therefore it is empty. If a dharma truly existed, it should not be born from causes and conditions. Why? If it already existed in the causes and conditions, then the causes and conditions would have no use. If it did not already exist in the causes and conditions, then the causes and conditions would also have no use.

For example, if curd already existed in milk, then the milk would not be curd, because the curd already existed. If curd did not already exist in milk, just as there is no curd in water, then the milk also would not be its cause. If curd could exist without a cause, why would curd not be born in water? If milk is the cause and condition for curd, then milk also is not autonomous, for milk too is born from causes and conditions. Milk comes from a cow; the cow is born from water and grass. In this way the sequence is without limit, and all things have causes and conditions.

以是故因緣中果，不得言有，不得言無，不得言有無，不得言非有非無，諸法從因緣生，無自性，如鏡中像。如偈說：

若法因緣生，是法性實空；若此法不空，不從因緣有。

譬如鏡中像，非鏡亦非面，亦非持鏡人，非自非無因。

非有亦非無，亦復非有無，此語亦不受，如是名中道。

以是故，說諸法如鏡中像。

For this reason, with regard to the effect within causes and conditions, one cannot say it exists, cannot say it does not exist, cannot say it both exists and does not exist, and cannot say it neither exists nor does not exist. Dharmas are born from causes and conditions and have no self-nature, like an image in a mirror. As a verse says:

If a dharma is born from causes and conditions,
the nature of that dharma is truly empty.
If this dharma were not empty,
it would not exist through causes and conditions.

It is like an image in a mirror:
not the mirror and not the face,
not the person holding the mirror,
not from itself and not without a cause.

It is not existent and not nonexistent,
and also not both existent and nonexistent.
Even this statement is not accepted.
This is called the middle way.

For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like an image in a mirror.

Like a Magical Transformation

「如化」者，十四變化心：初禪二：欲界、初禪；二禪三：欲界、初禪、二禪；三禪四：欲界、初禪、二禪、三禪；四禪五：欲界、初禪、二禪、三禪、四禪。

是十四變化心，作八種變化：一者、能作小乃至微塵；二者、能作大乃至滿虛空；三者、能作輕乃至如鴻毛；四者、能作自在：能以大為小，以長為短，如是種種；五者、能有主力；六者、能遠到；七者、能動地；八者、隨意所欲盡能得：一身能作多身，多身能作一；石壁皆過，履水蹈虛，手捫日月；能轉四大：地作水，水作地，火作風，風作火，石作金，金作石。

"Like a magical transformation" refers to the fourteen transformation minds. In the first dhyāna there are two: the desire realm and the first dhyāna. In the second dhyāna there are three: the desire realm, the first dhyāna, and the second dhyāna. In the third dhyāna there are four: the desire realm, the first dhyāna, the second dhyāna, and the third dhyāna. In the fourth dhyāna there are five: the desire realm, the first dhyāna, the second dhyāna, the third dhyāna, and the fourth dhyāna.

These fourteen transformation minds perform eight kinds of transformations. First, they can make something small, even as small as a fine particle. Second, they can make something large, even filling empty space. Third, they can make something light, even as light as a swan's down. Fourth, they can exercise mastery: making the large small, the long short, and many other such things. Fifth, they can possess sovereign power. Sixth, they can reach far away. Seventh, they can shake the earth. Eighth, whatever one wishes can be obtained at will: one body can be made into many bodies, and many bodies can be made into one; one can pass through stone walls, walk on water, tread through empty space, and touch the sun and moon with the hand; one can transform the four great elements: earth into water, water into earth, fire into wind, wind into fire, stone into gold, and gold into stone.

是變化復有四種：欲界藥物、寶物、幻術能變化諸物；諸神通人神力故，能變化諸物；天龍鬼神輩得生報力故，能變化諸物；色界生報，修定力故，能變化諸物。

如化人無生、老、病、死，無苦無樂，異於人生，以是故空無實。一切諸法亦如是，皆無生、住、滅，以是故，說諸法如化。

Transformation is also of four kinds. In the desire realm, medicines, jewels, and magical arts can transform things. People with superknowledges, by the power of their spiritual abilities, can transform things. Devas, nāgas, ghosts, and spirits, by the resultant power of their birth, can transform things. Beings in the form realm, by the resultant power of their birth and by the power of cultivated concentration, can transform things.

A magically transformed person has no birth, aging, sickness, or death, and has no suffering or happiness. This is different from the life of a human being. For this reason, it is empty and without reality. All dharmas are also like this: they all have no arising, abiding, or ceasing. For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like a magical transformation.

復次，化生無定物，但以心生，便有所作，皆無有實；人身亦如是，本無所因，但從先世心，生今世身，皆無有實。以是故，說諸法如化。

如變化心滅則化滅；諸法亦如是，因緣滅果亦滅，不自在；如化事雖實空，能令眾生生憂苦、瞋恚、喜樂、癡惑；諸法亦如是，雖空無實，能令眾生起歡喜、瞋恚、憂怖等。

以是故，說諸法如化。

Furthermore, what is born by transformation has no fixed substance. It simply arises from mind and then performs actions, but none of it has reality. The human body is also like this. It originally has no independent cause; it is only from the mind of past lives that the body of this life is born. None of this has reality. For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like a magical transformation.

When the transformation mind ceases, the transformation ceases. All dharmas are also like this: when causes and conditions cease, the effect also ceases, and is not autonomous. Although a magical transformation is truly empty, it can cause beings to give rise to sorrow, suffering, anger, joy, pleasure, delusion, and confusion. All dharmas are also like this. Although they are empty and without reality, they can cause beings to give rise to joy, anger, sorrow, fear, and so on.

For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like a magical transformation.

復次，如變化生法，無初、無中、無後；諸法亦如是。如變化，生時無所從來，滅亦無所去；諸法亦如是。

復次，如變化相，清淨如虛空，無所染著，不為罪福所污；諸法亦如是，如法性，如如，如真際，自然常淨。

譬如閻浮提四大河，一河有五百小河屬，是水種種不淨，入大海水中皆清淨。

Furthermore, a dharma born by transformation has no beginning, no middle, and no end. All dharmas are also like this. When a transformation is born, it comes from nowhere; when it ceases, it goes nowhere. All dharmas are also like this.

Furthermore, the mark of a transformation is pure like empty space. It is not attached to anything and is not defiled by offenses or merits. All dharmas are also like this: like the nature of dharmas, like suchness, like the true limit, they are naturally and always pure.

For example, in Jambudvīpa there are four great rivers, and each river has five hundred small rivers connected to it. Their waters contain many kinds of impurities, but when they enter the waters of the great ocean, they all become pure.

問曰：不應言變化事空。何以故？變化心亦從修定得，從此心作種種變化，若人若法；是化有因有果，云何空？

答曰：如影中已答，今當更答，此因緣雖有，變化果空。如口言無所有，雖心生口言，不可以心口有故，所言無所有便是有；若言有第二頭，第三手，雖從心口生，不可言有頭有手。

Question: One should not say that transformed things are empty. Why? The transformation mind is also obtained from cultivating concentration, and from this mind one makes many kinds of transformations, whether persons or dharmas. This transformation has a cause and has an effect. How can it be empty?

Answer: This has already been answered in the section on shadows, and now it will be answered again. Although these causes and conditions exist, the transformed effect is empty. For example, when the mouth says, "There is nothing," although the mind gives rise to the spoken words, one cannot say that because the mind and mouth exist, the spoken phrase "there is nothing" therefore exists as a thing. If someone says, "There is a second head and a third hand," although this comes from mind and mouth, one cannot say that there really is a head or a hand.

如佛說：觀無生，從有生得脫；依無為，從有為得脫；雖觀無生法無，而可作因緣，無為亦爾；變化雖空，亦能生心因緣。

譬如幻、焰等九譬喻雖無，能生種種心。

復次，是化事於六因四緣中求不可得，是中六因、四緣不相應故空。

復次，空不以不見為空，以其無實用故言空。以是故，言諸法如化。

As the Buddha said, by contemplating non-arising one is liberated from what has arising; by relying on the unconditioned, one is liberated from the conditioned. Although one contemplates that the dharma of non-arising is nonexistent, it can still act as a cause and condition. The unconditioned is also like this. Although transformation is empty, it can still give rise to causes and conditions in the mind.

For example, although illusion, mirage, and the other nine similes do not exist, they can give rise to many kinds of mind.

Furthermore, if one seeks this transformed thing among the six causes and four conditions, it cannot be obtained. Because the six causes and four conditions do not correspond to it, it is empty.

Furthermore, emptiness is not called emptiness because something is not seen. It is called empty because it has no real function. For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like a magical transformation.

問曰：若諸法十譬喻皆空無異者，何以但以十事為喻，不以山河石壁等為喻？

答曰：諸法雖空而有分別，有難解空，有易解空，今以易解空喻難解空。

復次，諸法有二種：有心著處，有心不著處，以心不著處解心著處。

Question: If all dharmas in the ten similes are empty and no different from one another, why are only these ten things used as similes? Why are mountains, rivers, stone walls, and so on not used as similes?

Answer: Although all dharmas are empty, there are distinctions. There is emptiness that is difficult to understand, and there is emptiness that is easy to understand. Now emptiness that is easy to understand is used as a simile for emptiness that is difficult to understand.

Furthermore, dharmas are of two kinds: places where the mind becomes attached, and places where the mind does not become attached. Places where the mind does not become attached are used to explain places where the mind does become attached.

問曰：此十譬喻，何以是心不著處？

答曰：是十事不久住，易生易滅故，以是故，是心不著處。

復次，有人知十喻，誑惑耳目法，不知諸法空故，以此喻諸法。

若有人於十譬喻中心著，不解種種難論，以此為有，是十譬喻不為其用，應更為說餘法門。

Question: Why are these ten similes places where the mind does not become attached?

Answer: These ten things do not abide for long; they easily arise and easily cease. For this reason, they are places where the mind does not become attached.

Furthermore, some people know that the ten similes are dharmas that deceive and confuse the ears and eyes, but they do not know that all dharmas are empty. Therefore these similes are used for all dharmas.

If someone becomes attached in mind to the ten similes and does not understand the various difficult discussions, taking these similes to exist, then the ten similes do not serve their purpose for that person. One should teach that person another Dharma gateway.

問曰：若諸法都空，不生不滅，是十譬喻等種種譬喻，種種因緣論議，我已悉知為空；若諸法都空，不應說是喻；若說是喻，是為不空。

答曰：我說空，破諸法有；今所說者，若說有，先已破；若說無，不應難！

譬如執事比丘高聲舉手唱言：眾皆寂靜；是為以聲遮聲，非求聲也。以是故，雖說諸法空，不生不滅，愍念眾生故，雖說非有也。

以是故，說諸法如化。

Question: If all dharmas are entirely empty, unborn, and undestroyed, then these ten similes and other kinds of similes, causes and conditions, and discussions, all of which I already know to be empty, should not be spoken. If all dharmas are entirely empty, these similes should not be taught. If these similes are taught, then this is not empty.

Answer: I speak of emptiness in order to break the idea that dharmas exist. As for what is now being said, if one says that they exist, this has already been refuted; if one says that they do not exist, one should not object.

For example, when the monk in charge raises his hand and calls out loudly, "Everyone be silent," this uses sound to stop sound; it is not seeking sound. For this reason, although dharmas are spoken of as empty, unborn, and undestroyed, out of compassion for beings they are spoken of even though they are not existent.

For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like a magical transformation.
