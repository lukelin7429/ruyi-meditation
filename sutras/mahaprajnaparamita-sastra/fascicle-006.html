The Ten Similes

Fascicle 6 begins the explanation of ten similes for emptiness: illusion, mirage, moon in water, empty space, echo, gandharva city, dream, shadow, mirror image, and magical transformation.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: translation in progress Source base: Taishō T25, no. 1509 Ten similes for emptiness

Opening the Ten Similes

Chinese Source

大智度初品中十喻釋論第十一（第六卷）

Ru-Yi Translation

Explanation of the Ten Similes in the First Chapter of the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra, Part Eleven, Fascicle Six

﹝經﹞

解了諸法如幻、如焰、如水中月、如虛空、如響、如犍闥婆城、如夢、如影、如鏡中像、如化。

Sutra:

They understood all dharmas as like an illusion, like a mirage, like the moon in water, like empty space, like an echo, like a gandharva city, like a dream, like a shadow, like an image in a mirror, and like a magical transformation.

﹝論﹞

是十喻，為解空法故。

Treatise:

These ten similes are given in order to explain the Dharma of emptiness.

Like an Illusion

問曰：若一切諸法空如幻，何以故諸法有可見、可聞、可嗅、可甞、可觸、可識者？

若實無所有，不應有可見乃至可識。復次，若無而妄見者，何以不見聲、聞色？若皆一等空無所有，何以有可見、不可見者？以諸法空故，如一指第一甲無，第二甲亦無，何以不見第二甲，獨見第一甲？以是故知：第一甲實有故可見，第二甲實無故不可見。

Question: If all dharmas are empty like an illusion, why are there dharmas that can be seen, heard, smelled, tasted, touched, and known?

If they truly do not exist at all, there should be nothing that can be seen, and so on, up to nothing that can be known. Furthermore, if what does not exist is falsely seen, why does one not see sounds and hear forms? If all are equally empty and without anything existent, why are there things that can be seen and things that cannot be seen? Since all dharmas are empty, then just as the first fingernail of one finger does not exist, the second fingernail also does not exist. Why, then, is the second fingernail not seen, while only the first fingernail is seen? For this reason one knows: because the first fingernail truly exists, it can be seen; because the second fingernail truly does not exist, it cannot be seen.

答曰：諸法相雖空，亦有分別可見、不可見。

譬如幻化象、馬及種種諸物，雖知無實，然色可見，聲可聞，與六情相對，不相錯亂。

諸法亦如是，雖空而可見、可聞，不相錯亂。

Answer: Although the marks of all dharmas are empty, there are still distinctions between what can be seen and what cannot be seen.

It is like an illusionist conjuring elephants, horses, and many other things. Although one knows they are not real, their forms can be seen and their sounds can be heard. They stand in relation to the six faculties without being confused with one another.

All dharmas are also like this. Although they are empty, they can still be seen and heard, without confusion.

如《德女經》說：「德女白佛言：『世尊！如無明內有不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『外有不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『內外有不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『世尊！是無明從先世來不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『從此世至後世不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『是無明有生者、滅者不？』

「佛言：『不！』

「『有一法定實性，是名無明不？』

「佛言：『不！』

As the Sutra of the Virtuous Woman says, "The Virtuous Woman said to the Buddha, 'Blessed One, does ignorance exist internally?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Does it exist externally?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Does it exist both internally and externally?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Blessed One, does this ignorance come from a former life?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Does it go from this life to a later life?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Does this ignorance have one who produces it or one who destroys it?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

"'Is there one dharma with a fixed real nature that is called ignorance?'

"The Buddha said, 'No.'

「爾時德女復白佛言：『若無明無內，無外，亦無內外，不從先世至今世、今世至後世，亦無真實性者，云何從無明緣行乃至眾苦集？世尊！譬如有樹，若無根者，云何得生莖、節、枝、葉、華、果？』

「佛言：『諸法相雖空，凡夫無聞無智故，而於中生種種煩惱，煩惱因緣作身、口、意業，業因緣作後身，身因緣受苦受樂。是中無有實作煩惱，亦無身、口、意業，亦無有受苦樂者。譬如幻師，幻作種種事。於汝意云何？是幻所作內有不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『外有不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『內外有不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『從先世至今世，今世至後世不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『幻所作有生者滅者不？』

「答言：『不！』

「『實有一法是幻所作不？』

「答言：『不！』

"At that time the Virtuous Woman again said to the Buddha, 'If ignorance is not internal, not external, and not both internal and external; if it does not come from a former life to this life or go from this life to a later life; and if it has no real nature, then how can formations arise conditioned by ignorance, and so on up to the gathering of all suffering? Blessed One, suppose there is a tree. If it has no roots, how can it produce trunk, joints, branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit?'

"The Buddha said, 'Although the marks of all dharmas are empty, ordinary beings are without learning and without wisdom. Therefore, within them they produce many kinds of afflictions. Conditioned by afflictions, they create bodily, verbal, and mental karma. Conditioned by karma, they create a later body. Conditioned by the body, they experience suffering and pleasure. Within this, there is no real one who creates afflictions, no bodily, verbal, or mental karma, and no one who experiences suffering and pleasure. It is like an illusionist creating many illusory things. What do you think? Do the things made by illusion exist internally?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Do they exist externally?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Do they exist both internally and externally?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Do they come from a former life to this life, or go from this life to a later life?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Do the things made by illusion have one who produces them or one who destroys them?'

"She answered, 'No.'

"'Is there truly one dharma that is made by illusion?'

"She answered, 'No.'

「佛言：『汝頗見頗聞幻所作妓樂不？』

「答言：『我亦聞亦見。』

「佛問德女：『若幻空，欺誑無實，云何從幻能作伎樂？』

「德女白佛：『世尊！是幻相爾，雖無根本而可聞見。』

「佛言：『無明亦如是，雖不內有，不外有，不內外有；不先世至今世，今世至後世，亦無實性，無有生者、滅者，而無明因緣諸行生，乃至眾苦陰集。如幻息，幻所作亦息；無明亦爾，無明盡，行亦盡，乃至眾苦集皆盡。』」

復次，是幻譬喻，示眾生一切有為法空不堅固；如說：「一切諸行如幻，欺誑小兒，屬因緣，不自在，不久住。」

是故說「諸菩薩知諸法如幻」。

"The Buddha said, 'Have you ever seen or heard music and performances made by illusion?'

"She answered, 'I have both heard and seen them.'

"The Buddha asked the Virtuous Woman, 'If illusion is empty, deceptive, and unreal, how can music and performances be made from illusion?'

"The Virtuous Woman said to the Buddha, 'Blessed One, such is the mark of illusion. Although it has no root, it can still be heard and seen.'

"The Buddha said, 'Ignorance is also like this. Although it does not exist internally, does not exist externally, and does not exist both internally and externally; although it does not come from a former life to this life or go from this life to a later life; although it has no real nature and has no one who produces it or destroys it, still, conditioned by ignorance, formations arise, and so on up to the gathering of all aggregates of suffering. When illusion ceases, what was made by illusion also ceases. Ignorance is the same. When ignorance is exhausted, formations are also exhausted, and so on until the whole mass of suffering is also exhausted.'"

Furthermore, the simile of illusion shows beings that all conditioned dharmas are empty and not solid. As it is said, "All formations are like illusions. They deceive children. They depend on causes and conditions, are not autonomous, and do not abide for long."

For this reason, it says, "Bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like illusions."

Like a Mirage

「如炎」者，炎以日光風動塵故，曠野中見如野馬，無智人初見謂之為水。

男相、女相亦如是，結使煩惱日光熱諸行塵，邪憶念風，生死曠野中轉，無智慧者謂為一相，為男、為女，是名如炎。

"Like a mirage" means this: because sunlight, wind, and moving dust come together, in a wilderness one sees something like a shimmering haze. When people without wisdom first see it, they think it is water.

The marks of male and female are also like this. The sunlight of fetters and afflictions heats the dust of formations; the wind of wrong recollection moves through the wilderness of birth and death. Those without wisdom take it to be one fixed mark, calling it male or female. This is called being like a mirage.

復次，若遠見炎，想為水，近則無水想。無智人亦如是，若遠聖法，不知無我，不知諸法空，於陰、界、入性空法中，生人相、男相、女相；近聖法，則知諸法實相，是時虛誑種種妄想盡除。

以是故說：「諸菩薩知諸法如炎。」

Furthermore, when one sees a mirage from far away, one imagines it to be water. When one comes near, there is no perception of water. People without wisdom are also like this. When they are far from the noble Dharma, they do not know non-self and do not know that all dharmas are empty. Within the empty nature of the aggregates, elements, and sense fields, they produce the mark of a person, the mark of male, and the mark of female. When they draw near to the noble Dharma, they know the true mark of all dharmas, and at that time all kinds of false and deceptive imaginings are removed.

For this reason, it says, "Bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like a mirage."

Like the Moon in Water

「如水中月」者，月實在虛空中，影現於水；實法相月在如、法性、實際虛空中，而凡天人心水中有我、我所相現。以是故名「如水中月」。

"Like the moon in water" means this: the moon truly exists in empty space, while its reflection appears in water. The moon of the true mark of dharmas abides in the empty space of suchness, Dharma-nature, and the true limit, while in the mind-water of ordinary devas and humans the marks of self and what belongs to self appear. For this reason it is called "like the moon in water."

復次，如小兒見水中月，歡喜欲取，大人見之則笑。無智人亦如是，身見故見有吾我；無實智故，見種種法；見已歡喜，欲取諸相：男相、女相等；諸得道聖人笑之。如偈說：

如水中月炎中水，夢中得財死求生；有人於此實欲得，是人癡惑聖所笑！

Furthermore, it is like a child who sees the moon in water, becomes delighted, and wants to grasp it. An adult who sees this laughs. People without wisdom are also like this. Because of the view of the body, they see a self. Because they lack true wisdom, they see many kinds of dharmas. Having seen them, they become delighted and want to grasp all their marks: the mark of male, the mark of female, and so on. Noble ones who have attained the path laugh at them. As a verse says:

The moon in water, water in a mirage,
wealth gained in a dream, life sought in death:
if someone truly wants to obtain these,
that person's delusion is laughed at by the noble ones.

復次，譬如靜水中見月影，攪水則不見。無明心靜水中，見吾我、憍慢諸結使影；實智慧杖攪心水，則不見吾我等諸結使影。

以是故說：「諸菩薩知諸法如水中月。」

Furthermore, it is like seeing the moon's reflection in still water: when the water is stirred, the reflection is no longer seen. In the still water of the mind of ignorance, one sees the shadows of self, pride, and the fetters. When the staff of true wisdom stirs the water of the mind, the shadows of self and the other fetters are no longer seen.

For this reason, it says, "Bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like the moon in water."

Like Empty Space

「如虛空」者，但有名而無實法；虛空非可見法，遠視故，眼光轉見縹色。

諸法亦如是，空無所有，人遠無漏實智慧故，棄實相，見彼我、男女、屋舍、城郭等種種雜物心著。

如小兒仰視青天，謂有實色；有人飛上極遠而無所見，以遠視故，謂為青色。諸法亦如是，以是故，說如虛空。

"Like empty space" means this: it has only a name and no real dharma. Empty space is not a visible dharma, but because one looks from far away, the eye's light turns and sees a pale blue color.

All dharmas are also like this. They are empty and have nothing at all, but because people are far from undefiled true wisdom, they abandon the true mark and see self and other, male and female, houses, cities, and many other mixed things, and their minds cling to them.

It is like a child looking up at the blue sky and thinking there is a real color there. If someone flies up extremely far, there is nothing to be seen. Because it is viewed from far away, it is taken to be blue. All dharmas are also like this. For this reason, they are said to be like empty space.

復次，如虛空性常清淨，人謂陰曀為不淨。諸法亦如是，性常清淨，婬欲、瞋恚等曀故，人謂為不淨。如偈說：

如夏月天雷電雨，陰雲覆曀不清淨；凡夫無智亦如是，種種煩惱常覆心。

如冬天日時一出，常為昏氣雲蔭曀；雖得初果第二道，猶為欲染之所蔽。

若如春天日欲出，時為陰雲所覆曀；雖離欲染第三果，餘殘癡慢猶覆心。

若如秋日無雲曀，亦如大海水清淨；所作已辦無漏心，羅漢如是得清淨。

Furthermore, the nature of empty space is always pure, yet people say it is impure when it is covered by clouds and haze. All dharmas are also like this: their nature is always pure, but because they are obscured by lust, anger, and the like, people say they are impure. As a verse says:

In the summer sky, thunder, lightning, and rain:
clouds cover it, so it seems impure.
Ordinary beings without wisdom are also like this:
many afflictions always cover the mind.

When the winter sun first appears,
it is still shaded by dim vapors and clouds.
Even one who has attained the first fruit or the second path
is still covered by the stain of desire.

When the spring sun is about to rise,
it may still be covered by clouds.
Even one who has left the stain of desire and reached the third fruit
still has remaining delusion and conceit covering the mind.

When the autumn sun has no clouds or haze,
it is also like the great ocean with water clear and pure.
With what had to be done already done, the undefiled mind
of an arhat becomes pure in this way.

復次，虛空無初、無中、無後，諸法亦如是。

復次，如摩訶衍中，佛語須菩提：「虛空無前世，亦無中世，亦無後世，諸法亦如是。」彼經，此中應廣說。

是故說諸法如虛空。

Furthermore, empty space has no beginning, no middle, and no end. All dharmas are also like this.

Furthermore, as in the Mahayana, the Buddha said to Subhuti, "Empty space has no past life, no middle life, and no future life. All dharmas are also like this." That sutra should be explained extensively here.

For this reason, all dharmas are said to be like empty space.

問曰：虛空實有法。何以故？若虛空無實法者，若舉若下，若來若往，若屈若申，若出若入等，有所作應無有，以無動處故。

答曰：若虛空法實有，虛空應有住處。何以故？無住處則無法。若虛空在孔穴中住，是為虛空在虛空中住，以是故不應孔中住；若在實中住，是實非空，則不得住，無所受故。

復次，汝言：「住處是虛空。」如石壁實中，無有住處；若無住處則無虛空。以虛空無住處，故無虛空。

Question: Empty space is a real dharma. Why? If empty space were not a real dharma, then actions such as lifting and lowering, coming and going, bending and stretching, entering and exiting should not exist, because there would be no place for movement.

Answer: If the dharma of empty space truly existed, empty space should have an abiding place. Why? Without an abiding place, there is no dharma. If empty space abides in holes and openings, then empty space would be abiding in empty space; for this reason, it should not abide in holes. If it abides in something solid, that solid thing is not empty, and so it cannot abide there, because there is no room to receive it.

Furthermore, you say, "The abiding place is empty space." But in a solid stone wall there is no abiding place. If there is no abiding place, then there is no empty space. Because empty space has no abiding place, there is no empty space.

復次，無相故無虛空；諸法各各有相，相有故知有法。如地堅相，水濕相，火熱相，風動相，識識相，慧解相，世間生滅相，涅槃永滅相；是虛空無相故無。

問曰：虛空有相，汝不知故言無；無色處是虛空相。

答曰：不爾！無色是名破色，更無異法，如燈滅，更無法。以是故，無有虛空相。

復次，是虛空法無。何以故？汝因色故，以無色處是虛空相。若爾者，色未生時，則無虛空相。

復次，汝謂：「色是無常法，虛空是有常法。」色未有時，應先有虛空法，以有常故。若色未有，則無無色處；若無無色處，則無虛空相；若無相則無法，以是故虛空但有名而無實。

如虛空，諸法亦如是，但有假名而無實。

以是故，諸菩薩知諸法如虛空。

Furthermore, because empty space has no mark, there is no empty space. Each dharma has its own mark, and because there is a mark, one knows that there is a dharma. For example, earth has the mark of hardness, water has the mark of wetness, fire has the mark of heat, wind has the mark of movement, consciousness has the mark of knowing, wisdom has the mark of understanding, the world has the mark of arising and ceasing, and nirvana has the mark of permanent cessation. Because empty space has no mark, it does not exist.

Question: Empty space has a mark, but because you do not know it, you say it has none. The place without form is the mark of empty space.

Answer: That is not so. "Without form" is only the breaking apart of form; there is no separate dharma. It is like a lamp going out: there is no further dharma. For this reason, there is no mark of empty space.

Furthermore, this dharma of empty space does not exist. Why? You depend on form and take the place without form to be the mark of empty space. If that is so, then before form has arisen, there would be no mark of empty space.

Furthermore, you say, "Form is an impermanent dharma, while empty space is an existent permanent dharma." Before form existed, the dharma of empty space should already have existed, because it is permanent. But if form has not yet existed, then there is no place without form. If there is no place without form, then there is no mark of empty space. If there is no mark, then there is no dharma. For this reason, empty space has only a name and no reality.

Just as with empty space, all dharmas are also like this: they have only provisional names and no reality.

For this reason, bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like empty space.

Like an Echo

「如響」者，若深山狹谷中，若深絕澗中，若空大舍中；若語聲，若打聲，從聲有聲，名為響。無智人謂為有人語聲，智者心念是聲無人作，但以聲觸，故更有聲名為響；響事空，能誑耳根。

"Like an echo" means this: in deep mountains and narrow valleys, in deep and sheer ravines, or in a large empty hall, whether there is the sound of speech or the sound of striking, from one sound another sound arises. This is called an echo. People without wisdom think it is the voice of someone speaking. Wise people think, "This sound is not made by a person. It is only because sound has struck something that another sound arises and is called an echo." The event of an echo is empty, yet it can deceive the ear faculty.

如人欲語時，口中風名憂陀那，還入至臍，觸臍響出，響出時觸七處退，是名語言。如偈說：

風名憂檀那，觸臍而上去；是風七處觸，項及齗齒脣，舌咽及以胸，是中語言生。

愚人不解此，惑著起瞋癡；中人有智慧，不瞋亦不著，亦復不愚癡，但隨諸法相。

曲直及屈申，去來現語言，都無有作者。是事是幻耶？為機關木人？為是夢中事？我為熱氣悶，有是為無是？是事誰能知？是骨人筋纏，能作是語聲，如融金投水。

以是故，言諸菩薩知諸法如響。

When a person is about to speak, the wind in the mouth is called udāna. It returns inward to the navel, touches the navel, and sound comes out. When the sound comes out, it touches seven places and withdraws. This is called speech. As a verse says:

The wind is called udāna;
it touches the navel and rises upward.
This wind touches seven places:
the neck, gums, teeth, lips,
the tongue, throat, and chest.
From these, speech is born.

Foolish people do not understand this.
Confused and attached, they give rise to anger and delusion.
A person of moderate wisdom
does not become angry and does not cling,
and also does not become foolish,
but simply follows the marks of dharmas.

Bent or straight, contracted or extended,
coming and going, speech appears;
yet there is no maker at all.
Is this an illusion?
Is it a wooden puppet mechanism?
Is it something in a dream?
Am I oppressed by heat,
so that what exists seems not to exist?
Who can know this matter?
This person of bones wrapped in sinews
can make these sounds of speech,
like molten gold poured into water.

For this reason, it says that bodhisattvas know all dharmas as like an echo.

Like a Gandharva City

「如犍闥婆城」者，日初出時，見城門、樓櫓、宮殿、行人出入，日轉高轉滅；此城但可眼見而無有實，是名犍闥婆城。

有人初不見犍闥婆城，晨朝東向見之，意謂實樂；疾行趣之，轉近轉失，日高轉滅；飢渴悶極，見熱氣如野馬，謂之為水，疾走趣之，轉近轉滅；疲極困厄，至窮山狹谷中，大喚啼哭，聞有響應，謂有居民，求之疲極而無所見；思惟自悟，渴願心息。

"Like a gandharva city" means this: when the sun first rises, one sees city gates, watchtowers, palaces, and people coming and going. As the sun rises higher, it disappears. This city can only be seen by the eye, but it has no reality. This is called a gandharva city.

There may be someone who has never before seen a gandharva city. In the early morning, facing east, he sees it and thinks it is real and delightful. He quickly walks toward it, but the closer he gets, the more it disappears; as the sun rises higher, it vanishes. Extremely hungry, thirsty, and distressed, he sees hot vapors like a mirage and thinks they are water. He runs quickly toward them, but the closer he gets, the more they vanish. Exhausted and in distress, he reaches a poor mountain and narrow valley. He cries out loudly and weeps; hearing an echo reply, he thinks there are residents there. He searches until he is exhausted, but sees nothing. Reflecting on this, he awakens for himself, and his thirsty wish comes to rest.

無智人亦如是，空陰、界、入中見吾我及諸法，婬瞋心著，四方狂走，求樂自滿，顛倒欺誑，窮極懊惱；若以智慧知無我、無實法者，是時顛倒願息。

復次，犍闥婆城非城，人心想為城；凡夫亦如是，非身想為身，非心想為心。

People without wisdom are also like this. Within the empty aggregates, elements, and sense fields, they see self and dharmas. Their minds cling through lust and anger, and they run wildly in the four directions, seeking pleasure to satisfy themselves. Deceived by inverted views, they become utterly distressed. If, through wisdom, they know that there is no self and no real dharma, then at that time their inverted wishes come to rest.

Furthermore, a gandharva city is not a city, but the mind imagines it to be a city. Ordinary beings are also like this: what is not a body, they imagine to be a body; what is not mind, they imagine to be mind.

問曰：一事可知，何以多喻？

答曰：我先已答，是摩訶衍如大海水，一切法盡攝；摩訶衍多因緣故，多譬喻無咎。

復次，是菩薩甚深利智故，種種法門，種種因緣，種種喻，壞諸法；為人解故，應多引喻。

復次，一切聲聞法中，無犍闥婆城喻，有種種餘無常喻：「色如聚沫，受如泡，想如野馬，行如芭蕉，識如幻」；及《幻網經》中空譬喻。以是犍闥婆城喻異故，此中說。

Question: If one matter can be understood, why give many similes?

Answer: I have already answered this earlier. The Mahayana is like the water of the great ocean: it includes all dharmas completely. Because the Mahayana has many causes and conditions, there is no fault in giving many similes.

Furthermore, because these bodhisattvas have profound and sharp wisdom, they use many Dharma gateways, many causes and conditions, and many similes to break down dharmas. In order to help people understand, they should draw on many similes.

Furthermore, in all the Dharma of the śrāvakas, there is no simile of a gandharva city. There are many other similes for impermanence: "Form is like a mass of foam, feeling is like a bubble, perception is like a mirage, formations are like a plantain tree, and consciousness is like an illusion," as well as the emptiness similes in the Sutra of the Net of Illusions. Because the simile of a gandharva city is different, it is explained here.

問曰：聲聞法中以城喻身，此中何以說犍闥婆城喻？

答曰：聲聞法中城喻眾緣實有，但城是假名；犍闥婆城眾緣亦無，如旋火輪，但惑人目。聲聞法中為破吾我故，以城為喻；此中菩薩利根深入諸法空中故，以犍闥婆城為喻。

以是故，說如犍闥婆城。

Question: In the Dharma of the śrāvakas, a city is used as a simile for the body. Why does this text use the simile of a gandharva city?

Answer: In the Dharma of the śrāvakas, the city simile means that the many conditions truly exist, while the city is only a provisional name. In a gandharva city, even the many conditions do not exist. It is like a spinning wheel of fire: it only deceives the eye. In the Dharma of the śrāvakas, the city is used as a simile in order to break the notion of self. Here, because bodhisattvas have sharp faculties and enter deeply into the emptiness of all dharmas, a gandharva city is used as the simile.

For this reason, it is said to be like a gandharva city.
