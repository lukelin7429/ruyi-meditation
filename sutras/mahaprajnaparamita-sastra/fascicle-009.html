The Buddha's Incomparable Splendor

Chinese Source

〖經〗爾時，世尊在師子座上坐，於三千大千世界中，其德特尊，光明色像威德巍巍，遍至十方如恒河沙等諸佛世界。譬如須彌山王光色殊特，眾山無能及者。

English Translation

Sutra: At that time, the World-Honored One sat on the lion seat. Throughout the great trichiliocosm, his virtue was supremely honored, and the majestic splendor of his light, appearance, and presence extended to Buddha worlds in the ten directions as numerous as the sands of the Ganges. He was like Sumeru, king of mountains, whose exceptional radiance and color no other mountain can equal.

〖論〗問曰：佛以何力故於一切眾生中其德特尊，光明、威德巍巍乃如是耶？如轉輪聖王、諸天、聖人亦有大力、光明、威德，何以獨言佛德特尊？

Treatise: Question: Through what power is the Buddha's virtue supremely honored among all beings, with such majestic light and presence? Wheel-turning sage kings, devas, and noble ones also possess great power, radiance, and majesty. Why say that the Buddha's virtue alone is supremely honored?

答曰：此諸賢聖雖有光明、威德，有量有限。譬如眾星，日光既出，則沒不現。佛從無量阿僧祇劫集大功德，一切具足，因緣大故果報亦大；餘人無此。

Answer: Although these worthy and noble ones possess radiance and majesty, theirs have measure and limits. They are like stars, which disappear from sight when the sun rises. Through immeasurable asaṃkhyeya kalpas, the Buddha has accumulated great merit and brought everything to completion. Because the causes and conditions are great, their result is also great. Others do not possess this.

復次，佛世世修諸苦行，無量無數頭、目、髓、腦常施眾生，豈唯國、財、妻、子而已！一切種種戒、種種忍、種種精進、種種禪定，及無比清淨不可壞不可盡智慧，世世修行，已具足滿。此果力故，得不可稱量殊特威神，以是故言「因緣大故果報亦大」。

Furthermore, life after life the Buddha cultivated arduous practices, constantly giving beings immeasurable and countless heads, eyes, marrow, and brains—not merely kingdoms, wealth, wives, and children! Every kind of morality, patience, diligence, and meditative concentration, together with incomparable wisdom that is pure, indestructible, and inexhaustible, he cultivated life after life and has now fulfilled completely. Through the power of these results, he obtains extraordinary spiritual majesty beyond measure. Therefore, it is said, 'Because the causes and conditions are great, their result is also great.'

Why Does the Buddha Appear to Undergo Suffering?

Chinese Source

問曰：若佛神力無量，威德巍巍，不可稱說，何以故受九罪報？一者、梵志女孫陀利謗，五百阿羅漢亦被謗；二者、旃遮婆羅門女繫木盂作腹謗佛；三者、提婆達推山壓佛，傷足大指；四者、迸木刺腳；五者、毘樓璃王興兵殺諸釋子，佛時頭痛；六者、受阿耆達多婆羅門請而食馬麥；七者、冷風動故脊痛；八者、六年苦行；九者、入婆羅門聚落，乞食不得，空鉢而還。

English Translation

Question: If the Buddha's spiritual power is immeasurable and his majestic virtue beyond description, why does he undergo nine painful karmic results? First, he was slandered in the affair of the female ascetic Sundarī, and five hundred arhats were also slandered. Second, the brahmin woman Ciñcā tied a wooden bowl over her abdomen and slandered him. Third, Devadatta rolled down a rock to crush him, injuring his big toe. Fourth, a splinter of wood pierced his foot. Fifth, when King Virūḍhaka raised an army and killed the Śākyas, the Buddha suffered a headache. Sixth, after accepting the invitation of the brahmin Agnidatta, he ate barley meant for horses. Seventh, a disturbance of cold wind caused back pain. Eighth, he practiced austerities for six years. Ninth, he entered a brahmin village seeking alms but obtained nothing and returned with an empty bowl.

復有冬至前後八夜，寒風破竹，索三衣禦寒。又復患熱，阿難在後扇佛。如是等世界小事，佛皆受之。若佛神力無量，三千大千世界乃至東方恒河沙等諸佛世界，南西北方、四維、上下，光明色像威德巍巍，何以故受諸罪報？

Furthermore, during eight nights around the winter solstice, when the cold wind split bamboo, he asked for the three robes to keep out the cold. He also suffered from heat, and Ānanda stood behind him fanning him. The Buddha experienced all these minor worldly troubles. If his spiritual power is immeasurable, and the majestic splendor of his light and appearance extends throughout the great trichiliocosm and Buddha worlds as numerous as the sands of the Ganges in the east, south, west, north, intermediate directions, above, and below, why does he undergo these painful karmic results?

答曰：佛在人中生，人父母，受人身力，一指節力勝千萬億那由他白象力，神通力無量無數、不可思議。是淨飯王子，厭老、病、死苦，出家得佛道——是人豈受罪報，為寒熱等所困！如佛神力不可思議，不可思議法中，何有寒熱諸患？

Answer: The Buddha was born among humans, of human parents. Even the strength of his human body, in a single finger joint, surpasses that of countless myriads of nayutas of white elephants. His supernormal power is immeasurable, countless, and inconceivable. This son of King Śuddhodana became disenchanted with the suffering of aging, sickness, and death, went forth, and attained the Buddha path. How could such a person be subject to punitive karmic results or troubled by cold and heat? The Buddha's spiritual power is inconceivable. Within inconceivable dharmas, how could there be afflictions of cold, heat, and the like?

Two Kinds of Buddha Body

Chinese Source

復次，佛有二種身：一者法性身，二者父母生身。是法性身滿十方虛空，無量無邊，色像端正，相好莊嚴，無量光明，無量音聲，聽法眾亦滿虛空；常出種種身、種種名號、種種生處、種種方便度眾生，常度一切，無須臾息時。如是法性身佛，能度十方世界眾生。受諸罪報者是生身佛，生身佛次第說法如人法。以有二種佛故，受諸罪無咎。

English Translation

Furthermore, the Buddha has two kinds of body: the body of Dharma nature and the body born of parents. The body of Dharma nature fills space throughout the ten directions, immeasurable and boundless. Its appearance is beautiful, adorned with the major and minor marks, with immeasurable light and immeasurable voice. The assembly hearing its Dharma also fills space. It constantly manifests various bodies, names, places of birth, and skillful means to liberate beings. It continually liberates all, without resting for even a moment. Such a Buddha with the body of Dharma nature can liberate beings in the worlds of the ten directions. The Buddha who undergoes painful karmic results is the Buddha with a body born of parents. This embodied Buddha teaches the Dharma step by step, in accordance with human ways. Because there are these two kinds of Buddha, there is no contradiction in his undergoing such suffering.

Apparent Suffering as Skillful Means

Chinese Source

復次，佛即得道時，一切不善法盡斷，一切善法皆成就，云何今實有不善法報可受？但憐愍未來世眾生故，現方便受此諸罪。

English Translation

Furthermore, when the Buddha attained the path, he completely cut off all unwholesome dharmas and perfected all wholesome dharmas. How, then, could there now really be results of unwholesome actions for him to undergo? It is only out of compassion for beings of future ages that he manifests the experience of these sufferings as skillful means.

復次，如阿泥盧豆與一辟支佛食故受無量世樂，心念飲食應意即得；何況佛世世割肉、出髓以施眾生，而乞食不得空鉢而還？以是事故，知佛方便為度眾生故受此諸罪。

Furthermore, because Aniruddha once gave food to a pratyekabuddha, he enjoyed happiness through immeasurable lives, obtaining whatever food or drink he wished for as soon as he thought of it. How, then, could the Buddha, who life after life cut off his flesh and gave his marrow to beings, fail to obtain almsfood and return with an empty bowl? From this we know that he undergoes these sufferings as skillful means to liberate beings.

云何方便憐愍未來世？五眾佛弟子施福薄故，乞種種自活之具不能得。諸白衣言：「汝衣食不能得，有病不能除，何能得道以益於人？」

How does this skillful means express compassion for future beings? Disciples of the Buddha belonging to the five monastic groups may have little merit from giving and be unable to obtain the various necessities they seek as alms. Householders may say, 'You cannot obtain clothing and food or cure your illnesses. How can you attain the path and benefit others?'

是五眾當答：「我等雖無活身小事，有行道福德。我等今日眾苦，是先身罪報；今之功德，利在將來。我等大師佛入婆羅門聚落乞食，尚亦不得，空鉢而還；佛亦有諸病，釋子畢罪時佛亦頭痛，何況我等薄福下人！」諸白衣聞已，瞋心則息，便以四種供養、供給比丘，身得安隱，坐禪得道。是為方便故，非實受罪。

These disciples should answer, 'Although we lack the minor necessities for maintaining our bodies, we possess the merit of practicing the path. The sufferings we experience today are results of wrongdoing in former lives; the benefit of our present merit lies in the future. Even our teacher, the Buddha, entered a brahmin village seeking alms, obtained nothing, and returned with an empty bowl. The Buddha also experienced illnesses, and when the Śākyas exhausted the results of their wrongdoing, he too had a headache. How much more might this happen to people like us, of little merit and modest attainment!' Hearing this, the householders' anger subsides. They supply the monks with the four kinds of requisites, bringing bodily ease so that they can meditate and attain the path. Thus this is skillful means, rather than the Buddha actually undergoing punishment for wrongdoing.

Ānanda Seeks Milk for the Buddha

Chinese Source

如《毘摩羅詰經》中說：佛在毘耶離國，是時佛語阿難：「我身中熱風氣發，當用牛乳。汝持我鉢乞牛乳來。」阿難持佛鉢，晨朝入毘耶離，至一居士門立。

English Translation

As the Vimalakīrti Sūtra relates: While the Buddha was in Vaiśālī, he said to Ānanda, 'Heat and wind have become disturbed in my body, and I need cow's milk. Take my bowl and seek some milk for me.' Carrying the Buddha's bowl, Ānanda entered Vaiśālī early in the morning and stood at a householder's door.

是時，毘摩羅詰在是中行，見阿難持鉢而立，問阿難：「汝何以晨朝持鉢立此？」阿難答言：「佛身小疾，當用牛乳，故我到此。」毘摩羅詰言：「止！止！阿難！勿謗如來！佛為世尊，已過一切諸不善法，當有何疾？勿使外道聞此麁語，彼當輕佛，便言：佛自疾不能救，安能救人？」

Vimalakīrti was walking there. Seeing Ānanda standing with the bowl, he asked, 'Why are you standing here with a bowl so early in the morning?' Ānanda replied, 'The Buddha has a minor illness and needs cow's milk. That is why I have come.' Vimalakīrti said, 'Stop, stop, Ānanda! Do not slander the Tathāgata! The Buddha is the World-Honored One and has gone beyond all unwholesome dharmas. What illness could he have? Do not let non-Buddhist practitioners hear such crude words. They would disparage the Buddha, saying, “If he cannot cure his own illness, how can he save others?”'

阿難言：「此非我意，面受佛勅當須牛乳。」毘摩羅詰言：「此雖佛勅，是為方便，以今五惡之世故，以是像度脫一切。若未來世，有諸病比丘當從白衣求諸湯藥。白衣言：『汝自疾不能救，安能救餘人？』諸比丘言：『我等大師猶尚有病，況我等身如艸芥能不病耶？』以是事故諸白衣等以諸湯藥供給比丘，使得安隱，坐禪行道。

Ānanda said, 'This is not my own idea. I personally received the Buddha's instruction that he needs milk.' Vimalakīrti replied, 'Although the Buddha instructed you, this is skillful means. In this age of the five evils, he takes on this appearance to liberate all beings. In future ages, sick monks will need to seek medicinal preparations from householders. The householders may say, “If you cannot cure your own illness, how can you save others?” The monks will reply, “Even our great teacher had illnesses. How could bodies like ours, as frail as grass, be free from illness?” For this reason, householders will provide monks with medicines, bringing them ease so that they can meditate and practice the path.'

有外道、仙人能以藥艸、呪術除他人病，何況如來一切智德，自身有病而不能除？汝且默然，持鉢取乳，勿令餘人異學得聞知也。」以是故知佛為方便，非實病也。諸罪因緣皆亦如是。以是故言「佛其德特尊，光明色像，威德巍巍」。

'Some non-Buddhist practitioners and seers can cure others' illnesses with medicinal herbs and spells. How much less could the Tathāgata, with the virtue of all-knowledge, be unable to cure an illness in his own body? For now, remain silent, take the bowl, and obtain the milk. Do not let people of other teachings hear of this.' From this we know that the Buddha's illness is skillful means, not actual illness. The circumstances of all these apparent karmic sufferings are likewise so. Therefore, it is said, 'The Buddha's virtue is supremely honored, and the splendor of his light, appearance, and presence is majestic.'

The Buddha Shows His Ordinary Body

Chinese Source

〖經〗爾時，世尊以常身示此三千大千世界一切眾生。是時，首陀會天、梵眾天、他化自在天、化自樂天、兜率陀天、夜磨天、三十三天、四天王天，及三千大千世界人與非人，以諸天華、天瓔珞、天澤香、天末香、天青蓮華、赤蓮華、白蓮華、紅蓮華、天樹葉香，持詣佛所。

English Translation

Sutra: At that time, the World-Honored One showed his ordinary body to all beings in this great trichiliocosm. The devas of the Pure Abodes, Brahmā's Retinue, Mastery over Others' Creations, Delight in Creation, Tuṣita, Yāma, the Thirty-three, and the Four Heavenly Kings, together with humans and nonhumans throughout the great trichiliocosm, came to the Buddha carrying heavenly flowers, heavenly garlands, heavenly fragrant ointments, heavenly powdered incense, heavenly blue, red, white, and crimson lotuses, and fragrant leaves of heavenly trees.

〖論〗問曰：佛何以故以常身示此三千大千世界中一切眾生？

Treatise: Question: Why does the Buddha show his ordinary body to all beings in this great trichiliocosm?

答曰：佛欲說摩訶般若波羅蜜，入三昧王三昧，從足下相輪光明上至肉髻光焰大明。譬如劫盡燒時，諸須彌山王隨次燃盡。是光明遍滿三千大千世界，乃至十方恒河沙等諸佛世界皆悉大明。眾生見者畢至阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。是佛欲說般若波羅蜜初神力。

Answer: Wishing to teach the Mahāprajñāpāramitā, the Buddha entered the samādhi called King of Samādhis. Light blazed brilliantly from the wheel marks beneath his feet up to the protuberance on his crown, like the kings of mountains, the Sumerus, burning up in succession in the fire at the end of a kalpa. This light filled the great trichiliocosm and brilliantly illuminated Buddha worlds in the ten directions as numerous as the sands of the Ganges. Beings who saw it would ultimately attain unsurpassed complete awakening. This was the Buddha's first manifestation of spiritual power in preparation for teaching Prajñāpāramitā.

第二、一切毛孔皆悉微笑。第三、放常光明，面各一丈。第四、舌相遍覆三千大千世界而笑。第五、入師子遊戲三昧，三千大千世界六反震動。第六、佛坐師子座，現最勝身光明色像，威德巍巍。

Second, all his pores smiled. Third, he emitted his abiding radiance, extending one zhang on every side. Fourth, his tongue covered the entire great trichiliocosm, and he smiled. Fifth, he entered the samādhi called Lion's Play, and the great trichiliocosm shook in six ways. Sixth, seated on the lion seat, he manifested his supreme body, with majestic light, appearance, and presence.

以此神力感動眾生，其有信者皆至阿耨多羅三藐三菩提；其中疑者，示常身，便得信解，而各說言：「今所見者是佛真身。」以佛力故，此三千大千世界中人見佛常身，遠近無礙。是時，三千大千世界眾生皆大歡喜言：「此真是佛身！佛初生時、初成佛時、初轉法輪時皆以此身。」如是思惟，此真是佛身。

These manifestations of spiritual power moved beings, and all who had faith would attain unsurpassed complete awakening. To those who still doubted, he showed his ordinary body. They then gained faith and understanding, each saying, 'What we now see is the Buddha's true body.' Through his power, people throughout the great trichiliocosm saw his ordinary body without obstruction, whether near or far. All beings there rejoiced greatly, saying, 'This truly is the Buddha's body! This is the body he had at birth, when he first attained Buddhahood, and when he first turned the Dharma wheel.' Reflecting in this way, they recognized it as his true body.

The Pure Abodes and the Brahmā Heavens

Chinese Source

問曰：何以故名為「淨居天」、「梵世天」？

English Translation

Question: Why are they called the Pure Abodes and the Brahmā-world heavens?

答曰：第四禪有八種：五種是阿那含住處，是名淨居；三種，凡夫、聖人共住。過是八處，有十住菩薩住處，亦名「淨居」，號大自在天王。

Answer: There are eight kinds of abode in the fourth dhyāna. Five are abodes of non-returners and are called the Pure Abodes. In the other three, ordinary people and noble ones dwell together. Beyond these eight is an abode of bodhisattvas of the tenth abiding, also called a Pure Abode, whose ruler is called the Great Lord of Freedom.

「梵世天」者，生處有三種：一者、梵眾天，諸小梵生處；二者、梵輔天，貴梵生處；三者、大梵天，是名中間禪生處。

As for the Brahmā-world heavens, there are three places of birth: first, Brahmā's Retinue, where the lesser Brahmās are born; second, Brahmā's Ministers, where the higher-ranking Brahmās are born; and third, Great Brahmā, called the place of birth associated with the intermediate dhyāna.

問曰：離欲是同，何以故有貴賤異處？

Question: Since they are alike in being free from sensual desire, why are their abodes distinguished by higher and lower rank?

答曰：初禪三種：下、中、上。若修下禪生梵眾，若修中禪生梵輔，若修上禪生大梵。慈行亦如是。如妙眼師念言：「我為眾人說法皆生梵天中，我今不應與弟子同處，當修上慈。」修上慈故生大梵天中。復次，第一清淨心故，生大梵天中。

Answer: The first dhyāna has three degrees: lower, intermediate, and higher. Those who cultivate the lower degree are born among Brahmā's Retinue; those who cultivate the intermediate degree among Brahmā's Ministers; and those who cultivate the higher degree in the Great Brahmā heaven. The practice of loving-kindness is also like this. For example, the teacher Wondrous Eyes thought, 'The people to whom I teach the Dharma are all born in the Brahmā heaven. I should not now share the same abode as my disciples; I should cultivate a higher loving-kindness.' By cultivating this higher loving-kindness, he was born in the Great Brahmā heaven. Furthermore, one is born there through a supremely pure mind.

Why the First and Last Dhyāna Heavens Are Named

Chinese Source

問曰：何以故於四禪中但說初、後，不說中間？

English Translation

Question: Among the four dhyānas, why mention only the first and last, leaving out those in between?

答曰：初門離欲難故，最後微妙難得故；中間易入故不說。復次，言「梵世」已攝色界；以第四禪第一妙故別說。復次，以人多識梵天、不識餘天，是故但說梵天。以淨居天常憐愍眾生、常勸請佛故。

Answer: At the first gateway, becoming free from sensual desire is difficult. The last is subtle and difficult to attain. Those in between are easier to enter and therefore are not mentioned. Furthermore, speaking of the Brahmā world already includes the form realm; the fourth dhyāna is singled out because it is supremely excellent. Furthermore, people generally know Brahmā but not the other devas, so Brahmā alone is mentioned. The Pure Abode devas, for their part, constantly have compassion for beings and repeatedly request the Buddha to teach.

復次，佛說法聲至梵天；佛得道時諸天展轉唱告乃至淨居天。以是故說初、後，不說中間。復次，梵天近欲界故應聞；淨居天是色界主是故應聞。譬如守門人識客，客至其主，主則識之。中間無事故不說。復次，二禪大喜、三禪大樂，喜、樂放逸，是故不說。

Furthermore, the sound of the Buddha's teaching reaches the Brahmā heaven. When he attained the path, devas passed the proclamation from one to another up to the Pure Abodes. Therefore, the first and last are mentioned rather than those in between. Furthermore, the Brahmā heaven is near the desire realm and should hear, while the Pure Abode devas preside over the form realm and should also hear. It is like a gatekeeper recognizing a visitor and, when the visitor reaches the master, the master recognizing the visitor. Since those in between have no particular role, they are not mentioned. Furthermore, in the second dhyāna there is great joy, and in the third great happiness. These can give rise to heedlessness, so those heavens are not mentioned.

The Names and Abodes of the Devas

Chinese Source

問曰：何以名「他化自在」？

English Translation

Question: Why is it called Mastery over Others' Creations?

答曰：此天奪他所化而自娛樂，故言「他化自在」。「化自樂」者，自化五塵而自娛樂故言「化自樂」。「兜率」名知足天。「夜摩」名善分天。第二名「三十三天」。最下天是「四天王諸天」。

Answer: These devas take what others create by transformation and enjoy it themselves. Therefore, their heaven is called Mastery over Others' Creations. Those of Delight in Creation create the five sense objects themselves and enjoy them; hence that name. Tuṣita means the Heaven of Contentment. Yāma means the Heaven of Well-Divided Time. The second heaven is called the Thirty-three. The lowest is the heaven of the Four Heavenly Kings.

須彌山高八萬四千由旬，上有三十三天城。須彌山邊有山，名由揵陀羅，高四萬二千由旬。此山有四頭，頭各有城，四天王各居一城。夜摩等諸天，七寶地在虛空中，有風持之令住。乃至淨居亦復如是。

Mount Sumeru is eighty-four thousand yojanas high, with the city of the Thirty-three at its summit. Beside it is Mount Yugandhara, forty-two thousand yojanas high. This mountain has four peaks, each with a city; each of the Four Heavenly Kings dwells in one city. The Yāma devas and those above them have grounds made of the seven precious substances, suspended in space and supported by wind. It is likewise so up to the Pure Abodes.

English Translation

These devas saw the purity of the Buddha's body and the clarity of his great radiance. They brought offerings, including flowers of land and water. Among land flowers, sumanā is foremost; among water flowers, the blue lotus is foremost. Whether growing on trees or vines, these celebrated flowers have many different colors and fragrances. Each deva brought heavenly flowers to the Buddha. Because these flowers have beautiful colors and abundant fragrance, and are soft, delicate, and smooth, they serve as offerings.

Heavenly fragrant ointment was spread on the ground where he was, and heavenly powdered incense was scattered over him. The heavenly lotuses were blue, red, crimson, and white. Why were none yellow? Yellow belongs to fire, and fire is unsuitable for water flowers. The heavenly jeweled lotuses had stems of beryl, centers of diamond, and petals of Jāmbūnada gold, yet were soft and fragrant. 答曰：佛以神足放大光明，地六種震動。諸天雨種種妙華，滿三千大千世界，以供養佛；是人、非人或取此華而以供養。復次，天竺國法，名諸好物皆名天物。是人華、非人華，雖非天上華，以其妙好故名為天華。是故言「人、非人持諸天華」，是則無咎。

Answer: The Buddha emitted great light through his supernormal power, and the earth shook in six ways. To make offerings to him, the devas showered many kinds of wondrous flowers throughout the great trichiliocosm. Humans and nonhumans could gather these flowers and offer them. Furthermore, in Indian usage, excellent things are called heavenly. Although the flowers of humans and nonhumans are not from heaven, they are called heavenly flowers because of their exceptional beauty. Therefore, there is no fault in saying that humans and nonhumans brought heavenly flowers.

Each Being Sees the Buddha Teaching Directly Before Them

〖經〗爾時，三千大千世界及十方眾生各各自念：佛獨為我說法，不為餘人。

Sutra: At that time, beings throughout the great trichiliocosm and the ten directions each thought, 'The Buddha is teaching the Dharma for me alone, not for anyone else.'

〖論〗問曰：佛以一身示三千大千世界及十方，今諸眾生何以各各見佛在前說法？

Treatise: Question: The Buddha displays a single body to the great trichiliocosm and the ten directions. Why does each being now see him teaching directly before them?

答曰：佛有二種神力：一者、一處坐說法，令諸眾生遠處皆見、遠處皆聞；二者、佛在一處說法，能令一一眾生各自見佛在前說法。譬如日出，影現眾水。

Furthermore, beings differ. Some gain pure faith on seeing the Buddha's body filling the great trichiliocosm. Others gain purity of mind, joyful faith, and gladness on seeing him teaching directly before them. Therefore, the Buddha now appears before each of them to teach the Dharma.

Assemblies in Different Worlds See One Another

Chinese Source

〖經〗爾時，世尊在師子座熙怡而笑，光從口出，遍照三千大千世界。以此光故，此間三千大千世界中眾生皆見東方恒河沙諸佛及僧，彼間恒河沙等世界中眾生亦見此間三千大千世界中釋迦牟尼佛及諸大眾。南西北方、四維、上下亦復如是。

Sutra: At that time, the World-Honored One smiled joyfully on the lion seat. Light issued from his mouth and illuminated the entire great trichiliocosm. Through this light, beings here saw Buddhas and their Saṅghas in worlds in the east as numerous as the sands of the Ganges. Beings in those worlds likewise saw Śākyamuni Buddha and the great assembly in this great trichiliocosm. The same occurred in the south, west, north, the intermediate directions, above, and below.

〖論〗問曰：佛上已多放光明，今以何故復放斯光？

Treatise: Question: The Buddha has already emitted light many times. Why does he emit this light again?

答曰：先放光明，各各有事，如先說。今以彼此眾會兩未相見故，以光明神力，令彼此世界一切大會兩得相見。

Answer: Each earlier emission of light had its own purpose, as explained above. The assemblies here and elsewhere have not yet seen one another. Now, through the spiritual power of this light, all the great assemblies in these worlds can see one another.

問曰：如弟子中天眼第一大阿羅漢長老阿泥盧豆，暫觀見小千世界，諦觀見二千世界；大辟支佛暫觀見二千世界，諦觀見三千大千世界。今一切人云何能見東方恒河沙等諸佛世界？

Question: The elder Aniruddha, the great arhat foremost among disciples in the heavenly eye, sees a small thousandfold world system at a glance and a medium twice-thousandfold system on close examination. A great pratyekabuddha sees a medium system at a glance and a great trichiliocosm on close examination. How can everyone now see Buddha worlds in the east as numerous as the sands of the Ganges?

答曰：是佛神力令彼得見，非眾生力也。設阿羅漢及辟支佛等亦以佛力故所見無限。譬如轉輪聖王飛行，一切營從及諸象馬眾畜皆亦隨去。今佛神力故，眾生雖在遠處亦得相見。

Answer: The Buddha's spiritual power enables them to see; it is not their own power. Even arhats and pratyekabuddhas can see without limit through the Buddha's power. It is like a wheel-turning sage king traveling through the air: all his attendants, elephants, horses, and other animals follow him. Now, through the Buddha's spiritual power, beings can see one another even across great distances.

又如般舟三昧力故，雖不得天眼而見十方佛，眼耳無礙。亦如劫盡燒時，一切眾生自然皆得禪定，得天眼、天耳。佛以神力故，令一切眾生皆得遠見亦復如是。

Likewise, through the power of the Pratyutpanna Samādhi, one can see the Buddhas of the ten directions without having attained the heavenly eye, with sight and hearing unobstructed. Also, when the fire at the end of a kalpa occurs, beings naturally gain meditative concentration, the heavenly eye, and the heavenly ear. It is likewise through his spiritual power that the Buddha enables all beings to see far away.

As for 'at that time, the World-Honored One smiled on the lion seat': smiling has been explained above. What remains unexplained will now be addressed. Question: Beings here see those distant worlds through the Buddha's spiritual power. Through whose power do beings there see this world?

答曰：是釋迦牟尼佛力，令彼得見此間三千大千世界，及見釋迦牟尼佛并一切眾會；南西北方、四維、上下亦復如是。

Answer: It is through Śākyamuni Buddha's power that they see this great trichiliocosm, Śākyamuni Buddha, and the entire assembly. The same applies in the south, west, north, the intermediate directions, above, and below.

Part Fifteen: Bodhisattvas Arrive from the Ten Directions

Chinese Source

〖經〗是時，東方過如恒河沙等諸佛世界，其世界最在邊，世界名多寶，佛號寶積，今現在為諸菩薩摩訶薩說般若波羅蜜。

English Translation

Sutra: At that time, beyond Buddha worlds in the east as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, at the farthest edge, was a world called Many Jewels. Its Buddha was named Jewel Accumulation, and he was then teaching Prajñāpāramitā to bodhisattva mahāsattvas.

〖論〗問曰：如佛所說「一切世界無量無邊」，云何言「其世界最在邊」？「最在邊」者，是墮有邊相；若世界有邊，眾生應盡。何以故？無量諸佛，一一佛度無量阿僧祇眾生令入無餘涅槃，更無新眾生，故者應盡！

Treatise: Question: Since the Buddha says that all worlds are immeasurable and boundless, how can the text say that this world is at the farthest edge? A farthest edge implies a boundary. If worlds have a boundary, beings should eventually be exhausted. Why? There are immeasurable Buddhas, each bringing immeasurable asaṃkhyeyas of beings into nirvāṇa without remainder. With no new beings coming into existence, the existing ones should eventually be exhausted!

答曰：佛經雖言世界無量，此方便說，非是實教。如實無神，方便故說言有神。此十四難，世界有邊、無邊俱為邪見。若無邊，佛不應有一切智。何以故？智慧普知，無物不盡，是名一切智。若世界無邊，是有所不盡。若有邊，如先說咎。此二俱邪見。何以故？依無邊以破有邊故。

Answer: Although Buddhist scriptures speak of immeasurable worlds, this is an expedient expression, not an ultimate formulation. It is like speaking provisionally of a self when in reality there is no self. Among the fourteen difficult questions, asserting either that the world has a boundary or that it has none is a wrong view. If it were boundless, it might seem that the Buddha could not possess all-knowledge. Why? All-knowledge means wisdom that comprehensively knows everything, leaving nothing outside its scope. If worlds were boundless, something would remain unexhausted by that knowledge. If they had a boundary, the fault would be as stated above. Both positions are wrong views. Why? Boundlessness is invoked to refute a fixed boundary.

是多寶世界非一切世界邊，是釋迦牟尼佛因緣眾生可應度者最在邊。譬如一國中最在邊，不言一閻浮提最在邊。若無邊，佛不應一切智者，如上佛義中答，佛智無量故應知，譬如函大故蓋亦大。

The world Many Jewels is not at the boundary of all worlds. It is at the farthest edge of the range of beings connected with Śākyamuni Buddha whom he can appropriately liberate. It is like speaking of the farthest edge of one country, not of the whole of Jambudvīpa. As for the objection that boundlessness would prevent the Buddha from having all-knowledge, this was answered above in the explanation of a Buddha: because his wisdom is immeasurable, it can know immeasurable worlds. It is like a large box having a correspondingly large lid.

Why the World and Its Buddha Bear Names of Jewels

Chinese Source

問曰：世界名多寶。寶有二種：財寶、法寶，何等寶多名為「多寶世界」？

English Translation

Question: The world is called Many Jewels. There are two kinds of jewels: material treasures and treasures of Dharma. Which kind is abundant there, giving it this name?

答曰：二種皆有。又多菩薩照法性等諸寶（言此寶大菩薩所有，以為寶冠；寶冠中皆見諸佛。又了達一切諸法之性），多故名為「多寶」。是中有佛名「寶積」。以無漏根、力、覺、道等法寶集故，名為「寶積」。

Answer: Both kinds are present. Furthermore, there are many bodhisattvas and many treasures such as illumination of Dharma nature. An inline gloss explains: these jewels belong to great bodhisattvas, who use them as jeweled crowns; within these crowns all Buddhas can be seen, and the nature of all dharmas is fully understood. Because these treasures are abundant, the world is called Many Jewels. Its Buddha is called Jewel Accumulation because he has accumulated Dharma jewels such as the undefiled faculties, powers, awakening factors, and path.

問曰：若爾者，一切佛皆應號寶積！何以獨稱彼佛為「寶積」？

Question: If so, all Buddhas should be called Jewel Accumulation. Why is that Buddha alone given this name?

答曰：雖一切諸佛皆有此寶，但彼佛即以此寶為名。如彌勒名為慈氏，諸佛雖皆有慈，但彌勒即以慈為名。

Answer: Although all Buddhas possess these jewels, that Buddha takes them as his name. Likewise, Maitreya is named the Benevolent One. Although all Buddhas possess loving-kindness, Maitreya takes it as his name.

復次，如寶華佛生時，一切身邊有種種華色光明故，名寶華太子。如燃燈佛生時，一切身邊如燈故，名燃燈太子，作佛亦名燃燈（丹注云：舊名定光佛也）。寶積佛亦如是，應當初生時亦多諸寶物生，或地生、或天雨，種種寶集故名為「寶積」。

Furthermore, when Buddha Jewel Flower was born, lights of many flower colors appeared all around his body, so he was named Prince Jewel Flower. When Dīpaṃkara Buddha was born, his whole body was surrounded by light like lamps, so he was named Prince Lamp-Lighter. After attaining Buddhahood, he was still called Dīpaṃkara. An inline annotation, introduced as a Dan annotation, says that his older name was Buddha Steady Light. Jewel Accumulation Buddha is similar: presumably at his birth, many treasures appeared, either emerging from the earth or raining from heaven. Because many treasures gathered, he was called Jewel Accumulation.

Completing the Objection and Beginning the Reply

Chinese Source

如是種種方便，一時頃能度十方無量眾生；度眾生已，還入本處住佛臍中。爾時，世尊從日出三昧起，問阿難言：「汝見此三昧神通力不？」阿難白佛：「唯然！已見。」重白佛言：「若佛住世一日之中，所度弟子可滿虛空，何況在世八十餘年！」以是故言一佛功德神力無量，現化十方，無異佛也。

English Translation

Through these many skillful means, in a single moment he can liberate immeasurable beings in the ten directions. Having liberated them, the manifestations return to their original place within the Buddha's navel. The World-Honored One then emerged from the Sunrise Samādhi and asked Ānanda, 'Did you see the supernormal power of this samādhi?' Ānanda replied, 'Yes, I saw it.' He further said, 'If the disciples liberated by the Buddha in a single day could fill space, how much more those liberated during more than eighty years in the world!' Thus, the objection concludes, one Buddha possesses immeasurable merit and spiritual power, manifesting transformations in the ten directions; there are no other Buddhas.

復次，如佛所言「女人不得作轉輪聖王，不得作天帝釋、魔天王、梵天王，不得作佛」；「轉輪聖王不得一處並治，十力世尊亦無一世二佛」。又佛說言「佛言不虛，世無二佛」；「一法難值，是佛世尊也」；

Furthermore, the Buddha has said, 'A woman cannot become a wheel-turning sage king, Śakra lord of the devas, King Māra, King Brahmā, or a Buddha'; and, 'Two wheel-turning sage kings cannot rule the same place together, and likewise there are not two World-Honored Ones with the ten powers in a single world.' He has also said, 'The Buddha's words are not false: there are not two Buddhas in one world'; and, 'One thing is difficult to encounter: a Buddha, a World-Honored One.'

「無量億劫時時一有。是九十一劫中，三劫有佛。賢劫之前九十一劫，初有佛名鞞婆尸（秦言種種見）。第三十一劫中有二佛：一名尸棄（秦言火），二名鞞恕婆附（秦言一切勝）。是賢劫中有四佛：一名迦羅鳩飡陀，二名迦那伽牟尼（秦言金仙人也），三名迦葉，四名釋迦牟尼。除此餘劫皆空無佛，甚可憐愍」。若有十方佛，何以故言「餘劫無佛，甚可憐愍」？

'Only from time to time, over immeasurable hundreds of millions of kalpas, does one appear. Among these ninety-one kalpas, Buddhas occur in three. Ninety-one kalpas before the present Auspicious Kalpa, there was first a Buddha named Vipaśyin, whose name means Various Kinds of Seeing. Thirty-one kalpas ago there were two Buddhas: Śikhin, whose name means Fire, and Viśvabhū, whose name means Surpassing All. In this Auspicious Kalpa there are four Buddhas: Krakucchanda, Kanakamuni, whose name means Golden Sage, Kāśyapa, and Śākyamuni. All the other kalpas are empty of Buddhas—a deeply pitiable situation.' If there are Buddhas in the ten directions, why say that the other kalpas have no Buddhas and are deeply pitiable?

答曰：雖釋迦文尼佛有無量神力，能變化作佛，在十方說法、放光明、度眾生，亦不能盡度一切眾生，墮有邊故，則無未來世佛故。然眾生不盡，以是故應更有餘佛。

Answer: Although Śākyamuni Buddha possesses immeasurable spiritual power and can manifest Buddhas throughout the ten directions to teach the Dharma, emit light, and liberate beings, he cannot exhaustively liberate all beings. Otherwise beings would be finite, and there would be no future Buddhas. But beings are not exhausted. Therefore, there must be other Buddhas as well.

The Four Reliances

Chinese Source

復次，汝言：「佛自說：女人不得作五事；二轉輪聖王不得同時出世，佛亦如是，同時一世亦無二佛。」

English Translation

Furthermore, you say, 'The Buddha himself stated that a woman cannot occupy the five positions, that two wheel-turning sage kings cannot appear in the world at the same time, and that likewise there are not two Buddhas simultaneously in one world.'

汝不解此義！佛經有二義：有易了義，有深遠難解義。如佛欲入涅槃時，語諸比丘：「從今日應依法不依人，應依義不依語，應依智不依識，應依了義經不依未了義。」

You do not understand the meaning! Buddhist scriptures have two kinds of meaning: some are readily understood, while others are profound and difficult to understand. When the Buddha was about to enter nirvāṇa, he told the monks, 'From now on, rely on the Dharma, not on a person; rely on the meaning, not on the wording; rely on wisdom, not on consciousness; rely on scriptures of definitive meaning, not on those whose meaning requires interpretation.'

「依法」者，法有十二部，應隨此法，不應隨人。

To rely on the Dharma means that the Dharma has twelve divisions. One should follow this Dharma rather than a person.

「依義」者，義中無諍好惡、罪福、虛實故，語以得義，義非語也。如人以指指月以示惑者，惑者視指而不視月，人語之言：「我以指指月令汝知之，汝何看指而不視月？」此亦如是，語為義指，語非義也。是以故不應依語。

To rely on meaning means that, in meaning, there is no disputing over good and bad, wrongdoing and merit, truth and falsehood. Words convey meaning, but meaning is not the words themselves. It is like someone pointing at the moon to show a confused person, who looks at the finger instead of the moon. The first person says, 'I am pointing at the moon so that you will recognize it. Why are you looking at my finger rather than the moon?' It is the same here: words are a finger pointing toward meaning, not meaning itself. Therefore, one should not rely on wording.

「依智」者，智能籌量、分別善惡；識常求樂、不入正要。是故言「不應依識」。

To rely on wisdom means that wisdom can assess and distinguish wholesome from unwholesome. Consciousness constantly seeks pleasure and does not enter into what is truly essential. Therefore, it is said that one should not rely on consciousness.

「依了義經」者：有一切智人佛第一，一切諸經書中佛法第一，一切眾生中比丘僧第一；布施得大富，持戒得生天——如是等是了義經。

As for relying on scriptures of definitive meaning: the Buddha, the all-knowing one, is foremost; among all scriptures, the Buddha Dharma is foremost; among all beings, the community of monks is foremost. Giving brings great wealth, and keeping the precepts brings birth in heaven. Statements such as these belong to scriptures of definitive meaning.

如說法師說法有五種利：一者大富，二者人所愛，三者端正，四者名聲，五者後得涅槃——是為未了義。云何「未了」？施得大富，是為了了可解；說法無財施而言「得富」，「得富」者，說法人種種讚施，破人慳心，亦自除慳，以是因緣得富，是故言「未了」。

By contrast, it is said that a Dharma teacher gains five benefits from teaching: great wealth, being loved by others, a beautiful appearance, a good reputation, and eventual attainment of nirvāṇa. This requires interpretation. Why? That giving brings great wealth is clear and readily understood. But teaching the Dharma involves no material gift, so why say it brings wealth? The explanation is that the teacher praises giving in various ways, breaks through others' stinginess, and removes stinginess in himself. Through these causes and conditions, he obtains wealth. Therefore, the statement is said to require interpretation.

One Buddha in One World Does Not Mean Only One Buddha Everywhere

Chinese Source

是《多持經》方便說，非實義。是經中佛雖言世無二佛俱出，不言一切十方世界；雖言世無二轉輪聖王，亦不言一切三千大千世界無，但言四天下世界中無二轉輪聖王，作福清淨故獨王一世，無諸怨敵；若有二王不名清淨。

English Translation

The statement in the Duochi Sūtra (《多持經》) is an expedient expression, not its ultimate meaning. Although the Buddha says there that two Buddhas do not appear together in one world, he is not speaking of all worlds in the ten directions. Likewise, saying that there are not two wheel-turning sage kings in one world does not mean there cannot be two anywhere throughout the great trichiliocosm. It means that there are not two within one world of four continents. Through the purity of his meritorious actions, such a king rules that world alone, without hostile rivals. If there were two rulers, this result would not be called pure.

雖佛無嫉妬心，然以行業世世清淨故，亦不一世界有二佛出。百億須彌山，百億日月，名為三千大千世界。如是十方恒河沙等三千大千世界，是名為一佛世界。是中更無餘佛，實一釋迦牟尼佛。是一佛世界中，常化作諸佛種種法門、種種身、種種因緣、種種方便以度眾生。以是故，《多持經》中一時一世界無二佛，不言十方無佛。

Although the Buddha has no jealousy, because his actions have been pure life after life, two Buddhas likewise do not appear within a single world. A billion Mount Sumerus and a billion suns and moons constitute a great trichiliocosm. Great trichiliocosms in the ten directions as numerous as the sands of the Ganges together constitute one Buddha world. Within it there is no other Buddha: Śākyamuni alone is the actual Buddha. In this Buddha world, he continually manifests Buddhas with various Dharma approaches, bodies, causes and conditions, and skillful means to liberate beings. Thus, the Duochi Sūtra says there are not two Buddhas at the same time in one world; it does not say that there are no Buddhas in the ten directions.

Why a Buddha Is Difficult to Encounter

Chinese Source

復次，如汝言：「佛言：『一事難值，是佛世尊。』」又言：「九十一劫，三劫有佛，餘劫皆空無佛，甚可憐愍。」佛為此重罪不種見佛善根人，說言：「佛世難值，如優曇波羅樹華，時時一有。」如是罪人輪轉三惡道，或在人天中，佛出世時，其人不見。

English Translation

Furthermore, you cite the Buddha's statement, 'One thing is difficult to encounter: a Buddha, a World-Honored One,' and the statement, 'Of ninety-one kalpas, three contain Buddhas, while the others are empty of Buddhas—a deeply pitiable situation.' The Buddha says to those burdened by grave wrongdoing who have not planted wholesome roots for seeing a Buddha, 'A Buddha's appearance is difficult to encounter, like the udumbara flower that appears only from time to time.' Such people wander through the three bad destinies, or dwell among humans and devas, yet when a Buddha appears, they do not see him.

如說：舍衛城中九億家，三億家眼見佛，三億家耳聞有佛而眼不見，三億家不聞不見。佛在舍衛國二十五年，而此眾生不聞不見，何況遠者！

It is related that Śrāvastī had nine koṭis of households: three koṭis saw the Buddha, three heard that there was a Buddha but did not see him, and three neither heard of nor saw him. The Buddha stayed in Śrāvastī for twenty-five years, yet these beings neither heard of nor saw him. How much more could this happen to those far away!

復次，佛與阿難入舍衛城乞食，是時有一貧老母立在道頭。阿難白佛：「此人可愍，佛應當度。」佛語阿難：「是人無因緣。」阿難言：「佛往近之，此人見佛相好光明，發歡喜心，為作因緣。」

Furthermore, when the Buddha and Ānanda entered Śrāvastī seeking alms, a poor elderly woman stood by the roadside. Ānanda said, 'This woman is pitiable. The Buddha should liberate her.' The Buddha replied, 'She lacks the necessary conditions.' Ānanda said, 'Please approach her. Seeing your beautiful marks and radiance, she will rejoice, and that will establish the conditions.'

佛往近之，迴身背佛；佛從四邊往，便四向背佛，仰面上向；佛從上來，低頭下向；佛從地出，兩手覆眼不肯視佛。佛語阿難：「復欲作何因緣？」有如是人無度因緣，不得見佛。以是故佛言：「佛難得值，如優曇波羅樹華。」譬如水雨雖多，處處易得，餓鬼常渴，不能得飲。

When the Buddha approached, she turned her back. He approached from each of the four sides, and each time she turned away, finally looking upward. When he came from above, she lowered her head. When he emerged from the earth, she covered her eyes with both hands and refused to look. The Buddha said to Ānanda, 'What further conditions would you have me establish?' Such people lack the conditions for liberation and cannot see the Buddha. Therefore, the Buddha says that encountering a Buddha is as rare as the udumbara flower. It is like hungry ghosts who remain thirsty and cannot drink, although rainwater is abundant and easily obtained everywhere.

汝言：「九十一劫，三劫有佛。」為一佛世界故，不為一切餘諸世界。「是處劫空無有佛出，甚可憐愍」者，亦是此間一佛世界，非為一切餘諸世界也。以是故知有十方佛。

Your statement that Buddhas appear in three of ninety-one kalpas concerns one Buddha world, not all other worlds. Likewise, saying that kalpas there are empty of Buddhas and deeply pitiable concerns this one Buddha world, not every other world. From this we know that there are Buddhas in the ten directions.

Where the Causes for Buddhahood Exist, Results Can Arise

Chinese Source

復次，聲聞法中有十方佛，汝自不解。如《雜阿含經》中說：「譬如大雨連注，渧渧無間，不可知數。諸世界亦如是，我見東方無量世界，有成、有住、有壞，其數甚多，不可分別；如是乃至十方。是十方世界中，無量眾生有三種身苦：老、病、死；三種心苦：婬、瞋、癡；三種後世苦：地獄、餓鬼、畜生。」

English Translation

Furthermore, the śrāvaka teaching itself contains Buddhas of the ten directions; you simply do not understand it. As the Saṃyukta Āgama says, 'It is like a continuous downpour, its uninterrupted drops beyond counting. Worlds are like this. I see immeasurable worlds in the east, some forming, some persisting, and some disintegrating, too numerous to distinguish. The same applies throughout the ten directions. In these worlds, immeasurable beings experience three bodily sufferings—aging, sickness, and death; three mental sufferings—lust, anger, and delusion; and three sufferings in future lives—the hell, hungry ghost, and animal destinies.'

一切世界皆有三種人：下、中、上。下人著現世樂；中人求後世樂；上人求道，有慈悲心，憐愍眾生。有因緣云何無果報？佛言：「若無老、病、死，佛不出世。」是人見老、病、死苦惱眾生，心中作願：「我當作佛，以度脫之，拔其心病，濟後世苦。」

Every world has three kinds of people: lower, intermediate, and higher. Those of the lower kind cling to present-life pleasure; those of the intermediate kind seek happiness in future lives; and those of the higher kind seek the path, possess loving-kindness and compassion, and care for beings. Where causes and conditions exist, how could there be no result? The Buddha says, 'If there were no aging, sickness, or death, Buddhas would not appear in the world.' Seeing beings afflicted by these sufferings, such people vow, 'I will become a Buddha to liberate them, remove the illnesses of their minds, and rescue them from suffering in future lives.'

如是十方世界皆有佛出因緣，何以故獨言「此間有佛，餘處無」耶？譬如有人言「有木無火，有濕地而無水」，是不可信。佛亦如是，眾生身有老、病、死苦，心有婬、瞋、癡病，佛為斷此三苦，令得三乘故出世。一切世界中皆有此苦，云何無佛？復次，盲人無量而言唯須一醫，此亦不然。以是故應更有十方佛。

Thus, all worlds in the ten directions have the causes and conditions for a Buddha's appearance. Why insist that there is a Buddha here but none elsewhere? It would be like saying there is wood but no fire, or wet ground but no water. Such a claim is not credible. The same applies to Buddhas. Beings suffer bodily from aging, sickness, and death, and mentally from lust, anger, and delusion. Buddhas appear to cut off these groups of suffering and enable beings to attain the three vehicles. Since this suffering exists in every world, how could those worlds have no Buddhas? Furthermore, it is unreasonable to say that immeasurable blind people need only one physician. Therefore, there must also be Buddhas in the ten directions.

Homage to Buddhas of the Three Times

Chinese Source

復次，《長阿含》中有經言：有鬼神王守北方，與眾多百千萬鬼神後夜到佛所，頭面禮佛足，一面住，放清淨光，普照祇桓，皆令大明。合掌讚佛說此二偈：

English Translation

Furthermore, a scripture in the Dīrgha Āgama relates that a spirit king guarding the north came to the Buddha in the last watch of the night with hundreds of thousands and myriads of spirits. He bowed with his head at the Buddha's feet, stood to one side, and emitted pure light that brilliantly illuminated all of Jetavana. Joining his palms, he praised the Buddha in these two verses:

大精進人我歸命！佛二足中尊最上，智慧眼人能知見，諸天不解此慧事。

I take refuge in the greatly diligent one! The Buddha is supreme among two-footed beings. Possessing the eye of wisdom, he knows and sees; the devas do not comprehend this wisdom.

過去未來今諸佛，一切我皆稽首禮！如是我今歸命佛，亦如恭敬三世尊。

To all Buddhas, past, future, and present, I bow my head in homage! As I now take refuge in this Buddha, so I honor the World-Honored Ones of the three times.

如是偈中有十方佛。鬼神王稽首三世佛，然後別歸命釋迦牟尼佛。若無十方現在佛，當應但歸命釋迦文尼佛，不應言過去、未來、現在諸佛。是故知有十方佛。

These verses include Buddhas of the ten directions. The spirit king first bows to the Buddhas of the three times, then separately takes refuge in Śākyamuni Buddha. If there were no present Buddhas in the ten directions, he should take refuge only in Śākyamuni, rather than speaking of past, future, and present Buddhas. From this we know that there are Buddhas in the ten directions.

復次，過去世有無量佛，未來世亦有無量佛，以是故現在亦應有無量佛。

Furthermore, there were immeasurable Buddhas in the past and will be immeasurable Buddhas in the future. Therefore, there should also be immeasurable Buddhas in the present.

Urgency, Faith, and the Merit of Recollection

Chinese Source

復次，若佛於聲聞法中言有十方無數無量佛，眾生當言「佛易可遇」，不勤求脫；「若不值此佛，當遇彼佛」，如是懈怠，不勤求度。譬如鹿未被箭時不知怖畏，既被箭已，踔圍而出。人亦如是，有老、病、死苦，聞唯有一佛，甚難可遇，心便怖畏，勤行精進，疾得度苦。以是故佛於聲聞法中不言有十方佛，亦不言無。

English Translation

Furthermore, if the Buddha stated in the śrāvaka teaching that there are countless, immeasurable Buddhas in the ten directions, beings would think, 'Buddhas are easy to encounter,' and would not diligently seek liberation. Thinking, 'If I do not meet this Buddha, I will meet another,' they would become lazy and fail to strive for release. It is like a deer that knows no fear before being struck by an arrow, but afterward leaps out of the encirclement. People are also like this. Afflicted by aging, sickness, and death, they hear that there is only one Buddha and that he is extremely difficult to encounter. They become alarmed, practice diligently, and swiftly escape suffering. Therefore, in the śrāvaka teaching, the Buddha neither states that there are Buddhas in the ten directions nor that there are none.

若有十方佛，汝言無，得無限罪；若無十方佛，而我言有，生無量佛想，得恭敬福。所以者何？善心因緣福德力大故。譬如慈心三昧力觀一切眾生皆見受樂，雖無實益，以慈觀故，是人得無量福。十方佛想亦復如是。

If Buddhas exist in the ten directions and you deny them, you incur boundless wrongdoing. If they did not exist but I affirmed them, forming the thought of immeasurable Buddhas, I would gain the merit of reverence. Why? Because wholesome intentions have great power to produce merit. It is like someone who, through the samādhi of loving-kindness, contemplates all beings as enjoying happiness. Although no actual benefit has reached them, that person gains immeasurable merit through this contemplation. Recollecting Buddhas in the ten directions is likewise so.

若實有十方佛而言無，得破十方佛無量重罪。何以故？破實事故。肉眼人雖俱不知，但心信言有，其福無量；若實有而意謂無，其罪甚重。人自用心尚應信有，何況佛自說摩訶衍中言實有十方佛而不信耶？

If Buddhas truly exist in the ten directions and one denies them, one incurs the immeasurable grave wrongdoing of repudiating those Buddhas. Why? Because one denies what is real. Although people with only physical eyes do not know either way, faith that Buddhas exist brings immeasurable merit. If Buddhas do exist and one assumes they do not, the wrongdoing is very grave. Even on the basis of one's own reflection, one should have faith in their existence. How much more should one believe when the Buddha himself states in the Mahāyāna that there truly are Buddhas in the ten directions!

Why Beings Do Not See the Buddhas

Chinese Source

問曰：若有十方無量諸佛及諸菩薩，今此眾生多墮三惡道中，何以不來？

English Translation

Question: If there are immeasurable Buddhas and bodhisattvas in the ten directions, why do they not come here, where so many beings fall into the three bad destinies?

答曰：眾生罪重故，諸佛菩薩雖來不見。又法身佛常放光明、常說法，而以罪故不見、不聞。譬如日出，盲者不見；雷霆振地，聾者不聞。如是法身常放光明、常說法，眾生有無量劫罪垢厚重不見、不聞。如明鏡淨水，照面則見；垢翳不淨，則無所見。如是眾生心清淨則見佛；若心不淨則不見佛。今雖實有十方佛及諸菩薩來度眾生，而不得見。

Answer: Because beings are burdened by grave wrongdoing, they do not see the Buddhas and bodhisattvas even when they come. Moreover, the Dharma-body Buddha constantly radiates light and teaches the Dharma, yet beings neither see nor hear him because of their wrongdoing. When the sun rises, the blind do not see it; when thunder shakes the earth, the deaf do not hear it. Likewise, the Dharma body constantly radiates light and teaches, but the heavy accumulated stains of wrongdoing over immeasurable kalpas prevent beings from seeing and hearing. A clean mirror or clear water reflects a face; when obscured by dirt, it shows nothing. In the same way, beings see the Buddha when their minds are pure, but not when their minds are impure. Although Buddhas and bodhisattvas of the ten directions truly come to liberate beings, these beings cannot see them.

Even Those Nearby May Not See the Buddha

Chinese Source

復次，如釋迦牟尼佛在閻浮提中生，在迦毘羅國，多遊行東天竺六大城。有時飛到南天竺億耳居士舍受供養。有時暫來北天竺月氏國降阿波羅龍王。又至月氏國西，降女羅剎；佛在彼石窟中一宿，于今佛影猶在，有人就內看之則不見，出孔遙觀光相如佛。

English Translation

Furthermore, Śākyamuni Buddha was born in Kapilavastu, in Jambudvīpa, and traveled mostly among the six great cities of eastern India. Sometimes he flew to the home of the householder Yier (億耳) in southern India to receive offerings. Sometimes he briefly visited the country of the Yuezhi in northern India to subdue the dragon king Apalāla. He also went west of the Yuezhi country to subdue a female rākṣasa. He spent one night in a rock cave there, and his shadow remains there to this day. People entering the cave to look cannot see it, but when they come out through the opening and look from a distance, its radiant appearance resembles the Buddha.

有時暫飛至罽賓隸跋陀仙人山上，住虛空中，降此仙人；仙人言：「我樂住此中，願佛與我佛髮、佛爪，起塔供養。」塔于今現存（此山下有離越寺；離越，應云隸跋陀）。人與佛同國而生猶不遍見，何況異處！以是故，不可以不見十方佛故而言無也。

Sometimes he flew briefly to the mountain of the sage Libatuo (隸跋陀) in Jibin (罽賓), remained in the sky, and subdued the sage. The sage said, 'I enjoy dwelling here. Please give me some of your hair and nail clippings, so that I may build a stūpa and make offerings.' The stūpa still exists today. (At the foot of this mountain is Liyue monastery; Liyue [離越] should be written Libatuo [隸跋陀].) Even people born in the same country as the Buddha did not all see him. How much more could this apply elsewhere! Therefore, one cannot deny Buddhas of the ten directions simply because one does not see them.

復次，彌勒菩薩有大慈悲而在天宮不來此間，可以不來故便謂無彌勒耶？彌勒近而不來不以為怪，十方佛遠，何足怪也？

Furthermore, Maitreya Bodhisattva possesses great loving-kindness and compassion, yet remains in a heavenly palace and does not come here. Can one conclude that Maitreya does not exist because he does not come? You find nothing strange in Maitreya being nearby yet not coming. Why be surprised that Buddhas of the ten directions, who are far away, do not come?

復次，十方佛不來者，以眾生罪垢深重不種見佛功德，是故不來。

Furthermore, Buddhas of the ten directions do not come because beings are deeply stained by wrongdoing and have not cultivated the merit needed to see a Buddha.

Wisdom Knows the Time and Means for Liberation

Chinese Source

復次，佛知一切眾生善根熟、結使薄然後來度。如說：

English Translation

Furthermore, Buddhas come to liberate beings when they know that their wholesome roots have ripened and their fetters have weakened. As it is said:

諸佛先觀知有人，一切方便不可度，或有難度或易化，或復有遲或有疾，

Buddhas first observe and know that some cannot be liberated by any skillful means. Some are difficult to liberate, others easy to guide; some are slow, others swift.

或以光明或神足，種種因緣度眾生：有欲作逆佛愍除，或欲作逆佛不遮；

With light or supernatural powers, through various causes and conditions, they liberate beings. When some are about to commit a heinous offense, a Buddha compassionately prevents it; when others are about to do so, he does not intervene.

剛強難化用麁言，心柔易度用軟言；雖有慈悲平等心，知時智慧用方便。

For the stubborn and difficult to guide, he uses stern words; for the gentle and easy to liberate, gentle words. Though his loving-kindness and compassion are equal toward all, he applies skillful means with wisdom that knows the proper time.

以是故，十方佛雖不來，不應言無。復次，佛智慧力、方便、神通，舍利弗等大阿羅漢、大菩薩彌勒等尚不能知，何況凡人！

Therefore, even if Buddhas of the ten directions do not come, one should not say that they do not exist. Furthermore, even great arhats such as Śāriputra and great bodhisattvas such as Maitreya cannot fathom the power of a Buddha's wisdom, his skillful means, and his supernatural abilities. How much less can ordinary people!
