Fascicle 14 · 大智度論卷第十四

Perfecting Ethical Discipline

Fascicle 14 continues the explanation of the perfection of ethical discipline.

Translator: Lucien Hsieh (謝呂賢) Status: translation in progress Source base: Taishō T25, no. 1509 Morality and the Buddha path

Translation Method

Each Chinese paragraph appears beside its English counterpart.

Previous: Fascicle 13

The Perfection of Ethical Discipline — Continued

Chinese Source

問曰：已知尸羅相，云何為尸羅波羅蜜？

English Translation

Question: We now understand the characteristics of ethical discipline. What is the perfection of ethical discipline?

答曰：有人言：菩薩持戒，寧自失身，不毀小戒，是為尸羅波羅蜜。如上《蘇陀蘇摩王經》中說，不惜身命以全禁戒。

Answer: Some say that a bodhisattva would rather lose their own life than violate even a minor precept, and that this is the perfection of ethical discipline. As stated earlier in the Sūtra of King Sutasoma, one does not cling to body or life, in order to preserve the precepts completely.

The Bodhisattva as a Venomous Dragon

Chinese Source

如菩薩本身，曾作大力毒龍。若眾生在前，身力弱者，眼視便死；身力強者，氣往而死。是龍受一日戒，出家求靜，入林樹間思惟；坐久，疲懈而睡。龍法，睡時形狀如蛇，身有文章，七寶雜色。

English Translation

For example, in a former life the bodhisattva was a powerful venomous dragon. When living beings stood before him, the weak died at his glance, and the strong died when his breath reached them. This dragon undertook the one-day precepts, left his dwelling in search of quiet, and went into a forest to contemplate. After sitting for a long time, he became tired and fell asleep. When dragons sleep, they take the form of snakes; his body bore patterns in the varied colors of the seven treasures.

獵者見之驚喜，言曰：「以此希有難得之皮，獻上國王以為服飾，不亦宜乎？」便以杖按其頭，以刀剝其皮。

A hunter saw him and exclaimed with delight, “Would it not be fitting to present this rare and precious skin to the king for his clothing and ornaments?” He pressed down the dragon's head with a staff and began to skin him with a knife.

龍自念言：「我力如意，傾覆此國，其如反掌；此人小物，豈能困我？我今以持戒故，不計此身，當從佛語！」

The dragon thought, “My power is at my command. Overturning this kingdom would be as easy as turning my hand. How could this insignificant person trouble me? But since I now uphold the precepts, I shall disregard this body and follow the Buddha's teaching!”

於是自忍，眠目不視，閉氣不息；憐愍此人，為持戒故，一心受剝，不生悔意。

He therefore endured it, closing his eyes so as not to look and holding his breath so as not to breathe. Out of compassion for this person and to uphold the precepts, he single-mindedly endured being skinned, without regret.

既以失皮，赤肉在地，時日大熱，宛轉土中；欲趣大水，見諸小蟲來食其身，為持戒故，不復敢動。

Once his skin was gone, his raw flesh lay on the ground. The sun was intensely hot, and he writhed in the earth. Wishing to reach deep water, he saw small insects coming to eat his body. To uphold the precepts, he no longer dared to move.

自思惟言：「今我此身以施諸蟲，為佛道故，今以肉施，以充其身；後成佛時，當以法施，以益其心。」

He reflected, “I now give this body to these insects. For the sake of Buddhahood, I give my flesh now to nourish their bodies. Later, when I become a Buddha, I shall give the Dharma to benefit their minds.”

如是誓已，身乾命絕，即生第二忉利天上。爾時毒龍，釋迦文佛是；是時獵者，提婆達等六師是也；諸小蟲輩，釋迦文佛初轉法輪，八萬諸天得道者是。

Having made this vow, his body dried out and his life ended. He was immediately born in the second heaven, the Heaven of the Thirty-Three. The venomous dragon then was Śākyamuni Buddha; the hunter was among the six teachers, including Devadatta; and the small insects were the eighty thousand devas who attained the path when Śākyamuni Buddha first turned the Dharma wheel.

菩薩護戒，不惜身命，決定不悔，其事如是，是名尸羅波羅蜜。

Such is the way a bodhisattva safeguards the precepts, without clinging to body or life, resolute and without regret. This is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

Upholding Precepts to Liberate Living Beings

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩持戒，為佛道故，作大要誓，必度眾生！不求今世、後世之樂，不為名聞虛譽法故，亦不自為早求涅槃，但為眾生沒在長流，恩愛所欺，愚惑所誤，我當度之令到彼岸。

English Translation

Furthermore, a bodhisattva upholds precepts for the sake of Buddhahood and makes a great, binding vow to liberate living beings: “I do not seek the pleasures of this life or the next, nor do I act for fame or empty praise. Nor do I seek an early nirvāṇa for myself alone. Rather, living beings are submerged in the long current, deceived by attachment and misled by ignorance and delusion. I shall liberate them and bring them to the other shore.”

一心持戒，為生善處，生善處故見善人，見善人故生智慧，生智慧故得行六波羅蜜，得行六波羅蜜故得佛道。如是持戒名為尸羅波羅蜜。

One wholeheartedly upholds precepts in order to be born in a good place. Through birth in a good place, one meets good people; through meeting good people, wisdom arises; through wisdom, one can practice the six perfections; and through practicing the six perfections, one attains Buddhahood. Upholding precepts in this way is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

Ethical Discipline and the Six Perfections

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩持戒，心樂善清淨，不為畏惡道，亦不為生天，但求善淨；以戒熏心，令心樂善，是為尸羅波羅蜜。

English Translation

Furthermore, a bodhisattva upholds precepts with a mind that delights in goodness and purity, neither from fear of unfortunate destinies nor for birth in heaven, but simply in pursuit of wholesome purity. Through the influence of precepts, the mind comes to delight in goodness. This is the perfection of ethical discipline.

復次，菩薩以大悲心持戒，得至佛道，是名尸羅波羅蜜。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva upholds precepts with great compassion and thereby reaches Buddhahood. This is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

復次，菩薩持戒，能生六波羅蜜，是則名為尸羅波羅蜜。

Furthermore, when a bodhisattva's upholding of precepts gives rise to the six perfections, this is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

云何持戒能生戒？因五戒得沙彌戒，因沙彌戒得律儀戒，因律儀戒得禪定戒，因禪定戒得無漏戒，是為戒生戒。

How does upholding precepts give rise to further precepts? Through the five precepts one obtains the novice precepts; through the novice precepts, the precepts of restraint; through the precepts of restraint, the precepts associated with meditative concentration; and through these, the precepts free of outflows. This is how precepts give rise to precepts.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Giving

Chinese Source

云何持戒能生於檀？檀有三種：一者、財施，二者、法施，三者、無畏施。持戒自撿，不侵一切眾生財物，是名財施。

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to giving? Giving has three forms: giving material goods, giving the Dharma, and giving fearlessness. By upholding precepts and restraining oneself, one does not encroach upon any living being's property. This is called giving material goods.

眾生見者，慕其所行，又為說法，令其開悟。又自思惟：我當堅持淨戒，與一切眾生作供養福田，令諸眾生得無量福。如是種種，名為法施。一切眾生皆畏於死，持戒不害，是則無畏施。

Living beings who see such a person admire their conduct. One also teaches them the Dharma so that they may awaken to understanding. Furthermore, one reflects, “I shall firmly uphold pure precepts and become a field of merit to which all living beings may make offerings, enabling them to gain immeasurable blessings.” These and similar practices are called giving the Dharma. All living beings fear death; upholding precepts and doing no harm is therefore giving fearlessness.

復次，菩薩自念：「我當持戒，以此戒報，為諸眾生作轉輪聖王、或作閻浮提王、若作天王，令諸眾生滿足於財，無所乏短；然後坐佛樹下，降伏魔王，破諸魔軍，成無上道，為諸眾生說清淨法，令無量眾生度老、病、死海。」是為持戒因緣生檀波羅蜜。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “I shall uphold precepts and, through their reward, become a wheel-turning sage king, a king of Jambudvīpa, or a king of devas for the sake of living beings, satisfying their material needs so that they lack nothing. Then I shall sit beneath the tree of awakening, subdue Māra and defeat his armies, accomplish unsurpassed awakening, and teach the pure Dharma to living beings, enabling immeasurable beings to cross the ocean of aging, illness, and death.” This is how the conditions of upholding precepts give rise to the perfection of giving.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Patience

Chinese Source

云何持戒生忍辱？持戒之人心自念言：「我今持戒為持心故。若持戒無忍，當墮地獄；雖不破戒，以無忍故，不免惡道；何可縱忿不自制心？但以心故入三惡趣。是故應當好自勉強，懃修忍辱。」

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to patience? One who upholds precepts reflects: “I now uphold precepts in order to guard the mind. If I uphold them without patience, I shall fall into hell. Even without breaking precepts, I cannot escape unfortunate destinies if I lack patience. How could I indulge anger and fail to restrain my mind? It is through the mind alone that one enters the three unfortunate destinies. Therefore, I should exert myself well and diligently cultivate patience.”

復次，行者欲令戒德堅強，當修忍辱。所以者何？忍為大力，能牢固戒，令不動搖。復自思惟：「我今出家，形與俗別，豈可縱心如世人法？宜自勉勵，以忍調心。以身、口忍，心亦得忍；若心不忍，身、口亦爾。」是故行者當令身、口、心忍，絕諸忿恨。

Furthermore, a practitioner who wishes to make the virtue of precepts firm should cultivate patience. Why? Patience is a great strength that stabilizes precepts and keeps them from wavering. One further reflects: “I have now gone forth, and my appearance differs from that of laypeople. How could I indulge my mind in worldly ways? I should exert myself and tame the mind through patience. Through patience in body and speech, the mind also becomes patient. If the mind lacks patience, body and speech will likewise lack it.” Thus the practitioner should maintain patience in body, speech, and mind, cutting off anger and resentment.

復次，是戒略說則有八萬，廣說則無量，我當云何能具持此無量戒法？唯當忍辱，眾戒自得。

Furthermore, in brief there are eighty thousand precepts, and in an extensive account they are immeasurable. How can I uphold all these immeasurable precepts completely? I need only practice patience, and the many precepts will naturally be fulfilled.

譬如有人得罪於王，王以罪人載之刀車，六邊利刃，間不容間，奔逸馳走，行不擇路；若能持身，不為刀傷，是則殺而不死。持戒之人，亦復如是，戒為利刀，忍為持身，若忍心不固，戒亦傷人。

It is like someone who has offended a king. The king places the offender in a chariot lined on all six sides with sharp blades, leaving no room between them, and sends it hurtling along without choosing its route. If the person can hold the body steady and avoid the blades, they survive this attempt to kill them. One who upholds precepts is similar: the precepts are the sharp blades, and patience is what holds the body steady. If the patient mind is not firm, precepts too can injure a person.

又復譬如老人夜行，無杖則蹶；忍為戒杖，扶人至道，福樂因緣，不能動搖。如是種種，名為持戒生羼提波羅蜜。

It is also like an old person walking at night: without a staff, they stumble. Patience is the staff of ethical discipline, supporting one on the way to the path and providing unshakable conditions for merit and happiness. These and similar examples explain how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of patience.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Diligence

Chinese Source

云何持戒而生精進？持戒之人除去放逸，自力懃修，習無上法，捨世間樂，入於善道，志求涅槃，以度一切，大心不懈，以求佛為本，是為持戒能生精進。

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to diligence? One who upholds precepts removes heedlessness, diligently practices through personal effort, trains in the unsurpassed Dharma, relinquishes worldly pleasures, and enters the wholesome path. Aspiring to nirvāṇa in order to liberate all, one sustains a great and tireless resolve, taking the quest for Buddhahood as fundamental. This is how upholding precepts gives rise to diligence.

復次，持戒之人，疲厭世苦老、病、死患，心生精進，必求自脫，亦以度人。

Furthermore, one who upholds precepts grows weary of worldly suffering and the troubles of aging, illness, and death. Diligence arises in the mind, and one resolutely seeks one's own liberation while also liberating others.

譬如野干在林樹間，依隨師子及諸虎豹，求其殘肉以自存活。有時空乏，夜半踰城，深入人舍，求肉不得，屏處睡息，不覺夜竟；惶怖無計，走則慮不自免，住則懼畏死痛；便自定心，詐死在地。

For example, a jackal lived among forest trees, following lions, tigers, and leopards to sustain itself on their leftover meat. Once, finding nothing, it climbed over a city wall at midnight and went deep into a house. Unable to find meat, it slept in a secluded spot, unaware that the night had ended. Terrified and without a plan, it feared that running would not save it, but that staying would bring the agony of death. It therefore steadied its mind and lay on the ground pretending to be dead.

眾人來見，有一人言：「我須野干耳。」即便截取。野干自念：「截耳雖痛，但令身在。」次有一人言：「我須野干尾。」便復截去。野干復念：「截尾雖痛猶是小事。」次有一人言：「我須野干牙。」野干心念：「取者轉多，儻取我頭，則無活路。」即從地起奮其智力，絕踊間關，徑得自濟。

People came and saw it. One said, “I need a jackal's ears,” and cut them off. The jackal thought, “Although losing my ears is painful, at least my body remains.” Another said, “I need a jackal's tail,” and cut that off too. The jackal thought, “Although losing my tail is painful, it is still a small matter.” Then someone said, “I need a jackal's teeth.” The jackal thought, “More and more people are taking parts of me. If someone takes my head, there will be no way to survive.” It immediately sprang up, exerted its intelligence and strength, leaped through obstacles, and escaped to safety.

行者之心，求脫苦難，亦復如是：若老至時，猶故自寬，不能慇懃決斷精進；病亦如是，以有差期，未能決計；死欲至時，自知無冀，便能自勉，果敢慇懃，大修精進，從死地中畢至涅槃。

The practitioner's mind is similar in seeking escape from suffering. When aging arrives, one still indulges oneself and cannot earnestly resolve to practice diligently. Illness is the same: because recovery is possible, one has not yet made a firm decision. But when death approaches and one knows there is no hope, one can exert oneself, become resolute and earnest, and cultivate great diligence, proceeding from the realm of death all the way to nirvāṇa.

復次，持戒之法，譬如人射，先得平地，地平然後心安，心安然後挽滿，挽滿然後陷深。戒為平地，定意為弓，挽滿為精進，箭為智慧，賊是無明。若能如是展力精進，必至大道，以度眾生。

Furthermore, upholding precepts is like archery. First one finds level ground. On level ground the mind becomes steady; with a steady mind one draws the bow fully; with the bow fully drawn, the arrow penetrates deeply. Precepts are the level ground, mental concentration is the bow, drawing it fully is diligence, the arrow is wisdom, and the enemy is ignorance. If one exerts oneself diligently in this way, one will surely reach the great path and liberate living beings.

復次，持戒之人，能以精進自制五情，不受五欲；若心已去，能攝令還；是為持戒能護諸根。護諸根則生禪定，生禪定則生智慧，生智慧得至佛道。是為持戒生毘梨耶波羅蜜。

Furthermore, one who upholds precepts can diligently restrain the five sense faculties and refuse the five sensual pleasures. If the mind has wandered, one can gather it back. This is how upholding precepts safeguards the faculties. Safeguarding the faculties gives rise to meditative concentration; meditative concentration gives rise to wisdom; and wisdom leads to Buddhahood. This is how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of diligence.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Meditation

Chinese Source

云何持戒生禪？人有三業作諸善，若身、口業善，意業自然入善；譬如曲草生於麻中，不扶自直。

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to meditation? People perform wholesome deeds through the three kinds of karma. If bodily and verbal karma are wholesome, mental karma naturally turns toward goodness. It is like crooked grass growing among hemp: without being supported, it straightens by itself.

持戒之力，能羸諸結使。云何能羸？若不持戒，瞋恚事來，殺心即生；若欲事至，婬心即成。若持戒者，雖有微瞋，不生殺心；雖有婬念，婬事不成，是為持戒能令諸結使羸。

The power of upholding precepts weakens the fetters. How does it weaken them? If one does not uphold precepts, when something provokes anger, the intention to kill immediately arises; when an occasion for desire appears, sexual intent takes shape. If one upholds precepts, even when slight anger arises, it does not become an intention to kill; even when sexual thoughts arise, the sexual act is not carried out. This is how upholding precepts weakens the fetters.

諸結使羸，禪定易得。譬如老病失力，死事易得；結使羸故，禪定易得。

When the fetters are weak, meditative concentration is easily attained. Just as death comes readily to someone weakened by aging and illness, meditative concentration comes readily when the fetters are weak.

復次，人心未息，常求逸樂；行者持戒，棄捨世福，心不放逸，是故易得禪定。

Furthermore, an unquiet mind constantly seeks pleasure and ease. A practitioner who upholds precepts relinquishes worldly blessings and keeps the mind from heedlessness. Thus meditative concentration is easily attained.

復次，持戒之人，得生人中，次生六欲天上，次至色界，若破色相生無色界；持戒清淨，斷諸結使，得阿羅漢道；大心持戒，愍念眾生，是為菩薩。

Furthermore, one who upholds precepts may be born among humans, then in the six heavens of the desire realm, and then reach the form realm. Transcending the perception of form, one is born in the formless realm. By upholding precepts purely and cutting off the fetters, one attains arhatship. Upholding precepts with a great aspiration and compassion for living beings, one is a bodhisattva.

復次，戒為撿麁，禪為攝細。復次，戒攝身、口，禪止亂心；如人上屋，非梯不昇，不得戒梯，禪亦不立。

Furthermore, precepts restrain what is coarse, while meditation gathers in what is subtle. Precepts restrain body and speech, while meditation stills the distracted mind. Just as someone cannot climb onto a roof without a ladder, meditation cannot be established without the ladder of precepts.

復次，破戒之人，結使風強，散亂其心；其心散亂，則禪不可得。持戒之人，煩惱風軟，心不大散，禪定易得。如是等種種因緣，是為持戒生禪波羅蜜。

Furthermore, in one who breaks precepts, the winds of the fetters are strong and scatter the mind. With the mind scattered, meditation cannot be attained. In one who upholds precepts, the winds of affliction are gentle, the mind is not greatly scattered, and meditative concentration is easily attained. These and various other conditions explain how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of meditation.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Wisdom

Chinese Source

云何持戒能生智慧？持戒之人，觀此戒相從何而有，知從眾罪而生；若無眾罪，則亦無戒。戒相如是，從因緣有，何故生著？譬如蓮華出自污泥，色雖鮮好，出處不淨；以是悟心，不令生著，是為持戒生般若波羅蜜。

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to wisdom? One who upholds precepts examines where their characteristics come from and understands that they arise in dependence on offenses. If there were no offenses, there would be no precepts. Since the characteristics of precepts arise from causes and conditions in this way, why become attached to them? It is like a lotus emerging from dirty mud: although its color is fresh and beautiful, its place of origin is impure. Understanding this, one does not allow attachment to arise. This is how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of wisdom.

復次，持戒之人，心自思惟：若我以持戒貴而可取，破戒賤而可捨者，若有此心不應般若。以智慧籌量，心不著戒，無取、無捨，是為持戒生般若波羅蜜。

Furthermore, one who upholds precepts reflects: “If I regard upholding precepts as noble and worth grasping, and breaking precepts as base and worth rejecting, such a mind does not accord with wisdom.” Assessing this with wisdom, the mind does not attach to precepts; there is neither grasping nor rejecting. This is how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of wisdom.

復次，不持戒人，雖有利智，以營世務，種種欲求生業之事，慧根漸鈍；譬如利刀以割泥土，遂成鈍器。若出家持戒，不營世業，常觀諸法實相無相；先雖鈍根，以漸轉利。如是等種種因緣，名為持戒生般若波羅蜜。

Furthermore, even someone with sharp intelligence who does not uphold precepts gradually dulls the faculty of wisdom by engaging in worldly affairs and the many pursuits of desire and livelihood. It is like using a sharp knife to cut mud until it becomes blunt. If one goes forth, upholds precepts, does not engage in worldly occupations, and constantly contemplates the true character of phenomena as without characteristics, one's faculties gradually become sharp even if they were initially dull. These and various other conditions explain how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of wisdom.

如是名為尸羅波羅蜜生六波羅蜜。

This is what is called the perfection of ethical discipline giving rise to the six perfections.

復次，菩薩持戒不以畏故，亦非愚癡，非疑、非惑，亦不自為涅槃故持戒；但為一切眾生故，為得佛道故，為得一切佛法故。如是相名為尸羅波羅蜜。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva upholds precepts neither out of fear nor through ignorance, doubt, or confusion, nor for personal nirvāṇa. Rather, one does so for all living beings, to attain Buddhahood and obtain all the qualities of a Buddha. Such characteristics are called the perfection of ethical discipline.

Why Offense and Non-Offense Cannot Be Apprehended

Chinese Source

復次，若菩薩於罪、不罪不可得故，是時，名為尸羅波羅蜜。

English Translation

Furthermore, when a bodhisattva cannot apprehend either offense or non-offense, this is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

問曰：若捨惡行善，是為持戒，云何言「罪、不罪不可得」？

Question: Abandoning evil and practicing goodness is upholding precepts. How, then, can it be said that offense and non-offense cannot be apprehended?

答曰：非謂邪見、麁心言「不可得」也；若深入諸法相，行空三昧，慧眼觀故，罪不可得；罪無故，不罪亦不可得。

Answer: This does not mean saying, through wrong views or a crude understanding, that they cannot be apprehended. Rather, deeply entering the characteristics of phenomena, practicing the samādhi of emptiness, and observing with the eye of wisdom, one cannot apprehend offense. Since offense is absent, non-offense likewise cannot be apprehended.

復次，眾生不可得故，殺罪亦不可得；罪不可得故，戒亦不可得。何以故？以有殺罪故，則有戒；若無殺罪，則亦無戒。

Furthermore, since living beings cannot be apprehended, the offense of killing likewise cannot be apprehended. Since offense cannot be apprehended, precepts likewise cannot be apprehended. Why? Because the offense of killing exists, there is a precept. If there were no offense of killing, there would likewise be no precept.

問曰：今眾生現有，云何言「眾生不可得」？

Question: Living beings plainly exist now. How can it be said that they cannot be apprehended?

答曰：肉眼所見，是為非見；若以慧眼觀，則不得眾生。如上檀中說無施者、無受者、無財物，此亦如是。

Answer: What is seen by the physical eye is not true seeing. Observed with the eye of wisdom, a living being cannot be found. It is as explained earlier in the discussion of giving: there is no giver, no recipient, and no gift. The same applies here.

Living Beings and the Five Aggregates

Chinese Source

復次，若有眾生，是五眾耶？離五眾耶？若是五眾，五眾有五，眾生為一；

English Translation

Furthermore, if a living being exists, is it the five aggregates, or does it exist apart from them? If it is the five aggregates, there are five aggregates, whereas the living being is one.

如是者，五可為一，一可為五。譬如市易物直五匹，以一匹取之，則不可得。何以故？一不得作五故。以是故，知五眾不得作一眾生。

In that case, five could become one, and one could become five. It is like trying to buy something in a market worth five bolts of cloth with a single bolt: it cannot be done. Why? One cannot be made into five. Thus we know that the five aggregates cannot be made into a single living being.

復次，五眾生滅無常相；眾生法，從先世來、至後世，受罪、福於三界。若五眾是眾生，譬如草木自生自滅，如是則無罪縛，亦無解脫。以是故，知非五眾是眾生。

Furthermore, the five aggregates arise and cease and have the characteristic of impermanence, whereas a living being is held to come from a previous life, proceed to a future life, and experience the results of wrongdoing and merit in the three realms. If the five aggregates were the living being, it would be like plants and trees arising and perishing by themselves. There would then be neither bondage through wrongdoing nor liberation. Thus we know that the five aggregates are not the living being.

若離五眾有眾生，如先說神常遍中已破。

If a living being exists apart from the five aggregates, this has already been refuted in the earlier discussion of a permanent, all-pervading self.

復次，離五眾則我見心不生，若離五眾有眾生，是為墮常；若墮常者，是則無生、無死。何以故？生名先無今有，死名已生便滅。若眾生常者，應遍滿五道中，先已常有，云何今復來生？若不有生，則無有死。

Furthermore, apart from the five aggregates, the thought of a self does not arise. If a living being existed apart from the five aggregates, this would fall into eternalism. If eternalism were true, there would be neither birth nor death. Why? Birth means that what did not exist before now exists; death means that what has arisen then ceases. If a living being were permanent, it should pervade the five destinies. Since it would already have existed eternally, how could it now come to be born? Without birth, there is no death.

問曰：定有眾生，何以故言無？五眾因緣，有眾生法；譬如五指因緣，拳法生。

Question: A living being certainly exists. Why say that it does not? Through the conditions of the five aggregates, the phenomenon of a living being exists, just as a fist arises through the conditions of five fingers.

答曰：此言非也！若五眾因緣有眾生法者，除五眾，則別有眾生法，然不可得；眼自見色，耳自聞聲，鼻嗅香，舌知味，身知觸，意知法，空無我法；離此六事，更無眾生。

Answer: This statement is incorrect! If the conditions of the five aggregates produced a living being, there would be a separate living being beyond the five aggregates, but none can be found. The eye sees forms, the ear hears sounds, the nose smells odors, the tongue knows tastes, the body knows tangible objects, and the mind knows mental objects: all are empty, without a self. Apart from these six functions, there is no further living being.

諸外道輩倒見故，言眼能見色，是為眾生；乃至意能知法，是為眾生；又能憶念，能受苦樂，是為眾生；但作是見，不知眾生實。

Through inverted views, followers of other teachings say that what enables the eye to see forms is a living being, and so on up to what enables the mind to know mental objects. They also call that which remembers and experiences pleasure and pain a living being. They merely hold these views without knowing the reality of a living being.

譬如一長老大德比丘，人謂是阿羅漢，多致供養。其後病死，諸弟子懼失供養故，夜盜出之；於其臥處安施被枕，令如師在，其狀如臥。

For example, there was an elderly, highly respected bhikṣu whom people regarded as an arhat, and to whom they made many offerings. Later he fell ill and died. Fearing the loss of offerings, his disciples secretly carried him away at night. They arranged a quilt and pillow where he had lain, making it appear that their teacher was still there, lying down.

人來問疾：「師在何許？」諸弟子言：「汝不見床上被枕耶？」愚者不審察之，謂師病臥，大送供養而去，如是非一。

People came to inquire about his illness and asked, “Where is the teacher?” His disciples said, “Do you not see the quilt and pillow on the bed?” Foolish people did not investigate carefully. Assuming that the teacher was lying ill, they left generous offerings and departed. This happened more than once.

復有智人來而問之，諸弟子亦如是答。智人言：「我不問被枕、床褥，我自求人。」發被求之，竟無人可得。除六事相，更無我人；知者、見者，亦復如是。

Then a wise person came and asked, and the disciples gave the same answer. The wise person said, “I am not asking about quilts, pillows, beds, or bedding. I am looking for the person.” Lifting the quilt and searching, he found no person at all. Apart from the characteristics of these six functions, there is no further self or person. The supposed knower and seer are likewise.

復次，若眾生於五眾因緣有者，五眾無常，眾生亦應無常。何以故？因果相似故；若眾生無常，則不至後世。

Furthermore, if a living being existed through the conditions of the five aggregates, then, since the five aggregates are impermanent, the living being should also be impermanent. Why? Cause and result are alike. If the living being were impermanent, it would not continue into a future life.

復次，若如汝言「眾生從本已來常有」，若爾者，眾生應生五眾，五眾不應生眾生；今五眾因緣生眾生名字，無智之人逐名求實。以是故，眾生實無；若無眾生，亦無殺罪；無殺罪故，亦無持戒。

Furthermore, if, as you say, a living being has existed permanently from the very beginning, then the living being should produce the five aggregates, rather than the five aggregates producing the living being. In fact, the conditions of the five aggregates give rise to the designation “living being,” and the unwise follow the designation in search of a real entity. Therefore, no living being truly exists. If there is no living being, there is also no offense of killing; and without the offense of killing, there is also no upholding of precepts.

復次，是五眾深入觀之，分別知空，如夢所見，如鏡中像；若殺夢中所見及鏡中像，無有殺罪。殺五陰空相眾生，亦復如是。

Furthermore, deeply contemplating and analyzing the five aggregates, one knows them to be empty, like things seen in a dream or images in a mirror. Killing a figure seen in a dream or an image in a mirror incurs no offense of killing. The same applies to killing a living being whose five aggregates have the characteristic of emptiness.

復次，若人不樂罪、貪著無罪，是人見破戒罪人則輕慢，見持戒善人則愛敬；如是持戒，則是起罪因緣。以是故言「於罪、不罪不可得故，應具足尸羅波羅蜜」。

Furthermore, if someone dislikes offense and clings to non-offense, they look down on offenders who break precepts and feel affection and respect for good people who uphold them. Upholding precepts in this way becomes a condition for giving rise to offense. Thus it is said: “Because offense and non-offense cannot be apprehended, one should fulfill the perfection of ethical discipline.”
