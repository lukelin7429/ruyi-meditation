Fascicle 14 · 大智度論卷第十四

Ethical Discipline and Patience

Fascicle 14 completes the explanation of ethical discipline and introduces patience toward living beings.

Translator: Lucien Hsieh (謝呂賢) Status: translation complete Source base: Taishō T25, no. 1509 Morality and the Buddha path

Translation Method

Each Chinese paragraph appears beside its English counterpart.

The Perfection of Ethical Discipline — Continued

Chinese Source

問曰：已知尸羅相，云何為尸羅波羅蜜？

English Translation

Question: We now understand the characteristics of ethical discipline. What is the perfection of ethical discipline?

答曰：有人言：菩薩持戒，寧自失身，不毀小戒，是為尸羅波羅蜜。如上《蘇陀蘇摩王經》中說，不惜身命以全禁戒。

Answer: Some say that a bodhisattva would rather lose their own life than violate even a minor precept, and that this is the perfection of ethical discipline. As stated earlier in the Sūtra of King Sutasoma, one does not cling to body or life, in order to preserve the precepts completely.

The Bodhisattva as a Venomous Dragon

Chinese Source

如菩薩本身，曾作大力毒龍。若眾生在前，身力弱者，眼視便死；身力強者，氣往而死。是龍受一日戒，出家求靜，入林樹間思惟；坐久，疲懈而睡。龍法，睡時形狀如蛇，身有文章，七寶雜色。

English Translation

For example, in a former life the bodhisattva was a powerful venomous dragon. When living beings stood before him, the weak died at his glance, and the strong died when his breath reached them. This dragon undertook the one-day precepts, left his dwelling in search of quiet, and went into a forest to contemplate. After sitting for a long time, he became tired and fell asleep. When dragons sleep, they take the form of snakes; his body bore patterns in the varied colors of the seven treasures.

獵者見之驚喜，言曰：「以此希有難得之皮，獻上國王以為服飾，不亦宜乎？」便以杖按其頭，以刀剝其皮。

A hunter saw him and exclaimed with delight, “Would it not be fitting to present this rare and precious skin to the king for his clothing and ornaments?” He pressed down the dragon's head with a staff and began to skin him with a knife.

龍自念言：「我力如意，傾覆此國，其如反掌；此人小物，豈能困我？我今以持戒故，不計此身，當從佛語！」

The dragon thought, “My power is at my command. Overturning this kingdom would be as easy as turning my hand. How could this insignificant person trouble me? But since I now uphold the precepts, I shall disregard this body and follow the Buddha's teaching!”

於是自忍，眠目不視，閉氣不息；憐愍此人，為持戒故，一心受剝，不生悔意。

He therefore endured it, closing his eyes so as not to look and holding his breath so as not to breathe. Out of compassion for this person and to uphold the precepts, he single-mindedly endured being skinned, without regret.

既以失皮，赤肉在地，時日大熱，宛轉土中；欲趣大水，見諸小蟲來食其身，為持戒故，不復敢動。

Once his skin was gone, his raw flesh lay on the ground. The sun was intensely hot, and he writhed in the earth. Wishing to reach deep water, he saw small insects coming to eat his body. To uphold the precepts, he no longer dared to move.

自思惟言：「今我此身以施諸蟲，為佛道故，今以肉施，以充其身；後成佛時，當以法施，以益其心。」

He reflected, “I now give this body to these insects. For the sake of Buddhahood, I give my flesh now to nourish their bodies. Later, when I become a Buddha, I shall give the Dharma to benefit their minds.”

如是誓已，身乾命絕，即生第二忉利天上。爾時毒龍，釋迦文佛是；是時獵者，提婆達等六師是也；諸小蟲輩，釋迦文佛初轉法輪，八萬諸天得道者是。

Having made this vow, his body dried out and his life ended. He was immediately born in the second heaven, the Heaven of the Thirty-Three. The venomous dragon then was Śākyamuni Buddha; the hunter was among the six teachers, including Devadatta; and the small insects were the eighty thousand devas who attained the path when Śākyamuni Buddha first turned the Dharma wheel.

菩薩護戒，不惜身命，決定不悔，其事如是，是名尸羅波羅蜜。

Such is the way a bodhisattva safeguards the precepts, without clinging to body or life, resolute and without regret. This is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

Upholding Precepts to Liberate Living Beings

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩持戒，為佛道故，作大要誓，必度眾生！不求今世、後世之樂，不為名聞虛譽法故，亦不自為早求涅槃，但為眾生沒在長流，恩愛所欺，愚惑所誤，我當度之令到彼岸。

English Translation

Furthermore, a bodhisattva upholds precepts for the sake of Buddhahood and makes a great, binding vow to liberate living beings: “I do not seek the pleasures of this life or the next, nor do I act for fame or empty praise. Nor do I seek an early nirvāṇa for myself alone. Rather, living beings are submerged in the long current, deceived by attachment and misled by ignorance and delusion. I shall liberate them and bring them to the other shore.”

一心持戒，為生善處，生善處故見善人，見善人故生智慧，生智慧故得行六波羅蜜，得行六波羅蜜故得佛道。如是持戒名為尸羅波羅蜜。

One wholeheartedly upholds precepts in order to be born in a good place. Through birth in a good place, one meets good people; through meeting good people, wisdom arises; through wisdom, one can practice the six perfections; and through practicing the six perfections, one attains Buddhahood. Upholding precepts in this way is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

Ethical Discipline and the Six Perfections

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩持戒，心樂善清淨，不為畏惡道，亦不為生天，但求善淨；以戒熏心，令心樂善，是為尸羅波羅蜜。

English Translation

Furthermore, a bodhisattva upholds precepts with a mind that delights in goodness and purity, neither from fear of unfortunate destinies nor for birth in heaven, but simply in pursuit of wholesome purity. Through the influence of precepts, the mind comes to delight in goodness. This is the perfection of ethical discipline.

復次，菩薩以大悲心持戒，得至佛道，是名尸羅波羅蜜。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva upholds precepts with great compassion and thereby reaches Buddhahood. This is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

復次，菩薩持戒，能生六波羅蜜，是則名為尸羅波羅蜜。

Furthermore, when a bodhisattva's upholding of precepts gives rise to the six perfections, this is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

云何持戒能生戒？因五戒得沙彌戒，因沙彌戒得律儀戒，因律儀戒得禪定戒，因禪定戒得無漏戒，是為戒生戒。

How does upholding precepts give rise to further precepts? Through the five precepts one obtains the novice precepts; through the novice precepts, the precepts of restraint; through the precepts of restraint, the precepts associated with meditative concentration; and through these, the precepts free of outflows. This is how precepts give rise to precepts.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Giving

Chinese Source

云何持戒能生於檀？檀有三種：一者、財施，二者、法施，三者、無畏施。持戒自撿，不侵一切眾生財物，是名財施。

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to giving? Giving has three forms: giving material goods, giving the Dharma, and giving fearlessness. By upholding precepts and restraining oneself, one does not encroach upon any living being's property. This is called giving material goods.

眾生見者，慕其所行，又為說法，令其開悟。又自思惟：我當堅持淨戒，與一切眾生作供養福田，令諸眾生得無量福。如是種種，名為法施。一切眾生皆畏於死，持戒不害，是則無畏施。

Living beings who see such a person admire their conduct. One also teaches them the Dharma so that they may awaken to understanding. Furthermore, one reflects, “I shall firmly uphold pure precepts and become a field of merit to which all living beings may make offerings, enabling them to gain immeasurable blessings.” These and similar practices are called giving the Dharma. All living beings fear death; upholding precepts and doing no harm is therefore giving fearlessness.

復次，菩薩自念：「我當持戒，以此戒報，為諸眾生作轉輪聖王、或作閻浮提王、若作天王，令諸眾生滿足於財，無所乏短；然後坐佛樹下，降伏魔王，破諸魔軍，成無上道，為諸眾生說清淨法，令無量眾生度老、病、死海。」是為持戒因緣生檀波羅蜜。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “I shall uphold precepts and, through their reward, become a wheel-turning sage king, a king of Jambudvīpa, or a king of devas for the sake of living beings, satisfying their material needs so that they lack nothing. Then I shall sit beneath the tree of awakening, subdue Māra and defeat his armies, accomplish unsurpassed awakening, and teach the pure Dharma to living beings, enabling immeasurable beings to cross the ocean of aging, illness, and death.” This is how the conditions of upholding precepts give rise to the perfection of giving.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Patience

Chinese Source

云何持戒生忍辱？持戒之人心自念言：「我今持戒為持心故。若持戒無忍，當墮地獄；雖不破戒，以無忍故，不免惡道；何可縱忿不自制心？但以心故入三惡趣。是故應當好自勉強，懃修忍辱。」

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to patience? One who upholds precepts reflects: “I now uphold precepts in order to guard the mind. If I uphold them without patience, I shall fall into hell. Even without breaking precepts, I cannot escape unfortunate destinies if I lack patience. How could I indulge anger and fail to restrain my mind? It is through the mind alone that one enters the three unfortunate destinies. Therefore, I should exert myself well and diligently cultivate patience.”

復次，行者欲令戒德堅強，當修忍辱。所以者何？忍為大力，能牢固戒，令不動搖。復自思惟：「我今出家，形與俗別，豈可縱心如世人法？宜自勉勵，以忍調心。以身、口忍，心亦得忍；若心不忍，身、口亦爾。」是故行者當令身、口、心忍，絕諸忿恨。

Furthermore, a practitioner who wishes to make the virtue of precepts firm should cultivate patience. Why? Patience is a great strength that stabilizes precepts and keeps them from wavering. One further reflects: “I have now gone forth, and my appearance differs from that of laypeople. How could I indulge my mind in worldly ways? I should exert myself and tame the mind through patience. Through patience in body and speech, the mind also becomes patient. If the mind lacks patience, body and speech will likewise lack it.” Thus the practitioner should maintain patience in body, speech, and mind, cutting off anger and resentment.

復次，是戒略說則有八萬，廣說則無量，我當云何能具持此無量戒法？唯當忍辱，眾戒自得。

Furthermore, in brief there are eighty thousand precepts, and in an extensive account they are immeasurable. How can I uphold all these immeasurable precepts completely? I need only practice patience, and the many precepts will naturally be fulfilled.

譬如有人得罪於王，王以罪人載之刀車，六邊利刃，間不容間，奔逸馳走，行不擇路；若能持身，不為刀傷，是則殺而不死。持戒之人，亦復如是，戒為利刀，忍為持身，若忍心不固，戒亦傷人。

It is like someone who has offended a king. The king places the offender in a chariot lined on all six sides with sharp blades, leaving no room between them, and sends it hurtling along without choosing its route. If the person can hold the body steady and avoid the blades, they survive this attempt to kill them. One who upholds precepts is similar: the precepts are the sharp blades, and patience is what holds the body steady. If the patient mind is not firm, precepts too can injure a person.

又復譬如老人夜行，無杖則蹶；忍為戒杖，扶人至道，福樂因緣，不能動搖。如是種種，名為持戒生羼提波羅蜜。

It is also like an old person walking at night: without a staff, they stumble. Patience is the staff of ethical discipline, supporting one on the way to the path and providing unshakable conditions for merit and happiness. These and similar examples explain how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of patience.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Diligence

Chinese Source

云何持戒而生精進？持戒之人除去放逸，自力懃修，習無上法，捨世間樂，入於善道，志求涅槃，以度一切，大心不懈，以求佛為本，是為持戒能生精進。

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to diligence? One who upholds precepts removes heedlessness, diligently practices through personal effort, trains in the unsurpassed Dharma, relinquishes worldly pleasures, and enters the wholesome path. Aspiring to nirvāṇa in order to liberate all, one sustains a great and tireless resolve, taking the quest for Buddhahood as fundamental. This is how upholding precepts gives rise to diligence.

復次，持戒之人，疲厭世苦老、病、死患，心生精進，必求自脫，亦以度人。

Furthermore, one who upholds precepts grows weary of worldly suffering and the troubles of aging, illness, and death. Diligence arises in the mind, and one resolutely seeks one's own liberation while also liberating others.

譬如野干在林樹間，依隨師子及諸虎豹，求其殘肉以自存活。有時空乏，夜半踰城，深入人舍，求肉不得，屏處睡息，不覺夜竟；惶怖無計，走則慮不自免，住則懼畏死痛；便自定心，詐死在地。

For example, a jackal lived among forest trees, following lions, tigers, and leopards to sustain itself on their leftover meat. Once, finding nothing, it climbed over a city wall at midnight and went deep into a house. Unable to find meat, it slept in a secluded spot, unaware that the night had ended. Terrified and without a plan, it feared that running would not save it, but that staying would bring the agony of death. It therefore steadied its mind and lay on the ground pretending to be dead.

眾人來見，有一人言：「我須野干耳。」即便截取。野干自念：「截耳雖痛，但令身在。」次有一人言：「我須野干尾。」便復截去。野干復念：「截尾雖痛猶是小事。」次有一人言：「我須野干牙。」野干心念：「取者轉多，儻取我頭，則無活路。」即從地起奮其智力，絕踊間關，徑得自濟。

People came and saw it. One said, “I need a jackal's ears,” and cut them off. The jackal thought, “Although losing my ears is painful, at least my body remains.” Another said, “I need a jackal's tail,” and cut that off too. The jackal thought, “Although losing my tail is painful, it is still a small matter.” Then someone said, “I need a jackal's teeth.” The jackal thought, “More and more people are taking parts of me. If someone takes my head, there will be no way to survive.” It immediately sprang up, exerted its intelligence and strength, leaped through obstacles, and escaped to safety.

行者之心，求脫苦難，亦復如是：若老至時，猶故自寬，不能慇懃決斷精進；病亦如是，以有差期，未能決計；死欲至時，自知無冀，便能自勉，果敢慇懃，大修精進，從死地中畢至涅槃。

The practitioner's mind is similar in seeking escape from suffering. When aging arrives, one still indulges oneself and cannot earnestly resolve to practice diligently. Illness is the same: because recovery is possible, one has not yet made a firm decision. But when death approaches and one knows there is no hope, one can exert oneself, become resolute and earnest, and cultivate great diligence, proceeding from the realm of death all the way to nirvāṇa.

復次，持戒之法，譬如人射，先得平地，地平然後心安，心安然後挽滿，挽滿然後陷深。戒為平地，定意為弓，挽滿為精進，箭為智慧，賊是無明。若能如是展力精進，必至大道，以度眾生。

Furthermore, upholding precepts is like archery. First one finds level ground. On level ground the mind becomes steady; with a steady mind one draws the bow fully; with the bow fully drawn, the arrow penetrates deeply. Precepts are the level ground, mental concentration is the bow, drawing it fully is diligence, the arrow is wisdom, and the enemy is ignorance. If one exerts oneself diligently in this way, one will surely reach the great path and liberate living beings.

復次，持戒之人，能以精進自制五情，不受五欲；若心已去，能攝令還；是為持戒能護諸根。護諸根則生禪定，生禪定則生智慧，生智慧得至佛道。是為持戒生毘梨耶波羅蜜。

Furthermore, one who upholds precepts can diligently restrain the five sense faculties and refuse the five sensual pleasures. If the mind has wandered, one can gather it back. This is how upholding precepts safeguards the faculties. Safeguarding the faculties gives rise to meditative concentration; meditative concentration gives rise to wisdom; and wisdom leads to Buddhahood. This is how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of diligence.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Meditation

Chinese Source

云何持戒生禪？人有三業作諸善，若身、口業善，意業自然入善；譬如曲草生於麻中，不扶自直。

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to meditation? People perform wholesome deeds through the three kinds of karma. If bodily and verbal karma are wholesome, mental karma naturally turns toward goodness. It is like crooked grass growing among hemp: without being supported, it straightens by itself.

持戒之力，能羸諸結使。云何能羸？若不持戒，瞋恚事來，殺心即生；若欲事至，婬心即成。若持戒者，雖有微瞋，不生殺心；雖有婬念，婬事不成，是為持戒能令諸結使羸。

The power of upholding precepts weakens the fetters. How does it weaken them? If one does not uphold precepts, when something provokes anger, the intention to kill immediately arises; when an occasion for desire appears, sexual intent takes shape. If one upholds precepts, even when slight anger arises, it does not become an intention to kill; even when sexual thoughts arise, the sexual act is not carried out. This is how upholding precepts weakens the fetters.

諸結使羸，禪定易得。譬如老病失力，死事易得；結使羸故，禪定易得。

When the fetters are weak, meditative concentration is easily attained. Just as death comes readily to someone weakened by aging and illness, meditative concentration comes readily when the fetters are weak.

復次，人心未息，常求逸樂；行者持戒，棄捨世福，心不放逸，是故易得禪定。

Furthermore, an unquiet mind constantly seeks pleasure and ease. A practitioner who upholds precepts relinquishes worldly blessings and keeps the mind from heedlessness. Thus meditative concentration is easily attained.

復次，持戒之人，得生人中，次生六欲天上，次至色界，若破色相生無色界；持戒清淨，斷諸結使，得阿羅漢道；大心持戒，愍念眾生，是為菩薩。

Furthermore, one who upholds precepts may be born among humans, then in the six heavens of the desire realm, and then reach the form realm. Transcending the perception of form, one is born in the formless realm. By upholding precepts purely and cutting off the fetters, one attains arhatship. Upholding precepts with a great aspiration and compassion for living beings, one is a bodhisattva.

復次，戒為撿麁，禪為攝細。復次，戒攝身、口，禪止亂心；如人上屋，非梯不昇，不得戒梯，禪亦不立。

Furthermore, precepts restrain what is coarse, while meditation gathers in what is subtle. Precepts restrain body and speech, while meditation stills the distracted mind. Just as someone cannot climb onto a roof without a ladder, meditation cannot be established without the ladder of precepts.

復次，破戒之人，結使風強，散亂其心；其心散亂，則禪不可得。持戒之人，煩惱風軟，心不大散，禪定易得。如是等種種因緣，是為持戒生禪波羅蜜。

Furthermore, in one who breaks precepts, the winds of the fetters are strong and scatter the mind. With the mind scattered, meditation cannot be attained. In one who upholds precepts, the winds of affliction are gentle, the mind is not greatly scattered, and meditative concentration is easily attained. These and various other conditions explain how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of meditation.

How Ethical Discipline Gives Rise to Wisdom

Chinese Source

云何持戒能生智慧？持戒之人，觀此戒相從何而有，知從眾罪而生；若無眾罪，則亦無戒。戒相如是，從因緣有，何故生著？譬如蓮華出自污泥，色雖鮮好，出處不淨；以是悟心，不令生著，是為持戒生般若波羅蜜。

English Translation

How does upholding precepts give rise to wisdom? One who upholds precepts examines where their characteristics come from and understands that they arise in dependence on offenses. If there were no offenses, there would be no precepts. Since the characteristics of precepts arise from causes and conditions in this way, why become attached to them? It is like a lotus emerging from dirty mud: although its color is fresh and beautiful, its place of origin is impure. Understanding this, one does not allow attachment to arise. This is how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of wisdom.

復次，持戒之人，心自思惟：若我以持戒貴而可取，破戒賤而可捨者，若有此心不應般若。以智慧籌量，心不著戒，無取、無捨，是為持戒生般若波羅蜜。

Furthermore, one who upholds precepts reflects: “If I regard upholding precepts as noble and worth grasping, and breaking precepts as base and worth rejecting, such a mind does not accord with wisdom.” Assessing this with wisdom, the mind does not attach to precepts; there is neither grasping nor rejecting. This is how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of wisdom.

復次，不持戒人，雖有利智，以營世務，種種欲求生業之事，慧根漸鈍；譬如利刀以割泥土，遂成鈍器。若出家持戒，不營世業，常觀諸法實相無相；先雖鈍根，以漸轉利。如是等種種因緣，名為持戒生般若波羅蜜。

Furthermore, even someone with sharp intelligence who does not uphold precepts gradually dulls the faculty of wisdom by engaging in worldly affairs and the many pursuits of desire and livelihood. It is like using a sharp knife to cut mud until it becomes blunt. If one goes forth, upholds precepts, does not engage in worldly occupations, and constantly contemplates the true character of phenomena as without characteristics, one's faculties gradually become sharp even if they were initially dull. These and various other conditions explain how upholding precepts gives rise to the perfection of wisdom.

如是名為尸羅波羅蜜生六波羅蜜。

This is what is called the perfection of ethical discipline giving rise to the six perfections.

復次，菩薩持戒不以畏故，亦非愚癡，非疑、非惑，亦不自為涅槃故持戒；但為一切眾生故，為得佛道故，為得一切佛法故。如是相名為尸羅波羅蜜。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva upholds precepts neither out of fear nor through ignorance, doubt, or confusion, nor for personal nirvāṇa. Rather, one does so for all living beings, to attain Buddhahood and obtain all the qualities of a Buddha. Such characteristics are called the perfection of ethical discipline.

Why Offense and Non-Offense Cannot Be Apprehended

Chinese Source

復次，若菩薩於罪、不罪不可得故，是時，名為尸羅波羅蜜。

English Translation

Furthermore, when a bodhisattva cannot apprehend either offense or non-offense, this is called the perfection of ethical discipline.

問曰：若捨惡行善，是為持戒，云何言「罪、不罪不可得」？

Question: Abandoning evil and practicing goodness is upholding precepts. How, then, can it be said that offense and non-offense cannot be apprehended?

答曰：非謂邪見、麁心言「不可得」也；若深入諸法相，行空三昧，慧眼觀故，罪不可得；罪無故，不罪亦不可得。

Answer: This does not mean saying, through wrong views or a crude understanding, that they cannot be apprehended. Rather, deeply entering the characteristics of phenomena, practicing the samādhi of emptiness, and observing with the eye of wisdom, one cannot apprehend offense. Since offense is absent, non-offense likewise cannot be apprehended.

復次，眾生不可得故，殺罪亦不可得；罪不可得故，戒亦不可得。何以故？以有殺罪故，則有戒；若無殺罪，則亦無戒。

Furthermore, since living beings cannot be apprehended, the offense of killing likewise cannot be apprehended. Since offense cannot be apprehended, precepts likewise cannot be apprehended. Why? Because the offense of killing exists, there is a precept. If there were no offense of killing, there would likewise be no precept.

問曰：今眾生現有，云何言「眾生不可得」？

Question: Living beings plainly exist now. How can it be said that they cannot be apprehended?

答曰：肉眼所見，是為非見；若以慧眼觀，則不得眾生。如上檀中說無施者、無受者、無財物，此亦如是。

Answer: What is seen by the physical eye is not true seeing. Observed with the eye of wisdom, a living being cannot be found. It is as explained earlier in the discussion of giving: there is no giver, no recipient, and no gift. The same applies here.

Living Beings and the Five Aggregates

Chinese Source

復次，若有眾生，是五眾耶？離五眾耶？若是五眾，五眾有五，眾生為一；

English Translation

Furthermore, if a living being exists, is it the five aggregates, or does it exist apart from them? If it is the five aggregates, there are five aggregates, whereas the living being is one.

如是者，五可為一，一可為五。譬如市易物直五匹，以一匹取之，則不可得。何以故？一不得作五故。以是故，知五眾不得作一眾生。

In that case, five could become one, and one could become five. It is like trying to buy something in a market worth five bolts of cloth with a single bolt: it cannot be done. Why? One cannot be made into five. Thus we know that the five aggregates cannot be made into a single living being.

復次，五眾生滅無常相；眾生法，從先世來、至後世，受罪、福於三界。若五眾是眾生，譬如草木自生自滅，如是則無罪縛，亦無解脫。以是故，知非五眾是眾生。

Furthermore, the five aggregates arise and cease and have the characteristic of impermanence, whereas a living being is held to come from a previous life, proceed to a future life, and experience the results of wrongdoing and merit in the three realms. If the five aggregates were the living being, it would be like plants and trees arising and perishing by themselves. There would then be neither bondage through wrongdoing nor liberation. Thus we know that the five aggregates are not the living being.

若離五眾有眾生，如先說神常遍中已破。

If a living being exists apart from the five aggregates, this has already been refuted in the earlier discussion of a permanent, all-pervading self.

復次，離五眾則我見心不生，若離五眾有眾生，是為墮常；若墮常者，是則無生、無死。何以故？生名先無今有，死名已生便滅。若眾生常者，應遍滿五道中，先已常有，云何今復來生？若不有生，則無有死。

Furthermore, apart from the five aggregates, the thought of a self does not arise. If a living being existed apart from the five aggregates, this would fall into eternalism. If eternalism were true, there would be neither birth nor death. Why? Birth means that what did not exist before now exists; death means that what has arisen then ceases. If a living being were permanent, it should pervade the five destinies. Since it would already have existed eternally, how could it now come to be born? Without birth, there is no death.

問曰：定有眾生，何以故言無？五眾因緣，有眾生法；譬如五指因緣，拳法生。

Question: A living being certainly exists. Why say that it does not? Through the conditions of the five aggregates, the phenomenon of a living being exists, just as a fist arises through the conditions of five fingers.

答曰：此言非也！若五眾因緣有眾生法者，除五眾，則別有眾生法，然不可得；眼自見色，耳自聞聲，鼻嗅香，舌知味，身知觸，意知法，空無我法；離此六事，更無眾生。

Answer: This statement is incorrect! If the conditions of the five aggregates produced a living being, there would be a separate living being beyond the five aggregates, but none can be found. The eye sees forms, the ear hears sounds, the nose smells odors, the tongue knows tastes, the body knows tangible objects, and the mind knows mental objects: all are empty, without a self. Apart from these six functions, there is no further living being.

諸外道輩倒見故，言眼能見色，是為眾生；乃至意能知法，是為眾生；又能憶念，能受苦樂，是為眾生；但作是見，不知眾生實。

Through inverted views, followers of other teachings say that what enables the eye to see forms is a living being, and so on up to what enables the mind to know mental objects. They also call that which remembers and experiences pleasure and pain a living being. They merely hold these views without knowing the reality of a living being.

譬如一長老大德比丘，人謂是阿羅漢，多致供養。其後病死，諸弟子懼失供養故，夜盜出之；於其臥處安施被枕，令如師在，其狀如臥。

For example, there was an elderly, highly respected bhikṣu whom people regarded as an arhat, and to whom they made many offerings. Later he fell ill and died. Fearing the loss of offerings, his disciples secretly carried him away at night. They arranged a quilt and pillow where he had lain, making it appear that their teacher was still there, lying down.

人來問疾：「師在何許？」諸弟子言：「汝不見床上被枕耶？」愚者不審察之，謂師病臥，大送供養而去，如是非一。

People came to inquire about his illness and asked, “Where is the teacher?” His disciples said, “Do you not see the quilt and pillow on the bed?” Foolish people did not investigate carefully. Assuming that the teacher was lying ill, they left generous offerings and departed. This happened more than once.

復有智人來而問之，諸弟子亦如是答。智人言：「我不問被枕、床褥，我自求人。」發被求之，竟無人可得。除六事相，更無我人；知者、見者，亦復如是。

Then a wise person came and asked, and the disciples gave the same answer. The wise person said, “I am not asking about quilts, pillows, beds, or bedding. I am looking for the person.” Lifting the quilt and searching, he found no person at all. Apart from the characteristics of these six functions, there is no further self or person. The supposed knower and seer are likewise.

復次，若眾生於五眾因緣有者，五眾無常，眾生亦應無常。何以故？因果相似故；若眾生無常，則不至後世。

Furthermore, if a living being existed through the conditions of the five aggregates, then, since the five aggregates are impermanent, the living being should also be impermanent. Why? Cause and result are alike. If the living being were impermanent, it would not continue into a future life.

復次，若如汝言「眾生從本已來常有」，若爾者，眾生應生五眾，五眾不應生眾生；今五眾因緣生眾生名字，無智之人逐名求實。以是故，眾生實無；若無眾生，亦無殺罪；無殺罪故，亦無持戒。

Furthermore, if, as you say, a living being has existed permanently from the very beginning, then the living being should produce the five aggregates, rather than the five aggregates producing the living being. In fact, the conditions of the five aggregates give rise to the designation “living being,” and the unwise follow the designation in search of a real entity. Therefore, no living being truly exists. If there is no living being, there is also no offense of killing; and without the offense of killing, there is also no upholding of precepts.

復次，是五眾深入觀之，分別知空，如夢所見，如鏡中像；若殺夢中所見及鏡中像，無有殺罪。殺五陰空相眾生，亦復如是。

Furthermore, deeply contemplating and analyzing the five aggregates, one knows them to be empty, like things seen in a dream or images in a mirror. Killing a figure seen in a dream or an image in a mirror incurs no offense of killing. The same applies to killing a living being whose five aggregates have the characteristic of emptiness.

復次，若人不樂罪、貪著無罪，是人見破戒罪人則輕慢，見持戒善人則愛敬；如是持戒，則是起罪因緣。以是故言「於罪、不罪不可得故，應具足尸羅波羅蜜」。

Furthermore, if someone dislikes offense and clings to non-offense, they look down on offenders who break precepts and feel affection and respect for good people who uphold them. Upholding precepts in this way becomes a condition for giving rise to offense. Thus it is said: “Because offense and non-offense cannot be apprehended, one should fulfill the perfection of ethical discipline.”

Part Twenty-Four: The Perfection of Patience

Chinese Source

〖經〗「心不動故，應具足羼提波羅蜜。」

English Translation

Sutra: “Because the mind remains unmoved, one should fulfill the perfection of patience.”

〖論〗問曰：云何名羼提？

Treatise: Question: What is meant by kṣānti?

答曰：羼提，秦言「忍辱」。忍辱有二種：生忍、法忍。菩薩行生忍，得無量福德；行法忍，得無量智慧。福德、智慧二事具足故，得如所願；譬如人有目、有足，隨意能到。

Answer: Kṣānti means “patience.” Patience has two kinds: patience toward living beings and patience concerning phenomena. By practicing patience toward living beings, a bodhisattva gains immeasurable merit; by practicing patience concerning phenomena, immeasurable wisdom. With both merit and wisdom complete, one accomplishes one's wishes, just as someone with both eyes and feet can go wherever they wish.

菩薩若遇惡口罵詈，若刀杖所加，思惟知罪、福業因緣諸法，內、外畢竟空，無我、無我所，以三法印印諸法故，力雖能報，不生惡心，不起惡口業；爾時，心數法生，名為忍。得是忍法故，忍智牢固；譬如畫彩，得膠則堅著。

When a bodhisattva encounters harsh abuse or attacks with blades and clubs, they reflect on and understand the conditions of wrongdoing, merit, and karma. Internal and external phenomena are ultimately empty, without a self or anything belonging to a self. Applying the three Dharma seals to phenomena, even if able to retaliate, they arouse no evil intention and produce no evil verbal karma. The mental factor that arises at that time is called patience. Through this quality, the wisdom of patience becomes firm, just as colors in a painting adhere firmly when mixed with glue.

有人言：「善心有二種：有麁，有細；麁名忍辱，細名禪定。未得禪定心樂，能遮眾惡，是名忍辱；心得禪定樂，不為眾惡，是名禪定。」

Some say: “Wholesome mind has two forms, coarse and subtle. The coarse is called patience, and the subtle meditative concentration. Before attaining the mind's happiness in concentration, being able to ward off evils is called patience. Having attained that happiness and not committing evils is called meditative concentration.”

是忍，是心數法，與心相應，隨心行。非業，非業報，隨業行。有人言：「二界繫。」有人言：「但欲界繫，或不繫。色界無外惡可忍故。」亦有漏、亦無漏，凡夫、聖人俱得故。障「己心、他心不善法」，故名為善。善故，或思惟斷，或不斷。如是等種種，阿毘曇廣分別。

Patience is a mental factor associated with mind, following its activity. It is neither karma nor karmic retribution, but accompanies karma. Some say, “It belongs to two realms.” Others say, “It belongs only to the desire realm, or is unaffiliated with a realm, because in the form realm there is no external harm to endure.” It may be subject to outflows or free of outflows, since both ordinary people and noble ones attain it. Because it obstructs unwholesome states in one's own mind and others' minds, it is called wholesome. As a wholesome quality, it may be abandoned through cultivation or not abandoned. These and various other distinctions are extensively explained in the Abhidharma.

Patience toward Living Beings

Chinese Source

問曰：云何名生忍？

English Translation

Question: What is patience toward living beings?

答曰：有二種眾生來向菩薩：一者、恭敬供養，二者、瞋罵打害。爾時，菩薩其心能忍，不愛敬養眾生，不瞋加惡眾生。是名生忍。

Answer: Two kinds of living beings approach a bodhisattva: those who show respect and make offerings, and those who angrily abuse, strike, or harm them. At such times, the bodhisattva's mind remains patient, without attachment to those who offer respect and support or anger toward those who inflict harm. This is called patience toward living beings.

問曰：云何恭敬、供養名之為忍？

Question: Why is the response to respect and offerings called patience?

答曰：有二種結使：一者、屬愛結使，二者、屬恚結使。恭敬、供養雖不生恚，令心愛著，是名軟賊。是故於此應當自忍，不著不愛。

Answer: There are two kinds of fetters: those belonging to attachment and those belonging to anger. Although respect and offerings do not produce anger, they cause the mind to become attached. They are called gentle thieves. Therefore, one should exercise patience toward them, without clinging or craving.

云何能忍？觀其無常，是結使生處。如佛所說：「利養瘡深，譬如斷皮至肉，斷肉至骨，斷骨至髓。人著利養，則破持戒皮，斷禪定肉，破智慧骨，失微妙善心髓。」

How can one be patient? Contemplate their impermanence and recognize them as a source of fetters. As the Buddha said: “The wound of gain and support is deep. It is like cutting through skin to flesh, through flesh to bone, and through bone to marrow. Attachment to gain and support breaks the skin of ethical discipline, cuts the flesh of concentration, breaks the bones of wisdom, and destroys the marrow of the subtle wholesome mind.”

Devadatta and Attachment to Offerings

Chinese Source

如佛初遊迦毘羅婆國，與千二百五十比丘俱，悉是梵志之身。供養火故，形容憔悴；絕食苦行故，膚體瘦黑。淨飯王心念言：「我子侍從，雖復心淨清潔，竝無容貌，我當擇取累重多子孫者，家出一人，為佛弟子。」如是思惟已，勅下國中：簡擇諸釋貴戚子弟，應書之身，皆令出家。

English Translation

When the Buddha first visited Kapilavastu, he was accompanied by twelve hundred and fifty bhikṣus, all formerly brahmanical ascetics. Their service to the sacrificial fire had left them haggard, and fasting austerities had made them thin and dark. King Śuddhodana thought: “Although my son's attendants are pure in mind, none has a fine appearance. I shall select families with many children and grandchildren and have one person from each become the Buddha's disciple.” Having considered this, he issued an order throughout the kingdom to select young men of the noble Śākya families eligible for registration and have them all go forth.

是時，斛飯王子提婆達多，出家學道，誦六萬法聚，精進修行，滿十二年。其後為供養利故，來至佛所，求學神通。佛告憍曇：「汝觀五陰無常，可以得道，亦得神通。」而不為說取通之法。

At that time Devadatta, son of King Droṇodana, went forth to train in the path. He memorized sixty thousand collections of teachings and practiced diligently for a full twelve years. Later, seeking offerings and gain, he came to the Buddha and asked to learn supernormal powers. The Buddha addressed him as Gautama and said, “Contemplate the impermanence of the five aggregates. You can thereby attain the path and also supernormal powers.” But he did not teach him a method for obtaining those powers.

出求舍利弗、目揵連，乃至五百阿羅漢，皆不為說；言：「汝當觀五陰無常，可以得道，可以得通。」不得所求，涕泣不樂；到阿難所，求學神通；是時阿難未得他心智，敬其兄故，如佛所言以授。提婆達多受學通法，入山不久，便得五神通。

Devadatta then sought instruction from Śāriputra, Maudgalyāyana, and as many as five hundred arhats. None taught him the method; all said, “You should contemplate the impermanence of the five aggregates. You can thereby attain the path and supernormal powers.” Unable to obtain what he sought, he wept in distress. He went to Ānanda and asked to learn supernormal powers. At that time Ānanda had not yet attained knowledge of others' minds. Out of respect for his elder kinsman, he taught him according to what the Buddha had said. Having learned the method, Devadatta entered the mountains and soon attained the five supernormal powers.

得五神通已，自念：「誰當與我作檀越者？」如王子阿闍世，有大王相。欲與為親厚，到天上取天食；還到欝旦羅越，取自然粳米；至閻浮林中，取閻浮果，與王子阿闍世。

After attaining them, he thought, “Who will become my patron?” Prince Ajātaśatru had the marks of a great king. Wishing to become close to him, Devadatta went to heaven to obtain heavenly food, then to Uttarakuru for spontaneously grown rice, and to the jambu grove for jambu fruit, all of which he gave to the prince.

或時自變其身，作象寶、馬寶，以惑其心；或作嬰孩坐其膝上，王子抱之，嗚唼與唾；時時自說己名，令太子知之，種種變態以動其心。王子意惑，於奈園中大立精舍，四種供養，并種種雜供，無物不備，以給提婆達多；

Sometimes he transformed himself into a precious elephant or a precious horse to bewilder the prince's mind. Sometimes he became an infant sitting on the prince's lap; the prince held and kissed him, exchanging saliva with him. From time to time he stated his own name so that the prince would recognize him. Through various transformations he stirred the prince's mind. Bewildered, the prince built a large monastery in a mango grove and supplied Devadatta with the four requisites and many other offerings, leaving nothing lacking.

日日率諸大臣，自為送五百釜羹飯。提婆達多大得供養，而徒眾尠少，自念：「我有三十相，減佛未幾，直以弟子未集；若大眾圍繞，與佛何異？」如是思惟已，生心破僧，得五百弟子；舍利弗、目犍連說法教化，僧還和合。

Every day the prince led his ministers in personally delivering five hundred pots of soup and rice. Devadatta received abundant offerings but had few followers. He thought, “I have thirty marks, only slightly fewer than the Buddha. It is simply that disciples have not yet gathered around me. If I were surrounded by a great assembly, how would I differ from the Buddha?” Thinking this, he conceived the intention to divide the Saṅgha and gained five hundred disciples. Śāriputra and Maudgalyāyana taught and guided them, and the Saṅgha was reunited.

爾時，提婆達多便生惡心，推山壓佛，金剛力士以金剛杵而遙擲之，碎石迸來，傷佛足指。華色比丘尼呵之，復以拳打尼，尼即時眼出而死，作三逆罪。

Then Devadatta aroused an evil intention and pushed a rock down the mountain to crush the Buddha. The vajra-bearing guardian struck it from afar with his vajra, and flying fragments injured the Buddha's toe. The bhikṣuṇī Utpalavarṇā rebuked Devadatta; he struck her with his fist, and her eyes immediately protruded and she died. He thus committed three heinous offenses.

與惡邪師富蘭那外道等為親厚，斷諸善根，心無愧悔。復以惡毒著指爪中，欲因禮佛以中傷佛；欲去未到王舍城中，地自然破裂，火車來迎，生入地獄。

He became close to evil teachers of wrong views, including the non-Buddhist teacher Pūraṇa, cut off his wholesome roots, and felt no shame or remorse. Again, he placed a deadly poison beneath his fingernails, intending to wound and poison the Buddha while bowing to him. Before he could reach Rājagṛha, the ground split open of itself, a fiery chariot came to meet him, and he entered hell alive.

提婆達多身有三十相，而不能忍伏其心，為供養利故，而作大罪，生入地獄。以是故言利養瘡深，破皮至髓，應當除却愛供養人心。是為菩薩忍，心不愛著供養、恭敬人。

Although Devadatta possessed thirty bodily marks, he could not patiently subdue his mind. For the sake of offerings and gain, he committed grave offenses and entered hell alive. Thus it is said that the wound of gain and support runs deep, breaking through skin to marrow. One should remove attachment to those who make offerings. This is a bodhisattva's patience: the mind does not cling to those who offer support and respect.

Three Sources of Offerings

Chinese Source

復次，供養有三種：一者、先世因緣福德故；二者、今世功德，修戒、禪定、智慧故，為人敬養；三者、虛妄欺惑，內無實德，外如清白，以誑時人而得供養。

English Translation

Furthermore, offerings have three sources: first, the conditions of merit from previous lives; second, qualities cultivated in this life—ethical discipline, concentration, and wisdom—which lead others to offer respect and support; third, falsehood and deception, by which one lacks real virtue within but outwardly appears pure, deceiving people into making offerings.

於此三種供養中，心自思惟：「若先世因緣懃修福德，今得供養，是為懃身作之而自得耳，何為於此而生貢高？譬如春種秋穫，自以力得，何足自憍？」如是思惟已，忍伏其心，不著、不憍。

Concerning these three sources, one reflects: “If I now receive offerings because I diligently cultivated merit in previous lives, this is simply receiving what I myself worked to produce. Why become conceited about it? It is like sowing in spring and harvesting in autumn: the result comes through one's own effort. What is there to boast of?” Having reflected in this way, one patiently subdues the mind, without attachment or pride.

若今世功德故而得供養，當自思惟：「我以智慧，若知諸法實相，若能斷結，以此功德故，是人供養，於我無事。」如是思惟已，自伏其心，不自憍高；此實愛樂功德，不愛我也。

If offerings come because of qualities cultivated in this life, one should reflect: “Through wisdom I may understand the true character of phenomena or cut off the fetters. People make offerings because of these qualities; it has nothing to do with me personally.” Reflecting in this way, one subdues the mind and does not become conceited. What they truly cherish is virtue, not oneself.

譬如罽賓三藏比丘，行阿蘭若法，至一王寺，寺設大會，守門人見其衣服麁弊，遮門不前。如是數數，以衣服弊故，每不得前，便作方便，假借好衣而來，門家見之，聽前不禁；

For example, a bhikṣu from Jibin (罽賓), learned in the Tripiṭaka and practicing forest seclusion, came to a royal monastery holding a great assembly. Seeing his coarse, worn clothing, the gatekeeper barred his way. This happened repeatedly: because his clothing was shabby, he was never allowed to enter. He therefore devised a means, borrowed fine clothes, and returned. Seeing him, the gatekeeper let him enter without obstruction.

既至會坐，得種種好食，先以與衣。眾人問言：「何以爾也？」答言：「我比數來，每不得入，今以衣故得在此坐，得種種好食，實是衣故得之，故以與衣。」

After taking his seat at the assembly and receiving various fine foods, he first offered them to his clothes. People asked, “Why are you doing this?” He replied, “I came here several times but was never allowed in. Now, because of these clothes, I have obtained this seat and all these fine foods. It is really the clothes that obtained them, so I give them to the clothes.”

行者以修行功德，持戒智慧故而得供養，自念：「此為功德，非為我也。」如是思惟，能自伏心，是名為忍。

When a practitioner receives offerings because of cultivated qualities, ethical discipline, and wisdom, they reflect: “This is for the qualities, not for me.” By reflecting in this way, one can subdue the mind. This is called patience.

若虛妄欺偽而得供養，是為自害，不可近也！當自思惟：「若我以此虛妄而得供養，與惡賊劫盜得食無異，是為墮欺妄罪。」如是於三種供養人中，心不愛著，亦不自高，是名生忍。

If offerings are gained through falsehood and deceit, this is self-harm and must not be approached! One should reflect: “If I gain offerings through such falsehood, I am no different from a bandit obtaining food through plunder and theft. I am committing the offense of deception.” Thus, toward those who provide offerings through any of these three conditions, the mind is neither attached nor conceited. This is called patience toward living beings.

Question: For someone who has not yet attained the path, clothing and food are urgent needs. By what means can one attain patience and remain without attachment or craving toward those who provide them?

又如麞鹿為虎搏逐，追之不捨，雖得好草、美水飲食，心無染著；行者亦爾，常為無常虎逐不捨須臾，思惟厭患，雖得美味，亦不染著。是故行者於供養人中，心得自忍。

A practitioner is similar: constantly pursued by the tiger of impermanence, without a moment's respite, one reflects with disenchantment. Even fine flavors bring no attachment. Thus, toward those who make offerings, the practitioner can exercise patience in the mind.

Hearing this verse, the women thought, “He does not understand our pure heavenly bodies, and so speaks this verse.” They transformed themselves back into their original forms, radiating brilliant light throughout the forest. 是時見無常，天人樂皆苦，汝當厭欲樂，愛樂正真道！」

“Then you will see impermanence: the pleasures of the devas are all suffering. You should become disenchanted with sensual pleasures and delight in the true and right path!”

女聞偈已，心念：「此人大智無量，天樂清淨，猶知其惡，不可當也！」即時滅去。菩薩如是觀婬欲樂，能自制心，忍不傾動。

Hearing the verse, the women thought, “This person's great wisdom is immeasurable. Even in pure heavenly pleasures, he recognizes the harm. We cannot overcome him!” They immediately disappeared. Contemplating sensual pleasure in this way, the bodhisattva restrains the mind and remains patient and unmoved.

Contemplations for Restraining Sensual Attachment

Blades, fire, thunder, lightning, enemies, and venomous snakes can still be approached briefly; women, with stinginess, jealousy, anger, deceit, seductiveness, impurity, quarrelsomeness, greed, and envy, should not be approached. 桎梏、枷鎖，閉繫、囹圄，雖曰難解，是猶易開；女鎖繫人，染固根深，無智沒之，難可得脫。眾病之中，女病最重。

坐臥行立，迴眄巧媚；薄智愚人，為之心醉。執劍向敵，是猶可勝；女賊害人，是不可禁。蚖蛇含毒，猶可手捉；女情惑人，是不可觸。

One may still overcome an enemy wielding a sword, but the harm of this female thief cannot be restrained. A venomous snake can still be grasped by hand, but a woman's beguiling affections should not be touched.

有智之人，所應不視；若欲觀之，當如母姊。諦視觀之，不淨填積；婬火不除，為之燒滅！」

“The wise should not gaze at her. If one must look, one should regard her as a mother or sister. Examining closely, one sees an accumulation of impurities. Unless the fire of lust is removed, one will be burned and destroyed by it!”

A great fire that burns people can still be approached; a clear, formless wind could even be grasped; a venomous snake can still be touched, but a woman's heart cannot be reliably known. Why? In this description, women disregard wealth, rank, beauty, reputation, wisdom, virtue, family, skills, eloquence, intimacy, and affection. None of these is in their hearts; they look only to desire. They are like a flood dragon that does not distinguish beauty from ugliness but seeks only to kill.

又復女人不瞻視，憂苦憔悴；給養敬待，憍奢叵制。復次，若在善人之中，則自畜心高；無智人中，視之如怨；富貴人中，追之敬愛；貧賤人中，視之如狗。常隨欲心，不隨功德。

Furthermore, if women are neglected, they become anxious, distressed, and haggard; if supported and honored, they become proud, extravagant, and difficult to restrain. Among good people they grow conceited; they regard the unwise as enemies; they pursue and cherish the wealthy and honored, but regard the poor and lowly as dogs. They constantly follow desire rather than virtue.

When the time came, the princess told her father, “I have an inauspicious condition and must visit the shrine to seek good fortune.” The king said, “Very good!” Five hundred chariots were prepared, and she went to the shrine. 天神思惟：「此不應爾！王為世主，不可令此小人毀辱王女！」即厭此人，令睡不覺。王女既入，見其睡重，推之不悟，即以瓔珞直十萬兩金，遺之而去。

The deity thought, “This should not happen! The king is ruler of the world; this lowly man must not be allowed to dishonor his daughter!” The deity therefore overwhelmed the man and caused him to sleep without waking. 去後，此人得覺，見有瓔珞，又問眾人，知王女來；情願不遂，憂恨懊惱，婬火內發，自燒而死。

This is cited as evidence that a woman's mind follows desire without distinguishing high from low. Furthermore, in the past a king's daughter ran away with a caṇḍāla and had sexual relations with him; an ascetic's daughter also followed a lion. These and similar examples are cited to show that a woman's mind makes no distinctions. Through these various reflections, one removes sensual desire toward women and patiently refrains from attachment.

Thus the Buddha said, “Among all sufferings, the suffering of birth is the gravest.” There are also aging, illness, death, and many other hardships. How could a practitioner add to their suffering? 復次，菩薩自念：「我不應如諸餘人常隨生死水流，我當逆流以求盡源，入泥洹道。一切凡人，侵至則瞋，益至則喜，怖處則畏。我為菩薩，不可如彼，雖未斷結，當自抑制，修行忍辱，惱害不瞋，敬養不喜，眾苦艱難不應怖畏；當為眾生興大悲心！」

I should go against the current, seek its source to the end, and enter the path of nirvāṇa. Ordinary people become angry when harmed, pleased when benefited, and afraid in frightening circumstances. As a bodhisattva, I must not be like them. Though I have not yet severed the fetters, I should restrain myself and cultivate patience: not angry when harmed, not elated by respect and offerings, and not afraid of suffering and hardship. Furthermore, when a bodhisattva sees beings coming to cause trouble, they should think: “These are my close friends and my teachers. I should cherish them all the more and treat them respectfully. 復次，菩薩心知：「如佛所說：『眾生無始，世界無際，往來五道，輪轉無量。』我亦曾為眾生父母、兄弟，眾生亦皆曾為我父母、兄弟；當來亦爾。」以是推之，不應惡心而懷瞋害。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva understands: “As the Buddha said, ‘Living beings have no beginning, and worlds have no limit. Beings come and go through the five destinies, revolving without measure.’ I have been their parent or sibling, and they have all been my parents or siblings. The same will be true in the future.” Considering this, one should not harbor ill will, anger, or the wish to harm.

復次，思惟：「眾生之中，佛種甚多，若我瞋意向之，則為瞋佛；若我瞋佛，則為已了！如說鴿鳥當得作佛，今雖是鳥，不可輕也。」

Furthermore, one reflects: “Among living beings there are many seeds of Buddhahood. If I direct anger toward them, I am angry at Buddhas. If I am angry at Buddhas, I am finished! As it is said, a pigeon will one day become a Buddha. Although a bird now, it must not be despised.”

The Harm of Anger

Chinese Source

復次，諸煩惱中，瞋為最重，不善報中，瞋報最大；餘結無此重罪。如釋提婆那民問佛偈言：「何物殺安隱？何物殺不悔？何物毒之根，吞滅一切善？何物殺而讚？何物殺無憂？」

English Translation

Furthermore, among afflictions, anger is the gravest; among unwholesome results, those of anger are the greatest. Other fetters do not involve such grave wrongdoing. As Śakra, lord of devas, asked the Buddha in verse: “Killing what brings peace? Killing what brings no regret? What is the root of poison, swallowing up all goodness? Killing what is praised? Killing what brings freedom from sorrow?”

佛答偈言：「殺瞋心安隱，殺瞋心不悔；瞋為毒之根，瞋滅一切善；殺瞋諸佛讚，殺瞋則無憂！」

The Buddha answered in verse: “Killing anger brings peace to the mind; killing anger brings no regret. Anger is the root of poison; anger destroys all goodness. Killing anger is praised by Buddhas; killing anger brings freedom from sorrow!”

菩薩思惟：「我今行悲，欲令眾生得樂。瞋為吞滅諸善，毒害一切，我當云何行此重罪？若有瞋恚，自失樂利，云何能令眾生得樂？」

The bodhisattva reflects: “I now practice compassion, wishing to bring happiness to living beings. Anger swallows up wholesome qualities and poisons everything. How could I commit this grave offense? If I become angry, I myself lose happiness and benefit. How, then, could I bring happiness to living beings?”

復次，諸佛菩薩以大悲為本，從悲而出；瞋為滅悲之毒，特不相宜。若壞悲本，何名菩薩？菩薩從何而出？以是之故，應修忍辱。若眾生加諸瞋惱，當念其功德；今此眾生雖有一罪，更自別有諸妙功德；以其功德故，不應瞋。

Furthermore, Buddhas and bodhisattvas are rooted in great compassion and arise from it. Anger is a poison that destroys compassion and is wholly incompatible with it. If that compassionate root is destroyed, how could one be called a bodhisattva? From what could a bodhisattva arise? For this reason, one should cultivate patience. If a being angrily troubles one, recall that being's good qualities. Although they now have one fault, they also possess other excellent qualities. Because of these qualities, one should not be angry.

復次，此人若罵若打，是為治我；譬如金師煉金，垢隨火去，真金獨在；此亦如是。若我有罪，是從先世因緣，今當償之，不應瞋也，當修忍辱！

Furthermore, if this person abuses or strikes me, they are treating my faults. It is like a goldsmith refining gold: fire removes the impurities, leaving only pure gold. This is the same. If I bear wrongdoing from past conditions, I should now repay it without anger and cultivate patience!

復次，菩薩慈念眾生，猶如赤子。閻浮提人多諸憂愁，少有歡日；若來罵詈，或加讒賊，心得歡樂。此樂難得，恣汝罵之！何以故？我本發心，欲令眾生得歡喜故。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva regards living beings with the affection one feels for an infant. People in Jambudvīpa have many sorrows and few happy days. If they find pleasure in coming to abuse, slander, or injure me, such pleasure is hard to obtain: let them abuse me! Why? Because my original aspiration was to bring joy to living beings.

復次，世間眾生，常為眾病所惱，又為死賊常隨伺之，譬如怨家恒伺人便；云何善人而不慈愍復欲加苦？苦未及彼，先自受害。如是思惟，不應瞋彼，當修忍辱。

Furthermore, living beings in the world are constantly troubled by illnesses and pursued by the thief of death, which watches for an opportunity like an enemy. How could a good person lack compassion and wish to add to their suffering? Before the suffering reaches them, one has already harmed oneself. Reflecting in this way, one should not be angry with them but should cultivate patience.

復次，當觀瞋恚，其咎最深。三毒之中，無重此者；九十八使中，此為最堅；諸心病中，第一難治。瞋恚之人，不知善，不知非善；不觀罪福，不知利害；不自憶念，當墮惡道！善言忘失，不惜名稱；不知他惱，亦不自計身心疲惱；瞋覆慧眼，專行惱他。如一五通仙人，以瞋恚故，雖修淨行，殺害一國，如旃陀羅。

Furthermore, one should contemplate anger: its faults are most profound. None of the three poisons is graver; among the ninety-eight latent tendencies it is the strongest; among diseases of the mind it is the hardest to cure. An angry person does not know what is wholesome or unwholesome, does not consider wrongdoing and merit, and does not understand benefit and harm. Forgetting themselves, they will fall into an unfortunate destiny! They forget good counsel and disregard their reputation. They do not recognize others' distress or consider their own bodily and mental exhaustion. Anger covers the eye of wisdom, and they devote themselves to harming others. Thus an ascetic with five supernormal powers, though practicing pure conduct, killed an entire country out of anger, like a caṇḍāla.

復次，瞋恚之人，譬如虎狼，難可共止；又如惡瘡，易發、易壞。瞋恚之人，譬如毒蛇，人不憙見。積瞋之人，惡心漸大，至不可至，殺父、殺君，惡意向佛。

Furthermore, an angry person is like a tiger or wolf, difficult to live with, or like a malignant sore, easily inflamed and ruptured. An angry person is like a venomous snake that people dislike seeing. In one who accumulates anger, ill will gradually grows until it reaches what should never be reached: killing a father or ruler, or directing malice toward the Buddha.

The Quarrel at Kauśāmbī

Chinese Source

如拘睒彌國比丘，以小因緣，瞋心轉大，分為二部。若欲斷當，終竟三月，猶不可了。

English Translation

For example, the bhikṣus of Kauśāmbī became increasingly angry over a minor matter and divided into two factions. Attempts to settle the dispute continued for three months without resolution.

佛來在眾，舉相輪手，遮而告言：「汝諸比丘，勿起鬪諍！惡心相續，苦報甚重！汝求涅槃，棄捨世利，在善法中，云何瞋諍？世人忿諍，是猶可恕，出家之人，何可諍鬪？出家心中，懷毒自害；如冷雲中，火出燒身！」

The Buddha came into the assembly, raised his hand marked with a wheel, and restrained them, saying: “Bhikṣus, do not quarrel! When evil intentions continue, the painful results are very grave. You seek nirvāṇa and have abandoned worldly gain. How can you quarrel in anger within the wholesome Dharma? Worldly people's angry disputes may still be excused, but how can those who have gone forth quarrel? Harboring poison in the mind of renunciation, you harm yourselves, like fire emerging from a cool cloud and burning it!”

諸比丘白佛言：「佛為法王，願小默然！是輩侵我，不可不答！」佛念是人不可度也，於眾僧中凌虛而去，入林樹間寂然三昧。瞋罪如是，乃至不受佛語。以是之故，應當除瞋，修行忍辱。

The bhikṣus replied, “The Buddha is king of the Dharma. Please remain silent a moment! These people have wronged us; we cannot leave them unanswered!” The Buddha thought that these people could not be liberated then. Rising into the air from among the Saṅgha, he departed and entered tranquil samādhi in the forest. Such is the fault of anger: one refuses even the Buddha's words. Thus one should remove anger and cultivate patience.

復次，能修忍辱，慈悲易得；得慈悲者，則至佛道。

Furthermore, those who can cultivate patience readily attain loving-kindness and compassion. Those who attain loving-kindness and compassion reach Buddhahood.

Does Patience Invite Contempt?

Chinese Source

問曰：忍辱法皆好，而有一事不可：小人輕慢，謂為怖畏；以是之故，不應皆忍。

English Translation

Question: The practice of patience is entirely good, except for one thing: petty people despise it, thinking it is fear. For this reason, one should not endure everything.

答曰：若以小人輕慢，謂為怖畏而欲不忍，不忍之罪甚於此也！何以故？不忍之人，賢聖善人之所輕賤；忍辱之人，為小人所慢；二輕之中，寧為無智所慢，不為賢聖所賤。何以故？無智之人，輕所不輕；賢聖之人，賤所可賤。以是之故，當修忍辱。

Answer: If one refuses to be patient because petty people despise it and take it for fear, the fault of impatience is far worse! Why? The impatient are scorned by noble ones and good people; the patient are despised by petty people. Of these two kinds of contempt, it is better to be despised by the unwise than scorned by noble ones. Why? The unwise despise what should not be despised, while noble ones scorn what deserves scorn. For this reason, one should cultivate patience.

復次，忍辱之人，雖不行布施、禪定，而常得微妙功德，生天上、人中，後得佛道。何以故？心柔軟故。

Furthermore, even without practicing giving or meditative concentration, a patient person constantly gains subtle and excellent merit, is born among devas and humans, and later attains Buddhahood. Why? Because their mind is gentle.

復次，菩薩思惟：「若人今世惱我，毀辱、奪利，輕罵、繫縛，且當含忍。若我不忍，當墮地獄鐵垣熱地，受無量苦，燒炙燔煮，不可具說！」以是故，知小人無智，雖輕而貴；不忍用威，雖快而賤。是故菩薩應當忍辱。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “If people in this life trouble me, humiliate me, take away my benefits, insult me, or bind me, I should endure it. If I am not patient, I shall fall into hell, within iron walls and upon burning ground, and undergo immeasurable suffering—scorching, roasting, burning, and boiling—beyond full description!” Thus we know that being despised by petty and ignorant people is still honorable, whereas impatiently imposing one's power, though gratifying, is base. Therefore, a bodhisattva should practice patience.

The Bodhisattva as Healer and Parent

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩思惟：「我初發心，誓為眾生治其心病。今此眾生為瞋恚結使所病，我當治之，云何而復以之自病？應當忍辱！」

English Translation

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “When I first aroused the aspiration, I vowed to cure the illnesses of living beings' minds. This being is now afflicted by the fetter of anger. I should treat it; how could I instead let it make me ill too? I should practice patience!”

譬如藥師療治眾病，若鬼狂病，拔刀罵詈，不識好醜，醫知鬼病，但為治之而不瞋恚；菩薩若為眾生瞋惱罵詈，知其為瞋恚者煩惱所病，狂心所使，方便治之，無所嫌責，亦復如是。

It is like a physician treating illnesses. A patient afflicted by spirit-induced madness may draw a blade and shout abuse, unable to distinguish good from bad. Knowing the illness, the physician simply treats it without anger. Likewise, when beings angrily trouble and abuse a bodhisattva, the bodhisattva knows they are ill with the affliction of anger and driven by a frenzied mind. The bodhisattva skillfully treats them without resentment or blame.

復次，菩薩育養一切，愛之如子；若眾生瞋惱菩薩，菩薩愍之，不瞋、不責。譬如慈父撫育子孫，子孫幼稚未有所識，或時罵詈、打擲，不敬、不畏，其父愍其愚小，愛之愈至，雖有過罪，不瞋、不恚；菩薩忍辱，亦復如是。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva nurtures all beings and loves them as children. When beings angrily trouble a bodhisattva, the bodhisattva pities them without anger or blame. It is like a loving father caring for his children and grandchildren. Being young and without understanding, they may shout abuse, strike him, or throw things, showing neither respect nor fear. Pitying their ignorance and youth, their father loves them all the more. Despite their faults, he is not angry or resentful. A bodhisattva's patience is the same.

復次，菩薩思惟：「若眾生瞋惱加我，我當忍辱。若我不忍，今世心悔，後入地獄，受苦無量；若在畜生，作毒龍、惡蛇、師子、虎、狼；若為餓鬼，火從口出。譬如人被火燒，燒時痛輕，後痛轉重。」

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “If beings angrily trouble me, I should be patient. If I am not, I shall feel remorse in this life and later enter hell, undergoing immeasurable suffering. If born an animal, I may become a venomous dragon, a vicious snake, a lion, a tiger, or a wolf; if a hungry ghost, flames will issue from my mouth. It is like being burned by fire: the pain is slight while it burns, but becomes greater afterward.”

復次，菩薩思惟：「我為菩薩，欲為眾生益利；若我不能忍辱，不名菩薩，名為惡人。」

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “As a bodhisattva, I wish to benefit living beings. If I cannot be patient, I am not called a bodhisattva but an evil person.”

Conditions, Not a Substantial Person

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩思惟：「世有二種：一者、眾生數，二者、非眾生數。我初發心，誓為一切眾生。若有非眾生數：山石、樹木、風寒、冷熱、水雨侵害，但求䘘之，初不瞋恚。今此眾生是我所為，加惡於我，我當受之，云何而瞋？」

English Translation

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “The world has two categories: living beings and things that are not living beings. When I first aroused the aspiration, I vowed to act for all living beings. If nonliving things—mountains, stones, trees, wind, cold, heat, water, or rain—harm me, I simply seek protection and never become angry. These living beings are precisely those for whose sake I act. When they harm me, I should accept it. How could I become angry?”

復次，菩薩知從久遠已來，因緣和合，假名為人，無實人法，誰可瞋者？是中但有骨血、皮肉，譬如累墼；又如木人，機關動作，有去有來。知其如此，不應有瞋！若我瞋者，是則愚癡，自受罪苦。以是之故，應修忍辱。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva knows that, from the distant past onward, conditions have combined and been provisionally called a person. There is no substantial entity of a person. With whom could one be angry? There are only bones, blood, skin, and flesh, like stacked bricks, or like a wooden puppet that comes and goes through the movement of its mechanism. Knowing this, one should not be angry. If I become angry, I am foolish and bring punishment and suffering upon myself. For this reason, one should cultivate patience.

復次，菩薩思惟：「過去無量恒河沙等諸佛，本行菩薩道時，皆先行生忍，然後修行法忍。我今求學佛道，當如諸佛法；不應起瞋恚，如魔界法。」以是故，應當忍辱。如是等種種無量因緣故能忍，是名生忍。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “In the past, Buddhas as numerous as the sands of immeasurable Ganges rivers first practiced patience toward living beings and then cultivated patience concerning phenomena while following the bodhisattva path. Now that I seek to learn the Buddha's path, I should follow their way. I should not arouse anger, which belongs to Māra's realm.” For this reason, one should be patient. Through these and innumerable other conditions, one can be patient. This is called patience toward living beings.
