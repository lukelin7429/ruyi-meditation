Why the Buddha Addresses Śāriputra — Part Sixteen

Chinese Source

〖經〗佛告舍利弗。

English Translation

Sutra: The Buddha addressed Śāriputra.

〖論〗問曰：般若波羅蜜是菩薩摩訶薩法，佛何以故告舍利弗而不告菩薩？

Treatise: Question: Prajñāpāramitā is the teaching for bodhisattva mahāsattvas. Why does the Buddha address Śāriputra rather than a bodhisattva?

答曰：舍利弗於一切弟子中，智慧最第一，如佛偈說：

Answer: Śāriputra is foremost in wisdom among all the disciples. As the Buddha says in verse:

「一切眾生智，唯除佛世尊；欲比舍利弗，智慧及多聞，於十六分中，猶尚不及一。」

‘Of all beings, except the World-Honored Buddha, none can compare with Śāriputra in wisdom and learning. Their wisdom and learning do not reach even one sixteenth of his.’

Śāriputra’s Learning and Early Renown

Chinese Source

復次，舍利弗智慧多聞，有大功德。年始八歲，誦十八部經，通解一切經書義理。是時，摩伽陀國有龍王兄弟：一名姞利，二名阿伽羅。降雨以時，國無荒年，人民感之，常以仲春之月，一切大集至龍住處，為設大會，作樂談義，終此一日。

English Translation

Furthermore, Śāriputra possessed wisdom, extensive learning, and great merit. At only eight years old, he could recite eighteen divisions of scripture and thoroughly understand their teachings. At that time, Magadha had two dragon kings who were brothers, named Jili and Agaluo. They brought timely rain, and the country suffered no years of famine. In gratitude, every year in the second month of spring the people gathered at the dragons’ dwelling for a great festival, playing music and discussing teachings throughout the day.

自古及今，斯集未替，遂以龍名以名此會。此日常法，敷四高座：一為國王，二為太子，三為大臣，四為論士。

This gathering had continued from ancient times and was named after the dragons. By custom, four high seats were set out: one for the king, one for the crown prince, one for the chief minister, and one for a debater.

爾時，舍利弗以八歲之身，問眾人言：「此四高座，為誰敷之？」眾人答言：「為國王、太子、大臣、論士。」是時，舍利弗觀察時人婆羅門等，神情瞻向，無勝己者，便昇論床，結跏趺坐。

Eight-year-old Śāriputra asked, ‘For whom are these four high seats prepared?’ The people answered, ‘For the king, crown prince, chief minister, and debater.’ Observing the brahmins and others present, he saw no one whose bearing suggested an intellect greater than his own. He climbed onto the debater’s seat and sat in full lotus posture.

眾人疑怪，或謂愚小無知，或謂智量過人。雖復嘉其神異，而猶各懷自矜，恥其年小，不自與語，皆遣年少弟子傳言問之。其答酬旨趣，辭理超絕！時諸論師歎未曾有，愚智大小一切皆伏。

The crowd was astonished. Some thought him a foolish, ignorant child; others thought his intelligence extraordinary. Although impressed by his unusual ability, the scholars remained proud. Embarrassed to debate someone so young, they sent their younger pupils to question him instead. His replies were exceptional in meaning, expression, and reasoning. The debaters marveled at this unprecedented ability, and everyone, wise or foolish, young or old, acknowledged his superiority.

王大歡喜，即命有司封一聚落，常以給之。王乘象輿，振鈴告告宣示一切，十六大國，六大城中無不慶悅。

The king rejoiced and ordered officials to grant him a village for his continuing support. Riding an elephant carriage, the king had bells rung and the news proclaimed everywhere. Throughout the sixteen great countries and six great cities, everyone celebrated.

Two Friends Seek the Deathless

Chinese Source

是時，告占師子，名拘律陀，姓大目揵連，舍利弗友而親之。舍利弗才明見重，目揵連豪爽最貴。此二人者，才智相比，德行互同；行則俱遊，住則同止；少長繾綣，結要終始。

English Translation

At that time, Śāriputra befriended Kolita, son of a diviner and a member of the Mahāmaudgalyāyana clan. Śāriputra was respected for his brilliance; Maudgalyāyana was esteemed for his noble, generous character. They were comparable in intelligence and alike in conduct. They traveled and stayed together, remaining close as they grew up and pledging lifelong friendship.

後俱厭世，出家學道，作梵志弟子。情求道門，久而無徵，以問於師。師名刪闍耶，而答之言：「自我求道，彌歷年歲，不知為有道果無耶？我非其人耶？而亦不得！」

Later, both became disenchanted with the world, went forth to seek the path, and became pupils of a brahmin. After a long search without any evidence of attainment, they questioned their teacher, Sañjaya. He replied, ‘I have sought the path for many years, yet I do not know whether its fruit exists. Perhaps I am not suited to it, for I too have not attained it!’

他日其師寢疾，舍利弗在頭邊立，大目連在足邊立，喘喘然其命將終，乃愍爾而笑。二人同心，俱問笑意。師答之言：「世俗無眼，為恩愛所侵。我見金地國王死，其大夫人自投火𧂐，求同一處，而此二人行報各異，生處殊絕。」

One day their teacher fell ill. Śāriputra stood by his head and Maudgalyāyana by his feet. As he lay gasping, near death, he smiled with pity. Both asked why. He replied, ‘Worldly people lack vision and are overcome by attachment. I see that the king of the Land of Gold has died. His chief queen has thrown herself onto his funeral pyre, hoping to be with him. But their actions bring different results, and their places of rebirth are far apart.’

是時二人筆受師語，欲以驗其虛實。後有金地商人，遠來摩伽陀國，二人以䟽驗之，果如師語，乃憮然歎曰：「我等非其人耶？為是師隱我耶？」二人相與誓曰：「若先得甘露，要畢同味。」

They wrote down his words to test their truth. Later, merchants from the Land of Gold came to Magadha. Checking their record against what the merchants reported, they found that their teacher’s words were true. Disheartened, they sighed, ‘Are we unsuited to the path, or did our teacher conceal it from us?’ They pledged to each other, ‘Whoever first obtains the nectar of the deathless must share its taste with the other.’

Hearing the Verse on Causes and Conditions

Chinese Source

是時，佛度迦葉兄弟千人，次遊諸國，到王舍城，頓止竹園。二梵志師聞佛出世，俱入王舍城，欲知消息。

English Translation

The Buddha had by then liberated the Kāśyapa brothers and their thousand followers. Traveling onward through various countries, he reached Rājagṛha and stayed in the Bamboo Grove. Hearing that a Buddha had appeared, the two brahmin teachers entered the city together to learn more.

爾時，有一比丘，名阿說示（五人之一），著衣持鉢，入城乞食。舍利弗見其儀服異容，諸根靜默，就而問言：「汝誰弟子？師是何人？」

A monk named Aśvajit (one of the original five monks) put on his robe, took his bowl, and entered the city for alms. Seeing his distinctive bearing and dress, and the calm restraint of his faculties, Śāriputra approached and asked, ‘Whose disciple are you? Who is your teacher?’

答言：「釋種太子厭老、病、死苦，出家學道，得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，是我師也。」

He answered, ‘The prince of the Śākya clan became disenchanted with the suffering of aging, sickness, and death, went forth to seek the path, and attained unsurpassed complete awakening. He is my teacher.’

舍利弗言：「汝師教授為我說之！」即答偈曰：「我年既幼稚，學日又初淺，豈能宣至真，廣說如來義！」

Śāriputra asked, ‘Please tell me your teacher’s instruction!’ Aśvajit answered in verse: ‘I am young, and my learning is recent and limited. How could I proclaim the highest truth and explain the Tathāgata’s meaning at length?’

舍利弗言：「略說其要！」爾時，阿說示比丘說此偈言：「諸法因緣生，是法說因緣，是法因緣盡，大師如是說。」

Śāriputra said, ‘Briefly state its essence.’ Aśvajit then spoke this verse: ‘Dharmas arise through causes and conditions. Their causes and conditions are taught, as is the ending of those causes and conditions. Thus the great teacher teaches.’

舍利弗聞此偈已，即得初道，還報目連。目連見其顏色和悅，迎謂之言：「汝得甘露味耶？為我說之！」舍利弗即為其說向所聞偈。目連言：「更為重說！」即復為說，亦得初道。

Hearing this verse, Śāriputra immediately attained the first stage of the path and returned to tell Maudgalyāyana. Seeing his peaceful, joyful expression, Maudgalyāyana greeted him: ‘Have you tasted the nectar of the deathless? Tell me!’ Śāriputra repeated the verse. Maudgalyāyana asked to hear it again. After hearing it once more, he too attained the first stage of the path.

Becoming the Buddha’s Foremost Disciples

Chinese Source

二師與二百五十弟子，俱到佛所。佛遙見二人與弟子俱來，告諸比丘：「汝等見此二人，在諸梵志前者不？」諸比丘言：「已見！」佛言：「是二人者，是我弟子中，智慧第一、神足第一弟子。」

English Translation

The two teachers and their two hundred and fifty disciples went together to the Buddha. Seeing them approach, the Buddha asked the monks, ‘Do you see those two leading the brahmins?’ They answered, ‘We do.’ He said, ‘These two will be foremost among my disciples in wisdom and supernatural power.’

大眾俱來，以漸近佛，既到稽首，在一面立，俱白佛言：「世尊！我等於佛法中欲出家受戒！」佛言：「善來比丘！」即時鬚髮自落，法服著身，衣鉢具足，受成就戒。

The group drew near, bowed their heads in homage, and stood to one side. They said, ‘World-Honored One! We wish to go forth and receive ordination in the Buddha Dharma.’ The Buddha said, ‘Come, monks!’ Their hair and beards immediately fell away, Dharma robes covered them, and their robes and bowls were complete. They received full ordination.

過半月後，佛為長爪梵志說法時，舍利弗得阿羅漢道。所以半月後得道者，是人當作逐佛轉法輪師，應在學地，現前自入諸法，種種具知，是故半月後得阿羅漢道。如是等種種功德甚多，是故舍利弗雖是阿羅漢，佛以是般若波羅蜜甚深法，為舍利弗說。

Half a month later, while the Buddha was teaching the brahmin Long Nails, Śāriputra attained arhatship. Why did it take half a month? He was to become a teacher who followed the Buddha in turning the Dharma wheel. While still a trainee, he needed to enter the dharmas directly and know their many aspects completely. Therefore, he attained arhatship after half a month. His many virtues were like these. Thus, although Śāriputra was an arhat, the Buddha taught him the profound Dharma of Prajñāpāramitā.

Why Subhūti Also Teaches Prajñāpāramitā

Chinese Source

問曰：若爾者，何以初少為舍利弗說，後多為須菩提說？若以智慧第一故應為多說，復何以為須菩提說？

English Translation

Question: If so, why is only a little taught to Śāriputra at the beginning, while much more is later taught to Subhūti? If Śāriputra’s foremost wisdom is the reason, he should receive more of the teaching. Why address Subhūti?

答曰：舍利弗佛弟子中智慧第一；須菩提於弟子中，得無諍三昧最第一。無諍三昧相，常觀眾生不令心惱，多行憐愍。諸菩薩者，弘大誓願以度眾生，憐愍相同，是故命說。

Answer: Śāriputra is foremost in wisdom among the Buddha’s disciples. Subhūti is foremost in attaining the samādhi of non-contention. Its character is constantly to consider beings without causing distress and to practice much compassion. Bodhisattvas make vast vows to liberate beings. Because their compassion is alike, the Buddha instructs Subhūti to speak.

復次，是須菩提好行空三昧，如佛在忉利天，夏安居受歲已，還下閻浮提。爾時，須菩提於石窟中住，自思惟：「佛從忉利天來下，我當至佛所耶？不至佛所耶？」又念言：「佛常說：若人以智慧眼觀佛法身，則為見佛中最。」

Furthermore, Subhūti delights in practicing the samādhi of emptiness. After spending the rains retreat in the Heaven of the Thirty-Three and completing its closing observance, the Buddha returned to Jambudvīpa. Subhūti was staying in a rock cave and wondered, ‘The Buddha is descending from the Heaven of the Thirty-Three. Should I go to him or not?’ He then reflected, ‘The Buddha always says that seeing his Dharma body with the eye of wisdom is the highest way of seeing him.’

是時，以佛從忉利天下故，閻浮提中四部眾集，諸天見人，人亦見天。座中有佛，及轉輪聖王、諸天大眾，眾會莊嚴，先未曾有！

As the Buddha descended, the fourfold community gathered in Jambudvīpa. Devas saw humans, and humans saw devas. The Buddha, a wheel-turning sage king, and great assemblies of devas were present. The assembly’s splendor was unprecedented.

須菩提心念：「今此大眾，雖復殊特，勢不久停，磨滅之法，皆歸無常。」因此無常觀之初門，悉知諸法空無有實；作是觀時，即得道證。

Subhūti thought, ‘Though extraordinary, this great assembly will not last. Subject to dissolution, all of it is impermanent.’ Entering through the contemplation of impermanence, he knew all dharmas to be empty and without substantial reality. Through this contemplation he realized the path.

爾時，一切眾人皆欲求先見佛，禮敬供養。有華色比丘尼，欲除女名之惡，便化為轉輪聖王及七寶千子，眾人見之，皆避坐起去。化王到佛所已，還復本身，為比丘尼最初禮佛。

Everyone wished to be first to see the Buddha, bow, and make offerings. The nun Flower Color wished to remove the disadvantage attached to being called a woman. She manifested as a wheel-turning sage king with the seven treasures and a thousand sons. Seeing this, people rose and made way. On reaching the Buddha, she resumed her form as a nun and was the first to bow.

是時，佛告比丘尼：「非汝初禮，須菩提最初禮我。所以者何？須菩提觀諸法空，是為見佛法身，得真供養，供養中最，非以致敬生身為供養也。」以是故言須菩提常行空三昧，與般若波羅蜜空相相應。以是故佛命令說般若波羅蜜。

The Buddha told her, ‘You were not the first to bow. Subhūti bowed to me first. Why? By contemplating dharmas as empty, he saw the Buddha’s Dharma body and made a true offering, the highest offering. Merely paying respects to the body born of parents is not that offering.’ Thus, Subhūti constantly practices the samādhi of emptiness, which accords with the empty character of Prajñāpāramitā. Therefore, the Buddha instructs him to teach it.

復次，佛以眾生信敬阿羅漢諸漏已盡，命之為說，眾得淨信故。諸菩薩漏未盡，若以為證，諸人不信。以是故，與舍利弗、須菩提，共說般若波羅蜜。

Furthermore, beings trust and honor arhats whose influxes have ended. By instructing them to teach, the Buddha enables the assembly to gain pure faith. Bodhisattvas have not yet ended their influxes; if they were presented as witnesses, people would not believe them. Therefore, he teaches Prajñāpāramitā together with Śāriputra and Subhūti.

Śāriputra’s Family and His Father’s Debate

Chinese Source

問曰：何以名舍利弗？為是父母所作字？為是依行功德立名？

English Translation

Question: Why is he called Śāriputra? Did his parents give him this name, or was it established on the basis of his conduct and virtues?

答曰：是父母所作名字。於閻浮提中第一安樂，有摩伽陀國，是中有大城名王舍，王名頻婆娑羅，有婆羅門論議師，名摩陀羅，王以其人善能論故，賜封一邑，去城不遠。是摩陀羅遂有居家，婦生一女，眼似舍利鳥眼，即名此女為舍利。次生一男，膝骨麁大，名拘郗羅。

Answer: It is a name given by his parents. In Magadha, a country of exceptional well-being in Jambudvīpa, lies the great city Rājagṛha. Its king was Bimbisāra. A brahmin debater named Matuoluo (摩陀羅) was so skilled that the king granted him a village near the city. He married and had a daughter whose eyes resembled those of a śāri bird, so he named her Śāri. A son born afterward had large kneecaps and was named Kauṣṭhila.

拘郗羅（秦言大膝也）是婆羅門，既有居家，畜養男女，所學經書，皆已廢忘，又不業新。是時，南天竺有一婆羅門大論議師，字提舍，於十八種大經，皆悉通利。

(Kauṣṭhila means ‘Great Knees.’) Busy with family life and raising his children, the brahmin forgot the scriptures he had studied and learned nothing new. At that time, a great brahmin debater from southern India named Tiṣya had mastered all eighteen great scriptures.

是人入王舍城，頭上戴火，以銅鍱腹。人問其故，便言：「我所學經書甚多，恐腹破裂，是故鍱之。」又問：「頭上何以戴火？」答言：「以大闇故。」眾人言：「日出照明，何以言闇？」答言：「闇有二種：一者、日光不照，二者、愚癡闇蔽。今雖有日明，而愚癡猶黑。」

He entered Rājagṛha carrying a flame on his head and copper plates around his abdomen. Asked why, he said, ‘I have learned so many scriptures that I fear my belly will burst; therefore I bind it.’ Asked about the flame, he replied, ‘Because the darkness is great.’ People said, ‘The sun is shining. Why speak of darkness?’ He answered, ‘There are two kinds: the absence of sunlight and the obscurity of ignorance. Though the sun shines, ignorance remains dark.’

眾人言：「汝但未見婆羅門摩陀羅，汝若見者，腹當縮，明當闇。」是婆羅門逕至鼓邊，打論議鼓。國王聞之，問是何人？眾臣答言：「南天竺有一婆羅門，名提舍，大論議師，欲求論處，故打論鼓。」王大歡喜，即集眾人而告之曰：「有能難者，與之論議！」

They told him, ‘You have simply not met the brahmin Matuoluo. If you did, your belly would shrink and your light would dim.’ He went straight to the debating drum and struck it. Hearing it, the king asked who had come. His ministers said, ‘A great brahmin debater from southern India named Tiṣya seeks a debate.’ Delighted, the king assembled the people and announced, ‘Whoever can challenge him should debate him!’

摩陀羅聞之自疑，「我以廢忘，又不業新，不知我今能與論不？」僶俛而來。於道中見二特牛，方相觝觸，心中作想：「此牛是我，彼牛是彼，以此為占，知誰得勝？」此牛不如，便大愁憂而自念言：「如此相者，我將不如。」欲入眾時，見有母人，挾一瓶水，正在其前，躄地破瓶。復作是念：「是亦不吉。」甚大不樂。

Matuoluo doubted himself: ‘I have forgotten what I studied and learned nothing new. Can I still debate him?’ Reluctantly he set out. On the way he saw two bulls fighting and thought, ‘Let this bull represent me and that one him. This omen will show who wins.’ His bull lost, and he became deeply anxious, thinking it foretold his defeat. Near the assembly, a woman carrying a water jar fell in front of him and broke it. He regarded this too as inauspicious and felt deeply unhappy.

既入眾中，見彼論師，顏貌意色，勝相具足，自知不如。事不獲已，與共論議。論議既交，便墮負處。王大歡喜：「大智明人，遠入我國！」復欲為之封一聚落。

On entering, he saw that his opponent’s face, bearing, and confidence all displayed the signs of victory, and he knew himself outmatched. Having no choice, he debated and was defeated. The king rejoiced: ‘A man of great wisdom and brilliance has come from afar to my country!’ He wished to grant him a village.

諸臣議言：「一聰明人來，便封一邑，功臣不賞，但寵語論，恐非安國全家之道！今摩陀羅論議不如，應奪其封以與勝者；若更有勝人，復以與之。」王用其言，即奪與後人。

The ministers objected: ‘If every clever newcomer receives a village while meritorious ministers go unrewarded, we honor mere argument rather than secure the state and its households. Since Matuoluo has lost, his grant should go to the victor. If another defeats him, let it pass to that person.’ The king accepted their advice and transferred the grant.

是時摩陀羅語提舍言：「汝是聰明人，我以女妻汝，男兒相累；今欲遠出他國，以求本志。」提舍納其女為婦。

Matuoluo told Tiṣya, ‘You are an intelligent man. I give you my daughter in marriage and entrust my son to your care. I now wish to travel abroad to pursue my original aim.’ Tiṣya married his daughter.

The Names Upatiṣya and Śāriputra

Chinese Source

其婦懷妊，夢見一人，身被甲冑，手執金剛，摧破諸山，而在大山邊立。覺已白其夫言，我夢如是。提舍言：「汝當生男，摧伏一切諸論議師，唯不勝一人，當與作弟子。」

English Translation

During her pregnancy, she dreamed of a man wearing armor and holding a vajra, who shattered mountains and stood beside a great mountain. On awakening she told her husband. Tiṣya said, ‘You will bear a son who will defeat all debaters except one. He will become that person’s disciple.’

舍利懷妊，以其子故，母亦聰明，大能論議。其弟拘郗羅，與姊談論，每屈不如；知所懷子，必大智慧，未生如是，何況出生！即捨家學問，至南天竺，不剪指爪，讀十八種經書，皆令通利，是故時人號為長爪梵志。

Because of the child she carried, Śāri herself became brilliant and skilled in debate. Her brother Kauṣṭhila repeatedly lost arguments with her. Realizing that the unborn child must possess great wisdom—already so powerful before birth, and surely more so afterward—he left home to study in southern India. He did not trim his nails while mastering the eighteen scriptures, so people called him the brahmin Long Nails.

姊子既生，七日之後，裹以白疊，以示其父。其父思惟：「我名提舍，逐我名字，字為憂波提舍。」（憂波，秦言逐；提舍，星名）是為父母作字。

Seven days after his sister’s son was born, the baby was wrapped in white cloth and shown to his father. His father thought, ‘My name is Tiṣya. Following my name, I shall call him Upatiṣya.’ (Upa means ‘following’; Tiṣya is the name of a star.) This was the name given by his parents.

眾人以其舍利所生，皆共名之為舍利弗（弗秦言子）。

Because he was born of Śāri, people called him Śāriputra. (Putra means ‘son.’)

復次，舍利弗世世本願，於釋迦文尼佛所作智慧第一弟子，字舍利弗。是為本願因緣名字，以是故名舍利弗。

Furthermore, life after life he had vowed to become Śākyamuni Buddha’s foremost disciple in wisdom, named Śāriputra. His name thus also arose through the causes and conditions of his original vow.

問曰：若爾者，何以不言「憂波提舍」，而但言「舍利弗」？答曰：時人貴重其母，於眾女人中聰明第一，以是因緣故稱舍利弗。

Question: If so, why call him Śāriputra rather than Upatiṣya? Answer: People held his mother in high regard as the most intelligent of women. Therefore, they called him Śāriputra.

Answer: An ‘aspect’ is a gateway of wisdom. Some contemplate dharmas through one gateway, others through two, three, ten, a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand, or asaṃkhyeyas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges. Entering every aspect through all gateways of wisdom and contemplating all dharmas is called ‘every aspect.’

如凡夫人三種觀，欲求離欲、離色故：觀欲、色界麁惡、誑惑、濁重。

Ordinary people wishing to leave sensual desire and form employ three contemplations: they regard the desire and form realms as coarse and harmful, deceptive, and impure and burdensome.

佛弟子八種觀：無常、苦、空、無我、如病、如癰、如箭入體、惱患。是八種觀，入四聖諦中，為十六行之四。十六者，觀苦四種：無常、苦、空、無我；觀苦因四種：集、因、緣、生；觀苦盡四種：盡、滅、妙、出；觀道四種：道、正、行、跡。

The Buddha’s disciples employ eight contemplations: impermanence, suffering, emptiness, non-self, illness, a sore, an arrow lodged in the body, and affliction. These enter the four noble truths and include four of the sixteen aspects. The sixteen are: contemplating suffering as impermanent, suffering, empty, and without self; its origin as accumulation, cause, condition, and arising; its cessation as exhaustion, cessation, excellence, and release; and the path as path, rightness, practice, and the way.

出入息中復有十六行：一、觀入息，二、觀出息，三、觀息長息短，四、觀息遍身，五、除諸身行，六、受喜，七、受樂，八者、受諸心行，九、無作喜，十、心作攝，十一、心作解脫，十二、觀無常，十三、觀散壞，十四、觀離欲，十五、觀滅，十六、觀棄捨。

There are also sixteen aspects of breathing practice: observing inhalation; observing exhalation; observing long and short breaths; observing breath throughout the body; calming bodily activities; experiencing joy; experiencing pleasure; experiencing mental activities; joy without contrivance; collecting the mind; liberating the mind; contemplating impermanence; contemplating dissolution; contemplating dispassion; contemplating cessation; and contemplating relinquishment.

復有六種念：念佛者，佛是多陀阿伽陀、阿羅呵、三藐三佛陀，如是等十號。五念如後說。世智、出世智，阿羅漢、辟支佛、菩薩、佛智，如是等智慧知諸法，名為「一切種」。

There are also six recollections. Recollection of the Buddha recalls his ten titles, beginning with Tathāgata, Arhat, and Fully and Perfectly Awakened One. The other five are explained later. Worldly and supramundane wisdom, and the wisdom of arhats, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas, and Buddhas—knowing dharmas through these kinds of wisdom is called knowing ‘every aspect.’

「一切法」者，識所緣法，是一切法。所謂眼識緣色，耳識緣聲，鼻識緣香，舌識緣味，身識緣觸，意識緣法——緣眼、緣色、緣眼識，耳聲、鼻香、舌味、身觸亦如是，乃至緣意、緣法、緣意識。是名「一切法」，是為識所緣法。

‘All dharmas’ means everything that consciousness takes as its object. Visual consciousness takes forms as objects; auditory consciousness sounds; olfactory consciousness odors; gustatory consciousness tastes; bodily consciousness tangible objects; and mental consciousness dharmas. Mental consciousness can take the eye, forms, and visual consciousness as objects, and likewise the ear and sounds, nose and odors, tongue and tastes, body and tangible objects, through to mind, dharmas, and mental consciousness. These are all dharmas as objects of consciousness.

復次，智所緣法，是一切法。所謂苦智知苦，集智知集，盡智知盡，道智知道，世智知苦、集、盡、道及虛空、非數緣滅，是為智所緣法。

Furthermore, all dharmas are the objects of knowledge. Knowledge of suffering knows suffering; knowledge of its origin knows its origin; knowledge of cessation knows cessation; and knowledge of the path knows the path. Worldly knowledge knows suffering, origin, cessation, and path, as well as space and cessation not obtained through discernment. These are the objects of knowledge.

復次，二法攝一切法：色法、無色法，可見法、不可見法，有對法、無對法，有漏、無漏，有為、無為，心相應、心不相應，業相應、業不相應（丹注云：心法中除思，餘盡相應，業即是思，故除），近法、遠法等。如是種種二法，攝一切法（丹注云：現在及無為是名近法，未來、過去是名遠法）。

Furthermore, paired categories encompass all dharmas: material and immaterial; visible and invisible; resistant and non-resistant; with and without influxes; conditioned and unconditioned; associated and not associated with mind; associated and not associated with karma. (A Dan annotation says: among mental dharmas, volition is excluded; the others are associated. Karma is volition itself, so volition is excluded.) There are also near and distant dharmas. Such pairs encompass all dharmas. (A Dan annotation says: present and unconditioned dharmas are near; future and past dharmas are distant.)

復次，三種法攝一切法：善、不善、無記；學、無學、非學非無學；見諦斷、思惟斷、不斷。復有三種法：五眾、十二入、十八界。持如是等種種三法，盡攝一切法。

Furthermore, threefold classifications encompass all dharmas: wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate; belonging to trainees, those beyond training, and neither; abandoned through seeing the truths, through cultivation, and not abandoned. There are also the three schemes of five aggregates, twelve sense bases, and eighteen elements. These various threefold classifications encompass all dharmas.

復有四種法：過去、未來、現在法、非過去未來現在法；欲界繫法、色界繫法、無色界繫法、不繫法；因善法、因不善法、因無記法、非因善不善無記法；緣緣法、緣不緣法、緣緣不緣緣法、亦非緣緣非不緣緣法。如是等四種法，攝一切法。有五種法：色、心、心相應、心不相應、無為法。如是等種種五法，攝一切法。有六種法：見苦斷法、見習、盡、道斷法、思惟斷法、不斷法。如是等種種六法。乃至無量法攝一切法。是為一切法。

Fourfold classifications also encompass all dharmas: past, future, present, and outside these three times; bound to the desire realm, form realm, formless realm, and unbound; having wholesome, unwholesome, indeterminate causes, and none of these; taking conditioned objects, unconditioned objects, both, and neither. Such fourfold classifications encompass all dharmas. Fivefold classifications include form, mind, dharmas associated with mind, those dissociated from mind, and the unconditioned. Sixfold classifications include what is abandoned through seeing suffering, its origin, cessation, and path, through cultivation, and what is not abandoned. These and innumerable other classifications encompass all dharmas. This is what ‘all dharmas’ means.

問曰：諸法甚深微妙不可思議，若一切眾生尚不能得知，何況一人欲盡知一切法？譬如有人欲量大地，及數大海水渧，欲稱須彌山，欲知虛空邊際，如是等事皆不可知，云何欲以一切種知一切法？

Question: Dharmas are profound, subtle, and inconceivable. Even all beings together cannot know them. How could one person wish to know all dharmas completely? It is like measuring the earth, counting the ocean’s drops, weighing Mount Sumeru, or discovering the limits of space. Since such things cannot be known, how can one wish to know all dharmas in every aspect?

答曰：愚癡闇蔽甚大苦，智慧光明最為樂！一切眾生皆不用苦，但欲求樂。是故菩薩求一切第一大智慧，一切種觀，欲知一切法。是菩薩發大心，普為一切眾生求大智慧，是故欲以一切種知一切法。如醫為一人、二人，用一種、二種藥則足；若欲治一切眾生病者，當須一切種藥。菩薩亦如是，欲度一切眾生故，欲知一切種一切法。如諸法甚深微妙無量，菩薩智慧亦甚深微妙無量。先答破一切智人中已廣說，如函大蓋亦大。

Answer: The darkness of ignorance is great suffering; the light of wisdom is the greatest happiness. Beings reject suffering and seek happiness. Therefore, bodhisattvas seek supreme, great wisdom, contemplating every aspect and wishing to know all dharmas. They arouse expansive intentions and seek wisdom for all beings. A physician treating one or two people may need only one or two medicines; to treat every being’s illnesses requires every kind of medicine. Likewise, to liberate all beings, bodhisattvas wish to know all aspects of all dharmas. Just as dharmas are profound, subtle, and immeasurable, so is bodhisattva wisdom. This was explained fully earlier in answering objections to an all-knowing person: a large box has a correspondingly large lid.

復次，若不以理求一切法，則不可得；若以理求之，則無不得。譬如鑽火以木，則火可得；析薪求火，火不可得。如大地有邊際，自非一切智人，無大神力，則不能知；若神通力大，則知此三千大千世界地邊際。今此大地在金剛上，三千大千世界四邊則虛空，是為知地邊際。欲稱須彌山，亦如是。欲量虛空，非不能量，虛空無法，故不可量。

Furthermore, seeking dharmas without a proper method cannot succeed, whereas seeking them properly can. Fire can be obtained by drilling wood, but not by splitting firewood in search of it. The earth has limits, but someone lacking all-knowledge and great spiritual power cannot know them. With great spiritual power, one knows the boundaries of the land of this great trichiliocosm. This land rests upon vajra, with space on its four sides. That is knowing its boundaries. Weighing Mount Sumeru is similar. As for measuring space, it is not a failure of ability: space has no measurable substance, so it cannot be measured.

〖經〗舍利弗白佛言：「世尊！菩薩摩訶薩云何欲以一切種知一切法，當習行般若波羅蜜？」

Sutra: Śāriputra asked the Buddha, ‘World-Honored One! How should a bodhisattva mahāsattva who wishes to know all dharmas in every aspect train in and practice Prajñāpāramitā?’

〖論〗問曰：佛欲說般若波羅蜜故，種種現神變，現已即應便說，何以故令舍利弗問而後說？答曰：問而後說，佛法應爾。復次，舍利弗知般若波羅蜜甚深，微妙無相之法，難解難知。自以智力種種思惟：「若觀諸法無常，是般若波羅蜜耶？不是耶？」不能自了，以是故問。

Treatise: Question: The Buddha displayed various miracles in preparation for teaching Prajñāpāramitā. Why not teach immediately afterward, instead of waiting for Śāriputra’s question? Answer: Teaching in response to a question is the way of the Buddha Dharma. Furthermore, Śāriputra knew that Prajñāpāramitā is profound, subtle, without marks, and difficult to understand. He reflected in various ways: ‘Is contemplating dharmas as impermanent Prajñāpāramitā, or is it not?’ Unable to resolve this himself, he asked.

復次，舍利弗非一切智，於佛智慧中譬如小兒。如說《阿婆檀那經》中：佛在祇洹住，晡時經行，舍利弗從佛經行。是時有鷹逐鴿，鴿飛來佛邊住，佛經行過之，影覆鴿上，鴿身安隱，怖畏即除，不復作聲。後舍利弗影到，鴿便作聲，戰怖如初。

Furthermore, Śāriputra is not all-knowing. Compared with the Buddha’s wisdom, his is like a child’s. An Avadāna scripture relates that, while staying at Jetavana, the Buddha was walking in meditation in the late afternoon with Śāriputra following him. A pigeon fleeing a hawk came beside the Buddha. As he walked past and his shadow covered it, the pigeon became calm, its fear disappeared, and it stopped crying. When Śāriputra’s shadow reached it, it cried out and trembled as before.

舍利弗白佛言：「佛及我身，俱無三毒，以何因緣佛影覆鴿，鴿便無聲，不復恐怖？我影覆上，鴿便作聲，戰慄如故？」佛言：「汝三毒習氣未盡，以是故，汝影覆時恐怖不除。汝觀此鴿宿世因緣，幾世作鴿？」

Śāriputra asked, ‘Both the Buddha and I are free of the three poisons. Why does your shadow calm the pigeon, while mine leaves it crying and trembling?’ The Buddha replied, ‘Your residual tendencies of the three poisons have not ended. Therefore, your shadow does not remove its fear. Examine its past causes and conditions: for how many lives has it been a pigeon?’

舍利弗即時入宿命智三昧，觀見此鴿從鴿中來，如是一、二、三世，乃至八萬大劫，常作鴿身；過是已往，不能復見。舍利弗從三昧起，白佛言：「是鴿八萬大劫中，常作鴿身；過是已前，不能復知。」

Śāriputra entered the samādhi of knowledge of former lives. He saw that the pigeon had come from another pigeon life, and another, back through eighty thousand great kalpas, always as a pigeon. Beyond that he could not see. Emerging, he told the Buddha, ‘For eighty thousand great kalpas it has always been a pigeon. Earlier than that, I cannot know.’

佛言：「汝若不能盡知過去世，試觀未來世，此鴿何時當脫？」

The Buddha said, ‘If you cannot fully know its past, examine its future. When will it escape this condition?’

舍利弗即入願智三昧，觀見此鴿，一、二、三世，乃至八萬大劫，未脫鴿身；過是已往，亦不能知。從三昧起，白佛言：「我見此鴿從一世、二世、乃至八萬大劫，未免鴿身；過此已往，不復能知！我不知過去、未來齊限，不審此鴿何時當脫？」

Śāriputra entered the samādhi of knowledge through aspiration and saw one, two, three lives, through eighty thousand great kalpas, without escape from a pigeon’s body. Beyond that he could not know. Emerging, he said, ‘I see no escape from being a pigeon for eighty thousand great kalpas. Beyond that I cannot know. I do not know the limits of its past or future. When will it be freed?’

佛告舍利弗：「此鴿除諸聲聞、辟支佛所知齊限，復於恒河沙等大劫中常作鴿身，罪訖得出。輪轉五道中，後得為人，經五百世中，乃得利根。是時有佛，度無量阿僧祇眾生，然後入無餘涅槃。遺法在世，是人作五戒優婆塞，從比丘聞讚佛功德，於是初發心，願欲作佛。然後於三阿僧祇劫，行六波羅蜜，十地具足，得作佛，度無量眾生已而入無餘涅槃。」

The Buddha told him, ‘Beyond the range known to śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, this pigeon will remain a pigeon for great kalpas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges. When its wrongdoing is exhausted, it will escape. Wandering through the five destinies, it will eventually become human. After five hundred human lives, its faculties will become sharp. A Buddha will then liberate immeasurable asaṃkhyeyas of beings and enter nirvāṇa without remainder. During the period of his surviving Dharma, this person will become a lay follower observing the five precepts. Hearing a monk praise the Buddha’s virtues, he will first aspire to Buddhahood. He will then practice the six pāramitās for three asaṃkhyeya kalpas, fulfill the ten stages, become a Buddha, liberate immeasurable beings, and enter nirvāṇa without remainder.’

是時，舍利弗向佛懺悔，白佛言：「我於一鳥尚不能知其本末，何況諸法！我若知佛智慧如是者，為佛智慧故，寧入阿鼻地獄受無量劫苦，不以為難。」如是等，於諸法中不了故問。

Śāriputra then repented before the Buddha: ‘I cannot know the whole story of even one bird, much less all dharmas! Had I known your wisdom was like this, I would willingly have entered Avīci hell and endured immeasurable kalpas of suffering for the sake of that wisdom, without considering it difficult.’ Thus, he asked because his understanding of dharmas was incomplete.

The Perfection of Giving and Explanations of Prajñāpāramitā — Part Seventeen

Chinese Source

〖經〗佛告舍利弗：「菩薩摩訶薩以不住法住般若波羅蜜中，以無所捨法具足檀波羅蜜，施者、受者及財物不可得故。」

English Translation

Sutra: The Buddha told Śāriputra, ‘A bodhisattva mahāsattva abides in Prajñāpāramitā by not abiding in any dharma, and fulfills the perfection of giving without any dharma being relinquished, because giver, recipient, and material gift cannot be apprehended.’

〖論〗問曰：「般若波羅蜜」是何等法？答曰：有人言：無漏慧根是般若波羅蜜相。何以故？一切慧中第一慧是名般若波羅蜜；無漏慧根是第一，以是故，無漏慧根名般若波羅蜜。

Treatise: Question: What dharma is Prajñāpāramitā? Answer: Some say its character is the undefiled faculty of wisdom. Why? Prajñāpāramitā is the foremost wisdom, and the undefiled faculty of wisdom is foremost. Therefore it is called Prajñāpāramitā.

問曰：若菩薩未斷結，云何得行無漏慧？答曰：菩薩雖未斷結，行相似無漏般若波羅蜜，是故得名行無漏般若波羅蜜。譬如聲聞人行暖法、頂法、忍法、世間第一法，先行相似無漏法，後易得生苦法智忍。

Question: If bodhisattvas have not severed their fetters, how can they practice undefiled wisdom? Answer: Though their fetters are not severed, they practice Prajñāpāramitā resembling undefiled wisdom, and are therefore said to practice it. Likewise, śrāvakas practicing warmth, summit, acceptance, and the highest worldly dharma first practice dharmas resembling the undefiled, making it easier afterward to arouse acceptance based on knowledge of suffering as Dharma.

復有人言：菩薩有二種：有斷結使清淨，有未斷結使不清淨。斷結使清淨菩薩，能行無漏般若波羅蜜。

Others say there are two kinds of bodhisattvas: pure ones who have severed the fetters, and impure ones who have not. Those who have severed them can practice undefiled Prajñāpāramitā.

問曰：若菩薩斷結清淨，復何以行般若波羅蜜？答曰：雖斷結使，十地未滿，未莊嚴佛土，未教化眾生，是故行般若波羅蜜。

Question: If a bodhisattva is already purified of fetters, why continue practicing Prajñāpāramitā? Answer: Although the fetters have been severed, the ten stages are not fulfilled, Buddha lands are not adorned, and beings have not been taught. Therefore, the bodhisattva continues practicing.

復次，斷結有二種：一者、斷三毒心，不著人天中五欲；二者、雖不著人天中五欲，於菩薩功德果報五欲未能捨離——如是菩薩，應行般若波羅蜜。

Furthermore, severing fetters has two aspects: first, cutting off the mind of the three poisons and attachment to human and heavenly sense pleasures; second, relinquishing the sense pleasures that result from bodhisattva merit. Even without attachment to human and heavenly pleasures, a bodhisattva may not yet have relinquished the latter and should therefore practice Prajñāpāramitā.

譬如長老阿泥盧豆，在林中坐禪時，淨愛天女等，以淨妙之身來試阿泥盧豆。阿泥盧豆言：「諸姊作青色來，不用雜色。」欲觀不淨，不能得觀；黃、赤、白色，亦復如是。時阿泥盧豆閉目不視，語言：「諸姊遠去！」是時天女即滅不現。

For example, while the elder Aniruddha meditated in a forest, goddesses including Pure Love came in exquisitely beautiful forms to test him. He told them, ‘Sisters, appear in blue, not mixed colors.’ He wished to contemplate impurity but could not. The same happened with yellow, red, and white. He closed his eyes and said, ‘Sisters, go away!’ They disappeared.

天福報形，猶尚如是，何況菩薩無量功德果報五欲？

If forms produced by heavenly merit are like this, how much more so are the sense pleasures resulting from the immeasurable merit of bodhisattvas?

又如甄陀羅王與八萬四千甄陀羅來到佛所，彈琴歌頌，以供養佛。爾時，須彌山王及諸山樹木、人民、禽獸一切皆舞。佛邊大眾，乃至大迦葉，皆於座上不能自安。是時，天須菩薩問長老大迦葉：「耆年舊宿，行十二頭陀法之第一，何以在座不能自安？」

Likewise, a kiṃnara king and eighty-four thousand kiṃnaras came to the Buddha, playing strings and singing praises as offerings. Mount Sumeru, the other mountains, trees, people, birds, and beasts all danced. Even Mahākāśyapa and the assembly beside the Buddha could not sit still. Bodhisattva Heavenly Subhūti asked him, ‘Venerable elder, foremost in the twelve ascetic practices, why can you not remain still in your seat?’

大迦葉言：「三界五欲不能動我；是菩薩神通功德果報力故，令我如是，非我有心不能自安也。譬如須彌山，四邊風起，不能令動；至大劫盡時，毘藍風起，如吹爛草。」以是事故，知二種結中一種未斷；如是菩薩等，應行般若波羅蜜。

Mahākāśyapa replied, ‘The sense pleasures of the three realms cannot move me. It is the power of this bodhisattva’s supernatural abilities and meritorious results that makes me like this, not a restless intention of my own. Winds on all four sides cannot move Mount Sumeru, but at the end of a great kalpa the vairambha wind blows it away like rotten grass.’ Thus, one of these two kinds of fetter may remain unsevered, and such bodhisattvas should practice Prajñāpāramitā.

是阿毘曇中如是說，復有人言：般若波羅蜜是有漏慧。何以故？菩薩至道樹下乃斷結；先雖有大智慧，有無量功德，而諸煩惱未斷。是故言菩薩般若波羅蜜是有漏智慧。

This is explained in the Abhidharma. Others say Prajñāpāramitā is wisdom with influxes, because bodhisattvas sever their fetters only beneath the tree of awakening. Although they previously possess great wisdom and immeasurable merit, their afflictions have not been severed. Therefore their Prajñāpāramitā is called wisdom with influxes.

復有人言：從初發意乃至道樹下，於其中間所有智慧，是名般若波羅蜜。成佛時，是般若波羅蜜轉名薩婆若。

Others call all wisdom from the initial aspiration until sitting beneath the tree of awakening Prajñāpāramitā. On becoming a Buddha, it is renamed all-knowledge.

復有人言：菩薩有漏、無漏智慧，總名般若波羅蜜。何以故？菩薩觀涅槃，行佛道，以是事故，菩薩智慧應是無漏；以未斷結使，事未成辦故，應名有漏。

Others say a bodhisattva’s wisdom, both with and without influxes, is collectively called Prajñāpāramitā. Contemplating nirvāṇa and practicing the Buddha path make it undefiled; yet because fetters remain and the task is unfinished, it should also be called wisdom with influxes.

復有人言：菩薩般若波羅蜜，無漏無為，不可見無對。

Others say a bodhisattva’s Prajñāpāramitā is undefiled, unconditioned, invisible, and non-resistant.

復有人言：是般若波羅蜜，不可得相，若有若無，若常若無常，若空若實。是般若波羅蜜，非陰界入所攝，非有為、非無為，非法、非非法，無取無捨，不生不滅，出有無四句，適無所著。譬如火焰，四邊不可觸，以燒手故；般若波羅蜜相，亦如是不可觸，以邪見火燒故。

Others say its character cannot be apprehended as existing or not existing, permanent or impermanent, empty or substantial. It is not included in aggregates, elements, or sense bases; it is neither conditioned nor unconditioned, neither dharma nor non-dharma. It involves no grasping or relinquishing, arising or ceasing. Beyond the four alternatives concerning existence and nonexistence, it clings nowhere. Like a flame that cannot be touched from any side without burning the hand, Prajñāpāramitā cannot be grasped without being burned by the fire of wrong views.

問曰：上種種人說般若波羅蜜，何者為實？答曰：有人言：各各有理，皆是實。如經說：五百比丘各各說二邊及中道義，佛言皆有道理。

Question: Which of these various explanations is true? Answer: Some say each has its reasoning and all are true. A scripture relates that five hundred monks each explained the two extremes and the middle way, and the Buddha said all their explanations had sound reasoning.

有人言：末後答者為實。所以者何？不可破、不可壞故。若有法如毫氂許有者，皆有過失可破，若言無亦可破。此般若中，有亦無，無亦無，非有非無亦無，如是言說亦無，是名寂滅、無量、無戲論法。是故不可破、不可壞，是名真實般若波羅蜜，最勝無過者。

Others say the last answer is true because it cannot be refuted or destroyed. Asserting even a hair’s breadth of an existent dharma involves faults that can be refuted; asserting nonexistence can also be refuted. In this prajñā, existence is absent, nonexistence is absent, and neither-existence-nor-nonexistence is absent. Even such formulations are absent. This is the peaceful, immeasurable Dharma free of conceptual proliferation. Therefore it cannot be refuted or destroyed and is called true Prajñāpāramitā, supreme and unsurpassed.

Abiding without Grasping

Chinese Source

如轉輪聖王降伏諸敵而不自高；般若波羅蜜亦如是，能破一切語言戲論，亦不有所破。

English Translation

Like a wheel-turning sage king who defeats his enemies without becoming proud, Prajñāpāramitā refutes all verbal and conceptual proliferation without grasping at anything as refuted.

復次，從此已後，品品中種種義門，說般若波羅蜜，皆是實相。以不住法住般若波羅蜜中，能具足六波羅蜜。

Furthermore, the various gateways of meaning through which later sections explain Prajñāpāramitā all concern true reality. By abiding in Prajñāpāramitā without abiding in any dharma, one can fulfill the six pāramitās.

問曰：云何名不住法住般若波羅蜜中，能具足六波羅蜜？答曰：如是菩薩觀一切法，非常非無常，非苦非樂，非空非實，非我非無我，非生滅非不生滅。如是住甚深般若波羅蜜中，於般若波羅蜜相亦不取，是名不住法住；若取般若波羅蜜相，是為住法住。

Question: How does abiding without abiding in any dharma fulfill the six pāramitās? Answer: The bodhisattva contemplates all dharmas as neither permanent nor impermanent, neither suffering nor happiness, neither empty nor substantial, neither self nor non-self, neither arising and ceasing nor free from arising and ceasing. Abiding in profound Prajñāpāramitā in this way, one does not grasp even its character. This is abiding without abiding in dharmas. Grasping its character would be abiding through attachment to a dharma.

問曰：若不取般若波羅蜜相，心無所著，如佛所言：「一切諸法，欲為其本。」若不取者，云何得具足六波羅蜜？

Question: If one does not grasp Prajñāpāramitā and the mind clings nowhere, how can the six pāramitās be fulfilled? The Buddha says that aspiration is the basis of all dharmas.

答曰：菩薩憐愍眾生故，先立誓願，我必當度脫一切眾生。以精進波羅蜜力故，雖知諸法不生不滅如涅槃相，復行諸功德，具足六波羅蜜。所以者何？以不住法住般若波羅蜜中故。是名「不住法住般若波羅蜜中」。

Answer: Out of compassion for beings, the bodhisattva first vows, ‘I will certainly liberate all beings.’ Through the power of diligence, although knowing dharmas as neither arising nor ceasing, like nirvāṇa, one still cultivates merit and fulfills the six pāramitās. This is possible because one abides in Prajñāpāramitā without abiding in any dharma.

The Benefits of Giving — Part Eighteen

Chinese Source

問曰：檀有何等利益故，菩薩住般若波羅蜜中，檀波羅蜜具足滿？答曰：檀有種種利益：檀為寶藏，常隨逐人；檀為破苦，能與人樂；檀為善御，開示天道；檀為善府，攝諸善人（施攝善人，與為因緣，故言「攝」）；檀為安隱，臨命終時心不怖畏；檀為慈相，能濟一切；檀為集樂，能破苦賊；檀為大將，能伏慳敵；檀為妙果，天人所愛；檀為淨道，賢聖所遊；檀為積善福德之門；檀為立事聚眾之緣；檀為善行愛果之種；檀為福業善人之相；檀破貧窮、斷三惡道；檀能全護福樂之果；檀為涅槃之初緣；入善人聚中之要法；稱譽讚歎之淵府；入眾無難之功德；心不悔恨之窟宅；善法道行之根本；種種歡樂之林藪；富貴安隱之福田；得道涅槃之津梁；聖人大士智者之所行；餘人儉德寡識之所效。

English Translation

Question: What benefits does giving have, such that bodhisattvas abiding in Prajñāpāramitā fulfill its perfection? Answer: Giving has many benefits. It is a treasure that follows a person, destroys suffering, and brings happiness. It is a good guide showing the road to heaven, and a treasury gathering good people. (Giving gathers them by forming connections with them.) It brings safety and freedom from fear at death. It expresses loving-kindness and helps everyone, gathers happiness and defeats the robbers of suffering, and is a great general overcoming stinginess. It bears wondrous fruit loved by devas and humans and is a pure path traveled by noble ones. It is a gateway to accumulated goodness and merit, a condition for establishing endeavors and bringing people together, and a seed of wholesome conduct and desirable results. It marks the meritorious deeds of good people, breaks poverty, closes the three bad destinies, and safeguards the fruits of merit and happiness. It is an initial condition for nirvāṇa, an essential means of joining the good, a treasury of praise, and the virtue of entering assemblies without difficulty. It shelters the mind from regret, grounds wholesome practice, and is a forest of many joys, a field of merit for prosperity and safety, and a bridge to the path and nirvāṇa. Noble ones, great beings, and the wise practice it; those of little virtue and understanding emulate them.

復次，譬如失火之家，黠慧之人，明識形勢，及火未至，急出財物；舍雖燒盡，財物悉在，更修室宅。好施之人，亦復如是，知身危脆，財物無常，修福及時，如火中出物；後世受樂，亦如彼人更修宅業，福慶自慰。

When a house catches fire, a clever person understands the danger and quickly carries out possessions before the flames reach them. Though the house burns down, the possessions survive, and another dwelling can be built. Likewise, a generous person knows the body is fragile and wealth impermanent, and cultivates merit while there is time, like rescuing belongings from a fire. Happiness in future lives is like rebuilding the house, sustained by the blessings of merit.

愚惑之人，但知惜屋，怱怱營救，狂愚失智，不量火勢，猛風絕焰，土石為焦，翕響之間，蕩然夷滅。屋既不救，財物亦盡，飢寒凍餓，憂苦畢世。

A deluded person thinks only of saving the house and frantically struggles to rescue it. Losing judgment, that person fails to gauge the flames. Fierce winds fan the fire until even earth and stone are scorched, and everything is suddenly destroyed. Neither house nor possessions are saved, leaving a life of hunger, cold, anxiety, and suffering.

慳惜之人，亦復如是，不知身命無常，須臾叵保，而更聚歛守護愛惜，死至無期，忽焉逝沒，形與土木同流，財與委物俱棄，亦如愚人憂苦失計。

Likewise, the miser does not understand that life is impermanent and cannot be secured even briefly. Instead, one accumulates, guards, and cherishes wealth. Death comes unexpectedly; the body becomes like earth and wood, and possessions are abandoned like discarded objects. This is like the fool whose mistaken plan brings grief.

復次，大慧之人，有心之士，乃能覺悟，知身如幻，財不可保，萬物無常，唯福可恃，將人出苦，津通大道。復次，大人大心，能大布施，能自利己；小人小心，不能益他，亦不自厚。

Furthermore, wise and thoughtful people awaken to the fact that the body is like an illusion, wealth cannot be secured, and everything is impermanent. Only merit can be relied upon to lead one out of suffering toward the great path. Great people with expansive hearts can give greatly and benefit themselves. Small-minded people neither benefit others nor enrich themselves.

復次，譬如勇士見敵，必期吞滅；智人慧心，深得悟理，慳賊雖強，亦能挫之，必令如意。遇良福田，值好時節（時：應施之時也。遇而不施是名失時），覺事應心，能大布施。

A brave warrior meets an enemy determined to defeat it. Likewise, a wise person who deeply understands the principle can subdue even the powerful robber of stinginess and act as intended. Encountering a good field of merit at a suitable time, one recognizes the opportunity and gives greatly. (A suitable time means a time when giving is appropriate; failing to give then is missing the opportunity.)

復次，好施之人，為人所敬，如月初出，無不愛者；好名善譽，周聞天下，人所歸仰，一切皆信。好施之人，貴人所念，賤人所敬；命欲終時，其心不怖。如是果報，今世所得，譬如樹華。大果無量，後世福也。生死輪轉，往來五道，無親可恃，唯有布施若生天上、人中，得清淨果，皆由布施；象、馬畜生得好櫪養，亦是布施之所得也。布施之德，富貴歡樂；持戒之人，得生天上；禪智心淨，無所染著，得涅槃道。布施之福，是涅槃道之資糧也；念施故歡喜，歡喜故一心，一心觀生滅無常，觀生滅無常故得道。如人求蔭故種樹，或求華，或求果故種樹；布施求報亦復如是，今世、後世樂如求蔭，聲聞、辟支佛道如華，成佛如果。是為檀種種功德。

Generous people are respected and loved like the newly risen moon. Their good reputation spreads everywhere; people trust and look up to them. The high-ranking remember them, the lowly honor them, and they are fearless at death. These present-life results are like a tree’s flowers; immeasurable great fruit is the merit of future lives. Wandering through the five destinies, one has no relative to rely upon: giving alone provides support. Pure rewards among devas and humans arise from giving; even elephants and horses receiving good shelter and care obtain it through giving. Giving brings prosperity and joy; keeping precepts brings heavenly birth; meditation and wisdom purify the mind and lead without attachment to nirvāṇa. The merit of giving supplies provisions for that path. Recollecting giving brings joy, joy collects the mind, and a collected mind contemplates arising, ceasing, and impermanence, thereby attaining the path. Planting a tree may be for shade, flowers, or fruit. Likewise, the rewards sought through giving may be happiness now and later, like shade; śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha attainment, like flowers; or Buddhahood, like fruit. These are the many virtues of giving.

The Character of Giving — Part Nineteen

Chinese Source

問曰：云何名檀？答曰：檀名布施。心相應善思，是名為檀。有人言：從善思起身、口業，亦名為檀。有人言：有信、有福田、有財物，三事和合時，心生捨法，能破慳貪，是名為檀。譬如慈法，觀眾生樂而心生慈；布施心數法，亦復如是，三事和合，心生捨法，能破慳貪。

English Translation

Question: What is dāna? Answer: Dāna means giving. Wholesome volition associated with mind is called giving. Some also include the bodily and verbal actions arising from that volition. Others say that when faith, a field of merit, and possessions come together, relinquishment arises in the mind and breaks stinginess and greed: this is giving. Just as loving-kindness arises when contemplating beings’ happiness, the mental factor of giving arises when these three conditions meet, producing relinquishment and overcoming stinginess and greed.

檀有三種：或欲界繫，或色界繫，或不繫。（丹本注云：聖人行施故名不繫）心相應法，隨心行，共心生，非色法，能作緣。非業，業相應，隨業行，共業生，非先世業報生。二種修：行修、得修。二種證：身證、慧證。若思惟斷，若不斷，二見斷：欲界、色界盡見斷。有覺有觀法；凡夫、聖人共行。如是等，阿毘曇中廣分別說。

Giving may be bound to the desire realm, bound to the form realm, or unbound. (A Dan-edition annotation says that giving practiced by noble ones is called unbound.) It is associated with mind, follows and arises together with mind, is immaterial, and can serve as a condition. It is not karma itself, but is associated with karma, follows and arises together with it, and is not produced as a result of past karma. Its two modes of cultivation are cultivation through practice and through acquisition; its two modes of realization are bodily realization and realization through wisdom. It may be abandoned through cultivation or not abandoned; regarding abandonment through seeing, there are the desire-realm and form-realm categories. It involves initial and sustained mental application and is practiced by ordinary and noble people alike. The Abhidharma analyzes these matters extensively.

復次，施有二種：有淨，有不淨。不淨施者，直施無所為。或有為求財故施，或愧人故施，或為嫌責故施，或畏懼故施，或欲取他意故施，或畏死故施，或狂人令喜故施，或自以富貴故應施，或諍勝故施，或妬瞋故施，或憍慢自高故施，或為名譽故施，或為呪願故施，或解除衰求吉故施，或為聚眾故施，或輕賤不敬施。如是等種種，名為不淨施。淨施者，與上相違，名為淨施。

Furthermore, giving may be pure or impure. Impure giving includes giving without a considered purpose, to gain wealth, from embarrassment, to avoid blame, from fear, to win someone over, from fear of death, or to please a disturbed person. One may give because wealth and status seem to require it, to outdo another, from jealousy or anger, from pride, for reputation, for blessings, to remove misfortune and obtain good fortune, to gather followers, or with contempt and disrespect. Such giving is impure. Pure giving is the opposite.

復次，為道故施，清淨心生，無諸結使，不求今世後世報，恭敬憐愍故，是為淨施。淨施是趣涅槃道之資糧，是故言為道故施。若未得涅槃時施，是人天報樂之因。

Furthermore, pure giving arises from a pure mind for the sake of the path, free from fetters, seeking no reward in this or future lives, and motivated by respect and compassion. It supplies provisions for the path to nirvāṇa, hence the expression ‘giving for the path.’ Before nirvāṇa is attained, it also causes happy rewards among humans and devas.

淨施者，如華瓔珞，初成未壞，香潔鮮明；為涅槃淨施，得果報香，亦復如是。如佛說：「世有二人為難得：一者出家中非時解脫比丘，二者在家白衣能清淨布施。」

Pure giving resembles a newly made flower garland, fragrant, fresh, and intact. Giving purely for nirvāṇa likewise bears fragrant fruit. As the Buddha says, ‘Two kinds of people are hard to find: among monastics, a monk whose liberation does not depend on favorable occasions; among householders, someone who gives purely.’

是淨施相，乃至無量世，世世不失，譬如券要，終無失時。是布施果，因緣和合時便有。譬如樹得時節會，便有華葉果實；若時節未至，有因而無果。

The character of pure giving is not lost through immeasurable lives, like a deed of agreement that never fails. Its fruit appears when causes and conditions meet. A tree produces flowers, leaves, and fruit when the season arrives; before then, the cause exists but the fruit has not appeared.

是布施法，若以求道，能與人道。何以故？結使滅名涅槃。當布施時，諸煩惱薄故，能助涅槃。於所施物中不惜故除慳，敬念受者故除嫉妬，直心布施故除諂曲，一心布施故除調，深思惟施故除悔，觀受者功德故除不恭敬，自攝心故除不慚，知人好功德故除不愧，不著財物故除愛，慈愍受者故除瞋，恭敬受者故除憍慢，知行善法故除無明，信有果報故除邪見，知決定有報故除疑。如是等種種不善諸煩惱，布施時悉皆薄，種種善法悉皆得。

Giving directed toward the path can bring attainment. Why? Nirvāṇa is the ending of fetters. Giving weakens afflictions and thus assists nirvāṇa. Not begrudging the gift removes stinginess; respecting the recipient removes jealousy; straightforward giving removes deceit; single-minded giving removes restlessness; thoughtful giving removes regret; considering the recipient’s virtues removes disrespect; restraining the mind removes shamelessness; recognizing others’ esteem for virtue removes lack of moral concern; nonattachment to possessions removes craving; compassion removes anger; respect removes pride; knowing wholesome action removes ignorance; faith in results removes wrong views; and certainty about results removes doubt. During giving, these unwholesome afflictions weaken and wholesome dharmas are obtained.

布施時六根清淨，善欲心生，善欲心生故內心清淨；觀果報功德故信心生，身心柔軟故喜樂生，喜樂生故得一心，得一心故實智慧生。如是等諸善法悉皆得。

During giving, the six faculties become pure, wholesome aspiration arises, and the inner mind is purified. Considering the merits of the result brings faith; bodily and mental gentleness brings joy and happiness; these bring collectedness, and collectedness brings true wisdom. Such wholesome dharmas are all obtained.

復次，布施時心生相似八正道：信布施果故得正見，正見中思惟不亂故得正思惟，清淨說故得正語，淨身行故得正業，不求報故得正命，懃心施故得正方便，念施不廢故得正念，心住不散故得正定。如是等相似三十七品善法，心中生。

Furthermore, giving produces qualities resembling the noble eightfold path. Faith in its results brings right view; undistracted reflection within that view brings right intention; pure speech brings right speech; pure bodily conduct brings right action; not seeking reward brings right livelihood; diligent giving brings right effort; uninterrupted recollection brings right mindfulness; and a steady, undispersed mind brings right concentration. In this way, qualities resembling the thirty-seven aids to awakening arise in the mind.

Giving and the Thirty-Two Marks

Chinese Source

復次，有人言：布施是得三十二相因緣。

English Translation

Furthermore, some say giving causes the thirty-two marks. Firm resolve in giving produces well-planted feet. Giving accompanied by five factors, with recipients forming a retinue through karmic connections, produces wheels beneath the feet. Courageous giving produces broad, level heels; gathering people through giving produces webbed hands and feet. Offering fine food produces soft hands and feet and fullness in seven places. Gifts sustaining life produce long fingers and an upright body. Promising to give and increasing generosity produce high insteps and upward-turning hair. Attentively hearing requests and ensuring prompt delivery produce antelope-like calves. Neither resenting nor despising petitioners produces arms reaching below the knees. Anticipating wishes without waiting to be asked produces the concealed organ. Giving fine clothing, bedding, gold, silver, and jewels produces golden skin and delicate skin. Letting recipients freely use the gift produces one hair in each pore and the white tuft between the brows. Immediately promising a requested gift produces a lion-like upper body and rounded shoulders. Giving medicine to the sick and food and drink to the hungry and thirsty produces filled armpits and supreme taste. Encouraging and reassuring others in giving produces the crown protuberance and a body proportioned like a banyan tree. Gentle, truthful promises unfailingly fulfilled produce a broad, long tongue, a Brahmā-like voice, and a voice like the kalaviṅka bird. Truthful, beneficial speech produces lion-like cheeks. Respectful giving with a pure mind produces white teeth and even teeth. Truthful, harmonious speech produces closely set teeth and forty teeth. Looking equally upon recipients without anger or attachment produces blue eyes and eyelashes like a bull’s. These are causes of the thirty-two marks.

所以者何？施時與心堅固，得足下安立相；布施時五事圍繞，受者是眷屬業因緣故，得足下輪相；大勇猛力施故，得足跟廣平相；施攝人故，得手足縵網相；美味飲食施故，得手足柔軟、七處滿相；施以益命故，得長指、身不曲、大直相；施時言我當相與，施心轉增故、得足趺高、毛上向相；施時受者求之，一心好聽，慇懃約勅，令必疾得故，得伊泥延𨄔相；不瞋不輕求者故，得臂長過膝相；如求者意施，不待言故，得陰藏相；好衣服、臥具、金銀、珍寶施故，得金色身相、薄皮相；布施令受者獨得自在用故，得一一孔一毛生、眉間白毫相；求者求之，即言當與，以是業故得上身如師子、肩圓相；病者施藥，飢渴者與飲食故，得兩腋下滿、最上味相；施時勸人行施而安慰之，開布施道故，得肉髻相、身圓如尼拘盧相；有乞求者，意欲與時，柔軟實語，必與不虛故，得廣長舌相、梵音聲相、如迦陵毘伽鳥聲相；施時如實語，利益語故，得師子頰相；施時恭敬受者，心清淨故，得牙白、齒齊相；施時實語、和合語故，得齒密相、四十齒相；施時不瞋不著，等心視彼故，得青眼相、眼𥇒如牛王相。是為種三十二相因緣。復次，以七寶、人民、車乘、金銀、燈燭、房舍、香華布施故，得作轉輪王，七寶具足。

Furthermore, giving the seven jewels, attendants, vehicles, gold, silver, lamps, dwellings, incense, and flowers leads to becoming a wheel-turning king endowed with the seven treasures.

復次，施得時故，報亦增多。如佛說：「施遠行人，遠來人，病人，看病人，風寒眾難時施，是為時施。」

Furthermore, giving at the right time increases its reward. The Buddha says, ‘Giving to travelers departing or arriving, to the sick and their caregivers, and during hardship caused by wind and cold is timely giving.’

復次，布施時隨土地所須施故，得報增多。復次，曠路中施故，得福增多；常施不廢故，得報增多；如求者所欲施故，得福增多；施物重故，得福增多，如以精舍、園林、浴池等；若施善人故，得報增多；若施僧故，得報增多；若施者、受者俱有德故（丹注云：如菩薩及佛慈心布施，是為施者；若施佛及菩薩、阿羅漢、辟支佛是為受者故），得報增多；種種將迎恭敬受者故，得福增多；難得物施故，得福增多；隨所有物盡能布施故，得福增多。譬如大月氏弗迦羅城中，有一畫師，名千那。到東方多剎陀羅國，客畫十二年，得三十兩金，持還本國。於弗迦羅城中，聞打鼓作大會聲，往見眾僧，信心清淨，即問維那：「此眾中幾許物得作一日食？」維那答曰：「三十兩金足得一日食。」即以所有三十兩金付維那，「為我作一日食，我明日當來。」

Giving what a region needs also increases rewards. So do giving along desolate roads, giving continually, meeting petitioners’ wishes, giving valuable things such as monasteries, gardens, and bathing pools, giving to good people or the Saṅgha, and virtue in both giver and recipient. (A Dan annotation identifies compassionate giving by bodhisattvas and Buddhas with virtuous givers, and offerings to Buddhas, bodhisattvas, arhats, and pratyekabuddhas with virtuous recipients.) Respectful welcome, rare gifts, and giving everything one has also increase merit. For example, a painter named Qianna from the city of Fuluojialuo in the Great Yuezhi traveled east to Duochatuoluo. After twelve years painting there, he earned thirty taels of gold and returned home. Hearing drums announcing a great gathering, he visited the Saṅgha and developed pure faith. He asked its steward what one day’s meal would cost. The answer was thirty taels of gold. He gave all he had, saying, ‘Provide a meal for me to offer. I will come tomorrow.’

空手而歸，其婦問曰：「十二年作得何等物？」答言：「我得三十兩金。」即問：「三十兩金今在何所？」答言：「已在福田中種。」婦言：「何等福田？」答言：「施與眾僧。」婦便縛其夫送官治罪，斷事大官問：「以何事故？」婦言：「我夫狂癡，十二年客作得三十兩金，不憐愍婦兒，盡以與他人；依如官制，輒縛送來。」

Returning empty-handed, he told his wife that his earnings had been planted in a field of merit by giving them to the Saṅgha. She bound him and took him before an official, accusing him of foolishly giving away twelve years’ earnings without caring for his wife and children.

大官問其夫：「汝何以不供給婦兒，乃以與他？」答言：「我先世不行功德，今世貧窮，受諸辛苦；今世遭遇福田，若不種福，後世復貧，貧貧相續，無得脫時。我今欲頓捨貧窮，以是故，盡以金施眾僧。」

Asked why, he replied, ‘Because I cultivated no merit before, I am poor and suffer hardship now. If I fail to plant merit when I encounter a field of merit, poverty will continue in future lives without escape. Wishing to relinquish poverty completely, I gave all the gold to the Saṅgha.’

大官是優婆塞，信佛清淨，聞是語已，讚言：「是為甚難！懃苦得此少物，盡以施僧，汝是善人！」即脫身瓔珞及所乘馬，并一聚落，以施貧人，而語之言：「汝始施眾僧，眾僧未食，是為穀子未種，牙已得生，大果方在後身！」以是故言「難得之物盡用布施其福最多」。

The official, a lay Buddhist with pure faith, praised him: ‘How difficult! You worked hard for this little wealth and gave it all to the Saṅgha. You are a good person!’ He gave the poor man his own ornaments, horse, and a village, saying, ‘You have just made the offering, and the Saṅgha has not yet eaten. Before the seed is planted, its shoot has already appeared! The great fruit awaits a future life.’ Thus giving all of something difficult to obtain brings the greatest merit.

Worldly and Supramundane Giving

Chinese Source

復次，有世間檀，有出世間檀；有聖人所稱譽檀，有聖人所不稱譽檀；有佛菩薩檀，有聲聞檀。

English Translation

Furthermore, there is worldly and supramundane giving, giving praised and not praised by noble ones, and the giving of Buddhas and bodhisattvas as distinct from that of śrāvakas.

何等世間檀？凡夫人布施，亦聖人作有漏心布施，是名世間檀。復次，有人言：凡夫人布施，是為世間檀。聖人雖有漏心布施，以結使斷故，名出世間檀。何以故？是聖人得無作三昧故。

What is worldly giving? Giving by ordinary people, and giving by noble ones with a mind affected by influxes, is called worldly giving. Some, however, say only ordinary people’s giving is worldly. Even when noble ones give with a mind affected by influxes, their giving is supramundane because their fetters have been severed and they have attained the samādhi of wishlessness.

復次，世間檀者不淨，出世間檀者清淨。二種結使：一種屬愛，一種屬見。為二種結使所使，是為世間檀。無此二種結使，是為出世間檀。

Furthermore, worldly giving is impure and supramundane giving pure. Fetters are of two kinds: those belonging to craving and those belonging to views. Giving driven by either is worldly; giving without them is supramundane.

若三礙繫心，是為世間檀。何以故？因緣諸法實無吾我，而言我與彼取，是故名世間檀。復次，我無定處。我以為我，彼以為非；彼以為我，我以為非。以是不定故，無實我也。所施財者，從因緣和合有，無有一法獨可得者。如絹、如布，眾緣合故成，除絲除縷，則無絹、布；諸法亦如是，一相無相，相常自空。人作想念，計以為有，顛倒不實，是為世間檀。心無三礙，實知法相，心不顛倒，是為出世間檀。

When the three obstructions bind the mind, giving is worldly. Why? Conditioned dharmas contain no real self, yet one thinks, ‘I give and that person receives.’ Moreover, self has no fixed location: what I call myself, another does not; what another calls himself, I do not. Because it is indeterminate, there is no substantial self. The gift too exists through combined causes and conditions, with no independently obtainable dharma. Silk and cloth exist through assembled conditions; remove the threads and there is no silk or cloth. Likewise, all dharmas share the character of being without fixed characteristics, always empty of themselves. Imagining them to exist substantially is an unreal inversion: this is worldly giving. A mind free of the three obstructions, truly knowing dharmas without inversion, practices supramundane giving.

出世間檀為聖人所稱譽，世間檀聖人所不稱譽。

Supramundane giving is praised by noble ones; worldly giving is not.

復次，清淨檀，不雜結垢，如諸法實相，是聖人所稱譽；不清淨，雜結使，顛倒心著，是聖人所不稱譽。

Furthermore, pure giving, unmixed with the stains of fetters and in accord with true reality, is praised by noble ones. Impure giving mixed with fetters and attachment through an inverted mind is not.

復次，實相智慧和合布施，是聖人所稱譽；若不爾者，聖人所不稱譽。

Giving united with wisdom concerning true reality is praised by noble ones; giving otherwise is not.

復次，不為眾生，亦不為知諸法實相故施，但求脫生、老、病、死，是為聲聞檀；為一切眾生故施，亦為知諸法實相故施，是為諸佛菩薩檀。

Giving merely to escape birth, aging, sickness, and death, rather than for beings or knowledge of true reality, is śrāvaka giving. Giving for all beings and for knowledge of the true reality of dharmas is the giving of Buddhas and bodhisattvas.

於諸功德不能具足，但欲得少許分，是為聲聞檀；一切諸功德欲具足滿，是為諸佛菩薩檀。

Seeking only a small share of merit rather than its fulfillment is śrāvaka giving. Seeking complete fulfillment of every virtue is the giving of Buddhas and bodhisattvas.

畏老、病、死故施，是為聲聞檀；為助佛道，為化眾生，不畏老、病、死，是為諸佛菩薩檀。

Giving out of fear of aging, sickness, and death is śrāvaka giving. Giving to support the Buddha path and teach beings without fearing aging, sickness, or death is the giving of Buddhas and bodhisattvas.

Velāma’s Great Offering

Chinese Source

是中應說菩薩本生經，如說《阿婆陀那經》中：昔閻浮提中有王，名婆薩婆。爾時有婆羅門菩薩，名韋羅摩，是國王師，教王作轉輪聖王法。

English Translation

Here accounts of the bodhisattva’s former births should be related. An Avadāna scripture tells of a king named Vāsava in Jambudvīpa. A brahmin bodhisattva named Velāma was his teacher and instructed him in the conduct of a wheel-turning sage king.

韋羅摩財富無量，珍寶具足，作是思惟：「人謂我為貴人，財富無量，饒益眾生，今正是時，應當大施。富貴雖樂，一切無常，五家所共，令人心散，輕泆不定，譬如獼猴不能暫住；人命逝速，疾於電滅，人身無常，眾苦之藪。以是之故，應行布施。」

Velāma possessed immeasurable wealth and abundant jewels. He thought, ‘People consider me honored and immensely wealthy. Now is the time to benefit beings through great giving. Though wealth and status are pleasant, they are impermanent and shared with five claimants. They scatter the mind, making it restless and unstable like a monkey unable to pause. Life passes faster than a lightning flash. The body is impermanent, a thicket of suffering. Therefore, I should give.’

如是思惟已，自作手疏，普告閻浮提諸婆羅門及一切出家人：「願各屈德，來集我舍，欲設大施。」滿十二歲，飯汁行船，以酪為池，米麵為山，蘇油為渠，衣服、飲食、臥具、湯藥，皆令極妙。

He wrote invitations to brahmins and all who had gone forth throughout Jambudvīpa: ‘Please graciously assemble at my house. I wish to make a great offering.’ For twelve years, rice water flowed deep enough for boats, curds formed ponds, rice and flour formed mountains, and clarified butter and oil formed channels. Clothing, food, bedding, and medicines were all of the finest quality.

過十二歲，欲以布施八萬四千白象，犀甲金飾，珞以名寶，建大金幢，四寶莊嚴；八萬四千馬，亦以犀甲金飾，四寶交絡；八萬四千車，皆以金、銀、琉璃、頗梨、寶飾，覆以師子、虎豹之皮，若白劍婆羅寶𨏥雜飾，以為莊嚴；八萬四千四寶床，雜色綩綖種種茵蓐，柔軟細滑，以為挍飾；丹枕錦被，置床兩頭，妙衣盛服，皆亦備有；八萬四千金鉢盛滿銀粟，銀鉢盛金粟，琉璃鉢盛頗梨粟，頗梨鉢盛琉璃粟；八萬四千乳牛，牛出乳一斛，金飾其𧿵角，衣以白疊；八萬四千美女，端正福德，皆以白珠名寶瓔珞其身。略舉其要，如是種種，不可勝記。

After twelve years, he wished to give eighty-four thousand white elephants with rhinoceros-hide armor and gold decorations, renowned jewels, great golden standards, and adornments of four precious materials; eighty-four thousand horses similarly armored and adorned; and eighty-four thousand carriages decorated with gold, silver, vaiḍūrya, and crystal, covered with lion, tiger, and leopard skins or white kambala cloth, and ornamented with jeweled fittings. There were eighty-four thousand beds of four precious materials, fitted with multicolored coverings and soft, fine mattresses, red pillows and brocade quilts at both ends, and exquisite clothing. Eighty-four thousand gold bowls held silver grains, silver bowls gold grains, vaiḍūrya bowls crystal grains, and crystal bowls vaiḍūrya grains. Eighty-four thousand milk cows each yielded a hu of milk, their hooves and horns adorned with gold and their bodies draped in white cloth. Eighty-four thousand beautiful, fortunate women wore pearl and jewel garlands. These are only the chief items; the full variety cannot be recorded.

爾時，婆羅婆王及八萬四千諸小國王，并諸臣民豪傑長者，各以十萬舊金錢，贈遺勸助，設此法祠。具足施已，釋提婆那民來語韋羅摩菩薩，說此偈言：「天地難得物，能喜悅一切；汝今皆以得，為佛道布施！」

King Poluopo and eighty-four thousand lesser kings, together with ministers, citizens, leading men, and elders, each contributed a hundred thousand old gold coins to support the offering ceremony. When everything was prepared, Śakra came and spoke to Velāma: ‘You have obtained everything rare in heaven and earth that can delight others. Now give it all for the Buddha path!’

爾時，淨居諸天現身而讚，說此偈言：「開門大布施，汝所為者是；憐愍眾生故，為之求佛道！」

Devas of the Pure Abodes appeared and praised him in verse: ‘Opening your gates to great giving, you act rightly. Out of compassion for beings, you seek the Buddha path for their sake!’

Testing the Bodhisattva’s Aspiration

Chinese Source

是時，諸天作是思惟：「我當閉其金瓶，令水不下。所以者何？有施者，無福田故。」

English Translation

The devas thought, ‘We shall close his golden vessel so that no water pours out. There is a giver, but no field of merit.’

是時，魔王語淨居天：「此諸婆羅門皆出家持戒，清淨入道，何以故乃言無有福田？」淨居天言：「是菩薩為佛道故布施，今此諸人皆是邪見，是故我言無有福田。」

Māra asked the Pure Abodes devas, ‘These brahmins have gone forth, keep precepts, and enter the path in purity. Why say there is no field of merit?’ They replied, ‘This bodhisattva gives for the Buddha path, but these people all hold wrong views. That is why we say there is no field of merit.’

魔王語天言：「云何知是人為佛道故布施？」是時，淨居天化作婆羅門身，持金瓶，執金杖，至韋羅摩菩薩所，語言：「汝大布施，難捨能捨，欲求何等？欲作轉輪聖王，七寶、千子、王四天下耶？」菩薩答言：「不求此事！」「汝求釋提婆那民，為八千那由他天女主耶？」答言：「不！」「汝求六欲天主耶？」答言：「不！」「汝求梵天王，主三千大千世界，為眾生祖父耶？」答言：「不！」「汝欲何所求？」是時，菩薩說此偈言：「我求無欲處，離生老病死，欲度諸眾生，求如是佛道！」

Māra asked how they knew that Velāma gave for Buddhahood. A Pure Abodes deva then assumed a brahmin’s form, carrying a golden vessel and staff, and asked Velāma, ‘You give greatly, relinquishing what is hard to relinquish. What do you seek? To be a wheel-turning sage king with seven treasures, a thousand sons, and dominion over the four continents?’ He answered, ‘No.’ ‘Do you seek to become Śakra, lord of eight thousand nayutas of goddesses?’ ‘No.’ ‘Lord of the six desire heavens?’ ‘No.’ ‘Brahmā, lord of the great trichiliocosm and ancestor of beings?’ ‘No.’ Asked what he sought, the bodhisattva replied in verse: ‘I seek the state free of desire, beyond birth, aging, sickness, and death. To liberate beings, I seek this Buddha path!’

化婆羅門言：「布施主！佛道難得，當大辛苦；汝心軟串樂，必不能求成辦此道。如我先語轉輪聖王、釋提婆那民、六欲天王、梵天王，是易可得，不如求此！」

The disguised brahmin said, ‘Generous donor, the Buddha path is difficult and requires great hardship. You are gentle and accustomed to comfort; surely you cannot accomplish it. The positions I mentioned—wheel-turning king, Śakra, lord of the desire heavens, and Brahmā—are easy to obtain. Better seek one of those!’

菩薩答言：「汝聽我一心誓：「『假令熱鐵輪，在我頭上轉，一心求佛道，終不懷悔恨！若使三惡道，人中無量苦，一心求佛道，終不為此轉！』」

The bodhisattva replied, ‘Hear my wholehearted vow: Even if a wheel of heated iron revolved upon my head, I would single-mindedly seek Buddhahood without regret. Even amid the three bad destinies and immeasurable human suffering, my resolve for Buddhahood would never be turned aside!’

化婆羅門言：「布施主！善哉！善哉！求佛如是。」便讚偈言：「汝精進力大，慈愍於一切，智慧無罣礙，成佛在不久！」

The disguised brahmin said, ‘Excellent! Excellent! This is how to seek Buddhahood.’ He praised him in verse: ‘Great is your power of diligence and your compassion for all. Your wisdom is unobstructed; Buddhahood is not far away!’

是時，天雨眾華，供養菩薩。諸淨居天閉瓶水者，即隱不現。菩薩是時至婆羅門上座前，以金瓶行水，水閉不下。眾人疑怪：「此種種大施，一切具足，布施主人功德亦大，今何以故瓶水不下？」菩薩自念：「此非他事，將無我心不清淨耶？得無施物不具足乎？何以致此？」自觀祠經十六種書，清淨無瑕。

The devas showered flowers as offerings. The Pure Abodes devas who had stopped the water disappeared. Velāma approached the senior brahmin and tried to pour water from the golden vessel, but none flowed. Everyone wondered why, since the gifts were complete and the donor’s merit great. Velāma thought, ‘Perhaps the fault is mine. Is my mind impure? Are the gifts incomplete?’ Examining himself according to sixteen ritual texts, he found everything pure and faultless.

是時，諸天語菩薩言：「汝莫疑悔！汝無不辦！是諸婆羅門惡邪不淨故也。」即說偈言：「是人邪見網，煩惱破正智，離諸清淨戒，唐苦墮異道！」以是故，水閉不下。

The devas told him, ‘Do not doubt or regret. You have neglected nothing. These brahmins are corrupt, hold wrong views, and are impure.’ They spoke in verse: ‘Caught in the net of wrong views, their afflictions destroy right understanding. Far from pure precepts, they suffer in vain on mistaken paths.’ That was why the water did not flow.

如是語已，忽然不現。爾時，六欲天放種種光明，照諸眾會，語菩薩而說偈言：「邪惡海中行，不順汝正道；諸受施人中，無有如汝者！」

Having spoken, they vanished. The six desire heavens emitted lights illuminating the gathering and spoke this verse: ‘They travel in the sea of evil, not following your right path. Among those receiving your gifts, none is equal to you!’

說是語已，忽然不現。是時，菩薩聞說此偈，自念：「會中實自無有與我等者，水閉不下，其將為此乎？」即說偈言：「若有十方天地中，諸有好人清淨者，我今歸命稽首禮！右手執瓶灌左手，而自立願我一人，應受如是大布施！」

The devas vanished. Hearing the verse, Velāma thought, ‘Truly no one here is my equal. Is that why the water is stopped?’ He said: ‘To all good and pure people in heaven and earth throughout the ten directions, I take refuge and bow! Holding the vessel in my right hand, I pour upon my left, and vow that I alone am worthy to receive this great offering.’

是時，瓶水踊在虛空，從上來下而灌其左手。是時，婆薩婆王見是感應，心生恭敬而說偈言：「大婆羅門主，清琉璃色水，從上流注下，來墮汝手中！」

The water rose into the sky and descended onto his left hand. Seeing this response, King Vāsava felt reverence and said in verse: ‘Great brahmin lord! Water clear as vaiḍūrya streams down from above and falls into your hand!’

是時，大婆羅門眾恭敬心生，合手作禮，歸命菩薩。菩薩是時說此偈言：「今我所布施，不求三界福；為諸眾生故，以用求佛道！」

The brahmins joined their palms and bowed, taking refuge in the bodhisattva. He said: ‘My giving seeks no reward in the three realms. For the sake of beings, I use it to seek Buddhahood!’

說此偈已，一切大地、山川、樹木，皆六返震動。韋羅摩本謂此眾應受供養故與，既知此眾無堪受者，今以憐愍故，以所受物施之。如是種種檀本生因緣，是中應廣說。是為外布施。

The earth, mountains, rivers, and trees shook in six ways. Velāma had initially offered to the assembly because he thought them worthy recipients. Having learned otherwise, he now gave them the gifts out of compassion. Such former-birth accounts of giving should be related here at length. This is external giving.

Internal Giving

Chinese Source

云何名內布施？不惜身命，施諸眾生。如本生因緣說：釋迦文佛本為菩薩，為大國王時，世無佛、無法、無比丘僧，是王四出求索佛法，了不能得。時有一婆羅門言：「我知佛偈，供養我者，當以與汝。」王即問言：「索何等供養？」答言：「汝能就汝身上，破肉為燈炷供養我者，當以與汝。」王心念言：「今我此身危脆不淨，世世受苦，不可復數，未曾為法，今始得用，甚不惜也！」

English Translation

What is internal giving? It means giving to beings without clinging to one’s body or life. A former-birth account relates that Śākyamuni, while a bodhisattva and a great king, lived when there was no Buddha, Dharma, or monastic community. Searching everywhere, he could not obtain the Buddha Dharma. A brahmin said, ‘I know a Buddha verse. Make an offering to me and I will give it to you.’ Asked what he wanted, he demanded that the king cut his flesh to make lamp wicks on his body. The king thought, ‘This body is fragile and impure. Through countless lives it has suffered, yet never served the Dharma. Now it can finally be useful. I do not begrudge it!’

如是念已，喚旃陀羅，遍割身上以作燈炷，而以白疊纏肉，酥油灌之，一時遍燒，舉身火燃，乃與一偈。

He summoned a caṇḍāla to cut his flesh into wicks, wrap them in white cloth, and pour clarified butter and oil over them. They were lit together, and his whole body blazed. Only then did the brahmin give him a verse.

又復，釋迦文佛本作一鴿，在雪山中。時大雨雪，有一人失道，窮厄辛苦，飢寒并至，命在須臾。鴿見此人，即飛求火，為其聚薪然之；又復以身投火，施此飢人。如是等頭、目、髓、腦給施眾生，種種本生因緣經，此中應廣說。如是等種種，是名內布施。如是內、外布施無量，是名檀相。

Furthermore, Śākyamuni was once a pigeon in the snowy mountains. During a heavy snowstorm, a traveler lost his way and faced hunger, cold, and imminent death. Seeing him, the pigeon flew to find fire, gathered fuel, and lit it for him, then threw its own body into the fire as an offering to feed him. Accounts of giving heads, eyes, marrow, and brains to beings should likewise be related extensively here. Such acts are internal giving. These immeasurable forms of internal and external giving constitute the character of dāna.

Giving the Dharma — Part Twenty

Chinese Source

問曰：云何名法布施？答曰：有人言：常以好語，有所利益，是為法施。復次，有人言：以諸佛語妙善之法，為人演說，是為法施。復次，有人言：以三種法教人：一、修妬路，二、毘尼，三、阿毘曇，是為法施。復次，有人言：以四種法藏教人：一、修妬路藏，二、毘尼藏，三、阿毘曇藏，四、雜藏，是為法施。復次，有人言：略說以二種法教人：一、聲聞法，二、摩訶衍法，是為法施。

English Translation

Question: What is giving the Dharma? Answer: Some say it is speaking beneficial, kindly words. Others say it is explaining the excellent Dharma spoken by Buddhas. Others define it as teaching the three divisions: sūtra, Vinaya, and Abhidharma. Others give four collections: sūtra, Vinaya, Abhidharma, and miscellaneous teachings. Still others summarize it as teaching either the śrāvaka Dharma or the Mahāyāna.

問曰：如提婆達、呵多等，亦以三藏、四藏、聲聞法、摩訶衍法教人，而身入地獄，是事云何？

Question: Devadatta, Hata, and others also teach the three or four collections, śrāvaka Dharma, and Mahāyāna, yet fall into hell. How is this explained?

答曰：提婆達邪見罪多，呵多妄語罪多，非是為道清淨法施，但求名利恭敬供養。惡心罪故，提婆達生入地獄，呵多死墮惡道。

Answer: Devadatta committed much wrongdoing through wrong views, and Hata through false speech. Their teaching was not pure Dharma-giving for the path; they sought fame, profit, respect, and offerings. Because of these evil intentions, Devadatta entered hell while alive, and Hata fell into a bad destiny after death.

復次，非但言說名為法施，常以淨心善思以教一切，是名法施。譬如財施，不以善心，不名福德；法施亦爾，不以淨心善思，則非法施。復次，說法者，能以淨心善思，讚歎三寶，開罪福門，示四真諦，教化眾生，令入佛道，是為真淨法施。

Furthermore, speaking alone is not giving the Dharma. Teaching everyone with a pure mind and wholesome intention is Dharma-giving. Material giving without a wholesome mind is not meritorious; likewise, teaching without purity and wholesome intention is not Dharma-giving. A teacher who, with pure mind and wholesome intention, praises the Three Jewels, explains wrongdoing and merit, reveals the four truths, and guides beings into the Buddha path practices genuinely pure Dharma-giving.

復次，略說法有二種：一者、不惱眾生，善心慈愍，是為佛道因緣；二者、觀知諸法真空，是為涅槃道因緣。在大眾中興愍哀心，說此二法，不為名聞利養恭敬，是為清淨佛道法施。

Briefly, there are two teachings: not harming beings and acting with kindness and compassion, which cause the Buddha path; and contemplating and knowing the true emptiness of dharmas, which cause the path to nirvāṇa. Teaching these before an assembly with compassion, without seeking fame, gain, honor, or respect, is pure Dharma-giving for the Buddha path.

如說：阿輸伽王一日作八萬佛圖，雖未見道，於佛法中少有信樂，日日請諸比丘入宮供養，日日次第留法師說法。有一三藏年少法師，聰明端正，次應說法。在王邊坐，口有異香；王甚疑怪，謂為不端，欲以香氣動王宮人，語比丘言：「口中何等？開口看之！」即為開口，了無所有；與水令漱，香氣如故。王問：「大德！新有此香？舊有之耶？」比丘答言：「如此久有，非適今也。」

An account relates that King Aśoka built eighty thousand Buddhist shrines in one day. Though he had not realized the path, he had some faith and delight in the Dharma. He invited monks to the palace daily for offerings and had teachers take turns speaking. A young, learned, handsome teacher of the three collections came to speak. Seated beside the king, he emitted an unusual fragrance from his mouth. Suspicious, the king thought he was trying to attract palace women. He ordered him to open his mouth, but found nothing. Rinsing with water did not remove the fragrance. Asked whether it was new, the monk replied, ‘It has been with me a long time, not just today.’

又問：「有此久如？」比丘以偈答言：「迦葉佛時，集此香法，如是久久，常若新出。」

Asked how long, he replied in verse: ‘In Kāśyapa Buddha’s time, I cultivated this fragrant Dharma. Though so very long ago, the fragrance is always fresh.’

王言：「大德！略說未解，為我廣演！」答言：「王當一心，善聽我說：我昔於迦葉佛法中，作說法比丘，常在大眾之中，歡喜演說迦葉世尊無量功德、諸法實相、無量法門，慇懃讚歎，教誨一切。自是以來，常有妙香從口中出，世世不絕，恒如今日。」

The king asked for a fuller explanation. The monk said, ‘Listen attentively. During Kāśyapa Buddha’s dispensation I was a teaching monk. Before great assemblies, I joyfully explained that Buddha’s immeasurable virtues, the true reality of dharmas, and immeasurable Dharma gateways, earnestly praising and instructing everyone. Since then, this exquisite fragrance has issued from my mouth, uninterrupted life after life, just as today.’

而說此偈：「草木諸華香，此香氣超絕，能悅一切心，世世常不滅。」

He spoke this verse: ‘Surpassing the fragrances of grasses, trees, and flowers, this scent delights every heart and never disappears through successive lives.’

于時國王愧喜交集，白比丘言：「未曾有也！說法功德，大果乃爾！」比丘言：「此名為華，未是果也。」

The king felt both shame and joy and said, ‘Unprecedented! The merit of teaching brings such a great fruit!’ The monk replied, ‘This is called the flower, not yet the fruit.’

The Ten Fruits of Dharma-Giving and Its Excellence

Chinese Source

王言：「其果云何？願為演說！」答言：「果，略說有十，王諦聽之！」即為說偈言：「大名聞端政，得樂及恭敬，威光如日月，為一切所愛，辯才有大智，能盡一切結，苦滅得涅槃，如是名為十。」

English Translation

The king asked, ‘What is the fruit? Please explain.’ The monk replied, ‘Briefly, there are ten fruits. Listen carefully!’ He spoke in verse: ‘Great renown, beauty, happiness, respect, radiance like sun and moon, being loved by all, eloquence, great wisdom, the ending of all fetters, and cessation of suffering through nirvāṇa: these are the ten.’

王言：「大德！讚佛功德，云何而得如是果報？」爾時，比丘以偈答曰：「讚佛諸功德，令一切普聞，以此果報故，而得大名譽。讚佛實功德，令一切歡喜，以此功德故，世世常端正。為人說罪福，令得安樂所，以此之功德，受樂常歡豫。讚佛功德力，令一切心伏，以此功德故，常獲恭敬報。顯現說法燈，照悟諸眾生，以此之功德，威光如日曜。種種讚佛德，能悅於一切，以此功德故，常為人所愛。巧言讚佛德，無量無窮已，以此功德故，辯才不可盡。讚佛諸妙法，一切無過者，以此功德故，大智慧清淨。

The king asked, ‘Venerable one, how does praising the Buddha’s virtues bring these rewards?’ The monk replied in verse: ‘Making the Buddha’s virtues widely heard brings great renown. Praising his true virtues and making everyone rejoice brings beauty life after life. Explaining wrongdoing and merit leads others to happiness and brings lasting joy. Praising the power of the Buddha’s virtues wins people’s hearts and brings respect. Revealing the lamp of Dharma to awaken beings brings radiance like the sun. Praising his virtues in many ways delights everyone and makes one beloved. Skillfully praising his inexhaustible virtues brings inexhaustible eloquence. Praising his wondrous, unsurpassed teachings brings great, pure wisdom.’

讚佛功德時，令人煩惱薄，以此功德故，結盡諸垢滅。二種結盡故，涅槃身已證，譬如澍大雨，火盡無餘熱。」

‘Praising the Buddha’s virtues weakens others’ afflictions. Through this merit, fetters end and stains disappear. With both kinds of fetter ended, nirvāṇa is personally realized, like a downpour extinguishing a fire without residual heat.’

重告王言：「若有未悟，今是問時，當以智箭破汝疑軍。」王白法師：「我心悅悟，無所疑也。大德福人，善能讚佛。」如是等種種因緣，說法度人，名為法施。

He added, ‘If anything remains unclear, now is the time to ask. With the arrows of wisdom I will defeat your army of doubts.’ The king replied, ‘My mind is joyful and awakened; I have no doubts. Venerable one, you are blessed and skillfully praise the Buddha.’ Teaching the Dharma and liberating people through such causes and conditions is called giving the Dharma.

問曰：財施、法施，何者為勝？答曰：如佛所言：「二施之中，法施為勝。」所以者何？財施果報，在欲界中；法施果報，或在三界，或出三界。復次，口說清淨，深得理中，心亦得之，故出三界。

Question: Which is superior, material giving or Dharma-giving? Answer: As the Buddha says, ‘Of these two gifts, the gift of Dharma is superior.’ The results of material giving remain within the desire realm. Those of Dharma-giving may occur within the three realms or transcend them. Pure speech deeply accords with the principle; when the mind also realizes it, one goes beyond the three realms.

復次，財施有量，法施無量。財施有盡，法施無盡；譬如以薪益火，其明轉多。

Furthermore, material giving is limited, while Dharma-giving is immeasurable. Material gifts are exhausted, but Dharma-giving is inexhaustible, like adding fuel to a fire and increasing its light.

復次，財施之報，淨少垢多；法施之報，垢少淨多。復次，若作大施，必待眾力；法施出心，不待他也。

The rewards of material giving contain less purity and more defilement; Dharma-giving brings less defilement and more purity. A great material offering requires others’ support, while Dharma-giving arises from one’s own mind and does not depend on others.

復次，財施能令四大諸根增長；法施能令無漏根、力、覺、道具足。

Material giving nourishes the four elements and sense faculties; Dharma-giving fulfills the undefiled faculties, powers, awakening factors, and path.

復次，財施之法，有佛無佛，世間常有；如法施者，唯有佛世乃當有耳。是故當知法施甚難！云何為難？乃至有相辟支佛不能說法，直行乞食，飛騰變化而以度人。

Material giving exists whether or not a Buddha appears. Dharma-giving exists only in a Buddha’s dispensation, so it is exceedingly rare. Even a pratyekabuddha possessing the marks cannot teach the Dharma, but liberates people through seeking alms, flying, and transformations.

復次，從法施中能出生財施，及諸聲聞、辟支佛、菩薩及佛。

Furthermore, Dharma-giving gives rise to material giving and to śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas, and Buddhas.

復次，法施能分別諸法：有漏、無漏法，色法、無色法，有為、無為法，善、不善、無記法，常法、無常法，有法、無法。一切諸法實相清淨，不可破不可壞。如是等法，略說則八萬四千法藏，廣說則無量。如是等種種，皆從法施分別了知。以是故，法施為勝。是二施和合，名之為檀行。是二施願求作佛，則能令人得至佛道，何況其餘！

Dharma-giving distinguishes dharmas with and without influxes, material and immaterial, conditioned and unconditioned, wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate, permanent and impermanent, existent and nonexistent. The true reality of all dharmas is pure, irrefutable, and indestructible. Briefly, there are eighty-four thousand collections of Dharma; in detail, they are immeasurable. Through Dharma-giving, these are distinguished and understood. Therefore, it is superior. These two gifts together constitute the practice of giving. Dedicated to Buddhahood, they can lead one to the Buddha path, much more to lesser attainments!

問曰：四種捨，名為檀。所謂財捨、法捨、無畏捨、煩惱捨。此中何以不說二種捨？答曰：無畏捨，與尸羅無別，故不說。有般若故，不說煩惱捨。若不說六波羅蜜，則應具說四捨。

Question: There are four relinquishments called giving: possessions, Dharma, fearlessness, and afflictions. Why omit the last two here? Answer: Giving fearlessness is not separate from morality, so it is not discussed separately. Relinquishing afflictions is covered by prajñā. If the six pāramitās were not being discussed, all four should be explained.
