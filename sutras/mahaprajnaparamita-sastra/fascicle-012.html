Fascicle 12 · 大智度論卷第十二

The Perfection of Giving

Fascicle 12 continues the explanation of the perfection of giving and reaching the other shore.

Translation Method

Reaching the Other Shore — The Perfection of Giving, Continued

Chinese Source

問曰：云何名檀波羅蜜滿？答曰：「檀」義，如上說。「波羅（秦言彼岸）蜜（秦言到）」，是名渡布施河得到彼岸。

English Translation

Question: What is fulfillment of the perfection of giving? Answer: Giving has been explained above. Pāramitā is explained as crossing the river of giving and reaching the other shore: pāra means ‘the other shore,’ and mita is glossed as ‘arrival.’

問曰：云何名不到彼岸？答曰：譬如渡河未到而還，名為不到彼岸。

Question: What is failing to reach the other shore? Answer: It is like turning back before finishing a river crossing.

如舍利弗於六十劫中行菩薩道，欲渡布施河。時有乞人來乞其眼，舍利弗言：「眼無所任，何以索之？若須我身及財物者，當以相與！」答言：「不須汝身及以財物，唯欲得眼。若汝實行檀者，以眼見與！」爾時，舍利弗出一眼與之。乞者得眼，於舍利弗前嗅之嫌臭，唾而棄地，又以腳蹋。舍利弗思惟言：「如此弊人等，難可度也！眼實無用而強索之，既得而棄，又以腳蹋，何弊之甚！如此人輩，不可度也。不如自調，早脫生死。」思惟是已，於菩薩道退，迴向小乘，是名不到彼岸。

For sixty kalpas, Śāriputra practiced the bodhisattva path, wishing to cross the river of giving. A petitioner asked for his eye. Śāriputra said, ‘An eye is of no use to you. Why ask for it? If you need my body or possessions, I will give them.’ The petitioner insisted, ‘I need neither. If you truly practice giving, give me your eye!’ Śāriputra removed an eye and gave it. The petitioner sniffed it, complained of its smell, spat, threw it down, and stepped on it. Śāriputra thought, ‘Such wicked people are impossible to liberate! He insisted on an eye he could not use, then discarded and trampled it. Better to discipline myself and swiftly escape birth and death.’ He retreated from the bodhisattva path toward the lesser vehicle. This is failing to reach the other shore.

若能直進不退，成辦佛道，名到彼岸。復次，於事成辦，亦名到彼岸（天竺俗法，凡造事成辦皆言到彼岸）。復次，此岸名慳貪，檀名河中，彼岸名佛道。復次，有無見名此岸，破有無見智慧名彼岸，懃修布施是名河中。復次，檀有二種：一者魔檀，二者佛檀。若為結使賊所奪，憂惱怖畏，是為魔檀，名曰此岸。若有清淨布施，無結使賊，無所怖畏，得至佛道，是為佛檀，名曰到彼岸，是為波羅蜜。

Continuing without retreat until Buddhahood is accomplished is reaching the other shore. Completing a task is also called reaching the other shore. (In Indian convention, any completed undertaking is described this way.) Stinginess and greed are this shore; giving is the river; Buddhahood is the other shore. Alternatively, views of existence and nonexistence are this shore; wisdom refuting them is the other; diligent giving is the river. Giving has two kinds: Māra’s and the Buddha’s. Giving plundered by the robbers of fetters, accompanied by anxiety, distress, and fear, is Māra’s giving, this shore. Pure giving without those robbers or fear, leading to Buddhahood, is the Buddha’s giving, arrival at the other shore, or pāramitā.

如佛說《毒蛇喻經》中：有人得罪於王，王令掌護一篋，篋中有四毒蛇。王勅罪人，令看視養育。此人思惟：「四蛇難近，近則害人，一猶叵養，而況於四？」便棄篋而走，王令五人拔刀追之。復有一人，口言附順，心欲中傷，而語之言：「養之以理，此亦無苦！」其人覺之，馳走逃命，至一空聚。有一善人，方便語之：「此聚雖空，是賊所止處，汝今住此，必為賊害，慎勿住也！」於是復去，至一大河，河之彼岸，即是異國。其國安樂，坦然清淨，無諸患難。於是集眾草木，縛以為栰，進以手足，竭力求渡，既到彼岸，安樂無患。

The Buddha’s Discourse on the Simile of Poisonous Snakes tells of a man punished by a king and ordered to guard and nourish four poisonous snakes in a box. Thinking even one was dangerous and impossible to tend, he abandoned the box and fled. The king sent five men with drawn swords after him. Another man spoke agreeably while intending harm: ‘Care for them properly and there is no trouble!’ Recognizing the danger, the fugitive fled to an empty village. A good person warned him, ‘Though empty, this village is a haunt of robbers. If you stay, they will harm you. Do not remain!’ He continued to a great river. Across it was another country, peaceful, level, pure, and free from danger. Gathering grass and wood, he bound a raft and paddled with hands and feet, striving to cross. Reaching the other shore, he was safe and at ease.

王者，魔王；篋者，人身；四毒蛇者，四大；五拔刀賊者，五眾；一人口善心惡者，是染著；空聚是六情；賊是六塵；一人愍而語之是為善師；大河是愛；栰是八正道；手足懃渡是精進；此岸是世間；彼岸是涅槃；度者漏盡阿羅漢。

The king is Māra; the box, the body; the four snakes, the four elements; the five armed robbers, the five aggregates; the man with kindly words and evil intentions, attachment; the empty village, the six faculties; the robbers, the six sense objects; the compassionate adviser, a good teacher; the river, craving; the raft, the noble eightfold path; paddling diligently, effort; this shore, the world; the other shore, nirvāṇa; and the one who crosses, an arhat whose influxes have ended.

菩薩法中亦如是，若施有三礙：我與、彼受、所施者財，是為墮魔境界，未離眾難。如菩薩布施，三種清淨，無此三礙，得到彼岸，為諸佛所讚，是名檀波羅蜜。以是故名到彼岸。此六波羅蜜，能令人渡慳貪等煩惱染著大海，到於彼岸，以是故名波羅蜜。

The bodhisattva teaching is similar. Giving with three obstructions—‘I give,’ ‘that person receives,’ and ‘this is the gift’—falls within Māra’s domain and remains amid dangers. Giving purified in these three respects, without these obstructions, reaches the other shore and is praised by Buddhas. This is the perfection of giving. The six pāramitās ferry people across the ocean of stinginess, greed, affliction, and attachment; therefore, they are called pāramitās.

問曰：阿羅漢、辟支佛亦能到彼岸，何以不名波羅蜜？答曰：阿羅漢、辟支佛渡彼岸，與佛渡彼岸，名同而實異。彼以生死為此岸，涅槃為彼岸，而不能渡檀之彼岸。所以者何？不能以一切物、一切時、一切種布施；設能布施，亦無大心；或以無記心、或有漏善心、或無漏心施；無大悲心；不能為一切眾生施。菩薩施者，知布施不生不滅，無漏無為，如涅槃相，為一切眾生故施，是名檀波羅蜜。

Question: Arhats and pratyekabuddhas also reach the other shore. Why is their attainment not called pāramitā? Answer: Their crossing and a Buddha’s share a name but differ in reality. They regard birth and death as this shore and nirvāṇa as the other, but cannot reach the other shore of giving. They cannot give everything, at every time, and in every manner. Even when they give, they lack the expansive intention and great compassion to give for all beings; their minds may be indeterminate, wholesomely affected by influxes, or undefiled. A bodhisattva knows giving as neither arising nor ceasing, undefiled and unconditioned, like nirvāṇa, and gives for all beings. This is the perfection of giving.

復次，有人言：一切物、一切種內外物，盡以布施，不求果報；如是布施，名檀波羅蜜。

Some also call giving every kind of internal and external possession without seeking reward the perfection of giving.

復次，不可盡故，名檀波羅蜜。所以者何？知所施物畢竟空，如涅槃相；以是心施眾生，是故施報不可盡，名檀波羅蜜。如五通仙人，以好寶物，藏著石中；欲護此寶，磨金剛塗之，令不可破。菩薩布施亦復如是，以涅槃實相智慧磨塗之布施，令不可盡。復次，菩薩為一切眾生故布施，眾生數不可盡故，布施亦不可盡。復次，菩薩為佛法布施，佛法無量無邊，布施亦無量無邊。以是故，阿羅漢、辟支佛，雖到彼岸，不名波羅蜜。

It is also so called because it is inexhaustible. Knowing the gift as ultimately empty, like nirvāṇa, and giving with that mind makes its reward inexhaustible. A seer with five supernatural powers stores treasures in rock and protects them with a coating of ground diamond. Likewise, a bodhisattva coats giving with wisdom of nirvāṇa’s true reality, making it inexhaustible. Giving for all beings is inexhaustible because beings are inexhaustible. Giving for the Buddha Dharma is immeasurable and boundless because that Dharma is immeasurable and boundless. Therefore, although arhats and pratyekabuddhas reach the other shore, their attainment is not called pāramitā.

問曰：云何名「具足滿」？答曰：如先說，菩薩能一切布施，內外大小，多少麁細，著不著，用不用，如是等種種物，一切能捨，心無所惜，等與一切眾生，不作是觀：「大人應與，小人不應與；出家人應與，不出家人不應與；人應與，禽獸不應與。」於一切眾生平等心施，施不求報，又得施實相，是名具足滿。亦不觀時，無晝無夜，無冬無夏，無吉無衰，一切時常等施，心無悔惜，乃至頭目髓腦，施而無悋，是為具足滿。

Question: What is complete fulfillment? Answer: As explained earlier, a bodhisattva gives everything, internal and external, large or small, much or little, coarse or fine, valued or unvalued, useful or unused, without regret. Giving equally to all beings, one does not think, ‘Give to adults, not children; monastics, not householders; humans, not birds and beasts.’ Giving equally without seeking reward and realizing its true nature is fulfillment. One also does not discriminate among times—day or night, winter or summer, auspicious or inauspicious—but gives equally at all times without regret, even giving head, eyes, marrow, and brain without stinginess. This is complete fulfillment.

復次，有人言：菩薩從初發心，乃至菩提樹下三十四心，於是中間，名為布施具足滿。

Some say that the period from a bodhisattva’s initial aspiration through the thirty-four moments of mind beneath the bodhi tree constitutes fulfillment of giving.

復次，七住菩薩得一切諸法實相智慧，是時，莊嚴佛土，教化眾生，供養諸佛，得大神通，能分一身作無數身，一一身皆雨七寶、華香、幡蓋，化作大燈如須彌山，供養十方佛及菩薩僧；復以妙音讚頌佛德，禮拜供養，恭敬將迎。復次，是菩薩於一切十方無量餓鬼國中，雨種種飲食、衣被，令其充滿；得滿足已，皆發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。復至畜生道中，令其自善，無相害意，除其畏怖，隨其所須，各令充足；得滿足已，皆發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。於地獄無量苦中，能令地獄火滅湯冷，罪息心善，除其飢渴，得生天上、人中；以此因緣故，皆發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。若十方人，貧窮者給之以財，富貴者施以異味異色，令其歡喜；以此因緣故，皆發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。若至欲天中，令其除却天上欲樂，施以妙寶法樂，令其歡喜；以此因緣故，皆發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。若至色天中，除其樂著，以菩薩禪法而娛樂之；以此因緣故，皆發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。如是乃至十住，是名檀波羅蜜具足滿。復次，菩薩有二種身：一者結業生身，二者法身。是二種身中，檀波羅蜜滿，是名具足檀波羅蜜。

Furthermore, at the seventh abiding a bodhisattva attains wisdom concerning the true reality of all dharmas, adorns Buddha lands, teaches beings, and honors Buddhas. With great spiritual power, one manifests countless bodies, each showering jewels, flowers, incense, banners, and canopies, and lamps like Mount Sumeru, as offerings to Buddhas and bodhisattva communities throughout the ten directions. With wondrous voices one praises, bows, serves, and welcomes them. One showers food and clothing into immeasurable hungry-ghost realms, satisfying their needs so that they aspire to unsurpassed awakening. Among animals, one removes fear and hostility, supplies their needs, and brings forth that aspiration. In hells, one extinguishes fires, cools boiling liquids, quiets wrongdoing, brings wholesome thoughts, relieves hunger and thirst, and enables human or heavenly rebirth and aspiration to awakening. Among humans, one gives wealth to the poor and distinctive foods and beautiful things to the wealthy, bringing joy and that aspiration. Among desire-realm devas, one removes attachment to heavenly pleasures and gives the wondrous jewel of Dharma joy. Among form-realm devas, one removes attachment to their bliss and delights them with bodhisattva meditation. They too aspire to unsurpassed awakening. Continuing thus through the tenth abiding is complete fulfillment of giving. Bodhisattvas have two bodies: a body born of fetters and karma, and a Dharma body. Fulfillment in both is fulfillment of the perfection of giving.

問曰：云何名結業生身檀波羅蜜滿？答曰：未得法身，結使未盡，能以一切寶物、頭、目、髓、腦、國、財、妻、子，內、外所有，盡以布施，心不動轉。

Question: How is the perfection of giving fulfilled in a body born of fetters and karma? Answer: Before attaining the Dharma body, while fetters remain, one can give all jewels, head, eyes, marrow, brain, kingdom, wealth, wife, and children—everything internal and external—without wavering.

如須提拏太子（秦言好愛）以其二子布施婆羅門，次以妻施，其心不轉。又如薩婆達王（秦言一切施）為敵國所滅，身竄窮林。見有遠國婆羅門來，欲從己乞。自以國破家亡，一身藏竄，愍其辛苦，故從遠來而無所得，語婆羅門言：「我是薩婆達王，新王募人，求我甚重。」即時自縛以身施之，送與新王，大得財物。

For example, Prince Sudāna, whose name means ‘Dearly Loved,’ gave his two children to a brahmin and then gave his wife, without wavering. King Sarvadatta, whose name means ‘Giver of All,’ lost his kingdom to an enemy and hid in a remote forest. A brahmin from a distant country came seeking a gift. With his kingdom and household destroyed, the king pitied the traveler who had come so far in vain. He said, ‘I am King Sarvadatta. The new king offers a great reward for finding me.’ Binding himself, he gave his own person to the brahmin, who delivered him to the new king and obtained great wealth.

Offerings of the Body and the Dharma Body

Chinese Source

亦如月光太子，出行遊觀。癩人見之要車，白言：「我身重病，辛苦懊惱，太子嬉遊，獨自歡耶？大慈愍念，願見救療！」太子聞之，以問諸醫。醫言：「當須從生長大無瞋之人血髓，塗而飲之，如是可愈。」太子念言：「設有此人，貪生惜壽，何可得耶？自除我身，無可得處。」即命旃陀羅，令除身肉，破骨出髓以塗病人，以血飲之。

English Translation

Likewise, Prince Moonlight once went out sightseeing. A person afflicted with leprosy stopped his carriage and said, ‘I am gravely ill, distressed and suffering. Will you enjoy yourself alone? Please have great compassion and heal me!’ The prince consulted physicians, who said that blood and marrow from someone who had never been angry, applied externally and taken internally, could cure the disease. The prince thought, ‘Even if such a person exists, they will cherish life. Where could I obtain these except from myself?’ He ordered a caṇḍāla to remove his flesh and break his bones to obtain marrow for the patient, and gave his blood to drink.

如是等種種，身及妻子，施而無悋，如棄草木。觀所施物，知從緣有，推求其實，都無所得，一切清淨，如涅槃相，乃至得無生法忍，是為結業生身行檀波羅蜜滿。

In such ways, one gives one’s body, wife, and children without reluctance, as if discarding grass or wood. Contemplating gifts as existing through conditions, one seeks their reality but finds nothing apprehensible. All are pure, like nirvāṇa. Continuing until patient acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas is attained fulfills the perfection of giving in a body born of fetters and karma.

云何法身菩薩行檀波羅蜜滿？菩薩末後肉身得無生法忍，捨肉身得法身。於十方六道中，變身應適以化眾生，種種珍寶、衣服、飲食，給施一切。又以頭、目、髓、腦、國、財、妻、子，內、外所有，盡以布施。

How does a Dharma-body bodhisattva fulfill giving? In the final fleshly body, the bodhisattva attains acceptance of non-arising, relinquishes that body, and obtains the Dharma body. Throughout the six destinies in the ten directions, one manifests suitable bodies to teach beings, giving jewels, clothing, food, and drink to all. One also gives head, eyes, marrow, brain, kingdom, wealth, wife, children—everything internal and external.

譬如釋迦文佛曾為六牙白象，獵者伺便，以毒箭射之。諸象競至，欲來蹈殺獵者。白象以身捍之，擁護其人，愍之如子，諭遣群象。徐問獵人：「何故射我？」答曰：「我須汝牙！」即時以六牙內石孔中，血肉俱出，以鼻舉牙，授與獵者。雖曰象身，用心如是，當知此象非畜生行報。阿羅漢法中，都無此心，當知此為法身菩薩。

For example, Śākyamuni once became a white elephant with six tusks. A hunter shot him with a poisoned arrow. Other elephants rushed to trample the hunter, but the white elephant shielded him, compassionately protecting him like a child, and sent the herd away. He gently asked, ‘Why did you shoot me?’ ‘I need your tusks.’ The elephant thrust his six tusks into a hole in rock and pulled them out with flesh and blood attached, then lifted them with his trunk and gave them to the hunter. Although appearing as an elephant, his intention was like this. Such a being is not an animal produced by ordinary animal karma. Since even an arhat’s teaching does not contain this resolve, one should know this was a Dharma-body bodhisattva.

有時閻浮提人，不知禮敬。耆舊有德，以言化之，未可得度。是時，菩薩自變其身，作迦頻闍羅鳥。是鳥有二親友：一者大象，二者獼猴，共在必鉢羅樹下住。

At another time, people in Jambudvīpa did not know how to show respect. Virtuous elders could not guide them through words. A bodhisattva therefore became a kapiñjala bird. Its two close friends, an elephant and a monkey, lived with it beneath a pipal tree.

自相問言：「我等不知誰應為長？」象言：「我昔見此樹在我腹下，今大如是。以此推之，我應為長！」獼猴言：「我曾蹲地，手挽樹頭。以是推之，我應為長！」鳥言：「我於必鉢羅林中，食此樹果，子隨糞出，此樹得生。以是推之，我應最長！」

They asked who was eldest. The elephant said, ‘I remember this tree beneath my belly; now it is so large. I must be eldest!’ The monkey said, ‘I once crouched on the ground and grasped its crown. I must be eldest!’ The bird said, ‘I ate fruit in a pipal grove, and a seed passed out in my droppings and grew into this tree. Therefore, I must be eldest!’

鳥復說言：「先生宿舊，禮應供養！」即時大象背負獼猴，鳥在猴上，周遊而行。一切禽獸見而問之：「何以如此？」答曰：「以此恭敬供養長老！」禽獸受化，皆行禮敬，不侵民田，不害物命。眾人疑怪，一切禽獸不復為害。獵者入林，見象負獼猴，獼猴戴鳥，行敬化物，物皆修善。傳告國人，人各慶曰：「時將太平，鳥獸而仁！」人亦效之，皆行禮敬。自古及今，化流萬世。當知是為法身菩薩。

The bird added, ‘Those born earlier deserve respectful service.’ The elephant carried the monkey, with the bird above it, and they traveled about. Animals asked why. They answered, ‘This is respect for elders.’ The animals followed their example, behaving respectfully, sparing people’s fields, and not harming living things. People marveled that animals no longer caused harm. Hunters saw the elephant carrying the monkey and bird and teaching respect, with all creatures practicing goodness. They told the country’s people, who rejoiced, ‘Peace is approaching: even birds and beasts are kind!’ People likewise practiced respect. The influence of this teaching has continued through generations. Know that this was a Dharma-body bodhisattva.

復次，法身菩薩，一時之頃，化作無央數身，供養十方諸佛；一時能化無量財寶，給足眾生；能隨一切上中下聲，一時之頃，普為說法；乃至坐佛樹下。如是等種種，名為法身菩薩行檀波羅蜜滿。

Furthermore, a Dharma-body bodhisattva can instantly manifest innumerable bodies to honor Buddhas throughout the ten directions, create immeasurable wealth to satisfy beings, and simultaneously teach them according to every higher, intermediate, and lower voice. This continues until sitting beneath the tree of awakening. Such activities fulfill the perfection of giving through the Dharma body.

Three Forms of Giving and Their Conditions

Chinese Source

復次，檀有三種：一者、物施，二者、供養恭敬施，三者、法施。云何物施？珍寶、衣、食、頭、目、髓、腦——如是等一切內、外所有，盡以布施，是名物施。恭敬施者，信心清淨，恭敬禮拜，將送迎逆，讚遶供養——如是等種種，名為恭敬施。法施者，為道德故，語言論議，誦讀講說，除疑問答，授人五戒——如是等種種，為佛道故施，是名法施。是三種施滿，是名檀波羅蜜滿。

English Translation

Giving has three forms: material gifts, respectful service, and Dharma-giving. Material giving includes jewels, clothing, food, head, eyes, marrow, brain, and everything internal and external. Respectful giving means pure faith, reverence, bowing, accompanying and welcoming others, praising, circumambulating, and making offerings. Dharma-giving includes discussions, recitation, reading, exposition, resolving doubts, answering questions, and teaching the five precepts for the sake of virtue and the Buddha path. Fulfillment of these three is fulfillment of the perfection of giving.

復次，三事因緣生檀：一者、信心清淨，二者、財物，三者、福田。心有三種：若憐愍，若恭敬，若憐愍恭敬。施貧窮下賤及諸畜生，是為憐愍施；施佛及諸法身菩薩等，是為恭敬施；若施諸老病貧乏阿羅漢、辟支佛，是為恭敬憐愍施。施物清淨，非盜非劫，以時而施，不求名譽，不求利養。

Three conditions produce giving: pure faith, possessions, and a field of merit. The mind may act from compassion, respect, or both. Gifts to the poor, lowly, and animals are compassionate giving. Gifts to Buddhas and Dharma-body bodhisattvas are respectful giving. Gifts to aged, sick, or impoverished arhats and pratyekabuddhas combine respect and compassion. The gift should be pure, neither stolen nor plundered, given at a fitting time without seeking fame or gain.

或時從心大得福德，或從福田大得功德，或從妙物大得功德。第一從心，如四等心、念佛三昧、以身施虎，如是名為從心大得功德。

Sometimes great merit comes chiefly from the mind, sometimes from the field of merit, and sometimes from an excellent gift. The mind is foremost, as in the four immeasurables, samādhi of Buddha-recollection, or giving one’s body to a tiger. These illustrate great merit arising from intention.

福田有二種：一者、憐愍福田，二者、恭敬福田。憐愍福田，能生憐愍心；恭敬福田，能生恭敬心。如阿輸伽（秦言無憂）王以土上佛。復次，物施中，如一女人，酒醉沒心，誤以七寶瓔珞布施迦葉佛塔，以福德故，生三十三天。如是種種，名為物施。

Fields of merit have two kinds: those calling forth compassion and those calling forth respect. One produces compassion, the other reverence. For example, King Aśoka, whose name means ‘Without Sorrow,’ offered earth to the Buddha. As another example concerning a material gift, an intoxicated woman mistakenly offered a garland of seven jewels to Kāśyapa Buddha’s stūpa. Through that merit she was born in the Heaven of the Thirty-Three. Such cases illustrate material giving.

Giving without Anything Relinquished

Chinese Source

問曰：檀名捨財，何以言「具足無所捨法」？答曰：檀有二種：一者、出世間，二者、不出世間。今說出世間檀無相，無相故無所捨，是故言「具足無所捨法」。

English Translation

Question: Giving means relinquishing possessions. Why say ‘fulfilling giving without any dharma being relinquished’? Answer: Giving is either supramundane or not supramundane. Here supramundane giving is discussed. It has no fixed marks; therefore, nothing is relinquished. Hence this expression.

復次，財物不可得故，名為無所捨。

Furthermore, possessions cannot be apprehended, so nothing is relinquished. They are empty in past and future; analyzed in the present, no fixed dharma is found. Thus nothing is relinquished.

是物未來、過去空，現在分別，無一定法，以是故言無所捨。復次，以行者捨財時，心念此施大有功德，倚是而生憍慢、愛結等；以是故言無所捨。以無所捨故無憍慢，無憍慢故愛結等不生。

When relinquishing wealth, a practitioner may think, ‘This gift brings great merit,’ and rely on it to develop pride, craving, and other fetters. Therefore, the text speaks of nothing being relinquished. With no grasping at relinquishment, there is no pride; without pride, craving and related fetters do not arise.

復次，施者有二種：一者、世間人，二者、出世間人。世間人能捨財，不能捨施；出世間人能捨財，能捨施。何以故？以財物、施心俱不可得故。以是故言具足無所捨法。

Givers may be worldly or supramundane. Worldly people relinquish possessions but cannot relinquish attachment to giving. Supramundane people relinquish both, because neither possessions nor the giving mind can be apprehended. Therefore, giving is fulfilled without any dharma being relinquished.

復次，檀波羅蜜中，言：「財、施、受者，三事不可得。」問曰：三事和合，故名為檀；今言三事不可得，云何名「檀波羅蜜具足滿」？今有財、有施、有受者，云何三事不可得？如所施疊實有。何以故？疊有名，則有疊法，若無疊法，亦無疊名；以有名故，應實有疊。復次，疊有長、有短，麁、細、白、黑、黃、赤，有因有緣，有作有破，有果報，隨法生心。十尺為長，五尺為短；縷大為麁，縷小為細；隨染有色；有縷為因，織具為緣，是因緣和合故為疊。人功為作，人毀為破；御寒暑，弊身體，名果報。人得之大喜，失之大憂。以之施故，得福助道；若盜若劫，戮之都市，死入地獄。如是等種種因緣，故知有此疊，是名疊法，云何言施物不可得？

Furthermore, the perfection of giving states that gift, giver, and recipient cannot be apprehended. Question: Giving is named from the conjunction of these three. If they cannot be apprehended, how is giving fulfilled? Possessions, giving, and recipients exist. For example, donated cloth truly exists. Its name implies a corresponding dharma; without cloth there would be no name. Cloth has length and shortness, coarse and fine texture, white, black, yellow, and red colors, causes and conditions, production, destruction, and effects, and gives rise to corresponding thoughts. Ten chi is long, five short; large threads make coarse cloth and small threads fine cloth; dye gives color. Threads are its cause and weaving implements its conditions. Labor makes it and damage destroys it. Protection from cold and heat and covering the body are its effects. People rejoice on obtaining it and grieve on losing it. Giving it produces merit supporting the path; stealing it may bring public execution and rebirth in hell. These conditions show that cloth exists. Why say the gift cannot be apprehended?

Names, Conditions, and Three Kinds of Existence

Chinese Source

答曰：汝言有名故有，是事不然。何以知之？名有二種：有實、有不實。不實名，如有一草名朱利（朱利秦言賊也），草亦不盜不劫，實非賊而名為賊。又如兔角、龜毛，亦但有名而無實。

English Translation

Answer: Your claim that a name proves existence is incorrect. Names may or may not correspond to something real. A grass is called zhuli, meaning ‘thief,’ though it neither steals nor plunders and is not actually a thief. Rabbit horns and tortoise hair also have names without corresponding realities.

疊雖不如兔角、龜毛無，然因緣會故有，因緣散故無。如林、如軍，是皆有名而無實。譬如木人，雖有人名，不應求其人法。疊中雖有名，亦不應求疊真實。

Cloth is not nonexistent in the same way as rabbit horns or tortoise hair. It exists when conditions assemble and ceases when they disperse. Like a forest or an army, it has a name without an independent reality. A wooden figure may be called a person, but one should not seek human properties in it. Likewise, the name ‘cloth’ does not establish an independent reality.

疊能生人心念因緣，得之便喜，失之便憂，是為念因緣。心生有二因緣：有從實而生，有從不實而生。如夢中所見，如水中月，如夜見杌樹謂為人，如是名從不實中能令心生。是緣不定，不應言心生有故便是有。若心生因緣故有，更不應求實有。如眼見水中月，心生謂是月，若從心生便是月者，則無復真月。

Cloth can cause thoughts: joy on obtaining it and grief on losing it. But thoughts arise from both real and unreal objects. Dreams, the moon reflected in water, and mistaking a stump for a person at night can all produce thoughts without a real corresponding object. Such conditions are indeterminate. The arising of a thought does not prove its object exists. If it did, no further inquiry into reality would be needed. Seeing the moon reflected in water and thinking it a moon would make it a moon, leaving no distinction from the actual moon.

復次，「有」，有三種：一者、相待有，二者、假名有，三者、法有。相待者，如長短、彼此等，實無長短，亦無彼此，以相待故有名。長因短有，短亦因長；彼亦因此，此亦因彼；若在物東，則以為西，在西則以為東；一物未異而有東、西之別，此皆有名而無實也。如是等，名為相待有，是中無實法，不如色、香、味、觸等。

Furthermore, existence has three kinds: relative existence, nominal existence, and existence as a dharma. Relative existence includes long and short, here and there. Neither is independently real; their names depend on mutual comparison. Long depends on short, short on long; there depends on here, here on there. From east of an object one calls it west; from west of it one calls it east. The same object has not changed, yet it is distinguished as east or west. These names have no independent reality. Such relative existence contains no real dharma in the manner of color, smell, taste, or touch.

假名有者，如酪有色、香、味、觸，四事因緣合故，假名為酪。雖有，不同因緣法有；雖無，亦不如兔角、龜毛無；但以因緣合故，假名有酪。疊亦如是。

Nominal existence is like curds: color, smell, taste, and touch combine and are provisionally named curds. Their existence differs from that of the constituent conditions. Their nonexistence likewise differs from the absence of rabbit horns or tortoise hair. ‘Curds’ exists provisionally through assembled conditions. Cloth is similar.

復次，有極微色、香、味、觸，故有毛分，毛分因緣故有毛，毛因緣故有毳，毳因緣故有縷，縷因緣故有疊，疊因緣故有衣。若無極微色、香、味、觸因緣，亦無毛分，毛分無故亦無毛，毛無故亦無毳，毳無故亦無縷，縷無故亦無疊，疊無故亦無衣。

Furthermore, minute color, smell, taste, and touch make possible a portion of hair; portions make hair; hair makes fiber; fiber makes thread; thread makes cloth; cloth makes clothing. Without those minute constituents there are no portions of hair, hence no hair, fiber, thread, cloth, or clothing.

Analysis of Atoms and Emptiness in Contemplation

Chinese Source

問曰：亦不必一切物皆從因緣和合故有，如微塵至細故無分，無分故無和合。疊麁故可破，微塵中無分，云何可破？

English Translation

Question: Not everything must exist through combined conditions. An atom is so small that it has no parts and therefore no combination. Coarse cloth can be divided, but how could an atom without parts be divided?

答曰：至微無實，強為之名。何以故？麁細相待，因麁故有細，是細復應有細。復次，若有極微色，則有十方分；若有十方分，是不名為極微；若無十方分，則不名為色。復次，若有極微，則應有虛空分齊；若有分者，則不名極微。復次，若有極微，是中有色、香、味、觸作分，色、香、味、觸作分，是不名極微。以是推求，微塵則不可得。如經言：「色若麁若細，若內若外，總而觀之，無常無我。」不言有微塵。是名分破空。

Answer: An ultimate particle has no substantial reality; the name is imposed. Coarse and fine depend on each other: what is fine relative to something coarse should itself admit something finer. If a smallest material particle exists, it has parts facing the ten directions. Having these parts, it is not smallest. Without directional parts, it is not material form. If it occupies a bounded region of space, that too implies division. If color, smell, taste, and touch form its constituents, it is likewise not indivisible. Examined thus, atoms cannot be apprehended. A scripture says, ‘Contemplate form collectively, coarse or fine, internal or external, as impermanent and without self.’ It does not assert atoms. This is emptiness through analysis and division.

復有觀空：是疊隨心有。如坐禪人觀疊或作地、或作水、或作火、或作風，或青、或黃、或白、或赤，或都空，如十一切入觀。如佛在耆闍崛山中，與比丘僧俱，入王舍城。道中見大水，佛於水上敷尼師壇坐，告諸比丘：「若比丘入禪，心得自在，能令大水作地，即成實地。何以故？是水中有地分故。如是水、火、風、金、銀種種寶物即皆成實。何以故？是水中皆有其分。」

There is also emptiness through contemplation: cloth appears according to the mind. A meditator may contemplate it as earth, water, fire, wind, blue, yellow, white, red, or wholly empty, as in the ten totality contemplations. Once the Buddha and the monks were traveling from Vulture Peak to Rājagṛha and encountered a great body of water. The Buddha spread his sitting cloth upon it, sat down, and said, ‘A monk entering meditation and attaining mastery of mind can turn this water into earth, and it truly becomes earth. Why? Water contains an earth component. Likewise, it can truly become water, fire, wind, gold, silver, or other jewels, because it contains these components.’

復次，如一美色，婬人見之以為淨妙，心生染著；不淨觀人視之，種種惡露，無一淨處；等婦見之，妬瞋憎惡，目不欲見，以為不淨；婬人觀之為樂；妬人觀之為苦；行人觀之得道；無豫之人觀之，無所適莫，如見土木。若此美色實淨，四種人觀，皆應見淨；若實不淨，四種人觀，皆應不淨。以是故，知好醜在心，外無定也。觀空亦如是。

Furthermore, a beautiful person appears pure and exquisite to someone lustful, who develops attachment. A practitioner contemplating impurity sees foul substances and nothing pure. A rival woman sees that person with jealousy, anger, and aversion, as impure. The lustful see pleasure, the jealous suffering, a practitioner gains the path, and an uninvolved observer feels neither attraction nor aversion, as if seeing earth or wood. If beauty were intrinsically pure, all four would see purity; if intrinsically impure, all four would see impurity. Therefore, beauty and ugliness depend on mind, without fixed external determination. Contemplating emptiness is similar.

復次，是疊中有十八空相故，觀之便空，空故不可得。如是種種因緣財物空，決定不可得。

Furthermore, cloth bears the characteristics of the eighteen emptinesses. Contemplating it, one finds it empty; being empty, it cannot be apprehended. Through such causes and conditions, possessions are empty and definitively unapprehensible.

The Giver Cannot Be Apprehended

Chinese Source

云何施人不可得？如疊因緣和合故有，分分推之，疊不可得。施者亦如是，四大圍虛空，名為身，是身識動作來往坐起，假名為人。分分求之，亦不可得。

English Translation

How is the giver unapprehensible? Cloth exists through combined conditions, but examined part by part it cannot be found. The giver is similar. The four elements surrounding space are called a body. Conscious activity moves this body, going, coming, sitting, and rising; it is provisionally called a person. Examined part by part, that person likewise cannot be apprehended.

Why the Giver Is Not an Independent Self

Chinese Source

復次，一切眾、界、入中，我不可得，我不可得故，施人不可得。何以故？我，有種種名字：人、天，男、女，施人、受人，受苦人、受樂人，畜生等；是但有名，而實法不可得。

English Translation

Furthermore, no self can be apprehended in any aggregate, element, or sense base. Therefore, no giver can be apprehended. Why? Self has many names: human, deva, man, woman, giver, recipient, one experiencing suffering or pleasure, animal, and others. These are names alone; no independent reality can be apprehended.

問曰：若施者不可得，云何有菩薩行檀波羅蜜？答曰：因緣和合故有名字，如屋、如車，實法不可得。

Question: If the giver cannot be apprehended, how can a bodhisattva practice the perfection of giving? Answer: Names exist through assembled causes and conditions, like a house or carriage, but no independent reality can be apprehended.

問曰：云何我不可得？答曰：如上「我聞一時」中已說，今當更說。佛說六識：眼識及眼識相應法，共緣色，不緣屋舍、城郭種種諸名。耳、鼻、舌、身識，亦如是。意識及意識相應法，知眼、知色、知眼識，乃至知意、知法、知意識。是識所緣法，皆空無我，生滅故，不自在故。無為法中亦不計我，苦樂不受故。是中若強有我法，應當有第七識識我；而今不爾，以是故知無我。

Question: How is the self unapprehensible? Answer: This was explained above under ‘Thus have I heard, at one time,’ but will be explained again. The Buddha teaches six consciousnesses. Visual consciousness and its associated dharmas take forms as their common object, not the names of houses, cities, and so forth. The same applies to auditory, olfactory, gustatory, and bodily consciousness. Mental consciousness and its associated dharmas know the eye, forms, and visual consciousness, through to mind, dharmas, and mental consciousness. Their objects are empty and without self, because they arise and cease and are not autonomous. Nor is a self posited within the unconditioned, which experiences neither pleasure nor suffering. If one insisted on a self here, a seventh consciousness would be required to know it. There is no such consciousness here; therefore, we know there is no self.

問曰：何以識無我？一切人各於自身中生計我，不於他身中生我；若自身中無我，而妄見為我者，他身中無我，亦應於他身而妄見為我。復次，若內無我，色、識念念生滅，云何分別知是色青、黃、赤、白？復次，若無我，今現在人識，漸漸生滅，身命斷時亦盡，諸行罪福，誰隨誰受？誰受苦樂？誰解脫者？如是種種因緣故知有我。

Question: How do we know there is no self? Everyone identifies their own body as self, not another’s. If this were a false perception of a self where none exists, why not similarly perceive another’s body as self? Furthermore, if form and consciousness arise and cease each moment without an inner self, how can blue, yellow, red, and white be distinguished? If present consciousness gradually arises and ceases and ends with bodily life, who carries and receives the results of wrongdoing and merit? Who experiences pleasure and suffering, and who is liberated? These considerations show that a self exists.

答曰：此俱有難！若於他身生計我者，復當言「何以不自身中生計我」？復次，五眾因緣生故空無我，從無明因緣生二十身見，是我見自於五陰相續生。以從此五眾緣生故，即計此五眾為我，不在他身，以其習故。

Answer: This objection cuts both ways. If someone identified another’s body as self, you could equally ask why they did not identify their own body. The five aggregates arise from conditions and are empty of self. Ignorance produces twenty views of identity, and self-view arises within one’s own succession of aggregates. Arising in dependence on these aggregates, it identifies these, rather than another’s body, as self through habituation.

復次，若有神者，可有彼我。汝神有無未了，而問彼我！其猶人問兔角，答似馬角。馬角若實有，可以證兔角；馬角猶尚未了，而欲以證兔角。

Furthermore, only if a soul existed could there be such a distinction between self and other. Before establishing whether your soul exists, you ask about self and other! It is like answering a question about rabbit horns by comparing them with horse horns. Horse horns would need to exist to prove rabbit horns, but their existence is itself unestablished.

復次，自於身生我故，便自謂有神。汝言「神遍」，亦應計他身為我。以是故，不應言「自身中生計我心，於他身不生，故知有神」。

Furthermore, identifying one’s body as self leads one to claim a soul exists. But if, as you say, the soul pervades everything, you should also identify another’s body as self. Thus, identifying one’s own body rather than another’s does not establish a soul.

復次，有人於他物中我心生，如外道坐禪人，用地一切入觀時，見地則是我，我則是地，水、火、風、空，亦如是。顛倒故，於他身中亦計我。

Some people do identify other things as self. Non-Buddhist meditators practicing the earth-totality contemplation see earth as self and self as earth; the same applies to water, fire, wind, and space. Through inversion, one can also identify another’s body as self.

The Traveler and the Two Spirits

Chinese Source

復次，有時於他身生我，如有一人，受使遠行，獨宿空舍。夜中有鬼擔一死人來著其前，復有一鬼逐來，瞋罵前鬼：「是死人是我物，汝何以擔來？」先鬼言：「是我物，我自持來。」後鬼言：「是死人實我擔來！」二鬼各捉一手爭之。

English Translation

Sometimes another’s body is taken as self. A man traveling on an errand once lodged alone in an empty house. At night, a spirit brought a corpse and placed it before him. Another spirit followed, angrily saying, ‘That corpse is mine! Why have you carried it here?’ The first replied, ‘It is mine; I brought it.’ The second insisted that it had brought it. Each seized one hand of the corpse and argued.

前鬼言：「此有人可問。」後鬼即問：「是死人誰擔來？」是人思惟：「此二鬼力大，若實語亦當死，若妄語亦當死，俱不免死，何為妄語？」語言：「前鬼擔來。」後鬼大瞋，捉人手拔出著地，前鬼取死人一臂拊之即著。如是兩臂、兩腳、頭、脇，舉身皆易。於是二鬼共食所易人身，拭口而去。

The first spirit suggested asking the traveler. Asked who had brought the corpse, he thought, ‘Both spirits are powerful. Whether I tell the truth or lie, I will die. Why lie?’ He answered, ‘The first spirit brought it.’ Furious, the second tore off his arm and threw it down. The first attached an arm from the corpse in its place. In this way, both arms, both legs, head, and torso were replaced. The spirits ate the removed human body, wiped their mouths, and left.

其人思惟：「我人母生身，眼見二鬼食盡，今我此身盡是他肉。我今定有身耶？為無身耶？若以為有，盡是他身；若以為無，今現有身。」如是思惟，其心迷悶，譬如狂人。

The man thought, ‘I saw the two spirits eat the body my mother bore. My present body is entirely another’s flesh. Do I have a body or not? If I do, it is entirely another’s. If not, a body is plainly here.’ Reflecting thus, he became confused and distraught, like someone mad.

明朝尋路而去，到前國土，見有佛塔眾僧，不論餘事，但問己身為有為無？諸比丘問：「汝是何人？」答言：「我亦不自知是人、非人？」即為眾僧廣說上事。諸比丘言：「此人自知無我，易可得度。」而語之言：「汝身從本已來，恒自無我，非適今也。但以四大和合故，計為我身，如汝本身，與今無異。」諸比丘度之為道，斷諸煩惱，即得阿羅漢。

Next morning, he traveled onward and found a Buddhist stūpa and a community of monks. Speaking of nothing else, he asked whether his body existed. The monks asked who he was. He replied, ‘I do not even know whether I am human,’ and related everything. They said, ‘He already recognizes the absence of self and will be easy to guide.’ They told him, ‘Your body has always been without self, not merely now. Through the combination of the four elements you conceive it as your body. Your former body and present one are no different in this respect.’ They ordained and taught him; he ended his afflictions and became an arhat.

是為有時他身亦計為我。不可以有彼此故謂有我。

Thus, another’s body may sometimes be identified as self. The distinction between oneself and another does not prove a self exists.

Testing the Supposed Characteristics of a Soul

Chinese Source

復次，是我實性，決定不可得。若常相、非常相，自在相、不自在相，作相、不作相，色相、非色相，如是等種種皆不可得。若有相則有法，無相則無法，我今無相，則知無我。

English Translation

Furthermore, the real nature of this supposed self cannot be established as permanent or impermanent, autonomous or dependent, acting or nonacting, material or immaterial. None of these characteristics can be apprehended. If a dharma exists, it has a characteristic; without any characteristic, no such dharma exists. Since no characteristic of self is found, we know there is no self.

若我是常，不應有殺罪。何以故？身可殺，非常故；我不可殺，常故。問曰：我雖常故不可殺，但殺身則有殺罪。答曰：若殺身有殺罪者，《毘尼》中言：「自殺無殺罪。」罪福從惱他益他生，非自供養身、自殺身故有罪有福。以是故《毘尼》中言：「自殺身無殺罪，有愚癡、貪欲、瞋恚之咎。」

If the self were permanent, killing should incur no offense. The body can be killed because it is impermanent; a permanent self could not be killed. Question: Though the permanent self cannot be killed, killing the body incurs an offense. Answer: If killing a body as such incurred that offense, why does the Vinaya say that killing oneself does not constitute the offense of killing? Wrongdoing and merit arise from harming or benefiting others, not simply from serving or killing one’s own body. Thus the Vinaya says that killing oneself does not incur the killing offense, but does involve the faults of delusion, craving, or anger.

若神常者，不應死，不應生。何以故？汝等法神常，一切遍滿五道中，云何有死生？死名此處失，生名彼處出。以是故，不得言神常。

If a soul were permanent, it should neither die nor be born. Your teaching says it is permanent and pervades all five destinies. How could birth or death apply? Death means disappearing here, and birth appearing there. Therefore, a permanent soul cannot be asserted.

若神常者，亦應不受苦樂。何以故？苦來則憂，樂至則喜，若為憂喜所變者，則非常也。若常，應如虛空，雨不能濕，熱不能乾，亦無今世、後世。若神常者，亦不應有後世生、今世死。若神常者，則常有我見，不應得涅槃。若神常者，則無起無滅，不應有忘失；以其無神，識無常故，有忘有失，是故神非常也！如是等種種因緣，可知神非常相。

Nor could a permanent soul experience suffering and pleasure. Suffering produces sorrow; pleasure produces joy. What is changed by sorrow and joy is impermanent. Something permanent should be like space, neither wetted by rain nor dried by heat, with no present or future life. It should not die here or be born elsewhere. A permanent soul would also maintain self-view forever, preventing nirvāṇa. Without arising or ceasing, it should never forget or lose anything. Because there is no soul and consciousness is impermanent, forgetting and loss occur. Such reasons show that a soul cannot have permanence.

若神無常相者，亦無罪無福。若身無常，神亦無常，二事俱滅，則墮斷滅邊。墮斷滅，則無到後世受罪福者。若斷滅，則得涅槃不須斷結，亦不用後世罪福因緣。如是等種種因緣，可知神非無常。

If a soul were impermanent, there would likewise be no wrongdoing or merit. Both body and soul would perish, falling into annihilation, with no one continuing into another life to receive results. If annihilation were liberation, nirvāṇa would require neither severing fetters nor causes of future karmic results. These reasons show that the proposed soul cannot be established as impermanent either.

若神自在相、作相者，則應隨所欲得皆得；今所欲更不得，非所欲更得。若神自在，亦不應有作惡行，墮畜生惡道中。復次，一切眾生皆不樂苦，誰當好樂而更得苦？以是故，知神不自在，亦不作。又如人畏罪故，自強行善，若自在者，何以畏罪而自強修福？又諸眾生不得如意，常為煩惱愛縛所牽。如是等種種因緣，知神不自在、不自作。

If a soul were autonomous and an agent, whatever it wished should occur. Instead, desired things fail to happen and unwanted things occur. An autonomous soul should not perform evil actions and fall into animal or other bad destinies. No being desires suffering, yet beings suffer. Thus a soul is neither autonomous nor an independent agent. People force themselves to do good because they fear wrongdoing; an autonomous being would not need such fear. Beings continually fail to obtain their wishes and are dragged along by affliction and craving. These reasons show the soul is neither autonomous nor self-acting.

若神不自在、不自作者，是為無神相，言我者，即是六識，更無異事。復次，若不作者，云何閻羅王問罪人：「誰使汝作此罪者？」罪人答言：「是我自作。」以是故，知非不自作。

If it is neither autonomous nor self-acting, it has no characteristics of a soul. What is called ‘I’ is simply the six consciousnesses, without anything additional. Yet one cannot simply deny personal action: King Yama asks offenders, ‘Who made you commit this offense?’ They answer, ‘I did it myself.’ Thus, action cannot be denied.

The Supposed Subtle Body

Chinese Source

若神色相者，是事不然。何以故？一切色無常故。問曰：人云何言「色是我相」？答曰：有人言：「神在心中，微細如芥子，清淨名為淨色身。」更有人言「如麥」，有言「如豆」，有言「半寸」，有言「一寸」。初受身時，最在前受，譬如像骨；及其成身，如像已莊。有言：「大小隨人身，死壞時，此亦前出。」如此事，皆不爾也！何以故？一切色，四大所造，因緣生故無常。若神是色，色無常，神亦無常。

English Translation

A material soul is also untenable, because all material form is impermanent. Question: Who says the self is material? Answer: Some say a soul dwells in the heart, tiny as a mustard seed, pure and called a subtle pure body. Others say it is like a grain of wheat or a bean, half a cun or one cun in size. They say it first enters when a body is conceived, like a framework that is later furnished. Others say it matches the body’s size and leaves first at death. None of this holds. Material form is produced from the four elements through conditions and is impermanent. If the soul were material, it too would be impermanent.

若無常者，如上所說。問曰：身有二種：麁身及細身。麁身無常，細身是神，世世常去入五道中。答曰：此細身不可得。若有細身，應有處所可得，如五藏、四體一一處中求，皆不可得。

The consequences of impermanence were explained above. Question: There are coarse and subtle bodies. The coarse body is impermanent; the subtle body is the soul, continually entering the five destinies life after life. Answer: This subtle body cannot be found. If it existed, it should have a location, but examining the five organs and four limbs reveals none.

問曰：此細身微細，初死時已去，若活時則不可求得，汝云何能見？又此細身，非五情能見能知，唯有神通聖人乃能得見。答曰：若爾者，與無無異。如人死時，捨此生陰，入中陰中。是時，今世身滅，受中陰身，此無前後，滅時即生。

Question: It is too subtle to find while someone lives and has already departed when they die. It cannot be perceived by the five senses; only noble ones with supernatural powers can see it. Answer: Then your assertion does not distinguish it from something nonexistent. At death, one relinquishes the aggregates of this life and enters the intermediate state. As the present body ceases, an intermediate body arises, without an intervening interval.

譬如蠟印印泥，泥中受印，印即時壞，成壞一時，亦無前後。是時，受中陰中有，捨此中陰，受生陰有。汝言細身，即此中陰，中陰身無出無入。譬如然燈，生滅相續，不常不斷。佛言：「一切色眾，若過去、未來、現在，若內、若外，若麁、若細，皆悉無常。」汝神微細色者，亦應無常斷滅。如是等種種因緣，可知非色相。

It is like pressing a wax seal into clay: as its impression forms, the seal breaks; formation and destruction occur together, without one preceding the other. One then relinquishes intermediate existence and takes up the aggregates of a new birth. Your ‘subtle body’ is this intermediate body, but it does not enter or leave as a permanent thing. It is like a lamp’s flame, arising and ceasing in succession, neither permanent nor annihilated. The Buddha says, ‘All material aggregates—past, future, or present; internal or external; coarse or subtle—are impermanent.’ Your subtle material soul would also be impermanent and cease. Thus a material soul cannot be established.

神非無色相，無色者，四眾及無為。四眾無常故，不自在故，屬因緣故，不應是神。三無為中，不計有神，無所受故。如是等種種因緣，知神非無色相。

Nor is the soul immaterial. The immaterial consists of the four nonmaterial aggregates and the unconditioned. The four aggregates are impermanent, dependent, and conditioned, so cannot be the soul. Nor is a soul posited within the three unconditioned dharmas, which experience nothing. Thus an immaterial soul cannot be established.

Memory and Continuity without a Self

Chinese Source

如是天地間，若內若外，三世、十方，求我不可得。但十二入和合生六識，三事和合名觸，觸生受、想、思等心數法。是法中，無明力故身見生，身見生故謂有神。是身見，見苦諦，苦法智及苦比智則斷，斷時則不見有神。

English Translation

Seeking a self throughout heaven and earth, internally and externally, in the three times and ten directions, one finds none. The twelve sense bases condition the six consciousnesses. Their threefold conjunction is contact, which gives rise to feeling, perception, volition, and other mental factors. Through ignorance, identity-view arises and imagines a soul. On seeing the truth of suffering, knowledge of suffering in the Dharma and inferential knowledge of suffering sever this view; then no soul is perceived.

汝先言：「若內無神，色識念念生滅，云何分別知色青、黃、赤、白？」汝若有神，亦不能獨知，要依眼識故能知。若爾者，神無用也。眼識知色，色生滅，相似生，相似滅，然後心中有法生，名為念。是念相有為法，雖滅過去，是念能知。如聖人智慧力，能知未來世事；念念亦如是，能知過去法。若前眼識滅，生後眼識，後眼識轉利有力。色雖暫有不住，以念力利故能知。以是事故，雖念念生滅無常，能分別知色。

You asked how momentarily arising and ceasing form and consciousness could distinguish colors without an inner soul. Even with a soul, you would require visual consciousness to know them, making the soul unnecessary. Visual consciousness knows forms; forms arise and cease in similar succession. A mental dharma called recollection then arises. Although the conditioned event has ceased into the past, recollection can know it. Just as noble wisdom can know future events, recollection can know past dharmas. As earlier visual consciousness ceases, subsequent visual consciousness arises and becomes sharper and stronger. Though form does not remain, the strength of recollection makes it known. Thus colors can be distinguished despite momentary impermanence.

又汝言：「今現在人識新新生滅，身命斷時亦盡，諸行罪福，誰隨誰受？誰受苦樂？誰解脫者？」今當答汝：今未得實道，是人諸煩惱覆心，作生因緣業，死時從此五陰相續生五陰。譬如一燈，更然一燈。又如穀生，有三因緣：地、水、種子。後世身生，亦如是。有身，有有漏業，有結使，三事故後身生。是中身、業因緣，不可斷、不可破，但諸結使可斷。結使斷時，雖有殘身、殘業，可得解脫。如有穀子、有地，無水故不生。如是雖有身、有業，無愛結水潤則不生。是名雖無神，亦名得解脫。無明故縛，智慧故解，則我無所用。

You also asked who receives karmic results, experiences suffering and pleasure, or becomes liberated if consciousness ceases. Here is the answer: before realizing the true path, a person’s mind is covered by afflictions and creates karma conditioning rebirth. At death, aggregates arise in continuity from the present aggregates, like one lamp lighting another. Grain requires earth, water, and seed; likewise, a future body arises from a body, karma with influxes, and fetters. The conditions of body and karma cannot simply be destroyed, but fetters can be severed. Then, although a residual body and karma remain, liberation is possible. A seed and earth without water cannot sprout; likewise, without craving’s moisture, body and karma do not produce rebirth. Thus there is liberation without a soul. Ignorance binds; wisdom releases. A self is unnecessary.

復次，是名色和合，假名為人。是人為諸結所繫，得無漏智慧爪，解此諸結，是時，名人得解脫。如繩結、繩解，繩即是結，結無異法，世界中說結繩、解繩。名色亦如是，名色二法和合，假名為人。是結使與名色不異，但名為名色結、名色解。

Furthermore, the conjunction of name and form is provisionally called a person. Bound by fetters, this person gains the sharp claws of undefiled wisdom and loosens the knots: this is called liberation. A knot is not separate from the rope, yet people speak of tying and untying a rope. Likewise, name and form are provisionally a person. Fetters are not separate from them; one speaks of name and form bound or released.

受罪福亦如是，雖無一法為人實，名色故受罪福果，而人得名。譬如車載物，一一推之，竟無車實，然車受載物之名。人受罪福亦如是，名色受罪福，而人受其名。受苦樂亦如是。

Receiving the results of wrongdoing and merit is similar. No independent dharma is a person, but name and form receive the results, and the designation ‘person’ is used. A carriage carries a load although analysis finds no independent carriage; it still receives the name ‘carrier.’ Likewise, name and form receive karmic results while the person receives the designation. Experiencing suffering and pleasure is similar.

如是種種因緣，神不可得。神即是施者，受者亦如是。汝以神為人，以是故，施人不可得，受人不可得亦如是。如是種種因緣，是名財物、施人、受人不可得。

Through these reasons, a soul cannot be apprehended. Since the soul is what you take to be the giver, the giver cannot be apprehended; the recipient is likewise unapprehensible. Thus possessions, giver, and recipient cannot be apprehended.

Emptiness Does Not Destroy Reality

Chinese Source

問曰：若施於諸法，是如實相無所破、無所滅、無所生、無所作，何以故言「三事破析不可得」？

English Translation

Question: If giving accords with the true reality of dharmas, where nothing is destroyed, extinguished, produced, or made, why speak of analyzing these three things until they cannot be apprehended?

答曰：如凡夫人見施者、見受者、見財物，是為顛倒妄見，生世間受樂，福盡轉還。是故佛欲令菩薩行實道，得實果報，實果報則是佛道。佛為破妄見故，言三事不可得，實無所破。何以故？諸法從本已來畢竟空故。如是等種種無量因緣，不可得故，名為檀波羅蜜具足滿。

Answer: Ordinary people perceive a giver, recipient, and gift through inverted, false views. These produce worldly happiness, but when merit is exhausted, one returns to the cycle. The Buddha wishes bodhisattvas to practice the true path and attain its true fruit, Buddhahood. To refute false views, he says these three cannot be apprehended; in reality nothing is destroyed. Why? Dharmas have been ultimately empty from the beginning. Through these immeasurable reasons, their unapprehensibility is called complete fulfillment of the perfection of giving.

復次，若菩薩行檀波羅蜜，能生六波羅蜜，是時，名為檀波羅蜜具足滿。

Furthermore, when a bodhisattva’s practice of giving can give rise to all six pāramitās, this is called complete fulfillment of the perfection of giving.
