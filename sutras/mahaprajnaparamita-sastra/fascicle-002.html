How the Teaching Was Heard

Fascicle 2 continues the explanation of “Thus have I heard.” It asks how the words of the sutra can be called heard when the Buddha is self-awakened and does not learn from another teacher.

Why “Thus Have I Heard” Is Not a Fault

Chinese Source

大智度初品總說如是我聞釋論第三卷第二

「如是我聞：一時」，今當總說。問曰：若諸佛一切智人，自然無師，不隨他教，不受他法，不用他道，不從他聞而說法，何以言「如是我聞」？

Ru-Yi Translation

Chapter One of the Mahāprajñāpāramitā: Treatise Section Three, a General Explanation of “Thus Have I Heard,” Fascicle Two

“Thus have I heard: at one time” will now be explained generally. Question: The Buddhas are omniscient. They are naturally without a teacher; they do not follow another's instruction, do not receive another's Dharma, do not use another's path, and do not hear from another and then teach Dharma. Why, then, does the sutra say, “Thus have I heard”?

答曰：如汝所言，佛一切智人，自然無師，不應從他聞法而說。

佛法非但佛口說者是，一切世間真實善語、微妙好語，皆出佛法中。

如佛毘尼中說：「何者是佛法？佛法有五種人說：一者、佛自口說，二者、佛弟子說，三者、仙人說，四者、諸天說，五者、化人說。」

復次，如《釋提桓因得道經》，佛告憍尸迦：「世間真實善語、微妙好語，皆出我法中。」

Answer: It is as you say. The Buddha is omniscient and naturally without a teacher, so he should not hear Dharma from another and then teach it.

But the Buddha Dharma is not only what is spoken from the Buddha's own mouth. All true, wholesome speech and all subtle, beautiful speech in the world come forth from the Buddha Dharma.

As it says in the Buddha's Vinaya: “What is the Buddha Dharma? The Buddha Dharma may be spoken by five kinds of persons: first, spoken from the Buddha's own mouth; second, spoken by the Buddha's disciples; third, spoken by sages; fourth, spoken by devas; and fifth, spoken by transformation beings.”

Furthermore, in the Sutra on Śakra's Attainment of the Path, the Buddha told Kauśika, “All true, wholesome speech and all subtle, beautiful speech in the world come forth from my Dharma.”

如〈讚佛偈〉中說：「諸世善語，皆出佛法；善說無失，無過佛語。餘處雖有，善無過語，一切皆是，佛法之餘。諸外道中，設有好語，如虫食木，偶得成字。初中下法，自共相破，如鐵出金，誰當信者？如伊蘭中，牛頭栴檀；如苦種中，甘善美果。設能信者，是人則信，外經書中，自出好語。諸好實語，皆從佛出；如栴檀香，出摩梨山。除摩梨山，無出栴檀；如是除佛，無出實語。」

As the “Verses in Praise of the Buddha” say: “All wholesome speech in every world comes forth from the Buddha Dharma. Well-spoken words without fault do not surpass the Buddha's words. Even if there are elsewhere good and faultless words, all of them are remnants of the Buddha Dharma. If there are good words among non-Buddhists, they are like a worm eating wood and accidentally forming written characters. Their doctrines of beginning, middle, and end refute themselves and one another; if gold comes from iron, who would believe it? It is like ox-head sandalwood growing among foul eranda trees; it is like sweet and beautiful fruit growing from bitter seeds. If someone is able to believe this, then that person believes that good words arise on their own in non-Buddhist scriptures. All good and true words come from the Buddha, just as sandalwood fragrance comes from Mount Malaya. Apart from Mount Malaya, sandalwood does not arise; in the same way, apart from the Buddha, true speech does not arise.”

復次，「如是我聞」，是阿難等佛大弟子輩說，入佛法相故，名為佛法。

如佛般涅槃時，於俱夷那竭國薩羅雙樹間，北首臥，將入涅槃。爾時，阿難親屬愛未除，未離欲故，心沒憂海，不能自出。爾時，長老阿泥盧豆語阿難：「汝守佛法藏人，不應如凡人自沒憂海！一切有為法，是無常相，汝莫愁憂！又佛手付汝法，汝今愁悶，失所受事。汝當問佛：『佛般涅槃後，我曹云何行道？誰當作師？惡口車匿，云何共住？佛經初作何等語？』如是種種未來事，應問佛。」

阿難聞是事，悶心小醒，得念道力助，於佛末後臥床邊，以此事問佛。

Furthermore, “Thus have I heard” was spoken by Ānanda and other great disciples of the Buddha. Because it enters into the character of the Buddha Dharma, it is called Buddha Dharma.

When the Buddha was about to enter parinirvāṇa, he was lying with his head to the north between the twin śāla trees in the land of Kuśinagara. At that time, because Ānanda had not yet removed affection for relatives and had not yet become free from desire, his mind sank into an ocean of grief and he could not lift himself out. Then the elder Aniruddha said to Ānanda, “You are the one who guards the treasury of the Buddha Dharma. You should not sink into an ocean of grief like an ordinary person. All conditioned dharmas have the mark of impermanence; do not grieve. Moreover, the Buddha has placed the Dharma in your hands. Now, because you are distressed and confused, you are losing what has been entrusted to you. You should ask the Buddha: ‘After the Buddha enters parinirvāṇa, how should we practice the path? Who should be our teacher? How should we live together with the harsh-speaking Channa? What words should stand at the beginning of the Buddha's sutras?’ These and other matters concerning the future should be asked of the Buddha.”

When Ānanda heard this, his distressed mind cleared a little. Supported by the strength of mindfulness of the path, he went to the side of the Buddha's final bed and asked him about these matters.

佛告阿難：「若今現前，若我過去後，自依止，法依止，不餘依止。云何比丘自依止、法依止、不餘依止？於是比丘內觀身，常當一心智慧，勤修精進，除世間貪憂；外身、內外身觀，亦如是；受、心、法念處，亦復如是。是名比丘自依止、法依止、不餘依止。從今日，解脫戒經即是大師；如解脫戒經說身業、口業，應如是行。車匿比丘，我涅槃後，如梵法治；若心濡伏者，應教《刪陀迦旃延經》，即可得道。復次，我三阿僧祇劫所集法寶藏，是藏初應作是說：『如是我聞：一時，佛在某方、某國土、某處樹林中。』何以故？過去諸佛經初皆稱是語，未來諸佛經初亦稱是語，現在諸佛末後般涅槃時亦教稱是語。今我般涅槃後，經初亦應稱『如是我聞：一時』。」

The Buddha told Ānanda, “Whether I am present now or have passed away, rely on yourselves, rely on the Dharma, and do not rely on anything else. How does a monk rely on himself, rely on the Dharma, and not rely on anything else? Here, a monk observes the body internally, always with one-pointed wisdom, diligently cultivating vigor and removing greed and sorrow toward the world. Observing the body externally and observing the body internally and externally are also like this. The establishments of mindfulness with regard to feeling, mind, and dharmas are also like this. This is called a monk relying on himself, relying on the Dharma, and not relying on anything else. From today onward, the Prātimokṣa is the great teacher. Whatever bodily and verbal actions are taught in the Prātimokṣa should be practiced accordingly. As for the monk Channa, after I enter nirvāṇa, discipline him with the brahmā punishment. If his mind becomes gentle and subdued, he should be taught the Śāṇḍaka Kātyāyana Sutra, and then he can attain the path. Furthermore, the Dharma treasury I have gathered over three asaṃkhyeya kalpas should begin in this way: ‘Thus have I heard: at one time, the Buddha was in such-and-such a region, such-and-such a country, in such-and-such a forest.’ Why? Sutras of the Buddhas of the past all began with these words. Sutras of the Buddhas of the future will also begin with these words. Buddhas of the present, at the time of their final parinirvāṇa, also teach that these words should be used. After I enter parinirvāṇa, the sutras should also begin, ‘Thus have I heard: at one time.’”

是故當知是佛所教，非佛自言「如是我聞」。佛一切智人，自然無師故，不應言「我聞」。若佛自說「如是我聞」，有所不知者，可有此難。阿難問佛，佛教是語，是弟子所言「如是我聞」，無有咎。

Therefore one should know that these words were taught by the Buddha; they are not the Buddha himself saying, “Thus have I heard.” Since the Buddha is omniscient and naturally without a teacher, he should not say, “I heard.” If the Buddha himself had said, “Thus have I heard,” then one could raise this objection that there was something he did not know. But Ānanda asked the Buddha, and the Buddha taught these words. They are the disciple's words, “Thus have I heard,” and there is no fault.

Mahākāśyapa Preserves the Dharma Treasury

Chinese Source

復次，欲令佛法久住世間故，長老摩訶迦葉等諸阿羅漢問阿難：「佛初何處說法？說何等法？」阿難答：「如是我聞：一時，佛在波羅捺國仙人鹿林中，為五比丘說是苦聖諦。我本不從他聞，法中正憶念得眼、智、明、覺。」是經，是中應廣說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, because they wished the Buddha Dharma to remain long in the world, Elder Mahākāśyapa and the other arhats asked Ānanda, “Where did the Buddha first teach the Dharma? What Dharma did he teach?” Ānanda answered, “Thus have I heard: at one time, the Buddha was in the Deer Park of the Sages in the country of Vārāṇasī, teaching the noble truth of suffering to the five monks. I did not originally hear this from another; within the Dharma, through right recollection, I obtained eye, knowledge, illumination, and awakening.” This sutra should be explained fully there.

如《集法經》中廣說：佛入涅槃時，地六種動，諸河反流，疾風暴發，黑雲四起，惡雷掣電，雹雨驟墮，處處星流，師子惡獸哮吼喚呼，諸天、世人皆大號咷。諸天、人等皆發是言：「佛取涅槃，一何疾哉！世間眼滅！」當是時間，一切草木、藥樹，華葉一時剖裂；諸須彌山王盡皆傾搖，海水波揚，地大震動，山崖崩落；諸樹摧折，四面煙起，甚大可畏。陂池江河盡皆嬈濁，彗星晝出。諸人啼哭，諸天憂愁，諸天女等郁伊哽咽，涕淚交流。諸學人等默然不樂，諸無學人念有為諸法一切無常。如是天、人、夜叉、羅剎、犍闥婆、甄陀羅、摩睺羅伽及諸龍等，皆大憂愁。

As the Sutra on the Collection of Dharma explains in detail: when the Buddha entered nirvāṇa, the earth shook in six ways, rivers flowed backward, violent winds suddenly arose, black clouds gathered on all sides, terrible thunder flashed with lightning, hail and rain fell in torrents, and meteors streaked everywhere. Lions and fierce beasts roared and cried out, and devas and humans all wailed loudly. Devas and humans all said, “How quickly the Buddha has entered nirvāṇa! The eye of the world is extinguished!” At that time, all grasses, trees, and medicinal trees split open at once in their flowers and leaves. All the kings of Mount Sumeru shook and tilted; ocean waters surged; the great earth quaked; cliffs and mountains collapsed. Trees broke and fell, smoke rose in all four directions, and it was extremely terrifying. Ponds, lakes, rivers, and streams all became disturbed and muddy, and comets appeared in the daytime. People wept, devas grieved, and heavenly maidens choked with sorrow as tears flowed down. Learners were silent and unhappy, while those beyond learning reflected that all conditioned dharmas are impermanent. In this way, devas, humans, yakṣas, rākṣasas, gandharvas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, dragons, and others were all deeply grieved.

諸阿羅漢度老病死海，心念言：「已渡凡夫恩愛河，老病死券已裂破；見身篋中四大蛇，今入無餘滅涅槃！」

諸大阿羅漢各各隨意，於諸山林流泉谿谷，處處捨身而般涅槃；更有諸阿羅漢於虛空中飛騰而去，譬如鴈王現種種神力，令眾人心信清淨，然後般涅槃。六欲天乃至遍淨天等，見諸阿羅漢皆取滅度，各心念言：「佛日既沒，種種禪定解脫智慧弟子光亦滅。是諸眾生有種種婬怒癡病，是法藥師輩今疾滅度，誰當治者？無量智慧大海中生弟子蓮華，今已乾枯；法樹摧折，法雲散滅；大智象王既逝，象子亦隨去；法商人過去，從誰求法寶？」如偈說：「佛已永寂入涅槃，諸滅結眾亦過去；世界如是空無智，癡冥遂增智燈滅！」

The arhats had crossed the ocean of aging, sickness, and death. They thought: “We have already crossed the river of ordinary people's affection and attachment. The bond of aging, sickness, and death has already been torn apart. Seeing the four great snakes within the basket of the body, we now enter nirvāṇa without remainder.”

The great arhats each followed their own intention. In mountains, forests, flowing springs, ravines, and valleys, they abandoned their bodies everywhere and entered parinirvāṇa. Other arhats flew away through the sky, like kings of geese, displaying many kinds of spiritual power so that people's minds would become faithful and pure; only then did they enter parinirvāṇa. The devas of the six desire heavens up to the Śubhakṛtsna heavens saw that all the arhats were entering extinction, and each thought: “The sun of the Buddha has already set, and the light of his disciples, with their many meditative concentrations, liberations, and wisdom, is also extinguished. These beings have many illnesses of desire, anger, and delusion. Now that the physicians of Dharma are quickly entering extinction, who will treat them? The disciple-lotuses born in the great ocean of immeasurable wisdom have now withered. The Dharma tree is broken, the Dharma cloud has scattered. The great wise elephant king has departed, and the elephant calves follow him. The merchants of Dharma have passed away; from whom can we seek the jewels of Dharma?” As a verse says: “The Buddha has forever entered the stillness of nirvāṇa; the assembly who extinguished the fetters has also passed away. The world is thus empty of wisdom; the darkness of delusion increases, and the lamp of wisdom is extinguished.”

爾時，諸天禮摩訶迦葉足，說偈言：「耆年欲恚慢已除，其形譬如紫金柱，上下端嚴妙無比，目明清淨如蓮華！」

如是讚已，白大迦葉言：「大德迦葉！仁者知不？法船欲破，法城欲頹，法海欲竭，法幢欲倒，法燈欲滅；說法人欲去，行道人漸少，惡人力轉盛，當以大慈建立佛法！」

爾時，大迦葉心如大海，澄靜不動，良久而答：「汝等善說！實如所言，世間不久，無智盲冥。」於是大迦葉默然受請。爾時，諸天禮大迦葉足，忽然不現，各自還去。

At that time the devas bowed at Mahākāśyapa's feet and spoke this verse: “Elder, desire, anger, and conceit have been removed. Your form is like a column of purple gold. Above and below, you are upright, dignified, wondrous, and peerless; your bright, pure eyes are like lotus flowers.”

After praising him in this way, they said to Mahākāśyapa, “Great Virtuous Kāśyapa, do you know? The Dharma ship is about to break apart, the Dharma city is about to collapse, the Dharma ocean is about to dry up, the Dharma banner is about to fall, and the Dharma lamp is about to go out. Those who teach the Dharma are about to depart, those who practice the path are gradually becoming few, and the power of evil people is growing stronger. With great compassion, you should establish the Buddha Dharma.”

At that time Mahākāśyapa's mind was like the great ocean, clear, still, and unmoving. After a long while he answered, “What you have said is good. It is truly as you say: before long the world will be without wisdom, blind, and dark.” Then Mahākāśyapa silently accepted their request. The devas bowed at Mahākāśyapa's feet and suddenly disappeared, each returning to his own place.

是時，大迦葉思惟：「我今云何使是三阿僧祇劫難得佛法而得久住？」如是思惟竟，「我知是法可使久住，應當結集修妬路、阿毘曇、毘尼，作三法藏。如是佛法可得久住，未來世人可得受行。所以者何？佛世世勤苦慈愍眾生故，學得是法，為人演說；我曹亦應承用佛教，宣揚開化。」

是時，大迦葉作是語竟，住須彌山頂，撾銅揵稚，說此偈言：「佛諸弟子！若念於佛，當報佛恩，莫入涅槃！」

At that time Mahākāśyapa thought, “How can I now cause this Buddha Dharma, so difficult to obtain even over three asaṃkhyeya kalpas, to remain long?” After thinking in this way, he said, “I know how this Dharma can be made to remain long. We should gather together the sūtras, the abhidharma, and the vinaya, making the Three Dharma Treasuries. In this way the Buddha Dharma can remain long, and people of future generations can receive and practice it. Why? Because in life after life, through diligent hardship and compassionate care for beings, the Buddha learned this Dharma and taught it to people. We too should receive and apply the Buddha's instruction, proclaiming it widely and opening beings to transformation.”

After Mahākāśyapa had spoken these words, he stood on the summit of Mount Sumeru, struck the bronze gaṇḍī, and spoke this verse: “Disciples of the Buddha! If you are mindful of the Buddha, you should repay the Buddha's kindness. Do not enter nirvāṇa!”

是揵稚音、大迦葉語聲，遍至三千大千世界，皆悉聞知；諸有弟子得神力者，皆來集會大迦葉所。爾時，大迦葉告諸會者：「佛法欲滅，佛從三阿僧祇劫種種勤苦，慈愍眾生，學得是法。佛般涅槃已，諸弟子知法、持法、誦法者，皆亦隨佛滅度；法今欲滅，未來眾生甚可憐愍，失智慧眼，愚癡盲冥；佛大慈悲愍傷眾生，我曹應當承用佛教，須待結集經藏竟，隨意滅度。」

諸來眾會，皆受教住。爾時，大迦葉選得千人，除善阿難，盡皆阿羅漢，得六神通，得共解脫，無礙解脫；悉得三明，禪定自在，能逆順行諸三昧，皆悉無礙。誦讀三藏，知內外經書，諸外道家十八種大經，盡亦讀知，皆能論議，降伏異學。

The sound of that gaṇḍī and Mahākāśyapa's voice reached throughout the three-thousand great-thousand world system, and all heard and knew it. All disciples who had attained spiritual power came and gathered at Mahākāśyapa's place. At that time Mahākāśyapa told the assembly, “The Buddha Dharma is about to perish. Over three asaṃkhyeya kalpas, the Buddha underwent many kinds of hardship, compassionately caring for beings, and learned this Dharma. Now that the Buddha has entered parinirvāṇa, the disciples who know the Dharma, preserve the Dharma, and recite the Dharma are also following the Buddha into extinction. The Dharma is now about to perish. Future beings are deeply pitiable: they will lose the eye of wisdom and become foolish, blind, and dark. Because the Buddha, with great loving-kindness and compassion, took pity on beings, we should receive and apply the Buddha's instruction. We must wait until the scripture treasury has been gathered; after that, each may enter extinction as he wishes.”

All who had come to the assembly accepted the instruction and remained. At that time Mahākāśyapa selected one thousand people. Except for the virtuous Ānanda, all were arhats. They had attained the six spiritual powers, shared liberation, and unobstructed liberation. They had all attained the three knowledges; they were free in meditative concentration, able to enter samādhis in forward and reverse order, all without obstruction. They recited the Three Baskets, knew Buddhist and non-Buddhist scriptures, and had also fully read and understood the eighteen kinds of great scriptures of the non-Buddhist schools. They were all able to debate and subdue those of different teachings.

Why One Thousand Arhats Were Chosen

Chinese Source

問曰：是時，有如是等無數阿羅漢，何以故正選取千人，不多取耶？

答曰：頻婆娑羅王得道，八萬四千官屬亦各得道。是時，王教勅宮中，常設飯食，供養千人；阿闍貰王不斷是法。爾時，大迦葉思惟言：「若我等常乞食者，當有外道強來難問，廢闕法事；今王舍城常設飯食供給千人，是中可住結集經藏。」以是故，選取千人，不得多取。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: At that time there were countless arhats such as these. Why did Mahākāśyapa select exactly one thousand people and not more?

Answer: King Bimbisāra had attained the path, and his eighty-four thousand officials had each also attained the path. At that time the king ordered the palace to constantly prepare meals and offer them to one thousand people. King Ajātaśatru did not discontinue this practice. Then Mahākāśyapa thought, “If we always go on almsround, non-Buddhists may forcefully come and challenge us with questions, causing the Dharma work to be neglected. Since Rājagṛha constantly provides food for one thousand people, we can stay there and gather the scripture treasury.” For this reason he selected one thousand people and could not take more.

是時，大迦葉與千人俱，到王舍城耆闍崛山中，告語阿闍世王：「給我等食，日日送來，今我曹等結集經藏，不得他行。」

是中夏安居三月，初十五日說戒時，集和合僧。大迦葉入禪定，以天眼觀，今是眾中，誰有煩惱未盡應逐出者！唯有阿難一人不盡，餘九百九十九人諸漏已盡，清淨無垢。大迦葉從禪定起，眾中手牽阿難出，言：「今清淨眾中結集經藏。汝結未盡，不應住此！」

At that time Mahākāśyapa, together with the one thousand people, went to Vulture Peak at Rājagṛha and told King Ajātaśatru, “Provide food for us and send it every day. We are now gathering the scripture treasury and cannot go elsewhere.”

There they entered the three-month rains retreat. On the fifteenth day at the beginning, when the precepts were recited, the harmonious Saṅgha assembled. Mahākāśyapa entered meditative concentration and, with the divine eye, observed who in the assembly still had afflictions not yet exhausted and should be expelled. Only Ānanda had not yet exhausted them; the other nine hundred ninety-nine had ended all outflows and were pure and stainless. Mahākāśyapa arose from meditation, took Ānanda by the hand in the midst of the assembly, led him out, and said, “The scripture treasury is being gathered within this pure assembly. Your fetters have not yet been exhausted, so you should not remain here.”

是時，阿難慚恥悲泣，而自念言：「我二十五年隨侍世尊，供給左右，未曾得如是苦惱；佛實大德，慈悲含忍。」念已，白大迦葉言：「我能有力，久可得道；但諸佛法，阿羅漢者不得供給左右使令；以是故，我留殘結不盡斷耳。」

At that time Ānanda felt ashamed and wept sadly. He thought to himself, “For twenty-five years I attended the World-Honored One, serving at his side, and I never experienced such distress. The Buddha truly had great virtue: he was compassionate, forbearing, and patient.” After thinking this, he said to Mahākāśyapa, “I have the ability and could have attained the path long ago. But according to the Dharma of the Buddhas, an arhat may not serve at the side of a Buddha as attendant. For this reason, I left a remainder of fetters and did not completely cut them off.”

大迦葉言：「汝更有罪！佛意不欲聽女人出家，汝慇懃勸請，佛聽為道；以是故，佛之正法五百歲而衰微，是汝突吉羅罪！」阿難言：「我憐愍瞿曇彌，又三世諸佛法皆有四部眾，我釋迦文佛云何獨無？」

大迦葉復言：「佛欲涅槃時，近俱夷那竭城，脊痛，四疊漚多羅僧敷臥，語汝言：『我須水。』汝不供給，是汝突吉羅罪！」阿難答言：「是時，五百乘車，截流而渡，令水渾濁，以是故不取。」大迦葉復言：「正使水濁，佛有大神力，能令大海濁水清淨。汝何以不與？是汝之罪，汝去作突吉羅懺悔！」

Mahākāśyapa said, “You have another fault. The Buddha's intention was not to allow women to leave home, but you earnestly urged and requested him, and the Buddha allowed them to practice the path. Because of this, the Buddha's true Dharma will decline after five hundred years. This is your duṣkṛta offense.” Ānanda said, “I had compassion for Gautamī. Also, the Dharma of the Buddhas of the three times all has four assemblies. How could my Buddha Śākyamuni alone not have them?”

Mahākāśyapa again said, “When the Buddha was about to enter nirvāṇa near the city of Kuśinagara, his back hurt. He folded his uttarāsaṅga robe in four layers, lay down on it, and said to you, ‘I need water.’ You did not provide it. This is your duṣkṛta offense.” Ānanda answered, “At that time, five hundred carts crossed through the stream, making the water muddy. For this reason, I did not take it.” Mahākāśyapa again said, “Even if the water was muddy, the Buddha had great spiritual power and could have made the muddy water of the great ocean pure. Why did you not give it to him? This is your fault. Go and confess the duṣkṛta offense.”

大迦葉復言：「佛問汝：『若有人四神足好修，可住壽一劫，若減一劫；佛四神足好修，欲住壽一劫，若減一劫。』汝默然不答。問汝至三，汝故默然。汝若答佛：『佛四神足好修，應住一劫，若減一劫。』由汝故，令佛世尊早入涅槃，是汝突吉羅罪！」阿難言：「魔蔽我心，是故無言；我非惡心而不答佛。」

大迦葉復言：「汝與佛疊僧伽梨衣，以足蹈上，是汝突吉羅罪。」阿難言：「爾時，有大風起，無人助我捉衣，時風吹來墮我腳下，非不恭敬，故蹈佛衣。」

Mahākāśyapa again said, “The Buddha asked you, ‘If someone has well cultivated the four bases of spiritual power, that person can remain alive for a kalpa or a little less than a kalpa. The Buddha has well cultivated the four bases of spiritual power and, if he wishes, can remain alive for a kalpa or a little less than a kalpa.’ You remained silent and did not answer. He asked you three times, and still you remained silent. If you had answered the Buddha, ‘The Buddha has well cultivated the four bases of spiritual power and should remain for a kalpa or a little less than a kalpa,’ then because of you the Buddha, the World-Honored One, would not have entered nirvāṇa early. This is your duṣkṛta offense.” Ānanda said, “Māra covered my mind; therefore I said nothing. It was not from an evil mind that I failed to answer the Buddha.”

Mahākāśyapa again said, “When you folded the Buddha's saṅghāṭī robe, you stepped on it with your foot. This is your duṣkṛta offense.” Ānanda said, “At that time a great wind arose, and there was no one to help me hold the robe. The wind blew it down under my foot. It was not because I was disrespectful that I stepped on the Buddha's robe.”

大迦葉復言：「佛陰藏相，般涅槃後以示女人，是何可恥？是汝突吉羅罪！」阿難言：「爾時，我思惟：『若諸女人見佛陰藏相者，便自羞恥女人形，欲得男子身，修行佛相，種福德根。』以是故，我示女人，不為無恥而故破戒。」

大迦葉言：「汝有六種突吉羅罪，盡應僧中悔過！」阿難言：「諾！隨長老大迦葉及僧所教！」是時，阿難長跪合手，偏袒右肩，脫革屣，六種突吉羅罪懺悔。大迦葉於僧中，手牽阿難出，語阿難言：「斷汝漏盡，然後來入；殘結未盡，汝勿來也！」如是語竟，便自閉門。

Mahākāśyapa again said, “After the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, you showed women the Buddha's hidden-organ mark. How shameful! This is your duṣkṛta offense.” Ānanda said, “At that time I thought, ‘If these women see the Buddha's hidden-organ mark, they will feel ashamed of the female form, wish to obtain a male body, cultivate the marks of a Buddha, and plant roots of merit.’ For this reason, I showed it to the women. I did not deliberately break the precepts out of shamelessness.”

Mahākāśyapa said, “You have six kinds of duṣkṛta offenses. You should confess them all before the Saṅgha.” Ānanda said, “Yes. I will follow the instruction of Elder Mahākāśyapa and the Saṅgha.” At that time Ānanda knelt upright, joined his palms, bared his right shoulder, removed his leather sandals, and confessed the six kinds of duṣkṛta offenses. In the midst of the Saṅgha, Mahākāśyapa took Ānanda by the hand, led him out, and said to him, “Cut off your outflows completely, and then come in. As long as the remaining fetters are not yet exhausted, do not come.” Having said this, he closed the door himself.

Gavāṃpati and Ānanda's Breakthrough

The junior monk bowed with his head and face at the feet of the Saṅgha and circled to the right three times. Like a garuḍa bird, he flew up into the sky and went to Gavāṃpati's place. He bowed with his head and face, and said to Gavāṃpati, “Gentle and good Great Virtuous One, few in desires and content, always dwelling in meditative concentration, Mahākāśyapa sends greetings and has this message: the Saṅgha now has a great Dharma matter. Please come down quickly and see the jewel-gathering of the assembly.” At that time Gavāṃpati felt doubt arise in his mind and said to the monk, “Is the Saṅgha calling me because there is a dispute? Has no one split the Saṅgha? Has the sun of the Buddha entered extinction?” The monk said, “It is truly as you say. The Great Teacher, the Buddha, has already entered extinction.” Gavāṃpati said, “The Buddha has entered extinction so quickly! The eye of the world is extinguished. Where now is my preceptor, Śāriputra, the commander who was able to follow the Buddha in turning the wheel of Dharma?” The monk answered, “He entered nirvāṇa earlier.” Gavāṃpati said, “The Great Teacher and the Dharma commander are each separated from us. What can be done? Where is Mahāmaudgalyāyana now?” The monk said, “He too has entered extinction.” Gavāṃpati said, “The Buddha Dharma is about to scatter. Great beings have passed away; beings are pitiable.” He asked, “What is Elder Ānanda doing now?” The monk said, “After the Buddha entered extinction, Elder Ānanda was grieving, weeping, confused, and unable to compose himself.” Gavāṃpati said, “Ānanda is distressed because he still has the fetter of attachment; separation gives rise to suffering. And what of Rāhula?” The monk answered, “Because Rāhula attained arhatship, he has no grief and no sorrow; he only contemplates the impermanent marks of dharmas.” Gavāṃpati said, “Attachment, which is difficult to cut off, has already been cut off, so there is no grief or sorrow.” Gavāṃpati then said, “Having lost the Great Teacher free from desire, what purpose is there for me to remain in this Śirīṣa Tree Grove? My preceptor and the Great Teacher have both entered extinction. I can no longer descend again to Jambudvīpa. I will remain here and enter parinirvāṇa.”

說是言已，入禪定中，踊在虛空，身放光明；又出水火，手摩日月，現種種神變，自心出火燒身，身中出水，四道流下，至大迦葉所。水中有聲，說此偈言：「憍梵鉢提稽首禮，妙眾第一大德僧，聞佛滅度我隨去，如大象去象子隨！」

爾時，下坐比丘持衣鉢還僧。

After saying these words, he entered meditative concentration and rose into the sky. His body emitted light; he also produced water and fire, touched the sun and moon with his hands, and displayed many kinds of spiritual transformations. From his own mind he produced fire that burned his body; from his body came water, flowing down in four streams until it reached Mahākāśyapa. From within the water there was a voice speaking this verse: “Gavāṃpati bows his head in homage to the excellent and foremost great virtuous Saṅgha. Hearing that the Buddha has entered extinction, I follow him and depart, like a great elephant departing and the elephant calf following after.”

At that time the junior monk took Gavāṃpati's robe and bowl and returned to the Saṅgha.

是時中間，阿難思惟諸法，求盡殘漏；其夜坐禪經行，慇懃求道。是阿難智慧多，定力少，是故不即得道；定智等者，乃可速得。後夜欲過，疲極偃息，却臥就枕，頭未至枕，廓然得悟；如電光出，闇者見道。阿難如是入金剛定，破一切諸煩惱山；得三明、六神通、共解脫，作大力阿羅漢。

During this interval, Ānanda contemplated dharmas and sought to exhaust his remaining outflows. That night he sat in meditation and walked in meditation, earnestly seeking the path. Ānanda had much wisdom but little power of concentration, and for this reason he did not immediately attain the path. When concentration and wisdom are equal, attainment can come quickly. As the latter part of the night was about to pass, he was utterly exhausted and lay down to rest. As he reclined toward his pillow, before his head reached the pillow, he suddenly awakened. It was like a flash of lightning appearing, by which someone in darkness sees the path. In this way Ānanda entered vajra concentration and shattered all the mountains of affliction. He attained the three knowledges, the six spiritual powers, and shared liberation, becoming a powerful arhat.

即夜到僧堂門，敲門而喚。大迦葉問言：「敲門者誰？」答言：「我是阿難。」大迦葉言：「汝何以來？」阿難言：「我今夜得盡諸漏。」大迦葉言：「不與汝開門，汝從門鑰孔中來！」阿難答言：「可爾！」即以神力從門鑰孔中入，禮拜僧足懺悔：「大迦葉！莫復見責！」

大迦葉手摩阿難頭言：「我故為汝，使汝得道；汝無嫌恨，我亦如是，以汝自證。譬如手畫虛空，無所染著；阿羅漢心亦如是，一切法中得無所著。復汝本坐。」

That very night he went to the door of the Saṅgha hall, knocked, and called out. Mahākāśyapa asked, “Who is knocking?” He answered, “I am Ānanda.” Mahākāśyapa said, “Why have you come?” Ānanda said, “Tonight I have exhausted all the outflows.” Mahākāśyapa said, “I will not open the door for you. Come in through the keyhole.” Ānanda answered, “Very well.” Then, by spiritual power, he entered through the keyhole, bowed at the feet of the Saṅgha, and confessed, “Mahākāśyapa, please do not reproach me again.”

Mahākāśyapa placed his hand on Ānanda's head and said, “I did this deliberately for you, so that you would attain the path. You should have no resentment; I am the same toward you, because you have realized it for yourself. It is like drawing with one's hand in empty space: nothing is stained or attached. The mind of an arhat is also like this; among all dharmas, it attains nonattachment. Return to your original seat.”

At that time Elder Aniruddha spoke this verse: “Alas, the world is impermanent, like the moon in water or the plantain tree. Though his merit filled the three realms, it has been destroyed by the wind of impermanence.”

At that time Mahākāśyapa again spoke this verse: “The power of impermanence is very great. Foolish or wise, poor or rich, noble or lowly, those who have attained the path and those who have not: none can escape it. It is not overcome by clever words or wondrous treasures, not by deception, force, or dispute. Like fire burning all things, the mark of impermanence is simply the nature of dharmas.”

大迦葉語阿難：「從《轉法輪經》至《大般涅槃》，集作四阿含：《增一阿含》、《中阿含》、《長阿含》、《相應阿含》。是名修妬路法藏。」

The Meaning of Bhagavān

Chinese Source

〖經〗婆伽婆。

Chapter One of the Mahāprajñāpāramitā: Explanation of Bhagavān, Section Four

Sutra: Bhagavān.

〖論〗今當說。

釋曰：云何名婆伽婆？

Explanation: What is meant by Bhagavān?

復次，「婆伽」名「分別」，「婆」名「巧」，巧分別諸法總相別相，故名「婆伽婆」。

As for Bhagavān, bhaga means “virtue,” and va means “having.” Thus it means “one who has virtue.”

Furthermore, bhaga means “distinguishing,” and va means “skillful.” Because he skillfully distinguishes the general characteristics and particular characteristics of all dharmas, he is called Bhagavān.

復次，「婆伽」名「名聲」，「婆」名「有」，是名「有名聲」，無有得名聲如佛者。轉輪聖王、釋、梵、護世者，無有及佛，何況諸餘凡庶！

Furthermore, bhaga means “renown,” and va means “having.” Thus it means “one who has renown.” No one has attained renown like the Buddha. Wheel-turning sage kings, Śakra, Brahmā, and the world-protectors do not equal the Buddha, much less ordinary people.

所以者何？轉輪聖王與結相應，佛已離結；轉輪聖王沒在生、老、病、死泥中，佛已得渡；轉輪聖王為恩愛奴僕，佛已永離；轉輪聖王處在世間曠野災患，佛已得離；轉輪聖王處在無明闇中，佛處第一明中；轉輪聖王若極多領四天下，佛領無量諸世界；轉輪聖王財自在，佛心自在；轉輪聖王貪求天樂，佛乃至有頂樂亦不貪著；轉輪聖王從他求樂，佛內心自樂。

Why is this so? A wheel-turning sage king is still bound together with the fetters; the Buddha has already departed from the fetters. A wheel-turning sage king is sunk in the mud of birth, aging, sickness, and death; the Buddha has already crossed beyond it. A wheel-turning sage king is a servant of affection and craving; the Buddha has left them forever. A wheel-turning sage king dwells amid the calamities of the wilderness of the world; the Buddha has departed from them. A wheel-turning sage king dwells in the darkness of ignorance; the Buddha dwells in the foremost light. A wheel-turning sage king, at most, rules the four continents; the Buddha rules immeasurable worlds. A wheel-turning sage king is sovereign over wealth; the Buddha is sovereign over his mind. A wheel-turning sage king seeks heavenly pleasure; the Buddha is not attached even to the pleasure of the peak of existence. A wheel-turning sage king seeks happiness from others; the Buddha is inwardly happy in his own mind.

以是因緣，佛勝轉輪聖王。諸餘釋、梵、護世者，亦復如是，但於轉輪聖王小勝。

For these reasons, the Buddha surpasses the wheel-turning sage king. The same is true in relation to the other beings such as Śakra, Brahmā, and the world-protectors; they are only slightly superior to a wheel-turning sage king.

Breaking Desire, Anger, and Delusion

Chinese Source

復次，「婆伽」名「破」，「婆」名「能」，是人能破婬怒癡故，稱為「婆伽婆」。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, bhaga means “breaking,” and va means “able.” Because this person is able to break desire, anger, and delusion, he is called Bhagavān.

問曰：如阿羅漢、辟支佛亦破婬怒癡，與佛何異？

Question: Arhats and pratyekabuddhas also break desire, anger, and delusion. How are they different from the Buddha?

Answer: Although arhats and pratyekabuddhas have broken the three poisons, their residual scent is not exhausted. It is like fragrance in a vessel: although the fragrance has gone out, its remaining scent is still there. It is also like grass, wood, and firewood burning and producing smoke: the charcoal and ashes are not exhausted because the power of the fire is weak. The Buddha has forever exhausted the three poisons without remainder. This is like the fire at the end of a kalpa burning Mount Sumeru, so that the whole earth is completely exhausted, with no smoke and no charcoal. Śāriputra still had a residual scent of anger, Nanda still had a residual scent of desire, and Pilindavatsa still had a residual scent of conceit. It is like a person who has been bound in chains: when first released, he still cannot walk freely.

At that time the Buddha rose from meditation and walked in meditation. Rāhula followed the Buddha as he walked. The Buddha asked Rāhula, “Why are you thin and weak?” Rāhula answered the Buddha with a verse: “If a person eats oil, he gains strength. If he eats ghee, he gains good complexion. 佛問羅睺羅：「是眾中誰為上座？」羅睺羅答：「和上舍利弗。」佛言：「舍利弗食不淨食。」爾時，舍利弗轉聞是語，即時吐食，自作誓言：「從今日不復受人請。」

The Buddha asked Rāhula, “Who is the senior elder in this assembly?” Rāhula answered, “My preceptor Śāriputra.” The Buddha said, “Śāriputra eats impure food.” At that time, when Śāriputra heard these words indirectly, he immediately vomited up his food and made this vow: “From this day forward, I will no longer accept invitations from others.”

是時，波斯匿王、長者須達多等，來詣舍利弗所，語舍利弗：「佛不以無事而受人請，大德舍利弗復不受請，我等白衣云何當得大信清淨？」舍利弗言：「我大師佛言：『舍利弗食不淨食。』今不得受人請。」於是波斯匿等至佛所，白佛言：「佛不常受人請，舍利弗復不受請，我等云何心得大信？願佛勅舍利弗還受人請！」佛言：「此人心堅，不可移轉。」

At that time King Prasenajit, the elder Sudatta, and others came to Śāriputra and said to him, “The Buddha does not accept invitations without reason, and now the Great Virtuous Śāriputra also does not accept invitations. How can we laypeople gain great faith and purity?” Śāriputra said, “My great teacher, the Buddha, said, ‘Śāriputra eats impure food.’ Now I cannot accept invitations from others.” Then Prasenajit and the others went to the Buddha and said to him, “The Buddha does not always accept invitations, and Śāriputra also does not accept invitations. How can our minds gain great faith? May the Buddha command Śāriputra to accept invitations again.” The Buddha said, “This person's mind is firm and cannot be moved.”

At that time the physicians said, ‘Make the snake suck the poison back, and only then will the poison be exhausted.’ The physicians each performed spells, and the snake that had bitten the king came to the king's place. The physicians piled up firewood and lit a fire, commanding the snake, ‘Suck back your poison. If you do not, you must enter this fire.’ The poisonous snake thought, ‘Since I have already spit out the poison, how can I suck it back? This would be worse than death.’ Having fixed its thought, it immediately entered the fire. At that time, the poisonous snake was Śāriputra. Life after life, his mind has been firm and cannot be moved.”

As for all buddhas, world-honored ones, if one person cuts one of their arms with a knife and another person smears one of their arms with sandalwood paste, they regard them like the left and right eyes. Ciñcā, the brahmin woman, used a wooden bowl to slander the Buddha. In the midst of the great assembly she said, “You made me pregnant. Why are you not concerned? Give me clothing and food. How can you be so shameless, deceiving and confusing others?” At that time, five hundred brahmin teachers and others all raised their hands and cried out, “Yes, yes! We know this matter.” At that time the Buddha's complexion did not change, and he showed no shame. The matter was immediately exposed; the earth shook greatly, and the devas made offerings, scattering many famous flowers and praising the Buddha's virtue. The Buddha showed no sign of joy.

In all such matters, including many kinds of food and drink, clothing, bedding, praise and blame, contempt and respect, his mind was no different. It is like true gold: whether burned, forged, beaten, or polished, it neither increases nor decreases.

For this reason, although arhats have cut off the fetters and attained the path, they still have residual traces and cannot be called Bhagavān.

Question: Does Bhagavān have only this one name, or are there other names?

Answer: The Buddha's virtues are immeasurable, and his names are also immeasurable. This name selects one of the great names because many people recognize it. There are also other names, such as Tathāgata.

云何名多陀阿伽陀？如法相解，如法相說；如諸佛安隱道來，佛亦如是來，更不去後有中，是故名多陀阿伽陀。

What is meant by Tathāgata? He understands according to the characteristics of dharmas, and he speaks according to the characteristics of dharmas. Just as all buddhas come by the path of peace and security, this Buddha also comes in this way, and he no longer goes into any later existence. Therefore he is called Tathāgata.

復名阿羅呵。云何名阿羅呵？「阿羅」名「賊」，「呵」名「殺」，是名「殺賊」。如偈說：「佛以忍為鎧，精進為剛甲，持戒為大馬，禪定為良弓，智慧為好箭；外破魔王軍，內滅煩惱賊，是名阿羅呵。」

He is also called Arhat. What is meant by Arhat? Ara means “thief,” and ha means “to kill.” Thus it means “killer of thieves.” As a verse says: “The Buddha uses patience as armor, diligence as strong mail, moral discipline as a great horse, meditation as a fine bow, and wisdom as good arrows. Outwardly he breaks the army of Māra; inwardly he extinguishes the thieves of affliction. This is called Arhat.”

復次，「阿」名「不」，「羅呵」名「生」，是名「不生」。佛心種子，後世田中不生，無明糠脫故。

復次，「阿羅呵」名「應受供養」。佛諸結使除盡，得一切智慧故，應受一切天地眾生供養，以是故，佛名阿羅呵。

Furthermore, a means “not,” and raha means “birth.” Thus it means “not born.” The seeds in the Buddha's mind do not arise in the field of a later life, because the husk of ignorance has fallen away.

Furthermore, Arhat means “worthy of offerings.” Because the Buddha has completely removed all fetters and latent tendencies and has attained all wisdom, he is worthy to receive offerings from all beings in heaven and on earth. For this reason, the Buddha is called Arhat.

復名三藐三佛陀。云何名三藐三佛陀？「三藐」名「正」，「三」名「遍」，「佛」名「知」，是名正遍知一切法。

問曰：云何正遍知？

答曰：知苦如苦相，知集如集相，知滅如滅相，知道如道相。是名三藐三佛陀。

He is also called Samyaksaṃbuddha. What is meant by Samyaksaṃbuddha? Samyak means “right,” sam means “pervasively,” and buddha means “knowing.” Thus it means rightly and pervasively knowing all dharmas.

Question: What is right and pervasive knowing?

Answer: He knows suffering according to the characteristic of suffering; he knows arising according to the characteristic of arising; he knows cessation according to the characteristic of cessation; and he knows the path according to the characteristic of the path. This is called Samyaksaṃbuddha.

Rightly Knowing and the Fullness of Knowledge and Conduct

Chinese Source

復次，知一切諸法實不壞相，不增不減。云何名不壞相？心行處滅，言語道斷，過諸法如涅槃相不動。以是故，名三藐三佛陀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, he knows the true, unbroken character of all dharmas: it neither increases nor decreases. What is meant by the unbroken character? It is where the activity of mind ceases and the path of language is cut off. It goes beyond all dharmas and, like the character of nirvāṇa, is unmoving. For this reason, he is called Samyaksaṃbuddha.

復次，一切十方諸世界名號，六道所攝眾生名號；眾生先世因緣，未來世生處；一切十方眾生心相，諸結使、諸善根、諸出要，如是等一切諸法悉知，是名三藐三佛陀。

Furthermore, he completely knows the names of all worlds in the ten directions, the names of beings included within the six destinies, the past-life causes and conditions of beings, their places of birth in future lives, the mental characteristics of all beings throughout the ten directions, their fetters and latent tendencies, their roots of goodness, and their ways of liberation. He completely knows all dharmas such as these. This is called Samyaksaṃbuddha.

復名鞞侈遮羅那三般那，秦言明行具足。

云何名明行具足？

宿命、天眼、漏盡，名為三明。

He is also called Vidyā-caraṇa-sampanna, which means “complete in knowledge and conduct.”

What is meant by complete in knowledge and conduct?

Knowledge of past lives, the divine eye, and the exhaustion of outflows are called the three knowledges.

問曰：神通、明有何等異？

答曰：直知過去宿命事，是名通；知過去因緣行業，是名明。直知死此生彼，是名通；知行因緣，際會不失，是名明。直盡結使，不知更生不生，是名通；若知漏盡，更不復生，是名明。是三明，大阿羅漢、大辟支佛所得。

Question: What difference is there between spiritual power and knowledge?

Answer: Simply knowing matters of past lives is called spiritual power; knowing the causes, conditions, actions, and karma of the past is called knowledge. Simply knowing that one dies here and is born there is called spiritual power; knowing that the causes and conditions of actions meet without being lost is called knowledge. Simply exhausting the fetters and latent tendencies, without knowing whether there will be further birth or no further birth, is called spiritual power; if one knows that the outflows are exhausted and that there will be no further birth, this is called knowledge. These three knowledges are attained by great arhats and great pratyekabuddhas.

問曰：若爾者，與佛有何等異？

答曰：彼雖得三明，明不滿足，佛悉滿足，是為異。

問曰：云何不滿？云何滿？

Question: If this is so, how are they different from the Buddha?

Answer: Although they have attained the three knowledges, their knowledge is not complete. The Buddha is complete in all of them. This is the difference.

Question: How are they incomplete, and how is the Buddha complete?

答曰：諸阿羅漢、辟支佛宿命智，知自身及他人，亦不能遍；有阿羅漢知一世，或二世、三世，十、百、千、萬劫，乃至八萬劫，過是以往不能復知，是故不滿。天眼明未來世亦如是。佛一念中生、住、滅時，諸結使分，生時如是，住時如是，滅時如是。苦法忍、苦法智中所斷結使悉覺了。知如是結使解脫，得爾所有為法解脫，得爾所無為法解脫，乃至道比忍見諦道十五心中。諸聲聞、辟支佛所不覺知，時少疾故。如是知過去眾生因緣、漏盡，未來、現在亦如是，是故名佛明行具足。

Answer: The knowledge of past lives possessed by arhats and pratyekabuddhas can know their own lives and the lives of others, but it still cannot know them pervasively. Some arhats know one life, or two lives, three lives, ten, a hundred, a thousand, or ten thousand kalpas, and even up to eighty thousand kalpas; beyond that they cannot know further. Therefore it is incomplete. Their divine-eye knowledge of future lives is also like this. In a single thought-moment, when phenomena arise, abide, and cease, the Buddha knows the portions of the fetters and latent tendencies: when they arise, they are like this; when they abide, they are like this; when they cease, they are like this. He fully understands the fetters and latent tendencies abandoned in the patience toward the Dharma of suffering and the knowledge of the Dharma of suffering. He knows liberation from such fetters and latent tendencies, the corresponding liberation through conditioned dharmas, and the corresponding liberation through unconditioned dharmas, up to the fifteen moments of mind in the path of seeing the truths through the path-comparison patience. These are not awakened to by śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas because the time is extremely brief and swift. In this way, he knows the past causes and conditions of beings and the exhaustion of outflows; he knows the future and present in the same way. Therefore the Buddha is called complete in knowledge and conduct.

行名身口業，唯佛身、口業具足，餘皆有失，是名明行具足。

Conduct means bodily and verbal karma. Only the Buddha is complete in bodily and verbal karma; all others have faults. This is called complete in knowledge and conduct.

Sugata, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed, Guide, and Teacher

Chinese Source

復名修伽陀：「修」秦言「好」，「伽陀」或言「去」，或言「說」，是名「好去」、「好說」。

好去者，於種種諸深三摩提、無量諸大智慧中去，如偈說：

「佛一切智為大車，八正道行入涅槃。」

是名好去。

Ru-Yi Translation

He is also called Sugata. Su means “good,” and gata may mean “gone” or “spoken.” Thus it means “well-gone” and “well-spoken.”

“Well-gone” means that he has gone within many kinds of deep samadhi and immeasurable great wisdom. As a verse says:

“The Buddha’s all-wisdom is the great vehicle; by practicing the eightfold right path, he enters nirvana.”

This is called well-gone.

好說者，如諸法實相說，不著法愛說。觀弟子智慧力，是人正使一切方便神通智力化之，亦無如之何。是人可度是疾、是遲，是人應是處度；是人應說布施，或說戒，或說涅槃；是人應說五眾、十二因緣、四諦等諸法能入道。如是等種種知弟子智力而為說法，是名好說。

“Well-spoken” means that he speaks according to the true character of dharmas and does not speak out of attachment or love for dharmas. He observes the strength of his disciples’ wisdom. If a person cannot be transformed even by every kind of skillful means, spiritual power, and wisdom-power, he knows there is nothing more to be done. He knows whether a person who can be liberated should be liberated quickly or slowly, and in what circumstance that person should be liberated. He knows whether to teach generosity, moral discipline, or nirvana; or whether to teach the five aggregates, the twelve links of dependent arising, the four truths, and other dharmas by which one can enter the path. In these and many other ways, he knows the strength of his disciples’ wisdom and teaches Dharma for them. This is called well-spoken.

復名路迦憊：「路迦」秦言「世」，「憊」名「知」，是名知世間。

問曰：云何知世間？

答曰：知二種世間：一、眾生，二、非眾生。及如實相知世間、世間因、知世間滅、出世間道。

He is also called Lokavid. Loka means “world,” and vid means “knowing.” Thus it means knower of the world.

Question: What is meant by knowing the world?

Answer: He knows two kinds of world: first, sentient beings; second, what is not sentient. He also knows the world as it truly is, the cause of the world, the cessation of the world, and the path beyond the world.

復次，知世間，非如世俗知，亦非外道知；知世間無常故苦，苦故無我。

復次，知世間相，非有常非無常，非有邊非無邊，非去非不去，如是相亦不著，清淨、常不壞相如虛空，是名知世間。

Furthermore, his knowing of the world is not like worldly knowing, and it is not like the knowing of non-Buddhist teachers. He knows that the world is impermanent, therefore suffering; and because it is suffering, it is without self.

Furthermore, he knows the character of the world: it is not permanent and not impermanent, not finite and not infinite, not gone and not not-gone. He is not attached even to such characteristics. It is pure, constantly unbroken, and like space. This is called knowing the world.

復名阿耨多羅，秦言「無上」。

云何無上？

涅槃法無上。佛自知是涅槃不從他聞，亦將導眾生令至涅槃。如諸法中涅槃無上，眾生中佛亦無上。

He is also called Anuttara, which means “unsurpassed.”

What is meant by unsurpassed?

The Dharma of nirvana is unsurpassed. The Buddha knows this nirvana by himself, not from hearing it from another, and he also guides beings so that they reach nirvana. Just as nirvana is unsurpassed among all dharmas, the Buddha is unsurpassed among beings.

復次，持戒、禪定、智慧，教化眾生，一切無有與等者，何況能過！故言無上。

復次，「阿」名「無」，「耨多羅」名「答」。一切外道法，可答可破，非實非清淨故；佛法不可答、不可破，出一切語言道，亦實清淨故，以是故名無答。

Furthermore, in moral discipline, meditation, wisdom, and the teaching and transforming of beings, there is no one equal to him. How much less could anyone surpass him? Therefore he is called unsurpassed.

Furthermore, a means “without,” and nuttara means “answer.” All non-Buddhist teachings can be answered and refuted, because they are neither true nor pure. The Buddha Dharma cannot be answered and cannot be refuted, because it goes beyond every path of language and is also true and pure. For this reason, he is called “without answer.”

復名富樓沙曇藐婆羅提：「富樓沙」秦言「丈夫」，「曇藐」言「可化」，「婆羅提」言「調御師」，是名「可化丈夫調御師」。

佛以大慈大悲大智故，有時軟美語，有時苦切語，有時雜語，以此調御令不失道。如偈說：

「佛法為車弟子馬，實法寶主佛調御，若馬出道失正轍，如是當治令調伏。若小不調輕法治，好善成立為上道，若不可治便棄捨，以是調御為無上。」

He is also called Puruṣa-damya-sārathi. Puruṣa means “man” or “person,” damya means “to be tamed,” and sārathi means “charioteer” or “trainer.” Thus it means trainer of persons who can be tamed.

Because the Buddha has great kindness, great compassion, and great wisdom, at times he uses soft and beautiful words, at times sharp and painful words, and at times mixed words. By these he trains beings so they do not lose the path. As a verse says:

“The Buddha Dharma is the chariot, the disciples are the horses; the Buddha, master of the treasure of true Dharma, is the trainer. If a horse leaves the path and loses the right track, it should be treated in this way and brought under control. If it is only slightly untamed, it is treated lightly; one fond of goodness is established in the highest path. If one cannot be treated, then he is set aside. For this reason, this trainer is unsurpassed.”

復次，調御師有五種：初父母兄姊親里，中官法，下師法——今世三種法治，後世閻羅王治，佛以今世樂、後世樂及涅槃樂利益，故名師。上四種法治人不久畢壞，不能常實成就。佛成人以三種道，常隨道不失，如火自相不捨乃至滅；佛令人得善法亦如是，至死不捨。以是故，佛名可化丈夫調御師。

Furthermore, there are five kinds of trainers. First are parents, elder brothers and sisters, relatives, and clan members; next are government officials and laws; next are teachers’ methods. These three kinds of methods govern this present life. In a future life, King Yama governs. The Buddha brings benefit through the happiness of this life, the happiness of future lives, and the happiness of nirvana; therefore he is called teacher. The first four kinds of methods soon come to an end and decay, and cannot bring about a lasting and true accomplishment. The Buddha accomplishes persons by means of three paths, so they continually follow the path and do not lose it, just as fire does not abandon its own character until it is extinguished. When the Buddha enables people to attain good dharmas, it is also like this: they do not abandon them even until death. For this reason, the Buddha is called trainer of persons who can be tamed.

問曰：女人，佛亦化令得道，何以獨言丈夫？

答曰：男尊女卑故，女從男故，男為事業主故。

復次，女人有五礙：不得作轉輪王、釋天王、魔天王、梵天王、佛，以是故不說。

復次，若言佛為女人調御師，為不尊重。若說丈夫，一切都攝。譬如王來，不應獨來，必有侍從。如是說丈夫，二根、無根及女盡攝，以是故說丈夫。用是因緣故，佛名可化丈夫調御師。

Question: The Buddha also teaches women and enables them to attain the path. Why speak only of men?

Answer: It is because men were regarded as honored and women as lowly, because women followed men, and because men were treated as the heads of affairs.

Furthermore, women have the five obstructions: they cannot become a wheel-turning king, Śakra, king of the Māra heavens, a Brahmā king, or a Buddha. For this reason, they are not mentioned.

Furthermore, if one said that the Buddha is a trainer of women, it would not sound respectful. If one speaks of men, all are included. It is like a king coming: he should not come alone, but must have attendants. In the same way, when “men” are spoken of, those with both faculties, those without faculties, and women are all included. Therefore “men” is said. For these reasons, the Buddha is called trainer of persons who can be tamed.

復名舍多提婆魔[少/兔]舍喃：「舍多」秦言「教師」，「提婆」言「天」，「魔[少/兔]舍喃」言「人」，是名「天人教師」。

云何名天人教師？

佛示導是應作、是不應作，是善、是不善，是人隨教行、不捨道法，得煩惱解脫報，是名天人師。

He is also called Śāstā deva-manuṣyāṇām. Śāstā means “teacher,” deva means “devas,” and manuṣyāṇām means “humans.” Thus it means teacher of devas and humans.

What is meant by teacher of devas and humans?

The Buddha shows and guides them: this should be done, this should not be done; this is wholesome, this is unwholesome. When a person follows the teaching, practices it, and does not abandon the Dharma of the path, that person receives the fruit of liberation from afflictions. This is called teacher of devas and humans.

問曰：佛能度龍、鬼、神等墮餘道中生者，何以獨言天人師？

答曰：度餘道中生者少，度天、人中生者多。如白色人，雖有黑黶子，不名黑人，黑少故。

復次，人中結使薄，厭心易得；天中智慧利。以是故，二處易得道，餘道中不爾。

Question: The Buddha can liberate dragons, ghosts, spirits, and others born in the other destinies. Why speak only of teacher of devas and humans?

Answer: He liberates fewer of those born in the other destinies, and liberates more of devas and humans. It is like a white person: although there may be a black mole, that person is not called black, because the black part is small.

Furthermore, among humans the fetters and latent tendencies are thin, and the mind of disenchantment is easy to obtain. Among devas, wisdom is sharp. For this reason, these two places are easy places to attain the path. It is not so in the other destinies.

復次，言天則攝一切天，言人則攝一切地上生者。何以故？天上則天大，地上則人大，是故說天則天上盡攝，說人則地上盡攝。

復次，人中得受戒律儀、見諦道、思惟道及諸道果。或有人言：餘道中不得。或有人言：多少得。天、人中易得多得，以是故，佛為天人師。

復次，人中行樂因多，天中樂報多；善法是樂因，樂是善法報。餘道中善因報少，以是故，佛為天人師。

Furthermore, saying “devas” includes all devas, and saying “humans” includes all those born on the earth. Why? In the heavens, devas are predominant; on the earth, humans are predominant. Therefore, when devas are spoken of, all in the heavens are included; when humans are spoken of, all on the earth are included.

Furthermore, among humans one can receive the discipline of moral restraint, the path of seeing the truths, the path of contemplation, and the fruits of the various paths. Some say that these cannot be attained in the other destinies. Some say they can be attained there to some extent. Among devas and humans, they are easier to attain and more often attained. For this reason, the Buddha is teacher of devas and humans.

Furthermore, among humans there are many causes for practicing happiness, and among devas there are many results of happiness. Wholesome dharmas are the cause of happiness, and happiness is the result of wholesome dharmas. In the other destinies, wholesome causes and results are few. For this reason, the Buddha is teacher of devas and humans.
