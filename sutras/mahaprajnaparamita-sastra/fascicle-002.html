How the Teaching Was Heard

Fascicle 2 continues the explanation of “Thus have I heard.” It asks how the words of the sutra can be called heard when the Buddha is self-awakened and does not learn from another teacher.

Why “Thus Have I Heard” Is Not a Fault

Chinese Source

大智度初品總說如是我聞釋論第三卷第二

「如是我聞：一時」，今當總說。問曰：若諸佛一切智人，自然無師，不隨他教，不受他法，不用他道，不從他聞而說法，何以言「如是我聞」？

Ru-Yi Translation

Chapter One of the Mahāprajñāpāramitā: Treatise Section Three, a General Explanation of “Thus Have I Heard,” Fascicle Two

“Thus have I heard: at one time” will now be explained generally. Question: The Buddhas are omniscient. They are naturally without a teacher; they do not follow another's instruction, do not receive another's Dharma, do not use another's path, and do not hear from another and then teach Dharma. Why, then, does the sutra say, “Thus have I heard”?

答曰：如汝所言，佛一切智人，自然無師，不應從他聞法而說。

佛法非但佛口說者是，一切世間真實善語、微妙好語，皆出佛法中。

如佛毘尼中說：「何者是佛法？佛法有五種人說：一者、佛自口說，二者、佛弟子說，三者、仙人說，四者、諸天說，五者、化人說。」

復次，如《釋提桓因得道經》，佛告憍尸迦：「世間真實善語、微妙好語，皆出我法中。」

Answer: It is as you say. The Buddha is omniscient and naturally without a teacher, so he should not hear Dharma from another and then teach it.

But the Buddha Dharma is not only what is spoken from the Buddha's own mouth. All true, wholesome speech and all subtle, beautiful speech in the world come forth from the Buddha Dharma.

As it says in the Buddha's Vinaya: “What is the Buddha Dharma? The Buddha Dharma may be spoken by five kinds of persons: first, spoken from the Buddha's own mouth; second, spoken by the Buddha's disciples; third, spoken by sages; fourth, spoken by devas; and fifth, spoken by transformation beings.”

Furthermore, in the Sutra on Śakra's Attainment of the Path, the Buddha told Kauśika, “All true, wholesome speech and all subtle, beautiful speech in the world come forth from my Dharma.”

如〈讚佛偈〉中說：「諸世善語，皆出佛法；善說無失，無過佛語。餘處雖有，善無過語，一切皆是，佛法之餘。諸外道中，設有好語，如虫食木，偶得成字。初中下法，自共相破，如鐵出金，誰當信者？如伊蘭中，牛頭栴檀；如苦種中，甘善美果。設能信者，是人則信，外經書中，自出好語。諸好實語，皆從佛出；如栴檀香，出摩梨山。除摩梨山，無出栴檀；如是除佛，無出實語。」

As the “Verses in Praise of the Buddha” say: “All wholesome speech in every world comes forth from the Buddha Dharma. Well-spoken words without fault do not surpass the Buddha's words. Even if there are elsewhere good and faultless words, all of them are remnants of the Buddha Dharma. If there are good words among non-Buddhists, they are like a worm eating wood and accidentally forming written characters. Their doctrines of beginning, middle, and end refute themselves and one another; if gold comes from iron, who would believe it? It is like ox-head sandalwood growing among foul eranda trees; it is like sweet and beautiful fruit growing from bitter seeds. If someone is able to believe this, then that person believes that good words arise on their own in non-Buddhist scriptures. All good and true words come from the Buddha, just as sandalwood fragrance comes from Mount Malaya. Apart from Mount Malaya, sandalwood does not arise; in the same way, apart from the Buddha, true speech does not arise.”

復次，「如是我聞」，是阿難等佛大弟子輩說，入佛法相故，名為佛法。

如佛般涅槃時，於俱夷那竭國薩羅雙樹間，北首臥，將入涅槃。爾時，阿難親屬愛未除，未離欲故，心沒憂海，不能自出。爾時，長老阿泥盧豆語阿難：「汝守佛法藏人，不應如凡人自沒憂海！一切有為法，是無常相，汝莫愁憂！又佛手付汝法，汝今愁悶，失所受事。汝當問佛：『佛般涅槃後，我曹云何行道？誰當作師？惡口車匿，云何共住？佛經初作何等語？』如是種種未來事，應問佛。」

阿難聞是事，悶心小醒，得念道力助，於佛末後臥床邊，以此事問佛。

Furthermore, “Thus have I heard” was spoken by Ānanda and other great disciples of the Buddha. Because it enters into the character of the Buddha Dharma, it is called Buddha Dharma.

When the Buddha was about to enter parinirvāṇa, he was lying with his head to the north between the twin śāla trees in the land of Kuśinagara. At that time, because Ānanda had not yet removed affection for relatives and had not yet become free from desire, his mind sank into an ocean of grief and he could not lift himself out. Then the elder Aniruddha said to Ānanda, “You are the one who guards the treasury of the Buddha Dharma. You should not sink into an ocean of grief like an ordinary person. All conditioned dharmas have the mark of impermanence; do not grieve. Moreover, the Buddha has placed the Dharma in your hands. Now, because you are distressed and confused, you are losing what has been entrusted to you. You should ask the Buddha: ‘After the Buddha enters parinirvāṇa, how should we practice the path? Who should be our teacher? How should we live together with the harsh-speaking Channa? What words should stand at the beginning of the Buddha's sutras?’ These and other matters concerning the future should be asked of the Buddha.”

When Ānanda heard this, his distressed mind cleared a little. Supported by the strength of mindfulness of the path, he went to the side of the Buddha's final bed and asked him about these matters.

佛告阿難：「若今現前，若我過去後，自依止，法依止，不餘依止。云何比丘自依止、法依止、不餘依止？於是比丘內觀身，常當一心智慧，勤修精進，除世間貪憂；外身、內外身觀，亦如是；受、心、法念處，亦復如是。是名比丘自依止、法依止、不餘依止。從今日，解脫戒經即是大師；如解脫戒經說身業、口業，應如是行。車匿比丘，我涅槃後，如梵法治；若心濡伏者，應教《刪陀迦旃延經》，即可得道。復次，我三阿僧祇劫所集法寶藏，是藏初應作是說：『如是我聞：一時，佛在某方、某國土、某處樹林中。』何以故？過去諸佛經初皆稱是語，未來諸佛經初亦稱是語，現在諸佛末後般涅槃時亦教稱是語。今我般涅槃後，經初亦應稱『如是我聞：一時』。」

The Buddha told Ānanda, “Whether I am present now or have passed away, rely on yourselves, rely on the Dharma, and do not rely on anything else. How does a monk rely on himself, rely on the Dharma, and not rely on anything else? Here, a monk observes the body internally, always with one-pointed wisdom, diligently cultivating vigor and removing greed and sorrow toward the world. Observing the body externally and observing the body internally and externally are also like this. The establishments of mindfulness with regard to feeling, mind, and dharmas are also like this. This is called a monk relying on himself, relying on the Dharma, and not relying on anything else. From today onward, the Prātimokṣa is the great teacher. Whatever bodily and verbal actions are taught in the Prātimokṣa should be practiced accordingly. As for the monk Channa, after I enter nirvāṇa, discipline him with the brahmā punishment. If his mind becomes gentle and subdued, he should be taught the Śāṇḍaka Kātyāyana Sutra, and then he can attain the path. Furthermore, the Dharma treasury I have gathered over three asaṃkhyeya kalpas should begin in this way: ‘Thus have I heard: at one time, the Buddha was in such-and-such a region, such-and-such a country, in such-and-such a forest.’ Why? Sutras of the Buddhas of the past all began with these words. Sutras of the Buddhas of the future will also begin with these words. Buddhas of the present, at the time of their final parinirvāṇa, also teach that these words should be used. After I enter parinirvāṇa, the sutras should also begin, ‘Thus have I heard: at one time.’”

是故當知是佛所教，非佛自言「如是我聞」。佛一切智人，自然無師故，不應言「我聞」。若佛自說「如是我聞」，有所不知者，可有此難。阿難問佛，佛教是語，是弟子所言「如是我聞」，無有咎。

Therefore one should know that these words were taught by the Buddha; they are not the Buddha himself saying, “Thus have I heard.” Since the Buddha is omniscient and naturally without a teacher, he should not say, “I heard.” If the Buddha himself had said, “Thus have I heard,” then one could raise this objection that there was something he did not know. But Ānanda asked the Buddha, and the Buddha taught these words. They are the disciple's words, “Thus have I heard,” and there is no fault.

Key Terms in This Opening

如是我聞 · Thus have I heardThe standard opening formula of Buddhist sutras, here explained as words spoken by Ānanda and the disciples under the Buddha's instruction.
自依止、法依止 · rely on oneself, rely on the DharmaThe Buddha's final instruction that the disciples should ground their practice in mindfulness and the Dharma after his passing.
解脫戒經 · PrātimokṣaThe code of monastic discipline that becomes the teacher for conduct after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa.
法藏 · Dharma treasuryThe treasury of teachings gathered and preserved so the Dharma can remain in the world after the Buddha's passing.
