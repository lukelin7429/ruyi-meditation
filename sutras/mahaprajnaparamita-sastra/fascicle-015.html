Part Twenty-Five: Patience Concerning Phenomena

Chinese Source

云何名法忍？

English Translation

What is patience concerning phenomena?

忍諸恭敬、供養眾生及諸瞋惱、婬欲之人，是名生忍。忍其供養、恭敬法及瞋惱、婬欲法，是為法忍。

Patience toward beings who offer respect and support, or who are angry, harmful, or lustful, is called patience toward living beings. Patience concerning the phenomena of offerings and respect, anger and harm, and sexual desire is patience concerning phenomena.

復次，法忍者，於內六情不著，於外六塵不受，能於此二不作分別。何以故？內相如外，外相如內，二相俱不可得故；一相故，因緣合故，其實空故，一切法相常清淨故，如、真際、法性相故，不二入故，雖無二亦不一。如是觀諸法，心信不轉，是名法忍。

Furthermore, patience concerning phenomena means not clinging to the six internal sense faculties, not taking up the six external sense objects, and making no distinction between these two. Why? The internal is like the external, and the external like the internal; neither characteristic can be apprehended. They share one character, combine through conditions, and are empty in reality. The characteristics of all phenomena are always pure, having the character of suchness, the limit of reality, and Dharma nature. One enters nonduality; yet although there is no duality, neither is there oneness. Contemplating phenomena in this way, with unshakable confidence in the mind, is called patience concerning phenomena.

如《毘摩羅鞊經》中，法住菩薩說：「生、滅為二，不生、不滅是不二入法門。」乃至文殊尸利說：「無聞、無見，一切心滅，無說、無語，是不二入法門。」毘摩羅鞊默然無言，諸菩薩讚言：「善哉！善哉！是真不二入法門。」

As in the Vimalakīrti Sūtra, the bodhisattva Dharmasthiti said, “Arising and ceasing are two. Neither arising nor ceasing is the Dharma gateway of entering nonduality.” The discussion continued until Mañjuśrī said, “No hearing or seeing, all mental activity extinguished, no speaking or verbal expression: this is the Dharma gateway of entering nonduality.” Vimalakīrti remained silent. The bodhisattvas praised him: “Excellent! Excellent! This is truly the Dharma gateway of entering nonduality.”

Mental and Nonmental Phenomena

Chinese Source

復次，一切法有二種：一者、眾生，二者、諸法。菩薩於眾生中忍，如先說；今說法中忍。

English Translation

Furthermore, everything falls into two categories: living beings and phenomena. A bodhisattva's patience toward living beings has been explained above; now patience concerning phenomena is explained.

法有二種：心法，非心法。非心法中有內、有外：外有寒熱、風雨等，內有飢渴、老病死等。如是等種種，名為非心法。心法中有二種：一者、瞋恚、憂愁、疑等，二者、婬欲、憍慢等。是二名為心法。菩薩於此二法，能忍不動，是名法忍。

Phenomena have two kinds: mental and nonmental. Nonmental phenomena are internal or external. External examples include cold, heat, wind, and rain; internal examples include hunger, thirst, aging, illness, and death. These and similar things are called nonmental phenomena. Mental phenomena also have two kinds: first, anger, sorrow, doubt, and so forth; second, sexual desire, conceit, and so forth. These two are called mental phenomena. A bodhisattva's ability to endure these two kinds of phenomena without wavering is called patience concerning phenomena.

問曰：於眾生中若瞋惱害命得罪，憐愍得福；寒熱、風雨，無有增損，云何而忍？

Question: Among living beings, angrily causing harm or taking life incurs wrongdoing, while compassion brings merit. Cold, heat, wind, and rain are neither benefited nor harmed. Why exercise patience toward them?

答曰：雖無增損而自生惱亂憂苦，害菩薩道，以是故，應當忍。

Answer: Although they are neither benefited nor harmed, one may generate agitation, sorrow, and suffering within oneself, thereby damaging the bodhisattva path. Therefore, one should be patient.

復次，非但殺惱眾生故得罪，為惡心作因緣故有罪。所以者何？雖殺眾生而無記心，是便無罪；慈念眾生，雖無所與而大得福。以是故，寒熱、風雨，雖無增損，然以能生惡意故得罪。以是故，應當忍。

Furthermore, wrongdoing is not incurred only by killing or troubling living beings; it also arises through conditions for an evil mind. Why? Even if one kills a living being, when the mind is karmically indeterminate there is no offense. Conversely, compassionately regarding living beings brings great merit even when one gives them nothing. Therefore, although cold, heat, wind, and rain are neither benefited nor harmed, one incurs wrongdoing because they can occasion evil intentions. For this reason, one should be patient.

Patience with Bodily Hardships

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩自知宿罪因緣：「生此苦處，此我自作，我應自受。」如是思惟，是故能忍。

English Translation

Furthermore, a bodhisattva knows the conditions of past wrongdoing and reflects: “I have been born in this place of suffering through my own actions; I should bear their results.” Reflecting in this way, one can be patient.

復次，菩薩思惟：「國土有二種：有淨，有不淨。菩薩若生不淨國中，受此辛苦、飢寒眾惱，自發淨願：『我成佛時，國中無此眾苦。』此雖不淨，乃是我利。」

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “Lands are of two kinds, pure and impure. If a bodhisattva is born in an impure land and experiences hardship, hunger, cold, and many troubles, they personally arouse a pure vow: ‘When I become a Buddha, may my land be free of these sufferings.’ Although this land is impure, it benefits me.”

復次，菩薩思惟：「世間八法，賢聖所不能免，何況於我！」以是故，應當忍。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “Even noble ones cannot escape the eight worldly conditions. How much less can I!” For this reason, one should be patient.

復次，菩薩思惟：「知此人身無牢無強，為老、病、死所逐；雖復天身清淨，無老、無病，耽著天樂，譬如醉人，不得修行道福、出家離欲。」以是故，於此人身自忍修福，利益眾生。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “I know that this human body is neither firm nor strong and is pursued by aging, illness, and death. Although heavenly bodies are pure, without aging or illness, devas are engrossed in heavenly pleasures like drunken people and cannot cultivate the path and merit, go forth, or leave desire behind.” Therefore, in this human body one patiently cultivates merit and benefits living beings.

復次，菩薩思惟：「我受此四大、五眾身，應有種種苦分，無有受身而不苦者；富貴、貧賤，出家、在家，愚智、明闇，無得免者。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “Having received this body of the four great elements and five aggregates, I must have a share in various sufferings. No one receives a body without suffering. Wealthy or poor, noble or lowly, renunciant or householder, foolish or wise, clear-minded or deluded—none can escape.

何以故？富貴之人，常有畏怖，守護財物，譬如肥羊，早就屠机，如烏銜肉，眾烏逐之；貧賤之人，有飢、寒之苦。

“Why? The wealthy and honored are constantly afraid as they guard their possessions. They are like fattened sheep that soon reach the slaughterer's block, or a crow carrying meat and pursued by other crows. The poor and lowly suffer hunger and cold.

出家之人，今世雖苦，後世受福得道；在家之人，今世雖樂，後世受苦。愚人先求今世樂，無常對至，後則受苦；智人思惟無常苦，後則受樂得道。如是等受身之人，無不有苦。」是故菩薩應當行忍。

“Those who have gone forth may suffer in this life, but in a future life they receive blessings and attain the path. Householders may enjoy this life but suffer in a future life. Fools first seek pleasure in this life; when impermanence strikes, they suffer afterward. The wise reflect on impermanence and suffering, and afterward attain happiness and the path. All who receive bodies in these ways experience suffering.” Therefore, a bodhisattva should practice patience.

復次，菩薩思惟：「一切世間皆苦，我當云何於中而欲求樂？」

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “The entire world is suffering. How could I wish to seek pleasure within it?”

復次，菩薩思惟：「我於無量劫中常受眾苦，無所利益，未曾為法；今日為眾生求佛道，雖受此苦，當得大利。是故外、內諸苦，悉當忍受。」

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “For immeasurable kalpas I have constantly undergone suffering without benefit, never for the Dharma. Today I seek Buddhahood for living beings. Although I undergo this suffering, I shall gain great benefit. Therefore, I should patiently endure all external and internal sufferings.”

復次，菩薩大心誓願：「若阿鼻泥犁苦，我當忍之，何況小苦而不能忍？若小不忍，何能忍大？」如是種種外法中忍，名曰法忍。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva makes this great-hearted vow: “I would endure even the suffering of Avīci Hell. How much more should I be able to endure a little suffering! If I cannot endure what is small, how could I endure what is great?” These various forms of patience concerning external phenomena are called patience concerning phenomena.

Patience Concerning the Mind

Chinese Source

問曰：云何內心法中能忍？

English Translation

Question: How can one practice patience concerning the internal phenomena of the mind?

答曰：菩薩思惟：「我雖未得道，諸結未斷，若當不忍，與凡人不異，非為菩薩。」復自思惟：「若我得道，斷諸結使，則無法可忍。」

Answer: A bodhisattva reflects: “Although I have not yet attained the path or severed the fetters, if I fail to be patient I am no different from an ordinary person and am not a bodhisattva.” One further reflects: “If I had attained the path and severed the fetters, there would be no phenomenon left to endure.”

復次，「飢渴、寒熱，是外魔軍；結使、煩惱，是內魔賊。我當破此二軍以成佛道；若不爾者，佛道不成。」

Furthermore: “Hunger, thirst, cold, and heat are Māra's external army; fetters and afflictions are the inner bandits of Māra. I must defeat both armies to accomplish Buddhahood. Otherwise, Buddhahood will not be accomplished.”

如說佛苦行六年，魔王來言：「剎利貴人，汝千分生中正有一分活耳！速起還國，布施修福，可得今世後世、人中天上之樂；道不可得，汝唐勤苦。汝若不受軟言，守迷不起，我當將大軍眾來擊破汝！」

As the account tells, after the Buddha had practiced austerities for six years, Māra came and said: “Noble kṣatriya, of a thousand parts of your life, only one remains! Quickly rise and return to your kingdom. Give and cultivate merit, and you can gain happiness in this life and the next, among humans and devas. The path cannot be attained; your labor is in vain. If you reject my gentle words and persist in delusion without rising, I shall bring a great army to attack and destroy you!”

菩薩言：「我今當破汝大力內軍，何況外軍？」魔言：「何等是我內軍？」答曰：「欲是汝初軍，憂愁為第二，飢渴第三軍，渴愛為第四，睡眠第五軍，怖畏為第六，疑悔第七軍，瞋恚為第八，利養虛稱九，自高蔑人十。如是等軍眾，厭沒出家人；我以禪智力，破汝此諸軍，得成佛道已，度脫一切人。」

The bodhisattva said, “I shall defeat your powerful inner army. How much more your external army!” Māra asked, “What is my inner army?” He answered: “Desire is your first army; sorrow is the second. Hunger and thirst are the third; craving is the fourth. Sleep is the fifth army; fear is the sixth. Doubt and regret are the seventh; anger is the eighth. Gain, support, and empty praise are the ninth; exalting oneself and despising others are the tenth. Armies such as these overwhelm those who have gone forth. With the power of meditation and wisdom I shall defeat your armies. Having accomplished Buddhahood, I shall liberate all people.”

菩薩於此諸軍雖未能破，著忍辱鎧，捉智慧劍，執禪定楯，遮諸煩惱箭，是名內忍。

Although a bodhisattva has not yet defeated these armies, one puts on the armor of patience, grasps the sword of wisdom, and holds the shield of meditative concentration to ward off the arrows of affliction. This is called internal patience.

復次，菩薩於諸煩惱中，應當修忍，不應斷結。何以故？若斷結者，所失甚多，墮阿羅漢道中，與根敗無異。是故遮而不斷，以修忍辱，不隨結使。

Furthermore, amid afflictions, a bodhisattva should cultivate patience rather than sever the fetters. Why? Severing them would entail a great loss: falling into the arhat's path would be like having one's roots ruined. Thus one restrains the fetters without severing them, cultivating patience and not following them.

問曰：云何結使未斷而能不隨？

Question: How can one avoid following the fetters without having severed them?

答曰：正思惟故，雖有煩惱而能不隨。復次，思惟觀空、無常相故，雖有妙好五欲，不生諸結。

Answer: Through right reflection, one can have afflictions without following them. Furthermore, through contemplating emptiness and impermanence, even the finest objects of the five sensual pleasures do not give rise to fetters.

譬如國王有一大臣，自覆藏罪，人所不知。王言：「取無脂肥羊來，汝若不得者，當與汝罪。」大臣有智，繫一大羊，以草穀好養；日三以狼而畏怖之，羊雖得養，肥而無脂。牽羊與王，王遣人殺之，肥而無脂。

For example, a king had a minister who concealed an offense that no one knew about. The king said, “Bring me a fat sheep without fat. If you cannot, I shall punish you.” The minister was wise. He tethered a large sheep and fed it well with grass and grain, but frightened it with a wolf three times a day. Although well nourished, it grew large without fat. He brought it to the king, who had it slaughtered: it was indeed well fleshed but without fat.

王問：「云何得爾？」答以上事。菩薩亦如是，見無常、苦、空狼，令諸結使脂消，諸功德肉肥。

The king asked, “How did you achieve this?” The minister explained what he had done. A bodhisattva is similar: seeing the wolves of impermanence, suffering, and emptiness, one causes the fat of the fetters to waste away while the flesh of meritorious qualities grows.

復次，菩薩功德福報無量故，其心柔軟，諸結使薄，易修忍辱。譬如師子王，在林中吼，有人見之，叩頭求哀，則放令去；虎豹小物，不能爾也。何以故？師子王貴獸，有智分別故；虎豹賤蟲，不知分別故。又如壞軍，得值大將則活，值遇小兵則死。

Furthermore, because a bodhisattva's merit and its rewards are immeasurable, the mind is gentle and the fetters weak, making patience easy to cultivate. It is like a lion king roaring in a forest: if someone sees it, bows their head, and begs for mercy, it lets them go. Lesser creatures such as tigers and leopards cannot do this. Why? The lion king is a noble animal with wisdom and discernment; tigers and leopards are lowly creatures without discernment. Likewise, a defeated soldier lives if encountered by a great general but dies if encountered by an ordinary soldier.

復次，菩薩智慧力，觀瞋恚有種種諸惡，觀忍辱有種種功德，是故能忍結使。

Furthermore, through the power of wisdom, a bodhisattva sees anger's many evils and patience's many merits. Thus one can patiently endure the fetters.

復次，菩薩心有智力，能斷結使，為眾生故久住世間；知結使是賊，是故忍而不隨。菩薩繫此結賊，不令縱逸而行功德；譬如有賊，以因緣故不殺，堅閉一處而自修事業。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva possesses the wisdom to sever the fetters but remains long in the world for living beings. Knowing the fetters to be bandits, one patiently endures them without following them. One binds these bandits, preventing them from acting freely, while practicing meritorious deeds. It is like keeping a bandit firmly confined in one place, rather than killing him for certain reasons, while attending to one's own work.

復次，菩薩實知諸法相故，不以諸結使為惡，不以功德為妙；是故於結不瞋，功德不愛。以此智力故，能修忍辱。

Furthermore, because a bodhisattva truly knows the characteristics of phenomena, one does not regard the fetters as evil or meritorious qualities as marvelous. Thus one neither hates the fetters nor clings to merit. Through the power of this wisdom, one can cultivate patience.

如偈說：「菩薩斷除諸不善，乃至極微滅無餘；大功德福無有量，所造事業無不辦。菩薩大智慧力故，於諸結使不能惱；是故能知諸法相，生死涅槃一無二。」如是種種因緣，雖未得道，於諸煩惱法中能忍，是名法忍。

As a verse says: “A bodhisattva removes unwholesome qualities, extinguishing even the slightest without remainder. With immeasurable merit and blessings, no undertaking remains unaccomplished. Through the bodhisattva's great power of wisdom, the fetters cannot cause disturbance. Thus one knows the characteristics of phenomena: birth and death and nirvāṇa are one, without duality.” Through these various conditions, although not yet having attained the path, one can patiently endure the phenomena of affliction. This is called patience concerning phenomena.

Contemplating Phenomena as One, Two, and Three

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩於一切法，知一相無二。一切法可識相，故言一。眼識識色，乃至意識識法，是可識相法，故言一。

English Translation

Furthermore, a bodhisattva knows that all phenomena share one character without duality. All phenomena have the character of being cognizable, and so are called one. Eye-consciousness cognizes forms, and so on up to mind-consciousness cognizing mental objects. They are phenomena that can be cognized, and so are called one.

復次，一切法可知相，故言一。苦法智、苦比智，知苦諦；集法智、集比智，知集諦；滅法智、滅比智，知滅諦；道法智、道比智，知道諦。及善世智，亦知苦、集、滅、道、虛空、非智緣滅，是可知相法，故言一。

Furthermore, all phenomena have the character of being knowable, and so are called one. Dharma knowledge of suffering and inferential knowledge of suffering know the truth of suffering; Dharma knowledge and inferential knowledge of origination know the truth of origination; those of cessation know the truth of cessation; and those of the path know the truth of the path. Wholesome worldly knowledge also knows suffering, origination, cessation, the path, space, and cessation not dependent on knowledge. These phenomena are knowable, and so are called one.

復次，一切法可緣相，故言一。眼識及眼識相應法緣色；耳識、鼻識、舌識、身識亦如是；意識及意識相應法，亦緣眼、亦緣色、亦緣眼識，乃至緣意、緣法、緣意識。一切法可緣相，故言一。

Furthermore, all phenomena can serve as objects of cognition, and so are called one. Eye-consciousness and its associated factors take forms as their objects. Ear-, nose-, tongue-, and body-consciousness are similar. Mind-consciousness and its associated factors can take the eye, forms, and eye-consciousness as objects, and so on up to the mind faculty, mental objects, and mind-consciousness. All phenomena can serve as objects of cognition, and so are called one.

復次，有人言：一切法各皆一，一復有一名為二，三一名為三，如是乃至千萬，皆是一而假名為千萬。復次，一切法中有相，故言一；一相故，名為一。一切物名為法，法相故名為一。如是等無量一門，破異相，不著一，是名法忍。

Furthermore, some say that each phenomenon is one. One plus another one is called two; three ones are called three, and so on up to thousands and myriads. All are ones, provisionally designated as thousands and myriads. Furthermore, all phenomena possess characteristics, and so are called one; sharing a character, they are called one. All things are called phenomena; having the character of phenomena, they are called one. Through these and immeasurable other gateways of oneness, one refutes distinctness without clinging to oneness. This is called patience concerning phenomena.

復次，菩薩觀一切為二。何等二？二名內、外相。內、外相故，內非外相，外非內相。復次，一切法有、無相故為二。空、不空，常、非常，我、非我，色、非色，可見、不可見，有對、非有對，有漏、無漏，有為、無為，心法、非心法，心數法、非心數法，心相應法、非心相應法。如是無量二門，破一不著二，是名為法忍。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva contemplates everything as two. What are the two? They are the internal and external characteristics. Since these are internal and external, the internal is not external and the external is not internal. Furthermore, phenomena are two through the characteristics of existence and nonexistence: empty and nonempty, permanent and impermanent, self and non-self, material and nonmaterial, visible and invisible, resistant and nonresistant, subject to outflows and free of outflows, conditioned and unconditioned, mental and nonmental, mental factors and what are not mental factors, associated with mind and not associated with mind. Through these immeasurable gateways of two, one refutes oneness without clinging to duality. This is called patience concerning phenomena.

復次，菩薩或觀一切法為三。何等為三？下、中、上，善、不善、無記，有、無、非有非無，見諦斷、思惟斷、無斷，學、無學、非學非無學，報、有報、非報非有報。如是無量三門，破一不著異，是名為法忍。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva may contemplate all phenomena as three. What are the three? Lower, intermediate, and higher; wholesome, unwholesome, and karmically indeterminate; existent, nonexistent, and neither existent nor nonexistent; abandoned through seeing the truths, abandoned through cultivation, and not abandoned; belonging to trainees, those beyond training, and neither; karmic retribution, what has karmic retribution, and what is neither retribution nor possessed of retribution. Through these immeasurable gateways of three, one refutes oneness without clinging to difference. This is called patience concerning phenomena.

復次，菩薩雖未得無漏道，結使未斷，能信無漏聖法及三種法印：一者、一切有為生法無常等印，二者、一切法無我印，三者、涅槃實法印。得道賢聖人，自得自知；菩薩雖未得道，能信能受，是名法忍。

Furthermore, although a bodhisattva has not attained the path free of outflows or severed the fetters, one can trust the noble Dharma free of outflows and the three Dharma seals: first, the seal that all conditioned, arisen phenomena are impermanent, and so forth; second, the seal that all phenomena are without self; and third, the seal of nirvāṇa as reality. Noble ones who have attained the path attain and know this for themselves. Although a bodhisattva has not yet attained the path, one can trust and accept it. This is called patience concerning phenomena.

The Fourteen Unanswered Questions and the Poisoned Arrow

Chinese Source

復次，於十四難不答法中有常、無常等，觀察無礙，不失中道，是法能忍，是為法忍。

English Translation

Furthermore, concerning the fourteen unanswered questions, such as whether things are permanent or impermanent, one examines without obstruction and does not lose the middle way. Being able to patiently accept this is patience concerning phenomena.

The True Character Beyond Permanence and Impermanence

Chinese Source

問曰：云何觀諸法實相？

English Translation

Question: How does one contemplate the true character of phenomena?

答曰：觀知諸法無有瑕隟，不可破、不可壞，是為實相。

Answer: One observes and knows that phenomena have no flaw or opening, and cannot be refuted or destroyed. This is their true character.

問曰：一切語，皆可答、可破、可壞，云何言「不可破壞，是為實相」？

Question: Every statement can be answered, refuted, or destroyed. How can you say that what cannot be refuted or destroyed is the true character?

答曰：以諸法不可破故，佛法中一切言語道過，心行處滅，常不生不滅，如涅槃相。何以故？若諸法相實有，不應無；若諸法先有今無，則是斷滅。

Answer: Because phenomena cannot be refuted, in the Buddha's Dharma all pathways of language are transcended and the activity of mind ceases. There is never arising or ceasing, as with the character of nirvāṇa. Why? If the characteristics of phenomena truly existed, they should not become nonexistent. If phenomena existed before but are now absent, that would be annihilation.

復次，諸法不應是常。何以故？若常則無罪、無福，無所傷殺，亦無施命，亦無修行利益，亦無縛、無解，世間則是涅槃。如是等因緣故，諸法不應常。若諸法無常，則是斷滅，亦無罪、無福，亦無增損功德業，因緣果報亦失。如是等因緣故，諸法不應無常。

Furthermore, phenomena should not be permanent. Why? If they were permanent, there would be neither wrongdoing nor merit, neither harming nor killing, nor saving life, nor the benefit of cultivation, nor bondage or liberation. The world would itself be nirvāṇa. For these and other reasons, phenomena should not be permanent. If phenomena were impermanent, there would be annihilation, likewise neither wrongdoing nor merit, and no increase or decrease in meritorious karma; causes, conditions, and their results would also be lost. For these and other reasons, phenomena should not be impermanent.

問曰：汝言「佛法中常亦不實，無常亦不實」，是事不然！何以故？佛法中常亦實，無常亦實。常者，數緣盡、非數緣盡、虛空，不生、不住、不滅故，是常相；無常者，五眾生、住、滅故，無常相。汝何以言：「常、無常皆不實」？

Question: Your statement that neither permanence nor impermanence is real in the Buddha's Dharma is incorrect! Why? Both permanence and impermanence are real in the Buddha's Dharma. What is permanent includes cessation through discernment, cessation not through discernment, and space. Since they do not arise, abide, or cease, they have the character of permanence. What is impermanent is the five aggregates, since they arise, abide, and cease. Why do you say that permanence and impermanence are both unreal?

答曰：聖人有二種語：一者、方便語，二者、直語。方便語者，為人、為因緣故。為人者，為眾生說是常、是無常，如對治悉檀中說。若說無常，欲拔眾生三界著樂；佛思惟：「以何令眾生得離欲？」是故說無常法。如偈說：「若觀無生法，於生法得離；若觀無為法，於有為得離。」云何生？生名因緣和合，無常，不自在，屬因緣，有老病死相、欺誑相、破壞相，是名生；生則是有為法。

Answer: Noble ones use two kinds of speech: skillful speech and direct speech. Skillful speech is adapted to people and conditions. Adapting it to people means teaching permanence or impermanence to living beings, as explained in the counteractive siddhānta. When teaching impermanence, the aim is to uproot beings' attachment to pleasures in the three realms. The Buddha reflects, “How can I enable living beings to become free of desire?” Thus he teaches impermanence. As a verse says: “Contemplating the unborn, one becomes free of what is born; contemplating the unconditioned, one becomes free of the conditioned.” What is birth? Birth means the conjunction of causes and conditions: impermanent, without autonomy, dependent on conditions, marked by aging, illness, death, deception, and destruction. This is called birth, and what is born is conditioned.

如對治悉檀說。常無常，非實相，二俱過故。若諸法非有常非無常，是為愚癡論。所以者何？若非有則破無，若非無則破有；若破此二事，更有何法可說？

As explained in the counteractive siddhānta, permanence and impermanence are not the true character, because both involve faults. If phenomena are said to be neither permanent nor impermanent, this is foolish discourse. Why? Denying existence refutes nonexistence, and denying nonexistence refutes existence. If both are refuted, what further phenomenon can be spoken of?

問曰：佛法常空相中，非有、非無；空以除有，空空遮無，是為非有、非無，何以言愚癡論？

Question: In the Buddha's Dharma, phenomena are always characterized by emptiness, neither existent nor nonexistent. Emptiness removes existence, and the emptiness of emptiness blocks nonexistence. This is neither existence nor nonexistence. Why call it foolish discourse?

答曰：佛法實相，不受、不著。汝非有、非無受著故，是為癡論。若言非有、非無，是則可說、可破，是心生處，是鬪諍處。佛法則不然，雖因緣故說非有、非無，不生著；不生著則不可壞、不可破。

Answer: The true character in the Buddha's Dharma is neither grasped nor clung to. Because you grasp and cling to neither existence nor nonexistence, yours is foolish discourse. To assert neither existence nor nonexistence is to provide something that can be spoken of and refuted, a basis for mental constructions and disputes. The Buddha's Dharma is otherwise: although neither existence nor nonexistence is taught in response to conditions, no attachment arises. Without attachment, it cannot be destroyed or refuted.

諸法若有邊，若無邊，若有無邊，若非有無邊；若死後有去，若死後無去，若死後有去無去，若死後非有去非無去；是身是神，身異神異，亦如是，皆不實。於六十二見中觀諸法，亦皆不實。如是一切除却，信佛法清淨不壞相，心不悔、不轉，是名法忍。

Whether phenomena are finite, infinite, both finite and infinite, or neither; whether after death one goes on, does not go on, both goes on and does not, or neither; whether body and self are the same or different—all these positions are likewise unreal. Examining phenomena through the sixty-two views is also unreal. Removing all these views, one trusts the pure and indestructible character of the Buddha's Dharma, without regret or wavering in the mind. This is called patience concerning phenomena.

If something existed before but is now absent, or exists now but will later be absent, that is annihilation. If this were so, there would be no causes and conditions. Without causes and conditions, everything should arise from any single thing, or nothing at all should arise from anything. The same applies to future lives: if the conditions of wrongdoing and merit were cut off, there should be no differences of poverty and wealth or high and low status, nor any fall into unfortunate destinies or among animals.

It is what Buddhas and bodhisattvas of the past, future, and present, as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, know and teach. Because confidence is great, there is no doubt or regret; because the power of confidence is great, one can uphold and accept it. This is called patience concerning phenomena.

Pure Acceptance Without Clinging to Emptiness

Chinese Source

復次，禪定力故，心柔軟清淨，聞諸法實相，應心與會，信著深入，無疑、無悔。所以者何？疑、悔是欲界繫法，麁惡故不入柔軟心中，是名法忍。

English Translation

Furthermore, through the power of meditative concentration, the mind becomes gentle and pure. On hearing the true character of phenomena, one's mind accords with it, and confidence enters deeply, without doubt or regret. Why? Doubt and regret belong to the desire realm; being coarse and harmful, they do not enter this gentle mind. This is called patience concerning phenomena.

復次，智慧力故，於一切諸法中種種觀，無有一法可得者；是法能忍、能受，不疑、不悔，是名法忍。

Furthermore, through the power of wisdom, one examines all phenomena in various ways and finds not a single phenomenon that can be apprehended. Being able to patiently accept this without doubt or regret is called patience concerning phenomena.

復次，菩薩思惟：「凡夫人以無明毒故，於一切諸法中作轉相：非常作常想，苦作樂想，無我有我想，空謂有實，非有為有，有為非有。如是等種種法中作轉相，得聖實智慧，破無明毒，知諸法實相。」得無常、苦、空、無我智慧，棄捨不著，是法能忍，是名法忍。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects: “Through the poison of ignorance, ordinary people invert the characteristics of phenomena: they imagine permanence in the impermanent, pleasure in suffering, a self in what is without self, and substance in emptiness. They take what does not exist to exist and what exists not to exist. Through noble, true wisdom, one breaks the poison of ignorance and knows the true character of phenomena.” Attaining wisdom concerning impermanence, suffering, emptiness, and non-self, one relinquishes them without attachment. Being able to patiently accept this is called patience concerning phenomena.

復次，觀一切諸法，從本已來常空，今世亦空，是法能信、能受，是為法忍。

Furthermore, one contemplates that all phenomena have always been empty from the beginning and are empty now as well. Being able to trust and accept this is patience concerning phenomena.

問曰：若從本已來常空，今世亦空，是為惡邪，云何言法忍？

Question: If they have always been empty and are empty now, this is an evil, wrong view. Why call it patience concerning phenomena?

答曰：若觀諸法常畢竟空，取相心著，是為惡邪見；若觀空不著，不生邪見，是為法忍。如偈說：「諸法性常空，心亦不著空；如是法能忍，是佛道初相。」如是等種種入智慧門，觀諸法實相，心不退、不悔，不隨諸觀，亦無所憂，能得自利、利他，是名法忍。

Answer: If one contemplates phenomena as always ultimately empty but grasps at characteristics and becomes mentally attached, this is an evil, wrong view. If one contemplates emptiness without attachment and does not generate wrong views, this is patience concerning phenomena. As a verse says: “The nature of phenomena is always empty; the mind does not cling even to emptiness. Patiently accepting such a teaching is the first mark of the Buddha's path.” Through these and various other gateways into wisdom, one contemplates the true character of phenomena without retreat or regret in the mind, without being carried away by contemplations or experiencing distress, and can benefit both oneself and others. This is called patience concerning phenomena.

是法忍有三種行清淨：不見忍辱法，不見己身，不見罵辱人；不戲諸法，是時名清淨法忍。以是事故，說菩薩住般若波羅蜜中，能具足羼提波羅蜜，不動、不退故。云何名「不動不退」？瞋恚不生，不出惡言，身不加惡，心無所疑。菩薩知般若波羅蜜實相，不見諸法，心無所著故；若人來罵，若加楚毒、殺害，一切能忍。以是故說「住般若波羅蜜中，能具足羼提波羅蜜」。

This patience concerning phenomena has purity in three aspects of practice: one does not see a phenomenon of patience, oneself, or a person who insults one. Without conceptually proliferating about phenomena, it is then called pure patience concerning phenomena. For this reason, it is said that a bodhisattva abiding in the perfection of wisdom can fulfill the perfection of patience, because one is unmoved and does not retreat. What is “unmoved and not retreating”? Anger does not arise, evil words are not spoken, bodily harm is not inflicted, and the mind has no doubt. Knowing the true character of the perfection of wisdom, the bodhisattva does not apprehend phenomena or cling to anything in the mind. If people come to abuse, torture, or kill them, they can endure it all. Thus it is said: “Abiding in the perfection of wisdom, one can fulfill the perfection of patience.”

Part Twenty-Six: The Perfection of Diligence

Chinese Source

〖經〗「身心精進不懈息故，應具足毘梨耶波羅蜜。」

English Translation

Sutra: “Through diligence of body and mind, without slackening or resting, one should fulfill the perfection of diligence.”

〖論〗毘梨耶（秦言精進）。

Treatise: Vīrya means diligence.

問曰：如精進是一切善法本，應最在初，今何以故第四？

Question: Since diligence is the root of all wholesome qualities, it should come first. Why is it now fourth?

答曰：布施、持戒、忍辱，世間常有。如客主之義，法應供給，乃至畜生亦知布施；或有人種種因緣故能布施，若為今世、若為後世、若為道故布施，不須精進。

Answer: Giving, upholding precepts, and patience are common in the world. According to the proper relationship of host and guest, one should provide support; even animals know how to give. Some people give for various reasons: for this life, a future life, or the path. This does not require diligence.

如持戒者，見為惡之人，王法治罪，便自畏懼，不敢為非；或有性善，不作諸惡；有人聞今世作惡，後世受罪，而以怖畏，故能持戒；有人聞持戒因緣故，得離生、老、病、死，是中心生、口言：「我從今日，不復殺生。」如是等即是戒，豈須精進波羅蜜而能行耶？

As for upholding precepts, people see wrongdoers punished under royal law and become afraid, not daring to do wrong. Others are good by nature and do not commit evils. Some hear that wrongdoing in this life brings punishment in the next and uphold precepts out of fear. Others hear that upholding precepts can lead to freedom from birth, aging, illness, and death, and form the intention and say, “From this day onward, I shall no longer kill living beings.” These and similar acts are already precepts. Must one possess the perfection of diligence to practice them?

如忍辱中，若罵、若打、若殺，或畏故不報，或少力、或畏罪、或修善人法、或為求道故，默然不報，皆不必須精進波羅蜜乃能忍也。

Likewise, in patience, when abused, struck, or attacked with the intent to kill, one may not retaliate because of fear, weakness, fear of wrongdoing, the practice of good people's conduct, or the pursuit of the path. Remaining silent without retaliation does not necessarily require the perfection of diligence.

今欲得知諸法實相，行般若波羅蜜故，修行禪定；禪定是實智慧之門，是中應懃修精進，一心行禪。

Now, to know the true character of phenomena and practice the perfection of wisdom, one cultivates meditative concentration. Concentration is the gateway to true wisdom. Here one should diligently exert oneself and practice meditation wholeheartedly.

復次，布施、持戒、忍辱，是大福德，安隱快樂，有好名譽，所欲者得；既得知此福利之味，今欲增進，更得妙勝禪定、智慧。譬如穿井已見濕泥，轉加增進必望得水；又如鑽火，已得見煙，倍復力勵，必望得火。

Furthermore, giving, upholding precepts, and patience bring great merit, security, happiness, good reputation, and the fulfillment of wishes. Having tasted these blessings and benefits, one now wishes to advance further and attain still more excellent concentration and wisdom. It is like digging a well: once damp earth appears, one redoubles one's effort, expecting to reach water. Or it is like kindling fire by friction: once smoke appears, one doubles one's exertion, expecting fire.

欲成佛道，凡有二門：一者、福德，二者、智慧。行施、戒、忍是為福德門；知一切諸法實相，摩訶般若波羅蜜，是為智慧門。菩薩入福德門，除一切罪，所願皆得；若不得願者，以罪垢遮故。入智慧門，則不厭生死、不樂涅槃，二事一故。

There are two gateways to accomplishing Buddhahood: merit and wisdom. Giving, ethical discipline, and patience are the gateway of merit; knowing the true character of all phenomena, the great perfection of wisdom, is the gateway of wisdom. Entering the gateway of merit, a bodhisattva removes wrongdoing and fulfills wishes. If wishes remain unfulfilled, it is because the stains of wrongdoing obstruct them. Entering the gateway of wisdom, one neither rejects birth and death nor delights in nirvāṇa, because the two are one.

今欲出生摩訶般若波羅蜜，般若波羅蜜要因禪定門，禪定門必須大精進力。何以故？散亂心不能得見諸法實相。譬如風中然燈，不能照物；燈在密屋，明必能照。是禪定、智慧，不可以福願求，亦非麁觀能得，要須身、心精懃，急著不懈，爾乃成辦。如佛所說：血、肉、脂、髓皆使竭盡，但令皮、骨、筋在，不捨精進。如是乃能得禪定、智慧，得是二事，則眾事皆辦。以是故，精進第四，名為禪定、實智慧之根。上三中雖有精進，少故不說。

To bring forth the great perfection of wisdom, one must pass through the gateway of meditative concentration, which requires great diligence. Why? A distracted mind cannot see the true character of phenomena. A lamp burning in the wind cannot illuminate things; in a sheltered room, its light can surely shine. Concentration and wisdom cannot be obtained merely by wishing for blessings, nor by superficial contemplation. They require diligent exertion of body and mind, urgent application without slackening; only then are they accomplished. As the Buddha said, even if blood, flesh, fat, and marrow are exhausted and only skin, bones, and sinews remain, one must not abandon diligence. In this way one can attain concentration and wisdom. With these two attained, all undertakings can be accomplished. Thus diligence is fourth and is called the root of concentration and true wisdom. Although diligence is present in the preceding three perfections, it is slight and therefore is not mentioned.

Why Merit Alone Does Not Bring Awakening

Chinese Source

問曰：有人言：但行布施、持戒、忍辱故，得大福德；福德力故，所願皆得，禪定、智慧自然而至，復何用精進波羅蜜為？

English Translation

Question: Some say that giving, upholding precepts, and patience alone produce great merit. Through the power of merit, all wishes are fulfilled, and concentration and wisdom come naturally. What further need is there for the perfection of diligence?

答曰：佛道甚深難得，雖有布施、持戒、忍辱力，要須精進，得甚深禪定、實智慧及無量諸佛法。若不行精進，則不生禪定；禪定不生，則不得生梵天王處，何況欲求佛道？

Answer: The Buddha's path is profound and difficult to attain. Although one has the power of giving, ethical discipline, and patience, diligence is necessary to obtain profound concentration, true wisdom, and the immeasurable qualities of Buddhas. Without diligence, concentration does not arise. Without concentration, one cannot even be born in the realm of the Brahmā king, much less seek Buddhahood.

復次，有人如民大居士等，欲得無量寶物，則應意皆得。如頂生王王四天下，天雨七寶及所須之物，釋提婆那民分座與坐；雖有是福，然不能得道。如羅頻珠比丘，雖得阿羅漢道，乞食七日不得，空鉢而還；後以禪定火自燒其身而般涅槃。以是故知，非但福德力故得道；欲成佛道，要須懃大精進。

Furthermore, people such as the great householder Minda (民大) obtain immeasurable treasures whenever they wish. King Māndhātṛ ruled the four continents; the seven treasures and whatever he needed rained down from heaven, and Śakra shared his seat with him. Although possessing such merit, he could not attain the path. By contrast, the bhikṣu Luopinzhu (羅頻珠), though an arhat, obtained nothing after begging for seven days and returned with an empty bowl. Later, he burned his body with the fire of meditative concentration and entered parinirvāṇa. Thus we know that the path is not attained through merit alone. To accomplish Buddhahood, great diligence is essential.

The Benefits of Diligence

Chinese Source

問曰：菩薩觀精進，有何利益而懃修不懈？

English Translation

Question: What benefits does a bodhisattva see in diligence that lead to tireless practice?

答曰：一切今世、後世道德利益，皆由精進得。復次，若人欲自度身，尚當懃急精進，何況菩薩誓願欲度一切！

Answer: All benefits of the path and virtue in this life and future lives are obtained through diligence. Furthermore, even someone seeking only personal liberation should practice urgently and diligently. How much more a bodhisattva who vows to liberate everyone!

如讚精進偈中說：「有人不惜身，智慧心決定，如法行精進，所求事無難！如農夫懃修，所收必豐實；亦如涉遠路，懃則必能達。若得生天上，及得涅槃樂，如是之因緣，皆由精進力。

As verses praising diligence say: “One who does not cling to the body, whose wise mind is resolute, and who practices diligence according to the Dharma finds no undertaking difficult. A farmer who cultivates diligently surely reaps an abundant harvest; likewise, on a distant journey, diligence surely brings arrival. Birth in heaven and the happiness of nirvāṇa: the conditions for these all come through the power of diligence.

非天非無因，自作故自得，誰有智慧人，而不自勉勵？三界火熾然，譬如大炎火，有智決斷人，乃能得免離！以是故佛告：阿難正精進，如是不懈怠，直至於佛道。

“Not through a deity or without a cause, but through one's own action does one obtain the result. Who with wisdom would fail to exert themselves? The three realms burn fiercely, like a great blazing fire. Only those with wisdom and resolve can escape. Thus the Buddha told Ānanda to practice right diligence, without laziness, straight through to Buddhahood.

勉強而懃修，穿地能通泉；精進亦如是，無求而不得。能如行道法，精進不懈者，無量果必得，此報終不失！」

“Striving and cultivating diligently, one can dig through earth to reach a spring. Diligence is likewise: nothing sought remains unattained. Those who can follow the way of practice, diligent without slackening, will surely gain immeasurable fruits. This reward is never lost!”

復次，精進法是一切諸善法之根本，能出生一切諸道法，乃至阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，何況於小利！如毘尼中說：「一切諸善法，乃至阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，皆從精進不放逸生。」復次，精進能動發先世福德，如雨潤種，能令必生；此亦如是，雖有先世福德因緣，若無精進則不能生；乃至今世利尚不能得，何況佛道！

Furthermore, diligence is the root of all wholesome qualities and can produce all qualities of the path, up to unsurpassed perfect awakening. How much more can it produce lesser benefits! As the Vinaya says: “All wholesome qualities, up to unsurpassed perfect awakening, arise from diligence and heedfulness.” Furthermore, diligence activates merit from previous lives, like rain moistening seeds and causing them to sprout. Even with the conditions of past merit, nothing can arise without diligence. One cannot obtain even benefits in this life, much less Buddhahood!

復次，諸大菩薩荷負眾生，受一切苦，乃至阿鼻泥犁中苦，心亦不懈，是為精進。

Furthermore, great bodhisattvas take up the burden of living beings and endure every suffering, even that of Avīci Hell, without slackening in mind. This is diligence.

復次，一切眾事，若無精進，則不能成。譬如下藥，以巴豆為主；若除巴豆，則無下力。如是意止、神足、根、力、覺、道，必待精進；若無精進，則眾事不辦。如戒唯在八道，不在餘處；信在根、力，餘處則無。如精進者，無處不有，既總眾法而別自有門。譬如無明使，遍在一切諸使中，而別有不共無明。

Furthermore, no undertaking can be completed without diligence. It is like a purgative in which croton seed is the principal ingredient: remove it, and the purgative has no power. Likewise, the foundations of mindfulness, bases of spiritual power, faculties, powers, awakening factors, and path all depend on diligence; without it, nothing is accomplished. Ethical discipline occurs only in the eightfold path, not in the other groups; confidence occurs in the faculties and powers, but not elsewhere. Diligence, however, is present everywhere. It pervades these qualities yet also has a distinct gateway of its own. Similarly, the latent tendency of ignorance pervades all latent tendencies, while there is also a distinct, independent ignorance.

Increasing Diligence by Recognizing Laziness

Chinese Source

問曰：菩薩欲得一切佛法，欲度一切眾生，欲滅一切煩惱，皆得如意，云何增益精進而能得佛？譬如小火不能燒大林，火勢增益，能燒一切。

English Translation

Question: A bodhisattva wishes to obtain all Buddha qualities, liberate all living beings, and extinguish all afflictions, fulfilling every aspiration. How can diligence be increased so that Buddhahood is attained? A small fire cannot burn a great forest, but once its force increases, it can burn everything.

答曰：菩薩從初發心，作誓願：「當令一切眾生得歡樂，常為一切，不自惜身；若惜身者，於諸善法不能成辦。」以是故增益精進。

Answer: From the first arousing of the aspiration, a bodhisattva vows: “I shall bring happiness to all living beings, always acting for all without clinging to my body. If I cling to my body, I cannot accomplish wholesome qualities.” For this reason, one increases diligence.

復次，菩薩種種因緣，呵懈怠心，令樂著精進。懈怠黑雲，覆諸明慧，吞滅功德，增長不善。懈怠之人，初雖小樂，後則大苦；譬如毒食，初雖香美，久則殺人。懈怠之心，燒諸功德，譬如大火燒諸林野。懈怠之人失諸功德，譬如被賊，無復遺餘。

Furthermore, through various reflections, a bodhisattva reproaches the lazy mind and makes it delight in diligence. The dark cloud of laziness covers clear wisdom, devours merit, and increases what is unwholesome. A lazy person may at first enjoy a little pleasure but afterward suffers greatly, like poisoned food that tastes delicious at first but eventually kills. The lazy mind burns meritorious qualities like a great fire burning forests and fields. A lazy person loses merit completely, like someone robbed of everything.

如偈說：「應得而不得，已得而復失，既自輕其身，眾人亦不敬。常處大闇中，無有諸威德，尊貴智慧法，此事永以失。聞諸妙道法，不能以益身，如是之過失，皆由懈怠心。

As a verse says: “What should be gained is not gained; what was gained is lost again. One treats oneself lightly, and others show no respect. Always dwelling in great darkness, without dignity or strength, one forever loses honored and noble wisdom. Hearing the marvelous teachings of the path but failing to benefit oneself: such faults all come from a lazy mind.

雖聞增益法，不能得上及，如是之過罪，皆由懈怠心。生業不修理，不入於道法，如是之過失，皆由懈怠心。

“Though hearing teachings that lead to advancement, one cannot reach the higher attainment: such faults all come from a lazy mind. Neglecting one's livelihood and not entering the Dharma of the path: such faults all come from a lazy mind.

上智所棄遠，中人時復近，下愚為之沒，如猪樂在溷。若為世中人，三事皆廢失：欲樂及財利，福德亦復沒。若為出家人，則不得二事：生天及涅槃，名譽二俱失。

“Those of superior wisdom reject it and keep away; average people sometimes approach it; the lowest fools sink into it like pigs delighting in filth. For someone living in the world, three things are all lost: sensual pleasure, wealth, and merit. For one who has gone forth, two things remain unattained: birth in heaven and nirvāṇa. Reputation in either sphere is lost.

如是諸廢失，欲知其所由，一切諸賊中，無過懈怠賊！以是眾罪故，懶心不應作，馬井二比丘，懈怠墜惡道；雖見佛聞法，猶亦不自免！」

“If you wish to know the source of these losses, among all thieves none surpasses the thief of laziness! Because of these faults, one should not arouse a slothful mind. The two bhikṣus Ma and Jing (馬、井) fell into unfortunate destinies through laziness. Although they saw the Buddha and heard the Dharma, they could not save themselves!”

如是等種種觀懈怠之罪，精進增長。

Contemplating these and various other faults of laziness, diligence increases.

Diligence in the Bodhisattva's Work

Chinese Source

復次，觀精進之益，今世、後世、佛道、涅槃之利，皆由精進。

English Translation

Furthermore, contemplating the benefits of diligence, one sees that the benefits of this life, future lives, Buddhahood, and nirvāṇa all come through diligence.

復次，菩薩知一切諸法皆空無所有，而不證涅槃；憐愍眾生，集諸善法，是精進波羅蜜力。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva knows all phenomena to be empty and without anything to possess, yet does not realize nirvāṇa. Out of compassion for living beings, one gathers wholesome qualities. This is the power of the perfection of diligence.

復次，菩薩一人獨無等侶，以精進福德力故，能破魔軍及結使賊，得成佛道。既得佛道，於一切諸法，一相無相，其實皆空；而為眾生說諸法種種名字，種種方便，度脫眾生老病死苦。將滅度時，以法身與彌勒菩薩摩訶薩、迦葉、阿難等；然後入金剛三昧，自碎身骨令如芥子，以度眾生而不捨精進力。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva, alone and without an equal companion, can defeat Māra's army and the bandits of the fetters through the power of diligence and merit, and accomplish Buddhahood. Having attained Buddhahood, one knows that all phenomena share one character, that of no characteristics, and are empty in reality. Yet for living beings one teaches phenomena through various names and skillful means, liberating beings from aging, illness, and death. When about to enter final nirvāṇa, one entrusts the Dharma body to the bodhisattva mahāsattva Maitreya, Kāśyapa, Ānanda, and others. Then one enters vajra samādhi and breaks one's own bones into pieces like mustard seeds to liberate living beings, without abandoning the power of diligence.

復次，如阿難為諸比丘說七覺意，至精進覺意，佛問阿難：「汝說精進覺意耶？」阿難言：「說精進覺意。」如是三問、三答。佛即從坐起，告阿難：「人能愛樂修行精進，無事不得；得至佛道，終不虛也。」如是種種因緣，觀精進利而得增益。

Furthermore, when Ānanda was explaining the seven factors of awakening to the bhikṣus and reached the factor of diligence, the Buddha asked, “Ānanda, are you speaking of the awakening factor of diligence?” Ānanda replied, “I am speaking of the awakening factor of diligence.” The Buddha asked three times, and Ānanda answered three times. The Buddha then rose from his seat and told Ānanda, “If people delight in cultivating diligence, there is nothing they cannot attain. They can reach Buddhahood; it is never in vain.” Through these various reflections, contemplating the benefits of diligence enables it to increase.

Aspiration, Diligence, and Heedfulness

Chinese Source

如是精進，佛有時說為欲，或時說精進，有時說不放逸。譬如人欲遠行，初欲去時，是名為欲；發行不住，是為精進；能自勸勵，不令行事稽留，是為不放逸。以是故，知欲生精進，精進生故不放逸，不放逸故能生諸法，乃至得成佛道。

English Translation

The Buddha sometimes describes this effort as aspiration, sometimes as diligence, and sometimes as heedfulness. It is like someone wishing to travel far: the initial wish to go is called aspiration; setting out and continuing without stopping is diligence; encouraging oneself and preventing delay is heedfulness. Thus aspiration gives rise to diligence, diligence gives rise to heedfulness, and heedfulness gives rise to qualities of the path, up to the accomplishment of Buddhahood.

復次，菩薩欲脫生、老、病、死，亦欲度脫眾生，常應精進一心不放逸。如人擎油鉢行大眾中，現前一心不放逸故，大得名利。又如偏閣嶮道，若懸繩，若乘山羊；此諸惡道，以一心不放逸故，身得安隱，今世大得名利。求道精進，亦復如是；若一心不放逸，所願皆得。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva wishing to escape birth, aging, illness, and death and also to liberate living beings should always be diligent, wholehearted, and heedful. Someone carrying a bowl of oil through a crowd gains great reputation and reward through being wholeheartedly attentive and heedful. Likewise, those traveling along precarious galleries and dangerous paths, suspended from ropes or riding mountain goats, remain safe and gain great reputation and reward in this life through wholehearted heedfulness. Diligent pursuit of the path is the same: with wholehearted heedfulness, one's wishes are fulfilled.

復次，譬如水流，能決大石；不放逸心，亦復如是。專修方便，常行不廢，能破煩惱諸結使山。

Furthermore, flowing water can split a great rock. A heedful mind is similar: through focused, skillful practice, continued without interruption, one can break through the mountains of afflictions and fetters.

復次，菩薩有三種思惟：「若我不作，不得果報；若我不自作，不從他來；若我作者，終不失。」如是思惟，當必精進，為佛道故，懃修專精而不放逸。

Furthermore, a bodhisattva reflects in three ways: “If I do not act, I shall receive no result. If I do not act myself, the result will not come from another. If I act, the result will not be lost.” Reflecting in this way, one should surely be diligent, practicing earnestly and wholeheartedly for Buddhahood, without heedlessness.

The Forest Spirit and the Negligent Monk

Chinese Source

如一小阿蘭若，獨在林中坐禪而生懈怠。林中有神是佛弟子，入一死屍骨中，歌儛而來，說此偈言：「林中小比丘，何以生懈廢？晝來若不畏，夜復如是來！」

English Translation

For example, a young forest-dwelling bhikṣu was meditating alone in the woods when he became lazy. A spirit in the forest, a disciple of the Buddha, entered the bones of a corpse and approached singing and dancing, speaking this verse: “Young bhikṣu in the forest, why have you become lazy and neglectful? If you are not afraid when I come by day, I shall come again like this at night!”

是比丘驚怖起坐，內自思惟，中夜復睡。是神復現十頭，口中出火，牙爪如劍，眼赤如炎，顧語將從：「捉此懈怠比丘！此處不應懈怠，何以故爾？」是比丘大怖，即起思惟，專精念法，得阿羅漢道。是名自強精進不放逸力，能得道果。

Terrified, the bhikṣu rose to sit and reflected inwardly, but in the middle of the night he fell asleep again. The spirit then appeared with ten heads, flames issuing from its mouths, fangs and claws like swords, and eyes red as fire. Turning to its attendants, it said, “Seize this lazy bhikṣu! This is no place for laziness. Why is he behaving like this?” Greatly frightened, the bhikṣu immediately rose to contemplate, concentrated earnestly on the Dharma, and attained arhatship. This is the power of self-directed diligence and heedfulness, through which one can attain the fruit of the path.

Valuing the Fruit of Practice

Chinese Source

復次，是精進不自惜身而惜果報，於身四儀：坐、臥、行、住常懃精進，寧自失身，不廢道業。譬如失火，以瓶水投之，唯存滅火而不惜瓶。

English Translation

Furthermore, such diligence values the result rather than clinging to the body. In all four bodily postures—sitting, lying down, walking, and standing—one remains diligent, preferring to lose the body rather than abandon the work of the path. It is like throwing a jar of water onto a fire: one's sole concern is extinguishing the fire, not preserving the jar.

如仙人師教弟子說偈言：「決定心悅豫，如獲大果報，如願事得時，乃知此最妙！」如是種種因緣，觀精進之利，能令精進增益。

As an ascetic teacher instructed disciples in verse: “Resolute, the mind rejoices as though it had gained a great reward. When one's aspiration is fulfilled, one knows this to be most excellent!” Through these and various other reflections on its benefits, diligence increases.

復次，菩薩修諸苦行，若有人來求索頭、目、髓、腦，盡能與之，而自念言：「我有忍辱、精進、智慧、方便之力，受之尚苦，何況愚騃三塗眾生？我當為此眾生故，懃修精進，早成佛道而度脫之。」

Furthermore, while a bodhisattva practices austerities, if someone requests their head, eyes, marrow, or brain, they can give them all, reflecting: “Even with the powers of patience, diligence, wisdom, and skillful means, I still find this painful. How much more do the foolish beings in the three unfortunate destinies suffer! For their sake, I should diligently exert myself, swiftly accomplish Buddhahood, and liberate them.”
