Fascicle 13 · 大智度論卷第十三

The Perfection of Morality

Fascicle 13 explains the perfection of morality and the character of the precepts.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: translation in progress Source base: Taishō T25, no. 1509 Morality and the Buddha path

Translation Method

Each Chinese paragraph appears beside its English counterpart.

Previous: Fascicle 12

The Perfection of Morality — Part Twenty-One

Chinese Source

〖經〗「罪、不罪不可得故，應具足尸羅波羅蜜。」〖論〗尸羅（秦言性善），好行善道，不自放逸，是名尸羅。或受戒行善，或不受戒行善，皆名尸羅。

English Translation

Sutra: ‘Because wrongdoing and non-wrongdoing cannot be apprehended, one should fulfill the perfection of morality.’ Treatise: Śīla is glossed as goodness of character: delighting in wholesome conduct without heedlessness. Practicing goodness with or without formally receiving precepts is called śīla.

尸羅者，略說身、口律儀有八種：不惱害、不劫盜、不邪婬，不妄語、不兩舌、不惡口、不綺語，不飲酒，及淨命，是名戒相。

Briefly, śīla comprises eight forms of bodily and verbal restraint: refraining from harming, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, frivolous speech, and alcohol, together with pure livelihood. These constitute the character of morality.

若不護、放捨，是名破戒。破此戒者，墮三惡道中。若下持戒生人中，中持戒生六欲天中，上持戒又行四禪、四空定，生色、無色界清淨天中。上持戒有三種：下清淨持戒得阿羅漢，中清淨持戒得辟支佛，上清淨持戒得佛道。不著、不猗，不破、不缺，聖所讚愛，如是名為上清淨持戒。若慈愍眾生故，為度眾生故，亦知戒實相故，心不猗著；如此持戒，將來令人至佛道，如是名為得無上佛道戒。

Failing to guard and maintain these is breaking precepts, which leads to the three bad destinies. Lower observance brings human birth; intermediate observance brings birth in the six desire heavens; higher observance, together with the four dhyānas and four formless attainments, brings birth in the pure heavens of the form and formless realms. Higher observance itself has three degrees: lower pure morality leads to arhatship, intermediate to pratyekabuddhahood, and highest to Buddhahood. Morality without attachment or dependence, unbroken and intact, praised and cherished by noble ones, is highest pure morality. Observing precepts out of compassion to liberate beings, while knowing their true reality and not clinging to them, leads to Buddhahood and is called morality attaining unsurpassed awakening.

若人求大善利，當堅持戒，如惜重寶，如護身命。何以故？譬如大地，一切萬物有形之類，皆依地而住；戒亦如是，戒為一切善法住處。

Anyone seeking great wholesome benefit should firmly guard precepts like precious treasure or life itself. Why? Just as all material things rest upon the earth, morality supports all wholesome dharmas.

復次，譬如無足欲行，無翅欲飛，無船欲渡，是不可得；若無戒欲得好果，亦復如是。若人棄捨此戒，雖山居苦行，食果服藥，與禽獸無異。或有人但服水為戒，或服乳，或服氣；或剃髮，或長髮，或頂上留少許髮；或著袈裟，或著白衣，或著草衣，或木皮衣；或冬入水，或夏火炙；若自墜高巖，若於恒河中洗；若日三浴，再供養火；種種祠祀，種種呪願，受行苦行。以無此戒，空無所得。若有人雖處高堂大殿，好衣美食，而能行此戒者，得生好處及得道果。若貴若賤、若小若大，能行此淨戒，皆得大利。若破此戒，無貴無賤、無大無小，皆不得隨意生善處。

Walking without feet, flying without wings, or crossing without a boat is impossible. Likewise, good results cannot be obtained without morality. Someone who abandons it and practices mountain austerities, eating fruit and medicinal plants, differs little from animals. Some live only on water, milk, or air; shave their heads, grow long hair, or retain a topknot; wear dyed robes, white garments, grass, or bark; enter water in winter or expose themselves to fire in summer; leap from cliffs, wash in the Ganges, bathe three times daily, tend fire twice, or perform sacrifices, invocations, and austerities. Without morality, these gain nothing. Conversely, someone living in a lofty palace with fine clothes and food who observes precepts gains good rebirth and the fruits of the path. High or low, young or old, all benefit from pure morality; those who break it cannot obtain the good rebirth they wish.

復次，破戒之人，譬如清涼池而有毒蛇，不中澡浴；亦如好華果樹，而多逆刺。若人雖在貴家生，身體端政，廣學多聞，而不樂持戒，無慈愍心，亦復如是。如偈說：「貴而無智則為衰，智而憍慢亦為衰，持戒之人而毀戒，今世後世一切衰！」

A person who breaks precepts is like a cool pond containing poisonous snakes, unsuitable for bathing, or a fine fruit tree covered in sharp thorns. Noble birth, beauty, and extensive learning without delight in morality or compassion are similar. As a verse says: ‘Noble rank without wisdom is decline; wisdom with pride is decline. For one who has undertaken precepts, breaking them brings decline in this and future lives.’

人雖貧賤而能持戒，勝於富貴而破戒者。華香、木香不能遠聞；持戒之香，周遍十方。持戒之人，具足安樂，名聲遠聞，天、人敬愛，現世常得種種快樂。若欲天上、人中、富貴、長壽，取之不難；持戒清淨，所願皆得。

A poor, lowly person who keeps precepts surpasses a wealthy, honored person who breaks them. Flower and wood fragrances do not travel far, but the fragrance of morality pervades the ten directions. Those who keep precepts enjoy well-being, widespread good reputation, and the respect and affection of devas and humans. If they wish for heavenly or human birth, wealth, status, or long life, these are easily obtained. Pure morality fulfills their wishes.

復次，持戒之人，見破戒人刑獄考掠，種種苦惱，自知永離此事，以為欣慶。若持戒之人，見善人得譽，名聞快樂，心自念言：如彼得譽，我亦有分。持戒之人，壽終之時，刀風解身，筋脈斷絕，自知持戒清淨，心不怖畏。如偈說：「大惡病中，戒為良藥；大恐怖中，戒為守護；死闇冥中，戒為明燈；於惡道中，戒為橋樑；死海水中，戒為大船。」

Seeing offenders imprisoned and tortured, a precept-holder rejoices in being free of such conditions. Seeing good people praised and happy, one thinks, ‘I too share in such praise.’ At death, as knife-like winds break up the body and sever its sinews, knowing one’s precepts pure removes fear. As a verse says: ‘In grave illness, morality is good medicine; in great fear, a guardian; in death’s darkness, a bright lamp; among bad destinies, a bridge; in the ocean of death, a great ship.’

復次，持戒之人，常得今世人所敬養，心樂不悔，衣食無乏，死得生天，後得佛道。持戒之人，無事不得；破戒之人，一切皆失。

Furthermore, precept-holders receive respect and support, enjoy a mind without regret, lack no clothing or food, are born in heaven after death, and eventually attain Buddhahood. Nothing is beyond their attainment, while those who break precepts lose everything.

譬如有人常供養天，其人貧窮，一心供養，滿十二歲，求索富貴。天愍此人，自現其身而問之曰：「汝求何等？」答言：「我求富貴，欲令心之所願，一切皆得！」天與一器，名曰德瓶，而語之言：「所須之物，從此瓶出。」其人得已，應意所欲，無所不得。得如意已，具作好舍，象馬、車乘、七寶具足，供給賓客，事事無乏。客問之言：「汝先貧窮，今日所由得如此富？」答言：「我得天瓶，瓶能出此種種眾物，故富如是。」客言：「出瓶見示，并所出物！」即為出瓶，瓶中引出種種眾物。其人憍泆，立瓶上舞，瓶即破壞，一切眾物亦一時滅。持戒之人，亦復如是，種種妙樂，無願不得；若人破戒，憍泆自恣，亦如彼人破瓶失物。

A poor man once worshiped a deva single-mindedly for twelve years, seeking wealth. Taking pity, the deva appeared and asked his wish. He wanted prosperity and everything he desired. The deva gave him a ‘vessel of virtue,’ saying it would produce whatever he needed. He obtained fine dwellings, elephants, horses, vehicles, and seven treasures, and supplied guests without lack. Asked how he had become wealthy, he explained the heavenly vessel and displayed it, drawing out various objects. Becoming proud and reckless, he stood on it and danced. It shattered, and all his possessions disappeared at once. Likewise, precept-holders obtain wondrous happiness and fulfilled wishes; breaking precepts through arrogance and indulgence resembles breaking the vessel and losing its contents.

復次，持戒之人，名稱之香，今世、後世周滿天上及在人中。復次，持戒之人，人所樂施，不惜財物，不修世利而無所乏；得生天上、十方佛前，入三乘道而得解脫。唯種種邪見持戒，後無所得。復次，若人雖不出家，但能修行戒法，亦得生天。若人持戒清淨，行禪、智慧，欲求度脫老、病、死苦，此願必得。持戒之人，雖無兵仗，眾惡不加；持戒之財，無能奪者；持戒親親，雖死不離；持戒莊嚴，勝於七寶。以是之故，當護於戒，如護身命，如愛寶物。破戒之人，受苦萬端，如向貧人破瓶失物，以是之故應持淨戒。

Furthermore, the good reputation of precept-holders spreads throughout heavens and human worlds, now and later. People gladly give them possessions; without pursuing worldly gain, they lack nothing. They may be born in heaven or before Buddhas of the ten directions and enter the three vehicles to liberation. Only observance based on wrong views fails to yield the intended attainment. Even householders who practice precepts may attain heavenly birth. Pure morality combined with meditation and wisdom certainly fulfills the wish to escape aging, sickness, and death. Though unarmed, precept-holders are protected from evils. Their wealth of morality cannot be stolen; its kinship remains beyond death; its adornment surpasses seven jewels. Guard precepts as life and treasure. Breaking them brings manifold suffering, like the poor man breaking his vessel. Therefore, maintain pure precepts.

復次，持戒之人，觀破戒人罪，應自勉勵，一心持戒。

Furthermore, observing the faults of breaking precepts, one should encourage oneself and keep precepts single-mindedly.

The Faults of Breaking Precepts

Chinese Source

云何名為破戒人罪？破戒之人，人所不敬，其家如塚，人所不到。破戒之人，失諸功德，譬如枯樹，人不愛樂。破戒之人，如霜蓮花，人不喜見。破戒之人，惡心可畏，譬如羅剎。

English Translation

What are the faults of one who breaks the precepts? Such a person receives no respect; their home is like a tomb that no one visits. Deprived of wholesome qualities, they are like a withered tree in which no one delights. They are like a frost-stricken lotus that no one enjoys seeing. Their evil mind is frightening, like a rākṣasa.

破戒之人，人不歸向，譬如渴人，不向枯井。破戒之人，心常疑悔，譬如犯事之人，常畏罪至。破戒之人，如田被雹，不可依仰。破戒之人，譬如苦苽，雖形似甘種而不可食。破戒之人，如賊聚落，不可依止。破戒之人，譬如大病，人不欲近。破戒之人，不得免苦，譬如惡道難可得過。破戒之人，不可共止，譬如惡賊難可親近。

People do not turn to one who breaks the precepts, just as a thirsty person does not approach a dry well. Such a person is constantly troubled by doubt and remorse, like an offender who always fears punishment. They are unreliable, like a hail-stricken field. They are like a bitter gourd: although it resembles a sweet variety, it cannot be eaten. They offer no refuge, like a village of bandits. People avoid them as they would a grave illness. They cannot escape suffering, like a perilous road that is difficult to traverse. One cannot stay with them, just as one cannot safely approach a dangerous bandit.

破戒之人，譬如大坑，行者避之。破戒之人，難可共住，譬如毒蛇。破戒之人，不可近觸，譬如大火。破戒之人，譬如破船，不可乘渡。破戒之人，譬如吐食，不可更噉。

One who breaks the precepts is like a great pit that travelers avoid, or a venomous snake with which it is difficult to dwell. Such a person cannot be approached or touched, like a great fire. They are like a broken boat in which one cannot cross the water, or vomited food that cannot be eaten again.

破戒之人，在好眾中，譬如惡馬在善馬群。破戒之人，與善人異，如驢在牛群。破戒之人，在精進眾，譬如儜兒在健人中。破戒之人，雖似比丘，譬如死屍在眠人中。破戒之人，譬如偽珠在真珠中。破戒之人，譬如伊蘭在栴檀林。

Among good people, one who breaks the precepts is like a bad horse among good horses, or a donkey among cattle. Among diligent practitioners, they are like a weak child among the strong. Although resembling a bhikṣu, they are like a corpse among sleeping people, a counterfeit pearl among genuine pearls, or an eraṇḍa tree in a sandalwood grove.

破戒之人，雖形似善人，內無善法；雖復剃頭、染衣、次第捉籌，名為比丘，實非比丘。

Although one who breaks the precepts resembles a good person outwardly, they have no wholesome qualities within. Even if they shave their head, wear dyed robes, and receive a tally in due order, they are a bhikṣu in name but are not truly a bhikṣu.

破戒之人，若著法衣，則是熱銅鐵鍱以纏其身；若持鉢盂，則是盛洋銅器；若所噉食，則是吞燒鐵丸，飲熱洋銅；若受人供養供給，則是地獄獄鬼守之；若入精舍，則是入大地獄；若坐眾僧床榻，是為坐熱鐵床上。

For one who breaks the precepts, wearing Dharma robes is like wrapping the body in sheets of hot copper and iron; carrying an alms bowl is like carrying a vessel of molten copper. Eating is like swallowing burning iron balls and drinking molten copper. Receiving offerings and support is like being guarded by the wardens of hell. Entering a monastery is like entering a great hell, and sitting on the Saṅgha's beds and seats is like sitting on a bed of red-hot iron.

復次，破戒之人，常懷怖懅，如重病人，常畏死至。亦如五逆罪人，心常自念「我為佛賊」，藏覆避隈；如賊畏人，歲月日過，常不安隱。

Furthermore, one who breaks the precepts is always afraid, like a gravely ill person who constantly fears death. Like one who has committed the five heinous offenses, they keep thinking, “I am a thief against the Buddha,” and hide in secluded corners. Like a thief who fears others, they find no peace as the days, months, and years pass.

破戒之人，雖得供養利樂，是樂不淨；譬如愚人，供養莊嚴死屍，智者聞之，惡不欲見。如是種種無量破戒之罪，不可稱說，行者應當一心持戒。

Although such a person receives offerings, benefits, and pleasures, these pleasures are impure. It is like a fool who makes offerings to a corpse and adorns it: the wise, hearing of this, feel aversion and do not wish to see it. These and countless other faults of breaking the precepts cannot all be described. A practitioner should therefore uphold the precepts wholeheartedly.

Part Twenty-Two: The Characteristics of Precepts — Defining Abstention from Killing

Chinese Source

問曰：已知如是種種功德果報，云何名為「戒相」？

English Translation

Question: We now understand these various merits and their resulting rewards. What are the “characteristics of precepts”?

答曰：惡止不更作，若心生、若口言、若從他受，息身、口惡，是為戒相。

Answer: Ceasing evil and not committing it again—whether through a resolve formed in the mind, a verbal declaration, or receiving precepts from another—and bringing bodily and verbal evil to rest: these are the characteristics of precepts.

云何名為「惡」？若實是眾生，知是眾生，發心欲殺而奪其命，生身業有作色，是名殺生罪。其餘繫閉、鞭打等，是助殺法。

What is meant by “evil”? When there actually is a living being, one knows it to be a living being, intends to kill it, and takes its life, thereby producing bodily karma consisting of manifest form, this is called the offense of killing. Other acts, such as confinement and beating, are actions that assist killing.

復次，殺他得殺罪，非自殺身。心知眾生而殺是名殺罪，不如夜中見人謂為杌樹而殺者。故殺生得殺罪，非不故也。快心殺生得殺罪，非狂癡。命根斷，是殺罪，非作瘡。身業是殺罪，非但口教勅。口教是殺罪，非但心生。

Furthermore, it is killing another that incurs the offense of killing, rather than killing oneself. Killing with the knowledge that the victim is a living being constitutes this offense, unlike killing someone at night after mistaking them for a tree stump. Deliberate killing incurs the offense, rather than unintentional killing. Killing with a willing mind incurs it, rather than an act committed in madness or delusion. Severing the life faculty constitutes the offense, rather than merely inflicting a wound. Bodily action constitutes the offense of killing, rather than only issuing a verbal command. A verbal command to kill also constitutes the offense, rather than merely having the thought.

如是等，名殺罪；不作是罪，名為戒。若人受戒，心生、口言：「我從今日不復殺生！」若身不動，口不言，而獨心生自誓：「我從今日不復殺生！」是名不殺生戒。

These and similar acts are called offenses of killing; refraining from them is called a precept. When someone undertakes the precept, forming the resolve and saying, “From this day onward I shall no longer kill living beings,” or, without moving the body or speaking, simply makes this vow in the mind, this is called the precept of abstaining from killing.

有人言：「是不殺生戒，或善或無記。」問曰：如阿毘曇中說「一切戒律儀皆善」，今何以言無記？

Some say, “This precept of abstaining from killing may be wholesome or karmically indeterminate.” Question: The Abhidharma says that all restraint through precepts is wholesome. Why is it now said to be indeterminate?

答曰：如《迦栴延子阿毘曇》中言一切善，如餘《阿毘曇》中言不殺戒，或善、或無記。何以故？若不殺戒常善者，持此戒人，應如得道人，常不墮惡道。以是故，或時應無記；無記無果報故，不生天上、人中。

Answer: Kātyāyanīputra's Abhidharma says that all such restraint is wholesome, whereas other Abhidharma texts say that the precept of abstaining from killing may be wholesome or indeterminate. Why? If this precept were always wholesome, those who upheld it should, like those who have attained the path, never fall into the unfortunate destinies. It must therefore sometimes be indeterminate. What is indeterminate has no karmic retribution and thus does not bring about birth among devas or humans.

問曰：不以戒無記故墮地獄，更有惡心生故墮地獄！

Question: They do not fall into hell because the precept is indeterminate; they fall into hell because other evil states of mind arise!

答曰：不殺生，得無量善法，作、無作，福常日夜生故；若作少罪，有限、有量。何以故隨有量而不隨無量？以是故，知不殺戒中，或有無記。

Answer: Abstaining from killing produces immeasurable wholesome qualities: through manifest and unmanifest activity, merit continues to arise day and night. If one commits a minor offense, that offense is limited and measurable. Why should one follow what is measurable rather than what is immeasurable? From this we know that the precept of abstaining from killing may sometimes be indeterminate.

復次，有人不從師受戒，而但心生自誓：「我從今日不復殺生！」如是不殺，或時無記。

Furthermore, some people do not receive the precept from a teacher but simply vow in their minds, “From this day onward I shall no longer kill living beings.” Abstaining from killing in this way may sometimes be indeterminate.

Abhidharma Classifications of the Precept

Chinese Source

問曰：是不殺戒何界繫？

English Translation

Question: To which realm does this precept of abstaining from killing belong?

答曰：如《迦栴延子阿毘曇》中言：「一切受戒律儀，皆欲界繫。」餘《阿毘曇》中言：「或欲界繫，或不繫。」以實言之，應有三種：「或欲界繫，或色界繫，或無漏。」殺生法雖欲界，不殺戒，應隨殺在欲界；但色界不殺、無漏不殺，遠遮故，是真不殺戒。

Answer: Kātyāyanīputra's Abhidharma says, “All restraint through received precepts belongs to the desire realm.” Other Abhidharma texts say, “It may belong to the desire realm or be unaffiliated with a realm.” In fact, there should be three possibilities: it may belong to the desire realm, belong to the form realm, or be free of outflows. Since killing is an activity of the desire realm, the precept of abstaining from killing would accordingly belong there. Yet abstention in the form realm and abstention free of outflows thoroughly prevent killing and are therefore truly precepts of abstaining from killing.

復次，有人不受戒，而從生已來，不好殺生，或善或無記，是名無記。是不殺生法，非心，非心數法，亦非心相應；或共心生，或不共心生。

Furthermore, some people have never received precepts but have disliked killing since birth. This may be wholesome or indeterminate; the latter is called indeterminate abstention. This abstention from killing is neither mind nor a mental factor, nor is it associated with mind. It may arise together with mind or independently of mind.

《迦栴延子阿毘曇》中言：「不殺生是身、口業，或作色、或無作色，或時隨心行、或不隨心行（丹注云：隨心行：定共戒；不隨心意：五戒），非先世業報。

Kātyāyanīputra's Abhidharma says: “Abstaining from killing is bodily and verbal karma, consisting of either manifest or unmanifest form. Sometimes it accompanies the activity of mind, and sometimes it does not. (The Dan annotation explains: what accompanies mind is the precept accompanying meditative concentration; what does not follow mental activity is the five precepts.) It is not the retribution of karma from a previous life.

二種修應修，二種證應證（丹注云：身證、慧證）。思惟斷，一切欲界最後得；見斷、時斷，凡夫、聖人所得。

“It should be cultivated through the two kinds of cultivation and realized through the two kinds of realization. (The Dan annotation explains: realization through the body and realization through wisdom.) It is abandoned through cultivation and is acquired last among all phenomena of the desire realm; it is abandoned through seeing, abandoned at a particular time, and acquired by both ordinary people and noble ones.

是色法，或可見、或不可見法，或有對法、或無對法，有報法，有果法，有漏法，有為法，有上法（丹注云：非極故有上），非相應因。如是等分別，是名不殺戒。」

“It is a material phenomenon, either visible or invisible, either resistant or nonresistant. It has karmic retribution and a result; it is subject to outflows, conditioned, and surpassable. (The Dan annotation explains: because it is not ultimate, it can be surpassed.) It is not an associated cause. Classified in these and other ways, it is called the precept of abstaining from killing.”

問曰：八直道中戒，亦不殺生，何以獨言「不殺生戒有報、有漏」？

Question: The ethical discipline within the noble eightfold path also includes abstaining from killing. Why say only that the precept of abstaining from killing has karmic retribution and outflows?

答曰：此中但說受戒律儀法，不說無漏戒律儀。

Answer: This discussion concerns only restraint through received precepts; it does not concern restraint through precepts free of outflows.

復次，餘《阿毘曇》中言：「不殺法常不逐心行，非身口業，不隨心業行；或有報，或無報；非心相應法，或有漏，或無漏。」是為異法，餘者皆同。

Furthermore, other Abhidharma texts say: “Abstention from killing never follows the activity of mind. It is neither bodily nor verbal karma, and does not follow mental karma. It may or may not have karmic retribution. It is not associated with mind and may be either subject to outflows or free of outflows.” These are the points of difference; the other classifications are the same.

復有言：「諸佛賢聖不戲論諸法（丹注云：種種異說名為『戲』也），現前眾生各各惜命，是故佛言莫奪他命；奪他命，世世受諸苦痛。」眾生有、無，後當說。

Still others say: “Buddhas and noble ones do not engage in conceptual proliferation concerning phenomena. (The Dan annotation explains: the various divergent explanations are called ‘proliferation.’) The living beings before us each cherish their lives. Thus the Buddha says not to take another's life; those who take life undergo suffering and pain in life after life.” Whether living beings exist or do not exist will be discussed later.

The Benefits of Abstaining from Killing

Chinese Source

問曰：人能以力勝人，并國、殺怨，或田獵皮肉，所濟處大；令不殺生，得何等利？

English Translation

Question: By force, people can overcome others, annex countries, kill enemies, or hunt for hides and meat, obtaining considerable benefits. What benefit do they gain by refraining from killing?

答曰：得無所畏，安樂無怖。我以無害於彼故，彼亦無害於我，以是故，無怖、無畏。好殺之人，雖復位極人王，亦不自安；如持戒之人，單行獨遊，無所畏難。

Answer: They gain fearlessness and peaceful happiness without terror. Because I do not harm others, others do not harm me; thus I am free of fear and dread. Someone who delights in killing feels insecure even as a king of people, whereas one who upholds the precepts can travel alone without fearing danger.

復次，好殺之人，有命之屬皆不喜見；若不好殺，一切眾生皆樂依附。

Furthermore, no living being is pleased to see someone who delights in killing. If one does not delight in killing, all living beings gladly seek one's protection.

復次，持戒之人，命欲終時其心安樂，無疑、無悔；若生天上、若在人中，常得長壽；是為得道因緣，乃至得佛，住壽無量。

Furthermore, when one who upholds the precepts approaches death, the mind is peaceful and happy, without doubt or regret. Whether born among devas or humans, such a person always enjoys a long life. This is a condition for attaining the path, ultimately reaching Buddhahood with an immeasurable lifespan.

復次，殺生之人，今世、後世受種種身、心苦痛；不殺之人，無此眾難，是為大利。

Furthermore, those who kill undergo various forms of bodily and mental suffering in this life and future lives. Those who abstain from killing are free of these troubles. This is a great benefit.

復次，行者思惟：「我自惜命、愛身，彼亦如是，與我何異？」以是之故，不應殺生。

Furthermore, the practitioner reflects: “I cherish my life and care for my body; others do likewise. How are they different from me?” For this reason, one should not kill living beings.

復次，若人殺生者，為善人所訶、怨家所嫉；負他命故，常有怖畏，為彼所憎；死時心悔，當墮地獄，若畜生中；若出為人，常當短命。

Furthermore, one who kills is reproached by good people and resented by enemies. Having taken another's life, one is constantly afraid and hated by the victim. At death one feels remorse and falls into hell or among animals. If subsequently born human, one will always be short-lived.

復次，假令後世無罪，不為善人所訶、怨家所嫉，尚不應故奪他命。何以故？善相之人所不應行，何況兩世有罪，弊惡果報！

Furthermore, even supposing there were no offense in a future life, and no reproach from good people or resentment from enemies, one still should not deliberately take another's life. Why? It is conduct unworthy of a good person. How much more so when it brings guilt in both lives and wretched karmic results!

復次，殺為罪中之重。何以故？人有死急，不惜重寶，但以活命為先。

Furthermore, killing is among the gravest offenses. Why? When faced with imminent death, people disregard precious treasures and put survival first.

譬如賈客入海採寶，垂出大海，其船卒壞，珍寶失盡，而自喜慶，舉手而言：「幾失大寶！」眾人怪言：「汝失財物，裸形得脫，云何喜言幾失大寶？」答言：「一切寶中，人命第一；人為命故求財，不為財故求命。」

For example, a merchant went to sea in search of jewels. Just as he was about to leave the ocean, his ship suddenly broke apart and all his treasures were lost. Yet he rejoiced, raised his hands, and said, “I nearly lost the greatest treasure!” Astonished, the others said, “You have lost your wealth and escaped naked. Why do you rejoice and say you nearly lost the greatest treasure?” He replied, “Of all treasures, human life is foremost. People seek wealth for the sake of life, not life for the sake of wealth.”

以是故，佛說十不善道中，殺罪最在初；五戒中亦最在初。若人種種修諸福德，而無不殺生戒，則無所益。何以故？雖在富貴處生，勢力豪強而無壽命，誰受此樂？以是故，知諸餘罪中，殺罪最重；諸功德中，不殺第一。

For this reason, among the ten unwholesome courses of action taught by the Buddha, killing is listed first; it is also first among the five precepts. If someone cultivates many kinds of merit but lacks the precept of abstaining from killing, these bring no benefit. Why? Even if born into wealth, honor, and great power, without a lifespan who could enjoy these pleasures? Thus we know that killing is the gravest among offenses, and abstaining from killing is foremost among meritorious qualities.

世間中惜命為第一。何以知之？一切世人，甘受刑罰刑殘考掠以護壽命。

In the world, cherishing life comes first. How do we know? People everywhere willingly endure punishment, mutilation, and torture to preserve their lives.

復次，若有人受戒，心生：「從今日不殺一切眾生。」是於無量眾生中，已以所愛重物施與，所得功德亦復無量。如佛說：「有五大施，何等五？一者、不殺生，是為最大施；不盜、不邪婬、不妄語、不飲酒，亦復如是。」

Furthermore, when someone undertakes the precept and resolves, “From this day onward I shall not kill any living being,” they have already given innumerable beings what those beings cherish most. The merit gained is likewise immeasurable. As the Buddha said: “There are five great gifts. What are the five? First, abstaining from killing is a supreme gift. Abstaining from stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech, and drinking intoxicants are likewise great gifts.”

復次，行慈三昧，其福無量，水、火不害，刀、兵不傷，一切惡毒所不能中；以五大施故，所得如是。

Furthermore, the merit of practicing the samādhi of loving-kindness is immeasurable. Water and fire do not harm one, blades and weapons do not injure one, and no deadly poison can strike one. Such results are gained through the five great gifts.

復次，三世十方中尊，佛為第一。如佛語難提迦優婆塞：「殺生有十罪，何等為十？一者、心常懷毒，世世不絕；二者、眾生憎惡，眼不喜見；三者、常懷惡念，思惟惡事；四者、眾生畏之，如見蛇虎；五者、睡時心怖，覺亦不安；六者、常有惡夢；七者、命終之時，狂怖惡死；八者、種短命業因緣；九者、身壞命終，墮泥梨中；十者、若出為人，常當短命。」

Furthermore, among those honored throughout the three times and ten directions, the Buddha is foremost. As the Buddha told the lay follower Nandika: “Killing has ten faults. What are the ten? First, the mind constantly harbors malice, continuing from life to life. Second, living beings detest the killer and dislike seeing them. Third, they constantly harbor evil thoughts and contemplate evil deeds. Fourth, beings fear them as they would a snake or tiger. Fifth, they are frightened in sleep and uneasy when awake. Sixth, they often have bad dreams. Seventh, at life's end they die a terrible death in frenzy and fear. Eighth, they plant karmic causes and conditions for a short life. Ninth, when the body breaks up and life ends, they fall into hell. Tenth, if subsequently born human, they will always be short-lived.”

復次，行者心念：「一切有命，乃至昆虫，皆自惜身；云何以衣服、飲食自為身故而殺眾生？」

Furthermore, the practitioner thinks: “All living creatures, down to the smallest insects, cherish their own bodies. How could I kill living beings for clothing and food to support my own body?”

復次，行者當學大人法，一切大人中，佛為最大。何以故？一切智慧成就，十力具足，能度眾生，常行慈愍，持不殺戒，自致得佛，亦教弟子行此慈愍。行者欲學大人行故，亦當不殺。

Furthermore, the practitioner should learn the way of great persons. Among all great persons, the Buddha is the greatest. Why? He has perfected all wisdom, possesses the ten powers, can liberate living beings, and always acts with compassion. By upholding the precept of abstaining from killing, he himself attained Buddhahood, and he also teaches his disciples to practice this compassion. Since the practitioner wishes to learn the conduct of great persons, they too should refrain from killing.

Preserving the Precept under Threat

Chinese Source

問曰：不侵我者，殺心可息；若為侵害、強奪、逼迫，是當云何？

English Translation

Question: If someone does not attack me, I can put aside the intention to kill. But what should I do if someone harms me, forcibly robs me, or oppresses me?

答曰：應當量其輕重。若人殺己，先自思惟：「全戒利重？全身為重？破戒為失？喪身為失？」如是思惟已，知持戒為重，全身為輕。若苟免全身，身何所得？是身名為老、病、死藪，必當壞敗！若為持戒失身，其利甚重。

Answer: One should weigh the relative importance of the alternatives. If someone seeks to kill one, one should first reflect: “Is preserving the precept more beneficial, or preserving the body? Is breaking the precept the greater loss, or losing the body?” Having reflected in this way, one recognizes that upholding the precept is more important and preserving the body less important. If one merely escapes and preserves the body, what does the body gain? This body is a gathering place of aging, illness, and death; it must inevitably decay and perish. If one loses the body for the sake of upholding the precept, the benefit is very great.

又復思惟：「我前後失身，世世無數，或作惡賊、禽獸之身，但為財利諸不善事；今乃得為持淨戒故，不惜此身，捨命持戒，勝於毀禁全身百千萬倍，不可為喻。如是定心，應當捨身以護淨戒。」

One further reflects: “In the past I have lost my body countless times, life after life, sometimes as a bandit or an animal, merely for wealth, gain, and unwholesome deeds. Now, having the opportunity to uphold pure precepts, I will not cling to this body. Giving up life while preserving the precepts surpasses breaking them to preserve the body a hundred thousand myriad times, beyond comparison.” With this firm resolve, one would relinquish the body to protect pure precepts.

如一須陀洹人，生屠殺家；年向成人，應當修其家業而不肯殺生。父母與刀并一口羊，閉著屋中而語之言：「若不殺羊，不令汝出、得見日月、生活飲食！」

For example, a stream-enterer was born into a family of slaughterers. As he approached adulthood, he was expected to take up the family occupation, but he refused to kill living beings. His parents gave him a knife and a sheep, shut him inside a room, and told him, “Unless you kill the sheep, we will not let you out to see the sun and moon or have food and drink to sustain you!”

兒自思惟言：「我若殺此一羊，便當終為此業，豈以身故為此大罪？」便以刀自殺。父母開戶，見羊在一面立，兒已命絕。當自殺時，即生天上。

The son reflected: “If I kill this one sheep, I will end up doing this for the rest of my life. How could I commit this grave offense for the sake of my body?” He then killed himself with the knife. When his parents opened the door, they saw the sheep standing to one side and their son already dead. At the moment of his death, he was born in heaven.

若如此者，是為不惜壽命，全護淨戒。如是等義，是名不殺生戒。

Such a person is described as not clinging to life and as fully safeguarding pure precepts. Through these and similar explanations, this is called the precept of abstaining from killing.
