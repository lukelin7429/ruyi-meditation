Great Beings

Fascicle 5 explains the meaning of mahāsattva: a great being whose courageous mind takes responsibility for the liberation of many beings.

What Mahāsattva Means

Chinese Source

大智度論卷第五

釋初品中摩訶薩埵

Ru-Yi Translation

Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra, Fascicle Five

Explanation of Mahāsattva in the First Chapter

﹝經﹞

摩訶薩埵。

Sutra:

Mahāsattvas.

﹝論﹞

問曰：云何名摩訶薩埵？

答曰：摩訶名大，薩埵名眾生，或名勇心，此人心能為大事，不退不還大勇心故，名為摩訶薩埵。

Treatise:

Question: Why are they called mahāsattvas?

Answer: Mahā means great. Sattva means sentient being, or it may mean courageous mind. Because this person's mind is able to undertake great matters, and because it is a greatly courageous mind that does not retreat and does not turn back, such a person is called a mahāsattva.

復次，摩訶薩埵者，於多眾生中最為上首故，名為摩訶薩埵。

復次，多眾生中起大慈大悲，成立大乘，能行大道，得最大處故，名摩訶薩埵。

Furthermore, a mahāsattva is foremost among many sentient beings. For this reason, that person is called a mahāsattva.

Furthermore, among many sentient beings, that person gives rise to great loving-kindness and great compassion, establishes the Great Vehicle, is able to practice the great path, and attains the greatest state. For this reason, that person is called a mahāsattva.

復次，大人相成就故，名摩訶薩埵。摩訶薩埵相者，如讚佛偈中說：

唯佛一人獨第一，三界父母一切智，於一切等無與等，稽首世尊希有比！

凡人行惠為己利，求報以財而給施，佛大慈仁無此事，怨親憎愛以等利！

Furthermore, because such a person has accomplished the marks of a great person, that person is called a mahāsattva. As for the marks of a mahāsattva, they are described in verses praising the Buddha:

Only the Buddha alone is first;
parent of the three realms, all-knowing one.
Equal toward all, with none equal to him,
I bow to the Blessed One, rarely matched.

Ordinary people practice giving for their own benefit,
seeking reward when they give wealth.
The Buddha's great kindness and compassion have no such thing:
enemy and friend, hated and loved, all equally benefit.

復次，必能說法破一切眾生及己身大邪見、大愛慢、大我心等諸煩惱故，名為摩訶薩埵。

Furthermore, such a person is certainly able to teach the Dharma and break through the great wrong views, great craving and pride, great self-centered mind, and other afflictions of all sentient beings and of oneself. For this reason, that person is called a mahāsattva.

A Great Mind Like the Ocean

Chinese Source

復次，眾生如大海，無初、無中、無後；有明知算師，於無量歲計算，不能盡竟。如佛語無盡意菩薩：譬如十方一切世界，乃至虛空邊際，合為一水，令無數無量眾生，共持一髮取一渧而去，更有無央數眾生，如前共持一髮取一渧而去，如是令彼大水悉盡無餘，眾生故不盡。以是眾生等無邊、無量、不可數、不可思議，盡能救濟，令離苦惱，著於無為安隱樂中。有此大心，欲度多眾生故，名摩訶薩埵。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, sentient beings are like a great ocean: they have no beginning, no middle, and no end. Even a skilled calculator with clear knowledge could count for immeasurable years and still not reach the end. As the Buddha told Bodhisattva Akṣayamati: suppose all worlds in the ten directions, reaching to the limit of empty space, were combined into one body of water. Suppose numberless, immeasurable sentient beings together held one hair and took away one drop, and then countless more sentient beings did the same, each group holding one hair and taking away one drop. Even if they caused all that vast water to be exhausted without remainder, sentient beings still would not be exhausted. Because sentient beings are boundless, immeasurable, incalculable, and inconceivable, one who can rescue and relieve them all, free them from suffering, and place them in the secure peace of the unconditioned has this great mind. Because such a person wishes to liberate many sentient beings, that person is called a mahāsattva.

如不可思議經中，漚舍那優婆夷語須達那菩薩言：諸菩薩摩訶薩輩，不為度一人故發，阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，亦非為二、三乃至十人故，非百、非千、非萬、非十萬、非百萬，非一億十百千萬乃至億億。

As the Inconceivable Sutra says, the laywoman Āśā said to Bodhisattva Sudhana: Bodhisattva mahāsattvas do not give rise to the mind of unsurpassed complete awakening merely for the sake of liberating one person. Nor do they do so for two, three, or even ten people; not for a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand, one hundred thousand, one million, one hundred million, or even for numbers multiplied up to hundreds of thousands of millions upon millions.

They do not give rise to this mind for ayuta koṭis of sentient beings, nor for nayuta koṭis, nor for any of the vast numerical terms that continue through bimbara, asaṃkhyeya, immeasurable, boundless, unequaled, innumerable, incalculable, inconceivable, and inexpressible, even when those numbers are multiplied by themselves. Their resolve is not limited by any count that can be named.

非為一國土微塵等眾生故發心，非為二、三至十百千萬億千萬億阿由他、那由他，乃至不可說不可說國土微塵等眾生故發心。非為一閻浮提微塵等眾生故發心，非為拘陀尼，鬱怛羅越，弗婆提微塵等眾生故發心。非為小千世界、中千世界、大千世界微塵等眾生故發心，非為二、三至十百千萬億阿由他、那由他、乃至不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等眾生故發心。

They do not give rise to this mind merely for sentient beings as numerous as the dust motes in one land, nor for beings as numerous as the dust motes in two, three, and so on up to inexpressibly many lands. They do not do so merely for beings as numerous as the dust motes in one Jambudvīpa, nor for the dust motes in Godānīya, Uttarakuru, or Pūrvavideha. They do not do so merely for beings as numerous as the dust motes in a small-thousand world, a middle-thousand world, or a great-thousand world, nor for beings as numerous as the dust motes in two, three, and so on up to inexpressibly many three-thousand-great-thousand world-systems.

非為供養供給一佛故發心，乃至非為供養供給不可說不可說諸佛故發心；非為供養供給一國土微塵等諸佛故發心，乃至非為供養供給不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等諸佛故發心。非為淨一佛土故發心，乃至非為淨不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等佛土故發心。非為受持一佛法故發心，乃至非為受持不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等佛法故發心。

非為令一三千大千世界中佛種不斷故發心，乃至非為令不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等三千大千世界中佛種不斷故發心。非為分別知一佛願故發心，乃至非為分別知不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等佛願故發心。非為莊嚴一佛土故發心，乃至非為莊嚴不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等佛土故發心。非為分別知一佛會弟子眾故發心，乃至非為分別知不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等佛會弟子眾故發心。非為持一佛法輪故發心，乃至非為持不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等佛法輪故發心。非為知一人諸心故，非為知一人諸根故，非為知一三千大千世界中諸劫次第相續故，非為分別斷一人諸煩惱故發心；乃至非為分別斷不可說不可說三千大千世界微塵等人諸煩惱故發心。

They do not give rise to this mind merely to make offerings and provide support to one Buddha, nor even to inexpressibly many Buddhas. They do not do so merely to make offerings and provide support to Buddhas as numerous as the dust motes in one land, nor even to Buddhas as numerous as the dust motes in inexpressibly many three-thousand-great-thousand world-systems. They do not do so merely to purify one Buddha land, nor even to purify Buddha lands as numerous as those dust motes. They do not do so merely to receive and uphold the Dharma of one Buddha, nor even to receive and uphold the Dharma of Buddhas as numerous as those dust motes.

They do not give rise to this mind merely to keep the Buddha lineage from being cut off in one three-thousand-great-thousand world-system, nor even in three-thousand-great-thousand world-systems as numerous as inexpressibly many dust motes. They do not do so merely to distinguish and know the vow of one Buddha, nor even the vows of Buddhas as numerous as those dust motes. They do not do so merely to adorn one Buddha land, nor even Buddha lands as numerous as those dust motes. They do not do so merely to distinguish and know the assembly of disciples of one Buddha, nor even the assemblies of Buddhas as numerous as those dust motes. They do not do so merely to uphold the Dharma wheel of one Buddha, nor even the Dharma wheels of Buddhas as numerous as those dust motes. They do not give rise to this mind merely to know the minds of one person, the faculties of one person, or the successive order of kalpas in one three-thousand-great-thousand world-system, nor merely to distinguish and cut off the afflictions of one person, nor even the afflictions of people as numerous as inexpressibly many dust motes in three-thousand-great-thousand world-systems.

The Positive Vow of the Mahāsattva

Chinese Source

是諸菩薩摩訶薩願言：盡教化一切十方眾生，盡供養供給一切十方諸佛，願令一切十方諸佛土清淨，心堅受持一切十方諸佛法，分別知一切諸佛土故，盡知一切諸佛弟子眾故；分別知一切眾生諸心故，知斷一切眾生諸煩惱故，盡知一切眾生諸根故，諸菩薩發心住阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。如是等十門為首，乃至百千萬億阿僧祇門，是為道法門，菩薩應知、應入！略說如是諸菩薩實道。一切諸法皆入皆知，智慧知故，一切佛土菩薩道中莊嚴故。

Ru-Yi Translation

These bodhisattva mahāsattvas make this vow: "We will fully teach and transform all sentient beings in the ten directions. We will fully make offerings and provide support to all Buddhas in the ten directions. We vow to make all Buddha lands in the ten directions pure. With firm minds, we will receive and uphold the Dharma of all Buddhas in the ten directions. We will distinguish and know all Buddha lands, fully know all the assemblies of disciples of all Buddhas, distinguish and know the minds of all sentient beings, know how to cut off all sentient beings' afflictions, and fully know the faculties of all sentient beings." In this way, bodhisattvas give rise to the mind and abide in unsurpassed complete awakening. Taking these ten gateways as the beginning, extending even to hundreds of thousands of millions of asaṃkhyeyas of gateways, these are gateways of the path and Dharma that bodhisattvas should know and should enter. In brief, this is the true path of bodhisattvas. They enter and know all dharmas because they know them with wisdom, and because they adorn the bodhisattva path in all Buddha lands.

漚舍那言：善男子！我願如是：自有世界已來一切眾生盡清淨，一切煩惱悉斷。須達那言：是何解脫？漚舍那答言：是名無憂安隱幢。我知此一解脫門，不知諸菩薩大心如大海水，一切諸佛法能持能受。

諸菩薩心不動，如須彌山；諸菩薩如藥王，能除一切諸煩惱；諸菩薩如日，能除一切暗；諸菩薩如地，能含受一切眾生；諸菩薩如風，能益一切眾生；諸菩薩如火，能燒一切外道諸煩惱；諸菩薩如雲，能雨法水；諸菩薩如月，福德光明能照一切；諸菩薩如釋提桓因，守護一切眾生。是菩薩道法甚深，我云何能盡知！以是諸菩薩生大願，欲得大事，欲至大處故，名摩訶薩埵。

Āśā said, "Good man, my vow is this: from the beginning of the world onward, may all sentient beings become completely pure and may all afflictions be completely cut off." Sudhana asked, "What liberation is this?" Āśā answered, "It is called the Banner of Sorrowless Security. I know this one gateway of liberation, but I do not know the great mind of the bodhisattvas, which is like the waters of a great ocean and is able to hold and receive all the Dharmas of all Buddhas.

"The minds of bodhisattvas are unmoving, like Mount Sumeru. Bodhisattvas are like the king of medicines, able to remove all afflictions. Bodhisattvas are like the sun, able to dispel all darkness. Bodhisattvas are like the earth, able to contain and receive all sentient beings. Bodhisattvas are like the wind, able to benefit all sentient beings. Bodhisattvas are like fire, able to burn away the afflictions of all non-Buddhist paths. Bodhisattvas are like clouds, able to rain down Dharma water. Bodhisattvas are like the moon, whose light of merit illuminates everything. Bodhisattvas are like Śakra Devānām Indra, protecting all sentient beings. The Dharma of the bodhisattva path is extremely profound; how could I know it completely?" Because these bodhisattvas give rise to great vows, wish to accomplish great matters, and wish to reach the great place, they are called mahāsattvas.

復次，是般若波羅蜜經中，摩訶薩埵相，佛自說如是如是相，是摩訶薩埵相。舍利弗、須菩提、富樓那等諸大弟子各各說。彼品，此中應廣說。

Furthermore, in this Prajñāpāramitā Sutra, the Buddha himself explains the marks of a mahāsattva, saying that such-and-such marks are the marks of a mahāsattva. Śāriputra, Subhūti, Pūrṇa, and other great disciples each speak of them. This should be explained broadly in that chapter.

Bodhisattva Merits: Dhāraṇī, Samādhi, and Patience

釋初品中菩薩功德

﹝經﹞

皆得陀羅尼及諸三昧，行空、無相、無作，已得等忍。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explanation of the Bodhisattvas' Merits in the First Chapter

Sutra:

They had all attained dhāraṇīs and many samādhis. They practiced emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness, and had already attained the patience of equality.

﹝論﹞

問曰：何以故以此三事次第讚菩薩摩訶薩？答曰：欲出諸菩薩實功德故，應讚則讚，應信則信；以一切眾生所不能信甚深清淨法讚菩薩。

復次，先說菩薩摩訶薩名字，未說所以為菩薩摩訶薩，以得諸陀羅尼，三昧及忍等諸功德故，名為菩薩摩訶薩。

Treatise:

Question: Why are bodhisattva mahāsattvas praised in sequence by these three matters?

Answer: Because the real merits of bodhisattvas are being brought out. What should be praised is praised; what should be trusted is trusted. Bodhisattvas are praised by means of profound and pure dharmas that ordinary sentient beings cannot trust.

Furthermore, the name bodhisattva mahāsattva was first stated, but the reason why they are bodhisattva mahāsattvas had not yet been explained. Because they have attained dhāraṇīs, samādhis, patience, and other merits, they are called bodhisattva mahāsattvas.

問曰：已知次第義，何以名陀羅尼？云何陀羅尼？

答曰：陀羅尼，秦言能持，或言能遮。能持者，集種種善法，能持令不散不失。譬如完器盛水，水不漏散。能遮者，惡不善根心生，能遮令不生；若欲作惡罪，持令不作，是名陀羅尼。是陀羅尼，或心相應，或心不相應；或有漏，或無漏；無色不可見無對；一持、一入、一陰攝；（法持法入行陰）九智知；（除盡智）一識識；（一意識）阿毗曇法陀羅尼義如是。

Question: The meaning of the sequence is now understood. Why is it called dhāraṇī? What is a dhāraṇī?

Answer: Dhāraṇī means "able to hold," or it may mean "able to restrain." "Able to hold" means that one gathers many kinds of wholesome dharmas and can hold them so that they do not scatter or become lost. It is like an intact vessel holding water: the water does not leak away or scatter. "Able to restrain" means that when evil and unwholesome roots are about to arise in the mind, one can restrain them so that they do not arise; and when one is about to commit an evil offense, one holds oneself back so that one does not do it. This is called dhāraṇī. This dhāraṇī may be associated with mind or not associated with mind; it may be outflowing or free from outflows; it is formless, invisible, and without resistance. It is included in one basis of retention, one sense sphere, and one aggregate: the dharma basis of retention, the dharma sense sphere, and the formation aggregate. It is known by nine knowledges, excluding the knowledge of destruction, and cognized by one consciousness, namely mental consciousness. This is the Abhidharma explanation of dhāraṇī.

復次，得陀羅尼菩薩，一切所聞法，以念力故，能持不失。

復次，是陀羅尼法，常逐菩薩，譬如間日瘧病；是陀羅尼不離菩薩，譬如鬼著；是陀羅尼常隨菩薩，如善不善律儀。

復次，是陀羅尼持菩薩，不令墮二地坑。譬如慈父愛子，子欲墮坑，持令不墮。

復次，菩薩得陀羅尼力故，一切魔王、魔民、魔人無能動，無能破，無能勝。譬如須彌山，凡人口吹不能令動。

Furthermore, bodhisattvas who attain dhāraṇī can retain without losing all the Dharmas they have heard, because of the power of mindfulness.

Furthermore, this dhāraṇī Dharma constantly follows bodhisattvas, like a fever that returns every other day. This dhāraṇī does not leave bodhisattvas, like a spirit that has taken hold of someone. This dhāraṇī constantly follows bodhisattvas, like wholesome and unwholesome disciplines.

Furthermore, this dhāraṇī holds bodhisattvas and does not let them fall into the two pits of the two grounds. It is like a loving father who loves his child: when the child is about to fall into a pit, he holds the child back so that the child does not fall.

Furthermore, because bodhisattvas attain the power of dhāraṇī, all Māra kings, Māra subjects, and Māra beings are unable to move them, break them, or overcome them. It is like Mount Sumeru: the breath of ordinary people cannot make it move.

Kinds of Dhāraṇī

Chinese Source

問曰：是陀羅尼有幾種？

答曰：是陀羅尼甚多。有聞持陀羅尼，得是陀羅尼者，一切語言諸法，耳所聞者，皆不忘失。

復有分別知陀羅尼，得是陀羅尼者，諸眾生、諸法，大小好醜，分別悉知。如偈說：

諸象馬金，木石諸衣，男女及水，種種不同。

諸物名一，貴賤理殊，得此總持，悉能分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: How many kinds of dhāraṇī are there?

Answer: There are very many dhāraṇīs. There is the dhāraṇī of retaining what is heard. Those who attain this dhāraṇī do not forget or lose any language or Dharma they hear with the ear.

There is also the dhāraṇī of discriminating knowledge. Those who attain this dhāraṇī fully know, through discrimination, sentient beings and dharmas, whether great or small, good or bad. As a verse says:

Elephants, horses, gold,
wood, stone, clothing,
men, women, and water:
all are different in many ways.

Things may share one name,
yet noble and base differ in principle.
Those who attain this dhāraṇī
can distinguish them all.

復有入音聲陀羅尼，菩薩得此陀羅尼者，聞一切語言音，不喜不瞋。若一切眾生如恆河沙等劫，惡言罵詈，心不憎恨。

問曰：菩薩諸漏未盡，云何能如恆河沙等劫忍此諸惡？

答曰：我先言：得此陀羅尼力故能爾。

There is also the dhāraṇī of entering sounds. Bodhisattvas who attain this dhāraṇī hear all speech and sounds without delight and without anger. Even if all sentient beings, for kalpas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, used harsh words to revile them, their minds would not hate or resent them.

Question: Since bodhisattvas have not yet exhausted all outflows, how can they endure these evils for kalpas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges?

Answer: I have already said that they can do this through the power of attaining this dhāraṇī.

復次，是菩薩雖未盡漏，大智利根，能思惟除遣瞋心。作是念：若耳根不到聲邊，惡聲著誰？又如罵聲，聞便直過，若不分別，誰當瞋者？凡人心著吾我，分別是非而生恚恨。

復次，若人能知語言隨生隨滅，前後不俱，則無瞋恚。亦知諸法內無有主，誰罵誰瞋？若有人聞殊方異語，此言為好，彼以為惡，好惡無定，雖罵不瞋。若有人知語聲無定，則無瞋喜。

Furthermore, although these bodhisattvas have not yet exhausted the outflows, they have great wisdom and sharp faculties, and can contemplate in order to remove and send away anger. They think: If the ear faculty does not reach the place of sound, then to whom does the bad sound attach? Again, a sound of insult is simply heard and immediately passes. If one does not discriminate it, who is there to become angry? Ordinary people cling in their minds to "I" and "mine," discriminate right and wrong, and give rise to resentment and hatred.

Furthermore, if people can know that speech arises and ceases moment by moment, and that what comes before and what comes after do not exist together, then there is no anger. They also know that within dharmas there is no owner; who insults, and who becomes angry? If someone hears a language from another region, one person may consider these words good while another considers them bad. Since good and bad are not fixed, even when insulted one does not become angry. If people know that verbal sounds are not fixed, then there is neither anger nor delight.

如親愛罵之，雖罵不恨；非親惡言，聞則生恚。如遭風雨，則入舍持蓋；如地有刺，則著靴鞋；大寒然火，熱時求水；如是諸患，但求遮法而不瞋之。罵詈諸惡，亦復如是，但以慈悲息此諸惡，不生瞋心。

復次，菩薩知諸法不生不滅，其性皆空。若人瞋恚罵詈，若打若殺，如夢如化，誰瞋誰罵？

It is like someone dear and beloved insulting you: although there is insult, you do not resent it. But when someone who is not dear speaks harshly, hearing it gives rise to anger. If one meets wind and rain, one enters a house or holds up an umbrella. If the ground has thorns, one puts on boots or shoes. In great cold one lights a fire; in heat one seeks water. With such troubles, one simply seeks a way to block them and does not become angry at them. Insults, abuse, and other evils are also like this: one simply uses loving-kindness and compassion to quiet these evils and does not give rise to anger.

Furthermore, bodhisattvas know that all dharmas are neither born nor destroyed, and that their nature is empty. If people become angry, insult them, strike them, or kill them, these are like dreams and like illusions. Who becomes angry? Who insults?

復次，若有人如恆河沙等劫眾生，讚歎供養，衣食、臥具、醫藥、華香、纓絡，得忍菩薩其心不動，不喜不著。

問曰：已知菩薩種種不瞋因緣，未知實讚功德而亦不喜？

答曰：知種種供養恭敬，是皆無常；今有因緣故，來讚歎供養；後更有異因緣則瞋恚，若打若殺，是故不喜。

Furthermore, even if someone, together with sentient beings for kalpas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, praised and made offerings of clothing, food, bedding, medicine, flowers, incense, and necklaces, bodhisattvas who have attained patience would remain unmoved in mind, neither delighted nor attached.

Question: We already know the many reasons why bodhisattvas do not become angry. But if someone truly praises their merits, why are they still not delighted?

Answer: They know that all kinds of offerings and reverence are impermanent. Because certain causes and conditions are present now, people come to praise and make offerings; later, when different causes and conditions arise, those people may become angry, strike them, or kill them. For this reason, bodhisattvas are not delighted.

復次，以我有功德智慧故，來讚歎供養，是為讚歎功德，非讚我也，我何以喜？

復次，是人自求果報故，於我所作因緣，供養我作功德。譬如人種榖，溉灌修理，地亦不喜。

復次，若人供養我，我若喜受者，我福則薄，於他亦少，是故不喜。

復次，菩薩觀一切法如夢如響，誰讚誰喜？我於三界中未得脫，諸漏未盡，未得佛道，云何得讚而喜？若應喜者，唯佛一人；何以故？一切功德都已滿故。是故菩薩得種種讚歎、供養、供給，心不生喜。如是等相，名為入音聲陀羅尼。

Furthermore, if they come to praise and make offerings because I have merit and wisdom, this is praising merit, not praising me. Why should I be delighted?

Furthermore, these people seek karmic fruit for themselves. Through the causes and conditions they create toward me, they make offerings to me and cultivate merit. It is like people planting grain, irrigating it, and tending it; the earth is not delighted.

Furthermore, if people make offerings to me and I receive them with delight, my merit becomes slight and the benefit to others is also small. For this reason, I am not delighted.

Furthermore, bodhisattvas contemplate all dharmas as like dreams and like echoes. Who praises, and who is delighted? I have not yet been liberated from the three realms, have not exhausted the outflows, and have not attained the Buddha path. How could I be delighted at receiving praise? If anyone should be delighted, it would be the Buddha alone. Why? Because all his merits have been completely fulfilled. Therefore, when bodhisattvas receive all kinds of praise, offerings, and support, their minds do not give rise to delight. Such characteristics are called the dhāraṇī of entering sounds.

復有名寂滅陀羅尼，無邊旋陀羅尼，隨地觀陀羅尼，威德陀羅尼，華嚴陀羅尼，淨音陀羅尼，虛空藏陀羅尼，海藏陀羅尼，分別諸法地陀羅尼，明諸法義陀羅尼。如是等略說五百陀羅尼門，若廣說則無量。以是故，言諸菩薩皆得陀羅尼。

There are also dhāraṇīs called the dhāraṇī of quiescent cessation, the dhāraṇī of boundless turning, the dhāraṇī of contemplation according to the stages, the dhāraṇī of majestic virtue, the dhāraṇī of flower adornment, the dhāraṇī of pure sound, the dhāraṇī of the treasury of empty space, the dhāraṇī of the ocean treasury, the dhāraṇī of distinguishing the grounds of all dharmas, and the dhāraṇī of clarifying the meanings of all dharmas. In brief, there are five hundred gateways of dhāraṇī like these; if explained broadly, they are immeasurable. For this reason, the sutra says that all the bodhisattvas had attained dhāraṇī.

Three Samādhis

Chinese Source

諸三昧者，三三昧：空、無作、無相。有人言：觀五陰無我、無我所，是名為空；住是空三昧，不為後世故起三毒，是名無作；緣離十相法：五塵、男、女、生、住、滅故，是名無相。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the samādhis, there are three samādhis: emptiness, wishlessness, and signlessness. Some say: contemplating the five aggregates as without self and without what belongs to self is called emptiness. Abiding in this samādhi of emptiness, one does not give rise to the three poisons for the sake of a future life; this is called wishlessness. Taking as object dharmas that are free from the ten signs: the five sense objects, male, female, arising, abiding, and ceasing, is called signlessness.

有人言：住是三昧中，知一切諸法實相，所謂畢竟空，是名空三昧。知是空已無作，云何無作？不觀諸法若空若不空，若有若無等。如佛說法句中偈：

見有則恐怖，見無亦恐怖；是故不著有，亦復不著無。

是名無作三昧。

Others say: abiding in this samādhi, one knows the true mark of all dharmas, namely ultimate emptiness; this is called the samādhi of emptiness. Having known this emptiness, one is wishless. What is wishlessness? One does not contemplate dharmas as either empty or not empty, either existent or nonexistent, and so on. As the Buddha says in a verse from the Dhammapada:

Seeing existence, there is fear;
seeing nonexistence, there is also fear.
Therefore one does not cling to existence,
and also does not cling to nonexistence.

This is called the samādhi of wishlessness.

云何無相三昧？一切法無有相，一切法不受不著，是名無相三昧。如偈說：

言語已息，心行亦滅，不生不滅，如涅槃相。

復次，十八空，是名空三昧；種種（五道：生有、本有、死有、中有、業）有中心不求，是名無作三昧；一切諸相破壞不憶念，是名無相三昧。

What is the samādhi of signlessness? All dharmas have no signs. One does not receive or attach to any dharma. This is called the samādhi of signlessness. As a verse says:

Speech has already ceased;
mental activities have also stopped.
Not arising and not ceasing:
this is like the mark of nirvāṇa.

Furthermore, the eighteen emptinesses are called the samādhi of emptiness. In the many kinds of existence: the five destinies, birth-existence, original-existence, death-existence, intermediate-existence, and karma, the mind does not seek anything; this is called the samādhi of wishlessness. All signs are broken down and not remembered; this is called the samādhi of signlessness.

問曰：有種種禪定法，何以故獨稱此三三昧？

答曰：是三三昧中，思惟近涅槃故，令人心不高不下，平等不動，餘處不爾。以是故，獨稱是三三昧。餘定中或愛多，或慢多，或見多；是三三昧中第一實義實利，能得涅槃門。以是故，諸禪定法中，以是三定法為三解脫門。亦名為三三昧，是三三昧實三昧故，餘定亦得名定。

Question: There are many kinds of dhyāna and concentration practices. Why are only these three called the three samādhis?

Answer: In these three samādhis, because the contemplation is close to nirvāṇa, they cause the mind to be neither high nor low, equal and unmoving. This is not so elsewhere. For this reason, only these are called the three samādhis. In other concentrations, there may be much craving, much pride, or many views. Within these three samādhis, there is the foremost true meaning and true benefit, and they can obtain the gateways to nirvāṇa. Therefore, among all dhyāna and concentration practices, these three concentrations are the three gates of liberation. They are also called the three samādhis. Because these three samādhis are true samādhis, other concentrations may also be called concentrations.

復次，除四根本禪，從未到地乃至有頂地，名為定，亦名三昧，非禪；四禪亦名定，亦名禪，亦名三昧；諸餘定亦名定，亦名三昧：如四無量、四辯、六通、八背捨、八勝處、九次第定、十一切處等諸定法。

Furthermore, apart from the four root dhyānas, from the access stage up to the peak of existence, these are called concentrations and are also called samādhis, but they are not called dhyānas. The four dhyānas are also called concentrations, also called dhyānas, and also called samādhis. Other concentrations are also called concentrations and also called samādhis, such as the four immeasurables, the four kinds of unobstructed eloquence, the six superknowledges, the eight liberations, the eight spheres of mastery, the nine successive concentrations, the ten universal bases, and other concentration dharmas.

復有人言：一切三昧法有二十三種。有言：六十五種。有言：五百種。摩訶衍最大故，無量三昧，所謂遍法性莊嚴三昧，能照一切三世法三昧，不分別知觀法性底三昧，入無底佛法三昧，如虛空無底無邊照三昧，如來力行觀三昧，佛無畏莊嚴力嚬呻三昧，法性門旋藏三昧，一切世界無礙疾遍月三昧，遍莊嚴法雲光三昧；菩薩得如是等無量三昧。

復次，般若波羅蜜摩訶衍義品中，略說則有一百八三昧：初名首楞嚴三昧，乃至虛空、不著不染三昧；廣說則無量三昧。以是故，說諸菩薩得諸三昧，行空、無相、無作者。

Furthermore, some say that all samādhi dharmas number twenty-three kinds. Some say sixty-five kinds. Some say five hundred kinds. Because the Mahāyāna is greatest, its samādhis are immeasurable: the samādhi of adorning the whole Dharma-nature, the samādhi that can illuminate all dharmas of the three times, the samādhi of non-discriminating knowledge that contemplates the depth of Dharma-nature, the samādhi that enters the bottomless Buddha Dharma, the samādhi that illuminates like empty space, bottomless and boundless, the samādhi of contemplating the Tathāgata's power and practice, the samādhi of the Buddha's fearless adornment and powerful lion's roar, the samādhi of the turning treasury of the Dharma-nature gate, the samādhi of the unobstructed and swift moon pervading all worlds, and the samādhi of light from Dharma clouds adorning everywhere. Bodhisattvas attain immeasurable samādhis such as these.

Furthermore, in the Mahāyāna chapter of the Prajñāpāramitā, briefly stated, there are one hundred and eight samādhis, beginning with the Śūraṅgama Samādhi and continuing up to the samādhi of empty space, nonattachment, and undefilement. Broadly explained, they are immeasurable. For this reason, the sutra says that the bodhisattvas attained many samādhis and practiced emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness.

Patience of Equality

Chinese Source

問曰：前言菩薩得諸三昧，何以故復言行空、無相、無作？

答曰：前說三昧名，未說相，今欲說相，是故言行空、無作、無相。若有人行空、無相、無作，是名得實相三昧。如偈說：

若持戒清淨，是名實比丘；若有能觀空，是名得三昧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Earlier it said that bodhisattvas attain many samādhis. Why does it again say that they practice emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness?

Answer: Earlier, the names of the samādhis were given, but their marks were not explained. Now the marks are being explained. Therefore it says that they practice emptiness, wishlessness, and signlessness. If someone practices emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness, this is called attaining the samādhi of the true mark. As a verse says:

If one keeps the precepts purely,
this is called a true bhikṣu.
If one can contemplate emptiness,
this is called attaining samādhi.

If one can be diligent,
this is called a person practicing the path.
If one attains nirvāṇa,
this is called true happiness.

已得等忍者，

問曰：云何等？云何忍？

答曰：有二種等：眾生等，法等。忍亦二種：眾生忍，法忍。云何眾生等？一切眾生中，等心、等念、等愛、等利，是名眾生等。

As for "had already attained the patience of equality":

Question: What is equality? What is patience?

Answer: There are two kinds of equality: equality regarding sentient beings and equality regarding dharmas. Patience is also of two kinds: patience regarding sentient beings and patience regarding dharmas. What is equality regarding sentient beings? Toward all sentient beings, one has an equal mind, equal mindfulness, equal love, and equal benefit. This is called equality regarding sentient beings.

問曰：慈悲力故，於一切眾生中應等念，不應等觀。何以故？菩薩行實道，不顛倒，如法相；云何於善人、不善人、大人、小人及畜生，一等觀？不善人中實有不善相，善人中實有善相，大人、小人，人及畜生亦爾。如牛相牛中住，馬相馬中住，牛相非馬中，馬相非牛中，馬不作牛故。眾生各各相，云何一等觀而不墮顛倒？

Question: Because of the power of loving-kindness and compassion, one should have equal mindfulness toward all sentient beings, but one should not contemplate them as equal. Why? Bodhisattvas practice the true path, are not inverted, and accord with the marks of dharmas. How, then, can they contemplate good people, bad people, great people, small people, and animals as equal? Among bad people there truly are bad marks, and among good people there truly are good marks. Great and small, humans and animals, are also like this. The mark of an ox abides in an ox, and the mark of a horse abides in a horse. The mark of an ox is not in a horse, and the mark of a horse is not in an ox, because a horse does not become an ox. Sentient beings each have their own marks. How can one contemplate them as equal and not fall into inversion?

答曰：若善相不善相是實，菩薩應墮顛倒；何以故？破諸法故。以諸法非實善相、非實不善相，非多相、非少相，非人、非畜生，非一、非異；以是故，汝難非也。如說諸法相偈：

不生不滅，不斷不常，不一不異，不去不來；

因緣生法，滅諸戲論，佛能說是，我今當說。

Answer: If the marks of good and bad were real, then bodhisattvas would fall into inversion. Why? Because this would destroy all dharmas. Since dharmas are not truly marked as good, not truly marked as bad, not marked as many, not marked as few, not human, not animal, not one, and not different, your challenge is incorrect. As a verse on the marks of dharmas says:

Not born and not destroyed,
not cut off and not eternal,
not one and not different,
not going and not coming.

Dharmas arise from causes and conditions,
ending all conceptual proliferation.
The Buddha is able to teach this;
I will now explain it.

復次，一切眾生中，不著種種相，眾生相，空相，一等無異；如是觀，是名眾生等。若人是中心等無礙，直入不退，是名得等忍。得等忍菩薩，於一切眾生不瞋不惱，如慈母愛子。如偈說：

觀聲如呼響，身行如鏡像；如此得觀人，云何而不忍？

是名眾生等忍。

Furthermore, among all sentient beings, one does not attach to their many kinds of marks, to the mark of sentient beings, or to the mark of emptiness. One sees them as equally without difference. Contemplating in this way is called equality regarding sentient beings. If, within this, a person's mind is equal and unobstructed, directly enters and does not retreat, this is called attaining the patience of equality. Bodhisattvas who attain the patience of equality do not become angry at or trouble any sentient being, just as a loving mother loves her child. As a verse says:

Contemplate sound as an echo from a call;
bodily actions as images in a mirror.
When one can contemplate people in this way,
how could one fail to be patient?

This is called patience regarding the equality of sentient beings.

云何名法等忍？善法、不善法，有漏、無漏，有為、無為等法，如是諸法入不二法門，入實法相門，如是入竟。是中深入諸法實相時，心忍直入，無諍無礙，是名法等忍。如偈說：

諸法不生不滅，非不生非不滅，亦不生滅，非不生滅，亦非不生滅，非非不生滅。

已得解脫（於邪見得離故言解脫），空、非空（於空不取故言非）是等悉捨，滅諸戲論，言語道斷，深入佛法，心通無礙，不動不退，名無生忍，是助佛道初門。以是故，說已得等忍。

What is called patience regarding the equality of dharmas? Wholesome dharmas, unwholesome dharmas, outflowing dharmas, non-outflowing dharmas, conditioned dharmas, unconditioned dharmas, and so on: all these dharmas enter the Dharma gate of nonduality and enter the gate of the true mark of dharmas. Having entered in this way, when one deeply enters the true mark of dharmas, the mind patiently and directly enters, without dispute and without obstruction. This is called patience regarding the equality of dharmas. As a verse says:

All dharmas are not born and not destroyed;
they are not not-born and not not-destroyed.
Nor are they both born and destroyed;
nor are they not born and destroyed.
Nor are they not born and not destroyed;
nor are they not not-born and not not-destroyed.

Having attained liberation, called liberation because one is freed from wrong views, one abandons all these alike: emptiness and non-emptiness, called "non" because one does not grasp emptiness. One extinguishes all conceptual proliferation, and the path of language is cut off. One deeply enters the Buddha Dharma, and the mind passes through without obstruction, unmoving and non-retreating. This is called the patience with the non-arising of dharmas, the first gateway that assists the Buddha path. For this reason, the sutra says that they had already attained the patience of equality.

Unobstructed Dhāraṇī

Chinese Source

﹝經﹞

得無礙陀羅尼。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sutra:

They had attained unobstructed dhāraṇī.

﹝論﹞

問曰：前已說諸菩薩得陀羅尼，今何以復說得無礙陀羅尼？

答曰：無礙陀羅尼最大故。如一切三昧中，三昧王三昧最大，如人中之王；如諸解脫中無礙解脫；如是一切諸陀羅尼中，無礙陀羅尼大，以是故重說。

Treatise:

Question: Earlier it already said that the bodhisattvas had attained dhāraṇī. Why does it now again say that they had attained unobstructed dhāraṇī?

Answer: Because unobstructed dhāraṇī is the greatest. Among all samādhis, the king of samādhis is greatest, like a king among people. Among all liberations, unobstructed liberation is greatest. In the same way, among all dhāraṇīs, unobstructed dhāraṇī is great. For this reason it is mentioned again.

復次，先說諸菩薩得陀羅尼，不知是何等陀羅尼。有小陀羅尼，如轉輪聖王、仙人等所得；聞持陀羅尼，分別眾生陀羅尼，歸命救護不捨陀羅尼；如是等小陀羅尼，餘人亦有。是無礙陀羅尼，外道、聲聞、辟支佛、新學菩薩皆悉不得；唯無量福德智慧大力諸菩薩，獨有是陀羅尼，以是故別說。

復次，是菩薩輩自利已具足，但欲益彼，說法教化無盡，以無礙陀羅尼為根本。以是故，諸菩薩常行無礙陀羅尼。

Furthermore, when it first said that the bodhisattvas had attained dhāraṇī, it was not known what kind of dhāraṇī this was. There are small dhāraṇīs, such as those attained by wheel-turning kings, seers, and others: the dhāraṇī of retaining what is heard, the dhāraṇī of distinguishing sentient beings, and the dhāraṇī of taking refuge, rescuing, protecting, and not abandoning. Other people also have small dhāraṇīs such as these. But this unobstructed dhāraṇī is not attained by non-Buddhist teachers, śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, or newly training bodhisattvas. Only bodhisattvas of immeasurable merit, wisdom, and great power uniquely have this dhāraṇī. For this reason, it is explained separately.

Furthermore, these bodhisattvas have already fully completed their own benefit and only wish to benefit others. Their teaching of the Dharma and transformation of beings is inexhaustible, with unobstructed dhāraṇī as its root. For this reason, bodhisattvas constantly practice unobstructed dhāraṇī.

The Five Superknowledges

Chinese Source

﹝經﹞

悉是五通。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sutra:

They all possessed the five superknowledges.

﹝論﹞

如意、天眼、天耳、他心智、自識宿命。云何如意？如意通有三種：能到、轉變、聖如意。

能到有四種：一者、身能飛行，如鳥無礙；二者、移遠令近，不往而到；三者、此沒彼出；四者、一念能至。

Treatise:

These are miraculous ability, the divine eye, the divine ear, knowledge of others' minds, and knowledge of one's own past lives. What is miraculous ability? The superknowledge of miraculous ability has three kinds: the ability to arrive, transformation, and noble miraculous ability.

The ability to arrive has four kinds. First, the body can fly through the air without obstruction, like a bird. Second, one moves what is far away and makes it near, arriving without going. Third, one disappears here and appears there. Fourth, in a single thought one can arrive.

轉變者，大能作小，小能作大，一能作多，多能作一，種種諸物皆能轉變。外道輩轉變，極久不過七日；諸佛及弟子轉變自在，無有久近。

聖如意者，外六塵中不可愛不淨物，能觀令淨；可愛淨物，能觀令不淨；是聖如意法，唯佛獨有。是如意通，從四如意足生；是如意足通等，色緣故次第生，不可一時得。

Transformation means that one can make the great small and the small great, make one into many and many into one, and transform all kinds of things. When non-Buddhist practitioners transform things, the longest duration does not exceed seven days. The Buddhas and their disciples transform freely, with no fixed limit of long or short duration.

Noble miraculous ability means that among the six external sense objects, one can contemplate unlovely and impure things and make them pure; and one can contemplate lovely and pure things and make them impure. This Dharma of noble miraculous ability belongs to the Buddha alone. This superknowledge of miraculous ability arises from the four bases of miraculous power. The bases of miraculous power, the superknowledges, and so on arise in sequence because they take form as their condition; they cannot be attained all at once.

天眼通者，於眼得色界四大造清淨色，是名天眼。天眼所見，自地及下地六道中眾生諸物，若近若遠，若麤若細諸色，無不能照。是天眼有二種：一者、從報得；二者、從修得。是五通中天眼從修得，非報得；何以故？常憶念種種光明得故。

The superknowledge of the divine eye means that, in the eye, one obtains pure form produced from the four great elements of the form realm; this is called the divine eye. What the divine eye sees includes sentient beings and things in one's own level and in the lower levels throughout the six destinies. Whether near or far, coarse or subtle, there is no form it cannot illuminate. This divine eye is of two kinds: first, obtained through karmic retribution; second, obtained through cultivation. Among the five superknowledges, the divine eye is obtained through cultivation, not through karmic retribution. Why? Because it is obtained by constantly recollecting many kinds of light.

有人言：是諸菩薩輩得無生法忍力故，六道中不攝，但為教化眾生故，以法身現於十方三界中；未得法身菩薩，或修得，或報得。

Some say that these bodhisattvas, by the power of attaining the patience with the non-arising of dharmas, are not included within the six destinies. It is only for the sake of teaching and transforming sentient beings that, with the Dharma body, they appear in the three realms of the ten directions. Bodhisattvas who have not yet attained the Dharma body may obtain the divine eye either through cultivation or through karmic retribution.

問曰：是諸菩薩功德，勝阿羅漢、辟支佛，何以故讚凡夫所共小功德天眼，不讚諸菩薩慧眼、法眼、佛眼？

答曰：有三種天：一、假號天；二、生天；三、清淨天。轉輪聖王、諸餘大王等，是名假號天；從四天王天，乃至有頂生處，是名生天；諸佛、法身菩薩、辟支佛、阿羅漢，是名清淨天。是清淨天修得天眼，是謂天眼通；佛、法身菩薩清淨天眼，一切離欲五通凡夫所不能得；聲聞、辟支佛亦所不得。所以者何？小阿羅漢小用心，見一千世界；大用心，見二千世界。大阿羅漢小用心，見二千世界；大用心，見三千大千世界。辟支佛亦爾。是名天眼通。

Question: The merits of these bodhisattvas surpass those of arhats and pratyekabuddhas. Why, then, praise the divine eye, a lesser merit shared with ordinary people, and not praise the wisdom eye, Dharma eye, and Buddha eye of the bodhisattvas?

Answer: There are three kinds of devas. First, nominal devas; second, devas by birth; third, pure devas. Wheel-turning sage kings and other great kings are called nominal devas. Those born from the Heaven of the Four Heavenly Kings up to the peak of existence are called devas by birth. Buddhas, Dharma-body bodhisattvas, pratyekabuddhas, and arhats are called pure devas. When these pure devas obtain the divine eye through cultivation, this is called the superknowledge of the divine eye. The pure divine eye of Buddhas and Dharma-body bodhisattvas cannot be obtained by any desire-free ordinary person with the five superknowledges, nor can śrāvakas or pratyekabuddhas obtain it. Why? A lesser arhat, with a small application of mind, sees one thousand worlds; with a great application of mind, sees two thousand worlds. A great arhat, with a small application of mind, sees two thousand worlds; with a great application of mind, sees a three-thousand-great-thousand world-system. Pratyekabuddhas are also like this. This is called the superknowledge of the divine eye.

云何名天耳通？於耳得色界四大造清淨色，能聞一切聲：天聲、人聲、三惡道聲。云何得天耳通修得？常憶念種種聲，是名天耳通。

云何識宿命通？本事常憶念：日月年歲至胎中，乃至過去世中，一世、十世、百世、千萬億世，乃至大阿羅漢、辟支佛知八萬大劫；諸大菩薩及佛知無量劫，是名識宿命通。

云何名知他心通？知他心若有垢，若無垢；自觀心生、住、滅時，常憶念故得。

復次，觀他人喜相、瞋相、怖相、畏相，見此相已，然後知心，是為他心智初門。是五通略說。

What is called the superknowledge of the divine ear? In the ear, one obtains pure form produced from the four great elements of the form realm, and can hear all sounds: the sounds of devas, the sounds of humans, and the sounds of the three bad destinies. How is the superknowledge of the divine ear obtained through cultivation? By constantly recollecting many kinds of sounds. This is called the superknowledge of the divine ear.

What is the superknowledge of knowing past lives? One constantly recollects former events: days, months, years, the time in the womb, and even past lives: one life, ten lives, a hundred lives, thousands of millions of lives. Great arhats and pratyekabuddhas know eighty thousand great kalpas; great bodhisattvas and Buddhas know immeasurable kalpas. This is called the superknowledge of knowing past lives.

What is called the superknowledge of knowing others' minds? One knows whether another's mind has defilement or is free from defilement. It is obtained by constantly recollecting one's own mind as it arises, abides, and ceases.

Furthermore, one observes another person's signs of joy, anger, fear, and dread. Having seen these signs, one then knows that person's mind. This is the first gateway to knowledge of others' minds. This is a brief explanation of the five superknowledges.

Chinese Source

﹝經﹞

言必信受。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sutra:

Their words were surely trusted and accepted.

﹝論﹞

天、人、龍、阿修羅等，及一切大人，皆信受其語，是不綺語報故。諸綺語報者，雖有實語，一切人皆不信受。如偈說：

有墮餓鬼中，火焰從口出，四向發大聲，是為口過報！

雖復多聞見，在大眾說法，以不誠信業，人皆不信受。

Treatise:

Devas, humans, dragons, asuras, and all great people trusted and accepted their words. This is the karmic result of not engaging in frivolous speech. Those who receive the result of frivolous speech, even when they speak true words, are not trusted or accepted by anyone. As a verse says:

Some fall among hungry ghosts;
flames come out from their mouths.
They cry loudly in the four directions:
this is the result of faults of speech.

Although they have heard and seen much,
and teach the Dharma in a great assembly,
because of past actions of insincerity,
people do not trust or accept them.

If one wishes to hear broadly and learn much,
and to be trusted and accepted by others,
one should therefore be completely sincere
and should not engage in frivolous speech.

﹝經﹞

無復懈怠。

Sutra:

They no longer had laziness.

﹝論﹞

懈怠，破在家人財利、福利，破出家人生天樂、涅槃樂。在家、出家名聲俱滅，大失大賊，無過懈怠。如偈說：

懈怠沒善心，癡暗破智明，妙願皆為滅，大業亦已失！

以是故，說無復懈怠。

Treatise:

Laziness destroys the wealth, benefit, and merit of householders. It destroys the joy of rebirth in heaven and the joy of nirvāṇa for renunciants. For householders and renunciants alike, reputation is destroyed. There is no greater loss, no greater thief, than laziness. As a verse says:

Laziness drowns the wholesome mind;
the darkness of delusion destroys the light of wisdom.
Fine vows are all extinguished,
and great works are also lost.

For this reason, the sutra says that they no longer had laziness.

﹝經﹞

已捨利養、名聞。

Sutra:

They had already abandoned gain, support, and fame.

﹝論﹞

是利養法如賊，壞功德本。譬如天雹，傷害五榖，利養、名聞亦復如是，壞功德苗，令不增長。如佛說譬喻：如毛繩縛人，斷膚截骨；貪利養人斷功德本，亦復如是。如偈說：

得入栴檀林，而但取其葉；既入七寶山，而更取水精；

有人入佛法，不求涅槃樂，反求利供養，是輩為自欺！

是故佛弟子，欲得甘露味，當棄捨雜毒，勤求涅槃樂！

譬如惡雹雨，傷害於五榖；若著利供養，破慚愧頭陀。

今世燒善根，後世墮地獄；如提婆達多，為利養自沒。

以是故，言已捨利養、名聞。

Treatise:

Gain and support are like thieves: they destroy the root of merit. Just as hail from the sky damages the five grains, gain, support, and fame are also like this: they destroy the sprouts of merit and prevent them from growing. As the Buddha taught by analogy, it is like a person bound with a rope of hair that cuts through skin and bone. In the same way, people who crave gain and support cut off the root of merit. As a verse says:

Having entered a sandalwood forest,
one takes only its leaves.
Having entered a mountain of seven treasures,
one instead takes crystal.

Some enter the Buddha Dharma
but do not seek the joy of nirvāṇa.
Instead they seek gain and offerings;
such people deceive themselves.

Therefore, disciples of the Buddha
who wish to taste the flavor of ambrosia
should abandon mixed poison
and diligently seek the joy of nirvāṇa.

It is like a harmful hailstorm
that injures the five grains.
If one clings to gain and offerings,
one destroys shame, moral dread, and ascetic practice.

In this life one burns up wholesome roots;
in a future life one falls into hell.
Like Devadatta,
one sinks oneself for the sake of gain and support.

For this reason, the sutra says that they had already abandoned gain, support, and fame.

﹝經﹞

說法無所悕望。

Sutra:

When teaching the Dharma, they had no expectation of reward.

﹝論﹞

大慈憐愍，為眾說法，不為衣食、名聲、勢力故說；大慈悲故，心清淨故，得無生法忍故。如偈說：

多聞辯慧巧言語，美說諸法轉人心，自不如法行不正，譬如雲雷而不雨。

博學多聞有智慧，訥口拙言無巧便，不能顯發法寶藏，譬如無雷而小雨。

不廣學問無智慧，不能說法無好行，是弊法師無慚愧，譬如小雲無雷雨。

多聞廣智美言語，巧說諸法轉人心，行法心正無所畏，如大雲雷澍洪雨。

法之大將持法鏡，照明佛法智慧藏，持誦廣宣振法鈴，如海中船渡一切。

亦如蜂王集諸味，說如佛言隨佛意，助佛明法度眾生，如是法師甚難值！

Treatise:

With great loving-kindness and compassion, they teach the Dharma for the assembly. They do not teach for the sake of clothing, food, reputation, or influence. They teach because of great loving-kindness and compassion, because their minds are pure, and because they have attained the patience with the non-arising of dharmas. As a verse says:

One may be learned, eloquent, wise, and skilled in speech,
beautifully teaching dharmas and turning people's minds;
but if one does not practice according to Dharma and one's conduct is not right,
one is like clouds that thunder but do not rain.

One may be broadly learned and wise,
yet slow of speech and without skillful means;
unable to reveal the treasury of Dharma jewels,
one is like a small rain without thunder.

Without broad learning and without wisdom,
unable to teach Dharma and without good conduct,
such a poor Dharma teacher has no shame or moral dread,
like a small cloud without thunder or rain.

Broadly learned, widely wise, beautiful in speech,
skillfully teaching dharmas and turning people's minds,
practicing Dharma with upright mind and without fear,
one is like a great cloud thundering and pouring down heavy rain.

A great general of Dharma holds the Dharma mirror,
illuminating the wisdom treasury of the Buddha Dharma.
Reciting, widely proclaiming, and shaking the Dharma bell,
one is like a ship in the ocean ferrying everyone across.

Also like the king of bees gathering many flavors,
one teaches according to the Buddha's words and follows the Buddha's intent,
helping the Buddha clarify the Dharma and liberate sentient beings:
such a Dharma teacher is very difficult to encounter.

The Patience That Crosses Deep Dharmas

Chinese Source

﹝經﹞

度甚深法忍。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sutra:

They had crossed over into patience regarding very deep dharmas.

﹝論﹞

云何甚深法？十二因緣，是名甚深法。如佛告阿難：是十二因緣法甚深，難解難知！

復次，依過去，未來世生六十二邪見網永離，是名甚深法。如佛語比丘：凡夫無聞，若欲讚佛，所讚甚少，所謂若讚戒清淨，若讚離諸欲。若能讚是甚深難解難知法，是為實讚佛。是中，梵網經應廣說。

復次，三解脫門，是名甚深法。如佛說般若波羅蜜中，諸天讚言：世尊！是法甚深！佛言：甚深法者，空則是義，無作、無相則是義。

Treatise:

What are very deep dharmas? The twelve causes and conditions are called very deep dharmas. As the Buddha told Ānanda: "This Dharma of the twelve causes and conditions is very deep, difficult to understand and difficult to know."

Furthermore, to be forever free from the net of the sixty-two wrong views that arise depending on the past and future is called very deep Dharma. As the Buddha told the bhikṣus: "If ordinary uninstructed people wish to praise the Buddha, what they praise is very little, namely praising purity of precepts or praising separation from sensual desires. If they can praise this very deep Dharma, difficult to understand and difficult to know, that is truly praising the Buddha." Here the Brahmajāla Sūtra should be explained broadly.

Furthermore, the three gates of liberation are called very deep dharmas. In the Prajñāpāramitā, when the devas praised, "World-Honored One, this Dharma is very deep," the Buddha said, "Very deep Dharma means emptiness; it also means wishlessness and signlessness."

Very Deep Dharma and Fearless Power

Chinese Source

復次，解一切諸法相實，不可破不可動，是名甚深法。

復次，除內心想智力，但定心諸法清淨實相中住。譬如熱氣盛，非黃見黃；心想智力故，於諸法轉觀，是名淺法。譬如人眼清淨無熱氣，如實見黃是黃；如是除內心想智力，慧眼清淨，見諸法實相。譬如真水精，黃物著中則隨作黃色，青、赤、白色皆隨色變。心亦如是，凡夫人內心想智力故，見諸法異相。觀諸法實相，非空非不空，不有非不有；是法中深入不轉，無所罣礙，是名度深法忍。度名得甚深法，具足滿，無所礙，得度彼岸，是名為度。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, understanding the true marks of all dharmas, which cannot be broken and cannot be moved, is called very deep Dharma.

Furthermore, when one removes the power of inner mental imagining and intellectual construction, then with a concentrated mind one abides in the pure true mark of dharmas. It is like intense heat-haze: what is not yellow is seen as yellow. Because of the power of mental imagining and intellectual construction, one turns and contemplates dharmas in distorted ways; this is called shallow Dharma. It is like a person's eyes being clear and free from heat-haze: one sees yellow as yellow just as it is. In the same way, when one removes the power of inner mental imagining and intellectual construction, the wisdom eye is pure and sees the true mark of dharmas. It is like a true crystal: if a yellow object is placed in it, it appears yellow; blue, red, and white also change according to the color. The mind is also like this. Because ordinary people have the power of inner mental imagining and intellectual construction, they see different marks in dharmas. To contemplate the true mark of dharmas as neither empty nor not empty, neither existent nor not existent, and to deeply enter this Dharma without turning back and without obstruction, is called the patience that crosses deep Dharma. "Crossing" means attaining very deep Dharma, being fully complete, unobstructed, and reaching the other shore. This is called crossing.

﹝經﹞

得無畏力。

Sutra:

They had attained fearless power.

﹝論﹞

諸菩薩四無所畏力成就，

問曰：如菩薩所作未辦，未得一切智，何以故說得四無所畏？

答曰：無所畏有二種：菩薩無所畏，佛無所畏。是諸菩薩雖未得佛無所畏，得菩薩無所畏，是故名為得無畏力。

Treatise:

The bodhisattvas had accomplished the power of the four fearlessnesses.

Question: Since the bodhisattvas have not yet completed what they are to do and have not yet attained all-knowledge, why does it say that they have attained the four fearlessnesses?

Answer: Fearlessness is of two kinds: the fearlessness of bodhisattvas and the fearlessness of Buddhas. Although these bodhisattvas have not yet attained the fearlessness of Buddhas, they have attained the fearlessness of bodhisattvas. For this reason, they are said to have attained fearless power.

問曰：何等為菩薩四無所畏？

答曰：一者、一切聞能持故，得諸陀羅尼故，常憶念不忘故，眾中說法無所畏。二者、知一切眾生欲解脫因緣，諸根利鈍，隨其所應而為說法故，菩薩在大眾中說法無所畏。三者、不見若東方、南、西、北方，四維、上下，有來難問，令我不能如法答者：不見如是少許相故，於眾中說法無所畏。四者一切眾生聽受問難；隨意如法答，能巧斷一切眾生疑故。菩薩在大眾中說法無所畏。

Question: What are the four fearlessnesses of bodhisattvas?

Answer: First, because they can retain everything they hear, because they have attained dhāraṇīs, and because they constantly recollect and do not forget, they teach the Dharma in an assembly without fear. Second, because they know all sentient beings' causes and conditions for desiring liberation, and know whether their faculties are sharp or dull, they teach the Dharma according to what is appropriate for each; therefore bodhisattvas teach the Dharma in a great assembly without fear. Third, whether in the east, south, west, north, the four intermediate directions, above, or below, they do not see anyone coming to challenge them with questions in such a way that they could not answer according to Dharma. Because they do not see even the slightest sign of this, they teach the Dharma in an assembly without fear. Fourth, all sentient beings listen, receive, question, and challenge; bodhisattvas answer freely and in accordance with Dharma, skillfully cutting off all sentient beings' doubts. Therefore bodhisattvas teach the Dharma in a great assembly without fear.

Passing Beyond Māra's Work

Chinese Source

﹝經﹞

過諸魔事。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sutra:

They had passed beyond all works of Māra.

﹝論﹞

魔有四種：一者、煩惱魔；二者、陰魔；三者、死魔；四者、他化自在天子魔。是諸菩薩得菩薩道故，破煩惱魔；得法性身故，破陰魔；得道、得法性身故，破死魔；常一心故，一切處心不著故，入不動三昧故，破他化自在天子魔。以是故，說過諸魔事。

復次，是般若波羅蜜覺魔品中，佛自說魔業、魔事，是魔業、魔事盡已過故，是名已過魔事。

Treatise:

There are four kinds of Māra. First, the Māra of afflictions; second, the Māra of the aggregates; third, the Māra of death; fourth, the Māra who is the deva lord of Paranirmitavaśavartin Heaven. Because these bodhisattvas have attained the bodhisattva path, they break the Māra of afflictions. Because they have attained the body of Dharma-nature, they break the Māra of the aggregates. Because they have attained the path and the body of Dharma-nature, they break the Māra of death. Because they are constantly one-pointed in mind, because their minds do not attach anywhere, and because they enter the unmoving samādhi, they break the Māra who is the deva lord of Paranirmitavaśavartin Heaven. For this reason, the sutra says that they have passed beyond all works of Māra.

Furthermore, in the chapter on recognizing Māra in the Prajñāpāramitā, the Buddha himself explains Māra's karma and Māra's works. Because these Māra karmas and Māra works have been exhausted and passed beyond, this is called having passed beyond Māra's works.

The Armies of Māra

Chinese Source

復次，除諸法實相，餘殘一切法，盡名為魔；如諸煩惱、結、使，欲、縛、取、纏，陰、界、入，魔王、魔民、魔人，如是等盡名為魔。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, apart from the true mark of all dharmas, all remaining dharmas are called Māra: all afflictions, bonds, underlying tendencies, desires, fetters, graspings, entanglements, aggregates, elements, sense fields, Māra kings, Māra subjects, Māra beings, and so on are all called Māra.

問曰：何處說欲縛等諸結使名魔？

答曰：雜藏經中，佛說偈語魔王：

欲是汝初軍，憂愁軍第二，飢渴軍第三，愛軍在第四，

第五眠睡軍，怖畏軍第六，疑為第七軍，含毒軍第八，

第九軍利養，著虛妄名聞，第十軍自高，輕慢於他人。

汝軍等如是，一切世間人，及諸一切天，無能破之者。

我以智慧箭，修定智慧力，摧破汝魔軍，如坏瓶沒水。

一心修智慧，以度於一切；我弟子精進，常念修智慧，

隨順如法行，必得至涅槃。汝雖不欲放，到汝不到處！

是時魔王聞，愁憂即滅去；是魔惡部黨，亦復沒不現！

是名諸結使魔。

Question: Where does it say that desire, fetters, and other bonds and underlying tendencies are called Māra?

Answer: In the Miscellaneous Treasury Sutra, the Buddha spoke these verses to King Māra:

Desire is your first army;
sorrow and grief are your second army.
Hunger and thirst are your third army;
craving is the fourth.

The fifth army is sleepiness;
fear and dread are the sixth.
Doubt is the seventh army;
poisoned intention is the eighth.

The ninth army is gain and support;
clinging to empty fame.
The tenth army is self-exaltation,
looking down on others with arrogance.

Your armies are like this:
people of the whole world,
and all devas as well,
are unable to destroy them.

With the arrow of wisdom,
and with the power of cultivating concentration and wisdom,
I shatter your armies of Māra,
like an unbaked jar sinking into water.

With one mind I cultivate wisdom,
in order to liberate all.
My disciples are diligent,
always mindful in cultivating wisdom.

Following and practicing in accordance with Dharma,
they will certainly reach nirvāṇa.
Though you do not wish to release them,
they will reach the place you cannot reach.

When King Māra heard this,
his sorrow and grief immediately vanished.
The evil followers of Māra
also disappeared and were no longer seen.

This is called the Māra of the bonds and underlying tendencies.

問曰：五眾、十八界、十二入，何處說是魔？

答曰：莫拘羅山中，佛教弟子羅陀：色眾是魔，受、想、行、識亦如是。

復次，若欲作未來世作色身，是為動處；若欲作無色身，是亦為動處；若欲作有想、無想、非有想非無想身，是為一切動處。動是魔縛，不動則不縛，從惡得脫。此中說眾、界、入，是魔；自在天子魔，魔民、魔人即是魔，不須說。

Question: Where does it say that the five aggregates, eighteen elements, and twelve sense fields are Māra?

Answer: On Mount Makula, the Buddha taught his disciple Rādha: "The form aggregate is Māra; feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness are also like this."

Furthermore, if one wishes to produce a form body in a future life, this is a place of movement. If one wishes to produce a formless body, this too is a place of movement. If one wishes to produce a body with perception, without perception, or neither with perception nor without perception, all of these are places of movement. Movement is Māra's bondage; if there is no movement, then one is not bound and is freed from evil. Here it says that aggregates, elements, and sense fields are Māra. As for Māra the deva lord, Māra subjects, and Māra beings, these are Māra directly and need no explanation.

問曰：何以名魔？

答曰：奪慧命，壞道法功德善本，是故名為魔。諸外道人輩言：是名欲主，亦名華箭，亦名五箭，破種種善事。佛法中名為魔羅，是業是事，名為魔事。是何等魔事？如覺魔品中說。

Question: Why is it called Māra?

Answer: It steals the life of wisdom and destroys the wholesome roots of merit in the Dharma of the path. Therefore it is called Māra. Non-Buddhist practitioners call it the lord of desire, the flower-arrow, and the five arrows, because it destroys many kinds of wholesome affairs. In the Buddha Dharma it is called Māra. Its actions and affairs are called Māra's works. What are Māra's works? They are explained in the chapter on recognizing Māra.

復次，人展轉世間受苦樂，結使因緣，亦魔王力因緣；是魔名諸佛怨讎，一切聖人賊，破一切逆流人事，不喜涅槃，是名魔。是魔有三事：戲笑語言，歌舞邪視，如是等從愛生；縛打鞭拷，刺割斫截，如是等從瞋生；炙身、自凍，拔髮、自餓，入火、赴淵、投巖，如是等從愚癡生。又大過失不淨，染著世間，皆是魔事。憎惡利益，不用涅槃及涅槃道，亦是魔事。沒大苦海，不自覺知。如是等無量，皆是魔事。已棄已捨，是為過諸魔事。

Furthermore, as people turn through the world receiving suffering and pleasure, the causes and conditions of the bonds and underlying tendencies are also causes and conditions of the power of King Māra. This Māra is called the enemy of all Buddhas and the thief of all noble ones. It destroys the work of all who go against the current and does not rejoice in nirvāṇa. This is called Māra. This Māra has three kinds of activity: joking speech, singing and dancing, and crooked gazes arise from craving; binding, beating, whipping, torture, stabbing, cutting, chopping, and severing arise from anger; burning the body, freezing oneself, pulling out the hair, starving oneself, entering fire, jumping into abysses, and throwing oneself from cliffs arise from delusion. Also, great faults and impurities, and defiled attachment to the world, are all Māra's works. Hating benefit and having no use for nirvāṇa or the path to nirvāṇa are also Māra's works. One sinks in the great ocean of suffering and does not realize it oneself. Immeasurable things like these are all Māra's works. Having abandoned and relinquished them is called passing beyond all works of Māra.

Liberation from Karmic Obstruction

Chinese Source

﹝經﹞

一切業障悉得解脫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sutra:

They had all been liberated from every karmic obstruction.

﹝論﹞

一切惡業得解脫，是名業障得解脫，

問曰：若三種障：煩惱障、業障、報障；何以捨二障，但說業障？

答曰：三障中業力最大故；積集諸業，乃至百千萬劫中，不失、不燒、不壞，與果報合時不亡。是諸業能久住，和合時與果報；如榖草子在地中，得時節而生，不失不壞。是諸佛一切智，第一尊重，如須彌山王，尚不能轉是諸業，何況凡人！如偈說：

Treatise:

Being liberated from all evil karma is called liberation from karmic obstruction.

Question: There are three kinds of obstruction: the obstruction of afflictions, the obstruction of karma, and the obstruction of retribution. Why set aside two obstructions and speak only of karmic obstruction?

Answer: Because among the three obstructions, the power of karma is greatest. Accumulated karma, even through hundreds of thousands of millions of kalpas, is not lost, burned, or destroyed. When it meets with its result, it does not perish. These karmas can remain for a long time, and when the conditions come together, they give their results. It is like grain or grass seeds in the ground: when they obtain the proper season, they grow, without being lost or destroyed. Even the all-knowledge of the Buddhas, foremost and most honored like Sumeru, king of mountains, cannot turn away these karmas. How much less can ordinary people do so. As a verse says:

生死輪載人，諸煩惱結業，大力自在轉，無人能禁止。

先世業自作，轉為種種形，業力為最大，世界中無比！

先世業自在，將人受果報，業力故輪轉，生死海中迴。

大海水乾竭，須彌山地盡，先世因緣業，不燒亦不盡。

諸業久和集，造者自逐去，譬如債物主，追逐人不置。

是諸業果報，無有能轉者，亦無逃避處，非求哀可免。

三界中眾生，追之不暫離，如珂梨羅刺，是業佛所說。

如風不入實，水流不仰行，虛空不受害，無業亦如是。

諸業無量力，不逐非造者；果報時節來，不亡亦不失。

從地飛上天，從天入雪山，從雪山入海，一切處不離。

常恆隨逐我，無一時相捨，直至無失時，如星流趣月。

以是故，說一切諸業障悉得解脫。

The wheel of birth and death carries people along;
afflictions, bonds, and karma
turn with great power and freedom,
and no one can stop them.

Karma made by oneself in past lives
turns into many kinds of forms.
The power of karma is greatest;
nothing in the world compares with it.

Past karma has its own force,
leading people to receive its results.
Because of karmic power they revolve,
turning in the ocean of birth and death.

The waters of the great ocean may dry up;
Sumeru and the earth may come to an end.
But past causal karma
is neither burned nor exhausted.

Karmas long accumulated
follow the one who made them,
like a creditor pursuing a debtor,
never letting that person go.

The results of these karmas
cannot be turned away by anyone.
There is nowhere to escape,
and one cannot be spared by begging for pity.

They pursue beings in the three realms
and never leave them even for a moment,
like the thorn of a kherīra tree:
this is karma taught by the Buddha.

Just as wind does not enter solidity,
water does not flow upward,
and empty space is not harmed,
so it is with the absence of karma.

Karmas have immeasurable power,
but they do not pursue those who did not make them.
When the time for retribution arrives,
they do not perish and are not lost.

One may fly from earth up to heaven,
from heaven enter the Snow Mountains,
and from the Snow Mountains enter the sea;
in every place, karma does not leave.

It constantly follows me,
never separating even for a moment,
until the time of unfailing result arrives,
like a shooting star hastening toward the moon.

For this reason, the sutra says that they had all been liberated from every karmic obstruction.

Skillful Teaching of Causes and Conditions

﹝經﹞

巧說因緣法。

Sutra:

They skillfully taught the Dharma of causes and conditions.

﹝論﹞

十二因緣生法，種種法門能巧說煩惱、業、事，次第展轉相續生，是名十二因緣。是中無明、愛、取三事，名煩惱；行、有二事，名業；餘七分，名事。是十二因緣，初二過去世攝，後二未來世攝，中八現前世攝。是略說三事：煩惱，業，苦；是三事展轉更互為因緣：是煩惱業因緣，業苦因緣，苦苦因緣，苦煩惱因緣，煩惱業因緣，業苦因緣，苦苦因緣，是名展轉更互為因緣。過去世一切煩惱，是名無明。從無明生業，能作世界果，故名為行。從行生垢心，初身因，如犢子識母，自相識故，名為識。是識共生無色四陰，及是所住色，是名名色。是名色中生眼等六情，是名六入。情、塵、識合，是名為觸。從觸生受。受中心著，是名渴愛。渴愛因緣求，是名取。從取後世因業，是名有。從有還受後世五眾，是名生。從生五眾熟壞，是名老死。老死生憂、悲、哭、泣，種種愁惱，眾苦和合集。若一心觀諸法實相清淨，則無明盡，無明盡故行盡，乃至眾苦和合集皆盡。是十二因緣相，如是能方便不著邪見，為人演說，是名為巧。

復次，是十二因緣觀中，斷法愛，心不著，知實相，是名為巧。如說般若波羅蜜不可盡品中，佛告須菩提：癡如虛空不可盡，行如虛空不可盡，乃至眾苦和合集如虛空不可盡。菩薩當作是知！作是知者，為捨癡際，應無所入。作是觀十二因緣起者，則為坐道場，得薩婆若。

Treatise:

The Dharma arising through the twelve causes and conditions can, through many Dharma gateways, skillfully explain afflictions, karma, and events as they arise in successive continuity, each condition leading to the next. This is called the twelve causes and conditions. Among them, the three factors of ignorance, craving, and grasping are called afflictions; the two factors of formations and existence are called karma; the remaining seven factors are called events. Of these twelve causes and conditions, the first two belong to the past life, the last two belong to the future life, and the middle eight belong to the present life.

Stated briefly, there are three things: afflictions, karma, and suffering. These three things turn and serve as conditions for one another: afflictions condition karma, karma conditions suffering, suffering conditions suffering, suffering conditions afflictions, afflictions condition karma, karma conditions suffering, and suffering conditions suffering. This is called turning and serving as conditions for one another.

All the afflictions of the past life are called ignorance. From ignorance, karma is produced; because it can make the result of a world, it is called formations. From formations, a defiled mind arises, the cause of the first body; because it recognizes its own characteristic, like a calf recognizing its mother, it is called consciousness. Together with this consciousness arise the four formless aggregates and the form in which they abide; this is called name-and-form. Within name-and-form, the six faculties such as the eye are produced; this is called the six sense fields. The faculty, the object, and consciousness come together; this is called contact. From contact, feeling arises. When the mind clings within feeling, this is called thirsty craving. Seeking by means of thirsty craving is called grasping. From grasping, the karma that causes a future life arises; this is called existence. From existence, one again receives the five aggregates of a later life; this is called birth. From birth, the five aggregates mature and decay; this is called old age and death. From old age and death arise anxiety, sorrow, weeping, lamentation, and many kinds of distress; this is the coming together of the whole mass of suffering.

If one single-mindedly contemplates the pure true mark of all dharmas, then ignorance is exhausted. Because ignorance is exhausted, formations are exhausted, and so on until the coming together of the whole mass of suffering is also exhausted. Such is the characteristic of the twelve causes and conditions. To explain this skillfully for people by using skillful means, without clinging to wrong views, is called skill.

Furthermore, within this contemplation of the twelve causes and conditions, to cut off attachment to Dharma, for the mind not to cling, and to know the true mark is called skill. As it says in the Inexhaustible Chapter of the Prajnaparamita, the Buddha told Subhuti, "Delusion is inexhaustible like empty space. Formations are inexhaustible like empty space, and so on until the coming together of the whole mass of suffering is inexhaustible like empty space. A bodhisattva should know in this way. One who knows in this way, in order to relinquish the limit of delusion, should have nowhere to enter. One who makes this contemplation of the arising of the twelve causes and conditions is thereby seated at the place of awakening and attains sarvajna, all-knowledge."

Great Vows Made for Countless Kalpas

﹝經﹞

從阿僧祇劫已來，發大誓願。

Sutra:

For asamkhyeya kalpas, they had made great vows.

﹝論﹞

阿僧祇義，菩薩義品中已說。劫義，佛譬喻說：四十里石山，有長壽人百歲過，持細軟衣一來拂拭，令是大石山盡，劫故未盡。四十里大城，滿中芥子，不[既/木]令平；有長壽人百歲過，一來取一芥子，芥子盡，劫故不盡。菩薩如是無數劫，發大正願度脫眾生。願名大心要誓，必度一切眾生，斷諸結使，成阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，是名為願。

Treatise:

The meaning of asamkhyeya has already been explained in the chapter on the meaning of bodhisattva. As for the meaning of kalpa, the Buddha explained it with similes. Suppose there were a stone mountain forty li wide, and a person with a very long life came once every hundred years, holding a fine, soft cloth, and wiped it once. Even if that great stone mountain were worn away, the kalpa would still not be exhausted. Suppose there were a great city forty li wide, filled level with mustard seeds, and a person with a very long life came once every hundred years and took away one mustard seed. Even if the mustard seeds were exhausted, the kalpa would still not be exhausted.

For just such countless kalpas, bodhisattvas make great, correct vows to liberate beings. A vow means an essential pledge made by a great mind: to certainly liberate all beings, cut off all fetters and latent tendencies, and accomplish anuttara-samyak-sambodhi. This is called a vow.

Their complexion was gentle and joyful. They always greeted others first, and their words were not coarse.

若見乞道人，能以四種待；初見好眼視，迎逆敬問訊，

Because the root of anger had been pulled out, because jealousy had been removed, because they constantly cultivated great loving-kindness, great compassion, great joy, and great equanimity, and because the four kinds of wrong speech had been cut off, they obtained a gentle and joyful appearance. As a verse says:

If, on seeing a mendicant seeking the path,

first seeing him with kind eyes,

and fully giving what he wishes,

the verbal faults and poison of false speech,

and wishes to liberate all beings.

Fearlessness in the Great Assembly

﹝經﹞

於大眾中得無所畏。

Sutra:

In the great assembly, they had attained fearlessness.

﹝論﹞

大德故，堅實功德智慧故，得最上辯陀羅尼故，於大眾中得無所畏。如偈說：

內心智德薄，外善以美言；譬如竹無內，但示有其外。

內心智德厚，外善以法言；譬如妙金剛，中外力具足。

復次，無畏法成就故，端正、貴族、大力，持戒、禪定、智慧、語議等皆成就，是故無所畏。以是故，於大眾中無所畏。如偈說：

少德無智慧，不應處高座，如豺見師子，竄伏不敢出。

大智無所畏，應處師子座，譬如師子吼，眾獸皆怖畏！

無量無邊智慧福德力集，故無所畏。如偈說：

若人滅眾惡，乃至無小罪，如是大德人，無願而不滿。

是人大智慧，世界中無惱，是故如此人，生死涅槃一。

復次，獨得菩薩無所畏故，如毗那婆那王經中說。菩薩獨得四無所畏，如先說。

Treatise:

Because of their great virtue, because of their solid merit and wisdom, and because they had attained the highest dharani of eloquence, they attained fearlessness in the great assembly. As a verse says:

If inwardly one's wisdom and virtue are thin,
while outwardly one is skilled in beautiful words,
this is like bamboo with nothing inside,
showing only its outer surface.

If inwardly one's wisdom and virtue are thick,
and outwardly one is skilled in words of Dharma,
this is like marvelous vajra,
complete in strength inside and out.

Furthermore, because the Dharma of fearlessness had been accomplished, because upright appearance, noble birth, great strength, moral discipline, meditation, wisdom, and discourse were all accomplished, therefore they were fearless. For this reason, they were fearless in the great assembly. As a verse says:

One of little virtue and no wisdom
should not sit on a high seat.
Like a jackal seeing a lion,
one crouches and does not dare come out.

One with great wisdom is fearless
and should sit on the lion seat.
It is like the lion's roar:
all animals become afraid.

Because immeasurable and boundless wisdom, merit, and power had been gathered, they were fearless. As a verse says:

If a person has extinguished all evils,
until there is not even the slightest fault,
for such a person of great virtue,
there is no vow that will not be fulfilled.

This person has great wisdom,
and in the world has no distress.
Therefore for such a person,
samsara and nirvana are one.

Furthermore, they uniquely attained the bodhisattva's fearlessness, as explained in the Sutra of King Vinavata. Bodhisattvas uniquely attain the four fearlessnesses, as explained above.

Skillful and Outstanding in Teaching the Dharma

﹝經﹞

無數億劫說法巧出。

Sutra:

For countless kotis of kalpas, they had been skillful and outstanding in teaching the Dharma.

﹝論﹞

不放逸等諸善根，自身好修。是諸菩薩，非一世、二、三世，乃至無量阿僧祇劫集功德智慧。如偈說：

為眾生故發大心，若有不敬而慢者，其罪甚大不可說，何況而復加惡者！

復次，是菩薩無數無量劫中，修身、修戒、修心、修慧；生滅縛解中，自了了知諸法實相。有三種解：聞解，義解，得解。種種說法門中，無所罣礙，皆得說法方便、智慧波羅蜜。是諸菩薩所說，如聖人語，皆應信受。如偈說：

有慧無多聞，是不知實相；譬如大暗中，有目無所見。

多聞無智慧，亦不知實相；譬如大明中，有燈而無目。

多聞利智慧，是所說應受；無聞亦無智，是名人身牛。

問曰：應言無數億劫巧說法，復何以言出？答曰：於無智人中，及弟子中說法易；若多聞利智善論議人中說法難。若小智法師，是中退縮；若大學多聞，問難中大膽欣豫，一切眾中有大威德。如天會經中偈說：

面目齒光明，普照於大會；映奪諸天光，種種皆不現。

以是故，名為無數億劫巧說法中能得出。

Treatise:

They had well cultivated in themselves the roots of goodness such as non-negligence. These bodhisattvas gathered merit and wisdom not for one life, two lives, or three lives, but for immeasurable asamkhyeya kalpas. As a verse says:

For the sake of beings, they gave rise to the great mind.
If someone disrespects and arrogantly looks down on them,
that person's offense is so great it cannot be described;
how much more so if that person adds evil deeds!

Furthermore, throughout countless and immeasurable kalpas, these bodhisattvas cultivated the body, cultivated moral discipline, cultivated the mind, and cultivated wisdom. Amid arising and ceasing, bondage and liberation, they themselves clearly knew the true mark of all dharmas. There are three kinds of understanding: understanding from hearing, understanding of meaning, and understanding through realization. Within the many gateways of teaching the Dharma, they had no obstruction. They all attained skillful means in teaching the Dharma and the perfection of wisdom. What these bodhisattvas taught should all be trusted and accepted, like the words of noble ones. As a verse says:

Having wisdom but little learning,
one does not know the true mark.
It is like being in great darkness:
one has eyes, but sees nothing.

Having much learning but no wisdom,
one also does not know the true mark.
It is like being in bright daylight
with a lamp, but without eyes.

One who is learned and has sharp wisdom:
what such a person teaches should be accepted.
One who has neither learning nor wisdom
is called a human-bodied ox.

Question: It should say, "for countless kotis of kalpas, they skillfully taught the Dharma." Why does it also say "outstanding"?

Answer: It is easy to teach the Dharma among people without wisdom and among disciples. It is difficult to teach the Dharma among people who are learned, sharp in wisdom, and skilled in debate. A Dharma teacher of small wisdom draws back in such a setting. But one of great learning and broad study is bold and joyful in the midst of questions and challenges, and has great majestic virtue in every assembly. As a verse in the Sutra of the Assembly of Devas says:

The light from his face, eyes, and teeth
shines throughout the great assembly.
It outshines the light of the devas,
so that their various lights no longer appear.

For this reason, it is called being able to stand out through skillful Dharma teaching for countless kotis of kalpas.
