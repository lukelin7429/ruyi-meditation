Radiance and Truthful Speech

Fascicle 8 continues the explanation of the Buddha's radiance and the broad and long tongue as testimony to truthful speech.

The Buddha's Abiding Radiance

Chinese Source

〖經〗爾時，世尊以常光明遍照三千大千世界，亦至東方如恒河沙等諸佛世界，乃至十方亦復如是。若有眾生遇斯光者，必得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。

〖論〗問曰：上已舉身微笑及放毛孔光明，今何以復放常光而照十方？

答曰：有人見異光明，謂非佛光。見佛常光轉大，心則歡喜，此實佛光，便畢至阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。

問曰：云何為「常光」？

答曰：佛身四邊各一丈光明，菩薩生便有此，是三十二相之一，名為丈光相。

English Translation

Sutra: At that time, the World-Honored One illuminated the entire great trichiliocosm with his abiding radiance. It also reached Buddha worlds in the east as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, and likewise in all ten directions. Any beings who encountered this light would certainly attain unsurpassed complete awakening.

Treatise: Question: His whole body has already smiled, and he has emitted light from his pores. Why does he now also emit his abiding radiance to illuminate the ten directions?

Answer: Some people see an unusual light and think it is not the Buddha's light. When they see his abiding radiance expand, their hearts rejoice: 'This truly is the Buddha's light.' They will then ultimately attain unsurpassed complete awakening.

Question: What is 'abiding radiance'?

Answer: It is the light extending one zhang on every side of the Buddha's body. The bodhisattva has it from birth. It is one of the thirty-two marks, called the mark of a halo extending one zhang.

Why the Radiance Is Adapted to Beings

Chinese Source

問曰：佛何以故光常一丈而不多？

答曰：一切諸佛常光無量，常照十方世界。釋迦牟尼佛神通身光無量，或一丈、百丈、千萬億，乃至滿三千大千世界；乃至十方，如諸佛常法。但於五濁世為眾生少德、少智故，受一丈光明；若受多光，今眾生薄福鈍根，目不堪其明。如人見天身眼則失明，以光盛眼微故。若眾生利根福重，佛則為之現無量光明。

復次，有人見佛常光歡喜得度。譬如國王以常食之餘賜諸群下，得者大喜；佛亦如是，有人見佛種種餘光心不歡喜，見佛常光，必至阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。

English Translation

Question: Why does the Buddha's abiding light extend only one zhang and no farther?

Answer: The abiding radiance of all Buddhas is immeasurable and constantly illuminates the worlds in the ten directions. The light of Śākyamuni Buddha's body, manifested through his supernormal power, is immeasurable: it may extend one zhang, a hundred zhang, or countless vast distances, filling the great trichiliocosm and even the ten directions, as is the usual way of all Buddhas. But in this age of the five defilements, because beings have little virtue and wisdom, he assumes a radiance of one zhang. If he assumed a greater radiance, beings now, having little merit and dull faculties, could not bear its brightness with their eyes. It is like people losing their sight upon seeing a deva's body, because its light is strong and their eyes are weak. If beings have sharp faculties and abundant merit, the Buddha manifests immeasurable light for them.

Furthermore, some people rejoice upon seeing the Buddha's abiding radiance and attain liberation. It is like a king giving what remains of his regular meal to his subjects: those who receive it are greatly delighted. The Buddha is also like this. Some people do not rejoice at the many other kinds of light he manifests, yet upon seeing his abiding radiance, they are certain to attain unsurpassed complete awakening.

The Broad and Long Tongue as a Sign of Truthfulness

Chinese Source

〖經〗爾時，世尊出廣長舌相，遍覆三千大千世界，熙怡而笑。從其舌根出無量千萬億光，是一一光化成千葉金色寶華。是諸華上，皆有化佛結加趺坐，說六波羅蜜；眾生聞者，必得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。復至十方如恒河沙等諸佛世界，皆亦如是。

〖論〗問曰：如佛世尊大德尊重，何以故出廣長舌，似如輕相？

答曰：上三種放光，照十方眾生令得度脫。今欲口說摩訶般若波羅蜜；摩訶般若波羅蜜甚深，難解、難知、難可信受，是故出廣長舌為證。舌相如是，語必真實。

English Translation

Sutra: At that time, the World-Honored One extended his broad and long tongue, covering the entire great trichiliocosm, and smiled with joy. From the root of his tongue came immeasurable myriads of rays. Each ray transformed into a golden jeweled lotus with a thousand petals. On each lotus sat a manifested Buddha in full lotus posture, teaching the six perfections. Beings who heard them would certainly attain unsurpassed complete awakening. The same manifestations also reached Buddha worlds in all ten directions as numerous as the sands of the Ganges.

Treatise: Question: The Buddha, the World-Honored One, possesses great virtue and dignity. Why does he extend his broad and long tongue, seemingly making a frivolous gesture?

Answer: The three kinds of light described above illuminated beings in the ten directions and enabled them to attain liberation. Now he is about to teach the Mahāprajñāpāramitā in words. The Mahāprajñāpāramitā is extremely profound, difficult to understand and know, and difficult to believe and accept. Therefore, he extends his broad and long tongue as testimony. One possessing such a mark of the tongue must speak truthfully.

An Elderly Servant's Offering

Chinese Source

如昔一時佛於舍婆提國受歲竟，阿難從佛遊行諸國，欲到婆羅門城。婆羅門城王知佛神德，能化眾人感動群心，今來到此，誰復樂我？便作制限：「若有與佛食、聽佛語者，輸五百金錢。」作制限後，佛到其國，將阿難持鉢入城乞食。城中眾人皆閉門不應，佛空鉢而出。

是時，一家有一老使人，持破瓦器，盛臭𤄜淀，出門棄之，見佛世尊空鉢而來。老使人見佛相好：金色、白毛、肉髻、丈光，鉢空無食；見已思惟：「如此神人應食天廚，今自降身持鉢行乞，必是大慈愍一切故。」信心清淨，欲好供養，無由如願；慚愧白佛：「思欲設供，更不能得，今此弊食，佛須者可取！」佛知其心信敬清淨，伸手以鉢受其施食；佛時即笑，出五色光，普照天地，還從眉間相入。阿難合掌長跪白佛：「唯然！世尊！今笑因緣，願聞其意！」

佛告阿難：「汝見老女人信心施佛食不？」阿難言：「見。」佛言：「是老女人施佛食故，十五劫中天上人間受福快樂，不墮惡道；後得男子身，出家學道，成辟支佛，入無餘涅槃。」

English Translation

For example, once, after completing the rains retreat in Śrāvastī, the Buddha traveled through various countries with Ānanda attending him and was about to reach a city of brahmins. Its king knew the Buddha's spiritual power and virtue, and his ability to transform people and move their hearts. He thought, 'If he comes here, who will still take delight in me?' He therefore issued a prohibition: 'Anyone who gives food to the Buddha or listens to his words must pay five hundred gold coins.' After this decree was issued, the Buddha arrived in that country and entered the city with Ānanda, carrying his bowl to seek alms. Everyone in the city shut their doors and refused to respond, so the Buddha left with his bowl empty.

At that time, an elderly servant of one household came out to discard foul-smelling rice-rinsing sediment in a broken earthenware vessel. She saw the Buddha, the World-Honored One, approaching with an empty bowl. Seeing his beautiful marks—his golden complexion, white tuft, crown protuberance, and halo extending one zhang—and his bowl empty of food, she thought, 'Such a wondrous person ought to dine on heavenly food. Yet he humbles himself and carries a bowl to beg. This must be because of his great loving-kindness and compassion for all.' With pure faith, she wished to make a fine offering, but had no way to fulfill her wish. With a sense of shame, she said to the Buddha, 'I wish to prepare an offering, but can obtain nothing better. If you have use for this poor food, please accept it.' Knowing the purity of her faith and reverence, the Buddha held out his bowl and accepted her gift of food. He then smiled and emitted light of five colors, illuminating heaven and earth. The light returned and entered the mark between his eyebrows. Ānanda joined his palms, knelt upright, and said, 'World-Honored One, we wish to hear the meaning of your smile.'

The Buddha said to Ānanda, 'Did you see the elderly woman offer food to the Buddha with faith?' Ānanda replied, 'I did.' The Buddha said, 'Because this elderly woman has offered food to the Buddha, for fifteen kalpas she will enjoy blessings and happiness among devas and human beings, without falling into the bad destinies. Later she will obtain a male body, go forth to cultivate the path, become a pratyekabuddha, and enter nirvāṇa without remainder.'

A Small Cause and a Great Result

Chinese Source

爾時佛邊有一婆羅門立，說偈言：「汝是日種剎利姓，淨飯國王之太子，而以食故大妄語，如此臭食報何重？」

是時，佛出廣長舌，覆面上至髮際，語婆羅門言：「汝見經書，頗有如此舌人而作妄語不？」婆羅門言：「若人舌能覆鼻，言無虛妄，何況乃至髮際？我心信佛必不妄語，不解小施報多如是！」

佛告婆羅門：「汝頗曾見世所希有難見事不？」婆羅門言：「見！我曾共婆羅門道中行，見一尼拘盧陀樹，蔭覆賈客五百乘車，蔭猶不盡，是謂希有難見事也！」佛言：「此樹種子，其形大小？」答言：「大如芥子三分之一。」佛言：「誰當信汝言者？樹大乃爾，而種子甚小！」婆羅門言：「實爾！世尊！我眼見之，非虛妄也。」

佛言：「我亦如是，見老女人淨信心施佛，得大果報，亦如此樹，因少報多；又是如來福田良美之所致也。」婆羅門心開意解，五體投地悔過向佛：「我心無狀，愚不信佛。」佛為種種說法，得初道果；即時舉手大發聲言：「一切眾人！甘露門開，如何不出！」

城中一切諸婆羅門，皆送五百金錢與王，迎佛供養，皆言：「得甘露味，誰當惜此五百金錢！」眾人皆去，制限法破。是婆羅門王亦共臣民歸命佛法，城人一切皆得淨信。如是佛出廣長舌相，為不信者故。

English Translation

A brahmin standing beside the Buddha then spoke this verse: 'You are a kṣatriya of the solar lineage, the crown prince of King Śuddhodana. Yet for the sake of food you tell a great falsehood! How could such foul food bring so great a reward?'

The Buddha extended his broad and long tongue, covering his face up to his hairline, and said to the brahmin, 'In the scriptures you have read, have you ever found that someone with such a tongue tells lies?' The brahmin replied, 'If someone's tongue can cover his nose, his words are not false. How much more so if it reaches his hairline! I believe that the Buddha certainly does not lie, but I do not understand how so small a gift can bring so great a reward.'

The Buddha asked, 'Have you ever seen something in the world that is rare and difficult to encounter?' The brahmin replied, 'Yes. Once, while traveling with other brahmins, I saw a banyan tree whose shade covered five hundred merchants' carts, with shade still to spare. This was something rare and difficult to encounter.' The Buddha asked, 'How large is this tree's seed?' He replied, 'About one-third the size of a mustard seed.' The Buddha said, 'Who would believe what you say? The tree is so large, yet its seed is so small!' The brahmin replied, 'It is truly so, World-Honored One. I saw it with my own eyes; it is no falsehood.'

The Buddha said, 'It is the same with me. I see that this elderly woman, offering to the Buddha with pure faith, will obtain a great result. It is like this tree: a small cause brings a great result. It also comes about because the Tathāgata is such an excellent field of merit.' The brahmin's mind opened and he understood. Prostrating with all five parts of his body touching the ground, he confessed to the Buddha, 'My mind was unruly; in my foolishness I did not believe the Buddha.' The Buddha taught him the Dharma in various ways, and he attained the first fruit of the path. At once he raised his hands and cried aloud, 'Everyone! The gate to the deathless is open. Why do you not come out?'

All the brahmins in the city sent five hundred gold coins each to the king, welcomed the Buddha, and made offerings. They all said, 'Having gained the taste of the deathless, who would begrudge these five hundred gold coins?' Everyone went out, and the prohibition broke down. The brahmin king, together with his ministers and people, also took refuge in the Buddha Dharma, and everyone in the city gained pure faith. Thus the Buddha manifests his broad and long tongue for the sake of those who do not believe.

A Sign Seen throughout the Great Trichiliocosm

Chinese Source

問曰：如為婆羅門出舌相覆面，今舌相光明，何以乃至三千大千世界？

答曰：覆面髮際，為小信故；今為般若波羅蜜大事興故，廣長舌相覆三千大千世界。

問曰：是一城中人，盡得見此覆面舌相，猶尚為難；何況今說摩訶般若波羅蜜，一切大會，此及他方無量眾集而得盡見？又以人目所覩不過數里，今言遍三千大千世界，無乃大而難信！

答曰：佛以方便借其神力，能令一切皆見舌相覆此三千大千世界。若不加神力，雖復十住亦不知佛心；若加神力，乃至畜生能知佛心。如《般若波羅蜜》後品中說：「一切眾人皆見阿閦佛會，與眼作對。」亦如佛說阿彌陀佛世界種種嚴淨。阿難言：「唯願欲見。」佛時即令一切眾會皆見無量壽佛世界嚴淨。見佛舌相，亦復如是。佛以廣長舌相遍覆三千大千世界已，然後便笑。笑因緣，如上說。

English Translation

Question: When he manifested the mark of his tongue for the brahmin, it covered his face. Why does its radiance now reach throughout the great trichiliocosm?

Answer: Covering his face up to the hairline served to establish faith on a small scale. Now, because the great undertaking of Prajñāpāramitā is about to begin, his broad and long tongue covers the great trichiliocosm.

Question: It would already be difficult for everyone in a single city to see his tongue covering his face. How much more difficult is it now, when he teaches the Mahāprajñāpāramitā, for the immeasurable assembly gathered here and from other regions all to see it! Moreover, human eyes see no farther than a few li. To say that it extends throughout the great trichiliocosm—is this not so vast as to be difficult to believe?

Answer: Through skillful means, the Buddha lends them his spiritual power, enabling everyone to see his tongue covering this great trichiliocosm. Without the support of his spiritual power, even bodhisattvas of the tenth abiding cannot know the Buddha's mind. With its support, even animals can know his mind. As a later chapter of the Prajñāpāramitā says, 'Everyone in the assembly saw Akṣobhya Buddha's assembly directly before their eyes.' Likewise, when the Buddha described the many kinds of majestic purity in Amitābha Buddha's world, Ānanda said, 'We wish to see it.' The Buddha then enabled the entire assembly to see the majestic purity of the world of the Buddha of Immeasurable Life. Seeing the Buddha's tongue is also like this. After covering the great trichiliocosm with his broad and long tongue, the Buddha smiled. The reasons for smiling have been explained above.

Golden Lotuses in the Light

Chinese Source

問曰：前已出舌相光明，今何以故舌根復放光明？

答曰：欲令一切得重信故。又以舌相色如珊瑚，金光明淨共相發起，故復放光。復次，是諸光明變成千葉金色寶華。從舌相出此千葉金色寶華，光明徹照，如日初出。

問曰：何以故光明中變化作此寶華？

答曰：佛欲坐故。

問曰：諸床可坐，何必蓮華？

答曰：床為世界白衣坐法。又以蓮華軟淨，欲現神力，能坐其上令花不壞故；又以莊嚴妙法座故；又以諸華皆小，無如此華香淨大者。人中蓮華，大不過尺；漫陀耆尼池及阿那婆達多池中蓮華，大如車蓋；天上寶蓮華復大於此，是則可容結加趺坐。佛所坐華，復勝於此百千萬倍。又如此華華臺，嚴淨香妙可坐。

English Translation

Question: He has already manifested light from his tongue. Why does he now emit light from its root as well?

Answer: He wishes everyone to gain renewed faith. Furthermore, his tongue is coral-colored, and the pure golden light enhances its appearance as it in turn enhances the light. For this reason, he emits light again. Moreover, these rays transform into golden jeweled lotuses with a thousand petals. Emerging from his tongue, these thousand-petaled golden jeweled lotuses shine brilliantly, like the rising sun.

Question: Why does he create these jeweled lotuses within the light?

Answer: Because the Buddhas are going to sit upon them.

Question: There are many couches on which to sit. Why must they be lotuses?

Answer: Couches are the customary seats of householders in the world. Furthermore, lotuses are soft and pure. He wishes to manifest the spiritual power by which he can sit upon them without damaging the flowers. They also adorn the wondrous Dharma seat. Other flowers are small; none is so fragrant, pure, and large as this one. Lotuses in the human realm grow no larger than one chi. Those in Lake Mandākinī and Lake Anavatapta are as large as carriage canopies. Jeweled lotuses in the heavens are larger still, providing room to sit in full lotus posture. The flowers on which the Buddhas sit surpass even these hundreds of thousands of myriads of times. Moreover, their central platforms are splendid, pure, wonderfully fragrant, and suitable as seats.

The Lotus Seat and a Traditional Account of Brahmā

Chinese Source

復次，劫盡燒時，一切皆空；眾生福德因緣力故，十方風至，相對相觸，能持大水；水上有一千頭人，二千手足，名為韋紐；是人臍中出千葉金色妙寶蓮花，其光大明，如萬日俱照；華中有人結加趺坐，此人復有無量光明，名曰梵天王；此梵天王心生八子，八子生天地人民。是梵天王於諸婬瞋已盡無餘，以是故言：「若有人修禪淨行，斷除婬欲，名為行梵道。」佛轉法輪，或名法輪，或名梵輪。是梵天王坐蓮華上，是故諸佛隨世俗故，於寶華上結加趺坐，說六波羅蜜。聞此法者，畢至阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。

English Translation

Furthermore, when the fire at the end of a kalpa has consumed everything, all is empty. Through the power of the conditions created by beings' merit, winds arrive from the ten directions, meet and press against one another, and support a great mass of water. Upon the water is a being with a thousand heads and two thousand hands and feet, named Viṣṇu. From his navel emerges a wondrous golden jeweled lotus with a thousand petals, shining as brightly as ten thousand suns together. In the flower sits a being in full lotus posture, also possessing immeasurable light, called King Brahmā. From his mind this King Brahmā produces eight sons, and the eight sons produce heaven, earth, and human beings. This King Brahmā has completely exhausted lust and anger, leaving none behind. Hence the saying: 'When someone cultivates meditation and pure conduct, cutting off sensual desire, this is called practicing the Brahmā path.' The wheel the Buddha turns is sometimes called the Dharma wheel and sometimes the Brahmā wheel. Because this King Brahmā sits upon a lotus, the Buddhas, following worldly convention, also sit in full lotus posture upon jeweled flowers and teach the six perfections. Those who hear this teaching will ultimately attain unsurpassed complete awakening.

How Manifested Buddhas Can Teach Together

Chinese Source

問曰：釋迦文尼佛化作無量千萬億諸佛，云何一時能說法耶？如《阿毘曇》說：「一時無二心。」若化佛語時，化主應默；化主語時，化亦應默。云何一時皆說六波羅蜜？

答曰：如此說者，外道及聲聞變化法耳。如佛變化，無量三昧力不可思議，是故佛自語時，無量千萬億化佛亦一時皆語。又諸外道及聲聞，化不能作化；如佛世尊，化復作化。諸外道及聲聞，滅後不能留化；如佛世尊自身滅度後，復能留化如佛無異。

復次，《阿毘曇》中，一時無二心；今佛亦如是，當化語時，亦不有心；佛心念化，欲令化語即便皆語。

English Translation

Question: Śākyamuni Buddha creates immeasurable myriads of Buddhas by transformation. How can they all teach the Dharma at the same time? The Abhidharma says, 'There cannot be two minds at one time.' When a manifested Buddha speaks, the one producing the manifestation should be silent; when its producer speaks, the manifestation should be silent. How can all of them teach the six perfections at once?

Answer: What you describe applies only to the transformations of non-Buddhist practitioners and śrāvakas. The Buddha's transformations, empowered by immeasurable samādhis, are inconceivable. Therefore, when the Buddha himself speaks, immeasurable myriads of manifested Buddhas also speak at the same time. Furthermore, the manifestations of non-Buddhist practitioners and śrāvakas cannot produce further manifestations, whereas those of the Buddha, the World-Honored One, can do so. After their death, non-Buddhist practitioners and śrāvakas cannot leave manifestations behind, whereas after his own passing into nirvāṇa, the Buddha can leave manifestations that do not differ from a Buddha.

Furthermore, the Abhidharma states that there cannot be two minds at one time. The Buddha also accords with this here: when the manifestations speak, they do not themselves possess minds. The Buddha brings the manifestations to mind and wishes them to speak, and immediately they all speak.

Prajñāpāramitā and the Six Perfections

Chinese Source

問曰：佛今欲說般若波羅蜜，何以令化佛說六波羅蜜？

答曰：是六波羅蜜及般若波羅蜜，一法無異。是五波羅蜜不得般若波羅蜜，不名波羅蜜。如檀波羅蜜，不得般若波羅蜜，沒在世界有盡法中，或得阿羅漢、辟支佛道般涅槃；若得般若波羅蜜共合，是名波羅蜜，能至佛道。以是故般若波羅蜜與六波羅蜜，一法無異。

般若波羅蜜有二種：一者莊嚴，二者未莊嚴。如人著好瓔珞莊嚴其身，有人不著，名未莊嚴。亦如國王將諸官從，是名王來；若無官從，是名獨身。如是東方如恒河沙等世界，乃至十方亦爾。

English Translation

Question: Since the Buddha is about to teach Prajñāpāramitā, why does he have the manifested Buddhas teach the six perfections?

Answer: The six perfections and Prajñāpāramitā are one teaching, without difference. Without Prajñāpāramitā, the other five are not called perfections. For example, without Prajñāpāramitā, the practice of giving remains submerged among the world's perishable dharmas, or leads to arhatship or the pratyekabuddha path and entry into nirvāṇa. When joined with Prajñāpāramitā, it is called a perfection and can lead to the Buddha path. For this reason, Prajñāpāramitā and the six perfections are one teaching, without difference.

There are two forms of Prajñāpāramitā: adorned and unadorned. It is like someone adorning the body with fine necklaces, while someone who wears none is called unadorned. Or it is like a king accompanied by officials and attendants: this is called the king's arrival; without them, he is said to be alone. This occurs in worlds in the east as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, and likewise in all ten directions.

Why Not Everyone Hears and Attains Liberation

Chinese Source

問曰：若佛有如是大神力，無數千萬億化佛，乃至十方說六波羅蜜度脫一切，應盡得度，不應有殘！

答曰：有三障：三惡道中眾生不能解知；人中、天上，若大小，若大老，若大病；及上無色、無想天——皆不能聞、不能知。

問曰：諸能聞、能知者，何以不皆得道？

答曰：是亦不應盡得道。何以故？結使、業障故。有人於結使重，常為結使覆心，以是故不盡得道。

問曰：當令十方諸佛，亦應遣化說六波羅蜜，我等亦無三障，何以不聞？

答曰：當今眾生，生在惡世，則入三障中！生在佛後，不善業報，或有世界惡罪業障，或有厚重結使障。墮在佛後，人多為厚重結使所障：或婬欲薄而瞋恚厚，瞋恚薄而婬欲厚，婬欲薄而愚癡厚，愚癡薄而瞋恚厚，如是等展轉互有厚薄。是結使障故，不聞、不知化佛說法，不見諸佛光明，何況得道！譬如日出，盲人不見，便謂世間無有日月，日有何咎！又如雷電震地，聾人不聞，聲有何過！今十方諸佛常說經法，常遣化佛至十方世界說六波羅蜜，罪業、盲聾故，不聞法聲；以是故不盡聞見。雖復聖人有大慈心，不能令皆聞、皆見。若罪欲滅、福將生者，是時乃得見佛聞法。

English Translation

Question: If the Buddha has such great spiritual power, and countless myriads of manifested Buddhas teach the six perfections throughout the ten directions to liberate all beings, everyone should be liberated, with no one left over!

Answer: There are three kinds of obstruction: beings in the three bad destinies cannot understand; among humans and devas, some are too young, extremely old, or gravely ill; and there are beings in the higher formless realms and the heaven of non-perception. These cannot hear or understand.

Question: Among those able to hear and understand, why do not all attain the path?

Answer: It does not follow that all of them attain the path either. Why? Because of fetters, underlying tendencies, and karmic obstructions. Some have heavy fetters and underlying tendencies that constantly cover their minds. For this reason, not all attain the path.

Question: The Buddhas of the ten directions should also send manifestations to teach the six perfections. We do not have the three obstructions either, so why do we not hear them?

Answer: Beings today are born in a troubled age and thus fall within the three obstructions! Born after the Buddha, they experience the results of unwholesome karma: some are obstructed by the evil karma of their world, and some by heavy fetters and underlying tendencies. Among those born after the Buddha, many are obstructed by such heavy afflictions. Some have little sensual desire but much anger; some have little anger but much sensual desire; some have little sensual desire but much delusion; and some have little delusion but much anger. In this way their relative strength varies. Because of these obstructions, people neither hear nor understand the manifested Buddhas' teaching, nor see the Buddhas' light, much less attain the path. It is like the sun rising: a blind person does not see it and declares that there are no sun and moon in the world. What fault is there in the sun? Or it is like thunder and lightning shaking the earth: a deaf person does not hear them. What fault is there in the sound? The Buddhas of the ten directions constantly teach the scriptures and Dharma, and constantly send manifested Buddhas to worlds in the ten directions to teach the six perfections. Because of evil karma and blindness and deafness, beings do not hear the sound of the Dharma. For this reason, not everyone hears and sees. Although the noble ones possess great loving-kindness, they cannot make everyone hear and see. When someone's evil karma is about to be exhausted and merit is about to arise, that person can then see the Buddha and hear the Dharma.

Sutra: At that time, the World-Honored One, still on the lion seat, entered the samādhi called Lion's Play. Through his supernormal power, he caused the great trichiliocosm to shake in six ways.

Treatise: Question: Why is this samādhi called 'Lion's Play'?

Answer: It is like a lion pouncing upon deer, freely enjoying its play. The Buddha is also like this. Entering this samādhi, he can turn and move the earth in various ways, causing it to shake in six ways. When he enters it, he causes this great earth to shake in six ways, and all beings in the hells and other bad destinies are released and gain birth in the heavens. This is called play.

Why the Buddha Enters Lion's Play

Chinese Source

問曰：佛何以故入此三昧？

答曰：欲動三千大千世界，出三惡道眾生，著二道中故。

復次，上三種變化，出自佛身，人或信心不深；今動大地，欲令眾生知佛神力無量，能令外物皆動，信淨心喜，皆得離苦。

問曰：有諸阿羅漢及諸天亦能動地，何以獨言是佛神力？

答曰：諸阿羅漢及諸天不能具足動，唯佛世尊能令大地六種震動。

問曰：佛何以故震動三千大千世界？

答曰：欲令眾生知一切皆空、無常故。有諸人言：「大地及日月、須彌、大海，是皆有常。」是以世尊六種動地，示此因緣，令知無常。復次，如人欲染衣，先去塵土；佛亦如是，先令三千世界眾生見佛神力，敬心柔軟，然後說法，是故六種動地。云何六種動？

English Translation

Question: Why does the Buddha enter this samādhi?

Answer: He wishes to shake the great trichiliocosm, bring beings out of the three bad destinies, and establish them in the two good destinies, those of humans and devas.

Furthermore, the three kinds of transformations described above came from the Buddha's own body, and some people's faith may not have deepened. Now he shakes the great earth so that beings will know his spiritual power is immeasurable and can move external things as well. Their faith becomes pure and their hearts joyful, and all gain release from suffering.

Question: Arhats and devas can also shake the earth. Why speak of this as the Buddha's spiritual power alone?

Answer: Arhats and devas cannot shake it in the complete manner. Only the Buddha, the World-Honored One, can make the great earth shake in all six ways.

Question: Why does the Buddha shake the great trichiliocosm?

Answer: He wishes beings to know that everything is empty and impermanent. Some people say, 'The great earth, the sun and moon, Mount Sumeru, and the great ocean are all permanent.' Therefore, the World-Honored One shakes the earth in six ways, showing this occurrence so that they understand impermanence. Furthermore, it is like someone wishing to dye a garment who first removes its dust and dirt. The Buddha is also like this: he first enables beings throughout the great trichiliocosm to see his spiritual power, so that their hearts become reverent and gentle, and afterward he teaches the Dharma. For this reason, he shakes the earth in six ways. What are these six ways?

The Six Ways the Earth Shakes

Chinese Source

〖經〗東涌西沒，西涌東沒；南涌北沒，北涌南沒；邊涌中沒，中涌邊沒。

〖論〗問曰：何以故正有六種動？

答曰：地動有上、中、下。下者，二種動：或東涌西沒，或南湧北沒，或邊中。中者，有四：或東、西、南、北，或東、西、邊、中，或南、北、邊、中。上者，六種動。有種種因緣令地大動，如佛告阿難：「八因八緣，令地震動。」如別說。

English Translation

Sutra: The east rose and the west sank; the west rose and the east sank. The south rose and the north sank; the north rose and the south sank. The edges rose and the center sank; the center rose and the edges sank.

Treatise: Question: Why are there exactly six kinds of shaking?

Answer: Earth movements are greater, intermediate, or lesser. A lesser movement has two aspects: the east may rise while the west sinks, or the south while the north sinks, or there may be movement of the edges and center. An intermediate movement has four aspects: east, west, south, and north; or east, west, edges, and center; or south, north, edges, and center. A greater movement has all six kinds of shaking. Various causes and conditions make the earth shake greatly. As the Buddha told Ānanda, 'There are eight causes and eight conditions that make the earth shake.' These are explained elsewhere.

Great Events and the Teaching of Impermanence

Chinese Source

復次，地動因緣有小有大，有動一閻浮提，有動四天下、一千、二千、三千大千世界。小動以小因緣故：若福德人若生若死，一國地動，是為小動。大動大因緣故：如佛初生時、初成佛時、將滅度時，三千大千世界皆為震動，是為大動。今佛欲大集眾生故，令此地六種震動。

復次，《般若波羅蜜》中授諸菩薩記，當得作佛；佛為天地大主，是時地神大喜：「我今得主！」是故地動。譬如國主初立，臣民喜慶，皆稱萬歲，踊躍歌舞。

復次，三千大千世界眾生福德因緣故，有此大地山河樹木一切眾物，而眾生不知無常！是故佛以福德智慧大力，動此世界眾生福德，令知微薄，一切磨滅，皆歸無常。

English Translation

Furthermore, the causes and conditions of earth movements may be small or great. Some movements affect one Jambudvīpa, some the four continents, and some a small thousandfold, a medium twice-thousandfold, or a great thrice-thousandfold world system. Small movements arise from small causes and conditions. For example, when a meritorious person is born or dies, the earth of one country shakes: this is a small movement. Great movements arise from great causes and conditions. When the Buddha is born, first attains Buddhahood, or is about to enter nirvāṇa, the entire great trichiliocosm shakes: this is a great movement. Now the Buddha wishes to bring together a great assembly of beings, so he makes this earth shake in six ways.

Furthermore, in the Prajñāpāramitā, bodhisattvas receive predictions that they will become Buddhas. A Buddha is the great lord of heaven and earth. At that time the earth deity rejoices greatly, thinking, 'Now I have a lord!' Therefore, the earth shakes. It is like the accession of a ruler, when ministers and people celebrate, cry out 'Long life!', and leap, sing, and dance with joy.

Furthermore, through the causes and conditions of the merit of beings in the great trichiliocosm, there exist this great earth, mountains, rivers, trees, and all other things. Yet beings do not understand impermanence! Therefore, with the great power of his merit and wisdom, the Buddha shakes what is sustained by the merit of beings in this world, showing how slight that merit is: everything wears away and perishes, and all is subject to impermanence.

Soft Ground and Gentle Minds

Chinese Source

〖經〗地皆柔軟，令眾生和悅。

〖論〗問曰：地動，云何能令眾生心得和悅？

答曰：心隨身故，身得樂事，心則欣悅。悅者，共住之人及便身之具，能令心悅。今以是三千大千世界雜惡眾生其心麁獷，無有善事；是故世尊動此大地，令皆柔軟，心得利益。譬如三十三天王歡樂園中，諸天入者，心皆柔軟，歡樂和悅，麁心不生；若阿修羅起兵來時，都無鬪心。是時，釋提婆那民將諸天眾入麁澁園中，以此園中樹木華實氣不和悅，麁澁惡故，諸天人眾鬪心即生。佛亦如是，以此大地麁澁弊惡故，變令柔軟，使一切眾生心得喜悅。又如呪術藥草熏人鼻時，恚心便生，即時鬪諍；復有呪術藥草能令人心和悅歡喜，敬心相向。呪術草藥尚能如此，何況三千大千世界地皆柔軟？

English Translation

Sutra: The ground everywhere became soft, bringing beings harmony and joy.

Treatise: Question: How can the shaking of the earth make beings' minds harmonious and joyful?

Answer: The mind follows the body. When the body encounters something pleasant, the mind rejoices. The people with whom one lives and the things that bring bodily comfort can delight the mind. Now the minds of the many troubled beings in this great trichiliocosm are rough and harsh, without wholesome activity. Therefore, the World-Honored One moves this great earth and makes it soft everywhere, benefiting their minds. It is like the Garden of Joy belonging to the king of the Thirty-three: the devas who enter it become gentle, happy, harmonious, and joyful, and harsh thoughts do not arise. Even if the asuras arrive with an army, they have no wish to fight. At such a time, Śakra, lord of the devas, leads them into the Rough Garden. Because the scent of its trees, flowers, and fruits is unpleasant, harsh, and offensive, the devas' fighting spirit immediately arises. The Buddha is also like this: because this great earth is rough, harsh, and unpleasant, he transforms it and makes it soft, bringing joy to the minds of all beings. Likewise, certain herbs used with spells can provoke anger and immediate quarrelling when their fumes enter people's noses. Other herbs used with spells can make people's minds harmonious and joyful, so that they treat one another with respect. If spells and herbs can do this, how much more can the softening of all the ground in the great trichiliocosm!

Liberation and the Ripening of Merit

Chinese Source

〖經〗是三千大千世界中地獄、餓鬼、畜生及八難處，即時解脫，得生天上，從四天王天處乃至他化自在天處。

English Translation

Sutra: In this great trichiliocosm, beings in the hells, among hungry ghosts and animals, and in the eight difficult circumstances were immediately released and gained birth in the heavens, from the heaven of the Four Heavenly Kings up to the heaven of Mastery over Others' Creations.

Treatise: Question: If the Buddha, by entering the samādhi called Lion's Play, can release beings from the hells, the hungry ghost and animal realms, and the other difficult circumstances, enabling them to be born in the heavens from the Four Heavenly Kings up to Mastery over Others' Creations, why must they cultivate merit and do good in order to obtain such results?

Answer: This is as explained above. Those with abundant merit are liberated upon seeing the light; those whose stains of wrongdoing are deep awaken only when the earth shakes. It is like sunlight falling upon a lotus pond: the mature flowers open first, while the immature ones do not yet unfold. The Buddha is also like this. He first emits light, and those whose merit is ripe and whose wisdom is sharp are liberated first. Those whose merit is not yet ripe and whose understanding is not sharp do not yet attain release. With great loving-kindness and compassion, the Buddha liberates all equally, without aversion or favoritism. It is also like fruit on a tree: when someone shakes the tree, the ripe fruit falls first. The Buddha is also like this. The great trichiliocosm is like the tree, and the Buddha is the one shaking it. Those liberated first are like ripe fruit; those not yet liberated are like unripe fruit.

Question: Why do these wholesome states of mind lead to birth in the desire-realm heavens rather than in the form or formless realms?

Answer: The Buddha wishes to liberate these beings and bring them to realization of the path. In the formless realm, because they have no bodies, he cannot teach them the Dharma. In the form realm, they lack disenchantment and find it difficult to attain the path: because the pleasure of meditation is abundant, their wisdom is dull.

Furthermore, the Buddha moves the world through his supernormal power, making all the ground of the great trichiliocosm soft. Beings develop faith and joy, and therefore are born in the desire-realm heavens. Because they have not practiced the four dhyānas or the four formless attainments, they cannot be born in the form or formless realms.

Without a Self, Who Dies and Who Is Born?

Chinese Source

問曰：五眾無常、空、無我，云何生天人中？誰死誰生者？

答曰：是事，〈讚菩薩品〉中已廣說，今當略答：汝言「五眾無常、空、無我」者，是般若波羅蜜中，五眾無有常無常、有空無空、有我無我。若如外道求索實我，是不可得；但有假名，種種因緣和合而有，有此名字。譬如幻人相殺，人見其死；幻術令起，人見其生；生死名字，有而無實。世界法中實有生死，實相法中無有生死。

佛法相雖空，亦復不斷滅；雖生亦非常，諸行業不失。

諸法如芭蕉，一切從心生；若知法無實，是心亦復空。

若有人念空，是則非道行；諸法不生滅，念有故失相。

Answer: This has already been explained at length in the chapter praising the bodhisattvas. Now we will answer briefly. You say, 'The five aggregates are impermanent, empty, and without a self.' But in Prajñāpāramitā, the five aggregates are neither permanent nor impermanent, neither empty nor nonempty, neither possessed of a self nor without a self. If one searches for a real self as non-Buddhist practitioners do, it cannot be found. There are only provisional designations: various causes and conditions come together, and these names are applied. It is like illusory people killing one another: observers see them die. Through the illusionist's art they rise again, and observers see them come to life. The names 'birth' and 'death' exist, but have no underlying reality. At the level of worldly convention, birth and death do occur; at the level of the true nature of dharmas, there is neither birth nor death.

Furthermore, for those subject to birth and death, there is birth and death; for those no longer subject to them, there is neither. Why? Through great wisdom, those no longer subject to birth and death can break through the appearance of birth. As a verse says:

Though the nature of the Buddha Dharma is empty, it is not annihilation. Though there is arising, there is no permanence; the karma of actions is not lost.

Dharmas are like a plantain trunk; all arise from mind. If one knows that dharmas have no solid reality, this mind, too, is empty.

If someone conceives of emptiness, that is not the practice of the path. Dharmas neither arise nor cease; through conceiving of existence, one loses sight of their nature.

With conceptual fixation, one falls into Māra's net; without it, one escapes. The mind's agitation is not the path; freedom from agitation is the seal of the Dharma.
