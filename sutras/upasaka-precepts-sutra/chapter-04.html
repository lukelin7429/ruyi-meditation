Chapter 4 · 優婆塞戒經 · 解脫品第四 · Why a Handful of Grain Can Outweigh a Lifetime of Giving

The Beggar's Handful, and What It Actually Buys

Continuing directly from the chapter on compassion, the Buddha now names the specific attainment that compassionate practice ripens into: the "portion of liberation," a seed-stage of genuine insight that precedes full awakening. What follows is less a rulebook than a repeated, almost provocative demonstration that quantity has nothing to do with it — a lifetime of giving can fail where a single handful of grain succeeds, and a hell-dweller can outrank a god, depending entirely on the sincerity behind the act. The chapter closes by naming exactly what does determine attainment: four seeds of bodhi, eight supporting practices, and — perhaps most strikingly — the observation that a householder's path to this attainment is harder than a monastic's.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: complete — all 28 chapters translated Source: Taishō T24, no. 1488 Commentary guide: Grand Master Taixu's Lecture Notes on the Sutra on Upasaka Precepts (優婆塞戒經講錄)

1. Milestone: From Compassion to the Portion of Liberation

The chapter opens by picking up directly where the previous chapter on compassion left off: cultivating compassion in body, speech, and mind is what actually ripens into the attainment this chapter is named for — a "portion of liberation," not yet full liberation itself.

Chinese Source

善男子！若善男子、善女人有修悲者，當知是人得一切法體諸解脫分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! If a good son or good daughter cultivates compassion, know that this person attains the liberation-portions inherent in the substance of every dharma.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu notes that "liberation" is a word the tradition uses in several distinct technical senses — one of the three virtues named in the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, the fruit shared by all three vehicles, the three gates of emptiness/signlessness/wishlessness, the eight liberations tied to specific meditative attainments, or the fifty-three separate "gates of liberation" each of Sudhana's teachers claims in the Avataṃsaka. Here, he says, "liberation" points specifically to the resolute understanding (勝解) of the three vehicles together with the concentration and wisdom built on that understanding — a stage that precedes the true seeing of the path itself. Before genuine seeing of the path, this is preparatory wisdom and resolute conviction; only afterward does it become undefiled wisdom proper. Because this stage is what sets the arising of bodhicitta in motion, the tradition classifies it as a "completing cause" — and because it follows directly on the heels of the compassion chapter, Taixu reads this opening line as making the connection explicit: cultivate compassion, and this portion of liberation is what results.

2. Milestone: The Substance of the Path — Body, Speech, and Mind

Asked directly what this "substance" actually consists of, the Buddha answers concretely: not an abstract quality, but body, speech, and mind themselves, cultivated through specific means.

Chinese Source

善生言：「世尊！所言體者，云何為體？」「善男子！謂身、口、意。是身、口、意從方便得。方便有二：一者、耳聞，二者、思惟。復有三種：一者、惠施，二者、持戒，三者、多聞。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Sigāla said: World-Honored One, this "substance" you speak of — what exactly is this substance? Good son! I mean body, speech, and mind. This body, speech, and mind are attained through skillful means. There are two such means: first, hearing with the ear; second, reflection. There are also three further kinds: first, giving; second, upholding precepts; third, learning widely.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu reads "means" (方便) here as preparatory effort in the plainest sense — the same idea behind the saying that a craftsman must first sharpen his tools before he can do good work. Just as receiving the precepts through a formal ritual act is what actually produces the "precept-substance" a monastic then carries, this liberation-portion's substance is likewise produced through disciplined use of body, speech, and mind. "Hearing" here means the wisdom born of hearing the teaching; "reflection" means the wisdom born of thinking it through — a third kind, wisdom born of actual meditative cultivation, is implied as the natural next step. Of the threefold means that follow, Taixu further divides giving itself into three kinds: giving out of compassion to those who suffer, giving out of gratitude in return for kindness received, and giving out of reverence toward Buddhas, bodhisattvas, and teachers — each of which, along with precepts and broad learning, generates the specific wisdom this chapter is tracking, a wisdom the tradition calls a "completing cause" because it is what actually gives rise to bodhicitta.

3. Milestone: No Fixed Measure — It Is the Mind That Decides

Pressed on whether these three means require some minimum quantity, the Buddha's answer is emphatic: there is no fixed measure at all. What determines attainment is entirely the quality of mind behind the act, not its scale.

Chinese Source

善生言：「世尊！如佛所說從三方便得解脫分，是三方便有定數不？」「不也。善男子！何以故？有人雖於無量世中以無量財施無量人，亦不能得解脫分法；有人於一時中以一把施一乞兒，能得如是解脫分法。有人乃於無量佛所受持禁戒，亦不能得解脫分法；有人一日一夜受持八戒而能獲得解脫分法。有人於無量世無量佛所，受持讀誦十二部經，亦不能得解脫分法；有人唯讀一四句偈，而能獲得解脫分法。何以故？一切眾生心不同故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Sigāla said: World-Honored One, as the Buddha has taught, the portion of liberation is attained through these three means — do these three means have a fixed measure? No, good son. Why? There are people who, across countless ages, give countless riches to countless people, and yet still cannot attain the dharma of liberation-portion; and there are people who, in a single moment, give a single handful to a single beggar, and thereby attain this very dharma of liberation-portion. There are people who uphold the precepts before countless Buddhas and yet cannot attain the dharma of liberation-portion; and there are people who uphold the eight precepts for a single day and night and thereby obtain the dharma of liberation-portion. There are people who, across countless ages before countless Buddhas, uphold, read, and recite the twelvefold canon and yet cannot attain the dharma of liberation-portion; and there are people who recite only a single four-line verse and thereby obtain the dharma of liberation-portion. Why is this? Because the minds of all sentient beings are not the same.

Chinese Source

「善男子！若人不能一心觀察生死過咎、涅槃安樂，如是之人，雖復惠施、持戒、多聞，終不能得解脫分法。若能厭患生死過咎、深見涅槃功德安樂，如是之人，雖復少施、少戒、少聞，即能獲得解脫分法。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! If a person cannot single-mindedly contemplate the faults of birth-and-death and the peace and bliss of nirvāṇa, then even if such a person gives, upholds precepts, and learns widely, he will never attain the dharma of liberation-portion. But if he can grow weary of and repelled by the faults of birth-and-death, and deeply perceive the virtue, peace, and bliss of nirvāṇa, then even if such a person gives little, keeps few precepts, and learns little, he will immediately obtain the dharma of liberation-portion.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu reads this entire exchange as a single point made twice: what matters is never the amount of giving, precept-keeping, or learning, but where the mind is actually aimed. Merit dedicated toward mundane reward — rebirth as a human or a god — does not produce the liberation-portion, no matter how large the act; merit dedicated toward genuinely leaving birth-and-death behind produces it even from a small act. He extends the point to meditation practice itself: even a dedicated practitioner of Buddha-recitation or seated meditation who reaches real states of concentration, if that concentration is never turned toward bodhi and never gives rise to genuinely undefiled wisdom, has attained nothing more than the ordinary four dhyānas or four formless absorptions available to any meditator — worldly attainments, not liberation. The single handful given to a beggar succeeds precisely because sincerity, not scale, is what the portion of liberation actually tracks.

4. Milestone: The Three Times When Liberation Can Be Heard

The Buddha next specifies exactly when this attainment becomes available at all — not at any moment, but during one of three specific windows, illustrated by the Buddha's own account of his first resolve toward enlightenment.

Chinese Source

「善男子！得是法者，於三時中——佛出世時；緣覺出時；若無是二，阿迦尼咤天說解脫時——是人聞已得解脫分。善男子！我於往昔初發心時，都不見佛及辟支佛，聞淨居天說解脫法，我時聞已，即便發心。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! A person attains this dharma during one of three times: when a Buddha is in the world; when a pratyekabuddha appears; or, if neither of these is present, when the gods of Akaniṣṭha Heaven expound liberation — hearing it at such a time, a person attains the portion of liberation. Good son! When I myself first resolved on enlightenment in ages past, I had not yet seen any Buddha or pratyekabuddha at all; I heard the gods of the Pure Abodes expound the dharma of liberation, and upon hearing it, I resolved my mind then and there.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu widens "when a Buddha is in the world" to cover any period in which the Buddha's own teaching and scriptures still circulate, whether or not a living Buddha is physically present; the second window, "when a pratyekabuddha appears," covers periods when no such teaching survives at all — pratyekabuddhas do not actively preach, but their evident spiritual power and dignified bearing alone are enough to move onlookers toward genuine renunciation. Akaniṣṭha here refers to the Pure Abodes, the five non-returner heavens where those who have already attained the third fruit of arhatship, or bodhisattvas of advanced rank, dwell. Taixu ties the Buddha's own account directly to a well-known episode from his life as Prince Siddhārtha: the ascetic he encountered outside the palace gates, whose teaching on liberation first moved him to renounce the world, is understood in the tradition as a manifestation-body sent by these very Pure Abode gods.

5. Milestone: Who Can Attain It, Who Cannot

Having specified when, the Buddha now specifies who — ruling out, somewhat surprisingly, most of the heavenly realms, and confirming that only three kinds of beings can actually reach this attainment.

Chinese Source

「善男子！如是之法，非欲界天之所能得。何以故？以放逸故。亦非色天之所能得。何以故？無三方便故。亦非無色天之所能得。何以故？無身、口故。是法體者，是身、口、意。欝單曰人亦所不得。何以故？無三方便故。是解脫分，三人能得，所謂聲聞、緣覺、菩薩。眾生若遇善知識者，轉聲聞解脫得緣覺解脫，轉緣覺解脫得菩薩解脫；菩薩所得解脫分法，不可退轉，不可失壞。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! This dharma cannot be attained by the gods of the desire realm. Why? Because they are heedless and indulgent. Nor can it be attained by the gods of the form realm. Why? Because they lack the three means. Nor can it be attained by the gods of the formless realm. Why? Because they lack body and speech. The substance of this dharma is body, speech, and mind. Nor can the people of Uttarakuru attain it. Why? Because they too lack the three means. This portion of liberation can be attained by three kinds of persons: śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and bodhisattvas. If a sentient being encounters a good spiritual friend, the liberation of a śrāvaka can turn into the liberation of a pratyekabuddha, and the liberation of a pratyekabuddha can turn into the liberation of a bodhisattva; but the dharma of liberation-portion once attained by a bodhisattva can never regress, and can never be lost or destroyed.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu reads this passage as establishing the human realm as the only real ground for genuine seeing of the path. Desire-realm gods, absorbed in pleasure, never actually practice giving, precepts, or learning in any meaningful sense; formless-realm beings lack body and speech altogether, so the threefold substance of body-speech-mind simply cannot be completed there. Uttarakuru people, meanwhile, enjoy such surpassing worldly reward — no poverty, no early death — that giving, precepts, and learning serve no practical purpose for them at all, and the very absence of need removes the occasion to cultivate these three means in the first place. Taixu also draws out the upward mobility built into the passage: the three kinds of persons capable of attainment are not fixed castes but stages that can be crossed from one into the next, śrāvaka liberation ripening into pratyekabuddha liberation, and that in turn into bodhisattva liberation, which alone, once attained, can never be lost.

6. Milestone: Recognizing Who Has Truly Attained It

Sigāla now asks the practical question a teacher actually needs answered: how does one tell, from the outside, who has attained this portion of liberation and who merely appears to?

Chinese Source

善生言：「世尊！說法之人，復以何義能善分別如是等人有解脫分？如是等人無解脫分？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Sigāla said: World-Honored One, by what principle can a teacher of the dharma properly distinguish which people have the portion of liberation and which do not?

Chinese Source

「善男子！如是法者，二人所得，謂在家、出家。如是二人，至心聽法，聽已受持，聞三惡苦心生怖畏，身毛皆竪，涕泣橫流，堅持齋戒，乃至小罪不敢毀犯，當知是人得解脫分法。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! This dharma can be attained by two kinds of people: householders and renunciants. When such a person listens to the dharma with a truly sincere mind, and having heard it, upholds it; when, on hearing of the suffering of the three evil destinies, fear arises in the mind, the hairs of the body stand on end, and tears stream down uncontrollably; when he firmly holds to the fasting precepts and dares not violate even the smallest transgression — know that this person has attained the dharma of liberation-portion.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu underlines "sincere mind" as the operative phrase: it means not merely hearing the teaching but actually carrying it into practice afterward. The visceral fear described here — hair standing on end, uncontrollable tears — is not treated as an overreaction but as an appropriate response to genuinely understanding that the sufferings of hell, hungry ghosts, and animal rebirth all arise directly from the ten unwholesome deeds: killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech, and the rest. Someone who feels that fear in earnest, and consequently guards even the smallest precept without exception, is showing the outward mark of an inward attainment — which is exactly the observable test a teacher can actually use.

7. Milestone: Among the Five Destinies — Udraka and Devadatta

The Buddha now offers two startling test cases, drawn from beings at opposite extremes of worldly and karmic standing, to show that this attainment overturns every ordinary measure of status.

Chinese Source

「善男子！諸外道等獲得非想非非想定，壽無量劫，若不能得解脫分法，當觀是人為地獄人。若復有人阿鼻地獄經無量劫，受大苦惱，能得如是解脫分法，當觀是人為涅槃人。善男子！是故我於欝頭藍弗生哀愍心，於提婆達多不生憐念心。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Even among heterodox practitioners who attain the concentration of neither-perception-nor-non-perception and live for countless kalpas, if they have not attained the dharma of liberation-portion, they should be regarded as beings of hell. And even a person who has endured immense suffering in the Avīci hell for countless kalpas, if he has attained this dharma of liberation-portion, should be regarded as a person of nirvāṇa. Good son! This is why I feel sorrow and pity for Udraka Rāmaputra, yet feel no such pity for Devadatta.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu explains the logic behind each figure. A heterodox practitioner who reaches even the highest formless absorption will still fall back into saṃsāra, likely into hell, the moment that immense but temporary karmic reward is exhausted — hence "hell-being" even at the height of apparent attainment. A hell-dweller who already carries genuine resolute understanding, by contrast, is already effectively a "person of nirvāṇa," whatever their present circumstance. Udraka Rāmaputra was the most accomplished non-Buddhist teacher of his era; after his own awakening, the Buddha wished to seek him out and teach him, only to learn that Udraka had already been reborn into the very heaven of neither-perception-nor-non-perception this passage describes. Seeing that Udraka — who, while meditating by a river, had grown so resentful of fish and birds disturbing his concentration that he vowed to be reborn as an otter that preys on them — was consequently destined for an animal rebirth in his next life and hell in the one after that, the Buddha felt genuine sorrow for him. Devadatta, by contrast, though condemned to hell for crimes against the Buddha and the saṅgha, is prophesied to eventual Buddhahood in the Lotus Sūtra's Devadatta chapter — meaning he already possesses the liberation-portion this chapter is about, and so needs no pity at all.

8. Milestone: Śāriputra's Regression and the Three Grades of the Teaching

The Buddha turns to a case closer to home — his own great disciple Śāriputra — to distinguish genuine attainment from mere outward discipline, and to place this dharma within a threefold hierarchy running from śrāvaka to Buddha.

Chinese Source

「善男子！如舍利弗等六萬劫中求菩提道，所以退者，以其未得解脫分法；雖爾，猶勝緣覺根利。善男子！是法有三，謂下、中、上。下者聲聞，中者緣覺，上者佛也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Even Śāriputra and others sought the path of enlightenment for sixty thousand kalpas; the reason they regressed was that they had not yet attained the dharma of liberation-portion. Even so, this still surpasses the sharp faculties of a pratyekabuddha. Good son! This dharma has three grades: lower, middle, and upper. The lower is the śrāvaka; the middle is the pratyekabuddha; the upper is the Buddha.

Chinese Source

「善男子！有人勤求優婆塞戒，於無量世如聞而行，亦不得戒，有出家人求比丘戒、比丘尼戒，於無量世如聞而行，亦不能得。何以故？不能獲得解脫分法。是故可名修戒，不名持戒。善男子！若諸菩薩得解脫分法，終不造業求生欲界、色、無色界，常願生於益眾生處；若自定知有生天業，即迴此業求生人中。業者，所謂施、戒、修定。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! There are people who diligently seek the upāsaka precepts, and who, across countless ages, practice exactly as they have heard, and yet still do not attain the precepts. There are renunciants who seek the precepts of a bhikṣu or bhikṣuṇī, and who, across countless ages, practice exactly as they have heard, and yet still cannot attain them. Why is this? Because they have not been able to attain the dharma of liberation-portion. For this reason such practice can only be called "cultivating precepts," not "upholding precepts." Good son! If bodhisattvas have attained the dharma of liberation-portion, they will never again create karma seeking rebirth in the desire realm, the form realm, or the formless realm; they constantly vow to be reborn in places where they can benefit sentient beings. If they know for certain that they hold karma leading to rebirth in a heaven, they will redirect that karma to seek rebirth among human beings instead. By "karma" here I mean giving, upholding precepts, and cultivating meditative concentration.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu reads Śāriputra's sixty thousand kalpas as proof of how exalted the bodhisattva path remains even where it falls short: Śāriputra had already attained Mahāyāna resolute understanding, and his eventual regression still outranks a pratyekabuddha's sharp faculties. The distinction between "cultivating precepts" and "upholding precepts" is, for Taixu, the crux of this whole passage: without the liberation-portion behind it, even a lifetime of scrupulous rule-following never rises above outward cultivation — it never becomes the deeper "precepts bound to concentration" or "precepts bound to the path" that mark real attainment. He also notes the practical logic behind redirecting heaven-bound karma toward human rebirth: the human realm, precisely because it mixes suffering with the capacity for reflection, is simply the easiest realm in which to teach and be taught — which is why a bodhisattva who could ascend to a heaven instead chooses, deliberately, to be born human again.

9. Milestone: The Tempered Ground, the Four Kinds of People, and the Ground of Joy

Having distinguished the three grades of this teaching, the Buddha now names the specific ground a bodhisattva reaches upon attainment, and maps four broader types of people against the current of birth-and-death itself.

Chinese Source

「善男子！若聲聞人得解脫分，不過三身得具解脫；辟支佛人，亦復如是；菩薩摩訶薩得解脫分，雖復經由無量身，中常不退轉，不退轉心，出勝一切聲聞、緣覺。善男子！若得如是解脫分法，雖復少施，得無量果；少戒、少聞亦復如是。是人假使處三惡道，終不同彼三惡受苦。若諸菩薩獲得如是解脫分法，名調柔地。何故名為調柔地耶？一切煩惱漸微弱故，是名逆流。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! If a śrāvaka attains the portion of liberation, he will attain complete liberation within no more than three more bodies; the same is true of a pratyekabuddha. But when a bodhisattva-mahāsattva attains the portion of liberation, even though he passes through countless bodies, his mind of non-regression remains constant throughout, and this non-regressing mind surpasses that of every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha. Good son! Once a person has attained this dharma of liberation-portion, even a small act of giving yields boundless fruit, and the same is true of even a little precept-keeping or a little learning. Even were such a person to fall into one of the three evil destinies, he would never suffer there as other beings do. When bodhisattvas attain this dharma of liberation-portion, it is called the Tempered Ground. Why is it called the Tempered Ground? Because all afflictions gradually grow weak there; this is also called "turning against the stream."

Chinese Source

「若善男子！有四種人：一者、順生死流，二者、逆生死流，三者、不順不逆，四者、到於彼岸。善男子！如是法者，於聲聞人名柔軟地，於諸菩薩亦名柔軟，復名喜地。以何義故名為喜地？聞不退故，名為喜地。以何義故名為菩薩？能常覺悟眾生心故。如是菩薩雖知外典，自不受持，亦不教人。如是菩薩不名人天，非五道攝，是名修行無障礙道。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! There are four kinds of people: first, those who follow along with the stream of birth-and-death; second, those who turn against the stream of birth-and-death; third, those who neither follow nor turn against it; fourth, those who have reached the far shore. Good son! This dharma, for a śrāvaka, is called the Soft Ground; for bodhisattvas it is likewise called the Soft Ground, and also the Ground of Joy. Why is it called the Ground of Joy? Because, having heard it, one does not regress — that is why it is called the Ground of Joy. And why is one called a bodhisattva? Because one is able constantly to awaken the minds of sentient beings. Such a bodhisattva, even though he may know the teachings of outside paths, neither upholds them himself nor teaches them to others. Such a bodhisattva cannot be classed among gods and men, nor is he included among the five destinies; this is called practicing the path free of obstruction.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu notes that a stream-enterer who has the liberation-portion reaches nirvāṇa within at most three more lifetimes rather than the seven traditionally required by duller faculties — the liberation-portion itself is what accelerates the timeline. He offers a striking illustration of merit unbound from ordinary measure: even Devadatta, suffering in hell, is said to have told Ānanda, when asked whether he suffered there, that he felt a bliss comparable to the third dhyāna — a direct result of the meritorious resolute-understanding he still carries. The "Tempered Ground" corresponds, in Taixu's mapping, to the stage of resolute conduct at or approaching the first ground proper; the four kinds of people track, in order, ordinary beings carried along by the current, the two vehicles who row against it, bodhisattvas who navigate without either following or opposing it outright, and Buddhas, who have already reached the far shore and now use nirvāṇa's own wondrous function to ferry others across. The Ground of Joy, he explains, is the genuine first ground: before reaching it, one is a bodhisattva "in name only"; from this point onward, one is a bodhisattva in substance and reality.

10. Milestone: The Four Seeds of Bodhi and the Eight Dharmas That Nurture Them

Having named the ground a bodhisattva reaches, the Buddha now supplies what amounts to a cultivation manual: the four seeds from which bodhi actually grows, and the eight further practices — five, then six, then two sets of four — that make those seeds flourish.

Chinese Source

「善男子！夫菩提者有四種子：一者、不貪財物，二者、不惜身命，三者、修行忍辱，四者、憐愍眾生。善男子！增長如是菩提種子，復有五事：一者、於己身中不生輕想，言我不能得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提；二者、自身受苦，心不厭悔；三者、勤行精進，不休不息；四者、救濟眾生無量苦惱；五者、常讚三寶微妙功德。有智之人修菩提時，常當修集如是五事。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Bodhi has four seeds: first, not being greedy for wealth and possessions; second, not begrudging one's own body and life; third, cultivating patience; fourth, having compassion for sentient beings. Good son! There are also five further things that make these seeds of bodhi grow: first, not belittling oneself by thinking, "I cannot attain unsurpassed, perfect enlightenment"; second, when one's own body suffers, the mind feels no weariness or regret; third, diligently exerting effort without rest or pause; fourth, rescuing sentient beings from their boundless suffering and affliction; fifth, constantly praising the subtle and wondrous virtues of the Three Jewels. A wise person, when cultivating bodhi, should constantly practice and gather these five things.

Chinese Source

「增長熾然菩提種子，復有六事，所謂檀波羅蜜乃至般若波羅蜜。是六種事因一事增，謂不放逸；菩薩放逸，不能增長如是六事，若不放逸，則能增長。善男子！菩薩求於菩提之時，復有四事：一者、親近善友，二者、心堅難壞，三者、能行難行，四者、憐愍眾生。復有四事：一者、見他得利心生歡喜，二者、常樂稱讚他人功德，三者、常樂修集六念處法，四者、勤說生死所有過咎。善男子！若有說言離是八法得菩提者，無有是處。」

Ru-Yi Translation

To make the seeds of bodhi grow and blaze up further, there are also six things — namely, the perfection of giving up through the perfection of wisdom. These six things all grow through a single further thing: not being heedless. If a bodhisattva is heedless, he cannot make these six things grow; only if he is not heedless can he make them grow. Good son! When a bodhisattva seeks bodhi, there are also four further things: first, drawing near to good spiritual friends; second, a mind so firm it cannot be broken; third, being able to carry out what is difficult to carry out; fourth, having compassion for sentient beings. There are also four more: first, feeling joy on seeing another person gain some benefit; second, constantly delighting in praising the virtues of others; third, constantly delighting in cultivating and gathering the six recollections; fourth, diligently speaking of all the faults inherent in birth-and-death. Good son! If anyone should say that bodhi can be attained apart from these eight dharmas, there is no such possibility.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu makes a point of showing that the four seeds are not a separate topic from what has come before: they are compassion itself, cultivated through body, speech, and mind, and so cultivating these seeds is precisely what the earlier chapter on compassion was already describing. The five practices that follow are what allow those seeds to take root; the six that follow — mapped directly onto the six pāramitās, giving through wisdom — all grow, Taixu stresses, from a single shared condition: freedom from heedlessness. Without it, none of the six can develop no matter how sincerely they are intended; with it, all six develop together. The two further sets of four bring the total to eight, and Taixu underlines the Buddha's closing claim as deliberately absolute — there is, on this account, no alternate route to bodhi that bypasses these eight.

11. Milestone: The Newly Resolved Bodhisattva as Supreme Field of Merit

A brief but striking claim follows: the moment bodhicitta is first resolved upon, before any of the preceding practices have been fully carried out, that resolve alone already confers an extraordinary status.

Chinese Source

「善男子！若有菩薩初發無上菩提心時，即得名為無上福田，如是菩薩出勝一切世間之事及諸眾生。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! The moment a bodhisattva first resolves upon unsurpassed bodhi, he is immediately worthy of the name "unsurpassed field of merit"; such a bodhisattva already surpasses every worldly affair and every sentient being.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu explains this in terms of underlying principle rather than actual attainment: in terms of the buddha-nature every being already possesses, a being who has only just resolved upon bodhicitta is, at the level of principle, already no different from a Buddha. It is precisely this identity in principle — not any claim of completed practice — that entitles even the newly resolved bodhisattva to the name "unsurpassed field of merit."

12. Milestone: Why Buddhahood Is So Hard to Attain

The chapter turns to a question that could easily unsettle the confidence just built up: if worlds and Buddhas are truly countless, why is Buddhahood still described as almost impossibly rare? The Buddha's answer works through the logic of infinity itself, then explains a puzzling silence in his own earlier teaching.

Chinese Source

「善男子！雖有人言無量世界有無量佛，然此佛道甚為難得。何以故？世界無邊，眾生亦爾；眾生無邊，佛亦如是。假使佛道當易得者，一佛世尊則應化度一切眾生！若爾者，世界眾生則為有邊。善男子！佛出世時，能度九萬九那由他人，聲聞弟子度一那由他，而諸眾生猶不可盡，故名無邊。是故我於聲聞經說無十方佛。所以者何？恐諸眾生輕佛道故——諸佛聖道非世所攝，是故如來說無虛妄，如來世尊無有妬心——以難得故，說無十方諸佛世尊。善男子！無量眾生發菩提心，不能究竟行菩薩道。若人難言：若有現在無量諸佛，何故經中但說過去、未來二世有無量佛，不說現在無量佛耶？善男子！我一國說過去、未來有恒沙佛，現在世中唯一佛耳。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Although some say that countless worlds have countless Buddhas, this path to Buddhahood is still exceedingly hard to attain. Why? Because worlds are boundless, and sentient beings are likewise so; sentient beings are boundless, and so, in the same way, are Buddhas. Suppose the path to Buddhahood were something easily attained — then a single World-Honored Buddha ought to be able to teach and liberate every single sentient being! But if that were so, worlds and sentient beings would then have to be finite. Good son! When a Buddha appears in the world, he is able to liberate nine hundred ninety thousand koṭis of beings, and his śrāvaka disciples liberate one koṭi more — and yet sentient beings still cannot be exhausted; this is why they are called boundless. This is exactly why, in the śrāvaka scriptures, I taught that there are no Buddhas in the ten directions. Why did I teach this? I feared that sentient beings would come to regard the path to Buddhahood lightly — the sagely path of the Buddhas is not something the world can fully contain, and so the Tathāgata's teaching here holds no falsehood; the World-Honored Tathāgata harbors no jealousy — it is precisely because Buddhahood is so hard to attain that I taught there are no Buddha World-Honored Ones in the ten directions. Good son! Countless sentient beings resolve upon bodhi, yet cannot carry the bodhisattva path through to its very end. Suppose someone raises this challenge: if countless Buddhas exist right now in the present, why do the scriptures speak only of countless Buddhas in the past and future, and never speak of countless Buddhas existing now, in the present? Good son! In any single world-realm, I teach that there are Buddhas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges in the past and in the future — but in the present time, there is only a single Buddha.

Chinese Source

「善男子！真實義者能得佛道，無量眾生修行佛道多有退轉，時有一人乃能得度，如菴羅花及魚子等。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! Only those who reach the truly real meaning can attain the path to Buddhahood. Among the countless sentient beings who practice this path, most fall away and regress; only once in a great while does a single person manage to cross over and be liberated — just as the mango tree bears many blossoms but few fruit, and fish lay countless eggs but few become fish.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu treats the boundlessness argument as strict logic rather than rhetorical flourish: if a single Buddha's liberating power — vast as it is — still cannot exhaust the beings in even one world, and worlds themselves are without limit, then both worlds and beings, and by extension Buddhas among them, are all genuinely infinite together; none of the three can be made finite without making all three finite. On the puzzling silence about "Buddhas of the ten directions" in the earlier, śrāvaka-oriented scriptures, Taixu reads the Buddha's own explanation at face value: this was a deliberate teaching choice made to guard beings from the complacency of assuming Buddhahood is common and therefore not worth genuine striving, not a denial made out of envy or falsehood. He treats the later apparent puzzle — scriptures naming past and future Buddhas but rarely present ones — the same way: a framing convention suited to a particular audience's capacity, not a final metaphysical claim that no other Buddha exists right now. The closing image, of countless mango blossoms yielding only a fruit or two, and countless fish eggs yielding only a fish or two, is for Taixu the chapter's plainest statement yet of why Buddhahood is precious: not because few beings aspire to it, but because so few of those who aspire actually complete the path.

13. Milestone: The Household Bodhisattva's Harder Path

The chapter closes with a pointed observation that runs against any assumption that renunciation is simply the harder, more admirable choice: for this particular attainment, it is the householder's path that is actually more difficult.

Chinese Source

「善男子！菩薩有二種：一者、在家，二者、出家。出家菩薩得解脫分法，是不為難；在家得者，是乃為難。何以故？在家之人，多惡因緣所纏遶故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! There are two kinds of bodhisattvas: householders and renunciants. For a renunciant bodhisattva, attaining the dharma of liberation-portion is not difficult; for a householder to attain it, however, is truly difficult. Why? Because a person living at home is entangled by so many adverse conditions.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu reads this closing line as the chapter's deliberate final word rather than an afterthought: a householder is inevitably surrounded by far more adverse entanglements — family obligations, livelihood, social ties — than a renunciant ever has to contend with, which is exactly why the sutra singles out the householder's attainment of this dharma as the harder-won and, by that measure, the more praiseworthy accomplishment. Coming at the close of a chapter otherwise devoted to Sigāla, himself a householder, the observation reads less as a caveat than as an honor deliberately extended to the very audience this sutra as a whole is addressed to.
