Chapter 21 · 優婆塞戒經 · 八戒齋品第二十一 · The one-day fast that outvalues four kingdoms' treasure and a century under Maitreya

One Day and Night, Beyond All Treasure

Shansheng asks a direct question: what reward actually comes to someone who takes the three refuges and then the eight-precepts fast? The Buddha's answer opens with almost extravagant proof — four kingdoms, each guarding a treasure-vault so vast that no one could ever carry it all away, and every one of those vaults still outweighed by the merit of a single sincere reception of the fast. From there the chapter turns practical: exactly how the one-day, one-night fast is received, why it must come from another person and never before a Buddha image alone, why two people cannot receive it together, and why even a ruler already in the middle of ordering an execution is not beyond its reach. It closes by comparing eras — a single day now worth more than a hundred years under a future Buddha — and by naming which kind of bodhisattva, lay or monastic, finds this particular teaching hardest to pass on.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Status: complete — all 28 chapters translated Source: Taishō T24, no. 1488 Commentary guide: Grand Master Taixu's Lecture Notes on the Sutra on Upasaka Precepts (優婆塞戒經講錄)

1. Milestone: Shansheng's Question

The chapter opens with Shansheng asking exactly what reward follows from a specific pair of acts — not refuge alone, but refuge followed by the fast — a question that only makes sense coming directly after the sutra's preceding chapter on the pure three refuges.

Chinese Source

善生言：「世尊！若有人能受三歸齋戒，是人當得何等果報？」

Ru-Yi Translation

Shansheng said: "World-Honored One, if there is a person able to receive the three refuges and the fast precepts, what reward will this person obtain?"

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu glosses the intent behind the question quite precisely: Shansheng is asking specifically about the reward that follows for someone who, having already received the three refuges, goes on to also receive the eight-precepts fast built on top of them — not a general question about refuge by itself. Read this way, the question is a deliberate bridge from the chapter just before it, carrying the sutra's discussion of refuge forward into its next concrete application.

2. Milestone: Four Kingdoms' Treasure, and a Reward That Outshines It

The Buddha answers first with scale rather than argument: four named kingdoms, each holding a treasure-vault so immense that its entire population, working for years on end with every cart and pack-animal available, still cannot carry it all away — and every one of these vaults is declared to fall short of what a single sincere reception of the three-refuge fast delivers.

Chinese Source

「善男子！若人能受三歸依者，當知是人所得福報不可窮盡！

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son! If a person is able to receive the three refuges, know that the blessed reward this person obtains is inexhaustible!

Chinese Source

善男子！迦陵伽國有七寶藏，名賓伽羅，其國人民——大、小、男、女——於七日中、七月、七年，常以車乘、象、馬、駝、驢擔負持去，猶不能盡。若有至心受三歸齋，是人所得功德果報，出勝彼藏所有寶物。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! In the country of Kalinga there is a treasure-vault of seven precious things called Bingala. The people of that country — great and small, men and women — carrying it away for seven days, seven months, or seven years, constantly loading it onto carts, elephants, horses, camels, and donkeys, still cannot exhaust it. Yet if there is a person who receives the three-refuge fast with utmost sincerity of heart, the meritorious reward this person obtains surpasses every treasure held in that vault.

Chinese Source

善男子！毘提呵國有七寶藏，名半陸迦，其國人民——男、女、大、小——於七日中、七月、七年，常以車乘、象、馬、駝、驢擔負持去，猶不能盡。若有至心受三歸齋，是人所得功德果報，出勝彼藏所有寶物。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! In the country of Videha there is a treasure-vault of seven precious things called Bandaraka. The people of that country — men and women, great and small — carrying it away for seven days, seven months, or seven years, constantly loading it onto carts, elephants, horses, camels, and donkeys, still cannot exhaust it. Yet if there is a person who receives the three-refuge fast with utmost sincerity of heart, the meritorious reward this person obtains surpasses every treasure held in that vault.

Chinese Source

善男子！波羅㮈國有七寶藏，名曰蠰佉，其國人民——男、女、大、小——於七日中、七月、七年，常以車乘、象、馬、駝、驢擔負持去，亦不能盡。若有至心受三歸齋，是人所得功德果報，出勝彼藏所有寶物。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! In the country of Varanasi there is a treasure-vault of seven precious things called Shankha. The people of that country — men and women, great and small — carrying it away for seven days, seven months, or seven years, constantly loading it onto carts, elephants, horses, camels, and donkeys, likewise cannot exhaust it. Yet if there is a person who receives the three-refuge fast with utmost sincerity of heart, the meritorious reward this person obtains surpasses every treasure held in that vault.

Chinese Source

善男子！乾陀羅國有七寶藏，名伊羅鉢多，其國人民——男、女、大、小——於七日中、七月、七年，常以車乘、象、馬、駝、驢擔負持去，亦不能盡。若有至心受三歸齋，是人所得功德果報，勝出彼藏所有寶物。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! In the country of Gandhara there is a treasure-vault of seven precious things called Airavata. The people of that country — men and women, great and small — carrying it away for seven days, seven months, or seven years, constantly loading it onto carts, elephants, horses, camels, and donkeys, likewise cannot exhaust it. Yet if there is a person who receives the three-refuge fast with utmost sincerity of heart, the meritorious reward this person obtains surpasses every treasure held in that vault.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu identifies this passage as the classical "four similes revealing the virtue" and glosses each name in turn. Kalinga takes its name from a bird plentiful in that land, and its vault Bingala means "blue eyes." Videha means "surpassing body," and its vault Bandaraka means "not male." Varanasi is the very city where the Buddha first turned the wheel of the Dharma, its name traditionally rendered "the river-encircled city," and its vault carries the separate name Shankha. Gandhara, in ancient northern India, means "pervading fragrance," and its vault Airavata borrows the name of a great elephant.

He otherwise lets the fourfold repetition make its own case: the identical claim, restated four times across four different kingdoms and four different named vaults, so that no single vault's exceptional size can be mistaken for a one-off exaggeration. The point being pressed is cumulative rather than singular — this is true everywhere, not just somewhere.

3. Milestone: How the One-Day, One-Night Fast Is Properly Received

Having established the reward's scale, the Buddha turns to procedure: the fast must be received from another living person, is lost the instant dawn appears, and depends on six distinct kinds of purity for its full effect — purifying, the text says, every sin except the five heinous crimes.

Chinese Source

「善男子！若有從他三受三歸，三受八戒，是名得具一日一夜優婆塞齋。明相出時，是時則失，是故不得佛像邊受，要當從人。根本清淨，受已清淨，莊嚴清淨，覺觀清淨，念心清淨，求報清淨，是名三歸清淨齋法。善男子！若能如是清淨歸依受八戒者，除五逆罪，餘一切罪悉皆消滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son! If a person receives the three refuges three times over from another, and receives the eight precepts three times over, this is called fully obtaining the one-day, one-night upasaka fast. The moment the sign of dawn appears, the fast is then lost. For this reason it cannot be received before an image of the Buddha — it must be received from a person. The root pure, the receiving itself pure once undertaken, the adornment pure, the awareness and examination pure, the mindful intention pure, and the seeking of reward pure — this is called the pure fast-method of the three refuges. Good son! If a person is able in this way to purely take refuge and receive the eight precepts, then except for the five heinous crimes, every other sin is entirely dissolved.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu notes that commentators have long disagreed about the fast's proper length: some hold that it can only be received for one day and one night at a stretch, to be received fresh again the next day if the practice continues; others hold it may be taken at will for three, five, or seven days running, treating it as a form of short-term monastic renunciation. He glosses "receiving three times" as meaning the formula of refuge and precepts is recited through in full three separate times, not that three distinct ceremonies must be held.

4. Milestone: The Precept's Own Power — Never Shared, Never Idle

Two further provisions guard the fast's integrity: it cannot be received jointly by two people at once, since its outcome depends on one particular person's own resolve rather than any shared title — and anyone already in the middle of ordering violence, down to a ruler with an execution already under way, must actually halt it before the fast can be said to succeed at all.

Chinese Source

如是戒者，不得一時二人並受。何以故？若一時中二人共受，何因緣故一人毀犯、一人堅持？是戒力故，後世生時不能造惡，受已作罪，復不永失。若先遣信，欲刑戮人，信遲未至，其人尋後發心受齋，當受齋時，信至即殺，雖復一時，以戒力故，不得殺罪。

Ru-Yi Translation

A precept of this kind cannot be received by two people together at one and the same time. Why is this? If two people were to receive it jointly at the same time, by what cause and condition would it come about that one of them violates it while the other holds it firmly? Because of the power of this precept, in future lives one becomes unable to commit evil; and even if, having received it, one still commits some offense, that offense is still not permanently unaddressed. Suppose someone has already dispatched a messenger, intending to have a person executed, and the messenger is delayed and has not yet arrived; if that person then, in the meantime, resolves to receive the fast, then even though the messenger arrives and the execution is carried out at that very moment, still — because of the power of the precept — no offense of killing is incurred.

Chinese Source

若諸貴人，常勅作惡，若欲受齋，先當勅語，遮先諸惡乃得成就；若先不遮，輒便受齋者，不名得齋。欲受齋者，先當宣令所屬國境、我欲受齋，凡是齋日，悉斷諸惡罰戮之事。若能如是清淨受持八戒齋者，是人則得無量果報至無上樂。

Ru-Yi Translation

If any noble person habitually issues orders to commit evil deeds, and wishes to receive the fast, they must first issue an order rescinding those earlier evil commands before the fast can actually be accomplished. If the earlier commands are not first rescinded, and the person simply proceeds to receive the fast regardless, this is not called truly obtaining the fast. One who wishes to receive the fast should first proclaim throughout the territory under their rule: "I am about to receive the fast; on this fast day, all evil punishments and executions are to cease entirely." If a person is able to receive and uphold the eight-precepts fast purely in this way, this person then obtains an immeasurable reward, reaching even unsurpassed bliss.

Reading note

The logic behind the two-person restriction is precept-internal rather than social: the text does not appeal to any external enforcement to explain why two people cannot jointly receive the fast in a single ceremony, but to the plain fact that its outcome — one person keeping it, another breaking it — cannot be explained at all unless the precept is understood as taking hold within one particular person's own resolve at that moment, not as a title that could simply be held in common. The execution scenario pushes the same logic to an extreme case: karmic responsibility is tracked to the state of mind actually present in the moment, so a resolve genuinely taken up before an already-irreversible chain of events completes still counts, however narrow the timing.

The nobles-and-proclamation pair then reads as a single practical safeguard against taking the fast in name only while evil already set in motion continues unchecked. A ruler accustomed to ordering punishment or death must actually rescind those standing orders — only then does the fast "succeed" rather than remain a fast in name alone. The public proclamation asked of anyone about to receive it extends the same principle outward: the compassion internal to the precept cannot coexist with violence still authorized in the taker's own name elsewhere in the realm, so that violence, too, must visibly stop for the day.

5. Milestone: Why a Single Day Now Outweighs a Century Under Maitreya

The chapter's comparison across eras is deliberately extreme: a hundred years of the identical fast, kept in the far easier world to come under Maitreya, is declared to fall short of a single day and night kept now — and the reward is so accessible, the text insists, that even something incapable of practicing at all would attain it, if only it could.

Chinese Source

「彌勒出時百年受齋，不如我世一日一夜。何以故？我時眾生具五滓故。是故我為鹿子母說：『善女！若娑羅樹能受八齋，是亦得受人、天之樂至無上樂。』

Ru-Yi Translation

"When Maitreya appears in the world, receiving the fast for a hundred years there will not equal a single day and night received in my own era. Why is this? Because the beings of my own time are fully endowed with the five turbidities. It is for this reason that I said to Deer Mother: 'Good woman, if even a sala tree could receive the eight-precepts fast, it too would obtain the bliss of humans and gods, reaching even unsurpassed bliss.'

Chinese Source

善男子！是八戒齋，即是莊嚴無上菩提之瓔珞也！如是齋者，既是易作而能獲得無量功德。若有易作而不作者，是名放逸。

Ru-Yi Translation

Good son! This eight-precepts fast is itself the necklace that adorns unsurpassed bodhi! A fast of this kind is easy to perform, and yet is able to obtain immeasurable merit. If something is easy to do and yet is not done, this is called negligence.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu explains the comparison concretely: when Maitreya appears in the world, that world will be vast and at ease, its people living to eighty thousand years, free of the turbidity, evil, and suffering that mark the present age — which is exactly why a hundred years of fasting there still cannot match a single day and night of it kept now, in a far harder world. He identifies Deer Mother as a well-known upasika of the Buddha's own time, already introduced earlier in the sutra, and reads the sala tree quite literally: an unknowing, insentient piece of wood, chosen precisely because it represents the least capable thing imaginable — so that even it, were it somehow able to receive the eight precepts, would still reach the fruit of supreme bliss, and how much more so an actual person able to choose the practice freely.

He also connects the passage to a living custom still visible in his own day: laypeople who go to a temple at the appointed time, lodge overnight, fast, and recite sutras are, he notes, carrying on the surviving form of exactly this precept-fast — though he adds, with some regret, that most who still do so no longer understand what the fast itself actually means.

6. Milestone: Two Kinds of Bodhisattva — Lay and Monastic

The chapter closes by naming an asymmetry between the two forms a bodhisattva's life can take: teaching this fast is straightforward for someone already living a monastic life built around precepts, and genuinely difficult for a lay bodhisattva teaching from the middle of ordinary entanglement.

Chinese Source

「善男子！菩薩二種：一者、在家，二者、出家。出家菩薩能教眾生淨八戒齋，是不為難；在家菩薩教他清淨，是乃為難。何以故？在家之人多惡因緣所纏繞故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son! There are two kinds of bodhisattva: first, the lay bodhisattva; second, the monastic bodhisattva. For a monastic bodhisattva to teach sentient beings to purely observe the eight-precepts fast is not difficult; for a lay bodhisattva to teach others to be pure in this way is genuinely difficult. Why is this? Because lay people are, for the most part, entangled by many causes and conditions of evil."

Reading note

The chapter's own explanation for this asymmetry is spare but exact: it is not that a lay bodhisattva's realization is inferior, but that lay life is structurally more entangled — surrounded by family obligation, livelihood, and ordinary social pressure, all of which make evil easy and purity harder to hold up convincingly as an example to others. A monastic bodhisattva teaches from within a life already organized around the precepts being taught; a lay bodhisattva teaches while still standing in the middle of exactly the entanglements being taught against — which is precisely what makes the second task, though no less real a form of bodhisattva practice, the harder of the two to carry off.
