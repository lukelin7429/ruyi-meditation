Chapter 24 · 優婆塞戒經 · 業品第二十四 · The Sutra's Longest Sustained Teaching on Karma, Spanning Two Fascicles

How Mind Paints a World

Shansheng asks what could possibly serve as precepts before any Buddha had ever appeared to teach them — and the Buddha's answer becomes the longest sustained teaching in the entire sutra, spanning what were originally two separate fascicles. Chapter 24 works out, in exhaustive technical detail, exactly how the ten wholesome and ten unwholesome ways operate: the precise anatomy of root act, preparatory means, and culmination behind each of the ten; how weight, intention, and circumstance make one act graver than another; a sustained philosophical defense of how karma can be real without denying that all things arise and perish moment by moment; the five crimes that shatter the deepest fields of merit; the four kinds of karmic fruit and how fixed retribution can still be transformed; the exact lifespans of every realm from the lowest hell to the highest heaven; and, at the very edge of the chapter, an account of the birth and destruction of entire world-systems — ending on the deceptively simple observation that nothing paints a body, a life, or a world quite like the mind.

1. Milestone: What Serves as Precepts Before a Buddha Appears

Shansheng opens the chapter with a question that sounds almost paradoxical: if precepts are what a Buddha teaches, what could a bodhisattva possibly rely on in an age with no Buddha in it at all? The answer names the ten wholesome ways as exactly this — a teaching so fundamental that it survives, imperfectly, even without a Buddha present to explain it.

Chinese Source

善生言：「世尊！諸佛如來未出世時，菩薩摩訶薩以何為戒？」「善男子！佛未出世，是時無有三歸依戒，唯有智人求菩提道，修十善法。是十善法，除佛無能分別說者，過去佛說流轉至今，無有漏失，智者受行。善男子！眾生不能受持修集十善法者，皆由過去不能親近諮承佛故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Shansheng said: "World-Honored One, before the Buddhas and Tathāgatas appear in the world, what serves as precepts for the bodhisattva-mahāsattva?" "Good son, before a Buddha appears in the world, there is at that time no precept of taking the Three Refuges — there is only the wise person who seeks the path to bodhi, cultivating the ten wholesome ways. As for these ten wholesome ways, none but a Buddha is able to explain and distinguish them fully; they were spoken by Buddhas of the past, have circulated down to the present without loss or omission, and the wise receive and practice them. Good son, that sentient beings are unable to receive, uphold, and cultivate the ten wholesome ways is entirely because, in past lives, they failed to draw near to and seek instruction from a Buddha.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu explains that the ten wholesome ways — not killing, not stealing, not engaging in sexual misconduct in body; not lying, not speaking divisively, not speaking harshly, not speaking idly in word; not coveting, not harboring ill will, not holding wrong view in mind — function as precepts whether or not any formal vow is ever administered. Taken directly from a Buddha, as in this sutra, they count as a genuine precept; but even in an age with no Buddha present, something resembling them can still arise on its own, however imperfectly — he points to the Confucian virtues of benevolence, righteousness, propriety, wisdom, and trustworthiness in pre-Buddhist China as one example, tracing without full clarity back to the same root.

He stresses that Buddhism does not compete with or exclude other systems of worldly goodness but actually contains and surpasses them, encompassing them within a wider and ultimately world-transcending framework. He adds a note of caution against underestimating what looks, at first glance, like an elementary teaching: although the deeper meditative concentrations of the four dhyānas and four formless absorptions surpass the ten wholesome ways as ordinary worldly goods go, it is only once these ten are purified all the way through by the bodhisattva precepts that they reach their true and ultimate fulfillment.

2. Milestone: How a Mixed Mind Creates a Mixed World

Before naming the ten ways themselves, the Buddha traces the entire chain that makes them necessary in the first place — from an ordinary mixed mind, through affliction and karma, all the way to the shape of the physical cosmos itself.

Chinese Source

「善男子！一切眾生皆有雜心，雜心因緣有雜煩惱，雜煩惱故造作雜業，雜業因緣受於雜有，雜有因緣受於雜身。善男子！一切眾生得雜身已，見於雜色；見雜色已，生惡思惟，是惡思惟名為無明。無明因緣，生於求心，名之為愛；因愛所作，名之為業；是業因緣，獲得果報，有智之人，能破析之。由內煩惱、外有因緣，則能繫縛；修十善已，則能解之，是故如來初得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提時，分別演說十善之法。因十善故，世間則有善行、惡行、善有、惡有乃至解脫，是故眾生應當至心分別體解十善之道。若有風雲，為持大水；阿修羅宮、大地、大山、餓鬼、畜生、地獄、四天王處乃至他化自在天處，悉因眾生十業道故；轉輪聖王所有四輪——金、銀、銅、鐵——七眾受戒求三菩提，亦因十善業因緣故。是十善業道因緣故，一切眾生內外之物、色之與命皆有增減，是故智者應當具足修十善道。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, every sentient being without exception has a mixed mind; because of a mixed mind there is mixed affliction; because of mixed affliction, mixed karma is created; because of mixed karma one undergoes mixed existence; because of mixed existence one receives a mixed body. Good son, once sentient beings have obtained a mixed body, they perceive mixed forms; having perceived mixed forms, an unwholesome thought arises, and this unwholesome thought is called ignorance. Because of ignorance, a seeking mind arises, called craving; what is done because of craving is called karma; because of this karma, a fruit of retribution is obtained — one who is wise is able to break this chain apart. Bound from within by affliction and from without by conditioned existence, one is thereby fettered; but once the ten wholesome ways have been cultivated, one is freed from this. This is why, when the Tathāgata first attained unsurpassed, perfect, complete awakening, he expounded the dharma of the ten wholesome ways in detail. Because of the ten wholesome ways, the world has wholesome conduct and unwholesome conduct, wholesome existence and unwholesome existence, up to and including liberation itself — and for this reason sentient beings should, with a wholehearted mind, discern and truly understand the path of the ten wholesome ways. Wherever there is wind and cloud that hold up the great waters; the palace of the asuras, the great earth, the great mountains, the hungry ghosts, the animals, the hells, the realm of the Four Heavenly Kings up to the realm of the Heaven of Free Enjoyment of Others' Transformations — all of these exist because of the ten courses of action of sentient beings. The wheel-turning sage king's four wheels — gold, silver, copper, and iron — and the seven assemblies who receive precepts in pursuit of the three kinds of bodhi likewise all come about because of the ten wholesome courses of karma. Because of these ten wholesome courses of karma, all things internal and external belonging to sentient beings, both their bodily form and their lifespan, undergo increase and decrease — and for this reason the wise should cultivate the ten wholesome path in full.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu glosses "mixed" as the blending of wholesome, unwholesome, and neutral together, and "ignorance" here as simply not understanding the actual workings of cause and effect. Since every teaching the Buddha gave from the moment of his awakening onward — he points out that even the Avataṃsaka Sutra is ultimately an elaboration of the ten wholesome ways at cosmic scale — is at bottom this same teaching about karmic fruit, he warns against a common mistake: practitioners who chase after lofty, exotic teachings while looking down on the ten wholesome ways as too basic to bother with, not realizing that fully realized, this same basic teaching leads all the way to Buddhahood itself. He adds a cosmological aside: the "wind" here is the wind-wheel at the base of the world-system, atop which sits water, and beneath which lies the palace of the asuras.

His larger point is that the principle of karmic cause and effect is the single most fundamental and most universal principle in the entire Buddhadharma — every teaching, without exception, is simply this principle opened out in one direction or another. Anyone who genuinely grasps it, he says, comes to understand every phenomenon vertically through all three times and horizontally across the ten directions, and the mind set on unsurpassed bodhi arises naturally from that understanding; anyone who studies Buddhism without grasping it inevitably drifts back toward non-Buddhist views or merely worldly unwholesome conduct.

3. Milestone: Ten Roads, Ten Ways — Body, Speech, and Mind

Having explained why the ten wholesome ways matter at all, the text now defines them precisely — three of the body, four of speech, three of mind — and works through several technical points about when an act actually counts as karma and when it does not.

Chinese Source

「若諸眾生少、壯、老時，春、秋、冬、夏所起煩惱，各各別異；小、中、大劫所起煩惱，亦復如是各各別異。眾生初修十善業時，得無量命，色、香、味具，因貪、瞋、癡一切皆失。是十惡業道因緣故，時節、年歲、星辰、日月、四大變異；若人能觀如是事者，當知是人能得解脫。眾生皆由苦因緣故則生信心，既得信心能觀善惡，如是觀已修十善法。「意、行十處，故名十道：身三道者，謂殺、盜、婬；口四道者，惡口、妄語、兩舌、無義語；心三道者，妬、瞋、邪見。是十惡業，悉是一切眾罪根本。若諸眾生異界、異有、異生、異色、異命、異名，以是因緣應名無量，不但有十。如是十事，三名為業，不名為道；身、口七事，亦業亦道；是故名十。是十業道，自作、他作、自他共作，從是而得善、惡二果，亦是眾生善惡因緣；是故智者尚不應念，況身故作？若人令業煩惱諸結得自在者，當知即是行十惡道；若有能壞煩惱諸結不令自在，是人即是行十善道。若人始設方便，若先不思惟，當時卒作，是人不得業所攝罪。是故智者應當勤修十善業道；證四真諦，亦復如是。作期為惡，若失期者，亦不得罪。是故智者應修十善，因是十善眾生修已，增長壽命及內外物。煩惱因緣故，十惡業增；無煩惱因緣故，十善業增。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Depending on whether sentient beings are young, in their prime, or old, and depending on the season — spring, autumn, winter, or summer — the afflictions that arise differ, each from the others; and in a small kalpa, a middling kalpa, or a great kalpa, the afflictions that arise likewise differ, each from the others. When sentient beings first cultivate the ten wholesome karmic ways, they gain an immeasurable lifespan and are furnished with form, fragrance, and flavor in full — yet because of greed, hatred, and delusion, all of this is lost. Because of these ten unwholesome courses of karma, the seasons, the years, the stars, the sun and moon, and the four elements themselves undergo change; whoever is able to observe this should be understood to be able to attain liberation. It is because of the causes and conditions of suffering that sentient beings give rise to a mind of faith; having attained faith, they are able to observe what is wholesome and what is unwholesome, and having observed in this way, they cultivate the ten wholesome ways.

"Mind and conduct operate through ten locations, and this is why they are called the ten ways: the three bodily ways are killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct; the four verbal ways are harsh speech, false speech, divisive speech, and meaningless speech; the three mental ways are envy, hatred, and wrong view. These ten unwholesome acts are, every one of them, the root of all offenses. Because sentient beings differ in realm, in mode of existence, in birth, in form, in lifespan, and in name, on account of these very differences they ought properly to be named without limit, not merely as ten. Yet of these ten matters, the three of mind are called karma but not called "way"; the seven of body and speech are both karma and way — and this is why they are named "ten." These ten courses of karma may be done by oneself, done by another, or done jointly by oneself and another, and from them one obtains the two fruits, wholesome and unwholesome — they are likewise the very causes and conditions of a sentient being's good and evil. For this reason the wise ought not even to entertain such an act in thought, let alone actually carry it out in body. If a person allows the fetters of karma and affliction free rein, know that this is to walk the ten unwholesome ways; if a person is able to break the fetters of affliction and not let them run free, this person walks the ten wholesome ways. If a person first sets up the preparatory means but had not thought it through beforehand, and then suddenly acts on the spot, this person does not incur the offense that karma properly entails. For this reason the wise should diligently cultivate the ten wholesome courses of karma; and the same holds for realizing the Four Noble Truths. If a deed was resolved upon for a set time and that time then lapses, no offense is incurred either. For this reason the wise should cultivate the ten wholesome ways — and because of these ten wholesome ways, once sentient beings have cultivated them, their lifespan and their possessions, both internal and external, increase. Because of affliction, the ten unwholesome karmas increase; in the absence of affliction, the ten wholesome karmas increase."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu notes that the world's earliest inhabitants, at the very beginning of a world-cycle, all arose through cultivating the ten wholesome ways, which is why their form, fragrance, and flavor were originally complete and undiminished. He points out that sexuality itself only exists in the desire realm precisely because a "mixed," dualistic body exists there; in the form and formless realms, where there is no such duality, killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct simply have nothing to attach to and vanish of their own accord. He is careful about the boundary of what counts as "karma" at all: the three mental factors of covetousness, ill will, and wrong view are called karma but not "way," because without an accompanying act of body or speech, no genuine offense is actually incurred by thought alone — good and evil require a functioning, deliberate consciousness behind them, not mere impulse.

He closes with what he calls the practical heart of the whole passage: understanding that mind itself creates every good and evil, and therefore every world, puts the entire universe, in a real sense, within one's own hand — a sobering thought meant to make diligence in the ten wholesome ways feel urgent rather than merely dutiful.

4. Milestone: The Anatomy of Wrongdoing — Root, Preparation, and Culmination

The sutra now works through all ten unwholesome acts one by one, each analyzed into exactly the same three stages: the root act itself, the preparatory means that lead up to it, and the culmination — the aftermath of enjoyment, pride, and absence of any regret — that completes the offense.

Chinese Source — Killing

「善男子！是十業道，一一事中各有三事：一者、根本，二者、方便，三者、成已。根本者，若有他想，有眾生想，若以疑心斷其命根，若動身作相，或口說殺，是名根本。求刀、磨利、置毒、作索，是名方便。殺已手觸，稱量提持，若自食噉，若與人食，得物用度，任意施與，歡喜受樂，無有慚愧，心不悔恨，自讚其身，生大憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, each of these ten courses of karma has, within itself, three factors: first, the root act; second, the preparatory means; third, the culmination. As for the root act — if one holds the notion 'this is another,' the notion 'this is a sentient being,' or, even with a doubting mind, cuts off its life-faculty; if the body moves to make some sign, or the mouth speaks of killing — this is called the root act. Seeking out a knife, sharpening its blade, preparing poison, or fashioning a noose — this is called the preparatory means. Having killed, touching the body with one's hand, weighing and lifting it; eating the flesh oneself or giving it to another to eat; taking whatever goods are gained from it and using them, spending them, giving them away freely; feeling joy and pleasure in it, without shame or remorse, the heart untouched by regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to great arrogance — this is called the culmination.

Chinese Source — Stealing

「是他財有，亦作他想，若自往取，若遣人取，若以疑心移置異處，是名根本。若壞垣牆，諮問計數，置梯緣牆，入舍求覓乃至手觸，是名方便。若得物已，負擔藏隱，任意施與，賣用賜遺，歡喜受樂，無有慚愧，心不悔恨，自讚其身，生大憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Where the property genuinely belongs to another and one recognizes it as another's, whether one goes to take it oneself, sends someone else to take it, or — even with a doubting mind — moves it to another place, this is called the root act. Breaking down a wall or fence, inquiring about and reckoning up the amount, setting a ladder to scale a wall, entering a house to search for it, up to actually laying a hand on it — this is called the preparatory means. Once the goods are obtained, carrying them off and hiding them, giving them away freely, selling them, spending them, or bestowing them on others; feeling joy and pleasure in it, without shame or remorse, the heart untouched by regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to great arrogance — this is called the culmination.

Chinese Source — Sexual Misconduct

「若是婦女，繫屬他人，起於他想，若以疑心作非梵行，是名根本。若遣使往，若自眼見，若與信物，若以手觸，若濡細語，是名方便。若事已竟，遺以瓔珞，共坐飲食，歡喜受樂，無有慚愧，心不悔恨，自讚其身，生大憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If a woman is bound to another, and, recognizing that she belongs to another — or even with a doubting mind — one engages in unchaste conduct with her, this is called the root act. Sending a messenger to her, going to see her with one's own eyes, exchanging tokens of good faith, touching her with the hand, or speaking soft and intimate words — this is called the preparatory means. Once the deed is done, giving her jewelry, sitting and dining together, feeling joy and pleasure in it, without shame or remorse, the heart untouched by regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to great arrogance — this is called the culmination.

Chinese Source — False Speech

「若於大眾捨離本相，若於三時、若二時中，虛妄說之，是名根本。若於先時，次第莊嚴，搆言語端，或受他語，起往彼說，是名方便。若事成已，受取財物，任意施與，歡喜受樂，無有慚愧，不生悔恨，自讚其身，生於憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Departing before a gathering from what one actually saw, heard, or knew, and speaking falsely of it, whether at all three times or at two of them, this is called the root act. Beforehand, carefully arranging the matter in sequence, constructing the opening of one's speech, or taking up another's words, and going to that place to speak it, this is called the preparatory means. Once the deed is accomplished, receiving goods and wealth for it, giving them away freely, feeling joy and pleasure in it, without shame or remorse, not giving rise to regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to arrogance — this is called the culmination.

Chinese Source — Divisive Speech

「是妄語中，雜有兩舌，能壞和合，是名根本。若說他過及餘惡事，言和合者必有不可，若離壞者則有好事，是名方便。和合既離，受他財物，任意施與，歡喜受樂，無有慚愧，不生悔恨，自讚其身，生大憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Mixed in with false speech is divisive speech, which is able to break apart harmony between people — this is called the root act. Speaking of another's faults and other unwholesome matters, telling one party that harmony with the other will surely bring something intolerable, or that breaking with them will bring something good, this is called the preparatory means. Once the harmony is broken, receiving goods and wealth for it, giving them away freely, feeling joy and pleasure in it, without shame or remorse, not giving rise to regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to great arrogance — this is called the culmination.

Chinese Source — Harsh Speech

「若變容色，惡口罵詈，是名根本。若聞他罪，莊嚴辭章，起去到彼欲說是惡，是名方便。若罵詈已，還受他物，任意施與，歡喜受樂，無有慚愧，不生悔恨，自讚其身，生大憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Changing one's expression in anger and cursing or reviling with harsh words — this is called the root act. Hearing of another's fault, elaborating the wording of one's speech, and setting out toward that person intending to speak this harshness — this is called the preparatory means. Having reviled and cursed, then receiving goods from another for it, giving them away freely, feeling joy and pleasure in it, without shame or remorse, not giving rise to regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to great arrogance — this is called the culmination.

Chinese Source — Idle Speech

「若說欲事、非時之言，是名根本。若歌、若頌、無義章句，隨人所憙，造作百端，是名方便。若教他已，還受財物，任意施與，歡喜受樂，無有慚愧，不生悔恨，自讚其身，生大憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Speaking of matters of desire, or words spoken at an improper time and occasion — this is called the root act. Singing, chanting, or reciting meaningless verses, fashioning them in a hundred different ways to suit whatever a person delights in, this is called the preparatory means. Having taught this to another, then receiving goods and wealth for it, giving them away freely, feeling joy and pleasure in it, without shame or remorse, not giving rise to regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to great arrogance — this is called the culmination.

Chinese Source — Covetousness

「於他財物，生貪欲得，是名根本。發煩惱心，是名方便。作已得財，任意施與，歡喜受樂，復向餘說，無有慚愧，不生悔恨，自讚其身，生大憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Giving rise to a covetous wish to obtain another's property — this is called the root act. Giving rise to the afflicted mind that drives this wish — this is called the preparatory means. Having acted on it and obtained the wealth, giving it away freely, feeling joy and pleasure in it, and going on to tell others of it, without shame or remorse, not giving rise to regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to great arrogance — this is called the culmination.

Chinese Source — Ill Will

「若打罵人，是名根本。若捉杖石，問其過罪，是名方便。打已生喜，受取財物，任意施與，歡喜受樂，無有慚愧，不生悔恨，自讚其身，生大憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Striking or cursing another person — this is called the root act. Taking up a stick or a stone, and demanding an account of the other's supposed fault — this is called the preparatory means. Having struck the person and feeling delight in it, receiving goods and wealth for it, giving them away freely, feeling joy and pleasure in it, without shame or remorse, not giving rise to regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to great arrogance — this is called the culmination.

Chinese Source — Wrong View

「若誹謗業、因果、真諦、賢聖之人，是名根本。若讀誦、書寫、信受邪書，讚歎稱譽，是名方便。受已，向他分別演說，增其邪見，受邪財物，任意施與，歡喜受樂，無有慚愧，不生悔恨，自讚其身，生大憍慢，是名成已。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Slandering the reality of karma, of cause and effect, of the noble truths, or of sages and worthy persons — this is called the root act. Reading, reciting, copying out, and giving one's faith to heterodox writings, praising and extolling them, this is called the preparatory means. Having taken them up, expounding and explaining them to others in detail, so as to increase their wrong view; receiving ill-gotten wealth for it, giving it away freely, feeling joy and pleasure in it, without shame or remorse, not giving rise to regret, praising oneself for it and giving rise to great arrogance — this is called the culmination."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu points out that the sexual-misconduct passage is phrased from the vantage point of a male upāsaka, hence its language of "if it is a woman"; for a laywoman disciple receiving these same precepts, the identical principle applies with a man as the object of the offense. He also notes in passing the classical distinction, drawn on elsewhere in the commentary tradition, between the graded ranks of "worthy" and "sage": in the Hinayana framework, the seven preparatory stages are called worthy and the four fruits of realization are called sage, while in the Mahayana framework the ten abidings, ten practices, and ten transferences of merit form the three worthy ranks, and the ten grounds form the ten sage ranks.

What strikes Taixu most about this whole passage is its psychological realism: every single one of the ten acts culminates not merely in the deed but in enjoying it afterward, telling others about it, and feeling proud rather than ashamed — it is this final layer of unashamed satisfaction, repeated identically across all ten, that the sutra treats as completing the offense, more than the bare act itself.

5. Milestone: Acting in Combination — What Counts as "at Once"

Several unwholesome acts can, in principle, be committed within a single moment — but not all combinations are actually possible, and the sutra works out exactly which pairings can and cannot occur together, along with a first ranking of which of the ten are heaviest.

Chinese Source

「或復有人，於十業道一時作二——妄語、兩舌——或一時三，所謂妄語、兩舌、惡口；又復有三，所謂邪見、惡口、妄語。如是說者，即是無義，是名為四。瞋之與貪，不得一時，其餘八事，可得一時。云何一時？六處遣使，自作二事——一者、婬他妻婦，二者、謂無業果——先作期要，一時得業。是十惡業，或得作色，無無作色，或有作色及無作色；若無方便及成已者，則得作色，無無作色；若有莊嚴及成已者，則得作色及無作色。是十業道有輕有重：若殺父母及辟支佛，偷三寶物，於所生母及羅漢尼作非梵行，妄語壞僧，是名為重。

Ru-Yi Translation

"There are also cases where a person, in a single moment, commits two of the ten courses of karma at once — false speech together with divisive speech; or three at once — false speech, divisive speech, and harsh speech; or, again, three others — wrong view, harsh speech, and false speech. And when such speech is uttered, it is itself meaningless speech, so that four are committed at once. Ill will and covetousness, however, cannot occur in the very same moment; the remaining eight may. How can they occur in the same moment? Consider the case where all six sense-organs send out their messengers at once, and one commits two acts oneself: first, committing sexual misconduct with another man's wife; second, holding the view that there is no fruit of karma — having first resolved on this beforehand, both acts are incurred in that single moment. As for these ten unwholesome acts: some result in manifest form without latent form; some result in both manifest and latent form. Where there is no preparatory means and no culmination, one obtains manifest form only, without latent form; where there is elaborate preparation and culmination, one obtains both manifest and latent form. These ten courses of karma are, moreover, some light and some heavy: killing one's father or mother, or a pratyekabuddha; stealing property belonging to the Three Jewels; committing unchaste conduct with one's own birth-mother or with a nun who is an arahant; or speaking falsely so as to cause a schism in the saṅgha — these are called heavy.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu draws out the distinction between "manifest form" (作色), the outward visible deed, and "latent form" (無作色) — the potent seed impressed within the mind by that deed, invisible but real. This pairing becomes the central theoretical concept for the rest of the chapter: a bare act without real preparation or follow-through leaves only its outward trace, while a fully premeditated and completed act leaves both the outward trace and a lasting inward force. He treats the ranking of "heavy" offenses here as a preview: killing a parent or a pratyekabuddha, robbing the Three Jewels, violating one's own mother or an arahant nun, and causing a schism in the saṅgha all involve destroying an unusually precious field of merit or trust, which is exactly why their weight so far outstrips an ordinary instance of the same act.

6. Milestone: Three Roots — Greed, Hatred, and Delusion Behind Every Act

Each of the ten unwholesome acts, the sutra now shows, can spring from any of three underlying roots — and the same outward deed carries a different character, and different weight, depending on which root actually produced it.

Chinese Source

「善男子！是十業道，各有三種：一、從貪生，二、從瞋生，三、從癡生。若為貪利故害命者，是名從貪；若殺怨家，是名從瞋；殺老父母，是名從癡。劫盜他財，亦復三種：自為己身、妻子、眷屬，貪他財物而往劫奪，是名從貪；盜怨家物，是名從瞋；劫奪下姓，是名從癡。邪婬亦三：若為自樂行非梵行，是名從貪；婬怨眷屬，是名從瞋；於所生母作非梵行，是名從癡。妄語三種：若為財利自受快樂，是名從貪；為壞怨故，是名從瞋；若畏他死，是名從癡。兩舌三種：為財利故，是名從貪；為壞怨故，是名從瞋；破壞和合，邪見之眾是名從癡。惡口三種：為財利故，罵詈婦兒，是名從貪；故向怨家說所惡事，是名從瞋；說他往昔先人過罪，是名從癡。無義語亦三種：若為歡樂，歌叫諠譁，是名從貪；為勝他故，歌叫諠譁，是名從瞋；為增邪見，歌叫諠譁，是名從癡。從貪生者，是名為妬；從瞋生者，是名為瞋；從癡生者，是名邪見。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, each of these ten courses of karma is, moreover, of three kinds: arising from greed, arising from hatred, and arising from delusion. If one takes life for the sake of profit, this is called arising from greed; if one kills an enemy, this is called arising from hatred; if one kills one's own aged father or mother, this is called arising from delusion. Plundering and stealing another's wealth is likewise of three kinds: seizing another's property by force for the sake of oneself, one's spouse and children, or one's dependents, out of greed for it, is called arising from greed; stealing from an enemy is called arising from hatred; plundering from someone of low station is called arising from delusion. Sexual misconduct is likewise threefold: engaging in unchaste conduct for one's own pleasure is called arising from greed; violating the wife or family of an enemy is called arising from hatred; committing unchaste conduct with one's own birth-mother is called arising from delusion. False speech is of three kinds: speaking falsely for profit and one's own pleasure is called arising from greed; speaking falsely in order to destroy an enemy is called arising from hatred; speaking falsely out of fear that another might otherwise die is called arising from delusion. Divisive speech is of three kinds: for the sake of profit, this is called arising from greed; to destroy an enemy, this is called arising from hatred; breaking apart a harmonious assembly given to wrong views is called arising from delusion. Harsh speech is of three kinds: cursing one's wife and children for the sake of profit is called arising from greed; deliberately speaking of hateful matters to an enemy is called arising from hatred; speaking of the past faults of another's forebears is called arising from delusion. Idle speech is likewise threefold: singing and clamoring for the sake of pleasure is called arising from greed; singing and clamoring in order to outdo another is called arising from hatred; singing and clamoring so as to increase wrong view is called arising from delusion. What arises from greed is called envy; what arises from hatred is called ill will; what arises from delusion is called wrong view.

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu connects this list directly to the well-known repentance verse recited in daily liturgy — "all the unwholesome karma I have created since time without beginning arises from greed, hatred, and delusion" — noting that this is exactly why the tradition calls these three the three unwholesome roots, or the three poisons. He observes how the sutra's examples deliberately escalate in ugliness as they move from greed to hatred to delusion within each act, as though tracking not just the motive but the depth of self-deception involved: greed at least still recognizes what it wants, while an act like killing one's own aged parent out of confused delusion shows a mind that has lost even that much clarity about its own situation.

7. Milestone: The Fruits of the Ten Ways, Realm by Realm

Cultivating the ten wholesome ways brings liberation; abandoning them to the ten unwholesome ways brings a specific catalogue of retribution — not only personal misfortune but a visibly degraded world. The sutra then maps how fully these ten ways are actually present across different realms of existence.

Chinese Source

「修十善已，一一事中得三解脫。是十惡業決定當得地獄果報、或有餓鬼、或有畜生；餘果則得人中短命，貧窮乏財，婦不貞廉，有所言說人不信受，無有親厚，常被誹謗，耳初不聞善好之言，能令外物四大衰微，無有真實，惡風暴雨，爛臭敗壞，土地不平，無有七寶，多有石沙、荊棘、惡刺，時節轉變，無有常定，果蓏少實，味不具足。若欲破壞如是等事，應當至心修行十善。是十善法，三天下具，或有戒攝，或非戒攝；北欝單曰唯有四事；地獄有五；餓鬼、畜生、天中具十，非戒所攝；欲界六天無有方便，唯有根本、成已二事。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Once the ten wholesome ways have been cultivated, each single one of them yields three kinds of liberation. These ten unwholesome karmas will certainly bring the fruit of rebirth in hell, or else as a hungry ghost or an animal; their remaining fruit, when the retribution ripens as a human birth, is a shortened lifespan, poverty and want, an unchaste and unreliable spouse, words that others do not believe or accept, no close or affectionate relations, being constantly slandered, ears that from birth never hear anything good spoken; it can even cause the four great elements of the outer world to decline and fail, so that nothing is genuine and real — harsh winds and violent storms, rot and stench and ruin, uneven ground, an absence of the seven treasures, an abundance of stone, sand, thorn, and bramble, seasons that shift out of their proper order with no fixed regularity, and fruit that is scant, unripe, and lacking in flavor. Anyone wishing to overturn all such things should, with a wholehearted mind, cultivate the ten wholesome ways. As for this dharma of the ten wholesome ways: it is present in full in the three continents; in some cases it is bound up with formal precepts, in others not. In the northern continent of Uttarakuru there are only four of these present; in hell there are five; among hungry ghosts, animals, and the gods, all ten are present, but not bound up with formal precepts; in the six heavens of the desire realm there is no preparatory means at all — only the root act and the culmination are present."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu identifies "the three continents" as the eastern, southern, and western continents of traditional cosmology, distinct from the northern continent of Uttarakuru. Conduct formally "bound up with precepts" means received directly from the Buddhadharma; conduct like the moral teaching of Confucius, though genuinely wholesome, is not formally bound by precept in this technical sense. He explains why the northern continent's inhabitants lack only four of the ten unwholesome acts automatically — they simply enjoy the karmic fruit of past good deeds without deliberate cultivation — while hell-beings, consumed entirely by suffering, have no leisure even to create further evil, and the desire-realm gods, likewise coasting on past merit, have no need of deliberate preparatory effort.

8. Milestone: The Case of Killing, Examined in Full

The sutra now turns to a sustained philosophical examination, using killing as its central example, of exactly when an act becomes a genuine karmic offense — raising and answering a series of objections along the way about timing, intention, and what "manifest act" and "latent force" really mean.

Chinese Source

「夫業道者，一念中得。如其殺者、可殺俱死，是則不得根本業果。若作莊嚴，事竟不成，唯得方便，不得根本。作莊嚴已便得殺者，得根本罪。如其殺已，不追成已，無無作罪。若殺者一念中死，可殺之者次後念死，殺者不得根本業罪。若遣使殺，使得作罪，口勅之者得無作罪；若惡口勅，亦得作罪及無作罪。若其殺已，心善、無記，亦得作罪及無作罪。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Now, a course of karma is obtained within a single thought-moment. If the killer and the one to be killed die together, then the root fruit of that karma is not obtained. If elaborate preparation is made but the deed in the end does not succeed, only the preparatory means is obtained, not the root act. If, once the preparation is made, the killing is then actually accomplished, the root offense is obtained. If, having killed, one does not go on to complete the culmination, then no offense of latent force is incurred. If the killer himself dies in the very thought-moment of the act, while the one to be killed dies only in a subsequent thought-moment, the killer does not obtain the root karmic offense. If one sends another to do the killing, the one who carries it out obtains the offense of manifest act; the one who gave the verbal order obtains the offense of latent force; and if the order was given with a cursing, hateful voice, that person obtains both offenses together. If, having killed, the mind afterward turns wholesome, or becomes neutral, one still obtains both the offense of manifest act and of latent force.

Chinese Source

「若有說言『過去已滅，未來未生，現在無住，云何名殺？一念不殺，微塵不壞，若一不殺，多亦不能，云何言殺？』是義不然！何以故？雖復現在一念不殺，能遮未來使不起故，故得名殺。以是義故，不可以見一處無殺，舉一切處悉便無殺。有人刺手則便命終，或有截足而命全者；頭則不爾，刺、截俱死。若有作已得大罪者，是名業道；三業自得，七業自、他。若無作者，亦無無作。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose someone objects: 'The past has already ceased, the future has not yet arisen, and the present does not abide — so how can this be called killing? In a single thought-moment nothing is actually killed, not even a single mote of dust is destroyed; and if it cannot be done in one moment, it cannot be accumulated into many moments either — so how can one speak of killing at all?' This objection does not hold. Why not? Because even though, in the present single thought-moment, nothing is literally killed, that moment still blocks the arising of the future continuity of the victim's life — and it is for this reason that it is properly called killing. For this same reason, one cannot conclude that because no killing is seen to occur at any single point, killing therefore occurs nowhere at all. There are cases where a person is stabbed in the hand and dies at once, and cases where a foot is severed yet life continues intact — but this is not so of the head: whether stabbed or severed, death follows either way. Whenever an act performed results in a grave offense, this is called a course of karma; the three mental acts are obtained by oneself alone, while the seven acts of body and speech may be obtained by oneself or by another. Where there is no manifest act, there is likewise no latent force.

Chinese Source

「或有說言『身業三事有作、無作，口不如是』，是義不然！何以故？若口有作、無無作者，口勅殺已，不應得罪，是故口業亦應有作及以無作。心則不爾，何以故？賢聖之人不得罪故。何因緣故名作、無作？是業墮於三惡道故，生於人中壽命短故，所有六入常受苦故，餘果相似，根本正果或有相似或不相似。受果報時，在活地獄、黑繩地獄，餓鬼、畜生、人中三處受於餘果。若於一人作殺莊嚴，作莊嚴已，有二人死，當知唯於本所為人得作、無作。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose someone objects: 'The three bodily acts of killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct have both manifest act and latent force, but this is not so for verbal acts.' This objection does not hold. Why not? Because if verbal karma had manifest act but no latent force, then one who gives the verbal order to kill would incur no offense — yet this is not so; therefore verbal karma must likewise have both manifest act and latent force. The mind, however, is different: because a sage or worthy person, in giving instruction, does not thereby incur this kind of offense. And for what reason are they called 'manifest act' and 'latent force' at all? Because this karma casts one down into the three lower realms, because it causes a short lifespan when born among humans, and because it causes the six sense-faculties to suffer constantly; the secondary fruits it brings resemble the root offense, though the primary fruit of the root act may or may not resemble it precisely. When the retribution actually ripens, it is undergone in the hell of Repeated Revival, the Black-Rope Hell, or as a hungry ghost, an animal, or in the human realm — three of these being where the secondary fruit is received. If, in preparing to kill one person, once the preparation is made, two people happen to die, one should know that only with respect to the person originally intended is the manifest act and latent force obtained.

Chinese Source

「若有說言『色是無記，命亦無記，如是無記，云何殺已而得殺罪？』是義不然！何以故？如是身命是善惡心器，若壞是器，遮於未來善惡心故，是故得罪。若王勅殺，侍臣稱善，是王與臣罪無差別；獵亦如是。若有垂終，其命餘殘有一念在，若下刀殺，是得殺罪；若命已盡而下刀者，不得殺罪。若先作意規欲撾打，然下手時，彼便命終，不得殺罪。若作毒藥與懷妊者，若破歌羅羅，是人則得作、無作罪。若自刑者，不得殺罪。何以故？不起他想故，無瞋恚心故，非他自因緣故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose someone objects: 'Physical form is karmically neutral, and life itself is karmically neutral — being neutral in this way, how can killing incur the offense of killing at all?' This objection does not hold. Why not? Because this body and this life are the very vessel of the wholesome or unwholesome mind; to destroy this vessel is to block the arising of wholesome or unwholesome mind in the future — and it is for this reason that an offense is incurred. If a king orders a killing and his attending minister praises the order as good, the king and the minister incur an identical offense; and the same holds for hunting. If a person is on the very verge of death, with only a single thought-moment of life remaining, and one then strikes the fatal blow, the offense of killing is incurred; but if life has already been exhausted before the blow falls, no offense of killing is incurred. If one first forms the intention merely to strike or beat someone, and then, in the act of striking, the person actually dies, no offense of killing is incurred. If one gives poison to a pregnant woman, or destroys the embryo in its earliest stage, this person obtains both the offense of manifest act and of latent force. One who injures only oneself does not incur the offense of killing. Why not? Because no notion of 'another being' arises, because there is no mind of hatred involved, and because the cause and condition are not of self acting upon another.

Chinese Source

「或有說言『若心在善、不善、無記，悉得殺罪，猶如火、毒，雖復善心、不善、無記，觸、食之者悉皆死』者，是義不然！何以故？世間有人捉火不燒，食毒不死，非惡心殺，亦復如是不得殺罪，如諸醫等。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose someone objects: 'If the offense of killing is incurred regardless of whether the mind is wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral — just as fire burns and poison kills anyone who touches or ingests it, whether their mind is wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral' — this objection does not hold. Why not? Because there are people in the world who grasp fire and are not burned, who ingest poison and do not die; and in the same way, killing that is not done with a malicious mind does not incur the offense of killing — as is the case with physicians.

Chinese Source

「或有說言『婆藪仙人說呪殺人、殺羊祀天，不得殺罪』，是義不然！何以故？斷他命故，癡因緣故。若見人死心生歡喜，當知是人得成已罪；見他殺已，心生歡喜，出財賞之，亦復如是。若使他殺，受使之人到已，更以種種苦毒而殺戮之，口勅之者，唯得作罪，受使之人，兼得二罪：作以無作。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose someone objects: 'The sage Vasu taught that killing a person by incantation, or killing a sheep to sacrifice to the gods, incurs no offense of killing.' This objection does not hold. Why not? Because another's life is thereby cut off, and this itself is a cause and condition rooted in delusion. If one sees a person die and one's heart rejoices at it, know that this person obtains the offense of culmination; and the same holds for one who, seeing another commit a killing, rejoices in his heart and rewards the killer with wealth. If one sends another to carry out a killing, and the one who receives the order, upon arriving, goes on to torment the victim with all manner of cruelty before finally killing them, then the one who gave the verbal order obtains only the offense of manifest act, while the one who received the order and carried it out obtains both offenses together, manifest act and latent force."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu identifies the underlying target of this whole passage as a common misreading of Buddhist emptiness teaching: someone might argue that because all things, analyzed either by breaking them into parts or by seeing their essential emptiness directly, ultimately have no fixed self-nature, "killing" therefore has no real referent and cannot be an actual offense. He rejects this outright — even though no single thought-moment literally destroys anything by itself, that moment still prevents the victim's five aggregates from continuing into the next moment, and this is precisely what constitutes killing in any meaningful sense.

He explains "way" (道) here as the path by which unwholesome karma actually leads a being down into the three lower realms — which is also why the three purely mental acts do not themselves count as a "way": sages who keep precepts are held responsible chiefly for what they do in body and speech, not for a bare mental event with no accompanying action. Body and life, he stresses, are simply the instrument of a good or evil mind; destroying that instrument breaks the mind's continuity, and it is this breaking of continuity, not the bare physical fact, that grounds the offense.

9. Milestone: Theft, Sexual Misconduct, and False Speech — the Fine Print

Having modeled the method on killing, the sutra now applies the same case-by-case precision to the remaining bodily and verbal offenses — working through edge cases of theft, sexual misconduct, and speech where the offense either does or, surprisingly, does not apply.

Chinese Source — Theft

「若發惡心奪取他物，是人亦得作、無作罪。若數時取，若寄時取，因市易取，亦得偷罪。若自不取、不貪、不用，教他令取，是人亦得作、無作罪。若欲偷金，取時得銀，出外識已，還置本處，是人不得偷盜之罪。若欲偷金，得已即念無常之想，心生悔恨，欲還本主而復畏之，設餘方便還所偷物，雖離本處，不得偷罪。奴僕財產，先悉生意與主同共，後生貪想輒取主物，取已生疑而便藏避，復思是物同共無異，雖離本處，不得偷罪。若人行路為賊所剝，既至村落，村主問言：『汝失何物？我當償之。』若說過所失，取他物者，是得偷罪。若有發心，施他二衣，受者取一，云不須二，輒還留者，是得偷罪。若人發心，欲以房舍、臥具、醫藥、資生所須施一比丘，未與之間，更聞他方有大德來，輒迴施之，是得偷罪。若取命過比丘財物，誰邊得罪？若羯磨已，從羯磨僧得；若未羯磨，從十方僧得；若臨終時隨所與處，因之得罪。若偷佛物，從守塔人主邊得罪。若暴水漂財物、穀米、果蓏、衣服、資生之物，取不得罪。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If one gives rise to a malicious mind and seizes another's property, one likewise obtains both offense of manifest act and of latent force. Taking something on a recurring basis, taking something entrusted for safekeeping, or taking something through fraudulent trade all likewise incur the offense of theft. If one does not take the thing oneself, does not covet it, does not use it, but merely instructs another to take it, one still obtains both offenses. If, intending to steal gold, one takes it but discovers outside that it is actually silver, and then puts it back in its original place, no offense of theft is incurred. If, intending to steal gold, one obtains it, but then, reflecting on impermanence, one's heart fills with regret, and one wishes to return it to its owner but is afraid to do so, and by some other means manages to return the stolen goods, then even though the goods left their original place, no offense of theft is incurred. As for a servant's property: if one had from the start regarded it as held jointly with the master, but later gives rise to a covetous thought and simply takes the master's property, and having taken it becomes anxious and hides it away, yet still thinks of it as jointly held without real distinction, then even though the goods left their original place, no offense of theft is incurred. If a person traveling the road is robbed by bandits, and upon reaching a village the village headman asks, 'What did you lose? I will make it good to you,' and the traveler, in naming what was lost, claims more than what was actually lost and so takes another's property, this incurs the offense of theft. If someone resolves to give two robes to another, and the recipient takes only one, saying he has no need of two, and the giver then simply keeps the remaining robe for himself, this incurs the offense of theft. If a person resolves to offer a dwelling, bedding, medicine, or other necessities of life to one particular monk, but before the gift is actually given, hears that an eminent teacher has arrived from elsewhere and redirects the offering to him instead, this incurs the offense of theft. If one takes the property of a monk who has died, from whom is the offense incurred? If the formal saṅgha procedure has already been carried out, the offense is incurred against the saṅgha that carried out that procedure; if it has not yet been carried out, the offense is incurred against the saṅgha of the ten directions; if the monk, at the point of death, had already designated where his property should go, the offense is incurred in relation to that designated recipient. If one steals property belonging to the Buddha, the offense is incurred against the caretaker in charge of the stūpa. If property, grain, fruit, clothing, or other necessities are simply carried off and lost in a flash flood, and someone then takes them, no offense is incurred.

Chinese Source — Sexual Misconduct

「若於非時、非處、非女、處女、他婦、若屬自身，是名邪婬。唯三天下有邪婬罪，欝單曰無。若畜生、若破壞、若屬僧、若繫獄、若亡逃、若師婦、若出家人，近如是人，名為邪婬。出家之人無所繫屬，從誰得罪？從其親屬、王所得罪。惡時、亂時、虐王出時、怖畏之時，若令婦妾出家剃髮，還近之者，是得婬罪。若到三道，是得婬罪。若自若他在於道邊、塔邊、祠邊、大會之處，作非梵行，得邪婬罪。若為父母、兄弟、國王之所守護，或先與他期，或先許他，或先受財，或先受請，木埿、畫像及以死尸，如是人邊作非梵行，得邪婬罪。若屬自身而作他想，屬他之人而作自想，亦名邪婬；如是邪婬亦有輕重，從重煩惱則得重罪，從輕煩惱則得輕罪。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Engaging in intercourse at an improper time, in an improper place, with an improper partner — with a virgin, with another's wife, or even with one's own wife under improper conditions — this is called sexual misconduct. Only in the three continents does the offense of sexual misconduct exist; in Uttarakuru it does not. Approaching an animal, a woman already violated or otherwise compromised, a woman belonging to the saṅgha, a woman in prison, a fugitive woman, a teacher's wife, or a renunciant — nearness to any such person constitutes sexual misconduct. A renunciant belongs to no one in particular — so from whom is the offense then incurred? It is incurred in relation to the renunciant's own relatives, or in relation to the ruler. In times of calamity, of chaos, when a tyrannical king reigns, or in a time of terror, if a wife or concubine is made to leave the household and shave her head to become a renunciant, and one then approaches her still, this incurs the offense of sexual misconduct. Engaging in intercourse through any of the three improper orifices likewise incurs the offense. Whether by oneself or with another, engaging in unchaste conduct by the roadside, beside a stūpa, beside a shrine, or in a place of public assembly incurs the offense of sexual misconduct. If a woman is under the protection of her parents, brothers, or the king; or if she has already made an appointment with someone else, already promised herself to someone else, already received payment, or already accepted an invitation of betrothal; or even with a wooden or clay image, a painted image, or a corpse — engaging in unchaste conduct with any such incurs the offense of sexual misconduct. Whether with one's own spouse but under the mistaken notion that she belongs to another, or with another's spouse under the mistaken notion that she is one's own, this too is called sexual misconduct; and such misconduct likewise has degrees of severity — from a heavier affliction comes a heavier offense, from a lighter affliction a lighter offense.

Chinese Source — False, Divisive, and Harsh Speech

「若有疑心，若無疑心，若見、若聞、若覺、若知、若問、不問，異本說者，是名妄語；若言不本見、聞、覺、知，亦是妄語，不名具足；若破相說，無覆藏相，是非妄語；若異音說，前人不解，亦是妄語，不名具足；若顛倒語，若發大聲不了了語，若有所說前人不解，亦是妄語，不名具足；兩舌、惡口，若壞前人，不壞前人，作已得罪；無義語，亦復如是：如是七事，亦道亦業；其餘三事，是業非道。何以故？自不行故，妨於自他得大罪故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Whether with a doubting mind or without one, whether concerning what one has seen, heard, sensed, or known, whether asked or unasked, speaking of it in a way that differs from how it actually happened is called false speech; claiming to have seen, heard, sensed, or known something one did not actually see, hear, sense, or know is likewise false speech, though not complete in every respect. Openly contradicting oneself, with nothing concealed, is not false speech. Speaking in a foreign or unintelligible tongue that the listener cannot understand is also false speech, though not complete in every respect. Speaking in a garbled way, shouting loudly without being intelligible, or saying something the listener does not understand is likewise false speech, though not complete in every respect. Divisive speech and harsh speech, whether or not they succeed in actually damaging the other person's relationships, still incur the offense once spoken; and the same is true of idle speech. These seven are both 'way' and 'karma' together; the remaining three are karma but not 'way.' Why is that? Because they are not themselves carried out in action, while the seven obstruct both self and other and thereby incur a grave offense."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu treats this whole passage simply as a rounding-out of the detailed casuistry already modeled for killing — the same fine-grained attention to intention, circumstance, and consequence now applied systematically to theft, sexual misconduct, and speech. He notes how many of the theft examples turn on the presence or absence of an actual covetous intention rather than the bare fact of the goods changing hands — the servant who still regards the master's property as jointly held, or the thief overcome by regret who manages to return what he took, both escape the offense not because nothing physically moved but because the offense, in the end, is a matter of mind.

10. Milestone: Defending Karma Against the Charge of Emptiness

If everything perishes moment by moment, how can any lasting karmic force possibly exist? The sutra now answers this challenge directly, appealing to the two truths — ultimate and conventional — and to a series of vivid similes: a reflection in water, a shooting arrow, a spinning top, a whirling firebrand.

Chinese Source

「或有說言『一切微塵次第而住，亦念念滅，滅已無住；若無住者，尚無有作，況有無作？』是義不然！何以故？世間之法有因有果，無因無果。如面水、鏡則有像現，離面無像；作亦如是，從身有作，從是作法則出無作，如面水、鏡則有像現。譬如有人，發惡心故則惡色現，發善心故則善色現；作以無作，亦復如是。若因善業得善妙色，若因惡業得麁惡色；作以無作，亦復如是。若以念念常滅無有作、無作者，如先所說燈、河等喻，雖念念滅，以二諦故，說作、無作。微塵雖復次第不住，亦復不破世諦法也；正以微塵次第得名。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose someone objects: 'All the fine particles that make up any phenomenon abide only in sequence, perishing thought-moment by thought-moment; once perished, there is nothing that abides — and if nothing abides, there cannot even be a manifest act, let alone a latent force.' This objection does not hold. Why not? Because worldly phenomena do have causes and do have effects — they are never without cause and without effect. It is like a face reflected in water or in a mirror: the image appears, yet apart from the face there is no image. The same holds for manifest karmic action: it arises from the body's action, and from this karmic act, latent force in turn comes forth — just as the image appears in water or in a mirror. Just as, when a person's mind gives rise to malice, an unwholesome appearance shows in the face, and when the mind gives rise to goodness, a wholesome appearance shows, so it is likewise with manifest act and latent force: wholesome karma yields a fine and excellent form, unwholesome karma a coarse and unwholesome one. If it is objected that because everything perishes moment by moment there can be no manifest act or latent force at all, recall the earlier similes of the lamp-flame and the river: though they perish moment by moment, it is precisely because of the two truths that manifest act and latent force can still meaningfully be spoken of. Though the fine particles proceed only in sequence without truly abiding, this still does not violate the conventional truth — for it is exactly by their sequential succession that they are named at all.

Chinese Source

「父母、羅漢，其有殺者，得無量罪；父母、羅漢及以他人，陰、界、入等等無差別，所以得重，以是福田、報恩田故。如說二字不得一時，然此二字終不和合義不可說，雖念念滅，亦名妄語，不破世諦。猶如射箭，雖念念滅，因於身業微塵力故，到不到處；作以無作，亦復如是。如舞獨樂，雖念念滅，因於身業微塵力故而能動轉；作以無作，亦復如是。如旋火輪，雖念念滅，因於身業微塵力故，火得圓匝。初發心異，方便心異，作時心異，說時心異，眾緣和合故得名作，以作因緣生於無作；如威儀異，其心亦異，不可得壞，故名無作。從此作法得無作已，心雖在善、不善、無記，所作諸業無有漏失，故名無作。若身作善，口作不善，當知是人獲得雜果；若身善業有作、無作，口不善業唯有有作，無有無作，當知是人唯得善果，不得惡果。是故經中說七種業有作、無作。如人重病，要須眾藥和合治之，若少一種，則不能治。何以故？其病重故。一切眾生亦復如是，具諸惡故，要須眾戒然後治之，若少一戒，則不能治。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Killing one's father, mother, or an arahant incurs an immeasurable offense; though father, mother, an arahant, and any other person are, in terms of the bare aggregates, elements, and sense-bases, no different from one another, the offense is heavier in these cases because they are fields of merit and fields of requiting kindness. Just as it is said that two written characters forming a single word cannot be uttered in the very same instant, and yet, unless these two characters are joined together, their meaning cannot be expressed at all — so, though each moment perishes, false speech, likewise built up moment by moment, is still properly called false speech, without violating the conventional truth. It is like shooting an arrow: though each moment of its motion perishes, still, because of the force carried in the fine particles of the bodily act, the arrow reaches its target or fails to reach it — so it is with manifest act and latent force. It is like a spinning top: though each moment perishes, still, because of the force carried in the fine particles of the bodily act, it continues to turn — so it is with manifest act and latent force. It is like a whirling firebrand: though each moment perishes, still, because of the force carried in the fine particles of the bodily act, the fire forms a complete circle. The mind at the moment the intention first arises differs from the mind during the preparatory means, which differs again from the mind at the moment of the act itself, which differs again from the mind at the moment of speaking of it — and it is because these many conditions come together that the act is properly called 'manifest'; and it is from this manifest act, as its cause and condition, that latent force is then produced. Just as outward bearing differs from moment to moment while the mind behind it likewise differs, yet cannot be destroyed — this indestructible residue is called latent force. Once latent force has been produced from this manifest act, then regardless of whether the mind afterward turns wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral, the karma already performed suffers no loss or leakage — and this is why it is called latent, or unmanifest, force. If the body acts wholesomely while speech acts unwholesomely, one should know that this person obtains a mixed fruit; but if the body's wholesome karma has both manifest act and latent force, while speech's unwholesome karma has only manifest act and no latent force, one should know that this person obtains only the wholesome fruit, not the unwholesome one. This is why the sutras speak of seven kinds of karma as having both manifest act and latent force. It is like a person gravely ill, who requires many medicines combined together to be cured — lacking even one ingredient, the treatment fails, because the illness is so severe. All sentient beings are exactly the same: because they are furnished with every kind of unwholesome tendency, they require the full array of precepts together to be cured — lacking even a single precept, the cure cannot be completed."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu identifies this whole passage as refuting a nihilistic misreading of emptiness that denies cause and effect altogether: from the standpoint of ultimate truth, phenomena are indeed empty of any fixed self-nature, but from the standpoint of conventional truth they still proceed in an unbroken continuity — and it is this conventional continuity, not any denial of emptiness, that the entire theory of karma actually depends on.

His favorite example in the passage is the two characters that together spell out "cause and effect" (因果): they cannot literally be spoken in one instant, since one syllable necessarily follows the other, yet unless the two are taken together, the very meaning "cause and effect" collapses into nonsense. This, he says, is exactly how something can be built entirely out of strictly successive, individually vanishing moments and still be perfectly real and meaningful on the conventional level — precisely the same relationship that holds between karma's momentary acts and its lasting latent force.

11. Milestone: Undisciplined Evil and the Fifteen Wrong Livelihoods

Fascicle six of the Taishō edition ends and fascicle seven begins within this very chapter — direct physical evidence, in the manuscript tradition itself, of just how long this single chapter originally ran. The text resumes with a distinction between two very different kinds of wrongdoing: offenses committed under a standing vow to keep doing evil, and offenses committed with no such vow at all.

Chinese Source

「善男子！眾生作罪，凡有二種：一者、惡戒，二者、無戒。惡戒之人，雖殺一羊，及不殺時，常得殺罪。何以故？先發誓故。無戒之人，雖殺千口，殺時得罪，不殺不得。何以故？不發誓故。是故一切善、不善法，心為根本。因根本故，說諸比丘犯有二種：一者、身犯，二者、口犯，無心犯也。如是戒者，時不具足，支不具足，則不得戒。譬如鑽火，有燧、有力、有乾糞草，然後得火，若少一法，則不得火，戒法亦爾。如是戒者，若得、若捨、若持、若毀，皆隨於心；如來了了知諸法性，是故制之。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, the offenses sentient beings commit are, broadly speaking, of two kinds: those committed under an evil vow, and those committed with no vow at all. A person bound by an evil vow, even if he kills only a single sheep, incurs the offense of killing continuously, even during the times he is not actually killing. Why is that? Because he has already made his vow in advance. A person with no such vow, even if he kills a thousand creatures, incurs the offense only at the moment of killing, and incurs nothing when not killing. Why is that? Because he has made no such vow. For this reason, in every wholesome and unwholesome dharma, the mind is the root. Because the mind is the root, it is said that monks commit offenses in two ways — by body and by speech — and never merely by mind alone. As for precepts of this kind, if the proper time is not complete, or the proper components are not complete, the precept is not obtained. It is like starting a fire by friction: one needs the fire-drill, sufficient force, and dry tinder together before fire results — lacking even one of these, no fire results; and precepts are exactly the same. Whether such a precept is gained, relinquished, upheld, or broken all depends on the mind; the Tathāgata, understanding the nature of all dharmas completely and clearly, established these rules on that basis.

Chinese Source

「若復有人，因於善業思惟力故，不造諸惡，名如法戒；若從他得，名為受戒。若離戒受有功德者，一切惡獸——師子、虎、狼——應得功德，然實不得。以是因緣，受善戒者得無量福，受惡戒者得無量罪。是故經中說惡律儀——一者、畜羊，二者、畜雞，三者、畜猪，四者、釣魚，五者、網魚，六者、殺牛，七者、獄卒，八、畜獵狗，九、作長摾，十、作獵師，十一、呪龍，十二、殺人，十三、作賊，十四、兩舌，十五、以苦鞭撻、枷鎖、押額、鐵釘、燒炙加人——國王、大臣受寄抵謾不知恩者，惡性惡心大惡村主典稅物者，毀戒比丘心無慚悔，如是之人皆無戒也；雖復不名不善業道，而得大罪。何以故？盡壽作故。如是等事，若不立誓，不從人受，則不成就。如是惡戒四時中捨：一者、得二根時，二者、捨壽命時，三者、受善戒時，四者、斷欲結時。

Ru-Yi Translation

"If, moreover, a person, through the power of wholesome reflection, simply refrains from committing any evil, this is called 'lawful discipline' as a matter of conduct; but only when it is actually received from another is it properly called 'receiving precepts.' If merit could be obtained from mere restraint apart from formally receiving a precept, then every fierce animal — lions, tigers, wolves — ought to obtain merit for it, yet in fact they do not. For this reason, one who receives a wholesome vow obtains immeasurable merit, and one who takes up an evil vow obtains immeasurable offense. This is why the sutras speak of 'undisciplined conduct': first, raising sheep for slaughter; second, raising chickens for slaughter; third, raising pigs for slaughter; fourth, angling for fish; fifth, netting fish; sixth, slaughtering cattle; seventh, serving as a prison warden; eighth, keeping hunting dogs; ninth, setting long snares; tenth, working as a hunter; eleventh, using incantations against dragons; twelfth, killing people; thirteenth, working as a bandit; fourteenth, habitual divisive speech; fifteenth, inflicting cruel floggings, shackles, head-clamps, iron nails, or burning upon others. Kings and ministers who betray a trust and show no gratitude; village headmen of cruel disposition who administer taxes with cruelty; monks who break the precepts with a mind devoid of any shame or remorse — all such people likewise have no true discipline; though what they do may not each individually be reckoned among the ten unwholesome courses of karma, they nonetheless incur a grave offense. Why is that? Because they persist in it for their entire lives. Matters such as these, if no vow is made and nothing is formally received from another, do not become fully established as 'undisciplined conduct.' Such an evil vow is relinquished on four occasions: first, when one acquires a second sexual characteristic; second, at the end of one's life; third, upon receiving a wholesome precept; fourth, upon severing the fetter of desire."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu illustrates this with the example of one who vows to slaughter sheep as a livelihood: having sworn that he will always kill a sheep on sight, he continuously bears the latent offense of killing even during the periods he is not actively killing. Most forms of undisciplined conduct, he notes, become lifelong occupations organized into guild-like groups, where joining requires an explicit vow to keep committing the harmful act — this formal vow is exactly what makes it a "received" evil discipline rather than mere occasional wrongdoing, and why the offense attaches to the person continuously, day and night, rather than only in the moment of the act itself.

12. Milestone: Why Good Precepts Are Harder to Gain Than Evil Vows

An evil vow, the sutra observes, is remarkably easy to take up — a single oath is enough. A genuine wholesome precept demands far more, and the text explains why, along with several objections about whether the lay precepts really count as complete.

Chinese Source

「或有說言『如善戒具足，惡戒亦爾』，是義不然！何以故？惡戒易得故，一因緣得故，所謂立誓。善戒不爾，有五方便，所謂五根，是故難得；以難得故，要須具足。若有說言『優婆塞戒無無義語、兩舌、惡口，是故優婆塞戒、八戒齋法，沙彌、比丘不具足得』，是義不然！何以故？我今受持淨口業故。若有說言『我受五戒淨身、口、意』，心若不淨，當知是人不得具戒。譬如有人受惡戒已，雖不殺生，是人常有惡戒成就；毀禁比丘亦復如是。何以故？受持戒已，一一戒邊多業多果故。眾生無量，戒亦無量；物無量故，戒亦無量。是善、惡戒，俱有三種，謂上、中、下。若不受惡戒，雖多作罪不名惡戒。若有難言：『何緣五戒盡形壽受，八戒齋法一日一夜？』當言：『如來善知法相，通達無礙，作如是說。』

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose someone objects: 'Just as a wholesome vow requires full completeness, so too must an evil vow.' This objection does not hold. Why not? Because an evil vow is easily obtained — obtained through a single cause and condition, namely, the making of an oath. A wholesome vow is not like this; it requires five preparatory conditions, namely, the five spiritual faculties, and for this reason it is difficult to obtain; and precisely because it is difficult to obtain, it must be received in its full completeness. Suppose someone objects: 'The upāsaka precepts contain no explicit prohibition of idle speech, divisive speech, or harsh speech, and so the upāsaka precepts, the eight precepts of the fasting rite, and the precepts of novices and monks are not received in full completeness.' This objection does not hold. Why not? Because in receiving the precepts I am, even now, undertaking to keep my speech pure. Suppose someone claims, 'I receive the five precepts in order to purify body, speech, and mind' — yet if the mind itself is not pure, one should know that this person does not actually obtain the precepts in full. It is like a person who has taken up an evil vow: even during a period when he does not actually kill, this person continuously bears the completed evil vow; and it is the same for a monk who breaks the precepts. Why is that? Because once precepts have been received and upheld, each single precept carries with it many kinds of karma and many kinds of fruit. Sentient beings are without limit, and so precepts are likewise without limit; objects of conduct are without limit, and so precepts are likewise without limit. Both wholesome and unwholesome vows are, moreover, of three grades: superior, middling, and inferior. If one has not formally taken up an evil vow, then even committing many offenses does not constitute 'undisciplined conduct' in this technical sense. If someone raises the objection, 'Why are the five precepts received for the whole of one's life, while the eight precepts of the fasting rite are received only for a single day and night?' — one should answer: 'The Tathāgata, understanding the nature of all dharmas thoroughly and without any obstruction, taught it to be so for good reason.'"

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu observes that doing evil comes easily to people while doing good is genuinely difficult — like rowing a boat against the current — and that wholesome conduct requires the five spiritual faculties of faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom together before the precept can truly be obtained in full. The eight precepts of the fasting rite are received for only a single day and night precisely as a skillful accommodation on the Buddha's part, since receiving them for an entire lifetime would be extremely difficult for most laypeople to actually sustain.

13. Milestone: The Five Heinous Crimes

Two kinds of field make up the world's store of merit — virtue itself, and the debt of gratitude owed to parents. Destroying either is what constitutes the five heinous crimes, and the sutra sets out, with unusual precision, exactly what conditions must be present for such a crime to actually be committed.

Chinese Source

「善男子！世間福田凡有二種：一、功德田，二、報恩田。壞此二田，名五逆罪。是五逆罪，有三因緣：一者、有極惡心，二者、不識福德，三者、不見正果。若人異想殺阿羅漢，不得逆罪，父母亦爾。若無慚愧、不觀報恩、心無恭敬，但作方便，不作根本，雖非逆罪，亦得大報。善教授故，生憐愛故，能堪忍故，難作作故，受大苦故，是故父母名報恩田。若復有人殺父母已，雖復修善，是善無報。是故我說人所蔭處乃至少時，慎勿毀折枝條花葉。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, there are, broadly speaking, two kinds of field of merit in the world: the field of virtue, and the field of requiting kindness. To destroy these two fields is what is called the five heinous offenses. These five heinous offenses depend on three conditions: first, an extremely malicious mind; second, failing to recognize the merit involved; third, failing to see the true fruit that will result. If a person, under a mistaken notion, kills someone believing them to be an arahant when they are not, no heinous offense is incurred; and the same holds for parents. If a person, lacking any sense of shame or remorse, giving no thought to requiting kindness, harboring no respect at all, undertakes only the preparatory means but not the root act itself, though this is not the heinous offense proper, it still brings a grave retribution. Because parents instruct their children well, because they give rise to tender love, because they are able to endure hardship for their children's sake, because they do what is difficult to do, and because they undergo great suffering in raising a child — for these reasons parents are called the field of requiting kindness. If a person kills his own parents, then even if he afterward cultivates wholesome deeds, this later goodness brings no fruit of reward. This is why I say: even in the shade of a tree under which one has briefly rested, one should take care not to break its branches or damage its flowers and leaves.

Chinese Source

「善男子！我涅槃後，有諸弟子當作是說『若以異想異名殺父母，不得逆罪』，即曇無德。或復說言『雖以異想殺於父母，故得逆罪』，即彌沙塞。或復有說『異想異名殺於父母，俱得逆罪』，即薩婆多。何以故？世間真實，是可信故，父母真實，想亦不轉，惡心殺之，即得逆罪。實是父母，無父母想，不發惡心，父母雖死，不得逆罪。何以故？具足四事，乃得逆罪：一者：實是父母作父母想，二者、惡心，三者、捨心，四者、作眾生想。具是四事，逆罪成就；若不具者，則不成就。若為憐愍故，若為恭敬故，若為受法故，若為怖畏故，若為名稱故，授與死具，雖不手殺，亦得逆罪。若為他使令殺父母，啼哭憂愁而為之者，如是罪相，初、中、後輕。欲殺父母，誤中他人，不得逆罪；欲殺他人，誤中父母，亦復如是。欲殺母時，誤殺相似，殺已藏刀，復中母身，不得逆罪。母有異見，兒有異殺，但得殺罪，不得逆罪。是五逆罪，殺父則輕，殺母則重，殺羅漢重於殺母，出佛身血重殺羅漢，破僧復重出佛身血。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, after my final nirvana, there will be disciples who will teach as follows: 'If, under a mistaken notion, or believing the victim to be someone else, one kills one's own parent, no heinous offense is incurred' — this is the position of the Dharmagupta school. Others will teach: 'Even if one kills one's parent under a mistaken notion, the heinous offense is still incurred' — this is the position of the Mahīśāsaka school. Still others will teach: 'Whether under a mistaken notion or believing the victim to be someone else, killing one's parent always incurs the heinous offense' — this is the position of the Sarvāstivāda school. Why is the correct position what it is? Because in worldly truth what is real can be trusted as real: if the parent is genuinely present and the perception of them as parent never wavers, and one kills with a malicious mind, the heinous offense is thereby incurred. But if the victim is in fact one's parent, yet one holds no notion at all that this is one's parent, and no malicious mind arises, then even though the parent dies, no heinous offense is incurred. Why not? Because four conditions together are required before the heinous offense is incurred: first, that the victim genuinely is one's parent and one holds the notion that this is one's parent; second, a malicious mind; third, the mind of forsaking; fourth, holding the notion that this is a sentient being. Only when all four conditions are present is the heinous offense fully constituted; lacking any one of them, it is not constituted. If, out of compassion, out of respect, in order to receive some teaching, out of fear, or for the sake of reputation, one supplies the means of death without personally striking the fatal blow, the heinous offense is nevertheless incurred. If one is compelled by another to kill one's own parent and does so weeping and grieving all the while, the offense in this case — at its beginning, its middle, and its completion — is comparatively light. If one intends to kill one's parent but by mistake strikes another person instead, no heinous offense is incurred; and the same holds in reverse, if one intends to kill another but by mistake strikes one's parent. If, intending to kill one's mother, one mistakenly kills someone who merely resembles her, and then, having hidden the knife, later strikes one's actual mother as well, no heinous offense is incurred for that second blow. If the mother mistook her own identity in some way that led to the confusion while the son's killing was likewise founded on that same confusion, only the ordinary offense of killing is incurred, not the heinous offense. Among these five heinous offenses, killing one's father is the lighter, killing one's mother the heavier; killing an arahant is heavier still than killing one's mother; drawing a Buddha's blood is heavier than killing an arahant; and causing a schism in the saṅgha is heavier still than drawing a Buddha's blood.

Chinese Source

「有物重意輕，有物輕意重，有物重意重，有物輕意輕。物重意輕，如無惡心殺於父母；物輕意重者，如以惡心殺於畜生；物重意重者，以極惡心殺所生母；物輕意輕者，如以輕心殺於畜生。如是惡業，有方便重，根本、成已輕；有方便、根本輕，成已重；有方便、根本重，成已輕；有根本輕，方便、成已重。物是一種，以心力故得輕、重果。

"There are cases where the object is grave but the intention slight; where the object is slight but the intention grave; where both object and intention are grave; and where both are slight. Object grave, intention slight: as when one kills one's parent without a truly malicious mind. Object slight, intention grave: as when one kills an animal with a truly malicious mind. Object grave, intention grave: killing one's own birth-mother with an extremely malicious mind. Object slight, intention slight: killing an animal with only a mild mind. In unwholesome karma of this kind, sometimes the preparatory means is grave while the root act and culmination are light; sometimes the preparatory means and root act are light while the culmination is grave; sometimes the preparatory means and root act are grave while the culmination is light; and sometimes the root act alone is light while the preparatory means and culmination are grave. The object may be one and the same, and yet, because of the varying force of the mind behind it, the resulting fruit is correspondingly light or grave."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu identifies the three schools named here with their respective Vinaya lineages: Dharmagupta with the Four-Part Vinaya, Mahīśāsaka with the Five-Part Vinaya, and Sarvāstivāda with the Vinaya of the school that "asserts everything exists." He explains the escalating gravity of the five heinous crimes in turn: a mother endures more hardship in raising a child than a father does, which is why matricide outweighs patricide; an arahant is a supreme field of merit for the entire world, so killing one outweighs even matricide; a Buddha cannot actually be killed, only injured, which is why drawing a Buddha's blood occupies this place in the sequence; and the saṅgha sustains the Buddhadharma's very presence in the world, so causing a schism within it is graver still than wounding a Buddha.

14. Milestone: Fixed and Unfixed Karma — How Retribution Can Change

Not every karmic fruit is set in stone. The sutra explains which conditions actually lock a fruit into place, and how genuine cultivation of body, precepts, mind, and wisdom can still lighten, delay, or even bring forward a fruit that would otherwise ripen unchanged.

Chinese Source

「善男子！有人以食欲施於我，未與我間轉施餓狗，我亦稱讚如是人者是大施主，若是福田、若非福田，心不選擇而施與者，是人獲得無量福德。何以故？心善淨故。是業四種：一者、現報，二者、生報，三者、後報，四者、無報。業有四種：一者、時定果報不定，二者、報定時不必定，三者、時定果報亦定，四者、時、果二俱不定。時定者，所謂現在、次生、後世。若時不定，果報不定，是業可轉。若果報定應後受者，是業可轉現在受之。何以故？善心智慧因緣力故。惡果定者亦可轉輕。何因緣故名果報定？常作無悔故，專心作故，樂喜作故，立誓願故，作已歡喜故，是故是業得果報定。除是之外，悉名不定。眾生行業有輕有重、有遠有近，隨其因緣，先後受之。如有修身、修戒、修心、修慧，定知善惡當有果報，是人能轉重業為輕，輕者不受。若遭福田，遇善知識，修道修善，是人能轉後世重罪現世輕受。若人具有欲界諸業，得阿那含果，能轉後業現在受之；阿羅漢果，亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, if someone intends to offer food to me, and before it is given diverts it instead to a starving dog, I still praise such a person as a great donor. Whether or not the recipient is a field of merit, if one gives without discriminating in one's mind, one obtains immeasurable merit — because the mind itself is wholesome and pure. Karma is of four kinds: karma with retribution in the present life; karma with retribution in the next life; karma with retribution in a later life; and karma with no retribution at all. Karma is also of four kinds in another sense: karma whose time is fixed but whose fruit is not fixed; karma whose fruit is fixed but whose time is not necessarily fixed; karma whose time and fruit are both fixed; and karma whose time and fruit are both unfixed. 'Fixed time' means the present life, the next life, or a life further in the future. If the time is unfixed, the fruit too is unfixed, and such karma can be altered. If the fruit is fixed such that it ought to be received in some future life, this karma can, through the force of a wholesome mind and wisdom, be altered so as to be received in the present life instead. Even a fixed unwholesome fruit can be lightened in this way. And for what reason does a karmic fruit come to be fixed at all? Because the act was done repeatedly without regret, done with focused intent, done with delight and enjoyment, done under a formal vow, and followed afterward by rejoicing — for these reasons the fruit of that karma becomes fixed. Apart from these conditions, karma is generally unfixed. Sentient beings' actions vary in weight and in how near or distant their ripening is, and they are received sooner or later according to their respective causes and conditions. One who cultivates the body, cultivates precepts, cultivates the mind, and cultivates wisdom, and who thereby knows for certain that good and evil will bring their respective fruits, is able to convert a heavy karma into a light one, so that even the light residue is not received at all. If one meets a field of merit, encounters a good spiritual friend, and cultivates the path and wholesome deeds, one is able to convert a heavy offense that would otherwise ripen in a future life into something lightly experienced in the present life instead. If a person possesses the full range of karma belonging to the desire realm and attains the fruit of non-returner, he is able to convert future karma so that it is experienced in the present instead; and the same holds for one who attains the fruit of arahantship.

Chinese Source

「善男子！智者若能修身、修戒、修心、修慧，是人能壞極重之業，如阿伽陀、呪及除毒寶破壞惡毒。若作小罪，初方便輕，後成已重，是人不修身、戒、心、慧，令輕作重。眾生若作一種、二種乃至種種，有作不具足，有作具足。先念後作，名作具足；先不生念直造作者，名作不具足。復有作已不具足者，謂作業已果報不定；復有作已亦具足者，謂作業已定當得報。復有作已不具足者，果報雖定，時節不定；復有作已亦具足者，時、報俱定。復有作已不具足者，持戒、正見；復有作已亦具足者，毀戒、邪見。復有作已不具足者，信因信果；復有作已亦具足者，不信因果。復有作已不具足者，作惡之時有善圍遶；復有作已亦具足者，作惡之時惡來圍遶。復有作已不具足者，雖作眾惡，人中受報；復有作已亦具足者，人中作惡，地獄受報。復有作已不具足者，有正念心；復有作已亦具足者，無有念心。復有作已不具足者，三時生悔；復有作已亦具足者，三時不悔。如惡，善亦如是。因是作已亦具足故，作小得大，作大得小。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, if a wise person is able to cultivate the body, cultivate precepts, cultivate the mind, and cultivate wisdom, this person is able to break apart even the gravest karma — just as the medicine agada, or a mantra, or a wonder-jewel that removes poison, can break apart even the deadliest venom. If, in committing even a minor offense, the initial preparatory means is light but the eventual culmination is grave, this shows a person who has not cultivated body, precepts, mind, and wisdom, and who thereby turns something light into something grave. When sentient beings commit one, two, or any number of such acts, some are done incompletely and some completely. Thinking the act through beforehand and then carrying it out is called 'complete'; acting suddenly without any prior thought is called 'incomplete.' There is, further, incomplete action where, once the deed is done, its fruit remains unfixed, as against complete action where, once the deed is done, its fruit is certain to be received. There is incomplete action where the fruit, though fixed, has an unfixed timing, as against complete action where both timing and fruit are fixed. There is incomplete action performed by one who upholds the precepts and holds right view, as against complete action performed by one who has broken the precepts and holds wrong view. There is incomplete action performed by one who has faith in cause and effect, as against complete action performed by one who has no such faith. There is incomplete action where, at the time of committing the evil, wholesome influences still surround the person, as against complete action where, at that same time, only unwholesome influences surround him. There is incomplete action where, though many evils are committed, the retribution is received within the human realm, as against complete action where evil committed as a human brings retribution in hell. There is incomplete action performed with a mind of right mindfulness present, as against complete action performed with no such mindfulness at all. There is incomplete action followed by regret at all three times, as against complete action followed by no regret at any of the three times. Exactly the same distinctions apply to wholesome action as to unwholesome. And it is precisely because of this distinction between complete and incomplete action that a small act can yield a great fruit, and a great act a small one."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu explains that because a virtuous figure like the Buddha naturally receives abundant offerings while lowly, virtueless beings receive almost none and are truly to be pitied, redirecting an offering meant for the Buddha to a starving animal is itself praised as an act of boundless merit — the purity of the giving mind, not the status of the recipient, is what actually matters. He glosses agada as a legendary cure-all medicine and the poison-dispelling jewel as fashioned from a garuḍa's heart-essence, both serving as images for how cultivating precepts, concentration, and wisdom can dissolve even the heaviest karma.

As a rough rule, he adds, lighter unwholesome acts tend to ripen within the present life, while graver ones are usually too much for this present body to bear and must ripen later — which is exactly why the deliberate cultivation described here matters: it is the difference between a fruit simply happening to a person and a person actively shaping which fruits, and how heavily, actually arrive.

15. Milestone: How Mind Paints the Body — Four Kinds of Karma

The chapter's most memorable image arrives here: mind as an artist, painting affliction, which paints karma, which in turn paints the very body a being will inhabit. The sutra sorts all karma into four resulting combinations of cause and fruit.

Chinese Source

「一意摸身，身既成就，有無量意，摸身初意，即是善也。身既成就，得二種果：雜善、不善。如人，天亦如是。地獄眾生惡意摸身，身既成已，一向不善。餓鬼、畜生亦惡意摸身，身既成已，雜善、不善。善惡中陰以善惡摸身，身既成已，俱得雜報，善以不善。歌羅羅時乃至老時，亦得雜報，善以不善。是故經說有四種業：黑業黑報，白業白報，雜業雜報，不黑不白是業無報。黑業黑報，所謂地獄；白業白報，所謂色天；雜業雜報，所謂欲天、人中、畜生、餓鬼；不白不黑無報，所謂無漏。善男子！若人不解如是業緣，無量世中流轉生死。何以故？不解如是業因緣者，雖生非想非非想處，壽八萬劫，福盡還墮三惡道故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"A single intention shapes the body: once the body is fully formed, countless further intentions follow — but the very first intention that shapes the body is itself wholesome. Once the body is formed, it obtains two kinds of fruit thereafter, mixed wholesome and unwholesome — this holds for both the human and the heavenly realms alike. Beings in hell shape their bodies with an unwholesome initial intention, and once the body is formed, it remains uniformly unwholesome thereafter. Hungry ghosts and animals likewise shape their bodies with an unwholesome initial intention, but once formed, their subsequent experience is mixed, wholesome and unwholesome. The intermediate-state being between death and rebirth shapes its body with a mixture of wholesome and unwholesome intention, and once formed, receives a correspondingly mixed retribution, wholesome and unwholesome together — and this mixed retribution continues from the earliest embryonic stage all the way to old age. This is why the sutras speak of four kinds of karma: black karma with black retribution; white karma with white retribution; mixed karma with mixed retribution; and karma neither black nor white, with no retribution at all. Black karma with black retribution refers to hell; white karma with white retribution refers to the form-realm heavens; mixed karma with mixed retribution refers to the desire-realm heavens, the human realm, animals, and hungry ghosts; and karma neither black nor white, with no retribution, refers to the undefiled path. Good son, if a person does not understand these causes and conditions of karma, he wanders through birth and death for countless ages. Why is that? Because one who does not understand these causal conditions of karma, even if born into the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception with a lifespan of eighty thousand great kalpas, will, once that merit is exhausted, still fall back down into the three lower realms.

Chinese Source

「善男子！一切摸畫，無勝於意，意畫煩惱，煩惱畫業，業則畫身。貪因緣故，色、聲妙好，威儀詳序；瞋因緣故，色、聲麁惡，威儀卒暴；如瞋，癡亦如是。無量世界，一百三十六地獄處，無量畜生，無量餓鬼，皆因業作；人、天亦爾。無量眾生獲得解脫，亦因於業。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, of all things that paint and mold, none surpasses the mind: the mind paints affliction, affliction paints karma, and karma in turn paints the body. Because of greed as its cause and condition, one's form and voice become fine and beautiful, and one's bearing becomes measured and composed; because of hatred as its cause and condition, one's form and voice become coarse and unpleasant, and one's bearing becomes abrupt and violent — and delusion works exactly the same way as hatred. In countless world-systems, the hundred and thirty-six hells, the countless realms of animals, and the countless realms of hungry ghosts all come about because of karma; and the human and heavenly realms are exactly the same. The countless sentient beings who attain liberation likewise do so because of karma."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu reads this as illuminating how consciousness itself, through karma, actually shapes the physical form of each subsequent body. He divides intention into two functions: a "projecting" karma powerful enough on its own to determine the general shape of an entire coming lifetime — corresponding to what the classical treatise on the eight consciousnesses calls "projecting-and-fulfilling karma pulling beings along" — and a "fulfilling" karma that fills in the details afterward. He connects this to the old Chinese notion that "nature is what Heaven mandates": what philosophy calls an inborn, basically good nature is, on this reading, simply the residue of that same initial "projecting" intention that first shaped the human body — which is why a human birth counts as fundamentally wholesome even though a mixture of good and bad still follows once the body is formed.

He is careful to distinguish the "intermediate-state being" from the ordinary folk notion of a ghost or spirit: it is not a detached soul but a subtle body composed anew, moment to moment, by intention alone during the interval between death and the next rebirth. This "mind as an artist painting every possible world," he notes, is exactly what the Avataṃsaka Sutra's famous verse describes when it says the mind is like a skilled painter, able to paint every world there is.

16. Milestone: The Many Kinds of Precepts, Gained and Lost

The ten wholesome ways themselves work like medicine — blocking affliction, producing a wholesome mind, strengthening discipline. The sutra now catalogues the different grades of precept a person can hold at once, what each is called and why, and the many different occasions on which a precept can be relinquished.

Chinese Source

「善男子！是十善道有三事：一者、能遮煩惱，二者、能作善心，三者、能增長戒。如除毒藥，凡有三事：一者、阿伽陀藥，二者、神呪，三者、真寶。若人善修不放逸行，具足正念，分別善惡，當知是人決定能修十善業道；若多放逸，無有慚愧及以信心，當知是人決定能作十不善業道。是十業道，復有三事：一者、方便，二者、根本，三者、成已。若復有人能勤禮拜供養父母、師長、和上、有德之人，先意問訊，言則柔軟，是名方便；若作已竟，能修念心，歡喜不悔，是名成已；作時專著，是名根本。善男子！是十業道，復有三種，謂上、中、下。或方便上，根本中，成已下；或方便中，根本上，成已下；或方便下，根本上，成已中。是十業道，三法圍遶，所謂無貪、恚、癡，有貪、瞋、癡。是十業道，有共戒行、不共戒行。捨戒有六：一者、斷善根時，二者、得二根時，三者、捨壽命時，四者、受惡戒時，五者、捨戒時，六者、捨欲界身時。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, the ten wholesome ways involve three functions: first, they are able to block affliction; second, they are able to produce a wholesome mind; third, they are able to strengthen precepts. This is like a remedy for poison, which likewise has three components: the medicine agada, a protective mantra, and a genuine treasure-gem. If a person is skilled in cultivating vigilant, non-heedless conduct, fully endowed with right mindfulness, and able to discern wholesome from unwholesome, know that such a person will certainly be able to cultivate the ten wholesome courses of karma; if a person is given over to heedlessness, without any sense of shame or remorse and without faith, know that such a person will certainly commit the ten unwholesome courses of karma. These ten courses of karma also involve three stages: the preparatory means, the root act, and the culmination. If a person diligently pays reverence and makes offerings to parents, teachers, preceptors, and persons of virtue, greeting them first with respectful inquiry and speaking to them gently, this is the preparatory means; if, once the act is done, the person continues to hold it in mind with joy and no regret, this is the culmination; and giving one's whole attention to the act at the moment it is performed is the root act. Good son, these ten courses of karma are, moreover, of three grades — superior, middling, and inferior — and these grades can combine unevenly: the preparatory means may be superior while the root act is middling and the culmination inferior; or the preparatory means middling, the root act superior, and the culmination inferior; or the preparatory means inferior, the root act superior, and the culmination middling. These ten courses of karma are surrounded, so to speak, by three factors on either side: freedom from greed, hatred, and delusion on the wholesome side, and the presence of greed, hatred, and delusion on the unwholesome side. These ten courses of karma also divide into conduct shared in common with non-Buddhist ethical systems, and conduct not shared in common. A precept-vow is relinquished on six occasions: first, when the roots of goodness are cut off; second, when one acquires a second sexual characteristic; third, at the end of one's life; fourth, upon taking up an evil vow; fifth, upon formally relinquishing the precept; sixth, upon leaving behind the body of the desire realm.

Chinese Source

「或復說言『佛法滅時便失戒者』，是義不然！何以故？受已不失，未受不得。斷身、口、意惡，故名戒戒；根本四禪，四未到禪，是名定戒；根本四禪，初禪未到，名無漏戒。捨身後世更不作惡，名無作戒。守攝諸根，修正念心，見、聞、覺、知，色、聲、香、味、觸、法不生放逸，名攝根戒。何因緣故得名為戒？戒者名制，能制一切不善之法，故得名制。又復戒者名曰迮隘，雖有惡法，性不能容，故名迫迮。又復戒者名曰清涼，遮煩惱熱不令得入，是故名涼。又復戒者名上，能上天、上至無上道，是故名上。又復戒者名學，學調伏心、智慧諸根，是故名學。善男子！或時有人具足一戒，所謂波羅提木叉戒；或具二戒，加定共戒；或具三戒，加無漏戒；或具四戒，加攝根戒；或具五戒，加無作戒。善男子！波羅提木叉戒現在得，定共戒者三世中得。善男子！若復有人欲受戒時，至心能觀生死罪過、解脫功德，信心歡喜，是人兼得作、無作戒。如是戒者，隨命長短，命長長得，命短短得。是無作戒，三因緣捨：一者、小莊嚴故，二者、心放捨故，三者、作不堅故。不捨因緣復有三事：一者、有本願故，二者、作業堅故，三者、至心不放逸故。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Suppose someone claims that a precept is lost the moment the Buddhadharma itself disappears from the world. This is not so. Why not? Because once a precept has been received it is not lost in this way, and if it was never received it was never obtained to begin with. Cutting off unwholesome conduct of body, speech, and mind is called 'precept as restraint'; the four root dhyānas together with the four preliminary approaches to them are called 'concentration-associated precept'; the four root dhyānas together with the preliminary approach to the first dhyāna alone, when free of outflows, are called 'undefiled precept.' Never again committing evil, life after life, once this body is relinquished, is called 'unmanifest precept.' Guarding and restraining the sense faculties, cultivating right mindfulness, and not giving way to heedlessness with respect to what is seen, heard, sensed, or known — form, sound, smell, taste, touch, and mental object — is called 'sense-restraining precept.' Why is any of this called 'precept' at all? Because 'precept' means restraint: it is able to restrain every kind of unwholesome dharma, and so it is called restraint. Precept is also called 'narrow confinement': though unwholesome dharmas exist, its very nature will not admit them, and so it is called confinement. Precept is also called 'coolness': it blocks the heat of affliction and does not let it enter, and so it is called coolness. Precept is also called 'ascent': it is able to raise one up to the heavens, and further up to the unsurpassed path, and so it is called ascent. Precept is also called 'training': it trains one in subduing the mind and in the faculties of wisdom, and so it is called training. Good son, there are times when a person possesses only a single precept, namely the prātimokṣa precept; or possesses two, adding the concentration-associated precept; or three, adding the undefiled precept; or four, adding the sense-restraining precept; or five, adding the unmanifest precept. Good son, the prātimokṣa precept is obtained only in the present; the concentration-associated precept may be obtained across all three times. Good son, if a person, at the time of receiving the precepts, is able with a wholehearted mind to contemplate the faults of birth and death and the merit of liberation, and receives them with a joyful mind of faith, this person obtains both manifest and unmanifest precept together. Such a precept lasts exactly as long as the person's life — a long life obtains it for long, a short life obtains it for a shorter time. This unmanifest precept is relinquished for three reasons: because of insufficient solemnity in receiving it, because the mind grows lax and lets it go, and because the act of receiving it was not firmly resolved. There are, correspondingly, three reasons it is not relinquished: because of an underlying vow behind it, because the karmic act was firmly resolved, and because the mind remains wholehearted and unheedless.

Chinese Source

「善男子！除十善業及十惡業、善戒惡戒已，更有業、戒所不攝者，謂善惡法。如是善惡有作、無作，有人具足作及無作，若現在作善未捨之頃，具作、無作；第二念中成就過去作、無作；作已過去，唯有無作，無有作也。若人得戒，雖作不善，是人現世成就二法：惡法有作，善法無作。是作、無作，二因緣捨：一者、所施物盡，二者、心捨善。作，二世成就——過去、現在——無作三世。定戒二因緣捨：一者、退時，二者、斷善根時。復有三時；一者、捨身時，二者、退時，三者、生上時。無漏戒有三時捨：一者、退時，二者、轉鈍作利時，三者、得上果時。心善業一時失，謂上生退時；身、口、意善，斷善根時一時俱失。善男子！若得具足戒、定戒、無漏戒、攝根戒，是人了了解十業道。

"Good son, apart from the ten wholesome and ten unwholesome courses of karma and the wholesome and unwholesome vows already described, there remain further wholesome and unwholesome dharmas not covered by 'karma' or 'precept' in this technical sense. Some people possess both manifest and unmanifest together: while a wholesome act is presently being performed and not yet relinquished, both manifest and unmanifest are present together; in the very next thought-moment, the manifest and unmanifest of that now-past act are both established; and once the act has fully receded into the past, only the unmanifest remains, with no manifest aspect left at all. If a person has received the precepts, yet still commits some unwholesome act, this person, in the present life, establishes two things simultaneously: the unwholesome dharma with its manifest aspect, and the wholesome precept with its unmanifest aspect. This manifest and unmanifest pairing is relinquished for two reasons: because what was offered is used up, or because the mind relinquishes its wholesome resolve. The manifest aspect is established across two times, past and present; the unmanifest aspect across all three times. Concentration-associated precept is relinquished for two reasons: at the time of falling back from concentration, and at the time the roots of goodness are cut off — and, described differently, at three times: at the relinquishing of the body, at falling back, and at rising to a higher level. Undefiled precept is relinquished at three times: at falling back, at the moment one's faculties turn from dull to sharp, and at the attainment of a higher fruit. Wholesome karma of mind is lost all at once, namely at the moment of falling back after having ascended; wholesome karma of body, speech, and mind together are all lost at once at the moment the roots of goodness are cut off. Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu explains that the "superior, middling, inferior" grading of the ten courses of karma is measured by the intensity of mind at the time of the act. He notes, following the Yingluo Sutra's account of precepts, that "acquiring a second sexual characteristic" refers to a change of sex — a man who acquires female characteristics, or ceases to be clearly male, or the reverse for a woman — either of which causes the precept to be relinquished. He clarifies the four levels of dhyāna-related precept as corresponding to the four form-realm heavens and their preliminary "not-yet-arrived" approach stages.

He draws a comparison to the bodhisattva precept, sometimes called the "vajra precept," which — unlike these — is not lost even at death; he suggests this upāsaka bodhisattva precept, though nominally taken "for the length of one's life," functions similarly: so long as it is not actively broken, its unmanifest essence survives even the relinquishing of the body. He likens receiving the precepts to a road marked out by a plumb-line: not that there is literally no way to stray from it, but that its very nature will not "hold" or accommodate straying, hence "narrow confinement." He explains that concentration-associated precept can be obtained "in all three times" because it may derive from a past life, be received and obtained in the very same present act, or be received now and only fully obtained later; and he illustrates "falling back" from undefiled precept with the case of an arahant falling back to the third fruit, or the third fruit falling back further still.

17. Milestone: Lifespans Across the Six Realms and the Hells

Every realm's lifespan, the sutra shows, is itself a direct product of the ten courses of karma — and it lays the whole cosmic scale out in precise, almost dizzying detail, right down to the exact ratio between a single day in one heaven and an entire lifetime in the hell beneath it.

Chinese Source

「善男子！因十業道，眾生壽命有增有減：減者，壽命十年，增者，至無量年。北欝單曰定壽千年，此壽百年，東、西二方二百五十；此壽無量，彼亦無量。四天王壽人數九百萬歲，命亦不定，如三天下。三十三天壽千八百萬歲，命亦不定。焰摩天上壽三千六百萬歲，命亦不定。兜率天壽七千二百萬歲，除後身菩薩，餘一切命皆亦不定。化樂天壽萬四千四百萬歲，命亦不定。他化自在天壽二萬八千八百萬歲，命亦不定。他化自在天上一年，即熱地獄一日一夜；如是三十日為一月，十二月為一歲，彼地獄壽命二萬八千八百萬歲，命亦不定。化樂天上一年，即是大聲地獄一日一夜；如是三十日為一月，十二月為一歲，彼獄壽命萬四千四百萬歲，命亦不定。兜率天一年，即是小聲地獄一日一夜；如是三十日為一月，十二月為一歲，彼地獄壽命七千二百萬歲，命亦不定。焰天一年，即眾合地獄一日一夜；如是三十日為一月，十二月為一歲，彼地獄壽命三千六百萬歲，命亦不定。三十三天一年，即是黑繩地獄一日一夜；如是三十日為一月，十二月為一歲，彼地獄壽命一千八百萬歲，命亦不定。四天王上一年，即是活地獄中一日一夜；如是三十日為一月，十二月為一歲，彼地獄壽命九百萬歲，命亦不定。阿鼻地獄壽命一劫，大熱地獄壽命半劫，唯此二處，壽命決定。人中五百年，是餓鬼中一日一夜；如是三十日為一月，十二月為一歲，彼鬼壽命萬五千歲，命亦不定。畜生道中，除難陀婆難陀，其餘一切命亦不定。阿鼻地獄一年，即是非想非非想處一日一夜；如是三十日為一月，十二月為一歲，彼天壽命八萬大劫；無所有處六萬劫，識處四萬劫，空處二萬劫。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, because of the ten courses of karma, the lifespans of sentient beings both increase and decrease: at their least, lifespan is ten years; at their greatest, it extends to an immeasurable number of years. In the northern continent of Uttarakuru, the fixed lifespan is a thousand years; in this continent it is a hundred years; in the eastern and western continents it is two hundred and fifty years; the lifespans native to the northern continent are boundless, and so too are those of the other continents in their own way. The Heaven of Free Enjoyment of Others' Transformations has a lifespan of two hundred and eighty-eight million years, also not fixed.

One year in the Heaven of Delight in Transformation equals a single day and night in the Hell of the Great Cry; reckoned the same way, that hell's lifespan is a hundred and forty-four million years, also not fixed. One year in Tuṣita Heaven equals a single day and night in the Hell of the Lesser Cry; reckoned the same way, that hell's lifespan is seventy-two million years, also not fixed. Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu identifies "the Heaven of the Thirty-Three" with Trāyastriṃśa, glosses the "Hell of the Great Cry" and "Hell of the Lesser Cry" as what other texts call the Great and Lesser Wailing hells, and the "Hell of Repeated Revival" as the hell where the dead are tortured, revived, and tortured again without end. He explains "Avīci" as meaning "without interval" — the retribution specifically for the five heinous crimes, which is why those crimes are also called the "five without-interval" offenses, and why this hell has the longest fixed lifespan of all.

He identifies "Nanda and Upananda" as a particular pair of long-lived nāga kings who live out a full great kalpa, and explains that the sphere of infinite space, the first of the four formless absorptions, is where one who has actually realized this concentration experiences body, mind, and world alike as utterly empty of any fixed content.

18. Milestone: Cosmic Cycles — the Great Conflagration and the Birth of Brahma

The chapter's furthest horizon: the sutra describes how entire world-systems form, endure, and are destroyed by fire and water across vast cycles — and, along the way, offers its own explanation for how the mistaken idea of a single all-powerful creator god ever got started.

Chinese Source

「若有發起輕微煩惱，愛著空定，當知是人生四無色。從十年增至八萬歲，從八萬歲減還至十年，如是增減滿十八反，名為中劫。穀貴三災，疾病三災，刀兵一災，名一小劫。水火二災各五段過，有一風災，五風災過，名一大劫。閻浮提中刀兵起時，東西二方人暫生瞋，此病起時，彼小頭痛，力少微弱，此穀貴時，彼則念食，如是惡事，欝單曰無。因不殺故，壽命增長；偷因緣故，壽命減少。有二種劫：一者、水劫，二者、火劫。火劫起時，地獄眾生若報盡者，悉得出離，若未盡者，移至他方大地獄中。若此世間八大地獄空無眾生，是名眾生脫於地獄。四大海中所有眾生，業若盡者，悉皆得脫；若未盡者，悉轉生於他方海中。若是海中無一眾生，是名得脫。閻浮提地直下過於五百由延，有閻羅王城，周遍縱廣七萬五千由延。如是城中，餓鬼眾生業已盡者，悉得出離；業未盡者，轉生他方閻羅王所。若是城中乃至無有一眾生者，是名得脫。爾時有人內因緣故，獲得初禪，得已即起大聲唱言：『初禪寂靜！初禪寂靜！』諸人聞已，即各思惟，一切皆共獲得初禪，即捨人身生初禪地。時初禪中復有一人，內因緣故，修得二禪，得已即起大聲唱言：『二禪寂靜！二禪寂靜！』眾生聞已，各自思惟，復獲二禪，捨初禪身，生二禪處。當爾之時，從阿鼻獄上至初禪，乃至無有一眾生在。

When the fire-kalpa begins, all seven suns appear together, and they burn up every kind of grain and plant, the mountains, rivers, and great earth, Mount Sumeru itself, and everything up to the first dhyāna. The beings of the second dhyāna, witnessing this conflagration, are struck with terror; but among them there are gods born there earlier who say to the gods born there later: 'Do not be afraid — we ourselves once witnessed just such a fire in the past; it reached exactly this far and stopped, and never came any further than this.' Then, from the intermediate dhyāna-level, a great rain begins pouring down, and over another full cycle of lifespans rising and falling, through the power of sentient beings' karmic conduct, seven layers of wind and cloud arise beneath to hold up this water. Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu identifies the being who first falls from the second dhyāna into the newly-formed first-dhyāna realm as Mahābrahmā himself — a being who mistakes his own mere temporal priority for creative sovereignty over those born after him. This passage, he points out, is the sutra's own explanation for a widespread cosmological error: the various traditions that credit a single creator-god with fashioning all worlds, all humanity, and all things, and ruling over them as their maker, trace back to exactly this kind of misunderstanding — an accident of sequence mistaken for an act of creation.

He adds a brief, characteristically curious aside comparing this passage's account of astronomical phenomena, such as the seven suns, to modern discoveries of previously unknown stars in his own day, suggesting that scientific cosmology, itself still far from complete, should not be too quick to rule out everything the sutra describes as mere fable.

19. Milestone: Conclusion — Why the Path Is Harder for a Householder

After the chapter's vast sweep — from the definition of a single unwholesome act to the birth and death of entire universes — the Buddha closes with a plain, almost understated observation about who actually has the harder task in living out everything just taught.

Chinese Source

「善男子！菩薩二種：一者、在家，二者、出家。出家菩薩能觀如是十業道者，是不為難；在家觀者，是乃為難。何以故？在家之人多惡因緣所纏遶故。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good son, there are two kinds of bodhisattva: the householder bodhisattva, and the renunciant bodhisattva. For a renunciant bodhisattva to contemplate these ten courses of karma in this way is not difficult; for a householder bodhisattva to contemplate them in this way is genuinely difficult. Why is that? Because a person living at home is bound about on every side by so many unwholesome causes and conditions."

Reading note — after Grand Master Taixu

Taixu closes by stressing just how much harder it is for someone living amid a household's entanglements to truly understand and practice the ten wholesome ways than it is for a renunciant free of those same distractions — not because the teaching itself differs for each, but because the surrounding conditions of lay life constantly pull attention elsewhere, toward family, livelihood, and obligation.

He notes that this chapter, together with the four preceding it, has been devoted entirely to explaining the perfection of moral discipline; this final, exhaustive treatment of the ten wholesome ways stands as its capstone, drawing together every precept discussed so far along with every further wholesome and unwholesome dharma that falls outside the strict technical scope of "precept" altogether — which is exactly why a chapter that began with a simple question about what serves as precepts before any Buddha appears ends, so many milestones later, still circling back to the same single insight: that mind alone is what paints every one of these worlds into being.
