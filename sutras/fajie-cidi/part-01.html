From Name and Form to the Six Wondrous Gates

The Gateway to the Sequence of the Dharma Realm (T1925) is Zhiyi's handbook of the basic number-lists of Buddhist doctrine. His preface gives three purposes: to help readers who stumble over names and numbers in the sūtras and treatises, to show the order of shallow and deep among the dharma-gates, and to give students of the three contemplations material to pass through the mind one item at a time. Each entry gives a heading, the list of items, a paragraph on why the category comes next and what its general name means, and a definition of each item. Part 1 contains the author's preface, the table of contents of all sixty gateways, and gateways 1 to 18. It moves from the analysis of experience (name and form, aggregates, sense-fields, elements), through the false views and afflictions that arise from it (the sixteen views of a self, the three poisons, five hindrances, ten agents, ninety-eight agents), to conduct (the ten evils and ten wholesome deeds, the three refuges, the five precepts), and then to meditation: the four dhyānas with their factors, the four immeasurable minds, the four formless concentrations, and the six wondrous gates of breath meditation. Many of these categories are treated at length in Zhiyi's Explanation of the Sequential Gates of Dhyāna-Pāramitā, already on this site.

General Preface to The Gateway to the Sequence of the Dharma Realm

Chinese Source

天台山修禪寺沙門釋智顗，輒依經附論，撰《法界次第初門》三百科，裁為七卷，流傳新學。略為三意：一、為讀經尋論隨見法門，脫有迷於名數者；二、為未解聖教所制法門淺深之次第；三、為學三觀之者，當以此諸法名相義理，一一歷心而轉作，則觀解無礙，觸境不迷。若於一念心中通達一切佛法者，則三觀自然了了分明也。故出此三百科名教，仍當名下略辨體相，始得三卷。

Ru-Yi Translation

Zhiyi, a śramaṇa of Xiuchan Monastery on Mount Tiantai, relying on the sūtras and drawing on the treatises, has ventured to compose The Gateway to the Sequence of the Dharma Realm in three hundred topics, cut into seven fascicles, to circulate among new students. There are, in brief, three intentions: first, for those who, reading the sūtras and searching the treatises, come upon dharma-gates as they appear and may be confused by their names and numbers; second, for those who have not yet understood the sequence, shallow and deep, of the dharma-gates established by the sagely teaching; third, for those who study the three contemplations: they should take the names, marks, meanings, and principles of these dharmas and, one by one, pass them through the mind and turn them over in practice; then contemplation and understanding will be unobstructed, and whatever object they touch they will not be confused. If in a single thought of mind one penetrates all the Buddha-dharmas, then the three contemplations will naturally be clear and distinct. Therefore I have set out these three hundred topics of names and teachings, and under each name I briefly distinguish their essence and marks; this has come to only three fascicles.

Chinese Source

名色初門第一

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of Name and Form, Number 1

Chinese Source

一名、二色。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, name; second, form.

Chinese Source

今辨法界初門，先從名、色而始者，至論諸法本原清淨、絕名離相，尚非是一，何曾有二？不二而辨其二者，以行者初受一期妄報，歌羅邏時但有名、色二法。當知名、色即是一切世間、出世間法之根本，能生一切法、普攝一切法，即是一切法。若諸大聖分別說一切法門，皆約名、色而分別之，無有一法出於名、色。故《智度論》偈云：

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, in distinguishing the gateway of the dharma realm, the reason we begin first from name and form is this. If we discuss the original source of all dharmas, it is pure, cut off from names and apart from marks; it is not even one — how could it ever be two? Not being two, yet distinguishing it as two, [is done] because when the practitioner first receives the deluded retribution of one lifetime, at the time of the kalala there are only the two dharmas of name and form. One should know that name and form are the root of all worldly and world-transcending dharmas: they can give rise to all dharmas, universally include all dharmas, and are themselves all dharmas. When the great sages expound all the dharma-gates distinctly, they all distinguish them by way of name and form; there is not a single dharma that goes beyond name and form. Therefore a verse of the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom says:

Chinese Source

「一切諸法中，但有名與色。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Among all dharmas / there are only name and form.

Chinese Source

若欲如實觀，但當觀名色。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one wishes to contemplate as things truly are, / one should contemplate only name and form.

Chinese Source

雖癡心多想，分別於異事，<br>更無有一法，出於名色者。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Though the mind of folly has many perceptions / and discriminates different matters, / there is not a single dharma further / that goes beyond name and form."

Chinese Source

一、名。心但有字，故曰名也。即是心及相應數法，雖有能緣之用，而無質礙可尋。既異於色，而有心、意、識及諸數法種種之別名，故謂之為名也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, name. The mind has only a word [for it]; hence it is called "name." This is the mind together with its associated mental factors: although they have the function of taking objects, there is no material obstruction to be found. Since they differ from form, and there are various separate names such as mind, intent, consciousness, and the mental factors, they are called "name."

Chinese Source

二、色。有形質礙之法，謂之為色。是十入及一入少分，皆是質礙之法，並無知覺之用。既異於心、意、識法，故稱為色也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, form. A dharma that has shape and material obstruction is called form. This is the ten sense-fields and a small portion of one sense-field; all are dharmas of material obstruction and have no function of knowing or awareness. Since they differ from the dharmas of mind, intent, and consciousness, they are called form.

The Five Aggregates

Chinese Source

五陰初門第二

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Five Aggregates, Number 2

Chinese Source

一色陰、二受陰、三想陰、四行陰、五識陰。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the aggregate of form; second, the aggregate of feeling; third, the aggregate of perception; fourth, the aggregate of formations; fifth, the aggregate of consciousness.

Chinese Source

次名、色而辨五陰者，以惑者迷名偏重故。大聖教門，開名則為四心，對色合為五也。此五通稱為陰者，一往而釋，陰以陰覆為義，能覆出世真明之慧而增長生死、集散不絕，故通名為陰。

Ru-Yi Translation

The five aggregates are distinguished next after name and form because the deluded are confused chiefly about name. The teaching-gate of the great sages opens name into four [aggregates of] mind and, together with form, combines them into five. These five are all called "aggregates" (陰): explaining it at first glance, 陰 has the meaning of covering over (陰覆). They can cover the wisdom of true illumination that transcends the world and increase birth and death, gathering and dispersing without cease; hence they are all named aggregates.

Chinese Source

一、色陰。有形質礙之法名為色。色有十四種，所謂四大、五根、五塵，此之十四並是色法也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the aggregate of form. A dharma that has shape and material obstruction is named form. Form is of fourteen kinds, namely the four great elements, the five faculties, and the five sense-objects; these fourteen are all form-dharmas.

Chinese Source

二、受陰。領納所緣名為受。受有六種，謂六觸因緣生六受，但境既有違、順、非違非順之別，故六受亦各有苦受、樂受、不苦不樂受之異也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the aggregate of feeling. Taking in what is cognized is named feeling. Feeling is of six kinds: the six contacts, as causes and conditions, give rise to the six feelings. But since objects differ as adverse, agreeable, and neither adverse nor agreeable, each of the six feelings also differs as the feeling of suffering, the feeling of happiness, and the feeling of neither suffering nor happiness.

Chinese Source

三、想陰。能取所領之緣相名為想。想有六種，謂取所領六塵之相，為六想也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the aggregate of perception. Taking up the marks of the objects that have been taken in is named perception. Perception is of six kinds: taking up the marks of the six sense-objects that have been taken in makes the six perceptions.

Chinese Source

四、行陰。造作之心能趣於果名為行。行有六種，《大品經》中說為六思，思即是行。謂於六想之後，各起不善業、善業、無動業也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the aggregate of formations. The mind of creating and making, which can go toward a result, is named formation. Formations are of six kinds; in the Larger Sūtra they are spoken of as the six volitions, and volition is formation. That is, after the six perceptions there arise, in each case, unwholesome karma, wholesome karma, and motionless karma.

Chinese Source

五、識陰。了別所緣之境名為識。識有六種，即是六識。若諸論師多云識在二心之前，諸大乘經中明識最居後。今依經為次料簡。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the aggregate of consciousness. Discerning the object cognized is named consciousness. Consciousness is of six kinds, namely the six consciousnesses. Many treatise masters say that consciousness stands before the two [aggregates of] mind; the Mahāyāna sūtras make clear that consciousness stands last. Here the order is sorted out according to the sūtras.

The Twelve Sense-Fields

Chinese Source

十二入初門第三

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Twelve Sense-Fields, Number 3

Chinese Source

內六根入：一眼入、二耳入、三鼻入、四舌入、五身入、六意入。

Ru-Yi Translation

The sense-fields of the six inner faculties: first, the eye sense-field; second, the ear sense-field; third, the nose sense-field; fourth, the tongue sense-field; fifth, the body sense-field; sixth, the intent sense-field.

Chinese Source

外六塵入：一色入、二聲入、三香入、四味入、五觸入、六法入。

Ru-Yi Translation

The sense-fields of the six outer sense-objects: first, the form sense-field; second, the sound sense-field; third, the smell sense-field; fourth, the taste sense-field; fifth, the touch sense-field; sixth, the dharma sense-field.

Chinese Source

次五陰而辨十二入者，以惑者迷色偏重故。大聖教門，開色為十、心但為二，合為十二也。通稱入者，入以涉入為義，根、塵相對則有識生，識依根、塵仍為能入，根、塵即是所入。今此十二從所入受名，故通受入名。內六入者，此之六法親故屬內，為識所依，故名為入。亦名根者，根以能生為義，此六既並有生識之功，故通名根也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The twelve sense-fields are distinguished next after the five aggregates because the deluded are confused chiefly about form. The teaching-gate of the great sages opens form into ten and makes mind only two, combining them into twelve. They are all called "sense-fields" (入) because 入 has the meaning of "entering into": when faculty and sense-object meet, consciousness arises; consciousness, relying on faculty and sense-object, is that which enters, and faculty and sense-object are what is entered. These twelve take their name from what is entered; hence they all receive the name "sense-field." As for the six inner sense-fields: these six dharmas are close, hence they belong to the inner; they are what consciousness relies on, hence they are named sense-fields. They are also named "faculties" (根): 根 has the meaning of being able to give rise; since all six have the function of giving rise to consciousness, they are all named faculties.

Chinese Source

一、眼入。身分對色，能見色之處名眼。眼是四大造色，體為十色共成，所謂四大、四微、身根微、眼根微也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the eye sense-field. The part of the body that faces form, the place able to see form, is named the eye. The eye is form derived from the four great elements; its essence is made jointly of ten forms, namely the four great elements, the four subtle elements, the subtle element of the body faculty, and the subtle element of the eye faculty.

Chinese Source

二、耳入。身分對聲，能聞聲之處名耳。耳是四大造色，亦為十色共成，謂四大、四微、身根微、耳根微。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the ear sense-field. The part of the body that faces sound, the place able to hear sound, is named the ear. The ear is form derived from the four great elements; it too is made jointly of ten forms, namely the four great elements, the four subtle elements, the subtle element of the body faculty, and the subtle element of the ear faculty.

Chinese Source

三、鼻入。身分對香，能聞香之處名鼻。鼻是四大造色，亦為十色共成，謂四大、四微、身根微、鼻根微。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the nose sense-field. The part of the body that faces smell, the place able to smell smells, is named the nose. The nose is form derived from the four great elements; it too is made jointly of ten forms, namely the four great elements, the four subtle elements, the subtle element of the body faculty, and the subtle element of the nose faculty.

Chinese Source

四、舌入。身分對味，能知味之處名為舌。舌是四大造色，亦為十色所成，謂四大、四微、身根微、舌根微也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the tongue sense-field. The part of the body that faces taste, the place able to know taste, is named the tongue. The tongue is form derived from the four great elements; it too is made of ten forms, namely the four great elements, the four subtle elements, the subtle element of the body faculty, and the subtle element of the tongue faculty.

Chinese Source

五、身入。六分假合之體對觸，能覺觸處皆名為身。身是四大造色，但有九色所成，謂四大、四微、身根微。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the body sense-field. The essence provisionally combined from six parts faces touch; the places able to feel touch are all named the body. The body is form derived from the four great elements, but it is made of only nine forms, namely the four great elements, the four subtle elements, and the subtle element of the body faculty.

Chinese Source

六、意入。心對一切法，即有能知法之用，名之為意。意者即心王也，是中除諸心數法，但取心王以為意入。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, the intent sense-field. When the mind faces all dharmas, it has the function of being able to know dharmas; this is named intent. Intent is the mind-king. Here the various mental factors are excluded, and only the mind-king is taken as the intent sense-field.

Chinese Source

外六入者，此六法疎故屬外，識所遊涉，故名為入。亦名塵者，塵以染污為義，以能染污情識，故通名為塵也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the six outer sense-fields: these six dharmas are distant, hence they belong to the outer; they are where consciousness roams and passes through, hence they are named sense-fields. They are also named "dusts" (塵), that is, sense-objects: "dust" has the meaning of staining; because they can stain the consciousness of sentient feeling, they are all named dusts.

Chinese Source

一、色入。一切對眼，所見之色名為色。色有二種色，攝一切色：一、正報可見色，眾生身色，青、黃、赤、白、黑色等；二、依報可見色，外無知青、黃、赤、白、黑色等也。

Chinese Source

一見煩惱、二愛煩惱

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the affliction of views; second, the affliction of craving.

Chinese Source

Now the four dhyānas are distinguished next after the categories of precepts because, although the marks of the precepts made clear above differ as coarse and fine, in the end they all alike guard against the outward evils of body and speech in the desire realm. Since subtle disturbance has not yet been removed, how could one pass beyond the realm of desire? If one's own śīla is spotless and one's aspiration lies in the gate of dhyāna, single-mindedly cultivating the five dharmas (the categories of the five dharmas are set out separately below), then the pure four great elements of the form realm naturally appear in the body. Through this one attains in sequence the root four dhyānas and the merits of their various superior and wondrous groves of factors; then, as cause, one passes beyond the net of desire, and, as fruit, one dwells in the form realm. They are all named "dhyāna" (禪): dhyāna is a word of the Western Land, which is translated here as "abandoning evil," since it can abandon all evils such as the five hindrances of the desire realm; hence it is called abandoning evil. It is also translated "grove of merit," or translated "cultivation through deliberation"; these are not fully explained here. They are called "root" because the various dhyānas and samādhis — the immeasurable minds, the detachments, the stations of mastery, the totality-bases, the supernormal powers and transformations, and uncontaminated contemplation and wisdom — all come out from the four dhyānas; hence they are called the root.

Chinese Source

初禪有五支：一覺支、二觀支、三喜支、四樂支、五一心支。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first dhyāna has five factors: first, the factor of initial thought; second, the factor of sustained reflection; third, the factor of joy; fourth, the factor of happiness; fifth, the factor of one-pointedness of mind.

Chinese Source

一、覺支。初心在緣名為覺。行者依未到地發初禪，色界清淨色法觸欲界身根，心大驚悟，爾時即生身識，覺此色觸未曾有功德利益，故名覺支。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the factor of initial thought. The initial mind being on its object is named initial thought (覺). The practitioner, relying on the not-yet-arrived ground, brings forth the first dhyāna: the pure form-dharma of the form realm touches the body faculty of the desire realm, and the mind is greatly startled and awakened. At that moment body-consciousness arises and is aware of the merit and benefit of this form-contact, which it never had before; hence it is named the factor of initial thought.

Chinese Source

二、觀支。細心分別名為觀。行者既證初禪功德，即以細心分別此禪定中色法諸妙功德境界分明，無諸蓋覆，如是等功德，欲界之所未有，故名觀支。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the factor of sustained reflection. A subtle mind that discriminates is named sustained reflection (觀). The practitioner, having realized the merit of the first dhyāna, uses a subtle mind to discriminate the various wondrous merits of the form-dharma within this dhyāna-concentration, its realm distinct and clear, without the coverings of the hindrances. Such merits the desire realm never had; hence it is named the factor of sustained reflection.

Chinese Source

三、喜支。欣慶之心名為喜。行者初發禪時，乃有喜生，但分別未了，故喜心未成。若觀心分別所捨欲界之樂甚少，今獲得初禪利益甚多。如是思惟已，則歡喜無量，故名喜支。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the factor of joy. A mind of glad rejoicing is named joy. When the practitioner first brings forth dhyāna, joy arises, but because discrimination is not yet complete, the joyful mind is not yet fully formed. If the contemplating mind discriminates that the happiness of the desire realm given up was very slight and the benefit of the first dhyāna now attained is very great — having deliberated thus, one rejoices immeasurably; hence it is named the factor of joy.

Chinese Source

四、樂支。怡悅之心名為樂。行者發初禪時乃即有樂，但分別喜動踊心息，則恬然靜慮，受於樂觸怡悅之安快，故名樂支。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the factor of happiness. A mind of delight is named happiness. When the practitioner brings forth the first dhyāna, happiness is already present; but once the leaping mind of discriminating joy subsides, he becomes calm, in quiet absorption, receiving the delightful ease of the touch of happiness; hence it is named the factor of happiness.

Chinese Source

五、一心支。心與定法一，故名曰一心。行者初證禪時乃即著定，而心猶依覺、觀、喜、樂之法，故有細微之散。若受喜樂心，自然與定法一，故名一心支。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the factor of one-pointedness of mind. Mind is one with the dharma of concentration; hence it is named one-pointedness of mind. When the practitioner first realizes dhyāna, he is already settled in concentration, yet the mind still relies on the dharmas of initial thought, sustained reflection, joy, and happiness; hence there is subtle dispersal. If the mind that receives joy and happiness naturally becomes one with the dharma of concentration, it is therefore named the factor of one-pointedness of mind.

The Second, Third, and Fourth Dhyānas

Chinese Source

二禪有四支：一內淨支、二喜支、三樂支、四一心支

Ru-Yi Translation

The second dhyāna has four factors: first, the factor of inner purity; second, the factor of joy; third, the factor of happiness; fourth, the factor of one-pointedness of mind.

Chinese Source

一、內淨支。心無觀覺之渾濁，故名內淨。行者欲離初禪時，種種訶責覺觀。覺觀既滅，則心內靜，心與靜色法相應，豁爾明淨，故名內淨支也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the factor of inner purity. Because the mind is without the turbidity of sustained reflection and initial thought, it is named inner purity. When the practitioner wishes to leave the first dhyāna, he reproaches initial thought and sustained reflection in various ways. Once initial thought and sustained reflection are extinguished, the mind is inwardly still; the mind corresponds with the still form-dharma and is suddenly bright and pure; hence it is named the factor of inner purity.

Chinese Source

二、喜支。欣慶之心名之為喜。行者初得內淨時，即與喜俱發，而喜心未成。次心自慶，得免覺觀之患，獲得勝定內淨之喜，歡喜無量，故名為喜支。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the factor of joy. A mind of glad rejoicing is named joy. When the practitioner first attains inner purity, it arises together with joy, but the joyful mind is not yet fully formed. Next, the mind congratulates itself on having escaped the trouble of initial thought and sustained reflection and on having attained the joy of the inner purity of a superior concentration; it rejoices immeasurably; hence it is named the factor of joy.

Chinese Source

三、樂支。怡悅之心名之為樂。行者喜踊之情既息，則恬然靜慮，受於內淨喜中之悅樂，故名為樂支也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the factor of happiness. A mind of delight is named happiness. Once the practitioner's leaping feeling of joy has subsided, he becomes calm, in quiet absorption, receiving the delightful happiness within the joy of inner purity; hence it is named the factor of happiness.

Chinese Source

四、一心支。心與定法一，謂之一心。行者受樂心息，則心與定一，澄停不動，故名為一心支。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the factor of one-pointedness of mind. Mind being one with the dharma of concentration is called one-pointedness of mind. When the practitioner's mind that receives happiness subsides, the mind is one with concentration, clear, still, and unmoving; hence it is named the factor of one-pointedness of mind.

Chinese Source

三禪有五支：一捨支、二念支、三慧支、四樂支、五一心支。

Ru-Yi Translation

The third dhyāna has five factors: first, the factor of equanimity; second, the factor of mindfulness; third, the factor of wisdom; fourth, the factor of happiness; fifth, the factor of one-pointedness of mind.

Chinese Source

一、捨支。離喜不悔，此心名捨。行者欲離二禪時，種種因緣訶責於喜。喜既滅謝，三禪即發。若證三禪之樂，則捨二禪之喜，不生悔心，故名為捨。亦名三禪樂初生時，是樂三界第一，能生心著。心著則禪壞，故須行捨。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the factor of equanimity. Leaving joy without regret — this mind is named equanimity (捨). When the practitioner wishes to leave the second dhyāna, he reproaches joy through various causes and conditions. Once joy has ceased and faded, the third dhyāna arises. If one realizes the happiness of the third dhyāna, one relinquishes the joy of the second dhyāna without giving rise to a mind of regret; hence it is named equanimity. It is also named [so because] when the happiness of the third dhyāna first arises, this happiness is the foremost in the three realms and can give rise to attachment of mind; if the mind is attached, the dhyāna is ruined; hence one must practice equanimity.

Chinese Source

二、念支。念名愛念。行者既發三禪之樂，樂從內起，應須愛念將息，則樂得增長，乃至遍身，如慈母念子，愛念將養，故名念支。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the factor of mindfulness. Mindfulness (念) is named affectionate mindfulness. Having brought forth the happiness of the third dhyāna, happiness arising from within, the practitioner must attend to it with affectionate mindfulness and nurture it; then the happiness can increase until it pervades the body, like a loving mother mindful of her child, attending to it affectionately and nourishing it; hence it is named the factor of mindfulness.

Chinese Source

三、慧支。解知之心名慧。行者既發三禪之樂，此樂微妙難得，增長遍身。若非善巧之解慧，則不能方便長養此樂得遍身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the factor of wisdom. A mind of understanding and knowing is named wisdom. Having brought forth the happiness of the third dhyāna, the practitioner [finds] this happiness subtle, wondrous, and hard to attain, increasing until it pervades the body. Without skillful understanding and wisdom, one cannot by expedient means nourish this happiness so that it pervades the body.

Chinese Source

四、樂支。怡悅之心名之為樂。行者發三禪樂已，若能善用捨念慧將護此樂，樂既無過，則增長遍身怡悅受樂安，快三禪之樂。三禪為最樂，若離三禪，餘地更無遍身之樂也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the factor of happiness. A mind of delight is named happiness. Once the practitioner has brought forth the happiness of the third dhyāna, if he can skillfully use equanimity, mindfulness, and wisdom to protect this happiness, the happiness, being without fault, increases and pervades the body, and he delightfully receives the ease of happiness — the delight of the third dhyāna's happiness. The third dhyāna is the most happy; apart from the third dhyāna, no other ground has happiness that pervades the body.

Chinese Source

五、一心支。心與定法一，名曰一心。行者受樂心息，則心自與定法一，澄渟不動，名一心支。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the factor of one-pointedness of mind. Mind being one with the dharma of concentration is named one-pointedness of mind. When the practitioner's mind that receives happiness subsides, the mind is naturally one with the dharma of concentration, clear, still, and unmoving; it is named the factor of one-pointedness of mind.

Chinese Source

四禪四支：一不苦不樂支、二捨支、三念支、四一心支。

Ru-Yi Translation

The fourth dhyāna [has] four factors: first, the factor of neither suffering nor happiness; second, the factor of equanimity; third, the factor of mindfulness; fourth, the factor of one-pointedness of mind.

Chinese Source

一、不苦不樂支。中庸之心不苦不樂。行者欲離三禪時，種種因緣訶責於樂。樂既謝滅，則不動之定與捨俱發，故內心湛然，不苦不樂也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the factor of neither suffering nor happiness. The mind of the middle mean is neither suffering nor happy. When the practitioner wishes to leave the third dhyāna, he reproaches happiness through various causes and conditions. Once happiness has faded and ceased, the unmoving concentration arises together with equanimity; hence the inner mind is clear and calm, neither suffering nor happy.

Chinese Source

二、捨支。離樂不悔，此心名捨。行者既得第四禪不動真定，則捨三禪難捨之樂，不生悔心，故名捨支。亦云：證四禪不動定時，不應取定起動念心。若心行捨，則無動念之乖也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the factor of equanimity. Leaving happiness without regret — this mind is named equanimity. Having attained the unmoving true concentration of the fourth dhyāna, the practitioner relinquishes the hard-to-relinquish happiness of the third dhyāna without giving rise to a mind of regret; hence it is named the factor of equanimity. It is also said: when one realizes the unmoving concentration of the fourth dhyāna, one should not grasp the concentration and give rise to a moving thought; if the mind practices equanimity, there is no fault of moving thought.

Chinese Source

三、念清淨支。念者，愛念也。行者既得四禪真定，當念下地之過，念自功德，方便將養，令不退失，進入勝品，故名為念。亦云是四禪中有不動照了正念分明，故名念支。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the factor of purity of mindfulness. Mindfulness is affectionate mindfulness. Having attained the true concentration of the fourth dhyāna, the practitioner should be mindful of the faults of the lower grounds and mindful of his own merits, nourishing them by expedient means so that they do not regress and are not lost, and advancing into the superior grade; hence it is named mindfulness. It is also said that within this fourth dhyāna there is unmoving, illuminating right mindfulness, distinct and clear; hence it is named the factor of mindfulness.

Chinese Source

四、一心支。心與定法一，名一心支。行者既得四禪捨俱之定，捨念將息，則心無所依，泯然凝寂，一心在定，猶如明鏡，不動淨木無波湛然，而照萬像皆現。何故此四禪中獨名不動定也？初禪覺觀動、二禪喜所動、三禪樂所動，是四禪中先離憂喜，今復除苦樂，故名真定也。三界勝定無復過此。若三乘行人善巧照了分明，則因此定發真無漏。有漏外道無慧方便，入此定時，不壞身色，直滅其心入無想定，謂為涅槃，是為邪倒，非涅槃也。從初禪至四禪，有十八法，皆名支者，支，派也。從四禪中分派出十八功德，故名支也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the factor of one-pointedness of mind. Mind being one with the dharma of concentration is named the factor of one-pointedness of mind. Having attained the concentration of the fourth dhyāna accompanied by equanimity, when equanimity and mindfulness have nurtured it, the practitioner's mind has nothing to rely on; it vanishes into stillness, one-pointed in concentration, like a bright mirror that does not move, or like still water (淨木) without waves, clear and calm, illuminating the myriad images so that all appear. Why is it that only this fourth dhyāna is named the unmoving concentration? The first dhyāna is moved by initial thought and sustained reflection, the second dhyāna is moved by joy, and the third dhyāna is moved by happiness; in this fourth dhyāna, sorrow and joy were already left behind earlier, and now suffering and happiness are removed as well; hence it is named true concentration. Among the concentrations of the three realms, none surpasses this. If practitioners of the three vehicles illuminate skillfully and distinctly, they bring forth the true uncontaminated by means of this concentration. Contaminated non-Buddhists, lacking wisdom and expedient means, when they enter this concentration, without destroying bodily form, simply extinguish their minds and enter the no-perception concentration, taking it as nirvāṇa; this is wrong and inverted, not nirvāṇa. From the first dhyāna to the fourth dhyāna there are eighteen dharmas, all named "factors" (支): 支 means branch. From the four dhyānas eighteen merits branch out; hence they are named factors.

The Four Immeasurable Minds

Chinese Source

四無量心初門第十六

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Four Immeasurable Minds, Number 16

Chinese Source

一慈無量心、二悲無量心、三喜無量心、四捨無量心。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the immeasurable mind of loving-kindness; second, the immeasurable mind of compassion; third, the immeasurable mind of joy; fourth, the immeasurable mind of equanimity.

Chinese Source

次四禪而辨四無量心者，四禪但是自證禪定功德，而未有利他之功，故樂大功德者，當憐愍一切眾生，修慈、悲、喜、捨四無量定。此四通名無量心者，從境以得名，以所緣眾生無量故，能緣之心亦隨境無量，故悉受無量心名。

Ru-Yi Translation

The four immeasurable minds are distinguished next after the four dhyānas because the four dhyānas are only the merits of dhyāna-concentration realized for oneself and do not yet have the merit of benefiting others. Hence one who delights in great merit should have pity on all sentient beings and cultivate the four immeasurable concentrations of loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity. These four are all named "immeasurable minds": the name is gained from the object. Because the sentient beings taken as object are immeasurable, the mind that takes them as object is also immeasurable along with its object; hence all receive the name immeasurable minds.

Chinese Source

一、慈無量心。能與他樂之心，名之為慈。若行者於禪定中，念眾生令得樂時，心數法中生定，名為慈定。是慈相應心，無瞋無恨、無怨無惱，善修得解，廣大無量遍滿十方，名慈無量心。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the immeasurable mind of loving-kindness. The mind able to give happiness to others is named loving-kindness. If the practitioner, within dhyāna-concentration, bears sentient beings in mind so that they obtain happiness, concentration arises among the mental factors; this is named the concentration of loving-kindness. The mind associated with this loving-kindness is without anger and without resentment, without enmity and without vexation; well cultivated, it attains understanding, vast and immeasurable, filling the ten directions; this is named the immeasurable mind of loving-kindness.

Chinese Source

二、悲無量心。能拔他苦之心，名之為悲。若行者於禪定中，念受苦眾生令得解脫時，心數法中生定，名為悲定。是悲相應心，無瞋無恨、無怨無惱，善修得解，廣大無量遍滿十方，是為悲無量心也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the immeasurable mind of compassion. The mind able to remove the suffering of others is named compassion. If the practitioner, within dhyāna-concentration, bears suffering sentient beings in mind so that they obtain liberation, concentration arises among the mental factors; this is named the concentration of compassion. The mind associated with this compassion is without anger and without resentment, without enmity and without vexation; well cultivated, it attains understanding, vast and immeasurable, filling the ten directions; this is the immeasurable mind of compassion.

Chinese Source

三、喜無量心。慶他得樂，生歡悅心，名之為喜。若行者於禪定中，念眾生令離苦得樂歡喜時，心數法中生定，名為喜定。是喜相應心，無瞋無恨、無怨無惱，善修得解，廣大無量遍滿十方，是為喜無量心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the immeasurable mind of joy. Rejoicing that others obtain happiness, giving rise to a gladdened mind, is named joy. If the practitioner, within dhyāna-concentration, bears sentient beings in mind so that they leave suffering, obtain happiness, and rejoice, concentration arises among the mental factors; this is named the concentration of joy. The mind associated with this joy is without anger and without resentment, without enmity and without vexation; well cultivated, it attains understanding, vast and immeasurable, filling the ten directions; this is the immeasurable mind of joy.

Chinese Source

四、捨無量心。若緣於他無憎無愛之心，名之為捨。行者於禪定中，念眾生悉念同得無憎無愛，如證涅槃，寂然清淨。如是念時，心數法中生定，名為捨定。是捨相應心，無瞋無恨、無怨無惱，善修得解，廣大無量遍滿十方，是為捨無量心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the immeasurable mind of equanimity. The mind that takes others as object without hatred and without love is named equanimity. The practitioner, within dhyāna-concentration, bears sentient beings in mind, being mindful that they all alike attain freedom from hatred and love, as in realizing nirvāṇa, quiescent and pure. When he is mindful in this way, concentration arises among the mental factors; this is named the concentration of equanimity. The mind associated with this equanimity is without anger and without resentment, without enmity and without vexation; well cultivated, it attains understanding, vast and immeasurable, filling the ten directions; this is the immeasurable mind of equanimity.

The Four Empty Concentrations

Chinese Source

四空定初門第十七

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Four Empty Concentrations, Number 17

Chinese Source

一虛空處定、二識處定、三無所有處定、四非有想非無想處定。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the concentration of the sphere of space; second, the concentration of the sphere of consciousness; third, the concentration of the sphere of nothingness; fourth, the concentration of the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception.

Chinese Source

次無量心而辨四空處定者，四無量心中雖有大功德，而未免形質之患累。若行人厭色如牢獄者，則心心樂欲出離色籠，故次無量以明四空處定。通言空者，此四定體無形色，故名為空。各依所證之境為處，境法持心，心無分散，故名定也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The concentrations of the four empty spheres are distinguished next after the immeasurable minds because, although the four immeasurable minds have great merit, they have not yet escaped the trouble and burden of bodily form. If a practitioner loathes form as a prison, then mind after mind delights in and desires escape from the cage of form; hence the concentrations of the four empty spheres are made clear next after the immeasurables. They are generally called "empty" because the essence of these four concentrations is without shape or form; hence they are named empty. Each takes the object realized as its sphere (處); the object holds the mind, and the mind is without dispersal; hence they are named concentrations.

Chinese Source

一、空處定。若滅三種色、緣空而入定者。名空處定。行者厭患色如牢獄，心欲出離，即修觀智，破於色故，過一切色相。滅有對相，不念種種相，入無邊虛空處。心與虛空之法相應，是為虛空處定。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the concentration of the sphere of space. Extinguishing the three kinds of form and entering concentration by taking space as object is named the concentration of the sphere of space. The practitioner loathes form as a prison, and his mind wishes to escape; so he cultivates contemplative knowledge to break form, passes beyond all marks of form, extinguishes the marks of resistance, does not attend to the various marks, and enters the sphere of boundless space. The mind corresponds with the dharma of space; this is the concentration of the sphere of space.

Chinese Source

二、識處定。若捨空、緣識而入定者，名識處定。行者厭患虛空，虛空無邊，緣多則散，能破於定。即捨虛空，轉心緣識，心與識法相應，名為識處定。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the concentration of the sphere of consciousness. Abandoning space and entering concentration by taking consciousness as object is named the concentration of the sphere of consciousness. The practitioner loathes space: space is boundless, and when objects are many there is dispersal, which can break concentration. So he abandons space and turns the mind to take consciousness as object; the mind corresponds with the dharma of consciousness; this is named the concentration of the sphere of consciousness.

Chinese Source

三、無所有處定。若捨識處心，依無所有法而入定者，名無所有處定。行者厭患於識，三世之識無邊，緣多則散，能破於定。故捨緣識，轉心依無所有法，心與無所有法相應，名為無所有處定。有人解云：捨多識取少識，緣之入定，名無所有處定也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the concentration of the sphere of nothingness. Abandoning the mind of the sphere of consciousness and entering concentration in reliance on the dharma of nothingness is named the concentration of the sphere of nothingness. The practitioner loathes consciousness: consciousness of the three times is boundless, and when objects are many there is dispersal, which can break concentration. So he abandons taking consciousness as object and turns the mind to rely on the dharma of nothingness; the mind corresponds with the dharma of nothingness; this is named the concentration of the sphere of nothingness. Some explain: abandoning much consciousness, taking little consciousness, and entering concentration by taking it as object, is named the concentration of the sphere of nothingness.

Chinese Source

四、非有想非無想定。若捨二邊之想而入定者，名非有想非無想定。行者厭患無所有處想如癡、有想處如癰如瘡，更有定名非有想非無想處，即捨無所有處，緣念非有非無想之法，心與非有非無想法相應，是為非有想非無想處定。亦云：凡夫外道得此定，謂證涅槃，斷一切想，故言非有想。佛弟子如實知有細想，依四眾而住，故云非無想。得失合而立名，故云非有想非無想處定。略明三界十二門禪，極在於此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception. Abandoning the perceptions of the two extremes and entering concentration is named the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception. The practitioner loathes the perception of the sphere of nothingness as being like folly, and the sphere of perception as being like a boil and like a sore; there is, further, a concentration named the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception. So he abandons the sphere of nothingness and takes as his object the dharma of neither perception nor non-perception; the mind corresponds with the dharma of neither perception nor non-perception; this is the concentration of the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception. It is also said: ordinary people and non-Buddhists who attain this concentration say that they have realized nirvāṇa and cut off all perception; hence it is said to be "not having perception." Disciples of the Buddha know, as it truly is, that there is subtle perception, abiding in dependence on the four groups; hence it is said to be "not without perception." Combining gain and loss, the name is established; hence it is called the concentration of the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception. In brief, the dhyānas of the twelve gates of the three realms reach their limit here.

The Six Wondrous Gates

Chinese Source

六妙門初門第十八

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Six Wondrous Gates, Number 18

Chinese Source

一數、二隨、三止、四觀、五還、六淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, counting; second, following; third, stilling; fourth, contemplating; fifth, returning; sixth, purifying.

Chinese Source

次四空定而辨六妙門者，前來所明禪定雖復深遠，而並是世間舊法。從初至後，厭下攀上，地地之中，都未有觀慧照了出世方便，故凡夫、外道修得。此十二門禪不能發真悟道，是以生死無絕，意在此也。今之六法，前三是定，後三是慧。定愛慧察，能發真明，出離生死，豈同上也。此六通言妙門者，涅槃為妙門，謂能通六法次第相通，能至真妙泥洹，故云妙門。一家所明有十種六妙門，今但略出次第相生一科。六門以為次者，此六門既是亦有漏亦無漏禪，於餘亦有漏亦無漏禪中淺而且局，故以為次也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The six wondrous gates are distinguished next after the four empty concentrations because the dhyāna-concentrations made clear so far, though deep and far-reaching, are all old worldly dharmas. From first to last, they loathe the lower and climb to the higher; in ground after ground there is as yet no expedient means of contemplative wisdom and illumination that transcends the world; hence ordinary people and non-Buddhists cultivate and attain them. These dhyānas of the twelve gates cannot bring forth the true and awaken to the path; this is why birth and death are without end — the point lies here. Now, of these six dharmas, the first three are concentration and the last three are wisdom. With concentration cherishing and wisdom examining, they can bring forth true illumination and escape birth and death; how could they be the same as the above? These six are generally called "wondrous gates": nirvāṇa is the wondrous [goal of these] gates, meaning that through the six dharmas, each passing on to the next in sequence, one can reach the true and wondrous nirvāṇa (泥洹); hence they are called wondrous gates. Our school makes clear ten kinds of six wondrous gates; here only one category, in which they give rise to one another in sequence, is briefly set out. The six gates are placed next because these six gates are dhyāna both contaminated and uncontaminated, and among the other dhyānas that are both contaminated and uncontaminated they are shallow and limited; hence they are placed next.

Chinese Source

一、數息門。攝心在息，從一至十，名之為數。行者為修無漏真法，先須調心入定。欲界麁散難攝，非數不治，故須善調身息。從一至十，則麁亂靜息，心神停住，是為入定之要，故以數息為妙門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the gate of counting the breath. Gathering the mind on the breath, from one to ten, is named counting. In order to cultivate the uncontaminated true dharma, the practitioner must first regulate the mind and enter concentration. The desire realm is coarse and scattered and hard to gather in; without counting it cannot be treated; hence one must skillfully regulate body and breath. From one to ten, coarse disturbance quiets and ceases, and mind and spirit stay and abide; this is the key to entering concentration; hence counting the breath is taken as a wondrous gate.

Chinese Source

二、隨息門。細心依息，知入知出，故曰為隨。行者雖因數息心住，而禪定未發。若猶存數，則心有起念之失，故須放數修隨。心依於息，入時知入、出時知出，長短、冷暖皆悉知之。若心安明淨，因是則諸禪自發，故以隨為門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the gate of following the breath. A subtle mind relying on the breath, knowing it entering and knowing it leaving, is therefore called following. Although through counting the breath the practitioner's mind abides, dhyāna-concentration has not yet arisen. If one still keeps counting, the mind has the fault of producing thoughts; hence one must let go of counting and cultivate following. The mind relies on the breath: when it enters, one knows it is entering; when it leaves, one knows it is leaving; long or short, cold or warm, all are known. If the mind is at peace, bright, and pure, through this the various dhyānas arise of themselves; hence following is taken as a gate.

Chinese Source

三、止門。息心靜慮，名之為止。行者雖因隨息，心安明淨，而定猶未發。若心依隨，則微有起想之亂。澄渟安隱，莫若於止，故捨隨修止，是中多用凝心止也。凝心寂慮，心無波動，則諸禪定自然開發，故以止為門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the gate of stilling. Stilling the mind in quiet absorption is named stilling. Although through following the breath the practitioner's mind is at peace, bright, and pure, concentration has still not arisen. If the mind relies on following, there is the slight disturbance of producing perceptions. For clear stillness and peaceful security there is nothing like stilling; hence one lets go of following and cultivates stilling, and here one mostly uses the stilling of concentrating the mind. When the mind is concentrated and reflection is quiet, the mind without ripples, the various dhyāna-concentrations naturally open forth; hence stilling is taken as a gate.

Chinese Source

四、觀門。分別推析之心名為觀。行者雖因止證諸禪定，而解慧未發。若住定心，則有無明味著之乖，故須推尋檢析所證禪定，是中多用實觀四念處也。若觀心分明，則知五眾虛誑，破四顛倒及我等十六知見。顛倒既無，無漏方便因此開發，故以觀為門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the gate of contemplating. A mind that discriminates and analyzes is named contemplation. Although through stilling the practitioner has realized the various dhyāna-concentrations, understanding and wisdom have not yet arisen. If one abides in the concentrated mind, there is the fault of ignorance, savoring and attachment; hence one must search out and examine the dhyāna-concentrations realized, and here one mostly uses the real contemplation of the four foundations of mindfulness. If the contemplating mind is distinct and clear, one knows that the five groups are hollow and deceptive, and one breaks the four inversions and the sixteen kinds of knowing and seeing such as self. Once inversions are no more, the uncontaminated expedient means unfold through this; hence contemplation is taken as a gate.

Chinese Source

五、還門。轉心反照，名之為還。行者雖修觀照，而真明未發。若計有我能觀，析破於顛倒，則計我之惑還附觀而生，同於外道，故云是諸外道計著觀空智慧，不得解脫。若覺此患，即當轉心反照能觀之心。若知能觀之心虛誑無實，即附觀執我之倒自亡，因是無漏方便自然而朗，故以還為門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the gate of returning. Turning the mind back to illuminate is named returning. Although the practitioner cultivates contemplative illumination, true illumination has not yet unfolded. If he reckons that there is a self that can contemplate and that analyzes and breaks the inversions, then the delusion of reckoning a self arises again, attached to contemplation, the same as non-Buddhists; hence it is said, "These non-Buddhists reckon and attach to the wisdom of contemplating emptiness, and do not attain liberation." If one becomes aware of this trouble, one should turn the mind back to illuminate the mind that contemplates. If one knows that the contemplating mind is hollow, deceptive, and without reality, the inversion of grasping a self attached to contemplation vanishes of itself, and through this the uncontaminated expedient means naturally becomes clear; hence returning is taken as a gate.

Chinese Source

六、淨門。心無所依，妄波不起，名之為淨。行者修還之時，雖能破觀之倒，若真明未發，而住無能所，即是受念，故令心智穢濁。覺知此已，不住不著，泯然清淨，因此真明開發，即斷三界結使，證三乘道。故云：「其清淨得一心者，則萬邪滅矣。」以淨為門，意在此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, the gate of purifying. The mind with nothing to rely on, the waves of delusion not arising, is named purity. When the practitioner cultivates returning, although he can break the inversion of contemplation, if true illumination has not unfolded and he abides in the absence of subject and object, this is a received thought (受念); hence it makes mind and knowledge defiled and turbid. Having become aware of this, he neither abides nor attaches and vanishes into purity; through this true illumination unfolds, he severs the fetters and agents of the three realms, and he realizes the path of the three vehicles. Hence it is said: "When one is pure and attains one-pointedness of mind, then the myriad wrongs are extinguished." The point of taking purity as a gate lies here.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

Three hundred topics, seven fascicles, three fascicles. The preface speaks of "three hundred topics cut into seven fascicles" but says the present work "has come to only three fascicles"; the table of contents lists sixty gateways. The sixth fascicle that Zhiyi several times promises (for the full lists of afflictions, evil deeds, and precepts) is not part of the received text.

The verse on name and form. Quoted from the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom (T1509; the verse ends "出於名色者"). Zhiyi cites the same verse in Calming and Contemplation and in the Explanation of the Sequential Gates of Dhyāna-Pāramitā (site: Shi Chan Boluomi). The Treatise's simile of physician, medicine, nurse, and dietary prohibition (Part 1, Gateway 14) is also in T1509 ("佛如醫王，法如良藥，僧如瞻病人，戒如服藥禁忌"); Zhiyi words it as "recollecting" the Buddha, Dharma, Saṅgha, and precepts.

The Necklace Sūtra. The quotation in Gateway 6 ("The two dharmas of views and attachment…") paraphrases a passage of the Bodhisattva Necklace Original Acts Sūtra (T1485), which speaks of "見著" within "one dharma realm"; the exact wording was not located. The title follows the site's Mohe Zhiguan ("Necklace Sūtra").

"Agents" (使). The site's Mohe Zhiguan and Sijiaoyi use "agents" for 使; this draft keeps it and translates Zhiyi's gloss ("使 has the meaning of driving and employing"). "Five divisions" (五行) means the four truths plus the path of cultivation, under which the agents are counted; it is distinct from the "sixteen practices" (十六行) of Part 2.

Initial thought and sustained reflection (覺／觀). As on the site's Shi Chan Boluomi, 覺觀 in the dhyāna factors are "initial thought" and "sustained reflection"; elsewhere 觀 is "contemplation." The Chinese is given in parentheses at the definitions.

Intent (意). 意 is "intent" throughout (as in the site's Mohe Zhiguan: mind, intent, and consciousness), so 意入／意界／意識 are "the intent sense-field," "the intent element," "intent-consciousness." In Gateway 4, "intent serves as intent-consciousness" (意為意識) is literal; alternatively: "intent [as faculty] gives rise to intent-consciousness."

Folly and delusion (癡／惑). 癡 is "folly" and 惑 "delusion," following the round-two Jueyi Sanmei draft, so that the three poisons (greed, anger, folly) stay distinct from 惑 (the deluded, delusive obstructions).

Two views of the body. Gateway 9 says that "in one division, passing through the three realms, there are two views of the body," but for extreme views "three." By the count in Gateway 10 (view of the body under the truth of suffering in all three realms) one expects three; 二 may be a copying error for 三.

Repeated "entanglements." The list 結流愛扼纏蓋纏 (Gateway 6) names 纏 twice; translated as it stands. 扼 is read as 軛 "yoke."

用僧鬘物. Read as "using the property of the Saṅgha"; 鬘 ("garland") is probably a copying error for 祇 (僧祇物, Saṅgha property).

淨木 "still water." In Gateway 15 the simile "不動淨木無波湛然" makes sense only if 木 is a copying error for 水; the English follows the sense and gives the Chinese.

受念 (Gateway 18). "This is a received thought" is literal; the point seems to be that dwelling in the absence of subject and object is itself still something taken up by the mind. Alternatively: "this is [still] feeling and thought."

Trapuṣa (提謂). Zhiyi follows the apocryphal Trapuṣa Sūtra (提謂經) for the first lay refuges and precepts; the quotation on the five precepts and the five planets is from that tradition (cited also in Fanyi Mingyi Ji, T2131). Source text not located in the Taishō folder.

"These non-Buddhists reckon and attach…" and "When one is pure and attains one-pointedness of mind…" (Gateway 18): sources not located; the second is cited in Zhiyi's Mohe Zhiguan (T1911).

Site parallels. Gateways 15–18 correspond to fascicles 5–7 of the site's Shi Chan Boluomi; factor names (inner purity, equanimity, mindfulness, wisdom, one-pointedness of mind) follow it.
