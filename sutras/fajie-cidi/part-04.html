From the One Hundred and Eight Samādhis to the Three Foundations of Mindfulness

Part 4 covers the last fourteen gateways, 47 to 60, which form the second half of the Lower Fascicle. They move from what the bodhisattva attains to what belongs to buddhas alone. The first group continues the bodhisattva's equipment: the one hundred and eight samādhis and the five hundred dhāraṇīs, of which Zhiyi explains only a few samples taken from the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom; then the four means of gathering beings, the six harmonious respects that keep the bodhisattva and those he gathers together, the eight kinds of transformation that win their faith, and the four kinds of unhindered knowledge that let him teach. With the ten powers the text turns to the buddhas' own dharmas: the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses, the eighteen unshared qualities, and great loving-kindness and compassion, which differ from the four immeasurables because they really give happiness and remove suffering. The final four gateways show how this compassion appears to beings: the thirty-two marks and eighty minor features of the body, the eight qualities of the Buddha's voice, and the three foundations of mindfulness, by which the Buddha's mind stays even whether listeners attend or turn away.

The One Hundred and Eight Samādhis

Chinese Source

百八三昧初門第四十七

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the One Hundred and Eight Samādhis, Number 47

Chinese Source

百八之數既多非，可具列於後。

Ru-Yi Translation

The number one hundred and eight is large; they cannot all be listed below.

Chinese Source

次十喻而辯百八三昧者，菩薩若善以十喻開曉其心，則所修十八空觀自然明了。以是空慧，照諸禪定種種法門，無染無著，則能出生諸菩薩百八三昧，諸佛三昧不動等，百則有二十，如是乃至無量三昧，於諸三昧遊戲自在。是諸三昧不可思議，不與二乘之所共也。今此百八乃至無量，通名三昧者，三昧名通，猶同前翻釋。但首楞嚴等百八境界，體用微妙深廣，故次而辯之。今於百八中，略出初三後一，以成次第章門。百八等名相既多，豈可具辯？若欲遍知，當尋《大智度論》。

Ru-Yi Translation

The one hundred and eight samādhis are discussed next after the ten similes. If the bodhisattva skillfully opens and enlightens his mind with the ten similes, the contemplation of the eighteen emptinesses that he cultivates naturally becomes clear. When, with this wisdom of emptiness, he illuminates all kinds of dharma-gates of dhyāna-concentration without defilement and without attachment, he can produce the bodhisattvas' one hundred and eight samādhis and the samādhis of the buddhas — the unmoving and the rest, of which there are one hundred and twenty — and so on to immeasurable samādhis, and he roams at ease with mastery in all samādhis. These samādhis are inconceivable and are not shared by the two vehicles. Now these, from one hundred and eight up to the immeasurable, are all named "samādhi"; the name samādhi is general, and its translation and explanation are the same as before. But the one hundred and eight realms, the Śūraṅgama and the rest, are subtle, wondrous, deep, and broad in essence and function; hence they are discussed next. Now, out of the one hundred and eight, I briefly give the first three and the last one, to make up a chapter-heading in the sequence. Since the names and marks of the one hundred and eight are so many, how could they be discussed in full? Those who wish to know them all should search the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom.

Chinese Source

一、首楞嚴三昧。首楞嚴三昧者，首楞嚴，秦言健相，分別知諸三昧行相多少、深淺，如大將知諸兵力多少也。菩薩得是三昧，諸煩惱魔及魔人無能壞者。譬如轉輪聖王主兵寶將所住至處無能壞伏，故名健相三昧也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the Śūraṅgama samādhi. As for the Śūraṅgama samādhi: Śūraṅgama in the language of Qin is "the mark of the heroic." It distinguishes and knows the marks of practice of all samādhis — how many there are and how shallow or deep — just as a great general knows how great or small the strength of all his troops is. When the bodhisattva attains this samādhi, none of the māras of affliction or the people of Māra can destroy him. It is like the general who is the military treasure of a wheel-turning sage king: wherever he goes and stays, none can destroy or subdue him. Hence it is named the samādhi of the mark of the heroic.

Chinese Source

二、寶印三昧。寶印三昧者，能印諸三昧於諸寶中，法寶是實，今世、後世乃至涅槃，能為利益。如佛語比丘：「為汝說法，所謂法印。」法即是實，印即是解脫門。若三藏教門以三法為法印，若摩訶衍教門但有諸法實相一法印。與實相般若相應三昧，名為寶印三昧也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the jewel-seal samādhi. As for the jewel-seal samādhi: it can seal all samādhis. Among all jewels, the Dharma jewel is the real one; in this life, in later lives, and up to nirvāṇa, it can bring benefit. As the Buddha told the bhikṣus: "I teach you the Dharma, namely the dharma-seal." The Dharma is precisely the real; the seal is precisely the gate of liberation. In the teaching-gate of the Tripiṭaka, three dharmas are taken as the dharma-seal; in the teaching-gate of the Mahāyāna there is only the single dharma-seal of the true mark of all dharmas. The samādhi that corresponds to the prajñā of the true mark is named the jewel-seal samādhi.

Chinese Source

三、師子遊步三昧。師子遊步三昧者，菩薩得是三昧，於一切三昧中出入遲速，皆得自在。譬如眾獸戲時，若見師子，率皆怖攝。師子戲時，於諸群獸，強者則殺，伏者則放。菩薩亦如是，得是三昧，於諸外道，強者破之，信者度之，故名師子遊步三昧。下一百四三昧亦應如是，具出《大智度論》。若欲知之，自當尋彼論。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the lion's-stride samādhi. As for the lion's-stride samādhi: when the bodhisattva attains this samādhi, in entering and emerging from all samādhis, slowly or quickly, he attains mastery in every case. It is like this: when the beasts are at play, if they see a lion, they are all frightened and cower. When the lion plays among the herds of beasts, the strong it kills and the submissive it lets go. The bodhisattva is also like this: having attained this samādhi, among the non-Buddhists he breaks the strong and delivers those who have faith; hence it is named the lion's-stride samādhi. The remaining one hundred and four samādhis below should also be like this; they are set out in full in the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom. Those who wish to know them should search that treatise for themselves.

Chinese Source

一百八、離著虛空不染三昧。離著虛空不染三昧者，菩薩行般若波羅蜜，觀諸法畢竟空，不生不滅，如虛空，無物可喻。鈍根菩薩著此虛空，得此三昧，故離著虛空等諸法，亦不染著是三昧。如沒在泥中，有人挽出，鎖脚為奴。有三昧離著虛空而復著，此三昧亦如是。今是三昧能離著虛空，亦自離著，故名離著虛空不染三昧也。如是諸佛菩薩無量不可思議諸三昧，是中應具列其名目，略釋其相，是事云云，廣出餘法門也。

Ru-Yi Translation

One hundred and eighth, the samādhi of being free from attachment to empty space and undefiled. As for the samādhi of being free from attachment to empty space and undefiled: the bodhisattva practices prajñā-pāramitā and contemplates all dharmas as utterly empty, neither arising nor ceasing, like empty space, with nothing to which they can be compared. Bodhisattvas of dull faculties become attached to this empty space; attaining this samādhi, they are therefore freed from attachment to empty space and all dharmas, and are also not attached to this samādhi. It is like someone sunk in mud whom another person pulls out, then shackles his feet and makes him a slave. There is a samādhi that frees one from attachment to empty space but is then itself clung to; this samādhi is also like this. Now this samādhi can free one from attachment to empty space and is itself also free from attachment; hence it is named the samādhi of being free from attachment to empty space and undefiled. Such immeasurable, inconceivable samādhis of the buddhas and bodhisattvas ought to have their names and headings listed in full here and their marks briefly explained; this matter (and so on) is set out at length in other dharma-gates.

The Five Hundred Dhāraṇīs

Chinese Source

五百陀羅尼初門第四十八

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Five Hundred Dhāraṇīs, Number 48

Chinese Source

五百之數既多，非可具列於第下也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The number five hundred is large; they cannot all be listed below.

Chinese Source

次諸三昧而辯陀羅尼門者，若依論解三昧，但是心相應法。陀羅尼，或心相應、或不相應，故異於三昧。若《法華》所明普現色身三昧，猶是解一切眾生語言陀羅尼。以三昧、陀羅尼既是相成之法，故次而辯之。此五百通名陀羅尼者，陀羅尼是西土之言，此土翻云能持，或言能遮。言能持者，集種種善法，持令不散不失。譬如完器盛水，水不漏散。能遮者，惡不善根生，能遮不令起，故云能遮。又翻為總持，隨有若名若義、若行地功德，皆悉能持，故名總持。今此五百並有持、遮總持之義，故通名陀羅尼。陀羅尼者，略說則有五百，廣明則有八萬四千，乃至無量，悉是菩薩諸佛所得法門，名義皆不與二乘人共也。今依《大智度論》略辯三陀羅尼，以成次第章門。五百之數名義既多，豈可具辯？

Ru-Yi Translation

The dhāraṇī-gates are discussed next after the samādhis. If one explains according to the Treatise, a samādhi is only a dharma associated with the mind, while a dhāraṇī is either associated with the mind or not associated [with it]; hence it differs from samādhi. The samādhi of universally manifesting form-bodies made clear in the Lotus is still the dhāraṇī of understanding the languages of all sentient beings. Since samādhi and dhāraṇī are dharmas that accomplish each other, they are discussed next. These five hundred are all named "dhāraṇī": dhāraṇī is a word of the Western Land, which in this land is translated "able to hold," or else "able to block." "Able to hold" means gathering all kinds of wholesome dharmas and holding them so that they do not scatter or get lost, like a sound vessel holding water so that the water does not leak or spill. "Able to block" means that when evil, unwholesome roots arise, it can block them and not let them arise; hence it is called "able to block." It is also translated "total retention": whatever there is — names, meanings, or the merits of practice and grounds — it can hold them all; hence it is named total retention. These five hundred all have the meaning of holding, blocking, and total retention; hence they are all named dhāraṇī. Of dhāraṇīs, explained briefly there are five hundred; made clear at length there are eighty-four thousand, up to immeasurable; all are dharma-gates attained by bodhisattvas and buddhas, and neither their names nor their meanings are shared with persons of the two vehicles. Now, according to the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom, I briefly discuss three dhāraṇīs, to make up a chapter-heading in the sequence. Since the names and meanings of the five hundred are so many, how could they be discussed in full?

Chinese Source

一、聞持陀羅尼。得此陀羅尼者，一切語言諸法，耳所聞者皆不忘失。所謂十方諸佛及弟子眾有所演說，一時能聞，憶持不忘，故名聞持陀羅尼，即是名持。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the dhāraṇī of hearing and retaining. One who attains this dhāraṇī does not forget or lose any words or dharmas that the ear hears. That is, whatever the buddhas of the ten directions and their assemblies of disciples expound, one can hear at one time and remember and retain without forgetting; hence it is named the dhāraṇī of hearing and retaining. This is retention of names.

Chinese Source

二、分別陀羅尼。得是陀羅尼，諸眾生諸法，大小好惡，分別悉知，故分別陀羅尼，即是義持。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the discerning dhāraṇī. One who attains this dhāraṇī discerns and knows completely all sentient beings and all dharmas, great and small, fine and ugly; hence the discerning dhāraṇī. This is retention of meanings.

Chinese Source

三、入音聲陀羅尼。得此陀羅尼者，聞一切語言音聲，不喜不瞋。一切眾生，如恒沙等劫壽惡言罵詈，心不增恨。一切眾生，如恒沙等劫讚歎供養，其心不動不喜不著。是為入音聲陀羅尼，即是行持也。復有寂滅陀羅尼、無邊陀羅尼、威德陀羅尼、隨地觀陀羅尼、華嚴陀羅尼、虛空藏陀羅尼、海藏陀羅尼、分別諸陀羅尼、明諸法義陀羅尼，如是等略說，乃至五百陀羅尼，廣明則無量陀羅尼。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the dhāraṇī of entering sounds. One who attains this dhāraṇī, on hearing all words and sounds, is neither delighted nor angered. Though all sentient beings were to revile and abuse him with evil words for eons as many as the sands of the Ganges, his mind would not increase in resentment. Though all sentient beings were to praise him and make offerings to him for eons as many as the sands of the Ganges, his mind would not be moved, not delighted, not attached. This is the dhāraṇī of entering sounds; it is retention of practice. There are also the dhāraṇī of quiescence, the boundless dhāraṇī, the dhāraṇī of majestic virtue, the dhāraṇī of contemplation according to the grounds, the flower-adornment dhāraṇī, the dhāraṇī of the treasury of space, the dhāraṇī of the ocean treasury, the dhāraṇī of discerning all dhāraṇīs, the dhāraṇī of illuminating the meaning of all dharmas — explained briefly like this, up to five hundred dhāraṇīs; made clear at length, there are immeasurable dhāraṇīs.

The Four Means of Gathering

Chinese Source

四攝初門第四十九

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Four Means of Gathering, Number 49

Chinese Source

一布施、二愛語、三利行、四同事。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, giving; second, kind speech; third, beneficial action; fourth, working together.

Chinese Source

次諸陀羅尼而辯四攝者，菩薩若內具諸三昧陀羅尼，自行既充，必須外引含識，同己所行之道。然大士利物廣濟，莫若四無量心與四攝法。但四無量心名目既已先辯，豈繁重出？四攝善巧接引之要，故次而明之。此四通言攝者，眾生情所愛者，即是此之四法。若大士用此四法，同情接引，則物之所歸焉。若眾生依附，方乃導以大乘正道而度脫之。故云：「先以欲鉤牽，後令入佛道。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The four means of gathering are discussed next after the dhāraṇīs. If the bodhisattva is inwardly complete in all samādhis and dhāraṇīs and his own practice is already full, he must outwardly draw in the beings with consciousness to the same path that he practices. Now, for the great being's benefiting of beings and broad relief, nothing equals the four immeasurable minds and the four dharmas of gathering. But since the names and headings of the four immeasurable minds have already been discussed earlier, why repeat them at length? The four [means of] gathering are the key to skillfully receiving and drawing [beings] in; hence they are made clear next. These four are generally called "gathering" because what the feelings of sentient beings love is precisely these four dharmas. If the great being uses these four dharmas, receiving and drawing [beings] in by sharing their feelings, then beings turn to him. When sentient beings rely on and attach themselves [to him], he then guides them with the right path of the Great Vehicle and delivers them to liberation. Hence it is said: "First draw them with the hook of desire; afterward cause them to enter the buddha-path."

Chinese Source

一、布施攝。菩薩以無所捨心行於二種布施，能攝眾生：一者財施、二者法施。若為樂財眾生，即以財施而攝取之；若以樂法眾生，即以法施而攝取之。以此二施能利益一切，眾生既蒙恩利，因是生親愛心而隨受道，得住真理，故名檀布施攝也。二者施相，略如前檀波羅蜜中分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, gathering by giving. The bodhisattva, with a mind of nothing given up, practices two kinds of giving and can gather sentient beings: first, the giving of goods; second, the giving of the Dharma. For sentient beings who delight in goods, he gathers them in with the giving of goods; for sentient beings who delight in the Dharma, he gathers them in with the giving of the Dharma. With these two kinds of giving he can benefit all. Since sentient beings receive kindness and benefit, they therefore give rise to a mind of affection, follow him and receive the path, and come to abide in true principle; hence it is named gathering by dāna, giving. The marks of the two kinds of giving are roughly as distinguished earlier under dāna-pāramitā.

Chinese Source

二、愛語攝。菩薩若以善軟之言隨順一切根情，安慰開喻，則一切眾生之所樂聞，因是生親愛心，依附菩薩而隨受道，得住真理，故名愛語攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, gathering by kind speech. If the bodhisattva, with good and gentle words that accord with the faculties and feelings of all, comforts, opens, and instructs them, then all sentient beings are glad to hear him; because of this they give rise to a mind of affection, rely on the bodhisattva and follow him to receive the path, and come to abide in true principle; hence it is named gathering by kind speech.

Chinese Source

三、利行攝。菩薩隨起身、口、意行，能令一切各霑利益。眾生既蒙勝利，以欣所得利故，因是生親愛心，依附受道得真理，故名利行攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, gathering by beneficial action. The bodhisattva, in whatever actions of body, speech, and intent he undertakes, can cause all to be steeped in benefit. Since sentient beings receive excellent benefit and rejoice in the benefit they have gained, they therefore give rise to a mind of affection, rely on him, receive the path, and attain true principle; hence it is named gathering by beneficial action.

Chinese Source

四、同事攝。菩薩用法眼明見眾生根緣，故一切隨有同欣之者，即分形散影，普和其光，同彼事業，各使霑益。既巧同其事，因是生親愛心，依附受道，得住涅槃，故同事益物名為攝也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, gathering by working together. The bodhisattva, with the Dharma-eye, clearly sees the faculties and conditions of sentient beings; hence, wherever there are those who share in some delight, he divides his form and scatters his reflections, universally dims his light to blend in, joins in their undertakings, and causes each to be steeped in benefit. Since he skillfully joins in their affairs, they therefore give rise to a mind of affection, rely on him, receive the path, and come to abide in nirvāṇa; hence benefiting beings by working together is named gathering.

The Six Harmonious Respects

Chinese Source

六和敬初門第五十

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Six Harmonious Respects, Number 50

Chinese Source

一同戒、二同見、三同行、四身慈、五口慈、六意慈。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the same precepts; second, the same views; third, the same practice; fourth, bodily loving-kindness; fifth, verbal loving-kindness; sixth, mental loving-kindness.

Chinese Source

次四攝而辯六和敬者，菩薩既能善用四種同情之法攝得眾生，為成就故，必須久處。若不和同愛敬，則兩不和合，不得盡成般若，是為魔事。若善用六和，則與一切冥同，必得善始令終，則能安立一切於菩提大道，故次四攝而明之也。此六通名和敬者，外同他善謂之為和，內自謙卑名之為敬。菩薩與物共事，外則同物行善，內則常自謙卑，故名和敬。

Ru-Yi Translation

The six harmonious respects are discussed next after the four means of gathering. Once the bodhisattva has been able, by skillfully using the four dharmas of sharing feelings, to gather sentient beings, he must remain with them for a long time in order to bring them to accomplishment. If they are not in harmony, sharing affection and respect, the two sides will not come together and he will not be able to accomplish prajñā fully; this is the work of Māra. If he skillfully uses the six harmonies, he becomes one with all in a hidden way and will surely make a good beginning and bring it to a [good] end; then he can establish all in the great path of bodhi. Hence they are made clear next after the four means of gathering. These six are all named "harmonious respect" because outwardly being the same as others in the good is called harmony, and inwardly being humble oneself is named respect. When the bodhisattva shares affairs with beings, outwardly he practices the good together with them, and inwardly he is always humble; hence it is named harmonious respect.

Chinese Source

一、同戒和敬。菩薩通達實相，知罪不可得。為欲安立眾生於實相理，以戒方便巧同一切，持諸戒品，無有乖諍。亦知眾生同此戒善，不斷不常，未來必得菩提大果，是以敬之如佛，故說同戒為和敬。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, harmonious respect in the same precepts. The bodhisattva penetrates the true mark and knows that offenses cannot be obtained. Wishing to establish sentient beings in the principle of the true mark, he skillfully shares all [their ways] by the expedient of precepts and keeps all the classes of precepts, without any conflict or dispute. He also knows that sentient beings, sharing the good of these precepts, neither cut off nor permanent, will surely attain the great fruit of bodhi in the future; this is why he respects them like buddhas. Hence the same precepts are taught as harmonious respect.

Chinese Source

二、同見和敬。菩薩通達實相，不得諸法，不知不見。為欲安立眾生於實相正見，方便巧同一切，種種知見無有乖諍。亦知眾生，因此知見分別，增進開解，必得種智圓明，是以敬之如佛，故說同見為和敬。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, harmonious respect in the same views. The bodhisattva penetrates the true mark, obtains no dharma, does not know and does not see. Wishing to establish sentient beings in the right view of the true mark, he skillfully shares, by expedients, all [their] various kinds of knowing and seeing, without any conflict or dispute. 八十種好初門第五十八

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Eighty Minor Features, Number 58

Chinese Source

八十種好名目既多，非可具列第下。

Ru-Yi Translation

The names and headings of the eighty minor features are many; they cannot all be listed below.

Chinese Source

次三十二相而辯八十種好者，相、好乃同是色法，皆為莊嚴，顯發佛身。但相總而好別，相若無好則不圓滿。輪王、釋、梵亦有相，以無好故相不微妙，故次相而辯好也。通云好者，可愛樂也。以八十種好莊嚴身，故天人一切之所愛樂，故云好也。即是以慈修身，故有此清淨相好之身業也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The eighty minor features are discussed next after the thirty-two marks. Marks and features are alike dharmas of form, and all serve to adorn and display the Buddha's body. But the marks are general and the features are particular; if the marks lacked the features, they would not be complete. Wheel-turning kings, Śakra, and Brahmā also have marks, but because they have no features, their marks are not subtle and wondrous; hence the features are discussed next after the marks. They are generally called "features" (好) because they are lovable and delightful. Because the body is adorned with the eighty minor features, all gods and humans love and delight in it; hence they are called features. This is precisely cultivating the body with loving-kindness, so that there is bodily karma of these pure marks and features.

Chinese Source

六十一、身持重；六十二、其身大；六十三、身長；六十四、手足軟淨滑澤；六十五、四邊光各一丈長；六十六、光照身而行；六十七、等視眾生；六十八、不輕眾生；六十九、隨眾生音聲不增不減；七十、說法不著；七十一、隨眾生語言而說法；七十二、發音報眾聲；七十三、次第有因緣說法；七十四、一切眾生不能盡觀相；七十五、觀無厭足；七十六、髮長好；七十七、髮不亂；七十八、髮旋好；七十九、髮色青珠；八十、手足有德相（《坐禪三昧經》云「胸有德字，手足有吉字。」）

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixty-first, the body bears itself with gravity; sixty-second, his body is large; sixty-third, the body is tall; sixty-fourth, the hands and feet are soft, clean, smooth, and glossy; sixty-fifth, light extends one zhang on all four sides; sixty-sixth, the light shines on the body as he walks; sixty-seventh, he regards sentient beings equally; sixty-eighth, he does not slight sentient beings; sixty-ninth, according to the voices of sentient beings, [his voice] neither increases nor decreases; seventieth, he teaches the Dharma without attachment; seventy-first, he teaches the Dharma according to the languages of sentient beings; seventy-second, his utterance answers the many voices; seventy-third, he teaches the Dharma in sequence and with causes and conditions; seventy-fourth, no sentient being can see all his marks completely; seventy-fifth, one never tires of looking at him; seventy-sixth, the hair of the head is long and fine; seventy-seventh, the hair of the head is not disordered; seventy-eighth, the hair of the head curls finely; seventy-ninth, the hair of the head is the color of blue pearls; eightieth, the hands and feet have the marks of virtue (the Sūtra on the Samādhi of Seated Meditation says: "On the chest there is the character 德 [virtue]; on the hands and feet there is the character 吉 [auspicious].").

The Eight Voices

Chinese Source

八音初門第五十九

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Eight Voices, Number 59

Chinese Source

一極好、二柔軟、三和適、四尊慧、五不女、六不誤、七深遠、八不竭。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the utmost good; second, soft; third, harmonious; fourth, venerable and wise; fifth, not feminine; sixth, not mistaken; seventh, deep and far-reaching; eighth, inexhaustible.

Chinese Source

次相好而辯八音者，若佛以相好端嚴，發見者之善心；音聲理當清妙，起聞者之信敬，故次相好而明八音也。此八通云音者，詮理之聲謂之為音。佛所出聲，凡有詮辯，言辭清雅，聞者無厭，聽之無足。能為一切作與樂拔苦因緣，莫若聞聲之益，即是以慈修口，故有八音清淨之口業。

Ru-Yi Translation

The eight voices are discussed next after the marks and features. If the Buddha, with the upright adornment of his marks and features, brings forth wholesome minds in those who see him, his voice, as a matter of principle, should be clear and wondrous, arousing faith and respect in those who hear him; hence the eight voices are made clear next after the marks and features. These eight are generally called "voice" because sound that expresses principle is called voice (音). Whatever the sound the Buddha utters expresses and discusses, his words are clear and refined; those who hear never weary of it, and those who listen are never satisfied. For acting as a cause and condition of giving happiness and removing suffering for all, nothing equals the benefit of hearing his voice. This is precisely cultivating speech with loving-kindness, so that there is the pure verbal karma of the eight voices.

Chinese Source

一、極好音。一切諸天賢聖雖各有好音，好之未極。佛報圓極，故出音聲清雅，能令聞者無厭，皆入好道。好中之最好，故名極好音也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the voice of the utmost good. Although all the gods, worthies, and sages each have a good voice, its goodness is not yet utmost. The Buddha's reward is perfect and ultimate; hence the sound of his voice is clear and refined and can cause those who hear it never to weary of it, and all enter the good path. Since it is the best of the good, it is named the voice of the utmost good.

Chinese Source

二、柔軟音。佛德慈善故，所出音聲巧順物情，能令聞者喜悅，聽之無足，皆捨剛強之心，自然入律行，故名柔軟音。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the soft voice. Because the Buddha's virtue is kind and good, the sound of the voice he utters skillfully accords with the feelings of beings; it can make those who hear it glad, never satisfied in listening, and all give up their hard and stubborn minds and enter naturally into the practice of discipline. Hence it is named the soft voice.

Chinese Source

三、和適音。佛居中道之理，巧解從容，故所出音聲調和中適，能令聞者心皆和融，因聲會理，故名和適音。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the harmonious voice. Because the Buddha dwells in the principle of the middle way and his skillful understanding is unhurried, the sound of the voice he utters is harmonious and well-tempered; it can make the minds of those who hear it all harmonious and melted, and through the voice they meet principle. Hence it is named the harmonious voice.

Chinese Source

四、尊慧音。佛德尊高，慧心明徹，故所出音聲能令聞者尊重，解慧開明，故名尊慧音。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the venerable and wise voice. Because the Buddha's virtue is venerable and lofty and his wise mind is clear and penetrating, the sound of the voice he utters can make those who hear it revere him, and their understanding and wisdom open and become clear. Hence it is named the venerable and wise voice.

Chinese Source

五、不女音。佛住首楞嚴定，常有世雄之德，久已離於雌軟之心，故所出言聲能令一切聞者敬畏，天魔外道莫不歸伏，名不女音。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the voice that is not feminine. The Buddha abides in the Śūraṅgama concentration and always has the virtue of the hero of the world; he has long since left behind the timid, soft mind. Hence the sound of the words he utters can make all who hear them respectful and awed, and the heavenly māras and non-Buddhists all turn to him and submit. It is named the voice that is not feminine.

Chinese Source

六、不誤音。佛智圓明，照了無謬，故所出音聲詮論無失，能令聞者各獲正見，離於九十五種之邪非，故名不誤音。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, the voice that is not mistaken. The Buddha's knowledge is perfect and clear, illuminating without error; hence the sound of the voice he utters expresses and discusses without fault, and can cause those who hear it each to obtain right view and leave behind the wrongs of the ninety-five kinds [of non-Buddhist teaching]. Hence it is named the voice that is not mistaken.

Chinese Source

七、深遠音。佛智照窮如如實際之底，行位高極，故所出音聲從臍而起，徹至十方，令近聞非大、遠聞不小，皆悟甚深之理，梵行高遠，故名深遠音也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, the deep and far-reaching voice. The Buddha's knowledge illuminates to the very bottom of suchness and the real limit, and his practice and stage are lofty and ultimate; hence the sound of the voice he utters arises from the navel and penetrates the ten directions, so that heard near it is not loud and heard far it is not faint; all awaken to the most profound principle, and their pure conduct is lofty and far-reaching. Hence it is named the deep and far-reaching voice.

Chinese Source

八、不竭音。如來極果，願行無盡，是以住於無盡法藏，故出音聲滔滔無盡，其響不竭，能令聞者尋其語義無盡無遺，至成無盡常住之果，故名不竭音也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, the inexhaustible voice. The ultimate fruit of the Tathāgata — his vows and practice are endless; therefore he abides in the inexhaustible treasury of the Dharma. Hence the sound of the voice he utters flows on without end and its resonance is inexhaustible; it can make those who hear it pursue the meaning of his words endlessly and without omission, until they accomplish the inexhaustible, eternally abiding fruit. Hence it is named the inexhaustible voice.

The Three Foundations of Mindfulness

Chinese Source

三念處初門第六十

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Three Foundations of Mindfulness, Number 60

Chinese Source

一不一心聽法不以為憂去、二一心聽者不以為喜、三常行捨心行。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, not regarding it as cause for grief when [listeners] do not listen to the Dharma single-mindedly and go away; second, not regarding it as cause for joy when listeners are single-minded; third, always practicing the practice of the mind of equanimity.

Chinese Source

次八音而辯三念處者，既八音為物開演正法，聽者善惡不同，必有信毀違順之別。若無三念之德，豈能心地坦若虛空，泯無憂喜之相？故次八音而辯三念處也。此三通名念處者，慧心能緣，名之為念。平等之理不增不減，謂之為處。佛以慧心緣於平等不增不減之理，是以違順學者心無憂喜之相，故三通名念處。即是以慈修意，能現平等清淨之意業也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three foundations of mindfulness are discussed next after the eight voices. When the Dharma is expounded to beings with the eight voices, the listeners are not alike, good and evil, and there will surely be differences of believing or reviling, opposing or according. If [the Buddha] lacked the virtue of the threefold mindfulness, how could the ground of his mind be as level as empty space, with no mark of grief or joy at all? Hence the three foundations of mindfulness are discussed next after the eight voices. These three are all named "foundations of mindfulness" (念處) because the wise mind that can take [something] as its object is named mindfulness (念), and the principle of equality, neither increasing nor decreasing, is called its foundation (處). Because the Buddha, with the wise mind, takes as his object the principle of equality that neither increases nor decreases, his mind has no mark of grief or joy toward learners who oppose or accord; hence the three are all named foundations of mindfulness. This is precisely cultivating intent with loving-kindness, so that he can manifest impartial, pure mental karma.

Chinese Source

一、不一心聽法不以為憂。佛智了達不一心聽法之人，平等法界中，減退相畢竟不可得，故無憂相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, not regarding it as cause for grief when [listeners] do not listen to the Dharma single-mindedly. The Buddha's knowledge penetrates that, for people who do not listen to the Dharma single-mindedly, within the impartial dharma realm a mark of decline utterly cannot be obtained; hence there is no mark of grief.

Chinese Source

二、聽者一心不以為喜。佛智了達聽者，平等法界中，增進相畢竟不可得，故無喜相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, not regarding it as cause for joy when listeners are single-minded. The Buddha's knowledge penetrates that, for listeners, within the impartial dharma realm a mark of advance utterly cannot be obtained; hence there is no mark of joy.

Chinese Source

三、常行捨心。佛智了達一切眾生即大涅槃，不可復滅，故於一切言說利益眾生中，常行捨心也。故《金剛般若經》云：「如是滅度無量眾生，實無眾生得滅度。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, always practicing the mind of equanimity. The Buddha's knowledge penetrates that all sentient beings are themselves the great nirvāṇa and cannot be extinguished again; hence, in all his speaking and benefiting of sentient beings, he always practices the mind of equanimity. Hence the Vajra Prajñā Sūtra says: "Though immeasurable sentient beings are thus brought to extinction, in reality no sentient being attains extinction."

Reading Notes for Part 4

Translator's notes

The Treatise as main source. Gateways 47–48 and 53–55 follow the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom (T1509) closely: the Śūraṅgama samādhi as "the mark of the heroic" (健相), the jewel-seal samādhi and the dharma-seal, the three dhāraṇīs ("like a sound vessel holding water"), the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses (the Buddha's "sincere words"), and the list of eighteen unshared qualities. The thirty-two marks follow T1509's list with small differences.

Lion's stride and the last samādhi. The lion's-stride samādhi (師子遊步) is not found under that name in T1509 as searched; the one hundred and eighth samādhi, "free from attachment to empty space and undefiled," is in T1509's list. The mud-and-slave simile is translated as it stands.

The Vimalakīrti. "First draw them with the hook of desire; afterward cause them to enter the buddha-path" (Gateway 49) is from the Vimalakīrti verses (T0475), where the second line reads 後令入佛智 "the buddha-wisdom." The site's Vimalakīrti page did not yield an English line for this verse.

The Nirvāṇa Sūtra. Zhiyi says the Great Nirvāṇa Sūtra uses the eight transformations to explain the meaning of self; T0374/T0375 list "eight kinds of mastery" (八自在) under the great self. The site's Nirvāṇa page renders 八自在 "eight kinds of mastery"; here 八自在 is "the eight masteries" and 自在 "mastery," to match. The eight transformations themselves resemble T1509's list of transformations.

Loving-kindness without object (無緣慈). Follows the site's Guanyin Xuanyi "without object".

Wish-fulfilling-jewel king body / medicine-tree king body. These two names at the end of Gateway 56 follow the site's Guanyin Xuanyi.

"Hence the body is without fear" (身無畏, Gateway 55). The first unshared quality is 身無失 "the body without fault," but the entry ends 故身無畏 "the body is without fear." 畏 is probably a slip for 失; translated literally.

The eighty minor features. Zhiyi's list does not match T1509 exactly. The closing note, "On the chest there is the character 德 [virtue]; on the hands and feet there is the character 吉 [auspicious]," is in the Sūtra on the Samādhi of Seated Meditation (坐禪三昧經, T0614), item 80. Item 55, 孔門相具, is literally "the marks of the orifices are complete"; the sense is uncertain.

The eight voices. The same eight names appear in later Huayan commentaries, which cite the Brahmāyu Sūtra (梵摩喻經). Zhili's Notes on the Textual Commentary on the Golden Light (T1786) quotes Zhiyi's explanation of "the voice that is not feminine" word for word.

Not feminine (不女). The text says the Buddha "has long since left behind the timid, soft mind" (雌軟之心, literally "a female, soft mind"); the translation keeps "not feminine" as the name of the quality, as the Chinese does.

The three foundations of mindfulness. The first item in the list reads 不以為憂去, with an extra 去 "go away"; the entry heading has only 不以為憂. Both are translated as they stand. The closing quotation is from the Vajra Prajñā Sūtra (T0235): "如是滅度無量、無數、無邊眾生，實無眾生得滅度者"; Zhiyi gives it in shortened form.

Ninety-five kinds. "The wrongs of the ninety-five kinds" (Gateway 59) means the ninety-five kinds of non-Buddhist teaching; "non-Buddhist teaching" is supplied in brackets.

Measures. One zhang (丈) is about three meters; "four inches" renders 四寸.
