From the Sixteen Special Excellences to the Thirty-Seven Factors

Part 2 covers gateways 19 to 36 of The Gateway to the Sequence of the Dharma Realm. It continues the meditative sequence begun in Part 1 and then turns to the doctrinal "wisdom-practice" categories. First come the two remaining kinds of breath-based dhyāna, the sixteen special excellences (each matched to one of the four foundations of mindfulness) and the thorough-and-bright dhyāna, which Zhiyi presents only through quotations from the Great Collection Sūtra. Then follow the practices that counteract afflictions: the nine contemplations of a corpse, the eight recollections that remove the fear they cause, the ten perceptions, the eight detachments, the eight stations of mastery, the ten totality-bases, the fourteen transformations, the six supernormal powers, the nine sequential concentrations, the three samādhis, and the lion's-sport and transcendence samādhis, which train the meditator to move through the dhyānas in order, in reverse, and by leaps. The middle fascicle's second part begins the wisdom categories: the four truths, their sixteen practices, the two emptinesses of beings and dharmas, and the thirty-seven factors of the path. Throughout, Zhiyi explains why each category follows the one before it, so the order itself is part of the teaching.

The Sixteen Special Excellences: Body and Feeling

Chinese Source

十六特勝初門第十九

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Sixteen Special Excellences, Number 19

Chinese Source

一知息入、二知息出、三知息長短、四知息遍身、五除諸身行、六受喜、七受樂、八受諸行心、九心作喜、十心作攝、十一心作解脫、十二觀無常、十三觀出散、十四觀離欲、十五觀滅、十六觀棄捨。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, knowing the breath entering; second, knowing the breath leaving; third, knowing the breath long and short; fourth, knowing the breath pervading the body; fifth, removing every bodily formation; sixth, receiving joy; seventh, receiving happiness; eighth, receiving every formation of mind; ninth, the mind making joy; tenth, the mind making restraint; eleventh, the mind making liberation; twelfth, contemplating impermanence; thirteenth, contemplating going out and dispersing; fourteenth, contemplating separation from desire; fifteenth, contemplating cessation; sixteenth, contemplating relinquishment.

Chinese Source

次六妙門而辨十六特勝者，此二種禪定大意雖同，而六妙門一往竪淺橫廣，十六特勝則竪長橫局。長則位遠難窮，次後而明也。皆稱特勝者，解釋別有因緣事具出（云云）。但此禪始從調心，終至非想，地地皆有觀照能發無漏，而無厭惡自害之失，故受特勝之名。諸師多以此十六對四念處觀。若作此釋，則進退約位，但與六妙門齊。分別二種，對特勝之相，竪橫不同，略如下辨，適取意用之。

Ru-Yi Translation

The sixteen special excellences are distinguished next after the six wondrous gates. The general intent of these two kinds of dhyāna-concentration is the same, but the six wondrous gates, taken at first glance, are vertically shallow and horizontally broad, while the sixteen special excellences are vertically long and horizontally limited. Being long, their stages are far-reaching and hard to exhaust; hence they are made clear afterward. They are all called "special excellences": the explanation has its own causes and conditions, and the matter is set out in full [elsewhere] (and so on). But this dhyāna begins from regulating the mind and ends at non-perception; in ground after ground there is contemplative illumination that can bring forth the uncontaminated, and yet there is no fault of loathing and self-harm; hence it receives the name "special excellence." Many masters match these sixteen to the contemplation of the four foundations of mindfulness. If this explanation is made, then, advancing and retreating with reference to stages, it is merely on a par with the six wondrous gates. Distinguishing the two kinds and comparing the marks of the special excellences, the vertical and the horizontal differ, as roughly distinguished below; take from it what suits your use.

Chinese Source

一、知息入。修習特勝之初，正依隨息，故以知息入為門，即代初數息調心之法。所以然者，數息則闇心而數，故觀慧不明。今知息入，則照息分明，故解慧易發，是以用知息入為調心法也。若將十六特勝橫對四念處者，從知息入去有五特勝，並屬身念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, knowing the breath entering. At the beginning of cultivating the special excellences, one relies precisely on following the breath; hence knowing the breath entering is taken as the gate, replacing the initial method of regulating the mind by counting the breath. Why so? In counting the breath, one counts with a dim mind, so contemplative wisdom is not clear. Now, in knowing the breath entering, one illuminates the breath distinctly, so understanding and wisdom easily arise; this is why knowing the breath entering is used as the method of regulating the mind. If the sixteen special excellences are matched horizontally to the four foundations of mindfulness, from knowing the breath entering on there are five special excellences, all belonging to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of the body.

Chinese Source

二、知息出。修特勝者，當以此知息出代數息初調心法也，意如前釋。若對念處，猶屬身念處觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, knowing the breath leaving. Those who cultivate the special excellences should take this knowing of the breath leaving in place of the initial method of regulating the mind by counting the breath; the intent is as explained before. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of the body.

Chinese Source

三、知息長短。修特勝者藉觀以調心，心既靜細，則照了漸明。若得麁住、細住及欲界定，定中暗障薄，即便覺息入出長短之相也。若對念處，猶屬身念處觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, knowing the breath long and short. Those who cultivate the special excellences regulate the mind by means of contemplation; once the mind is still and subtle, illumination gradually becomes clear. If one attains coarse settling, fine settling, and the desire-realm concentration, the darkness and obstruction within concentration are thin, and one then becomes aware of the marks of the breath entering and leaving, long and short. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of the body.

Chinese Source

四、知息遍身。修特勝者，從欲界定與觀相扶，入未到地，故證未到地定時，即覺身及定法悉皆虛假，息之入出遍身微微，如有如無。既於定中照了分明，染著心薄也。若對念處，猶屬身念處也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, knowing the breath pervading the body. Those who cultivate the special excellences, from the desire-realm concentration supported by contemplation, enter the not-yet-arrived ground; hence when they realize the not-yet-arrived concentration, they are aware that the body and the dharma of concentration are all hollow and provisional, and that the breath, entering and leaving, pervades the body faintly, as if existing and as if not existing. Since within concentration they illuminate distinctly, the attached mind becomes thin. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, it still belongs to the foundation of mindfulness of the body.

Chinese Source

五、除諸身行。修特勝者，從未到地欲入初禪時，常應觀析，因此若發初禪覺觀之法，則身心豁然開朗，如明眼人開倉，即自了了見倉中所有，分別所證境界皆虛假，空無人我。既無人我，誰作諸事？誰受禪定？是則顛倒所起身業皆悉壞滅，故云除諸身行。若對念處，齊此猶屬身念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, removing every bodily formation. Those who cultivate the special excellences, when from the not-yet-arrived ground they wish to enter the first dhyāna, should always contemplate and analyze. Through this, if the dharmas of initial thought and sustained reflection of the first dhyāna arise, body and mind suddenly open up and brighten, as when a person with clear eyes opens a granary and at once clearly sees everything in the granary; one discerns that the realm realized is all hollow and provisional, empty and without person or self. Since there is no person or self, who does the various things? Who receives dhyāna-concentration? Thus the bodily actions arising from inversion are all destroyed; hence it is called removing every bodily formation. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, up to this point it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of the body.

Chinese Source

六、受喜。修特勝者既常與觀慧相應，若證初禪，喜支即能照了。因是喜生無過，故云受喜。若對念處，從此有三特勝，並屬受念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, receiving joy. Those who cultivate the special excellences, being always in correspondence with contemplative wisdom, if they realize the first dhyāna, can illuminate the factor of joy. Because of this, joy arises without fault; hence it is called receiving joy. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, from this point there are three special excellences, all belonging to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of feeling.

Chinese Source

七、受樂。修特勝者既常與觀慧相應，若證初禪，得樂支時即能覺了，便於樂支不起見著，以無所受而受樂觸，故云受樂。若對念處，猶屬受念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, receiving happiness. Those who cultivate the special excellences, being always in correspondence with contemplative wisdom, if they realize the first dhyāna, can be aware of the factor of happiness when they attain it, and so do not give rise to views and attachment toward the factor of happiness; with nothing received, they receive the touch of happiness; hence it is called receiving happiness. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of feeling.

Chinese Source

八、受諸心行。修特勝者既常與觀慧相扶，若證初禪一心支時，即能照了一心，不起顛倒，於一心中獲得正受，故云受諸心行。若對念處，齊此猶屬受念處觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, receiving every mental formation. Those who cultivate the special excellences, always being supported by contemplative wisdom, when they realize the factor of one-pointedness of mind of the first dhyāna, can illuminate one-pointedness of mind and do not give rise to inversion; within one-pointedness of mind they attain right reception; hence it is called receiving every mental formation. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, up to this point it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of feeling.

The Sixteen Special Excellences: Mind and Dharmas

Chinese Source

九、心作喜。修特勝者，離初禪、入二禪時，常自照了。因是若發二禪內淨喜，則真喜從觀慧而生，故名心作喜。若對念處，從此有三特勝，並屬心念處觀也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, the mind making joy. Those who cultivate the special excellences, when they leave the first dhyāna and enter the second dhyāna, constantly illuminate for themselves. Because of this, if the joy of inner purity of the second dhyāna arises, true joy is born from contemplative wisdom; hence it is named the mind making joy. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, from this point there are three special excellences, all belonging to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of mind.

Chinese Source

十、心作攝。修特勝者，既因觀慧得二禪一心支，即照了一心，因是倒想不起，故云心作攝也。若對念處，猶屬心念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, the mind making restraint. Those who cultivate the special excellences, having attained the factor of one-pointedness of mind of the second dhyāna through contemplative wisdom, illuminate one-pointedness of mind; because of this, inverted perceptions do not arise; hence it is called the mind making restraint. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of mind.

Chinese Source

十一、心作解脫。修特勝者，離二禪、入三禪，常有觀照，是故若發三禪，即能照了。雖得妙樂，心不耽著，無累自在，故云心作解脫。若對念處，齊此猶屬心念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eleventh, the mind making liberation. Those who cultivate the special excellences, leaving the second dhyāna and entering the third dhyāna, constantly have contemplative illumination; therefore, if the third dhyāna arises, they can illuminate it. Although they attain wondrous happiness, the mind does not indulge in or attach to it, free of burdens and at liberty; hence it is called the mind making liberation. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, up to this point it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of mind.

Chinese Source

十二、觀無常。修特勝者，離三禪、入四禪時，常修觀照，是故若發四禪不動定時，即自觀達定中心識虛誑，念念生滅，故云觀無常也。若對念處，從此有五特勝，皆屬法念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Twelfth, contemplating impermanence. Those who cultivate the special excellences, when leaving the third dhyāna and entering the fourth dhyāna, constantly cultivate contemplative illumination; therefore, if the unmoving concentration of the fourth dhyāna arises, they themselves contemplate and penetrate that the mind and consciousness within concentration are hollow and deceptive, arising and ceasing thought after thought; hence it is called contemplating impermanence. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, from this point there are five special excellences, all belonging to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of dharmas.

Chinese Source

十三、觀出散。修特勝者，從四禪入虛空處時，加修觀智，內外照了，是故若證空定之時，即知能離色界。緣空之識，自在逍散而虛誑不實，心不愛著，故云觀出散。若對念處，猶屬法念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thirteenth, contemplating going out and dispersing. Those who cultivate the special excellences, when from the fourth dhyāna they enter the sphere of space, additionally cultivate contemplative knowledge, illuminating inner and outer; therefore, when they realize the empty concentration, they know that they can leave the form realm. The consciousness that takes emptiness as its object disperses and roams at liberty, yet is hollow, deceptive, and unreal; the mind does not love or attach to it; hence it is called contemplating going out and dispersing. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of dharmas.

Chinese Source

十四、觀離欲。修特勝者，離虛空處定、入識處時，常以觀慧內自推撿，欲離虛空處。離欲之心，是故發識處定，即能觀達識定虛誑不實，心不愛著，故云觀離欲。若對念處，猶屬法念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourteenth, contemplating separation from desire. Those who cultivate the special excellences, when leaving the concentration of the sphere of space and entering the sphere of consciousness, constantly search and examine inwardly with contemplative wisdom, wishing to separate from the sphere of space. With the mind separated from desire — therefore, when the concentration of the sphere of consciousness arises, they can contemplate and penetrate that the concentration of consciousness is hollow, deceptive, and unreal; the mind does not love or attach to it; hence it is called contemplating separation from desire. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of dharmas.

Chinese Source

十五、觀滅。修特勝者，離識處、入無所有時，以智照了所修之境、能修之心，是故若發無所有處定，即自觀達無所有處虛誑不實，心不住著，故云觀滅。若對念處，猶屬法念處觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifteenth, contemplating cessation. Those who cultivate the special excellences, when leaving the sphere of consciousness and entering nothingness, illuminate with knowledge the object cultivated and the mind that cultivates; therefore, if the concentration of the sphere of nothingness arises, they themselves contemplate and penetrate that the sphere of nothingness is hollow, deceptive, and unreal; the mind does not abide or attach; hence it is called contemplating cessation. If matched to the foundations of mindfulness, it still belongs to the contemplation of the foundation of mindfulness of dharmas.

Chinese Source

十六、觀棄捨。修特勝者，離無所有，修非有想非無想定時，即自以智觀察所修之法、能修之心，是故若發非想定時，即觀照分明，知非想處兩捨之定猶有細想，四眾和合而有，虛誑不實，非是涅槃安樂真法，則心不愛著。特勝行者，若於地地修觀照了，則地地之中顛倒不起，心不染著，隨其因緣會處，即於是地發真無漏，證三乘道。略辨特勝竟。言少則意難見，讀者必須細心比類，令知地地之中與根本禪、四空證定一往雖同，而觀慧有別也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixteenth, contemplating relinquishment. Those who cultivate the special excellences, leaving nothingness, when cultivating the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception, themselves contemplate and examine with knowledge the dharma cultivated and the mind that cultivates; therefore, when the concentration of non-perception arises, they contemplate and illuminate distinctly, knowing that the concentration of the sphere of non-perception, in which both are relinquished, still has subtle perception, exists through the combination of the four groups, is hollow, deceptive, and unreal, and is not the true dharma of the peace and happiness of nirvāṇa; then the mind does not love or attach to it. If practitioners of the special excellences cultivate contemplative illumination in ground after ground, then in ground after ground inversions do not arise and the mind does not become attached; wherever their causes and conditions meet, there, in that ground, they bring forth the true uncontaminated and realize the path of the three vehicles. The brief distinction of the special excellences is finished. Where the words are few the meaning is hard to see; readers must, with a careful mind, compare by analogy, so as to know that in ground after ground, although at first glance these are the same as the root dhyānas and the realization of the four empty concentrations, their contemplative wisdom differs.

The Thorough-and-Bright Dhyāna

Chinese Source

通明禪初門第二十

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Thorough-and-Bright Dhyāna, Number 20

Chinese Source

初禪、二禪、三禪、四禪、虛空處、識處、少處、非有想非無想處、滅受想定。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first dhyāna, the second dhyāna, the third dhyāna, the fourth dhyāna, the sphere of space, the sphere of consciousness, the sphere of the few, the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception, and the concentration of the cessation of feeling and perception.

Chinese Source

次十六特勝而辨通明禪，此禪竪深橫細，定觀精巧，過於特勝，故次後辨之。而不次九想背捨後辨者，此禪雖是實觀深細，而未具無漏得解廣大對治之用，於破煩惱義劣，故不次背捨等安之。亦以非其氣類，今次十六之後，正應從容得所也。言通明者，辨此禪相具出《大集經》中，但經不別出名目，而北國諸禪師坐證此法者，欲以教人，必須標名傳世。若用根本禪說，雖定名一往相似，而行相逈異，還用此名說者，行人便作常解，則大乖其妙。若安十六特勝觀法，雖小相似，而名目都不相關。若對背捨、勝處等，名之與觀條然併異，既進退並不同餘禪，豈可用餘禪名說？故別為立自名，名曰通明。所言通明者，修此禪時，必須三事通觀，故云通明。亦以能發六通三明，故云通明。但此禪境界繁多，非可傳述。今止列《大集經》中所出科目，示知有此一法門，異常所傳禪也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The thorough-and-bright dhyāna is distinguished next after the sixteen special excellences. This dhyāna is vertically deep and horizontally fine, its concentration and contemplation precise and skillful, surpassing the special excellences; hence it is distinguished after them. It is not distinguished after the nine contemplations and the detachments because, although this dhyāna is real contemplation, deep and subtle, it does not yet possess the broad counteractive function of the uncontaminated [contemplation of] understanding attained, and it is inferior in the sense of breaking afflictions; hence it is not placed alongside the detachments and the rest. Also, since it is not of their kind, placing it now after the sixteen is properly fitting, in an unhurried way. As for the name "thorough-and-bright": the marks of this dhyāna are set out in full in the Great Collection Sūtra, but the sūtra gives no separate name for it; yet the dhyāna masters of the northern lands who sat and realized this dharma, wishing to teach it to others, had to label it with a name to pass on to the world. If it were explained with the names of the root dhyānas, then although the names of the concentrations seem at first glance similar, the marks of practice are entirely different; if those names were still used to explain it, practitioners would understand them in the usual way and greatly miss its wonder. If it were placed under the contemplative method of the sixteen special excellences, although somewhat similar, the names have nothing to do with each other. If compared with the detachments, the stations of mastery, and the rest, both the names and the contemplations are completely different. Since in every respect it is not the same as the other dhyānas, how could it be explained with the names of the other dhyānas? Hence a name of its own was separately established for it, called "thorough-and-bright." What is called thorough-and-bright: when cultivating this dhyāna, one must contemplate three matters thoroughly together; hence it is called thorough-and-bright. Also, because it can bring forth the six supernormal powers and the three insights, it is called thorough-and-bright. But the realms of this dhyāna are very many and cannot be transmitted in full. Here we only list the categories given in the Great Collection Sūtra, to show that this one dharma-gate exists, different from the dhyānas commonly transmitted.

Chinese Source

初禪六支。《大集經》云：「言初禪者，亦名為具，亦名為離。離者謂離五蓋，具者謂具足五支。言五支者，覺、觀、喜、樂、安、定。一、覺支者，云何名覺？如心覺大覺，思惟大思惟，觀於心性，是名覺。云何名觀？心行大行，遍行隨喜，是名為觀。云何名喜？如真實知大知小，心動其心，是名為喜。云何名為樂。行於此法。心悅受於樂觸。是名為樂。云何名為安？謂心安身安，受於樂觸，是名為安。云何名為定？謂若心住大住不亂，於緣不謬，無有顛倒，是名為住。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The first dhyāna has six factors. The Great Collection Sūtra says: "The first dhyāna is also named 'possessing' and also named 'separating.' 'Separating' means separating from the five hindrances; 'possessing' means being fully possessed of the five factors. The five factors are initial thought, sustained reflection, joy, happiness, ease, and concentration. First, the factor of initial thought: what is named initial thought? When the mind is aware (覺) with great awareness and deliberates with great deliberation, contemplating the nature of mind — this is named initial thought. What is named sustained reflection? The mind moves with great movement, moving everywhere and rejoicing accordingly — this is named sustained reflection. What is named joy? When one truly knows great knowing and small [knowing], and the mind moves its own mind — this is named joy. What is named happiness? Practicing this dharma, the mind is delighted and receives the touch of happiness — this is named happiness. What is named ease? The mind is at ease and the body is at ease, receiving the touch of happiness — this is named ease. What is named concentration? When the mind abides with great abiding and is undisturbed, is not mistaken about its object, and is without inversion — this is named abiding."

Chinese Source

二禪三支。《大集經》云：「言二禪者，亦名為離，亦名為具。離者同離五蓋，具者具足三支，謂喜、安、定也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The second dhyāna has three factors. The Great Collection Sūtra says: "The second dhyāna is also named 'separating' and also named 'possessing.' 'Separating' is likewise separating from the five hindrances; 'possessing' is being fully possessed of three factors, namely joy, ease, and concentration."

Chinese Source

三禪五支。《大集經》云：「言三禪者，亦名為離，亦名為具。離者謂離五蓋，具者具足五支，謂念、捨、慧、安、定。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The third dhyāna has five factors. The Great Collection Sūtra says: "The third dhyāna is also named 'separating' and also named 'possessing.' 'Separating' means separating from the five hindrances; 'possessing' is being fully possessed of five factors, namely mindfulness, equanimity, wisdom, ease, and concentration."

Chinese Source

四禪四支。《大集經》云：「言四禪者，亦名為離，亦名為具。離者同離五蓋，具者具足四支，謂念、捨、不苦不樂、定。」

The Thirty-Seven Factors: Faculties, Powers, Awakening, and the Path

Chinese Source

五根：一信根、二精進根、三念根、四定根、五慧根。

Ru-Yi Translation

The five faculties: first, the faculty of faith; second, the faculty of vigor; third, the faculty of mindfulness; fourth, the faculty of concentration; fifth, the faculty of wisdom.

Chinese Source

一、信根。信正道及助道法，是名信根也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the faculty of faith. Believing in the principal path and the auxiliary dharmas of the path — this is named the faculty of faith.

Chinese Source

二、精進根。行是正道及諸助道善法時，勤求不息，是名精進根。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the faculty of vigor. When practicing this principal path and the various wholesome dharmas of the auxiliary path, seeking diligently without rest — this is named the faculty of vigor.

Chinese Source

三、念根。念正道及諸助道善法，更無他念，是名念根也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the faculty of mindfulness. Being mindful of the principal path and the various wholesome dharmas of the auxiliary path, with no other thought — this is named the faculty of mindfulness.

Chinese Source

四、定根。攝心在正道及諸助道善法中，相應不散，是為定根也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the faculty of concentration. Gathering the mind within the principal path and the various wholesome dharmas of the auxiliary path, corresponding and not scattering — this is the faculty of concentration.

Chinese Source

五、慧根。為正道及諸助道善法，觀無常等十六行，是名慧根。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the faculty of wisdom. For the sake of the principal path and the various wholesome dharmas of the auxiliary path, contemplating the sixteen practices such as impermanence — this is named the faculty of wisdom.

Chinese Source

此五通名根者，能生也。行者既得四如意足，智、定安隱，即信等五種善法，若似、若真任運而生。譬如陰陽調適，一切種子悉有根生，故名根也。

Ru-Yi Translation

These five are all named "faculties" (根) because they can give rise. Once the practitioner has attained the four bases of supernormal power, wisdom and concentration are at peace, and the five wholesome dharmas such as faith, whether in semblance or in truth, arise of their own accord. It is like when yin and yang are in harmony, all seeds put forth roots and grow; hence they are named faculties.

Chinese Source

五力：一信力、二精進力、三念力、四定力、五慧力。

Ru-Yi Translation

The five powers: first, the power of faith; second, the power of vigor; third, the power of mindfulness; fourth, the power of concentration; fifth, the power of wisdom.

Chinese Source

一、信力。信正道及諸助道法時，若信根增長，能遮疑惑，破諸邪信及煩惱，故名信力。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the power of faith. When believing in the principal path and the various auxiliary dharmas, if the faculty of faith increases, it can block doubt and break wrong beliefs and afflictions; hence it is named the power of faith.

Chinese Source

二、精進力。行是正道及諸助道法時，若精進根增長，破種種身心懈怠，成辦出世之事，是為精進力。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the power of vigor. When practicing this principal path and the various auxiliary dharmas, if the faculty of vigor increases, it breaks the various kinds of laziness of body and mind and accomplishes the world-transcending task; this is the power of vigor.

Chinese Source

三、念力。念正道及諸助道法時，若念根增長，破諸邪念，成就一切出世正念功德，是為念力。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the power of mindfulness. When being mindful of the principal path and the various auxiliary dharmas, if the faculty of mindfulness increases, it breaks the various wrong thoughts and accomplishes all the merits of world-transcending right mindfulness; this is the power of mindfulness.

Chinese Source

四、定力。攝心在正道及諸助道法時，若定根增長，則能破諸亂想，發諸事理禪定，是為定力。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the power of concentration. When gathering the mind within the principal path and the various auxiliary dharmas, if the faculty of concentration increases, it can break the various scattered perceptions and bring forth the dhyāna-concentrations of phenomena and principle; this is the power of concentration.

Chinese Source

五、慧力。為正道及諸助道法，觀無常十六行時，若慧根增長，則能遮三界見思之惑，發真無漏，故名慧力。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the power of wisdom. When, for the sake of the principal path and the various auxiliary dharmas, one contemplates the sixteen practices of impermanence [and the rest], if the faculty of wisdom increases, it can block the delusions of views and habituated thought of the three realms and bring forth the true uncontaminated; hence it is named the power of wisdom.

Chinese Source

此五通名力者，能壞諸有漏不善，成辦出世善事，故名力也。

Ru-Yi Translation

These five are all named "powers" because they can destroy the various contaminated unwholesome [dharmas] and accomplish world-transcending wholesome tasks; hence they are named powers.

Chinese Source

七覺分：一擇法覺分、二精進覺分、三喜覺分、四除覺分、五捨覺分、六定覺分、七念覺分。

Ru-Yi Translation

The seven factors of awakening: first, the factor of awakening of discerning dharmas; second, the factor of awakening of vigor; third, the factor of awakening of joy; fourth, the factor of awakening of removal; fifth, the factor of awakening of equanimity; sixth, the factor of awakening of concentration; seventh, the factor of awakening of mindfulness.

Chinese Source

一、擇法覺分。智慧觀諸法時，善能簡別真偽，不謬取諸虛偽法，故名擇法覺分。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the factor of awakening of discerning dharmas. When wisdom contemplates dharmas, it can skillfully distinguish true from false and does not mistakenly take hollow and false dharmas; hence it is named the factor of awakening of discerning dharmas.

Chinese Source

二、精進覺分。精進修諸道法時，善能覺了，不謬行於無益之苦行，常勤心在真法中行，故名精進覺分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the factor of awakening of vigor. When cultivating the dharmas of the path with vigor, one can skillfully be aware and understand, and does not mistakenly practice useless ascetic practices, but always diligently keeps the mind practicing within the true Dharma; hence it is named the factor of awakening of vigor.

Chinese Source

三、喜覺分。若心得法喜，善能覺了，此喜不依顛倒之法而生，歡喜住真法喜，故名喜覺分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the factor of awakening of joy. If the mind attains dharma-joy, it can skillfully be aware and understand that this joy does not arise relying on inverted dharmas, and, rejoicing, it abides in true dharma-joy; hence it is named the factor of awakening of joy.

Chinese Source

四、除覺分。若斷除諸見煩惱之時，善能覺了，除諸虛偽、不損真正善根，故名除覺分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, the factor of awakening of removal. When severing and removing the afflictions of the various views, one can skillfully be aware and understand, removing what is hollow and false without harming true and right wholesome roots; hence it is named the factor of awakening of removal.

Chinese Source

五、捨覺分。若捨所見念著之境時，善能覺了，所捨之境虛偽不實，永不追憶，是為捨覺分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the factor of awakening of equanimity. When relinquishing objects that one sees, thinks of, and clings to, one can skillfully be aware and understand that the objects relinquished are hollow, false, and unreal, and never recalls them again; this is the factor of awakening of equanimity.

Chinese Source

六、定覺分。若發諸禪定之時，善能覺了諸禪虛假，不生見愛妄想，是為定覺分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, the factor of awakening of concentration. When the various dhyāna-concentrations arise, one can skillfully be aware and understand that the various dhyānas are hollow and provisional, and does not give rise to the deluded thoughts of views and craving; this is the factor of awakening of concentration.

Chinese Source

七、念覺分。若修出世道時，善能覺了，常使定、慧均平。若心沈沒，當念用擇法、精進、喜等三覺分察起。若心浮動，當念用除、捨、定等三分攝故。念覺常在二盈之間，調和中適，是念覺分。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, the factor of awakening of mindfulness. When cultivating the world-transcending path, one can skillfully be aware and understand, always keeping concentration and wisdom balanced. If the mind sinks, one should be mindful to use the three factors of awakening of discerning dharmas, vigor, and joy to examine and raise it. If the mind floats and moves, one should be mindful to use the three factors of removal, equanimity, and concentration to gather it in. The awakening of mindfulness always stays between the two excesses, harmonizing and keeping to the middle; this is the factor of awakening of mindfulness.

Chinese Source

此七通名覺分者，無學實覺七事能到，故通名覺分。

Ru-Yi Translation

These seven are all named "factors of awakening" because the real awakening of no more learning can be reached through these seven matters; hence they are all named factors of awakening.

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

The Great Collection Sūtra. The quotations in Gateway 20 come from the Mahāvaipulya Great Collection Sūtra (T0397), in a passage addressed to Kauṇḍinya; the formless-concentration passages (e.g., "觀身厭患," "少識處定," "見於非想非非想為寂靜") are there with small differences. The first-dhyāna passage ("亦名為具，亦名為離") was not matched word for word. Zhiyi stresses that he adds no interpretation of his own.

Five factors, six names. The first-dhyāna quotation announces "five factors" but lists six (initial thought, sustained reflection, joy, happiness, ease, concentration), while the heading says "six factors." Its last definition ends "this is named abiding" (住) rather than "concentration." Both are translated as they stand.

"The sphere of the few" (少處). The Great Collection Sūtra has 少識處 ("the sphere of little consciousness"), its name for the sphere of nothingness; compare the "some explain" view in Part 1, Gateway 17. In Gateway 32, 不用處 ("the sphere of nothing-to-use") is another old name for the same sphere.

Non-perception (非想) and neither perception nor non-perception (非有想非無想). The two Chinese names are kept apart: 非想／非非想 are "non-perception" / "non-non-perception," 非有想非無想 is "neither perception nor non-perception," and 無想 is "no-perception."

Nine contemplations, but "perceptions." As on the site's Shi Chan Boluomi (fascicle 9), the set 九想 is "the nine contemplations" while each member is "the perception of …"; Zhiyi's own gloss explains 想 as "perception." The Treatise (T1509) has "九相如縛賊，十想如斬殺"; Zhiyi's wording differs slightly.

Detachment (背捨) and liberation (解脫). Gateway 24 distinguishes the two: detachment becomes "liberation" only when the fetters are severed. "Detachment" follows the site's Shi Chan Boluomi fascicle 10 (its table of contents has "liberations"). The definition "背是淨潔五欲" is in T1509; the horse simile for the stations of mastery and the line "背捨為初門…" were not located there.

念增 (Gateway 22). "是為念增也" is read as 念僧 "recollection of the Saṅgha."

The perception of exhaustion (Gateway 23). The sentence "若涅槃清淨結使及生死，未盡結使及生死業，證涅槃也" seems garbled; the bracketed words supply the sense of the parallel entries.

Three senses, two given. Gateway 28 says "there are three different senses" but gives two (powers as retribution, powers through cultivation).

The Larger Sūtra. The lion's-sport and transcendence quotations are from the Larger Prajñāpāramitā (T0223: "菩薩依師子奮迅三昧，入超越三昧"). "If one can deeply contemplate the four foundations of mindfulness, this is sitting at the site of awakening" was not located in T0223; Zhiyi cites it also in Si Nianchu (T1918) and the Profound Meaning (T1716).

Principal path / auxiliary path (正道／助道). In this pairing 正 is "principal"; in the eightfold path and right efforts it is "right," as Zhiyi's own gloss "正 means not being wrong" requires.

Five aggregates subject to grasping (五受陰). Here 受 renders upādāna, not "feeling"; 五不受陰 is "the five aggregates not subject to grasping."

Vigor (精進) and diligence (勤). 精進 is "vigor" so that 正勤 can keep the site's "right efforts".

The thorough-and-bright dhyāna. 通明 is explained twice: one "contemplates three matters thoroughly together" (通觀), and it brings forth the six supernormal powers (通) and three insights (明). "Thorough-and-bright" follows the site's Shi Chan Boluomi (fascicle 8).
