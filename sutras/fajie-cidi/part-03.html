From the Three Liberations to the Ten Similes

Part 3 covers gateways 37 to 46 of The Gateway to the Sequence of the Dharma Realm, from the end of the Middle Fascicle into the first half of the Lower Fascicle. The first four gateways complete the "shared" path of the two vehicles: the three gates of liberation (emptiness, signlessness, non-action), the three uncontaminated faculties that arise on the paths of vision, cultivation, and no-more-learning, the eleven kinds of knowledge, and the twelve links of dependent origination, which Zhiyi presents in three ways: across three lifetimes, across two lifetimes of retribution, and within a single thought, the last two quoted from the Great Collection Sūtra. At the opening of the Lower Fascicle he announces a turn: the remaining twenty gateways concern only what bodhisattvas practice and what buddhas realize, unshared with ordinary beings or the two vehicles. They begin with the four universal vows, read against two kinds of four truths, then the six pāramitās, each made a pāramitā by "five minds," the four reliances, the nine kinds of great dhyāna, quoted in full from the Bodhisattva-bhūmi, the eighteen emptinesses, and the ten similes, which make emptiness easy to grasp. As before, each entry begins by explaining why it follows the one before.

The Three Liberations

Chinese Source

三解脫初門第三十七

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Three Liberations, Number 37

Chinese Source

一空解脫門、二無相解脫門、三無作解脫門。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the gate of liberation of emptiness; second, the gate of liberation of signlessness; third, the gate of liberation of non-action.

Chinese Source

次三十七品而辯三解脫門者，《大智度論》云：「三十七品是趣涅槃道，行是道已到涅槃。涅槃城有三門，謂空、無相、無作。」既已說道，故次應說到處門也。此三通名解脫門者，解脫即是涅槃門，謂能通此三法，能通行者得入涅槃，故名解脫門也。亦云三三昧，三昧義如前說。但三昧即是當體得名，解脫從能通之用以受稱也。此無別法，有師解云：因時名三昧，證果則變名解脫。此類如八背捨、八解脫也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three gates of liberation are discussed next after the thirty-seven factors. The Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom says: "The thirty-seven factors are the path that leads to nirvāṇa; having traveled this path, one arrives at nirvāṇa. The city of nirvāṇa has three gates, namely emptiness, signlessness, and non-action." Since the path has already been explained, the gates of the place one arrives at should be explained next. These three are all named "gates of liberation" because liberation is precisely nirvāṇa, and [these are its] gates: that is, these three dharmas can lead through, and can lead the practitioner through to enter nirvāṇa; hence they are named gates of liberation. They are also called the three samādhis; the meaning of samādhi is as explained before. But "samādhi" is a name taken from the thing itself, while "liberation" is a designation received from its function of leading through. There is no separate dharma here. Some masters explain: in the time of the cause it is named samādhi; when the fruit is realized, the name changes to liberation. This is like [the relation of] the eight detachments and the eight liberations.

Chinese Source

一、空解脫門。云何名空解脫門？觀諸法無我、我所故空。所以者何？諸法從因緣和合生，無有作者、無有受者。能如是通達者，是名空解脫門。是空解脫門緣二行，謂空、無我。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the gate of liberation of emptiness. What is named the gate of liberation of emptiness? Contemplating that all dharmas are without self and what belongs to a self, [one sees that they are] therefore empty. Why so? All dharmas arise from the coming together of causes and conditions; there is no doer and no receiver. One who can penetrate in this way — this is named the gate of liberation of emptiness. This gate of liberation of emptiness takes two practices as its object, namely emptiness and no-self.

Chinese Source

二、無相解脫門。云何名無相解脫門？觀男女相、一異相等，是相中求實皆不可得，故無相。所以者何？若諸法無我、我所故空，空故無男無女、一異等法。我、我所中名字是異，以是故男女、一異等相實不可得。能如是通達者，是為無相解脫門。是解脫緣四行，謂盡、滅、妙、出。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the gate of liberation of signlessness. What is named the gate of liberation of signlessness? Contemplating the signs of male and female, the signs of one and different, and so on, when one seeks something real within these signs, none can be obtained; hence signlessness. Why so? If all dharmas are empty because they are without self and what belongs to a self, then because they are empty there is no male and no female, no dharmas of one and different, and so on. Within self and what belongs to a self, the names are different; for this reason the signs of male and female, one and different, and so on cannot really be obtained. One who can penetrate in this way — this is the gate of liberation of signlessness. This liberation takes four practices as its object, namely exhaustion, cessation, the wondrous, and leaving.

Chinese Source

三、無作解脫門。云何名無作解脫門？若知一切法無相，即都無所作，是名無作。所以者何？若於法有所得者，即於三界而有願求，因是造作三有之業。今一切相皆不可得故，則於三界無所願求，不造一切三有生死之業，無業故無報，是為無作解脫門。是無作解脫門緣十行，謂無常、苦、集、因、緣、生、道、正、進、乘也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the gate of liberation of non-action. What is named the gate of liberation of non-action? If one knows that all dharmas are signless, then there is nothing at all to be done; this is named non-action. Why so? If one has something to obtain with regard to dharmas, then one has wishes and seekings in the three realms, and because of this one creates the karma of the three existences. Now, because no sign whatever can be obtained, one has no wishes or seekings in the three realms and does not create any karma of birth and death in the three existences; because there is no karma, there is no retribution. This is the gate of liberation of non-action. This gate of liberation of non-action takes ten practices as its object, namely impermanence, suffering, accumulation, cause, condition, birth, path, right, track, and vehicle.

The Three Uncontaminated Faculties

Chinese Source

三無漏根初門第三十八

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Three Uncontaminated Faculties, Number 38

Chinese Source

一未知欲知根、二知根、三知已根。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the faculty of coming to know what is not yet known; second, the faculty of knowing; third, the faculty of having known.

Chinese Source

次三解脫門而辯三無漏根者，解脫既是涅槃之門，若善修三解脫，必定發真無漏，證有餘涅槃。得有餘涅槃自有三道不同，謂見道、修道、無學道也。證三道時必發三根，故次三解脫而辯之。通名根者，根以住立、能生為義。得此三法，住立不退，生真智照，故名根也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three uncontaminated faculties are discussed next after the three gates of liberation. Since liberation is the gate of nirvāṇa, if one cultivates the three liberations well, one will certainly bring forth the truly uncontaminated and realize nirvāṇa with residue. In attaining nirvāṇa with residue there are, of themselves, three different paths, namely the path of vision, the path of cultivation, and the path of no-more-learning. When the three paths are realized, three faculties are certainly brought forth; hence they are discussed next after the three liberations. They are all named "faculties" because 根 (faculty) has the meaning of standing firm and being able to produce. Attaining these three dharmas, one stands firm and does not regress, and true wisdom and illumination are produced; hence they are named faculties.

Chinese Source

一、未知欲知根。無漏九根和合信行、法行人於見諦道中，名未知欲知根。所謂九根者，信根、精進根、念根、定根、慧根、喜根、樂根、捨根、意根也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the faculty of coming to know what is not yet known. The nine uncontaminated faculties together, in the faith-follower and the dharma-follower within the path of seeing the truths, are named the faculty of coming to know what is not yet known. The so-called nine faculties are the faculty of faith, the faculty of vigor, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of concentration, the faculty of wisdom, the faculty of joy, the faculty of happiness, the faculty of equanimity, and the faculty of intent.

Chinese Source

二、知根。信解見得人，思惟道中，是九根轉名知根。九根如未知欲知根中分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the faculty of knowing. In the one resolved through faith and the one attained through view, within the path of deliberation, these nine faculties change and are named the faculty of knowing. The nine faculties are as distinguished under the faculty of coming to know what is not yet known.

Chinese Source

三、知已根。若至無學道中，是九根轉，名知已根。九亦如未知欲知根中分別也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the faculty of having known. When one reaches the path of no-more-learning, these nine faculties change and are named the faculty of having known. The nine are likewise as distinguished under the faculty of coming to know what is not yet known.

The Eleven Kinds of Knowledge

Chinese Source

十一智初門第三十九

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Eleven Kinds of Knowledge, Number 39

Chinese Source

一法智、二比智、三他心智、四世智、五苦智、六集智、七滅智、八道智、九盡智、十無生智、十一如實智。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, dharma-knowledge; second, subsequent knowledge; third, knowledge of others' minds; fourth, worldly knowledge; fifth, knowledge of suffering; sixth, knowledge of accumulation; seventh, knowledge of cessation; eighth, knowledge of the path; ninth, knowledge of exhaustion; tenth, knowledge of non-arising; eleventh, knowledge as it really is.

Chinese Source

次三無漏根而辯十一智者，以三根能生十一智故。所以者何？未知欲知根生法智、比智；知根生苦智、集智、滅智、道智及他心智、世智；知已根生盡智、無生智及如實智。是以次三根而辯十一智也。通名智者，決定了知，故名為智。若發此十一智時，各齊位照了分明，故通名為智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The eleven kinds of knowledge are discussed next after the three uncontaminated faculties because the three faculties can produce the eleven kinds of knowledge. Why so? The faculty of coming to know what is not yet known produces dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge; the faculty of knowing produces the knowledge of suffering, the knowledge of accumulation, the knowledge of cessation, the knowledge of the path, and also the knowledge of others' minds and worldly knowledge; the faculty of having known produces the knowledge of exhaustion, the knowledge of non-arising, and knowledge as it really is. This is why the eleven kinds of knowledge are discussed next after the three faculties. They are all named "knowledge" because one knows decisively and clearly; hence it is named knowledge. When these eleven kinds of knowledge are brought forth, each, according to its stage, illuminates clearly and distinctly; hence they are all named knowledge.

Chinese Source

一、法智。欲界繫法中無漏智，欲界繫因中無漏智，欲界法智滅中無漏智，為斷欲界繫法道中無漏智，及法智品中無漏智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, dharma-knowledge. The uncontaminated knowledge regarding the dharmas bound to the desire realm; the uncontaminated knowledge regarding the causes bound to the desire realm; the uncontaminated knowledge regarding the cessation of the desire realm's dharma-knowledge; the uncontaminated knowledge regarding the path for severing the dharmas bound to the desire realm; and the uncontaminated knowledge within the class of dharma-knowledge.

Chinese Source

二、比智。於色界、無色界中，約四諦辯四種無漏智，亦如法智中所明，但有法、比之殊也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, subsequent knowledge. In the form realm and the formless realm, four kinds of uncontaminated knowledge are distinguished with reference to the four truths, also as made clear under dharma-knowledge; there is only the difference between "dharma" and "subsequent."

Chinese Source

三、他心智。知欲界、色界繫現在心、心數法，及無漏心、心數法少分，是為他心智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, knowledge of others' minds. Knowing the present mind and mental factors bound to the desire realm and the form realm, and a small part of the uncontaminated mind and mental factors — this is the knowledge of others' minds.

Chinese Source

四、世智。諸世間有漏智慧，亦名等智。凡夫、聖人同有此智，故名等智。亦云名字智，是智但有名而無理。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, worldly knowledge. All the contaminated knowledge and wisdom of the world; it is also named common knowledge. Ordinary beings and sages alike have this knowledge; hence it is named common knowledge. It is also called nominal knowledge: this knowledge has only names and no principle.

Chinese Source

五、苦智。五陰無常、苦、空、無我觀時，得無漏智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, knowledge of suffering. When one contemplates the five aggregates as impermanent, suffering, empty, and without self, one obtains uncontaminated knowledge.

Chinese Source

六、集智。知諸法因，因、集、生、緣觀無漏智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, knowledge of accumulation. Knowing the causes of all dharmas: the uncontaminated knowledge [arising] in contemplating cause, accumulation, birth, and condition.

Chinese Source

七、滅智。滅、止、妙、出觀時無漏智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, knowledge of cessation. The uncontaminated knowledge at the time of contemplating cessation, stilling, the wondrous, and leaving.

Chinese Source

八、道智。道、正、行、遠觀時無漏智也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, knowledge of the path. The uncontaminated knowledge at the time of contemplating path, right, practice, and far-reaching.

Chinese Source

九、盡智。我見苦已、斷集已、證滅已、修道已，如是念時，無漏智慧見明覺也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, knowledge of exhaustion. When one is mindful thus — "I have seen suffering, have severed accumulation, have realized cessation, have cultivated the path" — the uncontaminated wisdom, vision, insight, and awakening.

Chinese Source

十、無生智。我見苦已不復更見，斷集已不復更斷，盡證已不復更證，修道已不復更修，如是念時，無漏智慧見明覺也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, knowledge of non-arising. When one is mindful thus — "I have seen suffering and will not see it again; I have severed accumulation and will not sever it again; I have realized exhaustion and will not realize it again; I have cultivated the path and will not cultivate it again" — the uncontaminated wisdom, vision, insight, and awakening.

Chinese Source

十一、如實智。一切法總相、別相，如實正智，無有罣礙，是為如實智。此智獨在佛心中有，二乘之所無也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eleventh, knowledge as it really is. The right knowledge, as it really is, of the general marks and particular marks of all dharmas, without any obstruction — this is knowledge as it really is. This knowledge exists only in the mind of a buddha; the two vehicles do not have it.

The Twelve Links in Three Lifetimes

Chinese Source

十二因緣初門第四十

Ru-Yi Translation

The Gateway of the Twelve Links of Dependent Origination, Number 40

Chinese Source

一無明、二行、三識、四名色、五六入、六觸、七受、八愛、九取、十有、十一生、十二老死

Ru-Yi Translation

First, ignorance; second, formations; third, consciousness; fourth, name-and-form; fifth, the six sense-fields; sixth, contact; seventh, feeling; eighth, craving; ninth, grasping; tenth, existence; eleventh, birth; twelfth, aging-and-death.

Chinese Source

次十一智而辯十二因緣者，除如實智，其餘十智皆是二乘共得。今一往明：若聲聞人，但約一世，總觀四諦，成十智則智劣。智劣故，不能侵除習氣，功德神用亦減少。若緣覺人，通約三世，細分別觀十二因緣，若成十智，則智強。智強故，能侵除習氣功德，神用亦廣。是以大聖教門別開出中乘之道，意在此也。通稱因緣者，是十二法展轉能感果故名因，互相由藉而有謂之緣也。因緣相續，則生死往還無際。若知無明，不起取有，則三界二十五有生死皆息，是為出世之要術也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The twelve links of dependent origination are discussed next after the eleven kinds of knowledge. Apart from knowledge as it really is, the other ten kinds of knowledge are all attained in common by the two vehicles. Now, to make it clear at first glance: the śrāvaka contemplates the four truths as a whole with reference to a single lifetime only, and when he accomplishes the ten kinds of knowledge, his knowledge is inferior. Because his knowledge is inferior, he cannot encroach on and remove residual habituation, and his merit and supernormal function are also lesser. The person awakened through conditions contemplates the twelve links of dependent origination, finely distinguished, with reference to all three lifetimes together; when he accomplishes the ten kinds of knowledge, his knowledge is strong. Because his knowledge is strong, he can encroach on and remove residual habituation, and his merit and supernormal function are also broad. That the teaching-gate of the Great Sage opens out a separate path of the middle vehicle — the intention lies here. They are all called "dependent origination" (因緣, cause and condition) because these twelve dharmas, one after another, can bring about fruits, hence they are named causes; and because they rely on one another and so exist, they are called conditions. When causes and conditions continue one after another, birth and death goes and returns without limit. If one knows ignorance and does not give rise to grasping and existence, then the birth and death of the twenty-five existences in the three realms all comes to rest; this is the essential art for leaving the world.

Chinese Source

教門十二因緣有三種不同：一者約三世明十二因緣、二者約果報二世辯十二因緣、三者約一念一世辯十二因緣。今無明三世十二因緣者，初二過去世攝，後二未來世攝，中八現在世攝。是中略說三事：煩惱、業、苦。是三事展轉，更互為因緣，是煩惱業因緣、業苦因緣、苦苦因緣、苦煩惱因緣。煩惱業因緣、業苦因緣、苦苦因緣，是為展轉更互為因緣，故云三世十二因緣也。

Ru-Yi Translation

In the teaching-gates, the twelve links of dependent origination are of three different kinds: first, the twelve links made clear with reference to three lifetimes; second, the twelve links discussed with reference to two lifetimes of retribution; third, the twelve links discussed with reference to a single thought in a single lifetime. Now, as for the twelve links of the three lifetimes, beginning with ignorance: the first two are included in the past lifetime, the last two are included in the future lifetime, and the middle eight are included in the present lifetime. Within these, three matters are taught in brief: affliction, karma, and suffering. These three matters, one after another, are causes and conditions for each other: affliction is the cause and condition of karma, karma is the cause and condition of suffering, suffering is the cause and condition of suffering, and suffering is the cause and condition of affliction. Affliction being the cause and condition of karma, karma being the cause and condition of suffering, suffering being the cause and condition of suffering — these, one after another, are causes and conditions for each other; hence it is called the twelve links of dependent origination of the three lifetimes.

Chinese Source

一、無明。過去世一切煩惱通是無明，以過去未有智慧光明故，則一切煩惱得起故，是以過去煩惱悉是無明也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, ignorance. All the afflictions of the past lifetime are, in general, ignorance. Because in the past lifetime there was not yet the bright light of wisdom, all afflictions were able to arise; this is why the afflictions of the past are all ignorance.

Chinese Source

二、行。從無明生業，業即是行。以善、不善業能作世界果故，故名為行也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, formations. From ignorance karma is produced, and karma is precisely formation. Because wholesome and unwholesome karma can make the fruit of a world, it is named formation.

Chinese Source

三、識。從行生垢心初身因，如犢子識母自相識，故名識。即是父母交會初，欲託胎時之名。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, consciousness. From formations is produced the defiled mind, the cause of the first body. It is like a calf's consciousness recognizing its own mother; hence it is named consciousness. This is the name for the moment when the father and mother first come together and [the consciousness] is about to take up residence in the womb.

Chinese Source

四、名色。從識生非色四陰及所任色陰，是名名色，即是歌羅邏時之名也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, name-and-form. From consciousness are produced the four aggregates that are not form, and the aggregate of form that they rest on; this is named name-and-form. It is the name for the time of the kalala.

Chinese Source

五、六入。從名色中生眼等六情，是名六入。從五皰初開已來，即是六入名也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, the six sense-fields. From name-and-form are produced the six sense-organs, the eye and the rest; this is named the six sense-fields. From the time when the five protuberances first open onward, it is named the six sense-fields.

Chinese Source

六、觸。由入對塵，情塵識合，是名為觸。以六塵觸六根故，即有六識生，故名情塵識合也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, contact. Through the sense-fields facing the sense-objects, organ, sense-object, and consciousness come together; this is named contact. Because the six sense-objects contact the six faculties, the six consciousnesses are produced; hence it is named the coming together of organ, sense-object, and consciousness.

Chinese Source

七、受。從觸生受，故名為受。即是因六觸，觸六根，即領受六塵，為六受也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, feeling. From contact feeling is produced; hence it is named feeling. That is, because of the six contacts, contacting the six faculties, one takes in and receives the six sense-objects; these are the six feelings.

Chinese Source

八、愛。從受中心著，名之為愛。謂於所領受六塵中，心生渴愛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, craving. When, from feeling, the mind becomes attached, it is named craving. That is, toward the six sense-objects one has taken in, the mind gives rise to thirsty craving.

Chinese Source

九、取。從渴愛因緣求，是名為取。謂求取所愛之塵也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, grasping. Seeking because of the condition of thirsty craving — this is named grasping. That is, seeking to grasp the sense-objects one craves.

Chinese Source

十、有。從取則後世業因成，是名為有。因能有果，故名為有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, existence. From grasping, the karmic cause of the later life is completed; this is named existence. Because the cause can make the fruit exist, it is named existence.

Chinese Source

十一、生。從有還受後世五眾之身，是名生。所謂四生六道中受生也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eleventh, birth. From existence one again receives a body of the five groups in a later life; this is named birth. That is, receiving birth within the four modes of birth and the six paths.

Chinese Source

十二、老死。從生五眾身熟壞，是為老死。老死則生憂、悲、哭泣種種愁苦，眾惱合集。若正觀諸法實相清淨，則無明盡。無明盡故行盡，乃至眾苦和合皆盡。若能如是正觀三世十二因緣，發真無漏，成辟支佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

Twelfth, aging-and-death. From birth, the body of the five groups ripens and decays; this is aging-and-death. Aging-and-death then produces grief, sorrow, weeping, and all kinds of anxiety and suffering; many vexations gather together. If one rightly contemplates the true mark of all dharmas as pure, then ignorance is exhausted. Because ignorance is exhausted, formations are exhausted, and so on until the whole mass of suffering is exhausted. If one can rightly contemplate the twelve links of dependent origination of the three lifetimes in this way, one brings forth the truly uncontaminated and becomes a pratyekabuddha.

The Twelve Links in Two Lifetimes of Retribution

Chinese Source

次明從果報約二世觀十二因緣相，具出《大集經》。今略出經文：是十二因緣，從歌羅邏而辯無明，故云果報也。約二世明者，前十因緣屬現在，後二因緣屬未來，二世合為十二也。

The six pāramitās are discussed next after the four universal vows. On the bodhisattva path, vows and practice support each other; having made great vows, one must cultivate practice. Now the six pāramitās are the root of the bodhisattva's principal practice; this is why the Lotus Sūtra says: "For those who seek the bodhisattva path, he taught what corresponds to the six pāramitās." Now I briefly give three translations: some translate it as "the matter brought to completion," some as "reaching the other shore," and some as "crossing over without limit." Riding on these six dharmas, he can go from the near shore of the two kinds of birth and death to the other shore of the two kinds of nirvāṇa; this is called "reaching the other shore." By means of these six dharmas he can cross the vast distance of all dharmas, phenomena and principle of the two kinds, shared and distinct; hence "crossing over without limit." If one follows the separate explanations, each of the three translations has its own emphasis; if one follows the general understanding, then although the three translations differ, their meaning is the same, with no distinction.

First, what is named knowing the true mark of giving? At the time of giving one vows, by this giving, to attain the unsurpassed fruit of buddhahood, and does not seek the retribution of ordinary beings or of the three vehicles; this is making vows. Fifth, what is named being complete in expedient means? It means being able, in the single dharma of giving, to turn about and penetrate all the Buddha-dharmas, and to cultivate all practices everywhere; this is being complete in expedient means. Third, kṣānti-pāramitā. Kṣānti in the language of Qin is "patience." The inner mind can calmly endure the outer objects that insult it; hence it is named patience. Ru-Yi Translation

First, rely on the Dharma, not on the person. As for relying on the Dharma: the true mark, and all wholesome dharmas that accord with the true mark, are generally named the Dharma; it is also named the Dharma-body. If one relies on the true mark, the Dharma-body, and cultivates the merit of the myriad practices of the pāramitās, all is complete and everything is pure, and one can reach bodhi; hence it is said, "rely on the Dharma." As for not relying on the person: a person is a body of excellent marks and features, a provisional name formed by gathering the five aggregates. If one relies on a body of excellent marks and features and cultivates the merit of the myriad practices of the pāramitās, all falls into inversion, and in the end one cannot see the true and real Dharma-body; hence it is said, "not on the person." Why so? As the Nirvāṇa teaches, "Māra Pāpīyas can even make [himself] a buddha" — how much more can he not make [himself] a person of the four reliances? Therefore, even an ordinary person, if what he teaches and practices corresponds to the true mark, may be relied upon and believed; even one who displays the excellent marks and features of a buddha's body, if what he teaches and practices goes against the dharma of the true mark, should not be relied upon — how much less anyone else.

If something did not come from the coming together of causes and conditions, it would be a non-existent dharma. Thus the nature of all dharmas cannot be obtained; hence it is named the emptiness of nature.

First, like an illusion. As for "like an illusion": it is like an illusionist conjuring elephants, horses, and all kinds of things. Although they have no reality, there are illusory forms that can be seen and illusory sounds that can be heard; they face the senses without confusion, and those without wisdom, not understanding, call them real. All dharmas are also like this: all are conjured by ignorance; although empty, they can be seen and heard and are not confused with one another; the confused mind, not understanding, falsely clings to them as real. If one who cultivates the contemplation of emptiness knows that all dharmas are alike in having the mark of an illusion, then the mind obtains nothing, opens suddenly into understanding, and awakens to all dharmas as empty and quiescent; hence "like an illusion" is taught.

Chinese Source

二、如炎。如炎者，炎以日光風動塵，故曠野中如野馬，無智之人初見謂為水。男相女相及一切法相亦如是，結使煩惱光諸行塵，邪憶念風，生死曠野中轉。無智慧者謂為一相，為男為女，是名為炎。復次，若遠見炎想為水，近則無水相。無智之者亦如是，若遠聖法，不知無我，不知諸法空，於陰、入、界性空法中生人想、男想、女想。近聖法則知諸法實相，是時虛誑種種妄想盡除，以是故說如炎。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, like a mirage. As for "like a mirage": a mirage arises from sunlight and wind stirring dust, so that in the open wilderness it looks like wild horses; people without wisdom, seeing it at first, take it for water. The mark of male, the mark of female, and the marks of all dharmas are also like this: the light of the fetters and afflictions [shining on] the dust of all formations, the wind of wrong recollection, revolving in the wilderness of birth and death. Those without wisdom take them as a single mark, as male or as female; this is named a mirage. Further, when one sees a mirage from afar, one perceives it as water; up close, there is no mark of water. Those without wisdom are also like this: if they are far from the holy Dharma, they do not know no-self, do not know that all dharmas are empty, and within the dharmas of the aggregates, sense-fields, and elements, whose nature is empty, they give rise to the perception of a person, the perception of male, the perception of female. When near the holy Dharma, they know the true mark of all dharmas; at that time all kinds of hollow, deceptive, false thinking are wholly removed. For this reason "like a mirage" is taught.

Chinese Source

三、如水中月。如水中月者，月在虛空中，影現於水。實法相在如法性實際虛空中，凡夫心水中有我、我所相現，以是故如水中月。如小兒見水中月，歡喜欲取，大人見之則笑。無智人亦如是，身見故見有吾我，無實智故，見種種法，見已歡喜，欲取諸相、男相、女相等。諸得道聖人，愍之而笑也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, like the moon in water. As for "like the moon in water": the moon is in the sky, and its reflection appears in the water. The mark of real dharmas is in the sky of suchness, dharma-nature, and the real limit, while in the water of the ordinary being's mind the marks of self and what belongs to a self appear; for this reason it is like the moon in water. It is like a small child who sees the moon in the water, is delighted, and wants to grab it, while adults who see this laugh. People without wisdom are also like this: because of the view of the body they see a self; because they lack real wisdom they see all kinds of dharmas, and having seen them they are delighted and want to grasp the marks — the mark of male, the mark of female, and so on. The sages who have attained the path pity them and laugh.

Chinese Source

四、如虛空。如虛空者，虛空但為有名而無實法，虛空非可見，遠視故眼光轉見彯色。諸法亦如是，空無所有，人遠無漏實智慧故，棄實見彼我、男女、屋舍種種物。若修空觀，入實相理，則一切皆無所有，故說如虛空也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, like empty space. As for "like empty space": empty space has only a name and no real dharma. Empty space cannot be seen; because one looks from afar, the light of the eye turns and sees a blue color. All dharmas are also like this: empty, with nothing at all. Because people are far from uncontaminated, real wisdom, they abandon the real and see self and other, male and female, houses, and all kinds of things. If one cultivates the contemplation of emptiness and enters the principle of the true mark, then everything is nothing at all; hence "like empty space" is taught.

Chinese Source

五、如響。如響者，若深山溪谷中，及空大舍中，若語聲、若打聲，從聲有聲，無智者謂為有人語聲。智者了是聲無人作，但以聲觸，故名為響。響事空而能誑耳根，一切音聲言語亦如是。人欲語時，口中出風，名憂陀那，還入至臍。響出時觸七處退還，是名語言如響。無智者不識語之因緣，謂為實，心取生憂喜。智者知語因緣無實如響，聞之心不生著，諸法如是，故說如響。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, like an echo. As for "like an echo": in deep mountain ravines and valleys, or in a large empty hall, whether there is the sound of speech or the sound of striking, from a sound there comes a sound, and those without wisdom take it to be the sound of a person speaking. The wise understand that this sound is made by no person; it is only because sound strikes [the surroundings], hence it is named an echo. The matter of an echo is empty and yet can deceive the ear faculty; all sounds and words are also like this. When a person wishes to speak, wind comes out of the mouth, named udāna, and returns, entering as far as the navel. When the echo [of speech] comes out, it strikes seven places and returns; this is named speech being like an echo. Those without wisdom, not recognizing the causes and conditions of speech, take it as real, and the mind grasps it and gives rise to grief or joy. The wise know that the causes and conditions of speech are without reality, like an echo; hearing it, the mind does not give rise to attachment. All dharmas are like this; hence "like an echo" is taught.

The Ten Similes: Gandharva City to Transformation

Chinese Source

六、如揵闥婆城。如揵闥婆城者，日初出時，見城門樓櫓宮殿，行人出入。日轉高轉轉滅，但可眼見而無有實。無智人不識，謂之為實。智者見之，即知無實。諸法亦如是，無智不了，妄計有假名、吾我、陰、入、界之實。智者了達，皆無所有，故說如揵闥婆城。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, like a gandharva city. As for "like a gandharva city": when the sun first comes up, one sees a city with gates, towers, and palaces, and people going in and out. As the sun climbs higher, it gradually fades away; it can only be seen by the eye but has no reality. People without wisdom do not recognize this and take it as real. The wise, seeing it, know at once that it has no reality. All dharmas are also like this: those without wisdom, not understanding, falsely reckon that the provisional name, the self, the aggregates, the sense-fields, and the elements are real. The wise penetrate that all is nothing at all; hence "like a gandharva city" is taught.

Chinese Source

七、如夢。如夢者，夢中無實事，謂之有實，覺已而還自笑。人亦如是，是諸結使、眠中實無而著。得道覺時，乃知無實，亦復自笑，以是故說如夢。又夢以眠力故，無法而見有。人亦如是，無明眠力故，種種無而見有，所謂我、我所、男女等也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, like a dream. As for "like a dream": in a dream there are no real things, yet one takes them as real; having woken, one laughs at oneself. People are also like this: in the sleep of all the fetters and agents, things that are really nonexistent are clung to. At the time of awakening upon attaining the path, one knows that they have no reality and also laughs at oneself; for this reason "like a dream" is taught. Further, in a dream, through the power of sleep, one sees as existent what is not. People are also like this: through the power of the sleep of ignorance, they see all kinds of nonexistent things as existent, namely self, what belongs to a self, male and female, and so on.

Chinese Source

八、如影。如影者，但可見而不可捉。諸法亦如是，雖眼根等見聞覺知，實不可得。又如影映光則現，不映則無。諸結使煩惱遮正見光，則有我相、法相影。若結使煩惱滅，則我相、法相皆無。又如影，人去則去、人住則住。善惡業影亦如是，後世去時亦去，今世住時亦住，報不斷故也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, like a shadow. As for "like a shadow": it can only be seen and cannot be grasped. All dharmas are also like this: although the eye faculty and the rest see, hear, sense, and know them, they really cannot be obtained. Further, a shadow appears when light is blocked and is absent when it is not blocked. When the fetters, agents, and afflictions block the light of right view, there are the shadows of the mark of self and the mark of dharmas. If the fetters, agents, and afflictions cease, the mark of self and the mark of dharmas are both absent. Further, a shadow goes when the person goes and stays when the person stays. The shadow of wholesome and evil karma is also like this: when one goes to a later life it goes too, and while one stays in this life it stays too, because retribution is not cut off.

Chinese Source

九、如鏡中像。如鏡中像者，鏡中之像，非鏡作、非面作，非鏡面和合作，亦非無因緣作，雖無定有，而亦可見分別。諸法亦如是，非是自有、非他有、非共有，亦非無因緣有，雖有不可得，但以名字而有分別。如鏡中像，實無所有，而誑惑小兒，令生憂喜。智者雖見，即知非實，故無憂喜。諸法亦如是，誑惑凡夫，生諸煩惱。實智慧者雖復見聞，既知無實，不生結業，故說如鏡中像。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ninth, like an image in a mirror. As for "like an image in a mirror": the image in the mirror is not made by the mirror, not made by the face, not made by the mirror and face together, and also not made without causes and conditions. Although it does not definitely exist, it can still be seen and distinguished. All dharmas are also like this: they do not exist of themselves, do not exist through another, do not exist through both together, and also do not exist without causes and conditions. Although they exist, they cannot be obtained; they are distinguished only by means of names. Like an image in a mirror, which really is nothing at all, yet deceives small children and causes them to give rise to grief or joy — the wise, though they see it, know at once that it is not real, and so have no grief or joy. All dharmas are also like this: they deceive ordinary beings and give rise to all afflictions. Those with real wisdom, though they see and hear, know that these have no reality and do not give rise to fetters or karma; hence "like an image in a mirror" is taught.

Chinese Source

十、如化。如化者，若諸天仙聖得神通者，能有變化諸物，如化人無生老病死、無苦、無樂，亦異於人生，以是故空無實。一切諸法亦如是，皆無生滅，又如化主無定物，但以心生便有所作，皆無有實。人生亦如是，本無所因，但從先世心生今世身，皆無有實。以是故，說諸法如化。

Ru-Yi Translation

Tenth, like a transformation. As for "like a transformation": those among the gods, seers, and sages who have attained supernormal powers can transform all kinds of things. A transformed person, for example, has no birth, aging, sickness, or death, no suffering and no happiness, and differs also from human birth; for this reason it is empty and without reality. All dharmas are also like this: all are without arising and ceasing. Further, just as the transformer has no fixed thing, but as soon as the mind arises there is something made, and all of it is without reality — human birth is also like this: originally it has nothing as its cause; only from the mind of a former life arises the body of this life, and all of it is without reality. For this reason it is taught that all dharmas are like a transformation.

Reading Notes for Part 3

Translator's notes

The Treatise as main source. Most of Gateways 37–39 and 45–46 follow the Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom (T1509) closely: the "city of nirvāṇa has three gates" (Gateway 37), the definitions of dharma-knowledge and subsequent knowledge (Gateway 39), the eighteen emptinesses, and the ten similes (including the udāna wind that "strikes seven places," and the gold-and-copper example for nature and mark). The calf recognizing its mother (Gateway 40) is also in T1509.

A copying slip in dharma-knowledge (Gateway 39). Our text reads 欲界法智滅中無漏智; T1509 has 欲界繫法滅中無漏智 ("regarding the cessation of the dharmas bound to the desire realm"), which fits the series. The translation follows our text literally.

The Great Collection Sūtra. The two-lifetime and single-thought versions of the twelve links (Gateway 40) are from the Mahāvaipulya Great Collection Sūtra (T0397), as Zhiyi says. In the single-thought series he notes that the sūtra skips "craving" and supplies a definition of his own, which is marked as such in the text.

The Necklace Sūtra. The four lines "Those not yet delivered from the truth of suffering, I cause to be delivered …" (Gateway 41) are in the Bodhisattva Necklace Original Karma Sūtra (T1485), not T0656.

Two kinds of birth and death. 分段生死 "segmented birth and death" and 變易生死 "the birth and death of change" follow the site's Mohe Zhiguan. 四住地 / 無明住地 / 五住 are "the four abiding-grounds / the abiding-ground of ignorance / the five abidings," in line with the round's change from "abodes" to "abidings."

Four truths with and without fabrication. 有作／無作四聖諦 are rendered "with fabrication / without fabrication," following the site's Mohe Zhiguan ("origin without fabrication"). In Gateways 37 and 30 無作 as a samādhi or liberation remains "non-action."

The Lotus quotations. "For those who seek the bodhisattva path, he taught what corresponds to the six pāramitās" is from the Lotus Sūtra (T0262, chapter 1; there "為諸菩薩說應六波羅蜜"); the matching English on the site's Lotus page was not located. "Up to the mark of the eternal, quiescent cessation that is final nirvāṇa, returning in the end to emptiness" (Gateway 45) reuses the site's Lotus wording, adjusted to Zhiyi's "乃至."

The Vimalakīrti. "There is no illness left at all, except the illness of emptiness" (Gateway 45) follows the site's Vimalakīrti page.

The Nirvāṇa Sūtra and the four reliances. The four reliances are in the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374/T0375); the site renders them "rely on the Dharma, not on the person … rely on the definitive teaching, not on the provisional teaching." Zhiyi's headings say 了義經 "sūtras of definitive meaning," so "sūtras" is kept. "Māra Pāpīyas can even make [himself] a buddha" was not matched word for word in T0374/T0375.

The Bodhisattva-bhūmi. Gateway 44 quotes the nine kinds of dhyāna from the Bodhisattva-bhūmi Sūtra (菩薩地持經, T1581), which Zhiyi attributes to Maitreya. The headings in our text differ slightly from T1581 (e.g., 除煩惱禪 in the list but 除惱禪 in the entry; 此世他世禪 / 此世他世樂禪). Item 8 reads 略說九種？ with a question mark, probably a slip for 云何…略說九種; it is translated as a statement. "Dhyāna is the greatest, like a king" is in T1509.

The Treatise on śīla. "Śīla in the language of Qin is 'fond of the good'" and "being fond of walking the path of the good" follow the site's Mohe Zhiguan, which quotes the same Treatise passage.

Rādha (Gateway 45). Our text has 羅那; T1509 names the monk Rādha (羅陀) in this context. The quoted words "Break up, disperse, and extinguish this form" were not located word for word.

借事四理 (Gateway 46). "Borrows things to [illustrate] principle": 四 here makes no sense and is probably a copying error (for 況 or 喻).

The perception of a mirage. "Like a mirage" (如炎) follows T1509, where 炎 means a heat-shimmer; the "wild horses" (野馬) are the shimmering air.
