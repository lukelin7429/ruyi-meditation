# Abhidharmasamuccaya Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of the *Abhidharmasamuccaya* (大乘阿毘達磨集論, Taishō T1605), composed by Asaṅga and translated into Chinese by Xuánzàng. They exist so that vocabulary stays consistent across all fourteen-plus parts, and matches the site's larger Yogācāra corpus — especially the *Cheng Weishi Lun* (which shares the same fifty-one-factor system of mind) and the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* (which shares the same author and translator).

## Source and independence from prior translations

- Root text: Taishō T1605, 大乘阿毘達磨集論本, Asaṅga, translated by Xuánzàng, 7 fascicles.
- Commentary guide: Taishō T1606, 大乘阿毘達磨雜集論, composed by Sthiramati (安慧) working from Jinaputra's (覺師子) exposition, also translated by Xuánzàng, 16 fascicles. Unlike the *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*'s commentaries (which quote the root text and then comment on it as a separate work), T1606 restates T1605's own content in question-and-answer form and interleaves glosses directly — closer to an expanded edition of the root text than an independent commentary. We draw reading notes from its glosses on a passage-by-passage basis, matched by content rather than by fascicle number (T1606's sixteen-fascicle division does not line up with T1605's seven).
- The title is given as *Abhidharmasamuccaya*, "Compendium of Abhidharma" — some scholarship uses the fuller *Mahāyānābhidharmasamuccaya*; we use the shorter form as the page title, noting the fuller Sanskrit on first use in the index page.
- This page translates directly from Xuánzàng's Chinese. Existing English scholarship may be consulted privately to check difficult passages, but the English wording, sentence structure, and section framing on this site must be Ru-Yi's own.

## Structure

The treatise divides into two major sections named in its own opening verse (T1605 line 32): 本事 ("the foundations," 本事分) and 決擇 ("the determinations," 決擇分).

- **本事分** (four topics): 三法品 the aggregates/elements/sense-fields, 攝品 how they subsume each other, 相應品 association, 成就品 possession.
- **決擇分** (four topics): 諦品 the four noble truths (by far the longest single topic — it covers roughly 39% of the whole treatise), 法品 the twelvefold scriptural genres and the three trainings' approach to Dharma, 得品 the types of persons and forms of direct realization, 論議品 the rules of formal debate.

Because content is very unevenly distributed across the eight topics (諦品 alone is longer than the entire 本事分), pages are split at natural content boundaries rather than by fascicle or by topic-heading — see the index page for the running table of contents, which is added to incrementally as parts are translated.

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 大乘阿毘達磨集論 | *Abhidharmasamuccaya* | "Compendium of Abhidharma." Full Sanskrit on first appearance per page; "the treatise" thereafter. |
| 本事分 / 決擇分 | the Foundations / the Determinations | The treatise's own two-part division. |
| 三法品 | the chapter on the three categories | 三法 = 蘊/界/處, the aggregates, elements, and sense-fields — matches Abhidharmakośa's "aggregate(s) / element(s) / sense field(s)" for 蘊/界/處. |
| 蘊 | aggregate(s) | skandha — matches Abhidharmakośa usage. |
| 界 | element(s) | dhātu, the eighteen elements — matches Abhidharmakośa usage. |
| 處 | sense field(s) | āyatana, the twelve sense fields — matches Abhidharmakośa usage. |
| 五種我事 | the five bases for a sense of self | the treatise's own explanation for why there are exactly five aggregates: the body-as-possession basis, the basis of enjoyment, the basis of speech, the basis of acting rightly or wrongly, and the basis that everything else depends on. |
| 阿賴耶識 | the ālayavijñāna | Matches Cheng Weishi Lun and Mahāyānasaṃgraha usage. Also called 阿陀那識, the ādāna-consciousness, in this treatise. |
| 五十一心所法 | the fifty-one mental factors | the same system used throughout the Cheng Weishi Lun; individual factor names below are matched to that page's established English wherever the same factor appears. |
| 遍行 / 別境 / 善 / 煩惱 / 隨煩惱 / 不定 | universal / particular / wholesome / root affliction / secondary affliction / indeterminate | the six categories of mental factors; matches Cheng Weishi Lun's "the six categories of mental factors." |
