The Five Faculties and Five Powers

The thirty-seven factors of awakening continue with the five faculties — faith, diligence, recollection, concentration, and wisdom, cultivated in strict sequence against the four noble truths — and the five powers, the very same five qualities considered again once they can no longer be overturned by their opposing obstacles.

1. The Five Faculties

Chinese Source

五根所緣者，謂四聖諦。自體者，謂信、精進、念、定、慧。助伴者，謂彼相應心心所等。修習者，謂信根於諸諦起忍可行修習。精進根於諸諦生忍可已，為覺悟故起精進行修習。念根於諸諦發精進已，繫念起不忘失行修習。定根於諸諦既繫念已，起心一境性行修習。慧根於諸諦心既得定起簡擇行修習。修果者，謂能速發諦現觀，及能修治煖、頂，引發忍、世第一法。

Ru-Yi Translation

The five faculties take as their object: the four noble truths. Their essence is faith, diligence, recollection, concentration, and wisdom. Their accompaniment is whatever mind and mental factors are associated with these.

Their cultivation: the faculty of faith is cultivated by giving rise, with respect to the truths, to an attitude of acceptance. Once acceptance of the truths has arisen, the faculty of diligence is cultivated by giving rise, for the sake of awakening, to diligence itself. Once diligence has been aroused with respect to the truths, the faculty of recollection is cultivated by fixing recollection on them so that it is never lost. Once recollection has been fixed on the truths, the faculty of concentration is cultivated by giving rise to one-pointedness of mind. Once the mind has attained concentration with respect to the truths, the faculty of wisdom is cultivated by giving rise to discernment.

Their fruit is the ability to swiftly bring forth direct realization of the truths, and the ability to cultivate and perfect heat and summit, giving rise in turn to forbearance and the supreme mundane dharma.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 marks this cultivation sequence as strictly ordered, and the order itself carries the whole point: one must first simply accept the four truths as true (faith) before diligence has anything to attach itself to; diligence, once aroused, needs recollection to keep the truths continuously in view rather than losing them the moment attention wanders; recollection, once steady, becomes the very basis on which one-pointed concentration can be built; and only once the mind is genuinely concentrated can wisdom's own discerning activity actually operate with any precision. Each faculty, in other words, is the specific precondition the next one requires — faith enables diligence, diligence enables recollection, recollection enables concentration, and concentration enables wisdom — five capacities growing directly out of one another in a single unbroken chain, aimed squarely at the four stages (heat, summit, forbearance, the supreme mundane dharma) that make up the path of preparation immediately before the path of seeing itself.

2. The Five Powers

Chinese Source

如五根，五力亦爾。差別者，由此能損減所對治障不可屈伏，故名為力。

Ru-Yi Translation

The five powers are exactly the same as the five faculties. The difference is this: because they are able to diminish their respective obstacles to the point that those obstacles can no longer overturn them, they are specifically called powers.

Reading note — after T1606

The treatise itself is characteristically terse here, and T1606 explains exactly why so little needs to be added: the five powers share the very same object, the very same essence, the very same accompaniment, and the very same cultivation as the five faculties already just described — faith, diligence, recollection, concentration, and wisdom are not a second, different set of five qualities but the identical five, simply named again from a different angle. The distinction lies entirely in their fruit. As faculties, these five qualities are still capable of being overpowered by their opposing obstacles — faith by doubt, diligence by laziness, recollection by forgetfulness, concentration by distraction, wisdom by delusion — even while they are actively growing stronger. Once cultivation has advanced far enough that each quality can diminish its corresponding obstacle without itself being overturned in return, that same quality is no longer merely called a faculty but a power. The two terms thus mark two different points along a single continuous line of growth, not two different lists of qualities to memorize side by side.
