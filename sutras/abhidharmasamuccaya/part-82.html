Five Marks of Skill in the Dharma, and 法品 Complete

法品 reaches its close: five marks by which a bodhisattva genuinely skilled in concentration on the Dharma may be recognized, rooted in calm-abiding's and insight's own praised qualities and culminating in the dharma-body itself, and the treatise's own final word on why the bodhisattva basket alone, among the twelve genres, generates merit without measure when honored.

1. Five Marks of a Bodhisattva Skilled in Concentration on the Dharma

Chinese Source

復次方廣分中於法三摩地善巧菩薩相云何可知？謂由五種因故，一剎那剎那消除一切麁重所依、二出離種種想得樂法樂、三了知無量無分別相、四順清淨分無分別相恒現在前、五能攝受轉上轉勝圓滿成就佛法身因。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, within the vast teachings, how is the mark of a bodhisattva skilled in concentration on the Dharma to be recognized? By five causes: first, moment by moment, the basis of every coarse heaviness is dissolved; second, having gone beyond every manner of conceptual notion, one attains the happiness of the Dharma's own delight; third, one comes to know the immeasurable, non-discriminating characteristic; fourth, the non-discriminating characteristic that accords with the purified portion remains continuously present; and fifth, one is able to draw in the very cause that ever more fully and excellently perfects and accomplishes the buddha's own dharma-body.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains that these five marks are not five independent qualities but a structured set, resting on the two familiar praised qualities of calm-abiding and insight together with the fruit their combination produces. The first two marks belong to calm-abiding's own praised quality: the first, since calm-abiding's continuous, uninterrupted pliancy — moment after moment growing more refined — is precisely what steadily dissolves the very basis of coarse heaviness itself; the second, since calm-abiding's resolute, penetrating vision, seeing every teaching as ultimately of a single undifferentiated flavor, is what allows one to set aside the many distinct conceptual notions of aggregates, elements, and the rest, and thereby taste the Dharma's own genuine delight. The next two marks belong to insight's own praised quality: the third, since insight's uninterrupted, unforgetting discernment illuminates the immeasurable, non-discriminating teachings through the power of recollection; the fourth, since the very images that transformation of basis leaves behind remain continuously present, without discrimination and without any further exertion required to sustain them. And the fifth mark belongs to the fruit both qualities together produce: the dharma-body itself, attained through the permanent severing of the cognitive obstruction and the transformation of basis this severing brings about — called complete at the tenth ground, and fully accomplished only at the ground of buddhahood itself. These five causes, T1606 adds, correspond to five distinct kinds of cultivation and their five distinct fruits: cultivation that stills characteristics, that unites them, that removes signs altogether, that requires no further exertion, and that transforms them at their very root.

2. Why the Bodhisattva Basket Generates Boundless Merit

Chinese Source

聲聞藏法菩薩藏法等，從如來法身所流，何因緣故以香鬘等供養恭敬菩薩藏法便生廣大無邊福聚，非聲聞藏法？以菩薩藏法是一切眾生利益安樂所依處故、能建大義故、無上無量大功德聚所生處故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The teachings of the śrāvaka basket and the teachings of the bodhisattva basket alike flow forth from the Tathāgata's own dharma-body — so why is it that making offerings of incense, garlands, and the like to the bodhisattva basket, and showing it reverence, generates a vast and boundless accumulation of merit, while the śrāvaka basket does not generate merit in the very same measure? It is because the bodhisattva basket is the very basis of benefit and happiness for every sentient being without exception, because it is able to establish the great purpose of complete awakening, and because it is the very source from which the unsurpassed, immeasurable accumulation of great qualities is itself generated.

Reading note — after T1606

This closing question deliberately returns to a point already raised in Part 78: since both baskets ultimately flow from the same single source — the Tathāgata's own dharma-body — why should reverence shown to one generate incomparably vaster merit than reverence shown to the other? The treatise's answer rests entirely on scope, not on origin. The śrāvaka basket leads a practitioner to their own personal liberation alone; the bodhisattva basket, by contrast, is oriented from the very outset toward the benefit and happiness of every sentient being without exception, aims at nothing less than the great purpose of complete and unsurpassed awakening, and is itself the very wellspring from which the buddha's own immeasurable qualities are generated. Reverence offered to a teaching is, in effect, reverence offered to everything that teaching makes possible — and it is because the bodhisattva basket makes possible an entire path oriented toward every being's welfare, rather than one being's own, that honoring it generates merit correspondingly without measure.
