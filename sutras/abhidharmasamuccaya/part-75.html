The Four Investigations, and the Four Resultant True Knowledges

Having established the principles by which the Dharma is to be reasoned about, the treatise now turns to a specific method of investigation applied to name and thing themselves: four kinds of inquiry into whether names, things, and the natures and distinctions imputed to them can actually be found, and the four true knowledges that arise once each investigation reaches its own negative conclusion.

1. The Four Investigations

Chinese Source

於諸法中正勤觀察，云何於法而起尋思？謂起四種尋思：一名尋思、二事尋思、三自體假立尋思、四差別假立尋思。云何名尋思？謂推求諸法名身、句身、文身自相皆不成實。云何事尋思？謂推求諸法蘊界處相皆不成實。云何自體假立尋思？謂於諸法能詮所詮相應中推求自體，唯是假立名言因性。云何差別假立尋思？謂於諸法能詮所詮相應中推求差別，唯是假立名言因性。

Ru-Yi Translation

While diligently investigating the dharmas, how does investigation into the Dharma itself proceed? Four kinds of investigation are undertaken: investigation into name, investigation into the thing, investigation into imputed own-nature, and investigation into imputed distinction.

What is investigation into name? It is examining whether the collections of names, phrases, and syllables that make up the dharmas are, in their own specific character, actually established as real — and finding that they are not. What is investigation into the thing? It is examining whether the characteristics of the aggregates, elements, and sense fields that make up the dharmas are actually established as real — and finding that they are not. What is investigation into imputed own-nature? It is examining, within the correlation between what expresses and what is expressed with respect to the dharmas, whether their own-nature is anything more than a merely imputed basis for verbal designation. What is investigation into imputed distinction? It is examining, within that very same correlation between what expresses and what is expressed, whether their distinctions are anything more than a merely imputed basis for verbal designation.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains why these four investigations must proceed in exactly this sequence, each building on the conclusion of the one before it. Investigation into name asks first whether the mere collection of names, phrases, and syllables used to designate a dharma has, in itself, any real existence corresponding to its own specific character — since these are all merely provisional, the answer is no. Investigation into the thing asks the same question of the actual referent those names point to — the aggregates, elements, and sense fields themselves — with the same negative result. Having established that neither side of the naming relation, taken alone, is ultimately real, the third and fourth investigations turn to the relation between them: what expresses (the name) and what is expressed (the thing) are mutually dependent for their own intelligibility — a competent speaker who hears only the name is able, through recollection, to understand the referent, and one who grasps only the referent is able, through the same recollection, to supply the name. Investigation into imputed own-nature asks whether, within this very correlation, the dharma's own intrinsic nature amounts to anything beyond what this merely conventional pairing has provisionally established; investigation into imputed distinction asks the same question of every distinguishing feature attributed to it — permanent or impermanent, superior or inferior, having form or formless, visible or invisible, and so on. In every case, the investigation discovers the same thing: what looked like a solid, findable referent turns out, on close examination, to be nothing more than a provisional label resting on a provisional correlation.

2. The Four Resultant True Knowledges

Chinese Source

於法正勤修尋思已，云何於法起如實智？謂起四種如實智：一名尋思所引如實智、二事尋思所引如實智、三自體假立尋思所引如實智、四差別假立尋思所引如實智。云何名尋思所引如實智？謂如實知名不可得智。云何事尋思所引如實智？謂如實知事相亦不可得智。云何自體假立尋思所引如實智？謂如實知實有自性不可得智。云何差別假立尋思所引如實智？謂如實知實有差別不可得智。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once diligent investigation into the Dharma has been carried out, how does true knowledge with respect to the Dharma arise? Four kinds of true knowledge arise: true knowledge induced by investigation into name, true knowledge induced by investigation into the thing, true knowledge induced by investigation into imputed own-nature, and true knowledge induced by investigation into imputed distinction.

What is true knowledge induced by investigation into name? It is the knowledge that truly knows that name is unfindable. What is true knowledge induced by investigation into the thing? It is the knowledge that truly knows that the characteristic of the thing, likewise, is unfindable. What is true knowledge induced by investigation into imputed own-nature? It is the knowledge that truly knows that a real, substantial own-nature is unfindable. What is true knowledge induced by investigation into imputed distinction? It is the knowledge that truly knows that a real, substantial distinction is unfindable.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 notes simply that each of these four true knowledges is nothing more than the direct fruit of the corresponding investigation just completed — investigation is the diligent search itself, still open to the possibility of finding something; true knowledge is the settled, confirmed result of that search, now knowing with certainty that nothing was there to be found. This fourfold movement — investigate name, thing, imputed nature, and imputed distinction in turn, and arrive at the same unfindability in each case — gives the treatise's own compact method for approaching the deeper teaching, developed at length elsewhere in the Yogācāra corpus, that named things possess no substantial reality of their own apart from the provisional, mutually dependent correlation between language and what language is used to designate.
