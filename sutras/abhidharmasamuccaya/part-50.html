The Truth of Cessation Begins

The treatise's third noble truth opens with its own twelvefold analysis — far more compact than the truth of origination, yet no less precise. This part covers the first six angles: cessation's defining mark, its genuine philosophical profundity, and the graded scale running from a merely mundane suppression of affliction's seed up through an arhat's full attainment, distinguished further by whether it comes adorned with further exceptional qualities.

1. Cessation's Own Mark

Chinese Source

云何滅諦？謂相故、甚深故、世俗故、勝義故、不圓滿故、圓滿故、無莊嚴故、有莊嚴故、有餘故、無餘故、最勝故、差別故，分別滅諦。何等相故？謂真如聖道煩惱不生，若滅依、若能滅、若滅性，是滅諦相。如世尊說：眼耳及與鼻舌身及與意，於此處名色究竟滅無餘。又說：是故汝今當觀是處，所謂此處眼究竟滅遠離色想，乃至意究竟滅遠離法想。由此道理顯示所緣真如境上有漏法滅，是滅諦相。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the truth of cessation? Twelve angles distinguish it: mark, profundity, conventional and ultimate senses, incompleteness and completeness, being unadorned and being adorned, having and lacking remainder, supremacy, and distinct varieties.

What is its mark? Suchness itself, together with the noble path's own non-arising of affliction — the basis of cessation, what actually accomplishes cessation, and cessation's own nature — this is the mark of the truth of cessation. As the World-Honored One said: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mental faculty, together with name-and-form, are here utterly, completely extinguished without remainder. And it is further said: therefore you should now contemplate this place — namely, the place where the eye is utterly extinguished, free of the very notion of form, all the way through to the place where the mental faculty is utterly extinguished, free of the very notion of dharmas. By this reasoning it is shown that the extinction of every contaminated dharma, with respect to suchness as its own object, is the mark of the truth of cessation.

2. Cessation's Genuine Profundity

Chinese Source

何等甚深故？謂彼諸行究竟寂滅，如是寂滅望彼諸行，不可說異、不可說不異、不可說亦異亦不異、不可說非異非不異。所以者何？無戲論故。於此義中若生戲論，非正思議、非道、非如，亦非善巧方便思故。如世尊說：此六觸處盡、離欲、滅寂、靜、沒等。若謂有異、若謂無異、若謂亦有異亦無異、若謂非有異非無異者，於無戲論便生戲論，乃至有六處可有諸戲論。六處既滅絕諸戲論，即是涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is its profundity? Formations' own ultimate stilling — this stilling, with respect to those same formations, cannot be said to be different from them, cannot be said to be not different, cannot be said to be both different and not different, and cannot be said to be neither different nor not different. Why? Because it is free of conceptual proliferation. Within this matter, giving rise to conceptual proliferation is neither correct reflection, nor the path, nor suchness, nor a skillful application of thought.

As the World-Honored One said: these six fields of contact are exhausted, freed of desire, ceased, stilled, and utterly vanished. Should one claim they are different, or not different, or both, or neither, one thereby generates conceptual proliferation regarding what is genuinely free of it — for it is only while the six sense-bases remain that conceptual proliferation of any kind can occur at all. Once the six sense-bases have themselves ceased, every conceptual proliferation is cut off entirely — and this is precisely nirvāṇa.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains why the treatise refuses all four positions of this tetralemma at once, rather than simply picking whichever seems most reasonable: were cessation genuinely different from formations, the two would have to be entirely unrelated, separate substances — but were it genuinely not different, cessation would have to share formations' own defiling character, which contradicts everything already established about it. Since neither "different" nor "not different" can be sustained, "both" and "neither" collapse along with them, since each simply combines or negates the same two untenable options. The deeper point, T1606 makes clear, is that this refusal is not evasiveness but precision: cessation genuinely transcends the entire framework within which "same" and "different" have meaning in the first place, which is exactly why any attempt to pin it down in those terms — however sophisticated the attempt — only ever generates further conceptual proliferation rather than genuine understanding.

3. Conventional Versus Ultimate Cessation

Chinese Source

何等世俗故？謂以世間道摧伏種子所得滅，是故世尊別名說為彼分涅槃。何等勝義故？謂以聖慧永拔種子所得滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is its conventional sense? The cessation obtained by a mundane path merely suppressing affliction's own seed — the World-Honored One specifically gives this a distinct name, "partial nirvāṇa." What is its ultimate sense? The cessation obtained by noble wisdom actually, permanently uprooting that same seed.

Reading note — after T1606

This distinction matters practically as well as doctrinally: a mundane meditative path — the kind of concentration available even to non-Buddhist practitioners — can genuinely suppress an affliction's active manifestation for a long time, producing something that outwardly resembles liberation, which is exactly why the Buddha felt the need to give it its own careful name, "partial nirvāṇa," rather than simply calling it nirvāṇa outright. Only noble wisdom — the direct, penetrating insight unique to the Buddhist path — genuinely uproots the affliction's seed itself, rather than merely holding its active expression in check; the treatise's terminology is designed precisely to prevent conflating these two very different achievements.

4. Incomplete Versus Complete

Chinese Source

何等不圓滿故？謂諸有學或預流果攝、或一來果攝、或不還果攝等所有滅。何等圓滿故？謂諸無學阿羅漢果攝等所有滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is incomplete cessation? The cessation possessed by trainees — whether at the level of stream-entry, once-return, or non-return. What is complete cessation? The cessation possessed by one beyond further training, at the level of arhatship.

5. Unadorned Versus Adorned

Chinese Source

何等無莊嚴故？謂慧解脫阿羅漢所有滅。何等有莊嚴故？謂俱分解脫三明六通阿羅漢等所有滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is unadorned cessation? The cessation possessed by an arhat liberated through wisdom alone. What is adorned cessation? The cessation possessed by an arhat with both-part liberation, endowed with the three knowledges and six supernormal powers.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains this distinction plainly: an arhat liberated through wisdom alone has genuinely, completely severed every affliction, achieving the exact same fundamental liberation as any other arhat — but without also having cultivated the eight meditative liberations and the accompanying supernormal accomplishments, so their cessation, while fully genuine, is not further "adorned" by those additional exceptional qualities. An arhat with both-part liberation has severed affliction through the very same wisdom and additionally possesses the three knowledges (recollection of past lives, knowledge of others' future rebirths, and knowledge that contaminants are exhausted) and six supernormal powers — the same underlying liberation, but accompanied by a wealth of further attainments the treatise likens to ornamentation.

6. With Remainder, Without Remainder, and Supreme

Chinese Source

何等有餘故？謂有餘依滅。何等無餘故？謂無餘依滅。何等最勝故？謂佛菩薩無住涅槃攝所有滅，以常安住一切有情利樂事故。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is cessation with remainder? The nirvāṇa element with remaining basis. What is cessation without remainder? The nirvāṇa element without remaining basis. What is supreme cessation? The cessation possessed by buddhas and bodhisattvas, belonging to non-abiding nirvāṇa — since it constantly abides in benefiting and bringing happiness to every sentient being.

Reading note — after T1606

Cessation with remainder describes an arhat who has severed every affliction yet still possesses a living body and mind, the remaining "basis" from past karma not yet exhausted; cessation without remainder describes that same liberation once the physical body itself has finally passed away, with nothing further remaining at all. Supreme cessation — non-abiding nirvāṇa — is this treatise's own consistent Mahāyāna signature, already familiar from this site's Mahāyānasaṃgraha and Cheng Weishi Lun pages: a buddha's or advanced bodhisattva's liberation that abides neither in ordinary saṃsāra (since every affliction has genuinely been severed) nor in the two lesser vehicles' own final cessation (since compassion keeps such a being continuously active on behalf of others) — the treatise's own graded scale, running from a merely mundane suppression up through this final, unrestricted supremacy, closes exactly where the whole section began, with cessation's own ultimate, most complete form.

Part 50 Complete

Six of the truth of cessation's twelve angles are now examined: mark, profundity, conventional versus ultimate sense, incomplete versus complete, unadorned versus adorned, and the scale from remainder through supremacy. Part 51 closes the truth of cessation entirely with its longest single component: some two dozen further names for nirvāṇa, each with its own precise etymological justification, followed by the four aspects that together summarize what cessation ultimately is.
