Sharpening the Faculties, and 得品 Complete

得品 reaches its close with the fifth and final skill a bodhisattva must cultivate — sharpening one's own faculties — and a careful resolution of an apparent puzzle: bodhisattvas are already, by lineage, of sharp faculties, so why does the treatise still call for the further cultivation of sharpening at all?

Sharpening the Faculties

Chinese Source

若諸菩薩性是利根，云何復令修練根？謂令依利軟根引發利中根，復依利中根引發利利根故。

Ru-Yi Translation

If bodhisattvas are, by their very lineage, already of sharp faculties, why does the treatise nonetheless call for cultivating the sharpening of the faculties? It is so that, relying on the mild grade within that sharp lineage, the middling grade may be brought forth, and relying in turn on the middling grade, the sharpest grade of all may be brought forth.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 addresses this apparent tension directly. It has already been established, throughout this treatise's account of the classification of persons, that bodhisattvas as a class possess sharp faculties by lineage, in clear contrast to the dull-facultied śrāvaka and the middling-facultied pratyekabuddha already examined in Part 84. Yet the treatise still calls for sharpening the faculties from time to time, precisely because even this single sharp lineage itself contains its own internal gradation — a mild, a middling, and a sharpest grade, each drawing forth the next in succession. Sharpening the faculties, in this specific sense, does not mean converting a dull faculty into a sharp one, since a bodhisattva's faculty was never dull to begin with; it means advancing within the sharp lineage's own internal sequence, from its mildest expression to its most fully realized one.

Were this not the case, T1606 notes, every person of sharp lineage would necessarily possess faculties of a single, undifferentiated grade, and no distinction in faculty sharpness among bodhisattvas themselves would ever be observable. But such a distinction plainly is observable — some bodhisattvas manifestly display sharper faculties than others, even while all alike belong to the same sharp lineage. It is precisely this observable fact that confirms sharp faculties themselves admit of further internal differentiation, and it is cultivating this internal progression — mild sharp faculties advancing to middling sharp faculties, and middling sharp faculties advancing to the sharpest faculties of all — that constitutes this fifth and final skill.

Part 122 Complete — 得品 in Full

With sharpening the faculties now examined, the treatise's entire account of the five kinds of bodhisattva skill — in the object of knowledge, in skillful means, in false discrimination, in non-discrimination, and in sharpening the faculties — is complete. And with it, 得品, the chapter on attainment, spanning from the sevenfold classification of persons through the tenfold classification of direct realization and its full catalogue of qualities, comes to its own conclusion. Part 123 turns to 論議品, the treatise's fourth and final chapter, on the sevenfold classification of formal debate — beginning with determination through the six meanings of self-nature, cause, fruit, karma, association, and function.
