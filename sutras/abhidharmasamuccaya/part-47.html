What Makes an Act of Giving Complete

Among the classical purified acts of karma, the treatise singles out generosity for close analysis: its fourfold basic structure, then two further, more demanding sets of criteria — seven marks that make the act of giving itself genuinely complete, and five conditions the gift itself, as an object, must meet to count as a genuinely pure offering at all.

1. Generosity's Fourfold Structure

Chinese Source

復有施等諸清淨業。云何施業？謂因緣故、等起故、處所故、自體故，分別施業。因緣者，謂無貪無瞋無癡善根。等起者，謂彼俱行思。處所者，謂所施物。自體者，謂正行施時身語意業。

Ru-Yi Translation

There are, further, purified acts of karma such as generosity. What is the karma of giving? It is distinguished through four components: its causal condition, its co-arising factor, its location, and its own essence. The causal condition is the wholesome roots of non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion. The co-arising factor is the volition that accompanies them. The location is the object actually given. And the essence is the bodily, verbal, and mental karma present at the very moment giving is actually carried out.

2. Seven Marks of a Complete Act of Giving

Chinese Source

云何施圓滿？謂數數施故、無偏黨施故、隨其所欲圓滿施故，施得圓滿。又無所依施故、廣清淨施故、極歡喜施故、數數施故、田器施故、善分布新舊施故，施得圓滿。

Ru-Yi Translation

What makes an act of giving complete? Giving repeatedly, giving without partiality, and giving as fully as one is genuinely able — through these, giving is brought to completion.

Further, giving without any self-interested dependence, giving with broad and thorough purity, giving with the utmost joy, giving repeatedly, giving to a genuinely worthy recipient, and skillfully distributing both new and long-accumulated wealth in one's giving — through these as well, giving is brought to completion.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 unpacks each mark with a concrete illustration drawn from familiar scriptural language. "Repeatedly" describes one who has so habituated generosity that giving becomes their settled character, a great, constant benefactor. "Without partiality" describes giving equally to any renunciant or seeker, without favoring one over another based on personal preference. "As fully as one is genuinely able" describes giving in accordance with one's actual wish — whatever food, drink, or other resource the recipient genuinely needs, given as fully as circumstances allow. "Without self-interested dependence" means giving without any covert expectation of reward, whether worldly benefit or a favorable future rebirth attached to the gift. "With broad and thorough purity" describes an open hand — giving generously, without any inner reluctance or clutching, and grounded in wisdom rather than mere sentiment. "To a genuinely worthy recipient" specifically names giving to those in genuine need or of genuine spiritual merit. And "skillfully distributing new and long-accumulated wealth" means giving evenhandedly from whatever resources are actually on hand when a request arises, not reserving one's finest or most recently acquired possessions while giving away only what is old or unwanted.

3. Five Conditions for a Genuinely Pure Gift

Chinese Source

云何應知施物圓滿？謂所施財物非誑詐得故、所施財物非侵他得故、所施財物非穢離垢故、所施財物清淨故、所施財物如法所引故，如是應知施物圓滿。

Ru-Yi Translation

How should the gift's own completeness be known? The property given must not have been obtained through deceit; must not have been obtained by encroaching on another; must be free of impurity and stain; must be genuinely clean; and must have been acquired through lawful means. This is how the gift's own completeness should be recognized.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains why the gift's own provenance matters just as much as the giver's own state of mind: property obtained through deceit means wealth acquired by fraud, such as fabricating a claim on goods that are actually held in trust for someone else; property obtained by encroaching on another means wealth seized through another's own effort and hardship, taken by force or imposition rather than earned; unclean, stained property means gifts contaminated by association with harm, such as items connected to intoxicants, poison, or weapons; and property not lawfully acquired means wealth obtained through outright dishonest commerce — rigged scales, false measures, and the like. The underlying principle T1606 draws out is a genuinely important one: an act of giving cannot be made pure and meritorious purely through the giver's own good intentions if the object being given is itself tainted at its source — genuine generosity requires both a pure motive and a pure gift.
