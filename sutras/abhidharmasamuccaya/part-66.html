The Three Faculties Across the Path

The treatise returns to the faculties introduced at the very start of this treatise's discussion of the twenty-two faculties, now sorting them across the entire path: the not-yet-known-will-know faculty spanning the path of preparation and the sixteen instants of seeing, the already-known faculty spanning everything a trainee still cultivates afterward, and the fully-known faculty belonging to the arhat alone.

1. The Three Faculties

Chinese Source

三根者，謂未知當知根、已知根、具知根。云何未知當知根？謂於加行道及於見道十五心剎那中所有諸根。云何已知根？謂從第十六見道心剎那已上，於一切有學道中所有諸根。云何具知根？謂於無學道所有諸根。

Ru-Yi Translation

The three faculties are: the not-yet-known-will-know faculty, the already-known faculty, and the fully-known faculty.

What is the not-yet-known-will-know faculty? It is whatever faculties are present throughout the path of preparation and throughout the fifteen mind-instants of the path of seeing. What is the already-known faculty? It is whatever faculties are present, from the sixteenth mind-instant of the path of seeing onward, throughout the entire path of one still in training. What is the fully-known faculty? It is whatever faculties are present on the path beyond training.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 identifies exactly which faculties make up each of these three, and the underlying set turns out to be a single group of ten, simply relabeled at three different points along the path. The not-yet-known-will-know faculty consists of the mental faculty together with the five faculties of faith, diligence, recollection, concentration, and wisdom already examined earlier — plus, depending on the specific meditative level involved, one of the four faculties of pleasure, joy, distress, or equanimity. Distress specifically belongs here because, T1606 explains, a practitioner still on the path of preparation, having already left the mundane path behind but not yet directly realized the supramundane liberation being sought, can still feel a kind of anxious yearning for what has not yet been attained — a real feeling, but one confined entirely to this earliest stretch of the path. These same ten faculties, once the sixteenth mind-instant of the path of seeing has been reached and direct realization has actually occurred, continue on as the already-known faculty throughout the entire remaining path of the trainee, all the way up to the vajra-like concentration that marks its very end — called "already-known" because, from that first moment of realization onward, no object still needing to be known through this path remains yet unknown. And the fully-known faculty is simply the same set again, minus distress alone — since an arhat, having nothing left to be trained in, no longer feels this particular anxious yearning — now belonging entirely to the arhat and to those who share that same accomplishment.

2. Cultivating the Lower Realms' Wholesome Roots from Above

Chinese Source

依初靜慮地現修道時，亦修欲界繫所有善根，於彼得自在故。如依初靜慮地修欲界善根，如是依一切上地現修道時，皆能修習下界下地所有善根，於彼得自在故。

Ru-Yi Translation

When one is actually cultivating the path while resting on the level of the first dhyāna, one also thereby cultivates every wholesome root bound to the desire realm, since one has already attained mastery over it. Just as it is when resting on the first dhyāna that the wholesome roots of the desire realm are cultivated, so too, when actually cultivating the path while resting on any higher level whatsoever, one is able to cultivate every wholesome root belonging to every lower realm and lower level, since one has already attained mastery over them as well.

Reading note — after T1606

This brief note answers a natural question about how cultivation actually spreads once real mastery has been reached: does progress made on a higher meditative level leave the wholesome roots of the levels already passed through simply behind, untouched from that point on? The treatise's answer is no. Because a practitioner resting on a higher level has already attained genuine mastery over every level beneath it, actual cultivation undertaken from that higher vantage automatically carries the lower levels' own wholesome roots forward as well — cultivating the first dhyāna, for instance, simultaneously strengthens the desire realm's own wholesome roots, and this same pattern repeats at every subsequent level all the way up. Mastery already achieved, on this account, is never left idle behind as cultivation advances; it continues doing active work in support of everything still being built on top of it.
