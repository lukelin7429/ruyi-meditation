The Supramundane Path

The mundane path's counterpart is the supramundane path: the very same eight meditative levels, now infused with the wisdom that directly knows the four truths. The treatise pauses on a genuine puzzle at the very top of the mundane scale — why the highest formless sphere alone can never actually house the noble path at all — before resolving a further puzzle about when the attainment of cessation can and cannot actually occur.

1. Why the Highest Formless Sphere Has No Noble Path

Chinese Source

云何出世間道？謂於修道中法智類智品所攝苦智、集智、滅智、道智，及彼相應三摩地等，或未至定所攝、或初靜慮乃至無所有處所攝，非想非非想處，唯是世間不明了想恒現行故，由此道理故名無相。如世尊言：乃至有想三摩鉢底，方能如實照了通達。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the supramundane path? Within the path of cultivation, the knowledge of suffering, of origination, of cessation, and of the path, belonging to the dharma-knowledge and inferential-knowledge families, together with their associated concentration and the rest — occurring either within the not-yet-attained level, or within the first dhyāna up through the sphere of nothing whatsoever. The sphere of neither perception nor non-perception, by contrast, is exclusively mundane, since an unclear, indistinct perception constantly operates there — and it is for this very reason that it is called "signless." As the World-Honored One said: it is only up through an attainment that still retains perception that genuine, accurate illumination and penetration remain possible at all.

Reading note — after T1606

This is a genuinely striking structural point: of the eight classical meditative levels, only the very highest — the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception — is permanently barred from ever housing the noble path itself, even though it stands, in the ordinary cosmological ranking, above every other formless sphere. T1606 explains why: the noble path requires sufficiently clear, sharp perception to actually take the four truths as its object and directly penetrate them, but by the time meditative refinement has advanced all the way to this final sphere, perception itself has become so attenuated, so nearly indistinguishable from its own absence, that it can no longer sustain the kind of vivid, incisive discernment the path's own direct realization demands. Being the most refined attainment available does not, in other words, make it the most useful for actually breaking through to liberation — a sobering point against simply equating "more subtle" with "more advanced on the path to awakening."

2. When the Attainment of Cessation Can and Cannot Occur

Chinese Source

滅定亦是出世間攝，由聖道後所證得故。要於人趣方能引發，或於人趣或於色界能現在前，生無色界多不現起，由住寂靜解脫異熟者於此滅定多不發起，勤方便故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The attainment of cessation is likewise included among the supramundane, since it is attained specifically after the noble path itself. It can only ever be first cultivated while in the human realm; once cultivated, it can then actually manifest either in the human realm or in the form realm — but for one already born into the formless realm, it largely fails to manifest at all, since one who already abides in the ripened result of that realm's own still liberation is largely disinclined to exert the further effort this attainment would require.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 works out this restriction as a four-cornered analysis worth stating explicitly. Someone already free of form-realm desire, resting merely at the not-yet-attained level, can be free of that desire without being able to enter this attainment at all. A noble one who has already mastered the fourth dhyāna, without specifically seeking rebirth in the formless realm, can — through disenchantment and continued cultivation of that same fourth dhyāna, proceeding by way of the path of further advancement — gradually enter this still, liberated attainment. That same practitioner, actively seeking freedom from desire and proceeding instead by way of the path that actually severs the corresponding fetters, can likewise gradually enter it. And every remaining combination falls outside all three of these specific cases. The underlying reason a being already reborn into the formless realm rarely manifests this attainment, T1606 adds, is not incapacity but simple lack of motivation: already resting in that realm's own profoundly still ripened result, such a being feels little pull to exert the further deliberate effort the attainment of cessation would require — it is a genuine option, on this account, simply one seldom actually taken up once the formless realm's own attainment has already been reached.
