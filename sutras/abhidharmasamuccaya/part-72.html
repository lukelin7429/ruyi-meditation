What Apprehends the Dharma, and the Four Kinds of Objects

Having catalogued the Dharma itself, the treatise turns to what actually engages it: the mind and mental factors born of hearing, reflection, and cultivation, analyzed through the fourfold framework of object, aspect, basis, and association — and the first of four kinds of object such contemplation may take, the all-pervasive object, mapped in full onto the sixteen aspects of the four truths and the three gates of liberation.

1. What Apprehends the Dharma: Object, Aspect, Basis, and Association

Chinese Source

如是三藏所攝諸法為誰所行？是聞所成、思所成、修所成心心所法所行。如契經說：諸心心所法有所緣、有行相、有所依及相應。彼於此法為何所緣？謂契經等。作何行相？謂蘊等相應義。為何所依？謂他表了憶念習氣。何等相應？謂互為助伴，於所緣行相平等解了。

Ru-Yi Translation

The dharmas comprehended within these three baskets — for whom, then, are they actually an object of activity? They are the object of activity of the mind and mental factors born of hearing, born of reflection, and born of cultivation. As a sūtra says: every mind and mental factor has an object, an aspect, a basis, and an association.

What is the object these have with respect to this Dharma? It is discourse and the rest of the twelve genres. What aspect do they take? The meaning associated with the aggregates and the rest. What is their basis? Another's own act of communication, together with recollection and habitual imprint. What is their association? That they act as mutual companions to one another, understanding the object and its aspect in a shared and equal way.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 traces this basis through a natural sequence of three stages, each one taking over as the previous one recedes. When the teaching is actually being delivered, it is another person's own act of communicating — hearing a teacher's living voice — that directly serves as the basis for the mind and mental factors engaging with it. Once that moment of hearing has passed, recollection becomes the basis instead, as what was heard is repeatedly called back to mind. And later still, once recollection itself is no longer being actively exercised, the habitual imprint left behind by that repeated recollection becomes the basis on which the teaching's content continues to appear to the mind, even without any deliberate act of remembering — the imprint alone now strong enough to make the teaching present again as if it were still being recalled.

2. The Four Kinds of Objects

Chinese Source

云何於法所緣差別？若略說有四種，謂遍滿所緣、淨行所緣、善巧所緣、淨惑所緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

What are the distinctions among objects taken with respect to the Dharma? In brief, there are four kinds: the all-pervasive object, the object that purifies conduct, the object of skillful means, and the object that purifies affliction.

3. The All-Pervasive Object, Fourfold

Chinese Source

遍滿所緣復有四種，謂有分別影像所緣、無分別影像所緣、事邊際所緣、所作成就所緣。有分別影像所緣者，謂由勝解作意所有奢摩他、毘鉢舍那所緣境界。無分別影像所緣者，謂由真實作意所有奢摩他、毘鉢舍那所緣境界。事邊際所緣者，謂一切法盡所有性、如所有性。盡所有性者，謂蘊界處。如所有性者，謂四聖諦十六行相真如，一切行無常、一切行苦、一切法無我、涅槃寂靜、空無願無相。所作成就所緣者，謂轉依，如是轉依不可思議。

Ru-Yi Translation

The all-pervasive object is, further, of four kinds: the object of the image accompanied by discrimination, the object of the image free of discrimination, the object at the limit of actual things, and the object of accomplished activity.

The object of the image accompanied by discrimination is the object of calm-abiding and insight that belongs to mental application through resolute conviction. The object of the image free of discrimination is the object of calm-abiding and insight that belongs to mental application through genuine reality.

The object at the limit of actual things is the full extent of every dharma's own nature, together with its true nature. Its full extent is the aggregates, elements, and sense fields. Its true nature is the suchness of the sixteen aspects of the four noble truths — that all conditioned phenomena are impermanent, that all conditioned phenomena are suffering, that all dharmas are without self, that nirvāṇa is still, and emptiness, wishlessness, and signlessness.

The object of accomplished activity is transformation of basis — and this transformation of basis is itself inconceivable.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 distinguishes the first two of these four by which kind of mental application — resolute conviction or genuine reality — actually generates them. The object accompanied by discrimination belongs to a wholly mundane mode of mental application, one still working through resolute conviction rather than direct seeing; the object free of discrimination belongs instead to a wholly supramundane mode, together with whatever subsequently arises from it, actually engaging reality as it is rather than through conviction alone. The object at the limit of actual things is called this specifically because it names the two outer bounds of everything knowable at once: its sheer extent, comprehended through the aggregates, elements, and sense fields that establish just how much there is to know in the first place, and its true nature, comprehended through the four truths' sixteen aspects, the summary formulas of impermanence and the rest, and the three gates of liberation, each a different lens onto that same single reality. And the object of accomplished activity is transformation of basis itself — inconceivable, T1606 notes, precisely because it lies beyond the reach of any object still accompanied by discrimination to fully capture.

4. The Sixteen Aspects Mapped onto the Three Gates of Liberation

Chinese Source

十六行相中空攝幾行相？謂二。無願攝幾行相？謂六。無相攝幾行相？謂八。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the sixteen aspects, how many are comprehended within emptiness? Two. How many are comprehended within wishlessness? Six. How many are comprehended within signlessness? Eight.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 spells out exactly which of the sixteen fall under each of the three gates of liberation. Emptiness comprehends two: the aspects of emptiness and of no-self, since both directly negate any substantial self to be found. Wishlessness comprehends six: impermanence, suffering, cause, origination, arising, and condition — the four aspects belonging to the truths of suffering and origination together with the two additional aspects of cause and condition that also fall under origination — since a practitioner who has truly seen these six aspects has nothing left in the three realms still worth wishing for. And signlessness comprehends the remaining eight: the four aspects of cessation and the four aspects of the path, since a mind that has realized cessation and the path is no longer able to grasp at any of the ordinary characteristic-marks that discrimination usually traffics in. Taken together, these three gates simply reorganize the very same sixteen aspects already established across the four truths, now sorted by which specific kind of grasping — a self, a wish, or a sign — each aspect is capable of releasing.
