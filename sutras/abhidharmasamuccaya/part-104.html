The Four Unhindered Understandings

Four distinct analytical masteries that together let a teacher explain the Dharma without ever faltering: unhindered understanding of the differentiated names of every dharma, of the characteristics and intended meanings behind those names, of regional dialects and the etymological reasoning behind technical terms, and of every distinction that can be drawn among the dharmas themselves.

The Four Unhindered Understandings

Chinese Source

無礙解者，謂四無礙解。云何法無礙解？謂依止靜慮，於一切法名差別無礙具足中若定若慧，餘如前說。云何義無礙解？謂依止靜慮，於諸相及意趣無礙具足中若定若慧，餘如前說。云何訓詞無礙解？謂依止靜慮，於諸方言音及訓釋諸法言詞無礙具足中若定若慧，餘如前說。云何辯才無礙解？謂依止靜慮，於諸法差別無礙具足中若定若慧，餘如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

The unhindered understandings are the four unhindered understandings. What is unhindered understanding of the teaching? It is, resting on the foundation of dhyāna, the concentration and wisdom fully present within an unhindered mastery of the differentiated names belonging to every dharma, the remainder exactly as already stated.

What is unhindered understanding of its meaning? It is, resting on that same foundation, the concentration and wisdom fully present within an unhindered mastery of characteristics and of intended meanings, the remainder exactly as already stated.

What is unhindered understanding of expression? It is, resting on that same foundation, the concentration and wisdom fully present within an unhindered mastery of the various regional dialects and of the etymological explanations underlying the terms used for the dharmas, the remainder exactly as already stated.

What is unhindered understanding of eloquence? It is, resting on that same foundation, the concentration and wisdom fully present within an unhindered mastery of every distinction among the dharmas, the remainder exactly as already stated.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 supplies a concrete illustration for each. Unhindered understanding of the teaching means mastering, without hindrance, the many distinct names by which a single underlying reality can be designated — as when ignorance itself is variously named the absence of wisdom, the absence of vision, and the failure of direct realization, each a differently nuanced label for the same underlying condition. Unhindered understanding of its meaning means mastering both the specific and shared characteristics belonging to the dharmas so named, together with whatever further, more particular meaning a given passage actually intends. Unhindered understanding of expression means mastering the countless distinct forms of speech that arise across the many different regions and communities, together with the actual etymological reasoning behind technical terms — as when "the world" is so called because it is subject to destruction, or "form" is so called because it is subject to change and disintegration. And unhindered understanding of eloquence means mastering every distinction that can be drawn among the dharmas themselves — as between what is really existent and what exists only nominally, or between what exists only conventionally and what exists in the ultimate sense.

Taken together, these four cover everything a teacher needs to explain the Dharma without ever being caught short: knowing what things are called, knowing what those names actually mean, knowing how to render that meaning correctly across different languages and audiences, and knowing how to draw the further distinctions a genuinely penetrating explanation requires.
