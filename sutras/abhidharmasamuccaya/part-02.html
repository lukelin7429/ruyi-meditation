Building the Aggregate of Form

Having given form its one-line defining mark in Part 1 — altering under physical contact, or manifesting at a location — the treatise now builds the aggregate of form from the ground up: the four great elements that everything physical reduces to, the five physical sense faculties, and the objects of all five physical senses, ending with five further kinds of "form" that belong to the dharma sense field rather than to ordinary sense perception at all.

1. The Four Great Elements and the Five Physical Faculties

Chinese Source

云何建立色蘊？謂諸所有色，若四大種及四大種所造。云何四大種？謂地界、水界、火界、風界。何等地界？謂堅鞕性。何等水界？謂流濕性。何等火界？謂溫熱性。何等風界？謂輕等動性。

Ru-Yi Translation

How is the aggregate of form established? As whatever form there is, whether the four great elements themselves or what the four great elements construct.

What are the four great elements? The earth element, the water element, the fire element, the wind element. What is the earth element? Solidity. What is the water element? Fluid moisture. What is the fire element? Warmth and heat. What is the wind element? Lightness and mobility.

云何所造色？謂眼根、耳根、鼻根、舌根、身根，色聲香味所觸一分，及法處所攝色。何等眼根？謂四大種所造眼識所依清淨色。何等耳根？謂四大種所造耳識所依清淨色。何等鼻根？謂四大種所造鼻識所依清淨色。何等舌根？謂四大種所造舌識所依清淨色。何等身根？謂四大種所造身識所依清淨色。

What is constructed form? The eye faculty, the ear faculty, the nose faculty, the tongue faculty, and the body faculty; form, sound, smell, and taste, one portion of what is touched, and form included in the dharma sense field.

What is the eye faculty? The pure, translucent form constructed by the four great elements, which eye-consciousness depends on. What is the ear faculty? The pure, translucent form constructed by the four great elements, which ear-consciousness depends on. What is the nose faculty? The pure, translucent form constructed by the four great elements, which nose-consciousness depends on. What is the tongue faculty? The pure, translucent form constructed by the four great elements, which tongue-consciousness depends on. What is the body faculty? The pure, translucent form constructed by the four great elements, which body-consciousness depends on.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains "constructed" (所造) through five kinds of causal relationship the great elements bear to everything built from them: the great elements are the generative cause (nothing constructed can arise apart from them), the supporting cause (constructed form has no capacity to stand on its own, occupying its own distinct location), the establishing cause (constructed form varies precisely as the great elements it depends on vary), the sustaining cause (constructed form continues in an unbroken, similar succession only because the great elements sustain it), and the nurturing cause (the great elements are what allow constructed form to grow). One classical point worth flagging for later: the "pure, translucent form" that each physical faculty is defined as is not the visible eyeball, ear, or the like, but a subtler, imperceptible form seated within it — what later Abhidharma literature calls the faculty's own "net-form," distinct from the flesh-form anyone can see.

2. Visible Form: Twenty-Five Kinds

Chinese Source

何等為色？謂四大種所造眼根所行義，謂青、黃、赤、白、長、短、方、圓、麁、細、高、下、正、不正、光、影、明、闇、雲、煙、塵、霧、逈色、表色、空一顯色。此復三種，謂妙、不妙、俱相違色。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is visible form? The object constructed by the four great elements that the eye faculty operates on: blue, yellow, red, white; long, short, square, round; coarse, fine, high, low; regular, irregular; light, shadow; brightness, darkness; cloud, smoke, dust, mist; distant space-form, manifest form, and the single hue of open space. These, further, are of three kinds: agreeable, disagreeable, and neither.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 sorts these twenty-five into six causal groupings — a characteristic mark shared by four items, a way of designating boundaries shared by ten, an effect of benefit or harm shared by eight, and three single-item categories each established on its own distinct ground (as a basis for something else, as a mark in its own right, and as an ornament). It also glosses two of the more obscure terms: "distant space-form" (逈色) is form perceived at a distance, free of any obstruction by a nearer object; "the single hue of open space" (空一顯色) is simply the color one sees when looking at open sky above — blue, in ordinary daylight, but named generically to cover whatever hue empty space itself presents.

3. Sound, Smell, and Taste

Chinese Source

何等為聲？謂四大種所造耳根所取義，或可意、或不可意、或俱相違，或執受大種為因、或不執受大種為因、或俱大種為因，或世所極成、或成所引、或遍計所起，或聖言所攝、或非聖言所攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is sound? The object constructed by the four great elements that the ear faculty takes hold of: agreeable, disagreeable, or neither; caused by great elements that are part of a living body, by great elements that are not, or by both together; conventionally established by common worldly use, brought about by realization, or arising from mere conceptual construction; and either included among noble speech, or not.

何等為香？謂四大種所造鼻根所取義，謂好香、惡香、平等香、俱生香、和合香、變異香。何等為味？謂四大種所造舌根所取義，謂苦、酢、甘、辛、醎、淡，或可意、或不可意、或俱相違，或俱生，或和合，或變異。

What is smell? The object constructed by the four great elements that the nose faculty takes hold of: good smell, bad smell, neutral smell; smell that is innate, smell produced by mixture, and smell produced by transformation.

What is taste? The object constructed by the four great elements that the tongue faculty takes hold of: bitter, sour, sweet, pungent, salty, bland; agreeable, disagreeable, or neither; innate, produced by mixture, or produced by transformation.

Reading note — after T1606

For sound, T1606 glosses "caused by great elements that are part of a living body" as speech and the like, and "not part of a living body" as the sound of a tree or a river; "conventionally established" is ordinary colloquial speech, "brought about by realization" is what a noble one says, and "arising from mere conceptual construction" is what outsiders to the teaching say. For smell and taste, "innate" means a smell or taste already present in a substance by nature (sandalwood is T1606's example for innate smell), "produced by mixture" means a blend of substances such as incense, and "produced by transformation" means the smell or taste a substance takes on once it has ripened or spoiled, as in ripened fruit.

4. Twenty-One Kinds of the Touched

Chinese Source

何等所觸一分？謂四大種所造身根所取義，謂滑性、澁性、輕性、重性、軟性、緩、急、冷、飢、渴、飽、力、劣、悶、癢、黏、病、老、死、疲、息、勇。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is one portion of the touched? The object constructed by the four great elements that the body faculty takes hold of: smoothness, roughness; lightness, heaviness; softness; slackness, tension; cold; hunger, thirst, satiety; strength, weakness; faintness, itching; stickiness; sickness, old age, death; fatigue, rest; vigor.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 calls this "one portion" of the touched because the touch sense field as a whole also includes the four great elements themselves (solidity, moisture, warmth, and mobility, already given their own definitions above) — this list of twenty-one is specifically the touch-objects constructed from those four elements, not the elements' own bare, defining qualities. It sorts the twenty-one by eight causal grounds and offers brief glosses for the less obvious pairs: stickiness arises when the water and wind elements are imbalanced within a mixture, itching when water and earth are imbalanced; rest, strength, and vigor arise when all four elements are in balance, with "vigor" specifically meaning a fearless steadiness of body; and satiety, unlike the other imbalance-caused sensations, can arise from either an excess or a deficiency of the elements.

5. Form Included in the Dharma Sense Field

The aggregate of form closes with five further kinds of "form" that no physical sense faculty perceives at all — form known only through the mental-faculty element, included in the dharma sense field alongside every non-physical dharma.

Chinese Source

何等法處所攝色？有五種應知，謂極略色、極逈色、受所引色、遍計所起、色定自在所生色。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is form included in the dharma sense field? Five kinds should be known: form reduced to its finest analysis; form abstracted at a distance; form drawn forth by the precept a monastic undertakes; form arising from conceptual construction; and form produced by mastery in concentration.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 glosses each of the five directly. "Form reduced to its finest analysis" (極略色) is what remains when ordinary visible form is analyzed down to the level of the atom — a purely conceptual limit-point, not something anyone could ever actually perceive. "Form abstracted at a distance" (極逈色) is that same finest-analysis form considered specifically once it is free of any obstructing nearer object, as when a distant mountain range is analyzed the same way. "Form drawn forth by the precept a monastic undertakes" (受所引色) is what later Abhidharma calls unmanifest form (無表色) — the subtle, ongoing moral potency generated by taking a monastic vow, which persists even while the monastic is asleep or otherwise not actively doing anything virtuous. "Form arising from conceptual construction" (遍計所起) is the mere image present in an unreliable, non-veridical act of imagination — a mental picture with no corresponding external object at all. And "form produced by mastery in concentration" (色定自在所生色) is the form that an accomplished meditator, through mastery of concentration, can freely bring into being or transform at will — a capacity discussed further when the treatise later takes up the higher trainings.

Part 2 Complete

The aggregate of form is now fully built: the four great elements, the five physical faculties, twenty-five kinds of visible form, eleven kinds of sound, six kinds of smell, six kinds of taste, twenty-one further kinds of the touched, and five further kinds of form known to mental consciousness alone.
