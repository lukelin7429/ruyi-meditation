Non-Restraint, and When Karma Actually Ripens

Non-restraint closes the treatise's threefold division of restraint-related karma — not a single unwholesome act, but a standing, deliberate commitment to a livelihood built on ongoing harm. The treatise then turns to timing: which realm's karma is conducive to which feeling, and the three distinct windows — this very life, the next life immediately following, or some life further still — in which any given act can actually come to fruition.

1. Non-Restraint: A Livelihood Built on Harm

Chinese Source

云何不律儀業？謂諸不律儀者，或由生彼種姓中故、或由受持彼事業故，所期現行彼業決定。何等名為不律儀者？所謂屠羊、養鷄養猪、捕鳥捕魚、獵鹿罝兔、劫賊、魁膾、控牛縛象、立壇呪龍、守獄、讒搆、好為損等。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is non-restraint karma? One committed to non-restraint, either through being born into a family already devoted to it, or through personally taking up such an occupation, is thereby settled on actually carrying out its expected activity. Who is such a person? One who slaughters sheep, raises chickens or pigs for slaughter, hunts birds, catches fish, hunts deer or snares rabbits, robs or executes, drives cattle or shackles elephants, practices snake-charming as a livelihood, works as a prison guard, makes a living through slander and treachery, or otherwise delights in causing harm.

Reading note — after T1606

What defines non-restraint karma, T1606 explains, is not simply performing an occasional harmful act — that alone would make almost anyone at some point guilty of "non-restraint" — but a settled, ongoing commitment, whether inherited by birth into a certain lineage or deliberately adopted as one's own occupation, such that harmful action is no longer occasional but the expected, decided-upon shape of one's entire livelihood. This is why non-restraint is treated as a genuinely distinct karmic category from ordinary unwholesome acts committed in passing: it names a whole way of life oriented toward harm, not a single lapse.

2. Neither Restraint nor Non-Restraint

Chinese Source

云何非律儀非不律儀業？謂住非律儀非不律儀者所有善不善業。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is karma belonging to neither restraint nor non-restraint? Whatever wholesome or unwholesome karma belongs to one who abides in neither of the above — neither restraint nor non-restraint.

Reading note — after T1606

This third category is, in practice, by far the largest of the three, covering the great bulk of ordinary human activity — an ordinary person's everyday acts of generosity, casual unkindness, or morally neutral daily business, none of which is undertaken under a formal vow of restraint, and none of which constitutes a settled livelihood devoted to harm either. Restraint and non-restraint each name a genuine, deliberate commitment, one way or the other; this third category is simply everything else.

3. Karma Conducive to Pleasant, Painful, or Neutral Feeling

Chinese Source

又業差別有三種，謂順樂受業、順苦受業、順不苦不樂受業。順樂受業者，謂從欲界乃至第三靜慮所有善業。順苦受業者，謂不善業。順不苦不樂受業者，謂第三靜慮已上所有善業。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma, further, is of three kinds by what it is conducive to: karma conducive to pleasant feeling, to painful feeling, and to neither pleasant nor painful feeling. Karma conducive to pleasant feeling is every wholesome act from the desire realm up through the third dhyāna. Karma conducive to painful feeling is unwholesome karma. Karma conducive to neither pleasant nor painful feeling is every wholesome act belonging above the third dhyāna.

Reading note — after T1606

The boundary at the third dhyāna tracks a fact about meditative cosmology already established elsewhere on this site: the third dhyāna is the highest level at which genuine bodily and mental pleasure (sukha) is still present at all; from the fourth dhyāna upward, feeling settles permanently into pure equanimity, with neither pleasure nor pain remaining to be felt. Wholesome karma performed at or below the level where pleasure is still possible is naturally conducive to a pleasant result; wholesome karma performed at or oriented toward the levels beyond that point is instead conducive only to neutral feeling — not because it is somehow less wholesome, but simply because pleasant feeling itself is no longer part of what those higher, more refined states of experience actually offer.

4. Karma Conducive to This Life, the Next, or Later

Chinese Source

又業差別有三種，謂順現法受業、順生受業、順後受業。順現法受業者，若業於現法中異熟成熟。謂從慈定起已，於彼造作若損若益，必得現異熟。如從慈定起，從無諍定起、從滅定起、從預流果起、從阿羅漢果起亦爾。又於佛為上首僧中造善惡業，必得現異熟。又有餘猛利意樂方便所行善不善業，亦得現異熟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Karma, further, is of three kinds by when it is conducive to ripening: karma conducive to ripening in this very life, in the immediately following life, and in some later life still. Karma conducive to ripening in this very life is karma whose ripened result matures within this same lifetime — as when, having just arisen from the absorption of loving-kindness, whatever harm or benefit one then directs at another is certain to ripen in this very life. So too when arising from the absorption free of contention, from the attainment of cessation, from the fruit of stream-entry, or from the fruit of arhatship. Further, wholesome or unwholesome karma performed with respect to the Saṅgha headed by the Buddha is certain to ripen in this very life. Further, wholesome or unwholesome karma carried out with especially vigorous underlying intention and deliberate effort likewise ripens in this very life.

順生受業者，若業於無間生中異熟成熟，謂五無間業。復有所餘善不善業，於無間生異熟熟者，一切皆名順生受業。順後受業者，若業於無間生後異熟成熟，是名順後受業。

Karma conducive to ripening in the immediately following life is karma whose ripened result matures in the very next life — the five acts of immediate retribution being the paradigm case. Any further wholesome or unwholesome karma whose ripened result matures in the immediately following life is likewise called karma conducive to ripening in the immediately following life. Karma conducive to ripening later still is karma whose ripened result matures only after that immediately following life has already passed — this is what "conducive to ripening later" means.

Reading note — after T1606

The examples given for guaranteed same-life ripening are worth noting for what they have in common: each names a state of unusually concentrated, purified mental power — deep loving-kindness absorption, the absorption free of contention (a state specifically cultivated to prevent even inadvertently provoking others' afflictions), the attainment of cessation, or the moment of a major fruit of realization such as stream-entry or arhatship. T1606's underlying principle is that karma performed from within such an unusually powerful mental state, or performed with respect to an unusually powerful object (the Buddha's own community), or performed with unusually intense deliberate resolve, all share a common feature: the sheer intensity of the act itself is what forces its result to manifest immediately, without waiting for the ordinary, more gradual unfolding across future lives that most karma undergoes.
