The Five Stages of the Yogic Ground

The treatise now describes the actual concentration cultivated in dependence on the Dharma as a five-stage ground — support, application, mirror, illumination, and basis — culminating in a verse the World-Honored One himself spoke on watching one's own meditative image dissolve, first the grasped and then the grasper, into mind alone.

1. Support, Application, and Mirror

Chinese Source

依法勤修三摩地者，瑜伽地云何當知？有五種：一持、二作、三鏡、四明、五依。云何持？謂已積集菩提資糧，於煖等位、於諸聖諦所有多聞。云何作？謂緣此境如理作意。云何鏡？謂緣境有相三摩地。

Ru-Yi Translation

For one who diligently cultivates concentration in dependence on the Dharma, how is the yogic ground to be understood? It has five stages: support, application, mirror, illumination, and basis.

What is support? It is the extensive learning concerning the noble truths that belongs to one who has already accumulated the provisions for awakening, standing at the level of heat and the stages that follow it. What is application? It is correct mental application taking this very object as its support. What is mirror? It is the concentration, still bearing a sign, that takes this object as its own object.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains why "support" is the right name for the first stage: extensive learning about the four noble truths, gathered by a practitioner who has already built up the necessary provisions for awakening, is what all the subsequent stages of concentration will be built upon and supported by — hence the name. The second stage, application, is simply the correct, deliberate mental attention that then takes what has been learned as its own working object, thinking it through without distortion. The third stage, mirror, is called this because the concentration reached at this point, though it still bears a sign — meaning it still carries a mental image of its object rather than the object's own bare, unmediated nature — is nonetheless capable of clearly reflecting back what is being examined, the way a mirror reflects an image without itself being the thing reflected.

2. Illumination, and the World-Honored One's Verse

Chinese Source

云何明？謂能取所取無所得智。依此道理佛薄伽梵妙善宣說： 「菩薩於定位，　觀影唯是心， 義想既滅除，　審觀唯自想。 如是住內心，　知所取非有， 次能取亦無，　後觸無所得。」

Ru-Yi Translation

What is illumination? It is the knowledge of the utter non-attainment of both what grasps and what is grasped. In accordance with this very principle, the Buddha, the World-Honored One, spoke this excellent teaching:

"The bodhisattva in meditation
sees that the image is mind alone.
Once the notion of an object has been dissolved,
one examines closely: it is only one's own thought.

Abiding thus within the mind,
one knows that what is grasped does not exist.
Next, that what grasps is likewise absent.
Afterward, one touches upon utter non-attainment."

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 walks through this verse as a literal step-by-step meditation instruction, matching each line to a specific stage of insight. A bodhisattva seated in meditative concentration first observes the image appearing within that concentration and recognizes it as nothing other than mind itself, dependent entirely on that mind rather than existing as some external object being separately perceived. Once the very notion that this image corresponds to a real external object has thereby dissolved, the practitioner examines closely and finds only one's own thought remaining — the image was never anything but a projection of mind all along. Dwelling with the mind fixed on this recognition, one first comes to know that the grasped object simply does not exist. Since grasping and what is grasped are correlative — there being no grasper without something grasped — one next comes to know that the grasping subject is equally absent. And once both the grasped object and the grasping subject have in this way fallen away together, what remains is direct contact with utter non-attainment itself: a mind no longer divided into a knower standing over against a known.

3. Basis

Chinese Source

云何依？謂轉依。捨離諸麁重得清淨轉依故。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is basis? It is transformation of basis — since every coarse heaviness is abandoned and a pure transformation of basis is attained.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 notes that this five-stage ground is best understood as explained through two distinct halves: the first four stages — support, application, mirror, and illumination — explain the ground's own cause, the successive cultivation that actually produces it, while this fifth and final stage, basis, explains the ground's own fruit, the transformation of basis already examined at length in the treatise's account of the culminating path. What began, at the first stage, as ordinary extensive learning about the noble truths thus culminates, by the fifth, in the very same permanent removal of coarse heaviness and attainment of a purified basis that marks the path's own completion — the whole sequence forming a single continuous arc from hearing the Dharma to being transformed by it entirely.
