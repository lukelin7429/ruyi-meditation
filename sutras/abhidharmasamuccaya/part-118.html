Five Kinds of Bodhisattva Skill Begin

得品 turns, in its closing stretch, to five kinds of skill every bodhisattva must cultivate: in the object of knowledge, in skillful means, in false discrimination, in non-discrimination, and in sharpening one's own faculties. This part examines the first two — a sixfold analysis of the object of knowledge itself, and the four kinds of skillful means that carry a bodhisattva's entire activity forward.

1. The Five Kinds of Skill, Named

Chinese Source

又諸菩薩於所知境應修善巧，於諸方便應修善巧，於虛妄分別應修善巧，於無分別應修善巧，於時時中應修練根。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, bodhisattvas should cultivate skill with respect to the object of knowledge, should cultivate skill with respect to skillful means, should cultivate skill with respect to false discrimination, should cultivate skill with respect to non-discrimination, and should, from time to time, cultivate the sharpening of their own faculties.

2. Six Kinds of the Object of Knowledge

Chinese Source

云何所知境？謂略有六種：一迷亂、二迷亂所依、三不迷亂所依、四迷亂不迷亂、五不迷亂、六不迷亂等流。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the object of knowledge? In brief, there are six: delusion, the basis of delusion, the basis of non-delusion, both delusion and non-delusion together, non-delusion itself, and what flows forth from non-delusion.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains each of these six in turn, tracing a single path from ordinary confusion through to its final resolution. Delusion is simply the grasping at a perceiving subject and a perceived object as though these were genuinely, separately real. The basis of delusion is what the noble wisdom actually operates on — the false discrimination that consists purely in mistaken mental activity, and it is the presence of this very basis that causes every ordinary, unawakened being to fall into delusion and grasp accordingly. The basis of non-delusion is suchness itself, the very ground on which non-discriminating wisdom depends. Both delusion and non-delusion together names every wholesome dharma born of hearing and the like that accords with supramundane wisdom — still discriminating its object in the manner of delusion, yet already oriented toward, and in accordance with, the non-discriminating wisdom that lies ahead. Non-delusion itself is simply that non-discriminating wisdom, directly. And what flows forth from non-delusion is every wholesome dharma subsequently attained after the noble path itself.

Read in sequence, these six trace the entire arc of a bodhisattva's own relationship to knowing: beginning in ordinary delusion, tracing that delusion back to its own basis in false discrimination, recognizing the basis of non-delusion already present as suchness, passing through a transitional stage where mundane wholesome practice already accords with what lies ahead, arriving at non-discriminating wisdom directly, and finally continuing on in the wholesome activity that flows forth from it. Skill in the object of knowledge means correctly recognizing which of these six any given state of mind actually belongs to.

3. Four Kinds of Skillful Means

Chinese Source

云何方便善巧？謂略有四種：一成熟有情方便善巧、二圓滿佛法方便善巧、三速證通慧方便善巧、四道無斷絕方便善巧。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is skillful means? In brief, there are four: skillful means for ripening sentient beings, skillful means for perfecting the qualities of buddhahood, skillful means for swiftly realizing the higher knowledges, and skillful means for keeping the path from ever breaking off.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 identifies the specific practice behind each of the four. Skillful means for ripening sentient beings is the four means of attraction — generosity, kind speech, beneficial conduct, and cooperation — through which beings are drawn in and established in wholesome states. Skillful means for perfecting the qualities of buddhahood is specifically the perfection of wisdom, as a scripture states directly: if a bodhisattva-mahāsattva wishes to perfect generosity and every other perfection, up to and including the wisdom of every kind and mode itself, that bodhisattva should train in the perfection of wisdom — since it is wisdom alone that brings every other quality to its own genuine completion.

Skillful means for swiftly realizing the higher knowledges is the practice of confessing wrongdoing, rejoicing in others' merit, entreating the buddhas to remain and to teach, and dedicating one's own wholesome roots, all carried out across all six watches of day and night, exactly as set forth at length in the Sūtra of Noble Maitreya's Questions. And skillful means for keeping the path from ever breaking off is the non-abiding nirvāṇa itself — since it is precisely this that allows a bodhisattva's own activity, pursued again and again without ever reaching a final, terminal completion, to pervade every world-system in the ten directions, manifesting whatever buddha- or bodhisattva-conduct each situation calls for, without interruption.
