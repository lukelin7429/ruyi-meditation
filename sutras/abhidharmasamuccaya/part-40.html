Decisive Karma, and the Ten Karma-Paths' Three Fruits

The treatise now asks what makes a karmic act absolutely certain to ripen at all, then works out precisely what the ten unwholesome and ten wholesome karma-paths each produce — not a single fruit, but three distinct kinds at once — before turning to how attracting-karma and completing-karma jointly determine the specific shape of a single rebirth.

1. Three Kinds of Decisiveness

Chinese Source

如契經言：決定受業。云何名為決定受業？謂作業決定、受異熟決定、分位決定。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the sūtra says: decisive karma. What is decisive karma? Three kinds of decisiveness: decisiveness in the act's own performance, decisiveness that a ripened result will be received at all, and decisiveness as to precisely when.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains each in turn: decisiveness in the act's own performance means that, once a prior karmic force has already fixed a certain continuity of ripened result in motion, this present life is bound, by that same fixed force, to actually carry out this specific act — a determinacy so strong, T1606 states pointedly, that not even a buddha's own great supernormal power can prevent it, since a fixed cause's own power simply continues transforming into its fixed result regardless. Decisiveness that a result will be received at all is simply the deliberately-volitional karma already discussed in Part 39 — acts performed out of firmly rooted conviction or distorted reasoning invariably ripen. And decisiveness as to timing is what the next three parts on this site will examine in detail: whether an act ripens in this very life, in the immediately following life, or only after that.

2. The Unwholesome Karma-Paths' Three Fruits

Chinese Source

十不善業道異熟果者，於三惡趣中隨下中上品，受傍生、餓鬼、那落迦異熟。等流果者，各隨其相於人趣中感得自身眾具衰損。增上果者，各隨其相感得所有外事衰損。廣說如經。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten unwholesome karma-paths produce a ripened fruit: according to their lower, middle, or upper grade, one respectively undergoes rebirth as an animal, a hungry ghost, or a hell-being among the three unfortunate destinies. They produce an outflow fruit: each, in its own corresponding way, brings about a decline in one's own person and its supporting circumstances once reborn among humans. They produce a dominant fruit: each, in its own corresponding way, brings about a decline in the surrounding external circumstances one lives among. This is explained at length in the sūtras.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 spells out the outflow fruit for each of the ten unwholesome paths with striking specificity: killing yields a short lifespan once reborn human; taking what is not given yields chronic poverty; sexual misconduct yields an unfaithful spouse; false speech yields being widely slandered by others; divisive speech yields estrangement from friends and family; harsh speech yields constantly hearing unwelcome words; frivolous speech yields being spoken to without dignity or respect; covetousness yields ever-intensifying craving; ill will yields ever-intensifying hatred; and wrong view yields ever-intensifying delusion. The dominant fruit, meanwhile, plays out in the external world itself: killing yields a world where crops and produce lose their vitality and luster; taking what is not given yields frequent hailstorms and frost damage; sexual misconduct yields excessive dust and grime; false speech yields foul odors pervading the environment; divisive speech yields treacherous, uneven terrain; harsh speech yields salty, barren, unusable soil; frivolous speech yields unpredictable, disordered seasons; covetousness yields scant fruit and harvest; ill will yields bitter-tasting fruit; and wrong view yields fruit that is bitter or entirely absent. Each unwholesome act, on this account, leaves its mark simultaneously on the destiny one is reborn into, the person one becomes within that destiny, and the very world one inhabits.

3. The Wholesome Karma-Paths' Three Fruits

Chinese Source

十善業道異熟果者，於人、天趣中受人天異熟。等流果者，即於彼處各隨其相感得自身眾具興盛。增上果者，即於彼處各隨其相感得所有外事興盛。

Ru-Yi Translation

The ten wholesome karma-paths produce a ripened fruit: rebirth as a human or a god among the fortunate destinies. They produce an outflow fruit: within that same rebirth, each, in its own corresponding way, brings about flourishing in one's own person and its supporting circumstances. They produce a dominant fruit: within that same rebirth, each, in its own corresponding way, brings about flourishing in the surrounding external circumstances one lives among.

Reading note — after T1606

The wholesome karma-paths' three fruits mirror the unwholesome ones' exactly, item for item and in reverse: a long lifespan instead of a short one, wealth instead of poverty, a faithful spouse instead of an unfaithful one, and so on, down to a flourishing, fragrant, fertile world instead of a barren and foul one. The treatise leaves the wholesome parallels unstated in detail here precisely because the pattern has already been established in full by the unwholesome case just given — each unwholesome fruit's precise reverse is simply the corresponding wholesome fruit.

4. Attracting Karma and Completing Karma

Chinese Source

善不善業於善趣惡趣中感生異熟時，有招引業、圓滿業。招引業者，謂由此業能感異熟果。圓滿業者，謂由此業生已，領受愛不愛果。或有業由一業力牽得一身，或有業由一業力牽得多身，或有業由多業力牽得一身，或有業由多業力牽得多身。

Ru-Yi Translation

When wholesome and unwholesome karma bring about a ripened result in a fortunate or unfortunate destiny, this occurs through two kinds of karma: attracting karma and completing karma. Attracting karma is the karma that draws forth the ripened fruit itself — the specific destiny and general life-form one is born into. Completing karma is the karma that, once that rebirth has occurred, determines exactly what agreeable or disagreeable circumstances are then actually experienced within it.

There are cases where a single act of karma draws forth a single life; cases where a single act draws forth many lives; cases where many acts of karma together draw forth a single life; and cases where many acts together draw forth many lives.

若一有情成就多業，云何次第受異熟果？於彼身中重者先熟，或將死時現在前者、或先所數習者、或最初所行者，彼異熟先熟。

If a single sentient being possesses many accumulated acts of karma, in what order are their ripened results actually received? Within that being's own continuum, the heaviest ripens first; or, whichever act happens to be foremost in mind at the moment of death; or whichever has been most habitually practiced; or whichever was performed earliest of all — any of these, correspondingly, ripens first.

Reading note — after T1606

The attracting/completing distinction is one of this section's most practically important pieces of Abhidharma psychology, and it explains a puzzle anyone might otherwise wonder about: why do two people born into apparently identical circumstances — the same family, the same era — nonetheless live such different lives, one wealthy and content, another poor and miserable within the very same broad social position? The answer, on this account, is that attracting karma determines only the broad rebirth itself (human birth, into roughly this kind of circumstance), while a separate, further layer of completing karma then fills in the specific texture of agreeable and disagreeable experience within that rebirth — meaning a single rebirth is never the product of one uniform karmic cause, but of at least two distinct karmic forces operating at different levels of specificity. The closing four-way combinatorial breakdown (one act/one life, one act/many lives, many acts/one life, many acts/many lives) reinforces the same point from a different angle: there is no fixed one-to-one correspondence between a given deed and a given rebirth, and the ranking rule for which of many accumulated karmas ripens first — by weight, by what is uppermost in the dying mind, by habitual force, or by seniority — gives a concrete, ordered account of how a being's own complex karmic history actually resolves into the single next life that follows it.
