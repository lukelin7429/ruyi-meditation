Seven Mental Applications, and Naming Immeasurable Absorptions

The mundane path's remaining three structural aspects: the seven mental applications by which a practitioner actually moves into a new attainment, the threefold grading of how thoroughly any given dhyāna has been cultivated, and a striking closing admission — that the specific absorptions available to buddhas and advanced bodhisattvas number in the hundreds and beyond, far outrunning what any ordinary practitioner could ever hope to name or count.

1. Seven Mental Applications

Chinese Source

云何等至建立？謂由七種作意證入初靜慮，如是乃至非想非非想處。何等名為七種作意？謂了相作意、勝解作意、遠離作意、攝樂作意、觀察作意、加行究竟作意、加行究竟果作意。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is establishment by attainment? Entering the first dhyāna, and correspondingly every attainment through the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception, occurs through seven mental applications: the mental application of discerning the mark, the mental application of resolved understanding, the mental application of withdrawal, the mental application of gathering joy, the mental application of examination, the mental application of the preparatory approach's culmination, and the mental application of the preparatory approach's resultant fruit.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 walks through all seven as a single continuous sequence for entering the first dhyāna, generalizable to every higher attainment in turn. Discerning the mark first recognizes the desire realm's own coarse, unsettled character by contrast with the first dhyāna's stillness. Resolved understanding then moves beyond merely hearing or reflecting on this contrast, actively cultivating calm-abiding and insight focused directly on that coarse-versus-still distinction. Withdrawal is the actual severing of the strongest grade of affliction on the strength of that cultivation. Gathering joy, arising once a measure of genuine release and joy has already been tasted, deliberately cultivates further wholesome, uplifting states specifically to counter any remaining torpor or dullness. Examination periodically checks whether affliction has actually been abandoned or not, attending to whichever pure signs of progress have already arisen. The preparatory approach's culmination is the final, sustained effort that brings every remaining desire-realm affliction to a complete, if temporary, standstill. And the preparatory approach's resultant fruit is the first dhyāna's own root attainment itself, arising immediately afterward with no further gap. T1606 also draws a useful practical distinction between "coarse" and "still" marks generally: whatever realm lies below one's current level of attainment, from the desire realm up through the sphere of nothing whatsoever, is marked by heavy, unsettled suffering and a comparatively brief lifespan; whatever realm lies above is marked, correspondingly, by its opposite.

2. Grading, and Its Threefold Ripened Result

Chinese Source

云何品類建立？謂初靜慮具軟、中、上三品熏修。如初靜慮，餘靜慮及無色三品熏修亦爾。由軟中上品熏修初靜慮故，於初靜慮中還生三異熟。如初靜慮，於餘靜慮中若熏修若生果各三品亦爾。於無色界中無別處所故，不立生果處所差別。然由三品熏修無色定故，彼異熟生時有高有下、有劣有勝。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is establishment by grade? The first dhyāna admits of three grades of cultivation — mild, moderate, and intense. So too for every remaining dhyāna and formless sphere. Cultivating the first dhyāna through mild, moderate, or intense grade correspondingly gives rise to three distinct kinds of ripened result within the first dhyāna itself; the same threefold pattern holds, both in cultivation and in resultant fruit, for every remaining dhyāna. Within the formless realm, since there is no distinct physical location to speak of, no such difference in resultant place is established there — yet, on the strength of the same threefold grading in cultivating a formless attainment, the resulting rebirth still differs in rank, some higher and some lower, some inferior and some superior.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 grounds the concrete result of grading in the first dhyāna's own three named heavens — the Heaven of Brahmā's Retinue, the Heaven of Brahmā's Ministers, and the Great Brahmā Heaven, already familiar from this site's Abhidharmakośa pages — mild, moderate, and intense cultivation of the very same first dhyāna respectively producing rebirth into each. Since the formless realm has no physical place at all to differentiate in this way, the same threefold cultivation instead shows up purely in the quality of the resulting rebirth itself: differences of lifespan account for "higher and lower," while differences in whether that rebirth remains partly defiled or is fully purified, and in how thoroughly the ripened result manifests, account for "inferior and superior."

3. Naming: Countless, Largely Unnamed Absorptions

Chinese Source

云何名想建立？謂於初靜慮所攝定中，諸佛世尊及得究竟大威德菩薩摩訶薩所入三摩地，彼三摩地一切聲聞及獨覺等尚不了其名，豈能知數？況復證入？如於初靜慮所攝定中，於餘靜慮無色所攝定中亦爾。如是所說皆依靜慮波羅蜜多。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is establishment by name and concept? Among the attainments included within the first dhyāna, there are absorptions entered by buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, and by bodhisattva mahāsattvas who have reached the ultimate consummation and great majestic power — absorptions whose very names remain unknown even to every śrāvaka and solitary realizer, let alone their number, let alone actual entry into them. The same holds true, correspondingly, for the attainments included within every remaining dhyāna and formless sphere. All of this pertains to the perfection of meditative concentration.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 cites the Prajñāpāramitā scriptures directly on this point — naming well over a hundred distinct absorptions there alone — and notes that further Mahāyāna sūtras describe the total number as genuinely immeasurable, beyond any finite count at all. This closing observation deliberately punctures any illusion that the classical, tidy scheme of four dhyānas and four formless spheres, however thoroughly analyzed across this and the preceding part, exhausts what meditative attainment actually is: the entire structural apparatus just laid out — five factors, seven mental applications, three grades — describes only the outer skeleton shared by every practitioner's path, while the specific, named absorptions a fully accomplished being can actually enter within that same skeleton are, on this treatise's own account, effectively without limit.

4. Refinement: The Edge-Point Attainments

Chinese Source

何等清淨故？謂初靜慮中邊際定，乃至非想非非想處邊際定，是名清淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is refinement? The edge-point attainment of the first dhyāna, and correspondingly the edge-point attainment all the way through the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception — this is what refinement means.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains "edge-point attainment" (邊際定) as the furthest, most fully mastered form of a given dhyāna or formless sphere — cultivated specifically to bring about exceptional qualities and complete mastery over that level, and sustained all the way to its own outer limit before moving on. Together, the four aspects examined across this and the preceding part — defilement-versus-purity, structure (factor, attainment, grade, and name), and now refinement — give a genuinely complete account of the mundane path in every dimension the treatise considers relevant: what can go wrong within it, how it is internally built, how it is graded, how vast its actual scope really is, and how far any single level of it can ultimately be pushed.
