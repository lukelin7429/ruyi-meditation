Twenty-Eight Wrong Views

The Great Dharma Mirror Sūtra's own catalogue of twenty-eight wrong views is now examined — not twenty-eight unrelated errors but a single misreading of emptiness cascading, step by step, from denying the three natures into denigrating the śrāvaka teaching, refusing correction from others, and finally claiming a false attainment that was never actually reached.

1. The Sūtra's Warning, and the List of Twenty-Eight

Chinese Source

依此密意，薄伽梵於《大法鏡經》中說如是言：若諸菩薩隨言取義，不如正理思擇法故，便生二十八不正見。何等名為二十八不正見？謂相見、損減施設見、損減分別見、損減真實見、攝受見、轉變見、無罪見、出離見、輕毀見、憤發見、顛倒見、出生見、不立宗見、矯亂見、敬事見、堅固愚癡見、根本見、於見無見見、捨方便見、不出離見、障增益見、生非福見、無功果見、受辱見、誹謗見、不可與言見、廣大見、增上慢見。

Ru-Yi Translation

In accordance with this hidden intention, the World-Honored One, in the Great Dharma Mirror Sūtra, spoke these words: if bodhisattvas grasp at words for their meaning, failing to investigate the Dharma in accordance with correct reasoning, then twenty-eight wrong views arise. What are these twenty-eight wrong views? The view of characteristics; the denigrating view of designation; the denigrating view of discrimination; the denigrating view of reality; the view of appropriation; the view of distortion; the view of no wrongdoing; the view of deliverance; the view of contempt; the view of indignation; the view of inversion; the view of generation; the view of refusing to establish a position; the view of evasive confusion; the view of reverent service; the view of obstinate ignorance; the root view; the view that sees no-view within a view; the view of abandoning skillful means; the view of non-deliverance; the view that obstructs by augmentation; the view that generates demerit; the view of fruitless effort; the view of receiving disgrace; the view of slander; the view that cannot be reasoned with; the view of grandiosity; and the view of overweening pride.

2. How the Twenty-Eight Cascade from a Single Error

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains that these twenty-eight are not a random assortment of separate mistakes but a single chain reaction, each view generating the next once the first misstep has been taken — and the whole sequence begins from exactly the literal misreading warned against in Part 78: hearing that all dharmas are said to lack own-nature, and concluding from this that nothing exists at all. Taking hold of this mistaken "characteristic" of universal non-existence is itself the view of characteristics. Holding this view, one then denies, one after another, the three natures this treatise has examined throughout — the merely designated existence of the discriminated nature, the dependent nature's own provisional causal existence, and the perfected nature's own true reality — giving rise to the three denigrating views of designation, discrimination, and reality in turn.

Having denied all three natures outright, one then works to justify this denial: seizing on some small fragment of correct reasoning to prop it up, and forcibly reinterpreting every scripture of fully explicit meaning until it seems to agree — this is the view of appropriation paired with the view of distortion. From here one concludes that, since nothing ultimately exists, no action — wholesome or unwholesome — carries any real consequence, and that whatever afflictions one has already accumulated are simply already free of them: the view of no wrongdoing paired with the view of deliverance. Holding this conviction, one turns to belittle the aggregates and other categories taught in the śrāvaka basket as beneath consideration, and grows hostile toward those who reject this very belittling: the view of contempt paired with the view of indignation. One then claims to establish emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness while actually mistaking their genuine meaning for its opposite, and further imagines that anyone who penetrates or is led to penetrate this distorted teaching thereby generates immeasurable merit: the view of inversion paired with the view of generation.

When someone correctly challenges this position, rather than defend it honestly one simply refuses to commit to any stated position at all, meeting honest challenge instead with mockery: the view of refusing to establish a position paired with the view of evasive confusion. One then comes to regard this very conduct as the truest form of reverence for the buddhas — the view of reverent service — and, when others skillfully try to correct this error through genuine reasoning, one clings to the mistaken conviction all the more rigidly, certain that only this view is true and every other position false — the view of obstinate ignorance. The accumulated habitual imprint of all these errors together is what is called the root view.

T1606 then explains that ten further names in the list are not ten additional errors but the same seventeen just described, viewed once more from the angle of their specific faults: the view of characteristics is also called the view that sees no-view within a view, since it clings rigidly to "no-nature" as itself a fixed characteristic; the three denigrating views are also called the view of abandoning skillful means, since they treat diligent practice as pointless; appropriation and distortion together are the view of non-deliverance, since denying all reality removes any genuine motive to strive further; no-wrongdoing and deliverance together are the view that obstructs by augmentation, since believing a fruit already attained without the actual practice needed to attain it only adds a further obstacle; contempt and indignation together are the view that generates demerit, since this conduct can never be exhausted through its own devices; inversion and generation together are the view of fruitless effort, since this same misguided conduct toward genuine practitioners brings great loss rather than the merit imagined; refusing to establish a position and evasive confusion together are the view of receiving disgrace, since neither the one holding this view nor anyone persuaded by it can ever actually reach a genuinely superior result; reverent service alone is also the view of slander, since arguing from bad premises can never yield genuine victory; and obstinate ignorance alone is also the view that cannot be reasoned with, since further argument with someone so entrenched serves no purpose. The root view, T1606 adds, is also called the view of grandiosity, since this same accumulated error only continues to expand across future lives.

Finally, all twenty-seven of these — the original seventeen and their ten restated counterparts alike — are collectively named the twenty-eighth and final view, the view of overweening pride, since every one of them rests on a false and groundless confidence in what has, in reality, never actually been attained. The sūtra itself confirms this collapsing structure directly: the seventeen are the same as the ten, and the ten are the same as the seventeen; the twenty-seven are the same as the one, and the one is the same as the twenty-seven — a single cascading error, counted twenty-eight ways.
