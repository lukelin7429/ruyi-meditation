Realm, and Persons Beyond the Ordinary Threefold Scheme

The sixth distinction sorts every ordinary being, trainee, and one beyond training across all three realms of existence — before the treatise names three persons this same threefold scheme cannot fully capture: the bodhisattva who deliberately declines rebirth in the formless realm, the pratyekabuddha who arises specifically in the desire realm, and the inconceivable Tathāgata.

1. Ordinary Being, Trainee, and One Beyond Training, Across the Three Realms

Chinese Source

云何界差別？謂欲界異生有學無學。如欲界有，三色無色界亦爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the distinction of realm? It is: the ordinary being, the trainee, and the one beyond training, each within the desire realm. Just as the desire realm has these three, so too do each of the three form realms and the formless realm.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 defines each of the three plainly: the ordinary being is one who, born and raised within a given realm, has not yet attained any of the noble teachings at all. The trainee is one who, born and raised within that same realm, has already attained the noble teachings but still retains some remaining fetter yet to be severed. And the one beyond training is one who, born and raised within that same realm, has already attained the noble teachings and retains no remaining fetter whatsoever. This same threefold pattern — ordinary being, trainee, one beyond training — repeats identically across all four realms of rebirth, the desire realm together with each level of the form and formless realms, giving a total of twelve distinct combinations of person and realm.

2. Three Persons Beyond the Ordinary Scheme

Chinese Source

又有欲色界菩薩，又有欲界獨覺及不可思議如來補特伽羅差別故。何等欲色界菩薩補特伽羅？謂與滅離無色界生靜慮相應住靜慮樂而生欲界或生色界。何等欲界獨覺補特伽羅？謂無佛出世時生於欲界，自然證得獨覺菩提。何等不可思議如來補特伽羅？謂且於欲界始從示現安住覩史多天妙寶宮殿，乃至示現大般涅槃，示現一切諸佛菩薩所行大行。

Ru-Yi Translation

There is, further, the bodhisattva of the desire and form realms, and there is, further, the pratyekabuddha of the desire realm and the inconceivable Tathāgata, each a distinct classification of person in its own right.

What is the bodhisattva of the desire and form realms? One who, possessing the meditative absorption that dispels rebirth in the formless realm and abiding in the happiness of meditative absorption, nonetheless takes rebirth in the desire realm or in the form realm.

What is the pratyekabuddha of the desire realm? One who, in an age when no buddha has appeared in the world, is born within the desire realm and spontaneously realizes pratyekabuddha awakening.

What is the inconceivable Tathāgata? One who, taking the desire realm alone as the immediate frame of reference, manifests everything from dwelling in the wondrous jeweled palace of the Tuṣita heaven all the way through to manifesting great final nirvāṇa — displaying every great deed carried out by all buddhas and bodhisattvas alike.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains why the bodhisattva of the desire and form realms deserves its own separate classification: such a bodhisattva has, in fact, already mastered the very meditative absorption that would allow rebirth in the formless realm, and could take such rebirth if desired, yet deliberately declines it and remains within the desire or form realm instead. The reason is entirely practical — a bodhisattva of genuine, superior spiritual power takes rebirth wherever it can actually benefit and bring happiness to sentient beings, and the formless realm, lacking any physical form through which such beings could be reached and matured, is simply not a place where that work can be carried out. Skillfully turning back from the formless realm's own deeper absorption while still not losing the meditative happiness already attained, such a bodhisattva instead takes rebirth in whichever of the two lower realms will actually serve the beings still to be matured.

The pratyekabuddha of the desire realm stands apart from the śrāvaka-vehicle and Mahāyāna persons already classified earlier in this same chapter because this particular vehicle's own defining feature — awakening spontaneously, without a living teacher, in an age when no buddha has yet appeared — locates it specifically within the desire realm rather than distributing it, as the other vehicles are, across all three realms alike.

And the inconceivable Tathāgata is named this way precisely because a single, complete account of the buddha's own activity cannot be confined to any one of the ordinary categories already given. T1606 specifies what the full span of this manifestation actually includes: everything a bodhisattva does, from dwelling in the Tuṣita heaven's own palace all the way through to manifesting the great miraculous display that subdues Māra's own army, together with everything a buddha then does, from manifesting complete and perfect awakening all the way through to manifesting great final nirvāṇa itself — the entire arc of a buddha's career, taken as a single, unified person that the ordinary categories of ordinary being, trainee, and one beyond training were never designed to capture in the first place.
