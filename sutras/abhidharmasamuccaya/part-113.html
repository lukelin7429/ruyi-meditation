How These Qualities Are Generated

Having catalogued all eighteen categories of qualities across thirteen parts, the treatise pauses to explain how any of them is actually brought forth in the first place: the pure fourth dhyāna as their shared foundation, who is actually capable of generating each one, the specific skillful means of repeated reflection, and the two distinct modes — actively manifesting or simply abiding — in which any such quality can be present at a given moment.

1. Basis, Practitioner, and Skillful Means

Chinese Source

云何引發如是等功德？謂依止清淨四靜慮。若外道若聲聞若菩薩等，引發四無量五神通，多分依止邊際第四靜慮，若聲聞若菩薩若如來等引發所餘功德。何因引發如是功德？謂依止靜慮數數思惟，隨所建立法故。

Ru-Yi Translation

How are these qualities actually generated? They rest on the four pure dhyānas. Non-Buddhist practitioners, śrāvakas, and bodhisattvas alike generate the four immeasurables and the five superknowledges — for the most part resting specifically on the edge-point attainment of the fourth dhyāna. Śrāvakas, bodhisattvas, and Tathāgatas alike generate every remaining quality.

By what cause are these qualities generated? By resting on dhyāna and reflecting on them again and again, in accordance with whatever teaching establishes each one.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 clarifies who is actually capable of generating which qualities. The four immeasurables and the first five superknowledges — every one except the exhaustion of the contaminants, already noted in Part 105 as unique to the liberated — are open even to non-Buddhist practitioners who have never taken up the Buddhist path at all, provided they have mastered the fourth dhyāna's own edge-point attainment; śrāvakas and bodhisattvas generate these same qualities on the very same basis. Every remaining quality in this catalogue — from the eight liberations through the eighteen exclusive buddha-qualities — is reserved specifically for śrāvakas, bodhisattvas, and Tathāgatas, since these depend on a degree of realization non-Buddhist practice alone cannot reach.

T1606 also names the specific skillful means by which any of these qualities is actually cultivated: taking up whatever teaching establishes a given quality and, resting on dhyāna, bringing repeated, deliberate mental attention to bear on it again and again through a concentrated mind. When generating the immeasurables, for instance, one rests on dhyāna and repeatedly reflects, through wisdom-associated mental attention, on the teaching concerning a mind joined with loving-kindness, free of resentment and enmity. When generating the superknowledges, one rests on dhyāna and repeatedly reflects, in the very same manner, on the teaching concerning transforming one body into many. This same pattern of repeated, deliberate reflection on the relevant teaching, T1606 notes, applies correspondingly to every quality in the entire catalogue.

2. Two Modes of Presence: Actively Manifesting, and Simply Abiding

Chinese Source

如是等功德略有二種：一現前發起自所作用、二安住自性。若現發起自所作用，以出世後所得世俗智為體。若安住自性，用出世智為體。

Ru-Yi Translation

These qualities are, in brief, of two kinds: actively giving rise, in the present, to their own specific function, and simply abiding in their own essential nature. When actively giving rise to their own function in the present, their essence is the conventional wisdom subsequently attained after the supramundane; when simply abiding in their own essential nature, their essence is the supramundane wisdom itself.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains this twofold distinction through what each mode actually accomplishes. Actively giving rise to a quality's own function means the noble ones, as circumstances require, actually carry out the various specific tasks that quality is meant for — severing whatever obstruction it counteracts, and the like — an active engagement whose essence is the ordinary, conventional wisdom that arises subsequently, once the supramundane path itself has already been traversed. Simply abiding in a quality's own essential nature, by contrast, means resting in the most excellent, still, non-discriminating wisdom itself — an objectless, boundless dwelling in present happiness, whose essence is the supramundane wisdom directly. Every quality in this entire catalogue, T1606 notes, can be understood through this same twofold lens: at times actively deployed to accomplish some specific task in the world, and at times simply present as the settled, non-discriminating condition of a mind that has already realized what these qualities themselves ultimately point toward.
