The Six Kinds of Arhat

Attaining arhatship does not, on this classical Abhidharma analysis, guarantee identical stability across every arhat alike. The treatise now sorts arhats into six kinds by how susceptible each remains to losing their present dwelling in the happiness of this very life — a susceptibility governed by faculty sharpness and, in the case of the two mildest kinds, by whether restlessness or distraction is actually present.

1. Prone to Retrogression, and Guarding Through Reflection

Chinese Source

何等退法阿羅漢？謂鈍根性，若遊散若不遊散、若思惟若不思惟，皆可退失現法樂住。何等思法阿羅漢？謂鈍根性，若遊散若不遊散，若不思惟即可退失現法樂住，若思惟已能不退失。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the arhat prone to retrogression? One of dull faculties who, whether distracted or undistracted, and whether having reflected or not having reflected, may in every case lose their present dwelling in the happiness of this very life. What is the arhat who guards through reflection? One of dull faculties who, whether distracted or undistracted, may lose that same dwelling if reflection has not occurred, but who, once reflection has occurred, is able not to lose it.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains "reflection" here in a specific, technical sense proper to this classical analysis: it refers to the arhat's readiness to actively end their own present existence, through the arhat's own spiritual power, rather than risk an actual retrogression from the fruit already attained — "having reflected" means having formed this specific readiness; "not having reflected" means not having formed it. On this account, the arhat prone to retrogression is so weak in faculty that even this readiness offers no protection at all — distraction alone, whether or not this readiness has been formed, is enough to cause the loss of their present dwelling in meditative happiness, referring specifically to the mundane dhyāna-level absorptions this dwelling rests on, not to arhatship's own underlying liberation from the contaminants, which every one of the six kinds shares alike and none of them ever loses. The arhat who guards through reflection stands one degree steadier: distraction alone is enough to cause loss only when this protective readiness has not been formed, but once it has been formed, that same distraction no longer succeeds in dislodging the present dwelling.

2. Guarding Through Vigilance, and Unshakable

Chinese Source

何等護法阿羅漢？謂鈍根性，若遊散便可退失現法樂住，若不遊散即能不退。何等住不動阿羅漢？謂鈍根性，若遊散若不遊散，皆能不退現法樂住，亦不能練根。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the arhat who guards through vigilance? One of dull faculties who may lose their present dwelling if distracted, but who is able not to lose it so long as they remain undistracted. What is the unshakable arhat? One of dull faculties who, whether distracted or undistracted, is able in every case not to lose their present dwelling — though also unable to sharpen their own faculties.

Reading note — after T1606

Each kind in this sequence stands one degree steadier than the one before it. The arhat who guards through vigilance no longer needs the special readiness to end their own existence in order to hold their attainment — simple ongoing vigilance against distraction is already sufficient — but distraction, once it does occur, is still enough to cause loss. The unshakable arhat has advanced further still: distraction itself no longer poses any threat at all, whether present or absent, and the present dwelling remains secure regardless. T1606 notes one specific limitation that still applies even here: this same unshakable arhat, despite this stability, remains unable to sharpen their own faculties — that is, unable to convert their still-dull faculties into sharp ones — a further capacity reserved for the kind examined next.

3. Capable of Sharpening the Faculties, and Unshakable by Nature

Chinese Source

何等堪達阿羅漢？謂鈍根性，若遊散若不遊散，皆能不退現法樂住，然堪能練根。何等不動法阿羅漢？謂利根性，若遊散若不遊散，皆能不退現法樂住。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the arhat capable of sharpening the faculties? One of dull faculties who, whether distracted or undistracted, is able in every case not to lose their present dwelling, and who is furthermore capable of sharpening their own faculties. What is the arhat unshakable by nature? One of sharp faculties from the very outset who, whether distracted or undistracted, is able in every case not to lose their present dwelling.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 clarifies precisely what distinguishes this fifth kind from the unshakable arhat examined just before it: both share the very same stability against distraction, but only this fifth kind retains the further capacity to actually convert their own still-dull faculties into sharp ones, "sharpening the faculties" meaning exactly this conversion from a lower, dull faculty into a higher, sharp one. The sixth and final kind, unshakable by nature, requires no such conversion at all, since sharp faculties were already present from the very beginning rather than needing to be cultivated afterward — the same unshakable stability shared by the two kinds before it, but paired from the outset with sharp faculties rather than dull ones sharpened only later. Taken as a whole, these six kinds trace a single continuous progression: from an arhat whose attainment remains fully vulnerable to ordinary distraction, through several intermediate degrees of increasing security, to one whose attainment was never, from the very start, capable of being shaken at all.
