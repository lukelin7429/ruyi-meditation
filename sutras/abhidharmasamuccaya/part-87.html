Stream-Entry, and the Three Fetters

The treatise now opens its account of the four classic pairs of path and fruit with the very first: one approaching stream-entry, spanning the entire path of preparation and the first fifteen mind-instants of the path of seeing, and one who has actually attained it at the sixteenth — together with a careful explanation of why the noble path is said to sever only three fetters at this moment, though every affliction belonging to the path of seeing has, in fact, already been left behind.

1. Approaching and Attaining Stream-Entry

Chinese Source

何等預流向補特伽羅？謂住順決擇分位，及住見道十五心剎那位。何等預流果補特伽羅？謂住見道第十六心剎那位。即此見道，亦名趣入正性決定，亦名於法現觀。若於欲界未離欲者，後入正性決定位，得預流果。若於欲界倍離欲者，後入正性決定位，得一來果。若已離欲界欲者，後入正性決定位，得不還果。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the person approaching stream-entry? One standing at the aid toward penetrating insight, and one standing within the first fifteen mind-instants of the path of seeing. What is the person of the fruit of stream-entry? One standing at the sixteenth mind-instant of the path of seeing. This very path of seeing is also called entering into the certainty of correctness, and is also called direct realization with respect to the Dharma.

If one who has not yet become free of desire for the desire realm subsequently enters the certainty of correctness, the fruit of stream-entry is attained. If one who has already become twice free of desire for the desire realm subsequently enters the certainty of correctness, the fruit of once-returning is attained. If one who has already become entirely free of desire for the desire realm subsequently enters the certainty of correctness, the fruit of non-returning is attained.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 clarifies exactly what "twice free" and "entirely free" mean here: a practitioner may, prior to ever entering the path of seeing at all, have already used the ordinary mundane path to suppress a certain number of the nine graded levels of affliction bound to the desire realm. One who has already suppressed the first six of these nine grades through the mundane path alone is called "twice free of desire," and such a person, upon reaching the sixteenth mind-instant of the path of seeing, attains the fruit of once-returning directly, skipping over the fruit of stream-entry entirely. One who has already suppressed all nine grades through the mundane path is called "entirely free of desire," and such a person likewise attains the fruit of non-returning directly at that same sixteenth instant. This is why the very same sixteenth mind-instant of the path of seeing can mark three entirely different fruits depending on what mundane progress a given practitioner had already made beforehand — the path of seeing itself is identical in every case, but what it delivers depends on the ground already covered before it began.

2. Why Only Three Fetters Are Named

Chinese Source

若已永斷見道所斷一切煩惱得預流果，何故但言永斷三結得預流果？最勝攝故。何故最勝？以於解脫是不發趣因故，雖已發趣復為邪出離因故，及為不正出離因故。又此三結是迷所知境因故、迷見因故、迷對治因故。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the fruit of stream-entry is attained by the permanent severing of every affliction belonging to the path of seeing, why is it said only that the fruit of stream-entry is attained by the permanent severing of the three fetters? Because these three are comprehended as the most excellent among them. Why are they the most excellent? Because self-view is the cause of never even setting out toward liberation at all; because attachment to rules and rituals, even once one has set out, is the cause of a mistaken form of deliverance; and because doubt is the cause of an incorrect form of deliverance. Further, these three fetters are, respectively, the cause of confusion regarding the object to be known, the cause of confusion regarding correct view, and the cause of confusion regarding the correct antidote.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains why self-view specifically prevents anyone from even beginning the journey toward liberation: one who grasps the five appropriated aggregates as constituting a self, or as belonging to a self, comes to deeply cherish that very self, and consequently never develops genuine disenchantment with the great mass of suffering these aggregates actually involve — without disenchantment, the mind never inclines toward liberation in the first place. Some beings do get further than this, genuinely setting out toward liberation, but two further errors can still derail even a sincere search: attachment to rules and rituals leads a practitioner to mistake some other, incorrect practice for the genuine path to purity, taking a distorted view for a clean way out — a mistaken form of deliverance; and doubt leaves a practitioner unable to settle with certainty on the three jewels as the actual correct antidote, wavering indefinitely instead — an incorrect form of deliverance. Taken together, then, these three fetters cover the three points at which the entire enterprise can go wrong from the very start: confusion about the object of knowledge itself (self-view, distorting the very aggregates that are to be correctly known), confusion in correct view (attachment to rules and rituals, substituting a wrong view of the path for the right one), and confusion regarding the genuine antidote (doubt, preventing confidence in the three jewels that alone can actually counteract affliction). It is specifically because overcoming these three removes every obstacle capable of blocking the path at its very root — even though every other affliction belonging to the path of seeing is, in strict fact, equally and simultaneously severed at this same moment — that these three alone are singled out as what defines the attainment of stream-entry.
