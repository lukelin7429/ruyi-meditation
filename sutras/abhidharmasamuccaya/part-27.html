The Eight Kinds of Suffering

Having mapped suffering's own physical and cosmological extent, the treatise now analyzes suffering itself: the eight classical kinds named in the scriptures, why each one specifically hurts, how they compress to six and re-expand to eight without contradiction, and how this same eightfold list maps cleanly onto both the three kinds of suffering already introduced in Part 23 and a further, sharper distinction between conventional and ultimate suffering.

1. Eight Kinds of Suffering, and Why Each One Hurts

Chinese Source

復次苦相差別有八，謂生苦、老苦、病苦、死苦、怨憎會苦、愛別離苦、求不得苦，略攝一切五取蘊苦。生何因苦？眾苦所逼故、餘苦所依故。老何因苦？時分變壞苦故。病何因苦？大種變異苦故。死何因苦？受命變壞苦故。怨憎會何因苦？合會生苦故。愛別離何因苦？別離生苦故。求不得何因苦？所悕不果生苦故。略攝一切五取蘊何因苦？麁重苦故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Suffering's own distinct marks, further, are eight: the suffering of birth, of old age, of sickness, of death, of encountering what is hateful, of separation from what is loved, of not obtaining what is sought, and — in brief compass — the suffering of the five grasped aggregates themselves.

By what cause is birth suffering? Because it is beset by every other kind of suffering, and serves as the basis every further kind of suffering depends on. By what cause is old age suffering? Because it is the suffering of one's own span of time altering and decaying. By what cause is sickness suffering? Because it is the suffering of the great elements themselves becoming disordered. By what cause is death suffering? Because it is the suffering of one's own allotted life altering and decaying. By what cause is encountering what is hateful suffering? Because such an encounter itself gives rise to suffering. By what cause is separation from what is loved suffering? Because such a separation itself gives rise to suffering. By what cause is not obtaining what is sought suffering? Because failing to attain what one hopes for itself gives rise to suffering. By what cause is the five grasped aggregates, in brief compass, suffering? Because of the underlying heaviness they carry.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 unpacks the first two causes with unusual physical specificity: birth is beset by suffering because of what a fetus itself endures — surrounded, in the womb, by wholly unclean substances between the stomach and the bowels, and then, at the moment of emergence, crushed and squeezed through the birth canal — and it further serves as "the basis every further suffering depends on" simply because, without birth occurring at all, none of old age, sickness, or death could follow. The eighth item — "the five grasped aggregates, in brief compass" — is worth noting as a genuine summary category rather than an eighth coordinate suffering alongside the other seven: it names the underlying heaviness (麁重, a term already established in this treatise's account of the formations dissociated from mind) that pervades the very fact of having aggregates capable of suffering at all, prior to and beneath any of the seven specific instances already listed.

2. Eight Compress to Six, and Six Expand Back to Eight

Chinese Source

如是八種略攝為六，謂逼迫苦、轉變苦、合會苦、別離苦、所悕不果苦、麁重苦。如是六種廣開為八，若六若八平等平等。

Ru-Yi Translation

These eight kinds compress, in brief, to six: the suffering of oppression, the suffering of alteration, the suffering of encounter, the suffering of separation, the suffering of unfulfilled hope, and the suffering of underlying heaviness. These same six kinds, opened out fully, expand back to eight — the six and the eight are, in every respect, exactly equivalent.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains the compression directly: birth's own oppressive character stands alone as "the suffering of oppression"; old age, sickness, and death together — since all three are, at bottom, forms of alteration and decay — collapse into a single "suffering of alteration"; encountering what is hateful, separation from what is loved, and not obtaining what is sought each keep their own separate slot; and the fifth aggregate-suffering keeps its own slot as "the suffering of underlying heaviness." The six-to-eight expansion simply reverses this move, splitting "alteration" back into its three original components. Neither count is more correct than the other — the treatise's own point in stating this explicitly is that both numbers describe the identical underlying reality, sorted at two different levels of granularity.

3. How Three Kinds of Suffering, and Two Truths of Suffering, Both Map onto the Eight

Chinese Source

如說三苦，此中八苦，為三攝八、八攝三耶？展轉相攝。所謂生苦、老苦、病苦、死苦、怨憎會苦，能顯苦苦；愛別離苦、求不得苦，能顯壞苦；略攝一切五取蘊苦，能顯行苦。

Ru-Yi Translation

As is said of the three kinds of suffering, do these three subsume the eight, or do the eight subsume the three? They mutually subsume one another. Birth, old age, sickness, death, and encountering what is hateful together display the suffering of suffering. Separation from what is loved and not obtaining what is sought together display the suffering of change. The five grasped aggregates, in brief compass, display the suffering of conditioned existence itself.

如說二苦，謂世俗諦苦、勝義諦苦。何者世俗諦苦？何者勝義諦苦？謂生苦乃至求不得苦，是世俗諦苦。略攝一切五取蘊苦，是勝義諦苦。

As is said of the two kinds of suffering — conventional-truth suffering and ultimate-truth suffering — which is which? Birth through not obtaining what is sought are conventional-truth suffering. The five grasped aggregates, in brief compass, are ultimate-truth suffering.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains why the first five items (birth, old age, sickness, death, encountering what is hateful) all display the suffering of suffering specifically: each is itself a state that accords with painful feeling's own particular character — pain simply is what they consist of, directly. The next two (separation from what is loved, not obtaining what is sought) display the suffering of change instead, because what makes them hurt is precisely the loss or non-arrival of something already or potentially pleasant — the suffering here is parasitic on a prior or hoped-for pleasure being disrupted, not pain in its own right. And the aggregate-suffering alone displays the suffering of conditioned existence itself, since it is present whether feeling is painful, pleasant, or neutral — the deepest, most pervasive layer, as already established in Part 23.

The conventional/ultimate split then draws the sharpest possible line within this same eightfold scheme: the first seven kinds of suffering are all conventional-truth suffering, because they are exactly the kind of experience an ordinary, worldly mind can recognize and name as suffering without any special training. Only the eighth — suffering as the mere fact of possessing grasped aggregates at all, entirely independent of whether any particular moment feels painful — counts as ultimate-truth suffering, since seeing it clearly requires the kind of penetrating insight only a supramundane wisdom, oriented toward suchness, can actually provide. This is the treatise's own way of explaining why ordinary people, quite reasonably, do not experience most of their lives as pure suffering, while the Buddha's teaching nonetheless insists that all conditioned existence is suffering without exception: both claims are true, at two different levels of the very same eightfold analysis.
