Five Ways the Non-Returner Attains Final Nirvāṇa

Following the Seven Fine Men Sūtra's own classic sevenfold division, the treatise now catalogues five distinct ways a non-returner reaches final nirvāṇa without ever again taking rebirth in the desire realm — three timings within the intermediate state, an effortless and an effortful path immediately upon rebirth, and a final course that streams upward through every level of the form realm before reaching its very summit.

1. Final Nirvāṇa in the Intermediate State, Three Ways

Chinese Source

何等中般涅槃補特伽羅？謂生結已斷、起結未斷，或中有纔起即便聖道現前得盡苦際。或中有起已為趣生有，纔起思惟即便聖道現前得盡苦際。或思惟已發趣生有，未到生有即便聖道現前得盡苦際。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the person of final nirvāṇa in the intermediate state? One in whom the fetter that generates rebirth has already been severed, though the fetter that causes further arising has not yet been severed. Either the noble path manifests the very moment the intermediate state first arises, thereby reaching the very limit of suffering; or the intermediate state has already arisen, and the very moment reflection turns toward the next rebirth-existence, the noble path manifests, thereby reaching the very limit of suffering; or reflection has already turned toward the next rebirth-existence and one has already set out toward it, and before that existence is actually reached, the noble path manifests, thereby reaching the very limit of suffering.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 clarifies the subtle distinction behind "the fetter that generates rebirth" and "the fetter that causes further arising": the active afflictive force that would actually drive a being onward toward taking a new rebirth-existence has, in every one of these three cases, already been exhausted, but the residual underlying tendency that still causes the aggregates to continue arising in sequence after death — dormant rather than actively operating — has not yet been fully exhausted. Following the Seven Fine Men Sūtra, these three variants are distinguished purely by timing, measured against how far a being has progressed toward the next existence at the moment the noble path finally manifests and burns away this last remaining tendency: in the very first variant, this happens the instant the intermediate state itself first arises, before any movement toward the next existence has even begun; in the second, it happens the instant reflection first turns toward that next existence, already somewhat underway; and in the third, it happens only once that movement has been sustained for some time, right at the final threshold, just before the next existence is actually reached. In every case, the crucial point is the same: final nirvāṇa is reached entirely within the intermediate state itself, never requiring the next rebirth to actually occur at all.

2. Final Nirvāṇa Immediately upon Rebirth, With and Without Further Effort

Chinese Source

何等生般涅槃補特伽羅？謂二結俱未斷，纔生色界已即便聖道現前得盡苦際。何等無行般涅槃？謂生彼已，不由加行聖道現前得盡苦際。何等有行般涅槃補特伽羅？謂生彼已，由加行力聖道現前得盡苦際。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the person of final nirvāṇa immediately upon rebirth? One in whom neither of the two fetters has yet been severed, who, the very moment rebirth into the form realm occurs, has the noble path manifest, thereby reaching the very limit of suffering. What is final nirvāṇa without further effort? One who, having taken that same rebirth, has the noble path manifest without needing any further deliberate exertion, thereby reaching the very limit of suffering. What is final nirvāṇa through further effort? One who, having taken that same rebirth, has the noble path manifest specifically through the power of further deliberate exertion, thereby reaching the very limit of suffering.

Reading note — after T1606

Where the previous three variants all reach final nirvāṇa before ever completing rebirth into the form realm, this pair — and the general case preceding it — waits until that rebirth has actually occurred. "Without further effort" means that the very power of habits already thoroughly cultivated in previous lives is sufficient for the uncontaminated noble path to manifest spontaneously, requiring no additional deliberate exertion at all once that rebirth has taken place. "Through further effort" is simply the opposite case: the very same rebirth has occurred, but the noble path manifests only once further deliberate exertion is actually applied. Both variants share the same starting point — full rebirth into the form realm, with neither fetter yet severed at the moment of that rebirth — differing only in whether the final push toward liberation still requires effort or arises entirely on its own.

3. Streaming Upward to the Very Summit

Chinese Source

何等上流補特伽羅？謂於色界地地中皆受生已，乃至最後入色究竟，於彼無漏聖道現前得盡苦際。復有乃至往到有頂，聖道現前得盡苦際。又雜修第四靜慮有五品差別：一下品修、二中品修、三上品修、四上勝品修、五上極品修。由此五品雜修第四靜慮故，如其次第生五淨居。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the person who streams upward? One who, having taken rebirth in every single level of the form realm one after another, finally enters the level of the highest form heaven, and there the uncontaminated noble path manifests, thereby reaching the very limit of suffering. There is, further, one who continues all the way to the very peak of existence itself, where the noble path manifests, thereby reaching the very limit of suffering.

Further, the mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna is of five distinct grades: mild cultivation, moderate cultivation, strong cultivation, superior-strong cultivation, and utmost-strong cultivation. It is through these five grades of mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna that rebirth, in corresponding order, occurs in each of the five pure abodes.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 distinguishes two distinct routes within this final variant, sorted by how deeply attached a practitioner remains to the taste of meditative experience itself. One who continues to savor the distinct flavor of each successive dhyāna along the way takes rebirth in every single level in careful sequence, from the very first form heaven all the way through to the highest, only reaching final nirvāṇa once that last and highest level has actually been reached. One who instead cultivates the fourth dhyāna without mixing in that same lingering attachment to lower flavors, bypassing the five pure abodes reserved for non-returners, presses onward level by level all the way to the very peak of existence itself before final nirvāṇa is reached there instead. And for those who do take the more gradual route through the five pure abodes specifically, T1606 explains that it is the very grade of mixed cultivation of the fourth dhyāna itself — mild through utmost-strong — that determines which of the five pure abodes, in matching order, becomes the destination of rebirth: the finer the mixed cultivation, the more refined the corresponding abode reached.
