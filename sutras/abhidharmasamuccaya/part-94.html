Five Stages of Bodhisattva Practice, and 建立補特伽羅 Complete

1. The Five Stages of Bodhisattva Practice

Chinese Source

云何修行差別？略有五種：一勝解行菩薩、二增上意樂行菩薩、三有相行菩薩、四無相行菩薩、五無功用行菩薩補特伽羅差別故。何等勝解行菩薩補特伽羅？謂住勝解行地中成就菩薩下中上忍。何等增上意樂行菩薩補特伽羅？謂十地中所有菩薩。何等有相行菩薩補特伽羅？謂住極喜、離垢、發光、焰慧、極難勝、現前地中所有菩薩。何等無相行菩薩補特伽羅？謂住遠行地中所有菩薩。何等無功用行菩薩補特伽羅？謂住不動、善慧、法雲地中所有菩薩。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the distinction of practice? In brief, there are five: the bodhisattva of resolute conviction, the bodhisattva of superior aspiration, the bodhisattva of practice still marked by signs, the bodhisattva of practice free of signs, and the bodhisattva of effortless activity.

What is the bodhisattva of resolute conviction? One who, standing on the ground of resolute conviction, has attained the mild, moderate, and strong forms of bodhisattva forbearance. What is the bodhisattva of superior aspiration? Any bodhisattva standing on any of the ten grounds. What is the bodhisattva of practice still marked by signs? Any bodhisattva standing on the grounds of Utter Joy, Freedom from Defilement, Emitting Light, Blazing Wisdom, Extremely Difficult to Conquer, or Face to Face. What is the bodhisattva of practice free of signs? Any bodhisattva standing on the ground of Traveling Far. What is the bodhisattva of effortless activity? Any bodhisattva standing on the grounds of the Immovable, Excellent Wisdom, or the Cloud of Dharma.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 clarifies how these five stages actually overlap with, rather than simply repeat, the ten bodhisattva grounds. The bodhisattva of resolute conviction occupies the ground of resolute conviction that precedes the ten grounds proper — abiding within the bodhisattva lineage from the very first arousal of the great vow toward awakening, yet not having entered even the first ground, and so not yet possessing any genuine supramundane realization gained through direct inner experience. The bodhisattva of superior aspiration is simply any bodhisattva who has entered any of the ten grounds at all, now possessing that genuine supramundane realization the first stage still lacks. The remaining three stages then subdivide this same span of the ten grounds by an entirely different criterion — how effortful the practice still is. Bodhisattvas on the first six grounds still practice with signs mixed in, since even genuinely joyful practice at this stage remains interrupted by the arising of conceptual characteristics. The bodhisattva on the seventh ground, Traveling Far, has advanced to the point of being able, through deliberate effort exercised at will, to keep such signs from arising at all — hence "free of signs," though still requiring some degree of exertion to achieve this. And bodhisattvas on the eighth, ninth, and tenth grounds have gone one step further still, having attained a thoroughly matured, non-discriminating wisdom that no longer requires any exertion whatsoever to remain free of such signs — activity now entirely effortless.

2. Sudden Liberation

Chinese Source

復次如說預流果補特伽羅，此有二種：一漸出離、二頓出離。漸出離者，如前廣說。頓出離者，謂入諦現觀已，依止未至定發出世間道，頓斷三界一切煩惱。品品別斷唯立二果，謂預流果、阿羅漢果。如是補特伽羅多於現法或臨終時善辨聖旨；設不能辨，由願力故，即以願力還生欲界，出無佛世成獨勝果。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, with respect to the person of the fruit of stream-entry as already described, there are, in fact, two kinds: gradual liberation and sudden liberation. Gradual liberation is exactly as extensively described above. Sudden liberation is this: having entered direct realization of the truths, and relying on the not-yet-attained level, one gives rise to the supramundane path and thereby severs, all at once, every affliction throughout the three realms.

Though this severing proceeds grade by grade, only two fruits are established for it: the fruit of stream-entry and the fruit of arhatship. Such a person, in most cases, either in this very life or at the moment of death, is able to properly bring the noble task to completion. Should this not prove possible, then through the power of a prior vow, that same power causes rebirth back into the desire realm, where, appearing in an age without a buddha, this person attains the solitary superior fruit instead.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains what makes this sudden path genuinely distinct from the gradual path already examined at such length across Parts 87 through 89: "grade by grade" here means that the practitioner severs every one of the nine graded levels of affliction across all three realms — from the very coarsest down to the very subtlest — in a single unbroken sweep, in the same total, undifferentiated manner the path of seeing itself already severs affliction, rather than through the mundane path's own gradual, level-by-level suppression realm by realm. T1606 cites the Sūtra of the Fingertip in support: gathering everything belonging to form and the remaining aggregates — past, future, or present, far or near, all of it without exception — into a single undivided mass, and then contemplating that entire mass at once as impermanent and as suffering, admits no intermediate stopping points. Because this single sweeping contemplation leaves no natural place to pause partway through, only the very first fruit and the very last fruit — stream-entry and arhatship — can actually be established for it; the second and third fruits, once-returning and non-returning, mark specific partial degrees of severing that this undivided contemplation simply never passes through as distinct, nameable stages. It is for this same reason, T1606 notes, that a scripture elsewhere places arhatship immediately after stream-entry with nothing named in between, describing exactly this sudden path. And should death intervene before the task can be brought to completion in this present life, the very same vow that first set this whole course in motion is what draws such a person back into the desire realm to complete it — appearing, like the pratyekabuddha already examined in Part 84, in an age without a living buddha, and there swiftly bringing the interrupted task to its final conclusion.

Part 94 Complete — 建立補特伽羅 in Full

With the five stages of bodhisattva practice and the treatise's own account of sudden liberation now examined, 建立補特伽羅, the classification of persons — all seven distinctions, and the extensive twenty-seven-fold catalogue of fruits they together generate — is complete.
