The Many Names of Nirvāṇa

The truth of cessation closes with its own longest stretch: roughly two dozen further names for nirvāṇa, each given its own precise etymological justification in turn — a sustained demonstration that every name scripture uses for liberation, however poetic it may sound, actually tracks something exact and specific about what cessation is.

1. Eight Core Names

Chinese Source

何等差別故？謂無餘、永斷、永出、永吐盡、離欲、滅、寂靜、沒等。何故名無餘永斷？由餘句故。何故名永出？永出諸纏故。何故名永吐？永吐隨眠故。何故名盡？見道對治得離繫故。何故名離欲？修道對治得離繫故。何故名滅？當來彼果苦不生故。何故名寂靜？於現法中彼果心苦永不行故。何故名沒？餘所有事永滅沒故。

Ru-Yi Translation

What are cessation's distinct varieties? Permanent severance without remainder, permanent expulsion, permanent expectoration, exhaustion, freedom from desire, extinction, stillness, and disappearance.

Why is it called permanent severance without remainder? Why permanent expulsion? Because every entanglement is permanently expelled. Why permanent expectoration? Because every latent tendency is permanently expectorated. Why exhaustion? Because release from bondage is attained through the path of seeing's own antidote. Why freedom from desire? Because release from bondage is attained through the path of cultivation's own antidote. Why extinction? Because that future fruit — suffering — no longer arises. Why stillness? Because, within this very life, the mental suffering that fruit produces no longer operates at all. Why disappearance? Because everything else that remained has now permanently, utterly vanished.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains the logic linking exhaustion and freedom-from-desire specifically to the two stages of the path already established in this treatise: "exhaustion" names release attained through the path of seeing's own antidote, since it is the very act of seeing directly that thins the mass of remaining affliction down almost to nothing; "freedom from desire" names release attained through the path of cultivation's own antidote, since cultivation is what gradually, level by level, removes whatever desire for each successive realm still remains after the initial breakthrough of seeing. Together, these two stages account for cessation's entire release from bondage — which is why the treatise can then say "because of this joint release from bondage, future suffering no longer arises," moving directly from these two technical terms into "extinction" itself as the natural next name in the sequence.

2. Twenty-One Further Names

Chinese Source

何故此滅復名無為？離三相故。何故此滅復名難見？超過肉眼、天眼境故。何故此滅復名不轉？永離諸趣差別轉故。何故此滅名不卑屈？離三愛故。何故此滅復名甘露？離蘊魔故。何故此滅復名無漏？永離一切煩惱魔故。何故此滅復名舍宅？無罪喜樂所依事故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why is this cessation further called the unconditioned? Because it is free of the three conditioned marks. Why further called hard to see? Because it surpasses the range of both the physical eye and the divine eye. Why further called unmoving? Because it is permanently free of turning through the various destinies. Why called unbowed? Because it is free of the three cravings. Why further called ambrosia? Because it is free of the māra of the aggregates. Why further called uncontaminated? Because it is permanently free of the māra of affliction. Why further called a dwelling place? Because it is the very basis of blameless joy.

何故此滅復名洲渚？三界隔絕故。何故此滅復名弘濟？能遮一切大苦災橫故。何故此滅復名歸依？無有虛妄意樂方便所依處故。何故此滅名勝歸趣？能為歸趣一切最勝聖性所依處故。何故此滅復名不死？永離生故。何故此滅名無熱惱？永離一切煩惱熱故、永離一切求不得苦大熱惱故。何故此滅名無熾然？永離一切愁歎憂苦諸惱亂故。

Why further called an isle? Because it stands apart from all three realms. Why further called great rescue? Because it wards off every great calamity of suffering. Why further called refuge? Because it is free of any basis for delusive intention or expedient. Why called the supreme resort? Because it is the very basis one resorts to for the most supreme noble nature. Why further called deathless? Because it is permanently free of birth. Why called free of feverish distress? Because it is permanently free of every affliction's own fever, and permanently free of the great fever brought on by not obtaining what is sought. Why called free of conflagration? Because it is permanently free of every sorrow, lament, grief, and disturbance.

何故此滅復名安隱？離怖畏住所依處故。何故此滅復名清涼？諸利益事所依處故。何故此滅復名樂事？第一義樂事故。何故此滅名趣吉祥？為證得彼易修方便所依處故。何故此滅復名無病？永離一切障礙病故。何故此滅復名不動？永離一切散動故。何故此滅復名涅槃？無相寂滅大安樂住所依處故。

Why further called tranquil security? Because it is the very basis of an abiding free of dread. Why further called cool relief? Because it is the very basis of every beneficial quality. Why further called a state of bliss? Because it is bliss in the ultimate sense. Why called the auspicious resort? Because it is the very basis for the easy means of practice by which it is actually realized. Why further called free of illness? Because it is permanently free of every obstructing malady. Why further called unmoved? Because it is permanently free of every kind of agitation. Why further called nirvāṇa? Because it is the very basis of a signless, utterly still, and greatly blissful abiding.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 elaborates a few of the more evocative names with extra precision. "Ambrosia" (甘露) marks freedom specifically from the "māra of the aggregates" — the way one's own five grasped aggregates function as a constant, built-in vulnerability to death, since nirvāṇa permanently sets this vulnerability aside. "The isle" describes cessation as a promontory rising clear above the ocean of birth and death, standing apart from all three realms the way solid ground stands apart from open water. "The supreme resort" is distinguished specifically as what an arhat's own realization actually relies on and points toward — the object that direct spiritual attainment itself takes as its own genuine reference point. And "unmoved" names freedom specifically from the endless agitation of conceptual proliferation about sense objects, the same "戲論" (needless elaboration) already flagged as cessation's core defining absence back at the start of this section on profundity.

3. Cessation's Opposition to Suffering's Own Marks

Chinese Source

何故此滅復名無生？離續生故。何故此滅復名無起？永離此後漸生起故。何故此滅復名無造？永離前際諸業煩惱勢力所引故。何故此滅復名無作？不作現在諸業煩惱所依處故。何故此滅復名不生？永離未來相續生故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why further called unborn? Because it is free of continued rebirth. Why further called unarisen? Because it is permanently free of any further, gradual arising after this. Why further called uncreated? Because it is permanently free of whatever prior karmic and afflictive force once drew it forth. Why further called unmade? Because it does not serve as the basis for any present karma or affliction. Why further called non-arising? Because it is permanently free of continued arising into the future.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explicitly frames this final cluster of five names as a deliberate mirror-image of the truth of suffering's own defining marks, already established across Parts 26–31: suffering's own mark is that it continues arising, life after life, within each of countless sentient beings — cessation's "unborn" directly negates precisely this. Suffering's mark is that a being's own aggregates, once born, gradually reach their own full completion — "unarisen" negates this gradual completing. Suffering's mark is that present suffering is itself produced by prior karma and affliction's own force — "uncreated" negates this backward-looking dependency. And present suffering itself, in turn, serves as the basis for further karma and affliction yet to come — "unmade" negates this forward-looking role. Reading this entire long list of names as a systematic, point-by-point negation of the truth of suffering, rather than as merely accumulated poetic praise, is exactly the interpretive key T1606 offers for making sense of why the treatise felt the need to give nirvāṇa so many distinct names at all: each one targets a specific, corresponding feature of the very suffering it brings to an end.

4. Cessation's Four Closing Marks

Chinese Source

如是滅諦總有四種行相差別，謂滅相、靜相、妙相、離相。云何滅相？煩惱離繫故。云何靜相？苦離繫故。云何妙相？樂靜事故。云何離相？常利益事故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The truth of cessation, altogether, has four distinct aspects: the mark of cessation, the mark of stillness, the mark of excellence, and the mark of release. What is the mark of cessation? Release from bondage to affliction. What is the mark of stillness? Release from bondage to suffering. What is the mark of excellence? Being a matter of genuine bliss and purity. What is the mark of release? Being a matter of constant, lasting benefit.

Reading note — after T1606

These four closing marks give the truth of cessation the same tightly compressed final summary already given for the truth of origination in Part 49: cessation, considered as freedom from affliction, is the mark of cessation proper; considered as freedom from the suffering that affliction produces, it is the mark of stillness; considered as its own positive, blissful character rather than merely an absence, it is the mark of excellence; and considered as a permanent, never-lost condition rather than a temporary reprieve, it is the mark of release. Taken together with the roughly two dozen names just given, these four marks close the truth of cessation on the same note that has run through this entire section: nirvāṇa is not a single simple idea but something rich and multi-faceted enough to require dozens of different, precisely justified names — and yet, for all that richness, still reducible, at its very core, to these four essential aspects.

The Truth of Cessation Complete

This closes the truth of cessation in full — its twelve angles, its two dozen names, and its four essential marks. Part 52 opens the treatise's fourth and final noble truth: the truth of the path, beginning with the five stages of the path and the treatise's own account of the resource stage that comes before any of them.
