Subsumption, Association, and Possession

Three short chapters close 本事分, the Foundations half of the treatise: eleven distinct ways the aggregates, elements, and sense fields subsume one another; six kinds of association explaining how dharmas actually go together; and three kinds of possession — a dormant seed, a mastered capacity, and something actually manifesting right now.

1. Eleven Kinds of Subsumption

Chinese Source

云何攝？略說攝有十一種，謂相攝、界攝、種類攝、分位攝、伴攝、方攝、時攝、一分攝、具分攝、更互攝、勝義攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is subsumption? Briefly, subsumption is of eleven kinds: subsumption by own-mark, by seed, by kind, by occasion, by companionship, by direction, by time, as a portion, as the whole, mutually, and ultimately.

何等相攝？謂蘊界處一一自相，即體自攝。何等界攝？謂蘊界處所有種子阿賴耶識能攝彼界。何等種類攝？謂蘊界處其相雖異，蘊義界義處義等故展轉相攝。何等分位攝？謂樂位蘊界處即自相攝。苦位不苦不樂位亦爾，分位等故。何等伴攝？謂色蘊與餘蘊互為伴故即攝助伴，餘蘊界處亦爾。

What is subsumption by own-mark? Each aggregate, element, and sense field subsumes only itself, by its own particular characteristic. What is subsumption by seed? The ālayavijñāna's own seeds subsume the aggregates, elements, and sense fields that will arise from them. What is subsumption by kind? Though the aggregates, elements, and sense fields each have distinct characteristics, they mutually subsume one another by sharing the general sense of "aggregate," "element," and "sense field" respectively. What is subsumption by occasion? The aggregates, elements, and sense fields present in a pleasant state subsume only themselves; likewise for a painful or neutral state, since occasion matches occasion. What is subsumption by companionship? The aggregate of form, as a companion to the other aggregates, subsumes them as its companions, and likewise for every other aggregate, element, and sense field.

何等方攝？謂依東方諸蘊界處還自相攝，餘方蘊界處亦爾。何等時攝？謂過去世諸蘊界處還自相攝，未來現在諸蘊界處亦爾。何等一分攝？謂所有法蘊界處所攝但攝一分非餘，應知一分攝。何等具分攝？謂所有法蘊界處所攝能攝全分，應知具分攝。

What is subsumption by direction? The aggregates, elements, and sense fields present in the east subsume only themselves, and likewise for every other direction. What is subsumption by time? The aggregates, elements, and sense fields present in the past subsume only themselves, and likewise for the future and present. What is subsumption as a portion? Whatever dharma is subsumed under the aggregates, elements, and sense fields, but only partially, not entirely — this should be understood as subsumption as a portion. What is subsumption as the whole? Whatever dharma is subsumed under the aggregates, elements, and sense fields entirely — this should be understood as subsumption as the whole.

何等更互攝？謂色蘊攝幾界幾處？十全一少分。受蘊攝幾界幾處？一少分。如受蘊，想行蘊亦爾。識蘊攝幾界幾處？七界一處。眼界攝幾蘊幾處？色蘊少分一處全。如眼界，耳鼻舌身色聲香味觸界亦爾。意界攝幾蘊幾處？一蘊一處。法界攝幾蘊幾處？三蘊全色蘊少分一處全。眼識界攝幾蘊幾處？識蘊意處少分。如眼識，耳鼻舌身意識界亦爾。眼處攝幾蘊幾界？色蘊少分一界全。如眼處，耳鼻舌身色聲香味觸處亦爾。意處攝幾蘊幾界？一蘊七界。法處攝幾蘊幾界？三蘊全一少分一界全。

What is mutual subsumption? How many elements and sense fields does the aggregate of form subsume? Ten entire, and a portion of one further. How many does the aggregate of feeling subsume? A portion of one. So too for the aggregates of perception and formations. How many does the aggregate of consciousness subsume? Seven elements and one sense field entire.

How many aggregates and sense fields does the eye element subsume? A portion of the aggregate of form, and one sense field entire. So too for the ear, nose, tongue, body, form, sound, smell, taste, and touch elements. How many does the mental-faculty element subsume? One aggregate and one sense field. How many does the dharma element subsume? Three aggregates entire, a portion of the aggregate of form, and one sense field entire. How many does the eye-consciousness element subsume? A portion of the aggregate of consciousness and of the mental-faculty sense field. So too for the ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mental-consciousness elements.

How many aggregates and elements does the eye sense field subsume? A portion of the aggregate of form, and one element entire. So too for the ear, nose, tongue, body, form, sound, smell, taste, and touch sense fields. How many does the mental-faculty sense field subsume? One aggregate and seven elements. How many does the dharma sense field subsume? Three aggregates entire, a portion of one further aggregate, and one element entire.

如是諸餘法以蘊界處名說，及餘非蘊界處名說，如實有假有、世俗有、勝義有，所知、所識、所達，有色無色、有見無見，如是等如前所顯，隨其所應與蘊界處更互相攝盡當知。何等勝義攝？謂蘊界處真如所攝。於攝善巧得何勝利？得於所緣略集勝利。隨彼彼境略聚其心，如是如是善根增勝。

Every other dharma spoken of either by the names "aggregate," "element," and "sense field," or by other names entirely — real or nominal existence, conventional or ultimate truth, the knowable, the discriminated, the penetrated, having form or formless, visible or invisible, and every such category already given above — should likewise be understood to mutually subsume, and be subsumed by, the aggregates, elements, and sense fields, applied as is fitting.

What is ultimate subsumption? Suchness alone subsumes the aggregates, elements, and sense fields. What benefit does skill in subsumption bring? The benefit of gathering one's objects into a manageable compass — the mind, drawn together around whatever object is at hand in this condensed way, allows one's wholesome roots to grow progressively stronger.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 closes this chapter by offering a second, simpler lens on the same eleven-fold scheme: subsumption, as ordinary people already use the word, works six ways — subsumption by location (as Jambudvīpa is said to "contain" its human population), by holding-together (as a rope holds a bundle of firewood), by shared undertaking (as people who cooperate on a common task are said to "cover for" one another), by ownership (as a master is said to "keep" his servants), by containment against dispersal (as a jar holds water), and by condensed gathering (as the ocean gathers every river that flows into it). T1606 explicitly identifies this final sense — condensed gathering — as what the entire eleven-fold technical scheme has really been elaborating all along: subsumption, at bottom, is simply the mind's own capacity to gather a scattered field of experience into a more manageable compass, which is exactly the "benefit" the treatise names at the chapter's close.

2. Six Kinds of Association

Chinese Source

云何相應？略說相應有六種，謂不相離相應、和合相應、聚集相應、俱有相應、作事相應、同行相應。何等不相離相應？謂一切有方分色與極微處互不相離。何等和合相應？謂極微已上，一切有方分色更互和合。何等聚集相應？謂方分聚色展轉集會。何等俱有相應？謂一身中諸蘊界處，俱時流轉同生住滅。何等作事相應？謂於一所作事展轉相攝，如二苾芻隨一所作更互相應。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is association? Briefly, association is of six kinds: inseparable association, combined association, aggregated association, co-existent association, cooperative association, and concurrent association.

What is inseparable association? Every spatially-extended form and the atomic locations that compose it, mutually inseparable from one another. What is combined association? Every spatially-extended form, from the level of the atom upward, mutually combining together. What is aggregated association? Spatially-extended, massed form successively gathering together. What is co-existent association? The aggregates, elements, and sense fields within a single body, flowing together at once, arising, abiding, and ceasing in unison. What is cooperative association? Mutual reinforcement toward a single shared undertaking, as two monastics mutually support each other in whatever task either one is doing.

何等同行相應？謂心心所於一所緣展轉同行。此同行相應復有多義，謂他性相應非己性、不相違相應非相違、同時相應非異時、同分界地相應非異分界地。復有一切遍行同行相應。謂受想思觸作意識。復有染污遍行同行相應，謂於染污意四種煩惱。復有非一切時同行相應，謂依止心，或時起信等善法、或時起貪等煩惱隨煩惱法。復有分位同行相應，謂與樂受諸相應法、與苦受不苦不樂受諸相應法。

What is concurrent association? Mind and its mental factors mutually operating together toward a single object. Concurrent association carries several further senses: association across distinct kinds, not within one's own single kind — as mind is not "associated" with another mind, nor one feeling with another feeling. Association among what does not conflict, not among what does — as craving is never associated with hatred, nor wholesome with unwholesome. Association at the same moment, not across different moments. Association within a shared realm and level, not across differing ones.

There is, further, universal concurrent association: feeling, perception, volition, contact, attention, and consciousness, always associated together in every state. There is defiled universal concurrent association: the four afflictions constantly bound to the defiled manas. There is concurrent association not present at every moment: mind, sometimes accompanied by faith and other wholesome factors, sometimes by craving and other afflictions. There is concurrent association tied to a specific occasion: whatever accompanies pleasant feeling, and whatever accompanies painful or neutral feeling, respectively.

復有無間同行相應，謂在有心位。復有有間同行相應，謂無心定所間。復有外門同行相應，謂多分欲界繫心心所。復有內門同行相應，謂諸定地所有心心所。復有曾習同行相應，謂諸異生所有心心所，及有學者一分心心所。復有未曾習同行相應，謂出世間諸心心所，及初後時出世後所得諸心心所。於相應善巧得何勝利？能善了悟唯依止心有受想等染淨諸法相應不相應義。由此了悟，即能捨離計我能受能想能思能念染淨執著，又能善巧速入無我。

There is, further, unbroken concurrent association: present whenever mind is active at all. There is interrupted concurrent association: broken specifically by the mindless attainments. There is outward-facing concurrent association: mostly the mind and mental factors bound to the desire realm. There is inward-facing concurrent association: the mind and mental factors of every meditative-absorption realm. There is habituated concurrent association: an ordinary being's own mind and mental factors, and a portion of a trainee's. And there is unhabituated concurrent association: every supramundane mind and mental factor, together with whatever mind and mental factors arise, both at the very first moment and afterward, on the basis of that same supramundane attainment once it has occurred.

What benefit does skill in association bring? Genuine insight that feeling, perception, and the rest — whether defiled or pure — are associated or not associated purely by depending on mind, nothing more. Through this insight, one is able to abandon the presumption of a self that supposedly does the feeling, perceiving, thinking, and remembering, in either its defiled or pure guise, and one is thereby skillfully able to swiftly enter selflessness.

Reading note — after T1606

The first five kinds of association — inseparable, combined, aggregated, co-existent, and cooperative — deal with physical form and ordinary cooperation; concurrent association, by far the richest of the six, is what actually explains how mind and mental factors go together at all, and the treatise's closing rule of thumb is worth isolating: a mind and a mental factor are genuinely associated only when they share the same object, the same moment, the same realm and level, and are not themselves mutually conflicting states. This chapter's stated purpose closes the loop with the last several chapters' recurring theme: seeing clearly that feeling, perception, thought, and memory are simply mental factors variously associated with mind — never evidence of some further self standing behind mind, doing the feeling and remembering — is itself already most of the way to realizing selflessness directly.

3. Three Kinds of Possession

Chinese Source

云何成就？謂成就相如前所說。此差別有三種，謂種子成就、自在成就、現行成就。何等種子成就？謂若生欲界，欲色無色界繫煩惱隨煩惱，由種子成就故成就；及生得善。若生色界，欲界繫煩惱隨煩惱，由種子成就故成就。亦名不成就，色無色界繫煩惱隨煩惱，由種子成就故成就；及生得善。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is possession? Possession's own mark is as already given above — the increase or decrease of a wholesome, unwholesome, or morally neutral dharma. This, distinguished further, is of three kinds: possession as a seed, possession as mastery, and possession as active manifestation.

What is possession as a seed? One born in the desire realm possesses, as a seed, the afflictions and secondary afflictions bound to all three realms, together with one's own innately acquired wholesome dispositions. One born in the form realm possesses, as a seed, the afflictions bound to the desire realm — which can, in this sense, equally be called "not possessed" — together with the afflictions bound to the form and formless realms as a genuine seed, and one's own innately acquired wholesome dispositions.

若生無色界，欲色界繫煩惱隨煩惱，由種子成就故成就。亦名不成就，無色界繫煩惱隨煩惱，由種子成就故成就；及生得善。若已得三界對治道，隨如是如是品類對治已生，如此如此品類由種子成就得不成就。隨如是如是品類對治未生，如此如此品類由種子成就故成就。

One born in the formless realm possesses, as a seed, the afflictions bound to the desire and form realms — likewise equally callable "not possessed" — together with the afflictions bound to the formless realm as a genuine seed, and one's own innately acquired wholesome dispositions. Once one has attained the path counteracting all three realms, whatever grade of affliction that path's antidote has already arisen for is, correspondingly, no longer possessed even as a seed; whatever grade its antidote has not yet arisen for remains possessed as a seed still.

何等自在成就？謂諸加行善法。若世出世靜慮、解脫、三摩地、三摩鉢底等功德及一分無記法，由自在成就故成就。何等現行成就？謂諸蘊界處法隨所現前，若善若不善若無記，彼由現行成就故成就。若已斷善者，所有善法由種子成就故成就。亦名不成就，若非涅槃法一闡底迦，究竟成就雜染諸法。由闕解脫因亦名阿顛底迦，以彼解脫得因必竟不成就故。

What is possession as mastery? Every wholesome dharma cultivated through deliberate effort — mundane and supramundane dhyāna, liberation, concentration, and meditative attainment, together with a portion of morally neutral dharmas — is possessed through mastery once mastered. What is possession as active manifestation? Whatever aggregate, element, or sense field, wholesome, unwholesome, or morally neutral, is presently, actively manifesting, is possessed through active manifestation.

One whose wholesome roots have already been severed still possesses their wholesome dharmas as a seed — which can equally be called "not possessed." One who lacks the very capacity for nirvāṇa at all — one of incorrigible disposition — permanently possesses only defiled dharmas, lacking the cause for liberation altogether, and is for this reason also called one without an endpoint, since the cause enabling liberation will, without exception, never be possessed at all in such a case.

於成就善巧得何勝利？能善了知諸法增減。知增減故，於世興衰離決定想，乃至能斷若愛若恚。

What benefit does skill in possession bring? Genuine knowledge of every dharma's increase and decrease. Through knowing this increase and decrease, one abandons any fixed notion about worldly rise and fall, and is thereby able to sever craving and hatred alike.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 offers a useful gloss on the treatise's own puzzling phrase "possessed, which can equally be called not possessed": once the antidote for a given grade of affliction has actually arisen, that affliction's seed, though technically still present as a mere latent capacity, has already had its "capability" permanently disabled — it can genuinely never again produce its effect, which is why the treatise is comfortable calling the very same seed both "possessed" (it is still there, in some minimal sense) and "not possessed" (it has lost every capacity to actually function) at once. The closing case — one of "incorrigible disposition," permanently lacking the cause for liberation — is a genuine, if rare, category this treatise takes seriously, though it is worth noting the wider Yogācāra tradition debates at length whether such a fixed, permanent incapacity for liberation is truly possible for any being, or whether every being ultimately carries the buddha-nature capable, in time, of overturning even this state.
