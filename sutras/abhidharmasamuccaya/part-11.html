The Hundred-Gate Analysis Begins

Having built the three categories from the ground up, the treatise now examines the very same aggregates, elements, and sense fields again — this time sorted through dozens of further paired and grouped categories, each probed with the same three questions: what is it, how much of everything falls under it, and why bother examining it at all. The answer to that third question is always a specific self-view the examination is designed to dismantle.

1. The Opening Verse, and the Recurring Method

Chinese Source

復次蘊界處廣分別云何？嗢柁南曰：

實有性等所知等，　色等漏等已生等，

過去世等諸緣等，　云何幾種為何義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Furthermore, how are the aggregates, elements, and sense fields broadly distinguished further? Here is a summary verse:

"Real existence and the rest, the knowable and the rest,

having form and the rest, contaminated and the rest, already arisen and the rest,

past and the rest, conditions and the rest —

what each is, how much falls under it, and for what purpose."

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains why every single category from here through the end of this chapter is probed with exactly the same three questions — what is it, how much falls under it, and for what purpose is it examined — rather than just stated outright: this fixed pattern is designed to sever two specific kinds of ignorance and one specific tendency at once. The "what is it" question severs ignorance about the category's own defining mark; the "how much" question severs ignorance about the actual scope of things it covers; and the "for what purpose" question, whose answer is always some particular way of clinging to a self, severs the tendency to superimpose something onto reality that was never there. This three-part method repeats, unchanged, across every remaining category in this chapter — dozens of times — so this note applies throughout, not just here.

2. Real Existence Versus Nominal Existence

Chinese Source

蘊界處中云何實有？幾是實有？為何義故觀實有耶？謂不待名言此餘根境，是實有義。一切皆是實有。為捨執著實有我故，觀察實有。云何假有？幾是假有？為何義故觀假有耶。謂待名言此餘根境，是假有義。一切皆是假有。為捨執著實有我故，觀察假有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among the aggregates, elements, and sense fields, what is real existence? How much of everything is really existent? For what purpose is real existence examined? A faculty or object that does not depend on language to be apprehended is what real existence means. Everything whatsoever is really existent, in this sense. Real existence is examined in order to abandon clinging to a self conceived as really existent.

What is nominal existence? How much of everything is nominally existent? For what purpose is nominal existence examined? A faculty or object that does depend on language to be apprehended is what nominal existence means. Everything whatsoever is nominally existent, in this sense. Nominal existence is examined in order to abandon that same clinging to a self conceived as really existent.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 clarifies with a contrast: apprehending blue, or a body's own particular touch-sensation, requires no prior grasp of the word "blue" or "touch" — the object is simply, directly cognized, which is what "not depending on language" means. Apprehending something like "a vase," by contrast, requires already knowing the concept "vase" and its associated features (color, shape, and the rest) before the composite object can be recognized as such at all — this dependence on prior conceptual and linguistic scaffolding is what "nominal existence" means. The striking result — that literally everything can be truthfully described both ways, depending on which aspect of it is being apprehended — is exactly the treatise's point: nothing is exclusively one or the other, which already undercuts any attempt to locate a fixed, "really existent" self anywhere within the aggregates.

3. Conventional Truth Versus Ultimate Truth

Chinese Source

云何世俗有？幾是世俗有？為何義故觀世俗有耶？謂雜染所緣，是世俗有義。一切皆是世俗有。為捨執著雜染相我故，觀察世俗有。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is conventional existence? Whatever can serve as an object of defilement is what conventional existence means. Everything whatsoever is conventionally existent, in this sense. Conventional existence is examined in order to abandon clinging to a self conceived as defiled in character.

云何勝義有？幾是勝義有？為何義故觀勝義有耶？謂清淨所緣，是勝義有義。一切皆是勝義有。為捨執著清淨相我故，觀察勝義有。

What is ultimate existence? Whatever can serve as an object of purification is what ultimate existence means. Everything whatsoever is ultimately existent, in this sense. Ultimate existence is examined in order to abandon clinging to a self conceived as pure in character.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 glosses "conventional existence" as anything capable of giving rise to defilement when taken as an object, and "ultimate existence" as anything capable of yielding purification — and then makes an important move: everything whatsoever counts as ultimately existent too, because no dharma is ever separate from suchness (真如), and suchness itself — the absence of self in all things — is genuinely, ultimately real. This is not a contradiction with calling the same things "conventionally existent" a moment earlier: the very same aggregate can be an object of defiled clinging when misapprehended, and an object of purifying insight when correctly apprehended for what it actually is — nothing here changes about the object itself, only how it is taken.

4. Three Kinds of Knowability

The next three categories examine what it means for something to be an object of knowledge at all, cut three different ways: the knowable in general, what is discriminated by consciousness specifically, and what is penetrated through the six supernormal powers.

Chinese Source

云何所知？幾是所知？為何義故觀所知耶。謂所知有五種：一色、二心、三心所有法、四心不相應行、五無為。若於是處雜染清淨，若所雜染及所清淨，若能雜染及能清淨，若於此分位若此清淨性，由依此故一切皆是所知。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is the knowable? How much of everything is knowable? For what purpose is the knowable examined? The knowable is of five kinds: form, mind, mental factors, formations dissociated from mind, and the unconditioned. Wherever defilement and purity occur; whatever becomes defiled or purified; whatever it is that defiles or purifies; whatever occasion this happens on; and whatever this state of purity itself is — on this basis, everything whatsoever is knowable.

又所知法者，謂勝解智所行故、道理智所行故、不散智所行故、內證智所行故、他性智所行故、下智所行故、上智所行故、厭患智所行故、不起智所行故、無生智所行故、智智所行故、究竟智所行故、大義智所行故，是所知義。一切皆是所知。為捨執著知者見者我故，觀察所知。

Furthermore, the knowable is the domain traversed by resolved-conviction knowledge, by knowledge through reasoning, by undistracted knowledge, by knowledge through direct realization, by knowledge of another's mind, by initial knowledge, by subsequent knowledge, by knowledge of revulsion, by knowledge of non-arising, by knowledge of no further birth, by knowledge of knowledge itself, by ultimate knowledge, and by knowledge of the great purpose. Everything whatsoever is knowable, in this sense too. The knowable is examined in order to abandon clinging to a self conceived as a knower, a seer.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 identifies the thirteen technical kinds of knowledge named here with the standard classical list: knowledge born of hearing, born of reflection, and born of mundane cultivation correspond to "resolved-conviction," "reasoning," and "undistracted" knowledge respectively; ultimate knowledge is direct realization; knowledge of another's mind is exactly what its name says; knowledge of dharmas is called "initial" because it is the very first to arise regarding any of the four noble truths; and inferential knowledge is called "subsequent" because it arises only after knowledge of dharmas has already occurred. The final six — revulsion, non-arising, no further birth, knowledge itself, the ultimate, and the great purpose — correspond to knowledge of suffering, its origin, its cessation, its path, the two knowledges that close out an arhat's realization (that afflictions are exhausted and will never arise again), and the distinctly Mahāyāna knowledge whose "great purpose" is benefiting both oneself and others together.

5. What Is Discriminated by Consciousness

Chinese Source

云何所識？幾是所識？為何義故觀所識耶？謂無分別故、有分別故、因故、轉故、相故、相所生故、能治所治故、微細差別故，是所識義。一切皆是所識。為捨執著能見者等我故，觀察所識。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is discriminated by consciousness? How much of everything is so discriminated? For what purpose is this examined? Being non-discriminating, being discriminating, being a cause, being an active occurrence, being a mark, being what is produced by a mark, being an antidote or what it counters, and being a subtle distinction — all of these are what "discriminated by consciousness" means. Everything whatsoever is discriminated by consciousness, in this sense. This is examined in order to abandon clinging to a self conceived as a seer and the rest.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 maps this list onto specific features of consciousness itself: "non-discriminating" names the five sense-consciousnesses, "discriminating" names mental consciousness; "cause" is the ālayavijñāna, "active occurrence" is every other consciousness that depends on it. Most striking is T1606's list of seven specific things about consciousness that are genuinely hard to grasp and therefore called "subtle": how an ever-present background awareness of one's surroundings operates without any deliberate discrimination at all; how a single dharma can present many different aspects at once; how several consciousnesses can arise together, each somehow discriminating its own distinct object without interference; how a mind bound by affliction can shift, in a single instant, into a mind free of that same affliction; how a habitual trace, left behind by a completed action, is neither simply identical with mind nor something existing entirely apart from it; how the karmic imprint from one lifetime can, at the exact moment consciousness dims toward death, redirect a continuum into an entirely different form of rebirth; and how an arhat's mind, having exhausted every trace of past conditioned activity, can still be said to continue functioning at all. Each of these, T1606 says, resists easy explanation — which is exactly why they count as "subtle distinctions" within what consciousness discriminates.

6. What Is Penetrated Through the Six Supernormal Powers

Chinese Source

云何所通達？幾是所通達？為何義故觀所通達耶？謂轉變故、隨聞故、入行故、來故、往故、出離故，是所通達義。一切皆是所通達。為捨執著有威德我故，觀察所通達。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is penetrated through supernormal power? How much of everything is so penetrated? For what purpose is this examined? Transformation, hearing at will, entering another's mental activity, coming, going, and liberation — these are what "penetrated through supernormal power" means. Everything whatsoever falls within this reach, in this sense. This is examined in order to abandon clinging to a self conceived as possessed of majestic power.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 matches each of the six terms to one of the six classical supernormal powers: transformation is the power of psychic mastery, freely manifesting or altering things at will; hearing at will is the divine ear, hearing sounds across every destiny; entering another's mental activity is knowledge of others' minds; coming is recollection of former lives, tracing what has already occurred; going is the divine eye, seeing what is yet to occur in future rebirths; and liberation is the power of exhausting contaminants, knowing the means to freedom from all three realms. T1606 closes with a small but important qualification on "everything whatsoever falls within this reach": strictly, only the last three of the six powers — mind-reading, past-life recollection, and the divine eye — actually range over every possible object without restriction; the reach of the remaining three is comparatively more limited.

Part 11 Complete

The hundred-gate analysis has opened with seven categories: real versus nominal existence, conventional versus ultimate truth, and the three kinds of knowability. Each dismantles a different way of imagining a self hiding somewhere within experience — as something really existent, as something defiled or pure in its own right, as a knower, a discriminator, or a possessor of power.
