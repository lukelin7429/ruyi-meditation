Purifying Conduct, Skillful Means, and Purifying Affliction

The remaining three kinds of objects are now examined: the object that purifies conduct, prescribed according to which of five dominant temperaments a practitioner actually has; the object of skillful means, spanning expertise in the aggregates, elements, sense fields, dependent origination, and what is and is not possible; and the object that purifies affliction, resting on suchness and the four noble truths themselves.

1. The Object That Purifies Conduct

Chinese Source

淨行所緣復有五種，謂多貪行者緣不淨境、多瞋行者緣修慈境、多癡行者緣眾緣性諸緣起境、憍慢行者緣界差別境、尋思行者緣入出息念境。

Ru-Yi Translation

The object that purifies conduct is, further, of five kinds: one of predominantly greedy conduct takes the impure as object; one of predominantly hateful conduct takes the cultivation of loving-kindness as object; one of predominantly deluded conduct takes the many-conditioned nature of dependent origination as object; one of predominantly arrogant conduct takes the differentiation of the elements as object; and one of predominantly discursive conduct takes recollection of the in-breath and out-breath as object.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 explains the single principle underlying all five prescriptions at once: each object is specifically chosen because contemplating it directly stills the very excess it is paired against. Contemplating the body's own impurity works directly against greed's own attraction to what appears desirable. Cultivating loving-kindness toward all beings works directly against hatred's own aversion. Reflecting on how every phenomenon arises through the interplay of manifold conditions works directly against delusion, since delusion is precisely a failure to see this very interdependence. Reflecting on the many distinct elements that make up one's own body and mind works directly against arrogance, since seeing this multiplicity undercuts the sense of a single, superior self there is anything to be arrogant about. And recollecting the breath, entering and leaving in a steady, countable rhythm, works directly against a mind given to excessive discursive wandering, since it gives that very restlessness a single, simple object to keep returning to.

2. The Object of Skillful Means

Chinese Source

善巧所緣亦有五種，謂蘊善巧、界善巧、處善巧、緣起善巧、處非處善巧。處非處善巧應云何觀？應如緣起善巧觀。處非處善巧、緣起善巧有何差別？若以諸法流潤諸法，令離無因，不平等因生故，是緣起善巧；因果相稱，攝受生故，是處非處善巧。

Ru-Yi Translation

The object of skillful means is likewise of five kinds: skill in the aggregates, skill in the elements, skill in the sense fields, skill in dependent origination, and skill in what is and is not possible.

How should skill in what is and is not possible be contemplated? It should be contemplated in the same way as skill in dependent origination. What, then, is the difference between skill in what is and is not possible and skill in dependent origination? Where one dharma's own flowing, nourishing influence upon another dharma is discerned, such that arising is understood to be free of any causeless origin and free of any origin from a mismatched, unequal cause — this is skill in dependent origination. Where cause and fruit are discerned as genuinely corresponding to one another, such that arising is understood to occur only through a cause that actually corresponds to and sustains it — this is skill in what is and is not possible.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 draws out this distinction with a concrete example: wholesome conduct produces a desirable ripened result, and unwholesome conduct produces an undesirable one — this specific correspondence, cause matched reliably to its own proper fruit, is exactly what skill in what is and is not possible discerns. Skill in dependent origination looks at the very same causal process from a different angle, focused not on which particular cause matches which particular fruit but on the more general point that arising itself is never uncaused and never produced by some arbitrary, mismatched cause — ruling out, for instance, the notion of a single controlling creator standing entirely outside the causal web. The two forms of skill thus examine the identical causal reality — nothing arises without appropriate conditions — but one focuses on the specific correspondence between a given cause and its given fruit, while the other focuses on the general principle that causation itself is neither absent nor arbitrary.

3. The Object That Purifies Affliction

Chinese Source

淨惑所緣者，謂下地麁性、上地靜性、真如及四聖諦，是名淨惑所緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

The object that purifies affliction is the coarseness of the lower level together with the stillness of the higher level, and suchness together with the four noble truths — this is what is called the object that purifies affliction.

Reading note — after T1606

T1606 distinguishes these two halves of the definition by which path each belongs to. Contemplating a lower meditative level's own coarseness against a higher level's comparative stillness is the mundane path's own approach to purifying affliction — it is by this contrast alone that the fetters binding one to the coarser level are actually suppressed, without yet uprooting them. Contemplating suchness and the four noble truths, by contrast, is the supramundane path's own approach — suchness stated in its most concise form, the four truths stated in their fuller, expanded form — and it is only this second approach that is capable of permanently eradicating the underlying tendencies toward affliction altogether, rather than merely holding them temporarily in check.
