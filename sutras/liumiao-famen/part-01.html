The Six Gates and Their First Six Uses

Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597), the founder of the Tiantai school, laid out three kinds of calming-and-contemplation: the gradual, set out in the Shi Chan Boluomi Cidi Famen (translated in full on this site); the perfect-and-sudden, in the Mohe Zhiguan; and the indeterminate, in this short manual. Its subject is six "wondrous gates" — counting, following, stilling, contemplating, returning, and purity — which begin with the breath and end in nirvāṇa, and which, the opening lines say, the Buddha himself used beneath the bodhi tree. A note under the title says Zhiyi set it out at Waguan Monastery in the capital (Jiankang). Rather than present the six gates once, the text turns them ten different ways. This first part covers the first six: the gates matched one by one to the various dhyānas; the gates arising in sequence, each with its stage of cultivating and its stage of realizing; the gates used as each practitioner finds convenient; the gates as remedies for the three obstacles; the gates containing one another; and the same gates practiced by ordinary people, non-Buddhists, hearers, solitary buddhas, and bodhisattvas, with very different results. The instructions are practical and precise, and the translation keeps their step-by-step form.

Title

Chinese Source

六妙法門

Ru-Yi Translation

The Six Wondrous Dharma-Gates

Chinese Source

天台大師於都下瓦官寺略出此法門

Ru-Yi Translation

The Great Master of Tiantai set out this dharma-gate in brief at Waguan Monastery in the capital.

The Meaning of "Six Wondrous Gates"

Chinese Source

「六妙門」者，蓋是內行之根本，三乘得道之要逕。故釋迦初詣道樹，跏趺坐草，內思安般：一、數，二、隨，三、止，四、觀，五、還，六、淨，因此萬行開發，降魔成道。當知佛為物軌，示跡若斯，三乘正士豈不同遊此路？

Ru-Yi Translation

The "six wondrous gates" are the root of inner practice and the essential path by which the three vehicles attain the Way. So when Śākyamuni first went to the tree of the Way, sat cross-legged on grass, and inwardly reflected on ānāpāna — first, counting; second, following; third, stilling; fourth, contemplating; fifth, returning; sixth, purity — the myriad practices opened out from this, and he subdued Māra and attained the Way. One should know that the Buddha, as the model for beings, displayed his traces in this way; how could the true practitioners of the three vehicles not travel this same road?

Chinese Source

所言「六」者，即是數法，約數明禪，故言六也。如佛或約一數辯禪，所謂一行三昧；或約二數，謂一、止，二、觀；或約三數，謂三三昧；或約四數，所謂四禪；或約五數，謂五門禪；或約六數，謂六妙門；或約七數，謂七依定；或約八數，謂八背捨；或約九數，謂九次第定；或約十數，謂十禪支。如是等，乃至百千萬億阿僧祇不可說諸三昧門，悉是約數說諸禪也。雖數有多少，窮其法相，莫不悉相收攝，以眾生機悟不同故，有增減之數分別利物。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is called "six" is a numerical dharma: dhyāna is explained in terms of number, so it says "six." Thus the Buddha sometimes discussed dhyāna in terms of the number one, namely the single-practice samādhi; sometimes in terms of two, namely first stilling and second contemplating; sometimes in terms of three, namely the three samādhis; sometimes in terms of four, namely the four dhyānas; sometimes in terms of five, namely the five-gate dhyāna; sometimes in terms of six, namely the six wondrous gates; sometimes in terms of seven, namely the seven supporting concentrations; sometimes in terms of eight, namely the eight forms of detachment; sometimes in terms of nine, namely the nine sequential concentrations; sometimes in terms of ten, namely the ten factors of dhyāna. In this way, up to hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of asaṃkhyeyas of inexpressible gates of samādhi — all are dhyānas taught in terms of number. Though the numbers are larger or smaller, when their dharma-marks are traced to the end, there is none that does not contain the others. Because the capacities and understanding of beings differ, numbers are increased or decreased, and distinctions are made, to benefit beings.

Chinese Source

今言「六」者，即是約數法而標章也。「妙」者，其意乃多，若論正意，即是滅諦涅槃，故滅四行中，言滅、止、妙、離。涅槃非斷非常，有而難契，無而易得，故言「妙」也。六法能通，故名為「門」。門雖有六，會妙不殊，故經言：「泥洹真法寶，眾生從種種門入。」此則通釋六妙門之大意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Here, "six" marks the heading in terms of a numerical dharma. "Wondrous" has many meanings, but its main meaning is the truth of cessation, nirvāṇa; that is why, among the four aspects of cessation, it is said: cessation, stillness, the wondrous, and release. Nirvāṇa is neither annihilation nor permanence; it exists yet is hard to accord with; it is nonexistent yet easy to attain. That is why it is called "wondrous." These six dharmas can give passage, so they are called "gates." Though the gates are six, the wondrous they meet is not different. So a sūtra says: "Nirvāṇa, the true Dharma-jewel — beings enter it through gates of many kinds." This is a general explanation of the main idea of the six wondrous gates.

Chinese Source

六妙門大意有十：第一、歷別對諸禪六妙門，第二、次第相生六妙門，第三、隨便宜六妙門，第四、隨對治六妙門，第五、相攝六妙門，第六、通別六妙門，第七、旋轉六妙門，第八、觀心六妙門，第九、圓觀六妙門，第十、證相六妙門。

Ru-Yi Translation

The main idea of the six wondrous gates has ten aspects: first, the six wondrous gates matched one by one to the various dhyānas; second, the six wondrous gates arising one from another in sequence; third, the six wondrous gates used as is convenient; fourth, the six wondrous gates used as remedies; fifth, the six wondrous gates containing one another; sixth, the six wondrous gates as shared and distinct; seventh, the six wondrous gates revolving; eighth, the six wondrous gates of contemplating the mind; ninth, the six wondrous gates of perfect contemplation; tenth, the six wondrous gates in their marks of realization.

1. The Six Gates Matched One by One to the Various Dhyānas

Chinese Source

釋「第一、歷別對諸禪定明六妙門」，即為六意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining "First, clarifying the six wondrous gates by matching them one by one to the various dhyānas and concentrations": this has six points.

Chinese Source

一者依「數」為妙門。行者因數息故，即能出生四禪、四無量心、四無色定，若於最後非非想定，能覺知非是涅槃，是人必定得三乘道。何以故？此定陰、界、入和合故有，虛誑不實，雖無麁煩惱，而亦成就十種細煩惱。知已破折，不住不著，心得解脫，即證三乘涅槃故。此義如須跋陀羅，佛教斷非非想處惑，即便獲得阿羅漢果。「數」為妙門，意在於此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, counting is taken as a wondrous gate. Because the practitioner counts the breath, he or she is able to bring forth the four dhyānas, the four immeasurable minds, and the four formless concentrations. If, at the last of these, the concentration of neither perception nor non-perception, one can recognize that it is not nirvāṇa, this person will certainly attain the path of the three vehicles. Why? Because this concentration exists through the coming together of the aggregates, elements, and sense-bases; it is false and unreal, and although it has no coarse afflictions, it still carries ten kinds of subtle affliction. Knowing this, one breaks it down, does not abide in it or cling to it, the mind attains liberation, and one realizes the nirvāṇa of the three vehicles. This is like Subhadra: the Buddha taught him to cut off the delusion of the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception, and he at once attained the fruit of arhatship. Counting as a wondrous gate — its meaning lies here.

Chinese Source

二者「隨」為妙門者，行者因隨息故，即能出生十六特勝，所謂：一、知息入，二、知息出，三、知息長短，四、知息遍身，五、除諸身行，六、心受喜，七、心受樂，八、受諸心行，九、心作喜，十、心作攝，十一、心作解脫，十二、觀無常，十三、觀出散，十四、觀離欲，十五、觀滅，十六、觀棄捨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, following as a wondrous gate. Because the practitioner follows the breath, he or she is able to bring forth the sixteen special excellences, namely: first, knowing the breath entering; second, knowing the breath leaving; third, knowing the breath as long or short; fourth, knowing the breath pervading the body; fifth, removing all bodily formations; sixth, the mind experiencing joy; seventh, the mind experiencing happiness; eighth, experiencing all mental formations; ninth, the mind making joy; tenth, the mind making restraint; eleventh, the mind making liberation; twelfth, contemplating impermanence; thirteenth, contemplating dispersal; fourteenth, contemplating freedom from desire; fifteenth, contemplating cessation; sixteenth, contemplating relinquishment.

Chinese Source

云何觀棄捨？此觀破非想處惑。所以者何？凡夫修非想時，觀有想處如癕、如瘡，觀無想處如癡也。第一妙定，名曰「非想」。作是念已，即棄捨有想、無想，名「非有想非無想」，故知非想即是兩捨之義。今佛弟子觀行破折，義如前說，是故深觀棄捨，不著非想，能得涅槃。「隨」為妙門，意在此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is contemplating relinquishment? This contemplation breaks the delusion of the sphere of neither perception [nor non-perception]. Why? When ordinary people cultivate [the sphere of] neither perception, they contemplate the sphere with perception as a boil or a sore, and the sphere without perception as stupor. "The foremost wondrous concentration is called 'neither perception.'" Having thought this, they relinquish both perception and non-perception, and it is called "neither perception nor non-perception." So we know that "neither perception" means relinquishing both. Now the Buddha's disciples break it down through contemplative practice, in the sense explained above; therefore they contemplate relinquishment deeply, do not cling to [the sphere of] neither perception, and are able to attain nirvāṇa. Following as a wondrous gate — its meaning lies here.

Chinese Source

三者「止」為妙門者，行者因止心故，即便次第發五輪禪：一者地輪三昧，即未到地；二者水輪三昧，即是種種諸禪定善根發也；三者虛空輪三昧，即五方便人覺因緣無性如虛空；四者金沙輪三昧，即是見思解脫，無著正惠如金沙也；五者金剛輪三昧，即是第九無礙道，能斷三界結使，永盡無餘，證盡智、無生智，入涅槃。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, stilling as a wondrous gate. Because the practitioner stills the mind, the five-wheel dhyāna arises in sequence: first, the earth-wheel samādhi, which is the not-yet-arrived [concentration]; second, the water-wheel samādhi, which is the arising of the wholesome roots of the various dhyānas and concentrations; third, the space-wheel samādhi, in which people of the five expedient stages awaken to causes and conditions as without nature, like space; fourth, the golden-sand-wheel samādhi, which is liberation from views and habituated thought, unattached correct wisdom like golden sand; fifth, the vajra-wheel samādhi, which is the ninth unobstructed path, able to cut off the fetters of the three realms forever without remainder, realizing the knowledge of exhaustion and the knowledge of non-arising, and entering nirvāṇa.

Chinese Source

「止」為妙門，意在此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Stilling as a wondrous gate — its meaning lies here.

Chinese Source

四者「觀」為妙門者，行者因修觀故，即能出生九想、八念、十想、八背捨、八勝處、十一切處、九次第定、師子奮迅三昧、超越三昧、練禪、十四變化心、三明、六通及八解脫，得滅受想，即入涅槃。「觀」為妙門，意在此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, contemplating as a wondrous gate. Because the practitioner cultivates contemplation, he or she is able to bring forth the nine contemplations, the eight recollections, the ten perceptions, the eight forms of detachment, the eight stations of mastery, the ten totalities, the nine sequential concentrations, the lion's-frolic samādhi, the samādhi of transcendence, refining dhyāna, the fourteen transforming minds, the three clear knowledges, the six supernormal powers, and the eight liberations; attaining the cessation of sensation and perception, one enters nirvāṇa. Contemplating as a wondrous gate — its meaning lies here.

Chinese Source

五者「還」為妙門者，行者若用惠行，善巧破折，反本還源，是時即便出生空、無想、無作、三十七品、四諦、十二因緣、中道正觀，因此得入涅槃。「還」為妙門，意在此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, returning as a wondrous gate. If the practitioner uses the practice of wisdom, skillfully breaking things down, turning back to the root and returning to the source, then at that time there arise emptiness, signlessness, wishlessness, the thirty-seven factors, the Four Noble Truths, the twelve links of dependent origination, and the correct contemplation of the middle way, and through these one enters nirvāṇa. Returning as a wondrous gate — its meaning lies here.

Chinese Source

六者「淨」為妙門者，行者若能體識一切諸法本性清淨，即便獲得自性禪也。得此禪故，二乘之人定證涅槃。若是菩薩入鐵輪位，具十信心，修行不止，即便出生九種大禪，所謂自性禪、一切禪、難禪、一切門禪、善人禪、一切行禪、除惱禪、此世他世樂禪、清淨禪。菩薩依是禪故，得大菩提果，已得、今得、當得。「淨」為妙門，意在此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, purity as a wondrous gate. If the practitioner can embody and recognize that the original nature of all dharmas is pure, he or she attains the dhyāna of self-nature. Through attaining this dhyāna, people of the two vehicles certainly realize nirvāṇa. If it is a bodhisattva, who enters the iron-wheel stage, is complete in the ten minds of faith, and cultivates without stopping, there arise the nine kinds of great dhyāna, namely: the dhyāna of self-nature, the dhyāna of all, the difficult dhyāna, the dhyāna of every gate, the dhyāna of the good person, the dhyāna of every practice, the dhyāna that removes affliction, the dhyāna of joy in this world and the next, and the pure dhyāna. Relying on these dhyānas, bodhisattvas attain the fruit of great bodhi — have attained it, now attain it, and will attain it. Purity as a wondrous gate — its meaning lies here.

2. The Six Gates Arising One from Another in Sequence

Chinese Source

次釋「第二、次第相生六妙門」者，次第相生入道之階梯也。若於欲界中巧行六法，第六淨心成就，即發三乘無漏，況復具足諸禪三昧？

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "Second, the six wondrous gates arising one from another in sequence": arising one from another in sequence is the staircase for entering the Way. If, within the desire realm, one skillfully practices the six dharmas and the sixth, the pure mind, is accomplished, the uncontaminated [wisdom] of the three vehicles arises — how much more if one is complete in the various dhyānas and samādhis?

Chinese Source

此即與前有異。所以者何？如數有二種：一者修數，二者證數。修數者，行者調和氣息，不澁不滑，安詳徐數，從一至十，攝心在數，不令馳散，是名修數。證數者，覺心任運，從一至十，不加功力，心住息緣，覺息虛微，心相漸細。

Ru-Yi Translation

This differs from the preceding. Why? Counting, for example, is of two kinds: first, cultivating counting; second, realizing counting. Cultivating counting: the practitioner harmonizes the breath so that it is neither rough nor slippery, and counts calmly and slowly from one to ten, gathering the mind onto the counting and not letting it run off and scatter. This is called cultivating counting. Realizing counting: one feels the mind going of itself from one to ten without applying effort; the mind rests on the breath as its object; one feels the breath to be empty and subtle, and the mind's marks become gradually finer.

Chinese Source

患數為麁，意不欲數。爾時行者應當放數修隨。隨亦有二：一者修隨，二者證隨。修隨者，捨前數法，一心依隨息之出入，攝心緣息，知息入出，心住息緣，無分散意。是名修隨。證隨者，心既微細，安靜不亂，覺息長短，遍身入出，心息任運相依，意慮恬然凝靜。

Ru-Yi Translation

One is troubled that counting is coarse, and the mind no longer wishes to count. At that time the practitioner should let go of counting and cultivate following. Following, too, is of two kinds: first, cultivating following; second, realizing following. Cultivating following: abandoning the earlier method of counting, one single-mindedly follows the going out and coming in of the breath, gathering the mind onto the breath as its object, knowing the breath entering and leaving; the mind rests on the breath as its object, with no thought of scattering. This is called cultivating following. Realizing following: the mind having become subtle, calm, and unconfused, one feels the breath as long or short, entering and leaving through the whole body; mind and breath rely on each other of themselves, and thought is placid, settled, and still.

Chinese Source

覺隨為麁，心厭欲捨，如人疲極欲眠，不樂眾務。爾時行者應當捨隨修止。止亦有二：一者修止，二者證止。修止者，息諸緣慮，不念數隨，凝寂其心，是名修止。證止者，覺身心泯然入定，不見內外相貌，定法持心，任運不動。

Ru-Yi Translation

One feels that following is coarse, and the mind tires of it and wishes to let it go, like a person exhausted and wanting to sleep who takes no pleasure in any task. At that time the practitioner should let go of following and cultivate stilling. Stilling, too, is of two kinds: first, cultivating stilling; second, realizing stilling. Cultivating stilling: one rests all thinking about objects, does not think of counting or following, and makes the mind settled and still. This is called cultivating stilling. Realizing stilling: one feels body and mind dissolve as one enters concentration; one does not see the appearances of inner or outer; the dharma of concentration holds the mind, which of itself does not move.

Chinese Source

行者是時即作是念：「今此三昧雖復無為寂靜、安隱快樂，而無惠方便，不能破壞生死。」復作是念：「今此定者，皆屬因緣，陰界入法和合而有，虛誑不實。我今不見不覺，應須照了。」作是念已，即不著止，起觀分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

The practitioner then thinks: "Although this samādhi is unconditioned and still, peaceful and blissful, it lacks the skillful means of wisdom and cannot break up birth and death." He or she thinks further: "This concentration belongs entirely to causes and conditions; it exists through the coming together of the dharmas of the aggregates, elements, and sense-bases, and is false and unreal. I do not now see or feel this; I must illuminate it clearly." Having thought this, one no longer clings to stilling, and gives rise to contemplation to discern it.

Chinese Source

觀亦有二：一者修觀，二者證觀。修觀者，於定心中以惠分別，觀於微細出入息相，如空中風；皮、肉、筋、骨……三十六物，如芭蕉不實；心識無常剎那不住；無有我、人。身、受、心、法皆無自性。不得人法，定何所依？是名修觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Contemplation, too, is of two kinds: first, cultivating contemplation; second, realizing contemplation. Cultivating contemplation: within the concentrated mind one discerns with wisdom. One contemplates the subtle marks of the breath going out and coming in as like wind in empty space; the skin, flesh, sinews, bones … the thirty-six substances as like a plantain tree, without substance; mind and consciousness as impermanent, not remaining for an instant; there is no self and no person. Body, sensation, mind, and dharmas are all without self-nature. When neither person nor dharma can be found, on what does the concentration rely? This is called cultivating contemplation.

Chinese Source

證觀者，如是觀時，覺息出入遍諸毛孔，心眼開明，徹見三十六物及諸虫戶，內外不淨，剎那變易，心生悲喜，得四念處，破四顛倒，是名證觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

Realizing contemplation: while contemplating in this way, one feels the breath going out and coming in through all the pores; the mind's eye opens and becomes clear, and one sees straight through the thirty-six substances and the many colonies of worms, impure within and without, changing from instant to instant. Sorrow and joy arise in the mind; one attains the four foundations of mindfulness and breaks the four inversions. This is called realizing contemplation.

Chinese Source

觀相既發，心緣觀境，分別破折，覺念流動，非真實道。爾時應當捨觀修還。還亦有二：一者修還，二者證還。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once the marks of contemplation have arisen, the mind takes the object of contemplation as its object, discerning and breaking it down; one feels that thought is flowing and moving, and that this is not the true path. At that time one should let go of contemplation and cultivate returning. Returning, too, is of two kinds: first, cultivating returning; second, realizing returning.

Chinese Source

修還者，既知觀從心生，若從折境，此即不會本源。應當反觀觀心，此觀心者，從何而生？為從觀心生？為從非觀心生？若從觀心生，即已有觀，今實不爾。所以者何？數、隨、止等三法中，未有即觀故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Cultivating returning: one knows that contemplation arises from the mind; if one follows the breaking down of objects, this does not meet the original source. One should turn back and contemplate the contemplating mind. This contemplating mind — from what does it arise? Does it arise from a contemplating mind, or from a non-contemplating mind? If it arises from a contemplating mind, then there was already contemplation; but in fact this is not so. Why? Because in the three dharmas of counting, following, and stilling, there was as yet no contemplation.

Chinese Source

若從不觀心生，不觀心為滅生？為不滅生？若不滅生，即二心並。若滅法生，滅法已謝，不能生觀。若言亦滅亦不滅生，乃至非滅非不滅生，皆不可得。當知觀心本自不生，不生故不有，不有故即空，空故無觀心。若無觀心，豈有觀境？境智雙亡，還源之要也，是名修還相。

Ru-Yi Translation

If it arises from a non-contemplating mind, does the non-contemplating mind give rise to it by ceasing, or without ceasing? If without ceasing, then two minds exist together. If a ceased dharma gives rise to it — a ceased dharma is already gone and cannot give rise to contemplation. If one says it arises from [a mind that] both ceases and does not cease, or from [one that] neither ceases nor does not cease, none of these can be found. One should know that the contemplating mind has never, from the beginning, arisen. Because it does not arise, it does not exist; because it does not exist, it is empty; because it is empty, there is no contemplating mind. If there is no contemplating mind, how could there be an object of contemplation? Object and knowing both vanish: this is the key to returning to the source. This is called the mark of cultivating returning.

Chinese Source

證還相者，心惠開發，不加功力，任運自能破折，反本還源，是名證還。

Ru-Yi Translation

The mark of realizing returning: the wisdom of the mind opens out, and without applying effort it is able of itself to break things down, turn back to the root, and return to the source. This is called realizing returning.

Chinese Source

行者當知，若離境智，欲歸無境智，不離境智縛，以隨二邊故。爾時當捨還門，安心淨道。

Ru-Yi Translation

The practitioner should know: if one leaves object and knowing and wishes to return to [a state] without object or knowing, one has not left the bondage of object and knowing, because one is following the two extremes. At that time one should let go of the gate of returning and settle the mind on the path of purity.

Chinese Source

淨亦有二：一者修淨，二者證淨。修淨者，知色淨故，不起妄想分別，受、想、行、識亦復如是。息妄想垢，是名修淨；息分別垢，是名修淨；息取我垢，是名修淨。舉要言之，若能心如本淨，是名修淨；亦不得能修、所修及淨、不淨，是名修淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Purity, too, is of two kinds: first, cultivating purity; second, realizing purity. Cultivating purity: knowing that form is pure, one does not give rise to deluded thinking and discrimination; and so too with sensation, perception, formations, and consciousness. Resting the defilement of deluded thinking is called cultivating purity; resting the defilement of discrimination is called cultivating purity; resting the defilement of grasping at a self is called cultivating purity. To put it briefly: if the mind can be as it originally is, pure, this is called cultivating purity; and also not finding one who cultivates, what is cultivated, purity, or impurity — this is called cultivating purity.

Chinese Source

證淨者，如是修時，豁然心惠相應，無礙方便任運開發，三昧正受，心無依恃。證淨有二：一者相似證，五方便相似無漏道惠發；二者真實證，苦法忍乃至第九無礙道等真無漏惠發也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Realizing purity: while cultivating in this way, suddenly the mind accords with wisdom; unobstructed skillful means open out of themselves; one is in the right reception of samādhi, and the mind relies on nothing. Realizing purity is of two kinds: first, approximate realization, in which, in the five expedient stages, a wisdom of the path that resembles the uncontaminated arises; second, true realization, in which true uncontaminated wisdom arises, from the patient acceptance of the dharma of suffering up to the ninth unobstructed path.

Chinese Source

三界垢盡，故名證淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because the defilements of the three realms are exhausted, it is called realizing purity.

Chinese Source

復次，觀眾生空，故名為觀；觀實法空，故名為還；觀平等空，故名為淨。復次，空三昧相應，故名為觀；無相三昧相應，故名為還；無作三昧相應，故名為淨。復次，一切外觀名為觀；一切內觀名為還；一切非內非外觀名為淨。故先尼梵志言：「非內觀故，得是智惠；非外觀故，得是智惠；非內外觀故，得是智惠；亦不無觀故，得是智惠也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Further: contemplating the emptiness of beings is therefore called contemplation; contemplating the emptiness of real dharmas is therefore called returning; contemplating the emptiness of equality is therefore called purity. Further: according with the samādhi of emptiness is therefore called contemplation; according with the signless samādhi is therefore called returning; according with the wishless samādhi is therefore called purity. Further: all outward contemplation is called contemplation; all inward contemplation is called returning; all contemplation that is neither inward nor outward is called purity. So the wandering ascetic Senika said: "Not by inward contemplation does one attain this wisdom; not by outward contemplation does one attain this wisdom; not by inward-and-outward contemplation does one attain this wisdom; and yet not without contemplation does one attain this wisdom."

3. The Six Gates Used as Is Convenient

Chinese Source

次釋「第三、隨便宜六妙門」。夫行者欲得深禪定智慧，乃至實相涅槃，初學安心必須善巧。云何善巧？當於六妙門法，悉知悉覺，調伏其心，隨心所便，可以常用。所以者何？若心不便，修治即無益。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "Third, the six wondrous gates used as is convenient." A practitioner who wishes to attain deep dhyāna, concentration, and wisdom, up to the nirvāṇa of the true mark, must be skillful in settling the mind when first learning. What is this skill? One should know and be aware of all the dharmas of the six wondrous gates, tame the mind, and use constantly whatever the mind finds convenient. Why? If the mind does not find it convenient, cultivation and remedy are of no benefit.

Chinese Source

是故初坐時，當識調心學數，次當學隨，復當學止、觀、還等，各各經數日。學已，復更從數、隨，乃至還、淨，安心修習，復各經數日。如是數反，行者即應自知心所便宜。

Ru-Yi Translation

Therefore, when first sitting, one should know how to tune the mind and learn counting; next learn following; then learn stilling, contemplating, returning, and the rest, each for several days. Having learned them, again from counting and following through returning and purity, settle the mind and practice, again each for several days. After going through them several times like this, the practitioner should know for himself or herself what the mind finds convenient.

Chinese Source

若心便數，當以數法安心，乃至淨亦如是。隨便而用，不簡次第。

Ru-Yi Translation

If the mind finds counting convenient, one should settle the mind by means of counting; and so on through purity. Use whatever is convenient, without regard to sequence.

Chinese Source

如是安心時，若覺身安息調，心靜開明，始終安固，當專用此法，必有深利。若有妨生，心散闇塞，當更隨便轉用餘門。安即為善，可以長軌。是則略明初學善巧安心六妙門，是知便宜用心大意。

Ru-Yi Translation

When settling the mind in this way, if one feels the body at ease and the breath harmonized, the mind quiet, open, and clear, steady and secure from beginning to end, one should use this method exclusively, and there will certainly be deep benefit. If hindrances arise, and the mind is scattered, dark, and blocked, one should change again, as is convenient, to using another gate. What brings ease is good and can serve as a lasting rule. This briefly explains the six wondrous gates for skillfully settling the mind when first learning, and gives the main idea of knowing how to use the mind as is convenient.

Chinese Source

復次，行者心若安穩必有所證。云何為證？所謂得持身及麁住、細住、欲界、未到地、初禪等種種諸禪定。得諸定已，若心住不進，當隨定深淺修六妙門開發。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if the practitioner's mind is settled and stable, there will certainly be some realization. What is realization? Namely, attaining the holding of the body, coarse abiding, fine abiding, the desire-realm [concentration], the not-yet-arrived [concentration], the first dhyāna, and the various other dhyānas and concentrations. Having attained these concentrations, if the mind stays where it is and does not advance, one should cultivate the six wondrous gates according to the depth of the concentration, to open it out.

Chinese Source

云何名淺定不進，修六門令進？如行者初得持身法及麁細住法，經於日月而不增進，爾時應當細心修數；數若不進，復當修隨；隨若不進，當細凝心修止；止若不進，當定中觀陰入界法；觀若不進，當還更反檢心源；還若不進，當寂然體淨。用此六法，若偏於一法增進之時，當即善修之。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is meant by a shallow concentration that does not advance, and cultivating the six gates to make it advance? Suppose the practitioner has first attained the dharma of holding the body and the dharmas of coarse and fine abiding, and for days and months there is no further progress. At that time one should cultivate counting with a careful mind. If counting does not advance, one should next cultivate following. If following does not advance, one should finely settle the mind and cultivate stilling. If stilling does not advance, one should, within concentration, contemplate the dharmas of the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements. If contemplation does not advance, one should return and examine the source of the mind once more. If returning does not advance, one should, in stillness, embody purity. When using these six dharmas, if progress comes particularly with one dharma, one should then cultivate it well.

Chinese Source

既漸進入深禪定，便過數境。數相既謝，進發隨禪。於此定中，若不增進，當善修隨、止、觀、還、淨等五法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once one has gradually advanced into deep dhyāna and concentration, one has passed beyond the realm of counting. When the marks of counting have gone, one advances and the dhyāna of following arises. If within this concentration there is no further progress, one should cultivate well the five dharmas of following, stilling, contemplating, returning, and purity.

Chinese Source

定進漸深，隨境已度，若發止禪，禪若不進，當善修止及觀、還、淨等四法。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the concentration advances and gradually deepens, the realm of following is crossed. If the dhyāna of stilling arises and this dhyāna does not advance, one should cultivate well the four dharmas of stilling, contemplating, returning, and purity.

Chinese Source

止定進漸深，觀心開發，雖有止法，知從緣生，無有自性。止相已謝，若觀禪不進，當更善巧修觀及還、淨等三法。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the concentration of stilling advances and gradually deepens, the contemplating mind opens out; though there is the dharma of stilling, one knows that it arises from conditions and has no self-nature. When the marks of stilling have gone, if the dhyāna of contemplation does not advance, one should further cultivate skillfully the three dharmas of contemplating, returning, and purity.

Chinese Source

觀禪既進，進已若謝，轉入深定，惠解開發，唯覺自心所有法相，知觀虛誑不實，亦在妄情，如夢中所見，知已不受，還反照心源。還禪經久，又不進，當復更善反觀心源及體淨當寂。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once the dhyāna of contemplation has advanced and, having advanced, falls away, one turns into deep concentration, and wisdom and understanding open out. One feels only the dharma-marks belonging to one's own mind, and knows that contemplation is false and unreal, also within deluded feeling, like what is seen in a dream. Knowing this, one does not accept it, but returns and turns back to illuminate the source of the mind. If the dhyāna of returning goes on for a long time and again does not advance, one should once more turn back well to contemplate the source of the mind, and embody purity, stilled.

Chinese Source

還禪既進，進已若謝，便發淨禪。此禪「念相觀已除，言語法皆滅，無量眾罪除，清淨心常一」。是名淨禪。淨若不進，當善却垢心，體真寂虛，心如虛空，無所依倚。

Ru-Yi Translation

Once the dhyāna of returning has advanced and, having advanced, falls away, the dhyāna of purity arises. Of this dhyāna it is said: "The marks of thought and contemplation are removed; the dharmas of speech all cease; countless offenses are removed; the pure mind is always one." This is called the dhyāna of purity. If purity does not advance, one should remove the defiled mind well, embody the true, stilled and empty; the mind is like space, relying on nothing.

Chinese Source

爾時淨禪漸深寂，豁然明朗，發真無漏，證三乘道。此則略說六妙門隨便宜而用，增長諸禪功德智惠，乃至入涅槃也。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the dhyāna of purity gradually deepens and stills; suddenly it is bright and clear, true uncontaminated [wisdom] arises, and one realizes the path of the three vehicles. This briefly describes using the six wondrous gates as is convenient, increasing the merits and wisdom of the various dhyānas, up to entering nirvāṇa.

Chinese Source

復次，行者於其中間，若有內外障起，欲除却者，亦當於六門中隨取一法，一一試用却之。若得差者，即為藥也。治禪障及禪中魔事、病患，功用六門悉得差也。上來所說，其意難見。行者若用此法門，當善思惟取意，勿妄行也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if along the way inner or outer obstacles arise and the practitioner wishes to remove them, he or she should likewise take any one dharma from among the six gates and try them one by one to remove the obstacle. Whatever brings recovery is the medicine. For treating obstacles to dhyāna, and the work of Māra and illnesses within dhyāna, the six gates, when applied, all bring recovery. The meaning of what has been said above is hard to see. A practitioner who uses this dharma-gate should reflect well and grasp the meaning, and not practice recklessly.

4. The Six Gates as Remedies

Chinese Source

次釋「第四、對治六妙門」。三乘行者修道會真，悉是除障顯理，無所造作。所以者何？二乘之人，四住惑除，名得聖果，更無別法；菩薩大士，破塵沙、無明障盡故，菩提理顯，亦不異修。此而推之。若能巧用六門對治，破內外障，即是修道，即是得道，更無別道。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "Fourth, the six wondrous gates as remedies." Practitioners of the three vehicles cultivate the path and meet the true, and in every case this is removing obstacles and revealing the principle; nothing is fabricated. Why? For people of the two vehicles, when the delusions of the four abodes are removed, it is called attaining the noble fruit; there is no other dharma. For bodhisattvas, the great beings, because the obstacles of dust-and-sand and of ignorance are broken and exhausted, the principle of bodhi is revealed; this, too, is no different from cultivation. Infer from this: if one can skillfully use the six gates as remedies to break inner and outer obstacles, that is cultivating the path, that is attaining the path; there is no other path.

Chinese Source

云何功用六門對治？行者應當知病識藥。云何知病？所謂三障：一者報障，即是今世不善、麁動、散亂障界入也；二者煩惱障，即三毒、十使等諸煩惱也；三者業障，即是過去、現在所起障道惡業，於未受報中間，能障聖道也。行者於坐禪中，此三障發，當善識其相，用此法門對治除滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

How are the six gates applied as remedies? The practitioner should know the illness and recognize the medicine. How does one know the illness? There are the three obstacles. First, the obstacle of retribution: the unwholesome, coarse, agitated, and scattered [states] of this life that obstruct the elements and sense-bases. Second, the obstacle of affliction: the three poisons, the ten latent tendencies, and the other afflictions. Third, the obstacle of karma: evil karma, done in the past or present, that obstructs the path; in the interval before its retribution is received, it can obstruct the noble path. When these three obstacles arise while the practitioner is sitting in dhyāna, he or she should recognize their marks well and use this dharma-gate as a remedy to remove them.

Chinese Source

云何坐中知報障起相？云何對治等？分別覺觀心散動，攀緣諸境，無暫停住故，名報障起。浮動明利，攀緣諸境，心散縱橫，如猿猴得樹，難可制錄。爾時行者應用數門，調心數息，當知即真對治也。故佛言：「覺觀多者，教令數息。」

Ru-Yi Translation

How does one know, while sitting, the marks of the obstacle of retribution arising? And how is it remedied? Because the mind, discriminating with initial thought and sustained reflection, is scattered and agitated, clinging to objects without stopping for a moment, it is called the obstacle of retribution arising. It is buoyant, agitated, bright, and sharp, clinging to objects; the mind scatters in every direction, like a monkey that has reached a tree, hard to restrain. At that time the practitioner should use the gate of counting, tuning the mind and counting the breath; one should know that this is the true remedy. So the Buddha said: "Those with much initial thought and sustained reflection should be taught to count the breath."

Chinese Source

二者於坐禪中，或時其心亦昏亦散，昏即無記，心闇即睡眠，散即心浮越逸。爾時行者當用隨門，善調心隨息，明照入出，心依息緣，無分散意，照息出入，治無記昏睡。心依於息，治覺觀攀緣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, while sitting in dhyāna, sometimes the mind is both dull and scattered. Dull means indeterminate: when the mind is dark, there is sleep. Scattered means the mind floats and runs off. At that time the practitioner should use the gate of following, tuning the mind well to follow the breath, clearly illuminating its entering and leaving; the mind relies on the breath as its object, with no thought of scattering. Illuminating the breath going out and coming in remedies indeterminate dullness and sleep; the mind relying on the breath remedies the clinging of initial thought and sustained reflection.

Chinese Source

三者於坐禪中，若覺身心急氣麁，心散流動，爾時行者當用止門，寬身放息，制心凝寂，止諸憶慮，此為治也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, while sitting in dhyāna, if one feels the body and mind tense and the breath coarse, and the mind scattered and flowing, at that time the practitioner should use the gate of stilling: relaxing the body and releasing the breath, restraining the mind into settled stillness, stilling all recollection and deliberation. This is the remedy.

Chinese Source

復次，云何煩惱障起？云何對治？煩惱有三種。一者於坐禪中，貪欲煩惱障起。爾時行者當用觀心門中，九想、初背捨、二勝處、諸不淨門為對治也。二者於坐禪中，瞋恚煩惱障起。爾時行者當用觀心門中，慈悲喜捨等為對治也。三者於坐禪中，愚癡邪見煩惱障起。爾時行者當用還門，反照十二因緣、三空道品，破折心源，還歸本性，此為治也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, how does the obstacle of affliction arise, and how is it remedied? Afflictions are of three kinds. First, while sitting in dhyāna, the obstacle of the affliction of greed arises. At that time the practitioner should use, within the gate of contemplating the mind, the nine contemplations, the first detachment, the second station of mastery, and the gates of impurity as the remedy. Second, while sitting in dhyāna, the obstacle of the affliction of anger arises. At that time the practitioner should use, within the gate of contemplating the mind, loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, equanimity, and the like as the remedy. Third, while sitting in dhyāna, the obstacle of the affliction of delusion and wrong views arises. At that time the practitioner should use the gate of returning: turning back to illuminate the twelve links, the three emptinesses, and the factors of the path, breaking down the source of the mind and returning to the original nature. This is the remedy.

Chinese Source

復次，云何對治障道業起？業即三種，治法亦三。一者於坐禪中，忽然垢心昏闇，迷失境界，當知黑闇業障起。爾時行者，當用淨門中，念方便淨應身三十二相清淨光明，為對治也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, how is the karma that obstructs the path remedied when it arises? Karma is of three kinds, and the remedies too are three. First, while sitting in dhyāna, the mind suddenly becomes defiled, dull, and dark, and loses its object; one should know that the karmic obstacle of darkness has arisen. At that time the practitioner should use, within the gate of purity, recollection of the response-body of expedient purity, its thirty-two marks and pure radiance, as the remedy.

Chinese Source

二者於坐禪中，忽然惡念思惟，貪欲無惡不造，當亦是過去罪業所之作也。爾時行者，當用淨門中，念報佛一切種智圓淨常樂功德，為對治也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, while sitting in dhyāna, evil thoughts suddenly arise, with greed ready to commit every evil; one should know that this too is the work of past offenses. At that time the practitioner should use, within the gate of purity, recollection of the reward-body buddha, the wisdom knowing all modes, and the merits of perfect purity, permanence, and bliss, as the remedy.

Chinese Source

三者於坐禪中，若有種種諸惡境界相現，乃至逼迫身心，當知悉是過去今世所造惡業障發也。爾時行者當用淨門中，念法身本淨，不生不滅，本性清淨，為對治也。此則略說六門對治斷除三障之相；廣說不異十五種障也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, while sitting in dhyāna, if marks of various evil states appear, even to the point of oppressing body and mind, one should know that all of this is the arising of the obstacle of evil karma created in past lives and this one. At that time the practitioner should use, within the gate of purity, recollection of the Dharma-body, originally pure, neither arising nor ceasing, pure in its original nature, as the remedy. This briefly describes the six gates as remedies for cutting off and removing the three obstacles; explained at length, it is no different from the fifteen kinds of obstacle.

Chinese Source

復次，行者於坐禪中，若發諸餘禪深定、智惠解脫，有種種障起，當於六門中，善巧用對治法也。麁細障法既除，真如實相自顯，三明六通自發，十力、四無所畏、一切諸佛菩薩功德行願，自然現前，不由造作。故經云「又見諸如來，自然成佛道。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if, while sitting in dhyāna, the practitioner gives rise to other dhyānas, deep concentrations, wisdom, and liberation, and various obstacles arise, he or she should skillfully use the remedies among the six gates. Once the dharmas of coarse and subtle obstacles have been removed, true suchness, the true mark, reveals itself; the three clear knowledges and the six supernormal powers arise of themselves; the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses, and the merits, practices, and vows of all buddhas and bodhisattvas appear spontaneously, without being fabricated. So a sūtra says: "And I saw the tathāgatas spontaneously attain the Buddha way."

5. The Six Gates Containing One Another

Chinese Source

次釋「第五、相攝六妙門」。夫六妙門相攝，近論則有二種，遠尋則有多途。何等為二？一者六門自體相攝，二者巧修六門出生勝進相攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "Fifth, the six wondrous gates containing one another." The six wondrous gates contain one another; discussed closely, there are two kinds; traced further, there are many routes. What are the two? First, the six gates containing one another in their own substance; second, the six gates, skillfully cultivated, containing one another as they bring forth superior progress.

Chinese Source

云何名自體相攝？行者修六門時，於一數息中，任運自攝隨、止、觀、還、淨等五法。所以者何？如行者善調心數息之時，即體是數門；心依隨息而數故，即攝隨門；息諸攀緣，制心在數故，即攝心門；分別知心數法及息，了了分明故，即攝觀門；若心動散，攀緣五欲，悉是虛誑，心不受著，緣心還歸數息故，即攝還門；攝數息時，無有五蓋及諸麁煩惱垢，身心寂然，即攝淨門。

Ru-Yi Translation

What is meant by containing one another in their own substance? When the practitioner cultivates the six gates, within the single [practice of] counting the breath, the five dharmas of following, stilling, contemplating, returning, and purity are contained of themselves. Why? When the practitioner tunes the mind well and counts the breath, that very substance is the gate of counting. Because the mind relies on and follows the breath as it counts, it contains the gate of following. Because one rests all clinging and restrains the mind onto the counting, it contains the gate of the mind. Because one discerns and knows the mind, the counting, and the breath, clearly and distinctly, it contains the gate of contemplating. If the mind stirs and scatters, clinging to the five desires, [one knows that] all of this is false, the mind does not accept or cling to it, and the clinging mind returns to counting the breath; so it contains the gate of returning. While gathered in counting the breath, there are none of the five hindrances or the defilement of coarse afflictions, and body and mind are still; so it contains the gate of purity.

Chinese Source

當知於數息中即有六門，隨、止、觀、還、淨等一一皆攝六門，此則六六三十六妙門。上來雖復種種運用不同，悉有今意，若不分別，行人不知。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that within counting the breath there are the six gates; and following, stilling, contemplating, returning, and purity each likewise contain the six gates. This makes six times six, thirty-six wondrous gates. Although the various uses described above differ, all of them have the present meaning; if it is not distinguished, practitioners will not know it.

Chinese Source

此則略說六妙門自體相攝，一中具六相也。

Ru-Yi Translation

This briefly describes the six wondrous gates containing one another in their own substance, each one possessing the marks of all six.

Chinese Source

復次，云何名巧修六妙門出生勝進相攝相？行者於初調心數息，從一至十，心不分散，是名數門。當數息時，靜心善巧，既知息初入、中間經遊至處，乃至入已還出亦如是，心悉覺知，依隨不亂，亦成就數法，從一至十，是則數中成就隨門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, what are the marks of the six wondrous gates, skillfully cultivated, containing one another as they bring forth superior progress? When the practitioner first tunes the mind and counts the breath from one to ten, without the mind scattering, this is called the gate of counting. While counting the breath, with a quiet and skillful mind, one knows where the breath first enters, where it travels in between, and where it arrives, and likewise, having entered, how it goes out again; the mind is aware of it all, following without confusion, and also accomplishes the dharma of counting from one to ten. This is accomplishing the gate of following within counting.

Chinese Source

復次，行者當數息時，細心善巧，制心緣數法及息，不令細微覺觀得起，剎那異念分別不生，是則於數中成就止門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, while counting the breath, the practitioner, with a fine and skillful mind, restrains the mind onto the counting and the breath, not letting even subtle initial thought and sustained reflection arise, so that not even an instant of other thought or discrimination arises. This is accomplishing the gate of stilling within counting.

Chinese Source

復次，行者當數息時，成就息念巧惠方便，用靜鑒之心，照息生滅；兼知身分、剎那思想、陰入界法，如雲如影，空無自性，不得人法，是時於數息中成就息念巧惠觀門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, while counting the breath, the practitioner accomplishes the skillful wisdom and means of mindfulness of the breath, and with a quiet, mirroring mind illuminates the arising and ceasing of the breath; he or she also knows the parts of the body, momentary thoughts, and the dharmas of the aggregates, sense-bases, and elements as like clouds and shadows, empty and without self-nature, and finds neither person nor dharma. This is accomplishing, within counting the breath, the gate of contemplation by the skillful wisdom of mindfulness of the breath.

Chinese Source

復次，行者當數息時，非但成就觀智，識前法虛假，亦復善巧覺了觀照之心無有自性，虛誑不實，離知覺想，是則於數息中成就還門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, while counting the breath, the practitioner not only accomplishes contemplative knowing and recognizes the preceding dharmas as false and provisional, but also skillfully realizes that the mind that contemplates and illuminates has no self-nature, is false and unreal, and is apart from knowing, feeling, and perception. This is accomplishing the gate of returning within counting the breath.

Chinese Source

復次，行者當數息時，非但不得所觀、能觀，以惠方便亦不得無能觀、所觀，以本淨法性，如虛空不可分別故。爾時行者心同法性，寂然不動，是則於數息中成就淨門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, while counting the breath, the practitioner not only finds neither the contemplated nor the contemplator, but, by the skillful means of wisdom, also does not find the absence of a contemplator or of the contemplated, because the originally pure Dharma-nature is like space and cannot be discriminated. At that time the practitioner's mind is the same as the Dharma-nature, still and unmoving. This is accomplishing the gate of purity within counting the breath.

Chinese Source

以五門莊嚴數息，隨、止、觀、還、淨，皆亦如是，今不別說。此則六六三十六，亦名三十六妙門。行者若能如是善巧修習六妙門者，當知必得種種諸深禪定智惠，入三乘涅槃也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The five gates adorn counting the breath; and following, stilling, contemplating, returning, and purity are all likewise, which I will not now describe separately. This too makes six times six, thirty-six, also called the thirty-six wondrous gates. If a practitioner can cultivate the six wondrous gates skillfully in this way, one should know that he or she will certainly attain the various deep dhyānas, concentrations, and wisdom, and enter the nirvāṇa of the three vehicles.

6. The Six Gates as Shared and Distinct

Chinese Source

次釋「第六、通別六妙門」。所以言通別六門者，凡夫、外道、二乘、菩薩通觀數息一法，而解惠不同，是故證涅槃殊別；隨、止、觀、還、淨亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "Sixth, the six wondrous gates as shared and distinct." They are called the shared and distinct six gates because ordinary people, non-Buddhists, the two vehicles, and bodhisattvas all alike contemplate the single dharma of counting the breath, yet their understanding and wisdom differ, and so their realization of nirvāṇa differs. Following, stilling, contemplating, returning, and purity are likewise.

Chinese Source

所以者何？凡夫鈍根行者，當數息時唯知從一至十，令心安定，欲望此入禪，受諸快樂，是名於數息中而起魔業，以貪生死故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why? When a practitioner who is an ordinary person of dull faculties counts the breath, he or she only knows to count from one to ten to make the mind settled, hoping by this to enter dhyāna and enjoy its pleasures. This is called giving rise to the work of Māra within counting the breath, because it craves birth and death.

Chinese Source

復次，如諸利根外道，見心猛盛，見因緣故，當數息時非但調心數息，從一至十，欲求禪定，亦能分別現在有息、無息、亦有亦無、非有非無；過去息如去、不如去、亦如去亦不如去、非如去非不如去；未來息有邊、無邊、亦有邊亦無邊、非有邊非無邊；現在息有常耶，無常耶，亦常亦無常耶，非常非無常耶？及心亦爾，隨心所見，計以為實，謂他所說悉為忘語。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, take the non-Buddhists of sharp faculties: because their minds bent on views are fierce and strong, and because of [their] views about causes and conditions, when they count the breath they do not only tune the mind and count the breath from one to ten, seeking dhyāna and concentration; they can also discriminate whether the present breath exists, does not exist, both exists and does not exist, or neither exists nor does not exist; whether the past breath has gone thus, has not gone thus, both has and has not gone thus, or neither has nor has not gone thus; whether the future breath has an end, has no end, both has and has no end, or neither has nor has no end; whether the present breath is permanent, impermanent, both permanent and impermanent, or neither permanent nor impermanent. And so too with the mind. Whatever the mind sees, they reckon to be real, and say that whatever others teach is false speech.

Chinese Source

是人不了息相，隨妄見生分別，即是數息戲論。四邊火燒，生煩惱處，長夜貪著邪見，造諸邪行，斷滅善根，不會無生，心行理外，故名外道。如是二人鈍利雖殊，三界生死輪迴無別。

Ru-Yi Translation

These people do not understand the marks of the breath; following deluded views, they give rise to discrimination. This is counting the breath as idle speculation. The four extremes burn like fire, giving rise to afflictions; through the long night they cling to wrong views, commit wrong practices, cut off their wholesome roots, and do not meet the unarisen; their minds move outside the principle, so they are called those outside the Way. Though these two kinds of people differ in being dull or sharp, they do not differ in revolving in the birth and death of the three realms.

Chinese Source

復次，云何名為聲聞數息相？行者欲速出三界，自求涅槃故，修數息以調其心。爾時於數息中，不離四諦正觀。云何於數息中觀四真諦？行者知息依身，身依心，三事和合，名陰、界、入。陰、界、入者，即是苦也。若人貪著陰、界、入法，乃至隨逐見心，分別陰、界、入法，即名為集。若能達息真性，即能知苦無生，不起四受，四行不生，即鈍使、利使諸煩惱結寂然不起，故名為滅。知苦正惠，能通理無壅，故名為道。若能如是數息，通達四諦，當知是人必定得聲聞道，畢故不造新。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, what are called the marks of a hearer's counting of the breath? Because the practitioner wishes to leave the three realms quickly and seeks nirvāṇa for himself or herself, he or she cultivates counting the breath to tune the mind. Then, within counting the breath, he or she does not depart from the correct contemplation of the Four Noble Truths. How are the four true truths contemplated within counting the breath? The practitioner knows that the breath relies on the body and the body relies on the mind; the coming together of these three things is called the aggregates, elements, and sense-bases. The aggregates, elements, and sense-bases are suffering. If one clings to the dharmas of the aggregates, elements, and sense-bases, or even follows the mind of views and discriminates the dharmas of the aggregates, elements, and sense-bases, that is called origination. If one can penetrate the true nature of the breath, one can know that suffering is unarisen; the four kinds of sensation do not arise, the four [kinds of] activity do not arise, and the knots of affliction, the dull and sharp latent tendencies, remain still and do not arise; so it is called cessation. The correct wisdom that knows suffering can penetrate the principle without obstruction; so it is called the path. If one can count the breath in this way and penetrate the Four Noble Truths, one should know that this person will certainly attain the hearer's path, finishing off the old and not creating the new.

Chinese Source

復次，云何於數息中入緣覺道？行者求自然惠，樂獨善寂，深知諸法因緣。當數息時，即知數息之念即是有支，有緣取，取緣愛，愛緣受，受緣觸，觸緣六入，六入緣名色，名色緣識，識緣行，行緣無明。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, how does one enter the path of the solitary buddha within counting the breath? The practitioner seeks spontaneous wisdom, delights in solitary goodness and stillness, and deeply knows the causes and conditions of dharmas. While counting the breath, one knows that the thought of counting the breath is itself the link of existence; existence is conditioned by grasping, grasping by craving, craving by sensation, sensation by contact, contact by the six sense-bases, the six sense-bases by name-and-form, name-and-form by consciousness, consciousness by formations, and formations by ignorance.

Chinese Source

復觀此息念之有，名善有為業。有善因緣，必定能感未來世人天受。受因緣故，必有老死憂悲苦惱，三世因緣，生死無際，輪轉不息。本無有生，亦無有死，不善思惟心行所造。

Ru-Yi Translation

One further contemplates that the existence of this thought of the breath is called wholesome conditioned karma. Having wholesome causes and conditions, it will certainly bring about rebirth as a human or a god in a future life. Because of the causes and conditions of that rebirth, there will certainly be old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, suffering, and distress; the causes and conditions of the three times, birth and death without limit, revolving without rest. Originally there is no birth and no death; they are created by the movements of an unwise mind.

Chinese Source

若知無明體性本自不有，妄想因緣和合而生，無所有故，假名無明。無明尚爾，亦不可得，當知行等諸因緣法，皆無根本。既無行等因緣，豈有今之數息之實？爾時行者深知數息屬因緣，空無自性，不受、不著，不念、不分別，心如虛空，寂然不動，豁然無漏心生，成緣覺道。

Ru-Yi Translation

If one knows that the essential nature of ignorance originally does not exist, that it arises through the coming together of the causes and conditions of deluded thinking, and that because it has no existence it is provisionally called ignorance — if even ignorance is like this and cannot be found, one should know that formations and the other dharmas of causes and conditions are all without root. Since there are no causes and conditions such as formations, how could there be any reality to the present counting of the breath? At that time the practitioner deeply knows that counting the breath belongs to causes and conditions and is empty, without self-nature; he or she does not accept it, does not cling to it, does not think about it or discriminate it; the mind is like space, still and unmoving; suddenly the uncontaminated mind arises, and one accomplishes the path of the solitary buddha.

Chinese Source

復次，云何名為菩薩數息相？行者為求一切智、佛智、自然智、無師智、如來知見、力、無所畏，愍念安樂無量眾生，故修數息，欲因此法門入一切種智。所以者何？如經中說：「阿那般那，三世諸佛入道之初門。」是故新發心菩薩，欲求佛道，應先調心數息。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, what are called the marks of a bodhisattva's counting of the breath? In order to seek the wisdom that knows all things, buddha-wisdom, spontaneous wisdom, wisdom without a teacher, the knowledge and vision of the Tathāgata, the powers, and the fearlessnesses, and out of compassion and the wish to bring peace and happiness to countless beings, the practitioner cultivates counting the breath, wishing through this dharma-gate to enter the wisdom knowing all modes. Why? As a sūtra says: "Ānāpāna is the first gate by which the buddhas of the three times entered the Way." Therefore a bodhisattva who has newly given rise to the mind [of awakening] and seeks the Buddha way should first tune the mind and count the breath.

Chinese Source

當數息時，知息非息，猶如幻化，是故息非是生死，亦非是涅槃。爾時於數息中，不得生死可斷，不得涅槃可入，是故不住生死。既無二十五有繫縛，不證涅槃，則不墮聲聞辟支佛地。以平等大惠，即無取捨心，入息中道，名見佛性，得無生忍，住大涅槃常樂我淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

While counting the breath, one knows that the breath is not the breath, like an illusion or a magical creation; therefore the breath is not birth and death, nor is it nirvāṇa. Then, within counting the breath, one finds no birth and death to be cut off and no nirvāṇa to be entered; therefore one does not abide in birth and death. Since there is no bondage of the twenty-five kinds of existence, and one does not realize [the lesser] nirvāṇa, one does not fall to the ground of the hearers and pratyekabuddhas. Through great wisdom that is equal, there is no mind of grasping or rejecting; one enters the middle way of the breath, which is called seeing buddha-nature, attains the patient acceptance of the unarisen, and abides in the great nirvāṇa of permanence, bliss, self, and purity.

Chinese Source

故經云：「譬如大水能突蕩一切，唯除楊柳，以其軟故。生死大水，亦復如是，能漂沒一切凡夫之人，唯除菩薩住於大乘大般涅槃，心柔軟故。」是名大乘行者於數息中入菩薩位。

Ru-Yi Translation

So a sūtra says: "It is like a great flood that can sweep away everything except the willow, because it is supple. The great flood of birth and death is also like this: it can sweep away and drown all ordinary people, except the bodhisattvas who abide in the great vehicle and the great parinirvāṇa, because their minds are supple." This is called a practitioner of the great vehicle entering the bodhisattva stage within counting the breath.

Chinese Source

此則略說數息妙門，凡聖大小乘通別之相。數息雖通，須解殊別之相。當知數息雖同共修，隨其果報差降。餘隨、止、觀、還、淨一一妙門，凡聖大小乘通別，亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

This briefly describes the wondrous gate of counting the breath in its shared and distinct marks for the ordinary and the noble, for the great and the lesser vehicles. Though counting the breath is shared, one must understand the marks of its differences. One should know that although counting the breath is practiced in common, its fruits and rewards differ in grade. The remaining wondrous gates — following, stilling, contemplating, returning, and purity — are likewise shared and distinct for the ordinary and the noble, for the great and the lesser vehicles.

Reading Notes for Part 1

Translator's notes

The title note. 瓦官寺 (Waguan Monastery) in Jinling (present-day Nanjing), where Zhiyi taught for eight years from 568. 都下, "the capital," is the Chen dynasty capital.

Ānāpāna (安般). Mindfulness of breathing in and out. The opening claim that the Buddha practiced the six gates beneath the bodhi tree, and the later quotation "Ānāpāna is the first gate by which the buddhas of the three times entered the Way," reflect sūtras on breath meditation translated into Chinese from the second century onward; the Shi Chan Boluomi (fascicle 7) cites the Ruiying jing (瑞應經) to the same effect.

The list of numbered dhyānas. Most items match this site's Shi Chan Boluomi: the four dhyānas, the five-gate dhyāna, the eight forms of detachment, the nine sequential concentrations. "The seven supporting concentrations" (七依定) are the seven states on which uncontaminated wisdom can rely (the four dhyānas and the first three formless attainments, in the Abhidharma count); "the ten factors of dhyāna" (十禪支) are the factors of the four dhyānas counted without repetition. "The three samādhis" here are most naturally the samādhis of emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness named later in this part; this site's Shi Chan Boluomi also uses 三三昧 for the samādhis with and without initial thought and sustained reflection.

"Cessation, stillness, the wondrous, and release" (滅、止、妙、離). The four aspects under which the truth of cessation is contemplated in the Abhidharma (nirodha, śānta, praṇīta, niḥsaraṇa).

"Nirvāṇa, the true Dharma-jewel." The source of this quotation has not yet been identified; the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra speaks in similar terms of entering the Buddha way through many gates.

"Ten kinds of subtle affliction." The text does not list them; Tiantai commentary counts the subtle afflictions remaining in the highest formless attainment.

Subhadra (須跋陀羅). The last disciple converted by the Buddha, on the night of his parinirvāṇa. He had attained the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception and took it for nirvāṇa.

"The foremost wondrous concentration is called 'neither perception'" (第一妙定，名曰「非想」). Read here as the thought of the ordinary practitioner, which is why it is set in quotation marks; it can also be read as the author's own statement.

The five-wheel dhyāna. This site's Shi Chan Boluomi (fascicle 4) lists earth, water, wind, golden-sand, and vajra wheels; this text has a space wheel (虛空輪) in third place instead of a wind wheel. Both are kept as written. "The five expedient stages" (五方便) are the stages of preparation before the path of seeing (in the Abhidharma count: the five stillings of the mind, the specific and general foundations of mindfulness, and the stages of warmth, summit, forbearance, and the supreme worldly dharma, variously grouped). "The not-yet-arrived [concentration]" (未到地) is the access concentration just before the first dhyāna.

Cultivating and realizing (修／證). Each of the six gates has a stage of deliberate effort ("cultivating") and a stage in which it goes on of itself ("realizing"). The Shi Chan Boluomi, fascicle 7, gives a nearly identical passage in which the second member is called "correspondence with" (相應) rather than "realizing"; this translation follows the wording of this text.

"Neither rough nor slippery" (不澁不滑). The breath should neither catch nor rush.

The thirty-six substances (三十六物) and the colonies of worms (虫戶). The traditional inventory of the body's parts, and the creatures believed to live in it; both are part of the contemplation of the body's impurity.

The four inversions (四顛倒). Taking the impure as pure, suffering as happiness, the impermanent as permanent, and what is without self as self — the four errors the four foundations of mindfulness overcome.

Senika (先尼梵志). A wandering ascetic of the Buddha's day (as on this site's Shi Chan Boluomi). The formula quoted here comes from the Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra as quoted and explained in the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra, which says that Senika saw this wisdom "not by inward contemplation" and so on.

"Holding the body, coarse abiding, fine abiding" (持身、麁住、細住). The early signs of concentration before the desire-realm concentration proper: the body held upright of itself, then the mind settling, coarsely and then finely.

"The marks of thought and contemplation are removed …" The first two lines echo a verse in the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra (念想觀已除，言語法亦滅); the wording here differs slightly and is kept as written.

"Contains the gate of the mind" (即攝心門). By the logic of the passage this should be the gate of stilling (止門), and the sentence describes stilling. The translation keeps "the gate of the mind" as written.

The three obstacles (三障). Here: of retribution (報), of affliction (煩惱), of karma (業). "The ten latent tendencies" (十使) are the five "dull" (greed, anger, ignorance, pride, doubt) and the five "sharp" (the five wrong views) tendencies, the 鈍使 and 利使 named later in this part.

"Indeterminate" (無記). A mental state that is neither wholesome nor unwholesome; here, blank dullness.

"The first detachment, the second station of mastery" (初背捨、二勝處). The first of the eight forms of detachment and the first two of the eight stations of mastery, both forms of the contemplation of impurity; the Chinese 二勝處 can mean either "the second station" or "two stations."

The three emptinesses (三空). Emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness, as gates to liberation.

The three buddha-bodies as remedies. The three karmic obstacles are remedied by recollecting, in turn, the response-body (方便淨應身), the reward-body (報佛), and the Dharma-body — a sequence that rises from form to the formless.

"The work of past offenses" (當亦是過去罪業所之作也). The wording 所之作 is irregular; the sense is clear.

The fifteen kinds of obstacle. Treated in full in the Shi Chan Boluomi's account of obstacles to dhyāna.

"And I saw the tathāgatas spontaneously attain the Buddha way." From the Lotus Sūtra, introductory chapter.

"Said that whatever others teach is false speech" (謂他所說悉為忘語). 忘 is read as 妄, "false."

The four extremes (四邊). The tetralemma just listed — exists, does not exist, both, neither — applied to the breath in the present, past, and future, as in the fourteen unanswered questions.

"The four kinds of sensation do not arise, the four [kinds of] activity do not arise" (不起四受，四行不生). 四受 may be the four kinds of sensation (painful, pleasant, neither, and their mental counterparts) or the four clingings (四取); 四行 may be the four postures of activity. The translation keeps the literal wording.

"Finishing off the old and not creating the new" (畢故不造新). Exhausting old karma without making new karma.

The willow and the flood. From the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra.
