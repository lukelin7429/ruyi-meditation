Revolving, Contemplating the Mind, Perfect Contemplation, and the Marks of Realization

The first six treatments of the six gates are, in the text's own words, "shared practices," open to ordinary people and the two lesser vehicles as well as bodhisattvas. This second part turns to what belongs to bodhisattvas alone. The seventh treatment, "revolving," moves from emptiness back out into the provisional: seeing that the breath is empty, the bodhisattva is still able to distinguish every worldly and world-transcending dharma, the Four Noble Truths, the twelve links, and the six perfections, and to teach them. The eighth, "contemplating the mind," is for those of great capacity, who skip the sequence and find all six gates in the mind itself. The ninth, "perfect contemplation," sees all dharmas in one thought and one dharma in all. The tenth sets out the marks of realization — sequential, mutual, revolving, and perfect — and ends by mapping the six gates onto the bodhisattva stages from the first abiding to wondrous awakening, closing with a chain of scriptural citations to the effect that beginning and end are not two.

7. The Six Gates Revolving

Chinese Source

次釋「第七、旋轉六妙門」。上來所說六妙門，悉是共行，與凡夫、二乘共故。今此旋轉六妙門者，唯獨菩薩所行，不與聲聞、緣覺共，況諸凡夫。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "Seventh, the six wondrous gates revolving." The six wondrous gates described so far are all shared practices, because they are shared with ordinary people and the two vehicles. The six wondrous gates revolving, described now, are practiced by bodhisattvas alone; they are not shared with hearers or solitary buddhas, let alone ordinary people.

Chinese Source

所以者何？前第六通別妙門觀中說名，從假入空觀，得惠眼一切智。慧眼一切智，是二乘、菩薩共法。今明從空出假旋轉六妙門，即是法眼道種智。法眼道種智，不與聲聞、辟支佛共。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why? The contemplation in the sixth, the shared and distinct wondrous gates, is called the contemplation moving from the provisional into emptiness; through it one attains the wisdom-eye and the wisdom that knows all things. The wisdom-eye and the wisdom that knows all things are dharmas shared by the two vehicles and bodhisattvas. Now I explain the six wondrous gates revolving, emerging from emptiness into the provisional; this is the dharma-eye and the wisdom of the paths. The dharma-eye and the wisdom of the paths are not shared with hearers or pratyekabuddhas.

Chinese Source

云何菩薩於數息道中，修從空出假觀，起旋轉出一切諸行功德相？

Ru-Yi Translation

How does a bodhisattva, on the path of counting the breath, cultivate the contemplation that emerges from emptiness into the provisional, and give rise to the marks of revolving out all practices and merits?

Chinese Source

所謂菩薩行者，當數息時，當發大誓願怜愍眾生，雖知眾生畢竟空，而欲成就眾生，淨佛國土，盡未來際。作是願已，即當了所數息不生不滅，其性空寂，即息是空，非息滅空。息性自空，息即是空，空即是息，離空無息，離息無空。一切諸法，亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

A bodhisattva practitioner, when counting the breath, should make a great vow out of compassion for beings: though knowing that beings are ultimately empty, he or she wishes to bring beings to maturity and purify the buddha-land to the end of the future. Having made this vow, one should understand that the breath being counted neither arises nor ceases; its nature is empty and still. The breath is itself empty; it is not that the breath ceases and then is empty. The nature of the breath is empty of itself: the breath is emptiness, emptiness is the breath; apart from emptiness there is no breath, apart from the breath there is no emptiness. All dharmas are likewise.

Chinese Source

息空故，非真、非假，非世間、非出世間，求息不得息與非息，而亦成就息念。其所成就息念，如夢、如幻、如響、如化，雖無實事可得，而亦分別幻化所作事。菩薩了息，亦復如是。雖無息性可得，而亦成就息念，從一至十，了了分明，深心分別如幻息相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because the breath is empty, it is neither the real nor the provisional, neither worldly nor world-transcending. Seeking the breath, one finds neither breath nor non-breath, and yet one also accomplishes mindfulness of the breath. The mindfulness of the breath that one accomplishes is like a dream, an illusion, an echo, a magical creation: though there is no real thing to be found, one still distinguishes what the illusion produces. The bodhisattva's understanding of the breath is likewise. Though no nature of breath can be found, one still accomplishes mindfulness of the breath, from one to ten, clearly and distinctly, and with a profound mind distinguishes the marks of the illusory breath.

Chinese Source

以有無性如幻息故，即有無性世間、出世間法。所以者何？無明顛倒，不知息性空故，妄計有息，即生人法執著、愛見諸行，故名世間。因有息故，即有陰、界、入等世間苦樂之果。當知息雖空，亦能成辦一切世間善惡因果、二十五有諸生死事。

Ru-Yi Translation

Because there is the natureless, illusory breath, there are the natureless worldly and world-transcending dharmas. Why? Through the inversion of ignorance, not knowing that the nature of the breath is empty, one falsely reckons that there is a breath, and then clinging to person and dharma, and the practices of craving and views, arise; therefore it is called worldly. Because there is a breath, there are the worldly fruits of suffering and pleasure — the aggregates, elements, sense-bases, and so on. One should know that although the breath is empty, it can bring about all the worldly causes and effects of good and evil, and all the affairs of birth and death in the twenty-five kinds of existence.

Chinese Source

復次，息相空中，雖無出世間相，而非不因息分別出世間法。所以者何？不知息相空故，即無明不了，造世間業；知息空無所有故，即無無明妄執，一切諸結煩惱無所從生，是名出世間因。因滅故，得離後世世間二十五有等果，名出世間果。能出世間顛倒因果法故，是名出世間法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, although within the emptiness of the marks of the breath there are no world-transcending marks, it is not that world-transcending dharmas are not distinguished by means of the breath. Why? Not knowing that the marks of the breath are empty, one does not understand, because of ignorance, and creates worldly karma. Knowing that the breath is empty and without anything, there is no ignorant false clinging, and all the knots and afflictions have nowhere to arise from; this is called the world-transcending cause. Because [the worldly] cause ceases, one is freed from the worldly fruits of the twenty-five kinds of existence in later lives; this is called the world-transcending fruit. Because it can transcend the inverted dharmas of worldly cause and fruit, it is called world-transcending dharma.

Chinese Source

於出世間真正法中，亦有因果。因者，知息空正智惠，為出世間因。妄計息中人我、無明顛倒及苦果滅故，名為出世間果。故知菩薩觀息非息，雖不得世間、出世間，亦能分別世間及出世間。

Ru-Yi Translation

Within the true and correct world-transcending dharma there is also cause and fruit. The cause: the correct wisdom that knows the breath is empty is the world-transcending cause. Because the false reckoning of a person and self in the breath, the inversion of ignorance, and the fruit of suffering cease, it is called the world-transcending fruit. So we know that the bodhisattva contemplates the breath as not the breath, and though finding neither the worldly nor the world-transcending, is still able to distinguish both the worldly and the world-transcending.

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩觀息性空時，不得四諦而亦通達四諦。所以者何？如上所說世間果者，即是苦諦；世間因者，即是集諦；出世間果者，即是滅諦；出世間因者，即是道諦。故觀於息相，不見四諦而能了了分別四諦，為聲聞眾生廣演分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, when the bodhisattva contemplates the nature of the breath as empty, he or she does not find the Four Noble Truths and yet penetrates them. Why? The worldly fruit described above is the truth of suffering; the worldly cause is the truth of origination; the world-transcending fruit is the truth of cessation; the world-transcending cause is the truth of the path. So in contemplating the marks of the breath, one does not see the Four Noble Truths and yet can clearly distinguish them, and expound and distinguish them at length for beings of the hearer vehicle.

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩了息空中，不見十二因緣而亦通達十二因緣。所以者何？過去息性空無所有，妄見有息而生種種顛倒分別，起諸煩惱，故名無明。無明因緣，則有行、識、名色、六入、觸、受、愛、取、有、生、老、死、憂悲苦惱等，輪轉不息，皆由不了息如虛空，無所有故。若知息空寂，即破無明，無明滅故，則十二因緣皆滅。菩薩如是了息非息，雖不得十二因緣，亦能了了通達十二因緣，為求緣覺乘人廣演分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the bodhisattva, within the understanding that the breath is empty, does not see the twelve links and yet penetrates them. Why? The nature of the past breath is empty and without anything; falsely seeing a breath, one gives rise to all kinds of inverted discrimination and gives rise to afflictions; therefore it is called ignorance. With ignorance as cause and condition, there are formations, consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense-bases, contact, sensation, craving, grasping, existence, birth, old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, suffering, and distress, revolving without rest — all because one does not understand that the breath is like space, without anything. If one knows that the breath is empty and still, ignorance is broken; because ignorance ceases, the twelve links all cease. Understanding the breath as not the breath in this way, the bodhisattva, though not finding the twelve links, is still able to penetrate them clearly, and expounds and distinguishes them at length for those who seek the vehicle of the solitary buddha.

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩了息無性。爾時尚不見有息，何況於息道中，見有六蔽及六度法。雖於息性中不見六蔽及六度法，而亦了了通達六蔽、六度。所以者何？行者當數息時，即自了知，若於非息之中而見息者，是必定成就慳貪蔽法。慳有四種：一者慳惜財物，見息中有我，為我生慳故；二者慳身，於息中起身見故；三者慳命，於息中不了，計有命故；四者慳法，於息中不了，即起見執法心生故。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the bodhisattva understands the breath as without nature. At that time he or she does not even see a breath, much less see, on the path of the breath, the six coverings and the dharmas of the six perfections. Though not seeing the six coverings and the six perfections within the nature of the breath, he or she still penetrates the six coverings and the six perfections clearly. Why? When counting the breath, the practitioner knows for himself or herself: if one sees a breath within what is not the breath, one will certainly bring about the covering of stinginess and greed. Stinginess is of four kinds: first, stinginess with wealth and possessions, because one sees a self in the breath and becomes stingy for the sake of that self; second, stinginess with the body, because one gives rise to the view of a body in the breath; third, stinginess with life, because, not understanding the breath, one reckons that there is a life; fourth, stinginess with the Dharma, because, not understanding the breath, the mind of views clinging to dharmas arises.

Chinese Source

行者為破壞如是慳蔽惡法故，修四種檀波羅密。一者知息空非我，離息亦無我，既不得我，聚諸財物，何所資給？爾時慳財之心，即便自息，捨諸珍寶如棄涕唾。當知了達息性，即是財施檀波羅蜜。

Ru-Yi Translation

To destroy these evil dharmas of the covering of stinginess, the practitioner cultivates four kinds of dāna-pāramitā. First, knowing that the breath is empty and not a self, and that apart from the breath there is also no self — since no self can be found, whom would amassing wealth and possessions supply? At that time the mind stingy with wealth comes to rest of itself, and one gives away treasures as if discarding spit. One should know that penetrating the nature of the breath is the dāna-pāramitā of giving wealth.

Chinese Source

復次，菩薩知無身性，息等諸法不名為身；離息等法，亦無別身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, the bodhisattva knows that there is no nature of a body: the breath and other dharmas are not called a body, and apart from the breath and other dharmas there is no separate body.

Chinese Source

爾時知身非身，即破慳身之執。既不慳於身，即能以身為奴僕給使，如法施與前人。當知了知息非息，即能具足成就捨身檀波羅蜜。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, knowing the body as not the body, one breaks the clinging of stinginess with the body. No longer stingy with the body, one can make one's body a servant to wait on others and give it, in accord with the Dharma, to the person before one. One should know that understanding the breath as not the breath is fully accomplishing the dāna-pāramitā of giving up the body.

Chinese Source

復次，行者若能了息性空，不見即息是命、離息有命，既不得命，破慳命心。爾時即能捨命給施眾生，心無驚畏。當知了達息空，即能具足捨命檀波羅蜜。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if the practitioner can understand that the nature of the breath is empty, he or she does not see that the breath is life, or that apart from the breath there is life. Since life cannot be found, the mind stingy with life is broken. At that time one can give up one's life to beings without fear. One should know that penetrating the emptiness of the breath is fully accomplishing the dāna-pāramitā of giving up life.

Chinese Source

復次，行者若達息空，即不見陰、入、界等諸法，亦不見世間、出世間種種法相。為破眾生種種橫計、迷執諸法、輪迴六趣故，有所說而實無說無示，以聽者無聞無得故。是時雖行法施，不執法施，無恩於彼而利一切。譬如大地、虛空、日、月，利益世間而無心於物，不求恩報。菩薩達息性空，行平等法施檀波羅蜜，利益眾生，亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if the practitioner penetrates the emptiness of the breath, he or she does not see the aggregates, sense-bases, elements, or other dharmas, nor the various dharma-marks of the worldly and the world-transcending. To break the various perverse reckonings of beings, their deluded clinging to dharmas, and their revolving in the six destinies, one teaches; yet in reality there is no teaching and no showing, because the hearer neither hears nor attains. At that time, though one practices the giving of the Dharma, one does not cling to the giving of the Dharma; one makes no claim of kindness on others, yet benefits all. It is like the great earth, space, the sun, and the moon, which benefit the world without any intention toward things and seek no repayment. The bodhisattva who penetrates the empty nature of the breath and practices the equal dāna-pāramitā of giving the Dharma, benefiting beings, is likewise.

Chinese Source

當知菩薩知息性空，不得慳、度，而能了了分別慳、度，以不可得故。知息性空，具足尸羅、羼提、毘梨耶、禪那、般若波羅蜜，亦復如是。是中應一一廣旋轉諸波羅蜜相，為求佛道善男子、善女人，開示分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

One should know that the bodhisattva who knows the nature of the breath as empty does not find stinginess or its perfection, and yet can clearly distinguish stinginess and its perfection, because they cannot be found. Knowing the nature of the breath as empty, one is complete in śīla-, kṣānti-, vīrya-, dhyāna-, and prajñā-pāramitā in the same way. Here one should revolve the marks of each of the pāramitās at length, and disclose and distinguish them for the good men and good women who seek the Buddha way.

Chinese Source

是即略說於數息門中，修旋轉陀羅尼菩薩所行無礙方便。菩薩若入是門，直說數息調心，窮劫不盡，況復於隨、止、觀、還、淨等，種種諸禪，智惠、神通，四辯、力、無所畏、諸地行願、一切種智、無盡一切功德，旋轉分別而可盡乎？

Ru-Yi Translation

This briefly describes the unobstructed skillful means practiced by bodhisattvas who cultivate the revolving dhāraṇī within the gate of counting the breath. If a bodhisattva enters this gate, merely describing counting the breath and tuning the mind could not be finished in an eon — how much less could one finish revolving and distinguishing following, stilling, contemplating, returning, purity, the various dhyānas, wisdom, spiritual powers, the four kinds of eloquence, the powers, the fearlessnesses, the practices and vows of the grounds, the wisdom knowing all modes, and all inexhaustible merits?

8. The Six Gates of Contemplating the Mind

Chinese Source

次釋「第八、觀心六妙門」。觀心六妙門者，此為大根行人，善識法要，不由次第，懸照諸法之源。何等為諸法之源？所謂眾生心也。一切萬法由心而起，若能反觀心性，不可得心源，即知萬法皆無根本。約此觀心說六妙門，非如前也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "Eighth, the six wondrous gates of contemplating the mind." The six wondrous gates of contemplating the mind are for practitioners of great capacity who recognize the essentials of the Dharma well and, without going through a sequence, illuminate from afar the source of all dharmas. What is the source of all dharmas? It is the mind of sentient beings. All the myriad dharmas arise from the mind. If one can turn back and contemplate the nature of the mind, and the source of the mind cannot be found, one knows that the myriad dharmas are all without root. The six wondrous gates explained in terms of this contemplation of the mind are not like those before.

Chinese Source

所以者何？如行者初學觀心時，知一切世間、出世間諸數量法，皆悉從心出，離心之外更無一法，是則數一切法，皆悉約心故數。當知心者，即是數門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Why? When the practitioner first learns to contemplate the mind, he or she knows that all the countable dharmas, worldly and world-transcending, come from the mind, and that apart from the mind there is not a single dharma. Thus counting all dharmas is counting them in terms of the mind. One should know that the mind is the gate of counting.

Chinese Source

復次，行者當觀心時，知一切數量之法，悉隨心王。若無心王，即無心數。心王動故，心數亦動。譬如百官臣民悉皆隨順大王，一切諸數量法依隨心王，亦復如是。如是觀時，即知心是隨門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, when contemplating the mind, the practitioner knows that all countable dharmas follow the mind-king. If there were no mind-king, there would be no mental factors. Because the mind-king moves, the mental factors move as well. Just as the hundred officials, ministers, and people all follow the great king, so all countable dharmas rely on and follow the mind-king. Contemplating in this way, one knows that the mind is the gate of following.

Chinese Source

復次，行者當觀心時，知心性常寂，即諸法亦寂，寂故不念，不念故即不動，不動故名止也。當知心者，即是止門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, when contemplating the mind, the practitioner knows that the nature of the mind is always still, and so all dharmas are still as well. Because they are still, there is no thinking; because there is no thinking, there is no movement; because there is no movement, it is called stilling. One should know that the mind is the gate of stilling.

Chinese Source

復次，行者當觀心時，覺了心性猶如虛空，無名無相，一切語言道斷，開無明藏，見真實性，於一切諸法得無著惠。當知心者，即是觀門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, when contemplating the mind, the practitioner realizes that the nature of the mind is like space, without name or mark, where the path of all language is cut off; he or she opens the treasury of ignorance, sees the true nature, and attains unattached wisdom toward all dharmas. One should know that the mind is the gate of contemplating.

Chinese Source

復次，行者當觀心時，既不得所觀之心，亦不得能觀之智。爾時心如虛空無所依倚，以無著妙惠，雖不見諸法，而還通達一切諸法，分別顯示。入諸法界，無所缺減，普現色身，垂形九道，入變通藏，集諸善根，迴向菩提，莊嚴佛道。當知心者，即是還門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, when contemplating the mind, the practitioner finds neither the mind that is contemplated nor the wisdom that contemplates. At that time the mind is like space, relying on nothing. With unattached, wondrous wisdom, though not seeing dharmas, one returns and penetrates all dharmas, distinguishing and revealing them. Entering all the dharma-realms without lack, one manifests form-bodies everywhere, lets down one's form into the nine destinies, enters the treasury of transformations and spiritual powers, gathers wholesome roots, dedicates them to bodhi, and adorns the Buddha way. One should know that the mind is the gate of returning.

Chinese Source

復次，行者當觀心時，雖不得心及諸法，而能了了分別一切諸法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, when contemplating the mind, though the practitioner finds neither the mind nor dharmas, he or she can clearly distinguish all dharmas.

Chinese Source

雖分別一切法，不著一切法，成就一切法，不染一切法。以自性清淨，從本以來，不為無明惑倒之所染故。故經云：「心不染煩惱，煩惱不染心。」行者通達自性清淨心故，入於垢法，不為垢法所染，故名為淨。當知心者，即是淨門。如是六門，不由次第，直觀心性，即便具足也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Though distinguishing all dharmas, one does not cling to any dharma; accomplishing all dharmas, one is not stained by any dharma; because the self-nature is pure and, from the very beginning, has not been stained by ignorance, delusion, or inversion. So a sūtra says: "The mind is not stained by afflictions; afflictions do not stain the mind." Because the practitioner penetrates the mind whose self-nature is pure, he or she enters defiled dharmas without being stained by them; therefore it is called purity. One should know that the mind is the gate of purity. In this way the six gates, without going through a sequence, are complete simply by directly contemplating the nature of the mind.

9. The Six Gates of Perfect Contemplation

Chinese Source

次釋「第九、圓觀六妙門」。夫圓觀者，豈得如上所說，但觀心源具足六妙門，觀餘諸法不得爾乎？

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining "Ninth, the six wondrous gates of perfect contemplation." As for perfect contemplation: how could it be as described above, where only in contemplating the source of the mind are the six wondrous gates complete, and in contemplating other dharmas it is not so?

Chinese Source

今行者觀一心，見一切心及一切法；觀一法，見一切法及一切心。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now the practitioner contemplates one mind and sees all minds and all dharmas; contemplates one dharma and sees all dharmas and all minds.

Chinese Source

觀菩提，見一切煩惱生死；觀煩惱生死，見一切菩提涅槃。觀一佛，見一切眾生及諸佛；觀一眾生，見一切佛及一切眾生。一切皆如影現，非內非外，不一不異，十方不可思議，本性自爾，無能作者。

Ru-Yi Translation

Contemplating bodhi, one sees all afflictions and birth and death; contemplating afflictions and birth and death, one sees all bodhi and nirvāṇa. Contemplating one buddha, one sees all beings and all buddhas; contemplating one being, one sees all buddhas and all beings. All appear like reflections, neither inside nor outside, neither one nor different, inconceivable in the ten directions; their original nature is so of itself, and there is no one who makes them.

Chinese Source

What are called the marks of realizing revolving practice? Realization is of two kinds. When seeing in this way, the eye-faculty and its consciousness are still and unmoving; therefore it is called the gate of stilling. Not seeing the buddha-lands in terms of two marks, one penetrates without obstruction, distinguishes skillfully, and illuminates the Dharma-nature; therefore it is called the gate of contemplating. Returning, within the realm of the eye-faculty, one penetrates the ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind; the realms of all the faculties are clear and unobstructed, because they are marked as neither one nor different; therefore it is called the gate of returning. Further, seeing that the realm of one's own eye-faculty appears in turn within the eye-realms of the ordinary and the noble in the ten directions — this too is called the gate of returning. Though one penetrates clearly and sees such things, one does not give rise to deluded thinking and discrimination. Knowing that the original nature is always pure, with no dharma that can stain it, one does not abide, does not cling, and does not give rise to love of the Dharma; therefore it is called the gate of purity. This briefly describes the marks of approximate realization of the six wondrous gates within the purity of the eye-faculty. The other five faculties are likewise.

Reading Notes for Part 2

Translator's notes

"Shared practices" (共行). The first six treatments are said to be shared with ordinary people and the two vehicles; the seventh through tenth are for bodhisattvas. This tracks the Tiantai sequence of the three contemplations: from the provisional into emptiness (shared, the wisdom-eye), from emptiness out into the provisional (the bodhisattva's, the dharma-eye), and the middle (the perfect contemplation of the ninth and tenth treatments).

Revolving (旋轉). To turn one insight around so that it opens onto every other dharma. Here the single insight that the breath is empty is "revolved" to yield the worldly and the world-transcending, the Four Noble Truths, the twelve links, and the six perfections. The "revolving dhāraṇī" (旋轉陀羅尼, 旋陀羅尼) is one of the three dhāraṇīs named in the Lotus Sūtra's chapter on Samantabhadra.

"The breath is itself empty; it is not that the breath ceases and then is empty" (即息是空，非息滅空). Modeled on the Vimalakīrti Sūtra's "form is itself empty; it is not that form ceases and then is empty."

The six coverings (六蔽). Stinginess, breaking precepts, anger, laziness, distraction, and foolishness — the opposites of the six perfections. Only the first is worked out in detail, with four kinds of giving to answer four kinds of stinginess; the text then says the other five perfections are "likewise."

"Does not find stinginess or its perfection" (不得慳、度). 度 here is the perfection (pāramitā) of giving that answers stinginess.

The mind-king and the mental factors (心王／心數). 心數 is an older term for the mental factors (later 心所). The text plays on 數, which is also the word for "counting": the mental factors "follow" the mind-king, so the mind itself is the gate of following.

The nine destinies (九道). The nine realms other than buddhahood among the ten dharma-realms.

"The mind is not stained by afflictions; afflictions do not stain the mind." Compare the Śrīmālādevī Sūtra, which says that afflictions do not touch the mind and the mind does not touch afflictions, in its teaching on the mind whose self-nature is pure.

"One vehicle" (一乘) in "lesser bodhisattvas or [those of] one vehicle." The text has 一乘, which ordinarily means the one Buddha-vehicle; here, set beside "lesser bodhisattvas," it seems to mean followers of a single [lesser] vehicle. Kept as written.

"Enters the Tathāgata's room, wears the Tathāgata's robe, sits on the Tathāgata's seat." From the Lotus Sūtra's chapter on the teacher of the Dharma: the room is great compassion, the robe is gentleness and patience, the seat is the emptiness of all dharmas.

"At the time of the first resolve, one attains perfect awakening." From the Avataṃsaka Sūtra's chapter on the merit of the first resolve.

The sixteen touches (十六觸). Physical sensations that arise as the first dhyāna begins (movement, itching, cold, warmth, lightness, heaviness, roughness, smoothness, and their eight counterparts), treated in this site's Shi Chan Boluomi.

"Dark realizations … concealed, indeterminate, and defiled" (闇證、隱沒、無記、有垢). Signs of concentration that are still unclear or mixed; the same terms are used for unclear dhyāna in the Shi Chan Boluomi.

"[The section on] inner skillful means in seated meditation, examining the roots of good and evil." A cross-reference to the Shi Chan Boluomi (its section on inner skillful means, 內方便, and verifying good and bad roots), which treats this at length.

The two realizations (解證／會證). Realization in understanding, and realization by "meeting" or fitting together with the dharma-realm.

The purity of the six faculties. The Lotus Sūtra's chapter on the merits of the Dharma teacher, on this site "The Purified Six Senses." Each of the six gates is shown in the purified eye alone, and the text says the other five faculties are the same.

The bodhisattva stages. The ten abidings, ten practices, ten dedications, ten grounds, equal awakening, and wondrous awakening of the perfect teaching. "The abiding of the initial resolve" (初發心住) is the first of the ten abidings; the text then calls it "the first ground" (初地), following the usage in which the first abiding of the perfect teaching corresponds to the first ground of the distinct teaching.

The letters a and ḍha (阿字門, 茶字門). The first and last of the forty-two letters of the arapacana syllabary in the Large Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra, standing for the first and the final stage. This site's Mohe Zhiguan renders 荼 as "the last syllable."

"The three worthy and ten noble stages …" From the Yingluo Sūtra (菩薩瓔珞本業經), on the bodhisattva stages.

"The initial resolve and the ultimate are not two …" From the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra, Kāśyapa chapter (verses in praise of the Buddha).

"Such is the ultimate consistency of root and branch" (如是本末究竟等). The last of the ten suchnesses in the Lotus Sūtra's chapter on skillful means; rendered as on this site's Mohe Zhiguan.
