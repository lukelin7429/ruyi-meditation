Fascicle Four: The Emptiness Chapter

The fourth fascicle of Zhiyi's Textual Commentary covers a single chapter of the Golden Light Sūtra, Chapter 5, "Emptiness" — forty-five lines of verse that he reads with great care. The opening explains why the chapter speaks only of "emptiness": of four kinds of emptiness it means the extremes themselves being empty, and six phrases show how emptiness and "neither existent nor nonexistent" refute, cultivate, and are identical to one another. Zhiyi then defends the sūtra's claim that other sūtras have taught emptiness "at length" and explains what "brief" and "dull" mean here. The body of the commentary follows the verse in two movements. The first sets out the object: the body as an "empty village," the six faculties, twelve entrances, and eighteen elements, the four great elements as four snakes in a basket, and the decaying corpse as an aid to the path. The second sets out the contemplation: the emptiness of beings and of dharmas, contrasted as analyzed (Small Vehicle and shared teaching) and embodied (distinct teaching), the twenty and forty views of the body, and the twelve links read through the image of a whirling fire-brand. The fascicle ends with the fruit — wisdom and severance fulfilled — mapped onto the stages of several sūtras, and the four universal vows. Lemmas follow this site's translation of the sūtra (Part 1).

Explaining the Chapter "Emptiness"

Chinese Source

夫空者，應有四種，謂滅色入空、即色是空、滅邊入空、即邊是空。此經通諸乘懺悔，應須四種空，而今品但標空者，專是即邊而空也。何故爾？經云「無量餘經已廣說空」，是故此中略而解說也。又空者，空有、空無。空有者，空二十五之塊有。空無者，空二乘之灰無。兩邊清淨，名之為空。直作此說，惑者迷名濫理，不能超悟。今作六句分別：空破非有非無、非有非無破空、空修非有非無、非有非無修空、空即非有非無、非有非無即空。

Ru-Yi Translation

Emptiness ought to be of four kinds, namely: entering emptiness by extinguishing form; form itself being empty; entering emptiness by extinguishing the extremes; and the extremes themselves being empty. Since the repentance of this sūtra is common to all the vehicles, it ought to require the four kinds of emptiness; yet the present chapter announces only "emptiness," meaning solely the extremes themselves being empty. Why so? The sūtra says, "Immeasurable other sūtras have already taught emptiness at length"; therefore here it is explained briefly. Further, emptiness empties existence and empties nonexistence. Emptying existence empties the lump-like existence of the twenty-five [existences]. Emptying nonexistence empties the ash-like nonexistence of the two vehicles. Both extremes being pure is called emptiness. If one simply says this, the deluded confuse the names and blur the principle and cannot rise to awakening. Now we distinguish six phrases: emptiness refutes neither-existent-nor-nonexistent; neither-existent-nor-nonexistent refutes emptiness; emptiness cultivates neither-existent-nor-nonexistent; neither-existent-nor-nonexistent cultivates emptiness; emptiness is identical to neither-existent-nor-nonexistent; neither-existent-nor-nonexistent is identical to emptiness.

Ru-Yi Translation

Emptiness refuting neither-existent-nor-nonexistent: the view of neither-existent-nor-nonexistent held by ordinary and heretical people, the realization of neither-existent-nor-nonexistent in which the two vehicles dwell one-sidedly, and the gate of neither-existent-nor-nonexistent clung to in the teaching-path of the distinct teaching are all refuted by the emptiness of the "Emptiness" chapter. The views of ordinary and heretical people are of many kinds: first, the simple fourfold views; second, the compound fourfold views; third, the complete fourfold views. The simple fourfold views can be understood. The compound fourfold views are: existence-existence and existence-nonexistence; nonexistence-existence and nonexistence-nonexistence; both-existence-and-nonexistence existence and both-existence-and-nonexistence nonexistence; neither-existence-nor-nonexistence existence and neither-existence-nor-nonexistence nonexistence — these are the compound fourfold views. The complete fourfold views: within one phrase all four phrases are present; four fours are sixteen phrases; hence they are called complete. Whether simple, compound, or complete, all are vast suffering and accumulation. Though they reckon "neither existent nor nonexistent," it is in fact a deluded view; hence it is refuted by the emptiness of the "Emptiness" chapter.

Ru-Yi Translation

The realization of neither-existent-nor-nonexistent in which the two vehicles dwell one-sidedly: because they have cut off the view of permanence it is called "not existent"; because they have cut off the view of annihilation it is called "not nonexistent." The two views of existence and nonexistence are extinguished without remainder, the views and habituated thought of the three realms are forever exhausted; they form the thought that they have been delivered and the thought that they are at peace — "the pure life is established, what was to be done is done, there is no further existence." They keep this and that is all, and no longer advance to seek the three bodhis. But when the two vehicles give rise to the true, cutting off the view of permanence and the view of annihilation, their gates are not the same: some enter by the gate of existence, as in the Abhidharma; some by the gate of nonexistence, as in the Satyasiddhi Treatise; some by the gate of both existence and nonexistence, as in the Kunle; some by the gate of neither existence nor nonexistence, as in the Sūtra [Taught] to Kātyāyana. Departing from annihilation and departing from permanence is called the sages' middle way. The four gates all cut off annihilation and permanence and are called the same middle way; in fact they keep to one side and grasp realization; hence they are refuted by the emptiness of the "Emptiness" chapter.

別教教道執非有非無門者，佛為鈍根菩薩方便權巧作四門說中道，如彼栰喻。不得意者，四門成諍。故《涅槃》云「真善妙有大般涅槃空，佛性亦色、非色、非色非非色。若各執一門，則於如來有諍訟心，不見中道。」執此教門，猶為〈空品〉空所破也。新本云「初地菩薩欲行有相道」，斯即一門之意也，文中悉有四門之說。《大經》云「自此之前，我等悉名邪見人也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The gate of neither-existent-nor-nonexistent clung to in the teaching-path of the distinct teaching: for bodhisattvas of dull faculties the Buddha, with expedient skill, taught the middle way through four gates, as in the simile of the raft. Those who do not grasp the intent turn the four gates into disputes. Hence the Nirvāṇa says: "True, good, wondrous existence; the emptiness of great parinirvāṇa; buddha-nature is also form, non-form, neither form nor non-form. If each clings to one gate, they have a mind of dispute toward the Tathāgata and do not see the middle way." Clinging to this teaching-gate is still refuted by the emptiness of the "Emptiness" chapter. The new version says, "The bodhisattva of the first ground wishes to practice the path of marks"; this is the intent of one gate, and the text contains accounts of all four gates. The Great Sūtra says, "Before this, we were all called people of wrong views."

Ru-Yi Translation

Neither-existent-nor-nonexistent refuting emptiness: the empty view of ordinary and heretical people, the empty realization of the two vehicles, and the empty gate of the teaching-path all fall into the two extremes; hence they are refuted by the middle way, neither existent nor nonexistent. Mutual cultivation: the view of emptiness, the realization of emptiness, and the emptiness of the teaching-path should cultivate the middle way, neither existent nor nonexistent. The view of neither-existent-nor-nonexistent, the realization of neither-existent-nor-nonexistent, and the teaching-gate of neither-existent-nor-nonexistent should cultivate the emptiness of the middle way. Mutual identity: the emptiness that refutes the two extremes is identical to the middle way, neither existent nor nonexistent; the middle way, neither existent nor nonexistent, is identical to the emptiness that refutes the two extremes, not two and not different. "Prajñā is one dharma; the Buddha speaks of it by all kinds of names." Emptiness is identical to neither-existent-nor-nonexistent; it is not named "neither existent nor nonexistent" because it is spoken of briefly; hence it says the "Emptiness" chapter.

此品來意者，〈懺品〉破惡、〈讚品〉生善，〈空品〉導成滅惡生善也。亦是導成用宗體等，故《釋論》云「若以無此空，一切無所作。」導成上品，故明〈空品〉也。又常果顯體，滅惡生善非不明空，利者已解，為鈍根故起大悲心，更明五陰生法本性空寂爾◎

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the intent of this chapter's coming: the "Repentance" chapter destroys evil, the "Praise" chapter produces good, and the "Emptiness" chapter guides and accomplishes the extinguishing of evil and the producing of good. It also guides and accomplishes the function, the cardinal doctrine, the essence, and the rest. Hence the Great Treatise says, "If there were no such emptiness, nothing could be done." Because it guides and accomplishes the chapters above, it makes clear the "Emptiness" chapter. Further, the permanent fruit reveals the essence, and the extinguishing of evil and producing of good do not fail to make clear emptiness; the sharp have already understood, but for the sake of those of dull faculties [the Buddha] arouses a mind of great compassion and further makes clear that the five aggregates, beings and dharmas, are by original nature empty and still.

Why Emptiness Is Taught Briefly

Chinese Source

此品有四十五行偈，分為二：初四行半敘欲說空、次四十行半正說空。無量餘經者，指廣而明略，亦是標略以顯廣。若指《般若》，則此經非方等攝；若不指《般若》，諸經不廣明空義。此復云何？答：諸經前分結集人應作次第，而其後分皆攝入前例。且舉一以類諸。如《阿含經》云「佛將涅槃，舍利弗不忍見佛滅，前佛而去。均頭頭擎衣鉢來至佛所。」此是窮後之事。而在十二年前《阿含》中集，當知《阿含》後分至《涅槃》也。又方等次第在《法華》前，而云「先於靈山已為聲聞授記」，豈非方等至於《涅槃》。又《大品》次第在《法華》前，《釋論》云「須菩提於法華中授記故，而諸菩薩為畢定？為不畢定？」當知《般若》亦至《涅槃》。

Ru-Yi Translation

This chapter has forty-five lines of verse, divided into two: the first four and a half lines narrate the wish to teach emptiness; the next forty and a half lines teach emptiness proper. "Immeasurable other sūtras" points to the extensive to make clear the brief; it is also announcing the brief to reveal the extensive. [Question:] If it points to the Prajñā[pāramitā], then this sūtra is not included in the vaipulya [period]; if it does not point to the Prajñā, the [other] sūtras do not make clear the meaning of emptiness extensively. How is this? Answer: In the earlier part of each sūtra the compilers should arrange things in sequence, but its later part they all draw into the earlier, by analogy. Let us raise one and infer the others. As the Āgama says: "When the Buddha was about to enter nirvāṇa, Śāriputra could not bear to see the Buddha's extinction and departed before the Buddha. Cunda carried his robe and bowl and came to where the Buddha was." This is an event at the very end, yet it is compiled in the Āgamas of the first twelve years; you should know that the later part of the Āgamas reaches to the Nirvāṇa. Further, in sequence the vaipulya [sūtras] come before the Lotus, yet they say, "Earlier, on Vulture Peak, predictions had already been given to the śrāvakas" — is this not the vaipulya reaching to the Nirvāṇa? Further, in sequence the Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] comes before the Lotus, yet the Great Treatise says, "Because Subhūti received a prediction in the Lotus, are the bodhisattvas certain or not certain?" You should know that the Prajñā also reaches to the Nirvāṇa.

以此推之，言次第者是前分也，互相指者是後分也。結集者以後分明義氣類若同，向前集之；或者不知言乖次第，實不乖也。此經屬方等後分，指《般若》為廣說，於義無妨。略而解說者，佛有略廣二門，應作四句分別：或名義俱廣，如十八空、二十空、二十四空是也；或名義俱略，如一獨空是也；或名廣義略，如法性、實相、實際、如如、法界等眾多名，共名一義是也；或名略義廣，如生法二空之名，而義大廣。迄從凡地至于極佛皆名眾生。《釋論》云「眾生無上者佛是」。從凡夫之五陰極至佛地，亦稱色解脫、受想行識解脫。《釋論》云「法無上者涅槃是」。

Ru-Yi Translation

Inferring from this, what is called "sequence" is the earlier part; what points from one to another is the later part. The compilers collected toward the front whatever in the later part clarified a meaning of the same kind; some, not knowing this, say it goes against the sequence, but in fact it does not. This sūtra belongs to the later part of the vaipulya [period]; pointing to the Prajñā as the extensive teaching does no harm to the meaning. "Explain it briefly": the Buddha has the two gates of brief and extensive, which should be distinguished in four phrases. Sometimes both name and meaning are extensive, as with the eighteen emptinesses, the twenty emptinesses, the twenty-four emptinesses. Sometimes both name and meaning are brief, as with the single "sole emptiness." Sometimes the name is extensive and the meaning brief, as with dharma-nature, true mark, reality-limit, suchness, dharma-realm, and many other names sharing one meaning. Sometimes the name is brief and the meaning extensive, as with the name "twofold emptiness of beings and dharmas," whose meaning is very broad: all, reaching from the ordinary ground to the ultimate buddha, are called beings — the Great Treatise says, "Among beings the unsurpassed is the Buddha"; from the five aggregates of the ordinary being to the buddha ground, it is also called the liberation of form and the liberation of feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness — the Great Treatise says, "Among dharmas the unsurpassed is nirvāṇa."

今言略說生法二空，下文云「五陰舍宅，觀悉空寂。善女當觀何處有人及以眾生？」即其義也。眾生根鈍者根緣不同，或廣說得悟名利根，或略說得悟名鈍根。此語似倒？身子一聞得悟，是略是利；目連再聞得悟，是廣是鈍。此經意不就得悟邊明利鈍，乃是聞持邊論利鈍。利人廣聞則能持，鈍人略聞方能持。今機但有得悟之機，無有廣持之機，故言不能廣知也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now it speaks briefly of the twofold emptiness of beings and dharmas; the text below says, "The dwelling of the five aggregates I contemplate as all empty and still," and "Good woman, you should contemplate . where is there a person or a sentient being?" This is the meaning. "Beings' faculties are dull": faculties and conditions differ. Some attain awakening by hearing extensive teaching and are called of sharp faculties; some attain awakening by hearing brief teaching and are called of dull faculties. Does this statement seem reversed? Śāriputra attained awakening on a single hearing — brief and sharp; Maudgalyāyana attained awakening on hearing twice — extensive and dull. The intent of this sūtra is not to make clear sharp and dull with regard to attaining awakening, but to discuss sharp and dull with regard to hearing and retaining. The sharp, hearing extensively, can retain it; the dull can retain only on hearing briefly. The present capacities have only the capacity to attain awakening, not the capacity to retain extensively; hence it says "cannot know broadly."

Chinese Source

無量空義者，二乘真諦是有量空義，菩薩中道是無量空義。此經明法性實相，即是無量空義也。異妙方便者，即是悉檀方便，巧作上來辦果明因、滅惡生善種種分別等是也。起大悲心者，一段眾生著有病重，故大悲亦重也。我今演說者，演名為廣，與略相違。上論生法二空是名略，今論生法二義，故言演爾。知眾生意者，知此一機樂略宜略、對略悟略，故言知意也。敘欲說空意竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

"The immeasurable meanings of emptiness": the truth of the real of the two vehicles is a measured meaning of emptiness; the middle way of the bodhisattvas is the immeasurable meaning of emptiness. This sūtra makes clear the dharma-nature and the true mark, which is the immeasurable meaning of emptiness. "Different and wondrous skillful means" are the skillful means of the siddhāntas, skillfully making all the distinctions above — establishing the fruit and making clear the cause, extinguishing evil and producing good, and so on. "Arousing a mind of great compassion": a group of beings are heavily ill with attachment to existence; hence the great compassion is also heavy. "I now proclaim": "proclaim" (演) means extensive, the opposite of brief. Above, the twofold emptiness of beings and dharmas was discussed, which is called brief; now the two meanings of beings and dharmas are discussed; hence "proclaim." "Knowing the intentions of beings": knowing that this one capacity delights in the brief, suits the brief, faces the brief, and awakens through the brief; hence "knowing the intentions." The narration of the wish to teach emptiness is finished.

The Object of Emptiness: The Body and the Six Entrances

Chinese Source

從「是身虛偽」下四十行半偈，是第二正明於空。又為二：初從「身偽」下十九行半，明生法二空境；二從「善女當觀」下二十一行，明生法二空觀。無境觀不正、無觀境不顯，應引《止觀》中十番檢境智明不可思議。下文云「不可思議智境，不可思議智照」，新本云「如如法，如如智」，即其義也。明空境又二：初從「是身虛偽」下十七行半明實法境、二從「水火風種」下二行明假想境。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "this body is false" on, forty and a half lines of verse, is the second, making clear emptiness proper. It again has two: first, from "this body is false" on, nineteen and a half lines, making clear the object of the twofold emptiness of beings and dharmas; second, from "Good woman, you should contemplate" on, twenty-one lines, making clear the contemplation of the twofold emptiness of beings and dharmas. Without the object the contemplation is not correct; without the contemplation the object is not revealed. One should cite the tenfold examination of object and wisdom in Calming and Contemplation to make clear the inconceivable. The text below says, "inconceivable sphere of wisdom, inconceivable illumination of wisdom"; the new version says, "the dharma of suchness, the wisdom of suchness"; this is the meaning. Making clear the object of emptiness again has two: first, from "this body is false" on, seventeen and a half lines, making clear the object of real dharmas; second, from "the seeds of water, fire, and wind" on, two lines, making clear the object of provisional visualization.

Chinese Source

實法者，觀五陰無法，觀十六無人。空觀詣理，故名實法；身雖未死，虛假臭穢，故名假想。亦名慧行、行行。緣空直入，名為慧行；帶事兼修，名為行行。亦名正道、助道。空觀順理，名為正道；不淨破貪，名為助道。小乘修正道斷結，名慧解脫人；修助道斷結，名俱解脫人。《大品》明菩薩發心與薩婆若相應者是修正道，遊戲神通淨佛國土者是修助道。《法華》名為大車儐從，即此意也。《涅槃》明正慧遠離，遠離十相，住大涅槃。又諦觀白骨，破二十五有，成王三昧。眾經同論此二義，而互有廣略。今經略明，正助意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Real dharmas: contemplating the five aggregates as without dharmas, contemplating the sixteen [knowers and views] as without a person. The contemplation of emptiness reaches principle, hence "real dharma"; though the body has not yet died, it is false, provisional, foul, and filthy, hence "provisional visualization." They are also called the practice of wisdom and the practice of conduct: taking emptiness as object and entering directly is called the practice of wisdom; cultivating with phenomena alongside is called the practice of conduct. They are also called the correct path and the aiding path: the contemplation of emptiness accords with principle and is called the correct path; [the contemplation of] impurity breaks greed and is called the aiding path. In the Small Vehicle one who cultivates the correct path and cuts off the fetters is called a person liberated by wisdom; one who cultivates the aiding path and cuts off the fetters is called a person liberated both ways. The Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] makes clear that the bodhisattva's arousing the aspiration in accord with sarvajñatā is cultivating the correct path, and that playing with spiritual powers and purifying buddha-lands is cultivating the aiding path. The Lotus calls it the great cart and its retinue; this is the intent. The Nirvāṇa makes clear that correct wisdom departs far — departing far from the ten marks — and dwells in great nirvāṇa. Further, contemplating the white bones attentively breaks the twenty-five existences and accomplishes the king samādhi. Many sūtras alike discuss these two meanings, each more extensively or more briefly. The present sūtra makes clear, briefly, the intent of the correct and the aiding.

Lemmas. Quotations from the sūtra follow this site's Golden Light Sūtra, Part 1 (Chapter 5); other chapters are cited from Parts 1 and 4 ("roaming in the immeasurable, very deep dharma-nature," "to seek quiescent extinction, unsurpassed nirvāṇa"). The new version. "Greedy desire brings old age ." is from the Auspicious Response as cited by Zhiyi (also in his Vimalakīrti commentary, T1778).
