Fascicle Six: The Right Treatise to Praising the Buddha

The sixth and last fascicle explains Chapters 11 to 18. "The Right Treatise" is named through the four siddhāntas and read as Brahmā's answer to four questions about why a human king is called a "son of the gods"; Zhiyi draws out six meanings (protection, shared virtue, holding happiness, blocking evil, father and mother, showing cause and effect) and shows how a bad king loses all of them. "Good Gathering" is read through the six perfections, "Ghosts and Spirits" through the three buddhas and the "perfect offering" of listening to the sūtra, followed by Chinese glosses of the spirits' names. "Predictions" answers the tree spirit's doubt, and "Curing Illness," following Paramārtha's commentary, sets out the medical verses on seasons, humors, diet, and treatment in detail, ending with a contemplation of the three poisons as inner illness. Jalavāhana's two "abilities" (making water flow and giving water), the reasons the prince gave up his body to the tigress, and the sequence of all eighteen chapters as praise of the Golden Light close the commentary.

Chapter 11: The Right Treatise

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Chapter "The Right Treatise"

Chinese Source

正論者，正名為聖。聖有二種：一世聖、二出世聖。論名覈實，一覈事實、二覈理實。此品是先王舊法，世世相傳。先王傳力尊相，力尊相傳於信相，信相又傳其子，其子又傳於後世。世世正聖、世世善實，即是先王之法言，亦是世界悉檀立名，名〈正論品〉。王行此法，法律相應，陰陽以之調、日月以之順、百穀以之豐、萬民以之樂、社稷以之安、治化以之美，即是民用和睦上下無怨，亦是為人悉檀立名，名〈正論品。王用此法外敵不敢謀。內姦不敢驚。妖星不敢現〉。惡虹不敢行、暴風不敢動、疾雨不敢零，是則禍亂不作、災害不生，亦是對治悉檀立名，名〈正論品〉。此之世善本金光明，從金光明出此正論，善用此正論，天宮天身以之光明、天力天威以之增長、天心為之倍樂、天之法味為之倍更深遠，即是先王之至德要道，亦是第一義悉檀立名，名〈正論品〉也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Right treatise": "right" means sage. Sages are of two kinds: first, worldly sages; second, world-transcending sages. "Treatise" means examining the real: first, examining the reality of affairs; second, examining the reality of principle. This chapter is the old law of the former kings, handed down generation after generation. A former king handed it to Power-Honored Mark, Power-Honored Mark handed it to Faith-Mark, Faith-Mark again handed it to his son, and his son again handed it to later generations. Generation after generation right and sagely, generation after generation good and real — this is the Dharma words of the former kings, and it is also naming by the mundane siddhānta: the chapter "The Right Treatise." When a king practices this law, law and statute accord with each other; by it yin and yang are harmonized, sun and moon keep their order, the hundred grains are abundant, the myriad people are happy, the altars of the state are secure, and governing and transforming are fine; this is "the people living in harmony, with no resentment between above and below," and it is also naming by the individual siddhānta: the chapter "The Right Treatise." When a king uses this law, outer enemies dare not plot, inner traitors dare not alarm, baleful stars dare not appear, evil rainbows dare not move, violent winds dare not stir, sudden rains dare not fall; then disorder does not arise and calamities are not born; this is also naming by the therapeutic siddhānta: the chapter "The Right Treatise." This worldly good is rooted in the Golden Light; this right treatise comes forth from the Golden Light. Using this right treatise well, the gods' palaces and bodies are made bright by it, the gods' power and majesty increase by it, the gods' minds are made doubly happy, and the gods' Dharma-taste becomes doubly deep and far-reaching; this is the supreme virtue and essential way of the former kings, and also naming by the siddhānta of ultimate meaning: the chapter "The Right Treatise."

The Four Questions

Chinese Source

此文是流通中第二意，明人王弘經感通冥聖，天王佐助善政興隆。文為二：一長行說事本、二偈頌說正論。長行中對告地神說昔尊相，如文。偈有八十二行，文為四：初二行半集眾；次三行半發問；次一行結問開答；後七十五行梵天答，即說正論也。集眾文為三：初一行是佛敘尊相欲為天子，說先王本法；次一行佛敘尊相誡聽；次半行明說論處所。次四王發四問：一問云何呼人為天；二問非天所生而名天子；三問處王宮殿何故名天；四問以人法治世那得名天。次一行成前起後，可見（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

This text is the second intent within the propagation, clarifying that a king of humans spreads the sūtra and resonates with the unseen sages, and that the heavenly kings assist him and good government flourishes. The text has two [parts]: first, prose telling the origin of the affair; second, verse telling the right treatise. In the prose, addressing the earth goddess, he tells of Power-Honored Mark of old, as in the text. The verses have eighty-two lines; the text has four [parts]: the first two and a half lines gather the assembly; the next three and a half lines raise the questions; the next line concludes the questions and opens the answer; the last seventy-five lines are Brahmā's answer, which is the teaching of the right treatise. The text gathering the assembly is three: first, one line is the Buddha narrating that Power-Honored Mark, wishing [to speak] for the son of the gods, teaches the original law of the former kings; next, one line is the Buddha narrating that Power-Honored Mark admonishes them to listen; next, half a line clarifies the place where the treatise is taught. Next, the four kings raise four questions: first, asking how a person is called a god; second, asking how one not born of the gods is called a son of the gods; third, asking why one dwelling in a royal palace is called a god; fourth, asking how one who governs the world by human law can be called a god. The next line completes the former and raises the latter; it can be seen (and so on).

Brahmā's Brief Answer

Chinese Source

問既有四，答亦為四：一答天護其入胎，雖是人子而稱天子。三十三天各分己德，雖是於人而稱為天。雖處人宮殿，用天律治世，雖是人主而稱為天。雖是人法治世，令眾生行善多生天上，以因中說果故稱為天。從「汝今雖以」下，是梵答。文為二：初十行半略答、後六十四行半廣答。略中有四：初一行半許答、次「因集業故」一行略舉昔因今果答其問王義、次五行舉三義答其問天義、次三行舉三義重答問王義。答天舉三義者，未入胎、入胎分、德力加是也。以護胎故稱為天子，答第二問也。以分德故有其天德，故稱為天，答第一問也。神力所加，修善遮惡，後必生天。因中說果，故稱為天。答第三第四正法治世名為天問也。答「半名為天」三義竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Since there are four questions, the answers too are four. First, the answer that the gods protect his entering the womb: though he is a human's son, he is called a son of the gods. The Thirty-three Heavens each share their virtue: though he is human, he is called a god. Though he dwells in a human palace, he governs the world by heavenly law: though a lord of humans, he is called a god. Though he governs the world by human law, he makes beings practice good and mostly be born in the heavens; because the fruit is spoken of within the cause, he is called a god. From "Although you now" on is Brahmā's answer. The text has two [parts]: first, ten and a half lines answer briefly; then sixty-four and a half lines answer extensively. In the brief there are four: first, one and a half lines grant the answer; next, from "Because of accumulated karma," one line briefly raises past cause and present fruit to answer their question about the meaning of "king"; next, five lines raise three meanings to answer their question about the meaning of "god"; next, three lines raise three meanings to answer again the question about the meaning of "king." Answering "god" with three meanings: [protection] before entering the womb and in entering the womb, the sharing of virtue, and the addition of power. Because they protect the womb, he is called a son of the gods — this answers the second question. Because they share their virtue, he has heavenly virtue and is called a god — this answers the first question. Because spiritual power is added, he cultivates good and blocks evil and later will surely be born in heaven; because the fruit is spoken of within the cause, he is called a god — this answers the third and fourth questions, about governing the world by the true Dharma being called a god. The three meanings of "half is called a god" are finished.

Chinese Source

從「半名人王」已下，答有三義，故稱半為王。一名執樂者，樂由於王，王執此樂使天下和平，五日一風、十日一雨，老者擊壤、小騎竹馬，誰不歸德？故執樂者名王。二者遮惡，為民除害，天不亢旱、地不洪流、草不折傷、民無疾癘者，誰不歸德？故遮惡名王。三父母者，誨示禍福、導語善惡，制禮作樂而民知禁，誰不歸德？故父母名之為王。能為民下作父母，故諸天護之，名為天子。以遮惡不起，諸天分德，名之為天。以執樂故，因中說果，復名為天。非但半名人王義成，半名為天三義亦顯。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "Half [of him] is called king of humans" on, the answer has three meanings; hence the [other] half is called king. First, "holder of happiness": happiness comes from the king; the king holds this happiness and makes the world harmonious and peaceful, with a wind every five days and a rain every ten days; old men beat the clods and little children ride bamboo horses — who would not turn to his virtue? Hence the holder of happiness is called king. Second, blocking evil, removing harm for the people: the sky has no drought, the earth no flood, the grass is not broken, the people have no epidemics — who would not turn to his virtue? Hence blocking evil is called king. Third, father and mother: teaching and showing fortune and misfortune, guiding and speaking of good and evil, instituting rites and creating music so that the people know what is forbidden — who would not turn to his virtue? Hence father and mother is called king. Because he can be father and mother to the people below, the gods protect him, and he is called a son of the gods. Because he blocks evil so that it does not arise, the gods share their virtue, and he is called a god. Because he holds happiness, the fruit being spoken of within the cause, he is again called a god. Not only is the meaning of "half is called king of humans" established; the three meanings of "half is called a god" are also revealed.

The Six Meanings Lost and Kept

Chinese Source

從「若有惡事」下三十九行三句，廣明非法不得名天不得名王。六義俱失，三十三天各生瞋恨，是天不護，不護故非天子。是諸天王各相謂言，是天不分德，不分德故不得名天。捨遠善法增長惡法則無天因，寧得因中說果歎人為天也。生大愁苦者，無執樂義。疾疫流遍，無遮惡義。縱惡不顧善，無父母義（云云）。從「當正治罪」下二十四行三句，更廣說六義。當正治罪，即父母義。諸天護持，即天子義。以滅惡法，即魁膾義。魁膾名典軍，遮制惡鬼。鬼畏典軍，不敢亂行也。修習善法，即執樂義。應各為說，即示因果義。諸天，即分德義。還以六義消文，皆可尋（云云）。此中應明觀義，自思之（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "If there are evil deeds" on, thirty-nine lines and three phrases extensively clarify that one contrary to the Dharma cannot be called a god nor called a king, for the six meanings are all lost. "The Thirty-three Heavens each give rise to anger": these gods do not protect; not protecting, he is not a son of the gods. "These heavenly kings said to one another": these gods do not share their virtue; not sharing virtue, he cannot be called a god. "Abandons and keeps far from wholesome dharmas" and increases evil dharmas: then there is no heavenly cause; how could one, speaking of the fruit within the cause, praise a human as a god? "Giving rise to great sorrow and distress": there is no meaning of holding happiness. "Epidemics … flow throughout": no meaning of blocking evil. Indulging evil and not regarding good: no meaning of father and mother (and so on). From "should be rightly punished" on, twenty-four lines and three phrases further extensively explain the six meanings. "Their offenses should be rightly punished" is the meaning of father and mother. "The gods protect and uphold" is the meaning of son of the gods. "Because he extinguishes evil dharmas" is the meaning of chief executioner. The chief executioner is called the commander of troops, who restrains evil ghosts; ghosts fear the commander of troops and dare not roam in disorder. "Cultivates good" is the meaning of holding happiness. "He should explain to each" is the meaning of showing cause and effect. "The gods" is the meaning of sharing virtue. Using the six meanings again to interpret the text, all can be traced (and so on). Here one should clarify the meaning of contemplation; consider it yourself (and so on).

Sharing virtue is light: the reward-body merges with the dharma-nature, and this is sharing virtue. The addition of spiritual power is brightness (and so on). Further, father and mother is gold, because the dharma-nature is father and mother; blocking evil is light; holding happiness is brightness. Taking this as the root, one can make a right treatise. As with "half is called king of humans" and "half is called a god": [one] half is the worldly right treatise; [the other] half is called the world-transcending right treatise. Root and branch are connected — this is the meaning.

釋善集品

Explaining the Chapter "Good Gathering"

Chinese Source

此轉輪王集眾善法，如海導師。善海無涯，六度則攝。六度又廣，二度略攝，謂檀與智。提如意殊捨四大地，滿中珍寶以用布施，即集檀行也。合掌而立，請寶冥尊者宣揚顯說是金光明，即集智行也。檀智既然，餘法亦爾。從行得名，故稱〈善集品〉也。此六度不同，是世界悉檀集善也；修於五度，是為人集善也；修於智度，是對治集善也；皆波羅蜜，是第一義集善也。從此四集得名，故言〈善集品〉。此品是第二人王弘經。上明世間正見感動天地，此明出世正見感動賢聖（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

This wheel-turning king gathered the many good dharmas, like a guide on the sea. The ocean of good has no shore; the six perfections gather it in. The six perfections are again broad; two perfections gather them in briefly, namely dāna and wisdom. Holding up the wish-fulfilling [jewel] and giving up the great earth of the four [continents], filling it with precious jewels to use in giving — this is gathering the practice of dāna. Joining his palms and standing, he requested the venerable Jewel Profound to proclaim and clearly teach this Golden Light — this is gathering the practice of wisdom. Dāna and wisdom being so, the other dharmas are likewise. Named from practice, it is called the chapter "Good Gathering." The six perfections being different is the mundane gathering of good; cultivating five perfections is the individual gathering of good; cultivating the perfection of wisdom is the therapeutic gathering of good; all being pāramitā is the gathering of good of the ultimate meaning. Named from these four gatherings, it is called the chapter "Good Gathering." This chapter is the second [instance of] a king of humans spreading the sūtra. The previous [chapter] clarified worldly right view moving heaven and earth; this clarifies world-transcending right view moving the worthies and sages (and so on).

Explaining the Chapter "Ghosts and Spirits"

Chinese Source

鬼字訓歸，又云畏也。報多怖畏，如阿修羅（云云）。又云威也，能令他畏其威也。神者能也，大力者能移山填海，小力者能隱顯變化。此品通列一切天龍江海日月諸神。上已天題品竟，無容再出。雖通列諸道，而鬼神文多，從多故以之題品。此品是第三，一切天龍鬼神、天靈地祇咸皆發誓溥遍弘宣以勸流通。文為二：一長行、二偈頌。長行中，先舉事別、次圓供養。事別者，佛從慈悲中起受供養者，蓋應佛也。佛從如中起覺智，智與如合者，報佛也。一切法悉是諸佛行處者，法佛也。作如此解者，三佛歷別。若修事之供養，供養亦別也。圓供養者，勸聽經。聽經是法之供養，諸供養中最為第一，第一供養者供養一切佛。能說文字是應佛、能詮是報佛、所詮是法佛，能敬文字即敬三佛，亦是敬三世佛。諸佛從此生，故供養文字即供養一切諸佛（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

鬼 (ghost) is glossed as 歸, "returning"; it is also said to mean "fear": their retribution has much terror, like the asuras (and so on). It is also said to mean "majesty": they can make others fear their majesty. 神 (spirit) means "ability": those of great power can move mountains and fill seas; those of small power can become hidden or visible and transform. This chapter lists in general all the gods, dragons, rivers, seas, sun, moon, and spirits. Since the gods have already been used as chapter titles above, there is no room to bring them out again. Though it lists the paths in general, the text on ghosts and spirits is extensive; following the majority, it takes them as the chapter title. This chapter is the third: all the gods, dragons, ghosts and spirits, heavenly numina and earth deities vow to proclaim it universally, to encourage propagation. The text has two [parts]: first, prose; second, verse. In the prose, first it raises the distinct [offerings] in phenomena, next the perfect offering. As for the distinct in phenomena: the Buddha arising from compassion to receive offerings is the response-buddha. The Buddha arising from suchness with awakened wisdom, wisdom uniting with suchness, is the reward-buddha. "All dharmas are the sphere of practice of the buddhas" is the Dharma-buddha. Explained thus, the three buddhas are distinct one by one. If one cultivates phenomenal offerings, the offerings too are distinct. The perfect offering is the urging to listen to the sūtra. Listening to the sūtra is the offering of the Dharma; among all offerings it is foremost; the foremost offering is offering to all buddhas. The written words that can teach are the response-buddha; what can express is the reward-buddha; what is expressed is the Dharma-buddha. To revere the written words is to revere the three buddhas, and also to revere the buddhas of the three times. The buddhas are born from this; hence offering to the written words is offering to all buddhas (and so on).

Chinese Source

又別時重聞更記之。長行舉四願欲而作一勸。若欲知佛行處，行處者即如如境，法佛也。欲知者，知是如如智，報佛也。能聽此經，此經即文字，文字即應佛也。能聽者，隨順佛教即法供養，法供養即妙供養也。聽經能生覺智，覺智生故，即是佛受供養也。聽經一事具諸願欲，秪圓一事具別諸事也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, having heard it again at another time, I record it further. The prose raises four wishes and makes one exhortation. "Wish to know the … sphere of practice of the buddhas": the sphere of practice is the object of suchness, the Dharma-buddha. "Wish to know": the knowing is suchness-wisdom, the reward-buddha. "Listen to this … scripture": this sūtra is written words, and written words are the response-buddha. "Listen": according with the Buddha's teaching is the offering of the Dharma, and the offering of the Dharma is the wondrous offering. Listening to the sūtra can give rise to awakened wisdom; because awakened wisdom arises, the Buddha receives the offering. The one affair of listening to the sūtra is complete with all the wishes; just the perfect one affair is complete with all the distinct affairs.

The Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra, Fascicle Six

Reading Notes for Fascicle 6

Translator's notes

Sūtra quotations. All lemmas reuse the English of this site's Golden Light Sūtra, Parts 3–4 (Chapters 11–18). "Half is called a god" (半名為天) is Zhiyi's counterpart to the sūtra's "Half [of him] is called king of humans"; it is not in the sūtra.

Four siddhāntas (units 4, 7). The mundane / individual / therapeutic siddhānta and the siddhānta of ultimate meaning, as in Parts 1–2 of this commentary. "The people living in harmony, with no resentment between above and below" echoes the Classic of Filial Piety.

The four answers (unit 5). 未入胎、入胎分、德力加 is punctuated as "[protection] before and in entering the womb, the sharing of virtue, and the addition of power"; other punctuations are possible. 尊相欲為天子 is translated literally ("wishing [to speak] for the son of the gods"); the sūtra says he spoke for his crown prince.

"Chief executioner" (unit 5). 魁膾 follows the sūtra ("a rākṣasa, a chief executioner"); Zhiyi glosses it as 典軍, "commander of troops," who restrains evil ghosts.

Spirit names (unit 10). Names follow the sūtra translator's forms (Part 3 notes). Zhiyi's 波利羅睺 is treated as one name, "Bali-Rāhu"; 鳩羅擅提 is the sūtra's 鳩羅檀提, Kuladantī. 摩醯首羅 "Majestic Numen Emperor" (威靈帝) is in the Consecration Sūtra (T1331): 「神名摩醯首羅，字威靈帝」. The spirit of sorcery 彌栗頭虔伽陀 "Good Class" is also listed in the Consecration Sūtra (T1331); the other glosses' source not located. "Liberated from the cause / fruit," "crossing all existences" are not in Dharmakṣema's verses; source not located.

Predictions (units 12–13). 授記 giving predictions; 授莂 giving the tally; 授決 giving the decision. "Take an awl and point to the ground" alludes to the Lotus (ch. 12): there is no place the size of a mustard seed where the bodhisattva did not give up his life. The sūtra's 以隨相修 ("cultivated in accord with the marks") is read by Zhiyi as according with the true mark.

The medical section (units 16–17). Zhiyi says this exposition comes from Paramārtha's commentary (lost). Terms follow the sūtra translator: wind / heat / lung illness / the balanced [illness]. "The three-by-three is the basic grouping" (三三本攝) is read by Zhiyi as 本 "basis" plus 攝 "absorbing." 王 is "reigns" in the five-phase sense (wood, fire, metal, water, earth); "earth lodged" (土寄) is the eighteen days of earth at the end of each season. 迦提月 is the Kārttika month, when the kaṭhina robe is given after the retreat; 後安居 the later summer retreat.

Apparent copying error (unit 17). "From the sixteenth of the second month to the fifteenth of the second month" should probably read "… to the fifteenth of the fourth month"; translated as written.

六大 (unit 17). The sūtra's "six great elements" are glossed by Zhiyi as the six bowels (六腑), including the triple burner (三焦) of Chinese medicine.

Contemplation of the three poisons (unit 19). 數息、不淨、慈心、因緣 counting breaths, impurity, kindness, causes and conditions; 信行／法行 faith-practitioners / Dharma-practitioners; 覺觀 initial and sustained thought. 淫 is "lust" (unit 17 "too much lust").

流水 (unit 21). The name Jalavāhana is translated by the sūtra as "Flowing Water"; Zhiyi explains the two meanings "making water flow" and "giving water." The "ten meanings of 'elder'" are in Zhiyi's Lotus Commentary (Fahua wenju, T1718, ch. 4, the ten virtues of the great elder).

Repaying kindness (unit 22). The Nirvāṇa passage on giving food is a paraphrase; T0374 has 「夫施食者則為施命」 ("giving food is giving life") in the discussion of long life. Exact source not located.

Princes' names (unit 25). 大無罪 "Great Without Offense," 大度 "Great Crossing," 大天 "Great God," 大心 "Great Mind" are Zhiyi's glosses. The Sūtra of Mañjuśrī's Questions (T0468) gloss of 羅陀 as "middle" was not located. The seven dots on the cubs' heads are attributed to the Classic of Mountains and Seas; not found in the received text.

私謂 "My own view" (unit 27). Guanding's additions, as in Parts 1–2. "Four buddhas or five buddhas" refers to the four buddhas of the directions in Chapter 2 together with Śākyamuni.
