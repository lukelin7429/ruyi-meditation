Fascicle Three: The Repentance and Praise Chapters

The third fascicle of Zhiyi's Textual Commentary covers two chapters of the Golden Light Sūtra: Chapter 3, "Repentance," and Chapter 4, "Praise." For the "Repentance" chapter Zhiyi first gives a general treatment in four headings: the name (ten explanations of the two characters chan and hui), the place of repentance (the dharma-nature, understood through the three repentances of the Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra), the methods (correct contemplation of the dharma-nature, aided by bowing, recitation, and urging on contemplation), and the stages, arguing that everyone short of buddhahood, up to equal awakening, still repents. He then sets out the three kinds of repentance — by ritual action, by seizing a mark, and of no-generation — and what each can remove. The line-by-line commentary reads the dream of the golden drum through the three bodies and divides Faith-Mark's long verse into five parts: one's own repentance of the three obstructions, offerings, praise, vows, and the conclusion. The "Praise" chapter is explained in terms of the four siddhāntas, the choice of the earth goddess as listener, the name Golden Dragon Honored, and the marks of the birth-body, the specially honored body, and the dharma-nature body. Every lemma uses the English of this site's translation of the sūtra (Part 1).

Explaining the Name "Repentance"

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Chapter "Repentance"

Chinese Source

◎諸大乘經多分散明懺悔，此經專以懺悔當品。今先釋名。懺者首也，悔者伏也。如世人得罪於王，伏欵順從、不敢違逆，不逆為伏，順從為首。行人亦爾，伏三寶足下，正順道理、不敢作非，故名懺悔。又懺名白法，悔名黑法，黑法須悔而勿作，白法須企而尚之，取捨合論，故言懺悔。又懺名修來，悔名改往，往日所作惡不善法鄙而惡之，故名為悔；往日所棄一切善法，今日已去誓願勤修，故名為懺。棄往求來，故名懺悔。

Ru-Yi Translation

Many Mahāyāna sūtras make clear repentance in a scattered way; this sūtra devotes a whole chapter to repentance. Now we first explain the name. Chan (懺) means "turning oneself in" (首); hui (悔) means "submitting" (伏). Just as a person in the world who has offended the king submits sincerely and complies, not daring to disobey — not disobeying is submitting, complying is turning oneself in — so too the practitioner: submitting at the feet of the Three Jewels, rightly complying with the way and its principle, not daring to do wrong; hence it is called repentance (懺悔). Again, chan is called the white dharma and hui the black dharma: the black dharma must be regretted and not done; the white dharma must be aspired to and esteemed. Discussing taking up and casting off together, it is called repentance. Again, chan is called cultivating what is to come and hui is called reforming what is past: the evil and unwholesome dharmas done in past days one despises and loathes, hence it is called hui; all the wholesome dharmas abandoned in past days one vows, from today on, to cultivate diligently, hence it is called chan. Abandoning the past and seeking what is to come is called repentance.

Chinese Source

又懺名披陳眾失發露過咎不敢隱諱，悔名斷相續心厭悔捨離，能作所作合棄故言懺悔。又懺者名慚，悔者名愧，慚則慚天、愧則愧人，人見其顯、天見其冥，冥細顯麁，麁細皆惡，故言懺悔。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, chan is called laying out all one's faults, revealing one's errors without daring to hide or conceal them; hui is called cutting off the continuing mind, loathing and regretting it, giving it up and departing from it. What does and what is done are abandoned together; hence "repentance." Again, chan is called shame (慚) and hui is called remorse (愧): shame is shame before heaven, remorse is remorse before people. People see what is evident, heaven sees what is hidden; the hidden is subtle and the evident coarse, and coarse and subtle are both evil; hence "repentance."

Chinese Source

又人是賢人、天是聖人，不逮賢聖之流，是故懺悔。又賢聖俱是人，天是第一義天，第一義天是理、賢聖是事，不逮事理，俱皆懺悔。又慚三乘之聖天、愧三乘之賢人，不逮此天人，故名慚愧，慚愧名懺悔。又三乘賢聖皆是人，第一義理為天，約此人天慚愧，故名懺悔。又三乘賢聖尚非菩薩之賢，況菩薩之聖。今慚愧三十心之賢、十地之聖，故名慚愧懺悔。總此賢聖皆是人，第一義理名為天，約此人天論慚愧，故名懺悔。又三十心去自判聖人，十信是賢人，約此賢聖論慚愧懺悔。總此賢聖皆名人，第一義理名為天，約此人天論慚愧懺悔。合十番釋名也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, "people" are the worthies and "heaven" is the sages; one does not attain to the ranks of the worthies and sages, and therefore one repents. Again, worthies and sages are both "people," and "heaven" is the heaven of the ultimate meaning; the heaven of the ultimate meaning is principle, the worthies and sages are phenomena; one does not attain to phenomena or principle, and repents of both. Again, one is ashamed before the sage-heavens of the three vehicles and feels remorse before the worthy-people of the three vehicles; not attaining to these heavens and people is called shame and remorse, and shame and remorse are called repentance. Again, the worthies and sages of the three vehicles are all people, and the principle of the ultimate meaning is heaven; shame and remorse are by way of these people and heaven; hence it is called repentance. Again, the worthies and sages of the three vehicles are not yet even the worthies of the bodhisattvas, let alone the sages of the bodhisattvas. Now one feels shame and remorse before the worthies of the thirty minds and the sages of the ten grounds; hence it is called shame and remorse, repentance. Taking all these worthies and sages together as people, the principle of the ultimate meaning is called heaven; shame and remorse are discussed by way of these people and heaven; hence it is called repentance. Again, from the thirty minds on, [the bodhisattvas] themselves are judged to be sages, and the ten faiths are worthies; shame, remorse, and repentance are discussed by way of these worthies and sages. Taking all these worthies and sages together as people, the principle of the ultimate meaning is called heaven; shame, remorse, and repentance are discussed by way of these people and heaven. Together these make ten rounds of explaining the name.

The Place of Repentance

Chinese Source

次明懺悔處者，《大經》闍王偈云「麁言及輭語，皆歸第一義。是故我今日，歸依於世尊。」又〈梵行品〉云「我昔與汝等，不見四真諦，是故久流轉，生死大苦海。若能見四諦，則得斷生死，生死既盡已，則不受諸有。」《法華》云「行處近處住忍辱地，亦不行不分別。」又云「寶處在近，汝可前進。即滅化城，即至寶所。」此經云「我當為是作歸依處」。歸依處者，即甚深無量法性也。法性秖是諦理，諦理秖是妙境。諸佛所師寂滅真如祕密之藏，十方眾聖安住其中。若得其本，本立則道生；不得其處，則平地顛墜。如盲人入棘林，動轉罣礙。為是義故，須識懺悔處也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the place of repentance. In the Great Sūtra, King Ajātaśatru's verse says: "Coarse words and gentle speech all return to the ultimate meaning. Therefore today I take refuge in the World-Honored One." Further, the "Pure Conduct" chapter says: "In ages past, you and I together, not seeing the Four Noble Truths, wandered on for so long within the great ocean of suffering that is birth-and-death. Could we but see the Four Truths, we would thereby sever birth-and-death; birth-and-death once exhausted, we would take on no further existence." The Lotus says: "Proper conduct and proper company: dwelling in the ground of forbearance . and neither acts nor discriminates." It also says: "The place of treasure is near; you may go forward. At once [the guide] caused the phantom city to vanish, and at once they reached the place of treasure." This sūtra says: "I shall be a place of refuge for these." The place of refuge is the very deep, immeasurable dharma-nature. The dharma-nature is just the principle of the truths; the principle of the truths is just the wondrous object. It is the teacher of the buddhas, the secret treasury of quiescent extinction and true suchness, in which the sages of the ten directions abide at peace. If one obtains its root, "when the root is established, the path arises"; if one does not obtain its place, one stumbles and falls on level ground, like a blind man entering a thicket of thorns, snagged and obstructed at every move. For this reason one must recognize the place of repentance.

Chinese Source

故《普賢觀》云「端坐念實相，眾罪如霜露，慧日能消除。我心自空，罪福無主，是名大懺悔、是名莊嚴懺悔、是名無罪相懺悔。」無罪相者，此約空為處也。莊嚴懺悔者，約俗諦為處也。大懺悔者，約中道為處也。若三種差別者，此是歷別論處爾。即一而三即三而一者，此圓妙懺悔也，諸大菩薩修學佛法而懺悔也。若識此法而懺悔者，最妙最上懺悔處也。《大經》云「譬如有人在大海浴，當知是人已用一切諸河之水。」《大品》云「譬如負債人依投於王，債主反更供養，何敢就其覓物？」書云「如牆頭草，非其莖葉能高能長，所依得處也。」行人若依法性為懺悔處者，高出一切諸處所也。行人若識此意，先當求覓法性道理為懺悔處也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence the Samantabhadra Contemplation [Sūtra] says: "Sit upright and be mindful of the true mark; the many offenses are like frost and dew; the sun of wisdom can melt them away. My mind is itself empty; offense and merit have no master. This is called the great repentance, called the adorned repentance, called the repentance of the mark of no offense." The repentance of the mark of no offense takes emptiness as its place. The adorned repentance takes the conventional truth as its place. The great repentance takes the middle way as its place. If the three kinds are distinct, this discusses the places separately one by one. Identical to the one yet three, identical to the three yet one — this is the perfect, wondrous repentance, the repentance with which the great bodhisattvas study the Buddha-dharma. If one repents recognizing this dharma, it is the most wondrous, the highest place of repentance. The Great Sūtra says: "It is like a person bathing in the great ocean: you should know that this person has already used the water of all the rivers." The Larger [Prajñāpāramitā] says: "It is like a debtor who relies on and submits to the king: the creditors instead make offerings to him — how would they dare to come seeking their goods from him?" A book says: "Like grass on the top of a wall: it is not that its stalks and leaves can grow high and long; it is that what it relies on has obtained its place." If the practitioner relies on the dharma-nature as the place of repentance, it stands high above all places. If the practitioner recognizes this intent, he should first seek out the principle of the dharma-nature as the place of repentance.

The Methods of Repentance

Chinese Source

次明懺悔法者，法為二種：一正法、二助法。正法者，即是觀法性之慧也。法性常故，是觀亦常；法性樂我淨故，觀慧亦爾；法性不可思議、至深至妙、無上無等等者，觀慧亦爾。境智相冥無二之法如如不異者，境如智、智如境，故言如如不異也。經言「說智及智處，皆名為般若。說智及智處，皆名為實相。」說如不說、不說如說，說不說無二無別。以此觀慧歷一切法亦復如是，故云毘盧遮那遍一切處，若行若住、若明若闇皆得不離見佛世尊，六根所對無非佛法。耆婆攬草無非藥者，普能愈病。釋摩男所執一切砂礫皆變為寶，阿那律空器悉滿甘露，若如是者，所觀之罪非復是罪，罪即實相；所觀之福福即非福，福即實相。純是實相，是名大懺悔也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, making clear the methods of repentance. The methods are of two kinds: first, the correct method; second, the auxiliary method. The correct method is the wisdom that contemplates the dharma-nature. Because the dharma-nature is permanent, this contemplation is also permanent; because the dharma-nature is bliss, self, and purity, the contemplative wisdom is likewise; the dharma-nature is inconceivable, utmost in depth and wonder, unsurpassed and unequaled, and the contemplative wisdom is likewise. As for the dharma in which object and wisdom merge without duality, "such and such, not different": the object is such as wisdom, and wisdom is such as the object; hence "such and such, not different." A sūtra says: "Both the teaching of wisdom and the sphere of wisdom are alike called prajñā. Both the teaching of wisdom and the sphere of wisdom are alike called the true mark." Teaching is like not teaching, not teaching is like teaching; teaching and not teaching are not two and not different. Passing through all dharmas with this contemplative wisdom is likewise. Hence it is said that Vairocana pervades all places; whether walking or standing, whether in light or in darkness, one never departs from seeing the Buddha, the World-Honored One; whatever the six faculties face is nothing but the Buddha-dharma. When Jīvaka picked grasses there were none that were not medicine, and they could cure every illness. All the sand and gravel that Mahānāma took hold of turned into jewels; Aniruddha's empty bowl was filled with sweet dew. If it is like this, the offense contemplated is no longer an offense: offense is the true mark; the merit contemplated — merit is not merit: merit is the true mark. It is purely the true mark; this is called the great repentance.

Chinese Source

助道懺悔者，若純用正懺，亦不須助；若正道闇昧不明了者，修助以助之，所謂灰汁澡豆、皂莢木槵以助清水爾。略言勤用身口意而為助也，身謂旋禮、口謂讀誦、心謂策觀，而助開門，如順流順風助之以篙棹，去則疾也。如是略論正助也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for repentance as an aid to the path: if one uses purely the correct repentance, there is no need for an aid; if the correct path is dim and unclear, one cultivates the aid to assist it — just as lye, bath-powder, soap-pods, and soapberries assist clear water. Briefly, one diligently uses body, mouth, and mind as the aid: body means circumambulating and bowing, mouth means reading and reciting, mind means urging on contemplation. These assist in opening the gate, as when going with the current and with the wind one assists with pole and oar, and goes quickly. Thus is a brief discussion of the correct and the auxiliary.

The Stages of Repentance

Chinese Source

四明懺悔位者，若作一種解者，謂鄙濁凡夫應須懺悔，離垢清淨者何用懺悔？此不如是，故新本〈業障滅品〉中說：人從父母稟身，十月懷抱、三年鞠養，撫念惟惟，始能升頭戴髮。教方教數，始解作人。那忽違恩背義而行殺逆？天雖大，不覆此人；地雖厚，不載此人。此人命終直入地獄。如是逆罪應須懺悔，滅除業障。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, making clear the stages of repentance. If one takes a single kind of understanding, [one says] that defiled and turbid ordinary beings must repent, but what use is repentance for those free of defilement and pure? This is not so. Hence in the "Eradicating the Obstruction of Karma" chapter of the new version it is said: A person receives a body from father and mother, carried in the womb ten months and nursed and raised three years, cherished with constant care, before he can lift his head and wear his hair. Taught the directions and taught numbers, only then does he understand how to be a person. How could he suddenly turn against kindness, betray duty, and commit killing and rebellion? Though heaven is great, it does not cover this person; though earth is thick, it does not bear this person. When this person's life ends, he goes straight into hell. Such rebellious offenses must be repented, to eradicate the obstruction of karma.

Chinese Source

佛為人天師，師嚴道尊，凡有所說若有違犯罪莫大焉。初篇後聚那可違負？爾則欺佛負心，復負三師七僧。此則佛海死屍、華園爛肉。此四重人，應須懺悔滅除業障。多瞋墮蛇虺、多欲墮鳩鴿、多癡墮蝨蟻、多慢墮飛鳥、多諂墮修羅、外慳墮餓鬼，餓鬼常飢渴，畜生相殘害，修羅多怖畏。是四惡道，聞名尚不可，況復當之？應須懺悔滅除業障。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha is the teacher of humans and gods; when the teacher is stern, the path is honored. If one violates anything he has taught, no offense is greater. How could one transgress the first category and the later groups [of precepts]? To do so is to deceive the Buddha and betray one's own mind, and further to betray the three teachers and seven monks. Such a one is a corpse in the ocean of the Buddha, rotten flesh in a flower garden. Persons [guilty of] these four grave [offenses] must repent, to eradicate the obstruction of karma. Those of much anger fall among snakes and vipers; those of much desire fall among doves and pigeons; those of much delusion fall among lice and ants; those of much arrogance fall among flying birds; those of much flattery fall among the asuras; those outwardly stingy fall among the hungry ghosts. Hungry ghosts are always hungry and thirsty; animals harm and kill one another; asuras are full of fear. These four evil paths — even hearing their names is unbearable, how much less undergoing them? One must repent, to eradicate the obstruction of karma.

Chinese Source

人中八苦，一苦尚不可忍，況八苦交橫？應當懺悔滅除業障。天上有五衰地獄等苦，色界天不得速入定、求不得苦，無色界天有四心苦，三界籠樊生死窟宅，應須懺悔滅除業障。若出家人雖欲修道，為五煩惱所障心不得停，心為四顛倒所惑，不得入四念處，亦須懺悔除滅業障。念處治彼四倒，二惡不勤斷、二善不勤生，不得入如意足，煖法不發，亦須懺悔。五根不生，喜有退墮。根生未有力，雖有力未隣真如。是四善根中，應須懺悔滅除業障。

Ru-Yi Translation

Among humans there are the eight sufferings; even one suffering is unbearable, how much less the eight sufferings crisscrossing? One should repent, to eradicate the obstruction of karma. In the heavens there are the five signs of decay and sufferings like those of hell; the gods of the form realm cannot swiftly enter concentration — the suffering of not getting what one seeks; the gods of the formless realm have the suffering of the four [kinds of] mind. The three realms are a cage, the den and dwelling of birth-and-death; one must repent, to eradicate the obstruction of karma. Though a person who has left home wishes to cultivate the path, he is obstructed by the five afflictions and his mind cannot settle; his mind is deluded by the four inversions and cannot enter the four applications of mindfulness; he too must repent, to eradicate the obstruction of karma. The applications of mindfulness counteract those four inversions; [yet if] he does not diligently cut off the two [kinds of] evil and does not diligently give rise to the two [kinds of] good, he cannot enter the bases of spiritual power, and the dharma of warmth does not arise; he too must repent. The five faculties do not arise, and he is liable to backslide and fall. When the faculties have arisen there is not yet power; even with power, he is not yet near true suchness. Within these four wholesome roots one must repent, to eradicate the obstruction of karma.

Chinese Source

苦忍明發雖不墮三途，欲界七生次第應受。一生尚苦，何況七生？雖斷欲界五下分六品，餘三品在，亦應懺悔。雖斷五下八品，盡餘一品在，雖斷色盡，餘無色分在，亦須懺悔。雖入有餘涅槃，猶有果身在，身子風熱、畢陵伽眼痛，欲棄有餘入無餘，亦須懺悔。雖斷三界正使盡，習氣尚存，亦須懺悔。支佛亦爾，亦須懺悔。

Ru-Yi Translation

When the light of the forbearance of suffering has arisen, though one does not fall into the three paths, one must still receive seven births in the desire realm in sequence. Even one birth is suffering, how much more seven births? Though one has cut off six grades of the desire realm's five lower portions, three grades remain; one should still repent. Though one has cut off eight grades of the five lower portions, one grade still remains; though one has cut off the form [realm] completely, the formless portion remains; one must still repent. Though one has entered nirvāṇa with residue, the body of the fruit still remains — Śāriputra had wind and fever, Pilindavatsa had eye pain — and wishing to abandon [nirvāṇa] with residue and enter [nirvāṇa] without residue, one must still repent. Though one has cut off all the main agents of the three realms, the habitual residue still remains; one must still repent. Pratyekabuddhas are likewise; they too must repent.

Chinese Source

若乾慧地，未得理水霑心，故言乾慧；性地未能見理；八人見地猶有愛惑；薄地神通未能，還生欲界；離欲地猶有上界惑；已辦地不能除習；辟支佛地但作神通，不能達文字；菩薩地未窮至極，如是等位皆須懺悔滅除業障。十信但信，未能稱理；十住但入偏理；十行但事，未能入中；十迴向但修中，未能證中；十地雖證中，地地皆有障，未窮於學，不得無學，應須懺悔滅除業障。又十信雖三智圓修，但是方便陀羅尼；十住已去乃至等覺已來，秖如十四日月，非十五日月，匡郭未圓，光未頓足、闇未頓盡，應須懺悔滅除業障。

Ru-Yi Translation

On the ground of dry wisdom the water of principle has not yet moistened the mind; hence "dry wisdom." On the ground of nature one cannot yet see principle. On the ground of the eighth person and the ground of seeing there are still the delusions of craving. On the ground of thinning one is not yet capable of spiritual powers and is reborn again in the desire realm. On the ground free of desire there are still the delusions of the upper realms. On the ground of completion one cannot remove habitual residue. On the pratyekabuddha ground one only performs spiritual powers and cannot penetrate written words. Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the eight difficulties: these are the eight difficulties discussed within good and evil, not the eight difficulties of [being born] before or after a buddha. The text has two: the first four lines point to evil as difficulty; the next line and a half point to evil blocking good as difficulty, or point to good blocking the path as difficulty. The first four phrases are the difficulty of the obstruction of retribution. "The existences" are the twenty-five existences. A body obtained as retribution creates many evils — is this not an obstruction of retribution? "The perilous difficulties of birth-and-death" has two explanations: if one takes its cause, it is the obstruction of karma; if one takes its fruit, it is the obstruction of retribution. "The various kinds of lust" is the obstruction of afflictions. "A light and restless mind" is again the obstruction of retribution. Lightness and restlessness are initial and sustained thought, and initial and sustained thought belong to the dharmas of retribution. For example, an arhat has exhausted the afflictions, yet on leaving contemplation he still has the scattered mind of initial and sustained thought. You should know that lightness and restlessness are an obstruction of retribution, not affliction. Further, take people of the world who in their studies keep turning around and changing tracks and accomplish nothing: this is not an obstruction of karma; it all comes from the fault of lightness and restlessness — is it not an obstruction? "The difficulty of keeping close to evil friends": evil friends can defile a person's three kinds of action; this belongs to the obstruction of karma — like moving a stable next to a butcher's, like Mencius's mother living next to the mourners and the peddlers; [near] vermilion one turns red, [near] ink one turns black; this is its meaning. The three poisons are the obstruction of afflictions.

Ru-Yi Translation

From "the difficulty of meeting [a place] free of difficulties" on, there are two meanings here. First: if one does not cultivate good, obstructions and difficulties do not arise; if one wishes to cultivate good, the obstructions of evil arise. Good is not an obstruction; evil comes and blocks good, and good is called difficult. This meaning is easy to know, and ordinary people all take this explanation. Second: the goods themselves are difficulties; good can obstruct the path — is it not a difficulty? This meaning will now be explained. "The difficulty of meeting [a place] free of difficulties": thinking oneself without evil, one is unwilling to cultivate good — as when the two vehicles enter emptiness, form the thought that they have been delivered and the thought that they are at peace, and no longer advance to seek bodhi; this is its meaning.

"The difficulty of cultivating merit": doing much that is conditioned, seeking agreeable fruits — like the bhikṣu who practiced only merit and did not cultivate the precepts, and fell among white elephants, his body hung with the seven jewels and his dung received in a golden basin. The praise proper has two: the first sixteen lines praise by means of words; the last three lines praise by cutting off words. The brief praise praises, among the marks and features, briefly the golden color. Gold has four meanings: firm and indestructible, a simile for permanence; whoever obtains it is rich, a simile for bliss; its substance without flaw or blemish, a simile for purity; its color wondrous and shimmering, a simile for self. Among all the virtues the four virtues are the general ones. Explaining the Chapter "Praise"

Chinese Source

讚歎，凡有四意：一從能讚人、二從生善、三從滅惡、四從所讚人、一從能讚人者。前品明信相思疑佛壽，四尊盡降其室。一心信解，夜夢金鼓出聲，旦向耆山說夢。今品佛述其昔為龍尊，面讚法王，願我當來夜夢晝說。說斯人之本事，故言〈讚歎品〉。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Praise" has in all four intents: first, from the one who praises; second, from producing good; third, from extinguishing evil; fourth, from those praised. First, from the one who praises: the previous chapter made clear that Faith-Mark doubted the Buddha's lifespan and the four honored ones all descended into his chamber. Believing and understanding single-mindedly, at night he dreamed that a golden drum gave forth a sound, and by day he went to Gṛdhrakūṭa and told the dream. In the present chapter the Buddha relates how he was formerly Dragon Honored, who praised the Dharma King face to face and vowed that in times to come he would dream by night and tell by day. It tells the past deeds of this person; hence it is called the "Praise" chapter.

Chinese Source

二從生善得名者，夫善不孤運，生必託緣。緣中勝者無過於佛，龍尊讚佛能生妙善，從生善緣得名，故言〈讚歎品〉。三從滅惡得名者，罪之尤者無過毀佛，若翻滅斯罪應須讚歎。讚歎治於毀訾，從能治得名，故言〈讚歎品〉。四從所讚人得名者，即是去來現在三世諸佛，諸佛極尊甚深，無量稱揚顯說，故名〈讚歎品〉。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, gaining the name from producing good: good does not move alone; when it arises it must rely on conditions. Among conditions none surpasses the Buddha. Dragon Honored's praise of the buddhas can produce wondrous good; it gains its name from the condition of producing good; hence it is called the "Praise" chapter. Third, gaining the name from extinguishing evil: no offense is worse than disparaging the Buddha; to overturn and extinguish this offense one must praise. Praise counteracts disparagement; it gains its name from what counteracts; hence it is called the "Praise" chapter. Fourth, gaining the name from those praised: these are the buddhas of the three times, past, future, and present; the buddhas are utmost in honor and very deep, extolled and openly proclaimed without measure; hence it is called the "Praise" chapter.

If one wishes to distinguish [the two characters]: relating what they have attained is called 讚 ("praise"), commending them by similes is called 歎 ("extolling"); and they can also be distinguished the other way round. The Great Treatise, fascicle thirty, says: "Lauding his merit is called 讚; when lauding is not enough and one further extols him, it is called 歎." In this chapter there is reverence, which is the karma of body; honoring, which is the karma of mind; praise, which is the karma of speech. If the karma of speech is not released, body and mind are not unimpeded. To make body and mind unimpeded, voice and sound do the Buddha's work; hence he extols and openly proclaims, commending the true merit of the Tathāgata; hence it is called the "Praise" chapter. Concluding these four meanings: all are the causes and conditions of the four siddhāntas establishing this chapter's name; hence it is called the "Praise" chapter.

此品雖從四悉檀立名，正是生善之用。文為二：一長行、二偈頌。而對告地神者，主此大地，菩薩行行皆寄其上，壽命長久見去來今事，證義事強。如《瑞應》云「積功累德誰為證？佛時指地，是知我。」今說往昔金龍尊事，所以對告地神也。又對善女天者，男天陽表權、女天陰表實。實智能生眾善，善生故宜對善女，證往故宜對堅牢（云云）。今論生善之用，故對告善女。

Ru-Yi Translation

Though this chapter's name is established from the four siddhāntas, it is properly the function of producing good. The text has two: first, prose; second, verse. As for addressing the earth goddess: she governs this great earth, and all the practices of bodhisattvas rest upon it; her lifespan is long and she has seen past, future, and present events; her witness to the meaning is strong. As the Auspicious Response says: "Accumulating merit and building up virtue — who is the witness? The Buddha then pointed to the earth: 'She knows me.'" Now [the Buddha] tells the past events of Golden Dragon Honored; therefore he addresses the earth goddess. Further, as for addressing [her as] "Good goddess": male gods signify the provisional; female gods, being yin, signify the real. Real wisdom can produce the many goods; since good is produced, it is fitting to address [her as] "Good goddess"; to witness the past, it is fitting to address Dṛḍhā (and so on). Now, since the function of producing good is being discussed, he addresses [her as] "Good goddess."

金龍尊者，此王往昔修金光明法門，依法性理，故言金；能以智慧讚三世佛，辯如雲雨，故言龍；能為眾生作大利益，為物所仰，故言尊。從行得名，故言金龍尊也。總有六十二行半偈，文為三：一三十五行讚三世佛、二二十五行半發來世願、三二行結會二世事。就讚佛，文為五：初一行半總讚、次二十三行別讚、次三行遍類讚、次五行半絕言讚、次二行迴向。總讚者，竪總三世、橫總十方。世方是總事、微妙寂滅是總理。總理是總法身、總事是總報應二身。總讚三身，亦是總讚三德、三寶等種種三法門。如是諸佛總皆清淨。清淨者，即是總讚之辭也。非但清淨，亦常樂我（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for Golden Dragon Honored: this king in the past cultivated the Dharma-gate of the Golden Light, relying on the principle of the dharma-nature, hence "Golden"; with wisdom he could praise the buddhas of the three times, his eloquence like clouds and rain, hence "Dragon"; he could bring great benefit to beings and was looked up to by them, hence "Honored." Named from his practice, he is called Golden Dragon Honored. There are in all sixty-two and a half lines of verse. The text has three: first, thirty-five lines praising the buddhas of the three times; second, twenty-five and a half lines making vows for future ages; third, two lines concluding and connecting the events of the two ages. On praising the buddhas, the text has five: first, a line and a half, general praise; next, twenty-three lines, particular praise; next, three lines, praise extended by kind; next, five and a half lines, praise cutting off words; next, two lines, dedication. General praise: vertically it covers the three times, horizontally the ten directions. Times and directions are the general phenomena; "subtle, in quiescent extinction" is the general principle. The general principle is the general Dharma-body; the general phenomena are the general reward and response bodies. Praising the three bodies in general is also praising in general the three virtues, the Three Jewels, and all the various Dharma-gates of three dharmas. Thus the buddhas are all, in general, pure. "Pure" is the word of the general praise. Not only pure, but also permanent, blissful, and self (and so on).

The Marks of the Three Bodies

Chinese Source

從「色中上色」下二十三行，是歷相別讚。其文間出，分為六意：初六行半讚七大相海，亦讚髮紺之好；次二行讚兩小相海；次兩句遍讚大相海；次十三行又讚四大相海；次兩句讚一小相海；次兩句讚手足柔軟，復是一大相海。所以然者？大相小相更相間填，共嚴佛身。龍尊巧智如法相解，如法相讚，故大相小相相間而讚爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "the highest color among colors" on, twenty-three lines, is the particular praise going through the marks. Its text alternates, and is divided into six intents: first, six and a half lines praise seven oceans of major marks, and also praise the feature of dark-blue hair; next, two lines praise two oceans of minor marks; next, two phrases praise the oceans of major marks universally; next, thirteen lines again praise four oceans of major marks; next, two phrases praise one ocean of minor marks; next, two phrases praise the softness of the hands and feet, which is again one ocean of major marks. Why so? Major marks and minor marks are interspersed and fill each other in, together adorning the Buddha's body. Dragon Honored, with skillful wisdom, understood according to the marks of the Dharma and praised according to the marks of the Dharma; hence major and minor marks are praised alternately.

Chinese Source

夫相好本莊嚴佛身，佛身多種，父母生身、尊特身、法性身，身既不同相好亦異，相體不同相用亦異，相用不同相業亦異。三身異者，如林微尼園舉手攀樹，化右脇生，天地大動，阿夷甚驚，披㲲而相，相相炳明決定成佛，悲不能聲。此是生身佛相也，如《釋論》說。尊特身佛巍巍堂堂，譬如須彌映臨大海，所有大相小相亦巍巍堂堂，不同常身常光常相，此即尊特佛身相也。法性身佛者，非是凡夫二乘下地之所能見，唯應度者亦令得見，此即無身之身無相之相。一切智為頭，第一義諦髻，八萬四千法門髮，大悲眼，中道白毫，無漏鼻，十八空舌，四十不共齒，四弘誓肩，三三昧腰，如來藏腹，權實智手，定慧足，如是等相莊嚴法性身佛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now the marks and features are originally to adorn the Buddha's body. The Buddha's bodies are of many kinds: the birth-body from father and mother, the specially honored body, the dharma-nature body. Since the bodies differ, the marks and features also differ; since the essence of the marks differs, the function of the marks also differs; since the function of the marks differs, the karma of the marks also differs. As for the difference of the three bodies: in Lumbinī Garden, [his mother] raised her hand and grasped the tree, and he was born by transformation from her right side; heaven and earth shook greatly; Asita was greatly startled, opened the swaddling cloth and examined the marks — each mark shining clear, certain to become a buddha — and grieved so that he could not speak. This is the mark of the birth-body buddha, as the Great Treatise says. The specially honored body of the Buddha is towering and majestic, like Sumeru shining over the great ocean; all its major and minor marks are likewise towering and majestic, not like the ordinary body, ordinary light, and ordinary marks; this is the mark of the specially honored buddha-body. The dharma-nature body of the Buddha is not what ordinary beings, the two vehicles, or those of the lower grounds can see; only those who should be delivered are also enabled to see it. This is the body of no body, the mark of no mark: the wisdom that knows all things is the head; the truth of the ultimate meaning, the topknot; the eighty-four thousand Dharma-gates, the hair; great compassion, the eyes; the middle way, the white tuft; the uncontaminated, the nose; the eighteen emptinesses, the tongue; the forty unshared [dharmas], the teeth; the four universal vows, the shoulders; the three samādhis, the waist; the Tathāgata-garbha, the belly; provisional and real wisdom, the hands; concentration and wisdom, the feet. Marks such as these adorn the dharma-nature-body buddha.

Chinese Source

種相業者，如《釋論》修百福德成於一相，三千二百福德成三十二相，此即生身佛種相義也。若以空慧導諸相業，一一業悉與空相應，諸相應中空相應最為第一，此是尊特身佛種相義也。若以實相慧導成諸業，一一業無非實相法界，此是法性身佛種相義。三身、三種相、三種相業不得縱橫並別。若一異者則不清淨，非微妙寂滅。以不縱橫並別，故是絕言，言歎所不及也。今經正讚尊特身相，上兼法性、下攝生身，處中而明，實讚者之巧（云云）。一一相皆明其用，如足下安平相，一切魔邪無能傾動者，一切有無無能動者，一切邊無能動者。如頂肉髻相，法不禮、人亦不禮、聖亦不禮，分中別有所出（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the karma that plants the marks: as in the Great Treatise, cultivating a hundred merits accomplishes one mark, and three thousand two hundred merits accomplish the thirty-two marks; this is the meaning of planting marks for the birth-body buddha. If with the wisdom of emptiness one guides the karma of the marks, each karma accords with emptiness; among all accordances, accordance with emptiness is foremost; this is the meaning of planting marks for the specially honored buddha-body. If with the wisdom of the true mark one guides and accomplishes the karmas, each karma is nothing but the true mark and the dharma-realm; this is the meaning of planting marks for the dharma-nature-body buddha. The three bodies, the three kinds of marks, and the three kinds of mark-karma cannot be [taken as] longitudinal or horizontal, together or separate. If one or different, they are not pure, not "subtle, in quiescent extinction." Because they are not longitudinal or horizontal, together or separate, they cut off words: words of praise do not reach them. The present sūtra properly praises the marks of the specially honored body, above including the dharma-nature [body] and below gathering in the birth-body, making it clear from the middle — truly the skill of the one who praises (and so on). For each mark its function is made clear: as with the mark of the soles of the feet being level and even — no māras or heretics can move it, no existence or nonexistence can move it, no extremes can move it. As with the mark of the fleshy topknot on the crown — the Dharma does not bow, people do not bow, sages do not bow — within each portion there is a separate source (and so on).

The Textual Commentary on the Golden Light Sūtra, Fascicle Three

Reading Notes for Fascicle 3

Translator's notes

Lemmas. Quotations from the sūtra follow this site's Golden Light Sūtra, Part 1 (Chapters 3–4). Zhiyi cites the verse by counts of "lines" (行, four phrases of four characters) and "phrases" (句); these counts are his and are translated as they stand.

Chan and hui. In the opening explanation of the name, and again in the "glosses of the characters," Zhiyi treats 懺 and 悔 separately; the two characters are therefore given in romanization, while the compound 懺悔 stays "repentance" as everywhere in this series. 慚 and 愧 are kept apart as "shame" (before heaven) and "remorse" (before people).

Ajātaśatru's verse and the "Pure Conduct" verse. Both are from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T374); the second follows the wording of this site's Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra (the geya example in the "Pure Conduct" chapter). The "bathing in the great ocean" simile is also T374. The Lotus lines are a compressed allusion to "Peaceful Practices" and the phantom-city parable; the wording follows the site's Lotus Sūtra.

The three repentances. "The great repentance / the adorned repentance / the repentance of the mark of no offense" and "My mind is itself empty; offense and merit have no master" follow the Samantabhadra Contemplation Sūtra as drafted in this round (`puxian-guan-jing`, Part 1). The three kinds — by ritual action, by seizing a mark, of no-generation — follow the wording fixed in Zhili's Essentials of Repentance (`xiuchan-yaozhi`, Part 1).

The debtor and the king. From the Larger Prajñāpāramitā (T223), where the debtor "draws near the king and serves at his side." "A book says . grass on the top of a wall": source not located.

The "new version." The "Eradicating the Obstruction of Karma" chapter is in the combined Golden Light (T664). The long passage on parents' kindness that follows does not occur there verbatim and reads as Zhiyi's own expansion. The Sūtra of the Fifty Reckonings (五十校計經) is known from catalogs; the sentence attributed to it was not located.

Stages. The shared teaching's ten grounds (dry wisdom, nature, the eighth person, seeing, thinning, free of desire, completion, pratyekabuddha, bodhisattva, buddha) follow the English of Fourfold Teachings in this round, and 十住 is "the ten abidings," matching the sūtra's own line "settled in the ten abidings."

"Repenting of the mind, ten are given up." The parallel (body three, mouth four) would lead one to expect three; the text is translated as it stands.

Unmanifest offense. 違無作罪 is the offense of violating the unmanifest (avijñapti) precept-body received at ordination; 性罪 "offense by nature" is an act wrong in itself (killing, etc.), as distinct from a breach of rule.

Quotations in the "Praise" sections. "The multitude of worldly toil are his disciples ." follows this site's Vimalakīrti ("Buddha Path" chapter). The Great Treatise definition of 讚 and 歎 is in T1509, fascicle 30 as Zhiyi says. The Auspicious Response (T185) has Māra's challenge "who is the witness?"; the Buddha's gesture toward the earth is narrated there in verse, not in Zhiyi's words. "Recollecting the merit of one buddha ." is from Mañjuśrī's Exposition of the Prajñāpāramitā (T232).

"You are young and strong; I am old and declining." Not located in the Lotus in this form; the burning-house elder is described as "old and declining" (其年衰邁).

The two readings of 難. At the end of the repentance verses Zhiyi weighs reading 難 as "hard" (evil makes good hard) or as "difficulty/obstruction" (good itself obstructs the path); the English "difficulty" covers both, and the sūtra English is kept.

Three bodies. 生身 "birth-body," 尊特身 "specially honored body," 法性身 "dharma-nature body" follow Part 1 of this commentary; 縱橫並別 "longitudinal or horizontal, together or separate" follows the Profound Meaning drafts (縱 longitudinal / 橫 horizontal).
