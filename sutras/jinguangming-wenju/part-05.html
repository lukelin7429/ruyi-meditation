Fascicle Five: The Four Heavenly Kings to the Spirit Saṃjñeya

The fifth fascicle opens the propagation section of the sūtra, which Zhiyi counts as the thirteen chapters from "The Four Heavenly Kings" to "Praising the Buddha," grouped by seven intents. It covers Chapters 6 to 10, in which the heavenly kings and the goddesses vow to protect the sūtra: the Four Heavenly Kings protect the country, Sarasvatī the teacher, Śrī the assembly, the earth goddess the ground, and Saṃjñeya wards off calamity. The long Chapter 6 is read as six rounds of address and the Buddha's confirmation, with the norms a king should follow matched point by point to his six wishes, and the kings' closing verse read through the three bodies (space, sun, moon, and the moon in water). Each chapter name is also explained as a Dharma-gate: Sarasvatī's "great eloquence" in four kinds, Śrī as "merit," Dṛḍhā's "firmness" as the four virtues. The longest single passage takes Saṃjñeya's name as "secret" and reads his fifteen phrases of self-description three times over, as the three contemplations, the three truths and three liberations, and the three bodies, and then as the five buddha-natures. Contemplation-of-mind readings, with the four truths as the four kings and the mental factors as their people, run throughout.

Ru-Yi Translation

Explaining the Chapter "The Four Heavenly Kings"

Chinese Source

四天王者，上升之元首、下界之初天，居半須彌。東黃金埵，王名提頭賴吒，此翻持國，領乾闥婆、富單那；南瑠璃埵，王名毘留勒叉，此翻增長，領鳩槃荼、薜荔多；西白銀埵，王名毘留博叉，此翻雜語，領毘舍闍、毒龍；北水精埵，王名毘沙門，此翻多聞，領夜叉、羅剎。此四王聞經歡喜，各領五百眷屬，發誓護經，從此標章，故稱〈四天王品〉。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Four Heavenly Kings are the first of the ascending [heavens] and the first heaven of the lower realm; they dwell halfway up Sumeru. On the east is the terrace of yellow gold; its king is named Dhṛtarāṣṭra, rendered here "Upholding the Country"; he leads the gandharvas and pūtanas. On the south is the terrace of lapis lazuli; its king is named Virūḍhaka, rendered here "Increase and Growth"; he leads the kumbhāṇḍas and pretas. On the west is the terrace of white silver; its king is named Virūpākṣa, rendered here "Mixed Speech"; he leads the piśācas and the poisonous dragons. On the north is the terrace of crystal; its king is named Vaiśravaṇa, rendered here "Much Hearing"; he leads the yakṣas and rākṣasas. These four kings, hearing the sūtra, rejoice; each leads a retinue of five hundred and vows to protect the sūtra. The chapter is headed from this; hence it is called "The Four Heavenly Kings."

Chinese Source

觀心釋者，東集、南苦、西道、北滅，四諦理是四天，觀四諦智為四王，護四諦境名護國，護心數是護眾生。世者他也，為他說心數，名護他眾生。八部者，苦諦下有利鈍見思，《法華》指此為鬼神，乃至滅諦下亦有見思，是為八部也。若不照四諦理，見思二惑侵害心王、毀損境界，心王亡、境國敗，心數人民迸散，境智俱為鬼神所惱；能觀苦集控作見思則國安民寧。能為他說四諦，是護他國土，遮彼見思使得安樂。是為觀心護世四天王也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Explanation by contemplation of mind: the east is accumulation, the south suffering, the west the path, the north extinction. The principle of the four truths is the four heavens; the wisdom contemplating the four truths is the four kings; protecting the object of the four truths is called protecting the country; protecting the mental factors is protecting beings. "World" means "others": teaching the mental factors for others is called protecting other beings. As for the eight classes: under the truth of suffering there are views and habituated thought, keen and dull; the Lotus points to these as ghosts and spirits; and so on down to the truth of extinction, under which there are also views and habituated thought; these are the eight classes. If one does not illuminate the principle of the four truths, the two delusions of views and habituated thought invade and harm the mind-king and damage its realm; the mind-king perishes, the country of the object is ruined, the people of the mental factors scatter, and object and wisdom are both troubled by the ghosts and spirits. If one can contemplate suffering and accumulation and bring views and habituated thought under control, then the country is at peace and the people tranquil. Being able to teach the four truths for others is protecting their land, blocking their views and habituated thought so that they obtain peace and happiness. This is the four heavenly kings who protect the world, by contemplation of mind.

The Thirteen Chapters of Propagation

Chinese Source

此下十三品是流通段。佛慈季末，使邪惡不翳於正真、經王不壅於來代，有緣之者得正聞正聽，故曰流通。凡為七意：〈四天王〉至〈散脂〉，明天王發誓勸獎人王弘宣此經，正論善集，明人王弘經、天王祐助，亦是示住日弘經方軌。〈鬼神品〉明聽經功德，天神地祇若河若海、菩提薩埵咸守衛之。〈授記品〉證聽經功德之不虛。〈除病〉、〈流水〉引昔聽經之功德，證今護持之非謬。〈捨身品〉引昔行經不惜軀命，誡勸師弟勿吝法財。〈讚佛品〉明諸菩薩稱揚佛法，能宣所宣利益深重（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

The thirteen chapters from here on are the propagation section. The Buddha, compassionate toward the final age, wished that the perverse and evil should not cloud the true and real, and that the king of sūtras should not be blocked from coming ages, so that those with the conditions might rightly hear and rightly listen; hence it is called "propagation." In all there are seven intents. From "The Four Heavenly Kings" to "The Spirit Saṃjñeya" clarifies the heavenly kings making vows and urging kings of humans to proclaim this sūtra. "The Right Treatise" and "Good Gathering" clarify kings of humans spreading the sūtra with the heavenly kings' help, and also show the norms for spreading the sūtra in the days of [its] abiding. "Ghosts and Spirits" clarifies the merit of listening to the sūtra: the gods of heaven and the spirits of earth, whether of rivers or of seas, and the bodhisattvas all guard it. "Predictions" proves that the merit of listening to the sūtra is not false. "Curing Illness" and "Jalavāhana" cite the merit of listening to the sūtra in the past to prove that protecting and upholding it now is not mistaken. "Giving Up the Body" cites the past practice of the sūtra without sparing body and life, to admonish and urge teacher and disciple not to be stingy with Dharma or wealth. "Praising the Buddha" clarifies the bodhisattvas extolling the Buddha's Dharma; the benefit of the proclaimer and the proclaimed is deep and weighty (and so on).

Chinese Source

天王發誓又為五：〈四王〉以天力擁護請者；〈大辯品〉以辯充益說者；〈功德天品〉誓以資財潤請者說者；〈地神品〉誓以地味膏腴味請處說處；〈散脂品〉誓以威武摧外敵壤內難，安於請說聽等也。又〈天王〉護其國，〈大辯〉護其師，〈功德〉護其眾，〈地神〉護其地，〈散脂〉禳其災，令經法大行也（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

The heavenly kings' vows again are five. "The Four Heavenly Kings" supports those who request [the teaching] with heavenly power; "The Goddess Sarasvatī" fills and benefits the teacher with great eloquence; "The Goddess Śrī" vows to enrich those who request and those who teach with wealth; "Dṛḍhā the Earth Goddess" vows to flavor the places of requesting and teaching with the rich fatness of the earth's flavor; "The Spirit Saṃjñeya" vows with martial might to crush outer enemies and ward off inner troubles, giving peace to those who request, teach, listen, and so on. Further, "The Heavenly Kings" protects the country, "Sarasvatī" protects the teacher, "Śrī" protects the assembly, "The Earth Goddess" protects the ground, and "Saṃjñeya" averts calamities, so that the Dharma of the sūtra greatly prevails (and so on).

The First Round: Their Ability to Protect the Country

Chinese Source

〈四天王品〉者，有六番問答，即為六段：第一白佛述有護國之能、第二白佛述其護國之事、第三白佛示其軌模、第四白佛要其法利、第五白佛雙述興衰、第六白佛說偈頌德。初番為二：一白佛、二佛述成。白佛又二：一經家敘、二正白佛。敘敬如文。正白佛文為二：一歎經、二述能護。諸天信法有力，是故歎經。欲得經弘，述其能護。

Ru-Yi Translation

The chapter "The Four Heavenly Kings" has six rounds of question and answer, which make six sections. First, addressing the Buddha, they state that they have the ability to protect the country; second, addressing the Buddha, they state the affairs of protecting the country; third, addressing the Buddha, they show its norms; fourth, addressing the Buddha, they seek its benefit of the Dharma; fifth, addressing the Buddha, they state both flourishing and decline; sixth, addressing the Buddha, they speak verses praising virtue. The first round is two: first, addressing the Buddha; second, the Buddha confirms it. Addressing the Buddha is again two: first, the compilers' narration; second, the address proper. The narration of reverence is as in the text. The text of the address proper is two: first, praising the sūtra; second, stating their ability to protect. The gods believe in the Dharma and have power; therefore they praise the sūtra. Wishing the sūtra to be spread, they state their ability to protect it.

Chinese Source

歎經為三：從「是金光明」下歎經體、從「莊嚴菩薩」下歎經宗、從「此經能照」下歎經用。正說乃多，歎三則略攝於廣也。法性之理佛所護念，文詮此理故言經王。既言經王，知是歎體。約體修行，能令菩薩具二莊嚴成於極果。既言莊嚴，知是歎宗。世天淨天義天皆宗仰極地，施三業供養，恭敬是身、歡喜是意、讚歎是口。又下從地獄上至菩薩，無明未盡通有熱惱，此經能除如月清涼，知是歎用（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Praising the sūtra is three: from "This … the Golden Light" on praises the sūtra's essence; from "it adorns … the bodhisattvas" on praises the sūtra's cardinal doctrine; from "This sūtra can illuminate" on praises the sūtra's function. The main teaching is extensive; praising these three gathers the broad into the brief. The principle of the dharma-nature is "held in mind and protected by the buddhas," and the text expresses this principle; hence "king of sūtras." Since it says "king of sūtras," we know it praises the essence. Cultivating practice on the basis of the essence can make bodhisattvas complete in the two adornments and accomplish the ultimate fruit. Since it says "adorns," we know it praises the cardinal doctrine. The worldly gods, the pure gods, and the gods of meaning all revere the ultimate ground as their cardinal doctrine and offer the three kinds of action: "revered" is the body, "rejoice" is the mind, "praised" is the mouth. Further, from the hells below up to the bodhisattvas above, while ignorance is not yet exhausted, all alike have burning distress; this sūtra can remove it, cool like the moon. We know this praises the function (and so on).

Chinese Source

從「世尊是金光明」下，是第二述能護國。文為二：一內以法護國、二外以策護國。法護國又四：一護國之由、二以法護國、三以天黨護國、四以天眼護國。護國由者，由聞此經獲於四益，身益光明、力益勇猛、心益增進、德益尊嚴。理獲二益，謂法身慧命皆得增長。由國弘經，致斯法潤，寧得不護？述所以者，此義正與觀心相應，以四諦智護四諦境，即是修行正法。以四諦智導諸心數，使諸心數不行，故名行般若波羅蜜。即是能說正法，內有如此護國所以，名護世王正治國土爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "World-Honored One! When this … the Golden Light" on is the second, stating their ability to protect the country. The text has two [parts]: first, protecting the country inwardly by the Dharma; second, protecting the country outwardly by strategy. Protecting the country by the Dharma is again four: first, the reason for protecting the country; second, protecting the country by the Dharma; third, protecting the country with the heavenly host; fourth, protecting the country with the heavenly eye. The reason for protecting the country: because they hear this sūtra they gain four benefits — the body benefits in light, strength benefits in courage, the mind benefits in advancing, virtue benefits in majesty. In principle they gain two benefits, namely that the Dharma-body and the wisdom-life both grow. Since this moisture of the Dharma comes from the country's spreading the sūtra, how could they not protect it? As for stating the reason: this meaning corresponds exactly with contemplation of mind. Protecting the object of the four truths with the wisdom of the four truths is "cultivating the true Dharma." Guiding the mental factors with the wisdom of the four truths so that the mental factors do not act is therefore called practicing prajñāpāramitā. This is "able to teach the true Dharma." Having within such a reason for protecting the country, they are called "world-protecting kings" who rightly govern the land.

Chinese Source

帥黨護國者，此亦與觀心相應。心王帥心數黨，降伏見思利鈍諸使，如諸天王共眷屬遮諸惡鬼，如轉輪王與七寶千子有所至處四方歸德。四王共五百所臨之地，何惡不除邪？用天眼護國者，以報得天眼徹視，無幽不燭，防萌杜漸，何惡不除邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

Leading the host to protect the country: this too corresponds with contemplation of mind. The mind-king leads the host of mental factors to subdue the keen and dull agents of views and habituated thought, just as the heavenly kings together with their retinues "hold back the evil ghosts," and just as a wheel-turning king with his seven jewels and thousand sons, wherever he arrives, the four directions turn to his virtue. On the ground where the four kings come with their five hundred, what evil is not removed? Using the heavenly eye to protect the country: with the heavenly eye obtained as reward they see through everything, and nothing dark is unlit; guarding against the sprout and stopping the gradual growth, what evil is not removed?

Protecting the Country by Wise Strategy

Chinese Source

二從「若此國土有諸衰耗」下是智策護國，令內外因緣和合。文為三：一若王國多災、種種艱難，謂兵饑疾者，我以智策勸法師往、或威神勸往、或現形勸往、或降夢勸往。法師若往廣宣此經，如日出朝陽雰霧自歇。此勸外緣也。次王心無智照，災承闇入；若有明慧，變怪不生。師既秉法來儀，王須專心聽受。王若勤聽，天亦勤護，所以加於可加、護於可護，一人有慶賴及萬方，王身與國安隱無患。此內因也。若外雖有弘法人，王內心不殷重，則不和合，不能禳災也。二者王無惠施則寡於福祿，如不勤田，倉厨少穀。勸王傾財供給四眾，四眾得安福資於王，舉國眷屬一切無患。此內外因緣和合，能致豐年流衍（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, from "If in this land there are declines and losses" on is protecting the country by wise strategy, making inner and outer causes and conditions come together. The text has three [parts]. First, if the king's country has many calamities and "all kinds of hardship," namely war, famine, and disease, "we" by wise strategy urge the Dharma master to go, or urge him to go by majestic spiritual power, or urge him to go by appearing in form, or urge him to go by sending down dreams. If the Dharma master goes and widely proclaims this sūtra, it is like the sun rising in the morning: the mist settles of itself. This is urging the outer condition. Next, if the king's mind has no illumination of wisdom, calamities follow and enter in the dark; if he has clear wisdom, uncanny changes do not arise. Since the master comes in fine bearing holding the Dharma, the king must listen and receive with concentrated mind. If the king listens diligently, the gods also protect diligently; therefore they add to what can be added and protect what can be protected; when the one man has blessing, the myriad regions rely on it, and the king's person and country are secure and free of trouble. This is the inner cause. If outwardly there is someone to spread the Dharma but inwardly the king's mind is not earnest, then they do not come together, and calamities cannot be averted. Second, if the king does not give, he will be poor in blessings and stipends, just as when one does not work the fields, the granary and kitchen have little grain. [They] urge the king to pour out his wealth to provide for the fourfold assembly; when the fourfold assembly is at peace, the blessings aid the king, and the whole country and household are free of trouble. This is inner and outer causes and conditions coming together, which can bring abundant years flowing on (and so on).

Chinese Source

三者王身無先王之德行，臣民不從；口無先王之法言，隣國不詠。今勸王三業供養，恭敬是身業、尊重是意業、讚歎是口業。夫高以下為基、辯以訥為師，屈尊敬卑，功亦大矣。以天威力使隣國遙崇，羽檄稱讚、歌詠羨慕。三業顒顒，上之化下如風之靡草。隣國既然，國人牛馬草葉無不低迴內向。此則能讚所讚因緣和合，頌聲溢於隣國（云云），經文分明尋之可見。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, if the king's person lacks the virtuous conduct of the former kings, ministers and people do not follow him; if his mouth lacks the Dharma words of the former kings, neighboring countries do not sing his praises. Now [they] urge the king to make offerings with the three kinds of action: "revere" is the action of body, "honor" is the action of mind, "praise" is the action of speech. Now, the high takes the low as its foundation, and eloquence takes reticence as its teacher; bending the honored to revere the lowly — great indeed is the achievement. By heavenly majestic power they make neighboring countries look up to him from afar, with feathered dispatches praising, singing, and admiring. When the three kinds of action are solemn and earnest, the transformation of those below by the one above is like the wind bending the grass. Since the neighboring countries are so, the people of the country, the cattle and horses, the grass and leaves, all bow and turn inward. This is the causes and conditions of the praiser and the praised coming together, so that songs of praise overflow into neighboring countries (and so on). The sūtra text is clear; seek it and it can be seen.

The Buddha Confirms the First Round

Chinese Source

次佛述成，文為二：初合述歎經、二述其能護國。四王初歎經說既合理，佛述而成之。成其上體、宗、用三歎，故言佛合述善哉善哉。其上總歎一教，佛述成其遍讚百千諸佛。諸佛從是法生，故舉多讚成於一也。從「於諸佛所」下，述成其能護國。又為二：初述以法護國、二述以智眼護國。法護國有四，佛皆述成。上明護國之由，由聞經得益。佛述今益，良由先種。「發心畢竟二不別，如是二心前心難，是故敬禮初發心。」此舉前以成後，述後以顯前。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next the Buddha confirms it. The text has two [parts]: first, confirming together the praise of the sūtra; second, confirming their ability to protect the country. The four kings first praise the sūtra; since what they say accords with principle, the Buddha confirms and completes it. He completes the three praises above — essence, cardinal doctrine, and function — hence it says the Buddha together confirms: "Excellent, excellent." Above, they praised a single teaching in general; the Buddha confirms it by praising everywhere hundreds of thousands of buddhas. The buddhas are born from this Dharma; hence he raises the many to praise and complete the one. From "in the presence of the buddhas" on confirms their ability to protect the country. This again is two: first, confirming protection of the country by the Dharma; second, confirming protection of the country by the eye of wisdom. Protecting the country by the Dharma has four [parts], and the Buddha confirms them all. Above, it clarified the reason for protecting the country: benefit gained by hearing the sūtra. The Buddha states that the present benefit is truly due to earlier planting. "Arousing the mind and the ultimate are not two different things; of these two minds, the former is the harder; therefore I pay homage to the first arousing of the mind." This raises the earlier to complete the later, and states the later to reveal the earlier.

Chinese Source

從「說於正法」下，述其以法護世。上云能說能行所以名為護世，佛亦述成能說能行得名護世（云云）。從「汝等今日」下，超述其天眼護國，小不次第，於義無失。上云以淨天眼過於人眼，佛述長夜利益。夫天眼夜照不假日光，故言長夜也。從「汝等四王及諸眷屬」下，追述其天黨護國。上直言護國，佛今加讚是護三世諸佛正法也。從「汝等四王及餘天眾」下，述其智眼和合護國。上云請法師入境禳惡，此功歸己，能却修羅之陣。汝勸王聽經、供給四眾，佛述其內智外福實是消伏諸苦能致安樂。如文（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "taught the true Dharma" on confirms their protecting the world by the Dharma. Above they said that because they can teach and can practice they are called world-protectors; the Buddha also confirms that by teaching and practicing they get the name world-protectors (and so on). From "You today" on, skipping ahead, he confirms their protecting the country with the heavenly eye; it is slightly out of order, but there is no loss to the meaning. Above it said "with pure heavenly eyes surpassing human eyes"; the Buddha states the benefit "through the long night." The heavenly eye illuminates at night without depending on sunlight; hence "the long night." From "You four kings and your retinues" on, going back, he confirms their protecting the country with the heavenly host. Above they only said that they protect the country; the Buddha now adds praise that this is protecting the true Dharma of the buddhas of the three times. From "you four kings and the other hosts of gods" on, he confirms their protecting the country by bringing together the eye of wisdom. Above they said that they request the Dharma master to enter the territory and avert evil; this merit returns to themselves and can drive back the ranks of the asuras. "You" urge the king to listen to the sūtra and provide for the fourfold assembly; the Buddha states that this inner wisdom and outer blessing truly "dispel all sufferings" and can bring peace and happiness. As in the text (and so on).

The Second Round: The Affairs of Protecting the Country

Chinese Source

第二番白佛述護國之事。文為二：一白佛、二佛述成。白佛又二：一是經流布之處，其王自能頂受，又供給四眾者，我及眷屬誓當隱形令其顯益。二者惡隣興兵侵斥善國，我當隱形起諸怖懅、種種難起，令其軍兵顯然退散。尚不擾邊，況能壞中（云云）？

Ru-Yi Translation

The second round: addressing the Buddha, they state the affairs of protecting the country. The text has two [parts]: first, addressing the Buddha; second, the Buddha confirms it. Addressing the Buddha is again two. First, wherever this sūtra spreads, if the king himself can receive it reverently and also provides for the fourfold assembly, "we and our retinues" vow, with our forms hidden, to bring them manifest benefit. Second, if an evil neighbor raises troops and invades a good country, "we" will hide our forms and raise fears and all kinds of difficulties, so that their troops plainly retreat and scatter. They will not even disturb the frontier; how much less could they ruin the center (and so on)?

次佛述成，文以為二：從「爾時佛讚下至無鬪訟之事」，是述成初意。其上隱形護國欲使經弘，佛述隱形是護三寶，我是佛寶、修習菩提是法寶、諸王無鬪訟是僧寶，述其護一而能成三也。從「四王當知此閻浮提」下，是述成後意。上以天威懅之、天力退之，似若憎惡愛善。佛勸慈心平等向之，為惡者自懅、為善者自豫，非薄惡而厚善，成其懅退之意也。又勸諸國各守本業，住境自樂，勿起貪企，諸王和則民無夭，法興盛則熏諸天。佛告帝釋，鬪諍因緣人天損減，善能慈和天下，非止供養於我，則是供養三世如來；非止安於一王，遍安諸王；非止安於一國，遍安一切。故以慈和述成第二意也（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next the Buddha confirms it; the text has two [parts]. From "At that time the Buddha praised" down to "free of misfortune, decline, and strife" confirms the first intent. Above, protecting the country with hidden forms was meant to spread the sūtra; the Buddha states that protecting with hidden forms is protecting the Three Jewels: "I" is the Buddha Jewel; "cultivated … awakening" is the Dharma Jewel; the kings' being "free of … strife" is the Saṅgha Jewel. He states that their protecting the one can accomplish the three. From "Four kings, you should know: in this Jambudvīpa" on confirms the later intent. Above, they frightened [the enemy] with heavenly majesty and drove them back with heavenly power, as if hating the evil and loving the good. The Buddha urges them to face [all] equally with a kind mind: those who do evil will be frightened of themselves, those who do good will be at ease of themselves; it is not slighting the evil and favoring the good; [thus] he confirms their intent of frightening and driving back. He further urges the countries each to keep to its own work and be content in its own territory, not giving rise to greed and covetous designs; when the kings are in harmony the people do not die young, and when the Dharma flourishes it perfumes the gods. The Buddha tells Śakra: the causes and conditions of strife diminish humans and gods; to be well able to bring kindness and harmony to the world is not only making offerings to me but making offerings to the tathāgatas of the three times; not only bringing peace to one king, but peace to all kings everywhere; not only peace to one country, but peace to all everywhere. Thus he confirms the second intent with kindness and harmony (and so on).

The Third Round: The Norms for a King

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

The third address to the Buddha shows the norms for kings of humans. The text has two [parts]: first, setting out their wishes; second, showing the norms. The wishes are six: first, wishing to keep themselves secure; second, wishing to keep wives and children secure; third, wishing to keep palaces secure; fourth, wishing the domain they rule to be outstanding; fifth, wishing to gather blessings and virtue; sixth, wishing the country to be without worry and suffering. The six wishes are as in the text. From "World-Honored One, such a king of humans" on is the second, showing the norms. Though not in order, the six are complete. Above he wished to keep himself secure; now it shows "should not be heedless": when the mind is controlled, the body is secure. Above he wished to keep his palace secure; now it shows adorning the Dharma hall: banners and canopies shine above, incense and flowers are beautiful below; when the Three Jewels make use of it, the Cypress-Beam [Terrace] suffers no disaster. Above he wished his domain to be outstanding; now it shows bathing, anointing with fragrance, being sincere and reverent, and removing arrogance: when one person is reverent here, the eight bounds rest there. Above he wished to gather a heap of blessings; now it shows listening to the sūtra with right mindfulness. Listening to the sūtra with right mindfulness can bring immeasurable merit: the gods of heaven exhaust their strength to cover him, the earth spirits exhaust their strength to bear him, the ghosts and spirits exhaust their strength to protect him, ministers and people exhaust their strength to love him. Above he wished to keep wives and children secure; now it shows "speak to them with a gentle face" and urge them to produce blessings; when inner and outer cultivate good, the benefits evoked are many. Above he wished his country to be without worry and suffering; now it shows that he should himself rejoice and exert himself, forget weariness, and do benefit doubly. "When the one man is pleased, the four seas are tranquil" — this is what is meant.

Next the Buddha confirms it. The text has two [parts]: first, confirming the six methods; next, confirming the six wishes. The four kings spoke in terms of six matters, and what they said was little, stopping at the present world. The Buddha speaks in terms of one matter, but its benefit is much, surpassing immeasurable ages; he confirms it doubly. From "the Buddha told the Four Great Heavenly Kings" on: just spreading and listening to the sūtra confirms the method of keeping oneself secure. Above it showed only that the mind is not heedless; now it adds going out of the palace in ceremonial array to welcome [the teacher], meeting nayutas of buddhas at every step. Since the method is doubled, the person too is all the more secure. "Further he transcends the difficulties … for as many eons" confirms the method of keeping the country secure. Above it was only one age without worry and suffering; now he transcends the difficulties of birth-and-death of that many [eons]. A wheel-turning king has the jade maiden and a thousand sons, none of them hostile; through that many eons wife and children are always secure. "He also obtains such present-life … sovereign power" confirms the method of keeping the domain secure above. "He always obtains most excellent … palaces of the seven jewels" confirms the method of keeping palaces secure above. "Wherever he is born … complete in an immeasurable heap of blessings" confirms the method of gathering blessings above.

The Fourth Round: The Canopy of Incense

Chinese Source

第四白佛，要其人王施善，此由第三段末。文為二：一白佛、二佛述成。白佛又二：一人王運心、二天宮相現。人王心存至典，是故香作金光。迴施必得，是故天宮相現。香至天宮、龍宮、鬼神等宮，總至三法界爾。觀心解者，以智慧火然實相香，起戒定慧煙。實相是真法，故言金光。戒慧與理冥，故徹照無礙也（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

The fourth address to the Buddha seeks the good offered by the king of humans; this follows from the end of the third section. The text has two [parts]: first, addressing the Buddha; second, the Buddha confirms it. Addressing the Buddha is again two: first, the king of humans applies his mind; second, signs appear in the heavenly palaces. Because the king's mind dwells on the supreme scripture, the incense becomes golden light. Because what is turned over and given will surely be received, signs appear in the heavenly palaces. The fragrance reaches the palaces of the gods, the palaces of the dragons, the palaces of the ghosts and spirits, and so on — in all, it reaches three dharma-realms. Explanation by contemplation of mind: with the fire of wisdom one burns the incense of the true mark, raising the smoke of precepts, concentration, and wisdom. The true mark is the true Dharma; hence "golden light." Precepts and wisdom merge with principle; hence it illuminates through without obstruction (and so on).

Chinese Source

次佛述成，文為二：先述香光非但至天宮等三法界，遍至百億諸宮，又至恒沙佛上。總而言之，遍至十法界也。從「諸佛世尊聞是香氣」下，述成人王運善奉施諸天，為諸佛所讚。先讚因成，次讚果滿，如文。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next the Buddha confirms it. The text has two [parts]. First, he states that the light of the fragrance reaches not only the three dharma-realms of the heavenly palaces and so on, but reaches everywhere to a hundred koṭis of palaces, and further to [the space] above buddhas [as many as] the Ganges sands. In sum, it reaches everywhere in the ten dharma-realms. From "The buddhas, World-Honored Ones, smelling this wondrous fragrance" on confirms that the king of humans who applies his good and offers it to the gods is praised by the buddhas. First the cause accomplished is praised, next the fruit fulfilled, as in the text.

Why is there no Dharma-food? The king does not spread the sūtra. As in the text.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Praising the bodily marks, the text has five [parts]: the first line praises two marks above, namely the eyes and the teeth. The next line and a half praises the two virtues of wisdom and cutting off, namely wisdom and samādhi. The next two lines praise two marks below, namely the level [soles] and the web. The next two phrases praise beyond words, namely "inconceivable." The next two phrases conclude the praise. The marks and characteristics are not solely in the response-body; the reward- and Dharma[-bodies] share them too. Adorning the body born of father and mother — these are the marks and characteristics of response. Adorning the specially honored body — these are the marks and characteristics of reward. The verses begin by setting forth the Buddha-moon; now it first concludes the Buddha-moon as the three bodies, then concludes that the three bodies are interfused; hence "without obstruction." Next it concludes the Buddha-sun; hence "like a flame." Next it concludes the Buddha's transformation-body, which is the meaning of four bodies; hence "like a phantom." All are complete with three bodies and four bodies; this then is "without obstruction." Having concluded, they bow; bowing to the Buddha-moon is also bowing to the Buddha-sun and the Buddha-transformation. At the beginning of the chapter they praise the sūtra, praising its essence, cardinal doctrine, and function; at the end of the chapter they praise the Buddha: the words differ but the meaning is the same. "The true Dharma-body of the Buddha" is the essence; "the Buddha-moon is pure" is the cardinal doctrine; responding and manifesting "like the moon in water" is the function. The heavenly kings' heavenly eloquence is as wondrous as this.

Question: space is a simile for the Dharma-body, the moon for the reward-body; does space make the moon or not make the moon? If space makes the moon, [yet] the moon is not made by space; if space does not make the moon, how does the moon rely on space? "See directly inconceivable light of wisdom": the light is real wisdom, like the light of sun and moon, always bright without ceasing; this real wisdom illuminates the inconceivable object of the truth of the real and accomplishes the perfect-pure liberation. "Inconceivable torch of wisdom," "practice of wisdom," and "heap of wisdom" are all provisional wisdom, like a person holding a torch, bending and turning to light up things, now raised, now set down, following and according with the conditions of capacity, now here, now there. "Practice" means cause; "heap" means fruit. From the cause one goes toward the fruit; when the fruit arises the cause is set aside; all is provisional wisdom illuminating the inconceivable conventional truth and accomplishing the expedient liberation. "Inconceivable sphere of wisdom" is the suchness-wisdom of dharmas. This wisdom merges with the suchness of dharmas; hence "inconceivable sphere of wisdom." This wisdom illuminates the inconceivable truth of the middle way of the first meaning and accomplishes the nature-pure liberation. If the three truths and the three liberations were one or different, longitudinal or horizontal, together or separate, they would not be inconceivable. Because they are not one or different, together or separate, they are called inconceivable; because they are inconceivable, they are called secret.

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, explaining by the five kinds of buddha-nature: the three names correct nature, conditioning nature, and revealing nature do not differ. One school takes the fruit-nature and the object-nature to make five. Another school takes the fruit-nature and the fruit-of-fruit nature to make five. If one makes the fruit-of-fruit nature, the object-nature is submerged and absorbed into the conditioning-cause nature. If one opens out the object-nature, the fruit-of-fruit nature is submerged and absorbed into the fruit-nature. Because opening and combining differ, in the end the number is five. Now the five "knows" are matched to the five buddha-natures. "I know all dharmas": knowing that "within all dharmas there is the nature of peace and happiness." The nature of peace and happiness is the correct-cause buddha-nature. "All conditioned dharmas": the good of immeasurable merit, [even] lowering the head or raising a hand, is the conditioning-cause buddha-nature. "Understand all dharmas" is the empty wisdom of prajñā, the revealing-cause buddha-nature. "Know the divisions of dharmas": the cause and fruit of the worldly and the world-transcending are not confused — this is the object-cause buddha-nature. "In accord with the Dharma abide in the suchness-nature" is the fruit-nature, ultimately abiding at peace in suchness. "Contain and receive all dharmas" is again the fruit-nature, able to illuminate both, containing and receiving. If one makes the fruit-of-fruit nature, one takes "know the divisions of dharmas" as the fruit-nature — the wisdom of the attained fruit illuminating distinctly is "divisions" — and "abide in the suchness-nature" and "contain and receive" as the fruit-of-fruit nature (and so on). Reading Notes for Fascicle 5

Translator's notes

Sūtra quotations. All lemmas reuse the English of this site's Golden Light Sūtra, Parts 2–3 (Chapters 6–10). Where Zhiyi quotes loosely (e.g. 「復得超越爾許劫難」 for the sūtra's 「復得超越如是等劫生死之難」), the sūtra English is kept with ellipses.

述成 "confirms." Each address of the kings is followed by the Buddha's 述成, "confirming [and completing]" what they said; rendered "the Buddha confirms it" throughout.

The four kings' names (unit 4). Zhiyi's glosses 持國 "Upholding the Country," 增長 "Increase and Growth," 雜語 "Mixed Speech," 多聞 "Much Hearing" are translated; the Sanskrit names follow the sūtra.

Chapter names explained. 大辯 is Sarasvatī's Chinese name, literally "great eloquence"; 功德天 "merit goddess" is Śrī; 堅牢 "firm" is Dṛḍhā. The Chinese and literal meaning are added where Zhiyi explains the name.

"In the days of [its] abiding" (unit 5). 示住日弘經方軌 is translated literally. Alternatively, 住 may be a copying error for 往, "the norms for spreading the sūtra in former days," which fits the past stories of Chapters 11–12.

"The Buddha tells Śakra" (unit 10). 佛告帝釋 does not match the sūtra, where the Buddha addresses the four kings; translated as written.

Cypress-Beam [Terrace] (unit 11). The Boliang Terrace of Emperor Wu of Han, which burned down; "the eight bounds" (八紘) are the ends of the world.

The three treatises (unit 15). The four Vedas, Vyākaraṇa (grammar), and the Sāṃkhya, Vaiśeṣika, and Ṛṣabha (勒沙婆, the Jain founder) schools; the sūtra's "treatises on leaving desire," "good treatises," and "treatises of the immortals."

Quotations. "Arousing the mind and the ultimate are not two different things …" is the Nirvāṇa Sūtra verse, in the English of the site's Fahua Xuanyi. "Within all dharmas there is the nature of peace and happiness" is a verse line of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374, Kāśyapa's questions). The Middle Treatise verse (T1564) follows the site's Mohe Zhiguan. The Viśeṣacinti line paraphrases T0586 (「一切法正，一切法邪」), in the site's wording "false / correct." The Samādhi of Pure Deliverance (淨度三昧經) is an apocryphal sūtra; source not located. The Prajñā line "none surpasses the Dharma" is not located in the Prajñā sūtras; Guanding's Nirvāṇa commentary (T1767) quotes a similar line. The Peacock King Sūtra count of twenty-eight divisions was not located verbatim in T0982–T0988.

Saṃjñeya as "secret" (units 27–28). Zhiyi glosses the name as 密; his reading of the fifteen phrases by the three contemplations, three truths, and three bodies is his own. In the sūtra 解 and 了 are both "understand"; where Zhiyi separates 覺 and 了 the Chinese is added. 境 is "object" in the commentary and "sphere" in the sūtra phrase "sphere of wisdom."

"If so" (unit 28). 「若然者」下兩種五句 does not match anything in Dharmakṣema's Chapter 10; it may refer to a passage in another version. Translated literally.

Five buddha-natures (unit 28). 正性／緣性／了性 correct / conditioning / revealing nature; 果性 fruit-nature; 果果性 fruit-of-fruit nature; 境界性 object-nature. The "Master" who did not explain the second set is Zhiyi; the remark is Guanding's.

Saṃjñeya's colleagues (unit 27). 樂欲 Delight-Desire, 檀帝 Tandi (no reconstruction), 善現 Good Appearance. "Saṃjñeya-suma" (散脂修摩) is Zhiyi's form of the full name.
