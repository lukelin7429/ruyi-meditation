Fascicle Two: The Obstructions Turned and the Chapter "Lifespan"

The second fascicle finishes Zhiyi's reading of the sūtra's opening verses and then covers the whole of Chapter 2, "Lifespan." It first separates affliction from karma and reads the sūtra's evil stars, portents, and dreams as outer signs of karma about to ripen, all traceable to the five precepts; the sūtra's instructions to bathe, listen, and keep the mind utmost are read as a method that turns the three obstructions into the three virtues. The chapter title "Lifespan" (壽量, literally "life and measure") is then explained by the three bodies: the Dharma-body's "life" and "measure" are only designations, the reward-body's are wisdom according with principle, and the response-body's are long or short as beings require. Faith-Mark's doubt — why a buddha who never killed and always gave food lives only eighty years — is unfolded through the goods of restraint and action, Dharma-food, and the "giving" of skin, blood, bone, and marrow as precepts, concentration, wisdom, and the middle way. The ten auspicious marks in his chamber are matched to the ten bodhisattva grounds, and the four buddhas' similes of water, mountains, earth, and space are read in turn through the four truths, the four foundations of mindfulness, and the four virtues, to show that the brief span of the response-body does not measure the Buddha's life.

Affliction and Karma

Chinese Source

報障，如上可解。煩惱與業云何？數人云：數起而輕名煩惱，數起而重名為業。天台師云：任運常有是煩惱，卒起決定心發動身口、必牽來報者是業。若爾、惡星災異都不關心，云何是業障？此乃外相表業將起，是業責報之相，即得是業障也。若煩惱業轉，報未必轉；若報轉業，煩惱必轉。通論，見思煩惱皆有愁憂恐怖；別論，愁憂屬見煩惱，恐怖屬愛煩惱。今不具記（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

The obstruction of retribution can be understood as above. What about affliction and karma? The Abhidharma masters say: what arises repeatedly and is light is called affliction; what arises repeatedly and is heavy is called karma. The Master of Tiantai says: what is always present spontaneously is affliction; what arises suddenly with a decisive mind, moves body and speech, and must draw a future retribution is karma. If so, evil stars and calamitous portents have nothing to do with the mind; how are they the obstruction of karma? These are outer signs indicating that karma is about to arise; they are the marks of karma demanding its retribution, and so they may be the obstruction of karma. If affliction and karma turn, retribution does not necessarily turn; if retribution turns, karma and affliction must turn. Speaking generally, the afflictions of views and of habituated thought both involve sorrow, worry, and fear; speaking specifically, sorrow and worry belong to the afflictions of views, and fear to the afflictions of craving. Here we do not record it all (and so on).

Destroying the Obstruction of Karma

Chinese Source

三、破業障者、從「惡星災異」下是也。業將感報故其相前現，相名表發，意在此也。惡星者，別有客星也。亦是五星二十八宿，一方有七，四七二十八也。違其度數、失其分野。若熒惑亂行、麻彗暴出。是客星也。災異者，風雨雪霜乖候等是也。眾邪者有三：人邪、鬼邪、法邪是也。蟲道者，毒鬼也。又言三毒是蟲也。變怪者，詭怪也，謂禽獸醜惡形聲等是也。惡夢者，心靈潛密，業現其中，夢見不適意事是也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, destroying the obstruction of karma: from "evil stars and calamitous portents" on. When karma is about to bring its retribution, its marks appear beforehand; "marks" means indications, and the intent lies here. "Evil stars" are, in particular, guest stars. They are also the five planets and the twenty-eight lunar mansions, seven in each direction, four sevens making twenty-eight. When they depart from their measured courses and lose their allotted fields — as when Mars moves erratically or comets suddenly appear — these are guest stars. "Calamitous portents" are wind, rain, snow, and frost out of season, and the like. "The many perverse" are of three kinds: human perversity, ghostly perversity, and perversity of dharmas. "Arts of sorcery" (蟲道, "the way of vermin") are poisonous ghosts; it is also said that the three poisons are the vermin. "Uncanny events" are weird and strange things, such as the ugly and evil forms and sounds of birds and beasts. "Evil dreams": the numinous mind is hidden and secret, and karma appears within it; dreaming of disagreeable things is this.

Chinese Source

夫諸業表報不出五罪，若惡星表亡身失命者，殺生業相也；惡星表神棄困窮者，竊盜業相也：惡星表親離幽厄者，淫罪業相也；惡星表誣枉讒謗者，妄語業相也；惡星表喪失財產者，飲酒業相也。其餘災異怪禽惡夢等，隨其時節各有所表，細心推詳，不出五罪之報。行者知解推之，何須折篾鑽龜問管公明邪？

Ru-Yi Translation

All karmic indications of retribution do not go beyond the five offenses. If evil stars indicate loss of body and life, these are the karmic marks of killing; if evil stars indicate being abandoned by the spirits and poverty, these are the karmic marks of stealing; if evil stars indicate separation from relatives and imprisonment, these are the karmic marks of the offense of lust; if evil stars indicate false accusation and slander, these are the karmic marks of false speech; if evil stars indicate loss of wealth and property, these are the karmic marks of drinking wine. The other calamities, portents, uncanny birds, and evil dreams each indicate something according to their time; examined with a careful mind, they do not go beyond the retribution of the five offenses. If practitioners know how to understand and infer this, what need is there to split bamboo, bore turtle shells, or consult Guan Gongming?

The Method That Empties Evil

Chinese Source

從「當淨洗浴」下，二、舉方法能空於惡也。前業相外彰，報對不久；內無方法，何以禳之？約其三業作三德之方，以事表理也。洗浴臭體，擬作法身；緘脣攝耳，擬聽般若；至心清淨，擬作解脫。前令洗浴內身，後更勸淨外服，內外相成爾。前但令聽，後誡令專聽，鄭重緘口爾。前令至心，後示至心之境，成其方法爾。夫人身本於不淨，蓮華本於淤泥，譬如栴檀生于伊蘭，世間現見也。今近因三業規矩、遠成三德妙義，可不信哉？洗浴法身能禳報障，攝耳緘口能禳煩惱障，至心能禳業障（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "they should bathe themselves clean" on is, second, raising the method that can empty evil. Above, the marks of karma are manifest outwardly and retribution is not far off; if inwardly there is no method, how can it be averted? In terms of the three kinds of action, he makes a method of the three virtues, using phenomena to indicate principle. Bathing the foul body is likened to making the Dharma-body; closing the lips and gathering in the ears is likened to listening to prajñā; utmost mind, pure, is likened to making liberation. First he has them bathe the inner body, then further urges them to clean the outer garments: inner and outer complete each other. First he only has them listen, then admonishes them to listen single-mindedly, seriously closing the mouth. First he has them [have] utmost mind, then shows the object of utmost mind, completing the method. The human body is at root impure, and the lotus is rooted in mud; just as sandalwood grows among eraṇḍa trees — this is seen in the world. Now, near at hand, by the rules of the three kinds of action, and far off, one accomplishes the wondrous meaning of the three virtues; how can one not believe it? Bathing, the Dharma-body, can avert the obstruction of retribution; gathering in the ears and closing the mouth can avert the obstruction of afflictions; utmost mind can avert the obstruction of karma (and so on).

Chinese Source

從「是經威德」下，四、結成也。能悉消除者，明三障轉也。今其寂滅者，三德成也。寂滅秖是涅槃，涅槃秖是三德。前三業方法既成，三障理數應轉。三障既其已轉，理數成於三德，報障轉成法身德、煩惱障轉成般若德、業障轉成解脫德。前寄事相，將淺以表深；後明寂滅，將深以結淺。經文繡密，見之者寡（云云）。「護世四王」下，敘流通中品，皆如上說。

Ru-Yi Translation

From "The majestic virtue of this sūtra" on is, fourth, the conclusion. "Can wholly remove" makes clear that the three obstructions are turned. "Bring them to quiescent extinction" means the three virtues are accomplished. Quiescent extinction is just nirvāṇa, and nirvāṇa is just the three virtues. Since the method of the three kinds of action has been accomplished, the three obstructions by rights must turn. Since the three obstructions have turned, by rights they become the three virtues: the obstruction of retribution turns into the virtue of the Dharma-body, the obstruction of afflictions turns into the virtue of prajñā, the obstruction of karma turns into the virtue of liberation. The former relies on phenomenal marks, using the shallow to indicate the deep; the latter clarifies quiescent extinction, using the deep to conclude the shallow. The sūtra's text is finely embroidered, and few see it (and so on). From "The four world-protecting kings" on narrates the chapters within the propagation; all is as explained above.

Explaining the Chapter "Lifespan"

Chinese Source

佛本無身無壽亦無於量，隨順世間而論三身，亦隨順世間而論三壽量。法身者，師軌法性，還以法性為身。此身非色質亦非心智，非陰界入之所攝持，彊指法性為法身爾。法性壽者，非報得命根亦無連持，彊指不遷不變名之為壽。此壽非長量亦非短量，無延無促，彊指法界同虛空量。此即非身之身、無壽之壽、不量之量也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha at root has no body, no life, and no measure; in accord with the world we discuss three bodies, and in accord with the world we also discuss three lives and measures. The Dharma-body: the teacher takes the dharma-nature as norm and in turn takes the dharma-nature as body. This body is neither material form nor mental wisdom, not held or included by the aggregates, realms, and entrances; the dharma-nature is forcibly designated the Dharma-body. The life of the dharma-nature is not a life-faculty obtained as retribution, nor has it continuity; its not shifting and not changing is forcibly designated life. This life is neither a long measure nor a short measure, neither extended nor contracted; the dharma-realm, equal to empty space, is forcibly designated its measure. This is the body that is no body, the life that is no life, the measure that is no measure.

Chinese Source

報身者，修行之所感也。《法華》云「久修業所得」，《涅槃》云「大般涅槃修道得故，如如智照如如境，菩提智慧與法性相應相冥。」相應者，如函蓋相應也。相冥者，如水乳相冥也。法身非身非不身，智既應冥亦非身非不身，彊名此智為報身也。法壽非常非無常，智既應冥亦非常非無常，彊名常為壽也。法既非量非無量，智既應冥亦非量非無量，彊名無量為量也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The reward-body is what is brought about by practice. The Lotus says, "attained through long cultivation of [good] deeds"; the Nirvāṇa says, "Great parinirvāṇa is attained by cultivating the path; suchness-wisdom illuminates the suchness-object; the wisdom of bodhi accords and merges with the dharma-nature." "Accords" is like box and lid according; "merges" is like water and milk merging. The Dharma-body is neither body nor not body; since wisdom accords and merges [with it], it too is neither body nor not body; this wisdom is forcibly named the reward-body. The life of the Dharma is neither permanent nor impermanent; since wisdom accords and merges, it too is neither permanent nor impermanent; permanence is forcibly named life. The Dharma is neither measure nor immeasurable; since wisdom accords and merges, it too is neither measure nor immeasurable; the immeasurable is forcibly named measure.

Chinese Source

應身者，應同物身為身也，應同連持為壽也，應同長短為量也。智與體冥能起大用，如水銀和真金能塗諸色像。功德和法身處處應現往，能為身為非身、能為常壽為無常壽、能為無量能為有量。有量有二義：一為無量之量、二為有量之量。如七百阿僧祇及八十等，是有量之量。如山斤海滴，實有齊限，凡夫所不知；阿彌陀實有期限，人天莫數，此是有量之無量。應佛皆為兩量，逐物隨緣，參差長短。然此三身、三壽、三量不可並別一異，則乖法體；即一而三、即三而一乃會玄文。故下文云「如深法性，即於此典金光明中而得見我釋迦牟尼」，即其義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The response-body: responding by sharing beings' bodies is its body; responding by sharing continuity is its life; responding by sharing long and short is its measure. Wisdom merges with the essence and can give rise to great function, like mercury mixed with true gold, which can coat all colored images. Merit mixed with the Dharma-body responds and appears everywhere: it can be body or not body, can be permanent life or impermanent life, can be immeasurable or can have measure. Having measure has two senses: first, the measure of the immeasurable; second, the measure of having measure. Seven hundred asaṃkhyeyas, eighty, and so on are the measure of having measure. The pounds of the mountains and drops of the seas truly have a limit, but ordinary people do not know it; Amitābha truly has a limited span, but humans and gods cannot count it: this is the immeasurable of having measure. Response-buddhas all have both measures, following beings and according with conditions, uneven, long or short. Yet these three bodies, three lives, and three measures cannot [be taken as] side by side or separate, one or different, for that goes against the Dharma-essence; only "identical to one yet three, identical to three yet one" accords with the profound text. Hence the text below says, "in the deep dharma-nature … then within this scripture, the Golden Light, one gets to see me, Śākyamuni"; this is just its meaning.

Chinese Source

但信相偏疑應身之有量，四佛偏舉應佛之無量斷其有量，迹疑既除，深達報法。若從信相所疑，應言壽有量；若從四佛釋疑，應言壽無量。而今不道壽有量、不道壽無量，直言壽量者，意欲圓論三佛之壽量，故不偏題取意為釋。若從義便，正是報身之壽量。何故取此？報身上冥下應，上冥法性即非量非無量，下應機緣能量能無量。量疑牽無量之答，即達於圓。經家從其生圓解而題品，舉一而蔽諸。從此義故，題為壽量品。又一時重解，〈壽量品〉亦作三重：一玄義、二引證、三還源。

Ru-Yi Translation

But Faith-Mark doubts only one side, the response-body's having measure, and the four buddhas raise only one side, the response-buddha's immeasurability, to cut off its having measure; once the doubt about the trace is removed, he deeply penetrates the reward-dharma. Following what Faith-Mark doubted, one should say "life has measure"; following the four buddhas' resolving of the doubt, one should say "life is immeasurable." Yet now it says neither "life has measure" nor "life is immeasurable," but directly "life and measure" (壽量): the intent is to discuss perfectly the life and measure of the three buddhas, so it is not titled one-sidedly; it takes the intent as explanation. If we follow what suits the meaning, it is precisely the life and measure of the reward-body. Why take this? The reward-body merges above and responds below: merging above with the dharma-nature, it is neither measure nor immeasurable; responding below to capacities and conditions, it can be measured and can be immeasurable. The doubt about measure draws out the answer of immeasurability, and so reaches the perfect. The compilers titled the chapter from the perfect understanding it produced, raising one to cover all. Because of this meaning it is titled the "Lifespan" chapter. At another time it was explained again: the "Lifespan" chapter is also treated in three layers: first, the profound meaning; second, citing proof; third, returning to the source.

Three Layers: Profound Meaning, Proof, and Return to the Source

Chinese Source

玄義者，壽者命也，謂報得命根連持不斷名之為壽，延促期數名之為量，故言壽量也。此釋應佛因緣之壽量也。又壽者受也，境智和合共相盛受。謂無分別智盛受無分別境，無分別境盛受無分別智，如函大蓋大，故壽是受義也。量者相應也，境智相應故言量。此釋報佛之壽量也。又壽者久也，常不變易稱之為久。量者銓量也，常久之壽非多數、非少數，非相應盡知、非相應不盡知，非可說、非不可說，無以名之，彊以銓量說其長久。此釋法身之壽量也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The profound meaning: 壽 is life — the life-faculty obtained as retribution, continuing without break, is called life; extended or contracted periods are called measure; hence "life and measure." This explains the life and measure of the response-buddha's causes and conditions. Again, 壽 means "receiving" (受): object and wisdom join and together hold each other. That is, nondiscriminating wisdom holds the nondiscriminating object, and the nondiscriminating object holds nondiscriminating wisdom, like a large box with a large lid; hence life has the sense of receiving. 量 means "according": object and wisdom accord, hence "measure." This explains the life and measure of the reward-buddha. Again, 壽 means "long-lasting": the permanent and unchanging is called long-lasting. 量 means "weighing": a permanent, long-lasting life is neither a great number nor a small number, neither fully known by according nor not fully known by according, neither speakable nor unspeakable; with nothing to name it, it is forcibly weighed and described as long-lasting. This explains the life and measure of the Dharma-body.

Chinese Source

初番為二：一有量量。如釋迦之壽，方八十是也。二量無量。如彌陀之壽，實是有量，人天所不能測，故言量無量也。此量無量皆應佛所為也。第二番亦二義。佛以如如智稱如如境，境無分別，智亦無分別；若境稱於智，智有分別，境亦有分別。此知與不知，皆報佛所明也。第三番亦二義：一者深寂不可說、二以慈悲方便亦可得說。此可說不可說，皆法佛之法也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The first round has two: first, measured measure, like Śākyamuni's life of only eighty; second, the measure of the immeasurable, like Amitābha's life, which truly has measure but which humans and gods cannot fathom; hence "the measure of the immeasurable." These measures and immeasurables are all done by the response-buddha. The second round also has two senses: the Buddha with suchness-wisdom matches the suchness-object; the object being without discrimination, wisdom too is without discrimination; if the object matches wisdom, wisdom has discrimination and the object too has discrimination. This knowing and not knowing are both clarified by the reward-buddha. The third round also has two senses: first, deep and tranquil, it cannot be spoken; second, by kindness, compassion, and skillful means, it can also be spoken. This speakable and unspeakable are both the dharma of the Dharma-buddha.

Chinese Source

二、引證者，方八十年證有量也。諸天世人八部之眾無能思算，山斤海滴不可數知，此證量無量也。虛空分界不可齊限，證境無分別也。又唯除如來證智可分別也。又下文去，智淵無邊法水具足，亦是證智也。壽不可計，此證不可說也。將欲宣暢釋迦如來所得壽命，此證慈悲可說也。又聞是四佛宣說如來壽命無量，證大悲可說也。新本明婆羅門求佛舍利如粟大，求六天報，即證有量也。王子說金光明難思難解福報無邊，福報無邊是證量無量，難思難解即總證智境不可說等（云云）。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, citing proof: "only eighty years" proves having measure. That the gods, the people of the world, and the assemblies of the eight classes cannot reckon it, and that the pounds of the mountains and drops of the seas cannot be counted, proves the measure of the immeasurable. "The divisions of empty space," which cannot be limited, prove the object without discrimination. Further, "except the Tathāgata" proves that wisdom can discriminate. Further, the text below — "his deep pool of wisdom is boundless, complete with the water of Dharma" — also proves wisdom. "His lifespan is incalculable" proves the unspeakable. "About to proclaim the lifespan attained by Śākyamuni Tathāgata" proves that compassion can speak. Further, "hearing these four buddhas proclaim that the Tathāgata's lifespan is immeasurable" proves that great compassion can speak. The new version makes clear that a brahmin seeks a relic of the Buddha the size of a millet grain, seeking the reward of the six heavens: this proves having measure. The prince teaches that the blessings and rewards of the Golden Light are hard to conceive and hard to understand and are boundless: blessings and rewards boundless proves the measure of the immeasurable; hard to conceive and hard to understand proves in general that wisdom and object are unspeakable, and so on (and so on).

Chinese Source

三、還源者，亦云復宗。釋此壽量雖作三身六義，勿作異解，秖是經題金光明義爾。初番量無量者，秖是明義。以其明故，大小長短、延促數量悉現明中，還是明義爾。第二番智境函蓋體解相應，色大故般若亦大，以法常故諸佛亦常，還是光義也。第三番可說不可說，常樂我淨說滿法界，法性無所益，都無一言；法性無所損，還是金義。夫解一則千從，迷一則萬惑，即此義也。既是兩時之聞，兼而錄之（云云）。此品正說，而序文未盡，分別如上。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, returning to the source, also called returning to the cardinal [doctrine]. Although this life and measure is explained by three bodies and six senses, do not form different understandings: it is only the meaning of the sūtra's title, Golden Light. In the first round, measure and the immeasurable are just the meaning of brightness: because of its brightness, great and small, long and short, extended and contracted, number and measure all appear within the brightness; it is still the meaning of brightness. In the second round, wisdom and object, box and lid, essence and understanding accord: "because form is great, prajñā too is great"; "because the Dharma is permanent, the buddhas too are permanent"; it is still the meaning of light. In the third round, speakable and unspeakable: permanence, bliss, self, and purity are taught filling the dharma-realm, yet the dharma-nature gains nothing — there is not a single word at all — and the dharma-nature loses nothing; it is still the meaning of gold. Understanding one, a thousand follow; deluded about one, ten thousand are confused; this is just that meaning. Since these were heard at two times, both are recorded (and so on). This chapter is the main teaching, but the introductory text is not yet finished; it is divided as above.

The Introduction of Harboring Doubt

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

From "the city of Rājagṛha" on is the third, the introduction of harboring doubt. The text has two: first, setting forth the person; second, clarifying the doubt. Setting forth the person has four: first, setting forth the place; second, clarifying the stage; third, setting forth the name; fourth, praising the virtue. The place is as in the text. "Bodhisattva": bodhi means path, sattva means mind; practicing this path oneself and also able to transform others, hence "mahāsattva"; a superior stage is established on superior practice. "Faith-Mark": the mark of the house of faith lies in the resembling path; the distinct [teaching] classifies it as the thirty minds; the perfect classifies it as the iron-wheel stage. The text below says, "He saw a person resembling a brahmin who struck the drum with a drumstick." The drum is the Dharma-body, and striking is the stirring of capacities; the capacities arising at the resembling stage, we know it is not true striking.

Like Samantabhadra, whose practice of Samantabhadra's practice is full and whose stage borders on the ultimately honored: this resembling is high. Faith-Mark is called [one of] the resembling path, but we dare not judge whether high or low. An objector says: If [his resembling] is the same as Samantabhadra's, why does he have doubt? The beginning mind and the later mind always contemplate nirvāṇa and practice the path; hence it is the dwelling place.

The Two Causes and Conditions of Long Life

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

From "He further thought" on is the actual arising of the doubt. "As the Buddha has taught": this is holding to the teaching and doubting the principle. The teaching expresses the two goods of restraint and action, which bring about long life. The Buddha formerly practiced many causes, yet now the fruit, his life, is extremely short; principle and teaching contradict each other, and he cannot but be confused. If there were no such principle, the teaching would be set up in vain; if it is necessarily so, where is the long life? This is holding to principle and being confused about the teaching.

Ru-Yi Translation

"There are two causes and conditions" differs from the former. In the ten wholesome [deeds], each wholesome [deed] has both restraint and action: not killing is the good of restraint, releasing life the good of action; not stealing is the good of restraint, giving food the good of action. Now the sūtra raises the good of restraint belonging to not killing and the good of action belonging to not stealing, each raising one side to clarify restraint and action together. If fully discussed, each has restraint and action; restraint and action must be made clear. Now, within each there is a cause and a condition. Life is what all beings cherish; to take it and harm it is surely great suffering; to spare and release it is happiness. The kind mind is the cause, not keeping implements of killing is the condition: this is the cause and condition of the good of restraint. Food is the dependent reward: obtaining it, life survives; losing it, life perishes. The mind of giving is the cause, the things given are the condition: this is the cause and condition of the good of action. As with not killing, so on up to no wrong views: in all there are twenty goods of restraint and action and forty causes and conditions, and these causes and conditions all bring about long life.

止善／行善. "The good of restraint / the good of action" follows the rendering already used elsewhere in this round.

Quotations. "There is a river whose whirlpools drown beings" and "practicing dāna-pāramitā, one obtains long life …" are from the Nirvāṇa Sūtra (T0374/T0375). "Attained through long cultivation of [good] deeds" is Lotus ch. 16 verse; "whatever Dharma he teaches reaches the ground …" is Lotus ch. 5 (the site's Lotus page renders 一切智地 "the ground of the wisdom of all modes"; here the glossary's "the wisdom that knows all things" is kept for 一切智); "the mark of eternal quiescent extinction, finally returning to emptiness" is also Lotus ch. 5; "by means of the other deep dharmas …" is Lotus ch. 22; "As for one person, so for the many" is Lotus ch. 3 verse. "Whoever wishes to measure the immeasurable Dharma …" is in the Great Treatise (T1509). "Upholding the precepts is the skin …" is attributed to the Great Treatise; source not located in the extant text. The Śūraṅgama line and the Nirvāṇa line on "suchness-wisdom" were not located; source not located.

The ten grounds. 歡喜 Joy, 離垢 Free of Defilement, 明 Illumination, 炎 Blazing, 難勝 Hard to Conquer, 現前 Manifesting, 遠行 Going Far, 不動 Immovable, 善慧 Good Wisdom, 法雲 Dharma Cloud; each is paired with one of the ten pāramitās.

Faith-Mark's stage. 似道／似位 "the resembling path / stage" (identity in resemblance); 鐵輪位 "the iron-wheel stage" = the ten faiths, and 銅輪位 "the copper-wheel stage" = the ten abidings, as in the site's Fahua Xuanyi. "The thirty minds" are the ten abidings, practices, and dedications of the distinct teaching.

Four foundations of mindfulness. "Withered" and "flourishing" foundations (枯／榮) refer to the image of the paired śāla trees: the withered contemplate impurity, suffering, impermanence, and no-self against ordinary people's inversions; the flourishing contemplate purity, bliss, permanence, and self against the two vehicles' inversions. "The foundation of mindfulness of perception and formations" is Zhiyi's term for the fourth (dharmas).

"Bodhi made a name for him." 菩提為我作名 is obscure; it is translated literally. Alternatively: "[He said,] 'Bodhi gives me my name'."

Guan Gongming is Guan Lu, the third-century diviner; "splitting bamboo" and "boring turtle shells" are divination methods.

冥道. "The unseen paths" refers to the realms of spirits and gods that are not seen by humans.
