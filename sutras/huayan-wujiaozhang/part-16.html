The Body That Practices — Karmic Birth and Transformation-Birth

Differences in What Is Taught, VIII — The Body Practice Depends On所詮差別・修行所依身

The fifth and final sub-topic of this block asks the most physical question yet: what kind of body does a bodhisattva actually have while practicing? An ordinary body still driven by karma into one birth after another, or a subtler "transformation-body," no longer bound by karma at all? Fazang works through the Five Teachings' differing answers, then pauses over ten separate scriptural passages that correlate an arhat's attainment with a bodhisattva's grounds — a catalog worth reading slowly, since almost every earlier sub-topic in this gate has left some trace in it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1866, vol. 45, 華嚴一乘教義分齊章 Fazang 法藏, 643–712, Tang Reading notes: Ru-Yi, original

1. Karmic Body Throughout, or Only Up to a Point

The Hīnayāna keeps a single, real, karma-bound body all the way to buddhahood itself. The Initial Teaching gives two separate accounts for its two kinds of practitioner, and already begins to draw the line between karmic birth and transformation at a specific ground.

Chinese Source · 修行所依身

◎第五修行所依身者。若依小乘。但有分段身至究竟位佛亦同然。是實非化。若始教中。為迴心聲聞。亦說分段至究竟位。佛身亦爾。然此是化非實也。若依直進中有二說。一謂寄位。顯十地之中功用無功用麁細二位差別相故。即說七地已還有分段。八地已上有變易。二就實報。即說分段至金剛已還。以十地中煩惱障種未永斷。故留至金剛故。既有惑障何得不受分段之身。故十地經云。第十地已還有中陰者。是此義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, on the body practice depends on: according to the Hīnayāna, there is only the karmic body of birth and death, all the way to the ultimate stage — the Buddha himself is no different — and this is a real body, not a transformation. In the Initial Teaching, for the śrāvakas turning their minds toward the Mahāyāna, the karmic body of birth and death is likewise said to continue all the way to the ultimate stage, the buddha-body included — but here it is a transformation, not a real body. As for the direct path, there are two accounts. The first is by way of provisional position: in order to show the difference between the exertion-requiring and the effortless, the coarse and the subtle positions among the Ten Grounds, it is said that the karmic body of birth and death continues through the seventh ground, while the transformation-body appears from the eighth ground onward. The second is in terms of real retribution: it holds that the karmic body of birth and death continues all the way up to the diamond-like stage, since the seeds of the afflictive obstruction within the Ten Grounds are not yet permanently severed, and so are retained right up to the diamond-like stage. Given that this obstruction of affliction is still present, how could one not receive the karmic body of birth and death? Hence the Daśabhūmika Sūtra says: "up through the tenth ground, there is still an intermediate-state existence" — this is exactly what is meant.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

分段身, the karmic body of birth and death, and 變易身, the transformation-body, name two very different ways of taking a body across a lifetime. The karmic body is what any ordinary being has: a fixed lifespan and form, produced by past karma and driven onward by present affliction, ending in death and rebirth in the usual sense. The transformation-body belongs only to beings advanced enough to have subdued that affliction — a body taken on freely, by vow rather than by karmic compulsion, without the ordinary birth-and-death cycle's coercive quality, though it can still change form and duration according to purpose. Fazang's question throughout this part is simply where, across the Ten Grounds and across each teaching, the line between the two actually falls — and the answer turns out to depend heavily on whether a given teaching is describing "real retribution" (a fact about what a bodhisattva's body actually is) or "provisional position" (a fact about how the stages are being taught to a given audience) — the same distinction that has organized nearly every sub-topic in this gate.

2. Why Would an Advanced Bodhisattva Still Take Birth at All?

If a bodhisattva from the eighth ground onward has permanently subdued the afflictive obstruction — as thoroughly, it would seem, as an arhat — how can he still be taking the karmic body of birth and death rather than the transformation-body alone? Fazang's answer turns on the difference between an affliction still active and its seed merely retained.

Chinese Source

問八地已上一切菩薩。於煩惱障永伏不行。以無漏智果恒相續故。如阿羅漢。既無現行惑。何得更受分段之身耶。答若是凡夫。即以現惑潤業受生。聖人不爾。但留惑種用以受生。故雜集論云。一切聖人皆以隨眠力故結生相續。又梁攝論云。異凡夫故永伏上心。異二乘故留彼種子。解云。聖人受生非現潤。彼復留種子。如何不受分段身耶。若言八地已上以智障為緣受變易者。所留惑種即便無用。何不於此第八地初永害一切煩惱種耶。彼既不爾此云何然。若約迴向菩提聲聞已斷煩惱者。彼即可以所知障受變易身。通諸位也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: all bodhisattvas from the eighth ground upward have permanently subdued the afflictive obstruction, so that it no longer operates at all, since the fruit of outflow-free wisdom continues without interruption — just like an arhat, who has no active affliction whatsoever. How, then, could such a bodhisattva still receive the karmic body of birth and death? Answer: an ordinary person takes rebirth by way of active affliction moistening his karma; a sage does not. He takes rebirth only by retaining the seeds of affliction for that purpose. Hence the Abhidharmasamuccaya says: all sages take continuous rebirth solely by the power of the latent tendencies. And the Mahāyānasaṃgraha says: he differs from the ordinary person in that his higher mind is permanently subdued, and differs from the two vehicles in that he retains those seeds. Explanation: a sage's rebirth is not moistened by active affliction, and yet he still retains the seeds — how, then, could he fail to receive the karmic body of birth and death? If it is said that, from the eighth ground onward, the transformation-body is received solely on the condition of the cognitive obstruction, then the retained seeds of affliction would be entirely without use — why, then, would this not simply eliminate all the seeds of affliction outright at the very start of the eighth ground? Since this is evidently not the case, how could that position hold? If, however, one is speaking of a śrāvaka who has turned toward bodhi and already cut off the afflictive obstruction, then such a person may indeed receive the transformation-body on the condition of the cognitive obstruction alone, and this would apply uniformly across all the positions.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The distinction doing the work here is between an affliction actually operating — "moistening" karma the way water lets a seed germinate — and an affliction's seed merely being retained, inert, for a purpose. An arhat has genuinely uprooted the seed itself; an advanced bodhisattva has only suppressed its activity while deliberately keeping the seed intact, precisely so that he can still take birth among ordinary beings rather than escaping into the transformation-body's freedom prematurely. The argument that follows — if the retained seed were truly useless, why not eliminate it outright at the eighth ground? — is a small but effective proof by elimination: the fact that Buddhist texts do not describe total eradication at the eighth ground is itself evidence that the seed is still doing real work there.

3. Ten Scriptural Ways of Matching an Arhat to a Ground

Fazang now explains why scripture sometimes seems to say the eighth ground depends on the cognitive obstruction alone: it is a provisional comparison with the two vehicles, meant to display relative rank rather than state a bare fact. He then lays out ten separate scriptural passages that correlate arhat-attainment with bodhisattva grounds — nearly a summary, in miniature, of every teaching covered across this whole eight-part block.

Chinese Source

問若爾何故聖教說八地已上唯有所知障為依止故受變易身。答此等為欲寄對二乘顯其優劣故。經作此說。然此寄對依諸聖教。約位不同略辨十門。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: if that is so, then why do the sacred teachings say that, from the eighth ground onward, the transformation-body is received on the sole basis of the cognitive obstruction? Answer: this is stated only in order to display, by way of a provisional comparison with the two vehicles, their relative superiority and inferiority. This provisional comparison, drawn from the various sacred teachings, may be briefly analyzed, according to the differing positions involved, into ten gates.

一說羅漢即同於佛。更不分位。如律中說。佛度五人已。即云通佛有六然羅漢出於世間。又同坐等。當知此約小乘教說。二亦於佛地分出羅漢。如對法論說。得菩提時頓斷煩惱障及所知障。頓成羅漢及如來故。此中雖約煩惱盡邊名阿羅漢。而亦為生諸聲聞中心勝欲樂故。寄於佛果以分大小也。三第十地名阿羅漢佛地超過故。非彼也。如涅槃經中說四依。云阿羅漢者。住第十地。此寄因異果以分大小也。此上二門。約始教中迴二乘教說。四七地已還寄同羅漢。八地。已去寄菩薩位。如仁王經云。遠行菩薩伏三界習因果業滅。唯後身位中。住第七地阿羅漢位。此寄因中自在未自在位。以分大小也。此約始教直進中說。此中既寄聲聞至七地故。煩惱障及分段身俱至此位。八地已去寄此菩薩。行位勝前。是故唯有彼所知障及變易身。

First: it is said that the arhat is simply identical to the Buddha, with no further division of position — as the Vinaya says, once the Buddha had brought the first five disciples to liberation, it was said that, the Buddha included, there were then six arhats in the world; and the arhat sits together with the Buddha, and so on. One should know that this is spoken according to the Hīnayāna teaching. Second: the arhat is also distinguished separately within the buddha-ground itself — as the Abhidharmasamuccaya says, upon attaining bodhi, one suddenly cuts off both the afflictive and cognitive obstructions at once, suddenly accomplishing arhatship and tathāgatahood together. Here, although the term "arhat" names only the exhaustion of affliction, it is spoken of at the very point of buddha-fruition, dividing great from small, precisely in order to give rise, among the śrāvakas, to a superior aspiration. Third: the tenth ground is named arhatship, since the buddha-ground surpasses it and is not the same — as the Nirvāṇa Sūtra, in speaking of the four kinds of reliance, says that the arhat abides on the tenth ground. This distinguishes great from small by way of cause differing from fruit. These first two gates are spoken according to the mind-turning teaching within the Initial Teaching. Fourth: through the seventh ground, one is provisionally likened to an arhat; from the eighth ground onward, one is provisionally likened to the bodhisattva position — as the Renwang Sūtra says, the far-going bodhisattva subdues the causes and effects habituated within the three realms and extinguishes their karma, and only in his final body abides in the arhat-position of the seventh ground. This distinguishes great from small by way of a position of self-mastery not yet attained within the cause. This is spoken according to the direct path within the Initial Teaching. Since, here, the śrāvaka is provisionally likened up to the seventh ground, both the afflictive obstruction and the karmic body of birth and death continue together up to this position; from the eighth ground onward, this is provisionally likened to the bodhisattva, whose position and practice surpass what came before — and so only the cognitive obstruction and the transformation-body remain.

五初二三地寄同世間。以得世間四禪等故。四地寄是須陀洹等。以得道品故。此初出世故。五地寄此聲聞羅漢。以得四諦法故。六地寄此辟支佛。以得十二緣生法故。七地已去寄此菩薩。以得無生法忍故。此如本業經說。上來唯約三乘教說。以未分出一乘法故。六世間二乘同前至六地。第七地寄三乘菩薩以未自在故。八地已去寄一乘法。以得自在故。如梁攝論等說。此約一乘三乘等分相而說。七於初地之中。已過世間及二乘故。如地論等說。此約三乘中比證分齊說。八地前三賢位已過二乘地。如起信論說。又仁王經說。習種性中有十種心。已超過二乘一切善地。此約終教不退說。九信滿心後。即過一切小乘三乘等。如賢首品說。此約一乘說。十初在凡夫地。創發心時即過二乘。如智論說。羅漢比丘知沙彌發心推在前等。此通一乘及三乘說。由有如是寄對法門故。諸說前後準此而知。

Fifth: the first, second, and third grounds are provisionally likened to the mundane world, since the four mundane dhyānas and the like are attained there; the fourth ground is provisionally likened to the stream-enterer and the like, since the limbs of the path are attained there, this being the very first transcendence of the world; the fifth ground is provisionally likened to the śrāvaka-arhat, since the truth of the four noble truths is attained there; the sixth ground is provisionally likened to the pratyekabuddha, since the dharma of the twelve links of dependent origination is attained there; from the seventh ground onward, it is provisionally likened to the bodhisattva, since the forbearance of the non-arising of dharmas is attained there — this is as the Sūtra of the Original Acts explains. All of the above is spoken solely in terms of the three-vehicle teaching, since the One Vehicle's own dharma has not yet been distinguished out from it. Sixth: the mundane and the two vehicles are, as before, matched up through the sixth ground; the seventh ground is provisionally likened to the three-vehicle bodhisattva, since self-mastery is not yet attained there; from the eighth ground onward, it is provisionally likened to the One Vehicle's own dharma, since self-mastery is then attained — as the Mahāyānasaṃgraha and similar texts explain. This is spoken in terms of the shared marks of the One Vehicle and the three vehicles alike. Seventh: already within the first ground, one has surpassed the mundane world and the two vehicles — as the Daśabhūmika-śāstra and similar texts explain. This is spoken in terms of the boundary of comparative realization within the three vehicles. Eighth: the three worthy positions before the grounds have already surpassed the stage of the two vehicles — as the Awakening of Faith explains; and the Renwang Sūtra says that, within the lineage-by-habituation, there are ten kinds of mind that have already transcended every wholesome stage of the two vehicles. This is spoken in terms of the Final Teaching's own account of non-retrogression. Ninth: after the fulfillment of the mind of faith, one has already surpassed the Hīnayāna, the three vehicles, and all the rest — as the "Chapter of the Worthy Leader" explains. This is spoken in terms of the One Vehicle. Tenth: from the very stage of the ordinary person, at the moment one first generates the mind, one has already surpassed the two vehicles — as the Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra explains: an arhat-monk, recognizing that a novice has generated the mind, defers to him and places him first, and so on. This applies commonly to both the One Vehicle and the three vehicles. Because there exist provisional correspondences of this kind, the various statements, earlier and later, may all be understood on this model.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Ten gates is a great deal to hold in view at once, so it is worth having the shape of the list rather than only its details. Each gate answers the same underlying question — where does a bodhisattva's attainment finally outrank an arhat's? — and the ten answers slide steadily earlier as the teaching gets more advanced:

GateWhere the bodhisattva surpasses the arhatTeaching
1Nowhere — the arhat simply is a buddhaHīnayāna
2Only at buddhahood itself, where both titles arise togetherInitial (mind-turning)
3The tenth ground, arhatship's outer limitInitial (mind-turning)
4The eighth groundInitial (direct path)
5The seventh ground (the two vehicles matched to grounds four through six)Three vehicles generally
6The eighth ground, this time marking the One Vehicle's own self-masteryOne Vehicle / three vehicles compared

8Already at the three worthy stages, before any groundFinal Teaching
9At the fulfillment of faith, before the abodesOne Vehicle
10The very first thought of bodhicitta, in the ordinary personOne Vehicle and three vehicles alike

Read as a sequence, the ten gates trace exactly the same trajectory this entire eight-part block has followed for consciousness, lineage, stages, and duration alike: the point at which real progress begins keeps being pulled earlier and earlier, from "nowhere before buddhahood" to "the very first thought of faith," as the analysis moves from Hīnayāna through the Initial and Final Teachings to the One Vehicle's own account.

4. The Final Teaching's Own Account, and a Popular Theory Rejected

Having finished with provisional comparisons, Fazang states the Final Teaching's own considered view — a single, permanent severing of affliction at the first ground — and then takes apart a theory, evidently already in circulation, that would divide advanced bodhisattvas into a compassion-heavy type and a wisdom-heavy type.

A note on the text: at this point the source manuscript carries a repeated fascicle header — "Huayan Wujiaozhang, Fascicle Two" immediately followed by "Fascicle Three" — marking the treatise's own internal division between its second and third fascicles. The heading is not part of the running argument and has been passed over here as a stray artifact of the text's transmission, exactly where it falls, in the middle of this one sub-topic.

Chinese Source · 修行所依身・終教

◎若依終教。地前留惑受分段身。於初地中永斷一切煩惱使種。亦不分彼分別俱生。於所知障中。又斷一分麁品正使。是故地上受變易身至金剛位。餘義如下斷惑中說。問若於地上不留煩惱。云何大悲同事攝生。答若於地前及始教中。願智力劣故。留煩惱助願受生。今此不爾。願智勝故自在同生。

Ru-Yi Translation

According to the Final Teaching: before the grounds, the bodhisattva retains affliction and receives the karmic body of birth and death; within the first ground, he permanently severs the seeds of all afflictions at once, without distinguishing the discriminated kind from the innate kind. Within the cognitive obstruction, he also severs one portion of its coarse, active manifestations. Hence, from the grounds onward, he receives the transformation-body, all the way to the diamond-like stage. The remaining meaning is explained below, under eliminating affliction. Question: if no affliction is retained from the grounds onward, how can great compassion still engage and gather beings through shared action? Answer: before the grounds, and within the Initial Teaching, the power of vow and wisdom is still weak, and so affliction is retained to assist the vow in receiving rebirth. Here it is not so: because the power of vow and wisdom is superior, rebirth is undertaken together with beings freely and at will.

問如說八地已還菩薩略有二類。一悲增上。二智增上。悲增者。留惑受分段身故。智增者。伏惑受變易身故。此義云何。答如此所說良恐未然。何者。若悲增上其慧必劣。劣慧導悲悲應滯見。滯見之悲豈名增上。縱悲智齊均。尚不名悲增上。況以劣慧導悲而言增上。若智增上其悲必劣。劣悲導智智應滯寂。滯寂之智何名增上。以諸菩薩從初已來異凡小故。悲智相導念念雙修。如車二輪。如鳥二翼。何得說彼有增減耶。當知由此始終二教麁細異故。有二生死。非由悲智互增上也。

Question: it is said that bodhisattvas through the eighth ground fall, in brief, into two types — one in whom compassion predominates, one in whom wisdom predominates. The compassion-predominant type retains affliction and receives the karmic body of birth and death; the wisdom-predominant type subdues affliction and receives the transformation-body. What is the meaning of this? Answer: this account, I fear, does not really hold up. Why is this? If compassion predominates, the accompanying wisdom must be inferior — and compassion guided by inferior wisdom would be mired in wrong views. How could compassion mired in views be called predominant? Even were compassion and wisdom perfectly equal, this still would not be called "compassion predominant" — how much less so when inferior wisdom guides compassion, and this is nonetheless called predominant! Likewise, if wisdom predominates, the accompanying compassion must be inferior — and wisdom guided by inferior compassion would be mired in quiescence. How could wisdom mired in quiescence be called predominant? Since bodhisattvas, from the very outset, differ from the ordinary person and the lesser vehicles, compassion and wisdom guide each other and are cultivated together in every single thought-moment, like the two wheels of a cart, like the two wings of a bird — how, then, could one speak of either as increasing relative to the other? One should know that it is because the Initial and Final Teachings differ in coarseness and subtlety that two kinds of birth-and-death are spoken of — not because compassion and wisdom increase relative to each other.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Fazang's rejection of the compassion-predominant / wisdom-predominant typology is one of the few moments in this entire gate where he sets aside the usual work of harmonizing scriptural voices and simply argues, on the merits, that a popular theory is confused. The refutation turns on a dilemma: predominance implies imbalance, but an imbalance in either direction is disqualifying rather than praiseworthy — compassion led by weak wisdom collapses into attachment to wrong views, and wisdom led by weak compassion collapses into a self-absorbed quiescence, neither of which anyone would actually want to call an advanced bodhisattva's defining excellence. The real explanation for why some bodhisattvas keep the karmic body and others take the transformation-body, he concludes, is not a difference of inner character at all, but simply the difference between the Initial and Final Teachings' own coarser and subtler accounts of the same path — one more instance of a difference this whole gate has traced back, again and again, to which teaching is speaking rather than to which kind of bodhisattva is being described.

5. The Sudden and Perfect Teachings: No Body to Speak Of, and No Transformation Needed

The block closes with the Sudden Teaching's now-familiar silence and the Perfect Teaching's most striking claim yet: it does not need the transformation-body at all, because the ordinary karmic body, pushed all the way to the threshold of the Tenth Ground, was never really the limited thing the other teachings took it to be.

Chinese Source

若言彼智增上者。有小乘習怖諸煩惱故。永伏者。若於地前未證真如。可約本習容有此類。以未純熟故。初地已上行解純熟。同證同行同修同斷。如何得有如是差別故起信論云。地上菩薩種性發心修行皆無差別也。又此教中。地上變易寄位不同有其四種等。亦如下指。若依頓教。一切行位既不可說。所依身分亦準此知。廣如大般若經那伽室利分說。若依圓教不說變易。但分段身至於十地離垢定前。以至彼位得普見肉眼故知是分段也。又如善財等。以分段身窮於因位故也。問何故此中不說變易。答如世界性等以上身分。甚極微細出過諸天應同變易。但以此教不分生死麁細之相。總就過患以為一際。至信滿後頓翻彼際故不說也。餘準而知之。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the claim that some are "wisdom-predominant," having permanently subdued their afflictions out of a Hīnayāna-habituated fear of them — if this occurs before the grounds, while suchness has not yet been realized, it may, in terms of prior habituation, allow for a type of this kind, since ripening is not yet complete. But from the first ground onward, practice and understanding are already ripened, and bodhisattvas equally realize, equally practice, equally cultivate, and equally cut off the very same things — how, then, could such a difference exist? Hence the Awakening of Faith says: bodhisattvas on the grounds are, in lineage, in the arising of the mind, and in practice, entirely without difference from one another. In this teaching, moreover, the transformation-body on the grounds is provisionally correlated with position in four distinct ways, as further indicated below. According to the Sudden Teaching, since all stages and positions whatsoever cannot be spoken of, the body one depends on likewise cannot be spoken of, and may be understood on this same model — as explained at length in the Nāgaśrī chapter of the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Sūtra. According to the Perfect Teaching, the transformation-body is not spoken of at all — there is only the karmic body of birth and death, all the way up to the stainless samādhi just before the Tenth Ground, since, upon reaching that position, one attains the flesh-eye of universal sight, and thereby knows this is still the karmic body. So too with Sudhana and others, who exhaust the causal stage entirely within the karmic body. Question: why does this teaching not speak of the transformation-body at all? Answer: bodily forms above the level of the World of Worlds and the like are already so extremely subtle as to surpass even the heavenly beings, and ought properly to be classed together with the transformation-body. But because this teaching does not distinguish coarse and subtle marks within birth-and-death at all, treating them together, as a single whole, under the single heading of affliction — and because this whole boundary is suddenly overturned all at once after the fulfillment of faith — it is therefore not spoken of separately here. The rest may be understood on this model.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The Perfect Teaching's answer closes this whole eight-part block on exactly the note it opened with in Part 9: an ordinary category, examined closely enough, turns out to have always been unlimited. There, an ordinary consciousness turned out to already be the dependent origination of the whole dharma-realm; here, the youth Universal Adornment's ordinary flesh-eye — already discussed in Part 14 — and Sudhana's own ordinary karmic body, carried all the way to the threshold of the Tenth Ground, turn out never to have needed replacing by anything subtler. Rather than distinguishing a coarse body from a subtle one the way every other teaching in this part does, the Perfect Teaching simply declines to draw that line inside birth-and-death at all, treating the whole of it as a single undifferentiated matter that is overturned entirely, all at once, the moment the fulfillment of faith is reached — the same "sudden overturning" this collection will return to when it takes up the doctrine of eliminating affliction in the parts ahead.

Part Sixteen Complete, and the Block Concluded

This closes Part Sixteen, and with it the eight-part block covering the first five sub-topics of Gate Nine — consciousness, lineage, stages, duration, and the body of practice. The Hīnayāna's single karmic body; the Initial Teaching's two accounts, one drawing the karmic-transformation line at the eighth ground by provisional position, one at the diamond-like stage by real retribution; the argument from retained-but-inert seeds explaining why advanced bodhisattvas still take birth; ten scriptural gates correlating an arhat's attainment with a bodhisattva's grounds, tracing the same steadily-earlier trajectory this whole block has followed throughout; the Final Teaching's single, permanent severing of affliction at the first ground, and its rejection of any compassion-versus-wisdom typology among advanced bodhisattvas; the Sudden Teaching's silence; and the Perfect Teaching's refusal to distinguish a coarse body from a subtle one at all, keeping only the karmic body, understood as never having been limited in the first place.

Part Seventeen turns to the sixth sub-topic of Gate Nine — eliminating affliction — opening with the Initial Teaching's detailed abhidharma anatomy of the afflictive and cognitive obstructions and the śrāvaka path's own stages of elimination.
