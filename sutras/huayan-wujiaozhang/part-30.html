Two Mysterious Gates — Mutual Containment, and a Single Thought of Awakening

The One and the Many, and the Bodhisattva Who Is Already a Buddha一多相容不同門・諸法相即自在門

With the ten coins now retired as a teaching device, Fazang names two more of the Ten Mysterious Gates directly in terms of principle. The second gate states that the one and the many contain one another without ever blurring into each other. The third goes further still — one is exactly all, and all is exactly one — and Fazang reaches for the Avataṃsaka's single most startling claim to illustrate it: that a bodhisattva's very first thought of resolving upon awakening is already, in that same instant, complete buddhahood.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1866, vol. 45, 華嚴一乘教義分齊章 Author: Fazang 法藏, 643–712, Tang Reading notes: Ru-Yi, original

Gate 2 — The One and the Many, Contained Without Losing Their Difference

The second gate restates the same-substance insight from Part 29 in general terms, then adds a crucial qualification the coins already implied but that deserves saying outright: containing one another is not the same as collapsing into one another.

Chinese Source

二者一多相容不同門。此上諸義。隨一門中即具攝前因果理事一切法門。如彼初錢中即攝無盡義者。此亦如是。然此一中雖具有多。仍一非即是其多耳。多中一等準上思之。餘一一門中。皆悉如是重重無盡故也。故此經偈云。以一佛土滿十方。十方入一亦無餘。世界本相亦不壞。無比功德故能爾。然此一多雖復互相含受自在無礙。仍體不同也。所由如上錢義中釋。此有同體異體。準上思之可解。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second is the gate of the one and the many mutually containing one another without losing their distinctness 一多相容不同門. Within any single one of the topics discussed above, all the dharma-gates of cause and fruit, principle and phenomenon discussed before are already gathered in and contained — just as, in the case of the first coin, the meaning of inexhaustibility was already contained within it. This is exactly the same. Yet although the one, in this way, contains the many within it, the one is never simply identical with that many; the many within the one should be considered the same way, by the pattern given above. Every other single topic, too, is entirely likewise, layer upon layer, inexhaustible.

Hence the Avataṃsaka's own verse says: "With a single buddha-land filling the ten directions, the ten directions enter into the one, leaving nothing left over; and yet the original character of each world is not thereby destroyed — it is because of virtue beyond compare that this can be so."

Yet although the one and the many, in this way, mutually contain and receive one another, autonomously and without obstruction, their substances still remain distinct — for the reason already explained above, in the discussion of the coins. This gate has both same-substance and different-substance aspects, and may be understood by the pattern given there.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The verse Fazang quotes is doing careful work: a single buddha-land can hold all ten directions within it, and all ten directions can hold that one land, "leaving nothing left over" — and yet, in the very same breath, "the original character of each world is not thereby destroyed." This is the qualification that keeps Huayan interpenetration from collapsing into a vague mysticism where everything is secretly the same thing. The one genuinely contains the many, and the many genuinely contain the one, without either ceasing to be exactly what it distinctly is. A jeweled hall reflecting every other jewel within a single facet — the kind of scene this site's Gaṇḍavyūha narrates concretely at Maitrāyaṇī's hall of mirrors — is this same principle rendered as a story rather than argued as a doctrine.

Gate 3 — All Dharmas Mutually Identical and Free

The third gate pushes past containment to outright identity: one is exactly all, and all is exactly one. Fazang immediately reaches for the Avataṃsaka's own boldest illustration of what this means for the bodhisattva path.

Chinese Source

三者諸法相即自在門。此上諸義一即一切。一切即一。圓融自在無礙成耳。若約同體門中。即自具足攝一切法也。然此自一切復自相入。重重無盡故也。然此無盡皆悉在初門中也。故此經云。初發心菩薩。一念之切德。深廣無邊際。如來分別說。窮劫不能盡。何況於無邊無數無量劫。具足修諸度諸地功德行。義言一念即深廣無邊者。良由緣起法界一即一切故爾。如彼同體門中一錢即得重重無盡義者。即其事也。何況無邊劫者。即餘一一門中。各現無盡義者是也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third is the gate of all dharmas being mutually identical and freely autonomous 諸法相即自在門. All the topics discussed above, one being exactly all, and all being exactly one, are perfectly fused, autonomous, and unobstructed. Considered in terms of the same-substance gate, any one topic is already, in itself, replete with and gathers in every dharma; and this "itself" and "every dharma" in turn mutually include one another, layer upon layer, inexhaustibly — yet all of this inexhaustibility, in every case, already lies within that very first topic.

Hence the sūtra says: "The excellent qualities within a single thought-moment of a bodhisattva who has newly resolved upon awakening are deep and vast, without bound or limit; were the Tathāgata to explain them one by one, he could not exhaust them though the kalpas ran out — how much more so the qualities of practice, fulfilling every pāramitā and every ground, cultivated throughout kalpas beyond number, beyond measure!" The meaning of "a single thought-moment is itself deep and vast without bound" is precisely because the dharma-realm of dependent origination is such that one is exactly all. It is exactly the same matter as when, within the same-substance gate, a single coin was found to hold layer-upon-layer inexhaustibility. How much more so, then, kalpas without bound — this is exactly what appears, as inexhaustible meaning, within each and every one of the other topics as well.

所以爾者。此經又云。初發心菩薩。即是佛故也。由是緣起妙理始終皆齊。得始即得終。窮終方原始。如上同時具足故得然也。又云。在於一地普攝一切諸地功德也。是故得一即得一切。又云。知一即多多即一故也。十信終心即作佛者即其事也。

The reason it is so is that the sūtra elsewhere also says: "A bodhisattva who has newly resolved upon awakening is exactly a buddha." It is because of this that the wondrous principle of dependent origination is, from beginning to end, entirely level: to attain the beginning is exactly to attain the end, and to reach the very limit of the end is precisely to find the origin — this holds true for the reason already given above, that all things are simultaneously and fully replete together. The sūtra also says: "Abiding within a single ground, a bodhisattva universally gathers in the excellent qualities of every ground." This is exactly why attaining the one is attaining the all. It also says: "knowing the one is exactly the many, and the many is exactly the one" — this is exactly the matter of one whose mind, at the very culmination of the Ten Faiths, is already made a buddha.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

"A bodhisattva who has newly resolved upon awakening is exactly a buddha" — 初發心菩薩即是佛 — is one of the Avataṃsaka's most quoted lines, and Fazang is using it exactly as intended: not as poetic exaggeration, but as the sharpest possible instance of Gate 3's "one is exactly all." An ordinary reading of the bodhisattva path treats the first resolve to seek awakening as the barest beginning of an almost endless road — countless lifetimes and immeasurable practice still lie ahead before buddhahood is reached. The Avataṃsaka does not deny any of that labor; it insists, in addition, that the beginning and the end of that road are, considered rightly, identical — "to attain the beginning is exactly to attain the end." This is the same relation the ten coins were built to demonstrate, now applied to the bodhisattva path itself: the first coin was never separate from the tenth to begin with.

How Does It Appear — All at Once, or Layer by Layer?

A pair of questions asks how, exactly, this total mutual gathering-in is to be pictured — and Fazang's answer previews two of the gates still to come.

Chinese Source

問如同體一門中即攝一切無盡者。為一時俱現耶。為前後耶。答於一門中。一時炳然現一切者。屬微細攝。隱映互現重重者。屬因陀羅攝。餘義即同即異。即多即少。即有即無。即始即終。如是自在具足一切無盡法門。仍隨舉為首。餘即為伴。道理一不差失舊來如此。亦辯同體一門中具足自在無窮德耳。餘異體等門中亦準思之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: When a single, same-substance gate gathers in the whole of the inexhaustible in this way, does everything appear all at once, or does it appear in sequence, before and after? Answer: Within a single gate, that everything appears at once, radiantly and all together, belongs to what is gathered under "the subtle"; that things appear hidden and revealed, reflecting one another layer upon layer, belongs to what is gathered under "Indra's Net." As for the remaining senses — being at once identical and different, many and few, existent and non-existent, beginning and end — in this way, autonomously replete with every inexhaustible dharma-gate, whichever one is raised up becomes the head, and all the rest become its retinue, the principle in every case remaining exactly as it has always, from of old, been. This clarifies that a single, same-substance gate is replete with autonomous and boundless virtue; the other gates, including different substance, should be considered by the same pattern.

問若一門中。即具足一切無盡自在者。餘門何用為。答餘門如虛空。何以故。同體一門并攝一切無不盡故。問此同體中所攝一切者。但應攝自門中一切。豈可攝餘門中一切耶。答既攝自一切。復攝餘一一門中無盡一切。如是重重窮其法界也。何以故。圓融法界無盡緣起。無一一切並不成故。此但論法性家實德故。不可說其邊量。

Question: If a single gate is already replete with the autonomous, inexhaustible whole, what use are the other gates? Answer: The other gates are, to it, like empty space. Why? Because the single, same-substance gate already gathers in the entire whole, leaving nothing unexhausted. Question: What this same-substance gate gathers in — should it gather in only the whole within its own gate, or can it also gather in the whole within every other gate? Answer: Having already gathered in its own whole, it further gathers in the inexhaustible whole within each and every other gate as well — layer upon layer in this way, exhausting the dharma-realm to its very limit. Why? Because within the perfectly fused dharma-realm of inexhaustible dependent origination, unless the one and the all are both established together, neither could be constituted at all. This is spoken of only in terms of the real virtue proper to the household of the nature of dharmas, and its bounds and measure cannot be stated.

故此經偈云。不可言說諸劫中。演說一切不可說。不可說劫猶可盡。說不可說不可盡。又偈云。一切眾生心。悉可分別知。一切剎微塵。尚可算其數。十方虛空界。一毛猶可量。菩薩初發心究竟不可惻。良由此一乘圓極自在無礙法門得一即得一切故耳。

Hence the sūtra's own verse says: "Throughout kalpas beyond all telling, one could proclaim everything that is beyond telling; the kalpas beyond telling could yet be exhausted, but proclaiming what is beyond telling could never be exhausted." Another verse says: "The minds of all sentient beings could, every one, be distinguished and known; the motes of dust in every land could still be counted and numbered; the realm of empty space in the ten directions could yet be measured, even down to a single hair — but a bodhisattva's initial resolve upon awakening can, in the end, never be fathomed." This is truly because this One Vehicle's perfectly consummate, autonomous, unobstructed dharma-gate is such that attaining the one is attaining the all.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

"The subtle" and "Indra's Net" named here as two ways this all-at-once gathering can appear are, in fact, the names of Gates 4 and 5 — Fazang is previewing, in passing, the very gates Part 31 will take up in full. Hold onto the distinction he draws here in its bare form: the subtle is when everything that could be said is present at once, laid out side by side, fully visible in a single instant; Indra's Net is when things are present through endless reflection, each thing showing itself only by showing every other thing showing it, hidden and revealed in the same movement. Both, he insists, are equally real descriptions of the same total interpenetration — they simply answer the question "how is it there?" in two different, equally valid ways, the way a single fact can be reported either all at once or traced out link by link.

Cause and Fruit, Bound Together by the Six Characteristics

Fazang now draws in the Daśabhūmika Treatise's own vocabulary — the Total and the Particular characteristic — to explain how cause and fruit, ordinarily thought of as sequential, are here simultaneous.

Chinese Source

因果俱齊無前後別。故地論云。以信地菩薩乃至與不可思議佛法為一緣起。以六相總別等義而用括之。明知。因果俱時相容相即。各攝一切互為主伴。深須思之此事不疑。

Ru-Yi Translation

Cause and fruit are, in this way, entirely level, with no distinction of before and after. Hence the Daśabhūmika Treatise says: "From the bodhisattva at the stage of faith up to the inconceivable qualities of buddhahood, all constitute a single dependent origination — bound together by means of the Six Characteristics, the Total and the Particular and the rest." This makes it clear that cause and fruit, being simultaneous, mutually contain and are mutually identical with one another, each gathering in the whole and each in turn serving as host or retinue to the other. This matter should be pondered deeply, without any doubt.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This is worth flagging even in passing: the Six Characteristics — the Total, the Particular, and the four others Fazang will name and fully unfold only in Parts 33 and 34 — are already doing quiet work here, cited from the Daśabhūmika Treatise as the very glue holding cause and fruit together across the entire bodhisattva path. The Ten Mysterious Gates and the Six Characteristics are not two separate doctrines bolted together at the end of the treatise; Fazang treats the second as one working-out, in a different key, of exactly the interpenetration the first has been describing all along. Readers who continue on to Part 33 will meet this same citation again, expanded into its own full analysis.

Why the "First Thought" Doctrine Is Not Just Praise

Fazang anticipates the obvious objection — surely all this is just poetic praise of a beginner's resolve, not a literal claim about the fruit — and answers it by insisting on the same identity of cause and fruit just established.

Chinese Source

又此經云。何以故此初發心菩薩即是佛故。悉與三世諸如來等。亦與三世佛境界等。悉與三世佛正法等。得如來一身無量身三世諸佛平等智慧。所化眾生皆悉平等。又云。初發心時便成正覺。具足慧身不由他悟。如是云云無量。廣如經文。

Ru-Yi Translation

The sūtra also says: "Why? Because this bodhisattva who has newly resolved upon awakening is exactly a buddha — equal in every way to the tathāgatas of the three times, equal to the buddhas' own realm across the three times, equal to the true Dharma of the buddhas across the three times; attaining the Tathāgata's one body as boundless bodies, and the equal wisdom of the buddhas of the three times, so that the beings transformed by that wisdom are all, likewise, equal." It also says: "At the very moment of first resolving upon awakening, one straightaway accomplishes true awakening, replete with the body of wisdom, awakened not through any other." Sayings of this kind are without measure; the sūtra's own text may be consulted at length.

問此等歎因中德耳。豈可即滿德果耶。答此一乘義。因果同體成一緣起。得此即得彼。由彼此相即故。若不得果者因即不成因。何以故。不得果等非因也。

Question: Passages such as these only praise the virtue present within the cause — how could they possibly mean that the fruit's virtue is thereby already complete? Answer: In the meaning of this One Vehicle, cause and fruit are of a single substance, constituting a single dependent origination; to attain the one is to attain the other, because the two are mutually identical. If the fruit were not attained, the cause could not even be established as a cause at all — why? Because whatever fails to lead to the attainment of the fruit is not, properly speaking, a cause in the first place.

Three Lifetimes to the Fruit — Sudhana Among Them

A further question reconciles this teaching with Fazang's own earlier claim, back at the start of Part 28, that the buddha's fruit "cannot be spoken of" — and answers it by naming the very figures whose stories this site's own Gaṇḍavyūha collection tells at length.

Chinese Source

問上言果分離緣不可說相。但論因分者。何故十信終心。即辯作佛得果法耶。答令言作佛者。但從初見聞已去。乃至第二生即成解行。解行終心因位窮滿者。於第三生即得彼究竟自在圓融果矣。由此因體依果成故。但因滿者即沒於果海中也。為是證境界故。不可說也。此如龍女及普莊嚴童子善財童子并兜率天子等。於三生中即克彼果義等。廣如經辯應準思之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: It was said above that the fruit-portion is apart from conditions and its features cannot be spoken of, and that only the cause-portion is properly discussed — why, then, is it explained that one whose mind reaches the very culmination of the Ten Faiths thereby becomes a buddha and attains the fruit? Answer: What is meant by "becoming a buddha" here is this: from the very first seeing and hearing of the teaching onward — by the second lifetime, understanding and practice are accomplished; and once the causal stage is brought to its culmination at the completion of understanding and practice, then, in the third lifetime, that ultimate, autonomous, and perfectly fused fruit is attained. Because this causal substance is established precisely in dependence on the fruit, once the cause is fulfilled it is thereby submerged within the ocean of the fruit itself — and because this belongs to the domain of realization, it cannot be spoken of. This is exactly as with the Nāga Girl, and the youth Universal Adornment, the youth Sudhana, and the son of the gods of Tuṣita, and others, who in the course of three lifetimes achieved exactly that meaning of the fruit. This is explained at length in the sūtra and should be considered accordingly.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

善財童子, "the youth Sudhana," names the very pilgrim whose fifty-three-teacher journey this site translates in full as the Gaṇḍavyūha — Fazang is citing him here by name as a concrete case of the "three lifetimes" pattern he has just laid out. What reads, in Sudhana's own narrative, as a single lifetime's pilgrimage from teacher to teacher is, on Fazang's doctrinal accounting, itself already an instance of the deeper claim: causal practice culminating, without a hard boundary, directly into the fruit it was never really separate from. The Nāga Girl belongs to the Lotus Sūtra rather than the Avataṃsaka, attaining buddhahood in a single instant in front of a skeptical assembly; Fazang treats her here as making exactly the same point from a different scripture. This is a case where the treatise's abstract argument and this site's own narrative translations directly meet — a reader who has followed Sudhana south from teacher to teacher already knows, concretely, what Fazang is here arguing in the abstract.

Not the Three Vehicles' "One Thought," but Something Further

A final question distinguishes the One Vehicle's version of this doctrine from a similar-sounding claim already found within the three vehicles, before Fazang closes the gate.

Chinese Source

問上言一念即得作佛者。三乘中已有此義。與彼何別。答三乘望理。為一念即得作佛。今此一乘。一念即得具足一切教義。理事因果等如上一切法門。及與一切眾生皆悉同時同時同時同時同時同時同時同時同時同時作佛。後皆新新斷惑。亦不住學地而成正覺。具足十佛以顯無盡逆順德故。及因陀羅微細九世十世等遍通諸位。謂說十信終心已去。十解．十行．十迴向．十地及佛地等。同時遍成無有前後。具足一切耳。然此一念與百千劫無有異也。直須思之。此即第三諸法相即自在門訖。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: The claim that a single thought-moment already attains buddhahood is a meaning already found within the three vehicles — how does this differ from that? Answer: Within the three vehicles, this is said only with respect to principle: a single thought-moment attains buddhahood in that sense alone. Within this One Vehicle, however, a single thought-moment attains, in full, every teaching and meaning, every dharma-gate of principle and phenomenon, cause and fruit, and all the rest discussed above — and, together with this, all sentient beings, every one, simultaneously-simultaneously-simultaneously-simultaneously-simultaneously-simultaneously-simultaneously-simultaneously-simultaneously-simultaneously become buddhas. And afterward, they go on, ever anew, cutting off affliction, without ever remaining fixed at the stage of a trainee, and yet already accomplishing true awakening — replete with the ten buddhas, so as to manifest inexhaustible virtue, both counter-flow and with-flow, and pervading, by way of Indra's Net and the subtle, all nine and ten times, universally penetrating every stage. That is to say: from the culmination of the Ten Faiths onward — the Ten Understandings, the Ten Practices, the Ten Transferences, the Ten Grounds, and the Ground of Buddhahood, and so on — all are accomplished simultaneously and universally, with no distinction of before or after, each replete with the whole. And this single thought-moment is, in this way, no different at all from a hundred thousand kalpas. This should be pondered directly.

This concludes the third gate, the gate of all dharmas being mutually identical and freely autonomous.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The ten-fold repetition of "simultaneously" 同時 in the source text is the same device already met in Part 29's ten-fold "inexhaustible," and we have kept it for the same reason — Fazang is not merely stating simultaneity, he is making the sentence itself refuse to resolve into a simple sequence, the way a stutter of "at once, at once, at once" outlasts a reader's patience for finding a "then" hidden anywhere inside it.

Note, too, the stage-list running from the Ten Faiths through "the Ten Understandings" 十解 — an older name for what this site's Gaṇḍavyūha pages call the Ten Abodes 十住, the stage Sudhana himself works through with his first ten teachers — up through the Ten Practices, Ten Transferences, and Ten Grounds Sudhana completes with his later teachers, to the Ground of Buddhahood itself. The three-vehicle doctrine, Fazang says, lets a single thought-moment reach buddhahood "with respect to principle" only — a formal truth about emptiness or suchness. The One Vehicle's version claims something considerably larger: that a single thought-moment already carries the entire sweep of practice just listed, and every being along with it, without in any way excusing the bodhisattva from actually walking the road stage by stage afterward. The two claims are meant to stand together, not to make practice pointless — which is exactly why Sudhana's pilgrimage, cited two exchanges above, still takes him through fifty-three teachers rather than none at all.
