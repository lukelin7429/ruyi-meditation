# The One Vehicle's Distinctions in Teaching and Meaning — Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of 《華嚴一乘教義分齊章》 (*Huayan Yisheng Jiaoyi Fenqi Zhang*), Taishō T1866, vol. 45, by Fazang (法藏, 643–712), commonly called the *Wujiao Zhang* — the "Essay on the Five Teachings." They exist so that vocabulary stays consistent across all 34 parts, and so that a later Huayan-adjacent text on this site can inherit these renderings rather than re-coin them.

## What this text is, and why it matters

Every other piece of Huayan material already on this site is either the Avataṃsaka Sūtra itself — the [Gaṇḍavyūha](/sutras/gandavyuha/), the Chapter of Pure Conduct, the Vows of Samantabhadra — or Vasubandhu's Indian commentary on one of its chapters, the [Treatise on the Sūtra of the Ten Grounds](/sutras/dasabhumika-shastra/). This treatise is different in kind: it is not the sūtra, and not a line-by-line commentary on it, but the founding Chinese systematizer of the Huayan school standing entirely outside the text and explaining, in his own architecture, what kind of teaching the Avataṃsaka is, how it ranks against everything else the Buddha ever taught, and what makes its vision of reality cohere. Fazang was the third patriarch of Huayan in the traditional count (after Dushun and Zhiyan) but is generally credited as its effective systematizer — the one who gave the school the vocabulary, the classification scheme, and the formal doctrines by which it would be known ever after. This treatise, composed sometime in the 690s, is that systematization in its most complete surviving form.

Two of its contributions are among the most cited passages in all of Chinese Buddhist scholarship: the **Five Teachings** (五教) doctrinal classification in Gate 4, ranking Hīnayāna below the Initial, Final, Sudden, and — at the summit — Perfect Teaching reserved for the Avataṃsaka alone; and the **Ten Mysterious Gates** (十玄門) and **Six Characteristics** (六相) of Gate 10, the formal statement of how any one thing fully contains, and is contained by, everything else. A reader who has only ever encountered Huayan through the Avataṃsaka's own narrative imagery — Indra's Net, Maitreya's tower, a single thought containing every kalpa — will find here the abstract machinery underneath those images, worked out with the patience of a philosopher.

## Structure: Fazang's own Ten Gates, not the source's four fascicles

The base text divides into four fascicles (卷第一 through 卷第四), but Fazang's own topical outline — stated at the very opening of Part 1 — is a set of **Ten Gates** (十門) that rarely align with those fascicle breaks. This site's 34 parts follow the Ten Gates, not the fascicles; where a part happens to straddle a fascicle boundary (Part 16, crossing 卷二/卷三; Part 24, opening 卷四), a note flags it.

| 10 | 義理分齊 | The Principle Itself | 24–34 | ~15,295 |

Gate 9, by far the longest, runs ten sub-topics in sequence — consciousness, spiritual lineage, path-stages, practice-duration, the practicing body, eliminating affliction, the two vehicles turning to Mahāyāna, buddhahood's nature, a buddha's teaching-domain, and the buddha-bodies — analyzing each across all Five Teachings in turn. Gate 10 opens with the causal theory (the Three Natures, the Six Meanings of a Cause) that the Ten Mysterious Gates and Six Characteristics are built on, then delivers them.

Total base text: approximately 48,384 Chinese characters, split across 34 parts averaging roughly 1,420 characters each, ranging from about 610 (Part 3's close of Gate 2) to 2,290 (Part 25's dense dialectical Q&A on the three natures).

## Reading-note sourcing

Per this site's commentator-vetting convention, reading notes are normally grounded in a named premodern commentator. This text is the exception by design: Fazang himself is the primary, authoritative voice defining Huayan doctrine here, and no separate ancient commentary stands between his prose and the reader the way, say, Jingying Huiyuan's commentary stands beside the *Daśabhūmika-śāstra* elsewhere on this site. All reading notes across all 34 parts are Ru-Yi Meditation Center's own, stated honestly as such, written to make a genuinely dense scholastic text approachable without softening its actual argumentative structure — the objections, citations, and rebuttals are translated in full, not paraphrased into summary.

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 一乘 / 三乘 / 小乘 | the One Vehicle / the three vehicles / the Hīnayāna | Standard throughout. |
| 別教(一乘) / 同教(一乘) | the Separate Teaching / the Common Teaching | Fazang's own two-way division of how the One Vehicle relates to the three vehicles. **Deliberately worded differently** from Tiantai's own 別教/通教, already rendered "the distinct teaching"/"the shared teaching" on this site's [*Fahua Xuanyi*](/sutras/fahua-xuanyi/) pages — these are two unrelated classification schemes from two different schools, and reusing Tiantai's exact wording here would wrongly suggest a connection that isn't there. |
| 五教: 小乘教 / 大乘始教 / 終教 / 頓教 / 圓教 | the Hīnayāna Teaching / the Initial Teaching / the Final Teaching / the Sudden Teaching / the Perfect Teaching | Fazang's central classification (Gate 4, Part 5). Note that Tiantai *also* calls its own summit teaching 圓教, rendered identically as "the Perfect Teaching" on this site's Tiantai pages — this is not a translation collision but a genuine historical rivalry: both schools claimed the same honorific term for their own doctrine, and each meant something different by it. |
| 判教 | doctrinal classification (panjiao) | The genre of the whole exercise; Gate 3 surveys ten predecessors, Gate 4 gives Fazang's own. |
| 十宗 | the Ten Doctrines | Fazang's second, finer-grained classification in Gate 4, reaching back through six named early Buddhist scholastic schools (Sarvāstivāda, Sautrāntika, etc.); each translated descriptively rather than given a fixed short label. |
| 阿賴耶識 | ālayavijñāna (the storehouse consciousness) | Matches [*Cheng Weishi Lun*](/sutras/cheng-weishi-lun/)'s glossary. |
| 種性 | lineage (spiritual lineage, gotra) | 五種性 = "the five lineages"; 一闡提 kept as the loanword icchantika. |
| 三性: 遍計所執性 / 依他起性 / 圓成實性 | the discriminated, attached-to nature / the dependent nature / the perfectly accomplished nature | Kept identical to the [*Cheng Weishi Lun*](/sutras/cheng-weishi-lun/) and [*Mahāyānasaṃgraha*](/sutras/mahayanasamgraha/) glossaries — Parts 24–26 discuss the same three natures at length. |
| 真如 | suchness | Standard throughout. |
| 十地 | the Ten Grounds | Matches [*Daśabhūmika-śāstra*](/sutras/dasabhumika-shastra/). |
| 波羅蜜 | pāramitā | Left untransliterated, matching *Daśabhūmika-śāstra*. |
| 煩惱障 / 所知障 | the afflictive hindrance / the cognitive hindrance | The two-hindrance framework running through Parts 17–20's account of eliminating affliction. |
| 因陀羅網 | Indra's Net | Gate 10, Part 31. |
| 十玄(緣起無礙法)門 | the Ten Mysterious Gates | Of unobstructed dependent origination; Parts 28–32. |
| 六相(圓融) | the Six Characteristics | 總相 the Total characteristic, 別相 the Particular characteristic, 同相 the Identical characteristic, 異相 the Different characteristic, 成相 the Formative characteristic, 壞相 the Disintegrating characteristic — Parts 33–34. |
| 因六義 | the six meanings of a cause | Empty/existent, potent/impotent, condition-dependent/condition-independent — Fazang's causal theory underlying the Ten Mysterious Gates; Parts 26–27. |
| 法界緣起 | dependent origination of the dharma-realm | Huayan's own name for how all things arise together, mutually conditioning and containing one another without obstruction. |
| 主伴(具足) | host and retinue (fully present) | The recurring image of one central item surrounded by every other item as its supporting retinue, used throughout Gate 9 and Gate 10. |

## A note on CBETA transcription quirks

The CBETA base text carries a handful of small idiosyncrasies that translators corrected silently rather than reproducing literally: a handful of decorative ◎ section-divider glyphs (dropped, as they carry no semantic content); the translator-colophon line "唐大薦福寺沙門法藏述" repeated at the head of each of the source's four fascicles (kept once, at the very opening of Part 1, rather than four times); at least one evident single-character transcription slip corrected in context ("伏斷波障" read as "伏斷彼障," *suppress and cut that hindrance*, since "wave hindrance" does not parse); and the closing eight-line gāthā verse, which the source punctuates with none of the usual sentence-final marks at all — formatted here as verse rather than forced into prose sentences. One genuine content gap was caught during final source-coverage verification and restored: a short transitional sentence in Part 4's doxography, noting that Vinaya Master Guangtong's own disciples, led by Zunentong, continued to uphold his three-teaching scheme after him.

## For any future Huayan-adjacent text

Several renderings coined specifically for this treatise — "the Separate Teaching"/"the Common Teaching" for 別教/同教, "the Ten Mysterious Gates," and the six fixed English names for the Six Characteristics — have no prior precedent elsewhere on this site and were chosen with an eye to reuse. A future translator taking up Chengguan's or Zongmi's later Huayan writings, which inherit and extend this exact vocabulary, should check this glossary before coining fresh renderings for the same terms.
