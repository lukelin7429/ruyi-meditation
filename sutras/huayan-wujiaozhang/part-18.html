Eliminating Affliction II

Why Bodhisattvas Keep What Arhats Cut斷惑分齊・終教

Against the Initial Teaching's careful bookkeeping in Part 17, the Final Teaching says something closer to the opposite: that śrāvakas cannot fully cut even the afflictive hindrance, only suppress it, and that a bodhisattva does not follow their order of cutting at all. Instead the bodhisattva deliberately keeps a portion of affliction alive — not from weakness, but as a chosen instrument, so as to stay reborn among suffering beings while directing every effort at the deeper cognitive hindrance instead.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1866, vol. 45, 華嚴一乘教義分齊章 Author: Fazang 法藏, 643–712, Tang Reading notes: Ru-Yi, original

The Text

The Final Teaching: Śrāvakas Suppress, but Do Not Cut

Chinese Source

若依終教。諸聲聞於煩惱障尚不能斷。但能折伏。何況能斷所知障。故彌勒所問經論云。一切聲聞辟支佛人。不能如實修四無量。不能究竟斷諸煩惱。但能折伏一切煩惱等。楞伽經文亦如上說。

Ru-Yi Translation

According to the Final Teaching, śrāvakas cannot even cut the afflictive hindrance — they can only suppress it — still less can they cut the cognitive hindrance. Hence the treatise on the Sūtra of Maitreya's Questions says: "All śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas are unable to cultivate the four immeasurables as they truly are, and are unable to cut off every affliction to its very root; they can only suppress every affliction," and so on. The text of the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra likewise says the same as above.

問此說何故與前教不同耶。答彼為引二乘故。未深說故。是故以上就下說煩惱障同彼二乘至佛地智方盡。又以下同上。亦許二乘全斷惑障分斷所知障。今此就實。以愚法二乘無廣大心故。不究竟斷煩惱障。又亦可。前約三乘中聲聞。此中約愚法故不同也。

Question: Why does this account differ from the teaching given before? Answer: That earlier teaching was given in order to draw the two vehicles onward, and so did not speak to the matter at its full depth. This is why, accommodating the lower to the higher, it said that the afflictive hindrance, like the cognitive hindrance, is exhausted for śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas only at the wisdom of the ground of buddhahood; and, accommodating the higher to the lower, it likewise allowed that the two vehicles fully cut the afflictive hindrance while only partially cutting the cognitive hindrance. This present account speaks to the matter as it truly is: because the naive two vehicles lack the vast, expansive mind, they do not cut the afflictive hindrance to its very root. Or again, it may be said that the earlier teaching spoke of the śrāvaka within the three vehicles, while this one speaks of the naive śrāvaka — hence the difference.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This is the same move already seen at the end of Part 17, run in reverse. There, the Initial Teaching's "sudden liberation" showed the Hīnayāna to be inferior by claiming a capacity it lacked. Here, Fazang has the Final Teaching openly contradict the Initial Teaching's claim that śrāvakas fully cut the afflictive hindrance — and resolves the contradiction not by picking a winner, but by treating both statements as true relative to their audience: what looks like disagreement between two teachings is, on this reading, two different pedagogical accommodations of the same underlying reality.

The Bodhisattva's Own Reckoning: Direct Affliction and Its Residue

Chinese Source

其菩薩人。於二障中不分俱生及分別。但有正使及習氣。地前伏使現。初地斷使種。地上除習氣。佛地究竟清淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the bodhisattva: within the two hindrances, no distinction is drawn between the innate and the discriminated; there is only the direct affliction itself and its habitual residue. Before the first ground, its manifest activity is suppressed; at the first ground, its seed is cut; on the grounds above, the habitual residue is removed; and at the ground of buddhahood, purity is ultimately complete.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Notice what has quietly dropped out of the picture. The Initial Teaching's whole apparatus in Part 17 — discriminated versus innate, ten root afflictions, 128 counted instances — was built around the distinction between what a deliberate wrong view generates and what arises spontaneously. The Final Teaching's bodhisattva does not track that distinction at all. What matters now is only whether an affliction is still actively present ("direct affliction," 正使) or merely lingering as a worn groove ("habitual residue," 習氣) after the affliction itself is gone — the same vāsanā distinction already met in this site's Hundred Treatise homage verses, where only a buddha, not an arhat, is said to have worn away the traces as well as the affliction.

Retaining the Afflictive Hindrance, Through the First Ground

Chinese Source

然彼地前三賢位中。初既不墮二乘地中。於煩惱障自在能斷。留故不斷。為除所知障等故。是故梁攝論云。十解已去得出世淨心。又云。十解心已上名聖人。不墮二乘地故。仁王經云。地前得人空而不取證等。又起信論得少分見法身作八相等皆此義也。以此菩薩唯怖智障故。修唯識真如等觀。伏斷彼障。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet within the three worthy stages before the first ground, since from the outset one does not fall into the stage of the two vehicles, one is fully capable of cutting the afflictive hindrance — but retains it, and does not cut it, in order to remove the cognitive hindrance and the rest. Hence the Liang translation of the Mahāyānasaṃgraha says: "From the Ten Abodes onward, one attains a mind pure and transcending the world," and again: "From the mind of the Ten Abodes onward one is called a noble person, since one does not fall into the stage of the two vehicles." The Sūtra for Humane Kings says: "Before the first ground, one attains the emptiness of the person, yet does not take this as one's final realization," and the like; and the Awakening of Faith's account of one who attains a partial vision of the dharma-body and performs the eight marks expresses this same meaning. Because this bodhisattva fears only the cognitive hindrance, he cultivates the contemplation of consciousness-only, of suchness, and the rest, thereby suppressing and cutting that hindrance.

然於煩惱障非但不怖不修對治。亦乃故留助成勝行。初地已上斷於所知障一分麁故。於煩惱障不復更留。是故二障不分見修。至初地時正使俱盡。

Yet with respect to the afflictive hindrance, he not only does not fear it and does not cultivate any antidote against it — he deliberately retains it, so as to assist superior practice. From the first ground onward, once a gross portion of the cognitive hindrance has been cut, he no longer needs to retain the afflictive hindrance any further. Hence, without dividing the two hindrances into what belongs to the path of seeing and what belongs to the path of cultivation, by the time of the first ground the direct affliction of both is entirely exhausted.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This is the doctrine the whole part is named for. A bodhisattva below the first ground could, on this account, cut the afflictive hindrance just as an arhat does — and chooses not to. The three worthy stages (the Ten Abodes, Ten Practices, and Ten Dedications together, the long preparatory path before the Ten Grounds proper) are where this choice is made and held. The reasoning is unsentimental: affliction that keeps one bound to ordinary rebirth is, for a bodhisattva who has already secured freedom from falling into a śrāvaka's narrower nirvāṇa, no longer a danger but a resource — it is what keeps him circulating among suffering beings rather than exiting into a private peace, while his actual effort goes toward the cognitive hindrance, the obstacle a compassionate rebirth cannot dissolve on its own. Once the first ground is reached and a bodhisattva has cut into the cognitive hindrance directly, the afflictive hindrance has done its work and is released along with it. Vasubandhu's own Daśabhūmikasūtra-śāstra gives the positive side of this same schedule, ground by ground, from the vantage of what each ground newly accomplishes rather than what it newly cuts.

Why the Bodhisattva Does Not Follow the Śrāvaka's Order

Chinese Source

故彌勒所問經論云。問曰。若聲聞人先斷見道所斷煩惱。然後漸斷修道煩惱。何故菩薩不同聲聞。答曰。菩薩之人無量世來。為諸眾生作利益事。後見真如甘露法界。觀彼一切諸眾生身。而實不異我所求處。是故菩薩見修道中。一切煩惱能障利益眾生行故。即見道中一切俱斷。此文為證。

Ru-Yi Translation

Hence the treatise on the Sūtra of Maitreya's Questions says: "Question: If the śrāvaka first cuts the afflictions to be cut on the path of seeing, and only afterward gradually cuts the afflictions of the path of cultivation, why does the bodhisattva not do the same as the śrāvaka? Answer: The bodhisattva, across measureless ages, has performed deeds of benefit for sentient beings; and afterward, seeing the ambrosial dharma-realm of suchness, he beholds the bodies of all those sentient beings as, in truth, no different from the very place he himself seeks. For this reason, since every affliction of both the path of seeing and the path of cultivation is equally capable of obstructing the bodhisattva's practice of benefiting sentient beings, he cuts them all together, at once, within the path of seeing itself." This passage stands as the proof.

Manas, and What "Transformed into Purity" Means

Chinese Source

其末那煩惱。亦初地斷麁後除殘習。故無性攝論云。轉染污末那得平等性智。初現觀時先已證得。修道位中轉復清淨。解云。轉淨者除習氣也。以正使先斷故。後但云轉淨更不云斷也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for the affliction of manas: at the first ground its gross portion too is cut, and only afterward is its remaining habitual residue removed. Hence Asvabhāva's commentary on the Mahāyānasaṃgraha says: "By transforming defiled manas one attains the wisdom of the equality of natures; this is first realized at the moment of direct realization, and, within the stage of the path of cultivation, is transformed into ever greater purity." Explanation: "transformed into purity" means the habitual residue is removed. Since the direct affliction has already been cut earlier, what follows is spoken of only as "transformed into purity," and no longer as "cutting."

若云此是入真見道時暫伏不起非正斷者。即不得言轉復清淨。以總未斷何名轉淨。若依始教容如彼說。是故當知此教地上但除習氣故。

Were one to say that this is merely a temporary suppression at the moment of entering the true path of seeing, and not a genuine cutting, then one could not speak of "being transformed into ever greater purity" — for if nothing at all had yet been cut, how could one call it "transformed into purity"? Such an account would be admissible within the Initial Teaching. One should therefore understand that, in this teaching, what remains to be removed on the grounds is only habitual residue.

Three Kinds of Impurity, and Residue Born Without Interval

Chinese Source

寶性論云。不淨者。一切凡夫有煩惱障故。有垢者。以諸聲聞辟支佛等有智障故。有點者。以諸菩薩摩訶薩等。依被二種習氣障故。解云。論釋云地上。名摩訶薩。故知但有習氣障也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The Ratnagotravibhāga says: "The impure are so called because all ordinary beings have the afflictive hindrance; the stained are so called because śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas have the cognitive hindrance; the spotted are so called because bodhisattva-mahāsattvas are subject to the hindrance of the two kinds of habitual residue." Explanation: since the treatise's own commentary glosses "those on the grounds" as "mahāsattvas," one knows they are subject only to the hindrance of habitual residue.

此中習氣從斷正使種子。無間方有習氣。何以故。未斷已前無微薄故。種斷無間方有微薄故。論說名無間生習氣。

Here, the habitual residue arises immediately upon the cutting of the direct affliction's seed, with no interval between the two. Why is this? Because before that cutting there is as yet no attenuation at all; only immediately upon the seed's being cut does attenuation appear. Hence the treatise speaks of it as "the habitual residue born without interval."

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Three grades of impurity, mapped to three kinds of being, run through this passage: ordinary beings carry the afflictive hindrance itself; śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, having left that behind, still carry the cognitive hindrance; bodhisattvas on the grounds carry neither in active form, only the habitual residue both leave behind. The image is of impurity growing progressively fainter rather than disappearing all at once — dirt, then a stain the dirt has left, then a mere spot too faint to call a stain.

Why Superior Wisdom Does Not Collapse Into the Two Vehicles' Stage

Chinese Source

問云何地上煩惱使盡。而得不墮二乘地耶。答智力勝故。智障正使亦已斷除故。如始教小乘頓出利人斷欲界惑六品盡時。上二界六品亦同斷盡故。猶尚不得彼一來果。況此菩薩道力殊勝。又況已斷智障使故。若地前及始教容有彼失此中無也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: How can it be that, once the direct affliction is exhausted on the grounds, one still does not fall into the stage of the two vehicles? Answer: Because the power of wisdom is superior, and because the direct affliction of the cognitive hindrance too has already been cut away. Consider how, in the Initial Teaching's account of the Hīnayāna, when a sharp practitioner of sudden liberation has exhausted the six grades of desire-realm affliction, the corresponding six grades in the two higher realms are cut away at the very same time — and yet he still does not attain the fruit of once-returning. How much more, then, for a bodhisattva, whose power on the path is far more excellent still — and all the more so, given that the direct affliction of the cognitive hindrance has already been cut. Such a defect might be admissible before the first ground, or within the Initial Teaching; here it does not occur.

Cutting and Wisdom Together, and Affliction as Suchness Itself

Chinese Source

又此教中正斷惑時智起惑滅。非初非中後。前中後取故。云何滅。如虛空本來清淨。如是滅。廣如十地論說。又此教中煩惱等法。皆是真如隨緣所作。是故不異真如。是煩惱義。如起信論說。又經云。一切法即如如等。煩惱準此。是故當知。與前諸教施設不同。宜應知之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, within this teaching, at the very moment affliction is genuinely cut, wisdom arises and affliction ceases — this belongs to neither the beginning, the middle, nor the end, since any of the beginning, middle, or end could equally be taken as the moment. In what sense, then, does it "cease"? As space is, from the very outset, originally pure — it is in just this sense that it "ceases." This is set out at length in the Daśabhūmikasūtra-śāstra. Further, within this teaching, affliction and the other such dharmas are all made by suchness according to conditions, and are for this reason no different from suchness — this is the very meaning of "affliction," as the Awakening of Faith explains; and scripture says, "All dharmas are precisely suchness itself," and the rest. Affliction should be understood in just the same way. One should therefore know that this teaching's own account differs from what the earlier teachings established, and should attend to it accordingly.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The closing move undercuts everything counted so carefully in this part and the last. If affliction is, at bottom, nothing but suchness appearing under conditions, then talk of cutting it "at the first ground" or "grade by grade" is itself only a conventional way of speaking — the space simile makes the point bluntly: space was never dirty to begin with, so its "purification" was never the removal of an actual stain. This is the seam along which Part 20's Sudden and Perfect Teachings will eventually split the topic open completely, holding first that there was never anything to cut at all, and then that cutting one affliction cuts every affliction at once.
