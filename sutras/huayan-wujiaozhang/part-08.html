Ten Marks That Set the One Vehicle Apart

The Distinguishing Marks That Are Set Forth施設異相

Gate Eight closes the first half of this treatise by making concrete everything argued so far. Where earlier parts distinguished the One Vehicle through parable, logic, and doctrine, this part points to ten plain facts about it — the moment it was taught, the place, who taught it, who was in the room, what it rests on, how it is spoken, its positions, its practice, its dharma-gates, and, most strikingly, its ordinary physical settings, down to a single grain of dust. Ten marks, and with them, Fazang closes the whole first half of his ten-gate treatise.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1866, vol. 45, 華嚴一乘教義分齊章 Author: Fazang 法藏, 643–712, Tang Reading notes: Ru-Yi, original

The Text

1. Time and Place 一・時異　二・處異

Chinese Source

第八施設異相者。然此異相繁多。略約十門以顯無盡。何者十異。一者時異。謂此一乘要在初時第二七日說。猶如日出先照高山等。故論云。此示法勝故。在初時及勝處說也。若爾何故不初七日說。思惟因緣行等如論釋。又此即是時因陀羅網等故。即攝一切時。若前若後各不可說劫。通前際後際。並攝在此一時中也。三乘等不爾。以隨逐機宜時不定故。或前或後亦不一時收一切劫等。二者處異。謂此一乘要在蓮華藏世界海中眾寶莊嚴菩提樹下。則攝七處八會等及餘不可說不可說世界海並在此中。以一處攝一切處故。是故不動道樹。遍昇六天等者。是此義也。又此華藏世界。通因陀羅網故周測諸塵。於此稱法界處。說彼一乘稱法界法門也。三乘等則不爾。在娑婆界木樹等處。亦無一處即一切處等。問若爾何故佛地經等。亦在淨土中說耶。答彼經但云在光曜宮殿等具十八種圓滿。亦不別指摩竭提國等。以彼為地上菩薩說佛地功德故。在三界外受用土中。此三乘終教及一乘同教說。若此華嚴。皆云在華嚴界內摩竭國等。不云娑婆內。亦不云三界外。故知別也。餘義準知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Eighth, the distinguishing marks that are set forth: these distinguishing marks are numerous indeed. Here, in brief, ten gates are used to display their inexhaustibility. What are the ten distinctions?

One, the distinction of time. This One Vehicle is necessarily taught at the second seven-day period of the first time, just as the sun, on rising, first shines on the high mountains, and the like. Hence the treatise says: this shows that the Dharma is superior, and is therefore taught in the first period and in the superior place. If so, why was it not taught in the very first seven-day period? This is explained by the treatise in terms of contemplating causes and conditions and their functioning, and the like. Moreover, this is itself "time" in the sense of Indra's Net and the rest, and so it gathers in every time whatsoever: whether earlier or later, each and every inexpressible kalpa, spanning the furthest limit before and the furthest limit after, is all gathered together within this single moment. The three vehicles and the rest are not like this, since, following what suits each capacity, their time is not fixed — sometimes earlier, sometimes later — and they do not, in a single moment, gather in every kalpa.

Two, the distinction of place. This One Vehicle is necessarily taught beneath the bodhi tree adorned with every kind of jewel, within the ocean of the lotus-treasury world — and it gathers in the seven places and eight assemblies and the rest, together with every other inexpressible, inexpressible ocean of worlds, all together within this one place, since one place gathers in every place. This is the meaning of "not moving from the tree of the Way, yet universally ascending to the six heavens," and the like. Moreover, this lotus-treasury world interpenetrates Indra's Net, and so encompasses and measures every mote of dust; it is at this place that accords with the dharma-realm that that One Vehicle's Dharma-gate, which itself accords with the dharma-realm, is taught. The three vehicles and the rest are not like this: they are taught in this Sahā world, beneath ordinary trees and the like, and there is no sense in which one place simply is every place.

Question: if so, why do texts like the Buddhabhūmi Sūtra likewise say it was taught within a pure land? Answer: that sūtra only says it was in a radiant palace and the like, endowed with eighteen kinds of perfection; it does not separately specify Magadha or the like, since it was taught for the sake of bodhisattvas already on the grounds, expounding the virtues of a buddha's own ground, within an enjoyment-land beyond the three realms. This is spoken by the three vehicles' Final Teaching and the One Vehicle as the Common Teaching. As for this Avataṃsaka, it says throughout that it takes place within the Avataṃsaka world's own Magadha and the like — never saying it is within the Sahā world, and never saying it is beyond the three realms. Hence one knows it is distinct. The rest may be understood by analogy.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

"Not moving from the tree of the Way, yet universally ascending to the six heavens" describes a structural fact about the Avataṃsaka that a reader who has not seen its full contents laid out might not expect: the sūtra's traditional "seven places, eight [or nine] assemblies" 七處八會 has the Buddha simultaneously teaching beneath the bodhi tree on earth and in successive heavenly palaces — the Trāyastriṃśa Heaven, the Yāma Heaven, the Tuṣita Heaven, and beyond — without ever once rising from his seat. Vairocana's body does not travel between these locations the way an ordinary teacher would; each assembly is, rather, one more place where the same single teaching becomes visible, all of it already gathered within the "one place" this passage claims for the ocean of the lotus-treasury world. The Gaṇḍavyūha's own pilgrimage, translated in full elsewhere on this site, takes place within the last and longest of these assemblies.

2. Who Teaches, and Who Is Present 三・主異　四・眾異

Chinese Source

三者主異。謂此一乘。要是盧舍那十身佛。及無盡三世間說。如普賢行品云。佛說菩薩說剎說眾生說三世一切說等。不同三乘等是化身及受用身等說。餘義準知。四者眾異。謂此一乘經首唯列普賢等菩薩及佛境界中諸神王眾。不同三乘等。或唯聲聞眾或大小二眾等。問若爾何故第九會中有聲聞眾耶。答彼中列聲聞意者有二種。一寄對顯法故。為示如聾如盲顯法深勝也。二文殊出會外所攝六千比丘。非是前所引眾。此等皆是已在三乘中。令迴向一乘故。作是說也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Three, the distinction of the host. This One Vehicle is necessarily taught by Rocana in his ten bodies, together with the inexhaustible three realms of worlds. As the "Chapter on Samantabhadra's Practice" says: "The Buddha teaches, bodhisattvas teach, buddha-lands teach, sentient beings teach, the three time-periods teach, all things teach," and so on. This is not the same as the three vehicles and the rest, which are taught by a transformation-body or an enjoyment-body and the like. The rest may be understood by analogy.

Four, the distinction of the assembly. This One Vehicle's sūtra lists, at its very opening, only bodhisattvas such as Samantabhadra and the assemblies of the various spirit-kings within the Buddha's own domain — unlike the three vehicles and the rest, whose assembly is either śrāvakas alone, or a mixed assembly of the great and the small. Question: if so, why does the ninth assembly include an assembly of śrāvakas? Answer: the intent behind listing śrāvakas there is twofold. One, it is done by way of contrast, in order to disclose the Dharma — to show them as if deaf and as if blind, thereby making the Dharma's own profundity and superiority manifest. Two, the six thousand bhikṣus whom Mañjuśrī, going out beyond the assembly, gathered in are not the same as the assembly cited earlier — these were all already within the three vehicles, and this is said in order to turn them toward the One Vehicle.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

"As if deaf and as if blind" 如聾如盲 is one of the harshest images in the whole treatise, and it is aimed squarely at the śrāvakas who are physically present in the Avataṃsaka's assembly yet, on this reading, cannot perceive what is actually being taught around them — not absent, simply incapable of registering it, the way this treatise's own opening parts described a Dharma-ignorant disciple as unable even to perceive the Great Vehicle being taught. Mañjuśrī's group of six thousand bhikṣus, deliberately distinguished here from that unperceiving assembly, are the same monks who, in the Avataṃsaka's own narrative, follow Mañjuśrī as he departs the main assembly and travels south — the episode that leads directly into the Gaṇḍavyūha's opening scene, where the youth Sudhana, inspired by Mañjuśrī's teaching, sets out on the pilgrimage to fifty-three teachers that occupies the whole of that sūtra's closing chapter. What Fazang calls "turning toward the One Vehicle" here is Sudhana's own entire pilgrimage, described in a single clause.

3. What It Rests On, and How It Is Spoken 五・所依異　六・說異

Chinese Source

五者所依異。謂此一乘教起要依佛海印三昧中出。不同三乘等依佛後得智出。六者說異。謂此一乘。此一方說一事一義一品一會等時。必結通十方一切世界皆同此說。主伴共成一部。是故此經隨一文一句皆遍十方。多文多句亦皆遍十方。三乘等則不爾。但隨一方一相說。無此主伴該通等也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Five, the distinction of the basis relied upon. This One Vehicle's teaching necessarily arises out of the Buddha's ocean-seal absorption, unlike the three vehicles and the rest, which arise out of the Buddha's subsequently attained wisdom.

Six, the distinction in how it is taught. As for this One Vehicle: whenever, in this one location, one matter, one meaning, one chapter, or one assembly is taught, it is necessarily concluded by linking it through to every world of the ten directions, all alike teaching this same thing at once, host and retinue together completing a single text. This is why, in this sūtra, a single passage or a single sentence pervades the ten directions, and many passages and many sentences likewise all pervade the ten directions. The three vehicles and the rest are not like this: they are taught only according to one location and one aspect, without this host-and-retinue universality.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

"Subsequently attained wisdom" 後得智 is a standard Yogācāra technical term, ordinarily paired with "fundamental wisdom" 根本智: fundamental wisdom is a buddha's or bodhisattva's direct, non-conceptual realization of suchness, while subsequently attained wisdom is the wisdom that arises afterward, back within conventional, differentiated cognition, capable of perceiving particular things and communicating in ordinary language suited to particular listeners. Fazang's claim that the three vehicles are taught out of "subsequently attained wisdom" places them, by definition, within the realm of conventional, differentiated address — teaching pitched to one listener's capacity, one place, one occasion. The ocean-seal absorption that grounds the One Vehicle instead, precisely because it is not "subsequently attained" but the fundamental absorption itself, needs no such narrowing down to a single audience: whatever it discloses is automatically disclosed everywhere at once.

4. Position and Practice 七・位異　八・行異

Chinese Source

七者位異。謂此一乘所有位相上下皆齊。仍一一位中攝一切位。是故乃至佛等諸位。在信等位中。餘位亦然。三乘中則不爾。但隨當位上下階降。皆不相雜也。餘如下說。八者行異。謂隨一菩薩則具信等六位。一一位中。所有定散等差別行相並一時修。如東方一切世界中常入定等。西方世界中。常供養佛等。如是十方世界中。盡窮法界行。亦不分身。一時皆遍滿。一念皆遍修。一一念中亦如此。信位滿心已去。一一位皆如是修。更無優劣。又一行即一切行等通因陀羅網等。三乘則不爾。地上菩薩猶各有分齊。況地前者乎。餘如下說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seven, the distinction of position. As for this One Vehicle: all its positions, high and low, are equal to one another, and each single position gathers in every other position — hence even the position of buddhahood itself is present within the stage of faith and the rest, and the same holds for every other position. Within the three vehicles it is not so: each position is arranged according to its own rank, higher or lower, and none intermix with any other. The rest is discussed further below.

Eight, the distinction of practice. As for any single bodhisattva: he is endowed with all six positions, from faith onward, and within each single position, every differentiated aspect of practice, both meditative and non-meditative, is cultivated all at the same time — as in constantly entering samādhi throughout all the worlds of the east, and constantly making offerings to buddhas throughout the worlds of the west, and so through the worlds of the ten directions, exhausting the whole of the dharma-realm's practice, without even dividing his body to do so: all at once, everywhere fully present; within a single thought, all of it fully cultivated; and each and every thought is likewise so. From the point where the mind of the stage of faith is fulfilled onward, every single position is cultivated in just this way, with no superior or inferior among them. Moreover, one practice simply is every practice, interpenetrating through Indra's Net, and the like. Within the three vehicles it is not so: even bodhisattvas on the grounds still each have their own delimited scope, let alone those before the grounds. The rest is discussed further below.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

"Even the position of buddhahood itself is present within the stage of faith" is one of this treatise's most quoted single claims, taken up at far greater length in Part 14's discussion of stages and positions: an ordinary three-vehicle path treats the stage of faith, the ten grounds, and buddhahood as strictly sequential, each reachable only after the one before it is complete. Fazang's One Vehicle instead holds that any single position, taken seriously and completely, already contains every other position within it — not as a promise of future attainment, but as a present, mutual inclusion, the same "host and retinue" pattern already seen governing carts, teachings, and now practice itself.

5. Dharma-Gates, Doubled into Ten 九・法門異

Chinese Source

九者法門異。謂略舉十種以明之。一彼有三佛此有十佛。二彼有六通此有十通。三彼有三明此有十明。四彼有八解脫此有十解脫。五彼有四無畏此有十無畏。六彼有五眼此有十眼。七彼說三世此說十世。八彼有四諦此有十諦。九彼有四辯此有十辯。十彼百十八不共法此有十不共法。餘門無量。廣如經說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Nine, the distinction of dharma-gates. Here, in brief, ten instances are given to make this clear. One, they have three buddha-bodies; this has ten buddhas. Two, they have six superknowledges; this has ten superknowledges. Three, they have three insights; this has ten insights. Four, they have eight liberations; this has ten liberations. Five, they have four fearlessnesses; this has ten fearlessnesses. Six, they have five eyes; this has ten eyes. Seven, they speak of three time-periods; this speaks of ten time-periods. Eight, they have four truths; this has ten truths. Nine, they have four kinds of eloquence; this has ten kinds of eloquence. Ten, they have a hundred and eighteen dharmas exclusive to a buddha; this has ten dharmas exclusive to a buddha. The remaining gates are measureless, set out at length as the sūtra teaches.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Ten side-by-side comparisons, every one following the identical pattern: whatever standard three-vehicle Abhidharma counts in a familiar smaller number, the Avataṃsaka recounts as ten. This is not arithmetic inflation for its own sake — "ten" functions throughout Huayan literature as the number of totality and inexhaustibility itself (compare the "ten grounds," the "Ten Mysterious Gates," and the "ten times" already mentioned in this very part), so that recasting the three vehicles' three bodies, six superknowledges, or four truths as ten of the same is a compact way of asserting that the One Vehicle's version of each category is complete and inexhaustible where the three vehicles' version is partial. The single exception proves the rule from the other direction: item ten inverts the pattern, with the three vehicles' famously large number, the 118 dharmas exclusive to a buddha, condensed into the One Vehicle's own ten — a reminder that the point was never really about which number is bigger, but about which number is totalizing.

6. Even a Single Grain of Dust 十・事異

Chinese Source

十者事異。謂隨有舍林池地山等事。皆是法門。或是行或是位或教義等。而不壞其事。仍一一塵中。皆具足法界一切差別事。因陀羅微細成就。隨一事起皆悉如是。三乘等則不爾。但可說即空即真如等故。不同此也。又若以神通不思議力。容得暫現。非是彼法自恒如是。餘可準知。第八門竟。後有所詮義理二門。成中下二卷。畢十門矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Ten, the distinction of phenomena. Wherever there are things such as a dwelling, a grove, a pond, the earth, or a mountain, every one of them is itself a dharma-gate — whether as practice, as position, or as teaching and meaning, and the like — without this destroying the phenomenon as it is. Moreover, within each and every mote of dust, the whole of the dharma-realm's every differentiated phenomenon is completely present, accomplished through Indra's subtle and fine interpenetration — wherever any single phenomenon arises, it is, every time, entirely so. The three vehicles and the rest are not like this: one can, at most, say of them that they simply are emptiness, or simply are suchness, and the like — they are not the same as this. And even where, through the power of an inconceivable spiritual ability, something of this kind is made to appear for a time, this is not that dharma's own constant, inherent nature. The rest may be understood by analogy.

This concludes the eighth gate. What follows — the two gates of what is taught and the principle itself — make up the middle and lower fascicles, completing the ten gates.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The tenth mark is the quiet climax of the whole part, and arguably of the entire first half of this treatise. The first nine marks distinguish the One Vehicle through things that are, in their own way, still extraordinary — a unique moment beneath the bodhi tree, a buddha's own ten bodies, an assembly of spirit-kings. The tenth mark locates the very same totality inside the most ordinary objects imaginable: a dwelling, a grove, a pond, a mountain, a single grain of dust — "without this destroying the phenomenon as it is." The pond remains a pond; nothing about its everyday reality is dissolved or explained away. It is simply also, at the very same time, a complete dharma-gate containing the whole differentiated dharma-realm within it. This is the seed of the doctrine of mutual inclusion that the treatise will develop at enormous length much later, in the Ten Mysterious Gates' simile of the ten coins and Indra's Net proper — but it is worth meeting here first, in its plainest form, before the technical apparatus arrives.

Part Eight Complete — and the First Half of the Treatise

This closes Part Eight, and with it the eighth of Fazang's ten gates: ten concrete marks — time, place, teacher, assembly, basis, manner of speech, position, practice, dharma-gates, and phenomena themselves — through which the One Vehicle as its own Separate Teaching is shown to differ from the three vehicles at every point, down to the plainest object in the world.

With Part Eight complete, the first eight of Fazang's ten gates are finished: the One Vehicle established, its teaching and benefit clarified, ten earlier teachers surveyed, Fazang's own Five Teachings and Ten Doctrines proposed, the vehicles and teachings combined and dated, their sequence explained through ten kinds of disciple, and, now, ten marks setting the whole of it apart in concrete terms. The two gates that remain — Gate Nine, on what is actually taught, spanning consciousness, lineage, the stages of practice, and much more across fifteen further parts; and Gate Ten, the treatise's own account of the principle itself, including the Ten Mysterious Gates and the Six Characteristics that would become Huayan philosophy's most lasting technical legacy — carry the remaining twenty-six parts of this thirty-four-part collection, continuing in Part 9 with the storehouse consciousness as it appears across all five of the teachings just classified.
