Stages and Positions II — One Position Holding All Positions

Differences in What Is Taught, VI — Stages and Positions, Part Two所詮差別・行位分齊

Part 13 left the Final Teaching's three worthy stages mid-argument, over an apparent conflict about exactly when a bodhisattva stops being able to fall back. Fazang resolves it, finishes the Final Teaching's account, then moves through the Sudden Teaching's total silence on stages to the Perfect Teaching's most audacious claim in this sub-topic: any single position on the path — even the very first — already contains every other position, buddhahood included, illustrated through Sudhana's one-lifetime path and Maitreya's promise that Sudhana will see him become a buddha.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1866, vol. 45, 華嚴一乘教義分齊章 Fazang 法藏, 643–712, Tang Reading notes: Ru-Yi, original

1. The Final Teaching Completed: Practice That Outruns Its Own Definitions

Fazang first resolves the seeming clash left hanging at the end of Part 13 — the Renwang Sūtra places non-retrogression at the very first of the ten abodes, while the Sūtra of the Original Acts seems to allow retrogression as late as the sixth mind. The Awakening of Faith is brought in to settle it, and then a portrait follows of just how much a bodhisattva at this stage can already do.

Chinese Source · 行位分齊・終教（續）

者。起信論中。釋彼文為示現退也。為慢緩者策勵其心故。而實菩薩入發心住。即得不退也。其行相者。起信論說。三賢初位中少分得見法身。能於十方世界。八相成道利益眾生。又以願力受身自在。亦非業繫。又依三昧亦得少分見於報身佛。其所修行皆順真性。謂知法性體無慳貪。隨順修行檀波羅蜜等。廣如彼說。

Ru-Yi Translation

...at the sixth mind of the ten abodes — the Awakening of Faith explains that passage as merely displaying an appearance of retrogression, done in order to spur on those who are lax and complacent; in reality, once a bodhisattva enters the abode of the arising of the mind, he attains non-retrogression at once. As for the marks of practice: the Awakening of Faith says that, already within the initial position of the three worthy stages, one attains a partial vision of the dharma-body, becoming able to benefit sentient beings throughout the worlds of the ten directions by manifesting the eight marks of accomplishing the path; and, by the power of one's vow, one receives bodies at will, no longer bound by karma. By the power of samādhi, one also attains a partial vision of the reward-body buddha. All the practice one cultivates accords with the true nature — knowing that the essence of the dharma-nature is without stinginess or greed, one cultivates the pāramitā of giving and the rest in accordance with it. This is explained at length there.

又梁攝論中。十信名凡夫菩薩。十解名聖人菩薩等。其地上行位倍前準知。是故當知此中行位。與前始教淺深之相差別顯矣。問此教豈不通引二乘。何故行位不似小乘說耶。答此後諸教並皆深勝。所引二乘亦是純熟高勝機故。不假似彼也。

And the Mahāyānasaṃgraha says: the ten faiths are named the ordinary-person bodhisattva, and the ten understandings are named the holy-person bodhisattva, and so on; the positions and practice above the grounds may be understood on the same model, redoubled beyond what came before. One should therefore know that the difference in depth between the stages and positions here and in the preceding Initial Teaching has now become plain. Question: does this teaching not likewise draw the two vehicles on? Why, then, are its stages and positions not spoken of in a manner resembling the Hīnayāna? Answer: all the later teachings are progressively deeper and more excellent, and the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas they draw on are themselves already ripened to a lofty and superior capacity — so there is no need to borrow any resemblance to the Hīnayāna for them.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The "eight marks of accomplishing the path" — descent from Tuṣita, entry into the womb, birth, renunciation, defeating Māra, awakening, turning the Dharma-wheel, and final nirvāṇa — are the standard biography of a buddha, and the striking claim here is how early they appear: not at buddhahood itself, but already, partially, within the very first of the three worthy stages, well before the Ten Grounds. This is the same widening pattern this gate has traced from Part 9 onward — each more advanced teaching pulls a capability further back toward the beginning of the path. It also quietly answers Part 13's own portrait of the wavering bodhisattva in the abode of resolute conduct: the Final Teaching's bodhisattva, once he actually enters the position (rather than merely aspiring toward it), is already doing what the Initial Teaching reserved for buddhahood alone.

2. The Sudden Teaching: No Sequence to Describe

The Sudden Teaching dissolves the entire question of stages the way it dissolved consciousness and lineage in Parts 9 and 12 — not by denying practice, but by denying that practice has any describable structure at all.

Chinese Source

若依頓教。一切行位皆不可說。以離相故。一念不生即是佛故。若見行位差別等相。即是顛倒故。若寄言顯者。如楞伽云。初地即八地。乃至云無所有何次等。又思益經云。若人聞是諸法正性。勤行精進如說修行。不從一地至一地。若不從一地至一地。是人不住生死涅槃。如是等也。

Ru-Yi Translation

According to the Sudden Teaching, all stages and positions whatsoever cannot be spoken of at all, since they are apart from every mark — a single thought unarisen is itself the buddha. To perceive any mark of difference among stages and positions is itself a case of inverted perception. If one wishes to point toward it provisionally, in words: the Laṅkāvatāra says, "the first ground is precisely the eighth ground" — and further says, "there being nothing whatsoever, what sequence could there be?" And the Sūtra of Resolving Doubts says: "if a person hears this true nature of all dharmas, and diligently exerts himself, practicing as it is taught, he does not go from one ground to another ground. And because he does not go from one ground to another ground, this person abides neither in saṃsāra nor in nirvāṇa" — and so on.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

"The first ground is precisely the eighth ground" sounds, out of context, like an early and cruder version of the Perfect Teaching's own claim that one position holds all positions — the doctrine Fazang is about to spend the rest of this part unfolding at length. The difference is important: the Sudden Teaching collapses the grounds together by removing all distinguishing marks from them, leaving nothing describable behind; the Perfect Teaching, as the next section shows, keeps every position fully distinct and describable, and holds that each one, in its distinctness, still contains all the others. One arrives at "no stages" by erasing structure; the other arrives at "every stage" by making structure infinitely interpenetrating. They sound similar and mean opposite things.

3. The Perfect Teaching: One Position, Every Position

The Perfect Teaching's Separate account of position opens with its central and most quoted claim in this entire sub-topic: from the ten faiths to the buddha-ground there are six distinct positions, and yet attaining any single one of them already means attaining every one of them, by the same principles of interpenetration this collection will unfold fully in Parts 28 through 32.

Chinese Source · 行位分齊・圓教

若依圓教者有二義。一攝前諸教所明行位。以是此方便故。二據別教有其三義。一約寄位顯。謂始從十信乃至佛地六位不同。隨得一位得一切位。何以故。由以六相收故。主伴故。相入故。相即故。圓融故。經云。在於一地普攝一切諸地功德。是故經中十信滿心勝進分上得一切位及佛地者。是其事也。又以諸位及佛地等相即等故。即因果無二始終無礙。於一一位上即是菩薩即是佛者。是此義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

According to the Perfect Teaching, there are two accounts. The first takes up and includes the stages and positions set out by the preceding teachings, since these serve as its own skillful means. The second, based on the Separate Teaching, comprises three aspects. The first is clarification by way of provisional position: from the ten faiths all the way to the buddha-ground there are six distinct positions, yet attaining any one of them is to attain all of them. Why is this? Because they are gathered together by the six characteristics, by host and retinue, by mutual inclusion, by mutual identity, and by perfect interfusion. As the sūtra says: within a single ground, all the merits of every ground are universally gathered in. This is exactly what is meant when the sūtra says that, at the culminating advancement of the fulfillment of the ten faiths, one attains all the positions, and the buddha-ground as well. And because the various positions and the buddha-ground are all mutually identical, cause and fruit are not two, and beginning and end are unobstructed — so that, at any single position whatsoever, one is precisely a bodhisattva and precisely a buddha at once. This is the meaning intended here.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This paragraph names, for the first time in this collection, several of the technical terms this site will unfold at full length much later: the six characteristics (六相, Parts 33–34), host and retinue (主伴), mutual inclusion (相入), mutual identity (相即), and perfect interfusion (圓融, central to Parts 28–32's Ten Mysterious Gates). Fazang is not yet explaining what these mean — he is using them, confident the reader will meet their full explanation later in Gate Ten. For now the claim to hold onto is simply this: the six positions from the ten faiths to buddhahood remain six genuinely distinct positions — nothing is collapsed the way the Sudden Teaching collapses them — and yet, by these principles, reaching any one of the six already means having reached all of them. Cause (the bodhisattva positions) and fruit (buddhahood) are, on this view, not sequential achievements but two names for a single, already-complete reality.

4. Three Lifetimes: Seeing and Hearing, Understanding and Practice, the Ocean of the Fruit

The Perfect Teaching's second account of position describes the same doctrine unfolding not all at once but across three lifetimes — illustrated by Sudhana himself, and by Maitreya's own promise to him.

Chinese Source

二約報明位相者。但有三生。一成見聞位。謂見聞此無盡法門。成金剛種子等。如性起品說。二成解行位。謂兜率天子等。從惡道出已。一生即得離垢三昧前。得十地無生法忍及十眼十耳等境界。廣如小相品說。又如善財始從十信乃至十地。於善友所一生一身上皆悉具足如是普賢諸行位者。亦是此義也。三證果海位。謂如彌勒告善財言。我當來成正覺時汝當見我。如是等。當知此約因果前後分二位故。是故前位但是因。圓果在後位故。說當見我也。

Ru-Yi Translation

The second is clarification of the marks of position by way of retribution across lifetimes: here there are only three lifetimes. The first is accomplishing the position of seeing and hearing — meaning that, upon seeing and hearing this inexhaustible dharma-gate, one forms the vajra seed and the like, as the "Chapter on the Arising of the Nature" explains. The second is accomplishing the position of understanding and practice — meaning, for instance, the son of a Tuṣita god and others who, having emerged from the unfortunate destinies, in a single lifetime attain, just prior to the stainless samādhi, the Ten Grounds' forbearance of the non-arising of dharmas, together with the ten eyes, ten ears, and other such states, as the "Chapter on Lesser Marks" explains at length. And likewise, Sudhana, beginning from the ten faiths and proceeding all the way to the Ten Grounds, becoming, within a single lifetime and a single body, at the place of his good friends, entirely complete in all these practices and positions of Samantabhadra — this too is an instance of this same meaning. The third is the position of realizing the ocean of the fruit — meaning, for instance, when Maitreya tells Sudhana: "when I attain true awakening in time to come, you shall see me" — and so on. One should know that this speaks in terms of cause and fruit divided into two positions, before and after: hence the preceding position is only the cause, while the perfected fruit lies in the position that follows — and this is why it says, "you shall see me."

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Sudhana's pilgrimage through fifty-three teachers, completing every position from the ten faiths to the Ten Grounds within a single lifetime and a single body, is exactly the story this site's own Gaṇḍavyūha translation tells in full — and Maitreya's promise quoted here, "when I attain true awakening in time to come, you shall see me," is Maitreya's own words to Sudhana near the close of that pilgrimage, at the threshold of his tower. Fazang's gloss is precise about what this promise does and does not mean: it divides cause and fruit into two positions across time — Sudhana's present attainment is the cause, Maitreya's own future buddhahood the fruit that lies ahead — rather than collapsing the distinction between them the way the first account, the six mutually-identical positions above, already had. The three-lifetime account and the six-position account are not competing doctrines; they are the same interpenetration described at two different scales, one compressed into simultaneity, the other spread, quite deliberately, across a promise still to be kept.

5. A Boy Who Sees What a Buddha Sees

The third account of position measures a practitioner by only two categories — one's own present portion, and advancement beyond it — and Fazang illustrates it with two contrasting figures: the youth Universal Adornment, whose physical eye already outperforms the three vehicles' entirely, and the bodhisattva who has completed the fulfillment of faith, whose cosmic feats outstrip even that.

Chinese Source

三約行明位即唯有二。謂自分勝進。此門通前諸位解行及以得法分齊處說。如普莊嚴童子等也。其身在於世界性等上處住。當是白淨寶網轉輪王位。得普見肉眼。見十佛剎微塵數世界海等。若三乘肉眼。即不如此故。智論云。肉眼唯見三千世界內事。若見三千世界外者。何用天眼為。故知不同也。又彼能於一念中。化不可說不可說眾生。一時皆至離垢三昧前。餘念念中皆亦如是。其福分感一定光頗璃鏡。照十佛剎微塵數世界等。當知此是前三生中。解行位內之行相也。以約因門示故。

Ru-Yi Translation

The third, clarification of position by way of practice, has only two aspects: one's own portion, and advancement beyond it. This gate applies throughout the preceding positions, wherever understanding-and-practice and the boundary of what is attained are discussed — as with, for instance, the youth Universal Adornment and the like. His body abides in a place above the World of Worlds and the like, in the position of a wheel-turning king of the pure white jeweled net, and he attains the flesh-eye of universal sight, seeing ocean-worlds as numerous as motes of dust in ten buddha-lands. This is unlike the flesh-eye of the three vehicles — for the Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra says: the flesh-eye sees only within the confines of a three-thousand-world system; if it could see beyond a three-thousand-world system, what use would there be for the heavenly eye? Hence one knows this is not the same. He is, moreover, able within a single thought-moment to transform beings beyond all reckoning, bringing them all at once to the very threshold of the stainless samādhi — and so it is again in every successive thought-moment. His store of merit calls forth a fixed, light-filled crystal mirror, illuminating world-oceans as numerous as motes of dust in ten buddha-lands. One should know that this is the mark of practice within the position of understanding-and-practice, among the three lifetimes described above, shown here in terms of the gate of cause.

若約信滿得位已去。所起行用皆遍法界。如經能以一手覆大千界等。手出供具與虛空法界等。一時供養無盡諸佛。作大佛事饒益眾生不可說也。廣如信位經文說。又云。不離一世界。不起一坐處。而能現一切無量身所行等。又於一念中。十方世界一時成佛轉法輪等。乃至廣說。是故當知。與彼三乘分齊全別。何以故。以三乘行位是約信解阿含門中作如是說也。

As for what unfolds once the culminating fulfillment of faith and its resulting position are attained, the practice and function that arise from it pervade the entire dharma-realm — as the sūtra says: able with a single hand to cover the great trichiliocosm and the like, from that hand producing offerings equal in extent to empty space and the dharma-realm, making offerings all at once to inexhaustible buddhas, performing great buddha-deeds and benefiting sentient beings beyond all telling — as explained at length in the sūtra text on the position of faith. It also says: without leaving a single world, without rising from a single seat, one is able to manifest the practice of all one's immeasurable bodies at once; and, in a single thought-moment, buddhahood is attained and the Dharma-wheel turned simultaneously throughout the worlds of the ten directions — and so on, at length. One should therefore know that this is utterly distinct from the boundary set for the three vehicles. Why is this? Because the three vehicles' own stages and positions are spoken of only within the gate of faith-and-understanding based on āgama teaching.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The comparison with the flesh-eye is a nice piece of technical argument in miniature: ordinarily, seeing beyond a single three-thousand-world system requires the superior "heavenly eye" — that is simply what distinguishes the two, according to the Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra. But the youth Universal Adornment sees ocean-worlds as numerous as dust motes with his ordinary flesh-eye, which means the categories themselves have stopped applying in the usual way: on this teaching, physical sight, pushed far enough, does not need to become something else in order to see what only a heavenly eye is supposed to see. This is one recurring shape of the Perfect Teaching's claims throughout this gate — not a new, higher faculty replacing an ordinary one, but an ordinary faculty revealed to have always been unlimited.

6. Real Practice Versus Temporary Display

A sharp question follows: didn't the Final Teaching, back in section 1 of this part, already grant a bodhisattva the eight marks of buddhahood at the very same point, the fulfillment of faith? Fazang distinguishes a temporary display from an irreversible, universal attainment.

Chinese Source

問前終教中不退際上。亦得如是八相等用。與此何別。答彼於此位示成佛時。於後諸位皆不自在。以未得故。但是當位暫起化故。此即不爾。於初位中起此用時。於後諸位並同時起。皆以得故。是實行故。該六位故。問義既不同。何故一種同是信滿勝進分上起此用耶。答為欲方便顯此一乘信滿成佛令易信受故。於彼教先作此說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: earlier, in the Final Teaching, at the point of non-retrogression, the same eight marks and their functions were also said to be attained — how does this differ from the present case? Answer: there, when this position displays the attainment of buddhahood, one is not yet self-mastering with regard to any of the later positions, since they have not yet been attained — it is merely a temporary arising of transformative activity within that one position alone. Here it is not so: when this function arises within the very first position, it arises simultaneously throughout all the later positions as well, since all of them have already been attained together — because this is genuine practice, comprehending all six positions at once. Question: since the meanings differ in this way, why is it that, in both cases alike, this function is said to arise at the culminating advancement of the fulfillment of faith? Answer: this is done in order to show, by skillful means, that the One Vehicle's attainment of buddhahood at the fulfillment of faith is easy to believe and accept — and so that teaching speaks of it in these same terms first.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The distinction is worth holding precisely, since the two cases can look identical from the outside: a bodhisattva at the fulfillment of faith displaying the eight marks of a buddha's life. In the Final Teaching, this is a temporary, local event — that bodhisattva remains unfree with regard to every later position, which he has not actually reached. In the Perfect Teaching, the identical-looking display is evidence that all six positions have already, genuinely, been attained together. Fazang even explains why the Final Teaching borrows this display at all: it is a deliberate rehearsal, staged in more familiar terms, for a claim the Perfect Teaching will make in full.

7. If the First Position Already Holds the Last, Why Speak of Sequence at All?

A chain of four increasingly pointed questions closes the entire sub-topic, pressing on the logical cost of saying that one position holds all positions. Fazang's answer reaches for the Common and Separate Teaching distinction one final time, to show that sequence and simultaneity are not actually in competition.

Chinese Source

問既一位中有一切位。及信滿心即得佛者。何須更說後諸位耶。答說後諸位即是初中之一切也。如初後亦爾。問若初即具後得初。即得後者亦可後亦具初。既不得後應亦不得初耶。答實爾。但以得初無不得後故。是故無有未得後而不得初也。問若爾云何說得諸位階降次第。答以此經中安立諸位有二善巧。一約相就門分位前後。寄同三乘引彼方便是同教也。二約體就法前後相入。圓融自在異彼三乘。是別教也。但以不移門而恒相即不壞即而恒前後。是故二義融通不相違也。問若爾是初門即一切者。何不說信位初心即得。而說滿心等耶。答若自別教。即不依位成。今寄三乘終教位說。以彼教中信滿不退方得入位。今即寄彼得入位處。一時得此一切前後諸位行相。是故不於信初心說。以未得不退未成位相。但是行故。問若爾應言住位成佛。何名信滿。答由信成故。是故是行佛非位佛也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: if all positions are already present within a single position, and buddhahood is already attained at the fulfillment of the mind of faith, then why is there any need to speak further of the later positions at all? Answer: to speak of the later positions is simply to speak of everything already contained within the first — and the first is, in exactly the same way, contained within the later. Question: if the first already comprises the later, so that attaining the first is attaining the later, then the later should equally comprise the first — and so, if one has not yet attained the later, one should equally not yet have attained the first either. Answer: this is indeed so. But because attaining the first entails that there is no failing to attain the later, there is consequently no case of attaining the first without also attaining the later. Question: if that is so, then why does the sūtra still speak of attaining the various positions in a graduated, descending sequence at all? Answer: because this sūtra establishes the positions by way of two skillful devices. The first, in terms of the gate of appearance, divides the positions into a sequence of before and after, provisionally resembling the three vehicles in order to draw them on by this skillful means — this is the Common Teaching. The second, in terms of the gate of essence, has the various dharmas mutually included before and after, perfectly interfused and self-mastering, distinct from the three vehicles — this is the Separate Teaching. But since, without the gates ever shifting from each other, they remain ever mutually identical without destroying the sequence, and remain ever sequential without destroying the identity, the two meanings are thus fused together without contradiction. Question: if that is so, and the first gate already is the whole, then why does the sūtra not say that attainment occurs at the very first thought-moment of the position of faith, but instead speaks of the fulfillment of that mind? Answer: were this spoken purely from the Separate Teaching's own standpoint, it would not depend on position at all. But here it speaks provisionally, in terms of the three-vehicle Final Teaching's own positional scheme, since in that teaching non-retrogression, and hence entry into position, is only attained at the fulfillment of faith. So it is provisionally in terms of that very point of entry into position that the marks of all the preceding and following positions are attained all at once — which is why it is not spoken of at the very first thought-moment of faith, since at that point non-retrogression is not yet attained, and the mark of position is not yet formed; there is only practice. Question: if that is so, then one should simply say that buddhahood is attained upon entering the abode — why call it the fulfillment of faith? Answer: because it is accomplished through faith. This is why it is called a buddha of practice, not a buddha of position.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The final line, "a buddha of practice, not a buddha of position" — 行佛非位佛 — is a carefully chosen label that lets Fazang have both claims at once. Buddhahood attained "by practice" at the fulfillment of faith is genuine and complete, by the Perfect Teaching's own logic of mutual identity; but it is pinned to the specific vocabulary of "fulfillment," borrowed from the Final Teaching's own positional scheme, rather than asserted as a bare, position-independent fact from the very first thought-moment of faith. The whole closing chain of questions works the same way the sequence-versus-simultaneity problem was resolved for consciousness in Part 9 and lineage in Part 12: the Common Teaching preserves the sequence three-vehicle audiences already expect and can be drawn along by, while the Separate Teaching states the deeper truth that the sequence itself was never more than one gate onto a reality that was, from the very outset, already whole.

Part Fourteen Complete

This closes Part Fourteen, and with it the whole sub-topic of stages and positions: the Final Teaching's non-retrogression resolved and its bodhisattva shown already displaying a buddha's marks within the three worthy stages; the Sudden Teaching's total refusal to describe any sequence; the Perfect Teaching's six mutually-identical positions, its three-lifetime account illustrated through Sudhana and Maitreya's promise, its two-aspect account illustrated through the youth Universal Adornment's unlimited flesh-eye; the distinction between a temporary display of buddhahood and genuine, universal attainment; and the closing resolution of sequence and simultaneity through the Common and Separate Teachings.

Part Fifteen turns to the fourth sub-topic: not what stages exist, but how long it takes to pass through them — the three incalculable kalpas, counted five different ways across the Five Teachings, closing with the Perfect Teaching's claim that a single thought-moment and countless kalpas are, in the end, not different at all.
