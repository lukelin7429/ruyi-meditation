Lineage III — The Icchantika Resolved, and the One Vehicle's Own Lineage

Differences in What Is Taught, IV — Spiritual Lineage, Part Three所詮差別・種性差別

Part 11 left the increase-decrease dilemma suspended mid-citation. Here Fazang completes it, turns the case for permanently lineage-less beings back against itself, and then explains something the Final Teaching owes an explanation for: how suchness — supposedly unconditioned and unchanging — can be lineage at all. The sub-topic closes with the Sudden Teaching's refusal of the whole question, the One Vehicle's own lineage as vast as the dharma-realm, and a fivefold summary that mirrors, almost gate for gate, the one Part 9 used to close the storehouse consciousness.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1866, vol. 45, 華嚴一乘教義分齊章 Fazang 法藏, 643–712, Tang Reading notes: Ru-Yi, original

1. The Realm of Beings Is Like Empty Space

The Mañjuśrī Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra citation broken off in Part 11 resumes here, and delivers the image Fazang has been building toward: a realm of beings without a single fixed characteristic, no more countable — and therefore no more subject to increase or decrease — than empty space itself.

Chinese Source · 種性差別・終教（續）

晝夜說法心無暫息。各各度於無量河沙眾生皆入涅槃。而眾生界亦不增減。乃至十方諸佛世界亦復如是。一一諸佛說法教化。各度無量河沙眾生皆入涅槃。於眾生界亦不增不減。何以故。眾生定相不可得故。義言說眾生界猶如虛空。假使無量勝神通之者。各無量劫飛行虛空。求空邊際終不可盡。非以不盡不名遊行。非以遊行令其得際。當知此中道理亦爾。非以當得令其有終。非以無終說有無得。是故諸難無不通也。

Ru-Yi Translation

...teach the Dharma day and night without a moment's rest, each saving beings as immeasurable as the sands of the Ganges and bringing every one of them into nirvāṇa — and yet the realm of sentient beings neither increases nor decreases. And so it is, moreover, throughout the buddha-worlds of the ten directions: each and every buddha, teaching and transforming beings, saves beings as immeasurable as the sands of the Ganges and brings every one of them into nirvāṇa, and yet in the realm of sentient beings there is likewise neither increase nor decrease. Why is this? Because no fixed characteristic of sentient beings can ever be found at all. This is to say that the realm of sentient beings is like empty space. Suppose there were beings of measureless, surpassing spiritual power, each flying through empty space for measureless kalpas in search of its outer limit — they could never, in the end, exhaust it. Yet this does not mean that, because it cannot be exhausted, there is therefore no such thing as their flying; nor does it mean that their flying could ever actually bring them to its limit. One should know that the principle here is exactly the same: it is not that beings attaining buddhahood brings the realm of beings to an end, nor is it that, because it has no end, one must therefore say there is no attaining at all. For this reason, there is no objection whatsoever that cannot be resolved.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The flight-through-empty-space simile does real philosophical work: it separates two claims that the increase-decrease dilemma had wrongly bundled together. "The realm of beings has no findable limit" does not entail "no being ever reaches buddhahood," any more than "space has no findable edge" entails "nothing can fly through it." And "beings keep attaining buddhahood" does not entail "the realm of beings is shrinking toward zero," any more than a flight through space brings its far edge any closer. Once the realm of sentient beings is understood to have no fixed characteristic at all — not even the characteristic of being a determinate, countable set — the entire dilemma posed in Part 11 dissolves rather than resolves: there was never a stock of beings there to increase or decrease in the first place.

2. The Case Against "No-Lineage" Beings, Turned Back on Itself

Fazang now shows that positing a permanently lineage-less reservoir of beings — meant to guarantee the buddhas' compassionate work never runs out — actually produces the very failure it was designed to prevent, and worse.

Chinese Source

又為成諸佛利他功德無斷盡故。立一分無性眾生者。是即令彼諸佛但有變化利他功德。亦即斷彼隨他受用諸功德也。以無菩薩證諸地故。又化他中。亦但有麁斷滅彼細。以無一人得二乘無漏故。又今已後諸佛。無有一佛得說三乘等教。以無得聖機故。即斷諸佛同體大悲也。又若定意謂悉有性故必皆有盡。恐最後佛闕利他行故。立一分定無性者。然彼後佛終於利他行不圓滿。以其所化無一有情得聖果故。但佛菩薩二利之中利他為最。何有不令一人得聖果而於自身得成佛耶。又本皆發弘誓願云。令諸眾生悉得菩提。是故令得故本願不虛。而眾生界不可盡故本願不斷。若不爾者違本願故。行願虛故。虛行成佛不應理故。是故雖欲避上諸失建立無性。不謂彼過還墮此宗。是故無性非為究竟了義也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again: to posit a portion of beings without lineage, for the sake of preserving the buddhas' meritorious work of benefiting others as inexhaustible, in fact reduces those buddhas to possessing only the meritorious work of benefit performed through transformation-bodies, and thereby cuts off their meritorious activity of enjoyment for others' sake as well — since, on this view, no bodhisattva would ever realize the grounds at all. And within the transformation of others it would likewise leave only the coarse elimination of affliction, without the subtle, since not a single being would ever attain the outflow-free realization of the two vehicles. Moreover, from that point on, not a single buddha could ever again teach the three-vehicle teachings and the rest, there being no capable being of holy potential left to receive them — and so the buddhas' great compassion, one in substance with all beings, would itself be cut off. Furthermore, if it is held with certainty that, because all beings possess the nature, all must eventually be exhausted, and one fears that the last buddha would then lack anyone to benefit, and so posits a portion of beings decisively without the nature to prevent this — even so, that final buddha's own practice of benefiting others would still, in the end, remain incomplete, since among those he transforms not a single being would ever attain the sagely fruit. But of the two benefits a buddha and a bodhisattva bring about, benefiting others is the foremost — how could there be a buddha who brings not even one being to the sagely fruit, and yet attains buddhahood himself? Moreover, all buddhas originally raise the great vow, saying: "I will cause all sentient beings, without exception, to attain bodhi." Because beings are thereby caused to attain it, the original vow is not rendered empty; and because the realm of sentient beings can never be exhausted, the original vow is never cut off either. Were it otherwise, the original vow would be violated, the vow and its practice rendered empty, and an empty practice culminating in buddhahood would be unreasonable. Therefore, although one wishes, by establishing "no-lineage," to avoid the faults stated above, one fails to notice that those very faults fall right back upon this position instead. This is why "no-lineage" is not a doctrine of ultimate, definitive meaning.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The argument is a full reductio, and its force depends on ranking two kinds of buddha-activity: work performed through a transformation-body (化身), visible and provisional, versus the deeper "enjoyment for others' sake" that only becomes possible once bodhisattvas actually realize the Ten Grounds and beings attain genuine, outflow-free realization. Positing permanently lineage-less beings, Fazang argues, guarantees that this deeper work can never fully occur — not once, anywhere, for any buddha — because some beings are fixed by definition to never receive it. Worse, it guarantees that the very last buddha to appear, whenever that might be, will have failed at the one thing that makes a buddha rather than a lesser attainment: benefiting every single being he encounters. A doctrine adopted to protect the buddhas' inexhaustible compassion turns out to make total compassion permanently and provably impossible. This, and not any scriptural citation, is Fazang's final word against no-lineage as ultimate teaching.

3. How Can Suchness Itself Be Lineage?

A different kind of objection follows: lineage sounds like a conditioned fact about a particular being, so how can the Final Teaching identify it with suchness, which is supposed to be unconditioned? Fazang's answer returns to the storehouse consciousness discussed in Part 9, and to the concept of original enlightenment as an inward force turning beings back against the current of saṃsāra.

Chinese Source

問夫論種性必是有為。如何此教約真如為種性耶。答以真如隨緣與染和合成本識時。即彼真中有本覺無漏內熏眾生為返流因。得為有種性。梁攝論說為黎耶中解性。起信論中。說黎耶二義中本覺是也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: lineage, whenever it is discussed at all, must surely be a conditioned thing — how then can this teaching take suchness itself as lineage? Answer: because, when suchness, following conditions, combines with defilement to form the root consciousness, within that very suchness there is the outflow-free original enlightenment, which perfumes sentient beings from within and serves as the cause of their turning back against the stream of saṃsāra — and it is this that constitutes the lineage that a being possesses. The Mahāyānasaṃgraha calls this the capacity for understanding within the ālaya; in the Awakening of Faith it is spoken of as the original enlightenment among the two meanings of the ālaya.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

返流, "turning back against the stream," names the reversal of the ordinary current of saṃsāra — birth, craving, further birth — back toward liberation, and Fazang locates its cause inside the very consciousness that Part 9 showed the Final Teaching reading as suchness genuinely combined with arising-and-ceasing. Lineage, on this account, is not a separate conditioned fact layered onto suchness; it is simply what original enlightenment, already present within the storehouse consciousness, does when it perfumes a being from within. The Awakening of Faith's own two aspects of the ālaya — the arising-and-ceasing aspect and the aspect of original enlightenment — are here explicitly identified with lineage's answer to the objection: suchness is unconditioned in itself, but its activity of self-perfuming, once combined with a particular being's defiled consciousness, is what appears, from within saṃsāra, as that being's own lineage.

4. Coarse and Subtle: The Same Word, Read Two Depths

Fazang draws the same Initial-versus-Final contrast that closed Part 9's account of consciousness, now applied to the technical term "lineage-by-nature" as it appears in the Yogācārabhūmi and in the Ratnagotravibhāga.

Chinese Source

又彼論中如來藏具足無漏。常熏眾生為淨法因。又寶性論云。及彼真如性者。彼本云如六根聚經說。六根如是從無始來。究竟諸法為體故。解云。以真如通一切法。今揀去非情故。故約六處眾生數中。取彼畢竟真如理。以為性種性也。此與瑜伽所說名同。但彼約始教。以理從事麁相而說故。約事中明種性故也。地持云。種性麁相我已略說。此之謂也。寶性論中。約此終教。以事從理深細而說故。就真如明性種性。是故佛性論云。自性清淨心名為道諦。又涅槃經云。佛性者名第一義空。第一義空名為智慧。此等並就本覺性智說為性種。其習種亦從真如所成故。攝論云。多聞熏習。從最清淨法界所流等。又起信論中。以真如體相二大為內熏因。真如用大為外熏緣。以與無明染法合故。是故三大內外說熏。以熏力故無明盡時冥合不二。唯一真如也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Again, in that same treatise, the tathāgatagarbha, complete with the outflow-free, constantly perfumes sentient beings, serving as the cause of pure dharmas. And the Ratnagotravibhāga says: "and that nature of suchness" — its root text says, as the Ṣaḍāyatana Sūtra states, the six sense-faculties, being thus since beginningless time, form the ultimate essence of all dharmas. Explanation: since suchness pervades all dharmas, but here what is non-sentient is screened out, so — restricted to the six sense-bases as they occur among sentient beings — the ultimate principle of suchness found there is taken as the lineage-by-nature. This shares its name with what the Yogācārabhūmi states, but that treatise speaks according to the Initial Teaching, deriving principle from the coarse mark of phenomena, and so clarifies lineage in terms of phenomena. As the Bodhisattvabhūmi says: "I have already briefly explained the coarse mark of lineage" — this is what is meant. The Ratnagotravibhāga, by contrast, speaks according to this Final Teaching, deriving phenomena from principle in its deep and subtle aspect, and so clarifies the lineage-by-nature in terms of suchness itself. Hence the Buddha-Nature Treatise says: the mind pure in its own nature is called the truth of the path. And the Nirvāṇa Sūtra says: buddha-nature is called emptiness in the ultimate sense, and emptiness in the ultimate sense is called wisdom. All of this speaks of the lineage-by-nature in terms of the wisdom-nature of original enlightenment. As for the lineage-by-habituation, this too is formed from suchness — as the Mahāyānasaṃgraha says: the perfuming of extensive Dharma-hearing flows forth from the utterly pure dharma-realm, and so on. And in the Awakening of Faith, the two great aspects of suchness's own essence and characteristics serve as the cause of internal perfuming, while the great aspect of suchness's function serves as the condition of external perfuming; since these combine with the defiled dharma of ignorance, the three greatnesses are therefore said to perfume both from within and from without — and by the power of this perfuming, once ignorance is exhausted, essence and phenomena merge together indistinguishably, without duality, as suchness alone.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The same "one citation, two readings" method from Part 9 reappears here, applied to the exact same technical phrase, 性種性, "lineage-by-nature." The Yogācārabhūmi and the Ratnagotravibhāga use the identical name, but Fazang shows they mean it at two different depths: the Yogācārabhūmi, speaking for the Initial Teaching, locates the innate lineage in the six sense-bases considered as phenomena — a "coarse mark," in its own words — while the Ratnagotravibhāga, speaking for the Final Teaching, locates the identical lineage in the ultimate principle of suchness underlying those same sense-bases, a "deep and subtle" reading of the very same ground. Both statements are true; they simply describe the same lineage from two different depths of analysis, exactly as Part 9 showed the ālayavijñāna itself read two ways from the identical Abhidharma-sūtra verse.

5. The Sudden Teaching: Lineage Beyond Words

Where the Final Teaching still names lineage, however subtly, the Sudden Teaching refuses the naming itself — lineage, like everything else in this teaching, collapses into a single undifferentiated mark beyond language.

Chinese Source

三約頓教明者。唯一真如離言說相名為種性。而亦不分性習之異。以一切法由無二相故。是故諸法無行經云。云何是事名為種性。文殊師利。一切眾生皆是一相。畢竟不生離諸名字。一異不可得故。是名種性。以此準之。上來約三乘說竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarified according to the Sudden Teaching: it is simply the one suchness, apart from every mark of verbal expression, that is named lineage — and here, too, no distinction is drawn between the lineage-by-nature and the lineage-by-habituation, since all dharmas are, by their very nature, without any mark of duality. Hence the Sūtra on the Non-Activity of All Dharmas says: "What is it that is called lineage? Mañjuśrī: all sentient beings are of a single mark, ultimately unarisen, apart from every name and word — since one and different can never be found there, this is called lineage." This may be taken as the standard. The above completes the account given in terms of the three vehicles.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Fazang closes the phrase "上來約三乘說竟" — "the above completes the account given in terms of the three vehicles" — marking a real structural boundary: Hīnayāna, Initial, Final, and Sudden Teaching are all, in his classification, varieties of the three vehicles' own doctrine, however much they differ among themselves. What follows next belongs to a different register entirely — the One Vehicle's own account, which does not merely add a fifth item to the same list.

6. The One Vehicle: Lineage as Vast as the Dharma-Realm

The One Vehicle speaks of lineage in two registers at once: the Common Teaching, which simply gathers up everything the three vehicles already said, and the Separate Teaching, which makes the boldest claim in the whole sub-topic — that a bodhisattva's lineage is already, from the very outset, as vast and complete as the dharma-realm itself.

Chinese Source

第三約一乘有二說。一攝前諸教所明種性。並皆具足主伴成宗。以同教故攝方便故。二據別教種性甚深因果無二。通依及正盡三世間。該收一切理事解行等諸法門。本來滿足已成就訖。故大經云。菩薩種性甚深廣大。與法界虛空等。此之謂也。若隨門顯現。即五位之中位位內六決定義等。名為種性。亦即此法名為果相。以因果同體唯一性故。廣如經說。餘可準知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, according to the One Vehicle, there are two accounts. The first takes up and includes all the lineages set out in the preceding teachings, each fully complete with host and retinue, forming its own school — this being the Common Teaching, since it gathers those teachings in as its own skillful means. The second, based on the Separate Teaching, holds that lineage is profound in itself, cause and fruit not two, pervading both the dependent and the proper realms throughout the three kinds of world, comprehending within itself every gate of principle, phenomena, understanding, and practice — originally complete, and already accomplished from the very outset. Hence the great sūtra says: "The bodhisattva's lineage is profound and vast, equal to the dharma-realm and to empty space" — this is what is meant. When it manifests according to a particular gate, then, within each of the five stages, the six decisive meanings and the like are what is called lineage — and this very dharma is likewise called the fruit-mark, since cause and fruit share one and the same substance, being of a single nature. This is explained at length in the sūtra; the rest may be understood on this model.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

"The great sūtra" is the Avataṃsaka, and the claim it is cited for — lineage "equal to the dharma-realm and to empty space" — is the same rhetorical register this site's own Gaṇḍavyūha translation carries throughout Sudhana's pilgrimage, where each of the fifty-three teachers reveals a liberation already as boundless as the dharma-realm itself, never a partial attainment awaiting completion. Fazang's phrase "cause and fruit not two" makes the same point in more technical language: on the Separate Teaching's own view, a bodhisattva's lineage is not a seed that will, someday, ripen into the fruit of buddhahood — the fruit is already latent, complete, within the lineage from the very first moment it can be spoken of at all. This is the most extreme statement of lineage in the entire sub-topic, and it sets up the doctrine Part 14 will develop at length: that any one position on the bodhisattva path already contains every other position, buddhahood included.

7. One Lineage, Five Gates — The Sub-Topic Closed

Fazang ends the lineage sub-topic exactly as he ended the consciousness sub-topic in Part 9: with the same fivefold analytic, now applied to lineage instead of the storehouse consciousness, confirming that this is a general method rather than a one-off device.

Chinese Source

問云何種性。約諸教差別不同耶。答此亦有二義。一約法辨隱顯相收。二約機明得法分齊。初義者。由此種性緣起無礙具五義門。是故諸教各述一門隨機攝化。義不相違。何者為五。一是隨執非有門。如小乘說。二隨事虧盈門。如始教說。三隨理遍情門。如終教說。四絕相離言門。如頓教說。五性備眾德門。如圓教說。義雖有五。然種性圓通隨攝遍收。隱顯齊致也。二明得法分齊者。或一切皆無。唯除佛一人。如小乘說。或一切皆有。唯除草木等。如終教說。或亦有亦無。如始教說。以許一分無性故。或非有非無。如頓教說。以離相故。或具前四。如一乘方便處說。或即因具果通三世間。如圓教說。餘可準知。◎

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: why do the accounts of lineage differ across the various teachings? Answer: here too there are two meanings. The first is comprehension in terms of the dharma, distinguishing what is hidden from what is manifest, each gathering the other in; the second is the boundary of what is attained according to the capacity of those being taught. As to the first meaning: because this dependently-arisen, unobstructed lineage possesses five gates of meaning, each teaching expounds one gate according to its audience's capacity in order to gather beings in and transform them, without contradiction in the underlying meaning. What are the five? First, the gate of mere attachment, in which lineage does not exist at all — as the Hīnayāna states it. Second, the gate of following phenomena, deficient in some and complete in others — as the Initial Teaching states it. Third, the gate of following principle, which pervades all sentient feeling equally — as the Final Teaching states it. Fourth, the gate that cuts off every mark, apart from words — as the Sudden Teaching states it. Fifth, the gate of the nature complete with every virtue — as the Perfect Teaching states it. Although the meaning is fivefold, lineage itself is perfectly interfused, gathering all five in without remainder — hidden and manifest reaching the very same end. As to the second meaning, clarifying the boundary of what is attained according to capacity: in some cases, all beings are altogether without it, the Buddha alone excepted — as the Hīnayāna states. In some cases, all beings altogether possess it, only plants and the like excepted — as the Final Teaching states. In some cases, it is both possessed and not possessed — as the Initial Teaching states, since it allows that one portion is without nature. In some cases, it is neither possessed nor not possessed — as the Sudden Teaching states, since it is apart from all marks. In some cases, a single teaching comprises all four of the above — as is stated in the One Vehicle's own gate of skillful means. Or again, cause already comprises fruit, pervading the three kinds of world — as the Perfect Teaching states. The rest may be understood on this model.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

This is the same fivefold instrument Part 9 used to close the storehouse consciousness — comprehension by the dharma itself, and by the capacity of the audience — confirming that Fazang treats it as a repeatable analytic for closing out each of Gate Nine's ten sub-topics, not a device invented once for consciousness alone. Watch for its structure again as later parts of this gate reach their own closing summaries.

Notice, too, how the second half's scale of "who possesses lineage" has widened even further than Part 9's parallel scale for consciousness. There, the Hīnayāna held only the bare name; here, its exclusion is total but for the Buddha. The Final Teaching excludes only the non-sentient; the Initial Teaching sits in the middle, both possessing and lacking; the Sudden Teaching escapes the entire framework of possession; and the Perfect Teaching, in its final formulation, folds cause into fruit so completely that the very question of "who has lineage" gives way to a claim about how the three kinds of world are structured. The scale, across five teachings, moves from near-total exclusion to a claim that dissolves the category of exclusion altogether — which is exactly the trajectory the icchantika debate itself traced across these three parts.

Part Twelve Complete

This closes Part Twelve, and with it the whole sub-topic of lineage: the realm of sentient beings shown to be like empty space, neither increasing nor decreasing; the case for permanently lineage-less beings shown to guarantee, rather than prevent, a buddha's compassionate work remaining forever incomplete; suchness argued to serve as lineage through the outflow-free original enlightenment that perfumes beings from within; the Yogācārabhūmi and the Ratnagotravibhāga shown to name the identical lineage at two different depths; the Sudden Teaching's lineage beyond words and names; the One Vehicle's own lineage, vast as the dharma-realm and already containing its own fruit; and the closing fivefold summary that mirrors Part 9's.

Part Thirteen turns to the third sub-topic of Gate Nine: not who can attain enlightenment, but what stages a practitioner passes through on the way there — beginning with the Hīnayāna's four positions and the Initial Teaching's carefully calibrated stages for śrāvakas returning to the Mahāyāna.
