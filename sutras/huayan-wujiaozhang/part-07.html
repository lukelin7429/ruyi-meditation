Ten Kinds of Disciple, Ten Ways of Hearing the Sequence

Discerning the Intent Behind the Teachings' Sequence決擇前後意

Part 6 left a puzzle sitting in plain sight: the root teaching and the branch teachings were, on Fazang's own account, delivered at the same moment, yet scripture after scripture describes the Buddha's teaching career unfolding in flatly incompatible sequences — three weeks of silence here, twelve years there, not a single word ever spoken according to another source entirely. Fazang resolves this not by picking a winner among the timelines, but by relocating the disagreement: it was never really about when the Buddha taught. It is about what each of ten different kinds of listener, according to their own capacity, was able to hear at all.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1866, vol. 45, 華嚴一乘教義分齊章 Author: Fazang 法藏, 643–712, Tang Reading notes: Ru-Yi, original

The Text

1. Fixed in the Lesser, or Advancing Through Emptiness to Suchness 一・二・三

Chinese Source

第七決擇前後意者。然諸教前後差別難知。略以十門分別其意。一或有眾生。於此世中小乘根性始終定者。即見如來從初得道乃至涅槃唯說小乘。未曾見轉大乘法輪。如小乘諸部執不信大乘者是。二或有眾生。於此世中小乘根不定故。堪進入大乘初教。即便定者。即見如來初時轉於小乘法輪翻諸外道。後時見轉大乘初教即空法輪迴諸小乘。如中論初說者是。三或有眾生。於此世中於小乘及初教根不定故堪入終教即便定者。即初時見轉小乘法輪。中時見轉空教法輪。後時見轉不空法輪。如解深密經等說者是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Seventh, discerning the intent behind the teachings' sequence: the differences among the teachings' sequence are, in truth, difficult to fathom. Here, in brief, ten gates are used to distinguish their intent.

One, there are sentient beings whose Hīnayāna disposition, in this world, is fixed from beginning to end — such beings see the Tathāgata, from his first attainment of the Way all the way to his final nirvāṇa, teaching only the Lesser Vehicle, and never once see him turn the wheel of the Great Vehicle's Dharma. This is as with the Hīnayāna schools that hold fast to their own tenet and do not believe in the Great Vehicle.

Two, there are sentient beings whose Hīnayāna faculties, in this world, are not fixed, so that they are capable of advancing into the Initial Teaching of the Great Vehicle, and there become fixed — such beings see the Tathāgata, in the first period, turn the wheel of the Lesser Vehicle's Dharma to overturn the various non-Buddhist schools, and, in a later period, see him turn the wheel of the Initial Teaching of the Great Vehicle, the wheel of emptiness itself, to turn the Lesser Vehicle's followers back toward it. This is as at the opening of the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā.

Three, there are sentient beings whose faculties, in this world, are not fixed within either the Lesser Vehicle or the Initial Teaching, so that they are capable of entering the Final Teaching, and there become fixed — such beings see, in the first period, the wheel of the Lesser Vehicle's Dharma turned; in the middle period, the wheel of the teaching of emptiness turned; and, in a later period, the wheel of the non-empty teaching turned. This is as the Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra and the like teach.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Notice what these first three kinds of disciple have in common: each perceives exactly as much of Xuanzang's three Dharma-wheels from Part 4 as their own final resting-point requires, and not one wheel more. A disciple permanently fixed in the Lesser Vehicle literally cannot perceive a Great Vehicle ever being taught — not because the Buddha withheld it, but because that disciple's own capacity has no place to register it. A disciple capable of advancing only as far as the Initial Teaching perceives exactly two wheels; one capable of reaching the Final Teaching perceives all three. Sequence, on this account, is not a fact about the Buddha's calendar; it is a shadow cast by the shape of the listener's own eventual ceiling. This is the same insight Bodhiruci's One-Voice Teaching reached for, back in Part 4 — a single voice, understood differently according to each hearer's own capacity — now given far more machinery to work with.

The Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, cited here for its own opening turn from refuting non-Buddhist views into the specifically Mahāyāna teaching of emptiness, is translated in full elsewhere on this site as the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā.

2. Fixed in the Sudden, or Never Hearing a Word at All 四・五

Chinese Source

四或有眾生。於此漸教中。根不定故堪入頓教。即便定者。即見初示言說之教猶非究竟。後顯絕言之教方為究竟。如維摩經中初三十二菩薩及文殊等所說不二並在言說中。後維摩所顯絕言之教。以為究竟者是。五或有眾生。於此世中頓悟機熟即便定者。即見佛從初得道乃至涅槃不說一字。如楞伽說。又涅槃經云。若知如來常不說法。是名菩薩具足多聞等。

Ru-Yi Translation

Four, there are sentient beings whose faculties, within this Gradual Teaching, are not fixed, so that they are capable of entering the Sudden Teaching, and there become fixed — such beings see the teaching first shown through verbal expression as still not final, and only the teaching that afterward reveals what is cut off from words as truly final. This is as in the Vimalakīrti Sūtra, where the thirty-two bodhisattvas at first, and Mañjuśrī as well, all speak of non-duality while still remaining within verbal expression, and only Vimalakīrti's own later revelation, cut off from words, is taken as final.

Five, there are sentient beings whose capacity for sudden awakening, in this world, is already ripe, and who are therefore fixed from the very start — such beings see the Buddha, from his first attainment of the Way all the way to his final nirvāṇa, as not speaking a single word. This is as the Laṅkāvatāra teaches; and the Nirvāṇa Sūtra likewise says: "One who knows that the Tathāgata never at any time teaches the Dharma is said to be a bodhisattva replete with extensive learning," and so on.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The fifth kind of disciple perceives the most radical timeline of all: a Buddha who, across an entire teaching career traditionally reckoned at forty-nine years, never spoke a single word. This is not presented as a rhetorical flourish. It follows directly from the Sudden Teaching's own definition in Part 5 — a teaching identified not by its content but by the total absence of verbal mediation — pushed to its limit: if the deepest teaching truly requires no words, then a listener whose capacity is ripe enough should, in principle, be able to receive the entirety of the Dharma from a Buddha who appears to say nothing at all. The paradox is not resolved here; it is simply named as the most advanced of the ten capacities before Fazang moves on to something higher still.

3. Fixed in the Three, or Turning Toward the One Two Ways 六・七・八

Chinese Source

六或有眾生。於此世中三乘根性定者。見佛從初即說三乘教法。乃至涅槃更無餘說。如上密迹力士經及大品經說者是。七或有眾生。於此世中三乘根不定故。堪進入同教一乘者。即見自所得三乘之法皆依一乘無盡教起。是彼方便阿含施設。是故諸有所修。皆迴向一乘。如會三歸一等。又如上所引三乘與一乘同時說者等。八或有眾生。於此世中三乘根不定故。堪可進入別教一乘者。即知彼三乘等法。本來不異別教一乘。何以故。為彼所目故。更無異事故。如法華經同教說者是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Six, there are sentient beings whose disposition toward the three vehicles, in this world, is fixed — such beings see the Buddha, from the very start, teaching the dharma of the three vehicles, and, all the way to his nirvāṇa, teaching nothing else besides. This is as the Sūtra of the Vajra-Wielding Guardian and the Larger Perfection of Wisdom, cited above, teach.

Seven, there are sentient beings whose faculties toward the three vehicles, in this world, are not fixed, so that they are capable of advancing into the One Vehicle as the Common Teaching — such beings see that the three-vehicle dharma they themselves have attained arises, in every case, in dependence on the inexhaustible teaching of the One Vehicle, being merely an expedient, Āgama-level arrangement made on its behalf; and so whatever they cultivate, they turn and dedicate it all toward the One Vehicle. This is as in "gathering the three back into the one," and the like, and also as in what was cited above of the three vehicles being taught at the same time as the One Vehicle.

Eight, there are sentient beings whose faculties toward the three vehicles, in this world, are not fixed, so that they are capable of advancing into the One Vehicle as its own Separate Teaching — such beings know that the dharma of the three vehicles was, from the very beginning, not different from the One Vehicle as its own Separate Teaching. Why is this? Because the three vehicles were always merely what that Separate Teaching was pointing at, with no separate matter apart from it. This is as the Lotus Sūtra, spoken from within the Common Teaching, teaches.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

Seven and eight are easy to conflate, and Fazang is careful to keep them apart: both kinds of disciple move from the three vehicles toward some version of the One, but by different roads, arriving at different destinations. The seventh disciple travels the Common Teaching's own two-stage road from Part 3 — first genuinely completing three-vehicle practice, only afterward turning and dedicating it toward the One, the way the Lotus Sūtra's own parable unfolds in time. The eighth disciple skips the turning altogether, realizing all at once that the three vehicles were never actually separate from the Separate Teaching to begin with — this is the gate of inclusion from Part 2, "not different, not one," recognized directly rather than arrived at through a sequence of stages. One road runs through time; the other simply sees through it.

4. Samantabhadra's Capacity, and Entry into the Ocean of Fruition 九・十

Chinese Source

九或有眾生。於此世中具有普賢機者。即見如來從初成道乃至涅槃一切佛法普於初時第二七日海印定中。自在演說無盡具足主伴無窮因陀羅網微細境界。本末不見說三乘小乘等法。如華嚴經別教中說者是。此約普賢教分見聞及解行處說。十或有眾生。於一乘別教。解行滿足已證入果海者即見上來諸教並是無盡性海隨緣所成更無異事。是故諸教即是圓明無盡果海具德難思不可說不可說也。此約一乘入證分齊處說。餘可準知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Nine, there are sentient beings who, in this world, are fully endowed with Samantabhadra's own capacity — such beings see that the Tathāgata, from his first accomplishing of the Way all the way to his nirvāṇa, universally expounds the whole of the Buddha-Dharma within that first second seven-day period's ocean-seal absorption, self-masteredly proclaiming the inexhaustible domain of host and retinue fully present, boundless, the subtle and fine reaches of Indra's Net — and, from root to branch, never see him teaching the dharma of the three vehicles or the Lesser Vehicle at all. This is as the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, within its own Separate Teaching, teaches. This is spoken in terms of the place where Samantabhadra's own teaching divides into seeing-and-hearing and understanding-and-practice.

Ten, there are sentient beings who, within the One Vehicle's own Separate Teaching, have already fulfilled understanding and practice and entered into realization of the ocean of fruition — such beings see that all the teachings described above are, every one, formed out of the inexhaustible ocean of nature according to conditions, with no separate matter apart from it at all. This is why all the teachings are simply the perfectly clear, inexhaustible ocean of fruition itself, replete with virtues beyond conception, inexpressible upon inexpressible. This is spoken in terms of the boundary at which one enters realization within the One Vehicle. The rest may be understood by analogy.

Reading note — Ru-Yi

The tenth and final disciple closes a circle opened at the very start of this treatise. Part 1 divided the One Vehicle into an inexpressible "fruition-share of the ocean of nature" and an expressible "cause-share" belonging to Samantabhadra — everything since has been spoken from within that second, expressible share. Here, at last, a disciple appears who has crossed back over: having fulfilled the One Vehicle's own practice, such a person sees every teaching surveyed across these first seven parts — Hīnayāna, Initial, Final, Sudden, Common, Separate, all ten kinds of listener described in this very part — as nothing but the single, inexhaustible ocean of fruition, appearing differently only according to conditions. From this vantage, the entire elaborate apparatus of ranking, classifying, and distinguishing that Fazang has built across seven parts is itself seen as one more provisional device — true and necessary for beings still on the path, and dissolved, without being contradicted, once the path is walked to its own end.

Part Seven Complete

This closes Part Seven: ten kinds of disciple, each perceiving the Buddha's single, simultaneous teaching as a different sequence — fixed in the Lesser Vehicle alone, advancing stage by stage through emptiness to suchness, fixed in wordless sudden realization, turning from the three vehicles toward the one by two different roads, or, at the summit, seeing every teaching dissolve into a single inexhaustible ocean of fruition.

Part Eight turns from why the sequence is perceived differently to what makes the One Vehicle's own teaching different in kind from anything the three vehicles offer — ten distinguishing marks, from the moment and place it was taught to the very phenomena, down to a single grain of dust, through which it is disclosed.
