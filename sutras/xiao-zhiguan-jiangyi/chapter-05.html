Chapter 5 · 方便行第五 · Complete

Skillful Means

The last of the five preparatory chapters, and the shortest: five inward qualities a practitioner must bring to the practice itself, closing the preparatory half of the text before chapter 6 turns to actual practice. Baojing closes the chapter with the same line — "no wisdom without dhyāna, no dhyāna without wisdom" — that has run underneath every chapter so far.

Status: complete Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: book.bfnn.org Covers: the five skillful means — aspiration, diligence, mindfulness, discerning wisdom, singleness of mind

The General Principle 總明方便之要

Chinese Source

〖講〗此科明行方便：方者，法也；便者，宜也。凡大小乘一切法門，概稱方便，即善巧投機，運用適宜之方法。今此之方便，即進行五法之方便也。謂前具五緣，具備辦道之助緣，次訶外五欲，外塵不入，次棄五蓋，內障不起，又善調五事，身心適當，若再能進行五法，則所作所修，一切行門，決能成就。若於此法有虧，亦恐難達其目的，所有一切禪觀，及念佛三昧，亦無由現前矣！言五法方便者，謂欲、進、念、慧、一心、是也。凡修一切行門，務須具此五法為方便，缺一不可。

Ru-Yi Translation

Commentary: This section explains the skillful means to be applied. "Fāng" (方) means a method; "biàn" (便) means fitting or suitable — every dharma-gate of the greater and lesser vehicles alike is generally called a "skillful means," that is, a method applied with skill, matched to the occasion, and suitably employed. The "skillful means" spoken of here refers specifically to the skillful means of carrying forward these five practices.

Having, in what came before, been provided with the five conditions — the supporting conditions needed to take up the path at all — and then having rebuked the five outward desires, so that outward dust cannot enter, and then discarded the five hindrances, so that inward obstruction does not arise, and further having skillfully regulated the five things, so that body and mind are properly settled — if one is further able to carry forward these five practices, then whatever one does, whatever one cultivates, every avenue of practice will certainly succeed. Should there be any deficiency in this, it may well prove hard to reach the goal at all, and no dhyāna-contemplation, nor the samādhi of buddha-recollection, will have any way to manifest! What is meant by "the five skillful means" is: aspiration, diligence, mindfulness, discerning wisdom, and singleness of mind. Anyone cultivating any avenue of practice at all must, without exception, have all five of these skillful means in place — not one of them can be missing.

1. Aspiration 欲

Chinese Source

〖文〗夫修止觀，須具方便法門，有其五法：一者欲。欲離世間一切妄想顛倒，欲得一切諸禪智慧法門故。亦名為志，亦名為願，亦名為好，亦名為樂。是人志願好樂一切諸深法門故，故名為欲。如佛言曰：一切善法，欲為其本。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Now, cultivating calming and insight requires this skillful method, in five parts. The first is aspiration: the wish to leave behind every idle, upside-down thought of the world, and the wish to attain every dharma-gate of dhyāna and wisdom. This is also called resolve, also called vow, also called fondness, also called delight — this person's resolve, vow, fondness, and delight all being directed toward every profound dharma-gate, hence it is called aspiration. As the Buddha said: "Every wholesome dharma has aspiration as its root."

〖講〗一者欲，欲是樂欲，希須為義。凡辦世間一切事，皆應有希慕願望之心，然後得成，出世法亦然。欲離世間一切虛妄想相，顛倒輪迴之厭離，欲得一切諸禪三昧，神通智慧法門之欣求欲，足見此欲，不特為一切惡之本，抑可為一切善之源，無論若善若惡，若離若得，皆以此欲字而為其首。又此欲亦名為志，志者立志於止觀功夫故。亦名為願，發願修止觀功夫故。亦名為好樂，如好樂修止觀功夫。是人指即修行之人，行人若能立志，欲求於一切諸深禪定法門，能發願樂欲，一切諸深智慧法門，有如是等種種因緣，故名為欲。佛言一切善法，欲為其本。欲乃樂欲歡喜之異名，今擬修止觀法門，若有好樂歡喜之心，則決定可以成就。否則不肯前進，斷難圓成。故云：「一切善法，欲為其本。」是知此欲字，為修行人之第一方便，最為切要耳！

Commentary: The first is aspiration: aspiration is a joyful wish, carrying the sense of longing and needing. Accomplishing any undertaking in the world at all requires a heart of longing and aspiration before it can be brought to completion — and transmundane practice is exactly the same. The wish to leave behind every illusory, false appearance of the world, a revulsion from the upside-down wheel of rebirth, and the wish to attain every samādhi of dhyāna, every dharma-gate of spiritual power and wisdom, a joyful longing to seek them out — this is enough to show that this aspiration is not only the root of every evil, but can equally be the source of every good. Whether toward good or evil, whether toward leaving something behind or attaining something, this single word "aspiration" stands at the very head of it all.

This aspiration is also called resolve, since one sets one's resolve on the very effort of calming and insight; also called vow, since one gives rise to the vow to cultivate the effort of calming and insight; also called fondness or delight, as in taking fond delight in cultivating the effort of calming and insight. "This person" refers precisely to the practitioner: if a practitioner is able to set their resolve, wishing to seek out every profound dharma-gate of dhyāna-concentration, able to give rise to the vow and joyful longing for every profound dharma-gate of wisdom — through causes and conditions of this kind, it is called aspiration.

The Buddha said that every wholesome dharma has aspiration as its root. "Aspiration" is another name for joyful wishing and delight; now, in setting out to cultivate the dharma-gate of calming and insight, if one has a heart of fondness and delight in it, one will certainly be able to accomplish it — otherwise, one is unwilling to press forward, and completing it becomes decisively difficult. Hence it is said: "Every wholesome dharma has aspiration as its root." From this we know that this word "aspiration" is the very first skillful means for a practitioner, and matters more than almost anything else!

2. Diligence 進

Chinese Source

〖文〗二者精進。堅持禁戒，棄於五蓋，初夜後夜，專精不廢，譬如鑽火未熱，終不休息，是名精進善道法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The second is diligence: holding firmly to the precepts, discarding the five hindrances, applying oneself single-mindedly without pause through the first and last watches of the night alike — just as one drilling for fire never rests until heat is finally produced. This is called diligence in the wholesome path.

〖講〗此第二行精進之方便。精者不雜，進者不退。行人修持時，專心一意，不雜不亂，不休不息，精進勇猛做功夫。凡修何種功夫，貴在唯勤，若不精一無雜，今日念佛，明日聽經，有時參禪，有時持咒，如是三心二意，時時變遷，雖終日用功，而到老一事無成，豈不大可悲憫者乎？推其過咎，即在不精不進之故也，故行人欲了生死成佛道，須精進勇猛不可。精進者何？謂堅固持禁戒，棄於五蓋六欲，內外清淨，如是身心內外精進，於初夜、後夜、中夜，當從當認，精進修持止觀，惺惺寂寂，寂寂惺惺；乃至晝精進，夜精進，晝夜六時恆精進；譬如鑽木求火一般，若火未出時，則終不休息。是則名為精進勇猛，善行於止觀之道法。

Commentary: This second is the skillful means of diligence. "Diligent" (精) means undiluted; "advancing" (進) means never retreating. While a practitioner is engaged in practice, the mind should be single and undivided, neither mixed nor confused, never resting, applying courageous, vigorous effort. Whatever practice one cultivates, what matters most is unbroken diligence alone — if one is not single-minded and undivided, reciting the Buddha's name today, listening to a sūtra tomorrow, sometimes sitting in Chan meditation, sometimes reciting a mantra, shifting like this from one intention to another all the time — even applying effort the whole day through, one arrives at old age having accomplished nothing at all. Is this not deeply lamentable? Tracing the fault to its source, it lies precisely in a lack of this undiluted, unbroken diligence — and so a practitioner who wishes to resolve birth-and-death and accomplish the buddha-path has no choice but to apply courageous, vigorous diligence.

What, then, is diligence? It means holding firmly to the precepts, discarding the five hindrances and six desires, pure both inwardly and outwardly — body and mind, inward and outward alike, applying diligence through the first watch, the last watch, and the middle watch of the night, taking each one up in turn, diligently cultivating and upholding calming and insight, alert yet still, still yet alert — even applying diligence by day and by night, constantly diligent through all six periods of the day and night alike; just as one drilling wood to produce fire never rests until the fire has actually appeared. This is what is called courageous, vigorous diligence, skillfully practicing the path of calming and insight.

3. Mindfulness 念

Chinese Source

〖文〗三者念。念世間為欺誑可賤，念禪定為尊重可貴，若得禪定，即能具足，發諸無漏智，一切神通道力，成等正覺，廣度眾生，是為可貴；故名為念。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The third is mindfulness: bearing in mind that the world is deceitful and base, and bearing in mind that dhyāna-concentration is to be honored and treasured. If one attains dhyāna-concentration, one is thereby able to bring forth every outflow-free wisdom in full, every spiritual power and strength of the path, accomplishing perfect, complete awakening and broadly bringing sentient beings across — this is precisely what is to be treasured; hence it is called mindfulness.

〖講〗念者憶念也，即正念昭彰之義，謂念世間五欲之法，虛妄不實，乃欺騙於人，誑惑於人，實為可賤可惡；念出世禪定智慧，勝妙殊絕，為可尊可貴之法，若能得諸禪定，即能具發無漏智，及一切神通道力，成等正覺。無漏智即無生智，無生即無滅，即是不生不滅之無漏智慧。神通即六神通，道謂七科道品，力即如來十種智力。成等正覺，即是成無上佛道。言正覺者，謂凡夫妄覺，外道邪覺，二乘偏覺，菩薩二邊覺，及但中覺，悉未能稱為真等正覺，唯獨如來方稱為真等正覺，豎窮三際之底，橫賅法界之邊，一切無與相等，而能等一切法，故名真等。如來三智圓明，五眼洞照，故名正覺。證至極妙覺之後，從此興無緣慈，運同體悲，所以垂形六道，廣度眾生。常正思念，此等殊勝妙好之事，最為可尊貴，故名為念。

Commentary: "Mindfulness" means bearing in mind — that is, the sense of correct mindfulness shining forth clearly. It means bearing in mind that the world's own dharma of the five desires is illusory and unreal, deceiving people and misleading them, in truth base and detestable; and bearing in mind that transmundane dhyāna-concentration and wisdom, excellent and wondrous beyond compare, are precisely what is to be honored and treasured. If one is able to attain dhyāna-concentration, one is thereby able to bring forth every outflow-free wisdom in full, together with every spiritual power and strength of the path, accomplishing perfect, complete awakening.

"Outflow-free wisdom" is precisely the wisdom of the unborn — the unborn being precisely the unceasing, that is, the outflow-free wisdom of what is neither born nor ceases. "Spiritual power" refers to the six supernormal powers; "the path" refers to the seven categories of factors of the path; "strength" refers to the Tathāgata's own ten powers of wisdom. "Accomplishing perfect, complete awakening" is precisely accomplishing the unsurpassed buddha-path.

As for "correct" awakening: the ordinary being's false awakening, the non-Buddhist path's deviant awakening, the two lesser vehicles' partial awakening, the bodhisattva's awakening still caught between two extremes, and even a merely middling awakening — none of these can properly be called true, perfect, correct awakening. Only the Tathāgata's own awakening deserves this name: reaching, vertically, to the very depth of the three time periods, and encompassing, horizontally, the entire boundary of the Dharma-realm, equaled by nothing at all, yet able itself to equal every dharma — hence it is called "true" and "equal." The Tathāgata's own three kinds of wisdom shine perfectly complete, the five eyes penetrating and illuminating everything — hence it is called "correct awakening."

Having realized the very summit of wondrous awakening, one then gives rise to compassion with no object at all, moving with a sense of shared identity with all beings, and so manifests a body throughout the six destinies, broadly bringing sentient beings across. Constantly, correctly bearing in mind matters of this excellent, wondrous kind — this is what is most to be honored and treasured; hence it is called mindfulness.

4. Discerning Wisdom 巧慧

Chinese Source

〖文〗四者巧慧。籌量世間樂，禪定智慧樂，得失輕重。所以者何？世間之樂，樂少苦多，虛誑不實，是失是輕；禪定智慧之樂，無漏無為，寂然閒曠，永離生死，與苦長別，是得是重。如是分別，故名巧慧。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The fourth is discerning wisdom: weighing the pleasure of the world against the pleasure of dhyāna-concentration and wisdom, and their relative gain and loss, weight and lightness. Why is this? The pleasure of the world holds little pleasure and much suffering, illusory and unreal — this is loss, this is the lighter side. The pleasure of dhyāna-concentration and wisdom is outflow-free and unconditioned, still and boundlessly at ease, permanently leaving birth-and-death behind, parting with suffering for good — this is gain, this is the weightier side. Discerning things in this way is what is called discerning wisdom.

〖講〗此第四明行巧慧之方便。凡用功之人，須以善巧之智慧，籌量世間之快樂，及出世間禪定智慧之快樂，孰得孰失，誰輕誰重，須明了清楚，辨別無滯，如是方能出生厭下欣上之心，否則真偽莫辨，邪正不分，易於錯入歧途，故須以巧慧觀察。世間之樂，乃有為之樂，無常不實，縱使得樂，此樂亦是苦，樂不常久，終歸敗壞故，敗壞即是壞苦，所以云樂少苦多，虛誑不實。樂此是喪法身失慧命之法，是可輕賤之法。出世間禪定智慧之樂，乃是無漏無為之法，無漏謂不漏於生死，無為即不作於有為，湛湛然，寂寂然，清閒逸曠於寂滅性中，永離於生死樊籠，長別三界苦輪，此等為最可尊貴最可珍重之法。若能如是分明辨別清楚，故名巧慧。

Commentary: This fourth explains the skillful means of discerning wisdom. Anyone applying effort in practice must use skillful wisdom to weigh worldly pleasure against the pleasure of transmundane dhyāna-concentration and wisdom — which is gain and which is loss, which is weightier and which lighter — understanding this clearly, discerning it without any hesitation. Only in this way can a heart arise that turns away from the lower and delights in the higher; otherwise, unable to distinguish true from false, unable to tell right from wrong, one easily strays onto a mistaken path — hence one must observe this through discerning wisdom.

Worldly pleasure is a conditioned pleasure, impermanent and unreal — even should one obtain it, this pleasure is itself suffering, since pleasure never lasts, and in the end always comes to ruin; and ruin is itself the suffering of decay. Hence it is said that it holds little pleasure and much suffering, illusory and unreal. Taking delight in this is a dharma that destroys the Dharma-body and the life of wisdom — a dharma to be held lightly and disdained. The pleasure of transmundane dhyāna-concentration and wisdom, by contrast, is an outflow-free, unconditioned dharma — "outflow-free" meaning it does not leak into birth-and-death, "unconditioned" meaning it engages in no conditioned activity — deep and still, tranquil, at ease and unbounded within the nature of cessation itself, permanently leaving behind the cage of birth-and-death, parting for good with the wheel of suffering across the three realms. This is the dharma most to be honored, most to be treasured. Being able to discern all of this clearly in just this way is what is called discerning wisdom.

5. Singleness of Mind 一心

Chinese Source

〖文〗五者一心分明。明見世間可患可惡，善識定慧功德可尊可貴，爾時應當一心決定，修行止觀。如金剛，天魔外道不能沮壞，設使空無所獲，終不回易，是名一心。譬如人行，先須知道通塞之相，然後決定一心涉路而進，故說巧慧一心。經云：非智不禪，非禪不智，義在此也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The fifth is clarity of singleness of mind: clearly seeing that the world is to be feared and detested, and skillfully recognizing that the merit of concentration and wisdom is to be honored and treasured — at such a time one should, with singleness of mind, resolve decisively to cultivate and practice calming and insight, firm as vajra, unable to be shaken or ruined by any heavenly demon or non-Buddhist path, so that even should one gain nothing at all in the end, one still never turns back or changes course — this is called singleness of mind. It is like a traveler who must first know which roads are open and which are blocked, and only then resolves, with singleness of mind, to set out and press forward along the road — hence it is spoken of as "discerning wisdom" together with "singleness of mind." A sūtra says: "No dhyāna without wisdom, no wisdom without dhyāna" — the meaning lies precisely in this.

〖講〗上既以巧慧分明辨別世間上一切有為之事，悉是可患可惡，並能認識出世間禪定智慧，是可貴可尊之法，爾時即須一心一意修止觀。此一心乃是決定之一心，非是入定之一心，即專心不雜，一門深入做功夫之心也。金剛，最堅最利，能壞一切物，而不被一切物所壞。言修止觀之心，猶如金剛，而不被他人所誘，亦不為魔外所壞，縱使空無所獲，終不回易。喻如參禪，於中決定欲得個好消息。譬如念佛，務使至一心不亂，不可半途而廢，無有三心二意，一門心思，無有餘念，故名一心。譬如行人，先須識知道路通塞之相，如生死道是塞，即不可行，涅槃路是通，即可行，然後決定一心，涉路而進。所謂不起思量分別，驀直做去，最為要緊，切不可回頭轉腦，改途移轍，是故說為巧慧一心。經云：「非智不禪，非禪不智。」即止觀不二也。智即是慧，禪即是定，先明決定心，定心即是智慧。縱一門深入，入一無妄，即是定。若無智慧，不能修禪定，若無禪定，不能生智慧，彼此互相由藉，互相顯發。故云：「非智不禪，非禪不智。」意在此耳。

Commentary: Having, through discerning wisdom, already clearly distinguished that every conditioned matter in the world is entirely to be feared and detested, and having come to recognize that transmundane dhyāna-concentration and wisdom are a dharma to be treasured and honored, one must then, with a single, undivided mind, cultivate calming and insight. This "singleness of mind" is the singleness of mind of resolve itself, not the singleness of mind of already being in concentration — that is, a mind wholly focused and undivided, entering deeply through a single gate to put in real effort.

Vajra is the hardest and sharpest of substances, able to destroy every other thing while being destroyed by nothing. To say that the mind cultivating calming and insight is like vajra means it cannot be lured astray by others, nor ruined by demons or non-Buddhist paths — so that even should one gain nothing at all in the end, one still never turns back or changes course. It is like working a Chan meditation topic, resolved from the start to obtain a genuine breakthrough; or like reciting the Buddha's name, determined to reach a mind single-pointed and unconfused, never abandoning the effort halfway, with no divided intentions, focused single-mindedly with no thought left over — hence it is called singleness of mind.

It is like a traveler who must first know which roads are open and which blocked — the road of birth-and-death being blocked, not to be traveled, and the road of nirvāṇa being open, to be traveled — and only then resolves, with singleness of mind, to set out and press forward along it. What matters most is giving rise to no discriminating thought at all, and pressing straight ahead — never turning back, never changing course — hence it is spoken of as "discerning wisdom" together with "singleness of mind."

The sūtra says: "No dhyāna without wisdom, no wisdom without dhyāna" — precisely the non-duality of calming and insight themselves. Wisdom is precisely discernment; dhyāna is precisely concentration. First clarifying the resolved mind, this resolved mind is itself wisdom; and entering deeply through a single gate, entering the one without any falseness, is itself concentration. Without wisdom, one cannot cultivate dhyāna-concentration; without dhyāna-concentration, wisdom cannot be brought forth — the two relying on each other, each bringing forth the other in turn. Hence it is said: "No dhyāna without wisdom, no wisdom without dhyāna" — the meaning lies precisely in this.

Reading note: This short chapter closes the entire preparatory half of the text (chapters 1 through 5), and its own final line — "no dhyāna without wisdom, no wisdom without dhyāna" — is worth reading as a deliberate echo back to the very opening of the whole work: Fazi's preface and the title's own five-fold exegesis both already framed 止 and 觀 as a single, inseparable pair, never one without the other. Chapter 6, "Actual Practice," is where the text finally turns from preparing a practitioner to cultivate calming and insight to actually walking through the method itself.
