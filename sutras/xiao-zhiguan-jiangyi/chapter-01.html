Being Provided with the Conditions

Before a single word is said about how to practice calming and insight, Zhiyi insists on five things being in place first. Master Baojing's commentary walks through each of the five in turn, moving — true to his own preaching style — from strict monastic detail straight into plain pastoral advice for lay students who will never take an ordination platform vow in their lives.

Status: complete Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: book.bfnn.org, cross-checked against book853.com Covers: the five conditions — precepts, clothing and food, a quiet dwelling, setting affairs aside, good spiritual friends

Project Background

This is Ru-Yi's translation of chapter 1 of Master Baojing's (寶靜法師, 1899–1940) Xiūxí Zhǐguān Zuòchán Fǎyào Jiǎngshù (修習止觀坐禪法要講述) — a series of 1934 lectures on Zhiyi's short beginner's meditation manual, the Tóngméng Zhǐguān (童蒙止觀), taken down by two of Baojing's students and later revised by Baojing himself before publication. See the project index for Baojing's own biography and this commentary's background, and the translation notes for the running glossary.

Zhiyi's own root text already exists in a published English translation by Bhikshu Dharmamitra (The Essentials of Buddhist Meditation, Kalavinka Press, 2009) — but that edition translates only Zhiyi's spare original, not a word of Baojing's own commentary. No English translation of Baojing's commentary is otherwise known to exist. Every passage below, including Zhiyi's own root-text quotations, is translated independently from the Chinese, without reference to Dharmamitra's published wording.

I. Purity of Precept-Observance 持戒清淨

The first of the five conditions, and the one Baojing's commentary spends by far the longest on: a practitioner's own relationship to the precepts, graded into three levels, with the lower level's own path back to purity through repentance worked out in careful detail.

The General Principle 總明持戒之要

Chinese Source

〖文〗夫發心起行，欲修止觀者，要先外具五緣：第一持戒清淨。如經中說，依因此戒，得生諸禪定，及滅苦智慧，是故比丘應持戒清淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Now, for one who has given rise to the resolve to act, and wishes to cultivate calming and insight, five outward conditions must first be in place. The first is purity of precept-observance. As a sūtra says: "It is by relying on this precept-observance that one is able to give rise to every dhyāna-concentration, and to the wisdom that extinguishes suffering." This is why a monk should keep the precepts purely.

〖講〗止觀分十大章，此為十章之第一章，名為具緣。欲修止觀，必須具足五緣，方能進修。第一持戒清淨，第二衣食具足，第三閒居靜處，第四息諸緣務，第五近善知識。夫者，發端之辭。凡欲發心修止觀做工夫者，要先外具五緣，然後進修，方克有效。比如造屋，必須先將基礎堅固，然後或一層二層七層九層乃至多層，皆可隨意起造。如經中說，依因此戒，得生禪定智慧。若持戒不清淨，禪定無由發生。所謂戒淨，則定生，定生則慧發，因戒生定，因定發慧。經云：「尸羅不清淨，三昧不現前，無礙清淨慧，皆從禪定生。」當慧光朗現時，即能滅一切諸苦。所有三苦八苦無量諸苦，皆仗此智慧之功而滅除之，故云及滅苦智慧。楞嚴經中所注重四種清淨明誨，即戒是也。若不持戒，縱有多智禪定，悉是魔道之業，終為魔王，如蒸沙焉能成飯，蓋沙非飯本，是故比丘，應持戒清淨。說此止觀，乃為四眾而說，雖與比丘眾言，其餘三眾亦在內，每一法會之中，具有四眾弟子，言一即具三，且比丘為四眾之首，故誥比丘，即該餘眾也。

Commentary: Calming and insight is divided into ten major chapters; this is the first of the ten, called "being provided with the conditions." To cultivate calming and insight, one must first have five conditions in place before one can actually make progress: first, purity of precept-observance; second, sufficiency of clothing and food; third, a quiet dwelling place; fourth, setting worldly affairs aside; fifth, staying close to a good spiritual friend.

Anyone who wishes to give rise to the resolve to put real effort into calming and insight must first have these five outward conditions in place, and only then take up the practice, so that it can actually bear fruit. It is like building a house: the foundation must first be made solid, and only then can one, one story after another, build up to two, seven, nine, or as many stories as one likes.

As the sūtra says, it is by relying on precept-observance that concentration and wisdom are able to arise. If one's precept-observance is not pure, no dhyāna-concentration has any way to arise. This is what is meant by saying that once precepts are pure, concentration arises; once concentration arises, wisdom is brought forth — concentration arises because of precepts, and wisdom is brought forth because of concentration. A sūtra says: "When śīla is not pure, samādhi does not appear; the unobstructed, pure wisdom all arises from dhyāna-concentration." Once the light of wisdom shines forth in full, it is able to extinguish every kind of suffering — the three sufferings, the eight sufferings, every measureless kind of suffering, all are extinguished through the power of this very wisdom. This is why the text says "and the wisdom that extinguishes suffering." The four kinds of pure, clear instruction that the Śūraṅgama Sūtra so emphasizes are precisely this same precept-observance.

If one does not keep the precepts, then no matter how much learning or dhyāna-concentration one has, all of it is Māra's own karma, and one ends up, in the end, as a demon-king oneself — like steaming sand and expecting it to become rice: sand was never rice's own basis to begin with. This is why a monk should keep the precepts purely. And though calming and insight is here addressed to monks specifically, it is spoken for all four assemblies — the remaining three are included as well. In any single Dharma assembly, all four kinds of disciples are present together; naming one already implies the other three, and since monks stand foremost among the four assemblies, addressing monks already covers the rest.

The Three Grades of Precept-Observance 三品持戒

Upper Grade 上品持戒

Chinese Source

〖文〗然有三種行人持戒不同：一者若人未作佛弟子時，不造五逆，後遇良師，教受三皈五戒，為佛弟子。若得出家，受沙彌十戒，次受具足戒，作比丘、比丘尼。從受戒來，清淨護持，無所毀犯，是名上品持戒人也。當知是人修行止觀，必證佛法，猶如淨衣，易受染色。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Yet there are three kinds of practitioners, differing in how they hold the precepts. The first: someone who, even before becoming a disciple of the Buddha, committed none of the five heinous offenses, and who afterward meets a good teacher and is taught to take the three refuges and the five precepts, becoming a disciple of the Buddha. If they go on to leave the household life, receiving the ten novice precepts and then the full precepts, becoming a monk or a nun — and from the moment of receiving these precepts onward, keeps them purely, with nothing broken or violated — this is called a person of upper-grade precept-observance. Know that such a person, cultivating calming and insight, will certainly realize the Buddhadharma — just as a clean garment readily takes a dye.

〖講〗初二句總標。然，轉語之辭。持戒有上中下三品持戒之不同。第一者，指上品持戒人，若修行之人，未曾發心學佛之先，未作佛弟子之時，即能循規蹈矩，不造五逆之罪。五逆者，謂弒父、弒母、弒阿羅漢、出佛身血、破和合僧，造此五種，則傷法身，失慧命，逆涅槃海，順生死流，墮泥犁，受劇報。今此行人，能不造五逆，則為世間之良人，不僅如此，後又遇良師善知識，教其受三皈，皈依佛法僧三寶，復進受五戒，即不殺、不盜、不淫、不妄、不酒之五戒，彼受皈戒後，能清淨受持無犯，後為佛弟子。出家作沙彌，受沙彌十戒……如是十戒，為沙彌應受持者。次則進受比丘二百五十戒……如是行人一切大小輕重諸戒，一一悉能清淨護持，絲毫無所毀犯，是則名為上品持戒之人也。當知如是清淨持戒人，修行止觀，必證佛法。清淨持淨戒，猶如護明珠，戒淨起修，易證佛法，猶如清淨白色之衣，易受染色，以白色為眾色之本，易受染污。故上品持戒之人，易修止觀也。

Commentary: The first two sentences state the general heading. "Yet" signals a shift. Precept-observance divides into upper, middle, and lower grades. The first names the person of upper-grade observance: a practitioner who, even before ever setting their mind on learning the Buddhadharma, before becoming a disciple of the Buddha at all, already conducted themselves within bounds and committed none of the five heinous offenses. The five heinous offenses are: killing one's father, killing one's mother, killing an arhat, drawing a buddha's blood, and causing a schism in the harmonious saṅgha — committing any of these five wounds the Dharma-body, destroys the life of wisdom, runs against the ocean of nirvāṇa and with the stream of birth-and-death, and brings one down into hell to suffer severe retribution.

This practitioner, able to have committed none of the five, is already a good person by the world's own standard. And beyond this, they later meet a good teacher, a good spiritual friend, who teaches them to take the three refuges — taking refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha — and then to go further and receive the five precepts: not killing, not stealing, no sexual misconduct, no false speech, and no intoxicants. Having received the refuges and precepts, they keep them purely, without violation, and thereafter become a genuine disciple of the Buddha. Leaving the household life, they become a novice and receive the ten novice precepts... these ten are what a novice ought to uphold. Next, they go on to receive the two hundred and fifty precepts of a full monk... A practitioner who is able to uphold every one of these precepts, major and minor alike, purely, without the slightest violation, is what is called a person of upper-grade precept-observance.

Know that a person who keeps the precepts as purely as this will, in cultivating calming and insight, certainly realize the Buddhadharma. Holding the precepts purely is like guarding a bright pearl; beginning practice from pure precepts makes realizing the Buddhadharma easy — just as a clean white garment readily takes a dye, since white is the basis of every color and so readily takes on whatever is applied to it. This is why a person of upper-grade precept-observance finds calming and insight easy to cultivate.

Middle Grade 中品持戒

Chinese Source

〖文〗二者，若人受得戒已，雖不犯重，於諸輕戒多所毀損。為修定故，即能如法懺悔，亦名持戒清淨，能生定慧。如衣曾有垢膩，若能浣淨，染亦可著。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The second: someone who, having received the precepts, has not broken any of the major ones, yet has damaged many of the minor precepts through various lapses. For the sake of cultivating concentration, they are able to repent in accordance with the Dharma — this too is called purity of precept-observance, and is able to give rise to concentration and wisdom. It is like a garment that once had grease-stains on it: if it can be properly washed clean, it too can take a dye.

〖講〗此明第二種中品持戒之人。若人得受戒已，由受戒而得戒，如受戒時，最為重要。正當登戒壇，自四羯摩時，即獲無作戒體，得戒體已，能持戒清淨者，則福等虛空。若受得戒而復毀犯，則罪上加罪。重戒即指四波羅夷之根本大戒，如此人雖於重戒無犯，而於輕戒多所損毀。輕戒，即指尼薩耆，婆逸提等。知過能改，從此發露懺悔，懺即懺其前愆，悔則悔其後過。自知有罪當懺悔，懺悔則安樂，不懺悔罪益深，故以至誠心，懇切懺悔。懺悔之法，有事有理……諸懺之中，此理懺功能最大。古德云：「重罪如霜露，慧日能消除。」若欲懺悔者，端坐念實相，行人自知罪障深重，依此如法懺悔，此亦名持戒清淨，此種即中品持戒之人。若能知懺悔，即屬難得之人。何以故？古云：「人非聖賢，誰能無過，知過能改，善莫大矣！」若能懺悔，即生定慧；如衣有垢膩，若能浣濯洗淨，染色亦可得著。衣如戒體，垢膩如犯戒，浣洗如懺悔。

Commentary: This explains the second kind, the person of middle-grade precept-observance. Once a person has received the precepts and actually obtained the precept-substance through that reception — the moment of ordination itself matters enormously: it is exactly upon ascending the ordination platform, at the fourfold karman act, that the "non-active precept-substance" is obtained. Having obtained this precept-substance, one who keeps it purely gains merit equal to the whole of empty space. But if, having obtained the precepts, one goes on to violate them, this is offense heaped upon offense.

The "major precepts" here refer to the four root pārājika offenses; this person, though committing none of these major offenses, has damaged many of the minor precepts through repeated lapses — the minor precepts being those such as the niḥsargika and pāyattika categories. Knowing one's own fault and being able to correct it, one thereupon confesses and repents openly — "confession" (懺) addresses the fault already committed, "repentance" (悔) addresses not repeating it going forward. Knowing one has an offense, one ought to repent it; repenting brings peace of mind, while failing to repent only lets the offense grow deeper — hence one repents with utterly sincere, earnest intent.

The method of repentance has both a ritual-form aspect and a principle aspect... Among the various forms of repentance, this repentance through principle has the greatest power. An ancient worthy said: "A grave offense is like frost and dew — the sun of wisdom can dissolve it away." One who wishes to repent this way sits upright and contemplates true reality itself; a practitioner who knows their own offense to be grave, repenting in this manner in accordance with the Dharma, is likewise called pure in precept-observance — this is the person of middle-grade precept-observance.

Anyone who is able to recognize the need to repent is already a rare person. Why is this? As the ancients said: "No one but a sage is without fault; to know one's fault and correct it — nothing is more excellent than this!" Being able to repent, one gives rise to concentration and wisdom, just as a garment with grease-stains, once properly washed clean, can still take a dye. The garment is like the precept-substance; the grease-stain is like the violation; the washing is like the repentance.

Lower Grade 下品持戒

Chinese Source

〖文〗三者，若人受得戒已，不能堅心護持，輕重諸戒，多所毀犯。依小乘教門，即無懺悔四重之法；若依大乘教門，猶可滅除。故經云：「佛法有二種健人：一者不作諸惡，二者作已能悔。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The third: someone who, having received the precepts, is unable to hold to them with a firm mind, and violates many precepts, both major and minor. According to Hīnayāna teaching, there is no method of repentance for the four major offenses at all; but according to Mahāyāna teaching, they can still be dissolved and removed. Hence a sūtra says: "In the Buddhadharma there are two kinds of vigorous person: one who commits no evil at all, and one who, having committed it, is able to repent."

〖講〗此是第三種下品人持戒。若人受得戒已，於一切戒品，不能堅心護持，無論輕重戒相，多所毀犯，如人將頭已斬，決無復活之望，若依小乘教門，則無懺除重罪之法。所謂千佛出世，不通懺悔。若依大乘教法，則不然，亦可有懺除重罪之方法，足見大乘法門，不可思議。經云：佛法有二種健康之人，第一不作諸惡，終日渾渾噩噩，天真瀾漫，法爾為世之好人，不作一切諸惡，此為第一健人。第二作已能悔，平素雖作諸惡，後能悔過自責，發露懺悔，革故不造新，亦得稱為健人。因此二人，善根猛利，智慧力強，超生死，證菩提，所以稱為佛法中之二健人也。

Commentary: This is the third kind, the person of lower-grade precept-observance. Having received the precepts, they are unable to hold to any of them with a firm mind, and violate many, whether major or minor — like someone whose head has already been cut off, with no hope left of coming back to life. According to Hīnayāna teaching, there is no method for repenting away a grave offense at all — it is said that even were a thousand buddhas to appear in the world, such an offense would still admit no repentance. But according to Mahāyāna teaching, this is not so — there is indeed a method for dissolving even a grave offense, which is enough to show how inconceivable the Mahāyāna's own gate of teaching truly is.

The sūtra says there are two kinds of vigorous, healthy person in the Buddhadharma. The first commits no evil at all — living out their days without pretense, guileless by nature, simply a good person in the world's own eyes, who does nothing evil whatsoever; this is the first kind of vigorous person. The second, having committed evil, is able to repent it — though they may formerly have done evil, they are later able to regret it, blame themselves, and confess and repent it openly, discarding the old and never committing it again; this too deserves to be called a vigorous person. Because both of these have keen, vigorous wholesome roots and strong power of wisdom, both are able to transcend birth-and-death and realize bodhi — hence both are called the Buddhadharma's own two kinds of vigorous person.

Ten Methods That Aid Repentance 十法助懺

Chinese Source

〖文〗夫欲懺悔者，須具十法，助成其懺：一者明信因果；二者生重怖畏；三者深起慚愧；四者求滅罪方法。所謂大乘經中明諸行法，應當如法修行；五者發露先罪；六者斷相續心；七者起護法心；八者發大誓願，度脫眾生；九者常念十方諸佛；十者觀罪性無生。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Now, one who wishes to repent must have ten methods in place, to help bring the repentance to completion: first, clearly believing in cause and effect; second, giving rise to profound dread; third, giving rise to deep shame; fourth, seeking a method for extinguishing the offense — that is, the practices set out in Mahāyāna sūtras, which should be carried out as the Dharma prescribes; fifth, confessing the past offense openly; sixth, cutting off the mind that would continue it; seventh, giving rise to a mind that protects the Dharma; eighth, giving rise to the great vow to bring sentient beings across; ninth, constantly being mindful of the buddhas of the ten directions; and tenth, contemplating that the very nature of the offense is unborn.

〖講〗夫者，發端之辭。倘欲懺悔所作諸惡，必須具足十法，幫助成就其懺悔之功。一者，首先明其因果報應，絲毫不爽，惡因感惡果，善因招善果，所謂種瓜得瓜，種豆得豆。二者生重怖畏，自知有惡因必感惡果，墮獄受劇苦，即生大怖畏，毛骨悚然，所謂戰戰兢兢，如臨深淵，如履薄冰，惡因可畏，至為悚懼。第三深起慚愧，慚者慚己，愧者愧他，慚自己毀戒下劣，愧諸賢之超脫明淨，釋迦因何成佛，吾何久為眾生，有見賢思齊之心，生大慚愧，痛改前非。四者，滅罪方法，須常求滅除自己罪業之方法，何法能除滅罪障，常尋求之。五者，發露先罪，倘若已作之惡，或自知、或不知，或有心、或無心等，所造之罪，對三寶前，或對善知識前，克誠披露，求哀懺悔；若能懺悔，罪即消滅，若不肯發露，覆藏於心，則日久月深，則罪根深結，懺悔不易矣。六者，斷相續心，若雖自知有罪，對三寶前懺悔已竟，但相續心不斷，仍繼續造惡，則前之懺悔，雖懺無益，非將起惡之相續心，截斷不可，隨緣消舊業，更不造新殃，故須斷相續心。七者起護法心，發起衛護佛法之心，若自己奉持佛法，即自能保護佛法；若他人敬奉佛法，他人即是保護佛法。若己若他，皆當維護，可獲福滅罪，故須起護法心。八者發大誓願，即發四弘誓願之心。所謂眾生無邊誓願度，煩惱無盡誓願斷，法門無量誓願學，佛道無上誓願成，即是發自利利他，上求下化之心，度脫一切眾生，不分人我彼此。彼苦即我苦，故急而拔之，彼樂即我樂，故急與之樂，拔彼之苦，即是拔己之苦，與彼之樂，即我之樂，苦樂亦如是。所言誓願者，發誓以要其心，起願以策其身，不可改變，能發廣大願，亦可滅罪。九者常念十方諸佛，念佛即能成佛，若念眾生則做眾生。吾人既為佛子，必學佛行，故須念佛。但十方諸佛無量，名號亦無量，取其簡略而易得益者，莫如彌陀名號，所謂十方三世佛，阿彌陀第一。能持念此佛名號，即持念十方諸佛聖號。念彌陀一聲，能滅八十億劫生死重罪，亦即懺悔一切罪障。十者觀罪性無生，觀者觀察所造之罪，皆由心起，既眾罪從心所起，自當將心懺，試觀此罪相，從何而起，覓罪了不可得，以其不自生，不他生，不共生，不無因而生，四性推究，當體無生。所謂罪性本空，如四祖至三祖處，求哀懺悔己之罪障。師曰：「將罪來與汝懺。」四祖返觀自心，了不可得，答曰：「覓罪了不可得。」三祖云：「我與汝懺悔竟。」此一種乃理懺，前來九種都是事懺。故知此理懺，功能最大，觀一切罪惡之性，當體無生，心滅罪空，是名真懺悔，然又須知事懺必須具理懺，理懺復須藉事懺，理事圓融，故名真懺。

Commentary: "Now" opens the passage. If one wishes to repent whatever evil one has done, one must have all ten methods in place to help bring one's repentance to completion.

First, clearly understanding cause and effect above all — that retribution never fails by the slightest degree: an evil cause draws an evil result, a wholesome cause draws a wholesome result, exactly as sowing melon yields melon and sowing beans yields beans.

Second, giving rise to profound dread — knowing that an evil cause must draw an evil result, that one will fall into hell and suffer severe pain, one gives rise to great dread, hair standing on end, trembling as though standing at the edge of an abyss or walking on thin ice: the evil cause is truly to be feared, truly to be dreaded.

Third, giving rise to deep shame — 慚 is shame before oneself, 愧 is shame before others: ashamed of one's own inferior conduct in breaking the precepts, and ashamed before the transcendent purity of the worthy ones — asking oneself, how did Śākyamuni come to attain buddhahood, while I remain, for so long, an ordinary being? — having a mind that, on seeing the worthy, wishes to become their equal, one gives rise to great shame and thoroughly corrects one's past faults.

Fourth, seeking a method for extinguishing the offense — one must constantly seek out a method for extinguishing one's own karmic offense, constantly searching for whatever method is able to remove this obstruction of offense.

Fifth, confessing the past offense openly — whatever evil has already been done, whether known to oneself or not, whether done intentionally or not, one discloses it with utter sincerity before the Three Jewels, or before a good spiritual friend, and begs for compassionate forgiveness through repentance. If one is able to repent in this way, the offense is thereby dissolved; but if one refuses to disclose it, hiding it away in the mind instead, then as months and years pass, the root of the offense only knots itself deeper, and repentance becomes far harder.

Sixth, cutting off the mind that would continue the offense — even if one knows one has an offense and has already repented it before the Three Jewels, if the mind that continues the pattern is not itself cut off, and one goes on committing the same evil, then the prior repentance, however sincerely performed, brings no benefit. One must sever this continuing mind that gives rise to evil outright — resolving old karma as conditions allow, and never again creating new misfortune — hence the mind that would continue it must be cut off.

Seventh, giving rise to a mind that protects the Dharma — giving rise to a mind that guards and protects the Buddhadharma: if one upholds the Buddhadharma oneself, one is thereby already protecting it; and if another person reveres and upholds it, that person too is protecting it. Whether it concerns oneself or another, both should be safeguarded — this can bring merit and dissolve offense, hence the mind that protects the Dharma must be raised.

Eighth, giving rise to the great vow — that is, giving rise to the mind of the four universal vows: sentient beings are numberless, I vow to bring them across; afflictions are endless, I vow to sever them; dharma-gates are measureless, I vow to study them; the buddha-path is unsurpassed, I vow to accomplish it. This is giving rise to the mind that benefits both self and other, seeking above and transforming below, bringing every sentient being across without distinguishing self from other, this person from that. Their suffering is my own suffering, so I hasten to relieve it; their happiness is my own happiness, so I hasten to give it — relieving their suffering is relieving my own, giving them happiness is giving myself happiness, and suffering and happiness are just the same in this regard. What is called a "vow" is this: an oath binds the mind to it, and a vow spurs the body to act on it, unwavering — and giving rise to a vast vow of this kind can also dissolve offense.

Ninth, constantly being mindful of the buddhas of the ten directions — being mindful of a buddha, one is able to become a buddha; being mindful of sentient beings, one becomes a sentient being. Since we are already children of the Buddha, we must learn a buddha's own conduct, hence we must be mindful of a buddha. But the buddhas of the ten directions are measureless, and their names likewise measureless; taking the simplest and most readily beneficial among them, none surpasses the name of Amitābha — as it is said, "of all the buddhas of the ten directions and three times, Amitābha is foremost." Holding this one buddha's name in mind is, at the same time, holding in mind the sacred names of every buddha of the ten directions. A single recitation of Amitābha's name is able to extinguish the grave offenses of birth-and-death accumulated across eighty koṭis of kalpas — which is to say, it is itself a repentance of every obstruction of offense.

Tenth, contemplating that the very nature of the offense is unborn — "contemplating" here means observing that whatever offense has been committed arises entirely from the mind; since every offense arises from the mind, one should, accordingly, repent by means of the mind itself. Examine closely: from where does this mark of an offense actually arise? Search for the offense, and it can nowhere be found — for it does not arise of itself, does not arise from another, does not arise from both together, and does not arise without any cause at all. Investigated through these four possibilities, its very substance is, throughout, unborn. This is what is meant by saying the offense's own nature is fundamentally empty.

It is like the story of the Fourth Patriarch going before the Third Patriarch, begging in anguish for his own offenses to be repented. The master said: "Bring me the offense, and I will repent it for you." The Fourth Patriarch turned to look within his own mind, and found nothing at all to be gotten — he answered: "Searching for the offense, it cannot be found." The Third Patriarch said: "Then I have already repented it for you." This one method is repentance through principle; the nine that came before are all repentance through ritual form. From this we know that this repentance through principle has the very greatest power — contemplating that the nature of every offense is, throughout its substance, unborn, so that when the mind is extinguished the offense is empty: this is called true repentance. Yet it must also be understood that repentance through ritual form must itself already contain repentance through principle, and repentance through principle must in turn rely on repentance through ritual form — the two, form and principle, perfectly interfused, is what is truly meant by genuine repentance.

How Long to Practice, and the Signs That It Has Worked 懺法之時間與罪滅之相

Chinese Source

〖文〗若能成就如此十法，莊嚴道場，洗浣清淨，著淨潔衣，燒香散花於三寶前，如法修行一七、三七日、或一月、三月，乃至經年，專心懺悔所犯重罪，取滅方止。云何知重罪滅相？若行者如是至心懺悔時，自覺身心輕利，得好瑞夢；或復睹諸靈瑞異相，或覺善心開發，或自於坐中，覺身如雲如影，因是漸證得諸禪境界；或復豁然解悟心生，善識法相，隨所聞經即知義趣，因是法喜，心無憂悔；如是等種種因緣，當知即是破戒障道罪滅之相。從是已後，堅持禁戒，亦名尸羅清淨，可修禪定，猶如破壞垢膩之衣，若能補治浣洗清淨，猶可染著。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: If one is able to accomplish all ten of these methods, adorning a place of practice, washing oneself clean, wearing clean garments, burning incense and scattering flowers before the Three Jewels, and practicing as the Dharma prescribes for a week, three weeks, a month, three months, or even a full year — repenting one-pointedly whatever grave offense has been committed, not stopping until it is extinguished — how does one know the sign that a grave offense has been extinguished? If, repenting this earnestly, the practitioner spontaneously feels body and mind grow light and at ease, and has auspicious dreams; or witnesses various numinous, extraordinary signs; or feels a wholesome mind opening up; or, while seated, feels the body as insubstantial as cloud or shadow, and through this gradually attains states of dhyāna; or a sudden understanding arises, recognizing the marks of dharmas clearly, so that whatever sūtra one hears, its meaning is at once grasped, bringing joy in the Dharma with no more anxiety or regret — all such causes and conditions as these are the sign that the offense obstructing the path through breaking the precepts has been extinguished. From this point on, holding firmly to the precepts is likewise called purity of śīla, and one may cultivate dhyāna-concentration — just as a torn, grease-stained garment, once mended and washed clean, can still take a dye.

〖講〗倘能具足如上所立十種懺悔之方法，莊嚴懺悔之道場，將內身沐浴清淨，外衣浣洗清淨，以淨身，著淨衣，然後入懺悔堂，燒一切上妙好香，如沈水香，栴檀香，末香等諸香；並及供諸微妙寶花，於三寶前依十種法，翹誠禮拜，求哀懺悔。依此如法修行，或一七日，不得益，乃至三七日，若再不得益，於是或一月二月，三四五月，乃至經年，專心懺悔從前所犯之重罪，不論時之多少，惟至滅罪方止。若行者，如是至誠懇切，一心懺悔之時，自覺身心，發現一種輕安快利之相，夜間並得祥瑞之好夢，或見諸佛放光，或見佛來摩頂……或覺善心開發，或自於坐中，覺身如雲如影，此正是欲界定之相狀，從此或漸漸證得諸禪境界……或復豁然解悟心生，善識諸法之名相，豁然貫通，解悟心生……同時即發生一種殊勝妙解，隨時所聞一切佛經，即了知甚深微妙之理，及其所歸之義趣，所謂聞一知十，聞十知百。因是得法喜充滿，既獲法喜，則自心無所憂悔……當知如此懺悔，即是滅罪除障之惟一方法。從是懺悔之後，即能以堅固心，精持諸佛禁戒，絲毫無犯，亦可名為尸羅清淨。梵語尸羅，此云持戒。如破壞垢膩之衣，若能補治浣洗，猶可染著，破壞而能補，垢而能洗，破壞者補治完美，垢膩者浣洗清淨，即無用而成有用，故云猶可染著。

Commentary: If one can bring together all ten of the repentance methods laid out above, one adorns a place of practice for repentance, bathes the body clean and washes one's outer robe clean, so that both body and clothing are pure, and then enters the repentance hall, burning every kind of fine incense — aloeswood, sandalwood, powdered incense, and the rest — and offering exquisite jeweled flowers, and before the Three Jewels, following the ten methods, bows with utmost sincerity and begs for compassionate forgiveness through repentance. Practicing this way as the Dharma prescribes, if one week brings no benefit, one continues to three weeks; if that still brings no benefit, then one month, two months, three, four, five months, even a full year, single-mindedly repenting the grave offense committed in the past — regardless of how much time it takes, one does not stop until the offense is extinguished.

If the practitioner repents this sincerely and earnestly, one-pointedly, they will spontaneously feel body and mind manifest a mark of lightness, ease, and swiftness; at night they will have auspicious, good dreams — perhaps seeing buddhas emit light, or seeing a buddha come and place a hand on their crown... or feeling a wholesome mind opening up, or, while seated, feeling the body as insubstantial as cloud or shadow — this is precisely the mark of desire-realm concentration, from which one may gradually go on to attain the various states of dhyāna... or a sudden understanding may arise, clearly recognizing the names and marks of every dharma, thoroughly penetrating, understanding spontaneously arising... at the same time, a particular kind of excellent, wondrous comprehension arises, so that whatever buddha-sūtra one happens to hear, its deep and subtle principle, and the meaning it ultimately points toward, are at once grasped — hearing one point, one knows ten; hearing ten, one knows a hundred. Through this, one is filled with joy in the Dharma, and having attained this joy, the mind itself has nothing more to be anxious or regretful about... Know that repentance of this kind is the one and only method for extinguishing offense and removing obstruction.

From this point of repentance onward, one is able, with a firm mind, to hold precisely to every precept the buddhas have set down, without the slightest violation — this too may be called purity of śīla. In Sanskrit, śīla; in Chinese, "precept-observance." It is like a torn, grease-stained garment: if it can be mended and washed clean, it can still take a dye — what was torn can be mended, what was stained can be washed, and what was ruined, once fully repaired, and what was soiled, once washed clean, becomes useful again out of what was useless. Hence it is said that it can still take a dye.

Repentance Through Principle Alone 大乘無相理懺

Chinese Source

〖文〗若人犯重禁已，恐障禪定，雖不依諸經修諸行法，但生重慚愧，於三寶前，發露先罪，斷相續心，端身常坐，觀罪性空，念十方佛。若出禪時，即須至心燒香禮拜、懺悔誦戒、及誦大乘經典，障道重罪，自當漸漸消滅，因此尸羅清淨，禪定開發。故妙勝定經云：若人犯重罪已，心生怖畏，欲求除滅，若除禪定，餘無能滅。是人應當在空閑處，攝心常坐，及誦大乘經，一切重罪悉皆消滅，諸禪三昧自然現前。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: If someone has already broken a grave prohibition and fears it will obstruct dhyāna-concentration, even without following the ritual practices set out in the various sūtras, they need only give rise to profound shame, disclose the past offense before the Three Jewels, cut off the mind that would continue it, sit upright at length with body composed, contemplate that the very nature of the offense is empty, and be mindful of the buddhas of the ten directions. On rising from meditation, they should, with utmost sincerity, burn incense, bow, repent and recite the precepts, and recite Mahāyāna sūtras — and the grave offense obstructing the path will, of itself, gradually be dissolved; through this, śīla becomes pure, and dhyāna-concentration opens up. Hence the Sūtra of Wondrously Excellent Concentration says: "If someone has already committed a grave offense, and dread arises in the mind, wishing to seek its removal — apart from dhyāna-concentration, nothing else can remove it. Such a person should, in a quiet, secluded place, gather the mind and sit at length, and recite Mahāyāna sūtras; every grave offense will thereby be entirely dissolved, and the various dhyānas and samādhis will naturally manifest."

〖講〗上來約大乘作法事懺，亦具有理懺，如第十明觀罪性無生。此約大乘無相理懺，亦具有事懺，如出禪時燒香禮拜，誦戒誦經等是。若人犯重戒已，恐障禪定，了知尸羅不清淨，三昧不現前，然不能依諸修多羅經，修諸行法，但生重慚愧，於佛法僧三寶之前，發陳披露，見前所作之重罪，至誠懺悔，並斷相續之心，然後收攝身心，端身靜坐，常常反觀罪性，微密追究，則知罪業無性，其體本空。又念十方佛以懺罪，念茲在茲，或持念諸佛萬德洪名，或單念阿彌陀佛聖號。若開靜出禪時，即以至誠心，燒香禮拜懺悔，誦梵網經，菩薩戒，及誦法華經，楞嚴經等，一切大乘經典，所有障道重罪，認真求懺，如是自當漸漸消除，滅罪斷惡，尸羅之戒體，復從而清淨潔白，於是禪智開發；此又是懺除罪業之一簡單方法。此處引經作證。妙勝定經，此經專為詮顯禪定，言妙勝者，正顯禪定為最殊勝，最為微妙，故云妙勝定。此經云：若人犯重罪已，心中生恐怖畏懼之心，欲求除滅，除修禪定以滅罪外，餘無能滅。是人應當於空閒處，阿蘭若所，攝心常坐，及誦大乘方等經典，一切重罪悉皆消滅，諸禪三昧，自然現前。梵語三昧，此云正定，亦云正受，亦云調直定，因為吾等眾生，終日妄想紛飛，攀攬塵境，彎彎曲曲，不得調直，若修禪定，則心自能行歸正道，法爾調直，猶如蛇之行路，蜿曲不直，待入竹筒之時，不求直而自直矣。無論聽經誦經，念佛修觀，皆須持戒清淨為第一。此章為台宗二十五方便之第一科，足知持戒清淨，為修習止觀第一要緊之方便也。

Commentary: The passage above concerns Mahāyāna repentance through ritual form, which likewise already contains repentance through principle — as in the tenth method, contemplating that the offense's nature is unborn. This passage concerns Mahāyāna signless repentance through principle, which likewise already contains repentance through ritual form — as in burning incense and bowing, reciting the precepts and sūtras, upon rising from meditation.

If someone has already broken a grave precept and fears it will obstruct dhyāna-concentration, understanding that when śīla is impure, samādhi does not appear, yet is unable to follow the ritual practices set out in the various sūtras, they need only give rise to profound shame, and before the Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha, openly disclose whatever grave offense was previously committed, repenting it with utmost sincerity, and cut off the mind that would continue it. Then, gathering body and mind together, sitting upright and still, constantly turning to examine the offense's own nature in return, investigating it minutely and closely, one comes to know that karmic offense has no fixed nature at all — its substance is, from the start, empty. And being mindful of the buddhas of the ten directions in order to repent the offense, holding this mindfulness continuously, one either recites the ten-thousand-virtued great names of all the buddhas, or simply recites the sacred name of Amitābha alone. When meditation ends and one rises, one should, with utmost sincerity, burn incense, bow, and repent, reciting the Brahma Net Sūtra's bodhisattva precepts, and reciting the Lotus Sūtra, the Śūraṅgama Sūtra, and other Mahāyāna sūtras — earnestly seeking to repent whatever grave offense obstructs the path. In this way it will, of itself, gradually be dissolved, the offense extinguished and the evil severed, and the precept-substance of śīla will thereby become pure and unblemished once more, so that dhyāna-wisdom opens up. This too is a simple method for repenting away karmic offense.

Here the text cites a sūtra as proof. The Sūtra of Wondrously Excellent Concentration is a sūtra devoted entirely to expounding dhyāna-concentration; the word "wondrously excellent" reveals precisely that dhyāna-concentration is most excellent, most subtle — hence it is called "wondrously excellent concentration." This sūtra says: if someone has already committed a grave offense, and dread and terror arise in the mind, wishing to seek its removal, then apart from cultivating dhyāna-concentration to extinguish the offense, nothing else is able to extinguish it. Such a person should, in a quiet, secluded place — an araṇya dwelling — gather the mind and sit at length, and recite Mahāyāna vaipulya sūtras; every grave offense will be entirely dissolved, and the various dhyānas and samādhis will naturally manifest.

In Sanskrit, samādhi; in Chinese, "correct concentration," also called "correct reception," also called "the concentration that straightens what is crooked." For we sentient beings, from morning to night, our idle thoughts fly about in confusion, grasping and clinging to dusty objects, twisting this way and that, never becoming straight — like muddy water in a jar that has not settled clear. But if one cultivates dhyāna-concentration, then the mind is naturally able to return of itself to the correct path, straightened as a matter of course — just as a snake's path is bent and crooked, yet once it enters a bamboo tube, it becomes straight of itself, without seeking straightness at all.

Whether listening to a sūtra, reciting a sūtra, reciting the Buddha's name, or cultivating contemplation — all require purity of precept-observance as the very first thing. This chapter forms the first of the Tiantai school's own twenty-five preliminary expedients, which is enough to show that purity of precept-observance is the single most essential preliminary expedient for practicing calming and insight.

Reading note: Notice how much further Baojing pushes the "lower grade" of precept-observance than a stricter reading might expect. Zhiyi's own root text already opens the Mahāyāna's repentance to someone who has broken even the four root offenses — offenses Hīnayāna teaching treats as flatly unrepentable — and Baojing's commentary spends by far its longest stretch in this chapter unpacking exactly how: ten supporting methods, a set period of ritual practice, concrete signs that it has actually worked, and finally a second path through principle alone, for anyone too pressed for time or resources to carry out the full ritual form. His own closing move is characteristic of his style throughout this commentary: he does not rank repentance through ritual form above or below repentance through principle, but insists the two already contain each other, and immediately pivots to reciting Amitābha's name as itself both a completion of the ten methods and a shortcut through them — the same Tiantai-and-Pure-Land pairing Fazi's preface names as this whole commentary's own governing theme.

II. Sufficiency of Clothing and Food 衣食具足

The second condition: enough by way of clothing and food that the body's own needs stop being a distraction — but, in Baojing's own framing, no more than that, since excess on either side becomes its own obstruction to practice.

Clothing, in Three Grades 衣法三品

Chinese Source

〖文〗第二衣食具足者。衣法有三種：一者如雪山大士，隨得一衣蔽形即足，以不遊人間，堪忍力成故。二者如迦葉常受頭陀法，但畜糞掃三衣，不畜餘長。三者若多寒國土，及忍力未成之者，如來亦許三衣之外，畜百一等物，而要須說淨，知量知足。若過貪求積聚，則心亂妨道。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The second condition is sufficiency of clothing and food. As for clothing, there are three grades. The first: like the Great Snow-Mountain Ascetic, content with whatever single garment covers the body, since he did not travel among people, and his own power of endurance was already accomplished. The second: like Kāśyapa, who constantly practiced the dhutaṅga observances, keeping only three robes made of discarded rags, and nothing more besides. The third: for lands with much cold, and for those whose own power of endurance is not yet accomplished, the Tathāgata also permits, beyond the three robes, keeping up to a hundred and one further items — but these must be formally consecrated as offered to the community, and one must know moderation and be content. Excessive craving and accumulation only disturbs the mind and obstructs the path.

〖講〗上來說持戒第一，因戒為菩提之本，欲修習止觀，務須持戒清淨。第二要衣食具足，衣食乃助道之資糧，若衣食不具足，則裸餒不安，何以能修行辦道？所謂身安則道隆，又所謂法輪未轉食輪先，是知衣食為修行之助道。然衣食不可過求於奢華，奢華則增長欲念，反為障道因緣矣。衣食二法，各有三種，以分三根行人。初明衣之三品，一者如雪山大士。大士者，即釋迦牟尼佛，為度生故，於雪山苦行六年……雪山大士修苦行時，隨得一衣蔽形即足……全憑堪忍之力而成就也，著如是衣，名之為上根。二者如迦葉常受頭陀法……迦葉尊者……行頭陀之法，共有十二……衣有二種：一者所謂糞掃衣……二者但三衣……迦葉尊者，但畜三衣，自能辦道，是名第二種中根人所著衣。三者若多寒國土……以中國地處寒帶……如來特開方便，亦許三衣之外，準畜百一物……而必須說淨。此說淨之法……謂將此物，供養三寶大眾，請慈悲納受，既已發心，供養大眾，為大眾所賜，非我所有，非己所屬，即名說淨；若不如是，則為己有，即謂之不淨耳。而且要知量知足，所謂一針一草，當知來處不易，應當要節量知足，不可過於貪求，否則積聚不捨，則心被惑亂，妨礙道業也，故不可不慎。

Commentary: The passage above explained the first condition, precept-observance — for precepts are the very basis of bodhi, and one who wishes to cultivate calming and insight must, above all, keep the precepts purely. The second condition is sufficiency of clothing and food: clothing and food are the provisions that aid the path. If clothing and food are insufficient, one is left bare and hungry, unable to settle — how then could one cultivate practice at all? As it is said, when the body is at ease, the path flourishes; and again, before the wheel of Dharma turns, the wheel of food must turn first. From this we know that clothing and food are what aid practice along the path. Yet clothing and food should never be sought in excess or luxury — luxury only increases desire, and becomes, on the contrary, a cause obstructing the path. Clothing and food each have three grades, dividing practitioners into three levels of capacity.

First, the three grades of clothing. The first is like the Great Snow-Mountain Ascetic — that is, Śākyamuni Buddha himself, who, for the sake of saving beings, practiced austerities in the Snow Mountains for six years... When the Great Snow-Mountain Ascetic practiced austerities, whatever single garment he happened to obtain, sufficient to cover the body, was enough for him... entirely through the power of his own endurance that this was accomplished. One who wears clothing in this manner is called of upper capacity.

The second is like Kāśyapa, who constantly practiced the dhutaṅga observances... the Venerable Kāśyapa... The dhutaṅga practices number twelve in all... There are two kinds of robe: the first, the so-called "discarded-rag robe"... the second, simply the three robes... The Venerable Kāśyapa kept only these three robes, and was able to practice the path on this alone — this is the clothing worn by the second, middle-capacity practitioner.

The third concerns lands with much cold... China, situated in a cold region... the Tathāgata specially opened this expedient, permitting, beyond the three robes, up to a hundred and one further items to be kept... but these must be formally consecrated as offered to the community. This method of consecration... means offering the item to the assembly of the Three Jewels, requesting that it be compassionately received; having already made the resolve to offer it to the assembly, it becomes something bestowed by the assembly, no longer one's own personal possession — this is what is meant by "consecrated." Without doing so, it remains one's own, and is thereby called impure. And one must, moreover, know moderation and be content — even a single needle or blade of grass did not come easily, and one ought to be moderate and content, never seeking to excess; otherwise, accumulating without letting go, the mind becomes confused and disturbed, obstructing one's practice along the path — hence one cannot be too careful about this.

Food, in Three (or Four) Grades 食法三品

Chinese Source

〖文〗次食法有四種：一者若上人大士，深山絕世，草果隨時，得資身者。二者常行頭陀受乞食法，是乞食法，能破四種邪命，依正命自活，能生聖道故。邪命自活者，（一）下口食，（二）仰口食，（三）維口食，（四）方口食。邪命之相，如舍利弗為青目女說。三者阿蘭若處，檀越送食。四者，於僧中潔淨食。有此等食緣具足，名衣食具足。何以故？無此等緣，則心不安隱，於道有妨。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: As for food, there are four kinds. The first: for a person of superior capacity, a great being, who dwells deep in the mountains, cut off from the world, sustaining the body on whatever wild grasses and fruit happen to be at hand. The second: constantly practicing the dhutaṅga observance of accepting alms-food. This method of alms-begging is able to break the four kinds of wrong livelihood, sustaining life instead through right livelihood, which is able to give rise to the noble path. The four kinds of wrong livelihood are: mouth facing down, mouth facing up, mouth facing every direction, and mouth facing the four quarters. The marks of wrong livelihood are as Śāriputra explained them to the woman Qingmu. The third: dwelling in an araṇya, receiving food sent by a donor. The fourth: taking clean food within the community of monks. Having these conditions of food in place is called sufficiency of clothing and food. Why is this? Without these conditions, the mind has no peace or stability, and this becomes an obstruction to the path.

〖講〗上來所言衣，有上中下之三種不同。其次明食法，則有四種之各異：一者上根食，若上根利智之人，謂之上人，菩薩謂之大士，而不願與人相處，樂居山巖石窟，水邊林下而住，故曰深山絕世，而不作飲食……而上根人，則以草果松葉代食，如世尊苦行時，並不作食，日吃一麻一麥，以資身體，此為上根人，食上食也。二者常行頭陀受乞食法……乞食能破四種邪命……邪命者：（一）下口食，以口向下，如種田耕地，而謀生活，此名下口邪命食。（二）仰口食，即仰觀星宿，專言天文……（三）者維口食，想種種方法，維持生計，或結交豪富，或走遍四方，到處謀食，故名維口食。（四）者方口食，遊行四方，如卜算吉凶，醫藥看相，悉稱為方口食。出家比丘，責任在弘揚佛法，教化眾生，為自己之本職。所謂弘法是家務，度生為事業，邪命之食，須遠棄之。三者阿蘭若處……前來二種，為上中二根之食，此為第三住清淨蘭若，自不作食，惟受檀越送食……此為下品食。四者於僧中潔淨食，謂於叢林稠人廣眾之中，肅供齋法，謂之潔淨之食，以此一缽之飯，供養十方三寶，乃至施大鵬之鳥，曠野鬼神，然後進食此飯，已屬供養所遺，非己所有，如是則無我相矣，故稱之為淨食，此為下下品食。住於叢林之下，所謂十指不黏水，百事不操心，筷來伸手，飯來開口，有此等食緣具足，名衣食具足。何以故？下徵起，倘若無此等食緣，則心不能安隱，則不能修道，於道業有所妨礙，所謂身安則道隆，故衣食具足最為重要耳。

Commentary: The passage above concerned clothing, which has three differing grades. Next, food, which has four distinct kinds.

The first is food for one of upper capacity — a person of sharp faculties and superior capacity is called a "superior person," or, if a bodhisattva, a "great being," who has no wish to dwell among others, preferring instead to live in mountain caves or by streams and forests, hence "dwelling deep in the mountains, cut off from the world," and does not prepare food at all... A person of this upper capacity subsists instead on wild grasses, fruit, and pine needles — just as the World-Honored One, during his own austerities, prepared no food at all, sustaining his body each day on a single hemp seed and grain of wheat. This is the upper-capacity person's own "superior food."

The second is constantly practicing the dhutaṅga observance of accepting alms-food... Alms-begging is able to break the four kinds of wrong livelihood... The kinds of wrong livelihood are: (1) mouth facing down — facing the mouth downward, as in farming the fields to make a living, called wrong livelihood by "mouth facing down." (2) Mouth facing up — gazing upward at the stars, speaking authoritatively of astronomy... (3) Mouth facing every direction — scheming in every possible way to maintain a livelihood, whether by cultivating connections with the wealthy and powerful or traveling the four directions seeking a living wherever it can be found, hence "mouth facing every direction." (4) Mouth facing the four quarters — wandering the four quarters, telling fortunes, practicing medicine, or reading physiognomy, all called "mouth facing the four quarters." A monk's own responsibility is to spread the Buddhadharma and teach sentient beings — that is one's proper vocation. As it is said, spreading the Dharma is one's household duty, and saving beings is one's true occupation; the food of wrong livelihood must be cast far away.

The third is dwelling in an araṇya... The two kinds above are food for the upper and middle capacities; this third is dwelling in a pure araṇya, preparing no food oneself, receiving only what a donor sends... this is lower-grade food. The fourth is taking clean food within the community of monks — that is, within the great assembly of a monastic community, food solemnly offered according to the proper method, called "clean food": this single bowl of rice is offered to the Three Jewels of the ten directions, and even given to the great garuḍa bird and to the spirits of the wilderness, and only afterward is this rice itself eaten — already something left over from an offering, no longer one's own possession, and so there is, in this, no mark of self at all; hence it is called clean food, the lowest of the lower grades. Dwelling within such a community, as it is said, "ten fingers never touch water, a hundred concerns never trouble the mind" — the chopsticks come to the hand, the food comes to the mouth.

Having these conditions of food in place is called sufficiency of clothing and food. Why is this — the text now asks, taking up the question itself. Without these conditions of food in place, the mind cannot be at peace and settled, and one is thereby unable to cultivate the path, an obstruction arising for one's own practice along it. As it is said, when the body is at ease, the path flourishes — hence sufficiency of clothing and food matters more than almost anything else.

Reading note: The three-grade structure for clothing and four-grade structure for food are graded by self-sufficiency, not by comfort — the "highest" practitioner needs nothing from anyone at all, while the "lowest" grade (clean food eaten last, after even the birds and wilderness spirits have been offered a share) is still perfectly workable, just more socially embedded. Baojing's own aside on the four kinds of wrong livelihood is worth noting for how bluntly contemporary he makes it: farming, weather-forecasting, networking with the wealthy, and fortune-telling are all named as unfit means of support for a monk specifically because they compete with — rather than support — the actual vocation of teaching the Dharma, not because the activities themselves are inherently wrong.

III. A Quiet Dwelling Place 閑居靜處

Chinese Source

〖文〗第三得閒居靜處。閒者：不作眾事，名之為閒；無憒鬧故，名之為靜；有三處可修禪定。一者深山絕人之處。二者頭陀蘭若之處，離於聚落極近三四里，此則放牧聲絕，無諸憒鬧。三者遠白衣住處，清淨伽藍中，皆名閒居靜處。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The third condition is obtaining a quiet, secluded dwelling place. "Secluded" means engaging in no worldly affairs; "quiet" means free of clamor and disturbance. There are three kinds of place suited to cultivating dhyāna-concentration. The first: deep in the mountains, cut off from all people. The second: a dhutaṅga-style araṇya, kept at the very least three or four li from any settlement, so that the sounds of herding and grazing cannot reach it, and it is free of every disturbance. The third: at a remove from where lay people dwell, within a pure monastic community — this too is called a quiet, secluded dwelling place.

〖講〗上來明具五緣中衣食二種已竟，今講靜住之處。閒居靜處，即阿蘭若處，所謂水邊林下，山巖石窟，獨自用功，謂境空則心易空。閒謂清閒，即不與世間一切有為之法相應。無憒鬧者，即遠離一切塵勞煩惱，名之為寂靜處。約有三種處所，可修禪定。一者深山絕人之處。一心不亂則為定，若身坐蘭若，而心猿意馬，則雖坐深山而仍同城市也。而修行者必至空閒處者，因初心人，離憒鬧之環境，易得益也。一者遠居於深林高山之中，衣食不求於人，亦無喧噪……如世尊之雪山苦行，即是絕人之處，此為上根人閒居靜處。二者頭陀蘭若之處：頭陀有十二種，已如上說；蘭若之處，即是清閒之所，離於村莊聚落，極近者三四里或十里二十里不定，如是則放牛聲絕，放羊牧牛之童，亦難至此，牧童樵夫所不能到，雞鳴狗吠之聲遠絕，故云無諸憒鬧，此名為中根人閒居靜處。如往昔佛在世時，有五比丘，住山修行，每於飯間，則托缽至村莊乞化，但往返百里之遙，終日無非行路而已，如是行者，十有餘年，而功夫毫無見效，遂大生慚愧，世尊悲而憫之，乃化一道人送供，並告之曰：汝等懃懇不輟，甚為難得，但隔人村路遙，光陰有限，有時成功，應須擇一不近不遠之地而棲住，即可用功，可免徒勞無益之虞矣。三者遠白衣住處：第三種住於叢林寺院之中，終日隨眾起倒，亦甚相應，但須遠去白衣。白衣者，即在家之通稱。僧伽所著乃緇衣，即黑色衣，不與俗人相同。清淨伽藍中，伽藍乃梵語略稱，具云僧伽藍婆，此云和合眾，為大眾聚會之處，清淨伽藍之中，皆名閒居靜處。探珠宜浪靜，水清則珠現，此一定之理，若終日塵勞煩惱境界風所動，欲求自性之清淨實相寶珠顯現在前，無有是處。

Commentary: Having now finished the two conditions of clothing and food among the five, we turn to the place of quiet dwelling. A quiet, secluded dwelling place is precisely an araṇya — beside a stream, beneath a forest, in a mountain cave, practicing alone, since an empty surroundings make an empty mind easier to attain. "Secluded" means unoccupied, that is, not engaged with any conditioned worldly affair at all. "Free of clamor" means far removed from every kind of dusty toil and affliction — this is called a place of stillness. There are, roughly, three such places suited to cultivating dhyāna-concentration.

The first, deep in the mountains, cut off from all people. It is one-pointedness of mind, undistracted, that makes concentration — if one sits in an araṇya with a mind like a monkey or a galloping horse, then even sitting deep in the mountains is no different from sitting in the middle of a city. And it is precisely because a beginning practitioner benefits so much more easily by leaving behind a clamorous environment that a practitioner must go to a quiet, unoccupied place. One dwells far off, deep within forest and high mountain, seeking neither clothing nor food from others, with no clamor at all... as when the World-Honored One practiced austerities in the Snow Mountains — this too was a place cut off from people. This is the quiet dwelling place for one of upper capacity.

The second, a dhutaṅga-style araṇya: the dhutaṅga practices number twelve, as already explained above; an araṇya is precisely a place of quiet leisure, removed from any village or settlement — at the very least three or four li away, or as much as ten or twenty li, not fixed — so that the sounds of cattle-herding cannot be heard there, and even shepherd children cannot easily reach it, unreachable by herd-boy or woodcutter, with the crowing of roosters and barking of dogs entirely out of earshot — hence it is said to be free of every disturbance. This is the quiet dwelling place for one of middle capacity.

Long ago, in the Buddha's own time, there were five monks who dwelt on a mountain to practice; at each mealtime they would take their alms-bowls to the village to beg for food, but the round trip was a hundred li, and the entire day was spent doing nothing but walking. Practicing this way for over ten years, they made no progress whatsoever, and grew deeply ashamed. The World-Honored One, moved by compassion for them, transformed himself into a wandering teacher who brought them offerings, and told them: "That you have persevered so diligently without pause is truly rare and admirable — but with the village so far removed by road, and time so limited, you will succeed far sooner if you choose a place neither too near nor too far, and dwell there instead — you can then put your effort to use, and be spared this wasted, fruitless toil."

The third, at a remove from where lay people dwell: the third kind is dwelling within a monastic community, following the assembly's own daily rhythm — this too accords well enough with practice, but one must still keep a remove from lay people. "Lay people" is the general term for householders; what the saṅgha wears is the black robe, distinct from ordinary lay dress. Within a pure monastic community, "saṃghārāma" being the Sanskrit term abbreviated, in full saṃghārāma, meaning "the harmonious assembly," the place where the great assembly gathers together — within such a pure monastic community, too, is called a quiet, secluded dwelling place. Fishing for a pearl requires calm waves; only when the water is clear does the pearl appear — this is simply how it is. If one is, day after day, stirred by the winds of dusty toil and affliction, wishing for the jewel of one's own true, pure, real nature to appear before one is, in that condition, simply impossible.

Reading note: The three grades here track the same upper/middle/lower structure as clothing and food, but Baojing's own commentary adds something the root text alone does not: the story of the five monks who nearly wasted a decade walking a hundred li round-trip for alms because they picked a dwelling too far from any village, told immediately after the "middle capacity" grade. It reframes the whole three-grade scheme as practical, not merely hierarchical — the "right" distance from a settlement is a matter of sober planning, not simply choosing the most extreme option available.

IV. Setting Worldly Affairs Aside 息諸緣務

Chinese Source

〖文〗第四息諸緣務。有四意：一息治生緣務，不作有為事業。二息人間緣務，不追尋俗人朋友親戚知識，斷絕人事往還。三息工巧技術緣務，不作世間工匠技術醫方禁咒卜相書數算計等事。四息學問緣務，讀誦聽學等悉皆棄捨。此為息諸緣務。所以者何？若多緣務，則行道事廢，心亂難攝。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The fourth condition is setting all worldly affairs aside. This has four aspects. First, setting aside affairs of livelihood — not taking up any conditioned worldly occupation. Second, setting aside affairs of ordinary human relations — not pursuing friendships, relatives, or acquaintances among lay people, cutting off social exchange with them. Third, setting aside affairs of craft and skill — not taking up worldly trades, crafts, medicine, incantations, fortune-telling, physiognomy, writing, or calculation. Fourth, setting aside affairs of scholarship — reading, reciting, and studying are all likewise set aside entirely. This is what is meant by setting all worldly affairs aside. Why is this necessary? Because if one is burdened with many such affairs, one's practice along the path is neglected, and the mind, thrown into confusion, becomes difficult to gather in.

〖講〗上來三緣已具，第四須將世緣俗務摒棄之，方能進修道業。若身雖住茅蓬，或當家，或做事，仍是忙忙碌碌，所謂有名無實，亦無益也。如香港山頂所住之洋房高樓，宛然阿蘭若，但彼等終日做生貿易，忙碌不休，或為生活逼迫，或為事務纏擾，所謂山林中塵市；若於人煙稠密之中，息諸緣務，則為塵市之山林。息緣務有四意：一息治生緣務，不作有為事業，如世之士農工商等，悉謂之治生緣務，皆屬有作有為之事業。修止觀者，當遠離有為事業，並及息滅治生緣務。二息人間緣務，前者對己而言，此是對他而論。不追者，不與之往來相逐也。接交朋友，來往親戚，本為世法所不離，若修行之人，則不然。了達人事本空，生死事大，結交應酬，皆為障道因緣，故須拒絕斷除。第三息工巧技術緣務，謂不作世間工匠技術，及學拳術，研究機械等悉名技術；或作良醫，治病開方；或念禁咒，療人之病，此乃是邪咒，非佛教之真言；或卜卦看相，或書寫算盤等事，悉能妨礙道業，在家人為謀生活起見，則宜為之，若學佛人則萬萬不可為也，若作此種緣務，心則為之散亂，欲望其專一，則甚難矣。是故欲修止觀，須息滅世間緣務，古人所謂放得下一事，則能消滅一分妄想。又所謂消除一分妄想，得一分本智，除滅一分境界，證一分法身，謂明生則暗滅，暗生則明滅，明暗之不能相即，如水火之不相容，故須遠離之。第四息學問緣務，學問本為長知識，博見聞，莫不由學問而來，然既學佛，則遠棄世間學問，所謂捨棄詩賦文章，科哲學說世間學問，一概放捨，不僅如此，且欲放棄出世經典，謂所讀誦之經論，或聽學之經論，悉皆棄捨。夫世間學問，乃有漏之法，捨之故然；而出世學問，乃出世無漏無為之法，詮究竟之理，何故亦置之不顧耶？蓋今乃注重在修止觀，不重見聞，故須棄之，但於見解未明之先，必須聽經明理，所謂見解開而修行易，可免趨入歧途之歎，待開解後，即將一切見解，悉皆棄捨，從此起行用功，則可免說食數寶之嫌。倘若一味執著文言名相，不肯起行，真實用功，則終難免入海算沙之譏，謂畫餅無益於飢腸也。故禪宗之打七時，不誦經，不禮拜，唯是照顧話頭，單刀直入者，即免多緣務之患也。所以者何，是疑問之辭。若多諸緣務，則心為之纏繞，行道之事廢棄，謂解多心亦多，難以收攝故也。當知前所言讀誦聽經學經，悉皆棄捨，切勿誤會，因入道法門，雖有八萬四千，皆各有正行助行，若讀誦法華經，亦可謂之修止觀，是則以法華為正，餘者為助。今既以止觀為正行，則其餘諸法悉皆為助行矣，但以專一不雜為美，切不可三舊兩新，是為至要。

Commentary: With the three conditions above now in place, the fourth requires setting worldly affairs aside entirely, so that one's practice can actually move forward. If, though physically dwelling in a thatched hut, one is still managing a household or handling business, one remains just as busy and occupied as ever — what is called "a name without the substance," of no real benefit. Even the mansions on the Peak in Hong Kong might look, on the surface, exactly like an araṇya, yet if their occupants spend their days doing business, endlessly busy, whether driven by the pressure of livelihood or entangled in affairs, this is "a city amid the mountains and forest." Conversely, if one can set worldly affairs aside even in the midst of a crowded city, this is "mountain and forest amid the city."

Setting worldly affairs aside has four aspects. First, setting aside affairs of livelihood — not taking up any conditioned occupation, such as scholar, farmer, artisan, or merchant, every such thing being called an affair of livelihood, all belonging to conditioned, effortful occupation. One who cultivates calming and insight should keep far from such conditioned occupations, and set aside altogether any affair of livelihood.

Second, setting aside affairs of ordinary human relations — the first concerned oneself, this one concerns others. "Not pursuing" means not going back and forth chasing after them. Making friends and visiting relatives is, ordinarily, inseparable from worldly life, but for a practitioner it is not so: understanding that human affairs are fundamentally empty, and that the great matter of birth-and-death presses, social exchange and obligation all become causes obstructing the path, and so must be firmly declined and cut off.

Third, setting aside affairs of craft and skill — that is, not taking up worldly trades and crafts, whether martial arts, mechanical study, or anything else called a "skill"; or acting as a physician, treating illness and prescribing remedies; or reciting incantations to heal others' sickness — these being deviant spells, not the true mantras of Buddhism; or fortune-telling, physiognomy, calligraphy, or arithmetic — all such things are capable of obstructing one's practice along the path. A lay person may reasonably take these up for the sake of a livelihood, but for one who studies the Buddhadharma, they are absolutely not to be undertaken; taking up affairs of this kind scatters the mind, making singleness of purpose extremely difficult to achieve. This is why one who wishes to cultivate calming and insight must set worldly affairs aside — as the ancients said, letting go of even one single thing dissolves that much idle thought; and again, dissolving one portion of idle thought gains one portion of one's own fundamental wisdom, removing one portion of the worldly realm one clings to realizes one portion of the Dharma-body — for where brightness arises, darkness is dispelled, and where darkness arises, brightness is dispelled; brightness and darkness cannot coincide, any more than water and fire can mix — hence such affairs must be kept at a distance.

Fourth, setting aside affairs of scholarship — scholarship is, in itself, meant to expand knowledge and broaden what one has seen and heard, and there is nothing that does not come from scholarship in some form. Yet having already taken up the study of the Buddhadharma, one sets worldly scholarship far aside — abandoning poetry, belles-lettres, the various worldly sciences and philosophies, casting them all away entirely. And beyond even this, one should set aside even transmundane scriptures — meaning that whatever sūtras and treatises one reads, recites, or has studied by hearing them taught, all of these too are set aside. Worldly scholarship is a conditioned, outflow-bound dharma, and so is naturally to be set aside — but transmundane scholarship is itself an unconditioned, outflow-free dharma, expounding the ultimate principle: why, then, should this too be disregarded?

Because the present concern is specifically cultivating calming and insight, not accumulating what has been seen and heard, and so it too must be set aside. But before one's own understanding is yet clear, one must still listen to sūtras and clarify their principle — as it is said, once understanding opens up, practice becomes easy, sparing one the regret of straying onto a wrong path; and once understanding has opened up, one then sets every last view aside, and from there takes up genuine effort in practice — this spares one the reproach of merely describing food, or counting another's treasure. But should one instead cling single-mindedly to literary phrases and conceptual distinctions, unwilling to take up genuine effort in practice, one can hardly avoid the mockery of counting grains of sand in the ocean — a painted cake, after all, does the hungry stomach no good. This is why, during a Chan meditation retreat, no sūtras are recited and no prostrations performed — only watching the meditation topic itself, going straight in with a single blade — precisely to be spared the trouble of too many such affairs.

"Why is this necessary?" is a question raised to introduce the reason: if one is burdened with many such affairs, the mind becomes entangled by them, and one's practice along the path is neglected — for the more one has to work out, the more scattered the mind becomes, and it grows difficult to gather back in. One should know, however, that what was said above — setting aside reading, reciting, listening to, and studying sūtras — must not be misunderstood: for although the gates into the path number eighty-four thousand, each one has its own primary practice and its own supporting practices. If one reads and recites the Lotus Sūtra, this too can be called cultivating calming and insight — in that case, the Lotus Sūtra itself is the primary practice, and everything else becomes supporting practice. Here, since calming and insight is already taken as the primary practice, every other dharma becomes, accordingly, a supporting practice — but singleness of purpose, without mixture, is what matters most; one must, above all, never chop and change between old and new. This is the essential point.

Reading note: Baojing's own image of "a city amid the mountains and forest" versus "mountain and forest amid the city" reframes this whole condition as a matter of mind rather than geography — a point his closing paragraph makes explicit in a different way, when he clarifies that "setting aside scholarship" does not actually forbid listening to Dharma teaching, only forbids treating that listening as a substitute for practice once understanding has already been reached. His own metaphor for the failure mode — "describing food," "counting another's treasure," a "painted cake" that does not feed a hungry stomach — is a running theme across this commentary's several chapters, and worth remembering as Baojing's own recurring diagnosis of merely intellectual Buddhist study.

V. Staying Close to Good Spiritual Friends 近善知識

Chinese Source

〖文〗第五近善知識。善知識有三：一外護善知識，經營供養，善能將護行人，不相惱亂。二者同行善知識，共修一道，互相勸發，不相擾亂。三者教授善知識，以內外方便禪定法門，示教利喜。略明五種緣務竟。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The fifth condition is staying close to a good spiritual friend. Good spiritual friends are of three kinds. The first is the protecting friend, who manages and provides material support, skillfully looking after the practitioner without causing any disturbance. The second is the fellow-practitioner, who cultivates the same path together with one, mutually encouraging and inspiring one another, without disturbing one another. The third is the teaching friend, who instructs one in the inward and outward expedient means of the gate of dhyāna-concentration, showing, teaching, benefiting, and delighting one. This concludes the brief explanation of the five conditions.

〖講〗前來四緣已竟，此為第五緣近善知識，佛言善知識為得道之全因緣。所言知識者，謂善知一切眾生病，善識一切眾生藥。所謂知病識藥，應病與藥，如阿闍世王，遇耆婆之善知識，勸令皈佛，得解脫痛苦，如嬰兒之須賴傍於母，故善知識者，不可須臾離也。善知識有三種：一外護善知識，謂善能於外面擁護之成就之，修行辦道者，如禪宗外護，照應衣食等等。言外護者，於其曾用過功夫，故能盡心守護，無所缺乏，如虎之愛子然，又能種種供養，故謂經營，且善巧能將養愛護，行人自不惱亂，亦不使外人惱亂，故曰不相惱亂，此明外護善知識。次明同行者，謂同參同時修行，共同一種工夫，共修一道，彼此共修止觀，並且互相助發，互相砥礪，互相警策，互相規勉，所謂如切如磋，如琢如磨，同舟共濟之義。如古云：求師不如訪友，誠哉是言。三教授善知識，教謂教導，授者與也，即是教導傳授之善知識，以善巧方便，教之調伏內外身心，禪定法門，曉示教訓，使行者得大利益，如破五陰，超五濁，及一切魔境，悉能圓明了達，如智者大師，妙悟法華，南嶽大師謂非汝莫證，非吾莫識，此即善知識之益處。所謂欲知山下路，當問過來人。當知不修止觀則已，若修止觀，善知識為最緊要。略明五種緣務竟。

Commentary: The four conditions above now complete, this is the fifth condition, staying close to a good spiritual friend. The Buddha said that a good spiritual friend is the whole cause and condition for attaining the path. What is called "knowing" (知識) here means skillfully knowing every sentient being's own sickness, and skillfully knowing the medicine for it — knowing the sickness, recognizing the medicine, prescribing according to the sickness. Just as King Ajātaśatru, meeting the good spiritual friend Jīvaka, was urged to take refuge in the Buddha and thereby freed from his own suffering — as an infant depends utterly on its mother's side — so too a good spiritual friend cannot be left, even for a moment.

Good spiritual friends are of three kinds. The first, the protecting friend, means one who is skillfully able to protect and support the practitioner from without, bringing their practice to completion — as in Chan tradition's own external protectors, who look after clothing and food and the like. "Protecting from without" means one who has already put in the effort of practice themselves, and so is able to guard the practitioner wholeheartedly, leaving nothing lacking, cherishing them as a tiger cherishes its cub, and providing every kind of support — hence "managing" — and, skillfully caring for and cherishing them, ensures the practitioner is disturbed neither by themselves nor by others — hence "without causing any disturbance." This explains the protecting friend.

Next, the fellow-practitioner: one who studies and practices together at the same time, sharing the same effort, cultivating the same path together, jointly cultivating calming and insight, mutually helping and inspiring one another, mutually honing and encouraging, mutually admonishing and exhorting one another — as it is said, "as if cut, as if filed, as if carved, as if polished," sharing the same boat to cross the same river together. As the ancients said, "seeking a teacher is not as good as seeking a friend" — truly so.

Third, the teaching friend: "teach" means to instruct, "instructing" means to give — this is the good spiritual friend who instructs and transmits, using skillful means to teach one how to regulate body and mind, inward and outward alike, and the gate of dhyāna-concentration itself, showing, teaching, and admonishing, so that the practitioner attains great benefit — breaking through the five aggregates, transcending the five turbidities, and every demonic state, all thoroughly understood in full. Just as Zhiyi, having wondrously awakened to the Lotus Sūtra, was told by Master Nanyue, "None but you could have realized it; none but I could have recognized it" — this is precisely the benefit of a good spiritual friend. As it is said, if you wish to know the road down the mountain, you must ask someone who has already walked it.

One should know: if one does not cultivate calming and insight at all, this is one matter — but if one does cultivate it, a good spiritual friend is the single most essential thing of all. This concludes the brief explanation of the five conditions.

Reading note: This closes chapter 1's own fivefold structure, and Baojing's choice of closing example is pointed: he reaches for Zhiyi's own relationship to his teacher Nanyue Huisi — the same relationship the Shì Chán Bōluómì Cìdì Fǎmén project's own account of Zhiyi's biography describes from the other side — to illustrate the "teaching friend" specifically, rather than any more abstract example. The threefold division itself (protecting, fellow-practicing, and teaching friend) is a fixed classification worth carrying forward, since later chapters may well return to it when Baojing discusses obstacles a practitioner cannot recognize or resolve alone.
