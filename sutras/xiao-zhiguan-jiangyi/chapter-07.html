Chapter 7 · 善根發第七 · Complete

The Arising of Wholesome Roots

Sustained practice eventually produces real signs of progress — but Baojing's own warning runs through this whole chapter: genuine and demonic signs can look identical from the inside, and the difference is not always obvious. Five kinds of wholesome root, how to tell each one's genuine form from its counterfeit, and how calming and insight themselves nurture what has already begun to arise.

Status: complete Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: book.bfnn.org Covers: outward and inward wholesome roots, distinguishing genuine from false, and nurturing them further

The General Principle 總明善根發之要

Chinese Source

〖文〗行者若能如是從假入空觀中善修止觀者，則於坐中身心明淨，爾時當有種種善根開發，應須識知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: If a practitioner is able to cultivate calming and insight skillfully in this way, through the contemplation moving from the conventional into emptiness, then, while seated, body and mind become clear and pure — and at that time, various wholesome roots will begin to arise, which one must be able to recognize.

〖講〗此章由前正修章來，因正修止觀於功用中，發動夙世種種善根，如不修行，則諸善之根，無由開發。行者於此種善根相開發之時，須一一識之……行者善能如是修習從假入空觀者，則於安坐功用中得身心明淨，此時當有種種善根開發之相，須明識之，不可忽略也。

Commentary: This chapter follows on from the actual-practice chapter above, since cultivating calming and insight in earnest effort stirs into activity the various wholesome roots planted in past lives — without actual practice, these wholesome roots would have no way to arise at all. When a wholesome root of this kind begins to arise, a practitioner must be able to recognize it, one by one... A practitioner who is genuinely able to cultivate the contemplation moving from the conventional into emptiness in this way attains, through the very effort of sitting settled, a body and mind that are clear and pure — and at this time, various signs of wholesome roots arising should appear, which must be clearly recognized and never overlooked.

Outward Wholesome Roots 外善根發相

Chinese Source

〖文〗今略明善根發相，有二種不同：一外善根發相，所謂布施、持戒、孝順父母尊長。供養三寶、及諸聽學等善根開發，此是外事。若非正修，與魔境相濫，今不分別。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Now, briefly explaining the mark of wholesome roots arising, there are two distinct kinds. The first is the mark of an outward wholesome root arising — such as the wholesome root of generosity, precept-observance, filial devotion to parents and elders, offerings to the Three Jewels, and listening to and studying the Dharma, all beginning to arise. These are outward matters. Since they are not the direct fruit of actual practice, and can be confused with demonic states, they are not further distinguished here.

〖講〗此下文中自分科解釋，今略明善根發相有二種不同。明善根發相雖多，略明不出二種：一外善根發相，二內善根發相，善根雖多，內外二種，收攝殆盡。言外善根發相者，如於坐中修習止觀時，忽於心中發一種歡喜布施之心，或欲於財物施人，或以修行之法說與人聽，或勢力助人，施以無畏，此即發布施善根之相。由夙世曾行布施故，或忽發心歡喜持戒……此即夙世有持戒善根。今因修習止觀，發動持戒之夙根，如是或發孝順父母尊重師長之善根，或發燒香散花，供養三寶等之善根，及其他聽經學教禮拜等種種善根之相開發，此屬外表一切事相法門，所發之善根，是良由宿世薰修之習。由內心中開發，與用功無妨也。

Commentary: What follows below now divides into sections to explain this. Briefly, the mark of wholesome roots arising is of two distinct kinds. However many wholesome roots there are, briefly they do not exceed two: the mark of an outward wholesome root arising, and the mark of an inward wholesome root arising — between these two, outward and inward, every wholesome root is essentially encompassed.

As for the mark of an outward wholesome root arising: while cultivating calming and insight during a sitting, a joyful heart of generosity may suddenly arise in the mind — wishing to give material goods to others, or to teach the method of practice to others, or to help others with one's own strength, giving them freedom from fear. This is the mark of the wholesome root of generosity arising, since generosity was practiced in a past life. Or a joyful resolve to keep the precepts may suddenly arise... this is because a wholesome root of precept-observance already exists from a past life, now stirred into activity through the cultivation of calming and insight. In the same way, a wholesome root of filial devotion to parents and reverence for teachers may arise, or a wholesome root of burning incense and scattering flowers in offering to the Three Jewels, or the various wholesome roots of listening to sūtras, studying the teaching, bowing, and the rest. These all belong to the realm of outward, ritual-form dharma-gates — the wholesome roots that arise here come precisely from habits cultivated in past lives, opening up from within the mind, and pose no hindrance to one's own effort in practice.

Inward Wholesome Roots 內善根發相

Baojing structures this section, by far the longest in the chapter, in three parts: the five kinds of inward wholesome root as they arise, distinguishing the genuine from the demonic counterfeit, and nurturing what has arisen through calming and insight.

1. Five Kinds of Inward Wholesome Root 善根發相五種

i. The Breath-Method 息道善根發相

Chinese Source

〖文〗二內善根發相，所謂諸禪定法門善根開發，有三種意。第一明善根發相，有五種不同：一息道善根發相。行者善修止觀故，身心調適，妄念止息，因是自覺其心，漸漸入定，發於欲界及未到地等定，身心泯然空寂，定心安隱，於此定中，都不見有身心相貌。於後或經一坐二坐，乃至一日二日、一月二月將息，不得、不退、不失、即於定中忽覺身心運動八觸而發者，所謂覺身痛痒冷煖輕重澀滑等；當觸法時，身心安定，虛微悅豫，快樂清淨，不可為喻。是為知息道根本禪定善根發相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Second, the mark of an inward wholesome root arising — that is, the wholesome roots of the various dharma-gates of dhyāna-concentration opening up. This has three aspects; the first explains the mark of wholesome roots arising, of which there are five distinct kinds. The first is the mark of the breath-method wholesome root arising. Because a practitioner skillfully cultivates calming and insight, body and mind become properly regulated, idle thought comes to rest, and, through this, one becomes aware of one's own mind gradually entering concentration, giving rise to desire-realm concentration and access concentration — body and mind dissolving into empty stillness, the settled mind at peace, and within this concentration one no longer perceives any appearance of body or mind at all. Afterward, whether after one or two sittings, or even one or two days, one or two months, this state, neither lost nor regressing, may suddenly give rise, within concentration, to a sensation of body and mind stirred by the eight sensations — that is, feeling the body as painful, itchy, cold, warm, light, heavy, rough, or smooth. When these sensations occur, body and mind become settled, subtly joyful, blissful and pure beyond any comparison. This is the mark of the fundamental dhyāna wholesome root of the breath-method arising.

〖講〗第二明內心之中，宿世善根，發宿修諸禪定之薰習善根。今因修止觀故，於內心中開發，亦有三種意：第一明善根發相，第二分別真偽，第三止觀長養……一明息道善根發相者。行者善修止觀故，端身正坐，調和氣息，即調鼻息也，令其身心安隱，麤浮之念不起，以不起故，自覺其心，漸漸入於禪定，或發於欲界定及未到地等定……若修習止觀由數息故，無諸妄念。泯然澄靜，身體猶如雲影，虛豁清淨，冏冏光明，然見有身心之相，雖未忘身心之相，卻如身同浮雲，心若微影，內外輕安，是名欲界定相。從是欲界定相，後復加功用行，更進一步，至此一心凝然湛寂，泯然一轉，所謂以有相轉成無相，於中忽然虛徹，不見有身首床眠，如太虛空，寂然安隱，不見有身世依正，此時身相頓空，事障已空故也，而其煩惱性障猶在……今事障已去，則自空寂，欲界垢染色境俱空，未來禪定得發，而理性之障未去，尚未能入初禪根本定，故未到定相。當知念佛參禪者，亦能得此未到定境界，但須識之，不可執著。每見闇禪愚迷之流，遇到此外妄世界，內空身心之境界，便認為威音那畔，空劫以前之事，執為究竟之功夫，從此生安隱想，生究竟想，廢修道業，唐喪光陰，豈不可悲憫……然得此定時，時間有長短不同，或經一枝香，或坐數小時，或竟夜或半日，乃至一日二日，或一月二月不定，以其有禪定力，資助身體，妄念不流動，雖經長久時間，於身體無損，但不得執於禪味，致礙前進，如此定不退不失，久而久之，即於定中當發八觸，或忽覺自身疼痛，或遍身發癢，或覺身寒冷如入冰水，或覺遍身發熱如火灸燒，或覺自身輕浮如鴻毛，或覺自身沈重如大石，或覺閉澀，或覺柔滑，當知此八種觸境，於禪定隨發一二不定，即非同時而發，亦非八觸全發。每見行者，因發八觸而退失功夫，行人不了，以為魔境發現，故退道心。當八觸發時，了達是定中一種境界，不為所動，身心安定。若八觸發過之時，身心即得寂然虛明，清淨快樂，不可以譬喻而比況之，以其禪定之樂，別有一種勝味，迥非人間五欲之樂可比也，是名息道。

Commentary: This second part explains how, within the inward mind, wholesome roots from past lives — the habitual traces of dhyāna-concentration cultivated in former lives — now open up through cultivating calming and insight in the present. This too has three aspects: first, explaining the mark of wholesome roots arising; second, distinguishing the genuine from the false; third, nurturing them through calming and insight.

First, explaining the mark of the breath-method wholesome root arising: because a practitioner skillfully cultivates calming and insight, sitting upright with body composed and the breath regulated — that is, regulating the breath through the nose — body and mind become settled, and coarse, floating thoughts fail to arise. Because they fail to arise, one becomes aware of one's own mind gradually entering dhyāna-concentration, giving rise to desire-realm concentration, access concentration, and the like... Through cultivating calming and insight by means of counting the breath, idle thought disappears entirely, the body dissolving into a state like the shadow of a cloud, empty and open, pure, luminously bright — yet one still perceives some appearance of body and mind; though this appearance is not yet entirely forgotten, the body feels like a floating cloud and the mind like a faint shadow, light and at ease both inwardly and outwardly. This is the mark of desire-realm concentration.

From this mark of desire-realm concentration, applying further effort still, one advances a further step, until the single mind congeals into deep stillness, and shifts entirely — that is, what had a mark turns into what is markless — and within this, suddenly, everything becomes utterly transparent: one no longer perceives any body, head, bed, or sleep, exactly like vast empty space, still and at peace, with no perception of any body, world, dependent or direct existence at all. At this point, the mark of the body is suddenly empty, since the obstruction of actual affliction is already emptied out — yet the underlying obstruction of afflictive nature still remains... Now that the obstruction of actual affliction is gone, one is empty and still of itself; the defiled, colored realm of the desire-world is entirely empty, and future dhyāna is able to arise — yet the obstruction of one's own underlying nature is not yet gone, and one is not yet able to enter the fundamental concentration of the first dhyāna itself — hence this is called access concentration.

One should know that those who recite the Buddha's name or practice Chan meditation are equally able to attain this state of access concentration — but one must recognize it for what it is, and never cling to it. One often sees confused, deluded practitioners who, encountering this state of an outwardly illusory world and an inwardly empty body and mind, immediately take it to be the state prior to the eon of emptiness, beyond even the sound of majesty itself, clinging to it as the ultimate achievement — from this, giving rise to a notion of having settled, a notion of finality, abandoning further practice on the path, wasting their own time — is this not deeply lamentable?

The duration of this concentration varies: sometimes lasting the burning of a single stick of incense, sometimes several hours, sometimes an entire night or half a day, even one or two days, or one or two months, not fixed — since the power of dhyāna-concentration sustains the body, idle thought does not flow, and even over a long duration, the body suffers no harm from it. But one must not cling to the flavor of this dhyāna itself, or it will obstruct further progress. When this concentration neither regresses nor is lost, in time, within concentration, the eight sensations will arise: one may suddenly feel pain throughout the body, or itching all over, or cold as though plunged into ice water, or heat as though scorched by fire, or lightness as though floating like a goose feather, or heaviness as though weighed down by a great stone, or a sense of blockage, or a sense of smoothness. One should know that these eight sensory states arise, one or two at a time, not fixed, not simultaneously, and not all eight at once.

One often sees practitioners lose their own hard-won progress because of these eight sensations arising, not understanding them, mistaking them for a demonic state appearing, and so retreating from their own resolve on the path. When the eight sensations arise, one should fully understand that this is simply one state within concentration, and remain unmoved by it, body and mind settled. Once the eight sensations have passed, body and mind attain a state of still, empty brightness, pure and blissful, beyond any comparison at all — for the joy of dhyāna-concentration carries a particular, superior flavor entirely incomparable to any of the five worldly pleasures of ordinary human life. This is called the breath-method.

Chinese Source

〖文〗行者或於欲界未到地中，忽然覺息出入長短，遍身毛孔皆悉虛疏，即以心眼見身內三十六物，猶如開倉見諸麻豆等，心大驚喜，寂靜安快，是為隨息特勝善根發相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: A practitioner, within desire-realm or access concentration, may suddenly become aware of the breath's own length as it passes in and out, and every pore of the body opening freely — at once seeing, with the mind's own eye, the thirty-six constituent parts within the body, exactly as if a storehouse had been opened to reveal the hemp and beans stored within. The mind is greatly astonished and delighted, still, settled, and at ease. This is the mark of the excellent breath-following wholesome root arising.

〖講〗根本禪，即色界之初二三四禪也，此四雖為世間禪定，而為一切禪之根本。行者於欲界未到定中，經過八觸之關頭，即另得一境界，忽覺鼻中之氣息出入長短，不特鼻中出入，而遍身八萬四千毛孔，皆悉虛豁入出，即以心眼觀見身內三十六物，猶如開倉，見諸麻豆，清楚分明……行者於未到定中，見諸三十六物，心大驚喜，謂從未曾見而今見，故大驚；他人所不能見，而我能見，故大喜，如是心中寂靜輕安，爽快異常，是謂隨息特勝善根發相。特勝，即十六特勝也，謂六事一一皆勝於四念處等諸禪觀也。始自調心，終至非想，地地皆有觀照，能發無漏善業，而無厭惡自害之失，故受特勝之名也。

Commentary: "Fundamental dhyāna" refers to the first through fourth dhyānas of the form realm — these four, though still worldly forms of concentration, are the very root of every kind of dhyāna. A practitioner, within access concentration, having passed through the threshold of the eight sensations, attains a further state: suddenly aware of the breath's own length passing in and out through the nose — and not only through the nose, but through all eighty-four thousand pores of the entire body, opening freely — one sees, with the mind's own eye, the thirty-six constituent parts within the body, exactly as if a storehouse had been opened to reveal the hemp and beans within it, utterly clear and distinct...

A practitioner, seeing these thirty-six parts within access concentration, is greatly astonished and delighted — astonished because one has never seen this before and now sees it; delighted because what others cannot see, one is oneself able to see — and in this way the mind becomes still, light, and at ease, refreshingly clear beyond the ordinary. This is called the mark of the excellent breath-following wholesome root arising. "Excellent" refers to the sixteen excellent practices — meaning that each of the six elements of this practice surpasses the four foundations of mindfulness and other forms of dhyāna-contemplation. Beginning with regulating the mind and extending all the way to the state of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, every single stage carries its own contemplative illumination, able to bring forth outflow-free wholesome karma without any fault of aversion or self-harm — hence it receives the name "excellent."

ii. The Contemplation of Impurity 不淨觀善根發相

Chinese Source

〖文〗二不淨觀善根發相。行者若於欲界未到地定，於此定中身心虛寂，忽然見他男女身死，死已臌脹爛壞，蟲膿流出，見白骨狼籍，其心悲喜，厭患所愛，此為九想善根發相。或於靜定之中，忽然見內身不淨，外身臌脹狼籍，自身白骨從頭至足，節節相柱，見是事已，定心安隱，驚悟無常，厭患五欲，不著我人，此是背捨善根發相。或於定心中，見於內身及外身，一切飛禽走獸、衣服飲食、屋舍山林，皆悉不淨，此為大不淨善根發相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Second, the mark of the contemplation of impurity wholesome root arising. A practitioner, within desire-realm or access concentration, body and mind empty and still, may suddenly see the death of another man or woman's body — once dead, swelling, rotting, and decaying, worms and pus flowing out, scattered white bones left in disarray — the mind moved by both sorrow and a certain gladness, growing weary of what was formerly loved. This is the mark of the ninefold contemplation wholesome root arising. Or, within still concentration, one may suddenly see one's own inward body as impure, one's own outward body swollen and disordered, one's own body's white bones, from head to foot, joint against joint — seeing this, the settled mind remains at peace, startled awake to impermanence, weary of the five desires, no longer attached to self or other. This is the mark of the liberations wholesome root arising. Or, within concentration, one may see both one's own body and the bodies of others, every bird and beast, every garment and food, every house and forest, all of it entirely impure. This is the mark of the great contemplation of impurity wholesome root arising.

〖講〗九想：（1）胖脹，（2）青瘀，（3）壞，（4）血塗漫，（5）膿爛，（6）蟲噉，（7）散壞，（8）骨想，（9）燒想。此九想觀上文已詳說……由宿世修過此九想之觀，故今於止觀中，忽發現九想不淨觀相……行者見是事已，忽然驚覺醒悟無常，當知凡夫眾生，曠劫沈淪，皆由妄想顛倒，諸法本自無常而計常，非樂計樂，非我計我，非淨計淨，因顛倒故，不能脫離生死，超出輪迴。今於定中，驚悟無常，心心厭患五欲之境，不執人我之相，此是背捨善根發相。背捨即八背捨，亦名八解脫……大智度論云：背此淨潔五欲，捨棄著心，故名背捨。若修此觀，能發無漏智慧，斷三界見思惑盡，證羅漢果，即名八解脫也……大不淨觀即是八勝處，即修八背捨後，觀心純熟，轉變自在，若淨不淨等色，隨意能破也，故名勝處……所以須修九想、背捨、勝處等觀者，蓋凡夫眾生，六識妄心，於順境上，起種種貪愛，引取無厭……故如來大慈，教修不淨觀對治，良以眾生貪欲不一，謂有外貪，內外貪，遍一切處貪之別，若外貪男女身分，互相愛著，用九想觀對治。若於己身他身而貪愛，用八背捨對治。若遍一切處貪，資生五塵等物，用大不淨觀對治之。以其宿世修此三種不淨觀故，於止觀中，有如是大不淨觀善根發相。

Commentary: The nine contemplations: (1) swelling, (2) discoloration, (3) rupture, (4) blood-smeared, (5) putrefaction, (6) being devoured by worms, (7) scattering apart, (8) bare bones, (9) being burned. These nine contemplations were already explained in detail above [in chapter 6]... Because this ninefold contemplation was cultivated in a past life, it now suddenly appears within calming and insight as the mark of the ninefold contemplation of impurity arising... Having seen this, a practitioner suddenly starts awake to impermanence. One should know that ordinary sentient beings, sunk in delusion across measureless kalpas, all through inverted, upside-down thought, take what is inherently impermanent to be permanent, what is not truly pleasurable to be pleasurable, what is not truly self to be self, what is not truly pure to be pure — and, because of this inversion, cannot escape birth-and-death or transcend the wheel of rebirth. Now, within concentration, startled awake to impermanence, the mind grows weary of the realm of the five desires at every turn, no longer clinging to the mark of self or other. This is the mark of the liberations wholesome root arising.

"Liberations" here means the eight liberations, also called the eightfold emancipation... The Great Perfection of Wisdom Treatise says: "turning one's back on these clean, pure five desires, casting aside the clinging mind — hence it is called 'turning one's back and casting aside.'" Cultivating this contemplation is able to bring forth outflow-free wisdom, entirely severing the afflictions of views and thought within the three realms, realizing the fruit of arhatship — this is called the eightfold emancipation...

The great contemplation of impurity is precisely the eight stations of mastery: once the eight liberations have been cultivated and the contemplating mind has matured, transformation becomes entirely free, and whether an object appears pure or impure, one is able, at will, to break through it — hence it is called a "station of mastery"...

As for why the nine contemplations, the liberations, and the stations of mastery must all be cultivated: it is because ordinary sentient beings' own deluded mind, encountering an agreeable circumstance, gives rise to every kind of craving attachment, drawing it in without ever being satisfied... Hence the Tathāgata, out of great compassion, teaches the cultivation of the contemplation of impurity to counter this — for the craving of sentient beings is not all of one kind: there is outward craving, inward-and-outward craving, and craving pervading every place. If the outward craving is for the physical form of a man or woman, mutual attached affection, one counters it with the ninefold contemplation. If the craving is for one's own body or another's body, one counters it with the eight liberations. If the craving pervades every place, extending even to the five sense-objects and material goods that sustain life, one counters it with the great contemplation of impurity. Because these three contemplations of impurity were cultivated in past lives, this mark of the great contemplation of impurity wholesome root now arises within calming and insight.

iii. Loving-Kindness 慈心善根發相

Chinese Source

〖文〗三慈心善根發相。行者因修止觀故，若得欲界未到地定，於此定中，忽然發心慈念眾生，或緣親人得樂之相，即發深定，內心悅樂清淨，不可為喻，中人怨人，乃至十方五道眾生，亦復如是。從禪定起，其心悅樂，隨所見人，顏色常和。是為慈心善根發相；悲喜捨心發相。類此可知也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Third, the mark of the loving-kindness wholesome root arising. A practitioner, through cultivating calming and insight, upon attaining desire-realm or access concentration, may suddenly give rise, within this concentration, to a compassionate, loving mindfulness of sentient beings — perhaps taking as its object a loved one's own happiness, giving rise to deep concentration, the inward mind joyful and pure beyond any comparison — and the same holds equally for a neutral person, an enemy, and, further, every sentient being of the five destinies throughout the ten directions. Rising from this concentration, the mind remains joyful, and whoever one happens to meet, one's own countenance remains constantly warm. This is the mark of the loving-kindness wholesome root arising; the marks of compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity arising can be understood in just the same way.

〖講〗此第三種明慈心觀善根發相。吾人自無量劫來，何曾未有修過止觀功夫，及大慈大悲，大喜大捨等行……行者因宿世修過慈悲喜捨，四無量心，但未成功，故今修止觀。得欲界未到地定，於此定中，忽然發動宿根，起慈悲愍念眾生之心，或緣親人得樂之相。親人有三種：（1）上親，謂父母師長；（2）中親，謂兄弟姊妹；（3）下親，即朋友知識種種親人。得樂亦有三種之別：或緣上親，得諸佛之樂；或緣中親，得菩薩羅漢之樂；或緣下親，得諸天之樂。以是種種快樂之相，即發深定，內心之中，悅怡快樂，清淨輕安之境界，不可為喻。或緣中人，即不親不疏之人，怨人即仇敵之人，亦有三種：害下親者，為下冤；害中親者，為中冤；害上親者，為上冤。無論冤人中人，亦復緣其得樂之相，如是乃至十方五道眾生，亦復緣其得樂之相。行人於禪定之中，遍緣十方六道一切眾生，無論親冤，一一莫不以大慈大悲心而憫念之，所謂冤親平等，人我俱空，於定中生如是慈念，從禪定起，則其心悅豫快樂，謂隨所見人，無論親冤，不生一念厭恨之心，顏色時常柔和，是為慈心觀善根發相。慈心既如是，悲喜捨心亦復然，故云類此可知也。

Commentary: This third section explains the mark of the contemplation of loving-kindness wholesome root arising. Across measureless kalpas, when have we ourselves never cultivated the effort of calming and insight, or great loving-kindness, great compassion, great sympathetic joy, and great equanimity?... A practitioner has, in a past life, cultivated the four immeasurables of loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity, but not yet brought them to completion — hence, upon cultivating calming and insight in the present and attaining desire-realm or access concentration, this root from a past life is suddenly stirred into activity, giving rise, within this concentration, to a compassionate, loving mindfulness of sentient beings, perhaps taking as its object a loved one's own happiness.

There are three kinds of loved one: the higher kind, meaning parents and teachers; the middle kind, meaning brothers and sisters; and the lower kind, meaning friends and acquaintances. Happiness likewise divides into three: taking the higher loved one as object, one experiences a buddha's own happiness; taking the middle loved one as object, one experiences a bodhisattva's or arhat's own happiness; taking the lower loved one as object, one experiences the happiness of the heavens. Through happiness of this kind, deep concentration arises, the inward mind joyful and content, pure and light beyond any comparison.

Taking a neutral person as object — that is, one neither loved nor disliked — and an enemy — that is, a hated foe — these too are of three kinds: one who has harmed a lower loved one is a lower enemy; one who has harmed a middle loved one is a middle enemy; one who has harmed a higher loved one is a higher enemy. Whether toward an enemy or a neutral person, one likewise takes their own happiness as the object of contemplation — and so, further, toward every sentient being of the five destinies throughout the ten directions, one takes their own happiness as the object as well.

A practitioner, within dhyāna-concentration, extends this mindfulness comprehensively to every sentient being of the six destinies throughout the ten directions, loved one and enemy alike, every one of them met with great loving-kindness and great compassion, this loving mindfulness — enemy and loved one equal, self and other both empty. Giving rise to this loving mindfulness within concentration, and rising from concentration, the mind remains joyful and content — whoever one happens to meet, loved one or enemy alike, not a single thought of aversion or resentment arises, and one's own countenance remains constantly warm. This is the mark of the contemplation of loving-kindness wholesome root arising. As loving-kindness is like this, so too are compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity — hence it is said, "these can be understood in just the same way."

iv. The Contemplation of Dependent Origination 因緣觀善根發相

Chinese Source

〖文〗四因緣觀善根發相。行者因修止觀故，若得欲界未到地身心靜定，忽然覺悟心生，推尋三世無明行等諸因緣中，不見人我，即離斷常，破諸執見，得定安隱，解慧開發，心生法喜，不念世間之事，乃至五陰十二處十八界中，分別亦如是。是為因緣觀善根發相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Fourth, the mark of the contemplation of dependent origination wholesome root arising. A practitioner, through cultivating calming and insight, upon attaining a body and mind still and settled in desire-realm or access concentration, may suddenly give rise to a mind of realization, tracing through the causes and conditions of ignorance, volitional formations, and the rest across the three time periods, finding no self or other within them, thereby leaving behind both the extreme of annihilation and the extreme of permanence, breaking through every clinging view, attaining a settled, peaceful concentration, understanding and wisdom opening up, the mind filled with joy in the Dharma, no longer dwelling on worldly matters — and this same discernment extends likewise to the five aggregates, the twelve sense-fields, and the eighteen elements. This is the mark of the contemplation of dependent origination wholesome root arising.

〖講〗第四因緣觀善根發相。因即親因，緣謂助緣，即三世十二因緣也……四教儀云：十二因緣有三種不同：（一）三世十二因緣……（二）二世十二因緣……（三）一念十二因緣……三世因緣破斷常，二世因緣破著我，一念因緣破實性，吾人從無始以來，何嘗不修此三種因緣觀，祗以隔陰之昏，入胎之迷，所以惘然不知。今生修止觀故，身心寂靜，忽然覺悟心生，推尋三世無明行等諸因緣中，不見人我，即離斷常，破諸執見。最初從無明緣行，行緣識，乃至有緣生，生緣老死，故成惑業苦之三道。如逆推此老死由何所而來，乃由生而來，生由有來，有從取來，乃至識由行來，行由無明來，無明由何而來，乃由妄想顛倒而來，推其顛倒妄想，來無來處，去無去處，其體原來虛妄無明，當體了不可得，無明滅，則行滅，乃至老死滅矣！……若能遠離斷常，不計我人，則定心安隱，解慧開發，心中明明了了，清清楚楚，法喜充滿，不念世間一切塵境之事。五陰即色受想行識，此五種能蓋覆真性，故曰陰。十二處，即六根六塵，復加六識，即十八界，乃至推尋陰入處界，皆不可得，亦復如是。為由止觀故，發動宿生所修因緣觀之善根相也。

Commentary: This fourth section explains the mark of the contemplation of dependent origination wholesome root arising. "Cause" here means the direct cause; "condition" means the supporting condition — that is, the twelve links of dependent origination across the three time periods... The Fourfold Teaching Ceremonial says that the twelve links of dependent origination take three distinct forms: across three time periods, across two time periods, and within a single thought...

The threefold-time version breaks the extremes of annihilation and permanence; the twofold-time version breaks attachment to a self; the single-thought version breaks belief in any real, fixed nature. From beginningless time, when have we ourselves not already cultivated these three contemplations of dependent origination? It is only through the confusion between one life and the next, the delusion of entering the womb again, that we remain oblivious to this fact. Now, in this present life, through cultivating calming and insight, body and mind grow still, and a mind of sudden realization arises, tracing through the causes and conditions of ignorance, volitional formations, and the rest across the three time periods, finding no self or other within them, thereby leaving behind both extremes and breaking through every clinging view.

From the very start, ignorance conditions volitional formations, volitional formations condition consciousness, and so on, through existence conditioning birth, birth conditioning old age and death — forming the three paths of delusion, karma, and suffering. Tracing this backward: from where does old age and death actually come? It comes from birth. Birth comes from existence. Existence comes from grasping. And so on, until consciousness comes from volitional formations, and volitional formations come from ignorance. From where, then, does ignorance itself come? It comes from inverted, upside-down thought — and tracing this inverted, upside-down thought further, it has no place it comes from and no place it goes to; its very substance is, from the start, illusory ignorance, its own substance ultimately unfindable. Once ignorance ceases, volitional formations cease, and so on, until old age and death themselves cease!

...If one is able to leave both extremes of annihilation and permanence far behind, no longer reckoning a fixed self or other, the settled mind remains at peace, understanding and wisdom open up, the mind utterly clear and distinct, filled with joy in the Dharma, no longer dwelling on any dusty circumstance of the world. The five aggregates are form, sensation, perception, formation, and consciousness — these five are able to cover over one's own true nature, hence they are called "aggregates" [literally, "shadows"]. The twelve sense-fields are the six faculties and six sense-objects; adding the six consciousnesses gives the eighteen elements — and tracing through the aggregates, sense-fields, and elements in just the same way, none of them can ultimately be found either. This is the mark of the wholesome root of the contemplation of dependent origination, cultivated in a past life, now stirred into activity through calming and insight.

v. Buddha-Recollection 念佛善根發相

Chinese Source

〖文〗五念佛善根發相。行者因修止觀故，若得欲界未到地定，身心空寂，忽然憶念諸佛功德、相好不可思議。所有十力、無畏、不共三昧、解脫等法，不可思議，神通變化、無礙說法，廣利眾生不可思議，如是等無量功德不可思議。作是念時，即發愛敬心生，三昧開發，身心快樂，清淨安隱，無諸惡相，從禪定起，身體輕利，自覺功德巍巍，人所愛敬，是為念佛三昧善根發相。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Fifth, the mark of the buddha-recollection wholesome root arising. A practitioner, through cultivating calming and insight, upon attaining desire-realm or access concentration, body and mind empty and still, may suddenly recollect the buddhas' own merit and inconceivable, excellent marks — every one of their ten powers, fearlessnesses, unshared samādhis, liberations, and other inconceivable qualities; their inconceivable spiritual powers, transformations, unhindered teaching of the Dharma, and vast benefiting of sentient beings; every one of these measureless, inconceivable merits. As this recollection occurs, a loving, reverent mind arises, samādhi opens up, body and mind become blissful, pure and settled, with no evil appearance at all — and, rising from this concentration, the body feels light and agile, and one becomes aware of one's own towering merit, loved and revered by others. This is the mark of the buddha-recollection samādhi wholesome root arising.

〖講〗此第五種明念佛善根發相……若能一聞千悟，亦可謂之總持……但念佛亦有三種不同，一念法身佛，二念報身佛，三念應身佛。因眾生起惑造業，有種種諸障不同，故世尊投藥，亦有各異，若有昏沈闇塞障，如昏睡無記等，須念應身佛，觀佛三十二相，八十種好治之。若是惡念思維障，如欲作五逆十惡等事，須念報身佛治之……若有境界逼迫障……須念清淨法身毗盧遮那佛治之……因行人夙世修過此三種念佛，故今修止觀，身心空寂，忽然憶念諸佛功德相好，不可思議，此中境界，乃是得定之後，將外身內心，皆悉空寂；當知此空，乃寂其著有之相，但空而不空，即是妙有，故忽然於中憶念，諸佛功德相好，不可思議，此即應身善根發相……即念報身佛，善根發動之相。神通變化，無礙說法，利益眾生，即是念法身佛善根發相……有如是等，無量無邊諸佛功德，不可思議，心中作是思維時，即發愛敬之心，三昧從斯開發，身心便覺安隱，清淨快樂，無諸一切惡覺觀相，此為定中之相，若從禪定起，則身體輕利，自覺自己功德巍巍，孤迥超絕，為眾人之所愛敬，修止觀定中，有如此等境界者，是名念佛觀之善根發相。當知吾人自心本來是佛，但因妄想顛倒，不自證得，若能薰修，則是心是佛，是心作佛；但是心作佛之實現，貴在當人自修，如魚在水中，冷煖自知耳！

Commentary: This fifth section explains the mark of the buddha-recollection wholesome root arising... Buddha-recollection itself takes three distinct forms: recollecting the Dharma-body buddha, recollecting the reward-body buddha, and recollecting the response-body buddha. Because sentient beings give rise to delusion and create karma in different ways, carrying different kinds of obstruction, the World-Honored One's own remedy differs accordingly. If one carries the obstruction of dullness and darkness — such as drowsiness or a blank, neutral state — one should recollect the response-body buddha, contemplating the Buddha's thirty-two major marks and eighty minor excellent features, to remedy it. If one carries the obstruction of evil, deliberate thought — wishing to commit one of the five heinous offenses or ten evils — one should recollect the reward-body buddha to remedy it... If one carries the obstruction of circumstances pressing in upon oneself... one should recollect the pure Dharma-body, Vairocana Buddha, to remedy it...

Because a practitioner has, in a past life, cultivated these three forms of buddha-recollection, now, upon cultivating calming and insight, body and mind empty and still, one suddenly recollects the buddhas' own merit and inconceivable, excellent marks. This state occurs after concentration has been attained, when both the outward body and the inward mind have become entirely empty and still — one should know that this emptiness is the stilling of the mark of clinging to existence, yet, being empty, it is not truly empty at all, but wondrous existence itself — hence one suddenly recollects, within it, the buddhas' own merit and inconceivable, excellent marks. This is the mark of the response-body wholesome root arising... [reflecting on the ten powers and fearlessnesses] is the mark of the reward-body wholesome root arising. Spiritual powers, transformations, unhindered teaching of the Dharma, and benefiting sentient beings are the mark of the Dharma-body wholesome root arising...

Having measureless, boundless, inconceivable merits of the buddhas of this kind — as this reflection occurs in the mind, a loving, reverent mind arises, samādhi opens up from this, and body and mind feel settled, pure and blissful, with no unwholesome discursive thought at all — this is the mark within concentration. Rising from this concentration, the body feels light and agile, and one becomes aware of one's own towering merit, solitary and transcendent, loved and revered by others. A practitioner who, while cultivating calming and insight in concentration, has a state of this kind, is said to have the mark of the buddha-recollection contemplation wholesome root arising.

One should know that our own mind is, from the start, itself buddha — it is only through inverted, upside-down thought that we fail to realize this ourselves. If one is able to cultivate and steep oneself in it, then this very mind is buddha, this very mind creates buddhahood — but the actual realization of this mind creating buddhahood depends entirely on one's own practice, exactly as a fish in the water alone knows whether it is warm or cold!

Reading note: Baojing closes this fivefold list with a brief mention of further dharma-gates that can arise the same way — the four foundations of mindfulness, the six recollections, and the thirty-seven factors of the path among them — but treats these five (breath-method, impurity, loving-kindness, dependent origination, buddha-recollection) as the essential set. Notice that "buddha-recollection" here names something considerably richer than simple name-recitation: three distinct objects of recollection (Dharma-body, reward-body, response-body), each matched to a different obstruction a practitioner might be carrying. This threefold structure is worth remembering wherever Baojing's own recurring turn to nembutsu practice appears elsewhere in this commentary — it is never presented as a single undifferentiated technique.

2. Distinguishing the Genuine from the False 分別真偽

i. Recognizing False, Demonic Marks 辨邪偽禪發相

Chinese Source

〖文〗二分別真偽者有二：一者辨邪偽禪發相。行者若發如上諸禪時，隨因所發之法，或身搔動，或時身重，如物鎮壓，或時身輕欲飛，或時如縛，或時逶迤垂熟，或時煎寒，或時壯熱，或見種種諸異境界，或時其心闇蔽，或時起諸惡覺，或時念外散辭諸雜善事，或時歡喜躁動，或時憂愁悲思，或時惡觸，身毛驚豎，或時大樂昏醉，如是種種邪法與禪俱發，名為邪偽。此之邪定，若人愛著，即與九十五種鬼神法相應，多好失心顛狂。或時諸鬼神等知人念著其法，即加勢力，令發諸邪定、邪智、辯才、神通、惑動世人。凡愚見者，謂得道果，皆悉信伏。而其內心顛倒，專行鬼法，惑亂世間。是人命終，永不值佛，還墮鬼神道中。若坐時多行惡法，即墮地獄。行者修止觀時，若證如是等禪，有此諸邪偽相，當即卻之。云何卻之？若知虛誑，正心不受不著，即當謝滅。應用正觀破之，即當滅矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Second, distinguishing the genuine from the false has two parts. The first is recognizing the mark of a false, demonic form of dhyāna arising. When a practitioner brings forth any of the states of dhyāna explained above, various things may occur alongside it: the body twitching, or feeling heavy as though pressed down by something, or feeling light as though about to fly, or feeling bound, or slack and drowsy, or chilled, or feverish; various strange states may be seen; the mind may grow dark and obscured, or evil thoughts may arise, or the attention may scatter outward toward various worldly, wholesome-seeming matters; there may be an agitated excitement, or sorrowful, melancholy thought; a repulsive sensation, hair standing on end; or an intoxicating bliss. States of this deviant kind, arising alongside dhyāna, are called false and demonic.

If a person loves and clings to a deviant concentration of this kind, this accords with one of ninety-five kinds of demonic spirit-practice, and often brings on a loss of one's own mind, madness and derangement. At times these spirits, knowing that a person craves and clings to their own particular practice, add their own power to it, causing all manner of deviant concentration, deviant wisdom, eloquence, and spiritual power to arise, deceiving and stirring up the people of the world. Ordinary, foolish onlookers, seeing this, take it to be the fruit of the path attained, and place their faith and submission in it entirely — while the practitioner's own inward mind remains upside-down, devoted entirely to a demonic practice, deceiving and disturbing the world. When such a person's life ends, they will never again encounter a buddha, and fall back into the realm of ghosts and spirits instead. If, while seated, one carries out much evil practice of this kind, one falls into hell.

When a practitioner, cultivating calming and insight, realizes a form of dhyāna of this kind, bearing marks of this false and demonic sort, one should reject it at once. How does one reject it? Recognizing it as illusory and deceptive, keeping the mind correct, neither accepting nor clinging to it, it will fade away of itself. Applying correct contemplation to break through it will likewise dissolve it at once.

〖講〗上來明內善根發相，三科不同，一明善根發相有五種，前已明訖，此第二辨別真偽……然此義有二種：一者辨邪偽禪發相，二者辨真正禪發相……行者於止觀中，發如上所發諸善根相時，隨因其所發之法，或鎮壓之重物相發，或逶迤者之精神衰頹，然無論搔動，鎮壓垂熟，煎寒壯熱等，種種諸異境界，悉是邪偽，非是真正之禪發相，自心之中，或覺自心昏闇覆蔽，糊塗不開；或時起諸惡覺，邪念思維；或歡喜躁動，憂愁悲思；或惡觸身毛驚豎，大樂昏醉，如是種種邪法，與禪同時而發，皆名為邪。用功人此種境界，亦是難免，蓋吾人於正修止觀，加功用行時，真心與妄心交共，致內魔與外魔群起，故須識之……古云：「枯木堂前錯路多。」若不以正慧分明，少有不慎，則白雲萬里，其害無窮，毫釐之差，天淵之別，用功者，不可不慎重之。上來所說邪定境界，繁多異常，但行人切不可生一念愛著之心。若生愛著，即與印度九十五種邪魔外道法相應，多失其正念真心，每每令人發顛發狂，魔鬼雖不及羅漢之神通殊勝，亦具有漏五通，有他心通，故能知其行者，隨心愛著何法，諸鬼神隨其所好，復加其勢力，使愛心轉盛，且令行人發諸邪定、邪慧、邪辨、邪通、種種境界，其目的在破毀行者之道業，令成魔王眷屬，永沈輪迴而後已……當知佛者，覺也悟也，即背塵合覺之謂也。鬼神之法，迷而不覺，即背覺合塵之謂也。一迷一悟，迥然不同，一個向西行，一個向東走，欲求彼此相見，無有是處，故云永不值佛……所以行者，於修止觀時，若遇證如是種種邪偽之相，當即卻之，切不可貪著，須了達凡所有相，皆是虛妄，凡所有見，皆是妄見。若遇有邪智神通發時，即知其虛妄，當知佛法，不以神通為解脫，當以了生脫死，圓成佛道為究竟。言正心不受不著者，行人修禪時，須正其心，誠其意，不受不著，不驚不怖，不喜不怒，所謂能所雙忘，憎愛齊遣，於中絲毫不生分別。一念不起，以正慧之紅日，照破邪魔之霜露，令諸邪定邪慧，不消滅而自消滅矣！

Commentary: The three parts explaining the mark of inward wholesome roots arising are now explained — the first, the mark of the five kinds arising, already explained above; this second explains distinguishing the genuine from the false... This has two aspects: recognizing the mark of a false, demonic form of dhyāna arising, and recognizing the mark of a genuine, correct form of dhyāna arising.

When a practitioner, within calming and insight, brings forth any of the wholesome-root marks explained above, various things may occur alongside it — a sensation of being pressed down by something heavy, or a slack, listless decline of spirit — and whatever the particular form, whether twitching, heavy pressure, drowsy sluggishness, chills, or fever, every such strange state is false and demonic, not a genuine mark of dhyāna arising at all. Within one's own mind, one may feel a dull, covering darkness, muddled and closed; or evil, deviant thoughts may arise; or an agitated excitement, or sorrowful, melancholy thought; or a repulsive sensation with hair standing on end, or an intoxicating bliss — states of this deviant kind, arising at the same time as the dhyāna itself, are all called false. A state of this kind is, in truth, hard for anyone applying real effort to avoid entirely, since when one genuinely cultivates calming and insight with real effort, the true mind and the false mind contend with each other, and both inward and outward demons arise together as a result — hence one must recognize this for what it is.

As the ancients said: "Before the hall of the withered tree, wrong paths abound." Without the clarity of correct wisdom, the slightest carelessness can lead one ten thousand miles astray, with boundless harm resulting — a hairsbreadth's error making the difference between heaven and the depths. Anyone applying real effort cannot be too careful about this.

The deviant states of concentration described above are extraordinarily numerous, but a practitioner must never, under any circumstance, give rise to even a single thought of fondness or attachment toward them. Should such fondness and attachment arise, this accords with one of India's own ninety-five kinds of deviant, demonic, non-Buddhist practice, and one very often loses one's own correct mindfulness and true mind, frequently driving a person to madness and derangement. Though demons and spirits do not match an arhat's own excellent spiritual powers, they nonetheless possess the five outflow-bound supernormal powers, including knowledge of others' minds — and so are able to know exactly what practice a given practitioner happens to be fond of and attached to, adding their own power to whatever it is, making that fondness grow stronger still, and causing the practitioner to bring forth every kind of deviant concentration, deviant wisdom, deviant eloquence, and deviant spiritual power, in state after state — their whole aim being to destroy the practitioner's own progress on the path, making them a follower of the demon-king instead, permanently sunk in the wheel of rebirth thereafter.

One should know that "Buddha" means awakening, realization — that is, turning one's back on the dust of the world and joining oneself to awakening. The way of ghosts and spirits is deluded, unawakened — that is, turning one's back on awakening and joining oneself to the dust of the world instead. One delusion, one awakening, utterly different from each other — one walking west, the other walking east — hoping the two might ever meet is simply impossible; hence it is said, "will never again encounter a buddha."

This is why a practitioner, cultivating calming and insight, upon encountering any mark of this false and demonic kind, must reject it at once, never craving or clinging to it, but must fully understand that whatever has a mark at all is entirely illusory, and whatever is seen is entirely a false view. Should some deviant wisdom or spiritual power arise, one should recognize it at once as illusory — one should know that the Buddhadharma does not take spiritual power itself as liberation, but takes resolving birth-and-death and fully accomplishing the buddha-path as the true goal. "Keeping the mind correct, neither accepting nor clinging" means that a practitioner cultivating dhyāna must keep the mind correct and the intention sincere, neither accepting nor clinging, neither startled nor afraid, neither delighted nor angered — subject and object both forgotten, aversion and craving both dismissed together, giving rise to not the slightest discrimination within it. With not a single thought arising, the red sun of correct wisdom breaks through the frost and dew of deviant demons, so that every deviant concentration and deviant wisdom dissolves of itself, without even needing to be forcibly dissolved at all!

〖講〗古時有一行者，起初精進勇猛，久後於定時生歡樂，喜笑顏開，有一知識，詢其定中何笑為，答謂吾坐於中時，觀有金毛獅子來相戲，故而歡悅。善知識曰：「此是魔王擾亂，投其所好，當知定中所見，皆是虛妄，切勿執著。」乃授之一翦，告再來時，即以此刺之。行者從命，果於定中又見，即以翦刺之，忽覺己之肉痛，觀之乃己之腿也。足見魔王，專以惱亂行者，破毀道業為己任，行者用功須時時提防，如護眼珠相似，勿可忽略也。

Commentary: Long ago there was a practitioner who, at first, applied himself with courageous diligence, but who, after a long time, began to feel a certain joy while in concentration, laughing with delight. A spiritual friend asked him what he was laughing about within concentration, and he answered: "While seated within it, I see a golden-maned lion come and play with me — hence my joy." The spiritual friend said: "This is Māra himself causing disturbance, catering to exactly what you happen to be fond of. You should know that whatever is seen within concentration is entirely illusory — never cling to it." He then gave the practitioner a pair of scissors, and told him: the next time it appears, stab it with these. The practitioner did as instructed, and, sure enough, saw the lion appear again within concentration; he stabbed it with the scissors, and suddenly felt his own flesh in pain — looking down, it was his own leg. This is enough to show that Māra makes it his own particular task to disturb a practitioner and destroy their progress on the path — a practitioner applying real effort must remain constantly on guard against this, exactly as one guards the pupil of one's own eye, never to be treated carelessly.

ii. Recognizing Genuine Marks 辨真正禪發相

Chinese Source

〖文〗二者辨真正禪發相。行者若於坐中發諸禪時，無有如上所謂諸邪法等，隨一一禪發時，即覺與定相應，空明清淨，內心喜悅，憺然快樂，無有覆蓋，善心開發，信敬增長，智鑒分明，身心柔軟，微妙虛寂，厭患世間，無為無欲，出入自在，是為正禪發相。譬如與惡人共事，恆相觸惱，若與善人共事，久見其美。分別邪正二種，禪發之相，亦復如是。三明用止觀長養諸善根者。若於坐中諸善根發時，應用止觀二法修令增進。若宜用止，則以止修之；若宜用觀，則以觀修之，具如前說，略示大意矣！

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Second, recognizing the mark of a genuine, correct form of dhyāna arising. When a practitioner, while sitting, brings forth any form of dhyāna, with none of the deviant states described above present, then, whatever form of dhyāna arises, it feels immediately in accord with concentration itself — empty, bright, and pure, the inward mind joyful, calm and content, with nothing covering it over; the wholesome mind opens up, faith and reverence increase, wisdom and insight grow clear, body and mind become soft and pliant, subtle, wondrous, and empty-still, weary of the world, free of striving and craving, entering and leaving concentration entirely at ease. This is the mark of genuine dhyāna arising. It is like keeping company with an evil person, who constantly causes friction and trouble, as against keeping company with a good person, whose fine qualities become apparent only over time — distinguishing the deviant and correct marks of dhyāna arising is exactly the same.

Third, nurturing every wholesome root through calming and insight. When, while seated, any wholesome root arises, one should apply the two methods of calming and insight to cultivate and advance it. Where calming is fitting, cultivate it through calming; where insight is fitting, cultivate it through insight — exactly as explained before; only the general outline is given here.

〖講〗此第二明真正禪定發相。行者於坐中發諸禪時，心中不愛不憎，不驚不怖，並無有如上所說種種邪法諸禪，但隨一一禪發時，則空空明明，清清淨淨，猶如萬里青天，了無一物，唯覺自心中，有一種天然妙樂，亦無有蓋覆，以智鑒分別故，自然而然，不與邪偽染法相應，是名真正禪定發相。譬喻以下乃是假喻，總辨邪正二種禪定發相。當知吾人之心，本非善亦非惡，但有隨緣之可能性，所以能善能惡……第三明止觀長養者，謂行人若於坐中，內外一切善根開發之時，應須修止觀，令其增進，但不可亂投藥石，所謂宜止則止，宜觀則觀。此種修止觀法門，具如正修章廣釋，故略示其意而已。

Commentary: This second part explains the mark of genuine dhyāna-concentration arising. When a practitioner, while seated, brings forth any form of dhyāna, the mind neither craves nor detests it, neither startled nor afraid, with none of the various deviant forms of dhyāna described above present — whatever form of dhyāna arises, it is empty, bright, pure and clear, exactly like a clear sky stretching ten thousand miles, with nothing at all there — one is aware, within one's own mind, only of a certain natural, wondrous bliss, with nothing covering it over, and through wisdom's own discernment, quite naturally, one does not accord with any false, defiling state at all. This is called the mark of genuine dhyāna-concentration arising. The simile that follows is a constructed comparison, distinguishing, overall, the two marks of deviant and genuine dhyāna-concentration arising. One should know that our own mind is, at root, neither good nor evil, but carries the capacity to follow either condition, and so is able to become either good or evil...

The third part explains nurturing wholesome roots through calming and insight: this means that when a practitioner, while seated, has any inward or outward wholesome root opening up, one should cultivate calming and insight to advance it further — but one must never apply the wrong remedy carelessly, that is, calming where calming is fitting, insight where insight is fitting. This method of cultivating calming and insight is explained fully in the actual-practice chapter; only the general outline is given here.

Reading note: The golden-maned lion story is Baojing's sharpest illustration in this chapter of a point he makes repeatedly: a genuinely blissful, vivid experience in meditation is not, by itself, evidence that it is trustworthy — the test is never how it feels, but whether the practitioner can treat it with the same detachment ("neither accepting nor clinging") regardless of whether it turns out to be genuine or demonic. This is the same warning Baojing gave in chapter 6's fourth approach (countering subtle attachment within concentration) and will return to again in chapter 8's extended treatment of demonic affairs specifically — a running theme across the second half of this commentary.
