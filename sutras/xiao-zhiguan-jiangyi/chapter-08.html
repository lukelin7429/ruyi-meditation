Chapter 8 · 覺知魔事第八 · Complete

Awareness of Demonic Affairs

"The higher the path, the fiercer the demons" — genuine progress is exactly what draws demonic interference in the first place. Baojing works through three kinds of spirit-demon a practitioner may actually encounter, then two methods for driving any of them off, before landing, once again, on his own recurring answer: reciting Amitābha's name as the most secure refuge of all.

Status: complete Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: book.bfnn.org Covers: the four kinds of demon, three kinds of spirit-demon in detail, and the two methods for driving off demonic disturbance

What "Demonic Affairs" Means 魔事釋名

Chinese Source

〖文〗梵音魔羅，秦言殺者，奪行人功德之財，殺行人智慧之命，是故名之為惡魔。事者，如佛以功德智慧度脫眾生，入涅槃為事，魔常以破壞眾生善根，令流轉生死為事。若能安心正道，是故道高方知魔盛，仍須善識魔事，但有四種：一煩惱魔，二陰入界魔，三死魔，四鬼神魔。三種皆是世間之常事，及隨人自心所生，當須自心正除遣之，今不分別，鬼神魔相，此事須知，今當略說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: In Sanskrit, "māra"; in Chinese, "the killer" — one who plunders a practitioner's own treasure of merit and kills a practitioner's own life of wisdom; hence it is called an evil demon. "Affairs" means this: just as the Buddha's own affair is bringing sentient beings across through merit and wisdom, entering nirvāṇa, so a demon's constant affair is destroying sentient beings' own wholesome roots, keeping them revolving in birth-and-death. If one is able to settle the mind on the correct path, the higher the path, the more one comes to know how numerous the demons truly are — and so one must skillfully recognize demonic affairs. There are, in fact, four kinds: the demon of affliction, the demon of the aggregates, sense-fields, and elements, the demon of death, and the demon of ghosts and spirits. The first three are all ordinary matters of the world, arising from within a person's own mind, and must be corrected and dispelled through one's own mind's own correct effort — they are not further distinguished here. The mark of the demon of ghosts and spirits, however, is something one must specifically know; it will now be briefly explained.

〖講〗此書共十章，上來七章已竟，今明第八，令行者覺知魔事。行者之功夫，略加純熟，則魔王乘機破壞，故魔來務須覺知，切不可被其所迷……當知魔事與佛事，迥然不同。佛事者何？不外六度萬行，令眾生改惡向善，轉迷為悟，離苦得樂……魔王則反之，彼常以破壞眾生善根，令流轉生死為事……吾人不用功則已，若用功魔王即來擾亂……若一念不生，發本明耀，令魔宮震動，如世尊於雪山苦行，將成道時，魔王宮殿，動搖不安……道高方知魔盛，道高一尺，魔高一丈，能善識魔事，所謂見魔不魔，其魔自壞，見怪不怪，其怪自敗，以平等無際之正念力，滅有限之魔力，猶如沸湯之消冰耳！然魔有四種：一者煩惱魔，此是內心之魔……二者陰魔，即五蘊也……三死魔，死者四大分散身喪命殞也……四者鬼神魔，亦名天子魔。但天子魔，則指欲天頂之波旬魔王也……前三種皆是常事，及隨人自心所生，當須自心正念除遣之……於此四魔，唯第四魔相，須深識之。

Commentary: This text has ten chapters in all; the seven above are now complete, and this eighth chapter now explains awareness of demonic affairs, so that a practitioner can recognize them. Once a practitioner's own effort has grown even somewhat mature, the demon-king seizes the opportunity to destroy it — hence, when a demon comes, one must, without fail, be aware of it, and never be deluded by it.

One should know that demonic affairs and buddha-affairs are entirely different from each other. What is a buddha-affair? Nothing other than the six pāramitās and the ten thousand practices, turning sentient beings from evil toward good, from delusion into awakening, freeing them from suffering and bringing them happiness... The demon-king does exactly the opposite: his constant affair is destroying sentient beings' own wholesome roots, keeping them revolving in birth-and-death... If we do not apply effort at all, this is one thing — but the moment we do apply effort, the demon-king comes to disturb it. If not even a single thought arises, and one's own inherent brightness shines forth, this shakes the very demon-palace itself — just as, when the World-Honored One practiced austerities in the Snow Mountains and was about to accomplish the path, the demon-king's own palace was shaken and thrown into turmoil...

"The higher the path, the more one knows how numerous the demons are" — the path rises a single foot, the demons rise ten feet higher still; but one who is skillfully able to recognize demonic affairs finds that, seeing a demon without treating it as a demon, the demon collapses of itself; seeing something strange without treating it as strange, the strangeness defeats itself — through the power of equal, boundless correct mindfulness, one dissolves the demon's own limited power, exactly as boiling water melts ice.

Demons are of four kinds. The first is the demon of affliction — this is an inward demon of the mind itself... The second is the demon of the aggregates — that is, the five aggregates... The third is the demon of death — death being the dispersal of the four great elements, the body lost and life ended... The fourth is the demon of ghosts and spirits, also called the demon of the god-son — though "the demon of the god-son" refers specifically to Māra, the demon-king at the very summit of the desire realm's heavens... The first three are all ordinary matters, arising from within a person's own mind, and must be corrected and dispelled through one's own mind's own correct mindfulness... Among these four demons, only the mark of this fourth demon needs to be deeply understood.

Three Kinds of Spirit-Demon 鬼神魔三種

1. Nature-Spirits 精魅

Chinese Source

〖文〗鬼神魔有三種：一者精魅。十二時獸，變化作種種形色。或作少女、老宿之形，乃至可畏身等非一，惱惑行人。此諸精魅欲惱行人，各當其時而來，善須別識。若於寅時來者，必是虎獸等；若於卯時來者，必是兔鹿等……行者若見常用此時來，即知其獸精，說其名字訶責，即當謝滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The demon of ghosts and spirits is of three kinds. The first is the nature-spirit: any of the twelve double-hour beasts, transformed into various shapes and forms — sometimes the form of a young girl, sometimes an aged elder, and various other frightening bodies as well — troubling and deluding a practitioner. These nature-spirits, wishing to trouble a practitioner, each come at their own particular hour, and one must skillfully distinguish which. If it comes during the hour of the tiger, it must be a tiger-kind creature; if during the hour of the rabbit, it must be a rabbit or deer-kind creature... A practitioner who observes it habitually coming at a particular hour thereby knows which beast-spirit it is, and, naming it aloud and rebuking it, causes it to fade away at once.

〖講〗今略說鬼神魔有三種：精魅鬼即魑魅、魍魎之輩也，本是朽木頑石，因受日月之精華，年久即成精怪，亦名時媚鬼。若行者，邪想坐禪，多著時媚，或作少男少女，老男老女，禽獸之像，殊形異貌，種種不同，或娛樂人，或教人紿人，或作可愛之形，或作可怖之狀，施種種殊形異狀，惱亂行人之禪定。當知此之精魅，欲惱行人之時，各當其時而來，不致紊亂，行者善須分別，識知獸名，默而擯之，鬼即謝滅。今欲分別鬼魅，變作時獸者，當觀察十二時，隨何時而來，隨其時來，即知其獸，若寅時是虎，乃至丑時是牛。又一時約三，十二時即有三十六獸……以其鬼法懼人識名，喚名尚不敢來，況復識形，故識其形名，媚即不敢為非。治媚之法，古有一隱士頭陀，畜一方鏡，掛之座後，媚不能變鏡中色相，覽鏡識之，可以自遣，此亦內外兩治之唯一方法也。

Commentary: Now briefly explaining the three kinds of ghost-and-spirit demon. The nature-spirit is precisely the kind of goblin or sprite that begins as a decayed piece of wood or a lifeless stone, and, absorbing the essence of sun and moon over long years, becomes a spirit-goblin — also called a "seasonal charm-ghost." If a practitioner, sitting in meditation with deviant thoughts, becomes especially prone to attracting such charm-spirits, they may take the form of a young man or woman, an old man or woman, or the image of a bird or beast, in every kind of strange, varied shape — sometimes entertaining the practitioner, sometimes deceiving and misleading them, sometimes taking a lovable form, sometimes a terrifying one, displaying every kind of strange, varied appearance, disturbing the practitioner's own dhyāna-concentration.

One should know that these nature-spirits, wishing to trouble a practitioner, each come at their own particular hour, never at random — a practitioner must skillfully distinguish this, recognizing the beast's own name, and, silently rejecting it, the spirit fades away at once. To distinguish which seasonal beast a given spirit has transformed into, one observes the twelve double-hours, and whichever hour it comes, one thereby knows which beast it is — the hour of the tiger is a tiger, and so on down to the hour of the ox. Further dividing each hour into three gives thirty-six beasts in all... Since these spirits, by their very nature, fear being recognized by name, they dare not even come once called by name, much less once their form is recognized — hence, recognizing both their form and their name, the charm-spirit no longer dares to cause mischief. As for a method of dealing with such charm-spirits, an ancient hermit-ascetic once kept a square mirror hung behind his own seat — a charm-spirit is unable to alter its own reflected image within a mirror, and, viewing the mirror, one recognizes it and is thereby able to dispel it oneself. This too is a single method that treats both the inward and outward aspects of the problem at once.

2. The Harassing Spirit 堆剔鬼

Chinese Source

〖文〗二者堆剔鬼。亦作種種惱觸行人，或如蟲蝎緣人頭面鑽刺熠熠，或擊櫪人兩腋下，或乍抱持於人，或言說音聲喧鬧，及作諸獸之形，異相非一，來惱行人。應即覺知，一心閉目，陰而罵之，作是言：我今識汝，汝是閻浮提中食火嗅香偷臘吉支，邪見喜破戒種，我今持戒，終不畏汝。若出家人，應誦戒本；若在家人，應誦三歸五戒等，鬼便卻行匍匐而去。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The second is the harassing spirit. This too troubles a practitioner in various ways — sometimes like an insect or scorpion crawling over the head and face, pricking with a sharp, gleaming sensation; sometimes striking beneath both armpits; sometimes suddenly embracing the practitioner; sometimes making loud, disruptive sounds and speech, or taking the shape of various beasts, in every kind of strange form, troubling a practitioner. One should be aware of this at once, and, with a single, undivided mind, close the eyes and silently rebuke it, saying: "I now recognize you — you are one of the fire-eating, scent-smelling, offering-stealing spirits of this world, born of wrong views and delight in breaking the precepts. I now keep the precepts, and will never fear you." If a monastic, one should recite the precept code; if a lay person, one should recite the three refuges and five precepts — and the spirit will then retreat, crawling away on all fours.

〖講〗堆剔鬼者，即最畏之惡夜剎鬼也。若行者坐禪時，鬼即來惱亂，亦作種種之形像惱亂行人，或如蟲蝎遍緣人之頭面，鑽刺熠熠，熠熠即鮮明也。有時或緣人身體，墮而復上，翻覆不已，雖無痛苦，而屑屑難耐。有或鑽入人耳眼鼻，或擊櫪人兩腋之下，或乍抱持於人，似如有物，捉不可得，驅已復來，有時啾啾作聲，喧鬧人耳，並及其他種種之異相非一，來惱行人，此鬼面似琵琶，四目兩口，最為可畏，行人若遇斯鬼發現時，急須設法對治，此鬼乃於古往過去拘那含佛時，末法中一比丘，喜亂眾僧，僧擯驅之，即發惡願，常惱坐禪人，此是源祖之鬼。報或已謝，而同業所生，亦能惱亂，今呵其宗祖，聞即羞去，行者知已，即一心閉目，陰而罵之，作是呵言：「我今識汝名字，汝是閻浮提中食火嗅香偷臘吉支，邪見喜破戒種，我今持戒，不畏於汝。」……若出家緇流，應誦梵網四分五分等戒律。若在家白衣，須誦三歸五戒，以其五戒，每戒各有五護戒神，五五即有二十五護戒神。此等最懼戒法，若誦之，則諸鬼魅便卻行，匍匐而去。

Commentary: The harassing spirit is precisely the most dreaded of evil night-yakṣa spirits. If, while a practitioner sits in meditation, this spirit comes to disturb them, it too takes on various shapes to trouble the practitioner — sometimes like an insect or scorpion crawling all over the head and face, pricking with a sharp, gleaming sensation; sometimes crawling over the body, dropping down and climbing back up again without end — though causing no real pain, it is exhausting and hard to endure. Sometimes it burrows into the ears, eyes, or nose; sometimes it strikes beneath both armpits; sometimes it suddenly seems to embrace the practitioner, as though some object were there, yet impossible to grasp, driven off only to return again; sometimes it makes a shrill, chattering sound, disturbing the ears — and various other strange manifestations as well, troubling a practitioner. This spirit's face resembles a lute, with four eyes and two mouths, the most terrifying of all.

Should a practitioner encounter this spirit, one must urgently apply a method to counter it. This spirit was, long ago, in the age of the buddha Kanakamuni, a monk in the final age of that buddha's teaching, who delighted in disrupting the assembly of monks; the assembly expelled him, and he thereupon made an evil vow to constantly trouble anyone sitting in meditation — this is the founding ancestor of this kind of spirit. Though the karmic retribution of that original spirit may already be exhausted, others born of the same karma continue to trouble practitioners in just the same way — and rebuking this spirit by naming its own founding ancestor causes it to depart in shame upon hearing it. A practitioner, recognizing this, should, with a single, undivided mind, close the eyes and silently rebuke it, saying: "I now recognize your very name — you are one of the fire-eating, scent-smelling, offering-stealing spirits of this world, born of wrong views and delight in breaking the precepts. I now keep the precepts, and do not fear you."

...A monastic should recite the Brahma Net Sūtra precepts, or the Four-Part or Five-Part Vinaya; a lay person should recite the three refuges and five precepts — for each of the five precepts has its own five guardian spirits protecting it, twenty-five guardian spirits in all. These spirits fear the precepts more than anything else — reciting them causes every such ghost and goblin to retreat at once, crawling away on all fours.

3. Māra's Own Disturbance 魔惱

Chinese Source

〖文〗三者魔惱。是魔多化作三種五塵境界相來破善心：一作違情事，則可畏五塵，令人恐懼；二作順情事，則可愛五塵，令人心著；三非違非順事，則平等五塵，動亂行者。是故魔名殺者，亦名華箭，亦名五箭，射人五情故，名色中作種種境界惑亂行人。作順情境者，或作父母兄弟、諸佛形象、端正男女可愛之境，令人心著；作違情境界者，或作虎狼師子羅剎之形，種種可畏之像，來怖行人；作非違順境者，則平常之事，動亂人心，令失禪定，故名為魔。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The third is Māra's own disturbance. This demon most often transforms into three kinds of state among the five sense-objects to destroy the wholesome mind. The first creates a disagreeable circumstance — dreadful sense-objects that instill fear. The second creates an agreeable circumstance — lovable sense-objects that draw the mind into attachment. The third creates a circumstance neither disagreeable nor agreeable — neutral sense-objects that simply stir the practitioner into agitation. This is why the demon is named "the killer," also called "the flower-arrow," also called "the five arrows," for it shoots a person's five senses — appearing amid form and creating every kind of state to delude and confuse a practitioner. In creating an agreeable state, it may take the form of parents or siblings, the very image of the buddhas, or the lovable form of a fine man or woman, drawing the mind into attachment. In creating a disagreeable state, it may take the form of a tiger, wolf, lion, or rākṣasa, every kind of terrifying image, to frighten a practitioner. In creating a state neither disagreeable nor agreeable, it stirs up the mind through ordinary matters, causing one to lose dhyāna-concentration — hence it is called Māra.

〖講〗三明魔惱，即天子魔也。目的在破二善增二惡，或遣魔兵魔將來，或天魔自來，此魔最難制伏。前堆剔時媚二魔，乃波旬遠屬，今魔惱皆波旬近屬……然魔惱亦名華箭，亦名五箭者，謂如飛來一朵花，眼看是花，其實中一箭，能傷身命，故名花箭。大論問曰：何名為魔？答：魔者，破慧命，壞道法，是故名魔……然此魔所現三種境界，不出乎於三毒：若作順情之境，謂父母兄弟諸佛形像，以及戲笑語言，歌舞邪觀等，是從愛生；若作違情，如作虎狼師子羅剎夜叉之形，及縛打鞭拷，斫刺剖截等，是從瞋生；若從非違非順之境，如五熱灸身，自餓投岩等，是從癡生。此等即是三賊之流，乃至貪染世間，皆是魔事。如婆沙論云：佛著衣持缽，入城乞食，波旬作是念：應當壞其道法。便現作御車人類，執鞭覓中，著敝壞衣，頭髮蓬亂，往至佛所，問言見我牛否？佛念魔惱我，即語魔言：惡魔，何處有牛，用是牛為。天魔即作是念，沙門知我是魔，即白佛言：眼觸入處是我乘，乃至意觸入處亦是我乘，沙門何所之？佛言：我到彼無六觸入處，汝所不到處，我當往彼，波旬意以我如御者，六觸如乘，能御此乘，運諸眾生，至於三界，所以涅槃非其到處。故佛言：我到無六觸，及汝所不到處，即涅槃也。

Commentary: This third section explains Māra's own disturbance — that is, the demon-son of the heavens. Its aim is to destroy two kinds of wholesome quality already attained and increase two kinds of evil not yet arisen; sometimes sending demon-soldiers and demon-generals, sometimes coming in person — this demon is the hardest of all to subdue. The nature-spirit and harassing spirit discussed above are Māra's distant relations; Māra's own disturbance is his close relation.

As for why Māra's own disturbance is also called "the flower-arrow" and "the five arrows": it is like a single flower flying toward one — to the eye it looks like a flower, but in truth one has been struck by an arrow, able to harm body and life. Hence it is called "the flower-arrow." The Great Treatise asks: what is called a demon? Answer: a demon is what breaks the life of wisdom and ruins the dharma of the path — hence it is called a demon...

The three kinds of state this demon manifests do not exceed the three poisons. In creating an agreeable state — parents, siblings, images of the buddhas, playful laughter and speech, song, dance, and deviant views — this arises from craving. In creating a disagreeable state — the form of a tiger, wolf, lion, rākṣasa, or yakṣa, binding, beating, whipping, cutting, and dismembering — this arises from anger. In creating a state neither disagreeable nor agreeable — such as scorching the body with the five fires, or starving and throwing oneself from a cliff — this arises from delusion. States of this kind belong to the very same class as the three poisons themselves, and even craving attachment to the world in general is likewise a demonic affair.

As the Abhidharma Mahāvibhāṣā Treatise says: the Buddha, having put on his robe and taken up his alms-bowl, entered the city to beg for food. Māra thought to himself: "I ought to destroy this monk's own dharma of the path." He then manifested as a cart-driver, whip in hand, wearing tattered clothes, hair disheveled, and approached the Buddha, asking: "Have you seen my ox?" The Buddha, recognizing this as Māra's own disturbance, said to him: "Evil demon, where would there ever be an ox at all, that you should need one?" The heavenly demon thought to himself: "This monk knows I am a demon" — and said to the Buddha: "The sense-field of visual contact is my own vehicle, and so on through the sense-field of mental contact, likewise my own vehicle. Monk, where do you think you are going?" The Buddha replied: "I am going to that place where none of the six kinds of sensory contact exist at all — the very place you yourself cannot reach. That is where I am going." Māra had taken himself to be like a charioteer, and the six kinds of sensory contact to be his own vehicle, able to drive this vehicle and carry every sentient being throughout the three realms — and so nirvāṇa itself is a place he can never reach. Hence the Buddha said: "I am going to the place free of the six kinds of sensory contact, the very place you cannot reach" — that is, nirvāṇa itself.

Chinese Source

〖文〗如經偈中說：
欲是汝初軍　憂愁為第二　饑渴第三軍　渴愛第四
睡眠第五軍　怖畏為第六　疑悔第七軍　瞋恚為第八
利養虛稱九　自高慢人十　如是等眾軍　壓沒出家人
我以禪智力　破汝此諸軍　得成佛道已　度脫一切人

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: As a sūtra verse says:
"Desire is your first army; sorrow the second. Hunger and thirst the third army; craving thirst the fourth.
Sleep the fifth army; dread the sixth. Doubt and regret the seventh army; anger the eighth.
Gain and false renown the ninth; self-conceit and contempt of others the tenth. Armies such as these overwhelm and bury the renunciant.
I, through the power of dhyāna and wisdom, break every one of your armies — and, having accomplished the buddha-path, will bring every person across."

〖講〗前來所明，鬼神魔及天子魔，謂堆剔鬼魔。或作種種好惡之音聲等，即色香味等五塵境界，以此五種惱亂於人，令失其善法，起諸煩惱，流轉生死……如雜寶經偈中所說：欲念是汝初軍，猶世間之兵有幾軍，其魔王亦分二三等之魔軍，最初第一軍者，即汝之欲念也。有如是等眾軍，壓沒出家人，我以禪定智慧之力，摧伏汝眾軍，汝雖不欲放，到汝不到處，即是得成無上真等正覺，證清淨無礙實相之涅槃也。從此起同體慈悲，以大雄大力大慈悲，大無畏之精神，隨流九界，廣度一切癡闇倒迷之苦惱眾生，故云得成佛道已，度脫一切人也。

Commentary: What has been explained above concerns the ghost-and-spirit demon and Māra's own demon-son — that is, the harassing spirit-demon among others, which manifests every kind of pleasing or displeasing sound and the like — the five sense-objects of form, sound, smell, taste, and touch — using these five to disturb a person, causing them to lose their own wholesome dharma, giving rise to affliction, and keeping them revolving in birth-and-death...

As the verse in the Miscellaneous Treasures Sūtra says: "Desire is your first army" — just as a worldly army has several divisions, so too the demon-king's own army divides into ranks — the very first rank being nothing other than your own desire. Armies of this kind overwhelm and bury the renunciant — yet I, through the power of dhyāna-concentration and wisdom, subdue and break every one of your armies; though you have no wish to let me pass, I will reach the very place you cannot reach — that is, accomplishing unsurpassed, true, complete awakening, realizing the pure, unobstructed true reality of nirvāṇa itself. From this, I give rise to compassion with a sense of shared identity, and, with great heroic strength, great compassion, and a spirit of great fearlessness, follow the stream throughout the nine realms of being, broadly bringing across every suffering, afflicted sentient being lost in ignorance and delusion — hence it is said, "having accomplished the buddha-path, will bring every person across."

Reading note: Baojing's own graded structure here — nature-spirits (distant relations of Māra, dealt with by simply naming them), the harassing spirit (a closer relation, dealt with by invoking the precepts), and Māra's own disturbance (his "close relation," the hardest to subdue) — makes an implicit point about escalation: as a practitioner's progress becomes more genuine, so does the demonic opposition to it, up to and including Māra questioning the Buddha himself directly. Notice, too, that the story of Māra as the cart-driver reframes the whole idea of "the six senses" as themselves Māra's own vehicle — the point being that liberation is not escaping demons through cleverness, but reaching a state (nirvāṇa) structurally outside anything sensory contact can touch at all.

How to Drive Off a Demon 卻魔方法

Chinese Source

〖文〗行者既覺知魔事，即當卻之。卻法有二：一者修止卻之。凡見一切外諸惡魔境，悉知虛誑，不憂不怖，亦不取不捨，妄計分別，息心寂然，彼自當滅。二者修觀卻之。若見如上所說種種魔境，用止不去，即當反觀能見之心，不見處所，彼何所惱，如是觀時，尋當滅謝。若遲遲不去，但當正心，勿生懼想，不惜軀命，正念不動，知魔界如，即佛界如，若魔界如、佛界如，一如無二如，如是了知，則魔界無所捨，佛界無所取，佛法自當現前，魔境自然消滅。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Once a practitioner is aware of a demonic affair, one should drive it off at once. There are two methods. The first is driving it off through calming: whenever one sees any outward, evil demonic state, recognizing it entirely as illusory and deceptive, one is neither anxious nor afraid, neither grasping nor rejecting it, giving rise to no false discrimination — the mind settles into stillness, and the demon fades away of itself. The second is driving it off through insight: if, seeing any of the various demonic states described above, calming alone fails to remove it, one should turn instead to observe the very mind that sees, finding it to have no location at all — what, then, is there left for the demon to trouble? Contemplating in this way, it will soon fade away. Should it still fail to depart, one need only keep the mind correct, giving rise to no thought of dread, never sparing one's own body or life, correct mindfulness unmoved — knowing that the suchness of the demon-realm is precisely the suchness of the buddha-realm: one and the same suchness, not two different kinds of suchness. Understanding this fully, there is nothing in the demon-realm to reject and nothing in the buddha-realm to grasp — the Buddhadharma manifests of itself, and the demonic state dissolves of itself.

〖講〗此明卻魔之方法。上來既發魔事，若不卻之，則為彼所惑亂。卻法有二，（1）者修止卻之，謂凡見外來一切違順諸魔境界，悉知其虛誑不實，心中不生憂怖，亦不生取捨，但息心寂然，魔自消滅矣！如智者大師，修觀於天台華頂，魔王變化其親戚眷屬，乃至親愛父母，皆來其前，大師知為魔境，師唯深念實相，安心空寂，了達一切，皆是幻化，本無所見，未幾魔即退滅，此全以止之功夫，卻諸魔羅。（2）者修觀，若用止不去，即當反觀能見之心，誰為能見，誰為所見，不見有所之相，內外覓求，了不可得，彼何所惱，如是觀時，尋當滅謝。此數句宜加注意，實為破魔之最良方法，若再遲遲不去，應當正其心誠其意，了知四大本空，五蘊非我，不生一念恐怖懼畏之想，且不惜軀命，正念不動，了達邪正，懷抱淡然，知魔界如佛界如，一如無二如，唯平等一相，不以魔為戚，佛為欣。安之實際，置之於寂滅。若能如是，則邪不干正，斯時正照昭彰，縱有魔等來惱亂，正可藉此魔事，而作不思議觀。即此魔境，當體具十界百法，一切法趣魔，是趣不過。如一夢法，具一切事。一魔一切魔，一切魔一魔，非一非一切，當下即空即假即中。一切魔一魔，真空也；一魔一切魔，妙假也；非一非一切，中道也。魔即法界，清淨本然，不出魔界，即是佛界，無二無別，無有異相，如是則魔界無所捨，佛界無所取。有佛無佛，性相常住，故佛法自當現前，魔境自然消滅矣。當知眾生，皆以取捨欣厭，故為魔所惱，不能了生脫死，亦即所謂魔不惱人，人自惱也。若心中不取不捨，不欣不厭，了達魔如佛如，即魔而了道，是為根本之法也。

Commentary: This explains the method for driving off a demon. Once a demonic affair has arisen, if it is not driven off, one falls prey to its deception. There are two methods. The first is driving it off through calming: whenever one sees any outward, agreeable or disagreeable demonic state, recognizing it entirely as illusory and unreal, giving rise to no anxiety or fear, and neither grasping nor rejecting it, but simply letting the mind settle into stillness — the demon dissolves of itself! Just as Great Master Zhiyi, cultivating contemplation at Huading on Mount Tiantai, had the demon-king transform into his own relatives and family members, even his own beloved parents, all appearing before him — the master, recognizing this as a demonic state, simply held true reality deeply in mind, settling the mind in empty stillness, fully understanding all of it to be illusory transformation, with, at root, nothing there to be seen at all — and before long the demon withdrew and vanished. This was accomplished entirely through the effort of calming, driving off every demon.

The second is cultivating insight: if calming alone fails to remove it, one should turn instead to observe the very mind that sees — who, in fact, is the seer, and what, in fact, is seen? Finding no object there at all, searching both inwardly and outwardly, it ultimately cannot be found — what, then, is there left for the demon to trouble? Contemplating in this way, it will soon fade away. These few lines deserve particular attention — they are, in truth, the very best method for breaking through a demon. Should it still fail to depart even then, one should correct the mind and make the intention sincere, fully understanding that the four great elements are, at root, empty, and the five aggregates are not a self, giving rise to not a single thought of dread or fear, never sparing one's own body or life, correct mindfulness unmoved, fully understanding both the deviant and the correct, holding them both with equanimity, knowing that the suchness of the demon-realm and the suchness of the buddha-realm are one and the same suchness, not two — a single, equal mark alone, treating the demon neither as an enemy nor the Buddha as a friend, settling into this reality, resting in still cessation itself.

If one is able to do this, the deviant cannot interfere with the correct — at this point correct illumination shines forth clearly, and even should demons come to disturb one, this very demonic affair can be turned into an object of inconceivable contemplation instead. This very demonic state, in its own substance, already contains the hundred dharmas of the ten realms — every dharma converges on this demon, going no further. It is like a single dream containing every event within it: one demon is every demon, every demon is one demon, neither one nor every — right where it stands, at once empty, at once conventional, at once the middle. Every demon being one demon is true emptiness; one demon being every demon is wondrous conventional existence; neither one nor every is the middle way.

The demon-realm is itself the Dharma-realm, inherently pure from the start — the demon-realm never departs from being the very buddha-realm itself, not two, not different, with no distinguishing mark between them at all. In this way there is nothing in the demon-realm to reject and nothing in the buddha-realm to grasp; whether a buddha appears or not, nature and characteristic remain ever-abiding — hence the Buddhadharma manifests of itself, and the demonic state dissolves of itself.

One should know that sentient beings, through grasping and rejecting, delight and aversion, are troubled by demons precisely because of this, and are unable to resolve birth-and-death — which is to say, it is not the demon that troubles a person, but the person who troubles themselves. If the mind neither grasps nor rejects, neither delights nor is averse, fully understanding that the demon's suchness and the Buddha's suchness are the same, then the very demon itself becomes the completion of the path — this is the most fundamental method of all.

Closing Guidance 結語

Chinese Source

〖文〗復次，若見魔境不謝，不須生憂；若見滅謝，亦勿生喜。所以者何？未曾有人坐禪，見魔化作虎狼來食人，亦未曾見魔化作男女來為夫婦。當其幻化，愚人不了，心生驚怖、及起貪著，因是心亂，失定發狂，自致其患，皆是行人無智受患，非魔所為。若諸魔境惱亂行人、或經年月不去，但當端心正念堅固，不惜身命，莫懷憂懼。當誦大乘方等諸經治魔咒，默念誦之，存念三寶。若出禪定，亦當誦咒自防，懺悔慚愧、及誦波羅提木叉。邪不干正，久久自滅。魔事眾多，說不可盡，善須識之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Further, should a demonic state fail to fade away, one need not grow anxious about it; should it fade away, one need not feel glad about it either. Why is this? No one, sitting in meditation, has ever actually been eaten by a demon transformed into a tiger or wolf, nor has anyone ever actually become husband or wife with a demon transformed into a man or woman. When such an illusory transformation occurs, a foolish person, failing to understand it, gives rise to fright or craving attachment, and, through this, the mind grows disordered, concentration is lost, and derangement follows — this affliction is entirely brought on by the practitioner's own lack of wisdom, not by anything the demon has actually done. Should demonic states trouble a practitioner and fail to depart for months or even years, one need only keep the mind correct, mindfulness firm, never sparing one's own body or life, harboring no anxiety or fear. One should recite mantras for countering demons from the great Mahāyāna vaipulya sūtras, reciting them silently, holding the Three Jewels in mind. Upon rising from concentration, one should likewise recite mantras to protect oneself, and repent with a sense of shame, reciting the prātimokṣa as well. The deviant cannot interfere with the correct, and in time it will fade away of itself. Demonic affairs are numerous beyond telling; one must skillfully recognize them.

〖講〗大乘方等經，即楞嚴、維摩、思益、解脫深密等經。治魔咒，即楞嚴、大悲等咒。三寶即佛法僧，皆為世間可尊可貴，故稱為寶。然有住持、同體、別相、大小乘之不同。波羅提木叉，此云戒，亦分大小乘之別。若行者於定中為魔惱亂時，默念一切戒相，及三寶諸大乘經，魔羅自然消滅，三昧自然現前。是故初心行人，必須親近善知識，為有如此等難事。是魔入人心，能令行者心神狂亂，或喜或憂，因是成患致死；或時令得諸邪禪定、智慧、神通、陀羅尼，說法教化，人皆信伏，後即壞人出世善事、及破壞正法。如是等諸異非一，說不可盡，今略示其要，為令行人於坐禪中，不妄受諸境界。取要言之，若欲遣邪歸正，當觀諸法實相，善修止觀，無邪不破。故釋論云：除諸法實相，其餘一切皆是魔事。如偈中說：
若分別憶想　即是魔羅網
不動不分別　是則為法印

Commentary: The great Mahāyāna vaipulya sūtras are the Śūraṅgama, the Vimalakīrti, the Sūtra on the Contemplation of the Mind's Own Purification, the Sūtra of Liberation Through Understanding the Profound and Secret, and others. Mantras for countering demons are the Śūraṅgama Mantra, the Great Compassion Mantra, and others. The Three Jewels are the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha — all things honored and treasured throughout the world, hence called "jewels," though there are distinctions between the abiding, the identical-substance, the distinct-aspect, and the greater- and lesser-vehicle senses of the term. "Prātimokṣa" means the precepts, likewise divided into greater- and lesser-vehicle forms. If a practitioner, troubled by a demon within concentration, silently recites every precept and the Mahāyāna sūtras of the Three Jewels, the demon naturally dissolves, and samādhi naturally manifests.

This is why a beginning practitioner must, without fail, stay close to a good spiritual friend — for difficulties of this kind do occur. A demon entering a person's own mind is able to throw a practitioner's mind and spirit into derangement, alternating between elation and distress, and can, through this, bring on genuine affliction and even death; or, at times, it grants a certain deviant dhyāna-concentration, wisdom, spiritual power, or dhāraṇī, teaching and instructing others so that people place their faith and submission in it — only afterward destroying that person's own transmundane, wholesome undertaking, and ruining the correct Dharma. Manifestations of this kind, in all their variety, are more than can be told; only their essential outline is given here, so that a practitioner, while sitting in meditation, does not carelessly accept whatever state arises.

Stated most essentially: if one wishes to dispel the deviant and return to the correct, one should contemplate the true reality of every dharma, skillfully cultivating calming and insight — nothing deviant then remains unbroken. Hence the Treatise says: "Apart from the true reality of every dharma, everything else is a demonic affair." As a verse says:
"Wherever there is discrimination and conceptual thought, this is precisely Māra's own net; where there is no movement and no discrimination at all, this is precisely the seal of the Dharma."

Reading note: Baojing's closing move in this chapter is worth reading against his own opening definition of a demon as "the killer" — by the end, the practical instruction has shifted almost entirely away from combat imagery and toward equanimity: "should it fail to depart, don't be anxious; should it depart, don't be glad." The two methods of driving off a demon (calming, then insight if calming alone doesn't work) exactly mirror the two-tiered structure Baojing has used throughout this commentary for every other obstacle — desire in chapter 2, the hindrances in chapter 3, sinking or floating attention in chapter 6 — reinforcing that a demonic disturbance is, in his own account, ultimately just one more object calming and insight are already equipped to handle, not a categorically different kind of threat.
