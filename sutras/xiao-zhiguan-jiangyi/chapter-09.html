Curing Illness

Meditation itself can stir up illness the same way it stirs up wholesome roots — and a body too sick to sit is a body that cannot practice at all. Baojing works through how to recognize illness by its source (the four elements, the five organs, a ghost, or one's own past karma), then a wide survey of methods for treating it through calming and insight themselves, closing with ten practical habits that make any of these methods actually work.

Why This Chapter Is Needed 治病之要

Chinese Source

〖文〗行者安心修道，或四大有病，因今用觀心息鼓擊發動本病；或時不能善調適身心息三事，內外有所違犯，故有病患。夫坐禪之法，若能用心者，則四百四病自然除差；若用心失所，則四百四病因之發生。是故若自行化他，應當善識病源，善知坐中內心治病方法，一旦動病，非惟行道有障，則大命慮失。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: A practitioner settling the mind to cultivate the path may find that the four great elements fall ill, because the very effort of contemplation stirs the breath and, in turn, stirs an underlying illness into activity; or, at times, one is simply unable to properly regulate body, mind, and breath together, violating some rule inwardly or outwardly, and illness results from this. As for the method of sitting in meditation: if one applies the mind well, the four hundred and four illnesses are naturally cured of themselves; if one applies the mind wrongly, the four hundred and four illnesses arise because of it. This is why, whether for one's own practice or for guiding others, one must skillfully recognize the source of illness, and skillfully know the inward methods for treating illness while seated — for once illness has actually taken hold, it is not merely an obstruction to practicing the path, but a genuine risk to one's very life.

〖講〗止觀共十章，前八章已竟，今第九明治病。夫吾人眾生所秉之五蘊色身，乃四大所成，有身即是有病，以其四蛇異性，水火相違，鴟梟共棲，蟒鼠同穴故也。但病分權實不同，（1）因中實病，（2）果中權病。權即權巧也，如淨名居士，偃臥毗耶，託疾興教……此皆是權病……如此權病，非今所治，今所治者，即業報生身，四蛇動作，互相侵毀所起之病也。若身長病，是禪定之大障，久染身疾，失所修福，起無邊罪。經云：破壞浮囊，發撤橋樑，妄失正念，即此義也……以是因緣，須對治病魔，當知法不孤起，仗緣乃生。今病魔由何而起，不無因緣，行者安心修道，或四大不順有病，或飲食不節故病，或時於禪定中，不能善巧調適身心息三事故病，或時守心失慎，鬼神得便故病，或魔所為，或業報發起種種病患……修行坐禪，要在用心，此心用得好，則獲無量利益，若用不好，則生許多過失。此身四大所成，一大不調，則有一百一病，四大不調，即有四百四病；用心謹慎，則諸病自然得差，若用心失所，則一切病患因之發生。故吾人欲期成就自利利他之德，上求下化之功，應當善識病患根源。又須知坐中內心治病方法，能知病識藥，則可應病與藥也，否則一人修道，獨住茅蓬，居於深山窮谷之中，若一旦病發，自無治病之法，非但道業為之障礙，且大命亦有危險殞亡之慮。然略生微疾，亦無食藥之必要，以身有拒抗力故，如富人病多，因多食病反增，窮人病少，雖不服藥，而有自身拒抗力反易愈也。

Commentary: Calming and insight comprises ten chapters in all; the eight above are now complete, and this ninth explains curing illness. This body of the five aggregates that we sentient beings carry is made up of the four great elements, and to have a body at all is to have the potential for illness — for these four "serpents" differ in nature, water and fire opposed to each other, an owl and a kite forced to share the same roost, a python and a rat forced to share the same den.

Illness, however, divides into the expedient and the actual. Actual illness belongs to the causal stage; expedient illness belongs to the fruit stage. "Expedient" means a skillful device — as when the layman Vimalakīrti lay ill in Vaiśālī, using his own illness as an occasion to teach... All of this is expedient illness... An illness of this expedient kind is not what this chapter addresses. What this chapter addresses is precisely the illness that arises from one's own karmically-produced body, the four "serpents" acting up and encroaching upon one another.

Should the body fall into a prolonged illness, this is a great obstruction to dhyāna-concentration — a body long afflicted with illness loses the very merit one was cultivating, and gives rise to boundless offense besides. A sūtra says: "breaking the floating raft, dismantling the very bridge, falsely losing correct mindfulness" — this is precisely the meaning here... For reasons of this kind, one must counter this demon of illness. One should know that no dharma arises on its own — it always relies on some condition to arise. From what, then, does this demon of illness actually arise? It is not without its own causes and conditions: a practitioner settling the mind to cultivate the path may fall ill because the four great elements have fallen out of balance, or because of immoderate eating and drinking, or because one was, at some point during dhyāna-concentration, unable to skillfully regulate body, mind, and breath together, or because one grew careless in guarding the mind and a spirit found its opening, or because of a demon's own doing, or because of karmic retribution giving rise to various afflictions...

The practice of sitting in meditation depends, above all, on how the mind is applied — applied well, it yields measureless benefit; applied poorly, it gives rise to many faults instead. This body, made up of the four great elements, falls into a hundred and one illnesses should even one element fall out of balance, and four hundred and four illnesses should all four fall out of balance; applying the mind carefully, every illness is naturally cured of itself, while applying it wrongly gives rise to every kind of affliction because of it. This is why anyone who hopes to accomplish the virtue of benefiting both self and other, seeking the path above and transforming beings below, must skillfully recognize the root source of illness — and must further know the inward methods for treating illness while seated, so that, knowing the illness, one can recognize the proper remedy and apply it accordingly. Otherwise, a person cultivating the path alone, dwelling by themselves in a thatched hut deep in the mountains and valleys, has, should illness suddenly strike, no method at all for treating it — not only obstructing one's own practice along the path, but placing one's very life in real danger. Yet for a merely minor, passing ailment, taking medicine is not strictly necessary either, since the body has its own power of resistance — just as a wealthy person, prone to illness, often makes it worse by overmedicating, while a person of modest means, less prone to illness, more easily recovers precisely by relying on the body's own natural resistance without medicating at all.

Recognizing the Mark of Illness 明病發相

1. Imbalance in the Four Great Elements 四大增損病相

Chinese Source

〖文〗今明治病法中有二意：一明病發相，二明治病方法。一明病發相者。病發雖復多途，略出不過二種：一者四大增損病相。若地大增者，則腫結沈重，身體枯瘠，如是等百一患生。若水大增者，則痰陰脹滿，食飲不消，腹病下痢等百一患生。若火大增者，即煎寒壯熱，支節皆痛，口氣大小，便痢不通等百一患生。若風大增者，則身體虛懸，戰掉疼痛，肺悶脹氣，嘔逆氣急，如是等百一患生。故經云：一大不調，百一病起；四大不調，四百四病一時俱動。四大病發，各有相貌，當於坐時及夢中察之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Explaining the method of treating illness now has two aspects: first, recognizing the mark of illness arising; second, the method for treating illness. As for recognizing the mark of illness arising: illness arises through many paths, but briefly falls into two kinds. The first is the mark of imbalance among the four great elements. If the earth element increases, one develops swelling, hardness, and heaviness, the body withered and gaunt — a hundred and one such afflictions arise. If the water element increases, one develops phlegm and distended bloating, indigestion, abdominal pain and diarrhea — a hundred and one such afflictions arise. If the fire element increases, one develops chills and high fever, pain throughout every joint, foul breath, and blocked urination or bowel movements — a hundred and one such afflictions arise. If the wind element increases, the body feels hollow and unsteady, trembling and aching, the lungs congested and the abdomen bloated, retching and labored breathing — a hundred and one such afflictions arise. Hence a sūtra says: one element out of balance gives rise to a hundred and one illnesses; all four out of balance, and four hundred and four illnesses arise together at once. Each of the four elements' own illnesses carries its own particular mark, to be observed both while seated and in dreams.

〖講〗今明以下是分科解釋，一明四大增損病相者，以吾人之身，地水火風四大組成，若一大有增或減，則生疾病，故四大非均等不可。若地大增於其他三大，則身體苦重，堅結疼痛，枯癉痿瘠，如是等一百一患生，是地大病相，餘三大增損如文。又復四大不順，亦能生種種病患，行役無時，強健擔負，常觸寒熱，外熱助火，火強則水力減，是增火病；若外寒助水，水增害火，是為水病；若外風助氣，氣吹於火，火動於水，是風病相；倘若水火風三大增害於地，名等分病；若身分增害水火風，亦是等分屬地病；若四大既動，則眾惱競生，故經云：一大不調，百一病起，若四大悉皆不調，則四百四病，一時俱動，諸如此等病患發動時，則一一病，各有其相貌。當於坐時，及夢中觀察之。觀其病魔由何而來，先欲知其病之根源，然後施以種種方法而對治之。

Commentary: What follows below now divides into sections to explain this. First, explaining the mark of imbalance among the four great elements: our own body is made up of earth, water, fire, and wind, and should any one of these increase or decrease, illness results — hence the four elements must, without exception, be kept in balance. If the earth element increases relative to the other three, the body grows heavy and burdened, hard, knotted, and aching, withered, jaundiced, and gaunt — a hundred and one such afflictions arise; this is the mark of the earth element's own illness, and the marks of the other three elements' imbalance follow the text in just the same way.

Overwork without rest, exerting oneself while carrying a heavy load, constant exposure to cold and heat — outward heat aiding the fire element, and the fire element growing strong while the water element's own strength diminishes, is an increase of the fire-illness; outward cold aiding the water element, harming the fire element, is the water-illness; outward wind aiding the qi, the qi fanning the fire, the fire stirring the water, is the mark of the wind-illness. Should water, fire, and wind together increase and harm the earth element, this is called the "equal-portion illness"; should the body itself increase and harm water, fire, and wind, this too is an equal-portion illness belonging to earth. Once the four elements are thrown into disorder, every kind of affliction competes to arise — hence the sūtra says: one element out of balance gives rise to a hundred and one illnesses; should all four fall entirely out of balance, four hundred and four illnesses arise together at once. Whenever afflictions of this kind arise, each carries its own particular mark, to be observed both while seated and in dreams — observing where this demon of illness has actually come from, first seeking to know the root source of the illness, and only then applying various methods to counter it accordingly.

2. Illness Arising From the Five Organs 五藏生患之相

Chinese Source

〖文〗二者五藏生患之相。從心生患者，身體寒熱，及頭痛口燥等，心主口故。從肺生患者，身體脹滿四肢煩疼，心悶鼻塞等，肺主鼻故。從肝生患者，多無喜心，憂愁不樂，悲思瞋恚，頭痛眼闇昏悶等，肝主眼故。從脾生患者，身體面上，遊風遍身，痼癢疼痛，飲食失味等，脾主舌故。從腎生患者，咽喉噎塞，腹脹耳聾等，腎主耳故。五藏生病眾多，各有其相，當於坐時及夢中察之可知。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: The second is the mark of illness arising from the five organs. Illness arising from the heart: the body alternating between cold and hot, headache, dry mouth, and the like, since the heart governs the mouth. Illness arising from the lungs: the body bloated and full, aching in all four limbs, a stifled feeling in the chest, a blocked nose, and the like, since the lungs govern the nose. Illness arising from the liver: little joy in the mind, melancholy and unhappiness, sorrowful brooding and anger, headache, dim eyesight and confusion, and the like, since the liver governs the eyes. Illness arising from the spleen: a roaming rash across the face and body, chronic itching and pain, loss of taste in food and drink, and the like, since the spleen governs the tongue. Illness arising from the kidneys: a choked, blocked throat, abdominal bloating, deafness, and the like, since the kidneys govern the ears. Illness arising from the five organs is extremely varied, each with its own particular mark, to be recognized by observing both while seated and in dreams.

〖講〗此第二明五藏生病之相……若欲知五藏病相，務須認識脈法，方能治病。善醫術者，巧知四大，謂上醫聽聲，中醫看色相，下醫診脈，但用功者，不須精明醫法，略得常識可耳……若五行相剋，而致五藏病生者，釋如正文，但須識得五行與五藏，及五根相屬相剋之義。謂東方甲乙木，其色青，屬肝，主眼；南方丙丁火，其色赤，屬心，主舌；西方庚辛金，其色白，屬肺，主鼻；北方壬癸水，其色黑，屬腎，主耳；中央戊己土，其色黃，屬脾，主身……然又須知六神病相，若覺多惛惛，是肝中無魂；若多忘失前後，是心中無神；若多諸恐怖癲病，是肺中無魂；若多好悲笑，是腎中無志；若多諸迴惑，是脾中無意；若多悵快，是陰中無精，此明六神中有病之相。當知五藏六腑，所生病患眾多，不勝枚舉，但亦各有其相貌，行者於坐時，及夢中察之可知。

Commentary: This second part explains the mark of illness arising from the five organs... To recognize the mark of illness in the five organs, one must be familiar with the method of pulse-diagnosis before illness can actually be treated. A skilled physician knows the four elements: the finest physician diagnoses by listening to the voice, a middling physician by observing complexion, and a lesser physician by feeling the pulse — but for one applying effort in practice, precise medical skill is not required, only a general working knowledge...

As for how the mutual conquest among the five phases gives rise to illness in the five organs, this is explained in the root text, but one must understand the correspondence between the five phases, the five organs, and the five faculties: the east, wood, its color green, belongs to the liver, governing the eyes; the south, fire, its color red, belongs to the heart, governing the tongue; the west, metal, its color white, belongs to the lungs, governing the nose; the north, water, its color black, belongs to the kidneys, governing the ears; the center, earth, its color yellow, belongs to the spleen, governing the body as a whole...

One must further know the marks of illness in the six spirits: a tendency toward dullness and confusion means the liver lacks its own spirit; frequent forgetfulness means the heart lacks its own spirit; frequent dread and fits of madness mean the lungs lack their own spirit; a tendency toward sorrowful laughter means the kidneys lack their own resolve; frequent confusion means the spleen lacks its own intention; frequent melancholy dejection means the yin lacks its own essence — these explain the marks of illness among the six spirits. One should know that illnesses arising from the five organs and six viscera are extremely numerous, too many to list individually, yet each carries its own particular mark, to be recognized by a practitioner observing both while seated and in dreams.

3. Three Causes of Illness 三種得病因緣

Chinese Source

〖文〗如是四大五藏病患因起非一，病相眾多，不可具說。行者若欲修止觀法門，脫有患生，應當善知因起。此二種病，通因內外發動。若外傷寒冷風熱，飲食不消，而病從二處發者，當知因外發動。若由用心不調，觀行違僻，或因定法發時，不知取與，而致此二處患生，此因內發病相。復次，有三種得病因緣不同。一者四大五藏增損得病如前說，二者鬼神所作得病，三者業報得病。如是等病，初得即治，甚易得差；若經久則病成，身羸病結，治之難愈。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: As explained, illnesses of the four elements and five organs arise through many causes and take numerous marks, more than can be told in full. A practitioner who wishes to cultivate the dharma-gate of calming and insight, should illness happen to arise, should skillfully understand its cause. These two kinds of illness both arise through causes both outward and inward. If it is from outward injury by cold, wind, or heat, or from indigestion, and illness arises from these two sources, one should know it to be caused outwardly. If it is from unregulated application of the mind, deviant contemplative practice, or, when a state of concentration arises, not knowing how to properly take it up or set it aside, and illness arises from these two sources, this is the mark of illness caused inwardly. Further, there are three distinct causes for the arising of illness: first, illness from imbalance in the four elements and five organs, as explained above; second, illness caused by ghosts and spirits; third, illness from karmic retribution. Illnesses of this kind, treated as soon as they first arise, are very easily cured — but should they persist over a long time, the illness becomes established, the body weakened and the illness deeply knotted, difficult to cure.

〖講〗如是二字，乃承上起下之辭……佛法之中，亦有五明之學，謂聲明、工巧明、醫方明、因明、內明，是則亦有學醫道一門也……然佛法雖非世間，而不離世間覺，故假世間之醫術，醫眾生之身病，喻如來所說之法藥，醫眾生之內心煩惱病。應知眾生種種病患，善識其所之因緣，於定發時，宜取則取，宜與則與，若不知取與，而致四大五藏二處患生，此因內發病相。

Commentary: "As explained" here carries forward what came before into what follows... Within the Buddhadharma there is also the study of the five sciences — grammar, arts and crafts, medicine, logic, and inner wisdom — and so there is indeed a place for the study of medicine as one of these... Though the Buddhadharma is not itself worldly, it is never separate from worldly awareness either, and so it borrows worldly medical skill to treat sentient beings' own bodily illness, just as the Tathāgata's own teaching serves as the Dharma-medicine treating sentient beings' own inward afflictions of the mind. One should recognize the various illnesses of sentient beings, skillfully understanding their own causes and conditions — when a state of concentration arises, taking it up where taking it up is fitting, setting it aside where setting it aside is fitting; failing to know which, illness in the two sources of the four elements and five organs results — this is the mark of illness caused inwardly.

〖講〗二明鬼神病者。當知四大五藏病非鬼病，鬼病非四大五藏病。若言無鬼病者，邪巫之徒，一向作鬼治，有時得愈；若言無四大病者，醫方一向作湯藥治，有時愈，足徵有鬼神病也。又如往昔舍利弗，在耆闍崛山中，入於金剛三昧，時有二鬼，從空中過，一名伽羅，二名優婆伽羅，彼遙見舍利弗，結跏趺坐，在然入定，伽羅鬼謂彼鬼曰：「我今堪能以拳打沙門頭。」時優婆伽羅鬼語之曰：「汝勿興此意打沙門頭，所以然者；因此沙門，乃世尊弟子，聰明智慧，最為第一。」是時再三言曰：「我能堪任打此沙門頭。」善鬼曰：「汝若不聽我語者，汝便住此，吾捨汝去。」惡鬼對曰：「汝畏此沙門乎？」善鬼曰：「我實畏之，設汝以手打此沙門者，地當分為二分，當暴風疾雨，地亦振動，諸天驚怖，四天王知，我等則不安其所。」惡鬼聞之勿聽，善鬼便捨去，時彼惡鬼，即往打舍利弗頭，爾時天地大動，四面暴風疾雨，驟然而至，地亦分為二分，惡鬼全身墮地獄中，時舍利弗從三昧起，正衣往詣迦蘭陀竹世尊所，頭面禮足，在一面坐，時佛告舍利弗：「汝今身體無疾病否？」答曰：「體素無患，唯苦頭痛。」世尊便告曰：「有伽羅鬼手打汝頭，若彼鬼手打須彌山者，便分為二分，幸汝入金剛三昧，仗此三昧神力，故無所傷害，否則危險極矣。」此因緣出增一阿含經，甚為詳細，故知鬼神作亂，亦能生病也。三業報病者，或專由先世宿業而生病，或因今生破戒，發動先世罪業而成病。但業力成病，還約五根知有所犯，若殺罪之業，是肝眼病；飲酒罪業，是心口病；淫欲罪業，是腎耳病；妄語罪業，是脾舌病；若偷盜罪業，是肺鼻病；破毀五戒之罪業，則有五藏五根病起，若業報謝滅，病乃得差；然亦須知今生嚴持禁戒，亦可引動先業而生病。若有重罪，理應受地獄重罪，而人中輕償，但頭病即除罪，此約業欲遷謝故病也。當知如是鬼神業報魔等病，初得治之甚易，久之則病成，如樹之根蒂固，欲拔之甚不易也。

Commentary: Second, explaining illness caused by ghosts and spirits. One should know that an illness of the four elements and five organs is not a ghost-illness, and a ghost-illness is not an illness of the four elements and five organs. If one claims ghost-illness does not exist, then how is it that deviant shamans, treating illness entirely through spirit-methods, sometimes succeed in curing it? If one claims illness of the four elements does not exist, then how is it that physicians, treating illness entirely through medicinal decoctions, sometimes succeed in curing it? This is enough to prove that ghost-and-spirit illness genuinely exists.

Long ago, Śāriputra, on Vulture Peak, entered the vajra samādhi. At that time two spirits passed by overhead — one named Kāla, the other Upakāla. Seeing Śāriputra seated in the lotus posture, deep in concentration, from a distance, the spirit Kāla said to his companion: "I am now capable of striking this monk's head with my fist." Upakāla replied: "Do not think of striking this monk's head — for this monk is the World-Honored One's own disciple, foremost in wisdom and intelligence." Kāla insisted, two or three times: "I am fully capable of striking this monk's head." The good spirit said: "If you will not heed my words, then stay here — I am leaving you." The evil spirit asked: "Are you afraid of this monk?" The good spirit answered: "I truly am afraid of him — should you strike this monk with your hand, the earth itself would split in two, a violent storm of wind and rain would follow, the earth would quake, the heavenly beings would be terrified, and the Four Heavenly Kings would take notice — we ourselves would then have no safe place left to dwell." The evil spirit did not heed this, and the good spirit departed. The evil spirit then went and struck Śāriputra's head — at that moment heaven and earth shook violently, a fierce storm of wind and rain broke out on every side, and the earth split in two; the evil spirit's entire body fell straight into hell.

Śāriputra then rose from samādhi, straightened his robe, and went to the World-Honored One at the Bamboo Grove, bowing at his feet and sitting to one side. The Buddha asked him: "Is your body free of illness now?" He answered: "My body is, in itself, free of affliction — only my head aches somewhat." The World-Honored One then told him: "The spirit Kāla struck your head with his hand — had that same spirit's hand struck Mount Sumeru itself, it would have split in two. Fortunate that you had entered the vajra samādhi, relying on this very samādhi's own spiritual power, you suffered no real harm — otherwise the danger would have been extreme indeed." This story comes from the Ekottara Āgama, told there in great detail — from this we know that disturbance caused by ghosts and spirits is genuinely able to produce illness.

Third, illness from karmic retribution: this may arise entirely from karma accumulated in a past life, or from breaking the precepts in this present life, stirring dormant karmic offense from a past life into activity as illness. Illness produced by karmic force corresponds to the five faculties according to the specific offense committed: the karma of killing produces illness of the liver and eyes; the karma of drinking alcohol produces illness of the heart and mouth; the karma of sexual misconduct produces illness of the kidneys and ears; the karma of false speech produces illness of the spleen and tongue; the karma of stealing produces illness of the lungs and nose; and breaking all five precepts together brings illness across all five organs and faculties at once. Once the karmic retribution is exhausted, the illness is cured.

One must further know that strictly upholding the precepts in this present life can itself stir dormant karma from a past life into activity as illness — if a grave offense would, by rights, call for suffering in hell, and is instead repaid lightly, in the human realm, through a mere headache, this too is a form of illness tied to karma being resolved and dissolved. One should know that illnesses of this kind — caused by ghosts and spirits, by karmic retribution, or by demons — are, if treated as soon as they first arise, very easily cured; but should they persist over a long time, the illness becomes as firmly rooted as a tree, very difficult indeed to uproot.

Methods for Treating Illness 明治病方法

1. Treating Illness Through Calming 以止治病

Chinese Source

〖文〗二明治病方法者。即深知病源起發，當作方法治之。治病之法，乃有多途。舉要言之，不出止觀二種方便。云何用止治病相。有師言：但安心止在病處，即能治病。所以者何？心是一期果報之主，譬如王有所至處，群賊迸散。次有師言：臍下一寸名憂陀那，此云丹田，若能止心守此不散，經久即多有所治。有師言：常止心足下，莫問行住寢臥，即能治病。所以者何？人以四大不調，故多諸疾患，此由心識上緣，故令四大不調；若安心在下，四大自然調適，眾病除矣！有師言：但知諸法空無所有，不取病相，寂然止住，多有所治。所以者何？由心憶想鼓作四大，故有病生，息心和悅，眾病即差。故淨名經云：何為病本？所謂攀緣。云何斷攀緣？謂心無所得。如是種種說，用止治病之相非一，故知善修止法，能治眾病。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Second, explaining the method of treating illness: understanding deeply the source from which the illness arose, one applies a method to treat it accordingly. The methods for treating illness are many, but, stated essentially, they do not exceed the two skillful means of calming and insight. What is treating the mark of illness through calming? One teacher says: simply settle the mind, fixed on the location of the illness, and this alone is able to cure it. Why is this? Because the mind is the very master of this present life's own karmic fruit — just as, wherever a king goes, a band of thieves scatters and flees. Another teacher says: one inch below the navel is called the elixir field; if one is able to fix the mind there, guarding it without scattering, in time much can be cured this way. Another teacher says: constantly fix the mind at the soles of the feet, whether walking, standing, sleeping, or lying down, and this alone is able to cure illness. Why is this? Because people, their four elements out of balance, fall prey to many afflictions precisely because the mind and consciousness are drawn upward — and so the four elements fall out of balance; settling the mind downward instead, the four elements naturally come into balance, and every illness is thereby removed! Another teacher says: simply knowing that every dharma is empty, with nothing there at all, not grasping at the mark of the illness, letting the mind settle into stillness — much can be cured this way. Why is this? Because it is precisely the mind's own recollection and imagination that stirs the four elements into activity, giving rise to illness in the first place; once the mind is stilled into harmonious contentment, every illness is cured. Hence the Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "What is the root of illness? It is attachment to objects." "How does one sever this attachment to objects? By the mind attaining nothing at all." Explanations of this kind, using calming to treat the mark of illness, are numerous — from this we know that skillfully cultivating calming is able to cure every illness.

〖講〗第二使行者知治病方法……若行役飲食而致病者，此須方藥來治；若坐禪不調而致患者，仍須坐禪善調息觀而治之，非湯藥所宜；若鬼魔二病者，須深剋以觀行之力，及大悲神咒力治；若業報病者，當內用觀行力，外加懇誠懺悔，乃可得差……然若依經律論所明治病方法，則多多無量，舉要言之，不出止觀二種方便。以止觀二法，乃愈病之阿伽陀藥，誠萬病總持之大陀羅尼也……又有一位大德所言：臍下一寸，名憂陀那，此云丹田……行者若能止心於此，守令不散，經久即多有所治，以其丹田乃是氣海，能銷吞萬病，若止心於此，則氣息調和，故能愈疾……又有師言：「常止心足下，即能治病。」以吾人所生諸患，皆由四大不調。四大不調，悉由心識上緣，心使風，風動火，火融水，水潤身，是則上分調，而下分亂，以致諸患迸起。心若緣下，則如吹火下溜，飲食消化，五藏順而四大亦調順矣……夫吾人為講道論學，宏揚佛法，何訣醫學為？蓋現欲了生脫死，剋證佛果，必須假此四大幻有之身而修，方能剋證，若無此身，即不能修道，所謂借假以修真也，故須四大調和……故又有師言：「但知諸法空無所有。」了知凡所有相，皆是幻矣！一切諸法，法法皆是無相，故如是寂然止住，不取病患之相，則病多有所治……維摩云：「何為病本？所謂攀緣。」因執故，所以攀緣。因攀緣故，有所得，有所得則有病。若欲求無所得心，須斷攀緣，欲斷攀緣，不生執著，最為要緊。云何斷攀緣？謂心無所得也。二祖云：「覓心了不可得。」楞嚴經中七處徵心，非內外中間，乃至心無所著，如是等即斷攀緣之證。

Commentary: This second part lets a practitioner know the method for treating illness... If illness comes from overwork or diet, medicine is what is needed to treat it; if it comes from unregulated seated meditation, one must still treat it through skillfully regulating breath and contemplation in meditation itself, not through medicine; if it is an illness caused by a ghost or demon, one must overcome it through the power of contemplative practice together with the power of the Great Compassion Mantra; if it is illness from karmic retribution, one should apply the power of contemplative practice inwardly, together with earnest, sincere repentance outwardly, before it can actually be cured...

The methods for treating illness explained across the sūtras, vinaya, and treatises are measureless in number, but, stated essentially, do not exceed the two skillful means of calming and insight — for calming and insight are themselves the very medicine that cures every illness, in truth the great dhāraṇī that holds every remedy for every disease within it...

A certain great worthy said: one inch below the navel is called the elixir field... A practitioner able to fix the mind here, guarding it without letting it scatter, can, in time, cure much this way — for the elixir field is itself an ocean of vital energy, able to dissolve and absorb every illness; fixing the mind here brings the breath into harmony, and illness is thereby cured...

Another teacher said: "Constantly fixing the mind at the soles of the feet is able to cure illness." Every affliction we suffer arises from the four elements falling out of balance, and the four elements fall out of balance entirely because the mind and consciousness are drawn upward — mind drives wind, wind stirs fire, fire melts water, water moistens the body — so that the upper portion of the body is regulated while the lower portion falls into disorder, and every kind of affliction bursts forth as a result. If the mind is instead directed downward, it is like blowing a flame downward — food and drink digest properly, the five organs fall into accord, and the four elements likewise come into balance...

Now, we who expound the path and discuss its teachings, spreading the Buddhadharma — why should we need any secret medical knowledge at all? Because, in wishing to resolve birth-and-death and realize the fruit of buddhahood in the present, one must rely on this very illusory body of the four elements to actually cultivate the path — without this body, one cannot cultivate the path at all; this is what is meant by "borrowing the illusory to cultivate the true" — hence the four elements must be kept in harmony...

Hence another teacher said: "Simply know that every dharma is empty, with nothing there at all." Fully understanding that whatever has a mark at all is entirely illusory, that every dharma, without exception, is markless — settling the mind into stillness in this way, not grasping at the mark of the illness, much can be cured this way... The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says: "What is the root of illness? It is attachment to objects." Because of clinging, one becomes attached to objects; because of this attachment, something is grasped; and where something is grasped, illness follows. If one wishes to attain a mind that grasps nothing at all, one must sever this attachment to objects — and to sever this attachment, giving rise to no clinging at all matters more than anything else. How does one sever this attachment to objects? By the mind attaining nothing at all. The Second Patriarch said: "Searching for the mind, it cannot ultimately be found." The Śūraṅgama Sūtra's own sevenfold search for the mind — neither inside, outside, nor in between, until the mind clings to nothing at all — these are all proof of severing this very attachment to objects.

2. Treating Illness Through Insight 以觀治病

Chinese Source

〖文〗次明觀治病者。有師言：但觀心想，用六種氣治病者，即是觀能治病。何等六種氣？一吹、二呼、三嘻、四呵、五噓、六呬、此六種息，皆於唇口之中，想心方便，轉側而作，綿微而用。頌曰：
心配屬呵腎屬吹　脾呼肺呬聖皆知
肝藏熱來噓字至　三焦壅處但言嘻

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Next, explaining treating illness through insight. One teacher says: simply contemplating the mind's own thought and using the six kinds of breath to treat illness — this is treating illness through insight. What are the six kinds of breath? Chuī, hū, xī, hē, xū, and sì. These six kinds of breath are all produced through the lips and mouth, skillfully applied by the mind's own intention, adjusted as needed, used subtly and continuously. A verse says:
"The heart matches hē, the kidneys chuī; the spleen uses hū, the lungs sì — the sages all know this. When heat gathers in the liver, xū arrives; wherever the triple burner is blocked, simply say xī."

〖講〗其次明以觀對治諸病。上言以止治病，其法非一，今以觀行治病，亦多多無量。有師言：「但觀心想，用六氣治，即能治病。」所謂觀其能，不觀其所也……治五藏者，應於牙齒唇舌調停而出，全以想心方便，轉側而作，勿令有麤暴氣息，綿綿密密，微細而用耳。當知此六氣，力用最大，能遍治四大五藏一切重病。若身冷則用吹，如吹火法；熱用呼，如呼冷風然；百節病痛用嘻……用此亦能治風；若心煩脹，上氣用呵；若痰痼用噓；若身體勞乏，則用呬。若六氣治五藏，則噓治肝，呵治心，呼治脾，呬治肺，吹治腎。又六氣可同治一藏，藏中有冷用吹，有熱用呼，有痛用嘻，有煩用呵，有痰用噓，有倦乏用呬……用六氣者，謂以口吹去冷，鼻徐內溫，安詳而入，切勿令其衝突，於一上坐時，則須七過為之，然後安心，安心少時，復更用氣，此是用氣之治病意義也……三焦者，焦謂焦腑，三焦為水穀之道路，乃氣之所終始也……但如此用功對治，乃佛教初心治病方便，非正修行法，外道於此別生知見，妄為秘寶妙法，抑何可笑！

Commentary: This next part explains treating illness through insight. As explained above, treating illness through calming does not follow one single method; treating illness through insight-contemplation is likewise measureless in its methods. One teacher says: "Simply contemplating the mind's own thought and using the six kinds of breath to treat illness is able to cure it" — meaning observing the subject rather than the object...

To treat the five organs, the breath should be released through the teeth, lips, and tongue, skillfully guided entirely by the mind's own intention, adjusted as needed, never coarse or forceful, but continuous, fine, and subtly applied. One should know that these six kinds of breath carry the very greatest power, able to treat every serious illness of the four elements and five organs. If the body is cold, use chuī, as in blowing on a fire; if hot, use hū, as in exhaling a cool breeze; for pain throughout every joint, use xī... this can also treat wind-illness; for a vexed, distended feeling with rising qi, use hē; for chronic phlegm, use xū; for bodily fatigue and exhaustion, use sì. Matched to the five organs: xū treats the liver, hē treats the heart, hū treats the spleen, sì treats the lungs, and chuī treats the kidneys. The six kinds of breath can also all treat a single organ: coldness within that organ calls for chuī, heat calls for hū, pain calls for xī, vexation calls for hē, phlegm calls for xū, and fatigue calls for sì...

To apply this method: exhale cold air out through the mouth, then slowly draw warm air in through the nose, calmly and gently, taking care never to force it — at the start of a single sitting, this should be done seven times, and only then should one settle the mind; after settling the mind briefly, apply the breath again — this is the meaning of using breath to treat illness...

The "triple burner" refers to the triple viscera — the pathway of food and drink, and the very beginning and end of the body's own vital energy... But this kind of effortful countermeasure is only a beginner's expedient method within Buddhist teaching for treating illness, not the actual method of genuine practice — it is a matter of some absurdity that non-Buddhist practitioners have taken this and generated their own separate views from it, falsely treating it as some secret, wondrous treasure!

The rising breath, the sinking breath, the filling breath, the scorching breath, the growing breath, the dissolving breath, the warming breath, the cooling breath, the piercing breath, the sustaining breath, the harmonizing breath, and the replenishing breath. Briefly, their respective remedies: the rising breath treats heaviness; the sinking breath treats a hollow, unsteady feeling; the filling breath treats emaciation; the scorching breath treats swelling and bloating; the growing breath treats weakness and depletion; the dissolving breath treats excess; the warming breath treats cold; the cooling breath treats heat; the piercing breath treats blockage; the sustaining breath treats trembling; the harmonizing breath generally treats disharmony among the four elements; and the replenishing breath restores the four elements' own depletion. Skillfully applying these breaths, one can treat every kind of illness across the board — the details can be worked out by the same reasoning.

Commentary: The six kinds of breath used to treat illness above come, in fact, from within this same twelfth-breath method; this second part now explains how breath is able to counter every illness. "Breath" here means the breath through the nose, differing from what was explained before. The physical body and mind depend on each other, breath expressing this relationship — just as firewood and its smoke depend on each other: observing whether the smoke is clear or murky tells one whether the wood is dry; observing whether the breath is strong or weak tells one whether the body is healthy or ill...

These twelve kinds of breath must be distinguished from the six kinds of breath explained earlier, since the method of application differs entirely between the two: these twelve kinds of breath carry with them, in addition, an element of contemplative visualization... "Dependent breath" means breath arising in dependence on the mind — as when, in a moment of anger or desire, the breath becomes forceful and pronounced; this is called dependent breath. The earlier six kinds of breath carry visualization alongside "resultant breath"; these twelve kinds of breath carry visualization alongside "dependent breath" — hence the two differ. What was explained earlier concerned illness of the five organs tied to the five colors; this twelfth method treats illness tied to the organs directly, hence "dependent breath" is used to treat it. When applying any of these twelve kinds of breath, each must be accompanied by its own corresponding visualization in the mind, carried through to completion, and one must, above all, know the root source of the illness in detail... This is why a practitioner, when applying any of these breaths, must never apply the wrong one by mistake — knowing the illness and recognizing the proper remedy, prescribing according to the illness, matters more than anything else.

〖文〗有師言：善用假想觀，能治眾病，如人患冷，想身中火氣起，即能治冷。此如雜阿含經治病秘法七十二種法中廣說。

Root Text: Another teacher says: skillfully applying contemplative visualization is able to cure every illness — as when a person suffering from cold visualizes the fire-element rising within the body, and is thereby able to cure the cold. This is explained at length among the seventy-two secret methods for treating illness in the Saṃyukta Āgama.

〖講〗上來氣息之中，復兼帶用想，似有多種，今唯專以假想為治。如昔時有一患致命瘡者，祈明醫診治，醫曰：「此瘡無關緊要，恐汝手臂所患一小疔，有性命關係，甚關重要，宜注意焉。」爾時病者，一心專注小疔，不注大瘡，久之瘡患全愈，此全以假想治病，唯心所成也……又假想治病，如雜阿含經中治病秘法七十二種中廣明……雜阿含中，備明七十二種治病秘法，一一皆以觀心方便為治，當知此種秘法，亦為被一種之機宜，今略述一二，以供參考。一如吞蛇法，其阿含第一本云：「如人噉食，吞於蛇影，即謂為蛇也。」因其念念執故，乃成為病，他人問之，即知起病之源，乃因吞蛇影而生，即以死蛇密著其痢盆，唱言蛇出，病即差也；壁畫蛇影，入酒盃中，亦復如是。阿含又云：「舍衛國有一長者，名曰晨居，家有一婢，面貌極醜……忽至野外，見有一泉，泉中有樹，樹上有一端正女人，自縊而死於樹上，影現泉中，婢女見之謂為己影，殊不知樹有一女屍，彼未之見，以是便瞋大家，自忖我端正如此……乃撲缾破歸家，入堂於寶帳中端坐，默默不語，大家謂其狂病，乃問之婢，即以前事見答……大家先知彼泉水處，而有死女，乃送婢女至泉處，見是死女子影在泉中，實非己之容貌端正，由是心解意明，生大慚愧。」因見影起病，亦須照影而愈病。阿含經中，七十二種治病秘法，一一皆是如此，但此假想，無論修九想觀，八背捨，十一切處，十六特勝，以及觀煉薰修等諸禪定，皆以此假想觀耳！所以古人望梅止渴，懸沙充饑等等，皆是假想觀得益之相。

Commentary: Among the breath-methods above, visualization was already combined with them to some degree; here the focus turns entirely to contemplative visualization alone as the remedy. Once there was a person suffering from a fatal wound, who sought out a skilled physician for treatment. The physician said: "This wound is not, in fact, serious — but I fear the small boil on your arm carries a real risk to your life, and matters far more; you should attend to it closely." The patient then fixed his mind single-mindedly on the small boil, no longer attending to the grave wound at all — and in time the grave wound healed completely. This was accomplished entirely through treating illness by visualization, produced by mind alone...

The Saṃyukta Āgama explains, at length, seventy-two secret methods for treating illness, each and every one of them a skillful means of mind-contemplation applied as treatment. One should know that secret methods of this kind are each suited to a particular occasion; a few are briefly recounted here for reference. The first is the method of "swallowing the snake": the Āgama's first version says: "It is like a person eating food who swallows the reflection of a snake, and believes they have swallowed an actual snake." Because of clinging to this thought from moment to moment, illness results. When another person asks about it, recognizing that the illness arose from swallowing this reflected image of a snake, one secretly places a dead snake in the patient's own basin and announces that the snake has come out — and the illness is cured at once. A snake painted on a wall, its reflection appearing in a wine cup, produces the very same effect.

The Āgama further says: "In the kingdom of Śrāvastī there was an elder named Chenju, whose household included a maidservant of extremely plain appearance... She happened to go out into the wilderness, where she found a spring with a tree growing beside it; on the tree hung the body of a beautiful woman who had hanged herself, its reflection appearing in the spring's own water. The maidservant, seeing this reflection, mistook it for her own reflection, not realizing there was an actual woman's corpse hanging in the tree above, which she had failed to notice. Because of this she grew angry at her employer, thinking to herself: 'Being as beautiful as this...' — and, smashing her water jar, she returned home, sat silently in a curtained seat within the hall, and would not speak a word. Her employer, thinking she had gone mad, questioned her, and she answered with the story as just described... Her employer, already knowing that a dead woman's body hung near that spring, sent the maidservant back to the spring, where she saw the dead woman's own reflection in the water, and realized that this fine appearance had never truly been her own reflection at all. From this her mind was unraveled and her understanding became clear, and she felt deep shame."

This is a case of illness arising from seeing a reflection, likewise cured by illuminating that same reflection. Every one of the seventy-two secret methods for treating illness in the Āgama follows this same pattern. This same contemplative visualization underlies every dhyāna practice as well — whether the nine contemplations, the eight liberations, the ten totalities, the sixteen excellent practices, or the various forms of refining and steeping contemplation — all of them rely on this same visualizing contemplation! This is why the ancients spoke of gazing at a plum tree to quench thirst, or imagining sand as food to relieve hunger — both instances of the benefit gained through visualizing contemplation.

Explanations of this kind, using insight to treat illness, differ in their application, but, skillfully grasping their underlying intent, every one of them is able to cure illness. Commentary: Our own body of the four great elements is a temporary aggregation of the outward four elements — without the outward four elements, there could be no inward four elements at all. Now, when the inward four elements give rise to illness, one must examine one's own physical body closely, investigating minutely: where, exactly, does this illness actually reside? If the earth element itself is ill, then the outward mountains, rivers, and earth should equally be ill... yet mountains, rivers, and earth, and every fire and lamp-flame, have never once been observed to fall ill. Investigating this closely, seeking the four elements' own illness, there is, at root, no mark of illness there at all — and once the outward illness is found not to exist, one further investigates the mark of illness within one's own mind, turning to observe one's own mind, finding it ultimately unfindable — if the mind itself cannot even be found, how much less the illness supposedly afflicting it?

Just as Great Master Nanyue, reciting the Lotus Sūtra within a mountain cave, suffered from a swollen, paralyzing illness, his four limbs too heavy to rise — the master then directly contemplated the very source of the mind, investigating that illness arises from karma, karma arises from delusion, and, exhausting this deluded mind, found it ultimately unfindable; investigating further the source of his own four elements' illness, this too could not be found — who, then, is being afflicted by this illness at all, and who is there to actually suffer it? Contemplating in this way, the illness was cured of itself!

The various teachers above, though each explaining treating illness through insight in their own different way, all point to the same underlying intent — grasping this intent, whatever effort one applies, every illness can be cured; this is what is meant by "grasping the meaning and forgetting the words used to express it."

...Calming and insight together are exactly like a universal medicine, curing every illness without exception — but in this present age, the Dharma already in its final decline, sentient beings' own faculties are shallow and dull; having been deluded since time immemorial, committing boundless offenses at every turn, when they attempt to cultivate calming and insight, their own coarse mind and violent energy prevent any real effort from succeeding at all. From ancient times to the present, those who have cultivated the visualization of the six breaths and twelve breaths have gained real benefit only rarely, and so this method is not widely transmitted in the world today. We, with our own fragile, perishable bodies, subject to unpredictable decline, may reasonably borrow this method to treat illness, so that the body is at ease and the path preserved — there is no fault in this. But should one seek fame and reputation through it, stirring up worldly attention, this is itself a demonic delusion, to be abandoned at once. For what matters most within the Buddhadharma is purity of view and understanding — one must never let the slightest deviant thought take hold in the mind. Should illness of the four elements arise, one should use the effort of calming and insight to counter it; and should one's own power on the path prove too weak for this, treating it with mineral, plant, or herbal medicine is likewise acceptable. Hence it is said: medicine suited to the illness in question may certainly be taken.

Ten Supporting Habits 十法

Chinese Source

〖文〗復次，用心坐中治病，仍須更兼具十法，無不有益，十法者：一信、二用、三勤、四常住緣中、五別病因法、六方便、七久行、八知取捨、九持護、十識遮障。云何為信？謂信此法必能治病。何為用？謂隨時常用。何為勤？謂用之專精不息，取得差為度。何為住緣中？謂細心念念依法，而不異緣。何為別病因起？如上所說。何為方便？謂吐納運心緣想，善巧成就，不失其宜。何為久行？謂若用之未即有益，不計日月，常習不廢。何為知取捨？謂知益即勤，有損即捨之，微細轉心調治。何為持護？謂善識異緣觸犯。何為遮障？謂得益不向外說，未損不生疑謗。若依此十法，所治必定有效不虛者也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Root Text: Further, applying the mind to treat illness while seated requires, in addition, ten supporting habits, every one of them beneficial. The ten are: faith, application, diligence, dwelling constantly within the object, distinguishing the illness's own cause, skillful means, sustained practice, knowing what to take up and what to set aside, maintaining protection, and recognizing what should be kept private. What is faith? Believing that this method is certainly able to cure illness. What is application? Applying it constantly, at all times. What is diligence? Applying it single-mindedly, without pause, until a cure is actually achieved. What is dwelling within the object? Keeping the mind fixed, thought after thought, in accordance with the method, without straying to any other object. What is distinguishing the illness's own cause? As already explained above. What is skillful means? Skillfully accomplishing the exhalation, inhalation, mental application, and visualization involved, never losing what is fitting to the occasion. What is sustained practice? If the method brings no immediate benefit, not counting the days or months, but continuing to practice it without ever abandoning it. What is knowing what to take up and what to set aside? Knowing that where it brings benefit, one applies it diligently, and where it brings harm, one sets it aside, subtly adjusting one's approach accordingly. What is maintaining protection? Skillfully recognizing and avoiding any conflicting circumstance that would violate the treatment. What is recognizing what should be kept private? Not speaking of any benefit gained to others, and, before any harm has occurred, giving rise to no doubt or slander about the method. Relying on these ten habits, whatever is treated will certainly show real effect, not in vain.

〖講〗復次若用心以坐中治病者，仍須更兼十法，必有良驗。謂（1）信，（2）用，乃至第（10）識遮。云何為信？謂信此法門，決定治病，不生一念疑惑之心，猶如癩人，信血是乳，敬駱駝骨是真舍利，然佛法大海，信為能入，智為能度，足見相信最為要緊。（2）明用，隨時常用也，若信而不用，於己無益，如執利劍不用擬賊，反為彼害，不用亦爾。（3）勤，初中後夜，朝暮專精，不休不息，以得差為度，如鑽火中息，火難可得，不勤亦爾。（4）常住緣中，恆用治法，念念在緣，而不動亂，如貓捕鼠相似。（5）別病因起，如上所說，若不識病，浪行治法，不相主對，於事無益。（6）方便，吐納運心，緣想成就，不失其宜，如人彈琴，須調絃之緩急，輾轉軫柱，輕重手指，聲韻方調，方便亦然。（7）久行，行人若用之，久未見有功效，須不計日月，常習不廢，修道用功之人，必須以恆常心，久久修習不可休廢，如古人用功，日不足繼之以夜，終日竟夜，無不在功用之中，久而久之，自然成就，故云道也者，不可須臾離也，可離非道也，治病亦然。（8）知取捨，用功人須具擇法眼，應取則取，應捨則捨，不能絲毫勉強，捨之如棄麻擔金相似，須微細轉心調治，吾等凡夫，竟日麤心暴氣，妄念流動，不能得佛法利益，故須轉麤成細，攝念歸心，方能與道相應。（9）護持，善識禁忌，行來飲食，四威儀中，善能將養調護，不使觸犯，乖諸道業。第（10）識遮障，得益不向人說，未損不生疑謗，所謂魚在水中，冷煖自知，有道無道，自己知道；每見高僧大德，為養道故，不求名聞利養，特意縮德露疵，安貧守道，或一舉萬里，雲遊他方，悉皆是遮障將護之義。若行者用功，倘若能依此十法，用上諸治，必定生效無疑，我當為汝保任此事，終不虛也。上來止觀病大章，至此已竟。

Commentary: Further, if one applies the mind to treat illness while seated, one still requires, in addition, these ten habits, which are certain to bring real, positive results. Faith, application, and so on through recognizing what should be kept private.

What is faith? Believing, without a single thought of doubt, that this method is certainly able to cure illness — just as a leper believes blood to be milk, or one who venerates a camel's bone as a true relic; the great ocean of the Buddhadharma is entered through faith, and crossed through wisdom — this shows clearly how much faith matters. Application means applying the method constantly, at all times — believing in it but never applying it brings no benefit to oneself, exactly like holding a sharp sword but never drawing it against a bandit, only inviting harm instead; failing to apply the method is just the same.

Diligence means applying it single-mindedly through the first, middle, and last watches of the night, morning and evening alike, without pause, until an actual cure is achieved — just as drilling for fire, if paused partway through, never actually produces fire; failing to be diligent is just the same. Dwelling within the object means constantly applying the method of treatment, thought after thought remaining fixed on the object, without wavering — exactly like a cat stalking a mouse. Distinguishing the illness's own cause is as explained above — failing to recognize the illness and applying treatment at random, mismatched to the actual condition, brings no benefit at all.

Skillful means refers to the exhalation, inhalation, mental application, and visualization, accomplished skillfully, never losing what is fitting to the occasion — just as a lute-player must adjust the tension of the strings, turning the pegs this way and that, the fingers pressing lightly or firmly, before the tone comes properly into tune; skillful means is just the same. Sustained practice means that if a practitioner applies the method without seeing any benefit for a long time, one should, without counting the days or months, continue practicing it without ever abandoning it — one who cultivates the path and applies real effort must sustain this same constancy of mind, practicing continuously without pause, exactly as the ancients applied effort day and night alike, never once outside this constant effort, until, in time, success naturally follows — hence it is said, "the path cannot be departed from even for a moment; what can be departed from is not the path" — treating illness is just the same.

Knowing what to take up and what to set aside means that one applying effort must possess the discerning eye of the Dharma, taking up what should be taken up and setting aside what should be set aside, without the slightest forcing of the issue — setting something aside as readily as discarding hemp upon finding gold instead — subtly adjusting one's own approach as needed; we ordinary beings, our minds coarse and our energy unruly the whole day through, idle thought flowing without pause, are unable to gain any real benefit from the Buddhadharma this way, and so must turn what is coarse into what is fine, gathering our attention back into the mind, before we can truly accord with the path.

Maintaining protection means skillfully recognizing and avoiding whatever taboo or conflicting circumstance might arise — in movement, food and drink, and every one of the four bodily postures, skillfully nourishing and protecting oneself, never allowing any violation that would work against one's own progress on the path. Recognizing what should be kept private means not speaking of any benefit gained to others, and, before any harm has occurred, giving rise to no doubt or slander — as it is said, only the fish in the water itself knows whether it is warm or cold; whether one has genuinely attained the path or not is something only oneself can truly know. One often sees eminent monks and great virtuous teachers who, precisely in order to nurture their own progress on the path, seek neither fame nor material gain, deliberately concealing their own virtues and revealing their own faults instead, dwelling contentedly in poverty and holding fast to the path, or suddenly departing ten thousand miles away, wandering like a cloud to some other place entirely — all of this is precisely the meaning of maintaining this kind of protective privacy.

Should a practitioner applying effort be able to rely on these ten habits, applying every one of the treatments described above, real effect is certain to follow without any doubt — I can personally guarantee this to be true, never in vain. This concludes the great chapter on treating illness through calming and insight.

Reading note: This chapter is, in effect, a complete medical handbook nested inside a meditation manual, and Baojing is careful, at several points, to mark where its authority actually ends — recommending ordinary medicine "should one's own power on the path prove too weak," and explicitly warning against turning breath-technique into "some secret, wondrous treasure" the way certain non-Buddhist practitioners had. The two illustrative reflection stories (the swallowed-snake illusion and the maidservant mistaking a dead woman's reflection for her own) both make the same point Baojing keeps returning to throughout this commentary: an illness produced entirely by the mind's own misperception can be undone the moment that misperception is actually seen through — treating illness, like everything else in this text, ultimately comes back to calming and insight rather than to any purely physical remedy.
