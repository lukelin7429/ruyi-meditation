Contemplation of Mind: The Three Paths, Consciousnesses, and Buddha-Natures, and Gushan's Second and Third Objections

Fascicle Four comments on the first half of the Profound Meaning's explanation of the name by contemplation of mind. Zhili first explains why contemplation is needed: the Buddha-dharma is too high for beginners, while contemplating one's own mind is easy, and hearing without contemplating is like a parrot or a man counting another's coins. He then defends Zhiyi's choice to "discard three [aggregates] and contemplate one," the aggregate of consciousness. This answers Gushan Zhiyuan's second objection, that the passage confuses the three kinds of contemplation and so must be a later addition. The core of the fascicle is the contemplation attached to the ten sets of three dharmas. The three paths are matched to the mind-king and the mental factors and contemplated with an auxiliary contemplation of emptiness (the body's six parts, the cause of the body, raising and lowering the foot). The three consciousnesses and the three buddha-natures are contemplated as the threefold contemplation in a single mind, with the Pratyutpanna Sūtra's "suchness of my mind" read in two passes. Twice Zhili answers the two parts of Gushan's third objection, "the principle is contrary." The first concerns the stage of resemblance ("seen with eyes closed, lost with eyes open"), which Gushan held contradicts the Commentary on the Contemplation Sūtra. The second concerns reading "buddha" as awakened wisdom and "nature" as the utmost of principle. Zhili defends both with the Fuxing, the Diamond Scalpel, and the Profound Meaning of Avalokiteśvara.

Why Contemplate the Mind

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, explaining, in three: first, setting up two questions and answers to show the reason for contemplation of mind, in two: first, clarifying that understanding must be completed by practice, and therefore contemplation is made on the mind, in two:

初、問起者，前已廣約譬喻、附文、當體釋金光明，足顯法性深廣圓融，今何更立觀心釋耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the question raised. Since the Golden Light has already been explained at length by simile, by attaching to the text, and by the thing itself — enough to reveal the dharma-nature as deep, broad, and perfectly fused — why further set up an explanation by contemplation of mind?

Chinese Source

二、釋出，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the explanation, in two:

Chinese Source

初、正釋。此一段文，須得心佛高下之意，方免疑情。《妙玄》云「佛法太高，眾生法太廣，初心為難。心佛及眾生，是三無差別，觀心則易。」今從上來至「不能開發自身寶藏」，是論佛法太高也。從「今欲」下明觀心則易也。上來等者，即前譬等釋金光明，一一無非竪徹三位、遍該諸法，說眾生皆如菩提涅槃本性具足。此顯法性無量甚深，而但是佛所游之法。佛是聖人，金光明是聖寶，尚過菩薩所行清淨，豈是凡夫己之智分？若但言議上之名句，不能觀察己之心性，則於聖人聖寶有何益乎？故引二喻斥其多聞無觀智者、

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the explanation proper. For this passage one must grasp the intent of mind and Buddha as high and low; only then does one avoid doubt. The Wondrous Profound [Meaning] says, "The Buddha-dharma is too high and the dharma of beings too broad; for the beginning mind they are difficult. Mind, buddha, and beings — these three are without distinction; contemplating the mind is easy." Now, from "so far" to "it cannot open the jewel treasury of one's own self" discusses the Buddha-dharma as too high. From "Now we wish" on, it clarifies that contemplating the mind is easy. "So far" and so on: the preceding explanations of the Golden Light by simile and the rest each without exception penetrate vertically through the three stages and cover all dharmas everywhere, teaching that beings all, like bodhi and nirvāṇa, are complete in their original nature. This reveals the dharma-nature as immeasurable and very deep, yet it is only the dharma in which the Buddha roams. The Buddha is the sage, and the Golden Light is the sacred jewel; it is beyond even the pure [sphere] practiced by the bodhisattvas — how could it be the share of an ordinary being's own wisdom? If one only discusses the names and phrases above and cannot contemplate the nature of one's own mind, what benefit is there from the sage and the sacred jewel? Hence it cites two illustrations to reject those of much learning without contemplative wisdom.

Chinese Source

鸚鵡學語者。〈曲禮〉云「鸚鵡能言，不離飛鳥；猩猩能言，不離禽獸。人而無禮，不亦禽獸之心乎！」今但借喻有聞無觀，徒學聖言，不離凡夫之心耳。客作數錢者，《華嚴》云「譬如貧窮人，日夜數他寶，自無半錢分，多聞亦如是。」今欲等者，攝前佛法入心成觀。心是心性，若陰若業若煩惱等即凡夫心地，既三障當體是金光明，故云珍寶。此乃立心為顯理境也，欲令行者即聞而修，開發自己金光明寶，免同學語、數錢之類也。

Ru-Yi Translation

"A parrot learning to talk": the "Summary of the Rules of Propriety" says, "The parrot can speak, yet it is no more than a flying bird; the ape can speak, yet it is no more than a beast. If a man is without propriety, is his not the mind of a beast?" Here the illustration is only borrowed for having learning without contemplation: vainly learning the sage's words, one does not leave the mind of an ordinary being. "A hired hand counting coins": the Avataṃsaka says, "It is like a poor man who day and night counts another's treasure, while he himself has not half a coin; much learning is also like this." "Now we wish" and so on gathers the preceding Buddha-dharma into the mind to accomplish contemplation. The mind is the mind-nature; the aggregates, karma, afflictions, and so on are the ground of the ordinary being's mind. Since the three obstructions in themselves are the Golden Light, it says "treasure." This sets up the mind to reveal the object of principle, wishing to make the practitioner cultivate as soon as he hears, opening his own jewel of the Golden Light and avoiding being like those who learn to talk or count coins.

Chinese Source

二、引證。初引《淨名》。諸佛解脫者，三解脫也。與十種三法不多不少，此是佛法。若緣佛修，則增念慮，理難可顯。故佛示要門，令諸眾生觀己心行即空假中，則三解脫當處發現。此乃心佛無差觀心則易也。又引《釋論》。彼論九十三云「有慧無多聞，亦不知實相，譬如大闇中，有目無所見。多聞無智慧，亦不見實相，譬如大明中，有燈而無照。無聞無智慧，譬如人身牛。」故《大論》云「如安息國邊地生人，雖生中國，不可教化。根不具、支不完，不識義理、著邪見等，皆名人身牛也。有聞有智慧，是所說應受。如人有目，日光照，見種種色。」今亦如是，若聞上來種種釋金光明、不觀己心者，即多聞無慧句也。若但觀心、不聞圓融說者，即有慧無聞句也。能攝上來無量甚深十種三法，觀於心性顯金光明者，即有聞有慧句也。有三觀目、圓教日照，則見三諦種種之色。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, citing proof. First it cites the Vimalakīrti. "The liberation of the buddhas" is the three liberations, neither more nor less than the ten sets of three dharmas; this is the Buddha-dharma. If one cultivates taking the Buddha as object, one increases thinking and deliberation, and principle is hard to reveal. Hence the Buddha shows the essential gate, making beings contemplate their own mental conduct as identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle; then the three liberations appear right where they are. This is "mind and Buddha without difference; contemplating the mind is easy." It further cites the Great Treatise. That treatise, in fascicle ninety-three, says: "One who has wisdom without much learning also does not know the true mark, like one in great darkness who has eyes but sees nothing. One of much learning without wisdom also does not see the true mark, like one in great brightness who has a lamp but no illumination. One without learning and without wisdom is like an ox with a human body." Hence the Great Treatise says: "Like people born in the border lands of the country of Parthia: though born in the central land, they cannot be taught and transformed; their faculties are not complete and their limbs not whole, they do not recognize meaning and principle, they cling to wrong views, and so on — all these are called oxen with human bodies. One who has learning and has wisdom — what such a one says should be accepted, like a person with eyes in the light of the sun, seeing all kinds of forms." Now it is the same: if one hears the various explanations of the Golden Light above and does not contemplate one's own mind, this is the line "much learning without wisdom." If one only contemplates the mind and does not hear the account of perfect fusion, this is the line "wisdom without learning." One who can gather up the immeasurable and very deep ten sets of three dharmas above and contemplate the mind-nature, revealing the Golden Light, is the line "has learning and has wisdom." With the eyes of the three contemplations and the sunlight of the perfect teaching, one sees all kinds of forms of the three truths.

Discarding Three and Contemplating One: Gushan's Second Point

Chinese Source

二、明心為行要故觀必研心，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying that the mind is the essential of practice, and therefore contemplation must examine the mind, in two:

Chinese Source

初、約簡數觀王問。若約三科論去就者，則棄界入但觀五陰，復於五陰簡四觀識。大師譚觀常論簡境，去丈就尺、去尺就寸。義既可知，故今但約觀心為問。既云觀心，五陰除色四皆屬心，何故棄三而獨觀識？然設此問，令知觀境唯在識陰也。孤山四意中，第二義疎破此文云「今家約行附法託事三種觀中，唯約行觀簡示陰境，其餘二種全不觀陰，但託事攝法明理觀耳。今附法觀秖合直攝三法以歸三諦，而發棄三觀一之問者，蓋不知三種觀心規矩，驗是後人擅加也。」

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the question in terms of sifting the factors and contemplating the king. If one discusses choosing and discarding in terms of the three categories, one discards the realms and entrances and contemplates only the five aggregates, and further among the five aggregates sifts out four and contemplates consciousness. The Great Master, speaking of contemplation, always discusses sifting the object: setting aside the ten feet to take up the foot, setting aside the foot to take up the inch. Since the meaning can be understood, here he only asks in terms of contemplating the mind: since it says "contemplating the mind," and of the five aggregates, apart from form, all four belong to mind, why discard three and contemplate consciousness alone? Setting up this question makes one know that the object of contemplation lies only in the aggregate of consciousness. Among Gushan's four points, the second, "the meaning is loose," refutes this text, saying: "Of our school's three kinds of contemplation — in terms of practice, attached to dharmas, and relying on phenomena — only the contemplation in terms of practice sifts and shows the object of the aggregates; the other two do not contemplate the aggregates at all, but only rely on phenomena or gather dharmas to clarify the contemplation of principle. The present contemplation attached to dharmas should only gather the three dharmas directly and return them to the three truths. Raising the question of discarding three and contemplating one shows that [the writer] did not know the norms of the three kinds of contemplation of mind; this proves it was added without authority by a later person."

Chinese Source

釋曰：《義例》立附法觀云「攝諸法相入一念心以為圓觀」，且一念心豈非陰耶？既觀於陰，簡有何過？《法華文句》託靈鷲山觀於五陰，《記》云「諸餘觀境不出五陰」，今此山等約陰便故，以諸文中直云境智。又云「亦應於此明方便正修簡境及心」，既諸觀境不出五陰，乃知託事及附法觀無不觀陰也。直云境智者，即諸文云「觀於一念即空假中」。一念是陰境，三觀是智也。又令明於方便正修簡境及心，須棄思議取不思議方名簡心，不於三科而論去取，安名簡境？又王城觀云「應如止觀十乘十境，下去皆爾。」記主意令講此觀時，人欲修者須敘私記簡陰境文及十乘等而委示之，令山城觀行法備足，非廢託事便自講說止觀全部。他之致意，直欲如斯。既云「下去皆爾」，信諸託事及附法觀，皆須簡陰及示十乘也。彼文不簡尚令簡之，今有簡文那成非義？據此棄三觀一之問云義疎者，義實不疎，蓋汝解疎耳。

Ru-Yi Translation

I explain: The Examples of Meaning, establishing the contemplation attached to dharmas, says, "Gathering the marks of dharmas into a single thought of mind to make the perfect contemplation." And is a single thought of mind not an aggregate? Since one contemplates an aggregate, what fault is there in sifting? The Textual Commentary on the Lotus, relying on Vulture Peak, contemplates the five aggregates; the Notes says, "The other objects of contemplation do not go beyond the five aggregates; here the mountain and so on are taken because they are convenient for the aggregates, since the various texts speak directly of object and wisdom." It further says, "Here too one should clarify the expedient and the proper cultivation, sifting the object and the mind." Since the objects of contemplation do not go beyond the five aggregates, we know that the contemplations relying on phenomena and attached to dharmas without exception contemplate the aggregates. "Speak directly of object and wisdom": that is, the various texts say, "contemplate a single thought as identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle." The single thought is the object of the aggregates; the three contemplations are wisdom. Further, he has one clarify the expedient and the proper cultivation, sifting the object and the mind: one must discard the conceivable and take the inconceivable, which is called sifting the mind; if one does not choose and discard among the three categories, how could it be called sifting the object? Further, on the contemplation of the royal city, it says, "It should be like the ten modes and ten objects of Calming and Contemplation; all that follows is likewise." The intent of the author of the Notes is that when this contemplation is lectured on, for people who wish to cultivate it one must set out the text of the private record on sifting the object of the aggregates, the ten modes, and so on, and show them in detail, so that the contemplative practice of the mountain and the city is fully provided; it does not mean abolishing [contemplation] relying on phenomena and lecturing on the whole of Calming and Contemplation by itself. His intended meaning is simply this. Since it says "all that follows is likewise," we can trust that all the contemplations relying on phenomena and attached to dharmas must sift the aggregates and show the ten modes. Where that text does not sift, he still has one sift; here there is a text that sifts — how does it become wrong? On this basis, calling the question of discarding three and contemplating one "the meaning is loose" — the meaning is in fact not loose; it is your understanding that is loose.

Chinese Source

又若直攝三法以歸三諦，不許簡陰便是觀心，則成偏觀清淨真如，何反宗之甚耶？是知彼人都昧一家三種觀法，如釋《觀經》十六觀云「是一心三觀，的非《義例》三種觀攝。」且《義例》云「夫三觀者，義唯三種。」豈應妙觀更有異塗？況諸文觀心，皆一家樞要，儻解之錯謬，徒成斐然。既失其本，餘皆枝詞矣。彼又於《金𰼺記》中云「若取止觀來消事法觀文，乃以止觀隨機面授。」深違大師遺囑也。囑云「止觀不須傳授，私記時為人說。」《輔行》釋云「囑意正言隨機面授意多不周，非後代所堪。」彼人曲解《輔行》之文成於己見也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, if one gathers the three dharmas directly and returns them to the three truths, not allowing the sifting of the aggregates, and this is contemplation of mind, it becomes a partial contemplation of pure suchness — how far it turns against our school! Hence we know that man is entirely in the dark about the three kinds of contemplation of our school. For example, in explaining the sixteen contemplations of the Contemplation Sūtra, he says, "This is the threefold contemplation in a single mind; it is definitely not included in the three kinds of contemplation of the Examples of Meaning." Yet the Examples of Meaning says, "As for the three contemplations, in meaning there are only three kinds." How could the wondrous contemplation have any other route? Moreover, the contemplation of mind in the various texts is the pivot of our school; if one understands it wrongly, one only produces a brilliant pattern [of words]. Having lost the root, all the rest is branching words. In his Notes on the Vajra Scalpel he further says, "If one takes Calming and Contemplation to interpret the texts of the contemplation of phenomena and of dharmas, one is giving Calming and Contemplation as oral instruction according to capacities," [which, he says,] deeply goes against the Great Master's bequeathed instruction. The instruction says, "Calming and Contemplation need not be handed on; the private record may at times be explained to people." The Fuxing explains: "The instruction means precisely that the oral instruction according to capacities is often incomplete in meaning and is not something later generations can bear." That man twists the text of the Fuxing into his own view.

Chinese Source

且《輔行》釋面授等意者，斯蓋隨逐大師修心之者，或觀道不進、或內外障起，有所諮問，師乃隨機面授口訣，一時取益，意多不周。若後代人心病既異，故非所堪，蓋不須用面授止觀而授後人，非謂不得。敘十卷中十境十乘消事法觀，以茲境觀載於私記。若其敘者，正以私記時為人說，雅合大師臨終遺囑。若全不許敘止觀，荊溪何故於山城觀令辨方便正修簡境及心十境十乘耶？敘此令修山城觀不？又若謂此是開其解心、非謂令其修習者，何故《妙玄》明觀心文中令即聞即修耶？《釋籤》云「隨聞一句攝事成理，不待觀境，方名修觀。」何公背吾祖之教乎？故知今辨棄三觀一，正符荊溪於山城觀中指授意也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Now, the Fuxing's explanation of the meaning of oral instruction and so on [is this]: those who followed the Great Master and cultivated the mind, when their path of contemplation did not advance or inner and outer hindrances arose, had questions to ask; the Master then gave oral instruction according to their capacities, a benefit for that occasion, often incomplete in meaning. Because the illnesses of mind of people in later generations differ, it is not something they can bear. That is, one need not use the orally given Calming and Contemplation to instruct later people; it does not mean one may not [use the text]. Setting out the ten objects and ten modes of the ten fascicles to interpret the contemplations of phenomena and of dharmas — these objects and contemplations are contained in the private record. Setting them out is precisely "the private record may at times be explained to people," fitting exactly the Great Master's instruction bequeathed at his death. If setting out Calming and Contemplation were not allowed at all, why did Jingxi, on the contemplation of the mountain and the city, have one distinguish the expedient and the proper cultivation, sifting the object and the mind, the ten objects and ten modes? Was he setting this out to have them cultivate the contemplation of the mountain and the city, or not? Further, if one says this is to open their understanding and not to have them cultivate it, why does the contemplation-of-mind text of the Wondrous Profound [Meaning] have one cultivate as soon as one hears? The Annotations says, "Following hearing a single phrase, gather phenomena to accomplish principle; without waiting for the object of contemplation, it is called cultivating contemplation." Why does the gentleman turn his back on the teaching of our patriarch? Hence we know that the present distinction of discarding three and contemplating one fits exactly Jingxi's pointing out and instruction in the contemplation of the mountain and the city.

The Mind as Gold, Light, and Brightness

Chinese Source

二、約心淨法融答，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the answer in terms of the mind pure and dharmas fused, in two:

Chinese Source

初、約離性先觀內心。上定三字非譬是法，法性可貴名之為金、法性能照名之為光、法性能益名之為明。今用此義觀於識心，若心不具金光明義，那可於心觀於法性？此文為三：初、約貴論金。欲顯心貴，先於萬物推人為貴。從劣至勝，見心不昧名為靈智。靈智雖貴，而通四陰分於王數。問已棄三數，今獨推王而為最貴。識心既貴，故觀心王即法性金。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, in terms of separating the nature, first contemplating the inner mind. Above it was settled that the three characters are dharma, not simile: the dharma-nature as valuable is named gold, the dharma-nature as able to illuminate is named light, the dharma-nature as able to benefit is named brightness. Now these meanings are used to contemplate the consciousness-mind. If the mind did not possess the meanings of gold, light, and brightness, how could one contemplate the dharma-nature in the mind? This text has three [parts]: first, discussing gold in terms of value. Wishing to reveal the mind as noble, it first infers that among the myriad things the human is noble. From the inferior to the superior, it sees that the mind, undarkened, is called numinous wisdom. Though numinous wisdom is noble, it runs through the four aggregates, divided into king and factors. The question has already discarded three factors; now the king alone is inferred as most noble. Since the consciousness-mind is noble, contemplating the mind-king is the gold of the dharma-nature.

Chinese Source

二、約照論光。光有勝劣，故先就劣比至心識最得名光，是故觀心即法性光。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, discussing light in terms of illumination. Light is superior or inferior; hence starting from the inferior and comparing up to the mind-consciousness, it most deserves the name light. Therefore contemplating the mind is the light of the dharma-nature.

Chinese Source

三、約益論明，即能充益色等四陰。益色陰者，良以色心性不二故，色隨心轉。《大品》佛現色像無邊，皆由般若性周遍故，色淨亦然。亦能等者，心王若正，心數亦正，化轉塵勞心數眾生，故心能益。是以觀心即法性明。此約心有貴等三義，故觀於心顯金光明法性三法。此文即是離性為三也。所觀之性既離為三，能照之智任運成三，所起之用亦合有三。文雖不言二修各三，以性顯之，其義合爾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, discussing brightness in terms of benefit, namely that it can fill and benefit the four aggregates of form and the rest. As for benefiting the aggregate of form: because the natures of form and mind are not two, form changes along with mind. In the Larger [Prajñāpāramitā], that the forms and images the Buddha manifests are boundless is all because the nature of prajñā pervades everywhere; "form is pure" is likewise. "Can also" and so on: if the mind-king is right, the mental factors are also right, transforming the mental factors of dust and toil, the beings [within]; hence the mind can benefit. Therefore contemplating the mind is the brightness of the dharma-nature. This, in terms of the mind having the three meanings of nobility and the rest, contemplates the mind to reveal the three dharmas of the Golden Light dharma-nature. This text is separating the nature into three. Since the nature contemplated is separated into three, the wisdom that illuminates becomes three effortlessly, and the function that arises should also be three. Gushan's objections: The second, "the meaning is loose," is answered in unit 8. In unit 49, CBETA closes the quotation after "a simile for the three virtues," but the sentences down to "only confuses later students" are plainly Gushan's attack too, so the English quotation runs to there. In unit 8, "deeply goes against the Great Master's bequeathed instruction" is taken as Gushan's claim; it could also be Zhili's own comment.

Three kinds of contemplation (unit 8): In terms of practice / attached to dharmas / relying on phenomena (約行／附法／託事), following `miaozong-chao` notes. "As for the three contemplations, in meaning there are only three kinds" is in the Zhiguan yili (T1913).
