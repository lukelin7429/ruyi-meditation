Paramārtha Refuted and the Ten Sets of Three Dharmas

Fascicle Two of Zhili's Gleanings comments on the rest of the Profound Meaning's explanation by simile. First comes Paramārtha's reading, in which the three characters gold, light, and brightness stand for three sets of three: the three bodies, the three virtues, and three stages (before, on, and after the path). Paramārtha assigns one member of each set to one stage. Zhiyi rejected this on three counts: cause and fruit no longer connect, it departs from both the perfect and the distinct teachings, and it does not accord with the dharma-nature. Zhili expands each count. All sets of three are one set under many names, every stage contains all three, and each body or virtue contains the others. The second half covers Zhiyi's own alternative, the ten sets of three dharmas, from the three virtues down to the three paths. Zhili explains the two sequences, reverse and forward, as the basis for teaching and for practice. He shows how "immeasurable" and "very deep" mean horizontal breadth and vertical depth through the stages and the six identities, then glosses each set in turn. Recurring themes are cultivation and nature ("cultivation two, nature one"), separation and combination, and the claim that evil names such as the ālaya consciousness, ignorance, and the three paths can be the wondrous principle without delusion being severed. This last point anticipates Zhili's teaching of inherent evil.

Ru-Yi Translation

First, explaining the three bodies. The three bodies of that sūtra and the Dharma, reward, and response [bodies]: the names of the two sets of three differ but their meaning is the same. The second, the "response-body" (應, read in the level tone): this is wondrous wisdom corresponding (相應) with dharmas, the same as the meaning of reward. The third, the transformation-body, responds (應, read in the departing tone) to capacities and transforms, the same as the meaning of response.

二、釋三德。金有四義，以譬法身具足四德，一一法譬其相顯然。光明各二義，譬般若、解脫各具二德。光云除闇、明云無闇，義有何別？光能破闇故名為除，闇更不生故名為無，乃以除闇譬般若除惑、無闇譬解脫眾累永盡。此師雖昧三德互具，以譬對法不無所以。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the three virtues. Gold has four meanings, as a simile for the Dharma-body being complete with the four virtues; in each, dharma and simile plainly show their features. Light and brightness each have two meanings, as similes for prajñā and liberation each possessing two virtues. Light is said to "remove darkness," brightness is said to be "absence of darkness"; what difference is there in meaning? Because light can break darkness it is called removing; because darkness no longer arises it is called absence. So "removing darkness" is a simile for prajñā removing delusion, and "absence of darkness" a simile for liberation, all encumbrances forever exhausted. Though this master is in the dark about the three virtues mutually containing one another, his matching of simile to dharma is not without reason.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, explaining the three stages. He further uses the three characters as similes for the correct, conditioning, and revealing [causes], matching the three natures to the three stages; text and meaning are also evident.

Chinese Source

三、料揀。揀三身者，法身是性故是實，二身修成故不實。揀三德者，法身是總體故具四德，二是別相故各二德。揀三位者，正因在性故本有，了因修證故現有，緣因在果故當有。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, sorting and sifting. Sifting the three bodies: the Dharma-body is the nature, hence real; the two [other] bodies are accomplished through cultivation, hence not real. Sifting the three virtues: the Dharma-body is the whole essence, hence possesses the four virtues; the two are particular features, hence each [possesses] two virtues. Sifting the three stages: the correct cause lies in the nature, hence it is originally existent; the revealing cause is cultivated and realized, hence presently existent; the conditioning cause lies in the fruit, hence to-be existent.

The Great Master's Refutation of Paramārtha

Chinese Source

二、破。以真諦釋義出諸師，語與今濫，慮其後學不見其過，執非為是。復欲對彼不融之義，顯今圓妙之談，是故破斥。其文稍廣。文二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, refuting. Because Paramārtha's explanation of the meaning surpasses the [other] masters and his words overlap with ours, [the Great Master] was concerned that later students would not see its faults and would hold the wrong to be right. He also wished, against that unmerged meaning, to reveal our perfect and wondrous discourse; therefore he refutes and rejects it. The text is somewhat extensive. The text has two [parts]:

初、總破。彼以三種三法解今題目，故云三三。大師評之三義不了：一因果義、二別圓義、三法性義。既其不通，有乖不稱，故云不了。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, refuting in general. He explains the present title by three kinds of three dharmas; hence it says "three sets of three." The Great Master judges that it "is not thorough in three senses": first, the meaning of cause and fruit; second, the meaning of the perfect and the distinct; third, the meaning of the dharma-nature. Since it does not connect, it departs [from the perfect and the distinct], and it does not accord, it says "not thorough."

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, refuting specifically, in three: first, raising the three faults; second, explaining the three faults, in three:

Chinese Source

初、因果不通。問：真諦但以三因分對三位，何故破云分置三德殘缺不足？答：一切三法秖一三法，以具眾德故有眾名，常樂我淨故名三德，可尊可重故名三寶，不生不滅故名三涅槃，諸法聚集故名三身，是如來種故名三因，即事通理故名三道。既其法門體本不別，故分置三因，即是分置三身三德。兩節注云云者，今準《大經》說圓三德互具之相。法身即云：直法身非法身，法身必具般若、解脫。般若即云：直般若非般若，般若必具解脫、法身。覈出解脫，合注云云，例上故略。以直解脫非解脫，解脫必具法身般若。彼既分置，乃令三位各唯一德，則因不攝果、果不攝因，故云不通。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, cause and fruit do not connect. Question: Paramārtha only matches the three causes to the three stages; why does the refutation say, "they are divided and placed apart, so the three virtues are broken and insufficient"? Answer: All sets of three dharmas are only one set of three dharmas; because it possesses many virtues, it has many names. Because it is permanence, bliss, self, and purity, it is called the three virtues; because it can be honored and weighed heavily, it is called the Three Jewels; because it neither arises nor ceases, it is called the three nirvāṇas; because all dharmas gather in it, it is called the three bodies; because it is the seed of the Tathāgata, it is called the three causes; because phenomena themselves lead through to principle, it is called the three paths. Since the essence of these Dharma-gates is originally not distinct, to divide and place apart the three causes is to divide and place apart the three bodies and the three virtues. As for the two places annotated "(and so on)": now, following the Great Sūtra, [we] state the features of the perfect three virtues mutually containing one another. For the Dharma-body it would say: the Dharma-body alone is not the Dharma-body; the Dharma-body necessarily contains prajñā and liberation. For prajñā it would say: prajñā alone is not prajñā; prajñā necessarily contains liberation and the Dharma-body. On examination, at liberation too "(and so on)" should be noted; it is omitted, following the cases above. For liberation alone is not liberation; liberation necessarily contains the Dharma-body and prajñā. Since he divides and places them apart, making each of the three stages have only one virtue, the cause does not include the fruit and the fruit does not include the cause; hence it says "do not connect."

Chinese Source

二、乖圓別。先舉圓別四德之相，然後方斥乖違之失。圓四德者，法身乃是性中三德：法身常我、般若故淨、解脫故樂。此四在性但名法身，全性發修必成三智，智冥性德同性具四。從照了義但名般若，智合性故解脫應機。既全性起，必成三脫，是故同性具於四德，從起用義但名解脫。般若契性，同性具四，其相易知，故不別示。解脫應機，起成外用，同性具德，其相難解，故今別示。果即二死，脫此苦故名為樂德。因即五住，脫此染故名為淨德。永無二縛，性即自在，故名我德。惑因死果是生滅法，本來解脫非此因果，故名常德。雖是離縛說此四德，然縛本空，是故四德全同於性。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, departing from the perfect and the distinct. First he raises the features of the four virtues in the perfect and the distinct [teachings], and only then rejects the fault of departing from them. The four virtues of the perfect: the Dharma-body is the three virtues within the nature: the Dharma-body is permanence and self; because of prajñā, purity; because of liberation, bliss. These four, lying in the nature, are only called the Dharma-body. When cultivation arises from the whole nature, it necessarily forms the three wisdoms; wisdom merges with the inherent virtue and, like the nature, contains the four. In terms of the meaning of illuminating and understanding, it is only called prajñā. Because wisdom unites with the nature, liberation responds to capacities. Since it arises from the whole nature, it necessarily forms the three liberations; therefore, like the nature, it contains the four virtues, and in terms of the meaning of giving rise to function it is only called liberation. Prajñā accords with the nature and, like the nature, contains the four; its features are easy to know, so they are not shown separately. Liberation responds to capacities and arises as outward function; that, like the nature, it contains the virtues is hard to understand, so it is shown separately here. "The fruit" is the two kinds of death; because it is freed from this suffering, it is called the virtue of bliss. "The cause" is the five abidings; because it is freed from this defilement, it is called the virtue of purity. Forever without the two bonds, the nature is sovereign; hence it is called the virtue of self. Delusion as cause and death as fruit are dharmas of arising and ceasing; original liberation is not this cause and fruit; hence it is called the virtue of permanence. Although these four virtues are spoken of as being apart from bonds, the bonds are originally empty; therefore the four virtues are wholly the same as the nature.

別四德者，約三身說。法身具二：常即常德。實即我者，法身堅實，方有主宰及自在義，是真我德。應身智慧照破惑染，別當淨德。化身三昧即首楞嚴，普現色身拔苦與樂，故名樂德。別是教道，故以三身分對四德。今明圓別二四德者，由此二教多無異部，聞說三身具於四德，失意之者分隔而解即當別教，其得意者互具而解名為圓教。知一一身皆即三身，故一一身皆具四德；若三身不融、四德乃別，故善談別教即共有四德，善談圓教即各具四德。融別即圓、分圓即別。明二教已，乃斥乖違。三藏所明，共四不成故乖別，各四不成故乖圓。

Ru-Yi Translation

The four virtues of the distinct are spoken of in terms of the three bodies. The Dharma-body has two: "permanent" is the virtue of permanence. "'Real' is the virtue of self": because the Dharma-body is firm and real, only then is there the meaning of being master and sovereign; this is the true virtue of self. The response-body's wisdom illuminates and breaks the defilement of delusion, and is particularly the virtue of purity. The transformation-body's samādhi is the Śūraṅgama; manifesting physical bodies everywhere, it removes suffering and gives happiness; hence it is called the virtue of bliss. The distinct is the path of teaching; hence it matches the four virtues separately to the three bodies. Now, as for clarifying the two kinds of four virtues, perfect and distinct: since these two teachings mostly do not have separate collections, on hearing it said that the three bodies possess the four virtues, those who miss the intent understand them as divided, and this is the distinct teaching; those who get the intent understand them as mutually containing, and this is called the perfect teaching. Knowing that each body is identical to the three bodies, each body possesses the four virtues; if the three bodies are not merged, the four virtues are separate. Hence the distinct teaching, well explained, is the four virtues held jointly; the perfect teaching, well explained, is the four virtues each fully possessed. The distinct merged is the perfect; the perfect divided is the distinct. Having clarified the two teachings, he then rejects the departure from them. What the Tripiṭaka [master] clarifies: the four held jointly is not achieved, hence it departs from the distinct; the four each fully possessed is not achieved, hence it departs from the perfect.

Chinese Source

三、不稱法性。三法不改，名之為性。一切三法皆二屬修：一在於性，逆順二修皆在於性。一性全在迷悟二修，故使三法橫該十界依正色心、竪徹三位迷悟因果，是故經稱「無量甚深之法性」也。若其稱此法性而談，則於三位位位具三、一一該徹。今具言此，即是破他也。此自為四：

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, not according with the dharma-nature. The three dharmas being unchanging are called the nature. In all sets of three dharmas, two belong to cultivation and one lies in the nature; the two cultivations, contrary and compliant, both lie in the nature. The one nature lies wholly in the two cultivations of delusion and awakening; thus the three dharmas horizontally encompass the dependent and the principal, form and mind of the ten realms, and vertically pass through the cause and fruit of delusion and awakening in the three stages. Therefore the sūtra calls it "the immeasurable, very deep dharma-nature." If one speaks in accord with this dharma-nature, then in the three stages each stage contains the three, and each one encompasses and passes through [all]. Saying this fully here is itself refuting the other. This in turn has four:

Chinese Source

初、引《淨名》破道前。據此三文，驗知道前不獨一法。然須了知菩提是智德，至果方證得；涅槃是斷德，至果盡滅惑。經既顯云「不可復得、不可復滅」，乃是性中已具果德，豈非道前具金光明？他云：一金安稱法性。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, citing the Vimalakīrti to refute [his account of the stage] before the path. On the basis of these three passages it can be verified that before the path there is not just a single dharma. One must understand, however, that bodhi is the virtue of wisdom, realized only on reaching the fruit, and nirvāṇa is the virtue of severance, delusion being wholly extinguished only on reaching the fruit. Since the sūtra plainly says "it cannot be attained again" and "it cannot be extinguished again," the virtues of the fruit are already contained in the nature; is this not the Golden Light being complete before the path? [Paramārtha] says "gold" alone; how could that accord with the dharma-nature?

Chinese Source

二、引《華嚴》破道中。初發心者發心住也，便成正覺者能現八相也。此是圓教十住位中第一位也。住前圓修，登住圓發，發於性三，即慧身等三身三德一切三法。且以一三以破真諦立道中位但一了因。初住之後至于等覺皆名道中，位位三法漸增如月。《華嚴》圓說乃稱法性無量甚深，證則俱證。驗彼分割，實為不稱。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, citing the Avataṃsaka to refute [his account of the stage] on the path. "The first arousing of the mind" is the abiding of arousing the mind; "at once attains true awakening" is being able to manifest the eight aspects [of a buddha's life]. This is the first stage among the ten abidings of the perfect teaching. Before the abidings, one cultivates the perfect; on ascending to the abidings, the perfect is brought forth; what is brought forth is the three of the nature, namely the wisdom-body and the rest — the three bodies, the three virtues, all sets of three dharmas. For now, one set of three is used to refute Paramārtha's establishing the stage on the path as having only the single revealing cause. From after the first abiding up to equal awakening, all are called on the path; stage by stage the three dharmas gradually increase, like the moon. The Avataṃsaka's perfect teaching accords with the dharma-nature, immeasurable and very deep; when realized, they are realized together. This verifies that his division truly does not accord.

Chinese Source

三、指前義破道後。具三如上說者，前破因果不通，文云「三身三德本是果上圓滿之名」。而今分置三德殘缺不足，又云「道後眾善溥會，何得獨有解脫？」彼義自壞，故不別引經。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, pointing to the earlier meaning to refute [his account of the stage] after the path. "Complete with the three, as said above": in the earlier refutation of cause and fruit not connecting, the text says, "The three bodies and the three virtues are originally names for what is perfected in the fruit." Yet now they are "divided and placed apart, so the three virtues are broken and insufficient." It also says, "after the path all goods come together universally; why should there be liberation alone?" His meaning breaks down by itself, so no sūtra is cited separately.

Chinese Source

四、約圓總斥。據前引經位位圓具，豈各一耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, rejecting in general in terms of the perfect. According to the sūtras cited above, every stage is perfectly complete; how could each have only one?

Chinese Source

三、約喻斥。經談法性，稱無量甚深。若金光明橫周竪亘，無德不備、無位不通，其猶鳳之威靈、龍之神異。真諦所釋，德既不備、位又不通，如蹙縮於鳩巢、若槃迴於兔窟，豈不辱禹門之鱗鬣、丹穴之羽儀俱無壯勢耶？上三句皆喻，後一句法合，故云非法性之圓談矣。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, rejecting by simile. The sūtra speaks of the dharma-nature and calls it immeasurable and very deep. The Golden Light, horizontally all-encompassing and vertically all-spanning, lacks no virtue and fails to pass through no stage — like the majestic numen of the phoenix or the divine marvel of the dragon. In Paramārtha's explanation, the virtues are not complete and the stages do not pass through — like being cramped into a dove's nest or coiled in a rabbit's burrow. Does this not disgrace the scales and mane of [the dragon of] Yu's Gate and the plumage of [the phoenix of] Cinnabar Cave, so that neither has a mighty bearing? The first three phrases are all simile; the last phrase matches it to the dharma; hence it says, "and no perfect discourse on the dharma-nature."

Ru-Yi Translation

First, raising how the present differs from the ancients. It differs from all the masters in common; therefore it says of them all, "holds the meaning to be otherwise."

Chinese Source

二、據經斥局。若論無量，不少於事，以從法性故增勝說，云理無不統也。中道經王，豈與理異？今且從事，故云何所不攝。此如法界之橫、三諦之竪，不分而分也。豈止三三九法者，別斥真諦也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, rejecting restriction on the basis of the sūtra. If one discusses the immeasurable, it is no less in phenomena; [the sūtra] speaks of what is predominant because it goes by the dharma-nature, saying "there is no principle it does not govern." The king of sūtras, the middle way — how could it differ from principle? Now, for the moment, going by phenomena, it says, "what does it not gather in?" This is like the horizontal of the dharma-realm and the vertical of the three truths: not divided, yet divided. "How could it stop at three sets of three, nine dharmas?" particularly rejects Paramārtha.

Chinese Source

三、稱法釋題。經云無量，意顯橫該。復云甚深，意彰竪徹。今以三字遍譬橫竪一切法門，方稱經意，不違王義。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, explaining the title in accord with the dharma. The sūtra says "immeasurable," intending to show the horizontal encompassing; it further says "very deep," intending to make evident the vertical passing through. Now using the three characters as similes throughout for all Dharma-gates, horizontal and vertical, at last accords with the sūtra's intent and does not go against the meaning of "king."

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, leaving the extensive and following the essentials. According to what the Golden Light illustrates, the Dharma-gates are long and broad without limit; what names and marks of any teaching does it not govern and gather in? Since it is both deep and vast, he was concerned that beginners' words and thoughts could not reach it; hence from all [of them] he takes the essentials and discusses ten, as the first gate for practitioners to awaken and enter. If one enters this gate, what dharma would one not see?

As for raising three illustrations: worldly images compared with the wondrous principle are all partial similes; one must settle them by the dharma, and only then are the partial and the perfect revealed. As with the nine similes of the Tathāgatagarbha Sūtra: in Calming and Contemplation they illustrate the distinct; in other texts they illustrate the perfect. The present ice and water and so on also serve both the perfect and the distinct. How so? If one says that the water of the buddha-realm freezes into the ice of the nine realms, and the ice of the nine realms melts and returns to the water of the buddha-realm, this still belongs to the distinct. If one knows that the ten realms mutually containing one another are like water, and the ten realms limited by deluded attachment are like ice, and melting the ice of deluded attachment forms the water of mutual containment, this is the perfect principle. Firewood and fire, bondage and release, can be understood by analogy. Hence, in the dharmas of the twelve links that turn in saṃsāra: taken as real, the three obstructions are solid; when the feelings are emptied, the three virtues are perfectly merged. Without harming the twelve links in the slightest, they are wholly the wondrous object. Each of delusion, karma, and suffering leads all the way through to the edge and bottom of the dharma-realm; this is called the three paths. Wishing to show the meaning of these three as perfectly merged, the names are established from what is superior; hence it says Dharma-body, prajñā, and liberation. It only changes their names and does not alter the essence of the dharmas; in truth it is only that they lead through in themselves.

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, applying [the simile] by analogy to the virtues and matching it, in terms of embodying and penetrating. Question: Before, in clarifying the three consciousnesses, the ninth, the one nature, was set against the eighth and seventh, the two cultivations, to clarify separation and combination, and so they were likened to the three virtues. Now in clarifying the three paths, all three are contrary cultivation; how can one speak of cultivation two and nature one? If this meaning is not established, then they are two and different from the various sets of three; how could they be the dharma illustrated by the three characters? Answer: Phenomena are themselves principle; phenomena and principle are without difference. Take, among phenomena, delusion giving rise to karma, karma bringing about suffering, and suffering again giving rise to delusion: these three, cultivated evil, are themselves inherent evil, and so they are called the three of the nature. They are also, as causal dharmas, transformed in name as the three consciousnesses, the three buddha-natures, the three prajñās, the three bodhis, and the three great vehicles; also, as dharmas of the fruit, transformed in name as the three bodies and the three nirvāṇas; also, as the function of the fruit, transformed in name as the Three Jewels; also, as the secret treasury, transformed in name as the three virtues. Hence we know that at every juncture only the name is transformed and the dharma is not altered. Hence the Gates of Nonduality says: "the nature points to the three obstacles; that is why it contains three. Cultivation is accomplished from the nature, and accomplishing the three is naturally so." The meaning being so, how can it be said that the three paths do not have separation and combination? Using the Golden Light as a simile for the three paths has roughly this intent.
