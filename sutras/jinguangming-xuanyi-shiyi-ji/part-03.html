Sifting the Ten Dharmas, the Name from the Text and the Thing Itself, and Gushan's First Objection

Fascicle Three of Zhili's Gleanings covers the last part of the Profound Meaning's explanation of the name. It begins with the sorting and sifting of the ten sets of three dharmas. For each set Zhili shows how the Tripiṭaka, shared, and distinct versions fall short of the four virtues of permanence, bliss, self, and purity, and why only the perfect version, in which each member contains the others, is what "gold, light, and brightness" stand for. Next come the two explanations Zhiyi based on the sūtra text itself. In explanation by attaching to the text, the name and matters of the Golden Light run through every chapter and all three times. In explanation by the thing itself, Golden Light directly names the dharma-nature. Here Zhili argues that the real truth has names and the conventional truth borrows them, the reverse of the older view. A short passage defends keeping the explanation by simile for those of dull faculties. The fascicle closes with the opening of the explanation by contemplation of mind. Zhili tells how Qing and Min, and later Gushan Zhiyuan, rejected this section as a later addition. He lists Gushan's four objections and answers the first, "the words are vulgar." The other three are answered in the following fascicles at the passages they target.

Sifting the Three Virtues

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, sifting the ten dharmas, in ten: first, sifting the three virtues, in three: first, marking. Second, the sorting and sifting proper, in two: first, rejecting the partial, in three:

Chinese Source

初、三藏。太子五陰久修五分，雖未無漏得名法身。在二德前。樹下真明方有般若。三十四心者，十六心破見、十八心斷愛。若頓證羅漢及辟支佛，此之二人皆一時得三十四心，羅漢但斷正使、支佛分侵習氣。若樹王下，用三十四，頓斷正習一時俱盡，是故此心獨在菩薩。解脫在後，其相可見。此之三法，以漏無漏存亡不同，故異而且縱。此教經部名為阿含，《釋論》明文以摩訶衍對三藏為小，《婆沙》翻數，此論廣說四階成佛。《阿含》唯經，《婆沙》唯論，三藏之名具經律論。此三所說但有三義，全無德義。何者？終歸灰斷故無常德，非大涅槃故無樂德，無八自在故無我德，不斷五染故無淨德。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the Tripiṭaka [teaching]. The prince's five aggregates had long cultivated the five divisions [of the Dharma-body]; though not yet undefiled, they get the name Dharma-body. It comes before the two [other] virtues. Only with the true illumination beneath the tree is there prajñā. "Thirty-four mind-moments": sixteen mind-moments break views, and eighteen mind-moments sever craving. When arhats and pratyekabuddhas realize suddenly, both of these attain the thirty-four mind-moments all at once; the arhat severs only the main latent tendencies, while the pratyekabuddha partly encroaches on the residual habits. Beneath the king of trees, using the thirty-four, [the bodhisattva] suddenly severs main [tendencies] and habits, all exhausted at one time; hence these mind-moments belong solely to the bodhisattva. "Liberation comes last": its features can be seen. Because these three dharmas differ in the presence and absence of the defiled and the undefiled, they are "different and, moreover, longitudinal." The sūtra portion of this teaching is called the Āgamas. The Great Treatise states plainly that the Mahāyāna, set against the Tripiṭaka, [regards it] as lesser. The Vibhāṣā is rendered "the Abhidharma" (數); this treatise explains at length becoming a buddha in four stages. The Āgamas are sūtras only, the Vibhāṣā is treatise only; the name Tripiṭaka includes sūtras, vinaya, and treatises. What these three teach has only the three meanings and no meaning of virtue at all. Why? Since it ends in ashes and annihilation, it lacks the virtue of permanence; since it is not the great nirvāṇa, it lacks the virtue of bliss; since it lacks the eight sovereign powers, it lacks the virtue of self; since it does not sever the five defilements, it lacks the virtue of purity.

Chinese Source

二、通教。通詮體觀，法本不生，非證後空。此為法身，是故本有。境雖本有，須依此境，體破見思，正習盡處正是般若，故屬現有。果縛盡時方是解脫，故屬當有。此之三法，空境無知、般若有照，如幻色心盡方名脫，故云異而且縱。前代成論師，見乾慧等十地中二乘證果，謂是小教所明人法俱空，乃取此義釋所弘之論，意謂小教探明大乘。故《妙玄》云「舊云《成論》探明大乘」，又云「成論師秖見共般若意，不見不共意」，即此義也。故知彼師不知藏實，是三藏空門與衍門永異，又不知衍門真諦含於但中及不但中。今就彼不知及鈍菩薩，故無四德。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the shared teaching. The shared [teaching] expresses the contemplation of embodying: dharmas are originally unarisen, not empty [only] after realization. This is the Dharma-body; hence it is "originally existent." Although the object is originally existent, one must rely on this object, embodying it to break views and [habituated] thought; the point where the main [tendencies] and habits are exhausted is exactly prajñā, hence it belongs to "existent now." Only when the bonds of the fruit are exhausted is there liberation; hence it belongs to "to-be existent." Among these three dharmas, the object of emptiness has no knowing, prajñā has illumination, and only when illusory form and mind are exhausted is it called liberation; hence it says "different and, moreover, longitudinal." The Satyasiddhi masters of former times, seeing that within the ten grounds of dry wisdom and so on the two vehicles realize the fruit, said that what the lesser teaching clarifies is the emptiness of both persons and dharmas, and took this meaning to explain the treatise they propagated, meaning that the lesser teaching probes and clarifies the Mahāyāna. Hence the Wondrous Profound [Meaning] says, "The old [masters] say the Satyasiddhi probes and clarifies the Mahāyāna," and further, "The Satyasiddhi masters saw only the intent of the shared prajñā and did not see the intent of the unshared." This is that meaning. Hence we know that those masters did not know the reality of the Tripiṭaka — that the Tripiṭaka's gate of emptiness is forever different from the gate of the [Mahā]yāna — nor did they know that the truth of the real in the [Mahā]yāna gate contains the mere middle and the not-mere middle. Now, in terms of what they did not know, and of the dull bodhisattvas, there are no four virtues.

Chinese Source

三、別教。前破真諦乖圓別者，蓋違本經別分四德對於三身，故云乖別。若以彼說四教收之，既談四德非前藏通，德既不融非後圓教。雖收屬別，然非別教通方之說，故云一途。問：真諦立云「般若、解脫各具二德」，今何斥云無德可稱？答若就別論，二各具一，亦可名德。今以圓斥，隨有所闕，德義不成。何者？若般若照境故常、破暗故淨，若無樂我，乃是有苦之常淨、不自在之常淨，豈成德耶？若解脫無暗故樂、廣遠故我，而無常淨，斯乃無常之樂我、垢染之樂我，豈成德耶？既德有增減，則法不高廣，焉稱經王無量甚深耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the distinct teaching. Above, Paramārtha was refuted as departing from the perfect and the distinct; this is because he goes against the sūtra itself, which matches the four virtues separately to the three bodies; hence it says he departs from the distinct. If his account is placed within the four teachings: since it speaks of the four virtues, it is not the earlier Tripiṭaka or shared [teaching]; since the virtues are not fused, it is not the later perfect teaching. Though it is placed under the distinct, it is not the all-round account of the distinct teaching; hence it says "one approach." Question: Paramārtha holds that "prajñā and liberation each possess two virtues"; why do you now reject this, saying they "have no virtue to speak of"? Answer: If discussed in terms of the distinct, the two each possess [part], and this too may be called virtue. Now, rejecting it from the perfect, whatever is lacking, the meaning of virtue is not achieved. Why? If prajñā is permanent because it illuminates the object and pure because it breaks darkness, yet lacks bliss and self, it is a permanence and purity that has suffering, a permanence and purity without sovereignty — how could that be virtue? If liberation is blissful because it is without darkness and is self because it is vast and far-reaching, yet lacks permanence and purity, it is an impermanent bliss and self, a defiled bliss and self — how could that be virtue? Since the virtues increase and decrease, the dharma is not high and broad; how could it be called "king of sūtras," immeasurable and very deep?

Sifting the Three Jewels

Chinese Source

二、簡三寶。相從者，從佛說法，從法有僧，從是三寶于今不絕。皆由歸佛、稟法成僧，故曰相從。復名階梯者，蓋喻等級非相亂也。此明別體四果之僧定不成佛，其猶下級不成上級。又樹王下迷真輕者，見如幻佛說無生法，三乘因地皆能斷結，有異三藏菩薩因中全不斷惑，眾即空故，名理和僧。雖異三藏，而其三乘共證之理既是偏空，亦無四德。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, sifting the Three Jewels. "Following one another": following the Buddha, the Dharma is taught; following the Dharma, there is the Saṅgha; following these, the Three Jewels have not been cut off down to today. All arise from taking refuge in the Buddha and receiving the Dharma to form the Saṅgha; hence it says "following one another." Further, the name "stepwise" is a metaphor for grades that are not jumbled. This clarifies that the Saṅgha of the four fruits, of a separate essence, will certainly not become buddhas, just as a lower step does not become an upper step. Further, as for those beneath the king of trees whose delusion about the real is light: seeing the illusion-like Buddha teach the dharma of non-arising, people of the three vehicles can all sever the fetters at the causal stage, unlike the bodhisattva of the Tripiṭaka [teaching], who severs no delusion at all in the causal stage; because the assembly is identical to the empty, it is called the Saṅgha in harmony with principle. Although it differs from the Tripiṭaka, the principle realized in common by the three vehicles is partial emptiness, so it too lacks the four virtues.

Chinese Source

若華王世界成盧舍那，雖通圓別，今就鈍根迷中重者，不知即性，作修成解，是故佛寶從報彰名，法寶但名無量四諦。既非無作，故從多數受河沙名。稟法之僧雖純菩薩，且非發心便成正覺，故四十一位分於賢聖。此之三寶，佛是僧果、僧是佛因、法是因果，所修所證實僧成佛、佛現權僧，永異階梯，高下不改。然從別相，未是同體。言同體者，三寶一體。此體覺了名佛、此體不覺名法、此體和合名僧，迷悟因果其體不分，一人一念無不具足，故《華嚴》三歸以體解大道為佛、深入經藏為法、統理大眾為僧，三雖在果而是一體。三寶若此，方與三德無二無別，是金光明所譬三寶也。

Ru-Yi Translation

As for becoming Rocana in the Flower-King world: although this is common to the perfect and the distinct, here it is taken in terms of those of dull faculties, heavily deluded about the middle, who do not know identity with the nature and form the understanding that it is accomplished through cultivation. Therefore the Buddha Jewel gets its name from the reward [body], and the Dharma Jewel is named only the immeasurable four noble truths. Since it is not the unconditioned, it receives the name "[as many as] the Ganges' sands" from their great number. The Saṅgha that receives the Dharma, though it consists purely of bodhisattvas, does not become rightly awakened at the arousing of the mind; hence the forty-one stages are divided into worthies and sages. In these Three Jewels the Buddha is the fruit of the Saṅgha, the Saṅgha is the cause of the Buddha, and the Dharma is cause and fruit, what is cultivated and what is realized; the real Saṅgha becomes Buddha, and the Buddha manifests a provisional Saṅgha. This is forever different from the stepwise [Jewels], but high and low do not change. Still, since it follows distinct marks, it is not yet of one essence. As for "one essence," the Three Jewels are one essence. This essence awakened is called Buddha; this essence not awakened is called Dharma; this essence in harmony is called Saṅgha. Delusion and awakening, cause and fruit — their essence is not divided; one person and one thought are complete with them all. Hence the threefold refuge of the Avataṃsaka takes "understanding the great path in essence" as the Buddha, "entering deeply into the treasury of sūtras" as the Dharma, and "governing the great assembly" as the Saṅgha; though the three are at the fruit, they are one essence. Only Three Jewels such as these are not two and not different from the three virtues; these are the Three Jewels of which gold, light, and brightness are a simile.

Sifting the Three Nirvāṇas

Chinese Source

三、簡三涅槃。般涅槃那，翻為安樂，故凡聖大小皆有涅槃。若世人適意，亦是涅槃。若外道不知非想非非想定十種細想及無想天第六心心數法暫爾不行，故皆計為永寂涅槃。若染欲心伏，名方便涅槃。若二乘菩薩論得未得，是三藏涅槃。若三乘同盡子果兩縛，是通教但空共小涅槃，簡不但空不共二乘。今就共論，故無四德。若中道理智及同緣示滅三種涅槃，此有得意及不得意。其得意者，一必收二，三皆圓具，即成圓教大般涅槃，是今所喻。今就失意互不相關者，故屬別耳。若言等者，即地論師也。但以實相名為性淨，修因所成為方便淨，不明緣因薪盡火滅隨機涅槃。既但二種攝義不周，即非三德圓融涅槃，故非今經所喻之法。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, sifting the three nirvāṇas. Parinirvāṇa is translated "peace and bliss"; hence ordinary beings and sages, lesser and greater, all have nirvāṇa. The satisfaction of worldly people is also nirvāṇa. Outsiders, not knowing that in the concentration of neither perception nor nonperception the ten kinds of subtle perception, and in the heaven without perception the mind and mental factors of the sixth consciousness, have merely ceased for the time being, all reckon these as nirvāṇa, everlasting quiescence. When the mind of defiled desire is subdued, this is called expedient nirvāṇa. When the two vehicles and the bodhisattvas are discussed as having attained or not yet attained, this is the nirvāṇa of the Tripiṭaka [teaching]. When the three vehicles alike exhaust the two bonds of seed and fruit, this is the shared teaching's nirvāṇa of mere emptiness, shared with the lesser, distinguished from the not-mere emptiness that is not shared with the two vehicles. Here it is discussed in terms of what is shared; hence it has no four virtues. As for the three kinds of nirvāṇa — the principle of the middle way, wisdom, and showing extinction in common with conditions — there are those who grasp the intent and those who do not. For those who grasp the intent, any one necessarily includes the [other] two and all three are perfectly complete; this is the great parinirvāṇa of the perfect teaching, of which the present [sūtra] makes a simile. Here it is taken in terms of those who lose the intent, [the three] unrelated to one another; hence it belongs to the distinct. "If one says" and so on: these are the Dilun masters. They take only the true mark as the nature-pure and what is accomplished by cultivating causes as the expedient-pure, and do not clarify the conditioning cause — the nirvāṇa that follows capacities, the firewood exhausted and the fire extinguished. Since with only two kinds the meaning is not fully included, it is not the nirvāṇa of the three virtues perfectly fused; hence it is not the dharma of which the present sūtra makes a simile.

Sifting the Three Bodies

Chinese Source

四、簡三身，二：初、簡偏，二：初、明藏通但二無三，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, sifting the three bodies, in two: first, sifting the partial, in two: first, clarifying that the Tripiṭaka and shared [teachings] have only two and not three, in two:

Chinese Source

初、三藏。樹王下佛為真身者，非即事而真，是證真之身，故名真身。神變為應，亦非無謀，全是作意。三藏之中唯明此二，無法報應三身之說，證真現變皆從析法觀智所成。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the Tripiṭaka [teaching]. "The buddha beneath the king of trees as the true body": this is not "phenomena themselves are the true"; it is the body that has realized the real, hence called the true body. Supernormal transformations are the response, and these too are not without deliberation; they are entirely intentional. Within the Tripiṭaka [teaching] only these two are clarified; there is no account of three bodies, Dharma, reward, and response. Realizing the real and manifesting transformations are both accomplished by the contemplative wisdom of analyzing dharmas.

二、通教，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, the shared teaching, in two:

Chinese Source

初、正明但二。此教雖云即事而真，但即偏空，非佛性真。真無實體，非任運應。此教及藏但詮二諦，未明三諦，是故論身唯二無三。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying directly that there are only two. Although this teaching says "phenomena themselves are the true," it is identical only to partial emptiness, not the true of buddha-nature. The true has no real essence, and is not effortless response. This teaching and the Tripiṭaka express only two truths and do not yet clarify three truths; therefore in discussing bodies they have only two and not three.

Chinese Source

二、徵釋真身。欲示即真，先詢色相。若爾者，領前也。事即真空方是佛體，是則丈六非是真佛。又作人形復非鹿馬，究論丈六為是何身？答中二意：初意是應、次意是真。初云應同人像者，以此丈六非愛業感，自己辦地誓扶餘習潤神通生，與物結緣、淨佛國土。群機既熟，出現王宮，故知丈六是神通身，應同人像。然未盡理，故云一往。故又一下正示真身，剋分大小理在空中。今以即空為真身者猶屬於小，故例大乘秖以中智所合之理便為法身。豈離色心別論中道？今但空真，亦即人像全體是空，色心不生色心不滅為真身也。依身起變，名為應身。是故此教唯有二身。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, questioning and explaining the true body. Wishing to show identity with the true, he first inquires about the form and mark. "If so" takes up what precedes. Phenomena identical to true emptiness are the Buddha's essence; in that case the sixteen-foot [body] is not the true buddha. And since it takes human form it is not a deer or a horse; examined to the end, which body is the sixteen-foot? In the answer there are two intents: the first intent is response, the second is the true. The first says "a response in the same image as humans": this sixteen-foot [body] is not brought about by the karma of craving; at the stage where one's own work is done, one vows to support residual habits, moistens [them], and is born through supernormal powers, forming ties with beings and purifying the buddha-land. When the capacities of the multitude have ripened, one appears in the royal palace. Hence we know the sixteen-foot [body] is a body of supernormal power, a response in the same image as humans. But this does not exhaust the principle; hence it says "at first approach." From "another" on, it shows the true body directly: distinguishing greater and lesser decisively, the principle lies in emptiness and the middle. Now, taking identity with emptiness as the true body still belongs to the lesser; hence it takes as an example the Mahāyāna, which takes only the principle united with the wisdom of the middle as the Dharma-body. How could one discuss the middle way apart from form and mind? Now, with only the real of emptiness, the human image too is wholly empty; form and mind not arising, form and mind not ceasing, is the true body. Transformations arising on the basis of the body are called the response-body. Therefore this teaching has only two bodies.

Chinese Source

二、明別教雖三且異。若其互融，那分虛實？驗其所立，體相各別，三身不圓，故今不用。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying that the distinct teaching has three, but separate. If they fused with one another, how could one divide the false from the real? Examining what it establishes, the essence and marks are each separate and the three bodies are not perfect; hence they are not used here.

Chinese Source

二、顯圓，三：初、明三身皆實，三：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, revealing the perfect, in three: first, clarifying that the three bodies are all real, in three:

Chinese Source

初、正明體實。理體既實，理智豈虛？實理實智，冥故起用，用豈不實？三既相即，二乃非虛。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying directly that the essence is real. Since the essence of principle is real, how could the wisdom of principle be false? Real principle and real wisdom, merged, give rise to function; how could the function not be real? Since the three are identical to one another, the two are not false.

Chinese Source

二、引經類顯。淨妙欲境作死壞觀，雖是假想能治貪心，虛有實益。例乎應身非生現生，故非本體，益物不虛故名為實。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, citing the sūtra as a parallel. Performing the contemplation of death and decay on a pure and wondrous object of desire, though it is an imagined thought, can cure the mind of greed; false, it has real benefit. By analogy, the response-body manifests birth where there is no birth; hence it is not the original essence; its benefiting beings is not false, hence it is called real.

Chinese Source

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, concluding by taking the intent.

Chinese Source

二、明四句俱融。圓說三身，舉一即三，各有四句。何者？若別分之，報身真證故實，應身假說故虛，法身平等遮照皆雙。雙照故亦實亦虛，雙遮故非實非虛，三身互具四句皆融，當細揀之。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying that the four propositions are all fused. In the perfect account of the three bodies, raising one is all three, and each has four propositions. Why? If they are divided separately, the reward-body is true realization, hence real; the response-body is a provisional teaching, hence false; the Dharma-body is impartial, negating and illuminating both. Because it illuminates both, it is both real and false; because it negates both, it is neither real nor false. The text has two [parts]: first, marking. Set against the preceding meaning of the teachings, it corresponds to the intent of the two gates of understanding and practice. Above, the ten sets of three dharmas were revealed by simile; attaching to the text attached to both texts, of principle and of phenomena; the thing itself made plain solely the designation of principle and nature. Though all are deep, broad, subtle, wondrous, and perfectly fused, they speak of the Buddha-dharma in terms of the teaching, producing faith and understanding in people. Hence the Great Master says, "Practitioners of the present time, lacking the knowledge-eye, should use faith and understanding to distinguish sameness and difference." As in the earlier arising in sequence of the ten sets of three dharmas, there were two passes: the first pass in terms of the teaching, the second pass in terms of contemplation. In terms of the teaching, to reveal the three virtues, the nine teaching dharmas arise in sequence, ending in the three paths; in terms of contemplation, beginning by overturning the three paths, the nine contemplative dharmas arise in sequence, ending in uniting with the three virtues. Further, the present contemplation of the ten sets of three dharmas does not only accomplish practice but also supports deep understanding. Why? Translator's notes

Lemmas: Phrases quoted from the Profound Meaning reuse the English of `jinguangming-xuanyi` Part 1 ("Sorting and Sifting" sections, "The Name Established According to the Sūtra Text," "The Name Obtained from the Thing Itself") and Part 2 ("Simile and Text: Both Kept"). Gushan's four points (unit 72): Gushan Zhiyuan (孤山智圓, 976–1022) objected to the contemplation-of-mind section in four ways: 詞鄙 "the words are vulgar," 義疎 "the meaning is loose," 理乖 "the principle is contrary," and 事誤 "the facts are mistaken." Only the first is answered here; Zhili says he will answer the others at the passages they target (Part 4 units 8, 49, 82). Gushan's words are quoted from his commentary on the Profound Meaning (the Biaowei ji 表微記). That commentary is not in the Taishō, so the quotation is not checked.

Qing and Min (unit 72): Yuanqing (源清) and Hongmin (洪敏), Shanwai masters, held that the "old version" lacked the contemplation-of-mind section. This led to the controversy over the expanded and abbreviated versions of the Profound Meaning (廣本／略本). Zhili's Record Resolving Difficulties and Supporting the School (釋難扶宗記) is his early reply. It is not in the Taishō but survives in the Siming jiaoxing lu (T1937); not checked here.

金論 (unit 72): "《金論》觀法" reads best as 今論, "the present discussion of contemplative dharmas." No work called Jin lun fits. Translated as 今 with the character noted.

姓 for 性 (unit 25): "圓教智行是姓本具" is taken as 性 (nature), as the parallel "cultivated without cultivation" requires.

出 misplaced (unit 22): Zhili says the character 出 in "開應出化" belongs after 應. The Profound Meaning as printed has "此則開出應化" ("this opens out response and transformation"); Zhili's reading is "opens out response and brings out transformation."

Printed edition error (unit 44): Zhili says the current printed edition reads 等 after "Elephant's Step" where the old manuscripts have 事. The Taishō Profound Meaning has 等 here (see `jinguangming-xuanyi` p1, "Rice Stalk, Axe Handle, Elephant's Step, City Sūtra, and so on").
