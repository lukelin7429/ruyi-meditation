Zhili's Preface, the Opening Praise, and the Common and Particular Names

This is the first fascicle of Siming Zhili's Gleanings on the Profound Meaning of the Golden Light Sūtra (T1784), a running commentary on Zhiyi's Profound Meaning of the Golden Light Sūtra. A short preface dated 1023 explains why Zhili wrote it: his teacher Baoyun's lectures had been recorded with gaps, and the record by the ācārya of Gushan (Zhiyuan) "destroys and removes the contemplation of mind." Zhili therefore gleans what his teacher left over and what later people discarded. This fascicle does not yet argue the point at length; the full defense of the section on contemplating the mind comes in Part 3. Most of Part 1 glosses the Profound Meaning's opening praise. Zhili insists from the first line that "Golden Light" names a dharma, the dharma-nature itself, and is not a simile. He reads the praise as a sequence: the principle is beyond words for buddhas, bodhisattvas, the two vehicles, and ordinary beings alike, yet it can be spoken in response to conditions, and the sages who protect the sūtra set the example Zhiyi follows. He then comments on the general explanation of the five sections (arising in sequence, sifting and distinguishing, the jewel-in-the-bag simile) and on the first parts of Explaining the Name. These are the common and particular names, which he says belong to the teaching, principle, and practice alike; the translation of the title; and the refutation of the Abhidharma and Dilun masters' similes.

Gleanings on the Profound Meaning of the Golden Light Sūtra, Fascicle One (with Preface)

Chinese Source

宋四明沙門知禮述

Ru-Yi Translation

Composed by Zhili, a śramaṇa of Siming, of the Song

Chinese Source

問曰：昔者寶雲法師嘗有撰集贊釋玄辯，近歲孤山闍梨又以章記表明微旨，今復纂述，其故何哉？答曰：寶雲講次，學徒隨錄，義或闕如，未及補治，不幸歸寂。孤山之製多事消文，復於中間毀除觀心。斯實不忍，今故秉筆，拾先師遺餘之義、拾後人遺棄之文，使教行二塗不至壅蔽。但諭新學達者，無誚吾之煩辭也。時天聖元年歲次癸亥四月望日序。

Ru-Yi Translation

Question: Formerly Dharma Master Baoyun compiled a Praise and Explanation, elucidating the Profound [Meaning]; in recent years the ācārya of Gushan has, in turn, made plain its subtle purport with a record by sections. Now you compile and set forth [another]; what is the reason for this? Answer: Baoyun's [explanations] were recorded by his students as he went along in lecturing; in places the meaning was lacking, and before he could supplement and correct it he unfortunately returned to quiescence. Gushan's work mostly concerns interpreting the text, and moreover, in the middle of it, it destroys and removes the contemplation of mind. This I truly cannot bear. Therefore I now take up the brush to glean the meanings left over by my late teacher and to glean the text cast away by later people, so that the two roads of teaching and practice may not come to be blocked. I only tell newcomers and the accomplished: do not ridicule me for my many words. Preface written on the full-moon day of the fourth month of the first year of Tiansheng, the year being guihai.

The Title and the One Who Spoke It

Chinese Source

題有六字，上四所釋、下二能釋。能釋乃通，由智者師解釋諸經皆立五義，故以所釋揀非他部。入文廣解經題四字，故不預敘。能釋二字者，玄謂幽微難見也，義謂理趣深有所以也。其幽微義而有五重，蓋一經始終能詮之名、所召之體、即體之宗、宗成力用，此四言教通局相狀。大師搜抉如是五義解釋一題，欲令學者預知經旨然後尋文，使於文文成智行故。斯是道場持因靜發，稱會佛心演茲奧旨，故不可以暗證者及尋文者同日而語也。幽微所以豈虛名哉？能說師號者，天台即棲真之處，智者是隋主所稱，大師乃群生模範，說者揀異他師握筆撰述也。若始終事迹，具彰別傳，今略不書。

Ru-Yi Translation

The title has six characters: the upper four are what is explained, the lower two are what does the explaining. What does the explaining is common, because Master Zhizhe, in explaining the sūtras, always set up the five meanings; hence what is explained is used to sift out other collections. Entering the text, [the Profound Meaning] explains the four characters of the sūtra title at length; hence they are not set out in advance [here]. As for the two characters that do the explaining: "profound" (玄) means hidden, subtle, and hard to see; "meaning" (義) means that the purport of the principle has deep reasons. This hidden and subtle meaning has five layers: the name that expresses the sūtra from beginning to end; the essence that [the name] summons; the cardinal doctrine that is identical to the essence; the power and function when the cardinal doctrine is accomplished; and the features, common and restricted, of these four as verbal teaching. The Great Master searched out these five meanings to explain a single title, wishing to let students know the purport of the sūtra in advance and then study the text, so that, text after text, it becomes wisdom and practice for them. This arose in stillness from what he upheld at the place of practice; matching the Buddha's mind, he set forth this recondite purport. Hence he cannot be spoken of in the same breath as those who realize in the dark or those who [merely] search the text. How could the reason for [calling it] hidden and subtle be an empty name? As for the title of the master who spoke it: Tiantai is the place where he dwelt in the true; Zhizhe is what the ruler of the Sui called him; "Great Master" [means he is] a model for the multitude of beings; "spoke" distinguishes him from other masters who took up the brush to compose. The events of his life from beginning to end are fully set out in the Separate Biography; here they are, in brief, not written.

The Opening Praise: The Essence of the Dharma Shown as a Whole

Chinese Source

二、釋文，二：初釋序文，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining the text, in two: first, explaining the preface, in two:

Chinese Source

初、總示法體。此者，指定之辭也。金光明者，所示法體也。甚深無量，明體德也。應知此經三字別題是法非譬。何以知然？經敘如來游於無量甚深法性諸佛行處，乃住此定而便唱云「是金光明，諸經之王」，豈非直指所游法性名金光明，不云法性如金光明。而下文所立譬喻一釋者，蓋以諸師解金光明為世物象，用譬如來所得深法。諸師雖乃用譬顯法，其實不知法相圓融，隨名局解，是故不能遍譬諸法。大師欲示金光明海無法不備、無法不融，故順諸師以金光明三字為譬，具足比況佛之所游，略則十種三法，廣則一切法門，一一互融皆不思議。此乃格他譬法不周，因此廣顯法性圓具。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, showing the essence of the dharma as a whole. "This" is a word that points and fixes. "Golden Light" is the essence of the dharma that is shown. "Very deep and immeasurable" clarifies the virtues of the essence. One should know that the three characters of this sūtra's particular title are a dharma, not a simile. How do we know this? The sūtra narrates that the Tathāgata was roaming in the immeasurable, very deep dharma-nature, the sphere of practice of the buddhas, and then, abiding in this concentration, declared: "This Golden Light is the king of sūtras." Is this not pointing directly at the dharma-nature in which he roams and calling it Golden Light? It does not say that the dharma-nature is like gold, light, and brightness. Yet the explanation by simile set up in the text below [is there] because the [other] masters explained gold, light, and brightness as images of worldly things, used as similes for the deep dharma the Tathāgata attained. Although those masters used simile to reveal the dharma, in fact they did not know that the marks of dharmas are perfectly merged; they explained restrictively according to the name, and therefore could not make similes for all dharmas universally. The Great Master, wishing to show that in the ocean of the Golden Light there is no dharma it does not contain and no dharma it does not merge, therefore went along with the masters in taking the three characters gold, light, and brightness as a simile, fully comparing what the Buddha roams in — briefly, the ten sets of three dharmas; extensively, all Dharma-gates — each one mutually merged and all inconceivable. This was to correct their similes for the dharma, which were not comprehensive, and thereby to reveal extensively that the dharma-nature perfectly contains [all].

Chinese Source

然雖順他以譬顯法，其如經題是法非譬，故後自立附文、當體二種解釋，斥彼義推譬喻疎遠，依經就法方為親切。斯由大師深解法性可尊可貴當體名金，寂而常照當體名光，大悲益物當體稱明。是知法性具金光明，真實名義究竟成就也。除法性外，所有名言皆無實義。故金光明三種法門，舉一即三、全三是一，非三非一而一而三，不縱不橫絕思絕議，是祕密藏佛所游處。

Ru-Yi Translation

Yet although he went along with them in using simile to reveal the dharma, the sūtra title is a dharma and not a simile; hence afterward he himself set up the two explanations, by attaching to the text and by the thing itself, rejecting their simile, inferred from meaning, as distant and remote, [and showing that] relying on the sūtra and going by the dharma is close and near. This is because the Great Master deeply understood that the dharma-nature, being honorable and precious, is in itself called gold; being tranquil yet always illuminating, is in itself called light; benefiting beings with great compassion, is in itself called brightness. Thus we know that the dharma-nature contains gold, light, and brightness, and its true and real name and meaning are ultimately accomplished. Apart from the dharma-nature, all names and words lack real meaning. Hence the three Dharma-gates of gold, light, and brightness: raise one and it is three; the whole three is one; neither three nor one, yet one and yet three; neither longitudinal nor horizontal, cut off from thought and cut off from discussion — this is the secret treasury, the place the Buddha roams.

Chinese Source

又復應知，以金光明示法體者，即五章體，蓋由此經以金光明為名、以金光明為體、以金光明為宗、以金光明為用、以金光明而為教相。亦可三字別對五章，以金為體、以光為宗、以明為用；總三為名，分別三名而為教相。法體既爾，體德合然，甚深是光之德窮法性底故，無量是明之德達法性邊故，此二不二是金之德，法性究竟尊貴義故。亦可三義皆甚深、皆無量、皆不二。五章之德莫不如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

Further, one should know that showing the essence of the dharma by the Golden Light is [showing] the essence of the five sections. For this sūtra takes the Golden Light as the name, the Golden Light as the essence, the Golden Light as the cardinal doctrine, the Golden Light as the function, and the Golden Light as the characteristics of the teaching. Alternatively, the three characters can be matched separately to the five sections: gold as the essence, light as the cardinal doctrine, brightness as the function; the three together as the name; and distinguishing the three names as the characteristics of the teaching. The essence of the dharma being so, the virtues of the essence are fittingly so: "very deep" is the virtue of light, because it exhausts the bottom of the dharma-nature; "immeasurable" is the virtue of brightness, because it reaches the edge of the dharma-nature; these two being nondual is the virtue of gold, because it is the meaning of the dharma-nature being ultimately honored and noble. Alternatively, all three meanings are very deep, all immeasurable, all nondual. The virtues of the five sections are all like this.

The Intent of the Teaching: Principle Cut Off from Words

Chinese Source

二、別明教意。上已總示五章法體，今乃別明起教之意。初敘說經意，即如來顯示五章；二敘宣通意，即是智者流行五章。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying separately the intent of the teaching. Above, the essence of the dharma of the five sections was shown as a whole; now the intent of giving rise to the teaching is clarified separately. First, narrating the intent of teaching the sūtra: this is the Tathāgata revealing the five sections. Second, narrating the intent of proclaiming and spreading it: this is Zhizhe circulating the five sections.

Chinese Source

初自為二：初、據理絕言，蓋由至理但可妙證難以狀名。二、赴緣可說。此約大悲無說而說，說必利人。初又為二：初、約我辨，上至極果下及庸凡皆不能令妙理有說。更分三：

Ru-Yi Translation

The first in turn has two: first, by principle, cut off from words — because the ultimate principle can only be wondrously realized and is hard to describe or name. Second, responding to conditions, it can be spoken — this is in terms of great compassion speaking without speaking, and its speaking surely benefits people. The first again has two: first, discussing it in our own terms: from the ultimate fruit above down to the common and ordinary below, none can make the wondrous principle spoken. This is further divided into three:

Chinese Source

初、明果人不能盡喻。四佛說偈，山斤、海滴、地塵、空界，皆不能比釋尊壽命。此之四喻，虛空最大，以山等三依空立故。虛空雖大，而是妄心變起之境，迷真故生、悟性則滅，與眼作對、心緣所及，安能盡喻不可思議金光明耶？故經云「空生大覺中，如海一漚發」，寧將一漚類乎大海？空向莫喻，三那可論？問：經云「空等莫比釋尊所得壽命」，今何得云不類大覺及金光明？答：覺性若少金等三義則不名大，釋尊壽命義當於明，不具金光則非永壽。一法不少、三法不多，生佛無差、體用不二，若不爾者非方等義。四佛世尊喻不能及，彰理絕言也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, clarifying that even persons of the fruit cannot fully illustrate it. The four buddhas spoke verses [saying that] the mountains in pounds, the sea in drops, the earth in dust-motes, and the bounds of space all cannot compare with Śākyamuni's lifespan. Of these four illustrations, empty space is the greatest, because the mountains and the other two are established relying on space. Though empty space is great, it is an object transformed and raised up by the deluded mind: it arises through being deluded about the true and perishes when the nature is awakened to; it stands opposite the eye and lies within the reach of the mind's objects. How could it fully illustrate the inconceivable Golden Light? Hence the sūtra says, "Space arises within great awakening like a single bubble arising in the sea." How could one take a single bubble as comparable to the great sea? If even space cannot illustrate it, how can the [other] three be discussed? Question: The sūtra says, "Space and the rest cannot compare with the lifespan Śākyamuni has attained." How can you now say that they are not comparable to great awakening and to the Golden Light? Answer: If the nature of awakening lacked the three meanings of gold and the rest, it would not be called great. Śākyamuni's lifespan corresponds in meaning to brightness; if it did not contain gold and light, it would not be an everlasting life. Not one dharma lacking, the three dharmas not too many; beings and buddhas without difference; essence and function nondual — were it not so, it would not be the meaning of the Vaipulya. That the four buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, cannot reach it with illustrations shows that principle is cut off from words.

Chinese Source

二、明因位未能窮源。上舉果佛證雖究竟，而法本寂滅，故言喻莫彰；今辨因人未到性源，故擬議非及。此自為二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying that those at the stage of cause cannot yet exhaust the source. Above, it raised that although the realization of the buddhas of the fruit is ultimate, the dharma is originally quiescent and extinct, so words and illustrations cannot make it evident; now it discusses that persons of the cause have not yet reached the source of the nature, so their conjectures cannot reach it. This in turn has two:

Chinese Source

初、約喻以智斷斥。日輪赫奕喻智德般若，嬰兒之眼喻空假觀慧，既違本智則非佛眼，故於智德赫日非所瞻仰。大舶樓櫓喻斷德解脫，新產之婦喻生法緣慈，既異無緣則無妙力，故於斷德樓櫓非所執持。此約圓果三智三脫，斥前三教菩薩悲智故也。若圓菩薩修既即性，則能從始不乖二德。然雖解即，若因望果，智有明昧、力分強弱，是故因人於果智斷亦非瞻仰及以執持，須了智斷名為光明。二德不二，即是法身，復名為金。雖用二斥，乃顯未窮三法源也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, rejecting by illustrations, in terms of wisdom and severance. "The blazing disk of the sun" illustrates the virtue of wisdom, prajñā; "an infant's" eyes illustrate the contemplative wisdom of emptiness and the provisional. Since these depart from the fundamental wisdom, they are not the buddha-eye; hence they cannot gaze up at the blazing sun of the virtue of wisdom. "The towers of a great ship" illustrate the virtue of severance, liberation; a woman who has newly given birth illustrates loving-kindness taking beings and dharmas as object. Since this differs from [loving-kindness] without object, it lacks wondrous power; hence it cannot hold the towers of the virtue of severance. This is in terms of the three wisdoms and three liberations of the perfect fruit, rejecting the compassion and wisdom of the bodhisattvas of the preceding three teachings. As for bodhisattvas of the perfect [teaching], since their cultivation is identical to the nature, they can from the very start not deviate from the two virtues. Yet although they understand identity, if the cause is viewed against the fruit, wisdom has clarity and dimness and power is divided into strong and weak; hence persons of the cause also cannot gaze up at or hold the wisdom and severance of the fruit. One must understand that wisdom and severance are called light and brightness. The two virtues being nondual is the Dharma-body, which is again called gold. Although two are used for the rejection, it shows that they have not exhausted the source of the three dharmas.

Chinese Source

二、約法以因果定。偏圓菩薩皆能伏斷，隨其所行悉名清淨。今圓極果所行法性超越一切，故言過也。於金光明極證之人尚不能喻，未窮源者寧可言耶？

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, settling by the dharma, in terms of cause and fruit. Bodhisattvas of the partial and the perfect [teachings] can all subdue and sever, and whatever they practice is called pure. Now the dharma-nature practiced at the perfect, ultimate fruit transcends all; hence it says "beyond." If even those who have ultimately realized the Golden Light cannot illustrate it, how could those who have not exhausted the source speak of it?

Ordinary Beings and the Lesser Vehicle

Chinese Source

三、明凡小全迷所以，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, clarifying why ordinary beings and the lesser [vehicle] are completely deluded, in two:

Chinese Source

初、小。偏圓菩薩發曠大心，有分證智，於金光明妙絕之理猶尚不能騰象立言，況復二乘滅心自度如聾望啞，豈能思說諸佛行處？

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the lesser. The bodhisattvas of the partial and perfect [teachings] arouse a vast mind and have the wisdom of partial realization, yet even they cannot raise images or set up words for the wondrous, cut-off principle of the Golden Light; how much less can the two vehicles, who extinguish the mind and save only themselves — like the deaf gazing at the mute — conceive of or speak of the sphere of practice of the buddhas?

Chinese Source

二、凡。三乘賢聖雖小異大、因不及果，而能修證三諦理智，尚莫言想金光明海。況凡外之徒本非其分，隨語生見故言則傷實、既執無言故默則致失。若具論於言，乃有單複及具足句；具論於默；則於三重四句之外各一無言，并犢子部我在第五不可說藏，此皆邪外發語默見也。若悠悠者及學佛人，惟理之心非語即默，於茲二處增見長非，雖非神我全當人執。故四教四門皆生語見，離四即起無言之見，故《起信論》明五人執，皆是執於如來藏起。今之所斥正在此人，故言與默皆云不可。如是具論凡夫起見之語默、二乘偏證之思說、菩薩未極之智辯，皆不能詮至圓之性。上至果佛純淨心口究竟說證亦不能喻者，蓋顯金光明本來祕密，離言說相、離心緣相，俾乎行者辭喪慮忘。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, ordinary beings. Although the worthies and sages of the three vehicles differ as lesser and greater, and the cause does not reach the fruit, they can cultivate and realize the principle and wisdom of the three truths, yet still cannot speak of or think of the ocean of the Golden Light. How much less the followers of the ordinary and outsiders, who originally have no share in it! Following words, they give rise to views; hence "speaking harms its reality." Clinging to wordlessness, "silence brings about its loss." If speech is discussed fully, there are the single, the compound, and the complete tetralemma; if silence is discussed fully, then beyond the three layers of the tetralemma there is in each case a wordlessness, together with the Vātsīputrīya school's self residing within a fifth, inexpressible category — these are all views of speech and silence arising among heterodox outsiders. As for the careless and those who study Buddhism, their minds, [taking] only principle, are either in speech or in silence, and in these two places they increase views and grow wrongs; although it is not the spiritual self, it is wholly the attachment to a person. Hence the four gates of the four teachings all give rise to views of speech, and apart from the four [gates] they give rise to the view of wordlessness. Hence the Awakening of Faith explains five attachments to a person, all of them attachments arising with regard to the tathāgatagarbha. What is rejected now is precisely these people; hence of both speech and silence it says "will not do." Discussed fully, then: the speech and silence of ordinary beings who give rise to views, the conceiving and speaking of the two vehicles with their partial realization, and the wise eloquence of the bodhisattvas who have not reached the ultimate — none can express the nature of the utmost perfection. As for even the buddhas of the fruit above, whose purely pure mind and mouth ultimately speak and realize, being unable to illustrate it — this shows that the Golden Light is originally secret, apart from the mark of words and speech, apart from the mark of the mind's objects, so that the practitioner's words perish and thoughts are forgotten.

Proof Texts: The Larger Prajñāpāramitā and the Great Sūtra

Chinese Source

二、引文證，二：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, citing texts as proof, in two:

Chinese Source

初、《大品》。彼經及論明先尼梵志本雖邪外道，機已熟，詣佛請云：「令我此坐不起得眼。」佛為開決，證須陀洹。佛還詰其悟理之智：「由內觀故得是智耶？」答言：「不也。」「外觀及以內外俱等得是智耶？」皆答：「不也。」此乃四句言想都絕，方得預流。小智尚爾，況金光明乎？

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the Larger [Prajñāpāramitā]. That sūtra and its treatise explain that the brahmacārin Śreṇika, though originally a heterodox outsider, his capacity being already ripe, came to the Buddha and requested: "Let me gain the eye without rising from this seat." The Buddha opened and resolved [the matter] for him, and he realized stream-entry. The Buddha then questioned him about the wisdom by which he had awakened to principle: "Did you attain this wisdom through inner contemplation?" He answered: "No." "Did you attain this wisdom through outer contemplation, or through both inner and outer, and so on?" To all he answered: "No." Here words and thoughts of the tetralemma are all cut off, and only then does one enter the stream. If even the lesser wisdom is so, how much more the Golden Light?

Chinese Source

"Apart from the mark of words and speech, apart from the mark of the mind's objects" is also its wording. The verse of Aśvaghoṣa in unit 28 is the treatise's opening refuge verse.
