The Remaining Contemplations, Emperor-Wisdom-King, and Gushan's Fourth Point

Fascicle Five continues Zhili's commentary on the contemplation-of-mind section of Zhiyi's Profound Meaning. Picking up after the three buddha-natures, it works through the remaining seven of the ten sets of three dharmas — the three prajñās, bodhis, great vehicles, bodies, nirvāṇas, Jewels, and virtues — showing in each case how a single thought of mind, contemplated as identical to the empty, the provisional, and the middle, is itself the Golden Light. Zhili explains why the threefold contemplation is set out in sequence though practiced at once, glosses the "four movements" of thought, reads the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's thirteen "not getting hold of the body" as the vanishing of all three truths, and defends Zhiyi's rebuke of practitioners of "deluded emptiness" who refuse to revere sūtras and images. Two of Gushan Zhiyuan's objections are answered here. The third part of his third point ("the principle is contrary") attacks the line calling the truth of the real "not awakening" yet the Buddha Jewel; Zhili replies that in this triad the Dharma Jewel includes the other two. Gushan's fourth point ("the facts are mistaken") attacks Zhiyi's reading of "emperor-king" as emperor, wisdom, and king; Zhili answers its four claims one by one, citing Yijing's later title Most Victorious King as confirmation. The fascicle ends with the ten sets of three dharmas as "kings of sūtras" gathering in Dharma-gates, sūtras, and stages.

Contemplating the Three Prajñās

Chinese Source

◎四、觀三般若，三：初、標。二、釋，三：

Ru-Yi Translation

Fourth, contemplating the three prajñās, in three: first, the heading; second, the explanation, in three:

Chinese Source

初、約圓總舉。總舉一心空假中三，是三般若。何者下，略示三相，以即一而多示假相，即多而一示空相，非一非多示中相。於一念心而論三相，不前不後亦不一時。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, a general presentation in terms of the perfect. It presents in general the empty, the provisional, and the middle of the one mind as the three prajñās. From "Why?" on, it briefly shows the three marks: being one yet many shows the mark of the provisional; being many yet one shows the mark of the empty; neither one nor many shows the mark of the middle. The three marks are discussed in a single thought of mind, neither before nor after, nor at one time.

Chinese Source

二、寄次別釋，三：初假、次空、後中。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining them separately by borrowing the sequential, in three: first the provisional, next the empty, last the middle.

Chinese Source

初、一心一切心，別示假也。假在初者，假有二種：若在空後，即建立假；若在空前，即生死假。欲明凡夫從心生過，警於初學有漏之心念念常造六道三障，令知其過，動習空中以求出離，故於三觀示假在前。日夜常生無量眾生者，謂一業成，百千萬生受報不盡，一一果報皆有假名。如諸經律所明來報，那不自省，輒謂無生？十二因緣，喻如鉤鎖相續無際，故云一心一切心。此生死假，即建立中所治之病。舉病顯藥，假觀立也。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, "one mind is all minds" shows the provisional separately. As for the provisional coming first: the provisional is of two kinds. If it comes after the empty, it is the provisional of establishing; if it comes before the empty, it is the provisional of birth-and-death. It wishes to make clear that ordinary beings produce faults from the mind, and to warn beginners that their contaminated minds, thought after thought, constantly create the six destinies and the three obstructions, so that they know their faults and are moved to practice the empty and the middle in order to seek escape; therefore, among the three contemplations, it shows the provisional first. "Day and night it constantly gives birth to immeasurable beings" means that once one karma is accomplished, the rewards received over hundreds, thousands, and myriads of births are not exhausted, and each fruit of reward has a provisional name. Such are the future rewards explained in the sūtras and vinayas; how could one not examine oneself, and rashly speak of no-arising? The twelve links are likened to hooked chains, continuing without bound; hence it says "one mind is all minds." This provisional of birth-and-death is the illness that is cured within the [provisional of] establishing. Raising the illness to reveal the medicine, the contemplation of the provisional is established.

Chinese Source

二、一切心一心，別示空也。既知心有則生諸心，欲寂諸心當觀心空。須約四性，檢一念心生滅叵得，一心既空一切安有？故舉小火小珠喻一心空，燒薪澄海喻一切空，故云能觀心空，從心所生一切諸心無不即空。欲明空觀其相顯故，故寄二乘分齊而說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, "all minds are one mind" shows the empty separately. Once one knows that if the mind exists all minds arise, one who wishes to still all minds should contemplate the mind as empty. One must, in terms of the four natures, examine a single thought of mind: its arising and ceasing cannot be found. Since one mind is empty, how could all exist? Hence it raises the small fire and the small jewel as similes for one mind being empty, and burning the firewood and clearing the sea as similes for all being empty; hence it says, "If one can contemplate the mind as empty, all the minds arising from the mind are without exception identical to the empty." Because it wishes to make clear that the mark of the contemplation of emptiness is evident, it speaks by borrowing the scope of the two vehicles.

Chinese Source

三、雙亡二邊故，煩惱非一非一切，別示中也。現前一念若定空者，下能舒出一切有心。若定有者，何能卷歸一空心耶？不空不有、無狀無名，強稱中道。復以識智示其邊中。經云「不依識者非真實識，是虛妄識」，凡小依之，著有、沈空二種之樂也。經云「依智」者，非二乘一切智及菩薩道種智，是一切種智也，故屬圓教。佛及菩薩達二邊中，故名求理；欲示中道觀相明故，故斥二觀。其實三諦一心圓照。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, "because both extremes vanish, afflictions are neither one nor all" shows the middle separately. If the present single thought were definitely empty, how could it unfold all the minds of existence? If it were definitely existent, how could it be rolled back into one empty mind? Neither empty nor existent, without shape or name, it is forcibly called the middle way. Further, it uses consciousness and wisdom to show its extremes and its middle. The sūtra says, "'Not relying on consciousness': [this consciousness] is not true and real consciousness; it is false consciousness." Ordinary beings and those of the Lesser [Vehicle] rely on it — the two kinds of bliss, attachment to existence and sinking in emptiness. The sūtra says "rely on wisdom": this is not the two vehicles' wisdom that knows all things nor the bodhisattvas' wisdom of the paths, but the wisdom knowing all modes; therefore it belongs to the perfect teaching. The buddhas and bodhisattvas penetrate the two extremes and the middle; hence it is called seeking principle. Because it wishes to show that the mark of contemplating the middle way is clear, it rejects the two contemplations. In reality the three truths are perfectly illuminated in one mind.

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, matching the wisdoms on the basis of the perfect, in two:

Chinese Source

初、對智。言如是觀者即一心三觀者，示三觀相須寄次第，為明對破三種惑故、顯三諦故。若能一心修此三者，自成圓觀。何則？頓破三惑則一空一切空也，頓顯三諦則一假一切假也，三皆妙故則一中一切中也。此三方是圓三般若。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, matching the wisdoms. "Contemplating thus is the threefold contemplation in a single mind" shows that the marks of the three contemplations must borrow the sequential, in order to clarify breaking the three kinds of delusion correspondingly and to reveal the three truths. If one can cultivate these three in one mind, it naturally becomes the perfect contemplation. Why? Breaking the three delusions all at once is "one empty, all empty"; revealing the three truths all at once is "one provisional, all provisional"; since all three are wondrous, it is "one middle, all middle." Only these three are the perfect three prajñās.

Chinese Source

二、明圓。據《大論》文，三種觀智實在一念，體是祕藏，故離前後及並別等。《大經》依智，智體如是，初心依止即名佛行。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the perfect. According to the text of the Great Treatise, the three kinds of contemplative wisdom are in reality in a single thought; their essence is the secret treasury; hence they are apart from before and after, side by side and separate, and so on. The Great Sūtra's "rely on wisdom": the essence of wisdom is thus, and for the beginning mind to rely on it is called the practice of the Buddha.

Chinese Source

三、結。例如前說。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, the conclusion. As said before, by analogy.

Contemplating the Three Bodhis

Chinese Source

五、觀三菩提，三：初、標。二、釋、三：

Ru-Yi Translation

Fifth, contemplating the three bodhis, in three: first, the heading; second, the explanation, in three:

Chinese Source

初、約圓總舉，如三般若。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, a general presentation in terms of the perfect, as with the three prajñās.

Chinese Source

二、寄次別釋。以次第三顯圓頓意，亦同前三般若說。但今假觀列於空後，復明藥病是建立假。又前般若體是三智，但於一念略明修相，不須借義示於觀法。今菩提翻道，是能通義。又菩提心，體是四弘。《大集經》云「未度者令度，未解者令解，未安者令安，未滅者令滅。」四皆度生，今三觀中皆云度心數之眾生，乃是借彼度他生義，成今三觀度已眾生，故知附法含託事義。文自為三：

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, explaining them separately by borrowing the sequential. Using the sequential three to reveal the meaning of the perfect and sudden is also the same as what was said of the three prajñās. But now the contemplation of the provisional is listed after the empty, and further it clarifies medicine and illness; this is the provisional of establishing. Further, the essence of the preceding prajñās is the three wisdoms; it only briefly clarified the mark of cultivation in a single thought and did not need to borrow a meaning to show the contemplative dharma. Now "bodhi" is translated "path," which means that which can clear [the way]. Further, the essence of the bodhi mind is the four great vows. The Great Collection Sūtra says, "Those not yet delivered, to make them delivered; those not yet liberated, to make them liberated; those not yet at peace, to make them at peace; those not yet extinguished, to make them extinguished." All four deliver beings. Now in each of the three contemplations it says "delivers the beings that are the mental factors"; this borrows the meaning of delivering other beings there to accomplish the present three contemplations' delivering of one's own beings. Hence we know that [contemplation] attached to dharmas contains the meaning of relying on phenomena. The text in turn has three [parts]:

Chinese Source

初、破假入空。先舉生死為所破假，即一切心也。起非次第，故交橫繚亂，乃舉四物喻繚亂相，如絲之亂、如沙之多、如蠶自縛、如蛾自然，此四喻於世間因果，是故總云為苦為惱。次若知下，正明即空菩提心觀。若空觀相，前三識中已曾略示，是故今文但云知空。此菩提心度義通義，並約見思即空而說。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, breaking the provisional and entering the empty. First it raises birth-and-death as the provisional to be broken; this is "all minds." Arising without sequence, they are therefore "crisscrossing and tangled"; it then raises four things as similes for the mark of being tangled: like tangled silk, like the multitude of sand, like the silkworm binding itself, like the moth burning itself. These four are similes for worldly cause and effect; therefore it says in sum, "making suffering and distress." Next, from "If one knows" on, it properly clarifies the contemplation of the bodhi mind identical to the empty. As for the mark of the contemplation of emptiness, it was already briefly shown in [the section on] the three consciousnesses above; therefore the present text only says "knows the empty." The meanings of delivering and clearing in this bodhi mind are both spoken in terms of views and habituated thought being identical to the empty.

Chinese Source

二、破空出假。先舉空過，經云「空亂意等」者，經即《涅槃》斥小之文。小乘詮空為寂滅之理，以有為妄亂；大乘詮中為寂滅性，乃以空有俱為亂意。雖離有亂，仍被空亂。今修觀時心若著空，即指此心數為空亂意眾生。此空心數望彼見思，而得名智。今論假觀，此智是亂，故云智亂甚盲闇。小乘證空得三無為，謂擇滅無為、非擇滅無為、虛空無為。此處滅心，菩提善根不得生長，故斥為坑。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, breaking the empty and emerging into the provisional. First it raises the fault of the empty. The sūtra's "confused by emptiness" and so on: the sūtra is the text of the Nirvāṇa rejecting the Lesser. The Lesser Vehicle sets forth emptiness as the principle of quiescent extinction and takes existence as false confusion; the Great Vehicle sets forth the middle as the nature of quiescent extinction, and so takes both emptiness and existence as confused minds. Though free of the confusion of existence, one is still confused by emptiness. Now, if in cultivating contemplation the mind clings to emptiness, it points to these mental factors as "beings whose minds are confused by emptiness." These empty mental factors, compared with views and habituated thought, get the name of wisdom; but now, discussing the contemplation of the provisional, this wisdom is confusion; hence it says, "the confusion of wisdom is very blind and dark." The Lesser Vehicle realizes emptiness and attains the three unconditioned, namely the unconditioned of cessation through discrimination, the unconditioned of cessation not through discrimination, and the unconditioned of space. In this place the mind is extinguished, and the good roots of bodhi cannot grow; hence it is rejected as a pit.

Chinese Source

是大乘怨鳥者，《大論》三十云「譬如空澤有樹名奢摩黎，枝觚廣大眾鳥集宿。一鴿後至住一枝上，枝觚即時為之而折。澤神問言：『鷳鷲皆能任持，何至小鳥便不自勝？』樹神答云：『此鳥從我怨家樹來，食彼樹子來棲我上。或當放糞子墮地者，惡樹復生為害必大。是故懷憂，寧捨一枝，所全者大。』菩薩摩訶薩亦復如是，於諸外道天魔等無如是畏而畏二乘，二乘於菩薩邊亦如彼鳥，壞彼大乘心永滅佛乘心。」今取此義，明破空出假成菩提觀。

Ru-Yi Translation

"It is the enemy-bird of the Mahāyāna": the Great Treatise, fascicle thirty, says: "It is like an empty marsh in which there is a tree called śālmalī, whose branches are broad and large, where flocks of birds gather to roost. One dove arrived later and perched on a branch, and the branch at once broke because of it. The marsh spirit asked, 'Pheasants and vultures you can all bear; why, when it comes to a small bird, can you not bear it?' The tree spirit answered, 'This bird has come from the tree of my enemy; it has eaten that tree's seeds and comes to roost on me. If it should drop its droppings and the seeds fall to the ground, the evil tree will grow again, and the harm will surely be great. Therefore I am anxious; I would rather give up one branch, for what is preserved is great.' The bodhisattva-mahāsattva is also like this: toward the non-Buddhists, the heavenly māras, and so on, he has no such fear, but he fears the two vehicles. The two vehicles, to the bodhisattva, are like that bird: they destroy his Great Vehicle mind and forever extinguish the mind of the buddha-vehicle." Now this meaning is taken to clarify breaking the empty and emerging into the provisional to accomplish the contemplation of bodhi.

Chinese Source

次若真下，正明假觀菩提心相。真即假故，依空建立也。此菩提心度義通義，並就塵沙即假而說。凡論假觀不出三義，謂知病識藥、應病授藥、令得服行。今從分別下，以四分別明四悉檀，寄此四悉總明三義：可不不同即世界，時宜生善是為人，以藥治病即對治，逗機會理是第一義。此四明了即假觀成也。

Ru-Yi Translation

Next, from "If the real" on, it properly clarifies the mark of the bodhi mind in the contemplation of the provisional. Because the real is identical to the provisional, [the provisional] is established relying on the empty. The meanings of delivering and clearing in this bodhi mind are both spoken in terms of dust-and-sand being identical to the provisional. In general, discussions of the contemplation of the provisional do not go beyond three meanings: knowing the illness and recognizing the medicine, giving medicine according to the illness, and making [beings] take it and practice. Now, from "distinguishing" on, it uses the four distinctions to clarify the four siddhāntas, borrowing these four siddhāntas to clarify the three meanings in sum: "what may and may not be," the differing, is the mundane; "what is timely," producing good, is the individual; "medicine and illness," curing illness by medicine, is the therapeutic; "fitting and meeting," fitting capacities and meeting principle, is the ultimate meaning. When these four are clear, the contemplation of the provisional is accomplished.

Chinese Source

三、破邊入中。先舉二觀未免生過，今為所捨，而以見思及塵沙惑為浮沈病，空假乃為二病之藥。以病偏增，故藥偏用。藥存成病著。若墮二邊，增無明病，故須兩捨。次非空下，正明中道菩提心觀。此中度義通義，皆約無明即中而說。心無能所，名不住法，此法方可住於中道。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, breaking the extremes and entering the middle. First it raises the two contemplations as not yet escaping the arising of faults, and now to be relinquished; it takes views and habituated thought and the delusions of dust-and-sand as the illnesses of floating and sinking, and the empty and the provisional as the medicines for the two illnesses. Because the illness leans one way, the medicine is used one way. If the medicine remains, it becomes the illness of clinging; if one falls into the two extremes, the illness of ignorance increases; therefore both must be relinquished. Next, from "Neither empty" on, it properly clarifies the contemplation of the bodhi mind of the middle way. The meanings of delivering and clearing here are both spoken in terms of ignorance being identical to the middle. The mind without subject or object is called the dharma of non-abiding; only this dharma can abide in the middle way.

Chinese Source

三、依圓對法，二：初、明圓。說欲相顯須寄次第，觀就理融則無前後，前三般若已明其意。

Ru-Yi Translation

Third, matching the dharmas on the basis of the perfect, in two: first, clarifying the perfect. When explained, wishing to make the marks evident, one must borrow the sequential; in contemplation, merged in principle, there is no before or after. The three prajñās above already made this intent clear.

Chinese Source

二、對法。今三菩提就異名說，真性菩提三皆妙絕故，亦名無上。實智菩提三皆蕩相故，亦名清淨。方便菩提三皆自在逗會無遺故，亦名究竟。三在一心故三各三，以體融故發即俱發，是故當體名金光明。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, matching the dharmas. The present three bodhis are explained in terms of their different names. The bodhi of true nature, because all three are wondrous and absolute, is also called unsurpassed. The bodhi of real wisdom, because all three sweep away marks, is also called pure. Expedient bodhi, because all three are sovereign and fit and meet [capacities] without omission, is also called ultimate. Because the three are in one mind, each of the three is three; because their essence is fused, when they arise they arise together; therefore in themselves they are called the Golden Light.

Chinese Source

Third, the conclusion. As before.

Contemplating the Three Great Vehicles

Chinese Source

六、觀三大乘，三：初、標：二、釋，三：

Ru-Yi Translation

Sixth, contemplating the three great vehicles, in three: first, the heading; second, the explanation, in three:

Chinese Source

初、總立觀法。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, setting up the contemplative dharma in general.

Chinese Source

二、約境明觀。附三大乘修圓三觀，必須境觀義符於乘，以乘是運義。三種大乘無法不運，性既具運故逆順修，法爾而運。今體逆修，念念四運，運運即性，性是三諦，乃成三觀順修妙運。此文分三：初、明四運為境觀一念者，趣舉一念也。心隨境遷，起滅更運，故一一念無不四運。從未至已終而復始，凡愚不覺為運所遷，故以閉目喻凡不覺，舟行喻於四運心疾。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying contemplation in terms of the object. To cultivate the perfect three contemplations attached to the three great vehicles, the meaning of object and contemplation must match the vehicle, because "vehicle" means conveying (運). The three kinds of great vehicle convey every dharma; since the nature inherently includes conveying, contrary and compliant cultivation are conveyed as a matter of course. Now, embodying contrary cultivation: thought after thought has the four movements; each movement is identical to the nature, and the nature is the three truths; thus it becomes the wondrous movement of the compliant cultivation of the three contemplations. This text is divided into three: first, clarifying the four movements as the object. "Contemplating a single thought" just picks up one thought. The mind shifts with objects, arising and ceasing, moving in turn; hence there is no single thought without the four movements. From not yet [thinking] to having [thought], ending and beginning again, the foolish do not notice and are carried off by the movements; hence "with eyes closed" is a simile for ordinary beings not noticing, and the boat's going is a simile for the speed of the mind of four movements.

Chinese Source

二、明三運為觀。圓教行者知剎那心性是祕藏，祕藏遍含未始暫缺，故無一運非空假中。得此意者，四運愈遷、三觀彌進。故《止觀》云「薪多火盛，風益求羅。」所以大師常示眾云：「實心繫實境，實緣次第生，實實迭相注，自然入實理。」實心繫實境者，三觀繫三諦也。實緣次第生者，四運迭遷也。四運是境，境為觀緣，如薪助火。實實迭相注者，三觀實心注三諦實境，此之實境還注實心，相注不已，自然從於觀行相似，得入初住實理之中。此乃以三觀運運於四運，亦是四運之運運三觀運，皆得名為以運運運。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the three movements as contemplation. The practitioner of the perfect teaching knows that the nature of the momentary mind is the secret treasury, and that the secret treasury contains everything everywhere without lacking for a moment; hence there is no movement that is not empty, provisional, and middle. For one who gets this intent, the more the four movements shift, the more the three contemplations advance. Hence Calming and Contemplation says, "The more firewood, the fiercer the fire; wind enlarges the qiuluo." Therefore the Great Master often instructed the assembly: "A real mind fastened to a real object; real conditions arise in sequence; real and real pour into each other in turn; naturally one enters real principle." "A real mind fastened to a real object" is the three contemplations fastened to the three truths. "Real conditions arise in sequence" is the four movements shifting in turn. The four movements are the object; the object is the condition for contemplation, as firewood aids the fire. "Real and real pour into each other in turn": the real mind of the three contemplations pours into the real object of the three truths, and this real object in turn pours into the real mind; pouring into each other without ceasing, naturally, from contemplative practice and resemblance, one gets to enter the real principle of the first abiding. Other sources located: "A real mind fastened to a real object …" (unit 24) is in T1913 Zhiguan yili and T1919. The site's Mohe Zhiguan renders this line differently ("wind only fanning the hunt's own pursuit"); see decisions. The enemy-bird story (unit 16) is in T1509 Great Treatise (the copy here places it in fascicle 26 or 27, not thirty); T1509 reads 鵰鷲 "eagles and vultures" where Zhili has 鷳鷲 (rendered literally "pheasants and vultures"). The Motuomo Sūtra gloss (unit 42) is quoted in T1717 Annotations; the sūtra itself was not located. The Humane Kings passage (unit 88) matches Amoghavajra's version T246 (with 大品等), not Kumārajīva's T245.

Sources not located in this wording: the Great Collection Sūtra line on the four great vows (unit 14; similar wording in T400 and elsewhere); the Viśeṣacintā line "Knowing awakening is called Buddha …" (unit 65). T586 has 知法名為佛，知離名為法，知無名為僧 "knowing the Dharma is called Buddha"; Zhili's 知覺 may be a variant. "True words seem contrary" (正言似反) is from the Laozi, chapter 78.

Vimalakīrti: "The five aggregates of grasping penetrated through, empty and without anything" (unit 40) is attributed to the Vimalakīrti. "Wisdom with expedients" / "expedients with wisdom" (unit 77) refers to the Vimalakīrti's "Inquiring after the Illness" chapter, which the site renders "skillful means together with wisdom"; this text keeps "expedient(s)" for 方便.

Four movements and conveying (units 23–27): 運 is "conveying" when glossing "vehicle" (乘是運義) and "movement" for the four movements of thought (as in the Profound Meaning); the Chinese plays on both senses. In unit 25, 若觀四觀 is read as 若觀四運 "contemplates the four [movements]."

Textual errors Zhili notes: in unit 41 he takes the Profound Meaning's "the same as the six destinies" (同六道) as a scribal error; in unit 90 he says printed copies misread the doubled 云云 as 以.

Terms: 理造／事造 creation in principle / in phenomena (following the Guanyin notes); 一體三寶 the Three Jewels of a single essence; 帝慧王 emperor-wisdom-king; 五即 the five identities; 三無為 the unconditioned of cessation through discrimination / of cessation not through discrimination / of space.
