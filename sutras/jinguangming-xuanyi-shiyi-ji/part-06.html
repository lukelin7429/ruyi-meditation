Essence, Cardinal Doctrine, Function, and the Classification of the Teaching

The last fascicle of Zhili's Gleanings finishes the section Explaining the Name and then comments on the remaining four of the five profound meanings in the Profound Meaning. Zhili first shows how each of the ten sets of three dharmas gathers in the six stages, and why gathering in Dharma-gates, teachings, and stages matches emperor, wisdom, and king. He closes the name section with a personal note defending the contemplation-of-mind passages, which Gushan Zhiyuan had called later additions. On the essence, he explains why the dharma-nature that the Buddha "roams in" must be the full, not-mere middle, and how the glosses of essence as rites, bottom, and penetration match gold, light, and brightness. On the cardinal doctrine, he argues that only the buddha-fruit, merged with the dharma-nature, can be both permanent and impermanent, and he reconciles the Profound Meaning with the Textual Commentary on the response-body. On the function, he explains how repentance, praise, and the wisdom of emptiness adorn the wisdom of the fruit. In the classification of the teaching, he answers the older view that the sūtra is "partial and indeterminate," the view that it belongs with the Lotus, and Paramārtha's dating, and he explains why a sūtra classified as the shared teaching can still be explained throughout as perfect. Yijing's later translation is cited twice as confirmation.

Gathering In the Stages

Chinese Source

◎二、明十法攝位。謂下攝於上、上攝於下、中攝上下，故一一三法皆攝六位。三障覆六位者，斯由三障從迷說、六即從解說耳。若即三障之非道，通達三障之佛道，即此佛道須論六位。此之六位攝一切位，理即攝博地位，名字攝一切學習位，觀行攝五品位，相似攝十信位，分真攝四十一位，究竟攝妙覺位。乃至三德等者，解於三障有六即位，解九三法各論六即。然性德中十種三法，皆須即障照之令顯，但約所顯而明十番六即之位。言三德既備攝等者，合例三道論於類攝，謂法身有三身及一切妙境，般若有三智及一切辯慧，解脫有三脫及一切神變。既就三德論於六位，須論六位皆即三德所攝之法，故云六位寧不備收。其間八三各各備攝，及八六位位位備收，準例可解。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying that the ten dharmas gather in the stages. That is, the lower gathers in the higher, the higher gathers in the lower, and the middle gathers in both higher and lower; hence each set of three dharmas gathers in all six stages. "The three obstructions obstruct and cover the six stages": this is because the three obstructions are spoken of from delusion, and the six identities from understanding. If one "penetrates" the non-path of the three obstructions as the buddha-path of the three obstructions, then in this buddha-path one must discuss the six stages. These six stages gather in all stages: identity in principle gathers in the stage of ordinary beings of the broad earth; identity in name gathers in all stages of study and practice; identity in contemplative practice gathers in the stages of the five grades; identity in resemblance gathers in the stages of the ten faiths; identity in a portion of the truth gathers in the forty-one stages; ultimate identity gathers in the stage of wondrous awakening. "And so on to the three virtues": having explained that the three obstructions have the stages of the six identities, the nine [other] sets of three dharmas are each explained as having six identities. Yet within inherent virtue, each of the ten sets of three dharmas must be illuminated right in the obstructions to be revealed; only in terms of what is revealed are the stages of the six identities clarified in ten sets. "Since the three virtues are fully gathered in" and so on: by analogy with the three paths, it should discuss gathering in by kind: the Dharma-body has the three bodies and all wondrous objects; prajñā has the three wisdoms and all eloquence and wisdom; liberation has the three liberations and all spiritual transformations. Since the six stages are discussed in terms of the three virtues, it must be discussed that each of the six stages is [complete with] the dharmas gathered in by the three virtues; hence it says, "how could the six stages not be fully taken in?" The eight sets of three in between, each fully gathering in, and the six stages of those eight, each stage fully taking in, can be understood by analogy.

二、明攝三意。以三番攝法合帝慧王者，前之三番即三十重論帝慧王；今乃攝褻法門十重，佛所師故，結歸於帝，合貴極義。攝教十重，鑑機說故，結歸於慧，合雄略義。攝位十重，皆趣果故，結歸於王，合朝會義。又十種法門，一一高廣不論優劣，乃是橫攝；六位皆即自下升高，故當竪攝；教詮法門復論六位，故當橫竪雙攝之義。如斯統攝，題稱帝王，諒無慚德。

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, clarifying the intent of the gathering in. "Making three rounds of gathering in to match emperor, wisdom, and king": the preceding three rounds are thirty layers discussing emperor-wisdom-king. Now, the ten layers of gathering in Dharma-gates, because [the Dharma] is what the buddhas take as teacher, are concluded under "emperor," matching the meaning of the noblest. The ten layers of gathering in the teachings, because [the teachings] are taught by discerning capacities, are concluded under "wisdom," matching the meaning of heroic strategy. The ten layers of gathering in the stages, because all advance to the fruit, are concluded under "king," matching the meaning of coming to court. Further, the ten kinds of Dharma-gate are each high and broad without discussion of superior and inferior; this is horizontal gathering in. The six stages, being identity, rise from low to high; hence they correspond to vertical gathering in. The teachings express the Dharma-gates and further discuss the six stages; hence they correspond to the meaning of gathering in both horizontally and vertically. Governing and gathering in like this, the title is called emperor-king; truly it has nothing to be ashamed of in its virtue.

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, merging it with the Golden Light to show the stages. Since emperor-wisdom-king and Golden Light are both names of the dharma-nature itself, the title "emperor-king" is set up again in order to make evident the meaning of the king of sūtras as governing and gathering in. Now, wishing practitioners to know that these two names alike express the dharma-nature, it therefore merges [them] with gold and the rest to clarify the stages. The text and meaning of the five stages can be known as before; only the words on identity in name are somewhat hard to understand. "The mind has only the name": [that is,] the name Golden Light. Beginners have only this name in a single thought of mind, and not yet this contemplation; hence it says, "the Golden Light in name." Second, concluding the intent. The intent lies in contemplating the mind, so that the wisdom from hearing is complete. If so, then one reaches the jewel mountain of the dharma-nature without taking half a step. Yet the purport of the various accounts of this contemplative practice is especially deep; none but those who have reached in mind the innermost chamber of the Mountain school may lightly discuss it. I have studied this meaning closely for many years; how would I dare to bend principle to follow feelings, affirming this and denying that? But the path of object and contemplation is plain and has its destination; moreover the corpora agree with one another, which proves that this text has not been lost. Alas! The patriarchs have passed away, but in every age there are wise and worthy ones. Let those who will know me by the contemplation of mind, and those who will condemn me by the contemplation of mind, not hold back.

Clarifying the Essence

Chinese Source

大章第二、辨體。前章釋名，總於三法含體、宗、用。利根之者，即達能詮忘情得體自成宗用；其鈍根人，以名具三、體混在內，心慮難遣、妙體莫彰，故次釋名別譚體等，俾於法性絕念而游。即於此典金光明中，而得見我釋迦牟尼。文先分二：初、標列。列於辨體三章門也。問：本為忘名故別示體，今還釋名，與前何異？又但釋名引證料簡，何意不立辨體章門？答：夫忘名者非謂默然，若善釋名，其名自泯。無離文字說解脫相，文字性離即是解脫。今所釋者但釋體名，前章總三與此永異。又體本寂滅，寄名詮之，故但釋名即當辨體，總持無字、字顯總持，斯之謂也。既釋體名，又引經論證成體義，復約說證料簡於體，辨體之旨曲盡其妙，那言不立辨體章門？

Ru-Yi Translation

Main section two: Clarifying the Essence. The preceding section, Explaining the Name, with its three dharmas in general, contained essence, cardinal doctrine, and function. Those of sharp faculties penetrate what expresses, forget feelings, and attain the essence, and cardinal doctrine and function are accomplished of themselves. For those of dull faculties, because the name includes the three and the essence is mixed within it, the mind's deliberations are hard to dispel and the wondrous essence is not manifest; hence after Explaining the Name it discusses the essence and the rest separately, so that they may roam in the dharma-nature with thought cut off, and "then within this scripture, the Golden Light, get to see me, Śākyamuni." The text is first divided into two: first, marking and listing — listing the three section headings of Clarifying the Essence. Question: It was originally in order to forget the name that the essence was shown separately; now it again explains the name — how does this differ from before? Further, it only explains the name, cites proof, and sorts and sifts; why does it not set up a section heading "clarifying the essence"? Answer: Forgetting the name does not mean being silent; if one explains the name well, the name dissolves of itself. "There is no speaking of the mark of liberation apart from written words; the nature of written words is detachment, and this is liberation." What is explained now is only the name "essence," which is entirely different from the three in general in the previous section. Further, the essence is originally quiescent and extinct and is expressed by lodging it in a name; hence just explaining the name is itself clarifying the essence. "Total retention has no written words; written words reveal total retention" — this is what is meant. Having explained the name "essence," it further cites sūtras and treatises to prove the meaning of essence, and again sorts and sifts the essence in terms of teaching and realization; the purport of clarifying the essence is exhausted in all its subtlety. How can one say that it does not set up a section heading for clarifying the essence?

初、法。佛以種智為能游入，是經之宗。深廣法性而為所游及為智本，即是經體。若偏真法性，體類太虛，非智之本。中道法性，體是本覺，能為始覺種智之根。今經以果而為宗要，果智乃是究竟始覺，始本不二、不二而二，體為宗本。若不然者，何名但是佛游入耶？功德眾行是經之用，所嚴所趣即是體也。滅惡為功、生善為德，功德乃是力用異名。以此力用莊嚴法身，懺悔讚歎空智導成，此乃以行而為力用。

Ru-Yi Translation

First, the dharma. The Buddha, with the wisdom knowing all modes as what roams and enters, is the cardinal doctrine of the sūtra. The deep and broad dharma-nature, as what is roamed in and as the root of wisdom, is the essence of the sūtra. If it were the dharma-nature of the one-sided real, its essence would be like empty space, not the root of wisdom. The dharma-nature of the middle way is in essence original awakening and can be the root of the wisdom knowing all modes of initial awakening. This sūtra takes the fruit as its cardinal essential, and the wisdom of the fruit is ultimate initial awakening. Initial and original are nondual; nondual yet two, the essence is the root of the cardinal doctrine. Otherwise, how could it be called "only what the Buddha roams in and enters"? "Merits" and "practices" are the function of the sūtra; what they "adorn" and "converge on" is the essence. Extinguishing evil is merit (功) and producing good is virtue (德); merit and virtue are different names for power-in-action. With this power-in-action one adorns the Dharma-body: repentance and praise, guided to accomplishment by the wisdom of emptiness — this is taking practice as power-in-action.

問：宗取佛果，用須佛力。功德屬佛，為用可爾；行在眾生，那為經用？答：眾生之心非佛威力，豈能立行？故《般舟》見佛論其三力：一佛威力、二三昧力、三是行者本功德力。若非感應，無一善生。故《起信》云「所言用大者，謂能生一切世間及出世間善因果故。」行是經用，其義昭然。皆遍十界，故云無量及種種也。言說問答能詮辨邊，即是經名及教相也。其所詮辨，豈非經體？名教二種俱是能詮，自行稟得故曰經名，為他詮辨乃曰教相。自他雖異，俱詮法性。問：名是經題，豈有問答詮辨等耶？答：一經始終，皆能詮名含幾問答，但以題目是經總名，故解題目稱為釋名，那謂經名不曾問答？

Question: The cardinal doctrine takes the buddha-fruit, so the function must be the Buddha's power. Merit belongs to the Buddha, and it may be the function; but practice lies with beings — how can it be the function of the sūtra? Answer: Without the Buddha's majestic power, how could the minds of beings establish practice? Hence the Pratyutpanna, on seeing the Buddha, discusses three powers: first, the Buddha's majestic power; second, the power of samādhi; third, the practitioner's own power of merit. Were there no appeal and response, not a single good would arise. Hence the Awakening of Faith says, "The greatness of function means that it can produce all the good causes and effects of the mundane and the supramundane." That practice is the function of the sūtra is clear. All extend through the ten realms; hence it says "immeasurable" and "all kinds." "Words, explanations, questions, and answers" are on the side of what expresses and sets forth, which is the name of the sūtra and the characteristics of the teaching. What they express and set forth — is it not the essence of the sūtra? Name and teaching are both what expresses: in terms of one's own practice and receiving, it is called the name of the sūtra; in setting forth for others, it is called the characteristics of the teaching. Though self and other differ, both express the dharma-nature. Question: The name is the sūtra's title; how could it have questions, answers, setting forth, and so on? Answer: The whole sūtra from beginning to end is the expressing name and contains many questions and answers; only because the title is the general name of the sūtra is the explanation of the title called Explaining the Name. How can one say that the sūtra's name never asks or answers?

In the trace gate it says, "the marks of the world are constantly abiding; having known this at the seat of enlightenment"; in the origin gate it says, "the Tathāgata sees with total clarity the mark of the three realms … nothing that is thus and nothing that is otherwise." All these are the essence of the middle way of the permanently abiding Dharma-body that is realized, and the place of treasure and the jewel in the topknot are used as similes for it. Since the Dharma-body realized is permanently abiding, how could the reward-wisdom that realizes, and the responsive function it lets down, be impermanent? The sūtra raises the dust of worlds as the number of eons since the original attainment in the past; as for the future, the sūtra text plainly says, "dwelling always, never extinguished." Is this not that this master, with the number of dust-of-worlds eons since attaining buddhahood in the distant past, screens the three bodies that the place of treasure illustrates?

When the Great Sūtra says, "From cream comes curdled butter; [this] is like the Vaipulya coming from the sūtras," this precisely names the teaching of the third period Vaipulya, which addresses the capacities of the three vehicles and the four teachings. When the Contemplation of Samantabhadra is called Vaipulya, this directly names perfect principle; it is not the designation of the corpus of the third period's teaching addressing all capacities. As for the Vaipulya text first cited here: lest people think it the same as the Contemplation of Samantabhadra and the like, which take the name from principle, it cites the text on the three vehicles repenting to determine that this name is precisely from the teaching-corpus; hence it concludes, "its meaning is beyond doubt."

Ru-Yi Translation

Second, determining it by meaning. First, clarifying that the Vaipulya corpus was never confined. Because we now establish that the Vaipulya teaching is shared by the three vehicles, [the challenger] cites the new version's text on there being no different vehicle, challenging that our establishing it as shared by the three does not hold; hence it says, "this harms the meaning of 'shared.'" From "But the Vaipulya" on, it resolves the challenge. "In the dharma-realm there is no different vehicle": both the distinct and the perfect teachings take the dharma-realm as their destination; therefore they can of themselves be called "no different vehicle." Since the full word of the Vaipulya extends to both these teachings, what hindrance is there? Second, clarifying that the text listing the assembly perhaps had not yet come. At the beginning of the sūtra no assembly listening together is listed, and others doubted that our master's classifying it as the third, the Vaipulya, was proper; therefore the Great Master pointed out that in India its text was still much longer, not stopping at the text of Dharmakṣema's four fascicles and Paramārtha's seven scrolls. When, in the Tang, Yijing retranslated this sūtra, naming it the Golden Light Sūtra of the Most Victorious King, it indeed had a listing of the assembly. First, the five flavors: since the text of the Nirvāṇa Sūtra likens the Vaipulya to curdled butter, and this sūtra clearly has the text of the Vaipulya and also its meaning, it must be included in the third flavor. Next, the four treasuries, namely the śrāvaka treasury, the bodhisattva treasury, the miscellaneous treasury, and the buddha treasury: these name collections of dharmas by persons. The name śrāvaka treasury intends to make evident the purely small; the bodhisattva and buddha treasuries express only the great; the miscellaneous treasury includes both great and small. Since this sūtra permits the three vehicles to repent together, it can contain and gather in the dharmas of śrāvakas, bodhisattvas, and buddhas; hence it belongs to the miscellaneous treasury. Last, the four teachings: the names of the five flavors and the four treasuries are shared with others, but classifying sūtras by the four teachings is used only by our school. In this sūtra the essence, being illusory, reveals the emptiness of the middle; it is not at all the clumsy crossing of the Tripiṭaka [teaching]'s analysis of dharmas. The three vehicles repenting together is moreover not the unshared dharma of the distinct and perfect; it is precisely the dharma of no arising and ceasing received in common by the three vehicles of the shared teaching. Bodhisattvas of sharp faculties know the permanent and penetrate the nature; hence it is called the shared teaching accompanied by the distinct and clarifying the perfect.
