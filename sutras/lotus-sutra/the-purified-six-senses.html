The Purified Six Senses (I)

法師功德品第十九・眼耳鼻

The Buddha now describes, faculty by faculty, what happens to anyone who upholds, reads, recites, explains, or copies this sūtra: eight hundred merits of the eye, twelve hundred of the ear, eight hundred of the nose, twelve hundred of the tongue, eight hundred of the body, and twelve hundred of the mind — not new organs, but the ordinary eyes, ears, and nose a person was born with, purified until they can see, hear, and smell the entire three-thousandfold universe without confusion. This first half covers the eye, ear, and nose.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
The Buddha's opening declaration to Constantly-Diligent Bodhisattva that whoever upholds this sūtra gains eight hundred merits of the eye, twelve hundred of the ear, and eight hundred of the nose (with the tongue, body, and mind to follow in the next part); the eye that sees every being and every karmic cause and result across the whole three-thousand-great-thousandfold world, still called only the "eye given by one's own parents"; the ear that hears every sound within that same world, from the cries of hell to the speech of Brahmā-heaven gods, without its own faculty ever being harmed; and the nose that distinguishes the fragrance of any flower, any being, or any of the gods' own dwellings, near or far.
Why it matters
Zhiyi reads this whole chapter as describing what he calls the "interpenetration" of the six senses (根互用) — each purified faculty gaining, as a mark of its purification, the functions of every other, so that the nose in this chapter's own account already sees, hears, and touches, not only smells. The eight-hundred and twelve-hundred figures are not a ranking of some senses as inferior; Zhiyi reads them as the same underlying six thousand merits, expressed either in full or in a contracted form depending on how each sense naturally faces its own field.

1. The Purified Eye

Chinese Source

爾時佛告常精進菩薩摩訶薩：「若善男子、善女人，受持是法華經，若讀、若誦、若解說、若書寫，是人當得八百眼功德、千二百耳功德、八百鼻功德、千二百舌功德、八百身功德、千二百意功德，以是功德、莊嚴六根，皆令清淨。是善男子、善女人，父母所生清淨肉眼，見於三千大千世界內外所有山林河海，下至阿鼻地獄，上至有頂，亦見其中一切眾生，及業因緣、果報生處，悉見悉知。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Buddha said to Constantly-Diligent Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva: "Should a good son or good daughter uphold this Lotus Sūtra, whether reading it, reciting it, explaining it, or copying it, that person shall attain eight hundred merits of the eye, twelve hundred merits of the ear, eight hundred merits of the nose, twelve hundred merits of the tongue, eight hundred merits of the body, and twelve hundred merits of the mind — and by these merits, all six faculties shall be adorned, and made entirely pure. This good son or good daughter, with the pure fleshly eye given by their own father and mother, shall see every mountain, forest, river, and sea within and beyond the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world, down to the depths of Avīci hell, and up to the highest heaven of form, and shall see, too, every being within it, together with the causes and conditions of their karma, and the places where its results are born — seeing all of it, and knowing all of it."

Chinese Source

爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「若於大眾中，　以無所畏心、 說是法華經，　汝聽其功德。 是人得八百、　功德殊勝眼， 以是莊嚴故，　其目甚清淨。 父母所生眼，　悉見三千界， 內外彌樓山，　須彌及鐵圍， 并諸餘山林、　大海江河水， 下至阿鼻獄，　上至有頂處， 其中諸眾生，　一切皆悉見。 雖未得天眼，　肉眼力如是。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to proclaim this meaning once more, spoke in verse: "If, before a great assembly, with a heart free of any fear, one teaches this Lotus Sūtra — listen now to the merit this brings. That person attains eight hundred merits, an eye of the finest quality, and by this very adornment, their sight becomes entirely pure. With the eye given by their own father and mother, they see the whole three-thousandfold world, Mount Meru and the encircling iron mountains within and beyond it, and every other mountain and forest, every great sea, river, and stream, down to the depths of the Avīci hell, up to the highest heaven of form — every being within it, all of it entirely seen. Though they have not yet attained the heavenly eye, such is the power of the fleshly eye alone."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi makes a point of the sūtra's own insistence that this remains the "fleshly eye given by one's own parents" (父母所生肉眼), the ordinary human eye a practitioner was born with, rather than the heavenly eye attained through meditative accomplishment or the wisdom eye of a bodhisattva's insight. Yet its actual range, on his reading, already functions as all five classical kinds of eye at once: seeing the whole universe is the heavenly eye's own reach; seeing beings' karmic causes and results is the Dharma eye's function; the purity of the seeing itself is the wisdom eye's mark; and the fact that all of this is seen in a single, undivided act reflects the buddha eye's own totality — while still, in name, being called nothing more than the eye a person's own mother and father gave them. He treats this as the chapter's real subject: not a superhuman replacement organ, but the practitioner's ordinary body, functioning at its true, unobstructed capacity once the sūtra's purification has taken hold.

2. The Purified Ear

Chinese Source

「復次，常精進！若善男子、善女人，受持此經，若讀、若誦、若解說、若書寫，得千二百耳功德。以是清淨耳，聞三千大千世界，下至阿鼻地獄，上至有頂，其中內外種種語言音聲——象聲、馬聲、牛聲、車聲，啼哭聲、愁歎聲，螺聲、鼓聲、鍾聲、鈴聲，笑聲、語聲，男聲、女聲、童子聲、童女聲，法聲、非法聲，苦聲、樂聲，凡夫聲、聖人聲，喜聲、不喜聲，天聲、龍聲、夜叉聲、乾闥婆聲、阿修羅聲、迦樓羅聲、緊那羅聲、摩睺羅伽聲，火聲、水聲、風聲，地獄聲、畜生聲、餓鬼聲，比丘聲、比丘尼聲，聲聞聲、辟支佛聲，菩薩聲、佛聲——以要言之，三千大千世界中一切內外所有諸聲，雖未得天耳，以父母所生清淨常耳，皆悉聞知，如是分別種種音聲而不壞耳根。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Constantly-Diligent! Should a good son or good daughter uphold this sūtra, whether reading it, reciting it, explaining it, or copying it, they shall attain twelve hundred merits of the ear. With this pure ear, they shall hear, throughout the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world, down to the depths of Avīci hell and up to the highest heaven of form, every kind of speech and sound within and beyond it — the sound of elephants, the sound of horses, of cattle, of carts, sounds of weeping and wailing, sounds of grief and sighing, the sound of conch shells, of drums, of bells, of hand bells, sounds of laughter and speech, the voices of men and women, of boys and girls, sounds of the Dharma and sounds contrary to it, sounds of suffering and of pleasure, the voices of ordinary people and of the noble, sounds pleasing and displeasing, the sounds of gods, of nāgas, of yakṣas, of gandharvas, of asuras, of garuḍas, of kiṃnaras, of mahoragas, the sound of fire, of water, of wind, the sounds of hell, of animals, of hungry ghosts, the voices of monks and of nuns, of śrāvakas and of pratyekabuddhas, of bodhisattvas and of buddhas — in brief, every sound whatsoever, within and beyond the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world. Though they have not yet attained the heavenly ear, with the pure, ordinary ear given by their own father and mother, they shall hear and know every one of these, distinguishing every kind of sound in this way, without their own ear faculty ever being harmed by it."

Chinese Source

爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「父母所生耳，　清淨無濁穢， 以此常耳聞，　三千世界聲。 象馬車牛聲，　鍾鈴螺鼓聲， 琴瑟箜篌聲，　簫笛之音聲， 清淨好歌聲，　聽之而不著， 無數種人聲，　聞悉能解了。 又聞諸天聲，　微妙之歌音， 及聞男女聲，　童子童女聲。 山川嶮谷中，　迦陵頻伽聲， 命命等諸鳥，　悉聞其音聲。 地獄眾苦痛，　種種楚毒聲， 餓鬼飢渴逼，　求索飲食聲， 諸阿修羅等，　居在大海邊， 自共語言時，　出于大音聲。 如是說法者，　安住於此間， 遙聞是眾聲，　而不壞耳根。 十方世界中，　禽獸鳴相呼， 其說法之人，　於此悉聞之。 其諸梵天上，　光音及遍淨， 乃至有頂天，　言語之音聲， 法師住於此，　悉皆得聞之。 一切比丘眾，　及諸比丘尼， 若讀誦經典，　若為他人說， 法師住於此，　悉皆得聞之。 復有諸菩薩，　讀誦於經法， 若為他人說，　撰集解其義， 如是諸音聲，　悉皆得聞之。 諸佛大聖尊，　教化眾生者， 於諸大會中，　演說微妙法， 持此法華者，　悉皆得聞之。 三千大千界，　內外諸音聲， 下至阿鼻獄，　上至有頂天， 皆聞其音聲，　而不壞耳根， 其耳聰利故，　悉能分別知。 持是法花者，　雖未得天耳， 但用所生耳，　功德已如是。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to proclaim this meaning once more, spoke in verse: "The ear given by one's own father and mother, pure and free of any defilement, with this ordinary ear hears every sound of the three-thousandfold world. The sound of elephants, horses, carts, and cattle, of bells, hand bells, conches, and drums, of zithers, lutes, and harps, of flutes and pipes, of fine and pure singing — hearing all this without attachment, they understand, upon hearing it, countless kinds of human voices. They hear, too, the voices of the gods, their subtle and wondrous song, and hear the voices of men and women, of boys and of girls. Amid the mountains, rivers, and steep ravines, the voice of the kalaviṅka bird, and of the jīvañjīva and every other bird — all of these sounds they hear. The countless cries of suffering in hell, every kind of torment's own sound, the hungry ghosts, pressed by hunger and thirst, crying out in search of food and drink, and the asuras, dwelling at the great ocean's edge, when they speak among themselves, sending forth a mighty sound — the teacher of the Dharma, dwelling calmly in this place, hears all these sounds from far away, yet the ear faculty itself is never harmed. In the worlds of the ten directions, birds and beasts calling out to one another — the teacher of the Dharma, in this very place, hears every one of them. In the Brahma heavens above, in the heaven of light and sound, in the heaven of universal purity, up to the very highest heaven — every word spoken there, the teacher of the Dharma, dwelling here, hears in full. Every assembly of monks, and every assembly of nuns, whether reading and reciting the scriptures, or teaching them to others — the teacher of the Dharma, dwelling here, hears them in full. There are bodhisattvas, too, reading and reciting the scriptures and their teachings, or teaching them to others, compiling and explaining their meaning — sounds such as these, too, they hear in full. Every buddha, the great and sagely honored ones, teaching and transforming beings, expounding the subtle and wondrous Dharma in every great assembly — whoever upholds this Lotus Sūtra hears all of it in full. Every sound within and beyond the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world, down to the depths of the Avīci hell, up to the very highest heaven — they hear every one of these sounds, yet their ear faculty is never harmed; because their hearing is so sharp and keen, they are able to distinguish and know every one of them. Whoever upholds this Lotus Sūtra, though they have not yet attained the heavenly ear, using only the ordinary ear they were born with, already possesses merit such as this."

Reading note

The catalog of sounds is worth reading in full rather than skimmed — it is deliberately total, running from the cries of hell and the hungry ghosts' desperate begging through ordinary human speech and music up to the speech of Brahmā-heaven gods, and its inclusion of "sounds pleasing and displeasing" and "sounds contrary to the Dharma" alongside the Dharma's own teaching is not incidental. Zhiyi's own gloss for this faculty's fifth and highest function — hearing all of this at once, without error, is itself the "buddha ear" — turns on precisely this point: the purified ear does not filter out suffering or discord to hear only what is pleasant, but hears everything, exactly as it is, without that hearing itself being disturbed or harmed by what it takes in.

3. The Purified Nose

Chinese Source

「復次，常精進！若善男子、善女人，受持是經，若讀、若誦、若解說、若書寫，成就八百鼻功德。以是清淨鼻根，聞於三千大千世界上下內外種種諸香——須曼那華香、闍提華香、末利華香、瞻蔔華香、波羅羅華香，赤蓮華香、青蓮華香、白蓮華香，華樹香、菓樹香，栴檀香、沈水香、多摩羅跋香、多伽羅香，及千萬種和香，若末、若丸、若塗香——持是經者，於此間住，悉能分別。又復別知眾生之香——象香、馬香、牛羊等香，男香、女香、童子香、童女香，及草木叢林香——若近、若遠、所有諸香，悉皆得聞，分別不錯。持是經者，雖住於此，亦聞天上諸天之香——波利質多羅、拘鞞陀羅樹香，及曼陀羅華香、摩訶曼陀羅華香、曼殊沙華香、摩訶曼殊沙華香、栴檀、沈水、種種末香，諸雜華香——如是等天香和合所出之香，無不聞知。又聞諸天身香——釋提桓因在勝殿上，五欲娛樂嬉戲時香；若在妙法堂上，為忉利諸天說法時香；若於諸園遊戲時香；及餘天等男女身香，皆悉遙聞。如是展轉乃至梵世，上至有頂諸天身香，亦皆聞之，并聞諸天所燒之香。及聲聞香、辟支佛香、菩薩香、諸佛身香，亦皆遙聞，知其所在。雖聞此香，然於鼻根不壞不錯，若欲分別為他人說，憶念不謬。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Constantly-Diligent! Should a good son or good daughter uphold this sūtra, whether reading it, reciting it, explaining it, or copying it, they shall accomplish eight hundred merits of the nose. With this pure nose faculty, they shall smell, above and below and within and beyond the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world, every kind of fragrance — the fragrance of the sumanā flower, of the jasmine flower, of the mallikā flower, of the campaka flower, of the pāṭala flower, of the red lotus, the blue lotus, and the white lotus, the fragrance of flowering trees and of fruit trees, of sandalwood, of aloeswood, of tamāla leaf, of tagara, and of a thousand myriad kinds of blended incense, whether powdered, rolled into pellets, or made into anointing paste — one who upholds this sūtra, dwelling in this very place, is able to distinguish every one of them. They shall know, further, the particular fragrance of every being — the fragrance of elephants, of horses, of cattle and sheep, of men, of women, of boys, of girls, and the fragrance of grasses, trees, and thickets — whether near or far, every one of these fragrances is smelled, and distinguished without error. One who upholds this sūtra, though dwelling here, shall also smell the fragrances of the heavens above — the fragrance of the pārijāta and kovidāra trees, and of the mandārava flower, the great mandārava flower, the mañjūṣaka flower, and the great mañjūṣaka flower, of sandalwood, of aloeswood, of every kind of powdered incense, and of every mingled flower — every fragrance that arises from the blending of such heavenly scents as these, none of it is left unsmelled or unknown. They shall smell, further, the very fragrance of the gods' own bodies — the fragrance of Śakra, lord of the gods, as he takes his pleasure amid the five desires in his own excellent palace; the fragrance that arises as he teaches the Trāyastriṃśa gods in the Hall of the Wondrous Dharma; the fragrance that arises as he wanders and takes his ease in the various gardens; and the fragrance of the bodies of every other god and goddess besides, all smelled from far away. In this way, extending upward even to the Brahma heavens, up to the very highest heaven of form, the fragrance of the gods' own bodies dwelling there is smelled as well, together with the incense the gods themselves burn. And the fragrance of śrāvakas, of pratyekabuddhas, of bodhisattvas, and of the very bodies of buddhas — all of this, too, is smelled from far away, and its location known. Yet though such fragrances as these are smelled, the nose faculty itself is never harmed or confused by them, and should one wish to distinguish them and explain them to others, the memory of them is never mistaken."

Chinese Source

爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「是人鼻清淨，　於此世界中， 若香若臭物，　種種悉聞知。 須曼那闍提、　多摩羅栴檀、 沈水及桂香，　種種華菓香， 及知眾生香，　男子女人香， 說法者遠住，　聞香知所在。 大勢轉輪王，　小轉輪及子， 群臣諸宮人，　聞香知所在。 身所著珍寶，　及地中寶藏， 轉輪王寶女，　聞香知所在。 諸人嚴身具，　衣服及瓔珞， 種種所塗香，　聞香知其身。 諸天若行坐，　遊戲及神變， 持是法華者，　聞香悉能知。 諸樹華菓實，　及酥油香氣， 持經者住此，　悉知其所在。 諸山深嶮處，　栴檀樹花敷， 眾生在中者，　聞香皆能知。 鐵圍山大海，　地中諸眾生， 持經者聞香，　悉知其所在。 阿修羅男女，　及其諸眷屬， 鬪諍遊戲時，　聞香皆能知。 曠野險隘處，　師子象虎狼， 野牛水牛等，　聞香知所在。 若有懷妊者，　未辯其男女， 無根及非人，　聞香悉能知。 以聞香力故，　知其初懷妊， 成就不成就，　安樂產福子。 以聞香力故，　知男女所念， 染欲癡恚心，　亦知修善者。 地中眾伏藏，　金銀諸珍寶， 銅器之所盛，　聞香悉能知。 種種諸瓔珞，　無能識其價， 聞香知貴賤，　出處及所在。 天上諸華等，　曼陀曼殊沙、 波利質多樹，　聞香悉能知。 天上諸宮殿，　上中下差別， 眾寶花莊嚴，　聞香悉能知。 天園林勝殿，　諸觀妙法堂， 在中而娛樂，　聞香悉能知。 諸天若聽法，　或受五欲時， 來往行坐臥，　聞香悉能知。 天女所著衣，　好華香莊嚴， 周旋遊戲時，　聞香悉能知。 如是展轉上，　乃至於梵世， 入禪出禪者，　聞香悉能知。 光音遍淨天，　乃至于有頂， 初生及退沒，　聞香悉能知。 諸比丘眾等，　於法常精進， 若坐若經行，　及讀誦經法， 或在林樹下，　專精而坐禪， 持經者聞香，　悉知其所在。 菩薩志堅固，　坐禪若讀誦， 或為人說法，　聞香悉能知。 在在方世尊，　一切所恭敬， 愍眾而說法，　聞香悉能知。 眾生在佛前，　聞經皆歡喜， 如法而修行，　聞香悉能知。 雖未得菩薩，　無漏法生鼻， 而是持經者，　先得此鼻相。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to proclaim this meaning once more, spoke in verse: "This person's nose is pure, and throughout this whole world, whether a thing is fragrant or foul, they smell and know every kind of it. The sumanā, the jasmine, the tamāla, the sandalwood, the aloeswood and cassia fragrance, every kind of flower and fruit fragrance, and the fragrance of every being too, of men and of women — though the teacher of the Dharma dwells far away, smelling the fragrance, they know exactly where it is. Mighty wheel-turning kings, lesser wheel-turning kings and their sons, ministers and palace attendants — smelling the fragrance, they know exactly where they are. Jewels worn upon the body, and treasures buried in the earth, the wheel-turning king's own treasure-consort — smelling the fragrance, they know exactly where they are. The ornaments people wear, their robes and their necklaces, and every kind of anointing scent — smelling the fragrance, they know the very body it comes from. Whether the gods walk or sit, at play or performing spiritual transformations, whoever upholds this Lotus Sūtra, smelling the fragrance, knows it all. Every tree's flowers and fruit, and the fragrance of ghee and oil — one who upholds this sūtra, dwelling here, knows exactly where each one is. In the deep and perilous places of every mountain, where sandalwood trees stand in bloom, the beings dwelling among them — smelling the fragrance, they know them all. Amid the iron ring-mountains, the great ocean, and the beings dwelling in the earth — one who upholds this sūtra, smelling the fragrance, knows exactly where they are. Asura men and women, and all their retinues, whether fighting or at play — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. In the wilderness, in perilous and narrow places, lions, elephants, tigers, wolves, wild oxen and water buffalo — smelling the fragrance, they know exactly where they are. Should there be a woman with child, whose son or daughter is not yet determined, or one without the faculty to bear, or a nonhuman being — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. By the power of this sense of smell, they know from the very moment of conception whether the child will come to term or not, and whether the birth will be at ease and blessed. By the power of this sense of smell, they know the very thoughts of men and women, hearts stained with desire, folly, or anger, and know, too, those who cultivate what is wholesome. Every hidden treasure buried in the earth, gold, silver, and every kind of jewel, held within vessels of bronze — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. Every kind of necklace, whose true worth no one else can tell — smelling the fragrance, they know its value, its origin, and its location. Every flower in the heavens above, the mandārava, the mañjūṣaka, the pārijāta tree — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. The palaces of the heavens above, in every rank, high, middling, and low, adorned with every kind of jeweled flower — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. The heavens' own gardens and groves, their finest palaces, their towers and the Hall of the Wondrous Dharma, and those who take their pleasure within them — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. Whether the gods are listening to the Dharma, or indulging in the five desires, coming and going, walking, sitting, or lying down — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. The robes the goddesses wear, adorned with fine flowers and fragrance, as they wander about at play — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. Extending upward in this way, even to the Brahma heavens, those entering and emerging from meditative absorption — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. The heaven of light and sound, the heaven of universal purity, up to the very highest heaven, beings there just born and just passing away — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. Every assembly of monks, constantly diligent in the Dharma, whether sitting or walking, reading and reciting the scriptures, or beneath a grove of trees, single-mindedly seated in meditation — one who upholds this sūtra, smelling the fragrance, knows exactly where they are. Bodhisattvas of firm resolve, seated in meditation or reading and reciting, or teaching the Dharma to others — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. Every World-Honored One, in every place, revered by all, teaching the Dharma out of pity for beings — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. Beings in the Buddha's own presence, hearing the sūtra and rejoicing, practicing exactly as the Dharma teaches — smelling the fragrance, they know it all. Though they have not yet attained the outflow-free nose born of a bodhisattva's own realization, one who upholds this sūtra already gains this very mark of the nose."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi treats the nose section as the clearest single demonstration, in this whole chapter, of what he calls the "interpenetration" of the purified six senses: reading the catalog closely, the nose here does far more than smell. It distinguishes a treasure's worth and origin, which Zhiyi calls the nose taking on the eye's own function; it locates a monk reading scripture aloud in a distant forest, the nose taking on the ear's function; it identifies fruit and clarified butter by their fragrance, which shades into a function more usually assigned to taste; it registers the eight sensations of entering and emerging from meditative absorption, a function of the body; and it reads directly the desire, delusion, or anger in another being's mind, a function ordinarily reserved for mental cognition alone. No single sense faculty, on this reading, is walled off from what the others perceive — each fully purified sense already carries the reach of all six.

On the numbers themselves — eight hundred for eye, nose, and body, twelve hundred for ear, tongue, and mind — Zhiyi's own account resists treating this as a hierarchy of stronger and weaker senses. His arithmetic derives twelve hundred from the ten Dharma-realms, each with its own ten suchnesses (a hundred), multiplied by the six sense-objects each faculty can register (six hundred), doubled again by the two adornments of concentration and wisdom (twelve hundred) — the same six thousand merits, he argues, underlie every one of the six faculties equally, and eight hundred is simply this same total's more contracted expression for a faculty whose ordinary field faces only one direction at a time, not a lesser endowment. Six senses, purified, are for Zhiyi six full expressions of a single capacity, not six different amounts of it.
