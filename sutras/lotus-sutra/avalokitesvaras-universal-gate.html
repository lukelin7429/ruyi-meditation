Avalokiteśvara's Universal Gate

觀世音菩薩普門品第二十五

Akṣayamati asks why this bodhisattva is named "Perceiver of the World's Sounds," and the Buddha answers with a chapter that has, on its own, become the most widely known part of this entire sūtra across East Asia: anyone who calls this bodhisattva's name in fire, flood, shipwreck, captivity, or mortal danger finds release, and this same bodhisattva takes on thirty-three different forms — a buddha, a king, a monk, a child — to teach whoever needs reaching, in whatever shape can actually reach them.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
Akṣayamati's question about why this bodhisattva bears the name Avalokiteśvara, and the Buddha's answer: release from fire, flood, shipwreck, execution, imprisonment, and bandits for anyone who calls his name with a single-minded heart; freedom from excessive desire, anger, and delusion for anyone who reveres him constantly; a worthy son or a fine daughter for a woman who bows to him seeking one; the thirty-three forms — from a buddha down to a child — this bodhisattva takes on to teach whoever needs him, in whatever shape actually reaches them; and the chapter's closing verse, among the most widely chanted passages in East Asian Buddhism on its own.
Why it matters
Zhiyi reads this chapter's two questions as deliberately paired: the first asks about the name Avalokiteśvara itself, and is answered with stories of compassion meeting suffering directly; the second asks how this bodhisattva actually teaches, and is answered with the catalog of thirty-three forms — one bodhisattva's compassion and his method of teaching, treated as two sides of a single "universal gate." This chapter's popular, devotional character is not a simplification added later; it is what the chapter itself was built to do.

1. Why "Perceiver of the World's Sounds"?

Chinese Source

爾時，無盡意菩薩即從座起，偏袒右肩，合掌向佛而作是言：「世尊！觀世音菩薩以何因緣名觀世音？」 佛告無盡意菩薩：「善男子！若有無量百千萬億眾生受諸苦惱，聞是觀世音菩薩，一心稱名，觀世音菩薩即時觀其音聲，皆得解脫。若有持是觀世音菩薩名者，設入大火，火不能燒，由是菩薩威神力故。若為大水所漂，稱其名號，即得淺處。若有百千萬億眾生，為求金、銀、琉璃、車璩、馬瑙、珊瑚、虎珀、真珠等寶，入於大海。假使黑風吹其船舫，飄墮羅剎鬼國，其中若有，乃至一人，稱觀世音菩薩名者，是諸人等皆得解脫羅剎之難。以是因緣，名觀世音。若復有人臨當被害，稱觀世音菩薩名者，彼所執刀杖尋段段壞，而得解脫。若三千大千國土滿中夜叉、羅剎欲來惱人，聞其稱觀世音菩薩名者，是諸惡鬼尚不能以惡眼視之，況復加害。設復有人，若有罪、若無罪，杻械、枷鎖檢繫其身，稱觀世音菩薩名者，皆悉斷壞，即得解脫。若三千大千國土滿中怨賊，有一商主將諸商人，齎持重寶經過嶮路。其中一人作是唱言：『諸善男子！勿得恐怖！汝等應當一心稱觀世音菩薩名號。是菩薩能以無畏施於眾生。汝等若稱名者，於此怨賊當得解脫。』眾商人聞，俱發聲言：『南無觀世音菩薩。』稱其名故，即得解脫。 「無盡意！觀世音菩薩摩訶薩威神之力，巍巍如是。若有眾生多於婬欲，常念恭敬觀世音菩薩，便得離欲。若多瞋恚，常念恭敬觀世音菩薩，便得離瞋。若多愚癡，常念恭敬觀世音菩薩，便得離癡。 「無盡意！觀世音菩薩有如是等大威神力，多所饒益。是故眾生常應心念。若有女人，設欲求男，禮拜供養觀世音菩薩，便生福德智慧之男；設欲求女，便生端正有相之女。宿殖德本，眾人愛敬。 「無盡意！觀世音菩薩有如是力。若有眾生恭敬禮拜觀世音菩薩，福不唐捐。是故眾生皆應受持觀世音菩薩名號。 「無盡意！若有人受持六十二億恒河沙菩薩名字，復盡形供養飲食、衣服、臥具、醫藥。於汝意云何？是善男子、善女人，功德多不？」 無盡意言：「甚多，世尊！」 佛言：「若復有人受持觀世音菩薩名號，乃至一時禮拜、供養。是二人福，正等無異，於百千萬億劫不可窮盡。無盡意！受持觀世音菩薩名號，得如是無量無邊福德之利。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Akṣayamati Bodhisattva rose from his seat, bared his right shoulder, joined his palms toward the Buddha, and said: "World-Honored One! By what cause and condition is Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva named 'Perceiver of the World's Sounds'?" The Buddha said to Akṣayamati Bodhisattva: "Good son! Should there be immeasurable hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of beings undergoing every kind of suffering and affliction, who hear of Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva and call his name with a single mind, Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva at once perceives the sound of their voices, and every one of them attains liberation. Should anyone hold to this Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's name, and should they enter a great fire, the fire shall not burn them, by this bodhisattva's own majestic and spiritual power. Should they be swept away by a great flood, and call his name, they shall at once reach shallow ground. Should there be hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of beings who enter the great ocean in search of gold, silver, lapis lazuli, giant clamshell, agate, coral, amber, pearls, and other treasures, and a black wind should strike their vessel, casting it adrift into the land of the rākṣasa demons, should there be, among them, even a single person who calls Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's name, every one of them shall be delivered from the peril of the rākṣasas. It is by this very cause and condition that he is named 'Perceiver of the World's Sounds.' Should there be, further, a person about to be put to death, who calls Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's name, the very sword or staff raised against them shall break at once into pieces, and they shall be set free. Should the whole three-thousand-great-thousandfold world be filled with yakṣas and rākṣasas wishing to come and torment people, on hearing them call Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's name, these evil spirits could not so much as look upon them with an evil eye, much less do them harm. Should there be, further, a person, whether guilty or innocent, bound in shackles and chains, and they call Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's name, these bonds shall break and shatter at once, and they shall be set free. Should the whole three-thousand-great-thousandfold world be filled with bandits and enemies, and a caravan leader, guiding merchants carrying precious treasures, pass through a perilous road, should one among them cry out: 'Good sirs, do not be afraid! You should call, with a single mind, upon Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's name — this bodhisattva is able to grant beings freedom from fear. If you call his name, you shall be delivered from these bandits' — should the merchants hear this and together cry out with one voice: 'Homage to Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva!' — by the very calling of his name, they shall at once be set free. "Akṣayamati! Such is the majesty of Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva's own spiritual power. Should there be beings much given to lustful desire, who constantly hold Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva in mind and revere him, they shall thereby become free of desire. Should they be much given to anger, and constantly hold him in mind and revere him, they shall become free of anger. Should they be much given to folly, and constantly hold him in mind and revere him, they shall become free of folly. "Akṣayamati! Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva possesses great spiritual power such as this, benefiting beings in many ways — for this reason, beings should constantly hold him in mind. Should there be a woman who wishes to have a son, and bows before and makes offerings to Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, she shall bear a son of merit, virtue, and wisdom; should she wish to have a daughter, she shall bear a daughter upright and fine in form, who has planted good roots in a former life, beloved and revered by all. "Akṣayamati! Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva possesses power such as this. Should any being revere and bow before Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, their good fortune shall not be spent in vain — for this reason, all beings should uphold Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's own name. "Akṣayamati! Should there be a person who upholds the names of sixty-two koṭis of Ganges-sands of bodhisattvas, and, further, for their entire life, makes offerings to them of food and drink, robes, bedding, and medicine — what do you think? Is the merit this good son or good daughter gains great?" Akṣayamati said: "Very great indeed, World-Honored One!" The Buddha said: "Should there be, further, a person who upholds Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's own name, even for the space of a single bow and a single offering, the good fortune of these two people would be exactly equal, with no difference between them, and could not be exhausted even across hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of kalpas. Akṣayamati! Upholding Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's own name brings a benefit of good fortune immeasurable and boundless, exactly as I have described."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi reads this whole first half of the chapter — the seven perils, the three poisons, and the prayer for a son or daughter — as answering the naming question directly: it is called "Perceiver of the World's Sounds" because it is compassion answering suffering wherever suffering actually calls out, not because of any abstract etymology. He glosses the prayer for a son or daughter as belonging to the same structure as everything before it in this chapter — an ordinary, urgent human want met on its own terms — while also noting elsewhere in his commentary that a wish for a son specifically reflects the social expectations of the audience this passage first addressed, not a claim that a son is worth more than a daughter in itself; the text's own language, describing the desired daughter as "upright... beloved and revered by all," is symmetrical with its language for the son.

2. The Thirty-Three Forms

Chinese Source

無盡意菩薩白佛言：「世尊！觀世音菩薩云何遊此娑婆世界？云何而為眾生說法？方便之力，其事云何？」 佛告無盡意菩薩：「善男子！若有國土眾生應以佛身得度者，觀世音菩薩即現佛身而為說法；應以辟支佛身得度者，即現辟支佛身而為說法；應以聲聞身得度者，即現聲聞身而為說法；應以梵王身得度者，即現梵王身而為說法；應以帝釋身得度者，即現帝釋身而為說法；應以自在天身得度者，即現自在天身而為說法；應以大自在天身得度者，即現大自在天身而為說法；應以天大將軍身得度者，即現天大將軍身而為說法；應以毘沙門身得度者，即現毘沙門身而為說法；應以小王身得度者，即現小王身而為說法；應以長者身得度者，即現長者身而為說法；應以居士身得度者，即現居士身而為說法；應以宰官身得度者，即現宰官身而為說法；應以婆羅門身得度者，即現婆羅門身而為說法；應以比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷身得度者，即現比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷身而為說法；應以長者、居士、宰官、婆羅門婦女身得度者，即現婦女身而為說法；應以童男、童女身得度者，即現童男、童女身而為說法；應以天、龍、夜叉、乾闥婆、阿修羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽、人非人等身得度者，即皆現之而為說法；應以執金剛身得度者，即現執金剛身而為說法。 「無盡意！是觀世音菩薩成就如是功德，以種種形，遊諸國土，度脫眾生。是故汝等應當一心供養觀世音菩薩。是觀世音菩薩摩訶薩於怖畏急難之中能施無畏，是故此娑婆世界皆號之為施無畏者。」 無盡意菩薩白佛言：「世尊！我今當供養觀世音菩薩。」即解頸眾寶珠、瓔珞，價直百千兩金，而以與之，作是言：「仁者！受此法施珍寶瓔珞。」時觀世音菩薩不肯受之。無盡意復白觀世音菩薩言：「仁者！愍我等故，受此瓔珞。」 爾時佛告觀世音菩薩：「當愍此無盡意菩薩及四眾，天、龍、夜叉、乾闥婆、阿修羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽、人非人等故，受是瓔珞。」 即時觀世音菩薩愍諸四眾，及於天、龍、人非人等，受其瓔珞，分作二分，一分奉釋迦牟尼佛，一分奉多寶佛塔。 「無盡意！觀世音菩薩有如是自在神力，遊於娑婆世界。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Akṣayamati Bodhisattva said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! How does Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva journey through this sahā world? How does he teach the Dharma to beings? What is the nature of his skillful means?" The Buddha said to Akṣayamati Bodhisattva: "Good son! Should a being in some land be one who is brought across by the form of a buddha, Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva at once manifests the form of a buddha and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a pratyekabuddha, he at once manifests the form of a pratyekabuddha and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a śrāvaka, he at once manifests the form of a śrāvaka and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of Brahmā, he at once manifests the form of Brahmā and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of Śakra, he at once manifests the form of Śakra and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of Īśvara, he at once manifests the form of Īśvara and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of Maheśvara, he at once manifests the form of Maheśvara and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a great heavenly general, he at once manifests the form of a great heavenly general and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of Vaiśravaṇa, he at once manifests the form of Vaiśravaṇa and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a lesser king, he at once manifests the form of a lesser king and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of an elder, he at once manifests the form of an elder and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a householder, he at once manifests the form of a householder and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a minister, he at once manifests the form of a minister and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a brahmin, he at once manifests the form of a brahmin and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a monk, a nun, a layman, or a laywoman, he at once manifests that very form and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of the wife of an elder, a householder, a minister, or a brahmin, he at once manifests a woman's form and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a boy or a girl, he at once manifests that very form and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a god, a nāga, a yakṣa, a gandharva, an asura, a garuḍa, a kiṃnara, a mahoraga, a human or a nonhuman being, he manifests every one of these forms and teaches the Dharma; should a being be one who is brought across by the form of a vajra-bearing spirit, he at once manifests the form of a vajra-bearing spirit and teaches the Dharma. "Akṣayamati! This Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva has accomplished merit such as this, journeying through every land in every kind of form, bringing beings across to liberation. For this reason, you should all make offerings to Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva with a single mind. This Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva, amid fear and urgent peril, is able to grant freedom from fear — for this reason, in this sahā world, he is called everywhere the Bestower of Fearlessness." Akṣayamati Bodhisattva said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! I shall now make an offering to Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva." He then removed the string of precious pearls and jewels from his own neck, worth a hundred thousand ounces of gold, and offered it to him, saying: "Noble one! Accept this necklace of precious jewels, this gift of the Dharma." At that time Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva was unwilling to accept it. Akṣayamati said to Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva once more: "Noble one! Out of pity for us, accept this necklace." At that time the Buddha said to Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva: "You should, out of pity for this Akṣayamati Bodhisattva and the fourfold assembly, and for the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans and nonhumans, accept this necklace." At that time Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, out of pity for the fourfold assembly, and for the gods, nāgas, humans and nonhumans, accepted the necklace, and divided it into two parts, offering one part to Śākyamuni Buddha, and the other part to Prabhūtaratna Buddha's own stūpa. "Akṣayamati! Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva possesses sovereign spiritual power such as this, journeying throughout the sahā world."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi counts thirty-three distinct forms in this list, and reads them as the direct answer to Akṣayamati's second question — not a repetition of the first half's rescue stories, but an account of method: this bodhisattva teaches by becoming, in each case, whatever form the listener is actually equipped to learn from, whether that listener needs a buddha, a king, a monk, or a child. The moment of Avalokiteśvara's own initial refusal of Akṣayamati's necklace, accepting it only once the Buddha himself asks on the whole assembly's behalf, is worth noting as a small but deliberate detail — a bodhisattva capable of appearing as a buddha still declines a gift meant for himself until it can be understood as an offering to others, dividing it in two the moment he does accept it.

3. The Verse of Universal Gate

Chinese Source

爾時無盡意菩薩以偈問曰： 「世尊妙相具，　我今重問彼， 佛子何因緣，　名為觀世音？ 具足妙相尊，　偈答無盡意： 『汝聽觀音行，　善應諸方所， 弘誓深如海，　歷劫不思議， 侍多千億佛，　發大清淨願。 我為汝略說，　聞名及見身， 心念不空過，　能滅諸有苦。 假使興害意，　推落大火坑， 念彼觀音力，　火坑變成池。 或漂流巨海，　龍魚諸鬼難， 念彼觀音力，　波浪不能沒。 或在須彌峯，　為人所推墮， 念彼觀音力，　如日虛空住。 或被惡人逐，　墮落金剛山， 念彼觀音力，　不能損一毛。 或值怨賊繞，　各執刀加害， 念彼觀音力，　咸即起慈心。 或遭王難苦，　臨刑欲壽終， 念彼觀音力，　刀尋段段壞。 或囚禁枷鎖，　手足被杻械， 念彼觀音力，　釋然得解脫。 呪詛諸毒藥，　所欲害身者， 念彼觀音力，　還著於本人。 或遇惡羅剎、　毒龍諸鬼等， 念彼觀音力，　時悉不敢害。 若惡獸圍遶，　利牙爪可怖， 念彼觀音力，　疾走無邊方。 蚖蛇及蝮蠍，　氣毒煙火燃， 念彼觀音力，　尋聲自迴去。 雲雷鼓掣電，　降雹澍大雨， 念彼觀音力，　應時得消散。 眾生被困厄，　無量苦逼身， 觀音妙智力，　能救世間苦。 具足神通力，　廣修智方便， 十方諸國土，　無剎不現身。 種種諸惡趣，　地獄鬼畜生， 生老病死苦，　以漸悉令滅。 真觀清淨觀，　廣大智慧觀， 悲觀及慈觀，　常願常瞻仰。 無垢清淨光，　慧日破諸闇， 能伏災風火，　普明照世間。 悲體戒雷震，　慈意妙大雲， 澍甘露法雨，　滅除煩惱焰。 諍訟經官處，　怖畏軍陣中， 念彼觀音力，　眾怨悉退散。 妙音觀世音，　梵音海潮音， 勝彼世間音，　是故須常念， 念念勿生疑。　觀世音淨聖， 於苦惱死厄，　能為作依怙， 具一切功德，　慈眼視眾生， 福聚海無量，　是故應頂禮。』」 爾時持地菩薩即從座起，前白佛言：「世尊！若有眾生聞是觀世音菩薩品自在之業、普門示現神通力者，當知是人功德不少。」 佛說是普門品時，眾中八萬四千眾生，皆發無等等阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Akṣayamati Bodhisattva asked in verse: "World-Honored One, complete with every wondrous mark, I ask of you once more: for what cause and condition is this child of the Buddha named 'Perceiver of the World's Sounds'?" The World-Honored One, complete with every wondrous mark, answered Akṣayamati in verse: "Listen to Avalokiteśvara's own conduct, responding skillfully in every place; his great vow, deep as the ocean, beyond conceiving across every kalpa, attending upon thousands of koṭis of buddhas, bringing forth a great and pure vow. I shall now describe it briefly for you: hearing his name, and seeing his form, and holding him in mind without this ever being in vain — he is able to extinguish every kind of suffering that exists. Suppose someone with harmful intent should push you into a great pit of fire — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, the pit of fire transforms into a pool. Or should you be swept adrift on the great ocean, in peril from dragons, fish, and every kind of demon — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, the waves cannot drown you. Or should you stand upon the peak of Sumeru, and be pushed off by another — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, you shall remain fixed in the sky like the sun. Or should you be pursued by evil people, and fall from a mountain of diamond — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, not a single hair of yours shall be harmed. Or should you be surrounded by bandit enemies, each one brandishing a blade to harm you — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, every one of them shall at once give rise to a compassionate heart. Or should you suffer the king's own punishment, your life about to end at the executioner's hand — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, the blade shall break at once into pieces. Or should you be imprisoned in chains and shackles, your hands and feet bound in fetters — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, you shall be released, set free at once. Should curses and every kind of poison be turned against your own body to do it harm — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, it shall return upon the one who sent it. Or should you encounter evil rākṣasas, poisonous dragons, and every kind of demon — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, none of them shall dare do you harm. Should fierce beasts surround you, their sharp fangs and claws a terror — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, they shall swiftly flee in every direction. Should venomous snakes, vipers, and scorpions, their poison breath like smoke and fire — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, at the very sound of your voice, turn back of themselves. Should clouds thunder, lightning flash, hail fall, and torrential rain pour down — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, all at once shall scatter and disperse. Beings beset by every hardship, pressed by immeasurable suffering — Avalokiteśvara's own wondrous power of wisdom is able to save the world from its suffering. Complete with the power of spiritual penetration, broadly cultivating wisdom and skillful means, throughout the lands of the ten directions, there is no land where he does not manifest himself. Every kind of evil destiny — the hells, the hungry ghosts, the animal realm, the suffering of birth, old age, sickness, and death — by degrees, he causes it all to be extinguished. True seeing, pure seeing, seeing with vast and great wisdom, seeing with pity and seeing with loving-kindness — constantly desired, constantly gazed upon in reverence. His stainless, pure light, a sun of wisdom breaking through every darkness, able to subdue the calamities of wind and fire, its universal brightness illuminating the whole world. His very being compassion, thundering forth the precepts, his very mind loving-kindness, a wondrous great cloud, raining down the sweet dew of the Dharma's own rain, extinguishing the flames of affliction. In litigation before a magistrate, or in terror amid the ranks of an army — holding in mind the power of Avalokiteśvara, every enemy shall retreat and scatter. Wondrous sound, Perceiver of the World's Sounds, the pure sound of Brahmā, the sound of the ocean's own tide, a sound surpassing every sound of this world — for this reason, hold him constantly in mind, in thought after thought never giving rise to doubt. Avalokiteśvara, pure and holy one, in suffering, affliction, death, and calamity, is able to be a refuge and support; complete with every merit, his compassionate eyes gazing upon every being, his sea of accumulated good fortune beyond measure — for this reason, you should bow your head before him." At that time Dharaṇiṃdhara Bodhisattva rose from his seat and came forward, saying to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! Should there be any being who hears this chapter on Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva's own sovereign deeds, and the spiritual power of his universal gate's manifestation, you should know that this person's merit is by no means small." When the Buddha taught this Universal Gate chapter, eighty-four thousand beings in the assembly all brought forth the resolve for unequaled, unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.

Reading note

This verse is, on its own, among the most widely chanted passages in East Asian Buddhism, recited independently of the rest of the sūtra far more often than it is read in this context — and reading it here, at the end of a chapter that opened with a formal doctrinal question about a bodhisattva's name, is worth doing deliberately rather than skipping past. The catalog of perils it lists — fire, flood, execution, bandits, wild beasts, poison, litigation, and battle — is not an abstract list; it names, in turn, nearly every ordinary catastrophe available to an audience in the world this sūtra was first taught in, and the verse's own claim is not that faith prevents these things from ever happening, but that calling this bodhisattva to mind, in the moment they do happen, is never in vain. That the same figure who takes on thirty-three different forms elsewhere in this chapter is, in this closing verse, addressed directly and personally by name is itself part of what makes this text function as devotion as much as doctrine — the "universal gate" opens outward toward every form a listener might need, and this verse is one register through which readers across many centuries have addressed what stands on the other side of it.
