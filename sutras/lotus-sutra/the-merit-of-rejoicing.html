The Merit of Rejoicing

隨喜功德品第十八

Part 18 measured the merit of hearing this sūtra directly from the Buddha and believing it. This chapter asks a smaller, more ordinary question: what does someone gain who hears only a secondhand account, fiftieth in an unbroken chain of retelling, and simply feels glad? The Buddha's answer sets that single moment of gladness against an almost absurdly generous donor — one who satisfies an entire universe's worth of beings in every desire, then personally leads them all to arhatship — and says the fiftieth listener's joy still exceeds it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
Maitreya's question about the reward for rejoicing at this sūtra even secondhand, and the Buddha's answer: a chain of retelling stretching to a fiftieth listener, whose single moment of gladness is set against a donor who satisfies every desire of every being across four hundred myriad koṭis of asaṃkhyeya worlds and then personally leads them all to arhatship — and still falls short. The chapter closes by describing the plainer, everyday rewards of going in person to hear this sūtra taught, offering one's own seat to a fellow listener, or simply encouraging someone else to go and listen.
Why it matters
Zhiyi reads this fiftieth listener as occupying the very first, faintest rung of a ladder this sūtra has spent seventeen chapters describing at its highest rungs — a single thought of rejoicing, with no accompanying practice at all, is still said to outweigh a feat of generosity and teaching most readers would consider already beyond any ordinary measure. The chapter's own logic is that if the diminished, hundredth-hand version of this gladness already exceeds that, the reader's own present, first-hand encounter with this sūtra is worth taking seriously.

1. Maitreya's Question, and the Parable of the Great Donor

Chinese Source

爾時，彌勒菩薩摩訶薩白佛言：「世尊！若有善男子、善女人，聞是法華經隨喜者，得幾所福？」 而說偈言： 「世尊滅度後，　其有聞是經， 若能隨喜者，　為得幾所福？」 爾時，佛告彌勒菩薩摩訶薩：「阿逸多！如來滅後，若比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷，及餘智者若長若幼，聞是經隨喜已，從法會出，至於餘處，若在僧坊，若空閑地，若城邑、巷陌、聚落、田里，如其所聞，為父母、宗親、善友、知識，隨力演說。是諸人等，聞已隨喜，復行轉教；餘人聞已，亦隨喜轉教。如是展轉，至第五十。 「阿逸多！其第五十善男子、善女人，隨喜功德，我今說之，汝當善聽。若四百萬億阿僧祇世界，六趣四生眾生——卵生、胎生、濕生、化生，若有形、無形，有想、無想，非有想、非無想，無足、二足、四足、多足，如是等在眾生數者——有人求福，隨其所欲娛樂之具，皆給與之。一一眾生，與滿閻浮提金、銀、琉璃、車璩馬腦、珊瑚、虎珀，諸妙珍寶，及象、馬車乘，七寶所成宮殿樓閣等。是大施主，如是布施滿八十年已，而作是念：『我已施眾生娛樂之具，隨意所欲，然此眾生，皆已衰老年過八十，髮白面皺，將死不久，我當以佛法而訓導之。』即集此眾生，宣布法化，示教利喜，一時皆得須陀洹道、斯陀含道、阿那含道、阿羅漢道，盡諸有漏，於深禪定皆得自在，具八解脫。於汝意云何，是大施主所得功德寧為多不？」 彌勒白佛言：「世尊！是人功德甚多，無量無邊。若是施主，但施眾生一切樂具，功德無量；何況令得阿羅漢果。」 佛告彌勒：「我今分明語汝，是人以一切樂具，施於四百萬億阿僧祇世界六趣眾生，又令得阿羅漢果，所得功德，不如是第五十人，聞法華經一偈隨喜功德，百分、千分、百千萬億分、不及其一，乃至算數譬喻所不能知。 「阿逸多！如是第五十人展轉聞法華經隨喜功德，尚無量無邊阿僧祇，何況最初於會中聞而隨喜者，其福復勝，無量無邊阿僧祇，不可得比。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Maitreya Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! Should there be a good son or good daughter who hears this Lotus Sūtra and rejoices in it, how much good fortune do they gain?" And he spoke this verse: "After the World-Honored One's parinirvāṇa, should there be one who hears this sūtra and is able to rejoice in it — how much good fortune do they gain?" At that time the Buddha said to Maitreya Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva: "Ajita! After the Tathāgata's parinirvāṇa, should a monk, a nun, a layman, or a laywoman, or any other person of wisdom, whether old or young, hear this sūtra and rejoice in it, and then, leaving the Dharma assembly, go to some other place — whether a monastery, or an open field, or a city, a lane, a village, or a field — and there, according to what they heard, teach it, as far as their own ability allows, to their parents, their kin, their good friends and acquaintances; and should these people, having heard it, likewise rejoice in it, and go on to teach it further; and should others still, having heard it from them, likewise rejoice in it and teach it further — and so it continues, passed on in this way, until it reaches a fiftieth person — "Ajita, I shall now tell you of the merit of rejoicing gained by that fiftieth good son or good daughter — listen well. Suppose there were four hundred myriad koṭis of asaṃkhyeya worlds, and within them, beings of the six realms and the four modes of birth — born from eggs, from wombs, from moisture, or by transformation; possessed of form or formless; possessed of perception, or without perception, or neither with nor without perception; footless, two-footed, four-footed, or many-footed, every being that can be counted among the ranks of beings — and suppose there were someone seeking merit, who gave to every one of these beings whatever means of pleasure and enjoyment they desired. To each and every being, he gave enough gold, silver, lapis lazuli, giant clamshell, agate, coral, and amber, and every kind of fine treasure, to fill all of Jambudvīpa, together with elephants, horses, and chariots, and palaces and towers built entirely of the seven jewels. This great benefactor, having given such gifts for a full eighty years, then thought to himself: 'I have already given these beings every means of pleasure they desired — yet these beings have all now grown old and weak, past eighty years of age, their hair white and their faces lined, soon to die. I should now guide and instruct them with the Buddha's own Dharma.' He then gathered these beings together, proclaimed the Dharma's transformation to them, revealing, teaching, benefiting, and delighting them, so that they all, at once, attained the fruits of stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning, and arhatship, exhausting every outflow, gaining mastery over profound meditative concentration, and complete with the eight liberations. What do you think — is the merit this great benefactor gained truly great?" Maitreya said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! This person's merit is exceedingly great, immeasurable and boundless. Were this benefactor only to have given beings every means of pleasure, the merit would already be immeasurable — how much more so, causing them to attain the fruit of arhatship." The Buddha said to Maitreya: "I shall now declare this to you plainly. The merit this person gained, giving every means of pleasure to the beings of the six realms across four hundred myriad koṭis of asaṃkhyeya worlds, and, further, causing them to attain the fruit of arhatship — this merit does not equal one part in a hundred, a thousand, a hundred thousand myriad koṭis, of the merit of rejoicing gained by that fiftieth person, on hearing even a single verse of the Lotus Sūtra — not even a number or a parable could express the difference. "Ajita! If the merit of rejoicing gained by this fiftieth person, hearing the Lotus Sūtra only after it has passed through so many hands, is already immeasurable, boundless, and beyond counting — how much more so the merit of the very first person in the assembly, who heard it directly and rejoiced. That person's own good fortune surpasses this by even more, immeasurable and boundless beyond counting, beyond any possible comparison."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi reads the fiftieth listener as occupying the very first, faintest position on the same ladder Part 18 measured at its highest rungs: a single moment of rejoicing, diminished by passing through forty-nine other tellers before it ever reaches this person, with no accompanying practice, no upholding, no recitation — nothing but gladness itself. That this diminished, hundredth-hand gladness should still exceed a benefactor's eighty years of universal generosity, culminating in bringing an entire universe's population to arhatship, is, on his reading, the chapter's whole argument in miniature: if merit this attenuated already surpasses a feat that scale of generosity, the reader's own present encounter with this sūtra — first-hand, not fiftieth-hand — is worth taking with full seriousness.

2. Four Ways of Listening, and Their Reward

Chinese Source

「又，阿逸多！若人為是經故，往詣僧坊，若坐、若立須臾聽受，緣是功德，轉身所生，得好上妙象、馬車乘、珍寶輦輿，及乘天宮。若復有人於講法處坐，更有人來，勸令坐聽，若分座令坐，是人功德，轉身得帝釋坐處，若梵王坐處，若轉輪聖王所坐之處。 「阿逸多！若復有人，語餘人言：『有經，名法華，可共往聽。』即受其教，乃至須臾間聞，是人功德，轉身得與陀羅尼菩薩共生一處，利根智慧，百千萬世終不瘖瘂，口氣不臭，舌常無病，口亦無病，齒不垢黑、不黃、不踈，亦不缺落，不差、不曲，脣不下垂亦不褰縮、不麁澁、不瘡胗，亦不缺壞，亦不喎斜，不厚、不大，亦不黧黑，無諸可惡。鼻不匾㔸，亦不曲戾，面色不黑，亦不狹長，亦不窊曲，無有一切不可喜相。脣舌牙齒悉皆嚴好，鼻修高直，面貌圓滿，眉高而長，額廣平正，人相具足，世世所生，見佛聞法、信受教誨。 「阿逸多！汝且觀是，勸於一人令往聽法，功德如此，何況一心聽說、讀誦，而於大眾為人分別、如說修行？」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Ajita! Should anyone, for this sūtra's sake, go to a monastery, and there sit or stand, listening to it and receiving it even for a brief moment, by the power of this merit they shall be born, in their next life, into possession of fine and excellent elephants, horses, and chariots, palanquins adorned with precious jewels, and even the chance to ride in the palaces of the gods. Should there be someone else, seated in a place where the Dharma is being taught, and another person then arrives, whom they urge to sit and listen, or to whom they yield part of their own seat, that person's merit shall bring them, in their next life, the seat of Śakra, or the seat of Brahmā, or the very seat of a wheel-turning sage-king. "Ajita! Should there be someone who says to another: 'There is a sūtra called the Lotus — let us go together and listen to it,' and that person accepts the invitation, and hears it even for a brief moment, that person's merit shall bring them, in their next life, to be born in the very same place as bodhisattvas who have attained dhāraṇīs, sharp in faculties and wisdom, and for a hundred thousand myriad lifetimes they shall never be mute, their breath never foul, their tongue never afflicted, their mouth never afflicted, their teeth never stained black, nor yellow, nor gapped, nor broken, nor crooked, nor twisted; their lips never drooping, nor drawn back, nor coarse, nor scarred, nor damaged, nor crooked to one side, neither too thick nor too large, nor darkly discolored — free of every unpleasant mark. Their nose shall never be flat or crooked, their complexion never dark, their face never too narrow or too long, never sunken or distorted — free of every unlovely feature. Their lips, tongue, and teeth shall all be finely formed, their nose slender, high, and straight, their countenance full and rounded, their brows high and long, their forehead broad and even — every mark of a fine human form complete, and in every life they are born, they shall see buddhas, hear the Dharma, and believe and accept its teaching. "Ajita! Consider this well: the merit of urging even a single person to go and listen to the Dharma is already as great as this — how much more so, then, the merit of one who listens with a single mind, who reads and recites it, and, before a great assembly, explains it to others, and practices exactly as it teaches?"

Chinese Source

爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「若人於法會，　得聞是經典， 乃至於一偈，　隨喜為他說， 如是展轉教，　至于第五十， 最後人獲福，　今當分別之。 如有大施主，　供給無量眾， 具滿八十歲，　隨意之所欲。 見彼衰老相，　髮白而面皺， 齒踈形枯竭，　念其死不久， 我今應當教，　令得於道果。 即為方便說，　涅槃真實法， 世皆不牢固，　如水沫泡焰， 汝等咸應當，　疾生厭離心。 諸人聞是法，　皆得阿羅漢， 具足六神通，　三明八解脫。 最後第五十，　聞一偈隨喜， 是人福勝彼，　不可為譬喻。 如是展轉聞，　其福尚無量， 何況於法會，　初聞隨喜者。 若有勸一人，　將引聽法華， 言此經深妙，　千萬劫難遇， 即受教往聽，　乃至須臾聞， 斯人之福報，　今當分別說。 世世無口患，　齒不踈黃黑， 脣不厚褰缺，　無有可惡相， 舌不乾黑短，　鼻高修且直， 額廣而平正，　面目悉端嚴， 為人所喜見，　口氣無臭穢， 優鉢華之香、　常從其口出。 若故詣僧坊，　欲聽法華經， 須臾聞歡喜，　今當說其福。 後生天人中，　得妙象馬車， 珍寶之輦輿，　及乘天宮殿。 若於講法處，　勸人坐聽經， 是福因緣得，　釋梵轉輪座。 何況一心聽，　解說其義趣， 如說而修行，　其福不可量。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to proclaim this meaning once more, spoke in verse: "If a person, in the Dharma assembly, comes to hear this sūtra, even a single verse of it, and rejoices, teaching it to another — and it is taught on in this way, passed hand to hand, until it reaches a fiftieth person, I shall now describe the good fortune this last person gains. It is like a great benefactor, providing for immeasurable beings, for a full eighty years, whatever they desired. Seeing in them the marks of old age, their hair white, their faces lined, their teeth gapped, their bodies withered, and knowing their death was not far off, he thought: 'I should now teach them, and cause them to attain the fruit of the path.' He then taught them, by skillful means, the true Dharma of nirvāṇa: 'Nothing in this world is firm or lasting — it is like foam on water, like a bubble, like a flame. All of you should swiftly give rise to a heart weary of it, and turn away.' Hearing this Dharma, everyone attained arhatship, complete with the six spiritual powers, the three insights, and the eight liberations. Yet this last, fiftieth person, hearing but a single verse and rejoicing, gains a good fortune greater than all of this — beyond any parable to express. If the good fortune of one who hears it only after passing through so many hands is already immeasurable, how much more so the good fortune of the very first one, in the Dharma assembly, who heard it and rejoiced. If one urges another, leading them to listen to the Lotus Sūtra, saying: 'This sūtra is profound and wondrous, rarely met with even in a thousand myriad kalpas' — and that person accepts the invitation and goes to listen, hearing it even for the briefest moment, I shall now describe this person's own reward. In life after life, they shall be free of any affliction of the mouth, their teeth never gapped, yellow, or black, their lips never thick, drawn back, or scarred, free of every unlovely mark, their tongue never dry, dark, or short, their nose high, slender, and straight, their forehead broad and even, their face and features entirely fine and dignified, a joy for others to behold, their breath never foul, the fragrance of the blue lotus constantly issuing from their mouth. If one deliberately goes to a monastery, wishing to hear the Lotus Sūtra, and listens even briefly, rejoicing, I shall now tell of the good fortune this brings. In a later life, born among gods and humans, they shall gain fine elephants, horses, and chariots, palanquins adorned with jewels, and rides in the palaces of the gods. If, in a place where the Dharma is taught, one urges another to sit and listen to the sūtra, this good fortune, through its own causes and conditions, brings the seat of Śakra, of Brahmā, or of a wheel-turning king. How much more so, then, one who listens with a single mind, understands its meaning fully, and practices exactly as it teaches — their good fortune cannot be measured at all."

Reading note

Zhiyi reads this second half of the chapter as moving from the interior merit of rejoicing to the exterior merit of the plainest possible actions — attending in person, however briefly; making room for one more listener; simply telling someone else the lecture exists. He notes that the physical rewards described here, particularly the catalog of a fine mouth, nose, and countenance free of every unlovely mark, anticipate in miniature what the next chapter will describe at far greater length and at a more advanced stage: the purification of all six sense faculties gained by one who actually upholds this sūtra, rather than merely having been urged, once, to go and listen to it.
