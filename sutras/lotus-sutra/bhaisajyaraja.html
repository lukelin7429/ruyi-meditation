Bhaiṣajyarāja's Former Life

藥王菩薩本事品第二十三

Bhadravyūhamati asks how the bodhisattva Bhaiṣajyarāja, the Medicine King, came to practice such difficult austerities, and the Buddha answers with the story of a much earlier bodhisattva, Sarvasattvapriyadarśana — "Gladly Seen by All Beings" — who anoints his own body in fragrant oil and sets it ablaze as an offering to a buddha's teaching, is reborn to make a second offering of his own arms, and is revealed, at the story's close, to have been Bhaiṣajyarāja himself. This is one of the sūtra's most extreme and most historically contested chapters; it is presented here with Zhiyi's classical reading held openly alongside the tension a reader today will likely feel against it.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
Bhadravyūhamati's question about Bhaiṣajyarāja's ascetic practices, and the Buddha's answer: the ancient story of Sarvasattvapriyadarśana, a bodhisattva who attains a samādhi of manifesting every form after long practice under the buddha Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī, then offers his own body in flames as homage to that buddha's teaching of this very sūtra; his rebirth, and a second offering of his own arms after that buddha's parinirvāṇa; the Buddha's revelation that this bodhisattva was Bhaiṣajyarāja himself; an extended comparison of this sūtra's supremacy among all teachings, using the images of the ocean among rivers, Sumeru among mountains, and the moon among stars; and the closing promise of protection and rebirth free of a female body for any woman who upholds this particular chapter.
Why it matters
This chapter has a documented history in East Asian Buddhism of being read, at times, as literal precedent for self-immolation, including by monks who cited it directly. Zhiyi's own commentary reads the burning through the lens of emptiness — "who burns, and what is burned?" — rather than as literal instruction, but that reading does not erase the chapter's plain narrative or its later reception. This site presents both without resolving the tension between them.

1. The Bodhisattva Who Offered His Own Body in Flames

Chinese Source

爾時宿王華菩薩白佛言：「世尊！藥王菩薩云何遊於娑婆世界？世尊！是藥王菩薩有若干百千萬億那由他難行苦行？善哉，世尊！願少解說。」諸天、龍、神、夜叉、乾闥婆、阿修羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽、人非人等，又他國土諸來菩薩，及此聲聞眾，聞皆歡喜。 爾時佛告宿王華菩薩：「乃往過去無量恒河沙劫，有佛號日月淨明德如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊。其佛有八十億大菩薩摩訶薩，七十二恒河沙大聲聞眾，佛壽四萬二千劫，菩薩壽命亦等。彼國無有女人、地獄、餓鬼、畜生、阿修羅等，及以諸難；地平如掌，琉璃所成，寶樹莊嚴，寶帳覆上，垂寶華幡，寶瓶香爐周遍國界，七寶為臺，一樹一臺，其樹去臺盡一箭道。此諸寶樹，皆有菩薩、聲聞而坐其下。諸寶臺上，各有百億諸天作天伎樂，歌歎於佛，以為供養。爾時彼佛為一切眾生憙見菩薩，及眾菩薩、諸聲聞眾，說法華經。 「是一切眾生憙見菩薩，樂習苦行，於日月淨明德佛法中，精進經行，一心求佛，滿萬二千歲已，得現一切色身三昧。得此三昧已，心大歡喜，即作念言：『我得現一切色身三昧，皆是得聞法華經力，我今當供養日月淨明德佛及法華經。』即時入是三昧，於虛空中，雨曼陀羅華、摩訶曼陀羅華、細末堅黑栴檀，滿虛空中，如雲而下，又雨海此岸栴檀之香——此香六銖，價直娑婆世界——以供養佛。作是供養已，從三昧起，而自念言：『我雖以神力供養於佛，不如以身供養。』即服諸香——栴檀、薰陸、兜樓婆、畢力迦、沈水、膠香，又飲瞻蔔諸華香油，滿千二百歲已，香油塗身，於日月淨明德佛前，以天寶衣而自纏身，灌諸香油，以神通力願而自然身，光明遍照八十億恒河沙世界。其中諸佛同時讚言：『善哉，善哉！善男子！是真精進，是名真法供養如來。若以華、香、瓔珞、燒香、末香、塗香、天繒、幡蓋及海此岸栴檀之香，如是等種種諸物供養，所不能及；假使國城、妻子布施，亦所不及。善男子！是名第一之施，於諸施中最尊最上，以法供養諸如來故。』作是語已而各默然。其身火燃千二百歲，過是已後，其身乃盡。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Bhadravyūhamati Bodhisattva said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! How does Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva journey through this sahā world? World-Honored One, this Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva has undertaken so many hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of nayutas of difficult ascetic practices — I beg you, World-Honored One, to explain this a little for us." Every god, nāga, spirit, yakṣa, gandharva, asura, garuḍa, kiṃnara, mahoraga, human and nonhuman, and the bodhisattvas who had come from other lands, and this assembly of śrāvakas, hearing this question, were all filled with joy. At that time the Buddha said to Bhadravyūhamati Bodhisattva: "Long ago, past immeasurable Ganges-sands of kalpas, there was a buddha named Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One. This buddha had eighty koṭis of great bodhisattva-mahāsattvas, and seventy-two Ganges-sands of great śrāvakas; the buddha's own lifespan was forty-two thousand kalpas, and the bodhisattvas' lifespans were equal to it. In that land there were no women, no hells, no hungry ghosts, no animals, no asuras, and none of the other difficult states of being; the ground was level as the palm of a hand, made entirely of lapis lazuli, adorned with jeweled trees, covered above with jeweled canopies, hung with jeweled flower banners, its jeweled vases and censers filling the whole land, with a platform of the seven jewels beside every tree, each tree standing the distance of a single arrow's flight from its platform. Beneath every one of these jeweled trees sat bodhisattvas and śrāvakas. On every jeweled platform, a hundred koṭis of gods made heavenly music, singing the buddha's praises as an offering to him. At that time this buddha, for the sake of Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva, and the assembly of bodhisattvas and śrāvakas, taught the Lotus Sūtra. "This Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana delighted in practicing austerities, and, within the teaching of Buddha Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī, diligently walked in meditation, seeking buddhahood with a single mind, and, after a full twelve thousand years, attained the samādhi of manifesting every form. Having attained this samādhi, his heart filled with great joy, and he thought to himself: 'That I have attained this samādhi of manifesting every form is entirely due to the power of hearing the Lotus Sūtra — I should now make an offering to Buddha Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī, and to the Lotus Sūtra itself.' At once he entered this samādhi, and in the sky above, rained down mandārava and mahā-mandārava flowers, and finely powdered, firm black sandalwood, filling the sky, falling like clouds, and rained down, too, the fragrance of sandalwood from beyond the sea — a fragrance of which a mere six śāṇas in weight was worth the whole sahā world — as an offering to the buddha. Having made this offering, he rose from the samādhi, and thought to himself: 'Though I have made this offering to the buddha by spiritual power, it is not equal to making an offering with my own body.' He then consumed every kind of fragrant substance — sandalwood, frankincense, tūrukka, pṛkkā, aloeswood, and gum resin — and drank, besides, the fragrant oil of campaka and other flowers, and after a full twelve hundred years had passed, anointed his own body with fragrant oil, and, before Buddha Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī, wrapped his own body in a heavenly jeweled robe, poured fragrant oil over it, and, by the power of his own spiritual penetration and his own vow, set his body ablaze, its light illuminating eighty koṭis of Ganges-sands of worlds entirely. Every buddha within those worlds, with one voice, praised him, saying: 'Excellent, excellent, good son! This is true diligence — this is called a true offering of the Dharma to the Tathāgata. Were one to make an offering of flowers, incense, necklaces, burning incense, powdered incense, ointment incense, heavenly silk, banners and canopies, and even the fragrance of sandalwood from beyond the sea, and every other kind of thing such as these, it could not equal this; even were one to give away one's own kingdom, cities, wife, and children, it could not equal this either. Good son, this is called the foremost form of giving, the most honored and supreme among every form of giving, because it is an offering of the Dharma itself to every tathāgata.' Having spoken these words, they all fell silent. His body burned in flames for twelve hundred years, and only after this had passed was his body at last consumed."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi's own gloss for what makes this a "true offering of the Dharma" turns entirely inward, toward contemplation rather than the literal act: he reads the burning as an exercise in emptiness, in which the practitioner observes that the body, the flame, the one making the offering, and the offering itself are all without any fixed, findable nature — "who is it that burns, and what is being burned? Neither the giver nor the gift can ultimately be found; this is why it is called a true offering." On this reading, the passage's real subject is a particular meditative insight into non-self, expressed through an extreme narrative image, rather than a literal instruction that setting one's body alight is itself the point.

That reading does not erase the passage's plain narrative, nor its later reception. This chapter has a documented history in Chinese Buddhism of being cited directly by monks who did burn a finger, an arm, or, in rarer and more extreme cases, their entire bodies, understanding themselves to be following Sarvasattvapriyadarśana's own example rather than only its emptiness-contemplation behind it. Zhiyi does not himself instruct anyone to reenact the story; the comparative teaching later in this same chapter, that burning even a single finger in offering surpasses giving away one's own kingdom, wife, and children, belongs to a broader classical Buddhist framework ranking gifts by how difficult they are to give, not a specific medical or safety instruction. This site presents the passage as it stands in Kumārajīva's translation and Zhiyi's commentary, without softening its content or endorsing any literal reenactment of it — a tension the text itself, and its historical readers, did not resolve, and this page will not resolve on their behalf.

2. A Second Offering, and a Buddha's Parinirvāṇa

Chinese Source

「一切眾生憙見菩薩作如是法供養已，命終之後，復生日月淨明德佛國中，於淨德王家結加趺坐，忽然化生。即為其父而說偈言： 「『大王今當知，　我經行彼處， 即時得一切，　現諸身三昧。 懃行大精進，　捨所愛之身， 供養於世尊，　為求無上慧。』 「說是偈已，而白父言：『日月淨明德佛，今故現在。我先供養佛已，得解一切眾生語言陀羅尼，復聞是法華經八百千萬億那由他甄迦羅、頻婆羅、阿閦婆等偈。大王！我今當還供養此佛。』白已，即坐七寶之臺，上昇虛空，高七多羅樹，往到佛所，頭面禮足，合十指爪，以偈讚佛： 「『容顏甚奇妙，　光明照十方， 我適曾供養，　今復還親覲。』 「爾時，一切眾生憙見菩薩說是偈已，而白佛言：『世尊！世尊猶故在世。』爾時日月淨明德佛，告一切眾生憙見菩薩：『善男子！我涅槃時到、滅盡時至，汝可安施床座，我於今夜當般涅槃。』又勅一切眾生憙見菩薩：『善男子！我以佛法囑累於汝及諸菩薩大弟子，并阿耨多羅三藐三菩提法，亦以三千大千七寶世界諸寶樹、寶臺及給侍諸天，悉付於汝。我滅度後，所有舍利亦付囑汝，當令流布，廣設供養，應起若干千塔。』如是日月淨明德佛勅一切眾生憙見菩薩已，於夜後分入於涅槃。 「爾時，一切眾生憙見菩薩見佛滅度，悲感、懊惱，戀慕於佛，即以海此岸栴檀為[卄/積]，供養佛身，而以燒之。火滅已後，收取舍利，作八萬四千寶瓶，以起八萬四千塔，高三世界，表剎莊嚴，垂諸幡蓋，懸眾寶鈴。 「爾時一切眾生憙見菩薩復自念言：『我雖作是供養，心猶未足，我今當更供養舍利。』便語諸菩薩大弟子及天、龍、夜叉等一切大眾：『汝等當一心念，我今供養日月淨明德佛舍利。』作是語已，即於八萬四千塔前，燃百福莊嚴臂七萬二千歲而以供養，令無數求聲聞眾、無量阿僧祇人，發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心，皆使得住現一切色身三昧。 「爾時，諸菩薩、天、人、阿修羅等，見其無臂，憂惱悲哀而作是言：『此一切眾生憙見菩薩，是我等師，教化我者，而今燒臂，身不具足。』于時一切眾生憙見菩薩，於大眾中立此誓言：『我捨兩臂，必當得佛金色之身，若實不虛，令我兩臂還復如故。』作是誓已，自然還復，由斯菩薩福德智慧淳厚所致。當爾之時，三千大千世界六種震動，天雨寶華，一切人、天得未曾有。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Having made this offering of the Dharma, Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana, after his death, was reborn once more within the land of Buddha Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī, born instantly, by transformation, seated cross-legged in the house of King Vimaladatta. He at once spoke this verse to his father: 'Great King, you should now know: I once walked in meditation in that place, and at that very time attained the samādhi of manifesting every form. Diligently practicing great effort, I gave up the body I loved, making an offering to the World-Honored One, seeking the unsurpassed wisdom.' "Having spoken this verse, he said to his father: 'Buddha Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī still remains in the world even now. Having once made my offering to the buddha, I attained the dhāraṇī of understanding the language of every being, and heard, besides, eight hundred thousand myriad koṭis of nayutas of kaṅkara, bimbara, and akṣobhya verses of this Lotus Sūtra. Great King, I shall now go once more to make an offering to this buddha.' Having said this, he seated himself upon a platform of the seven jewels, rose into the sky to a height of seven tāla trees, and went to where the buddha was, bowing his head to the buddha's feet, joining the tips of his ten fingers, and praising the buddha in verse: 'Your countenance is wondrous indeed, your light illuminating the ten directions; I made you an offering once before, and now return again to draw near you.' "At that time, having spoken this verse, Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana said to the buddha: 'World-Honored One! The World-Honored One still remains in the world.' At that time Buddha Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī said to Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana: 'Good son! The time of my own parinirvāṇa has come, the time of my extinction has arrived — you may now prepare my resting couch, for this very night I shall enter parinirvāṇa.' He further charged Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana: 'Good son! I entrust to you the Buddha's own Dharma, and to the great bodhisattva disciples besides, together with the Dharma of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening; I entrust to you, too, the whole three-thousand-great-thousandfold world of the seven jewels, its jeweled trees, its jeweled platforms, and the gods who attend and serve there. After my own parinirvāṇa, I entrust my relics to you as well — you should cause them to be spread abroad, make broad offerings to them, and raise up several thousand stūpas.' Having charged Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana in this way, Buddha Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī, in the latter part of that very night, entered parinirvāṇa. "At that time, Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana, seeing the buddha's own parinirvāṇa, grieved and mourned, longing for the buddha, and at once gathered sandalwood from beyond the sea into a pyre, made an offering of the buddha's own body, and burned it. Once the fire had gone out, he gathered the relics, made eighty-four thousand jeweled urns, and raised eighty-four thousand stūpas from them, each three worlds in height, their spires finely adorned, hung with banners and canopies, and hung, too, with every kind of jeweled bell. "At that time Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana thought to himself once more: 'Though I have made this offering, my heart is still not satisfied — I should now make a further offering to these relics.' He then said to the great bodhisattva disciples, and to the gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and the whole great assembly: 'You should, with a single mind, take note: I shall now make an offering to the relics of Buddha Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī.' Having said this, before the eighty-four thousand stūpas, he set ablaze his own arms, adorned with the marks of a hundred merits, for seventy-two thousand years, as an offering — causing countless śrāvaka-seekers, and immeasurable asaṃkhyeyas of people, to bring forth the resolve for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, and every one of them to attain and abide in the samādhi of manifesting every form. "At that time, the bodhisattvas, gods, humans, and asuras, seeing that he had no arms, grieved in sorrow, and said: 'This Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana is our own teacher, the one who taught and guided us — and now he has burned his own arms, and his body is no longer whole.' At that time Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana made this vow before the great assembly: 'I have given up both my arms; I shall surely attain the buddha's own body of golden color — if this is true, and not false, may my two arms be restored exactly as they were before.' Having made this vow, his arms were restored of themselves, entirely through the depth of this bodhisattva's own merit and wisdom. At that very time, the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world shook in six ways, the heavens rained down jeweled flowers, and every human and god gained what they had never known before."

Reading note

Zhiyi divides this bodhisattva's practice into a present offering and a future one, and reads the second — the burning of his own arms after Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī's parinirvāṇa — through the same lens as the first: an act performed in grief and devotion, immediately followed by a vow whose fulfillment (the arms restored, "exactly as they were before") the text presents as proof of the depth of his merit rather than as an argument for the act's necessity. It is worth noting plainly that the surrounding assembly's own reaction in the text is not celebration but grief — "our own teacher... his body is no longer whole" — a response the narrative records without correcting, immediately before the restoration resolves it.

3. "This Was Bhaiṣajyarāja" — and the Sūtra Foremost Among All Teachings

Chinese Source

佛告宿王華菩薩：「於汝意云何？一切眾生憙見菩薩，豈異人乎？今藥王菩薩是也。其所捨身布施，如是無量百千萬億那由他數。 「宿王華！若有發心欲得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提者，能燃手指，乃至足一指，供養佛塔，勝以國城、妻子，及三千大千國土山林河池、諸珍寶物而供養者，若復有人，以七寶滿三千大千世界，供養於佛，及大菩薩、辟支佛、阿羅漢，是人所得功德，不如受持此法華經，乃至一四句偈，其福最多。 「宿王華！譬如一切川流江河，諸水之中海為第一；此法華經亦復如是，於諸如來所說經中，最為深大。又如土山、黑山、小鐵圍山、大鐵圍山及十寶山，眾山之中，須彌山為第一；此法華經亦復如是，於諸經中最為其上。又如眾星之中，月天子最為第一；此法華經亦復如是，於千萬億種諸經法中最為照明。又如日天子能除諸闇；此經亦復如是，能破一切不善之闇。又如諸小王中，轉輪聖王最為第一；此經亦復如是，於眾經中最為其尊。又如帝釋於三十三天中王，此經亦復如是，諸經中王。又如大梵天王，一切眾生之父；此經亦復如是，一切賢聖，學、無學，及發菩薩心者之父。又如一切凡夫人中，須陀洹、斯陀含、阿那含、阿羅漢、辟支佛為第一；此經亦復如是，一切如來所說、若菩薩所說、若聲聞所說，諸經法中，最為第一。有能受持是經典者，亦復如是，於一切眾生中亦為第一。一切聲聞、辟支佛中，菩薩為第一；此經亦復如是，於一切諸經法中最為第一。如佛為諸法王；此經亦復如是，諸經中王。」

Ru-Yi Translation

The Buddha said to Bhadravyūhamati Bodhisattva: "What do you think — was that Bodhisattva Sarvasattvapriyadarśana at that time some other person? He is none other than Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva of today. The offerings he made of his own body, giving it up in this way, number immeasurable hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of nayutas. "Bhadravyūhamati! Should there be one who has resolved to attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, and is able to burn a finger, or even a single toe, as an offering to a buddha's stūpa, this surpasses making an offering of one's own kingdom, cities, wife, and children, or of the mountains, forests, rivers, and ponds of the whole three-thousand-great-thousandfold world, and every precious treasure besides. Should there be, further, one who fills the whole three-thousand-great-thousandfold world with the seven jewels, and offers it to the Buddha, to great bodhisattvas, to pratyekabuddhas, and to arhats, the merit such a person gains does not equal the merit of upholding this Lotus Sūtra, even a single four-line verse of it — that merit is the greatest of all. "Bhadravyūhamati! It is like every stream, river, and waterway — among all these waters, the ocean is foremost; this Lotus Sūtra, too, is exactly the same, the most profound and vast among every sūtra any tathāgata has taught. It is like the earthen mountains, the black mountains, the lesser and greater iron ring-mountains, and the ten jeweled mountains — among all these mountains, Sumeru is foremost; this Lotus Sūtra, too, is exactly the same, uppermost among every sūtra. It is like the moon, foremost among the stars; this Lotus Sūtra, too, is exactly the same, the most illuminating among thousands of myriads of koṭis of every kind of teaching. It is like the sun, able to dispel every darkness; this sūtra, too, is exactly the same, able to break through the darkness of every unwholesome thing. It is like the wheel-turning sage-king, foremost among every lesser king; this sūtra, too, is exactly the same, the most honored among every sūtra. It is like Śakra, king among the gods of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven; this sūtra, too, is exactly the same, king among every sūtra. It is like the great Brahmā king, father of every being; this sūtra, too, is exactly the same, father of every noble one, in training and beyond it, and of every one who has brought forth the bodhisattva's own resolve. It is like the stream-enterer, the once-returner, the non-returner, the arhat, and the pratyekabuddha, foremost among every ordinary person; this sūtra, too, is exactly the same, foremost among every teaching taught by any tathāgata, any bodhisattva, or any śrāvaka. Whoever is able to uphold this sūtra is, in exactly this way, foremost among every being. It is like the bodhisattva, foremost among every śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha; this sūtra, too, is exactly the same, foremost among every teaching there is. It is like the Buddha, king of every teaching; this sūtra, too, is exactly the same, king among every sūtra."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi reads each of these images as pointing to a specific ground for the sūtra's supremacy, not simply piling up praise: the ocean image marks this teaching as reaching all the way to the ultimate source rather than stopping at a provisional resting place; the moon and sun images distinguish it from teachings that present skillful means as though fixed and unchanging, since this sūtra alone discloses that the provisional and the real were never two different things; and the Brahmā-as-father image extends this sūtra's claim to be the common ground of every vehicle — not one teaching among several, but what every other teaching, including the earlier provisional ones this same sūtra itself taught, was always leading toward.

4. This Sūtra as Medicine, and Its Promise to Women

Chinese Source

「宿王華！此經能救一切眾生者，此經能令一切眾生離諸苦惱，此經能大饒益一切眾生，充滿其願。如清涼池！能滿一切諸渴乏者，如寒者得火，如裸者得衣，如商人得主，如子得母，如渡得船，如病得醫，如暗得燈，如貧得寶，如民得王，如賈客得海，如炬除暗；此法華經亦復如是，能令眾生離一切苦、一切病痛，能解一切生死之縛。若人得聞此法華經，若自書、若使人書，所得功德，以佛智慧籌量多少，不得其邊。若書是經卷，華、香、瓔珞、燒香、末香、塗香，幡蓋、衣服，種種之燈——酥燈、油燈、諸香油燈、瞻蔔油燈、須曼那油燈、波羅羅油燈、婆利師迦油燈、那婆摩利油燈——供養，所得功德，亦復無量。 「宿王華！若有人聞是藥王菩薩本事品者，亦得無量無邊功德。若有女人聞是藥王菩薩本事品，能受持者，盡是女身，後不復受。若如來滅後後五百歲中，若有女人聞是經典，如說修行。於此命終，即往安樂世界，阿彌陀佛、大菩薩眾，圍繞住處，生蓮華中，寶座之上，不復為貪欲所惱，亦復不為瞋恚愚癡所惱，亦復不為憍慢嫉妬諸垢所惱，得菩薩神通、無生法忍。得是忍已，眼根清淨，以是清淨眼根，見七百萬二千億那由他恒河沙等諸佛如來。是時諸佛遙共讚言：『善哉，善哉！善男子！汝能於釋迦牟尼佛法中，受持讀誦思惟是經，為他人說，所得福德無量無邊，火不能燒，水不能漂，汝之功德，千佛共說不能令盡。汝今已能破諸魔賊，壞生死軍，諸餘怨敵皆悉摧滅。善男子！百千諸佛，以神通力共守護汝，於一切世間天、人之中無如汝者，唯除如來，其諸聲聞、辟支佛、乃至菩薩，智慧禪定無有與汝等者。』宿王華！此菩薩成就如是功德智慧之力。若有人聞是藥王菩薩本事品，能隨喜讚善者，是人現世口中常出青蓮華香，身毛孔中常出牛頭栴檀之香，所得功德，如上所說。是故，宿王華！以此藥王菩薩本事品囑累於汝。我滅度後後五百歲中，廣宣流布於閻浮提，無令斷絕，惡魔、魔民、諸天、龍、夜叉、鳩槃茶等，得其便也。 「宿王華！汝當以神通之力守護是經。所以者何？此經則為閻浮提人，病之良藥。若人有病，得聞是經，病即消滅，不老不死。宿王華！汝若見有受持是經者，應以青蓮花盛滿末香，供散其上。散已，作是念言：『此人不久必當取草坐於道場，破諸魔軍，當吹法螺、擊大法鼓，度脫一切眾生老病死海。』是故求佛道者、見有受持是經典人，應當如是生恭敬心。」 說是藥王菩薩本事品時，八萬四千菩薩得解一切眾生語言陀羅尼。 多寶如來於寶塔中讚宿王華菩薩言：「善哉，善哉！宿王華！汝成就不可思議功德，乃能問釋迦牟尼佛如此之事，利益無量一切眾生。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Bhadravyūhamati! This sūtra is able to save every being; this sūtra is able to free every being from every kind of suffering and affliction; this sūtra is able to greatly benefit every being, and satisfy every one of their wishes. It is like a cool, clear pool, able to satisfy every one who thirsts; like fire to one who is cold, like clothing to one who is bare, like a caravan-leader to merchants, like a mother to a child, like a boat to one crossing a river, like a physician to the sick, like a lamp in darkness, like a treasure to the poor, like a king to the people, like the ocean to a trader, like a torch that dispels the dark — this Lotus Sūtra, too, is exactly the same, able to free beings from every suffering, every sickness and pain, able to loosen every bond of birth and death. Should a person come to hear this Lotus Sūtra, and copy it themselves, or cause others to copy it, the merit they gain could not be measured to its very limit even by the Buddha's own wisdom. Should one, having copied this sūtra scroll, make offerings to it with flowers, incense, necklaces, burning incense, powdered incense, ointment incense, banners, canopies, robes, and every kind of lamp — lamps of ghee, of oil, of every kind of fragrant oil, of campaka oil, of sumanā oil, of pāṭala oil, of vārṣika oil, of navamallikā oil — the merit gained from this, too, is immeasurable. "Bhadravyūhamati! Should anyone hear this chapter on Bhaiṣajyarāja's former life, they too shall gain immeasurable, boundless merit. Should a woman hear this chapter on Bhaiṣajyarāja's former life, and be able to uphold it, this shall be her very last female body — she shall not receive one again. Should there be, in the five hundred years after the Tathāgata's parinirvāṇa, a woman who hears this sūtra and practices exactly as it teaches, at the end of this present life she shall go at once to the World of Bliss, to the dwelling place of Amitābha Buddha, surrounded by his great bodhisattvas, born within a lotus flower, upon a jeweled seat, no longer troubled by greed and desire, no longer troubled by anger or folly, no longer troubled by the defilements of pride or envy, and shall attain a bodhisattva's own spiritual powers, and the acceptance that things are unborn. Having attained this acceptance, her eye faculty shall become pure, and with this pure eye faculty, she shall see seven million two thousand koṭis of nayutas of Ganges-sands of buddha-tathāgatas. At that time these buddhas, from far away, shall together praise her, saying: 'Excellent, excellent, good daughter! That you should be able, within Śākyamuni Buddha's own teaching, to uphold, read, recite, and reflect upon this sūtra, and teach it to others — the merit you gain from this is immeasurable and boundless; fire cannot burn it, water cannot sweep it away; your own merit could not be told to its end even were a thousand buddhas together to describe it. You are now already able to break the bandit-hordes of every demon, destroy the very army of birth and death, and every other enemy besides is entirely overcome. Good daughter! Hundreds of thousands of buddhas, by the power of their own spiritual penetration, together protect and guard you; among every being in the world, gods and humans alike, none is your equal, save the Tathāgata himself — not even the wisdom and meditative concentration of any śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, or bodhisattva can equal your own.' Bhadravyūhamati! This is the power of merit and wisdom this bodhisattva has accomplished. Should anyone hear this chapter on Bhaiṣajyarāja's former life, and be able to rejoice in it and praise it as good, that person's own mouth, in this very life, shall constantly emit the fragrance of the blue lotus, and the pores of their body shall constantly emit the fragrance of ox-head sandalwood — the merit they gain is exactly as described above. For this reason, Bhadravyūhamati, I entrust this chapter on Bhaiṣajyarāja's former life to you. In the five hundred years after my own parinirvāṇa, spread and proclaim it broadly throughout Jambudvīpa, and let it never be broken off, lest evil demons, the demons' own followers, gods, nāgas, yakṣas, and kumbhāṇḍas find their opportunity against it. "Bhadravyūhamati! You should protect this sūtra with the power of your own spiritual penetration. Why is this so? Because this sūtra is, for the people of Jambudvīpa, a fine medicine for their every sickness. Should a person have some illness, and come to hear this sūtra, that illness shall vanish at once, and they shall neither age nor die. Bhadravyūhamati! Should you see anyone who upholds this sūtra, you should fill a blue lotus flower with powdered incense, and scatter it over them as an offering. Having scattered it, you should think to yourself: 'Before long, this person shall surely take up a seat of grass at the very place of awakening, break every demon army, blow the Dharma conch, beat the great Dharma drum, and bring every being across the sea of old age, sickness, and death.' For this reason, those who seek the buddha-path, on seeing anyone who upholds this sūtra, should give rise to a reverent heart such as this." When this chapter on Bhaiṣajyarāja's former life was taught, eighty-four thousand bodhisattvas attained the dhāraṇī of understanding the language of every being. Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata, from within the jeweled stūpa, praised Bhadravyūhamati Bodhisattva, saying: "Excellent, excellent, Bhadravyūhamati! You have accomplished inconceivable merit, able to ask Śākyamuni Buddha of a matter such as this, benefiting immeasurable beings without limit."

Reading note

The promise that a woman who upholds this chapter "shall not receive a female body again" belongs to the same doctrinal current this site's commentary has already flagged at Part 13's account of the dragon king's daughter — the classical tradition's treatment of rebirth in a female body as itself a kind of obstacle to be transcended rather than a neutral circumstance. This site presents that promise as the text states it, without either erasing it or endorsing the premise behind it; readers approaching this passage after Part 13 will recognize the same tension raised there, unresolved here as well.
