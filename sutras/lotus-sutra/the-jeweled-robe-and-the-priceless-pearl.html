The Jeweled Robe, and the Priceless Pearl

五百弟子受記品第八・授學無學人記品第九

Pūrṇa receives a prediction unlike any given before it: the Buddha reveals that his foremost teacher of the Dharma has secretly been a bodhisattva all along, only appearing to be a śrāvaka. Five hundred arhats receive a single shared name for their future buddhahood. And the five hundred answer with a parable of their own — a poor man who spends years in hunger, never realizing a priceless jewel has been sewn into the lining of his own robe the whole time. The chapter closes with predictions for Ānanda, Rāhula, and two thousand more.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
The Buddha's prediction for Pūrṇa, revealing that he has been a bodhisattva all along, only appearing outwardly as a śrāvaka; the collective prediction given to Kauṇḍinya and five hundred arhats, all of whom will share the single future name Universal Brightness; the five hundred's own parable of a priceless pearl sewn secretly into a poor man's robe by a wealthy friend, unnoticed for years; and, in the chapter that follows, predictions for Ānanda, Rāhula, and two thousand disciples still in training and beyond it.
Why it matters
This is the parable Zhiyi drew on all the way back in Part 2 to explain what "skillful means" ultimately meant — not the truth dressed down for weaker minds, but the truth itself, worn in a form its wearer could not yet recognize. Here that image finally appears in the sūtra's own words, in the mouths of five hundred disciples describing exactly what happened to them.

1. Pūrṇa's Prediction

Chinese Source

爾時富樓那彌多羅尼子，從佛聞是智慧方便隨宜說法，又聞授諸大弟子阿耨多羅三藐三菩提記，復聞宿世因緣之事，復聞諸佛有大自在神通之力，得未曾有，心淨踊躍。即從座起，到於佛前，頭面禮足，却住一面，瞻仰尊顏目不暫捨，而作是念：「世尊甚奇特，所為希有！隨順世間若干種性，以方便知見而為說法，拔出眾生處處貪著。我等於佛功德，言不能宣，唯佛世尊能知我等深心本願。」 爾時佛告諸比丘：「汝等見是富樓那彌多羅尼子不？我常稱其於說法人中最為第一，亦常歎其種種功德，精勤護持助宣我法，能於四眾示教利喜，具足解釋佛之正法，而大饒益同梵行者。自捨如來，無能盡其言論之辯。汝等勿謂富樓那但能護持助宣我法，亦於過去九十億諸佛所，護持助宣佛之正法，於彼說法人中亦最第一。又於諸佛所說空法，明了通達，得四無礙智，常能審諦清淨說法，無有疑惑，具足菩薩神通之力。隨其壽命，常修梵行，彼佛世人咸皆謂之實是聲聞；而富樓那以斯方便，饒益無量百千眾生，又化無量阿僧祇人，令立阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。為淨佛土故，常作佛事，教化眾生。 「諸比丘！富樓那亦於七佛說法人中而得第一，今於我所說法人中亦為第一，於賢劫中當來諸佛，說法人中亦復第一，而皆護持助宣佛法。亦於未來，護持助宣無量無邊諸佛之法，教化饒益無量眾生，令立阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。為淨佛土故，常勤精進教化眾生，漸漸具足菩薩之道。過無量阿僧祇劫，當於此土，得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，號曰法明如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊。其佛以恒河沙等三千大千世界為一佛土，七寶為地，地平如掌，無有山陵谿澗溝壑，七寶臺觀充滿其中，諸天宮殿近處虛空，人天交接，兩得相見。無諸惡道，亦無女人，一切眾生，皆以化生，無有婬欲。得大神通，身出光明，飛行自在，志念堅固，精進智慧，普皆金色，三十二相而自莊嚴。其國眾生，常以二食：一者、法喜食，二者、禪悅食。有無量阿僧祇千萬億那由他諸菩薩眾，得大神通、四無礙智，善能教化眾生之類。其聲聞眾，算數校計所不能知，皆得具足六通、三明及八解脫。其佛國土有如是等無量功德莊嚴成就。劫名寶明，國名善淨。其佛壽命無量阿僧祇劫，法住甚久。佛滅度後，起七寶塔遍滿其國。」 爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「諸比丘諦聽！　佛子所行道， 善學方便故，　不可得思議。 知眾樂小法，　而畏於大智， 是故諸菩薩，　作聲聞緣覺， 以無數方便，　化諸眾生類。 自說是聲聞，　去佛道甚遠， 度脫無量眾，　皆悉得成就， 雖小欲懈怠，　漸當令作佛。 內祕菩薩行，　外現是聲聞， 少欲厭生死，　實自淨佛土。 示眾有三毒，　又現邪見相， 我弟子如是，　方便度眾生。 若我具足說，　種種現化事， 眾生聞是者，　心則懷疑惑。 今此富樓那，　於昔千億佛， 勤修所行道，　宣護諸佛法。 為求無上慧，　而於諸佛所， 現居弟子上，　多聞有智慧。 所說無所畏，　能令眾歡喜， 未曾有疲惓，　而以助佛事。 已度大神通，　具四無礙智， 知諸根利鈍，　常說清淨法。 演暢如是義，　教諸千億眾， 令住大乘法，　而自淨佛土。 未來亦供養，　無量無數佛， 護助宣正法，　亦自淨佛土。 常以諸方便，　說法無所畏， 度不可計眾，　成就一切智。 供養諸如來，　護持法寶藏， 其後得成佛，　號名曰法明。 其國名善淨，　七寶所合成， 劫名為寶明。　菩薩眾甚多， 其數無量億，　皆度大神通， 威德力具足，　充滿其國土。 聲聞亦無數，　三明八解脫， 得四無礙智，　以是等為僧。 其國諸眾生，　婬欲皆已斷， 純一變化生，　具相莊嚴身。 法喜禪悅食，　更無餘食想。 無有諸女人，　亦無諸惡道。 富樓那比丘，　功德悉成滿， 當得斯淨土，　賢聖眾甚多。 如是無量事，　我今但略說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇīputra, having heard from the Buddha this wisdom taught by skillful means according to what is fitting, and having heard as well the bestowal of predictions of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening on the great disciples, and having heard further the matter of their karmic connections from past lives, and having heard further of the buddhas' great, self-mastering spiritual power, gained what he had never had before, his heart pure and leaping with joy. He at once rose from his seat, went before the Buddha, bowed his head to the Buddha's feet, and withdrew to one side, gazing up at the Buddha's honored countenance without turning his eyes away, and thought to himself: "The World-Honored One is exceedingly extraordinary, and what he does is rare indeed! According to the every kind of nature found in the world, he teaches the Dharma by skillful means and knowledge and vision, drawing beings out from their attachments wherever they cling. We could never express in words the Buddha's own merit; only the Buddha, the World-Honored One, can know the deep wish at the root of our own minds." At that time the Buddha said to the monks: "Do you see this Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇīputra? I have always called him foremost among those who teach the Dharma, and have always praised his every kind of merit, his diligent upholding and proclaiming of my Dharma, his ability to teach, instruct, benefit, and delight the fourfold assembly, his complete explanation of the Buddha's true Dharma, and his great benefit to his fellow practitioners. Apart from the Tathāgata himself, none can match the full range of his eloquence. Do not suppose that Pūrṇa is able only to uphold and proclaim my own Dharma — in the past, in the presence of ninety koṭis of buddhas, he likewise upheld and proclaimed the true Dharma of those buddhas, and was likewise foremost among those who taught the Dharma there. He is, besides, clear and penetrating in the emptiness taught by every buddha, has attained the four unobstructed wisdoms, and is constantly able to teach the Dharma with careful precision and purity, without any doubt or uncertainty, complete with a bodhisattva's own power of spiritual penetration. Throughout his lifespan he has constantly cultivated pure conduct, so that the people of that buddha's world all took him to be truly a śrāvaka; yet Pūrṇa, by this very skillful means, benefited immeasurable hundreds of thousands of beings, and transformed immeasurable asaṃkhyeyas of people, causing them to establish themselves in unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. For the sake of purifying buddha-lands, he has constantly done the work of a buddha, teaching and transforming beings. "Monks! Pūrṇa was likewise foremost among those who taught the Dharma under each of the seven past buddhas, and is foremost among those who teach the Dharma under me now, and will likewise be foremost among those who teach the Dharma under the buddhas yet to come in this Fortunate Kalpa, upholding and proclaiming the Dharma of every one of them. In the future, too, he will uphold and proclaim the Dharma of immeasurable, boundless buddhas, teaching and benefiting immeasurable beings, causing them to establish themselves in unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. For the sake of purifying buddha-lands, he will constantly and diligently teach and transform beings, gradually becoming complete in the bodhisattva path. After immeasurable asaṃkhyeya kalpas, he shall, in this very land, attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, named Dharma Light Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One. That buddha will take, as a single buddha-land, a number of trichiliocosms equal to the sands of the Ganges; the ground will be of the seven treasures, level as the palm of a hand, without mountains, ridges, valleys, streams, or ravines, filled everywhere with towers and pavilions of the seven treasures; the palaces of the gods will stand near to the open sky, so that humans and gods intermingle, each able to see the other. There will be none of the evil destinies there, and no women; every being will be born by transformation, without any trace of sexual desire. They will attain great spiritual powers, their bodies emitting light, flying freely wherever they wish, their resolve firm, diligent and wise, every one of them golden in hue, adorned with the thirty-two marks. The beings of that land will constantly take two kinds of nourishment: the joy of the Dharma, and the delight of meditative concentration. There will be immeasurable asaṃkhyeya thousands of myriads of koṭis of nayutas of bodhisattvas there, who have attained great spiritual powers and the four unobstructed wisdoms, skillfully able to teach and transform every kind of being. Its śrāvakas will be beyond any count or calculation, all complete with the six spiritual powers, the three insights, and the eight liberations. That buddha-land will be adorned and accomplished with immeasurable merits such as these. The kalpa will be named Jeweled Light, and the land will be named Good Purity. That buddha's lifespan will be immeasurable asaṃkhyeya kalpas, and his Dharma will remain for a very long time. After that buddha's parinirvāṇa, stūpas of the seven treasures will be raised, filling the whole land." At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "Monks, listen carefully! The path a child of the Buddha practices, having skillfully learned every skillful means, is inconceivable. Knowing that beings delight in a lesser Dharma, and fear the great wisdom, bodhisattvas therefore appear as śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, transforming beings through countless skillful means. Saying of themselves that they are śrāvakas, far from the buddha-path, they deliver immeasurable beings across, every one of them brought to accomplishment; though these beings seem to have little desire and to be lax, the bodhisattvas gradually cause them to become buddhas. Secretly holding the practice of a bodhisattva, outwardly appearing as a śrāvaka, seeming to want little and to be weary of birth and death, they are in truth purifying their own buddha-land. They show the assembly that they have the three poisons, and manifest even the marks of wrong view — my disciples act in just this way, delivering beings across by skillful means. If I were to state in full every kind of transformation they manifest, beings hearing it would fall into doubt and confusion. Now this Pūrṇa, in the presence of thousands of koṭis of buddhas in the past, diligently practiced the path, proclaiming and upholding the Dharma of every buddha. Seeking the unsurpassed wisdom, in the presence of those buddhas he appeared foremost among their disciples, of broad learning and great wisdom. What he taught was fearless, and able to bring joy to the assembly; never once weary, he thereby aided the buddha's own work. Already having crossed over with great spiritual powers, complete with the four unobstructed wisdoms, knowing beings' faculties, sharp or dull, he constantly taught the pure Dharma. Expounding this same meaning, teaching thousands of koṭis of beings, causing them to abide in the Dharma of the Great Vehicle, he thereby purified his own buddha-land as well. In the future, too, he will make offerings to immeasurable, countless buddhas, upholding and proclaiming the true Dharma, likewise purifying his own buddha-land. Constantly using every skillful means, teaching the Dharma without fear, delivering incalculable beings across, he shall accomplish the wisdom of all modes. Having made offerings to every tathāgata, and upheld the treasury of the true Dharma, he shall afterward attain buddhahood, named Dharma Light. His land will be named Good Purity, formed entirely of the seven treasures; the kalpa will be named Jeweled Light. Its bodhisattvas will be exceedingly many, immeasurable koṭis in number, all having crossed over with great spiritual powers, complete with majesty and virtue, filling that land. Its śrāvakas will likewise be beyond number, complete with the three insights, the eight liberations, and the four unobstructed wisdoms, and these shall form the saṅgha. The beings of that land will have entirely cut off sexual desire, and will be born purely by transformation, their bodies adorned with every mark. Their food will be the joy of the Dharma and the delight of meditative concentration, with no thought of any other kind of food. There will be no women there, and none of the evil destinies. The monk Pūrṇa, his merit entirely fulfilled, shall attain this pure land, with a great multitude of sages and worthies. Such immeasurable matters as these I now speak of only in brief."

Reading note

Every prediction so far in this sūtra has followed the same underlying shape: a disciple who genuinely was a śrāvaka, and who will genuinely become a bodhisattva and then a buddha, moving forward from one stage to the next. Pūrṇa's prediction introduces something different. The Buddha states plainly that Pūrṇa has "secretly held the practice of a bodhisattva, outwardly appearing as a śrāvaka" — not a disciple advancing toward bodhisattvahood, but one who was already a bodhisattva the entire time, deliberately performing the role of a śrāvaka as his own chosen skillful means, so effectively that "the people of that buddha's world all took him to be truly a śrāvaka." The text does not say how many of the other śrāvakas named throughout this sūtra might belong to this same category — it makes the claim only for Pūrṇa, by name, and leaves the question open for everyone else.

2. Kauṇḍinya and the Five Hundred: One Name for All

Chinese Source

爾時千二百阿羅漢，心自在者，作是念：「我等歡喜，得未曾有。若世尊各見授記，如餘大弟子者，不亦快乎！」佛知此等心之所念，告摩訶迦葉：「是千二百阿羅漢，我今當現前次第與授阿耨多羅三藐三菩提記。於此眾中，我大弟子憍陳如比丘，當供養六萬二千億佛，然後得成為佛，號曰普明如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊。其五百阿羅漢：優樓頻螺迦葉、伽耶迦葉、那提迦葉、迦留陀夷、優陀夷、阿㝹樓馱、離婆多、劫賓那、薄拘羅、周陀、莎伽陀等，皆當得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，盡同一號，名曰普明。」 爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「憍陳如比丘，　當見無量佛， 過阿僧祇劫，　乃成等正覺。 常放大光明，　具足諸神通， 名聞遍十方，　一切之所敬， 常說無上道，　故號為普明。 其國土清淨，　菩薩皆勇猛， 咸昇妙樓閣，　遊諸十方國， 以無上供具，　奉獻於諸佛。 作是供養已，　心懷大歡喜， 須臾還本國，　有如是神力。 佛壽六萬劫，　正法住倍壽， 像法復倍是，　法滅天人憂。 其五百比丘，　次第當作佛， 同號曰普明，　轉次而授記。 我滅度之後，　某甲當作佛， 其所化世間，　亦如我今日。 國土之嚴淨，　及諸神通力， 菩薩聲聞眾，　正法及像法， 壽命劫多少，　皆如上所說。 迦葉汝已知，　五百自在者， 餘諸聲聞眾，　亦當復如是。 其不在此會，　汝當為宣說。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the twelve hundred arhats whose minds had attained mastery thought to themselves: "We rejoice, having gained what we never had before. If the World-Honored One were to bestow a prediction on each of us, just as he has for the other great disciples, would this not be a joy!" The Buddha, knowing what was in their minds, said to Mahākāśyapa: "These twelve hundred arhats — I shall now, before you, bestow on them, one after another, a prediction of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. Among this assembly, my great disciple the monk Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya shall make offerings to sixty-two thousand koṭis of buddhas, and shall afterward attain buddhahood, named Universal Brightness Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One. And the five hundred arhats — Uruvilvā-Kāśyapa, Gayā-Kāśyapa, Nadī-Kāśyapa, Kālodāyin, Udāyin, Aniruddha, Revata, Kapphiṇa, Vakkula, Cunda, Svāgata, and the rest — shall all likewise attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, every one sharing the same single name, Universal Brightness." At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "The monk Kauṇḍinya shall see immeasurable buddhas, and, after asaṃkhyeya kalpas, shall accomplish perfect and complete awakening. Constantly emitting great light, complete with every spiritual power, his renown shall fill the ten directions, revered by all, constantly teaching the unsurpassed path — and for this he shall be named Universal Brightness. His land shall be pure, its bodhisattvas all valiant, ascending together to wondrous towers, roaming through every land of the ten directions, offering unsurpassed gifts to every buddha. Having made these offerings, their hearts filled with great joy, they shall return, in but a moment, to their own land — such shall be their spiritual power. That buddha's lifespan shall be sixty thousand kalpas; the true Dharma shall remain twice that span, and the counterfeit Dharma twice that again; and when the Dharma at last comes to an end, gods and humans shall grieve. His five hundred monks shall, one after another, likewise attain buddhahood, all sharing the same name, Universal Brightness, each in turn receiving this same prediction as the one before him passes it on. 'After my parinirvāṇa, so-and-so shall become a buddha, and the world he transforms shall be just as mine is today.' The adornment and purity of their lands, and their every spiritual power, their bodhisattvas and śrāvakas, the span of their true and counterfeit Dharma, the length of their lifespans — all shall be just as already described above. Kāśyapa, you now know of these five hundred whose minds have attained mastery; the rest of the śrāvakas here shall likewise be just the same. For those not present in this assembly, you should proclaim this to them."

Reading note

Every prediction from Śāriputra's onward has given its recipient a distinct name, land, and kalpa of his own. Here, for the first time, five hundred disciples — beginning with Kauṇḍinya, the very first person to attain arhatship at Vārāṇasī in Part 3's account of the first turning of the wheel — receive not five hundred separate identities but a single shared one: "all sharing the same single name, Universal Brightness," one after another handing the same prediction to the next as each in turn attains it. Where earlier chapters demonstrated the one vehicle through parable — different-sized plants under one rain, different carts converging on one — this passage demonstrates it through a plainer device: giving five hundred individuals, quite literally, the same future name.

3. The Parable of the Priceless Pearl

Chinese Source

爾時五百阿羅漢於佛前得受記已，歡喜踊躍，即從座起，到於佛前，頭面禮足，悔過自責：「世尊！我等常作是念，自謂已得究竟滅度，今乃知之，如無智者。所以者何？我等應得如來智慧，而便自以小智為足。 「世尊！譬如有人至親友家，醉酒而臥。是時親友官事當行，以無價寶珠繫其衣裏，與之而去。其人醉臥，都不覺知。起已遊行，到於他國。為衣食故，勤力求索，甚大艱難；若少有所得，便以為足。於後親友會遇見之，而作是言：『咄哉，丈夫！何為衣食乃至如是。我昔欲令汝得安樂、五欲自恣，於某年日月，以無價寶珠繫汝衣裏。今故現在，而汝不知。勤苦憂惱，以求自活，甚為癡也。汝今可以此寶貿易所須，常可如意，無所乏短。』 「佛亦如是，為菩薩時，教化我等，令發一切智心。而尋廢忘，不知不覺。既得阿羅漢道，自謂滅度，資生艱難，得少為足。一切智願，猶在不失。今者世尊覺悟我等，作如是言：『諸比丘！汝等所得，非究竟滅。我久令汝等種佛善根，以方便故，示涅槃相，而汝謂為實得滅度。』 「世尊！我今乃知實是菩薩，得受阿耨多羅三藐三菩提記。以是因緣，甚大歡喜，得未曾有。」 爾時阿若憍陳如等，欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「我等聞無上，　安隱授記聲， 歡喜未曾有，　禮無量智佛。 今於世尊前，　自悔諸過咎， 於無量佛寶，　得少涅槃分， 如無智愚人，　便自以為足。 譬如貧窮人，　往至親友家， 其家甚大富，　具設諸餚饍， 以無價寶珠，　繫著內衣裏， 默與而捨去，　時臥不覺知。 是人既已起，　遊行詣他國， 求衣食自濟，　資生甚艱難， 得少便為足，　更不願好者。 不覺內衣裏，　有無價寶珠。 與珠之親友，　後見此貧人， 苦切責之已，　示以所繫珠。 貧人見此珠，　其心大歡喜， 富有諸財物，　五欲而自恣。 我等亦如是，　世尊於長夜， 常愍見教化，　令種無上願。 我等無智故，　不覺亦不知， 得少涅槃分，　自足不求餘。 今佛覺悟我，　言非實滅度， 得佛無上慧，　爾乃為真滅。 我今從佛聞，　授記莊嚴事， 及轉次受決，　身心遍歡喜。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the five hundred arhats, having received this prediction before the Buddha, leapt with joy, and at once rose from their seats, went before the Buddha, bowed their heads to his feet, and, repenting and reproaching themselves, said: "World-Honored One! We have constantly thought to ourselves that we had already attained the ultimate extinction; only now do we realize that this was the thinking of one without wisdom. Why is this so? Because we should rightfully have attained the Tathāgata's own wisdom, yet we contented ourselves instead with a lesser wisdom. "World-Honored One! It is like a man who went to the house of a close friend, and, having drunk wine, lay down and fell asleep. At that time his friend, having to leave on official business, sewed a priceless jewel into the lining of the man's robe, and left, giving it to him without his knowing. The man, drunk and asleep, was entirely unaware of it. When he awoke, he wandered off, and came at last to another land. For the sake of clothing and food, he labored and sought with great difficulty; whatever little he managed to gain, he considered enough. Later, his friend happened to meet him again, and said: 'Ah, my good man! Why have you come to such a state, for the sake of mere clothing and food? Long ago, wishing you to find peace and happiness, and to enjoy yourself freely with the five desires, on a certain day of a certain year I sewed a priceless jewel into the lining of your robe. It is still there, right now, and yet you do not know it. Toiling and suffering to keep yourself alive — this is a great folly indeed. You may now exchange this jewel for whatever you need, and always have exactly what you wish, with nothing lacking at all.' "The Buddha is likewise so. When he was a bodhisattva, he taught and transformed us, causing us to give rise to the mind of the wisdom of all modes. Yet we soon forgot and set this aside, unknowing and unaware of it. Having attained the path of the arhat, we supposed ourselves to have reached extinction, our means of livelihood difficult, and were content with the little we had. And yet our original vow toward the wisdom of all modes still remained, and was never lost. Now the World-Honored One has awakened us, saying: 'Monks! What you have attained is not the ultimate extinction. I long ago caused you to plant the good roots of buddhahood, and, by skillful means, showed you the mark of nirvāṇa — yet you supposed that you had truly attained extinction.' "World-Honored One! Only now do we know that we are truly bodhisattvas, and have received a prediction of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. By this cause and condition, we rejoice greatly, having gained what we never had before." At that time Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya and the others, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "We have heard the unsurpassed, peaceful voice of this prediction, and rejoice, having gained what we never had before; we bow before the buddha of immeasurable wisdom. Now, before the World-Honored One, we repent our own past faults: amid the immeasurable treasure of the Buddha's own Dharma, we attained only a small portion of nirvāṇa, and, like foolish people without wisdom, considered it enough of ourselves. It is like a destitute man who went to the house of a close friend, a house of very great wealth, where a full feast was laid out for him; his friend sewed a priceless jewel into the lining of his robe, and silently gave it to him and departed, while he lay sleeping, unaware. This man, once he had risen, wandered off to another land, seeking clothing and food to sustain himself, his livelihood exceedingly hard; whatever little he gained, he considered enough, and wished for nothing better. He did not know that within the lining of his own robe there lay a priceless jewel. The friend who had given him the jewel later met this destitute man again, and, having reproached him sharply, showed him the jewel that had been sewn there. The destitute man, seeing this jewel, was filled with great joy in his heart, now rich with every kind of wealth, free to enjoy himself with the five desires. We too are just the same: the World-Honored One, through the long night of saṃsāra, constantly took pity on us and taught and transformed us, causing us to plant the seed of the unsurpassed vow. Yet, through our own lack of wisdom, we did not perceive it and did not know it; attaining only a small portion of nirvāṇa, we were content with ourselves, and sought nothing further. Now the Buddha has awakened us, telling us that this was not the true extinction, and that only in attaining the Buddha's own unsurpassed wisdom is this the true extinction. Now, hearing from the Buddha of the adornments of this prediction, and of each of us receiving it in turn, our bodies and minds are filled entirely with joy."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

This is the parable this site's commentary anticipated all the way back in Part 2, when Zhiyi's own gloss on the title "skillful means" reached for exactly this image, several chapters before the sūtra itself told the story: a priceless pearl sewn secretly into the lining of a poor man's robe, offered there as evidence that skillful means was never a lesser truth dressed down for a weaker audience, but the same one truth, worn in a form its wearer could not yet recognize. Now the sūtra makes the same point in its own words, and it is worth noticing exactly where the parable places the fault, since it is nowhere the wealthy friend. The jewel was real and present the entire time; the poor man's years of hunger were not a punishment or a deception practiced on him, but simply what happens when a gift already given is not yet known to have been received. The five hundred arhats' own reading of themselves follows this same shape precisely: their vow toward buddhahood, planted lifetimes ago, "still remained, and was never lost" even through every year they believed themselves finished — the jewel does not need to be re-given at the moment of recognition. It only needs, finally, to be found.

4. Ānanda and Rāhula: Two Predictions Among Family

Chinese Source

爾時阿難、羅睺羅而作是念：「我等每自思惟：『設得受記，不亦快乎。』」即從座起，到於佛前，頭面禮足，俱白佛言：「世尊！我等於此亦應有分，唯有如來，我等所歸。又我等為一切世間天、人、阿修羅所見知識——阿難常為侍者，護持法藏；羅睺羅是佛之子——若佛見授阿耨多羅三藐三菩提記者，我願既滿，眾望亦足。」爾時，學、無學聲聞弟子二千人，皆從座起，偏袒右肩，到於佛前，一心合掌，瞻仰世尊，如阿難、羅睺羅所願，住立一面。 爾時佛告阿難：「汝於來世當得作佛，號山海慧自在通王如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊。當供養六十二億諸佛，護持法藏，然後得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。教化二十千萬億恒河沙諸菩薩等，令成阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。國名常立勝幡，其土清淨，琉璃為地。劫名妙音遍滿。其佛壽命，無量千萬億阿僧祇劫，若人於千萬億無量阿僧祇劫中算數校計，不能得知。正法住世倍於壽命，像法住世復倍正法。阿難！是山海慧自在通王佛，為十方無量千萬億恒河沙等諸佛如來所共讚歎，稱其功德。」 爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「我今僧中說，　阿難持法者， 當供養諸佛，　然後成正覺， 號曰：山海慧　自在通王佛。 其國土清淨，　名常立勝幡， 教化諸菩薩，　其數如恒沙。 佛有大威德，　名聞滿十方。 壽命無有量，　以愍眾生故， 正法倍壽命，　像法復倍是。 如恒河沙等、　無數諸眾生， 於此佛法中，　種佛道因緣。」 爾時會中新發意菩薩八千人，咸作是念：「我等尚不聞諸大菩薩得如是記，有何因緣而諸聲聞得如是決？」 爾時世尊知諸菩薩心之所念，而告之曰：「諸善男子！我與阿難等，於空王佛所，同時發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。阿難常樂多聞，我常勤精進，是故我已得成阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，而阿難護持我法，亦護將來諸佛法藏，教化成就諸菩薩眾，其本願如是，故獲斯記。」 阿難面於佛前，自聞授記及國土莊嚴，所願具足，心大歡喜，得未曾有。即時憶念過去無量千萬億諸佛法藏，通達無礙，如今所聞，亦識本願。 爾時阿難而說偈言： 「世尊甚希有，　令我念過去， 無量諸佛法，　如今日所聞。 我今無復疑，　安住於佛道， 方便為侍者，　護持諸佛法。」 爾時佛告羅睺羅：「汝於來世當得作佛，號蹈七寶華如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊。當供養十世界微塵等數諸佛如來，常為諸佛而作長子，猶如今也。是蹈七寶華佛，國土莊嚴，壽命劫數，所化弟子，正法、像法，亦如山海慧自在通王如來無異，亦為此佛而作長子。過是已後，當得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。」 爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「我為太子時，　羅睺為長子， 我今成佛道，　受法為法子。 於未來世中，　見無量億佛， 皆為其長子，　一心求佛道。 羅睺羅密行，　唯我能知之， 現為我長子，　以示諸眾生。 無量億千萬，　功德不可數， 安住於佛法，　以求無上道。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Ānanda and Rāhula thought to themselves: "We have each constantly reflected: would it not be a joy, if we too were to receive a prediction?" They at once rose from their seats, went before the Buddha, bowed their heads to his feet, and together said to him: "World-Honored One! We too should have a share in this; the Tathāgata alone is our refuge. And we are, besides, well known to every god, human, and asura in the whole world — Ānanda has constantly served as your attendant, upholding the treasury of the Dharma; Rāhula is the Buddha's own son. If the Buddha would bestow on us a prediction of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, our own wish would be fulfilled, and the hopes of the whole assembly satisfied as well." At that time the two thousand śrāvaka disciples, whether still in training or beyond it, all rose from their seats, bared their right shoulders, went before the Buddha, joined their palms with one mind, and, gazing up at the World-Honored One, just as Ānanda and Rāhula had wished, stood to one side. At that time the Buddha said to Ānanda: "In a future age you shall attain buddhahood, named Mountain-Sea-Wisdom, Self-Mastering, Penetrating King Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One. You shall make offerings to sixty-two koṭis of buddhas, uphold the treasury of the Dharma, and only afterward attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. You shall teach and transform twenty thousand myriad koṭis of bodhisattvas, as many as the sands of the Ganges, causing them to accomplish unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. Your land will be named Ever-Raised Victory-Banner, its ground pure, of lapis lazuli. The kalpa will be named Wondrous Sound Filling Everywhere. That buddha's lifespan shall be immeasurable thousands of myriads of koṭis of asaṃkhyeya kalpas — were a person to count and calculate it for thousands of myriads of immeasurable asaṃkhyeya kalpas, he could not reach its number. The true Dharma shall remain in the world twice the span of his lifespan, and the counterfeit Dharma shall remain twice the span of the true Dharma again. Ānanda! This Buddha Mountain-Sea-Wisdom, Self-Mastering, Penetrating King shall be praised together by the buddha-tathāgatas of the ten directions, immeasurable thousands of myriads of koṭis of them, as many as the sands of the Ganges, who shall proclaim his merit." At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "I now declare, in the midst of the saṅgha, that Ānanda, upholder of the Dharma, shall make offerings to the buddhas, and only afterward accomplish perfect awakening, named the Buddha Mountain-Sea-Wisdom, Self-Mastering, Penetrating King. His land shall be pure, named Ever-Raised Victory-Banner, and he shall teach and transform bodhisattvas whose number is like the sands of the Ganges. That buddha shall have great majesty and virtue, his renown filling the ten directions. His lifespan shall be without measure, for out of pity for beings; the true Dharma shall last twice his lifespan, and the counterfeit Dharma twice that again. Beings as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, countless in number, shall, within this buddha's Dharma, plant the cause and condition of the buddha-path." At that time the eight thousand newly-resolved bodhisattvas in the assembly all thought to themselves: "We ourselves have not yet heard of the great bodhisattvas receiving a prediction such as this — for what cause and condition do these śrāvakas receive such a prophecy?" At that time the World-Honored One, knowing what was in the bodhisattvas' minds, said to them: "Good sons! Ānanda and I, in the presence of the Buddha Empty King, gave rise, at the same time, to the resolve for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. Ānanda constantly delighted in broad learning, while I constantly applied myself with diligence; and for this reason I have already accomplished unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, while Ānanda upholds my own Dharma, and shall likewise uphold the treasuries of the Dharma of buddhas yet to come, teaching and bringing to accomplishment the assembly of bodhisattvas. Such was his original vow, and for this reason he has received this prediction." Ānanda, before the Buddha, hearing in person this prediction and the adornments of his future land, his own wish now fulfilled, was filled with great joy in his heart, having gained what he never had before. At that very moment he recalled the treasuries of the Dharma of immeasurable thousands of myriads of koṭis of buddhas in the past, penetrating them without obstruction, as clearly as what he had just now heard, and recognized as well his own original vow. At that time Ānanda spoke in verse: "The World-Honored One is exceedingly rare, causing me to recall the immeasurable Dharma of buddhas past, as clearly as what I hear today. I now have no more doubt, and abide securely in the buddha-path; by skillful means I became your attendant, upholding the Dharma of every buddha." At that time the Buddha said to Rāhula: "In a future age you shall attain buddhahood, named Treading-on-Seven-Jeweled-Flowers Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One. You shall make offerings to buddha-tathāgatas as numerous as the fine dust motes of ten worlds, and shall constantly be the eldest son of each of those buddhas, just as you are now. This Buddha Treading-on-Seven-Jeweled-Flowers — his land's adornments, his lifespan and kalpas, the disciples he transforms, and the span of his true and counterfeit Dharma, shall all be no different from those of the Tathāgata Mountain-Sea-Wisdom, Self-Mastering, Penetrating King; and you shall likewise be the eldest son of that buddha as well. After this, you shall attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening." At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "When I was a prince, Rāhula was my eldest son; now that I have accomplished the buddha-path, he has received my Dharma and become my son in the Dharma. In ages to come, he shall see immeasurable koṭis of buddhas, and shall be the eldest son to every one of them, single-mindedly seeking the buddha-path. Rāhula's own hidden practice, I alone am able to know it; he now appears as my eldest son, to show this to every being. His merits, immeasurable koṭis of thousands of myriads, are beyond counting, and he abides securely in the Buddha's own Dharma, seeking the unsurpassed path."

Reading note

The eight thousand newly-resolved bodhisattvas' puzzlement is worth taking seriously rather than reading past: from their vantage point, śrāvakas receiving individual predictions on a par with advanced bodhisattvas seems to require some explanation, and the Buddha gives them one that is notably ordinary. He and Ānanda took the same vow, at the same moment, under the same ancient buddha — the difference between them was never one of goal but of temperament: "Ānanda constantly delighted in broad learning, while I constantly applied myself with diligence." Diligence brought one to buddhahood first; a love of learning suited the other to the specific, essential work of hearing, remembering, and later transmitting everything the Buddha taught. Neither vocation is presented as the lesser one, and the prediction rewards the second exactly as fully as the first arrived at buddhahood itself.

Rāhula's prediction is framed differently again — not through shared vow or scholarly labor, but through the ordinary fact of being someone's son, and the sūtra makes the parallel explicit: as Rāhula was Śākyamuni's eldest son "when I was a prince," so, life after life to come, he will be the eldest son of buddha after buddha, "received my Dharma and become my son in the Dharma." His own "hidden practice" (密行) is named but not detailed, described as something only the Buddha can fully know — one more instance, alongside Pūrṇa's revealed bodhisattvahood earlier in this chapter, of the sūtra suggesting there is more happening beneath a familiar disciple's visible role than the assembly around him has been able to see.

5. The Two Thousand: A Shared Name

Chinese Source

爾時世尊見學、無學二千人，其意柔軟，寂然清淨，一心觀佛。佛告阿難：「汝見是學、無學二千人不？」 「唯然，已見。」 「阿難！是諸人等，當供養五十世界微塵數諸佛如來，恭敬尊重，護持法藏。末後同時於十方國各得成佛，皆同一號，名曰寶相如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊。壽命一劫。國土莊嚴，聲聞、菩薩，正法、像法、皆悉同等。」 爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「是二千聲聞，　今於我前住， 悉皆與授記，　未來當成佛。 所供養諸佛，　如上說塵數， 護持其法藏，　後當成正覺。 各於十方國，　悉同一名號， 俱時坐道場，　以證無上慧， 皆名為寶相。　國土及弟子， 正法與像法，　悉等無有異。 咸以諸神通，　度十方眾生， 名聞普周遍，　漸入於涅槃。」 爾時學、無學二千人，聞佛授記，歡喜踊躍，而說偈言： 「世尊慧燈明，　我聞授記音， 心歡喜充滿，　如甘露見灌。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One saw the two thousand śrāvakas, whether still in training or beyond it, their minds gentle and pliant, tranquil and pure, gazing at the Buddha with one mind. The Buddha said to Ānanda: "Do you see these two thousand, in training and beyond it?" "Yes, I see them." "Ānanda! These people shall make offerings to buddha-tathāgatas as numerous as the fine dust motes of fifty worlds, honoring and revering them, upholding the treasury of the Dharma. At the very end, at one and the same time, in lands throughout the ten directions, each shall attain buddhahood, all sharing the same single name, Jeweled Mark Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One. Their lifespan shall be one kalpa. Their lands' adornments, their śrāvakas and bodhisattvas, and the span of their true and counterfeit Dharma, shall all be entirely the same." At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "These two thousand śrāvakas, now standing before me, I now bestow on every one of them a prediction: in the future, they shall become buddhas. The buddhas they shall make offerings to are as numerous as the dust motes already described; upholding the treasury of that Dharma, they shall afterward accomplish perfect awakening. Each in a land of the ten directions, all sharing one and the same name, they shall together, at one time, sit at the seat of enlightenment, and realize the unsurpassed wisdom, every one named Jeweled Mark. Their lands and their disciples, their true Dharma and their counterfeit Dharma, shall all be equal, with no difference between them. Every one of them, by every kind of spiritual power, shall deliver beings of the ten directions across, their renown spreading everywhere, and shall gradually enter nirvāṇa." At that time the two thousand, in training and beyond it, hearing the Buddha's prediction, leapt with joy, and spoke in verse: "The World-Honored One, lamp of wisdom, bright and shining — hearing the voice of this prediction, our hearts are filled with joy, as though bathed in sweet dew."

Reading note

The pattern first introduced with Kauṇḍinya's five hundred is carried further here. Where the five hundred shared a single future name attained one after another, in succession, these two thousand are told they will attain buddhahood "at one and the same time," in different lands throughout the ten directions, under the identical name, with identical lands, disciples, and duration of Dharma "with no difference between them." It is the most complete statement of interchangeability the sūtra has offered for any group of individuals — and it closes, notably, not with the two thousand's own reflection on what this means, as the five hundred offered with the parable of the pearl, but simply with their joy. After two full chapters devoted to prediction after prediction, the sūtra allows this final one to stand without further explanation, as though the pattern by now speaks for itself.
