The Buddha's Hesitation, and the Turning of the Wheel

方便品第二（下）

In one long closing verse, the Buddha restates everything just declared in prose — and then tells a story he has never told this assembly before. Beneath the bodhi tree, newly awakened, he nearly decided not to teach at all, certain that beings mired in ignorance could never receive what he had found. Brahmā and the gods entreat him; the buddhas of the ten directions console him; and he goes to Vārāṇasī, turns the wheel of the Dharma for five ascetics, and — with this fascicle's closing line — declares that the twelve hundred arhats seated before him now will, every one of them, become buddhas.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
The Buddha's verse restatement of the previous chapter's declaration — his original vow to make all beings equal to himself, the nine divisions of his teaching, and the sole truth of the one vehicle; an extended passage on how even the smallest devotional acts, including a child's game of piling sand into a stūpa, are already causes for buddhahood; the Buddha's own account of nearly declining to teach at all beneath the bodhi tree; Brahmā's entreaty and the ten-direction buddhas' encouragement; the turning of the Dharma-wheel at Vārāṇasī for five ascetics; and the closing declaration that all twelve hundred arhats present will become buddhas.
Why it matters
This is the only place in the sūtra where the Buddha narrates his own uncertainty in the first person — not as a rhetorical device for the assembly's benefit, but as something he says he actually considered: simply not teaching, and passing at once into nirvāṇa. What follows it is this fascicle's answer to why he taught the three vehicles instead of staying silent, and why that was never a lesser truth than the one now being unfolded.

1. The Nine Kinds of Teaching, and the One Vehicle That Is True

Chinese Source

「比丘比丘尼，　有懷增上慢， 優婆塞我慢，　優婆夷不信， 如是四眾等，　其數有五千， 不自見其過，　於戒有缺漏， 護惜其瑕疵。　是小智已出， 眾中之糟糠，　佛威德故去， 斯人尠福德，　不堪受是法。 此眾無枝葉，　唯有諸貞實。 舍利弗善聽！　諸佛所得法， 無量方便力，　而為眾生說。 眾生心所念，　種種所行道， 若干諸欲性，　先世善惡業， 佛悉知是已，　以諸緣譬喻、 言辭方便力，　令一切歡喜。 或說修多羅、　伽陀及本事、 本生未曾有，　亦說於因緣、 譬喻并祇夜、　優波提舍經。 鈍根樂小法，　貪著於生死， 於諸無量佛，　不行深妙道， 眾苦所惱亂，　為是說涅槃。 我設是方便，　令得入佛慧， 未曾說汝等，　當得成佛道。 所以未曾說，　說時未至故， 今正是其時，　決定說大乘。 我此九部法，　隨順眾生說， 入大乘為本，　以故說是經。 有佛子心淨，　柔軟亦利根， 無量諸佛所，　而行深妙道， 為此諸佛子，　說是大乘經， 我記如是人，　來世成佛道， 以深心念佛，　修持淨戒故。 此等聞得佛，　大喜充遍身， 佛知彼心行，　故為說大乘。 聲聞若菩薩，　聞我所說法， 乃至於一偈，　皆成佛無疑。 十方佛土中，　唯有一乘法， 無二亦無三，　除佛方便說， 但以假名字，　引導於眾生， 說佛智慧故，　諸佛出於世。 唯此一事實，　餘二則非真， 終不以小乘，　濟度於眾生。 佛自住大乘，　如其所得法， 定慧力莊嚴，　以此度眾生。 自證無上道，　大乘平等法， 若以小乘化，　乃至於一人， 我則墮慳貪，　此事為不可。 若人信歸佛，　如來不欺誑， 亦無貪嫉意，　斷諸法中惡， 故佛於十方，　而獨無所畏。 我以相嚴身，　光明照世間， 無量眾所尊，　為說實相印。 舍利弗當知，　我本立誓願， 欲令一切眾，　如我等無異。 如我昔所願，　今者已滿足， 化一切眾生，　皆令入佛道。 若我遇眾生，　盡教以佛道， 無智者錯亂，　迷惑不受教。 我知此眾生，　未曾修善本， 堅著於五欲，　癡愛故生惱， 以諸欲因緣，　墜墮三惡道， 輪迴六趣中，　備受諸苦毒， 受胎之微形，　世世常增長。 薄德少福人，　眾苦所逼迫， 入邪見稠林，　若有若無等， 依止此諸見，　具足六十二， 深著虛妄法，　堅受不可捨， 我慢自矜高，　諂曲心不實， 於千萬億劫，　不聞佛名字， 亦不聞正法，　如是人難度。 是故舍利弗！　我為設方便， 說諸盡苦道，　示之以涅槃， 我雖說涅槃，　是亦非真滅， 諸法從本來，　常自寂滅相。 佛子行道已，　來世得作佛， 我有方便力，　開示三乘法， 一切諸世尊，　皆說一乘道， 今此諸大眾，　皆應除疑惑， 諸佛語無異，　唯一無二乘。 過去無數劫，　無量滅度佛， 百千萬億種，　其數不可量， 如是諸世尊，　種種緣譬喻， 無數方便力，　演說諸法相， 是諸世尊等，　皆說一乘法， 化無量眾生，　令入於佛道。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Monks and nuns who harbored arrogance, laymen with self-conceit, laywomen without faith — of these four assemblies, five thousand in number, who did not see their own faults, who had gaps and flaws in their discipline, and guarded their own blemishes closely — these, of little wisdom, have now gone out, the husks and chaff of the assembly, departing by the Buddha's own majestic power. Such people, of scant merit, could not bear to receive this Dharma. This assembly now has no more branches or leaves; there is only what is solid and true. Śāriputra, listen well! The Dharma the buddhas have attained, they teach to beings by immeasurable power of skillful means. Whatever beings have in mind, whatever paths they follow, whatever desires and natures they have, whatever good or evil karma from former lives — the Buddha, knowing all of this in full, uses causes and conditions, parables, words, and the power of skillful means to bring joy to everyone. He teaches sūtras, verses, accounts of past lives, birth-stories, and marvels; he also teaches by way of causes and conditions, parables, restated verses, and discourses of explanation. For those of dull faculties who delight in a lesser Dharma, who are attached to birth and death, who have not practiced the profound and subtle path in the presence of immeasurable buddhas, and who are troubled and afflicted by every suffering, he teaches nirvāṇa. I have established this skillful means so that they might come to enter the Buddha's wisdom; I have never yet said that you yourselves would attain the buddha-path. The reason I had not yet said it is that the time to speak of it had not yet arrived; now is exactly that time, and I shall now decisively teach the Great Vehicle. These nine divisions of my teaching I have taught in accordance with beings' own natures; entering the Great Vehicle is their foundation, and for this reason I now teach this sūtra. There are children of the Buddha whose minds are pure, gentle, and of sharp faculty, who in the presence of immeasurable buddhas have practiced the profound and subtle path; for these children of the Buddha I teach this sūtra of the Great Vehicle, and I predict that people such as these will, in ages to come, attain the buddha-path, because with a deep mind they are mindful of the Buddha and cultivate and uphold pure precepts. These, hearing that they will attain buddhahood, are filled through their whole bodies with great joy; the Buddha, knowing the conduct of their minds, therefore teaches them the Great Vehicle. Whether śrāvaka or bodhisattva, whoever hears the Dharma I teach — even a single verse of it — will without doubt become a buddha. In the buddha-lands of the ten directions, there is only the Dharma of the one vehicle — there is no second, and no third, except when a buddha speaks of them as skillful means. Only by way of provisional names does he guide beings; it is in order to teach the Buddha's wisdom that the buddhas appear in the world. Only this one thing is true; the other two are not real. Never would he use a lesser vehicle to ferry beings across. The Buddha himself abides in the Great Vehicle, adorned by the concentration and wisdom of the Dharma he has attained, and by this he ferries beings across. Having himself realized the unsurpassed path, the equal and impartial Dharma of the Great Vehicle — if he were to convert even a single person with a lesser vehicle, he would fall into stinginess and greed; such a thing cannot be. If a person believes in and takes refuge in the Buddha, the Tathāgata does not deceive him, nor does he harbor any thought of greed or envy — he has cut off every evil among all things. Therefore the Buddha, throughout the ten directions, is alone without any fear. I adorn my body with the marks of a buddha, and my light illuminates the world; honored by immeasurable multitudes, I teach for them the seal of the true mark. Śāriputra, you should know: I originally made a vow, wishing to cause every being to become no different from myself. As I vowed long ago, so it is now fulfilled: I convert every being, causing all of them to enter the buddha-path. If, whenever I encounter beings, I were to teach them all the buddha-path at once, those without wisdom would become confused, bewildered, and unable to receive the teaching. I know that such beings have never yet cultivated good roots; firmly attached to the five desires, they suffer affliction born of ignorance and craving. Through the causes and conditions of their desires, they fall into the three evil destinies, revolving through the six paths, undergoing every bitter suffering — taking on the tiny form of an embryo, life after life growing again and again. People of scant virtue and little merit, pressed and driven by every suffering, enter the dense forest of wrong views — views of existence, of non-existence, and the like. Relying on these views, sixty-two in full, deeply attached to false and empty teachings, holding to them firmly and unable to let go, arrogant and self-exalting, flattering and crooked, their minds untrue — for thousands of myriads of koṭis of kalpas they do not hear even the Buddha's name, nor do they hear the true Dharma; people such as these are hard to bring across. For this reason, Śāriputra, I have established skillful means, teaching the paths that exhaust suffering, and showing them nirvāṇa. Though I speak of nirvāṇa, this too is not a true extinction; all phenomena, from the very beginning, are always themselves marked by quiescent cessation. Once a child of the Buddha has practiced the path, in a future age he will become a buddha; I have the power of skillful means, and so I open and reveal the teaching of the three vehicles. Every world-honored one alike teaches the path of the one vehicle; this whole great assembly here should now remove every doubt — the words of the buddhas are never different from one another; there is only the one, and no second vehicle. In past ages, countless kalpas ago, immeasurable buddhas entered parinirvāṇa — hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of them, their number beyond reckoning. Such world-honored ones as these, through every kind of cause and parable, through countless powers of skillful means, expounded the marks of all things. All these world-honored ones alike taught the Dharma of the one vehicle, converting immeasurable beings and causing them to enter the buddha-path."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Nothing doctrinally new is announced in this section — it restates, in verse, exactly what Part 2 already declared in prose: the one vehicle, the vow, the reason three were taught as skillful means. On Zhiyi's account of the "three rounds" of teaching introduced in the previous part, both the prose and this verse belong together to the single "Dharma round" (法說一周) addressed to the sharpest faculties — Śāriputra hears the same claim twice, once stated and once sung, before the sūtra moves on to the parable that a less advanced listener will need. The one line worth pausing on for what it adds rather than repeats is the vow: "I originally made a vow, wishing to cause every being to become no different from myself" (我本立誓願，欲令一切眾，如我等無異) — followed at once by "as I vowed long ago, so it is now fulfilled." Everything argued so far as doctrine is here reframed, briefly, as a promise already kept.

2. Even a Child's Game of Sand

Chinese Source

「又諸大聖主，　知一切世間， 天人群生類，　深心之所欲， 更以異方便，　助顯第一義。 若有眾生類，　值諸過去佛， 若聞法布施，　或持戒忍辱、 精進禪智等，　種種修福慧， 如是諸人等，　皆已成佛道。 諸佛滅度已，　若人善軟心， 如是諸眾生，　皆已成佛道。 諸佛滅度已，　供養舍利者， 起萬億種塔，　金銀及頗梨、 車璩與馬腦、　玫瑰琉璃珠， 清淨廣嚴飾，　莊校於諸塔。 或有起石廟，　栴檀及沈水， 木櫁并餘材，　塼瓦泥土等。 若於曠野中，　積土成佛廟， 乃至童子戲，　聚沙為佛塔， 如是諸人等，　皆已成佛道。 若人為佛故，　建立諸形像， 刻雕成眾相，　皆已成佛道。 或以七寶成，　鍮石赤白銅、 白鑞及鉛錫，　鐵木及與泥， 或以膠漆布，　嚴飾作佛像， 如是諸人等，　皆已成佛道。 彩畫作佛像，　百福莊嚴相， 自作若使人，　皆已成佛道。 乃至童子戲，　若草木及筆， 或以指爪甲，　而畫作佛像， 如是諸人等，　漸漸積功德， 具足大悲心，　皆已成佛道。 但化諸菩薩，　度脫無量眾。 若人於塔廟、　寶像及畫像， 以華香幡蓋，　敬心而供養。 若使人作樂，　擊鼓吹角貝， 簫笛琴箜篌、　琵琶鐃銅鈸， 如是眾妙音，　盡持以供養。 或以歡喜心，　歌唄頌佛德， 乃至一小音，　皆已成佛道。 若人散亂心，　乃至以一華， 供養於畫像，　漸見無數佛。 或有人禮拜，　或復但合掌， 乃至舉一手，　或復小低頭， 以此供養像，　漸見無量佛， 自成無上道，　廣度無數眾， 入無餘涅槃，　如薪盡火滅。 若人散亂心，　入於塔廟中， 一稱南無佛，　皆已成佛道。 於諸過去佛，　在世或滅度， 若有聞是法，　皆已成佛道。 未來諸世尊，　其數無有量， 是諸如來等，　亦方便說法。 一切諸如來，　以無量方便， 度脫諸眾生，　入佛無漏智， 若有聞法者，　無一不成佛。 諸佛本誓願，　我所行佛道， 普欲令眾生，　亦同得此道。 未來世諸佛，　雖說百千億， 無數諸法門，　其實為一乘。 諸佛兩足尊，　知法常無性， 佛種從緣起，　是故說一乘。 是法住法位，　世間相常住， 於道場知已，　導師方便說。 天人所供養，　現在十方佛， 其數如恒沙，　出現於世間， 安隱眾生故，　亦說如是法， 知第一寂滅，　以方便力故， 雖示種種道，　其實為佛乘。 知眾生諸行，　深心之所念， 過去所習業，　欲性精進力， 及諸根利鈍，　以種種因緣、 譬喻亦言辭，　隨應方便說。 今我亦如是，　安隱眾生故， 以種種法門，　宣示於佛道。 我以智慧力，　知眾生性欲， 方便說諸法，　皆令得歡喜。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"And these great and holy lords, knowing what every being in the whole world — gods, humans, every kind of creature — desired deep in their hearts, made use of still other skillful means to help reveal the foremost truth. If there were beings who encountered the buddhas of the past, and who, on hearing the Dharma, practiced giving, or upheld precepts and patience, diligence, meditation and wisdom, and cultivated merit and wisdom in every kind of way — people such as these have all already accomplished the buddha-path. After the buddhas' parinirvāṇa, if there were people of a good and gentle mind, beings such as these have all already accomplished the buddha-path. After the buddhas' parinirvāṇa, those who made offerings to their relics, raising myriads of koṭis of kinds of stūpas — of gold, silver, and crystal, of giant clam-shell and carnelian, of rose quartz and lapis lazuli — adorning and decorating those stūpas in pure and lavish splendor, or raising shrines of stone, of sandalwood and aloeswood, of fragrant timber and other materials, of brick and tile and clay and earth, or, in the open wilderness, piling up earth to make a shrine for the Buddha — even children who, in play, gathered sand into a stūpa for the Buddha — people such as these have all already accomplished the buddha-path. If people, for the Buddha's sake, erected images, carving them into every kind of likeness, they have all already accomplished the buddha-path. Or made of the seven treasures, of brass, of red and white copper, of pewter and of lead and tin, of iron and wood and clay, or adorned with glue and lacquer and cloth, fashioning images of the Buddha — people such as these have all already accomplished the buddha-path. Those who painted images of the Buddha in color, with the marks adorned by a hundred kinds of merit, whether painting them themselves or having others paint them, have all already accomplished the buddha-path. Even children who, in play, with grass or a twig or a brush, or with their own fingernail, drew a likeness of the Buddha — people such as these, gradually accumulating merit and becoming complete in a mind of great compassion, have all already accomplished the buddha-path. He converts only bodhisattvas, and by this delivers immeasurable multitudes across. If people, before a stūpa or shrine, a bejeweled image or a painted one, made offerings with flowers, incense, banners, and canopies, with a reverent mind, or had music played — beating drums, blowing horns and conches, playing flutes and pipes, zithers and harps, lutes, cymbals and bronze bells — offering every one of these wondrous sounds up entirely as an offering, or, with a joyful mind, sang hymns in praise of the Buddha's virtue — even a single small note — they have all already accomplished the buddha-path. If a person, even with a distracted mind, offered even a single flower before a painted image, he will gradually come to see countless buddhas. Or if someone bowed in worship, or simply joined his palms, or even just raised one hand, or slightly bowed his head, and by this made an offering before an image, he will gradually come to see immeasurable buddhas, himself accomplishing the unsurpassed path, broadly delivering countless beings across, and entering nirvāṇa without remainder, as a fire dies out when its fuel is spent. If a person, even with a distracted mind, entered a stūpa or shrine and uttered even once, "Homage to the Buddha," he has already accomplished the buddha-path. Toward the buddhas of the past, whether while they were in the world or after their parinirvāṇa, whoever heard this Dharma has already accomplished the buddha-path. The world-honored ones of the future, whose number is beyond measure — these tathāgatas too will teach the Dharma by skillful means. All the tathāgatas, by immeasurable skillful means, deliver beings across into the Buddha's undefiled wisdom; of whoever hears the Dharma, not one will fail to become a buddha. The buddhas' original vow was this: the buddha-path that I myself have practiced, I wish to cause every being, universally, likewise to attain this same path. The buddhas of the future age, though they will teach hundreds of thousands of koṭis, countless gateways to the Dharma, in truth these are all for the one vehicle. The buddhas, honored among the two-footed, know that phenomena are always without any fixed nature of their own, and that the seed of buddhahood arises from conditions — and for this reason they teach the one vehicle. This Dharma abides in its own Dharma-position; the marks of the world are constantly abiding. Having known this at the seat of enlightenment, the Guide teaches it by skillful means. The present buddhas of the ten directions, honored with offerings by gods and humans, whose number is like the sands of the Ganges, appear in the world, and, in order to bring peace to beings, likewise teach this same Dharma. Knowing the foremost quiescent cessation, by the power of skillful means, though they display every kind of path, in truth it is all for the sake of the buddha vehicle. Knowing the conduct of beings, what lies deep in their minds, the karma they have cultivated in the past, the strength of their desires and diligence, and the sharpness or dullness of their faculties, they teach, through every kind of cause and condition, through parables and words, by skillful means suited to each. I too, now, am likewise so: in order to bring peace to beings, I proclaim and reveal the buddha-path through every kind of Dharma-gateway. By the power of my own wisdom, knowing beings' natures and desires, I teach all things by skillful means, causing every one of them to find joy."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi reads this whole passage as opening not one vehicle but five — the paths of humans, gods, śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, and bodhisattvas — into the single vehicle, and he is explicit that this section's plainer offerings (raising a stūpa, painting an image, singing a hymn) belong to the human and heavenly vehicles specifically, distinct from the six perfections practiced by bodhisattvas earlier in the same verse. He is unusually direct about how the line on children piling sand into a stūpa should be read. An earlier commentator had proposed reading "child" allegorically, as a technical term for a bodhisattva's stage of "true childhood," and "sand" as a symbol for non-attachment — turning a game into an advanced contemplative attainment. Zhiyi rejects this outright, calling it a reading that "twists the text and narrows it needlessly." His own reading takes the line at face value: an ordinary child, actual sand, a distracted moment of play — and he insists this is the more remarkable claim, not the lesser one, because it means the passage's real force is to include everyone, not only bodhisattvas already advanced enough to need no encouragement.

He anticipates the natural objection: shouldn't such a small, unintentional act of merit — a child's game, a single flower, one raised hand — simply ripen into an ordinary favorable rebirth, not buddhahood? His answer draws on the Nirvāṇa Sūtra's teaching of three kinds of buddha-nature, and in particular the "cause-condition" buddha-nature (緣因佛性) that even the smallest good root can plant, comparing it to the first basketful of earth in building a mountain, or the first hair's-breadth in encircling a tree with one's arms — not yet the mountain or the full embrace, but already, genuinely, the beginning of one. On this reading, the sūtra is not exaggerating when it says such acts have "already accomplished the buddha-path"; it is naming what that first basketful of earth already is, before it is anything more.

3. The Buddha's Own Hesitation, and Brahmā's Entreaty

Chinese Source

「舍利弗當知！　我以佛眼觀， 見六道眾生，　貧窮無福慧， 入生死嶮道，　相續苦不斷， 深著於五欲，　如犛牛愛尾， 以貪愛自蔽，　盲瞑無所見。 不求大勢佛、　及與斷苦法， 深入諸邪見，　以苦欲捨苦， 為是眾生故，　而起大悲心。 我始坐道場，　觀樹亦經行， 於三七日中，　思惟如是事， 我所得智慧，　微妙最第一。 眾生諸根鈍，　著樂癡所盲， 如斯之等類，　云何而可度？ 爾時諸梵王，　及諸天帝釋、 護世四天王，　及大自在天， 并餘諸天眾，　眷屬百千萬， 恭敬合掌禮，　請我轉法輪。 我即自思惟：　『若但讚佛乘， 眾生沒在苦，　不能信是法； 破法不信故，　墜於三惡道。 我寧不說法，　疾入於涅槃。 尋念過去佛，　所行方便力， 我今所得道，　亦應說三乘。』 作是思惟時，　十方佛皆現， 梵音慰喻我：　『善哉釋迦文！ 第一之導師，　得是無上法， 隨諸一切佛，　而用方便力。 我等亦皆得，　最妙第一法， 為諸眾生類、　分別說三乘。 少智樂小法，　不自信作佛， 是故以方便，　分別說諸果。 雖復說三乘，　但為教菩薩。』」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Śāriputra, you should know: I, surveying with the buddha-eye, saw beings of the six destinies, destitute of merit and wisdom, entering the perilous path of birth and death, their suffering continuing without break, deeply attached to the five desires, like a yak that loves its own tail, obscured by their own craving and blind, seeing nothing at all. They did not seek the buddha of great power, nor the Dharma that cuts off suffering; deeply sunk in every wrong view, they sought, through suffering, to cast off suffering. For the sake of such beings, I gave rise to a mind of great compassion. When I first sat at the seat of enlightenment, gazing at the tree and pacing back and forth, for three periods of seven days I reflected on this very matter: the wisdom I had attained was subtle and foremost of all. Beings' faculties are dull; they are attached to pleasure and blinded by ignorance. People of this kind — how could they possibly be brought across? At that time the Brahmā kings, and Śakra, lord of the gods, the Four Heavenly Kings who guard the world, and Maheśvara, together with the rest of the heavenly host, their retinues numbering hundreds of thousands of myriads, reverently joined their palms and bowed, entreating me to turn the wheel of the Dharma. I then thought to myself: 'If I only praise the buddha vehicle, beings sunk in suffering will be unable to believe this Dharma; and because they break the Dharma through disbelief, they will fall into the three evil destinies. I would rather not teach the Dharma at all, and swiftly enter nirvāṇa. Yet then, recalling the power of skillful means that buddhas of the past had practiced, I thought: the path I have now attained — I too should teach it as three vehicles.' As I had this thought, the buddhas of the ten directions all appeared, and in Brahmā-voices consoled and encouraged me: 'Excellent, Śākyamuni! Foremost of guides, who has attained this unsurpassed Dharma — follow all the buddhas, and make use of the power of skillful means. We too have all attained this most wondrous, foremost Dharma, and for the sake of every kind of being we distinguish it and teach it as three vehicles. Those of little wisdom who delight in a lesser Dharma do not believe they themselves can become buddhas; for this reason, by skillful means, we distinguish and teach the various fruits. Though we teach three vehicles, it is only in order to teach bodhisattvas.'"

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi first lays out how another text, the Sūtra of Past and Present Causes and Effects, accounts for these same three weeks in plain narrative terms: the first seven days spent reflecting that the Dharma he had found was too subtle for anyone to receive; the second spent considering beings' faculties, upper, middle, and lower; the third, deciding who should hear it first — after which he went to Vārāṇasī. He then raises, and does not soften, the obvious difficulty with taking this literally: the Buddha, he says, dwells always in the Dharma-body, silently and constantly illuminating everything with the buddha-eye — how could such a being have genuinely needed three weeks, seated under one tree, to work out what to do? His answer is that "three sevens" is not a literal duration but something "represented" (表) — standing in, symbolically, for the three rounds of teaching this sūtra will actually deliver to three levels of readiness: the first week for the direct Dharma-round now unfolding, the second for the parable-round about to begin, the third for the ancient causal story still several chapters away. He offers this alongside at least one further reading, mapping the same three weeks onto three levels of teaching in his own broader doctrinal system, and does not insist that only one of these readings is correct — he is more interested in establishing that a purely literal, chronological reading is not adequate to the text than in settling on a single alternative to replace it.

Whatever the three weeks are taken to mean, the passage's plainer claim is worth not softening either: the sūtra has the Buddha say, in his own voice, that he considered not teaching at all, and entering nirvāṇa without ever turning the wheel of the Dharma. What changes his mind is not a resolution of his own doubt about whether beings are ready — nothing in the text suggests that doubt was mistaken — but the memory of how every buddha before him faced the identical problem and chose skillful means over silence, confirmed immediately afterward by the buddhas of the ten directions themselves. The three vehicles that this whole chapter has just spent so much verse insisting were never the final truth are, in this telling, also not a compromise the Buddha made against his own better judgment. They are presented as the same choice every buddha before him made, for the same reason: an assembly not yet able to hear the one vehicle is still worth teaching something true enough to eventually lead them to it.

4. Turning the Wheel at Vārāṇasī

Chinese Source

「舍利弗當知！　我聞聖師子， 深淨微妙音，　喜稱南無佛。 復作如是念：　『我出濁惡世， 如諸佛所說，　我亦隨順行。』 思惟是事已，　即趣波羅柰。 諸法寂滅相，　不可以言宣， 以方便力故，　為五比丘說。 是名轉法輪，　便有涅槃音， 及以阿羅漢，　法僧差別名。 從久遠劫來，　讚示涅槃法， 生死苦永盡，　我常如是說。 舍利弗當知！　我見佛子等， 志求佛道者，　無量千萬億， 咸以恭敬心，　皆來至佛所， 曾從諸佛聞，　方便所說法。 我即作是念：　『如來所以出， 為說佛慧故，　今正是其時。』 舍利弗當知！　鈍根小智人、 著相憍慢者，　不能信是法。 今我喜無畏，　於諸菩薩中， 正直捨方便，　但說無上道， 菩薩聞是法，　疑網皆已除， 千二百羅漢，　悉亦當作佛。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Śāriputra, you should know: hearing the deep, pure, and subtle voices of these holy lions, I joyfully uttered, 'Homage to the buddhas.' I then thought further: 'I have appeared in this evil world of turbidity; as the buddhas have taught, I too shall follow and act accordingly.' Having reflected on this matter, I went at once to Vārāṇasī. The quiescent, ceased mark of all phenomena cannot be conveyed in words; by the power of skillful means, I taught it to the five monks. This is called the turning of the Dharma-wheel; and with it came the sound of the word 'nirvāṇa,' and the distinct names 'arhat,' 'Dharma,' and 'Saṅgha.' From a long, distant kalpa onward, I have praised and shown the Dharma of nirvāṇa, that the suffering of birth and death is forever ended; I have always taught in this way. Śāriputra, you should know: I saw children of the Buddha, those whose will was set on seeking the buddha-path, immeasurable thousands of myriads of koṭis of them, all coming with reverent minds to where the Buddha was, having once heard from the buddhas the Dharma taught by skillful means. I then thought: 'The very reason the Tathāgata appears is to teach the Buddha's wisdom — and now is exactly that time.' Śāriputra, you should know: people of dull faculties and little wisdom, attached to appearances and arrogant, are unable to believe this Dharma. Now, joyful and fearless, in the midst of the bodhisattvas, I set aside skillful means directly, and teach only the unsurpassed path. Hearing this Dharma, the bodhisattvas have had every net of doubt removed; and the twelve hundred arhats, all of them too, shall become buddhas."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Asked directly why the Buddha's first turning of the wheel was addressed to five human ascetics rather than to the gods who had just entreated him, Zhiyi gives a list of ordinary, almost administrative reasons rather than a doctrinal one: humans are the ones who can first realize the truths for themselves; a human audience is directly observable, so the event can be verified by others; humans can bear witness to what happened in a way that carries weight; the Buddha's own life and conduct had been lived among humans, sharing their form; and it is specifically among humans that the fourfold assembly of monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen exists at all, so that a human sangha could actually be founded. Devas benefit from the teaching too, he adds, but at one remove — through what humans establish, not by being the first to receive it. Read this way, the choice of Vārāṇasī and five ascetics is not a demotion from the more exalted entreaty just offered by Brahmā and Śakra; it is the most practically consequential place the wheel could first turn, because a human audience is where a Dharma, a Saṅgha, and a lasting teaching could actually take root.

The chapter's closing line is easy to read past after the density of everything leading up to it: the twelve hundred arhats seated in this very assembly — the same figures named by title in Part 1's roll call, the same audience whose three-fold plea through Śāriputra made the last several sections possible — are told, directly and without further qualification, that they will all become buddhas. Nothing in the text says this comes as a surprise to them; it is stated as the plain consequence of everything just taught. What is left unresolved, and will not be resolved until the chapters immediately following, is what becomes of that promise for any one of them by name — a question the sūtra takes up next with Śāriputra himself.

5. Rarer Than the Udumbara Flower

Chinese Source

「如三世諸佛，　說法之儀式， 我今亦如是，　說無分別法。 諸佛興出世，　懸遠值遇難， 正使出于世，　說是法復難， 無量無數劫，　聞是法亦難， 能聽是法者，　斯人亦復難。 譬如優曇花，　一切皆愛樂， 天人所希有，　時時乃一出。 聞法歡喜讚，　乃至發一言， 則為已供養，　一切三世佛， 是人甚希有，　過於優曇花。 汝等勿有疑，　我為諸法王， 普告諸大眾，　但以一乘道， 教化諸菩薩，　無聲聞弟子。 汝等舍利弗，　聲聞及菩薩， 當知是妙法，　諸佛之祕要。 以五濁惡世，　但樂著諸欲， 如是等眾生，　終不求佛道。 當來世惡人，　聞佛說一乘， 迷惑不信受，　破法墮惡道。 有慚愧清淨，　志求佛道者， 當為如是等，　廣讚一乘道。 舍利弗當知！　諸佛法如是， 以萬億方便，　隨宜而說法， 其不習學者，　不能曉了此。 汝等既已知，　諸佛世之師， 隨宜方便事，　無復諸疑惑， 心生大歡喜，　自知當作佛。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"As is the manner in which the buddhas of the three times teach the Dharma, I too now teach this Dharma free of discrimination in just the same way. That buddhas arise and appear in the world is a thing remote and hard to encounter; and even when one does appear in the world, that he should teach this Dharma is harder still. Through immeasurable, countless kalpas, even to hear this Dharma is hard; and one able to listen to this Dharma — such a person, too, is hard to find. It is like the udumbara flower, which everyone loves and delights in, rare among gods and humans, appearing only once in a great while. One who hears this Dharma and rejoices, praising it, even uttering a single word — that person has already made offerings to all the buddhas of the three times; such a person is exceedingly rare, rarer even than the udumbara flower. Have no doubt, all of you: I am the king of the Dharma, and I declare openly to this whole great assembly that I teach and transform bodhisattvas only by the path of the one vehicle — I have no disciples who are śrāvakas alone. You, Śāriputra, and the śrāvakas and bodhisattvas here, should know that this wondrous Dharma is the secret essence of the buddhas. Because, in this evil world of the five turbidities, beings delight only in attachment to their desires, beings such as these will, in the end, never seek the buddha-path. Evil people of the age to come, on hearing the Buddha teach the one vehicle, will be confused and will not believe or accept it; breaking the Dharma, they will fall into evil destinies. For those who have a sense of shame, who are pure, and whose will is set on seeking the buddha-path, one should broadly praise for them the path of the one vehicle. Śāriputra, you should know: the Dharma of the buddhas is like this — they teach the Dharma according to what is fitting, through myriads of koṭis of skillful means; those who have not studied and learned it cannot understand this. You have now already come to know how the buddhas, teachers of the world, act according to what is fitting through skillful means, and you no longer have any doubt; your minds give rise to great joy, and you know for yourselves that you shall become buddhas."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

The udumbara flower returns here for the third time since this chapter began — first in the Buddha's own prose declaration that "such a wondrous Dharma as this, the buddha-tathāgatas teach only at rare times, as the udumbara flower appears but once in a great while," and now twice more in this closing verse, first for the rarity of the teaching itself and then, more pointedly, for the rarity of a listener able to receive it with a single word of joy — a person said to be "rarer even than the udumbara flower." The comparison sharpens across its three appearances: it begins by describing how seldom a buddha teaches this Dharma at all, then how hard the Dharma itself is to encounter, and ends by placing the rarity not in the teaching or the teacher but in the listener capable of a single glad response to it. Whatever this chapter's audience makes of everything argued before it, the sūtra's own closing measure of the moment is this: five thousand who could not yet stay, weighed against twelve hundred who did — and stayed to hear that they, too, "shall become buddhas."
