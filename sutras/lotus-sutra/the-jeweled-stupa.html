Part 12 of 25 · 見寶塔品第十一

The Jeweled Stūpa

見寶塔品第十一

A stūpa of the seven treasures, five hundred yojanas high, rises whole out of the earth and hangs suspended in the sky — and a voice from within it praises the Buddha's teaching as true. It belongs to Prabhūtaratna, a buddha who entered parinirvāṇa ages ago and vowed to appear, bodily, wherever this sūtra is taught. To open it, Śākyamuni must first gather every buddha he has ever emanated across the ten directions — and the world itself must be remade to hold them all.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
A jeweled stūpa rising from the earth to hang in the sky, its voice confirming the truth of what the Buddha has taught; the Buddha's account of Prabhūtaratna, a long-extinct buddha whose ancient vow brings his stūpa, and his whole body within it, to appear wherever the Lotus Sūtra is taught in any world; the gathering of Śākyamuni's own emanation-buddhas from the ten directions, and the transformation of the entire world, twice over, to seat them all; the stūpa's doors opening to reveal Prabhūtaratna seated as though in meditation, who then shares his own seat with Śākyamuni; and the Buddha's call, addressed to the whole assembly now lifted into the open sky, for someone to carry this sūtra forward after his own parinirvāṇa.
Why it matters
Part 11 claimed that wherever this sūtra is kept, "the Tathāgata's entire body is already present there." This chapter makes that claim literal: an entire buddha, physically present, rises from the ground to confirm it. And the escalating series of impossible feats that closes this chapter — moving mountains, holding the sky in one's hand — exists to make one comparison unmistakable: every one of them is called easier than simply teaching this sūtra, honestly, in a difficult age.

1. The Jeweled Stūpa Erupts from the Earth

Chinese Source

爾時佛前有七寶塔，高五百由旬，縱廣二百五十由旬，從地踊出，住在空中，種種寶物而莊校之。五千欄楯，龕室千萬，無數幢幡以為嚴飾，垂寶瓔珞寶鈴萬億而懸其上。四面皆出多摩羅跋栴檀之香，充遍世界。其諸幡蓋，以金、銀、琉璃、車璩、馬腦、真珠、玫瑰、七寶合成，高至四天王宮。三十三天雨天曼陀羅華，供養寶塔。餘諸天、龍、夜叉、乾闥婆、阿修羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽、人非人等，千萬億眾，以一切華、香、瓔珞、幡蓋、伎樂，供養寶塔，恭敬、尊重、讚歎。爾時寶塔中出大音聲歎言：「善哉，善哉！釋迦牟尼世尊！能以平等大慧，教菩薩法，佛所護念，妙法華經，為大眾說。如是，如是！釋迦牟尼世尊！如所說者，皆是真實。」 爾時四眾，見大寶塔住在空中，又聞塔中所出音聲，皆得法喜，怪未曾有，從座而起，恭敬合掌，却住一面。爾時有菩薩摩訶薩，名大樂說，知一切世間天、人、阿修羅等心之所疑，而白佛言：「世尊！以何因緣，有此寶塔從地踊出，又於其中發是音聲？」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, before the Buddha, there appeared a stūpa of the seven treasures, five hundred yojanas high and two hundred fifty yojanas square, rising up out of the earth and standing suspended in the air, adorned with every kind of jewel. It had five thousand railings, and a thousand myriad niches and chambers, decorated with countless banners, and hung with jeweled garlands and myriads of koṭis of jeweled bells. On every side it sent forth the fragrance of tamālapattra and sandalwood, filling the entire world. Its banners and canopies were made of gold, silver, lapis lazuli, giant clam-shell, carnelian, pearl, and rose quartz, the seven treasures combined, and it rose as high as the palace of the Four Heavenly Kings. The gods of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven rained down heavenly māndārava flowers, making offerings to the jeweled stūpa. The rest of the gods, dragons, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans and non-humans, thousands of myriads of koṭis of them, made offerings to the jeweled stūpa with every kind of flower, incense, garland, banner, canopy, and music, honoring, revering, and praising it. At that time a great voice issued from within the jeweled stūpa, praising: "Excellent, excellent! Śākyamuni, World-Honored One! You have, with equal and impartial great wisdom, taught a Dharma for bodhisattvas, guarded and held in mind by the Buddha himself, the Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra, and expounded it for the great assembly. It is so, it is so! Śākyamuni, World-Honored One! Everything you have taught is entirely true." At that time the fourfold assembly, seeing this great jeweled stūpa suspended in the air, and hearing this voice issuing from within it, were all filled with the joy of the Dharma, marveling at what they had never had before, and rose from their seats, joined their palms in reverence, and withdrew to one side. At that time there was a bodhisattva-mahāsattva named Mahāpratibhāna, who, knowing the doubt in the minds of all the gods, humans, and asuras of the whole world, said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! For what cause and condition has this jeweled stūpa risen up out of the earth, and this voice issued from within it?"

Reading note

Part 11 closed with a claim stated almost in passing: wherever this sūtra is kept, a stūpa should be raised without need for relics, "because the Tathāgata's entire body is already present there." This chapter takes that claim and makes it literal, without metaphor. An entire buddha's stūpa, with the buddha's own body still seated within it, rises bodily from the ground — not a symbol of presence but presence itself, arriving to confirm in an audible voice that everything just taught "is entirely true." The bodhisattva who asks the question the whole assembly is wondering is named here for the first time: Mahāpratibhāna, "Great Delight in Preaching" — a fitting figure to voice the question that opens a chapter largely concerned with who will keep teaching this sūtra once its principal teacher is gone.

2. Many Treasures Buddha, and His Ancient Vow

Chinese Source

爾時佛告大樂說菩薩：「此寶塔中有如來全身，乃往過去東方無量千萬億阿僧祇世界，國名寶淨，彼中有佛，號曰多寶。其佛行菩薩道時，作大誓願：『若我成佛、滅度之後，於十方國土有說法華經處，我之塔廟，為聽是經故，踊現其前，為作證明，讚言善哉。』 「彼佛成道已，臨滅度時，於天人大眾中告諸比丘：『我滅度後，欲供養我全身者，應起一大塔。』其佛以神通願力，十方世界，在在處處，若有說法華經者，彼之寶塔皆踊出其前，全身在於塔中，讚言：『善哉，善哉！』 「大樂說！今多寶如來塔，聞說法華經故，從地踊出，讚言：『善哉，善哉！』」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Buddha said to Mahāpratibhāna Bodhisattva: "Within this jeweled stūpa is the entire body of a tathāgata. Long ago, in the past, in an immeasurable, asaṃkhyeya number of worlds to the east, in a land named Jeweled Purity, there was a buddha named Prabhūtaratna. When that buddha was practicing the bodhisattva path, he made a great vow: 'If, after I attain buddhahood and enter parinirvāṇa, there is, in any land of the ten directions, a place where the Lotus Sūtra is being taught, my own stūpa and shrine shall, in order to listen to this sūtra, rise up and appear before that place, to bear witness to it, and to praise it, saying: "Excellent."' "That buddha, having accomplished the path, when his parinirvāṇa drew near, declared to the monks, in the midst of the great assembly of gods and humans: 'After my parinirvāṇa, whoever wishes to make offerings to my entire body should raise a single great stūpa.' By that buddha's own power of spiritual penetration and the power of his vow, in every land of the ten directions, wherever the Lotus Sūtra is taught, that jeweled stūpa of his rises up and appears before that place, his entire body within the stūpa, praising: 'Excellent, excellent!' "Mahāpratibhāna! Now this stūpa of the Tathāgata Prabhūtaratna, hearing the Lotus Sūtra being taught, has risen up out of the earth, praising: 'Excellent, excellent!'"

Reading note

Prabhūtaratna's vow is worth setting beside the other ancient vows this sūtra has already described — Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū's decision, in Part 8, to teach three vehicles even in an age untouched by turbidity, or the vows this site's earlier translation of the Amitābha Sūtra recorded for a Pure Land of rebirth. This vow is narrower and, in its own way, stranger than either: not a vow to teach, to save, or to build a land, but a vow to keep showing up, indefinitely, as evidence — appearing bodily, across every world and every era, at the exact moment and place this one particular sūtra is being taught, solely to confirm that it is being taught truly. Prabhūtaratna's entire post-parinirvāṇa existence, on this account, consists of that single recurring act of witness.

3. Gathering the Buddhas of the Ten Directions

Chinese Source

是時大樂說菩薩，以如來神力故，白佛言：「世尊！我等願欲見此佛身。」 佛告大樂說菩薩摩訶薩：「是多寶佛，有深重願：『若我寶塔，為聽法華經故，出於諸佛前時，其有欲以我身示四眾者，彼佛分身諸佛——在於十方世界說法，盡還集一處，然後我身乃出現耳。』 「大樂說！我分身諸佛——在於十方世界說法者，今應當集。」 大樂說白佛言：「世尊！我等亦願欲見世尊分身諸佛，禮拜供養。」 爾時佛放白毫一光，即見東方五百萬億那由他恒河沙等國土諸佛，彼諸國土，皆以頗梨為地，寶樹、寶衣以為莊嚴，無數千萬億菩薩充滿其中，遍張寶幔，寶網羅上。彼國諸佛，以大妙音而說諸法，及見無量千萬億菩薩，遍滿諸國，為眾說法。南西北方、四維上下，白毫相光所照之處，亦復如是。爾時十方諸佛，各告眾菩薩言：「善男子！我今應往娑婆世界，釋迦牟尼佛所，并供養多寶如來寶塔。」 時娑婆世界即變清淨，琉璃為地，寶樹莊嚴，黃金為繩以界八道，無諸聚落、村營、城邑、大海、江河、山川、林藪。燒大寶香，曼陀羅華遍布其地，以寶網幔，羅覆其上，懸諸寶鈴。唯留此會眾，移諸天人置於他土。 是時，諸佛各將一大菩薩以為侍者，至娑婆世界，各到寶樹下。一一寶樹高五百由旬，枝、葉、華、菓次第莊嚴，諸寶樹下皆有師子之座，高五由旬，亦以大寶而校飾之。爾時諸佛，各於此座結加趺坐。如是展轉遍滿三千大千世界，而於釋迦牟尼佛一方所分之身猶故未盡。 時釋迦牟尼佛欲容受所分身諸佛故，八方各更變二百萬億那由他國，皆令清淨，無有地獄、餓鬼、畜生及阿修羅，又移諸天、人置於他土。所化之國，亦以琉璃為地，寶樹莊嚴，樹高五百由旬，枝、葉、華、菓次第嚴飾，樹下皆有寶師子座，高五由旬，種種諸寶以為莊校。亦無大海、江河，及目真隣陀山、摩訶目真隣陀山、鐵圍山、大鐵圍山、須彌山等諸山王，通為一佛國土。寶地平正，寶交露幔遍覆其上；懸諸幡蓋，燒大寶香，諸天寶華遍布其地。 釋迦牟尼佛為諸佛當來坐故，復於八方各更變二百萬億那由他國，皆令清淨，無有地獄、餓鬼、畜生及阿修羅，又移諸天、人置於他土。所化之國，亦以琉璃為地，寶樹莊嚴，樹高五百由旬，枝、葉、華、菓次第莊嚴，樹下皆有寶師子座，高五由旬，亦以大寶而校飾之。亦無大海、江河，及目真隣陀山、摩訶目真隣陀山、鐵圍山、大鐵圍山、須彌山等諸山王，通為一佛國土。寶地平正，寶交露幔、遍覆其上；懸諸幡蓋，燒大寶香，諸天寶華遍布其地。 爾時東方釋迦牟尼佛所分之身，百千萬億那由他恒河沙等國土中諸佛，各各說法，來集於此；如是次第十方諸佛皆悉來集，坐於八方。爾時一一方四百萬億那由他國土，諸佛如來遍滿其中。是時，諸佛各在寶樹下，坐師子座，皆遣侍者問訊釋迦牟尼佛，各齎寶華滿掬而告之言：「善男子！汝往詣耆闍崛山釋迦牟尼佛所，如我辭曰：『少病、少惱，氣力安樂，及菩薩、聲聞眾悉安隱不？』以此寶華散佛供養，而作是言：『彼某甲佛，與欲開此寶塔。』」諸佛遣使，亦復如是。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Mahāpratibhāna Bodhisattva, by the Tathāgata's own spiritual power, said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! We wish to see this buddha's body." The Buddha said to Mahāpratibhāna Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva: "This Buddha Prabhūtaratna has a deep and weighty vow: 'Whenever my jeweled stūpa, in order to listen to the Lotus Sūtra, appears before some buddha, if that buddha wishes to show my body to the fourfold assembly, then that buddha's own emanation-buddhas — teaching the Dharma throughout worlds of the ten directions — must first all gather together in one place, and only then shall my body appear.' "Mahāpratibhāna! My own emanation-buddhas, now teaching the Dharma throughout worlds of the ten directions, should now gather here." Mahāpratibhāna said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! We too wish to see the World-Honored One's own emanation-buddhas, to bow before them and make offerings to them." At that time the Buddha sent forth a single ray of light from the white curl between his brows, and at once the buddhas of five hundred myriad koṭis of nayutas of lands to the east, as many as the sands of the Ganges, became visible. Every one of those lands had a ground of crystal, adorned with jeweled trees and jeweled robes, filled everywhere with countless thousands of myriads of koṭis of bodhisattvas, spread over with jeweled curtains, and covered above with jeweled nets. The buddhas of those lands taught the Dharma with a great and wondrous voice, and immeasurable thousands of myriads of koṭis of bodhisattvas could be seen, filling every land, teaching the Dharma for their assemblies. In the south, west, and north, in the four intermediate directions, and above and below, wherever the light of the white curl reached, it was just the same. At that time the buddhas of the ten directions each said to their assembled bodhisattvas: "Good sons! We should now go to the sahā world, to where the Buddha Śākyamuni is, and make offerings as well to the jeweled stūpa of the Tathāgata Prabhūtaratna." At that time the sahā world at once transformed into a pure land, its ground of lapis lazuli, adorned with jeweled trees, bordered by cords of gold marking out eight roads, without any village, hamlet, town, or city, without great oceans, rivers, mountains, or forests. Great jeweled incense burned, and māndārava flowers covered the ground everywhere; jeweled nets and curtains were spread over it, hung with jeweled bells. Only this present assembly remained; every other god and human was moved and placed in another land. At that time each of the buddhas, taking one great bodhisattva as his attendant, came to the sahā world, and went, each of them, to beneath a jeweled tree. Each jeweled tree stood five hundred yojanas high, its branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit arranged in beautiful order, and beneath each jeweled tree there was a lion's seat, five yojanas high, likewise adorned with great treasures. At that time each buddha sat cross-legged upon this seat. In this way, one after another, they filled the entire trichiliocosm, and yet even so, of the buddhas emanated from Śākyamuni Buddha in only a single direction, not all had yet arrived. At that time Śākyamuni Buddha, wishing to make room for the buddhas emanated from himself, transformed, in each of the eight directions, two hundred myriad koṭis of nayutas of lands more, causing them all to become pure, without hells, hungry ghosts, animals, or asuras, and moving their gods and humans and placing them in other lands. These transformed lands likewise had a ground of lapis lazuli, adorned with jeweled trees five hundred yojanas high, their branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit arranged in beautiful order, and beneath each tree a jeweled lion's seat, five yojanas high, adorned with every kind of treasure. There were, besides, no great oceans or rivers, and none of the great mountain-kings — Cakravāḍa, Mahācakravāḍa, Mucilinda, Mahāmucilinda, or Sumeru — so that the whole became a single buddha-land. Its jeweled ground was level and smooth, covered over everywhere with curtains of jeweled dew, hung with banners and canopies; great jeweled incense burned, and heavenly jeweled flowers covered the ground everywhere. Śākyamuni Buddha, in order to make room besides for buddhas yet to come and take their seats, transformed, in each of the eight directions, two hundred myriad koṭis of nayutas of lands more still, causing them all to become pure, without hells, hungry ghosts, animals, or asuras, and moving their gods and humans and placing them in other lands. These transformed lands likewise had a ground of lapis lazuli, adorned with jeweled trees five hundred yojanas high, their branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit arranged in beautiful order, and beneath each tree a jeweled lion's seat, five yojanas high, likewise adorned with great treasures. There were, besides, no great oceans or rivers, and none of the great mountain-kings — Cakravāḍa, Mahācakravāḍa, Mucilinda, Mahāmucilinda, or Sumeru — so that the whole became a single buddha-land. Its jeweled ground was level and smooth, covered over everywhere with curtains of jeweled dew, hung with banners and canopies; great jeweled incense burned, and heavenly jeweled flowers covered the ground everywhere. At that time the buddhas emanated from Śākyamuni Buddha to the east, from hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of nayutas of lands, as many as the sands of the Ganges, each teaching the Dharma, came and gathered here; and in this same way, one after another, the buddhas of the ten directions all came and gathered, seated in each of the eight directions. At that time, in each and every direction, four hundred myriad koṭis of nayutas of lands were filled entirely with buddha-tathāgatas. At that time, each buddha, seated beneath a jeweled tree upon a lion's seat, sent his attendant to greet Śākyamuni Buddha, each bearing a double handful of jeweled flowers, and saying to him: "Good son! Go to where Śākyamuni Buddha is, on Vulture Peak, and say, on my behalf: 'Are you in good health, with little affliction, your strength and ease sufficient, and are your bodhisattvas and śrāvakas all at peace?' And scatter these jeweled flowers as an offering to the Buddha, and say further: 'Such-and-such a buddha wishes you to open this jeweled stūpa.'" Every buddha sent his messenger in just this same way.

Reading note

The world is remade twice in immediate succession here, in language the sūtra repeats almost word for word each time — once to seat the buddhas already arriving, and then again, before the first transformation has even finished, to make room for buddhas not yet arrived. This site's translation preserves that repetition exactly as the source gives it, rather than compressing the second instance: the doubling is not redundancy but the sūtra's own method for conveying a scale that a single description, however vast, could not convey on its own. Each round removes an entire cosmology's worth of familiar landmarks — oceans, rivers, the great mountain-kings including Sumeru itself — and each round is still, by the text's own account, not yet enough.

4. The Two Buddhas, Seated Side by Side

Chinese Source

爾時釋迦牟尼佛，見所分身佛悉已來集，各各坐於師子之座，皆聞諸佛與欲同開寶塔，即從座起，住虛空中。一切四眾，起立合掌，一心觀佛。於是釋迦牟尼佛，以右指開七寶塔戶，出大音聲，如却關鑰開大城門。即時一切眾會，皆見多寶如來於寶塔中坐師子座，全身不散，如入禪定。又聞其言：「善哉，善哉！釋迦牟尼佛！快說是法華經，我為聽是經故而來至此。」爾時四眾等，見過去無量千萬億劫滅度佛說如是言，歎未曾有，以天寶華聚，散多寶佛及釋迦牟尼佛上。 爾時多寶佛於寶塔中分半座與釋迦牟尼佛，而作是言：「釋迦牟尼佛！可就此座。」即時釋迦牟尼佛入其塔中，坐其半座，結加趺坐。爾時，大眾見二如來在七寶塔中師子座上結加趺坐，各作是念：「佛座高遠，唯願如來以神通力，令我等輩俱處虛空。」即時釋迦牟尼佛以神通力，接諸大眾皆在虛空，以大音聲普告四眾：「誰能於此娑婆國土廣說妙法華經，今正是時。如來不久當入涅槃，佛欲以此妙法華經付囑有在。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Śākyamuni Buddha, seeing that his emanated buddhas had all gathered and had each taken his seat upon a lion's throne, and hearing that every buddha wished the jeweled stūpa opened together, at once rose from his seat and stood suspended in the air. The whole fourfold assembly rose to their feet, joined their palms, and gazed at the Buddha with one mind. Then Śākyamuni Buddha, with the finger of his right hand, opened the door of the stūpa of the seven treasures, and a great sound issued forth, like the sound of a great city gate's lock being drawn back and the gate thrown open. At once the whole assembly saw the Tathāgata Prabhūtaratna, seated upon a lion's throne within the jeweled stūpa, his entire body unscattered, as though he had entered meditative concentration. And they heard him say: "Excellent, excellent! Śākyamuni Buddha! You have joyfully taught this Lotus Sūtra — it is in order to hear this sūtra that I have come to this place." At that time the fourfold assembly, seeing this buddha who had entered parinirvāṇa immeasurable thousands of myriads of koṭis of kalpas ago speak these words, marveled at what they had never had before, and scattered heaps of heavenly jeweled flowers over both the Buddha Prabhūtaratna and Śākyamuni Buddha. At that time the Buddha Prabhūtaratna, within the jeweled stūpa, offered half of his own seat to Śākyamuni Buddha, saying: "Śākyamuni Buddha! Please take this seat." Śākyamuni Buddha at once entered the stūpa, and sat down on the offered half of the seat, cross-legged. At that time the great assembly, seeing the two tathāgatas seated cross-legged together upon the lion's throne within the stūpa of the seven treasures, each thought: "The Buddha's seat is high and far away — we beg only that the Tathāgata, by his spiritual power, cause us all to be raised up into the air as well." At once Śākyamuni Buddha, by his spiritual power, drew the whole great assembly up into the air, and, with a great voice, declared to the fourfold assembly: "Who is able, in this sahā world, to broadly teach the Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra? Now is exactly the time for it. The Tathāgata shall, before long, enter nirvāṇa, and the Buddha wishes to entrust this Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra to one who will carry it onward."

Reading note

Two buddhas seated side by side within a single stūpa became, in the centuries after this sūtra spread through East Asia, one of its most recognizable images in its own right — carved, painted, and cast independently of the surrounding text, recognizable to viewers who might know nothing else about this chapter. What the image compresses is a small but exact gesture: Prabhūtaratna does not simply permit Śākyamuni to enter his stūpa, but offers him "half of his own seat" — the two buddhas, one who taught this sūtra long in the past and one teaching it now, sharing a single throne rather than one deferring to the other.

The assembly's request to be lifted into the sky, granted at once, marks a shift in setting that carries forward through the chapters that follow: from this point on, this sūtra's remaining audience is suspended in open air, no longer seated on the ground at Vulture Peak. The Buddha's declaration to that airborne assembly — "who is able... to broadly teach the Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra?" — is addressed to everyone present, named and unnamed alike, and is left, at the close of this chapter, without an answer. The next several chapters are, in different ways, this sūtra's account of who steps forward to answer it.

5. Verse: What Is Truly Difficult

Chinese Source

爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「聖主世尊，　雖久滅度，　在寶塔中， 尚為法來。　諸人云何　不勤為法？ 此佛滅度，　無央數劫，　處處聽法， 以難遇故。　彼佛本願，　我滅度後， 在在所往，　常為聽法。　又我分身， 無量諸佛，　如恒沙等，　來欲聽法。 及見滅度，　多寶如來，　各捨妙土， 及弟子眾、　天人龍神、　諸供養事， 令法久住，　故來至此。　為坐諸佛， 以神通力，　移無量眾，　令國清淨。 諸佛各各，　詣寶樹下，　如清淨池， 蓮華莊嚴。　其寶樹下，　諸師子座， 佛坐其上，　光明嚴飾，　如夜闇中， 燃大炬火。　身出妙香，　遍十方國， 眾生蒙薰，　喜不自勝。　譬如大風， 吹小樹枝。　以是方便，　令法久住。 告諸大眾：　『我滅度後，　誰能護持、 讀說斯經？　今於佛前，　自說誓言。 其多寶佛，　雖久滅度，　以大誓願， 而師子吼。　多寶如來，　及與我身， 所集化佛，　當知此意。　諸佛子等， 誰能護法，　當發大願，　令得久住。 其有能護，　此經法者，　則為供養， 我及多寶。　此多寶佛，　處於寶塔， 常遊十方。　為是經故，　亦復供養， 諸來化佛，　莊嚴光飾、　諸世界者。 若說此經，　則為見我、　多寶如來， 及諸化佛。　諸善男子！　各諦思惟， 此為難事，　宜發大願。　諸餘經典， 數如恒沙，　雖說此等，　未足為難。 若接須彌，　擲置他方，　無數佛土， 亦未為難。　若以足指，　動大千界， 遠擲他國，　亦未為難。　若立有頂， 為眾演說，　無量餘經，　亦未為難。 若佛滅後，　於惡世中，　能說此經， 是則為難。　假使有人，　手把虛空， 而以遊行，　亦未為難。　於我滅後， 若自書持，　若使人書，　是則為難。 若以大地，　置足甲上，　昇於梵天， 亦未為難。　佛滅度後，　於惡世中， 暫讀此經，　是則為難。　假使劫燒， 擔負乾草，　入中不燒，　亦未為難。 我滅度後，　若持此經、　為一人說， 是則為難。　若持八萬，　四千法藏， 十二部經，　為人演說，　令諸聽者， 得六神通，　雖能如是，　亦未為難。 於我滅後，　聽受此經，　問其義趣， 是則為難。　若人說法，　令千萬億， 無量無數，　恒沙眾生，　得阿羅漢， 具六神通，　雖有是益，　亦未為難。 於我滅後，　若能奉持，　如斯經典， 是則為難。　我為佛道，　於無量土， 從始至今，　廣說諸經，　而於其中， 此經第一。　若有能持，　則持佛身。 諸善男子！　於我滅後，　誰能受持、 讀誦此經，　今於佛前、　自說誓言。 此經難持，　若暫持者，　我則歡喜， 諸佛亦然。　如是之人，　諸佛所歎。 是則勇猛，　是則精進，　是名持戒， 行頭陀者，　則為疾得，　無上佛道。 能於來世，　讀持此經，　是真佛子， 住淳善地。　佛滅度後，　能解其義， 是諸天人，　世間之眼。　於恐畏世， 能須臾說，　一切天人，　皆應供養。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "The holy lord, the World-Honored One, though he entered parinirvāṇa long ago, still comes, within his jeweled stūpa, for the sake of the Dharma. How, then, could any of you fail to strive diligently for the Dharma's sake? This buddha's parinirvāṇa was countless kalpas ago, yet he goes everywhere to listen to the Dharma, because it is so hard to encounter. That buddha's original vow was this: after my own parinirvāṇa, wherever I may go, I shall always come to listen to the Dharma. And my own emanated buddhas, immeasurable in number, as many as the sands of the Ganges, have come as well, wishing to listen to the Dharma. And, wishing to see the Tathāgata Prabhūtaratna, who has entered parinirvāṇa, they have each forsaken their own wondrous lands, and their own disciples, gods, humans, dragons, and spirits, and every offering made to them, so that the Dharma might long endure, and have come to this place for that reason. In order to seat these buddhas, I have, by spiritual power, moved immeasurable multitudes elsewhere, and made these lands pure. Each of these buddhas has gone to sit beneath a jeweled tree, as though beside a pure pond adorned with lotus flowers. Beneath these jeweled trees, upon their lion's seats, the buddhas sit, their light and adornment like a great torch burning in the dark of night. Their bodies send forth a wondrous fragrance, filling the lands of the ten directions; beings, touched by this fragrance, feel a joy they cannot contain, as though a great wind were blowing through the branches of a small tree. By this skillful means, I cause the Dharma to long endure. I declare to this great assembly: 'After my parinirvāṇa, who is able to protect, uphold, read, and teach this sūtra? Let him now, before the Buddha, speak his own vow.' The Buddha Prabhūtaratna, though he entered parinirvāṇa long ago, by his own great vow, roars this lion's roar. The Tathāgata Prabhūtaratna, and I myself, together with all the emanated buddhas now gathered here — you should know our shared intent in this. Children of the Buddha, whoever among you is able to protect the Dharma should now bring forth a great vow, that it may long endure. Whoever is able to protect this sūtra and its Dharma thereby makes offerings to both me and to Prabhūtaratna. This Buddha Prabhūtaratna, dwelling within his jeweled stūpa, constantly travels through the ten directions; for the sake of this sūtra, he thereby also makes offerings to all the emanated buddhas who have come here, who have adorned and illumined every world. Whoever teaches this sūtra thereby sees me, and the Tathāgata Prabhūtaratna, and all the emanated buddhas as well. Good sons, each of you should reflect carefully: this is a difficult thing, and you should bring forth a great vow for it. As for every other sūtra, numerous as the sands of the Ganges — even to teach all of these would not be difficult enough to compare. Were one to lift up Mount Sumeru and hurl it into countless other buddha-lands, this too would not be difficult. Were one, with a single toe, to shake a whole trichiliocosm, and hurl it far off into another land, this too would not be difficult. Were one to stand at the very summit of existence, and expound for the assembly countless other sūtras, this too would not be difficult. But if, after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, in an evil age, one is able to teach this sūtra — this alone is truly difficult. Were a person to grasp empty space in his hand, and wander about with it, this too would not be difficult. But after my parinirvāṇa, to copy and uphold this sūtra oneself, or to have another copy it — this alone is truly difficult. Were one to place the whole great earth upon a single toenail, and ascend to the Brahmā heaven with it, this too would not be difficult. But after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, in an evil age, even to read this sūtra for a moment — this alone is truly difficult. Were one, at the time of the kalpa-ending fire, to carry a bundle of dry grass into the flames and not be burned, this too would not be difficult. But after my parinirvāṇa, to uphold this sūtra and teach it to even a single person — this alone is truly difficult. Were one to uphold the eighty-four thousand teachings of the Dharma, the twelve divisions of the scriptures, and expound them for others, causing every listener to attain the six spiritual powers — even were one able to do this, it would not be difficult enough to compare. But after my parinirvāṇa, to listen to and receive this sūtra, and to ask after its meaning — this alone is truly difficult. Were a person to teach the Dharma so that thousands of myriads of koṭis, immeasurable and countless beings, as many as the sands of the Ganges, attained arhatship, complete with the six spiritual powers — even with such benefit as this, it would not be difficult enough to compare. But after my parinirvāṇa, to be able to uphold a scripture such as this one — this alone is truly difficult. For the sake of the buddha-path, in immeasurable lands, from the beginning until now, I have broadly taught every sūtra — and among them all, this sūtra is foremost. Whoever is able to uphold it thereby upholds the Buddha's own body. Good sons! After my parinirvāṇa, whoever is able to uphold, read, and recite this sūtra should now, before the Buddha, speak his own vow. This sūtra is hard to uphold; if there is one who upholds it even for a moment, I shall rejoice, and so shall every buddha. Such a person is praised by every buddha. This is true valor, this is true diligence; this is called upholding the precepts, and practicing austerities — and such a one shall swiftly attain the unsurpassed buddha-path. Whoever, in the ages to come, is able to read and uphold this sūtra is a true child of the Buddha, dwelling in a ground of pure goodness. After the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, whoever is able to understand its meaning is the eye of the world for gods and humans alike. In an age of fear, whoever is able to teach it even for a moment should be honored with offerings by every god and human."

Reading note

This verse's structure is one of the sūtra's most direct rhetorical devices, and it is worth naming plainly: a sequence of physically impossible feats — hurling Mount Sumeru into another land, holding empty space in one hand, carrying dry grass unburnt through the fire that ends a kalpa — each introduced only to be dismissed, one after another, as "not difficult" by comparison. What the sequence builds toward is never another miracle. It is copying a sūtra, reading it for a moment, asking after its meaning, teaching it to a single person — acts with no visible spectacle attached to them at all, set apart from every feat of cosmic strength as the genuinely difficult thing. The comparison is not made to disparage supernatural power; earlier chapters of this very sūtra have described spiritual feats on exactly this scale without any such deflation. It is made to isolate one specific condition as what actually makes ordinary transmission hard: doing it "after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, in an evil age" — without the teacher present, without an easy audience, with nothing to recommend the effort but the act itself.
