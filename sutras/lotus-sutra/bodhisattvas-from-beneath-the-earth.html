Bodhisattvas from Beneath the Earth

從地踊出品第十五

Bodhisattvas from other lands offer to protect this sūtra after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa. He declines — he already has disciples of his own for that task — and the ground of the sahā world splits open as innumerable bodhisattvas, trained by him since some unnamed past, rise up from beneath it. Maitreya, unable to recognize a single one of them, asks where they came from and who taught them; the Buddha's answer only deepens the puzzle it was meant to solve, and this chapter ends with the question still hanging in the air.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
Bodhisattvas from other lands offering to protect this sūtra after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, and the Buddha declining their offer; the ground of the sahā world's three-thousandfold universe splitting open as innumerable golden-hued bodhisattvas rise up from the space beneath it, led by four foremost teachers who ask after the Buddha's health; Maitreya and eight thousand Ganges-sands of bodhisattvas failing to recognize a single face among this assembly and asking, in verse, where they came from; and the Buddha's answer — that he himself has taught and guided every one of them since his own awakening beneath the bodhi tree — which only deepens Maitreya's doubt rather than resolving it.
Why it matters
Zhiyi reads this chapter as the deliberate provocation of a doubt that only the following chapter resolves: the Buddha's claim to have personally trained this vast assembly in the roughly forty years since his awakening at Gayā is, on its face, not credible, and Maitreya says so. This chapter's whole purpose is to raise that impossibility as sharply as it can be raised — the chapter titled 如來壽量品, "the Tathāgata's lifespan," exists to answer it.

1. Other-Land Bodhisattvas Offer, and the Buddha Declines

Chinese Source

爾時他方國土諸來菩薩摩訶薩，過八恒河沙數，於大眾中起立，合掌作禮而白佛言：「世尊！若聽我等，於佛滅後，在此娑婆世界，懃加精進，護持、讀誦、書寫、供養是經典者，當於此土而廣說之。」 爾時佛告諸菩薩摩訶薩眾：「止，善男子！不須汝等護持此經。所以者何？我娑婆世界自有六萬恒河沙等菩薩摩訶薩，一一菩薩各有六萬恒河沙眷屬，是諸人等，能於我滅後，護持、讀誦、廣說此經。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the bodhisattva-mahāsattvas who had come from other lands, exceeding eight Ganges-sands in number, rose up in the midst of the great assembly, joined their palms, bowed, and said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! If you permit us, after the Buddha's own parinirvāṇa, to apply ourselves with diligence in this sahā world, upholding, reading, reciting, copying, and making offerings to this sūtra, we shall broadly teach it in this land." At that time the Buddha said to this assembly of bodhisattva-mahāsattvas: "Enough, good sons! You need not uphold this sūtra. Why is this so? Because in my own sahā world there are already bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to sixty thousand Ganges-sands in number, and each one of these bodhisattvas has, besides, a retinue equal to sixty thousand Ganges-sands. These are the ones able, after my own parinirvāṇa, to uphold, read, recite, and broadly teach this sūtra."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi reads the Buddha's refusal as doing three things at once: it keeps the visiting bodhisattvas at their own proper task rather than diverting them to a land not truly their own; it recognizes that a bond formed only in this single lifetime's assembly would not yet run deep enough to sustain the work; and — the reason this chapter exists at all — declining their offer is what makes room for the bodhisattvas beneath the earth to be summoned instead, since only their rising up, and not any other bodhisattvas' presence, is capable of the chapter's real business: breaking open the "trace" of a Buddha awakened forty years ago to disclose the far older "source" beneath it.

2. The Earth Splits Open, and the Four Leaders' Greeting

Chinese Source

佛說是時，娑婆世界三千大千國土地皆震裂，而於其中，有無量千萬億菩薩摩訶薩同時踊出。是諸菩薩，身皆金色，三十二相，無量光明，先盡在此娑婆世界之下、此界虛空中住。是諸菩薩，聞釋迦牟尼佛所說音聲，從下發來。一一菩薩皆是大眾唱導之首，各將六萬恒河沙眷屬；況將五萬、四萬、三萬、二萬、一萬恒河沙等眷屬者；況復乃至一恒河沙、半恒河沙、四分之一、乃至千萬億那由他分之一；況復千萬億那由他眷屬；況復億萬眷屬；況復千萬、百萬、乃至一萬；況復一千、一百、乃至一十；況復將五、四、三、二、一弟子者；況復單己，樂遠離行。如是等比，無量無邊，算數譬喻所不能知。 是諸菩薩從地出已，各詣虛空七寶妙塔多寶如來、釋迦牟尼佛所。到已，向二世尊頭面禮足，及至諸寶樹下師子座上佛所，亦皆作禮，右繞三匝，合掌恭敬，以諸菩薩種種讚法而以讚歎，住在一面，欣樂瞻仰於二世尊。是諸菩薩摩訶薩，從初踊出，以諸菩薩種種讚法而讚於佛，如是時間，經五十小劫。是時釋迦牟尼佛默然而坐，及諸四眾亦皆默然五十小劫；佛神力故，令諸大眾謂如半日。 爾時四眾，亦以佛神力故，見諸菩薩遍滿無量百千萬億國土虛空。

Ru-Yi Translation

As the Buddha spoke these words, the ground of the sahā world's three-thousand-great-thousandfold lands all shook and split open, and from within it, innumerable thousands of myriads of koṭis of bodhisattva-mahāsattvas rose up all at once. Every one of these bodhisattvas had a body the color of gold, the thirty-two marks, and immeasurable radiance, and had already been dwelling, before this, in the space beneath this very sahā world. Hearing the sound of Śākyamuni Buddha's voice, they set out from below and came forth. Each and every one of these bodhisattvas was himself a foremost leader of a great assembly, each bringing a retinue equal to sixty thousand Ganges-sands — how much more so those bringing retinues of fifty thousand, forty thousand, thirty thousand, twenty thousand, or ten thousand Ganges-sands; how much more so those bringing retinues down to a single Ganges-sands, half a Ganges-sands, a quarter, or even down to one part in a thousand myriad koṭis of nayutas; how much more so those bringing a retinue of a thousand myriad koṭis of nayutas; how much more so those bringing a retinue of a koṭi of myriads; how much more so those bringing a thousand myriad, a hundred myriad, down to a mere ten thousand; how much more so those bringing a thousand, a hundred, down to a mere ten; how much more so those bringing five, four, three, two, or a single disciple; how much more so those entirely alone, delighting in the practice of solitude. Multitudes such as these, immeasurable and boundless, could not be known by any number or any parable. Once these bodhisattvas had come forth from the earth, each one went to the jeweled stūpa hanging in the sky, to the place of Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata and Śākyamuni Buddha. Arriving there, they bowed their heads to the feet of the two World-Honored Ones, and then, going to where the Buddha sat upon the lion throne beneath each jeweled tree, likewise bowed before him, circled him three times to the right, joined their palms in reverence, and praised him with every kind of praise a bodhisattva knows, then stood to one side, gazing up at the two World-Honored Ones with joy. From the moment these bodhisattva-mahāsattvas first rose up, praising the Buddha with every kind of praise a bodhisattva knows, this length of time passed: fifty small kalpas. Throughout this time, Śākyamuni Buddha sat in silence, and the fourfold assembly, too, remained silent for these fifty small kalpas; yet by the Buddha's own majestic power, he caused the whole great assembly to feel that no more than half a day had passed. At that time the fourfold assembly, also by the Buddha's own majestic power, saw these bodhisattvas filling the empty space of immeasurable hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of lands.

Chinese Source

是菩薩眾中有四導師：一名上行，二名無邊行，三名淨行，四名安立行。是四菩薩，於其眾中最為上首唱導之師，在大眾前，各共合掌，觀釋迦牟尼佛而問訊言：「世尊！少病、少惱，安樂行不？所應度者，受教易不？不令世尊生疲勞耶？」 爾時四大菩薩而說偈言： 「世尊安樂，　少病少惱，　教化眾生， 得無疲惓。　又諸眾生，　受化易不？ 不令世尊，　生疲勞耶？」 爾時世尊，於菩薩大眾中而作是言：「如是，如是！諸善男子！如來安樂，少病、少惱；諸眾生等，易可化度，無有疲勞。所以者何？是諸眾生，世世已來常受我化，亦於過去諸佛供養尊重，種諸善根。此諸眾生，始見我身，聞我所說，即皆信受入如來慧；除先修習學小乘者。如是之人，我今亦令得聞是經，入於佛慧。」 爾時諸大菩薩而說偈言： 「善哉善哉！　大雄世尊！　諸眾生等， 易可化度。　能問諸佛，　甚深智慧， 聞已信行，　我等隨喜。」 於時世尊讚歎上首諸大菩薩：「善哉，善哉！善男子！汝等能於如來發隨喜心。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Among this assembly of bodhisattvas were four foremost teachers: the first named Viśiṣṭacāritra, the second Anantacāritra, the third Viśuddhacāritra, and the fourth Supratiṣṭhitacāritra. These four bodhisattvas were the very foremost among the assembly, its leading teachers, and standing before the great assembly, they joined their palms together and, looking upon Śākyamuni Buddha, greeted him, saying: "World-Honored One! Are you free of illness, free of affliction, and dwelling at ease? Are those who should be brought across easily receptive to your teaching? Does none of this cause the World-Honored One weariness?" At that time the four great bodhisattvas spoke this verse: "World-Honored One at peace, free of illness, free of affliction, teaching and transforming beings without any weariness — and are these beings, too, easily received into your teaching, causing the World-Honored One no weariness?" At that time the World-Honored One said, in the midst of this great assembly of bodhisattvas: "Just so, just so! Good sons! The Tathāgata is at peace, free of illness, free of affliction; and all these beings are easily brought across, without weariness. Why is this so? These beings, life after life until now, have constantly received my own teaching, and have, besides, in the presence of buddhas of the past, made offerings and shown reverence, planting every kind of good root. These beings, the very moment they first see my body and hear what I teach, at once believe and accept it, and enter into the Tathāgata's wisdom — excepting only those who have already trained themselves in, and grown attached to, the lesser vehicle. Even such people as these, I now cause to hear this sūtra as well, and to enter into the Buddha's wisdom." At that time the great bodhisattvas spoke this verse: "Excellent, excellent, great and courageous World-Honored One! These beings are indeed easily brought across. That you should be asked of the buddhas' own most profound wisdom, and, having heard it, believe and practice it — in this we too rejoice." At that time the World-Honored One praised these foremost great bodhisattvas: "Excellent, excellent, good sons! That you should give rise to a heart of rejoicing toward the Tathāgata."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi treats the descending list of retinues — sixty thousand Ganges-sands down to a single disciple, down to no disciple at all — as a deliberate inversion of ordinary expectation: read by worldly measure, the bodhisattva with sixty thousand followers seems greatest and the one entirely alone seems least, but the text's own arrangement, saving the solitary practitioner for last and noting that such figures "exceed" what came before, points the other way. Number is not the measure being offered; each of these bodhisattvas is himself, in Zhiyi's phrase, a guide capable of "leading others to the place of treasure," so that one alone is not truly solitary and sixty thousand is not truly many.

He also singles out the fifty small kalpas that the assembly experiences as half a day as itself a small rehearsal, in miniature, of the chapter's larger argument. What looks brief from where the assembly stands is disclosed, once the Buddha's power lifts the concealment, to have actually been vast — exactly the relationship, on Zhiyi's reading, between the "trace" of a Buddha awakened only forty years ago at Gayā and the far older "source" this whole chapter exists to gesture toward, without yet naming it directly.

3. Maitreya's Doubt, and the Buddha's Astonishing Answer

Chinese Source

爾時彌勒菩薩及八千恒河沙諸菩薩眾，皆作是念：「我等從昔已來，不見不聞如是大菩薩摩訶薩眾，從地踊出，住世尊前，合掌、供養，問訊如來。」 時彌勒菩薩摩訶薩，知八千恒河沙諸菩薩等心之所念，并欲自決所疑，合掌向佛，以偈問曰： 「無量千萬億，　大眾諸菩薩， 昔所未曾見，　願兩足尊說， 是從何所來，　以何因緣集？ 巨身大神通，　智慧叵思議， 其志念堅固，　有大忍辱力， 眾生所樂見，　為從何所來？ 一一諸菩薩，　所將諸眷屬， 其數無有量，　如恒河沙等。 或有大菩薩，　將六萬恒沙， 如是諸大眾，　一心求佛道。 是諸大師等，　六萬恒河沙， 俱來供養佛，　及護持是經。 將五萬恒沙，　其數過於是。 四萬及三萬、　二萬至一萬， 一千一百等，　乃至一恒沙、 半及三四分，　億萬分之一， 千萬那由他，　萬億諸弟子， 乃至於半億，　其數復過上。 百萬至一萬，　一千及一百， 五十與一十，　乃至三二一， 單己無眷屬，　樂於獨處者， 俱來至佛所，　其數轉過上。 如是諸大眾，　若人行籌數， 過於恒沙劫，　猶不能盡知。 是諸大威德，　精進菩薩眾， 誰為其說法，　教化而成就？ 從誰初發心？　稱揚何佛法？ 受持行誰經？　修習何佛道？ 如是諸菩薩，　神通大智力， 四方地震裂，　皆從中踊出。 世尊我昔來，　未曾見是事， 願說其所從，　國土之名號。 我常遊諸國，　未曾見是眾， 我於此眾中，　乃不識一人， 忽然從地出，　願說其因緣。 今此之大會，　無量百千億， 是諸菩薩等，　皆欲知此事。 是諸菩薩眾，　本末之因緣， 無量德世尊，　唯願決眾疑。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Maitreya Bodhisattva, and eight thousand Ganges-sands of bodhisattvas besides, all thought to themselves: "Never before, from the past until now, have we seen or heard of a great assembly of bodhisattva-mahāsattvas such as this — rising up from the earth, standing before the World-Honored One, joining their palms, making offerings, and greeting the Tathāgata." At that time Maitreya Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva, knowing what these eight thousand Ganges-sands of bodhisattvas were thinking in their hearts, and wishing besides to resolve his own doubt, joined his palms toward the Buddha and asked, in verse: "This immeasurable assembly of thousands of myriads of koṭis of bodhisattvas, never before seen until now — I beg the Two-Legged Honored One to tell us where they have come from, and by what cause and condition they have gathered here. Their bodies vast, their spiritual powers great, their wisdom beyond conceiving, their resolve firm and unshaken, possessed of great strength of forbearance, a joy for beings to behold — from where have they come? […] Such a great assembly as this, were one to count them one by one, could not be known to its end even through kalpas beyond the sands of the Ganges. These bodhisattvas of great and majestic virtue, this assembly given wholly to diligence — who has taught them the Dharma, and brought them to this accomplishment? From whom did they first set forth the resolve for awakening? Whose buddha-teaching do they proclaim? Whose sūtra do they uphold and practice? Whose buddha-path do they cultivate? Bodhisattvas such as these, with spiritual powers and great wisdom, from whom the earth itself split open in every direction — all of them rising up from within it. World-Honored One, from the past until now, I have never seen such a thing as this; I beg you to tell us where they came from, and the name of their land. I have constantly traveled through every land, and never before have I seen an assembly such as this; among this whole assembly, I do not recognize even a single face — and now, suddenly, they have risen from the earth. I beg you to tell us the cause and condition of this. This present great assembly, immeasurable hundreds of thousands of koṭis in number, every one of these bodhisattvas, too, wishes to know this matter. The cause and condition, the origin and outcome, of this whole assembly of bodhisattvas — World-Honored One of immeasurable virtue, I beg only that you resolve this whole assembly's doubt."

Chinese Source

爾時釋迦牟尼分身諸佛，從無量千萬億他方國土來者，在於八方諸寶樹下，師子座上，結加趺坐。其佛侍者，各各見是菩薩大眾，於三千大千世界四方，從地踊出，住於虛空。各白其佛言：「世尊！此諸無量無邊阿僧祇菩薩大眾，從何所來？」 爾時諸佛各告侍者：「諸善男子！且待須臾，有菩薩摩訶薩，名曰彌勒——釋迦牟尼佛之所授記，次後作佛——以問斯事，佛今答之，汝等自當因是得聞。」 爾時釋迦牟尼佛告彌勒菩薩：「善哉，善哉！阿逸多！乃能問佛如是大事。汝等當共一心，被精進鎧，發堅固意，如來今欲顯發宣示諸佛智慧、諸佛自在神通之力、諸佛師子奮迅之力、諸佛威猛大勢之力。」 爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「當精進一心，　我欲說此事， 勿得有疑悔，　佛智叵思議。 汝今出信力，　住於忍善中， 昔所未聞法，　今皆當得聞。 我今安慰汝，　勿得懷疑懼， 佛無不實語，　智慧不可量。 所得第一法，　甚深叵分別， 如是今當說，　汝等一心聽。」 爾時世尊說此偈已，告彌勒菩薩：「我今於此大眾，宣告汝等。阿逸多！是諸大菩薩摩訶薩，無量無數阿僧祇，從地踊出，汝等昔所未見者，我於是娑婆世界得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提已，教化示導是諸菩薩，調伏其心，令發道意。此諸菩薩，皆於是娑婆世界之下、此界虛空中住；於諸經典，讀誦通利，思惟分別，正憶念。阿逸多！是諸善男子等，不樂在眾多有所說；常樂靜處，懃行精進未曾休息；亦不依止人天而住。常樂深智，無有障礙，亦常樂於諸佛之法，一心精進，求無上慧。」 爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「阿逸汝當知！　是諸大菩薩， 從無數劫來，　修習佛智慧， 悉是我所化，　令發大道心。 此等是我子，　依止是世界， 常行頭陀事，　志樂於靜處， 捨大眾憒閙，　不樂多所說。 如是諸子等，　學習我道法， 晝夜常精進，　為求佛道故， 在娑婆世界，　下方空中住。 志念力堅固，　常懃求智慧， 說種種妙法，　其心無所畏。 我於伽耶城，　菩提樹下坐， 得成最正覺，　轉無上法輪。 爾乃教化之，　令初發道心， 今皆住不退，　悉當得成佛。 我今說實語，　汝等一心信， 我從久遠來，　教化是等眾。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time, Śākyamuni's own emanation buddhas, who had come from immeasurable thousands of myriads of koṭis of other lands, were seated cross-legged upon lion thrones beneath jeweled trees in every one of the eight directions. Their attendants, each seeing this great assembly of bodhisattvas rising up from the earth in all four directions of the three-thousand-great-thousandfold world and standing in the sky, each said to their own buddha: "World-Honored One! This immeasurable, boundless, incalculable great assembly of bodhisattvas — from where have they come?" At that time each of these buddhas said to his own attendant: "Good sons! Wait but a moment. There is a bodhisattva-mahāsattva named Maitreya — to whom Śākyamuni Buddha has given the prediction that he shall be the next to attain buddhahood — who has asked of this very matter, and the Buddha is now about to answer him. You shall, by means of this, come to hear it for yourselves." At that time Śākyamuni Buddha said to Maitreya Bodhisattva: "Excellent, excellent, Ajita! That you should ask the Buddha of so great a matter as this. All of you should, with a single mind, put on the armor of diligence, and give rise to firm resolve, for the Tathāgata now wishes to disclose and proclaim the wisdom of the buddhas, the power of the buddhas' own sovereign spiritual penetration, the power of the buddhas' lion's vigorous roar, and the power of the buddhas' own mighty and majestic strength." At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to proclaim this meaning once more, spoke in verse: "Be diligent, with a single mind — I am about to teach this matter; give rise to no doubt or regret, for the Buddha's wisdom is beyond conceiving. Bring forth now the power of your own faith, and stand firm within patience and goodness; what you have never heard before, you shall now hear in full. I now set your hearts at ease — hold no doubt or fear, for the Buddha speaks no word that is not true, and his wisdom cannot be measured. The foremost Dharma I have attained is profound beyond all distinction; this I shall now teach — listen, all of you, with a single mind." At that time the World-Honored One, having spoken this verse, said to Maitreya Bodhisattva: "I now proclaim this to you, before this whole great assembly. Ajita! These great bodhisattva-mahāsattvas, immeasurable and beyond number, incalculable, rising up from the earth, whom you have never seen before — it was I myself, since I attained unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening in this sahā world, who taught and guided these bodhisattvas, tamed their hearts, and caused them to bring forth the resolve for the path. Every one of these bodhisattvas has been dwelling beneath this very sahā world, in the space beneath it; in the various scriptures, they read and recite fluently, reflecting on and distinguishing their meaning, holding them rightly in mind. Ajita! These good sons do not delight in being among a crowd and having much to say; they constantly delight in quiet places, diligently exerting themselves without ever resting; nor do they dwell in dependence on gods or humans. They constantly delight in profound wisdom, free of every hindrance, and constantly delight, too, in the teachings of all buddhas, with a single mind seeking the unsurpassed wisdom with diligence." At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to proclaim this meaning once more, spoke in verse: "Ajita, you should know: these great bodhisattvas, from innumerable kalpas until now, have cultivated the buddha's own wisdom — every one of them taught and transformed by me, and caused to bring forth the great resolve for the path. These are my own children, dwelling in dependence on this world, constantly practicing the ascetic disciplines, their hearts set on quiet places, forsaking the clamor of the crowd, taking no delight in speaking at length. Such children as these study and practice my own teaching of the path, day and night constantly diligent, for the sake of seeking the buddha-path, dwelling in the sahā world, in the space beneath it. Their resolve and their strength are firm, constantly and diligently they seek wisdom, teaching every kind of wondrous Dharma, their hearts free of any fear. I, seated beneath the bodhi tree in the city of Gayā, attained the unsurpassed true awakening, and turned the wheel of the unsurpassed Dharma. Only then did I teach and transform them, causing them first to bring forth the resolve for the path — and now, every one of them dwells in the state that never turns back, and every one of them shall attain buddhahood. I now speak the truth — believe it, all of you, with a single mind: I have, from a time long, long past, taught and transformed this very assembly."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi reads this exchange as the sūtra's own hinge. Maitreya's question — asked, in the text's own logic, on behalf of an assembly that includes buddhas emanated across all ten directions, equally puzzled and equally unable to answer it — sets up the Buddha's reply as something closer to a provocation than a resolution: he does not say these bodhisattvas came from some other realm, but that he himself has personally taught and matured every one of them since his own awakening at Gayā, a plain historical claim that any listener doing the arithmetic — forty-odd years, against an assembly this vast, this accomplished, this ancient in bearing — should find impossible on its face. That is by design. Zhiyi's own name for what this chapter is doing is "briefly opening the trace to disclose the source, so as to unsettle attachment and give rise to doubt" (略開近顯遠，動執生疑) — not yet the fuller disclosure that resolves the doubt, only the deliberate provocation of it, so that the next chapter's answer can be heard as an answer to a question the audience has already been made to feel for itself.

4. "My Son Is Older Than I Am": The Parable Maitreya Cannot Yet Believe

Chinese Source

爾時，彌勒菩薩摩訶薩及無數諸菩薩等，心生疑惑，怪未曾有，而作是念：「云何世尊於少時間、教化如是無量無邊阿僧祇諸大菩薩，令住阿耨多羅三藐三菩提？」即白佛言：「世尊！如來為太子時，出於釋宮，去伽耶城不遠，坐於道場，得成阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。從是已來，始過四十餘年。世尊！云何於此少時，大作佛事，以佛勢力、以佛功德，教化如是無量大菩薩眾，當成阿耨多羅三藐三菩提？ 「世尊！此大菩薩眾，假使有人於千萬億劫數不能盡，不得其邊。斯等久遠已來，於無量無邊諸佛所，殖諸善根，成就菩薩道，常修梵行。世尊！如此之事，世所難信。譬如有人，色美髮黑，年二十五，指百歲人，言：『是我子。』其百歲人，亦指年少，言：『是我父，生育我等。』是事難信。佛亦如是，得道已來，其實未久，而此大眾諸菩薩等，已於無量千萬億劫，為佛道故，懃行精進，善入出住無量百千萬億三昧，得大神通，久修梵行，善能次第習諸善法，巧於問答，人中之寶，一切世間甚為希有。今日世尊方云：『得佛道時，初令發心，教化示導，令向阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。』世尊得佛未久，乃能作此大功德事。我等雖復信佛隨宜所說，佛所出言未曾虛妄，佛所知者皆悉通達。然諸新發意菩薩，於佛滅後，若聞是語，或不信受，而起破法罪業因緣。唯然，世尊！願為解說，除我等疑，及未來世諸善男子聞此事已，亦不生疑。」 爾時彌勒菩薩欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「佛昔從釋種，　出家近伽耶， 坐於菩提樹，　爾來尚未久。 此諸佛子等，　其數不可量， 久已行佛道，　住於神通力， 善學菩薩道，　不染世間法， 如蓮華在水，　從地而踊出。 皆起恭敬心，　住於世尊前。 是事難思議，　云何而可信？ 佛得道甚近，　所成就甚多， 願為除眾疑，　如實分別說。 譬如少壯人，　年始二十五， 示人百歲子，　髮白而面皺， 是等我所生；　子亦說是父。 父少而子老，　舉世所不信。 世尊亦如是，　得道來甚近。 是諸菩薩等，　志固無怯弱， 從無量劫來，　而行菩薩道， 巧於難問答，　其心無所畏， 忍辱心決定，　端正有威德， 十方佛所讚。　善能分別說， 不樂在人眾，　常好在禪定， 為求佛道故，　於下空中住。 我等從佛聞，　於此事無疑， 願佛為未來，　演說令開解。 若有於此經，　生疑不信者， 即當墮惡道。　願今為解說， 是無量菩薩，　云何於少時， 教化令發心，　而住不退地？」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Maitreya Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva, and countless bodhisattvas besides, gave rise to doubt and perplexity in their hearts, astonished at what they had never known before, and thought: "How could the World-Honored One, in so brief a span of time, have taught and transformed so immeasurable, boundless, and incalculable an assembly of great bodhisattvas, and caused them to abide in unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening?" He then said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! When the Tathāgata, as a prince, went forth from the Śākya palace, he traveled only a short way from the city of Gayā, sat at the place of awakening, and there attained unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. From that time until now, only a little more than forty years have passed. World-Honored One, how, in so brief a span of time, could you have accomplished such great buddha-work — by the Buddha's own power, by the Buddha's own merit, teaching and transforming so immeasurable a host of great bodhisattvas, that they shall attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening? "World-Honored One! Even were someone to try, for a thousand myriad koṭis of kalpas, they could not exhaust this great assembly of bodhisattvas, nor reach its outer limit. These bodhisattvas, from a time long, long past, have planted every good root in the presence of immeasurable, boundless buddhas, accomplished the bodhisattva path, and constantly cultivated the pure conduct. World-Honored One, a thing such as this is hard for the world to believe. It is as if there were a man, his complexion fine and his hair still black, only twenty-five years of age, who pointed to a man of a hundred years and said: 'This is my own son' — and that man of a hundred years, too, pointed to the young man and said: 'This is my own father, who gave birth to and raised me.' Such a thing is hard to believe. The Buddha is exactly the same: it has, in truth, not been long since he attained the path, and yet this whole great assembly of bodhisattvas has already, for immeasurable thousands of myriads of koṭis of kalpas, diligently exerted themselves for the sake of the buddha-path, skillfully entering, emerging from, and abiding within immeasurable hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of samādhis, attaining great spiritual powers, long cultivating the pure conduct, well able to practice every wholesome teaching in due order, skilled in question and answer, treasures among humankind, exceedingly rare in the whole world. Yet today the World-Honored One says: 'At the moment I attained the buddha-path, I first caused them to bring forth their resolve, taught and guided them, and led them toward unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening.' It has not been long since the World-Honored One attained buddhahood, and yet he has been able to accomplish so great a work of merit as this. Though we ourselves do believe what the Buddha teaches according to what is fitting — for the Buddha's own words are never false, and what the Buddha knows he understands in full — still, bodhisattvas newly resolved upon awakening, hearing these words after the Buddha's own parinirvāṇa, may not believe or accept them, and so give rise to the causes and conditions of the offense of destroying the Dharma. We beg only, World-Honored One, that you explain this for us, removing our own doubt, so that good sons of the age to come, too, upon hearing of this matter, may not give rise to doubt of their own." At that time Maitreya Bodhisattva, wishing to proclaim this meaning once more, spoke in verse: "The Buddha, in the past, went forth from the Śākya clan, and near the city of Gayā, sat beneath the bodhi tree — and it has still not been long since then. Yet these children of the Buddha, whose number cannot be measured, have long practiced the buddha-path, abiding in the power of spiritual penetration, well trained in the bodhisattva path, unstained by any worldly thing, like a lotus flower standing in water — rising up now from the earth itself. All of them give rise to a reverent heart, standing before the World-Honored One. This matter is beyond conceiving — how can it be believed? The Buddha attained the path only a very short time ago, and yet has accomplished so much. We beg you to remove this whole assembly's doubt, and explain it exactly as it truly is. It is as if a man in the prime of youth, only twenty-five years of age, were to show to others a son of a hundred years, his hair white, his face lined with age, saying: 'This is the one I gave birth to' — and the son, too, were to call the young man his own father. A father young, and a son old — this the whole world would fail to believe. The World-Honored One is exactly the same: he attained the path only a very short time ago. Yet these bodhisattvas, their resolve firm and without any timidity, have, from immeasurable kalpas until now, practiced the bodhisattva path, skilled in answering the hardest questions, their hearts free of fear, their resolve for forbearance decided and unshakable, upright and possessed of majestic virtue, praised by the buddhas of the ten directions — well able to explain and distinguish the Dharma, taking no delight in being among the crowd, constantly favoring meditative concentration, dwelling, for the sake of seeking the buddha-path, in the space beneath the earth. We ourselves, having heard this from the Buddha, hold no doubt in this matter — yet we beg the Buddha, for the sake of the ages to come, to teach and explain it, that they too may come to understand. For if there is anyone who, hearing this sūtra, gives rise to doubt and disbelief, that person shall at once fall into an evil path. We beg you now to explain: how, in so brief a span of time, could this immeasurable host of bodhisattvas have been taught, and caused to bring forth their resolve, and made to abide in the ground that never turns back?"

Reading note

This chapter ends here, with the question still entirely open — a structural choice worth noticing rather than reading past. Maitreya's own parable is doing careful work: it is not a claim that the Buddha is lying, but a claim that the plain reading of his words, taken at face value, describes something no one in the audience has ever seen happen, a father younger than his own son. Maitreya explicitly worries less for himself, who says he does not doubt, than for "bodhisattvas newly resolved upon awakening" who will hear this claim after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, with no one left to explain it — which is to say, for readers of this very sūtra, at whatever distance, encountering exactly this passage. The next chapter is titled 如來壽量品, "the Tathāgata's Lifespan," and its entire business is to answer the question this chapter has spent its full length making sure the reader feels needs answering.
