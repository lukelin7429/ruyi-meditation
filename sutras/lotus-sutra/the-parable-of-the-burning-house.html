The Parable of the Burning House

譬喻品第三（上）

Śāriputra, foremost of the Buddha's disciples in wisdom, confesses that he has spent years secretly reproaching himself, certain he had somehow failed. The Buddha answers by predicting his future buddhahood — and then, at Śāriputra's own request, tells the parable this whole sūtra is most remembered for: a father who lures his children from a burning house with the promise of three different carts, and gives every one of them, instead, the very same single, far greater cart. Was that a lie? Śāriputra says no — and his reasoning is the doctrinal hinge on which the last two chapters turn.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
Śāriputra's joyful confession that he had spent years secretly reproaching himself, believing he had failed to attain what the bodhisattvas were promised; the Buddha's prediction that Śāriputra will one day become a buddha named Flower Light; the assembly's rejoicing, and the devas' declaration that this prediction is itself a second turning of the Dharma-wheel; and, at Śāriputra's request, the parable of a father who rescues his children from a burning house by promising three different carts, then gives every child the identical single, far greater cart instead.
Why it matters
Everything Part 2 and Part 3 declared abstractly — that the three vehicles were always skillful means for the one — is here dramatized as a story with a father, children, a fire, and a promise. The question the parable exists to settle is asked directly, in the text's own words: was the father's promise of three different carts a lie, now that only one is actually given? Śāriputra's answer is the sūtra's own resolution of the doctrinal claim made in the two chapters before this one.

1. Śāriputra's Joy, and His Confession of Self-Doubt

Chinese Source

爾時舍利弗踊躍歡喜，即起合掌，瞻仰尊顏而白佛言：「今從世尊聞此法音，心懷勇躍，得未曾有。所以者何？我昔從佛聞如是法，見諸菩薩授記作佛，而我等不豫斯事，甚自感傷，失於如來無量知見。世尊！我常獨處山林樹下，若坐若行，每作是念：『我等同入法性，云何如來以小乘法而見濟度？是我等咎，非世尊也。所以者何？若我等待說所因成就阿耨多羅三藐三菩提者，必以大乘而得度脫。然我等不解方便隨宜所說，初聞佛法，遇便信受、思惟、取證。』世尊！我從昔來，終日竟夜每自剋責，而今從佛聞所未聞未曾有法，斷諸疑悔，身意泰然，快得安隱。今日乃知真是佛子，從佛口生，從法化生，得佛法分。」 爾時舍利弗欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「我聞是法音，　得所未曾有， 心懷大歡喜，　疑網皆已除。 昔來蒙佛教，　不失於大乘， 佛音甚希有，　能除眾生惱， 我已得漏盡，　聞亦除憂惱。 我處於山谷，　或在林樹下， 若坐若經行，　常思惟是事， 嗚呼深自責，　云何而自欺？ 我等亦佛子，　同入無漏法， 不能於未來，　演說無上道。 金色三十二，　十力諸解脫， 同共一法中，　而不得此事； 八十種妙好，　十八不共法， 如是等功德，　而我皆已失。 我獨經行時，　見佛在大眾， 名聞滿十方，　廣饒益眾生， 自惟失此利，　我為自欺誑。 我常於日夜，　每思惟是事， 欲以問世尊，　為失為不失？ 我常見世尊，　稱讚諸菩薩， 以是於日夜，　籌量如此事。 今聞佛音聲，　隨宜而說法， 無漏難思議，　令眾至道場。 我本著邪見，　為諸梵志師， 世尊知我心，　拔邪說涅槃， 我悉除邪見，　於空法得證， 爾時心自謂，　得至於滅度， 而今乃自覺，　非是實滅度。 若得作佛時，　具三十二相， 天人夜叉眾，　龍神等恭敬， 是時乃可謂，　永盡滅無餘。 佛於大眾中，　說我當作佛， 聞如是法音，　疑悔悉已除。 初聞佛所說，　心中大驚疑， 將非魔作佛，　惱亂我心耶？ 佛以種種緣、　譬喻巧言說， 其心安如海，　我聞疑網斷。 佛說過去世，　無量滅度佛， 安住方便中，　亦皆說是法， 現在未來佛，　其數無有量， 亦以諸方便，　演說如是法， 如今者世尊，　從生及出家、 得道轉法輪，　亦以方便說。 世尊說實道，　波旬無此事， 以是我定知，　非是魔作佛。 我墮疑網故，　謂是魔所為， 聞佛柔軟音，　深遠甚微妙， 演暢清淨法，　我心大歡喜， 疑悔永已盡，　安住實智中， 我定當作佛，　為天人所敬， 轉無上法輪，　教化諸菩薩。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Śāriputra, leaping for joy, at once rose, joined his palms, and, gazing up at the Buddha's honored countenance, said to him: "Now, hearing this voice of the Dharma from the World-Honored One, my heart leaps with courage, and I have gained what I never had before. Why is this so? Because in the past I heard the Buddha teach such a Dharma, and saw bodhisattvas receive predictions of buddhahood, while we ourselves had no part in this — and I grieved deeply over it, feeling that I had lost the Tathāgata's immeasurable knowledge and vision. World-Honored One! I have often dwelt alone in the mountain forests, whether sitting or walking, always thinking to myself: 'We too have entered equally into the nature of all things — why has the Tathāgata delivered us across by means of a lesser vehicle? This is our own fault, not the World-Honored One's. Why is this so? Because if we had waited for him to teach the causes by which unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening is accomplished, we would surely have been delivered by the Great Vehicle. But we did not understand that he was teaching according to what was fitting by skillful means, and when we first heard the Buddha's Dharma, we simply believed and accepted it, reflected on it, and took it as our realization.' World-Honored One! From that time on, day and night, I have constantly reproached myself; and now, hearing from the Buddha a Dharma never heard before, unprecedented, I have cut off every doubt and regret, my body and mind are at ease, and I have found true peace. Only today do I know that I am truly a child of the Buddha, born from the Buddha's own mouth, born through transformation by the Dharma, and one who has received a portion of the Buddha's own Dharma." At that time Śāriputra, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "Hearing this voice of the Dharma, I have gained what I never had before; my heart is filled with great joy, and every net of doubt has now been removed. From long ago I received the Buddha's teaching, and never lost the Great Vehicle; the Buddha's voice is exceedingly rare, able to remove the afflictions of beings. I have already exhausted my defilements, yet hearing it has removed my sorrow and affliction too. Dwelling in mountain valleys, or beneath forest trees, whether sitting or walking back and forth, I have constantly reflected on this matter — ah, how deeply I reproached myself: how could I have deceived myself so? We too are children of the Buddha, who have entered equally into the undefiled Dharma; yet we were unable, in the future, to expound the unsurpassed path. The golden-colored thirty-two marks, the ten powers, the various liberations — sharing together in this one same Dharma, and yet not attaining this; the eighty subtle excellences, the eighteen exclusive qualities — such merits as these, all of them I had already lost. When I walked alone, back and forth, and saw the Buddha in the great assembly, his renown filling the ten directions, broadly benefiting beings, I considered that I had lost this benefit, and that I had deceived and cheated myself. I constantly, day and night, reflected on this matter, wishing to ask the World-Honored One: have I lost it, or have I not? I constantly saw the World-Honored One praising the bodhisattvas, and for this reason, day and night, I pondered this very matter. Now, hearing the Buddha's voice teaching the Dharma according to what is fitting, undefiled and inconceivable, causing the assembly to reach the seat of enlightenment — I who was once attached to wrong views, a teacher among the brahmacārins, the World-Honored One, knowing my mind, drew out my wrong views and taught me nirvāṇa. I entirely removed my wrong views, and attained realization in the Dharma of emptiness; at that time I told myself in my own mind that I had reached extinction — yet only now do I realize for myself that this was not the true extinction. If, when one attains buddhahood, one is complete with the thirty-two marks, and is revered by gods, humans, yakṣas, dragons, and spirits — only then can it truly be called the eternal, utter extinction without remainder. The Buddha, in the midst of the great assembly, has said that I shall become a buddha; hearing this voice of the Dharma, every doubt and regret has now been entirely removed. When I first heard what the Buddha taught, my heart was greatly startled and in doubt: could this not be Māra, appearing as a buddha, troubling and confusing my mind? The Buddha, through every kind of cause and skillfully spoken parable, made my mind as settled as the sea; hearing him, the net of my doubt was severed. The Buddha spoke of the immeasurable buddhas of the past who entered parinirvāṇa, who, abiding within skillful means, likewise all taught this same Dharma; the buddhas of the present and future, whose number is beyond measure, likewise, by every skillful means, expound this same Dharma. As the World-Honored One of the present, from his birth and going forth from home, to his attainment of the path and turning of the Dharma-wheel — this too was taught by skillful means. The World-Honored One teaches the true path; Māra has no part in this. By this I know for certain: this is not Māra appearing as a buddha. Because I had fallen into a net of doubt, I supposed it might be Māra's doing; but hearing the Buddha's gentle voice, profound, far-reaching, and exceedingly subtle, expounding the pure Dharma, my heart was filled with great joy, my doubt and regret forever exhausted, and I now abide within true wisdom. I am certain that I shall become a buddha, revered by gods and humans, turning the unsurpassed wheel of the Dharma, teaching and transforming the bodhisattvas."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi divides the entire "first round" of teaching — the Dharma round addressed to the sharpest faculties, spanning what this site presents as Parts 2 through 5 — into five successive movements: the Buddha's direct statement (法說, Parts 2–3), Śāriputra's understanding (身子領解, this section), the Buddha's confirmation that Śāriputra has understood correctly (佛述成, the next section), the bestowal of Śāriputra's prediction (與授記, also the next section), and the fourfold assembly's rejoicing (四眾歡喜, Section 3 below). What is being read here, in other words, is not a new topic but the second of five formal stages in a single, deliberately structured teaching event.

The content of that understanding is worth sitting with on its own terms, apart from the scheme that contains it. Śāriputra — repeatedly named in this sūtra's own text as foremost among the Buddha's disciples in wisdom — describes years of private self-reproach, alone in mountain forests, certain that his own attainment was somehow less than what the bodhisattvas were being promised, and blaming himself rather than the Buddha for it. He even describes a moment of doubting the Buddha himself: on first hearing this new teaching, wondering whether it might be Māra in disguise. Nothing in the text suggests this doubt was foolish or a mark of weak faith; the sūtra offers no rebuke of it. What resolves it is not a demonstration but recognition — the sound of a voice he already trusted, teaching in a manner consistent with how he understood all buddhas to teach. The reasoning that settles his doubt about the teacher is, notably, the same reasoning by which the parable he is about to hear will settle the question of whether the teacher's earlier promises were kept.

2. The Buddha's Prediction: Śāriputra Will Become Flower Light Buddha

Chinese Source

爾時佛告舍利弗：「吾今於天、人、沙門、婆羅門等大眾中說，我昔曾於二萬億佛所，為無上道故，常教化汝，汝亦長夜隨我受學。我以方便引導汝故，生我法中。舍利弗！我昔教汝志願佛道，汝今悉忘，而便自謂已得滅度。我今還欲令汝憶念本願所行道故，為諸聲聞說是大乘經，名妙法蓮華，教菩薩法，佛所護念。 「舍利弗！汝於未來世，過無量無邊不可思議劫，供養若干千萬億佛，奉持正法，具足菩薩所行之道，當得作佛，號曰華光如來、應供、正遍知、明行足、善逝、世間解、無上士、調御丈夫、天人師、佛、世尊。國名離垢，其土平正，清淨嚴飾，安隱豐樂，天人熾盛。琉璃為地，有八交道，黃金為繩以界其側。其傍各有七寶行樹，常有華菓。華光如來亦以三乘教化眾生。 「舍利弗！彼佛出時，雖非惡世，以本願故，說三乘法。其劫名大寶莊嚴。何故名曰大寶莊嚴？其國中以菩薩為大寶故。彼諸菩薩，無量無邊，不可思議，算數譬喻所不能及，非佛智力無能知者。若欲行時，寶華承足。此諸菩薩，非初發意，皆久殖德本，於無量百千萬億佛所淨修梵行，恒為諸佛之所稱歎。常修佛慧，具大神通，善知一切諸法之門，質直無偽，志念堅固。如是菩薩，充滿其國。 「舍利弗！華光佛壽十二小劫，除為王子未作佛時。其國人民，壽八小劫。華光如來過十二小劫，授堅滿菩薩阿耨多羅三藐三菩提記。告諸比丘：『是堅滿菩薩次當作佛，號曰華足安行多陀阿伽度、阿羅訶、三藐三佛陀，其佛國土亦復如是。』 「舍利弗！是華光佛滅度之後，正法住世三十二小劫，像法住世亦三十二小劫。」 爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「舍利弗來世，　成佛普智尊， 號名曰華光，　當度無量眾。 供養無數佛，　具足菩薩行， 十力等功德，　證於無上道。 過無量劫已，　劫名大寶嚴， 世界名離垢，　清淨無瑕穢， 以琉璃為地，　金繩界其道， 七寶雜色樹，　常有華菓實。 彼國諸菩薩，　志念常堅固， 神通波羅蜜，　皆已悉具足， 於無數佛所，　善學菩薩道， 如是等大士，　華光佛所化。 佛為王子時，　棄國捨世榮， 於最末後身，　出家成佛道。 華光佛住世，　壽十二小劫， 其國人民眾，　壽命八小劫。 佛滅度之後，　正法住於世， 三十二小劫，　廣度諸眾生。 正法滅盡已，　像法三十二， 舍利廣流布，　天人普供養。 華光佛所為，　其事皆如是， 其兩足聖尊，　最勝無倫匹， 彼即是汝身，　宜應自欣慶。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Buddha said to Śāriputra: "Now, in the midst of this great assembly of gods, humans, śramaṇas, and brahmins, I declare: in the past, in the presence of twenty thousand koṭis of buddhas, for the sake of the unsurpassed path, I constantly taught and transformed you, and you too, through the long night of saṃsāra, followed me and received my teaching. By skillful means I guided you, and so you were born within my Dharma. Śāriputra! Long ago I taught you to set your will on the buddha-path, but you have now entirely forgotten this, and have simply supposed that you had already attained extinction. I now wish to cause you to remember once more the path of your original vow, and for this reason I teach this sūtra of the Great Vehicle, called the Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower, to the śrāvakas — a teaching for bodhisattvas, guarded and held in mind by the Buddha himself. "Śāriputra! In a future age, after immeasurable, boundless, inconceivable kalpas, having made offerings to some thousands of myriads of koṭis of buddhas, upholding the true Dharma, and being complete in the path a bodhisattva practices, you shall attain buddhahood, named Flower Light Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One. Your land will be named Free of Filth, its ground level and smooth, pure and adorned, peaceful and abundant, thronged with gods and humans. Its ground will be of lapis lazuli, with eight intersecting roads, bordered on their sides by cords of gold. Along them will stand rows of trees of the seven treasures, always bearing flowers and fruit. The Buddha Flower Light will likewise teach and transform beings by the three vehicles. "Śāriputra! At the time that buddha appears, although it will not be an evil age, he will teach the Dharma of three vehicles because of his original vow. That kalpa will be named Great Jewel Adornment. Why is it named Great Jewel Adornment? Because in that land the bodhisattvas themselves are its great jewel. Those bodhisattvas will be immeasurable, boundless, and inconceivable, beyond the reach of any calculation or parable — none but the Buddha's own power of wisdom could know their number. When they wish to walk, jeweled flowers will support their feet. These bodhisattvas will not be newly resolved in their intent; all will have long planted roots of virtue, purely cultivated pure conduct in the presence of immeasurable hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of buddhas, and been constantly praised by those buddhas. They will constantly cultivate the Buddha's wisdom, be complete in great spiritual powers, skillfully know the gateway to all things, be upright and without deceit, and firm in their resolve. Bodhisattvas such as these will fill that land entirely. "Śāriputra! The Buddha Flower Light's lifespan will be twelve small kalpas, not counting the time he was a prince before becoming a buddha. The people of his land will have a lifespan of eight small kalpas. After twelve small kalpas, the Tathāgata Flower Light will bestow on the bodhisattva Steadfast Fullness a prediction of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, declaring to the monks: 'This bodhisattva Steadfast Fullness shall next become a buddha, named Flower-Footed, Safely-Walking Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened; his buddha-land, too, will be just the same.' "Śāriputra! After this Buddha Flower Light's parinirvāṇa, the true Dharma will remain in the world for thirty-two small kalpas, and the counterfeit Dharma will remain in the world likewise for thirty-two small kalpas." At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "Śāriputra, in an age to come, shall become a buddha, honored for wisdom that reaches all, named Flower Light, and shall deliver immeasurable beings across. Having made offerings to countless buddhas, complete in the practice of a bodhisattva, the ten powers and every merit besides, he will realize the unsurpassed path. After immeasurable kalpas have passed, in a kalpa named Great Jewel Adornment, in a world named Free of Filth, pure and without blemish, with lapis lazuli for its ground, bordered by cords of gold along its roads, with trees of every color of the seven treasures, always bearing flowers and fruit — the bodhisattvas of that land, ever firm in their resolve, complete in every spiritual power and pāramitā, having well studied the bodhisattva path in the presence of countless buddhas — great beings such as these are those whom the Buddha Flower Light has transformed. When that buddha was a prince, he renounced his kingdom and cast aside the glory of the world; in his very last body, he went forth from home and accomplished the buddha-path. The Buddha Flower Light will abide in the world for a lifespan of twelve small kalpas; the people of his land will have a lifespan of eight small kalpas. After that buddha's parinirvāṇa, the true Dharma will abide in the world for thirty-two small kalpas, broadly delivering beings across. When the true Dharma has entirely come to an end, the counterfeit Dharma will last thirty-two kalpas more; his relics will be widely distributed, and gods and humans will everywhere make offerings to them. Everything the Buddha Flower Light does will be just as I have said; that most honored one among the two-footed will be supreme, without equal — that one is you yourself. You should rejoice in this."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

This is the sūtra's first individual prediction of buddhahood, and its formula — a future name, a land, a kalpa, a lifespan, the span of the true and counterfeit Dharma afterward, and often a further prediction handed on to a disciple of that future buddha in turn — recurs with variations for every disciple who receives one in the chapters ahead. Zhiyi treats the specific details less as independent symbolism to be decoded one by one than as evidence of scale and permanence: an immeasurable retinue of advanced bodhisattvas, a lifespan and a Dharma-duration measured in kalpas rather than years, are the sūtra's way of making the promise concrete rather than merely asserting it in the abstract. The sūtra itself does not pause to explain why this particular land is glass-floored or why the roads are bordered in gold; the plain sense of the passage is that these are the furnishings of an inconceivably vast and stable place, not details bearing individual doctrinal weight.

One detail Zhiyi does dwell on: this buddha, though appearing in a land that is "not an evil age" — unlike Śākyamuni's own five-turbid world — will nonetheless still teach the three vehicles first, "because of his original vow" (以本願故). Even in the best of circumstances, even for a buddha under no external pressure from a corrupted age, the pattern holds: skillful means comes before the single vehicle is opened. Whatever explanatory weight Part 3 placed on beings' turbidity as the reason three vehicles were taught, this passage adds that turbidity was not the only reason — a vow, freely made, can lead a buddha to the same sequence even where turbidity is absent.

3. The Assembly Rejoices, and Śāriputra Asks for the Parable

Chinese Source

爾時四部眾，比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷，天、龍、夜叉、乾闥婆、阿修羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽等大眾，見舍利弗於佛前受阿耨多羅三藐三菩提記，心大歡喜，踊躍無量，各各脫身所著上衣、以供養佛。釋提桓因、梵天王等，與無數天子，亦以天妙衣、天曼陀羅華、摩訶曼陀羅華等，供養於佛——所散天衣，住虛空中，而自迴轉；諸天伎樂百千萬種，於虛空中一時俱作，雨眾天華——而作是言：「佛昔於波羅㮈初轉法輪，今乃復轉無上最大法輪。」 爾時諸天子欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「昔於波羅㮈，　轉四諦法輪， 分別說諸法，　五眾之生滅。 今復轉最妙，　無上大法輪， 是法甚深奧，　少有能信者。 我等從昔來，　數聞世尊說， 未曾聞如是，　深妙之上法。 世尊說是法，　我等皆隨喜， 大智舍利弗，　今得受尊記， 我等亦如是，　必當得作佛， 於一切世間，　最尊無有上， 佛道叵思議，　方便隨宜說， 我所有福業，　今世若過世， 及見佛功德，　盡迴向佛道。」 爾時舍利弗白佛言：「世尊！我今無復疑悔，親於佛前得受阿耨多羅三藐三菩提記。是諸千二百心自在者，昔住學地，佛常教化言：『我法能離生老病死，究竟涅槃。』是學無學人，亦各自以離我見及有無見等謂得涅槃；而今於世尊前聞所未聞，皆墮疑惑。善哉，世尊！願為四眾說其因緣，令離疑悔。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the fourfold assembly — monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen — and the gods, dragons, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, and the rest of the great assembly, seeing Śāriputra receive, before the Buddha, a prediction of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, were filled with great joy, leaping and rejoicing beyond measure, and each of them took off the upper robe they were wearing and offered it up to the Buddha. Śakra, lord of the gods, and the Brahmā king, together with countless sons of the gods, likewise made offerings to the Buddha with wondrous heavenly robes and heavenly māndārava and great māndārava flowers — and the heavenly robes they scattered hung in the air, turning of themselves, while hundreds of thousands of myriads of kinds of heavenly music all played at once in the sky, and a rain of heavenly flowers fell — and they said: "Long ago the Buddha first turned the wheel of the Dharma at Vārāṇasī; now he turns once more the unsurpassed, greatest wheel of the Dharma." At that time the sons of the gods, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, spoke in verse: "Long ago, at Vārāṇasī, he turned the wheel of the Dharma of the Four Truths, distinguishing and teaching all things, the arising and passing of the five aggregates. Now he turns once more the most wondrous, unsurpassed great wheel of the Dharma; this Dharma is exceedingly profound and deep, and few are able to believe it. We, from long ago until now, have often heard the World-Honored One teach, yet never before have we heard so profound and subtle a supreme Dharma as this. The World-Honored One teaches this Dharma, and we all rejoice with him. The great wisdom of Śāriputra has now received this honored prediction; we too, in just the same way, shall surely attain buddhahood. Among all the worlds, most honored, without any above him, the Buddha's path is inconceivable, taught according to what is fitting by skillful means. Whatever merit we possess, in this present life or in lives past, and whatever we have gained from seeing the Buddha's virtues — all of it we dedicate entirely to the buddha-path." At that time Śāriputra said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! I now have no more doubt or regret, having received in person, before the Buddha, a prediction of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. But these twelve hundred whose minds have attained mastery, who once dwelt in the stage of training, the Buddha has constantly taught, saying: 'My Dharma is able to free you from birth, old age, sickness, and death, and to bring you to the ultimate nirvāṇa.' These, whether still in training or beyond it, have also each, by freeing themselves from the view of a self and from views of existence and non-existence, supposed that they had attained nirvāṇa; yet now, before the World-Honored One, hearing what they have never heard, they have all fallen into doubt and confusion. I beg you, World-Honored One, to explain for the fourfold assembly the cause and condition of this, so that they may be freed from doubt and regret."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

The devas' own gloss on what has just happened is worth taking at face value: they call Śāriputra's prediction a second turning of the Dharma-wheel, explicitly naming the first — the Four Noble Truths taught to five ascetics at Vārāṇasī, recounted in Śākyamuni's own first-person verse only one part earlier — as its precedent. This closes a loop this site's commentary has been tracking since Part 2's account of the "three rounds" of teaching: the wheel first turned for the dullest possible audience, in the plainest possible terms, and now turns again, for an audience able to receive something the first turning could not yet carry. Nothing is retracted in the second turning; the text calls it a further turning of the same wheel, not a different one.

Śāriputra's closing request is also a hinge the rest of this chapter depends on. His own doubt is resolved — but he asks the Buddha to explain the same matter for the twelve hundred others who received an identical promise of buddhahood in Part 3's closing line and are, the text says plainly, still confused about what it means. The parable that follows is the Buddha's answer to that specific request: not a general illustration, but a direct response to an assembly that has just been told something wonderful and does not yet understand how it can be true.

4. The Parable of the Burning House

Chinese Source

爾時佛告舍利弗：「我先不言：『諸佛世尊以種種因緣、譬喻言辭方便說法，皆為阿耨多羅三藐三菩提耶？』是諸所說，皆為化菩薩故。然，舍利弗！今當復以譬喻更明此義，諸有智者以譬喻得解。 「舍利弗！若國邑聚落有大長者，其年衰邁，財富無量，多有田宅及諸僮僕。其家廣大，唯有一門，多諸人眾，一百、二百乃至五百人，止住其中。堂閣朽故，牆壁隤落，柱根腐敗，梁棟傾危，周匝俱時歘然火起，焚燒舍宅。長者諸子，若十、二十，或至三十，在此宅中。長者見是大火從四面起，即大驚怖，而作是念：『我雖能於此所燒之門安隱得出，而諸子等，於火宅內樂著嬉戲，不覺不知、不驚不怖，火來逼身，苦痛切己，心不厭患，無求出意。』 「舍利弗！是長者作是思惟：『我身手有力，當以衣裓、若以机案，從舍出之。』復更思惟：『是舍唯有一門，而復狹小。諸子幼稚，未有所識，戀著戲處，或當墮落，為火所燒。我當為說怖畏之事，此舍已燒，宜時疾出，無令為火之所燒害。』作是念已，如所思惟，具告諸子，汝等速出。父雖憐愍、善言誘喻，而諸子等樂著嬉戲，不肯信受，不驚不畏，了無出心；亦復不知何者是火？何者為舍？云何為失？但東西走戲，視父而已。 「爾時長者即作是念：『此舍已為大火所燒，我及諸子若不時出，必為所焚。我今當設方便，令諸子等得免斯害。』父知諸子先心各有所好種種珍玩奇異之物，情必樂著，而告之言：『汝等所可玩好，希有難得，汝若不取，後必憂悔。如此種種羊車、鹿車、牛車，今在門外，可以遊戲。汝等於此火宅、宜速出來，隨汝所欲，皆當與汝。』爾時諸子聞父所說珍玩之物，適其願故，心各勇銳，互相推排，競共馳走，爭出火宅。是時長者見諸子等安隱得出，皆於四衢道中露地而坐，無復障礙，其心泰然，歡喜踊躍。時諸子等各白父言：『父先所許玩好之具，羊車、鹿車、牛車，願時賜與。』 「舍利弗！爾時長者各賜諸子等一大車，其車高廣，眾寶莊校，周匝欄楯，四面懸鈴；又於其上張設幰蓋，亦以珍奇雜寶而嚴飾之，寶繩絞絡，垂諸華纓，重敷綩綖，安置丹枕。駕以白牛，膚色充潔，形體姝好，有大筋力，行步平正，其疾如風；又多僕從而侍衛之。所以者何？是大長者財富無量，種種諸藏悉皆充溢，而作是念：『我財物無極，不應以下劣小車與諸子等。今此幼童，皆是吾子，愛無偏黨。我有如是七寶大車，其數無量，應當等心各各與之，不宜差別。所以者何？以我此物，周給一國猶尚不匱，何況諸子！』是時諸子各乘大車，得未曾有，非本所望。 「舍利弗！於汝意云何，是長者等與諸子珍寶大車，寧有虛妄不？」 舍利弗言：「不也，世尊！是長者但令諸子得免火難，全其軀命，非為虛妄。何以故？若全身命，便為已得玩好之具，況復方便於彼火宅而拔濟之。世尊！若是長者，乃至不與最小一車，猶不虛妄。何以故？是長者先作是意：『我以方便令子得出。』以是因緣，無虛妄也。何況長者自知財富無量，欲饒益諸子，等與大車。」 佛告舍利弗：「善哉，善哉！如汝所言。舍利弗！如來亦復如是，則為一切世間之父。於諸怖畏、衰惱、憂患、無明闇蔽，永盡無餘，而悉成就無量知見、力、無所畏，有大神力及智慧力，具足方便、智慧波羅蜜，大慈大悲，常無懈惓，恒求善事，利益一切，而生三界朽故火宅，為度眾生生老病死、憂悲、苦惱、愚癡、闇蔽、三毒之火，教化令得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。見諸眾生為生老病死、憂悲苦惱之所燒煮，亦以五欲財利故，受種種苦；又以貪著追求故，現受眾苦，後受地獄、畜生、餓鬼之苦；若生天上，及在人間，貧窮困苦、愛別離苦、怨憎會苦，如是等種種諸苦。眾生沒在其中，歡喜遊戲，不覺不知、不驚不怖，亦不生厭，不求解脫。於此三界火宅東西馳走，雖遭大苦，不以為患。 「舍利弗！佛見此已，便作是念：『我為眾生之父，應拔其苦難，與無量無邊佛智慧樂，令其遊戲。』 「舍利弗！如來復作是念：『若我但以神力及智慧力，捨於方便，為諸眾生讚如來知見、力無所畏者，眾生不能以是得度。所以者何？是諸眾生，未免生老病死、憂悲苦惱，而為三界火宅所燒；何由能解佛之智慧？』 「舍利弗！如彼長者，雖復身手有力而不用之，但以慇懃方便勉濟諸子火宅之難，然後各與珍寶大車。如來亦復如是，雖有力、無所畏而不用之，但以智慧方便，於三界火宅拔濟眾生，為說三乘——聲聞、辟支佛、佛乘，而作是言：『汝等莫得樂住三界火宅，勿貪麁弊色聲香味觸也。若貪著生愛，則為所燒。汝速出三界，當得三乘——聲聞、辟支佛、佛乘。我今為汝保任此事，終不虛也。汝等但當勤修精進。』如來以是方便誘進眾生，復作是言：『汝等當知此三乘法，皆是聖所稱歎，自在無繫，無所依求。乘是三乘，以無漏根、力、覺、道、禪定、解脫、三昧等而自娛樂，便得無量安隱快樂。』 「舍利弗！若有眾生內有智性，從佛世尊聞法信受，慇懃精進，欲速出三界，自求涅槃，是名聲聞乘，如彼諸子為求羊車出於火宅；若有眾生從佛世尊聞法信受，慇懃精進，求自然慧，樂獨善寂，深知諸法因緣，是名辟支佛乘，如彼諸子為求鹿車出於火宅；若有眾生從佛世尊聞法信受，勤修精進，求一切智、佛智、自然智、無師智，如來知見、力、無所畏，愍念、安樂無量眾生，利益天人，度脫一切，是名大乘，菩薩求此乘故，名為摩訶薩，如彼諸子為求牛車、出於火宅。 「舍利弗！如彼長者，見諸子等安隱得出火宅，到無畏處，自惟財富無量，等以大車而賜諸子。如來亦復如是，為一切眾生之父，若見無量億千眾生，以佛教門出三界苦，怖畏險道，得涅槃樂。 「如來爾時便作是念：『我有無量無邊智慧、力、無畏等諸佛法藏，是諸眾生皆是我子，等與大乘，不令有人獨得滅度，皆以如來滅度而滅度之。』是諸眾生脫三界者，悉與諸佛禪定、解脫等娛樂之具，皆是一相、一種，聖所稱歎，能生淨妙第一之樂。 「舍利弗！如彼長者，初以三車誘引諸子，然後但與大車，寶物莊嚴，安隱第一；然彼長者無虛妄之咎。如來亦復如是，無有虛妄，初說三乘引導眾生，然後但以大乘而度脫之。何以故？如來有無量智慧、力、無所畏諸法之藏，能與一切眾生大乘之法，但不盡能受。 「舍利弗！以是因緣，當知諸佛方便力故，於一佛乘分別說三。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Buddha said to Śāriputra: "Did I not already say that the buddhas, the world-honored ones, teach the Dharma through every kind of cause and condition, through parables and words and skillful means, all for the sake of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening? All of this teaching is for the sake of transforming bodhisattvas. Now, Śāriputra, I shall clarify this same meaning once more by way of a parable, for those with wisdom come to understanding through parables. "Śāriputra! Suppose that in some town or village there was a great elder, advanced in years, of immeasurable wealth, possessing many fields, houses, and servants. His house was vast, with only a single gate, and many people — a hundred, two hundred, even up to five hundred — dwelt within it. Its halls and towers had grown old and decayed, its walls had crumbled, the roots of its pillars had rotted, its beams and ridgepoles tilted and grown perilous, and all around it, all at once, a fire suddenly broke out, burning and consuming the house. The elder's sons — ten, twenty, or as many as thirty — were within that house. The elder, seeing this great fire arising on every side, was greatly alarmed, and thought: 'Though I myself am able to escape safely through this burning gate, my sons, within the burning house, delighting in their games, do not perceive it, do not know it, are not startled, are not afraid; the fire is closing in upon their bodies, and pain and suffering press upon them, yet their minds feel no distress, and they have no thought of escaping.' "Śāriputra! This elder thought further: 'My own body and hands have strength; I should carry them out of the house using the folds of my robe, or on a bench.' Then he thought again: 'This house has only a single gate, and it besides is narrow and small. My sons are young and small, without understanding, attached and clinging to their play, and may well stumble and fall, and be burned by the fire. I should tell them of the danger, that this house is already burning, and that they should come out quickly, so that they are not harmed and burned by the fire.' Having had this thought, just as he had considered, he told his sons in full: 'Come out quickly, all of you.' Though their father, out of pity, spoke kind and persuasive words, his sons, delighting in their games, were unwilling to believe and accept them; they were not startled, not afraid, and had no thought at all of coming out; nor did they even know what a fire was, what a house was, or what it meant to lose something — they only ran about here and there in play, glancing at their father from time to time. "At that time the elder had this thought: 'This house is already being consumed by a great fire; if my sons and I do not come out in time, we shall surely be burned. I must now devise some skillful means, to cause my sons to be spared this harm.' The father, knowing that his sons had each, from before, delighted in various rare and marvelous playthings, to which their hearts would surely be drawn, said to them: 'The playthings you love are rare and hard to obtain; if you do not take them now, you will surely regret it later. Look — goat-carts, deer-carts, and ox-carts of every kind are now outside the gate, for you to play with. Come out quickly from this burning house, and whatever you desire, I shall give to you all.' At that time the sons, hearing their father speak of these marvelous playthings, and finding them exactly to their liking, each grew bold and eager, pushing past one another, racing to run, competing to be first out of the burning house. The elder, seeing his sons come safely out, all sitting in the open ground at the crossroads, no longer hindered by anything, was at ease in his heart, and rejoiced with great joy. The sons then each said to their father: 'Father, please give us now the playthings you promised before — the goat-carts, the deer-carts, and the ox-carts.' "Śāriputra! At that time the elder gave to each of his sons a single great cart alike, high and broad, adorned with every kind of jewel, encircled by railings, hung with bells on every side, spread above with a canopy, likewise decorated with every kind of rare and marvelous jewel, strung with jeweled cords hung with garlands of flowers, spread thick with cushions, and fitted with a crimson pillow. It was drawn by a white ox, its skin sleek and clean, its form handsome, of great strength and sinew, walking level and even, swift as the wind, and attended besides by many servants for its escort. Why was this? Because that great elder's wealth was immeasurable, and every one of his many treasuries was filled to overflowing, and he thought: 'My wealth and possessions are without limit; I should not give my sons inferior, lesser carts. These are all my own children, young as they are, and my love for them is without partiality. I have great carts of the seven treasures such as these, in immeasurable number; I should give to each of them with an equal mind, and not differentiate among them. Why is this? Because even if I supplied the whole country with these treasures, they would still not be exhausted — how much less for my own sons!' At that time the sons each rode in a great cart, gaining what they had never had before, something beyond what they had originally hoped for. "Śāriputra, what do you think of this? When this elder gave his sons, equally, these jeweled great carts — was this in any way false?" Śāriputra said: "No, World-Honored One! This elder merely caused his sons to escape the danger of the fire and to preserve their lives whole — this was not false. Why is this so? If their lives were preserved whole, this was already the very plaything they had sought, all the more so since it was by skillful means that he rescued them from that burning house. World-Honored One, even if this elder had not given them so much as the smallest cart, it still would not have been false. Why is this so? Because the elder had already formed this intention beforehand: 'I shall use skillful means to bring my sons out.' By this cause and condition, it was not false — how much more so, when the elder, knowing his own wealth to be immeasurable and wishing to benefit his sons, gave them, equally, the great carts." The Buddha said to Śāriputra: "Excellent, excellent! It is just as you say. Śāriputra, the Tathāgata is likewise so — he is the father of the entire world. Every fear, every decay and affliction, every care and sorrow, every darkness of ignorance he has forever exhausted without remainder, and he has fully accomplished immeasurable knowledge and vision, power, and fearlessness; he possesses great spiritual power and the power of wisdom, is complete in the pāramitās of skillful means and wisdom, and, with great loving-kindness and great compassion, constantly without weariness, forever seeks what is good, and benefits all beings — and yet he is born into the decayed and ancient burning house of the three realms, in order to deliver beings from the fires of birth, old age, sickness, and death, of sorrow and grief, affliction, ignorance, and darkness, and the three poisons, teaching and transforming them so that they attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. He sees beings being burned and cooked by birth, old age, sickness, and death, by sorrow, grief, and affliction, and also undergoing every kind of suffering on account of the five desires and the pursuit of wealth and profit; and, because of their greedy attachment and craving, undergoing manifold suffering in the present, and, afterward, the sufferings of the hells, of animals, and of hungry ghosts; and whether born in the heavens or among humans, the suffering of poverty and hardship, the suffering of separation from what is loved, the suffering of union with what is hated — sufferings of every such kind. Beings, sunk within all of this, delight and play, not perceiving it, not knowing it, not startled, not afraid, giving rise to no weariness of it, and seeking no liberation from it. Within this burning house of the three realms, they run about here and there, and though they encounter great suffering, they do not regard it as any trouble at all. "Śāriputra! The Buddha, seeing this, then had this thought: 'I am the father of beings, and should draw them out of their suffering and hardship, and give them the immeasurable, boundless joy of the Buddha's own wisdom, for them to enjoy freely.' "Śāriputra! The Tathāgata thought further: 'If I were only to use my spiritual power and the power of wisdom, setting aside skillful means, to praise before beings the Tathāgata's knowledge and vision, power, and fearlessness, beings would not be able to be delivered by this means. Why is this so? Because these beings have not yet escaped birth, old age, sickness, and death, sorrow, grief, and affliction, and are being burned by the burning house of the three realms — how could they possibly understand the Buddha's wisdom?' "Śāriputra! Just as that elder, though his own body and hands had strength, did not make use of them, but by earnest skillful means alone worked to rescue his sons from the danger of the burning house, and only afterward gave to each of them a jeweled great cart — the Tathāgata is likewise so. Though he possesses power and fearlessness, he does not make use of them, but by wisdom and skillful means alone rescues beings from the burning house of the three realms, teaching them the three vehicles — the vehicle of the śrāvaka, of the pratyekabuddha, and of the buddha — and saying to them: 'You should not delight in dwelling within the burning house of the three realms; do not be greedy for coarse and worthless forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and touches. If, through greed and attachment, craving arises, you will be burned by it. Come out of the three realms quickly, and you shall attain the three vehicles — the vehicle of the śrāvaka, of the pratyekabuddha, and of the buddha. I now guarantee this to you myself; it will never be false. You need only diligently practice and strive.' By this skillful means the Tathāgata draws beings onward, and says further: 'You should know that this teaching of the three vehicles is praised by every sage, is free and unbound, and depends on and seeks nothing else. Riding these three vehicles, enjoying yourselves freely with undefiled faculties, powers, factors of awakening, the path, meditative concentration, liberation, and samādhis, you shall attain immeasurable peace and happiness.' "Śāriputra! If there are beings who, having within them the nature of wisdom, hear the Dharma from the Buddha, the World-Honored One, and believe and accept it, and, with earnest diligence, wish quickly to leave the three realms and seek nirvāṇa for themselves — this is called the śrāvaka vehicle, like those sons who sought the goat-cart to come out of the burning house. If there are beings who hear the Dharma from the Buddha, the World-Honored One, and believe and accept it, and, with earnest diligence, seek a wisdom that arises of itself, delighting in solitude and quiescence, deeply understanding the causes and conditions of all things — this is called the pratyekabuddha vehicle, like those sons who sought the deer-cart to come out of the burning house. If there are beings who hear the Dharma from the Buddha, the World-Honored One, and believe and accept it, and, diligently practicing, seek the wisdom of all modes, the Buddha's wisdom, the wisdom that arises of itself, the wisdom without a teacher, the Tathāgata's knowledge and vision, power, and fearlessness, out of pity bringing peace and happiness to immeasurable beings, benefiting gods and humans, and delivering all beings across — this is called the Great Vehicle; because bodhisattvas seek this vehicle, they are called mahāsattvas, like those sons who sought the ox-cart to come out of the burning house. "Śāriputra! Just as that elder, seeing his sons come safely out of the burning house and reach a place free of danger, and, considering his own wealth to be immeasurable, gave to each of his sons, equally, a great cart — the Tathāgata is likewise so. As the father of all beings, when he sees immeasurable koṭis of thousands of beings come out, by the gateway of the Buddha's teaching, from the suffering of the three realms and the perilous, fearful path, and attain the joy of nirvāṇa, the Tathāgata then has this thought: 'I possess an immeasurable, boundless treasury of the Dharma of the buddhas — wisdom, power, fearlessness, and the rest. All these beings are my own children; I shall give them, equally, the Great Vehicle, and not allow anyone to attain extinction alone, but bring them all to the very extinction of the Tathāgata himself.' To all these beings who have escaped the three realms, he gives, in full, the enjoyments of the buddhas' own meditative concentration and liberation — all of one single mark, one single kind, praised by every sage, able to give rise to the purest, most wondrous, foremost joy. "Śāriputra! Just as that elder first drew his sons onward with the promise of three carts, and only afterward gave them the single great cart, adorned with treasures, foremost in peace and ease — and yet that elder was not guilty of any falsehood — the Tathāgata is likewise so, and is without any falsehood: he first teaches the three vehicles to guide beings, and only afterward delivers them across by the Great Vehicle alone. Why is this so? Because the Tathāgata possesses an immeasurable treasury of wisdom, power, fearlessness, and all things, and is able to give the Dharma of the Great Vehicle to every being — only they are not all able to receive it in full. "Śāriputra! By this cause and condition, you should know that, because of the power of the buddhas' skillful means, they distinguish and teach three vehicles within the one buddha vehicle."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Commentators before and after Zhiyi divided this parable into a handful of matched similes, mapping each narrative beat back onto a specific claim from the previous two chapters — the house and its single gate for the three realms and the one path out of them, the old age and decay of the building for the age's five turbidities, the father's own strength held back for the Buddha declining to rely on power and fearlessness alone, the three carts for the three vehicles offered as bait. The schemes differ in how many similes they count — five, six, or as many as ten in Zhiyi's own more granular breakdown — but nearly every version singles out the same closing move as the point the whole parable exists to establish: what several call the "not-false simile" (不虛妄譬), the exchange in which the Buddha asks Śāriputra directly whether the father lied, and Śāriputra says no.

It is worth noticing exactly how Śāriputra's own reasoning works, since the text gives it to us twice, in two different strengths. His first argument is the weaker, more cautious one: getting the children out alive was already the real gift, so nothing further was strictly owed — the promise of carts was, in a sense, already redeemed by their survival alone. But he does not stop there. His second argument removes even that cushion: he says that even if the father had given nothing at all — "not so much as the smallest cart" — it still would not have been false, because the father's intention in speaking was never a false statement about the future; it was itself the skillful means by which the children were saved. A promise made in order to save a life, Śāriputra argues, is not held to the same standard as an ordinary factual claim, and its truth is measured by what it accomplished, not by whether its literal terms were later fulfilled to the letter. What the father actually gives — one identical, far greater cart to every child, exceeding what any of them separately asked for — is then not the minimum defense of the promise's honesty but something offered from a wealth "without limit," on top of an obligation Śāriputra has already argued was fully discharged. This is the sūtra's own answer to the question this whole chapter has been building toward: the three vehicles taught for so long were not a broken promise now quietly being replaced by something else. They were always, in Zhiyi's reading of Śāriputra's reasoning, already true in the only sense a promise made to save a life needs to be.
