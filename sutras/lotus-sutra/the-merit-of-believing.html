The Merit of Believing

分別功德品第十七

The disclosure of the Tathāgata's true lifespan does not simply end the previous chapter's argument — it visibly changes the assembly hearing it, immeasurable beings advancing at once through stage after stage of the bodhisattva path as the sky itself fills with falling flowers and celestial music. Maitreya answers in verse, and the Buddha then makes an even bolder claim: that a single thought of faith in what was just taught outweighs eighty myriad koṭis of nayutas of kalpas spent practicing five of the six perfections to their fullest.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
The immediate effect of Part 17's disclosure — ten distinct grades of the assembly advancing through the bodhisattva path, from those newly resolved on awakening to those now only a single lifetime from buddhahood — followed by a sky filled with falling flowers, celestial music, and banners rising in ranks to the Brahma heaven; Maitreya's verse restating both the assembly's attainment and the offering itself; and the Buddha's declaration that a single thought of faith and understanding in his own immeasurable lifespan outweighs eighty myriad koṭis of nayutas of kalpas spent perfecting giving, moral discipline, forbearance, diligence, and meditative concentration — every perfection but wisdom itself.
Why it matters
Zhiyi reads this chapter as the origin gate's own version of a prediction chapter — not naming individuals as Parts 4 through 10 did, but describing, by stage, what an entire assembly gains simply from hearing and accepting a teaching about who the Buddha actually is, rather than from any further practice at all. The claim that follows — that belief itself outweighs decades of near-perfect cultivation across five of the six pāramitās — is stated as plainly as anything in the sūtra, and is worth sitting with rather than softening.

1. The Assembly's Attainments, and the Celestial Offering

Chinese Source

爾時大會，聞佛說壽命劫數長遠如是，無量無邊阿僧祇眾生得大饒益。於時世尊告彌勒菩薩摩訶薩：「阿逸多！我說是如來壽命長遠時，六百八十萬億那由他恒河沙眾生，得無生法忍；復有千倍菩薩摩訶薩，得聞持陀羅尼門；復有一世界微塵數菩薩摩訶薩，得樂說無礙辯才；復有一世界微塵數菩薩摩訶薩，得百千萬億無量旋陀羅尼；復有三千大千世界微塵數菩薩摩訶薩，能轉不退法輪；復有二千中國土微塵數菩薩摩訶薩，能轉清淨法輪；復有小千國土微塵數菩薩摩訶薩，八生當得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提；復有四四天下微塵數菩薩摩訶薩，四生當得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提；復有三四天下微塵數菩薩摩訶薩，三生當得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提；復有二四天下微塵數菩薩摩訶薩，二生當得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提；復有一四天下微塵數菩薩摩訶薩，一生當得阿耨多羅三藐三菩提；復有八世界微塵數眾生，皆發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。」 佛說是諸菩薩摩訶薩得大法利時，於虛空中，雨曼陀羅華、摩訶曼陀羅華，以散無量百千萬億眾寶樹下、師子座上諸佛，并散七寶塔中師子座上釋迦牟尼佛及久滅度多寶如來，亦散一切諸大菩薩及四部眾。又雨細末栴檀、沈水香等。於虛空中，天鼓自鳴，妙聲深遠。又雨千種天衣，垂諸瓔珞、真珠瓔珞、摩尼珠瓔珞、如意珠瓔珞，遍於九方。眾寶香爐燒無價香，自然周至，供養大會。一一佛上，有諸菩薩，執持幡蓋，次第而上，至于梵天。是諸菩薩，以妙音聲，歌無量頌，讚歎諸佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the great assembly, hearing the Buddha teach that his lifespan extended across so vast a number of kalpas, immeasurable and boundless asaṃkhyeyas of beings gained great benefit. At that time the World-Honored One said to Maitreya Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva: "Ajita! As I taught this — the Tathāgata's own vast and immeasurable lifespan — six hundred and eighty myriad koṭis of nayutas of Ganges-sands of beings attained the acceptance that things are unborn; a thousand times that number of bodhisattva-mahāsattvas attained the dhāraṇī-gate of hearing and retaining the Dharma; bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to the fine dust of a single world attained unobstructed eloquence in teaching; bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to the fine dust of a single world attained hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of the immeasurable revolving dhāraṇīs; bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to the fine dust of a three-thousand-great-thousandfold world became able to turn the wheel of the Dharma that never turns back; bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to the fine dust of two thousand middling lands became able to turn the pure wheel of the Dharma; bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to the fine dust of a small thousandfold land shall, after eight more lifetimes, attain unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening; bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to the fine dust of four sets of four continents shall, after four more lifetimes, attain it; bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to the fine dust of three sets of four continents shall, after three more lifetimes, attain it; bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to the fine dust of two sets of four continents shall, after two more lifetimes, attain it; bodhisattva-mahāsattvas equal to the fine dust of a single set of four continents shall, after one more lifetime, attain it; and beings equal to the fine dust of eight worlds all brought forth the resolve for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening." As the Buddha taught that these bodhisattva-mahāsattvas had gained this great benefit of the Dharma, in the sky above there rained down mandārava and mahā-mandārava flowers, scattered over the immeasurable hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of buddhas seated on lion thrones beneath jeweled trees, and scattered too over Śākyamuni Buddha, seated on his lion throne within the seven-jeweled stūpa, and over Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata, extinguished so long ago, and scattered as well over every great bodhisattva and the fourfold assembly. There rained down, too, fine powdered sandalwood and aloeswood incense. In the sky above, heavenly drums sounded of themselves, their wondrous voice deep and far-reaching. There rained down, further, a thousand kinds of heavenly garments, hung with every kind of necklace — necklaces of pearl, of maṇi jewels, of wish-fulfilling jewels — filling all nine directions. Censers of every jewel burned incense beyond price, filling the whole assembly of themselves, making offerings to the great gathering. Above each and every buddha stood bodhisattvas holding banners and canopies, rising rank upon rank until they reached the Brahma heaven. These bodhisattvas, with wondrous voices, sang countless verses, praising every buddha.

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi reads this list of ten grades as this chapter's own equivalent of the individual predictions given by name in Parts 4 through 10 — except that here, no one is named, and what is being described is not a future event but something that has already happened, in this very moment, simply from hearing the Buddha's true lifespan disclosed and accepting it. He maps the ten grades onto the full fifty-two stages of the bodhisattva path, from the ten faiths through the ten abodes, ten practices, ten transferences, the ten grounds, and up to the diamond-like mind bordering final awakening — and notes that he reads the "advancing" and "diminishing" language differently from two commentators he names directly, Guangzhai and Vasubandhu's Lotus Sūtra treatise, both of whom he judges too quick to fix each grade to a single settled position in a scheme not native to this sūtra's own way of speaking. What is being measured, on his own reading, is progress in wisdom and reduction in the compulsion toward further rebirth — not a location on anyone else's map.

2. Maitreya's Verse of Praise

Chinese Source

爾時彌勒菩薩從座而起，偏袒右肩，合掌向佛，而說偈言： 「佛說希有法，　昔所未曾聞， 世尊有大力，　壽命不可量。 無數諸佛子，　聞世尊分別， 說得法利者，　歡喜充遍身。 或住不退地，　或得陀羅尼， 或無礙樂說，　萬億旋總持。 或有大千界，　微塵數菩薩， 各各皆能轉，　不退之法輪。 復有中千界，　微塵數菩薩， 各各皆能轉，　清淨之法輪。 復有小千界，　微塵數菩薩， 餘各八生在，　當得成佛道。 復有四三二，　如此四天下， 微塵諸菩薩，　隨數生成佛。 或一四天下，　微塵數菩薩， 餘有一生在，　當成一切智。 如是等眾生，　聞佛壽長遠， 得無量無漏，　清淨之果報。 復有八世界，　微塵數眾生， 聞佛說壽命，　皆發無上心。 世尊說無量，　不可思議法， 多有所饒益，　如虛空無邊。 雨天曼陀羅、　摩訶曼陀羅， 釋梵如恒沙，　無數佛土來。 雨栴檀沈水，　繽紛而亂墜， 如鳥飛空下，　供散於諸佛。 天鼓虛空中，　自然出妙聲， 天衣千萬種，　旋轉而來下。 眾寶妙香爐，　燒無價之香， 自然悉周遍、　供養諸世尊。 其大菩薩眾，　執七寶幡蓋， 高妙萬億種，　次第至梵天。 一一諸佛前，　寶幢懸勝幡， 亦以千萬偈、　歌詠諸如來。 如是種種事，　昔所未曾有， 聞佛壽無量，　一切皆歡喜。 佛名聞十方，　廣饒益眾生， 一切具善根，　以助無上心。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Maitreya Bodhisattva rose from his seat, bared his right shoulder, joined his palms toward the Buddha, and spoke this verse: "The Buddha has taught a rare Dharma, never heard until now — the World-Honored One possesses great power, his lifespan beyond all measure. Countless children of the Buddha, hearing the World-Honored One's own distinctions, of those who have gained this benefit of the Dharma — their joy fills their whole bodies. Some abide in the ground that never turns back, some have attained dhāraṇīs, some have unobstructed eloquence, some the myriad koṭis of the revolving total-retention. There are bodhisattvas equal to the fine dust of a great-thousandfold world, each and every one able to turn the wheel of the Dharma that never turns back. There are bodhisattvas, too, equal to the fine dust of a middling-thousandfold world, each and every one able to turn the pure wheel of the Dharma. There are bodhisattvas, too, equal to the fine dust of a small-thousandfold world, each with only eight lifetimes remaining before attaining the buddha-path. There are bodhisattvas, too, equal to the fine dust of four, or three, or two sets of these four continents, attaining buddhahood after that same number of lifetimes. There are bodhisattvas, too, equal to the fine dust of a single set of four continents, with only a single lifetime remaining before attaining the wisdom of all modes. Beings such as these, hearing that the Buddha's lifespan is vast beyond measure, attain an immeasurable, outflow-free, and pure result. There are beings, too, equal to the fine dust of eight worlds, who, hearing the Buddha teach of his own lifespan, all brought forth the unsurpassed resolve. The World-Honored One teaches an immeasurable Dharma, beyond all conceiving, its benefit reaching far and wide, boundless as empty space itself. Raining down heavenly mandārava and great mandārava flowers, Śakra and Brahmā, numerous as the sands of the Ganges, come from countless buddha-lands. Raining down sandalwood and aloeswood incense, falling in profusion, like birds descending from the sky, scattered as offerings over every buddha. Heavenly drums in the sky sound of themselves, a wondrous voice; heavenly garments of a thousand myriad kinds swirl down from above. Censers of every jewel, wondrous and fragrant, burn incense beyond all price, filling every place of themselves, making offerings to every World-Honored One. The great assembly of bodhisattvas holds banners of the seven jewels, lofty and wondrous in ten thousand koṭis of forms, rising rank upon rank to the Brahma heaven. Before each and every buddha, jeweled banner-poles hang with victorious pennants, and with a thousand myriad verses they sing in praise of every tathāgata. Every kind of thing such as this, never known until now — hearing that the Buddha's lifespan is immeasurable, every being rejoices. The Buddha's name is heard throughout the ten directions, its benefit reaching beings far and wide, every one of them complete with good roots, to aid their unsurpassed resolve."

Reading note

Maitreya's verse does not add new content so much as it performs, at the level of the whole assembly's emotion, what the previous section described at the level of doctrine — the same ten grades of attainment, restated, and the same falling flowers and rising banners, now sung rather than narrated. Zhiyi glosses the banners' ascent to the Brahma heaven as a deliberate image: the banner (幡) stands for turning, the canopy (蓋) for covering, and their rise in unbroken ranks toward the "pure" Brahma realm pictures wisdom's advance and compassion's protection moving together, stage by stage, without either ever outrunning the other.

3. The Merit of a Single Thought of Faith

Chinese Source

爾時佛告彌勒菩薩摩訶薩：「阿逸多！其有眾生，聞佛壽命長遠如是，乃至能生一念信解，所得功德，無有限量。若有善男子、善女人，為阿耨多羅三藐三菩提故，於八十萬億那由他劫，行五波羅蜜——檀波羅蜜、尸羅波羅蜜、羼提波羅蜜、毘梨耶波羅蜜、禪波羅蜜——除般若波羅蜜，以是功德比前功德，百分、千分、百千萬億分、不及其一，乃至算數譬喻所不能知。若善男子、善女人，有如是功德，於阿耨多羅三藐三菩提退者，無有是處。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Buddha said to Maitreya Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva: "Ajita! Should there be any being who, hearing that the Buddha's lifespan is vast in this way, gives rise to even a single thought of faith and understanding, the merit that being gains is beyond all limit. Suppose there were a good son or good daughter who, for the sake of unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, practiced, for eighty myriad koṭis of nayutas of kalpas, five of the six perfections — the perfection of giving, of moral discipline, of forbearance, of diligence, and of meditative concentration — every perfection but the perfection of wisdom. Compared to the merit described before, this merit would not equal one part in a hundred, a thousand, a hundred thousand myriad koṭis — not even a number or a parable could express the difference. If a good son or good daughter possesses merit such as the first, that they should ever turn back from unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening is simply not possible."

Chinese Source

爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「若人求佛慧，　於八十萬億， 那由他劫數，　行五波羅蜜。 於是諸劫中，　布施供養佛， 及緣覺弟子，　并諸菩薩眾。 珍異之飲食、　上服與臥具， 栴檀立精舍，　以園林莊嚴。 如是等布施，　種種皆微妙， 盡此諸劫數，　以迴向佛道。 若復持禁戒，　清淨無缺漏， 求於無上道，　諸佛之所歎。 若復行忍辱，　住於調柔地， 設眾惡來加，　其心不傾動。 諸有得法者，　懷於增上慢， 為此所輕惱，　如是亦能忍。 若復懃精進，　志念常堅固， 於無量億劫，　一心不懈息。 又於無數劫，　住於空閑處， 若坐若經行，　除睡常攝心。 以是因緣故，　能生諸禪定， 八十億萬劫、　安住心不亂。 持此一心福，　願求無上道， 我得一切智，　盡諸禪定際。 是人於百千，　萬億劫數中， 行此諸功德，　如上之所說。 有善男女等，　聞我說壽命， 乃至一念信，　其福過於彼。 若人悉無有，　一切諸疑悔， 深心須臾信，　其福為如此。 其有諸菩薩，　無量劫行道， 聞我說壽命，　是則能信受。 如是諸人等，　頂受此經典， 願我於未來，　長壽度眾生。 如今日世尊、　諸釋中之王， 道場師子吼，　說法無所畏。 我等未來世，　一切所尊敬， 坐於道場時，　說壽亦如是。 若有深心者，　清淨而質直， 多聞能總持，　隨義解佛語， 如是諸人等，　於此無有疑。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, wishing to proclaim this meaning once more, spoke in verse: "If a person, seeking the Buddha's own wisdom, were to practice, across eighty myriad koṭis of nayutas of kalpas, five of the perfections — throughout every one of these kalpas, giving offerings to buddhas, and to pratyekabuddha disciples, and to the assembly of bodhisattvas besides, rare and excellent food and drink, fine robes and bedding, dwellings of sandalwood, adorned with gardens and groves, giving such gifts as these, of every subtle and wondrous kind, throughout the whole span of these kalpas, dedicating it all toward the buddha-path; and if, further, they kept the precepts, pure and without the slightest flaw, seeking the unsurpassed path, praised by every buddha; and if, further, they practiced forbearance, dwelling in the ground of gentleness and pliancy, so that even should every kind of evil be brought against them, their heart would never waver — even should those who have attained the Dharma, filled with arrogance, treat them with contempt and affliction, they would bear even this with patience; and if, further, they were diligent and energetic, their resolve constantly firm, through immeasurable koṭis of kalpas, with a single mind never growing slack; and, further, for innumerable kalpas, dwelt in quiet and solitary places, whether sitting or walking, casting off sleep, constantly gathering in their mind; and by these causes and conditions were able to give rise to every meditative concentration, for eighty koṭis of myriads of kalpas dwelling at peace, their mind never disturbed; and, holding the merit of this single-minded practice, sought the unsurpassed path, saying: 'May I attain the wisdom of all modes, and reach the very limit of every meditative concentration' — such a person, throughout hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of kalpas, practicing every one of these merits just as described above — if there is, besides, a good son or good daughter who, hearing me teach of my own lifespan, gives rise to even a single thought of faith, their merit surpasses all of this. If a person is entirely free of every doubt and regret, and believes with a deep heart even for the briefest moment, their merit is exactly this. Those bodhisattvas who, having practiced the path for immeasurable kalpas, hear me teach of my own lifespan, and are then able to believe and accept it — people such as these receive this sūtra upon the crown of their heads, and vow: 'May I, too, in the age to come, live long and bring beings across, just as the World-Honored One does today, king among all the Śākyas, roaring his lion's roar at the place of awakening, teaching the Dharma without any fear. May we too, in the age to come, honored by all, when we sit at the place of awakening, teach of our own lifespan in exactly this way.' If there is one of deep heart, pure and upright, learned and able to hold all in memory, who understands the Buddha's own words according to their true meaning — such a person as this holds no doubt at all in this matter."

Reading note

The comparison here is stated with unusual bluntness, and is worth reading exactly as given rather than rounded down to a general statement about faith being important. Eighty myriad koṭis of nayutas of kalpas is not a modest span — earlier in this very chapter it was one of the terms used to measure the Buddha's own inconceivable lifespan — and the five perfections being practiced across it are not token gestures but decades of near-total cultivation, missing only the perfection of wisdom itself. Against this, a single thought of genuine faith and understanding in what this sūtra has just taught is said to outweigh it by a margin no number can express. The comparison is not a claim that ethical practice is worthless; the verse's own account of what that practice involves — patient endurance of contempt from "those who have attained the Dharma," years of diligence, unbroken meditative absorption — treats it as difficult and admirable. The claim is narrower and sharper than that: that correctly understanding who the Buddha actually is changes what all of that practice is oriented toward, in a way that practice alone, however sustained, cannot supply on its own.

4. Why Upholding This Sūtra Surpasses Building Stūpas

Chinese Source

「又，阿逸多！若有聞佛壽命長遠，解其言趣，是人所得功德無有限量，能起如來無上之慧。何況廣聞是經、若教人聞，若自持、若教人持，若自書、若教人書，若以華、香、瓔珞、幢幡、繒蓋、香油、酥燈，供養經卷，是人功德無量無邊，能生一切種智。 「阿逸多！若善男子、善女人，聞我說壽命長遠，深心信解，則為見佛常在耆闍崛山，共大菩薩、諸聲聞眾圍繞說法。又見此娑婆世界，其地琉璃，坦然平正，閻浮檀金以界八道，寶樹行列，諸臺樓觀皆悉寶成，其菩薩眾咸處其中。若有能如是觀者，當知是為深信解相。 「又復如來滅後，若聞是經而不毀呰，起隨喜心，當知已為深信解相，何況讀誦、受持之者，斯人則為頂戴如來。 「阿逸多！是善男子、善女人，不須為我復起塔寺，及作僧坊、以四事供養眾僧。所以者何？是善男子、善女人，受持讀誦是經典者，為已起塔、造立僧坊、供養眾僧。則為以佛舍利起七寶塔，高廣漸小至于梵天，懸諸幡蓋及眾寶鈴，華、香、瓔珞，末香、塗香、燒香，眾鼓、伎樂，簫、笛、箜篌，種種舞戲，以妙音聲歌唄讚頌，則為於無量千萬億劫作是供養已。 「阿逸多！若我滅後，聞是經典，有能受持，若自書、若教人書，則為起立僧坊，以赤栴檀作諸殿堂三十有二，高八多羅樹，高廣嚴好，百千比丘於其中止，園林、浴池、經行、禪窟，衣服、飲食、床褥、湯藥，一切樂具充滿其中。如是僧坊、堂閣，若干百千萬億，其數無量，以此現前供養於我及比丘僧。是故我說：『如來滅後，若有受持、讀誦、為他人說，若自書、若教人書，供養經卷，不須復起塔寺，及造僧坊、供養眾僧。』況復有人能持是經，兼行布施、持戒、忍辱、精進、一心、智慧，其德最勝，無量無邊。譬如虛空，東西南北、四維上下無量無邊；是人功德，亦復如是無量無邊，疾至一切種智。若人讀誦受持是經，為他人說，若自書、若教人書，復能起塔，及造僧坊、供養讚歎聲聞眾僧，亦以百千萬億讚歎之法讚歎菩薩功德，又為他人，種種因緣隨義解說此法華經，復能清淨持戒，與柔和者而共同止，忍辱無瞋，志念堅固，常貴坐禪得諸深定，精進勇猛攝諸善法，利根智慧善答問難。 「阿逸多！若我滅後，諸善男子、善女人，受持讀誦是經典者，復有如是諸善功德，當知是人已趣道場，近阿耨多羅三藐三菩提，坐道樹下。 「阿逸多！是善男子、善女人，若坐、若立、若行處，此中便應起塔，一切天人皆應供養如佛之塔。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Furthermore, Ajita! Should anyone hear that the Buddha's lifespan is vast in this way, and understand what it truly means, the merit that person gains is without limit, able to give rise to the Tathāgata's own unsurpassed wisdom. How much more so, then, one who broadly hears this sūtra, or causes others to hear it; who upholds it themselves, or causes others to uphold it; who copies it themselves, or causes others to copy it; or who, with flowers, incense, necklaces, banners, canopies, fragrant oil, or lamps of ghee, makes offerings to the sūtra scroll — that person's merit is immeasurable and boundless, able to give rise to the wisdom of all modes. "Ajita! Should a good son or good daughter, hearing me teach of my own vast lifespan, believe and understand it with a deep heart, it is as if they see the Buddha dwelling always on Vulture Peak, surrounded by great bodhisattvas and the assembly of śrāvakas, teaching the Dharma. They see, too, this very sahā world, its ground made of lapis lazuli, level and smooth, its eight paths bordered with Jambūnada gold, rows of jeweled trees, every terrace and tower made entirely of jewels, and the assembly of bodhisattvas dwelling everywhere within it. Whoever is able to see it in this way should be known to have the mark of deep faith and understanding. "And further, after the Tathāgata's parinirvāṇa, should anyone hear this sūtra and not revile or belittle it, but give rise to a heart of rejoicing, they should be known to already have the mark of deep faith and understanding — how much more so one who reads, recites, upholds, and keeps it. Such a person bears the Tathāgata upon the very crown of their head. "Ajita! Such a good son or good daughter need not, for my sake, build stūpas or temples, or construct monasteries, or make offerings of the four necessities to the assembly of monks. Why is this so? Because a good son or good daughter who upholds, reads, and recites this sūtra has, by that very fact, already built a stūpa, already constructed a monastery, already made offerings to the assembly of monks — it is as if, with the Buddha's own relics, they had raised a stūpa of the seven jewels, tall and broad, narrowing by degrees until it reached the Brahma heaven, hung with every banner and canopy and every kind of jeweled bell, with flowers, incense, and necklaces, with powdered incense, ointment incense, and burning incense, with every kind of drum and music, flute and pipe and harp, every kind of dance and performance, singing hymns of praise with wondrous voices — it is as if they had already made offerings such as these, for immeasurable thousands of myriads of koṭis of kalpas. "Ajita! Should anyone, after my own parinirvāṇa, hear this sūtra, and be able to uphold it, copying it themselves, or causing others to copy it, it is as if they had built a monastery, its thirty-two halls made of red sandalwood, eight tāla trees in height, tall, broad, and finely adorned, where a hundred thousand monks might dwell, its gardens and groves, bathing pools, walking paths, and meditation caves, its robes, food and drink, bedding, and medicine, every kind of comfort filling it entirely. Monasteries and halls such as this, some hundred, some thousand, some myriad koṭis in number, beyond all counting — it is as if, by this very act, they had already made this offering, present before me and the assembly of monks. It is for this reason that I say: 'After the Tathāgata's parinirvāṇa, should there be one who upholds, reads, recites, and teaches this sūtra for others, or copies it, or causes others to copy it, and makes offerings to the sūtra scroll, that person need not build stūpas or temples, or construct monasteries, or make offerings to the assembly of monks any further.' How much more so, then, one who is able to uphold this sūtra, and also practices giving, moral discipline, forbearance, diligence, single-mindedness, and wisdom — such a person's virtue is foremost of all, immeasurable and boundless. It is like empty space, boundless to the east, west, south, and north, in every intermediate direction, above and below; such a person's merit is, in exactly this way, immeasurable and boundless, swiftly reaching the wisdom of all modes. Should a person read, recite, and uphold this sūtra, teach it for others, copy it themselves, or cause others to copy it, and, further, be able to build stūpas, and construct monasteries, and make offerings in praise of the assembly of śrāvaka monks, and praise the bodhisattvas' own merit with hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of forms of praise, and, further, for the sake of others, by every kind of cause and condition, explain this Lotus Sūtra according to its true meaning, and, further, be able to keep the precepts purely, dwelling together with the gentle and compliant, forbearing and free of anger, their resolve firm, constantly valuing meditation and attaining every profound concentration, diligent and courageous, gathering in every wholesome teaching, of sharp faculties and wisdom, skillfully answering every hard question — "Ajita! Should good sons and good daughters, after my own parinirvāṇa, uphold and read and recite this sūtra, and possess, besides, every one of these wholesome merits, know that such a person has already set out for the place of awakening, drawing near to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, seated beneath the very tree of awakening. "Ajita! Wherever such a good son or good daughter sits, or stands, or walks, in that very place a stūpa should be raised, and every god and human should make offerings to it, exactly as to a stūpa of the Buddha himself."

Chinese Source

爾時世尊欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「若我滅度後，　能奉持此經， 斯人福無量，　如上之所說， 是則為具足，　一切諸供養。 以舍利起塔，　七寶而莊嚴， 表剎甚高廣，　漸小至梵天， 寶鈴千萬億，　風動出妙音。 又於無量劫，　而供養此塔， 華香諸瓔珞，　天衣眾伎樂， 燃香油酥燈，　周匝常照明。 惡世法末時，　能持是經者， 則為已如上，　具足諸供養。 若能持此經，　則如佛現在， 以牛頭栴檀，　起僧坊供養。 堂有三十二，　高八多羅樹， 上饌妙衣服，　床臥皆具足， 百千眾住處，　園林諸浴池， 經行及禪窟，　種種皆嚴好。 若有信解心，　受持讀誦書， 若復教人書，　及供養經卷， 散華香末香，　以須曼瞻蔔、 阿提目多伽，　薰油常燃之。 如是供養者，　得無量功德， 如虛空無邊，　其福亦如是。 況復持此經，　兼布施持戒， 忍辱樂禪定，　不瞋不惡口， 恭敬於塔廟，　謙下諸比丘， 遠離自高心，　常思惟智慧， 有問難不瞋，　隨順為解說， 若能行是行，　功德不可量。 若見此法師，　成就如是德， 應以天華散，　天衣覆其身， 頭面接足禮，　生心如佛想。 又應作是念：　『不久詣道樹， 得無漏無為，　廣利諸人天。』 其所住止處，　經行若坐臥， 乃至說一偈，　是中應起塔， 莊嚴令妙好，　種種以供養。 佛子住此地，　則是佛受用， 常在於其中，　經行及坐臥。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"If, after my own extinction, one is able to uphold this sūtra, that person's own good fortune is beyond measure, exactly as described above — it is as if they had already provided every kind of offering in full. Raising a stūpa with the relics, adorned with the seven jewels, its spire towering, broad, narrowing by degrees until it reaches the Brahma heaven, its thousand myriad koṭis of jeweled bells sounding a wondrous voice in the wind; and, for immeasurable kalpas besides, making offerings to this stūpa, with flowers, incense, and every necklace, heavenly garments and every kind of music, burning fragrant oil and lamps of ghee, their light shining all around without cease. In this evil age, at the Dharma's own final time, whoever is able to uphold this sūtra has, by that very fact, already provided every offering described above. If one is able to uphold this sūtra, it is as if the Buddha himself were present, and one had raised a monastery of ox-head sandalwood, and made offerings to it. Its thirty-two halls, eight tāla trees in height, its finest food and wondrous robes, its beds and bedding all provided in full, dwellings for a hundred thousand monks, gardens and groves and every bathing pool, walking paths and meditation caves, every kind of thing finely adorned. If there is one who, with a heart of faith and understanding, upholds, reads, recites, and copies this sūtra, or causes others to copy it, and makes offerings to the sūtra scroll, scattering flowers, incense, and powdered incense, with campaka and atimuktaka blossoms, burning fragrant oil constantly before it — one who makes offerings such as these gains immeasurable merit, boundless as empty space itself — their own good fortune is exactly the same. How much more so, then, one who upholds this sūtra, and also practices giving and moral discipline, forbearance and the delight of meditation, free of anger and harsh speech, reverent toward stūpas and shrines, humble before every monk, far removed from any thought of pride, constantly reflecting on wisdom, never angered by a hard question, but explaining it in accordance with what is asked — one who is able to practice conduct such as this, their merit cannot be measured. Should you see a teacher of the Dharma who has accomplished virtue such as this, you should scatter heavenly flowers over them, cover their body with heavenly garments, bow your head to their very feet, and hold in your heart the thought that they are a buddha. And you should think, further: 'Before long, they shall go to the very tree of awakening, attain what is free of outflow and unconditioned, and broadly benefit every god and human.' Wherever such a person dwells, whether walking, or sitting, or lying down — even where they speak but a single verse — a stūpa should be raised there, adorned and made beautiful, with every kind of offering. For the place where a child of the Buddha dwells is the very place the Buddha himself enjoys, constantly present there, walking, sitting, and lying down within it."

Reading note

The claim closing this chapter runs in the same direction as the one that opened its second half, and is stated just as directly: that upholding this sūtra does not merely earn merit comparable to building stūpas and monasteries, but already constitutes having done so, without the stūpa or the monastery ever needing to be physically built at all. Read alongside Part 11's teaching that wherever this sūtra is kept should be honored as though it were itself a stūpa, this chapter completes that claim's logical extension: not only the sūtra's physical location, but the person who upholds it, becomes a site worthy of the same reverence — the final verse's instruction to bow to such a teacher "with the thought that they are a buddha" is not a metaphor softened for effect, but the chapter's own precise conclusion.
