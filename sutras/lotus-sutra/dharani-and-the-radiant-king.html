Guardian Spells, and a King Won Back

陀羅尼品第二十六・妙莊嚴王本事品第二十七

Bhaiṣajyarāja and four other guardians — a bodhisattva, a heavenly general, and two of the Four Heavenly Kings, joined by ten rākṣasī sisters and the mother-goddess Hārītī — each offer a dhāraṇī spell to protect anyone who teaches this sūtra from harm. Then, reaching back to a far more ancient buddha, the Buddha tells of King Śubhavyūha, devoted to a rival teaching, and the two sons and one wife who use miraculous persuasion, and finally simple example, to bring him back to the Dharma — three figures revealed, at the story's close, to be bodhisattvas this sūtra's reader has already met.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
Five separate offers of a protective dhāraṇī — from Bhaiṣajyarāja, from Pradānaśūra, from Vaiśravaṇa, from Dhṛtarāṣṭra, and from ten rākṣasī sisters together with the mother-goddess Hārītī — each spoken to guard any teacher of this sūtra from harm; and the story of King Śubhavyūha, devoted to a rival brahminical teaching, whose two sons perform miraculous feats in the sky to win his attention, and whose wife guides the family's approach to him, until the whole household visits the buddha Jaladharagarjitaghoṣasusvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña together, the king himself takes ordination, and the Buddha reveals that this wife, these two sons, and this king are bodhisattvas already met earlier in this sūtra.
Why it matters
Zhiyi reads these two chapters as a matched pair on two different kinds of protection: the dhāraṇī chapter protects a teacher of this sūtra from external harm by hostile spirits, while the story of Śubhavyūha shows a subtler and, on Zhiyi's reading, harder-won protection — a family's patient, affectionate persuasion winning back someone attached to a teaching this sūtra does not affirm, without ever resorting to force or contempt.

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva rose from his seat, bared his right shoulder, joined his palms toward the Buddha, and said: "World-Honored One! Should a good son or good daughter be able to uphold this Lotus Sūtra, reading and reciting it fluently, or copying the sūtra scroll, how much good fortune do they gain?" The Buddha said to Bhaiṣajyarāja: "Suppose a good son or good daughter were to make offerings to eight hundred myriad koṭis of nayutas of Ganges-sands of buddhas. What do you think — is the good fortune they gain great?" "Very great indeed, World-Honored One." The Buddha said: "Should a good son or good daughter be able, with regard to this sūtra, to uphold even a single four-line verse of it, reading, reciting, and understanding its meaning, and practicing exactly as it teaches, their merit is greater still." At that time Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! I shall now give a dhāraṇī spell to teachers of this Dharma, to protect them." He then spoke this spell — a string of protective syllables, transmitted here, as throughout this sūtra's textual history, in Kumārajīva's own Chinese phonetic transliteration rather than translated for meaning. Reconstructing its underlying Sanskrit or Prakrit with real confidence is a specialized task of historical phonology beyond the scope of this translation; this site presents the dhāraṇī as the received Chinese text preserves it, in the original characters, rather than offer an uncertain reconstruction as though it were settled. "World-Honored One! This dhāraṇī spell has been taught by sixty-two koṭis of Ganges-sands of buddhas — should anyone do violence to this teacher of the Dharma, they shall have done violence to these very buddhas." At that time Śākyamuni Buddha praised Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva, saying: "Excellent, excellent, Bhaiṣajyarāja! That out of pity you should protect this teacher of the Dharma by teaching this dhāraṇī, greatly benefiting beings in this way." At that time Pradānaśūra Bodhisattva said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! I too shall speak a dhāraṇī to protect those who read, recite, and uphold the Lotus Sūtra. Should this teacher of the Dharma possess this dhāraṇī, no yakṣa, rākṣasa, pūtana, kṛtya, kumbhāṇḍa, or hungry ghost, watching for some fault to exploit, shall ever find their opportunity." He then spoke this spell before the Buddha, likewise transliterated rather than translated. "World-Honored One! This dhāraṇī spell has been taught by Ganges-sands of buddhas, who have all rejoiced in it as well — should anyone do violence to this teacher of the Dharma, they shall have done violence to these very buddhas." At that time Vaiśravaṇa, heavenly king and guardian of the world, said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! Out of pity for beings, I too shall speak this dhāraṇī, to protect this teacher of the Dharma." He then spoke this spell. "World-Honored One! By this spell I protect the teacher of the Dharma — and I myself shall protect whoever upholds this sūtra, so that within a hundred yojanas, no harm shall come to them." At that time Dhṛtarāṣṭra, heavenly king, present in this assembly, together with thousands of myriads of koṭis of nayutas of gandharvas reverently surrounding him, came forward before the Buddha, joined his palms, and said: "World-Honored One! I too, with a dhāraṇī spell, shall protect those who uphold the Lotus Sūtra." He then spoke this spell. "World-Honored One! This dhāraṇī spell has been taught by forty-two koṭis of buddhas — should anyone do violence to this teacher of the Dharma, they shall have done violence to these very buddhas." At that time there were rākṣasī sisters — the first named Lambā, the second Vilambā, the third Crooked Teeth, the fourth Flowery Teeth, the fifth Black Teeth, the sixth Much Hair, the seventh Insatiable, the eighth Necklace-Bearer, the ninth Kuntī, and the tenth Stealer of the Vital Essence of All Beings — these ten rākṣasī sisters, together with Hārītī, the mother of demon children, and her own children and retinue, came together before the Buddha, and said with one voice: "World-Honored One! We too wish to protect those who read, recite, and uphold the Lotus Sūtra, removing every harm from them. Should anyone watch for a fault in this teacher of the Dharma to exploit, we shall not let them find their opportunity." They then spoke this spell before the Buddha. "Let them climb upon our own heads rather than trouble this teacher of the Dharma. Whether yakṣa, rākṣasa, hungry ghost, pūtana, kṛtya, vetāla, skanda, umāraka, apasmāra, human-possessing yakṣa, or human-possessing kṛtya, whether a fever lasting one day, two days, three days, four days, up to seven days, or a fever that never ceases, whether it takes the form of a man, a woman, a boy, or a girl — even in dreams, let none of these ever trouble him." They then spoke this verse before the Buddha: "Should anyone fail to heed our spell, and trouble or disturb a teacher of the Dharma, their own head shall split into seven pieces, like the branches of the arjaka tree. Their offense shall be as the offense of killing one's own father or mother, or the offense of the oil-press that crushes life to increase its yield, or the offense of cheating others with false scales and measures, or the offense of Devadatta, who split the monastic community apart. Whoever offends against this teacher of the Dharma shall incur a calamity exactly such as this." Having spoken this verse, these rākṣasī sisters said to the Buddha: "World-Honored One! We too shall, with our own bodies, protect anyone who upholds, reads, recites, and practices this sūtra, causing them to dwell in peace, free of every harm, and dispelling every poison." The Buddha said to these rākṣasī sisters: "Excellent, excellent! That you should be able to protect even those who merely uphold the Lotus Sūtra's own name is already merit beyond measure — how much more so, protecting those who uphold it in full, making offerings to the sūtra scroll with flowers, incense, necklaces, powdered incense, ointment incense, burning incense, banners, canopies, and music, and lighting every kind of lamp — lamps of ghee, of oil, of every fragrant oil, of sumanā-flower oil, of campaka-flower oil, of vārṣika-flower oil, of blue-lotus oil — and every other kind of offering such as these, hundreds and thousands of kinds. Kuntī! You and your whole retinue should protect a teacher of the Dharma such as this." When this Dhāraṇī chapter was taught, sixty-eight thousand people attained the acceptance that things are unborn.

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi gives four different classical glosses for why a dhāraṇī is left as sound rather than translated for sense: that it names spirits whose own retinues, hearing their king's name spoken, dare not act against the one who speaks it; that it functions like a password in an army camp, marking whoever recites it correctly as safe from harm and whoever gets it wrong as exposed; that it works the way certain calming words worked on an angry prince in a story he recounts, silently defusing anger through their own sound rather than their sense; or, simplest of all, that a dhāraṇī is a kind of concentrated speech of the buddhas themselves, its power residing directly in the sound and not requiring the sound to be unpacked into ordinary meaning at all. This is also, on Zhiyi's account, the very reason Kumārajīva's translation team chose not to translate these syllables into Chinese in the first place — the same reasoning that governs this site's own decision, noted above, not to attempt a confident Sanskrit reconstruction where none is secure.

2. Two Sons Win Back Their Father

Chinese Source

爾時佛告諸大眾：「乃往古世，過無量無邊不可思議阿僧祇劫，有佛名雲雷音宿王華智多陀阿伽度、阿羅訶、三藐三佛陀，國名光明莊嚴，劫名憙見。彼佛法中有王，名妙莊嚴，其王夫人名曰淨德，有二子，一名淨藏，二名淨眼。是二子有大神力，福德智慧，久修菩薩所行之道，所謂檀波羅蜜、尸羅波羅蜜、羼提波羅蜜、毘梨耶波羅蜜、禪波羅蜜、般若波羅蜜、方便波羅蜜，慈悲喜捨，乃至三十七品助道法皆悉明了通達。又得菩薩淨三昧、日星宿三昧、淨光三昧、淨色三昧、淨照明三昧、長莊嚴三昧、大威德藏三昧，於此三昧亦悉通達。 「爾時彼佛欲引導妙莊嚴王，及愍念眾生故，說是法華經。時淨藏、淨眼二子到其母所，合十指爪掌白言：『願母往詣雲雷音宿王華智佛所，我等亦當侍從、親近、供養、禮拜。所以者何？此佛於一切天人眾中說法華經，宜應聽受。』母告子言：『汝父信受外道，深著婆羅門法，汝等應往白父，與共俱去。』淨藏、淨眼合十指爪掌白母：『我等是法王子，而生此邪見家。』母告子言：『汝等當憂念汝父，為現神變，若得見者，心必清淨，或聽我等，往至佛所。』於是二子念其父故，踊在虛空，高七多羅樹，現種種神變——於虛空中行住坐臥；身上出水、身下出火，身下出水、身上出火；或現大身滿虛空中，而復現小，小復現大；於空中滅，忽然在地；入地如水，履水如地。現如是等種種神變，令其父王心淨信解。時父見子神力如是，心大歡喜，得未曾有，合掌向子言：『汝等，師為是誰，誰之弟子？』二子白言：『大王！彼雲雷音宿王華智佛，今在七寶菩提樹下法座上坐，於一切世間天人眾中廣說法華經，是我等師，我是弟子。』父語子言：『我今亦欲見汝等師，可共俱往。』於是二子從空中下，到其母所，合掌白母：『父王今已信解，堪任發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。我等為父已作佛事，願母見聽，於彼佛所出家修道。』 「爾時二子欲重宣其意，以偈白母： 「『願母放我等，　出家作沙門， 諸佛甚難值，　我等隨佛學。 如優曇鉢羅，　值佛復難是， 脫諸難亦難，　願聽我出家。』 「母即告言：『聽汝出家。所以者何？佛難值故。』 「於是二子白父母言：『善哉，父母！願時往詣雲雷音宿王華智佛所，親近供養。所以者何？佛難得值，如優曇鉢羅華，又如一眼之龜，值浮木孔。而我等宿福深厚，生值佛法，是故父母當聽我等，令得出家。所以者何？諸佛難值，時亦難遇。』 「彼時妙莊嚴王後宮八萬四千人，皆悉堪任受持是法華經。淨眼菩薩，於法華三昧，久已通達；淨藏菩薩，已於無量百千萬億劫通達離諸惡趣三昧，欲令一切眾生離諸惡趣故。其王夫人，得諸佛集三昧，能知諸佛祕密之藏。 「二子如是以方便力善化其父，令心信解，好樂佛法。於是妙莊嚴王與群臣眷屬俱，淨德夫人與後宮婇女眷屬俱，其王二子與四萬二千人俱，一時共詣佛所。到已，頭面禮足，繞佛三匝，却住一面。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Buddha said to the great assembly: "Long ago, past immeasurable, boundless, inconceivable asaṃkhyeya kalpas, there was a buddha named Jaladharagarjitaghoṣasusvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, whose land was named Adorned with Light, and whose kalpa was named Delight in Seeing. Within this buddha's own teaching there was a king named Śubhavyūha, whose queen was named Vimaladattā, and who had two sons, the first named Vimalagarbha, and the second Vimalanetra. These two sons possessed great spiritual power, merit, virtue, and wisdom, and had long practiced the bodhisattva's own path — the perfections of giving, moral discipline, forbearance, diligence, meditative concentration, wisdom, and skillful means, loving-kindness, compassion, joy, and equanimity, and had, further, thoroughly mastered the thirty-seven factors that aid the path. They had, besides, attained the bodhisattva's pure samādhi, the samādhi of sun and constellations, the samādhi of pure light, the samādhi of pure form, the samādhi of pure illumination, the samādhi of lasting adornment, and the samādhi of the treasury of great majestic virtue, and had thoroughly mastered these samādhis as well. "At that time, this buddha, wishing to guide King Śubhavyūha, and out of pity for beings, taught this Lotus Sūtra. At that time the two sons, Vimalagarbha and Vimalanetra, went to their mother, joined the tips of their ten fingers, and said: 'We beg our mother to go to where Buddha Jaladharagarjitaghoṣasusvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña is — we too shall attend upon him, draw near to him, make offerings to him, and bow before him. Why is this so? Because this buddha teaches the Lotus Sūtra before the whole assembly of gods and humans, and it is fitting that we should hear and receive it.' Their mother said to her sons: 'Your father believes in and accepts a teaching of other paths, deeply attached to the brahmins' own doctrine — you should go and tell your father, and go there together with him.' Vimalagarbha and Vimalanetra joined the tips of their ten fingers and said to their mother: 'We are the sons of the Dharma-king, yet we were born into this house of wrong views.' Their mother said to her sons: 'You should show concern for your father, and manifest spiritual transformations for his sake — should he see them, his heart will surely become pure, and he may permit us to go to where the buddha is.' At this, the two sons, for their father's sake, leapt into the sky, rising seven tāla trees high, and manifested every kind of spiritual transformation — walking, standing, sitting, and lying down in the air; water issuing from the upper part of their bodies and fire from the lower, water from the lower and fire from the upper; manifesting a great body filling the whole sky, and then manifesting a small one, the small body becoming great once more; vanishing in the sky, and suddenly appearing on the ground; sinking into the earth as though it were water, and walking upon water as though it were solid ground. Manifesting every kind of spiritual transformation such as this, they caused their father the king's heart to become pure, and to believe and understand. At that time, their father, seeing his sons' spiritual power to be such as this, was filled with great joy, having gained what he had never known before, and, joining his palms toward his sons, said: 'Who is your own teacher? Whose disciples are you?' The two sons said: 'Great King! That Buddha Jaladharagarjitaghoṣasusvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña now sits upon the Dharma seat beneath a bodhi tree of the seven jewels, broadly teaching the Lotus Sūtra before the whole assembly of gods and humans in the world — he is our own teacher, and we are his disciples.' The father said to his sons: 'I too now wish to see your own teacher — let us go there together.' At this, the two sons descended from the sky, went to their mother, joined their palms, and said to her: 'Our father the king now believes and understands, and is capable of bringing forth the resolve for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. We have already accomplished this buddha-work for our father's sake — we beg our mother to permit us to go forth from home and cultivate the path at that buddha's own place.' "At that time the two sons, wishing to proclaim their own intent once more, said to their mother in verse: 'We beg our mother to release us, to go forth from home and become renunciants; buddhas are exceedingly difficult to encounter, and we wish to follow and study under a buddha. Like the udumbara flower, encountering a buddha is difficult in just this way, and escaping every difficulty is difficult too — we beg you to permit us to go forth.' "Their mother then said: 'I permit you to go forth. Why is this so? Because a buddha is difficult to encounter.' "At this, the two sons said to their father and mother: 'Excellent, father and mother! We beg you to go at once to where Buddha Jaladharagarjitaghoṣasusvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña is, and draw near to him and make offerings to him. Why is this so? A buddha is as difficult to encounter as the udumbara flower, or as a one-eyed turtle finding the single hole in a floating log — yet our own good fortune from a former life is deep indeed, that we should be born to encounter the Buddha's own Dharma. For this reason, father and mother, you should permit us to go forth. Why is this so? Because buddhas are difficult to encounter, and the right time for it is difficult to meet as well.' "At that time, eighty-four thousand people within King Śubhavyūha's own harem were all capable of upholding this Lotus Sūtra. Vimalanetra Bodhisattva had already, long before, thoroughly mastered the Lotus samādhi; Vimalagarbha Bodhisattva had already, across immeasurable hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of kalpas, mastered the samādhi of freedom from every evil destiny, wishing to cause every being to be free of the evil destinies. The king's own queen had attained the samādhi of the buddhas' own gathering, able to know the secret treasury of every buddha. "The two sons, by skillful means such as this, well transformed their father, causing his heart to believe and understand, and to delight in the Buddha's own Dharma. At this, King Śubhavyūha, together with his ministers and retinue, Queen Vimaladattā, together with the women of the harem and their retinue, and the king's two sons, together with forty-two thousand people, all at once went together to where the buddha was. Having arrived, they bowed their heads to his feet, circled the buddha three times, and withdrew to one side."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi traces this story to an account found outside this sūtra's own text: four monks in a Dharma-ending age, three devoted entirely to practice while a fourth left their hermitage to beg for their support, this fourth monk one day glimpsing a king's own procession — its horses, banners, and glittering escort — and feeling, in that unguarded moment, a flicker of longing for such glory, a seed that ripened, birth after birth, into repeated rebirth as a king trapped by exactly the splendor he had once envied from outside it. On Zhiyi's account, this chapter answers the question of how the other three monks, having attained the path themselves, went back for the one who had stayed in the world to support them: not by argument or force, but by being reborn as his own wife and two sons, using "the hook of love" — the household bonds a king would trust before he would trust any outside teacher — to draw him, gently and patiently, toward the very teaching his own attachment to power had kept him from for lifetimes.

3. A King's Ordination, and Four Bodhisattvas Revealed

Chinese Source

「爾時彼佛為王說法，示教利喜。王大歡悅。爾時妙莊嚴王及其夫人，解頸真珠瓔珞，價直百千，以散佛上，於虛空中化成四柱寶臺，臺中有大寶床，敷百千萬天衣，其上有佛結加趺坐，放大光明。爾時妙莊嚴王作是念：『佛身希有，端嚴殊特，成就第一微妙之色。』時雲雷音宿王華智佛告四眾言：『汝等見是妙莊嚴王，於我前合掌立不？此王於我法中作比丘，精勤修習，助佛道法，當得作佛，號娑羅樹王，國名大光，劫名大高王。其娑羅樹王佛，有無量菩薩眾及無量聲聞，其國平正，功德如是。』其王即時以國付弟，與夫人、二子并諸眷屬，於佛法中出家修道。 「王出家已，於八萬四千歲，常勤精進修行妙法華經。過是已後，得一切淨功德莊嚴三昧，即昇虛空，高七多羅樹，而白佛言：『世尊！此我二子，已作佛事，以神通變化轉我邪心，令得安住於佛法中，得見世尊。此二子者，是我善知識，為欲發起宿世善根，饒益我故，來生我家。』 「爾時，雲雷音宿王華智佛告妙莊嚴王言：『如是，如是！如汝所言。若善男子、善女人，種善根故，世世得善知識，其善知識，能作佛事，示教利喜，令入阿耨多羅三藐三菩提。大王！當知善知識者是大因緣，所謂化導令得見佛，發阿耨多羅三藐三菩提心。大王！汝見此二子不？此二子，已曾供養六十五百千萬億那由他恒河沙諸佛，親近恭敬，於諸佛所受持法華經，愍念邪見眾生，令住正見。』妙莊嚴王即從虛空中下，而白佛言：『世尊！如來甚希有，以功德智慧故，頂上肉髻光明顯照，其眼長廣而紺青色，眉間毫相白如珂月，齒白齊密常有光明，脣色赤好如頻婆菓。』 「爾時妙莊嚴王，讚歎佛如是等無量百千萬億功德已，於如來前，一心合掌，復白佛言：『世尊！未曾有也。如來之法，具足成就不可思議微妙功德，教誡所行，安隱快善。我從今日，不復自隨心行，不生邪見、憍慢、瞋恚諸惡之心。』說是語已，禮佛而出。」 佛告大眾：「於意云何？妙莊嚴王，豈異人乎？今華德菩薩是。其淨德夫人，今佛前光照莊嚴相菩薩是。哀愍妙莊嚴王及諸眷屬故，於彼中生。其二子者，今藥王菩薩、藥上菩薩是。是藥王、藥上菩薩，成就如此諸大功德，已於無量百千萬億諸佛所殖眾德本，成就不可思議諸善功德。若有人識是二菩薩名字者，一切世間諸天人民亦應禮拜。」 佛說是妙莊嚴王本事品時，八萬四千人遠塵離垢，於諸法中得法眼淨。

Ru-Yi Translation

"At that time this buddha taught the king the Dharma, revealing, teaching, benefiting, and delighting him, and the king was filled with great joy. At that time King Śubhavyūha and his queen removed the pearl necklace from their own necks, worth a hundred thousand pieces, and scattered it over the buddha, and in the sky it transformed into a jeweled platform on four pillars, and within this platform was a great jeweled couch, spread with hundreds of thousands of myriads of heavenly garments, and upon it sat a buddha, cross-legged, sending forth great light. At that time King Śubhavyūha thought to himself: 'This buddha's own body is rare indeed, upright and dignified beyond compare, accomplished with the finest and most subtle form.' At that time Buddha Jaladharagarjitaghoṣasusvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña said to the fourfold assembly: 'Do you see this King Śubhavyūha, standing with joined palms before me? This king shall, within my own teaching, become a monk, diligently cultivating and practicing what aids the buddha-path, and shall attain buddhahood, named Śālendrarāja, his land named Great Light, his kalpa named Great Lofty King. This Buddha Śālendrarāja shall have an immeasurable assembly of bodhisattvas and an immeasurable assembly of śrāvakas, his land level and upright, its merit exactly as I have described.' The king at once handed over his kingdom to his younger brother, and, together with his queen, his two sons, and his whole retinue, went forth from home within the buddha's own teaching, to cultivate the path. "Having gone forth, the king, for eighty-four thousand years, constantly and diligently cultivated and practiced the Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower Sūtra. After this time had passed, he attained the samādhi of every pure merit's adornment, and at once rose into the sky, seven tāla trees high, and said to the buddha: 'World-Honored One! These two sons of mine have already accomplished this buddha-work, using spiritual transformation to turn my own wrong-thinking heart, causing me to come to dwell at peace within the Buddha's own teaching, and to be able to see the World-Honored One. These two sons are my own good spiritual friends, who, wishing to awaken the good roots of my former lives and greatly benefit me, came to be born into my own house.' "At that time Buddha Jaladharagarjitaghoṣasusvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña said to King Śubhavyūha: 'Just so, just so! Exactly as you have said. Should a good son or good daughter, on account of the good roots they have planted, gain a good spiritual friend in life after life, that good spiritual friend is able to accomplish buddha-work, revealing, teaching, benefiting, and delighting them, causing them to enter unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. Great King! You should know that a good spiritual friend is a great cause and condition, namely, one who guides and transforms another, causing them to come to see a buddha, and to bring forth the resolve for unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening. Great King! Do you see these two sons? These two sons have already made offerings to sixty-five hundred thousand myriads of koṭis of nayutas of Ganges-sands of buddhas, drawing near to them and revering them, upholding the Lotus Sūtra in the presence of these buddhas, out of pity for beings of wrong view, causing them to abide in right view.' King Śubhavyūha then descended from the sky, and said to the buddha: 'World-Honored One! The Tathāgata is rare indeed — by the power of his own merit and wisdom, the fleshly crown atop his head shines with light, his eyes long and broad, the color of dark blue lotus petals, the curl of hair between his brows white as the moon's own pearl, his teeth white, even, and closely set, constantly shining with light, his lips red and fine as the bimba fruit.' "At that time King Śubhavyūha, having praised the buddha for merits such as these, immeasurable, hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of them, joined his palms before the Tathāgata with a single mind, and said further: 'World-Honored One! This is unprecedented indeed. The Tathāgata's own Dharma is complete and accomplished with inconceivable, subtle merit; its teachings and precepts, and the conduct they prescribe, bring peace and true excellence. From this day forward, I shall no longer follow my own heart's own inclinations, and shall give rise to no wrong view, no arrogance, no anger, nor any other evil thought.' Having said this, he bowed to the buddha and departed." The Buddha said to the great assembly: "What do you think — was King Śubhavyūha at that time some other person? He is none other than Padmaśrī Bodhisattva of today. His queen, Vimaladattā, is none other than the bodhisattva now before the Buddha, Illuminated-and-Adorned-in-Appearance, who, out of pity for King Śubhavyūha and his whole household, was born among them. His two sons are none other than Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva and Bhaiṣajyasamudgata Bodhisattva of today. These bodhisattvas Bhaiṣajyarāja and Bhaiṣajyasamudgata, having accomplished merit as great as this, have already, in the presence of immeasurable hundreds of thousands of myriads of koṭis of buddhas, planted every root of merit, and accomplished inconceivable, wholesome merit of every kind. Should anyone come to know the names of these two bodhisattvas, every god and human in the whole world should bow before them as well." When the Buddha taught this chapter on King Śubhavyūha's former life, eighty-four thousand people became free of dust and defilement, and attained the pure Dharma eye with regard to every teaching.

Reading note

The chain of identities revealed at this chapter's close is worth tracing back through the sūtra rather than read only as a final flourish: Bhaiṣajyarāja and Bhaiṣajyasamudgata, the king's two sons here, are the same Bhaiṣajyarāja whose own former life as Sarvasattvapriyadarśana filled Part 24, and Padmaśrī, the king himself, is the bodhisattva who asked Śākyamuni about that very story. The sūtra's practice of identifying its own present speakers with figures from stories just told — Never-Disparaging as Śākyamuni himself in Part 22, Sarvasattvapriyadarśana as Bhaiṣajyarāja in Part 24, and now this entire household as bodhisattvas already introduced — is not incidental decoration; it is one of this text's most consistent structural habits, collapsing the distance between the ancient story and the assembly listening to it in the same gesture, again and again.
