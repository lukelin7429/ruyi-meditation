The Assembly and the Six Signs

序品第一

Twelve thousand arhats, eighty thousand bodhisattvas, and the whole retinue of gods, dragons, and spirit-kings gather at Vulture Peak. The Buddha teaches, enters a wordless samādhi, and six signs appear — flowers, trembling earth, and a single ray of light that shows the entire cosmos in miniature. Then Mañjuśrī tells the assembly he has seen this exact scene once before, unimaginably long ago, and knows exactly what it means.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T262 · Kumārajīva's translation, early 5th century CE Commentary guide: Zhiyi's 妙法蓮華經文句 (6th c.)
This part covers
The full assembly at Vulture Peak; the Buddha's teaching of the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings and his entry into samādhi; the six auspicious signs, including a ray of light that reveals the entire cosmos; Maitreya's doubt and his question to Mañjuśrī; and Mañjuśrī's answer — the story of an ancient buddha, Candrasūryapradīpa, whose own assembly witnessed this same scene before he taught this same sūtra.
Why it matters
Nothing has been taught yet. This entire chapter is staging: it establishes, through an exact structural echo of the deep past, that what is about to happen next is not routine — and it quietly identifies Mañjuśrī with the bodhisattva of that ancient story, and Maitreya with the story's least promising character.

1. The Assembly at Vulture Peak

Chinese Source

如是我聞：一時，佛住王舍城耆闍崛山中，與大比丘眾萬二千人俱，皆是阿羅漢，諸漏已盡，無復煩惱，逮得己利，盡諸有結，心得自在。其名曰：阿若憍陳如、摩訶迦葉、優樓頻螺迦葉、伽耶迦葉、那提迦葉、舍利弗、大目揵連、摩訶迦旃延、阿㝹樓馱、劫賓那、憍梵波提、離婆多、畢陵伽婆蹉、薄拘羅、摩訶拘絺羅、難陀、孫陀羅難陀、富樓那彌多羅尼子、須菩提、阿難、羅睺羅，如是眾所知識大阿羅漢等。復有學、無學二千人。摩訶波闍波提比丘尼，與眷屬六千人俱。羅睺羅母耶輸陀羅比丘尼，亦與眷屬俱。菩薩摩訶薩八萬人，皆於阿耨多羅三藐三菩提不退轉；皆得陀羅尼；樂說辯才，轉不退轉法輪；供養無量百千諸佛，於諸佛所殖眾德本，常為諸佛之所稱歎；以慈修身，善入佛慧；通達大智，到於彼岸；名稱普聞無量世界，能度無數百千眾生。其名曰：文殊師利菩薩、觀世音菩薩、得大勢菩薩、常精進菩薩、不休息菩薩、寶掌菩薩、藥王菩薩、勇施菩薩、寶月菩薩、月光菩薩、滿月菩薩、大力菩薩、無量力菩薩、越三界菩薩、跋陀婆羅菩薩、彌勒菩薩、寶積菩薩、導師菩薩，如是等菩薩摩訶薩八萬人俱。 爾時釋提桓因，與其眷屬二萬天子俱。復有名月天子、普香天子、寶光天子、四大天王，與其眷屬萬天子俱。自在天子、大自在天子，與其眷屬三萬天子俱。娑婆世界主梵天王——尸棄大梵、光明大梵等，與其眷屬萬二千天子俱。有八龍王——難陀龍王、跋難陀龍王、娑伽羅龍王、和脩吉龍王、德叉迦龍王、阿那婆達多龍王、摩那斯龍王、優鉢羅龍王等，各與若干百千眷屬俱。有四緊那羅王——法緊那羅王、妙法緊那羅王、大法緊那羅王、持法緊那羅王，各與若干百千眷屬俱。有四乾闥婆王——樂乾闥婆王、樂音乾闥婆王、美乾闥婆王、美音乾闥婆王，各與若干百千眷屬俱。有四阿修羅王——婆稚阿修羅王、佉羅騫馱阿修羅王、毘摩質多羅阿修羅王、羅睺阿修羅王，各與若干百千眷屬俱。有四迦樓羅王——大威德迦樓羅王、大身迦樓羅王、大滿迦樓羅王、如意迦樓羅王，各與若干百千眷屬俱。韋提希子阿闍世王，與若干百千眷屬俱。各禮佛足，退坐一面。

Ru-Yi Translation

Thus have I heard: at one time, the Buddha was dwelling on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa near the great city of Rājagṛha, together with a great assembly of twelve thousand monks, all of them arhats whose defilements were exhausted, who were free of further affliction, who had attained their own good, who had ended every fetter of existence, and whose minds had attained mastery. Their names were: Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya, Mahākāśyapa, Uruvilvā-Kāśyapa, Gayā-Kāśyapa, Nadī-Kāśyapa, Śāriputra, Mahāmaudgalyāyana, Mahākātyāyana, Aniruddha, Kapphiṇa, Gavāṃpati, Revata, Piṇḍola-Bharadvāja, Vakkula, Mahākauṣṭhila, Nanda, Sundarananda, Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇīputra, Subhūti, Ānanda, and Rāhula — great arhats such as these, known to the whole assembly. There were also two thousand more, some still in training and some beyond it. Mahāprajāpatī the nun was present with a retinue of six thousand; Yaśodharā the nun, Rāhula's mother, was present with her own retinue as well. Eighty thousand bodhisattva-mahāsattvas were present, every one of them irreversible from unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening; every one of them had attained dhāraṇī; they delighted in eloquent speech and turned the wheel of the Dharma that never turns back; they had made offerings to immeasurable hundreds of thousands of buddhas, planted every root of merit in the presence of those buddhas, and were constantly praised by them; they cultivated their own bodies with loving-kindness and had entered well into the Buddha's wisdom; they had penetrated great wisdom and reached its far shore; their renown had spread through immeasurable worlds, and they were able to bring countless hundreds of thousands of beings across. Their names were: Mañjuśrī Bodhisattva, Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, Mahāsthāmaprāpta Bodhisattva, Constant-Diligence Bodhisattva, Never-Resting Bodhisattva, Jeweled-Palm Bodhisattva, Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva, Brave-Giving Bodhisattva, Jeweled-Moon Bodhisattva, Moonlight Bodhisattva, Full-Moon Bodhisattva, Great-Strength Bodhisattva, Immeasurable-Strength Bodhisattva, Beyond-the-Three-Realms Bodhisattva, Bhadrapāla Bodhisattva, Maitreya Bodhisattva, Ratnākara Bodhisattva, and Guide Bodhisattva — bodhisattva-mahāsattvas such as these, eighty thousand in all. At that time Śakra, lord of the devas, was present with a retinue of twenty thousand sons of the gods. There were also the god Candra, the god Sugandha, the god Ratnaprabha, and the Four Great Heavenly Kings, present with a retinue of ten thousand sons of the gods; the god Īśvara and the god Maheśvara, present with a retinue of thirty thousand sons of the gods; the Brahmā king who is lord of this sahā world — the great Brahmā Śikhin and the great Brahmā Jyotiṣprabha and others — present with a retinue of twelve thousand sons of the gods. There were eight dragon kings — Nanda, Upananda, Sāgara, Vāsuki, Takṣaka, Anavatapta, Manasvin, and Utpalaka — each present with a retinue of some hundreds of thousands. There were four kiṃnara kings, four gandharva kings, four asura kings, and four garuḍa kings, each present with a retinue of some hundreds of thousands. King Ajātaśatru, son of Vaidehī, was present with a retinue of some hundreds of thousands. Each of them bowed at the Buddha's feet and withdrew to sit to one side.

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi devotes an enormous share of his entire ten-fascicle commentary to this one opening chapter — more space, proportionally, than to any other part of the sūtra. His reason is structural: everything the sūtra will later reveal about the relationship between "trace" (迹, the Buddha's provisional teaching career) and "origin" (本, his actually immeasurable lifespan) is already implicitly at stake in how this assembly is described, so getting the frame right here does real work for every chapter that follows. This site's landing page lays out that 迹門–本門 framework in full; here it is enough to know that Zhiyi reads this list of arhats, bodhisattvas, and celestial beings as establishing the full scope of who this teaching is ultimately for — not a private instruction, but something meant, eventually, for every being in every realm.

2. The Buddha Teaches, and Six Signs Appear

Chinese Source

爾時世尊，四眾圍遶，供養、恭敬、尊重、讚歎。為諸菩薩說大乘經，名無量義，教菩薩法，佛所護念。佛說此經已，結加趺坐，入於無量義處三昧，身心不動。是時天雨曼陀羅華、摩訶曼陀羅華、曼殊沙華、摩訶曼殊沙華而散佛上及諸大眾。普佛世界，六種震動。爾時會中，比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷、天龍、夜叉、乾闥婆、阿修羅、迦樓羅、緊那羅、摩睺羅伽、人非人，及諸小王、轉輪聖王，是諸大眾，得未曾有，歡喜合掌，一心觀佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the World-Honored One, surrounded by the fourfold assembly, was made offerings to, honored, revered, and praised. For the bodhisattvas he taught a Mahāyāna sūtra called the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings, a teaching for bodhisattvas, guarded and held in mind by the Buddha himself. When the Buddha had finished teaching this sūtra, he sat cross-legged and entered the samādhi of the Station of Immeasurable Meanings, his body and mind unmoving. At that moment a rain of māndārava flowers, great māndārava flowers, mañjūṣaka flowers, and great mañjūṣaka flowers fell from the sky, scattering over the Buddha and over the whole great assembly, and the entire buddha-world shook in six ways. At that time, within the assembly, the monks and nuns, laymen and laywomen, gods and dragons, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans and non-humans, and all the lesser kings and wheel-turning kings — this whole great assembly, having gained what they had never had before, joined their palms in joy and gazed on the Buddha with one-pointed mind.

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi names and organizes this scene as "the six signs" (六瑞): the teaching itself, the entry into samādhi, the rain of flowers, the trembling of the earth, the assembly's wordless joy, and — in the next section — the ray of light. He reads each one as a coded announcement of a specific "wonder" (妙) that the sūtra will later unfold in full: the teaching-sign points to the wonder of the teaching and of wisdom itself; the samādhi-sign to the wonder of practice; the flower-sign to the wonder of spiritual rank, since he reads the four kinds of flowers as corresponding to four stages of the bodhisattva path; the earth-sign to the wonder of the objective world and of the vehicle that traverses it; and the assembly's joy to the wonder of the retinue and of the benefit it receives. None of this is stated outright in the sūtra's own words — it is Zhiyi's own interpretive key, laid over the text from the outside, and worth holding as exactly that: one systematic way of reading a scene that the text itself simply narrates.

He also addresses directly why any of this counts as unusual at all — the Buddha teaches and meditates constantly; why should this occasion be different? His answer is about context, not content: the assembly does not disperse after the teaching ends, and remains seated in evident anticipation of something more, which is itself the departure from ordinary practice. The stillness that follows is not the Buddha's habitual samādhi but is, in Zhiyi's phrase, entered "with a further open teaching already in mind" — a pause built for what comes next, not a routine withdrawal.

3. The Light, and Maitreya's Doubt

Chinese Source

爾時佛放眉間白毫相光，照東方萬八千世界，靡不周遍，下至阿鼻地獄，上至阿迦尼吒天。於此世界，盡見彼土六趣眾生，又見彼土現在諸佛，及聞諸佛所說經法。并見彼諸比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷，諸修行得道者。復見諸菩薩摩訶薩，種種因緣、種種信解、種種相貌，行菩薩道。復見諸佛般涅槃者。復見諸佛般涅槃後，以佛舍利起七寶塔。 爾時彌勒菩薩作是念：「今者世尊現神變相，以何因緣而有此瑞？今佛世尊入于三昧，是不可思議、現希有事。當以問誰？誰能答者？」復作此念：「是文殊師利，法王之子，已曾親近供養過去無量諸佛，必應見此希有之相。我今當問。」 爾時比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷，及諸天、龍、鬼神等，咸作此念：「是佛光明神通之相，今當問誰？」 爾時彌勒菩薩欲自決疑，又觀四眾比丘、比丘尼、優婆塞、優婆夷，及諸天，龍、鬼神等眾會之心，而問文殊師利言：「以何因緣而有此瑞神通之相，放大光明，照于東方萬八千土，悉見彼佛國界莊嚴？」於是彌勒菩薩欲重宣此義，以偈問曰： 「文殊師利！　導師何故，　眉間白毫， 大光普照。　雨曼陀羅、　曼殊沙華， 栴檀香風，　悅可眾心？　以是因緣， 地皆嚴淨，　而此世界，　六種震動。 時四部眾，　咸皆歡喜，　身意快然， 得未曾有。　眉間光明，　照于東方， 萬八千土，　皆如金色，　從阿鼻獄， 上至有頂。　諸世界中，　六道眾生， 生死所趣，　善惡業緣，　受報好醜， 於此悉見。　又覩諸佛、　聖主師子、 演說經典，　微妙第一。　其聲清淨， 出柔軟音，　教諸菩薩，　無數億萬， 梵音深妙，　令人樂聞。　各於世界， 講說正法，　種種因緣。　以無量喻， 照明佛法，　開悟眾生。　若人遭苦， 厭老病死，　為說涅槃，　盡諸苦際。 若人有福，　曾供養佛，　志求勝法， 為說緣覺。　若有佛子、　修種種行， 求無上慧，　為說淨道。　文殊師利！ 我住於此，　見聞若斯，　及千億事， 如是眾多，　今當略說。　我見彼土， 恒沙菩薩，　種種因緣，　而求佛道。 或有行施，　金銀珊瑚、　真珠摩尼、 車璩馬腦、　金剛諸珍，　奴婢車乘、 寶飾輦輿，　歡喜布施。　迴向佛道， 願得是乘，　三界第一，　諸佛所歎。 或有菩薩，　駟馬寶車、　欄楯華蓋、 軒飾布施。　復見菩薩，　身肉手足， 及妻子施，　求無上道。　又見菩薩， 頭目身體，　欣樂施與，　求佛智慧。 文殊師利！　我見諸王，　往詣佛所， 問無上道，　便捨樂土、　宮殿臣妾， 剃除鬚髮，　而被法服。　或見菩薩， 而作比丘，　獨處閑靜，　樂誦經典。 又見菩薩，　勇猛精進，　入於深山， 思惟佛道。　又見離欲，　常處空閑， 深修禪定，　得五神通。　又見菩薩， 安禪合掌，　以千萬偈、　讚諸法王。 復見菩薩，　智深志固，　能問諸佛， 聞悉受持。　又見佛子，　定慧具足， 以無量喻，　為眾講法。　欣樂說法， 化諸菩薩，　破魔兵眾，　而擊法鼓。 又見菩薩，　寂然宴默，　天龍恭敬， 不以為喜。　又見菩薩，　處林放光， 濟地獄苦，　令入佛道。　又見佛子， 未甞睡眠，　經行林中，　懃求佛道。 又見具戒，　威儀無缺，　淨如寶珠， 以求佛道。　又見佛子，　住忍辱力， 增上慢人，　惡罵捶打，　皆悉能忍， 以求佛道。　又見菩薩，　離諸戲笑， 及癡眷屬，　親近智者，　一心除亂， 攝念山林，　億千萬歲，　以求佛道。 或見菩薩，　餚饍飲食、　百種湯藥， 施佛及僧。　名衣上服，　價直千萬， 或無價衣，　施佛及僧。　千萬億種， 栴檀寶舍、　眾妙臥具，　施佛及僧。 清淨園林，　華菓茂盛，　流泉浴池， 施佛及僧。　如是等施，　種種微妙， 歡喜無厭，　求無上道。　或有菩薩， 說寂滅法，　種種教詔，　無數眾生。 或見菩薩，　觀諸法性，　無有二相， 猶如虛空。　又見佛子，　心無所著， 以此妙慧、　求無上道。　文殊師利！ 又有菩薩，　佛滅度後，　供養舍利。 又見佛子，　造諸塔廟，　無數恒沙， 嚴飾國界，　寶塔高妙，　五千由旬， 縱廣正等，　二千由旬。　一一塔廟， 各千幢幡，　珠交露幔，　寶鈴和鳴。 諸天龍神、　人及非人，　香華伎樂， 常以供養。　文殊師利！　諸佛子等， 為供舍利，　嚴飾塔廟，　國界自然， 殊特妙好，　如天樹王，　其華開敷。 佛放一光，　我及眾會，　見此國界， 種種殊妙。　諸佛神力、　智慧希有， 放一淨光，　照無量國。　我等見此， 得未曾有。　佛子文殊！　願決眾疑。 四眾欣仰，　瞻仁及我。　世尊何故， 放斯光明？　佛子時答，　決疑令喜。 何所饒益，　演斯光明？　佛坐道場， 所得妙法，　為欲說此？　為當授記？ 示諸佛土，　眾寶嚴淨，　及見諸佛， 此非小緣。　文殊當知，　四眾龍神， 瞻察仁者，　為說何等。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Buddha emitted a ray of light from the white curl of hair between his brows, illuminating eighteen thousand worlds to the east, leaving nothing unreached, down to the Avīci hell below and up to the Akaniṣṭha heaven above. Within this light, beings of the six destinies in those lands were seen in their entirety; the buddhas presently dwelling in those lands were seen as well, and the Dharma they were teaching could be heard. The monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen of those lands who had practiced and attained the path were seen too. Bodhisattva-mahāsattvas were seen as well, practicing the bodhisattva path through every kind of causal circumstance, every kind of faith and understanding, and every kind of outward form. Buddhas who had entered parinirvāṇa were seen; and after those buddhas' parinirvāṇa, stūpas of the seven treasures raised over their relics were seen as well. At that time Maitreya Bodhisattva had this thought: "The World-Honored One has now manifested this sign of spiritual transformation — for what cause and condition does this portent appear? The Buddha, the World-Honored One, has now entered samādhi; this is an inconceivable thing, a rare event now manifesting. Whom should I ask? Who is able to answer?" He thought further: "Mañjuśrī, the Dharma-prince, has already drawn near to and made offerings to immeasurable buddhas of the past; he must surely have seen such rare signs before. I will ask him now." At that time the monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen, and the gods, dragons, and spirits all had this same thought: "This sign of the Buddha's radiance and spiritual power — whom should we ask about it now?" At that time Maitreya Bodhisattva, wishing to resolve his own doubt, and observing as well the minds of the fourfold assembly and of the gods, dragons, and spirits gathered there, asked Mañjuśrī: "For what cause and condition does this portentous sign of spiritual power appear, this great light now cast forth, illuminating eighteen thousand lands to the east so that the adornments of those buddha-realms are seen in full?" Then, wishing to restate this same meaning once more, Maitreya Bodhisattva asked in verse: "Mañjuśrī! Why has the Guide sent forth this great light from the white curl between his brows, shining everywhere? Why has a rain of māndārava and mañjūṣaka flowers fallen, and a sandalwood-scented wind delighted every mind? For what cause has the ground become adorned and pure, and this whole world trembled in six ways? At that time the fourfold assembly all rejoiced, their bodies and minds at ease, gaining what they had never had before. The light from between his brows shone to the east, and eighteen thousand lands all turned the color of gold, from the Avīci hell below up to the peak of existence above. In all those worlds the beings of the six destinies, wherever birth and death led them, by whatever karmic cause of good or evil, receiving retribution fair or foul — all of this was seen entirely. And buddhas were seen as well, lion-lords of the sages, teaching their sūtras, subtle and wondrous, foremost of all; their voices pure, sending forth a gentle sound, teaching bodhisattvas beyond number, in a Brahmā-voice profound and wondrous, that all delighted to hear. Each in his own world taught the true Dharma through every kind of cause and condition, illuminating the Buddha's teaching with immeasurable similes, awakening beings. To one afflicted with suffering, wearied of old age, sickness, and death, he taught nirvāṇa, the utter end of every suffering. To one with merit, who had already made offerings to buddhas and set his will on the supreme Dharma, he taught the way of the solitary realizer. To a child of the Buddha who cultivated every kind of practice, seeking unsurpassed wisdom, he taught the path of purity. Mañjuśrī! Standing here, I have seen and heard all this, and a thousand million things besides — so many that I can only speak of them now in brief. I see in those lands bodhisattvas numerous as the sands of the Ganges, seeking the buddha-path through every kind of cause and condition. Some practice giving — gold, silver, coral, pearls, and maṇi gems, giant clam-shell and carnelian, diamonds and every kind of treasure, servants and carriages, bejeweled palanquins — giving them all with joy, dedicating the merit to the buddha-path, wishing to attain this vehicle, foremost in the three realms, praised by every buddha. Some bodhisattvas give jeweled chariots drawn by four horses, with railings and flowered canopies, finely adorned. I see bodhisattvas as well who give their own flesh, hands, and feet, and even their wives and children, seeking the unsurpassed path. And I see bodhisattvas who give their own heads and eyes and bodies, delighting to give them away, seeking the Buddha's wisdom. Mañjuśrī! I see kings going before the Buddha to ask about the unsurpassed path, then abandoning their pleasant lands, their palaces, ministers, and consorts, shaving their hair and beard, and putting on the Dharma robes. I see bodhisattvas as well, become monks, dwelling alone in stillness, delighting to recite the sūtras. I see bodhisattvas as well, brave and diligent, entering deep into the mountains to contemplate the buddha-path. And I see those free of desire, always dwelling in solitude, deeply cultivating meditative concentration, attaining the five spiritual powers. I see bodhisattvas as well, settled in meditation with palms joined, praising the Dharma-kings with a thousand myriad verses. I see bodhisattvas as well, deep in wisdom and firm in resolve, able to question the buddhas and, having heard, to hold every word in mind. And I see children of the Buddha, complete in concentration and wisdom, teaching the Dharma to the assembly with immeasurable similes, delighting to teach the Dharma, converting bodhisattvas, breaking the armies of Māra, and beating the Dharma-drum. I see bodhisattvas as well, still and silent in composure, honored by gods and dragons, yet unmoved to joy by it. I see bodhisattvas as well, dwelling in the forest, sending forth light, delivering beings from the sufferings of hell and bringing them into the buddha-path. And I see children of the Buddha who never sleep, walking back and forth in the forest, diligently seeking the buddha-path. I see those complete in discipline, their dignified conduct without flaw, pure as a jewel, seeking the buddha-path in this way. And I see children of the Buddha, abiding in the power of patience, who, when arrogant people abuse and strike them, are able to bear it entirely, seeking the buddha-path. I see bodhisattvas as well, who have left behind all frivolous laughter and foolish companions, drawing near instead to the wise, single-mindedly clearing away distraction, gathering their thoughts in the mountain forests for a thousand myriad years, seeking the buddha-path. I see bodhisattvas as well, giving fine food and drink, a hundred kinds of medicine, to the Buddha and the saṅgha; giving fine robes worth a thousand myriad pieces, or robes beyond price, to the Buddha and the saṅgha; giving a thousand myriad million kinds of sandalwood dwellings and every wondrous bedding to the Buddha and the saṅgha. Giving pure groves and gardens, luxuriant with flowers and fruit, flowing springs and bathing pools, to the Buddha and the saṅgha — giving of this kind, in every subtle and wondrous form, with joy that never wearies, seeking the unsurpassed path. And some bodhisattvas teach the Dharma of quiescent cessation, instructing countless beings through every kind of teaching. I see bodhisattvas as well, contemplating the nature of every phenomenon as without duality, just like empty space. And I see children of the Buddha whose minds cling to nothing, seeking the unsurpassed path with this wondrous wisdom. Mañjuśrī! There are bodhisattvas, too, who, after a buddha's parinirvāṇa, make offerings to his relics. And I see children of the Buddha building stūpas and shrines, countless as the sands of the Ganges, adorning the whole land — jeweled stūpas towering five thousand yojanas high, their breadth and length equally two thousand yojanas, each stūpa hung with a thousand banners and pennants, strung with pearls and dew-catching curtains, jeweled bells ringing in harmony. Gods, dragons, spirits, humans and non-humans constantly make offerings of incense, flowers, and music. Mañjuśrī! These children of the Buddha, in offering to the relics, adorn their stūpas and shrines until the whole land becomes, of itself, uniquely wondrous and fine, like the king of heavenly trees when its flowers open wide. The Buddha has sent forth a single ray of light, and I, together with this whole assembly, see this land in every kind of wondrous form. The spiritual power and rare wisdom of the buddhas send forth this one pure light, illuminating immeasurable lands. We, seeing this, have gained what we never had before. Mañjuśrī, child of the Buddha! I beg you to resolve the assembly's doubt. The fourfold assembly gazes up with delight, looking to you and to me. Why has the World-Honored One sent forth this light? Child of the Buddha, answer now, and resolve our doubt, bringing us joy. To what benefit does he display this light? Is it because he wishes to speak of the wondrous Dharma he attained at the seat of enlightenment? Or is it because he is about to bestow a prediction? To show us buddha-lands adorned in every treasure and pure, and to let us see the buddhas themselves — this is no small cause. Mañjuśrī, you should know: the fourfold assembly, the dragons and spirits, all look to you, waiting to hear what you will say."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi reads Maitreya's uncertainty here through what he calls the "trace" and "origin" (本迹) lens even at this early stage: on the surface, a bodhisattva simply does not know something and asks another bodhisattva who does. But Zhiyi's own gloss is blunt about what he thinks is actually happening beneath that surface — this is not real doubt in either bodhisattva, but a scripted exchange staged so that an audience that does need the explanation can receive it naturally, in dialogue, rather than as an unprompted lecture. Whether or not a reader accepts that particular framing, it is worth noticing what the text does either way: it makes Maitreya, the future buddha of this world, and Mañjuśrī, "the Dharma-prince," the two figures whose exchange opens the entire sūtra — and, as the next section shows, it is not a coincidence that these are the two names attached to the story Mañjuśrī is about to tell.

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Mañjuśrī said to Maitreya Bodhisattva-Mahāsattva and the other great beings: "Good sons! As I consider it, the Buddha, the World-Honored One, now wishes to teach the great Dharma, to rain down the great rain of Dharma, to blow the great Dharma-conch, to beat the great Dharma-drum, and to expound the great meaning of the Dharma. Good sons! In the presence of buddhas of the past, I have seen this same sign before — once such a light was sent forth, the great Dharma was taught at once. You should therefore know that the Buddha's manifesting this light now is likewise the same: he wishes to let every being hear and understand the Dharma that is hard for the whole world to believe, and so he manifests this sign. "Good sons! In ages past, immeasurable, boundless, and inconceivable asaṃkhyeya kalpas ago, there was then a buddha named Candrasūryapradīpa Tathāgata, Worthy of Offerings, Perfectly and Fully Awakened, Complete in Understanding and Conduct, Well-Gone, Knower of the World, Unsurpassed Being, Tamer of Those to be Tamed, Teacher of Gods and Humans, Buddha, World-Honored One. The Dharma each of them taught was good in the beginning, the middle, and the end. Seeing this same sign now, no different from before, I consider therefore: the Tathāgata today is about to teach a Mahāyāna sūtra called the Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower, a teaching for bodhisattvas, guarded and held in mind by the Buddha himself."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

Zhiyi calls this passage the "three sames" (三同): the same six signs, arising for the same reason, in an assembly built the same way. His point in laboring over the parallel is not antiquarian — it is an argument by structural proof. Mañjuśrī cannot simply assert that the Buddha is about to teach this sūtra; instead, he reasons from precedent, and the precedent is airtight only because every visible detail — the teaching of the Sūtra of Immeasurable Meanings first, the entry into the exact same samādhi, the same four flowers, the same six-fold trembling, the same ray of light reaching exactly eighteen thousand worlds — repeats without variation. If the signs are identical, Zhiyi reasons, the outcome that followed them before is the most reliable available guide to the outcome that will follow them now.

The two identifications at the story's close are easy to read past at pace, but they are the entire point of telling it here. Mañjuśrī reveals himself as Wondrous Light, the bodhisattva who upheld this sūtra for eighty small kalpas after the ancient buddha's parinirvāṇa — which is to say, the sūtra opens by quietly establishing that the figure now standing before this assembly has already done, in a previous world-age, exactly the work of transmission this current assembly is about to witness. And Maitreya — future buddha of this world, the very bodhisattva whose doubt opened this chapter — is revealed as Seeker of Fame, the one disciple in that ancient assembly singled out by name as distracted, unable to retain what he studied, chasing reputation rather than substance. Zhiyi does not read this as a rebuke; it is closer to a promise made honestly, without flattery: even the disciple least suited, by his own described character, to grasp what he was being taught did eventually become a buddha of the coming age. The sūtra's very first named example of someone helped by contact with the Lotus is also its least impressive one — which is itself a preview of what "skillful means" in the next chapter is going to argue about who this teaching is actually for.

5. Mañjuśrī's Verse Restatement

Chinese Source

爾時文殊師利於大眾中，欲重宣此義，而說偈言： 「我念過去世，　無量無數劫， 有佛人中尊，　號日月燈明。 世尊演說法，　度無量眾生， 無數億菩薩，　令入佛智慧。 佛未出家時，　所生八王子， 見大聖出家，　亦隨修梵行。 時佛說大乘，　經名無量義， 於諸大眾中，　而為廣分別。 佛說此經已，　即於法座上， 加趺坐三昧，　名無量義處。 天雨曼陀華，　天鼓自然鳴， 諸天龍鬼神，　供養人中尊。 一切諸佛土，　即時大震動， 佛放眉間光，　現諸希有事。 此光照東方，　萬八千佛土， 示一切眾生，　生死業報處。 有見諸佛土，　以眾寶莊嚴， 琉璃頗梨色，　斯由佛光照。 及見諸天人、　龍神夜叉眾、 乾闥緊那羅，　各供養其佛。 又見諸如來，　自然成佛道， 身色如金山，　端嚴甚微妙。 如淨琉璃中，　內現真金像， 世尊在大眾，　敷演深法義。 一一諸佛土，　聲聞眾無數， 因佛光所照，　悉見彼大眾。 或有諸比丘，　在於山林中， 精進持淨戒，　猶如護明珠。 又見諸菩薩，　行施忍辱等， 其數如恒沙，　斯由佛光照。 又見諸菩薩，　深入諸禪定， 身心寂不動，　以求無上道。 又見諸菩薩，　知法寂滅相， 各於其國土，　說法求佛道。 爾時四部眾，　見日月燈佛， 現大神通力，　其心皆歡喜， 各各自相問：　『是事何因緣？』 天人所奉尊，　適從三昧起， 讚妙光菩薩：　『汝為世間眼， 一切所歸信，　能奉持法藏， 如我所說法，　唯汝能證知。』 世尊既讚歎，　令妙光歡喜， 說是法華經，　滿六十小劫， 不起於此座。　所說上妙法， 是妙光法師，　悉皆能受持。 佛說是法華，　令眾歡喜已， 尋即於是日，　告於天人眾： 『諸法實相義，　已為汝等說， 我今於中夜，　當入於涅槃。 汝一心精進，　當離於放逸， 諸佛甚難值，　億劫時一遇。』 世尊諸子等，　聞佛入涅槃， 各各懷悲惱，　佛滅一何速。 聖主法之王，　安慰無量眾： 『我若滅度時，　汝等勿憂怖， 是德藏菩薩，　於無漏實相， 心已得通達。　其次當作佛， 號曰為淨身，　亦度無量眾。』 佛此夜滅度，　如薪盡火滅， 分布諸舍利，　而起無量塔。 比丘比丘尼，　其數如恒沙， 倍復加精進，　以求無上道。 是妙光法師，　奉持佛法藏， 八十小劫中，　廣宣法華經。 是諸八王子，　妙光所開化， 堅固無上道，　當見無數佛。 供養諸佛已，　隨順行大道， 相繼得成佛，　轉次而授記。 最後天中天，　號曰燃燈佛， 諸仙之導師，　度脫無量眾。 是妙光法師，　時有一弟子， 心常懷懈怠，　貪著於名利， 求名利無厭，　多遊族姓家， 棄捨所習誦，　廢忘不通利。 以是因緣故，　號之為求名。 亦行眾善業，　得見無數佛， 供養於諸佛，　隨順行大道， 具六波羅蜜，　今見釋師子。 其後當作佛，　號名曰彌勒， 廣度諸眾生，　其數無有量。 彼佛滅度後，　懈怠者汝是； 妙光法師者，　今則我身是。 我見燈明佛，　本光瑞如此， 以是知今佛，　欲說法華經。 今相如本瑞，　是諸佛方便， 今佛放光明，　助發實相義。 諸人今當知，　合掌一心待， 佛當雨法雨，　充足求道者。 諸求三乘人，　若有疑悔者， 佛當為除斷，　令盡無有餘。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time Mañjuśrī, wishing to restate this same meaning once more before the great assembly, spoke in verse: "I recall a past age, immeasurable, countless kalpas ago, there was a buddha, most honored among men, named Candrasūryapradīpa. The World-Honored One taught the Dharma, delivering immeasurable beings, and countless koṭis of bodhisattvas he brought into the Buddha's wisdom. Before the Buddha left his household, the eight royal sons he had fathered, seeing the great sage leave his household, likewise followed him and cultivated pure conduct. Then the Buddha taught the Great Vehicle, a sūtra named Immeasurable Meanings, and in the midst of the great assembly expounded it broadly and in detail. Having taught this sūtra, the Buddha, upon the Dharma-seat, sat cross-legged in a samādhi named the Station of Immeasurable Meanings. Heavenly māndārava flowers rained down, heavenly drums sounded of themselves, and the gods, dragons, and spirits made offerings to the one most honored among men. All the buddha-lands at once trembled greatly, and the Buddha sent forth light from between his brows, manifesting these rare events. This light illuminated eighteen thousand buddha-lands to the east, showing all beings the places where the karmic retribution of birth and death unfolds. Some saw buddha-lands adorned with every treasure, colored like lapis lazuli and crystal, all by the illumination of the Buddha's light. And they saw gods and humans, dragons, spirits, yakṣas, gandharvas, and kiṃnaras, each making offerings to their own buddha. And they saw the tathāgatas, of themselves accomplishing the buddha-path, their bodies the color of a mountain of gold, upright and majestic, subtle and wondrous. Like a true golden image appearing within pure lapis lazuli, the World-Honored Ones, in the midst of their assemblies, expounded the profound meaning of the Dharma. In each and every buddha-land, the assemblies of śrāvakas were beyond number; by the Buddha's light shining upon them, all those assemblies were seen in full. Some monks were seen dwelling in the mountain forests, diligently upholding the pure precepts, as though guarding a bright jewel. And bodhisattvas were seen, practicing giving, patience, and the rest, their number like the sands of the Ganges, all by the Buddha's light shining upon them. And bodhisattvas were seen, entering deeply into meditative concentration, body and mind still and unmoving, seeking the unsurpassed path. And bodhisattvas were seen, knowing the quiescent, ceased mark of all phenomena, each in his own land teaching the Dharma, seeking the buddha-path. At that time the fourfold assembly, seeing the Buddha Candrasūryapradīpa manifest this great spiritual power, all rejoiced in their hearts, and asked one another: 'For what cause does this happen?' The one honored by gods and humans, having just arisen from his samādhi, praised Wondrous Light Bodhisattva: 'You are the eye of the world, the one all take refuge in and trust, able to uphold the treasury of the Dharma; of the Dharma I have taught, you alone are able to bear witness and know it.' The World-Honored One, having praised him, brought joy to Wondrous Light, and taught this Lotus Sūtra for a full sixty small kalpas without rising from his seat. And this supreme, wondrous Dharma he taught, the Dharma Teacher Wondrous Light was able to uphold in its entirety. When the Buddha had taught this Lotus Sūtra and brought joy to the assembly, that very day he declared to the gathering of gods and humans: 'The meaning of the true mark of all phenomena I have now taught for you; tonight, at midnight, I shall enter nirvāṇa. Be diligent, single-mindedly, and free yourselves from negligence — buddhas are exceedingly hard to encounter; only once in a hundred million kalpas does one meet with them.' The Buddha's own sons, hearing that the Buddha would enter nirvāṇa, each held sorrow and distress in their hearts: how swiftly the Buddha's passing has come! The sagely lord, king of the Dharma, consoled the immeasurable assembly: 'When I have entered final nirvāṇa, you should have no grief or fear. This bodhisattva, Store of Virtue, has already penetrated the undefiled true mark; he shall next become a buddha, named Pure Body, and he too shall deliver immeasurable beings.' That night the Buddha entered nirvāṇa, as a fire dies out when its fuel is spent, and his relics were distributed, and immeasurable stūpas were raised over them. The monks and nuns, their number like the sands of the Ganges, redoubled their diligence, seeking the unsurpassed path. This Dharma Teacher Wondrous Light upheld the Buddha's treasury of the Dharma, and for eighty small kalpas proclaimed the Lotus Sūtra widely. These eight royal sons, opened and transformed by Wondrous Light, became firm in the unsurpassed path, and were destined to see countless buddhas. Having made offerings to those buddhas, they followed and practiced the great path, and one after another accomplished buddhahood, each in turn bestowing predictions on the next. The last of them, most honored among the gods, was named the Buddha Dīpaṃkara, guide and teacher of the sages, who delivered immeasurable beings. This Dharma Teacher Wondrous Light, at that time, had a disciple whose mind constantly held indolence, greedily attached to fame and profit; seeking fame and profit without satiety, he wandered often among noble households, abandoning what he had studied and recited, letting it lapse and fail to master it. For this reason he was named Seeker of Fame. Yet he too practiced many good deeds, and came to see countless buddhas; making offerings to those buddhas, he followed and practiced the great path, and, complete now in the six perfections, sees today the lion of the Śākyas. Hereafter he shall become a buddha, named Maitreya, broadly delivering beings, their number beyond measure. After that buddha's parinirvāṇa, the indolent one was you yourself; and the Dharma Teacher Wondrous Light — that is I myself, now. I saw, in the Buddha Candrasūryapradīpa's time, this same original sign of light — and by this I know that the Buddha now is about to teach the Lotus Sūtra. This present sign is like that original portent — it is the skillful means of the buddhas; the Buddha now sends forth this light to help disclose the meaning of the true mark. All of you should know this now: join your palms and wait with one-pointed mind — the Buddha is about to rain down the rain of the Dharma, satisfying all who seek the path. For all who seek the three vehicles, if any harbor doubt or regret, the Buddha shall remove and cut it off for them, until none remains at all."

Reading note — after Zhiyi

One detail this closing verse states plainly and worth not reading past: the ancient Buddha Candrasūryapradīpa's assembly heard him say, of that very sūtra, "the meaning of the true mark of all phenomena I have now taught for you" (諸法實相義，已為汝等說) — a phrase that will not be spoken again, of this present teaching, until the "true mark of all phenomena" is finally named directly in the next chapter. Mañjuśrī is not just promising that a Mahāyāna sūtra is coming; by choosing this particular ancient story to tell, in this much specific detail, he is already handing the assembly the technical name for what that teaching is going to be about, three verses before anyone in the current assembly has heard it used.

Zhiyi's summary of the whole chapter's function is worth holding onto going forward: nothing has actually been taught yet. Two full sections of prose and two full sections of verse have been spent establishing, through elaborate structural proof rather than direct statement, that something unprecedented is about to happen — and the sūtra will not turn to what that something actually is until Śāriputra, not yet named in this opening chapter, asks for it directly at the start of the next.
