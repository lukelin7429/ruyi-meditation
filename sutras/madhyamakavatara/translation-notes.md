# Madhyamakāvatāra Reading Notes — Terminology and Method Notes

These notes record recurring terminology and method decisions for Ru-Yi's reading-notes collection on Candrakīrti's *Madhyamakāvatāra* (入中論, "Entering the Middle Way"). They exist so that wording stays consistent across all eleven entries, which were written and published one at a time rather than all at once.

## This collection is not a translation project

Every other multi-part text on this site (*Cheng Weishi Lun*, the *Yogācārabhūmi*, Yuanying's Śūraṅgama commentary, etc.) works from a source that is either in the public domain or old enough that no rights holder is known to survive. This collection is different, and deliberately structured around that difference:

- The root verses of the *Madhyamakāvatāra* are ancient (Candrakīrti, 7th century CE) and not at issue.
- What we studied to prepare these notes is a specific, comparatively recent lecture transcript: Ven. Miao Jing's (妙境長老, 1930–2003) *入中論頌講記*, an oral teaching series transcribed by Fayun Publishing (法雲出版社) and hosted at [fayun.org](https://www.fayun.org). Ven. Miao Jing died in 2003; both his own rights and Fayun Publishing's editorial rights in this specific transcription are very likely still active under any reasonable reading of copyright term.
- Because of that, these pages are **original commentary that cites the transcript as a reference**, not a translation of it. Any quotation from the transcript in these notes is a brief excerpt used for illustration — never more than a sentence or two, always attributed. The English prose explaining the text's arguments is Ru-Yi's own composition throughout.
- Root verses quoted in these notes are translated fresh, by Ru-Yi, directly from Candrakīrti's text as rendered into Chinese by Venerable Fazun (法尊法師) — not copied or adapted from any existing published English edition (e.g. Huntington, Padmakara Translation Group).

See the pilot discussion that established this approach for more background before adding further entries.

## Core terminology

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 入中論 | *Madhyamakāvatāra* ("Entering the Middle Way") | Title kept in Sanskrit in running prose; English gloss on first use per page |
| 中觀 | Madhyamaka | The "middle way" school; capitalize as a proper noun |
| 中觀論 / 中論 | *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* ("Middle Way Treatise") | Nāgārjuna's root text, which the *Madhyamakāvatāra* is written to help readers enter |
| 十地 | the ten [bodhisattva] grounds | Kept as "ground," not "stage" or "level," across all entries |
| 波羅蜜多 / 波羅蜜 | perfection | The six (or ten) pāramitās; named individually (giving, discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, wisdom, skillful means, power, aspiration/vow, wisdom-as-tenth) rather than transliterated |
| 佛子 | a child of the Victorious Ones | Fazun's literal phrasing for "bodhisattva" in verse; kept literal in translated verses, glossed as "bodhisattva" in prose |
| 大悲心 | great compassion | |
| 無二慧 | non-dual wisdom | Wisdom free of grasping at a fixed self and fixed dharmas |
| 菩提心 | the mind of awakening / bodhicitta | Use "bodhicitta" sparingly; prefer the English gloss in prose |
| 薩迦耶見 | the view of a fixed self (satkāyadṛṣṭi) | The root of all afflictions; give the Sanskrit once per page, then use the English gloss |
| 三結 | the three fetters | Self-view, attachment to rites/vows as ends in themselves, and doubt — severed on entering the First Ground |
| 無生法忍 | acceptance of non-arising | What opens entry to the First Ground; not an intellectual conclusion but a direct realization |
| 自性 | inherent nature / inherent existence | Use "inherently existing" as the adjectival form |
| 自相 | own-characteristic | Distinct technical term from 自性 — used specifically in the "own-characteristic emptiness" (自相空) section |
| 依他起(性) | the dependent nature | Yogācāra's term for conditioned phenomena; kept as-is when describing the Yogācāra position being examined |
| 石女兒 | the son of a barren woman | Stock analogy for something empty in both truths at once, i.e. not existent even conventionally — distinguished from things merely empty of inherent nature |
| 法尊法師 | Venerable Fazun | Translator of the Chinese root verses quoted throughout this collection; not the author of the commentary |
| 妙境長老 | Ven. Miao Jing (1930–2003) | Author of the lecture transcript this collection is informed by — always cited as a reference, never as a translated source |

## Register and method

- **Root verses**: translated fresh from Fazun's Chinese for each entry, aiming for plain, readable English close to the Chinese rather than a loose paraphrase. Not checked against or adapted from any existing English translation.
- **Prose commentary**: original composition explaining the argument, informed by the structure and content of Ven. Miao Jing's lectures but not translating or closely paraphrasing his sentences. A "Reading Notes" divider marks where the translated verses end and this original commentary begins on each page.
- **Illustrative material** (stories, analogies, Q&A exchanges) from the lecture transcript: retold in our own words and summarized, never quoted at length. Direct quotation is limited to short phrases (a few words) with clear attribution.
- **Citation**: every entry opens with a note box stating the source relationship explicitly, and closes with a one-line source citation naming the specific lecture session(s) drawn on.
