Entering the Middle Way.

The Madhyamakāvatāra (入中論) is Candrakīrti's own guide into Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka philosophy — the path through the ten bodhisattva grounds to buddhahood, argued through the lens of emptiness. These are Ru-Yi's own reading notes on the text, organized by theme rather than session by session.

What this collection is

These pages are original commentary written by Ru-Yi, not a translation of any published work. They walk through the Madhyamakāvatāra's own argument — the meaning of "middle," the three causes of a bodhisattva, the ten grounds, and the refutation of a fixed self — organized around themes so a reader can enter wherever a question actually starts.

Root verses quoted in these notes are translated directly by Ru-Yi from Candrakīrti's Sanskrit text as rendered into Chinese by Venerable Fazun (法尊法師), not copied from any existing English edition.

Reference, not a source translated

In preparing these notes we studied Ven. Miao Jing's (妙境長老, 1930–2003) 入中論頌講記 — his oral lecture series on this text, transcribed by Fayun Publishing (法雲出版社) and hosted at fayun.org.

That transcript is listed here as a reference work we consulted, not reproduced. Any quotations from it in these notes are brief excerpts cited for illustration, and the English wording throughout is our own.
