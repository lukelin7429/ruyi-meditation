The Imperial Summons

宣詔品第九

Empress Wu Zetian and Emperor Zhongzong send an imperial edict summoning Huineng to the capital, on the recommendation of the two most eminent court chan masters, who admit that the real teaching lies elsewhere. Huineng declines and stays in the mountains — but sends the court envoy Xue Jian back with a compressed final statement of nearly everything this sūtra has taught so far, distilled into a single audience with a man who has to carry it back to the throne by memory.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T2008 · Zongbao's redaction, Yuan dynasty (1291 CE) Commentary guide: Hanshan Deqing's 六祖法寶壇經解義 (17th c.) — titled 護法品第九, "The Chapter of Protecting the Dharma," in Hanshan's own redivision
This part covers
The imperial edict of 705 CE, in which Empress Wu Zetian and Emperor Zhongzong summon Huineng to the capital on the recommendation of National Teacher Hui'an and Shenxiu themselves; Huineng's decline on grounds of age and illness; his teaching to the court envoy Xue Jian that the Way is realized through the mind and was never a matter of sitting or any posture at all; the exchange on brightness and darkness, affliction and bodhi, and true non-arising as against outside teachings' counterfeit of it; and the emperor's own edict of gratitude in reply, naming Huineng's former dwelling Guo'en Temple, "Temple of Gratitude to the State."
Why it matters
This is the sūtra's most condensed doctrinal chapter — nearly everything taught across the previous eight chapters is compressed here into a single audience, delivered to a court envoy who has to carry it back to the throne from memory alone.

1. "The Way Is Realized Through the Mind — How Could It Lie in Sitting?"

Chinese Source

神龍元年上元日，則天、中宗詔云：「朕請安、秀二師宮中供養。萬機之暇，每究一乘。二師推讓云：『南方有能禪師，密授忍大師衣法，傳佛心印，可請彼問。』今遣內侍薛簡，馳詔迎請，願師慈念，速赴上京。」師上表辭疾，願終林麓。薛簡曰：「京城禪德皆云：『欲得會道，必須坐禪習定。若不因禪定而得解脫者，未之有也。』未審師所說法如何？」師曰：「道由心悟，豈在坐也。經云：『若言如來若坐若臥，是行邪道。』何故？無所從來，亦無所去。無生無滅，是如來清淨禪。諸法空寂，是如來清淨坐。究竟無證，豈況坐耶。」簡曰：「弟子回京，主上必問。願師慈悲，指示心要，傳奏兩宮及京城學道者。譬如一燈，然百千燈，冥者皆明，明明無盡。」師云：「道無明暗，明暗是代謝之義。明明無盡，亦是有盡，相待立名故。《淨名經》云：『法無有比，無相待故。』」

Ru-Yi Translation

In the first year of the Shenlong era, on the fifteenth day of the first month, Empress Wu Zetian and Emperor Zhongzong sent down an edict, which read: "We have invited the two masters Hui'an and Shenxiu to receive offerings within the palace itself, and in the intervals of the ten thousand affairs of state, We constantly inquire into the one vehicle. The two masters, deferring to another, said: 'In the south there is Chan Master Neng, who secretly received the robe and Dharma of the great teacher Hongren, and transmits the very seal of the Buddha's mind — he may be asked about this instead.' We now send the palace attendant Xue Jian, bearing this edict, to welcome and receive the Master. We hope the Master will show compassion, and come quickly to the capital." The Master submitted a memorial declining on grounds of illness, wishing to end his days among the forests and hills. Xue Jian said, "The eminent Chan teachers of the capital all say: 'To attain understanding of the Way, one must without fail sit in chan and practice samādhi; there has never been anyone who attained liberation without relying on dhyāna and samādhi.' I do not know what the Master's own teaching on this might be." The Master said, "The Way is realized through the mind — how could it lie in sitting? The sūtra says, 'To say that the Tathāgata either sits or lies down is to walk a mistaken path.' Why is this so? He comes from nowhere, and goes nowhere; being without arising and without ceasing is the Tathāgata's own pure chan. Every phenomenon being empty and still is the Tathāgata's own pure sitting. Ultimately there is nothing at all to be realized — how much less could there be any sitting in it?" Xue Jian said, "When your disciple returns to the capital, the sovereign is certain to ask me about this. I beg the Master's compassion — please point out the essentials of the mind, so that I may report them to both palaces and to every student of the Way in the capital. It is like a single lamp that lights a hundred, a thousand further lamps — every darkness becomes light, and this brightness upon brightness never comes to an end." The Master said, "The Way itself has no light and no darkness; light and darkness are only a matter of one replacing the other in succession. Even 'brightness upon brightness never ending' is itself something that will eventually end, since it is a name set up only in relation to something else it is compared against. The Vimalakīrti Sūtra says, 'The Dharma has nothing to be compared with it at all, since it stands in relation to nothing else whatsoever.'"

Reading note — after Hanshan

Hanshan reads Xue Jian's opening claim — that the capital's own eminent chan teachers all insist liberation requires sitting in dhyāna without exception — as the same teaching this sūtra already diagnosed in an earlier chapter, when Shenxiu's own disciple Zhicheng described his teacher's method as "fixing the mind and contemplating stillness, sitting continuously without ever lying down." What was previously a critique of one monastery's practice has now become, in this chapter, the received wisdom of the entire capital, which is exactly why Huineng's answer needs to be pitched at the level of an empire rather than a single community: not a correction of technique, but a redefinition of what dhyāna itself actually is, tied to the Tathāgata's own condition rather than any bodily posture.

On Xue Jian's lamp-lighting-a-thousand-lamps image, Hanshan draws out a subtlety easy to miss: even an image of infinite, ever-expanding brightness remains, on Huineng's correction, still caught inside duality, because "brightness" only has meaning set against "darkness" — an infinite regress of lamps is still a regress within the pair, not an escape from it. This is why Huineng reaches for the Vimalakīrti Sūtra's claim that the Dharma "has nothing to be compared with it at all" — not a brighter light chasing away an ever-retreating darkness, but a reality to which the entire framework of comparison simply does not apply.

2. Non-Duality, True Non-Arising, and the Emperor's Gratitude

Chinese Source

簡曰：「明喻智慧，暗喻煩惱。修道之人，倘不以智慧照破煩惱，無始生死憑何出離？」師曰：「煩惱即是菩提，無二無別。若以智慧照破煩惱者，此是二乘見解。羊鹿等機，上智大根，悉不如是。」簡曰：「如何是大乘見解？」師曰：「明與無明，凡夫見二；智者了達，其性無二。無二之性，即是實性。實性者，處凡愚而不減，在賢聖而不增，住煩惱而不亂，居禪定而不寂。不斷不常，不來不去，不在中間及其內外，不生不滅，性相如如，常住不遷，名之曰道。」簡曰：「師說不生不滅，何異外道？」師曰：「外道所說不生不滅者，將滅止生，以生顯滅，滅猶不滅，生說不生。我說不生不滅者，本自無生，今亦不滅，所以不同外道。汝若欲知心要，但一切善惡都莫思量，自然得入清淨心體，湛然常寂，妙用恒沙。」簡蒙指教，豁然大悟。禮辭歸闕，表奏師語。 其年九月三日，有詔獎諭師曰：「師辭老疾，為朕修道，國之福田。師若淨名托疾毘耶，闡揚大乘，傳諸佛心，談不二法。薛簡傳師指授如來知見，朕積善餘慶，宿種善根，值師出世，頓悟上乘。感荷師恩，頂戴無已，并奉磨衲袈裟及水晶鉢，勅韶州刺史修飾寺宇，賜師舊居為國恩寺。」

Ru-Yi Translation

Xue Jian said, "Brightness is a metaphor for wisdom, and darkness for affliction. If a person cultivating the Way does not use wisdom to illuminate and break through affliction, then by what means could they ever escape birth and death, which has been going on since beginningless time?" The Master said, "Affliction is itself bodhi — they are not two different things, with no distinction between them. To use wisdom to illuminate and break through affliction is itself the view of the two lesser vehicles, the workings of the goat and the deer — those of the highest wisdom and greatest capacity are never like this at all." Xue Jian said, "Then what is the view of the Great Vehicle?" The Master said, "Brightness and the absence of brightness — ordinary people see these as two different things; the wise understand fully that their nature is not two. This nature without duality is itself the real nature. This real nature, present among the foolish and the ordinary, is not thereby diminished; present among the worthy and the sage, is not thereby increased. Dwelling amid affliction, it is not disturbed by it; abiding within meditative absorption, it is not made still by it either. It is not cut off, and it is not eternal; it does not come, and it does not go; it is not located in any middle, nor in any inside or outside. Without arising and without ceasing, its nature and its every mark remain just as they are, constantly abiding, never shifting — this alone is what is called the Way." Xue Jian said, "The Master speaks of no arising and no ceasing — how does this differ from what outside teachings also say?" The Master said, "What outside teachings mean by 'no arising and no ceasing' is taking cessation to bring arising to a halt, using arising to make cessation visible; in their teaching, cessation is still not truly ceasing, and arising is spoken of even while claiming there is no arising at all. What I mean by 'no arising and no ceasing' is this: it was, from the very beginning, never arisen at all, and so now there is nothing left that could cease either — this is why it is not the same as outside teachings. If you wish to know the essentials of the mind, simply refrain from thinking about anything at all, whether good or evil, and you will naturally enter the pure substance of the mind, profoundly still and constantly at peace, its wondrous function as numberless as the sands of the Ganges." Receiving this instruction, Xue Jian was suddenly and completely awakened. He took his leave with a bow and returned to the capital, where he submitted a memorial reporting the Master's own words. On the third day of the ninth month of that same year, an edict came down praising and instructing the Master: "The Master has declined our summons on grounds of age and illness, cultivating the Way on Our behalf — a field of blessing for the whole nation. The Master is like Vimalakīrti, feigning illness at Vaiśālī, proclaiming the Great Vehicle, transmitting the very mind of every buddha, and expounding the teaching of non-duality. Xue Jian has conveyed to Us the Master's own instruction in the Tathāgata's knowledge and vision; through the accumulated goodness of Our reign and the wholesome roots planted in past lives, We have had the good fortune to encounter the Master's appearance in this world, and have suddenly awakened to the highest vehicle. Moved by gratitude for the Master's kindness, We hold it upon Our head without end." Together with this the emperor sent a ceremonial robe and a crystal bowl as offerings, and decreed that the prefect of Shaozhou should restore and adorn the Master's temple, bestowing upon the Master's former dwelling the name Guo'en Temple, "Temple of Gratitude to the State."

Reading note — after Hanshan

Hanshan reads Xue Jian's wisdom-against-affliction model as still fundamentally a battle between two opposing forces, wisdom deployed as a weapon to defeat affliction as an enemy — precisely the "two lesser vehicles" framework Huineng's answer rejects. "Affliction is itself bodhi" restates, in this chapter's more formal register, the same substance-and-function pattern this sūtra has built again and again: samādhi and prajñā as lamp and light, precept and wisdom and samādhi as three names for one self-nature, and now affliction and bodhi as the identical case once more. Nothing needs to be defeated because nothing was ever a separate, opposing thing to begin with.

The distinction Huineng draws against "outside teachings" on non-arising and non-ceasing is, on Hanshan's reading, this chapter's most technically precise point in the entire sūtra: a heterodox account of non-arising is still a technique, an active effort of using cessation to suppress arising, which means something is still being manufactured and maintained by will. What Huineng describes instead was never arisen in the first place, and therefore requires no ongoing effort to keep from ceasing — the same distinction, in more exact philosophical language, that separated Zhihuang's twenty years of effortful sitting from the "great samādhi" that has no entering or leaving at all. Hanshan reads Xue Jian's own swift awakening, on hearing this single compressed exchange, as proof that a court envoy carrying the teaching back by memory alone could grasp in one sitting what Zhihuang's own two decades of practice, aimed at the wrong target, never reached.
