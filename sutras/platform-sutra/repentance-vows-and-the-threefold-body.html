Repentance, the Vows, and the Threefold Body

懺悔品第六

Before a crowd gathered from every direction, Huineng leads a formal ceremony — incense, repentance, vows, refuge — and relocates every element of it inside the mind of each listener. The buddhas taking refuge are not external; the sentient beings to be delivered by the great vows are the deluded states of one's own mind; and the threefold body of buddhahood, so often described as something a bodhisattva eventually acquires, is declared already complete within the self-nature of everyone present.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T2008 · Zongbao's redaction, Yuan dynasty (1291 CE) Commentary guide: Hanshan Deqing's 六祖法寶壇經解義 (17th c.)
This part covers
The five-fold incense of the Dharma-body of self-nature — precept, samādhi, wisdom, liberation, and the knowledge and vision of liberation; formless repentance, distinguishing repenting past wrongs from reforming against future ones; the four great vows, reinterpreted so that the "sentient beings" delivered are the deluded states of one's own mind; the formless threefold refuge in awakening, rightness, and purity; and the single-substance, threefold body of self-nature — the pure Dharma-body, the complete reward-body, and the hundred-thousand-koṭi transformation-body — closing with a Verse of Formlessness on true repentance.
Why it matters
This chapter takes the most ritually elaborate vocabulary in Buddhist practice — incense offerings, formal vows, the threefold refuge, the doctrine of the buddha's three bodies — and relocates every element of it inside the listener's own mind, without discarding any of the traditional forms.

1. The Five-Fold Incense of the Dharma-Body

Chinese Source

時，大師見廣韶洎四方士庶，駢集山中聽法，於是陞座，告眾曰：「來，諸善知識！此事須從自事中起，於一切時，念念自淨其心。 自修自行，見自己法身，見自心佛，自度自戒，始得不假到此。既從遠來，一會于此，皆共有緣。今可各各胡跪，先為傳自性五分法身香，次授無相懺悔。」眾胡跪。師曰：「一、戒香。即自心中無非無惡、無嫉妬、無貪瞋、無劫害，名戒香。二、定香。即覩諸善惡境相，自心不亂，名定香。三、慧香。自心無礙，常以智慧觀照自性，不造諸惡；雖修眾善，心不執著，敬上念下，矜恤孤貧，名慧香。四、解脫香。即自心無所攀緣，不思善、不思惡，自在無礙，名解脫香。五、解脫知見香。自心既無所攀緣善惡，不可沈空守寂，即須廣學多聞，識自本心，達諸佛理，和光接物，無我無人，直至菩提，真性不易，名解脫知見香。善知識！此香各自內熏，莫向外覓。」

Ru-Yi Translation

At that time the Great Master, seeing people of Guangzhou, Shaozhou, and every direction gathered together in the mountains to hear the Dharma, ascended his seat and told the assembly: "Come, all you good friends! This matter must arise from within your own being. At every moment, purify your own mind, thought after thought. Cultivate yourself, practice for yourself, see your own Dharma-body, see the buddha within your own mind, deliver yourself, and hold your own precepts — only then can this be attained without relying on anything outside. Since you have all come from far away and gathered together here, we surely share a bond. Now let each of you kneel, and I will first transmit the five-fold incense of the Dharma-body of self-nature, and then confer formless repentance." The assembly knelt. The Master said: "First, the incense of precept: to have, within one's own mind, no wrong and no evil, no envy, no greed or anger, and no thought of plunder or harm — this is called the incense of precept. Second, the incense of samādhi: to behold every kind of good or evil circumstance while one's own mind remains undisturbed — this is called the incense of samādhi. Third, the incense of wisdom: one's own mind unobstructed, constantly contemplating self-nature with the light of wisdom, committing no evil at all; and though one cultivates every kind of good, the mind remains unattached to it, respecting those above and caring for those below, showing compassion to the orphaned and the poor — this is called the incense of wisdom. Fourth, the incense of liberation: one's own mind with nothing at all to grasp onto, thinking neither of good nor of evil, at ease and unobstructed — this is called the incense of liberation. Fifth, the incense of the knowledge and vision of liberation: once one's own mind has nothing at all to grasp onto, whether good or evil, one must not sink into blank emptiness and cling to stillness, but must instead study widely and learn extensively, recognize one's own fundamental mind, penetrate the principles of every buddha, blend one's own light with that of others, be without a notion of self or of other, and go straight to bodhi, the true nature never changing — this is called the incense of the knowledge and vision of liberation. Good friends, each of these kinds of incense is to be burned within yourself — do not go searching for them outside."

Reading note — after Hanshan

Hanshan reads the five incenses as a deliberate sequence rather than five equal, interchangeable virtues: precept keeps new stains from forming, samādhi keeps the mind undisturbed by whatever it encounters, wisdom actively contemplates self-nature without producing evil, and liberation lets go even of the strain of holding good and evil apart. The fifth incense exists, on his reading, specifically to guard against a danger the fourth creates — that liberation, taken too far, collapses into passive withdrawal. "Must not sink into blank emptiness and cling to stillness" is his flag that liberation from grasping is not the same achievement as disengagement from the world; the fifth incense requires wide learning and active contact with others precisely because a liberated mind that has stopped engaging with anything has mistaken indifference for freedom.

Hanshan glosses "blend one's own light with that of others" (和光接物) as language drawn from outside the Buddhist canon proper — a phrase with roots in the Daoist classics — deployed here to describe a liberated person who does not stand apart from ordinary society but moves through it without friction, neither performing superiority nor being stained by contact. The whole five-fold sequence, on this reading, describes incense that is burned nowhere but within the practitioner; the ceremony's outward form — kneeling, listening, receiving instruction — is the occasion for something that has no location a bystander could point to.

2. Formless Repentance, and the Four Great Vows

Chinese Source

「今與汝等授無相懺悔，滅三世罪，令得三業清淨。善知識！各隨我語，一時道：『弟子等，從前念今念及後念，念念不被愚迷染。從前所有惡業愚迷等罪，悉皆懺悔，願一時銷滅，永不復起。弟子等，從前念今念及後念，念念不被憍誑染。從前所有惡業憍誑等罪，悉皆懺悔，願一時銷滅，永不復起。弟子等，從前念今念及後念，念念不被嫉妬染。從前所有惡業嫉妬等罪，悉皆懺悔，願一時銷滅，永不復起。』善知識！已上是為無相懺悔。云何名懺？云何名悔？懺者，懺其前愆，從前所有惡業，愚迷憍誑嫉妬等罪，悉皆盡懺，永不復起，是名為懺。悔者，悔其後過，從今以後，所有惡業，愚迷憍誑嫉妬等罪，今已覺悟，悉皆永斷，更不復作，是名為悔。故稱懺悔。凡夫愚迷，只知懺其前愆，不知悔其後過。以不悔故，前愆不滅，後過又生。前愆既不滅，後過復又生，何名懺悔？ 「善知識！既懺悔已，與善知識發四弘誓願，各須用心正聽。自心眾生無邊誓願度，自心煩惱無邊誓願斷，自性法門無盡誓願學，自性無上佛道誓願成。善知識！大家豈不道，眾生無邊誓願度。恁麼道，且不是惠能度。善知識！心中眾生，所謂邪迷心、誑妄心、不善心、嫉妬心、惡毒心，如是等心，盡是眾生。各須自性自度，是名真度。何名自性自度？即自心中邪見煩惱愚癡眾生，將正見度。既有正見，使般若智打破愚癡迷妄眾生，各各自度。邪來正度，迷來悟度，愚來智度，惡來善度；如是度者，名為真度。又煩惱無邊誓願斷，將自性般若智，除却虛妄思想心是也。又法門無盡誓願學，須自見性，常行正法，是名真學。又無上佛道誓願成，既常能下心，行於真正，離迷離覺，常生般若。除真除妄，即見佛性，即言下佛道成。常念修行，是願力法。」

Ru-Yi Translation

"Now I will confer upon you formless repentance, which extinguishes the offenses of the three periods of time and brings the three karmic actions to purity. Good friends, each repeat after me, all together: 'We disciples, from the thought before, the thought now, and the thought to come, in thought after thought will not be stained by foolishness and delusion. Whatever evil karma of foolishness and delusion we have committed in the past, we now entirely repent of it, wishing it extinguished all at once, never to arise again. We disciples, from the thought before, the thought now, and the thought to come, in thought after thought will not be stained by arrogance and deceit. Whatever evil karma of arrogance and deceit we have committed in the past, we now entirely repent of it, wishing it extinguished all at once, never to arise again. We disciples, from the thought before, the thought now, and the thought to come, in thought after thought will not be stained by envy. Whatever evil karma of envy we have committed in the past, we now entirely repent of it, wishing it extinguished all at once, never to arise again.' Good friends, all of this above is formless repentance. What is meant by 'repenting'? What is meant by 'reforming'? To repent is to repent of one's former transgressions — whatever evil karma of foolishness, delusion, arrogance, deceit, and envy there has been, to repent of it entirely, so that it never arises again — this is called repenting. To reform is to reform against future faults — from this moment on, whatever evil karma of foolishness, delusion, arrogance, deceit, and envy there might be, now that one has awakened to it, to cut it off forever and never commit it again — this is called reforming. This is why it is called repentance and reform together. Ordinary people, foolish and deluded, know only how to repent of their former transgressions, without knowing how to reform against their future faults. Because they do not reform, their former transgressions are not extinguished, and further faults arise besides. If the former transgressions are not yet extinguished, and further faults arise on top of them, how could this be called repentance and reform at all? "Good friends, having now repented, I will make the four great vows together with you, good friends — each of you should listen with full attention. 'The sentient beings of my own mind, boundless, I vow to deliver. The afflictions of my own mind, boundless, I vow to cut off. The teachings of my own nature, inexhaustible, I vow to learn. The unsurpassed buddha-path of my own nature, I vow to accomplish.' Good friends, do we not all say, 'the sentient beings, boundless, I vow to deliver'? But saying it in this way, it is, after all, not Huineng who does the delivering. Good friends, the sentient beings within your own mind — the mind of wrong views and delusion, the mind of deceit and falsehood, the unwholesome mind, the envious mind, the mind of malice and harm, minds such as these — all of them are the sentient beings meant here. Each of you must deliver them by your own self-nature; this alone is called true deliverance. What is meant by delivering them by your own self-nature? It means taking the sentient beings of wrong views, affliction, foolishness, and delusion within your own mind, and delivering them by right view. Once right view is present, use the wisdom of prajñā to break through these sentient beings of foolishness, delusion, and falsehood, and deliver each of them, one by one, by yourself: where wrong comes, deliver it by right; where delusion comes, deliver it by awakening; where foolishness comes, deliver it by wisdom; where evil comes, deliver it by good — delivering in this way is what is called true deliverance. As for 'the afflictions, boundless, I vow to cut off,' this means using the wisdom of prajñā in your own self-nature to remove the mind of false and empty thought. As for 'the teachings, inexhaustible, I vow to learn,' you must see your own nature for yourself, and constantly practice the true Dharma — this is called true learning. As for 'the unsurpassed buddha-path, I vow to accomplish,' once you are constantly able to hold your mind low, and act with true rightness, free of both delusion and of awakening itself, prajñā constantly arises; removing both the true and the false alike, you thereby see buddha-nature, and the buddha-path is accomplished the very moment these words are spoken. To constantly hold this practice in mind is the Dharma of the power of vows."

Reading note — after Hanshan

Hanshan's distinction between 懺 ("repenting") and 悔 ("reforming") is exact rather than merely rhetorical: repenting addresses what has already happened, while reforming addresses what has not happened yet, and the two are not the same act performed twice. His image is a river — extinguishing a past transgression without also committing to prevent the next one is like managing a single wave while the current itself keeps flowing; "if the former transgressions are not yet extinguished, and further faults arise on top of them" describes exactly this failure, repentance repeated forever because reform was never actually undertaken.

On the four vows, Hanshan gives particular weight to the parenthetical aside most readers pass over quickly — "it is, after all, not Huineng who does the delivering." He reads this as forestalling a specific misunderstanding of the bodhisattva vow to deliver all sentient beings: not a promise that some other, more advanced being will rescue you, and not even a promise that Huineng himself will rescue his own listeners, but an instruction that the beings to be delivered are the deluded mental states arising within one's own mind, deliverable only by one's own right view. On this reading, every external-sounding vow in Mahāyāna liturgy — to deliver beings, to cut off afflictions, to learn every teaching, to accomplish buddhahood — is relocated to exactly the same inward site this whole chapter keeps returning to.

3. The Formless Threefold Refuge

Chinese Source

「善知識！今發四弘願了，更與善知識授無相三歸依戒。善知識！ 歸依覺，兩足尊。歸依正，離欲尊。歸依淨，眾中尊。從今日去，稱覺為師，更不歸依邪魔外道，以自性三寶常自證明，勸善知識歸依自性三寶。佛者，覺也。法者，正也。僧者，淨也。自心歸依覺，邪迷不生，少欲知足，能離財色，名兩足尊。自心歸依正，念念無邪見，以無邪見故，即無人我貢高，貪愛執著，名離欲尊。自心歸依淨，一切塵勞愛欲境界，自性皆不染著，名眾中尊。若修此行，是自歸依。凡夫不會，從日至夜受三歸戒。若言歸依佛，佛在何處？若不見佛，憑何所歸，言却成妄。善知識！各自觀察，莫錯用心。經文分明言自歸依佛，不言歸依他佛。自佛不歸，無所依處。今既自悟，各須歸依自心三寶，內調心性，外敬他人，是自歸依也。 「善知識！既歸依自三寶竟，各各志心，吾與說一體三身自性佛，令汝等見三身了然，自悟自性。總隨我道：『於自色身，歸依清淨法身佛。於自色身，歸依圓滿報身佛。於自色身，歸依千百億化身佛。』善知識！色身是舍宅，不可言歸。向者三身佛，在自性中，世人總有；為自心迷，不見內性。外覓三身如來，不見自身中有三身佛。汝等聽說，令汝等於自身中，見自性有三身佛。此三身佛，從自性生，不從外得。何名清淨法身佛？世人性本清淨，萬法從自性生。思量一切惡事，即生惡行；思量一切善事，即生善行。如是諸法在自性中，如天常清，日月常明，為浮雲蓋覆，上明下暗。忽遇風吹雲散，上下俱明，萬象皆現。世人性常浮游，如彼天雲。善知識！智如日，慧如月，智慧常明。於外著境，被妄念浮雲蓋覆自性，不得明朗。若遇善知識，聞真正法，自除迷妄，內外明徹，於自性中萬法皆現。見性之人，亦復如是。此名清淨法身佛。善知識！自心歸依自性，是歸依真佛。自歸依者，除却自性中不善心、嫉妬心、諂曲心、吾我心、誑妄心、輕人心、慢他心、邪見心、貢高心，及一切時中不善之行，常自見己過，不說他人好惡，是自歸依。常須下心，普行恭敬，即是見性通達，更無滯礙，是自歸依。

Ru-Yi Translation

"Good friends, having now made the four great vows, I will further confer upon you, good friends, the formless threefold refuge. Good friends: take refuge in awakening, most honored among those complete in both wisdom and practice; take refuge in rightness, most honored in being free of desire; take refuge in purity, most honored among the assembly. From this day forward, call awakening your teacher, and never again take refuge in demonic or heterodox ways; let the threefold treasure of your own self-nature constantly bear witness to this for yourself. I urge you, good friends, to take refuge in the threefold treasure of your own self-nature. 'Buddha' means awakening; 'Dharma' means rightness; 'Saṅgha' means purity. When your own mind takes refuge in awakening, wrong views and delusion do not arise; you have few desires and know contentment, and are able to leave behind wealth and sensuality — this is called most honored among those complete in both wisdom and practice. When your own mind takes refuge in rightness, thought after thought is free of wrong views; and because it is free of wrong views, there is no notion of self and other, no arrogance, no greedy attachment — this is called most honored in being free of desire. When your own mind takes refuge in purity, self-nature remains unstained by every worldly circumstance of desire and craving — this is called most honored among the assembly. To practice in this way is to take refuge in yourself. Ordinary people do not understand this, and from morning to night they receive the threefold refuge precepts, yet if you ask them, 'if you take refuge in the Buddha, where is that Buddha?' — if they cannot see the Buddha, what have they taken refuge in at all? Their words become empty and false. Good friends, each of you should examine this for yourself, and do not misapply your mind. The sūtra text states plainly, 'take refuge in the Buddha within yourself' — it does not say 'take refuge in some other buddha.' If you do not take refuge in the buddha that is yourself, you have nowhere at all to rely upon. Now that you have awakened for yourselves, each of you must take refuge in the threefold treasure of your own mind: governing your own mind and nature within, and showing respect to others outwardly — this is taking refuge in yourself. "Good friends, having now taken refuge in the threefold treasure of your own self, let each of you set your mind fully, and I will explain to you the single-substance, threefold-body buddha of self-nature, so that you may see the three bodies clearly, and awaken to your own self-nature for yourselves. Repeat entirely after me: 'Within my own physical body, I take refuge in the pure Dharma-body buddha. Within my own physical body, I take refuge in the complete reward-body buddha. Within my own physical body, I take refuge in the hundred-thousand-koṭi transformation-body buddha.' Good friends, the physical body is only a dwelling — one does not speak of taking refuge in it. Those three bodies of buddhahood just named exist within self-nature, and every person already possesses them; it is only because one's own mind is deluded that one does not see this inner nature. Seeking the three bodies of the Tathāgata outside oneself, one fails to see that one's own body already contains all three bodies of buddhahood. Listen now, so that each of you may see, within your own body, that your own self-nature already possesses the three bodies of buddhahood. These three bodies of buddhahood arise from self-nature itself; they are not obtained from outside. What is meant by the pure Dharma-body buddha? The nature of people in this world is fundamentally pure, and the ten thousand things arise from self-nature itself. Thinking of every kind of evil deed gives rise to evil conduct; thinking of every kind of good deed gives rise to good conduct. All such things exist within self-nature, just as the sky is constantly clear and the sun and moon are constantly bright, yet may be covered over by drifting clouds, so that above is bright while below is dark; but the moment a wind arises and scatters the clouds, above and below both become bright, and the ten thousand forms all appear. The nature of people in this world constantly drifts about in just this way, like those clouds in the sky. Good friends, wisdom is like the sun, understanding is like the moon, and wisdom and understanding are constantly bright; but when attached outwardly to circumstances, self-nature is covered over by the drifting clouds of false thought, and cannot become clear and bright. If one meets a good friend and hears the true and correct teaching, one removes this delusion and falseness for oneself, becomes clear and bright within and without, and the ten thousand things all appear within self-nature. One who has seen their own nature is exactly the same. This is called the pure Dharma-body buddha. "Good friends, for your own mind to take refuge in your own self-nature is to take refuge in the true buddha. To take refuge in yourself is to remove, from within self-nature, the unwholesome mind, the envious mind, the mind of flattery and crookedness, the mind of self and other, the mind of falsehood and deceit, the mind that looks down on others, the mind of arrogance toward others, the mind of wrong views, the mind of pride — and every kind of unwholesome conduct at every moment; to constantly see your own faults for yourself, and not speak of others' good or bad qualities — this is taking refuge in yourself. You must constantly hold your mind low, and practice respect toward everyone universally — this is seeing your own nature and penetrating it fully, with no further obstruction at all — this too is taking refuge in yourself."

Reading note — after Hanshan

Hanshan reads the challenge Huineng poses to conventional refuge-taking — "if you take refuge in the Buddha, where is that Buddha? If you cannot see the Buddha, what have you taken refuge in at all?" — as aimed at a specific and common failure mode: people who receive the threefold refuge as a rite performed correctly, "from morning to night," without the ceremony ever connecting to anything they could point to in their own experience. His gloss on Buddha/Dharma/Saṅgha as awakening/rightness/purity treats the three as one thing under three names, the same pattern already established for samādhi and prajñā two chapters earlier — not three different objects of devotion, but three descriptions of a single self-nature already present in the listener.

On the threefold body, Hanshan stresses that nothing here is being newly acquired through practice — the Dharma-body, reward-body, and transformation-body are described as "already possessed by every person," obscured rather than absent, the way sunlight is obscured by cloud rather than extinguished by it. This is why the sky-and-cloud image recurs here in nearly the same form used for prajñā earlier in the sūtra: Hanshan reads the repetition as deliberate, since both passages are making the identical claim from different doctrinal angles — practice does not construct buddhahood, it only clears what was already covering it.

4. The Reward-Body, the Transformation-Body, and a Verse on True Repentance

Chinese Source

何名圓滿報身？譬如一燈能除千年闇，一智能滅萬年愚。莫思向前，已過不可得；常思於後，念念圓明，自見本性。善惡雖殊，本性無二，無二之性，名為實性。於實性中，不染善惡，此名圓滿報身佛。自性起一念惡，滅萬劫善因；自性起一念善，得恒沙惡盡。 直至無上菩提，念念自見，不失本念，名為報身。何名千百億化身？若不思萬法，性本如空，一念思量，名為變化。思量惡事，化為地獄；思量善事，化為天堂。毒害化為龍蛇，慈悲化為菩薩，智慧化為上界，愚癡化為下方。自性變化甚多，迷人不能省覺，念念起惡，常行惡道。迴一念善，智慧即生，此名自性化身佛。善知識！法身本具，念念自性自見，即是報身佛。從報身思量，即是化身佛。自悟自修自性功德，是真歸依。皮肉是色身，色身是舍宅，不言歸依也。但悟自性三身，即識自性佛。吾有一無相頌，若能誦持，言下令汝積劫迷罪一時銷滅。頌曰： 「迷人修福不修道，　只言修福便是道， 布施供養福無邊，　心中三惡元來造。 擬將修福欲滅罪，　後世得福罪還在， 但向心中除罪緣，　名自性中真懺悔。 忽悟大乘真懺悔，　除邪行正即無罪， 學道常於自性觀，　即與諸佛同一類。 吾祖惟傳此頓法，　普願見性同一體， 若欲當來覓法身，　離諸法相心中洗。 努力自見莫悠悠，　後念忽絕一世休， 若悟大乘得見性，　虔恭合掌至心求。」 師言：「善知識！總須誦取，依此修行，言下見性。雖去吾千里，如常在吾邊。於此言下不悟，即對面千里，何勤遠來。珍重！好去。」 一眾聞法，靡不開悟，歡喜奉行。

Ru-Yi Translation

"What is meant by the complete reward-body? It is like a single lamp able to dispel a thousand years of darkness, a single moment of wisdom able to extinguish ten thousand years of folly. Do not think of what lies ahead of you — what has already passed cannot be recovered; constantly think of what comes after, so that thought after thought remains fully bright, and you see your own fundamental nature for yourself. Good and evil may differ, but fundamental nature admits no duality; this nature without duality is called the real nature. Within this real nature, one is stained by neither good nor evil — this is called the complete reward-body buddha. Should self-nature give rise to a single evil thought, ten thousand kalpas of the causes of goodness are destroyed; should self-nature give rise to a single good thought, evils as numerous as the sands of the Ganges are entirely exhausted. Going straight to unsurpassed bodhi, seeing yourself in thought after thought, never losing your own fundamental thought — this is called the reward-body. "What is meant by the hundred-thousand-koṭi transformation-body? When you do not think of the ten thousand things at all, your nature is fundamentally like empty space; the moment a single thought of consideration arises, this is called transformation. Thinking of an evil deed transforms into hell; thinking of a good deed transforms into the heavens. Malice and harm transform into dragons and serpents; compassion transforms into bodhisattvas; wisdom transforms into the higher realms; folly transforms into the lower ones. Self-nature's transformations are exceedingly many, and the deluded are unable to notice or awaken to them; thought after thought giving rise to evil, they constantly walk the evil paths. Turn back a single thought toward good, and wisdom arises at once — this is called the transformation-body buddha of self-nature. "Good friends, the Dharma-body is fundamentally already complete; seeing your own self-nature for yourself, thought after thought, is itself the reward-body. Thinking further from the reward-body is itself the transformation-body. To awaken by yourself, cultivate by yourself, and realize the merit of your own self-nature — this is true refuge. The skin and flesh are the physical body; the physical body is only a dwelling, and one does not speak of taking refuge in it. Simply awaken to the three bodies of your own self-nature, and you thereby recognize the buddha of your own self-nature. I have a Markless Verse — if you are able to recite and uphold it, it will cause the accumulated, deluded transgressions of many kalpas to be extinguished for you all at once, the very moment these words are spoken. The verse says: "The deluded cultivate blessings but do not cultivate the Way, and say only that cultivating blessings is itself the Way; giving alms and making offerings brings blessings without limit, yet the three evils within the mind were, from the start, already being made. If you intend to cultivate blessings in order to extinguish your offenses, then in ages to come you will gain blessings while the offenses still remain; simply remove, from within your own mind, the very causes of offense — this alone, within each one's own self-nature, is true repentance. Suddenly awaken to the true repentance of the Great Vehicle: cast out what is wrong, practice what is right, and you are thereby without offense at all; a student of the Way who constantly contemplates within their own self-nature is thereby of the very same kind as every buddha. Our patriarchs have transmitted only this sudden Dharma, wishing only that all may see their own nature and become one single body with it; if you wish, in time to come, to seek out the Dharma-body, wash it clean within your own mind, apart from every mark of every thing. Strive to see this for yourself, and do not drift on carelessly — the moment your next thought is suddenly cut off, this entire life is over. If you awaken to the Great Vehicle and attain the seeing of your own nature, join your palms with reverence and seek it with a wholly sincere mind." The Master said, "Good friends, you must all recite and uphold this verse, and practice according to it. The moment you see your own nature at these very words, though you may be a thousand li from me, it will be as though you are constantly at my side. But if, at these very words, you fail to awaken, then even standing face to face, we are still a thousand li apart — what use was it, then, to have troubled yourself to come so far? Take good care of yourselves, and go well." Hearing this teaching, the whole assembly, every one of them awakened, received it with joy, and upheld it in practice.

Reading note — after Hanshan

Hanshan reads "do not think of what lies ahead of you — what has already passed cannot be recovered" not as a blanket instruction to forget the past, but as a specific redirection of effort toward the one place agency still exists: the next thought. Dwelling on a past that cannot be altered spends attention where it can do nothing; the reward-body, on his account, is built entirely out of what is done with the thought that is actually still arising, "thought after thought remaining fully bright," never the thought already gone.

Hanshan reads the closing verse's opening lines — the deluded who "cultivate blessings but do not cultivate the Way," giving alms without end while "the three evils within the mind were, from the start, already being made" — as a direct callback to Emperor Wu three chapters earlier, without naming him again. Wu's temple-building and alms-giving are the case history the verse's first two lines describe in the abstract, and Hanshan reads the whole verse as this chapter's own return to a claim first made there: that the distance between blessings and true repentance is not one of degree, more giving eventually adding up to merit, but one of kind — the mind's own causes of offense are never touched by any quantity of external offering at all, and only "true repentance" reaching those causes directly closes what alms-giving alone leaves open.
