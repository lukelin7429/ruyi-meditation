Fascicle 2

The three deeds of body, the four of speech, the six faculties《慈悲水懺法》卷中

Where Fascicle 1 confessed the mental poisons themselves, this fascicle confesses what those poisons actually produce: killing, theft, and sexual misconduct in the body; harsh, false, flowery, and divisive speech; and the faults committed through each of the six sense-faculties in turn — opened by a short doctrine of karma meant to answer the oldest complaint against it, that good people suffer and bad people prosper.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1910 Obstruction 2 of 3: Karma (業障) Status: complete
Chinese
《慈悲水懺法》卷中 — the second of three fascicles of the Water Repentance.
Covers
Four contemplations that prepare the ground for this fascicle's confession; a short doctrine of the three timings of karmic ripening; and the confession proper, moving through the three bodily deeds (killing, theft, sexual misconduct), the four verbal deeds (harsh speech, false speech, flowery speech, divisive speech), and the six sense-faculties in turn.
Genre note
Continuous liturgical prose meant to be recited, not narrative. This translation divides it into seven numbered sections for readability; the original carries no internal headings.
Content note
The confession of killing in Section 3 describes methods of hunting, trapping, and slaughter in specific, unsparing detail, in service of the text's own argument against eating meat. Nothing here is gratuitous to the original — the translation neither softens nor sharpens what the Chinese says.
Difficulty
★★☆☆☆ — more concrete than Fascicle 1, since it confesses specific deeds rather than abstract categories of affliction, though the lists of particular offenses within each deed are still extensive.

1. The Four Contemplations四種觀行

Chinese Source

一切諸佛愍念眾生，為說水懺道場總法，今當歸命一切諸佛。 南無毘盧遮那佛　南無本師釋迦牟尼佛　南無阿彌陀佛　南無彌勒佛　南無龍種上尊王佛　南無龍自在王佛　南無寶勝佛　南無覺華定自在王佛　南無袈裟幢佛　南無師子吼佛　南無文殊師利菩薩　南無普賢菩薩　南無大勢至菩薩　南無地藏菩薩　南無大莊嚴菩薩　南無觀自在菩薩 禮諸佛已，次復懺悔。某甲等相與，即今身心寂靜、無諂無障，正是生善滅惡之時。復應各起四種觀行，以為滅罪作前方便。何等為四？一者觀於因緣、二者觀於果報、三者觀我自身、四者觀如來身。第一觀因緣者，知我此罪藉以無明不善思惟，無正觀力不識其過，遠離善友諸佛菩薩，隨逐魔道行邪險徑，如魚吞鉤不知其患、如蠶作繭自纏自縛、如蛾赴火自燒自爛，以是因緣不能自出。第二觀於果報者，所有諸惡不善之業，三世輪轉苦果無窮，沈溺無邊巨夜大海，為諸煩惱羅剎所食，未來生死冥然無涯。設使報得轉輪聖王王四天下，飛行自在七寶具足，命終之後不免惡趣。四空果報、三界極尊，福盡還作牛領中蟲。況復其餘無福德者，而復懈怠不勤懺悔，此亦譬如抱石沈淵求出應難。第三觀我自身，雖有正因靈覺之性，而為煩惱黑暗叢林之所覆蔽，無了因力不能得顯。我今應當發起勝心，破裂無明顛倒重障，斷滅生死虛偽苦因，顯發如來大明覺慧，建立無上涅槃妙果。第四觀如來身，無為寂照，離四句、絕百非，眾德具足湛然常住，雖復方便入於滅度，慈悲救接未曾暫捨。生如是心，可謂滅罪之良津、除障之要行，是故志誠求哀懺悔。某甲等無始以來至于今日，長養煩惱日深日厚日滋日茂，覆蓋慧眼令無所見，斷除眾善不得相續，起障不得見佛、不聞正法、不值聖僧煩惱，起障不見過去未來一切善惡業行、出離煩惱，障受人天尊貴之煩惱，障生色無色界禪定福樂之煩惱，障不得自在神通飛騰隱顯遍至十方諸佛淨土聽法之煩惱，障學安那般那、數息、不淨、因緣觀等諸煩惱，障學煖、頂、忍、第一法、七方便等諸煩惱，障學慈、悲、喜、捨、聞、思、修等煩惱，障學空平等中道解三觀義煩惱，障學助道品念處、正勤、根、力、如意足諸煩惱，障學八正道示相之煩惱，障學七覺支不示相煩惱，障學八解脫、九空定煩惱，障學於十智三三昧煩惱，障學三明、六通、四無礙煩惱，障學六度、四等煩惱，障學四攝法廣化之煩惱，障學大乘心四弘誓願煩惱，障學十明、十行之煩惱，障學十回向、十願之煩惱，障學初地、二地、三地、四地明解之煩惱，障學五地、六地、七地諸知見煩惱，障學八地、九地、十地雙照之煩惱，如是乃至障學佛果百萬阿僧祇諸行之煩惱。如是行障無量無邊，弟子某甲今日至禱稽懇，向十方佛尊法聖眾慚愧懺悔願皆消滅。願藉此懺悔障於諸行一切煩惱所生功德，願在在處處自在受生，不為結集業行之所迴轉，以如意通於一念頃遍至十方淨諸佛土攝化眾生，於諸禪定甚深境界及諸知見通達無礙，心能普周一切諸法樂說無窮而不染著，得心自在、得法自在、方便自在，令此煩惱及無知結習畢竟永斷不復相續，無漏聖道朗然如日。發願已，歸命禮諸佛。

Ru-Yi Translation

All the Buddhas, mindful of living beings with compassion, have spoken this comprehensive rite of the Water Repentance. We shall now take refuge in all the Buddhas. Homage to the Buddha Vairocana. Homage to our fundamental teacher, the Buddha Śākyamuni. Homage to the Buddha Amitābha. Homage to the Buddha Maitreya. Homage to the Buddha King of the Dragon Race, Most Honored. Homage to the Buddha King of Sovereign Dragons. Homage to the Buddha Ratnaśrī. Homage to the Buddha King Ever Still in the Samādhi of the Awakening-Flower. Homage to the Buddha Banner of the Kāṣāya Robe. Homage to the Buddha Lion's Roar. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mañjuśrī. Homage to the Bodhisattva Samantabhadra. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta. Homage to the Bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Great Adornment. Homage to the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. Having paid homage to the Buddhas, we next continue with confession. We disciples, together, our bodies and minds now still, free of flattery and free of obstruction, stand exactly at the moment for generating good and extinguishing evil. We should each further arouse four kinds of contemplative practice, as the preliminary skillful means for extinguishing fault. What are the four? First, contemplating causes and conditions; second, contemplating the fruit of retribution; third, contemplating one's own self; and fourth, contemplating the body of the Tathāgata. First, contemplating causes and conditions: know that this fault of mine relies on ignorance and unwholesome thinking; lacking the power of right contemplation, I fail to recognize its fault, and I stray far from good companions, the Buddhas and bodhisattvas, following instead the path of Māra and walking a wrong and perilous road — like a fish swallowing a hook, unaware of the harm in it; like a silkworm spinning its cocoon, binding and entangling itself; like a moth flying into a flame, burning and destroying itself — and because of these causes and conditions, I am unable to free myself. Second, contemplating the fruit of retribution: every evil and unwholesome karma turns through the three periods of time, its bitter fruit without end, drowning one in the boundless great ocean of a vast night, devoured by the rākṣasa-demons of affliction, the future cycle of birth and death stretching on dark and unbounded. Even supposing one gained the retribution of a wheel-turning sage-king, ruling the four continents, flying freely through the air, endowed with all seven treasures — once that life ends, one still cannot escape the evil destinies. Even the retribution of the four formless heavens, the very summit of the three realms, once its merit is exhausted, one returns to being a maggot in the neck of an ox. How much more so, then, for all the rest who have no such merit, and who still remain idle and unwilling to confess diligently — this too is like a man who embraces a stone and sinks into a deep pool, and then seeks to climb back out: it can hardly be done. Third, contemplating my own self: although I possess the true cause, the nature of numinous awareness, it lies covered over by the dark forest-thicket of affliction, and lacking the power of the revealing cause, it cannot come to manifest. I should now arouse a superior mind, breaking apart the heavy obstruction of ignorance and inverted thinking, cutting off the false and empty causes of suffering in birth and death, bringing forth the Tathāgata's great, bright awakened wisdom, and establishing the unsurpassed, wondrous fruit of nirvāṇa. Fourth, contemplating the body of the Tathāgata: unconditioned, still, and illuminating, free of the four propositions and cut off from the hundred negations, complete with every virtue, serene and ever-abiding — though, as a skillful means, he enters into cessation, his compassion in saving and receiving beings has never for a moment been set aside. To give rise to a mind such as this may truly be called the good ford for extinguishing fault, the essential practice for removing obstruction. For this reason, with utmost sincerity, we beg for mercy in confession. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, have cultivated affliction until it has grown deeper and thicker, richer and more luxuriant day by day, covering over the eye of wisdom so that it sees nothing at all, cutting off every wholesome quality before it can continue — giving rise to the affliction that obstructs seeing the Buddha, hearing the true Dharma, and meeting the noble Saṅgha; the affliction that obstructs seeing every past and future good and evil karmic deed, and obstructs release; the affliction that obstructs receiving honor and nobility among humans and gods; the affliction that obstructs rebirth into the meditative bliss of the form and formless realms; the affliction that obstructs the free and unhindered supernormal power to fly, to appear and vanish at will, and to travel throughout the ten directions to the pure lands of every Buddha to hear the Dharma; the afflictions that obstruct learning mindfulness of the breath, breath-counting, the contemplation of impurity, and the contemplation of dependent origination; the afflictions that obstruct learning the stages of heat, summit, forbearance, and supreme worldly dharma, and the seven expedient stages; the afflictions that obstruct learning loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, equanimity, hearing, reflection, and cultivation; the affliction that obstructs learning the meaning of the three contemplations that resolve emptiness, equality, and the middle way; the afflictions that obstruct learning the auxiliary factors of the path — the foundations of mindfulness, right effort, the faculties, the powers, and the bases of supernormal power; the affliction that obstructs learning the eightfold noble path with its manifest marks; the affliction that obstructs learning the seven factors of awakening without manifest marks; the affliction that obstructs learning the eight liberations and the nine progressive absorptions of emptiness; the affliction that obstructs learning the ten kinds of knowledge and the three samādhis; the affliction that obstructs learning the three insights, the six supernormal powers, and the four unhindered eloquences; the affliction that obstructs learning the six perfections and the four immeasurables; the affliction that obstructs learning the four means of gathering beings for their broad transformation; the affliction that obstructs learning the Great Vehicle mind and the four universal vows; the affliction that obstructs learning the ten illuminations and the ten practices; the affliction that obstructs learning the ten dedications and the ten vows; the affliction that obstructs the clear understanding of the first, second, third, and fourth grounds; the afflictions that obstruct the various knowledge and views of the fifth, sixth, and seventh grounds; the affliction that obstructs the twofold illumination of the eighth, ninth, and tenth grounds — extending even to the affliction that obstructs the million incalculable eons of practice that lead to the fruit of buddhahood itself. Obstructions to practice of this kind are immeasurable and boundless. We disciples, today, implore and entreat most earnestly, and before the Buddhas, the venerable Dharma, and the noble assembly of the ten directions, in shame we confess them, praying that they all be dissolved. We pray that, by the merit born of confessing every affliction that obstructs these practices, we may be reborn freely wherever we will, no longer turned about by the accumulated force of karma; that with the power of wish-fulfillment we may, in the space of a single thought, reach throughout the ten directions to purify the buddha-lands and gather in living beings for their transformation; that in the profound states of meditative concentration and in every form of knowledge and vision we may pass through without hindrance; that the mind may pervade all phenomena everywhere, delighting to teach without end and yet remaining unstained; that we may attain mastery of mind, mastery of the Dharma, and mastery of skillful means; and that this affliction, together with the accumulated habits of ignorance, may be cut off once and for all, never to continue again, so that the undefiled noble path shines forth clear as the sun. Having made this vow, we take refuge in and pay homage to the Buddhas.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The long list of "obstructed practices" in the second half of this section runs through what amounts to a compressed map of the entire Mahāyāna path — the four wholesome roots, the seven factors of awakening, the ten bodhisattva grounds, and dozens of other fixed sets, each named only in passing as one more thing affliction blocks. This is not a teaching passage explaining any of these systems; it assumes a listener who already recognizes them, and its rhetorical work is cumulative rather than explanatory — the sheer length of the list is the argument, showing that there is no rung of the path affliction fails to reach. Readers wanting the content behind any single term will find it treated at length elsewhere in this site's Yogācāra and Abhidharma-adjacent translations.

2. Understanding Karma, and a General Confession明業、總懺

Chinese Source

禮諸佛已，次復懺悔。某甲等略懺煩惱障竟，今當次第懺悔業障。夫業者，能莊飾世趣，在在處處不復思惟求離世解脫。所以六道果報種種不同形類各異，當知皆是業力所作。佛十力中業力甚深，凡夫之人多於此中好起疑惑。何以故爾？現見世間行善之人觸向轗軻，為惡之者是事諧偶，謂言天下善惡無分。如此計者，皆是不能深達業理。何以故爾？經中說言：有三種業。何等為三？一者現報、二者生報、三者後報。現報業者，現在作惡、現身受報。生報業者，此生作善作惡、來生受報。後報業者，或是過去無量生中作善作惡於此生中受，或在未來無量生中方受其報。若今行惡之人現在見好者，此是過去生報、後報善業熟故，所以現在有此樂果，豈關現在作諸惡業而得好報？若今行善之人現在縈苦者，此是過去生報、後報惡業熟故，現在善根力弱不能排遣，是故得此苦報，豈關現在作善而招惡報？何以知然？現見世間為善之者，人所讚歎、人所尊重，故知未來必招樂果。過去既有如此惡業，所以諸佛菩薩教令親近善友共行懺悔。善知識者於得道中則為全利，是故今日至誠歸依於佛。某甲等無始以來至于今日，積惡如恒沙、造罪滿大地，捨身與受身，不覺亦不知。或作五逆深厚濁纏無間罪業，或造一闡提斷善根業、輕誣佛語謗方等業、破滅三寶毀正法業、不信罪福起十惡業、迷真反正癡惑之業、不孝二親反戾之業、輕慢師長無禮敬業、朋友不信無義之業，或作四重八重障聖道業、毀犯五戒破八齋業、五篇七聚多缺犯業、優婆塞戒輕重垢業，或菩薩戒不能清淨如說行業、前後方便污梵行業、月無六齋懈怠之業、年三長齋不常修業、三千威儀不如法業、八萬律儀微細罪業、不修身戒心慧之業、春秋八王造眾罪業、行十六種惡律儀業、於諸眾生無愍傷業、不矜不念無憐愍業、不拔不濟無救護業、心懷嫉妬無度彼業、於怨親境不平等業、耽荒五欲不厭離業，或因衣食園林池沼生蕩逸業、或以盛年放恣情欲造眾罪業、或作有漏善迴向三有障出世業。如是等罪無量無邊，今日發露向十方佛尊法聖眾皆悉懺悔。願某甲等承是懺悔，無明等罪、諸不善業願皆消滅，所生福善，願生生世世滅五逆罪、除闡提惑。如是輕重諸罪惡業從今已去，乃至道場誓不更犯，常習出世清淨善法，精持律行守護威儀，如渡海者愛惜浮囊，六度四等常標行首，戒定慧品轉得增明，速成如來三十二相、八十種好、十力、無畏、大悲三念、常樂妙智八自在我。歸依諸佛，願垂護念。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having paid homage to the Buddhas, we next continue with confession. We disciples have now briefly confessed the obstruction of affliction; we shall next, in order, confess the obstruction of karma. Now, karma is able to adorn and shape one's destiny in this world, so that everywhere, in every place, one no longer thinks to seek liberation from the world. This is why the fruits of retribution across the six destinies differ so greatly in kind and form — know that all of this is the work of the power of karma. Among the ten powers of the Buddha, the power of karma is especially profound, and ordinary people most often give rise to doubt on this very point. Why is this so? Because it is seen in this world that those who do good meet with misfortune at every turn, while those who do evil find their affairs going smoothly — and so people say that in this world good and evil bring no distinction at all. Such reckoning, in every case, comes from failing to penetrate deeply into the principle of karma. Why is this so? The sūtra says there are three kinds of karma. What are the three? First, retribution in the present life; second, retribution in the next life; third, retribution in a later life. Karma with retribution in the present life is this: evil committed now is met with retribution in this very body, now. Karma with retribution in the next life is this: good or evil committed in this life is met with retribution in the life to come. Karma with retribution in a later life is this: good or evil committed across immeasurable past lives is received in this present life, or good or evil committed now will only be received across immeasurable future lives to come. If a person doing evil now appears to prosper in the present, this is because good karma from a past life or a later-ripening life has come to fruition, and so he enjoys this pleasant fruit now — how could this have anything to do with the evil deeds committed now bringing a good reward? If a person doing good now finds himself bound up in suffering in the present, this is because evil karma from a past life or a later-ripening life has come to fruition, and the power of his present wholesome roots is too weak to dispel it, and so he receives this bitter retribution — how could this have anything to do with the good committed now inviting an evil result? How do we know this is so? It is seen in this world that those who do good are praised by others and honored by others — and from this we know that the future must bring them a pleasant fruit. Since such evil karma already exists from the past, the Buddhas and bodhisattvas teach us to draw close to good companions and practice confession together. A good spiritual friend is of complete benefit on the path to attaining the Way — and for this reason, today, with utmost sincerity, we take refuge in the Buddha. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, have accumulated evil as numerous as the sands of the river, and committed faults that fill the great earth, discarding one body and taking up another, without ever perceiving or knowing it. Whether committing the five heinous crimes, deeply mired in defilement without interruption; or the karma of an icchantika, cutting off the roots of goodness; or the karma of slighting and slandering the Buddha's word, defaming the Vaipulya teachings; or the karma of destroying the Three Jewels and injuring the true Dharma; or the karma of the ten evils, disbelieving in fault and merit; or the karma of confusion and delusion, mistaking the true for the false; or the karma of rebellion, being unfilial to one's two parents; or the karma of discourtesy, slighting one's teachers and elders; or the karma of disloyalty, being untrustworthy to one's friends; or committing the karma that obstructs the noble path through the four grave or eight grave offenses; or the karma of violating the five precepts and breaking the eight precepts of the observance day; or the karma of many transgressions among the five categories and seven groups of monastic offenses; or the karma of light and grave defilements of the lay precepts; or, unable to keep the bodhisattva precepts pure and act as taught, the karma of defiling pure conduct through improper skillful means before and after; or the karma of idleness in neglecting the six observance days each month; or the karma of failing to keep regularly the three long fasting periods of the year; or the karma of the three thousand dignified observances not kept according to the Dharma; or the karma of the eighty thousand subtle faults against monastic discipline; or the karma of failing to cultivate the body, precepts, mind, and wisdom; or the karma of committing every fault during the eight seasonal turning-points of spring and autumn; or the karma of practicing the sixteen kinds of evil observance; or the karma of showing no compassion or grief toward living beings; or the karma of withholding sympathy and pity, without compassion; or the karma of failing to rescue or relieve, without offering aid; or the karma of harboring jealousy without measure toward others; or the karma of failing to treat enemy and friend with impartiality; or the karma of indulging without restraint in the five desires, unwilling to turn away from them; or the karma of dissipation arising from clothing, food, gardens, and ponds; or the karma of committing every fault by giving free rein to passion and desire in the prime of life; or the karma of dedicating tainted wholesome deeds back toward the three realms of existence, thereby obstructing release from the world — faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless. Today we lay them bare, and before the Buddhas, the venerable Dharma, and the noble assembly of the ten directions, we confess them all. We pray that, by this confession, we disciples may see every fault of ignorance and every unwholesome karma entirely dissolved, and that the merit and goodness thereby produced may, in life after life, extinguish the fault of the five heinous crimes and remove the delusion of the icchantika. From this day forward, as for every such fault and evil karma, light or grave, we vow, even to the very seat of this rite, never to commit them again; we shall constantly practice the pure and wholesome dharmas that lead beyond the world, diligently keep the discipline, and guard our dignified conduct, cherishing it as a man crossing the sea cherishes his life-raft; we shall always hold the six perfections and the four immeasurables as the very forefront of our practice, so that the qualities of precepts, concentration, and wisdom grow ever brighter, and swiftly accomplish the Tathāgata's thirty-two marks, eighty minor characteristics, ten powers, fearlessnesses, the three foundations of mindfulness of great compassion, and the eight kinds of sovereign mastery of eternal bliss, wondrous wisdom, and true self. We take refuge in the Buddhas, and pray that they extend their protective mindfulness over us.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This section's three-timings doctrine is a direct answer to the oldest complaint against karma theory: that it plainly does not match observed experience, since good people visibly suffer and bad people visibly prosper. Rather than deny the observation, the text absorbs it — the visible mismatch is explained by karma ripening on a longer clock than a single lifetime, so that present fortune or misfortune may reflect a harvest sown long before the sower can remember planting it. Whether or not one finds this answer persuasive, it is worth recognizing as a real argument addressed to a real objection, not merely an assertion repeated past the difficulty.

3. Confessing the Killing of Life懺悔殺業

Chinese Source

某甲等前已總相懺悔一切諸業，今當次第更復一一別相懺悔。若總若別、若麁若細，若輕若重、若說不說，品類相從願皆消滅。別相懺者，先懺身三、次懺口四，其餘諸障次第稽顙。身三業者，第一殺害。如經所明恕己可為喻。勿殺勿行杖，雖復禽獸之殊，保命畏死其事是一。若尋此眾生，無始以來或是我父母兄弟六親眷屬，以業因緣輪迴六道，出生入死改形易報不復相識，而今興害食噉其肉，傷慈之甚。是故佛言：設得餘食，當如饑世食子肉想，何況食噉此魚肉耶？又言：為利殺眾生，以財網諸肉，二俱是惡業，死墮號叫獄。故知殺害及以食噉，罪深河海、過重丘嶽。然某甲等無始以來，不遇善友皆為此業。是故經言：殺害之罪，能令眾生墮於地獄、餓鬼受苦；若在畜生則受虎豹豺狼鷹鷂等身、或受毒蛇蝮蠍等身，常懷惡心；或受麞鹿熊羆等身，常懷恐怖。若生人中，得二種果報：一者多病、二者短命。殺害食噉既有如是無量種種諸惡果報，是故至誠求哀懺悔。某甲等自從無始以來至于今日，有此心識常懷慘毒無慈愍心，或因貪起殺，因瞋因癡及以慢殺，或興惡方便誓殺願殺及以呪殺，或破決湖池、焚燒山野、畋獵漁捕，或因風放火、飛鷹放犬，惱害一切。如是等罪今悉懺悔。或以檻弶坑撥扠戟弓弩彈射飛鳥走獸之類、或以罟網罾釣撩漉水性魚鼈黿鼉蝦蜆螺蚌濕居之屬，使水陸空行藏竄無地。或畜養鷄猪牛羊犬豕鵝鴨之屬自供庖厨，或貨他宰殺。使其哀聲未盡毛羽脫落、鱗甲傷毀身首分離、骨肉消碎剝裂屠割，炮燒煮炙楚毒酸切，橫加無辜，但使一時之快，口得味甚寡，不過三寸舌根而已，然其罪報殃累永劫。如是等罪，今日至誠皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，或復興師相伐疆場交爭，兩陣相向更相殺害。或自殺、教殺、聞殺歡喜，或習屠儈賃為刑戮，烹宰他命行於不忍，或恣暴怒揮戈舞刃或斬或刺、或推著坑塹、或以水沈溺、或塞穴壞巢、或土石磓磹、或以車馬雷轢踐踏一切眾生。如是等罪無量無邊，今日發露皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，或墮胎破卵、毒藥蠱道傷殺眾生。墾土掘地種植田園養蠶煮繭，傷殺滋甚。或打撲蚊蚋掐嚙蚤虱、或燒除糞掃開決溝渠，枉害一切。或噉果實、或用穀米、或用菜茹橫殺眾生，或然樵薪、或露燈燭燒諸蟲類，或取醬醋不先搖動、或瀉湯水澆殺蟲蟻。如是乃至行住坐臥四威儀中，恒常傷殺飛空著地微細眾生，凡夫識暗不覺不知，今日發露皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，或以鞭杖枷鎖桁械壓拉拷掠打擲、手脚蹴踏拘縛籠繫、斷絕水穀，如是種種諸惡方便苦惱眾生，今日至誠向十方佛尊法聖眾皆悉懺悔。願承是懺悔殺害等罪所生功德，生生世世得金剛身，壽命無窮，永離怨憎無殺害想。於諸眾生得一子地，若見危難急厄之者，不惜身命方便救脫，然後為說微妙正法，使諸眾生覩形見影皆蒙安樂，聞名聽聲恐怖悉除。我今稽顙歸依於佛。 南無毘盧遮那佛　南無本師釋迦牟尼佛　南無阿彌陀佛　南無彌勒佛　南無龍種上尊王佛　南無龍自在王佛　南無寶勝佛　南無覺華定自在王佛　南無袈裟幢佛　南無師子吼佛　南無文殊師利菩薩　南無普賢菩薩　南無大勢至菩薩　南無地藏菩薩　南無大莊嚴菩薩　南無觀自在菩薩

Ru-Yi Translation

We disciples have already, in general terms, confessed every karma; we shall now, one by one, in turn, confess each of them individually. Whether general or particular, whether coarse or subtle, whether light or grave, whether stated or unstated, may every category of fault, following in its kind, be entirely dissolved. As for confessing them individually: we first confess the three deeds of the body, next confess the four of speech, and then, in turn, bow our heads to the ground over the remaining obstructions. As for the three bodily deeds, the first is killing. As the sūtra makes clear, one may take one's own case as the standard for judging: do not kill, do not raise the rod against another — for although beasts and birds differ from us in kind, in cherishing life and fearing death, the matter is one and the same. If we trace back these living beings, since beginningless time, they may well have been our own parents, brothers, or kin in the six degrees of relation, who, through the causes and conditions of karma, have turned through the six destinies, passing out of one birth and into another death, changing form and changing retribution until we no longer recognize one another — and now we raise up harm against them and feed on their flesh: this is an injury to compassion of the gravest kind. This is why the Buddha said: even should you have other food, you should regard it as though, in a famine, you were eating your own child's flesh — how much more so, then, the flesh of fish that you eat and devour? And he said further: to kill living beings for profit, to net their flesh for money — both of these are evil karma, and one falls, upon death, into the hell of wailing and screaming. Hence we know that the fault of killing and of eating flesh is deeper than river or sea, heavier than hill or mountain. Yet we disciples, since beginningless time, not having encountered good companions, have all committed this karma. This is why the sūtra says: the fault of killing can cause living beings to fall into the hells and among the hungry ghosts, to suffer there; if born among animals, they receive the bodies of tigers, leopards, jackals, wolves, hawks, and falcons, or the bodies of venomous snakes, vipers, and scorpions, forever harboring a vicious heart; or they receive the bodies of river-deer, deer, bears, and brown bears, forever harboring terror. If born among humans, they receive two kinds of retribution: first, much illness; second, a short life. Since killing and the eating of flesh bring such immeasurable and various evil retributions, we therefore, with utmost sincerity, beg for mercy in confession. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, have carried this same consciousness, forever harboring cruelty and poison, without a compassionate heart — whether killing out of greed, or out of hatred, or out of delusion, or killing out of arrogance; or devising evil schemes, vowing to kill, wishing to kill, or killing by curse; or breaching lakes and ponds, burning mountains and wilds, hunting and fishing; or setting fires with the wind, releasing hawks and hounds — bringing harm and vexation upon every creature. Faults of this kind we now confess entirely. Or using cages, snares, pit-traps, forked spears, halberds, bows, crossbows, and pellet-bows to shoot flying birds and running beasts of every kind; or using nets, weirs, and hooks to trawl and drain the water for fish, turtles, soft-shelled turtles, alligators, shrimp, clams, snails, and mussels, and all creatures that dwell in wetness, leaving no place of refuge for any creature of water, land, or air to hide. Or raising chickens, pigs, cattle, sheep, dogs, boars, geese, and ducks to supply one's own kitchen, or selling them to others for slaughter — so that before their cries of anguish have even ceased, their feathers and fur are stripped away, their scales and shells broken, their bodies severed from their heads, their bones and flesh crushed, flayed, and butchered, roasted, boiled, steamed, and broiled in bitter torment — inflicted wrongfully upon the innocent, all merely for a moment's pleasure, the taste gained being so slight, no more than the three inches of the tongue's own root — and yet the retribution for this fault lingers on for endless kalpas. Faults of this kind, today, with utmost sincerity, we confess entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, whether raising armies to attack one another, contending over borders, two armies facing each other and slaughtering one another; or killing with our own hand, or ordering another to kill, or rejoicing on hearing that a killing has taken place; or practicing the butcher's or broker's trade, hired out for execution, cooking and slaughtering another's life without mercy; or, giving free rein to violent rage, brandishing spear and blade, cutting or stabbing, or shoving another into a pit or ditch, or drowning another in water, or blocking up a den or destroying a nest, or crushing with earth and stone, or trampling every living being beneath cart and horse — faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless. Today we lay them bare and confess them entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, whether aborting the unborn or breaking eggs, harming and killing living beings through poison or sorcery; or breaking ground and digging earth, planting fields and gardens, raising silkworms and boiling their cocoons, causing harm and killing to a still greater degree; or swatting mosquitoes and gnats, crushing fleas and lice; or burning refuse and clearing out ditches and channels, wrongfully harming every creature there; or eating fruit, or consuming grain, or eating vegetables while wrongfully killing the creatures within them; or lighting firewood, or leaving lamps and candles uncovered, burning insects of every kind; or drawing sauce or vinegar without first stirring it, or pouring out hot water and scalding ants and insects to death. Extending even to every one of the four postures — walking, standing, sitting, and lying down — we constantly injure and kill the tiny creatures that fly through the air or crawl upon the ground, which we, as ordinary beings with darkened awareness, neither perceive nor recognize. Today we lay this bare and confess it entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, whether using the whip and rod, the cangue and chains, the fetters and shackles, crushing, twisting, torturing, striking, and throwing; or trampling with hand and foot, binding and caging; or cutting off water and grain — through all these various evil devices tormenting living beings. Today, with utmost sincerity, before the Buddhas, the venerable Dharma, and the noble assembly of the ten directions, we confess all of this entirely. We pray that, by the merit born of confessing the fault of killing and the rest, we may, in life after life, attain a body like vajra, a life without end, forever free of enmity and hatred, without any thought of killing; that toward all living beings we may attain the ground of regarding them as an only child, and that whenever we see one in danger or urgent distress, we may, without begrudging body or life, find some skillful means to rescue and free them, and only then teach them the subtle and wondrous true Dharma, so that all living beings, upon seeing our form or even its mere reflection, may all receive peace and joy, and upon hearing our name or even the mere sound of our voice, may have every terror entirely removed. We now bow our heads to the ground and take refuge in the Buddha. Homage to the Buddha Vairocana. Homage to our fundamental teacher, the Buddha Śākyamuni. Homage to the Buddha Amitābha. Homage to the Buddha Maitreya. Homage to the Buddha King of the Dragon Race, Most Honored. Homage to the Buddha King of Sovereign Dragons. Homage to the Buddha Ratnaśrī. Homage to the Buddha King Ever Still in the Samādhi of the Awakening-Flower. Homage to the Buddha Banner of the Kāṣāya Robe. Homage to the Buddha Lion's Roar. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mañjuśrī. Homage to the Bodhisattva Samantabhadra. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta. Homage to the Bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Great Adornment. Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This section's argument against killing and meat-eating runs on kinship rather than abstract sentiment: the specific claim is that any given animal, traced back through beginningless rebirth, may well have been one's own parent or sibling in some former life, so that eating meat risks literally consuming one's own kin without recognizing them. The unsparing detail of the hunting and slaughtering methods listed — cages, nets, the animal's cries continuing as it is skinned — is doing real rhetorical work rather than dwelling on cruelty for its own sake: the text is deliberately closing the distance a diner normally keeps from the process that puts meat on a plate. This section, together with Fascicle 1's confession of "food and drink" attachments, is part of a broader case this liturgy makes for vegetarianism as a natural consequence of taking the confession of killing seriously.

4. Confessing Theft懺悔盜業

Chinese Source

禮諸佛已，次復懺悔。某甲等次懺劫盜之業。經中說言：若物屬他、他所守護，於此物中一草一葉不與不取，何況竊盜。但自眾生唯見現在利故，以種種不道而取，致使未來受此殃累。是故經言：劫盜之罪，能令眾生墮於地獄、餓鬼受苦；若在畜生，則受牛馬驢騾駱駝等形，以其所有身力血肉償他宿債；若生人中，為他奴婢，衣不蔽形、食不充口，貧寒困苦人理殆盡。劫盜既有如是苦報，是故今日至誠求哀懺悔。某甲等自從無始以來至于今日，或盜他財寶興刃強奪、或自奮身逼迫而取；或恃公威、或假勢力，高桁大械枉壓良善吞納姦貨、拷直為曲，為此因緣身罹憲網。或任邪治領他財物，侵公益私、侵私益公，損彼利此、損此利彼，割他自饒、口與心悋；或竊沒租估、偷渡關津、私匿公課、藏隱使役。如是等罪皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，或是佛法僧物不與而取，或經像物、或治塔寺物、或供養常住僧物、或擬招提僧物、或盜取誤用恃勢不還，或自借、或貸人、或復換貸漏忘；或三寶物混亂雜用，或以眾物穀米樵薪鹽豉醬醋、菜茹果實、錢帛竹木、繒綵旛蓋、香華油燭隨情逐意，或自用、或與人；或摘佛華果用僧鬘物，因三寶財物私自利己。如是等罪無量無邊，今日慚愧皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，或作周旋朋友師僧同學、父母兄弟六親眷屬共住同止，百一所須更相欺誷；或於鄉隣比近，移籬拓墻侵他地宅、改標易相虜掠資財、包占田園因公託私、奪人邸店及以屯野，如是等罪今悉懺悔。又復無始以來，或攻城破邑燒村壞柵、偷賣良民誘他奴婢；或復枉壓無罪之人，使其形殂血刃身彼徒鎖，家緣破散骨肉生離，分張異域生死隔絕。如是等罪無量無邊，今悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，或商估博貨邸店市易，輕秤小斗減割尺寸、盜竊分銖欺誷圭合、以麁易好以短換長，欺巧百端希望毫利，如是等罪今悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，穿窬墻壁斷道抄掠，抵捍債息、負情違要，面欺心取；或非道陵奪鬼神禽畜四生之物，或假託卜相取人財寶。如是乃至以利求利，惡求多求無厭無足。如是等罪無量無邊不可說盡，今日至禱向十方佛尊法聖眾皆悉懺悔。願承是懺悔劫盜等罪所生功德，生生世世得如意寶常雨七珍，上妙衣服百味甘饌種種湯藥隨其所須應念即至。一切眾生無偷奪想，皆能少欲知足不耽不染，常樂惠施行給濟道，捨頭目髓腦如棄涕唾，迴向滿足檀波羅蜜。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having paid homage to the Buddhas, we next continue with confession. We disciples next confess the karma of robbery and theft. The sūtra says: if an object belongs to another and is guarded by another, then not so much as a blade of grass or a single leaf of that object may be taken without its being given — how much less, then, outright theft! Yet living beings, seeing only present gain, take things through every manner of wrongdoing, bringing upon themselves this calamity to be received in time to come. This is why the sūtra says: the fault of robbery and theft can cause living beings to fall into the hells and among the hungry ghosts, to suffer there; if born among animals, they receive the forms of oxen, horses, donkeys, mules, and camels, repaying with all the strength of their body and their very flesh and blood the debts they owe from former lives; if born among humans, they become the slaves and servants of others, their clothing not enough to cover their bodies, their food not enough to fill their mouths, poor, cold, and utterly destitute of human dignity. Since robbery and theft bring such bitter retribution, today, with utmost sincerity, we beg for mercy in confession. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, whether stealing another's wealth by force of arms, or exerting ourselves to seize it by coercion; or relying on official authority, or borrowing the power of influence, using great cangues and heavy fetters to wrongfully oppress the innocent and good while swallowing up ill-gotten goods, twisting the straight into the crooked — and because of this, our very persons have become entangled in the net of the law. Or, holding office unjustly, managing another's property, encroaching on the public to benefit the private, or encroaching on the private to benefit the public, harming one party to profit another, or harming this one to profit that one, cutting away from others to enrich ourselves, promising with the mouth while begrudging in the heart; or secretly withholding taxes and levies, smuggling past the customs barrier, privately concealing public dues, or hiding away conscripted laborers — faults of this kind we confess entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, whether taking, without its being given, property belonging to the Buddha, the Dharma, or the Saṅgha — the property of sūtras and images, or the property for the upkeep of stūpas and monasteries, or the property offered for the resident Saṅgha, or the property intended for the wandering Saṅgha; or stealing it, misusing it, or relying on influence to avoid returning it; or borrowing it ourselves, or lending it to others, or exchanging or lending it and then forgetting to account for it; or mixing and misusing the property of the Three Jewels indiscriminately; or, with common goods — grain, firewood, salt, fermented paste, sauce and vinegar, vegetables and fruit, coin and cloth, bamboo and timber, silk and banners and canopies, incense, flowers, oil and candles — using them however we please, whether keeping them for ourselves or giving them to others; or plucking the Buddha's flowers and fruit, or using the Saṅgha's garland-offerings, profiting ourselves privately through the wealth of the Three Jewels — faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless. Today, in shame, we confess them entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, whether among friends and companions, teachers and monks, fellow students, parents, brothers, and kin dwelling and lodging together, deceiving one another over every small daily need; or, among neighbors close at hand, moving a fence or extending a wall to encroach on another's land and dwelling, altering boundary markers to plunder another's property, seizing fields and gardens under pretense of public duty while serving private ends, or seizing another's shop, lodging, or open land — faults of this kind we now confess entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time, whether attacking cities and breaching towns, burning villages and destroying stockades, secretly selling good people or luring away another's servants; or wrongfully oppressing the innocent, so that their bodies perish by the blade or their persons are bound in chains, their families scattered and broken, flesh and bone parted from one another, driven apart into distant lands, cut off from each other in life and death — faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless. Today we confess them entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, whether in trading goods, running a shop, or dealing in the marketplace, using light scales and small measures, shaving down the length of a bolt of cloth, stealing by the smallest fraction, cheating by the tiniest measure, exchanging the coarse for the fine or the short for the long, practicing deceit in a hundred forms in hope of the slightest profit — faults of this kind we now confess entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, tunneling through walls, cutting off the road to plunder travelers; refusing to pay a debt's interest, breaking one's word and violating an agreement, deceiving to the face while taking to heart; or, against all right, seizing the possessions of spirits, birds, and beasts of the four modes of birth; or, under pretense of divination and physiognomy, taking another's wealth. Extending even to seeking profit through profit itself, seeking evilly, seeking excessively, never satisfied and never content — faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless, beyond what words could ever exhaust. Today we implore, and before the Buddhas, the venerable Dharma, and the noble assembly of the ten directions, we confess all of this entirely. We pray that, by the merit born of confessing the fault of robbery, theft, and the rest, we may, in life after life, attain a wish-fulfilling jewel that constantly rains down the seven treasures, and that fine garments, food of a hundred flavors, and every kind of medicine may arrive the instant they are thought of, according to whatever is needed; that all living beings may be free of any thought of theft, all able to have few desires and know contentment, neither indulgent nor attached; that we may forever delight in generosity, practicing the way of giving and relief, giving up our very head, eyes, marrow, and brain as easily as spitting or wiping away a tear, dedicating it all toward the complete fulfillment of the perfection of giving.

5. Confessing Sexual Misconduct懺悔婬業

Chinese Source

某甲等次復懺悔貪愛之罪。經中說言：但為欲故關在癡獄，沒生死河莫知能出。眾生為是五欲因緣，從昔以來流轉生死，一切眾生歷劫生中所積身骨如王舍城毘富羅山，所飲母乳如四海水，身所出血復過於此。父母兄弟六親眷屬，命終哭泣所出目淚如四海水。是故說言：有愛則生，愛盡則滅。故知生死貪愛為本。所以經言：婬欲之罪，能令眾生墮於地獄、餓鬼受苦；若在畜生，則受鴿雀鴛鴦等身；若在人中，妻不貞良，得不隨意眷屬。婬欲既有如此惡果，是故今日至誠求哀懺悔。某甲等從無始以來至于今日，或偷人妻妾、奪他婦女；侵陵貞潔污比丘尼、破他梵行逼迫不道、濁心邪視言語嘲調；或復恥他門戶、污賢善名；或於男子五種人所起不淨行。如是等罪無量無邊，今日至誠皆悉懺悔。願承是懺悔婬欲等罪所生功德，生生世世自然化生不由胞胎，清淨皎潔相好光明，六情開朗聰利明達，了悟恩愛猶如桎梏，觀彼六塵如幻如化，於五欲境決定厭離，乃至夢中不起邪想，內外因緣永不能動。懺悔發願已，歸命禮三寶。

Ru-Yi Translation

We disciples next confess the fault of craving and desire. The sūtra says: it is desire alone that locks us within the prison of delusion, sinking us in the river of birth and death with no knowledge of how to escape. Living beings, because of the causes and conditions of the five desires, have from ages past turned through birth and death; the bones that every living being has accumulated through countless lifetimes would rise as high as Mount Vipula by Rājagṛha, the mother's milk each has drunk would fill the four seas, and the blood each has shed exceeds even that. The tears wept by parents, brothers, and kin at the death of a loved one would likewise fill the four seas. This is why it is said: where there is craving, there is birth; where craving is exhausted, there is cessation. Hence we know that craving and desire are the very root of birth and death. This is why the sūtra says: the fault of sexual misconduct can cause living beings to fall into the hells and among the hungry ghosts, to suffer there; if born among animals, they receive the bodies of doves, sparrows, or mandarin ducks; if born among humans, their spouse is unchaste, and they gain a household that will not comply with their wishes. Since sexual misconduct bears such an evil fruit, today, with utmost sincerity, we beg for mercy in confession. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, whether stealing another's wife or concubine, seizing another man's woman; violating chastity, defiling a bhikṣuṇī, breaking another's pure conduct through coercion and unlawful force; looking upon others with a defiled mind and lustful eyes, speaking to them with mockery and taunts; or bringing shame upon another's household, defiling the good name of the virtuous; or committing impure acts with any of the five kinds of men — faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless. Today, with utmost sincerity, we confess them all. We pray that, by the merit born of confessing the fault of sexual misconduct and the rest, we may, in life after life, be born spontaneously by transformation rather than through a womb, pure, radiant, and adorned with fine marks and a shining light; that our six faculties may be open and clear, sharp, keen, and understanding; that we may come to realize that love and attachment are but fetters and shackles, and see the six sense-objects as illusory and unreal as a magician's conjuring, resolved to turn away in revulsion from the objects of the five desires, so that not even in a dream does a wayward thought arise, and no cause or condition, inward or outward, can ever move us again. Having confessed and made this vow, we take refuge in and pay homage to the Three Jewels.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

"The five kinds of men" (男子五種人) is left untranslated in explanatory form above because the phrase is a technical vinaya classification of male sexual types relevant to determining what counts as misconduct in monastic law, and this translation was not able to confirm a single settled English rendering for it worth presenting as definitive. It is flagged here rather than silently smoothed over, so that a reader who needs precision on this point knows to consult a vinaya specialist rather than take this rendering as authoritative.

6. Confessing the Four Evils of Speech懺悔口四業

Chinese Source

前已懺悔身三業竟，今當次第懺悔口四惡業。經中說言：口業之罪，能令眾生墮於地獄、餓鬼受苦；若在畜生，則受鵂鶹鴝鵅鳥形，聞其聲者無不憎惡；若生人中，口氣常臭，有所言說人不信受，眷屬不和常好鬪諍。口業既有如此惡果，是故今日志誠歸依三寶皆悉懺悔。某甲等自從無始以來至于今日，以惡口業於四生六道造種種罪，出言麁獷發語暴橫，不問尊卑親疎貴賤，稍不如意便懷瞋怒，罵詈毀辱猥褻穢惡無所不至，使彼銜恨終身不忘，連禍結讎有無窮已。又或怨黷天地、訶責鬼神，貶斥聖賢、誣污良善。如是惡口所起罪業無量無邊，今日至誠皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，以妄語業作種種罪，意中希求名譽利養，匿情變詐昧心厚顏，指有言空。指空言有。見言不見、不見言見。聞言不聞、不聞言聞，知言不知。不知言知，作言不作、不作言作，欺誷賢聖、誑惑世人。至於父子君臣親戚朋舊，有所談說未嘗誠實，致使他人誤加聽信，亡家敗國咸此由之。或假妖幻每自稱讚，謂得四禪四無色定、安那般那十六行觀、得須陀洹至阿羅漢、得辟支佛不退菩薩、天來龍來鬼來神來、旋風土鬼皆至我所，顯異惑眾求其恭敬四事供養。如是妄語所起罪業無量無邊，今日至誠皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，以綺語業作種種罪，言辭華靡翰墨艶麗，文過飾非巧作歌曲，形容妖冶模寫婬態，使中下之流動心失性，耽荒酒色不能自返。或恣任私讎忘其公議，使彼忠臣孝子志士仁人，強作篇章文致其惡，後世披覽遂以為然，令其抱恨重泉無所明白。如是綺語所起罪業無量無邊，今日至誠皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，以兩舌業作種種罪，面譽背毀巧語百端，向彼說此、向此說彼，唯知利己不顧害他，讒間君臣、誣毀良善，使君臣猜忌、父子不和、夫妻生離、親戚疎曠、師資恩喪、朋友道絕。至於交扇二國渝盟失歡，結怨連兵傷殺百姓。如是兩舌所起罪業無量無邊，今日至誠向十方佛尊法聖眾發露求哀皆悉懺悔。願某甲等承是懺悔口四惡業所生功德，生生世世具八音聲、得四辯才，常說和合利益之語，其聲清雅一切樂聞，善解眾生方俗言語，若有所說應時應根，令彼聽者即得解悟，超凡入聖開發慧眼。懺悔發願已，歸命禮三寶。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having already confessed the three bodily deeds, we shall next, in order, confess the four evil deeds of speech. The sūtra says: the fault of verbal karma can cause living beings to fall into the hells and among the hungry ghosts, to suffer there; if born among animals, they receive the forms of owls and other birds whose cry no one hears without loathing; if born among humans, their breath is always foul, no one believes what they say, and their household is discordant and forever quarreling. Since verbal karma bears such an evil fruit, today, with sincere mind, we take refuge in the Three Jewels and confess it entirely. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, through the karma of harsh speech, have committed every kind of fault across the four modes of birth and the six destinies — speaking coarsely, uttering words violently and unreasonably, without regard for whether another is noble or humble, close or distant, exalted or lowly; the moment something displeases us we harbor anger and rage, cursing and reviling, humiliating and defiling, with nothing too obscene or foul left undone, causing others to bear the resentment for the rest of their lives, unable to forget it, binding calamity and enmity together without end. Or we resent and revile heaven and earth, rebuke gods and spirits, disparage sages and worthies, and slander the good and virtuous. Faults of this kind, arising from harsh speech, are immeasurable and boundless. Today, with utmost sincerity, we confess them all. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, through the karma of false speech, we have committed every kind of fault, our minds set on seeking fame and profit, concealing our true feelings and practicing deceit, a mind of darkness and a face of brazenness — pointing to what exists and calling it empty, pointing to what is empty and calling it existent; saying we have not seen what we have seen, and saying we have seen what we have not seen; saying we have not heard what we have heard, and saying we have heard what we have not heard; saying we do not know what we know, and saying we know what we do not know; saying we have not done what we have done, and saying we have done what we have not done — deceiving sages and worthies, and misleading the people of the world. Even with father and son, ruler and subject, relatives and old friends, whatever we have said to them has never once been sincere, causing others to mistakenly believe and trust it, so that the ruin of households and the fall of kingdoms have all come from this. Or, relying on false and deceptive claims, we have constantly praised ourselves, claiming to have attained the four dhyānas or the four formless absorptions, the sixteen-part contemplation of mindfulness of breathing, the fruit of stream-entry up to arhatship, the state of a pratyekabuddha or a non-retrogressing bodhisattva, claiming that gods, dragons, ghosts, and spirits have come to us, that whirlwind-demons and earth-spirits all gather at our place — displaying wonders to delude the crowd, seeking their reverence and the four kinds of offerings. Faults of this kind, arising from false speech, are immeasurable and boundless. Today, with utmost sincerity, we confess them all. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, through the karma of flowery speech, we have committed every kind of fault, our words ornate and extravagant, our writing florid and seductive, dressing up our faults and glossing over our wrongs, skillfully composing songs and verses, describing seductive appearances and depicting lascivious postures, so that people of middling and lesser capacity have their minds stirred and lose their proper nature, sinking into wine and lust unable to turn back on their own. Or, giving free rein to private grudges while forgetting public fairness, we have forced loyal ministers, filial sons, men of resolve, and men of benevolence into verses and writings that malign them, so that later generations, reading them, take them as true, leaving these wronged souls to nurse their resentment in the underworld with no way to make their innocence known. Faults of this kind, arising from flowery speech, are immeasurable and boundless. Today, with utmost sincerity, we confess them all. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, through the karma of divisive speech, we have committed every kind of fault, praising a person to the face while slandering them behind their back, speaking cleverly in a hundred different ways, telling this person one thing and that person another, knowing only to benefit ourselves without regard for the harm done to others, slandering and sowing discord between ruler and subject, defaming and maligning the good and virtuous, causing ruler and subject to suspect one another, father and son to be discordant, husband and wife to be separated, relatives to grow estranged, the bond of teacher and disciple to be lost, and the way of friendship to be severed. Even to the point of inflaming two countries against one another until their alliance is broken and their harmony lost, binding them in enmity and war, wounding and killing their common people. Faults of this kind, arising from divisive speech, are immeasurable and boundless. Today, with utmost sincerity, before the Buddhas, the venerable Dharma, and the noble assembly of the ten directions, we lay them bare and beg for mercy, confessing them all. We pray that, by the merit born of confessing the four evil deeds of speech, we disciples may, in life after life, be endowed with the eight kinds of Brahmā-voice and attain the four unhindered eloquences, ever speaking words of harmony and benefit, our voice clear and elegant, delighting all who hear it, skillfully understanding the languages and customs of every kind of being, so that whatever we say suits the moment and the listener's capacity, causing all who hear us to attain understanding at once, transcending the ordinary to enter the ranks of the noble, and opening wide the eye of wisdom. Having confessed and made this vow, we take refuge in and pay homage to the Three Jewels.

7. Confessing the Faults of the Six Sense-Faculties懺悔六根

Chinese Source

前已懺悔身三口四業竟，今當次第懺悔六根所作罪障。某甲等無始以來至于今日，或眼為色惑愛染玄黃紅綠朱紫珍玩寶飾，或取男女長短黑白之相姿態妖艶起非法想；或耳貪好聲宮商絃管妓樂歌唱，或取男女音聲語言啼笑之相起非法想；或鼻藉名香沈檀龍麝欝金蘇合起非法想；或舌貪好味鮮美甘肥眾生血肉資養四大，更增苦本起非法想；或身樂華綺綿繡繒縠一切細滑七珍麗服起非法想；或意多亂想觸向乖法。由此六根所造罪業無量無邊，今日至禱向十方佛尊法聖眾皆悉懺悔。願以懺悔眼根功德，願令此眼徹見十方諸佛菩薩清淨法身不以二相；願以懺悔耳根功德，願令此耳常聞十方諸佛賢聖所說正法如教奉行；願以懺悔鼻根功德，願令此鼻常聞香積入法位香，捨離生死不淨臭穢；願以懺悔舌根功德，願令此舌常飡法喜禪悅之食，不貪眾生血肉之味；願以懺悔身根功德，願令此身披如來衣、著忍辱鎧、臥無畏床、坐法空座；願以懺悔意根功德，願令此意成就十力、洞達五明，深觀二諦空平等理，從方便慧入法流水，念念增明顯發如來大無生忍。發願已，歸命禮三寶。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having already confessed the three bodily deeds and the four of speech, we shall next, in order, confess the faults and obstructions caused by the six sense-faculties. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, whether the eye has been deluded by form, drawn to and infatuated with black, yellow, red, green, vermilion, and purple, with precious ornaments and jeweled adornments, or has fixed upon the tall or short, dark or fair features of men and women, their alluring bearing giving rise to unlawful thoughts; or the ear has craved fine sounds — the tones of pipe and string, music and song — or has fixed on the voice, speech, laughter, and weeping of men and women, giving rise to unlawful thoughts; or the nose has relied on fine incense — aloeswood, sandalwood, musk, saffron, and storax — giving rise to unlawful thoughts; or the tongue has craved fine flavors, fresh and rich, nourishing the four elements with the flesh and blood of living beings, only deepening the very root of suffering, giving rise to unlawful thoughts; or the body has delighted in fine silk, brocade, and gauze, in every smooth and delicate thing, in the seven treasures and fine clothing, giving rise to unlawful thoughts; or the mind has been filled with scattered and confused thoughts, turning against the Dharma at every touch. Because of these six sense-faculties, the fault and karma produced are immeasurable and boundless. Today we implore, and before the Buddhas, the venerable Dharma, and the noble assembly of the ten directions, we confess it all entirely. We pray that, by the merit of confessing the eye-faculty, this eye may come to see thoroughly, throughout the ten directions, the pure Dharma-body of the Buddhas and bodhisattvas, without any duality of appearance. We pray that, by the merit of confessing the ear-faculty, this ear may constantly hear the true Dharma spoken by the Buddhas and sages of the ten directions, and act upon it as taught. We pray that, by the merit of confessing the nose-faculty, this nose may constantly smell the fragrance of accumulated merit that enters the station of the Dharma, forsaking the impure stench of birth and death. We pray that, by the merit of confessing the tongue-faculty, this tongue may constantly savor the food of the joy of Dharma and the bliss of meditation, no longer craving the taste of the flesh and blood of living beings. We pray that, by the merit of confessing the body-faculty, this body may be clothed in the Tathāgata's robe, armored in the armor of patience, resting on the bed of fearlessness, and seated on the seat of the emptiness of the Dharma. We pray that, by the merit of confessing the mind-faculty, this mind may accomplish the ten powers, penetrate thoroughly the five sciences, contemplate deeply the equal and empty principle of the two truths, and, through skillful wisdom, enter the flowing water of the Dharma, its brightness increasing thought after thought, until it brings forth the Tathāgata's great acceptance of the non-arising of all phenomena. Having made this vow, we take refuge in and pay homage to the Three Jewels.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The closing dedication here is built as a clean mirror of the confession that precedes it: eye answered with the vision of the Buddhas' Dharma-body, ear with hearing the true teaching, nose with the fragrance of merit in place of incense, tongue with the taste of meditative joy in place of flesh, body clothed in patience rather than fine silk, and mind arriving at the acceptance of non-arising rather than scattered thought. Each faculty's fault and its hoped-for transformation are stated in matching terms, so that the confession of the six faculties reads, structurally, as a promise rather than only an accusation.
