Fascicle 3

The hells, the lower destinies, and the closing dedication《慈悲水懺法》卷下

The liturgy's final fascicle confesses faults committed toward the Three Jewels themselves, then turns to the last of the three obstructions — retribution — through an extended description of the hell of Avīci and a litany of named hells, the animal, hungry-ghost, and asura destinies, and the lesser miseries of human and heavenly life, before closing with a single sustained dedication of merit extended to every being without exception.

Translator: Ru-Yi Meditation Center Source: Taishō T1910 Obstruction 3 of 3: Retribution (報障) Status: complete
Chinese
《慈悲水懺法》卷下 — the third and final fascicle of the Water Repentance.
Covers
Confession of faults toward the Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha, and a closing set of miscellaneous vices; a doctrinal case for why confession cannot be postponed; the full confession of the third obstruction, retribution (報障), across the hell of Avīci, a litany of named hells, the animal, hungry-ghost, and asura destinies, and the residual sufferings of human and heavenly life; and the liturgy's closing dedication of merit to all beings.
Genre note
Continuous liturgical prose meant to be recited, not narrative. This translation divides it into six numbered sections for readability; the original carries no internal headings.
Content note
Section 4 describes the tortures of the hell of Avīci and a long litany of other hells in graphic, sustained detail, as the source text does. This is deterrent literature by design — the vividness is the argument.
Difficulty
★★☆☆☆ — the language is direct throughout, though the hell litany in Section 4 names more than twenty distinct hells in quick succession, several transliterated rather than translated where the original itself preserves an onomatopoeic Sanskrit name.

1. Confessing Faults Toward the Three Jewels懺悔三寶門中罪障

Chinese Source

一切諸佛愍念眾生為說水懺道場總法，今當歸命一切諸佛。 南無毘盧遮那佛　南無本師釋迦牟尼佛　南無阿彌陀佛　南無彌勒佛　南無龍種上尊王佛　南無龍自在王佛　南無寶勝佛　南無覺華定自在王佛　南無袈裟幢佛　南無師子吼佛　南無文殊師利菩薩　南無普賢菩薩　南無大勢至菩薩　南無地藏菩薩　南無大莊嚴菩薩　南無觀自在菩薩 禮諸佛已，次復懺悔。已懺身三口四竟，次復懺悔佛法僧間一切諸障。經中佛說：人身難得、佛法難聞、眾僧難值、信心難生、六根難具、善友難得。而今相與宿植善根，得此人身、六根完具，又值善友、得聞正法。於其中間復各不能盡心精勤，恐於未來長溺萬苦無有出期，是故今日至誠求哀懺悔。某甲等自從無始以來至于今日，常以無明覆心、煩惱障意，見佛形像不能盡心恭敬，輕蔑眾僧殘害善友，破塔毀寺焚燒經像，出佛身血。或自處華堂，安置尊像卑猥之處，使煙薰日曝、風吹雨露、塵土污坌、雀鼠毀壞，共住同宿曾無禮敬。或裸露像前初不嚴飾，遮掩燈燭關閉殿宇，障佛光明。如是等罪，今日至誠皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，或於法間以不淨手把捉經卷，或臨經書非法俗語，或安置床頭坐起不敬，或關閉箱篋蟲蠧朽爛，或首軸脫落部帙失次，或挽脫漏誤紙墨破裂，自不修習不肯流傳。如是等罪，今日志誠皆悉懺悔。或眠地聽經、仰臥讀誦，高聲語笑亂他聽法，或邪解佛語僻說聖意，非法說法、法說非法，非犯說犯、犯說非犯，輕罪說重、重罪說輕。或抄前著後、抄後著前，前後著中、中著前後，綺飾文詞安置己典。或為利養名譽恭敬，為人說法無道德心，求法師過而為論義非理彈擊，不為長解求出世法。或輕慢佛語尊重邪教，毀呰大乘讚聲聞道。如是等罪無量無邊，皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，或於僧間有障殺阿羅漢、破和合僧，害發無上菩提心人，斷滅佛種使聖道不行。或剝脫道人鞭拷沙門、楚撻驅使苦言加謗、或破淨戒及破威儀。或勸他人捨於八正受行五法，或假託形儀闚竊常住。如是等罪今悉懺悔。或裸露身形輕衣傏𠊲，在經像前不淨脚履踏上殿塔，或著屧屐入僧伽藍，涕唾堂房污佛僧地，乘車策馬排揬寺舍。凡如是等於三寶間所起罪障無量無邊，今日至禱向十方佛尊法聖眾皆悉懺悔。願承是懺悔佛法僧間所有罪障，生生世世常值三寶，尊仰恭敬無有厭倦，天繒妙綵眾寶纓絡百千伎樂、珍異名香華果鮮明，盡世所有常以供養。若有成佛先往勸請開甘露門，若入涅槃願我常得獻最後供。於眾僧中修六和敬，得自在力興隆三寶，上弘佛道、下化眾生。

Ru-Yi Translation

All the Buddhas, mindful of living beings with compassion, have spoken this comprehensive rite of the Water Repentance. We shall now take refuge in all the Buddhas. Homage to the Buddha Vairocana. Homage to our fundamental teacher, the Buddha Śākyamuni. Homage to the Buddha Amitābha. Homage to the Buddha Maitreya. Homage to the Buddha King of the Dragon Race, Most Honored. Homage to the Buddha King of Sovereign Dragons. Homage to the Buddha Ratnaśrī. Homage to the Buddha King Ever Still in the Samādhi of the Awakening-Flower. Homage to the Buddha Banner of the Kāṣāya Robe. Homage to the Buddha Lion's Roar. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mañjuśrī. Homage to the Bodhisattva Samantabhadra. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta. Homage to the Bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Great Adornment. Homage to the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. Having paid homage to the Buddhas, we next continue with confession. Having already confessed the three bodily deeds and the four of speech, we shall next confess every obstruction that concerns the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha. The sūtra says the Buddha taught: a human body is hard to obtain, the Buddha-dharma is hard to hear, the Saṅgha is hard to encounter, faith is hard to arouse, the six faculties are hard to have complete, and a good companion is hard to find. And yet we ourselves, having planted wholesome roots in past lives, have obtained this human body, with the six faculties complete, and have moreover encountered good companions and been able to hear the true Dharma. Yet in the midst of all this, each of us has still failed to apply the mind fully and diligently, and we fear that in time to come we will long be sunk in ten thousand sufferings with no time of escape ever in sight. For this reason, today, with utmost sincerity, we beg for mercy in confession. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, have constantly let ignorance cover the mind and affliction obstruct the will — seeing the Buddha's image without honoring it with a whole heart, slighting the Saṅgha and injuring good companions, destroying stūpas and ruining monasteries, burning sūtras and images, and drawing the Buddha's own blood. Or we have kept ourselves in fine, ornamented halls while placing the sacred image in some low and neglected spot, letting it be blackened by smoke, bleached by the sun, exposed to wind and rain, soiled by dust, and damaged by sparrows and rats, dwelling and lodging alongside it without ever once showing it reverence. Or we have left the image bare before us, never once properly adorning it, while shading lamps and candles and shutting up the hall, obstructing the Buddha's own light. Faults of this kind, today, with utmost sincerity, we confess entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, whether handling a sūtra scroll with unclean hands, or speaking unlawful and vulgar words while facing the sūtra texts; or placing them carelessly at the head of a bed, sitting and rising before them without reverence; or shutting them away in a chest or box until they are eaten by insects and rot away; or letting their spindles and covers fall apart and their sections fall out of order; or tearing and dropping them, staining and tearing the paper and ink through carelessness, unwilling ourselves to study them or to let them be passed on to others. Faults of this kind, today, with sincere mind, we confess entirely. Or lying on the ground to listen to a sūtra, or reciting it while lying flat on our backs; talking and laughing loudly, disturbing others who are listening to the Dharma; or wrongly interpreting the Buddha's words, twisting the meaning of the sages, calling what is not the Dharma the Dharma, and calling the Dharma not the Dharma, calling what is not an offense an offense, and calling an offense not an offense, calling a light fault grave, and a grave fault light. Or cutting a passage from earlier and inserting it later, or from later and inserting it earlier, moving what belongs before or after into the middle, or what belongs in the middle to before or after, dressing up the wording with flourishes and passing it off as one's own composition. Or, for the sake of profit, fame, and reverence, teaching the Dharma to others without a mind of virtue, seeking out a Dharma-master's faults in order to attack him unreasonably in debate, rather than seeking a deeper understanding of the teachings that lead beyond the world. Or slighting the Buddha's own word while honoring false teachings, disparaging the Great Vehicle while praising only the path of the disciples. Faults of this kind are immeasurable and boundless; we confess them all entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, whether in matters concerning the Saṅgha, obstructing or killing an arhat, breaking apart the harmonious Saṅgha, harming one who has given rise to the unsurpassed mind of awakening, cutting off the seed of buddhahood so that the noble path can no longer proceed. Or stripping a monk of his robes, whipping and torturing a renunciant, driving him with lashes and harsh words and adding slander to it; or breaking the pure precepts and violating dignified conduct. Or urging another to abandon the eightfold path and instead follow the five heterodox practices, or, under the pretense of a monastic's outward form, secretly pilfering the property of the permanent Saṅgha. Faults of this kind we now confess entirely. Or going about with the body exposed, our robes carelessly worn and disheveled, standing before sūtras and images with unclean feet, or treading upon the halls and stūpas; or wearing wooden clogs into the monastery grounds, spitting and blowing our nose within the halls, soiling the ground of the Buddha and the Saṅgha, or riding in carriages and on horseback, crowding and jostling against the monastery buildings. All such faults as these, arising in matters concerning the Three Jewels, are immeasurable and boundless. Today we implore, and before the Buddhas, the venerable Dharma, and the noble assembly of the ten directions, we confess all of this entirely. We pray that, by the merit born of confessing every fault and obstruction concerning the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, we may, in life after life, constantly encounter the Three Jewels, honoring and revering them without ever growing weary, and that heavenly silks and fine brocades, garlands of every precious jewel, a hundred thousand kinds of music, rare and famous incense, and fresh flowers and fruit — everything the world contains — may always be ours to offer up. Should any being attain buddhahood, may we be the first to go and entreat him to open the gate of ambrosia; should he enter nirvāṇa, may we always be granted the chance to make the very last offering to him. Within the assembly of the Saṅgha, may we cultivate the six points of reverent harmony, attain the power of mastery, and cause the Three Jewels to flourish — above, spreading wide the Buddha's path, and below, transforming living beings.

2. Confessing the Remaining Faults懺悔餘惡

Chinese Source

如上所說於三寶間輕重諸罪皆已懺悔，其餘諸惡今當次第更復懺悔。經中佛說：有二種健兒，一者自不作罪、二者作已能悔。又云：有二種白法，能為眾生滅除眾障，一者慚自不作惡、二者愧不令他作。有慚愧者可名為人，若不慚愧與諸禽獸不相異也。是故今日至誠歸依於佛，如法懺悔。某甲等自從無始以來至于今日，或信邪倒見殺害眾生，解奏魑魅魍魎鬼神，欲希延年終不能得，或妄言見鬼假稱神語，如是等罪皆悉懺悔。又復無始以來至于今日，或行動傲誕自高自大，或恃種姓輕慢一切，以貴輕賤用強陵弱，或飲酒鬪亂不避親疎，惛醉終日不識尊卑，如是等罪今悉懺悔。或嗜飲食無有期度，或食生鱠、或噉五辛，薰穢經像排揬淨眾，縱心恣意不知限極，疎遠善人狎近惡友，如是等罪今悉懺悔。或貢高矯假偃蹇自用，跋扈抵揬不識人情，自是他非希望僥倖，如是等罪今悉懺悔。或臨財無讓不廉不恥，屠肉沽酒欺誑自活，或出入息利計時賣日，聚積慳剋貪求無厭，受人供養不慚不愧，或無戒德空納信施，如是等罪今悉懺悔。或捶打奴婢驅使僮僕，不問饑渴、不問寒暑，或伐撤橋梁杜絕行路，如是等罪今悉懺悔。或放逸自恣，無記散亂摴蒲圍棊，群會屯聚飲食酒肉更相擾餞，無趣談話論說天下，從年竟歲空喪天日，初中後夜禪誦不修，懈怠懶惰尸臥終日，於六念處心不經理，見他勝事便生嫉妬，心懷慘毒備起煩惱，致使諸惡猛風吹罪薪火常以熾然無有休息，三業微善一切俱焚，善法既盡為一闡提，墮大地獄無有出期。是故今日至禱稽顙，向十方三寶皆悉懺悔。上來所有一切眾罪，若輕若重、若麁若細，若自作、若教他作、若隨喜作、若以勢力逼迫令作，如是乃至讚歎行惡法者，今日志誠皆悉懺悔。願承是懺悔一切諸惡所生功德，生生世世慈和忠孝謙卑忍辱，知廉識恥先意問訊，循良正謹清潔義讓，遠離惡友常遇善緣，收攝六情守護三業，捍勞忍苦心不退沒，立菩提志不負眾生。發願已，歸命禮諸佛。 南無毘盧遮那佛　南無本師釋迦牟尼佛　南無阿彌陀佛　南無彌勒佛　南無龍種上尊王佛　南無龍自在王佛　南無寶勝佛　南無覺華定自在王佛　南無袈裟幢佛　南無師子吼佛　南無文殊師利菩薩　南無普賢菩薩　南無大勢至菩薩　南無地藏菩薩　南無大莊嚴菩薩　南無觀自在菩薩

Ru-Yi Translation

As set out above, we have already confessed every fault, light and grave, concerning the Three Jewels; we shall now, in turn, further confess every remaining evil. The sūtra records the Buddha's own teaching: there are two kinds of strong and capable persons — one who commits no fault to begin with, and one who, having committed a fault, is able to repent of it. It also says: there are two wholesome dharmas able to remove every obstruction for living beings — the first is shame before oneself, refusing to do evil; the second is shame before others, refusing to let them do evil. One who has such shame may truly be called a human being; one without it is no different from the birds and beasts. For this reason, today, with utmost sincerity, we take refuge in the Buddha and confess as the Dharma teaches. We disciples, from beginningless time until this very day, whether trusting in false and inverted views to kill living beings, or invoking goblins, sprites, and demons in sacrifice, hoping thereby to extend our lifespan and never succeeding; or falsely claiming to see ghosts, or falsely claiming to speak the words of a spirit — faults of this kind we confess entirely. Furthermore, from beginningless time until this very day, whether behaving with arrogance and conceit, exalting ourselves above others; or relying on lineage and pedigree to look down on everyone; using our rank to despise the humble, or our strength to oppress the weak; or drinking wine and brawling in disorder without regard for kin or stranger, drunk and stupefied all day long, unable to tell high from low — faults of this kind we now confess entirely. Or indulging in food and drink without any measure or restraint, eating raw minced meat, or eating the five pungent roots, polluting sūtras and images with their stench and crowding rudely among the pure assembly, giving free rein to our own whims without knowing any limit, keeping distant from the good and drawing close to bad companions — faults of this kind we now confess entirely. Or being conceited, pretentious, arrogant, and self-important, domineering and rude, ignorant of others' feelings, always taking ourselves to be right and others wrong, hoping for good luck we have not earned — faults of this kind we now confess entirely. Or, when facing wealth, showing no restraint, without integrity and without shame, butchering meat and selling wine to make our living through deceit; or lending at interest, reckoning our profit by the day, hoarding and grasping, greedy and never satisfied, accepting others' offerings without any shame or remorse; or, lacking the virtue of the precepts, emptily receiving the offerings given in faith — faults of this kind we now confess entirely. Or beating our male and female servants, working our young attendants without pity, giving no thought to whether they hunger or thirst, whether it is cold or hot; or tearing down bridges and blocking the roads that others must travel — faults of this kind we now confess entirely. Or living recklessly and self-indulgently, our minds scattered and undisciplined in gambling and board-games, gathering in crowds to feast on wine and meat, disturbing and toasting one another, engaging in idle talk about the affairs of the world with no purpose, wasting the days and months, letting the very light of heaven pass by in vain; neglecting meditation and recitation through the early, middle, and later watches of the night, idle and lazy, lying about like a corpse all day long, never once managing the mind through the six recollections; and, on seeing another succeed, at once giving rise to jealousy, harboring cruelty and poison in the heart, arousing every affliction in full — until the fierce wind of every evil fans the firewood of fault into flames that blaze without rest, burning away entirely the slight goodness in our three kinds of karma, until all wholesome dharma is exhausted and we become an icchantika, falling into the great hells with no time of escape ever in sight. For this reason, today we implore and bow our heads to the ground, and before the Three Jewels of the ten directions, we confess all of this entirely. As for every fault set out above, whether light or grave, whether coarse or subtle, whether done by ourselves, or caused by us through instructing another, or done through our own delight in another's wrongdoing, or done through our forcing another by threat or power — extending even to those who merely praise the doing of evil — today, with sincere mind, we confess all of this entirely. We pray that, by the merit born of confessing every evil, we may, in life after life, be compassionate, gentle, loyal, and filial, humble and forbearing, knowing integrity and shame, greeting others first with kind concern, upright, careful, pure, and yielding in what is right, keeping far from bad companions and constantly meeting with good conditions, gathering in the six faculties and guarding the three kinds of karma, enduring hardship and bearing suffering without the mind ever retreating or sinking, establishing the resolve for awakening and never failing living beings. Having made this vow, we take refuge in and pay homage to the Buddhas. Homage to the Buddha Vairocana. Homage to our fundamental teacher, the Buddha Śākyamuni. Homage to the Buddha Amitābha. Homage to the Buddha Maitreya. Homage to the Buddha King of the Dragon Race, Most Honored. Homage to the Buddha King of Sovereign Dragons. Homage to the Buddha Ratnaśrī. Homage to the Buddha King Ever Still in the Samādhi of the Awakening-Flower. Homage to the Buddha Banner of the Kāṣāya Robe. Homage to the Buddha Lion's Roar. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mañjuśrī. Homage to the Bodhisattva Samantabhadra. Homage to the Bodhisattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta. Homage to the Bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha. Homage to the Bodhisattva of Great Adornment. Homage to the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

The two-part maxim opening this section — "two kinds of strong and capable persons," "two wholesome dharmas" both built on shame — echoes and extends the first of the sevenfold mind set out at the start of Fascicle 1. There, shame before oneself opened a sequence moving toward emptiness; here, at the far end of the entire liturgy, shame is defined as the very line between being human and being an animal. The list of "remaining faults" that follows is deliberately miscellaneous — drunkenness, gambling, jealousy, exploiting servants, idling away the night watches meant for meditation — reading less like a doctrinal category and more like an examination of conscience for the layperson performing this rite, a catch-all for whatever the preceding, more systematic confessions did not already name.

3. Why Repentance Cannot Wait明報障、誡無常

Chinese Source

禮諸佛已，次復懺悔。向來已懺悔煩惱障、已懺悔業障，所餘報障今當次第披陳懺悔。經中說言：業報至時，非空非海中、非入山石間，無有地方所，脫之不受報。唯有懺悔力，乃能得除滅。何以知然？釋提桓因五衰相見，恐懼切心歸誠三寶，五相即滅，得延天年。如是等比，經教所明其事非一，故知懺悔實能滅禍。但凡夫之人若不值善友獎導，則靡惡而不造，致使大命將盡臨窮之際，地獄惡相皆現在前。當爾之時，悔懼交至，不預修善臨窮方悔，悔之於後將何及乎？殃禍異處宿預嚴待，當獨趣入到地獄所，但得前行入於火鑊，身心摧碎精神痛苦。如此之時，欲求一禮一懺豈可復得？眾等莫自恃盛年財寶勢力，懶惰懈怠放逸自恣。死苦一至，無問老少貧富貴賤皆悉摩滅，奄忽而至不令人知。夫人命無常喻如朝露，出息雖存入息難保，云何忽此而不懺悔？但五天使者既來、無常殺鬼卒至，盛年壯色無得免者。當爾之時，華堂邃宇何關人事？高車大馬豈得自隨？妻子眷屬非復我親，七珍寶飾乃為他玩。以此而言，世間果報皆為幻化。天上雖樂會歸敗壞，壽盡魂逝墮落三塗。是故佛語須跋陀言：「汝師欝頭藍弗利根聰明，能伏煩惱至於非非想處。命終還作畜生道中飛狸之身，況復餘者。」故知未登聖果已還，皆應輪轉備經惡趣。如不謹慎，忽爾一朝親嬰斯事，將不悔哉。如今被罪行詣公門己是小苦，情地慞惶、眷屬恐懼求救百端。地獄眾苦比於此者，百千萬倍不得為喻。眾等相與塵劫以來罪若須彌，云何聞此安然，不畏不驚不恐，令此精神復嬰斯苦，實為可痛。是故志誠求哀懺悔。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having paid homage to the Buddhas, we next continue with confession. Up to now we have confessed the obstruction of affliction and confessed the obstruction of karma; the obstruction of retribution that remains we shall now, in turn, lay open and confess. The sūtra says: when the time comes for karmic retribution, there is no emptiness, no ocean, no mountain crag, no place at all where one might escape without receiving it. Only the power of confession can truly remove and dissolve it. How do we know this is so? When Śakra, lord of the gods, saw in himself the five signs of heavenly decline, he was struck with such urgent dread that he turned with utter sincerity to the Three Jewels, and the five signs at once vanished, and his heavenly life was extended. Instances like this, made clear throughout the sūtras' teaching, are not just one or two — and so we know that confession can truly dissolve calamity. But ordinary people, if they do not encounter a good companion to guide and encourage them, will leave no evil undone, until, as their great span of life is about to end, the terrible visions of hell all appear before them. At that moment, regret and terror arrive together — but for one who has not cultivated good beforehand and only repents when death is upon him, what good can such late repentance possibly do? The calamity waits in a different place, prepared and severe from long before, and he must go alone to the place of hell, where he can only advance into the cauldron of fire, body and mind shattered, spirit in agony. At such a time, could he still hope to obtain so much as a single bow, a single act of confession? Let none of us rely on the prime of our years, our wealth, or our influence, growing idle, lazy, and self-indulgent. When the suffering of death arrives, it makes no distinction between old and young, rich and poor, noble and humble — all are equally erased, and it comes suddenly, without warning anyone. Human life is impermanent, like the morning dew — though the outgoing breath still remains, the incoming breath is hard to guarantee. How, then, can we so casually fail to confess? Once the five heavenly messengers have come and the demon of impermanence, the killer, arrives close behind, no one escapes, however youthful or handsome. At that moment, what have fine halls and deep chambers to do with human affairs any longer? How could tall carriages and fine horses follow us then? Wife, children, and kin are no longer truly our own; the seven treasures and fine adornments become mere playthings for others. Considered this way, every worldly fruit of retribution is nothing but illusion and transformation. Even the joys of heaven inevitably end in ruin, and when the heavenly life is spent, the spirit departs and falls into the three evil destinies. This is why the Buddha said to Subhadra: "Your former teacher Udraka Rāmaputra was keen and intelligent, able to subdue affliction all the way to the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception — and yet when his life ended, he was reborn among the animals, as a flying wildcat. How much more so, then, for everyone else!" Hence we know that all who have not yet ascended to the fruit of a noble one must still turn through the cycle, passing in full through the evil destinies. If we are not careful, and one morning suddenly find ourselves caught up in this very thing, will we not regret it then? Even now, to be arrested for a crime and brought before a magistrate's gate is already a small suffering — the heart thrown into panic, one's family terrified, begging for rescue by every means. Compared to this, the many sufferings of the hells are a hundred thousand times greater still, beyond any possible comparison. We ourselves, together, have accumulated faults across dust-laden kalpas as vast as Mount Sumeru — how, then, upon hearing this, can we remain at ease, without fear, without alarm, without dread, letting this very spirit of ours meet with such suffering again? This is truly a thing to grieve over. For this reason, with utmost sincerity, we beg for mercy in confession.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Two classical references anchor this section's urgency. The "five signs of decline" belong to a well-known Buddhist motif in which even Śakra, lord of the gods, is not exempt from impermanence, and is saved only by taking refuge rather than by his own heavenly status. The exchange with Subhadra draws on an episode from the account of the Buddha's final days: Subhadra was the Buddha's last direct disciple, and Udraka Rāmaputra was one of the two teachers under whom the Buddha himself had studied meditation before his awakening, famous for having mastered the very summit of formless absorption — the point being that not even the deepest meditative attainment available outside the Buddhist path secures a favorable rebirth, so long as the underlying afflictions remain merely suppressed rather than uprooted.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

Four of the named hells in this litany — Apapa, Ahaha, Aṭaṭa, and Araraḥ — are left transliterated rather than translated, because in the source language itself these are onomatopoeic names, imitating the sounds a person makes crying out in the extreme cold each hell inflicts, and any English translation would have to invent a different set of sounds rather than render an existing meaning. The whole passage belongs to a well-established genre within Buddhist literature, functioning as moral instruction through vivid, cumulative, almost architectural description rather than argument — the effect depends on the sheer inventory of named tortures, which this translation preserves in full rather than compress.

The sūtra records the Buddha's teaching: a person of many desires, seeking profit at every turn, suffers many troubles as well; a person who knows contentment, though he lie upon the bare ground, still counts himself at ease; one who does not know contentment, though he dwell in a heavenly palace, still finds nothing to his liking. We who together bear this span of life in Jambudvīpa, though it is said to run to a hundred years, few indeed live to see it fulfilled; and in the midst of it, those cut down wrongfully in the prime of life are beyond number. There is only the press of many sufferings pursuing us, the heart and body caught in sorrow, worry, and fear, never for a moment set free. All of this comes from wholesome roots too weak and evil karma too abundant, so that whatever we now attempt fails to satisfy us — know that all of this is the remaining retribution of evil karma from the past. For this reason, we disciples shall now confess: from beginningless time until this very day, whatever immeasurable remaining retribution exists now or is yet to come among humans and gods — we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of inherited calamity and long-standing debts, of crippling, of a hundred illnesses, and of the six faculties left incomplete; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of being born in a borderland with wrong views, or amid the three evil destinies and the eight conditions unfavorable to the Dharma; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of much illness, of wasting away, and of a life cut wrongfully short; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of being unable to keep one's kin and family together for long; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of the suffering of loved ones' deaths and separation from those we love; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of encountering our enemies and gathering with them in sorrow, worry, and dread; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of flood, fire, robbers, and war, of danger, terror, and cowardice; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of solitude, hardship, wandering displacement, and the loss of one's own land; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of imprisonment, confinement in darkness, forced standing, whipping, and torture; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of public and private slander, of being entangled and falsely accused by one another; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of a grave illness lasting years without recovery, confined to bed and unable to rise; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of winter plagues and summer epidemics, of pestilence and cold-damage disease; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of the disease-bearing wind, of swelling and blockage; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of evil spirits watching for their chance to bring calamity upon us; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of birds crying with a hundred ill omens, of flying corpses and evil spirits working strange misfortune; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of being harmed by tigers, leopards, jackals, wolves, and every evil beast of water and land; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of suicide by hanging; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of throwing oneself into a pit or into fire, drowning or falling to one's own death; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of having no dignity and no good reputation; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of clothing and livelihood never sufficient to satisfy the heart; we confess the retribution, in the human realm, of meeting, in all our comings and goings and undertakings, with bad companions who place obstacles in our way. All such immeasurable calamity, disaster, plague, and affliction, present and future, among humans and gods — we disciples, today, with utmost sincerity, before the Buddhas, the venerable Dharma, and the noble assembly of the ten directions, beg for mercy in confession, that they may all be dissolved.

6. The Great Dedication of Merit and Vow發願回向

Chinese Source

前已懺悔三業六根一切煩惱障、一切業障、四生六道一切報障，今當次第發願回向。某甲等願以此懺悔三障所生功德，悉皆回向施與一切眾生，俱同懺悔，願與一切眾生現生之內身心安樂，三災八難不吉祥事咸悉消除，衣食豐饒，正信三寶，捨此報身皆得往生極樂世界，親覲彌陀得授記莂，當來世中見彌勒佛，聽聞正法如教進修。又願生生世世在在處處，常值國王興隆三寶，不生外道邪見之家。又願生生世世在在處處，蓮華化生種族尊勝，安隱快樂衣食自然。又願生生世世在在處處，慈仁忠孝等心濟物，不生一念逆害之心。又願生生世世在在處處，常為諸佛之所護念，能降魔怨及諸外道，與諸菩薩俱會一處，菩提道心相續不斷。又願生生世世在在處處，興顯佛法修行大乘，分身無量救度眾生，直至道場無有退轉。如諸佛菩薩所發誓願，所修福智所行回向，我亦如是發願修集回向，虛空界盡、眾生界盡、眾生業煩惱盡，我此修行回向終無有盡。發願回向已，至心信禮常住三寶。

Ru-Yi Translation

Having now confessed the three deeds and six faculties, every obstruction of affliction, every obstruction of karma, and every obstruction of retribution across the four modes of birth and the six destinies, we shall now, in turn, make our vow and dedicate this merit. We disciples pray that all the merit born of this confession of the three obstructions be entirely dedicated and given over to all living beings, that they may join us in this same confession; we pray that all living beings, within this very life, may have peace of body and mind, that the three calamities, the eight difficulties, and every inauspicious thing may be entirely dissolved, that food and clothing may be abundant, that true faith in the Three Jewels may be theirs, and that, upon giving up this retributive body, all may be reborn in the Land of Ultimate Bliss, behold Amitābha in person, and receive from him the prediction of buddhahood; and that in the age to come, they may behold the Buddha Maitreya, hear the true Dharma, and advance in cultivation just as he teaches. We further pray that, in life after life, in every place, we may constantly encounter kings who cause the Three Jewels to flourish, and never be born into a household of heterodox and wrong views. We further pray that, in life after life, in every place, we may be born by transformation from a lotus flower, of an honored and excellent lineage, at peace and joyful, our clothing and food arising naturally of themselves. We further pray that, in life after life, in every place, we may hold a mind of compassion, benevolence, loyalty, and filial devotion, equally relieving all beings, without a single thought of hostility or harm ever arising. We further pray that, in life after life, in every place, we may constantly be held in the protective mindfulness of all the Buddhas, able to subdue the demon of Māra and every heterodox teaching, gathering together in one place with all the bodhisattvas, our resolve for awakening continuing without ever breaking off. We further pray that, in life after life, in every place, we may cause the Buddha-dharma to flourish and shine forth, practicing the Great Vehicle, dividing our bodies without limit to save and deliver living beings, advancing straight to the very seat of awakening without ever turning back. Just as all the Buddhas and bodhisattvas have made their vows, cultivated their merit and wisdom, and carried out their dedication, so too do we now make this vow, cultivate this accumulation, and carry out this dedication. Until the very reach of empty space is exhausted, until the very realm of living beings is exhausted, until the very karma and afflictions of living beings are exhausted, this dedication of our practice shall never come to an end. Having made this vow and dedication, with utmost sincerity we take refuge in and pay homage to the ever-abiding Three Jewels.

Reading note — Ru-Yi Meditation Center

This closing dedication is the hinge the entire three-fascicle liturgy has been building toward: everything before this point has been confession in the first person, "we disciples," working through fault after fault under our own names — and here, in the final movement, that same merit is handed away entirely to "all living beings," with rebirth in Amitābha's Pure Land offered not as a private reward but as something to be shared without exception. The vow's final lines — that this dedication continues only until space itself, the realm of beings, and the very afflictions of beings are exhausted, which is to say never — is a standard closing formula found across Mahāyāna vow literature, and it is worth reading slowly rather than passing over as a stock phrase: it commits the practitioner to a horizon with no terminus, precisely at the moment the liturgy itself is about to end.
