# Jiaoguan Gangzong — Translation Notes

These notes record recurring translation decisions for the Ru-Yi English translation of Ouyi Zhixu's *Jiaoguan Gangzong* (教觀綱宗, Taishō T46, no. 1939), with reading notes drawn throughout from Zhixu's own commentary, the *Jiaoguan Gangzong Shiyi* (教觀綱宗釋義, Xuzangjing X57, no. 974). They exist so that this treatise's dense classification vocabulary — much of it the very scaffolding the rest of Tiantai doctrine hangs on — stays consistent across all five parts, and so that later Tiantai texts on this site can inherit these renderings rather than re-coin them.

## The text and its author

Ouyi Zhixu (蕅益智旭, 1599–1655) was a late-Ming dynasty monk and one of the four eminent masters of that era, though he is not usually counted a strict lineage-holder of any single school — his own writing draws on Tiantai, Chan, Pure Land, and Vinaya alike. The *Jiaoguan Gangzong* is nonetheless his most concentrated statement of Tiantai doctrine: a short treatise, expanded from an earlier wall-chart of his own devising (as his own opening lines explain), that compresses the entire Tiantai classification of the Buddha's teaching career — the Five Periods, the Eight Teachings, and the Four Teachings of Content worked out in full doctrinal detail, complete with each teaching's own six identities and ten modes of contemplation — into a single continuous outline meant to be memorized. It is often read as the standard doctrinal key to the rest of the Tiantai corpus, including the far longer works already translated on this site.

The companion commentary, the *Jiaoguan Gangzong Shiyi*, is Zhixu's own explanation of his own treatise, expanding on nearly every compressed term and parenthetical gloss in the base text. Its structure tracks the base text closely enough that every reading note on this collection's five pages can be traced back to a specific passage of T1939.

Zhixu died in 1655 — both texts have been in the public domain for well over three centuries, under any jurisdiction's copyright term. ## Structure

The base text (479 lines in its CBETA markdown source) has no fascicle divisions of its own — it is a single short continuous treatise. This collection splits it into five pages at the treatise's own major doctrinal joints, not at any division CBETA or Zhixu imposed:

1. **Part 1** (base text lines 24–149): the opening pairing of teaching and contemplation, the Eightfold outline, the Five Periods (general and specific readings, with Guanding's polemic against a rigid twelve-year dating), and the Four Teachings of Method — the sudden, gradual, secret, and indeterminate.
2. **Part 2** (lines 150–222): the Tripiṭaka teaching in full.
3. **Part 3** (lines 224–290): the shared teaching in full.
4. **Part 4** (lines 292–354): the distinct teaching in full.
5. **Part 5** (lines 356–461): the perfect teaching in full, the fivefold transfer/graft/converge/gather/borrow scheme binding all Four Teachings together, and the closing dialogue.

Every page's own bilingual `source`/`translation` columns were verified against the CBETA base text for 100% character coverage; the whole five-page set was additionally cross-checked against the full base text (lines 24–461) as a single merged run, with no gaps found. Zhixu's commentary appends one further section, "On the Substance of the Three Kinds of Loving-Kindness" (附三慈體相), that answers no specific line of the base treatise; it is described but not translated in full on Part 5, since this project's coverage requirement applies to the base text itself.

## Terminology inherited from this site's three earlier Tiantai projects

## Terminology coined for this project

The following terms had no fixed rendering anywhere on this site before this project and are recorded here for later Tiantai texts — especially anything drawing on the *Fahua Xuanyi* or *Fahua Wenju*, which this treatise cites repeatedly — to inherit rather than re-coin.

| Chinese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 化儀四教 / 化法四教 | the Four Teachings of Method / the Four Teachings of Content | The treatise's own top-level split of the Eight Teachings (八教) — Method covering how the Buddha taught (頓漸祕密不定), Content covering what he taught (藏通別圓). |
| 頓 / 漸 / 祕密 / 不定 | the sudden / the gradual / the secret / the indeterminate | The Four Teachings of Method, each further split into "as a division of the canon" (教部/教相 pairing) and "as a mark" that cuts across those divisions. |
| 五時 | the Five Periods | Avataṃsaka, Āgama, Vaipulya, Prajñā, Lotus-and-Nirvāṇa — kept as the standard period names rather than translated as a set phrase, matching how this site already keeps sūtra titles like "the Lotus Sūtra" untranslated. |
| 通別五時 | the general and the specific [readings of the] Five Periods | Guanding's own distinction, preserved by Zhixu: "specific" readings have fixed date-ranges, "general" readings acknowledge each period actually runs through the Buddha's whole teaching career. |
| 半字 / 滿字 | half-word / full-word | The Nirvāṇa Sūtra's elementary-and-advanced-schooling simile for why the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings are "half" and the distinct and perfect are "full." |
| 真諦 / 俗諦 / 中諦 | the truth of the real / the conventional truth / the truth of the middle | Kept as "real" rather than "ultimate" to preserve the literal 真/俗/中 (real/conventional/middle) triad in English; 真諦 within the Tripiṭaka and shared teachings' own two-truth scheme specifically denotes emptiness realized, as distinct from the distinct and perfect teachings' third truth, 中諦. |
| 偏真 | the one-sided real | The Tripiṭaka and shared teachings' own terminal truth — "real" but only half the picture, lacking the middle. |
| 但中 / 圓中 | the mere middle / the perfect middle | The distinct teaching's middle is bare principle, not containing all dharmas ("mere"); the perfect teaching's middle already contains every dharma complete ("perfect"). This single distinction is what determines whether a sharp shared-teaching practitioner is grafted into the distinct or the perfect teaching. |
| 接 / 轉 / 同 / 會 / 借 | grafting / transfer / convergence / gathering / borrowing | The treatise's own closing fivefold scheme (附轉接同會借說) for how a practitioner or a technical term in one teaching relates to the teaching beyond it. Kept as five distinct English terms since the base text is explicit that each names a genuinely different relationship, not synonyms for "advancing." |
| 五行：聖行 / 梵行 / 天行 / 嬰兒行 / 病行 | the five practices: the noble practice / the divine practice / the heavenly practice / the practice of the infant / the practice of illness | One of the eight ways (教理智斷行位因果) the distinct teaching is said to stand apart from every teaching around it. |
| 五住煩惱 | the five categories of dwelling-affliction | Named wherever "perfectly subduing" (圓伏) or similar perfect-teaching formulas appear; not itemized in the base text itself. |
| 六度十度 | the six and ten pāramitās | The standard six pāramitās, with prajñā further opened into skillful means, vow, power, and wisdom (as a distinct tenth) once a teaching's own sophistication warrants it. |
| 三德：法身 / 般若 / 解脫 | the three virtues: the Dharma-body / prajñā / liberation | Mapped in this treatise onto three named nirvāṇas (intrinsic purity / perfect purity / expedient purity) and onto the correct, complete, and conditioning causes of buddha-nature; whether a teaching realizes all three simultaneously from the very first identity onward is what Zhixu uses to explain why only the perfect teaching's six identities are true "identities" at all. |
| 三因佛性：正因 / 了因 / 緣因 | the three causes of buddha-nature: the correct cause / the complete cause / the conditioning cause | Paired with the three virtues above. |
| 八忍八智 | the eight patiences and eight kinds of knowledge | Patience as uncontaminated concentration on the uninterrupted path, knowledge as uncontaminated wisdom on the path of liberation — one pair for each of the Four Noble Truths, in the desire realm and in the form-and-formless realms together. |
| 三十四心 | the thirty-four minds | The path of vision's sixteen (eight patiences plus eight knowledges) plus the path of cultivation's eighteen (one uninterrupted path and one path of liberation per each of the three realms' nine grounds), realized all at once by a Tripiṭaka-teaching buddha. |
| 十善巧安心 / 十乘觀法 (base text's own per-teaching versions) | the ten modes of contemplation | Each of the Four Teachings has its own ten-item version of this list; the perfect teaching's own version is illustrated throughout with the Lotus Sūtra's parable of the great cart, each mode mapped onto a physical part of the cart. |

## A note on the "current single thought"

Zhixu's reading note on the perfect teaching's ten modes of contemplation (Part 5) explains why every mode of the perfect teaching's own contemplation is anchored to "the current single thought of mind" (現前一念心) rather than a grander object — this is the same analytical move already tracked at length in the *Mo-ho Chih-kuan* translation notes' entries for 一念三千 and 現前一念. Readers encountering this treatise before the *Mo-ho Chih-kuan* may want to read that project's fascicle 5 notes alongside this collection's Part 5 for the fuller argument; this project's own reading note gives only the condensed version Zhixu's commentary itself provides for the *Jiaoguan Gangzong*.
